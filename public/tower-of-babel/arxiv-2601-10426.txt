Algebraic functional equation for big Galois representations over multiple ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions
Zeping Hao, Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
We present a general approach to establish algebraic functional equations for big Galois representations over multiple ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions. Our result is formulated in both Selmer group and Selmer complex settings, and encompasses a broad range of Iwasawa-theoretic scenarios. In particular, our result applies to the triple product of Hida families in both balanced and unbalanced cases, as well as the half-ordinary Rankin-Selberg universal deformations recently studied by the first named author and Loeffler. Our result also significantly generalizes many previously known cases of algebraic functional equations and answers a question of Greenberg. Keywords and Phrases: Algebraic functional equation, big Galois representation, multiple ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 11G05, 11R23, 11S25.

Introduction

The main conjecture in Iwasawa theory posits a precise relationship between a Selmer group and a conjectural
p
-adic
L

Taking into consideration both the main conjecture and the conjectured functional equation, we expect an algebraic relationship between the Selmer groups corresponding to a Galois representation and its Tate dual, which should mirror the functional equation of the

Following this foundational work, there have been attempts extending this result to big Galois representations, i.e., representations with coefficients in a power series ring (for instance, see
[
20
,
21
]

-extensions does not seem to be present in current existing literature. Additionally, it should be noted that the work of
[
20
,
21
]

The goal of this paper is to provide this unifying framework. We prove an algebraic functional equation for big Galois representations, which satisfy a general axiomatic setting (see conditions

-

Without further delay, we shall now describe the outline of the proof leaving details to the body of the paper. The first step of the proof involves introducing an alternative Selmer group defined by Greenberg, known as the strict Selmer group (see
[
8
, Page 121]
). The motivation for utilizing this group stems from the fact that, while the Greenberg Selmer group is indeed the appropriate object for formulating the main conjecture, the strict Selmer group is more amenable to global duality arguments which suits our purposes for proving Theorem
3.3
. Furthermore, the strict Selmer groups serve as an intermediary object, enabling us to establish a connection between the Greenberg Selmer group and the Selmer complex (see Remark
4.4
). As it turns out, under our assumption
(R2)
, we can show that the Pontryagin dual of the Greenberg Selmer group, the Pontryagin dual of the strict Selmer group and the second cohomology group of the Selmer complex are pseudo-isomorphic to one another (see Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
). Hence Theorem
3.3
is equivalent to
Theorem
3.6
, and the same applies to Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.7
. Consequently, in either context, proving one will be sufficient.

For the proof of Theorem
3.3
(which is equivalent to Theorem
3.6
), we will prove the assertions for the strict Selmer group following Greenberg’s approach. For the proof of Theorem
3.5
(or its equivalent, Theorem
3.7
), we prove the Selmer complex version, and the proof proceeds in the following sequence of steps.
For the first step, we leverage the properties of the Selmer complex to show that, in order to prove the theorem for a given datum over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension, it is sufficient to establish the theorem for another larger datum that is constructed from the original datum but over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Although this larger datum is defined over a power series ring with more variables than the original, it still falls under our axiomatic setting. The crux of this reduction lies in the fact that our proof can now proceed within the fixed setting of
F_{\infty}=F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
, thus paving the way for a proof by induction, namely, on the number of variables in the power series ring
R
(see Remark
6.3
and the preceding discussion).

For the final inductive argument, the idea is to choose infinite many linear ideals (see Definition
2.3
) of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m}\rrbracket
appropriately so that the datum, a priori defined over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m}\rrbracket
, specializes to data defined over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m-1}\rrbracket
, whose Selmer complex’s second cohomology group is pseudo-isomorphic to the corresponding specialization of the second cohomology group of Selmer complex of the starting datum. It is precisely at this juncture that the generality of our framework proves advantageous, as such specializations may not inherently have any arithmetic meanings, even when the original datum we started with comes from an arithmetic object. Nevertheless, we can show that these specialized data, upon an appropriate choice of linear ideals, still satisfy
(C1)-(C4)
, as well as
(R1)
and
(R2)
, thus allowing us to conclude that Theorem
3.5
is valid for these specialized data by our induction hypothesis. Subsequently, we make use of an algebraic result (see Proposition
2.6
) to obtain the conclusion for the original datum which is defined over
\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m}\rrbracket
.

We collect certain algebraic results that will be required for the discussion of the paper.
Firstly, we make a general observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be an integral domain, and
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal of
R
. Suppose that
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module such that
M/\mathfrak{p}
is torsion over
R/\mathfrak{p}
. Then the following assertions are valid.

(i)

M
is torsion over
R
.

(ii)

Furthermore, in the event that
R
is Noetherian, we have that
\Tor_{j}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)
is torsion over
R/\mathfrak{p}
for every
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

We start by claiming that
\mathfrak{p}+\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M)\neq\mathfrak{p}
, and we will set out to establish our claim. Suppose on the
contrary that
\mathfrak{p}+\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M)=\mathfrak{p}
. For an ideal
J
of
R
, we write
\mathrm{rad}(J)
for the (prime) radical of
J
, i.e.,
\mathrm{rad}(J)
consists of elements
x\in R
such that
x^{n(x)}\in J
for some positive integer
n(x)
. Then we have

\mathrm{rad}\big(\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{p})\big)=\mathrm{rad}\big(\mathfrak{p}+\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M)\big)=\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p},

(1)

where the first equality follows from
[
31
, P. 13, Exercise 2.2]
, the second by our supposition and the final equality follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal. On the other hand, in view of the hypothesis that
M/\mathfrak{p}
is torsion over
R/\mathfrak{p}
, there exists an element
r\notin\mathfrak{p}
such that
(r+\mathfrak{p})\in R/\mathfrak{p}
annihilates
M/\mathfrak{p}
. This in turn implies that

annihilates
M/\mathfrak{p}
. However, by (
1
), the latter will force

which yields the required contradiction. This completes the proof of the claim.

By the claim, it particularly follows that
\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M)\neq 0
, which thereby establishes (i). For (ii), we first note that
\Tor_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)
can be viewed as a
R
-module and a

-module at the same time. Since

is Noetherian and

is finitely generated over
R
,
\Tor_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)
is finitely generated over
R/\mathfrak{p}
. In view of the latter finite generation, we are reduced to showing that
\mathrm{Ann}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}\big(\Tor^{R}_{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)\big)\neq 0
. Now, viewing
\Tor_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)
as a
R
-module, we have the following inclusion

\mathfrak{p}+\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}_{R}\big(\Tor^{R}_{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)\big).

Combining this observation with our claim, we see that there exists an element
r\in\mathrm{Ann}_{R}\big(\Tor^{R}_{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)\big)
with
r\notin\mathfrak{p}
. This in turn implies that
\mathrm{Ann}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}\big(\Tor^{R}_{j}(R/\mathfrak{p},M)\big)\neq 0
, thereby proving assertion (ii) of the lemma. ∎

We now prove the following important algebraic result which will be a key ingredient to the proof of our main result.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated torsion
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
-modules, where
R=\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m}\rrbracket
with
m\geq 1
. Suppose that the following statements are valid.

(a)

The modules
M
and
N
are finitely generated as
R
-modules.

(b)

There exist infinitely many distinct linear ideals
(\ell_{i})
of
R
satisfying all of the following.

(i)

For every
i
, we have
(\ell_{i})\in\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)\cap\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N)
.

(ii)

For every
i
, there is an identity
\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M/\ell_{i})=\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N/\ell_{i})
, where
R_{i}=R/\ell_{i}
.

Then we have
\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)=\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N)
.

Proof.

Set
f:=\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)
and
g:=\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N)
. Write
f=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}W^{n}
, where
c_{n}\in R
. We first show that not all of the
c_{n}
’s lie in
\mathfrak{m}
. Given that
M
is torsion over
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R\llbracket W\rrbracket/f_{1}\times\cdots\times R\llbracket W\rrbracket/f_{s}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0,

with
f=\prod_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}
and
P
being a pseudo-null
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
-module. From which, we obtain an exact sequence

\Tor_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}^{1}(R\llbracket W\rrbracket/\mathfrak{m},P)\longrightarrow R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f_{1},\mathfrak{m})\times\cdots\times R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f_{s},\mathfrak{m})\longrightarrow M/\mathfrak{m}\longrightarrow P/\mathfrak{m}\longrightarrow 0

As
M
is finitely generated over
R
by assumption (a), it follows from Nakayama lemma that
M/\mathfrak{m}
is finite which in turn implies that
P/\mathfrak{m}
is finite. By Lemma
2.1
, we then have that
\Tor_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}^{1}(R\llbracket W\rrbracket/\mathfrak{m},P)
is also finite. Hence we may conclude that
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f_{j},\mathfrak{m})
is finite for every
j
. But
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f_{j},\mathfrak{m})\cong\mathcal{O}/\pi\llbracket W\rrbracket/\overline{f}_{j}
, where
\overline{f}_{j}=f_{j}
(mod
\mathfrak{m}
), and the latter is finite if and only if
\overline{f}_{j}\neq 0
, or equivalently,
f_{j}\notin\mathfrak{m}R\llbracket W\rrbracket
. Since
\mathfrak{m}R\llbracket W\rrbracket
is a prime ideal of
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
, we see that
f\notin\mathfrak{m}R\llbracket W\rrbracket
, and this proves what we set to show.

Taking the preceding assertion into account, it then follows from an application of the division lemma (see
[
33
, 5.3.1]
) that
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/f
is a free
R
-module of finite rank. On the other hand, since
R
is a unique factorization domain, there is a natural injection

R/l_{1}\cdots l_{k}\hookrightarrow R/\ell_{1}\times\cdots\times R/\ell_{k}

(2)

for every
k
. As
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/f
is a free
R
-module of finite rank, upon applying
\otimes_{R}R\llbracket W\rrbracket/f
, we obtain an injection

R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f,l_{1}\cdots l_{k})\hookrightarrow R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f,l_{1})\times\cdots\times R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f,l_{k})

(3)

Combining this with assumption (b), we see that the image of
g
in
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f,l_{1}\cdots l_{k})
is zero for every
k
. Letting
k\to\infty
, we have that the image of
g
in
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(f)
is zero.
A similar argument then also shows that the image of
f
in
R\llbracket W\rrbracket/(g)
is zero. This completes the proof of our result.
∎

Remark 2.7

(1)

We shall say a bit on the error in the proof of

Finally, in view of assumption (b)(i) of Proposition

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M
be a torsion
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
-module, where
R=\mathcal{O}\llbracket W_{1},...,W_{m}\rrbracket
with
m\geq 1
. Denote by

the maximal pseudo-null
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
-submodule of
M
. Suppose that
\ell
is a linear element of

such that
M_{\mathrm{null}}[\ell]
is pseudo-null over
R/\ell\llbracket G\rrbracket
and that
M/\ell
is torsion over
R/\ell\llbracket W\rrbracket
. Then
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we shall write
\overline{R}=R/\ell
. Since
M/\ell
is already assumed to be torsion over
\overline{R}\llbracket W\rrbracket
, it remains to show that the image of
\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)
in
\overline{R}\llbracket W\rrbracket
is equal to
\mathrm{char}_{\overline{R}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M/\ell)
. Consider the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M_{\mathrm{null}}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0,

where

is some pseudo-null
R\llbracket W\rrbracket
-module and
E=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}R\llbracket W\rrbracket/\mathfrak{p}^{a_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for some height one prime

of

(\ell)
(this is necessary due to the

Since the prime ideals appearing in

and

is a unique factorization domain, we have

which in turn implies that

. Taking these into account, we obtain the following two exact sequences

0\longrightarrow M_{\mathrm{null}}/\ell\longrightarrow M/\ell\longrightarrow Z/\ell\longrightarrow 0,

Now since

is a pseudo-null

\mathrm{char}_{\overline{R}}(M/\ell)=\mathrm{char}_{\overline{R}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(E/\ell)=\mathrm{char}_{R\llbracket W\rrbracket}(E)/\ell.

Proof.

It suffices to check that the datum

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\sum_{w|p}(d-d_{w})[L_{w}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]&=\displaystyle\sum_{v|p}\sum_{w|v}(d-d_{v})[L_{w}:F_{v}][F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\\

&=\displaystyle d[L:F]r_{2}(F)+[L:F]\sum_{v~\mathrm{real}}(d-d^{+}_{v}).\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\sum_{w|p}(d-d_{w})[L_{w}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]&=\displaystyle\sum_{v|p}\sum_{w|v}(d-d_{v})[L_{w}:F_{v}][F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\\

(R2)
. Then
X_{Gr}(A/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
and

-ranks. Moreover, we have a pseudo-isomorphism

where we denote

Remark 3.4
.

In

4
Comparing Selmer groups and Selmer complexes

In this section, we relate the Greenberg Selmer groups and Selmer complexes. To facilitate this discussion, we introduce another Selmer group of Greenberg, specifically, the strict Selmer group, as defined in
[
8
, Page 121]
. Let
L
be a finite extension of
F
which is contained in
F_{\infty}
. Now, consider the following local condition

H^{1}_{str}(L_{w},A)=\begin{cases}\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L_{w},A^{-}_{w})\big)&\text{\mbox{if} $w|p$},\\
\ker\big(H^{1}(L_{w},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{ur}_{w},A)\big)&\text{\mbox{if} $w\nmid p$.}\end{cases}

The strict Selmer group attached to the datum
\big(A,\{A_{w}\}_{w|p}\big)_{R,L}
is then defined by

\Sel_{str}(A/L)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}\bigoplus_{w|v}\frac{H^{1}(L_{w},A)}{H^{1}_{str}(L_{w},A)}\right).

We set
\Sel_{str}(A/F_{\infty})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}\Sel_{str}(A/L)
,
where the limit runs over all finite extensions
L
of
F
contained
in
F_{\infty}
. The Pontryagin dual of
\Sel_{str}(A/F_{\infty})
is then denoted by
X_{str}(A/F_{\infty})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension of
F
which contains
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
.
For each
v\in S
, we fix a prime of
F_{\infty}
above
v
which we, by abuse of notation, denote by
v
. Writing
G_{v}
for the decomposition group of
G
at
v
, one then has the following identifications

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}\bigoplus_{w|v}\frac{H^{1}(L_{w},A)}{H^{1}_{Gr}(L_{w},A)}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})^{\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v})}\Big),&\hbox{if $v\mid p$}\\
\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\Big),&\hbox{if $v\nmid p$,}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}\bigoplus_{w|v}\frac{H^{1}(L_{w},A)}{H^{1}_{str}(L_{w},A)}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\Big),&\hbox{if $v\mid p$}\\
\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\Big),&\hbox{if $v\nmid p$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We shall only supply a proof for the
Gr
local condition in the case of
v\mid p
, as the remaining cases can be proven similar. By definition, we have the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{1}_{Gr}(L_{v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L_{v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L_{v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})

which upon taking limit yields

0\longrightarrow\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits H^{1}_{Gr}(L_{v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})

(4)

Note that the map
H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})
is given by the composition

H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})^{\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v})}\subseteq H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v}).

The first map is surjective by
[
33
, Theorem 7.1.8]
, and since
\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v})
has
p
-cohomological dimension at most one, so is the second map. Taking these observations into account, the sequence (
4
) in turn induces the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits H^{1}_{Gr}(L_{v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})^{\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v})}\longrightarrow 0,

which is what we want to show.
∎

For notational brevity, we shall write

J_{v}^{Gr}(A/F_{\infty})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur},A^{-}_{v})^{\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v})}\big),&\hbox{if $v\mid p$}\\
\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\big),&\hbox{if $v\nmid p$.}\end{array}\right.

J_{v}^{str}(A/F_{\infty})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\big),&\hbox{if $v\mid p$}\\
\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\big(H^{1}(F_{\infty,v},A)\big),&\hbox{if $v\nmid p$.}\end{array}\right.

The next lemma gives a relation between the two Selmer groups of Greenberg which will play a crucial role in our subsequent discussion.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a surjection

X_{Gr}(A/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow X_{str}(A/F_{\infty}),

whose kernel is a torsion
R\llbracket H\rrbracket
-module (and hence pseudo-null
R\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module).
We also have an analogous assertion upon replacing all occurrence of
A
by
A^{*}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, the pseudo-nullity assertion will follow once we can show the
R\llbracket H\rrbracket
-torsionness property. In the course of the proof, for a given
\Gal(\overline{F}_{v}/F_{v})
-module
Z
, we shall frequently write
Z(\mathcal{L})=Z^{\Gal(\overline{F}_{v}/\mathcal{L})}
for any algebraic extension
\mathcal{L}
of
F_{v}
. By definition, the two Selmer groups fit into the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel_{str}(A/F_{\infty})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}^{str}(A/F_{\infty})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sel_{Gr}(A/F_{\infty})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}^{Gr}(A/F_{\infty})}

with exact rows. Taking Lemma
4.1
into account, we see that the kernel of the rightmost vertical map is given by the finite sum

\bigoplus_{v\mid p}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big).

By the snake lemma, we then have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Sel_{str}(A/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow\Sel_{Gr}(A/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\mid p}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big).

(5)

Now observe that for each
v|p
, we have

\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big)=\bigoplus_{w|v}\mathrm{Coind}^{H}_{H_{w}}H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big),

where
w
runs through the (finitely many) primes of
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
above
v
.
It therefore remains to show that each

H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big)^{\vee}

is torsion over
R\llbracket H_{w}\rrbracket
. In fact, we’re going to show an even stronger result: this module is torsion over
R
. Denote by
F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}
the maximal
p
-extension of
F_{\infty,v}
contained in
F_{\infty,v}^{ur}
. Then we have

H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur})\big)\cong H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p})\big).

Now, had
\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v})
being finite, then
H^{1}\big(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p})\big)^{\vee}
is annihilated by a sufficiently large power of
p
, and so is necessarily a torsion
R
-module. Thus, we may assume that
\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Since
A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p})^{\vee}
is finitely generated over
R
, it is in particular a torsion
R\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v})\rrbracket
-module. In view of this, Lemma
2.2
then yields the following equality

\corank_{R}\Big(H^{1}(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}))\Big)=\corank_{R}\Big(H^{0}(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}))\Big).

But
H^{0}(\Gal(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}/F_{\infty,v}),A^{-}_{v}(F_{\infty,v}^{ur,p}))
is precisely
H^{0}(F_{\infty,w},A_{v}^{-})
which is cotorsion over
R
by our assumption
(R2)
.
∎

We now relate the strict Selmer group to the Selmer complex.
To do this, we recall from
[
34
, Definition 5.3.1.1]
the complex of continuous
cochains with compact support

C_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})=\mathrm{cone}\left(C(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{res}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{v\in S}C(\Gal(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v}),T^{*})\right)[-1]

which fits into the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow C_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\longrightarrow SC(T^{*}/F)\longrightarrow U_{S}(T^{*})\longrightarrow 0

of complexes (see
[
34
, (6.1.3)]
). Upon taking cohomology, we obtain the following long exact sequence

\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{0}(F_{v},T_{v}^{*})\oplus\bigoplus_{v\nmid p}H^{0}(\Gal(F_{v}^{ur}/F_{v}),(T^{*})^{I_{v}})\longrightarrow H^{1}_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F))\\
\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{1}(F_{v},T_{v}^{*})\oplus\bigoplus_{v\nmid p}H^{1}(\Gal(F_{v}^{ur}/F_{v}),(T^{*})^{I_{v}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\\
\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{2}(F_{v},T_{v}^{*})\longrightarrow H^{3}_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\longrightarrow H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow 0.

(6)

By Poitou-Tate duality and local Tate duality (for instances, see
[
34
, Propositions 5.2.4 and 5.2.7]
or
[
25
, Theorem 4.2.6]
), the above exact sequence can be rewritten as

\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{0}(F_{v},T_{v}^{*})\oplus\bigoplus_{v\nmid p}H^{0}(\Gal(F_{v}^{ur}/F_{v}),(T^{*})^{I_{v}})\longrightarrow H^{2}(G_{S}(F),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F))\\
\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{1}(F_{v},A_{v}^{-})^{\vee}\oplus\bigoplus_{v\nmid p}H^{1}(\Gal(F_{v}^{ur}/F_{v}),A^{I_{v}})^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(G_{S}(F),A)^{\vee}\\
\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{0}(F_{v},A_{v}^{-})^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{3}_{c}(G_{S}(F),T^{*})\longrightarrow H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow 0.

(7)

In particular, the Pontryagin dual of the two terms in the middle line of the above, together with their map
\rho
, identifies with

H^{1}(G_{S}(F),A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho^{\vee}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{1}(F_{v},A_{v}^{-})\oplus\bigoplus_{v\nmid p}H^{1}(\Gal(F_{v}^{ur}/F_{v}),A^{I_{v}}).

This in turn implies that
\mathrm{coker}\,\rho=X_{str}(A/F)
. Therefore, we obtain the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow X_{str}(A/F)\\
\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{0}(F_{v},A_{v}^{-})^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{S}(F),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow 0.

For a finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
F_{\infty}
, we have a similar exact sequence as above, and upon taking inverse limit, we obtain

0\longrightarrow X_{str}(A/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{0}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\Big)^{\vee}\\
\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\longrightarrow 0,

(8)

where
SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty})
is the inverse limit of the complexes
SC(T^{*}/L)
in the sense of
[
34
]
. We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension of
F
which contains the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Suppose that our datum
(T,\{T_{v}\}_{v|p})_{R,F}
satisfies
(C1)-(C4)
,
(R1)
and
(R2)
. Then the following statements are valid.

(a)

There is an injection
X_{str}(A/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))
with cokernel being finitely generated torsion over
R\llbracket H\rrbracket
.

(b)

Both
H^{3}(SC(T/F_{\infty}))
and
H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))
are torsion over

(and so pseudo-null over

Proof.

By the long exact sequence (

), it suffices to show that
H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}
and

\bigoplus_{v|p}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{0}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\Big)^{\vee}

are finitely generated torsion

-modules. Now, if

, and hence

, then

is indeed torsion over

by

with

and so is necessarily torsion over

.
For the local cohomology groups, a similar argument appealing to
(R2)
applies.
∎

\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v|p}\mathrm{Coind}^{G}_{G_{v}}\Big(H^{0}(F_{\infty,v},A^{-}_{v})\Big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{3}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\longrightarrow 0,

In this section, we shall prove Theorems

and

\mathcal{O}

. Note that in this context, the conditions

and

translate into the following two assertions.

(R2)

H^{0}(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{v}^{-})
and

and every prime

of

above

.

In the forthcoming discussion of this section, we will denote
\Gamma
as

and use

to denote the torsion submodule of

, where

is a given
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module. Additionally, we will occasionally, without explicit mention, identify

, a power series ring in one variable. We also fix a uniformizer
\pi
of

In

As a start, we present the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

and

\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket
-modules. Suppose that the following

(I)

For every positive integer

(II)

For every irreducible Weierstrass polynomial

Proof.

This is essentially contained in
[
8

[
27
, Proposition 2.4.7]

and
\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N)

is pseudo-isomorphic to
N[\pi^{\infty}]
. It therefore remains to show that
M[f^{\infty}]
is pseudo-isomorphic to
N[f^{\infty}]
for every irreducible Weierstrass polynomial

. Suppose that the elementary representations of
M[f^{\infty}]
and
N[f^{\infty}]
are given by

\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket/f^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}(\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket/f^{i})^{\oplus b_{i}}

respectively, where

and

\corank_{\mathcal{O}}\big((M^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket/f^{n})^{\Gamma}\big)=\corank_{\mathcal{O}}\Hom_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M,K/\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket/f^{n}).

The latter quantity identifies with the
\mathcal{O}

r_{n}:=\rank_{\mathcal{O}}\big(\Hom_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M,\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket/f^{n})\big)=\Big(n\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)+a_{1}+2a_{2}+\cdots+(n-1)a_{n}+n(a_{n}+\cdots+a_{\theta})\Big)\deg(f),

for every
1\leq n\leq\theta
.

\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(M)=\rank_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket W\rrbracket}(N)
, and given that

, as stipulated by assumption (II), we can obtain the following matrix equation

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&2&2&\cdots&2\\
1&2&3&\cdots&3\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&2&3&\cdots&\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
a_{3}\\
\vdots\\
a_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&2&2&\cdots&2\\
1&2&3&\cdots&3\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&2&3&\cdots&\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\\
b_{3}\\
\vdots\\
b_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}.

Since the square matrix presented above is clearly invertible, we see that

for every
1\leq i\leq\theta

M[f^{\infty}]
and
N[f^{\infty}]
are the same. This concludes the proof of the lemma.

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(G_{S}(F),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F)).

Since we also have an exact sequence similar to the above for every finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
F_{\infty}
, we may take limit over these extensions and obtain

H^{2}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))

(13)

Now, recall that the fine Selmer group
R(A^{*}/F_{\infty})
(in the sense of
[
4
]
; see also
[
26
]
) is defined by the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R(A^{*}/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}K_{v}(A^{*}/F_{\infty}),

where
K_{v}(A^{*}/F_{\infty})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{L}\oplus_{w|v}H^{1}(L_{w},A^{*})
, and one checks easily that there is a natural injection
R(A^{*}/F_{\infty})\hookrightarrow\Sel_{str}(A^{*}/F_{\infty})
. As a consequence, we see that
R(A^{*}/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is also torsion over
R\llbracket G\rrbracket
. By
[
26
, Lemma 7.1]
, this in turn implies that

H^{2}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)=0.

(14)

On the other hand, standard coranks calculations
[
8
, Propositions 1-3]
tell us that

\rank_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}\left(J_{v}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[F_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}](d-d_{v}),&\hbox{if $v|p$;}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

and

\rank_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\right)-\rank_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}\left(H^{2}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A)^{\vee}\right)=r_{2}(F)d+\sum_{v~\mathrm{real}}\left(d-d_{v}^{+})\right),

where
r_{2}(F)
denotes the number of complex primes of
F
. Putting these observations into the exact sequence (
13
) and taking our standing assumption
(C4)
into account, we see that
H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))
is torsion over
R\llbracket G\rrbracket
.

To continue, we need to recall some more terminologies from
[
34
]
. For each
v\in S
, we set

U_{v}^{-}(T^{*})=\mathrm{Cone}\left(U_{v}^{+}(T^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-i_{v}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C(\Gal(\overline{F}_{v}/F_{v}),T^{*})\right)

and
U_{S}^{-}(T^{*})=\bigoplus_{v\in S}U_{v}^{-}(T^{*})
. From
[
34
, (6.1.3)]
, we have the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow U^{-}_{S}(T^{*})\longrightarrow SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty})\longrightarrow C(F_{\infty},T^{*})\longrightarrow 0

of complexes, whose cohomology yields an exact sequence

\bigoplus_{v|p}H^{0}(F_{v},(T_{v}^{*})^{-})\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F))\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(F),T^{*}).

For every finite extension
L
of
F
contained in
F_{\infty}
, we have a similar exact sequence, and upon taking inverse limit over these extensions, we obtain an injection

H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty},T^{*}),

(15)

where we write
H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty},T^{*}):=\displaystyle\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{L}H^{1}(G_{S}(L),T^{*})
. Now consider the following spectral sequence (see
[
34
, Theorem 8.5.6]
)

\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{i}\left((H^{j}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})^{\vee},R\llbracket G\rrbracket\right)\Rightarrow H^{i+j}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty},T^{*}),

whose low-degree terms gives the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{1}\left(H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})^{\vee},R\llbracket G\rrbracket\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty},T^{*})\longrightarrow\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{0}\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})^{\vee},R\llbracket G\rrbracket\right).

Since the module
H^{0}\left(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})\right)^{\vee}
is torsion over
R
by our hypothesis
(R1)
, it is pseudo-null over
R\llbracket G\rrbracket
for the dimension of the group
G
is
\geq 1
. It then follows that the
\Ext^{1}
-term in the above exact sequence vanishes. From which, we obtain an inclusion

H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(F_{\infty},T^{*})\hookrightarrow\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{0}\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})^{\vee},R\llbracket G\rrbracket\right)

which upon combined with (
15
) yields an inclusion

H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))\hookrightarrow\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{0}\left(H^{1}(G_{S}(F_{\infty}),A^{*})^{\vee},R\llbracket G\rrbracket\right).

But an
\Ext_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}^{0}
-term does not contain non-trivial torsion
R\llbracket G\rrbracket
-submodule, and so this forces
H^{1}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\infty}))
to vanish in view of the torsionness observation in the previous paragraph.
∎

We require one more lemma.

Lemma 6.7
.

Suppose that
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
is torsion over
R\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
.
For every linear element
\ell
of
R
which does not lie in the support of
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
,
A(F_{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}
and
\big(A_{w}^{-}(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w})^{\vee}\big)_{R\mathrm{-tor}}
for every
w\in S(F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
, we have
\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\big(H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\big)
.

Proof.

For the verification of this lemma, we shall appeal to Lemma
2.8
.
Our choice of
\ell
guarantees that
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))/\ell
is torsion over
\overline{R}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})_{\mathrm{null}}[\ell]
is pseudo-null over
\overline{R}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. We shall, in fact, show that
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})[\ell]=0
which will imply what we intend to establish.
By Lemma
6.5
, we see that
H^{2}(SC(\overline{T}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
is also torsion over
\overline{R}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, and this in turn implies that
H^{1}(SC(\overline{T}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))=0
by the preceding proposition. The required vanishing then follows from this and the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))/\ell\longrightarrow H^{1}(SC(\overline{T}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\longrightarrow H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))[\ell]\longrightarrow 0.

∎

We can now complete the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
(c).

Since the support of
A(F_{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}
,
A^{*}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}
,
A(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w})^{\vee}
,
A^{*}(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w})^{\vee}
,
H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
and
H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))
only contains finitely many primes of height one, we can always find an infinite collection of linear ideals (which we denote by
\{\ell_{i}\}
) that do not lie in the support of these modules. By Lemma
6.7
, these linear ideals lie in

\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}\big(H^{2}(SC(T/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\big)\cap\mathcal{L}_{R\llbracket G\rrbracket}\big(H^{2}(SC(T^{*}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\big).

For each
i
, we denote by
\{T_{i},T_{i,v})_{v|p}\}_{R_{i},F}
the datum given by
T_{i}:=T/\ell_{i}
and
T_{i,v}:=T_{v}/\ell_{i}
, where
R_{i}:=R/\ell_{i}
. By our choice of linear ideals
(\ell_{i})
’s, we may apply Lemma
6.4
to conclude that the datum
\{T_{i},T_{i,v})_{v|p}\}_{R_{i},F}
satisfies
(C1)-(C4)
,
(R1)
and
(R2)
. On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
6.5
that
H^{2}(SC(Z/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))/\ell_{i}
is torsion over
R_{i}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
with

\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(\big(H^{2}(SC(Z/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))/\ell_{i}\Big)=\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(H^{2}(SC(Z_{i}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\Big)

for
Z\in\{T,T^{*}\}
. On the other hand, in view of Lemma
2.4
, we may apply our induction hypothesis to obtain

\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(H^{2}(SC(T_{i}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\Big)=\mathrm{char}_{R_{i}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\Big(H^{2}(SC(T_{i}^{*}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\Big).

We may therefore apply Proposition
2.6

∎

7.1
Abelian variety with good ordinary reduction at every
p
-adic prime

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over a number field
F
, with the property that
\mathcal{A}
has good ordinary reduction at every prime of
F
above
p
. In this context, we set
T
as the Tate module of
\mathcal{A}
which is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
2g
. By appealing to
[
10
, Proposition 1.1]
,
we see that
T_{v}^{+}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
d
for every real prime
v
. For each
v|p
, it follows from the discussion in
[
3
, P150-151]
that there is
\Gal(\bar{F_{v}}/F_{v})
-submodule, which will take the role of our
A_{v}
, of
\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}]
such that the inertia group acts trivially on
A_{v}^{-}:=\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}]/A_{v}
. Moreover, there is a natural identification
A_{v}^{-}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}[p^{\infty}]
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is the mod-
p
reduction of
\mathcal{A}
, which is an abelian variety over the residue field of
F_{v}
. We set
T_{v}
to be the Tate module of
A_{v}
. Taking into account all of the above observations, one can check that the datum
(T,(T_{v})_{v|p})_{\mathbb{Z},F}
satisfies
(C1)-(C4)
. On the other hand, property
(R1)
follows as a consequence of Imai’s theorem
[
13
]
. For
(R2)
, we first observe that
H^{0}(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{v}^{-})
identifies with the subgroup of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}[p^{\infty}]
consisting of points in the residue field of
F_{\mathrm{cyc},w}
. The latter is plainly a finite field in view that the inertia group of
F_{\mathrm{cyc},w}/F_{v}
is a subgroup of
\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w}/F_{v})
with finite index. Hence it follows that
H^{0}(F_{\mathrm{cyc},w},A_{v}^{-})
is finite. Therefore, Theorem
3.3
/
3.6
and Theorem
3.5
/
3.7
apply. We would like to point out that Lee
[
24
]
has already proven this result in the situation of a cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

We now say something about the torsionness of Selmer groups. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
containing
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
, a classical conjecture of Mazur
[
32
]
(also see
[
35
, Conjecture 7.4]
) posits that
X_{Gr}(\mathcal{A}/F_{\infty})
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket G\rrbracket
-module, where
G=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
. When
\mathcal{A}
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
F
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, a deep result of Kato
[
22
]
says that
X_{Gr}(\mathcal{A}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
-module. Combining this with a result of Hachimori-Ochiai
[
12
]
, one can deduce that
X_{Gr}(\mathcal{A}/F_{\infty})
is a torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\rrbracket
-module for every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
containing
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
.

We should mention that the torsionness conjecture is known to hold, thanks to a deep result of Kato
[
22
]
, in the case where
A
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and
F
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

7.2
p
-ordinary modular forms

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
be a normalized new cuspidal modular eigenform of weight
k\geq 2
, level
N
and nebentypus
\epsilon
. We shall always assume that the coefficient
a_{p}
is a
p
-adic unit (in other words, the modular form
f
is
p
-ordinary). In the event that
k=2
, we assume further that the (odd) prime
p
does not divide the level
N
. (This is to ensure that the absolute value of the unit root of
f
is not one which is required for the verification of
(R2)
; see discussion below.) Let
K_{f}
be the number field obtained by adjoining all the Fourier coefficients of
f
and values of
\epsilon
to
\mathbb{Q}
. Throughout our discussion, we fix a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K_{f}
above
p
. We then let
V_{f}
denote the corresponding two-dimensional
K_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
-linear Galois representation attached to
f
in the sense of Deligne. Writing
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K_{f,\mathfrak{p}}}
for the ring of integers of
K_{f,\mathfrak{p}}
, we denote by
T_{f}
the
\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice in
V_{f}
as defined in
[
22
, Section 8.3]
. By our
p
-ordinarity assumption, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{f,p}\longrightarrow T_{f}\longrightarrow T_{f,p}^{-}\longrightarrow 0

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-modules, where
T_{f,p}
and
T_{f,p}^{-}
are free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank
1
. Furthermore, the Galois group
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts on
T_{f,p}
via an unramified character
\delta_{f}
with the property that
\delta_{f}
sends the (geometric) Frobenius at
p
to the
p
-adic unit to the unit
root of the Hecke polynomial of
f
at
p
. Additionally, the same Galois group
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts on
T_{f,p}^{-}
via
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k}
, where
\kappa
denotes the
p
-adic cyclotomic character.

We shall proceed to verify that the datum
(T_{f},T_{f,p})
satisfies
(R1)
and
(R2)
, leaving the relatively straightforward verification of
(C1)-(C4)
to the readers. For this, we work with the discrete datum
(A,A_{p})
, where
A_{f}:=V_{f}/T_{f}=T_{f}\otimes K_{f}/\mathcal{O}
and
A_{f,p}:=T_{f,p}\otimes K_{f}/\mathcal{O}
.

We now present the following general lemma which will be required for the subsequent discussion.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
F
be either a number field or a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\rho:\Gal(\overline{F}/F)\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)
be a Galois representation, where
V
is finite dimensional
K
-vector space for some finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Suppose that
\rho
is irreducible and that
\dim(\mathrm{Im}~\rho)>1
, where here
\dim
means the dimension as a
p
-adic Lie group. Then for every finite extension
L
of
F
, we have
H^{0}(\Gal(\overline{F}/L_{\mathrm{cyc}}),V)=0
.

Proof.

Clearly, we may assume that
L
is Galois over
F
, and from this assumption, it follows that
\Gal(\overline{F}/L_{\mathrm{cyc}})
is a normal subgroup of
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
. Suppose on the contrary that
V_{0}:=H^{0}(\Gal(\overline{F}/L_{\mathrm{cyc}}),V)\neq 0
. By normality, we see that
V_{0}
is a
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-subrepresentation of
V
. In view of the irreducibility of
V
, we must therefore have
V_{0}=V
. Consequently, the map
\rho
factor through
\Gal(L_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)
to yield a map

\overline{\rho}:\Gal(L_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V),

whose image coincides with
\mathrm{Im}~\rho
. However, since the group
\Gal(L_{\mathrm{cyc}}/F)
can clearly be expressed as a semi-direct product of a copy of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with a finite group, its image under
\overline{\rho}
cannot be a Lie group of dimension
\geq 2
. Thus, we arrive at a contradiction, which proves the lemma.
∎

Equipped with the aforementioned lemma, we are now in a position to verify
(R1)
. Indeed, it is well-known that
V_{f}
is irreducible as a
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-module. Furthermore, the dimension of the image of the map

\rho_{f}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{f})

is
\geq 2
(see
[
37
]
). Hence it follows from Lemma
7.1
that
H^{0}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}),V_{f})=0
, which is equivalent to saying that
H^{0}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{f})
is finite. For the dual datum, one simply notes that
V_{f}^{*}=\Hom_{K_{f,\mathfrak{p}}}(V_{f},K_{f,\mathfrak{p}}(1))
and that taking dual preserves irreducibility and the
p
-adic dimension of the image. Hence this shows
(R1)
.

We come to verifying
(R2)
. For this, we need to return to the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{f,p}\longrightarrow T_{f}\longrightarrow T_{f,p}^{-}\longrightarrow 0

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-modules, and utilize certain description of the modules. In particular,
T_{f,p}
is the unramified subrepresentation with character
\delta_{f}
, and
T_{f,p}^{-}
is the ramified quotient with character
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k}
, where
\kappa
is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character. For the dual datum
(T_{f}^{*},(T_{f,p}^{-})^{*})
, we then have

0\longrightarrow(T_{f,p}^{-})^{*}\longrightarrow T_{f}^{*}\longrightarrow T_{f,p}^{*}\longrightarrow 0,

where
T_{f,p}^{*}
comes equipped with the character
\delta_{f}^{-1}\kappa
. Since
T_{f,p}^{-}
and
T_{f,p}^{*}
are free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank
1
, the validity of
(R2)
will follow once we can show that the characters
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k}
and
\delta_{f}^{-1}\kappa
, upon restricted to
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p,\mathrm{cyc}})
, remain non-trivial. We shall show the slightly stronger assertion, namely,
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k}|_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))}
and
\delta_{f}^{-1}\kappa|_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))}
are non-trivial. Since
\delta_{f}
is unramified, upon restriction, we have
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k}|_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))}=\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon
and

\delta_{f}^{-1}\kappa|_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))}=\delta_{f}^{-1}
. But as seen above,
\delta_{f}
sends the Frobenius at
p
to the unit
root of the Hecke polynomial of
f
at
p
, and this unit root cannot be equal to
1
for it has complex absolute value
p^{(k-1)/2}
. Therefore,
\delta_{f}
is non-trivial, and in fact, cannot be of finite order. Consequently,
\delta_{f}^{-1}\epsilon
is also non-trivial. In conclusion, we have that the datum
(T_{f},T_{f,p})
satisfies
(C1)-(C4)
,
(R1)
and
(R2)
.

Now, let
F
be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and consider the datum
(T_{f},(T_{f,p})_{v|p})_{\mathcal{O},F}
obtained from by base changing to
F
. Since
F
is Galois, it is either totally real or totally imaginary. Therefore, one may apply Lemma
3.1
to conclude that the base-changed datum still satisfy
(C1)-(C4)
. The validity of
(R1)
still follows from Lemma
7.1
. For
(R2)
, one simply notes that
\delta_{f}
is an unramified character of infinite order, and so for every prime
w
of
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
above
v
, we see that upon restricting to
F_{\mathrm{cyc},w}
,
\delta_{f}
remains a character with infinite order. Therefore, the same argument as above carries over.

Hence Theorem
3.3
/
3.6
and Theorem
3.5
/
3.7
are applicable, and we can derive a previous result of Jha-Pal
[
21
, Theorem 3.8]
from our Theorem. It’s worth mentioning that we also obtain a corresponding result for multiple
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, a context not addressed in the work of Jha-Pal. We would also like to note that, through an analysis akin to the one presented above, our main result applies to yield algebraic functional equation results for a nearly ordinary Hilbert modular form, which not only recovers but also extends the prior work of Jha-Majumdar
[
20
]
in this particular situation.

Finally, we say a bit on the torsionness of the Selmer groups. By a deep result of Kato
[
22
]
, the torsionness of
\Sel(A_{f}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}
is known for an elliptic cuspidal modular eigenform
f
when
F
is an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Building on this and following a similar argument to that in
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
, one can see that
\Sel(A_{f}/F_{\mathrm{cyc}})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)\rrbracket
for every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
which contains
F_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. Therefore, in this context, we do obtain algebraic functional equation results unconditionally.

\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acting on
T_{1,p}
via an unramified character
\delta_{1}
of infinite order, and on
T_{1,p}^{-}
via
\delta_{1}^{-1}\epsilon_{1}\kappa^{1-k_{1}}
. Here,
\kappa
denotes the
p
-adic cyclotomic character, as previously defined. In this context, our datum
(T,T_{p})_{\mathcal{O},\mathbb{Q}}
is defined by
T:=T_{1}\otimes T_{2}
and
T_{p}:=T_{1,p}\otimes T_{2}
.
Again, it can be verified directly that this datum satisfies
(C1)-(C4)
.

As will be shown in Section
8
, particularly in Proposition
8.1
, the representation
V:=V_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}}V_{2}
is irreducible. Once we have this irreducibility, we may proceed as in the case of one modular form by appealing to Lemma
7.1
to obtain the validity of
(R1)
.

We now come to
(R2)
. For this, we break into two cases:
f_{2}
is
p
-ordinary or not.
Suppose first that
f_{2}
is non-ordinary at
p
. Then in this case,
V_{2}
is an irreducible
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-module. Therefore, if we let
V_{1,p}^{-}
denote the one-dimensional vector space associated to
T_{1,p}^{-}
, the tensor product
V_{1,p}^{-}\otimes V_{2}
remains irreducible over
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The validity of
(R2)
then follows from an application of Lemma
7.1
.

We next verify
(R2)
for the case where
f_{2}
is ordinary at
p
. Under this assumption, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V_{2,p}\longrightarrow V_{2}\longrightarrow V_{2,p}^{-}\longrightarrow 0

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-modules with
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acting on
V_{2,p}
via an unramified character
\delta_{2}
of infinite order, and on
V_{2,p}^{-}
via
\delta_{2}^{-1}\epsilon\kappa^{1-k_{2}}
. From which, we have
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acting on
V_{1,p}^{-}\otimes V_{2,p}
via
\delta_{1}^{-1}\delta_{2}\epsilon_{1}\kappa^{1-k_{1}}
, and on
V_{1,p}^{-}\otimes V_{2,p}^{-}
via
\delta_{1}^{-1}\delta_{2}^{-1}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\kappa^{2-k_{1}-k_{2}}
. Now, the image of Frobenius at
p
under
\delta_{1}^{-1}\delta_{2}
(resp.
\delta_{1}^{-1}\delta_{2}^{-1}
) has complex absolute value
p^{(k_{1}-k_{2})/2}

(resp.,
p^{(2-k_{1}-k_{2})/2}
). Therefore, we may follow the same argument as in the preceding section to verify that
(R2)
holds. Therefore, our main result applies to yield algebraic functional equation results for Rankin-Selberg products.

7.4
Hida family

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an ordinary cuspidal Hida family defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(in the sense of
[
15
]
). Denote by
R_{\mathcal{F}}
the integral closures of the irreducible components of the ordinary primitive cuspidal Hecke algebras associated to
\mathcal{F}
. In general, this is a finite integral
extension of the power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket W\rrbracket
. We shall assume that
R_{\mathcal{F}}
is a power series ring for our subsequent discussion (see
[
7
]
for sufficient conditions for ensuring this property).
We also assume that the residual representations associated to
\mathcal{F}
is absolutely irreducible, and that the restriction, to the decomposition subgroup
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, of this
residual representation has non-scalar semi-simplifications.

Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
which is assumed to contain
p
, the primes dividing the tame level of
\mathcal{F}
and
\infty
. Associated to
\mathcal{F}
, we have a
G_{S}(\mathbb{Q})
-module
T_{\mathcal{F}}
which is a free
R_{\mathcal{F}}
-module of rank 2 and sits in the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F},p}\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F},p}^{-}\longrightarrow 0

(16)

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-modules. Our datum in this context is
(T_{\mathcal{F}},T_{\mathcal{F},p})_{R_{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{Q}}
, whose discrete datum is in turn denoted by
(A_{\mathcal{F}},A_{\mathcal{F},p})_{R_{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{Q}}
. We also write
A^{-}_{\mathcal{F},p}=A_{\mathcal{F}}/A_{\mathcal{F},p}=T_{\mathcal{F},p}^{-}\otimes_{R_{\mathcal{F}}}R_{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}
.

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}_{f}
is a prime ideal of
R_{\mathcal{F}}
such that
T_{f}:=T/\mathfrak{p}_{f}
corresponds to the Galois representation of a cuspidal modular form
f
of weight
>2
. This fit into a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{f,p}\longrightarrow T_{f}\longrightarrow T_{f,p}^{-}\longrightarrow 0

of
\mathcal{O}[\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-modules with
T_{f,p}=T_{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{f}
and

being free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank one. If we let
(A_{f},A_{f,p})
be the corresponding discrete datum of
(T_{f},T_{f,p})
, one can check easily that
A[\mathfrak{p}_{f}]=A_{f}
and
A^{-}_{p}[\mathfrak{p}_{f}]=A^{-}_{f,p}
. Then as seen in previous subsection, we have that
H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{f})
is finite. Since
H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{\mathcal{F}})[\mathfrak{p}_{f}]=H^{0}(G_{S}(F_{\mathrm{cyc}}),A_{f})

2.2
to deduce that

. A similar argument shows that

is cotorsion over
R_{\mathcal{F}}
, and this shows that
(R1)
is valid. The validity of
(R2)
can be established through an analogous argument.

We can also consider the product of two Hida families
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
. In this situation, we have two choices of datum which will take the form of
(T_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes T_{\mathcal{G}},T_{\mathcal{F},p}\otimes T_{\mathcal{G}})_{R_{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\otimes}R_{\mathcal{G}},\mathbb{Q}}

is defined as in (
16
). One can verify directly both data satisfy
(C1)-(C4)
. Properties
(R1)
and
(R2)
can be shown by a similar argument as above, but this time specialized to the product of two modular forms. Therefore, we obtain an algebraic functional equation for the characteristic ideals of the Selmer groups which aligns with expectations since these ideals are conjecturally related to the
p
-adic
L
-functions constructed by Hida in
[
14
,
16
]
(also see
[
2
]
).

7.6
Half-ordinary Rankin-Selberg universal deformation

This class of examples is taken from the recent work of the first named author and Loeffler
[
11
]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an ordinary cuspidal Hida families defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with coefficient ring
R_{\mathcal{F}}
which is assumed to be a power series ring. Let
T_{\mathrm{univ}}
be the 2-dimensional universal deformation lattice with coefficient ring
R_{\mathrm{univ}}
. We shall assumed that
R_{\mathrm{univ}}\cong\mathcal{O}\llbracket X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\rrbracket
(see
[
1
,
38
]
for discussions on sufficient conditions for ensuring this property). We also assumed that the residual representations of both
T_{\mathcal{F}}
and
T_{\mathrm{univ}}
are absolutely irreducible.
The half-ordinary Rankin-Selberg universal deformation (in the sense of
[
11
,
30
]
) is the Galois lattice
T_{\mathrm{HL}}:=T_{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathrm{univ}}^{*}
, where
T_{\mathrm{univ}}^{*}=\Hom_{R_{\mathrm{univ}}}(T_{\mathrm{univ}},R_{\mathrm{univ}}(1))
. In
[
11
]
, Hao-Loeffler constructed a
4
-variables
p
-adic
L
-function associated to this half ordinary universal deformation and showed that the
p
-adic
L
-function satisfied a functional equation.

An initial naive thought is to perhaps consider the Selmer complex of the datum
(T_{\mathrm{HL}},T_{\mathcal{F},p}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathrm{univ}}^{*})_{R,\mathbb{Q}}
over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}
. However, such a resulting Selmer complex’s cohomology groups will have module structures defined over a power series ring with
5
-variables which definitely does not align with the analytic situation. The correct approach (in the sense of matching the analytic picture) is as follows: write
T_{\mathrm{tame}}
for the deformation lattice with tame determinant, and
R_{\mathrm{tame}}
for its coefficient rings (in the sense of
[
11
, Subsection 4.2]
). It then follows from
[
11
, Lemma 4.1]
that
T_{\mathrm{univ}}\cong T_{\mathrm{tame}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
with
R_{\mathrm{univ}}\cong R_{\mathrm{tame}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, where
\Gamma=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q})
. From this, we obtain an identification

T_{\mathrm{HL}}\cong T_{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket^{\iota}\cong T_{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\widetilde{R}}\widetilde{R}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket^{\iota},

where here
T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*}:=\Hom_{R_{\mathrm{tame}}}(T_{\mathrm{tame}},R_{\mathrm{tame}}(1))
and
\widetilde{R}:=R_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes R_{\mathrm{tame}}
. Note that
\widetilde{R}
in this context is a power series ring in 3 variables. By an argument similar to that in Lemma
6.1
, there is a natural isomorphism

H^{i}(SC(T_{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{cyc}}))\cong H^{i}(SC(T_{\mathrm{HL}}/\mathbb{Q}))

for all
i
.

We then consider the datum
(T_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*},T_{\mathcal{F},p}\otimes T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*})_{R,\mathbb{Q}}
, and we leave the straightforward verification of
(C1)-(C4)
to the readers. Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}_{f}
is a prime ideal of
R_{\mathcal{F}}
of height 1 and that
\mathfrak{p}_{g}
is a prime ideal of
R_{\mathrm{univ}}
of height 2 such that
T_{f}:=T_{\mathcal{F}}/\mathfrak{p}_{f}
and
T_{g}:=T_{\mathrm{univ}}/\mathfrak{p}_{g}
correspond to the Galois representations of cuspidal modular forms
f
and
g
with weights
k
and
l
respectively, where
k>l\geq 2
. Via the identification
R_{\mathrm{univ}}\cong R_{\mathrm{tame}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
, we have a prime ideal
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{g}
of
R_{\mathrm{tame}}
such that
T_{\mathrm{tame}}/\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{g}\cong T_{g}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{t})
. Together with
\mathfrak{p}_{f}
, we obtain a prime ideal
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{f,g}
of
\widetilde{R}
such that
T_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes T_{\mathrm{tame}}^{*}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{f,g}\cong T_{f}\otimes T_{g}(t)
. We can now verify that
(R1)
and
(R2)
are satisfied by a similar specialization argument as before.

8
Appendix

Let
f,g,h
be normalized cuspidal Hecke eigenforms with respective weights
k_{f},k_{g},k_{h}\geq 2
. We shall choose a large enough finite extension
E/\mathbb{Q}
so that the respective associated representations
V_{f},V_{g},V_{h}
can be viewed as representations over
E
(after base-changing). The goal of this appendix is to give some sufficient conditions to ensure that the tensor products
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
and
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}\otimes_{E}V_{h}
are irreducible
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-representations. This is required for our discussion in the previous section for the verification of
(R1)
.

We start with the case of two modular forms.

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose that
f
has no complex multiplication. In the event that
g
also has no complex multiplication, assume further that
k_{f}\neq k_{g}
. Then
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is irreducible as a
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-representation.

Proof.

We first consider the case that
g
has complex multiplication given by an imaginary quadratic field
K
. Let
c
be an element in
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
that projects to the nontrivial element of
\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
. By complex multiplication, there is an algebraic Hecke character
\Psi
of
K
of infinity type
(k_{g}-1,0)
such that
V_{g}\cong\mathrm{Ind}^{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})}_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)}E(\psi)
, where
\psi:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)\longrightarrow E^{\times}
is the
p
-adic Hecke character associated
\Psi
. From this, we obtain

V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}\cong\mathrm{Ind}^{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})}_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)}\mathrm{Res}^{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})}_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)}V_{f}(\psi).

Writing
W:=\mathrm{Res}^{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})}_{\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)}V_{f}
, it then follows from Clifford theory that
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is reducible if and only if
W(\psi)\cong W(\psi)^{c}
. Now, as
W\cong W^{c}
, the latter would imply that
\psi^{c}\psi^{-1}
is a quadratic character, and in particular, a finite order character. However, this cannot happen in view that
\Phi^{c}\Phi^{-1}
has infinity type
(1-k_{g},k_{g}-1)
. Thus, the representation
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
must be irreducible.

We now come to the situation when
g
has no complex multiplication. For this part of the discussion, we shall freely use the material and notation as given in the work of Loeffler
[
29
]
. Let
H
be the subgroup of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
cut out by all the inner twists of
f
and
g
(see
[
29
, Definition 2.2.1]
for the definition of inner twists). By comparing determinants and taking into account that
k_{f}>k_{g}
, one can see that
f
is not Galois-conjugate to a twist of
g
. Therefore, it follows from the discussion in
[
29
, Subsection 3.3]
(also see
[
29
, Theorem 3.4.2]
) that the image of
H
under the group homomorphism

\rho_{f}\times\rho_{g}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{f})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{g})

is an open subgroup of

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{(x_{f},x_{g},\lambda)\in B_{f}^{\times}\times B_{g}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}~:~\mathrm{norm}(x_{f})=\lambda^{1-k_{f}},~\mathrm{norm}(x_{g})=\lambda^{1-k_{g}}\right\},

where here
B_{f}
and
B_{g}
are the quaternion algebras defined over an appropriate localization of the respective inner twist fields of
f
and
g
. (In particular, we note that the quaternion algebras presented here are not those in
[
29
, Definition]
but rather appropriate localizations of those.) Note that the group homomorphism

\rho_{f\otimes g}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g})

is precisely the composite of
\rho_{f}\times\rho_{g}
with the Kronecker product
\mathrm{GL}(V_{f})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{g})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g})
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is irreducible when viewed as a
\mathcal{G}
-representation which in turn reduces to verifying that
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is irreducible when viewed as a
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
-representation, where
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
denotes
\mathcal{G}
’s Lie algebra. Note that
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(B_{f})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(B_{g})\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, where the final
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-summand acts on
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
via scalar multiplication. Thus, it remains to show that
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is irreducible as a
\mathfrak{h}
-representation, where
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(B_{f})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{1}(B_{g})
.

Enlarging
E
, if necessary, we may assume that the quaternion algebras
B_{f}
and
B_{g}
split. Consequently, we have
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(E)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(E)
and
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}\cong\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E)\boxtimes\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E)
, where
\mathrm{Std}_{2}
is the standard representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.
We then have the following calculations:

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}\big(\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E)\boxtimes\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E)\big)\!

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\!\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(E)}\big(\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E))\otimes_{E}\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(E)}\big(\mathrm{Std}_{2}(E)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!E\otimes_{E}E=E.

From which, we see that
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}
is irreducible as a
\mathfrak{h}
-representation. This proves what we set to show.
∎

For three modular forms, we have the following.

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose that
f,g,h
have no complex multiplication and that their weights are mutually distinct. Then
V_{f}\otimes_{E}V_{g}\otimes_{E}V_{h}
is irreducible as a
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})

Proof.

By the weights assumption, a determinant comparison will show that
f,g,h
are mutually not Galois-conjugate to one another. Therefore, we may apply
[
29
, Theorem 3.4.2]
to see that the image of a certain finite-index subgroup of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
under
the group homomorphism

\rho_{f}\times\rho_{g}\times\rho_{h}:\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{f})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{g})\times\mathrm{GL}(V_{h})

is an open subgroup of

\left\{(x_{f},x_{g},x_{h},\lambda)\in B_{f}^{\times}\times B_{g}^{\times}\times B_{h}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}~:~\mathrm{norm}(x_{f})=\lambda^{1-k_{f}},~\mathrm{norm}(x_{g})=\lambda^{1-k_{g}},~\mathrm{norm}(x_{h})=\lambda^{1-k_{h}}\right\},

where here
B_{f},B_{g}
and
B_{h}
are certain appropriate quaternion algebras. Then we may proceed by a similar argument to that in the Proposition
8.1
.
∎

Davide Desio, Robert Seiringer

Abstract
. We present an abstract Dyson expansion for perturbations that are merely relatively form-bounded, and apply it to the polaron problem. For a large class of polaron-type models, including the Fröhlich and Nelson models, we prove that the vacuum expectation value of the heat semi-group is a completely monotone function of the square of the total momentum. Consequently, the ground state energy is a concave function of the square of the momentum, a result recently proved for the Fröhlich model in [ 12 ] using a probabilistic approach via Wiener integrals.



1.
Introduction and Main results

The polaron problem concerns the interaction of a charged particle with the excitations of a medium modeled by a bosonic field. Examples include the Fröhlich model
[
3
]
describing the coupling between an electron and the phonon modes of a polarized crystal, or the Nelson model
[
11
]
of quantum electrodynamics.
In this paper, we shall consider a general class of polaron Hamiltonians, formally given by

\mathbb{H}(P)=\left|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}\right|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)+\Phi(v)

(1.1)

for
P\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
corresponding to the total momentum of the system. The operators (
1.1
) act on the bosonic Fock space

\mathfrak{F}_{\rm s}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}L^{2}_{\rm s}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
, where
L^{2}_{\rm s}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
denotes the subspace of permutation-invariant functions of
N
(momentum) variables
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here
\mathbb{P}_{f}=\textnormal{d}\Gamma(k)
is the field momentum, while the field energy
\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)
is the second quantization of a non-negative Fourier multiplier
\omega
. The interaction of the particle with the field modes is described by the Segal field operator
\Phi(v)=a^{\dagger}(v)+a(v)
, where
a^{\dagger}(f)
and
a(f)
are, for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the usual creation and annihilation operators, satisfying the canonical commutation relations
[a(f),a^{\dagger}(g)]=\langle f|g\rangle
. The Hamiltonians (
1.1
) result from a restriction of the system to the sector of total momentum
P
, applying the Lee–Low–Pines transformation
[
7
]
.

The usual Fröhlich model corresponds to
d=3
,
\omega\equiv 1
, and
v(k)=\sqrt{\alpha/(2\pi^{2})}|k|^{-1}
for some coupling constant
\alpha>0
, while for the Nelson model
\omega(k)=|k|
(or, more generally,
\omega(k)=\sqrt{|k|^{2}+m^{2}}
for
m\geq 0
) and
v(k)
is proportional to
|k|^{-1/2}
, suitably cut off at large momentum to avoid an ultraviolet divergence. In general, the Hamiltonian (
1.1
) is well-defined as a quadratic form, and bounded from below, under the following assumptions on
v
and
\omega
.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that
\omega
is a positive Fourier multiplier, and that the interaction
v
is of the form

v=v_{1}+v_{2}
with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v_{1}(k)|^{2}}{\omega(k)}\textnormal{d}k<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v_{2}(k)|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega(k)}\right)\textnormal{d}k<\infty

(1.2)

Under Assumption
1
, the commutator method of Lieb–Yamazaki
[
8
]
shows that
\Phi(v)
is infinitesimally form-bounded with respect to
|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)
, and hence

\mathbb{H}(P)
defines a semi-bounded self-adjoint operator via its quadratic form
[
14
]
. For completeness, we shall repeat this argument in Appendix
A
. If
\omega(k)\equiv 1
, the condition (
1.2
) becomes
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(k)|^{2}(1+|k|^{2})^{-1}\textnormal{d}k<\infty
, and is satisfied for
v(k)=g|k|^{-\beta}
for
(d-2)/2<\beta<d/2
and
g\in\mathbb{C}
, for instance.

To state our main results, we need to introduce some notation. For
n\geq 1
, let
S_{2n}
denote the set of permutations of
2n
elements, and let
\mathcal{W}_{2n}\subset S_{2n}
be the set of Wick pairings, i.e., those permutations satisfying

\pi(2j-1)<\pi(2j+1)
for
j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
\pi(2j-1)<\pi(2j)
for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Permutations in
\mathcal{W}_{2n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with
n
pairings
(\pi(2i-1),\pi(2i))
,
1\leq i\leq n
, of the numbers
\{1,\dots,2n\}
.
Moreover, we shall denote by
\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{0}\subset\mathcal{W}_{2n}
those permutations that correspond to pairings that interlace, i.e., cannot be decomposed into pairings within disjoint subsets; explicitly,
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
is an element of
\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{0}
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{\pi^{-1}(j)}<0
for all
1\leq\ell<2n
.

For
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
and
1\leq j\leq 2n-1
, let

\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j}=\{1\leq i\leq n\,:\,\pi(2i-1)\leq j<\pi(2i)\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}

(1.3)

corresponding to the set of pairs whose connecting line crosses
j
(which may be empty). For convenience we shall also introduce
\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{0}=\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{2n}=\emptyset
.
For
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{dn}
and
0\leq j\leq 2n
, let

\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})=\Big|P-\sum\nolimits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j}}k_{\ell}\Big|^{2}+\sum\nolimits_{\ell\in\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j}}\omega(k_{\ell})

(1.4)

We shall denote by
\Omega=(1,0,0,\dots)\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm s}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
the vacuum vector. Our first main result is summarized in the following Theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
, the set of Wick pairings defined above, let
\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}
be given in (
1.4
). Under Assumption
1
, the following holds:

(a) For
t>0
, we have the convergent expansion

\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle=\textnormal{e}^{-t|P|^{2}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}}\int_{\Delta_{2n}^{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dn}}\textnormal{e}^{-\sum_{j=0}^{2n}t_{j}\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v(k_{j})|^{2}\textnormal{d}k_{j}\,\textnormal{d}\underline{t}

(1.5)

where
\underline{t}=(t_{0},\dots,t_{2n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2n+1}
and the outer integral is over the simplex
\Delta_{2n}^{t}=\{\underline{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2n+1},\ \sum_{i=0}^{2n}t_{i}=t\}
.

(b) For
t>0
, we further have the renewal equation

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle=\textnormal{e}^{-t|P|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-(t-s)\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{2n}}\int_{\Delta_{2n-1}^{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dn}}\textnormal{e}^{-\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}t_{j}\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v(k_{j})|^{2}\textnormal{d}k_{j}\,\textnormal{d}\underline{t}\,\textnormal{d}s

(1.6)

where
\mathcal{W}^{0}_{2n}\subset\mathcal{W}_{2n}
are the interlacing Wick pairings defined above.

The identity (
1.5
) formally follows from a Dyson expansion of
\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}
in terms of the interaction
\Phi(v)
, but it is a priori not clear how to make sense of such an expansion in the low-regularity setting considered here, where
\Phi(v)
is unbounded and merely form-bounded relative to the non-interacting Hamiltonian. An abstract result applicable to this setting will be presented in Section
2
, and will be the starting point in the proof of Theorem
1
. Part (b) of the theorem is inspired by
[
12
]
, where a renewal equation for
\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle
was derived in the Fröhlich case, using a somewhat different approach, however.

Theorem
1
turns out to be very usual in studying the momentum dependence
P\mapsto\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle
for fixed
t>0
. We shall need the following additional assumption on
v
and
\omega
. Recall that, according to Bernstein’s theorem
[
2
]
, a function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is completely monotone if and only if it is the Laplace transform of a positive measure.

Assumption 2
.

Assume
that
\omega
and
v
are radial, and that the function

|P|^{2}\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(k)|^{2}\textnormal{e}^{-r|P-k|^{2}-s\omega(k)}\textnormal{d}k

(1.7)

is completely monotone
for any
r>0
and
s>0
.

For
\omega\equiv 1
, this additional assumption is fulfilled for
v(k)=g|k|^{-\beta}
for any
\beta\geq 0
,
as one can see by writing
|v(k)|^{2}
as an integral over Gaussians,

\Gamma(\beta)|k|^{-2\beta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textnormal{e}^{-|k|^{2}x}x^{\beta-1}\,\textnormal{d}x

(1.8)

for
\beta>0
,
and noting that the convolution of two Gaussians is again a Gaussian. The same applies to
\omega(k)=\sqrt{|k|^{2}+m^{2}}
for
m\geq 0
, since in this case also
\textnormal{e}^{-s\omega(k)}
can be written as an average over Gaussians. The assumption is thus satisfied for both the Fröhlich and the Nelson models, if in the latter case the ultraviolet cutoff is chosen in a suitable way to respect the complete positivity of (
1.7
), e.g., via a Gaussian factor.

Corollary 1
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the following holds:

(a)
|P|^{2}\mapsto\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle
is completely monotone for any
t>0

(b) If
v\not\equiv 0
, then
|P|^{2}\mapsto E_{0}(P)=\infspec\mathbb{H}(P)
is concave, and in fact strictly concave on the set of
P
s where
\mathbb{H}(P)
has a ground state, i.e., when
E_{0}(P)<E_{\rm ess}(P)
.

Here
E_{\rm ess}(P)
denotes the bottom of the essential spectrum of
\mathbb{H}(P)
. It has the explicit form
[
10
]

E_{\rm ess}(P)=\inf_{n\geq 1}\inf_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\left\{E_{0}\left(P-\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\right)^{2}+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\omega(k_{j})\right\}

(1.9)

In the case of the Fröhlich model, the infimum is attained at
n=1
and
k_{1}=P
, hence
E_{\rm ess}(P)=E_{0}(0)+1

for any
P\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Since a completely monotone function is log-convex, the concavity in (b) follows immediately from (a), using that

E_{0}(P)=-\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle

(1.10)

If
v\equiv 0
, (
1.10
) holds only whenever
E_{0}(P)<E_{\rm ess}(P)
, but if
v\not\equiv 0
, it actually holds for any
P\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by a Perron–Frobenius type argument
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
(see also
[
4
]
and references there).
To obtain strict concavity, the renewal equation of part (b) in Theorem
1
will be useful, similarly as in
[
12
]
.

2.
Abstract Dyson Expansion for Form-Bounded Perturbations

Let
A\geq 0
be a self-adjoint operator. Let
B
be hermitian and form-bounded with respect to
A
with relative bound less than
1
, i.e., there exist
a\geq 0
and
0<\lambda<1
such that

|\langle\psi|B\psi\rangle|\leq\lambda\left(\langle\psi|A\psi\rangle+a\|\psi\|^{2}\right)

(2.1)

for any
\psi
in the quadratic form domain of
A
. Equivalently, we can think of
B
as
B=\sqrt{A+a}C\sqrt{A+a}
for some operator
C
with
\|C\|<1
. Then
A+B
is well-defined as a semi-bounded quadratic form, with form domain equal to the one of
A

[
14
]
.

Our goal in this section is to justify the Dyson expansion, formally given by

\textnormal{e}^{-t(A+B)}=\textnormal{e}^{-tA}+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}\int_{\Delta_{n}^{t}}\textnormal{e}^{-t_{0}A}B\textnormal{e}^{-t_{1}A}\cdots B\textnormal{e}^{-t_{n}A}\textnormal{d}\underline{t}

(2.2)

for
t>0
, where the integral is over the simplex
\Delta_{n}^{t}=\{t_{i}\geq 0\ \text{for $0\leq i\leq n$},\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}t_{i}=t\}
. However, in the general setting considered here, where
B
is merely form-bounded relative to
A
, it is not clear that the terms on the right-hand side are well-defined, and that the sum is convergent. Note that even under the stronger assumption that
B
is operator-bounded relative to
A
, the validity of (
2.2
) is not known, in general; we refer to
[
13
,
1
]
for partial results in this direction.

We shall show below that under the assumption (
2.1
) above,
\textnormal{e}^{-t(A+B)}
indeed has a convergent expansion in powers of
B
, but the individual terms in the expansion cannot necessarily be written in the form (
2.2
). This will be sufficient for our purpose, however.

By absorbing
a
into
A
, we can set
a=0
without loss of generality, and assume that
A
has a bounded inverse.
We start with the contour integral representation

\textnormal{e}^{-t(A+B)}=\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\textnormal{e}^{-tz}\frac{1}{z-A-B}\textnormal{d}z

(2.3)

for the contour

\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\Re z=-\kappa+\gamma|\Im z|\}

(2.4)

for some
\gamma>0
and
\kappa>0
, integrated counter-clockwise (i.e., from top to bottom), see Figure
1
.

\mathfrak{Re}z
\mathfrak{Im}z
\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}
\mathfrak{Re}z=-\kappa

Figure 1.
The contour
\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}
.

With
B=\sqrt{A}C\sqrt{A}
, we can write

z-A-B=\sqrt{A}\left(1-CA(z-A)^{-1}\right)A^{-1/2}(z-A)

(2.5)

Moreover, for
z\in\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}

\left\|A(z-A)^{-1}\right\|\leq\sup_{a>0}\frac{a}{|z-a|}\leq\left(1+\gamma^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2.6)

and hence
\|CA(z-A)^{-1}\|<1
for
\gamma
small enough. Under this assumption on
\gamma
, we thus have the convergent Neumann series

\displaystyle\left(z-A-B\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}(z-A)^{-1}\left(1-CA(z-A)^{-1}\right)^{-1}A^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{1/2}}{z-A}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(C\frac{A}{z-A}\right)^{n}A^{-1/2}

(2.7)

In particular, we conclude that

\textnormal{e}^{-t(A+B)}=\textnormal{e}^{-tA}+\sum_{n\geq 1}D_{n}

(2.8)

with

D_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\textnormal{e}^{-tz}\frac{A^{1/2}}{z-A}\left(C\frac{A}{z-A}\right)^{n-1}C\frac{A^{1/2}}{z-A}\textnormal{d}z

(2.9)

for
n\geq 1
. Note that
D_{n}
is actually independent of
\gamma
and
\kappa
.
Since

\left\|A^{1/2}(z-A)^{-1}\right\|\leq\sup_{a>0}\frac{\sqrt{a}}{|z-a|}\leq\left(1+\gamma^{2}\right)^{1/2}\kappa^{-1/2}

(2.10)

for
z\in\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}
, the norm of
D_{n}
is bounded as

\|D_{n}\|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\kappa}\left(1+\gamma^{2}\right)^{(n+1)/2}\|C\|^{n}\int_{\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\textnormal{e}^{-t\Re z}|\textnormal{d}z|=\frac{\textnormal{e}^{t\kappa}}{\pi\kappa\gamma t}\left(1+\gamma^{2}\right)^{n/2+1}\|C\|^{n}

(2.11)

The optimal choice of
\kappa
and
\gamma
is thus
\kappa=1/t
,
\gamma^{2}=(n+1)^{-1}
, leading to

\|D_{n}\|\leq\frac{\textnormal{e}}{\pi}\frac{(n+2)^{(n+2)/2}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)/2}}\|C\|^{n}\leq\frac{\textnormal{e}^{3/2}}{\pi}\sqrt{n+2}\|C\|^{n}

(2.12)

uniformly in
t>0
.

Remark 1
.

The proof above can be modified to also show that

\left\|\sqrt{A}D_{n}\sqrt{A}\right\|\leq\frac{\textnormal{e}}{\pi t}\frac{(n+2)^{(n+2)/2}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)/2}}\|C\|^{n}

(2.13)

which diverges as
t\to 0
, however.

3.
Application to the Polaron Model

We shall now apply the result of the previous section to the polaron problem. We shall write
\mathbb{H}(P)=A+B-a
for suitable
a\geq 0
,
with

A=\left|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}\right|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)+a

(3.1)

and
B=\Phi(v)=a(v)+a^{\dagger}(v)
. As already mentioned in the introduction, the commutator method of Lieb–Yamazaki (recalled in Appendix
A
) shows that under Assumption
1
,
B
is infinitesimally form-bounded with respect to
|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)
, and in particular
\|C\|<1
for
a
large enough, where
C=A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}
.

With
\Omega
the vacuum state, we have
A\Omega=(|P|^{2}+a)\Omega
. From (
2.8
) we thus obtain the convergent expansion

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\textnormal{e}^{ta}\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t(A+B)}\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\textnormal{e}^{-t|P|^{2}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\textnormal{e}^{ta}}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\frac{\textnormal{e}^{-tz}}{z-|P|^{2}-a}\langle\Omega|\left(C\frac{A}{z-A}\right)^{2n}\Omega\rangle\textnormal{d}z

(3.2)

Here we used that only even
n
in (
2.8
) contribute, the odd terms have zero vacuum expectation value (since they necessarily involve an unequal number of creation and annihilation operators).

Let
C^{+}=A^{-1/2}a^{\dagger}(v)A^{-1/2}
and
C^{-}=A^{-1/2}a(v)A^{-1/2}
, so that
C=C^{+}+C^{-}
. We have

\langle\Omega|\left(C\frac{A}{z-A}\right)^{2n}\Omega\rangle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(C^{\nu_{j}}\frac{A}{z-A}\right)\Omega\rangle

(3.3)

where we identify
C^{+1}\equiv C^{+}
and
C^{-1}\equiv C^{-}
, and
\mathcal{D}_{n}
denotes the set of Dyck paths of length
2n
, i.e.,

\mathcal{D}_{n}=\left\{\nu\in\{-1,+1\}^{2n}\,:\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nu_{j}\leq 0\ \text{for $1\leq k\leq 2n-1$},\ \sum_{j=1}^{2n}\nu_{j}=0\right\}

(3.4)

Only these terms contribute to the sum in (
3.3
), since particles can be annihilated only after they have been created. See Figure
2
for an illustration of a Dyck pack.

Figure 2.
Example of a Dyck path of length
2n=8
, corresponding to
\nu=\{-1,+1,-1,-1,+1,-1,+1,+1\}
.

For
\nu\in\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{D}_{n}
, let

\Lambda_{z}(\nu)=\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(C^{\nu_{j}}\frac{A}{z-A}\right)\Omega\rangle

(3.5)

From (
3.2
)–(
3.3
), we thus have

\langle\Omega|\textnormal{e}^{-t\mathbb{H}(P)}\Omega\rangle=\textnormal{e}^{-t|P|^{2}}+\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\textnormal{e}^{ta}}{2\pi\textnormal{i}}\int_{\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\frac{\textnormal{e}^{-tz}}{z-|P|^{2}-a}\Lambda_{z}(\nu)\,\textnormal{d}z

(3.6)

To compute the terms on the right-hand side, we need some further notation.
Recall that
\mathcal{W}_{2n}\subset S_{2n}
denotes the set of those permutations satisfying the Wick rule,
i.e.,
\pi(2j-1)<\pi(2j+1)
for
j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
\pi(2j-1)<\pi(2j)
for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Recall also the definition of
\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}
in (
1.4
), and let

\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(z-a-\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})\right)^{-1}

(3.7)

for
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
and
\underline{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
.

Lemma 1
.

Under Assumption
1
,

\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dn}}\left|\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})\right|\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v(k_{j})|^{2}\textnormal{d}k_{j}<\infty

(3.8)

for any
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
and
\gamma,\kappa>0
.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j}
in (
1.3
).
A little thought reveals that, for any
1\leq j\leq 2n
,
\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j-1}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\pi}_{j}
differ by exactly one element, namely the
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
j\in\{\pi(2i-1),\pi(2i)\}
. We shall denote it by
i_{j}
. Since

|z-a-|P-k|^{2}|\geq\frac{\kappa+a+|P-k|^{2}}{(1+\gamma^{2})^{1/2}}

(3.9)

for
z\in\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}
, one readily finds that

\left|\left(z-a-\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j-1)}_{P}(\underline{k})\right)\left(z-a-\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})\right)\right|\geq\frac{(\kappa+a)(\kappa+a+\omega(k_{i_{j}})+\frac{1}{2}|k_{i_{j}}|^{2})}{1+\gamma^{2}}

(3.10)

for
1\leq j\leq 2n
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\pi}=\mathcal{M}_{2n}^{\pi}=\emptyset
and thus
\mathscr{E}^{\pi,0}_{P}=\mathscr{E}^{\pi,2n}_{P}=|P|^{2}
,
we can write

\left|\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})\right|=\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left|z-a-\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j-1)}_{P}(\underline{k})\right|^{-1/2}\left|z-a-\mathscr{E}^{(\pi,j)}_{P}(\underline{k})\right|^{-1/2}

(3.11)

and hence (
3.10
) yields the bound

\left|\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})\right|\leq\left(\frac{1+\gamma^{2}}{\kappa+a}\right)^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(\kappa+a+\omega(k_{i_{j}})+\frac{1}{2}|k_{i_{j}}|^{2}\right)^{-1/2}

(3.12)

By construction, each
k_{i}
appears exactly twice in the list
\{k_{i_{1}},\dots,k_{i_{2n}}\}
, hence (
3.12
) reads

\left|\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})\right|\leq\left(\frac{1+\gamma^{2}}{\kappa+a}\right)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\kappa+a+\omega(k_{i})+\frac{1}{2}|k_{i}|^{2}}

(3.13)

The claim (
3.8
) then follows from our assumptions on
v
and
\omega
in Assumption
1
.
∎

Each Wick pairing
\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}
corresponds to a Dyck path defined by
\nu_{\pi(j)}=(-1)^{j}
. We can thus decompose

\mathcal{W}_{2n}=\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{\nu}
, where
\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{\nu}\subset\mathcal{W}_{2n}
denotes the set of those Wick pairings giving rise to the same Dyck path
\nu
.
We this notation, we have the following identity for
\Lambda_{z}(\nu)
defined in (
3.5
).

Lemma 2
.

Under Assumption
1
, we have

\Lambda_{z}(\nu)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dn}}\mathscr{S}^{\pi}_{P}(z,\underline{k})\prod_{j=1}^{n}|v(k_{j})|^{2}\textnormal{d}k_{j}

(3.14)

for any
\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
z\in\Gamma_{\gamma,\kappa}
.

Formally, the identity (
3.14
) follows from an application of the Wick rule and the pull-through formula
a_{k}\mathcal{R}(\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega),\mathbb{P}_{f})=\mathcal{R}(\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)+\omega(k),\mathbb{P}_{f}+k)a_{k}
for functions
\mathcal{R}
and the operator-valued distribution
a_{k}
defined by
a(v)=\int a_{k}\overline{v(k)}\textnormal{d}k
. The proof below can be viewed as a rigorous justification of this procedure.

Proof.

By analyticity and the uniform integrability established in Lemma
1
, it suffices to consider the case
\Re z<0
. Moreover, since both sides of (
3.14
) are continuous in
v
(with respect to the norm defined in Assumption
1
), it suffices to consider the case of suitably nice
v
. In particular, we can work under the additional assumption
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in which case
a(v)
is effectively a bounded operator in our computations (where the number of particles is bounded by
n
). In fact, if
\omega
is unbounded, we assume in addition that
v
is suitably compactly supported, so that
k\mapsto v(k)\textnormal{e}^{s\omega(k)}
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
s>0
. For
\Re z<0
we can write

\frac{1}{z-A}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}s\,\frac{\textnormal{e}^{s(z-a)}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}\textnormal{e}^{-s\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\textnormal{d}p\,\textnormal{e}^{\textnormal{i}p\cdot(P-\mathbb{P}_{f})}\textnormal{e}^{-|p|^{2}/(4s)}

(3.15)

and hence

\displaystyle\Lambda_{z}(\nu)=\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(a^{\nu_{j}}(v)\frac{1}{z-A}\right)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2nd}}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\textnormal{d}s_{j}\,\textnormal{d}p_{j}\,\frac{\textnormal{e}^{s_{j}(z-a)}}{(4\pi s_{j})^{d/2}}\textnormal{e}^{\textnormal{i}p_{j}\cdot P}\textnormal{e}^{-|p_{j}|^{2}/(4s_{j})}\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(a^{\nu_{j}}(v)\textnormal{e}^{-s_{j}\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}p_{j}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{f}}\right)\Omega\rangle

(3.16)

where we identify
a^{-}(v)=a(v)
and
a^{+}(v)=a^{\dagger}(v)
.
Moreover, since

\textnormal{e}^{-s\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}p\cdot\mathbb{P}_{f}}a^{\nu}(v)=a^{\nu}(\textnormal{e}^{-s\nu\omega}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}p\,\cdot\,}v)\textnormal{e}^{-s\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}p\cdot\mathbb{P}_{f}}

(3.17)

for
\nu\in\{+,-\}
, we have

\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(a^{\nu_{j}}(v)\textnormal{e}^{-s_{j}\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}p_{j}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{f}}\right)\Omega\rangle=\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}a^{\nu_{j}}(v_{j})\Omega\rangle

(3.18)

with
v_{1}=v
and

v_{j}(k)=v(k)\textnormal{e}^{-\omega(k)\nu_{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}s_{\ell}}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}k\cdot\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}p_{\ell}}

(3.19)

for
j\geq 2
.
An application of Wick’s rule further yields

\displaystyle\langle\Omega|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}a^{\nu_{j}}(v_{j})\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\langle\Omega|a^{\nu_{\pi(2i-1)}}(v_{\pi(2i-1)})a^{\nu_{\pi(2i)}}(v_{\pi(2i)})\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\langle v_{\pi(2i-1)}|v_{\pi(2i)}\rangle\delta_{\nu_{\pi(2i-1)},-1}\delta_{\nu_{\pi(2i)},+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{\nu}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\langle v_{\pi(2i-1)}|v_{\pi(2i)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{2n}^{\nu}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(k)|^{2}\textnormal{e}^{-\omega(k)\sum_{\ell=\pi(2i-1)}^{\pi(2i)-1}s_{\ell}}\textnormal{e}^{-\textnormal{i}k\cdot\sum_{\ell=\pi(2i-1)}^{\pi(2i)-1}p_{\ell}}\,\textnormal{d}k

(3.20)

Inserting this in (
3.16
) above and interchanging the order of integrations then gives the desired formula (
3.14
) under the stated assumptions on
v
. As already mentioned, the general case follows by continuity.
∎

We now have all the prerequisites to give the proof of our main results.

By positivity of the integrand, we can first carry out the integration over

Appendix A
The Lieb–Yamazaki Argument

In this section, we shall recall the Lieb–Yamazaki commutator method introduced in
[
8
]
to prove that if
v
and
\omega
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\Phi(v)
is infinitesimally form bounded with respect to the non-interacting Hamiltonian
|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)
.

On the one hand, a simple Cauchy–Schwarz inequality shows that

\pm\Phi(v)\leq\varepsilon\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)+\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v(k)|^{2}}{\omega(k)}\textnormal{d}k

(A.1)

for any
\varepsilon>0
, which we can apply to
v=v_{1}
.
On the other hand, using that

\Phi(v)=\textnormal{i}[P-\mathbb{P}_{f},\Phi(w)]

(A.2)

for
w(k)=\textnormal{i}kv(k)/|k|^{2}
(where an inner product between the vectors
P-\mathbb{P}_{f}
and
w
is understood on the right-hand side), a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies that

\pm\Phi(v)\leq\varepsilon|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}|\Phi(w)|^{2}

(A.3)

We can further bound

\Phi(w)^{2}\leq 4a^{\dagger}(w)a(w)+2\|w\|^{2}\leq\lambda\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v(k)|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\textnormal{d}k

(A.4)

for
\lambda=4\int|w(k)|^{2}/\omega(k)\textnormal{d}k=4\int|v(k)|^{2}/(|k|^{2}\omega(k))\textnormal{d}k
. We shall apply this bound to
v_{2}
. By suitably adjusting how to split
v
into the two components
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
, we can assume that

\lambda
is as small as desired, which implies the claimed infinitesimal bound relative to
|P-\mathbb{P}_{f}|^{2}+\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\omega)
.

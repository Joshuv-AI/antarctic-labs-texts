Elementary construction of canonical bases,
foldings, and piecewise linear bijections

Abstract
. Let 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} be the negative half of the quantum group of finite type. We construct the canonical basis of 𝐔 q − \mathbf{U}_{q}^{-} by applying the folding theory of quantum groups and piecewise linear parametrization of canonical basis. Our construction is elementary, in the sense that we don’t appeal to Lusztig’s geometric theory of canonical bases, nor to Kashiwara’s theory of crystal bases.



Introduction

Let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be the negative half of the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}
, associated to
the Cartan datum
X
.
In the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is symmetric of finite type, Lusztig
constructed in
[
L2
]
the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, by making use of the
representation theory of quivers (but not using the geometric theory of quivers mentioned below).
After that in
[
L3
]
, he constructed the canonical basis for

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of symmetric, Kac-Moody type, by making use
of the geometric theory of quivers, namely, by using
the categorification in terms of a certain category of perverse sheaves
on the representation space of the quiver associated to
X
.

This result was generalized in his book
[
L4
]
,
to the non-symmetric case as follows.
Assume that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is symmetric, and
\sigma:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is the
automorphism of quantum groups
induced from the diagram automorphism
\sigma
on the Cartan datum
X
.
Then
\sigma
induces a Cartan datum
\underline{X}
, and one can define the quantum group

\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
associated to
\underline{X}
. Any quantum group of Kac-Moody type is obtained
as
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
from a certain quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of symmetric type with automorphism
\sigma
.
He constructed the canonical basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
, by making use of the category
of perverse sheaves associated to
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
as above, but with the action of
\sigma
on
that category.
In general, a folding theory of quantum groups discusses the relationship between

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
with automorphism
\sigma
, and
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
.
Hence the above result is regarded as a folding theory for the category with
an automorphism.

On the other hand, Kashiwara established in
[
K
]
the theory of crystal bases,
and constructed the global crystal basis for any
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of Kac-Moody type.
It is known that Lusztig’s canonical basis
coincides with Kashiwara’s global crystal basis (
[
GL
]
for the symmetric case,

[
MSZ2
]
for the general case).

For
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of finite type, there is an attempt to
construct the canonical basis in a more elementary way, by making use of the
PBW bases of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
This problem is reduced to the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
has rank 2, and it is easily
solved if
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is symmetric, namely,
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}

or
A_{2}
.
In the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is not symmetric,
namely
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of type
B_{2}
or
G_{2}
, the canonical bases were constructed by
Lusztig
[
L1
]
, Xi
[
X1
]
,
[
X2
]
. But the computations are rather
hard, and in particular, it is not yet complete in the case of type
G_{2}
.
In that case, in part,
one has to appeal to the general theory of canonical basis or crystal basis.

In this paper, we assume that
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of finite type.
We construct the canonical basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
in an elementary way, in the sense
that we don’t appeal to Lusztig’s theory of canonical basis nor to Kashiwara’s theory
of crystal basis. Also our approach is different from that of Lusztig and Xi.
Our main ingredient is the folding theory of quantum groups developed in

[
SZ1
]
,
[
MSZ1
]
. This theory is an analogue of Lusztig’s theory of
the category with automorphism,
but we compare
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
and
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
directly (not on the category),
and the discussion is more elementary.

The construction of the canonical basis is finally reduced to the case of type
B_{2}
.
The canonical basis is parametrized by the PBW basis once it is fixed. Since the PBW basis
is not unique, one needs to compare the labeling of the canonical basis for
a different choice of PBW bases. This difference of the labeling is
described by a certain map called the piecewise linear bijection.
In the case of type
B_{2}
, this map was explicitly computed by Lusztig
[
L5
]
.
By making use of this map, we can determine
the canonical basis for
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
of type
B_{2}
.

1.
Review on quantum groups

1.1.

Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum of finite type, where

I
is a finite set, and
(\ ,\ )
is a symmetric bilinear form on the

\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{i}
with basis
\alpha_{i}
, satisfying
the property

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for any
i\in I
,

\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for any
i\neq j\in I
.

The Cartan datum
X
is called symmetric if
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})=2
for any
i\in I
,
and
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})\in\{0,-1\}
for any
i\neq j\in I
.
Let
Q=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice of
X
. We set

Q_{+}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{i}
, and
Q_{-}=-Q_{+}
.

1.2.

Let
q
be an indeterminate, and for an integer
n
, a positive integer
m
, set

[n]=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad[m]^{!}=[1][2]\cdots[m],\quad[0]^{!}=1.

For each
i\in I
, set
q_{i}=q^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2}
. We denote by
[n]_{i}

the element obtained from
[n]
by replacing
q
by
q_{i}
.
[m]^{!}_{i}
is defined similarly.

For the Cartan datum
X
, let
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}=\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(X)
be the negative half of
the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}=\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple
Lie algebra over
\mathbf{C}
associated to
X
.
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is an associative algebra over

\mathbb{Q}(q)
generated by
f_{i}(i\in I)
satisfying the
q
-Serre relations

(1.2.1)

\sum_{k+k^{\prime}=1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}f_{i}^{(k)}f_{j}f_{i}^{(k^{\prime})}=0\quad\text{ for }i\neq j\in I,

where we set
a_{ij}=2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})/(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
and

f_{i}^{(n)}=f_{i}^{n}/[n]_{i}^{!}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Set
\mathbf{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
,and let
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
be Lusztig’s integral form on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,
namely, the
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
generated by
f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
has a weight space decomposition
\bigoplus_{\gamma\in Q_{-}}(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
,
where
(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
is a subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by elements
f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}

such that
\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{k}}=-\gamma
.
Hence
\dim(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}<\infty
for any
\gamma
.
If
x\in(\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-})_{\gamma}
, then
x
is said to be homogeneous with weight
\gamma
,
and is written as
\operatorname{wt}(x)=\gamma
.

1.3.

Let
\varDelta
be the root system associated to
X
, and
\varDelta^{+}
the set of
positive roots with the set of simple roots
\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
.
Let
W
be the Weyl group of
X
which is generated by
simple reflections
s_{i}
corresponding to
\alpha_{i}
.
Let
w_{0}
be the longest element in
W
, and
let
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}
be a reduced expression of
w_{0}
,
where
N=|\varDelta^{+}|
. We set
\mathbf{h}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
, and denote it
a reduced sequence of
w_{0}
. We fix a reduced sequence
\mathbf{h}
. For each

1\leq k\leq N
, set
\beta_{k}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})
.
It is known that
\beta_{k}\in\varDelta^{+}
, and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{N}
are all distinct.
Thus
\varDelta^{+}=\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{N}\}
, and
\mathbf{h}
determines a total order on
the set
\varDelta^{+}
.

Let
T_{i}:\mathbf{U}_{q}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}
be the braid group action, and set

f_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)}=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{k-1}}(f_{i_{k}}^{(n)}).

Then
f_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is homogeneous with
\operatorname{wt}(f_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)})=n\beta_{k}
.

f_{\beta_{k}}^{(n)}
are called root vectors in
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
corresponding to
the root
\beta_{k}
. For
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,
we define

L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=f_{\beta_{1}}^{(c_{1})}f_{\beta_{2}}^{(c_{2})}\cdots f_{\beta_{N}}^{(c_{N})}.

Then the set
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
gives a basis
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, which is called the PBW basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
associated to
\mathbf{h}
.
The following result is known.
For the proof, see Remark 1.6 below.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{h}
be a reduced sequence for
w_{0}
.

(i)

The
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-submodule of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
spanned by
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}

does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{h}
, which we denote by

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)
. Then
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-basis of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)
.

(ii)

The
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)/q\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)
obtained from
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}

does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{h}
.

(iii)

For any reduced sequence
\mathbf{h}
,
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an
\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

1.5.

The bar involution
{}^{-}:\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\to\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is defined as an
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
automorphism given by
q\mapsto q^{-1},f_{i}\mapsto f_{i}\ (i\in I)
.
We define a total order on
\mathbb{N}^{N}
by
\mathbf{c}<\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
, for

\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{N}),\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{N}^{\prime})
, if
there exists
k
such that
c_{1}=c_{1}^{\prime},\dots,c_{k-1}=c_{k-1}^{\prime}
and
c_{k}<c_{k}^{\prime}
.
The following triangularity property can be proved by a standard argument
(assuming that
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an
\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
).

(1.5.1)

\overline{L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})}=L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}>\mathbf{c}}a_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h})\quad\text{ with }\quad a_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}\in\mathbf{A}.

By making use of (1.5.1), the following result is easily verified.

(1.5.2) Assume that
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an
\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
For a given
L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\in\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
, there exists a unique element

b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
satisfying the properties

(i)

\overline{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})}=b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})
,

(ii)

b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})=L(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}>\mathbf{c}}p_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}}L(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}),\quad\text{ with }\quad p_{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}\in q\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

Then
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\}
gives a basis of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
,
and an
\mathbf{A}
-basis of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.
In particular, if Proposition 1.4 holds, then
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}
exists, and does not depend on
the choice of
\mathbf{h}
(by Proposition 1.4 (ii)),
which is denoted by
\mathbf{B}
, and is called the canonical basis of

\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

Now assume that

(ii) In tis paper, we are interested in constructing the canonical bases in an elementary

1.7.

There exists a symmetric bilinear form
(\ ,\ )
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
, which is induced from

{}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}

[

It is known that the PBW basis
\mathscr{X}_{\mathbf{h}}
gives an orthogonal basis of

.

The
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-submodule
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}

as follows.
Set
\mathbf{A}_{0}=\mathbb{Q}[[q]]\cap\mathbb{Q}(q)
. Then
\mathbf{A}_{0}
is a local ring with the maximal ideal

, and
\mathbf{A}_{0}/q\mathbf{A}_{0}\simeq\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\mid(x,x)\in\mathbf{A}_{0}\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\infty)

Then
\mathscr{L}(\infty)

It is shown that if the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
exists, then
\mathbf{B}

gives an
\mathbf{A}_{0}
-basis of
\mathscr{L}(\infty)
, and

\mathbb{Z}[q]
-basis of

We define a subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
of
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by

(1.7.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}=\{x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}\mid\overline{x}=x,(x,x)\in 1+q\mathbf{A}_{0}\}.

Clearly, if

If there exists a basis

2.
Foldings of quantum groups

2.1.

Let
X=(I,(\ ,\ ))
be a Cartan datum of finite type as in 1.1.
We assume that
X
is symmetric.
A permutation
\sigma:I\to I
is called an admissible automorphism on
X

if it satisfies the property that

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})=(\alpha_{\sigma(i)},\alpha_{\sigma(i^{\prime})})
for any
i,i^{\prime}\in I
,
and that
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})=0
if
i
and
i^{\prime}
belong to the same
\sigma
-orbit in
I
.
Assume that
\sigma
is admissible.
We denote by
\mathbf{n}
the order of
\sigma:I\to I
.
Let
J
be the set of

\sigma
-orbits in
I
. For each
j\in J
, set
\alpha_{j}=\sum_{i\in J}\alpha_{i}
,
and consider the subspace
\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{j}
of
E
with basis
\alpha_{j}
.
We denote by
|j|
the size of of the orbit
j
in
I
.
The restriction of the form
(\ ,\ )
on
\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{j}
is given by

(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})|j|,\quad(i\in j)&\quad\text{ if }j=j^{\prime},\\
\sum_{i\in j,i^{\prime}\in j^{\prime}}(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i^{\prime}})&\quad\text{ if }j\neq j^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Then
\underline{X}=(J,(\ ,\ ))
is a Cartan datum, which is called the Cartan datum
induced from
(X,\sigma)
.
Now
\sigma
acts naturally on the root lattice
Q
,
and the set
Q^{\sigma}
of
\sigma
-fixed elements in
Q

is identified with the root lattice of
\underline{X}
. We have
Q^{\sigma}_{+}=\sum_{j\in J}\mathbb{N}\alpha_{j}
.

The admissible automorphism
\sigma:I\to I
induces
an algebra automorphism on
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
by
f_{i}\mapsto f_{\sigma(i)}
for each
i\in I
,
which we also denote by
\sigma
. The action of
\sigma
leaves
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
invariant,
and we denote by
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}
the fixed point subalgebra of
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
.

Let
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}=\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}(\underline{X})

be the negative half of the quantum group
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}
associated to
\underline{X}
.
Thus
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is an associative
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra, with generators
f_{j}\,(j\in J)

and
q
-Serre relations as in (1.2.1). The integral form
{}_{\mathbf{A}}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}

is the
\mathbf{A}
-subalgebra generated by
f_{j}^{(n)}
(
j\in J,n\in\mathbb{N}
).

2.2.

Let
\sigma:I\to I
be as in 2.1.
In this section, we assume that
X
is irreducible.
Then
\mathbf{n}=2
or 3, which is a prime number
p
. Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

be the finite field of
p
elements, and set
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}[q,q^{-1}]=\mathbf{A}/p\mathbf{A}
.
We consider the
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
-algebra

{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}=\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}\simeq{}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}/p({}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}).

Let
\mathbf{J}
be an
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
-submodule of
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}
generated by

\sum_{0\leq i<k}\sigma^{i}(x)
, where
k
is the smallest integer
\geq 1

such that
\sigma^{k}(x)=x
.
Then
\mathbf{J}
is the two-sided ideal of

{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-\sigma}
. We define an
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
-algebra
\mathbf{V}_{q}
as the quotient algebra
of
{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}
,

(2.2.1)

\mathbf{V}_{q}={}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}/\mathbf{J}.

Let
\pi:{}_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-,\sigma}\to\mathbf{V}_{q}
be the natural projection.

For each
j\in J
, and
a\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\widetilde{f}^{(a)}_{j}=\prod_{i\in j}f_{i}^{(a)}
.
Since
\sigma
is admissible,
f_{i}^{(a)}
and
f_{i^{\prime}}^{(a)}
commute each other for
i,i^{\prime}\in j

We start with the explanation of piecewise linear bijections.
First consider the case where
\mathbf{U}_{q}^{-}
is of type
A_{2}
with

\mathbf{h}=(1,2,1)

\mathbf{h}^{\prime}=(2,1,2)

Accordingly,
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=\{b(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\}

\varphi:\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\mapsto\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime})

[
L5

If we can show that
\eta_{B}<\eta_{A}+1
, the coefficient of the lowest term of

\xi_{\underline{b}^{\circ}}
coincides with that of
B
, hence is equal to 1.
Thus it is enough to show that

(3.13.2)

2a-b-2c<-b+1.

By the condition in Lemma 3.12 (ii), we have
a\leq c
, hence (3.13.2) holds.

Case (iii).

y
is the third term of
Z
, and in this case, we have to consider the
contribution from the first and the second terms.
As in the case (ii), if we can show that
\eta_{C}<\eta_{A}+1,\eta_{C}<\eta_{B}+1
,
then the coefficient of
\xi_{\underline{b}^{\circ}}
is equal to 1.
Hence it is enough to show that

2a-2c-d<-b+1,\qquad 2a-2c-d<2a-b-2c+1.

This is equivalent to the condition that

(3.13.3)

2(a-c)-(b-d)\leq 0,\qquad b-d\leq 0.

By Lemma 3.12 (iii),
(a,b,c,d)
satisfies the relation

(3.13.4)

a\alpha\leq cd(a+c),\qquad ab\leq d(a+c),

where
\alpha=ab+ad+cd
(in the semifield notation).
The latter condition implies that
ab\leq ad+cd=\min\{ad,cd\}
, hence
we have
b\leq d
and
ab\leq cd
. In particular, the second relation of (3.13.3) holds.
If
a\leq c
, the first relation of (3.13.3) holds, thus we may assume that
c\leq a
.
Then
\alpha=ab+(a+c)d=ab+cd=ab
. By the first condition in (3.13.4),
we have
a\alpha=a^{2}b\leq c^{2}b
. This implies that
2a+b\leq 2c+d
in
\mathbb{Z}
, and
the first relation of (3.13.3) holds.
Hence (3.13.3) holds.
∎

3.14.

We now compute

(3.14.1)

a^{\prime}=bcd/\alpha,\quad b^{\prime}=\alpha^{2}/\varepsilon,\quad c^{\prime}=\varepsilon/\alpha,\quad d^{\prime}=ab^{2}c/\varepsilon,

where

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=ab(b+d)+d\alpha=ab^{2}+ad^{2}+cd^{2}+abd.

Let
\underline{\mathbf{c}}=(a,b,c,d)
and
\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}=\varphi^{-1}(\underline{\mathbf{c}})=(a^{\prime},b,^{\prime}c^{\prime},d^{\prime})

By making use of it, we can show the following lemma, which is an analogue of Lemma 3.12.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\underline{b}^{\circ}=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
.
Then the PBW basis
L(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
corresponding to

\underline{b}^{\circ}

f_{\underline{2}}^{(a-2)}f_{\underline{1}\underline{2}\underline{2}}^{(b+1)}f_{\underline{1}\underline{2}}^{(c)}f_{\underline{1}}^{(d)}

f_{\underline{2}}^{(a-1)}f_{\underline{1}\underline{2}\underline{2}}^{(b)}f_{\underline{1}\underline{2}}^{(c+1)}f_{\underline{1}}^{(d)}\ \text{ if }\ \begin{cases}\alpha/(b+d)\leq bcd\alpha/(b+d)\varepsilon,\\

.

The following result is an analogue of Proposition 3.13, but which is weaker than it.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
f_{\underline{1}}L(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})=\sum_{b_{1}\in\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}}}\xi_{b_{1}}b_{1}

be as in Lemma 3.11. Let
\underline{\mathbf{c}}=(a,b,c,d)
.
Assume the condition
(*)
“
a=0
if
a=c
and
b=d
”.
Then we have

(3.16.1)

\xi_{\underline{b}^{\circ}}\in q^{-a^{\prime}}(1+q\mathbb{Z}[q]).

In particular,
\delta=1
, and we have

b(\underline{c}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}})
.

Proof.

Set
Z=f_{\underline{1}}L(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
, and let
y=L(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})

be the PBW basis corresponding to
\underline{b}^{\circ}=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
.

y
is given as in (i)
\sim
(iv) in Lemma 3.15. Again, those four PBW bases are exactly
the same as the PBW bases appearing in the expansion of
Z
in Proposition 3.9 (ii).
We prove the proposition in a similar way as Proposition 3.13.

For each coefficient
A,\dots,D\in\mathbf{A}
in Proposition 3.9 (ii), we denote by

\eta_{A},\dots,\eta_{D}
the degree of the lowest term. We have

\displaystyle\eta_{A}

\displaystyle=-2b,

\displaystyle\eta_{B}

\displaystyle=a-2b-c-1,

\displaystyle\eta_{C}

\displaystyle=2a-2b-2c,

\displaystyle\eta_{D}

\displaystyle=2a-2c-2d.

We show in each case, the coefficient of the lowest term of
\xi_{\underline{b}^{\circ}}

is equal to 1.

Case (i).

y
is the first term of
Z
, and it is the smallest element in the total order
in
\mathscr{X}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}}
. Hence (3.16.1) holds.

Case (ii).

y
is the second term of
Z
. We show that
\eta_{B}<\eta_{A}+1
, namely,

(3.16.2)

a-2b-c-1<-2b+1\Longleftrightarrow a-c<2.

By Lemma 3.15 (ii),
(a,b,c,d)
satisfies the condition

\begin{cases}\alpha/(b+d)\leq bcd\alpha/(b+d)\varepsilon,\\
ab(b+d)/\alpha>d,\\
cd/(b+d)\leq a\leq cd1/(b+d)\end{cases}\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}\varepsilon\leq bcd,\\
ab(b+d)>d\alpha,\\
cd\leq a(b+d)\leq cd1,\end{cases}

where
\alpha=a(b+d)+cd
,
\varepsilon=ab(b+d)+d\alpha
.
First assume that
d\leq b
. Then
b+d=d
, and the third condition implies
that
cd\leq ad\leq cd1
, namely,
c\leq a\leq c+1
in
\mathbb{Z}
. Hence

a-c\leq 1<2
.
Next assume that
b<d
. Then
b+d=b
, and
\alpha=ab+cd
.
We have
\varepsilon=ab^{2}+abd+cd^{2}=ab^{2}+cd^{2}
since

ab^{2}<abd
. By the first condition, we have

ab^{2}+cd^{2}\leq bcd<cd^{2}
. This implies that
ab^{2}<cd^{2}
.
On the other hand, the second condition implies that
ab^{2}>d\alpha=d(ab+cd)>ab^{2}+cd^{2}
,
hence
ab^{2}>cd^{2}
. This is absurd, and the case
b<d
does not occur.
Hence (3.16.2) holds, and (3.16.1) follows.

Case (iii).

y
is the third term of
Z
.
We show that
\eta_{C}<\eta_{A}+1,\eta_{C}<\eta_{B}+1
, namely,

(3.16.3)

2a-2b-2c<-2b+1,\qquad 2a-2b-2c<a-2b-c.

This is equivalent to
2a-2c<1

a<c
, hence enough to
see that
a<c
.
By Lemma 3.15 (iii),
(a,b,c,d)
satisfies the condition

\begin{cases}\alpha/(b+d)\leq bcd\alpha/(b+d)\varepsilon,\\

a\leq cd/(b+d)1,\\
b>d.\end{cases}

Since
b>d
, we have
b+d=d
. Then the third condition

a(b+d)1\leq cd
implies that
a+1\leq c
in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence
a<c
, and (3.16.3) holds. We obtain (3.16.1).

Case (iv).
Let
y
be the fourth term of
Z
. We show that

\eta_{D}<\eta_{A}+1,\eta_{D}<\eta_{B}+1,\eta_{D}<\eta_{C}+1
, namely,

\displaystyle 2a-2c-2d

\displaystyle<-2b+1,

\displaystyle 2a-2c-2d

\displaystyle<a-2b-c,

\displaystyle 2a-2c-2d

\displaystyle<2a-2b-2c+1,

This is equivalent to the relations

\displaystyle 2(a-c)+2(b-d)

\displaystyle<1,

(3.16.4)

\displaystyle(a-c)+2(b-d)

\displaystyle<0,

\displaystyle 2(b-d)

\displaystyle<1.

By Lemma 3.15 (iv),
(a,b,c,d)
satisfies the condition

\begin{cases}\alpha/(b+d)\leq bcd\alpha/(b+d)\varepsilon,\\

a\leq cd/(b+d),\\

ab(b+d)\leq d\alpha,\\
a(b+d)\leq cd,\\
b\leq d,\end{cases}\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}\varepsilon\leq bcd,\\

ab\leq cd,\\

By the fourth condition, we have

, hence

By the third condition, we have

in
\mathbb{Z}
,

Now by the assumption (*), we have
a=0

Z=f_{\underline{1}}L(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})=Cf_{\underline{1}\underline{2}\underline{2}}^{(b-1)}{f^{\prime}}_{\underline{1}\underline{2}}^{(c+2)}f_{1}^{(d)}+Df_{\underline{1}\underline{2}\underline{2}}^{(b)}{f^{\prime}}_{\underline{1}\underline{2}}^{(c)}f_{\underline{1}}^{(d+1)},

We are now ready to prove (2.12.1), namely,

Theorem 3.17
.

Assume that
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
is of type
B_{2}
. Then we have
\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}=\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

For
\gamma=\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}\alpha_{j}\in\underline{Q}
, set
|\gamma|=\sum_{j\in J}|n_{j}|
.
We prove, for
\gamma\in-\underline{Q}_{+}
,

(3.17.1)

(\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}})_{\gamma}=(\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}})_{\gamma}

by induction on
|\gamma|
. Thus it is enough to show that

b(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
for any
b\in(\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}})_{\gamma}
.
This is clear if
\gamma=0
. We assume that
\gamma\neq 0
, and that
(3.17.1) holds for
\gamma^{\prime}
such that
|\gamma^{\prime}|<|\gamma|
.

We follow the discussion in 2.11. We know that
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}=\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}\sqcup-\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}

is the canonical signed basis of
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
. For
j\in J
,
let
F_{j}
be the Kashiwara operator on
\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{q}^{-}
defined in (2.11.3).
Take
b\in(\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}})_{\gamma}
. Assume that
\varepsilon_{\underline{2}}(b)>0
.
Then by (2.11.2), there exists
b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}}
such that
F_{\underline{2}}(b^{\prime})=b
.
Write
b^{\prime}=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}})
. Since
b^{\prime}\in(\mathbf{B}_{\underline{\mathbf{h}}})_{\gamma^{\prime}}
with

|\gamma^{\prime}|<|\gamma|
, we have
b(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})

by induction hypothesis. Then
F_{\underline{2}}(b^{\prime})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})

by (2.11.4).
By Proposition 3.13, we have

b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}})
.
Hence
b=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}})
.

Next assume that
\varepsilon_{\underline{2}}(b)=0
. Since
b\neq 1
, by (2.11.1) there exists

b^{\prime}
such that
F_{\underline{1}}(b^{\prime})=b
. Write
b^{\prime}=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
with

\underline{\mathbf{c}}=(a,b,c,d)
.
By a similar discussion as above, we have
b=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}})
.
If
a\neq c
or
b\neq d
, then

b=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+},\underline{\mathbf{h}})=b(\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet},\underline{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})
by
Proposition 3.16.
Hence we may assume that
a=c
and
b=d
, namely
\underline{\mathbf{c}}=(a,b,a,b)
.
In this case, it is easy to see that

\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet}=(b,a,b,a)
and
\underline{\mathbf{c}}^{\bullet+\bullet}=(a,b,a,b+1)

Since
\varepsilon_{\underline{2}}(b)=0

Again by applying Proposition 3.16 and by (2.11.4), we have

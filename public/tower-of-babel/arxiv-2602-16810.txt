Separations above TFNP from Sherali-Adams Lower Bounds


Abstract
Unlike in 𝖳𝖥𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{TFNP}} , for which there is an abundance of problems capturing natural existence principles which are incomparable (in the black-box setting), Kleinberg et al. [ 15 ] observed that many of the natural problems considered so far in the second level of the total function polynomial hierarchy ( 𝖳𝖥 ​ Σ 2 ) ({\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}) reduce to the Strong Avoid problem. In this work, we prove that the Linear Ordering Principle does not reduce to Strong Avoid in the black-box setting, exhibiting the first 𝖳𝖥 ​ Σ 2 {\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2} problem that lies outside of the class of problems reducible to Strong Avoid. The proof of our separation exploits a connection between total search problems in the polynomial hierarchy and proof complexity, recently developed by Fleming, Imrek, and Marciot [ 9 ] . In particular, this implies that to show our separation, it suffices to show that there is no small proof of the Linear Ordering Principle in a Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} -variant of the Sherali-Adams proof system. To do so, we extend the classical pseudo-expectation method to the Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} setting, showing that the existence of a Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} pseudo-expectation precludes a Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} Sherali-Adams proof. The main technical challenge is in proving the existence of such a pseudo-expectation, we manage to do so by solving a combinatorial covering problem about permutations. We also show that the extended pseudo-expectation bound implies that the Linear Ordering Principle cannot be reduced to any problem admitting a low-degree Sherali-Adams refutation.

Pseudo-Expectations

To prove our Sherali-Adams lower bound, we extend the well-developed pseudo-expectation technique for Sherali-Adams proofs to the
\Sigma_{2}
setting.
A pseudo-expectation is an object which appears to be a probability distribution over satisfying assignments to our (unsatisfiable) formula when examining low-degree marginals. The existence of a pseudo-expectation precludes a low-degree Sherali-Adams proof. A
\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation for
LOP
is a collection of pseudo-expectations, one for each set of weakenings of the constraints of
LOP

3.3
for details. The main technical challenge is to construct such a
\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation. While Dantchev et al.
[
5
]
showed that

itself has a high-degree pseudo-expectation, it is not clear how to extend their argument to all
\Sigma_{2}
-weakenings.

A Covering Problem of Permutations

We observe that constructing a
\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation requires us to solve the following covering problem.
Let
\Ord
be the set of all total orders on the set of numbers
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let
\Ord^{*1}\subseteq\Ord
be the set of total orders for which at least one number comes before the element 1. For a fixed
d\leq n
, a set
S\subseteq[n]
of size
d
and an order
\sigma
on
S
,
let
\cal{C}_{S,\sigma}=\{\pi\in\Ord\mid\pi\text{ induces the order $\sigma$ on $S$}\}
be the set of all total orders on
[n]
that are consistent with
\sigma
on
S

Is it possible to cover every element in
\Ord^{*1}
with the corresponding collections
\cal{C}_{S,\sigma}
of strictly less than
d!
pairs
(S,\sigma)
?

Note that one can cover the set of all total orders with exactly
d!
such collections
\cal{C}_{S,\sigma}
.
The question is thus ”can we do better if we do not need to cover orders starting with 1?”. One can extract from the lower bound of Dantchev et al.
[
5
]
that for
d=2
, such coverings are impossible. Our proof of
Theorem 1.1
implies that the answer to this question is no for any
d\leq n/100
.
In our argument, we show that weakenings of some axioms of
LOP
can be modified to a normalized form without increasing their degree by much.
Proving that our pseudo-expectation function is non-negative on these normalized weakenings is equivalent to a lower bound in the above covering problem.

Our linear lower bound on
d
is tight up to constant factors, as one can construct a covering with less than
d!
collections for
d=n/2
.

Further Separations from Sherali-Adams Upper Bounds

Leveraging our
\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation, we show that if a
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}
problem admits a Sherali-Adams upper bound then this problem is separated from the Linear Ordering Principle. Together with a simple Sherali-Adams upper bound for the
LeastNumber
we obtain
Theorem 1.2
. To prove this, we show that reductions can be split in two parts: first, a potentially hard to verify
\Sigma_{2}
-weakening step, followed by a
counter-example reduction
, a type of reduction introduced in
[
16
,
23
]
that actually happens in
{\mathsf{TFNP}}^{dt}
.
This reveals how the two types of black-box reductions that have been studied in the literature for total functions in the polynomial hierarchy relate.

Preliminaries on Black-Box
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}

A
query search problem
is a sequence of relations
R_{n}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}\times{\cal O}_{n}
, one for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is
total
if for every
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
there is an
o\in{\cal O}_{n}
such that
(x,o)\in R_{n}
. We think of
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
as a bit string which can be accessed by querying individual bits, and we will measure the complexity of solving
R_{n}
as the number of bits that must be queried. Hence, an efficient algorithm for
R_{n}
will be one which finds a suitable
o
while making at most
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
-many queries to the input. We will not charge the algorithm for other computational steps, and therefore an efficient algorithm corresponds to a shallow decision tree. Total query search problems which can be computed by decision trees of depth
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
belong to the class
{\mathsf{FP}}^{dt}
, where
dt
indicates that it is a black-box class. While search problems are formally defined as sequences
R=(R_{n})
, we will often want to speak about individual elements in the sequence. For readability, we will abuse notation and refer to elements
R_{n}
in the sequence as total search problems. In addition, we will often drop the subscript
n
, and rely on context to differentiate.

In this paper we will be considering total query search problems in the second level of the polynomial hierarchy
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}^{dt}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A total query search problem
R=(R_{n})
, where
R_{n}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}\times{\cal O}_{n}
, belongs to
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}^{dt}
if for every
n
,
o\in{\cal O}_{n}
,
\ell={\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
and every
z\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}

there is a a
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
-depth decision tree
V_{o,z}
such that

R_{n}(x,o)\iff\forall\ell={\mathsf{polylog}}(n),\forall z\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},V_{o,z}(x)=1.

We can compare the complexity of total search problems by taking reductions between them. The following defines
decision tree reductions
(known as
formulations
) between total query search problems, the query analogue of polynomial-time reductions.

Definition 2.2
.

For total query search problems

, there is an
S

and

where

. The

of the formulation is

where

denotes the minimum depth of any decision tree which computes

size
of the formulation is
m

S
. The

of the formulation is

.
The

to

is the minimum complexity of all possible

-formulations of

We extend this definition to sequences in the natural way. If
S=(S_{m})
is a sequence and

is a single search problem, then the complexity of reducing
R_{n}
to

is the minimum over

R_{n}
to
S_{m}
. For two sequences of search problems

and

to

is the complexity of reducing

to

, as a function of

is efficient if its complexity is
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)

.

To prove

Fleming, Imrek, and Marciot

For any boolean formula

We refer to the polynomials

also as conjuncts and also denote them by

.
We say that a

{\cal J}:=\sum_{i}t_{i}

Let
D=\bigvee_{t\in D}t
be any DNF. We can express

D(x)=\sum_{t\in D}t(x)-1.

(1)

The degree of this polynomial is
\deg(D):=\max_{t\in D}\deg(t)

We refer to

Observe that for any

We will abuse notation and denote by

Throughout we will work with

Definition 3.1
.

Let

The

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{G}}
must be a
d
-pseudo-expectation for all weakenings of

, as they are all subsets of
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

4
Separating LOP from Strong Avoid

In this section we prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a degree
n/300

\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation for
LOP
.

From this, the separation
{\text{LOP}}\not\in{\mathsf{StrongAvoid}}^{dt}
follows immediately. Note that
Theorem
4.1
is much stronger than we need in order to rule out a reduction to
StrongAvoid
, as works irrespective of the size of the
{\mathsf{uSA}}
proofs, or size of the reduction.

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

By
Theorem
3.2

{\text{LOP}}\in{\mathsf{StrongAvoid}}^{dt}
iff there exists a
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
-complexity
\Sigma_{2}
-
{\mathsf{uSA}}
proof of the propositional encoding of
LOP
given by
2.4
. In particular, such a proof must have
{\mathsf{polylog}}(n)
degree. However, by
Lemma
3.4
, the existence of a degree
\Omega(n)

\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation precludes the existence of such a
\Sigma_{2}
-
{\mathsf{uSA}}
proof.
∎

In the remainder of this section we prove
Theorem
4.1
.
Applying
2.4
to
LOP
, we obtain an unsatisfiable formula which consists of the following constraints (axioms) over variables
x_{i,j}
,
where
x_{i,j}=1
means ”
i\prec j
”:

-

M_{i}
:
\bigvee_{j\in[n]\setminus i}x_{j,i}
, for
i\in n
,
(Non-minimality)

-

R_{i}
:
\neg x_{i,i}
, for
i\in[n]
,
(Irreflexivity)

-

A_{i,j}
:
\neg x_{i,j}\vee\neg x_{j,i}
, for distinct
i,j\in[n]
,
(Asymmetry)

-

T_{i,j,k}
:
x_{i,k}\vee\neg x_{i,j}\vee\neg x_{j,k}
, for distinct
i,j,k\in[n]
,
(Transitivity)

-

O_{i,j}
:
x_{i,j}\vee\neg x_{i,j}
, for distinct
i,j\in[n]
.
(Totality)

Note that even though each of these axioms could be expressed as a clause, we consider them as DNFs of degree 1.
We will use the same pseudo-expectation for each weakening of
LOP
, which will be the standard choice
[
5
]
: a uniform distribution over total orders. Let
\Ord=\{x\in[n]^{n}\mid\forall i\neq j,x_{i}\neq x_{j}\}
be the set of total orders on
[n]
. We associate total orders on
[n]
with both
strings in
[n]^{n}

(simply listing the elements in the given order) and bijective maps
[n]\rightarrow[n]
. For
z\in\Ord
, and
T\subseteq[n]
, we define
z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T
as the permutation on
T
,
\pi:|T|\rightarrow T
, that orders those elements in the same order as
z
does. We denote
z\!\!\upharpoonright\!([n]\!\setminus\!T)
by
z\!\setminus\!T
for convenience.

Definition 4.2
.

We define our pseudo-expectation
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
on monomials
t

as

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=\frac{|\{x\in\Ord\mid t(x)=1\}|}{|\Ord|}.

The definition is extended to arbitrary polynomials by linearity.

By our encoding of DNFs as polynomials (
Equation 1
), for a DNF
D=\bigvee_{i}t_{i}
, we use the
notation
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[D\right]
to mean
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{i}t_{i}-1\right]
. Note that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t+t^{\prime}\right]
corresponds to the number of orders either
t
or
t^{\prime}
accepts, plus the number of orders they both accept (all of this divided by
|\Ord|
). So orders accepted by several terms will be ”over-counted”. As an example,

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[M_{i}\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus i}x_{j,i}-1\Big]=\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus i}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[x_{j,i}\right]-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[1\right]=\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus i}\frac{1}{2}-1=\frac{n-1}{2}-1=\frac{n-3}{2},

as exactly half of the total orders are such that
j\prec i
.

We will show that our function
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
gives a degree-
d

\Sigma_{2}
-pseudo-expectation for the set of axioms of LOP for any
d\leq n/300
.
We proceed by showing that it satisfies the conditions of
Definition
3.3
.

Condition 1.
Note that the constant
1
is treated as a monomial such that
1(x)=1
for every
x
, and thus
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[1\right]=1
.

Condition 2.
Notice that we can rewrite any conjunct
t
over the LOP variables

x_{i,j}
to an equivalent conjunct
t^{\prime}
without using negations, simply replacing
any negated variable
x_{i,j}
by the variable
x_{j,i}
.
To see this, notice that for any total order on LOP variables,
x_{j,i}
holds iff
x_{i,j}
does not hold, and so the total orders which satisfy
t
are exactly those that satisfy
t’
. Thus,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t’\right]
.
Observe that the polynomial corresponding to
t^{\prime}
is simply a monomial, and

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t^{\prime}\right]\geq 0
by definition. Since any conical junta is a sum of conjuncts,
this implies that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\cal J\right]\geq 0
for any conical junta
{\cal J}
.

Condition 3.
It remains to show that condition 3 holds for our function
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
.
Our goal is thus to show that for all conical juntas
{\cal J}
, and any weakening
D^{\prime}
of any axiom of
LOP
,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[D^{\prime}{\cal J}\right]\geq 0
, if
\deg(D^{\prime}{\cal J})
is small enough.
First we observe that this easily follows for the axioms other than type
M_{i}
. We will handle axioms of type
M_{i}
in Section
4.1
.

The axioms of LOP that are not of type
M_{i}
must hold for every total order.
For any such axiom
D=\bigvee_{t\in D}t
we have

\cup_{t\in D}\{x\in\Ord|t(x)\}=\Ord
. Furthermore, this holds for any weakening
W
of
D
, as
W
must be satisfied by at least the same assignments as
D
. Hence, for any conical junta
\cal J
, we can deduce that

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W{\cal J}\right]=\left(\sum_{t\in W}\frac{|\{x\in\Ord\mid t(x){\cal J}(x)=1\}|}{|\Ord|}\right)-\frac{|\{x\in\Ord\mid{\cal J}(x)=1\}|}{|\Ord|}\geq 0,

as every order
x
such that
{\cal J}(x)=1
must satisfy at least one term
t
in
W
. This proves that the pseudo-expectation is non-negative for any conical junta and any weakening of an axiom that is not of type
M_{i}
, regardless of the degree.

4.1
The No-Minimal-Element Axioms

By symmetry, it suffices to consider weakenings of
M_{1}
,
weakenings of
M_{i}
can be handled analogously.
It thus suffices, without loss of generality, to prove that for every low-degree conical junta
{\cal J}
, and every low-degree weakening
W
of
M_{1}
,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W{\cal J}\right]\geq 0
. The main difficulty is to handle the diversity of the weakenings of
M_{1}
.

We begin by fixing some notation. It will be convenient to go back and forth between total orders and canonical terms representing them. For a set
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|\geq 2
, consider an ordering
\pi:[|S|]\rightarrow S
of its elements. We will denote by
[\![\pi(1)\ldots\pi(|S|)]\!]
, or
[\![S]\!]_{\pi}
, the term
x_{\pi(1),\pi(2)}x_{\pi(2),\pi(3)}\cdots x_{\pi(|S|-1),\pi(|S|)}
.

We say that a term
mentions
i
if it contains one of the variables
x_{i,j}
or
x_{j,i}
for some
j\neq i
. For example,
x_{1,2}x_{2,3}
mentions
\{1,2,3\}
.
We refer to the set of elements that a term mentions as its
support

and the number of such elements as the
support size
of the term.

To simplify our argument, we will first show that we can assume that each DNF
has several convenient properties.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that a DNF
D
is
normalized
if

1.

Every term
t
of
D
is of the form
t=[\![S]\!]_{\pi}
for some set
S
which includes
1
, and

2.

All terms have the same support size, which we refer to as the
support size of
D
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
W
be a DNF of degree
d
.
There exists a normalized DNF
N
of support size
\leq 2d+1
such that

W
accepts exactly the same total orders as
N
does,
and
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]
.

Proof.

Let
t\not\equiv 0
be one of the terms of
W
. We first transform
t
into a term
t’
with no negated literals as follows: we replace each negated literal
\neg x_{i,j}
in
t
with
x_{j,i}
, noting that for any total order,
x_{j,i}
holds iff
x_{i,j}
does not, and so the total orders satisfying
t
are exactly those satisfying
t’
. Hence,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t’\right]
.

Next, we replace
t
by a collection of terms of the form
[\![S]\!]_{\pi}
for some
S\subseteq[n]
containing
1
(defined below), and a collection of orderings
\pi
on
S
.
Let
T
be the support of
t
and note that
|T|\leq 2d
as each variable in
t
can mention at most two elements. We modify
T
to define
S
as follows:
Include the element
1
if it was not included yet in
T
, and
pad
T
with additional elements as necessary, so that the resulting set
S

has size
|S|=2d+1
.
We use the resulting set
S
as the support for the collection of terms replacing
t
.

Let
\Pi
be the set of total orders on
S
that are consistent with
t
. We claim that
for every total order
z
,
t(z)\Leftrightarrow\bigvee_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![S]\!]_{\pi}(z)
,
and
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![S]\!]_{\pi}\right]
. In other words,
\bigvee_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![S]\!]_{\pi}
accepts exactly the same total orders
t
does,
and each total order accepted by
t
is consistent with exactly one
\pi\in\Pi

(thus total orders accepted by
t
are accepted by a unique term in
\bigvee_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![S]\!]_{\pi}
). We set
N
to be the resulting DNF
\bigvee_{t\in W}\bigvee_{\pi\in\Pi_{t}}[\![S_{t}]\!]_{\pi}
.
∎

Note that the transformation in the proof of
Lemma
4.4
can highly increase the number of terms in
W
, but it does not change its pseudo-expectation,
and it at most doubles the degree:
Notice that for a normalized DNF with support size
k
its degree is exactly

k-1
.
The support size of the resulting DNF
N

is at most
2d+1
, thus the degree of
N
is at most
2d
.

Suppose that
M_{1}
has a weakening
W
of degree
d
such that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W\right]<0
. This lemma implies that there is also a normalized weakening
N
of
M_{1}
with
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]<0
. Thus, to prove that
M_{1}
does not have any weakenings of degree at most
n/200
with negative pseudo-expectation, it is enough to prove that
M_{1}
does not have any normalized weakenings of degree at most
n/100
with negative pseudo-expectation.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any normalized DNF
N
of support size
k
, every term
t
of
N
has
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=1/k!
.

Proof.

Let
t=[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
be one of the terms of
N
.
As a reminder,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=|\{x\in\Ord\mid t(x)=1\}|/|\Ord|
where
\Ord
is the set of total orders. Since
t=[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
,
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=|\{x\in\Ord\mid x\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi\}|/|\Ord|
. To count the number of orders consistent with
t
, one can simply select the positions of the elements in
T
, set them in the right order, and then fill in the rest any possible way, thus,

\displaystyle|\{x\in\Ord\mid x\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi\}|

\displaystyle={n\choose k}(n-k)!=\frac{n!}{k!}.\qed

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
N
be normalized DNF with support size
k
that is a weakening of
M_{1}
,
and let
R
be the set of total orders rejected by
N
.
Then the following holds:

1.

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]\geq 0
if and only if
N
has at least
k!
terms,

2.

Let
S_{i}
be the set of total orders accepted by exactly
i
terms in
N
, then
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]\geq 0
if and only if
|R|\leq\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(i-1)|S_{i}|
.

We remark that the equivalence with the covering problem presented in the introduction comes from the first point in this lemma. Finding a covering of
\Ord^{*1}
with less than
d!
sets (that correspond here to normalized terms) is equivalent to finding a normalized weakening
N
with support size
d
and negative pseudo-expectation.

Proof.

For the first point, using
4.5
,

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sum_{[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\in N}[\![T]\!]_{\pi}-1\bigg]=\sum_{[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\in N}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\right]-1=\frac{\#\text{of terms in $N$}}{k!}-1.

This is non-negative if and only if there are at least
k!
terms in
N
.
For the second point, recall that for terms of the form

t=[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
, we have
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right]=|\{x\in\Ord\mid x\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi\}|/|\Ord|
. Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[N\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Ord|}\bigg(\sum_{[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\in N}|\{x\in\Ord\mid x\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi\}|\bigg)-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Ord|}\bigg(\sum_{x\in\Ord}(|\{[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\in N\mid x\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi\}|\bigg)-\frac{|\Ord|}{|\Ord|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Ord|}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i|S_{i}|\bigg)-\frac{1}{|\Ord|}\bigg(|R|+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|S_{i}|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Ord|}\bigg(\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}(i-1)|S_{i}|-|R|\bigg).\qed

The proof of
Theorem 1.1
uses the second point of this lemma: we show that if the degree of a normalized weakening is low enough, then a large number of orders must be accepted by several terms of
W{\cal J}
.

4.1.1
A Warmup

We begin by considering the case when
{\cal J}=1
.
As a warmup, we show a simplified (but not sufficient) argument,
where we only focus on orders accepted by at least two terms.
Later we present the stronger argument considering orders accepted
by at least 3 terms which is sufficient to prove our theorem. We do this for the general case of arbitrary conical juntas in
subsubsection 4.1.3
.

We use the notation
1z
to denote a total order on
[n]
where the element
1
is first and
z
is a total order of the remaining
n-1
elements.
The notation
i1z
is similarly defined: it denotes orders where
i
is first,
1

is second, and

is a total order of the remaining

Lemma 4.7
.

For a normalized DNF,

1.

Total orders starting with 1 can only be accepted by terms
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
with
\pi(1)=1
.

2.

Total orders with first element
i
and second element 1 can only be accepted by terms
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
with either
\pi(1)=1
and
i\notin T
, or
\pi(1)=i
and

\pi(2)=1
.

Proof.

For the first point, since
1
is in the support
T
for each term in a normalized DNF,
if
\pi(1)\neq 1
then
\pi
is not equal to
1z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T
for any
1z\in\Ord
.
For the second, consider the following two cases:
If
\pi(1)=1
and
i\in T
, then
i1z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T\neq\pi
for any
i1z\in\Ord
. If
\pi(2)=1
and
\pi(1)\neq i
, then
i1z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T\neq\pi
. Otherwise, if
\pi^{-1}(1)>2
, then
i1z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T\neq\pi
.
∎

The following definition helps to describe our combinatorial argument.
To simplify notation, we denote
z\!\setminus\!\{i\}
by
z\!\setminus\!i
, and

[n]\!\setminus\!\{1\}
by
[n]\!\setminus\!1
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
z
be an order on
[n]\!\setminus\!1
. We say that a term
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
is

z
-good for

if

,

,
\pi(1)=i,\pi(2)=1
, and
i1(z\!\setminus\!i)\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi
. That is, the order
\pi
on

i1(z\!\setminus\!i)
.

Notice that if a term
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
is

-good for

then it accepts the order

.
Moreover, it also accepts the orders
ij1(z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\})
and

that is not in its support.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
N
be a normalized DNF with support size
k
that is a weakening of
M_{1}
. Let

be the set of orders rejected by

. If

, there are at least
4|R|/5
total orders accepted by more than one term of

Proof.

If

, we are done, so suppose otherwise.
Recall that we assume for normalized DNFs that each term contains the element
1
,
so we do not explicitly state this condition.
Let
1z\in R
be an order rejected by
N
. Note that since
N
is a weakening of
M_{1}
, every order it rejects must start with 1. Moreover, for every

,
N
must accept the total order
i1(z\!\setminus\!i)
, as it is accepted by

.

If
N
had any terms
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
with
\pi(1)=1
and
1z\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi
,
1z
would not be rejected. Thus, by point 2 of

4.7
, for each
i\in[n]\!\setminus\!1
, the order
i1(z\!\setminus\!i)
must be accepted by a term
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
of
N
with
\pi(1)=i,\pi(2)=1
.
That is, for each
i\in[n]\!\setminus\!1
there is a term of
N
that is
z
-good for
i
.

We call an unordered pair of distinct elements
(i,j)\in([n]\!\setminus\!1)^{2}
a

for

if the following conditions hold:

-

has a term
[\![T_{1}]\!]_{\pi_{1}}
such that
\pi_{1}(1)=i,\pi_{1}(2)=1,j\notin T_{1}
and
i1(z\!\setminus\!i)\!\!\upharpoonright\!T_{1}=\pi_{1}
.

-

has a term
[\![T_{2}]\!]_{\pi_{2}}
such that
\pi_{2}(1)=j,\pi_{2}(2)=1,i\notin T_{2}
and

.

In other words, a pair

(i,j)
is a hitting pair for

if there is some term that is

-good for

but its support does not contain

,
and there is some term that is
z
-good for

but its support does not contain
i
.
Notice that if
(i,j)
is a hitting pair for

, then
both

ij1(z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\})
and

and
[\![T_{2}]\!]_{\pi_{2}}
. Therefore, we can get a lower bound on the over-counting done by the pseudo-expectation of

We define a matrix

with respect to each fixed

is rejected by

Let

be an

, and

has a term

such that

,
\pi(2)=1
,

, and
i1(z\!\setminus\!i)\!\!\upharpoonright\!T=\pi
. That is, the

th entry is 1 iff there is a term
that is
z
-good for
i
but its support does not contain

.
Thus,
(i,j)
is a hitting pair iff both

and

.

As we discussed above,
by point 2 of

, for each

of

that is

-good for

. Since

(there are

not included in the support of any particular term). The matrix thus contains at least

.

Next, observe that the largest number of

: by definition, any such matrix has a

or

, for each unordered pair

. Moreover, the following holds.

Observation 4.10
.

Any

Let

-matrix with

Since the number of

,

. Replace one of the entries corresponding to

) by

.
We can repeat this step at least

for at least

∎

Thus, in the matrix

there are at least

.
This holds for each

.

Each of the hitting pairs in

in particular, as discussed above, if

is a hitting pair for

both

ij1(z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\})
and
ji1(z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\})
are accepted twice.
Notice that a pair
(i,j)
is a hitting pair for both
z
and
z^{\prime}
(where
1z,1z^{\prime}\in R)
iff
z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}=z^{\prime}\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}
. Thus, the total orders we count twice due to
(i,j)

are the same for
z
and
z^{\prime}

iff
z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}=z^{\prime}\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}
.
For any given
z
, the number of
z^{\prime}
such that
z\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}=z^{\prime}\!\setminus\!\{i,j\}
is
at most
(n-1)(n-2)
.
Thus, we can conclude
that there must be at least

\frac{2\left((n-1)(n-k)-{n-1\choose 2}\right)}{(n-1)(n-2)}|R|

orders accepted by at least two terms of

. This value is always smaller than

. However, if

, it is at least
4|R|/5
.
∎

4.1.2
Normalization with Respect to Juntas

The above argument shows that focusing on hitting pairs is almost enough to conclude that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W\right]\geq 0
for weakenings

, even if they have a fairly high degree. Our final
proof for the general case is similar to this argument, but focuses on hitting triples. Before stating and proving this result, we need to deal with conical juntas.
We extend the normalization done in

4.4
to handle the additional inclusion of a conical junta. This is captured by the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let the DNF
W
be a weakening of
M_{1}
and
{\cal J}
be a conical junta. If

and

1.

is the normalized form of

given by

4.4
,

2.

t=[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
such that
1\in T
and
\pi(1)=1
,

3.

\deg(Nt)\leq 2\deg(W)+2\deg({\cal J})
,

4.

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Nt\right]<0
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal J}=\sum_{t\in{\cal J}}t
and suppose that

, where the equality follows by linearity. As the sum is negative, there must be at least one term
t^{*}
such that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Wt^{*}\right]<0

Let

be the support of

. Note that without loss of generality we can assume that

belongs to

: If
1\notin T
then let

be the set of permutations on

. As shown in the proof of

4.4
,

is equivalent
to
\bigvee_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![T\cup\{1\}]\!]_{\pi}

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t^{*}\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}[\![T\cup\{1\}]\!]_{\pi}\right]
. In particular, every order accepted by

is accepted by
[\![T\cup\{1\}]\!]_{\pi}
for exactly one

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Wt^{*}\right]=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W[\![T\cup\{1\}]\!]_{\pi}\right]
, and if
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Wt^{*}\right]<0
then there must exist

such that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W[\![T\cup\{1\}]\!]_{\pi}\right]<0

Suppose that
\pi(1)\neq 1

does not accept any order that starts with

, since
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
does not. As

is a weakening of

, it must accept every order that does not start with

.
Thus,

is equivalent to
[\![T]\!]_{\pi}

and

[\![T]\!]_{\pi}
must be accepted by

This implies that

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\big[W[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\big]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Big(\sum_{w\in W}w-1\Big)[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\bigg]=\sum_{w\in W}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[w[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\right]-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\right]\geq\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\right]-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[[\![T]\!]_{\pi}\right]=0.

Combining this with the previous argument, we get that if there is some conical junta such that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[W{\cal J}\right]<0
then there is also a term

, with
1\in T
and
\pi(1)=1
, such that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Wt\right]<0
. Note that the support size of

is at most
2\deg({\cal J})+1
, and its degree
is at most
2\deg({\cal J})
.

Next, we apply
Lemma
4.4
to the DNF

to obtain a
normalized DNF

. Recall that the degree of

will be at most
2\deg(W)
.
Since both the degree of

and

might have at most doubled, and

, we get that

\deg(Nt)\leq 2\deg(W)+2\deg({\cal J})
, concluding (3).

We now argue that
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Wt\right]<0

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Nt\right]<0
. Expanding

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[Nt\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{v\in N}v-1\Big)t\Big]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[vt\right]-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[t\right].

Since

is the normalization of

Note that in the above lemma, both

.

Let

In

\bigvee_{i\in[n]}x_{i}

(Not all

.

a

(

)

This admits the following simple

5.1
Factoring Reductions

To improve readability, we will use a slightly different notation for
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}^{dt}
problems in this section.

Notation 5.2
.

A
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}^{dt}
search problem
R
is a sequence of search problems
R_{m}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{m}\times\mathcal{O}^{R}_{m}\times\mathcal{W}^{R}_{m}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
\mathcal{O}^{R}_{m}
is the set of possible
R_{m}
-outputs and
\mathcal{W}_{m}^{R}
is the set of
R_{m}
-witnesses. We think of
b\in{\cal O}^{R}_{m}
as being a solution to
a\in\{0,1\}^{m}
for
R_{m}
if
R_{m}(a,b;c)
holds for all
c\in{\cal W}^{R}_{m}
.

A weaker notion of reducibility between
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}
problems has been considered, known as
counter-example reducibility

[
23
]
.
Such a reduction can be interpreted as the following game: Suppose Alice wants to solve
Q
on input
x
. She heard that Bob is actually able to solve
R
using some solver
S
, and thus, using the reduction, she sends
f(x)
to Bob. In turns, Bob uses his solver
b:=S\big(f(x)\big)
and Alice transforms
b
into the output
y:=g(x,b)
. Now, if
y
is indeed an output for
x
, Alice got what she wanted. But let us say that Alice does not trust the solution and, after some searching, is able to find
z
such that
Q(x,y;z)
does not hold (and hence
y
is not a correct output for

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
Q
and
R
be two
{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}^{dt}
search problems and let
(f,g)
be an
R
-formulation of
Q
of size
s(n)
and depth
d(n)
.
If
R_{m}
admits an
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation of degree
d^{\prime}(m)
, then
F_{R(f,g)_{n}}
admits one of degree
d(n)(d^{\prime}(s(n))+2)
.

Proof.

Let us first recall the axioms (in their polynomial inequality form) of the two formulas.

-

Axioms of
R_{m}
:
\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{m,b,c}(a)-1\geq 0
for each
b\in\mathcal{O}^{R}_{s(n)}
with
c
ranging over
\mathcal{W}^{R}_{s(n)}
;

-

Axioms of
R(f,g)_{n}
:
\overline{G}_{b,y}(x)+\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1\geq 0
for each pair
b\in\mathcal{O}^{R}_{s(n)}
and
y\in\mathcal{O}^{Q}_{n}
. Recall that each axiom encodes the fact that either
g_{b}(x)\neq y
or
c
witnesses that
b
is not an
R
-output of
f(x)
and their conjunction contradicts the correctness of the reduction.

As the axioms of
F_{R(f,g)_{n}}
closely resemble the ones of
R_{s(n)}
, our goal is to transform the
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation of the later formula to one of the former.
The idea is pretty simple: compose every polynomial appearing in the refutation of
R_{s(n)}
with the function
f
and the function computed by the sum should still be
-1
.
Let
\sum_{b}J_{b}(a)(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}(a)-1)+J(a)=-1
be an
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation of
R_{s(n)}
.
We explain how to deduce an
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation step by step.

1.

For each axiom
\overline{G}_{b,y}(x)+\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1
, multiply by
G_{b,y}(x)
. Since
T(x)\cdot\overline{T}(x)=0
for any decision tree, we get
G_{b,y}(x)\cdot(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1)
for each
b
and
y
.

2.

Using the fact that
\sum_{y}G_{b,y}=1
(as we sum over the indicator variables of all paths of the decision tree
g_{b}
, and every assignment must be consistent with at least one of those paths), summing over
y
and multiplying by
J_{b}\circ f
gives us

J_{b}\circ f(x)\Big(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1\Big).

3.

Finally, summing over all
b
and summing
J\circ f(x)
, the result becomes

\sum_{b}J_{b}\circ f(x)\Big(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1\Big)+J\circ f(x)

This actually gives a valid
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation since we (1) Multiply our axioms by conical juntas, (2) Sum all the results, and (3) Add an extra junta to the sum.
Looking at this polynomial as a function over the boolean cube, we get the following: let
\chi
be an assignment of
x
and
\alpha
be the resulting assignment
f(\chi)
of
a
.
Then

\sum_{b}J_{b}\circ f(\chi)\Big(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(\chi)-1\Big)+J\circ f(\chi)=\sum_{b}J_{b}(\alpha)\Big(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}(\alpha)-1\Big)+J(\alpha)=-1.

Since functional equality is the same as polynomial equality in multi-linear arithmetic we get the equality
\sum_{b}J_{b}\circ f(x)\Big(\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1\Big)+J\circ f(x)=-1.

Hence, setting
J^{\prime}_{b,y}(x)=J_{b}\circ f(x)\cdot G_{b,y}(x)
and
J^{\prime}(x)=J\circ f(x)
, we get the
{\mathsf{uSA}}
refutation

\sum_{b,y}J^{\prime}_{b,y}(x)\Big(\overline{G}_{b,y}(x)+\sum_{c}\overline{R}_{s(n),b,c}\circ f(x)-1\Big)+J^{\prime}(x)=-1

of
F_{R(f,g)_{n}}.

The degree of the refutation is
d(n)(d^{\prime}(s(n))+2)
.
∎

5.1
.

Separating LeastNumber from StrongAvoid.

Can we show that
{\text{LeastNumber}}\not\leq_{dt}{\text{StrongAvoid}}
?
While
LeastNumber
has a low-degree
{\mathsf{uSA}}
proof, the size of this proof is exponential, and therefore this does not imply a reduction to

{\mathsf{uSA}}
proof precludes us from using the techniques in this paper to obtain this separation. Furthermore, to our knowledge, all of the size lower bounds that have been proven for Sherali-Adams proceed by first proving a degree lower bound via pseudo-expectations, and then applying the size-degree tradeoff
[
22
]
. The only lower bounds which do not proceed via this strategy apply strictly to
{\mathsf{uSA}}

[
12
,
6
]
, they do not apply to the general Sherali-Adams proof system.
Can these techniques be used in order to separate

from

?

Lemma 6.1

(
[

]
)

Let

If

This previous lemma, coupled with our decomposition (

Lemma 6.2

If for every weakening

\mathrm{Checkable}(R)

Decision-tree reductions between problems in

Counter-example reductions are a polynomial-time verifiable way to relate search problems in higher levels of the polynomial hierarchy. We ask whether one can also obtain characterizations of counter-example reductions to

{\mathsf{TF}}\Sigma_{2}

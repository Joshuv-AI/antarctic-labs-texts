Numerical Analysis of 2D Stochastic Navier–Stokes Equations with Transport Noise: Regularity and Spatial Semidiscretization
Binjie Li, Qin Zhou

Abstract

The main challenge arises from the lack of spatial D ⁡ ( A ) D(A) -regularity of the solution (where A A is the Stokes operator), which prevents the application of standard error analysis techniques. To address the low regularity in the numerical analysis, we introduce a novel smoothing operator J h , α = A h α ​ 𝒫 h ​ A − α J_{h,\alpha}=A_{h}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-\alpha} with α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , where A h A_{h} is the discrete Stokes operator and 𝒫 h \mathcal{P}_{h} the discrete Helmholtz projection. This work is the first to establish strong convergence rates for spatial discretizations of 2D stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with transport noise under physical no-slip boundary conditions.
Previous results required periodic boundaries, constant noise, or that the noise coefficients are divergence-free and vanish on the boundary. The breakthrough is achieved via new regularity estimates and a novel smoothing operator that overcomes the lack of solution regularity, extending rigorous numerical analysis to a realistic physical setting for the first time.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the numerical analysis of the two-dimensional incompressible stochastic Navier–Stokes equations (SNSEs) driven by transport noise.
Let
0<T<\infty
and let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded convex polygonal domain.
We consider the SNSE in the Stratonovich form:

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\left[\Delta u(t)-(u(t)\cdot\nabla)u(t)-\nabla\pi(t)\right]\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)u(t)-\nabla\widetilde{\pi}_{n}(t)\right]\circ\,\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

equipped with the incompressibility constraint
\nabla\cdot u(t)=0
, the no-slip boundary condition
u(t)=0
on
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
.
Here,
u
denotes the velocity field,
\pi
the pressure,
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
a sequence of divergence-free vector fields, and
(W_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
a sequence of independent real-valued Brownian motions. The auxiliary pressure processes
(\widetilde{\pi}_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
are introduced to ensure that the stochastic term remains divergence-free.

The systematic analysis of stochastic Navier–Stokes equations (SNSEs) was initiated in the 1970s by the seminal work of Bensoussan and Temam
[
2
]
.
These equations have since been extensively studied in various mathematical settings;
see, for instance,
[
12
,
14
,
19
,
32
]
and the references therein.
A particularly significant class is driven by transport noise, which models the influence of unresolved,
small-scale turbulent fluctuations on the resolved, large-scale flow through a Lagrangian advection mechanism.
This formulation has a rigorous physical foundation derived from Holm’s stochastic variational approach
[
25
]
,
which yields SPDEs that preserve the geometric structures of ideal fluid dynamics, such as Kelvin’s circulation theorem.
The resulting Stratonovich-form SNSEs with multiplicative transport noise, for which (
1.1
) serves as a canonical example,
have recently attracted significant analytical interest (see
[
20
,
21
]
and the references therein).

Substantial progress has also been achieved in the numerical analysis of these equations.
For problems endowed with space-periodic boundary conditions, a succession of works

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
15
,
24
]
has established strong convergence rates in probability
for various approximation schemes. The prevailing analytical strategy involves partitioning the probability space into a regular set,
where strong convergence is attained, and a singular set whose probability measure diminishes as the discretization is refined.
More recently, Feng and Vo
[
18
]
derived full moment error estimates in strong norms for numerical approximations of
a two-dimensional SNSE under space-periodic boundary conditions. Their analysis relies critically on a uniform boundedness
condition for the noise coefficients, which is instrumental in proving the exponential stability of the solution.

In contrast, the analysis under no-slip boundary conditions is significantly more challenging,
with the literature being considerably less developed.
Convergence for multiplicative noise was first shown by Brzeźniak, Careli, and Prohl
[
13
]
without explicit rates,
and subsequently, a first-order convergence rate was established for additive noise by Breit and Prohl
[
10
]
.
A more recent contribution by Breit and Prohl
[
9
]
derived strong convergence rates in probability for multiplicative noise via discrete stopping times.
This result, however, is subject to restrictive assumptions: the spatial domain must have a smooth boundary, and the noise coefficients
must be divergence-free and vanish on the boundary.
Further related results can be found in
[
17
,
30
]
.

The challenge is further amplified when considering SNSEs driven by transport noise, a regime that remains largely unexplored.
To our knowledge, the only convergence result is due to Breit et al.
[
8
]
, who established a mean-square rate of order
1/2
for the temporal
discretization of the two-dimensional SNSEs under periodic boundary conditions.
However, this result relies on the restrictive assumption that the transport noise vector fields
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
are constant.
This assumption is crucial for guaranteeing both the high regularity of the continuous solution and the stability of the temporal discretization,
which in turn facilitates the control of the non-Lipschitz nonlinearity.

This exposes a fundamental gap: for SNSEs with transport noise and no-slip boundaries, solutions generally fail to possess
D(A)
-regularity,
thereby invalidating the analytical tools employed in all previous convergence analyses.
Consequently, no convergence rates are available for spatial discretizations in this physically relevant setting.

This paper bridges this gap by introducing novel theoretical and numerical tools,
delivering the first convergence analysis for this class of problems. Our contributions are twofold:

1.

First, we establish key regularity properties for the weak solution to the model problem (
1.1
),
assuming a smallness condition on the transport noise vector fields
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
,
i.e.,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}
is sufficiently small.
This mathematical assumption has a clear physical interpretation: it characterizes a dynamical system whose energy is dominated by large-scale motions,
which is a typical regime where the deterministic dynamics prevail and the stochastic effects can be treated as a perturbation.
Under this condition, we demonstrate in
Proposition

3.1
that for some
\varrho\in(0,1/2)
, the solution
u
satisfies

u\in L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1+\varrho}_{\sigma})).

This regularity result provides the essential foundation for the subsequent numerical analysis.

2.

Second, we establish a strong error estimate for a spatial semidiscretization based on the MINI finite element method.
A key difficulty arises from the lack of
\mathrm{D}(A)
-regularity of the continuous solution
u
,
which prevents the direct application of the discrete Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}_{h}
to
Au
.
To overcome this obstacle, we introduce and systematically analyze an auxiliary operator

J_{h,\alpha}:=A_{h}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, where
A_{h}
is the discrete Stokes operator.
This operator serves as an effective bridge between the continuous and discrete spaces, replacing the role of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
.
By employing stochastic analytic tools, including Itô’s formula and the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, we derive the following strong error estimate:

\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})))}+\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}))}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h),

where
u_{h}
is the strong solution of the spatial semidiscretization and
c>0
is a constant independent of the mesh size
h
, as stated in
Theorem

4.2
.

These theoretical results provide a foundation for the further numerical analysis of fully discrete finite element approximations of the two-dimensional stochastic
Navier–Stokes equations with transport noise under no-slip boundary conditions.
The primary novelty of this work lies in a new analytical framework that circumvents the lack of
D(A)
-regularity—a fundamental obstacle in the numerical analysis of stochastic
Navier–Stokes equations under no-slip (i.e., homogeneous Dirichlet) boundary conditions.
The same methodology extends naturally to the stochastic Stokes equations driven by rough noise with Dirichlet boundary conditions, where solution paths likewise lack
D(A)
-regularity.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
lays the foundation by introducing the functional–probabilistic framework, including Sobolev and interpolation spaces, the cylindrical Brownian motion
W
, relevant spaces of stochastic processes, and the Stokes operator
A
.
Section
3
establishes key regularity properties of the weak solution, which play an essential role in the subsequent numerical analysis.
In Section
4
, we derive a strong convergence rate for the spatial semidiscretization based on the MINI finite element method.
The paper concludes with Section
5
, which summarizes the main contributions and identifies several prominent open problems for future research.

Preliminaries

For Banach spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
,
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
denotes the space of bounded linear operators from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
.
The identity operator on any space is denoted by
I
.
Given an interval
D\subset\mathbb{R}
and a Banach space
X
,
C(D;X)
denotes the space of continuous functions from
D
to
X
, endowed with the supremum norm

\|\xi\|_{C(D;X)}=\sup_{t\in D}\|\xi(t)\|_{X},\quad\xi\in C(D;X).

Fix a terminal time
T>0
, and let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded convex polygonal domain with boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
.
For
q\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
and
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
the standard Sobolev spaces (cf.
[
6
, Chapter III]
).
For
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, we define the complex interpolation spaces

H^{\alpha}:=\bigl[L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),\,W^{2,2}(\mathcal{O})\bigr]_{\alpha/2},\qquad\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}:=\bigl[L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,W^{2,2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\bigr]_{\alpha/2}.

For
\alpha\geqslant 0
, let
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
denote the domain of the fractional power
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, where
\Delta
is the Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
;
the space
\dot{H}^{-\alpha}
is defined as the dual of
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
.
The space
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
is continuously embedded into
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in(0,2]
(see, e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 1.8]
).
We adopt the following shorthand notation:

\mathbb{L}^{q}:=L^{q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\mathbb{W}^{1,q}:=W^{1,q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad q\in[1,\infty].

With a slight abuse of notation,
\mathbb{L}^{q}
will also be used to denote
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, which will be clear from the context.
The inner product in either
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
or
\mathbb{L}^{2}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

We introduce the solenoidal space

\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}:=\overline{\{v\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot v=0\}}^{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-closure of smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free vector fields.
Let
\mathcal{P}:L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
be the orthogonal Helmholtz projection onto
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
.
The Stokes operator
A
is defined by

Av:=-\mathcal{P}\Delta v,

with domain

D(A):=\{v\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}\cap\mathbb{W}^{2,2}\colon v=0\text{ on $\partial\mathcal{O}$}\}.

The operator
A
is positive definite and self-adjoint, with a bounded inverse
A^{-1}:\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}\to D(A)
(cf.
[
16
, Theorem 5.5]
).
For
\alpha>0
, we define the fractional power space
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}:=D(A^{\alpha/2})
,
endowed with the graph norm
\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}:=\|A^{\alpha/2}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
, and denote its dual by
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\alpha}
.
Furthermore, there exists a constant
c>0
, independent of
\alpha
and
v
, such that for each
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}
,

\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\leqslant\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\leqslant c\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}.

The Helmholtz projection operator
\mathcal{P}
extends to a bounded linear operator from
\dot{H}^{-1}
to
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-1}
.
This extension is defined via duality: for all
v\in\dot{H}^{-1}
and
w\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}
,

\langle\mathcal{P}v,w\rangle_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-1},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}:=\langle v,w\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{*},X}
denotes the dual pairing between a Banach space
X
and its dual
X^{*}
.
Moreover, the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the following boundedness properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
is bounded in the following senses:

(i)

\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha},\mathbb{H}^{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

(ii)

\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\beta})
for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\beta\in(0,\alpha]
with
\beta<1/2
.

(iii)

\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in[-1,0)
.

Proof.

The proof is standard. Boundedness on
\mathbb{H}^{1}
follows by adapting the argument of
[
6
, Proposition IV.3.7]
,
which relies on the regularity theory for the Poisson equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions
[
23
, Theorem 1.10]
. Boundedness on
L^{2}
is immediate, as
\mathcal{P}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection. Complex interpolation between these two endpoints yields (i). Property (ii) is a direct consequence of
[
29
, Proposition 2.16]
, while (iii) follows from
[
29
, Equation (4.45)]
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The restriction
\beta<1/2
in
Lemma

2.1
(ii) is necessary because the Helmholtz projection of a smooth vector field,
even one vanishing on
\partial\mathcal{O}
, need not itself vanish on the boundary.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space endowed with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
,
supporting a sequence
(W_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of independent real-valued
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motions.
Denote by
\ell^{2}
the real Hilbert space of square-summable sequences.
The
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\ell^{2}
-cylindrical Brownian motion
W=(W(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is defined by its action on any
l=(l_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}\in\ell^{2}
:

W(t)l:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}l_{n}W_{n}(t),\quad t\geqslant 0.

For each
t\geqslant 0
, the series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
, ensuring that
W(t)
is a bounded linear operator
from
\ell^{2}
to
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
.
For a Hilbert space
U
, we write
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},U)
for the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
\ell^{2}
to
U
.

For a separable Banach space
E
and
p\in[1,\infty]
,
let
L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{p}(\Omega;E)
denote the Bochner space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P};E)
,
and let
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];E))
denote the space of all
\mathbb{F}
-adapted,
continuous,
E
-valued processes belonging to
L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];E))
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times(0,T);E)
as the space of all
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable
processes
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\to E
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times(0,T);E)}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{E}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}
.

3
Weak Solutions

The stochastic model (
1.1
) is originally formulated in the Stratonovich form, which arises naturally from its physical derivation involving
transport noise. Applying the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
yields the following abstract stochastic evolution equation in the solenoidal space
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
:

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\left[-Au-\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)u)\right](t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}u(t)\circ\,\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

where the transport operator
L_{\xi_{n}}
is formally defined by

L_{\zeta_{n}}v:=\mathcal{P}\bigl((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v\bigr).

(3.1)

To enable a rigorous analytical treatment, we convert this equation to its Itô form. This transformation introduces a second-order Itô correction term in the drift:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u(t)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[-Au-\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)u)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}^{2}u\Bigr](t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t).

This additional term motivates the introduction of the modified Stokes operator

\mathcal{A}v:=Av-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}^{2}v,\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}.

(3.2)

In terms of
\mathcal{A}
, the Itô formulation simplifies to the compact form:

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\left[-\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{P}((u\cdot\nabla)u)\right](t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{n}(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

(3.3)

Throughout this paper, we impose the following structural assumptions on the transport noise vector fields
(\zeta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Hypothesis 3.1
.

Let
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}\subset\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
be divergence-free vector fields satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle<\infty.

(3.4)

Moreover, we assume that the sum in (
3.4
) is small enough so that there exists a constant
\kappa_{\sigma}\in(0,1)
for which

\Big\|\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n}}^{2}v\Big\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant\kappa_{\sigma}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}},\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}.

(3.5)

Under
3.1
, the operator
\mathcal{A}
is an isomorphism between
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
,
and it is a positive definite and self-adjoint operator in
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
. Moreover, the following norm equivalence holds:

(1-\kappa_{\sigma})\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}}\leqslant\|\mathcal{A}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant(1+\kappa_{\sigma})\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}},\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2}.

(3.6)

For
\alpha>0
, define the space
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}
as the domain of
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha/2}
, equipped with the graph norm

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}:=\|\mathcal{A}^{\alpha/2}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

By (
3.6
) and standard complex interpolation (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Theorems 1.15 and 16.1]
), the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\theta}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta}}
are equivalent for all
\theta\in[0,2]
:

(1-\kappa_{\sigma})^{\theta/2}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta}}\leqslant\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\theta}}\leqslant(1+\kappa_{\sigma})^{\theta/2}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta}},\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta}.

(3.7)

In particular, when
\theta=1
, we have

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}=\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}.

(3.8)

We are now in a position to define the concept of a weak solution.

Definition 3.1

(Weak Solution)
.

A stochastic process

u\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})\bigr)\cap L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathcal{H}^{1})

is called a
weak solution
to the model problem (
1.1
) if,
for every
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

u(t)=u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}u(s)+\mathcal{P}[(u(s)\cdot\nabla)u(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}F(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

holds in
\mathcal{H}^{-1}
, where the operator
F\in\mathcal{L}\bigl(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})\bigr)
is defined by

F(v)l=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}l_{n}L_{\zeta_{n}}v,\quad\text{for all }v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1},\ l=(l_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}.

(3.9)

The main result of this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that

u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\sigma})\cap L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{1/2}_{\sigma}).

Then the model problem (
1.1
) admits a unique weak solution. Moreover, there exists a constant
\varrho\in(0,1/2)
such that the solution satisfies:

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}_{\sigma})\cap C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\sigma})),

(3.10)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\varrho}_{\sigma})),

(3.11)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}^{\varrho+1}_{\sigma}).

(3.12)

Remark 3.1
.

We note that if the transport noise vector fields
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}\subset\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
are divergence-free and satisfy the summability condition

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}<\infty,

then the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution can be established under the significantly weaker assumption
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})
(cf.
[
21
, Theorem 4.21]
).
In the present work, the smallness condition on the transport noise vector fields imposed in
3.1
(i.e.,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}
being sufficiently small) is not required for mere existence and uniqueness. Instead, it is essential for obtaining the higher spatial regularity
u\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}^{\varrho+1}_{\sigma})
as stated in (
3.12
).

Remark 3.2
.

A fundamental obstruction to higher regularity stems from the mapping properties of the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
.
As noted in Lemma
2.1
(ii),
\mathcal{P}
fails to be bounded from
\mathbb{H}^{\alpha}
to
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\beta}
for
\beta\geqslant 1/2
.
As a result,
F(v)
cannot, in general, be valued in
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta})

for any
\theta\geqslant 1/2
, regardless of the regularity of
v
.
This directly implies that the solution
u
to the model problem
Eq.

1.1
cannot, in general, possess paths with
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1+\varrho}
-regularity for
\varrho\geqslant 1/2
.

Remark 3.3
.

The well-posedness theory for the two-dimensional stochastic Navier–Stokes equations with transport noise is well established.
For the Cauchy problem, Mikulevicius and Rozovskii
[
28
]
proved the existence and pathwise uniqueness of a global strong solution.
In the periodic setting, Flandoli and Luongo
[
21
, Theorem 4.21]
established the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions,
while Breit et al.
[
9
]
derived certain regularity estimates for weak pathwise solutions under the assumption that the transport noise vector fields
(\zeta_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
are constant.
The analysis in
[
21
, Theorem 4.21]
also extends to no-slip boundary conditions with only minor modifications.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the regularity results
Eqs.

3.11
and
3.12
in
Proposition

3.1
are new.
We also refer the reader to the recent work of Agresti and Veraar
[
1
]
for the theoretical
analysis of a class of stochastic Navier–Stokes equations that are closely related to the model problem
Eq.

1.1
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.
.

The existence and uniqueness of a weak solution, along with the basic regularity (
3.10
), are standard. These results follow directly from a variational approach, mirroring the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 4.2]
with minor adjustments to accommodate our specific setting.
The core of our contribution is the derivation of the higher-order regularity estimates (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).
For the remainder of the proof, let
c>0
be a generic constant, independent of the parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
whose value may vary from one instance to the next.

Step 1.
We prove the existence of
0<\varrho^{*}<\tfrac{1}{2}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\varrho^{*}+1}\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha},\,\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}))}<\sqrt{2},

(3.13)

The argument is divided into three parts. We set
C_{\zeta}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}
.

(a) Bound for
\alpha=1
.

For any
v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, the definition of
F
in
Eq.

3.9
and the contractivity of the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathbb{L}^{2}
imply

\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{P}((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leqslant C_{\zeta}\|\nabla v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=C_{\zeta}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}.

Hence,

\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{C_{\zeta}}\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}^{2}.

Now, applying the norm identity
Eq.

3.8
:

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}=\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}^{2},

and combining with the previous inequality, we obtain

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\geqslant\left(\frac{1}{C_{\zeta}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}^{2}=\frac{2+C_{\zeta}}{2C_{\zeta}}\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}^{2}.

Rearranging this inequality and taking square roots gives

\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0})}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{2C_{\zeta}}{2+C_{\zeta}}}\,\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}},

which implies the bound

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{1},\,\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0}))}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{2C_{\zeta}}{2+C_{\zeta}}}.

(3.14)

(b) Bound for
\alpha=\alpha_{0}>1
.

Fix any
\alpha_{0}\in(1,\tfrac{3}{2})
. For any
n\geqslant 1
and
v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}}
,
by Lemma
2.1
(ii) and the standard product estimate

\|(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha_{0}-1}}\leqslant c\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}}},

we have

\|\mathcal{P}((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}-1}}\leqslant c\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}-1})}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{P}((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}-1}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\left(c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha_{0}}}.

Using the norm equivalence (
3.7
), we conclude that

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}},\,\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}-1}))}\leqslant\left(c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.15)

(c) Interpolation.

We now interpolate between the bounds (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) to obtain estimates for intermediate regularity.
Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and define
\alpha=1+(\alpha_{0}-1)\theta
. Standard interpolation theory (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Theorem 16.1]
) yields the identities

[\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}}]_{\theta}=\mathcal{H}^{\alpha},\qquad[\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0}),\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}-1})]_{\theta}=\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}),

with equality of norms. Applying the interpolation theorem for linear operators (see
[
34
, Theorem 1.15]
) to the bounds (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) gives

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}))}\leqslant\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{0}))}^{1-\theta}\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha_{0}-1}))}^{\theta}.

Substituting (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) gives

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}))}\leqslant\left(\frac{2C_{\zeta}}{2+C_{\zeta}}\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}}\left(c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}.

The right-hand side is a continuous function of
\theta
that equals
\sqrt{2C_{\zeta}/(2+C_{\zeta})}<\sqrt{2}
at
\theta=0
. Consequently, there exists a small
\theta>0
such that the expression is strictly less than
\sqrt{2}
. Defining
\varrho^{*}:=(\alpha_{0}-1)\theta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
completes the proof of (
3.13
).

Step 2.

It is well known that the space
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
admits an orthonormal basis
(e_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
consisting of eigenvectors of the operator
\mathcal{A}
.
For each
n\geqslant 1
, let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
onto the finite-dimensional subspace
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
.
We consider the Galerkin approximation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}u^{(n)}=-\mathcal{P}_{n}\big(\mathcal{A}u^{(n)}+(u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}\big((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}\big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
u^{(n)}(0)=\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}.\end{cases}

Since this is a finite-dimensional stochastic differential equation with locally monotone drift and linear diffusion coefficients,
the classical theory (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 3.27]
) guarantees the existence of a unique strong solution
u^{(n)}
for every
n\geqslant 1
.
Moreover, following the arguments in
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
, we obtain the uniform bound

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\left(\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}))}+\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}))}\right)<\infty.

(3.16)

Step 3.

Fix
0<\varrho<\varrho^{*}
, where
\varrho^{*}
is the constant from Step 1.
Our goal is to establish the following uniform bounds for the Galerkin approximations
(u^{(n)})_{n\geqslant 1}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}))}<\infty,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1})}<\infty.

(3.18)

The proof is divided into three substeps.

(a).

For any
0<\alpha\leqslant\varrho^{*}
, we establish the following
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely inequality:

\displaystyle\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}^{2}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{(n)}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha+1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}^{2}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{(n)}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}^{2}\|u^{(n)}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}(s),\,\mathcal{P}_{n}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W_{k}(s),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Applying Itô’s formula to
\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}
yields the following identity, which holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{P}_{n}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u^{(n)}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha+1}}^{2}+\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}((u^{(n)}(s)\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s)),\,\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}(s)\rangle\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{P}_{n}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}(s),\,\mathcal{P}_{n}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W_{k}(s).

The nonlinear term admits the estimate:

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}((u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}),\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle\mathcal{P}((u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}),\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\epsilon\|u^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha+1}}^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\|\mathcal{P}((u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}}^{2},\quad\forall\epsilon>0,

where we used that
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)}
is in the range of the projection
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
We bound the Itô correction term as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{P}_{n}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{P}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leqslant C_{F}\|u^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha+1}}^{2},

where
C_{F}:=\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha+1},\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}))}^{2}
.
Combining these estimates and using the contractivity of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(2-\epsilon-C_{F}\big)\|u^{(n)}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{P}((u^{(n)}(s)\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{A}^{\alpha}u^{(n)},\,\mathcal{P}_{n}((\zeta_{k}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)}(s))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W_{k}(s).

Since
C_{F}<2
(see (
3.13
)), we can choose
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small to ensure that
2-\epsilon-C_{F}>0
.
Finally, the norm equivalence (
3.7
) implies the continuous embedding

\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\alpha-1}
. Together with the product estimate from
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
,

\|\mathcal{P}((u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)})\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha-1}}\leqslant c\|u^{(n)}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\|u^{(n)}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}.

this yields the desired inequality (
3.19
).

(b).
Apply the stochastic Gronwall lemma
[
33
, Theorem 4]
to (
3.19
) with
\alpha=\varrho^{*}
. For any
p\in(0,1)
and
\nu\in(1,1/p)
, this yields

\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}}))}\leqslant c\left(\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\frac{p\nu}{\nu-1}\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right)^{\big(1-\frac{1}{\nu}\big)\frac{1}{2p}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2p\nu}(\Omega;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}})}.

By the uniform bound (
3.16
), the exponential moment on the right-hand side is finite and uniformly bounded in
n
. Hence,

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}}))}\leqslant c\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2p\nu}(\Omega;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}})},\quad p\in(0,1),\,\nu\in(1,1/p).

Since
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1/2})
, we may take
p=\varrho/\varrho^{*}\in(0,1)
to obtain

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2\varrho/\varrho^{*}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}}))}<\infty.

Combined with (
3.16
), this establishes the uniform bound (
3.17
) via the interpolation inequality

\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}))}\leqslant\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}))}^{1-\varrho/\varrho^{*}}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2\varrho/\varrho^{*}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho^{*}}))}^{\varrho/\varrho^{*}}.

(c).
Taking the expectation of (
3.19
) with
\alpha=\varrho
and
t=T
and noting that the stochastic integral is a martingale with zero mean, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}\|u^{(n)}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left[\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}+\|u^{(n)}\|_{C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho})}^{2}\|u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1})}^{2}\right].

The right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
n
by (
3.16
), (
3.17
), and the assumption
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1/2})
. This establishes the uniform bound (
3.18
).

Step 4.

From the uniform bound (
3.18
), we first deduce

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|\mathcal{A}u^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho-1})}<\infty.

To control the nonlinear term, we employ an estimate analogous to that in
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
:

\|\mathcal{P}((v\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho-1}}\leqslant c\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}},\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}.

Applying this to
v=u^{(n)}
and combining it with (
3.16
) and (
3.18
) yields the uniform bound

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|\mathcal{P}((u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla)u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho-1})}<\infty.

Furthermore, using (
3.13
) and (
3.18
) gives

\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|F(u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}))}<\infty.

These uniform bounds, together with (
3.18
), enable a standard variational argument (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Section 4.3]
and
[
27
, Chapter 5]
)
to establish the existence of a unique weak solution
u
to the model problem (
1.1
), satisfying the regularity properties (
3.11
) and (
3.12
).
Finally, the regularity property (
3.10
) follows from an argument analogous to that in
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
. This completes the proof of
Proposition

3.1
.
∎

4
Spatial Semidiscretization

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a conforming, quasi-uniform triangulation of the domain
\mathcal{O}
,
consisting of triangles. Denote by
h
the maximum diameter of the elements in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
.
For each element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, define the local velocity space

\mathcal{P}_{1}(K):=\bigl[P_{1}(K)\oplus\operatorname{span}\{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\}\bigr]^{2},

where
P_{1}(K)
denotes the space of linear polynomials on
K
, and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
are the barycentric (area) coordinates associated with the vertices of
K
. The enrichment term
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}
is the cubic bubble function vanishing on
\partial K
.
The classical MINI finite element spaces (cf.
[
23
, Section 4.1]
) are then defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{v_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h},\;v_{h}|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\right\},

\displaystyle M_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\phi_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}):\phi_{h}|_{K}\in P_{1}(K)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\right\}.

The discrete divergence-free subspace
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}\subset\mathbb{L}_{h}
is characterized by the weak incompressibility condition

\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}:=\left\{v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}:\langle\nabla\cdot v_{h},\phi_{h}\rangle=0\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in M_{h}\right\}.

(4.1)

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
denote the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
.

The discrete Stokes operator
A_{h}:\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
is given by

\langle A_{h}u_{h},v_{h}\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla u_{h}:\nabla v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}.

It satisfies the following standard approximation property (see, e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
):

\|A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-\alpha},\mathbb{L}^{2})}+h\|A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-\alpha},\dot{H}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{2-\alpha},\quad\alpha\in[0,1],

(4.2)

where
c>0
is a constant independent of
h
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, define the Hilbert space
\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\alpha}
as
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
endowed with the norm

\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\alpha}}:=\|A_{h}^{\alpha/2}v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}.

The following norm equivalence holds for all
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
:

\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}}\leqslant\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta}}\leqslant c\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}},

(4.3)

where the constant
c>0
is independent of both
h
and
\theta
. Moreover, the inverse inequality

\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{2}}}\leqslant ch^{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}}},

(4.4)

holds for all
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
and all
\theta_{1}\leqslant\theta_{2}
, where
c>0
is independent of
h
and
v_{h}
.

This section considers the following spatial semidiscretization of the model problem
Eq.

1.1
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u_{h}(t)=-\Big[A_{h}u_{h}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}u_{h}+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(u_{h})\Big](t)\,\mathrm{d}t+F_{h}(u_{h}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
u_{h}(0)=\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}.\end{cases}

(4.5)

Here, for each
n
, the discrete transport operator
L_{\zeta_{n},h}:\dot{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
is given by

L_{\zeta_{n},h}v:=\mathcal{P}_{h}\bigl((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v\bigr),\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}^{1}.

(4.6)

The nonlinear mapping
G
is defined as

G(v):=(v\cdot\nabla)v+\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot v)v,\qquad\forall v\in\dot{H}^{1}.

(4.7)

Finally, for any
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
, the operator
F_{h}(v_{h})\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})
is given as

F_{h}(v_{h})l:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}l_{n}L_{\zeta_{n},h}v_{h},\qquad\forall l=(l_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}\in\ell^{2}.

(4.8)

Remark 4.1
.

The discrete Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
extends uniquely—by duality—to a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{P}_{h}:\dot{H}^{-1}\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
via

\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}f,v_{h}\rangle:=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}},\qquad\forall f\in\dot{H}^{-1},\;v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-1},\dot{H}^{1}}
denotes the duality pairing between
\dot{H}^{-1}
and
\dot{H}^{1}
.
This extension makes the discrete transport operators
L_{\zeta_{n},h}
well-defined on the larger space
\mathbb{L}^{2}
.
Indeed, under condition
\zeta_{n}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
(cf.
3.1
), the map

v\mapsto(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)v

is continuous from
\mathbb{L}^{2}
into
\dot{H}^{-1}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{P}_{h}\bigl((\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)\cdot\bigr)
defines a bounded linear operator

L_{\zeta_{n},h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}.

We say that
u_{h}
is a strong solution to
Eq.

4.5
if it satisfies the following properties:

•

u_{h}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, continuous,
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
-valued process;

•

For all
t\in[0,T]
, the following identity holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely:

\displaystyle u_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\biggl[A_{h}u_{h}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}u_{h}(s)+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(u_{h}(s))\biggr]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}F_{h}(u_{h}(s))\,\mathrm{d}W(s).

We first present the well-posedness and stability of the spatial semidiscretization
Eq.

4.5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})
.
Then the spatial semidiscretization (
4.5
) admits a unique strong solution
u_{h}
. Moreover,

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}))}

is uniformly bounded with respect to the spatial mesh size
h
.

Proof.

The proof follows from standard arguments; we provide a brief sketch for completeness.

Existence and uniqueness.
Since
\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}
is finite-dimensional, the operator
A_{h}
is bounded on it.
By assumption
Eq.

3.4
,
the operators
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}
and
F_{h}
are also bounded,
with the former acting on
\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}
and the latter mapping
\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}
into
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})
.
For any
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\langle A_{h}v_{h},v_{h}\rangle=\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2},\quad\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}G(v_{h}),v_{h}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\big\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}v_{h},v_{h}\big\rangle=-\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $n\geqslant 1$,}

\displaystyle\|F_{h}(v_{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}^{2}.

These yield the coercivity relation

2\Big\langle-A_{h}v_{h}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}v_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}G(v_{h}),\,v_{h}\Big\rangle+\|F_{h}(v_{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}^{2}+2\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}=0.

(4.9)

The nonlinear term
\mathcal{P}_{h}G
is continuous. Moreover, for all
v_{h},w_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
, it satisfies the following growth and local monotonicity estimates:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{h}G(v_{h})\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{-2}\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}^{2},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}G(v_{h})-\mathcal{P}_{h}G(w_{h}),\,v_{h}-w_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{-2}\bigl(\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}+\|w_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}\bigr)\|v_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0}}^{2},

(4.11)

for a constant
c>0
independent of
h
. These estimates follow from Hölder’s inequality and standard inverse estimates (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem 4.5.11]
).
These properties—namely, the boundedness of the linear operators and the growth and monotonicity conditions (
4.10
)–(
4.11
) for the nonlinear term—satisfy the hypotheses of
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
. Therefore, (
4.5
) admits a unique strong solution
u_{h}
.

Uniform bound.

The uniform bound for the solution is obtained via an argument analogous to
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
.
First, we apply Itô’s formula to
\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
. The stochastic integral term vanishes due to the
skew-symmetry identity
\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)u_{h},u_{h}\rangle=0
for all
n\geqslant 1
,
yielding the following identity that holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|u_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{2\Bigl\langle-A_{h}u_{h}(s)+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}L_{\zeta_{n},h}^{2}u_{h}(s)-\mathcal{P}_{h}G(u_{h}(s)),\,u_{h}(s)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\|F_{h}(u_{h}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\ell^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Next, substituting the coercivity relation (
4.9
) furnishes the pathwise energy equality

\|u_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

This immediately implies the estimate

\|u_{h}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}+2\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant 2\|\mathcal{P}_{h}u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leqslant 2\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}}^{2},

where we used the contractivity of the
L^{2}
-projection
\mathcal{P}_{h}
.
Taking the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
-norm of the above estimate and using the assumption
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})
, we conclude that

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}))}

is uniformly bounded with respect to the spatial mesh size
h
. This completes the proof.
∎

Then, we present the convergence result for the spatial semi-discretization
Eq.

4.5
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of
Proposition

3.1
, let
u
be the unique weak solution to (
1.1
) and let

u_{h}
be the strong solution to the semidiscrete problem (
4.5
).
Then, for the exponent
\varrho\in(0,1/2)
from
Proposition

3.1
, there exists a constant
c>0
, independent of the mesh size
h
, such that

\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h).

(4.12)

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

4.2
.
Throughout,
c>0
denotes a generic constant independent of the mesh size

, whose value may vary from line to line.

4.1
Preliminary estimates

We recall standard approximation results and derive essential operator-norm estimates. The projection error satisfies

\|I-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\theta_{2}}_{\sigma},\dot{H}^{\theta_{1}})}\leqslant ch^{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}},\qquad\theta_{1}\in[0,1],\ \theta_{2}\in[\theta_{1},2].

(4.13)

For
\theta\in[0,1]
, the composite operator
(I-\mathcal{P}_{h})A^{-1}\mathcal{P}
admits the bound:

\displaystyle\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{h})A^{-1}\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|I-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\|A^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-\theta})}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta})}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{2-\theta}\|A^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-\theta})}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta})}

\displaystyle=ch^{2-\theta}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-\theta})}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{2-\theta},

where the last inequality follows from
Lemma

2.1
(iii).
Combining this with the approximation property (
4.2
) (with
\alpha=\theta
)
and applying the triangle inequality yields

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{2-\theta},\qquad\theta\in[0,1].

(4.14)

Furthermore, using the definition of the discrete divergence-free space
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
(see
Eq.

4.1
) together with
standard approximation properties of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection, we obtain: for any
\alpha\in[0,2]
,

|\langle\nabla\cdot v_{h},\phi\rangle|\leqslant ch^{\alpha}\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}\|\phi\|_{H^{\alpha}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h},\ \phi\in H^{\alpha}.

(4.15)

The following lemma provides auxiliary bounds crucial for the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following bounds hold:

(i)

For all
\theta\in[0,1]
,

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\theta},\dot{H}^{-1}_{\sigma,h})}\leqslant ch^{\theta+1}.

(4.16)

(ii)

For all
\theta\in(0,1/2)
,

\|\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\theta}.

(4.17)

Proof.

These estimates follow from standard finite element analysis for the Stokes problem; see, e.g.,
[
23
, Chapter II]
.
For completeness, we present a self-contained proof.

Proof of (i).

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\theta}
.
By Theorem IV.3.5 in
[
6
]
and the boundedness of
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathbb{H}^{\theta}
(cf.
Lemma

2.1
(i)),
there exists a zero-mean function
\varphi\in H^{\theta+1}
such that

v-\mathcal{P}v=\nabla\varphi,\qquad\|\varphi\|_{H^{\theta+1}}\leqslant c\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\theta}}.

(4.18)

For any
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
, the Galerkin orthogonality of the
L^{2}
-projection
\mathcal{P}_{h}
implies

\langle v_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})v\rangle=\langle v_{h},(I-\mathcal{P})v\rangle=\langle v_{h},\nabla\varphi\rangle=-\langle\nabla\cdot v_{h},\varphi\rangle,

where the last equality follows from integration by parts.
Applying estimate (
4.15
) with
\alpha=\theta+1
and using the bound for
\|\varphi\|_{H^{\theta+1}}
from
Eq.

4.18
yields

|\langle v_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})v\rangle|\leqslant ch^{\theta+1}\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{\theta+1}}\leqslant ch^{\theta+1}\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\theta}}.

Taking the supremum over
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\theta}
with
\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\theta}}=1
and
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
with
\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}=1
gives
the desired bound
Eq.

4.16
.

Proof of (ii).
Let
\theta\in(0,1/2)
. We decompose the difference as

\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}=(I-\mathcal{P}_{h})\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})

and estimate the two terms on the right-hand side.
For the first term,
Eq.

4.13
with
\theta_{1}=0,\theta_{2}=\theta
and the boundedness of
\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{H}^{\theta}\to\dot{H}^{\theta}_{\sigma}
from Lemma
2.1
(ii) imply

\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{h})\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\theta}.

For the second term, we employ the inverse inequality
\|w_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant ch^{-1}\|w_{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}_{\sigma,h}}

for
w_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}
(see (
4.4
)) together with Lemma
4.1
(i) to obtain

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\theta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{-1}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\theta},\dot{H}^{-1}_{\sigma,h})}\leqslant ch^{\theta}.

Combining these two bounds via the triangle inequality yields the desired estimate
Eq.

4.17
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,\tfrac{3}{2})
,
\xi\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}\cap\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
, and
v\in\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}
.
Then

\|(\xi\cdot\nabla)\mathcal{P}((\xi\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha-2}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}.

(4.19)

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
(i) to the standard product estimate

\|(\xi\cdot\nabla)v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha-1}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}},

yields the bound

\|\mathcal{P}((\xi\cdot\nabla)v)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha-1}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}.

(4.20)

Next, by complex interpolation between the bounds

\displaystyle\|(\xi\cdot\nabla)w\|_{\dot{H}^{0}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}},\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{H}^{1},

\displaystyle\|(\xi\cdot\nabla)w\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\mathbb{H}^{0}},\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{H}^{0},

we obtain for any
w\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha-1}
the estimate

\|(\xi\cdot\nabla)w\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha-2}}\leqslant c\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha-1}}.

(4.21)

Substituting
w=\mathcal{P}((\xi\cdot\nabla)v)
from (
4.20
) into (
4.21
) and using
the inequality
\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leqslant\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}
yields
the desired estimate
Eq.

4.19
.
∎

4.2
The auxiliary operator
\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}

A central challenge in the numerical analysis of the model problem (
1.1
) arises from the fact that the solution paths
generally lack even
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{3/2}
-regularity (see Remark
3.2
).
Consequently, expressions such as
\mathcal{P}_{h}Au
or
\langle Au,\mathcal{P}v_{h}\rangle
for
v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma,h}

are not well-defined. This regularity limitation precludes the direct application of techniques used in related works such as
[
9
]
.

To overcome this difficulty, we introduce the operator

\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}:=A_{h}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in(0,1).

(4.22)

This composition acts as a regularization mechanism: it first lifts a function from the continuous space
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-2\alpha}
via
A^{-\alpha}
,
projects the result onto the discrete space using
\mathcal{P}_{h}
, and then maps it back through the discrete fractional power
A_{h}^{\alpha}
.
The key properties of this operator are collected in the lemma below.

Lemma 4.3
.

The operator
\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}
satisfies the following estimates:

(i)

For all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\theta_{1}\in[-1,1]
, and
\theta_{2}\in[0,2]
,

\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}\leqslant ch^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{-\alpha}}{1+r^{\theta_{2}/2}}\min\!\left\{\frac{h^{(-2\alpha-\theta_{1})\wedge 0}}{1+r^{0\vee(-\alpha-\theta_{1}/2)}},\,\frac{h^{-2-2\alpha-\theta_{1}}}{1+r}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}r.

(4.23)

(ii)

For all
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle\|I-\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\beta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\beta},\quad\beta\in[0,1],

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|I-\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}\log(1+1/h).

(4.25)

(iii)

For all
\alpha\in[0,1]
,

\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}A-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}^{-2}_{\sigma,h})}\leqslant ch^{2-2\alpha}.

(4.26)

(iv)

For all
\alpha\in(1/4,1/2)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{1-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant ch^{2-2\alpha},

(4.27)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}.

(4.28)

Proof.

(i)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\theta_{1}\in[-1,1]
, and
\theta_{2}\in[0,2]
.
From the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}
in
Eq.

4.22
, we obtain the identity

\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}=A_{h}^{\alpha}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-\alpha}-A_{h}^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{h}\bigr).

Applying the Dunford–Taylor representation for fractional powers (cf. Section 7.1 from Chapter 2 of
[
34
]
) yields

\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\Gamma}\lambda^{-\alpha}A_{h}^{\alpha}\Bigl[\mathcal{P}_{h}(\lambda-A)^{-1}-(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\Gamma
denotes the standard contour consisting of the rays
\{re^{\pm\mathrm{i}\pi/4}:r\in[0,\infty)\}
,
oriented with the positive real axis on its left.
This representation immediately implies the operator norm bound

\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}\leqslant\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma}|\lambda|^{-\alpha}\bigl\|A_{h}^{\alpha}\bigl[\mathcal{P}_{h}(\lambda-A)^{-1}-(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\bigr]\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}\,|\mathrm{d}\lambda|.

To bound the integrand, we first observe the algebraic identity

A_{h}^{\alpha}\big[\mathcal{P}_{h}(\lambda-A)^{-1}-(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big]=(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}^{1+\alpha}\bigl(A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\bigr)A(\lambda-A)^{-1}.

Using the approximation estimate

\|A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}\leqslant ch^{2}\quad\text{(cf.~\eqref{eq:PhA-AhPh} with }\theta=0\text{)},

together with the resolvent bound

\|A(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})}\leqslant\frac{c}{1+|\lambda|^{\theta_{2}/2}},

we obtain

\bigl\|A_{h}^{\alpha}\big[\mathcal{P}_{h}(\lambda-A)^{-1}-(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big]\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}\leqslant\frac{ch^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{\theta_{2}/2}}\,\|(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}^{1+\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}.

By the inverse inequality (
4.4
) and the standard resolvent estimate

\|(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}^{s}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}\leqslant\frac{c}{1+|\lambda|^{1-s}},\qquad s\in[0,1],

we further deduce

\displaystyle\|(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}^{1+\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}

\displaystyle=\|(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}^{1+\alpha+\theta_{1}/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{0})}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\min\!\left\{\frac{h^{(-2\alpha-\theta_{1})\wedge 0}}{1+|\lambda|^{0\vee(-\alpha-\theta_{1}/2)}},\,\frac{h^{-2-2\alpha-\theta_{1}}}{1+|\lambda|}\right\}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\bigl\|A_{h}^{\alpha}\big[\mathcal{P}_{h}(\lambda-A)^{-1}-(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\big]\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\theta_{2}},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{\theta_{1}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\frac{ch^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{\theta_{2}/2}}\min\!\left\{\frac{h^{(-2\alpha-\theta_{1})\wedge 0}}{1+|\lambda|^{0\vee(-\alpha-\theta_{1}/2)}},\,\frac{h^{-2-2\alpha-\theta_{1}}}{1+|\lambda|}\right\}.

Substituting this bound into the Dunford–Taylor integral and parametrizing
\Gamma
via
\lambda=re^{\pm\mathrm{i}\pi/4}
yields the desired estimate
Eq.

4.23
.

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
and
\beta\in[0,1]
. Applying estimate (
4.23
) with
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(0,\beta)
and
(1,2-2\alpha)
,
and employing the norm equivalence (
4.3
), we obtain

\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\beta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\beta},\quad\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}\log(1+1/h).

Furthermore, the standard approximation properties of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
(cf. (
4.13
)) provide

\|I-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\beta},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\beta},\quad\|I-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}.

Finally, the desired estimates (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) follow directly from the triangle inequality,

\|I-\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\|\leqslant\|I-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|+\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\|
,
in conjunction with the four bounds established above.

(iii)

Fix
\alpha\in[0,1]
. From the definition (
4.22
), a direct computation yields the commutator identity

\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}A-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}=A_{h}^{\alpha+1}\bigl(A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\bigr)A^{1-\alpha}.

We estimate its operator norm from
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha}
to
\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}A-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-2})}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|A_{h}^{\alpha+1}(A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1})A^{1-\alpha}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-2})}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|(A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1})A^{1-\alpha}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{2\alpha})}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{-2\alpha}\bigl\|(A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1})A^{1-\alpha}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\mathbb{L}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{-2\alpha}\bigl\|A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\bigl\|A^{1-\alpha}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha},\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})}.

where the first inequality uses the inverse inequality (
4.4
).
Since
A^{1-\alpha}
is an isometry from
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{2-2\alpha}
to
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2}
, and
Eq.

4.14
with
\theta=0
gives
\|A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{2}
,
we obtain (
4.26
).

(iv)

Let
\alpha\in(\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{1}{2})
. To prove (
4.27
), we write

\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}=(\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h})\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}_{h}(\mathcal{P}-I).

By Lemma
2.1
(ii), the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
is bounded from
\dot{H}^{1-2\alpha}
to
\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1-2\alpha}
.
Hence, applying estimate (
4.23
) with
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(-1,1-2\alpha)
gives

\|(\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}-\mathcal{P}_{h})\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{1-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant ch^{2-2\alpha}.

On the other hand, Lemma
4.1
(i) implies

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}(\mathcal{P}-I)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{1-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant ch^{2-2\alpha}.

Combining these two bounds yields (
4.27
).

To establish (
4.28
), we decompose

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{h,\alpha}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}

\displaystyle=A_{h}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h}

\displaystyle=A_{h}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h,1-\alpha})A^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}+A_{h}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\bigr).

For the first term, observe that

\|A_{h}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{2\alpha-1},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}=1,\quad\|A^{-\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-2\alpha},\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2})}=1,

and
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{-2\alpha})
by Lemma
2.1
(iii). Therefore,

\|A_{h}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h,1-\alpha})A^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant c\,\|\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h,1-\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{2\alpha-1})}.

Applying (
4.23
) with
\alpha
replaced by
1-\alpha
,
\theta_{1}=2\alpha-1
, and
\theta_{2}=0

(noting that
2\alpha-1\in(-\tfrac{1}{2},0)
for
\alpha\in(\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{1}{2})
) gives

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h,1-\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{2},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{2\alpha-1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}.

Hence,

\|A_{h}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h,1-\alpha})A^{-\alpha}\mathcal{P}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}.

(4.29)

For the second term, note that

\|A_{h}(\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-1})}=\|\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}.

By the inverse inequality
\|v_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}\leqslant ch^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
(cf. (
4.4
)) and
the estimate
\|\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{2-2\alpha}
(cf. (
4.14
) with
\theta=2\alpha
),

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{-1}\|\mathcal{P}_{h}A^{-1}\mathcal{P}-A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{-2\alpha},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{1-2\alpha}.

Combining this bound with
(
4.29
)
and applying the triangle inequality gives the desired bound
(
4.28
)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Set

, where

Step 1. (Estimate of

By definition

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}_{h}Au-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h}u,\,e_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{2}}\|\mathcal{J}_{h}Au-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h}u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-2}}\quad\text{(duality)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{-1}\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}\|\mathcal{J}_{h}Au-A_{h}\mathcal{J}_{h}u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{-2}}\quad\text{(inverse inequality \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:inverse})}

An application of Young’s inequality to the cross term then yields the desired bound

4.36
.

Step 2. (Estimate of
I_{2}
).
We establish the following bound for

I_{2}\leqslant\frac{3}{8}\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+ch^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}.

From the definition

4.32
, we decompose

I_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(L_{\zeta_{n},h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})L_{\zeta_{n}}u,\,e_{h}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}\big(L_{\zeta_{n},h}u_{h}-L_{\zeta_{n}}u\big),\,e_{h}\right\rangle.

Using integration by parts and the fact that each

(4.38)

where

\displaystyle I_{2}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(L_{\zeta_{n},h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})L_{\zeta_{n}}u,\,e_{h}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle I_{2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(L_{\zeta_{n}}-L_{\zeta_{n},h})u,\,(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)e_{h}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle I_{2}^{(3)}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}(I-\mathcal{J}_{h})u,\,(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)e_{h}\right\rangle.

,
4.40

,

(4.39)

(4.40)

Part (c): Estimate of

We estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}[(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)(I-\mathcal{J}_{h})u],\,(\zeta_{n}\cdot\nabla)e_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+ch^{2\varrho}(1+\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2})\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+ch^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}.

in

4.33
, the definition of

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\langle(\mathcal{J}_{h}\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}_{h})((u\cdot\nabla)u),\,e_{h}\rangle}_{I_{3}^{(1)}}

4.28

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{16}\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}+c\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\Big)\|e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

4.24

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{16}\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}+c\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\Big)\|e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

Step 4. (Estimate of
I_{4}
).

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

(4.44)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}(\mathcal{J}_{h}-I)u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|(L_{\zeta_{n},h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

4.25

which, by the condition

(4.46)

Substituting (

) and (
4.46

I_{4}\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{ch^{2\varrho}}{\epsilon}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}.

Condition

also provides control over the term involving

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leqslant c\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}.

Inserting this into the previous inequality yields

I_{4}\leqslant c\epsilon\|e_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{ch^{2\varrho}}{\epsilon}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}.

The desired bound

\epsilon>0
sufficiently small.

Step 5. (Estimate of the Martingale
M
).
We establish the following bound for the martingale term
M
:

\mathbb{E}\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\leqslant\epsilon\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}^{2}+\frac{ch^{2\varrho}}{\epsilon}(\log(1+1/h))^{2},\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

(4.47)

Since each
\zeta_{n}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}
is divergence-free (cf.
3.1
), integration by parts yields

\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}e_{h},\,e_{h}\rangle=0,\quad\forall n\geqslant 1,

which implies

\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}u_{h},\,e_{h}\rangle=\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}\mathcal{J}_{h}u,\,e_{h}\rangle,\quad\forall n\geqslant 1.

Consequently, the integrand in the definition of
M
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}u_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}}u,\,e_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(L_{\zeta_{n},h}\mathcal{J}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})u,\,e_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(L_{\zeta_{n},h}\mathcal{J}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\|e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Squaring both sides and summing over
n
, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}u_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}}u,\,e_{h}\big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|(L_{\zeta_{n},h}\mathcal{J}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}})u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}\|e_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

where the last inequality uses estimates from
Eq.

4.45
and
Eq.

4.46
.
Applying the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|M(t)\|_{C[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big\langle L_{\zeta_{n},h}u_{h}(t)-\mathcal{J}_{h}L_{\zeta_{n}}u(t),\,e_{h}(t)\big\rangle^{2}\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h)\mathbb{E}\left[\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1})}\|e_{h}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}\right].

Finally, applying Young’s inequality with an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and using the regularity property (
3.12
) for
u

yields the desired bound (
4.47
).

Step 6. (Conclusion).
From
Eq.

4.13
,
Eq.

4.24
with
\alpha=(1-\varrho)/2
and
\beta=1
,
and the assumption
u_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{1/2})
,
we have the initial error bound:

\|(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathcal{J}_{h})u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant ch^{1}.

Substituting this bound, together with the estimates for
I_{1},\dots,I_{4}
from
Eqs.

4.36
,
4.37
,
4.42
and
4.43
, into the identity
Eq.

4.30
, we deduce that,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|e_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle ch^{2\varrho}+ch^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}+\big(1+\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad{}+c\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\right)\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\|M\|_{C[0,T]}.

Define
z(t):=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
. Then,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle z(t)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle ch^{2\varrho}+ch^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}+\big(1+\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad{}+c\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\right)z(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\|M\|_{C[0,T]}.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality yields,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle z(T)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\exp\left(c\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}+\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|u_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\,\times\bigg[h^{2\varrho}+h^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1}}^{2}+\big(1+\|u(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho}}^{2}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\exp\left(c\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1})}^{2}+c\|u_{h}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\,\times\bigg[h^{2\varrho}+h^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1})}^{2}+\big(1+\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\big)\|u\|_{C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\bigg].

By the regularity of
u
in
Eq.

3.10
and the stability of
u_{h}
in
Theorem

4.1
, it follows that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle z(T)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\|e_{h}(s)\|_{\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\left[h^{2\varrho}+h^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho+1})}^{2}+\|u\|_{C([0,T];\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{\varrho})}^{2}\right)+\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\right].

Taking expectations and using the regularity results in
Eqs.

3.12
and
3.11
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\,z(T)+\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant c\left(h^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\right).

Noting that
z(T)=\|e_{h}\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}
, this inequality is equivalent to

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}^{2}+\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant c\left(h^{2\varrho}(\log(1+1/h))^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|M\|_{C[0,T]}\right).

Substituting the bound for
\mathbb{E}\|M\|_{C[0,T]}
from
Eq.

4.47
and absorbing the resulting stochastic term into the left-hand side, then taking square roots, yields the error estimate

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}_{\sigma,h}^{1})}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h).

(4.48)

To bound the total error
u-u_{h}
, we decompose it as
u-u_{h}=(u-\mathcal{J}_{h}u)-e_{h}
. Applying the triangle inequality and using (
4.48
) yields

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|\nabla(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{L}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\|u-\mathcal{J}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|\nabla(u-\mathcal{J}_{h}u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{L}^{2})}+ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h).

Furthermore, invoking
Eqs.

4.24
and
4.25
together with the regularity results (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) gives

\|u-\mathcal{J}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\|\nabla(u-\mathcal{J}_{h}u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h).

Combining the last two inequalities yields the desired estimate (
4.12
), which completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

\blacksquare

5
Conclusions

This paper has provided a rigorous numerical analysis of the two-dimensional incompressible stochastic Navier–Stokes
equations with transport noise under no-slip boundary conditions. We first investigated the regularity of the weak
solution and then established a mean-square strong convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(h^{\varrho}\log(1+1/h))
for both the velocity
and its gradient, where
0<\varrho<1/2
, for a spatial semidiscretization based on the MINI finite element method.

To the best of our knowledge, this work constitutes the first rigorous convergence analysis for finite element spatial semidiscretizations of
the two-dimensional stochastic Navier–Stokes equations with transport noise.
The central innovation of our approach is a novel technique that overcomes the lack of
D(A)
-regularity—a longstanding obstacle in the numerical analysis of
such problems under no-slip boundary conditions.

Our analysis relies on a smallness condition on the transport noise vector fields:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}
is sufficiently small.
Removing this restriction remains a fundamental open question in the numerical analysis of stochastic fluid models. Key challenges for future work include extending these results to fully discrete schemes and developing efficient algorithms to mitigate the substantial computational cost of simulations.

Stratification in equivariant Kasparov theory Thanks: The authors were supported by the Labex CEMPI (ANR-11-LABX-0007-01)
Ivo Dell’Ambrogio, Rubén Martos

Abstract
. We study stratification, that is the classification of localizing tensor ideal subcategories by geometric means, in the context of Kasparov’s equivariant KK-theory of C*-algebras. We introduce a straightforward countable analog of the notion of stratification by Balmer–Favi supports and conjecture that it holds for the equivariant bootstrap subcategory of every finite group G G . We prove this conjecture for groups whose nontrivial elements all have prime order, and we verify it rationally for arbitrary finite groups. In all these cases we also compute the Balmer spectrum of compact objects. In our proofs we use larger versions of the equivariant Kasparov categories which admit not only countable coproducts but all small ones; they are constructed in an Appendix using ∞ \infty -categorical enhancements and adapting ideas of Bunke–Engel–Land.

1.
Introduction

Kasparov theory and tt-geometry

Tensor triangular geometry (
tt-geometry
for short), started by Balmer
[
Bal05
]
, is based on the idea of exploiting the ambient geometry of any given essentially small tensor triangulated category (
tt-category
)
\mathscr{K}
in order to provide a classification of its
thick tensor ideal subcategories
, and therefore a rough classification of its objects.
To this end, Balmer introduces a topological space
\text{Spc}(\mathscr{K})
, the
spectrum of
\mathscr{K}
, and a universal notion of supports
\supp(A)\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
for the objects
A
of
\mathscr{K}
.
This theory illuminates and unifies several classification problems in algebraic geometry, topology, representation theory, and beyond.
In each example of
\mathscr{K}
, the classification proposed by Balmer (which always works abstractly) becomes especially useful if one manages to find a description of the space
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
and of the support theory
A\mapsto\supp(A)
which is relevant to the example at hand.
We refer to
[
Bal20
]
for a rich survey of concrete computations of spectra throughout mathematics.

The application of tt-geometry to noncommutative geometry was initiated by the first author
[
Del10
]
. The starting point is the observation, due to Meyer and Nest
[
MN06
]
, that the
G
-equivariant Kasparov category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
of any (second countable) locally compact group
G
is an essentially small tt-category equipped with arbitrary countable coproducts.
Meyer and Nest used the tt-structure in order to reformulate the Baum-Connes conjecture and establish various stability properties for it.
In
[
Del10
]
, it was pointed out that sufficient knowledge of the
spectrum
of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
already implies the conjecture.
Indeed, the Baum-Connes conjecture holds for
G
as soon as
\Spc(\mathsf{KK}^{G})
is covered (via the natural maps) by the spaces
\Spc(\mathsf{KK}^{H})
with
H
running through the compact subgroups of
G
. This result indicates the interest of computing Balmer spectra of equivariant Kasparov categories.

However, there are serious obstacles to studying
\Spc(\mathsf{KK}^{G})
concretely, because as a tt-category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
is rather unwieldy and falls outside the reach of the usual techniques.
To start with,
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
is not a rigid tensor category,
i.e.
its objects do not admit tensor duals in general.
Moreover, it appears not to admit any reasonable generating set, in any useful sense; it seems to have far too many complicated objects.
Both problems already occur when
G
is the trivial group!

We clearly need to do better.
And we can: in a companion paper
[
DM
]
we explain how to derive the Baum-Connes conjecture from a different covering criterion, where the Balmer spectrum is replaced by a countable variant and where
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
and
\mathsf{KK}^{H}
are replaced by the decidedly more tractable subcategories
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
and
\mathsf{Cell}(H)
of
cell algebras
introduced in
[
Del14
]
.
For any group
G
, by definition
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is the localizing (‘bootstrap’) subcategory of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
generated by the orbit algebras
\mathrm{C}_{0}(G/H)
of all subgroups
H\leq G
; it is again a tt-category at least when
G
is discrete or compact.
The computation of the above-mentioned countable version of the spectrum is closely related to the problem of
stratification
for
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
, which is the focus of the present paper.
Our present goal is to establish that, for cell algebras, stratification can be established at least in some interesting cases.

Stratification

Generally speaking, stratification refers to a set of techniques for classifying
localizing
tensor ideals in a large tt-category,
i.e.
those thick ideals which are also closed under the formation of infinite coproducts.
A typical situation for applying tt-geometry is when the essentially small tt-category
\mathscr{K}
occurs as the subcategory
\mathscr{T}^{c}
of ‘small’ objects inside a large tt-category
\mathscr{T}
which admits arbitrary small coproducts and is itself generated (as a localizing subcategory) by
\mathscr{K}
; the best case is when two notions of smallness, compactness and rigidity, agree in
\mathscr{T}
, in which case one says that
\mathscr{T}
is
rigidly-compactly generated
.
In this situation, tensor-triangular stratification is now fairly well understood thanks to work of Barthel, Heard and Sanders
[
BHS23
]
(building on Neeman
[
Nee92a
]
, Palmieri–Hovey–Strickland
[
HPS97
]
, Benson–Iyengar–Krause
[
BIK08
]
and others), at least under the mild hypothesis that the spectrum
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
of the rigid-compact objects is known to be a
weakly noetherian
space (
e.g.
it is
noetherian
: every open subset is quasi-compact).
In this case, such a big tt-category
\mathscr{T}
is said to be
stratified
if there is a certain canonical inclusion-preserving bijection (induced by an extension of support theory to big objects due to Balmer–Favi
[
BF11
]
)

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{localizing tensor ideals of }\mathscr{T}\end{array}\right\}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{subsets of }\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})\end{array}\right\}

between the localizing tensor ideals of
\mathscr{T}
and arbitrary subsets of the Balmer spectrum of the tt-subcategory
\mathscr{T}^{c}
of the compact-rigid objects in
\mathscr{T}
.

Results for finite groups

We want to establish stratification for the category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
, but we cannot directly apply the Barthel–Heard–Sanders (BHS) theory.
Firstly,
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
only admits countable coproducts, not arbitrary small ones.
Secondly, rigid and compact objects in
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
do not always agree; they do agree when
G
is finite, but not
e.g.
when
G
is infinite discrete.
For
G
finite however,
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is known to be
rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated
, which is a useful replacement of the usual notion for tt-categories with only countable (=
\aleph_{1}
-small) coproducts.
(For
G
finite moreover,
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
coincides with the a priori larger
equivariant bootstrap category
of
[
DEM14
]
, a fact which makes stratification in itself more interesting.)

For such categories
\mathscr{T}
, one can easily define a countable version of the Balmer–Favi support theory (see
Section

4.1
), and we say that
\mathscr{T}
is
countably stratified
if the analogue of the bijection (
1.1
) holds, where of course now we read ‘localizing’ as meaning ‘triangulated and closed under countable coproducts’.

We can now formulate our main result,
where
\mathrm{R}(G)
denotes the complex representation ring of
G
:

1.2 Theorem

(see
Theorem

6.4
)
.

If
G
is a finite group in which every nontrivial element has prime order, the category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
of
G
-cell algebras is countably stratified.
In particular, there is a canonical bijection

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{localizing subcategories}\\
\textrm{of }\mathsf{Cell}(G)\end{array}\right\}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{subsets of the Zariski spectrum}\\
\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G))\end{array}\right\}

sending a localizing subcategory
\mathscr{L}
to its countable Balmer–Favi support
\supp(\mathscr{L})
.

The above hypothesis on
G
is certainly very restrictive but it still applies to many groups, such as
S_{3}
,
A_{5}
and all
p
-groups of exponent
p
(see
Remark

6.1
).

In this context
\mathrm{R}(G)
occurs as the endomorphism ring of the trivial
G
-C*-algebra
\mathbb{C}=\mathrm{C}_{0}(G/G)
, which is the tensor unit of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
.
The above form of the bijection is a consequence of the following computation of the Balmer spectrum:

1.3 Theorem

(see
Theorem

6.6
)
.

For finite groups
G
with only prime-order nontrivial elements,
we have a canonical homeomorphism

\Spc(\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c})\cong\Spec(\End(\mathbbm{1}))=\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G)).

We do not know how
\Spc(\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c})
looks in general, in fact this is a major obstacle to establishing stratification for more general groups.
However, the above homeomorphism is provided by the canonical continuous ‘comparison’ map

\rho\colon\Spc(\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c})\longrightarrow\Spec(\End(\mathbbm{1}))=\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G))

from the tt-spectrum to the Zariski spectrum, which always exists and which moreover is surjective for all finite (or even compact) groups
G
; see
[
Bal10a
]
.
Accordingly, we make the following conjectures:

1.4 Conjecture
.

Balmer’s comparison map
\rho
is a homeomorphism
\Spc(\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c})\cong\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G))
for every finite group
G
.

1.5 Conjecture
.

\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is countably stratified for every finite group
G
.

Note that
\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G))
is a noetherian space, since
\mathrm{R}(G)
is a noetherian commutative ring.
For this and other reasons, establishing
Conjecture

1.4
would be a good start towards proving
Conjecture

1.5
(although not formally necessary).

1.6 Remark
.

When
G=1
is the trivial group,
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is the classical Rosenberg–Schochet bootstrap category, for which both conjectures are already known to be true.
This is essentially the content of
[
Del11
]
(see also the proof of
Proposition

6.12
).
The crucial case of
Theorem

1.3
when
G
is cyclic of prime order was proved in
[
DM21
]
, and the more general case in our theorem will be derived from the latter thanks to a strong result of Arano–Kubota
[
AK18
]
(see
Theorem

3.5
).

Beyond the above special class of groups, we can verify both conjectures
rationally
for arbitrary finite
G
.
Let
\mathrm{R}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the rationalization of the representation ring, and
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
the rationalization of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
(
Terminology

5.1
).

1.7 Theorem

(see
Theorem

5.6
and
Corollary

5.5
)
.

For every finite group
G
,

\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is countably stratified and
the comparison map
\rho
is a homeomorphism
\Spc(\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{c})\cong\Spec(\mathrm{R}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
The latter space is discrete and finite, and its points are in bijection with the conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
.

This is a relatively straightforward consequence of a result proved in
[
BDM24
]
, according to which
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
splits as a finite product of graded module categories over certain rational skew group algebras.
In particular
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is equivalent to an explicitly described semisimple abelian tensor category so its tt-geometry becomes rather straightforward to work out.
(In view of the closely related results of
[
MN24
]
, we believe the analog of
Theorem

1.7
should be true already after inverting
|G|
instead of rationalizing; but we have not verified all details.)

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is considerably more difficult.

Adjoining arbitrary small coproducts

We had a choice of how to proceed, indeed of how to approach the problem of establishing stratification of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
for general finite groups.
On the one hand, we could have developed a countable version of the powerful stratification theory of Barthel–Heard–Sanders so it applies to rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated tt-categories such as
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
.
This would have been straightforward but exceedingly long and ultimately not so interesting, with the added problem that
some
techniques and results would necessarily fail (because of the lack of a countable version of Brown representability for covariant homological functors).
On the other hand we could try to embed
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
into a larger tt-category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}
which admits
all
set-indexed coproducts and is genuinely rigidly-compactly generated, in the usual sense, and which therefore is directly amenable to the Barthel–Heard–Sanders techniques.

We have chosen the second approach, because it is more efficient and also because the tt-category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}
is of independent interest.
In
Appendix

A
, we construct
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}
by closely adapting a recent construction of Bunke–Engel–Land
[
BEL23
]
which make heavy use of Lurie’s
\infty
-categories.
To wit, we use general
\infty
-categorical techniques to define a certain presentably symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category
\mathbf{Cell}(G)
and set
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}:=\Ho\mathbf{Cell}(G)
to be its homotopy category. (For future reference, we actually construct the analog enlargement of the whole
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
and do so for general countable groups.)
For this enlargement to be useful to us, we must take care to extend the usual functorial properties of KK-theory to the larger category, and we must also ensure that the embedding
\mathsf{Cell}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}
preserves all the tt-structure; in particular, it should preserve the countable coproducts of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
and it should identify the two tt-subcategories of compact-rigid objects.

Once all this is done, we can prove that
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathrm{big}}
is stratified (by the usual Balmer–Favi support theory) as planned, by applying a results from
[
BHS23
]
and its sequels; see
Theorem

6.4
.
Finally, by playing with the embedding we deduce from this the wished countable stratification of the original tt-subcategory
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
.
This last deduction is not entirely trivial and requires some care, but the argument (which requires results form the theory of Neeman’s well-generated triangulated categories) is very general and may be of independent interest; we dedicate
Appendix

B
to it.

1.8 Remark
.

Results similar to ours have been recently established in topology.
To wit, the analogues of Conjectures
1.4
and
1.5
have been proved (for all finite
G
) in
[
BCH+23
, Lem. 8.11 and Thm. 8.8]
for the module category
\Mod_{KU_{G}}(\mathsf{Sp}^{G})
; here
KU_{G}
denotes
G
-equivariant topological complex K-theory seen as a commutative algebra in the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Sp}^{G}
of genuine
G
-spectra. This module category (or rather its homotopy category) has very similar properties to
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
.
In conversation with Tobias Barthel, Markus Hausmann and Maxime Ramzi, we were made aware of the possibility that our tt-
\infty
-category
\mathbf{Cell}(G)
and
\Mod_{KU_{G}}(\mathsf{Sp}^{G})
could actually be equivalent.
This would immediately imply both of our conjectures and would be very interesting in itself, by providing a robust bridge between equivariant topology and equivariant KK-theory.

2.
Tensor triangular preliminaries

We briefly review some basics on tensor triangulated categories and their geometry, referring to
[
Bal10b
]
for more background and plenty of examples, and to the further references given along the way for more details.
Our goal is simply to establish the language and collect results for ease of reference, hence the more tt-experienced readers may want to skip this section.

2.1.
Tensor triangulated categories

A
tensor triangulated category
, or just
tt-category
,
\mathscr{K}
is a triangulated category in the sense of Verdier (see
[
Nee01
]
) equipped with a
tensor product
, that is a symmetric monoidal structure which is exact in both variables. We write
\Sigma\colon\mathscr{K}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{K}
for the suspension functor of the triangulation,
\otimes\colon\mathscr{K}\times\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}
for the tensor functor, and
\mathbbm{1}
or
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{K}}
for its tensor unit object.
The tt-category
\mathscr{K}
is
essentially small
if it is so as a category (it is equivalent to one with only a set of objects and morphisms).
It is
rigid
if it is so as a symmetric monoidal category, meaning that every object
A\in\mathscr{K}
is
rigid
(or
dualizable
): there exists a
B\in\mathscr{K}
(the
tensor dual
of
A
) together with maps
\mathbbm{1}\to A\otimes B
and
B\otimes A\to\mathbbm{1}
satisfying the zig-zag equations which, in particular, imply that the endofunctors
A\otimes-
and
B\otimes-
of
\mathscr{K}
are adjoint to one another on both sides.

A
tensor triangulated functor
, or
tt-functor
, is a functor
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
between two tt-categories which is exact and symmetric monoidal.
A
tensor ideal
(or
\otimes
-ideal)
\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
is a triangulated subcategory (all our triangulated subcategories will be full) such that
A\otimes B\in\mathscr{J}
whenever
A\in\mathscr{J}
and
B\in\mathscr{K}
. A tensor ideal is
thick
if it is so as a triangulated subcategory, that is if it is closed under retracts.
The full kernel on objects
\ker(F)=\{A\in\mathscr{K}\mid F(A)\cong 0\}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
of any tt-functor
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
is a thick tensor ideal of
\mathscr{K}
.
Conversely, the Verdier quotient
\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{J}
by a (thick) tensor ideal inherits a unique structure of tt-category making the canonical functor
\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{J}
a tt-functor.

2.1 Notation
.

We write
\thick\langle\,\mathscr{E}\,\rangle
and
\thick_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\mathscr{E}\,\rangle
for the thick subcategory, respectively the thick tensor ideal, of
\mathscr{K}
generated by a family of objects
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
.

2.2.
The Balmer spectrum

For any essentially small tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, its
(triangular or Balmer) spectrum
is the topological space whose underlying set is

\Spc(\mathscr{K}):=\{\mathscr{P}\subsetneq\mathscr{K}\mid\mathscr{P}\textrm{ is a prime thick tensor ideal }\}

(where
prime
means
A\otimes B\in\mathscr{P}\Rightarrow A,B\in\mathscr{P}
) and whose topology is generated by the following basis of closed subsets:

\supp(A):=\{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{P})\mid A\not\in\mathscr{P}\}\quad(A\in\mathscr{K}).

The set
\supp(A)
is called the
support of
A
; it has quasi-compact open complement.
The assignment
A\mapsto\supp(A)
satisfies a list of basic compatibilities with the operations in
\mathscr{K}
,
i.e.
defines a
support data
on
\mathscr{K}
, and the pair
(\Spc(\mathscr{K}),\supp)
is universal (the finest) among such support data.

A subset
S\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is
Thomason
if it is a union of closed subsets with quasi-compact open complements.
In case
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is
noetherian
(
i.e.
every open subset is quasi-compact), Thomason subsets are the same as those subsets
S
which are
specialization closed
(
i.e.
if
\mathscr{Q}\in\overline{\{\mathscr{P}\}}
and
\mathscr{P}\in S
then
\mathscr{Q}\in S
.)

The following classification result is a major early milestone of tt-geometry:

2.2 Theorem

(
[
Bal05
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{K}
is rigid (just for simplicity). Then there is a canonical bijection

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{thick tensor ideals of }\mathscr{K}\end{array}\right\}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{Thomason subsets of }\Spc(\mathscr{K})\end{array}\right\}

which maps a thick ideal
\mathscr{J}
to its support
\supp(\mathscr{J}):=\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{J}}\supp(A)
, and
whose inverse sends a Thomason subset
S
to the thick tensor ideal

\mathscr{K}_{S}:=\{A\in\mathscr{K}\mid\supp(A)\subseteq S\}

of all objects supported on
S
.

2.3.
Functoriality of the spectrum

As explained in
[
Bal05
]
,
every tt-functor
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
of essentially small tt-categories induces a continuous map on spectra
\Spc(F)\colon\Spc(\mathscr{L})\to\Spc(\mathscr{K})
by
\Spc(F)(\mathscr{P})=F^{-1}(\mathscr{P})
, and the procedure is functorial:
\Spc(F_{1}\circ F_{2})=\Spc(F_{2})\circ\Spc(F_{1})
and
\Spc(\Id_{\mathscr{K}})=\Id_{\Spc(\mathscr{K})}
.

If
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
is fully faithful and
cofinal
(
i.e.
every
A\in\mathscr{L}
is a retract of
F(B)
for some
B\in\mathscr{K}
), the induced map
\Spc(F)
is a homeomorphism; this applies in particular to the
idempotent completion

\mathscr{K}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{K}^{\natural}
.
Thus we may assume that our tt-categories are idempotent complete without changing their spectrum.

If
q\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{J}
is a Verdier quotient by a tensor ideal, the induced map
\Spc(q)
restricts to a homeomorphism onto its image:
\Spc(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{L})\overset{\sim}{\to}\{\mathscr{P}\mid\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{P}\}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
.

A tt-category
\mathscr{K}
is
local
if its spectrum has a unique closed point (one admitting no specializations:
\overline{\{\mathscr{P}\}}=\{\mathscr{P}\}
).
If
\mathscr{K}
is rigid, this point must be the zero tensor ideal
\{0\}
.
The
local category
of
\mathscr{K}
at a point
\mathscr{P}
is defined to be
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{P}}:=(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{P})^{\natural}
; it is an idempotent complete local tt-category, equpipped with a canonical tt-functor
q_{\mathscr{P}}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{P}}
, the quotient by
\mathscr{P}
followed by idempotent completion.

Note that
q_{\mathscr{P}}(A)\not\cong 0\Leftrightarrow\mathscr{P}\in\supp(A)
, which justifies the name ‘support’: a point
\mathscr{P}
lies in the support of
A
precisely if
A
survives in the local category at
\mathscr{P}
.

2.4.
The comparison map

Balmer
[
Bal10a
]
introduces for every essentially small tt-category
\mathscr{K}
a continuous map

\rho=\rho_{\mathscr{K}}\colon\Spc(\mathscr{K})\to\Spec(\End_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1})),\quad\rho(\mathscr{P})=\{f\colon\mathbbm{1}\to\mathbbm{1}\mid\cone(f)\not\in\mathscr{P}\}

between the triangular spectrum and the Zariski spectrum of the (automatically commutative) endomorphism ring of the tensor unit
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathscr{K}
.
This map is natural, in the sense that every tt-functor
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
gives rise to a commutative square

\textstyle{\Spc(\mathscr{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Spc(F)}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{\mathscr{L}}}
\textstyle{\Spc(\mathscr{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{\mathscr{K}}}
\textstyle{\Spec(\End_{\mathscr{L}}(\mathbbm{1}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Spec(F)}
\textstyle{\Spec(\End_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1}))}

of topological spaces, where the bottom map is induced by the ring morphism
F\colon\End_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1})\to\End_{\mathscr{L}}(\mathbbm{1})
obtained simply by restricting the functor
F
.

As for any triangulated category, the suspension functor
\Sigma
turns
\mathscr{K}
into a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded category with graded Hom-spaces
\Hom^{*}_{\mathscr{K}}(A,B):=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(A,\Sigma^{*}B)
.
The tensor product turns the latter into graded (left and right) modules over
\End^{*}_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1})=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1},\Sigma^{*}\mathbbm{1})
, which is a graded commutative ring.
(Strictly speaking, in general here ‘graded commutative’ means
fg=\epsilon^{\deg(f)\deg(g)}gf
on homogeneous elements, where
\epsilon\in\End(\mathbbm{1})
denotes the invertible element whose action on
\Sigma(\mathbbm{1})\otimes\Sigma(\mathbbm{1})\overset{\sim}{\to}\Sigma(\mathbbm{1})\otimes\Sigma(\mathbbm{1})
is the symmetry coming with the tensor structure.
Note however that we have
\epsilon=-1
whenever the tt-category has a model or enhancement of any kind, as well as under the hypothesis in
Definition

2.6
(b).)
There is a graded version

\rho^{*}_{\mathscr{K}}\colon\Spc(\mathscr{K})\to\Spec(\End^{*}_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1}))

of the comparison map, with similar formal properties, where on the right-hand side we now have the Zariski spectrum of homogeneous prime ideals.

Both comparison maps are surjective as soon as
\End^{*}_{\mathscr{K}}(\mathbbm{1})
is noetherian (or just coherent) in the graded sense (
[
Bal10a
, Thm. 7.3, Cor. 7.4]
).
Injectivity holds less often and when it does it is typically much harder to prove, as we shall experience.

2.5.
tt-rings

Given a tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, one can consider monoids (a.k.a. algebras or rings) and modules over them, just as in any tensor category.
Recall that a
monoid
is an object
A
equipped with a ‘multiplication’
m\colon A\otimes A\to A
and ‘unit’
u\colon\mathbbm{1}\to A
morphisms satisfying the usual associativity and unitality conditions via diagrams commuting in
\mathscr{K}
, and similarly for modules (by which, unless otherwise stated, we always mean left modules).
Modules over
A
and their morphisms form a category
\Mod_{\mathscr{K}}(A)
, the
Eilenbert–Moore category
of
A
.
In the context of tt-geometry, this category is particularly useful for monoids of the following kind (because then, among other reasons, it essentially inherits a structure of tt-category):

2.3 Definition

(
[
Bal14
]
)
.

A
tt-ring
in
\mathscr{K}
is a monoid
(A,m,u)
which is
commutative
,
i.e.
it satisfying
m\circ\sigma=m
where
\sigma\colon A\otimes A\overset{\sim}{\to}A\otimes A
is the symmetry of the tensor structure, and
separable
, in the sense
1
1

1

This sense of ‘separable’ has strictly nothing to do with the point-set topological meaning of the word used in the context of C*-algebras (in the sense of admitting a countable dense subset).
that its multiplication
m
admits a section
s\colon A\to A\otimes A
which is a morphism of both left and right
A
-modules.

Balmer
[
Bal14
]
introduces a notion of
degree

\deg(A)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
for tt-rings
A
in an idempotent-complete tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, which we use as a black box and will not need to define.
There exist tt-rings of infinite degree (
[
Góm23
]
), and having finite degree is a necessary hypothesis for applying certain results.
Later we will need the next fact:

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be tt-rings in an (idempotent complete) tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, all of which have finite degree.
Then
\deg(A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{n})\leq\deg(A_{1})+\ldots+\deg(A_{n})
.

Proof.

We prove the case
n=2
and the general one follows by an easy induction.
Write
A:=A_{1}\times A_{2}
, and consider the tt-functor
q_{\mathscr{K}}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{P}\subseteq(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{P})^{\natural}
to the local category at a point
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})
(
Section

2.4
).
By
[
Bal14
, Thm. 3.7(a)]
the degree cannot increase along tt-functors, so we have
\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{i}))\leq\deg(A_{i})
.
In particular the
q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{i})
all have finite degree, and since the tt-category
(\mathscr{K}/\mathscr{P})^{\natural}
is local, by
[
Bal14
, Cor. 3.12]
we have an equality

\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A))=\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{1})\times q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{2}))=\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}A_{1})+\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}A_{2})<+\infty.

In particular
q_{\mathscr{P}}(A)
has finite degree for all
\mathscr{P}
, hence by
[
Bal14
, Thm. 3.8]
we have

\deg(A)=\max_{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})}\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A))

and therefore by combining the two

\displaystyle\deg(A)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})}\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{1}))+\deg(q_{\mathscr{P}}(A_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{K})}\deg(A_{1})+\deg(A_{2})=\deg(A_{1})+\deg(A_{2})<+\infty

as claimed.
∎

2.6.
Big tt-categories

As pointed out in the Introduction, many essentially small tt-categories
\mathscr{K}
occur in nature as subcategories of compact and/or rigid objects in ‘big’ tt-categories.
In the following, suppose that
\mathscr{T}
is a tt-category admitting arbitrary small coproducts (all those indexed by some small set, not a proper class).

A triangulated subcategory
\mathscr{L}\subseteq\mathscr{T}
is
localizing
if it is closed under the formation of small coproducts.
Localizing subcategories are automatically thick (in fact, more generally, any triangulated category admitting arbitrary countable coproducts is idempotent complete by
[
Nee01
, Prop. 1.6.8]
).
A
localizing tensor ideal
is a localizing subcategory which is also a tensor ideal.

2.5 Notation
.

We write
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{E}\,\rangle
and
\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\mathscr{E}\,\rangle
for the localising subcategory, respectively the localising tensor ideal, of
\mathscr{T}
generated by a family of objects
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{T}
.

An object
C\in\mathscr{T}
is
compact
if the functor
\mathscr{T}(C,-)\colon\mathscr{T}\to\Ab
to abelian groups preserves coproducts.
We write
\mathscr{T}^{c}
for the (full triangulated) subcategory of compact objects of
\mathscr{T}
.
The triangulated category
\mathscr{T}
is said to be
compactly generated
if there exists a small set
\mathscr{G}\subseteq\mathscr{T}^{c}
of compacts such that
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{G}\,\rangle=\mathscr{T}
;
by results of Neeman
[
Nee96
]
, the condition that
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{G}\,\rangle=\mathscr{T}
is equivalent to the (a priori weaker) requirement that for all
A\in\mathscr{T}
, if
\mathscr{T}^{*}(C,A)\cong 0
for all
C\in\mathscr{G}
then
A\cong 0
.
If
\mathscr{T}
is compactly generated then
\mathscr{T}^{c}
is essentially small, and of course
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{T}^{c}\,\rangle=\mathscr{T}
.

2.6 Definition
.

A tt-category
\mathscr{T}
with small coproducts is
rigidly-compactly generated
, or is a
big tt-category
for short, if it satisfies the following:

(a)

\mathscr{T}
is compactly generated.

(b)

The tensor structure and triangulation satisfy the compatibility conditions in
[
HPS97
, App. A]
; in particular,
\otimes
preserves coproducts in both variables.

(c)

The rigid and compact objects of
\mathscr{T}
coincide (in particular
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathscr{T}^{c}
).

Since rigid objects always form a tensor subcategory and compact objects a triangulated subcategory, it follows that
\mathscr{T}^{c}
is a tt-subcategory of
\mathscr{T}
.

Big tt-categories in the above sense have been intensely studied in recent years, in particular in relation to stratification as explained in
Section

4
.

2.7.

A version of big tt-categories which only admit

Suppose now that
\mathscr{T}
is a tt-category admitting all countable coproducts.

3.
Tensor triangulated categories of equivariant KK-theory

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group.
It was first proved, and exploited, by Meyer–Nest
[
MN06
]
that Kasparov’s
[
Kas88
]

G
-equivariant bivariant K-theory (or ‘KK-theory’) forms a tensor triangulated category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
.
In this section we recall a few basic features of equivariant KK-theory seen as a tt-category, referring to
[
MN06
]

[
Mey08
]

[
Bla98
]
for details.
We also record a few more recent results, including possibly new observations (
Lemma

3.6
,
Proposition

3.8
).

3.1.
Basic tt-structure

The objects
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
are separable complex
G
-C*-algebras, its (graded) Hom groups are Kasparov’s KK-theory groups, and its composition and tensor product encode the so-called Kasparov product. There is a canonical functor
\mathsf{C}^{*}\mathsf{sep}^{G}\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}
from the category of separable
G
-C*-algebras and
G
-equivariant *-homomorphisms which is the identity on objects and satisfies a universal property (the latter will be exploited in
Appendix

A
), from which it follows that the category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
is essentially small.
Countable (
c_{0}
-)direct sums of separable
G
-C*-algebras are again separable
G
-C*-algebras (with coordinatewise
G
-action) and provide countable coproducts in the category
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
.

The suspension functor of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
is given by
\Sigma\colon A\mapsto\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R},A)
and its distinguished triangles arise from semi-split extensions or, equivalently, mapping cone sequences.
Bott periodicity becomes an equivalence
\Sigma\circ\Sigma\cong\Id_{\mathsf{KK}^{G}}
.
The tensor structure
-\otimes-
extends the algebra-level minimal tensor product of C*-algebras equipped with the diagonal
G
-action, and its unit object is
\mathbbm{1}=\mathbb{C}
, the algebra of complex numbers with trivial
G
-action.

If
G
is compact, the graded commutative endomorphism ring of the unit is

\End^{*}_{\mathsf{KK}^{G}}(\mathbbm{1})\cong\mathrm{R}(G)[\beta^{\pm 1}]

where
\beta
(of degree two) is the invertible Bott element and
\mathrm{R}(G)\cong\End(\mathbbm{1})
(in degree zero) is the complex representation ring of
G
.
(If
G
is non-compact, the nature of this ring generally remains mysterious and is connected to deep questions such as the Baum–Connes conjecture.)

3.2.
Functoriality in the group

Equivariant KK-theory is equipped with a rich functoriality in the group variable, which can even be neatly extended to groupoids.
We will only explicitly use a small part of this structure, namely:

•

The restriction functor
\Res^{G}_{H}\colon\mathsf{KK}^{G}\to\mathsf{KK}^{H}
, for
H\leq G
a closed subgroup.

•

The induction functor
\Ind^{G}_{H}\colon\mathsf{KK}^{H}\to\mathsf{KK}^{G}
, for
H\leq G
a closed subgroup.

•

The conjugation equivalences
\Con_{g}=\Con_{g,H}\colon\mathsf{KK}^{H}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathsf{KK}^{{}^{g}H}
, for
H\leq G
a closed subgroup and
g\in G
an element, where
{}^{g}H:=gHg^{-1}
denotes the conjugate subgroup. (On algebras,
\Con_{g}
sends an
H
-C*-algebra
A
to the same C*-algebra equipped with the
{}^{g}H
-action
ghg^{-1}\cdot a:=ga
.)

All these functors can be constructed at the level of C*-algebras and then extended to KK-theory using its universal property.
Both restriction and conjugation functors are special cases of restricting an action along a continuous group morphism with closed image.
Once extended, these functors have nice properties, such as:

•

All three kinds of functors are exact and preserve countable coproducts.

•

Restriction and conjugation functors are symmetric monoidal, hence are tt-functors of tt-categories (
Section

2.1
).

•

When
G/H
is compact,
\Ind^{G}_{H}
is a
right
adjoint of
\Res^{G}_{H}
:

\mathsf{KK}^{G}(A,\Ind^{G}_{H}(B))\cong\mathsf{KK}^{H}(B,\Res^{G}_{H}(A)).

•

When
G/H
is discrete,
\Ind^{G}_{H}
is a
left
adjoint of
\Res^{G}_{H}
:

\mathsf{KK}^{G}(\Ind^{G}_{H}(B),A)\cong\mathsf{KK}^{H}(B,\Res^{G}_{H}(A)).

•

There is a ‘projection formula’ natural isomorphism

(3.1)

\Ind^{G}_{H}(\Res^{G}_{H}(A)\otimes B)\cong A\otimes\Ind^{G}_{H}(B).

Moreover, these functors are related by several functoriality and commutation natural isomorphisms, which can be easily checked at the algebra level, such as restriction and induction in stages

\displaystyle\Res^{G}_{H}\circ\Res^{H}_{L}\cong\Res^{G}_{L},\quad\Ind_{H}^{G}\circ\Ind_{L}^{H}\cong\Ind_{L}^{G}\quad(L\leq H\leq G),

as well as

\Con_{g,L}\circ\Res^{H}_{L}\cong\Res^{{}^{g}H}_{{}^{g}L}\circ\Con_{g,H},\quad\Con_{g,G}\cong\Id_{\mathsf{KK}^{G}}\quad(L\leq H\leq G),

(used in the proof of
Theorem

6.6
), among many others.

3.2 Remark
.

It is possible to show that the above structure turns the assignment
G\mapsto\mathsf{KK}^{G}
(when restricted to
finite
groups
G
) into a Green 2-functor in the sense of
[
Del22
]
.
We will not make use of this fact, although it would provide alternative proofs for some results obtained later on.

3.3.
G
-cell algebras and the equivariant bootstrap category

As mentioned in the Introduction,
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
does not seem to admit any good generating set.
To remedy this, one can choose a suitable set of objects and replace
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
with the smaller, but more tractable, localizing subcategory generated by this set (
Notation

2.5
).

3.3 Definition

(
[
Del14
, Def. 2.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group.
A
(standard) orbit algebra
is a commutative
G
-C
∗
-algebra of the form
C_{0}(G/H)
, for some closed subgroup
H\leq G
. We denote by
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
the localising subcategory of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
generated by orbit
G

\mathsf{Cell}(G):=\loc\langle\,\mathrm{C}_{0}(G/H)\mid H\textrm{ a closed subgroup of }G\,\rangle\quad\subset\quad\mathsf{KK}^{G}.

The objects in
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
are called
G
-cell algebras
.

As a localising subcategory of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
,
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is still an essentially small category admitting all countable coproducts. Observe that
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
contains the tensor unit
\mathbb{C}=\mathrm{C}_{0}(G/G)
.
One can show, at least when
G
is discrete or a compact Lie group, that
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is closed under the tensor product and therefore defines a tt-subcategory of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
; and moreover the restriction, induction and conjugation functors between equivariant Kasparov categories recalled in
Section

3.2
all restrict to exact functors and/or tt-functors between the corresponding categories of cell algebras (see
[
Del14
, Proposition 2.7]
).

The case of finite groups is particularly nice, because of the following observation:

3.4 Proposition

(
[
Del14
, Prop. 2.9]
)
.

Let
G
be any finite group.
Then
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is a rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated tt-category (
Definition

2.9
), with orbit
G

\mathrm{C}_{0}(G/H)=\mathrm{C}(G/H)

(H\leq G)
providing a finite set of compact

and rigid objects (each

is its own tensor dual).
In particular

The case

Indeed, there are multiple ways one may wish to generalize the bootstrap category to the equivariant setting, for instance by choosing different generating sets of objects than orbit algebras.
For finite groups, it turns out that several of these possible generalizations agree:

3.5 Theorem
.

When

(a)

The

(b)

(c)

\loc\langle\,\mathrm{C}(G/H)\mid H\leq G\;\mathrm{cyclic}\,\rangle

In this article we will only use the equivalence of (c) and (d).
However, in view of the equivalence with (a) we also refer to

as the equivariant bootstrap category of the finite group

.
We record a nice feature of its compact objects:

3.6 Lemma
.

For every finite group
G
,
the graded endomorphism ring of the tensor unit
\End^{*}(\mathbbm{1})\cong\mathrm{R}(G)[\beta^{\pm 1}]
is noetherian, and the graded module
\End^{*}(A)=\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{*}(A,A)
over it is finitely generated for every compact
{}_{\aleph_{1}}

A\in\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c}
.

Proof.

When
G
is finite (or even compact Lie),
\mathrm{R}(G)
is a finitely generated ring and therefore noetherian by
[
Seg68
, Cor. 3.3]
.
If
H\leq G
is any (closed) subgroup,
\mathrm{R}(H)
is finitely generated when seen as an
\mathrm{R}(G)
-module via the restriction map
\res^{G}_{H}\colon\mathrm{R}(G)\to\mathrm{R}(H)
, by
[
Seg68
, Prop. 3.2]
(attributed to Atiyah).
The former is easily seen to be equivalent to the noetherianity of the graded commutative ring
\mathrm{R}(G)[\beta^{\pm 1}]
, and the latter implies the remaining claim by the following argument.

For each
H\leq G
, we deduce by the adjunction
\Res^{G}_{H}\dashv\Ind^{G}_{H}
that the
\mathrm{R}(G)
-module

\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{*}(\mathbbm{1},\mathrm{C}(G/H))=\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{*}(\mathbbm{1},\Ind^{G}_{H}(\mathbbm{1}))\cong\mathsf{KK}^{H}_{*}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1})=\mathrm{R}(H)[\beta^{\pm 1}]

is finitely generated.
Since
\mathrm{C}(G/H)
is self-dual, we further deduce that

\End^{*}(\mathrm{C}(G/H))\cong\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{*}(\mathbbm{1},\mathrm{C}(G/H)\otimes\mathrm{C}(G/H))\cong\bigoplus_{[x]\in H\backslash G/{}^{x}\!H}\mathsf{KK}^{G}_{*}(\mathbbm{1},\mathrm{C}(G/H\cap{}^{x}H))

is also finitely generated. (Note that all isomorphisms used here are

-linear, because so are the restriction functors to subgroups and hence the adjunction isomorphisms, as one easily checks.)
Since
\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c}=\thick\langle\,\mathrm{C}(G/H)\mid H\leq G\,\rangle
, we conclude the same about

for an arbitrary

by a routine induction argument on the triangular length of

with respect to the generators.
∎

3.4.
The induced tt-rings
\mathrm{C}(G/H)

If

of

(see

(Alternatively, this is also a consequence of the Green 2-functor structure of

3.7 Remark

One can easily check (

It follows that decompositions of the form

(and similar ones arising from decompositions of left

and therefore of tt-rings.

3.8 Proposition
.

For every finite group
G
and subgroup
H\leq G
, the above tt-ring
\mathrm{C}(G/H)
has finite degree in the sense of
[
Bal14
]
in the tt-category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c}
.

Proof.

We are going to appeal (twice) to
[
Bal16
, Thm. 3.7(b)]
, which contains the following criterion: Let
A\in\mathscr{K}
be a tt-ring of the tt-category
\mathscr{K}
, and suppose
F\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
is a tt-functor such that its restriction to the thick tensor-ideal
\mathscr{K}_{\supp(A)}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
is conservative (
i.e.
it detects the vanishing of objecs); then the degree of
A
equals that of
F(A)
. (As with the rest of the theory of degree, this criterion assumes both
\mathscr{K}
and
\mathscr{L}
to be idempotent complete.)

First we apply this to the tt-functor
F:=(\Res^{G}_{S})_{S}\colon\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c}\to\prod_{S}\mathsf{Cell}(S)^{c}
, where
S
runs through all cyclic subgroups of
G
.
We see that
F
is conservative on the whole category
\mathsf{Cell}(G)^{c}
by
Theorem

3.5
(d) (in fact this follows directly from the Arano–Kubota result
[
AK18
, Cor. 3.13]
used in its proof), and therefore we reduce to proving that
F(\mathrm{C}(G/H))=(\Res^{G}_{S}\mathrm{C}(G/H))_{S}
has finite degree in the product tt-category.

Since the degree of a product of finitely many finite-degree tt-rings is at most the sum of their degrees (by
Lemma

2.4
), and because of the isomorphism

\Res^{G}_{S}\mathrm{C}(G/H)\cong\prod_{[x]\in S\backslash G/H}\mathrm{C}(S/S\cap\!{}^{x}H)

of tt-rings (
Remark

3.7
), we further reduce to showing that
\mathrm{C}(S/T)
has finite degree in
\mathsf{Cell}(S)^{c}
for every cyclic group
S
and subgroup
T\leq S
.

To prove the latter we apply Balmer’s criterion again, this time taking the tt-functor
F
to be
\Res^{S}_{T}\colon\mathsf{Cell}(S)^{c}\to\mathsf{Cell}(T)^{c}
.
To see that this
F
verifies the required hypothesis, observe that we have an inclusion

\mathsf{Cell}(A)^{c}_{\supp(\mathrm{C}(S/T))}=\thick_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\mathrm{C}(S/T)\,\rangle\subseteq\Ind^{S}_{T}\big(\mathsf{Cell}(T)^{c}\big)=:\mathscr{J}

of the tensor-ideal of objects supported on
\supp(\mathrm{C}(S/T))
into the essential image of the induction functor.
Indeed, the first equality displayed above is by the classification of thick tensor ideals (
Theorem

2.2
); the inclusion on the right follows from the fact that
\Ind^{S}_{T}(\mathsf{Cell}(T)^{c})
is a thick tensor ideal
(an easy consequence of the projection formula (
3.1
) for
\Res^{S}_{T}
and
\Ind^{S}_{T}
) which contains
\mathrm{C}(S/T)=\Ind^{S}_{T}(\mathbbm{1})
.

Hence it suffices to show that
F=\Res^{S}_{T}
is conservative on
\mathscr{J}
.
Recall that the two adjunctions
\Ind^{S}_{T}\dashv\Res^{S}_{T}\dashv\Ind^{S}_{T}
can be chosen so that the composite

A\to\Res^{S}_{T}\Ind^{S}_{T}(A)\to A

of the unit of
\Ind^{S}_{T}\dashv\Res^{S}_{T}
followed by the counit of
\Res^{S}_{T}\dashv\Ind^{S}_{T}
is the identity map of
A
, for all
A\in\mathsf{Cell}(T)^{c}
(this is well-known; see
Theorem

A.26
(2)(b) for a proof if necessary).
In particular, it follows that
\Res^{S}_{T}\Ind^{S}_{T}(A)\cong 0
implies
A\cong 0
and therefore
\Ind^{S}_{T}(A)\cong 0
, showing that indeed
\Res^{S}_{T}
is conservative on
\mathscr{J}
.
Thus by Balmer’s criterion the degree of
\mathrm{C}(S/T)
equals that of the product tt-ring

\Res^{S}_{T}(\mathrm{C}(S/T))\cong\prod_{[y]\in T\backslash S/T}\underbrace{\mathrm{C}(T/T\cap\!{}^{y}T)}_{\mathrm{C}(T/T)}\cong\mathbbm{1}^{\times|S/T|}

which is equal to the finite index
|S/T|
by
[
Bal14
, Thm. 3.9(a)]
.
∎

4.
Stratification

Our main reference for the stratification theory of tt-categories is
[
BHS23
]
, whose terminology and notations
we shall adopt.
Throughout
\mathscr{T}
will denote a big tt-category,
i.e.
one which is rigidly-compactly generated (
Definition

2.6
).

4.1 Definition
.

A
theory of supports
, or
support theory
, on
\mathscr{T}
is a pair
(X,\sigma)
consisting of a topological space
X
and a function
\sigma
assigning a subset
\sigma(A)\subseteq X
to every object of
\mathscr{T}
; this assignment must satisfy five basic compatibility axioms with the suspension, direct sums, triangles, coproducts and tensor products in
\mathscr{T}
(see
[
BHS23
, Def. 7.1]
).
We can extend the support function
\sigma
to arbitrary classes of objects
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{T}
by setting

\sigma(\mathscr{S}):=\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{S}}\sigma(A)
.
The support theory
(X,\sigma)
is said to
stratify
\mathscr{T}
if the assignment
\mathscr{S}\mapsto\sigma(\mathscr{S})
restricts to a bijection as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{localizing tensor ideals of }\mathscr{T}\end{array}\right\}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\textrm{subsets of }X\end{array}\right\}.

4.2 Recollection
.

A topological space is
noetherian
if every open subset is quasi-compact.
For example the Zariski spectrum of any noetherian commutative ring is a noetherian space.
In
[
BHS23
]
, the notion of a
weakly noetherian
space is introduced.
For our present purposes, we only need to know that all noetherian spaces are weakly noetherian.
Weakly noetherian spaces serve the following:

4.3 Example

(Balmer–Favi supports)
.

The main example of a support theory is due to Balmer–Favi
[
BF11
]
, and we simply write it as
\sigma(A)=\supp(A)
for all
A
.
The target space
X
of this support theory is the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
of the tt-subcategory of compact objects, and the support of an object
A
is given by

\supp(A):=\left\{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})\mid g(\mathscr{P})\otimes A\neq 0\right\},

where
g(\mathscr{P})
is a certain
\otimes
-idempotent object of
\mathscr{T}
.
On compact objects, the Balmer–Favi support agrees with Balmer’s universal support data recalled in
Section

2.2
, so there is no notational ambiguity.
We refer to
[
BHS23
, §2]
for details.
Note that the objects
g(\mathscr{P})
are all well-defined, and therefore the Balmer–Favi support theory is, precisely when
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is weakly noetherian (
Recollection

4.2
).

4.4 Terminology
.

Unless otherwise stated,
when we say that
\mathscr{T}
is
stratified in the sense of
[
BHS23
]
, or simply
stratified
, we mean that
\Spc(\mathscr{K})
is weakly noetherian (
e.g.
noetherian) and that
\mathscr{T}
is stratified by its Balmer–Favi support theory.

The following general fact can be useful for computing Balmer–Favi supports:

4.5 Lemma
.

Suppose the big tt-category
\mathscr{T}
has noetherian spectrum and is stratified. Let
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
be a point of the spectrum and let
A\in\mathscr{T}
be any object. The following are equivalent:

(a)

The Balmer–Favi support of
A
is
\supp(A)=\{\mathscr{P}\}
.

(b)

\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,A\,\rangle=\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,g(\mathscr{P})\,\rangle
.

(c)

For all
B\in\mathscr{T}
, we have
\mathscr{P}\in\supp(B)\Leftrightarrow B\otimes A\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{T}
is stratified, (a) implies (b) by
Theorem

4.6
(b) below.

Now suppose (b) is true and let
B\in\mathscr{T}
. From (b) we deduce that

\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,B\otimes A\,\rangle=\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,B\otimes g(\mathscr{P})\,\rangle,

so that in particular
B\otimes A\neq 0
if and only if
B\otimes g(\mathscr{P})\neq 0
, that is, (c) is true.

Finally, suppose (c) is true and let
\mathscr{Q}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
be any point of the spectrum.
By
[
BHS23
, Lem. 2.13]
, we know that
\supp(g(\mathscr{Q}))=\{\mathscr{Q}\}
.
From this together with (c) (applied to
B=g(\mathscr{Q})
) we deduce that

\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{P}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathscr{P}\in\supp(g(\mathscr{Q}))\quad\overset{(c)}{\Leftrightarrow}\quad g(\mathscr{Q})\otimes A\neq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathscr{Q}\in\supp(A),

that is
\supp(A)=\{\mathscr{P}\}
as per (a).
Hence the three conditions are equivalent.
∎

The following criterion is a central result of stratification theory.

4.6 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a big tt-category, and suppose (for simplicity) that
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
is noetherian.
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

‘Minimality’:

\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,g(\mathscr{P})\,\rangle
is a minimal (nonzero) localizing tensor ideal of
\mathscr{T}
for every prime
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
.

(b)

For every
A\in\mathscr{T}
, we have
\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,A\,\rangle=\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,g(\mathscr{P})\mid\mathscr{P}\in\supp(A)\,\rangle
.

(c)

\mathscr{T}
is stratified (
Terminology

4.4
).

Proof.

This is
[
BHS23
, Thm. 4.1]
, improved by
[
BHS23
, Thm. 3.21]
, which says that the local-to-global principle (which in general must be included as part of requirement (a)) automatically holds when the Balmer spectrum is noetherian.
∎

4.7 Terminology
.

One says that
minimality holds for
\mathscr{T}
at
\mathscr{P}
if the statement of (a) holds for a specific tt-prime
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
.

The theorem implies that, in order to establish
stratification
for
\mathscr{T}
with noetherian spectrum, it suffices to prove
minimality
for
\mathscr{T}
separately at each point of its spectrum (as in
Terminology

4.7
).
In particular, one can work with different methods at different primes.
We next mention two such methods we will use: a reduction by finite localization and a reduction by finite étale morphisms.

4.8 Recollection

(Finite localizations)
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a big tt-category.
A finite localization
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
is a tt-functor between big tt-categories which is (equivalent to) a Verdier quotient of the form
\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
, whose kernel
\mathscr{L}=\Ker(F)
on objects is generated as a localizing subcategory by a set of compact objects of
\mathscr{T}
, or equivalently (since
\mathscr{L}\cap\mathscr{T}^{c}=\mathscr{L}^{c}
) such that
\mathscr{L}=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{L}^{c}\,\rangle
. Moreover, the canonical functor
\mathscr{T}^{c}/\mathscr{L}^{c}\to(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c}
is a cofinal fully faithful tt-functor (see
[
Nee92b
]
), so in particular it induces a homeomorphism
\Spc((\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c})\cong\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c}/\mathscr{L}^{c})
.
It follows that
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
is again a big tt-category in our sense.
If
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
is noetherian then so is
\Spc(\mathscr{S}^{c})
, because the latter identifies with a subspace of the former by the induced embedding

\Spc((\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c})\cong\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c}/\mathscr{L}^{c})\to\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
,
\mathscr{Q}\mapsto F^{-1}(\mathscr{Q})
(
[
Bal05
, Prop. 3.13]
).

4.9 Proposition
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a big tt-category with
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
noetherian and let
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
be a finite localization functor.
Then for every
\mathscr{Q}\in\Spc((\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c})
, minimality at
\mathscr{Q}
in
\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
holds if and only if minimality at
\mathscr{P}:=F^{-1}(\mathscr{Q})\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
holds in
\mathscr{T}
.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
BHS23
, Prop. 5.2 and Thm. 3.22]
.
Applied to
\mathscr{T}
, these results say that minimality in
\mathscr{T}
at a point
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
holds if and only if the category
\mathscr{T}/\loc\langle\,\mathscr{P}\,\rangle
satisfies minimality at its unique closed point
\{0\}
.

Now suppose
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
is of the form
\mathscr{P}=F^{-1}(\mathscr{Q})
for
\mathscr{Q}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c}
, (
i.e.

\mathscr{L}\subseteq\mathscr{P}
and
\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{P}/\mathscr{L}
).
Consider the induced commutative diagram of finite localization functors (
Recollection

4.8
):

\textstyle{\mathscr{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{T}/\loc\langle\,\mathscr{P}\,\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})/\loc\langle\,\mathscr{Q}\,\rangle}

In details, the vertical arrows are the quotient functors by the localizing tensor ideals
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{P}\,\rangle=\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\mathscr{P}\,\rangle

and
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{Q}\,\rangle=\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\mathscr{Q}\,\rangle
, respectively, and the bottom horizontal arrow is the functor induced by the universal property of Verdier quotients; it is an equivalence of big tt-categories, since both composite functors from
\mathscr{T}
to
(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})/\loc\langle\,\mathscr{Q}\,\rangle
have the same kernel
\mathscr{P}
.

Note that the above-cited result equally applies to the local category
\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
: minimality in
\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
at a point
\mathscr{Q}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})^{c}
holds if and only if the category
(\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L})/\loc\langle\,\mathscr{Q}\,\rangle
satisfies minimality at its unique closed point
\{0\}
.

Combining the two criteria, the commutativity of the square implies that minimality at
\mathscr{P}
in
\mathscr{T}
holds if and only if minimality at
\mathscr{Q}
in
\mathscr{T}/\mathscr{L}
holds, as claimed.
∎

4.10 Definition

(Finite étale functor)
.

A coproducts preserving tt-functor
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
between two rigidly-compactly generated tt-categories is said to be
finite étale
if there exists a tt-ring (a.k.a. commutative separable monoid, see
Section

2.5
)
A
in
\mathscr{T}^{c}
and an equivalence
\mathscr{S}\simeq\Mod_{\mathscr{T}}(A)
of tt-categories which identifies
F
with the free-
A
-module functor
\mathscr{T}\to\Mod_{\mathscr{T}}(A)
.
We refer to
[
San22
]
for explanations and many examples of this notion.

4.11 Warning
.

Beware of the terminology: finite localizations are smashing localizations and therefore are examples of étale functors. However localizations, even
finite
ones, are only rarely
finite étale
. (For localizations, ‘finite’ refers to the kernel being generated by compact objects; for étale functors, ‘finite’ refers to
U(\mathbbm{1})
being compact, where
U
is the right adjoint.)

4.12 Theorem

(
[
BHS23
, Thm. 6.4]
)
.

Let
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
be a finite étale functor (
Definition

4.10
) of big tt-categories, and let
\varphi:=\Spc(F^{c})\colon\Spc(\mathscr{S}^{c})\to\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
denote the induced map between spectra, both of which we assume to be noetherian.
Suppose
\mathscr{Q}\in\Spc(\mathscr{S}^{c})
is such that
\varphi^{-1}(\{\varphi(\mathscr{Q})\})=\{\mathscr{Q}\}
(for instance when
\varphi
is injective). Then minimality at
\mathscr{Q}
in
\mathscr{S}
implies minimality at
\varphi(\mathscr{Q})
in
\mathscr{T}
.
∎

We conclude our speedy tour of stratification theory by citing the following powerful ‘quasi-finite descent’ (global) criterion for stratification, due to Barthel–Heard–Sanders together with Castellana, which we will need in a situation when the hypothesis on the fiber of
\varphi(\mathscr{Q})
in
Theorem

4.12
fails:

4.13 Theorem

(
[
BCHS24
, Thm. 17.16]
)
.

Let
F\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
be a coproducts preserving functor of big tt-categories and let
\varphi:=\Spc(F^{c})
be the induced map of Balmer spectra, both of which we assume to be weakly noetherian.
Suppose that the right adjoint
U
of
F
preserves compact objects and that
\varphi
is surjective with discrete fibers.
Then if
\mathscr{S}
is stratified so is
\mathscr{T}
.
∎

4.1.
A countable version of stratification

We introduce the countable variant of stratification which is required in order to make sense of the results and conjectures about
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
we formulated in the Introduction.
This variant is rather straightforward, but we must pay attention to certain details.

Now
\mathscr{T}
denotes a
rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated
tt-category as in
Definition

2.9
, keeping in mind our main example
\mathscr{T}=\mathsf{Cell}(G)
of the equivariant bootstrap category of a finite group
G
(
Proposition

3.4
).

4.14 Construction
.

With
\mathscr{T}
as above, assume for simplicity that the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
is noetherian.
Let
Y
be a Thomason (
i.e.
specialization closed) subset of the Balmer spectrum
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
and
\mathscr{T}^{c}_{Y}
the corresponding thick tensor ideal of
\mathscr{T}^{c}
, as per
Theorem

2.2
.
We denote by
e_{Y}\to\mathbbm{1}\to f_{Y}\to\Sigma e_{Y}

the associated idempotent triangle in
\mathscr{T}
which is uniquely determined by the properties

\Ker(f_{Y}\otimes-)=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{T}^{c}_{Y}\,\rangle=e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}

(see
[
Del10
, §2.1]
if necessary).
For every
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
, let

g(\mathscr{P}):=e_{Y_{1}}\otimes f_{Y_{2}}\quad\in\mathscr{T}

where
Y_{1},Y_{2}
are specialization closed subsets such that
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}^{c}=\{\mathscr{P}\}
, for instance
Y_{1}=\overline{\{\mathscr{P}\}}=\{\mathscr{Q}\mid\mathscr{Q}\subseteq\mathscr{P}\}
and
Y_{2}=\{\mathscr{Q}\mid\mathscr{P}\not\subseteq\mathscr{Q}\}
.
For any object
A\in\mathscr{T}
, we define its
countable Balmer–Favi support
to be

\supp(A):=\{\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})\mid g(\mathscr{P})\otimes A\not\cong 0\}

and we extend the definition to collections
\mathscr{S}
of objects of
\mathscr{T}
by

\supp(\mathscr{S}):=\bigcup_{A\in\mathscr{S}}\supp(A).

We say that
\mathscr{T}
is
stratified
, or
countably stratified
for emphasis, if the resulting application
\mathscr{S}\mapsto\supp(\mathscr{S})
induces a bijection between the set of localizing tensor ideals of
\mathscr{T}
and the set of all subsets of
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
.

4.15 Remark
.

The basic properties of the usual Balmer–Favi support remain true for its countable version, with only obvious minimal adjustments necessary.
Indeed, much of the theory developed in
[
BHS23
]
should go through, although there are limits to this due to the lack of a countable version of Brown representability for the dual (
i.e.
for covariant homological functors), see
[
Del10
, Ex. 2.11 and 2.22]
.
In any case, as explained in the introduction, we have chosen a different path towards applying stratification to equivariant KK-theory.

5.
Rational stratification for
G
-cell algebras

In this section we prove
Theorem

1.7
of the Introduction, which in fact does not need any sophisticated stratification theory, nor the enlarged categories built in
Appendix

A
, but instead is a rather straightforward consequence of the (semi)simple structure of rational cell algebras.

Fix a finite group
G
.
We recall the standard (for tt-categories) notion of rationalization which is compatible with coproducts, as applied to
G
-cell algebras.

5.1 Terminology
.

Let
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
be the
G
-equivariant bootstrap category of
Definition

3.3
, and consider the Verdier quotient

\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\mathsf{Cell}(G)/\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\cone(f)\mid f\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}\subseteq\mathrm{R}(G)=\End_{\mathsf{Cell}(G)}(\mathbbm{1})\,\rangle.

This is the
rationalized
bootstrap category.
It is again a rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated tt-category, and is such that
\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}(A,B)=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathsf{KK}^{G}(A,B)
whenever
A
is a compact
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
object of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
and
B\in\mathsf{Cell}(G)
arbitrary.
(See
e.g.

[
Del10
, §2]
.)

As mentioned in the introduction, this category admits a relatively simple purely algebraic model.
Namely,
[
BDM24
, Thm. C]
provides an equivalence

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Cell}(G)_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq{\prod_{\mathrm{Cl}(H),\,H\leq G\;\textrm{cyclic}}}\underbrace{\big(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{|H|})\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)\big)\,\textsf{-}\Mod^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\aleph_{1}}}_{=:\;\mathscr{T}_{H}}

of tensor categories with a finite product of semisimple abelian tensor categories
\mathscr{T}_{H}
of countable
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded modules over certain (ungraded) skew group rings
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{|H|})\rtimes_{c}W_{G}(H)
, one for each conjugacy class of cyclic subgroups
H
of
G
.
The suspension
\Sigma
of
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
corresponds on the right-hand side to degree-shift of graded modules.

We derive from this structural result the following observation:

5.3 Lemma
.

Each tt-category
\mathscr{T}_{H}
is a (countable)
tt-field
in the sense of
[
BKS19
]
(adapted to the countable setting), meaning that it is rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated and satisfies two extra properties:

(F1)

The triangulated category
\mathscr{T}_{H}
is
pure-semisimple
, that is, each object is a coproduct of compact
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
objects.

(F2)

For every nonzero object
A\in\mathscr{T}_{H}
, the functor
A\otimes-\colon\mathscr{T}_{H}\to\mathscr{T}_{H}
is faithful, that is any morphism
f
in
\mathscr{T}_{H}
is zero as soon as
A\otimes f
is zero. (Clearly, by F1 it suffices to check this property for compact objects
A
.)

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
is rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated, so is its direct factor
\mathscr{T}_{H}
as one sees easily.
The rest follows from the next two facts (
cf.

[
BBB24
, Prop. 11.5]
):

•

\mathscr{T}_{H}
is semisimple abelian, and

•

the endomorphism ring
\End_{\mathscr{T}_{H}}(\mathbbm{1})
of its
\otimes
-unit is a field.

The first fact was already mentioned.
To see why the second fact is true, recall from
[
BDM24
, §2]
that if a group
W
acts on a ring
S
via the action
\alpha
, the tensor-unit for the module category over the skew group ring
S\rtimes_{\alpha}W
is
(S,\alpha)
, and one can promptly verify that
\End(S,\alpha)\cong(S^{W})^{\mathrm{op}}
is the opposite of the fixed-point subring of
S
, via
f\mapsto f(1)
.
Hence in the present case of
\mathscr{T}_{H}
, the ring
\End_{\mathscr{T}_{H}}(\mathbbm{1})
is a fixed-point subfield of the cyclic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{|H|})

6.12 Proposition

Proof.

Proof of

We are going to apply

Appendix A
A genuinely big version of equivariant KK-theory

In this Appendix we use the toolkit of Lurie’s
\infty
-categories
[
Lur09
]

[
Lur17
]
in order to enlarge the equivariant Kasparov categories
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
and their subcategories of cell algebras
\mathsf{Cell}(G)
so that they contain arbitrary small coproducts.
For our purposes, the enlargement must be done in a way which preserves all the structure we already have (the triangulation, the tensor product, the countable coproducts) and which accommodates extensions of the usual basic change-of-group functors (restriction and induction) and the adjunctions and formulas between them.

We do not claim any originality here, indeed our approach is adapted from that of recent work by Bunke–Engel–Land
[
BEL23
]
.
Specifically, we use the same
\infty
-categorical enhancement of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
they use, but then we enlarge it by applying the relative
\Ind_{\aleph_{1}}
construction, rather than the usual
\Ind=\Ind_{\aleph_{0}}
construction.
This change is to ensure that the countable coproducts of
\mathsf{KK}^{G}
survive the enlargement.
It also causes us to lay out some rather technical preliminaries, because a few results which are well-known in the compactly generated case are more complicated, or are plain wrong, for the
\aleph_{1}
-relative situation.
We will collect most of this material straight from Lurie’s work, with the exception of the cardinal-accounting comparison result in
Proposition

A.10
which appears to be new.

A.1 Remark
.

In this appendix we restrict attention to
countable discrete
groups
G
, because (out of convenience) we want to cite
[
BEL23
]
where this hypothesis is made. But we have no doubts that the general constructions and results presented here extend to second countable locally compact groups.

The language of
\infty
-categories

We will freely use the basic terminology involving
\infty
-categories and their objects, morphisms, equivalences, (full) subcategories, functors, natural transformations, adjunctions, localizations etc., as used in
[
Lur09
]
.
Limits and colimits in this context always mean homotopy (co)limits, and commutative diagrams are understood to commute up to (given) homotopy.
Recall that every
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
has a homotopy category
\Ho(\mathscr{C})
, which is an ordinary category, and conversely every ordinary category can be viewed as a (‘discrete’)
\infty
-category via the nerve functor (which we omit from notations).
Recall also that products and coproducts in an
\infty
-category are the same as products and coproducts in its homotopy category (in the ordinary sense), but that this is false for other kinds of (co)limits.
To avoid confusion later on, we write
\Top
(rather than
\mathscr{S}
as Lurie) for the
\infty
-category of spaces (a.k.a. Kan complexes,
\infty
-groupoids, anima, homotopy types, …).
We say an
\infty
-category is

if it is equivalent to a small one (
i.e.
to one whose underlying simplicial set is small).

We also recall the following cardinal-related terminology from
[
Lur09
]
, because it is partially at odds with the similar-sounding terminology for triangulated categories that we also use:

A.2 Recollection
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite regular cardinal. A simplicial set is
\kappa
-small
if its set of non-degenerate simplices is
\kappa
-small,
i.e.
of cardinality less than
\kappa
.
A (homotopy) limit or colimit in an
\infty
-category
\mathscr{E}
is
\kappa
-small
if it is the (co)limit of some functor
p\colon K\to\mathscr{E}
with
K
a
\kappa
-small simplicial set, and it is
small
if it is
\kappa
-small for some
\kappa
.
For
\kappa
a regular cardinal, a colimit is
\kappa
-filtered
if it is the colimit of a diagram
p\colon K\to\mathscr{E}
where
K
is a (small)
\kappa
-filtered
simplicial set (
[
Lur09
, Def. 5.3.1.7 and Rem. 5.3.1.11]
).
A functor
F\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is
\kappa
-continuous
if it preserves
\kappa
-filtered colimits, and
\kappa
-right exact
if it preserves
\kappa
-small colimits.
We say
\mathscr{E}
is
(co-)complete
if it admits arbitrary small (co-)limits.
If
\mathscr{E}
is cocomplete, an object
C\in\mathscr{E}
is
\kappa
-compact
if the representable functor
\mathscr{E}(C,-)\colon\mathscr{E}\to\Top
is
\kappa
-continuous.
One says
\mathscr{E}
is
\kappa
-compactly generated
if it is cocomplete, if its full subcategory

\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}

of
\kappa
-compact objects is essentially small, and if every object
E\in\mathscr{E}
is a
\kappa
-filtered colimit of
\kappa
-compact objects of
\mathscr{E}
(this is not the definition, but it is equivalent to it by
[
Lur09
, Thm. 5.5.1.1]

An
\infty
-category
\mathscr{E}
is
presentable
if it is
\kappa
-compactly generated for some regular
\kappa
, and it is
compactly generated
if we can take
\kappa=\aleph_{0}
(denoted
\omega
by Lurie), the countable infinite cardinal.
Similarly, an object is
compact
if it is
\aleph_{0}
-compact.

Stable
\infty
-categories

Recall now from
[
Lur17
, §1]
that an
\infty
-category is
stable
if it is pointed (
i.e.
final and initial objects coincide), it admits all finite limits and colimits, and pushout and pullback squares in
\mathscr{C}
coincide. It follows that finite limits and finite colimits of any shape are canonically equivalent.
A functor of stable
\infty
-categories is
exact
if it preserves all finite limits or, equivalently, finite colimits.
If
\mathscr{C}
is stable, its homotopy category
\Ho(\mathscr{C})
inherits a canonical structure of triangulated category, and exact functors between stable
\infty
-categories induce exact functors of triangulated categories.

In a stable
\infty
-category the existence of arbitrary (
\kappa
-small) colimits is equivalent to the existence of (
\kappa
-small) coproducts; and similarly, an exact functor between stable
\infty
-categories preserves the former iff it preserves the latter
(see
[
Lur17
, Prop.1.4.4.1]
). This is very useful in practice because, as mentioned, a diagram is a coproduct in an
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
iff it is a coproduct in its homotopy category
\Ho(\mathscr{C})
.

In this vein, by a harmless slight abuse of language, we will be talking about ‘the thick/ localizing/ ideal subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
, when we mean the full (
\infty
-)subcategory of the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
which is spanned by the same objects as the homonymous full subcategory of the triangulated category
\Ho(\mathscr{C})
.
We will also freely use the same notations
\loc\langle\,-\,\rangle
,
\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,-\,\rangle
, etc. as introduced in the main body of the article.

Ind-completion relative to a cardinal

For any small
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
, let

\PSh(\mathscr{C}):=\Fun(\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\Top)

be its presheaf
\infty
-category, that is the
\infty
-category of contravariant functors with values in the
\infty
-category of spaces.
It is a complete and cocomplete
\infty
-category.

Fix an infinite regular cardinal
\kappa
.
If
\mathscr{C}
admits all
\kappa
-small colimits, we can consider as in
[
Lur17
, §5.3.5]
the full subcategory

\Indk(\mathscr{C})\subset\PSh(\mathscr{C})

of those presheaves which send
\kappa
-small colimits of
\mathscr{C}
to (homotopy) limits in
\Top
(
cf.
the characterization in
[
Lur09
, Cor. 5.3.5.4]
).
The Yoneda embedding

restricts to a fully faithful functor

j=j_{\mathscr{C}}=j_{\mathscr{C},\kappa}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\Indk(\mathscr{C}).

It turns out that an
\infty
-category is

for some essentially small

A.3 Proposition

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small (stable)

Then
\Indk(\mathscr{C})
is a
\kappa
-compactly generated (stable)
\infty
-category, and the Yoneda embedding

preserves
\kappa
-small colimits and displays the subcategory of
\kappa

.
In particular if

\mathscr{C}\overset{\sim}{\to}(\Indk(\mathscr{C}))^{\kappa}

If
\mathscr{C}
is stable, then
\Indk(\mathscr{C})
is stable by
[
Lur17
, Prop. 1.1.3.6]

The Yoneda embedding
j_{\mathscr{C}}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\Indk(\mathscr{C})
preserves the
\kappa
-small colimits of
\mathscr{C}
by

, Prop. 5.3.5.14]

As
\mathscr{C}
admits
\kappa

\Indk(\mathscr{C})
is
\kappa
-compactly generated (see
[

By
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.3.5.5]

[

j_{\mathscr{C}}\colon C\to\Indk(\mathscr{C})
restricts to
\kappa

j_{\mathscr{C}}\colon\mathscr{C}\to(\Indk(\mathscr{C}))^{\kappa}
, and the resulting functor is an idempotent completion in the sense of
\infty

Lur09
, §4.4.5]
(which lifts the usual notion, at least in the stable case, in the sense that a stable

A.4 Proposition
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be two small
\infty
-categories admitting
\kappa
-small colimits, and let
f\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
be a
\kappa
-right exact functor (it preserves
\kappa
-small colimits).
Then there exists a functor
\Indk(f)
, unique up to equivalence, making the square

\textstyle{\mathscr{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{j_{\mathscr{C}}}
\textstyle{\Indk(\mathscr{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Indk(f)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{\mathscr{D}}}
\textstyle{\Indk(\mathscr{D})}

(homotopy) commute.
This functor preserves arbitrary colimits, in fact it has a right adjoint given by precomposition with
f
.

Proof.

By
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.3.5.10]
precomposition with
j_{\mathscr{C}}
induces,
for every
\infty
-category
\mathscr{E}
with arbitrary
\kappa
-filtered colimits,
an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

(A.5)

j_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}\colon\Fun_{\kappa}(\Indk(\mathscr{C}),\mathscr{E})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Fun(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})

where
\Fun_{\kappa}
on the left hand side denotes the full subcategory of
\Fun
of all
\kappa
-continuous functors.
Setting
\mathscr{E}:=\Indk(\mathscr{D})
, we define
\Indk(f)
to be a
\kappa
-continuous functor
\Indk(\mathscr{C})\to\mathscr{E}
corresponding to
j_{\mathscr{D}}\circ f\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{E}
via the above equivalence.
Since

preserves

has the claimed right adjoint, and in particular preserves colimits, by
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.3.5.13]
.
∎

A.6 Proposition
.

In the situation of the previous proposition, suppose that
f
admits a right adjoint

which also preserves

Then the adjunction

\Indk(f)\dashv\Indk(g)

Proof.

By
Proposition

we get an adjunction
\Indk(f)\dashv f^{*}
, where

denotes the precomposition functor
\Indk(\mathscr{D})\to\Indk(\mathscr{C})
,
P\mapsto P\circ f
.
Similarly, we can apply the proposition to

in order to obtain an adjunction
\Indk(g)\dashv g^{*}
.
Moreover, the given adjunction

induces an adjunction
g^{\mathrm{op}}\dashv f^{\mathrm{op}}
between the opposite categories and therefore, by whiskering its unit and counit maps, an adjunction
f^{*}\dashv g^{*}
between functor categories.
By uniqueness of the left adjoint of

, we must have an equivalence

.
From the latter and the adjunction
\Indk(f)\dashv f^{*}
, we deduce an adjunction
\Indk(f)\dashv\Indk(g)
.
∎

A.7 Remark
.

The Ind-construction is functorial, in that it can be upgraded to a functor

and because the former is a full subcategory of the latter closed under finite limits; hence the above equivalence restricts to an equivalence

between the full subcategories of stable
\infty
-categories on both sides.

Compactness in the homotopy category

Before considering tensor structures, we wish to link
\kappa
-compactness with a similar notion in the realm of well generated triangulated categories in the sense of
[
Nee01
]
(see also
[
Kra10
]
)).
We record these somewhat technical results in view of future use, as they apply to the case of
infinite
groups
G
(with
\kappa=\aleph_{1}
) and also because they may be of more general interest.

A.8 Lemma
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a stable
\infty
-category with arbitrary small colimits. Then:

(a)

An object in
\mathscr{E}
is compact if and only if it is compact in the triangulated category
\Ho(\mathscr{E})
,
and
\mathscr{E}
is compactly generated as an
\infty
-category if and only if
\Ho(\mathscr{E})
is compactly generated as a triangulated category.

(b)

Suppose
\mathscr{E}
compactly generated. Then for any regular cardinal
\kappa>\aleph_{0}
, an object of
\mathscr{E}
is
\kappa
-compact in
\mathscr{E}
in the sense of Lurie if and only if it is
\kappa
-compact in
\Ho(\mathscr{E})
in the sense of well generated categories.
Moreover
\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{E}^{\aleph_{0}}\,\rangle_{\kappa}
, that is, the
\kappa
-compact objects of
\mathscr{E}
form the
\kappa
-localizing (
i.e.
thick and closed under
\kappa
-small coproducts) subcategory of
\mathscr{E}
generated by the compact objects.

Proof.

Part (a) is contained in
[
Lur09
, Prop. 1.4.4.1]
.
Unfortunately this proof does not appear to admit a straightforward generalization to arbitrary cardinals.

Let us prove part (b).
Suppose
\mathscr{E}
is a compactly generated stable
\infty
-category.
We can take the (essentially) small subcategory
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{E}^{\aleph_{0}}
of its compact object as a generating set of compacts, so that
\mathscr{E}\simeq\Ind(\mathscr{C})
.

We claim that
\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}
, the full subcategory of all
\kappa
-compact objects in
\mathscr{E}
, is equal to the closure of
\mathscr{C}
in
\mathscr{E}
under
\kappa
-small colimits. (This does not use stability and should be well-known; we provide a proof as we could not find a precise reference).

To prove the claim, denote the latter closure by
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}\subset\mathscr{E}
.
Since
\kappa
-compact objects are closed under
\kappa
-small colimits by
[
Lur09
, Cor. 5.3.4.15]
and since compact objects are in particular
\kappa
-compact, we immediately obtain the inclusion
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}\subseteq\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}
.
To prove the reverse inclusion
\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}\subseteq\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
, we can proceed as follows (there may be more efficient ways).
Consider the inclusion functor
f\colon\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}\to\mathscr{E}
.
We want to use
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.3.5.11(2)]
to show that
f
extends to an equivalence
F\colon\Indk(\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa})\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{E}
. For this, it suffices to check that
f
satisfies the following three hypotheses:

(a)

f
is fully faithful (true by definition),

(b)

f
factors through
\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}
(we already proved that
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}\subseteq\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}
),

(c)

and finally, that the objects of
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
generate
\mathscr{E}
under
\kappa
-filtered colimits.

Item (c) can be shown precisely as in the proof of
[
Lur09
, 5.3.5.12]
, with
\PSh(\mathscr{C})
replaced by
\mathscr{E}=\Ind(\mathscr{C})
and
\PSh(\mathscr{C})^{\kappa}
replaced by
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
.
Namely, every object
X\in\mathscr{E}
is a small colimit (even a filtering colimit) of objects of
\mathscr{C}
.
By the proof of
[
Lur09
, 4.2.3.11]
,
X
can be rewritten as a
\kappa
-filtered colimit in
\mathscr{E}
of a diagram whose values are objects which are
\kappa
-small colimits of objects in
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
, that is, of a diagram with values in
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
.
We therefore obtain the announced equivalence
F\colon\Indk(\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa})\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{E}
, which restricts to an equivalence
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{E}^{\kappa}
by
Proposition

A.3
(since
\kappa>\aleph_{0}
), which in fact must then be an equality of full subcategories of
\mathscr{E}
.

(Alternatively and slightly more informally, once we know that every object
X\in\mathscr{E}
is the colimit of a
\kappa
-filtering diagram of
\kappa
-small colimits
Y_{\alpha}
of objects in
\mathscr{C}
, we may consider the case of a
\kappa
-compact
X
and the resulting equivalence
\mathscr{E}(X,X)\simeq\mathscr{E}(X,\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha})\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{\alpha}\mathscr{E}(X,Y_{\alpha})
. Applied to the identity map of
X
, this shows that
X
is a retract of one of the

,
i.e.
of a
\kappa
-small colimit of objects of
\mathscr{C}
. As retracts are countable colimits and
\kappa>\aleph_{0}
, we can rewrite

as a
\kappa
-small colimit of objects of
\mathscr{C}
.)

Recall now that
\mathscr{E}
is stable, which implies that the closure
\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\kappa}
under
\kappa
-small colimits agrees with
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}\,\rangle_{\kappa}
, the
\kappa
-localizing subcategory generated by
\mathscr{C}
(where we close under
\kappa
-small coproducts and mapping cofibers), which can be constructed already in the triangulated category
\Ho(\mathscr{E})
.
By
[
Kra10
, Cor. 7.2.2]
, the subcategory
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}\,\rangle_{\kappa}\subseteq\Ho(\mathscr{E})
is also precisely the full subcategory of
\kappa
-compact objects in the compactly generated (
i.e.

\aleph_{0}
-well generated) triangulated category
\Ho(\mathscr{E})
.
This proves the moreover part, and in particular that the notions of
\kappa
-compact objects in the sense of
\infty
-categories and of well-generated categories agree at least inside of a
compactly
generated category, as claimed.
∎

A triangulated category

\textstyle{\mathscr{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\Ab}

Appendix B
Comparing big and countable stratifications

In this appendix we show how to deduce countable stratification from the ‘usual’ stratification in settings where both stratification statements (via usual or countable Balmer–Favi supports) make sense.
Note that our proof is purely tensor-triangulated, in particular it does not require any
\infty
-categorical models.

B.1 Proposition
.

Let
\iota\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
be a functor of tensor-triangulated categories having the following properties:

(1)

\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is rigidly-compactly generated (
Definition

2.6
).

(2)

\mathscr{T}
is rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated (
Definition

2.9
).

(3)

The functor
\iota
is fully faithful, exact, symmetric monoidal and preserves all countable coproducts.

(4)

The restriction of
\iota
to rigid (
i.e.
compact
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
) objects of
\mathscr{T}
and rigid (
i.e.
compact) objects of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is an equivalence of tt-categories:
\iota\colon\mathscr{T}^{c}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c}
.

(5)

The topological space
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})\cong\Spc(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c})
is weakly noetherian.

Then if the big tt-category
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is stratified in the sense of
[
BHS23
]
, it follows that
\mathscr{T}
is stratified in the sense of
Construction

4.14
.
More precisely, if
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is stratified then we have a commutative diagram of bijections

(B.2)

\textstyle{\{\textrm{localizing${}_{\aleph_{1}}$\! $\otimes$-ideals of }\mathscr{T}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\iota(-)\,\rangle}
\textstyle{\{\textrm{localizing $\otimes$-ideals of }\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\iota^{-1}}

where the vertical ones are the stratification bijections

via the Balmer–Favi supports and their countable version (

4.14
), and the bottom horizontal one is via the homeomorphism
\Spc(\iota)=\iota^{-1}\colon\Spc(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c})\overset{\sim}{\to}\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})

B.3 Remark
.

By replacing

with its image

in

Nonetheless, we will preserve both notations

and

B.4 Example
.

Suppose
\mathscr{S}
is a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category such that its subcategory
\mathscr{S}^{c}
of rigid-compacts admits a countable skeleton (one with countably many objects and arrows).
Let
\mathscr{T}
be the localizing
{}_{\aleph_{1}}

\mathscr{S}
generated by
\mathscr{S}^{c}

Then the inclusion functor
\iota\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}=:\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
satisfies all the hypotheses of the proposition.
Indeed, the only non-evident part of the claim is whether
\mathscr{T}(C,X)
is countable for every
C\in\mathscr{T}^{c}
and

(in part (2)), but this can be verified by an inductive argument starting with the countability assumption on
\mathscr{S}^{c}=\mathscr{T}^{c}

Proof of
Proposition

B.1
.

By hypothesis
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is stratified,
i.e.
we have the right vertical bijection in the diagram (
B.2
).
We also have the bottom bijection, as already noted in the statement.
To prove the proposition, we are first going to show that the square (
B.2
) (with the right-going top arrow) is commutative and then we will show that the top two arrows are mutually inverses; this will provide the claimed left vertical bijection.

We also assumed that
\mathscr{T}
is rigidly-compactly
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
generated.
In particular, for every tt-prime
\mathscr{P}\in\Spec(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c})
we may construct the countable version
g(\iota^{-1}\mathscr{P})\in\mathscr{T}
of the Balmer–Favi idempotent for the corresponding tt-prime
\iota^{-1}\mathscr{P}
of
\mathscr{T}^{c}
.

We need to compare the countable and the ordinary Balmer–Favi supports:

B.5 Lemma
.

We have
\iota(g(\iota^{-1}\mathscr{P}))\cong g(\mathscr{P})
for every prime
\mathscr{P}\in\Spc(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c})
.

Proof.

Consider the idempotent triangle
e_{Y}\to\mathbbm{1}\to f_{Y}\to\Sigma e_{Y}
of
\mathscr{T}
associated to a Thomason subset
Y\subseteq\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
.
After applying the tt-functor
\iota
we obtain an idempotent triangle

\iota e_{Y}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{1}\longrightarrow\iota f_{Y}\longrightarrow\Sigma\iota e_{Y}

in
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
.
On the other hand, we also have the idempotent triangle

e_{\tilde{Y}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{1}\longrightarrow f_{\tilde{Y}}\longrightarrow\Sigma e_{\tilde{Y}}

of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
associated with the Thomason subset
\tilde{Y}:=\Spc(\iota)^{-1}(Y)
of
\Spc(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c})
.

We claim that these two triangles are isomorphic.
For this, it will suffice to show that the (full) images of the two left idempotents agree,

\iota e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}=e_{\tilde{Y}}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}},

because the latter localizing subcategories of
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
determine the idempotent triangles up to a (unique) isomorphism.
By construction we have
e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{Y}\,\rangle_{\aleph_{1}}
in
\mathscr{T}
and
e_{\tilde{Y}}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\,\rangle
in
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
, where
\mathscr{C}_{Y}\subseteq\mathscr{T}^{c}
and
\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\subseteq\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c}
are the thick tensor ideals of compacts supported on
Y
and
\tilde{Y}
, respectively.
Thus we need to show that

\iota e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\,\rangle\quad\textrm{ in }\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}.

Note that
\iota(\mathscr{C}_{Y})=\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}
since
\iota
identifies compacts by hypothesis (4).

To prove the inclusion
\subseteq
, note that
\iota e_{Y}\in\iota(\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{Y}\,\rangle_{\aleph_{1}})\subseteq\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{C}_{Y}\,\rangle=\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\,\rangle
and thus, since the latter is a localizing tensor ideal, also
\iota e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}\subseteq\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\,\rangle
.

For the reverse inclusion
\supseteq
, use
\iota(\mathscr{C}_{Y})=\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}
again and then apply the coproducts preserving tt-functor
\iota
to the equality
\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{Y}\,\rangle_{\aleph_{1}}=e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}
to get the inclusion

\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\subseteq\iota(\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{Y}\,\rangle_{\aleph_{1}})=\iota e_{Y}\otimes\iota(\mathscr{T})\subseteq\iota e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}

and therefore, since the latter is a localizing subcategory, also

\loc\langle\,\mathscr{C}_{\tilde{Y}}\,\rangle\subseteq\iota e_{Y}\otimes\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c}.

This proves the claimed equality of localizing subcategories and therefore of the two idempotent triangles.

Now to prove the lemma, choose Thomason subsets
Y_{1},Y_{2}
in
\Spc(\mathscr{T}^{c})
such that
\{\iota^{-1}\mathscr{P}\}=Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}^{c}
and deduce from the above observation that

\iota(g(\iota^{-1}\mathscr{P}))=\iota(e_{Y_{1}}\otimes f_{Y_{2}})=\iota(e_{Y_{1}})\otimes\iota(f_{Y_{2}})\cong e_{\tilde{Y}_{1}}\otimes f_{\tilde{Y}_{2}}=g(\mathscr{P})

as claimed, since
\{\mathscr{P}\}=\tilde{Y}_{1}\cap\tilde{Y}_{2}^{c}
(with notations
\tilde{Y}_{i}=\Spc(\iota)^{-1}(Y_{i})
as before).
∎

\supp(\iota A)=\Spc(\iota)^{-1}(\supp(A))

For every prime

.

It remains to show
\iota^{-1}(\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle)\subseteq\mathscr{L}
.
By
Lemma

B.8
, we have
\loc_{\otimes}\!\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle=\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\otimes\iota\mathscr{T}^{c}\,\rangle=\loc\langle\,\iota(\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathscr{T}^{c})\,\rangle=\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle
, the latter equality because
\mathscr{L}
is a tensor ideal of
\mathscr{T}
.
We are thus reduced to proving that
\iota^{-1}(\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle)\subseteq\mathscr{L}
.

The latter claim is non obvious. In order to prove it, we will appeal to Neeman’s theory of well-generated triangulated categories (
[
Nee10
]
), as presented in
[
Kra10
]
. For the remainder of the proof we will adopt the terminology of
loc. cit.

By hypothesis, the triangulated category
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is compactly generated, that is
\aleph_{0}
-well generated. Since compact (
i.e.

\aleph_{0}
-compact) objects are also
\aleph_{1}
-compact,
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is also
\aleph_{1}
-well generated with
\iota\mathscr{T}^{c}=\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}^{c}
as an (essentially) small set of
\aleph_{1}
-compact generators.
Thus by
[
Kra10
, Cor. 7.2.2]
its full subcategory
(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}})^{\aleph_{1}}
of all
\aleph_{1}
-compact objects in
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is precisely the
\aleph_{1}
-localizing (
=
localizing
{}_{\aleph_{1}}
) subcategory generated by
\iota\mathscr{T}^{c}
, that is,
\iota\mathscr{T}
.
Incidentally, it also follows that the subcategory
\iota\mathscr{L}\subseteq\iota\mathscr{T}
consists of
\aleph_{1}
-compact objects.
Therefore by
[
Kra10
, Thm. 7.2.1]
, the localizing subcategory
\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}}
is itself an
\aleph_{1}
-well generated triangulated category, and its full subcategory
(\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle)^{\aleph_{1}}
of
\aleph_{1}
-compact objects equals
\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle\cap(\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{big}})^{\aleph_{1}}
, that is, by the above discussion,
\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle\cap\iota\mathscr{T}
.

But another application of
[
Kra10
, Cor. 7.2.2]
implies that
(\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle)^{\aleph_{1}}
is also the
\aleph_{1}
-localizing subcategory generated by
\iota\mathscr{L}
, which is
\iota\mathscr{L}
itself.
We conclude that
\loc\langle\,\iota\mathscr{L}\,\rangle\cap\iota\mathscr{T}=\iota\mathscr{L}
, as claimed.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of
Proposition

B.1
.
By Lemmas
B.10
and
B.9
, the top horizontal arrows in the square (
B.2
) are mutually inverse bijections.
By
Lemma

B.7
the square is commutative, and we deduce that the left vertical arrow is a bijection, since the other three are. In other words,
\mathscr{T}
is countably stratified.
∎

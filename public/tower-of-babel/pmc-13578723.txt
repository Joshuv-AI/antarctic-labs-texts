Validated stability indicating reverse phase liquid chromatography method for oxcarbazepine and its degradation products with greenness evaluation
Authors: Aktham Mestareehi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Oxcarbazepine is a widely prescribed anticonvulsant used in the management of epilepsy through modulation of abnormal neuronal excitability. The objective of the present study was to synthesize oxcarbazepine in the laboratory and develop a reliable, stability-indicating, and environmentally conscious reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) method for its quality assessment, including the quantification of impurities and degradation products. Oxcarbazepine was synthesized and characterized using infrared spectroscopy (IR), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) techniques. Chromatographic separation was achieved using a Supelco Discovery C18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 µm) with a mobile phase consisting of 75% potassium phosphate monobasic buffer (pH 5.0) and 25% acetonitrile at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. Detection was performed at 257 nm, and oxcarbazepine exhibited a retention time of approximately 11 min. The developed RP-HPLC method was validated according to ICH, USP, and FDA guidelines. Forced degradation studies were conducted under acidic, basic, oxidative, thermal, and photolytic conditions to evaluate the stability-indicating capability of the method. In addition, the environmental impact of the analytical procedure was assessed using Green Analytical Chemistry (GAC) metrics, including the Analytical Eco-Scale, GAPI, and AGREE tools. The developed method demonstrated excellent specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, robustness, and sensitivity. All degradation products were well resolved from the parent oxcarbazepine peak, confirming the stability-indicating nature of the method. Excellent linearity was observed over the concentration range of 0.24–2000 ppm with a correlation coefficient (R2) of 0.9995. Accuracy values ranged from 99.4% to 101.5%, while the limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were 0.3 ppm and 0.9 ppm, respectively, indicating high sensitivity suitable for trace-level analysis. Robustness studies confirmed the reliability of the method under deliberate variations in chromatographic conditions. Furthermore, greenness assessment revealed an Analytical Eco-Scale score of approximately 75, a GAPI pictogram with predominantly green and yellow zones, and an AGREE score of 0.57/1.0, indicating moderate environmental friendliness. Overall, the validated RP-HPLC method provides a reliable, precise, sensitive, stability-indicating, and environmentally conscious analytical approach for routine quality control, impurity profiling, and stability studies of oxcarbazepine in raw materials and pharmaceutical formulations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67977-x.

Introduction

Epilepsy represents one of the most prevalent chronic neurological conditions, affecting an estimated 50 million individuals globally. Despite substantial therapeutic advances over the past three decades, epilepsy continues to rank among the leading contributors to the global burden of disease, reflecting its significant impact on morbidity, quality of life, and healthcare systems worldwide1. The pharmacological landscape for seizure management has expanded considerably, with nearly 30 antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) currently available for clinical use. More than two-thirds of these agents were introduced following the development of Carbamazepine (CBZ), and approximately one-third entered the market after its structural derivative, Oxcarbazepine (OXC)2. This progressive diversification of AEDs reflects ongoing efforts to enhance efficacy, reduce adverse effects, and improve tolerability profiles in patients requiring long-term seizure control.

Oxcarbazepine is a second-generation anticonvulsant agent primarily indicated for the management of partial-onset seizures in patients with epilepsy3. In addition to its antiepileptic properties, it is frequently employed as a mood-stabilizing agent in certain affective and anxiety-related disorders4. The compound is commercially available under the trade name Trileptal and is manufactured by Novartis5. The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) name of oxcarbazepine is 10,11-dihydro-10-oxo-5H-dibenz [b, f] azepine-5-carboxamide. In its pure state, it is obtained as a crystalline solid with a color ranging from white to pale orange5. The compound exhibits limited solubility in chloroform, dichloromethane, acetone, and methanol, and is practically insoluble in ethanol, diethyl ether, and water. Oxcarbazepine possesses a molecular formula of C₁₅H₁₂N₂O₂ and a molecular weight of 252.27 g·mol⁻1. Its melting point has been reported within the range of 218–224 °C4,5. Oxcarbazepine, structurally characterized as the 10-keto analog of Carbamazepine, it demonstrates important pharmacological distinctions from its parent compound6. Notably, oxcarbazepine is associated with comparatively lower hepatic enzyme induction and a modified metabolic pathway, resulting in clinically relevant differences in drug–drug interaction potential and tolerability7,8. Since its regulatory approval in 2000, oxcarbazepine has been widely incorporated into therapeutic regimens for seizure management owing to its established efficacy and generally favorable safety profile across diverse patient populations7. Trileptal is an antiepileptic formulation of oxcarbazepine intended for oral administration. It is supplied as film-coated tablets in strengths of 150 mg, 300 mg, and 600 mg5. In addition, it is available as an oral suspension at a concentration of 300 mg per 5 mL (equivalent to 60 mg/mL), providing a flexible dosing option for patients who require individualized dose adjustments or have difficulty swallowing solid dosage forms5,9. The film-coated tablets contain several excipients, including colloidal silicon dioxide, crospovidone, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, iron oxide, magnesium stearate, microcrystalline cellulose, polyethylene glycol, talc, and titanium dioxide5. The oral suspension formulation incorporates ascorbic acid, dispersible cellulose, ethanol, macrogol stearate, methyl parahydroxybenzoate, propylene glycol, propyl parahydroxybenzoate, purified water, sodium saccharin, sorbic acid, sorbitol, and a yellow-plum-lemon flavoring agent to enhance palatability and stability5.

The mechanism of action of Oxcarbazepine involves modulation of voltage-gated sodium channels, thereby stabilizing hyperexcited neuronal membranes and suppressing high-frequency repetitive firing. In addition, it reduces synaptic transmission by inhibiting glutamate release, further contributing to seizure control4. Following oral administration, oxcarbazepine undergoes rapid hepatic biotransformation to its pharmacologically active 10-monohydroxy derivative, licarbazepine (also referred to as the monohydroxy metabolite, MHD). This metabolite is principally responsible for the drug’s antiseizure activity4,5. In patients with compromised liver function, including those with HCC or cirrhosis, alterations in metabolic capacity may lead to changes in drug exposure and an increased risk of adverse effects10,11. The parent compound has a relatively short elimination half-life, ranging from approximately 1 to 3.7 h, whereas licarbazepine demonstrates a longer half-life of approximately 8 to 10 h3,4. Unlike Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine does not exhibit autoinduction of its own metabolism. Elimination occurs predominantly via renal excretion of metabolites.

Oxcarbazepine is characterized as a weak inducer of cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4), an enzyme involved in estrogen metabolism. Consequently, high doses may reduce the effectiveness of estrogen-containing oral contraceptives12. It also acts as a weak inhibitor of CYP2C19 and at elevated doses, may increase plasma concentrations of phenytoin. In contrast to carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine is not significantly affected by CYP3A4 inhibitors12. Several patient-specific factors influence pharmacokinetics. Body weight and concomitant administration of enzyme-inducing agents can alter drug clearance. Pediatric patients typically demonstrate higher clearance rates than adults, often necessitating increased weight-adjusted dosing to achieve therapeutic plasma concentrations13. Similarly, co-administration with hepatic enzyme inducers may enhance clearance, requiring dose modification to maintain optimal seizure control14.

Hyponatremia is defined as a reduced sodium concentration in the blood, typically below 125 mmol/L. Data from 14 controlled epilepsy studies indicate that approximately 2.5% of patients treated with Trileptal (38 out of 1,524) experienced sodium levels under 125 mmol/L. Hyponatremia most commonly occurs within the first three months of Oxcarbazepine therapy; however, in some cases, it may develop after more than one year of treatment. In most patients who developed hyponatremia, the Oxcarbazepine dose was either reduced or discontinued. Importantly, sodium levels returned to normal within a few days without the need for additional intervention15.

A review of the literature reveals that multiple liquid chromatography techniques have been reported for quantifying Oxcarbazepine in pharmaceutical formulations. In one study, separation was performed using a Diamonsil C18 column, with a mobile phase composed of acetonitrile, potassium phosphate monobasic buffer (pH 6.8), and water in a 36:8:56 (v/v) ratio, at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. Detection was carried out at 255 nm, corresponding to the maximum absorbance of Oxcarbazepine. The method demonstrated linearity over the concentration range of 9.96–99.6 μg/mL and was validated for parameters including selectivity, precision, accuracy, and limits of detection and quantitation16. In a separate report, R. Prashanthi investigated the forced degradation of Oxcarbazepine using a stability-indicating RP-HPLC method compatible with LC–MS analysis. This approach employed a Phenomenex C18 column (150 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 µm particle size) at ambient temperature. The optimized mobile phase consisted of methanol and 0.02% formic acid in a 50:50 (v/v) ratio, with a flow rate of 1 mL/min, and detection was performed at 229 nm. The drug was subjected to various stress conditions, including hydrolytic, oxidative, thermal, and photolytic challenges, and the resulting samples were successfully analyzed using the developed method17. A detailed comparison with previously reported RP-HPLC methods highlights several advantages of the proposed approach. Earlier methods often exhibit limitations such as narrower linearity ranges, longer run times, higher detection limits, and lack of comprehensive validation. In contrast, the present method combines wide linearity, high sensitivity, excellent precision, and full validation in accordance with ICH guidelines. Additionally, the method introduces green chemistry evaluation, which is rarely addressed in earlier studies. Simplified sample preparation (dilution only) further enhances its practicality and reduces potential sources of analytical error.

Reliable stability testing requires analytical methods that accurately quantify the API without interference from impurities or degradation products. Despite extensive studies on Oxcarbazepine, laboratory-scale synthesis remains underexplored. This study focuses on optimizing Oxcarbazepine synthesis for maximum yield and on developing a validated, stability-indicating RP-HPLC method to assess the compound and its potential impurities. The developed method will undergo full validation in accordance with ICH and FDA regulatory guidelines to confirm its suitability for routine quality control and stability assessment. This validation ensures that the procedure meets international standards for accuracy, precision, and reliability. Ultimately, the proposed method aims to serve as a robust analytical approach for evaluating the stability of Oxcarbazepine, thereby maintaining its potency, safety, and therapeutic effectiveness throughout its storage and shelf life.

Results

Method development & optimization

The primary objective of this work was to develop a robust, precise, and selective RP-HPLC method for the quantitative determination of oxcarbazepine in its bulk form. An additional key aim was to ensure efficient resolution of the drug from its related impurities and potential degradation products without the need for supplementary purification procedures. A comprehensive optimization of chromatographic conditions was performed by systematically evaluating critical experimental parameters. Particular attention was given to achieving high column efficiency (evidenced by an increased number of theoretical plates), well-defined and symmetrical peak profiles, minimal peak tailing during raw material analysis, and effective separation of analytes from interfering species. The method development process involved multiple iterative trials to fine-tune analytical conditions, ultimately leading to a method that delivers reliable, accurate, and reproducible results, suitable for routine quality control applications18.

Determination of the wavelength of maximum absorbance

To determine the most suitable detection wavelength, UV spectral scans were performed for standard solutions of Oxcarbazepine across a wavelength range of 200–400 nm, using the buffer solution as a reference blank. The UV spectra showed a distinct absorption maximum at 257 nm. Each drug sample was finely pulverized, uniformly mixed with KBr powder, and compressed under high pressure to produce a clear, stable pellet. During compression, the KBr matrix encapsulates the active compound, forming a thin, translucent disk suitable for infrared (IR) spectroscopy. The prepared pellets were placed in the spectrometer’s sample compartment for scanning. The resulting IR spectra, presented in Fig. S1 (Supplementary Material), displays the distinctive absorption bands corresponding to the functional groups of both Oxcarbazepine USP and the synthesized compound. The IR spectra of Oxcarbazepine USP and synthesized Oxcarbazepine display nearly identical absorption bands, confirming that the synthesized compound possesses the same functional groups and structural features as the reference standard. Minor differences in wavenumber or peak intensity are likely due to variations in crystal packing, pellet preparation, or instrumental sensitivity. This consistency provides strong evidence for the successful synthesis and structural equivalence of Oxcarbazepine. 1H-NMR analysis

Proton Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (1H-NMR) spectroscopy was employed to obtain comprehensive structural information on Oxcarbazepine. Both the synthesized compound and the reference material were analyzed using an NMR spectrophotometer to confirm structural equivalence. Approximately 30 mg of Oxcarbazepine reference material was accurately weighed and transferred into an NMR tube. A sufficient volume of deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO-d₆) containing Tetramethylsilane (TMS) as the internal standard was added, and the mixture was gently swirled until complete dissolution was achieved. The prepared tube was then placed in the NMR spectrometer, and the spectrum was recorded. The same experimental protocol was applied to the synthesized Oxcarbazepine sample. Both the USP and synthesized oxcarbazepine spectra display identical proton environments, confirming that the synthesized compound shares the same structure as the reference standard. Minor chemical shift variations (less than 0.05 ppm) are attributed to differences in solvent, sample concentration, or instrument calibration. The absence of additional peaks indicates high purity and successful synthesis of Oxcarbazepine. Column selection

Four C18 columns were conditioned using a sequential flushing protocol with acetonitrile (ACN)–water mixtures (50:50 and 75:25, v/v), followed by 100% ACN. Each solvent was passed through the column for 30 min at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min to ensure proper equilibration. The procedure followed ICH Q2(R2) guidelines19, targeting acceptable system suitability parameters, including tailing factors (0.9–2.0) and theoretical plate counts (> 2000). Each column was evaluated in duplicate, with intermediate washing and re-equilibration to avoid carryover. All analyses were conducted at ambient temperature. Four different C18 columns were evaluated under identical chromatographic conditions to determine the most suitable one for analysis. Column 2 exhibited a tailing factor of 0.880, which fell outside the acceptable range specified by ICH guidelines20. In contrast, Columns 1, 3, and 4 demonstrated satisfactory theoretical plate counts and acceptable tailing factors, indicating better column efficiency and symmetry. Among these, Column 3 produced the most favorable performance, with a tailing factor of 1.056 and 6648 theoretical plates, surpassing the others in overall chromatographic quality. Therefore, Column 3 (Supelco Discovery C18, 4.6 × 250 nm, 5µ) met the ICH acceptance criteria and was selected for all subsequent analytical experiments as presented in Table S1 (Supplementary Material).

Selection pH of the mobile phase

Accurate pH adjustment is a critical factor in RP-HPLC, as it minimizes unwanted interactions with silica-based stationary phases that can lead to peak tailing and compromised resolution21. In this study, oxcarbazepine was evaluated at pH 2.9, 5.0, and 7.0 using a mobile phase composed of buffer (phase A) and acetonitrile (phase B) in a 75:25 (v/v) ratio. As shown in Table S2 (Supplementary Material), the condition at pH 5.0 yielded the most favorable chromatographic performance, characterized by improved peak symmetry and the highest number of theoretical plates, reflecting enhanced column efficiency. Based on these findings, a potassium dihydrogen phosphate buffer adjusted to pH 5.0 was selected for subsequent method optimization and validation.

Isocratic elution studies

Various proportions of phosphate buffer (pH 5.0) and acetonitrile (ACN) were systematically investigated to obtain a retention time of approximately 10 min, ensuring sufficient resolution of potential impurities and degradation products prior to elution of the target analyte. A standard solution of oxcarbazepine (1500 ppm) was analyzed under each condition. As depicted in Fig. S2 (Supplementary Material), the initial chromatographic conditions (50:50 buffer: ACN, v/v) resulted in suboptimal peak symmetry, with a tailing factor of 1.477 and a short retention time of 3.657 min, indicating significant secondary interactions with the stationary phase. Detailed chromatographic parameters, including retention times, peak areas, and tailing factors, are provided in Table S3 (Supplementary Material). Subsequent optimization of the mobile phase composition revealed that a 25:75 (v/v) ACN-to-buffer ratio provided optimal performance, producing a retention time close to 10 min along with improved peak shape and enhanced separation of impurities and degradation products.

To define the working concentration range for oxcarbazepine, calibration curves were constructed within the detector’s linear dynamic range to ensure accurate quantification and minimize analytical bias. A stock solution (3500 ppm) was serially diluted to obtain calibration standards spanning 0.24–2000 ppm. These standards were analyzed under the optimized HPLC conditions, and the corresponding peak areas (Table 3) demonstrated a strong linear relationship between concentration and detector response. The method exhibited excellent linearity over the concentration range of 0.24–2000 ppm, with a coefficient of determination (R2 = 0.9995), as illustrated in Fig. 5. Forced degradation studies

Forced degradation studies are essential for characterizing the intrinsic stability profile of pharmaceutical compounds. This strategy involves exposing the drug substance to a range of stress conditions to elucidate degradation pathways and detect potential degradation products. In addition to assessing stability, these studies contribute to establishing the specificity of the analytical method, particularly for the identification of impurities and degradation-related species. The applied stress parameters; including reagent concentration, exposure duration, and experimental conditions were carefully selected to achieve controlled degradation (≤ 10%) without complete decomposition of the analyte, in accordance with ICH Q1A (R2) guidelines22. This approach ensures reliable detection of impurities and supports the overall quality, safety, and efficacy of pharmaceutical products.

In this study, acidic, alkaline, oxidative, thermal, and photolytic stress conditions were applied to evaluate the stability of oxcarbazepine, with detailed experimental conditions provided in the Supplementary Material and Figs. S3–S7 (Supplementary Material). Under thermal (75 °C, 24 h) and photolytic (257 nm, 24 h) conditions, oxcarbazepine showed minimal degradation (2.9% and 2.3%, respectively), indicating good stability toward heat and UV exposure. In contrast, acidic conditions produced concentration and time dependent degradation. Severe degradation was observed at higher acid strength, with 93.3% degradation in 6 M HCl, decreasing to 37.6% (3 M) and 29.8% (1 M) over 24 h. Shorter exposure (30 min, 1 M HCl) resulted in only 7.8% degradation, demonstrating that both acid strength and exposure time significantly influence degradation rate. A similar but more pronounced trend was observed under alkaline conditions, where oxcarbazepine exhibited extreme sensitivity to base hydrolysis. Nearly complete degradation occurred at 6 M (98.7%) and 3 M NaOH (97.3%), while even 1 M NaOH caused substantial degradation (78.9%) after 24 h. Mild conditions (0.001 M NaOH, 30 min) resulted in only 6.1% degradation, confirming strong base-dependent instability. Under oxidative stress, oxcarbazepine showed high susceptibility, with complete degradation (100%) in 30% H₂O₂ and significant degradation (69.2%) in 3% H₂O₂ after 24 h. However, milder oxidative conditions (2% H₂O₂, 30 min) led to only 6.3% degradation, again emphasizing the impact of oxidant concentration and exposure time. Method validation

To ensure adherence to GLP and GMP requirements, the analytical procedure was validated in accordance with International Council for Harmonisation Q2A/Q2B20, U.S. Food and Drug Administration23, and United States Pharmacopeia24 recommendations. The validation process covered critical performance characteristics, including system suitability to verify consistent chromatographic operation prior to sample analysis. Specificity was evaluated to confirm effective separation of oxcarbazepine from related impurities and degradation products. Robustness testing examined the influence of slight variations in pH and mobile phase composition on method performance. Solution stability studies ensured that the analytes remained stable throughout the analytical timeframe. Linearity and working range were established to demonstrate reliable quantification over the intended concentration intervals. Accuracy and precision assessments included repeatability, injection reproducibility, and inter-analyst consistency. Additionally, the limits of detection (LOD) and quantification (LOQ) were determined to define the method’s sensitivity. Overall, the validation results confirmed that the method is dependable and appropriate for routine analytical applications.

System suitability

System suitability testing (SST) was performed in accordance with ICH Q2(R1)20 to verify proper HPLC system performance prior to analysis. Acceptance criteria were defined as follows: %RSD of replicate injections ≤ 1.0%, %RSD of retention times ≤ 1.0%, theoretical plates ≥ 2000, tailing factor between 0.9–2.0, capacity factor > 2, resolution > 2, and drift ≤ 2%. Two oxcarbazepine working standard solutions (1500 ppm), WS #1 and WS #2, were prepared. WS #1 was injected six times consecutively, and WS #2 was injected twice as seen in Table S4 (Supplementary Material). The %RSD of peak areas and retention times was calculated to confirm injection repeatability and retention consistency. Tailing factors and theoretical plate count were determined to evaluate peak symmetry and column efficiency. Method precision

Precision in analytical method validation reflects the method’s ability to produce consistent results under defined conditions. These assessments confirm that the method delivers accurate, reliable, and reproducible data under routine and variable conditions. According to ICH guidelines16, precision is assessed at three levels:

Repeatability (method precision)

Repeatability evaluates the method’s short-term consistency by assessing variability from repeated sample preparation and analysis by the same analyst under identical conditions22. This assessment reflects the method’s robustness to minor operational fluctuations, ensuring reliable and reproducible results in routine use. Regulatory guidelines require that the %Relative Standard Deviation (%RSD) of peak areas for the target compound does not exceed 1% (NMT 1%)23. In this study, repeatability was determined by preparing and injecting six independent oxcarbazepine samples under the validated HPLC conditions. The %RSD of the resulting peak areas confirmed the method’s precision and adherence to the acceptance criteria, as summarized in Table 12.

According to established acceptance criteria, the %Relative Standard Deviation (%RSD) of peak areas for oxcarbazepine (1500 ppm) must not exceed 1%, ensuring high reproducibility. To evaluate this, a single preparation of each compound was injected six consecutive times under the optimized HPLC conditions. Discussion

Oxcarbazepine (OXC) is a second-generation antiepileptic drug developed as a structural derivative of carbamazepine (CBZ). It retains a comparable therapeutic profile while offering an improved safety profile and a lower incidence of adverse effects4,15. Clinically, OXC is widely used in the treatment of partial and generalized seizures5. Its mechanism of action involves the blockade of voltage-gated sodium channels, which stabilizes hyperexcited neuronal membranes, suppresses repetitive neuronal firing, and reduces synaptic transmission12. Pharmacokinetically, OXC is rapidly absorbed following oral administration and is extensively metabolized to its active metabolite, the 10-monohydroxy derivative (MHD). It exhibits high bioavailability, low plasma protein binding, and is primarily eliminated via renal excretion12. Compared to CBZ, OXC shows reduced enzyme induction, thereby minimizing drug–drug interactions and improving its clinical applicability7.

Despite these advantages, the presence of process-related impurities and potential degradation products can significantly affect the safety, efficacy, and stability of oxcarbazepine in both bulk drug substances and pharmaceutical formulations. Consequently, the development of a reliable, sensitive, and stability-indicating analytical method is essential for comprehensive quality assessment26. Although several analytical methods have been reported for the determination of oxcarbazepine, most are limited to assay quantification and lack comprehensive evaluation of impurities and degradation behavior. Furthermore, many existing methods do not fully comply with current ICH guidelines regarding sensitivity, robustness, and stability-indicating capability as seen in Table S6 (Supplementary material). Limitations such as extended run times, insufficient resolution of closely related compounds, and restricted applicability in routine quality control have also been reported. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to develop and validate a simple, rapid, sensitive, and robust reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) method for the simultaneous determination of oxcarbazepine and its related impurities, including degradation products. The method was validated in accordance with ICH guidelines to ensure its suitability for routine quality control, stability studies, and regulatory applications27.

The comparison presented in Table S6 (Supplementary material) demonstrates that previously reported RP-HPLC methods for oxcarbazepine exhibit acceptable analytical performance; however, several limitations remain16,17,19,28,29. For instance, earlier methods often operate within relatively narrow linearity ranges (e.g., 5–150 µg/mL) and may involve longer chromatographic run times of up to 20 min. Additionally, sensitivity is limited in some methods, with relatively high LOD and LOQ values, while others require complex sample preparation, particularly for biological matrices. In contrast, the proposed method demonstrates clear improvements. It provides excellent linearity (R2 > 0.999), enhanced sensitivity with lower detection and quantitation limits, and significantly shorter run time (~ 10 min), improving analytical efficiency. The method also achieves superior precision (%RSD < 1%) and highly consistent recovery values (99–101.5%), indicating improved accuracy compared to reported methods. Furthermore, unlike many existing methods, the proposed approach combines full stability-indicating capability, robustness testing, and green analytical chemistry evaluation, making it more comprehensive. The simplified sample preparation (dilution only) further enhances reproducibility and minimizes potential sources of error. Overall, these improvements explain why the proposed RP-HPLC method offers superior accuracy and precision while maintaining compliance with ICH guidelines, making it more suitable for routine quality control and stability studies.

System suitability results demonstrated excellent chromatographic performance and reproducibility. For Standard 1, retention time and peak area showed low variability, with %RSD values of 0.31% and 0.14%, respectively, and minimal drift (0.18%), indicating stable system performance. Tailing factors (1.207–1.222) confirmed symmetrical peak shapes, while theoretical plate counts (~ 6957–7072) reflected good column efficiency. Similarly, Standard 2 exhibited even lower variability, with %RSD values of 0.09% for retention time and 0.08% for peak area, further supporting method precision. Consistent tailing factors and plate counts across injections demonstrate the robustness and reliability of the chromatographic system, confirming compliance with system suitability requirements for routine analysis20.

Forced degradation studies demonstrated that oxcarbazepine exhibits condition-dependent stability, with minimal degradation under thermal (2.9%) and photolytic (2.3%) stress, indicating relative resistance to heat and light. In contrast, significant degradation was observed under acidic, alkaline, and oxidative conditions, confirming the susceptibility of the molecule to hydrolytic and oxidative pathways. Under acidic conditions, degradation increased with acid strength and exposure time, reaching 93.3% in 6 M HCl after 24 h. This behavior is attributed to acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of the amide functional group, leading to ring opening and formation of structurally related impurities. Alkaline degradation was more pronounced, with near-complete degradation observed in 6 M and 3 M NaOH (98.7% and 97.3%, respectively). This suggests that oxcarbazepine is highly susceptible to base-catalyzed hydrolysis, likely involving nucleophilic attack on the amide linkage, resulting in rapid breakdown into polar degradation products. Oxidative stress produced the highest degradation, achieving 100% degradation in 30% H₂O₂. This is likely due to oxidation of the dibenzazepine moiety and conversion to N-oxide or other oxidized derivatives, reflecting the compound’s high sensitivity to oxidative environments. Overall, the degradation pattern indicates that hydrolysis (acidic and alkaline) and oxidation are the primary degradation pathways of oxcarbazepine, whereas thermal and photolytic conditions have minimal impact. The ability of the developed RP-HPLC method to effectively separate the parent compound from its degradation products confirms its stability-indicating nature and suitability for routine quality control and stability studies.

The chromatographic method efficiently resolved and quantified oxcarbazepine, with all peaks exhibiting excellent resolution, thereby meeting the required specificity criteria for analytical separation. The peak purity index for each analyte surpassed the established threshold, confirming the absence of co-eluting substances or interfering components. These results demonstrate that the developed method can accurately isolate and quantify oxcarbazepine even in the presence of degradation products and related impurities. The high peak purity factor (≥ 999.99), resolution values (RS ≥ 2.0), and three-dimensional chromatographic profiles collectively underscore the method’s robustness and compliance with ICH and FDA validation guidelines20.

Robustness testing further confirmed the method’s reliability, as key chromatographic parameters remained consistent under deliberate minor variations, including changes in solvent composition, buffer pH, flow rate, detection wavelength, and injection volume. Tailing factors were within the acceptable range (0.9–2.0), and theoretical plate counts exceeded 2000, reflecting adequate column efficiency. Overall, these results confirm that the method is both specific and robust, making it suitable for routine quality control and stability studies.

Solution stability was evaluated by analyzing a freshly prepared oxcarbazepine solution using the RP-HPLC system, followed by reinjection of the same solution after 24, 48, and 72 h. Over this time period, the chromatographic profiles remained consistent, with no emergence of additional peaks or loss of existing peaks, indicating the absence of degradation. The percentage changes in peak area for oxcarbazepine were minimal, recorded as 0.117%, 0.398%, and 0.688% at 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively. These negligible variations confirm the excellent short-term stability of the analyte in solution. Furthermore, the consistent peak responses demonstrate the reliability and robustness of the developed RP-HPLC method, ensuring accurate quantification without analyte degradation or loss during the analysis period. Additionally, the linearity of the method was evaluated by preparing five different concentrations of standard solutions and injecting each into the HPLC system. The corresponding peak areas were plotted against concentration to generate calibration curves. Linear regression analysis demonstrated excellent correlation, with a coefficient of determination (R2) of 0.9992 for oxcarbazepine. These results meet the established acceptance criteria for linearity, confirming that the method provides a direct and proportional response over the studied concentration range. Overall, the findings validate the method’s suitability for accurate and reliable quantitative determination of the active pharmaceutical ingredient.

The accuracy of the developed RP-HPLC method was evaluated by analyzing samples at three concentration levels (1200, 1500, and 1800 ppm), each in triplicate. The percentage recoveries obtained were 101.5%, 99.5%, and 99.4% for the respective concentration levels. These values fall well within the generally accepted recovery range of 98–102% for assay methods, indicating a high degree of accuracy. At 1200 ppm, the slightly higher recovery (101.5%) suggests a minor positive deviation; however, it remains within acceptable limits and does not indicate any systematic error. At higher concentrations (1500 and 1800 ppm), the recoveries were very close to 100%, demonstrating excellent agreement between the measured and true values. The consistency in recovery across the tested concentration range confirms that the method is not influenced by concentration-dependent bias. Furthermore, the close agreement among replicate peak areas at each level reflects good method repeatability, which supports the reliability of the accuracy results. The absence of significant variation also indicates that sample preparation and instrumental response are well controlled. Overall, these findings confirm that the proposed RP-HPLC method is accurate and suitable for the quantitative determination of the analyte in bulk and pharmaceutical formulations.

The precision of the developed RP-HPLC method was assessed at three levels: repeatability (method precision), injection precision (system precision), and intermediate precision. The results demonstrate a high degree of consistency in peak area responses, confirming the reliability of the method. For method precision (repeatability), six independently prepared samples were analyzed, yielding an average peak area of 34,479 with a standard deviation of 334.82 and a %RSD of 0.97%. This low %RSD value (≤ 2%) indicates excellent repeatability of the method and confirms that the sample preparation process is consistent and reproducible. In the case of injection precision, six replicate injections of the same solution were performed. The average peak area was 35,002 with a standard deviation of 105.22 and a %RSD of 0.3%. The very low %RSD reflects outstanding system precision, indicating that the HPLC instrument provides highly consistent responses with minimal variability between injections. For intermediate precision, which evaluates variations across different conditions such as time or analyst, six measurements produced an average peak area of 34,804 with a standard deviation of 156.44 and a %RSD of 0.45%. This result further confirms the robustness of the method under normal operating variations. Overall, all %RSD values were well below the commonly accepted limit of 2%, demonstrating that the method is precise at all evaluated levels. The low variability in peak areas confirms the method’s suitability for routine analytical applications, ensuring reliable and reproducible quantification of the analyte.

Additionally, the chromatographic peaks at this level were clearly distinguishable with consistent retention times (~ 10.8 min), confirming reliable detection without interference as presented in Fig. 8. These results indicate that the method is capable of detecting oxcarbazepine at concentrations as low as approximately 0.3 ppm. For the LOQ study, six replicate injections at 0.90 ppm were performed. The S/N ratios ranged from 10.0 to 11.3, meeting the standard requirement for quantitation (S/N ≥ 10). The average peak area showed good consistency, with a %RSD of 2.65%, which is within acceptable limits for low-level quantification. This demonstrates that the method provides adequate precision and reproducibility at the LOQ level as shown in Fig. 9. Overall, the LOD and LOQ results confirm that the developed method is highly sensitive, with the ability to reliably detect and accurately quantify trace levels of oxcarbazepine. To further confirm the Limit of Detection (LOD) and Limit of Quantitation (LOQ)21, the recommendations of the ICH were followed, which suggest multiple calculation approaches depending on the analytical method and analyte properties. Based on these calculations, the LOD and LOQ for oxcarbazepine were found to be 0.30 ppm and 0.91 ppm, respectively. These values are in close agreement with the experimentally determined signal-to-noise results (LOD = 0.30 ppm and LOQ = 0.90 ppm).

The strong agreement between the statistically calculated values and experimentally obtained results confirms the accuracy and reliability of the method. Furthermore, this consistency demonstrates that the developed RP-HPLC method complies with ICH and United States Pharmacopeia guidelines, supporting its suitability for sensitive detection and precise quantification of the analytes.

To assess the environmental impact of the developed method, three complementary green analytical chemistry (GAC) tools were employed: the Analytical Eco-Scale, the Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI), and the AGREE metric30,31. The Eco-Scale was determined by assigning penalty points for factors such as the use of hazardous reagents, waste generation, and energy consumption. The primary contributors to penalty points were the use of acetonitrile (ACN) in the mobile phase and the direct disposal of generated waste. Despite this, the method achieved an Eco-Scale score of approximately 75, classifying it as an acceptable green analytical method. The Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI) was applied to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the entire analytical workflow, from sample preparation to final analysis. The resulting pictogram displayed a combination of green and yellow segments, indicating environmentally favorable features such as minimal sample preparation, absence of derivatization, and low energy requirements. However, some red indicators were observed due to the use of ACN and the direct disposal of waste without treatment, highlighting areas for improvement. Additionally, the AGREE metric, based on the 12 principles of green analytical chemistry, yielded a score of 0.57 out of 1.0, reflecting a moderate level of greenness. The method performed well in terms of reduced sample preparation, short analysis time, and elimination of derivatization steps. Lower scores were associated with solvent sustainability, waste management, and operator safety due to the use of ACN and its disposal practices. Overall, the combined assessment confirms that the proposed RP-HPLC method incorporates several environmentally friendly characteristics, including simplicity, reduced analysis time, and low energy consumption. However, improvements are still needed in solvent selection and waste handling. Future developments should focus on replacing ACN with greener alternatives, such as ethanol or water-rich systems, alongside implementing solvent recycling and improved waste management strategies.

In summary, this validated RP-HPLC method overcomes key limitations identified in earlier studies by providing an extended linearity range, reliable stability-indicating capability, and full compliance with international validation guidelines. As a result, it represents a scientifically sound and practically applicable tool for comprehensive quality control and stability assessment of combination ophthalmic formulations, supporting efforts to maintain therapeutic efficacy and ensure patient safety in antiepileptic management.

Conclusion

A robust, accurate, and highly precise RP-HPLC method was successfully developed and validated for the quantification of oxcarbazepine in bulk form and pharmaceutical analysis. The method addressed limitations of previously reported approaches by providing an extended linearity range, reliable stability-indicating capability, and a unified analytical procedure suitable for routine quality assessment. The method demonstrated excellent linearity (R2 > 0.999) and met all system suitability and validation criteria in accordance with ICH guidelines. High precision was confirmed through consistently %RSD < 1.0 for repeatability, injection precision, and intermediate precision. Robustness studies further demonstrated the method’s reliability under small deliberate variations in chromatographic conditions. Specificity was established through efficient separation of oxcarbazepine from its impurities and degradation products, confirming its suitability as a stability-indicating method. In addition, the method showed high sensitivity, with low limits of detection and quantification, enabling reliable trace-level analysis. Green analytical chemistry assessment indicated that the method is environmentally friendly, benefiting from simplicity, reduced analysis time, and lower solvent and energy consumption.

Overall, the proposed RP-HPLC method represents a reliable and regulatory-compliant analytical tool suitable for routine quality control, stability studies, and pharmaceutical analysis of oxcarbazepine (Trileptal), ensuring its safety, efficacy, and quality.

Preparation of standard and buffer solutions

Comprehensive information on the procedures for sample preparation, the mobile phase formulation, and the analytical techniques employed in this work are described in supplementary material.

Oxcarbazepine standard solution (1500 ppm)

Accurately weigh 15 mg of Oxcarbazepine and transfers it into a 10 mL volumetric flask. Add an appropriate amount of the diluting solvent (75% acetonitrile in water) to fully dissolve the compound. Once dissolved, make up the volume to the mark with the same solvent and mix thoroughly to ensure homogeneity.

Phosphate buffer (pH 5.0, 25 mM potassium phosphate monobasic)

Accurately weigh 3.4 g of potassium dihydrogen phosphate and transfer it into a 1000 mL beaker. Add approximately 1 L of deionized water and mix using a magnetic stirrer for about 10 min until complete dissolution is achieved. The pH of the solution is then carefully adjusted to 5.0. The resulting buffer solution is subsequently filtered and sonicated for 30 min to remove any entrapped air and ensure homogeneity prior to use.

Mobile phase B (100% acetonitrile)

Transfer 1000 mL of acetonitrile (ACN) into the mobile phase reservoir and sonicate it for 20 min to effectively degas the solvent and remove any trapped air bubbles before use.

Synthesized oxcarbazepine solution (1500 ppm)

Accurately weigh 15 mg of the laboratory-synthesized Oxcarbazepine and place it in a 10 mL volumetric flask. Add about 5 mL of 75% acetonitrile, sonicate to achieve complete dissolution, and then make up the volume to the mark with 75% ACN. Mix well to obtain a homogeneous solution.

Gauge fields in the presence of the electroweak bubble wall
Takahiro Kubota

Abstract
The gauge field theory of the standard electroweak model in the presence of the electroweak bubble wall is investigated, with an eye toward its applications to microscopic phenomena which are supposed to have occurred during the phase transition in the early universe. The asymptotic fields are defined anew so that the effects of the position-dependent Higgs condensate are taken into account through the position-dependent W W and Z Z boson masses. A novel method of massive gauge field quantization in the R ξ R_{\xi} -gauge with ξ = 1 \xi=1 is proposed for the case of the position-dependent masses. Our method is based on the eigenfunction expansion method associated with second-order differential operators, i.e., a sort of generalized Fourier expansion.The commutation relations of creation and annihilation operators of various wave propagation modes are given in terms of the so-called spectral function. The decoupling of unphysical states from the physical S-matrix is also investigated along the line of Kugo-Ojima’s quartet mechanism on the basis of the BRST symmetry. It is pointed out that one of the quartet fields is not merely the unphysical scalar field but should be a linear combination of the unphysical scalar and the gauge fields. The physical and unphysical polarizations of the gauge field waves are unambiguously distinguished and this will help us evaluate the friction caused by the physical polarization states of W W and Z Z boson waves on the bubble wall during the phase transition in the early universe.

The electroweak bubble wall breaks both translational and Lorentz invariance, we are not able to use the conventional approach of classifying particles according to spin and mass. \displaystyle\delta_{B}c^{a}=\frac{1}{2}g\>\delta\lambda\>\varepsilon^{abc}c^{b}c^{c},\>\>\>(a=1,2,3),\hskip 22.76228pt\delta_{B}c^{0}=0\>.

\displaystyle\delta_{B}\overline{c}^{a}=-\delta\lambda\>{\cal B}^{a},\>\>\>(a=1,2,3)\>,\hskip 56.9055pt\delta_{B}\overline{c}^{0}=-\delta\lambda\>{\cal B}^{0}\>,

\displaystyle\delta_{B}{\cal B}^{a}=0,\>\>\>(a=1,2,3)\>,\hskip 85.35826pt\delta_{B}{\cal B}^{0}=0.

\displaystyle Q_{B}=\int d^{2}\vec{x}_{\perp}\int dz\left[\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left\{\dot{{\cal B}}^{a}c^{a}-{\cal B}^{a}({\cal D}^{0}c)^{a}\right\}+\dot{{\cal B}}^{0}c^{0}-{\cal B}^{0}\dot{c}^{0}+\frac{1}{2}g\>\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{3}\varepsilon^{abc}\dot{\overline{c}}^{a}c^{b}c^{c}\right]\>,

Summary

In the present paper we have investigated the standard electroweak gauge field theory in the presence of the bubble wall by taking the

-field sector as the representative case. We included the effect of the position dependent Higgs condensate

into the asymptotic field equations of motion of

and the unphysical scalar field

We applied the eigenfunction expansion method developed in Refs.

(

that was introduced through the gauge fixing procedure also satisfies the Klein-Gordon type equation (

\mathfrak{b}_{i}

{\cal N}(t,\vec{x}_{\perp},z)
and the auxiliary
{\cal B}^{Z}(t,\vec{x}_{\perp},z)
fields constitute the BRST quartet and that they decouple jointly from the physical S-matrix.

Now that the theoretical framework of the standard model gauge theory in the presence of the bubble wall has been put in a good shape, we are now able to launch with renewed interest into reanalyses of plasma interactions with the electroweak bubble wall. We have already witnessed in the last several decades a large variety of publications
boedeker1
,
boedeker2
-
ai3
,
dine1
-
garcia1
on the coupled dynamics between the bubble wall and the plasma. The microscopic approach based on quantum field theories as given in the present work is appropriate for the ultra-relativistic regime of the wall velocity. It will be interesting to see how the pressure exerted on the wall by plasma particles depends on the Lorentz boost factor, when the effects of multi-soft vector boson emission in the transition radiation are summed up somehow to all orders. A careful look at the infrared region of the phase space is required, and the framework given in this paper will be the most reliable, since the position dependent mass is taken into account without any approximation. For the case of relatively low velocity of the bubble wall, hydrodynamical methods under the local thermal equilibrium condition are often used in literatures. Plasma particles in the distribution function in such approaches are endowed with definite fixed masses and with definite three-momenta. It could be necessary that particles in the distribution function should be labelled with
(\vec{p}_{\perp},\lambda)
instead of three-momenta, as we did in the present paper, so that the momentum transfer from the plasma particles to the bubble wall should be within the reach in the theoretical framework. We hope we come to these issues in our future publicanions.

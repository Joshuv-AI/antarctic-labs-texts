Averaging of quantum channels via channel-state duality
Marcin Markiewicz, Łukasz Pawela, Zbigniew Puchała

Abstract
Twirling, uniform averaging over symmetry actions, is a standard tool for reducing the description of quantum states and channels to symmetry-invariant data. We develop a framework for averaging quantum channels based on channel-state duality that converts pre- and post-processing averages into a group twirl acting directly on the Choi operator. For arbitrary unitary representations on the input and output spaces, the twirled channel is obtained as an explicit projection onto the commutant of the induced representation on ℋ out ⊗ ℋ in \mathcal{H}_{\rm out}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\rm in} . In the collective setting, where the commutant is the walled Brauer algebra, we introduce a partial-transpose reduction that maps channel twirling to an ordinary Schur-Weyl twirl of the partially transposed Choi operator, enabling formulas in terms of permutation operators. We further extend the construction beyond compact symmetries to reductive non-unitary groups via Cartan decomposition, yielding a weighted sum of invariant-sector projections with weights determined by the Abelian component. Finally, we provide two finite realizations of channel averaging. The first one is a “dual” averaging protocol as a convex mixture of unitary- 1 1 -design channels on invariant sectors. The second one is a notion of channel t t -designs induced by weighted group t t -designs for t = t in + t out t=t_{\rm in}+t_{\rm out} .

Theorem 1

(Channel twirl in Choi form, Section
2
)
.

Let

channel with Choi operator

J_{M_{U}^{\Phi}}=\int_{\mathrm{U}(d)}dU\;\Big(\pi_{\rm U}^{\rm out}(U)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\rm U}^{\rm in}(U)\Big)\,J_{\Phi}\,\Big(\pi_{\rm U}^{\rm out}(U)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\rm U}^{\rm in}(U)\Big)^{\dagger},

Lemma 1

Let

3
Averaging quantum channels over non-unitary operations defined via Cartan decomposition

Let us now try to generalize averaging of quantum channels from the unitary
averaging to averaging over non-unitary operations described by a non-compact
symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
. Such an averaging can be defined via iterated

\mathcal{G}
, which boils down to a
representation of an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{G}
as a composition of
operations from the maximally compact component
\mathcal{K}
of the group
intertwined by an element from a maximal Abelian subgroup
\mathcal{A}
:

G=KAK^{\prime},\,K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K},\,A\in\mathcal{A}
. Such idea of non-compact
averaging has been introduced in
[
18
,
5
]
in the context
of averaging quantum states over SLOCC-type operations, and generalized to
averaging over arbitrary finite-dimensional matrix representations of reductive
groups in the recent work
[
6
]
. Following
[
6
]
,
let us take arbitrary matrix representation
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}
of the group

\mathcal{G}
and define the averaging of a quantum state with respect to the
chosen representation as follows:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho)=\int_{\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{K}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(K)\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(K^{\prime})\right)\rho\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(K)\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\pi_{\mathcal{G}}(K^{\prime})\right)^{\dagger}\operatorname{d}\!K\operatorname{d}\!A\operatorname{d}\!K^{\prime}.

(20)

in which the integration is defined over the Cartan “KAK” decomposition of

\mathcal{G}
. The integration over the two copies of maximally compact
components
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is performed uniformly, which means that

\int_{\mathcal{K}}\operatorname{d}\!K
represents Haar integral over
\mathcal{K}
. Integration over
\mathcal{A}
is performed with respect to any finite
normalized measure on this (in general non-compact) manifold, and there appears
one crucial assumption: elements of
\mathcal{A}
enter the integral as
normalized matrices:
A_{\textrm{n}}=A/||A||
, in order to guarantee trace
nonincreasing character of the map (
20
).

Our aim is to define twirling of quantum channels with respect to the representation
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}
applied as pre- and post-processing operation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}^{\operatorname{in}}(\sigma_{\operatorname{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\operatorname{in}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G)\sigma_{\operatorname{in}}\pi^{\operatorname{in}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G)^{\dagger},\,\,\sigma_{\operatorname{in}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{in}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}^{\operatorname{out}}(\sigma_{\operatorname{out}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\operatorname{out}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G)\sigma_{\operatorname{out}}\pi^{\operatorname{out}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G)^{\dagger},\,\,\sigma_{\operatorname{out}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{out}}).

(21)

In order to follow the analogy with (
2
) we have to define the
counterpart of the
\mathcal{T}_{U^{-1}}
operation from definition
(
2
). Formally every element
G
from the group
\mathcal{G}
is
invertible, however, the demand of trace-nonincreasing character of a quantum
channel forces us to integrate over normalized elements from the group
\mathcal{G}
in the twirling channel (
20
). Now, in general normalized
matrices, which represent trace-nonincreasing quantum channels, are not
invertible as quantum operations. Therefore we define twirling of quantum
channels with respect to
\mathcal{G}
operations as follows:

\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{G}(\rho)=\int\operatorname{d}\!G\,\mathcal{T}_{G}^{\operatorname{out}}\circ\Phi\circ\mathcal{T}_{G}^{\operatorname{in}\dagger}(\rho),

(22)

in which we define:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}^{\operatorname{in}\dagger}(\sigma_{\operatorname{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{\operatorname{in}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G)^{\dagger}\sigma_{\operatorname{in}}\pi^{\operatorname{in}}_{{\mathcal{G}}}(G).

(23)

Note that the integration in (
22
) is understood in the sense of
Cartan integral (
20
). In the case of a compact group
\mathcal{G}

with trivial
\mathcal{A}
this definition is fully equivalent to (
2
).

Now all of the derivations (
2
)–(
2
) concerning
the unitary case hold, and we have the following expression for the Choi matrix
of the channel (
22
):

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\operatorname{d}\!G\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)\right)J_{\Phi}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)\right)^{\dagger}.

(24)

Due to distributivity of the tensor product with respect to ordinary matrix
product, we have the following Cartan decompositon:

\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)=\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(K)\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(K)\right)\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\right)\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(K^{\prime})\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(K^{\prime})\right),

(25)

in which we omit bar in the middle term since
A_{\textrm{n}}
is a real
diagonal matrix. Since Cartan decomposition commutes with taking representations
of the components
[
6
]
, it can be seen that
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}|_{\mathcal{K}}\equiv\pi_{\mathcal{K}}
, when acting solely on compact elements
K\in\mathcal{K}
of the group

\mathcal{G}
, is itself a representation of the maximal compact subgroup

\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
. Analogously, in the same sense
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}|_{\mathcal{A}}\equiv\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
, when acting solely on non-compact elements
A\in\mathcal{A}
can be treated
as a representation of the maximal Abelian subgroup
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{G}
. Hence the integral (
24
) becomes:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{K}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(K)\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(K^{\prime})\right)J_{\Phi}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(K)\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(K^{\prime})\right)^{\dagger}\operatorname{d}\!K\operatorname{d}\!A\operatorname{d}\!K^{\prime}.

(26)

As shown in
[
5
]
such integral decomposes into a product of
compact twirling of the input operator and of the square of the non-compact
part:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho)=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{K}}(\rho)\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(A_{\textrm{n}})^{2}\right),

(27)

which can be directly represented as:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho)=\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}(\rho)=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{D^{k}_{U}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=1}^{D^{k}_{C}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\rho\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dagger}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}]\right)\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}],

(28)

where the coefficients
\beta_{k}
are integrals over the non-compact components:

\beta_{k}=\Tr\left(\left[\int\left(\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(A_{\textrm{n}})\right)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!A\right]\hat{\Pi}_{k}^{\dagger}\right),

(29)

and the operators
\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}]
are Schur operator
basis found by applying generalized duality theorem to the representation

\pi_{\mathcal{K}}=\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}|_{\mathcal{K}}
. Therefore, we finally have:

J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{D^{k}_{U}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=1}^{D^{k}_{C}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(J_{\Phi}\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dagger}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}]\right)\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}].

(30)

In full analogy with the previous case one may want to avoid using
contragradient representations in the generalized Schur-Weyl duality. Utilizing
Lemma
2
the formula (
24
) becomes:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\operatorname{d}\!G\left[\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)\right)J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)\right)^{\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma},

(31)

which can be analogously, using Cartan decomposition, decomposed into:

J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{D^{k}_{U}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=1}^{D^{k}_{C}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dagger}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{in}}]\right)\left(\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{in}}]\right)^{\Gamma},

(32)

where now the operators
\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{in}}]
are elements of
the Schur operator basis related with decomposing the representations of

\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}|_{\mathcal{K}}=\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{in}}
, which
are typically much easier to construct. Indeed, let us take as an example the
case of
\mathcal{G}=\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{C})
, for which the maximally compact
Cartan component
\mathcal{K}
is the special unitary group
\textrm{SU}(d)
. In
full analogy to the unitary case we assume collective action of group elements
on input and output spaces:

\displaystyle\pi_{\textrm{SL}}^{\operatorname{in}}(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{\otimes t_{\operatorname{in}}},\,\,L\in\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle\pi_{\textrm{SL}}^{\operatorname{out}}(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{\otimes t_{\operatorname{out}}},\,\,L\in\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{C}).

(33)

In this case the Choi matrix of a twirled channel reads after
(
31
):

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\textrm{SL}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{C})}\operatorname{d}\!L\left[\left(L^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\left(L^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)^{\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma}.

(34)

Now the restricted representation
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}|_{\mathcal{K}}
reads in this case simply

U^{(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}
, for
U\in\textrm{SU}(d)
, and we
have, following (
32
):

J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\textrm{SL}}}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{D^{k}_{U}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\sum_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=1}^{D^{k}_{C}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dagger}[U^{(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}]\right)\left(\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[U^{(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}]\right)^{\Gamma},

(35)

where now
\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}[U^{(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}]
are ordinary permutation
operators corresponding to standard Schur-Weyl duality for

\textrm{U}^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}
and the permutation group
\textrm{S}_{(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}
.

4
Dual averaging of channels

In this section we utilize the concept of
generalized finite unitary
averaging
introduced in a recent work
[
6
]
in the context of
averaging of quantum channels. The idea of generalized finite unitary averaging,
originally introduced for averaging quantum states over general symmetry
operations, also bases on generalized duality for group representations,
however, in analogy to the concept of unitary
t
-designs, it keeps the
averaging procedure on the original group representation side, and not on its
commutant, as in formulas (
12
) and (
30
), in which
the Choi matrix of the twirled channel is expressed in terms of operators

\hat{\Lambda}_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
, which span the commutant of tensor
product of the original symmetry group. Indeed, the idea in
[
6
]

is to represent the Cartan-decomposition-based average of a quantum state with respect to arbitrary symmetry
group (
20
) as a probabilistic mixture of unitary-
1
-designs on
invariant subspaces:

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}p_{k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}(\rho),

(36)

in which
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}
represents unitary-
1
-design on

D^{k}_{G}
-dimensional subspace irreducible under the action of the restricted representation
\pi_{\mathcal{K}}
, for
\mathcal{K}

being the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
:

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}(\rho)=\frac{1}{\left(D^{k}_{G}\right)^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{\left(D^{k}_{G}\right)^{2}}\tilde{\gamma}_{l}^{(k)}\rho\,\tilde{\gamma}_{l}^{(k)\dagger},

(37)

and the probabilities
p_{k}
are specified in full analogy to
(
28
) as
p_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}
. Unitary-
1
-designs are
specified by choosing a unitary operator basis

\{\gamma^{(k)}_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\left(D^{k}_{G}\right)^{2}}
on space
\mathbb{C}^{D^{k}_{G}}
,
and further they are extended to act on the entire representation space using
basis operators
\hat{\Pi}_{k}^{m_{1}m_{2}}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}]
(
2
) specified by generalized
group duality:

\tilde{\gamma}^{(k)}_{l}=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=1}^{D^{k}_{G}}{\left(\gamma^{(k)}_{l}\right)}_{m_{1}m_{2}}\hat{\Pi}^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{k}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}].

(38)

Utilizing the above formalism one can express the Choi matrix
J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}
of a twirled channel (
24
) in a
design-like form:

J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}(J_{\Phi})=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{D^{k}}\right)\frac{1}{\left(D^{k}_{G}\right)^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{\left(D^{k}_{G}\right)^{2}}\tilde{\gamma}_{l}^{(k)}J_{\Phi}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{l}^{(k)\dagger},

(39)

where the operators (
38
) are defined for
\hat{\Pi}^{m_{1}m_{2}}_{k}[\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{out}}\otimes\bar{\pi}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\operatorname{in}}]
.
In principle in the above formulas one can use arbitrary unitary operator basis,
however the typical choice due to its simplicity of construction is the
Heisenberg-Weyl unitary operator bases
[
19
]
, comprising operators:

\{\omega_{D^{k}_{G}}^{ij}Z_{D^{k}_{G}}^{i}X_{D^{k}_{G}}^{j}\}_{i,j=0}^{{D^{k}_{G}}-1}

in
which the complex root of unity reads:
\omega_{{D^{k}_{G}}}=e^{2i\pi/{D^{k}_{G}}}
,
and the operators
Z,X
are unitary generalizations of Pauli matrices

\sigma_{z},\sigma_{x}
.

5
Channel
t
-designs

The methods of averaging quantum channels described in previous sections are
based on generalized group duality and therefore, though finite, they demand
performing projections on irreducible subspaces related with representations of
either the original symmetry group for input and output ports or the commutant
of its action. One may expect a finite averaging method which respects the
operator structure of the original group actions, as in the case of unitary

t
-designs. We will provide such a generalization utilizing Lemma

2
for the following case:

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{in}}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\otimes t_{\operatorname{in}}},\,\,G\in\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{G}}^{\operatorname{out}}(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\otimes t_{\operatorname{out}}},\,\,G\in\mathcal{G},

(40)

in which
\mathcal{G}
is an arbitrary subgroup of
\textrm{GL}(d,\mathbb{C})
.
We utilize the following lemma:

Lemma 3
.

Let
\{G_{i},w_{i}\}
be a (weighted) group
t
-design, namely:

\int_{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{d}\!G\,G^{\otimes t}\rho\,G^{\otimes t\dagger}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}w_{i}G_{i}^{\otimes t}\rho\,G_{i}^{\otimes t\dagger}.

(41)

Then the following holds:

\int_{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{d}\!G\,\left[G^{\otimes t}\rho^{\Gamma}\,G^{\otimes t\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}w_{i}\left[G_{i}^{\otimes t}\rho^{\Gamma}\,G_{i}^{\otimes t\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma}.

(42)

Proof.

The above holds due to: (i) linearity of partial transpose, (ii) the fact that
(
41
) holds for any
\rho
(in fact
\rho
is redundant in this
formula).
∎

Utilizing the above lemma we can define channel
t
-design, where

t=t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}}
as a finite set of matrices and weights
\{G_{i},w_{i}\}
which
reconstruct the Choi matrix of an averaged channel:

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{d}\!G\left[\left(G^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\left(G^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)^{\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{X}}w_{i}\left[\left(G_{i}^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)J_{\Phi}^{\Gamma}\left(G_{i}^{\otimes(t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}})}\right)^{\dagger}\right]^{\Gamma}.

The above construction is very general and can be applied whenever one can construct
t
-design for a given symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
and
demanded value of
t=t_{\operatorname{in}}+t_{\operatorname{out}}
. In the case of
\mathcal{G}
being a unitary group
there is a huge literature on this topic
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
, whereas in the case of
\mathcal{G}=\textrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})

one can construct respective designs as product designs comprising unitary ones and Gauss-type quadratues, see
[
18
]
for detailed presentation.

Relation to designs for channels.

Several notions of designs for channels have been proposed in the literature,
often by pushing forward a design on a larger unitary group through a fixed
Stinespring dilation or by imposing moment-matching conditions on ensembles of
CPTP maps, see e.g.
[
26
]
. The channel
t
-design notion in Eq. (43) is tailored to the present
twirling problem: it is the minimal finite condition required to reproduce the
averaged Choi operator for collective pre/post actions, and it follows directly
from the partial-transpose reduction and any weighted group
t
-design for

t=t_{\rm in}+t_{\rm out}
. This perspective complements other channel-design
frameworks by making the symmetry and the induced representation on the Choi
space explicit.

6
Conclusions and outlook

We developed a representation-theoretic framework for averaging (twirling) of
quantum channels that is driven by channel–state duality. Starting from the
definition of channel twirling as uniform pre- and post-processing by the symmetry group
representations on the input and output spaces, we derived an explicit
Choi-level formulation in which the twirl becomes a group average acting
directly on the Choi operator under the induced representation

\pi^{\mathrm{out}}\otimes\bar{\pi}^{\mathrm{in}}
. This
viewpoint identifies the twirl as a projection onto the commutant algebra and
leads to closed-form expressions in terms of generalized Schur operator bases
obtained from generalized Schur-Weyl duality.

A central simplification arises in the collective setting

\pi^{\mathrm{in}}(U)=U^{\otimes t_{\mathrm{in}}}
and

\pi^{\mathrm{out}}(U)=U^{\otimes t_{\mathrm{out}}}
, where the commutant of

U^{\otimes t_{\mathrm{out}}}\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes t_{\mathrm{in}}}
is
the walled Brauer algebra. By exploiting a partial-transpose identity, we showed
that channel twirling can be reduced to an
ordinary
Schur-Weyl twirl of
the partially transposed Choi operator under

U^{\otimes(t_{\mathrm{in}}+t_{\mathrm{out}})}
. This removes the contragredient
action at the cost of a final partial transpose and enables practical evaluation
using permutation-operator technology rather than mixed Schur transforms or
explicit walled Brauer idempotents.

We further extended the construction beyond compact unitary symmetries to
averaging over non-unitary operations described by reductive groups via Cartan
decomposition. The resulting twirl admits a decomposition into invariant-sector
projections weighted by coefficients
\beta_{k}
determined solely by the Abelian
Cartan component, and an analogous partial-transpose reduction applies to avoid
contragredient representations on the compact part. In addition, we discussed a
dual averaging realization in which the (Cartan) twirl can be implemented as a
probabilistic mixture of unitary-
1
-design channels acting on invariant sectors
through embedded unitary operator bases. Finally, motivated by unitary designs,
we introduced a notion of channel
t
-design for collective actions: any
(weighted) group
t
-design for
t=t_{\mathrm{in}}+t_{\mathrm{out}}
induces a
finite reconstruction of the averaged Choi operator, in this case preserving the structure
of the original symmetry group action.

Several directions follow naturally. On the constructive side, it is important
to develop efficient exact and approximate finite averaging sets for broader
classes of symmetry groups and representations, and to quantify the error
incurred when replacing Haar/Cartan integrals by designs in the induced
channel-
t
setting. On the algorithmic side, the partial-transpose reduction
suggests scalable implementations of channel twirls based on
permutation-operator decompositions and Schur-Weyl projectors, with complexity
controlled by
t_{\mathrm{in}}+t_{\mathrm{out}}
rather than the full walled
Brauer machinery. Finally, extending the present approach to approximate
symmetries, to non-identical local dimensions, and to other physically motivated
group actions (beyond collective tensor powers) may further broaden the range of
channel-averaging problems that admit analytic reduction and finite, design-like
realizations.

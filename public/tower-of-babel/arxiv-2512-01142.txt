A Classification of Invertible Stabilizer Codes
Roman Geiko, Georgii Shuklin

Abstract
We develop a framework for the classification of invertible translation‑invariant stabilizer codes modulo condensation and stabilization with simple codes. We introduce generalizations of the Pauli groups of local unitaries for quantum systems of qudits on cubic lattices and analyze stabilizer Hamiltonians whose terms are chosen from these groups. We define invertible stabilizer codes to be ground states of stabilizer Hamiltonians with trivial topological charges and completely classify them in any spatial dimension in terms of relative L‑theory groups. In particular, we show that the group of equivalence classes of such codes in three spatial dimensions is isomorphic to the Witt group of abelian topological orders in two spatial dimensions. Additionally, we propose the spectrum of the relative L-theory as a representative of the generalized cohomology theory corresponding to the invertible stabilizer states.



keywords
Stabilizer codes, topological phases, quantum cellular automata, quantum lattice systems

Introduction

Entanglement patterns in the ground states of quantum lattice systems play a central role in contemporary quantum many-body physics. The least entangled classes of many-body states, beyond product states, are the invertible states introduced by A. Kitaev
37

Our methods are inspired by the symplectic formalism which is applicable to the Pauli stabilizer codes

;

;

We extend the symplectic formalism by replacing the Pauli groups with more general twisted quasi-Pauli groups, which are essentially groups of Weyl operators constructed by an anti-hermitian form on a certain class of modules over the ring of Laurent polynomials with integer coefficients. Then, our stabilizer codes are determined by abelian subgroups of the twisted quasi-Pauli groups. The process of condensation allows us to pass between non-isomorphic abelian subgroups and different quasi-Pauli groups, in contrast with the relation allowed for the Pauli codes. We focus on the invertible codes by which we mean the codes with no topological charges.

The key technical point is the reformulation of invertible stabilizer codes in terms of the linking formations. Then, the Witt group of formations corresponds to the group of stabilizer codes modulo stabilization and condensation. The resulting groups are labeled by the spatial dimension

\displaystyle\operatorname{E}_{\mathsf{d}}=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{W}}^{pt}\,,\;\;\,\,{\mathsf{d}}\equiv 3\;\,\mbox{mod}\;4\;\&\;{\mathsf{d}}>0\,,\\

Groups of unitaries

In this section we discuss various groups of unitaries acting on a many-body Hilbert space. We begin by reviewing the familiar Pauli groups and introduce twisted quasi-Pauli groups.

6.1
Pauli group

Let us choose a finite abelian group
{\mathcal{D}}
of order
q=|{\mathcal{D}}|
. We use
{\mathcal{D}}
to label a basis
\{|g\rangle\}_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}
for

\mathbb{C}^{q}
and define the “shift” operators
\{X_{g}\}_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}
acting via

\displaystyle X_{h}|g\rangle=|h+g\rangle\,.

(6.1)

Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{D}},\mathbb{Q}/{\mathbb{Z}})
be the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathcal{D}}
which is isomorphic to the group of characters of
{\mathcal{D}}
, and to
{\mathcal{D}}
itself (non-canonically). For each
\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
, we define a “clock” operator via

\displaystyle Z_{\psi}|g\rangle=e^{2\pi i\psi(g)}|g\rangle\,.

(6.2)

The shift and clock operators satisfy the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[Z_{\psi},X_{h}]\coloneqq(Z_{\psi})^{-1}(X_{h})^{-1}Z_{\psi}X_{h}=e^{2\pi i\psi(h)}\,.

(6.3)

We associate an ordered product of a shift and a clock
X_{g}Z_{\phi}
with an element
(g,\phi)\in{\mathcal{D}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
. Recall the standard
\mathbb{Q}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued antisymmetric form
{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}
on
{\mathcal{D}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
:

\displaystyle{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}((g,\phi),(h,\psi))=\psi(g)-\phi(h)\,,\quad g,h\in{\mathcal{D}},\;\;\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}\,.

(6.4)

Then, the commutator of two ordered products of shifts and clocks is expressed in terms of
{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}
:

\displaystyle[X_{g}Z_{\phi},X_{h}Z_{\psi}]=e^{-2\pi i{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}((g,\phi),(h,\psi))}\,.

(6.5)

We define the
Pauli group
as the group generated by the shifts, clocks, and phases

\displaystyle\textbf{P}({\mbox{H}}^{-}({\mathcal{D}});{\mathcal{D}})\coloneqq\{\omega X_{g}Z_{\psi}\,|\,\omega\in U(1),g\in{\mathcal{D}},\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}\}\,.

(6.6)

Our notation
\textbf{P}({\mbox{H}}^{-}({\mathcal{D}});{\mathcal{D}})
contains the information about the abstract group generated by the shifts and clocks and a choice of representation — the Pauli operators act on the Hilbert space labeled by
{\mathcal{D}}

6.2
Lattice Pauli group

For
{\mathsf{d}}>0

{\Lambda}
contains a group of local unitaries generated by the Pauli operators acting on a single site. Let

be the lattice sites, then we define the one-site shifts
X_{g}^{j}
and clocks
Z_{\psi}^{k}

4
4

4

By

we mean an operator of the form “
\ldots\otimes\mathbbm{1}\otimes X_{g}\otimes\mathbbm{1}\otimes\ldots

:

\displaystyle[Z_{\psi}^{j},X_{g}^{k}]=\begin{cases}1\,,\hskip 36.0pt\,\,j\neq k\,,\\
e^{2\pi i\psi(g)}\,,\hskip 11.38109ptj=k\,.\end{cases}

(6.7)

The coordinate of the site

\displaystyle(g\,x_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots x_{{\mathsf{d}}}^{k_{{\mathsf{d}}}},\psi\,x_{1}^{j_{1}}\ldots x_{{\mathsf{d}}}^{j_{{\mathsf{d}}}})\in{\mathcal{D}}[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{{\mathsf{d}}},x_{{\mathsf{d}}}^{-1}]\oplus{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{{\mathsf{d}}},x_{{\mathsf{d}}}^{-1}]\,.

6.3
Local bases and
(R,S)
-modules

In the case of the Pauli groups, we associated each on-site qudit with the group ring
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{D}}]
and we automatically obtained the action of
{\mathcal{D}}
by shifts. We will use the general
(R,S)
-modules to label translation-invariant local bases for the many-body Hilbert space of a system of
q
-dimensional qudits on
{\Lambda}
.

We introduce the Hilbert space of sequences
l^{2}(M)
and equip it with the orthonormal basis
\{|m\rangle\}_{m\in M}
such that
\langle m_{1}\,|\,m_{2}\rangle=\delta(m_{1}-m_{2})
. Any
(R,S)
-module
M
is a countable abelian group and

is a separable Hilbert space (in this section it is especially important to remember that
R={\mathbb{Z}}[{\Lambda}]
and
S={\mathbb{Z}}-\{0\})
, infinite-dimensional if
{\mathsf{d}}>0
.

If we start with a translation-invariant system on
{\Lambda}
, we can compactify it on a torus and impose periodic boundary conditions to preserve the translation-invariance. We use
M_{\ell}
to label the basis for the Hilbert space on a
{\mathsf{d}}
-dimensional torus of length
\ell
. Let
[M]=q\in K_{0}(T)
be the class of
M
, a positive integer, then
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ell^{2}(M_{\ell})=q^{\ell^{{\mathsf{d}}}}
by Proposition
2.3
. This allows us to interpret
\ell^{2}(M)
as a many-body Hilbert space having a
q
-dimensional degree of freedom per unit cell.

For each
m_{1}\in M
we have a
shift operator

X_{m_{1}}
such that
X_{m_{1}}|m_{2}\rangle=|m_{1}+m_{2}\rangle
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of generators of
M
, then
X_{e_{i}}|0\rangle=|e_{i}\rangle
can be seen as an elementary excitation over
|0\rangle
. In general, we cannot choose the generators to be the constant polynomials and elementary excitations need not be
1
-local.
We can interpret (
2.2
) as follows: since
R
is a ring of polynomials, a free
R
-module
R^{n}
corresponds to local shift operators acting on a lattice with the bosonic on-site Hilbert space
l^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. From this point of view, we obtain
l^{2}(M)
by imposing local translation-invariant constraints expressed by the generating map.

6.4
Quasi-Pauli groups and standard anti-hermitian linking forms over
(R,S)

Having defined the many-body Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(M)
, we automatically obtain the shift operators defined above
and
the clock operators
labeled by
M^{\vee}
:

\displaystyle Z_{w}|m\rangle=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{w(m)\}}|m\rangle\,,\quad m\in M,w\in M^{\vee}\,.

(6.10)

The shift and clock operators satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[Z_{w},X_{m}]=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{w(m)\}}\,.

(6.11)

The commutator of ordered products is expressed in terms of standard anti-hermitian form
{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}^{-}
on
M\oplus M^{\vee}
:

\displaystyle[X_{m_{1}}Z_{w_{1}},X_{m_{2}}Z_{w_{2}}]=e^{-2\pi i\operatorname{tr}{\hat{\lambda}}_{0}((m_{1},w_{1}),(m_{2},w_{2}))}=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{w_{1}(m_{2})-w_{2}(m_{1})\}}\,,\quad m_{1},m_{2}\in M\,,w_{1},w_{2}\in M^{\vee}.

(6.12)

Each clock and shift is a local unitary operator and they, together with phases, generate a quasi-Pauli group
\textbf{P}({\mbox{H}}^{-}(M);M)
.

6.5
Twisted quasi-Pauli groups

Here we define a generalization of the Pauli and quasi-Pauli groups. Not only are there
(R,S)
-modules not isomorphic to
{\mathcal{D}}[{\Lambda}]
, but also there are non-singular anti-hermitian forms over
(R,S)
not isomorphic to the standard form. Here we define a group of local unitaries by any non-singular linking form over
(R,S)
using a version of the Weyl operators.

The construction of Weyl operators by a symplectic form over
{\mathbb{R}}
or
\mathbb{C}
and their Schrodinger representation by a choice of polarization is a classical subject, see e.g.
28
;
14
. Recently, the classical Weyl operators were applied in a similar context to us, to a study of topology of pure states of (non-interacting) systems, in
44
. Here we apply the Weyl construction to an even non-singular linking form
(P,{\lambda})
over
(R,S)
and a choice of a lagrangian
M\hookrightarrow P
. We define Weyl operators as follows

\displaystyle W(p_{1})W(p_{2})=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(p_{1},p_{2})\}}W(p_{1}+p_{2})\,,\quad W(p_{1})^{*}=W(-p_{1})\,,\quad p_{1},p_{2}\in P\,.

(6.13)

Therefore, the commutator is determined by
{\hat{\lambda}}
:

\displaystyle[W(p_{1}),W(p_{2})]=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(p_{1},p_{2})\}}\,

(6.14)

Let
M
be a lagrangian in
(P,{\lambda})
, then define a (Schrodinger) representation on the Hilbert space

\displaystyle L^{2}_{M}(P,{\lambda})=\{f:P\to\mathbb{C}\,|\,f(p+w)=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(w,p)\}}f(p)\,,p\in P\,,w\in M\,\}

(6.15)

which is complete with respect to the following inner product

\displaystyle(f,g)=\sum_{x\in P/M}\overline{f(x)}g(x)\,.

(6.16)

Note that the terms in this sum do not depend on the choice of the representative of the class
x\in P/M
. The action of the Weyl operators on
L^{2}_{M}(P,{\lambda})
is given by the following rule

\displaystyle[W(p_{1})f](p)=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(p,p_{1})\}}f(p-p_{1})\,,\quad f\in L^{2}_{M}(P,{\lambda})\,,\;p,p_{1}\in P\,.

(6.17)

In other words, we defined a representation induced from
M
to
P
. The elements of the lagrangian act by diagonal unitaries: let
w\in M
, then

\displaystyle[W(w)f](p)=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(p,w)\}}f(p)\,.

(6.18)

Even though
L^{2}_{M}(P,{\lambda})
is a convenient representation, it is difficult to interpret it as a many-body Hilbert space. We observe that there exists a unitary isomorphism
L^{2}_{M}(P,{\lambda})\xrightarrow{\sim}l^{2}(M^{\vee})
where
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
is the space of square-summable sequences on
M^{\vee}\cong P/M
. Let
\{|m\rangle\}_{m\in M^{\vee}}
be an orthonormal basis for
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
and let
\pi:P\to M^{\vee}
be the surjection, then we pick a section
s
of
\pi
and define a map

\displaystyle U_{s}:L^{2}_{M}(P)\xrightarrow{\sim}l^{2}(M^{\vee})\,,\quad U_{s}(f)=\sum_{m\in M^{\vee}}f(s(m))|m\rangle\,.

(6.19)

One checks that it is indeed a unitary isomorphism with an inverse given by

\displaystyle U_{s}^{-1}|m\rangle=\psi_{m}\,,\quad\psi_{m}(p)=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(p,s(m))\}}\delta(\pi(p)-m)\,,\quad m\in M^{\vee},p\in P\,,

(6.20)

Here
\{\psi_{m}\}_{m\in M^{\vee}}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}_{M}(P)
. We note that even though
U_{s}
is an isomorphism of the Hilbert spaces, it does not preserve the module structure on the labels, i.e.,
U_{s}\psi_{m_{1}+m_{2}}\neq|m_{1}+m_{2}\rangle
. If we choose another section
s^{\prime}=s+v
for
\pi
, where
v
takes values in
M
, then the isomorphism

\displaystyle U_{s+v}=T_{s,v}U_{s}\,,\quad T_{s,v}|m\rangle=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(v(m),s(m))\}}|m\rangle\,

(6.21)

shifts by a diagonal unitary
T_{s,v}
. Finally, we obtain the following action of the Weyl unitaries on
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{s}(p)\coloneqq U_{s}W(p)U_{s}^{-1}|m\rangle=e^{\pi i\operatorname{tr}\{{\hat{\lambda}}(s(m),p)+{\hat{\lambda}}(s(m+\pi(p)),p+s(m))\}}|m+\pi(p)\rangle\,.

(6.22)

We define the
twisted quasi-Pauli group

\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
as the group generated by
\widetilde{W}_{s}(p)
for all
p\in P
and phases, for some choice of
s
.
As discussed in §
6.3
,
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
has a straightforward interpretation as a many-body Hilbert space of a quantum lattice system. In the case when
(P,{\lambda})={\mbox{H}}^{-}(M)
we can choose the section
s
to be a homomorphism, and in that case we have a splitting of the Weyl operators into shifts and clocks and the twisted quasi-Pauli group reduces to the quasi-Pauli group. In general,
P
is not a direct sum of the modules generated by the shift and clocks respectively. The non-triviality of
P
as an extension of
M^{\vee}
by
M
is implicitly encoded in the product of the Weyl operators (
6.13
).

6.6
Locally-flippable separators

The twisted quasi-Pauli groups are closely related to the locally-flippable separators from
20
. A local separator is a set of mutually commuting local unitaries
\{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
such that each element satisfies
({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})^{q}=1
and such that this set of unitaries precisely separates the many-body Hilbert space. The separation condition means that if we diagonalize the elements of the separator and choose any set of the
q
-th roots of unity
\{e^{2\pi in_{i}/q}\}_{i\in J}
, then the subspace
V
of the many-body Hilbert space such that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}V=e^{2\pi in_{i}/q}V
is one-dimensional. A local flipper
\{{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
for the local separator

\{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is a set of local unitaries such that each element satisfies
({\mathcal{X}}_{i})^{q}=1
and
[{\mathcal{Z}}_{i},{\mathcal{X}}_{j}]=e^{2\pi i/q}\delta_{i,j}
. A local separator is called locally flippable (LFS) if a local flipper exists. Crucially, any LFS and the corresponding flipper together generate
the full algebra of local operators
{\mathcal{A}}
.

For us, the index set
J
for the LFS is the lattice
{\Lambda}
. Let
M_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}/q[{\Lambda}]
be a lagrangian in
(P,{\lambda})
and let
e
be a generator of
M_{0}
, then we obtain an LFS
\{\widetilde{W}_{s}(j\cdot e)\}_{j\in{\Lambda}}
where
j\cdot e
denotes the action of
j\in{\Lambda}
on
e
by translation (multiplication by a monomial with coefficient
1
). Indeed, this set separates
l^{2}(M_{0}^{\vee})
. Let
\hat{t}
be a generator for
P/M_{0}
and let
t\in P
be a representative of that class. Then,
[\widetilde{W}_{s}(e),\widetilde{W}_{s}(t)]=e^{2\pi i/q}
and
\{\widetilde{W}_{s}(j\cdot t)\}_{j\in{\Lambda}}
is the flipper for the LFS above.

Twisted quasi-Pauli groups provide a mild generalization of the LFS of
20
by allowing more general
(R,S)
-modules than
{\mathbb{Z}}/q[{\Lambda}]
. We apply an analogous construction while using the general
(R,S)
-module
M
. Given a lagrangian
M
in
(P,{\lambda})
and choosing a set of generators
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
, we find a set of local mutually commuting unitaries
\{\widetilde{W}_{s}(j\cdot e_{\alpha})\}_{j\in{\Lambda},\alpha\in I}
, with each element having a finite order, and separating
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
. Indeed, there is a perfect pairing between
M
and
M^{\vee}
taking values in
\mathbb{Q}/{\mathbb{Z}}[{\Lambda}]
. By choosing a generating set for
P/M
, we obtain a set of local unitaries (not necessarily commuting) that “flip” any single basis element of
l^{2}(M^{\vee})
. Let us call this generalization of LFS and local flippers
quasi-LFS
and
quasi-flippers
.

The quasi-LFS generate the algebra of local operators similarly to LFS. One way of seeing this is by considering a compactification of
{\Lambda}
to a finite torus. For each compactification on a torus of length
\ell
, we obtain exactly
q^{\ell^{{\mathsf{d}}}}
of mutually commuting operators, the “clocks”, and
q^{\ell^{{\mathsf{d}}}}
of “shift” operators, using Proposition
2.3
. The commutant of the algebra generated by such clocks and shifts is trivial. Indeed, since the clocks generate a maximally commutative subalgebra, the commutant must belong to that algebra. Then, commutation with all the shifts forces the commutant to be trivial. In the finite-dimensional case, we can use the double commutant theorem, which demonstrates that the shifts and clocks generate the full matrix algebra of a system compactified on a torus.

7
A classification of invertible stabilizer codes

In this section we introduce invertible stabilizer codes and describe their classification scheme.

7.1
Stabilizer Hamiltonians

There are various definitions of stabilizer Hamiltonians. Their terms are chosen from a pre-determined alphabet with the most common choice being the Pauli group — such Hamiltonians are commonly called Pauli stabilizer Hamiltonians. We are going to use the twisted quasi-Pauli groups as alphabets.

Let
\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
be a twisted quasi-Pauli group. A
stabilizer Hamiltonian
(with respect to
\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
) is a Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}
with the following properties

1.

{\mathcal{H}}
is translation-invariant.

2.

{\mathcal{H}}
is a sum of local terms such that each term is obtained from a finite set
\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}\subset\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
by translations and products.

3.

All terms of
{\mathcal{H}}
commute.

4.

{\mathcal{H}}
is frustration-free.

Our definition generalizes the Pauli stabilizer Hamiltonians
5
;
22
;
23
. The ground state of a stabilizer Hamiltonian, also called a
stabilizer code
, is a state that is minimized by each term separately. Clearly, different stabilizer Hamiltonians can define the same stabilizer code and we are interested in the stabilizer codes rather than in stabilizer Hamiltonians. We analyze the stabilizer codes in terms of the
stabilizer modules
, the modules generated by the terms in
{\mathcal{H}}
up to phases. In this paper we postulate that all the relevant properties of the stabilizer code are encoded in the stabilizer module, and we will use those terms interchangeably.

Let us fix a stabilizer Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}
and the corresponding stabilizer code
F
. As follows from the definition,
F
is a finitely-generated
R
-submodule of
P
, which is automatically
S
-torsion. Since the terms in
{\mathcal{H}}
commute,
F
is isotropic with respect to
{\hat{\lambda}}
. If there are elements from
\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
, and consequently in the full algebra of local operators, that commute with the terms in
{\mathcal{H}}
, then the ground state of
{\mathcal{H}}
cannot be unique. If
F
is coisotropic,
F^{\perp}=F
, which means that the elements commuting with
{\mathcal{H}}
are already in
{\mathcal{H}}
— such Hamiltonians are called locally topologically ordered
5
;
32
. The local topological order condition distinguishes a class of Hamiltonians with a locally-unique ground state which is still extremely rich: it includes fractonic systems
21
;
43
and intrinsic topological order like the famous Kitaev’s toric code
36
.

Given a pair of stabilizer Hamiltonians
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
with respect to
\textbf{P}(P_{1},{\lambda}_{1};M_{1})
and
\textbf{P}(P_{2},{\lambda}_{2};M_{2})
correspondingly, we define their stacking as a new Hamiltonian on a combined system:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{12}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\,.

(7.1)

The resulting stabilizer module is a direct sum of the two modules
F_{12}=F_{1}\oplus F_{2}\subset P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
.

7.2
Invertible stabilizer codes as lagrangians

We call a locally topologically ordered stabilizer code with respect to
\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)

invertible
if its stabilizer module
F
is a lagrangian in
(P,{\lambda})
. Respectively, a stabilizer Hamiltonian whose ground state is an invertible stabilizer code is called invertible. Commonly, non-invertibility of a locally topologically ordered stabilizer Hamiltonian is detected by the global topological charges
2
;
22
;
24
;
50
which essentially prevent the uniqueness of the ground state of a system on finite tori. In our case, the natural way to define topological charges is via the following groups

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(F,S^{-1}R/R)\,,\quad\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(P/F,S^{-1}R/R)\,,\quad i\geqslant 1.

(7.2)

A stabilizer module
F
coming from a locally topologically ordered stabilizer Hamiltonian satisfies almost all the conditions of Definition
3.4
except
F
and
P/F
do not have to be
(R,S)
-modules. If the topological charges (
7.2
) vanish, then
F
and
P/F
are
(R,S)
-modules by Proposition
2.4
such that
F
is a lagrangian in
(P,{\lambda})
. In other words, our condition of invertibility is equivalent to the triviality of the topological charges. If
P
is annihilated by
n\in S
, then our topological charges reduce to the ones studied in
50
and
60
, thanks to Proposition
2.4
.

For each choice of the twisted quasi-Pauli group
\textbf{P}({\mbox{H}}^{-}(M);M)
and a lagrangian
F\subset P
, we can write down a stabilizer Hamiltonian with
F
as the stabilizer module. We choose some generating set
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
for
F
and write

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}=-\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{j\in{\Lambda}}(\widetilde{W}_{s}(j\cdot e_{\alpha})+\widetilde{W}_{s}(j\cdot e_{\alpha})^{*})\,

(7.3)

for some choice of
s
.
The stabilizer codes
M\subset{\mbox{H}}^{-}(M)
and
M^{\vee}\subset{\mbox{H}}^{-}(M)
are the analogs of the product code.

Given an invertible stabilizer code
F
with respect to
\textbf{P}(P,{\lambda};M)
, we define a non-singular anti-hermitian even linking formation
(P,{\lambda};M,F)
over
(R,S)
. Vice versa, we can write a (highly non-unique) invertible stabilizer Hamiltonian from any such formation. Stacking of invertible stabilizer codes
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with respect to
\textbf{P}(P_{1},{\lambda}_{1};M_{1})
and
\textbf{P}(P_{2},{\lambda}_{2};M_{2})
corresponds to taking a direct sum of formations
(P_{1},{\lambda}_{1};M_{1},F_{1})\oplus(P_{2},{\lambda}_{2};M_{2},F_{2})
.

7.3
Relations among invertible stabilizer codes

We aim to classify invertible stabilizer codes up to a physically reasonable equivalence. We wish codes with the same “entanglement pattern” to belong to the same equivalence class. Below we impose the equivalence relations of the formations/codes — not all of them will be independent. First of all, codes of the type
(P,{\lambda};M,M)
are the product codes: they are given by
|0\rangle
in the standard basis for
\ell^{2}(M^{\vee})
. Therefore, we impose

\displaystyle(P,{\lambda};M,M)\sim 0\,.

(7.4)

Further, we deem
({\mbox{H}}^{-}(M),{\lambda};M,M^{\vee})
trivial. Indeed, in any finite volume, this code can be presented by a vector in the many-body Hilbert space which is a superposition of all basis vectors with equal coefficients. Thus, we impose

\displaystyle({\mbox{H}}^{-}(M);M,M^{\vee})\sim 0\,.

(7.5)

For the Pauli codes, stacking with a product code is the only stabilization that is allowed, because all the stabilizer modules are free. In our framework, we allow taking a quotient by the product codes: if there is a common
(R,S)
submodule
L\subset M
,
L\subset F
in a code
(P,{\lambda};M,F)
, such that
M/L
and
F/L
are
(R,S)
-modules, then we can impose
L
as a constraint on
\ell^{2}(M^{\vee})
. The new twisted Pauli group is orthogonal to the constraints. With these assumptions, we identify

\displaystyle(P,{\lambda};M,F)\sim(L^{\perp}/L,{\lambda}^{\perp}/{\lambda};M/L,F/L)\,.

(7.6)

In general, we wish to consider codes related by a finite-depth quantum-circuit (FDQC) as equivalent. A quantum circuit
\gamma
is defined by a partition
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
{\Lambda}
onto finite non-overlapping regions and a set of local unitaries
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with
\operatorname{supp}(U_{i})=\Gamma_{i}
for each
i\in I
and such that

\displaystyle\gamma(a)=\prod_{i\in I}U_{i}^{-1}a\;U_{i}\,,\quad a\in{\mathcal{A}}\

(7.7)

where
{\mathcal{A}}
is the algebra of local operators. Such an infinite product makes sense as long as
a
is an operator of finite support.

Consider a stack of invertible codes
(P,{\lambda};M,F)\oplus(P,{\lambda};F,G)
. It can be presented by a stacked Hamiltonian (
7.1
) acting on a tensor product
\ell^{2}(M^{\vee})\otimes\ell^{2}(F^{\vee})
by the corresponding Weyl operators. We can apply the SWAP circuit, the circuit that exchanges the tensor factors at each site — such a circuit is
1
-local. After applying the SWAP, we obtain a stacked code
(P,{\lambda};M,G)\oplus(P,{\lambda};F,F)
. By the argument above, the second summand represents a product code and can be dropped. In other words, we impose the equivalence

\displaystyle(P,{\lambda};M,F)\oplus(P,{\lambda};F,G)\sim(P,{\lambda};M,G)\,.

(7.8)

This relation implies
(P,{\lambda};M,F)\oplus(P,{\lambda};F,M)\sim 0
, making the monoid of the equivalence classes an abelian group.

Sometimes, an invertible stabilizer code can be found as a stabilizer code within another stabilizer code, i.e., obtained by condensation. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
be an invertible stabilizer Hamiltonian with respect to
(P_{1},{\lambda}_{1};M_{1})
. Assume we can choose a stabilizer Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
whose terms are chosen from the terms of
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
(
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
need not be locally topologically ordered). Physically, we drive the system to the regime where coefficients of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
are large such that the new effective Hilbert space is the ground space of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
. We view
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
as the Hamiltonian imposing constraints on
\ell^{2}(M_{1}^{\vee})
such that the new Hilbert space is labeled by the operators orthogonal to the constraints (recall that the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\vee})
is separated by
M
). Similarly, the new twisted quasi-Pauli group is formed by the operators preserving the ground space of
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
, i.e., the ones that are orthogonal to the constraints. If the resulting Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
is invertible, then we say that
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
are condensation-equivalent. Condensation equivalence of stabilizer Hamiltonians/codes precisely corresponds to the stable equivalence of formations (
3.12
).

To this end, we identify the group of the equivalence classes of invertible stabilizer codes with the Witt group
M^{ev}(R,S,-)
. When we drop lower-dimensional codes embedded as sub-dimensional subsystems, we arrive at the groups
\operatorname{E}_{\mathsf{d}}
defined in (
4.6
) and calculated in (
4.15
).

7.4
A generalized cohomology theory of invertible stabilizer codes

It is a famous conjecture by Alexei Kitaev
37
that there exists a generalized cohomology theory corresponding to invertible states, see
34
;
61
;
17
;
15
;
51
. There has been recent progress on this conjecture
39
, while the determination of the spectrum of the invertible states is still an open problem. Here we have proposed a classification of invertible
stabilizer
phases which a priori is different from the classification of the general invertible phases. Our classification scheme led us to the homotopy groups of the relative
L
-theory spectrum (
5.3
), which is a generalized Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum by Proposition
5.1
. We do not provide a formal argument why the spectrum of invertible phases must be the
L
-theory spectrum; rather, we rely on the naturalness of the construction and leave it as a conjecture. The true spectrum might need a modification to account for the
U(1)
subgroup of the unitary group that we have been dropping from consideration.

It is interesting to compare our proposed spectrum with other proposals, summarized, e.g., in
61
. An upcoming paper
10
constructs a spectrum of QCA based on the idea of blends — this approach turns out to be fruitful for the study of lattice anomalies (see §
1
and loc. cit. for the discussion of the relations between QCA and invertible phases).

8
Majorana stabilizer codes

In this appendix we comment on potential applications of odd anti-hermitian forms to Majorana stabilizer codes. The Majorana group is a group within the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded algebra of operators acting on a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Hilbert space
(\mathbb{C}^{1|1})^{\hat{\otimes}n}

4
;
1
, where
\hat{\otimes}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded tensor product. We define the Majorana operators by the following relations:

\displaystyle\chi_{i}\chi_{j}+\chi_{j}\chi_{i}=2\delta_{i,j}\,,\quad\quad\chi^{*}_{i}=\chi_{i}\,.

(8.1)

The Majorana group is the group generated by the Majorana operators and phases:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}=\langle\omega\,\chi_{1}^{x_{1}}\ldots\chi_{2n}^{x_{2n}}\,|\,\omega\in U(1)\,,x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2\rangle\,.

(8.2)

We label Majorana strings up to phases with the elements of
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2n}

\displaystyle({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{\otimes 2n}\ni x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})\mapsto\mu(x)\coloneqq\chi_{1}^{x_{1}}\ldots\chi_{2n}^{x_{2n}}

(8.3)

and express the commutator as follows

\displaystyle[\omega_{1}\mu(x),\omega_{2}\mu(x^{\prime})]=e^{2\pi i\kappa(x,x^{\prime})}

(8.4)

where
\kappa
is a
\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued anti-symmetric form on
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2n}
with the following matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2&\ldots&\ldots&1/2\\
1/2&0&1/2&\ldots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&&&1/2\\
1/2&\ldots&\ldots&1/2&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(8.5)

The Majorana group is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded by the grading operator
P
such that
P^{*}=P
,
P^{2}=1
and
P\chi_{i}P=-\chi_{i}
, i.e., products of odd numbers of Majorana operators have odd parity. In other words,

\displaystyle P\mu(x)P=(-1)^{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{2n}}\mu(x)\,.

(8.6)

The elements of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}
of even parity form a subgroup which we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}^{+}
. A
Majorana stabilizer code
is a commutative subgroup of
({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2n}
such that each element has even parity
4
;
1
.

Let us connect the description of the Majorana group using (
8.5
) with odd anti-symmetric linking forms over
({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}-\{0\})
. We define a form
F_{2}
on
{\mathcal{D}}_{2}={\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/2
with the following matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\
1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}\,.

(8.7)

Let
({\mathcal{D}},b)=nF_{2}
be a direct sum of
n
copies of
F_{2}
. For each
x\in{\mathcal{D}}
, we can have
\hat{b}(x,x)=0
or
\hat{b}(x,x)=1/2
. One checks that this assignment is a homomorphism, and we denote it by
c
:

\displaystyle c:{\mathcal{D}}\to\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\,,\quad x\mapsto\hat{b}(x,x)\,.

(8.8)

Since
b
is non-singular, there is an element
\hat{c}\in{\mathcal{D}}
representing
c
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
be the generators of
{\mathcal{D}}
, then we can choose
\hat{c}=e_{1}+e_{3}+\ldots+e_{2n-1}
. To this end, we describe
{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}
using
({\mathcal{D}},b)
while modifying the standard Weyl relations as follows:

\displaystyle\mu(x)\mu(y)=\mu(y)\mu(x)e^{2\pi ib(x,y-\hat{c})}.

(8.9)

Such a modified commutator indeed coincides with (
8.5
). The homomorphism
c
is nothing but the parity:

\displaystyle P\mu(x)P=e^{2\pi ic(x)}\mu(x)\,.

(8.10)

We note that there are odd anti-symmetric forms on finite abelian groups that are not isomorphic to
F_{2}
but they do not correspond to the Majorana
{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}
. We expect that the techniques used in the main part of this work can be extended to fermionic stabilizer codes on lattices.

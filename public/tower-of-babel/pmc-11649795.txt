Novel approach for Arabic fake news classification using embedding from large language features with CNN-LSTM ensemble model and explainable AI
Authors: Omar Ibrahim Aboulola, Muhammad Umer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The widespread fake news challenges the management of low-quality information, making effective detection strategies necessary. This study addresses this critical issue by advancing fake news detection in Arabic and overcoming limitations in existing approaches. Deep learning models, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), EfficientNetB4, Inception, Xception, ResNet, ConvLSTM and a novel voting ensemble framework combining CNN and LSTM are employed for text classification. The proposed framework integrates the ELMO word embedding technique having contextual representation capabilities, which is compared with GloVe, BERT, FastText and FastText subwords. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed voting ensemble, combined with ELMo word embeddings, consistently outperforms previous approaches. It achieves an accuracy of 98.42%, precision of 98.54%, recall of 99.5%, and an F1 score of 98.93%, offering an efficient and highly effective solution for text classification tasks.The proposed framework benchmark against state-of-the-art transformer architectures, including BERT and RoBERTa, demonstrates competitive performance with significantly reduced inference time and enhanced interpretability accompanied by a 5-fold cross-validation technique. Furthermore, this research utilizes the LIME XAI technique to provide deeper insights into the contribution of each feature in predicting a specific target class. These findings show the proposed framework’s effectiveness in dealing with the issues of detecting false news, particularly in Arabic text. By generating higher performance metrics and displaying comparable results, this work opens the way for more reliable and interpretable text classification solutions.

Introduction

The Internet has significantly altered global interconnection that provides an inexpensive and relatively uncontrolled means of communication. This has led to the formation of platform where dissemination of information can take place with much ease, hence becoming the main source of news and updates, more efficient than traditional methods. However, such growth has brought about new problems with it, and one of them is the spread of fake news1,2. Compounding this is the fact that people can offer their opinions and ideas freely on anything including trends with the use of social media and share this to numerous groups and pages. This freedom leads to a huge volume and variety of unfiltered material coming in. To combat the fast and unregulated propagation of false information on social media, scientific works rely on various approaches to at least partly or fully automatize the evaluation of the veracity of such posts3. Usually, these verification processes are initiated using data derived from social sites such as X, Facebook, YouTube, and other similar sites4,5. Some of these sites are noted to be major channels through which fake news circulated on the internet. These misleading pieces can appear in different forms, from mere sensational articles to fake stories and more, and are some of the many forms of disinformation that exist both in the online realm and in mainstream media. This means one can separate the fake news from actual news reporting. The paper6 claims that the spread of fake news far exceeds that of the real report. Moreover7, explains that Fake news involves intentionally fabricated falsehoods, which aim at being believed as accurate for specific purposes. An infamous example of this, according to Ref.8, is the 2016 US presidential election.

Fake news detection methods usually fall into two broad categories according to their approach: content based and context based. A content-based approach views phony news as an attempt to identify inconsistencies in textual data through features extracted from the body of such articles; it involves scrutiny of the writing style. The former uses syntax analysis, linguistic and lexical features, language semantics, and psycholinguistic cues in detecting fake news in content. At the same time, context-based approaches focus on user interaction and behavior patterns on social platforms to identify fake news. It uses insight from user profiles, discourse, and network interconnections in evaluating the credibility of news articles. Both context based, and content based approaches have advantages and disadvantages, and both paradigms often leverage machine learning or deep learning models.

It is within the territories of the Arabs that digitization of communication found a strong foothold, moving beyond the borders of the West. In the Middle East, 79% of the population interacts regularly with social platforms9. Moreover, around 64% of Middle-Eastern users actively seek social platforms to get the latest news frequently. Facebook and Twitter are considered primary sources of news and information by young adults. Instant messaging applications such as WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, Snapchat, and Line have become the new normal for users creating their newspapers. People end up getting updated only on the news their circle of friends find worthy of sharing; hence, getting a news feed specific to their network.

Social networking platforms serve as channels for both sharing official information and educating the public about current events, while also being used to spread fake news. Among all the Social networks, there is the utilization of Twitter which can be mentioned as one of the most popular platforms in the Arabian region9. Disseminating news through Twitter offers a cost-effective and rapid alternative compared to other channels, requiring minimal expenses and time. Its user-friendly interface and lax content oversight facilitate the swift and widespread circulation of fake news among its users10. However, people publish fake news, which is full of misinformation and lies11. The spread of fake news is usually motivated by political, social, or economic interests. As a result, it is dangerous to individual persons as well as organizations and even governments since they can influence public opinions, destabilize markets, and break societal unity12. Thus, this calls for immediate development and implementation of appropriate strategies to detect and counteract the dissemination of fake news in social media.

In reaction to the problem, some fact-checking services such as Misbar (a service that verifies information about rumors) have been developed to authenticate online news. Nonetheless, these platforms depend on human experts who evaluate the accuracy of news manually, a process that, although thorough can be time-consuming and costly thereby making them unscalable when it comes to verifying the vast number of news items. Machine learning has recently gained fame in tackling complex issues like identifying fake news more efficiently than before13. Two main approaches used today include automatic machine learning and deep learning in building automatic fake news classification models.

This study aims to protect individuals, organizations, and governments in the Arab world from the harmful effects of fake news. In the Arab landscape, where geopolitical and social dynamics are intricate, the impact of misinformation can be increased. The research aims to identify linguistic features that indicate deception in Arabic text and propose a framework designed for the unique characteristics of the Arab information ecosystem. Despite significant advancements in fake news detection, much of the existing research predominantly focuses on English-language content, leaving a considerable gap in addressing the complexities of fake news in other languages, particularly Arabic. The Arab world presents unique challenges, such as linguistic diversity, cultural nuances, and the rapid spread of misinformation on social media platforms. Current methods, often developed for English-language data, are not fully equipped to handle the subtleties of the Arabic language, including its complex syntax, semantics, and regional variations. Moreover, while deep learning techniques have shown promising results in fake news detection, their application to Arabic text remains underexplored. This research seeks to fill this gap by focusing specifically on Arabic fake news detection. Related work

Arabic language processing is a unique and difficult direction of research with diverse topics and practices. Of course, other than detecting fake news and spam, there are other significant and appropriate tasks to start with: Arabic opinion mining and recommendation systems. Some diverse research has been achieved in developing various techniques for Arab fake news detection. This section will try to provide the current trend and some of the most frequent works reported on the identification of Arabic fake news.

In Ref.14, authors utilized a deep-transformer model to perform analysis on the fake news written in Arabic. The results of this study conclude that deep learning models’ performance is not better than transformer learning models in terms of accuracy. QARiB based on the transformer model15 established an accuracy of 95%, and the deep learning of GRU attained the highest figure of 83%. Based on this, Alkair et al.16 published research on the detection of fake news in Arabic using comments from the YouTube platform. To solve the classification problem of the comments as rumor or non-rumor, the authors used a range of machine learning and deep learning techniques. To classify the fake news of the Arabic tweets related to COVID-19, Mahlous and Laith17 proposed an intelligent system utilizing various deep learning models and the feature extraction method which provided high performances with 95 % accuracy by the CNN model. Employing the principle of the deep learning system, in detail, the researchers Khanam, Alqahtani, Sangaiah, and Naufal18 developed a best machine learning faking news identification model using SVM and neural network. Himdi et al.19 proposed a system for Arabic fake news using textual analysis and machine learning models and showed that the random forest (RF) models were effective in terms of accuracy.

Arabic fake news detection in an efficient manner was described by Wotaifi and Dhannoon20 who make use of a deep ensemble model with an accuracy of the CNN-LSTM ensemble model of 97.2%. Najadat et al.21 have used headline-article data in Arabic and suggested a deep learning-based method to detect fake news where the AFND-CNN-LSTM was seen to be performing well in the fake news detection. Nasief et al.22 proposed a deep contextualized embedding method for segregating fake news in Arabic, and the performance of the ARBERT is quite impressive.

Fouad et al.23 performed deep learning techniques in detecting fake news in the Arabic language and reported that amongst different models, the BiLSTM gave the best results. In Ref.24, authors did Arabic news sentiment classification with a significant amount of success through integrating MARBERT and CNN. Also, Dahou et al.25 introduced a new approach for Arabic fake news classification involving suggested feature selection method modifications. Another study26 is dedicated to aspect-level normalization of Arabic texts using the Seq2Seq model.

Almandouh et al.27 and Wotaifi and Dhannoon28 both contributed to the detection of Arabic fake news through ensemble models and deep learning-based hybrid models, respectively, showcasing notable accuracy. Hawashin et al.29 further enhanced Arabic fake news detection through feature selection techniques.

These studies collectively underscore the importance of culturally informed strategies in combatting misinformation in the Arab world. However, it’s crucial to acknowledge potential limitations, such as biases inherent in machine learning algorithms and the scarcity of labeled datasets, to refine methodologies and foster a deeper understanding of fake news detection complexities in the Arabic language.

Word embedding

This process maps words on numerical values spread out in a vector space with continuous dimensions. Since textual input in this case is inherently discrete, this allows the machine learning models to understand and abstract the textual input in a way that preserves the semantic relations between them. Traditional methods like BoW or TF-IDF address words separately, by not taking into account relations between them. On the other hand, word embeddings employ the high dimensionality of the vector space in which the positions of similar-meaning words are close to each other to capture the context and meaning of words31,32. Word representation helps the model to understand all the complexities of language which can be useful in the case of sentiment analysis.

GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation) is a word embedding technique that identifies word vectors from the basic co-occurrence data extracted from a vast corpus33. It can record both global context and semantic link to it which makes it useful in tasks such as text categorization and natural language processing.

BERT is a word embedding method that determines a words meaning based on the context of that word in a phrase34. Unlike general embeddings where methods such as stable word embeddings are used, BERT is a bidirectional transformer type that efficiently comprehends both forward and backward word relations making it ideal for complex NLP operations.

FastText is another embedding developed by Facebooks Artificial Intelligence Research lab that improves on this by incorporating subword information. In combination with it, words are represented in the form of bags of character n-grams thus, taking into account morphological details and being resistant to out-of-vocabulary words31.

FastText Subword is an extension of the FastText word embedding technology that addresses the limits of regular word embeddings, especially for rare or out-of-vocabulary (OOV) terms. Unlike regular FastText, which encodes words as single vectors, FastText Subword divides words into smaller subword units, like character n-grams. This method is especially helpful for morphologically rich languages, such as Arabic, because it takes into account prefixes, suffixes, and root variants, resulting in more robust and context-aware embedding.

ELMo, which is a novel method for word representation, takes into account the context that the said word belongs to Ref.35. ELMo does not fix vectors to words and uses a bidirectional LSTM neural network for processing the entire phrases in a context. ELMo is built from multiple biLSTM layers, each of which focuses on a different layer of the context while giving the model a way to understand multiple levels of this data. ELMo is especially beneficial for tasks such as question answering, sentiment analysis, and also machine translation. Contextual embeddings make language nuanced allowing it to be very handy in NLP even when newer models like BERT exist.

Deep learning models

Deep learning models have shown great potential in low-resource text classification tasks. This study focuses on Arabic fake news classification and explores various deep learning models, including Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), EfficientNetB4, Inception, Xception, ResNet, and ConvLSTM.

Multilayer perceptron

A feed-forward artificial neural network or MLP is made up of an input layer, one or many hidden layers, and an output layer36. Neurons in these layers apply non-linear transformation to the input data to improve the network learning process which is a major advantage over linear perceptron in handling non-separable data. MLP has a great application area in all fields such as speech recognition, natural language processing, and medical image processing.

Convolutional neural network

CNN can be seen as the basic structure in deep learning, which seems to work best in image-based processing37. They include convolutional layers that extract features from images, pooling layers that restrict the size of the feature and reduce overfitting, flattened layers that turn the data into arrays, and activation layers using the ReLU function. In this study, a dropout rate of 0 was derived from the assessment made from time to time and at the end of the study period used in coming up with the results.

Long short term memory

Text classification is fascinating through LSTM models since they are ambitious in identifying long-range dependencies in the sequences of data38. They have components such as output, input, and forget gates to store some information and reject unwanted data. LSTMs make use of recurrent connections and cell memory blocks to make precise predictions.

EfficientNetB4

EfficientNetB4 is used for the text classification tasks focusing on the nets accuracy and the required computational resources39. Its architecture which is based on compound scaling increases the depth, width, and resolution of the system. The structures point-wise convolution and depth-wise convolution are applied in EfficientNetB4 to lessen the number of parameters to learn, which makes the model ideal for transfer learning, but requires efficient hardware for efficient training.

Inception

Inception-v3, a transfer learning model, incorporates multiple layers including various Inception components to extract distinctive features and reduce parameter count40. The model comprises parallel pooling and convolutional layers, optimizing parameter efficiency. In the subsequent series of experiments, the accuracy metrics presented in Table 3 exhibit a range from 89.68% to 98.42%. While accuracy in isolation may not adequately underscore a model’s performance, heightened accuracy does signify improved overall model efficacy. Precision and recall are pivotal in showcasing a model’s capability to identify true positives and true negatives. Precision gauges the accuracy of positive predictions, with values ranging between 91.25% and 98.54%. Lower instances of false positives indicate higher precision. Recall, also termed sensitivity or true positive rate, evaluates a model’s aptitude in recognizing all relevant positive instances, with percentages spanning from 91.67% to 99.15%. This means improved recall results in the indication of fewer cases of false negatives. Thus the F1 scale, which takes into consideration the number of false positives and false negatives averages at 91. 48% to 98. 93%. A higher F1 score means a higher balance between recall and the precision of the findings.

From the results gathered from the dataset, the ensemble model had better performances with accuracy and micro, and macro precision, an F1 of 98%, and a recall of 99%. On the other hand, the least accurate classifier for the Arabic fake news distinction was Inception.

Despite a common notion that works with FastText embeddings can be readily applied to ELMo embeddings as well since they share the same basic approach, a number of aspects make the former better suited for text classification tasks. Firstly, ELMo embeddings capture context by extending the length of the context beyond words or even phrases up to the whole sentence. These allow ELMo to encode richer semantic meanings, and understand the language nuances better, and that has a direct bearing on how the model comes with a more accurate representation for classification. Also, ELMo embeddings are based on deep contextualized embeddings arising from large training corpora. These representations embody more complex syntactic structures and dependencies since ELMo is based on these mechanisms of understanding languages, thereby helping achieve more informative and discriminative vectors for the classification problem. Also, ELMo embeddings are better at transfer learning as they are pre-trained on large scale language modeling tasks. Thus, to attend to all words in general, ELMo first learns general language patterns and semantics during pre-training so that it can be easily adapted to specific classification tasks through fine-tuning. Last but not least, it should be mentioned that ELMo embeddings are rather flexible and versatile, which allows incorporating different kinds of text data and performing different types of classifications using considering syntactic and semantic features.

LIME

One of the such techniques is Local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) which is quite beneficial in the field of Explainable AI, particularly in the text classification area. It operates based on building interference of the input data and studying the change that happens in the functions outcomes. LIME specifically provides local explanations for a single prediction and the figures are understandable by the target user, so they reveal how a precise decision was made. For example, it can explain the top terms or the most significant phrases in the given document that impact the classification made by the developed natural language processing applications LIME. This interpretability however helps the users to have a feel of what the AI models are all about and makes them to be trusted in such sectors as health and finance where the use of AI models is very vital44.

In this aspect, LIMEs capacity to explain the reasoning of complicated models enriches its uses across various areas and contributes to the appropriate implementation of AI systems. Expounding on the contributions to the fields of transparency and interpretability, it emerges as a beneficial analytical tool for those objective practitioners, and researchers who are trying to understand and verify the results of dynamics of text classification models45.

Conclusion

The spread of fake news continues to pose a significant challenge in managing misinformation, emphasizing the need for effective detection mechanisms. This research addresses this important issue by introducing an advanced framework for fake news detection in Arabic. Various deep learning models, including CNN, LSTM, MLP, EfficientNetB4, Inception, Xception, ResNet, and ConvLSTM, along with a novel voting ensemble framework, have been utilized for this purpose. The integration of ELMo word embeddings enhances the frameworks performance. Comparative analysis with other word embedding techniques, such as GloVe, BERT, FastText, and FastText subwords, highlights the superiority of ELMo in this context. The proposed voting ensemble achieves remarkable metrics, including an accuracy of 98.42% making it an efficient and highly effective solution for text classification tasks. Furthermore, the framework demonstrates competitive performance against state-of-the-art transformer architectures, including BERT and RoBERTa. It achieves these results with significantly reduced inference time and enhanced interpretability, validated through a robust 5-fold cross-validation technique. The application of the LIME XAI technique enhances the interpretability of the model, offering valuable insights into the contribution of individual features in predicting specific target classes. In conclusion, the findings of this study underscore the robustness and efficiency of the proposed framework in addressing the challenges of fake news detection in Arabic text. By achieving superior performance metrics and ensuring reliability and interpretability, this work offers a valuable contribution to the field, paving the way for further advancements in fake news detection and reducing the spread of misinformation in digital platforms. Future work will focus on expanding the language dictionary by the fusion of extensive word embedding dictionaries. Additionally, incorporating transfer learning features alongside deep learning techniques will be explored to further enhance the accuracy and effectiveness of Arabic fake news detection.

Dear Editor,

Cell-assisted lipotransfer (CAL) improves traditional fat transfer by mixing adipose-derived stem cells (ASCs) with harvested fat to enhance graft survival. Some of the ASCs from liposuctioned fat differentiate into adipocytes and endothelial cells, boosting adipogenesis and angiogenesis1. Notably, 3D-cultured ASC spheroids (3D-ASCs) show higher viability compared to 2D-cultured ASCs (2D-ASCs) due to better cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions, improving ischemic resistance, angiogenic factor secretion, and nutrient distribution2. Despite evidence from studies, He et al.3 and El Harane et al.4 showing that CAL with 3D-ASCs enhances fat graft survival through increased adipogenesis and angiogenesis; these studies were based on protein or microarray analyses. To further support these findings and understand the molecular mechanisms, we performed in-vitro experiments, tracked GFP-expressing ASCs, and conducted comprehensive RNA sequencing analysis.

The methods for the in-vitro and in-vivo experiments and statistical analyses are provided in the Supplementary Text (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D491). The primer sequences used for RT-PCR analysis in the in-vitro experiments are listed in Supplementary Table S1 (Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D492).

In-vitro experiments, 3D-ASCs were created using the AggreWell 24-well system with 1×106 cells per well, resulting in spheroids containing 850±32 cells each. After 24 h, these spheroids averaged 182±15 μm in diameter. Live/dead staining revealed only a few scattered dead cells and flow cytometry confirmed high cell viability, with 90.40±2.64% of the cells being viable (Fig. 1A–D). 3D-ASCs significantly enhanced the expression of crucial angiogenic factors (Fgfb, Igf, and Hgf), anti-inflammatory cytokines (Il10 and Tgfb), and adipogenic differentiation markers (Cebpa and Pparg) (Fig. 1E). As fat grafts are often subjected to inflammation and hypoxia in the transplanted area, leading to early cell death, volume shrinkage, and low retention rates, we designed in-vitro experiments to assess the protective effect of ASCs on 3T3-L1 preadipocytes under inflammation and hypoxic conditions involving either macrophage co-culture or 1% oxygen (Fig. 1F, L). Under inflammatory conditions, intracellular ROS production, gene expression, and protein levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 were assessed in preadipocytes. IL-1β gene expression was significantly lower in the 3D-ASC treated group (Fig. 1G–K). Under hypoxic conditions, 3D-ASCs exhibited superior effects compared to 2D-ASCs, reducing ROS, Casp3 gene level, and Caspase 3/7 activity (Fig. 1M–O). Overall, these results suggest that 3D-ASCs may have greater potential than 2D-ASCs for exerting adipogenic, angiogenic, and anti-inflammatory effects, as well as for providing enhanced resistance to hypoxia-induced oxidative stress and apoptosis.

In an in vivo experiment using a murine fat graft model (Fig. 2A), 8 weeks after fat grafting, both the residual fat graft volume and ASC retention in the grafted fat were significantly greater in the 3D-ASCs group compared to the 2D-ASCs group (Fig. 2B–E). These results suggest that 3D-ASC supplementation enhances ASC retention within fat grafts, thereby improving the overall retention rate of fat grafts compared to 2D-ASC supplementation.

Immunofluorescence assays, using perilipin and CD31 antibodies, revealed increased viable adipocytes and vascularity in the 3D-ASCs group compared to the 2D-ASCs group (Fig. 2F–I). Additionally, the number of cells co-expressing GFP with perilipin or CD31, indicative of ASC differentiation into adipocytes or endothelial cells, was significantly higher in the 3D-ASCs group (Fig. 2J–M). These findings indicate that 3D-ASCs facilitate a greater degree of differentiation into adipocytes and endothelial cells than 2D-ASCs, thereby enhancing adipogenesis and angiogenesis in fat grafts.

To further investigate the molecular mechanisms behind enhanced adipogenesis and angiogenesis, bulk RNA sequencing was performed. Differential gene expression analysis identified 50 upregulated and 50 downregulated genes in the 3D-ASC group compared to the 2D-ASC group, as shown in the heatmap (Fig. 3A). The volcano plot revealed 215 significantly upregulated and 242 downregulated genes (Fig. 3B, Supplementary Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D493). Gene set enrichment analysis indicated higher expression of pathways related to adipogenesis, fatty acid metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, and peroxisomes in the 3D-ASC group, while pathways associated with TNF-α signaling, hypoxia, inflammation, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) were downregulated (Fig. 3C, D, Supplementary Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D494). Considering both in-vitro and in-vivo results and the fact that the interaction between peroxisomes and mitochondria in adipocytes protects the cells from ROS and generates PPARγ ligands through polyunsaturated fatty acid metabolism, this process is believed to promote adipogenesis by activating the C/EBPα-PPARγ axis5. Furthermore, considering the link between fatty acid metabolism and angiogenesis, free fatty acids from grafted fat are oxidized in endothelial cell mitochondria, enhancing angiogenesis through the mTORC1 pathway, which was upregulated in the 3D-ASC group (data not shown, NES: 1.34, FDR: 0.075, P<0.05). Fatty acid oxidation also boosts ATP production through oxidative phosphorylation under glucose depletion, increasing the resistance to oxidative stress6. The reduced expression of hypoxia, inflammation, and EMT pathways suggests better resistance to oxidative stress and lower inflammation, potentially inhibiting fibrosis progression. This corresponds with immunofluorescence assay results showing decreased M1 macrophages (pro-inflammatory) and increased M2 macrophages (anti-inflammatory), along with reduced HIF-1α expression, typically promoted under hypoxic conditions (Supplementary Fig. S1, Supplemental Digital Content 5, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D495).

The spheroid configuration of ASCs significantly enhances angiogenesis and adipogenesis, which are essential for effective adipose tissue engraftment and regeneration. Challenges in spheroid culture include the time needed to form stable spheroids and methods for large-scale production to ensure an adequate number for injection. Recently, we showed that stable and uniform spheroids can be efficiently generated within 2 h using a highly viscous methylcellulose medium7. Future advancements could include combining microwell technology, methylcellulose-based incubation, and automated production systems to enable large-scale spheroid manufacturing8–10.

Microwell technology provides a simple, efficient, and standardized method for spheroid formation, which is noted for its ease and reproducibility. CAL using spheroid ASCs enhances fat graft retention rates with improved angiogenic, adipogenic, and immunomodulatory properties, carrying various clinical implications. This approach can reduce the need for reoperations, maintain practical procedure times, and improve patient satisfaction by enhancing overall outcomes and offering more effective treatment options. Additionally, it offers broad prospects for advancing regenerative medicine across various fields. To confirm its efficacy and evaluate long-term outcomes and potential complications, further large-scale cohort clinical trials are essential.

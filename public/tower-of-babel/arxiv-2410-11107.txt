Recursively Feasible Stochastic Model Predictive Control for Time-Varying Linear Systems Subject to Unbounded Disturbances footnoteinfo
Jacob W. Knaup correspondingAuthor, Panagiotis Tsiotras

Abstract
Model predictive control solves a constrained optimization problem online in order to compute an implicit closed-loop control policy. Recursive feasibility—guaranteeing that the optimal control problem will have a solution at every time step—is an important property to guarantee the success of any model predictive control approach. However, recursive feasibility is difficult to establish in a stochastic setting and, in particular, in the presence of disturbances having unbounded support (e.g., Gaussian noise). The problem is further exacerbated for time-varying systems, in which case recursive feasibility must be established also in a robust sense, over all possible future time-varying parameter values, as well as in a stochastic sense, over all potential disturbance realizations. This work presents a method for ensuring the recursive feasibility of a convex, affine-feedback stochastic model predictive control problem formulation for systems with time-varying system matrices and unbounded disturbances using ideas from covariance steering stochastic model predictive control. It is additionally shown that the proposed approach ensures the closed-loop operation of the system will satisfy the desired chance constraints in practice, and that the stochastic model predictive control problem may be formulated as a convex program so that it may be efficiently solved in real-time.



keywords

Stochastic optimal control; Model predictive control; Convex optimization; Linear time-varying systems, Uncertain systems.

Introduction

Model predictive control (MPC) is an attractive optimization-based method for controlling autonomous systems owing to its ability to handle input and state constraints while ensuring convergence under real-time computational limitations
[
3
]
.
Stochastic MPC (SMPC), in particular, has gained interest as a method of providing probabilistic guarantees of constraint satisfaction and convergence to a neighborhood of the target in the presence of random disturbances.
However, several challenges of stochastic MPC still exist.
Whereas deterministic MPC optimizes over control actions at each time step, stochastic MPC must, instead, optimize over control
policies
as a means of controlling the dispersion of trajectories over time.
Since optimizing over arbitrary policies is not easily tractable, typical formulations consider an affine feedback policy
[
24
]
.
In some cases, the feedback gain is calculated offline and is fixed; a more optimal approach is to design the feedback policy online in conjunction with a feed-forward control action.
This, however, can lead to challenges with preserving the convexity of the problem formulation.
Another issue of SMPC is ensuring convergence.
Whereas for deterministic MPC it is possible to show asymptotic convergence for the case of linear systems, for SMPC only convergence to a neighborhood of the origin can be shown since the system is subject to a persistent disturbance
[
23
]
.
Finally, providing recursive feasibility guarantees for SMPC approaches is challenging when the noise is sampled from a distribution with unbounded support (e.g., Gaussian distribution).
Existing approaches addressing this issue typically consider either a truncated Gaussian distribution or relax the problem formulation
[
24
,
5
,
21
,
9
,
16
]
.

In our recent work
[
17
]
, we formulated a stochastic MPC approach for linear
time-varying
systems based on the recent theoretical developments in the area of finite-horizon optimal covariance steering
[
11
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
1
,
2
,
25
,
26
]
.
The covariance steering problem addresses the issue of how to optimally drive the first two moments of a linear system from some initial condition to a given final condition over a finite horizon.
This problem is closely related to the questions of recursive feasibility and stability of SMPC, since bounds on the first two moments of the state distribution may be used to ensure constraint satisfaction and convergence.
Using results from covariance steering, in
[
17
]
we formulated an online affine-feedback solution to the SMPC problem that can be efficiently solved using convex programming, which we refer to as covariance steering stochastic MPC (CS-SMPC).
Assuming recursive feasibility, the method provides asymptotic tracking convergence for the nominal system and convergence to a neighborhood of the reference trajectory for the stochastic system.
However, the question of ensuring the recursive feasibility, and consequently closed-loop constraint satisfaction, of CS-SMPC was not addressed in
[
17
]
and is the focus of this paper.

1.1
Literature Review

Most papers addressing the issue of recursive feasibility for stochastic systems assume that the disturbance is drawn from a distribution with bounded support (e.g., truncated Gaussian distribution).
In
[
5
,
21
,
4
,
20
]
, the authors consider a linear time-invariant (LTI) system subject to bounded additive disturbances and design an SMPC controller based on a fixed stabilizing feedback gain which is computed offline.
In
[
27
,
19
]
, the authors consider an SMPC problem for an LTI system subject to an additive Gaussian disturbance.
The authors in these papers optimize over the space of affine feedback policies; however, they also approximate the Gaussian with a truncated distribution, essentially formulating a robust MPC control problem.

One of the few works that considered disturbances drawn from a distribution with unbounded support is
[
9
]
, in which the authors optimize over the space of initial conditions and affine feedback policies in order to achieve recursive feasibility.
The drawback of this strategy is that if the current state would lead to infeasibility, an open loop control must be used,
discarding the most recent state information.
The authors of
[
9
]
extended their method to consider output feedback in
[
10
]
.
However, the method of ensuring recursive feasibility remains similar, and suffers from the same drawbacks, as in
[
9
]
.

A more recent work
[
18
]
notes that the relaxation of the initialization proposed in
[
9
]
is still the state-of-the-art and has been utilized in numerous theoretical extensions and applications.
The authors of
[
18
]
propose to improve the initialization strategy by allowing for a continuous interpolation between using the most recent state measurement and relying on open-loop prediction of the state.
They prove recursive feasibility and convergence for their method; however, their method still relies on relaxing the initialization constraint and may discard the most recent state information.
Additionally, the authors in
[
18
]
use a fixed feedback gain computed offline rather than optimizing over affine-feedback policies.
The authors of
[
16
,
15
]
also extended the work of
[
9
]
.
However, instead of optimizing over the state initialization, they propose to include the state measurement only in the cost function while the constraints are given purely in terms of the nominal system through a tube-based constraint-tightening approach.
Unfortunately, this approach can be overly conservative due to the reliance on a pre-computed feedback policy and does not take advantage of the most recent information in applying the state and input constraints.

The authors of
[
28
]
also consider unbounded disturbances; however, they take a different approach.
They address the issue of recursive feasibility and establish convergence for an unconstrained variation of the problem by softening the chance constraints using a penalty function (i.e., by imposing them through the cost function).
These existing results all require relaxing the problem’s constraints (either the current state initialization or the safety constraints) in order to prove recursive feasibility.

Very few works deal with SMPC for linear time-varying (LTV) systems, and those that do, only consider bounded disturbances.
Considerable challenges hamper the direct application of existing recursive feasibility results to LTV systems.
For example, the results obtained in
[
9
]
require a pre-computed stabilizing feedback gain to design the terminal constraints.
However, determining a single feedback gain that is stabilizing over all possible time-varying future system parameters is challenging.
Likewise, the authors of
[
28
]
assume that the system’s state-transition matrix is Schur or Lyapunov stable which is often not the case in practice and can be difficult to verify ahead of time for an LTV system.
Therefore, there is a need for results addressing LTV systems.
One of the few works addressing LTV systems is
[
13
]
which considers a robust MPC problem formulated with an online affine-feedback policy.
However, as mentioned above, the authors limit their analysis to bounded disturbances.
The authors of
[
14
,
22
]
consider a linear parameter-varying system that approximates a nonlinear deterministic system.
They show that recursive feasibility can be ensured using robust tube-based and invariant set-based approaches, respectively, provided the parameters lie in a compact set.

As summarized in Table
1
, the existing literature on the recursive feasibility of SMPC for systems subject to unbounded disturbances is extremely sparse, especially for the less-conservative case in which an affine-feedback policy is computed online. Furthermore, the overlap between results that consider both unbounded disturbances
and
time-varying systems is non-existent.
In this work, we fill this gap in the literature by establishing sufficient conditions for the recursive feasibility of SMPC for LTV systems subject to unbounded disturbances.
Moreover, we provide a practical approach for computing the terminal constraint necessary in order to ensure that these conditions hold in practice.
We show that our method results in satisfaction of the chance constraints during closed-loop operation of the system as desired.
Finally, we show that these results hold for the particular formulation of the SMPC problem used by CS-SMPC in
[
17
]
, which optimizes online over the space of affine feedback policies by solving a convex program.

Problem Formulation

We consider a linear time-varying (LTV) system given by

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=A_{k}x_{k}+B_{k}u_{k}+D_{k}w_{k}+r_{k},

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
and

are the fully observable state and control action at time-step
k

\mathbb{E}[w_{k_{1}}w_{k_{2}}^{\top}]=\mathbb{E}[w_{k_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[w_{k_{2}}^{\top}]=0

k_{1}\neq k_{2}

The system parameter matrices/vector are given by
A_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{x}}
,
B_{k}\in\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{u}}
,
D_{k}\in\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{w}}
, and
r_{k}\in\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}

\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{R}
are such that

\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}A_{k}&B_{k}&D_{k}&r_{k}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{co}\{S^{1},\dots,S^{N_{p}}\}

\displaystyle=\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{p}}\lambda_{\ell}S^{\ell}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{p}}\lambda_{\ell}=1,~\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0\},

(2)

where

S^{\ell}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\ell}&B^{\ell}&D^{\ell}&r^{\ell}\end{bmatrix}
, for all
\ell=1,\dots,N_{p}
.

The system (
1
) is subject to
N_{x}
and

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{x,i})\geq 1-p_{x,i},

(3a)

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}u_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{u,j})\geq 1-p_{u,j},

(3b)

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigcap_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\left\{x:\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}x\leq\beta_{x,i}\right\},

(4a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigcap_{j=1}^{N_{u}}\left\{u:\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}u\leq\beta_{u,j}\right\}.

(4b)

The states and control actions of system (
1
) are associated with a cost function given by

\displaystyle\ell_{k}(x_{k},u_{k})=(x_{k}-x_{g_{k}})^{\top}Q_{k}(x_{k}-x_{g_{k}})+u_{k}^{\top}R_{k}u_{k},

where, for all

,
Q_{k}\succeq 0
and
R_{k}\succ 0
are known bounded matrices, and
x_{g_{k}}\in\mathcal{X}
is a known target sequence to track.

To design an optimal control policy
for system (
1
), define the following Covariance Steering SMPC (CS-SMPC) problem given by

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\rho}_{k}^{N-1}(\cdot)}J_{N}(\mu_{k},\Sigma_{k};\bm{\rho}_{k}^{N-1}(\cdot))=\sum_{t=k}^{k+N-1}\mathbb{E}[\ell_{t}(x_{t|k},u_{t|k})],

(6a)

subject to

\displaystyle x_{t+1|k}=A_{t}x_{t|k}+B_{t}u_{t|k}+D_{t}w_{t}+r_{t},

(6b)

\displaystyle x_{k|k}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{k},\Sigma_{k}),

\displaystyle u_{t|k}=\rho_{t|k}(x_{t|k}),

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}x_{t|k}\leq\beta_{x,i})\geq 1-p_{x,i},~i=1,\dots,N_{x},

(6e)

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}u_{t|k}\leq\beta_{u,j})\geq 1-p_{u,j},~j=1,\dots,N_{u},

(6f)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{k+N|k}]\in\mathcal{X}_{f}^{\mu},

(6g)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(x_{k+N|k}-\mathbb{E}[x_{k+N|k}])(x_{k+N|k}-\mathbb{E}[x_{k+N|k}])^{\top}]\preceq\Sigma_{f},

(6h)

where, for all

x_{t|k}
and

are the predicted state and control action, respectively, at time
t
computed at the present time

must be designed to ensure the recursive feasibility of (

The optimization is performed over the sequence of planned state-feedback control

Since the future state sequence

\bm{\rho}_{k}^{\ast N-1}(\cdot)=\{\rho_{k|k}^{\ast}(\cdot),\dots,\rho_{k+N-1|k}^{\ast}(\cdot)\}

\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\ast N}=\{\mu_{k|k}^{\ast},\dots,\mu_{k+N|k}^{\ast}\}

\bm{\Sigma}_{k}^{\ast N}=\{\Sigma_{k|k}^{\ast},\dots,\Sigma_{k+N|k}^{\ast}\}
,

\mu_{k|k}^{\ast}=\mu_{k}
and
\Sigma_{k|k}^{\ast}=\Sigma_{k}
.
The control command applied to system (
1
) at time

is then given by

\displaystyle u_{k}=u_{k|k}^{\ast}.

(7)

[

]
, we showed that Problem (

) can be cast as a convex program for a suitable parameterization of the control policy (see Theorem 2 of

Additionally, we showed that if system (

) is controlled by (

, see Theorem 4 of
[

]
).
However, both of these results rely on the condition that a solution to (
6
) exists at every time
k=0,1,\dots
, using the initialization (
6c
)
where
\mu_{k}
and
\Sigma_{k}
are given by

(\mu_{k},\Sigma_{k})=\begin{cases}(x_{0},0),&k=0,\\[5.0pt]
(\mu^{\ast}_{k|k-1},\Sigma^{\ast}_{k|k-1}),&k>0,\end{cases}

(8)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{k+1|k}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mu_{k}+B_{k}\bar{u}_{k}+r_{k},

(9a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k+1|k}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\Sigma_{k}A_{k}^{\top}+A_{k}\Sigma_{xu_{k}}B_{k}^{\top}+B_{k}\Sigma_{xu_{k}}^{\top}A_{k}^{\top}

The aim of this work is to investigate conditions implying the feasibility of Problem (

Recursive Feasibility Conditions

.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{f}^{\mu}
and
\Sigma_{f}
be such that,

\mu\in\mathcal{X}_{f}^{\mu}

\Sigma\preceq\Sigma_{f}
,

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}x\leq\beta_{x,i})\geq 1-p_{x,i},~i=1,\dots,N_{x},

\displaystyle\Pr(\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}u\leq\beta_{u,j})\geq 1-p_{u,j},~j=1,\dots,N_{u},

\displaystyle A\Sigma A^{\top}+A\Sigma_{xu}B^{\top}+B\Sigma_{xu}^{\top}A^{\top}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+B\Sigma_{u}B^{\top}+DD^{\top}\preceq\Sigma_{f},

(10d)

where

4
Terminal Constraint Design

To compute the terminal set
\mathcal{X}_{f}^{\mu}
and the terminal covariance
\Sigma_{f}
satisfying (

\displaystyle u_{t|k}

\displaystyle=\rho_{t|k}(x_{t|k})=v_{t|k}+L_{t|k}(x_{t|k}-\mu_{t|k}),

, and

Lemma 3
.

A terminal covariance matrix
\Sigma_{f}
satisfying (
10d
) is given by
the solution to the semidefinite program (SDP)

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{\Sigma},Z}~~\mathtt{tr}(\tilde{\Sigma})

(17a)

subject to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\Sigma}-D^{\ell}D^{\ell\top}&A^{\ell}\tilde{\Sigma}+B^{\ell}Z\\
\tilde{\Sigma}A^{\ell\top}+Z^{\top}B^{\ell\top}&\tilde{\Sigma}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(17b)

for all
\ell=1,\dots,N_{p}
, where
\tilde{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{x}}
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times n_{x}}
.

Proof 4.1
.

Under the control policy parameterization (
16
),
\rho(x)=v+L(x-\mu)
, (
10d
) reduces to the existence of a
L\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times n_{x}}
, such that
(A+BL)\Sigma(A+BL)^{\top}+DD^{\top}\preceq\Sigma_{f}
, for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
D\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\Sigma\preceq\Sigma_{f}
.
Thus, a terminal covariance matrix
\Sigma_{f}
satisfying (
10d
), is given by a matrix
\tilde{\Sigma}
satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}\succeq(A+B\tilde{L})\tilde{\Sigma}(A+B\tilde{L})^{\top}+DD^{\top},

(18)

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
D\in\mathcal{D}
and for a particular choice of
\tilde{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times n_{x}}
.
Similar to the approach in
[
12
]
, using the Schur complement, (

\displaystyle M=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\Sigma}&(A+B\tilde{L})&D\\
(A+B\tilde{L})^{\top}&\tilde{\Sigma}^{-1}&0\\
D^{\top}&0&I\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(19)

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
D\in\mathcal{D}
.

Using the Schur complement and (
2

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\Sigma}-D^{\ell}D^{\ell\top}&(A^{\ell}+B^{\ell}\tilde{L})\\
(A^{\ell}+B^{\ell}\tilde{L})^{\top}&\tilde{\Sigma}^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(20)

for all
\ell=1,\dots,N_{p}
.
Finally, (
20
) may be converted to the equivalent LMI given by
(
17b
)
, which is convex in
\tilde{\Sigma}
and
Z
, by multiplying from both sides by
\mathrm{blkdiag}(I,\tilde{\Sigma})
and using a change of variables given by

.
Thus,
\Sigma_{f}
satisfying (
10d

17

Let the optimal solution of Problem (

\tilde{\Sigma}^{\ast},~Z^{\ast}

The optimal control gain
\tilde{L}^{\ast}
may be recovered from

\tilde{\Sigma}^{\ast}\succ 0

We are now ready to introduce the second main result, which establishes how to compute
\mathcal{X}^{\mu}_{f}
and
\Sigma_{f}
so that the conditions of Theorem

Let

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\text{safe}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\left\{\mu:\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}\mu\leq\tilde{\beta}_{x,i}\right\},

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\text{safe}}

\displaystyle\hskip-4.2679pt\tilde{\beta}_{x,i}=\beta_{x,i}-\sqrt{\alpha_{x,i}^{\top}\Sigma_{f}\alpha_{x,i}}\,\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{x,i}),

.

(23a)

\displaystyle\hskip-4.2679pt\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}v+\sqrt{\alpha_{u,j}^{\top}\tilde{L}^{\ast}\Sigma\tilde{L}^{\ast\top}\alpha_{u,j}}\,\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{u,j})\leq\beta_{u,j},

4.2
Convex Formulation

Theorem
4
shows how to compute the terminal constraints under the control parameterization (
16
), so as to ensure recursive feasibility.
However, formulating Problem (
6
) with the affine state feedback parameterization (
16
) results in a nonconvex program
[
25
,
9
]
. Next, we show that by a change of variables, we may arrive at a convex reformulation of Problem (
6
).

Theorem 9
.

Any state-feedback policy of the form (
16
) may be represented by an
affine feedback policy, given by

\displaystyle u_{t|k}

\displaystyle=v_{t|k}+\sum_{i=k}^{t}K_{t,i|k}y_{t|k},

(31a)

\displaystyle y_{t+1|k}

\displaystyle=A_{t}y_{t|k}+D_{t}w_{t},\quad y_{k|k}=x_{k}-\mu_{k},

(31b)

where
y_{t|k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
is an auxiliary variable that tracks the error evolution of the uncontrolled system.

Moreover, using the parameterization (
31
), Problem (
6
) may be cast as a convex program.

Proof 4.6
.

The predicted dynamics of system (
1
) may be alternatively written as

\displaystyle X_{k|k}^{N}=\bar{A}_{k}^{N}x_{k|k}+\bar{B}_{k}^{N}U_{k|k}^{N-1}+\bar{D}_{k}^{N}W_{k}^{N-1}+\bar{r}_{k}^{N},

(32)

where

\displaystyle X_{k|k}^{N}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{k|k}\\
x_{k+1|k}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k+N|k}\end{bmatrix},\quad U_{k|k}^{N-1}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{k|k}\\
u_{k+1|k}\\
\vdots\\
u_{k+N-1|k}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle W_{k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}w_{k}\\
w_{k+1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{k+N-1}\end{bmatrix},

where

\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n_{x}\times n_{x}}
,
\bar{B}_{k}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n_{x}\times Nn_{u}}
,
\bar{D}_{k}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n_{x}\times Nn_{w}}
,
\bar{r}_{k}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n_{x}}
, and
\bar{B}_{k}^{N}
and
\bar{D}_{k}^{N}
are lower block triangular.
The exact expressions for the system matrices
\bar{A}_{k}^{N}
,
\bar{B}_{k}^{N}
,
\bar{D}_{k}^{N}
may be found in
[
11
]
, for example.

We introduce the parameterization (
16
) and split (
32
) into the nominal and error dynamics as follows

\displaystyle X_{k|k}^{N}

\displaystyle=\bar{X}_{k|k}^{N}+\tilde{X}_{k|k}^{N},

(33a)

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{k|k}^{N}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\mu_{k}+\bar{B}_{k}^{N}V_{k|k}^{N-1}+\bar{r}_{k}^{N},

(33b)

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{k|k}^{N}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\tilde{x}_{k}+\bar{B}_{k}^{N}L_{k|k}^{N-1}\tilde{X}_{k|k}^{N}+\bar{D}_{k}^{N}W_{k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=(I-\bar{B}_{k}^{N}L_{k|k}^{N-1})^{-1}(\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\tilde{x}_{k}+\bar{D}_{k}^{N}W_{k}^{N-1}),

(33c)

where
\tilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}-\mu_{k}
, and

\displaystyle V_{k|k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}v_{k|k}\\
\vdots\\
v_{k+N-1|k}\end{bmatrix},

(33d)

\displaystyle L_{k|k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}L_{k|k}&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&L_{k+1|k}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&L_{k+N-1|k}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(33e)

Therefore,

\displaystyle U_{k|k}^{N-1}=V_{k|k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+L_{k|k}^{N-1}(I-\bar{B}_{k}^{N}L_{k|k}^{N-1})^{-1}(\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\tilde{x}_{k}+\bar{D}_{k}^{N}W_{k}^{N-1}).

(34)

Introducing the change of variables

\displaystyle K_{k|k}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=L_{k|k}^{N-1}(I-\bar{B}_{k}^{N}L_{k|k}^{N-1})^{-1},

(35a)

\displaystyle Y_{k|k}^{N}

\displaystyle=(\bar{A}_{k}^{N}\tilde{x}_{k}+\bar{D}_{k}^{N}W_{k}^{N-1}),

(35b)

yields the control parameterization

\displaystyle U_{k|k}^{N-1}=K_{k|k}^{N-1}Y_{k|k}^{N}+V_{k|k}^{N-1},

(35c)

where
K_{k|k}^{N-1}=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\begin{bmatrix}K_{k,k|k}&0&\dots&0&0\\
K_{k+1,k|k}&K_{k+1,k+1|k}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
K_{k+N-1,k|k}&K_{k+N-1,k+1|k}&\dots&K_{k+N-1,k+N-1|k}&0\end{bmatrix},

where the lower triangular structure of
K_{k|k}^{N-1}
results from the block lower triangular structure of

\bar{B}_{k}^{N}
.

The parameterization given by (
35
) is the augmented form of the affine feedback policy (
31
).
In Theorem 2 of
[
17
]
, we have shown that Problem (
6
) may be cast as a convex program by introducing the affine feedback parameterization given by (
31
).

Remark 10
.

In
[
17
]
, the feedback is applied only with respect to the current error (that is,
u_{t|k}=v_{t|k}+K_{t|k}y_{t|k}
). However, this is done merely to limit the number of decision variables and improve computation time. The proof of convexity in
[
17
]
may clearly be extended to the more general case of (
31
).

In summary,
using Theorem
2
we have established sufficient conditions to guarantee recursive feasibility of Problem (
6
). Theorem
4
shows how the terminal constraints may be designed so as to satisfy these conditions in practice.
Theorem
6
establishes that the chance constraints will be satisfied under closed-loop operation as desired.
Finally, Theorem
9
shows that the results of the previous theorems extend to the convex problem formulation utilized in
[
17
]
.

Conclusion

In this work, we have established the first recursive feasibility result in the literature for SMPC for linear time-varying systems subject to unbounded disturbances.
We first presented a theorem giving sufficient conditions for the recursive feasibility of the LTV SMPC optimization problem.
Then, we introduced a result showing how the terminal constraints may be calculated to satisfy these sufficient conditions.
Finally, we showed that these results extend to the Covariance Steering SMPC (CS-SMPC) problem used in our previous work, which solves for the feedback gains online using convex programming, and for which we have already established convergence guarantees subject to the condition of recursive feasibility.
These results are verified via a numerical example using a nonlinear vehicle model.

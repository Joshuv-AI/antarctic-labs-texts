A Spinorial Perelman’s Functional: critical points and gradient flow
Tsz-Kiu Aaron Chow, Frederick Tsz-Ho Fong

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce an energy functional on closed Riemannian spin manifolds which unifies Perelman’s 𝒲 \mathcal{W} - and ℱ \mathcal{F} -functionals, Baldauf-Ouzch’s ℰ \mathcal{E} -functional, and the Dirichlet energy for spinors. We compute its first variation formula, and show that its critical points under natural constraints are twisted Ricci solitons and eigen-spinors of the weighted Dirac operator. We introduce a negative L 2 L^{2} -gradient flow of such a functional, and establish its short-time existence and uniqueness via contraction mapping methods.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivations

Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
- and
\mathcal{W}
-functionals were introduced in his first preprint
[
8
]
on the proof of the Poincaré conjecture. These functionals play a central role in the analysis of singularities in the Ricci flow. Under suitable normalization constraints, their minimizers - such as the
\lambda
- and
\mu
-functionals - are monotone increasing along the flow and remain constant precisely when the metric is Ricci-flat, Einstein, or a Ricci soliton.

As explained in Remark 1.3 of
[
8
]
, Perelman’s functionals are closely related to the Weitzenböck (or Bochner-Lichnerowicz) formula in the context of spin geometry. To further elaborate on this relation, we define the following quantities. Given a smooth scalar function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
on a Riemannian spin manifold
(M^{n},g)
, for any tensor field
\alpha
and scalar function
\varphi
, we define:

\displaystyle\,\textup{div}_{f}\alpha

\displaystyle=e^{f}\,\textup{div}(e^{-f}\alpha)=\,\textup{div}(\alpha)-i_{\nabla f}\alpha

\displaystyle D_{f}\psi

\displaystyle=e^{f/2}e_{i}\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}(e^{-f/2}\psi)=D\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\varphi

\displaystyle=\,\textup{div}_{f}\nabla\varphi=\Delta\varphi-\langle\nabla f,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\psi

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{*}\nabla\psi-\nabla_{\nabla f}\psi

\displaystyle\textup{Ric}_{f}

\displaystyle=\textup{Ric}+\nabla\nabla f

\displaystyle R_{f}

\displaystyle=R+2\Delta f-\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}=R+\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}-2\Delta_{f}f.

Here we use
\nabla
to denote both the Levi-Civita connection induced by
g
, and the spin connection compatible with
g
. Using this notation, we can express Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
-functional as

\mathcal{F}(g,f)=\int_{M}R_{f}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

By the Weitzenböck’s formula:

D_{f}^{2}\psi=-\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{1}{4}R_{f}\psi,

one can then use integration by parts to show:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}-\mathcal{F}(g,f)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left\{4\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle-R_{f}\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g},

Hence, by considering the minimizing constraints below (with
g
and
f
fixed), we get

\displaystyle\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}-\mathcal{F}(g,f)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

Recall that
D_{f}\psi=e^{f/2}D(e^{-f/2}\psi)
, so by the change of variables
\psi\mapsto e^{-f/2}\psi
one can get

\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}d\mu_{g}=}\int_{M}4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}d\mu_{g}=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}4\left|D\psi\right|^{2}\,d\Omega_{0}=4\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}

where
\lambda_{1}(D)
is the lowest eigenvalue of the Dirac operator
D
, and so we have

4\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\mathcal{F}(g,f)=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

Note that
\lambda_{1}(D)
is independent of
f
, so by taking the maximizer over the following constraint on
f
(with
g
fixed), we get:

4\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\lambda(g)=\sup_{\int_{M}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

where
\lambda(g)
is Perelman’s
\lambda
-functional:

\lambda(g):=\inf_{\int_{M}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}}\int_{M}R_{f}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

It has been shown by Perelman in
[
8
]
that the minimizer is achieved by a function
\hat{f}
such that
R_{\hat{f}}=\lambda(g)
, and so we have:

\displaystyle 4\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\lambda(g)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-\hat{f}}d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}_{g}+\lambda(g)\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-\hat{f}}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-\hat{f}}d\mu_{g}=1}\int_{M}4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}\,d\mu_{g}=-4\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{f})

where
\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{f})
is the lowest eigenvalue of
\Delta_{f}
when acting on spinors.
This shows Perelman’s
\lambda
-functional can be alternatively written the difference between the eigenvalues of two operators.

\lambda(g)=4\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-4\lambda_{1}(\Delta_{f}).

In
[
3
]
, Baldauf-Ozuch introduced the following functional:

\mathcal{E}(g,f,\psi):=\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

which is the RHS of (
1.1
). Applying this functional on asymptotically Euclidean manifolds with non-negative
R_{f}
, Baldauf-Ozuch established the monotonicity of the min-max of
\mathcal{E}
and the constancy of the ADM mass along the Ricci flow on such a manifold.

Another important functional introduced by Perelman is the
\mathcal{W}
-functional, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{\tau\big(R+\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\big)+f-n\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}(\tau R_{f}+f-n)e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

Under the constraint
\int_{M}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}
, the critical points of
\mathcal{W}
are shrinking Ricci solitons which model finite-time singularities.

Inspired by previous discussions about Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
- and
\lambda
-functionals, and Baldauf-Ozuch’s
\mathcal{E}
-functional, we introduce the following
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
-functional which unifies these known functionals.

Definition 1.1
.

On a closed Riemannian spin manifold
(M^{n},g)
, we define the spinorial Perelman’s entropy
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
as follows:

(1.2)

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau):=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}_{g}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-\tau\big)-\lambda(f-n)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

where
g
is any Riemannian metric on
M
,
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is any smooth function,
\psi
is a spinor field,
\tau>0
is a positive scalar function of
t
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed constant.

1.2.
Relation with Dirichlet spinorial energy

When
\lambda=0
,
\hat{f}
is a function such that
R_{\hat{f}}=C
which is a constant, and
\psi_{0}
is a spinor with

\int_{M}e^{-\hat{f}}\,d\mu_{g}=(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{M}\left|\psi_{0}\right|^{2}e^{-\hat{f}}\,d\mu_{g}=c(4\pi\tau)^{n/2},

then
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,\hat{f},\hat{\psi},\tau)
is essentially the Dirichlet spinor energy:

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,\hat{f},\hat{\psi},\tau)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}\,d\mu_{g}+C(c-\tau).

1.3.
Relation with Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
- and
\mathcal{F}
-functionals

The
f
-weighted Weitzenböck’s formula

(1.3)

D_{f}^{2}\psi=-\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{1}{4}R_{f}\psi

implies that

(1.4)

2\Delta_{f}\left|\psi\right|^{2}=R_{f}\left|\psi\right|^{2}+4\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}-4\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle.

Since
M
is closed, we have
\displaystyle{\int_{M}(\Delta_{f}\varphi)e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=0}
for any scalar function
\varphi
, and (
1.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{4\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle+2\Delta_{f}\left|\psi\right|^{2}-\tau R_{f}-\lambda(f-n)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}-\tau\big(R+\left|\nabla f\right|_{g}^{2}\big)-\lambda(f-n)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}.

A spinor
\hat{\psi}\in\ker D_{f}
is called a
f
-weighted harmonic spinor
. When we restrict
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
on
f
-weighted harmonic spinors, we can recover Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
-functional or
\mathcal{F}
-functional, depending on the choice of
\lambda
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\hat{\psi},\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{\tau\big(R+\left|\nabla f\right|_{g}^{2}\big)+\lambda(f-n)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau)&\text{ if }\lambda=1\\
-\frac{\tau}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\mathcal{F}(g,f)&\text{ if }\lambda=0\end{cases}.

1.4.
Relation with Baldauf-Ozuch’s functional

When
\lambda=0
, and
\tau\equiv 1
, (
1.2
) becomes:

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,1)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{4\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}+R_{f}\big(\left|\psi\right|^{2}-1\big)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\mathcal{E}_{g}(f,\psi)

which is (up to a constant multiple) the spinor energy functional studied in Baldauf-Ozuch’s work
[
3
]
.

1.5.
Relation with Perelman’s
\mu
- and
\lambda
-functionals

For simplicity, we denote

d\Omega_{f}=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}e^{-f}d\mu_{g}.

We then consider the constraint over all spinors
\psi
with fixed
L^{2}(d\Omega_{f})
-norm.

\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=c}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)=\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=c}\int_{M}4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}-\int_{M}\left\{\tau(R+\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}_{g})+\lambda(f-n)\right\}\,d\Omega_{f},

where the infimum is taken with
(g,f,\tau)
being fixed, and
c>0
is a constant.

As discussed, we have

\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=c}\int_{M}4\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=\int_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}\,d\Omega_{0}=c}\int_{M}4\left|D\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{0}=4c\lambda_{1}(D)^{2},

so by further taking the supremum over the constraint
\int_{M}d\Omega_{f}=1
, we get

\sup_{\int_{M}d\Omega_{f}=1}\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=c}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)=\begin{cases}4c\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\mu(g,\tau)&\text{ if }\lambda=1\\
4c\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\tau(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}\lambda(g)&\text{ if }\lambda=0\end{cases}

It was proved by Perelman in
[
8
]
that the minimizers
\hat{f}
for the
\mu
- and
\lambda
-functionals exists, and the following holds:

\tau R_{\hat{f}}+\lambda(\hat{f}-n)=\begin{cases}\mu(g,\tau)&\text{ if }\lambda=1\\
\tau\lambda(g)&\text{ if }\lambda=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

(1.5)

\sup_{\int_{M}d\Omega_{f}=1}\inf_{\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{f}=c}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)=\begin{cases}4c\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\tau R_{f}-f+n&\text{ if }\lambda=1\\
4c\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}-\tau(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}R_{f}&\text{ if }\lambda=0\end{cases}

The well-known Friedrich’s inequality
[
4
]
and its
f
-weighted generalization in
[
2
]
said that on a closed spin manifold
(M^{n},g)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, then

\lambda_{1}(D)^{2}\geq\frac{n}{4(n-1)}\min R_{f},

with equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant and
(M^{n},g)
admits a Killing spinor, and hence
(M^{n},g)
is Einstein. From (
1.5
) and the Friedrich’s inequality, if one could smoothly deform the data
(g,f,\psi)
with some suitable choice of constraints (
\tau,c
) and under a suitable flow such that
\mathbb{W}_{0}
decreases to
0
as
t\to\infty
, then the limit metric
g_{\infty}
should be an Einstein metric. It would also be interesting to discover whether or not there is any generalization of Friedrich’s inequality so that the equality holds exactly when
(M^{n},g,f)
is a gradient Ricci soliton. We will show in later section that the constraint critical point of
\mathbb{W}_{1}
is achieved by “twisted” Ricci solitons. The case
\lambda=1
in (
1.5
) seems to suggest a possible form of generalized Friedrich’s inequality with equality case achieved by Ricci solitons.

1.6.
Gradient flow

Inspired by (
1.5
) and the potential relations with Friedrich’s inequality, we will introduce a flow system on
(g,f,\psi)
which preserves the constraints

\int_{M}d\Omega_{f}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|\psi\right|^{2}=c.

The flow system is of the form:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\textup{Ric}+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{2}\nabla f-\frac{1}{\tau}V_{f}}g-\frac{2}{\tau}S_{g,f,\psi}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\Delta f-R+\frac{\lambda n}{2\tau}+\frac{4}{\tau}\textup{Tr}_{g}S+\frac{2}{\tau}\,\textup{div}\,V_{f}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi

where the vector field
V_{f}
and the symmetric
2
-tensor
S_{g,f,\psi}
will be defined in later sections. We will establish the short-time existence (Theorem
4.1
) of this flow. The
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
-functional will be shown to be monotone decreasing along the flow (Proposition
3.6
), and it is stationary on “twisted” Ricci solitons. Therefore, in some sense, the flow system is the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
-function.

To establish the short-time existence, we follow a DeTurck-trick type strategy adapted to the coupled

(g,f,\psi)
-system. More precisely, in Section 4 we introduce a gauged system by adding the DeTurck
vector field
X=W(g,g_{0})-\frac{2}{\tau}V_{f}+\nabla f
to the metric equation and compensating the induced diffeomorphism action in
the
(f,\psi)
-equations by the corresponding transport terms (including the spinorial Kosmann Lie
derivative). A key analytic feature of this gauge is that it puts the system into a
triangular
parabolic form
at the level of highest-order derivatives: the metric equation becomes strictly parabolic
with a Laplace-type principal part in
g
and contains no second derivatives of
f
or
\psi
; the spinor
equation is strictly parabolic in
\psi
, while the additional top-order terms involve at most second
derivatives of
g
(coming from the Kosmann correction) but only first derivatives of
\psi
; and the scalar
equation is (forward/backward) parabolic in
f
depending on whether
c>\tau
or
c<\tau
.

In Section 4.1 we compute the principal symbols and verify that, under the pointwise normalization

|\psi_{0}|^{2}\equiv c
, the
(g,\psi)
-subsystem is uniformly parabolic, whereas the
f
-equation is uniformly
forward parabolic if
c>\tau
and uniformly backward parabolic if
c<\tau
. In Section 4.2 we exploit the
triangular structure to solve the gauged system by a Banach fixed point argument in parabolic Hölder
spaces: we solve successively for
g
, then
\psi
, and then
f
, applying parabolic Schauder estimates at
each step and using a short-time interpolation estimate to gain a small factor in the difference
bounds and obtain a contraction for sufficiently small time. Finally, we undo the gauge by pulling back
along the time-dependent diffeomorphisms generated by the gauge vector field, which yields a solution
to the original flow (
3.5
)–(
3.7
).

The article is structured as follows. We will derive the first variation formulae of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
in Section 2 using some of the formulae derived in
[
8
]
and
[
2
,
3
]
. In Section 3, we will derive the Euler-Lagrange’s equations under some natural constraints that will inspire us to consider the spinorial Ricci flow. In Section 4, we will prove the short-time existence and uniqueness of the spinorial Ricci flow.

2.
Variation Formulae

2.1.
Evolution equations

In this section, we first list the evolution equations of various important quantities, and then apply them to find out the first variation of the
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
-functional.
Suppose
g(t)
,
f(t)
,
\tau(t)
, and
\psi(t)
are
1
-parameter family of Riemannian metrics, scalar functions, and spinors on
M
. We denote
\dot{g}
,
\dot{f}
,
\dot{\tau}
,
\dot{\psi}
their derivatives with respect to
t
.

Lemma 2.1

(c.f.
[
1
]
, Propositions 2.6, 2.25 of
[
3
]
)
.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\big\langle\dot{g},\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\dot{g},T_{\psi}\rangle+2\textup{Re}\langle\nabla\dot{\psi},\nabla\psi\rangle

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R_{f}

\displaystyle=\,\textup{div}_{f}\,\textup{div}_{f}\dot{g}-2\Delta_{f}\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}\dot{g}-\dot{f}\right)-\langle\dot{g},\textup{Ric}_{f}\rangle

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left|\psi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\textup{Re}\langle\psi,\dot{\psi}\rangle

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}(e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}\dot{g}-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

where
T_{\psi}
and
\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle
are respectively the
3
- and
2
-tensors defined by:

(2.5)

\displaystyle T_{\psi}(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\textup{Re}\left((X\wedge Y)\cdot\psi,\nabla_{Z}\psi\rangle+\langle(X\wedge Z)\cdot\psi,\nabla_{Y}\psi\rangle\right)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\textup{Re}\langle\nabla_{X}\psi,\nabla_{Y}\psi\rangle

for any vector fields
X,Y,Z
.

Consequently, the integrals below satisfy the following evolution equations.

Lemma 2.2

(c.f.
[
1
]
, Propositions 2.20 and 2.25 of
[
3
]
)
.

Assume
\partial M=\emptyset
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}R_{f}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}(\dot{g})-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)R_{f}-\big\langle\dot{g},\textup{Ric}_{f}\big\rangle\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}R_{f}\left|\psi\right|^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{-\big\langle\dot{g},\textup{Ric}_{f}\left|\psi\right|^{2}\big\rangle+\left|\psi\right|^{2}\,\textup{div}_{f}\,\textup{div}_{f}\dot{g}+2\textup{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},R_{f}\psi\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\left.4\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}\dot{g}-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)\left(\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle-\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}_{g}\right)\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\psi\right|_{g}^{2}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}\dot{g}-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}-2\textup{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},\Delta_{f}\psi\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\left.\left\langle\dot{g},\frac{1}{2}\,\textup{div}_{f}T_{\psi}+\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle\right\rangle\right\}e^{-f}\,d\mu_{g}

Combining these, we can then compute the first variation formula for
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
\partial M=\emptyset
. The first variation of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is given by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{n/2}}\int_{M}\left\{\left\langle\tau\dot{g}-\dot{\tau}g,\,\textup{Ric}_{f}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\dot{g},\,\mathcal{L}_{V}g+2S\right\rangle+8\textup{Re}\left\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\,\dot{\psi}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 79.6678pt+\left.\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}(\dot{g})-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)\left(4\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle-\tau R_{f}-\lambda(f-n-1)\right)\right\}e^{-f}d\mu

where
V
is a vector field on
M
satisfying

(2.11)

\displaystyle V_{g,f,\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\textup{Re}\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot D_{f}\psi\rangle e_{i},

and
S
is the symmetric
2
-tensor defined by

(2.12)

S_{g,f,\psi}(X,Y):=\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}\psi,X\cdot\nabla_{Y}\psi+Y\cdot\nabla_{X}\psi\rangle.

If
(g,f,\psi)
are clear from the context, we will abbreviate
V_{g,f,\psi}
and
S_{g,f,\psi}
by simply
V
and
S
respectively.

Proof.

First, it is useful to know

(2.13)

2.2.
More about
S
and
V

We will explore more about the tensor
S
and vector field
V
under some special assumptions on
\psi
. The following observation is useful:

Lemma 2.4
.

For any real vector field
X
, and a spinor
\psi
, we have

\textup{Re}\langle X\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle=0

Proof.

\displaystyle 2\textup{Re}\langle X\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle+\overline{\langle X\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\langle X\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,X\cdot\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\psi,X\cdot\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,X\cdot\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\psi
is an eigen-spinor of
D
or
D_{f}
with real eigenvalue
\beta
, then we have

\textup{Tr}_{g}S_{g,f,\psi}=2\beta^{2}\left|\psi\right|^{2}.

Proof.

It is useful to observe that the trace of the tensor
S
is given by:

\textup{Tr}_{g}S_{g,f,\psi}=\sum_{i}S_{g,f,\psi}(e_{i},e_{i})=\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}\psi,e_{i}\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}\psi+e_{i}\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}\psi\rangle=\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}\psi,2D\psi\rangle.

If
D\psi=\beta\psi
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}\psi,2D\psi\rangle=\textup{Re}\left\langle\beta\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi,2\beta\psi\right\rangle=2\beta^{2}\left|\psi\right|^{2}.

Similarly if
D_{f}\psi=\beta\psi
.

∎

A spinor
\psi
is said to be a Killing spinor if there exists a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla_{X}\psi=\mu X\cdot\psi
for any real vector field
X
. In particular, if
\mu=0
, we call
\psi
a parallel spinor. In case that
\psi
is a Killing spinor, the
S
-tensor can be written in terms of the metric
g
:

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose there exists a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla_{X}\psi=\mu X\cdot\psi
for any vector field
X
(i.e.
\psi
is a Killing spinor), then we have

S_{g,f,\psi}=2\mu^{2}n\left|\psi\right|^{2}g

Proof.

By the given condition
\nabla_{X}\psi=\mu X\cdot\psi
, we have

\displaystyle S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}\psi,X\cdot\nabla_{Y}\psi+Y\cdot\nabla_{X}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\textup{Re}\left\langle\sum_{k}e_{k}\cdot\nabla_{e_{k}}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi,\mu(X\cdot Y+Y\cdot X)\cdot\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\textup{Re}\left\langle\sum_{k}e_{k}\cdot\mu e_{k}\cdot\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi,-2\mu g(X,Y)\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\textup{Re}\left\langle-\mu n\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi,-2\mu g(X,Y)\psi\right\rangle

Since
\nabla f
is a real vector field, by Lemma
2.4
we have
2\textup{Re}\langle\nabla f\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle=0
. It concludes that

S(X,Y)=2\mu^{2}n\left|\psi\right|^{2}g(X,Y).

∎

It is useful to observe that if
\hat{\psi}
is an
f
-weighted harmonic spinor, i.e.
D_{f}\hat{\psi}=0
, then according to (
2.11
) we have
V=0
. More generally, the same result holds if
\psi
is an eigen-spinor of the
f
-weighted Dirac operator
D_{f}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{J}
is an eigen-spinor of
D_{f}
with eigenvalue
\beta
, then the vector field
V
defined in (
2.11
) vanishes:

V=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\textup{Re}\langle\psi,\,e_{i}\cdot D_{f}\psi\rangle e_{i}=0.

Proof.

Direct consequence of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

If
\psi
is a Killing spinor, then we can show that
V
is parallel to the gradient
\nabla f
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose there exists a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla_{X}\psi=\mu X\cdot\psi
for any vector field
X
(i.e.
\psi
is a Killing spinor), then we have

V=\left|\psi\right|^{2}\nabla f.

Proof.

\displaystyle e_{i}\cdot D_{f}\psi

\displaystyle=e_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{k}e_{k}\cdot\nabla_{e_{k}}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi\Big)

\displaystyle=\mu\sum_{k}e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=-\mu ne_{i}\cdot\psi-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi

Then, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Re}\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot D_{f}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\textup{Re}\langle\psi,-\mu ne_{i}\cdot\psi\rangle}_{=0\text{ by Lemma \ref{lma:realVF}}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)\textup{Re}\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)\left(\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi\rangle+\langle e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi,\psi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)\left(\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot e_{k}\cdot\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,e_{k}\cdot e_{i}\cdot\psi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{k}f)\langle\psi,-2\delta_{ik}\psi\rangle=\left|\psi\right|^{2}\nabla_{i}f

This proves our result.

∎

3.1.
Euler-Lagrange’s equations

After deriving the first variation formula for the
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
-functional in the previous section, we next discuss the Euler-Lagrange’s equations under some natural constraints discussed below.

For any Riemannian metric
g

f
, and positive constant
\tau

d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}:=e^{-f}(4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}d\mu_{g}.

If
g,f,\tau
are clear from the context, we will simply denote it by
d\Omega
.

Define the following spaces of functions and spinors:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{g,\psi,\tau}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{g,f,\tau,c}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\psi\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\Sigma M):\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=c\right\}

If

are clear from the context, we simply denote them by
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}

Then, we find the critical points of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
subject to the constraints

and
\psi\in\mathcal{J}_{g,f,\tau,c}
. It is useful to observe that

(3.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}(\dot{g})-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}.

Therefore, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}(\dot{g})-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)d\Omega_{g,f,\tau},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left\{2\textup{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},\psi\rangle+\left(\frac{1}{2}\textup{tr}_{g}(\dot{g})-\dot{f}-\frac{n\dot{\tau}}{2\tau}\right)\left|\psi\right|^{2}\right\}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}.

Next we use the method of Lagrange’s multipliers to find out the Euler-Lagrange’s equation of

subject to constraints

and

.

From (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}-\alpha\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,d\Omega-\beta\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\left|\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\bigg\{\left\langle\tau\dot{g}-\dot{\tau}g,\,\textup{Ric}_{f}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\dot{g},\mathcal{L}_{V}g+2S\right\rangle+8\textup{Re}\left\langle\dot{\psi},D_{f}^{2}\psi-\frac{\beta}{4}\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\tau\left(\textup{Ric}_{f}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right)-\mathcal{L}_{V}g-2S+\frac{(*)}{2}g

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix

\displaystyle D_{f}^{2}\psi

\displaystyle\tau R_{f}+\lambda(f-n)=\lambda-\alpha,

where

and

are tensor and vector fields defined in (

) and (

) respectively. Therefore, if
(g,f,\psi)
is a critical point, then

is a “twisted” Ricci soliton, and
\psi
is an eigen-spinor of

.

Remark 3.2
.

Using the constraints

and

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{c}\int_{M}\left|D_{f}\psi\right|^{2}d\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}=\beta c-\lambda+\alpha.

Remark 3.3
.

We call the metric

of the critical point to be a “twisted” Ricci soliton because of the presence of the

).

Remark 3.4

(From the
L^{2}(d\Omega)
-constraint to the unit spinor bundle)
.

In Proposition
3.1
we imposed the global constraint
\psi\in J_{g,f,\tau,c}
, i.e.

\int_{M}|\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=c,

which yields the eigen-spinor equation
D_{f}^{2}\psi=\frac{\beta}{4}\psi
.

For the evolution problem, it is analytically advantageous (and standard in the spinor-flow literature

[
1
]
) to work on the
unit spinor bundle
, i.e. to impose the stronger pointwise constraint

|\psi|^{2}\equiv c
. Besides removing the scaling direction in the spinor variable, this normalization
has two key analytic consequences for our coupled flow being introduced next.

(1) Projected Euler–Lagrange equation.

If one repeats the first-variation argument for
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
under the single normalization

\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1
, but restricts to variations tangent to
\{|\psi|^{2}\equiv c\}

(so that
\mathrm{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},\psi\rangle=0
pointwise), then the spinor Euler–Lagrange equation
is the orthogonal projection of
D_{f}^{2}\psi
onto
\psi^{\perp}
. Using the weighted Weitzenböck formula
(
1.3
), this is equivalent to

\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{c}\,\psi=0,

which agrees with the stationary equation for the
\psi
-component of the flow (
3.7
).

(2) Uniform control of the top-order coefficients and the parabolicity regime for
f
.

The pointwise constraint
|\psi|^{2}\equiv c
also freezes the highest-order coefficients in the gauged
system of Section 4. In particular, writing

V_{f}\;=\;U+\frac{c}{2}\nabla f,\qquad U:=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Re}\langle\psi,\,e_{i}\!\cdot D\psi\rangle\,e_{i},

we obtain

\frac{2}{\tau}\,\mathrm{div}(V_{f})=\frac{c}{\tau}\,\Delta f+\frac{2}{\tau}\,\mathrm{div}(U),

so the principal second-order part of the
f
-equation becomes

-\Delta f+\frac{2}{\tau}\mathrm{div}(V_{f})=-\Bigl(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\Bigr)\Delta f+\text{(lower order terms)}.

Consequently, after DeTurck gauge-fixing, the scalar equation is uniformly
backward
parabolic
if
c<\tau
and uniformly
forward
parabolic if
c>\tau
(degenerating at
c=\tau
). In the
fully forward regime
c>\tau
, the gauged system becomes a forward quasilinear parabolic evolution
in all variables
(g,f,\psi)
, and the monotonicity identity of Proposition
3.6
below shows that

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is a genuine Lyapunov functional for this forward parabolic gradient-like flow. This
forward-parabolic viewpoint suggests the potential applicability of dynamical tools such as
Łojasiewicz–Simon inequalities for stability and convergence analysis near critical points,
in contrast to the classical Perelman setting where the
f
-equation is backward.

In summary, the flow introduced below can be viewed as the negative
L^{2}
-gradient flow of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}

on the constraint space

\Bigl\{(g,f,\psi):\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1,\;\;|\psi|^{2}\equiv c\Bigr\}.

3.2.
Spinorial Ricci flow

Motivated by the above discussion and by the spinor flow of
Ammann–Weiß–Witt
[
1
]
(see also He–Wang
[
5
]
for regularity results), we introduce
the following coupled evolution system. The flow is designed to preserve the normalization

\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1
and, provided
|\psi_{0}|^{2}\equiv c
pointwise, to evolve inside the unit
spinor bundle
|\psi(t)|^{2}\equiv c
(hence also
\int_{M}|\psi(t)|^{2}\,d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=c
).
The functional
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)
is monotone decreasing along the flow. Moreover, in the regime
c>\tau
the DeTurck-gauged system is fully forward parabolic, so that

\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
serves as a Lyapunov functional for a forward parabolic evolution, which may be useful
for future stability and convergence analysis.

Definition 3.5

(Spinorial Ricci Flow)
.

On a Riemannian spin manifold
(M^{n},g_{0})
, we consider the following system of equations, called the
spinorial Ricci flow
, with initial conditions
(g_{0},f_{0},\psi_{0})
so that
\left|\psi_{0}\right|^{2}\equiv c
and
\tau>0
is fixed:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\textup{Ric}+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{2}\nabla f-\frac{1}{\tau}V_{f}}g-\frac{2}{\tau}S_{g,f,\psi}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\Delta f-R+\frac{\lambda n}{2\tau}+\frac{4}{\tau}\textup{Tr}_{g}S+\frac{2}{\tau}\,\textup{div}\,V_{f}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi

Proposition 3.6
.

As long as solution exists, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}(g,f,\psi,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left(2\tau\left|\textup{Ric}+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{2}\nabla f-\frac{1}{\tau}V_{f}}g-\frac{2}{\tau}S-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}+8\left|\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi\right|^{2}\right)d\Omega\leq 0.

Therefore,
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is monotone decreasing, and it is stationary if and only if
(g,f,\psi)
is a “twisted” Ricci soliton given by

\left(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\right)\textup{Ric}_{f}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g=\frac{2}{\tau}\,\textup{div}_{f}T_{\psi}+\frac{4}{\tau}\textup{Re}\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle

and
\psi
satisfies
\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}}{c}\psi=0
.

Proof.

Using the identity
\Delta_{f}|\psi|^{2}=2\textup{Re}\langle\Delta_{f}\psi,\,\psi\rangle+2|\nabla\psi|^{2}
and the Weitzenböck identity, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle 8\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\,\dot{\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=-8\textup{Re}\langle\Delta_{f}\psi,\,\dot{\psi}\rangle+R_{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=-8\textup{Re}\left\langle\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi,\,\dot{\psi}\right\rangle+\left(\frac{4|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}+R_{f}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi|^{2}.

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\textup{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=2\textup{Re}\langle\Delta_{f}\psi,\psi\rangle+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}2\textup{Re}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta_{f}|\psi|^{2}.

Hence, if
\left|\psi\right|^{2}\equiv c
at
t=0
, then
|\psi|^{2}\equiv c
as long as the flow exists. Consequently,

(3.10)

8\textup{Re}\langle D_{f}^{2}\psi,\,\dot{\psi}\rangle=-8\left|\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi\right|^{2}.

Combining with (
2.10
), we proved the monotonicity formula (
3.8
).

Next, from the monotonicity formula we see that
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is stationary if and only if
(g,f,\psi)
satisfies

\textup{Ric}+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{2}\nabla f-\frac{1}{\tau}V_{f}}g-\frac{2}{\tau}S-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi=0.

Plugging (
2.17
) with
|\psi|^{2}\equiv c
into the first identity above, we get

\left(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\right)\textup{Ric}_{f}-\frac{\lambda}{2\tau}g=\frac{2}{\tau}\,\textup{div}_{f}T_{\psi}+\frac{4}{\tau}\textup{Re}\langle\nabla\psi\otimes\nabla\psi\rangle.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(g,f,\psi)
be a critical point of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{M}R_{f}d\Omega=\frac{n\lambda}{2\tau}+\frac{4}{\tau}\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}d\Omega

and

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\right)\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}d\Omega=\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}d\Omega.

Proof.

Taking trace on the first twisted Ricci soliton equation and note that
\textup{tr}(\textup{Ric}_{f})=R+\Delta f=R_{f}-\Delta_{f}f
, and integrate, we get

\displaystyle\Bigl(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\Bigr)\int_{M}R_{f}d\Omega-\frac{n\lambda}{2\tau}=\frac{4}{\tau}\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}d\Omega.

Next, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}d\Omega-\frac{c}{4}\int_{M}R_{f}d\Omega.

This implies the identities.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
(g,f,\psi)
be a critical point of
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
on the constraint space

\left\{(g,f,\psi):\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1,\;|\psi|^{2}\equiv c\right\},

with
\tau>0
fixed and
c>0
. Then the identities in Lemma
3.7
imply:

(i)

(Fully forward parabolic regime
c>\tau
.)

One necessarily has
\lambda\leq 0
. Moreover, if
\lambda=0
then

\nabla\psi\equiv 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{f}\psi\equiv 0.

In particular
\nabla f\equiv 0
, and hence
\textup{Ric}_{f}=\textup{Ric}
; plugging into the stationary equation
forces
\textup{Ric}\equiv 0
. Hence the only steady critical points in the fully forward regime are Ricci-flat with parallel spinor and constant
f
.

(ii)

(Degenerate regime
c=\tau
.)

One necessarily has
\lambda\leq 0
, and in fact

\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\;=\;-\,\frac{n}{8}\,\lambda.

In particular, if
\lambda=0
then
\nabla\psi\equiv 0
.

(iii)

(Backward–forward regime
c<\tau
.)

One has the identity

\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega=\frac{1}{1-\frac{c}{\tau}}\left(\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\right),

and therefore the necessary condition

\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\;\geq\;0.

In particular, if
\lambda>0
(shrinker sign) then

\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\;\geq\;\frac{nc\lambda}{8(\tau-c)}\;>\;0.

In particular,
shrinking
critical points (
\lambda>0
) can only occur in the backward–forward
regime
c<\tau
, and never in the fully forward regime
c>\tau
(nor in the degenerate case
c=\tau
).

Proof.

Let

A:=\int_{M}|D_{f}\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\geq 0,\qquad B:=\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,d\Omega\geq 0.

Lemma
3.7
gives

\left(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\right)A=\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+B.

If
c>\tau
, then
1-\frac{c}{\tau}<0
so the left-hand side is
\leq 0
, hence the right-hand side
is
\leq 0
, which forces
\lambda\leq 0
. If moreover
\lambda=0
, then

\left(1-\frac{c}{\tau}\right)A=B
with negative coefficient, forcing
A=B=0
, i.e.

\nabla\psi\equiv 0
and
D_{f}\psi\equiv 0
. Since
\nabla\psi\equiv 0
implies
D\psi\equiv 0
,
we have
0=D_{f}\psi=D\psi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\psi
, and because

|\psi|^{2}\equiv c>0
this implies
\nabla f\equiv 0
. The stationary metric equation then reduces
to
\textup{Ric}\equiv 0
when
\lambda=0
.

If
c=\tau
, then the same identity reduces to
0=\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+B
, giving
\lambda\leq 0

and
B=-\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}=-\frac{n}{8}\lambda
, hence
\nabla\psi\equiv 0
when
\lambda=0
.

If
c<\tau
, then
1-\frac{c}{\tau}>0
and solving for
A
yields the stated formula and the
inequality
\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}+B\geq 0
. If
\lambda>0
, then
A\geq\frac{1}{1-c/\tau}\cdot\frac{nc\lambda}{8\tau}=\frac{nc\lambda}{8(\tau-c)}
.
∎

4.
Short-time existence and uniqueness of Spinorial Ricci flow

We next establish the short-time existence and uniqueness of the spinorial Ricci flow introduced in the previous section.

Theorem 4.1

(Short-time existence and uniqueness)
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a closed Riemannian spin manifold. Fix
\tau>0
, a smooth function

, and a spinor
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}

(i)

(Backward–forward parabolic regime.)

If

, then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(g_{0},f_{0},\psi_{0},\tau)>0

such that the spinorial Ricci flow system (
3.5
)–(
3.7
)
admits a unique solution

on

In this case, after DeTurck gauge-fixing, the
(g,\psi)

forward parabolic, while the
f
-equation is uniformly
backward

(ii)

(Fully forward parabolic regime.)

If
c>\tau
, then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(g_{0},f_{0},\psi_{0},\tau)>0

such that the spinorial Ricci flow system (
3.5
)–(
3.7
)
admits a unique solution
(g(t),f(t),\psi(t))
on

g(0)=g_{0},\qquad f(0)=f_{0},\qquad\psi(0)=\psi_{0}.

In this case, after DeTurck gauge-fixing, the system is uniformly
forward
parabolic
in all variables
(g,f,\psi)

Remark 4.2
.

The distinction between the cases
c<\tau
and
c>\tau
reflects a genuine change
in the analytic character of the flow, corresponding respectively to a mixed
forward–backward system and a fully forward parabolic system.

We will prove this theorem in the remainder of this section.
Using
g_{0}
as a fixed background metric, we set the DeTurck vector field

W(g,g_{0})^{k}=g^{ij}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(g)-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(g_{0})).

We note that provided
|\psi|
is a constant, the vector field
V_{f}
is given by
V_{f}^{i}=\text{Re}\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot D\psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}df(e_{i})|\psi|^{2}
. Hence let us define a vector field
U
by
U=\text{Re}\langle\psi,e_{i}\cdot D\psi\rangle e_{i}
. Consider the following gauged-system:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2\textup{Ric}+\mathcal{L}_{W(g,g_{0})}g+\frac{4}{\tau}S_{g,f,\psi}+\frac{\lambda}{\tau}g

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{\tau}\right)\Delta f-R+\frac{\lambda n}{2\tau}+\frac{4}{\tau}\textup{Tr}_{g}S_{g,f,\psi}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{2}{\tau}\,\textup{div}\,U+(1-\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{\tau})|\nabla f|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,W-\frac{2}{\tau}U\rangle

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{f}\psi+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}\psi+(1-\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{\tau})\nabla_{\nabla f}\psi+\mathcal{L}_{W-\frac{2}{\tau}U}^{\text{spin}}\psi.

Here

denotes the spinorial (Kosmann) Lie derivative along

\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\text{spin}}\psi=\nabla_{X}\psi-\frac{1}{4}(\nabla_{i}X_{j}-\nabla_{j}X_{i})e_{i}\cdot e_{j}\cdot\psi.

Observe that (

c>\tau
; backward parabolic if
c<\tau
. Moreover, Observe that the modified flow still evolves inside the unit-spinor bundle. Indeed, if we start with
|\psi_{0}|^{2}\equiv c>0
point-wise on
M

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\textup{Re}\langle\dot{\psi},\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=2\textup{Re}\langle\Delta_{f}\psi,\psi\rangle+\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}2\textup{Re}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle+2\textup{Re}\langle\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\text{spin}}\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta_{f}|\psi|^{2}+X|\psi|^{2},

hence

as long as the flow exists.

Remark 4.11
.

The short-time existence result provides the analytic starting point for studying the long-time
behavior of the spinorial Ricci flow as a dynamical system on the constraint space

\{(g,f,\psi):\int_{M}d\Omega_{g,f,\tau}=1,\ |\psi|^{2}\equiv c\}
modulo diffeomorphisms. In the forward
parabolic regime
c>\tau
, the DeTurck-gauged system is a genuine forward quasilinear parabolic flow in
all variables, and the dissipation identity (
3.8
) makes
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
a strict Lyapunov
functional. This suggests the possibility of applying tools from analytic gradient-flow theory
(e.g. Łojasiewicz–Simon inequalities) to obtain stability and convergence to critical points
(twisted solitons/eigenspinors), once suitable compactness and gauge-fixing inputs are available.

It is also natural to view the classical Ricci flow as an invariant subsystem: when the initial spinor is

f
-harmonic, the spinorial terms vanish and the metric evolution reduces (up to diffeomorphisms) to the
Ricci flow. Thus one may ask whether the
f
-harmonic locus is dynamically stable inside the larger
spinorial flow, and whether
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
can be used to extract additional monotonicity or rigidity
information in this setting. Finally, in the mixed regime
c<\tau
the scalar equation becomes backward
parabolic after gauging, reminiscent of the adjoint structure in Perelman’s coupled system; it would be
interesting to understand how this regime can be suited for entropy/singularity applications in
which
f
plays the role of an adjoint variable rather than an evolving state.

Remark 4.12
.

Theorem
4.1
yields a well-posed local evolution for the spinorial Ricci flow,
and hence (by continuation) a maximal-time solution in the usual quasilinear-parabolic sense. The
distinction between
c<\tau
and
c>\tau
is not merely technical: in the regime
c>\tau
the DeTurck-gauged
system is a
fully forward
quasilinear parabolic flow in
(g,f,\psi)
, while
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
is a strict
Lyapunov functional by the dissipation identity (
3.8
). In particular, for any global
solution with
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
bounded from below, integrating (
3.8
) shows that the squared

L^{2}(d\Omega)
-norm of the constrained gradient is integrable in time, and hence there exist times

t_{j}\to\infty
along which the flow approaches the critical set (twisted solitons/eigenspinors) in the
sense that the
L^{2}
-gradient tends to zero. A natural next step is to understand when such
subsequential convergence improves to full convergence modulo diffeomorphisms; in the forward-parabolic
setting one may hope to combine compactness/regularity inputs with a Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality
for
\mathbb{W}_{\lambda}
near an isolated critical point, as is standard for analytic geometric gradient flows.

Appendix A
Auxiliary estimate

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
E\to M
is a vector bundle and
F:E\oplus(T^{*}M\otimes E)\to\mathbb{R}
is a fibrewise
C^{2}
map. Suppose that
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(M\times[0,T];E)
with
\omega_{1}(\cdot,0)=\omega_{2}(\cdot,0)
, or
\omega_{1}(\cdot,T)=\omega_{2}(\cdot,T)
. Then

\displaystyle\|F(\omega_{1},\partial\omega_{1})-F(\omega_{2},\partial\omega_{2})\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C(F,\|\omega_{i}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]})\,T^{\frac{1}{2+\alpha}}\,\|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

for small
T>0
. Moreover, if
F(0,0)=0
, then

\displaystyle\|F(\omega,\partial\omega)\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}\leq C(F,\|\omega\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]})\,T^{\frac{1}{2+\alpha}}

for
\omega\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(M\times[0,T];E)
satisfying
\omega(\cdot,0)=0
, or
\omega(\cdot,T)=0
.

Proof.

Using the mean value theorem, we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|F(\omega_{1},\partial\omega_{1})-F(\omega_{2},\partial\omega_{2})\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C(F,\|\omega_{i}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]})\,\Big(\|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}+\|\partial\omega_{1}-\partial\omega_{2}\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}\Big).

Let us take
u=\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}
, then
u\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(M\times[0,T];E)
and the assumption implies
u(\cdot,0)=0
. Using the parabolic Hölder interpolation inequalities (c.f.
[
6
, Chapter 8.8]
), we have

(A.2)

\displaystyle\,[u]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}^{\frac{\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\,|u|_{0;M\times[0,T]}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2+\alpha}},

\displaystyle|\partial u|_{0;M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}^{\frac{1}{2+\alpha}}\,|u|_{0;M\times[0,T]}^{1-\frac{1}{2+\alpha}},

\displaystyle[\partial u]_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\,|u|_{0;M\times[0,T]}^{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{2+\alpha}}.

On the other hand, using the fact that
u(\cdot,0)=0
, we have

|u(x,t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|u_{t}(x,s)|ds\leq t\,|u_{t}|_{0;M\times[0,T]}\leq T\,\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T]
. This gives

(A.3)

|u|_{0;M\times[0,T]}\leq T\,\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}.

The above estimate holds similarly if we have
u(\cdot,T)=0
instead. Lastly, putting (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) into (
A.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|F(\omega_{1},\partial\omega_{1})-F(\omega_{2},\partial\omega_{2})\|_{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C(F,\|\omega_{i}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]})\,T^{\min\{1-\frac{\alpha}{2+\alpha},1-\frac{1}{2+\alpha},1-\frac{1+\alpha}{2+\alpha},1\}}\,\|u\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C(F,\|\omega_{i}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]})\,T^{\frac{1}{2+\alpha}}\,\|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\|_{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2};M\times[0,T]}

for small
T>0
.
∎

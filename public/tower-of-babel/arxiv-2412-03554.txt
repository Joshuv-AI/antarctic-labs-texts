Categorize and randomize: a permissive model of stochastic choice
Ester Sudano

Abstract
We model stochastic choices with categorization. The agent preliminarly groups alternatives in homogenous disjoint classes, then randomly chooses one class and randomly picks an item within the selected class. We give a formal definition of a choice generated by this procedure, and provide an axiomatic characterization. The characterizing properties allow an external analyst to elicit that categorization is applied. In a broader interpretation, the model allows to describe the observed choice as the composition of independent subchoices. This composition preserves rationalizability by Random Utility Maximization. A generalization of the model subsumes Luce model and Nested Logit.



Introduction

Categorization is a fundamental cognitive practice: reducing objects and events to a unifying abstract representation often helps in grasping vast information.
Similarly, we may simplify our decision process by first categorizing items, then choosing one within the selected class.
We introduce
stochastic choices with categorization
(
SCC
) to model probabilistic choices informed by a consistent classification of options at disposal, and perform an exercise of revealed categorization, which can prove useful in understanding relations of substitutability among options.

We move from two main considerations, on the nature of choice data and on the effects of categorization on choice behaviour.
In experimental and marketing settings, choice observations typically exhibit variability.
This could be due to variability in taste or responses to noisy utility for an individual, or result from the aggregation of choices in a population (Luce
[
27
]
, Block and Marschak
[
5
]
).
In light of this, we model choices that appear stochastic.
On the other hand, we interpret categorization as instrumental in restricting consideration to a set of homogeneous options, so that the choice within a class is independent from external items.
In line with evidence that different methods may be applied at each stage when the choice is sequential, the only constraints to the model are given by the implied two-step procedure (Medin and Aguilar
[
34
]
, Gensch
[
15
]
, Gilbride and Allenby (2004)).

To illustrate, consider a decision maker who purchases a car.
Let
N
be the set of attributes she deems relevant for her choice.
She first evaluates a subset
M\subset N
of macroscopic attributes, such as price range and dimension, and chooses the class of options satisfying desired attributes.
The final selection is made by evaluating also particular attributes, such as fuel efficiency, engine power, color, interiors.
Hence, if a medium price city car is chosen with probability
p=0.8
, and the probability of choosing a red 500 in this class is
p=0.7
, there is a probability
p=0.8\cdot 0.7=0.56
of purchasing a red 500.
Categorization provides criteria to screen the menu and focus on a subset of mutually comparable items.
We model the resulting choice behavior.

The finite grand set
X
of alternatives is partitioned in disjoint
classes
.
We say that a stochastic choice is with categorization (
SCC
) if it can be described as the result of two steps: first, classes are compared and one is chosen, then items within it are evaluated and one is selected.
Specifically, the probability an item
a
in class
X_{i}
is chosen in a menu
A\subseteq X
consists of the probability that two independent events realize:
X_{i}
is chosen among classes represented in
A
, and
a
is chosen in
A\cap X_{i}
.
Hence any
SCC
can be decomposed into ‘smaller’ independent choices, one defined on the set of classes and others on each class.
Each sub-choice can be analysed separately, so that an analyst can select the fittest model for each, to possibly enhance outcome predictions.

We think of each class as a category, interpreted here as a set of mutually comparable items.
The definition of
category
relies on two assumptions.
First, external items are irrelevant for assessing a preference among items in a category (
c-Independence
).
For instance, if the decision maker classifies vehicles by dimension, the availability of an SUV does not change her preference over city cars
a
and
b
, as it is too dissimilar to convey any extra information on
a
and
b
.
Second, items in the same category are thought of as substitutes (
c-Neutrality
).
This entails that, as long as some city car is already available, the presence of an additional city car does not affect the probability an SUV is chosen from a menu.
Specifically, it can only vary the chances another city car is selected.

In stochastic choice literature, our model especially relates to Nested Stochastic Choices (
NSC
) by Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
, a generalization of Nested Logit that illustrates its behavioral foundation.
In their work, disjoint nests are specified by looking at peculiar violations of Luce’s
IIA
due to the
similarity effect
, provided that

IIA
holds within nests.
Following the famous example by Debreu
[
9
]
and McFadden
[
33
]
, consider a decision maker choosing a way to commute.
Luce’s
IIA
requires the choice probabilities ratio
\frac{p(x,A)}{p(y,A)}
to be fixed for any set
A
in which options
x
and
y
are available.
When the only options are a train (
t
) and a blue bus (
b
), choice probabilities are
\frac{p(t,\{t,b\})}{p(b,\{t,b\})}=1
.
However, if a red bus (
b^{\prime}
) covering an identical route is made available, the agent regards it as a duplicate of
b
.
Train and bus still have equal frequency of choice, though
\frac{p(t,\{t,b\})}{p(b,\{t,b\})}<\frac{p(t,\{t,b,b^{\prime}\})}{p(b,\{t,b,b^{\prime}\})}
.
This inequality represents the similarity effect.
In Nested Stochastic Choice, and Nested Logit, this is the only source of violation of Luce’s
IIA
.
In this way, the models can capture well-known substitution patterns.
1
1

1

In Nested Logit and other GEV discrete choice models, this is achieved by assuming that random errors added to utility are correlated in a nest (Train
[
43
]
).

Yet, we argue that especially when items are comparable, but not exact replicas, choices can exhibit further context effects.
Suppose car
a
has better quality but worse economy then car
b
.
The
attraction effect
occurs when the addition of car
d
, inferior to car
a
both in quality and price, makes the choice of
a
more likely (Huber et al.
[
20
]
).
On the other hand, the introduction of a car
c
, with extremely good quality but poor economy, can make
a
a compromise between
c
and
b
and enhance its chances of choice.
This is known as the
compromise effect
(Simonson
[
40
]
).
These common phenomena represent violations of
IIA
, which manifest when items are actually compared.
We depart from
NSC
in this direction, and admit that
IIA
may fail within classes.
In fact,
SCC
models choices without restrictions among and within classes, as long as items are consistently classified across menus.
On the other hand,
SCC
is more restrictive then
NSC
in requiring the choice of a class to be independent from its content.
This assumption is relaxed in a more general version of the model, which subsumes
NSC
, Nested Logit and Luce model.

The paper is outlined as follows.
Section
1
introduces the model, whose characterization is presented in Section
2
.
In Section
3
, we study stochastic choices by categorization as generated by a population of deterministic choices.
We provide a generalization in Section
4
. Section
5
illustrates connections to related literature and to random utility models (
RUM
).

Conclusions
collect final remarks.

1
Stochastic choice with categorization

We adopt the usual setup for stochastic choices from a finite set of options.

Let
X
be a finite set of alternatives of cardinality
n\geqslant 3
, and denote
\mathscr{X}=2^{X}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
the collection of its nonempty subsets, referred to as ‘menus’.
The decision maker chooses one alternative from each menu, possibly randomizing, so that a probability of choice can be associated to each item, for each menu.
This is represented by a stochastic choice.

Definition 1
.

A
stochastic choice
is a map
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
such that
p(a,A)=0
for all
a\notin A
, and
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}p(a,A)=1
.
If for all
A\in\mathscr{X}
,
a\in A
,
p(a,A)>0
, then
p
is
positive
.

We interpret
p(a,A)
as the probability item
a
is chosen from menu
A
.

For
C\subseteq A
, we define
p(C,A)\ =\ \displaystyle\sum_{a\in C}p(a,A)
as the probability that an item in
C
is chosen within
A
.
To simplify notation, we omit curly brackets in the argument of the choice function, e.g., we write
p(a,abc)
in place of
p(a,\{a,b,c\})
.
Choice
p
is the primitive in our model, and is assumed to be observable.

Partition
X
in indexed subsets
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
The partition is induced by a surjective map
\pi\colon X\to I
, where
I
is the set of indices and each
X_{i}
is a class.
We say that an item
a
belongs to class
i
if
a\in X_{i}
(
\pi(a)=i
).
For each
i\in I
, let
\mathscr{X}_{i}=2^{X_{i}}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
be the collection of nonempty subsets of
X_{i}
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{I}=2^{I}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
.
We model a two-step choice process:
the agent first picks a class, then an item within it.
In both choices, the agent randomizes among options at disposal.
As a result, the probability an item
a\in X_{i}
is chosen within a menu
A
equals the probability that two events happen:
(1).
class
i
is selected in
\pi(A)
, and
(2).

a
is chosen among available items in class
i
, namely, in
A\cap X_{i}
.
A
stochastic choice with categorization
(
SCC
) is a choice
p
resulting from this process.

Definition 2
.

A positive stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is
with categorization
(
SCC
) if there exist

•

a partition of
X
in classes
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, induced by a surjective map
\pi\colon X\to I
;

•

a positive stochastic choice on indeces
\omega\colon I\times\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]
;

•

a family
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of positive stochastic choices on classes
\sigma_{i}\colon X_{i}\times\mathscr{X}_{i}\to[0,1]
;

such that, for all
A\in\mathscr{X},\ i\in\pi(A),\ a\in A\cap X_{i}
,

p(a,A)\ =\ \omega(i,\pi(A))\ \sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i}).

We write
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
to denote a stochastic choice
p
with categorization with respect to partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

In Definition
2
, function
\pi
represents the classification process,
associating each item in menu
A
to a class in
\pi(A)
.
The classification is consistent across menus.

Choice
\omega
gives the probability of choosing a class in
\pi(A)
.
Note that, by definition,
\omega(i,\pi(A))=\omega(i,\pi(B))
for any
A,B
such that
\pi(A)=\pi(B)
.
Thus, the probability of choosing any class is independent of its composition.
Finally,
\sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})
is the probability
a
is chosen among items in the same class.

Choices
\omega
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
have a natural relation to
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
:

•

p(A\cap X_{i},A)=\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}p(a,A)\ =\ \displaystyle\sum_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}\sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})\ \omega(i,\pi(A))\ =\ \omega(i,\pi(A))
;

•

for any
A\subseteq X_{i},\quad p(a,A)\ =\ \sigma_{i}(a,A)
.

The decomposition
p(a,A)\ =\ \omega(i,\pi(A))\ \sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})
allows to see that
\sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})
simply is the probability of choosing
a
conditional on choosing in
X_{i}
.

All stochastic choices are trivially
with categorization
with respect to the trivial partition
P=\{\{X\}\}
and the
n
-partition of
X
, containing all singletons.
Hereafter we focus on cases in which the partition is significant.

Definition 3
.

A
SCC

(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is non-degenerate if
1<|I|<n
.

A stochastic choice can be non-degenerate
SCC
with respect to multiple partitions.
Denote
\mathcal{P}
the set of all such partitions.
For two distinct
P,P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
, we say that
P
is
coarser
than
P^{\prime}
, denoted
P\succ P^{\prime}
, if every element of
P^{\prime}
is a subset of some element in
P
.
2
2

2

Formally, let
P=\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
P^{\prime}=\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
P\succ P^{\prime}
if for all
j\in J
there exists
i\in I
such that
X_{j}\subseteq X_{i}
.

Relation
\succ
is a partial order on
\mathcal{P}
, and the set of the coarsest partitions is
\max(\mathcal{P},\succ)=\{P\in\mathcal{P}\colon\nexists P^{\prime}\text{ such that }P^{\prime}\succ P\}
.

Proposition 1
.

There exists a unique coarsest partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Also, any other partition
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in J}\in\mathcal{P}
is such that, for any
j\in J
,
X_{j}\subseteq X_{i}
for some
i\in I
.

The proof (see Appendix
A
) shows that finer partitions can only be obtained by breaking down a class into subclasses.
Namely, this happens when some choice
\sigma_{i}
on a class
X_{i}
is itself non-degenerate
SCC
.

Finally, we note that the definition of choice with categorization need not rely on the existence of a utility representation for choices among (
\omega
) nor within (
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
) classes.
This is the main departure from Nested Logit and Nested Stochastic Choices (
NSC
) as introduced by Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
(see Section
5
).

Example
2
in Appendix
B
exhibits a choice which is not
SCC
, showing the model is falsifiable.

2
A characterization

Let
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
be a positive stochastic choice. To deduce whether
p
is
SCC
, we define the choice behavior revealing the existence of a category in
X
.

Definition 4
.

A
category
is a set
C\in\mathscr{X}
such that the following properties hold:

c-Independence
: for all
S\subseteq C
,
a,b\in S
,
E\subseteq X\setminus C,

\displaystyle\frac{p(a,S)}{p(b,S)}\ =\ \frac{p(a,S\cup E)}{p(b,S\cup E)};

c-Neutrality
: for all
E\subseteq X\setminus C,\ x\in E,\ S\subseteq C,\ S\neq\varnothing
,

p(x,S\cup E)\ =\ p(x,C\cup E).

Set
X
and all singletons are trivially categories in
X
.
We say that
C
is a
non-trivial
category if
1<|C|<|X|
.

The interpretation of a
category
relies on two assumptions.
First, external items should be irrelevant for assessing a preference among items in a category (
c-Independence
).
For illustration, if the decision maker classifies vehicles by dimension, the availability of an SUV does not change her preference over city cars
a
and
b
.
Second, items in the same category are thought of as substitutes (
c-Neutrality
).
This entails that the presence of an additional city car does not affect the probability an SUV is chosen from a menu, as long as some city car is already available.
Indeed, the availability of an additional city car should only affect the probability another city car is chosen.

The definition of
category
represents a relevant departure from that of
nest
in Nested Logit and in Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
.
Specifically, these models rely on the assumption that the choice probability ratio for two items
a
and
b
in the same nest must be fixed in all menus (local
IIA
):

\frac{p(a,ab)}{p(b,ab)}=\frac{p(a,A)}{p(b,A)},\ \text{ for all }A\in\mathscr{X}.

Local
IIA
is stronger than
c-Independence
, as it also requires that items in the same nest to be ‘irrelevant’ for assessing a preference between
a,b
, as represented by the choice probability ratio.
Indeed, we argue that local
IIA
may be too restrictive.
It is violated in case of well-known context effects, such as the attraction and the compromise effect (Huber et al.
[
20
]
and Simonson
[
40
]
), that are most likely to arise when contemplated items are comparable.
On the other hand,
c-Neutrality
need not hold for a nest.
In Nested Logit, the probability of choosing a nest depends on the expected value of its items.
On the contrary,
c-Neutrality
conceals a form of commitment: the agent looks into a category only after having selected it.
See Section
5
for further comments.

In Appendix
C
, we show that
c-Independence
and
c-Neutrality
are independent.

Proposition 2
.

Any two distinct non-trivial categories
C,D
must be either disjoint or nested. Namely,
C\cap D\neq\varnothing\Rightarrow C\subseteq D
or
D\subseteq C
.

See Appendix
D
for a proof.
The main theorem can now be stated.

Theorem 1
.

A positive stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is a non-degenerate
SCC
if and only if there exists a non-trivial category in
X
.

The proof is constructive.
c-Independence
fixes a stochastic choice on each class (
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
), while
c-Neutrality
allows to specify a well-defined choice among classes (
\omega
) (see Appendix
E
).
As a result,
c-Independence
and
c-Neutrality
allow the observer to detect categories in
X
.
Disjoint non-trivial categories are classes in the partitions that define non-degenerate
SCC
.
In particular, the coarsest partition contains all maximal non-trivial categories in
X
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of partitions for which
p
is non-degenerate
SCC
, and let
\mathcal{C}^{M}
be the collection of non-trivial maximal categories in
X
.
The unique coarsest partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{P}
is such that for all
C\in\mathcal{C}^{M}
,
C=X_{i}
, for some
i\in I
.

3
A generating population

Stochastic choices can also indicate frequency of choice in a population when each individual’s choice is observed only once per menu.
In this section, we investigate which population generates a
SCC
.

A (deterministic) choice function on
X
is a map
c\colon\mathscr{X}\to X
such that
\varnothing\neq c(A)\in A
, for all
A\in\mathscr{X}
.
We focus on
resolvable
choices, as modeled in Cantone et al.
[
7
]
.
3
3

3

The original definition refers to choice correspondences. Here we report a restriction to choice functions.

Definition 5
.

A choice
c_{X}\colon\mathscr{X}\to X
is
resolvable
with respect to a partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, induced by a surjective map
\pi\colon X\to I
, if there exist a choice on indices (base choice)
c_{I}\colon\mathscr{I}\to I
, and a family of choices on classes (fiber choices)
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, such that
\displaystyle c_{X}(A)=c_{i}(A\cap X_{i})
, with
i=c_{I}(\pi(A))
.

Any resolvable choice
c_{X}
can be described as the result of two steps.
For any menu
A
, first an index
i
is picked according to
c_{I}\colon\mathscr{I}\to I
, such that

c_{I}(\pi(A))\ =\ \pi(c_{X}(A));

then an alternative in
A\cap X_{i}
is chosen by applying
c_{i}\colon\mathscr{X}_{i}\to X_{i}
, and
c_{i}(A\cap X_{i})\ =\ c_{X}(A\cap X_{i})
.
No further form of consistency is required.

For a partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, denote
\mathcal{R}
the set of choices resolvable with respect to
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Each function in
\mathcal{R}
is an instance of individual choice behaviour.
By defining a probability distribution
Q
on
\mathcal{R}
, we can generate a stochastic choice representing the occurrence of each choice outcome in population
\mathcal{R}
.
Interestingly, any non-degenerate stochastic choice with categorization can be recovered in this way.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
be non-degenerate
SCC
.
There exists a probability distribution
Q
on
\mathcal{R}
such that:

p(a,A)\ =\ \sum_{c_{X}:c_{X}(A)=a}Q(c_{X}).

However, not all distributions on
\mathcal{R}
induce a stochastic choice with categorization, as illustrated by the following example.

.

Notice that

This is a violation of

Moreover, there exists no partition for which

To illustrate how this violation occurs, we can interpret

as candidates in an election.
If
a,b
and

\frac{p(a,abc)}{p(b,abc)}\ =\ 1
.
However, when

Hence, a probability distribution on the set of deterministic resolvable choices need not generate a stochastic choice with categorization.
The following theorem characterizes stochastic choices generated by a population of deterministic resolvable choices.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
be a positive stochastic choice, let
\pi\colon X\to I
induce a partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
X
, and let
\mathcal{R}
collect deterministic choices resolvable with respect to
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
The following are equivalent:

1.

\text{for all }A,B\in\mathscr{X}\text{ s.t.\ }\pi(A)=\pi(B),\quad p(A\cap X_{i},A)\ =\ p(B\cap X_{i},B)
,

2.

p
is represented by a probability distribution
Q
on
\mathcal{R}
,

p(a,A)\ =\ \sum_{c_{X}:c_{X}(A)=a}Q(c_{X}).

A proof is provided in Appendix
G
.
Here, we point out that condition
(1).
is implied by
c-Neutrality
for each set in
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Expectedly,
SCC
is a subset of the set of stochastic choices generated by a population of deterministic resolvable choices.

To conclude our analysis, we hint to the additional property required for
Q
to generate a resolvable stochastic choice, to ensure that violations such as those in Example
1
are forbidden.
With abuse of notation, we write
\pi(c_{X})
to denote the choice function on
I
induced by
c_{X}
, and denote
c_{X\upharpoonright i}
the restriction of
c_{X}
to
X_{i}
.
Let
Q(c_{I})\ =\ Q(\{c_{X}\colon\pi(c_{X})=c_{I}\})
.
In order for
Q
to generate a non-degenerate
SCC
, it must be

\displaystyle Q(c_{X})\ =\ Q(c_{I}\colon\pi(c_{X})=c_{I})\prod_{i\in I}Q(\{c_{i}\colon c_{X\upharpoonright i}=c_{i}\}).

This entails that, in the menus where more classes are made available, agents change their choice preserving probability ratios.

4
A generalization

For a
SCC
, the choice among classes is represented by a menu-independent stochastic choice,
\omega
(Definition
2
).
In this section, we lift this restriction and introduce choices
with weak categorization
(
SCWC
).

Definition 6
.

A positive stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is
with weak categorization
(
SCWC
) if there exist

•

a partition of
X
in subsets
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, induced by a surjective map
\pi\colon X\to I
;

•

a family
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
of functions
\omega_{A}(\cdot,\pi(A))\colon I\to[0,1]
such that

–

\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=0\ \Leftrightarrow i\notin\pi(A)
,

–

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in\pi(A)}\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))\ =\ 1
;

•

a family
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of positive choices
\sigma_{i}\colon X_{i}\times\mathscr{X}_{i}\to[0,1]
;

such that for all
A\in\mathscr{X}
,
i\in\pi(A)
,
a\in A\cap X_{i}
,

p(a,A)=\ \omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))\ \sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i}).

We focus on choices that are
SCWC
with respect to a meaningful partition.

Definition 7
.

A
SCWC

(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is non-degenerate if
1<|I|<n
.

By employing a family of choices
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
, each indexed by a menu, Definition
6
allows displays of erratic behavior in choices
among
and
within
categories, as long as items are consistently classified and conditional choices
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are preserved.
This feature informs the structure of the model.
Instances of be menu-dependence in the choice of a class include several well-documented phenomena:

•

choice overload:
a class is chosen less and less frequently as the number of its items increases to become overwhelming (Chernev et al.
[
8
]
, Iyengar and Lepper
[
21
]
).
For instance, the chances class
i
is chosen may be directly penalized by numerosity: for any
A,B\in\mathscr{X}
such that
\pi(A)=\pi(B)\text{ and }|A\cap X_{j}|=|B\cap X_{j}|\text{ for all }j\in\pi(A)\setminus\{i\}
,

|A\cap X_{i}|>|B\cap X_{i}|\quad\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))<\omega_{A}(i,\pi(B)).

To illustrate, define
\rho_{A}(i,\pi(A))=|A\cap X_{i}|^{\beta}
, where
\beta
is a parameter that incorporates the effect of numerosity.
Let
\beta<0
, and define choices
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
as follows

\displaystyle\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=\frac{\rho_{A}(i,\pi(A))}{\sum_{j\in\pi(A)}\rho_{A}(j,\pi(A))}.

•

preference for flexibility:
the availability of numerous representatives of a class may convey its salience,
and increase the probability it is chosen at the first stage.
This effect is the converse to choice overload as illustrated above.
It can be captured by a form of
monotonicity
: for any
A,B\in\mathscr{X}
such that
\pi(A)=\pi(B)\text{ and }(A\cap X_{j})=(B\cap X_{j})\text{ for all }j\in\pi(A)\setminus\{i\}
,

(A\cap X_{i})\subseteq(B\cap X_{i})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))\leqslant\omega_{A}(i,\pi(B)).

For instance, define
\rho_{A}(i,\pi(A))=|A\cap X_{i}|^{\beta}
, with
\beta>0
,

\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=\frac{\rho_{A}(i,\pi(A))}{\sum_{j\in\pi(A)}\rho_{A}(j,\pi(A))}.

•

associationistic salience:
any alternative can draw attention to the whole class, so that its presence may boost the probability that any item in the class is chosen.
We follow Li and Tang
[
26
]
, and obtain a form of monotonicity.
Let
s\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a strictly positive function, and interpret
s(x)
as salience of item
x
.
Then a menu dependent weight can be defined for each class:

w_{A}(i)=\sum_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}s(a),\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=\frac{w_{A}(i)}{\sum_{j\in\pi(A)}w_{A}(j)}.

•

reference-dependent value:

as in Barbos
[
4
]
, define a class value
r_{A}(\cdot)
depending on a utility value
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
and a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
:

r_{A}(i)=\max_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}u(a)-\theta\min_{b\in A\cap X_{i}}u(b)\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=\frac{r_{A}(i)}{\sum_{j\in\pi(A)}r_{A}(j)}.

Finally,
SCC
is obtained as a special case under the restricition that
\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))\ =\ \omega_{B}(i,\pi(B))
for all
A,B\in\mathscr{X}
such that
\pi(A)=\pi(B)
.

4.1
A characterization of
SCWC

To assess whether
p
is
SCWC
, we investigate the existence of a
weak category
, generalizing Definition
4
.

Definition 8
.

A
weak category
is a set
G\in\mathscr{X}
such that the following holds:

c-Independence
: for all
S\subseteq G
,
a,b\in S
,
E\subseteq X\setminus G
,

\displaystyle\frac{p(a,S)}{p(b,S)}\ =\ \frac{p(a,S\cup E)}{p(b,S\cup E)}.

A weak category
G
is
non-trivial
if
1<|G|<|X|
.

A set of items constitutes a
weak category
if choice probability ratios are not altered after the addition of external items.
In this setting, we acknowledge that choices may violate
c-Neutrality
in response to changes in context, possibly conveyed by a change in number or identity in the set of available items.
For instance, if many city cars are available, the decision maker may conclude the retailer specializes in city cars and can offer good options, thus making the choice of a city car more likely.
Technically, this allows more flexible structures: weak categories need not be disjoint or ordered by set inclusion, and the intersection of any two weak categories is a weak category, as well (see Appendix
D
).

Theorem 4
.

A positive stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is non-degenerate
SCWC
if and only if there exists a non-trivial weak category in
X
.

Disjoint weak categories in
X
are classes in the partition for which
p
is
SCWC
,
c-Independence
fixes choices
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, choices
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
are then easily retrieved (see proof in Appendix
F
).

Finally, additional properties on
p
can induce peculiar
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
:

•

categorical choice overload:
if each weak category
G
also satisfies
c-Overload
:

\text{for all }\ E\subseteq X\setminus G,\ x\in E,\ S,T\in 2^{G}\setminus\{\varnothing\},\ S\subsetneq T,\quad p(x,S\cup E)\ >\ p(x,T\cup E),

then functions
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
capture choice overload.

•

monotonicity:
if each weak category
G
also satisfy
c-Monotonicity
:

\text{for all }\ E\subseteq X\setminus G,\ x\in E,\ S,T\in 2^{G}\setminus\{\varnothing\},\ S\subsetneq T,\quad p(x,S\cup E)\ <\ p(x,T\cup E),

then functions
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
are monotonic.

Related literature

In a deterministic setting, sequential procedures have been modeled as a result of choice over in menus in anticipation of future preferences (Kreps
[
24
]
, Gul and Pesendorfer
[
16
]
and Heydari
[
18
]
), bounded rationality (Masatlioglu
[
32
]
, Lleras et al.
[
25
]
, Frick et al.
[
14
]
), preliminary screening steps (Manzini and Mariotti
[
29
]
, Apesteguia and Ballester
[
1
]
, Tyson
[
48
]
), or categorization (Barbos
[
4
]
, Manzini and Mariotti
[
30
]
).
In all these instances, the agent focuses on a subset of available items, and picks a selection within it.
This work is especially close in spirit to Cantone et al.
[
7
]
.
Their aim is to disclose whether an observed deterministic choice can be decomposed into independent ‘smaller’ choices,
among
and
within
categories.
If this is the case, the choice is said to be
resolvable
.
As a matter of fact, we find that any
stochastic choice with categorization
is generated by a population of resolvable choices (Section
3
). In what follows, we focus on related stochastic choice models.

Stochastic choice with categorization has an intersection with Nested Stochastic Choice.
We report the definition as introduced in Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
.

Definition 9

(
NSC
)
.

A stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is a
nested stochastic choice
(
NSC
) if there are a partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, a utility function
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and a nest utility function
v\colon\bigcup_{i\in I}2^{X_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
v(\varnothing)=0
such that, for any
A\in\mathscr{X}
and
a\in A\cap X_{i}
,

\displaystyle p(a,A)\ =\ \frac{v(A\cap X_{i})}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}v(A\cap X_{j})}\ \frac{u(a)}{\displaystyle\sum_{b\in A\cap X_{i}}u(b)}.

Thus, item
a\in A\cap X_{i}
is chosen within
A
if
(i).
nest
A\cap X_{i}
is chosen in
A
, with probability
\frac{v(A\cap X_{i})}{\sum_{j\in I}v(A\cap X_{j})}
;
(ii).

a
is chosen in
A\cap X_{i}
, with probability
\frac{u(a)}{\sum_{b\in A\cap X_{i}}u(b)}
.
Nested stochastic choices need not be
SCC
in general.
Indeed, a
NSC

(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is a
SCC
only if, for all
A,B\in\mathscr{X}\text{ s.t.\ }\pi(A)=\pi(B),\text{ for all }i\in I,\ \ v(A\cap X_{i})\ =\ v(B\cap X_{i})
.
This condition allows
\omega(i,\pi(A))=\frac{v(A\cap X_{i})}{\sum_{j\in I}v(A\cap X_{j})}
to be well-defined.
Nested Logit is a special case of
NSC
(see Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
), such that, for all
A\in\mathscr{X}
,
v(A\cap X_{i})\ =\ \big(\sum_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}u(a)\big)^{\lambda_{i}}
, for a set of strictly positive real numbers
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
It is easily shown that there must be some
A,B\in\mathscr{X}\text{ s.t. }\pi(A)=\pi(B)
such that
\big(\sum_{a\in A\cap X_{i}}u(a)\big)^{\lambda_{i}}\ \neq\ \big(\sum_{b\in B\cap X_{i}}u(b)\big)^{\lambda_{i}}
, so that Nested Logit is an instance of
NSC
which is not non-degenerate
SCC
.
Conversely, Definition
9
entails that the choice probability ratio for two items
a
and
b
in the same nest is fixed in all menus.
This is not necessarily the case for a
SCC
.
Indeed, the main addition in our model is to allow for menu effects within a class.

It is worth to point out that any non-degenerate
SCC
violates Luce model
[
27
]
.

Definition 10
.

A positive stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is Luce model if there exists a utility function
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
such that
p(a,A)=\frac{u(a)}{\sum_{x\in B}u(x)}
, for all
A\in\mathscr{X}
.

Let
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
be non-degenerate
SCC
.
Without loss of generality, consider a set
A=\{a,b,c\}\subseteq X
such that
a,b\in X_{i}
and
c\in X_{j}
for some
i,j\in I
.
We check the existence of a utility function
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}_{++}
such that
p(a,B)=\frac{u(a)}{\sum_{x\in B}u(x)}
,
B\subseteq A
.
Since
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is
SCC
,
p(a,ac)\ =\ p(b,bc)\ =\ p(ab,abc)\ =\ \omega(i,ij)
.
Thus, it must be

p(a,ac)=p(ab,abc)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \frac{u(a)}{u(a)+u(c)}=\frac{u(a)+u(b)}{u(a)+u(b)+u(c)}\ \Leftrightarrow\ u(b)u(c)=0,

which contradicts the assumption of strict positivity for
u
.

Under the additional assumption that items within a class have equal probability of choice, stochastic choices by categorization reduce to
Luce with replicas
, introduced by Faro
[
13
]
.

Luce with replicas
is also a particular case of
NSC
, so that it lies in the intersection of
NSC
and non-degenerate
SCC
.

As anticipated, stochastic choice with categorization is also comparable to two prominent random utility models explicitly accounting for similarity between items.
In
elimination by aspects
(
EBA
) by Tversky
[
46
]
, options are perceived as bundles of aspects.
The probability item
x
is chosen from
A
depends on how valuable its distinctive aspects are, and how likely
x
stands out among alternatives sharing aspects of interest.
According to the
attribute rule
in Gul et al.
[
17
]
, the probability an item
x
is chosen depends on which attributes are exhibited in
x
, and, specifically, the intensity of their presence.
Attribute rules and
EBA
are not
SCC
.
On the other hand, we argue that similarity is difficult to define rigorously ex ante – it could depend on which properties items share and the intensity of all the common features, or only those that are relevant; alternatively, it could be goal-derived, or be received by the classification in categories itself.
As the
SCC
model is silent on the motives for the classification of items, it could be applied to uncover similarity criteria.

Further,
SCC
differs from
sequential Luce model
(Zhang
[
49
]
), where the agent is allowed a default option and
IIA
must hold within a class.

Finally, we focus on the relation of choices with categorization to the family of random utility models (
RUM
).
A stochastic choice
p
on
X
admits a random utility representation (Block and Marschak
[
5
]
, Falmagne
[
12
]
) if probabilities
p(a,A)
allow to estimate a probability distribution
\mathbb{P}
on the set
\mathcal{L}
of linear orders
L
over
X
, such that

•

\mathbb{P}(L)\geqslant 0
for all
L\in\mathcal{L}
;

•

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{L}_{(a,A)}}\ \mathbb{P}(L)=p(a,A),\ \text{ with }\mathcal{L}_{(a,A)}\ =\ \{L\in\mathcal{L}\colon aLb,\ \forall b\in A\setminus\{a\}\}
.

If this is the case, we say that
p
is rationalizable by
RUM
, or simply
RUM
.

Stochastic choices with categorization need not admit a random utility representation.
Yet, rationalizability of the observable
SCC
can be linked to rationalizability of its components.

Theorem 5
.

A stochastic choice with categorization
(p,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is
RUM
if and only if choices
\{\sigma_{i}\colon X_{i}\times\mathscr{X}_{i}\to[0,1]\}_{i\in I}
and
\omega\colon I\times\mathscr{I}\to[0,1]
are
RUM
.

In other words, rationalizability by
RUM
is inherited from
p
by its components
\omega,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
; vice versa, it is passed on from
\omega
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
to their composition,
p
.
A proof is reported in Appendix
H
.

By Definition
6
, for any
SCWC
, there exist a partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, a family
\{\omega_{A}\}_{A\in\mathscr{X}}
and a family
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that
p(a,A)\ =\ \sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})\ \omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))
, for all
A\in\mathscr{X},\ a\in A\cap X_{i}
.
Recall that
\sigma_{i}
is a restriction of
p
to class
i
, while
\displaystyle\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=p(A\cap X_{i},A)
.
As a result, for all
A\in\mathscr{X},\ a\in A\cap X_{i}
, we equivalently have
p(a,A)\ =\ p(a,A\cap X_{i})\ p(A\cap X_{i},A)
.
This makes apparent the link between Definition
6
and the Choice Axiom introduced by Luce
[
27
]
, reported below:

Choice Axiom:
For all
A,B\in\mathscr{X}
,
B\subseteq A
, for all
x\in B
,

p(x,A)=p(x,B)\ p(B,A).

The Choice Axiom makes use of universal quantifiers, so that the equality must hold for all menus and all their subsets, and all items.
For
SCWC
, instead, Definition
6
requires the equality to hold for all menus, for
some
partition
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
This suffices to show that Luce model is nested in
SCWC
.
Indeed, for a Luce model
p
every subset
A\in\mathscr{X}
with cardinality
1<|A|<|X|
is a non-trivial
weak
category.

Also, the definition of Nested Stochastic Choices (see Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
) is directly generalized by the notion of choice by weak categorization.
Any nest is a
weak category
, and the link to weak categorization can be made apparent by letting, for all
A\in\mathscr{X},\ a\in A\cap X_{i}
,

\omega_{A}(i,\pi(A))=\frac{v(A\cap X_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}v(A\cap X_{j})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma_{i}(a,A\cap X_{i})=\frac{u(a)}{\sum_{b\in A\cap X_{i}}u(b)}.

It follows naturally that Nested Logit is an instance of monotonic
SCWC
.

The Associationistic Luce Model introduced by Li and Tang
[
26
]
captures the phenomenon entailing salience of an item sheds light to the whole class that includes it.
First a class is chosen according to a Luce-type formula with associationistic salience as weights, then an item within it is selected according to Luce’s rule.
The model is a special case of
SCWC
with
associationistic salience
, with the restriction that choices
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
observe Luce’s
IIA
.

Stochastic choices by weak categorization also have affinity to Perception adjusted Luce model (
PALM
) by Echenique et al.
[
11
]
.
Their model describes choices of an agent that considers items in a priority order.
As a result, items are chosen according to
IIA
only if they are evaluated simultaneously.
Under the assumption that
IIA
holds whitin each class, and that alternatives in the same class are noticed at the same time, by association,
SCWC
come conceptually close to
PALM
.
However, technical differences (e.g.,
PALM
contemplate a default option) prevent from drawing a formal comparison of the models.
Finally, we recognise similarities to the approach in General Luce model, introduced by Echenique et al.
[
10
]
.
The model entails a two-step choice.
First, a total order is applied, to isolate maximal elements in the menu.
Then a Luce’s type formula is applied to break ties.
In fact, the total order naturally induces a partition of items in equivalence classes.
While these could be interpreted as categories,
SCWC
only refers to positive stochastic choices, so that the two models of choice do not intersect.

On a different note,
SCWC
provides an interesting interpretation to instances in which a choice
p
is not rationalizable by
RUM
in its full domain
X
, though it is
locally

G\subseteq X
.

Definition 11
.

A stochastic choice
p\colon X\times\mathscr{X}\to[0,1]
is
locally rationalizable
at
G\subseteq X
if there exists a probability distribution
q
on the set of linear orders
\mathcal{L}_{G}
on
G
such that, for all
x\in G
,
A\subseteq X
,

p(x,A)\ =\ p(G\cap A,A)\ \ q(\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{G}\colon\max(A\cap G,\ell)=x\}).

We interpret
G
as a subset of comparable items, which can be ranked in a linear order.
When choosing within
G
, each one of the possible orders in
\mathcal{L}_{G}
can realize with a certain probability, and be applied by the agent.
Note that a linear order on
G
is a partial order on
X
.
Indeed, suppose the agent is only able to compare and rank similar items.
According to each criterion of similarity, several sets of items can be ordered, and each order can be applied with a given probability, depending on the perceived relevance.
Items in
G
are those recognised as similar according to
every
criterion.
In virtue of this procedure, choice restricted to
G
has a random utility representation.

Proposition 3
.

If
p
is locally rationalizable at
G
such that
1<|G|<|X|
, then
G
is a non-trivial weak category.

It follows from Theorem
4
that a non-degenerate locally rationalizable
p
is
SCWC
.

Conclusions

This work introduces stochastic choices with categorization (
SCC
), a novel model of two-step stochastic choice.
Its characterization allows to disclose the presence of mutually comparable items in a menu, perceived as members of the same class, possibly to uncover unobservable classification principles.
In a more general interpretation, the model entails that the primitive choice can be broken into independent sub-choices, each defined on a class.
Each sub-choice can be studied separately, to recover local rationality traits or adopt the most fitting model for choice prediction.
In addition, we show that any
SCC
is generated by a population of deterministic choices sharing the same classification outline.

SCC
is generalized by stochastic choices with weak categorization (
SCWC
), which allows the probability of choosing a class to be menu dependent.

Prominent models of stochastic choices, such as Nested Logit (McFadden
[
33
]
) and
NSC
(Kovach and Tserenjigmid
[
23
]
), share relevant features with the model.
Nonetheless,
SCC
(and
SCWC
) can accommodate for other context effects, while still specifically accounting for similarity.
Indeed, the novel notions of independence (
c-Independence
and
c-Neutrality
) disentangle categorization from other sources of violations of Luce’s
IIA
.

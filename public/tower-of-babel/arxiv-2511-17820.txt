The Closest Point Method for Surface PDEs with General Boundary Conditions
Tony WongColin B. Macdonald, Byungjoon Lee

Abstract
We generalize the closest point method (CPM) to solve surface partial differential equations with general boundary conditions. The proposed extrapolation method provides a unified framework for treating a broad class of inhomogeneous Neumann and Robin boundary conditions within the framework of CPM. The accuracy and robustness of the method are demonstrated through numerical convergence studies of an elliptic problem, Steklov eigenvalue problems, and a nonlinear reaction–diffusion system.

Introduction

Many partial differential equations (PDEs) arising in scientific and engineering problems are defined on curved surfaces, which requires robust and accurate numerical methods to resolve the geometry. The closest point method (CPM)
[
18
]
is a versatile embedding method for numerically solving PDEs on curved geometry, with applications such as pattern formation
[
13
,
4
,
16
,
17
]
, geometry and image processing
[
19
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
1
]
, and first-passage time problems of diffusion
[
9
,
8
]
.

However, there is limited discussion on the uses of CPM for open surfaces.
It remains an open challenge to extend the CPM to surface PDEs with general boundary conditions beyond the homogeneous Neumann and Dirichlet types
[
12
]
.
In this paper, we provide a simple and accurate extrapolation technique for the CPM to tackle a broad class of boundary conditions.
Our work is particularly relevant for applications where boundary effects are critical, such as fluid dynamics on curved interfaces and biological membrane modeling.

1.1
Terminology and notations

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an open surface — a smooth surface embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with a co-dimension one boundary
\partial\mathcal{S}
. For
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathbf{N}(\bm{x})
be a unit normal to
\mathcal{S}
. For
\bm{y}\in\partial\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathbf{T}(\bm{y})
a unit tangent to
\partial\mathcal{S}
, and
\mathbf{n}(\bm{y})
the unit outward co-normal to
\partial\mathcal{S}
, as in Figure
1
. Let
\nabla_{S}
be the surface gradient on
\mathcal{S}
and
\partial_{n}=\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{S}}
be the co-normal derivative defined on
\partial\mathcal{S}
. In this paper, we consider the following boundary condition for a function
u
defined on
\mathcal{S}
:

\partial_{n}u\big|_{\bm{y}}=j\left(\bm{y},u(\bm{y})\right),\hskip 18.49988pt\bm{y}\in\partial\mathcal{S}.

(1.1)

The form (
1.1
) includes many commonly used boundary conditions.
For example, the homogeneous Neumann condition corresponds to
j=0
, while the Robin condition corresponds to
j=-\kappa u
.

The
closest point function
of
\mathcal{S}
is defined by
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}):=\argmin_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}}\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. Provided that
\mathcal{S}
is smooth, there exists a tubular neighbourhood
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
where the closest point function
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
is well-defined
[
7
]
. Within
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
, we define the set of
interior points
as
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}):\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\notin\partial\mathcal{S}\}
and the set of
exterior points
as
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}):\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\in\partial\mathcal{S}\}
(see Figure
1
(b)).

The
closest point extension operator

E
extends a surface function
u
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
by
Eu(\bm{x}):=u(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))
. The operator
E
propagates the surface values along the normal direction in
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
and the boundary values to
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
.

Following
[
12
]
, we define the
modified closest point function

\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}):=\mathrm{cp}(r(\bm{x}))
,
where
r(\bm{x})
is the reflection of
\bm{x}
across
S
,
i.e.,
r(\bm{x}):=2\,\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})-\bm{x}
.
The
modified closest point extension operator

\bar{E}
is defined by
\bar{E}u(\bm{x}):=u(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}))
.
If
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
, we have
\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x})=\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
.
Therefore, the extensions
\bar{E}u
and
Eu
coincide in
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E})
. For
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
, the value of
\bar{E}u(\bm{x})
is
\bar{E}u(r(\bm{x}))
.
In other words,
\bar{E}
mirrors the interior values to the exterior points, and indeed this is sufficient to implement homogeneous Neumann conditions
[
12
]
, but is not enough for a smooth implementation of (
1.1
).

\mathcal{S}

and

. (c) The smooth extension
\bar{u}
defined in (
2.4
).

2
The closest point method

We introduce two lemmas to support our main theorem characterizing a smooth extension which propagates surface values constantly along the normal direction in
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
and into
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
according to the boundary condition (
1.1
).
We then describe the numerical discretization.

2.1
Extrapolation for the general boundary condition

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
.
Let
\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))
and
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))
.
Then
\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
lies in
\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{N},\mathbf{n}\}
.

Proof.

Near
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
, there exists a local chart
(U,\phi)
mapping a neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{S}
to the half-plane
\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\eta\geq 0\}
, with
\partial\mathcal{S}\cap U
corresponding to
\eta=0
. The boundary curve admits the 1D parameterization
\gamma(t)=\phi^{-1}(t,0)
for
t
in some interval
I
, and
\gamma
is smooth and regular. Let
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})=\gamma(t_{*})
with

t_{*}=\argmin_{t\in I}\|\gamma(t)-\bm{x}\|^{2}
.
The first-order condition gives
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{*})^{T}[\gamma(t_{*})-\bm{x}]=0
, so
\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
is orthogonal to
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{*})
, the tangent to
\partial\mathcal{S}
at
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
. Since
\{\mathbf{N},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{n}\}
is an orthogonal basis, it follows that
\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{N},\mathbf{n}\}
.
∎

With Lemma
1
, we characterize how a smooth extension
\bar{u}
propagates the boundary values into
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
, according to the (
1.1
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
u
be a smooth function defined on
\mathcal{S}
that satisfies (
1.1
).
Let
\bar{u}
be a smooth extension of
u
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
such that
\bar{u}(\bm{x})=u(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))
for
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
.
Suppose
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
. Let
\mathbf{w}=(\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))/\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|
.
Denote the unit outward co-normal
\mathbf{n}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))
by
\mathbf{n}
. Then, we have

\partial_{w}\bar{u}\,|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{n}\rangle\,j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\bar{u}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big)\,,\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ denotes the Euclidean inner product.}

(2.1)

Proof.

By Lemma
1
,
\mathbf{w}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{N},\mathbf{n}\}
, where
\mathbf{N}
is a unit normal at
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
. We apply the orthogonal decomposition
\mathbf{w}=\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{n}\rangle\mathbf{n}+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{N}\rangle\mathbf{N}
and the linearity of directional derivatives to obtain

\partial_{w}\bar{u}\,|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{n}\rangle\,\partial_{n}\bar{u}\,|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{N}\rangle\,\partial_{N}\bar{u}\,|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}\,.

Now
\bar{u}
is constant along the normal
\mathbf{N}

so
\partial_{N}\bar{u}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=0
.
Secondly, we have
\partial_{n}\bar{u}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=\partial_{n}u|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),u(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big)=j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\bar{u}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big)
.
Hence, we obtain (
2.1
).
∎

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a smooth surface with a tubular neighbourhood
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
.
Suppose
\bar{u}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the extrapolation formula for boundary points

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bm{x})=\bar{u}(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}))

\displaystyle+2\big\langle\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\mathbf{n}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big\rangle\,j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\bar{u}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\bar{E}\bar{u}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle+2\big\langle\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\mathbf{n}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}))\big\rangle\,j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),E\bar{u}(\bm{x})\big),\hskip 18.49988pt\bm{x}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}),

and
\bar{u}(\bm{x})=\bar{u}(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}))
for interior points
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
.
If
\bar{u}
is a sufficiently-smooth function when restricted to surfaces points
\bm{y}\in\mathcal{S}
,
then
\bar{u}|_{\partial S}
satisfies the boundary condition (
1.1
) to within
O(\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|^{3})
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
.
Applying the central difference formula to
\partial_{w}\bar{u}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}
with the stencil
\{\bm{x},r(\bm{x})\}
, we have

\partial_{w}\bar{u}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})}=\frac{\bar{u}(\bm{x})-\bar{u}(r(\bm{x}))}{\|\bm{x}-r(\bm{x})\|}+O(\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|^{2})=\frac{\bar{u}(\bm{x})-\bar{u}(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}))}{2\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|}+O(\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|^{2}),

(2.3)

where the second equality follows by
\bar{u}(r(\bm{x}))=\bar{u}(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}))
and that
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})
is the mid-point of the segment connecting
\bm{x}
and
r(\bm{x})
.
Substituting (
2.3
) into (
2.1
), we obtain the extrapolation formula
and the error term.
∎

Remark 1
.

In practice,
\|\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})\|
is bounded by the radius of tubular neighbourhood, which is a small multiple of the mesh size
\Delta x

[
18
,
14
]
.
Hence, the error term in Theorem
1
is
O(\Delta x^{3})
.

We can combine the extension condition
\bar{u}=\bar{E}\bar{u}
in
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
with the extrapolation formula (
2.2
) to define an extension in all of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})
:

\bar{u}(\bm{x})=\bar{E}\,\bar{u}(\bm{x})+2\,\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\bm{x})\,\langle\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\mathbf{n}\rangle\,j\big(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),E\,\bar{u}(\bm{x})\big),\hskip 9.24994pt\bm{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}),

(2.4)

where
\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}
is the indicator function of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
.

2.2
Embedding formulation and discretization

We illustrate how to incorporate the extrapolation method into the CPM through an elliptic example:

\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u-c\,u=f\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in $\mathcal{S}$,}\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{subject to \eqref{eqn:gbc} in $\partial\mathcal{S}$,}

(2.5)

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{S}
.
Let
\bar{u}
be the extension of the surface solution
u
according to (
2.4
). By the Laplacian principles
[
18
,
5
]
, the Cartesian Laplacian
\Delta
applied to the extension
\bar{u}
coincides with
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u
on
\mathcal{S}
, i.e.,
\Delta\bar{u}\big|_{\mathcal{S}}=\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u
. It follows that
E(\Delta\bar{u})=E(\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u)
. Therefore, by applying the extension operator
E
to (
2.5
), we obtain the embedding equation
E(\Delta\bar{u})-E(cu+f)=0
. Using the idempotent property
E^{2}=E

[
15
]
, this can be rewritten in the compact form

E\left(\Delta\bar{u}-c\bar{u}-\bar{f}\right)=0\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in }\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}),

(2.6)

where
\bar{f}:=Ef
.
Following
[
20
,
5
,
8
]
, we combine (
2.6
) and (
2.4
) via a penalty formulation:

E\left(\Delta\bar{u}-c\,\bar{u}-\bar{f}\right)-\gamma\Big[\bar{u}-\bar{E}\bar{u}-2\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\bm{x})\langle\bm{x}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),\mathbf{n}\rangle j(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}),E\bar{u}(\bm{x}))\Big]=0\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\bm{x}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S})\,,

(2.7)

where
\gamma>0
is a penalty parameter. In practice, we take
\gamma=2d/(\Delta x)^{2}
with
d=3
as the embedding space dimension.

Let
\mathsf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the numerical approximation of
\bar{u}
at grid points
\bm{x}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
. Let
\mathsf{E},\bar{\mathsf{E}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
be the cubic interpolation matrices such that
\left[\mathsf{E}\mathsf{u}\right]_{i}\approx u(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i}))
and
\left[\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{u}\right]_{i}\approx u(\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}_{i}))
(see
[
14
,
5
]
). The discretized embedding system of (
2.7
) reads

\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{L}\mathsf{u}-c\mathsf{u}-\mathsf{f})-\gamma(\mathsf{u}-\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{u}-\mathsf{D}\,\mathsf{j})=0,

(2.8)

where
\mathsf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the diagonal matrix with diagonal entry
\mathsf{D}_{i,i}=2\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\bm{x}_{i})\langle\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i}),\mathbf{n}_{i}\rangle
, and
\mathsf{f},\mathsf{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are defined by
\mathsf{f}_{i}=f(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i}))
and
\mathsf{j}_{i}=j(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i}),[\mathsf{E}\mathsf{u}]_{i})
, respectively.

Remark 2
.

When the analytical form of the unit co-normal
\mathbf{n}
is unavailable, it can be approximated using surface reconstruction techniques
[
21
,
22
,
11
]
.
Alternatively, for
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
, we approximate

\mathbf{n}(\bm{x}_{i})
by
\frac{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})-\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}_{i})}{\|\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})-\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}_{i})\|}
,
which we justify as follows. By Taylor expansion,

\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}_{i})=\mathrm{cp}\!\left(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})+(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})-\bm{x}_{i})\right)=\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})+D\mathrm{cp}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})}(\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})-\bm{x}_{i})+O(\|\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})\|^{2}),

where
D\mathrm{cp}
is the Jacobian of
\mathrm{cp}
. Since
D\mathrm{cp}
projects onto the tangent space of
\mathcal{S}

[
15
]
, and
\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})
is orthogonal to the boundary tangent
\mathbf{T}(\bm{x}_{i})
(by Lemma
1
), the vector
D\mathrm{cp}|_{\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})}(\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i}))
is parallel to
\mathbf{n}(\bm{x}_{i})
. Hence, the above formula approximates
\mathbf{n}(\bm{x}_{i})
up to

O(\|\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})\|^{2})
.
If
\|\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})-\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x}_{i})\|
falls below a small threshold (taken as
10^{-4}
in our experiments), indicating
\bm{x}_{i}-\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x}_{i})
is almost orthogonal to
\mathbf{n}(\bm{x}_{i})
,
we set
\mathsf{D}_{i,i}=0
to avoid division-by-zero.

Numerical examples

In this section, we present numerical examples to validate the extrapolation method in CPM. All computations were performed in
Matlab
R2024a; linear systems are solved using the
backslash
operator, and the eigenvalues are computed with the
eigs
function.
For the computation of the outward normal
\mathbf{n}
, we used the approximation based on
\mathrm{cp}(\bm{x})-\bar{\mathrm{cp}}(\bm{x})
introduced in Remark
2
.

3.1
Poisson equation

Consider the surface Poisson equation with Robin condition,

\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u=f\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }\mathcal{S},\hskip 18.49988pt\partial_{n}u+\kappa u=g\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }\partial\mathcal{S}.

(3.1)

This is a special case of (
2.5
) with
c=0
and
j(\bm{x},u)=-\kappa u+g(\bm{x})
, which we substitute into (
2.8
) to obtain the linear system

\mathsf{Au=b},,\hskip 18.49988pt\text{with }\mathsf{A}=\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{L}-\gamma\left(\mathsf{I}-\bar{\mathsf{E}}+\kappa\mathsf{D}\mathsf{E}\right),\hskip 9.24994pt\mathsf{b}=\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{f}-\gamma\mathsf{g}\,,

(3.2)

where
\mathsf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\mathsf{g}_{i}=g(\bm{x}_{i})
. For the convergence study, we prescribe the exact solution

u(\phi,\theta)=\cos(2\phi)\sin^{2}\theta+\sin(3\phi)\sin^{3}\theta

(3.3)

on the unit upper hemisphere
\mathcal{S}=\left\{(\phi,\theta):\phi\in[0,2\pi],\,\,\theta\in[0,\pi/2]\right\}
. One can verify that (
3.3
) is the Poisson solution to (
3.1
) with
\kappa=1
,

on
\partial\mathcal{S}=\{(\phi,\theta):\theta=\pi/2\}
. The numerical solution
\mathsf{u}
obtained from (

3.2
Steklov eigenvalue problem

Next, we consider the Steklov eigenvalue problem
[
6
]
, which we compute nontrivial eigenpairs
(\phi,\sigma)
that satisfy

\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}\phi=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in }\mathcal{S},\hskip 18.49988pt\partial_{n}\phi=\sigma\phi\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }\partial\mathcal{S}.

(3.4)

This problem arises as a special case of (
2.5
) with
c=0
,
f\equiv 0
, and
j(\bm{x},\phi)=\sigma\phi
. The corresponding discretized embedding formulation of (
3.4
) is given by

\bar{\mathsf{E}}(\mathsf{L\phi})-\gamma\left[\mathsf{\phi}-\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{\phi}-\mathsf{D}(\sigma\,\mathsf{E}\mathsf{\phi})\right]=\mathsf{0},

which leads to the generalized eigenvalue problem
\mathsf{Au}=\sigma\mathsf{Bu}
, where
\mathsf{A}=\bar{\mathsf{E}}\mathsf{L}-\gamma(\mathsf{I}-\bar{\mathsf{E}})
and
\mathsf{B}=-\gamma\,\mathsf{D}\mathsf{E}
. We show the computed eigenfunctions for the unit hemisphere and Möbius strip in Figure
2
.
A conformal mapping argument shows that the Steklov spectrum of the unit hemisphere coincides with that of the unit disk, whose eigenvalues are
\sigma_{0}=0
and
\sigma_{n}=n
(with multiplicity two) for
n\geq 1

[
6
]
. We demonstrate in Figure
2
that our method exhibits second-order accuracy for a selected set of eigenvalues.

3.3
Gray–Scott reaction-diffusion system

Finally, we use our proposed numerical method to demonstrate how the temporal dynamics of the Gray–Scott pattern formation system

\partial_{t}u=D_{u}\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}u-uv^{2}+F(1-u)\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\partial_{t}v=D_{v}\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}v+uv^{2}-(F+k)v\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in $\mathcal{S}$},

are affected by Robin-type boundary conditions

\partial_{n}u+\kappa u=0,\partial_{n}v+\kappa v=0
on
\partial\mathcal{S}
. The Robin condition represents leakage of the concentrations
u
and
v
through the boundary, controlled by the leakage parameter
\kappa>0
.
We choose
\mathcal{S}
to be a Möbius strip and set the parameters to
F=0.010
,
k=0.042
,
D_{u}=8\times 10^{-5}
, and
D_{v}=0.4D_{u}
.
Figure
3
shows that the system exhibits weak patterns that dissipate in finite time under no-flux conditions (
\kappa=0
; homogeneous Neumann condition),
whereas the case
\kappa=10
induces chaotic and persistent spatiotemporal patterns.

u

\kappa=0
(homogeneous Neumann condition), with total simulation time

. Bottom row:
\kappa=10
,

.

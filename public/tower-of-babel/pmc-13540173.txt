Unraveling the drug resistance web in IBD: The gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics axis
Authors: Qing Li, Mingrong Cheng, Xiaoyue Wang, Weiwei Li, Feng Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDrug resistance in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) precision therapy remains a critical barrier to clinical outcomes, with traditional studies focusing on single molecules or isolated pathways but failing to systematically dissect the bidirectional, context-dependent dynamic crosstalk among gut microbiota, immunity, and epigenetic modifications. This review dissects resistance mechanisms of key biologics (e.g., antitumor necrosis factor-α agents, vedolizumab) and small-molecule drugs (e.g., janus kinase inhibitors), proposing and validating the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model”—gut microbiota dysbiosis as the initiator, immune dysregulation as the amplifier, and epigenetic maintenance as the stabilizer, with a bidirectional feedback loop sustaining resistance. It identifies the interaction network as the central regulatory axis, outlining a cascade where altered microbiota composition, signature metabolites (e.g., short-chain fatty acids), epigenetic modification (e.g., acetylation), and Th17/Treg imbalance may collectively contribute to the emergence of drug-resistant phenotypes. Four distinct subtypes (immunogenic, metabolic, epigenetic, barrier-deficient) are defined, with targeted strategies: precise microbiota regulation (e.g., fecal microbiota transplantation), immunity-epigenetics intervention (e.g., histone deacetylase inhibitors), and synergistic schemes that could partially rescue resistant clinical presentations. It also discusses multi-omics biomarkers for early prediction and formulation translation challenges, emphasizing cutting-edge technologies (single-cell multi-omics, organoid-microbiota co-cultures) and interdisciplinary collaboration. This review provides a comprehensive framework for overcoming IBD drug resistance and advancing personalized therapies.

Introduction

The treatment of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) has evolved from traditional anti-inflammatory therapy (e.g., 5-aminosalicylic acid, glucocorticoids) to the era of precision targeted therapy. The development and application of biologics and small-molecule targeted drugs have significantly improved patient prognosis. Anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α agents (e.g., infliximab, adalimumab), as the first approved biologics, inhibit the inflammatory cascade by neutralizing TNF-α, and clinical studies have shown that they can achieve clinical remission in 40%–60% of patients with moderate-to-severe IBD. 1 , 2 Anti-interleukin(IL)-23/IL-17 axis agents (e.g., ustekinumab, guselkumab) target the T helper(Th)17 cell-mediated inflammatory pathway and still yield a clinical response rate of 30%–50% in patients with anti-TNF-α resistance. 3 , 4 Vedolizumab, a gut-selective α4β7 integrin inhibitor, reduces mucosal inflammation by blocking T cell homing to the gut, with a clinical remission rate of 40%–50% in patients with ulcerative colitis (UC). 5 , 6 Small-molecule targeted drugs such as Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib) regulate multiple inflammatory factors by inhibiting the JAK/signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway, and the clinical remission rate of tofacitinib in the treatment of moderate-to-severe UC is 30%–40%. 7 , 8 These precision therapies not only significantly reduce the surgical rate (e.g., anti-TNF-α agents reduce the Crohn's disease (CD) surgical rate by 20%–30%) but also improve patients' quality of life by achieving mucosal healing (MH). For example, the MH rate of vedolizumab can reach 30%–40% after 16 weeks of treatment. 2 The core value of precision therapy lies in achieving individualized intervention through the “treatment-target” strategy. For instance, therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) can optimize medication regimens by detecting serum trough drug concentrations and anti-drug antibody (ADA) levels. Prospective studies have shown that the active TDM strategy can increase the sustained response rate of anti-TNF-α agents by 15%–20%. 2 In addition, combination therapy regimens (e.g., ustekinumab combined with infliximab) have shown a clinical remission rate of 60%–70% in the combination-UC trial, which is significantly higher than monotherapy. 9 These advances mark that IBD treatment has entered the era of “precision and individualization”, but its clinical value is still limited by drug resistance.

Drug resistance in IBD precision therapy can be divided into primary nonresponse (PNR, no response within 8–12 weeks of initial treatment) and secondary loss of response (LOR, loss of response within 6–12 months after initial response). The primary resistance rate of anti-TNF-α agents is 20%–30%, and the secondary resistance rate increases by 10%–15% annually; 10 , 11 the primary resistance rate of anti-IL-23/IL-17 axis agents is 15%–25%, and the secondary resistance rate is 5%–10% annually; 3 , 4 the primary resistance rate of vedolizumab is 20%–30%, and the secondary resistance rate is 8%–12% annually; 5 , 6 the primary resistance rate of JAK inhibitors is 15%–25%, and the secondary resistance rate is 6%–10% annually. 7 , 8 Drug resistance is associated with elevated odds of subsequent treatment failure in IBD patients. (the subsequent treatment failure rate of patients with anti-TNF-α resistance reaches 40%–50%), an elevated risk of disease progression (e.g., the incidence of intestinal stricture in CD patients increases by 20%–30%), and a significantly higher risk of complications (e.g., the incidence of colorectal cancer in UC patients increases by 1.5–2 times). 12 , 13 Current clinical strategies for addressing drug resistance have obvious limitations: blind switching of drugs (e.g., switching from anti-TNF-α agents to anti-IL-23 agents) only yields a clinical response rate of 20%–30%; 4 dose escalation (e.g., increasing the dose of infliximab from 5 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg) is only effective in 30%–40% of patients with low ADA levels; 11 combination with immunomodulators (e.g., azathioprine) can reduce the incidence of ADA (from 29.8% to 5.7%) while simultaneously elevating infectious risk, such as a 2–3-fold higher incidence of herpes zoster. 2 , 14 These strategies fail to fundamentally address the core mechanisms of drug resistance, highlighting the urgency of exploring new intervention paradigms.

This review focuses on two core scientific questions: (1) How does the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network regulate the drug resistance process of IBD precision therapy through the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model”? (2) Based on this model and drug resistance subtype classification, can a new targeted intervention paradigm be constructed to overcome the drug resistance dilemma? The logical framework of the review is as follows: first, dissect the resistance mechanisms of IBD precision therapy (biologics and small-molecule targeted drugs) and clarify the limitations of existing studies; second, systematically elaborate the core logic and evidence support of the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” and propose precise subtyping criteria for drug resistance based on the model; third, reveal the role of the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network as the core regulatory axis of drug resistance, including the crosstalk between gut microbiota and immunity, the regulation of gut microbiota-immunity crosstalk by epigenetics, and the dynamic interaction model of the three; fourth, propose strategies to overcome drug resistance based on the model and subtyping, covering precise regulation of gut microbiota, targeted intervention of immunity-epigenetics crosstalk, and synergistic intervention schemes of the three; finally, discuss the prospects (biomarker development, translation of novel formulations) and challenges of clinical translation, and look forward to the role of cutting-edge technologies and interdisciplinary collaboration in promoting the implementation of the new paradigm. Through this framework, this review aims to provide a systematic framework for the mechanism research and clinical intervention of drug resistance in IBD precision therapy.

Drug resistance mechanisms of IBD precision therapy

As a chronic non-specific intestinal inflammatory disease, IBD mainly includes UC and CD, and its treatment has entered the era of precision targeting. However, the drug resistance of precision therapies such as biologics and small-molecule inhibitors has become increasingly prominent, becoming a key bottleneck restricting efficacy.

Main types and clinical characteristics of drug resistance in IBD precision therapy

Drug resistance in IBD precision therapy is divided into PNR and secondary LOR, with significant differences in the incidence and clinical characteristics of drug resistance among different drugs. Anti-TNF-α drugs (infliximab, adalimumab) are the most common type of drug resistance. Hungarian real-world data show that their PNR rate is 11.6% and LOR rate is 36.5%, and the dose escalation rate of adalimumab (19.8%) is higher than that of infliximab (10.9%) with an earlier adjustment time; follow-up of Polish CD patients shows that the 6-month remission rate of this type of drug is 73.08%, which decreases to 40.38% at 12 months, and 38.46% of patients need to restart treatment due to recurrence. 15 , 16 Among non-TNF target biologics, when vedolizumab is used to treat UC, the high expression of exosomal α4β7 integrin in patients with previous anti-TNF-α treatment enhances drug sequestration capacity and reduces bioavailability; long-term data on ustekinumab in the treatment of CD show that high monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1) expression and previous biologic exposure are independent risk factors for long-term drug resistance, and the 140-week sustained remission rate of treatment-naive patients with low MCP-1 expression is 100%. 17 , 18 Small-molecule JAK inhibitors still carry the risk of drug resistance. Tofacitinib is used to treat moderate-to-severe UC with multiple biologic resistance, and 50% of patients with a history of using more than 2 previous biologics discontinue treatment at 12 months, presumably due to drug resistance; upadacitinib can achieve remission in pediatric UC patients after tofacitinib failure, suggesting that target specificity affects the response to drug resistance. 19 , 20 These differences are influenced by previous treatment history, biomarker expression, etc., highlighting the importance of drug resistance subtyping and individualized assessment.

Core drug resistance mechanisms of IBD precision therapy

Drug resistance in IBD precision therapy is the result of the synergistic effect of multiple links such as immune abnormalities and target disorders, and the mechanisms are interrelated and amplified. Immunity-related mechanisms are the core driving force and a key entry point for dissecting the essence of drug resistance.

(1) Immunity-related drug resistance mechanisms

(1) Immunity-related drug resistance mechanisms

Immunity-related drug resistance mechanisms

Abnormal function and subtype imbalance of immune cells are the core driving factors of IBD drug resistance, among which the imbalance of CD4+ T-cell subsets plays a key role. For example, First, in the TNBS-induced CD-like colitis model, transmural granulomatous inflammation disrupts Th1/Th2 and Th17/Treg immune homeostasis, and pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-6 and IL-17A are oversecreted. Notably, TNBS administration induces transmural intestinal inflammation with granuloma formation, which recapitulates key pathological features of Crohn's disease rather than superficial ulceration typical of UC. Although some traditional Chinese medicine compounds (e.g., Huanglian Ejiao Decoction) can regulate this balance, they do not target drug resistance-related targets such as sustained STAT3 activation and IL-6 receptor mutation. Furthermore, long-term use of anti-TNF-α therapy may partially drive compensatory elevation of intestinal IL-17 and interferon (IFN)-γ in intestinal tissues, suggesting compensatory activation of cytokine pathways, which is more significant in pediatric CD patients; 21 , 22 Moreover, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-22, IL-1β also accumulate in intestinal tissue, multiple receptors including IL-23R, STAT3, TLR4 continuously activate downstream inflammation without clear sequential relationship. In turn, immunogenicity-mediated drug resistance is a unique mechanism of biologics. The incidence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAbs) to anti-TNF-α drugs is related to the type of drug (infliximab 4.5% vs. adalimumab 0.2%, up to 21.4% in combination therapy), 23 and antibiotic classes (cephalosporins and penicillin-β-lactamase inhibitors increase the risk, while macrolides and fluoroquinolones decrease the risk) 24 and host age (the incidence of ADAbs in elderly patients is 34.1%, more than twice that of young patients [16.1%]) also affect the production of ADAbs. 25 Active TDM can reduce this risk (adjusted hazard ratio = 0.36). 23-25 Importantly, readers can reference Table 2 to distinguish immunogenic resistance from other resistance subtypes defined by distinct core molecular signatures. It can be seen that immunity-related drug resistance mechanisms involve both the disorder of cell subsets and cytokine networks, as well as the immunogenicity of drugs and the host genetic background, requiring multi-dimensional integrated analysis to formulate targeted intervention strategies.

(2) Target and signaling pathway abnormality mechanisms

(2) Target and signaling pathway abnormality mechanisms

Target and signaling pathway abnormality mechanisms

This module covers two tiers of molecular defects underlying resistance: altered drug target structure/expression and persistent hyperactivation of downstream inflammatory signaling cascades.

First, target mutation or aberrant protein expression may potentially mediate impaired drug-target binding and reduced therapeutic occupancy. For example, the drug resistance of vedolizumab is related to α4β7 integrin mutation, abnormal expression of mucosal addressin cell adhesion molecule-1 (MAdCAM-1), and activation of alternative homing pathways (e.g., α4β1-vascular cell adhesion molecule-1). Some patients also have abnormal function of gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), resulting in the inability of drugs to effectively block the homing of naive B/T cells to the gut. The drug resistance of IL-23/IL-17 axis-targeted therapy is related to IL-23R mutation and compensatory activation of the IL-17 signaling pathway. Moreover, anti-IL-17 drugs may contribute to aggravated or induced IBD, and anti-IL-23 drugs are ineffective for axial spondyloarthritis, suggesting the tissue specificity of pathway activation. 26 , 27

Furthermore, sustained activation of signaling pathways is an important downstream mechanism of drug resistance. Sustained activation of the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathway has been found in various drug resistance models. Although Canna x generalis rhizome ethanol extract can downregulate the expression of toll-like receptor (TLR) 4 and NF-κB to improve UC, it does not target drug resistance-related targets such as mutations leading to sustained NF-κB activation. Abnormalities in the JAK-STAT pathway may contribute to tofacitinib resistance. In vitro evidence indicates that TNF-α and IFN-γ synergistically trigger intestinal epithelial cell apoptosis via the CASP8-JAK1/2-STAT1 module; however, this cascade still lacks systematic validation in in vivo drug-resistant IBD animal models, which can be blocked by JAK inhibitors but not verified in drug resistance models). Excessive activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway is also involved in drug resistance. Quzhou Aurantii Fructus flavonoids can inhibit this pathway to improve UC but not target drug resistance-related abnormalities such as PI3K/AKT mutations. 28-30 It can be seen that target and signaling pathway abnormalities are the direct molecular basis of drug resistance, and their diversity and complexity require intervention strategies to have target specificity and pathway synergy.

(3) Gut microbiota-mediated drug resistance mechanisms

(3) Gut microbiota-mediated drug resistance mechanisms

Gut microbiota-mediated drug resistance mechanisms

Intestinal dysbiosis participates in the formation of drug resistance through multiple pathways. Patients with IBD drug resistance generally have characteristics of reduced microbiota diversity, increased abundance of pathogenic bacteria, and decreased abundance of beneficial bacteria. First, UC patients have increased intestinal abundance of Enterococcus and the Escherichia/Shigella complex and decreased Bacteroides, and this microbiota change is closely related to the activation of the IL-23/IL-17 axis. In patients with anti-TNF-α drug resistance, abnormal metabolites of intestinal microbiota may also affect drug metabolism. Cefuroxime pretreatment can significantly increase ADA levels in mice, while the azithromycin group decreases them, and germ-free mice do not produce ADA, confirming the microbiota-mediated immunogenicity regulation. 23 , 31

Furthermore, the drug resistance regulatory effect of probiotics and their metabolites has not been fully utilized. Lactobacillus plantarum HNU082 (Lp082) can improve UC by enriching beneficial bacteria such as Akkermansia muciniphila and promoting the production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), but its reversal effect on microbiota-mediated drug resistance has not been verified in drug resistance models. As a key metabolite of microbiota, rare mutations in SCFA receptors GPR41/GPR43 may potentially blunt the protective signaling of short-chain fatty acids, which could partially limit the efficacy of metabolite supplementation interventions; however, large-scale clinical cohorts confirming the prevalence and functional impact of such mutations in drug-resistant IBD patients remain lacking. Notably, this putative genetic variant effect must be strictly separated from the well-validated population-level phenotype of universally reduced luminal SCFA concentrations observed across most drug-resistant IBD cohorts, the latter representing a consistent, clinically confirmed driver of impaired mucosal anti-inflammatory signaling. Although valeric acid can improve UC by upregulating this receptor, it does not target drug resistance-related targets such as receptor mutations. 32 , 33 To date, direct causal links between GPR41/GPR43 rare variants and clinical drug resistance outcomes have not been prospectively validated in human trials, representing a critical outstanding research gap that prevents definitive conclusions on their clinical relevance. It can be seen that as an initiating factor of drug resistance, abnormalities in the structure and metabolic function of intestinal microbiota can regulate the drug resistance process through multiple pathways. Targeted intervention of microbiota needs to take into account the synergy of microbiota colonization, metabolite regulation, and host immune response.

(4) Intestinal barrier function defect mechanisms

(4) Intestinal barrier function defect mechanisms

Intestinal barrier function defect mechanisms

Intestinal barrier damage exacerbates the formation of a drug-resistant microenvironment. Patients with IBD drug resistance generally have reduced expression of intestinal epithelial tight junction proteins (zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), Occludin, Claudin-1) and increased intestinal permeability, which may partially drive pathogenic bacterial translocation and persistent inflammation. Fully unregulated bacterial translocation, massive release of LPS, simultaneous activation of TLR2/4/6, cascading release of dozens of interleukins and chemokines, create an unbreakable inflammatory loop that completely reverses treatment response. First, Quinoa bran soluble dietary fiber (QBSDF) and Tetrastigma hemsleyanum polysaccharides soluble dietary fiber indirectly facilitate intestinal barrier restoration by boosting the production of microbiota-derived SCFAs, which further modulate epithelial tight junction protein expression and suppress mucosal inflammation. This functional improvement follows a clear mechanistic chain: soluble fiber → microbiota fermentation → SCFA accumulation → epigenetic and immune regulation → auxiliary barrier protection. Importantly, soluble fiber merely provides adjunct protective effects and cannot reverse irreversible epithelial barrier damage caused by long-term inflammatory erosion. Notably, soluble fiber only provides adjunct barrier protection rather than complete repair, and it cannot reverse irreversible epithelial barrier damage induced by long-term severe inflammatory erosion, meaning its therapeutic potency is insufficient to resolve drug resistance originating from permanently compromised mucosal structure.

Furthermore, the expression of intestinal tight junction proteins is significantly increased in responders to vedolizumab treatment, but not in non-responders, suggesting that barrier function repair is closely related to drug resistance reversal; 26 , 34 , 35 at the same time, abnormalities in mucosal barrier-related molecules are also involved in drug resistance. Mucin 2 (Muc2) deficiency leads to the loss of the intestinal mucin layer, allowing direct contact between microbiota and epithelial cells and exacerbating inflammation and drug resistance. Although supplementation with L-fucose can partially restore microbiota balance, it cannot improve barrier defect-related drug resistance. Abnormal apoptosis of intestinal epithelial cells also plays a role in drug resistance. Tocilizumab can improve UC by inhibiting caspase-3 activity but does not target drug resistance caused by abnormal activation of the apoptotic pathway. 36 , 37 It can be seen that intestinal barrier function defect is an important link in the formation of a drug-resistant microenvironment, and its repair requires multi-dimensional intervention combining microbiota regulation, inflammation inhibition, and epithelial cell function repair.

(5) Other drug resistance mechanisms

(5) Other drug resistance mechanisms

Other drug resistance mechanisms

Abnormal drug metabolism and delivery affect the exertion of efficacy. First, in obese IBD patients using adalimumab, body mass index (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2) is an independent predictor of loss of treatment response (LOR) (HR = 3.2), which may be related to increased drug clearance and decreased trough concentration, while LOR of infliximab is not related to BMI because it adopts weight-dependent intravenous administration. Although nano-carrier delivery systems (e.g., adalimumab-modified nanoparticles) can improve drug stability and targeting, they do not target drug resistance-related factors such as abnormal activity of drug-metabolizing enzymes. 38 , 39 Furthermore, exosome-mediated drug sequestration is a novel drug resistance mechanism. During vedolizumab treatment, circulating exosomes express α4β7 integrin, which can compete with CD4 + T cells for binding to drugs, leading to drug sequestration. This phenomenon is more significant in patients with previous anti-TNF-α treatment, becoming an important cause of vedolizumab resistance. 18 It can be seen that novel drug resistance mechanisms such as abnormal drug metabolism, delivery, and exosome-mediated drug sequestration provide new entry points for optimizing drug formulation design and administration strategies.

Limitations of existing intervention strategies

Although significant progress has been made in IBD precision targeted therapy, drug resistance remains a bottleneck for efficacy. Existing intervention strategies fail to fundamentally overcome drug resistance due to not matching the complex pathophysiological characteristics of drug resistance, not focusing on core targets and subtypes based on the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model”, and lacking targeting.

(1) Lack of drug resistance targeting in mechanism research

(1) Lack of drug resistance targeting in mechanism research

Lack of drug resistance targeting in mechanism research

Most existing intervention strategies do not focus on core drug resistance targets. For example, probiotics such as Lactiplantibacillus plantarum 1201 and Saccharomyces boulardii can regulate intestinal microbiota and promote SCFAs production to improve IBD, but do not target drug resistance-related targets such as GPR43 mutations and TNF-α receptor mutations. Traditional Chinese medicine compounds such as Baitouweng Decoction and Ganluyin exert their effects by regulating microbiota and inhibiting inflammatory pathways, but do not involve key drug resistance abnormalities such as IL-6 receptor mutations and sustained STAT3 activation. Even targeted drugs such as tofacitinib, although effective in patients with multiple biologic resistance, have not clearly clarified their regulatory effects on drug resistance-related targets such as JAK pathway mutations. 20 , 40-44

Furthermore, the lack of verification in drug resistance models is widespread. Most studies adopt conventional IBD models. For example, soybean peptide Lunasin and eucalyptus leaf extract active component oenothein B only show anti-inflammatory effects in ordinary IBD models, lacking intervention data in drug resistance models. Some clinical studies also do not include drug-resistant patients. For example, a study on adalimumab in the treatment of Korean UC patients confirmed that previous anti-TNF-α treatment history does not affect efficacy, but did not separately analyze the response differences in drug-resistant patients. 45-47 It can be seen that the core limitation of existing intervention strategies lies in the lack of focus on drug resistance-specific targets, models, and subtype characteristics. Future intervention research design needs to strengthen drug resistance mechanism orientation.

(2) Obvious limitations in clinical application

(2) Obvious limitations in clinical application

Obvious limitations in clinical application

This subsection separates two clinical translational barriers: unoptimized combination therapy regimens and immature multi-omics monitoring workflows for early resistance prediction. First, combination therapy strategies lack precise optimization. Although the combination of immunomodulators and anti-TNF-α drugs can reduce the risk of ADAbs production, there is no significant difference in clinical remission rate, drug concentration, and ADAbs incidence between low-dose azathioprine (<2 mg/kg/d) and standard dose (2–2.5 mg/kg/d). Moreover, the combination medication schemes for different drug resistance subtypes have not been clarified. The synergistic effects of adjuvant interventions such as probiotics and dietary fiber with precision therapy drugs have not been fully explored (e.g., QBSDF has not explored the synergistic effect with drug-resistant drugs). 34 , 48

Furthermore, the treatment monitoring and drug resistance early warning system are not perfect. Although active TDM can improve the efficacy of anti-TNF-α drugs and reduce drug resistance, its application value in drugs such as vedolizumab and ustekinumab has not been clarified. Most existing biomarkers can only predict treatment response (e.g., serum nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase and nicotinic acid phosphoribosyltransferase can predict adalimumab response), but cannot distinguish between primary and secondary drug resistance and different drug resistance subtypes, nor evaluate the effect of treatment adjustment based on biomarkers on drug resistance reversal. Moreover, most biomarkers need to be obtained through invasive methods such as colon biopsy, limiting their routine clinical application. 49-51 It can be seen that the limitations of clinical application focus on the insufficient precision of combination therapy and the imperfection of the monitoring system. It is necessary to establish an individualized treatment monitoring scheme based on drug resistance subtypes and biomarkers.

(3) Insufficient evaluation of long-term safety and drug resistance risk

(3) Insufficient evaluation of long-term safety and drug resistance risk

Insufficient evaluation of long-term safety and drug resistance risk

This final sub-paragraph addresses gaps in long-term cohort follow-up and limited evidence for pediatric, elderly, and pregnant resistant IBD populations. First, the drug resistance risk of long-term use has not been fully followed up. Most studies have a short follow-up period. For example, a 6-month follow-up of tofacitinib in the treatment of UC shows a significant steroid-sparing effect, but some patients discontinue treatment due to drug resistance after 12 months. The 16-week response rate of ustekinumab in the treatment of CD is 58%, but the long-term drug resistance incidence and inducements are not clear. Moreover, long-term use may induce adaptive drug resistance (e.g., long-term oral L-theanine may lead to drug resistance of intestinal microbiota or immune cells), and relevant studies have not been carried out. 20 , 52 , 53

Furthermore, drug resistance research in special populations is insufficient. The drug resistance mechanism of pediatric IBD patients may be different from that of adults, but most existing studies focus on adults, and data on the incidence of ADAbs and drug resistance target mutation characteristics in their anti-TNF-α treatment are limited. Elderly patients have a higher drug resistance risk due to immune aging and more comorbidities, but targeted optimization strategies are lacking. The drug resistance management of pregnant and lactating patients is even lacking relevant data. Only case reports show that tofacitinib can be safely used for maintenance treatment of drug-resistant UC patients during pregnancy, and the long-term drug resistance risk is unknown. 24 , 54-56 It can be seen that the lack of research on long-term safety and drug resistance in special populations restricts the wide application of precision therapy in the real world. It is necessary to carry out clinical studies with longer cycles and more representative populations.

Gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network

The precision treatment of IBD has entered the era of targeting, but drug resistance restricts efficacy. Studies have confirmed that intestinal microbiota, the immune system, and epigenetic modifications regulate drug resistance through an interaction network, and the core mechanism can be analyzed through the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model”.

Gut microbiota-immunity crosstalk: Initiation and amplification mechanisms of IBD drug resistance

Intestinal microbiota dysbiosis is an important initiating factor of IBD drug resistance, which directly affects drug sensitivity by regulating immune cell function, inflammatory factor network, and intestinal barrier integrity. The crosstalk between gut microbiota and immunity is not a unidirectional regulatory cascade, but a tightly coupled bidirectional feedback circuit. Commensal microorganisms shape the differentiation and functional homeostasis of intestinal immune cells via structural components and secreted metabolites, while immune-derived inflammatory mediators in turn remodel intestinal niche conditions to alter microbiota composition and abundance, eventually forming self-sustaining vicious cycles that lock in drug-resistant inflammatory phenotypes.

(1) Microbiota structure remodeling mediates abnormal immune tolerance

(1) Microbiota structure remodeling mediates abnormal immune tolerance

Microbiota structure remodeling mediates abnormal immune tolerance

First, patients with drug-resistant IBD generally have characteristics of reduced microbiota diversity, enriched pathogenic bacteria, and decreased beneficial bacteria. The feces of patients with anti-TNF-α drug resistance have significantly higher abundance of pathogenic bacteria such as Enterococcaceae and Escherichia/Shigella complex than responders, and cannot achieve beneficial bacteria enrichment during treatment. Distinct microbial subsets exert diametrically opposite effects on intestinal immune tolerance. Anti-inflammatory beneficial commensals including Akkermansia muciniphila and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii dominate immune homeostasis maintenance: their secreted metabolites facilitate naive CD4+ T-cell differentiation toward anti-inflammatory Treg subsets, upregulate IL-10 secretion from macrophages, and restrain excessive pro-inflammatory signaling to establish steady-state immune tolerance, which substantially elevates therapeutic response to biologics. Conversely, pathobionts represented by adherent-invasive Escherichia coli (AIEC) and Enterococcaceae break this tolerogenic state. AIEC invades intestinal epithelial cells and activates the TLR4/NF-κB signaling cascade, while Enterococcaceae overgrowth amplifies mucosal innate immune stimulation; both pathobiont groups jointly drive robust Th17 polarization and overproduction of IL-17A and IL-6, tipping the Th17/Treg balance toward pro-inflammatory dominance and laying the foundational immune disorder for drug resistance. 66

Furthermore, the decreased abundance of beneficial bacteria producing SCFAs such as Akkermansia muciniphila and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii will weaken their inhibitory effect on macrophage TNF-α secretion and exacerbate inflammatory responses; 66 , 67 probiotic intervention can improve drug resistance by remodeling the microbiota structure. Lactobacillus plantarum Lp082 can enrich beneficial bacteria such as Akkermansia muciniphila and Parabacteroides distasonis, inhibit the growth of pathogenic bacteria Helicobacter hepaticus, and simultaneously promote SCFAs production, activate the GPR43 receptor of intestinal epithelial cells, inhibit the NF-κB pathway, and enhance the sensitivity to anti-TNF-α drugs. This protective axis relies on intact receptor signaling; rare germline variants in GPR41/GPR43 could theoretically weaken this therapeutic benefit, yet population-wide data proving consistent clinical impairment from these mutations in drug-resistant IBD is absent. Engineered probiotic ectonucleotide pyrophosphatase/phosphodiesterase 3 (ENPP3) can continuously produce propionate in the gut by heterologously expressing propionate synthesis pathway genes, restore the function of anti-inflammatory macrophages, and reverse some drug-resistant phenotypes. 32 , 68 It can be seen that microbiota structure remodeling directly affects drug resistance formation by regulating immune tolerance, and probiotics and engineered probiotics provide feasible tools for targeting this link, but attention should be paid to strain specificity and colonization efficiency. After microbial communities polarize immune cells toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes, the resulting cytokine storm initiates reciprocal regulation on gut microbiota to close the bidirectional feedback loop, as elaborated in the following metabolite-mediated immune regulatory subsection.

(2) Microbiota metabolites regulate immune inflammatory pathways

(2) Microbiota metabolites regulate immune inflammatory pathways

Microbiota metabolites regulate immune inflammatory pathways

Microbiota metabolites are key messengers connecting microbiota and immunity, and their abnormalities are associated with immune pathway disorders and induce drug resistance. First, as core metabolites, SCFAs can exert anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting HDAC activity under steady-state conditions, but in the presence of TLR agonists such as lipopolysaccharide, butyrate and propionate can instead activate the NOD-like receptor family, pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome of macrophages, promote IL-1β release, and amplify inflammatory responses. 69 The butyrate level of IBD patients with anemia is significantly reduced, leading to downregulated expression of macrophage iron transporter (ferroportin (FPN)), and iron accumulation mediates increased TNF-α secretion, reducing the efficacy of anti-TNF-α treatment. 70 Isoallolithocholic acid (IsoalloLCA) synthesized by Bacteroidota can promote Foxp3 transcription and induce Treg cell differentiation by activating the nuclear hormone receptor nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group A member 1 (NR4A1), enhancing immune tolerance. 71 Notably, AIEC can degrade intestinal propionate through the methylcitrate pathway, offsetting its anti-inflammatory effect and weakening drug efficacy; 66 the regulation of immune inflammatory pathways by microbiota metabolites is dualistic, and their effects depend on the state of the intestinal microenvironment, which provides important references for precise targeted metabolite intervention of drug resistance.

In turn, sustained secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines shaped by dysregulated microbial metabolites subsequently reshapes gut microbiota composition to reinforce dysbiosis, forming a self-amplifying vicious cycle central to persistent drug resistance. High levels of Th17-derived IL-17A and lymphocyte-secreted IFN-γ alter intestinal mucus layer thickness, luminal nutrient availability, and epithelial metabolic microenvironment to selectively suppress the colonization and proliferation of anti-inflammatory commensals like Akkermansia muciniphila. In vitro and preclinical evidence confirms that IL-17A directly inhibits the growth of Akkermansia by inducing pathobiont-sourced sialidase activity that degrades the mucosal glycans supporting beneficial bacterial survival; 65 this immune-derived suppression of protective microbes further depletes SCFA pools and amplifies pathobiont overgrowth, locking the gut into a drug-resistant inflammatory state that cannot be reversed by monotherapies targeting only immune cytokines.

(3) Microbiota-mediated intestinal barrier damage exacerbates drug resistance

(3) Microbiota-mediated intestinal barrier damage exacerbates drug resistance

Microbiota-mediated intestinal barrier damage exacerbates drug resistance

The destruction of intestinal barrier integrity is an important pathological basis of IBD drug resistance, and microbiota dysbiosis is a key factor leading to barrier damage. Drug-resistant patients have significantly reduced expression of intestinal tight junction proteins (ZO-1, Occludin, Claudin-1) and increased intestinal permeability, leading to pathogenic bacterial translocation and persistent inflammation. 34 , 35 The expression of intestinal tight junction proteins is significantly increased in responders to vedolizumab treatment, but not in non-responders. 26 Microbiota affects drug resistance by regulating the expression of barrier-related genes. Ginseng-derived exosome-like nanoparticles can increase the abundance of beneficial bacteria such as Lactobacillus and Alistipes, upregulate the expression of ZO-1 and Occludin, repair the intestinal barrier, and improve anti-TNF-α drug resistance. 72 Muc2 deficiency leads to the loss of the intestinal mucin layer, and direct contact between microbiota and epithelial cells exacerbates inflammation and drug resistance. Although supplementation with L-fucose can partially restore microbiota balance, it cannot improve barrier defect-related drug resistance; 37 microbiota-mediated intestinal barrier damage is an important link in drug resistance amplification. Intervention needs to take into account both microbiota balance and barrier function repair to form a synergistic effect. This auxiliary barrier-improving property shared by soluble fibers relies entirely on microbial fermentation metabolites, and soluble fiber itself lacks direct epithelial regenerative capacity, which explains its limited efficacy against advanced, irreversible mucosal injury in drug-resistant IBD.

Epigenetic modifications: Regulatory hub of gut microbiota-immunity crosstalk

Epigenetic modifications (DNA methylation, histone modifications, non-coding RNA regulation) act as the core regulatory hub of the gut microbiota-immunity interaction network by regulating the expression of immune-related genes, directly affecting the IBD drug-resistant phenotype.

(1) Histone modifications mediate gut microbiota-immunity signal transduction

(1) Histone modifications mediate gut microbiota-immunity signal transduction

Histone modifications mediate gut microbiota-immunity signal transduction

First, the balance of histone acetylation/deacetylation plays a key role in drug resistance regulation. As HDAC inhibitors, SCFAs can affect the expression of immune genes by regulating histone acetylation. Butyrate inhibits HDAC8 activity, blocks NF-κB p65 phosphorylation, enhances the expression of Slc26a3 and tight junction proteins, repairs the intestinal barrier, and improves drug resistance. 73 Angelicin can activate GPR43 and GPR109A receptors through SCFAs, promote Sp1 phosphorylation, competitively inhibit the binding of HDAC1 to hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α), increase the acetylation level of HIF-1α, and repair the intestinal mucosal barrier. 74

Furthermore, abnormal expression of histone acetyltransferases and HDAC directly leads to drug resistance. The expression of HDAC1, HDAC2, and HDAC7 is increased in fibroblasts of stricturing CD, and the level of histone H3 lysine 27 acetylation (H3K27ac) is decreased. HDAC inhibitor valproic acid (VPA) can restore the level of H3K27ac, inhibit collagen gene transcription, and reverse fibrosis-related drug resistance. 75 p300 inhibitor C646 can block NLRP3 acetylation, inhibit the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, and improve anti-TNF-α drug resistance; 76 histone modifications, as key intermediaries between microbiota metabolites and immune signals, their abnormalities are important mechanisms for drug resistance maintenance, and HDAC inhibitors and other epigenetic modulators have broad application prospects.

(2) DNA methylation regulates immune cell functional plasticity

(2) DNA methylation regulates immune cell functional plasticity

DNA methylation regulates immune cell functional plasticity

Abnormal DNA methylation participates in drug resistance formation by regulating immune cell differentiation and function. DNA methyltransferase inhibitor (DNMTi) decitabine can induce specific demethylation of the EBI3 gene, synergistically with TNFα to significantly upregulate EBI3 expression, promote IL-35 formation, enhance anti-inflammatory effects, and reverse partial drug resistance. 77 HSP90β can enhance Th17 cell glycolysis, reduce lactic acid production, affect the methylation of the Foxp3 CNS2 region through the “lactic acid-STAT5-TET2” cascade, inhibit the conversion of Th17 to Treg, and exacerbate drug resistance. 78 Microbiota metabolites can affect drug resistance by regulating DNA methylation. Butyrate enhances histone acetylation in the solute carrier family 40 (iron-regulated transporter), member 1 promoter region by inhibiting the binding of HDAC1 to Sp1, upregulates FPN expression, promotes macrophage iron export, breaks the anemia-inflammation vicious cycle, and enhances the efficacy of anti-TNF-α treatment; 70 DNA methylation participates in drug resistance by regulating immune cell functional plasticity, and its crosstalk with microbiota metabolites provides a new direction for the development of combined intervention strategies.

(3) Non-coding RNA-mediated drug resistance regulatory network

(3) Non-coding RNA-mediated drug resistance regulatory network

Non-coding RNA-mediated drug resistance regulatory network

miRNA and long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) have become important molecules for drug resistance regulation by targeting immune-related genes. miR-222-3p is highly expressed in the colonic mucosa of UC patients, directly binds to SOCS1 to inhibit its expression, activates the JAK2/STAT3 pathway, promotes the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors, downregulates vitamin D receptor expression, and aggravates drug resistance. 62 lncRNA TUG1 can relieve the inhibition of SOCS1 by targeting miR-142-5p, inhibit the activation of the TNF-α-mediated inflammatory signaling pathway, and improve drug resistance. 79 Microbiota can affect drug resistance by regulating the expression of non-coding RNAs. Epstein-Barr virus infection can upregulate the expression of pro-inflammatory miRNAs by activating TLR9-mediated immune response, exacerbating intestinal inflammation and drug resistance. 80 Baicalin can downregulate the expression of pro-inflammatory miRNAs and upregulate the expression of their target genes SOCS1 and forkhead box O3a by regulating intestinal microbiota, improving the sensitivity to anti-TNF-α drugs; 81 the non-coding RNA-mediated regulatory network is an important molecular carrier for gut microbiota-immunity crosstalk, and its targeted intervention provides a precise tool for drug resistance reversal.

Triple crosstalk of gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics

This subsection integrates three fully interconnected triple-feedback loops that lock in persistent resistant inflammation, with the complete integrated network visualized in Figure 3 to reduce text-based molecular complexity. The triple crosstalk loop formed by gut microbiota, immunity, and epigenetics is the core regulatory mechanism of IBD drug resistance, and its abnormalities will lead to the persistence and amplification of drug-resistant phenotypes (Figure 3).

Microbiota-immunity-epigenetics tripartite interaction network. Integrated schematic of the tripartite gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network mediating IBD drug resistance, highlighting three core crosstalk circuits and key molecular mediators driving metabolic, immune, and epigenetic aberrations.

(1) Gut microbiota-epigenetics-immune cell differentiation loop

(1) Gut microbiota-epigenetics-immune cell differentiation loop

Gut microbiota-epigenetics-immune cell differentiation loop

First, gut microbiota regulates immune cell differentiation through epigenetic modifications, directly affecting drug resistance. IsoalloLCA synthesized by Bacteroidota can increase the chromatin accessibility of the Foxp3 locus, create conditions for NR4A1 binding, promote the differentiation of naive CD4⁺T cells into Treg cells, and enhance immune tolerance. 71 AIEC infection might upregulate HDAC expression through the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, inhibit Foxp3 acetylation, reduce Treg cell production, and potentially mediate the development of drug resistance. 66

In turn, abnormal epigenetic modifications of immune cells will feedback affect the microbiota structure. The upregulated expression of HDAC1/3 in Th17 cells can inhibit the growth of beneficial bacteria and promote the enrichment of pathogenic bacteria by secreting pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-17A, forming a vicious cycle of “epigenetic abnormalities—immune dysregulation—gut microbiota dysbiosis”. 82 HDAC1/3 inhibitor RGFP109 can inhibit Th17 cell differentiation, downregulate IL-17A levels, restore microbiota balance, and reverse drug resistance; 82 this loop constitutes the core driving mechanism for the persistence of drug resistance. Breaking the loop requires simultaneous targeting of three links: microbiota remodeling, epigenetic modifications, and immune cell differentiation.

(2) Gut microbiota metabolites-epigenetics-inflammatory pathway loop

(2) Gut microbiota metabolites-epigenetics-inflammatory pathway loop

Gut microbiota metabolites-epigenetics-inflammatory pathway loop

First, gut microbiota metabolites regulate inflammatory pathways through epigenetic modifications, participating in drug resistance regulation. SCFAs can inhibit HDAC, change the modification pattern of histone H3K27ac, downregulate the transcriptional expression of cFLIP and IL-10, activate caspase-8, and then trigger the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, amplifying the pro-inflammatory response. 69 The decreased level of SCFAs in the feces of IBD patients leads to insufficient HDAC inhibition, sustained activation of the NF-κB pathway, and reduced drug sensitivity. 73

Furthermore, the activation of inflammatory pathways can affect gut microbiota-host crosstalk through epigenetic modifications. TNF-α and IFN-γ can synergistically induce intestinal epithelial cell death through the CASP8-JAK1/2-STAT1 module, activate the NF-κB pathway, promote the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors, further inhibit the growth of beneficial bacteria, and exacerbate metabolite disorders. 29 JAK inhibitors can block this pathway, restore microbiota balance and metabolite levels, and improve drug resistance; 43 as the initiating factor of the loop, the regulation of epigenetic modifications by gut microbiota metabolites is the key to the sustained activation of inflammatory pathways. The combined intervention of targeted metabolites and epigenetic enzymes is of great value.

(3) Intestinal barrier-gut microbiota-epigenetics crosstalk loop

(3) Intestinal barrier-gut microbiota-epigenetics crosstalk loop

Intestinal barrier-gut microbiota-epigenetics crosstalk loop

First, intestinal barrier damage promotes drug resistance by disrupting microbiota homeostasis and epigenetic balance. Drug-resistant patients have reduced expression of intestinal tight junction proteins and increased intestinal permeability, leading to pathogenic bacterial translocation, activating the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, promoting HDAC expression, inhibiting the transcription of tight junction protein genes, and forming a vicious cycle of “barrier damage—gut microbiota dysbiosis—epigenetic abnormalities”. 73

Furthermore, epigenetic modifications can improve microbiota balance and drug resistance by repairing the intestinal barrier. Vedolizumab can block the α4β7-MAdCAM-1 axis, inhibit the entry of naive B/T cells into GALT, induce GALT atrophy, reduce inflammatory cell infiltration, upregulate the expression of tight junction proteins, repair the intestinal barrier, and simultaneously restore microbiota balance, reversing partial drug resistance. 26 β-glucan-coated Lactobacillus plantarum (Lp@CGN) can release colon-targeted, remodel intestinal microbiota, promote SCFAs production, upregulate the expression of tight junction proteins through epigenetic modifications, and improve drug resistance; 83 this loop highlights the importance of the intestinal barrier as the interface for gut microbiota-epigenetics crosstalk, and barrier repair is a key entry point for breaking the loop and reversing drug resistance.

Strategies to overcome drug resistance based on the interaction network

Drug resistance in IBD precision therapy is a core clinical problem. Traditional single-target intervention cannot fundamentally reverse drug resistance due to neglecting the dynamic interaction among intestinal microbiota, immune system, and epigenetic modifications. The interaction network of the three is the core of drug resistance regulation. Based on the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” and drug resistance subtype classification, an integrated intervention new paradigm can be constructed.

Gut microbiota-targeted intervention

This translational section constructs a tiered integrated intervention framework aligned with the triple-loop model and four resistance subtypes, separating microbiota-targeted, immune-epigenetic, and multi-modal synergistic therapeutic modules (summarized in Figures 4 and 5).

(1) Microecological transplantation and reconstruction technology

(1) Microecological transplantation and reconstruction technology

Microecological transplantation and reconstruction technology

FMT, by completely transplanting healthy intestinal microbiota, has become a core intervention method for microbiota-mediated drug resistance. For UC patients with anti-TNFα treatment failure, FMT can significantly increase intestinal microbiota diversity, increase the abundance of beneficial bacteria such as Butyricicoccus and Faecalibacterium, reduce ADA levels, and make the trough concentration of infliximab detectable from undetectable. 84 Whole intestinal microbiota transplantation (WIMT) can more completely retain the functions of the healthy intestinal microecosystem compared with traditional FMT, enrich beneficial bacteria such as Bifidobacterium, and has a better anti-inflammatory effect. 85 The step-up FMT strategy, through the combined scheme of “FMT sequential hormone/anti-TNF-α antibody”, can make some anti-TNF-α non-responders re-tolerate the treatment. 86 The standardized fecal microbial community (capsule-based (c)FMT) cultured with brain heart infusion solves the problems of donor dependence and component heterogeneity of traditional FMT, can be mass-produced with stable efficacy, providing the possibility for large-scale application; 87 the core advantage of microecological transplantation technology lies in the rapid remodeling of intestinal microbiota homeostasis, but donor screening, standardized preparation, and colonization efficiency are still key issues to be optimized.

(2) Precise intervention of probiotics and synbiotics

(2) Precise intervention of probiotics and synbiotics

Precise intervention of probiotics and synbiotics

Functional probiotic strains can specifically improve drug-resistant phenotypes by colonizing the gut and regulating metabolism. Lactobacillus plantarum Lp082 can enrich beneficial bacteria such as Akkermansia muciniphila and Parabacteroides distasonis, inhibit the growth of pathogenic bacteria Helicobacter hepaticus, and simultaneously promote SCFAs production, activate the GPR43 receptor of intestinal epithelial cells, and enhance the sensitivity to anti-TNF-α drugs. 32 Genetically engineered probiotics further improve intervention precision. The engineered propionate-producing probiotic ENPP3 can continuously produce propionate in the gut by heterologously expressing propionate synthesis pathway genes, restore the function of anti-inflammatory macrophages, and could partially rescue some drug-resistant phenotypes. 68 Synbiotics strengthen the intervention effect through the synergistic effect of “probiotics + prebiotics”. The synbiotic derived from Thai traditional fermented tea (Lactobacillus pentosus A14-6 + xylooligosaccharide) can selectively promote probiotic colonization, upregulate IL-10 expression, and its local anti-inflammatory effect is superior to sulfasalazine; 88 the precise intervention of probiotics and synbiotics needs to select strains and prebiotic combinations based on patients' microbiota characteristics. Genetically engineered probiotics provide new tools for targeted metabolite regulation, but attention should be paid to their clinical safety and long-term colonization ability.

(3) Targeted supplementation of gut microbiota metabolites

(3) Targeted supplementation of gut microbiota metabolites

Targeted supplementation of gut microbiota metabolites

Gut microbiota metabolites are key messengers connecting microbiota, immunity, and epigenetics. Direct supplementation or regulation of their production could partially rescue drug resistance. High-concentration acetate (100 mM) can increase the transepithelial electrical resistance of intestinal epithelial cells in UC patients, upregulate the expression of hypoxia-inducible factor HIF1α and mucin Muc2, repair the intestinal barrier, and has no toxicity to intestinal epithelial cells. 89 Butyrate inhibits HDAC8 activity, blocks NF-κB p65 phosphorylation, enhances the expression of Slc26a3 and tight junction proteins, and improves drug resistance. 73 This therapeutic effect assumes functional GPR41/GPR43 signal transduction; rare receptor mutations may hypothetically diminish butyrate responsiveness, though robust clinical evidence to confirm this causal relationship in drug-resistant IBD populations is still insufficient. IsoalloLCA synthesized by Bacteroidota can activate the nuclear hormone receptor NR4A1, promote Foxp3 transcription, induce Treg cell differentiation, and enhance immune tolerance; 71 the targeted supplementation of gut microbiota metabolites can directly act on the core links of the interaction network, but their bioavailability, administration methods, and dose optimization are still the key to clinical translation.

Immunity-epigenetics crosstalk targeting

The dynamic crosstalk between immunity and epigenetics is the key to maintaining intestinal immune homeostasis. Its abnormal regulation is associated with the sustained activation of inflammatory pathways and the dysfunction of immune cells, thereby consolidating the drug-resistant phenotype. For this core crosstalk link, it is necessary to accurately target key molecules such as epigenetic modification enzymes and non-coding RNAs, as well as reshape immune cell functions, to block the abnormal transmission of core drug resistance pathways from the source.

(1) Epigenetic enzyme inhibitor intervention

(1) Epigenetic enzyme inhibitor intervention

Epigenetic enzyme inhibitor intervention

HDAC inhibitors have shown significant potential in drug resistance intervention. Vorinostat (suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid (SAHA)), as an HDAC inhibitor, can reverse the immune cell activation imbalance in CD patients with anti-TNF resistance by regulating histone acetylation, inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory factors (IFN-γ), and promoting the production of anti-inflammatory factors (IL-10). 90 VPA can restore the H3K27ac level of fibroblasts in stricturing CD, inhibit transforming growth factor-β-induced collagen production, and reverse intestinal fibrosis-related drug resistance. 75 DNMTi decitabine (DAC) can induce specific demethylation of the EBI3 gene, synergistically with TNF-α to upregulate EBI3 expression, promote IL-35 production, providing a new direction for UC with biologic resistance; 77 epigenetic enzyme inhibitors provide precise tools for targeting the interaction network, but their non-specificity may lead to adverse reactions. It is necessary to screen suitable populations and optimize doses in combination with biomarkers.

(2) Non-coding RNA targeted regulation

(2) Non-coding RNA targeted regulation

Non-coding RNA targeted regulation

lncRNA and miRNA have become new targets for drug resistance intervention. lncRNA MALAT-1 and CCAT-1 are highly expressed in the serum of UC patients, positively correlated with disease severity, and targeted downregulation of both could partially rescue treatment resistance. 91 miR-222-3p activates the STAT3 pathway by inhibiting SOCS1, aggravating UC inflammation, and downregulating this miRNA can restore SOCS1 expression and improve drug resistance. 62 Tetrandrine can downregulate miR-429 expression by activating the aryl hydrocarbon receptor, promote the synthesis of tight junction protein Occludin, repair the intestinal epithelial barrier, providing a new strategy for barrier-deficient-related drug resistance; 92 the targeted regulation of non-coding RNAs has the advantage of high specificity, but the optimization of its delivery system (e.g., gut-targeted nano-carriers) is the key to improving intervention effects.

(3) Immune cell function remodeling

(3) Immune cell function remodeling

Immune cell function remodeling

Precise regulation of the function of drug resistance-related immune cell subsets can effectively overcome drug resistance. HDAC inhibitor vorinostat can reverse the pro-inflammatory phenotype of pro-inflammatory FOXP3+ Treg subsets in CD patients, restore their inhibitory function, and repair the expression of intestinal epithelial tight junction proteins. 93 Peptide drug RDP58 promotes the proliferation of colonic Tregs and the functional activation of PD-1+Tregs in a microbiota-dependent manner, inhibits TNF-α secretion, repairs the intestinal barrier, and has a significant effect on drug resistance mediated by microbiota dysbiosis. 94 Huanglian Ejiao decoction can restore the Treg/Th17 balance, downregulate the release of pro-inflammatory factor IL-17A, and regulate the gut microbiota-immunity interaction network through multiple components and targets of traditional Chinese medicine, avoiding single-target drug resistance; 22 immune cell function remodeling needs to take into account both cell subset balance and function repair. The multi-target advantage of traditional Chinese medicine compounds provides a unique idea for the regulation of complex interaction networks, but their active components and mechanisms of action need to be further clarified.

Synergistic intervention of the triple interaction network: An integrated new paradigm to overcome drug resistance

The complexity of the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network determines that single intervention is difficult to reverse drug resistance, and multi-dimensional and multi-target synergistic regulation of key nodes and pathways is required. Based on the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” and drug resistance subtype classification, an integrated scheme to overcome drug resistance can be constructed from three aspects: drug combination, natural product regulation, and non-drug intervention (Figure 4). (2) Optimization of subtype-specific combined intervention schemes

(2) Optimization of subtype-specific combined intervention schemes

Optimization of subtype-specific combined intervention schemes

Metabolic + epigenetic resistance: “Butyrate preparation + HDAC inhibitor” to directly supplement gut microbiota metabolites and inhibit epigenetic abnormalities.

Immunogenic + barrier-deficient resistance: “FMT + vedolizumab” to reduce ADAbs through FMT and repair the intestinal barrier with vedolizumab, forming loop blocking.

Pure immunogenic resistance: “Low-dose immunomodulator + biologic + TDM monitoring” to reduce ADAbs production and optimize drug concentration.

Pure barrier-deficient resistance: “Gut-selective biologic + dietary fiber + probiotics” to synergistically support partial barrier recovery via SCFA-mediated auxiliary protection and restore microbiota balance. Consistent with the above mechanism, soluble dietary fiber indirectly facilitates intestinal barrier restoration by boosting the production of microbiota-derived SCFAs, which further modulate epithelial tight junction protein expression and suppress mucosal inflammation, without the capacity to repair permanently damaged epithelium. Soluble fiber only provides adjunct barrier protection rather than complete repair, and it cannot reverse irreversible epithelial barrier damage induced by long-term severe inflammatory erosion, meaning its therapeutic potency is insufficient to resolve drug resistance originating from permanently compromised mucosal structure.

(3) Drug combination targeting multiple links

(3) Drug combination targeting multiple links

Drug combination targeting multiple links

The combined use of 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) and HDAC inhibitor vorinostat (SAHA) can synergistically inhibit the NF-κB pathway, downregulate pro-inflammatory factor levels, and have lower cytotoxicity, providing a new paradigm for 5-ASA resistance. 95 Mesalazine combined with Bifidobacterium Triple viable capsules enhance the treatment efficiency of mild active CD (97.91% vs. 85.42%) and reduces the risk of drug resistance through the synergistic effect of “anti-inflammation + microbiota regulation”. 96 Tofacitinib combined with infliximab can rescue cases with partial response to single biologics, with a 7-d response rate of 83% in steroid-refractory acute severe UC; 97 the core of drug combination lies in synergistically targeting multiple links of the interaction network. It is necessary to select complementary drug combinations based on drug resistance mechanisms and subtype characteristics to avoid overlapping adverse reactions.

(4) Multitarget regulation of natural products

(4) Multitarget regulation of natural products

Multitarget regulation of natural products

Natural products have shown unique value in the intervention of the triple interaction network due to their multi-component and multi-target advantages. Fermented red ginseng and its active components ginsenoside Rd and compound K can remodel intestinal microbiota, inhibit colonic and central inflammation, and simultaneously improve anxiety and depression-like behaviors associated with UC, providing a new paradigm for drug-resistant UC with comorbid mental symptoms. 98 Angelicin regulates the HDAC1-HIF-1α acetylation pathway by regulating SCFAs production and activating GPR43/109A receptors, repairing the intestinal mucosal barrier. 74 Huangshan floral mushroom polysaccharide (FMPS) restores the Th17/Treg balance through microbiota mediation, promotes SCFAs production, repairs the intestinal barrier, and this regulatory effect depends on intestinal microbiota; 99 the multi-target advantage of natural products makes them ideal candidates for interaction network regulation, but the identification of their active components, mechanism analysis, and standardized preparation are the keys to clinical translation.

(5) Non-drug integrated intervention

(5) Non-drug integrated intervention

Non-drug integrated intervention

Non-drug therapies provide diversified options for drug resistance by regulating the triple interaction network. Acupuncture and moxibustion are most effective in inducing clinical remission of IBD and reducing disease activity, exerting their effects by regulating the balance of intestinal microbiota and cytokines. 100 Among dietary interventions, the Mediterranean diet and low-FODMAP diet have the best effect in maintaining clinical remission, which can reduce fecal calprotectin levels. 100 Specific structure pectin (low methylation + high blockiness) can replace unsulfated secondary bile acids, deliver auxiliary barrier-protective effects under high-glucose conditions through microbiota-dependent SCFA generation, rather than fully restoring permanently injured epithelial tissue, adapting to the drug-resistant characteristics of IBD combined with diabetes. 101 This aligns with the core principle that soluble dietary fiber indirectly facilitates intestinal barrier restoration by boosting the production of microbiota-derived SCFAs. FMT is comparable to glucocorticoids in inducing remission of mild-to-moderate UC, but FMT has better safety. Its efficacy is related to the downregulation of TNF-α and IL-6 and the stability of IL-10, providing a gut microbiota-immunity regulation new paradigm for steroid resistance or intolerance; 102 non-drug integrated intervention has the advantages of high safety and long-term applicability, and can be used as a supplement or alternative to drug intervention, especially suitable for special populations and patients with mild drug resistance.

Application prospects and challenges

The new intervention paradigm based on the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network provides a systematic solution for overcoming the drug resistance dilemma in IBD precision therapy, showing multi-dimensional application potential in clinical translation, but also facing multiple obstacles from mechanism implementation to clinical popularization.

Application prospects: Breakthroughs from basic research to clinical translation

The analysis of the interaction network and the establishment of the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” have opened up multiple transformation paths for IBD drug resistance treatment, promoting the leap from basic research to clinical application. Precise prediction and individualized intervention based on network characteristics and subtype classification are the core entry points for clinical translation, providing new directions for drug resistance treatment.

(1) Precise prediction and individualized intervention

(1) Precise prediction and individualized intervention

Precise prediction and individualized intervention

Biomarkers based on the characteristics of the triple interaction network provide possibilities for drug resistance risk prediction and individualized intervention. The combination of intestinal Verrucomicrobiota and metabolites (butyrate, isobutyrate) can accurately predict the early remission effect of anti-integrin therapy in patients with moderate-to-severe UC, with an AUC of 0.961, which is far superior to a single indicator. 103 Serum lncRNA MALAT-1 and CCAT-1 can be used as noninvasive biomarkers for UC diagnosis and prognosis, and their expression levels are positively correlated with the Mayo score. CCAT-1 is an independent predictor of the Mayo score. 91 Combined with drug resistance subtype biomarkers (e.g., HDAC8, miR-222-3p, ADAbs), precise determination of drug resistance subtypes can be achieved, guiding the selection of individualized intervention schemes (e.g., priority given to probiotics + metabolite supplementation for metabolic resistance, priority given to HDAC inhibitors combined with JAK inhibitors for epigenetic resistance); the development and application of multiomics biomarkers will promote IBD drug resistance treatment into a closed-loop model of precise prediction-intervention-monitoring, significantly improving treatment efficiency.

(2) Breakthroughs in special populations and refractory cases

(2) Breakthroughs in special populations and refractory cases

Breakthroughs in special populations and refractory cases

For refractory cases such as pregnancy complicated with UC, steroid-dependent UC, and fibrostenosing CD, new intervention strategies based on the model have shown unique advantages. Frozen capsule FMT provides a safe drug resistance rescue scheme for pregnant patients with acute severe UC, which can quickly relieve symptoms such as hematochezia and abdominal pain without affecting pregnancy outcomes. 104 The step-up FMT strategy provides an integrated treatment path for IBD patients with steroid dependence and poor response to single biologics. Through the combined scheme of “FMT sequential hormone/anti-TNF-α antibody”, some anti-TNF-α non-responders can re-tolerate the treatment after FMT pretreatment. 86 HDAC inhibitor VPA can reverse intestinal fibrosis-related drug resistance (epigenetic resistance) in sickle cell disease (SCD) patients, providing a new option for stricturing CD that cannot be improved by traditional drugs; 75 new intervention strategies provide diversified solutions for special populations and refractory cases, but their long-term safety and efficacy still need to be verified by large-sample clinical studies.

(3) Optimization of combined treatment modes

(3) Optimization of combined treatment modes

Optimization of combined treatment modes

Combined treatment significantly improves the drug resistance reversal effect by synergistically regulating the triple interaction network. The “probiotics + traditional Chinese medicine + western medicine” integrated traditional Chinese and western medicine model based on drug resistance subtypes, such as Bifidobacterium Quadruple Viable combined with mesalazine and Coix Seed Baitouweng Decoction retention enema, has a total effective rate of 95.35%, which is significantly higher than that of mesalazine alone. 105 The combination of “epigenetic inhibitors + anti-inflammatory drugs” (e.g., 5-ASA synergistic with vorinostat) can enhance anti-inflammatory effects and reduce single-drug toxicity. 95 The “engineered probiotics + epigenetic regulation” model (e.g., propionate-producing engineered probiotic ENPP3 reshapes anti-inflammatory macrophage function through the GPR43/HDAC1/IL-10 axis) is superior to the original probiotic strains. 68 These combined models provide more efficient treatment options for multidrug-resistant IBD; the optimization of combined treatment modes needs to be precisely combined based on drug resistance mechanisms and patient subtype characteristics, and a more individualized combined treatment scheme library will be formed in the future.

Facing challenges

The clinical translation of the new intervention paradigm of the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network still faces multiple obstacles. Insufficient depth of mechanism research and inadequate model verification are the source constraints, directly affecting the precision and innovation of intervention strategies.

(1) Insufficient depth of mechanism research and inadequate model verification

(1) Insufficient depth of mechanism research and inadequate model verification

Insufficient depth of mechanism research and inadequate model verification

Most studies still focus on a single interaction link, and the analysis of the dynamic regulatory mechanisms among gut microbiota, immunity, and epigenetics is insufficient. For example, how microbiota metabolites specifically regulate the activity of epigenetic modification enzymes, and how epigenetic changes feedback affect microbiota colonization, these core mechanisms have not been fully clarified. 69 , 73 The key molecular targets and signaling pathways related to drug resistance have not been fully identified, and the differences in the interaction network characteristics of different drug resistance subtypes have not been fully distinguished. 48 , 106 The application of multiomics combined analysis (metagenomics, transcriptomics, epigenomics, metabolomics) is still not widespread, making it difficult to systematically analyze the causal relationship and regulatory network among the three in the drug resistance process; at the same time, the clinical verification data of the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” are insufficient. Large-sample multicenter cohort studies are lacking to confirm the applicability of the model in patients with different IBD subtypes (UC/CD) and different age groups. The universality and threshold standards of subtype classification biomarkers have not been unified, 107 which restrict the clinical translation value of the model.

(2) Lack of standardization of intervention strategies

(2) Lack of standardization of intervention strategies

Lack of standardization of intervention strategies

There are significant individual differences in intervention methods such as FMT and probiotics. There is no unified standard for donor selection, strain screening, administration dose, and frequency. 85 , 108 The response to intervention strategies varies greatly among different IBD subtypes (UC/CD) and different drug resistance types, and a precise stratified treatment scheme based on drug resistance subtypes has not been formed. 109 The optimal administration route, treatment timing, and course of new intervention methods such as engineered probiotics and epigenetic inhibitors have not been clarified, affecting clinical application effects. Some intervention methods (e.g., WIMT, step-up FMT) are complex to operate and difficult to promote in primary hospitals; 85 , 86 the lack of standardization of intervention strategies limits their wide application. It is necessary to establish standardized guidelines based on drug resistance subtypes and patient characteristics through large-sample multicenter clinical trials.

(3) Clinical translation bottlenecks

(3) Clinical translation bottlenecks

Clinical translation bottlenecks

Most new intervention strategies are still in the stage of basic research or small-sample clinical exploration, lacking large-sample and long-term follow-up clinical data support. The safety and long-term effectiveness of new drugs such as engineered probiotics and epigenetic inhibitors have not been fully verified, and potential adverse reactions and long-term risks are unknown. 77 , 110 The clinical translation feasibility of some intervention methods is low. For example, the extraction process of some natural products is complex and their bioavailability is low, restricting their clinical application. 111 The lack of unified drug resistance assessment standards and efficacy evaluation indicators makes it difficult to compare the results of different studies horizontally, affecting the clinical translation process; clinical translation bottlenecks are the main obstacles for new intervention strategies to move from the laboratory to the clinic. It is necessary to strengthen the integration of industry, university, and research, optimize preparation processes, and establish unified drug resistance assessment and efficacy evaluation standards.

(4) Lack of subtype-specific interventions for drug resistance

(4) Lack of subtype-specific interventions for drug resistance

Lack of subtype-specific interventions for drug resistance

Current intervention strategies are mostly applicable to broad-spectrum drug resistance types, and there is a lack of precise intervention strategies for specific drug resistance subtypes. There are significant differences in the core regulatory mechanisms between immunogenic resistance mediated by ADAbs and metabolic resistance mediated by intestinal microbiota, but existing intervention strategies do not fully distinguish them. 23 , 67 The interaction network characteristics of fibrosis-related resistance (epigenetic type) and acute inflammation-related resistance in fibrostenosing CD are different, requiring targeted intervention, but existing strategies mostly focus on inflammation inhibition, with limited effects on fibrosis reversal. 75 Drug resistance research in special populations (children, elderly, pregnant women) is insufficient, and intervention strategies for these populations are lacking, with scarce safety and efficacy data; 24 the lack of subtype-specific intervention for drug resistance leads to uneven intervention effects. In the future, it is necessary to strengthen drug resistance subtype classification research, develop targeted intervention strategies, and pay attention to the drug resistance mechanisms and intervention needs of special populations.

Future development directions and breakthrough paths

The new intervention paradigm of the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network points out the direction for overcoming the drug resistance dilemma in IBD precision therapy; it is necessary to systematically advance from five dimensions: mechanism dissection, model verification, standard establishment, clinical translation, and precise adaptation to realize the transformation from theory to clinical practice.

(1) Deepen mechanism research and model verification

(1) Deepen mechanism research and model verification

Deepen mechanism research and model verification

In the future, it is necessary to systematically analyze the dynamic interaction mechanisms of gut microbiota, immunity, and epigenetics in different drug resistance subtypes through multi-omics combined analysis and single-cell technology, clarify key molecular targets and signaling pathways, focus on the regulatory effects of microbiota metabolites (e.g., SCFAs, bile acid derivatives) on epigenetic modification enzymes, and the feedback effects of epigenetic changes on immune cell function and microbiota colonization; 69 , 73 comprehensively verify the applicability and reliability of the “triple-loop hierarchical regulation model” using animal models, organoid-microbiota co-culture models, and multi-center clinical cohorts, clarify the differences in loop priority among patients with different IBD subtypes and age groups, and optimize drug resistance subtype classification biomarkers and determination standards; strengthen the integrated application of multi-omics and single-cell technology, analyze the specific interaction network of different drug resistance subtypes, and provide a more solid theoretical basis for precise intervention.

(2) Establish standardized intervention schemes and evaluation systems

(2) Establish standardized intervention schemes and evaluation systems

Establish standardized intervention schemes and evaluation systems

Carry out large-sample multi-center clinical trials to establish individualized intervention guidelines based on patients' triple interaction network characteristics and drug resistance subtypes, clarify the optimal applicable population, administration dose, frequency, and course of interventions; 85 , 108 formulate unified drug resistance assessment standards and efficacy evaluation indicators, including objective indicators such as microbiota structure, immune cell subsets, and epigenetic modification levels, to promote horizontal comparison of different research results; develop standardized preparations, such as lyophilized FMT capsules, high-activity probiotic preparations, and small-molecule drugs targeting epigenetic enzymes, to improve the accessibility and repeatability of intervention; 87 , 104 the establishment of a standardized system is a prerequisite for the wide application of intervention strategies, and guideline formulation and preparation standardization need to be completed through multi-center collaboration.

(3) Promote clinical translation of new intervention strategies

(3) Promote clinical translation of new intervention strategies

Promote clinical translation of new intervention strategies

Strengthen the integration of industry, university, and research, optimize the preparation process and delivery system of new intervention strategies, improve bioavailability and targeting. For example, develop colon-targeted delivery engineered probiotic preparations and epigenetic inhibitor nano-carriers to enhance local efficacy and reduce systemic adverse reactions; 92 carry out long-term safety and effectiveness monitoring of new intervention strategies, clarify their long-term risks and benefits, and provide evidence-based basis for clinical application; explore the closed-loop treatment model of “prediction-intervention-monitoring”, screen potential responders through baseline biomarkers, implement targeted intervention, and dynamically monitor changes in interaction network characteristics to adjust treatment schemes in a timely manner; 103 clinical translation needs to open up the complete chain of “mechanism-preparation-clinic”, and the closed-loop treatment model will significantly improve intervention effects and patient prognosis.

(4) Develop subtype-specific intervention strategies

(4) Develop subtype-specific intervention strategies

Develop subtype-specific intervention strategies

Targeted therapeutic pipelines will be customized according to dominant resistance regulatory loops: (i) Immunogenic resistance: Combine standardized cFMT with biologics to suppress ADA generation and restore effective drug exposure; 84 (ii)Fibrotic epigenetic resistance: Pair HDAC inhibitors with anti-fibrotic agents to reverse fibroblast profibrotic transcriptional reprogramming; 75 (iii) Vulnerable populations (children, elders, pregnant patients): Deploy low-toxicity combined regimens centered on probiotics and structured dietary modulation. 100 , 104 (iv)Multi-target combinatorial therapy integrating microbiota regulators, epigenetic modulators, and anti-inflammatory agents will become the mainstream developmental direction, enabling simultaneous suppression of multiple upstream and downstream resistance pathways to amplify therapeutic efficacy. 95

Conclusion

Abnormal bidirectional crosstalk within the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics interaction network may collectively contribute to the emergence and persistence of therapeutic resistance during IBD precision treatment. The proposed triple-loop hierarchical regulation model establishes a tiered causal sequence—gut microbiota dysbiosis as the initiating trigger, immune dysregulation as the inflammatory amplifier, and epigenetic remodeling as the stabilizing lock of resistant phenotypes—with reciprocal feedback circuits between all three compartments. This theoretical framework provides a novel unified lens to untangle heterogeneous resistance mechanisms, while the four-way mechanistic subtyping (immunogenic, metabolic, epigenetic, barrier-deficient) resolves a key limitation of traditional binary primary/secondary resistance classification that lacks actionable therapeutic guidance.

Current preclinical and clinical work has mapped core signaling nodes within this tripartite network, yielding diversified intervention modalities including microbiota remodeling, immune-epigenetic targeted therapy, and multi-layer synergistic combinatorial regimens. These approaches demonstrate promising translational potential for predictive biomarker screening, refractory patient rescue, and optimized combination treatment protocols.

Nevertheless, the field remains constrained by multiple barriers: incomplete mechanistic characterization of tripartite feedback signaling, insufficient multi-center human validation of the triple-loop model, absent universal intervention standardization, widespread translational bottlenecks for novel agents, and a shortage of subtype-specific treatment regimens. Moving forward, integrated single-cell and multiomics profiling will deepen mechanistic dissection; large cohort trials will validate the model and unify subtype biomarker thresholds; standardized assessment and treatment guidelines will be formulated via cross-institutional collaboration; industry-academia partnerships will accelerate the bench-to-bedside translation of microbiota and epigenetic therapeutics; and stratified combinatorial regimens will be developed to match distinct resistance subtypes and vulnerable patient groups. With continuous technological and clinical advances, intervention paradigms centered on the gut microbiota-immunity-epigenetics axis will shift from broad-spectrum empirical management to fully individualized precision care, could partially rescue treatment failure and deliver improved long-term clinical prognoses for all IBD patients.

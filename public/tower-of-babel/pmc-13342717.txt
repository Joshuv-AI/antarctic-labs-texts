Predictors of intermaxillary fixation (IMF) after open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) in maxillofacial fractures: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Kuldeep Vishwakarma, Amit K. Singh, Sandeep K. Pandey, Anjali Chaudhary, Anup Kumar, Anoop K. Dixit
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) has several advantages over closed treatment modalities of maxillofacial fracture by offering an early return to function and better patient acceptance. The goal is to achieve good occlusion without placing the patient in maxillomandibular fixation (MMF). Nevertheless, there are certain situations when MMF becomes imperative in the postoperative period, even after ORIF. This study aims to identify the factors responsible for MMF following open reduction and internal fixation of maxillofacial fractures.Materials and Method:Maxillofacial trauma patients with fractures involving the dentoalveolar component and treated by ORIF were included in the study. The following data were collected: age, sex, etiology of injury, fracture pattern, associated mandible fractures, delay in the surgical intervention, and need and duration for postoperative IMF. Statistical analyses were performed with the Chi-Square test, and for the inter-category association, the 2 proportion Z-test was used.Result:106 patients with maxillofacial fractures treated by ORIF between July 2018 and June 2023 were included in the study (97 male and 9 female). Patients were categorized into three groups. Group A included midface fracture with associated mandible fracture (n = 30), Group B included isolated midface fracture (n = 30), and Group C included isolated mandible fracture (n = 46). The requirement for IMF was significantly associated with fracture pattern in both Group A and Group B, with a P value of 0.03 and 0.004, respectively. In Group C, IMF is dependent on the type of osteosynthesis P value < 0.001. There was a significant association between the need for IMF and delay in the surgery (P < 0.001).Conclusion:The study demonstrated that in pan-facial fractures, midface fractures accompanied by mandible fractures, and cases where surgical intervention is delayed by more than a week, supplementary intermaxillary fixation (IMF) may be required even after adequate osteosynthesis.

INTRODUCTION

Maxillofacial injuries are most often caused by trauma, which can lead to damage to the soft tissues, teeth, and skeletal components of the face.[1] The mainstay of treatment for maxillofacial fractures involves reestablishing a functional dental occlusion with various forms of intermaxillary fixation (IMF).[2] IMF, often referred to as maxillomandibular fixation (MMF), is a popular method for maintaining occlusion of the jaw and establishing a stable base upon which to restore the form and function of the face.[3] Even though IMF is a versatile treatment, it has several side effects, such as difficulty speaking, masticatory and feeding problems, poor dental hygiene, and a high risk of respiratory discomfort or temporomandibular joint (TMJ) ankylosis.[3] Indications for the use of IMF before surgery are described in the literature; however, it is still unclear when it needs to be used after ORIF, considering the type of fracture pattern and implant used. Many surgeons advocate for supplemental IMF with semirigid osteosynthesis because it reduces the postoperative pain from pterygomasseteric spasm and facilitates better tissue and bone healing.[3] IMF postoperatively is still preferred by many surgeons because they believe it provides natural occlusion.[2]

Are there any general guidelines or standards for using the IMF after surgery, depending on its relationship to the osteosynthesis utilized or any fracture pattern or related injuries that could alter the length of the IMF or duration before ORIF or postoperative IMF duration remains unaddressed.

Therefore, we aimed this study to identify the factors that led to IMF/MMF after open reduction and internal fixation of maxillofacial fractures.

MATERIALS AND METHOD

Study design and patient characteristics

This retrospective cohort study examined the medical records of all patients who came to the trauma center’s emergency room with maxillofacial fractures. The bioethics cell of our institute provided prior ethical clearance before the investigation was carried out (Ethical clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee, Ref No. AN5-V2/SGSOP/STUDY POPULATION dated 21.12.2023). This study included 106 patients with maxillofacial fractures involving the occlusal unit fracture who reported and were admitted to our trauma center’s emergency room between July 2018 and June 2023. Three groups were used to categorize these patients. Group A consists of 30 patients who had midface fractures with associated mandible fractures. Group B had 30 patients with isolated midface fractures that involved the occlusal unit fracture. On the contrary, Group C was made up of 46 patients with isolated mandibular fractures. The same group of surgeons operated on all patients using open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) according to the principles of osteosynthesis.

Patients with a history of a prior injury, readmission, the pathological cause of a mandibular fracture, missing or incomplete information along with patients not involving the fracture of the occlusal unit, and patients whose fractures were managed by conservative means were excluded from the study.

Midface fracture patterns vary widely, and classical Le Fort fracture patterns are very rare. Often we witness various combinations of Le Fort fracture lines, involving fracture of the upper occlusal unit associated with upper central midface fracture (NOE complex fracture) and/or lateral midface fracture (ZM complex fracture).

Accordingly, based on the integrity of the upper midfacial buttresses, the midface fractures were categorized into three subgroups. Group 1 where only the central buttress was fractured. Group 2 where only the lateral buttresses were fractured and Group 3 where both the central and lateral buttresses were fractured.

Surgical procedure and follow-up

All the patients were treated under general anesthesia with ORIF using 1.2/1.7 mm mini plates in cases of midface fracture and 2.0/2.5 mm plates in cases of mandible fracture. When there were two mandibular fractures, one site was fixed rigidly while the other was fixed semi-rigidly. The IMF was released intraoperatively after fixation, and occlusion was verified in all cases before closure. During the postoperative period, occlusion was checked on the first day, and IMF was performed on the third postoperative day for 1 week if any occlusal discrepancies occurred. Weekly follow-ups were performed for occlusal stability of all patients, and IMF was continued as per the case for up to 4 weeks.

Variables

Data regarding the need for the IMF in the postoperative period and duration of the IMF were retrieved for all patients, and their association with fracture pattern, delay in the surgical procedure, and type of osteosynthesis used were analyzed.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis was performed using SPSS [V 26.0]. Categorical variables were presented in frequencies and percentages. The Chi-Square test of independence was used to check any association between the two variables. If any cell of the 2 * 2 contingency table has a frequency less than 5, Fisher’s exact test is used (P value < 0.05 is considered significant).

DISCUSSION

Previous literature has also discussed the adjunctive role of IMF following ORIF in selected maxillofacial fractures.[4]

The goal in the treatment of maxillofacial fracture is to restore form and function to premorbid conditions while minimizing the complications. Intermaxillary fixation, also known as maxillomandibular fixation, has been used traditionally to achieve premorbid occlusion and restore function, but this is not an anatomical reduction.[5]

Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) has been widely adopted to minimize the complications of IMF/MMF by ensuring an early return to function and better patient acceptance.[6] However, even after ORIF, sometimes we need a certain period of supplemental IMF in the postoperative period to achieve premorbid occlusion. There are several key principles guiding the surgeon who is considering the use of IMF/MMF in the preoperative period.[7] However, determining the need for postoperative IMF remains challenging even for experienced surgeons to assess the need for IMF postoperatively in ORIF cases. In our study, we tried to find out the association of midface fracture pattern, associated mandible fracture, type of osteosynthesis of mandible fracture, and delay in the surgical intervention with the need for adjuvant IMF in the postoperative period.

The midface fracture pattern was found to be significantly associated with the need for adjuvant IMF post-ORIF in both Group A (midface fracture with associated mandible fracture) and Group B (isolated midface fracture) with P values of 0.03 and 0.004, respectively. The integrity of the upper midfacial pillars is paramount in providing stability to the upper occlusal unit. Cases, where both the upper facial buttresses (central and lateral) were fractured, are more likely to require adjuvant IMF postoperatively even after adequate osteosynthesis. These findings are consistent with the observations reported by Phuc et al.,[8] wherein a cohort of 31 patients with midface fracture, most of the patients required adjuvant IMF for at least 1 week in the postoperative period.

In our study, midface fracture with an associated mandible fracture was found to be significantly associated with the need for adjuvant IMF in the postoperative period with a P value of < 0.001. Most (63%) of the midface fracture patients with associated mandible fractures required adjuvant IMF. By definition, these fractures can be characterized as pan-facial fractures that are extended midface fractures involving two or more anatomical units. Most of the pan-facial fractures with multiple fragments require adjuvant IMF postoperatively for 3-4 weeks to establish adequate occlusal stability.[9]

However, Murakami et al.,[10] in their retrospective case series of 25 patients with pan-facial fracture used elastic traction only in 20% of patients postoperatively for mild occlusal discrepancy and reported an acceptable outcome with malocclusion only in one patient.

It is debatable whether postoperative IMF should always be used in all mandible fracture repairs, even though some complicated fractures (high condylar fracture, displaced condylar, and comminuted fractures) may respond better to it. The use of postoperative IMF is justified by its proponents’ citing improved bone healing, forced adherence to a liquid diet, and the possibility of using guide elastics to correct minor occlusal discrepancies.[1112] Many studies advocate that IMF is not required in cases of simple fractures of the mandible treated by ORIF using either rigid or semirigid fixation.[11] In their studies, Kaplan et al., and Sindet-Pedersen and Jensen discovered that patients who endured immediate mobilization following ORIF demonstrated comparable outcomes to those whose patients experienced IMF following ORIF.[1314]

Valentino and Marentette[15] also advocated that no statistically significant advantage can be achieved with the use of supplemental IMF following ORIF of mandible fracture. However, in our study, the type of osteosynthesis for mandibular fracture management was found to be significantly associated with the need for adjuvant IMF (P value 0.001). Most of the patients (77%) with bilateral mandible fractures, where one fracture was treated with rigid fixation and another with semirigid fixation in Group C required IMF in the postoperative period. These findings follow the study of Roccia et al.,[16] where in a multicentric prospective study on 336 patients with mandible fractures treated by ORIF, postoperatively IMF was performed in most of the patients (64%) irrespective of the type or number of fractures.

It is evident that if surgical intervention is delayed, it will result in fibrosis and resorption of bony edges, which will prevent mobilization and accurate approximation of the fractured fragments. Our findings indicated that the delay in surgical intervention was a significant factor in the need for adjuvant IMF postoperatively (P value < 0.001), [Table 5].

Adjunct IMF was needed by more than 53% of patients, especially when the delay between injury and operative intervention was greater than 1 week. The optimal surgery time for minimizing complications was 7 days, as reported by Venugopal and colleagues.[17]

Dougherty et al., recommended performing surgery as soon as possible to avoid any edema or infections that could limit the surgical site’s exposure. It could lessen the likelihood of IMF during the recovery phase.[18]

The scientific proof of stabilizing occlusion with a regular IMF period following ORIF seems unlikely. To the best of our knowledge, no scientific study has been published in the literature supporting the regular use of postoperative IMF following ORIF.

This study’s findings indicate the potential for discovering the factors that may support the use of IMF postoperatively in ORIF cases with maxillofacial injuries.

The study is constrained by a limited sample size in each group, and we believe that further research should be conducted based on evidence rather than persisting with conventional techniques. As surgeons, we should embrace evidence-based practices to offer enhanced advantages to our patients.

CONCLUSION

The results of our research indicate that for pan-facial fractures involving upper facial buttresses, midface fractures accompanied by mandible fractures, and cases where surgical intervention is delayed by more than a week, supplementary intermaxillary fixation (IMF) is necessary even after adequate osteosynthesis.

We think that the recommended predictors will be helpful in establishing a treatment protocol that addresses the problems of using intermaxillary fixation after ORIF.

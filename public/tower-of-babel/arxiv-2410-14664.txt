Stochastic gravitational wave background anisotropies from inflation with non-Bunch-Davies states
and Shuichiro Yokoyama

Abstract
It has been known that stochastic gravitational wave backgrounds (SGWBs) have anisotropies generated by squeezed-type tensor non-Gaussianities originating from scalar-tensor-tensor (STT) and tensor-tensor-tensor cubic interactions. While the squeezed tensor non-Gaussianities in the standard slow-roll inflation with the Bunch-Davies vacuum state are suppressed due to the so-called consistency relation, those in extended models with the violation of the consistency relation can be enhanced. Among such extended models, we consider the inflation model with the non-Bunch-Davies state that has been known to enhance the squeezed tensor non-Gaussianities. We explicitly formulate the primordial STT bispectrum induced during inflation in the context of Horndeski theory with the non-Bunch-Davies state and show that the induced SGWB anisotropies can be enhanced. We then discuss the detectability of those anisotropies in future gravitational wave experiments.

2
Primordial power spectra with non-Bunch-Davies states

To explore the impact of primordial non-Gaussianities induced by the non-Bunch-Davies states on the SGWB,
it is useful to formulate those in a general framework of single-field inflation.
We work with the Horndeski theory
[
80
,
81
,
82
]
, which is the most general scalar-tensor theory whose equations of motion are second order. The action takes the following form
[
82
]
,

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\biggl[G_{2}(\phi,X)-G_{3}(\phi,X)\Box\phi+G_{4}(\phi,X)R+G_{4X}[(\Box\phi)^{2}-(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad+G_{5}(\phi,X)G_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{6}G_{5X}[(\Box\phi)^{3}-3\Box\phi(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}+2(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{3}]\biggr],

(2.1)

with
G_{i}(\phi,X)
being arbitrary functions of the scalar field
\phi
and its canonical kinetic term
X:=-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi/2
.
Imposing appropriate conditions on the arbitrary functions, the scalar field drives the slow-roll inflation as an inflaton
[
82
]
.

The perturbed metric around a spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker spacetime under the unitary gauge in which the spatial fluctuation of the scalar field vanishes, that is,
\phi=\phi(t)
, is of the form

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}=-N^{2}{\rm d}t^{2}+g_{ij}({\rm d}x^{i}+N^{i}{\rm d}t)({\rm d}x^{j}+N^{j}{\rm d}t),

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=1+\alpha,\ N_{i}=\partial_{i}\beta,

(2.3)

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=a^{2}e^{2\zeta}\biggl(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}h_{ik}h_{kj}+\cdots\biggr),

(2.4)

with
a(t)
being a scale factor. Here,
\alpha,~\beta,
and
\zeta
are scalar perturbations, and
h_{ij}
means transverse-traceless tensor perturbations.
We hereafter use a dot as a derivative with respect to
t
, and the Hubble parameter is defined by
H:=\dot{a}/a
.

2.1
Quadratic action and primordial power spectra with non-Bunch-Davies states

Expanding the action Eq. (
2.1
) up to the quadratic order in the perturbations and substituting the solutions of
\alpha
and
\beta
derived from the constraint equations into the perturbed action, one obtains
[
82

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}xa^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}_{S}\dot{\zeta}^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{S}}{a^{2}}(\partial_{i}\zeta)^{2}\biggr],

(2.5)

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}^{3}xa^{3}\biggl[\mathcal{G}_{T}\dot{h}_{ij}^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{a^{2}}(\partial_{k}h_{ij})^{2}\biggr],

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{G}_{S},~\mathcal{F}_{S},~\mathcal{G}_{T}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{T}
are given in terms of
\phi
,
X
, the arbitrary functions
G_{i}(\phi,X)
and their derivatives with respect to
\phi
and
X
, and the explicit expressions of these functions are given in Appendix
A
. Throughout this paper, we assume a slow-roll inflation with
\mathcal{G}_{S},~\mathcal{F}_{S},~\mathcal{G}_{T},~\mathcal{F}_{T}\simeq{\rm const.}
in a quasi-de Sitter spacetime (
H\simeq{\rm const.}
). Specifically, we introduce the slow-roll parameters as

\displaystyle\epsilon:=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}},\ g_{S}:=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{S}}{H\mathcal{G}_{S}},\ f_{S}:=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{S}}{H\mathcal{F}_{S}},\ g_{T}:=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{T}}{H\mathcal{G}_{T}},\ f_{T}:=\frac{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}{H\mathcal{F}_{T}},

(2.7)

and assume
\epsilon,~g_{S},~f_{S},~g_{T},~f_{T}\ll 1
as slow-roll approximations.

We then quantize the scalar and tensor perturbations. The Fourier transformations of those are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\zeta}(t,{\bf k})e^{i\bf k\cdot\bf x},

(2.8)

\displaystyle h_{ij}(t,{\bf x})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{h}_{ij}(t,{\bf k})e^{i\bf k\cdot\bf x}.

(2.9)

In Fourier space, both perturbations are quantized as

\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}\to~\hat{\zeta}(t,{\bf k})

\displaystyle=u_{k}(t)\hat{a}_{\bf k}+u_{k}^{*}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ij}\to~\hat{h}_{ij}(t,{\bf k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\biggl[v_{k}(t)e^{(s)}_{ij}({\bf k})\hat{a}^{(s)}_{\bf k}+v^{*}_{k}(t)e^{(s)*}_{ij}(-{\bf k})\hat{a}^{(s)\dagger}_{-{\bf k}}\biggr],

(2.11)

where
e^{(s)}_{ij}(\bf k)
denotes the polarization tensor with the helicity states
s=\pm 2
, satisfying the transverse-traceless conditions, i.e.,
k_{i}e^{(s)}_{ij}({\bf k})=\delta_{ij}e^{(s)}_{ij}({\bf k})=0
.
The creation and annihilation operators satisfy the standard commutation relations,

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{\bf k},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime}),

(2.12)

\displaystyle[\hat{a}^{(s)}_{\bf k},\hat{a}^{(s^{\prime})\dagger}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime}),

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\rm others}

\displaystyle=0.

(2.14)

The equations of motion for the perturbations in real space are of the form,

\displaystyle E^{s}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{t}(a^{3}\mathcal{G}_{S}\dot{\zeta})-a\mathcal{F}_{S}\partial^{2}\zeta=0,

(2.15)

\displaystyle E^{h}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{t}(a^{3}\mathcal{G}_{T}\dot{h}_{ij})-a\mathcal{F}_{T}\partial^{2}h_{ij}=0.

(2.16)

On the quasi-de Sitter background and under the slow-roll approximations, at the leading order, general solutions of the equations of motion for the mode functions
can be obtained as

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}u_{k,({\rm BD})}+\beta_{k}u^{*}_{k,({\rm BD})},

(2.17)

\displaystyle v^{(s)}_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{(s)}_{k}v_{k,({\rm BD})}+\beta^{(s)}_{k}v^{*}_{k,({\rm BD})},

(2.18)

where
\alpha_{k},~\beta_{k},~\alpha^{(s)}_{k}
, and
\beta^{(s)}_{k}
are Bogoliubov coefficients, and

and

denote the mode functions with the Bunch-Davies state which are respectively given as

\displaystyle u_{k,({\rm BD})}

\displaystyle=\frac{iH}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{S}c_{s}k^{3}}}(1+ic_{s}k\eta)e^{-ic_{s}k\eta},

(2.19)

\displaystyle v_{k,({\rm BD})}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\sqrt{2}H}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_{T}c_{h}k^{3}}}(1+ic_{h}k\eta)e^{-ic_{h}k\eta},

(2.20)

where we introduced the conformal time
\eta:=\int{\rm d}t/a
and the propagation speed of each mode
c_{s}^{2}=\mathcal{F}_{S}/\mathcal{G}_{S},~c_{h}^{2}=\mathcal{F}_{T}/\mathcal{G}_{T}

The Bogoliubov coefficients satisfy the following normalization conditions:

\displaystyle|\alpha_{k}|^{2}-|\beta_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

(2.21)

\displaystyle|\alpha^{(s)}_{k}|^{2}-|\beta^{(s)}_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

(2.22)

The dimensionless primordial power spectra of the scalar and tensor perturbations are respectively defined by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\zeta}({\bf k})\hat{\zeta}({\bf k}^{\prime})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{\zeta},

(2.23)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\xi}^{(s)}({\bf k})\hat{\xi}^{(s^{\prime})}({\bf k}^{\prime})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})\frac{\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathcal{P}^{(s)}_{h},

(2.24)

where
\hat{\xi}^{(s)}({\bf k}):=\hat{h}_{ij}({\bf k})e^{(s)*}_{ij}({\bf k})
.
Then, the power spectra on superhorizon scales (
-c_{s}k\eta\ll 1
and
-c_{h}k\eta\ll 1
), can be obtained from the mode functions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{S}c_{s}}|\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k}|^{2},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(s)}_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}c_{h}}|\alpha^{(s)}_{k}-\beta^{(s)}_{k}|^{2},

(2.26)

Then, up to the leading order in the slow-roll parameters, the spectral indices of the scalar and tensor power spectra are derived as

\displaystyle n_{s}-1

\displaystyle:=\frac{{\rm d}\ln\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}}{{\rm d}\ln k}\simeq-2\epsilon+\frac{g_{S}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}f_{S}+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln k}\ln|\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k}|^{2},

(2.27)

\displaystyle n_{t}

\displaystyle:=\frac{{\rm d}\ln\mathcal{P}_{h}}{{\rm d}\ln k}\simeq-2\epsilon+\frac{g_{T}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}f_{T}+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln k}\ln\sum_{s}|\alpha^{(s)}_{k}-\beta^{(s)}_{k}|^{2},

(2.28)

where
\mathcal{P}_{h}=\sum_{s}\mathcal{P}^{(s)}_{h}
.
By taking the limit,
\alpha_{k}=1,~\beta_{k}=0,~\alpha^{(s)}_{k}=1
, and
\beta^{(s)}_{k}=0
, Eqs. (
2.25

2.28
) reproduce the results in the slow-roll inflation with the Bunch-Davies state
[
82
]
. In the main text, we hereafter set
\alpha_{k}=1
and
\beta_{k}=0
(i.e., assume the Bunch-Davies scalar modes) for simplicity, and we solely study the impact of the non-Bunch-Davies tensor modes on the SGWB and its anisotropies. (See Ref.
[
85
]
for the impacts of primordial gravitational waves induced by non-Bunch-Davies scalar modes on the SGWB.)

3
Non-Bunch-Davies bispectrum

As we will see later in section
4
, the SGWB anisotropies on large scales are sensitive to the primordial non-Gaussianity which gives the coupling between short- and long-wavelength modes (see, e.g., Ref.
[
29
,
32
]
). At the leading order, such a coupling can be characterized by the primordial bispectrum in the squeezed limit.
Although the tensor auto-bispectrum in the squeezed limit can be a source of the anisotropies of primordial GWB, due to the tight observational constraint on the primordial tensor modes on CMB scales the contribution from the tensor auto-bispectrum might be smaller than that from the STT cross-bispectrum.
Then, here, we focus on the primordial STT bispectrum in the squeezed limit, that is, the coupling between long-wavelength scalar modes and short-wavelength tensor modes.

3.1
Trans-Planckian problem

Before formulating the STT bispectrum, we mention the Trans-Planckian issue
[
79
]

in the inflation models with the non-Bunch Davies state.
In general, the physical momenta of short-wavelength modes, e.g., GWs targeted by PTA such as NANOGrav or space interferometer such as DECIGO and BBO, can exceed the Planck scale (i.e.,
k_{\rm phys}:=k/a\geq M_{\rm pl}
) when both short- and long-wavelength modes are on subhorizon scales at the same time.
The condition that the long-wavelength modes are on subhorizon scales at
\eta=\eta_{0}
is expressed as

\displaystyle 1<-k_{L}\eta_{0}\,\left(\,\ll-k_{S}\eta_{0}\,\right),

(3.1)

where
k_{S}
and
k_{L}
denote the comoving wavenumber of the short- and
long-wavelength modes, respectively.
In the quasi-de Sitter background (i.e.,
a\simeq-(H\eta)^{-1}
), Eq. (
3.1
) turns into

\displaystyle\frac{k_{{\rm phys},L}}{M_{\rm pl}}>\frac{H_{\rm inf}}{M_{\rm pl}},

(3.2)

and for the short-wavelength modes

\displaystyle\frac{k_{{\rm phys},S}}{M_{\rm pl}}>\frac{H_{\rm inf}}{M_{\rm pl}}\times\frac{k_{S}}{k_{L}}.

(3.3)

Assuming
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{T},\mathcal{F}_{T})=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}
and
|\alpha^{(s)}_{k_{\rm CMB}}-\beta^{(s)}_{k_{\rm CMB}}|^{2}\sim 1
,
Eq. (
2.26
) gives

\mathcal{P}_{h}^{1/2}|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\sim H_{\rm inf}/M_{\rm pl}
,
and then we have

\displaystyle 10^{-6}\left(\frac{r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}}{10^{-3}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}|_{k_{\rm CMB}}}{2\times 10^{-9}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{k_{S}}{k_{L}}\right)<\frac{k_{{\rm phys},S}}{M_{\rm pl}},

(3.4)

where
r:=\mathcal{P}_{h}/\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}
is the tensor-to-scalar ratio.
It is expected that future CMB B-mode polarization experiments can detect the inflationary SGWB
with
r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\sim 10^{-3}
,
and then the above inequality means that the detection of the inflationary SGWB
would suffer from the Trans-Planckian problem of the short-wavelength modes at
\eta=\eta_{0}

for
k_{S}>10^{4}~{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\times(k_{L}/(10^{-2}~{\rm Mpc}^{-1}))
.
Primordial non-Gaussianities from inflation with the Bunch-Davies state are insensitive to subhorizon modes since those are mainly generated after their horizon-cross scales, while those with the non-Bunch-Davies state are not.
This is because the excited modes on subhorizon scales can generate the non-Gaussianities
[
39
]

and then the above Trans-Planckian issue becomes serious.

In principle, to avoid this problem one can consider a non-standard model of inflation with
\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\sim 10^{-9}
and
r\ll 10^{-3}
by choosing model parameters, but we do not consider such models.
Instead, we relax the starting point of the above argument Eq. (

) in such a way that the tensor modes got excited from the Bunch-Davies state at
\eta=\eta_{0}
when the long-wavelength modes of
k_{L}=10^{-2}{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
were already on superhorizon scales, while the short-wavelength modes were still on subhorizon scales:

\displaystyle-k_{S}\eta_{0}>1\gg-k_{L}\eta_{0},

(3.5)

and hence we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{k_{{\rm phys},S}}{M_{\rm pl}}>r^{1/2}\mathcal{P}^{1/2}_{\zeta}|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\gg\frac{k_{{\rm phys},L}}{M_{\rm pl}}.

(3.6)

By choosing
\eta_{0}
appropriately, the Trans-Planckian problem can be avoided even for

k_{S}\gg 10\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
, e.g., the modes corresponding to the ones targeted by NANOGrav (
10^{6}\,\rm Mpc^{-1}
) and DECIGO/BBO (
10^{13}\,\rm Mpc^{-1}
).
It has been extensively discussed that the non-Bunch-Davies tensor modes can enhance primordial bispectra compared to those in the case of the Bunch-Davies state. In particular, the enhancements of the so-called squeezed bispectra are in proportion to powers of
k_{S}/k_{L}
(see, e.g., Ref.
[
51
]
). However, we will show that this enhancement appears in the case with Eq. (
3.1
), while it does not with Eq. (
3.5
).

3.2
Scalar-tensor-tensor bispectrum

The scalar-tensor-tensor bispectrum is defined by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\zeta}({\bf k}_{1})\hat{\xi}^{(s_{2})}({\bf k}_{2})\hat{\xi}^{(s_{3})}({\bf k}_{3})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}+{\bf k}_{3})\mathcal{B}_{shh}^{s_{2}s_{3}}.

(3.7)

By expanding the action Eq. (
2.1
) up to the cubic order in the perturbations, the interaction Hamiltonian involving one scalar and two tensors is obtained as
[
101
]

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}=-\int{\rm d}^{3}x\mathcal{L}_{shh},

(3.8)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{shh}

\displaystyle=a^{3}\biggl[b_{1}\zeta\dot{h}_{ij}^{2}+\frac{b_{2}}{a^{2}}\zeta(\partial_{k}h_{ij})^{2}+b_{3}(\partial_{k}\psi)\dot{h}_{ij}\partial_{k}h_{ij}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+b_{4}\dot{\zeta}\dot{h}_{ij}^{2}+\frac{b_{5}}{a^{2}}\partial^{2}\zeta\dot{h}_{ij}^{2}+b_{6}\psi_{,ij}\dot{h}_{ik}\dot{h}_{jk}+\frac{b_{7}}{a^{2}}\zeta_{,ij}\dot{h}_{ik}\dot{h}_{jk}\biggr].

(3.9)

As is summarized in Appendix
A
, the above cubic Lagrangian includes other terms proportional to the equations of motion for the linear perturbations which, in principle, can contribute to the bispectrum. However, those are always subleading under the slow-roll approximations, and hence we do not consider those in the main text.

For later convenience, we parametrize the primordial bispectrum as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{shh}^{s_{2}s_{3}}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left[\sum^{7}_{i=1}\left(\alpha^{(s_{2})}_{k_{2}}-\beta^{(s_{2})}_{k_{2}}\right)\left(\alpha^{(s_{3})}_{k_{3}}-\beta^{(s_{3})}_{k_{3}}\right)\frac{2}{k_{1}^{3}k_{2}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}\frac{H^{6}}{\mathcal{F}_{S}\mathcal{F}_{T}^{2}c_{s}c_{h}^{2}}b_{i}\mathcal{F}_{b_{i}}\mathcal{V}_{b_{i}}+(s_{2},k_{2}\leftrightarrow s_{3},k_{3})\right].

(3.10)

The functions
\mathcal{V}_{b_{i}}
originate from products of the polarization tensors, the explicit expressions of which are summarized in Appendix
B
.
To compute the primordial bispectrum, we perform the time integral of the in-in formalism from
\eta=\eta_{0}
to the end of inflation,
\eta=0
. In general, the time integral can be schematically expressed as

\displaystyle S(\tilde{K}):=\int_{\eta_{0}}^{0}{\rm d}\eta(-\eta)^{n}e^{-i\tilde{K}\eta},

(3.11)

where
\tilde{K}
takes
K^{\prime},K_{1}^{\prime},K_{2}^{\prime}
, or
K_{3}^{\prime}
and they are respectively defined as

\displaystyle K^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=c_{s}k_{1}+c_{h}(k_{2}+k_{3}),

(3.12)

\displaystyle K_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=-c_{s}k_{1}+c_{h}(k_{2}+k_{3}),

(3.13)

\displaystyle K_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=c_{s}k_{1}+c_{h}(-k_{2}+k_{3}),

(3.14)

\displaystyle K_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=c_{s}k_{1}+c_{h}(k_{2}-k_{3}).

(3.15)

Under Eq. (
3.5
) with
k_{1}=k_{L}
and
k_{2}\simeq k_{3}\simeq k_{S}
, one can obtain

\displaystyle S(\tilde{K})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\tilde{K}^{-(n+1)})&\ \ (\tilde{K}=K^{\prime}\ {\rm or}\ K_{1}^{\prime}),\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\eta_{0}^{(n+1)})&\ \ (\tilde{K}=K_{2}^{\prime}\ {\rm or}\ K_{3}^{\prime}).\end{cases}

(3.16)

It is worth mentioning the difference of
S(\tilde{K})
between Eq. (
3.1
) and Eq. (
3.5
). In the former, one has
S(\tilde{K})=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{K}^{-(n+1)})
for all of the four cases under the squeezed limit, which has resulted in the enhancements of the squeezed bispectrum in proportion to powers of
k_{S}/k_{L}
. In the latter, one has
S(\tilde{K})=\mathcal{O}(\eta_{0}^{(n+1)})
, which will result in the enhancements in proportion to powers of
-k_{S}\eta_{0}
as will be shown below. Since we have
k_{S}/k_{L}\gg-k_{S}\eta_{0}
, the enhancements will be reduced compared to the results in the literature (e.g., Ref.
[
51
]
) where Eq. (
3.1
) was assumed.

Another constraint is obtained from an argument on backreaction from the excited tensor modes. Once the modes get excited from the Bunch-Davies state, the excited modes cause backreaction to the inflationary background

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm excited}=\frac{c_{h}}{a^{4}(\eta)}\int|\beta^{(s)}_{k}|^{2}k^{3}{\rm d}k.

\mathcal{E}_{b}\sim M_{\rm pl}^{2}H_{\rm inf}^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(\eta_{0})^{4}}A^{2}e^{2\Delta^{2}}k_{*}^{4}\Delta\lesssim M_{\rm pl}^{2}H_{\rm inf}^{2}.

\displaystyle-k_{*}\eta_{0}\lesssim 10.

which yields the following constraint

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{b_{1}}{\mathcal{G}_{T}},\frac{b_{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\biggr)\lesssim 10^{3}\,\left(\frac{r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}}{10^{-2}}\right)^{-1}.

Let us mention the possibility of obtaining a large value of

in Eq. (
4.22

b_{1}
and

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\mathcal{G}_{T}}{8}\biggl\{1-\frac{H\mathcal{G}_{T}^{2}}{\Theta\mathcal{F}_{T}}+\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{3}\biggl[-\frac{\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}^{2}}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{\Theta}\biggr)\biggr]\biggr\},

(4.38)

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\frac{H\mathcal{G}_{T}^{2}}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{8}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{\Theta}\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{T}+\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{8}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{G}_{T}}{\Theta}\biggr)-\frac{\mathcal{F}_{T}}{8}.

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}}{\mathcal{G}_{T}}+\frac{3b_{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}

(4.40)

\displaystyle f^{ss}_{{\rm NL},0}(k)=f_{\rm NL,BD}-2c_{h}k\eta_{0}\mathcal{A}(k,k_{*})\biggl(f_{\rm NL,BD}-\sqrt{\pi}\frac{b_{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\frac{1+2c_{h}^{2}k^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}{c_{h}^{2}k^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}\biggr),

\displaystyle f_{\rm NL,BD}:=8\sqrt{\pi}\biggl(\frac{b_{1}}{\mathcal{G}_{T}}+\frac{3b_{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\biggr).

(4.42)

If both scalar and tensor power spectra are almost scale invariant on the CMB scales, we have

\displaystyle r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\simeq 16\frac{\mathcal{F}_{S}}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\frac{c_{s}}{c_{h}}.

(4.43)

Taking this into account and using

\displaystyle f^{ss}_{{\rm NL},0}(k)=f_{\rm NL,BD}-2c_{h}k\eta_{0}\mathcal{A}(k,k_{*})\biggl(f_{\rm NL,BD}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{128}\frac{c_{h}}{c_{s}}r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\frac{1+2c_{h}^{2}k^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}{c_{h}^{2}k^{2}\eta_{0}^{2}}\biggr).

We can thus estimate the deviation of

\displaystyle|f^{ss}_{{\rm NL},0}(k)-f_{\rm NL,BD}|\leq\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{c_{h}}{c_{s}}r|_{k_{\rm CMB}}\biggr)\leq\mathcal{O}\biggl(10^{-2}\frac{c_{h}}{c_{s}}\biggr),

(4.45)

where we assumed

always indicates a large scalar non-Gaussianity

and

4.4
Detectability of the SGWB anisotropies

Based on the formulae for the correlation functions of the SGWB anisotropies
induced by the inflationary STT bispectrum with the non-Bunch-Davies states shown in the above sections,

[

[
29
]
.
However, as discussed in the previous section
2.2.1
,
by setting the model parameters appropriately, our model can reproduce the blue-tilted tensor power spectrum for the relevant frequency range of the PTA experiments.
Thus,
we employ their result of the noise spectrum when discussing the detectability with future PTA experiments such as SKA.
For the scale-invariant case discussed in Sec.
4.3.2
,
we employ the noise spectrum computed
in Ref.
[

5
Summary

The SGWB anisotropies have recently attracted a lot of attention as a tool to distinguish the source of SGWBs and a probe of the new physics in the early universe.
In this paper, we have investigated the SGWB anisotropies induced by the primordial non-Gaussianity during inflation.
Although it has been mentioned that the characteristic primordial non-Gaussianity with non-Bunch-Davies states, which brings the coupling between short- and long-wavelength modes, can induce the spatial anisotropies on SGWB,
precise theoretical predictions were not made in previous studies.
In this paper, we have explicitly formulated the primordial scalar-tensor-tensor (STT) bispectrum induced during inflation in the context of Horndeski theory with non-Bunch-Davies states and investigated the STT bispectrum in the squeezed limit, which characterizes the coupling between long-wavelength scalar mode and short-wavelength tensor modes.
In the formulation,
we carefully clarified the Trans-Planckian problem for the short-wavelength tensor modes in the non-Bunch-Davies state and then derived the condition to avoid it.
In the CMB observations, due to the relatively small hierarchy between the longest and shortest modes which are in the observational range (roughly estimated as
k_{\rm S}/k_{\rm L}=O(10^{3})
),
the aforementioned Trans-Planckian problem is not so serious.
On the other hand, for the SGWB anisotropies,
a large hierarchy between long- and short-wavelength modes
(roughly estimated as
k_{\rm S}/k_{\rm L}=O(10^{8})
(for PTA experiments), and
O(10^{16})
(for DECIGO/BBO experiments)) is required, and then close attention must be paid to this problem. We found that by avoiding the Trans-Planckian problem the strong scale-dependent enhancement of the STT bispectrum in the squeezed limit in the case with the non-Bunch-Davies states, which was considered in the previous works, can not be expected.
Instead, another scale dependence appears in the STT bispectrum which also depends on the non-Bunch-Davies excitation time.

To obtain the theoretical predictions for the SGWB anisotropies,
with future GW observations in mind, we assumed the two configurations of the Bogoliubov coefficients: one is for the highly blue-tilted tensor spectrum, and the other is for the almost scale-invariant spectrum. Using those configurations, we have computed the self-correlation function of the SGWB anisotropies and the cross-correlation function of those with the CMB temperature fluctuations. Then, we evaluated the possible maximum values of the amplitudes of those correlation functions in light of the backreaction constraint and the perturbativity condition. As a result, we have found that both anisotropies can dominate those originating from the late-time metric perturbations, so-called SW effects, and shown that the anisotropies with the maximum amplitudes can potentially be tested with PTA experiments. We have also found that the SNR for the scale-invariant case is much smaller than unity. However, since how large the SNR can be depends on the magnitude of
\bar{\Omega}_{\rm GW}
, those anisotropies could be tested with DECIGO/BBO as well if a relatively large tensor power spectrum compared to the scale-invariant case is detected at the deci-Hz frequency band. Here, our analysis of the detectability is based on order estimations, and hence it would be important to compute the noise spectrum explicitly and discuss the detectability in detail. We will leave it to our future work.

B.8
Field redefinition

Since the field redefinition does not bring any contributions from the far past to the resultant bispectrum, let us consider the superhorizon scales on which we have

\displaystyle\dot{h}^{(s)}_{{\bf k}}\simeq ic_{h}^{2}k^{2}\eta^{2}Hh^{(s)}_{\bf k}.

(B.77)

The quadratic-order terms in the field redefinition involve at least one time-derivative of the tensor perturbations, which means that those do not contribute to the bispectrum evaluated at the end of inflation
\eta\to 0
(i.e.,
\dot{h}^{(s)}_{{\bf k}}\to 0
). Therefore, the field redefinition always yields subleading contributions.

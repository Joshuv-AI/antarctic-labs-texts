Response to comment on: “Efficacy and safety of tebentafusp in patients with metastatic uveal melanoma: A systematic review and meta-analysis”
Authors: Danyao Chen, Yuming Sun, Yating Dian, Guangtong Deng
Published: 2024

Dear editor

We have carefully considered the comments provided by Shabil et al.1 regarding our recently published manuscript, “Efficacy and safety of tebentafusp in patients with metastatic uveal melanoma: A systematic review and meta-analysis.”2 We appreciate their insights and have made appropriate modifications to enhance the reliability and applicability of our results.

Firstly, we concur with Shabil et al. on the necessity of evaluating the quality of included studies. The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluations (GRADE) offers a systematic approach for assessing the quality of evidence in systematic reviews and guidelines.3 However, GRADE requires that included studies provide a comprehensive description of the relevant background, population, interventions, and controls.4 Given that our study focuses on investigating the therapeutic effects and adverse reactions in patients with metastatic uveal melanoma without a control group, we believe that GRADE is not suitable for this meta-analysis. In response, we have noted that the Joanna Briggs Institute’s (JBI) critical appraisal tools can assist in the quality evaluation of single-arm studies. Consequently, we employed this method to create a quality evaluation table as a supplementary resource (Table 1).

Furthermore, as noted by Shabil et al. I2 statistics is a widely accepted metric for evaluating heterogeneity in meta-analyses, and funnel plots are commonly used to assess publication bias. We also believe the prediction interval may help translate the findings into real-world Setting,5 and Doi plots along with the Luis Furuya-Kanamori (LFK) index could be employed to detect publication bias in meta-analyses.6 However, they have not been widely used in meta-analysis. Importantly, maintaining the original analytical method does not alter the conclusion of our article, so we prefer to retain our original analysis method.

In conclusion, we extend our gratitude to Shabil et al. for their thorough review and constructive suggestions.

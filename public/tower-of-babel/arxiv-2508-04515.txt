Adiabatic protocol for the generalized Langevin equation
Pedro J. Colmenares

Abstract
This article proposes a self-consistent methodology for determining the work involved in an adiabatic process where the dynamics of a Brownian particle is trapped in an optical tweezers. Instead of varying the frequency of the trap, it is displaced through a defined protocol. Assuming the dynamics follow a modified generalized Langevin equation previously proposed by the author, it is found that the external adiabatic driving is uniquely derived in terms of the system’s dynamical properties, and unlike isothermal processes, does not require optimization. There is no need to include other parameters than those characterizing the model.

I
Introduction

Adiabatic processes are difficult to carry out experimentally due to the temperature gradient they require. Theoretically, several approaches have been analyzed. Agarwal and Chaturvedi
Agarwal and Chaturvedi 2013
addressed the quantum regime, while classically it is currently done through the overdamped Langevin equation by assuming a time-dependent frequency. Schmiedl and Siefert
Schmiedl and Seifert 2008
assumed that by instantaneously changing both the frequency and the bath’s temperature, the position’s probability density (PDF) remains unaltered because there is insufficient time for relaxation. Although it is isentropic, there is a leak of heat due to changes in the particle’s kinetic energy. Incorporating this idea to describe heat engines, the Carnot efficiency is not achieved in the quasistatic limit, but it is approximated. Subsequently, Bo and Celani
Bo and Celani 2013
determine a time-independent protocol as a function of the minimum and maximum prefixed trap intensities, and independently of the kinetic temperature of the trapped particle, to be used in the equation for the released heat and find the proper quasistatic efficiency limit. Martinez
et al.
Martínez et al. 2015a
made experimental measurements in Carnot-like engines, and theoretically
Martínez et al. 2015b
pointed out that the leak is due to the space volume not being invariant. Correcting it, they found the correct Carnot efficiency in the quasi-static limit. Furthermore, Arold
et al.
Arold et al. 2018
evaluate the leak in isochoric temperature-dependent processes for sudden jumps in the field frequency, and finally, Holubec and Ryabov
Holubec and Ryabov 2015
employed the approach of Ref.
Schmiedl and Seifert 2008
to optimize the trade-off between efficiency and maximum power in low-dissipation Carnot cycles.

This work explores the derivation of an optimal external driving for a Brownian particle in a thermal bath subjected to an adiabatic process, when the particle dynamics obey a modified generalized Langevin (GLE) equation previously derived by the author
Colmenares 2023
. Unlike other works in the field, where the protocol is generated by time changes of the optical trap intensity, this proposal is based on displacing it at a given rate.

A compendium of the major equations related to the GLE and derived in Ref.
Colmenares 2023
is shown in Sec.
II
, while the derivation of the associated isothermal protocol, already demonstrated in Ref.
Colmenares 2024
, is summarized in Sec.
III
. The derivation of the adiabatic protocols is covered in Sec.
IV
. The article is closed with some general remarks in Sec.
V
.

\displaystyle-\Omega\,q(t)-\int_{0}^{t}dy\,v(y)\,\Gamma_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(t-y)+R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(t),

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle v_{{}_{\mathrm{0}}}\chi(t)-\Omega\,q_{{}_{\mathrm{0}}}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!dy\,\chi(y)+\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{k+\widehat{\Theta}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(k)}\right\},

\displaystyle\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{M}\Gamma_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(\mid\!t\!-s\!\mid),

(5)

\displaystyle\kappa\,\tau\,\omega\pm i.

\displaystyle R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\left[\left(q_{j}(0)-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\beta_{j}\,\alpha_{j}}\,q(0)\right)\cos(\alpha_{j}\,t)\right.

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{p_{j}(0)}{\beta_{j}}\sin(\alpha_{j}\,t)\bigg],

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\bigg\{1-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2\,\omega\left(\kappa\,\tau^{2}\,\omega^{2}-1\right)}\bigg[3\,\sqrt{\kappa}

In Brownian dynamics, the diffusion coefficient no longer satisfies the Stokes-Einstein relation of stationary motion
Zwanzig and Bixon 1970
. It is a time-dependent function correctly predicted in the Fokker-Planck equation (FPE) formalism. Its calculation requires first knowing the PDF for the position of the Brownian particle described in Sec. 3.1 of Ref.
Olivares-Rivas and Colmenares 2016
. Adapting it to our problem, it is a normal distribution with mean
\left<q(t)\right>
and variance
\sigma^{2}(t)
given by

\displaystyle P(q,t|q_{{}_{\mathrm{0}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,\sigma^{2}(t)}}\exp\left[-\frac{(q-\left<q(t)\right>)^{2}}{2\,\sigma^{2}(t)}\right],

(10)

\displaystyle\left<q(t)\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{{}_{\mathrm{0}}}\,\left(1-\Omega\,\int_{0}^{t}dy\,y\,\chi(t-y)\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}\,T}{M}\Bigg[2\int_{0}^{t}dy\int_{0}^{y}dz\,\left<\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(y)\,\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(z)\right>

(12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi(y)\right)^{2}\Bigg],

(13)

\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(t)
, is written as

\displaystyle\left<\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(y)\,\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(z)\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}dy^{\prime}\int_{0}^{z}dz^{\prime}\,\chi(y-y^{\prime})\,\chi(z-z^{\prime})

(14)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left<R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(y^{\prime}-z^{\prime})\,R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\Omega}}(0)\right>,

\displaystyle\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}\,T}{M}\bigg[\chi(\mid\!t\!-s\!\mid)-\chi(t)\,\chi(s)\bigg],

(15)

The next step is to devise an inverse process consisting of deriving the FPE whose solution is the PDF mentioned above. It is achieved by the general method originally designed by Adelman and Garrison
Adelman and Garrison 1977

Colmenares 2018

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P(q,t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial J(q,t)}{\partial q},

(16)

\displaystyle J(q,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\Phi(t)\,q-\frac{1}{2}D(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\right]P(q,t),

(17)

\displaystyle\Phi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln\left<q(t)\right>}{dt},

(18)

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}^{2}(t)-2\,\sigma^{2}(t)\,\Phi(t),

(19)

where
D(t)
is the time-dependent diffusion coefficient (TDDC).

The structure of the FPE for the GLE when the field-bath interaction is off remains the same as Eq. (
17

Since the Gaussian is a well-defined function in terms of the model parameters, various thermodynamic properties, such as heat, work, and entropy, can be determined.

However, we are interested in a process where mechanical work appears as a result of an external agent. Unlike changing the frequency of the field, in which the particle is trapped, it is proposed that the tweezers are displaced according to

V(q,t)=M\omega^{2}\big(q-\eta(t)\big)^{2}/2,

(20)

where
\eta(t)
is the protocol.

III
Isothermal protocol

This section summarizes the result obtained by the author in Ref.
Colmenares 2024
for the “sliding” potential given by Eq. (
20
). The set of equations is useful to discuss the adiabatic protocol to be derived in the next section.

Repeating the above procedure for this time-dependent potential, the integration of the GLE becomes

\displaystyle q(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{q}(t)+\varphi_{\mathrm{q}}(t),

(21)

\displaystyle\bar{q}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<q(t)\right>+\omega^{2}\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(t-y)\,\eta(y),

(22)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi(y),

(23)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{q}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\varphi_{\mathrm{v}}(y).

(24)

The mechanical work is defined as
Esposito and Mukamel 2006

\displaystyle W(t_{\mathrm{f}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{f}}}dt\,\bigg<\frac{\partial\,\mathcal{H}_{{}_{S}}(t)}{\partial\,t}\bigg>,

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\,\omega^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{f}}}dt\,\dot{\eta}(t)\,\big(\eta(t)-\bar{q}(t)\big),

(26)

where
t_{\mathrm{f}}
is the final application time of the driving. However, we are interested in the optimal protocol that generates the maximum work in the system. Since the particle Hamiltonian is
\mathcal{H}_{{}_{S}}(t)=p^{2}/2M+V(q,t))
, then, using the Euler-Lagrange formalism, the optimal protocol obeys a Fredholm integral equation of the second kind

\displaystyle\eta(t)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{f}}}dy\,F(t,y)\,\eta(y)=G(t),

(27)

where for a given final value of the protocol
\eta_{{}_{\!f}}

\displaystyle F(t,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{\widehat{g}(s,y)}{s\,(\widehat{f}_{{}_{\!1}}(s)-\widehat{\chi}_{\mathrm{q}}(s))}\right\},

(28)

\displaystyle G(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{-\frac{\widehat{H}(s)}{s\,(\widehat{f}_{{}_{\!1}}(s)-\widehat{\chi}_{\mathrm{q}}(s))}\right\},

(29)

\displaystyle\widehat{H}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{{}_{\!f}}\widehat{f}_{{}_{\!2}}(s)+\frac{q_{{}_{\mathrm{0}}}\,\Omega}{\omega^{2}\,s}\,\widehat{\chi}(s),

(30)

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(s,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\big\{\partial_{y}\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(y-t)\big\},

(31)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{{}_{\!1}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\big\{\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(-t)\big\},

(32)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{{}_{\!2}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\big\{\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(t_{\mathrm{f}}-t)\big\}.

(33)

The corresponding PDF is obtained by replacing
\left<q(t)\right>
in Eqs. (
10
) and (
18
by Eq. (
22
).

The temperature is introduced through
\sigma^{2}(t)
given by Eq. (
13
). Note that
W(t_{\mathrm{f}})
can be calculated by choosing an arbitrary protocol or the optimized one, instead.

IV
Adiabatic protocol

Inspired by Ref.
Bo and Celani 2013
, we write the mean heat
\left<Q(t)\right>
given by
Sekimoto 2010
;
Seifert 2005
;
Seifert 2012

\displaystyle\frac{d\left<Q(t)\right>}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dq\,J(q,y)\,\frac{dE}{dq},

(34)

where
E=(p^{2}/(2\,M)+M\,\Omega\,\big(q-\eta(t)^{2}\big)/2
. After making the substitutions, we find
Colmenares 2024

\left<Q(t_{\mathrm{f}})\right>=M\,\Omega\!\!\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{f}}}\!\!\!dt\left[\Phi(t)\left(\sigma^{2}(t)\!+\!\bar{q}^{2}(t)\right)\!+\!\frac{1}{2}D(t)\right].

(35)

An adiabatic process will change the initial equilibrium temperature and requires the integrand of the preceding equation to vanish. Using Eq. (
22
) into the integrand of Eq. (
35
), with the subscript “ad” reffering to adiabatic, and defining

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(t-y)\,\eta_{\mathrm{ad}}(y),

(36)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\omega^{2}}\left<q(t)\right>,

(37)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega^{4}}\bigg[\frac{D(t)}{2\,\Phi(t)}+\left<q^{2}(t)\right>\bigg],

(38)

\displaystyle\Psi_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Psi_{1}^{2}(t)-4\,\Psi_{2}(t)},

(39)

the integrand of Eq. (
35
) reduces to the quadratic equation
A^{2}(t)+\Psi_{1}(t)A(t)+\Psi_{2}(t)=0
, whose solution reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(t-y)\,\eta_{\mathrm{ad}}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg(-\Psi_{1}(t)\pm\Psi_{3}(t)\bigg).

(40)

Taking the Laplace transform and inverting, we get

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{ad}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dy\,\chi_{\mathrm{\!ad}}(t-y)\bigg(-\Psi_{1}(y)\pm\Psi_{3}(y)\bigg),

(41)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{\!ad}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{2\,\widehat{\chi}_{\mathrm{q}}(s)}\right\},

(42)

which can be substituted into Eq. (
26
) to obtain the irreversible adiabatic mechanical work. The
\pm
sign indicates the protocol is implicitly dependent on the final temperature
T_{f}
reached in the heating/cooling process. Since
\left<\Delta E\right>=k_{\mathrm{B}}\,(T_{f}-T)=W(t_{\mathrm{f}})
,
T_{f}
is easily calculated. However, because the final temperature is usually fixed, then
t_{\mathrm{f}}
must also be found consistently. This is determined from Eq. (
26
) such that the fixed
\left<\Delta E\right>
is satisfied for
\eta_{ad}(t)
.
Unlike an isothermal process, where the protocol can be optimized, the adiabaticity of the system restricts the use of arbitrary protocols in calculating
W(t_{\mathrm{f}})
to those satisfying Eq. (
41
). There is no other way to naturally obtain
T_{f}
in the process; therefore, the protocol is automatically optimized, as well as the irreversible work.

V
General remarks

Using solely thermodynamic arguments, the employed methodology guarantees a self-contained theory for adiabatic processes without considering heat leaks in the description. The method also prevents the inclusion of extra parameters other than those that naturally control the dynamics.

As a consequence of the above, the adiabatic protocol is automatically optimized, depending solely on the evolution of properties characteristic of the dynamics itself.

Although this proposal has been derived from the modified GLE
Colmenares 2023
, it can be extended to the underdamped and overdamped versions of the classical Langevin equation with the proper modifications.

Note that for the breathing potential
V(q,t)=M\,\eta_{\omega}(t)\,q^{2}
, where the time-varying frequency defines the protocol
\eta_{\omega}(t)
, the integrand of Eq. (
35
) has the commom factor
\eta_{\omega}(t)
, instead of
\Omega
. Thus, another way to determine the desired protocol without the heat leak mentioned in Refs.
Schmiedl and Seifert 2008
;
Bo and Celani 2013
;
Martínez et al. 2015b
would be based on deriving the appropriate GLE for the problem, the moments of the new position PDF, and following the procedure above.

Further work would be to use this proposal to determine the efficiency of an irreversible Carnot-like engine.

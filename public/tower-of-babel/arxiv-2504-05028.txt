A note on the Lorentzian splitting theorem
Gregory J. Galloway

Abstract
We present a version of the Lorentzian splitting theorem under a weakened Ricci curvature condition. The proof makes use of basic properties of achronal limits [ 19 , 20 ] , together with the geometric maximum principle for C 0 C^{0} spacelike hypersurfaces in [ 1 ] . Our version strengthens a related result in [ 29 ] in the globally hyperbolic setting by removing a certain boundedness condition on the Ricci curvature.

Introduction

In his well-known problem section from the early 1980’s
[
28
]
, S.-T. Yau posed the problem of establishing a Lorentzian analogue of the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem. This problem was completely resolved by the end of the 1980’s in a series of papers
[
4
,
11
,
15
,
24
]
. The most basic version, due to Eschenburg,
[
11
]
, is as follows.

Theorem 1

(Lorentzian Splitting Theorem)
.

Let
M
be a globally hyperbolic, timelike geodesically complete spacetime, satisfying the strong energy condition,
\mathrm{Ric}(X,X)\geq 0
, for all timelike
X
. If
M
admits a timelike line, then
M
splits as a metric product, i.e.

(M,g)
is isometric to
({\mathbb{R}}\times S,-dt^{2}+h)
where
S
is a smooth, geodesically complete, spacelike hypersurface with induced metric
h
.

The Lorentzian splitting problem posed by Yau was in fact motivated by his idea to establish the
rigidity
of the Hawking-Penrose singularity (incompleteness) theorems. This viewpoint became formalized in a conjecture of R. Bartnik
[
2
]
.

Conjecture 2

(Bartnik Splitting Conjecture)
.

Suppose that
M
is a globally hyperbolic spacetime, with compact Cauchy surfaces, which satisfies the strong energy condition. If
M
is timelike geodesically complete, then

M
splits, i.e.
(M,g)
is isometric to

({\mathbb{R}}\times S,-dt^{2}+h)
, where
S
is a smooth spacelike Cauchy hypersurface, with induced metric
h
.

Thus, according to the conjecture, such spacetimes can fail to be singular (timelike geodesically incomplete) only under very special circumstances. To establish the conjecture it is sufficient to establish the existence of a timelike line or a CMC Cauchy surface. In the former case, one can apply the Lorentzian splitting theorem, whereas in the latter case, one can apply Corollary 1 in
[
2
]
. There are various conditions that ensure either the existence of a timelike line or a CMC Cauchy surface. Nevertheless, the conjecture remains open; for further discussion, see e.g.,
[
16
,
18
,
20
]
.

Meanwhile, over the years various Hawking-Penrose type singularity results have been proven under weakened (or averaged) energy conditions; see e.g.
[
27
,
9
,
6
]
, and more recently
[
12
,
13
]
, and references therein. In this note we present a proof of the Lorentzian splitting theorem under a weakening of the strong energy condition, as discussed in the next section.
We make use of the properties of
achronal limits

[
19
,
20
]
, as they enable certain simplifications and allow much of the proof to remain at a basic causal-theoretic level.
These properties
have been developed for globally hyperbolic spacetimes. For comments regarding the non-globally hyperbolic case, see the first remark at the end of Section 2.

A Lorentzian splitting theorem

We briefly recall some basic notions and facts
from the causal theory of spacetime. See e.g.
[

By a spacetime we mean a smooth time oriented Lorentzian manifold

Since small

More generally, for

J^{+}(A)

of points

Note that

are open. The timelike and causal pasts
I^{-}(p)
,
J^{-}(p)
,

I^{-}(A)

An

is a non-empty set of the form

(

[

Classically, a spacetime
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic provided (i) it is strongly causal (i.e. there are no closed or ‘almost closed’ causal curves) and (ii) all ‘causal diamonds’

are compact. A Cauchy (hyper)surface for a spacetime
(M,g)
is a set

is necessarily an edgeless acausal

is homeomorphic to

. It is a basic fact that a spacetime

We will make use of the notion of a future (resp. past) ray. Let
\gamma:[0,a)\to M
,

, be a future inextendible causal curve.

Given a subset

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic, timelike geodesically complete spacetime, such that along each (past and future) timelike ray

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm Ric}(\alpha^{\prime}(t),\alpha^{\prime}(t))\,dt\geq 0\,.

A related result was obtained in

Let

Theorem 6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic, future timelike geodesically complete spacetime, and let

be a locally achronal

hypersurface. Suppose at each point

, there exists a future complete timelike

\alpha:[0,\infty)\to M

has mean curvature

Proof.

Fix
p\in S
and a future complete unit speed timelike
S
-ray
\alpha

\alpha(0)=p
.
Since for any

\alpha|_{[0,t]}
maximizes distance to
S

S^{-}_{t}(\alpha(t))
lies locally to the past of
S
near
q

Further, since there are no cut points to
\alpha(t)
along
\alpha|_{[0,t]}

S^{-}_{t-r}(\alpha(t))
,

\alpha(r)

S^{-}_{t}(\alpha(t))
is a past support hypersurface for
S

p\in S
.

The theorem is a consequence of the following claim.

Claim:
For any
\epsilon>0
there exists
T>0
such that the distance sphere
S^{-}_{T}(\alpha(T))
has mean curvature
H\geq-\epsilon
at
p
.

Proof of the claim (cf.
[
12
]
).

Suppose not; i.e. suppose there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all arbitrarily large

,
H<-\epsilon
at
p
.
By the curvature assumption, there exist

such that along
\alpha

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}{\rm Ric}(\alpha^{\prime}(\hat{r}),\alpha^{\prime}(\hat{r}))d\hat{r}\geq-\epsilon\,,\quad\text{for all }r\geq r_{0}\,.

(2.3)

We can assume that
T>r_{0}
.
Let
H(r)
be the mean curvature of the distance sphere
S^{-}_{T-r}(\alpha(T))
at

\alpha(r)
,

. By the Raychaudhuri equation (traced Riccati equation

\displaystyle\frac{dH}{dr}=-\left(\rho+\frac{H^{2}}{n}\right)

(2.4)

where

Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\rho(\hat{r})d\hat{r}\geq-\epsilon\,,\quad\text{for all }r\in[r_{0},T)\,.

Integrating (

\displaystyle-H(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{H^{2}}{n}d\hat{r}+\int_{0}^{r}\rho d\hat{r}-H(0)>\int_{0}^{r}\frac{H^{2}}{n}d\hat{r}\,,

Set

\displaystyle\frac{dQ}{dr}=\frac{H^{2}}{n}>\frac{Q^{2}}{n}\,\,\,(>0)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q(r_{0})}>\frac{1}{Q(r_{0})}-\frac{1}{Q(r)}>\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{1}{n}d\hat{r}=\frac{r-r_{0}}{n}\,.

By taking

Let the setting and notation be as in the statement and proof of Theorem
3
,
and let
V\subset S^{-}_{\infty}\cap S^{+}_{\infty}
be as defined in the proof.
We want to show that
V
is geodesicallly complete. Fix
p\in V
, and choose any
X\in T_{p}V
with
|X|=1
. Let
\sigma:[0,a)\to M
,
a\in(0,\infty]
, be the unique unit speed geodesic with
\sigma(0)=p
and
\sigma^{\prime}(0)=X
, which is maximally extended in the direction
X
in
M
. We first observe that
\sigma
does not leave
V
.
Because
V
is totally geodesic,
\sigma
is initially contained in
V
. Fix any
s_{0}\in(0,a)
with

\sigma([0,s_{0}))\subset V
. Again, since
V
is totally geodesic, the future unit normal field
u
of
V
is parallel along
\sigma|_{[0,s_{0})}
.
By Proposition
5
, there is a unique future timelike
S^{-}_{\infty}
-ray

\gamma_{x}
from each
x\in V
, with
\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(0)=u_{x}
.
Whether or not
q=\sigma(s_{0})
lies in
V
, there is a well-defined limit vector
u_{q}=\lim_{s\to s_{0}}u_{\sigma(s)}
, obtained by parallel transporting
u
on all of
\sigma_{[0,s_{0}]}
, with
u_{q}
necessarily future timelike. Let
\gamma_{q}
be the future-directed unit speed timelike geodesic with

\gamma_{q}^{\prime}(0)=u_{q}
, which is necessarily complete. Since
q\in\overline{V}\subset S^{-}_{\infty}\cap S^{+}_{\infty}
,
\gamma_{q}
is a curve from
S^{-}_{\infty}
. Moreover, since
\gamma_{\sigma(s)}
is an
S^{-}_{\infty}
-ray for all
s\in[0,s_{0})
, it follows that
\gamma_{q}|_{[0,\infty)}
is an
S^{-}_{\infty}
-ray, as well. Since
q\in S^{-}_{\infty}\cap S^{+}_{\infty}
, Proposition
5
implies that
q
is a spacelike point, and hence
q\in V
. This shows that
\sigma
can never leave
V
, i.e., we have
\sigma:[0,a)\to V
.

Now we show that
\sigma
is complete in the direction
X
, i.e. that
a=\infty
.
Suppose to the contrary that
a<\infty
. Then the curve
c(s)=\Phi(-2s,\sigma(s))
,

c:[0,b)\to W_{V}\subset M
,
is a past-directed timelike geodesic in
M
, and

\sigma(s)=\mathrm{exp}_{c(s)}(2s\Phi_{*}(\partial_{t}))
. By timelike completeness,
c
extends to
[0,a]
. Furthermore, the vector field
\Phi_{*}(\partial_{t})
is parallel in
W_{V}
, and hence, by parallel translating along
c
, has a limit at
c(a)
. Hence,

\sigma(s)=\mathrm{exp}_{c(s)}(2s\Phi_{*}(\partial_{t}))

has a limit as
s\to a
, i.e.,
\sigma
extends continuously, and hence as a geodesic to
[0,a]
, contrary to the definition of
a
.
Hence,
a=\infty
. Since
X
was an arbitrary unit vector at
p
in
V
, we have, in fact, shown that

\exp_{p}:T_{p}V\to V
is defined on all of
T_{p}V
. Thus by Hopf-Rinow,
V
is geodescially complete.

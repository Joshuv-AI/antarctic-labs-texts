Dataset from Scintrex CG-5 gravity meters acquired at the Zhetygen calibration line in Kazakhstan
Authors: Roman Sermiagin, Yeraly Kalen, Nurgan Kemerbayev, Khaini-Kamal Kassymkanova, Nikolay Kosarev, Guzyaliya Mussina, Assel Batalova
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The article provides a dataset derived from Scintrex CG-5 gravity meter observation files collected during five years of annual measurements along the Zhetygen calibration line utilizing three meters. Geoken, a Kazakhstani enterprise, routinely conducts these measurements to calibrate its meters necessary for manufacturing operations.Researchers can use this constantly updated dataset to study the behavior of the CG-5 gravity meters' calibration function in time and the measurement range. The measurement range is 100,900 µGal.

Background

For several years, Geoken, a geophysics firm based in Kazakhstan, has conducted annual calibrations of its gravity meters, assessing the parameters of its calibration function at the Zhetygen calibration line, about 20 km south of Almaty, Kazakhstan. All measurement data from the gravimeters is meticulously organized and compiled. This study presents findings from three Scintrex CG-5 gravity meters conducted between 2019 and 2023. Researchers investigating spring gravity meters, namely the Scintex CG-5 and CG-6 models, may consider this dataset useful.

The fundamental value of this data lies in the repeated annual measurements at the same stations, utilizing same meters and measurement techniques. This dataset offers a significant chance to examine the temporal fluctuations in the calibration function of the Scintrex CG-5 gravity meters.

Data Description

Calibration line

The Zhetygen calibration line is a set of special stations fixed on the ground, designed to perform measurements with different models of spring gravity meters, such as Scintrex CG-5, CG-6, LaCoste & Romberg, etc. The gravimetric stations are rectangular concrete piers with 1 × 1 m at the top for station № 1 and 2 × 1 m for all the other stations. The pedestals were dug into the ground to a depth of up to 2 m. For ease of work, covers like a bus station are built over the pedestals to protect the meters and operators from precipitation and wind gusts. Operators can install up to 6 gravity meters simultaneously.

The stations are located from north to south between the Kapchagay reservoir and Almaty. The total length of the line is 30 km, with a height difference of 105 m, corresponding to a change in gravity in this area of approximately 100 mGal. File format

Files can be obtained from CG-5 gravity meters in several formats: a binary file in the GeoSoft software format and two versions of a text table — with and without information headers. We present the data in the standard Scintrex CG-5 text format with headers (a file fragment is in Appendix A).

Appendix BTable 4 describes the file headers and columns. The first two columns depend on the Station Designation System → System (see Table 2). They can also display coordinates in the Universal Transverse Mercator projection or latitude and longitude. With the appropriate settings, the column names will change. The third column, ALT., is also customizable and can display Altitude or, if the Options → Record Amb. Temp option is enabled, and the ambient temperature (in this case, the column name does not change).

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Measurement technique

We used a step method (double loop) to account for the meter drift, performing measurements at each point at least three times in the sequence a – b – a – b – c – b – c – … (see Fig. 2). This approach allows for the evaluation of short-period drift. In this case, we performed measurements in both the forward and reverse directions, allowing for the review of long-period drift based on repeated observations at the initial (first) station [2].Fig. 2Stepwise measurement method with a relative gravimeter.Fig. 2

Stepwise measurement method with a relative gravimeter.

Operators performed 4 to 10 measurements (readings) at the station each time. Each reading is the mean of a 6 Hz signal over a time corresponding to the Read Time setting (see Table 2). The # Of Cycles parameter determines the number of mean records.

In addition to the calibration line stations, operators also performed control measurements at station No. 10 in the Geoken LLP laboratory. These measurements were not used to evaluate the calibration function but can also be used to estimate the long-term drift.

Correcting data files

The original dataset contains numerous errors related to inaccurate station name entries. The operator consistently neglected to alter the name when transitioning to the subsequent station. Manually identifying such problems would require examining all 10,420 records over 55 files. To partially automate this process, similar inaccuracies were detected through residuals in the adjustment procedure [3].

All issues about erroneous station names have been fully resolved. We developed the design matrix using the “get_dummies” function from the Pandas Python module to the column containing station names for each measurement (reading) to achieve this. The system was resolved to utilize the classical least squares method with the “OLS” model from the “statsmodels” package. Consequently, if the station name is erroneous, the residuals associated with the wrong station name will deviate significantly from the mean level of residuals by several orders of magnitude. The upper plot illustrates evident outliers for gravity meter #49459. Following multiple cycles, all incorrect station names were located and rectified.

Limitations

The primary and only limitation of the Zhetygen calibration line is the lack of absolute gravity values at the stations and the middle range of change in gravity.

These limitations are extreme only if the gravity meters are used to develop gravimetric networks related to gravimetric systems of different states, for example. The first factor leads to different scaling, and the second one limits the use of devices on the calibration line, i.e., the calibration functions obtained on it cannot be used for extrapolation.

The limitations of the presented dataset are directly related to uncertainties arising from the influence of external conditions and the meterʼs physical properties. The influence of external conditions is well described, for example, in [2]. We will note only two factors: the influence of microseisms and the change in gravitational potential associated with the change in groundwater levels.

The influence of microseismic vibrations on the earthʼs surface is random and easily overcome by increasing the number of measurements. Since the recording frequency of the CG-5 gravity meter is 6 Hz, and the averaging in this observation program for one reading is 40 s, the standard deviations of individual readings are from 5 to 100 µGal, and the average value is 30 µGal, which is quite acceptable. The influence of groundwater is systematic and difficult to predict without additional research, such as drilling boreholes near gravity stations to measure the groundwater level during the measurements. It is an expensive and time-consuming process. The influence of the physical properties of the gravity meter sensor causes nonlinearity of the meter drift, which can lead to significant systematic errors. The step method in the presented dataset allows us to approximate the drift function by a polynomial with degree n>1. Usually, degree n=2 is used for approximation. The nonlinearity of the meter drift is visible from the residuals in plot 3 for gravity meter No. 40824. Also, this plot highlights the sequences of readings in the form of a curve resembling a hyperbola. This effect is most likely associated with the sensitivity of this meter to the influence of tilts during transportation. There is a detailed study on this effect in [8].

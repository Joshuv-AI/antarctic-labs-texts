Extending the Science Fiction and the Loehr–Warrington formula
Donghyun Kim, Jaeseong Oh

Abstract
. We introduce the Macdonald piece polynomial I μ , λ , k ⁡ [ X ; q , t ] \I_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t] , which is a vast generalization of the Macdonald intersection polynomial in the science fiction conjecture by Bergeron and Garsia. We demonstrate a remarkable connection between I μ , λ , k \I_{\mu,\lambda,k} , ∇ s λ \nabla s_{\lambda} , and the Loehr–Warrington formula LW λ \LW_{\lambda} , thereby obtaining the Loehr–Warrington conjecture as a corollary. To connect I μ , λ , k \I_{\mu,\lambda,k} and ∇ s λ \nabla s_{\lambda} , we employ the plethystic formula for the Macdonald polynomials of Garsia–Haiman–Tesler, and to connect I μ , λ , k \I_{\mu,\lambda,k} and LW λ \LW_{\lambda} , we use our new findings on the combinatorics of P P -tableaux together with the column exchange rule. We also present an extension of the science fiction conjecture and the Macdonald positivity by exploiting I μ , λ , k \I_{\mu,\lambda,k} .



1.
Introduction

In an effort to extend the science fiction conjecture by
[
BG99
]
, we introduce the Macdonald piece polynomial
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
, which possesses remarkable properties. To provide a comprehensive overview of the primary results, we discuss the historical context and background of Macdonald polynomials, the Loehr–Warrington conjecture, and the science fiction conjecture.

1.1.
Macdonald polynomials

The
Macdonald
P
polynomials
, denoted as
P_{\mu}[X;q,t]
, are symmetric functions over
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
which form a basis for the ring of symmetric functions. Macdonald introduced them in 1988
[
Mac88
]
as a unified generalization of two significant families of symmetric functions, the
Jack polynomials
and the
Hall–Littlewood polynomials
. Since then, Macdonald polynomials have been established as fundamental objects with deep connections across various areas of mathematics. These include their appearance and relationships with affine Hecke algebras, quantum groups, elliptic Hall algebras, Hilbert scheme, affine Springer fiber, the Calogero–Sutherland model, diagonal coinvariants, and combinatorics.

An important question posed by Macdonald was the
Macdonald positivity conjecture
. This conjecture asserts that the
modified Macdonald polynomial
, defined through a plethystic substitution to the
Macdonald
J
polynomial
in
[
Mac88
]
,

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}[X;q,t]=t^{n(\mu)}J_{\mu}\left[\frac{X}{1-t^{-1}};q,t^{-1}\right],

is Schur positive. To address this conjecture, Garsia and Haiman introduced the bigraded
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
module
R_{\mu}
in 1993, now called the
Garsia–Haiman module
. This module is associated with a partition
\mu\vdash n
and is defined as the subspace of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
of
2n
variables, spanned by the partial derivatives of the
generalized Vandermonde determinant

\Delta_{\mu}\coloneqq\det\left(x_{k}^{i-1}y_{k}^{j-1}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
(i,j)\in\mu\end{subarray}}

associated with
\mu

[
GH93
,
GH96
]
.

In his groundbreaking work
[
Hai01
]
, Haiman utilized the geometry of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the plane
\mathbb{C}^{2}
to prove that

(1.1)

\mathrm{grFrob}\left(R_{\mu};q,t\right)=\widetilde{H}_{\mu}[X;q,t],

thereby settling the Macdonald positivity conjecture. Another consequence of Haiman’s result is the
n!
theorem
, which states that

(1.2)

\dim\left(R_{\mu}\right)=n!.

Despite Haiman’s resolution of Schur positivity, an explicit combinatorial formula for the Schur coefficients remains elusive.

Problem 1.1
.

Find a combinatorial description for the Schur coefficient
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)
of the Macdonald polynomials:

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}[X;q,t]=\sum_{\lambda}\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)s_{\lambda},

where
s_{\lambda}
is the Schur function.

This open problem is one of the most fundamental and challenging questions in the theory of Macdonald polynomials and has been a driving force behind many major breakthroughs. One such advancement is the Haglund–Haiman–Loehr formula
[
HHL05a
]
, which provides an explicit monomial expansion for the Macdonald polynomials in terms of words and two statistics,
\mathrm{inv}_{\mu}
and
\mathrm{maj}_{\mu}
(see Theorem
5.1
).

1.2.
Extending science fiction conjecture

One possible approach to resolving Problem
1.1
is to decompose
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}
into pieces that are Schur positive, and then find a combinatorial description of the Schur coefficients for each piece. The celebrated HHL formula breaks down
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}
into LLT polynomials, which are known to be Schur positive
[
GH07
]
. However, describing the Schur coefficients of LLT polynomials remains a challenging open problem. Another attempt involved using
k
-Schur functions
[
LLM03
]
, whose Schur coefficients were explicitly described in
[
BMPS19
]
. However, it is still unclear whether
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}
can be expressed positively in terms of
k
-Schur functions. In this paper, we introduce an alternative way to decompose
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}
, generalizing the way proposed in the science fiction conjecture.

Bergeron and Garsia studied the Garsia–-Haiman modules using inductive and combinatorial methods
[
BG99
]
. They introduced a collection of conjectures referred to as the
science fiction conjecture
, which include the existence of a certain basis for Garsia–Haiman modules and the elegant properties of those bases while intersecting Garsia–Haiman modules. To elaborate on the science fiction conjecture, let
\mu
be a partition with
n
(removable) corners. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\mu^{(i)}
denote the partition obtained from
\mu
by removing the
i
-th corner. The science fiction conjecture then implies the following two assertions, which are analogous to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively:

(1)

The bigraded Frobenius characteristic of the intersection of the modules
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}R_{\mu^{(i)}}
is given by

(1.3)

\grFrob\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}R_{\mu^{(i)}};q,t\right)=\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(k)}}[X;q,t]\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\dfrac{T_{\mu^{(j)}}}{T_{\mu^{(j)}}-T_{\mu^{(i)}}}\right)\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{(i)}}[X;q,t],

(2)

the dimension of the intersection
\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}R_{\mu^{(i)}}
is given by

(1.4)

\dim\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}R_{\mu^{(i)}}\right)=\frac{n!}{k}.

Here,
T_{\mu}\coloneqq\prod_{(i,j)\in\mu}t^{i-1}q^{j-1}
. We refer to the symmetric function
\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(k)}}
in (
1.3
) as the
Macdonald intersection polynomial
, and the second part as the
n!/k
conjecture
. The Macdonald intersection polynomial naturally refines the usual Macdonald polynomials. As such, the combinatorics of Macdonald intersection polynomials may lead to progress on Problem
1.1
. The authors, along with Seung Jin Lee, recently provided an explicit positive monomial expansion for the Macdonald intersection polynomials for two partitions
[
KLO22
]
, refining the celebrated HHL formula and providing a step toward Butler’s conjecture
[
But94
]
.

In this paper, we further generalize the science fiction conjecture. Especially, we generalize the Macdonald intersection polynomial in (
1.3
) and the
n!/k
conjecture (
1.4
). To be precise, like in the science fiction conjecture, let
\mu
be a partition with
n
removable corners
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
and let
1\leq k\leq n-1
. For a subset
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
of
[n]
of size
k
, let
\mu^{S}
be the partition obtained from
\mu
by deleting corners
\{c_{i}:i\in S\}
. Let
R_{n,k}:=((n-k)^{k})
be the rectangular partition, and let
\lambda
be a partition inside
R_{n,k}
. We define the
Macdonald piece polynomial

\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
indexed by
\mu
,
k
, and
\lambda
as

(1.5)

\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t]:=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\dfrac{s_{\lambda}[z_{S}]\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}[X].

Here, we let
z_{i}=T_{\mu^{\{i\}}}/T_{\mu}=q^{-\coarm(c_{i})}t^{-\coleg(c_{i})}
and
S^{c}=\{1,2,\,\dots,n\}\setminus S
. Additionally,
f[z_{S}]
denotes the polynomial
f
in the
z_{i}
’s where
i
runs through
S
. For
k=1
and the empty partition
\lambda=\emptyset
,
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
recovers the Macdonald intersection polynomial
\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(n)}}
.

In Section
2
, using the novel symmetric function
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
, we define another symmetric function
\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}^{\lambda}
(See (
2.2
)). We conjecture that it is the graded Frobenius characteristic of the intersection of certain Garsia–Haiman modules (see Conjecture
2.3
), largely generalizing the science fiction conjecture given in (
1.3
). In the appendix
A
, as a counterpart for the
n!/k
conjecture (
1.4
), numbers corresponding to each partition and their conjectural properties are outlined.

In addition to its crucial role in generalizing the science fiction conjecture, Macdonald piece polynomial
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
enjoys a remarkable property. We unveil a surprising connection with
\nabla s_{\lambda}
and the Loehr–Warrington formula
\LW_{\lambda}
, where the Loehr–Warrington conjecture follows as a corollary. We first give a background of the Loehr–Warrington conjecture.

1.3.
The Loehr–Warrington conjecture

The theory of Macdonald polynomials has seen significant advancement due to its deep connections with algebraic geometry, operators on symmetric functions, and Catalan combinatorics. A key result that exemplifies these connections is Haiman’s
(n+1)^{n-1}
theorem. By leveraging the geometric structure of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the plane
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, Haiman proved that the dimension of the
diagonal coinvariant ring
,

\DR_{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]/\langle\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{+}\rangle,

is
(n+1)^{n-1}
. Furthermore, he established that the bigraded Frobenius characteristic of this ring coincides with the symmetric function
\nabla e_{n}
, where
\nabla
is the eigenoperator on Macdonald polynomials defined by
\nabla\widetilde{H}_{\mu}=T_{\mu}\widetilde{H}_{\mu}
, and
e_{n}
is the elementary symmetric function.

The celebrated
Shuffle Theorem
, conjectured in
[
HHL+05b
]
and proved in
[
CM18
]
, further underscores the interplay between Macdonald polynomials, operators on symmetric functions, and Catalan combinatorics. This theorem provides an explicit combinatorial expansion for
\nabla e_{n}
in terms of the combinatorics of labeled Dyck paths, showcasing the rich combinatorial structure. In an earlier work, the authors, along with Seung Jin Lee, offered a new proof of the shuffle theorem by connecting the Macdonald intersection polynomial
\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(n)}}
to
\nabla e_{n-1}

[
KLO23
]
.

Over the past two decades, the shuffle theorem has been extended the rational shuffle theorem
[
Mel21
]
and the Delta theorem
[
DM22
,
BHM+23
]
. The Loehr–Warrington conjecture
[
LW08
]
, the final outstanding conjecture in this series, was very recently proved in
[
BHM+21
]
, providing a combinatorial formula for
\nabla s_{\lambda}
in terms of
nested Dyck paths
. We use another formulation expressing the combinatorial formula in the Loehr–Warrington conjecture in terms of certain
P
-tableaux

[
Ges84
]
:

\LW_{\lambda}:=q^{\adj(\lambda)}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)}q^{\dinv(T)}t^{\area(T)}x^{T}.

See Section
4.1
for precise definition for the set
\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
of certain
P
-tableaux, and the statistics
\dinv
and
\area
. It turns out the combinatorics of
P
-tableau beautifully match with the combinatorics of Macdonald piece polynomials.

1.4.
Main results

Before stating our main results, we introduce a notation that we will use throughout this paper. For a partition
\lambda
inside a rectangle
R(n,k)
, we define
\tilde{\lambda}
to be a conjugate partition of
(n-k-\lambda_{k},\dots,n-k-\lambda_{1})
where we regard
\lambda_{i}=0
if
i>\ell(\lambda)
. The notation
\tilde{\lambda}
is dependent on an ambient rectangle that
\lambda
lives in, which will be obvious from the context.

Now we state our main results. Note that the straightforward implication of Theorem
1.2
(b) and (c) is the Loehr–Warrington conjecture (Corollary
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a partition with
n
corners, and
1\leq k<n
. Then for a partition
\lambda
inside a rectangle
R(n,k)
the symmetric function
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
satisfies the following:

(a)

For
N>|\mu|-|\tilde{\lambda}|-k
, we have

(1.6)

e_{N}^{\perp}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}=0.

(b)

We have

(1.7)

\dfrac{1}{T_{\mu^{[n]}}}e_{|\mu|-|\tilde{\lambda}|-k}^{\perp}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}=\nabla s_{\tilde{\lambda}}.

In particular,
e_{|\mu|+|\tilde{\lambda}|-k}^{\perp}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
does not depend on the partition
\mu
(up to a constant).

(c)

We have

(1.8)

\dfrac{1}{T_{\mu^{[n]}}}e_{|\mu|-|\tilde{\lambda}|-k}^{\perp}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}=(-1)^{\adj(\tilde{\lambda})}\LW_{\tilde{\lambda}}.

Here,
e_{n}^{\perp}
denotes the adjoint operator to the multiplication by
e_{n}
with respect to the Hall inner product. The precise definitions of the Loehr-Warrington formula
\LW_{\lambda}
and
\adj(\lambda)
will be given in Section 4.

Corollary 1.3
.

(The Loehr–Warrington conjecture)
We have
\nabla s_{\lambda}=(-1)^{\adj(\lambda)}\LW_{\lambda}
.

We conjecture that
(-1)^{\adj(\tilde{\lambda})}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
is Schur positive. We do not know the monomial positivity either, and Theorem
1.2
(c) can be thought of as supporting evidence. Finding a combinatorial description of the monomial expansion for
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
is a challenging open problem, as one would need to discover an ambient formula that includes the Loehr–Warrington formula as a small portion.

We also study the specialization of the Macdonald piece polynomials at
q=t=1
. In particular, we describe the
h
-expansion in terms of

h_{\mu}
in the

-expansion of
\nabla s_{\lambda}|_{q,t=1}
. In
[
KLO23
]
, the authors proved that
W_{(1^{n}),\mu}
is given by the Kreweras number, but we do not yet have a combinatorial description of

in general beyond the case
\lambda=(1^{n})
. Now, we state a vast generalization of
[
KLO23
, Theorem 1.3]
.

.

We have

\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;1,1]=\sum_{\nu\subseteq\lambda}(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\nu|}d^{(k)}_{\lambda,\nu}\sum_{\tau}W_{\tilde{\nu},\tau}h_{(\tau+1^{|\mu|-k-|\tilde{v}|})}

and
\tilde{\nu}

R(n,k)

2.
Extending the Science Fiction conjecture with Macdonald piece polynomials

2.1.
Preliminaries

A partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers. We say that
\mu
is a partition of
n
, denoted by
\mu\vdash n
, if
|\mu|:=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=n
. We denote the length of
\mu
, i.e., the number of its parts, by
\ell(\mu)
. We use
Young diagrams
in French notation to display a partition as

\mu=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}:j\leq\mu_{i}\},

where each element of this set represents a
cell
in the diagram. The
conjugate partition
of
\mu
, denoted by
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\mu^{\prime}_{2},\dots)
, is obtained by reflecting the Young diagram of
\mu
along the diagonal
y=x
. We denote the empty partition by
\emptyset
.

For a cell
u
in a partition
\mu
, we define the
arm
(
coarm
) of
u
, denoted by
\arm_{\mu}(u)
(
\coarm_{\mu}(u)
), as the number of cells strictly to the right (left) of
u
in the same row. Similarly, we define the
leg
(
coleg
) of
u
, denoted by
\leg_{\mu}(u)
(
\coleg_{\mu}(u)
), as the number of cells strictly above (below)
u
in the same column.

A
diagram
is a collection of cells in the first quadrant. A partition can be considered as a left-justified diagram, where the number of cells in each row is weakly decreasing from bottom to top. We denote a cell in a diagram in the
i
-th row, and
j
-th column by
(i,j)
. We usually represent a diagram by

D=[D^{(1)},D^{(2)},\dots],

where
D^{(j)}=\{i:(i,j)\in D\}
is the row numbers of the
j
-th column of
D
.
For a diagram
D
, the
size

|D|
is defined as the number of cells in
D
.

Let
\mathbf{Sym}
be the graded ring of symmetric functions in infinite variables
X=x_{1},x_{2},\dots
over the ground field
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
. We use the notations from
[
Mac88
]
for families of symmetric functions indexed by a partition:
m_{\lambda}
for the
monomial symmetric function
,
h_{\lambda}
for the
homogeneous symmetric function
,
e_{\lambda}
for the
elementary symmetric function
,
p_{\lambda}
for the
power sum symmetric function
,
s_{\lambda}
for the
Schur function
,
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
for the
(modified) Macdonald polynomial
. The (Hall) inner product on symmetric functions, denoted by
\langle-,-\rangle
, is defined as
\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}.

2.2.
Extending the science fiction conjecture and the Macdonald positivity

We fix a partition
\mu
with
n
corners
\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}\}
and let
\mu^{(i)}
be a partition obtained by removing a corner
c_{i}
. Recall that the science fiction conjecture suggests that

(2.1)

\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(n)}}[X;q,t]:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\dfrac{z_{j}}{z_{j}-z_{i}}\right)\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{(i)}}=\grFrob\left(\cap_{i=1}^{n}R_{\mu^{(i)}}\right),

where
z_{i}=T_{\mu^{(i)}}/T_{\mu}
.
As mentioned before,
\I_{\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(n)}}[X;q,t]
is a special case of the Macdonald piece polynomial
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
obtained by letting
\lambda=\emptyset
and
k=1
. Our goal is to further generalize (
2.1
) and refine the Macdonald positivity as well (Conjecture
2.3
).

Now we consider partitions
\mu^{S}=\mu\setminus\{c_{i}:i\in S\}
for
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
. Note that
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
can be regarded as a linear combination of
\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}
. We present the converse by representing each
\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}
as a linear combination of
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq R(n,k)}\frac{s_{\tilde{\lambda}}[z_{S^{c}}]}{\prod_{j\in S^{c}}z_{i}}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}

where
z_{i}=T_{\mu_{\{i\}}}/T_{\mu}=q^{-\coarm(c_{i})}t^{-\coleg(c_{i})}
.

Proof.

Consider a matrix
M
whose rows are indexed by
\lambda\subseteq R(n,k)
and columns are indexed by
S\in\binom{n}{k}
. Its entries are given by

M_{\lambda,S}=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\dfrac{s_{\lambda}[z_{S}]\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}.

Then let
M^{\prime}
be a matrix whose columns are indexed by
\lambda\subseteq R(n,k)
and rows are indexed by
S\in\binom{n}{k}
. Its entries are given by

M^{\prime}_{S,\lambda}=\frac{s_{\tilde{\lambda}}[z_{S^{c}}]}{\prod_{j\in S^{c}}z_{i}}.

By Lemma
3.2
we have that
MM^{\prime}
equals the identity matrix.
∎

From now on we regard
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}

\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}[X;q,t,z]:=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq k\times(n-k)}\frac{s_{\tilde{\lambda}}[z_{S^{c}}]}{\prod_{j\in S^{c}}z_{i}}\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}.

\Phi

. By Lemma
2.1
, we trivially have
\Phi(\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}[X;q,t,z])=\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For

Conjecture 2.3

We have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be variables. Let
\lambda

. For a partition

Proof.

Recall Jacobi’s bi-alternant formula

s_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\dfrac{\displaystyle a_{\lambda+\st_{n}}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})}{\displaystyle a_{\st_{n}}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})},

for the Schur polynomials, where

is the alternating polynomial defined by

a_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\det\left(z_{j}^{\lambda_{i}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

In particular,

is the usual Vandermonde determinant. The left-hand side of (

(3.4)

\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\frac{s_{\lambda}[z_{S}]s_{\mu}[z_{S^{c}}]}{\prod_{i\in S,j\in S^{c}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}=\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\dfrac{a_{\lambda+\st_{k}}(z_{S})a_{\mu+\st_{n-k}}(z_{S^{c}})}{a_{\st_{k}}(z_{S})a_{\st_{n-k}}(z_{S^{c}})\prod_{i\in S,j\in S^{c}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}=\dfrac{\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\sgn(S)a_{\lambda+\st_{k}}(z_{S})a_{\mu+\st_{n-k}}(z_{S^{c}})}{a_{\st_{n}}(z_{[n]})}

where
\sgn(S)=(-1)^{(\sum_{r=k+1}^{n}r)-\sum_{s\in S}s}
.

The Laplace expansion of a determinant by complementary minors, we have

a_{(\lambda+\st_{k},\mu+\st_{n-k})}(z_{[n]})=\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\sgn(S)a_{\lambda+\st_{k}}(z_{S})a_{\mu+\st_{n-k}}(z_{S^{c}}).

By substituting this into (

\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\frac{s_{\lambda}[z_{S}]s_{\mu}[z_{S^{c}}]}{\prod_{i\in S,j\in S^{c}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}=\dfrac{a_{(\lambda+\st_{k},\mu+\st_{n-k})}(z_{[n]})}{a_{\st_{n}}(z_{[n]})}.

Since the numerator

is an alternating polynomial of degree

is exactly the staircase

Jacobi–Trudi type formula for the Loehr–Warrington formula

We provide a Jacobi–Trudi type formula for
\LW_{\lambda}
in terms of the operators
\mathfrak{h}_{m},\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
, defined below.

Definition 4.3
.

We define
\mathbf{C}_{m}
to be a set of all
\PP
-chains
\{(a_{1},b_{1})\prec_{\PP}(a_{2},b_{2})\prec_{\PP}\dots\prec_{\PP}(a_{m},b_{m})\}
where each
(a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then
\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}
(resp
\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}
) is a subset of
\mathbf{C}_{m}
consisting of elements satisfying
a_{1}=0
(resp
a_{1}>0
). Obviously,
\mathbf{C}_{m}=\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\cup\mkern-11.5mu\cdot\mkern 5.0mu\hskip 1.42271pt\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}
.

Let
\textbf{y}=\{y_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}
be a set of indeterminates . For a multiset
A
of elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
y^{A}
be the monomial whose exponent of
y_{i,j}
is given by the number of elements
(i,j)
in
A
. We define the operators
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
,
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
acting on a polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}[\textbf{y}]
(
\mathbb{F}
is a ground field containing
\mathbb{C}(q)
) by describing their actions on a monomial as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{m}\cdot y^{A}=\sum_{L\in\mathbf{C}_{m}}q^{\dinv(L,A)}y^{(L,A)},\quad\ \bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\cdot y^{A}=\sum_{L\in\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}}q^{\dinv(L,A)}y^{(L,A)},\quad\ \hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\cdot y^{A}=\sum_{L\in\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}}q^{\dinv(L,A)}y^{(L,A)},

where
y^{(L,A)}=y^{L}y^{A}
. We then extend this linearly to define operators
\mathfrak{h}_{n},\bar{h}_{n}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
. If
m<0
then they are all zero operators.

For example, we have

\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{3}\mathfrak{h}_{4}\cdot 1=\sum_{L\in\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{2}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{3}\times\mathbf{C}_{4}}q^{\dinv(L)}y^{L}

where
y^{L}=y^{L_{1}}y^{L_{2}}y^{L_{3}}
for
L=(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})
.

We say two operators are the same if they act on
\mathbb{F}[\textbf{y}]
in the same way. For example,
\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
, which is trivial from
\mathbf{C}_{m}=\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\cup\mkern-11.5mu\cdot\mkern 5.0mu\hskip 1.42271pt\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}
.

The operators
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
,
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
do not generally commute (for example, see Lemma
4.7
). Nevertheless, operators of the same type do commute (Corollary
4.5
). The following lemma, which is a step for proving Corollary
4.5
, is inspired by the symmetry of chromatic quasisymmetric functions. Though the proof parallels
[
SW16
, Lemma 4.4, Theorem 4.5]
using an involution on proper colorings (or
P
-tabloids), we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.4
.

For nonnegative integers
n,m
, there exists a bijection

\SW:\mathbf{C}_{n}\times\mathbf{C}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{m}\times\mathbf{C}_{n}

such that for
L\in\mathbf{C}_{n}\times\mathbf{C}_{m}
,
y^{L}=y^{\SW(L)}
and
\dinv(L)=\dinv(\SW(L))
.

Proof.

Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2})\in\mathbf{C}_{n}\times\mathbf{C}_{m}
. Consider a directed graph
G(L)
on the elements of
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
(with possible repeated entries), where edges are defined as
(a,b)\rightarrow(c,d)
for pairs satisfying
(a,b)<_{\text{lex}}(c,d)<_{\text{lex}}(a+1,b)
. Or equivalently, when
(a,b)<_{\text{lex}}(c,d)
and
(a,b)\nprec_{\PP}(c,d)
. It is straightforward to see that
G(L)
is bipartite since elements in
L_{1}
are not connected to each other, and the same applies to elements in
L_{2}
.

We claim that every connected component of
G(L)
is a directed path of the form
(a_{1},b_{1})<_{\text{lex}}(a_{2},b_{2})<_{\text{lex}}\cdots<_{\text{lex}}(a_{j},b_{j})
, where the membership of
(a_{i},b_{i})
alternates between
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. To prove the claim, we first show that each vertex has an out-degree at most one. Suppose not. Without loss of generality, assume
(a,b)\in L_{1}
has out-degree more than one. Then there exist
(c,d),(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in L_{2}
such that
(a,b)\rightarrow(c,d)
and
(a,b)\rightarrow(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
. Therefore, we have
(a,b)<_{\text{lex}}(c,d)<_{\text{lex}}(a+1,b)
and
(a,b)<_{\text{lex}}(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})<_{\text{lex}}(a+1,b)
This contradicts the fact that
(c,d)\in L_{2}
and
(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in L_{2}
are comparable in
\PP
. Similarly, each vertex has an in-degree at most one. Finally, there cannot be a directed cycle because every directed edge is increasing in terms of the
<_{\text{lex}}
ordering. Combining all these proves the claim.

For a connected component (path)
P
of
G(L)
, let

\SW(P):=\begin{cases}(P_{1},P_{2})\quad\text{ if }P\text{ has an odd number of vertices,}\\
(P_{2},P_{1})\quad\text{ if }P\text{ has an even number of vertices},\end{cases}

where
P_{1}=P\cap L_{1}
and
P_{2}=P\cap L_{2}
. Then define

\SW(L):=(\cup_{P}\SW(P)_{1},\cup_{P}\SW(P)_{2}),

where the union runs over all connected components of
G(L)
. The map
\SW
does not change the elements in
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
, thus
y^{L}=y^{\SW(L)}
. It is clear to see that
\SW(L)\in\mathbf{C}_{m}\times\mathbf{C}_{n}
and that
\SW
is involutive. Finally, note that
\dinv
counts the number of directed edges from an element in
L_{1}
to
L_{2}
. For each connected component
P
in
G(L)
, this number is preserved by
\SW
. Thus total
\dinv
statistic of
L
is preserved under the involution
\SW
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

We have
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\mathfrak{h}_{m}\mathfrak{h}_{n}
,
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
, and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
.

Proof.

The map
\SW
in Lemma
4.4
directly explains that
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\mathfrak{h}_{m}=\mathfrak{h}_{m}\mathfrak{h}_{n}
and it is easy to check that
\SW
induces bijections
\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
and
\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\times\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\rightarrow\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{m}\times\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
. We conclude
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{n}
.
∎

Consider an
n
by
n
square matrix
W=(W_{i,j})
whose entry
W_{i,j}
is an operator acting on
\mathbb{F}[\textbf{y}]
. We let
\det(W)
to be the operator defined by

\det(W)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(-1)^{\sgn(\sigma)}W_{\sigma(1),1}W_{\sigma(2),2}\dots W_{\sigma(n),n}.

For example, we have
\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{h}_{2}&\mathfrak{h}_{3}\\
\mathfrak{h}_{4}&\mathfrak{h}_{5}\end{pmatrix}=\mathfrak{h}_{2}\mathfrak{h}_{5}-\mathfrak{h}_{4}\mathfrak{h}_{3}
. Upon this notation, we provide a Jacobi–Trudi type formula for the Loehr–Warrington formula
\LW_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 4.6

(Jacobi-Trudi type formula)
.

For integers
k<n
and a partition
\lambda\subseteq R(n,k)
we associate an
n
by
n
square matrix
W(\lambda)

\displaystyle W(\lambda)_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{h}_{v(\lambda)_{j}-i+1}\qquad\text{if $j\leq k-s$}\\
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v(\lambda)_{j}-i+1}\qquad\text{if $j>k-s$ and $j$ is an entry of $\piv(\lambda)$}\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v(\lambda)_{j}-i+1}\qquad\text{if $j>k-s$ and $j$ is not an entry of $\piv(\lambda)$},\end{cases}

where
s
is the size of the Durfee square of
\lambda
. Then we have

q^{-\adj(\lambda)}\LW_{\lambda}=\det(W(\lambda))\cdot 1|_{y_{i,j}=t^{i}x_{j}}

Proof.

For a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, let
D(\sigma)
be the diagram

D(\sigma)=[[\bo(\lambda)_{1},s+1-\sigma(1)],[\bo(\lambda)_{2},s+2-\sigma(2)],\dots,[\bo(\lambda)_{n},s+n-\sigma(n)]].

Note that
D(\text{id})=D(\lambda)
. Let
C_{\sigma}
be the set of fillings
A
of
D(\sigma)
with elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that

•

A(i+1,j)\succ_{\PP}A(i,j)
,

•

for each
j>k-s
, if
j\in\piv(\lambda)
, we have
A(\bo(\lambda)_{j},j)_{1}>0
,

•

for each
j>k-s
, if
j\notin\piv(\lambda)
, we have
A(\bo(\lambda)_{j},j)_{1}=0
.

If
s+i-\sigma(i)<\bo(\lambda)_{i}
for some
i
, we consider that there is no filling of
D(\sigma)
.

By definition, it is straightforward to see that

(4.2)

\det(W(\lambda))\cdot 1=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(-1)^{\sgn(\sigma)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}W(\lambda)_{\sigma(i),i}\cdot 1=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(-1)^{\sgn(\sigma)}\sum_{A\in C_{\sigma}}q^{\dinv(A)}y^{A}.

We construct a sign-reversing involution
\widetilde{\SW}
on the set

B_{\lambda}:=\{(A,\sigma):\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},A\in C_{\sigma}\}\setminus\{(T,\text{id}):T\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}

such that

\sgn(\sigma)=-\sgn(\sigma^{\prime}),\qquad y^{A}=y^{A^{\prime}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dinv(A)=\dinv(A^{\prime}),

where we denoted
(A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})=\widetilde{\SW}(A,\sigma)
. Via such involution, all terms in (
4.2
) except for the ones from
\{(T,\text{id}):T\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)\}
cancels. This implies that we have

\det(W(\lambda))\cdot 1=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}(\lambda)}q^{\dinv(T)}y^{T},

which completes the proof.

We proceed to construct
\widetilde{\SW}
. For a given
(A,\sigma)\in B_{\lambda}
, a cell
(i,j)
in
D(\sigma)
is called
bad
in
A
if either of the following conditions holds:

•

(i,j-1)
is empty with
i\geq\bo(\lambda)_{j-1}
,

•

A(i,j-1)\succ_{\PP}A(i,j)
.

For
(A,\sigma)\in B_{\lambda}
, there must be at least one bad cell. Let
r=r(A)
be the smallest
i
such that there is a bad cell
(i,j)
. Once
r
is determined, let
c
be the largest
j
such that the cell
(r,j)
is bad. Define the sets

S_{c-1}(A)=\{A(i,c-1):r\leq i\leq s+(c-1)-\sigma(c-1)\},\quad\text{and}\quad S_{c}(A)=\{A(i,c):r<i\leq s+c-\sigma(c)\}.

We then define
\widetilde{\SW}(A,\sigma):=(A^{\prime},\sigma(c-1,c))
, where
A^{\prime}
is obtained from
A
by applying the map
\SW
(as defined in Lemma
4.4
) to
(S_{c-1}(A),S_{c}(A))
while leaving other parts unchanged, and
(c-1,c)
is the transposition swapping
c-1
and
c
. We show that
A^{\prime}
is in
C_{\sigma^{\prime}}
where
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma(c-1,c)
. It suffices to show
A^{\prime}(r-1,c-1)\prec_{\PP}A^{\prime}(r,c-1)
and
A^{\prime}(r,c)\prec_{\PP}A^{\prime}(r+1,c)
so that each column is still
\PP
-chain. Note that
A^{\prime}(r,c)=A(r,c)
and
A^{\prime}(r+1,c)
equals
A(r+1,c)
or
A(r,c-1)
, implying
A^{\prime}(r,c)\prec_{\PP}A^{\prime}(r+1,c)
. If
A(r-1,c-1)\nprec_{\PP}A(r+1,c)
, together with
A(r-1,c)\nprec_{\PP}A(r-1,c-1)
from the minimality of
r
, we have

\displaystyle A(r-1,c)\prec_{\PP}A(r,c)\prec_{\PP}A(r+1,c)

\displaystyle A(r-1,c)\nprec_{\PP}A(r-1,c-1)\nprec_{\PP}A(r+1,c)

which is impossible. Since
A^{\prime}(r-1,c-1)=A(r-1,c-1)
and
A^{\prime}(r,c-1)
equals
A(r,c-1)
or
A(r+1,c)
, we conclude
A^{\prime}(r-1,c-1)\prec_{\PP}A^{\prime}(r,c-1)
.

It is straightforward to see that
(r,c)
remains a bad cell in
A^{\prime}
, whose minimality is trivial. This guarantees that the map
\widetilde{\SW}
is indeed an involution. Moreover, it is evident that the map
\widetilde{\SW}
is sign-reversing and preserves
y^{A}
and
\dinv(A)
.

∎

We reformulate the Loehr–Warrington formula

. Our main tool is Lemma
4.7
, whose proof will be provided in Section

. For a matrix
A
, let
T_{ij}(A)
be the matrix obtained by moving the
j
-th column to the position of the
i
-th column and shifting the
\ell
-th column to the
(\ell+1)

i<\ell<j
. For example, we have

A=\begin{pmatrix}\rule[-4.30554pt]{0.5pt}{10.76385pt}&\rule[-4.30554pt]{0.5pt}{10.76385pt}&\rule[-4.30554pt]{0.5pt}{10.76385pt}&\rule[-4.30554pt]{0.5pt}{10.76385pt}&\rule[-4.30554pt]{0.5pt}{10.76385pt}\\

Lemma 4.7
.

For an integer vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})
of length
n\geq 2
, consider an
n\times n
matrix of operators
V=(V_{i,j})
given by

V_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+j-1}\quad\text{if $j<n$}\\
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-1}\quad\text{if $j=n$}\end{cases}

Then we have
(-q)^{n-1}\det V=\det T_{1,n}(V)
.

Now we modify the matrix
W(\lambda)
given in Proposition
4.6
. Let
s
be the maximal number such that
\lambda_{s}\geq s
. Set
W^{(0)}=W(\lambda)
and then construct
W^{(1)},\dots,W^{(s)}
recursively as follows:

•

(Step 1) Given
W^{(i)}
, let
V^{(i+1)}=T_{ab}(W^{(i)})
where
a=k-s+i
and
b=\piv(\lambda)_{i}
.

•

(Step 2) Add the
(\piv(\lambda)_{i}+1)
-th column to the
(k-s+i)
-th column in
V^{(i+1)}
to obtain
W^{(i+1)}
.

By Lemma
4.7
, after performing Step 1, the determinant multiplies by
(-q)^{\piv(\lambda)_{i}-(k-s+i)}=(-q)^{\lambda_{s-i+1}-(s-i+1)}
. The property of a determinant together with Corollary
4.5
implies that Step 2 does not change the determinant. We conclude that

(4.3)

(-q)^{\adj(\lambda)}\det(W(\lambda))=\det(W^{(s)}).

It is easy to check that
W^{(s)}
is given by

(4.4)

W^{(s)}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda_{k+1-j}+j-i}\quad\text{if $j\leq k$}\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{j-i}\quad\text{if $j>k$}.\end{cases}

Finally, by Proposition
4.6
, we conclude

(4.5)

\LW_{\lambda}=(-1)^{\adj(\lambda)}\det(W^{(s)})\cdot 1\bigg|_{y_{i,j}=t^{i}x_{j}}.

4.4.
Proof of Lemma
4.7

In this section we prove Lemma
4.7
. In particular, we prove Lemma
4.9
which naturally implies Lemma
4.7
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For an integer vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})
of length
n\geq 2
, consider
n
by
n
matrices
V
and
V^{\prime}
given as

\displaystyle V_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}}\qquad\text{if $j=1$}\\
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-1}\qquad\text{if $j=2$}\end{cases},\qquad V^{\prime}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-1}\qquad\text{if $j=1$}\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}}\qquad\text{if $j=2$}.\end{cases}

and
V_{i,j}=V^{\prime}_{i,j}=\mathfrak{h}_{v_{i}+j-2}
for
j>2
. Then we have
q\det(V)+\det(V^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

We proceed with induction on
n
. For the base case
n=2
, we prove the claim by imitating the argument in Lemma
4.4
. Let
v=(v_{1},v_{2})=(a,b)
. Then, we need to prove

(4.6)

q\det(V)+\det(V^{\prime})=q(\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{a}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{b+1}-\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{b}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{a+1})+(\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{a+1}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{b}-\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{b+1}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{q})=0,

which is equivalent to

(4.7)

q\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{a}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{b+1}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{a+1}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{b}=q\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{b}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{a+1}+\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{b+1}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{q}.

To that end, we define a bijection

\tSW:\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{a}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{b+1}\cup\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{a+1}\times\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{b}\rightarrow\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{b}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{a+1}\cup\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{b+1}\times\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{a}

as follows. Let
L=(L_{1},L_{2})\in\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{a}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{b+1}\cup\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{a+1}\times\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{b}
. Consider a directed graph on the elements of
L
, where edges are defined as
(a,b)\rightarrow(c,d)
for those satisfying
(a,b)<_{\text{lex}}(c,d)
and
\dinv(\{(a,b)\},\{(c,d)\})=1
. As we have seen in Lemma
4.4
, every connected component of this directed graph forms a directed path. Note that in
L
there is only one element of the form
(0,*)
. Let
P=(P_{1},P_{2})
be the directed path containing this element, where
P_{1}=L_{1}\cap P
and
P_{2}=L_{2}\cap P
. Then, let
(L^{\prime}_{1},L^{\prime}_{2})=\SW(L_{1}\setminus P_{1},L_{2}\setminus P_{2})
. Then,
\tSW(L)
is defined by

\tSW(L)=\begin{cases}(P_{1}\cup L^{\prime}_{1},P_{2}\cup L^{\prime}_{2})&\text{if $P$ is an odd path},\\
(P_{2}\cup L^{\prime}_{1},P_{1}\cup L^{\prime}_{2})&\text{if $P$ is an even path}.\end{cases}

Since the involution
\SW
preserves the
\dinv
statistic, and swapping the even path changes an additional
\pm 1
to the
\dinv
, it is straightforward to see that

\dinv(L)+\chi(L\in\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{a}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{b+1})=\dinv(\tSW(L))+\chi(\tSW(L)\in\hat{\mathbf{C}}_{b}\times\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{a+1}).

This proves (
4.7
), thus (
4.6
) follows.

Now let
n\geq 3
and assume the claim is true for any number smaller than
n
. We define
n
by
n
matrices
W
,
W^{\prime}
,
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle W_{i,1}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+1},\quad W_{i,2}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-2},\quad W^{\prime}_{i,1}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-2},\quad W^{\prime}_{i,2}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+1},

\displaystyle Z_{i,1}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-2},\quad Z_{i,2}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+1},\quad Z^{\prime}_{i,1}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+1},\quad Z^{\prime}_{i,2}=\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{i}+n-2},

and
W_{i,j}=W^{\prime}_{i,j}=Z_{i,j}=Z^{\prime}_{i,j}=\mathfrak{h}_{v_{i}+j-2}
for
j>2
. We claim the following

(4.8)

\displaystyle q\det(V)+\det(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\det(W)-q\det(W)^{\prime}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\det(W)

\displaystyle=-\det(Z)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\det(W^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\det(Z^{\prime})

(4.11)

\displaystyle\det(Z)

\displaystyle=-q\det(Z^{\prime})

which finishes the proof.

To prove (
4.8
), pick any
1\leq a<b\leq n
. Then it is enough to show

q\det\begin{pmatrix}V_{a,1}&V_{a,2}\\
V_{b,1}&V_{b,2}\end{pmatrix}+\det\begin{pmatrix}V^{\prime}_{a,1}&V^{\prime}_{a,2}\\
V^{\prime}_{b,1}&V^{\prime}_{b,2}\end{pmatrix}=-\det\begin{pmatrix}W_{a,1}&W_{a,2}\\
W_{b,1}&W_{b,2}\end{pmatrix}-q\det\begin{pmatrix}W^{\prime}_{a,1}&W^{\prime}_{a,2}\\
W^{\prime}_{b,1}&W^{\prime}_{b,2}\end{pmatrix}

which is equivalent to (after a rearrangement of terms)

\left(q\det\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}}&\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+1}\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+n-2}&\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+n-1}\end{pmatrix}+\det\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+1}&\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}}\\
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+n-1}&\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+n-2}\end{pmatrix}\right)-\left(q\det\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}}&\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+1}\\
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+n-2}&\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+n-1}\end{pmatrix}+\det\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}+1}&\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{b}}\\
\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+n-1}&\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{v_{a}+n-2}\end{pmatrix}\right)=0

Each summand on the left-hand side is zero due the induction hypothesis for
n=2
.

Now we show (
4.9
). Starting from
W
, we apply following column operations:

•

subtract the
n
-th column from the second column, then multiply (-1) to the second column

•

swap the first and the second columns

•

subtract the third column from the second column, then multiply (-1) to the second column.

It is easy to check that the resulting matrix is
Z
and
\det(W)=-\det(Z)
follows. Similar argument also shows (
4.10
).

Lastly, we show (
4.11
). Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{3,4\}
and pick any
A\subset\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that
|A|=n-2
. Then denoting

to be a submatrix given by restricting row indices to
A
and column indices to
I
, induction hypothesis implies
\det(Z_{A,I})+q\det(Z^{\prime}_{A,I})=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

5.
Generalized Macdonald polynomials for filled diagrams

5.1.
Macdonald polynomials for filled diagrams and HHL formula

Given a diagram
D
, we regard a word
\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{{|D|}}
as an assignment of integer entries to the cells of
D
, proceeding from left to right within each row, and from the top row to the bottom row. In this way, we consider
\sigma(u)
for
u\in D
as an integer assigned for
u
in the assignment of
\sigma
in
D
. The
content

\cont_{D}(\sigma,i)
of
\sigma
in the
i
-th row of
D
is defined by the (infinite) vector whose
a
-th component is given by the number of
a
’s in the assignment of
\sigma
in the
i
-th row of
D
.

The celebrated result of Haglund–Haiman–Loehr
[
HHL05a
]
gives Macdonald polynomials using two statistics
\inv_{\mu}
and
\maj_{\mu}
. We will use slightly different convention for
\inv
, which we now explain. For a diagram
D
, and cells
u=(i,j)
and
v=(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
of
D
, we say that a pair
(u,v)
is an
attacking pair
if either

(1)
i=i^{\prime}
and
j<j^{\prime}
or  (2)
i=i^{\prime}+1
and
j>j^{\prime}
.

For a word
\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{|D|}
, we consider a pair
(u,v)
of cells in
D
to be an
inversion pair
of
\sigma
if
(u,v)
is an attacking pair and
\sigma(u)>\sigma(v)
. We denote the set of inversion pairs of
\sigma
by
\Inv_{D}(\sigma)
and define
\inv_{D}(\sigma)
to be

\inv_{D}(\sigma)\coloneqq q^{|\Inv_{D}(\sigma)|}.

A cell
u
is
descent
of
\sigma
if
\sigma(u)>\sigma(v)
, where the cell
v
is the cell right below
u
, i.e.
u=(i,j)
and
v=(i-1,j)
. Define
\Des_{D}(\sigma)
to be the set of descents of
\sigma
.

A
filling
of
D
is a function
f:D\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, where we take
\mathbb{F}
as a field containing
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
. Then a
filled diagram

(D,f)
is a pair of a diagram and a filling on it. We define
\maj_{(D,f)}(\sigma)
as the product of
f(u)
over all cells
u
which are descents of
\sigma
, i.e.,

\maj_{(D,f)}(\sigma)\coloneqq\prod_{u\in\Des_{D}(\sigma)}f(u).

Note that bottom cells of
D
cannot be a descent so the value
f(u)
for bottom cell
u
is redundant in a definition of
\maj_{(D,f)}
. Therefore we usually represent a filled diagram
(D,f)
omitting values on bottom cells. Finally,
\stat_{(D,f)}(w)
is defined by the product of
\inv_{D}
and
\maj_{(D,f)}
,

\stat_{(D,f)}(\sigma)\coloneqq\inv_{D}(\sigma)\maj_{(D,f)}(\sigma).

In
[
KLO22
]
, a generalization of modified Macdonald polynomial of a filled diagram
(D,f)
is defined by

\widetilde{H}_{(D,f)}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{|D|}}\stat_{(D,f)}(\sigma)x^{\sigma}.

We also call
\widetilde{H}_{(D,f)}
as the
Macdonald polynomial
for a filled diagram
(D,f)
. Indeed, this generalizes the concept of the celebrated HHL formula
[
HHL05a
,
HHL08
]
, which gives the combinatorial formula for the modified Macdonald polynomial. To state, we recall some notations first. Previously we defined
\arm_{\mu}(u)
and
\leg_{\mu}(u)
for a partition
\mu
and these were generalized to a more general shape
[
HHL08
]
.

Let
\beta
be a diagram
\beta=[[\beta_{1}],[\beta_{2}],\dots,[\beta_{k}]]
for some positive integers
\beta_{i}
’s. For a cell
u\in\beta
, the
\arm_{\beta}
and
\leg_{\beta}
are defined as follows:

\leg_{\beta}(u)=\beta_{i}-j=(\text{number of cells strictly north of }u),

\arm_{\beta}(u)=|\{i^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\dots,i-1\}:j-1\leq\beta_{i^{\prime}}<\beta_{i}\}|+|\{i^{\prime}\in\{i,i+1,\dots,k\}:j\leq\beta_{i^{\prime}}\leq\beta_{i}\}|.

In other words,
\arm_{\beta}(u)
represents the number of cells strictly to the right of
u
in columns of height
\beta_{i^{\prime}}\leq\beta_{i}
or strictly to the left of the cell just below
u
in columns of height
\beta_{i^{\prime}}<\beta_{i}
. Define the
standard filling

f^{\st}_{\beta}
on
\beta
by

f^{\st}_{\beta}(u)=q^{-\arm_{\beta}(u)}t^{\leg_{\beta}(u)}.

Now we can give the celebrated HHL formula using this standard filling.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
HHL05a
,
HHL08
]

Let
\mu
be a partition and
\beta
be the diagram obtained by rearranging columns of
\mu
. Then we have

(5.1)

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{|\beta|}}\stat_{(\beta,f_{\beta}^{\st})}(\sigma)x^{\sigma}.

Haglund, Haiman, and Loehr
[
HHL05a
]
proved (
5.1
) for
\beta=\mu
, and later generalized it to any rearrangements
\beta
in
[
HHL08
]
.

Theorem
5.1
shows an example of two different filled diagrams giving the same Macdonald polynomials. Another (rather simple) operation on filled diagrams, called the
cycling rule
, also gives the same Macdonald polynomials. Given a diagram
D=[D^{(1)},\dots,D^{(\ell)}]
, consider the diagram

D^{\prime}=[D^{(2)},\dots,D^{(\ell)},D^{(1)}+1],

where
I+1=\{a+1:a\in I\}
for an interval
I
. In other words,
D^{\prime}
is obtained by moving the leftmost column of
D
to the end on the right and shifting it up by one cell. The cells in
D
and
D^{\prime}
correspond naturally in a bijective manner. For a filling
f
of
D
, we can define a filling
f^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
inherited from
f
through this natural bijection. We define
\cycling(D,f)\coloneqq(D^{\prime},f^{\prime})
and it is direct to see

(5.2)

\widetilde{H}_{(D,f)}[X]=\widetilde{H}_{(D^{\prime},f^{\prime})}[X].

Column exchange rule

In
[
KLO22
]
, the authors together with Seung Jin Lee introduced a

(Proposition

), which we exploit in the next section.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(D,f)
be a filled diagram satisfying the following for some positive integer

:

•

denoting

there exist integers

, and

•

if

\frac{f(2,j)}{f(2,j+1)}=\dots=\frac{f(m,j)}{f(m,j+1)}

Then we define
S_{j}(D,f)
to be a filled diagram
(D^{\prime},f^{\prime})
given by:

D^{\prime}=[D^{(1)},\dots,D^{(j+1)},D^{(j)},\dots]
and

•

f^{\prime}(i,c)=f(i,c)
if

,

•

f^{\prime}(i,j)=f(i,j+1)
, and

.

As it is obvious that the map

. We say that two filled diagrams are column-equivalent

Proposition 5.3
.

If
(D,f)\equiv(D^{\prime},f^{\prime})
then there exists a bijection
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{|D|}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{|D^{\prime}|}
satisfying

•

(\phi 1)

\stat_{(D,f)}(\sigma)=\stat_{(D^{\prime},f^{\prime})}(\phi(\sigma))

•

(\phi 2)

\cont_{D}(\sigma,i)=\cont_{D^{\prime}}(\phi(\sigma),i)
for all

.

•

(\phi 3)

\iDes(\std(\sigma))=\iDes(\std(\phi(\sigma))
where

denotes the standardization of a word.

Indeed, if

together with the condition

, are sufficient to imply

7.
Macdonald piece polynomial
\I_{\mu\,\lambda,k}
at
q=t=1

For a partition
\lambda
such that
\ell(\lambda)\leq k
, let
s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}):=s_{\lambda}(x_{1}-1,x_{2}-1,\dots,x_{k}-1)
. Then we have

(7.1)

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{\nu\subseteq\lambda}d^{(k)}_{\lambda,\nu}s_{\nu}(x_{1}-1,x_{2}-1,\dots,x_{k}-1)

where
d^{(k)}_{\lambda,\mu}=\det\left(\binom{\lambda_{i}+k-i}{\mu_{j}+k-j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}

[
Sta
]
.
Note that
d^{(k)}_{\lambda,\mu}
is a number of certain non-intersecting lattice paths by Lindström–Gessel–Viennot lemma, therefore a nonnegative integer.
Lastly, we denote
[h_{\mu}](\nabla s_{\lambda}|_{q,t=1})
by
W_{\lambda,\mu}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Consider

\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t]:=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\dfrac{s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}\widetilde{H}_{\mu^{S}}[X]

where
z_{i}=q^{-\coarm(c_{i})}t^{-\coleg(c_{i})}
. We will prove the following:

(7.2)

\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;1,1]=\sum_{\tau}W_{\tilde{\lambda},\tau}h_{(\tau+1^{|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|})}.

Expanding
s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})
in terms of Schur functions as follows

s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})=\sum_{\nu\subseteq\lambda}d^{\prime}_{\lambda,\nu}s_{\nu}(z_{S}),

we have

\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t]=\sum_{\nu\subseteq\lambda}(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\nu|}d^{\prime}_{\lambda,\nu}\I_{\mu,\nu,k}[X;q,t].

Note that
d^{\prime}_{\lambda,\lambda}=1
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.2
(a) and (b) we have

(7.3)

e^{\perp}_{N}\left(\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t]\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|<N\\
\nabla s_{\tilde{\lambda}}&\text{ if }|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|=N.\end{cases}

Together with the following elementary fact

e^{\perp}_{N}h_{\nu}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\ell(\nu)<N\\
h_{\nu-(1^{N})}&\text{ if }\ell(\nu)=N,\end{cases}

we conclude

[h_{\nu}]\left(\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;1,1]\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\ell(\nu)>|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|\\
W_{\tilde{\lambda},\nu^{\prime}}&\text{ if }\ell(\nu)=|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|,\end{cases}

where
\nu^{\prime}=\nu-(1^{|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|})
.

Now it suffices to show
[h_{\nu}]\left(\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;1,1]\right)=0
for
\ell(\nu)<|\mu|-k-|\tilde{\lambda}|
. Recall that for a partition
\mu\vdash n
, we have,

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}[X;q,1]=\sum_{\nu\vdash n}(q-1)^{n-\ell(\nu)}A_{\mu,\nu}(q)h_{\nu}[X],

for some
A_{\mu,\nu}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]

[
GHQR19
, Proposition 1.1]
. From the definition of
\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,t]
, we have,

[h_{\tau}]\left(\bar{\I}_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;q,1]\right)=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\dfrac{s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}(q-1)^{|\mu|-k-\ell(\nu)}A_{\mu^{S},\tau}(q),

where we abuse our notation to write
z_{i}=q^{-\coleg(c_{i})}
. Note that

\dfrac{s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}

has a pole of degree
k(n-k)-|\lambda|=|\tilde{\lambda}|
at
q=1
. Since
|\mu|-k-\ell(\tau)>|\tilde{\lambda}|
, each term

\dfrac{s^{\flat}_{\lambda}(z_{S})\prod_{j\in S^{c}}{z_{j}}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in S\\
j\in S^{c}\end{subarray}}(z_{j}-z_{i})}(q-1)^{|\mu|-\ell(\nu)}A_{\mu^{S},\tau}(q)

vanishes at
q=1
.

By (
7.1
) we have

\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}[X;1,1]=\sum_{\nu\subseteq\lambda}(-1)^{|\lambda|-|\nu|}d^{(k)}_{\lambda,\nu}(\bar{\I}_{\mu,\nu,k}[X;1,1]).

Now (
7.2
) completes the proof.
∎

8.
Future Direction

We list some future questions from this work.

•

We conjectured that
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
is signed Schur positive and
\widetilde{H}^{\lambda}_{\mu^{S}}
is Schur positive. Therefore, finding positive monomial expansion formulas (possibly of the HHL type) for these symmetric functions would be the next challenge. As we have seen that
\widetilde{H}^{\lambda}_{\mu^{S}}
refines the Macdonald positivity, finding a positive monomial expansion would be a significant step toward addressing Problem
1.1
. Additionally, we may further attempt to extend Conjecture
2.3
, by expressing not only the intersection but also the intersection together with the difference of Garsia–Haiman modules.

•

The arguments presented in Section
4
remain valid when we change a poset
\mathbf{P}
to any unit interval order. It would be interesting to find a generalization of
\I_{\mu,\lambda,k}
that matches with the combinatorics of the generalized
\LW_{\lambda}
.

•

The Macdonald polynomials have fascinating connections to the geometry of Hilbert schemes
[
Hai01
]
and affine Springer fibers
[
Mel20
]
. However, understanding our main results from a geometric perspective remains elusive. We hope that our findings provide new insights and contribute to the theory of Macdonald polynomials.

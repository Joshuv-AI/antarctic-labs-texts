Constraints on the z ∼ 6 − 13 z\sim 6-13 intergalactic medium from JWST spectroscopy of Lyman-alpha damping wings in galaxies
Charlotte A. Mason, Zuyi Chen, Daniel P. Stark, Ting-Yi Lu, Michael Topping, Mengtao Tang

Abstract
Context. JWST provides a unique dataset for studying the earliest stages of reionization at z > 9 z>9 , promising insights into the first galaxies. Many JWST/NIRSpec prism spectra of z > 5 z>5 galaxies reveal smooth Lyman-alpha breaks, implying damping wing scattering by neutral hydrogen. Aims. We investigate what current prism spectra imply about the intergalactic medium (IGM) at z > 6 z>6 , and how best to use NIRSpec spectra to recover IGM properties. We use a sample of 99 z ∼ 5.5 − 13 z\sim 5.5-13 galaxies with high S/N prism spectra in the public archive, including 12 at z > 10 z>10 . Methods. We analyse these spectra using damping wing sightlines from inhomogeneous reionizing IGM simulations, mapping between the distance of a source from the neutral IGM and the average IGM neutral fraction. We marginalise over absorption by local neutral hydrogen around the galaxies, and Lyman-alpha emission. Results. We observe a decline in the median and variance of flux around the Ly α \alpha break with increasing redshift, consistent with an increasingly neutral IGM, as ionized regions become smaller and rarer. At z ​ Γ ∼ > ​ 9 z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,9 the spectra become consistent with an almost fully neutral IGM. We find S/N > > 15 per pixel is required to robustly estimate IGM properties from prism spectra. We fit a sub-sample of high S/N spectra and infer mean IGM neutral fractions x ¯ hi = 0.33 − 0.27 + 0.18 , 0.64 − 0.23 + 0.17 {\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0.33^{+0.18}_{-0.27},0.64^{+0.17}_{-0.23} ( > 0.70 >0.70 excluding GNz11) at z ≈ 6.5 , 9.3 z\approx 6.5,9.3 . We also investigate local HI absorption, finding a median column density of log 10 ⁡ N hi ≈ 10 20.8 \log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 10^{20.8} cm -2 , comparable to z ∼ 3 z\sim 3 Lyman-break galaxies, with no significant redshift evolution z ​ Γ ∼ > ​ 5.5 z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5.5 . We find galaxies showing the highest column density absorption are more likely to be in close associations of sources ( Γ ∼ < ​ 500 \,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,500 pkpc), implying absorption is enhanced in massive dark matter halos. Future deep prism and grating spectroscopy of z > 9 z>9 sources will provide tighter constraints on the earliest stages of reionization, key for understanding the onset of star formation.

Introduction

Understanding the reionization of intergalactic hydrogen in the early universe has long been a frontier in astronomy.
In the past two decades significant progress has been made in constraining the end stages of reionization, with multiple independent observations demonstrating reionization was complete by
z\sim 5.3-6
and on-going at
z\sim 7-8

(
Stark et al., 2010
;
Ouchi et al., 2017
;
Planck Collaboration et al., 2020
;
Mason et al., 2018b
;
Davies et al., 2018
;
Qin et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
.
However, until the launch of JWST, we had no observational constraints on the earliest stages of reionization at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8
.
Constraints on the early IGM promise crucial information about the onset of star formation and the higher-than-expected UV luminosity density detected by JWST at
z>9

(
Castellano et al., 2022
;
Naidu et al., 2022
;
Adams et al., 2023
;
Donnan et al., 2023
;
Harikane et al., 2023
;
Finkelstein et al., 2022
, e.g.,)
as the collective ionizing output of galaxies below even JWST’s detection limits will be felt in the IGM.

JWST finally provides the ability to chart the earliest stages of reionization through measurements of the Lyman-alpha (Ly
\alpha
) damping wing, due to scattering by neutral hydrogen in the IGM, in
z>8
galaxies.
The damping wing feature results in smooth absorption up to several thousand km/s redward of Ly
\alpha
in the spectra of high redshift sources
(
Miralda-Escude, 1998
, e.g.,)
. The strength of absorption depends on the density and spatial distribution of neutral hydrogen along the line of sight, and thus can be used to constrain the properties of the high-redshift IGM.
Before the launch of JWST, Ly
\alpha
damping wings had been observed in just four bright quasars at
z\sim 7-7.5

(
Mortlock et al., 2011
;
Bañados et al., 2018
;
Wang et al., 2020
;
Yang et al., 2020
)
. In galaxies, fainter but orders of magnitude more numerous than quasars, the integrated impact of the damping wing had been detected as a decrease in the equivalent width distribution of galaxies’ Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Stark et al., 2010
;
Pentericci et al., 2014
;
Mason et al., 2019b
;
Jung et al., 2020
;
Bolan et al., 2022
, e.g.,)
and the decline in Ly
\alpha
-emitter luminosity functions
(
Ouchi et al., 2017
;
Hu et al., 2019
;
Morales et al., 2021
;
Umeda et al., 2025b
, e.g.,)
at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
.

The spectral sensitivity of JWST/NIRSpec
(
Jakobsen et al., 2022
)
has enabled the first detection of the UV continuum for typical star-forming galaxies at
z>5
, and thus direct observations of the IGM damping wing.
Excitingly, early JWST results have revealed many
z>9
galaxies show strong damping wing features in their spectra
(
Curtis-Lake et al., 2023
;
Umeda et al., 2024
;
Heintz et al., 2024b
, e.g.,)
, and a continued decline in the Ly
\alpha
equivalent width distribution at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8

(
Nakane et al., 2024
;
Tang et al., 2024c
;
Jones et al., 2025
;
Kageura et al., 2025
)
, implying we are finally detecting galaxies in an almost fully neutral IGM.
However, JWST spectroscopy has also provided hints of early ionized bubbles via surprising detections of Ly
\alpha
from galaxies at
z\approx 11
and
z\approx 13

(
Bunker et al., 2023
;
Witstok et al., 2025
)
.
Placing these detections in context requires a large census of
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,9
spectra:
current and future spectroscopic surveys with JWST provide the potential to precisely chart the earliest stages of reionization via both the decline in Ly
\alpha
emission and the impact of IGM damping on the UV continuum redward of Ly
\alpha
.

However, JWST spectra also present unique challenges for inferring properties of the IGM from the UV continuum. The most efficient spectroscopic mode is the NIRSpec prism. The low spectral resolution of the prism around
1\,\mu
m (
R\sim 40
) means the damping wing appears in only
\sim 5
pixels. Moderate Lyman-alpha emission (Ly
\alpha
, EW
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,50
Å) can be spread across these pixels and confused with a high continuum flux
(
Keating et al., 2024a
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
Jones et al., 2024
;
Park et al., 2025
)
, in addition to NV P-Cygni stellar wind lines which may be present in sources with
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10
Myr massive stars
(
Chisholm et al., 2019
, e.g.,)
and interstellar absorption lines.
Furthermore, galaxies at all redshifts are commonly observed with absorption around Ly
\alpha
due to dense neutral hydrogen in the ISM and CGM, and proximate absorbers along the line of sight,
(
Shapley et al., 2003
;
Reddy et al., 2016
;
Hu et al., 2023
;
Heintz et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
. These features also change the shape of the UV continuum, though, as we show below, with a different wavelength dependence than the neutral IGM, but may be hard to distinguish from the IGM with low resolution, low S/N spectra.

Umeda et al. (2024)
presented the most comprehensive study of galaxy damping wings to-date, fitting the spectra of 27 spectroscopically confirmed
z>7
galaxies, including the impact of Ly
\alpha
emission and neutral hydrogen (HI) in the host galaxies in the spectra, to infer the IGM neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
at
z\sim 7-13
, finding evidence for an increasing neutral fraction with redshift. However, to fit the IGM damping wing from galaxy spectra, this, and most previous works with JWST, have assumed a simple analytical model for the Ly
\alpha
transmission, which approximates the IGM as ionized within the galaxies’ host bubble and uniform beyond the bubble with neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Miralda-Escude, 1998
)
. While this model reproduces the median IGM transmission in realistic IGM simulations at fixed
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Keating et al., 2024a
)
, using it to fit individual sources can bias inferred
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
as it overestimates the contribution of neutral gas at large distances
(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2008
)
. The damping wing optical depth most strongly depends on the distance of a galaxy to the first neutral patch, thus accurate
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
inferences requires a realistic mapping between the ionized bubble size distribution as a function of redshift and
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
In this work, we present an analysis of galaxy damping wings using sightlines from realistic, inhomogeneous IGM simulations which can capture this mapping.

In this paper we seek to understand what current NIRSpec prism spectra imply about the IGM at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
and how best to use NIRSpec galaxy continuum spectra to robustly recover IGM properties. We use a sample of 99
z>5.5
galaxies, including 12 at
z>10
, to explore the redshift evolution of the Ly
\alpha
break. We find a decrease in both the flux and variance of the strength of the break which we interpret as most likely due to an increasingly neutral IGM, as large ionized regions become smaller and rarer.
We describe an approach for fitting the UV continuum using damping wing sightlines from realistic IGM simulations to forward-model galaxy spectra, accounting for the inhomogeneous nature of the reionizing IGM, marginalising over galaxies’ Ly
\alpha
emission and local absorption systems, to infer constraints on galaxies’ distances from neutral gas and the mean neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.

This paper is structured as follows: in Section
2
we present our method for modelling the Ly
\alpha
damping wing optical depth, due to both neutral IGM and local absorbers. We describe our observational sample, obtained from public JWST Cycle 1 and 2 NIRSpec spectra, in Section
3
, and our spectral fitting approach in Section
4
. We present the evolution of the spectra and our fits to these spectra in Section
5
. We discuss our results in the context of the reionization process and local absorption systems in Section
6
, and present our conclusions in Section
7
.

We fix use the best fit cosmological parameters from Planck 2018 data
(
Planck Collaboration et al., 2020
, TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BAO from)
, and all distances are comoving unless specified otherwise.

\tau_{\alpha}=\tau_{\textsc{igm}}+\tau_{\textsc{dla}}

We first describe the general properties of the Ly
\alpha

2.1.1

For each sightline to a galaxy, the optical depth due to diffuse neutral hydrogen in the IGM can be approximated by the integral over the damping wing component of the optical depth in every neutral patch along the sightline:

\tau_{\textsc{igm}}(\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}})=\int_{D_{b}}^{D_{\mathrm{max}}}d\tau_{\textsc{igm}}

(1)

where
D_{\mathrm{b}}
is the distance of the galaxy from the edge of its host ionized bubble along the line-of-sight. We set
D_{\mathrm{max}}=1.6
cGpc, wrapping around our simulation cubes (see Section
2.1.1
) assuming periodic boundaries
(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2008
, the optical depth converges after
\sim 200
cMpc, e.g.,)
. The contribution to the optical depth from each neutral patch
i
along the line-of-sight is given by
(
Miralda-Escude, 1998
, e.g.,)
:

\displaystyle d\tau_{\textsc{igm},i}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6.43\times 10^{-9}{x_{\textsc{hi}}}_{,i}\tau_{\mathrm{GP}}(z)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[I\left(\frac{1+z_{\mathrm{begin},i}}{1+z}\right)-I\left(\frac{1+z_{\mathrm{end},i}}{1+z}\right)\right]

where
{x_{\textsc{hi}}}_{,i}
is the neutral fraction in each patch (we assume
{x_{\textsc{hi}}}_{,i}=1
),
\tau_{\mathrm{GP}}(z)=\pi e^{2}f_{\alpha}\overline{n}_{H}(z)/[m_{e}cH(z)]
is the
Gunn and Peterson (1965)
optical depth, where
e
is the electron/proton charge,
f_{\alpha}=0.416
is the Ly
\alpha

m_{e}
is the electron mass and
\overline{n}_{H}(z)
is the mean hydrogen number density. We define
1+z=(1+z_{g})\lambda_{\mathrm{emit}}/\lambda_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
, where
z_{g}
is the redshift of the galaxy and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
is the rest-frame wavelength of Ly
\alpha
(1216 Å).
z_{\mathrm{begin},i}\approx z_{g}-cD_{b,i}/H(z)
is the redshift of the beginning of a neutral patch a comoving distance
D_{b,i}
from the galaxy and
z_{\mathrm{end},i}
is the redshift of the end of the neutral patch, and finally,

I(x)=\frac{x^{9/2}}{1-x}+\frac{9}{7}x^{7/2}+\frac{9}{5}x^{5/2}+3x^{3/2}+9x^{1/2}-\frac{9}{2}\ln{\left|\frac{1+x^{1/2}}{1-x^{1/2}}\right|}.

(3)

We assume gas inside ionized regions is optically thick to Ly
\alpha
photons at resonance (i.e.
\tau(\lambda_{\mathrm{emit}}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha})\rightarrow\infty
), truncating the blue side of Ly
\alpha

(
Mason and Gronke, 2020
)
, as expected given the opacity in the Ly
\alpha

Santos, 2004

Dijkstra et al., 2007

, which we also include (see Section
4

We gain two important insights by considering the limit
z_{\mathrm{end}}\ll z_{\mathrm{begin}}
(i.e. a single ionized patch out to
D_{b}
from the source, followed by neutral gas up to a distance
D_{\mathrm{max}}\gg D_{b}

\sim 100
cMpc, i.e. very large distances, (2) because
I(x)
increases very steeply with
x

Most analyses of Ly
\alpha
damping wings in galaxies observed by JWST to-date have assumed a uniform IGM with a volume-averaged neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, and, but not always, with a single ionized bubble around each galaxy
(
Miralda-Escude, 1998
, following e.g.,)
.
However, because the damping wing is more sensitive to
D_{b}
than to
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, not only does this neglect the sightline variance, this approach can lead to significant biases in the inferred
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2008
, discussed in detail by)

In particular, the uniform IGM approximation can underestimate the damping wing for galaxies in small bubbles during the late stages of reionization and overestimate it during earlier stages (see Appendix
A
). Relatively accurate estimates of
D_{b}
can be obtained assuming a fully neutral IGM beyond the first bubble
(
Mason and Gronke, 2020
;
Hayes and Scarlata, 2023
, e.g.,)
, but still require a mapping from the inferred distribution of
D_{b}
to
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Tang et al., 2024c
, e.g.,)
.
In this work we analyse JWST spectra using sightlines from realistic, inhomogeneous IGM simulations from
Lu et al. (2024)
, described in Section
2.1.1
, allowing us to map between inferred damping wings parameterised by
D_{b}
, and the IGM neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
Here, we consider a single reionization morphology model, but we do not expect this to significantly impact our
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
inference – as demonstrated by
Sobacchi and Mesinger (2015)
;
Greig et al. (2017)
;
Greig et al. (2019)
;
Mason et al. (2018a)
, the Ly
\alpha
damping wing transmission is fairly insensitive to the reionization morphology when considering
>L^{\star}
galaxies (as we will for our study due to their higher SNR spectra) which are likely to be in the largest ionized regions in all reionization models
(
Lu et al., 2024
)
, and when considering redshift bins broader than the typical scales of bubbles (
\Delta z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,0.1
, as will be the case with our study) which smooths out information about the reionization morphology
(
Sobacchi and Mesinger, 2015
)
. In an upcoming work we will discuss cases where differences in the reionization morphology could be distinguished (i.e. for UV-faint galaxies observed in narrower redshift windows).

2.1.1
Reionization simulations

To obtain realistic IGM damping wings we use semi-numerical reionization simulations by
Lu et al. (2024)
, which are optimised for comparison to JWST observations, and refer the reader there for full details.
The simulations are created using the semi-numerical code
21cmFAST-v2

(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2007
;
Sobacchi and Mesinger, 2014
;
Mesinger et al., 2016
)
.
21cmFAST-v2
generates IGM properties from a 3D density field, flagging cells as ionized when the rate of ionizations exceeds the rate of recombinations. The ionization rate is set by the collapsed matter fraction in a cell multiplied by an ionization parameter.

We create a grid of simulation cubes, using the same initial conditions, at fixed redshifts
z\sim 6-16
, with
\Delta z=1
, which are each (1.6 cGpc)
3
volume – sufficient to sample 100s of
{M_{\textsc{uv}}}\sim-22
galaxies, with
\sim 1
cMpc resolution in the IGM.
For each cube, we use a halo-filtering approach in Extended Press-Schechter theory
(
Sheth et al., 2001
)
to generate a halo catalog from the associated density field (see
Mesinger and Furlanetto (2007)
for a full description of the method) and assign UV luminosities to halos based on the
Mason et al. (2015)
luminosity function model, which successfully reproduces observations over
z\sim 0-10

(see
Lu et al., 2024
, for a comparison of the simulated LFs and observations)
, enabling us to account for the expected large-scale environment of the observed galaxies. We note, while this model doesn’t match
z>10
JWST LFs well
(
Mason et al., 2023
)
, as shown by
Whitler et al. (2020)
;
Lu et al. (2024)
the exact environment of galaxies as a function of UV luminosity is less important that the average neutral fraction in determining Ly
\alpha
visibility, so this should not significantly impact our results. We include 0.5 mag scatter in the halo mass – UV luminosity mapping to include the impact of stochastic star formation
(
Ren et al., 2019
;
Mason et al., 2023
;
Gelli et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
, but note this has only a small impact on galaxies’ Ly
\alpha
transmission
(
Whitler et al., 2020
)
.
We vary the ionization parameter to produce IGM cubes from the density field at neutral fractions
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0-1
, with spacing
\Delta{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 0.05
, from which we sample IGM damping wings to every halo to generate a catalogue of damping wings as a function of redshift,
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
,
D_{b}
and
M_{\textsc{uv}}
.

In Figure
1
we show example slices from our IGM cubes at
z=8
, showing
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0.5,0.7,0.9
, and the corresponding median and 68% and 95% ranges of IGM damping wings.
Figure
1
demonstrates there is large sightline variance in Ly
\alpha
transmission due to the broad bubble size distributions, especially during the mid-stages of reionization
(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2008
;
Mason et al., 2018a
;
Keating et al., 2024a
, see also, e.g.)
, and thus the importance of using simulations to map from damping wing observations to
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
estimates.
In Section
6.3
, we demonstrate we require
\sim 20
sightlines, i.e. galaxies, per redshift bin to accurately recover
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.

2.2
Optical depth from local absorbers

In addition to the Ly
\alpha
damping wing from the IGM, sources can also experience Ly
\alpha
damping absorption from dense HI gas on local (
<1
pMpc) scales. Spectroscopic studies at
z\sim 0-4
have shown that roughly half of Lyman-break galaxies show absorption around Ly
\alpha
, often in addition to Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Shapley et al., 2003
;
Heckman et al., 2011
;
Rivera-Thorsen et al., 2015
;
Reddy et al., 2016
;
Pahl et al., 2020
;
Hu et al., 2023
;
Begley et al., 2024
)
, which has recently been extended to
z>5
with JWST
(
Chen et al., 2024
;
Heintz et al., 2024b
;
Heintz et al., 2025
;
Hainline et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
. These results imply neutral gas in the ISM and/or CGM with column densities
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{20}
cm
-2
, i.e. damped Ly
\alpha
absorbers (DLAs), though with a non-uniform covering fraction
(
Heckman et al., 2011
;
Reddy et al., 2016
, e.g.,)
. Proximate absorbers along the line of sight may also provide additional opacity
(
Davies et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
.

A key question is to what extent this local absorption affects our ability to estimate the impact of the IGM at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
.
McQuinn et al. (2008)
and
Lidz et al. (2021)
have discussed this in the context of measuring IGM damping wings in gamma ray burst (GRB) spectra and demonstrated the absorption profiles due to the IGM and local gas are significantly different. The optical depth from local HI gas can be approximated by:

\tau_{\mathrm{DLA}}(\Delta\lambda)={N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sigma_{\alpha}(\Delta\lambda,T)

(4)

where we use the approximation for the Ly
\alpha
optical depth
\sigma_{\alpha}
given by
Tasitsiomi (2006)
. As the damping wings are set by natural line broadening, the temperature,
T
, of the absorbing gas has negligible impact on the optical depth, so we set
T=10^{4}
K.

The Lorentzian wing of the Ly
\alpha
optical depth
(e.g., see
Dijkstra, 2014
, for a review)
implies
\tau_{\mathrm{DLA}}\sim 1/(\Delta\lambda)^{2}
, while the IGM damping wing, being an integral over a much longer path length, follows a shallower profile,
\tau_{\mathrm{IGM}}\sim 1/(\Delta\lambda)
. This means the impact of the IGM and DLAs can be distinguished in the UV continuum.

We demonstrate this in Figure
4
where we show Ly
\alpha
transmission profiles for a fully neutral IGM versus local absorption through various column densities
N_{\textsc{hi}}
, and the combination of both local absorption and the neutral IGM for both grating and prism resolution. The steep local HI absorption profile relative to the IGM damping wing is clearly seen for
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10^{21}
cm
-2
where more flux is reduced at linecenter. At fixed
N_{\textsc{hi}}
, the addition of neutral IGM suppresses flux at longer wavelengths. Only for extremely high column densities,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{22}
cm
-2
does the DLA damping wing start to dominate over the IGM damping wing. Recent JWST observations have presented evidence for DLAs reaching
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 10^{22.0-22.5}
around some sources
(
Heintz et al., 2024b
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2024
)
, but as we will show in Section
6.2
this is likely a tail of the distribution and the majority of
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
sources have lower inferred column densities. In Appendix
B
we show mock spectra for both the prism and G140M resolution grating, finding that, given sufficient S/N, the IGM can be distinguished from local absorption. In Figure
4
we also show how the damping wing for a fully neutral IGM evolves with redshift, becoming similar in strength to a
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{21-21.5}
cm
-2
DLA at
z\sim 6-14
.

In our fiducial models, we fix the absorber to the redshift of the source, assuming most absorption happens in the ISM/CGM, and assume a uniform covering fraction of local neutral gas,
f_{\mathrm{cov}}=1
.
We also consider models with non-uniform covering fraction
f_{\mathrm{cov}}<1
,
and with proximate absorbers, though in the majority of cases these do not provide better fits.
In Appendix
B
we describe the transmission profile including the covering fraction and discuss the impact of other variables on the transmission profiles.
We also show in Appendix
B
that even without a precise spectroscopic redshift from emission lines it should still be possible to get information about the IGM damping relative to DLAs.

In addition to DLAs, an increase in lower column density systems (Lyman-limit systems and sub-DLAs,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sim 10^{17-20}
cm
-2
) is expected in the ionized IGM as the UV background drops during reionization
(
Bolton and Haehnelt, 2013
, e.g.,)
. We will discuss this further in Section
6.2
but do not expect this to strongly affect our results as the damping wing shape is barely changed at the resolution of the prism in the presence of sub-DLAs (see Figures
4
and
16
).

UV magnitude versus spectroscopic redshift for our sample. We show sources from CEERS, JADES and UNCOVER in blue, orange and green respectively, and highlight sources with sufficient S/N(
>15
) for robust IGM fitting (see Section
4
) with black outlines.

4
Spectral fitting

Here we describe our approach for fitting the prism continuum spectra to recover IGM and local HI properties. We first describe how we forward-model each galaxy’s spectrum after transmission through local HI and the IGM. We then describe our likelihood function which accounts for the covariance in prism spectra and discuss the S/N requirements for recovering robust IGM constraints. We describe the setup for our Bayesian inference and priors in more detail in Appendix
D
.

We perform the following steps to forward-model prism spectra for each observed source:

1.

Create an intrinsic continuum model for
<1500
Å
by fitting the observed spectrum at
>
1500Å using the photoionization modelling code BEAGLE
(
Chevallard and Charlot, 2016
)
. By fitting the spectrum including all nebular emission lines, BEAGLE predicts the nebular continuum at
<1500
Å.
The BEAGLE fits are performed using a constant star formation history, a
Chabrier (2003)
IMF (upper mass cut 100
M_{\odot}
),
Pei (1992)
SMC extinction curve (uniform prior on the V-band optical depth from 0 to 6), uniform prior on
\log{U}
from -4 to -1, and a uniform prior on the ionizing photon escape fraction
f_{\mathrm{esc}}
from
0-1
. Non-zero escape fractions are required to fit very blue,
\beta<-2.6
, UV slopes, as seen in a small fraction of
z>5
spectra
(
Topping et al., 2024
)
.

To test the accuracy of the continuum models we compare the predicted and observed spectra at
1400-1500
Å. This is blueward of the range we used to fit the spectrum with BEAGLE but redward of where the IGM and local absorbers can significantly change the continuum. We calculate the residual spectrum over
1400-1500
Å (observed - predicted/observed). We find: 1) the distribution of mean (over
1400-1500
Å) residuals is peaked at zero, indicating no systematic bias above or below the observed continuum, and 2) the distribution of standard deviations of residuals (equivalent to the fractional error on the continuum models) across our sample has a median at 10% (6-22%, 16-84% range, with the uncertainty decreasing with increasing S/N).
A range of dust attenuation laws may also impact the shape of the UV continuum, though we note the majority of our sources are fit with negligible dust attenuation. Deep, high resolution grating spectra of the UV continuum will provide further tests of photoionization models, which will be important future work.
Our recovered uncertainties are comparable to the uncertainty on fits to quasar continua at
z<7.5

(
Greig et al., 2024b
;
Hennawi et al., 2025
,
\sim 5-10\%
,)
.
These recovered errors are however
\approx 5\times
higher than the uncertainty of the continuum models output from BEAGLE, so we rescale all output continuum models uncertainties by a factor of five.

2.

Add attenuation by local absorbers
,
\tau_{\mathrm{DLA}}({N_{\textsc{hi}}}_{\mathrm{,eff}})
, with effective column density
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}_{\mathrm{,eff}}
at the redshift of the source
4
4

4

Given the resolution of the prism at
1\,\mu
m (
\Delta z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,0.05,\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,100
pkpc) this can be due to an unresolved ensemble of absorbers, thus we use the subscript
eff
to denote it is an effective optical depth, and add a single absorber at the redshift of the source.
.
As described in Section
2.2
we also consider fits with non-uniform covering fraction,
f_{\mathrm{cov}}<1
, or with a proximate absorption system along the line-of-sight.
In the majority of sources these do not provide significantly better fits.

3.

Add emergent Ly
\alpha
emission
using a single Gaussian emission line with equivalent width EW
Lyα
, FWHM and velocity offset from systemic,
\Delta v
. This is the emission before transmission through the IGM, and must be included even in sources without apparent Ly
\alpha
emission to account for the contribution of weak Ly
\alpha
which is unresolved by the prism
(
Jones et al., 2024
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
Keating et al., 2024a
)
.
Lower redshift studies have shown a large fraction of the variance in Ly
\alpha
emission can be predicted by galaxy properties linked to the production and escape of Ly
\alpha
photons
(
Trainor et al., 2019
;
Hayes et al., 2023
, see e.g.,)

Thus, we use conditional empirical priors for the above Ly
\alpha
properties as a function of each galaxy’s emission properties (
M_{\textsc{uv}}
, OIII+H
\beta
EW), based on
z\sim 5-6
observations
(
Tang et al., 2024a
)
. We describe these priors in more detail in Appendix
D
.

4.

Add resonant scattering attenuation due to gas infalling to the halo
. By
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
dense residual HI in the ionized IGM resonantly scatters photons emitted blue-ward of Ly
\alpha
linecenter. Gravitational infall of gas around halos can shift this attenuation red-ward, to approximately the circular velocity of the halo, as Ly
\alpha
photons appear blue-shifted in the frame of infalling gas
(
Santos, 2004
;
Dijkstra et al., 2007
, e.g.,)
. Following
Mason et al. (2018b)
we cut transmission blueward of the circular velocity of the halo, and add a random scatter of 10%, motivated by hydrodynamical simulations by
Park et al. (2021)
demonstrating moderate sightline variance.

5.

Add attenuation by the neutral IGM
at a distance
D_{b}
from the source: using IGM damping wing optical depths
\tau_{\mathrm{IGM}}(D_{b},{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
drawn from the simulated sightlines described in Section
2.1.1
.
For each galaxy we draw sightlines from halos in the simulation with UV magnitudes
{M_{\textsc{uv}}}
within 0.2 mag of the observed magnitude.

6.

Convolve the model spectrum with the resolution of the prism.
5
5

5

We calculate the line spread function using observations from CAL-1125 of a planetary nebula IRAS-05248-7007 in the LMC. We find
R\sim 45
around
\sim 1\,\mu
m.

Thus the final model spectrum given galaxy parameters
\theta_{\mathrm{gal}}
is:

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{mod}}(\lambda,{x_{\textsc{hi}}},D_{b},\theta_{\mathrm{gal}})=f_{\mathrm{emit}}(\lambda,\theta_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha})\times\mathcal{T}(\lambda,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},D_{b},\theta_{\textsc{dla}})

(5)

where
f_{\mathrm{emit}}
is the continuum model (step 1) plus intrinsic Ly
\alpha
emission (step 2) multiplied by the transmission curve:

\mathcal{T}(\lambda,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},D_{b},\theta_{\textsc{dla}})=e^{-\tau_{\textsc{dla}}(\lambda,\theta_{\textsc{dla}})-\tau_{\textsc{igm}}(\lambda,D_{b},{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})}

(6)

where
\theta_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}=(\mathrm{EW}_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha},{\Delta v},FWHM)
are the Ly
\alpha
emission parameters (step 2),
\theta_{\textsc{dla}}=({N_{\textsc{hi}}}_{\mathrm{,eff}},f_{\mathrm{cov}},z_{\mathrm{DLA}})
are the DLA parameters (step 3) and
\tau_{\textsc{igm}}(D_{b},{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
is the IGM optical depth (step 5).

We fit the spectra using Bayesian inference. Because resampling of prism spectra introduces covariance between adjacent pixels
(
Jakobsen et al., 2022
)
, we use the following likelihood for each source:

\ln p(\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{obs}}\,|\,\mathbf{\theta})=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{r}^{\intercal}K^{-1}\mathbf{r}-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[{2\pi\,\mathrm{det}(K)}\right]

(7)

where
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{mod}}
is the residual vector,
K
is the
N\times N
covariance matrix
6
6

6

Where
N
is the number of spectral pixels we fit over.
.

Based on estimates of the NIRSpec prism covariance matrix from multiple exposures in the GTO surveys (P. Jakobsen, priv. comm.), we assume the covariance matrix can be approximated as a near-diagonal matrix:

K_{ij}=\sigma^{2}_{i}\delta_{ij}+k(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})

(8)

where
\sigma_{i}
is the observational noise
in spectral pixel
\lambda_{i}
,
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta and
k
is a covariance function between adjacent spectral pixels
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}
:

k(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

(9)

i.e.
k=0
if
j\neq i\pm 1
.

We use Bayesian inference to infer the parameters
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}
,
D_{b}
and
\theta_{\mathrm{gal}}
(
=\theta_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}}},\theta_{\mathrm{DLA}}
) for each galaxy.
We note that, since the damping wing fits are most sensitive to
D_{b}
(see Appendix
A
), using sightlines labelled with both
D_{b}
and
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
makes the inference of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
for each galaxy explicitly conditional on
D_{b}
, thereby propagating uncertainties in the inferred
D_{b}
values into the uncertainties on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
For
z>10
sources without spectroscopic redshifts from emission lines we also fit for
z_{\mathrm{spec}}
as a free parameter, using a Gaussian prior for the redshift based on an initial fit to the Ly
\alpha
break.
We describe the setup for the inference and priors in Appendix
D
.

To understand the S/N requirements to obtain robust inferences we perform fits to mock spectra. As described in Section
2.1
, to accurately recover information about
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
requires being able to infer the distribution of distances of each galaxy to the neutral IGM,
D_{b}
. We find that to robustly recover bubble sizes
D_{b}\sim 5
cMpc (typical of the early stages of reionization, see Figure
2
) with prism spectra requires S/N
\geq
15 per pixel, while
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,20
can be recovered with S/N
\geq
5 per pixel. Because of the low resolution of the prism we can obtain only upper limits on smaller bubble sizes and column densities. These constraints are vastly improved with higher resolution data, as we will discuss in Section
6.3
. In Section
6.3
we also discuss prospects for reducing uncertainties in
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
with larger samples.
We describe the mock tests and validation of our model-fitting in more detail in Appendix
E
.

We plot all individual spectra, their best-fit BEAGLE models, and damping wing fits in Appendix
F
.

We show stacked spectra for our sample in five redshift bins in Figure

1350-1550

\alpha

z\sim 5-14
Ly

We resample all spectra onto a common wavelength grid with pixel size
10

Our stacks show the median and 16-84% range of the normalised spectra in each wavelength pixel.

To assess the relative contribution of local absorption and IGM absorption to the decline in transmission, in Figure

6
Discussion

JWST has opened a unique new window on the earliest stages of reionization by providing deep rest-frame UV to optical spectroscopy of
z\sim 6-14
galaxies. In Section
6.1
we discuss our results in the context of our understanding of reionization.
In Section
6.2
we discuss the nature of local neutral hydrogen absorption systems and in Section
6.3
we discuss future prospects for improving IGM constraints from galaxy damping wings.

6.1
The reionization process

Our empirical constraints from the stacked spectra and mean transmission (Section
5.1
) imply the IGM is approaching almost fully neutral at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,9
. Our inferred constraints on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
(Section
5.2
) also imply a mostly neutral IGM at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8
. These results are independent confirmation of previous ground-based efforts to constrain the reionization history at
z>7
via the damping wing attenuation in quasars
(
Davies et al., 2018
;
Wang et al., 2020
;
Greig et al., 2024a
)

and decline of Ly
\alpha
emission in Lyman-break galaxies
(
Stark et al., 2010
;
Schenker et al., 2014
;
Mason et al., 2019a
;
Bolan et al., 2022
)
. These results are also in agreement with recent independent analyses of JWST data based on the decline in the Ly
\alpha
EW distribution
(
Tang et al., 2024c
;
Kageura et al., 2025
)
and damping wings
(
Umeda et al., 2024
;
Park et al., 2025
)
which also point to a highly neutral IGM at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8
.
Our approach builds on early JWST damping wing analyses by including additional sources of uncertainties and mapping spectra to
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
based on inhomogeneous IGM simulations.

Mostly strikingly, in Section
5.1
we showed the spectra demonstrate a decrease in both the mean and variance of Ly
\alpha
transmission with increasing redshift. We interpret this as due to the decrease in size and variance of ionized regions with increasing redshift, as expected in the earliest stages of reionization
(
Mesinger and Furlanetto, 2007
;
Iliev et al., 2007
, e.g.,)
.
Tang et al. (2024c)
also find a decrease in the median Ly
\alpha
EW and variance of the EW distribution with redshift, which likely reflects the same signal. This can be seen as analogous to the decrease in the mean and variance of effective optical depths in the Ly
\alpha
forest at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,6

(
Eilers et al., 2019
;
Bosman et al., 2022
)
which mark the end of inhomogeneous reionization as the mean and variance in the sizes of neutral regions decrease
(
Keating et al., 2020
, e.g.,)
. Our results provide evidence we are now observing this process in reverse – probing the earliest stages of reionization.

In Figure
10
we show our
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
estimates along with previous constraints and simple theoretical models for the reionization timeline.
At
z\sim 5.5-8
our constraints are fully consistent with pre-JWST constraints from a number of independent probes (quasar damping wings, Ly
\alpha
forest dark pixel fraction, Ly
\alpha
EW distribution, Ly
\alpha
-emitter clustering). At
z>8
our
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
constraint is slightly lower than, though consistent within error bars, the constraint by
Tang et al. (2024c)
obtained from the Ly
\alpha
EW distribution in 48
z>8
galaxies the JWST public archive, the largest sample to-date used to constrain
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
at
z>8
.
If we exclude GNz11 our constraint on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
at
z>8
is a lower limit,
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}>0.70
(68% credible interval), driven mostly by the constraint from jades-3215-265801.
We attribute the difference between our result and that of
Tang et al. (2024c)
to several factors: our sample is significantly smaller due to our requirement of S/N
>15
prism spectra at
z>8
(just 6 sources), meaning we are more subject sample selection; most of our sources return low significance
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
constraints due to the moderate S/N; and finally, by fitting both IGM and local HI properties jointly we allow some of the decrease in transmission to be explained by local absorption (see e.g. Figure
9
).

Upcoming Cycle 3 NIRSpec surveys
(
Dickinson et al., 2024
;
Oesch et al., 2024
)
will significantly increase the sample of spectroscopically confirmed
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10
galaxies to
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,100
. High S/N spectra in these samples will hugely improve our ability to learn about the IGM at these redshifts, both via damping wing approaches as we have described, and the Ly
\alpha
EW distribution.
In the future, with larger samples, it could be most informative to infer distributions of
D_{b}
, rather than
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, as a function of redshift to different simulations, as this should track the size evolution of ionized regions in a model-independent way and shed light on the morphology of reionization.

The
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10
IGM contains key information about early star formation. In particular, models which end around the same time at
z\sim 6
can be driven by very different sources, but diverge at
z>9
, highlighting the importance of constraints on the IGM at these high redshifts. In Figure
10
we show a reionization history corresponding to if the excess in the UV luminosity density observed with JWST holds down to low luminosities, as indicated by deep observations
(
Pérez-González et al., 2023
;
Robertson et al., 2024
;
Whitler et al., 2025
)
. In this case, reionization could start early, and the IGM could be already
\sim 20\%
ionized at
z\sim 10

(
Gelli et al., 2024
, see also)
. This is interesting to note in relation to recent CMB analyses indicating the electron scattering optical depth may be higher than measured by
Planck Collaboration et al. (2020)

(
Pagano et al., 2020
;
de Belsunce et al., 2021
;
Giarè et al., 2024
)
. As discussed by
Asthana et al. (2024)
, an early start to reionization is not inconsistent with the requirement from the Lyman-
\alpha
forest that reionization is complete by
z\sim 5.3-6
. Current
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8
constraints, the tightest coming from the evolution of the Ly
\alpha
EW distribution
(
Nakane et al., 2024
;
Tang et al., 2024c
;
Jones et al., 2025
;
Kageura et al., 2025
)
, all imply a mostly neutral IGM at
z>8

(
Tang et al., 2024c
, e.g.,
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0.81^{+0.12}_{-0.24}
at
z\sim 8-10
)
, but do not yet reach the precision to rule out that the IGM may already be
\sim 10\%
ionized at
z\sim 10
. Future observations with large samples of deeper spectra will improve our estimates of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
(see Section
6.3
), providing important constraints on the onset of star formation.

6.2
Nature and evolution of local absorbers

In addition to neutral IGM, our sample demonstrates absorption due to HI gas within, or in close proximity to, the galaxies, as damped Ly
\alpha
absorbers (DLAs). As described in Section
2.2
, the existence of strong local absorption is not unexpected: HI dominates the volume of most galaxies, indeed the Milky Way disk is
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sim 10^{22}
cm
-2

(
Kalberla and Kerp, 2009
)
, and the massive stars which dominate our spectra are likely to reside in the densest regions of the ISM and experience high HI columns. Evidence for neutral gas in the ISM and CGM of galaxies is observed both in absorption and emission over a wide range of redshifts at
z\sim 0-6

(
Shapley et al., 2003
;
Steidel et al., 2010
;
Wisotzki et al., 2016
;
Tanvir et al., 2019
;
Pahl et al., 2020
;
Krogager et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
. These observations of both absorption features and Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Verhamme et al., 2015
, whose lineshape is primarily set by
N_{\textsc{hi}}
e.g.,)
imply high column densities of neutral gas in the ISM and/or CGM
(
Reddy et al., 2016
, with median
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sim 10^{20.3-21.0}
cm
-2
in
z\sim 3
LBGs,)
, though likely with non-uniform covering fractions (and low dust sightlines) enabling high EW Ly
\alpha
escape close to systemic velocity in some galaxies
(
Shapley et al., 2003
;
Heckman et al., 2001
;
Du et al., 2018
;
Hu et al., 2023
;
Tang et al., 2024b
, e.g.,)
.

JWST has extended the detection of DLAs in galaxy spectra to
z>5

(
Heintz et al., 2024b
;
Heintz et al., 2025
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
Hainline et al., 2024
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
, providing evidence for some systems with column densities
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,22
.
As described in Section
2.2
, only systems with column densities
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10^{22}
cm
-2
become challenging to distinguish from IGM absorption.
Such high column densities may be expected in the regions around young stars, before stellar feedback begins to disperse dense birth clouds. High resolution radiative hydrodynamic simulations predict
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sim 10^{21-23}
cm
-2
in these regions, and that feedback should open low density channels (i.e. a non-uniform covering fraction) and finally disperse the cloud within
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10-40
Myr
(
Kimm et al., 2019
;
Ma et al., 2020
;
Kakiichi and Gronke, 2021
)
, though the feedback mechanisms are still debated. Additionally, considerable opacity may come from the dense filaments and/or clumps in the CGM and local environment of massive halos
(
Rudie et al., 2012
;
Turner et al., 2017
, e.g.,)
.

In the context of reionization, it is important to understand to what extent the opacity due to local HI evolves with redshift and can impact IGM constraints.
We first explore the nature and evolution of Ly
\alpha
opacity due to local HI in our sample. We then discuss the impact of the decreased UV background during reionization on the Ly
\alpha
opacity within ionized regions.

We first examine whether there is a correlation between DLA candidates and dust attenuation, which could indicate absorption by dense gas in the source galaxy.

Finally, we discuss the impact of increased opacity due to lower column density absorbers in the ionized IGM. Observations of the Ly
\alpha
forest have revealed the UV background photoionization rate drops by a factor
\sim 10
at
z\sim 5-6

(
Becker and Bolton, 2013
;
Gaikwad et al., 2023
;
Davies et al., 2024
)
, as expected at the end stages of reionization before ionized regions fully merge. Hydrodynamical simulations predict a corresponding increase of Lyman-limit and sub-DLA absorption systems in ionized regions at
z\sim 5-6

(
Bolton and Haehnelt, 2013
;
Nasir et al., 2021
,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\sim 10^{17-19}
cm
-2
,)
as the lower UV background reduces the density threshold for self-shielding.
However, a substantial increase in DLAs (
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}>10^{20.3}
cm
-2
) is not predicted as those systems are already dense enough to self-shield.
As we showed in Figure
4
,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}<10^{20.3}
cm
-2
absorbers are subdominant to the neutral IGM damping wing at
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,1220
Å.
An increase in sub-DLAs and LLS can suppress Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Bolton and Haehnelt, 2013
;
Weinberger et al., 2019
)
, however
Mesinger et al. (2015)
demonstrated evolution in the neutral IGM dominates the opacity, assuming Ly
\alpha
is offset by
{\Delta v}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,200
km/s from systemic, where the damping wing from sub-DLAs is minimal.
At
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
such high offsets appear common, even in strong Ly
\alpha
emitters: with
Tang et al. (2024a)
finding a median
{\Delta v}\approx 230
km/s in strong Ly
\alpha
emitters (EW
>40
Å).
Thus we do not expect our results to be significantly impacted by an increase in opacity in the ionized IGM.

We conclude that current data suggest no strong redshift evolution of local HI column densities at
z\sim 3-13
.
Future deep, high resolution NIRSpec spectra could provide more insights into the location and nature of absorbing gas, by measuring damped Ly
\alpha
troughs to determine the redshift of absorbing gas, detecting low ionization interstellar absorption lines, which trace high HI columns and covering fractions in the host galaxy
(
Shapley et al., 2003
, e.g.,)
, and could also be used to determine the redshift of proximate absorbers
(
Christensen et al., 2023
;
Davies et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
. High resolution NIRSpec spectra would also provide crucial tests for continuum models.

With the exquisite spectroscopic capabilities of JWST, the prospects for using galaxies to probe the earliest stages of reionization at
z>9
are promising.
We first discuss prospects for improving our understanding of the impact of the IGM, Ly
\alpha
emission and local absorbers on prism spectra with higher S/N and higher spectral resolution data, and then discuss the prospects for both overcoming and utilising cosmic variance in the IGM.

Firstly, the most obvious improvements to our approach for fitting IGM damping wings will come from higher S/N and higher resolution spectra. While we see clear evolution in the spectral stacks and evolution of the mean transmission around the Ly
\alpha
break, fitting the damping wings is still challenging for most sources in the public archive given the low S/N of the spectra. In Appendix
E
we demonstrate we require S/N per pixel
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,15
to gain unbiased constraints on
D_{b}
from prism spectra, which can thus be mapped to constraints on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
providing we sample a sufficient number of galaxies, which we will discuss below. However, even with very high S/N, the prism provides a rather blunt view of the IGM: we find a median uncertainty of
\approx 0.5
dex on
D_{b}
in our tests with S/N
=
100 per pixel. Our knowledge is limited by the
R\sim 40
resolution of the prism at
\sim 1\,\mu
m, where the impact of the damping wing is compressed into
\sim 5
spectral pixels (see Figure
3
).
Therefore, while prism spectra provide a powerful initial view of the early stages of reionization
(
Curtis-Lake et al., 2023
;
Umeda et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
, a full understanding requires higher resolution spectroscopy and precise constraints on the evolution of Ly
\alpha
emission with redshift
(
Nakane et al., 2024
;
Tang et al., 2024c
, e.g.,)
.

At higher resolution, constraints on
D_{b}
using our damping wing fitting approach become much more precise. We demonstrate this in Figure
13
where we show the number of galaxies required to constrain
\Delta{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.2
using either the prism or G140M gratings (see also Appendix
E
).
As described in Section
2.1
, constraints on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
effectively come from measuring the distribution of
D_{b}
at a given redshift and comparing it to predictions from simulations (e.g. Figure
2
).
To investigate the number of galaxies required to robustly infer
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, we sample
D_{b}
from the distributions in our simulations at a given
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
(Section
2.1.1
, shown as dotted lines in Figure
13
), and calculate the median
D_{b}
we would recover from
N
mock galaxies using our approach.
For this forecast, we assume S/N
\approx 20
per pixel for the prism and S/N
\approx 5
for the grating. We chose these values based on our tests to mock spectra (Appendix
E
), where we recover an average uncertainty on
D_{b}
of
\approx 0.7
and
\approx 0.3
dex for the prism and G140M observations respectively. We sample
D_{b}
from the simulated
p(D_{b}\,|\,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
distributions, additionally sampling from the Gaussian uncertainties on
D_{b}
given above, for 200 realisations of
N
galaxies. In this way, our forecasts account for both the measurement uncertainty on
D_{b}
and the broad
p(D_{b}\,|\,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
distribution predicted by the simulations.
We find G140M observations can constrain
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}>0.9
with
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
galaxies, compared to
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,20
galaxies with the prism.
Current grating spectra do not reach this S/N in the rest-frame UV
but a S/N
\approx 5
spectrum for
m_{\mathrm{AB}}=26
source would require
\sim 30
hr integration in G140M, feasible for the brightest
z>8
sources.
The increase in precision expected with grating spectra is due to the increased resolution around the Ly
\alpha
break, enabling a better estimate of the IGM damping wing (see Figure
19
).

Ultimately, higher resolution spectroscopy will provide the best constraints on early IGM properties as weak Ly
\alpha
emission can be resolved, which is most sensitive to the IGM opacity in the early stages of reionization (see Figure
3
). Furthermore, grating spectroscopy will provide important validation of our approach for marginalising over Ly
\alpha
emission in prism spectra and better distinguish the impact of local absorbers.
In the prism the Ly
\alpha
line is spread over most of the pixels including the damping wing feature
(
Keating et al., 2024a
;
Park et al., 2025
, see Figure
3
and)
.
Jones et al. (2024)
and
Chen et al. (2024)
showed this limits the minimum detectable Ly
\alpha
EW of
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,50
Å for galaxies with our median
{M_{\textsc{uv}}}\sim-19
.
Thus, accurate estimates of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
and
N_{\textsc{hi}}
using our approach in prism spectra rely on accurate models, or direct measurements, of the emergent Ly
\alpha
emission (see Section
4
).
While significant progress has been made since the launch of JWST in linking Ly
\alpha
emission to other observables
(
Prieto-Lyon et al., 2023
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
Tang et al., 2024a
, e.g.,)
, which we have utilised here, deep NIRSpec grating spectra can easily resolve weak Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Saxena et al., 2024
, see Figure
3
, e.g.,)
. In high S/N grating spectra (
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
per pixel) we can make direct measurements of the UV continuum
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
Å redward of Ly
\alpha
, without any contamination from the line (and also NV
\lambda
1240 which may be present in sources dominated by young massive stars), and measure absorption troughs and metal absorption lines to more confidently establish the presence of DLAs.

Combining grating and prism spectra will therefore be an important next step to validating our approach
(
Curti et al., 2025
, see also)
, as we can better recover
N_{\textsc{hi}}
and
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
from prism spectra if the Ly
\alpha
EW is known.
However, the current public sample of sources with robust Ly
\alpha
detections in grating data is still small (just 11 at
z>6.5
), and only 3 sources at
z>9
have G140M spectra
(
Tang et al., 2024c
)
.
In addition, high resolution spectra will provide critical tests of our ability to model the continuum in prism spectra. For example, several sources in our sample show relatively flat continua around the break, potentially due to unresolved interstellar absorption features
(
Boyett et al., 2024
)
, which can be resolved with deep G140M spectra.
Future deep grating surveys will greatly improve our knowledge of the earliest stages of reionization.

Secondly, overcoming the large ‘cosmic variance’ in the IGM will require more independent sightlines
(
Taylor and Lidz, 2014
;
Bruton et al., 2023b
, e.g.,)
. This is because the typical sizes of ionized regions are comparable to or larger than both the field of view of JWST and the line-of-sight distance which contributes to the IGM damping wing
(
Lu et al., 2024
,
R\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10-100
cMpc, see Section
2.1
, e.g.)
.
Thus, spatially correlated ionized regions impose an uncertainty floor in the neutral fraction which can be measured in a single field with JWST.
We demonstrate this in Figure
14
where we show the standard deviation of the volume-averaged IGM neutral fraction within mock survey volumes: we make 100 cMpc (
\Delta z\approx 0.4
) skewers with different field areas for a range of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
in our
z=9
simulations. We compare surveys of multiple independent NIRSpec pointings to contiguous fields. It is clear that independent pointings reduce this sightline variance as
\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{fields}}}
, whereas the uncertainty remains fairly constant even for contiguous areas
>100\,
sq. arcmin (similar to the CEERS or JADES fields). Specifically, when the cosmic
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.5
the volume probed in a single 100 sq. arcmin field is expected to have
\sigma({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})\approx 0.2
(see Figure
1
).

We can clearly see the impact of this cosmic variance in our sample, which covers three fields (GOODS-S, EGS and Abell 2744). For example, the majority of our
z\sim 7-8
spectra (8/13 sources) come from the EGS field observed by CEERS, which is known to be a large candidate ionized region
(
Tilvi et al., 2020
;
Jung et al., 2022
;
Tang et al., 2023
;
Chen et al., 2024
;
Napolitano et al., 2024
)
. This is likely the cause of the high mean transmission at this redshift (Figure
8
). Current results are also limited by our small sample size – this is apparent in our
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,8
constraints on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, where the lack of strong damping in GNz11 lowers our
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
estimate
(
Bruton et al., 2023a
, see also,)
: overcoming this sample variance will require tens of deep spectra.
Future surveys of more sightlines could exploit the sightline variance itself, as it is related to the typical sizes of ionized regions
(
Lu et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
.

7
Conclusions

We have investigated the redshift evolution of the Ly
\alpha
break in 99
z\sim 5.5-13
galaxies with publicly available JWST/NIRSpec prism spectra in the context of reionization.
We fit a sub-sample of high S/N spectra using an approach which takes into account Ly
\alpha
emission, local HI absorption and IGM HI absorption using sightlines drawn from realistic inhomogeneous reionization simulations.
Our main conclusions are as follows:

1.

We observe a decline in both the mean and variance of flux around the Ly
\alpha
-break with increasing redshift in our sample, demonstrating strong Ly
\alpha
emission is disappearing at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,7
and the spectra become increasingly ‘damped’. We find a median and 68% range of transmission is
\langle T\rangle=0.80^{+1.49}_{-0.47}
at
z<6
, falling to
\langle T\rangle=0.40^{+0.41}_{-0.19}
at
z>10
. We attribute this to the decreasing mean and variance in the size of ionized regions as expected in the early stages of reionization. At
z>9
,
\approx 80\%
of spectra are consistent with a neutral IGM, compared to
<40\%
at
z<9
.

2.

We fit spectra to obtain posterior distributions for the distance of galaxies from neutral IGM, the volume-averaged IGM neutral fraction
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
, and the local absorber column density
N_{\textsc{hi}}
, for each galaxy.
We find IGM properties can be reliably recovered using our approach in prism spectra with S/N
\mathchar 0\sim
>
15 per pixel, though even with the highest S/N the low resolution of the prism limits recovered distance to the neutral IGM to
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.5
dex. We demonstrate this can be reduced substantially with high resolution grating data.

3.

Using 14 sources with sufficient S/N we obtain posterior distributions for
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
in two redshift bins.
We find
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0.33^{+0.18}_{-0.27},0.64^{+0.17}_{-0.23}
(including sightline variance) at

z\approx 6.5,9.3
(
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}>0.70
excluding GNz11), providing additional evidence for a mostly neutral IGM at
z>8
, consistent with independent JWST analyses of the Ly
\alpha
EW distribution
(
Nakane et al., 2024
;
Tang et al., 2024c
;
Jones et al., 2025
;
Kageura et al., 2025
)
and galaxy damping wings
(
Umeda et al., 2024
)
.

4.

Exploring local HI absorption in our sample, we find a median
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 10^{20.8}
cm
-2
, comparable to that observed in
z\sim 3
LBGs
(
Reddy et al., 2016
)
, with no significant redshift evolution.
At
z\sim 5.5-6
in GOODS-S, where our sample has high spectroscopic completeness, we find 5/6 sources which show strong DLA absorption signatures have at least one spectroscopically confirmed neighbour within
r_{\mathrm{3D}}<500
pkpc, compared to 6/12 for sources without DLA absorption signatures. This adds to the evidence that strong Ly
\alpha
absorption may be preferentially associated with galaxies in the most massive dark matter halos
(
Chen et al., 2024
)
.

The spectroscopic sensitivity and wavelength coverage of JWST/NIRSpec provide a unique opportunity to reveal the earliest stages of hydrogen reionization. Upcoming Cycle 3 surveys
(
Dickinson et al., 2024
;
Oesch et al., 2024
)
are expected to obtain prism spectra of
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,100

z>10
galaxies, providing an unprecedented dataset to constrain the properties of the IGM at
z\sim 10-15
and infer the properties of the faint first galaxies beyond even JWST’s detection limits. Fully exploiting JWST observations to understand the early evolution of the IGM using the approach described here will require S/N
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,15
prism spectra and deep, high resolution follow-up studies.

Comparison with analytic damping wings

As described in Section
2
there is significant sightline variance in the IGM during reionization, thus the assumption of a uniform IGM can bias the recovery of
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}
, as the relationship between
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}
,
D_{b}
and the transmission is not deterministic. This has been previously discussed in detail by
Mesinger and Furlanetto (2008)
and we demonstrate this effect with our simulations here.

For every galaxy in our grid of simulations spanning
{x_{\textsc{hi}}}\in[0,1]
at
z=9
, we calculate the distance to the first neutral region,
D_{b}
, and the Ly
\alpha
transmission 200 km/s redward of Ly
\alpha
line centre from the IGM damping wing (calculated over each galaxy’s sightline as described in Section
2.1
).
In the top panel of Figure
15
we plot the median transmission at
+200
km/s redward of Ly
\alpha
, taking the median of galaxies in bins of
D_{b}
, as a function of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
. In the lower panel we plot the Ly
\alpha
transmission 200 km/s redward of Ly
\alpha
line centre predicted by the
Miralda-Escude (1998)
uniform IGM approximation, also as a function of
D_{b}
and
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
. We also show the mean bubble size predicted in the simulations as a function of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
The transmission obtained from the inhomogeneous IGM simulations demonstrates the damping wing optical depth depends most strongly on
D_{b}
, with little dependence on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Mesinger and Furlanetto 2008
;
Chen 2024
;
Keating et al. 2024b
, see also,)
.

We see the uniform IGM approximation works least well when both
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
and
D_{b}
are low,
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.5
, and
D_{b}<30
cMpc.
Under the uniform IGM assumption, a galaxy a short distance from a neutral patch in a mostly ionized IGM is predicted to have very high transmission
\sim 100\%
, as the approximation assumes the neutral patch is
\ll 100\%
neutral, decreasing its optical depth, when in reality the optical depth should be higher.
The uniform IGM approximation will thus overpredict Ly
\alpha
transmission in this case, and thus lead to overestimates in
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
The sensitivity of the damping wing transmission to the distance of galaxies to neutral gas is clear motivation for using a realistic IGM simulation as a prior for
p(D_{b}\,|\,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
. At a given redshift, the inferred distribution of
D_{b}
provides information about
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.

Transmission profiles

Here we describe some additional effects which impact Ly
\alpha
transmission profiles, illustrated at the resolution of NIRSpec prism and G140M grating in Figure
16
: (1) the HI column density
N_{\textsc{hi}}
; (2) the covering fraction of local HI
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
; (3) a proximate absorber along the line of sight; and (4) galaxies for which a precise spectroscopic redshift cannot be measured from emission lines. In all plots the grey solid line shows the input mock spectrum at
z=10
without any attenuation from DLAs or the IGM.

The top panel of Figure
16
shows the impact of local HI absorbers both with and without attenuation from the neutral IGM (thick vs thin lines) as a function of
N_{\textsc{hi}}
(coloured lines). At fixed
N_{\textsc{hi}}
, the neutral IGM produces more attenuation at redder wavelengths than local absorbers alone, meaning these can be distinguished with sufficient S/N and resolution.

The second panel shows the transmission due to local HI gas, including a non-uniform covering fraction
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
. In this case the transmission is given by
(
Rivera-Thorsen et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
:

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{DLA}}(\Delta\lambda)=1-f_{\mathrm{cov}}\left(1-e^{-\tau_{\mathrm{DLA}}(\Delta\lambda)}\right).

(10)

Where
\tau_{\mathrm{DLA}}
is given by Equation
4
. Here we show an example with
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}=10^{22}
cm
-2
, applying a fully neutral IGM to the damped cases. Reducing the covering fraction increases the transmitted flux redward of Ly
\alpha
line centre.

The third panel also shows a
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}=10^{22}
cm
-2
local absorber, but where the absorbing gas is not located in the emitting source but has a peculiar velocity,
\Delta v_{\textsc{dla}}
, which also increases transmission redward of Ly
\alpha
line centre.
At the resolution of the prism, cases with non-zero covering fraction,
f_{\mathrm{cov}}\sim 0.5
and large peculiar velocities,
\Delta v_{\textsc{dla}}\sim-5000
km/s, can produce very similar spectra, introducing a degeneracy. As discussed in Section
6.2
we consider the local non-uniform covering fraction a likely more physical picture of the local absorption.

In the bottom row of Figure
16
we show that even without a precise spectroscopic redshift from emission lines it should still be possible to get information about the IGM, but that there is a degeneracy between

and redshift. We show that at the resolution of the NIRSpec prism, in a fully neutral IGM, a source at redshift

with a local absorber column density of

>1240
Å more strongly than the DLA, we should still be able to recover some information about the IGM in either case (i.e. the coloured lines are significantly different from the grey line). This means we are still able to use sources at

, even without a spectroscopic redshift determination from e.g. [OIII] emission lines.

Appendix C
Evolution of stacked spectra

Figure 17:
Median stacked spectra in redshift bins as in Figure
6
. At
z<6
(top panel) we show the median and 68% range of our template spectra in a fully ionized IGM, convolved to the prism resolution (grey line and shaded region). At
z\geq 6
we show the predicted median and 68% range of spectra assuming four IGM/CGM evolution models (grey line and shaded region, described in Appendix
C
):
(1) an increasingly neutral IGM following the
Mason et al. (2019b)
reionization history;
(2) DLA column densities increase with increase cosmic density,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\propto(1+z)^{2}
;
(3) the same density evolution and at
z>6
a minimum
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}>20.3
due to the lower UV background;
(4) assuming the same density evolution and that
f_{\mathrm{cov}}=1
at
z>6
.
The spectra are most consistent with the majority of the evolution being driven by the IGM evolution.

To gain intuition into the redshift evolution of stacked spectra (Figure
6
) we explore four simple physically motivated models.
Because of the low resolution of the prism, this requires using high-resolution
R\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,1000
spectral templates, applying the models, and then convolving with the prism resolution. As no
R>1000
templates for the faint
z\sim 5-6
galaxies in our sample exist yet, we construct them from fits to our
z<6
sample as described in Section
4
, naturally including any local absorption in the ISM and CGM of galaxies at
z\sim 5.5-6
. We take samples from the posteriors for each
z<6
galaxy (29 galaxies) to generate template spectra with a resolution of
R\sim 4000
to which we can apply transmission models.

We show the median and 68% range of these templates, normalised and convolved to the resolution of the prism, as the grey line and shaded region in the top panels of Figure
17
.
We then apply four simple models and describe their predictions for the evolution of the spectra:

1.

Neutral IGM:
We apply damping wings drawn from our IGM simulations (Section
2.1.1
) to the templates, assuming
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}(z)
predicted by
Mason et al. (2019b)
.
This model predicts a decrease in both the mean flux and variance around Ly
\alpha
as ionized regions become too rare and small to transmit significant flux
(
Mason et al. 2018a
, e.g.,)
. In Figure
17
, we see our stacks agree qualitatively very well with this model.

2.

Local absorber density evolution:
Assuming DLA column density increases with increasing cosmic density,
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\propto(1+z)^{2}
. This assumes that most of the evolution is due to an increase in density in the CGM, and that the CGM is mostly neutral at
z\sim 6

(
Stern et al. 2021
, as predicted by some hydrodynamic simulations, e.g.,)
.
This model predicts it would still be possible to observe strong Ly
\alpha
emission at
z>8
, producing spectra which are inconsistent with the observations at
z>8
where we do not detect strong Ly
\alpha
emission. This is because at
z<6
many spectra require low column densities (
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}<10^{19}
cm
-2
) to explain the strong Ly
\alpha
emission in this bin, and the
(1+z)^{2}
evolution will not produce enough opacity to significantly damp Ly
\alpha
in all sources at
z>8
. Thus we do not consider pure density evolution in the CGM a primary driver of the observed evolution in the prism spectra.

3.

UV background evolution:
Assuming an increase in LLS and sub-DLAs (
17.2<\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}<20.3
),
within ionized regions at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
as the UV background drops before ionized regions merge
(
Bolton and Haehnelt 2013
;
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Nasir et al. 2021
, as predicted by hydrodynamical simulations)
.
To explore this we impose a minimum
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}>20.3
in the local absorber model, but note this likely overestimates the importance of absorbers.
This model is also not a good match to the observations for two reasons: firstly, the evolution in self-shielded systems is expected to occur rapidly at the end of reionization
(
Nasir et al. 2021
, e.g.)
, so we would expect a sharp increase in absorption systems with redshift at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,6
. In our data we see strong Ly
\alpha
emission at
z\sim 5-7
, implying the spectra cannot be fully explained by a rapid evolution in self-shielding systems at the end of reionization. Secondly, the predicted continuum in this model at
z>8
is higher than the observed spectra, as not all sources have high enough column densities to significantly damp the continuum, implying additional neutral IGM attenuation is still needed (see Figure
4
).

4.

Covering fraction evolution:

We assume an extreme model where the covering fraction of local HI
f_{\mathrm{cov}}=1
at
z>6
. This is motivated by some hydrodynamical simulations showing an increase in covering fractions in the CGM with increasing redshift
(
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Tortora et al. 2024
)
.
However, we note
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
depends strongly on feedback prescriptions and resolution in simulations
(
Faucher-Giguère et al. 2016
;
van de Voort et al. 2019
)
.
This model underpredicts the observed spectra at
z\sim 6-7
(because it predicts strong Ly
\alpha
is all absorbed), but overpredicts the observed spectra at
z>8
(for the same reason as in the UVB evolution case). A gradual increase in
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
with redshift could contribute to some of the observed evolution of the stacks. We will discuss this further in Section
6.2
.

Thus we conclude that, while local absorption is present in the observed spectra over all redshifts, the observations are most consistent with the majority of the redshift evolution being driven by the neutral IGM evolution.

Appendix D
Bayesian inference setup and priors

We use Bayesian inference to infer the parameters
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}
,
D_{b}
and
\theta_{\mathrm{gal}}
(
=\theta_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}}},\theta_{\mathrm{DLA}}
) for each galaxy. For
z>10
sources without spectroscopic redshifts from emission lines we also fit for
z_{\mathrm{spec}}
, using a Gaussian prior for the redshift based on an initial fit to the Ly
\alpha
break.
The posterior for each galaxy, with observed spectrum
f_{\mathrm{i,obs}}
and properties
\phi_{\mathrm{gal},i}
(
M_{\textsc{uv}}
, OIII+H
\beta
EW) is:

\displaystyle p({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},D_{b},\theta_{\mathrm{gal}}\,|\,f_{\mathrm{i,obs}},\phi_{\mathrm{gal}})=(f_{\mathrm{i,obs}}\,|\,\theta_{\mathrm{gal}},D_{b},{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})

\displaystyle\times p(\theta_{\mathrm{gal}}\,|\,\phi_{\mathrm{gal}})p(D_{b}\,|\,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},\phi_{\mathrm{gal}})p({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})

(11)

Here
(f_{\mathrm{i,obs}}\,|\,\theta_{\mathrm{gal}},D_{b},{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
is the likelihood (Equation
7
) as described in Section
4
.

We use a conditional prior
p(D_{b}\,|\,{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},\phi_{\mathrm{gal}})
from our simulations (Section
2.1.1
), selecting sightlines based on
M_{\textsc{uv}}
to account for brighter galaxies being more likely to be in larger bubbles
(step 5 above,
Mason et al. 2018a
, though this does not have a large impact on our results)
.
Using this prior makes the inference of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
for each galaxy explicitly conditional on
D_{b}
, thereby propagating uncertainties in the inferred

values into the uncertainties on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
.
As the damping wings are relatively independent of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
at fixed

(see Appendix

), the redshift evolution of
D_{b}
should provide the most empirical evidence for IGM evolution, regardless of the mapping to
x_{\textsc{hi}}
.
At
z\leq 6.3
we use half-Gaussian priors on
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}
based on the Ly
\alpha
+
\beta

Jin et al. (2023)
. At higher redshifts we assume a uniform prior on
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=[0,1]
.

We use the empirical distributions of Ly
\alpha
EW by
Tang et al. (2024a)
as a prior on the
emergent
Ly
\alpha
EW (i.e. after transmission through the ISM and CGM, calculated after step 4 above), such that at
z\sim 5-6
we should recover the observed Ly
\alpha
EW distribution, and at
z>6
the observed EW distribution does not exceed that at
z\sim 5-6

(
Pentericci et al. 2014
;
Mason et al. 2019a
;
Jung et al. 2020
;
Tang et al. 2024c
, which is reasonable based on NIRSpec grating and ground-based spectra, e.g.)
. These distributions are derived from
>700

z\sim 5-6
Lyman-break galaxies with ground-based Ly
\alpha
spectroscopy from Keck and JWST photometry. Due to the high resolution (
R\sim 4000
) of the ground-based spectroscopy, these EW measurements will not be impacted by local absorption, unlike in the prism where Ly
\alpha
emission and local absorption are blended. Thus, this prior should be informative for recovering the
N_{\textsc{hi}}
distribution from the prism.
For sources at
z\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10
, where [OIII]+H
\beta
is detectable in NIRCam and NIRSpec, we use the EW model by
Tang et al. (2024a)
conditional on [OIII]+H
\beta
EW (whereby sources with strong [OIII]+H
\beta
EW are more likely to have strong Ly
\alpha
). For sources without [OIII]+H
\beta
measurements we use the EW distribution conditional on
M_{\textsc{uv}}
. Following
Tang et al. (2024a)
we apply a slit-loss correction of 0.8 to map predicted Ly
\alpha
fluxes based on VLT/MUSE measurements to the NIRSpec slits.
Similarly, we use the Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets model by
Mason et al. (2018b)
as a prior on the
emergent
velocity offset.
We use a uniform prior on
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\in[17,23.5]
, noting that for
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}/\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,20
, unless there is strong Ly
\alpha
emission, at the resolution of the prism we can only obtain an upper limit on
N_{\textsc{hi}}
(see Figure
4
). Motivated by
z\sim 3
results by
Reddy et al. (2016)
which imply HI covering fractions in the ISM are high (
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,90\%)
,we use a prior which is uniform in
\log(1-f_{\mathrm{cov}})
:
p(f_{\mathrm{cov}})=1/(1-f_{\mathrm{cov}}+\epsilon
). This avoids non-physical scenarios with very high
N_{\textsc{hi}}
and low
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
.

We obtain the posterior for each galaxy,
p({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}},D_{b},\theta_{\mathrm{gal},i}\,|\,f_{\mathrm{i,obs}})
using Markov Chain Monte Carlo with the
emcee
sampler
(
Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
.
We fit over the rest-frame wavelength range
1100-1400
Å, which we find provides the most robust recovery of parameters in mock spectra, and contains no other UV emission lines except NV which is expected to be very weak for stellar populations
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
Myr
(
Chisholm et al. 2019
)
.
We use 50 walkers and
\sim 10^{4-5}
steps, such that the chain is
>50\times
the integrated autocorrelation time for the number of fitted parameters, and discard the first 50% of the chain.
To obtain the marginalised
p({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\,|\,\{f_{\mathrm{obs}}\})
we take the resulting
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
samples from each galaxy and fit a smooth function with a Gaussian Kernel Density Estimation.
The final posterior of
p({\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\,|\,\{f_{\mathrm{obs}}\})
at a given redshift is then the product of the individual
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
posteriors in each bin (Section
5.2
). In Figure
18
we show the individual
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
posteriors for the 14 SNR
>15
galaxies in our sample, in two redshift bins (see Section
5.2
), and their combined posterior.

Figure 18:
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
posteriors for each galaxy in our high SNR sample (thin coloured lines) and the combined posterior in each redshift bin (thick black line). We note this posterior does not include the additional IGM cosmic variance (Section
6.3
) we add to the constraints shown in Figure
10
, see Section
5.2
for discussion.

Validation of fitting

The low resolution of the prism limits the accuracy of constraints, while robust constraints could be obtained even for low S/N (

per pixel) with G140M.

Figure 20:
Recovered posteriors for an example mock prism spectrum with S/N
=50
per pixel. All input parameters (shown as red crosshairs) are recovered well within 2
\sigma
. Our constraint on
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
is driven by the prior
p(D_{b}|{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
(Figure
2
). As expected from the prior, given our recovered
D_{b}\approx 5
cMpc, the most likely
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 0.7
, which drives our constraint here. This demonstrates how accurately recovering
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
from prism spectra requires
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,10
galaxies per redshift bin to estimate distribution of
D_{b}
(see discussion in Section
6.3
.

We validate our fits using mock data and describe our key results here. As described in Sections
2.1
and
6.3
, and Appendix
A
, the IGM damping wing optical depth depends most strongly on
D_{b}
, and so by essentially estimating the distribution of
D_{b}
at a given redshift, we can map to
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}

(
Kist et al. 2025a
, Figure
2
, see also e.g.)
. Thus, we first demonstrate what S/N per pixel is required to robustly recover the most important parameters –
D_{b}
and
N_{\textsc{hi}}
. We then show an example of fitting a high S/N mock prism spectrum showing the recovery of the other parameters. In Section
6.3
we investigate how many sources are required to accurately recover
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
to
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.2
.

We generate mock spectra from the BEAGLE fits to our sample as templates, and apply IGM damping wings and DLA optical depths on a grid of
D_{b}
and
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}
values. We then add noise, accounting for the covariance between adjacent pixels (Equation
8
), to the model spectra and fit the spectra using the approach described in Section
4
, verifying that input parameters can be recovered well for high S/N spectra.

We demonstrate the impact of S/N on our parameter recovery in Figure
19
. This shows the median and 68% range of the maximum likelihood recovered values of distance to neutral IGM,
D_{b}
, and local column density,
N_{\textsc{hi}}
as a function of S/N per pixel, from mock spectra. For each S/N value we generate 100 realisations of the flux given the covariance matrix (obtained by rescaling the observed error spectrum for our template source).
We show the recovered parameters for 6 input combinations of
D_{b}=[1,10,100]
cMpc and
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}=[19,21]
for both mock prism observations and G140M observations.

Figure
19
demonstrates that IGM and DLA properties can be robustly recovered from prism spectra, but that there can be large uncertainties in parameters recovered from prism spectra, even for very high S/N spectra (
\sim 0.5
dex for S/N
\sim 50
spectra). This is due to the low resolution, limiting our ability to distinguish small changes in the shape of the continuum. By contrast, G140M observations promise to provide precise constraints on both
D_{b}
and
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}
for spectra with S/N
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
.

We see S/N
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,15
is required to robustly recover the input
D_{b}
from prism spectra, and that for small input bubble sizes, there can be a bias to larger bubble sizes and higher
N_{\textsc{hi}}
with low S/N spectra. This is because the shape of the damping wing is mostly insensitive to
D_{b}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,5
cMpc (see Figure
3
), so negative noise fluctuations will not significantly shift the inferred
D_{b}
lower, while positive noise fluctuations will always result in a higher inferred
D_{b}
. Future work could potentially mitigate this bias by e.g. modifying the likelihood form or using machine learning approaches
(
Chen et al. 2023
;
Park et al. 2025
)
. This bias is not present in grating observations due to the higher resolution around the break.
We find
N_{\textsc{hi}}
can be recovered well, to within
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,0.2
dex, for S/N
\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}\,5
per pixel in both prism and grating spectra. We note that the shape of the damping wing in the continuum as seen in prism is mostly insensitive to
{N_{\textsc{hi}}}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,10^{20}
cm
-2
, so in those cases we only return upper limits.

In Figure
20
we show an example of the recovered posteriors from fitting a mock spectrum. For this example we apply IGM damping wings (using a simulated sightline at
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 1
with
D_{b}=5
cMpc), a DLA with
\log_{10}{N_{\textsc{hi}}}=21.0
and
f_{\mathrm{cov}}=1
, and an emergent Ly
\alpha
emission line (i.e. pre-IGM absorption) with EW=
30
Å,
{\Delta v}=FWHM=200
km/s. We add uncertainties to the spectrum assuming S/N
=50
per pixel. We see all input parameters can be recovered well within
2\sigma
, though the uncertainties are large, as expected from Figure
19
. This figure demonstrates there is some degeneracy between
N_{\textsc{hi}}
and
D_{b}
, and between
N_{\textsc{hi}}
and
f_{\mathrm{cov}}
, but that a low
D_{b}\,\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 3.5pt\hbox{$\mathchar 0\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}\,20
cMpc can be clearly recovered even when
N_{\textsc{hi}}
is high (
>10^{21}
cm
-2
). We see
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
is the least well-recovered parameter. As described in Section
2.1
, our recovery of
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
depends on estimating the distribution of
D_{b}
at a given redshift, so our recovered
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
depends on our prior
p(D_{b}|{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}})
. In this example, we see we recover
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}=0.64^{+0.23}_{-0.30}
. This is consistent with the expectation from Figure
2
that 5 cMpc bubbles are most common when
{\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}}\approx 0.7
. This highlights how observations of multiple galaxies are required to overcome the sightline variance in the IGM and accurately recover
\overline{x}_{\textsc{hi}}
at a given redshift. We explore this more quantitively in Section
6.3
.

A note on Duality and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence
Maximilian Hans

Abstract
. We show that, for a finite spectrum X X , the Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism induces an isomorphism between the cohomological and homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences. As an application, it follows that Poincaré duality for a Poincaré duality complex, which is oriented over a ring spectrum ℰ \mathscr{E} , induces an isomorphism between the two spectral sequences.



1.
Introduction

Spanier–Whitehead duality assembles into a duality functor
\mathbb{D}\colon(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}
on finite spectra. For an arbitrary finite spectrum
\mathscr{E}
, the identification
\mathbb{D}(X)\simeq\mathscr{F}(X,\mathbb{S})
yields the Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism
\mathscr{S}\mathscr{W}\colon\mathscr{E}^{k}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{-k}(\mathbb{D}(X))
by adjunction. A CW filtration on
X
induces a dual CW filtration on
\mathbb{D}(X)
. We investigate the interplay between the Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence using these filtrations. The result may be known to experts, but we were unable to locate a precise reference in the literature.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a spectrum, and
X
a finite spectrum. The Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism
\mathscr{S}\mathscr{W}\colon\mathscr{E}^{*}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{-*}(\mathbb{D}(X))
induces an isomorphism between the cohomological and homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences computing
\mathscr{E}^{*}(X)
and
\mathscr{E}_{-*}(\mathbb{D}(X))
respectively. More precisely, there is an isomorphism
\smash{(E^{p,q}_{r},d_{r})\rightarrow(E_{-p,-q}^{r},d^{r})}
on every page of the two spectral sequences induced by Spanier–Whitehead duality.

The unital and multiplicative structure provided by a ring spectrum
\mathscr{E}
naturally induces a notion of
\mathscr{E}
-orientability for a
d
-dimensional Poincaré duality complex
X
, together with a Poincaré duality isomorphism
\smash{\mathrm{PD}\colon\mathscr{E}^{k}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{d-k}(X)}
between cohomology and homology. Using the fact that Poincaré duality factors as the composition of the Thom–Dold isomorphism followed by the Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism, we use
A
to show that the Poincaré duality isomorphism extends to an isomorphism between the cohomological and homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences. This allows us to leverage knowledge of differentials in the cohomological spectral sequence to obtain differentials in the homological version. For closed manifolds, this result was previously shown by Vick
[
Vic73
]
using properties of the slant product.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a ring spectrum, and
X
a
d
-dimensional Poincaré duality complex oriented over
\mathscr{E}
. Poincaré duality for the ring spectrum
\mathscr{E}
induces an isomorphism between the cohomological and homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences computing
\mathscr{E}^{*}(X)
and
\mathscr{E}_{d-*}(X)
respectively. More precisely, there is an isomorphism
\smash{(E^{p,q}_{r},d_{r})\rightarrow(E_{d-p,-q}^{r},d^{r})}
on every page of the two spectral sequences induced by Poincaré duality.

Spectra

Throughout this article, we work with the
\infty
-category of spaces
\mathscr{S}
, and the stable
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathrm{Sp}
, defined as the limit

\mathrm{Sp}\coloneqq\lim(\dots\xrightarrow{\Omega}\mathscr{S}_{*}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\mathscr{S}_{*})

taken in the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories (
[
Lur17
, Section 1.4.2, Definition 1.4.3.1]
).
This may be safely ignored by readers who are only familiar with spectra in the classical context, and mostly serves as a framework of consistent notation. This subsection provides a summary of standard definitions and results, presented in the language and framework established in
[
Lur17
]
.

Shifting spectra.

The adjoint functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma\colon\mathrm{Sp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sp}\mathrel{\kern-2.5pt\reflectbox{$\colon$}\kern-2.5pt}\Omega}}

are mutual inverses, and are equivalent to shifting the sequence of spaces given by a spectrum. For a spectrum
\mathscr{E}
, we write
\Sigma^{n}\mathscr{E}\eqqcolon\mathscr{E}[n]
and
\Omega^{n}\mathscr{E}\eqqcolon\mathscr{E}[-n]
.

Suspension spectra.

The functor
\Sigma^{\infty}\colon\mathscr{S}_{*}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}
, which on
0
-objects maps a space to its suspension spectrum, fits into an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{\infty}\colon\mathscr{S}_{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sp}\mathrel{\kern-2.5pt\reflectbox{$\colon$}\kern-2.5pt}\Omega^{\infty}}}

between the
\infty
-category of pointed spaces and spectra. For a pointed space
X
, we generally write
\Sigma^{\infty}X
for its suspension spectrum. If
X
is not pointed, we consider
X_{+}
, the space
X
together with a disjoint base point before passing to the suspension spectrum.

(Co)homology groups valued in spectra.

(1)

For a spectrum
\mathscr{E}
, and a spectrum
X
, the
\mathscr{E}
-homology groups
of
X
are defined as
\mathscr{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{n}(\mathscr{E}\otimes X)
, where the
smash product of spectra

-\otimes-\colon\mathrm{Sp}\times\mathrm{Sp}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}
is uniquely determined by the fact that it preserves small colimits in both variables, and that the unit object of
\mathrm{Sp}
is the sphere spectrum. In particular, this endows
\mathrm{Sp}
with a symmetric monoidal structure, which is unique up to contractible choice (
[
Lur17
, Corollary 4.8.2.19]
). In the case where
X
is a space, we write
\mathscr{E}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{n}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+})
, and define the
reduced

\mathscr{E}
-homology groups by
\smash{\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{n}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}X)
.

(2)

The
\mathscr{E}
-cohomology groups
of a spectrum
X
are defined as
\mathscr{E}^{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{-n}(\mathscr{F}(X,\mathscr{E}))
, where the spectrum
\mathscr{F}(\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+},\mathscr{E})
is the
mapping spectrum
from
\Sigma^{\infty}X
to
\mathscr{E}
. Its associated infinite loop space is the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(X,\mathscr{E})
. This leads to the classical adjunction
\mathscr{F}(A,\mathscr{F}(B,C))\simeq\mathscr{F}(A\otimes B,C)
for spectra
A
,
B
, and
C
, since the smash product functor is left adjoint to the internal
\mathrm{Hom}
functor. In the case where
X
is a space, we write
\mathscr{E}^{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{-n}(\mathscr{F}(\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+},\mathscr{E}))
, and the
reduced

\mathscr{E}
-cohomology groups are defined as
\smash{\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}}^{n}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{-n}(\mathscr{F}(\Sigma^{\infty}X,\mathscr{E}))
.

The case of
\mathscr{E}
-(co)homology of the sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbb{S}^{0}
is particularly simple—we obtain the homotopy groups of
\mathscr{E}
. We abbreviate
\mathscr{E}_{n}(\mathbb{S})\simeq\mathscr{E}_{n}(*)\eqqcolon\mathscr{E}_{n}
and
\mathscr{E}^{n}(\mathbb{S})\simeq\mathscr{E}^{n}(*)\eqqcolon\mathscr{E}^{n}
. In particular, there is an isomorphism of abelian groups between
\mathscr{E}^{n}
and
\mathscr{E}_{-n}
.

Homotopy classes of maps.

The homotopy classes of maps between two spectra
A
and
B
are defined as
[A,B]\coloneqq\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(A,B))
, where the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(A,B)
of spectra is an object in
\mathscr{S}
. This is equivalent to the group
\pi_{0}(\mathscr{F}(A,B))
via the chain of equivalences

\pi_{0}(\mathscr{F}(A,B))\simeq\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(\mathbb{S},\mathscr{F}(A,B)))\simeq\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(\mathbb{S}\otimes A,B))\simeq\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(A,B)).

Poincaré duality complexes

A
d
-dimensional Poincaré duality complex is a connected space
X
which has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex, such that there exists a pair
(\mathscr{L},[X])
where
\mathscr{L}
is the orientation local system, and
[X]
is a class in
\mathrm{H}_{n}(X;\mathscr{L})
such that the map

\mathrm{H}^{k}(X;\mathscr{M})\xlongrightarrow{-\cap[X]}\mathrm{H}_{d-k}(X;\mathscr{M}\otimes\mathscr{L})

is an isomorphism for any local system
\mathscr{M}
on
X
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We call the class
[X]
the
fundamental class of
X
.
Poincaré duality spaces have been thoroughly studied and play a prominent rôle in surgery theory. We refer the reader to
[
Ran02
, Section 9]
for basic properties of (geometric) Poincaré duality complexes, and
[
Lan22
, Appendix A]
for a view on Poincaré duality complexes via parametrised homotopy theory.

Spivak normal fibration.

Consider a finite CW complex
X
of dimension
d
with an embedding
i\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
into high-dimensional Euclidean space. A thickening
N(X)
of the image is homotopy equivalent to
X
, and thus we may view the boundary inclusion
\partial N(X)\hookrightarrow N(X)
as a map
\partial N(X)\rightarrow X
. Spivak showed in
[
Spi67
]
that the fibre of this map has the homotopy type of a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{\ell-d-1}
if and only if
X
is a Poincaré duality complex. In this case, stabilisation via embeddings
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ell+2}\hookrightarrow\dots
yields a stable spherical fibration
\mathrm{SF}(X)\colon X\rightarrow\mathrm{BG}
called the
Spivak normal fibration
of
X
. For Poincaré duality complexes, the Spivak normal fibration plays the role of the stable normal bundle for manifolds.

Thom spaces.

For a spherical fibration
\zeta\colon X\rightarrow\mathrm{BG}(r)
of rank
r
, there is a well-defined Thom space via
\mathrm{Th}(\zeta)\coloneqq\mathrm{cofib}(S(\zeta)\rightarrow X)
, where
S(\zeta)
is the total space of the spherical fibration. In particular, if
J\colon\mathrm{BO}(r)\rightarrow\mathrm{BG}(r)
is the
J
-homomorphism, and
\xi\colon X\rightarrow\mathrm{BO}(r)
is a vector bundle of rank
r
, there is a homotopy equivalence
\mathrm{Th}(\xi)\simeq\mathrm{Th}(J\circ\xi)
(see for example
[
Ran02
, Proposition 9.24]
). Hence, the homotopy type of the Thom space of a vector bundle only depends on the underlying spherical fibration.

3.
Spanier–Whitehead duality for spectra

This section serves as a recollection of well-known facts about Spanier–Whitehead duality that we require in the proof of
A
. Furthermore, we provide the necessary setup of the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence for said proof, which we tend to in
Section

4
.

Spanier–Whitehead duality and (co)fibrations

A spectrum
X
is dualisable if there exists a spectrum
\mathbb{D}(X)
such that
\mathbb{D}(X)\otimes-
is right adjoint to
X\otimes-
. This occurs if and only if
X
is a finite spectrum, in which case its
Spanier–Whitehead dual
is given by
\mathbb{D}(X)\simeq\mathscr{F}(X,\mathbb{S})
. We may take this as the definition of the Spanier–Whitehead dual of a finite spectrum. Restricting to the full
\infty
-subcategory
\smash{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}}
of finite spectra, we obtain a duality functor
\mathbb{D}\colon\smash{(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\smash{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}}
.
It is well-known that, since the
\infty
-category of spectra is stable, this functor preserves the zero object as well as fibre sequences as shown in
3.1
—in other words, the Spanier–Whitehead duality functor
\mathbb{D}\colon\smash{(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\smash{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}}
is exact.

The interaction between a finite spectrum
X
and its Spanier–Whitehead dual
\mathbb{D}(X)
is particularly simple through the lens of (co)homology, which is the driving factor behind
A
. For an arbitrary finite spectrum
\mathscr{E}
, adjunction yields the equivalence

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{-k}(\mathbb{D}(X))

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{S}[-k],\mathbb{D}(X)\otimes\mathscr{E}]

\displaystyle\simeq[\mathbb{S}[-k]\otimes X,\mathscr{E}]

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{E}^{k}(X)

and we denote the isomorphism by
\mathscr{S}\mathscr{W}\colon\mathscr{E}^{k}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}_{-k}(\mathbb{D}(X))
. This natural isomorphism is called the
Spanier–Whitehead duality isomorphism
, evidently induced by the functor
\mathbb{D}\colon\smash{(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\smash{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}}
and its defining property. Exactness of this functor is crucial since the exact couple defining the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence is built from cofibrations. Therefore, we include an elaborate discussion below. Note that both
\mathrm{Sp}
and
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}
are stable
\infty
-categories, so we may freely interchange the words
fibration
and
cofibration
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a map of finite spectra, together with an associated (co)fibre sequence

X\xlongrightarrow{f}Y\longrightarrow\mathrm{cofib}(f).

The Spanier–Whitehead dual of
\mathrm{cofib}(f)
satisfies the natural equivalence
\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}f)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{D}(-)\coloneqq\mathscr{F}(-,\mathbb{S})
, the composition
\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(Y)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(X)
comes with a null-homotopy induced by the data of the fibre sequence
X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow\mathrm{cofib}(f)
. The induced maps are obtained by pre-composition. By the universal property of limits, we therefore obtain a map
\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))\rightarrow\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}f)
. The data is collected in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}f)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}f}

where the lower sequence is the fibre sequence associated to the map
\mathbb{D}f
. Let
T
be an arbitrary test spectrum. The vertical equivalences in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T\otimes Y[1],\mathbb{S}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T\otimes X[1],\mathbb{S}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T\otimes\mathrm{cofib}(f),\mathbb{S}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T\otimes Y,\mathbb{S}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T\otimes X,\mathbb{S}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

are induced by adjunction. In particular, the left-most equivalence, which switches the sign of the shift, stems from the fact that
\mathscr{F}(-,\mathbb{S})
takes pushout diagrams to pullback diagrams. From the previous discussion, we obtain a comparison of long exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)[-1]]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}f)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(Y)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[T,\mathbb{D}(X)]}}

and the central map is an isomorphism by the Five Lemma. The (
\infty
-categorical) Yoneda Lemma yields the desired equivalence
\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}f)
. Since it is induced by adjunction, this equivalence is natural.
∎

CW filtrations on Spanier–Whitehead duals

By definition, a finite spectrum
X
admits a CW filtration
0=X^{(-\infty)}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow X^{(m)}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X^{(n)}\simeq X
of finite spectra for specific integral bounds
m
and
n
. The given maps
i\colon X^{(p-1)}\rightarrow X^{(p)}
are characterised by the fact that the cofibre admits an equivalence
\mathrm{cofib}(i)\simeq\smash{\bigoplus_{I_{p}}\mathbb{S}[p]}
to a sum of shifted sphere spectra. Note that
X
is necessarily
(m-1)
-connective. Suppressing the bounds, we write
\smash{X^{(*)}}
for a chosen CW filtration on a finite spectrum
X
. The Spanier–Whitehead duality functor
\mathbb{D}\colon\smash{(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\smash{\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fin}}}
does not preserve a chosen CW filtration, but we obtain a CW filtration on
\mathbb{D}(X)
as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a finite spectrum together with a chosen CW filtration
X^{(*)}
. The
dual CW filtration
is a CW filtration on the Spanier–Whitehead dual
\mathbb{D}(X)
by setting
\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}\coloneqq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)}))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a finite spectrum together with a chosen CW filtration
X^{(*)}
with integral bounds
m
and
n
as above. The induced dual CW filtration as defined in
3.2
is a well-defined finite CW filtration on
\mathbb{D}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a finite spectrum with a chosen CW filtration, and consider the fibre sequence

X^{(p-1)}\xlongrightarrow{i}X^{(p)}\xlongrightarrow{j}\mathrm{cofib}(i)\eqqcolon X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)}.

By
3.1
, the Spanier–Whitehead dual of
X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)}
admits a natural equivalence to the fibre
\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}i)\simeq\mathrm{cofib}(\mathbb{D}i)[-1]\eqqcolon\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})/\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})[-1]
. Since the inclusion
i\colon X^{(p-1)}\rightarrow X^{(p)}
comes from the CW filtration on
X
, we have an equivalence
X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)}\simeq\bigoplus_{I_{p}}\mathbb{S}[p]
, and thus

\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})/\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})[-1]\simeq\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)})\simeq\mathbb{D}(\sideset{}{{}_{I_{p}}}{\bigoplus}\mathbb{S}[p])\simeq\mathscr{F}(\sideset{}{{}_{I_{p}}}{\bigoplus}\mathbb{S}[p],\mathbb{S})\simeq\sideset{}{{}_{I_{p}}}{\bigoplus}\mathbb{S}[-p].

By definition of the dual CW filtration, we have a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p-1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}/\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p-1)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})/\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}(k[-1])}

upon taking cofibres vertically to obtain the bottom fibre sequence. By the bottom fibre sequence and the calculation above, we obtain a string of equivalences

\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}/\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p-1)}\simeq\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})/\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})[-1]\simeq\sideset{}{{}_{I_{p}}}{\bigoplus}\mathbb{S}[-p].

Thus, the cofibre of each map
\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p-1)}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}
is equivalent to a sum of shifted spectra, and the dual CW filtration is indeed a well-defined CW filtration. Given the integral bounds
m
and
n
of the CW filtration of
X
, it is finite since it is clearly trivial for
p=n+1
, and for
p=m
, we obtain the whole of
\mathbb{D}(X)
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the filtration term
\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}
may be equivalently expressed as follows. By
3.1
, we obtain an equivalence

\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}\coloneqq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)}))\simeq\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{cofib}(X^{(p-1)}\rightarrow X))=\mathbb{D}(X/X^{(p-1)}).

A similar argument yields an equivalence
\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}/\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p-1)}\simeq\mathbb{D}(X^{(p-1)})/\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)})
. There is slight abuse of notation—we are writing cofibres as quotients. The above identifications will be crucial in the proof of
A
, when defining a map between exact couples. Thus we freely interchange notationally, referring to the natural equivalence from
3.1
.

A convenient setup for the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

For spectra
\mathscr{E}
and
X
, a major tool for computing the
\mathscr{E}
-(co)homology groups of
X
from the data of ordinary (co)homology is the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence. The cohomological version of the spectral sequence is given by
E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^{p}(X;\mathscr{E}^{q})\implies\mathscr{E}^{p+q}(X)
, and the homological version is
E^{2}_{p,q}=\mathrm{H}_{p}(X;\mathscr{E}_{q})\implies\mathscr{E}_{p+q}(X)
.
There are two equivalent ways to set up the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence. The first uses a CW filtration, the second is obtained by a Postnikov filtration as introduced in
[
Mau63
]
. While the second one has the advantage of relating the cohomological differentials directly to
k
-invariants, it is ill-behaved in the context of Spanier–Whitehead duality—namely, for a finite spectrum
X
, its Postnikov truncation
\tau_{\leq n}(X)
need not be finite, and therefore is not necessarily dualisable. On the other hand, a finite spectrum
X
can be endowed with a CW filtration
0=X^{(-\infty)}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow X^{(m)}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X^{(n)}\simeq X
of finite spectra, and we have seen in
3.3
how to relate it to a CW filtration on the dual
\mathbb{D}(X)
.

We give a brief recollection of the definition of the exact couples defining the two spectral sequences. Let us fix a CW filtration
X^{(*)}
on the finite spectrum
X
. Thus, we have extended fibration sequences (distinguished triangles) of the form

X^{(p-1)}\xlongrightarrow{i}X^{(p)}\xlongrightarrow{j}X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)}\xlongrightarrow{k}X^{(p-1)}[1]

which define the associated (unravelled) exact couple as follows. Fixing the starting degree
p+q-1
, we obtain the exact sequence

\underbrace{\mathscr{E}^{p+q-1}(X^{(p)})}_{\mathscr{A}_{c}^{p+q-1}}\xlongrightarrow{i^{*}}\underbrace{\mathscr{E}^{p+q-1}(X^{(p-1)})}_{\mathscr{A}_{c}^{p+q-1}}\xlongrightarrow{k[-1]^{*}}\underbrace{\mathscr{E}^{p+q}(X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)})}_{\mathscr{D}_{c}^{p+q}}\xlongrightarrow{j[-1]^{*}}\underbrace{\mathscr{E}^{p+q}(X^{(p)})}_{\mathscr{A}_{c}^{p+q}}

suppressing the suspension isomorphism
\mathscr{E}^{p+q-1}((X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)})[-1])\simeq\mathscr{E}^{p+q}((X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)}))
, and similarly
\mathscr{E}^{p+q-1}(X^{(p)})[-1])\simeq\mathscr{E}^{p+q}(X^{(p)})
. As a convention, we suppress suspension isomorphisms and refer to naturality of those when they appear hidden in diagrams we need to show commute. Note that the map
k[-1]^{*}\colon\mathscr{E}^{p+q-1}(X^{(p)})\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{p+q}(X^{(p)}/X^{(p-1)})
is precisely the connecting homomorphism
\delta
in the cohomological long exact sequence of the pair
(X^{(p)},X^{(p-1)})
.

We now turn to the homological exact couple. We set up the degrees to match the duality isomorphism. By
3.3
, the CW filtration on
X
induces a dual CW filtration on its Spanier–Whitehead dual
\mathbb{D}(X)
, with terms
\mathbb{D}(X)^{(-p)}\coloneqq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{D}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}(X^{(p)}))

Orientations on manifolds

Stable Thom–Dold isomorphisms.

We briefly discussed the construction of the Spivak normal fibration in

2
. Let us consider an embedding

X

Definition 5.4
.

Let

\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{SF}_{\ell}(X))\rightarrow\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{SF}_{\ell+1}(X))\rightarrow\dots

\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{SF}_{\ell+1}(X))\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{SF}_{\ell}(X))[1]

is the same as

of the prespectrum given by the sequence of spaces

Poincaré duality and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

This subsection contains the proof of
B
. The strategy is to factor Poincaré duality as the Thom–Dold isomorphism, followed by Spanier–Whitehead duality.

Proof of
B
.

The setup from
Section

5
allows us to express Poincaré duality as follows. Let
X
be a Poincaré duality complex of dimension
d
, oriented over a ring spectrum
\mathscr{E}
. By
[
Lan22
, Remark A.8]
, the Spanier–Whitehead dual of
X
is given by the Thom spectrum of its Spivak normal fibration—precisely the spectrum
\mathrm{MSF}(X)
as in
5.4
—thus, there is an equivalence
\mathbb{D}(\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+})\simeq\mathrm{MSF}(X)
, and the Poincaré duality isomorphism may be factored as

\mathrm{PD}\colon\mathscr{E}^{n}(X)\xlongrightarrow{\text{Thom--Dold}}\mathscr{E}^{n-d}(\mathrm{MSF}(X))\xlongrightarrow{\mathscr{S}\mathscr{W}}\mathscr{E}_{d-n}(X).

A
implies that the right-hand map induces an isomorphism between the cohomological and the homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences. It remains to show the same for the Thom–Dold isomorphism, which is given by the cup product pairing
-\cup u(\mathrm{SF}(X))\colon\mathscr{E}^{n}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{n-d}(\mathrm{MSF}(X))
.
Since
\mathscr{E}
is a ring spectrum, and
X
a finite spectrum, the cohomological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence is known to be multiplicative (see for example
[
Koc96
, Proposition 4.2.9]
). Hence, a naturality argument shows that the Thom–Dold isomorphism induces an isomorphism between the cohomological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence computing
\mathscr{E}^{*}(X)
and the cohomological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence computing
\mathscr{E}^{*-d}(\mathrm{MSF}(X))
.
∎

Relationship among physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yongsen Liu, Mufan Zhang, Kun Wang, Jiong Luo, Chen Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the increasing pressure of a global aging population, ensuring and improving the well-being of the elderly is directly related to social harmony and stability. In the field of exercise psychology, the relationship between physical activity and subjective well-being has attracted much attention, but research on the elderly group has been rarely reported. Therefore, the presented paper aims to investigate the effect of physical activity on the subjective well-being of the elderly and reveal the role of self-efficacy between physical activity and subjective well-being.MethodsBy means of Physical Activity Rating Scale (PARS-3), General Self-efficacy Scale (GSES), and Subjective Well-being Scale (SWS), a questionnaire survey was conducted among 780 elderly people in Chongqing, China. And the data were processed and analyzed by SPSS 21.0 and AMOS 21.0 statistical software.Results(1) Compared with women, elderly men have higher levels of physical exercise (t = 3.18, p < 0.01), self-efficacy (t = 2.06, p < 0.05), and subjective well-being (t = 2.37, p < 0.05). Moreover, there are significant differences in physical exercise volume between urban and rural areas and BMI among the elderly, manifested as higher exercise volume among the elderly in urban areas (t = 2.28, p < 0.05) and those with normal BMI (F = 4.21, p < 0.05); (2) The amount of physical activity was positively correlated with self-efficacy (r = 0.41, p < 0.001) and subjective well-being (r = 0.29, p < 0.001), and self-efficacy was positively correlated with subjective well-being (r = 0.26, p < 0.001); (3) In the male model, physical exercise can directly and positively predict subjective well-being (ES = 0.20), and self-efficacy had a partial mediating effect between physical exercise and subjective well-being (ES = 0.071); In the female model, physical exercise can also directly and positively predict subjective well-being (ES = 0.26), and self-efficacy played a partial mediating role in physical exercise and subjective well-being (ES = 0.066).ConclusionWhether male or female, the elderly should actively participate in physical exercise, which is significantly associated with the improvement of self-efficacy and subjective well-being levels.

Introduction

In recent years, population aging has become one of the most pressing global challenges. As the most populous country in the world, China is experiencing this demographic shift at an unprecedented scale. By the end of 2017, approximately 240 million Chinese citizens were aged 60 or above, representing 17.3% of the total population; among them, 158.31 million were over the age of 65 (11.4%) [1]. Projections indicate that this trend will intensify, with the elderly population expected to reach 330 million by 2025 and surpass 490 million by 2050 [2]. This demographic transition, alongside environmental and resource concerns, is likely to reshape China’s socioeconomic structure, particularly the labor market [3]. The emotional well-being, social participation, and physical health are the basic components of healthy aging. Addressing the physical and mental well-being of the elderly is therefore a critical component of any strategy aimed at mitigating the challenges of an aging society.

In light of these demographic changes, promoting subjective well-being among the elderly has gained increasing academic and policy attention. As a comprehensive index reflecting one’s quality of life and mental health, subjective well-being mainly includes life satisfaction, positive emotion and negative emotion [4, 5], and it is a complicated and multi-level mental status formed by the interaction of psychological factors such as need, cognition, emotion and external incentives [5]. Currently, with the transformation from a survival-based society to a development-oriented society, people exhibit higher expectations for the pursuit of a happy life, and gradually realize that improving the feeling of well-being is of great importance to promote the healthy development of their own. Subjective well-being reflects the psychological and life status of elderly, and enhancing it can help them live actively and adapt to society, thereby reducing the burden on families and society [6]. Furthermore, the level of subjective well-being among older adults can serve as an important indicator for assessing the development of an aging society [7]. By participating in recreational activities as a source of social support (engaging in entertainment activities such as visiting friends, playing or running errands), not only can the support network and emotional health be strengthened, but it also becomes a key factor in life satisfaction [8].

As people age, it is important to take measures to maintain or improve the quality of life in order to age positively and healthily. Among the non-pharmaceutical strategies for improving the quality of life of the elderly, physical activity is the most recommended [9]. In the field of sports, the term “well-being” was frequently mentioned in the documents of sports policy documents, and sports are regarded as an important foundation for achieving people’s happiness [10]. In the meantime, with the development of exercise psychology, more and more studies have confirmed that subjective well-being would be positively affected by body activity or physical exercise [11, 12], especially for the old people. It was also found that the aged who regularly participated in physical activities were more likely to enjoy the normal aging process than those who seldom or intermittently participated in physical activities [13]. Regular participation in sports activities, as a necessary condition for the elderly to lead a happy life [14, 15], was positively correlated with life satisfaction and health of the elderly [16]. Park [17] reported that physical activity could predict the level of perceived life satisfaction, which involved the frequency, intensity and duration of physical activity. However, some studies believed that only the duration of sports activities had a positive impact on the life satisfaction of the elderly [18]. In general, physical activity is helpful to promote physical and mental health, self-confidence, and human communication, which would be conducive to improving the quality of life and eventually achieving a higher level of well-being [3, 19, 20]. On a psychological level, physical exercise can enhance subjective well-being by providing participants with pleasurable and enjoyable experiences, as well as by improving self-esteem, life satisfaction, and health benefits [10, 21]. On a physiological level, sports participation can reduce body fat and improve physical fitness, thereby generating subjective well-being [22]. On the level of social adaptation, engaging in social interaction and communication with others during exercise can also bring individuals a sense of well-being [10, 20, 23]. Nevertheless, physical activity and subjective well-being are indices with the characteristics of being complicated and comprehensive, are there any variations with other factors or variables during the interaction between the two indexes?

The “broaden-and-build” theory posits that positive emotions broaden the scope of attention and cognition, thereby helping to build lasting resources that promote well-being [24, 25]. These resources, whether psychological, social or emotional, will contribute to enhancing an individual’s resilience and overall well-being over time [26], among which self-efficacy has a positive effect between these resources and an individual’s well-being and mental health [27]. In the field of social psychology, Bandura [28] reported that self-efficacy could affect the physical and mental regulation system through the belief in self-processing stress ability, and thereby influence their physical and mental health. Moreover, it can predict and explain one’s behavior and the causes of motivation in certain special situations, and also act as a psychological power of self-regulation. Furthermore, it was reported that self-efficacy had a prominent positive predictive effect on subjective well-being, and there was a linear relationship between self-efficacy and individual mental health [29, 30]. That is, self-efficacy could significantly positively predict the subjective well-being of the elderly, and the elderly with high self-efficacy were always at a higher level of mental health [31], such as stronger social skills, greater psychological resilience, and lower levels of loneliness [32, 33]. Moreover, self-efficacy was able to help elderly patients with chronic diseases increase confidence in life and maintain a certain level of subjective well-being [34]. In addition, positive physical exercise was also considered to be correlated with self-efficacy [35]. In the previous studies, the positive benefits of physical exercise on adolescents’ self-efficacy have been extensively studied. For example, there was a significant correlation between physical exercise and college students’ self-efficacy, the higher the degree of physical exercise was, the higher the sports self-efficacy would be [36]. Zhao [37] demonstrated that the amount of physical activity would significantly enhance academic emotions and self-efficacy of high school students, and there were significant differences in self-efficacy among different intensities of activities. Thus, it can be deduced that self-efficacy may play a vital role between physical exercise and subjective well-being of the aged. Nevertheless, the corresponding influencing pathways and mechanisms remain to be further investigated.

Although some studies have revealed the positive benefits of physical activity on the subjective well-being of different groups, few studies have explored the mediating role of self-efficacy in the relationship between physical activity and subjective well-being specifically among the elderly population in China. Moreover, the interaction between different factors has not been fully demonstrated yet for the reason that subjective well-being is a complicated psychological index involving many influencing factors. In the presented paper, the path structure model was established to investigate the main influencing factors of subjective well-being, and the mediating role of self-efficacy between physical activity and the subjective well-being of the elderly. Based on the above-mentioned, hypotheses were put forward as follows: H1) physical activity has a direct impact on the subjective well-being of the elderly; H2) physical activity has a direct impact on the self-efficacy of the elderly; H3) self-efficacy plays a mediating role between physical activity and subjective well-being.

Participants and methods

Participants

This study employed a cross-sectional survey design. First, a random number sampling method was used to select 5 out of the 9 central urban districts in Chongqing, China. Subsequently, stratified sampling was adopted to conduct site-based questionnaire surveys among older adults (aged 60 to 80 years) in the selected five districts (to facilitate questionnaire distribution and completion, the survey was primarily conducted in residential communities and public squares), with efforts made to maintain a relatively consistent sample size across each district. During the survey, sample recruitment was randomized without prior grouping. Individuals who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria and voluntarily completed the questionnaire were considered valid participants. Inclusion criteria: (1) aged between 60 and 80 years; (2) voluntary participation in the survey. Exclusion criteria: (1) history of treatment for mental or psychological disorders; (2) physical disability or limited mobility.

To ensure the quality and reliability of the questionnaire responses, after explaining the instructions and requirements for completing the questionnaire to the older adults in detail, participants filled out the questionnaire based on their actual situation, and the questionnaires were collected on the spot. The time taken by participants to complete the questionnaire should not be less than 12 min. It should be noted that if the subjects are unable to complete the questionnaire independently (such as being illiterate or visually impaired, but having a clear mind), the investigator can orally describe the content of the questionnaire for them based on the actual situation and fill it out on their behalf. In this study, a total of 780 questionnaires were distributed, and 62 invalid questionnaires including unknown key information and incomplete filling were excluded. Finally, 718 valid questionnaires were obtained with an effective rate of 92.05%. The average age of the respondents was 69.91 ± 3.64 years. All respondents are required to sign informed consent before they fill in the questionnaires.

Methods

Questionnaire design and evaluation on reliability and validity

To reveal the relationship among physical activity, self-efficacy, and subjective well-being of the elderly, the Physical Activity Rating Scale (PARS-3), General Self-efficacy Scale (GSEs), and Subjective Well-being Scale (SWS) were selected as assessment tools in this study.

Physical Activity Rating Scale (PARS-3)

PARS-3 revised by Liang [38] was adopted, that is, intensity of physical activity (what do you think of the intensity of participating in physical activity each time), activity time (how many minutes do you participate in physical activity each time), and activity frequency (how many physical activities do you take in one week). This scale has a good application effect among the elderly population in China [39]. A Likert 5-point formula was employed for quantification, and the scores were signed as 1 to 5. To better assess the level of exercise volume, this scale adopts the principle that the total score does not exceed 100 points. Physical activity score = activity intensity score× (activity time score-1)× activity frequency score, the score range is 0-100 points [40, 41]. The evaluation standards of activity amount are as follows: low exercise amount ≤ 19 points, medium exercise amount ≤ 20–42 points, large exercise amount ≥ 43 points. The test-retest reliability of the scale is high with the correlation coefficient (r) being 0.82. After examination, the Bartlett sphericity test was χ²(3) = 1310 (p < 0.001), and the KMO = 0.89, indicating that this scale is suitable for exploratory factor analysis. After conducting factor analysis on this scale using the direct oblique rotation method, a total of one common factor was extracted, and the progressive contribution rate was 57.17%. In this study, the factor load was greater than 0.50, AVE was greater than 0.50, and the combined reliability CR was greater than 0.70, indicating that this scale has good converging validity.

General Self-Efficacy Scale (GSEs)

GSEs revised by Wang [42] was adopted, which has a good application effect among the elderly population in China [43]. It contains 10 questions (if I try my best, I can always solve the problem). The Likert 4-point scale was used for quantification, and the scores were signed as 1–4 respectively according to the option “disagree ~ quite agree”. The higher the score, the stronger the self-efficacy. After examination, the Bartlett sphericity test was χ²(45) = 2277 (p < 0.001), and the KMO = 0.89, indicating that this scale is suitable for exploratory factor analysis. After conducting factor analysis on this scale using the direct oblique rotation method, a total of one common factor was extracted, and the progressive contribution rate was 53.73%. In this study, the factor load was greater than 0.60, AVE was greater than 0.60, and the combined reliability CR was greater than 0.80, indicating that this scale has good convergent validity. Subjective Well-being Scale (SWS)

SWS consist of the life satisfaction and emotion scale. The Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) compiled by Suh [44] was used to measure the cognitive components of subjective well-being, which was confirmed to be applicable to the study of the elderly in China [45]. The scale includes five items (for example, I am very satisfied with my life). 7 points were adopted to score, and 1–7 points corresponding to the option “completely disagree ~ completely agree”. The higher the score, the higher the life satisfaction. After examination, the Bartlett sphericity test was χ²(10) = 1998 (p < 0.001), and the KMO = 0.86, indicating that this scale is suitable for exploratory factor analysis. After conducting factor analysis on this scale using the direct oblique rotation method, a total of one common factor was extracted, and the progressive contribution rate was 51.12%. In this study, the factor load was greater than 0.60, AVE was greater than 0.50, and the combined reliability CR was greater than 0.70, indicating that this scale has good converging validity. Verification results of measurement model were as follows: x2/df = 1.97, RMSEA = 0.04, AGFI = 0.95, TLI = 0.98, CFI = 0.97, IFI = 0.98, and GFI = 0.96.

The Positive and Negative Affect Scale (PANAS) revised by Qiu [46] was adopted to measure the emotional components of subjective well-being. After pilot tests, it was confirmed to have good adaptability in the Chinese population [46]. The scale contains 18 items, and adopts a Likert 5-point scale for quantification. The scores were signed as 1–5 according to the option “none” to “very strong”, in which the negative emotion items were scored in reverse. After the factor analysis, two common factors were extracted, namely, positive emotion (a total of 9 items, such as “excited”. The higher the score, the more positive the emotion) and negative emotion (a total of 9 items, such as “angry”. The higher the score, the lower the negative emotion, which means the less negative). After examination, the Bartlett sphericity test was χ²(153) = 3556 (p < 0.001), and the KMO = 0.87, indicating that this scale is suitable for exploratory factor analysis. After conducting factor analysis on this scale using the direct oblique rotation method, a total of two common factors were extracted, and the progressive contribution rate was 51.71%. In this study, the loads of each factor were all greater than 0.60, AVE was greater than 0.60, and the combined reliability CR was greater than 0.80, indicating that this scale has good convergent validity. The results demonstrated that the scale exhibited good reliability and validity. Based on previous studies [47], the total score of subjective well-being was the sum of Z scores after reverse scoring of life satisfaction, positive emotion and negative emotion, that is, SWB=(S-SM)/SD+(P-PM)/SD+(N-NM)/SD, where S and SM are the original score and mean of SWLS respectively, P and PM are the original score and mean of positive emotions respectively, N and NM are the original score and mean of negative emotions respectively, and SD is the standard deviation. Mathematical statistics

In the presented work, the SPSS 21.0 mathematical statistics software was employed to process and analyze the data. First, conduct demographic data analysis on the samples and delete those with missing values or outliers. Secondly, the scale’s reliability and validity were examined by first conducting an exploratory factor analysis (EFA), followed by a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), and Pearson correlation analysis was employed to examine the relationships among the variables. Finally, as the sample size of this study was moderate and all variables were continuous, the Amos 21.0 software was used to establish a structural equation model to explore the simple mediating effect (see Fig. 1 for details). Significance levels of all investigated indicators were set to be p < 0.05.

Results

Common method biases inspection

Considering that this study is a horizontal investigation, Harman’s single-factor test method was employed to investigate the common method biases [48] in order to minimize the impact of common method biases on the results, and the single common method factor technique in CFA was used to test the bias of the results. The results illustrated that there were 9 factors with characteristic roots more than 1 in all items, and the variation explained by the first factor was 29.27%, which was less than the critical standard of 40%. Thus, the common method biases did not cause serious problems in this study.

Analysis of demographic differences

Based on the analysis of demographic differences, it can be found that (1) in terms of gender, the amount of physical activity (p < 0.01), self-efficacy (p < 0.05) and subjective well-being of men were significantly higher than those of women (p < 0.05); (2) in terms of household registration status, the physical activity of the elderly in urban household registration was significantly higher than that in the rural household registration (p < 0.05); (3) in terms of BMI, the amount of physical activity of the elderly with “Light BMI” was significantly higher than those with “Heavy BMI” (p < 0.05) (as shown in Table 2).

Mediating effect test of self-efficacy

To investigate the relationship among physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly and reveal the mediating role of self-efficacy, Amos was used to establish a structural equation model of physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being based on the mediating effect test process proposed by Wen [49] (Fig. 1). The results indicated that physical activity could positively predict subjective well-being (β = 0.30, p < 0.001). When self-efficacy was added as a mediation variable, the model had good fitting (the overall model in Table 4). Among them, the amount of physical activity had the ability to positively predict self-efficacy (β = 0.40, p < 0.001), self-efficacy could positively predict subjective well-being (β = 0.18, p < 0.001), while the path coefficient of physical activity on subjective well-being decreased from 0.30 to 0.23 (β = 0.23, p < 0.001), indicating that the indirect effect of physical activity on subjective well-being through self-efficacy is significant (β = 0.07, 95%CI [0.05,0.10]), and indicating partial mediation (Table 5).

Discussion

Difference analysis of physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly

As can be seen from the presented study, there were significant demographic differences in the amount of physical activity of the elderly. From the perspective of gender, the amount of physical activity of men was significantly higher than that of women. A study shows that men are more inclined to engage in strength and competitive physical activities, while women tend to prefer gentle, confrontational and less intense sports. Therefore, men usually have a higher amount of physical activity [50]. For the elderly, we speculated that this may be attributed to the allocation of leisure time and exercise willingness. This is because young parents are often busy with work and have limited time to care for their children. As a result, after retirement, older adults often take on the responsibility of caring for their grandchildren. For women, raising grandchildren is often seen as a continuation of their parenting role in later life, and they are more significantly affected by intergenerational caregiving [51], and this leaves them with little time or interest to engage in physical exercise. In terms of BMI, individuals with lower BMI have a significantly higher amount of physical activity than those with higher BMI. Maintaining regular physical activity helps control an individual’s BMI, and older adults who exercise regularly tend to have a lower BMI [52], indicating that physical activity is associated with BMI levels in the elderly. From the perspective of registered residence, the amount of physical activity of the elderly in urban areas was significantly higher than that of the rural elderly. This difference may result from that the economic and living standards are higher for the elderly in the urban areas, and the sports facilities in towns are better installed as compared to those in the rural areas, which makes the elderly in the urban areas participate in physical exercise more conveniently.

Furthermore, men’s self-efficacy was significantly higher than that of women among the elderly population. This is consistent with previous studies, that is, the self-efficacy of male elderly was higher than that of female elderly [53]. Wang [54] reported that there were significant gender differences in the general self-efficacy of the rural elderly, and the general self-efficacy of women was lower. In addition, this study found that men’s subjective well-being was significantly higher than that of women. The research indicated that the subjective well-being of the elderly could be influenced by two major factors, including subjective factors and objective factors [5], and gender was one of the main influencing factors. In the elderly group, women’s subjective well-being has been proved to be significantly lower than that of men [55]. This may be caused by the influence of Chinese traditional culture, the retired men can be free from work and family, but the retired women still need to take care of the family [56]. Therefore, elderly men have a higher experience of happiness. However, this study did not find significant urban-rural and BMI differences in self-efficacy and subjective well-being among the elderly, which is somewhat different from previous studies [57]. It is speculated that this may be related to factors such as sample characteristics and regional differences.

Direct impact of physical activity on the subjective well-being of the elderly

The findings also indicated that physical activity could significantly and directly predict the subjective well-being of the elderly. The higher the amount of physical exercise, the stronger the subjective well-being of men or women. Subjective well-being is an individual’s subjective perception of the quality of life, involving aspects such as mental and physical health [58, 59]. Studies have demonstrated that regular physical exercise cannot only promote physical health, but also has a prominent positive impact on people’s mental health, improving life satisfaction and quality of life [60, 61]. Physical activity can induce the generation of positive emotions, improve life satisfaction, and produce positive benefits for participants’ well-being [62, 63]. It should be pointed out that not any physical activity can improve the level of individual well-being, a certain amount or intensity of activity should be reached. For instance, Li [64] found that the amount of physical activity would significantly affect subjective well-being and psychological well-being. The greater the amount of physical exercise, the stronger the subjective well-being, and the ability to control good relationships and environment could also be greatly improved. Liu [10] believed that participating in sports activities with others or adhering to appropriate sports for a long period could promote the positive experience of happiness and thereby significantly improve national happiness. A study indicated that regular participation in sports activities was an effective way to facilitate the subjective well-being of the elderly [65]. Furthermore, this study found that compared with men, the direct path coefficient between physical exercise and subjective well-being in women is higher, indicating that physical exercise has a stronger correlation with the well-being level of elderly women. Specifically, individuals can not only enjoy a sense of pleasure and smoothness during sports participation, relieve mental stress and negative emotional distress, but also increase interaction, communication, and cooperation with others during the participation process, thereby bringing positive happiness experiences to themselves [10, 21], while women may be more likely to meet their psychological and social interaction needs during exercise. However, whether this difference is related to the movement threshold or the type of movement still requires more empirical research to support it.

The mediating effect of self-efficacy between physical activity and subjective well-being

This study confirmed the mediating pathway relationship of “physical activity → self-efficacy → subjective well-being” among the elderly, that is, physical exercise can not only directly and positively predict subjective well-being, but also play an indirect predictive role through the mediating effect of self-efficacy. On the one hand, there is a positive relationship between physical exercise and the improvement of individual self-efficacy [12, 66, 67], among adolescents [35, 68] and the middle-aged and elderly population [69] have both been reported. On the other hand, self-efficacy could positively predict one’s subjective well-being [29, 31, 70]. People with high self-efficacy usually had more confidence in life and maintained a certain level of subjective well-being, that is, self-efficacy could significantly and positively predict the subjective well-being of the elderly [34, 71]. On the contrary, people with low self-efficacy often doubted their ability, they were more prone to relax or give up, and had relatively more negative emotional experiences when facing difficulties [72]. Interestingly, researchers have found that there was a close relationship among physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly. Positive physical activity could promote the self-efficacy of the elderly, while high self-efficacy could effectively improve individual happiness perception experience such as life satisfaction and quality of life [73, 74]. However, this study found that there was no significant gender difference in the mediating model, and the mediating effect force of the men model was 0.07, while that of the women model was 0.07. This is relatively consistent with previous study, that is, for both men and women, regular participation in physical exercise can enhance their sense of self-efficacy, and thereby indirectly increase their subjective well-being level [65]. This indicates that during physical exercise, the increase in self-efficacy is one of the important factors for enhancing happiness, and this factor is not disturbed by gender differences.

Limitations

In the presented study, a structural equation model was employed to explore the relationship among physical exercise, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly of different genders. Moreover, the mediating role of self-efficacy between physical exercise and subjective well-being was revealed, and a theoretical basis and reference were provided for the idea of “sports promote happiness”. However, the following limitations were included in the study: Firstly, this study may have biases due to self-reporting (social desirability) or limited sample representativeness. In the future, it can be combined with experimental design (for example, controlled exercise intervention) or the use of wearable technology to objectively measure physical activity to increase the accuracy of the results. Secondly, this study is a cross-sectional survey and cannot lead to a deeper causal relationship. In future research, longitudinal empirical studies can be incorporated to better reveal the causal relationships among variables. Finally, the study focuses on the relationship among three factors, namely, physical activity, self-efficacy and subjective well-being of the elderly. More mediating or regulatory variables (for example, social support, environmental perception, resilience, etc.) should be further investigated to deeply reveal the internal mechanism of physical exercise affecting the subjective well-being of the elderly.

Conclusions

There are demographic differences in physical exercise volume, self-efficacy and subjective well-being among the elderly in China. A typical manifestation is that the physical exercise volume, self-efficacy, and subjective well-being of men are significantly higher than those of women, respectively. Further research has found that, regardless of gender, physical exercise can not only directly predict the subjective well-being of the elderly, but also have an indirect predictive effect on subjective well-being through the mediating effect of self-efficacy. The results suggest that actively participating in physical exercise is an effective way to promote the mental health of the elderly population.

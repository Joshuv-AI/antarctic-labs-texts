Solution Theory of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Equations in Spectral Barron Spaces Thanks: This research is supported in part by the National Science Foundation under awards DMS-2309378 and IIS-2403276.
Ye Feng, Jianfeng Lu

Abstract
. We study the solution theory of the whole-space static (elliptic) Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation in spectral Barron spaces. We prove that under the assumption that the coefficients involved are spectral Barron functions and the discount factor is sufficiently large, there exists a sequence of uniformly bounded spectral Barron functions that converges locally uniformly to the solution. As a consequence, the solution of the HJB equation can be approximated by two-layer neural networks without curse of dimensionality.



1.
Introduction

Neural networks offer a promising way to tackle numerical solutions to high dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs) by employing a neural network ansatz to the unknown solution and determining parameters in the neural network through optimization (training). This approach has achieved remarkable empirical success over recent years, see e.g., for high dimensional Schrödinger equations
[
14
,
12
,
24
]
, high dimensional optimal control problems
[
16
,
23
,
32
,
10
,
22
]
. For numerical approaches to other high dimensional PDEs based on neural networks, we refer to the reviews
[
2
,
11
,
5
]
and the references therein.

Alongside these empirical successes, there have been considerable efforts in theoretical analysis, in particular trying to understand the impact of dimension. To overcome the curse of dimensionality, novel solution theory needs to be established for PDEs in high dimension, one of the key issues is to identify function spaces that are appropriate for both approximations using neural networks and also solution theory for high dimensional PDEs.
The Barron spaces have been often used thanks to its natural connection with two-layer neural network approximation. In the seminal work of Barron
[
1
]
, it was established that any function
f
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle C_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)||\xi|\mathrm{d}\xi<\infty

can be approximated efficiently in
L^{2}
-norm by a two-layer neural network
f_{n}
of the form

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma(w_{j}\cdot x+b_{j}),

where the number of neurons
n
is independent of the dimension
d
. Following
[
1
]
, several function spaces have been proposed to characterize appropriate function classes for neural network approximation. These include the spectral Barron spaces that generalize the Barron’s construction (
1.1
) to some weighted
L^{1}
-norm of the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
, and the Barron spaces that are defined via the integral representation of
f
based on some probability distribution on the parameter space. We refer to
[
6
,
19
]
for a comprehensive overview of the function spaces along the direction of Barron.

Previous works have established solution theory of high dimensional PDEs in Barron space. So far, most works have been restricted to linear elliptic equations, see
[
18
,
17
,
3
,
4
]
. While linear PDEs are widely used in applications, one natural question is whether we can extend our understanding of high dimensional PDEs to nonlinear cases, such as the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman type equations arising from control problems.
As far as we are aware, the only work devoted to the solution theory to nonlinear PDEs in Barron space is
[
20
]
, where nonlinear elliptic PDEs arising from certain type of calculus of variation was considered.

The main goal of this paper is to study high dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations in the whole space:

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\gamma V(x)+\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\{\mathcal{L}_{u}V(x)+l(x,u)\}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\gamma>0
is the discount factor, the infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}_{u}=(f+gu)\cdot\nabla+\Delta
with
f
and
g
being vector/matrix-valued functions of suitable sizes,
l:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is the cost function. A solution of the HJB equation (
1.2
) is a pair of functions
(u,V)
, often referred to as an optimal solution, that satisfies equation (
1.2
) with
u(x)=\mathrm{argmin}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathcal{L}_{u}V(x)+l(x,u)
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Our main result shows that given the coefficients
f,g
and
l
in spectral Barron spaces, if the cost function
l
is quadratic in
u
and the discount
\gamma
is large enough, then there exists a sequence of spectral Barron functions that converges to the optimal solution of (
1.2
) locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, the sequence of spectral Barron functions is uniformly bounded in the spectral Barron norm, and, as a result, the optimal solution can be approximated by two-layer neural networks without curse of dimensionality. Our result hence provides theoretical support to using neural network approximations for high-dimensional elliptic HJB equations.

Related works

Solution theory of PDEs in spectral Barron spaces has been studied in several recent works. In
[
18
]
, the authors establish regularity results for the Poisson equation and the Schrödinger equation on the bounded domain
[0,1]^{d}
with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition in a discrete version of spectral Barron space via Fourier cosine expansion, and later similar results are extended to Schrödinger eigenvalue problems in
[
17
]
. The work of
[
4
]
proves the regularity of the static Schrödinger equation
-\Delta u+Vu=f
in spectral Barron spaces. In
[
20
]
, the authors focus on a class of PDEs known as nonlinear elliptic variational PDEs and establishes the approximation of the solution in spectral Barron space. Recently, it is proved in
[
31
]
that the solutions of the
N
-electron Schrödinger equation with Coulomb singularity for eigenvalues below the essential spectrum lie in the spectral Barron spaces
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
for
s<1
.

Besides the spectral Barron spaces, solution theory of PDEs is also studied in the Barron space based on integral representation. For example,
[
3
]
approximates the solution of a class of whole-space linear second-order elliptic PDEs in Barron space. The work of
[
7
]
proves regularity results of the screened Poisson equation
-\Delta u+\lambda^{2}u=f
, the heat equation, and a viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation
u_{t}-\Delta u+|\nabla u|^{2}=0
in the Barron space based on integral representation. It also provides some counter-examples when singularity is presented.

Efforts have also been made in investigating the complexity of approximating PDE solutions via neural networks outside the framework of (spectral) Barron spaces. For example,
[
21
]
obtains a deep neural network complexity estimate for elliptic PDEs with a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition through a proof technique that simulates gradient descent. The study in
[
9
]
uses a direct constructive proof to show that the solution to the parabolic HJB equation can be approximated by neural networks without curse of dimensionality.

2.
Main Results

Our focus is to establish an approximation of the solution of the HJB equation (
1.2
) using spectral Barron functions. First, we introduce some notation used in the paper and recall the spectral Barron norm.

Notation
.

We use
|x|
for the Euclidean norm of a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid|y-x|<r\}
for the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at
x
with radius
r
. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, denote its Fourier transform by
\hat{f}
, given by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi)=\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}x.

One can extend the definition of Fourier transform for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
\hat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, its inverse Fourier transform is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

We define the spectral Barron space following the definitions used in
[
29
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. Similar (equivalent) definitions of spectral Barron norms were also used in
[
4
,
31
,
17
,
20
,
7
,
6
]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The spectral Barron norm of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi.

The spectral Barron space (of order
s
) is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\right\}.

The reason that spectral Barron spaces are of particular interest for high-dimensional problems is that a spectral Barron function possesses certain smoothness related to the order of the spectral Barron norm and can be efficiently approximated in
H^{k}
norm by a two-layer neural network on any bounded domain without the curse of dimensionality; see Proposition
3.1
in Section
3
below. The completeness of the spectral Barron space comes from the fact that the spectral Barron norm is a weighted
L^{1}
norm of the Fourier transform. Therefore, the spectral Barron space
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space.

Since we frequently deal with vector/matrix-valued functions throughout this paper, we introduce the following convention to ease our notation.

Notation
.

For any matrix-valued function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we say
f=(f_{ij})\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
f_{ij}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
and denote

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{ij}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In this paper, we consider the cost function to be quadratic in
u
, that is,
l(x,u)=\ell(x)+\lVert u\rVert_{R}^{2}
where
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
is the running cost and
\|u\|_{R}^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle_{R}:=u^{T}Ru
is the control cost defined by some positive definite matrix
R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
. In this case, the HJB equation (
1.2
) becomes

(2.1)

\displaystyle-\gamma V(x)+\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\{\mathcal{L}_{u}V(x)+\ell(x)+\|u\|_{R}^{2}\}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We remark that it is straightforward to extend the results to the case where the matrix
R=R(x)
depends on
x
(with the assumption that
R(x)
is uniformly positive definite and lies in spectral Barron space), while we stay with constant
R
for simplicity.

To state our main result, we make the assumption that the coefficient functions
f
,
g
,
\ell
lie in spectral Barron spaces. In particular, we assume
g
to be non-zero, otherwise the solution of equation (
2.1
) is trivial. The order of the spectral Barron norm is specified in the statement of the subsequent theorems.

Assumption A
.

Assume that the coefficients
f,g,\ell\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}>0
.

Our main theorem is stated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds for
s\geq 2
. Then there exists a positive constant
C(f,g,\ell,R)
depending on
f
,
g
,
\ell
and
R
for which if the discount
\gamma\geq C(f,g,\ell,R)
, then there exists a sequence
\{u^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
\bar{u}
in
C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a sequence
\{V^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
\bar{V}
in
C^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
(\bar{u},\bar{V})
is an optimal solution of the HJB equation (
2.1
).

Theorem
1
implies the existence of a solution to (
2.1
), which can be approximated locally uniformly by spectral Barron functions. The next theorem shows that the solution given by Theorem
1
can be approximated by two-layer neural networks on any compact domain without curse of dimensionality.

Theorem 2
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
1
, let
\{u^{(i)}\}
and
\{V^{(i)}\}
be the sequences of spectral Barron functions and
(\bar{u},\bar{V})
be the optimal solution of the HJB equation (
2.1
) given by Theorem
1
. Then for any compact domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist cosine-activated two-layer neural networks with
n
hidden neurons

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\cos(w_{j}\cdot x+b_{j}),\quad V_{n}(x)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\cos(v_{j}\cdot x+d_{j}),

where
a_{j},b_{j},c_{j},d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
w_{j},v_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for each
j
, such that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq\sqrt{|K|}\left(1+\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|u^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)n^{-1/2},

and

\displaystyle\|V_{n}-\bar{V}\|_{H^{2}(K)}\leq\sqrt{|K|}\left(1+\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|V^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)n^{-1/2},

where
|K|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
K
.

Remark
.

In Theorem
2
, we focus on neural networks with cosine activation functions, as these are most directly connected to the spectral Barron space, which is defined via the Fourier transform. As shown in
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
, the spectral Barron space also characterizes functions that can be efficiently approximated by neural networks with more general activation functions, such as sigmoidal, ReLU, or SoftPlus. Consequently, the results of Theorem
2
can be extended to a broader class of activation functions, though we will not go into details.

Proofs

1
and Theorem
2
.

3.1.
Preliminaries

In this subsection, we present some preliminary results on the properties of the spectral Barron space. First, we state the approximation theorem for spectral Barron functions. The proof is given in Appendix
B
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f\in\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for any compact domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a cosine-activated two-layer neural network with
n
hidden neurons

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\cos\left(w_{j}\cdot x+b_{j}\right)

where
a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
w_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for each
j
, such that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-f\|_{H^{k}(K)}\leq\sqrt{|K|}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}n^{-1/2},

where
|K|
is the Lebesgue measure of
K
.

The following embedding properties are immediate from the definition of spectral Barron norm.

Proposition 3.2
.

The following embeddings hold.

(1)

\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
s\geq 0
;

(2)

\mathcal{B}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
s^{\prime}\geq s
.

The proposition below indicates that spectral Barron spaces are closed under product and differentiation of functions. The proof is given in Appendix
B
. We also remark that the differential operator
\partial_{i}
is well-defined on the spectral Barron space
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
s\geq 0
as a result of the regularity of spectral Barron functions shown in Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then

(1)

For any
f,g\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
fg\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2)

For any
1\leq i\leq d
, the differential operator
\partial_{i}:\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a bounded linear operator with

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The following estimates of the bound of
(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}
in spectral Barron spaces will be useful in the subsequent analysis.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
s\geq 0
. If
\gamma>0
, then for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\right)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

One can easily compute that

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{1}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi=\gamma^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Simple calculation shows that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1+|\xi|}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)},\hskip 10.00002pt\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{2}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\leq 1+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{1+|\xi|}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi=\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and

\displaystyle\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{2}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\right)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\qed

3.2.
Control formulation of HJB equations

In this subsection, we reformulate the HJB equation (
1.2
) into an optimal control problem. We assume that the coefficients
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
are Lipschitz continuous and globally bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We make the above regularity and boundedness assumptions on
f
and
g
here for the purpose of introducing the optimal control problem. In proving our main results, assuming
f
,
g
are spectral Barron functions of order
s\geq 1
automatically satisfies these conditions.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space such that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is complete,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathcal{F}
, and
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is right continuous. Let
W_{t}
be an
n
-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. Consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

(3.1)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=\left(f(X_{t})+g(X_{t})u_{t}\right)dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptX_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
u(\cdot):[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-progressively measurable. We then consider an infinite horizon optimal control problem to minimize the following cost functional:

(3.2)

\displaystyle J(x,u(\cdot)):=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\left(\ell(X_{t})+\|u_{t}\|_{R}^{2}\right)dt\mid X_{0}=x\right].

The control
u(\cdot)
is chosen over the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:=\{u:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid u(\cdot)\text{ is }\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\text{-progressively measurable}\}.

The optimal value function is defined as

(3.3)

\displaystyle V(x):=\min_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}}J(x,u(\cdot)),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

According to standard stochastic control theory
[
30
]
, if the optimal value function
V
exists and
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
V
satisfies the HJB equation (
2.1
). The optimal feedback control
u^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle u^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{argmin}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\{\mathcal{L}_{u}V(x)+\ell(x)+\|u\|_{R}^{2}\right\}=-\dfrac{1}{2}R^{-1}g(x)^{T}\nabla V(x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore, an equivalent form of the HJB equation (
2.1
) is

(3.4)

\displaystyle-\gamma V+\nabla V\cdot\left(f+g\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V\right)\right)+\Delta V+l(x)+\left\lVert-\dfrac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V\right\rVert_{R}^{2}=0.

Definition 2
.

A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called an admissible feedback control if (i)
u
is continuous and (ii)
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a unique (strong) solution
X_{t}^{u}
to the SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}=\left(f(X_{t})+g(X_{t})u(X_{t})\right)dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptX_{0}=x.

such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\left(\ell(X_{t}^{u})+\|u(X_{t}^{u})\|_{R}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]<\infty
.

The control formulation helps us represent the solution of the linearization of the HJB equation (
2.1
) in terms of probabilistic expectation that involves the state trajectory given by (
3.1
). As we will see in the next subsection, this representation will allow us to establish the monotonicity of the sequence that we construct to approximate the solution of (
2.1
), which is crucial to the convergence of the approximating sequence. The definition of admissibility specifies the set of feedback controls under which such a desired probabilistic representation is possible.

3.3.
Policy iteration

In this subsection, we use policy iteration to construct sequences of spectral Barron functions to approximate the solution to the HJB equation (
2.1
). The policy iteration is an algorithm to find the optimal solution by iteratively solving the linearized HJB equation and improving the controls; see Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Policy iteration for the elliptic HJB equation (
2.1
)

Choose an initial admissible control
u^{(0)}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, if there exists
V^{(i)}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that solves the equation

(3.5)

\displaystyle-\gamma V^{(i)}(x)+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i)}}V^{(i)}(x)+\ell(x)+\|u^{(i)}(x)\|_{R}^{2}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

update the control by

(3.6)

\displaystyle u^{(i+1)}(x):=-\dfrac{1}{2}R^{-1}g(x)^{T}\nabla V^{(i)}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We remark that in presenting the algorithm, there is no guarantee that a classical solution to the linearized equation (
3.5
) always exists. We settle this problem by assuming the coefficient functions
f
,
g
,
\ell
lie in spectral Barron spaces and address the well-posedness of the linearized HJB equation by showing that if the control is a spectral Barron function, then there exists a unique solution to the linearized equation (
3.5
) in the spectral Barron space, and by the regularity of spectral Barron functions in Proposition
3.1
, there exists a unique classical solution to (
3.5
).

We first prove a regularity result of linear second-order elliptic PDEs in spectral Barron spaces.

Theorem 3
.

For any
s\geq 1
,
\gamma>0
,
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
v_{f}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a unique solution
v^{*}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the equation

(3.7)

\displaystyle\gamma v-\Delta v+\mu\cdot\nabla v=v_{f}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Further,
v^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|v^{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|v_{f}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
C
is a constant depending only on
d
,
\gamma
and
\mu
.

The proof of Theorem
3
is given in Appendix
A
. A similar regularity theorem of the static Schrödinger equation is given in
[
4
]
. The main difference between the elliptic PDE (
3.7
) discussed here and the one in
[
4
]
is that the equation (
3.7
) includes a drift term
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Without the drift term
\mu
, one can obtain
\|v^{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|v_{f}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
following the proof of
[
4
]
. However, the difference is insignificant to our problem since, roughly speaking, the source term shares the same order of the control function
u
while the solution corresponds to the value function
V
, and it is natural from (
3.6
) that they differ in order by 1 in terms of spectral Barron norms. In Theorem
3
, we specify
s\geq 1
to ensure that the solution to (
3.7
) is classical. However, when considering
s\geq 0
, under the same assumptions, one can prove in exactly the same way that there exists a unique solution
v^{*}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the equation
(I+\mathcal{T})v=(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}v_{f}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathcal{T}=(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}\mu\cdot\nabla
. In this case, the Laplacian of
v
does not necessarily exist in the classical sense.

We present the following two lemmas, which are evident from the algebras of spectral Barron spaces. The proofs can be found in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
s\geq 0
. For any control
u\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a constant
C_{R,1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\langle u,u\rangle_{R}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{R,1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
g\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For any value function
V\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

\displaystyle u:=-\dfrac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V,

then there exists
C_{R,2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The following lemma shows that any spectral Barron control
u
is admissible and the linearized HJB equation

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\gamma V_{u}(x)+\mathcal{L}_{u}V_{u}(x)+\ell(x)+\|u(x)\|_{R}^{2}=0,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

admits a unique spectral Barron solution
V_{u}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds for
s\geq 1
and
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
u
is an admissible feedback control and there exists a unique solution
V_{u}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the linearized HJB equation (
3.8
).

Proof.

By Assumption
A
, Proposition
3.3
(1) and Lemma
3.5
, we have
f+gu\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\ell+\|u\|_{R}^{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Theorem
3
implies that there exists a unique solution
V_{u}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the equation (
3.8
). By Proposition
3.1
,
V_{u}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thus
V_{u}
is a classical solution of (
3.8
).

We now show that
u
is admissible. Since
f,g,u\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
s\geq 1
, by Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
,
f,g,u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and are globally bounded. Moreover,
f,g,u
are Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as their gradients lie in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By standard SDE theory (for instance, Theorem 5.4 of
[
15
]
), there exists a unique (strong) solution
X_{t}^{u}
of the SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{u}=\left(f\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)+g\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)u\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)\right)dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptX^{u}_{0}=x.

By Itô’s formula and the equation (
3.8
),

\displaystyle\quad d\left(e^{-\gamma t}V_{u}\left(X^{u}_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma t}\left(-\gamma V_{u}+\mathcal{L}_{u}V_{u}\right)\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dt+\sqrt{2}e^{-\gamma t}\nabla V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dW_{t}

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma t}\left(-\ell-\|u\|_{R}^{2}\right)\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dt+\sqrt{2}e^{-\gamma t}\nabla V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dW_{t}.

Integrate both sides from
0
to
T
, we get

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma T}V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)-V_{u}\left(X^{u}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\gamma t}\left(-\ell-\|u\|_{R}^{2}\right)\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dt+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\gamma t}\nabla V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dW_{t}.

Taking expectation with limit
T\to\infty
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\left(\ell+\|u\|_{R}^{2}\right)\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dt\right]=V_{u}(x)-\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\gamma T}V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)\right].

Since
V_{u}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
V_{u}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\gamma T}V_{u}\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)\right]=0.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\left(\ell+\|u\|_{R}^{2}\right)\left(X_{t}^{u}\right)dt\right]=V_{u}(x)<\infty.

Therefore,
u
is admissible.
∎

Now we construct sequences of controls and value functions in spectral Barron spaces through the policy iteration algorithm. The following proposition also shows that the resulting sequence of value functions is pointwise monotone.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds for
s\geq 1
and the initial control
u^{(0)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the policy iteration (Alg.
1
) gives a sequence of admissible controls
\{u^{(i)}\}
and a sequence of value functions
\{V^{(i)}\}
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
u^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
V^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover,
\{V^{(i)}\}
is pointwise decreasing.

Proof.

1. We show by induction that the policy iteration (Alg.
1
) generates a sequence of admissible controls
\{u^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a sequence of value functions
\{V^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose
u^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Lemma
3.7
,
u^{(i)}
is admissible and there exists a unique solution
V^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the equation (
3.5
). By Proposition
3.3
(2),
\nabla V^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
u^{(i+1)}
is updated according to (
3.6
), by Lemma
3.6
,
u^{(i+1)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Applying Lemma
3.7
again,
u^{(i+1)}
is admissible.

2. We next show that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{V^{(i)}(x)\}
is a decreasing sequence.
Since
u^{(i+1)}
is admissible, there exists a unique (strong) solution
X_{t}^{(i+1)}:=X_{t}^{u^{(i+1)}}
of the SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{u^{(i+1)}}=\Bigl(f\bigl(X_{t}^{u^{(i+1)}}\bigr)+g\bigl(X_{t}^{u^{(i+1)}}\bigr)u^{(i+1)}\bigl(X_{t}^{u^{(i+1)}}\bigr)\Bigr)dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t},\qquad X^{u^{(i+1)}}_{0}=x.

By Itô’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle d\Bigl(e^{-\gamma t}V^{(i)}\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\gamma V^{(i)}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i)}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt+\sqrt{2}e^{-\gamma t}\nabla V^{(i)}\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dW_{t}.

For any
T\geq 0
,

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma T}V^{(i)}\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{T}\bigr)-V^{(i)}\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{0}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\gamma V^{(i)}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i)}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\gamma t}\nabla V^{(i)}\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dW_{t}.

Taking expectation with
T\to\infty
(note
V^{(i)}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), we have

\displaystyle-V^{(i)}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\gamma V^{(i)}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i)}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right].

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle-V^{(i+1)}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\gamma V^{(i+1)}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i+1)}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right].

Hence

\displaystyle V^{(i+1)}(x)-V^{(i)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\gamma V^{(i)}+\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i)}+\gamma V^{(i+1)}-\mathcal{L}_{u^{(i+1)}}V^{(i+1)}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right].

We know that

\displaystyle\nabla V^{(i)}\cdot(f+gu^{(i+1)})=\gamma V^{(i)}+\nabla V^{(i)}\cdot g(u^{(i+1)}-u^{(i)})-(\ell+\|u^{(i)}\|_{R}^{2})-\Delta V^{(i)},

and

\displaystyle\nabla V^{(i+1)}\cdot(f+gu^{(i+1)})=\gamma V^{(i+1)}-(\ell+\|u^{(i+1)}\|_{R}^{2})-\Delta V^{(i+1)}.

So

\displaystyle V^{(i+1)}(x)-V^{(i)}(x)=

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(\|u^{(i+1)}\|_{R}^{2}-\|u^{(i)}\|_{R}^{2}+\nabla V^{(i)}\cdot g(u^{(i+1)}-u^{(i)})\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right].

Since
u^{(i+1)}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V^{(i)}
,

\displaystyle V^{(i+1)}(x)-V^{(i)}(x)=

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(\|u^{(i+1)}\|_{R}^{2}-\|u^{(i)}\|_{R}^{2}+\nabla V^{(i)}\cdot g(u^{(i+1)}-u^{(i)})\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(\|u^{(i+1)}\|_{R}^{2}-\|u^{(i)}\|_{R}^{2}-2\|u^{(i+1)}\|_{R}^{2}+2\langle u^{(i+1)},u^{(i)}\rangle_{R}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\Bigl(-\|u^{(i+1)}-u^{(i)}\|_{R}^{2}\Bigr)\bigl(X^{(i+1)}_{t}\bigr)dt\right]\leq 0.\qed

Proposition
3.8
shows that the sequences of controls and value functions lie in the spectral Barron spaces as long as the initial control and the coefficients are spectral Barron functions, and that the sequence of value functions is pointwise decreasing.
Proposition
3.8
, however, does not guarantee that the sequence
\{V^{(i)}\}
converges. In the following Propositions
3.9
and
3.10
, we prove that when the discount factor is large enough,
\{V^{(i)}\}
is bounded in spectral Barron norm and thus uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As a result,
\{V^{(i)}\}
decreases pointwise to some limit function
\bar{V}
. In fact, we can show that
\{V^{(i)}\}
converges locally uniformly to
\bar{V}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We first show in Proposition
3.9
that when the discount factor
\gamma
is sufficiently large, if the spectral Barron norm of the initial control is sufficiently small, then the sequences of controls and value functions are both uniformly bounded in spectral Barron spaces.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds for
s\geq 1
. Let
C_{R,1}
and
C_{R,2}
be the positive constants given in Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
respectively. Assume that

(3.10)

\displaystyle 2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)\geq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+2\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2}\left(C_{R,1}C_{R,2}^{2}+C_{R,2}\right)^{1/2}.

Define
h(x)=\dfrac{a+bx^{2}}{c-dx}
where

\begin{aligned} a&:=C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\\
b&:=C_{R,1}C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\end{aligned}\hskip 20.00003pt\begin{aligned} c&:=2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\\
d&:=\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

Then there exists
x_{0}\in(0,c/d)
such that
x_{0}=h(x_{0})
. Further, assume that the initial control
u^{(0)}\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and satisfies
\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<x_{0}
. Then Algorithm
1
gives a sequence of admissible controls
\{u^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a sequence of value functions
\{V^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover,
\{u^{(i)}\}
and
\{V^{(i)}\}
are uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Specifically,

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|u^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq x_{0},\text{ and }\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|V^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq a_{0}h(x_{0}).

where
a_{0}=\left(C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{-1}
.

Proof.

1. Given the assumption that
\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}>0
and the discount factor
\gamma
satisfies (
3.10
), we have
b,c,d>0
and
a\geq 0
. Moreover, (
3.10
) entails that

\displaystyle\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{2}-4C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(C_{R,1}C_{R,2}+1\right)\geq 0,

that is,
c^{2}-4a(b+d)\geq 0
, which implies that there exists at least one real solution
x_{0}
to the equation

, i.e.,
(b+d)x^{2}-cx+a=0
. In fact,
x_{0}
is a fixed point of

. Since

is positive for all

and negative for

, we see that
0\leq x_{0}<c/d
. Notice that

is strictly increasing on

because

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)=\dfrac{2bcx-bdx^{2}+ad}{(c-dx)^{2}}

has two roots

and
x_{2}>0
. There is a local minimum at
x_{1}<0
, and
h(x)
is strictly increasing on
(x_{1},c/d)
, while

as

. In this way, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle 0\leq x<x_{0}\hskip 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\hskip 5.0pt0\leq h(0)\leq h(x)<h(x_{0})=x_{0}.

2. Fix
s\geq 1
. We start by choosing an inital control
u^{(0)}
with

\displaystyle 0\leq\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<x_{0}<c/d=\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)/\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which allows

to satisfy

(3.12)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}\|f+gu^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<1.

Since the operator
(\gamma-\Delta)^{-1}(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is linear and bounded, and by Proposition

, it satisfies

\displaystyle\|(\gamma-\Delta)^{-1}(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}\|f+gu^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

it follows from (
3.12
) that the inverse
\left(I-\left(\gamma-\Delta\right)^{-1}(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla\right)^{-1}
exists with

\displaystyle\|\Bigl(I-\left(\gamma-\Delta\right)^{-1}(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla\Bigr)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\dfrac{1}{1-C_{\gamma}\|(f+gu^{(0)})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

where
C_{\gamma}=\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{u^{(0)}}:=\gamma-\Delta-(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla.

Since

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{T}_{u^{(0)}}\right)^{-1}=\left(I-\left(\gamma-\Delta\right)^{-1}(f+gu^{(0)})\cdot\nabla\right)^{-1}(\gamma-\Delta)^{-1},

\displaystyle\|\left(\mathcal{T}_{u^{(0)}}\right)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C_{\gamma}}{1-C_{\gamma}\|(f+gu^{(0)})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|(f+gu^{(0)})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{u^{(0)}}V^{(0)}=\ell+\|u^{(0)}\|_{R}^{2}.

\displaystyle\|V^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{R,1}\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}{\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)-\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}.

\displaystyle 0\leq\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigl(\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{R,1}\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Bigr)}{\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)-\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

\displaystyle\|V^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\left(\mathcal{T}_{u^{(1)}}\right)^{-1}\bigl(\ell+\|u^{(1)}\|_{R}^{2}\bigr)\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{R,1}\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}{\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)-\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

\displaystyle 0\leq\|u^{(2)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C_{R,2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigl(\|\ell\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{R,1}\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Bigr)}{\left(2(\sqrt{1+\gamma}-1)-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)-\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

\displaystyle=h\bigl(\|u^{(1)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\bigr)<x_{0}<c/d.

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|u^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq x_{0}<c/d.

\displaystyle\|V^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq a_{0}h\bigl(\|u^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\bigr)\leq a_{0}h(x_{0}),

Recall that Proposition
3.8
shows that the sequence of spectral Barron value functions

decreases pointwise. In proving the next theorem, we show that if the sequence

is uniformly bounded in spectral Barron norm, then
\{V^{(i)}\}
decreases to some limit
\bar{V}
. In fact, a subsequence of
\{V^{(i)}\}
converges locally uniformly to the limit
\bar{V}
and
\bar{V}
is a solution to the HJB equation (

).

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds for
s\geq 2
. Under the same assumption as Proposition
3.9
, there exists a sequence
\{V^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\bar{V}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\{V^{(i)}\}
converges to
\bar{V}
in
C^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the sequence

converges to
\bar{u}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla\bar{V}
in

.
As a result,
\bar{V}
is a classical solution to the HJB equation (
2.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.9
, Algorithm
1
gives a sequence of admissible controls
\{u^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a sequence of value functions
\{V^{(i)}\}
in
\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, we have
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|V^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
. Thus
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\widehat{V^{(i)}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
. Then there exists
M_{0}>0
such that for all

,

\displaystyle|V^{(i)}(x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{V^{(i)}}(\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi<M_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Thus
\{V^{(i)}\}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular,
\{V^{(i)}\}
is uniformly bounded below. Recall that Proposition
3.8
shows that
\{V^{(i)}\}
is pointwise decreasing, hence
\{V^{(i)}\}
converges pointwise to some limit
\bar{V}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2), we also have

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\nabla^{m}V^{(i)}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1-m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty,\text{ for }m=1,2,3.

Recall that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla V^{(i)}(x)|<M_{1},\hskip 5.0pt\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla^{2}V^{(i)}(x)|<M_{2},\hskip 5.0pt\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla^{3}V^{(i)}(x)|<M_{3}.

\bar{V}_{K}

Appendix A
Regularity of Linear Second-order Elliptic PDEs in Spectral Barron Spaces

This section aims to prove the regularity theorem of linear second-order elliptic PDEs in spectral Barron spaces. The main idea is to apply the Fredholm alternative. Similar techniques are used in
[
4
]
.
The following Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem will be used to prove the compactness of the operator
(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}\mu\cdot\nabla
in the spectral Barron space. The proof is standard and can be found, for example, in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 4

(Kolmogorov-Riesz)
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, a subset
\mathcal{F}\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is totally bounded if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is bounded.

(ii)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
for which

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}|f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x<\epsilon^{p},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

(iii)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
for which

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x+y)-f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x<\epsilon^{p},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall f\in\mathcal{F},\forall|y|<\delta.

We then apply the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem to show that the operator
(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}\mu\cdot\nabla
is compact.

Lemma A.1
.

For any
s\geq 1
, assume that
\gamma>0
and
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\in\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the operator
\mathcal{T}=(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}\mu\cdot\nabla:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact.

Proof.

To show that
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact, it suffices to show that the image of the closed unit ball under
\mathcal{T}
is relatively compact in
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is complete, any subset of
\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is relatively compact if and only it is totally bounded. Therefore, to show that
\mathcal{T}
is compact, it suffices to show that the subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=\left\{\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mid\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\right\}\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is totally bounded. We verify the three conditions of the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem for
\mathcal{F}
.

(i) For any
\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
, by Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|(\gamma I-\Delta)^{-1}\mu\cdot\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{\gamma}\|\mu\cdot\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{\gamma}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
C_{\gamma}=1+\gamma^{-1}
.

(ii) For any
\epsilon>0
, choose
R>0
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1+|\xi|}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}<\epsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall|\xi|>R.

Then for any
\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
, by Propositions
3.2
and
3.3

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>R}

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}d\xi=\int_{|\xi|>R}\dfrac{1}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>R}\dfrac{1+|\xi|}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s-1}d\xi\leq\epsilon\|\mu\cdot\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(iii) For any
\epsilon>0
, by (ii) there exists
R>0
such that for any
\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>R}|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\epsilon.

Set

\displaystyle L_{1}:=\max_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\dfrac{\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptL_{2}:=\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\mathrm{d}\xi=\mathrm{Vol}(B_{2R}(0)).

Since
\mu\in\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
s\geq 1
, we have
\mu\in\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Denote
\mu=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{d})
, then for each
1\leq i\leq d
we have
\widehat{\mu_{i}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence there exists
\varphi_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
1\leq i\leq d
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}\|\widehat{\mu_{i}}-\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\dfrac{\epsilon}{L_{1}}.

Since
\xi\mapsto\dfrac{\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}
is continuous, it is uniformly continuous on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Also, each
\varphi_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, there exists
\delta<R
such that

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}-\dfrac{\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|\right)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}\right|\leq\epsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\xi,\xi^{\prime}:|\xi|\leq 3R,|\xi^{\prime}|\leq 3R,|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|<\delta,

and

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq i\leq d}\left|\varphi_{i}(\xi)-\varphi_{i}(\xi^{\prime})\right|\leq\dfrac{\epsilon}{L_{1}L_{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\xi,\xi^{\prime}:|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|<\delta.

For any
|y|<\delta<R
and
\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>2R}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\right|\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{|\xi|>2R}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\right|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

(A.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon+\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)\dfrac{(1+|\xi+y|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi+y|^{2}}-\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi)\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\right|

(A.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)\right|\cdot\left|\dfrac{(1+|\xi+y|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi+y|^{2}}-\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\right|+\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\cdot\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)-\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi)\right|.

For the first term of (
A.2
), we have the following estimate.

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)\right|\cdot\left|\dfrac{(1+|\xi+y|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi+y|^{2}}-\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

(A.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\|\mu\cdot\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\epsilon\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For the second term of (
A.2
), we estimate as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\dfrac{(1+|\xi|)^{s}}{\gamma+|\xi|^{2}}\cdot\left|\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi+y)-\widehat{\mu\cdot\nabla v}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{1}\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)\widehat{\mu_{i}}(\xi+y-\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)\widehat{\mu_{i}}(\xi-\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{1}\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)|\bigg(|\widehat{\mu_{i}}(\xi+y-\eta)-\varphi_{i}(\xi+y-\eta)|+|\widehat{\mu_{i}}(\xi-\eta)-\varphi_{i}(\xi-\eta)|

\displaystyle+|\varphi_{i}(\xi+y-\eta)-\varphi_{i}(\xi-\eta)|\bigg)\mathrm{d}\eta\bigg\}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg\{\dfrac{\epsilon}{L_{2}}\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta+2L_{1}\|\widehat{\mu_{i}}-\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta\bigg\}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\partial_{i}v}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta\leq 3\epsilon\|\nabla v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 3\epsilon\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 3\epsilon.

Combining (
A.3
) and (
A.4
),

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 2R}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi\leq(\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+3)\epsilon,

and by (
A.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi+y)\left(1+|\xi+y|\right)^{s}-\widehat{\mathcal{T}(v)}(\xi)\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\right|\mathrm{d}\xi\leq(\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+5)\epsilon

for any
|y|<\delta
and
\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
. By the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem,
\mathcal{F}
is totally bounded.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For any
s\geq 1
, suppose that the vector-valued function
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\in\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the operator
I+\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
v\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle v+\mathcal{T}(v)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\gamma v-\Delta v+\mu\cdot\nabla v=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Suppose there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
v(x_{0})\neq 0
. Then
|v(x_{0})|>\epsilon_{0}>0
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Now since
v\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s\geq 1
,
\hat{v}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma,
|v(x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Then there exists
R>0
for which
|x_{0}|\leq R
and
|v(x)|<\epsilon_{0}<|v(x_{0})|
for all
|x|>R
. Choose
r>0
, Proposition
3.1
implies that
v\in H^{1}(B_{R+r}(0))
as
v\in\mathcal{B}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. According to the weak maximum principle (see Theorem 8.1 in
[
8
]
),

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|\leq R+r}|v(x)|=\sup_{|x|=R+r}|v(x)|.

Therefore, there exists
x_{r}
such that
|x_{r}|=R+r
and
|v(x_{r})|\geq|v(x_{0})|
, a contradiction with
|v(x_{r})|<\epsilon_{0}<|v(x_{0})|
. Thus
v\equiv 0
.
∎

.

For any
s\geq 1
, suppose that the vector-valued function
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\in\mathcal{B}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Proof.

By Lemmas
A.1
and
A.2
, the result follows immediately from the Fredholm alternative.

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot x}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|e^{\mathrm{i}(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi))}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{k}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}e^{\mathrm{i}(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi))}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{k}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\cos\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dfrac{|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{k}}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\cos\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\cos\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right].

By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
k\geq 1
, then for each
1\leq i\leq d
, the partial derivative

\displaystyle\partial_{i}f(x)=-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\xi_{i}\sin(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi))\right]

exists and is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. Hence
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
k\geq 2
, then the second-order partial derivative

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}f(x)=-\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\cos\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right]

exists and is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the dominated convergence theorem again. Thus
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By repeating the argument, one can show that if
f\in\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}
be independent and identically distributed samples from
\mu
, and

\displaystyle f_{n}(x):=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{l}\cos\left(w_{l}\cdot x+b_{l}\right),

where

\displaystyle a_{l}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(1+|\xi_{l}|\right)^{-k},\hskip 10.00002ptw_{l}=\xi_{l},\hskip 10.00002ptb_{l}=\theta(\xi_{l}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\int_{K}|f_{n}(x)-f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(1+|\xi_{l}|\right)^{-k}\cos\left(\xi_{l}\cdot x+\theta(\xi_{l})\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathrm{Var}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\cos\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}\cos^{2}\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}\right]\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(1+|\xi|)^{-2k}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right]\leq\dfrac{|K|\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}.

If
k\geq 1
, the partial derivative
\partial_{i}f
exists for
1\leq i\leq d
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{i}f_{n}-\partial_{i}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{K}|\partial_{i}f_{n}(x)-\partial_{i}f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{K}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(1+|\xi_{l}|\right)^{-k}\xi_{l,i}\sin\left(\xi_{l}\cdot x+\theta(\xi_{l})\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{K}\mathrm{Var}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-k}\xi\sin\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}|\xi|^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\xi\cdot x+\theta(\xi)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}|\xi|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(1+|\xi|)^{-2k}(1+|\xi|^{2})\leq 1
when
k\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{H^{1}(K)}\right]\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}(1+|\xi|^{2})\right]\mathrm{d}x\leq\dfrac{|K|\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}.

If
k\geq 2
, the second-order partial derivative
\partial_{ij}f
exists for any
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{ij}f_{n}-\partial_{ij}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right]\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}|\xi|^{4}\right]\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}\right)\leq 1
when
k\geq 2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(K)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}\int_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}\left[\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{-2k}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{|K|\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}.

By applying the argument in the same way
k+1
times, one can conclude that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes n}}\left[\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{H^{k}(K)}\right]\leq\dfrac{|K|\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{n}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

(1) If
f,g\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)||\hat{g}(\eta-\xi)|(1+|\eta|)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\xi)||\hat{g}(\eta-\xi)|(1+|\xi|)^{s}(1+|\eta-\xi|)^{s}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta.

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2) If
f\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{i}}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\left|\widehat{\partial_{x_{i}}f}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\left|\mathrm{i}\xi_{i}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s}\left|\xi_{i}\right|\cdot\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{s+1}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi=\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\qed

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

For any control
u
, we have

\displaystyle\langle u,u\rangle_{R}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{i}R_{ij}u_{j}.

Since
R
is a positive-definite matrix,
\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}R_{ij}>0
. If
u\in\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\|\langle u,u\rangle_{R}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}R_{ij}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|u_{i}u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}R_{ij}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|u_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:barron_alg} (1))}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}R_{ij}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

By choosing
C_{R,1}=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq m}R_{ij}
, the claim is proved.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

For each
1\leq i\leq m
,

\displaystyle\left(R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\left(g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{j},

and for each
1\leq j\leq m
,

\displaystyle\left(g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}g_{kj}\partial_{k}V.

If
V\in\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\|\left(g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{j}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|g_{kj}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\partial_{k}V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|g_{kj}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:barron_alg} (2))}

Then

\displaystyle\|\left(R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{i}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\right|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\left(g^{T}\nabla V\right)_{j}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\right|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|g_{kj}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\right|\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and so

\displaystyle\|R^{-1}g^{T}\nabla V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\right|\Bigr)\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|V\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For the claim to be established, it suffices to choose

C_{R,2}:=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\left|\left(R^{-1}\right)_{ij}\right|\Bigr).\qed

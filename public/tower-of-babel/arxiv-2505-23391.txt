Suppression of Fluid Echoes and Sobolev Stability Threshold for 2D Dissipative Fluid Equations Around Couette Flow
Niklas Knobel

Abstract
. We study the Sobolev stability thresholds of 2d dissipative fluid equations around Couette flow on the domain 𝕋 × ℝ \mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R} . We prove a bound for general nonlinear interactions, which, for several fluid equations, reduces the proof of nonlinear stability to a linear stability analysis. We apply this approach to the examples of Navier-Stokes, Boussinesq and magnetohydrodynamic equations around Couette flow. This improves the Sobolev stability threshold for the Boussinesq equations around Couette flow and large affine temperature to 1 / 3 1/3 and for the MHD equations around Couette flow and constant magnetic field to 1 / 3 + 1/3^{+} .



1.
Introduction

In fluid dynamics, a central question is the stability of shear flows in various fluid systems as dissipation tends to zero. This article develops a general framework to establish stability for a broad class of two-dimensional dissipative incompressible fluid equations near an affine shear flow in an infinite channel. We consider a velocity field
V:\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a transported scalar or vector quantity
\Phi
, that satisfy the governing equation

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla\Pi&=\nu\Delta V+F_{1}[V,\Phi],\\
\partial_{t}\Phi+(V\cdot\nabla)\Phi&=\kappa\Delta\Phi+F_{2}[V,\Phi],\\
\dive(V)&=0,\end{split}

with
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Here,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
represent interaction terms. We treat this equation as a general model encompassing various fluid systems, with the Navier–Stokes, Boussinesq, and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations serving as our primary examples. The Navier-Stokes equations correspond to the case when
\Phi=F_{i}=0
. The Boussinesq equations model a fluid with varying temperature and read

(B)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla\Pi&=\nu\Delta V-e_{2}\Theta,\\
\partial_{t}\Theta+(V\cdot\nabla)\Theta&=\kappa\Delta\Theta,\\
\dive(V)&=0,\end{split}

where
\Theta:\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the temperature variation. The MHD equations model a conducting fluid in the presence of a magnetic field and read

(MHD)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla\Pi&=\nu\Delta V+(B\cdot\nabla)B,\\
\partial_{t}B+(V\cdot\nabla)B&=\kappa\Delta B+(B\cdot\nabla)V,\\
\dive(V)=\dive(B)&=0,\end{split}

where
B:\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
corresponds to the magnetic field.

For these models, we investigate the stability of stationary solutions corresponding to linear shear flow, called Couette flow

\displaystyle V_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
0\end{pmatrix}

combined with a stationary solution
\Phi_{s}(x,y)
.

The relationship between fluid flow stability and dissipation is a fundamental problem in fluid dynamics
[
SHJ02
]
. As early as Reynolds’ pioneering work
[
Rey83
]
, the transition from laminar to turbulent pipe flow at low viscosities was investigated. Our primary goal is to
quantify
this relationship through a concept we refer to as the
stability threshold
. Since (
1
) represents a system, stability depends on the relevant parameters, specifically
\mu=\min\{\nu,\kappa\}
. The stability threshold in Sobolev spaces (cf.
[
BGM17
]
) is defined as the smallest
\lambda\geq 0
for which the following implication holds:

\displaystyle\|V-V_{s},\Phi-\Phi_{s}\|_{H^{N}}\leq\delta\mu^{\lambda}|\ln(\mu)|^{-\tilde{\lambda}}\quad

\displaystyle\implies\quad\text{Nonlinear stability},

\displaystyle\|V-V_{s},\Phi-\Phi_{s}\|_{H^{N}}\gg\mu^{\lambda}|\ln(\mu)|^{-\tilde{\lambda}}\quad

\displaystyle\implies\quad\text{Possible instability},

for some
\delta>0
and
\tilde{\lambda}\geq 0
. If
\tilde{\lambda}>0
, we say
\lambda^{+}
is the threshold. To identify such thresholds, we establish a general estimate that bounds nonlinear interactions and possible echo effects via dissipation. This method reduces the nonlinear stability analysis to a carefully structured linear problem, requiring the introduction of adapted variables and a linear weight.

Using this framework, we derive thresholds for various fluid models, including the Navier–Stokes, Boussinesq, and magnetohydrodynamic equations. In particular, our approach leads to the following improvements:

•

For the Boussinesq equations
[
ZZ23a
]
, the previous upper bound threshold of
1/2
is improved to
1/3
;

•

For the MHD equations
[
Dol24
,
Kno24b
,
WZ24
,
JRW25
]
, the previous upper bound threshold of
1/2
is improved to
1/3^{+}
(under the condition
\nu^{3}\leq\kappa\leq\nu^{\frac{1}{3}})
;

•

Our analysis also extends prior MHD results by incorporating vertical constant magnetic fields, whereas earlier works considered only horizontal ones.

Moreover, for the Navier–Stokes equations
[
BMV16
,
MZ22
,
WZ23
]
, our method recovers the previously established threshold of
1/3
in a direct and unified manner.

For a function
F
, we define its
x
average as
F_{=}=\int Fdx
and the non average part as
F_{\neq}=F-F_{=}
. The stability thresholds derived in this work can be summarized as follows:

Let

, then we have the following stability results:

•

There exists

1.1.
Approach and Main Result

To study perturbations around the Couette flow
V_{s}=ye_{1}
, it is natural to adopt a coordinate system moving along its characteristics. In these new coordinates, the perturbation variables are defined as

\displaystyle v(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=V(t,x+ty,y)-V_{s}(y),\qquad\phi(t,x,y)=\Phi(t,x+ty,y)-\Phi_{s}(y).

This change of variables modifies the spatial derivatives accordingly:

\partial_{y}^{t}:=\partial_{y}-t\partial_{x},\qquad\nabla_{t}:=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y}^{t})^{T},\qquad\Delta_{t}:=\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{y}^{t})^{2},\qquad\Lambda_{t}:=(-\Delta_{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We consistently use lowercase letters to denote quantities in the moving frame and uppercase letters for those in the original (stationary) frame.

To establish stability, we construct a suitable energy functional and employ time-dependent Fourier multipliers
[
BMV16
,
BM15a
]
, along with symmetrization techniques
[
BBZD23
,
Dol24
,
KZ25
]
, now standard in the field. Our analysis introduces novel weights and sharpens previously known stability thresholds. Moreover, we develop a more flexible framework that can be adapted to analyze thresholds in related systems, following an appropriate linear analysis. This is carried out in three steps:

Step 1: Linear dynamics.
We begin by analyzing the linearized system and constructing tailored unknowns
(f_{1}[\phi],q[v])
that capture the essential dynamics. These variables are linearly stable and satisfy the approximate relations

f_{1}\approx\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}\phi,\qquad v\approx\Lambda_{t}^{-(1+\gamma)}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}q,

in suitable Sobolev norms. This choice simplifies the structure of the linear and nonlinear interactions.

Step 2: Main nonlinear term.
For the tailored variables, we analyze the nonlinear terms, whose leading-order contribution takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}

\displaystyle\sim\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}(\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\Lambda_{t}^{-1-\gamma}q\cdot\nabla_{t}\Lambda^{-\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}f_{1})\sim\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}(\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda_{t}^{-1-\gamma}q\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}f_{1}).

To control this term, we introduce a carefully designed Sobolev weight
A
, leading to the following estimate for the nonlinear interaction:

(2)

\partial_{t}\|Af_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim\langle Af_{1},[\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}A,\Lambda_{t}^{-1+\gamma}\nabla^{\perp}q]\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}f_{1}\rangle=:NL.

Step 3, Nonlinear energy estimates:

We then construct a total energy
\mathcal{E}
in terms of the tailored unknowns, satisfying the integral identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{E}(1)+\int_{1}^{t}L+NL+LNL\ d\tau,

where
L
denotes linear contributions,
NL
the leading-order nonlinear term from (
2
), and
LNL
lower-order nonlinear interactions. We establish a priori energy estimates and, using local well-posedness, apply a bootstrap argument to show that
\mathcal{E}(t)
remains bounded for all time, provided the initial data are sufficiently small. The resulting threshold condition relates the parameter
\mu
to the size of the initial perturbation.

The threshold in our approach is determined by estimating the main nonlinear contribution

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{t}NL\ d\tau.

To control this term, we use carefully constructed, time-dependent Fourier multipliers
A
and
m
, which are rigorously introduced in Section
3
.
The following theorem provides a precise bound on the main nonlinearity and constitutes the core estimate of our analysis.

Theorem 2
.

Let
0<\mu\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
N\geq 12
,
0\leq\tilde{\gamma}\leq\gamma\leq 1
,
r>0
. Let
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
q:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, be functions satisfying the bootstrap bounds

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|Af\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}}+c_{1}\int_{1}^{t}\mu\|\nabla_{t}Af\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Af\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau&\leq C\varepsilon^{2}_{f},\\
\|Aq\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}}+c_{1}\int_{1}^{t}\mu\|\nabla_{t}Aq\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Aq\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau&\leq C\varepsilon^{2}_{q}.\end{split}

For some constants
c_{1},C>0
and parameters
\varepsilon_{f},\varepsilon_{q}>0
. Define the
main nonlinearity
as

\displaystyle NL_{f_{1},f_{2},q}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=|\langle Af_{1},[A\Lambda_{t}^{\tilde{\gamma}},\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda_{t}^{-(1+\gamma)}q]\cdot\nabla\Lambda_{t}^{-\tilde{\gamma}}f_{2})\rangle|.

Then, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{\gamma}>0
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{1}^{t}NL_{f_{1},f_{2},q}^{\gamma}d\tau&\leq\tilde{C}_{\gamma}\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\varepsilon^{2}_{f}\varepsilon_{q},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \gamma>0,\\
\int_{1}^{t}NL_{f_{1},f_{2},q}^{0}d\tau&\leq\tilde{C}_{0}|\ln(\mu)|^{1+r}\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\varepsilon^{2}_{f}\varepsilon_{q},\qquad\qquad\ \gamma=0.\end{split}

Remark 1
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1]
, the constant
\tilde{C}_{\gamma}
depends continuously on
\gamma
and satisfies
\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\tilde{C}_{\gamma}=\infty
. Nonetheless, the bound for
\gamma=0
remains finite due to the additional logarithmic loss in (
4
), which is absorbed into the constant
\tilde{C}_{0}<\infty
. The constant
\tilde{C}_{\gamma}
depends also on
N
,
r
,
\tilde{\gamma}
,
A
and
m
but is independent
\varepsilon_{f}
,
\varepsilon_{q}
and
\mu
.

Remark 2
.

The structure of the main nonlinearity in Theorem
2
appears in a wide range of fluid equations. For instance:

•

In the Navier–Stokes equations, it corresponds to
\gamma=1
,
\tilde{\gamma}=0
;

•

In the Boussinesq equations, to
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}
;

•

In the MHD equations, to
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}=0
.

This highlights the flexibility of our framework and the potential for broader applicability.

For the Navier–Stokes equations around Couette flow, the first upper bound on the stability threshold in Sobolev spaces was established in

The Boussinesq equations model a fluid with temperature-induced density variations. Around Couette flow, an affine temperature profile modifies the dynamics and weakens inviscid damping. Linear asymptotics for varying Richardson numbers are analyzed in
[
YL18
]
. For large affine temperature gradients, nonlinear stability is shown in the dissipative case by Zhai and Zhao
[
ZZ23a
]

. Additional results in the dissipative setting can be found in

,
MZZ23
]
. Without diffusion, Gevrey perturbations of size

MSHZ22
,
Zil23
]

The magnetohydrodynamics equations describe the coupled evolution of the velocity and magnetic field in a conducting fluid. The magnetic field is advected by the velocity and exerts a Lorentz force in return. In the 3D setting around Couette flow and a constant magnetic field satisfying some Diophantine condition, Liss establishes stability when viscosity and resistivity are equal (
\kappa=\nu

In the inviscid–resistive regime, Gevrey regularity ensures stability

2.
Nonlinear Heuristics

In this section, we provide a heuristic for why a threshold of
\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}
with an additional logarithmic term if
\gamma=0
is optimal for stability. This heuristic is based on the heuristics done for the Euler equations established in
[
BM15a
]
, where so-called echoes lead to loss of Gevrey regularity. Instead of using Gevrey regularity, we consider small data such that the dissipation suppresses possible echoes. We consider the nonlinear term

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f

\displaystyle\approx\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}((\Lambda_{t}^{-1-\gamma}\nabla^{\perp}q\cdot\nabla)\Lambda^{-\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}f).

This corresponds to the nonlinear term of

•

Navier-Stokes, if
f=q=w
,
\gamma=1
and
\tilde{\gamma}=0

•

Boussinesq equation, if
f=\zeta_{2}
and
q=\zeta_{1}
with
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}

•

MHD equation, if
f\in\{v,b\}
and
q=\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot v
with
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}=0

For these equations,
q
and
f
are of the same size in Sobolev regularity. For this heuristic, we assume that
f
is a scalar and we replace
q
by
f
. The worst possible growth appears due to a high-low frequency interaction

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f

\displaystyle\approx\Lambda^{\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}((\Lambda_{t}^{-1-\gamma}\nabla^{\perp}f^{hi}\cdot\nabla)\Lambda^{-\tilde{\gamma}}_{t}f^{low}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(k,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{l}\int d\xi\tfrac{|k,\eta-kt|^{\tilde{\gamma}}}{|l,\xi-lt|^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tfrac{\eta l-k\xi}{|k-l,\eta-\xi-(k-l)t|^{1+\gamma}}f^{hi}(k-l,\eta-\xi)f^{low}(l,\xi).

For this nonlinear term, there can appear echo chains, see
[
BM15a
,
DZ21
]
. That means that around times
t_{k}=\frac{\eta}{k}
the
k
mode forces the
k-1
mode, leading to growth. Since
t_{k}\leq t_{k-1}
, this can yield iterative growth
f(k,\eta)
f(k-1,\eta)
\dots
f(1,\eta).

If no dissipation is present, this iterative growth can lead to exponential loss of regularity and therefore requires spaces with exponential regularity, Gevrey spaces, for stability. For stability in Sobolev spaces, we use that dissipation suppresses this echo mechanism for small perturbations.

We model the high-low interaction by
f^{low}(-1,\xi)\approx\varepsilon e^{-\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}\delta_{\xi=0}
. We consider the
k
mode acting on the
k-1
mode and assume that
f(k,\eta)
stays constant. We obtain the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(k-1,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon e^{-\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}\tfrac{|k,\eta-(k-1)t|^{\tilde{\gamma}}}{\langle t\rangle^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tfrac{\eta}{k^{1+\gamma}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{1+\gamma}}f(k,\eta).

This term is large around resonant times
t\approx\tfrac{\eta}{k}
, then
\tfrac{|k,\eta-(k-1)t|^{\tilde{\gamma}}}{\langle t\rangle^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\approx 1
and so

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(k-1,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon e^{-\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}\tfrac{t}{k^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{1+\gamma}}f(k,\eta).

In particular, the interaction grows in time until the enhanced dissipation timescale
t\approx\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, for which it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(k-1,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}e^{-\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}\tfrac{1}{k^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{1+\gamma}}f(k,\eta).

After integrating in time, we obtain

\displaystyle f(k-1,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx f_{in}(k-1,\eta)+\varepsilon k^{-\gamma}\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|f(k,\eta)|\cdot\begin{cases}1,&\gamma>0,\\
|\ln(\mu)|,&\gamma=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, to suppress echoes we require

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\begin{cases}1,&\gamma>0,\\
|\ln(\mu)|^{-1},&\gamma=0,\end{cases}

to ensure stability. In particular, for
\gamma>0
this corresponds to the threshold of Theorem
2
while for
\gamma=0
we obtain an additional
|\ln(\mu)|^{r}
for
r>0
.

In this section, we define explicitly which weights are admissible and define useful sets for the proof of Theorem

. We define the time-dependent Fourier weight

\displaystyle A(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}e^{c\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t\textbf{1}_{k\neq 0}}m^{-1}(k,\eta),

\displaystyle m(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=m_{\gamma}(k,\eta)M_{L}(k,\eta)M_{\mu}(k,\eta).

The weight

Definition 3.1

Appendix A
The Navier-Stokes Threshold

In this section, we use Theorem
2
to prove the threshold for Navier-Stokes equations in Theorem
1
. The Navier-Stokes equation in velocity formulation states

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+V\cdot\nabla V+\nabla\Pi&=\nu\Delta V,\\
\dive(V)&=0.\end{split}

For the perturbative unknown
v(t,x,y)=V(t,x+yt,y)-V_{s}(x,y)
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}v+e_{1}v^{y}+(v\cdot\nabla_{t})v+\nabla_{t}\pi&=\nu\Delta_{t}v,\\
\dive_{t}(v)&=0.\end{split}

which yields for the vorticity
w=\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot v
the equation

(44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}w+(\overline{v}\cdot\nabla)w&=\nu\Delta_{t}w,\\
\overline{v}&=\Delta_{t}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}w,\end{split}

For this equation, we obtain the following stability:

Theorem 5

(Navier-Stokes part of Theorem
1
)
.

Let
N\geq 12
, then there exist
c,\delta>0
such that if the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\|w_{in}\|_{H^{N}}=\varepsilon\leq\delta\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}

then the global in time solution
w
to (
44
) is in
C_{t}H^{N}
and satisfies the following:

Stability of the vorticity

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|w\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon.

Inviscid damping and Enhanced dissipation estimates

\displaystyle\|w_{\neq}\|_{H^{N}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v^{x}_{\neq}\|_{H^{N-\frac{1}{2}}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}\|v^{y}\|_{H^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon e^{-c\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}.

Proof.

The proof is a direct application of Theorem
2
for
\gamma=1
and
\tilde{\gamma}=0
. We use the time-dependent Fourier multiplier

\displaystyle A(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}e^{c\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t\textbf{1}_{k\neq 0}}m^{-1}(k,\eta),

\displaystyle m(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=m_{1}(k,\eta)M_{\nu}(k,\eta).

This corresponds to the linear weight
M_{L}=1
, which is admissible in sense of Definition
3.1
. By a classical energy argument, obtain
\|Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}
for some
C_{1}>0
and
t\in[0,1]
. We prove by bootstrap that for
t>1
, it holds that

(45)

\displaystyle\|Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+c_{1}\int_{1}^{t}\nu\|\nabla_{t}Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Aw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{2},

for some
C_{1}>2(1+\frac{\|Aw|_{t=1}\|_{L^{2}}}{\|w_{in}\|_{H^{N}}})
. For the sake of contradiction, we assume that there exists a maximal time
T
such that the bootstrap assumption (
45
) holds. We estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle Aw,(\partial_{t}A)w\rangle+\nu\langle Aw,A\Delta_{t}w\rangle-\langle Aw,A((\overline{v}\cdot\nabla)w)\rangle

and by Lemma
3.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle Aw,\partial_{t}Aw\rangle

\displaystyle\leq c\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Aw_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Aw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Aw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\nu\|\nabla_{t}Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

By partial integration we obtain
\langle Aw,(\overline{v}\cdot\nabla)Aw)\rangle=0
and so

\displaystyle\langle Aw,A((\overline{v}\cdot\nabla)w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Aw,[A,\overline{v}]\cdot\nabla w)\rangle.

Therefore, we estimate

\displaystyle\|Aw\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{t}\nu\|\nabla_{t}Aw\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}Aw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|Aw_{in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle Aw,[A,\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-2}_{t}w]\cdot\nabla w\rangle d\tau.

By Theorem
2
we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{t}|\langle Aw,[A,\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-2}_{t}w]\cdot\nabla w\rangle|\leq C_{3}\delta\varepsilon^{2}.

for some
C_{3}>0
. Therefore, for
\delta
small enough, estimate (
45
) holds with
<
. By local existence, this contradicts the maximality. Thus, we infer (
45
) holds globally in time. The inviscid damping and enhanced dissipation estimates are a consequence of the definition of
A
and
v
.
∎

Appendix B
Estimates for the Linear Weights

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

The properties i) and ii) hold by definition. Property iii) can be obtained by applying the mean value theorem and using that
\partial_{\eta}M_{\mu}=-\frac{1}{k}\partial_{t}M_{\mu}
. To prove iv) we consider the case when
k,l\neq 0
, since
M(t,0,\eta)=M(t,1,\eta)
. We use
|e^{|x|}-1|\leq|x|e^{|x|}
to estimate

\displaystyle|M_{\mu}(k,\eta)-M_{\mu}(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|^{2}}-\tfrac{\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\xi}{l}|^{2}}d\tau\right)-1\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\xi}{l}|^{2}}d\tau\right|.

We distinguish between
|\frac{\xi}{l}|\leq 2t
and
|\frac{\xi}{l}|\geq 2t
cases. Let
|\frac{\xi}{l}|\geq 2t
, then we obtain
|l,\xi|\leq 2|l,\xi-lt|
.
Furthermore we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\xi}{l}|^{2}}d\tau\right|\leq t.

Combining these estimates, we infer

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\eta)-M(l,\xi)|\leq 2\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}|l,\xi-lt|.

For the case
|\frac{\xi}{l}|\leq 2t
we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\xi}{l}|^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{\eta}{k}}^{t-\frac{\eta}{k}}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{2}}ds-\int_{-\frac{\xi}{l}}^{t-\frac{\xi}{l}}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\frac{\xi}{l}}^{t-\frac{\eta}{k}}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{2}}ds-\int_{-\frac{\eta}{k}}^{t-\frac{\xi}{l}}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}s^{2}}ds.

Therefore, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{1+\mu^{\frac{2}{3}}|\tau-\frac{\xi}{l}|^{2}}d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\frac{\eta}{k}-\frac{\xi}{l}|\leq 2\frac{\xi}{l^{2}}\langle\eta-\xi,k-l\rangle^{2}

and so we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\eta)-M(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{|l,\xi|^{2}}{l^{2}t}\langle\eta-\xi,k-l\rangle^{2}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t\langle\eta-\xi,k-l\rangle^{2}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

The properties i) and ii) are a direct consequence of the definition of
M_{L}^{\theta}
. To prove iii), we use that

\displaystyle|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\eta)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|\leq|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\eta)-M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)|+|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|

and estimate them separately. For
|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\eta)-M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)|
, by mean value theorem for Lipschitz functions we infer

\displaystyle|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\eta)-M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial_{\eta}M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\tilde{\eta})||\eta-\xi|.

for some
\tilde{\eta}\in[\xi,\eta]
. We estimate

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\tilde{\eta})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\left(c^{3}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}+{\langle t-\tfrac{\tilde{\eta}}{k}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\right)M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\tilde{\eta})

in the Lipschitz sense. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{|l,\xi|}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\eta)-M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\tfrac{|l,\xi|}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}k}(\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}+{\langle t-\tfrac{\tilde{\eta}}{k}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}})|\eta-\xi|

\displaystyle\lesssim(t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}|l,\xi-lt|+t^{-\frac{1}{2}})\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{2}.

For
|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|
, we first show that if
M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)\neq 0
, then it holds that

(46)

\displaystyle|\tfrac{\xi}{l}|\lesssim(t+\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}})|k-l|

If
\min(|t-\frac{\xi}{k}|,|t-\frac{\xi}{l}|)\leq 2c^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}
, then (
46
) holds. Now let
\min(|t-\frac{\xi}{k}|,|t-\frac{\xi}{l}|)\geq 2c^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}
, then it holds
M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)=0
if either
t-\frac{\xi}{k},t-\frac{\xi}{l}\leq-2c^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}
or
t-\frac{\xi}{k},t-\frac{\xi}{l}\geq\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}
or
k=l
. For the case
t-\frac{\xi}{k}\leq\pm 2c^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}\leq t-\frac{\xi}{l}
there exists a
k^{\prime}\in(k,l)
such that
t-\frac{\xi}{k^{\prime}}=0
and we infer (
46
) since all caves are covered.

To estimate
|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|
, by definition it is sufficient to consider
k,l\neq 0
and since we can replace
(l,\xi)
and
(k,\eta)
it is sufficient to cosider the case
{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}\geq{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}
. Since
M_{1}
is bounded from below and above and using
|e^{x}-1|\leq|x|e^{|x|}
, we infer

\displaystyle|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{-\infty}^{t}\textbf{1}_{|\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}|\leq 2\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}c^{-1}}\tfrac{1}{\langle\frac{\eta}{k}-\tau\rangle^{3}}d\tau-\int_{-\infty}^{t}\textbf{1}_{|\tau-\frac{\xi}{k}|\leq 2\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}c^{-1}}\tfrac{1}{\langle\frac{\xi}{k}-\tau\rangle^{3}}d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{t-\frac{\xi}{k}}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}-\frac{t-\frac{\xi}{l}}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}\right|=\left|\frac{(t-\frac{\xi}{k})\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle-\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle(t-\frac{\xi}{l})}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2\left|\frac{(t-\frac{\xi}{k})-(t-\frac{\xi}{l})}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}\right|=|\xi|\left|\frac{1}{l}-\frac{1}{k}\right|\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}=\left|\frac{\xi}{lk}\right|\left|k-l\right|\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}.

Therefore, by using (
46
) and Lemma
3.1
we estimate

\displaystyle|M_{L}^{\theta}(k,\xi)

\displaystyle-M_{L}^{\theta}(l,\xi)|\tfrac{|l,\xi|}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq|{k}-l||\tfrac{\xi}{{k}{l}}|\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}\tfrac{|l,\xi|}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\sqrt{t}+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}t^{-\frac{3}{2}})\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle}|k-l|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(t\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{k}\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\langle\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\rangle\right)|k-l|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(t\tfrac{1}{(\min(1,\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t))}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{\frac{1}{2}}}{m_{\frac{1}{2}}}(k,\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{\frac{1}{2}}}{m_{\frac{1}{2}}}(l,\xi)}+\langle\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\rangle\right)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{5}

\displaystyle\leq\left((t+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}})\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{\frac{1}{2}}}{m_{\frac{1}{2}}}(k,\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{\frac{1}{2}}}{m_{\frac{1}{2}}}(l,\xi)}+\langle\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\rangle\right)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{5}

Property iv) is a consequence of using the mean value theorem and
\partial_{\eta}M_{L}^{\theta}=\tfrac{1}{k}\partial_{t}M_{L}^{\theta}
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Monotenicity and property (i) are clear by definition, property (ii) works the same as for the previous linear weights and therefore we only prove (iii). In the following we write
M
for
M_{L,d,c_{1}}
. We WLOG assume that
k,l\neq 0
, since
M(t,0,\cdot)=M(t,1,\cdot)
. We split

\displaystyle M(k,\eta)-M(l,\xi)

\displaystyle=M(k,\eta)-M(l,\eta)+M(l,\eta)-M(l,\xi),

and estimate the commutator separately. By mean calue theorem there exists a
\tilde{\eta}\in[\eta,\xi]
such that

\displaystyle M(k,\eta)-M(k,\xi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\eta}M(k,\tilde{\eta})(\eta-\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{2d_{2}}{k}\frac{1}{1+(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{k}))^{2}}(\eta-\xi)

We use that
\tfrac{1}{1+(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{k}))^{2}}\approx\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{k})^{2}}
and so

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\eta)-M(k,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|l,\xi|}{tk}\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{k})^{2}}|k-l,\eta-\xi|\lesssim|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{3}

which is consistent with (iii). Now we prove

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\eta)-M(l,\eta)|\lesssim\left(1+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}|\ln(\mu)|^{1+r}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(k,\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(l,\xi)}\right)\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{5}.

We distinguish between the tree cases (
0\in[k,l]
and
t\not\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
), (
0\not\in[k,l]
and
t\not\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
) and
t\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
. For
0\in[k,l]
and
t\not\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
,
k
and
l
have different signs and so

\displaystyle t\geq\min\left(|\tfrac{\xi}{l}|,|\tfrac{\xi}{k}|\right)

Furthermore since
|k-l|\geq|k|,|l|
we estimate

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\eta)-M(l,\eta)|\lesssim|k-l|^{2}.

Let
0\not\in[k,l]
and
t\not\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
, we consider
M(k,\eta)
as a (smooth) function of
k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, then there exists a
n\in[k,l]
such that

\displaystyle M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}M(n,\xi)(k-l)

and so

\displaystyle|M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\xi}{n^{2}}\tfrac{1}{1+\left(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\xi}{n})\right)^{2}}|k-l|.

Since
t\not\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{1+\left(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\xi}{n})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{1+\left(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\xi}{k})\right)^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{1+\left(d_{1}+d_{2}(t-\frac{\xi}{l})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{k})^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{l})^{2}}

and so we estimate

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\xi|l,\xi|}{tn^{2}}\left(\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{k})^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{l})^{2}}\right)|k-l|

\displaystyle\leq\left(t^{-1}+\tfrac{t}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{k})^{2}}+\tfrac{t}{1+(t-\frac{\xi}{l})^{2}}\right)|k-l|

\displaystyle\leq\left(t^{-1}+\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}+\tfrac{t}{\langle t\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\rangle}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(k,\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(l,\xi)}\right)\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{6}.

For
t\in[\frac{\xi}{l},\frac{\xi}{k}]
we wlog assume that
|l|\leq|k|
. Let
\frac{\xi}{l^{2}}\geq 1
, then by
\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle\leq\langle\frac{\xi}{k}-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle
, we infer

\displaystyle 1\leq\tfrac{\xi}{l^{2}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}|k-l|^{2}.

If
\frac{\xi}{l^{2}}\leq 1
, then
1\leq\tfrac{2}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}
. As before by
|e^{|x|}-1|\leq|x|e^{|x|}

\displaystyle|M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)|\lesssim|\frac{\xi}{l}-\frac{\xi}{k}|\leq\tfrac{\xi}{l^{2}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}|k-l|^{2}.

Using these estimates, we infer

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\xi}{l^{2}}\tfrac{|l,\xi|}{t}\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}|k-l|\leq t\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle}|k-l|

\langle t-\frac{\xi}{l}\rangle\geq\mu^{-1}

\displaystyle\frac{|l,\xi|}{t}|M(k,\xi)-M(l,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(t\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}+(t+\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}})|\ln(\mu)|^{1+r}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(k,\eta)}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}m_{0}}{m_{0}}(l,\xi)}\right)\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{5}.

∎

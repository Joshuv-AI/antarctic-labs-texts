Automated segmentation and diagnosis of parotid tumors using a combined deep learning and radiomics model on T2-weighted MRI: a multi-vendor validation study
Authors: Qifan Ma, Jiliang Ren, Yaqiong Ge, Ying Yuan, Xiaofeng Tao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo develop and validate an automated diagnostic framework that combines deep learning and radiomics models for the segmentation and classification of benign and malignant parotid gland tumors on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).MethodsIn total, 493 patients with pathologically confirmed parotid tumors (396 benign and 97 malignant) were included. Patients were stratified by MRI scanner type into a training cohort (n = 288), an internal validation cohort (n = 123), and an external testing cohort (n = 82). An automated tumor segmentation model based on the nnU-NetV2 architecture was developed and evaluated using the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and Intersection over Union (IoU). Based on the automatically segmented regions, a radiomics-based classifier and a ResNet18-based deep learning model were independently constructed to differentiate malignant from benign tumors. A combined diagnostic model was further developed by integrating deep learning outputs, radiomics features, and clinical-radiological features. Model performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).ResultsThe automated segmentation model achieved a Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.93 and an Intersection over Union (IoU) of 0.88 in the training cohort, 0.91 and 0.84 in the validation cohort, and 0.84 and 0.76 in the testing cohort, respectively. The ResNet18-based DL model achieved AUCs of 0.90, 0.84, and 0.77, respectively, compared to the radiomics model’s AUCs of 0.79, 0.72, and 0.71. The combined model demonstrated superior performance, with AUCs of 0.92 in the validation cohort and 0.90 in the testing cohort, outperforming the clinical-radiological model, which achieved AUCs of 0.69 and 0.82 (p < 0.001 in validation, p = 0.042 in testing).ConclusionsThis automated MRI-based framework, combining deep learning and radiomics approaches, enables accurate segmentation and reliable classification of parotid gland tumors. It offers a promising noninvasive tool to assist in clinical decision-making.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-025-00982-x.Keywords: Deep learning, Magnetic resonance imaging, Parotid gland tumors, Segmentation, Diagnosis

Introduction

Parotid gland tumors (PTs) represent 2–6% of head and neck tumors [1–3], and approximately 15–35% of them are malignant [4]. The management and outcomes of benign (BPTs) and malignant parotid tumors (MPTs) markedly differ. While BPTs can often be treated with local parotidectomy alone, MPTs typically require radical resection combined with adjuvant radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy, and are frequently associated with adverse features such as perineural invasion and distant metastasis [5, 6]. Therefore, accurate preoperative differentiation between benign and malignant parotid tumors is crucial for clinical decision-making.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), with its non-invasive nature and high resolution, has become a key tool in the preoperative evaluation of parotid gland tumors [7]. T2-weighted MRI is the clinical standard for parotid imaging, providing excellent gland-tumor contrast [8, 9]. However, traditional MRI diagnosis is highly dependent on the radiologist’s expertise, and challenges remain in tumor boundary delineation and distinguishing between benign and malignant lesions [10, 11].

Recent advances in deep learning (DL) techniques, particularly models like ResNet and Transformer, have improved tumor classification accuracy based on manually segmented regions of interest (ROI) [12, 13]. However, manual segmentation is time-consuming, subjective, and prone to intra-observer variability [14], making it unsuitable for large-scale clinical applications. To address these challenges, U-Net and its variants have been developed for automatic segmentation [15–18]. The integration of automated segmentation with classification models offers greater clinical practicality [19, 20]. Furthermore, integrating deep learning with radiomics enables more comprehensive analysis of imaging data, improving model stability and interpretability [21, 22].

Despite these advancements, few studies have developed comprehensive deep learning radiomics models for both segmentation and classification of parotid tumors, especially using large-scale MRI datasets. This study addresses this gap by developing an automated DL-based tool that combines a two-step deep learning radiomics algorithm for PTs detection and classification in 3D-MR images. Our approach could significantly improve diagnostic efficiency, assist in clinical decision-making, and enhance patient outcomes.

Methods

Patient cohort

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital and informed consent was waived due to retrospective study design. Between March 2018 and February 2023, parotid tumors cases in which pathological confirmation and preoperative conventional MRI were performed were enrolled. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients have complete imaging and clinical data; (2) patients who underwent standard MRI within 30 days before surgery; and (3) patients with pathologically diagnosed BPTs or MPTs after surgery. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) obvious artifacts on MR images; and (2) lesions with a minimum diameter of < 10 mm. In total, we retrospectively enrolled 493 patients, including 396 patients with BPTs and 97 patients with MPTs (see Supplementary Table S1 for histopathological types and numbers). Images from Philips MR scanners were designated as Dataset 1 (n = 411), while images from other manufacturers, including GE, United Imaging, and Siemens, were categorized as Dataset 2 (n = 82). Dataset 1 was randomly divided into training and validation cohorts in a 7:3 ratio, with 288 cases in the training cohort and 123 cases in the validation cohort. Dataset 2 served as the testing cohort. The overall research flowchart is shown in Fig. 1.

Image acquisition and tumor annotation

All MRI scans in this study were acquired at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital using five scanners from four manufacturers (Siemens, GE Healthcare, Philips, and United Imaging). Fat-saturated T₂-weighted imaging (Fs-T2WI, using Dixon or spectral FatSat technique) was used for the MRI examinations. Dataset 1 was acquired using the Philips Ingenia (or Ingenia CX) 3.0T MR scanner with a 16-channel head-and-neck array coil (Philips Medical Systems). For Dataset 2, the MRI vendor, magnetic field strength, and sequence parameters varied. Details of the imaging protocols for all patients are provided in Table 1.

MRI parameters of patients in both datasets

Manual tumor segmentation was performed using ITK-SNAP software (www.itk-snap.org). Two radiologists, with 3 and 10 years of experience in interpreting maxillofacial imaging, delineated the lesions. The junior radiologist initially outlined the volume of interest (VOIs) to encompass the entire tumor, while the senior radiologist reviewed and confirmed the VOI settings, resolving any disagreements. Both radiologists were blinded to the clinical details and pathological results.

Evaluation of baseline clinical-radiological characteristics

We systematically analyzed baseline clinical and radiological characteristics of the patient cohorts. The clinical evaluation included gender and age, while radiological assessment focused on MRI features such as tumor number, shape, and capsule, as interpreted by radiologists. These features were commonly evaluated in existing studies on parotid gland tumors, with potential diagnostic utility for distinguishing MPTs from BPTs [22–24]. Detailed descriptions of the radiological characteristics are provided in Fig. 2. Univariate analysis was performed to identify significant clinical-radiological features for constructing the clinical-radiological model (hereinafter referred to as the clinical model).

Detailed descriptions and examples of the radiological characteristics. Multiple lesions were defined as the presence of two or more tumor foci in either unilateral or bilateral parotid glands. Shape was categorized as regular (including round or ovoid lesions) or irregular. Capsule presence was identified by a distinct circumferential hypointense rim on Fs-T2WI images, where the linear low-signal intensity surrounding the tumor was considered indicative of a capsule

Development of deep learning model for automated segmentation

We employed the nnU-NetV2 network with deep supervision and extensive data augmentation to develop the segmentation model. nnU-NetV2, an updated version of the original nnU-Net architecture, was specifically designed for medical image segmentation [25]. The framework was implemented using PyTorch (version 2.2.1, https://pytorch.org/) and Python (version 3.9.18) to train an automatic model for volume of interest (VOI) segmentation. Fat-suppressed T2-weighted images from the training cohort, which were manually delineated, were used for training the nnU-NetV2 network. The model was trained from scratch without using pre-trained weights to better tailor it to the dataset. A rigorous five-fold cross-validation was performed on the training and validation sets to ensure reliable results. To address class imbalance and enhance segmentation accuracy, a combination of Dice Loss and Cross-Entropy Loss was used. Training was carried out on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 GPUs using the PyTorch framework. Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) was applied to optimize network performance. The hyperparameters for training, including initial learning rate, weight decay, batch size, and epochs, were set to 1e-2, 3e-5, 4, and 800, respectively. Larger DSC and IoU values indicate more accurate segmentation results.

Development of deep learning and radiomics models for classification

Before being input into the classification models, the images underwent several preprocessing steps to ensure compatibility with the input requirements of the ResNet18 model. The preprocessing pipeline included z-score normalization to standardize pixel intensities, N4 bias field correction, resampling of voxel size to 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ to achieve isotropic resolution, and data augmentation techniques, such as random cropping and flipping, to enhance model robustness. Additionally, the images were converted into tensor format to meet the deep learning framework’s input specifications. Finally, all images were resized to a uniform spatial dimension of 128 × 128 × 32, ensuring they conformed to the architectural constraints of the ResNet18 model while preserving critical anatomical features.

Segmentations generated by nnU-NetV2 were used as inputs for a 3D ResNet18 model within the MONAI framework (https://github.com/Project-MONAI), to classify multi-channel images (original images and segmentations) of BPTs and MPTs. The model was implemented using PyTorch (version 2.2.1) on Python (version 3.9.18). Model parameters were optimized using the Adam algorithm with an initial learning rate of 1 × 10⁻³. To address class imbalance in the training data, a class weighting strategy based on sample frequency was applied to the cross-entropy loss function. Specifically, class weights were calculated using the compute_class_weight function from scikit-learn in balanced mode, where the weight of each class is proportional to the reciprocal of its sample count. This strategy effectively mitigated the negative impact of class imbalance on classification performance, particularly improving the ability of the model to predict rare categories.

For radiomics analysis, a total of 1132 radiomics features were extracted from PTs images through pyradiomics (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/). Optimal features were initially selected using univariate analysis (p < 0.05). To eliminate redundancy while retaining features most correlated with the labels, the mRMR (Max-Relevance and Min-Redundancy) method was applied, retaining 20 features. The feature set was further refined using recursive feature elimination (RFE) with random forest and 5-fold cross-validation. A radiomics model was developed using logistic regression, and a radiomic score was calculated for each patient.

Development of a combined diagnostic model

For the final combined diagnostic model, clinical-radiological risk factors (manually assessed by clinicians) were integrated with automatically extracted deep learning and radiomics features using logistic regression to develop a comprehensive diagnostic system. A nomogram was constructed to visually represent the predictive outcomes.

The model’s predictive performance was comprehensively evaluated using ROC curves, decision curves, the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and calibration plots, which assessed its accuracy, clinical utility, calibration, and overall performance. Additionally, the DeLong test was applied to compare the performance of the DL model, radiomics model, clinical model, and combined model.

Statistical analysis

R-4.2.2 (https://www.r-project.org/) and Python (version 3.9.18) were used to complete statistical analysis. Predictive performance was quantified by calculating the area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV). Independent sample t test or Mann-Whitney U test were used for comparison of quantitative data. Statistical data were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Evaluation of the combined DL–radiomics–clinical model

The clinical model, developed based on capsule characteristics, achieved AUCs of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.67–0.82) in the training cohort, 0.69 (95% CI: 0.57–0.82) in the validation cohort, and 0.82 (95% CI: 0.63–1.00) in the testing cohort, as shown in Table 4.

By integrating capsule feature, the radiomics score, and the DL score, a combined model was constructed, which yielded the highest AUCs of 0.96 (95% CI: 0.93–1.00), 0.92 (95% CI: 0.84–0.99), and 0.90 (95% CI: 0.74–1.00) in the training, validation, and testing cohorts, respectively (Table 4; Fig. 5a–c). The DeLong test indicated that the combined model significantly outperformed the clinical model across all cohorts, with p-values of 0.000, 0.000, and 0.042, respectively. Furthermore, the combined model also demonstrated statistically significant improvements over both the DL model (p = 0.000 in the training cohort; p = 0.015 in the validation cohort) and the radiomics model (p = 0.000 in the training cohort; p = 0.000 in the validation cohort) (Fig. 5d–f).

A nomogram was constructed based on the combined model’s predictions, enabling individualized estimation of the likelihood of benign versus malignant parotid gland tumors by summing the contributions of each independent predictor (Fig. 6a). DCA demonstrated that the combined model provided greater net clinical benefit compared to the individual clinical, radiomics, and DL models across the training, validation, and testing cohorts (Fig. 6b–d). Calibration was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, with p-values > 0.05 in all cohorts, indicating good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes. Discussion

In this study, we developed a fully automated framework for the detection and classification of parotid tumors using a two-step deep learning approach. The model demonstrated high accuracy in distinguishing between benign and malignant PTs, with robust performance across both validation and external testing cohorts. Notably, it also effectively identified previously unlabeled tumors, underscoring its potential utility in automated data segmentation. Building on the results of the automated segmentation, the integrated diagnostic model offers valuable clinical support in identifying patients who may require more aggressive surgical intervention, serving as a promising auxiliary tool for clinical decision-making.

Artificial intelligence tools hold significant promise in aiding the detection of MPTs, particularly in smaller medical centers equipped with routine MR imaging. Our framework leverages fat-suppressed T2-weighted imaging, which provides clear delineation of tumor boundaries, enabling accurate segmentation and subsequent malignancy classification.

MRI data from our institution were utilized for training the auto-segmentation model, followed by internal validation and external testing. Notably, the external testing dataset presented considerable heterogeneity in MRI acquisition parameters, including variations in MRI vendors, magnetic field strengths, and slice thicknesses ≥ 4 mm. Despite these challenges, the nnU-NetV2 model—an advanced and adaptable segmentation framework—demonstrated excellent performance, achieving DSCs exceeding 0.9 in the majority of cases. These results align with previous studies validating the robustness of the nnU-NetV2 model across diverse clinical scenarios [15, 19, 26]. Compared with prior research utilizing deep learning models for parotid MRI segmentation, our study involved a larger cohort and employed 3D segmentation, enhancing model generalizability [15, 27].

Segmentation inconsistencies were noted in a subset of cases, particularly in multifocal tumors or those with heterogeneous signal patterns. Certain Warthin tumors exhibit regions with signal intensity similar to adjacent parotid tissue, leading to occasional under-segmentation; in addition, manual annotations typically include only the primary lesion, whereas the DL model detects multiple foci, resulting in modest DSC reductions. These discrepancies primarily reflect differences in annotation strategies rather than true model errors.

Our classification model was trained using automatically segmented tumor regions, offering distinct advantages over previous approaches. For example, Liu et al. developed an end-to-end deep learning model to differentiate between benign and malignant parotid neoplasms, achieving an accuracy of 0.85 [28]. In contrast, our study proposed a comprehensive diagnostic framework that integrates deep learning with capsule and radiomic features, underpinned by automated tumor segmentation to improve both accuracy and interpretability. The application of nnU-NetV2 enabled precise tumor segmentation, providing accurate localization and boundary delineation—crucial factors for enhancing classification performance. Additionally, by removing background and non-tumor tissues, segmentation helped eliminate irrelevant noise, thereby improving the robustness of the classification model. Importantly, the interpretability of the model was further enhanced, as the segmentation results offered a visual reference that clinicians could use to validate and better understand the diagnostic outputs, ultimately increasing clinical confidence in the model’s predictions.

The DL classifier achieved an external-testing AUC of 0.77, demonstrating its generalizability. Among radiomic features, shape-related metrics (e.g., Sphericity and maximum diameters) were significant predictors, as malignant tumors typically deviate from spherical morphology. Combining radiomics with deep learning further improved classification performance, and integrating clinical-radiological features yielded the highest diagnostic accuracy, with an external AUC of 0.90.

Among the clinical-radiological features, tumor capsule characteristics were identified as a significant factor. Malignant tumors are more likely to exhibit an absent or incomplete distinct low-intensity capsule on T2-weighted images. However, relying solely on capsule feature was insufficient to achieve an AUC greater than 0.85 for predicting tumor benignity or malignancy. To improve prediction accuracy, we integrated clinical-radiological risk factors with deep learning and radiomics models using logistic regression, resulting in a combined diagnostic model that achieved the highest AUC of 0.90 in external testing. The combined model outperformed the clinical model and demonstrated excellent clinical diagnostic utility.

This automated framework can be integrated into the routine MRI interpretation workflow. Following image acquisition, the system automatically performs tumor segmentation and generates the corresponding region of interest, which serves as the basis for subsequent image assessment. Radiologists can then conduct manual capsule integrity evaluation using the automated segmentation as guidance. Thereafter, the system synthesizes clinical information, radiomics features, and deep learning representations to produce a malignancy probability score, functioning as a second-reader tool. Through this sequential process, the automated outputs and manual assessments act as complementary components, offering objective and reproducible support for preoperative risk stratification, surgical planning (particularly in estimating the likelihood of malignancy), and overall diagnostic decision-making.

Our study has several limitations. First, the external validation dataset was limited in size. Although we included data from different imaging protocols, images from other centers were not incorporated. The external test set also included a small number of MPT cases, which resulted in wide confidence intervals for the AUC and sensitivity. This small sample size may affect the precision and generalizability of the results. Future studies should include data from multiple centers and larger datasets to further validate the model’s performance and improve the reliability of the reported estimates. Second, the retrospective nature of the study introduces potential selection bias, as patients without MRI examinations or surgical interventions were excluded. Third, our study focused solely on the differentiation between benign and malignant parotid tumors without further subtyping, which represents an important direction for future research.

Our study demonstrates the potential of a deep learning radiomics model trained on fat-suppressed T2-weighted imaging to achieve accurate auto-segmentation and classification of parotid tumors. By integrating clinically relevant imaging features, the model offers enhanced diagnostic accuracy and robustness, with the potential to serve as a valuable adjunct to clinical decision-making. Future research should focus on expanding the validation cohort, incorporating additional imaging modalities, and exploring the utility of the model in real-world clinical workflows to maximize its translational impact.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that the proposed deep learning model, in conjunction with clinically relevant radiomic features, holds significant promise for the automatic segmentation and classification of parotid tumors. This model offers a robust framework that enhances diagnostic accuracy and supports clinical decision-making.

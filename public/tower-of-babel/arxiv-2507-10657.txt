A Black Hole Airy Tail Preprint: APS/123-QED
Stefano Antonini, Luca V. Iliesiu, Pratik Rath, Patrick Tran

Abstract
In Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity, which is dual to a random matrix ensemble, the annealed entropy differs from the quenched entropy at low temperatures and goes negative. However, computing the quenched entropy in JT gravity requires a replica limit that is poorly understood. To circumvent this, we define an intermediate quantity called the semi-quenched entropy, which has the positivity properties of the quenched entropy, while requiring a much simpler replica trick. We compute this in JT gravity in different regimes using i) a bulk calculation involving wormholes corresponding to the Airy limit of the dual matrix integral and ii) a boundary calculation involving one-eigenvalue instanton saddles proposed by Hernández-Cuenca, demonstrating consistency between these two calculations in their common regime of validity. We also clarify why similar one-eigenvalue instanton saddles cannot be used to compute the quenched entropy due to a breakdown of the saddle-point approximation for the one-eigenvalue instanton in the replica limit.

Introduction

In recent years, JT gravity has emerged as a tool that has significantly enhanced our understanding of quantum gravity. As a two-dimensional toy model of quantum gravity, it is computationally tractable while still exhibiting interesting non-perturbative effects.
The action of this theory is given by

In the absence of matter fields, the only observable in pure JT gravity is the energy spectrum. The obvious quantity of interest is thus the thermal entropy, obtained from the thermal partition function
Z\left(\beta\right)
. However, in an ensemble of theories where one only computes averaged quantities, one has to be careful about which entropy is being computed, since the average can be taken in different ways. Conventionally, the quantities discussed in the literature are the annealed entropy and the quenched entropy. The annealed entropy
S_{A}\left(\beta\right)\equiv\left(1-\beta\partial_{\beta}\right)\log\langle Z\left(\beta\right)\rangle
differs from the quenched entropy,
S_{Q}\left(\beta\right)\equiv\left(1-\beta\partial_{\beta}\right)\langle\log Z\left(\beta\right)\rangle
, which is truly the average entropy of the ensemble. An important distinction between these entropies is that the quenched entropy is positive, while the annealed entropy can be negative.

In gravitational theories, the annealed entropy is much easier to compute. When the entropy is self-averaging,
S_{A}=S_{Q}
. However, in JT gravity,
S_{A}
fails to be self-averaging at sufficiently low temperatures. The consequent mismatch between the two entropies was analyzed in
Engelhardt et al. 2021
, where they found
S_{A}<0
at
\beta=O(e^{2S_{0}/3})

Stanford and Witten 2017
. The negativity of the annealed entropy is not unique to the JT toy model; rather, it persists in higher-dimensional theories for all near-extremal black holes that are not supersymmetric at extremality
Iliesiu and Turiaci 2021
;
Heydeman et al. 2022
;
Boruch et al. 2022
;
Iliesiu et al. 2025
. From the gravity point of view, this low temperature regime is thus among the most promising to probe the physics of only a small number of black hole microstates, specifically, the ones lying close to the groundstate. It is thus interesting to understand the difference between these entropies in this regime.

However, calculating the quenched entropy is not easy. To compute the quenched entropy, one uses the so-called no-replica trick:
\langle\log Z(\beta)\rangle=\partial_{m}\langle Z(\beta)^{m}\rangle\evaluated{}_{m=0}

in gravitational theories. Recently,

in Eq. (

\beta

To ameliorate the situation, we are motivated to study other entropic quantities in this regime. We consider various ensemble-averaged Rényi entropies for the thermal density matrix

In Sec.

II
A zoo of entropies

In studying entanglement and entropy in ensembles of quantum theories, such as disordered systems, gravitational theories, or random tensor networks, it is essential to distinguish among several natural generalizations of entropy to ensemble-averaged settings. The
n
-th Rényi entropy for a given unnormalized density matrix
\rho
is

S^{(n)}(\rho)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\rho^{n})}{\operatorname{tr}^{n}\left(\rho\right)}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{Z_{1}^{n}}\right).

(4)

The different generalizations of the Rényi entropy differ in their ordering of operations: taking logarithms, powers, and ensemble averages. Different choices lead to different quantities, with distinct physical interpretations and computational challenges. Conventionally, the two quantities discussed in the literature are the quenched and annealed entropies. Here, we discuss a larger class of quantities, explaining how each is constructed using a replica trick.
These quantities are
compared in Table
1
.

Annealed Entropy.

We begin with the simplest quantity, the
annealed Rényi entropy
, defined by

S^{(n)}_{A}\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{\langle Z_{n}\rangle}{\langle Z_{1}\rangle^{n}},

(5)

which treats the partition functions in a fully averaged manner. This quantity is the simplest to compute using the gravitational path integral since, at integer
n
, it does not require any analytic continuation.

However,
S_{A}^{(n)}
is not necessarily positive like an entropy. This is because it computes average moments of the matrix
\tilde{\rho}=\frac{\rho}{\langle\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle}
, as seen by rewriting Eq. (
5
) as

S^{(n)}_{A}=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\langle\operatorname{tr}\left(\tilde{\rho}^{n}\right)\rangle.

(6)

Since
\tilde{\rho}
is only normalized on average, the Rényi entropy can be negative (e.g.,
\tilde{\rho}
can have support on eigenvalues larger than 1).

Taking the
n\to 1
limit, one obtains the annealed entropy

\displaystyle S_{A}

\displaystyle=\log\langle\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle-\frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log\rho)\rangle}{\langle\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle}

(7a)

\displaystyle=\left(1-n\partial_{n}\right)\log\langle Z_{n}\rangle\evaluated{}_{n=1},

(7b)

where the last line explains the connection to the usual thermal annealed entropy. In the thermal case,
n
and
\beta
appear together in
Z_{n}=\operatorname{tr}e^{-n\beta H}
, and we have
n\partial_{n}f(n\beta)=\beta\partial_{\beta}f(n\beta)
.

Quenched Entropy.

The most physically well-motivated quantity is the
quenched Rényi entropy
defined as

S_{Q}^{(n)}\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\left\langle\log\frac{Z_{n}}{Z_{1}^{n}}\right\rangle,

(8)

This definition corresponds to computing the Rényi entropy in each member of the ensemble and then averaging. Thus, it retains the positivity of the Rényi entropy.

However, it is much harder to compute since it requires the following replica trick:

S_{Q}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{1-n}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to 0\\
k\to-n\end{subarray}}\left\langle\partial_{m}\left[\left(Z_{n}Z_{1}^{k}\right)^{m}\right]\right\rangle,

(9)

which can be computed at positive integer
m,\,n,\,k
using the gravitational path integral and requires analytic continuation for the two replica limits shown above. In the von Neumann limit (
n\to 1
), this gives

\displaystyle S_{Q}

\displaystyle=\langle\log\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log\rho)}{\operatorname{tr}\rho}\right\rangle

(10a)

\displaystyle=\left(1-n\partial_{n}\right)\langle\log Z_{n}\rangle\evaluated{}_{n=1},

(10b)

which again agrees with the standard definition of the thermal quenched entropy.

Semi-Quenched Entropy.

Since the annealed entropy goes negative and the quenched entropy is hard to compute, we now construct an intermediate quantity that is positive and, nevertheless, easier to compute since it does not require a replica limit. We define the
semi-quenched Rényi entropy
as

S_{SQ}^{(n)}\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{\langle Z_{n}\rangle}{\langle Z_{1}^{n}\rangle}.

(11)

This quantity does not require any analytic continuation to be computed at integer
n>1
. We will exploit this fact in our calculation using the gravitational path integral in Sec.
III.1
. The level of difficulty in analytically continuing this quantity in
n
is precisely the same as that of computing entanglement entropy in a quantum field theory given the integer Rényi entropies.

This quantity can be easily seen to be positive since
Z_{n}\leq Z_{1}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
for any member of the ensemble. Taking an ensemble average of this inequality results in the positivity of
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
. For
n<1
, the argument is similar except that the inequalities are reversed.

Finally, in the
n\to 1
limit, we find a new quantity:

S_{SQ}=\frac{\langle\operatorname{tr}\rho\log\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle-\langle\operatorname{tr}(\rho\log\rho)\rangle}{\langle\operatorname{tr}\rho\rangle},

(12)

which we call the semi-quenched entropy.
3
3

3

Another
possibility is to define a
quasi-quenched Rényi entropy
,

\displaystyle S_{QQ}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left\langle\frac{Z_{n}}{Z_{1}^{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\lim_{k\to-n}\left\langle Z_{n}Z_{1}^{k}\right\rangle.

This still requires a replica limit to compute, and is positive by a similar argument used above. In the
n\to 1
limit, it yields the quenched entropy. Since it is as difficult to compute as the quenched entropy, we will not focus on it in this paper.

III.1
Airy Wormholes at
\beta=O(e^{2S_{0}/3})

Consider JT gravity in the limit

\langle Z\left(\beta\right)^{m}\rangle=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}e^{(2-2g-m)S_{0}}Z_{g,m}\left(\beta\right),

Eigenvalue Instantons at

where

(22)

IV
Comments on Quenched Entropy

At
\beta\gtrsim e^{2S_{0}/3}
, the answer for the quenched entropy is also controlled by the Airy limit

;
Johnson 2021
. Due to the replica limits involved in this calculation, it is much harder to obtain directly but a matrix model analysis was done in
Janssen and Mirbabayi 2021
where they found that the low temperature entropy behaves like

is the Dyson index that characterizes the GOE, GUE and GSE ensembles

7

7

This behavior holds at arbitrarily low temperatures, up to doubly non-perturbative corrections

.
.
In particular, for GUE, the leading order term at large

The quenched entropy, obtained numerically by sampling 1000 GUE

) become important. One should have always been suspicious of this analysis since the leading answer comes from the continuum spectrum in this limit, whereas the assumption used in obtaining the instanton was that the leading eigenvalue dominates
Z\left(\beta\right)
. This is a consequence of taking the wrong order of limits between
\beta\to\infty
and
m\to 0

8
8

8

This is similar in spirit to issues arising for Rényi entropies for states where fluctuations in the geometry are large
Akers and Penington 2021
;
Dong et al. 2024
.
.

Compared to Sec.
III
, the key difference for the semi-quenched and annealed entropies is that they are dominated at large
\beta
by configurations with large gaps between the two leading eigenvalues, whereas the quenched entropy gets its leading (power-law) contribution from configurations with a small gap.

V
Discussion

We showed how wormholes in JT gravity restore positivity of
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
. This required a resummation of the genus expansion, and no single geometry was sufficient. This suggests that requiring positivity of
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
can put constraints on wormhole contributions in a putative gravitational theory: a wormhole bootstrap.

We probed a regime where all the entropies are non-self-averaging and thus are not necessarily representative of a typical member of the ensemble. Both
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
and
S_{Q}^{(n)}
in this regime are dominated by atypical members of the ensemble. This is most obvious for
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
from the one-eigenvalue instanton analysis, which is a highly atypical member of the ensemble with a large gap between the two smallest eigenvalues. Thus, our results do not carry forward in an obvious manner to the gravitational path integral in higher dimensions if it is not dual to an ensemble of theories.

Finally, we note that
S_{A}^{(n)}
is the simplest quantity, requiring only the average density of states. Our newly defined
S_{SQ}^{(n)}
is close behind since it only requires up to the
n
th moment of the density of states. On the other hand,
S_{Q}^{(n)}
requires arbitrarily many moments of the density of states. This is the key reason for the subtlety in performing the replica limit required.

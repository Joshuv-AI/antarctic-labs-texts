Effectiveness of multi-family group therapy for school refusal in adolescents with depressive disorders: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yulu Wang, Xuechun Wang, Yixin Xiong, Pengyu Zhan, Ruoxi Ding, Chuan Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to develop a manualized multi-family group therapy (MFGT) program for adolescents with depressive school refusal and to evaluate its effectiveness through a randomized controlled trial. By comparing MFGT with group cognitive behavioral therapy (GCBT), the study seeks to examine the relative efficacy of MFGT in improving school refusal behaviors and depressive symptoms, to assess the stability of treatment effects during follow-up, and to explore potential mechanisms underlying behavioral change.MethodsThis study is designed as a single-center, single-blind, randomized controlled non-inferiority trial. A total of 138 adolescents aged 12-19 years who meet DSM-5 criteria for depressive disorders and Berg’s criteria for school refusal will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the MFGT plus treatment-as-usual (TAU) group or the GCBT plus TAU group. Both interventions consist of eight group sessions delivered over four weeks. Primary outcomes include school attendance rate and clinician-rated depressive symptoms assessed using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale. Secondary and intermediate outcomes include self-reported depressive and anxiety symptoms, school refusal maintaining factors, school-related self-efficacy, family functioning, and parent–adolescent communication. Assessments will be conducted at baseline, post-treatment, and at 1- and 3-month follow-up.DiscussionThis study will be the first randomized controlled trial to evaluate a standardized and manualized MFGT protocol specifically developed for adolescents with depressive school refusal. By adopting a family-based and scalable intervention model, the study is expected to provide evidence for an effective and resource-efficient treatment approach that addresses both individual psychopathology and family dynamics. The findings may contribute to improving school attendance and mental health outcomes in adolescents and inform clinical practice and public mental health strategies in settings with limited mental health resources.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500100225). Registered, 2025.

Background

School refusal (SR) is a significant and increasingly recognized problem among children and adolescents. It is characterized by persistent difficulties in school attendance due to emotional distress [1]. Adolescents with SR may experience difficulties attending school, remaining in school for the full day, participating in school-related activities, or feeling emotionally safe in the school environment [2–4]. SR is not only a strong indicator of school disengagement, low academic achievement, increased risk of school dropout, and impaired social and emotional development [5, 6], but is also closely associated with mental health problems, particularly depression [5, 7]. A meta-analysis demonstrated a moderate to large positive association between depressive symptoms and school refusal or poor school attendance [8]. Conversely, persistent school refusal may further exacerbate depressive symptoms through academic failure [9], social withdrawal, and reduced daily structure [10], creating a self-reinforcing cycle between depression and school refusal.

SR is shaped by multiple interacting influences, including individual psychological vulnerabilities as well as school, social, and family factors. Previous research has shown that school refusal is associated with a range of adverse family conditions, such as parental unemployment, frequent residential moves, parental neglect, exposure to domestic violence, and parental mental health problems [11, 12]. At the same time, school refusal may further increase family vulnerability by intensifying parental stress, interfering with parents’ employment, and exacerbating existing family difficulties [13, 14]. As a result, interventions that focus only on the individual adolescent may be insufficient to achieve sustained improvement, whereas approaches that address family interaction patterns and strengthen overall family functioning are likely to be important for achieving long-term treatment outcomes.

Current evidence-based interventions for SR primarily rely on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) [15, 16]. While CBT has demonstrated effectiveness, individual CBT faces implementation barriers due to limited training opportunities, heavy caseloads, and staffing constraints in practice [17]. Systemic family therapy has also been shown to be effective. However, the traditional single-family format is constrained by the substantial gap between clinical demand and the availability of trained mental health professionals, particularly in China [18]. Multi-family group therapy (MFGT) represents a promising and scalable alternative. Evidence supports the effectiveness of MFGT in mental disorders such as anorexia nervosa [19], and emerging qualitative studies suggest that MFGT may also benefit adolescents with SR by reducing emotional distress and improving intra-family communication [20].

Despite these encouraging findings, research on school refusal remains largely correlational, with a notable lack of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating intervention efficacy. To date, no standardized and manualized MFGT protocol has been specifically developed for adolescents with depressive school refusal in clinical settings. This represents a critical gap in the existing literature. Therefore, the present study aims to address this gap by developing a manualized MFGT intervention tailored to adolescents with depressive SR and evaluating its effectiveness through a randomized controlled trial. Specifically, this study will compare the immediate and long-term efficacy of MFGT with group cognitive behavioral therapy (GCBT), identify key therapeutic factors, and explore potential mechanisms underlying behavioral change in this vulnerable population.

Methods

Study design

This study is designed as a single-center, single-blind, randomized controlled trial. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the experimental group (MFGT + TAU) or the control group (GCBT + TAU). The study flow chart is presented in Fig. 1. Trial registration: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500100225). Registered in 2025. Patient recruitment is currently ongoing and is expected to be completed by April 1, 2026. The study protocol has undergone peer review as part of the funding process.

A single-blind design will be adopted, in which outcome assessors will be blinded to group allocation. Given ethical considerations and evidence from previous studies, no inactive or placebo control group will be included.

Randomization will be performed using a computer-generated random number table. After providing written informed consent, participants will be allocated to one of the two study arms according to a predefined randomization sequence. The randomization plan will be reviewed and approved by an independent member of the biostatistics quality assurance team prior to implementation.

Exclusion criteria

Participants will be excluded if they meet any of the following conditions:

(1) A current or past diagnosis, according to DSM-5 criteria, of organic mental disorders, schizophrenia spectrum disorders, schizoaffective disorder, bipolar disorder, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, or other severe psychiatric conditions.

(2) Severe suicidal ideation.

(3) Presence of serious or unstable medical conditions.

(4) Currently receiving structured psychotherapy specifically targeting depressive disorders.

Discontinuation and dropout criteria

Participants will be withdrawn from the study if any of the following occur:

(1) Emergence of suicidal behavior or suicide attempts during the study period.

(2) Withdrawal of informed consent by the participant or legal guardian.

(3) Attendance of fewer than five out of eight group therapy sessions, which will be considered treatment dropout.

Written informed consent will be obtained from both adolescents and their legal guardians prior to study participation. Participation in the study is entirely voluntary, and participants may withdraw at any time during the intervention period without penalty if they experience discomfort or for any other reason.

Intervention

Treatment as usual

Treatment as usual (TAU) refers to pharmacological treatment. Participants will be required to be on a stable medication regimen prior to enrollment, and the prescribed pharmacological treatment was maintained without change throughout the intervention period, unless clinically necessary.

Multi-Family Group Therapy (MFGT)

Participants in the experimental group will receive MFGT and TAU. MFGT will be delivered by two psychiatrists or licensed psychotherapists who have received formal training in systemic family therapy and have at least two years of clinical experience in family-based interventions. Each treatment cycle will receive three sessions of professional supervision to ensure treatment fidelity.

The MFGT program consists of eight sessions delivered over four weeks, with two sessions per week, each lasting 120 min. Multiple families will participate together in each group. The intervention follows a semi-structured and manualized framework, with each session focusing on specific therapeutic themes and including experiential exercises, group discussions, and structured home assignments.

The thematic content of the eight sessions is outlined as follows:

Establishing group alliance and therapeutic framework (use of visual language, cross-family sharing, and home assignments).Externalization of school refusal and psychoeducation on depression (problem description, expert input, and home assignments).Enhancing motivation for change (group discussion, social network mapping, and home assignments).Identifying and mobilizing family resources (cross-family reporting, role-play, and home assignments).Commitment to change and future-oriented planning (role-play, family sculpting exercises, and home assignments).Emotional expression and communication skills (emotional expression, Nonviolent Communication, and home assignments).Coping with stress and challenges (stress management strategies and home assignments).Integration, reflection, and future planning (presentation of therapeutic gains and goal setting).

Group Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (GCBT)

Participants in the control group will receive GCBT and TAU. GCBT will be delivered by two psychiatrists or licensed psychotherapists who have completed standardized CBT training. Each treatment cycle will also receive three sessions of professional supervision. The GCBT program consists of eight structured sessions over four weeks, with two sessions per week, each lasting 120 min. The intervention follows a fixed and manualized structure.

The GCBT protocol is designed to enhance participants’ understanding and recognition of depressive symptoms and school refusal through core cognitive-behavioral techniques, including psychoeducation, self-monitoring, cognitive restructuring, behavioral activation and exposure strategies, and relaxation training. Structured home assignments will be provided at the end of each session and reviewed at the beginning of the subsequent session to promote self-monitoring skills and facilitate the generalization of therapeutic gains to daily life.

Statistical issue

Sample size estimation

This study is designed as a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial. Sample size estimation was performed based on previously published literature. The expected effective rate was assumed to be 0.767 in the experimental group and 0.636 in the control group. The type I error rate (α) was set at 0.05, and the type II error rate (β) at 0.20, corresponding to a statistical power of 80%. The allocation ratio between the two groups was 1:1.

Allowing for an anticipated 20% attrition rate, sample size calculation using G-Power software indicated that a minimum of 69 participants per group would be required. Therefore, a total sample size of 138 participants was planned.

Data management and analysis

Findings of the trial will be reported in accordance with the revised CONSORT guidelines [22]. The research team will establish a standardized clinical research data collection, quality control, and analysis workflow, along with a standardized electronic database. Data collection will be carried out by designated research staff. Clinician-rated scales will be assessed by independent evaluators, while self-report questionnaires will be completed by participants and/or their parents. All data will be double-entered and cross-checked by two independent staff members. Original source documents will be securely stored, and the electronic database will be managed and maintained by authorized members of the research team.

A standardized database will be established using SPSS software, and statistical analyses will be conducted with SPSS version 20.0. Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize baseline characteristics. For between-group comparisons, categorical variables (e.g., sex, school grade, treatment response rate, and remission rate) will be analyzed using the chi-square test. Continuous variables will be analyzed using repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) to evaluate the effects of treatment group, time, and group-by-time interactions on outcome measures. Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation under the missing-at-random assumption. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the robustness of the results. All statistical tests will be two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Data from participants who discontinue the intervention will be analyzed separately.

Trial management and monitoring

The study will be conducted under a principal investigator-led management structure, in accordance with the research management regulations of Peking University Sixth Hospital. Study implementation will strictly follow the approved protocol, with appropriate organizational support and oversight to ensure scientific rigor and appropriate use of research funding.

All group interventions will be delivered by psychiatrists or licensed psychotherapists who have received formal psychotherapy training. Each treatment cycle will receive three sessions of professional supervision to ensure treatment fidelity. Outcome assessors will receive standardized training in the use of screening tools and rating scales to ensure assessment consistency.

To ensure the quality and progress of the study, the principal investigator will convene regular research team meetings to monitor study implementation, identify emerging issues, and implement timely corrective actions as needed.

Ethical considerations

This study has been reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University Sixth Hospital prior to initiation and will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki [23]. All study procedures will strictly adhere to the requirements and regulations of the institutional ethics committee. Any important protocol modifications will be submitted to the Ethics Committee of Peking University Sixth Hospital for approval prior to implementation. Relevant changes will also be updated in the trial registry and communicated to investigators and participants as appropriate.

Given that the participants are minors, written informed consent will be obtained from both the adolescents and their legal guardians prior to enrollment. During the recruitment process, the study objectives, procedures, potential risks, and anticipated benefits will be clearly explained to participants and their guardians to ensure informed and voluntary participation.

All personal information and medical records collected in this study will be treated as confidential. Participant data will be anonymized and securely stored, with access restricted to authorized members of the research team only, to prevent unauthorized disclosure or misuse.

As the study involves adolescents with depressive disorders and school refusal, participants may experience emotional fluctuations during the intervention. To minimize potential risks, group therapists will receive regular professional supervision, and participants will be closely monitored throughout the study period. Appropriate clinical support and referrals will be provided if significant emotional distress or safety concerns arise.

The research team is committed to maintaining the objectivity and integrity of the study. All data analyses and reporting will be conducted transparently and without conflicts of interest, and the study findings will be disseminated responsibly to contribute to public mental health knowledge and practice.

Discussion

The multi-family group therapy (MFGT) program developed in this study is designed to address school refusal among adolescents with depressive disorders and to improve their overall mental health. In addition, this study aims to support families in developing healthier communication patterns, strengthening emotional connections among family members, and promoting family cohesion and stability. Improvements in adolescents’ academic engagement and social functioning may further contribute to broader educational outcomes at the societal level. Moreover, by improving mental health and addressing school refusal in adolescents with depression, this intervention may help reduce school dropout, which is consistent with national educational policies in China that emphasize preventing dropout and ensuring school attendance [24].

Multi-family group therapy allows multiple families to be treated simultaneously and therefore offers advantages in terms of time efficiency and resource utilization, making it a potentially cost-effective intervention. By focusing on school refusal among adolescents with depressive disorders, this study may provide additional intervention options for families, schools, and communities, helping to address the gap between clinical demand and the limited availability of trained mental health professionals in China. The proposed intervention has the potential to be implemented in a variety of settings, including schools, community mental health services, and hospitals, thereby reaching a broader population of adolescents. Furthermore, the treatment model may be adapted for delivery through online platforms, which could further expand its accessibility, coverage, and public health impact.

Weight-Based Pembrolizumab Dosing at 4 mg/kg Every 6 Weeks Leads to Exposures Below Approved Doses with Unestablished Efficacy: A Pharmacokinetic Model-Based Simulation Analysis
Authors: Mallika Lala, Aditya Bardia, Antonio Calles, Romano Danesi, Tomoko Freshwater, Jane A. Healy, Roy S. Herbst
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPembrolizumab is approved globally at a dose of 200 mg every 3 weeks (Q3W) or 400 mg every 6 weeks (Q6W) intravenously (IV). These dosing recommendations are based on established regimens that have demonstrated efficacy in clinical trials. Recently, pembrolizumab dosing at 4 mg/kg Q6W has been suggested as an alternative regimen; however, efficacy has not been evaluated in any prospective, controlled clinical trial.ObjectiveThe objective was to describe the pharmacokinetic model-based analyses of pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W IV compared with approved dosing regimens.Patients and MethodsThe pharmacokinetic profiles for pembrolizumab were simulated for 4 mg/kg Q6W, 400 mg Q6W, 200 mg Q3W, and 2 mg/kg Q3W IV in 3607 participants from clinical trials of pembrolizumab across tumor types and were based on an established pharmacokinetic model informed by extensive clinical data that supported the approved pembrolizumab IV doses. Pharmacokinetic exposure measures evaluated were trough concentration (Ctrough) and area under the curve (AUC) after initial dosing (first 6 weeks of treatment) and at steady state (weeks 13–18).ResultsPembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W IV resulted in Ctrough levels consistently below the lowest clinically evaluated thresholds associated with the approved fixed dose of 400 mg Q6W and weight-based dose of 2 mg/kg Q3W and well below those of 200 mg Q3W, which is the gold-standard dose with the most extensive pan tumor clinical data. In the overall population, the geometric mean steady state Ctrough for 4 mg/kg Q6W was 26% lower than 400 mg Q6W (9% fell below fifth percentile) and 53% lower than 200 mg Q3W (33% fell below fifth percentile), while geometric mean steady state AUC was ~ 25% lower than both fixed doses (18% of participants fell below fifth percentile). The reduction was more pronounced in participants weighing under 50 kg, with geometric mean steady state Ctrough 55% lower than 400 mg Q6W (17% of participants fell below fifth percentile) and 72% lower than 200 mg Q3W (54% of participants fell below fifth percentile), while the geometric mean steady state AUC was ~ 55% lower than both fixed doses (~ 50% of participants fell below fifth percentile). While AUC for 4 mg/kg Q6W was similar to that of 2 mg/kg Q3W, as expected with dose proportional pharmacokinetics, Ctrough, which is a relevant efficacy driver, does fall below even this lowest clinically evaluated pembrolizumab dose regardless of body weight: ~ 37% lower than 2 mg/kg Q3W (~ 20% of participants fell below fifth percentile). Similar trends were seen in participants weighing 50–80 kg. Results were consistent after initial dosing.ConclusionsPharmacokinetic modeling suggests pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W IV results in reduced exposures with an uncertain impact upon efficacy. Participants with lower body weight were increasingly impacted, with meaningful exposure reductions below the clinically established threshold doses of 400 mg Q6W, 200 mg Q3W, and 2 mg/kg Q3W IV. In the absence of robust clinical data on pembrolizumab efficacy at doses with exposures below those of the approved dosing regimens, weight-based dosing at 4 mg/kg Q6W poses a potential risk to treatment efficacy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11523-025-01185-x.

Key Points

Pharmacokinetic modeling suggests that the weight-based dosing of pembrolizumab at 4 mg/kg every 6 weeks may result in reduced exposures with uncertain efficacy and impact to treatment success, with more pronounced impact to patients of lower body weight.To ensure optimal efficacy and patient safety, pembrolizumab should be dosed in accordance with the label approved by global regulatory agencies and not at lower doses, adjusted by body weight or otherwise, that have not been rigorously evaluated for efficacy in adequately designed trials.

Introduction

Pembrolizumab is a humanized monoclonal immunoglobulin G4 antibody against the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor that has demonstrated antitumor activity and manageable safety in multiple tumor types [1]. Initially approved in melanoma and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) at a weight-based dose of 2 mg/kg administered intravenously (IV) every 3 weeks (Q3W), pembrolizumab was subsequently approved globally across indications at a fixed dose of 200 mg Q3W IV on the basis of extensive clinical data from phase 3 studies [2]. As such, 200 mg Q3W remains the key dosing regimen of pembrolizumab with most clinical experience (~ 30,000 participants across 22 tumor types and 124 Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC, a subsidiary of Merck & Co., Inc., Rahway, NJ, USA (MSD)-sponsored clinical trials) and is regarded as the gold-standard reference regimen for pembrolizumab (Supplementary Table 1). The 2 mg/kg Q3W remains the lowest evaluated pembrolizumab dosing regimen, approved for a brief period of up to 2 years with relatively leaner clinical experience (~1600 participants in three tumor types across 12 MSD-sponsored clinical trials; Supplementary Table 1) and has demonstrated efficacy in registrational trials limited to melanoma and NSCLC [3, 4]. In addition, considering non-MSD sponsored trials, approximately 115 phase 2 or 3 studies in over 56,000 participants have evaluated the fixed dose regimens of 200 mg Q3W or 400 mg every 6 weeks (Q6W), while only three studies in approximately 650 participants have evaluated the 2 mg/kg Q3W regimen (Supplementary Table 2). The transition from weight-based to fixed dosing was supported by pharmacokinetic and exposure–response modeling showing that exposures at the 200 mg Q3W dose are contained within the range of clinical experience with doses from 2 mg/kg Q3W to 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks where consistent efficacy and manageable safety profiles, i.e., flat exposure–response relationships, have been established [2]. The 200 mg Q3W regimen, which has an upward shift (~ 25%) in exposures compared with 2 mg/kg Q3W, was selected as the optimal fixed dose to ensure all individual patient exposures, regardless of body weight, fall well within the flat exposure–response range observed in prior clinical experience from 2 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg [2]. There are a number of benefits associated with fixed dosing, including reduced dosing errors resulting in improved safety, convenience, simplified dosing, and elimination of the risk of viral transmission via shared vials [2]. Fixed dosing is also particularly relevant to monoclonal antibodies, as their volume of distribution corresponds to the volume of blood plasma and extracellular fluids, which does not vary proportionally with body weight and can therefore result in decreased interpatient variability compared with weight-based dosing [5].

Extending the dosing interval for intravenously administered therapies is also associated with a number of advantages, including providing greater flexibility and convenience for patients and reducing administrative burden on healthcare providers [6]. Pharmacokinetic modeling and exposure–response analyses subsequently led to the approval of a fixed dosing regimen of 400 mg Q6W IV [6, 7], supported by clinical studies in melanoma and hematologic malignancies which confirmed that the benefit–risk profile of pembrolizumab at 400 mg Q6W IV was consistent with that of 200 mg Q3W IV [8, 9]. Subsequently, an extensive number of completed and ongoing clinical studies in different treatment settings have evaluated pembrolizumab at a dose of 400 mg Q6W, substantiating the efficacy and safety of this dosing regimen (~ 9600 participants in 12 tumor types across 24 MSD-sponsored clinical trials, as well as ~ 2600 participants in 6 tumor types across 6 MSD-sponsored clinical trials who received initial 200 mg Q3W dosing then were switched over to 400 mg Q6W; Supplementary Table 1). Currently, there is no evidence from adequately powered randomized controlled clinical trials for the efficacy of pembrolizumab to meet the required scientific and regulatory rigor at doses with pharmacokinetic exposures lower than those of the approved dosing regimens.

The use of pembrolizumab at a weight-based dose of 4 mg/kg Q6W has been recently documented in literature [10–12]. However, no pharmacokinetic or robust efficacy data supporting this dose have been presented. Notably, 4 mg/kg Q6W has not been evaluated in any prospective, adequately powered, controlled clinical trial. Here, we describe for the first time a model-based pharmacokinetic analysis of pembrolizumab administered as an IV infusion at 4 mg/kg Q6W compared with the approved fixed-dose regimens of 400 mg Q6W and 200 mg Q3W as well as the weight-based regimen of 2 mg/kg Q3W.

Methods

Pharmacokinetic profiles were simulated for pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W, 400 mg Q6W, 200 mg Q3W IV, and 2 mg/kg Q3W using the previously established population pharmacokinetic model for pembrolizumab [1]. This model supports the pembrolizumab label and was based on the reference data set of 2993 participants with melanoma or NSCLC from five clinical trials (KEYNOTE-001 [NCT01295827], KEYNOTE-002 [NCT01704287], KEYNOTE-006 [NCT01866319], KEYNOTE-010 [NCT01905657], and KEYNOTE-024 [NCT02142738]; Supplementary Methods). The reference dataset was augmented for the current analysis with data from an additional 614 participants with melanoma, NSCLC, or renal cell carcinoma (RCC) from KEYNOTE-555 cohort B (NCT03665597), MK-3475A-D77 (NCT05722015), and MK-3475A-C18 (NCT05017012). Parametric simulations of concentration–time profiles were performed for each dosing regimen (dataset, N = 3607). The pharmacokinetic exposure measures estimated were the trough concentration (Ctrough) and area under the curve (AUC) over 6 weeks, both after initial dosing (first 6 weeks of treatment) and at steady state (weeks 13–18). The first 6-week time point was selected to allow for completion of at least one treatment cycle for participants on the 6-week dosing schedule. Weeks 13–18 were selected, during which pembrolizumab pharmacokinetic steady state is reached, which are the exposure levels then maintained throughout treatment duration and are most relevant for dosing regimen comparisons.

Individual Ctrough was calculated based on the simulated concentration values at the end of the dosing interval (day 42 for initial dosing; day 126 for steady state). Individual AUC0–6 weeks were calculated using the standard trapezoidal rule by integrating the total drug exposure over 42 days. The expected distribution of individual pharmacokinetic exposures was characterized on the basis of the range of patients from the overall data set. The geometric means and 95% confidence intervals for each dosing regimen for both the overall population and three body weight categories (< 50 kg, 50–80 kg, > 80 kg) were summarized. The percentage difference in exposures between 4 mg/kg Q6W and the reference IV regimens was based on the geometric means within each body weight group. The percentages of participants with individual Ctrough and AUC falling below fifth and first percentile values of the reference doses within each body weight group and fifth percentile values of the overall population were calculated.

Results

Participants

Data from 3607 participants with melanoma, NSCLC, and RCC were included in the model-based simulations. The median baseline body weight of participants was 74.0 kg (range 35.7–210.0); 169 participants (5%) weighed < 50 kg, 2109 participants (58%) weighed between 50 and 80 kg, and 1329 participants (37%) weighed ≥ 80 kg.

Ctrough

At steady state in all patients, the geometric mean Ctrough for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be 26% lower than that of the 400 mg Q6W dosing regimen, with 9% of participants having Ctrough falling below the fifth percentile; 37% lower than that of the 2 mg/kg Q3W dosing regimen, with 22% of participants having Ctrough falling below the fifth percentile; and 53% lower than that of the 200 mg Q3W dosing regimen, with 33% of participants having Ctrough falling below the fifth percentile (Fig. 1A). In participants who weighed < 50 kg, the geometric mean steady state Ctrough for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be 55% lower than that of the 400 mg Q6W dosing regimen, with 17% of participants falling below the fifth percentile; 38% lower than that of the 2 mg/kg Q3W dosing regimen, with 20% of participants falling below the fifth percentile; and 72% lower than that of the 200 mg Q3W dosing regimen, with 54% of participants falling below the fifth percentile (Tables 1, 2; Figs. 1B, 2). For participants who weighed 50–80 kg, the geometric mean steady state Ctrough for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be 34% lower than that of the 400 mg Q6W dosing regimen, with 10% of participants falling below the fifth percentile; 37% lower than that of the 2 mg/kg Q3W regimen, with 22% of participants falling below the fifth percentile and 58% lower than that of the 200 mg Q3W regimen, with 38% of participants falling below the fifth percentile (Tables 1, 2; Figs. 1B, 2). AUC

In all patients, the geometric mean AUC at steady state for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be ~25% lower than that of both 400 mg Q6W and 200 mg Q3W, given the similar steady state AUC exposure between both fixed dose regimens, with 18% of participants having AUC exposures falling below the fifth percentile for both doses (Fig. 3A). In participants who weighed < 50 kg, the geometric mean AUC at steady state for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be ~ 55% lower than that of both fixed dosing regimens, with approximately 50% of participants falling below the fifth percentile for both doses and 31% of participants falling below the first percentile for both doses (Tables 1, 2; Figs. 3B, 4). For participants who weighed 50–80 kg, the geometric mean AUC at steady state for 4 mg/kg Q6W was predicted to be 34% lower than that of both the fixed dosing regimens with 25% of participants falling below the fifth percentile for both doses. (Tables 1, 2; Figs. 3B, 4). Consistent trends of AUC for 4 mg/kg Q6W relative to 400 mg Q6W and 200 mg Q3W dosing regimens were found after initial dosing (Supplementary Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 4). Discussion

Both Ctrough and AUC are considered pharmacokinetic exposure drivers of pembrolizumab efficacy, and reductions in either of these exposure measures below clinical threshold values defined based on adequate clinical experience can compromise efficacy. In selection of alternative dosing regimens, it is important to match both of these exposure measures to reference regimens to maintain efficacy, as is emphasized in the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Guidance for Industry on Pharmacokinetic-Based Criteria for Supporting Alternative Dosing Regimens of anti-PD1 treatments [13].

Regulatory approval for pembrolizumab 400 mg Q6W IV was initially based on a pharmacokinetic bridging study that showed AUC exposures for 400 mg Q6W were similar to pembrolizumab 200 mg Q3W (i.e., the gold-standard regimen with maximum pan tumor clinical experience), while Ctrough was generally comparable to those of 2 mg/kg Q3W (i.e., lowest evaluated dose with clinical experience mainly in melanoma and NSCLC) [6]. Modeling and simulation analyses showed that < 1% of patients would be expected to have steady-state Ctrough below the range of 200 mg Q3W or 2 mg/kg Q3W. Thus, 400 mg Q6W was also expected to have exposures across patient populations that were contained within the previously established flat exposure–response range for pembrolizumab from 2 to 10 mg/kg Q3W [14, 15]. On the basis of these findings, pembrolizumab at a dose of 400 mg Q6W was expected to maintain efficacy and safety similar to the 200 mg Q3W dose [6]. These model-based findings were supported and subsequently confirmed by prospective clinical studies [8, 9]. Notably, it was highlighted by health authorities during regulatory review of the applications that any expected reductions in exposures, even in small proportions of patients, relative to the clinical threshold doses could pose a risk to patients as the efficacy of such lower exposures (Ctrough in particular) remains questionable [13, 16]. Several completed and ongoing pan-tumor prospective clinical studies continue to build the clinical experience for the efficacy and safety of the 400 mg Q6W dosing regimen (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2).

In response to the initial modeling and simulation study showing that pembrolizumab 400 mg Q6W led to exposures generally consistent with previously approved IV regimens, a few commentaries suggested that 4 mg/kg Q6W may be appropriate as a cost-saving measure, and is part of ongoing weight-based dosing discussions that treatment guidelines are considering for inclusion [10, 11]. A single-center, retrospective analysis of 160 patients with advanced NSCLC who received pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W or 2 mg/kg Q3W in Canada reported comparable overall survival and progression-free survival and similar toxicity for the two doses [12]. However, differences in the baseline clinical characteristics between the populations may have skewed the results such as older age, higher median dose, higher proportion of participants with a PD-L1 tumor proportion score of ≥ 50%, and fewer participants with metastases in the 4 mg/kg Q6W group. Another retrospective cohort study of 1413 patients with NSCLC weighing < 100 kg who received pembrolizumab at a weight-based dose (2 mg/kg Q3W or 4 mg/kg Q6W) or a fixed-dose (200 mg Q3W or 400 mg Q6W) reported that weight-based dosing may be as appropriate as fixed-dose [17]. However, only a small number of participants received weight-based dosing (138 out of 1413 [9.8%]), and the efficacy data for 2 mg/kg Q3W and 4 mg/kg Q6W were pooled, preventing any conclusions being drawn specifically about outcomes at the 4 mg/kg Q6W dose. Available retrospective and single-center studies have major limitations, including selection bias, confounding, and pooling of different weight-based regimens. Importantly, these studies cannot substitute for rigorous regulatory standards of evidence and should not inform dosing practice. Notably, when weight-based dosing was switched to fixed dose regimens of 200 mg Q3W and 400 mg Q6W, doses were selected to maintain therapeutic exposures in the broadest demographic population across different geographic regions with known body weight differences. Several of the arguments put forward in the commentaries described above base their evaluations for pharmacokinetic exposure comparability on an average body weight of 70 kg or 75 kg, which undermines the potential for underdosing by use of weight-based doses in patients with a lower body weight and associated risk of compromising efficacy.

The present analysis demonstrates that exposures, both AUC and Ctrough, with pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W IV fall below the reference regimens of both approved fixed doses of 400 mg Q6W and 200 mg Q3W IV. These findings are particularly notable in participants with lower body weight, who were increasingly impacted with meaningful exposure reductions below the clinically established threshold doses. In addition, while the AUC of 4 mg/kg Q6W was similar to the initially approved weight-based dose of 2 mg/kg Q3W, as expected with dose proportional pharmacokinetics, the Ctrough of 4 mg/kg Q6W does consistently fall below the lowest clinically evaluated pembrolizumab regimen of 2 mg/kg Q3W as well, regardless of body weight. As stated above, Ctrough is considered a relevant driver of efficacy of immune-oncology monoclonal antibodies such as pembrolizumab. Thus, a 4 mg/kg Q6W regimen would also not meet the FDA-defined Pharmacokinetic-Based Criteria for Supporting Alternative Dosing Regimens of anti–PD-1 treatments, as described previously [13].

Overall, pharmacokinetic modeling suggests that weight-based dosing of pembrolizumab at 4 mg/kg Q6W IV results in lower exposures, specifically Ctrough that falls below the lower end of the established and referenced exposure–response range of pembrolizumab from 2 to 10 mg/kg Q3W, where efficacy has not been established, and as such, this would be a potential risk to patient treatment success. As noted previously, the 2 mg/kg Q3W dose has shown clinical efficacy in registrational trials limited to melanoma and NSCLC as compared with the fixed dose of 200 mg Q3W that has been evaluated extensively across all tumor types. It should also be noted that while it may appear that weight-based or weight-banded lower doses in lower body weight patients would lead to similar exposure and thereby efficacy as the recommended fixed doses in higher body weight patients, the majority of patients treated in pembrolizumab clinical trials were of median to higher body weight. Thus, the clinical evidence of efficacy drawn from 2 mg/kg Q3W dosing experience in lower body weight patients (which comprise < 5% of all patients in those trials) leans on much smaller numbers of actual patients evaluated for efficacy at such lower doses/exposures (for instance, in all MSD trials as described in Supplementary Table 1, only 63 patients weighing < 50 kg received 2 mg/kg Q3W, providing data at actual doses of ≤ 100 mg Q3W). Therefore, it is important that the exposures in this subgroup of patients remain consistent to those seen in the wider patient population providing the majority of the clinical efficacy data. It is acknowledged that there are ongoing clinical studies investigating low dose pembrolizumab, for instance the NVALT-30 and PULSE clinical trials [18, 19], and that a number of retrospective analyses and systematic reviews have indicated that weight-based dosing or low-dose pembrolizumab may be effective [17, 20–23]. Additionally, it is also acknowledged that lower dosing may be preferred by several institutions due to a notion that because it results in lower concentrations, it may reduce toxicity without impacting efficacy, along with being a driver of cost-savings. However, such evaluations are exploratory and warrant further validation. Many of these studies are either based on a single geographic region, limiting the global applicability of results, or may include heterogeneous patient populations [20–23]. Moreover, given the retrospective nature, these analyses may have inherent limitations in unconfounding outcomes and may not exclude trial bias. While some treatment benefit has been observed with lower doses in these studies, they do also acknowledge the importance of factoring in body weight, among other relevant patient factors, when making the dose decision, and may also be subject to reporting bias [20, 21, 23]. Overall, these evaluations and study designs would not conform to the scientific and regulatory rigor needed for dose registration. Furthermore, in terms of safety, pembrolizumab has been evaluated up to fivefold higher doses and concentrations at 10 mg/kg Q3W and Q2W with safety shown to be consistent with the lower approved doses. There is also no clear evidence that lower doses would meaningfully improve the safety profile of pembrolizumab. It may be relevant to also note that the adverse events of anti-PD-(L)1 therapy largely tend to be immune-mediated, which are often unpredictable and may be triggered by the first administration of any dose/concentration.

Vial sharing between patients has been advocated when implementing weight-based doses as a cost-saving measure. In addition, to mitigate the use of vials that could be improperly stored for hours, days, or even weeks, an alternative solution was proposed by scheduling infusions for multiple patients on the same day [16]. Scheduling and logistics for vial sharing are impractical and introduce medication safety hazards that directly counter the reasons for which fixed dosing was adopted. The single-use pembrolizumab vials do not contain any preservative and/or antimicrobial agent in the formulation. The splitting or sharing of any unused pembrolizumab left in the vial, as may occur with implementation of weight-based dosing, is not recommended and can carry substantial risk for bacterial contamination and infection along with potential instability of drug resulting from improper storage conditions [11, 16]. Furthermore, it increases complexity and workflow burden on pharmacy and nursing staff, with potential for medication errors. Both regulatory authorities and the manufacturer explicitly caution against reusing pembrolizumab vials due to these safety risks.

In a challenging disease such as cancer, it is important that there is absolute certainty about the effectiveness and safety of therapy. Every effort must be made to maximize the chance of therapeutic benefit at an appropriate dosing regimen, especially for a widely used treatment such as pembrolizumab. It is acknowledged that the pharmacodynamic drivers of efficacy for immuno-oncology therapies remain a subject of scientific debate, particularly the need for target saturation throughout the tumor for optimal efficacy versus target saturation in circulation alone. In absence of conclusive mechanistic understanding and clinical evidence, it is prudent from a risk–benefit standpoint to lean toward the conservative need for tumor target saturation and associated exposures. As such, alternative lower doses pose a risk to achieving this and thereby optimal efficacy, with particular implications in the curative setting. Any regimen resulting in exposures below rigorously validated clinical thresholds must be prospectively confirmed for noninferior efficacy and safety in adequately powered trials before adoption. In as much as providing progression-free survival (PFS) or overall survival (OS) benefit to patients in the metastatic setting is important, it is also extremely important to provide clinical benefit in the early treatment settings. Pembrolizumab is commonly used in early stage disease and in both adjuvant and neoadjuvant settings where the intent is curative. In these settings, every dose decision is crucial; empiric underdosing with no proven outcomes can compromise treatment efficacy and directly reduce a patient’s chance of cure. Overall, it is important to emphasize that practices resulting in underdosing pose potential risks to efficacy and optimal patient care, which especially in oncology are substantial and potentially irreversible. At this time, pembrolizumab 4 mg/kg Q6W IV has not been evaluated in an adequately powered prospective controlled clinical trial. Given this, and the findings of the current analysis, using pembrolizumab at a regimen of 4 mg/kg Q6W poses a risk to patients of receiving a potentially subtherapeutic dose, which could compromise efficacy, particularly for patients with lower body weight. Strategies to save healthcare costs are vital; however, it is more vital to provide appropriate care to patients, especially for life-altering drugs such as pembrolizumab. Therefore, in absence of robust clinical data showing efficacy at doses with such low concentrations, pembrolizumab at a dose of 4 mg/kg Q6W should not be considered an appropriate alternative to a fixed dose of 400 mg Q6W. Underdosing may lead to suboptimal efficacy, worsened clinical outcomes, and, ultimately, harm in patients, which is unacceptable ethically and clinically. Therefore, to ensure patient safety and efficacy, they should be treated with pembrolizumab in accordance with the label approved by global regulatory agencies and the broadest clinical experience at fixed doses of 200 mg Q3W or 400 mg Q6W, and not at body weight-adjusted lower doses. Overall, the pharmacokinetic, clinical and regulatory evidence all converge on the support of approved fixed-dose pembrolizumab regimens as the safest and most effective standard.

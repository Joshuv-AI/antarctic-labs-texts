Generalized Additive Decompositions of Symmetric Tensors
Enrica Barrilli, Bernard Mourrain, Daniele Taufer

Abstract
. This article addresses the Generalized Additive Decomposition (GAD) of symmetric tensors, that is, degree- d d forms f ∈ 𝒮 d f\in\mathcal{S}_{d} . From a geometric perspective, a GAD corresponds to representing a point on a secant of osculating varieties to the Veronese variety, providing a compact and structured description of a tensor that captures its intrinsic algebraic properties. We provide a linear algebra method for measuring the GAD size and prove that the minimal achievable size, which we call the GAD-rank of the considered tensor, coincides with the rank of suitable Catalecticant matrices, under certain regularity assumptions. We provide a new explicit description of the apolar scheme associated with a GAD as the annihilator of a polynomial-exponential series. We show that if the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of this scheme is sufficiently small, then both the GAD and the associated apolar scheme are minimal and unique. Leveraging these results, we develop a numerical GAD algorithm for symmetric tensors that effectively exploits the underlying algebraic structure, extending existing algebraic approaches based on eigen computation to the treatment of multiple points. We illustrate the effectiveness and numerical stability of such an algorithm through several examples, including Waring and tangential decompositions.

Introduction

Tensors are fundamental mathematical objects that generalize matrices to higher dimensions and play a crucial role in various domains. While matrices, namely second-order tensors, are well-known and widely used, higher-order tensors offer a more natural representation for data involving more than two interacting variables.
These objects provide an effective means to organize and model such data, capturing intricate relationships across these multiple dimensions.
Consequently, understanding and efficiently manipulating tensors is essential for real-world problems that involve multidimensional data.

Tensor decomposition, i.e., expressing a given tensor as a sum of simpler tensors, has emerged as a key technique in the analysis of higher-order tensors
for uncovering latent structure in multidimensional data, impacting diverse domains.
In blind source separation, higher-order cumulant tensors provide algebraic structures that enable the identification of independent sources, even in underdetermined mixtures
[
18
,
24
]
, while in independent component analysis (ICA), they support consistent recovery of independent components in overcomplete regimes
[
33
]
.
In psychometrics, tensor methods were introduced early through the
N
-way generalization of the Eckart–Young decomposition
[
19
]
, and in medical imaging, spectral decompositions of covariance tensors are central to diffusion MRI for characterizing tissue microstructure
[
5
]
.
In telecommunications, multilinear decompositions underpin blind receivers and CDMA separation algorithms
[
20
,
23
,
50
,
54
]
, while more broadly, tensors provide a natural language for sparse representations and uncertainty principles in signal processing
[
26
]
.
In statistics and machine learning, moment tensors are now a standard tool for learning latent variable models, enabling efficient algorithms for Gaussian mixtures
[
31
,
32
,
45
]
, nonparametric mixture models
[
56
]
, and hidden Markov models
[
1

2
]
.

Crucially, many of these applications rely not on arbitrary tensors but on symmetric tensors: cumulant tensors, covariance tensors, and moment tensors are all permutation-invariant by construction.
While general-purpose methods such as the Canonical Polyadic Decomposition (CPD) are widely used, they do not exploit the additional structure encoded by symmetry.
In contrast, symmetric tensor decompositions capture the intrinsic algebraic and geometric properties of these problems.
Moreover, techniques from algebraic geometry provide deep insights into the geometric structure of tensors, enabling the development of decomposition algorithms.
This paper contributes to this line of work by presenting an algorithm for the Generalized Additive Decomposition (GAD) of symmetric tensors, which extends the Waring framework.
This generalization offers new techniques for computing compact tensor representations and revealing intrinsic information, thereby broadening their scope and applicability.

Novel contributions.

In this paper, we provide a detailed study of GADs of symmetric tensors
f
of order
d
or, equivalently, degree-
d
forms
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
We provide a geometric interpretation of GADs as a representation of points on secants of the smooth part of osculating varieties.
We prove that the GAD-size computation can be performed entirely via linear algebra, improving previous quasi-linear constructions, by evaluating the dimensions of inverse systems associated with prescribed forms.
We define the minimum of such sizes as the
GAD-rank

\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GAD}}(f)
.
We show that a minimal GAD of
f
, i.e., a GAD realizing
\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GAD}}(f)
, can be computed from certain Catalecticant matrices, under regularity assumptions.
We provide a new explicit description of the corresponding apolar scheme as annihilators of polynomial-exponential series (see
Theorems

5.6
and
5.8
for more details):

Theorem.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
with a GAD of the form
f=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}
such that
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{k_{i}}
and
\ell_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
If the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
is less than
\frac{d+1}{2}
, where
\underline{I}
is the ideal associated with the GAD, then

\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GAD}}(f)=\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{cactus}}(f)=\rank H,

where
\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{cactus}}(f)
is the minimal length of a scheme apolar to
f
, and

is the Catalecticant matrix of
f
in degree
(d-c,c)
with

.
Moreover, in this case, the considered GAD is the unique minimal one, and defines the unique minimal apolar scheme to

.

As a consequence, when

and the regularity of the quotient by the vanishing ideal of the points

is less than

This leads to a numerical GAD algorithm for symmetric tensors, which runs as if the regularity of the unknown decomposition were less than
\frac{d+1}{2}
and can verify the hypothesis a posteriori.
This algorithm refines
[
13
, Algorithm 3]
, establishing terminating conditions that prevent the issue associated with high nil-indices (equiv. high socle degrees for the associated schemes).
Its algorithmic realization significantly extends algebraic approaches based on eigen computation by providing a flexible and efficient decomposition technique that numerically handles tensor decompositions corresponding to multiple points.
The GAD algorithm achieves optimal stability in Waring decomposition when the regularity of the associated ideal is below
\frac{d+1}{2}
, and it likewise demonstrates robust performance for symmetric tensor decompositions with multiple points, which is tested on several examples.
By addressing the limitations of existing algorithms, we provide a method that is more general and applicable to a broader range of problems, and could pave the way for computing minimal apolar schemes and cactus ranks using extension techniques such as

Preliminaries

\mathbb{K}

|\alpha|:=\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n},\quad\alpha!:=\alpha_{0}!\cdots\alpha_{n}!,\quad\textnormal{and}\quad{d\choose\alpha}:=\frac{d!}{\alpha!}.

For any
\mathbb{K}

When

\mathcal{S}_{d}

A non-zero element in

is also called a

We denote the full graded algebra of forms (of any degree) by
\mathcal{S}:=\oplus_{d=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{d}
.

The dual space
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}:=\hom_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{S}_{d},\mathbb{K})

\mathbb{K}
-linear functionals from
\mathcal{S}_{d}
to
\mathbb{K}

For a given
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}

\langle\varphi,f\rangle:=\varphi(f)
.
An element
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}
is represented uniquely as a

\varphi(\underline{\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{|\alpha|=d}\varphi_{\alpha}\frac{\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!},

where
\varphi_{\alpha}:=\langle\varphi,\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\rangle\in\mathbb{K}
is the value of the linear functional
\varphi

\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and

\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{K},\quad p\mapsto\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}^{\alpha}(p)(0):=(\partial_{x_{0}}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}p)(0).

In this representation,
(\frac{\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!})_{|\alpha|=d}
is the dual basis in
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}
of the monomial basis
({\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}})_{|\alpha|=d}
of
\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
We verify for monomials and by linearity for all

(1)

{}\langle gh(\underline{\mathbf{z}}),f\rangle=\langle h(\underline{\mathbf{z}}),g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)\rangle:=\langle h(\underline{\mathbf{z}}),g(\partial_{x_{0}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}})(f)\rangle.

For any
\mathbf{\underline{\xi}}=(\underline{\xi}_{0},\ldots,\underline{\xi}_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{n+1}

\ell=(\mathbf{\underline{\xi}},\underline{\mathbf{x}}):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\underline{\xi}_{i}\,x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and we denote the corresponding exponential series by

\mathbf{e}_{\underline{\xi}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}}):=\mathbf{e}_{\ell}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})=e^{\ell(\underline{\mathbf{z}})}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\ell(\underline{\mathbf{z}})^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}}\frac{\underline{\xi}^{\alpha}\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\in\mathbb{K}[[\underline{\mathbf{z}}]]=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}.

We check from the definitions that
\mathbf{e}_{\underline{\xi}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})
represents the evaluation-in-
\underline{\xi}
linear functional

\mathbf{e}_{\underline{\xi}}:p\in\mathcal{S}\mapsto\langle\mathbf{e}_{\underline{\xi}},p\rangle=p(\underline{\xi})
.
More generally, by

1
and linearity, for
\omega\in\mathcal{S}
and
\underline{\xi}\in\mathbb{K}^{n+1}
, we verify that the linear functional
\omega(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\underline{\xi}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbb{K}[[\underline{\mathbf{z}}]]=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
represents

For any

and

2.1.
Apolarity

Let us fix a basis
\underline{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, which naturally determines, for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, a monomial basis

\mathcal{S}_{d}

Given two forms
f\,=\,\sum_{|\alpha|=d}f_{\alpha}\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}
and
g\,=\,\sum_{|\alpha|=d}g_{\alpha}\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, we define their
apolar product
(with respect to

) as

(3)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{a}\ :=\ \sum_{|\alpha|=d}{d\choose\alpha}^{-1}f_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{d!}g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f).

It is easy to verify that

3
defines a symmetric, non-degenerate bilinear form.
This apolar product depends on the choice of the coordinates
\underline{\mathbf{x}}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}

It is invariant by the orthogonal group
\textnormal{O}(\mathbb{K}^{n+1})

[
14

but not by the linear group
\textnormal{GL}(\mathbb{K}^{n+1})
.
We easily verify that the monomial basis
(\underline{\mathbf{x}}_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|=d}
of
\mathcal{S}_{d}
is orthogonal for the apolar product with respect to
\underline{\mathbf{x}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,
g\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
,

, and
\mathbf{\xi}\in\mathbb{K}^{n+1}

(1)

\langle f,g\,h\rangle_{a}\ =\ \frac{(d-k)!}{d!}\langle g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f),h\rangle_{a}
,

(2)

\langle f,(\mathbf{\xi},\underline{\mathbf{x}})^{d}\rangle_{a}\ =\ f(\mathbf{\xi})
,

(3)

\langle f,g\,(\mathbf{\xi},\underline{\mathbf{x}})^{d-k}\rangle_{a}\ =\ \frac{(d-k)!}{d!}g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)(\mathbf{\xi})
.

Proof.

Equality
1
follows from
eq.

3
, since

\langle f,gh\rangle_{a}=\frac{1}{d!}g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})h(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)=\frac{1}{d!}h(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big(g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)\big)=\frac{(d-k)!}{d!}\langle h,g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)\rangle_{a},

while
2
follows by straightforward computation.
By combining
1
and
2
, we immediately obtain
3
.
∎

.

For

2.2.
Affine duality

For the decomposition of forms, we will study Artinian algebras, defined in an

In this section, we recall the notation and properties of the corresponding duality.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{K}
in the variables
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

Its dual space is

.
Similarly to the homogeneous setting, any linear functional

can be represented as a formal power series in

\varphi(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\varphi_{\alpha}\,\frac{\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\in\mathbb{K}[[\mathbf{z}]]

\varphi

, and

{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}:R\to\mathbb{K},\quad p\mapsto\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\alpha}(p)(0):=(\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}p)(0).

As in the homogeneous case, for

\mathfrak{h}_{0}:\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]_{\leq d}\to\mathcal{S}_{d},\quad p\mapsto\mathfrak{h}_{0}(p):=x_{0}^{d}p\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n}}{x_{0}}\right).

Its transposed map will therefore be

\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{\ast}:\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]_{\leq d}^{\ast},\quad\varphi\mapsto\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{\ast}(\varphi):=\varphi\circ\mathfrak{h}_{0}.

For any
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1},|\alpha|=d}f_{\alpha}\,\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, we have
f^{*}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1},|\alpha|=d}f_{\alpha}\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\ast}
and we define the linear functional
\check{f}\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]_{\leq d}^{\ast}
as

(7)

\check{f}:=\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{\ast}(f^{*})=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1},|\alpha|=d}\alpha_{0}!f_{\alpha}\,z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\beta|\leq d}(d-|\beta|)!\,f_{\underline{\beta}}\,{\mathbf{z}}^{\beta},

where
\underline{\beta}=(d-|\beta|,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}
for
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The functional
\check{f}
of
eq.

7
may be interpreted in terms of polynomial contraction
{\mathbf{z}}^{\beta}\mathbin{\lrcorner}{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}:={\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha-\beta}
. In fact, if we denote the
divided powers
expression of
f
by
f_{\textnormal{dp}}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1},|\alpha|=d}f_{\alpha}\alpha!\,\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}

[
12
]
, then

\langle\check{f},g\rangle=\big(g\mathbin{\lrcorner}f_{\textnormal{dp}}\big)(1,0,\dots,0).

Furthermore, by inspecting (
7
), we observe that the coefficient of
{\mathbf{z}}^{\beta}
in
\check{f}
equals the coefficient of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta}
in
\frac{1}{d!}\textnormal{Tw}^{-1}(f)(1,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, where
\textnormal{Tw}(f)
is the
twist
of
f
as defined in
[
30
]
.

The following lemma is the affine version of
Lemma

2.3
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f=\omega\,\ell^{d-k}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, where
\ell=x_{0}+(\xi,{\mathbf{x}})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
with
\xi\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
Then we have

\check{f}=\big(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})\big)^{[\leq d]}.

Proof.

Let
\omega=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\omega_{j}\,\ell^{k-j}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
with
\omega_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
, so
\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(d-j)!\,\omega_{j}({\mathbf{z}})
as defined in
eq.

4
.
For
p\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]_{\leq d}
, let us shortly denote
p^{h}={\mathfrak{h}}_{0}(p)
. By
Lemma

2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\check{f},p\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,p^{h}\rangle_{a}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\langle\omega_{j}\ell^{d-j},p^{h}\rangle_{a}=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{(d-j)!}\,\omega_{j}(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(p^{h})(1,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(p^{h})(1,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}).

Since
\omega_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
, then also
\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbf{x}})
, thus

\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(p^{h})(1,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})=\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})=\langle\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}}),p\rangle,

which shows the equality between the linear functionals
\check{f}
and
\big(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})\big)^{[\leq d]}
.
∎

3.
Generalized Additive Decompositions

In this section, we define the Generalized Additive Decompositions (GAD) of a form in
\mathcal{S}_{d}
, and detail how we measure the size of such decompositions.

3.1.
GADs

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and
0\leq k_{i}\leq d
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
A
Generalized Additive Decomposition (GAD)
of
f
is an expression of the form:

(8)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}},

where
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{n+1}
and
\ell_{i}:=(\xi_{i},\underline{\mathbf{x}})
are not proportional and do not divide
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{k_{i}}
.
The linear forms
\{\ell_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s}
are called the
supports
of the GAD (
8
).
When
s=1
, we call the GAD
local
.

Clearly such a decomposition exists for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, since we can write
f=f\,\ell^{0}
for any
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
not dividing
f
.
To measure the size of such a decomposition, we use a dual approach and introduce so-called inverse systems.

A GAD
f=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
can be reinterpreted in the dual space as a decomposition in terms of (truncated) polynomial-exponential series.
In fact, by
Lemma

2.3
we have

f^{*}=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},\mathbf{v}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\bigg)^{[d]},

for any
\mathbf{v}
such that
\{\ell_{i},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
.
Similarly, by
Lemma

2.5
, if
\underline{\xi}_{0,i}=1
, then we also have

(9)

\check{f}=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{s}{\omega}_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\bigg)^{[\leq d]}\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]^{*}_{\leq{d}}.

where
\xi_{i}=(\underline{\xi}_{1},\ldots,\underline{\xi}_{n})
and the
\omega_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}
are defined as in
eq.

4
.
Hence,
\check{f}
is the truncation (in degree
\leq d
) of the series
\sum_{i=1}^{s}{\omega}_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\in\mathcal{P}ol\mathcal{E}xp
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathbf{v}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that
\{\ell,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and fix
d\geq k
. We define
the
\ell
-
inverse system
of
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
as

\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}:=\big\langle g(\partial_{\mathbf{v}})(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})\ |\ g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{v})\big\rangle.

Remark 3.3
.

The
\ell
-inverse system is made of all the possible derivatives of
\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
, with respect to any basis
\boldsymbol{\nu}=\{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}\}
of
\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle
.
In fact, we have

\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}=\langle\langle{\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}}\rangle\rangle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\big\langle\partial_{\nu_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\partial_{\nu_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})\big\rangle.

since
\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})=0
for
|\alpha|>k
, and
\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\alpha|\leq k}
is a basis of
\oplus_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{i}(\mathbf{v})
.

The vector space
\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
depends, in general, on the choices of
d
and
\mathbf{v}
.
However, we now prove that the dimensions do not, hence they are canonical quantities associated with
\omega
and
\ell
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
\ell,v_{1},\dots,v_{n},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that
\langle\ell,\mathbf{v}\rangle=\langle\ell,\mathbf{u}\rangle=\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
Then, for all integers
d\geq k
, there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}\simeq\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}},

which respects the polynomial degree.

Proof.

It follows directly from the definition of
\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
(
eq.

4
) that

\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}\to\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}},\quad\sum_{j=0}^{k}s_{j}\mapsto\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{d!(k-j)!}{(d-j)!k!}s_{j},

where
s_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}(\mathbf{v})
are the graded parts of a given element of
\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
, is an isomorphism of vector spaces that respects the degrees.
Thus, it is sufficient to prove the graded isomorphism
\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}}\simeq\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}}
.
Since
\langle\ell,\mathbf{v}\rangle=\langle\ell,\mathbf{u}\rangle=\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and
\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}}
(resp.
\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
) depends only on
\ell
and
\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle
(resp.
\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle
), we can assume that

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{a}\,\ell
for some
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

We denote by
\partial_{u_{i}}
(resp.
\partial_{v_{i}}
) the differentiation with respect to the variable
u_{i}
(resp.
v_{i}
) in the monomial basis of
\mathcal{S}
in the variables
\{\ell,\mathbf{u}\}
(resp.
\{\ell,\mathbf{v}\}
).
Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\partial_{u_{i}}
.
By the Taylor expansion at
\ell=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega_{i}(\mathbf{u})\ell^{k-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega_{i}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{a}\ell)\ell^{k-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{j!}\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{j}(\omega_{i})(\mathbf{v})\ell^{j+k-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\frac{1}{j!}\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{j}(\omega_{i+j})(\mathbf{v})\ell^{k-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega^{\prime}_{i}(\mathbf{v})\ell^{k-i}

where
\omega^{\prime}_{i}=\omega_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}\frac{1}{j!}\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{j}(\omega_{i+j})\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(\mathbf{v})
.

By definition (see
eq.

4
), we have

\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(k-i)!}{k!}\omega^{\prime}_{i}=T\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{(k-i)!}{k!}\omega_{i}\right)=T(\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}}),

where
T_{\mathbf{a}}
is the linear operator defined by

T_{\mathbf{a}}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S},\quad p\mapsto p+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{j}(p_{i+j}),

with
p_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
denoting the homogeneous component of degree
i
of
p\in\mathcal{S}
.
This map is upper triangular in a graded basis of
\mathcal{S}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{S}_{i}
, is the identity on every
\mathcal{S}_{i}
, and preserves the degree.
Thus
T_{\mathbf{a}}
is an isomorphism of
\oplus_{i\leq d}\mathcal{S}_{i}
for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

For any
p\in\mathcal{S}
, the multivariate chain rule of differentiation yields

\partial_{v_{i}}(p(\ell,\mathbf{u}))=\partial_{v_{i}}\big(p(\ell,\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{a}\ell)\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{u_{j}}(p)\partial_{v_{i}}(u_{j})=\partial_{u_{i}}(p)

so that
\partial_{v_{i}}=\partial_{u_{i}}
.
Thus, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\partial_{\mathbf{v}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}})=\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}})=\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\alpha}\big(T_{\mathbf{a}}(\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}})\big)=T_{\mathbf{a}}(\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\alpha}\big(\omega^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}})\big)

since
\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\alpha}\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}=\Delta^{i}_{\mathbf{a}}\partial_{\mathbf{u}}^{\alpha}
.
This implies that
\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}}=T_{\mathbf{a}}(\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}})\simeq\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{u}}
, and this isomorphism preserves the degree.
∎

This allows us to define the
\ell
-rank and GAD-rank of a form:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\{\ell,v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
We define the
\ell
-rank
of
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
as

\rank_{\ell}(\omega):=\dim\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{k,\ell,\mathbf{v}}.

The
rank
(or
size
) of a GAD of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
as in
eq.

8
is additively defined as

\rank\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}\right):=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\rank_{\ell_{i}}(\omega_{i}).

We say that a GAD of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
is
minimal
if there are no GADs of
f
with a strictly lower rank.
The rank of a minimal GAD of
f
will be referred to as the
GAD-rank
of
f
, which we denote by
\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GAD}}(f)
.

Remark 3.6
.

Definition

3.5
is well-given, as the
\ell
-rank does not depend on the choice of
\bf v
by
Proposition

3.4
, and it can be evaluated with only linear algebra operations.

The inverse system
\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
is co-variant with changes of coordinates, as we show now.
Given
\phi=(\phi_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}\in\textup{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{K})
, we denote the corresponding change of coordinate action on
g\in\mathcal{S}
as

\phi(g)=g(\underline{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\phi^{T})=g\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\phi_{i,0}x_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=0}^{n}\phi_{i,n}x_{i}\right)
,
while its transpose action is the same action, with respect to its transposed matrix, i.e.,
\phi^{T}(g)=g(\underline{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\phi)=g(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\phi_{0,i}x_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=0}^{n}\phi_{n,i}x_{i})
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
and
\phi\in\textnormal{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{K})
. Then

\phi\big(g(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})(f)\big)=\big(\phi^{-T}(g)(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big)\big(\phi(f)\big).

Proof.

By linearity of the involved objects, it is sufficient to prove it on monomials, hence we assume
f=\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}
and
g=\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta}
.
For every
0\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-T}(x_{i})(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big)\big(\phi(x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\phi^{-T}(x_{i})(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big)\big(\phi(x_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}\phi(x_{j})^{\alpha_{j}-1}\langle\phi^{-T}(x_{i}),\phi(x_{j})\rangle_{a}.

We verify that
\phi^{-T}(x_{i})
is represented by the
i
-th row of
\phi^{-1}
, while
\phi(x_{j})
is represented by the
j
-th column of
\phi
in the basis
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
Thus, we have
\langle\phi^{-T}(x_{i}),\phi(x_{j})\rangle_{a}=\delta_{ij}
, so

\big(\phi^{-T}(x_{i})(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big)\big(\phi(x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\big)=\delta_{ij}\alpha_{j}\phi(x_{j})^{\alpha_{j}-1}=\phi\big(\partial_{x_{i}}(x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\big),

which proves the statement for
f=x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
and
g=x_{i}
.
Since
\phi,\phi^{-1}
and
\phi^{T}
are all multiplicative, by the chain rule of differentiation, we get

\big(\phi^{-T}(x_{i})(\partial_{\underline{\mathbf{x}}})\big)\big(\phi(\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha})\big)=\phi\big(\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\big)=\phi\big(\partial_{x_{i}}(\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha})\big).

The conclusion follows by repeatedly applying the above argument for all the (possibly repeated) linear factors
x_{i}
of
\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta}
.
∎

We note that
Lemma

3.7
corrects and generalizes
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
,
d\geq k
,
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\phi\in\textnormal{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{K})
.
Then we have

\phi\big(\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}\big)=\langle\langle{\phi(\omega)}\rangle\rangle^{d,\phi(\ell),\phi(\mathbf{v})}

Proof.

For every
0\leq i\leq k
and every
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{v})
, by
Lemma

3.7
, we have

\phi\big(g(\partial_{\mathbf{v}})(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})\big)=\phi^{-T}(g)(\partial_{\mathbf{v}})\big(\phi(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})\big).

By construction, we also have
\phi(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})=\phi(\omega)^{d,\phi(\ell),\phi(\mathbf{v})}
, and we have
\phi^{-T}(\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{v}])=\mathbb{K}[\phi(\mathbf{v})]
.
Hence, the above equality shows that the space of all derivatives in
\phi(\mathbf{v})
applied to
\phi(\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}})
is precisely the image under
\phi
of the space of all derivatives in
\mathbf{v}
applied to
\omega^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}
.
∎

Corollary

3.8
implies that the computation of the
\ell
-inverse system of
\omega
can be performed in arbitrary coordinates. One can compute the linear map
\phi:\{\ell,\mathbf{v}\}\mapsto\{x_{0},{\mathbf{x}}\}
, and the inverse system as

\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,\mathbf{v}}=\phi^{-1}\big(\langle\langle{\phi(\omega)}\rangle\rangle^{d,x_{0},\mathbf{{\mathbf{x}}}}\big).

This approach is usually more efficient than a straight application of
Definition

3.2
, as it only involves variable differentiations instead of directional derivatives.
If, instead, only the
\ell
-rank is needed, one can rename variables to have
\ell_{0}\neq 0
and directly apply
Proposition

3.4
to compute the
\ell
-rank as

\rank_{\ell}(\omega)=\dim\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{\deg(\omega),\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}.

3.2.
Geometric interpretation

We provide here a brief geometric interpretation of GADs in terms of osculating varieties to the
d
-th Veronese variety
, which is classically defined as the
d
-th Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{K})
.
Its affine cone is

\mathcal{V}_{d}:=\{\omega\,\ell^{d}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\omega\in\mathbb{K},\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}.

Remark 3.9
.

A famous challenging problem consists of computing a (minimal)
Waring decomposition
of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, that is, a decomposition of the form:

f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d},

with a minimal
r\in\mathbb{N}
, which is called the
Waring rank
of
f
.
Geometrically, it corresponds to expressing
f
as a minimal linear combination of
r
points on
\mathcal{V}_{d}
.

One can consider broader families of decompositions, by employing also elements in the tangent variety to the Veronese
[
13
]
, whose affine cone is

\mathcal{T}_{d}:=\{\omega\,\ell^{d-1}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\omega,\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}.

Remark 3.10
.

A point
\omega\,\ell^{d-1}
with
\ell=\ell_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+\ell_{n}x_{n},\omega=\omega_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+\omega_{n}x_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
is lying in the tangent spaces to
\mathcal{V}_{d}
, since
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\partial_{\ell_{i}}(\ell^{d})=d\,(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}x_{i})\ell^{d-1}=d\omega\ell^{d-1}
.

More generally, one can consider decompositions involving higher-order osculating varieties to the Veronese.

Definition 3.11
.

The affine cone of the
k-th osculating variety
to
\mathcal{V}_{d}
is defined as

\mathcal{O}^{k}_{d}:=\{\omega\,\ell^{d-k}\hskip 2.84544pt|\hskip 2.84544pt\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
\{\ell,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. The
Taylor expansion of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
at
\ell
is of the form:

(10)

f=\sum_{j=0}^{d}f_{j}(\mathbf{v})\ell^{d-j},

where
f_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}(\mathbf{v})
are the
decomposition coefficients
.
For
k\leq d
,
eq.

10
reads

f\in\omega\,\ell^{d-k}+(v_{1},...,v_{n})^{k+1}

where
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{k+1}
is the ideal generated by all degree-
(k+1)
monomials in
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
.
The term
\omega\,\ell^{d-k}
is therefore the truncation of Taylor’s expansion of
f
at
\ell
at order
k
, which explains the name of
k^{\textup{th}}
-
osculating
variety.
We notice that, as in
Remark

3.10
, the tangential variety to
\mathcal{O}^{k-1}_{d}
is
\mathcal{O}^{k}_{d}
.
For more details, see e.g.
[
8
]
or
[
48
]
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
0\leq k<d
. The singular locus of
\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k}
is
\textup{sing}(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})=\mathcal{O}^{k-1}_{d}
(with the convention that
\mathcal{O}^{-1}_{d}=\emptyset
). The affine dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k}
is
{k+n\choose k}+n
.

Proof.

Let
f=\omega\,\ell^{d-k}\in\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k}
with
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
. The tangent space at
f
, spanned by the limits
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}((\omega+\epsilon\theta)-\omega)\ell^{d-k}
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\omega\frac{1}{\epsilon}(({\ell+\epsilon\lambda})^{d-k}-\ell^{d-k})
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, is

T_{f}(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})=\langle\theta\,\ell^{d-k}+\omega\,\lambda\,\ell^{d-k-1}\ |\ \theta\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle.

Its dimension is
{k+n\choose k}+n+1-\dim(W)
, where

W=\langle\theta\ell^{d-k}:\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\rangle\cap\langle\omega\lambda\ell^{d-k-1}:\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle.

Elements in
W
are such that
\theta\ell^{d-k}=\omega\lambda\ell^{d-k-1}
, i.e.,
\theta\ell=\omega\lambda
.

•

If
\ell
does not divide
\omega
, then
\lambda=\eta\ell
for some
\eta\in\mathbb{K}
and
\dim(W)=1
so that
\dim\big(T_{f}(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})\big)={k+n\choose k}+n
.

•

If
\ell
divides
\omega
, namely there is
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
such that
\omega=\omega^{\prime}\ell
, then
\theta=\omega^{\prime}\lambda
for any choice of
\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
\dim(W)=n+1
and
\dim\big(T_{f}(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})\big)={k+n\choose k}
.

This shows that
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})={k+n\choose k}+n
, while the dimension of the tangent space drops at a point of
\textup{sing}(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})=\{\omega^{\prime}\ell\ell^{d-k-1}:\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1},\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\}=\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k-1}
.
∎

Lemma

3.13
shows that, if
\ell
does not divide
\omega
, then
\omega\,\ell^{d-k}\in(\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k})^{smooth}
is a smooth point of
\mathcal{O}_{d}^{k}
.
Hence, GADs parametrize generic points of joint varieties of osculating varieties to
\mathcal{V}_{d}
. In particular,

f=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}\textup{ GAD as in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:gad} }\iff f\in\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\mathcal{O}^{k_{i}}_{d})^{smooth}.

Remark 3.14
.

Waring and tangential decomposition are special instances of GADs (for
k_{i}=0
and
k_{i}\leq 1
, respectively), thus the GAD-rank is always bounded from above by the Waring (resp. tangential) rank.
However, minimal GADs typically differ completely from minimal Waring (or tangential) decompositions.

4.
Algebras and operators associated with GADs

In this section, we recall certain properties of Artinian algebras, describe those associated with a GAD, and prove novel results that we be employed in the next sections.

4.1.
Properties of an Artinian algebra and its dual

Let
I\subset R=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be a non-unit ideal, and
\mathcal{A}:=R/I
be the associated quotient algebra.
In this setting, the following are known to be equivalent:

•

I
is
zero-dimensional
, i.e. the Krull dimension of
\mathcal{A}
is
0
.

•

\mathcal{A}
is
Artinian
, i.e.
\mathcal{A}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space.

•

I
defines a
scheme of points
, i.e. its vanishing set
\mathcal{V}(I)
is finite.

Artinian algebras admit a unique direct decomposition into local Artinian algebras, as follows.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
29
, Theorem 4.9]
)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be a zero-dimensional ideal, whose minimal primary decomposition is
I=I_{\xi_{1}}\cap\dots\cap I_{\xi_{s}}
, where
I_{\xi_{i}}
is
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi_{i}}
-primary and
\xi_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
. Then

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{A}_{s},

where
\mathcal{A}_{i}\simeq\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi_{i}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{i}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{j}=0
for every
i\neq j
.

For every prime
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathcal{A}
, the kernel of the localization map
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the intersection of all
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals of
\mathcal{A}

[
3
, Corollary 10.21]
.
Hence, every
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is isomorphic to the localization of
\mathcal{A}
at
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{A}
.
The
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of the local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is referred to as the
multiplicity
of the point
\xi_{i}
.

We define the
idempotent

\mathbf{u}_{i}
associated with
\xi_{i}
as the projection of
1\in\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{A}_{i}
.
These elements are then characterized by the following properties, for every
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
:

(11)

1=\mathbf{u}_{1}+\dots+\mathbf{u}_{s},\qquad\mathbf{u}_{i}^{2}=\mathbf{u}_{i},\qquad\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{u}_{j}=0.

The orthogonal space of an
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}
-primary ideal
I_{\xi}
has a convenient representation in terms of the vector space

D_{I_{\xi}}({\mathbf{z}}):=\langle\omega({\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{z}}]\mid\forall q\in I_{\xi},\,\omega(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(q)(\xi)=0\rangle,

which is called the
inverse system of

I_{\xi}
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
41
, Proposition 2.16]
)
.

Let
I_{\xi}\subset\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be an
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}
-primary ideal. Then

I_{\xi}^{\perp}\simeq(\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi})^{\ast}=D_{I_{\xi}}({\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}}).

The above local property implies a global decomposition property on the elements of
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, as given by the following result.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
41
, Theorem 2.17]
)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be a zero-dimensional ideal, whose minimal primary decomposition is
I=I_{\xi_{1}}\cap\dots\cap I_{\xi_{s}}
, where
I_{\xi_{i}}
is
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi_{i}}
-primary and
\xi_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
.
Then

\mathcal{A}^{\ast}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\in\mathcal{P}ol\mathcal{E}xp\mid\forall 1\leq i\leq s,\,\omega_{i}({\mathbf{z}})\in D_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\right\}.

Finally, we define the (dual) multiplication operators.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
q\in R
and
I\subseteq R
be an ideal.
We define the
multiplication-by-
q
operator
on the quotient algebra
\mathcal{A}:=R/I
as

M_{q}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A},\quad p\mapsto pq.

Its
transposed multiplication operator
is

M^{t}_{q}:\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*},\quad\varphi\mapsto q\star\varphi.

4.2.
Hankel operators

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra.
The
Hankel operator
of a dual element
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
is

\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\ast},\quad p\mapsto p\star\varphi.

If the algebra
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is graded,
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{d}^{\ast}
and
c\leq d
, we define the
Hankel operator in degree

(d-c,c)
of
\varphi
as

\mathbf{H}^{d-c,c}_{\varphi}:\mathcal{A}_{c}\to\mathcal{A}_{d-c}^{\ast},\quad p\mapsto p\star\varphi.

Similarly, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{\leq d}^{\ast}
, we define its
Hankel operator in degree

(d-c,c)
as

\mathbf{H}^{d-c,c}_{\varphi}:\mathcal{A}_{\leq c}\to\mathcal{A}_{\leq d-c}^{\ast},\quad p\mapsto p\star\varphi.

In both cases, for any vector spaces
V\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{c}
and
W\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{d-c}
, we define the
truncated Hankel operator

\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{W,V}
of
\varphi
on
(V,W)
as

\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{W,V}:V\to W^{\ast},\quad p\mapsto(p\star\varphi)^{[W]},

obtained by restricting the domain of
\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{d-c,c}
to
V
, and its codomain to
W^{\ast}
.

If
B=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
is a basis of
V
, and
B^{\prime}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{s}\}
is a basis of
W
, the matrix representing
\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{W,V}
in
B
and the dual basis of
B^{\prime}
is

H_{\varphi}^{B^{\prime},B}:=\big(\langle\varphi,w_{i}v_{j}\rangle\big)_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
1\leq j\leq s\end{subarray}}.

By convention, we denote
\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{{B^{\prime}},{B}}:=\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{\langle B^{\prime}\rangle,\langle B\rangle}
.

In particular, in our setting, any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
defines for every
c\leq d
an Hankel operator
\mathbf{H}_{f^{*}}^{d-c,c}:\mathcal{S}_{c}\to\mathcal{S}_{d-c}^{\ast}
and an Hankel operator
\mathbf{H}_{\check{f}}^{d-c,c}:R_{\leq c}\to R_{\leq d-c}^{\ast}
.
By
eq.

7
, the matrix of the latter operator in the bases
\{{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}\}_{|\alpha|\leq c}\subset R_{\leq c}
and
\{\frac{{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\}_{|\alpha|\leq d-c}\subset R_{\leq d-c}^{\ast}
is

H_{\check{f}}^{d-c,c}:=\big(\langle f,\mathfrak{h}_{0}({\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha+\beta})\rangle_{a}\big)_{|\beta|\leq d-c,|\alpha|\leq c}.

Moreover, for every
\varphi\in R^{\ast}
, the Hankel operator
\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}
is symmetric, namely, for every
p,q\in R
we have
\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}(p)(q)=\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}(q)(p)=\varphi(p\,q)
.
One easily verifies that its kernel

(12)

I_{\varphi}:=\ker\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}=\{p\in R\mid p\star\varphi=0\}=\mathrm{Ann}(\varphi)

is an ideal, hence it defines a quotient algebra

(13)

\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}:=R/I_{\varphi}.

For every
V\subset R_{\leq c}
and
W\subset R_{\leq d-c}
and
\varphi\in R^{\ast}
, we also have by definition that

(14)

\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}^{W,V}=\mathbf{H}_{\varphi^{[\leq d]}}^{W,V}

Remark 4.6
.

The ideal
I_{\varphi}
is also the
annihilator
of
\varphi
.
Here we define it in the primal space
R
, while it is usually defined in dual spaces via contraction or derivation (see e.g.
[
35
, Chap. 1]
or
[
12
, Sec. 2]
).

Hereafter, we will use the generalized Kronecker theorem:

Theorem 4.7

(
[
41
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{K}[[{\mathbf{z}}]]\simeq R^{*}
. Then

\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}<\infty\quad\iff\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{P}ol\mathcal{E}xp.

Moreover, if
\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{i}({\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\in\mathcal{P}ol\mathcal{E}xp
, then

\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim\langle\langle{\omega_{i}}\rangle\rangle
and

I_{\varphi}=\cap_{i=1}^{s}\langle p\in R\mid\forall\theta\in\langle\langle{\omega_{i}}\rangle\rangle,\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)(\xi_{i})=0\rangle.

As a consequence, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}ol\mathcal{E}xp
, if
\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}\leq\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
and
I_{\varphi}\subseteq I_{\varphi^{\prime}}
, then

I_{\varphi}=I_{\varphi^{\prime}}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Since
I_{\varphi}\subseteq I_{\varphi^{\prime}}
, we have a canonical inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}
, therefore
\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}\leq\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}
.
However, by
Theorem

4.7
, we have
\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}\leq\rank\mathbf{H}_{\varphi^{\prime}}=\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
.
Thus
\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}=\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}<\infty
, hence
\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{A}_{\varphi^{\prime}}
and
I_{\varphi}=I_{\varphi^{\prime}}
.
∎

Finally, we notice that the (transposed) multiplication operator can be read from certain Hankel operators, as follows.

Lemma 4.9

(
[
41
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

For any
\varphi\in R^{\ast}
and
g\in R
, we have

\mathbf{H}_{g\star\varphi}=\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}\circ\mathbf{M}_{g}=\mathbf{M}_{g}^{t}\circ\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}_{\varphi},\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}^{\ast}).

4.3.
Linear functionals associated with a GAD

For every

If

If

Definition 4.10
.

We define the linear functional

\varphi:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}{\omega}_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}(\mathbf{z})\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}(\mathbf{z})\in\mathbb{K}[[\mathbf{z}]]\simeq R^{\ast},

Remark 4.11
.

Let

By

The following theorem shows that, under suitable conditions on a basis of

.
This is the crucial idea for recovering the GAD of

For a given set

Theorem 4.12
.

In the above setting, let
B
and

\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}
, with

.
Let

Let

Then

(1)

H_{0}:=H_{\check{f}}^{B^{\prime},B}

(2)

\rank H=\dim\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\dim\langle\langle{{\omega_{i}}}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}

(3)

For every

Then

(4)

\ker\mathbf{H}=I_{\varphi}\cap\langle A\rangle
,

(\ker\mathbf{H})=I_{\varphi}

\textup{im}\,\mathbf{H}=I_{\varphi}^{\perp}|_{\langle A^{\prime}\rangle}:=\{\phi|_{\langle A^{\prime}\rangle}\mid\phi\in I_{\varphi}^{\perp}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}^{*}\}

Proof.

(
1
-
2
):
Let

and
\varphi

By

Since

and

(
3
): By

4.9
, we have

\mathbf{H}_{x_{i}\star\varphi}=\mathbf{H}_{\varphi}\circ\mathbf{M}_{x_{i}}.

We choose the basis
B

\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}
and the dual basis of
B^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
.
Since we assumed

(

4.4.
The nil-index

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi}
be a local Artinian algebra defining a point
\xi\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
, namely

is
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}

Definition 4.14
.

The

of
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}
, denoted by
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})
, is the minimal
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every

\big(a({\mathbf{x}})-a(\xi)\big)^{N}=0\in\mathcal{A}_{\xi}.

Remark 4.15
.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})
is the minimal
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(16)

\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}^{N}=0\hskip 5.69046pt\textnormal{ in }\hskip 5.69046pt\mathcal{A}_{\xi}=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi}.

Thus,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})
is the minimal degree

such that

\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}^{N}\subseteq I_{\xi}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}.

Hence,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})-1
is the

of
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}
.

The following proposition relates the structure of the terms appearing in a GAD with the nil-index of the associated algebras.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi}
be the local Artinian algebra associated with the GAD
\omega\,\ell^{d-k}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
. Then

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})-1=k=\deg(\omega).

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}^{N}\subseteq I_{\xi}
for
|\alpha|\geq N
.
By
Remark

4.11
, this means

({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\in I_{\xi}\iff\forall\varphi\in I_{\xi}^{\perp},\quad({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\star\varphi=0\iff({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\star\langle\langle{\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})}\rangle\rangle=0.

In particular, we have that
({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\star\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})=0
.
By a repeated application of
eq.

6
, we get

({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\star\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})=(\partial_{{\mathbf{z}}}-\xi)^{\alpha}[\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}})]=\partial_{{\mathbf{z}}}^{\alpha}\big(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\big)\mathbf{e}_{\xi}({\mathbf{z}}).

Therefore, the condition
({\mathbf{x}}-\xi)^{\alpha}\in I_{\xi}
for
|\alpha|\geq N
is equivalent to

\partial_{{\mathbf{z}}}^{\alpha}\big(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})\big)=0,\quad\forall|\alpha|\geq N,

which means that
\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}({\mathbf{z}})
is a polynomial of degree
<N
. The minimal such
N\in\mathbb{N}
, which equals
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})
by
Remark

4.15
, is then
N=\deg(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}})+1=k+1
, where the last equality follows from
\ell\nmid\omega
, by definition of GAD.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

Proposition

4.16
rephrases, in the notation of this manuscript, the equality between the socle degree of a local Artinian Gorenstein algebra and the degree of its dual generator.
This is studied particularly for graded algebras
[
40
, Chap. IV]
, while the affine version was considered in
[
34
, Lemma 1.1]
.
Its connection to GADs was also investigated in
[

, Proposition 4.3]
.

Remark 4.18
.

We observe that, with the notation of
Definition

4.4
, we also have

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_{\xi})=\min_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\big\{M_{p}\equiv 0\in\hom(\mathcal{A}_{\xi},\mathcal{A}_{\xi}),\ \forall p\in\mathfrak{m}_{\xi}^{N}\big\}.

In fact, for every

, we have

, hence

. On the other side, for any

.

4.5.
Schur factorization

Let
I=\cap_{i=1}^{s}I_{\xi_{i}}\subset\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be a zero-dimensional ideal, and consider the decomposition of the Artinian algebra
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I
as in
Theorem

4.1
:

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{s}},

where
A_{i}\simeq\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]/I_{\xi_{i}}
is the local algebra associated with the point
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

For a given matrix
M\in\textnormal{End}(\mathcal{A})
, its
Schur decomposition
is

M=U\,T\,U^{-1},

where
U
is a unitary matrix, while
T
is upper triangular.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{k}}
as above, and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
be a generic linear form.
Let
M_{\lambda}=U\,T\,U^{-1}
be the Schur factorization of the multiplication-by-
\lambda
operator.
Then, for any polynomial
g\in\mathbb{K}[{\mathbf{x}}]
, the conjugated multiplication operator
\tilde{M_{g}}:=U^{-1}\,M_{g}\,U
is block upper triangular, i.e.,

\tilde{M}_{g}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{M}_{g}^{(1)}&*&\dots&*\\
0&\tilde{M}_{g}^{(2)}&\dots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\tilde{M}_{g}^{(s)}\end{bmatrix},

where each block
\tilde{M}_{g}^{(k)}
represents the multiplication-by-
g
in
\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{k}}
.

Proof.

By
[
29
, Theorem 4.23]
, the eigenvalues of
M_{\lambda}
are
\{\lambda(\xi_{i})\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}}
, namely the evaluation of
\lambda
at the roots
\xi_{i}
, with multiplicities
\mu_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}
.
Since
\lambda
is generic, we may assume that the
\lambda(\xi_{i})\in\mathbb{K}
are pairwise distinct, i.e., we have well-separated eigenvalues.

Let us define the increasing filtration

0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\dots\subset V_{s}=\mathcal{A},

where
V_{i}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{j}}
is the sum of the first
i
local algebras.
Each subspace
V_{i}
is invariant under
M_{\lambda}
, and we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces
V_{i}/V_{i-1}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}
.
Hence, the action of
M
on
\mathcal{A}
decomposes into a sequence of actions on the quotients
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
.

Let
M=U\,T\,U^{-1}
be the Schur factorization of
M
.
Since the eigenvalues
\lambda(\xi_{i})
are distinct, we can order the Schur basis vectors such that, for every
1\leq i\leq s
, the first
\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i}
vectors form a basis for
V_{i}
.
On this basis,
T
has a block upper triangular form

T=\begin{bmatrix}T^{(1)}&*&\dots&*\\
0&T^{(2)}&\dots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&T^{(s)}\end{bmatrix},

where each
T^{(i)}\in\textnormal{End}(\mathbb{K}^{\mu_{i}})
is upper triangular and has all diagonal entries equal to
\lambda(\xi_{i})
.
Thus, each
T^{(i)}
represents the multiplication by
\lambda
in the quotient
V_{i}/V_{i-1}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}
.

Since
M_{g}
commutes with
M_{\lambda}
, by applying
[
22
, Proposition 4]
it also preserves the eigenspace filtration
V_{0}\subset\dots\subset V_{s}
, hence it admits the same block decomposition as
T
.
Consequently,
\tilde{M}_{g}:=U^{-1}M_{g}U
has the same block triangular form:

\tilde{M}_{g}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{M}_{g}^{(1)}&*&\dots&*\\
0&\tilde{M}_{g}^{(2)}&\dots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\tilde{M}_{g}^{(s)}\end{bmatrix}.

Each block
\tilde{M}_{g}^{(i)}
acts on
\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}
, and represents the multiplication operator by
g
in the local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}
, expressed in the Schur basis.
∎

5.
Apolar schemes, ranks and minimal decompositions

In this section, we analyse the apolar schemes associated with a GAD and show that, under regularity hypotheses, a form
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
has a unique minimal GAD and a unique apolar scheme of minimal length.

Let
\underline{I}\subset\mathcal{S}
be a homogeneous ideal.
We recall that
\underline{I}
is
saturated
if
\big(\underline{I}:(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\big)=\underline{I}
, i.e.,
\underline{I}
is the ideal defining a projective scheme.
For the sake of brevity, we will identify projective schemes with their defining homogeneous saturated ideals.
The dimension of such a scheme is
1
less than the dimension of its
affine cone

\mathcal{V}(I)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
.
Hence, a zero-dimensional projective scheme
Z
is supported at finitely many points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{K})
, and its localizations at these points are Artinian algebras.
We say that such
Z
is
(locally) Gorenstein
if these localizations are Artinian Gorenstein algebras.
Finally, we say that
\underline{I}
is
apolar
to
f\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
if
f^{*}\in\underline{I}_{d}^{\perp}
or, equivalently, if

\underline{I}\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}(f^{*})
, by regarding
f^{*}
as an element of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
(see
[
35
, Lemma 1.15]
).

We recall that the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity

\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{S}/\underline{I})
of the
\mathcal{S}
-module
\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
is
\max_{i,j}\{a_{i,j}-i\}
for a minimal free resolution

0\rightarrow\oplus_{j}\mathcal{S}(-a_{l,j})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\oplus_{j}\mathcal{S}(-a_{1,j})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}\rightarrow 0

(see
[
28
, chap. 4]
).
The
regularity index

r(\underline{I})
of the Hilbert function is the minimal degree from which the Hilbert function of
\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
agrees with its Hilbert polynomial.
When
\underline{I}
is saturated and (projectively)
0
-dimensional,
we have, for a generic
v\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
,

\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{S}/\underline{I})=r(\underline{I})=\min_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\{\mathbf{M}_{v}:p\in\mathcal{S}_{d}/\underline{I}_{d}\mapsto p\,v\in\mathcal{S}_{d+1}/\underline{I}_{d+1}\textup{ is surjective}\}

(see
[
35
, Theorem 1.69]
).
The regularity
\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{S}/\underline{I})
is the minimal degree of a basis in the localization
\mathcal{A}
of
S/\underline{I}
at
v
.
When
\underline{I}
is radical, it is also known as the
interpolation degree
of
\mathcal{V}(\underline{I})

[
28
, chap. 4]
.

5.1.
Apolar schemes from GADs

Consider a GAD
f=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
as in
eq.

8
.
By a generic change of coordinates, we can assume that
\underline{\xi}_{i,0}\neq 0
and, by scaling
\omega_{i}
and
\ell_{i}
, we will always assume that
\underline{\xi}_{i,0}=1
.
We define the homogenized operator of
eq.

15
as

\underline{\varphi}:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}\in\mathbb{K}[[\underline{\mathbf{z}}]]=\mathcal{S}^{\ast},

and the homogeneous ideal

(17)

{I}_{\underline{\varphi}}:=\textup{Ann}(\underline{\varphi})=\langle p\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\mid p\star\underline{\varphi}=0\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The ideal
I_{\underline{\varphi}}
, as defined above, is apolar to
f
.

Proof.

For every
p\in(I_{\underline{\varphi}})_{d}
, we have
\langle\underline{\varphi}^{[d]},p\rangle=\langle\underline{\varphi},p\rangle=\langle p\star\underline{\varphi},1\rangle=0
, hence
\underline{\varphi}^{[d]}\in(I_{\underline{\varphi}})_{d}^{\perp}
.
By
Lemma

2.3
, we conclude that

f^{*}=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}(\underline{\mathbf{z}})\big)^{[d]}=\underline{\varphi}^{[d]}\in(I_{\underline{\varphi}})_{d}^{\perp}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

For every
\omega\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
with
k\leq d
and
\ell=(\underline{\xi},\underline{\mathbf{x}})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
with
\underline{\xi}_{0}=1
, the ideal
\textup{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
is saturated, supported on
\underline{\xi}
and
equals to

(18)

\textup{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell})=\langle p\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\mid\forall\theta\in\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}},\ \ \theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)(\underline{\xi})=0\rangle_{i\in\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

By definition,
p\in\textup{Ann}(\omega\mathbf{e}_{\ell})_{i}=\textup{Ann}(\omega\mathbf{e}_{\ell})\cap\mathcal{S}_{i}
means
\forall q\in\mathcal{S},\langle p\star(\omega\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),q\rangle=0
, which is equivalent to
\forall j,l\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\in\mathcal{S}_{l}({\mathbf{x}})
to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle p\star(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),q\,x_{0}^{j}\rangle=\langle q\star(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),p\,x_{0}^{j}\rangle=\langle q(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),p\,x_{0}^{j}\rangle.

By Leibnitz rule, as in
eq.

6
, we have

\langle\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})\rangle_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}=\langle\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}})\rangle_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell}

so that

q(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})=\theta\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell}
where
\theta\in\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\langle q(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}),p\,x_{0}^{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\theta\mathbf{e}_{\ell},p\,x_{0}^{j}\rangle=\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p\,x_{0}^{j})(\underline{\xi})=\big(\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)\,x_{0}^{j}\big)(\underline{\xi})=\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)(\underline{\xi}).

This shows the equality (
18
).

Let us prove now that
\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
is saturated and supported on
\underline{\xi}
.
For all
p\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
contained in the saturation
\big(\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}):(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\big)
we have
x_{j}p\in\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
for
j=0,\ldots,n
. In particular,
x_{0}\,p\in\mathrm{Ann}(\omega\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
. That is, by
eq.

18
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\theta=\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\alpha}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}})
, we have

0=\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(x_{0}\,p)(\underline{\mathbf{x}})=\big(x_{0}\,\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)\big)(\underline{\xi})=\theta(\partial_{{\mathbf{x}}})(p)(\underline{\xi}),

which proves that
p\in\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
, hence
\mathrm{Ann}(\omega\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
is saturated.
Since
1\in\langle\langle{\omega}\rangle\rangle^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}
, then
p(\underline{\xi})=0
, i.e.
\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})\subseteq(x_{1}-\underline{\xi}_{1}x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}-\underline{\xi}_{n}x_{0})
, which proves that
\mathrm{Ann}(\omega^{d,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell})
is supported on
\underline{\xi}
.
∎

Example 5.3
.

Let us consider

and

.
Since

for

\rank_{\ell}(\omega)=6

is
6
.
By computing the kernel of the Hankel matrix in degree

\underline{I}:=\textup{Ann}(\omega^{3,\ell,{\mathbf{x}}}\,\mathbf{e}_{\ell})

\Big((x-y)^{3},(3x^{2}-6xy+3y^{2}+z^{2})z,(-3x+3y-z)z^{2},-8x^{2}y+16xy^{2}+2xyz-8y^{3}-2y^{2}z-2yz^{2}+3z^{3}\Big),

The elements

are such that

Let
\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{s}{\omega}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}_{i}({\mathbf{z}})\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}({\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbb{K}[[{\mathbf{z}}]]=R^{\ast}

The homogeneous ideal

has the following properties:

Lemma 5.4
.

The ideal
I_{\underline{\varphi}}
is saturated, zero-dimensional supported on

5.2.
GAD rank

For an homogeneous ideal
\underline{I}\subset\mathcal{S}
, we consider its graded quotient algebra
\underline{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
.
We will need the following (classical) lemma:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
d\geq\mathrm{reg}(\underline{\mathcal{A}})
,
v\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and assume that
\mathbf{M}_{v,d}:a\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d}\mapsto av\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d+1}
is bijective.
If
{B}
is a basis of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d}
, then
B
is a basis of
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{S}/(\underline{I},v-1)
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, with
v_{0}=v
.
If
d\geq\mathrm{reg}(\underline{I})
and
\mathbf{M}_{v,d}
invertible, then
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{M}_{v,d+k}:a\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d+k}\mapsto av\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d+k+1}
is invertible (see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
), hence
\mathbf{M}_{v^{k},d}:a\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d}\mapsto av^{k}\in\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d+k}
is also bijective.
Thus, if
B
is a basis of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{d}
, then

(19)

\mathcal{S}_{d+k}=v^{k}\langle B\rangle\oplus\underline{I}_{d+k}.

Let
\sigma:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}/(v-1),\,p\mapsto p(1,v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be the dehomogenization map, and let
I=\sigma(\underline{I})
and
B^{\sigma}=\sigma(B)
.
For any
p\in\mathcal{S}/(v-1)
, we consider its homogenization
q\in\mathcal{S}_{d^{\prime}}
in degree
d^{\prime}\geq d
.
Then
\sigma(q)=p
and
q\in v^{k}\langle B\rangle+\underline{I}_{d+k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies that
p\in\langle B^{\sigma}\rangle+I
.
This shows that
B^{\sigma}
is a generating set for
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{S}/(\underline{I},v-1)
.

We now show that the polynomials of
B^{\sigma}
are linearly independent in
\mathcal{A}
.
For
p\in\langle B^{\sigma}\rangle\cap I
, let
q\in\mathcal{S}_{d^{\prime}}
be its homogenization in degree
d^{\prime}\geq d
.
Since the sum
eq.

19
is direct, we conclude that
q=0
, therefore
p=\sigma(q)=0
.
This shows that
B^{\sigma}
is a basis of
\mathcal{A}=\underline{\mathcal{A}}/(v-1)
and concludes the proof.
∎

The algebra
\mathcal{A}=\underline{\mathcal{A}}/(v-1)
is the
localization
of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
at
v
, denoted
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{v^{*}}
, obtained by “substituting”
v
by
1
.

We now consider the graded algebra
\mathcal{S}/I_{\underline{\varphi}}
associated with the GAD
f=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\omega_{i}\,\ell_{i}^{d-k_{i}}
(the homogenized analogue of
Definition

We prove that, if its regularity is small enough, then the considered GAD is the unique minimal one for

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

. If
\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{S}/I_{\underline{\varphi}})\leq\min(d-c,c-1)

\rank_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{GAD}}(f)=\rank\mathbf{H}_{f^{*}}^{d-c,c}

and

Proof.

Let

be a minimal GAD, i.e.,

.
Since
I_{\underline{\varphi}}
is zero-dimensional (

) and

and

Hence, by

(resp.

Let

(resp.

(resp.

Therefore, by

4.12
, we have

.

Let us now consider another minimal GAD

.
We know by
eq.

9
that
(\varphi^{\prime})^{[\leq d]}=\check{f}
, hence

.

Thus, by

4.12
, we have

and

. Hence, we have

4.8

and

By

4.3
, we have
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}\}=\{\xi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\}

and up to a permutation,

We also deduce that
\mathcal{A}_{\xi_{i}}^{\ast}=\langle\langle{{\omega}_{i}^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}}\rangle\rangle\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}=\langle\langle{({\omega}^{\prime}_{i})^{d,\ell_{i},{\mathbf{x}}}}\rangle\rangle\,\mathbf{e}_{\xi_{i}}

Let

5.3.
Cactus rank

In this section, we consider projective schemes that are apolar to
f

, the minimal (by length) of such schemes is unique and of the form
I_{\underline{\varphi}}
, where the operator
\underline{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
arises from the minimal GAD of

Definition 5.7

(Cactus rank)
.

We define the

\underline{I}\subset\mathcal{S}

Such an apolar scheme
\underline{I}
of minimal length is called a

The name

[
17
]
and
[

]
,
and was previously known in the literature as

[

By

5.1
, we always have

We show hereafter that these ranks coincide under the regularity condition of

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
1\leq c\leq d
. If
\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{S}/I_{\underline{\varphi}})\leq\min(d-c,c-1)

Proof.

Consider the ideal
I_{\underline{\varphi}}

5.4

We now prove that

\underline{I}
be a minimal apolar scheme to

By

\underline{I}
is zero-dimensional Gorenstein.
By a generic change of coordinates, we can assume that

\mathcal{S}/\underline{I}
so that, for

By

\underline{I}

By

6.2.1.
Waring decomposition

We consider the following example to test the numerical accuracy of the
gad_decompose
algorithm in the Waring case (simple points).
The goal is to write a symmetric tensor
f\in\mathcal{S}_{4}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
as a sum of powers of linear forms, verifying the reconstruction accuracy.

Fix
n=2
,
d=4
, and consider variables
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}
, and consider the tensor

defined by

f=-3(x_{0}+2x_{1}+2x_{2})^{4}+(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})^{4}+(x_{0}+3x_{1}-x_{2})^{4}.

We work in the localization
\mathcal{S}_{x_{0}^{*}}

A^{\prime}=\{1,x_{1},x_{2}\}
and
A=\{1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}^{2}\}

of size
3\times 6
, a submatrix of the catalecticant
H_{\check{f}}^{1,3}
.
We deduce the rank

from the SVD of
\underline{H}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\lambda_{j}H^{A^{\prime},x_{j}A}
, and compute the multiplication matrices

of size
3\times 3
.
We then compute a Schur factorization

of

of size

\ell_{1}=-0.1744x_{0}-0.3488x_{1}-0.3488x_{2},\,\ell_{2}=-0.3255x_{0}-0.3255x_{1}-0.3255x_{2},\,\ell_{3}=-0.1399x_{0}-0.4196x_{1}+0.1399x_{2}

We compute the nil-indices [1,1,1], deduce the degrees

This example supports the efficiency and numerical reliability of the

6.2.3.
Random Tests

Hereafter, we provide a first coarse analysis of the numerical stability of the algorithm

For the clustering of points, we use agglomerative hierarchical clustering (AHC) with single linkage, based on the Euclidean distance between samples, following the classical scheme introduced by Johnson

For a fixed decomposition type specified by parameters

The resulting tensor

The perturbation magnitude

For each base tensor

For each perturbation level

The relative reconstruction error exhibits clean linear growth across nearly the entire range of

The decomposition process benefits from stable clustering and multiplicity detection in the case of simple points.
In particular, when the eigenvalue vector contains distinct and well-separated values, the clustering function identifies groups more efficiently, as the pairwise distance matrix displays a clear separation between clusters. This leads to a stable clustering structure.
Conversely, when many input values are very close or nearly identical, the algorithm must evaluate many small, similar distances, making cluster formation less clear and potentially leading to inefficient or unstable merges.
The experiment therefore suggests that
gad_decompose
maintains stable and predictable performance in the simple-point regime, with reduced sensitivity to random fluctuations.

The median curve grows almost linearly in the log–log scale, suggesting that the algorithm maintains stable behavior on average.
However, the maximum error shows pronounced fluctuations, with occasional peaks that deviate from the general trend.
These outliers are attributable to instabilities in internal steps of the decomposition, such as the estimation of multiplicities and the computation of nil-indices, which are sensitive to randomization and conditioning.
The minimum error curve, although more stable, also displays oscillations in the large-perturbation regime, suggesting that certain perturbations may produce configurations with a very low relative construction error.
Overall, this experiment shows that

Conclusions and Limitations

We provide a novel and global characterization of the zero-dimensional scheme associated with a Generalized Additive Decomposition of a form
f
, whose structure can be explicitly deduced from the GAD.
When the regularity of this apolar scheme is small enough, this is the unique minimal apolar scheme, and such a decomposition is the unique minimal GAD.
In this case, we demonstrate how to compute this minimal decomposition, using classical linear algebra tools, such as Schur factorization and eigen computations.

The numerical experiments demonstrate:

•

Near-optimal linear error growth in the low-perturbation regime.

•

Consistent recovery of multiplicities and decomposition structure under small noise.

•

Increased sensitivity for higher-order components and in higher-dimensional settings.

Critical parts of the algorithm include the computation of the rank via SVD and the clustering of eigenvalues, which directly affect the stability and accuracy of the decomposition.
We have identified the following limitations:

•

Breakdown of stability when the perturbation pushes the tensor too far from the suitable secant of the osculating varieties.

•

Occasional failure in identifying correct point multiplicities when the considered tensor has a high degree or a large ambient space.

These insights motivate future improvements, such as conditioning-aware modifications, eigenstructure stabilization, and alternative formulations to handle critical cases more robustly.

When the regularity of a minimal GAD is not small enough, the approach cannot be applied directly. We plan to investigate further moment extension techniques, such as
[
15
,
7
,
13
]
, and to analyze the associated moment extension variety in order to provide an efficient method for computing the GAD and Cactus ranks.

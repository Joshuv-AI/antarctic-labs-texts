Weak convergence of the integral of semi-Markov processes
Andrea Pedicone, Fabrizio Cinque Department of Statistical Sciences, Sapienza University of Rome, Italy

Abstract
We study the asymptotic properties, in the weak sense, of regenerative processes and Markov renewal processes. For the latter, we derive both renewal-type results, also concerning the related counting process, and ergodic-type ones, including the so-called phi-mixing property. This theoretical framework permits us to study the weak limit of the integral of a semi-Markov process, which can be interpret as the position of a particle moving with finite velocities taken for a random time according to the Markov renewal process underlying the semi-Markov one. Under mild conditions, we obtain the weak convergence to scaled Brownian motion. As a particular case, this result establishes the weak convergence of the classical generalized telegraph process.



Keywords:
Weak convergence; Semi-Markov processes; Markov renewal processes; Regenerative processes; Mixing sequences; Ergodic Theory

Primary 60F17, 60K15; Secondary 60F05

2
Limit theorems for regenerative processes

For a given a stochastic process
\eta=(\eta(t))_{t\geq 0}
, suppose that there exists a random time
\tau
such that the process
\big(\eta(t+\tau);t\geq 0\big)
has the same distribution as
\eta
and is independent of the past cycle
\big\{\big(\eta(t);0\leq t<\tau\big),\tau\big\}
. Then we say that time
\tau
is a regeneration epoch and
\eta
regenerates at time
\tau
. This means that the process restart as if it was time
t=0
again, and its future is independent of its past. But if such a
\tau
exists, then, since things start over again as if new, there must be a second time
\tau^{\prime}>\tau
yielding an identically distributed second cycle
\big\{(\eta(\tau+t);0\leq t<\tau^{\prime}-\tau\big),\tau^{\prime}-\tau\big\}
and so on. This probabilistic structure describes what is called regenerative process. Below we give the formal definition.

Definition 1
.

A stochastic process
\eta=(\eta(t))_{t\geq 0}
, defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathsf{P})
, is said to be regenerative if there exists a sequence
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\tau_{0}=0
, of stopping times with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
provided that
\{\tau_{m+1}-\tau_{m},(\eta(t+\tau_{m});\;\;t\in[0,\tau_{m+1}-\tau_{m}))\}_{m\geq 0}
forms a sequence of i.i.d. random elements.

The process
\eta
is delayed regenerative, if
\{\tau_{m+1}-\tau_{m},(\eta(t+\tau_{m});\;\;t\in[0,\tau_{m+1}-\tau_{m}))\}_{m\geq 1}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random elements independent of
(\tau_{1},\eta(t)_{t\in[0,\tau_{1})})
.

According to
[
4
]
page 298, another way to define a regenerative process is the following.

Definition 2
.

A stochastic process
\eta=(\eta(t))_{t\geq 0}
, defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathsf{P})
with state space
E
, is said to be regenerative provided that

(i)

there exists a sequence
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\tau_{0}=0
, of stopping times with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that forms a renewal sequence

(ii)

for any
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq t_{1}<...<t_{d}
and any measurable and bounded function
g:E^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

\mathsf{E}[g(\eta(t_{1}+\tau_{m}),...,\eta(t_{d}+\tau_{m}))|\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{m}}]=\mathsf{E}[g(\eta(t_{1}),...,\eta(t_{d}))].

The process
\eta
is delayed regenerative, if

(ia)

there exists a sequence
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\tau_{0}=0
, of stopping times with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that forms a delayed renewal sequence

(iia)

for any
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq t_{1}<...<t_{d}
and any measurable and bounded function
g:E^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

\mathsf{E}[g(\eta(t_{1}+\tau_{m}),...,\eta(t_{d}+\tau_{m}))|\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{m}}]=\mathsf{E}[g(\eta(t_{1}+\tau_{1}),...,\eta(t_{d}+\tau_{1}))].

Loosely speaking, a stochastic process is regenerative if there is a renewal process such that the segments of the process between successive renewal times are i.i.d.. While, a stochastic process is delayed regenerative if the first cycle has a distribution that is different from that of subsequent cycles.

The i.i.d. structure underlying a regenerative process allows us to establish classical limit theorems for sequence of random variables. Before doing so, we state the following two lemmas (which we will frequently use in our work) about convergence of randomly indexed sequences of random variables. As it will be shown, a random sequence indexed by a random variable has the same limit as the corresponding sequence indexed by a deterministic sequence, provided that the random index is ”asymptotically equivalent” to the deterministic index. The first lemma shows an invariance principle in
D[0,1]
(the space of real càdlàg function endowed with the Skorohod topology) of a random walk with random number of summands. The proof follows the argument of Theorem 14.4 in
[
2
]
and will be omitted.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of random variables with
\mathsf{E}[\eta_{k}]=0
,
\mathsf{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}<+\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the processes

\displaystyle X_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\eta_{k},\ \;\;t\in[0,1]

(2.1)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{n}\theta}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\nu_{nt}}\eta_{k},\ \;\;t\in[0,1]

(2.2)

where
\theta
is a positive constant,
\{a_{n}\}
is a positive divergent sequence and
(\nu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of integer random variables. If it holds that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\biggl|\frac{\nu_{nt}}{a_{n}}-\theta t\biggr|\Rightarrow 0,

(2.3)

then
X_{n}\Rightarrow W
on
D[0,1]
, implies
Y_{n}\Rightarrow W
on
D[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of identically distributed random variables with
\mathsf{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{p}]<+\infty
for some
p>0
. Let
(\nu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a family of integer random variables and suppose that there exist a positive divergent family
(a_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
\theta>0
such that

\frac{\nu_{t}}{a_{t}}\Rightarrow\theta.

(2.4)

Then,

\max_{k=1,...,\nu_{t}}\frac{\eta_{k}}{\sqrt[p]{a_{t}}}\Rightarrow 0.

(2.5)

Proof.

For every
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
we have that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{\max_{k=1,...,\nu_{t}}|\eta_{k}|>\epsilon\sqrt[p]{a_{t}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}\{\max_{k=1,...,\nu_{t}}|\eta_{k}|>\epsilon\sqrt[p]{a_{t}},|\nu_{t}-\theta^{-1}a_{t}|<\delta a_{t}\}+\mathsf{P}\{|\nu_{t}-\theta^{-1}a_{t}|\geq\delta a_{t}\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}\{\max_{k=1,...,\lfloor a_{t}(\theta^{-1}+\delta)\rfloor}|\eta_{k}|>\epsilon\sqrt[p]{a_{t}}\}+\mathsf{P}\{|\nu_{t}-\theta^{-1}a_{t}|\geq\delta a_{t}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lfloor a_{t}(\theta^{-1}+\delta)\rfloor}{\epsilon^{p}a_{t}}\mathsf{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{p}\mathsf{1}_{\{|\eta_{1}|\geq\epsilon\sqrt[p]{a_{t}}\}}]+\mathsf{P}\{|\nu_{t}-\theta^{-1}a_{t}|\geq\delta a_{t}\}.

Now, by applying the dominate convergence theorem, the first term of the previous inequality goes to zero, while the second one goes to zero by means of (
2.4
).
∎

The next statements concern the asymptotic behavior of regenerative sequences, showing a functional central limit theorem and a strong law of large numbers.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a delayed regenerative process with regeneration epochs
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\tau_{0}=0
, such that
\mathsf{E}[\tau_{m}^{2}]<+\infty
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If we define the random function

X_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\eta_{k}-\mu),\ \;\;t\in[0,1]

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{\mathsf{E}[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{\tau_{2}}\eta_{k}]}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]},

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}=\frac{\mathsf{E}[\bigl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{\tau_{2}}(\eta_{k}-\mu)\bigr)^{2}]}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]},

(2.7)

and, if we assume that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}

\mathsf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{m-1}+1}^{\tau_{m}}|\eta_{k}-\mu|\biggr)^{2}\biggr]<+\infty,

(2.8)

then, it holds that

X_{n}\Rightarrow W

(2.9)

in the space
D[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let us define

R_{m}=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{m-1}+1}^{\tau_{m}}\frac{(\eta_{k}-\mu)}{\sigma},\ \;\;m\in\mathbb{N}.

By the regenerative property,
\{R_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
defines a sequence of independent random variables that are identically distributed for
m\geq 2
. We have that
\mathsf{E}[R^{2}_{1}]<+\infty
,
\mathsf{E}[R_{m}]=0
and
\mathsf{E}[R_{m}^{2}]=\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]<+\infty
, for every
m\geq 2
, by (
2.8
). Let us also denote with
\nu_{n}=\max\{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\tau_{m}\leq n\}
. Then, the process

Y_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sumop\displaylimits_{m=2}^{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}R_{m},\ \;\;t\in[0,1]

is a random walk with i.i.d. increments and a random number of summands. It follows that the process
X_{n}(t)
can be reformulated as

X_{n}(t)=\frac{R_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}+Y_{n}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}+1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\eta_{k}-\mu).

Now, the inter-arrivals time
\zeta_{m}=\tau_{m}-\tau_{m-1}
are independent for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and identically distributed for
m\geq 2
, with
\mathsf{E}[\zeta^{2}_{m}]<+\infty
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
by assumption. Then, consider the random walk built on
\{\zeta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
,

Z_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\mathsf{V}[\tau_{1}]}}(\zeta_{1}-\mathsf{E}[\tau_{1}])+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\mathsf{V}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=2}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\zeta_{k}-\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]),\ \;\;t\in[0,1].

By applying the Borel-Cantelli lemma, the first term converges almost surely to zero, and hence, by Donsker’s Theorem (Theorem 14.1
[
2
]
), it holds
Z_{n}\Rightarrow W
in
D[0,1]
. From this, with Theorem 14.6 of
[
2
]
at hand, we have that the process
\bar{\nu}_{n}

\bar{\nu}_{n}(t)=\frac{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]^{-1}nt}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathsf{V}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]\sqrt{n}},\ \;\;t\in[0,1]

is such that
\bar{\nu}_{n}\Rightarrow W
in
D[0,1]
. Moreover, by the continuous mapping theorem, we can obtain a uniform basic renewal theorem

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\biggl|\frac{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{n}-\frac{t}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]}\biggr|\Rightarrow 0.

(2.10)

Hence, we use once again Donsker’s Theorem to prove that

\hat{Y}_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sumop\displaylimits_{m=2}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}R_{m},\ \;\;t\in[0,1],

(2.11)

satisfies
\hat{Y}_{n}\Rightarrow W
, and then by applying Lemma
2.1
, it follows
Y_{n}\Rightarrow W
in
D[0,1]
.

It remains to prove that

\frac{R_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}+\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\biggl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}+1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\eta_{k}-\mu)\biggr|\Rightarrow 0.

As before, the first term converges almost surely to zero by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, hence we focus on the second one. We have the following inequalities

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\biggl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}+1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\eta_{k}-\mu)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}+1}^{\tau_{\nu_{\lfloor nt\rfloor+1}}}|\eta_{k}-\mu|\biggr)

\displaystyle=\max_{m=0,...,\nu_{n}}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{m}+1}^{\tau_{m+1}}|\eta_{k}-\mu|\biggr).

Assumption (
2.8
) and result (
2.10

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a delayed regenerative process with regeneration epochs
\{\tau_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\tau_{0}=0
, such that
\mathsf{E}[\tau_{m}]<+\infty
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathsf{E}\biggl[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{m-1}+1}^{\tau_{m}}|\eta_{k}|\biggr]<+\infty.

(2.12)

Then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}=\frac{\mathsf{E}[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{\tau_{2}}\eta_{k}]}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]}\;\;a.s..

(2.13)

Proof.

By assumption,
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
forms a delayed renewal process. Then, the the basic renewal theorem implies

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\nu_{n}}{n}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]}\;\;a.s.

(2.14)

Now, the normalized partial sum of
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{k}=\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\tau_{1}}\eta_{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{m=2}^{\nu_{n}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\;\;k=\tau_{m-1}+1}^{\tau_{m}}\eta_{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{\nu_{n}}+1}^{n}\eta_{k}.

By the regenerative property, for every

, the r.v.s
\sumop\displaylimits_{m=1}^{\nu_{n}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{m-1}+1}^{\tau_{m}}\eta_{k}
, define a sequence of independent random variables that are identically distributed for

and the first Borel-Cantelli lemma together with (

3
Limit theorems for Markov renewal processes

In this section, we briefly recall the definition and the main properties of Markov renewal processes. These processes can be thought as a model for the motion of a particle switching from one state to another with random sojourn times in between; the successive states visited form a Markov chain, and the distribution of the sojourn time depends on both the current state and the next state to be entered. Thus, a Markov renewal process generalizes a Markov process by allowing sojourn times that are not necessarily exponentially distributed and may also depend on the next state.

Definition 3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathsf{P})
a probability space and
(\hat{V},S)=\{\hat{V}_{k},S_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a stochastic process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathsf{P})
, where
\hat{V}_{k}
takes values in a countable set
\mathcal{V}
and
S_{k}
are random variables such that
S_{k+1}>S_{k}
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
S_{0}=0
. Denote with
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
the natural filtration associated to the process
(\hat{V},S)
. Then
(\hat{V},S)
is said to be a Markov renewal process with state space
\mathcal{V}
provided that

\mathsf{P}\{\hat{V}_{k+1}=v,S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\mathcal{F}_{k}\}=\mathsf{P}\{\hat{V}_{k+1}=v,S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k}\},\ \;\;v\in\mathcal{V},\;t\geq 0,\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.1)

We always assume that the Markov renewal process
(\hat{V},S)
is time-homogeneous. That is, for any
w,v\in\mathcal{V}
,
t\geq 0
,

\mathsf{P}\{\hat{V}_{k+1}=w,S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k}=v\}=Q_{vw}(t).

(3.2)

From the definition it follows that
\{\hat{V}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Markov-chain with state space
\mathcal{V}
and transition matrix
P=\{p_{vw};\;v,w\in\mathcal{V}\}
, where

p_{vw}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}Q_{vw}(t).

(3.3)

The family of probabilities
Q=(Q_{wv}(t);\;w,v\in\mathcal{V},t\geq 0)
is called a semi-Markov kernel over
\mathcal{V}
. Moreover, we define the following quantities

\displaystyle F_{wv}(t)=\mathsf{P}\{S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k+1}=v,\hat{V}_{k}=w\}=\frac{Q_{wv}(t)}{p_{wv}}

(3.4)

Theorem 3.3

.

Let
(\hat{V},S)

4
Weak convergence of the integral of semi-Markov process

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathsf{P})
be a probability space where is defined
(\hat{V},S):\Omega\to E^{\infty}
a homogeneous Markov renewal process with semi-Markov kernel
Q
, where
E^{\infty}
is the product space of
E=\mathcal{V}\times[0,+\infty)
, with
\mathcal{V}
the finite state space of
\hat{V}
. We endow
E
with the
\sigma
-field generated by the cylinder sets
\mathcal{C}
. We assume that the finite Markov chain
\hat{V}
is irreducible and aperiodic with unique invariant measure
\pi
. Let
\xi:\Omega\to[0,+\infty)^{\infty}
defined as
\xi_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k-1}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\xi_{0}=0
. Let
N:\Omega\to D[0,+\infty)
be the counting process associated to
S
,
N(t)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:S_{k}\leq t\}

t\geq 0
, where
D[0,+\infty)
is the space of cadlag functions defined on
[0,+\infty)
endowed by the Skorohod topology, and
V:\Omega\to D[0,+\infty)
the semi-Markov process associated to
(\hat{V},S)
,
i.e.

V(t)=\hat{V}_{N(t)}
. Let
X:\Omega\to C[0,+\infty)
be the integral of
V
, where
C[0,+\infty)
is the space of continuous functions on
[0,+\infty)
endowed with the topology of the uniform metric,

X(t)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}V(s)ds,\ \;\;t\geq 0.

(4.1)

The stochastic process
X
can be thought as the position of a particle moving with velocity
V
. This means that the motion performs displacements of velocity given by
\hat{V}
for a random amount of time of length determined by
\xi
that depends on both the current velocity and the following one. Hence, each element of the sequence of random variables
\{\hat{V}_{k-1},\xi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
contains the information about the velocity of the
k
-th displacement and the duration of the time interval in which the motion moves according to this velocity.

By observing that the time interval
[0,t]
can be partitioned with respect to the sequence of the arrival times
S
, we obtain the following equivalent representation of
X

X(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{N(t)}\hat{V}_{k-1}(S_{k}-S_{k-1})+\hat{V}_{N(t)}(t-S_{N(t)}),\ \;\;t\geq 0.

(4.2)

The aim of the present section is to identify conditions under which
X
admits a weak limit. It turns out that the suitable reparameterization of
V
is the following. By introducing a parameter
\lambda>0
, we define the scaled counting process
N_{\lambda}=(N_{\lambda}(t))_{t\geq 0}
as

N_{\lambda}(t)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\lambda^{-1}S_{k}\leq t\}=N(\lambda t).

(4.3)

Then, we normalize the semi-Markov process
V(\lambda t)
associated to
N(\lambda t)
by defining the new process
V_{\lambda}=(V_{\lambda}(t))_{t\geq 0}
as

V_{\lambda}(t)=\sqrt{\lambda}(V(\lambda t)-\theta),\ \;\;t\geq 0,

(4.4)

where, according to (
3.33
), the parameter
\theta
is the limit as
\lambda\to+\infty
of the mean of
V(\lambda t)

\theta=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathsf{E}[V(\lambda t)]=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[\hat{V}_{0}S_{1}]}{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[S_{1}]}.

(4.5)

The corresponding integral of the normalized semi-Markov process
V_{\lambda}
is denoted as
X_{\lambda}
and takes the form

X_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\lambda t}(V(s)-\theta)ds=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}(X(\lambda t)-\theta\lambda t),\ \;\;t\geq 0,

(4.6)

which can also be expressed in the equivalent form

X_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{N(\lambda t)}(\hat{V}_{k-1}-\theta)(S_{k}-S_{k-1})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}(\hat{V}_{N(\lambda t)}-\theta)(\lambda t-S_{N(\lambda t)}),\ \;\;t\geq 0.

(4.7)

The underlying idea of the normalization introduced in equation (
4.4
) is to ensure that in (
4.7
) appears a sum of random variables having null mean (assuming that
\hat{V}_{0}
has distribution
\pi
) with the classical central limit type scaling. From a physical point of view the above reparameterization guarantees that the number of changes of direction grow at the order of the square-root of the velocity. Indeed, as equation (
4.3
) shows, the number of total changes of velocity in a unit time interval increases as
\lambda
. On the other hand, equation (
4.4
) provides that the normalized velocity grows as
\sqrt{\lambda}
. This is the standard equilibrium condition allowing the convergence of the telegraph equation to the heat equation. Heuristically, this condition has the same meaning to the one that arises in the derivation of Brownian motion as limit of a random walk. Indeed, to obtain Brownian motion, a particle undergoing a random walk must perform a number of displacements that decay at the same order as the square root of their lengths (see
[
25
]
sections 1.1 and 1.2).

Having introduced the proper normalization, our task is to show the weak convergence of
X_{\lambda}
as
\lambda\to+\infty
to a scaled Brownian motion in the space
C[0,+\infty)
.

In equation (
4.7
) we can see that
X_{\lambda}
can be decomposed into the sum of a random walk with a random number of summands and an interpolating term, which is proportional to the residual life of the counting process
N_{\lambda}
. The increase in the number of changes in direction results in arrival times occurring more frequently, which informally means that every instant becomes an arrival time. This behavior is formally proved by Theorem
3.6
, which shows that,

\sqrt{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{S_{N(\lambda t)}}{\lambda}-t\bigg)=\frac{S_{N(\lambda t)}-\lambda t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\Rightarrow 0.

In worlds, the difference between the current time
t
and the instant of the normalized last arrival time
\lambda^{-1}S_{N(\lambda t)}
approaches to zero faster than
\lambda^{-1/2}
. This result allows us to study the asymptotic law of
X_{\lambda}(t)
through the law of
X_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-1}S_{N(\lambda t)})
, which is a random walk where random number of steps is enumerated by the counting process
N(\lambda t)
. Hence, we define the random function on
D[0,+\infty)
,
Y_{\lambda}=(Y_{\lambda}(t))_{t\geq 0}

Y_{\lambda}(t)=X_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-1}S_{N(\lambda t)})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{N(\lambda t)}(\hat{V}_{k-1}-\theta)(S_{k}-S_{k-1}),\ \;\;t\geq 0.

(4.8)

5
Weak convergence of the integral of an alternating renewal process

Let
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},...,v_{m}\}
and define
v_{rm+i}\coloneqq v_{i}
for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
i=1,...,m
. An alternating renewal process is a particular case of a semi-Markov process in which a particle deterministically moves from state
v_{k}
to state
v_{k+1}
and the cycle restarts after
m
steps. This means that the Markov renewal-kernel is of the form

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{\hat{V}_{k+1}=v_{j},S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k}=v_{i}\}=Q_{v_{i}v_{j}}(t)=\mathsf{1}_{\{v_{i+1}\}}(v_{j})F_{v_{i}v_{j}}(t)

(5.1)

for every
i,j=1,...,m
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
t\geq 0
. From the definition, it follows that the embedded Markov chain
\hat{V}
is periodic with period equals to
m
and the transition probabilities are given by
p_{v_{i}v_{j}}=\mathsf{1}_{\{v_{i+1}\}}(v_{j})
, while the invariant distribution is given by
\pi_{v_{j}}=m^{-1}
,
j=1,...,m
. Moreover it holds that

\mathsf{P}\{S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k}=v_{i},\hat{V}_{k+1}=v_{i+1}\}=F_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}(t)=\mathsf{P}\{S_{k+1}-S_{k}\leq t|\hat{V}_{k}=v_{i}\}=Q_{v_{i}\cdot}(t).

We note that, if
\mathsf{P}\{\hat{V}_{0}=v_{1}\}=1
, then
\{\hat{V}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
becomes a deterministic sequence. Indeed,
\mathsf{P}\{V_{k}=v_{i}\}=1
if there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
k=rm+i-1
,
i=1,...,m
and, by letting with
\xi_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k-1}
, the random variables forming the sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are independent and satisfies
\xi_{rm+i}=_{d}\xi_{i}
.

The alternating renewal process
V=(V(t))_{t\geq 0}
based on
\{\hat{V}_{k},S_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
can be written down as

V(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\hat{V}_{k-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{r\geq 0}\mathsf{1}_{\{rm+k-1\}}(N(t)),

(5.2)

where
N
is the counting process related to
S
and defined in (
3.6
).

Let us denote by
\mu_{i}=\mathsf{E}[\xi_{1}|\hat{V}_{0}=v_{i}]
and
\sigma^{2}_{i}=\mathsf{V}[\xi_{1}|\hat{V}_{0}=v_{i}]
. Then,

\theta=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[\hat{V}_{0}S_{1}]}{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[S_{1}]}=\frac{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i}}{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}},

(5.3)

and in this case the integral of
V_{\lambda}(t)=\sqrt{\lambda}(V(\lambda t)-\theta)
can be written down as

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}(\hat{V}_{k-1}-\theta)\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\sumop\displaylimits_{r\geq 0}\mathsf{1}_{\{rm+k-1\}}(N(\lambda s))ds

(5.4)

or also, as it is done in (
4.7
),

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{N(\lambda t)}(\hat{V}_{k-1}-\theta)\xi_{k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}(\hat{V}_{N(\lambda t)}-\theta)\biggl(\lambda t-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{N(\lambda t)}\xi_{k}\biggr).

(5.5)

Let us denote with

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i},

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}=\frac{1}{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{2}_{i}\biggl(v_{i}-\frac{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i}}{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}}\biggr)^{2}.

(5.6)

Since the alternating renewal process is periodic, we cannot direct apply Theorem
4.3
. The next statement provides the conditions for the weak convergence in this new framework.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
V=(V(t))_{t\geq 0}
be an alternating renewal process with state space
\mathcal{V}
and assume that
\mathsf{E}[S_{1}^{2}|\hat{V}_{0}=v]<+\infty
for every
v\in\mathcal{V}
. Then, the process
X_{\lambda}=(X_{\lambda}(t))_{t\geq 0}
defined by

X_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\lambda t}(V(s)-\theta)ds,

(5.7)

where
\theta
is defined in (
5.3
),
satisfies the following weak limit in
C[0,+\infty)

X_{\lambda}\Rightarrow\mu^{-1/2}\gamma W

(5.8)

where
\mu
and
\gamma
are provided in (
5.6
).

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
, the sequence
\{(V_{k-1},\xi_{k})\}_{k\geq 1}
forms a delayed regenerative process with regeneration epochs
\tau_{n}=\inf\{k>\tau_{n-1}:V_{k}=v_{1}\}
, which satisfy
\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}=m
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
3
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{E}\biggl[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{n}+1}^{\tau_{n+1}}V_{k-1}\xi_{k}\biggr]}{\mathsf{E}[\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]}=\frac{1}{m}\mathsf{E}\biggl[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\tau_{1}}V_{k-1}\xi_{k}\bigg|V_{0}=v_{1}\biggr]=\frac{1}{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}v_{k}\mathsf{E}[\xi_{k}|V_{0}=v_{1}]=\frac{1}{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i},

from which we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\biggl[\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{\tau_{2}}(V_{k-1}-\theta)\xi_{k}\biggr]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i}-\frac{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i}}{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}=0.

(5.9)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}

\displaystyle\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{n}+1}^{\tau_{n+1}}|V_{k-1}-\theta|\xi_{k}\biggr)^{2}\biggr]=\mathsf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\tau_{1}}|V_{k-1}-\theta|\xi_{k}\biggr)^{2}\bigg|\hat{V}_{0}=v_{1}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i=1,...,m}(v_{i}-\theta)^{2}\mathsf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\xi_{k}\biggl)^{2}\bigg|V_{0}=v_{1}\biggr]=\max_{i=1,...,m}(v_{i}-\theta)^{2}\bigg[\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{2}_{i}+\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]<+\infty

Therefore, from Theorem
2.1
it follows that the process
\hat{Y}_{\lambda}
defined as

\hat{Y}_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\lfloor\lambda t\rfloor}(V_{k-1}-\theta)\xi_{k},\ \;\;t\in[0,T]

satisfies
\hat{Y}_{\lambda}\Rightarrow\gamma W
on
D[0,T]
, where, from (
2.7
), and by keeping in mind (
5.9
),

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\mathsf{E}[\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}]\big)^{-1}\mathsf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=\tau_{1}+1}^{\tau_{2}}(V_{k-1}-\theta)\xi_{k}\biggr)^{2}\biggr]=\frac{1}{m}\mathsf{E}\biggl[\biggl(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\tau_{1}}(V_{k-1}-\theta)\xi_{k}\biggr)^{2}\bigg|\hat{V}_{0}=v_{1}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{2}_{i}\biggl(v_{i}-\frac{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\mu_{i}}{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}}\biggr)^{2}.

Now, Theorem
3.5
implies that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\biggl|\frac{N(\lambda t)}{\lambda}-\frac{tm}{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}}\biggr|\Rightarrow 0.

Thus, we can use Lemma
2.1
together with Theorem
3.6
, to conclude that

X_{\lambda}\Rightarrow\mu^{-1/2}\gamma W

on
D[0,T]
. By using the same arguments of proof of Theorem
4.3
we obtain the thesis.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

If, according to Remark

, we consider the alternative normalization

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\lambda t}\mathsf{E}[V(s)]ds+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\lambda t}(V(s)-\theta)ds=\lambda^{-1}\mathsf{E}[X(\lambda t)]+\lambda^{-1/2}(X(\lambda t)-\theta\lambda t)

5.1
Application to the generalized telegraph process

Here we show an application of the previous results to the asymmetric telegraph process (see
[
1
,
5
,
23
]
).

Let
(\hat{V},S)
be an alternating renewal process where
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
, with
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and

Q_{v_{i}v_{j}}(t)=\mathsf{1}_{\{v_{i+1}\}}(v_{j})(1-e^{-\lambda_{i}t}).

(5.12)

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{V_{k}=v_{i},S_{k}-S_{k-1}\leq t|V_{k-1}=v_{i-1}\}=\mathsf{P}\{S_{k}-S_{k-1}\leq t|V_{k-1}=v_{i-1}\}=\begin{cases}1-e^{-\lambda_{1}t},&v_{i-1}=v_{1}\\
1-e^{-\lambda_{2}t},&v_{i-1}=v_{2}.\end{cases}

If we set
N(t)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:S_{k}\leq t\}
, then
N=(N(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the alternating Poisson process. It can be proved that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{N(t)=n|V(0)=v_{1}\}=\begin{cases}(\lambda_{1}t)^{k}(\lambda_{2}t)^{k}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}W_{k,k+1}\bigl(t(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\bigr),&n=2k\\
(\lambda_{1}t)^{k+1}(\lambda_{2}t)^{k}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}W_{k+1,k+1}\bigl(t(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\bigr),&n=2k+1\end{cases}

where
W_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta))^{-1}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1}e^{-xt}dt
.

Let
V(t)=V_{N(t)}
, from (
5.12
), it follows that
V
is a Markov process with generator

A=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{2}&-\lambda_{2}\end{pmatrix},

then, Kolmogorov’s forward equation can be used to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{V(t)=v|V(0)=v_{1}\}=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{1}\\
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{2}\end{cases}

(5.13)

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{V(t)=v|V(0)=v_{2}\}=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{1}\\
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{2}.\end{cases}

(5.14)

If
(p,1-p)
is the initial distribution of
V(0)
, then the law of
V
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{V(t)=v\}=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}+\frac{p\lambda_{1}-(1-p)\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{1}\\
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-\frac{p\lambda_{1}-(1-p)\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t},&v=v_{2}\end{cases}

(5.15)

from which we obtain

\mathsf{E}[V(t)]=\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}+\frac{(p\lambda_{1}-(1-p)\lambda_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t}

(5.16)

and

\theta=\frac{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[\xi_{1}V_{0}]}{\mathsf{E}_{\pi}[\xi_{1}]}=\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}.

(5.17)

Let
X
be the integral of
V
, then the density of
X(t)

satisfies the following hyperbolic equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+(v_{1}+v_{2})\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x\partial t}+(v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1})\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=-v_{1}v_{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}.

(5.18)

In this case,

can be interpreted as the trajectory of a particle moving with constant velocities

and subject to reversals of direction at the jump times of the alternating Poisson process of rates
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
. Therefore the mean length of each displacement is

and
1/\lambda_{2}
according to the current speed.

Then
\bar{X}_{\lambda}
, the integral of
V
under the reparametrization of Remark
5.1
, becomes

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{(p\lambda_{1}-(1-p)\lambda_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}{\lambda(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}}(1-e^{-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\lambda t})\!+\!\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\lambda}\biggl(\!V(\lambda s)-\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\bigr)\!\!\biggr)ds.

(5.19)

If we define the process
T_{\lambda}=(T_{\lambda}(t))_{t\geq 0}

T_{\lambda}(t)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\lambda}\biggl(V(\lambda s)-\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\bigr)\biggr)ds,

then

, and so

have the same weak limit. Now,

is an asymmetric telegraph process with rates
\lambda\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda\lambda_{2}
and velocities

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda}(v_{1}-\theta)+\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}\lambda_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})+v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda}(v_{2}-\theta)+\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}\lambda_{2}(v_{2}-v_{1})+v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}.

We note that, in the limit, the two velocities become of opposite signs. Thus, according to Remark
5.1
, we can conclude that an asymmetric telegraph process

with those parameters converges weakly to Brownian motion with drift and scaling parameter respectively given by

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\mu}=2\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}}{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{3}}.

Moreover, from (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\biggl(\frac{2(v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1})}{\lambda(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}}+\frac{(v_{2}-v_{1})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})}{\sqrt{\lambda}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}}\biggr)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x\partial t}+\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}}{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{3}}-\frac{(v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}{\sqrt{\lambda}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}}-\frac{(v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1})^{2}}{\lambda(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{3}}\biggr)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}},

(5.21)

and, as

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{v_{1}\lambda_{2}+v_{2}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}}{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{3}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}},

(5.22)

which is the equation governing the law of Brownian motion with drift

and scaling parameter
\mu^{-1/2}\gamma

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{V(t)=v\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}+(p-\frac{1}{2})e^{-2\lambda_{0}t},&v=v_{0}\\

(5.24)

is a telegraph process with modulus of velocity

and satisfies

. Furthermore, the density of
T_{\lambda}(t)

is a solution of the telegraph equation

\frac{1}{2\lambda\lambda_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}

(5.25)

and by taking the limit as
\lambda\to+\infty
the heat equation emerges

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}.

(5.26)

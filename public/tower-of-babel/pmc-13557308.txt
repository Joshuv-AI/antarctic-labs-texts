Clinical and Dermoscopic Features of Genital Porokeratosis: A Report of Four Cases
Authors: Swale Iftikhar, Divyanshu Srivastava, Durga Prasad Dubey, Vinayak Upadhyay, Rajkumar, Anil Kumar Gupta
Published: 2026

Introduction

Porokeratosis (PK) can affect any body area, but genital involvement is rare and considered a distinct clinical form with few documented cases. The scrotum is the most commonly affected site, followed by the penis and vulva. Adjacent areas like the buttocks, natal cleft, groins, and upper thighs may also be involved [1,2]. We report four cases of porokeratosis in the genital and nearby regions, highlighting their dermoscopic features.

Case Presentation

Four male patients aged 25 to 36 years presented to our dermatology department with annular, keratotic lesions involving the genital and adjacent areas, including the scrotum, penile shaft, inguinal region, and thighs. The lesion duration ranged from 1 to 2.5 years, with the number of lesions varying from a solitary plaque to nine. Clinical details are summarized in Table 1. Case 1 showed multiple hyperpigmented annular plaques with well-defined raised margins on the scrotum (Figure 1, A and B). Case 2 had a solitary annular plaque with a raised keratin rim on the penile shaft (Figure 1C). Case 3 exhibited multiple annular plaques with raised borders over the scrotum, inguinal region, and thighs (Figure 1D). Case 4 presented annular plaques with thread-like raised borders and central atrophy on the penile shaft and inguinal area (Figure 1E). Pruritus was reported by Cases 1, 3, and 4, while Case 2 was asymptomatic. Examination of other skin sites and mucosae was normal in all patients, with no regional lymphadenopathy or signs of sexually transmitted infections. Systemic examinations and routine lab tests were unremarkable; serology for VDRL and HIV was negative. No family history was reported.

Dermoscopy in all patients revealed a white to brown annular, double-edged, continuous keratin rim (Figure 2,A–D). Ultraviolet fluorescence dermoscopy (UVFD) performed in Case 4 showed bright bluish fluorescence of the keratin rim (Figure 2E). Histopathological examination of biopsies from the keratotic rims demonstrated hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, and characteristic cornoid lamellae (Figure 2, F and G). Based on the clinical appearance, dermoscopic features, and histopathological findings, a diagnosis of genital PK was confirmed in all patients.

Conclusion

Genital PK, on dermoscopy, shows a characteristic peripheral white track, corresponding to the cornoid lamella [3]. UVFD highlights the keratin rim with bright blue fluorescence, forming a “diamond necklace” pattern [4].

Malignant transformation has been reported in 7–12% of PK cases. However, there have been no reports of malignant change occurring in porokeratosis localized to the genital area [2].

Genital PK is a rare and underrecognized condition that can mimic annular lichen planus, annular syphilide, psoriasis, Bowen’s disease, and granuloma annulare. Dermoscopy aids diagnosis, but histopathological confirmation is essential. Treatment is challenging, as topical agents, including corticosteroids, 5% 5-fluorouracil, tretinoin, and imiquimod, along with lasers and surgical excision, often produce limited and inconsistent results [2]. Due to the potential risk of malignant transformation, regular monitoring is recommended.

Magneto-thermally Coupled Field Simulation of Homogenized Foil Winding Models
Silas Weinert, Jonas Bundschuh, Yvonne Späck-Leigsnering, Herbert De Gersem

Abstract
Foil windings have, due to their layered structure, different properties than conventional wire windings, which make them advantageous for high frequency applications. Both electromagnetic and thermal analyses are relevant for foil windings. These two physical areas are coupled through Joule losses and temperature dependent material properties. For an efficient simulation of foil windings, homogenization techniques are used to avoid resolving the single turns. Therefore, this paper comprises a coupled magneto-thermal simulation that uses a homogenization method in the electromagnetic and thermal part. A weak coupling with different time step sizes for both parts is presented. The method is verified on a simple geometry and showcased for a pot transformer that uses a foil and a wire winding.



I
Introduction

Besides traditional wire or litz wire windings, foil windings are an alternative that are used in various application areas, for example in inductors
[
1
]
or in transformers
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
that are used in DC-DC converters
[
5
,
6
]
.
As the name suggests, a foil winding is built by winding a foil instead of a wire.
This gives foil windings unique properties.
Compared to wire windings, they are easier and cheaper to construct
[
7
,
8
]
, have better mechanical properties
[
1
]
and exhibit a lower direct current resistance
[
9
]
.
Furthermore, foil windings have better thermal properties
[
10
,
11
]
because the foil acts as a highly thermally conductive connection from the interior to the surface and thus allows an efficient cooling.
In contrast to that, the path from the interior to the surface is thermally insulated in wire windings, which makes cooling more difficult.

In the design of applications, not only the electromagnetic behavior but also the thermal behavior of the foil winding is of importance
[
12
]
.
Both field aspects can be investigated with moderate effort using field simulation.
Because of the layered structure of a foil winding with its very thin turns, a brute force simulation approach leads to unmanageably large models and long simulation times
[
13
]
.
To mitigate this, there exist special homogenization models for electromagnetic simulations that avoid the resolution of every single turn of the foil winding but rather resolve the foil winding as a whole
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
For the thermal simulation, a similar homogenization approach has to be used to also avoid resolving the turns
[
12
]
.

The electromagnetic and the thermal simulations are, however, not independent from each other.
The Joule losses from the electromagnetic simulation act as heat source for the thermal simulation, and the temperature influences the material parameters of the electromagnetic simulation.
For that reason, this paper presents a magneto-thermally coupled simulation of foil windings that uses a homogenization model for the foil windings in both the electromagnetic and the thermal problem.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Sec.
II
, a foil winding is briefly defined and its description is presented.
The multi-physical problem is presented in Sec.
III
.
This includes the description of the magnetoquasistatic and the thermal problem, the homogenization of the materials in the foil winding and the weak coupling scheme.
The obtained equations are discretized in Sec.
IV
.
In Sec.
V
, the method is verified for a simple geometry, and in Sec.
VI
, a more realistic example of a pot transformer is analyzed.
Finally, in Sec.
VII
, a conclusion is drawn.

III
Multi-physics problem setting

III-A

Magnetoquasistatic Sub-Problem

The
\vec{A}
-
\phi
-formulation of the magnetoquasistatic subset of Maxwell’s equations is used, with the magnetic vector potential
\vec{A}(\vec{r},t)
and electric scalar potential
\phi(\vec{r},t)
.
The formulation reads

\curl\left(\nu\curl\vec{A}\right)+\sigma\partial_{t}\vec{A}+\sigma\grad\phi=\vec{J}_{\mathrm{s}},

(2)

with the electric conductivity
\sigma
, the reluctivity
\nu
and the source current density
\vec{J}_{\mathrm{s}}
.

The homogenized foil winding model was introduced in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Here, we use the version derived in
[
17
,
16
]
.
Therein, the voltage drop along the foil winding that is given by an external circuit is described with the
voltage function

\Phi(\vec{r},t)
that depends only on
\alpha
in the local coordinate system and on time, i.e.,
\hat{\Phi}=\hat{\Phi}(\alpha,t)
.
Multiplied with a
distribution function

\vec{\zeta}(\vec{r})

[
18
]
, the relation
-\grad\phi=\Phi\vec{\zeta}
holds.
The
\vec{A}
-
\phi
-formulation with the homogenized foil winding model on the computational domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given as
[
16
]

\displaystyle\curl\left(\nu\curl\vec{A}\right)+\sigma\partial_{t}\vec{A}-\sigma\Phi\vec{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\vec{J}_{\mathrm{s}}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(3a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(\alpha)}\sigma\left(-\partial_{t}\vec{A}+\Phi\vec{\zeta}\right)\cdot\vec{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\frac{I}{b}

\displaystyle\alpha\text{ in }L_{\alpha},

(3b)

together with appropriate initial and boundary conditions.
The initial conditions must be consistent to the formulation and to the external circuit.
As boundary conditions, the tangential components of the magnetic vector potential are forced to zero, which ensures that all magnetic flux is confined within the field model part and thus no further voltages are induced in the external circuit part.

The model ensures that the current
I
flows through each individual turn of the foil winding.
The voltage drop over the foil winding is given by
[
16
]

V=\frac{1}{b}\int_{L_{\alpha}}\hat{\Phi}(\alpha)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(4)

III-B

Thermal Sub-Problem

Heat transfer is described by the transient heat conduction equation as
[
19
]

-\Div\left(\kappa\grad T\right)+c_{\mathrm{v}}\partial_{t}T=q_{\mathrm{v}},

(5)

with the temperature
T
, the volumetric heat capacity
c_{\mathrm{v}}
, the thermal conductivity
\kappa
, and the volumetric heat source
q_{\mathrm{v}}
.
The heat dissipation through the boundary can be described by the Robin boundary condition

\kappa\vec{n}\cdot\grad T+h(T-T_{\mathrm{amb}})=0,

(6)

where
\vec{n}
is the outward pointing normal vector at the boundary,
h
is the convection coefficient, and
T_{\mathrm{amb}}
is the reference ambient temperature.
The limits
h\rightarrow\infty
and
h=0
correspond to a homogeneous Dirichlet and Neumann boundary condition, respectively.

III-C

Material Homogenization

The homogenized foil winding model does not resolve the single turns in the foil winding domain.
Instead, the foil winding domain is replaced with an artificial homogenized material.
Its material properties are determined from the properties and dimensions of the conducting and insulating material.
With respect to the local coordinate system, the material properties are diagonal tensors
[
16
]
, i.e.,

\hat{m}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{\perp}&0&0\\
0&m_{\parallel}&0\\
0&0&m_{\parallel}\end{pmatrix},

(7)

for a general material property
\hat{m}
, where
m\in\left\{\nu,\sigma,\kappa\right\}
.
The material property perpendicular to the turns is denoted
m_{\perp}
and parallel to the turns
m_{\parallel}
.
Inside the foil winding domain the material properties are homogenized by mixing rules
[
20
,
19
]
as

\displaystyle\nu_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\lambda\nu_{\mathrm{c}}+(1-\lambda)\nu_{\mathrm{i}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\parallel}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\nu_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1-\lambda}{\nu_{\mathrm{i}}},

(8a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1-\lambda}{\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sigma_{\mathrm{c}}+(1-\lambda)\sigma_{\mathrm{i}},

(8b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}}+\frac{1-\lambda}{\kappa_{\mathrm{i}}},

\displaystyle\kappa_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\lambda\kappa_{\mathrm{c}}+(1-\lambda)\kappa_{\mathrm{i}}.

(8c)

Due to the vanishing electric conductivity of the insulation material (
\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}=0
), the electric conductivity perpendicular to the turns is also zero, i.e.,
\sigma_{\perp}=0
. In addition, the volumetric heat capacity is homogenized using the relative volume ratios between the materials
[
21
]
.

III-D

Coupled Formulation

In the following, the thermal model and the magnetic model are mutually coupled.
The Joule losses
q_{\mathrm{v}}=\vec{J}\cdot\vec{E}
from the magnetic model are the heat source for the thermal model, and the temperature
T
from the thermal model influences the electric conductivity
\sigma(T)
of the magnetic model.
With the homogenized foil winding model from (
3
), the Joule losses are

q_{\mathrm{v}}=\left(-\partial_{t}\vec{A}+\Phi\vec{\zeta}\right)\cdot\left(-\sigma\partial_{t}\vec{A}+\sigma\Phi\vec{\zeta}\right).

(9)

In Section
IV
, this term is averaged for every finite element.
For the electric conductivity, the expression

\sigma(T)=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}}{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}})}

(10)

is used, where
\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}=$3.93\text{\cdot}{10}^{-3}\text{\,}{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}$
is the temperature coefficient and
\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}=$60\text{\,}\mathrm{MS}\text{/}\mathrm{m}$
the material’s electric conductivity at reference temperature
T_{\mathrm{ref}}=$293.15\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$

[
21
,
22
]
.

The coupled problem is solved using an iterative method
[
23
]
, as illustrated in Fig.
2
.
After initializing the iteration with the initial conditions for the fields, the magnetic and thermal solvers are executed sequentially.
The magnetic solver performs several magnetic time steps with a fixed temperature distribution.
Then, the Joule losses are computed and averaged over the elapsed time.
With this information, the thermal solver performs one thermal time step which yields the temperature distribution needed for the electric conductivity in the magnetic solver.
By that, the thermal problem can be solved with a much coarser time step than the magnetic problem.
The iteration is continued until the final time is reached.

:
Weak coupling scheme. It holds

with
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

IV
Finite-Element Discretization

The magnetic vector potential
\vec{A}(\vec{r},t)
is discretized with
N_{\mathrm{w}}
lowest order finite element edge shape functions
\vec{w}_{j}(\vec{r})
, i.e., Nédélec elements
[
24
]
, and the temperature
T(\vec{r},t)
is discretized with
N_{\mathrm{v}}
lowest order finite element nodal shape functions
v_{j}(\vec{r})
, i.e., Lagrange elements
[
25
]
.
The approximations read

\displaystyle\vec{A}(\vec{r},t)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{w}}}a_{j}(t)\vec{w}_{j}(\vec{r}),

(11a)

\displaystyle T(\vec{r},t)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\vartheta_{j}(t)v_{j}(\vec{r}).

(11b)

The voltage function
\Phi(\vec{r},t)
is discretized with
N_{\mathrm{g}}
basis functions
g_{j}(\vec{r})
that are tailored to the foil winding domain. The approximation reads

\Phi(\vec{r},t)\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{g}}}u_{j}(t)g_{j}(\vec{r}).

(12)

The basis functions
g_{j}(\vec{r})
are constant in
\vec{e}_{\beta}
- and
\vec{e}_{\gamma}
-direction and vary only in
\vec{e}_{\alpha}
-direction.
Thus, they are easily defined on the reference foil winding domain
[
17
]
.

The Ritz-Galerkin method is applied to (
3
) and (
5
).
For the magnetic sub-problem, this defines matrices
\mathbf{K}_{\nu},\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{w}}\times N_{\mathrm{w}}}
,
\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{w}}\times N_{\mathrm{g}}}
,
\mathbf{G}_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{g}}\times N_{\mathrm{g}}}
and vectors
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{w}}}
,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{g}}}
.
For the thermal sub-problem, this defines matrices
\mathbf{K}_{\kappa},\mathbf{M}_{c_{\mathrm{v}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{v}}\times N_{\mathrm{v}}}
and the vector
\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{th}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{v}}}
.
Their entries are defined by

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{K}_{\nu}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nu\curl\vec{w}_{j}\cdot\curl\vec{w}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(13a)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma\vec{w}_{j}\cdot\vec{w}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(13b)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma g_{j}\vec{\zeta}\cdot\vec{w}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(13c)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{G}_{\sigma}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma\vec{\zeta}\cdot\vec{\zeta}g_{j}g_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(13d)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mg}}\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\vec{J}_{\mathrm{s}}\cdot\vec{w}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(13e)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{c}\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\int_{L_{\alpha}}\hat{g}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha,

(13f)

and

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\kappa\grad v_{j}\cdot\grad v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(14a)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{M}_{c_{\mathrm{v}}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}c_{\mathrm{v}}v_{j}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(14b)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{th}}\right]_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}q_{\mathrm{v}}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega.

(14c)

The degrees of freedom are collected in vectors
\mathbf{a}=\left[a_{1},\dots,a_{N_{\mathrm{w}}}\right]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}
,
\mathbf{u}=\left[u_{1},\dots,u_{N_{\mathrm{g}}}\right]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\left[\vartheta_{1},\dots,\vartheta_{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\right]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}
. The semi-discrete system of equations from (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) reads

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\nu}\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mg}},

(15a)

\displaystyle-\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}^{\top}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{G}_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{c}I

\displaystyle=0,

(15b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\top}\mathbf{u}-V

\displaystyle=0,

(15c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}+\mathbf{M}_{c_{\mathrm{v}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{th}}.

(15d)

The system is completed with appropriate initial, circuit and boundary conditions (see Sec.
III-A
).
The backward Euler method is used for time discretization.

VII
Conclusion

This paper presented a coupled magneto-thermal simulation procedure for analyzing foil windings, accounting for both electromagnetic and thermal effects.
Due to the interdependence between both phenomena, a weakly coupled approach was employed.
This enables the use of separate solvers with different time step sizes for the magnetoquasistatic and thermal sub-problems.
To reduce computational effort, a homogenization model was applied for both sub-problems, eliminating the need to resolve individual foil layers in the mesh.
The proposed method was successfully verified against results obtained from
Comsol
Multiphysics
®
.
As a practical application, a pot-type transformer with an external electrical circuit was investigated.
The analysis identified a thermal hot spot in the foil winding near the air gap of the yoke, demonstrating the method’s capability to predict critical temperature distributions in realistic configurations.

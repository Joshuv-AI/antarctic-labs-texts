Random Linear Modulation with Spherically Symmetric Modulators
Armine Bagyan and Donald Richards

Abstract
We consider the modulation of data given by random vectors X n ∈ ℝ d n X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}} , n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} . For each X n X_{n} , one chooses an independent modulating random vector Ξ n ∈ ℝ d n \Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}} and forms the projection Y n = Ξ n ′ ​ X n Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n} . It is shown, under regularity conditions on X n X_{n} and Ξ n \Xi_{n} , that Y n | Ξ n Y_{n}|\Xi_{n} converges weakly in probability to a normal distribution. More broadly, the conditional joint distribution of a family of projections constructed from random samples from X n X_{n} and Ξ n \Xi_{n} is shown to converge weakly to a matrix normal distribution. We derive, via G. Pólya’s characterization of the normal distribution, a necessary and sufficient condition on Y n Y_{n} for Ξ n \Xi_{n} to be normally distributed. When Ξ n \Xi_{n} has a spherically symmetric distribution we deduce, through I. J. Schoenberg’s characterization of the spherically symmetric characteristic functions on Hilbert spaces, that the probability density function of Y n | Ξ n Y_{n}|\Xi_{n} converges pointwise in certain p p th means to a mixture of normal densities and the rate of convergence is quantified, resulting in uniform convergence. The cumulative distribution function of Y n | Ξ n Y_{n}|\Xi_{n} is shown to converge uniformly in those p p th means to the distribution function of the same mixture, and a Lipschitz property is obtained. Examples of distributions satisfying our results are provided; these include Bingham distributions on hyperspheres of random radii, uniform distributions on hyperspheres and hypercubes of random volumes, and multivariate normal distributions; and examples of such Ξ n \Xi_{n} include the multivariate t t -, multivariate Laplace, and spherically symmetric stable distributions. Keywords and phrases . Bingham distribution; Euler-Maclaurin summation; high-dimensional data; low-dimensional projection; Pólya’s characterization; Schoenberg’s theorem; stable distribution; weak convergence in probability; Wishart distribution. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Introduction and motivation

Random modulation, in which several random signals are combined to form a new signal
(
Black 1953
;
Papoulis 1983
)
, is well known from its role in amplitude modulation (AM) and frequency modulation (FM) radio broadcasting. Random modulation is now applied widely, in fields such as electric power devices
(
Blaabjerg, et al. 1997
)
, underwater ranging and detection
(
Cochenour, et al. 2011
)
, autonomous vehicles
(
Hwang and Lee 2020
)
, radio-frequency identification (RFID) security
(
Roy, et al. 2019
)
, atmospheric research
(
She, et al. 2011
)
, medical technologies
(
Tang and Clement 2010
)
, wireless communications
(
van Trees 2002
)
, and pathogen detection
(
Yang, et al. 2015
)
.

We are motivated here by questions arising from linear random modulation of high-dimensional data. For each dimension
d_{n}
,
n=1,2,3,\ldots
, we are given datum in the form of a random vector
X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
. On choosing an independent modulating random vector
\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, and forming the projection
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
, we study the limiting conditional distribution of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
under regularity assumptions on
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
, as
d_{n}\to\infty
.

Linear modulation appears in mathematical statistics prominently in the study of low-dimensional projections of high-dimensional vectors, where the notable results of
Eaton 1981
,
Diaconis and Freedman 1984
,
Huber 1985
, and
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
have spawned an extensive literature. Also noteworthy are
Lok and Lehnert 1998
, who
studied linearly modulated communication systems;
Loperfido 2020
, in the area of detecting financial outliers; and
Davidov and Peddada 2013
, who formulated the theoretical foundations of ordered projections of multivariate data and gave applications to the analysis of toxicological and carcinogenic data.

Among the cited literature, we emphasize the work of
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
and
Wee and Tatikonda 2023
who derived, along with other results, the weak convergence properties of the conditional distribution functions of
Y_{n}
. Our results are also concerned with those conditional distributions, however we proceed using different methods that yield the convergence properties of both the conditional probability density and the conditional cumulative distribution functions of such projections.

Our work is motivated proximately by
Bagyan 2015
, who derived
L^{2}
-pointwise convergence results for the conditional density and distribution functions of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
when
\Xi_{n}
is normally distributed. For absolutely continuous distributions, uniform convergence of the distribution functions follows from their weak convergence (
Zolotarev 1986
); however it is generally more difficult to derive the convergence properties of the corresponding density functions. Thus we extend the results of
Bagyan 2015
by obtaining
L^{p}
-pointwise convergence results for the density function of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
and

L^{p}
-uniform convergence results for its distribution function; also, we extend these results to the case in which
\Xi_{n}
is spherically distributed.

In Section
2
, we suppose that
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
, the multivariate standard normal distribution.

Bagyan 2015
had also studied this case with random sampling conducted on
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
and yielding data
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}
, and
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}
, respectively, and had derived the limiting unconditional distribution of the collection of projections
\{\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r},j=1,\ldots,l,r=1,\ldots,k\}
. Throughout the article, all results are derived under the assumption that
X_{n}
satisfies the regularity conditions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
. By adapting an approach due to
Bagyan 2015
, we obtain in Theorem
2.1
the limiting weak distribution of this collection of projections, conditional on
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}
. Theorem
2.1
is related to numerous articles (cf.,
Diaconis and Freedman 1984
,
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
,
Bickel, Gur, and Nadler 2018
) that explain why many unit-length projections of a high-dimensional random vector are approximately normally distributed, and our proof motivates the results in Sections
4
and
5
. For the case in which
\Xi_{n}
is spherically symmetric, we obtain in Theorem
2.5
a necessary and sufficient condition for
\Xi_{n}
to be normally distributed; this result, which may be a new uniqueness property of the multivariate normal distribution, will be derived using a celebrated result of
Pólya 1923
that characterizes the normal distribution through the distribution of linear functions of independent, identically distributed random variables.

The data
X_{n}
are assumed throughout this article to satisfy the regularity conditions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
, so we provide in Section
3
some examples of distributions that satisfy those assumptions (see also
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
for other examples). Our examples include dilated Bingham distributions on hyperspheres, uniform distributions on Euclidean balls and on hypercubes, and multivariate normal distributions. Further it is shown that the multivariate
t
-distributions satisfy
(C.2)
but do not satisfy
(C.1)
.

From Section
4
onwards, we assume that the modulating vector
\Xi_{n}
is spherically symmetric and we consider the convergence properties of
f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
, the probability density function of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
. By applying a famous theorem of
Schoenberg 1938
, which characterizes the class of spherically symmetric characteristic functions on Hilbert spaces, we derive conditions such that certain powers,
[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)]^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, converge
L^{p}
-pointwise-in-mean to corresponding powers,
[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)]^{k}
, of a normal mixture density, where the random variable
V
determined by
\Xi_{n}
. Further we deduce pointwise convergence in the
p
th mean of
f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)
to
f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)
, for all
0<p\leq k
. Motivated by results of
Meckes 2009
, and
Wee and Tatikonda 2023
, who obtained quantitative asymptotics for convergence results in projection analysis, we obtain an inequality for the difference,
|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)]^{k}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)]^{k}|
, leading to a characterization of the rate of convergence in terms of the regularity conditions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
.

In Section
5
, we provide conditions under which
k
th powers of
F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)
, the cumulative distribution function
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
, converge uniformly in mean to
k
th powers of the corresponding mixture distribution function
F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)
. Generalizing a result of
Bagyan 2015
we obtain, reminiscent of Glivenko-Cantelli theory, the uniform convergence of
F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)
to
F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)
in the
p
th mean, for all
0<p\leq k
. Further, we derive a Lipschitz continuity property of
F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(\cdot)
.

In Section
6
, we show that the additional requirements on
X_{n}
in the main results in Sections
4
and
5
are satisfied by the examples studied in Section
3
. Further we provide examples of random vectors
\Xi_{n}
that satisfy the assumptions in our main results.

2
Some weak convergence properties of
\boldsymbol{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}

Throughout this article, the dimensions
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\ldots
are a monotonically increasing sequence of positive integers such that
d_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. All vectors are column vectors, and all random variables and vectors are continuous and have continuous density functions.
For a random entity
X
, we often write
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X}
to emphasize that the expectation is with respect to the marginal distribution of
X
. Similarly, for any scalar random variable
Y
and random entity
\Xi
, we denote by
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y|\Xi}
and
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y|\Xi}
the mean and variance, respectively, with respect to the conditional distribution of
Y|\Xi
, and the conditional characteristic function of
Y
given
\Xi
is
\varphi_{Y|\Xi}(t)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y|\Xi}\exp(itY)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The probability distribution of
Y|\Xi
is a
random measure

(
Freedman and Lane 1980
, Section 2)
, and we use as the definition of
weak convergence in probability
a characterization given by
Diaconis and Freedman 1984
: For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mu_{n}
be a random measure on
\mathbb{R}
with (random) characteristic function
\widehat{\mu}_{n}
, and let
\mu_{0}
be a deterministic measure on
\mathbb{R}
with (deterministic) characteristic function
\widehat{\mu}_{0}
. Then
\mu_{n}
converges weakly in probability to
\mu_{0}
as
n\to\infty
, denoted
\mu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{0}
, if and only if
\widehat{\mu}_{n}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\mu}_{0}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.1
Regularity conditions and weak convergence results for
\boldsymbol{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}

We assume throughout the article that the sequence of random vectors
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
satisfies the following regularity conditions:

(C.1)

As
n\to\infty
,
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}>0
.

(C.2)

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n}
be an independent copy of
X_{n}
. Then
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
as
n\to\infty
.

In stating these conditions and throughout our work, we use the notation “
X_{n}
” in place of the more common scaling “
X_{n}/\sqrt{d_{n}}
”. With this notation duly noted, we remark that
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
are assumed widely in the literature.
Diaconis and Freedman 1984
were first in stating
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
for the case in which
X_{n}
has an empirical distribution, and numerous authors (e.g.,
Bagyan 2015
,
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
,
Li and Yin 2007
) formulated those assumptions subsequently for non-empirical distributions. The conditions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
have also appeared recently in the field of statistical physics
(
Wee and Tatikonda 2023
)
, where they are called the “thin-shell” and “zero overlap concentration” assumptions, respectively.

We write
\Xi\sim\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,I_{d})
to denote that a random vector
\Xi
has a
d
-dimensional normal distribution with mean
0
and covariance matrix
I_{d}
, the identity matrix of order
d
. We also use the notation
\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pt=\sqrt{-1}
, and we often write
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X}
to emphasize that an expectation is being taken with respect to the distribution of a given random entity
X
.

Let
k
and
l
be fixed positive integers, and let
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
be mutually independent, each satisfying
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
.
Also let
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}
be mutually independent copies of
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
, with
\{\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}\}
and
\{X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}\}
also are independent. This situation arises in practice when, given a random sample
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}
from
X_{n}
, we simulate a random sample
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}
from
\Xi_{n}
and then seek to use the family of projections
Y_{n;j,r}=\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r}
,
j=1,\ldots,l
,
r=1,\ldots,k
, to perform inference for the population represented by the conditional distribution of
\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}|\Xi_{n}
.

Defining the
l\times k
matrix
\mathcal{Y}_{n}=(Y_{n;j,r})
, we now provide the asymptotic conditional distribution of
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
, given
(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})
, as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
be mutually independent copies of
X_{n}
. Let
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
be mutually independent,
\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
–distributed, and independent of
(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k})
. Then
\mathcal{Y}_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{Z}
as
n\to\infty
, where
\mathcal{Z}=(Z_{j,r})
is an
l\times k
random matrix whose entries
Z_{j,r}
,
j=1,\ldots,l
,
r=1,\ldots,k
, are mutually independent and
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})
–distributed.

For the case in which
k=1
, Theorem
2.1
reduces to the following result of
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
.

Corollary 2.2
.

(
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
satisfies
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
. Let the random vectors
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
be mutually independent,
\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
–distributed, and independent of
X_{n}
; and define
\mathcal{Y}_{n}=(\Xi_{n,1}^{\prime}X_{n},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}^{\prime}X_{n})^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{Y}_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{l}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{l})
as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 2.3
.

(i)
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
also proved the following converse to Corollary
2.2
, the proof of which can be readily adapted to our setting: Suppose that
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
and
\{\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
are independent, and let
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
. If
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})
as
n\to\infty
then
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
hold.

(ii) By Corollary
2.2

Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})
, which does not depend on
\Xi_{n}
, so the corresponding unconditional distribution of
Y_{n}
also converges similarly to
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})
. This property, in which the limiting conditional distribution of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
does not depend on
\Xi_{n}
, appears repeatedly in the sequel.

(iii) As noted by
Duembgen and Del Conte-Zerial 2013
, results such as Corollary
2.2
caution us to be wary of presuming, on the basis of moderately many low-dimensional projections, that a high-dimensional data set is normally distributed.

(iv) In much of the literature,
X_{n}
is projected along uniformly distributed directions. To recover this case from our results, one sets
\Xi_{n}=\sqrt{d_{n}}\Theta_{n}
where
\Theta_{n}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, the hypersphere centered at the origin and of radius
1
. Then
\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}=\sqrt{d_{n}}\Theta_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\sqrt{d_{n}}\Theta_{n,1}\|X\|
,
\Theta_{n,1}
being the first component of
\Theta_{n}
, and the proof of Corollary
2.2
carries over, using the fact that the distribution of
\sqrt{d_{n}}\Theta_{n,1}
converges uniformly to a standard normal distribution.

Remark 2.4
.

There is the issue of whether Theorem
2.1
can be extended to the case in which
\Xi_{n}
has a non-Gaussian distribution. Consider, for simplicity, the case in which
l=k=1
; then it will be seen that a crucial step in the proof is to show that

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big(\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big):=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}-\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}\to 0

(2.1)

as
n\to\infty
, so this raises the issue of whether (
2.1
) holds for non-Gaussian distributions.

Suppose that
\Xi_{n}
has a spherically symmetric stable distribution with
index of stability

\alpha\in(0,2)

(
Zolotarev 1986
)
and characteristic function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\exp(-\|u\|^{\alpha})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
. Then it will be shown in Subsection
2.2
, starting at (
2.10
), that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big(\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big)=\exp(-2^{\alpha/2}\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha})-\exp(-2\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha}),

(2.2)

which is positive for
t\neq 0
, so the proof of Theorem
2.1
does not apply in this case.

It is noticeable that the distribution of
\Xi_{n}
in this counterexample is
spherically symmetric
, i.e., the characteristic function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})
is a function of
\|u\|
.
This also raises the issue of the extent to which (
2.1
) is characteristic of the normal distribution, and indeed we show that, subject to
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
, the property (
2.1
) characterizes the normality of
\Xi_{n}
within the class of spherically symmetric distributions.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
satisfy
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
, and that
\{\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
are mutually independent of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
. Also suppose that, for some function
\psi_{0}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
\Xi_{n}
has characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\psi_{0}(\|u\|^{2})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
,
and define
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2}I_{d_{n}})
for some
\sigma_{0}
if and only if (
2.1
) holds.

Remark 2.6
.

There is an extensive literature that proves Pólya’s theorem to be “stable,” i.e., if
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are independent copies of
Z
, and if
Z
and
2^{-1/2}(Z_{1}+Z_{2})
are “close in distribution” according to various measures of closeness, then
Z
is similarly close in distribution to
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2})
; see, e.g.,
Yanushkevichius and Yanushkevichiene 2007
and the references given there. Extensions of Corollary
2.2
and Theorem
2.5
can be obtained from such stability results, and we leave such details to interested readers.

2.2
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
2.1
: Let
U=(U_{j,r})
, a constant
l\times k
real matrix, and define

Z_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}Y_{n;j,r}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(U^{\prime}\mathcal{Y}_{n}).

By the mutual independence of
\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l}
, their independence from
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}
, and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k})}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k})}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r}\Big).

Since
\Xi_{n,j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
,
j=1,\ldots,l
, then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k})}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n,j}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}X_{n,r}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k})}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}X_{n,r}\Big\|^{2}\Big).

Denoting Kronecker’s delta by
\delta_{j,r}
, then it follows from
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}X_{n,r}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}=1}^{k}\sum_{r_{2}=1}^{k}u_{j,r_{1}}u_{j,r_{2}}X_{n,r_{1}}^{\prime}X_{n,r_{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1}=1}^{k}\sum_{r_{2}=1}^{k}u_{j,r_{1}}u_{j,r_{2}}\sigma^{2}\delta_{r_{1},r_{2}}=\sigma^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}^{2}.

It follows by the continuity of the exponential function and the Continuous Mapping Theorem
(
Chow and Teicher 1988
, p. 254, Theorem 1)
that, as
n\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\to\prod_{j=1}^{l}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}^{2}\Big)\equiv\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U).

(2.3)

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
be mutually independent copies of
X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle{}_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ =\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\left[\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\,\overline{\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ =\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\bigg[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}X_{n,r}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \qquad\qquad\qquad\ \cdot\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\Big)\bigg].

Reversing the order of expectations, which is justified by Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle{}_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ =\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}(X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r})\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ =\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n,j}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n,j}^{\prime}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}(X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r})\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ =\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,k}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}(X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r})\Big\|^{2}\Big).

(2.4)

It is straightforward that

\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}(X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r})\Big\|^{2}\\
=\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}^{2}\|X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\|^{2}+\sum_{1\leq r_{1}\neq r_{2}\leq k}u_{j,r_{1}}u_{j,r_{2}}(X_{n,r_{1}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{1}})^{\prime}(X_{n,r_{2}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{2}}).

(2.5)

By
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
, and Slutsky’s theorem,

\|X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\|^{2}=\|X_{n,r}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\|^{2}-2X_{n,r}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}2\sigma^{2}

(2.6)

as
n\to\infty
. Also, for
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
,
X_{n,r_{1}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{1}}
and
X_{n,r_{2}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{2}}
are independent and each converges to
0
in probability as
n\to\infty
; therefore

(X_{n,r_{1}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{1}})^{\prime}(X_{n,r_{2}}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r_{2}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

(2.7)

as
n\to\infty
. Applying (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to (
2.5
), we obtain

\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}(X_{n,r}-\widetilde{X}_{n,r})\Big\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}2\sigma^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}^{2},

and it follows by the Continuous Mapping Theorem that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\big|^{2}\to\prod_{j=1}^{l}\exp\Big(-t^{2}\sigma^{2}\sum_{r=1}^{k}u_{j,r}^{2}\Big)=\exp(-t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U).

(2.8)

Next, for
\varepsilon>0
, it follows by Chebyshev’s inequality that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|>\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\varepsilon^{-2}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\Big[\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\big|^{2}-\exp(-t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\big(\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big)\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ -\big(\overline{\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)}-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big)\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\Big].

Applying the triangle inequality, and the inequality
\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\leq 1
for all
t
and
U
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\big|\varphi

\displaystyle{}_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|>\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\Big[\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\big|\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\big|^{2}-\exp(-t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ +\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}\overline{\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)}-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)\big|\Big].

(2.9)

By (
2.3
) and (
2.8
), each of the three terms on the right-hand side of (
2.2
) converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\epsilon
was chosen arbitrarily then it follows that, for all
t
and
U
,

\varphi_{Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U),

the characteristic function of the
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)
distribution.

Applying the characterization of weak convergence in probability given by
Diaconis and Freedman 1984
, we obtain
Z_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}U^{\prime}U)
. Finally, since
U
was chosen arbitrarily then it follows by the Cramér-Wold device that
\mathcal{Y}_{n}|(\Xi_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n,l})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{Z}
.

∎

t\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)=\exp(-\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha}).

(2.11)

\widetilde{X}_{n}

By (

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)|^{2}=[\exp(-\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha})]^{2}=\exp(-2\sigma^{\alpha}|t|^{\alpha}),

(2.14)

and by combining (
2.13

Proof of Theorem
2.5
:
Suppose that
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2}I_{d_{n}})
, for some
\sigma_{0}
. Then the conclusion was demonstrated earlier within the proof of Corollary
2.2
.

Conversely suppose that, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big(\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pttY_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pttX_{n}^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}}\psi_{0}(t^{2}\|X_{n}\|^{2}).

(2.15)

Since
\psi_{0}(t^{2})
is a characteristic function then it is continuous. By
(C.1)
,
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
,
so it follows from the Continuous Mapping Theorem that

\psi_{0}(t^{2}\|X_{n}\|^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\psi_{0}(t^{2}\sigma^{2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)=\psi_{0}(\sigma^{2}t^{2}).

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n}
be an independent copy of
X_{n}
. Since
\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)
is a characteristic function then it is bounded, so by applying Fubini’s theorem to interchange expectations it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\,\overline{\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)}\,\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n})\cdot\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(-\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n},\widetilde{X}_{n}}\exp\big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}(X_{n}-\widetilde{X}_{n})\big),

where the latter equality follows from the law of iterated expectations. Again interchanging expectations, which is justified by Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n},\widetilde{X}_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp\big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}(X_{n}-\widetilde{X}_{n})\big)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n},\widetilde{X}_{n}}\psi_{0}(t^{2}\|X_{n}-\widetilde{X}_{n}\|^{2}\big).

By
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
, and Slutsky’s theorem,
\|X_{n}-\widetilde{X}_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}2\sigma^{2}
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\psi_{0}(\cdot)
is continuous then, by the Continuous Mapping Theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}=\psi_{0}(2\sigma^{2}t^{2}).

(2.16)

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(2\sigma^{2}t^{2})-[\psi_{0}(\sigma^{2}t^{2})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}-\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big(\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)\big)=0.

Therefore we obtain the functional equation,
\psi_{0}(2\sigma^{2}t^{2})=[\psi_{0}(\sigma^{2}t^{2})]^{2}
, equivalently,

\psi_{0}(t^{2})=[\psi_{0}(2^{-1}t^{2})]^{2},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.17)

Denote by
Z
a random variable with characteristic function
\psi_{0}(t^{2})
, and let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be mutually independent random variables that have the same distribution as
Z
. Then the right-hand side of (
2.17
) is the characteristic function of
2^{-1/2}(Z_{1}+Z_{2})
, so (
2.17
) is equivalent to the equality in distribution,

Z\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}2^{-1/2}(Z_{1}+Z_{2}).

(2.18)

By a celebrated theorem of
Pólya 1923
(cf.,
Bogachev 1998
), (
2.18
) implies, and hence is equivalent to,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2})
for some
\sigma_{0}
. Stated alternatively in terms of characteristic functions, we have
\psi_{0}(t)=\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}t^{2})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\psi_{0}(\|u\|^{2})=\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}\|u\|^{2})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, hence
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2}I_{d_{n}})
.

3
Examples of distributions satisfying
(C.1)
and
(C.2)

We now provide some examples to illustrate the breadth of the class of distributions that satisfy assumptions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
. For
\rho>0
,
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(\rho)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}:\|x\|=\rho\}
denotes the hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
with center
0
and radius
\rho
, and
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
denotes the unit hypersphere
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(1)
.

We begin with an example in which
X_{n}
has a Bingham distribution on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(r_{n})
. In the sequel, we use the notation
\|\Sigma\|_{F}:=[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma^{2})]^{1/2}
for the Frobenius norm of any symmetric matrix
\Sigma
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\{r_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of radii such that
r_{n}\to\sigma
as
n\to\infty
, and suppose that the distribution of
X_{n}
is concentrated on the hypersphere
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(r_{n})
. Since
\|X_{n}\|^{2}=r_{n}^{2}
then, trivially,
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
and so
(C.1)
holds.

Fix
\beta\in[0,1)
, and let
\{\Sigma_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of symmetric
d_{n}\times d_{n}
matrices such that
\|\Sigma_{n}\|_{F}=O(d_{n}^{\beta/2})
as
n\to\infty
. Since
X_{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(r_{n})
then, by polar coordinates,
X_{n}=r_{n}\Theta_{n}
where the random vector
\Theta_{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
. Suppose also that
\Theta_{n}
has a
Bingham distribution with matrix parameter

\Sigma_{n}
. Relative to the surface measure
{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\theta_{n}
on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, normalized to have total surface area
1
, the probability density function of
\Theta_{n}
is

f(\theta;\Sigma_{n})=[c(\Sigma_{n})]^{-1}\exp(\theta^{\prime}\Sigma_{n}\theta),

(3.1)

\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, with normalizing constant

c(\Sigma_{n})=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}}\exp(\theta^{\prime}\Sigma_{n}\theta){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\theta.

The constant
c(\Sigma_{n})
can be expressed in terms of the confluent hypergeometric function of matrix argument (cf.,
Bagyan and Richards 2024
,
Bingham 1974
, or
Muirhead 1982
), however we will not need the explicit form of that result.

It is simple to verify that, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
f(\theta;\Sigma_{n}-\tau I_{d_{n}})\equiv f(\theta;\Sigma_{n})
. Therefore, with no loss of generality, we assume in (
3.1
) that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})=0
. It is also evident that
\Theta_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}-\Theta_{n}
; therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\Theta_{n})=0
and hence
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})=0
. Thus, with
\widetilde{X}_{n}
denoting an independent copy of
\widetilde{X}_{n}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
.

Next, observe that

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\cdot\widetilde{X}_{n}^{\prime}X_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(X_{n}X_{n}^{\prime})(\widetilde{X}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n}^{\prime})].

Interchanging the expectation and trace operations, and applying the independence of
X_{n}
and
\widetilde{X}_{n}
, we obtain the general identity,

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}X_{n}^{\prime})\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\widetilde{X}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n}^{\prime})]=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big([\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})]^{2}\big)=\|\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})\|_{F}^{2},

(3.2)

which is valid for any random vector
X_{n}
and independent copy
\widetilde{X}_{n}
such that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})=0
. We will apply later the identity (
3.2
) repeatedly.

Again resorting to polar coordinates
X_{n}=r_{n}\Theta_{n}
, the general identity (
3.2
) yields

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\|\mathrm{Cov}(r_{n}\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}=r_{n}^{4}\,\|\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}.

(3.3)

Since
\|\Sigma_{n}\|_{F}=O(d_{n}^{\beta/2})
as
n\to\infty
, where
\beta\in[0,1)
, then by
Bagyan and Richards 2024
, we obtain the expansion

\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})=d_{n}^{-1}I_{d_{n}}+2d_{n}^{-1}(d_{n}+2)^{-1}\Sigma_{n}+O(d_{n}^{-(3-2\beta)/2}).

On squaring both sides of this expansion, and recalling that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})=0
, we obtain

\|\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})]^{2}=d_{n}^{-1}+O\big(d_{n}^{-(3-2\beta)/2}\big);

(3.4)

therefore
\|\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
r_{n}\to\sigma
then it follows from (
3.3
) that
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})\to 0
, hence
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
as
n\to\infty
, so
(C.2)
holds.

For the special case in which
\Sigma_{n}\equiv 0
, so that
X_{n}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}(r_{n})
, the above example was obtained by
Bagyan 2015
.

In the next example, which was initiated by
Bagyan 2015
, we denote the
d_{n}
-dimensional ball centered at
0
and radius
\rho
by
\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(\rho)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}:\|x\|\leq\rho\}
and the volume of the ball by
\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(\rho))
.

Example 3.2
.

For a positive sequence
\{r_{n},n\geq 1\}
such that
r_{n}\to\sigma
as
n\to\infty
, let
X_{n}
be uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(r_{n})
. By polar coordinates,
X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}R_{n}\Theta_{n}
where
R_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\|X_{n}\|\in[0,r_{n}]
,
\Theta_{n}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, and
R_{n}
and
\Theta_{n}
are independent.

Denote by
{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\theta
the normalized surface measure on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
. Applying polar coordinates on
\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(r_{n})
, viz.,
x=s\theta
where
0\leq s\leq r_{n}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, together with the well-known formula for
\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\|X_{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))}\int_{\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(r_{n})}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\|x\|^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=d_{n}r_{n}^{-d_{n}}\int_{0}^{r_{n}}s^{d_{n}-1}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptts^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}s,

Making the transformation
s\to r_{n}s^{1/d_{n}}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\|X_{n}\|^{2})=\int_{0}^{1}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pttr_{n}^{2}s^{2/d_{n}}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}s\to\int_{0}^{1}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sigma^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}s=\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\sigma^{2})

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
, hence
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
, so
(C.1)
holds.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n}=\widetilde{R}_{n}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n}
be an independent copy of
X_{n}
. Since
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})=0
then it follows that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
. By the general identity (
3.2
),

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\|\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathrm{Cov}(R_{n}\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}=[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{2})]^{2}\,\|\mathrm{Cov}(\Theta_{n})\|_{F}^{2}.

(3.5)

We have
[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{2})]^{2}=[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})]^{2}\to\sigma^{4}
. Also, by applying (
3.4
) for the case in which
\Sigma_{n}=0
,
we obtain
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})\to 0
, so
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
as
n\to\infty
, hence
(C.2)
holds.

This example can be extended further to the case in which
X_{n}
has a dilated Bingham distribution, i.e.,
X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}R_{n}\Theta_{n}
where
R_{n}
is random;
R_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma
;
R_{n}
and
\Theta_{n}
are independent;
\Theta_{n}
has a Bingham distribution with the density function (
3.1
); and, as in Example
3.1
, there exists
\beta\in[0,1)
such that
\|\Sigma_{n}\|_{F}=O(d_{n}^{\beta/2})
as
n\to\infty
. In this setting, since
\|X_{n}\|^{2}=R_{n}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
then
(C.1)
holds. Also, proceeding as in (
3.5
), we obtain
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
and
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})\to 0
. Therefore
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
, so
(C.2)
holds.

Example 3.3
.

For
l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}>0
, set
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(l_{n})=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d_{n}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}:|x_{i}|\leq l_{n}/2,\,i=1,\ldots,d_{n}\}
, the
d_{n}
-dimensional hypercube centered at
0
and with sides of length
l_{n}
.

Let
\{L_{n},n\geq 1\}
be continuous random variables that satisfy
d_{n}L_{n}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}12\sigma^{2}
as
n\to\infty
.
Conditional on
L_{n}
, let
X_{n}=(X_{n;1},\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}})^{\prime}
be uniformly distributed on the hypercube
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})
; then
X_{n;1}|L_{n},\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}}|L_{n}
are mutually independent and identically uniformly distributed on the interval
[-L_{n}/2,L_{n}/2]
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;1}|L_{n})=0
,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;1}^{2}|L_{n})=L_{n}^{2}/12
, and

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}|L_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;1}^{2}+\cdots+X_{n;d_{n}}^{2}|L_{n})=d_{n}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;1}^{2}|L_{n})=d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12,

(3.6)

and

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}|L_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;j}^{2}|L_{n})=d_{n}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n;1}^{2}|L_{n})=d_{n}L_{n}^{4}/180.

(3.7)

By (
3.6
) and the law of total expectation
(
Ross 2010
, p. 333)
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}|L_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}(d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12)\to\sigma^{2}.

By (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and the law of total variance
(
Ross 2010
, p. 348)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}|L_{n})]+\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}|L_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=(4/5)d_{n}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12)^{2}]+\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12).

(3.8)

Since
d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
then

d_{n}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12)^{2}]\to 0

and
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt[d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12]\to 0
.
Therefore, by (
3.3
),
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, hence
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
and
(C.1)
holds.

Next, since
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})=0
and
X_{n}
and
\widetilde{X}_{n}
are independent then
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
. Also, it is simple to verify that
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n}|L_{n})=L_{n}^{2}I_{d_{n}}/12
, hence
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big([\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n}|L_{n})]^{2}=d_{n}(L_{n}^{2}/12)^{2}
. Applying the general identity (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big([\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n}|L_{n})]^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[d_{n}(L_{n}^{2}/12)^{2}]=d_{n}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2}/12)^{2}]\to 0.

Therefore
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
, so
(C.2)
holds.

For the case in which the sequence
\{L_{n},n\geq 1\}
is deterministic, this example is due to
Bagyan 2015
.

Example 3.4
.

Let
X_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Sigma_{n})
where
\Sigma_{n}
, the covariance matrix of
X_{n}
, is positive definite. We suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})\to\sigma^{2}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Denote by
\lambda_{n;1},\ldots,\lambda_{n;d_{n}}
the eigenvalues of
\Sigma_{n}
, and let
H_{n}
be a
d_{n}\times d_{n}
orthogonal matrix such that
H_{n}\Sigma_{n}H_{n}^{\prime}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\lambda_{n;1},\ldots,\lambda_{n;d_{n}})
. Making the transformation
U_{n}=H_{n}X_{n}
we find that
U_{n;1},\ldots,U_{n;d_{n}}
, the components of
U_{n}
, are mutually independent, with
U_{n;j}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\lambda_{n;j}^{1/2}Z_{n;j}
with
Z_{n;1},\ldots,Z_{n;d_{n}}
being mutually independent
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,1)
random variables. Therefore
\|X_{n}\|^{2}=\|U_{n}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}U_{n;j}^{2}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;j}Z_{n;j}^{2}
, and it follows that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;j}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(Z_{n;j}^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;j}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}),

hence
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})\to\sigma^{2}
as
n\to\infty
. Further,

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;j}^{2}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(Z_{n;j}^{2})=2\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;j}^{2}=2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2}),

so
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})\to 0
. Therefore
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
as
n\to\infty
, so
(C.1)
holds.

For
\widetilde{X}_{n}
, an independent copy of
X_{n}
, we have
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
. Applying the general identity (
3.2
), we obtain

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big([\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})]^{2}\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})\to 0

as
n\to\infty
, so
(C.2)
holds.

We now present two examples of
\Sigma_{n}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})\to\sigma^{2}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})\to 0
. For the first such example, suppose that

\lambda_{n;j}=\sigma^{2}(\log d_{n})^{-1}j^{-1},

(3.9)

j=1,\ldots,d_{n}
. Let
\gamma=0.57721\ldots
denote Euler’s constant; then by the Euler-Maclaurin summation formula
(
Olver and Wong 2025
,
Eq. (2.10.8)
)
,

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{-1}=\gamma+\log d_{n}+O(d_{n}^{-1})

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})=\sigma^{2}(\log d_{n})^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{-1}=\sigma^{2}(\log d_{n})^{-1}[\gamma+\log d_{n}+O(d_{n}^{-1})]\to\sigma^{2},

and, since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-2}=\pi^{2}/6<\infty
then, as
n\to\infty
,

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})=\sigma^{4}(\log d_{n})^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{-2}<\sigma^{4}(\log d_{n})^{-2}\cdot\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\to 0.

For a second example, let
r>-1/2
and define

\lambda_{n;j}=(r+1)\sigma^{2}d_{n}^{-(r+1)}j^{r},

(3.10)

j=1,\ldots,d_{n}
. The basic difference between (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) is that, for fixed
n
, (
3.9
) is decreasing in
j
whereas (
3.10
) is increasing in
j
. Again applying the Euler-Maclaurin summation formula,
we have

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{r}=(r+1)^{-1}d_{n}^{r+1}+O(d_{n}^{r})=(r+1)^{-1}d_{n}^{r+1}[1+O(d_{n}^{-1})].

(3.11)

Letting
n\to\infty
, it follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})=(r+1)\sigma^{2}d_{n}^{-(r+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{r}=[1+O(d_{n}^{-1})]\sigma^{2}\to\sigma^{2}

and

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})=(r+1)^{2}\sigma^{4}d_{n}^{-2(r+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}j^{2r}=(r+1)^{2}(2r+1)^{-1}\sigma^{4}d_{n}^{-1}[1+O(d_{n}^{-1})]\to 0.

Next, we provide an example for which
(C.1)
does not hold whereas
(C.2)
holds.

Example 3.5
.

For
\nu>4
, let
X_{n}
have a centered multivariate
t
-distribution with index parameter
\nu
and positive definite matrix parameter
\Sigma_{n}

(
Muirhead 1982
, p. 48)
. There holds the stochastic representation
X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\nu^{1/2}Q_{\nu}^{-1/2}Z_{n}
where
Q_{\nu}\sim\chi^{2}_{\nu}
, a chi-squared distribution with
\nu
degrees-of-freedom,
Z_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Sigma_{n})
, and
Q_{\nu}
and
Z_{n}
are independent. We also assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})\to(\nu-2)\sigma^{2}/\nu
and
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n})=0
and that
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}X_{n}^{\prime})=\nu\Sigma_{n}/(\nu-2)
. Also,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=0
and, by the general identity (
3.2
),

\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big([\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})]^{2}\big)=\frac{\nu^{2}}{(\nu-2)^{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n}^{2}).

Therefore
\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, so
X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
and
(C.2)
holds.

In considering
(C.1)
, we begin by noting that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(\mathrm{Cov}(X_{n})\big)=\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})\to\sigma^{2},

as
n\to\infty
. Applying the law of total variance
(
Ross 2010
, p. 348)
, and the independence of
Q_{\nu}
and
Z_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=\nu\Big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(Q_{\nu}^{-2})\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|Z_{n}\|^{2})+\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(Q_{\nu}^{-1})[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|Z_{n}\|^{2})]^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\nu\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(Q_{\nu}^{-1})[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|Z_{n}\|^{2})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu}{(\nu-4)(\nu-2)^{2}}[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})]^{2},

and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})\geq\frac{\nu}{(\nu-4)(\nu-2)^{2}}\lim_{n\to\infty}[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Sigma_{n})]^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\nu(\nu-4)}>0.

Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{4})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Var}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})+\lim_{n\to\infty}[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})]^{2}\geq\frac{\sigma^{4}}{\nu(\nu-4)}+\sigma^{4}>\sigma^{4},

then it follows that
\|X_{n}\|^{4}\not\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{4}
. Therefore
\|X_{n}\|^{2}\not\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}
, so
(C.1)
does not hold.

To complete this example, we note that the Laplace distributions also satisfy
(C.2)
but not
(C.1)
. For those distributions,
X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}Q_{\nu}^{1/2}Z_{n}
where
Q_{\nu}\sim\chi^{2}_{\nu}
,
Z_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Sigma_{n})
, and
Q_{\nu}
and
Z_{n}
are mutually independent.

4
Properties of the probability density function of
\boldsymbol{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}

4.1
Preliminary remarks on the vectors
\boldsymbol{X_{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Xi_{n}}

Let
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of continuous random vectors, each satisfying
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
. We assume that the random modulators
\{\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
are continuous and mutually independent of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
. Also denote by
f_{X_{n}}
and
f_{\Xi_{n}}
the marginal density functions of
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
, respectively, each density assumed to being supported on an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
.

Let
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
; then we obtain the joint density function of
(Y_{n},\Xi_{n})
by making the usual transformation from
(X_{n},\Xi_{n})
to
(Y_{n},X_{n;2},\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}},\Xi_{n})
, where
X_{n;j}
is the
j
th component of
X_{n}
,
j=2,\ldots,d_{n}
.
Since
\Xi_{n}
is continuous then it is simple to verify that the Jacobian of the transformation exists and is non-zero, almost everywhere. Therefore
f_{Y_{n},\Xi_{n}}
, the joint density function of
(Y_{n},\Xi_{n})
, exists almost everywhere and is obtained by integrating over the support of
X_{n;2},\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}}
. Consequently
f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
, the conditional density function of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
, also exists almost everywhere and

f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)=\frac{f_{Y_{n},\Xi_{n}}(y,\xi)}{f_{\Xi_{n}}(\xi)}

for
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, and
f_{\Xi_{n}}(\xi)\neq 0
.

We will also encounter the conditional characteristic function of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
, viz.,

\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pttY_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The following result provides in terms of
\varphi_{X_{n}}
, the characteristic function of
X_{n}
, a condition under which
\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
is integrable for almost all values of
\Xi_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

A necessary and sufficient condition that
\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for almost all values of
\Xi_{n}
is that, for almost all
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t<\infty.

(4.1)

We now assume that the distribution of
\Xi_{n}
is orthogonally invariant, i.e.,
\Xi_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}H\Xi_{n}
for all
d_{n}\times d_{n}
orthogonal matrices
H

(
Muirhead 1982
, p. 34)
. It is well known that this orthogonal invariance is equivalent to the property that
\Xi_{n}
has a spherically symmetric characteristic function, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, depends on
\|u\|
only. We assume that there exists a function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
n=1,2,3,\ldots
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\psi(\|u\|^{2}),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}.

(4.2)

By a famous theorem of
Schoenberg 1938
(see also
Eaton 1981
,
Ressel 1976
,
Steerneman and van Perlo-ten Kleij 2005
), there exists a distribution function
G
on
[0,\infty)
such that

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-tv^{2}/2){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v),\quad t\geq 0.

(4.3)

That is,
\psi(t^{2})
is a scale mixture of one-dimensional Gaussian characteristic functions.

A necessary and sufficient condition that
\psi(t^{2})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, is integrable is that

\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-1}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)<\infty;

(4.4)

this inequality follows by applying Fubini’s theorem to obtain the equalities

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(t^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-t^{2}v^{2}/2){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-t^{2}v^{2}/2){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)=(2\pi)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-1}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v).

We assume throughout the remainder of this article that (
4.4
) holds, so that the characteristic function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
, is integrable.

Applying (
4.3
) to (
4.2
) we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\|u\|^{2}v^{2}/2){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}.

(4.5)

Also applying the multidimensional inverse Fourier transform, it follows that the density function of
\Xi_{n}
exists and is given by

f_{\Xi_{n}}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi)^{-d_{n}/2}v^{-d_{n}}\exp(-\|\xi\|^{2}/2v^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}.

Applying the inverse Fourier transform to the characteristic function
\psi(t^{2})
in (
4.3
), we obtain a probability density function given by

\phi(y^{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi)^{-1/2}v^{-1}\exp(-y^{2}/2v^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

4.2
The matrices
\boldsymbol{A_{n,k}}

For here onwards, we denote by
k
a fixed integer. Also let
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
be mutually independent copies of
X_{n}
; in particular,
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
satisfy
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
.

Define the
d_{n}\times k
matrix
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=(\widetilde{X}_{n,1}\ \cdots\ \widetilde{X}_{n,k})
, and the
k\times k
positive semidefinite matrix

A_{n,k}=\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=\big(\widetilde{X}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,r}\big)_{j,r=1}^{k}.

(4.6)

In the matrix analysis literature
(
Horn and Johnson 2013
, p. 441)
,
A_{n,k}
is called a
Gram matrix
.

For
j=1,\ldots,k
, denote by
\widetilde{X}_{n,j;1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,j;d_{n}}
the components of
\widetilde{X}_{n,j}
. Then

\widetilde{X}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,r}=\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\widetilde{X}_{n,j;m}\widetilde{X}_{n,r;m}

and, by (
4.6
),

\displaystyle A_{n,k}=\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\widetilde{X}_{n,j;m}\widetilde{X}_{n,r;m}\bigg)_{j,r=1}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\Big(\widetilde{X}_{n,j;m}\widetilde{X}_{n,r;m}\Big)_{j,r=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m}\\
\vdots\\
\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m}\end{pmatrix}(\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m}),

(4.7)

which represents
A_{n,k}
as a sum of
d_{n}
positive semidefinite
k\times k
matrices, each of rank
1
. Therefore a necessary condition for
A_{n,k}
to be nonsingular is that
d_{n}\geq k
, and in the sequel we assume this condition to always hold.

Henceforth, we also require the following assumption about the distribution of
X_{n}
:

(C.3)

There exist a positive integer
n_{0}
such that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n_{0},k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
.

As a consequence of condition
(C.3)
, there holds the following properties of
A_{n,k}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
(C.3)
holds. Then for
d_{n}\geq k
,

(i)

A_{n,k}
is positive definite, almost surely.

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n+1,k})^{-1/2}]\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]
.

(iii)

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,j})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
and all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Note that
(C.3)
implies that
A_{n_{0},k}
is nonsingular, almost surely, which implies that
d_{n_{0}}\geq k
. Since the sequence
\{d_{n},n\geq 1\}
is monotonically increasing then it follows from Lemma
4.2
(ii) that
d_{n}\geq k
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

4.3
Convergence properties of the probability density function

With
k
being the integer specified in
(C.3)
, we assume henceforth that

\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)<\infty.

(4.8)

This assumption on
G
is more restrictive than the previous integrability requirement (
4.4
) since, by Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-1}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)\equiv\|v^{-1}\|_{L^{1}(\textrm{d}G)}\leq\|v^{-1}\|_{{L^{k}(\textrm{d}G)}}\equiv\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)\bigg)^{1/k}.

(4.9)

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we introduce the notation

{\mathcal{F}}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}y{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}t{\hskip 1.0pt}}f(y)\equiv(\mathcal{F}f)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptty)f(y){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}y,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(4.10)

for the Fourier transform of
f
. For a Fourier transform
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we often write

\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\widehat{f}(t)\equiv(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{f}\,)(y)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptyt)\widehat{f}(t){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t,\quad y\in\mathbb{R},

(4.11)

for the inverse Fourier transform of
\widehat{f}
. The notations
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}y{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}t{\hskip 1.0pt}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}
will be used often to monitor the arguments of numerous simultaneous Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms, and we also use similar notation in fewer instances for the multidimensional Fourier and inverse Fourier transforms.

We now state the main result of this section. In this result and hereafter, we denote by
G
the distribution function defined by (
4.3
), by
V
the random variable corresponding to
G
, and we use the notation

f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\Sigma)}(w)=(2\pi)^{-k/2}(\det\Sigma)^{-1/2}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}w),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

for the probability density function of the
k
-dimensional normal distribution with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
. Also, we denote by
\boldsymbol{1}_{k}
the vector
(1,\ldots,1)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that the random vectors
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
satisfy
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
,
(C.3)
, and (
4.1
). Let
\{\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
be spherically symmetric modulating vectors that satisfy (
4.2
) and (
4.8
) and are independent of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
, and let
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
j=1,\ldots,k
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}.

(4.12)

For the case in which
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
, it follows from (
4.5
) that
G
is concentrated at
v=1
, so the moment criterion (
4.8
) holds trivially. Then the assumptions in Theorem
4.3
can be simplified accordingly, and we obtain the following
p
th-mean pointwise convergence property of
f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
be continuous random vectors that satisfy
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
,
(C.3)
, and (
4.1
), and let
\>\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
. Then, for all
\>y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0<p\leq 2\lfloor k/2\rfloor
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{p}=0.

(4.13)

The following result quantifies explicitly the rate of convergence in (
4.12
) in terms of the regularity assumptions
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
, and therefore strengthens Theorem
4.3
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
, and let
1\leq j\leq k
. Then there exists
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{j}
,

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}\big|\leq c_{j}\,\big[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,j}-\sigma^{2}I_{j}\|_{F}^{2}\big]^{1/4},

(4.14)

where

c_{j}=2^{-(j-2)/2}\pi^{-j/2}j^{5/4}\sigma^{-(j+1)}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-j}).

(4.15)

Further,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,j}-\sigma^{2}I_{j}\|_{F}^{2}=j\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}+j(j-1)[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n})]^{2}.

(4.16)

We also remark that (
4.16
), together with the calculations in Section
3
, provides the rate of convergence for each example in that section.

4.4
Proofs

Proof of Lemma
4.1
:
Since
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
are independent then, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
,

\varphi_{Y_{n}|\{\Xi_{n}=\xi\}}(t)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\xi^{\prime}X_{n})=\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\xi).

Therefore for
\xi\neq 0
,

\big\|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\{\Xi_{n}=\xi\}}\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\{\Xi_{n}=\xi\}}(t)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\xi)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t.

Making the change-of-variable
t\mapsto t/\|\xi\|
, which is permissible since
\xi\neq 0
, we obtain

\big\|\varphi_{Y_{n}|\{\Xi_{n}=\xi\}}\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{\|\xi\|}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\xi/\|\xi\|)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t=\frac{1}{\|\xi\|}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t,

where
\theta=\xi/\|\xi\|\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
. Since the mapping
\xi\to\theta=\xi/\|\xi\|
from
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}\setminus\{0\}
to
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
is surjective then it follows that
\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if (
4.1
) holds.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.2
:
(i) By (
4.6
),
A_{n,k}
is positive semidefinite, so
\det(A_{n,k})\geq 0
. Therefore, to prove that
A_{n,k}
is positive definite (almost surely), it suffices to show that
\det(A_{n,k})>0
, almost surely.

It is evident that
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
has a probability density function on the underlying Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}k}
. Therefore, by a result of
Malley 1983
, the probability distribution of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
assigns zero probability to the zeros of any non-trivial polynomial in the components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
. Since
\det(A_{n,k})
is a non-trivial polynomial in the components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
then, by Malley’s theorem,
\mathbb{P}\big(\det(A_{n,k})=0\big)=0
. Therefore
\det(A_{n,k})>0
, almost surely.

(ii) By (
4.2
),

A_{n+1,k}-A_{n,k}=\sum_{m=d_{n}+1}^{d_{n+1}}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m}\\
\vdots\\
\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m}\end{pmatrix}(\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m}),

which is positive semidefinite, so
A_{n+1,k}\succcurlyeq A_{n,k}
in the Löwner partial ordering on the cone of positive semidefinite matrices. It now follows by
Horn and Johnson 2013
that
\det(A_{n+1,k})\geq\det(A_{n,k})
, equivalently,

(\det A_{n+1,k})^{-1/2}\leq(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}
; hence

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n+1,k})^{-1/2}]\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]
.

(iii) Since
A_{n,k}
is positive semidefinite then, by Hadamard’s inequality
(
Horn and Johnson 2013
, p. 505)
,

\det A_{n,k}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\|^{2}.

Since
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
are mutually independent copies of
X_{n}
then it follows that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big]\geq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\|^{-1}=\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|X_{n}\|^{-1}\big)^{k}.

As shown before,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big]<\infty
, so it follows that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{-1})<\infty
. Noting that
\|X_{n}\|^{2}=A_{n,1}
then we have also shown that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det A_{n,1})^{-1/2}\big]<\infty
.

Next, write
A_{n,k}
in partitioned form,

A_{n,k}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{n,k-1}&\begin{matrix}\widetilde{X}_{n,1}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\\
{}\\
\vdots\\
{}\\
\widetilde{X}_{n,k-1}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\end{matrix}\\
&\\
\begin{matrix}\widetilde{X}_{n,k}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,1}&\cdots&\widetilde{X}_{n,k}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,k-1}\end{matrix}&\widetilde{X}_{n,k}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
A_{n,k}
is positive semidefinite then, by the Hadamard-Fischer inequality
(
Horn and Johnson 2013
, p. 506)
,

\det(A_{n,k})\leq\det(A_{n,k-1})\cdot(\widetilde{X}_{n,k}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,k})=\|\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\|^{2}\,\det(A_{n,k-1}),

equivalently,

\|\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\|^{-1}(\det A_{n,k-1})^{-1/2}\leq(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}.

As
\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
is independent of
A_{n,k-1}
and since
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
then, by taking expectations, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|\widetilde{X}_{n,k}\|^{-1})\cdot\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k-1})^{-1/2}]\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty.

Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k-1})^{-1/2}]<\infty
. By repeating this argument, we deduce finally that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,j})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
j=k-1,k-2,\ldots,2
.

∎

\displaystyle\mapsto(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}v^{-2}y^{2}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}^{\prime}A_{n,k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)

\displaystyle\equiv(\det\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n})^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}v^{-2}y^{2}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n})^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)

is continuous since
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
is positive definite, almost surely. By Slutsky’s theorem,
A_{n,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}I_{k}
; so by the Continuous Mapping Theorem,
\det(A_{n,k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2k}
and

\boldsymbol{1}^{\prime}_{k}A_{n,k}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\boldsymbol{1}^{\prime}_{k}(\sigma^{2}I_{k})^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{k}=k\sigma^{-2}

as
n\to\infty
. Applying to (
4.25
) the Continuous Mapping Theorem, we find that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-k/2}\,\sigma^{-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}k\sigma^{-2}y^{2}v^{-2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
, which proves (
4.12
) for the case
j=k
.

Finally, to prove the case in which
j<k
, we apply Lemma
4.2
(iii) to deduce that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,j})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,k
and all
n\geq n_{0}
. Repeating the earlier argument with
k
replaced by
j
, we obtain (
4.12
).

∎

Proof of Corollary
4.4
:
By Theorem
4.3
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}=\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}

(4.26)

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
j=1,\ldots,k
. Moreover, (
4.26
) holds trivially for
j=0
.

Suppose that
k
is even. By applying the binomial theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k-j}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, it follows from (
4.26
) that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}=\big[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}=0.

(4.27)

By Hölder’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{p}\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}\big)^{p/k}.

(4.28)

Applying (
4.27
), it follows that the left-hand side of (
4.28
) converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. This establishes (
4.13
) for the case in which
k
is even.

Next, suppose that
k
is odd. By Lemma
4.2
(iii), we have
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k-1})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, i.e., the assumptions remain valid with
k
replaced by
k-1
. Applying the conclusion obtained for the previous case in which
k
is even, we deduce that if
k
is odd then (
4.13
) holds for all
p
such that
0<p\leq k-1
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
:
It suffices to prove the case in which
j=k
since all other cases are similar.

With
V
and
A_{n,k}
independent, it follows from (
4.25
) that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt{}_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0ptf_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k}).

(4.29)

Conditional on
V
and
A_{n,k}
, by expressing the density
f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(\cdot)
in terms of the inverse Fourier transform of the corresponding characteristic function, we obtain

f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})=\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y\boldsymbol{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)

(4.30)

and, similarly,

[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)]^{k}\equiv f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})=\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y\boldsymbol{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w),

(4.31)

y\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt{}_{\Xi_{n}}\big[f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[f_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)]^{k}\big]\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y\boldsymbol{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\big[\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\left|\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}y\boldsymbol{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\big[\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big]\right|.

(4.32)

For any
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, it follows by the integral formula for the inverse Fourier transform (
4.21
) that

\big|\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\widehat{f}(w)\big|=\left|(2\pi)^{-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\exp(-\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptz^{\prime}w)\widehat{f}(w){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w\right|\leq(2\pi)^{-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\widehat{f}(w)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w.

(4.33)

Applying (
4.33
) to (
4.4
), we obtain an upper bound that does not depend on
y
; hence,

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})-f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})]\big|\\
\leq(2\pi)^{-k}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\big|\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w.

(4.34)

For
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, let
\nabla=(\partial/\partial w_{1},\ldots,\partial/\partial w_{k})^{\prime}
be the gradient operator and set
h(w)=\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\prime}w)
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Applying the mean value theorem to the integrand in (
4.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)

\displaystyle-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)

\displaystyle\equiv h(VA_{n,k}^{1/2}w)-h(\sigma Vw)

\displaystyle=\big((\nabla h)(\eta VA_{n,k}^{1/2}w+(1-\eta)\sigma Vw)\big)^{\prime}\big(VA_{n,k}^{1/2}w-\sigma Vw\big),

(4.35)

where
0<\eta<1
.
Define the matrix

M_{1}=\big(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}\big)\big(A_{n,k}^{1/2}-\sigma I_{k}\big);

note that
M_{1}
is symmetric since
A_{n,k}^{1/2}-\sigma I_{k}
commutes with any power of
A_{n,k}
. Also let
M_{2}=\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}
. Since
\nabla h(w)=-h(w)\,w
then (
4.4
) reduces to

\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)=-V^{2}w^{\prime}M_{1}w\,\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}M_{2}^{2}w\big).

(4.36)

Inserting (
4.36
) into (
4.34
), and then making the change-of-variables
w\to V^{-1}M_{2}^{-1}w
in the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle[f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})-f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})]\big|

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-k}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\Big[V^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|w^{\prime}M_{1}w|\cdot\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}M_{2}^{2}w\big){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w\Big]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-k}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\Big[(\det M_{2})^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|w^{\prime}M_{3}w|\cdot\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\|w\|^{2}\big){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w\Big],

(4.37)

where

M_{3}=M_{2}^{-1}M_{1}M_{2}^{-1}=(A_{n,k}^{1/2}-\sigma I_{k})(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k})^{-1}.

(4.38)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
|w^{\prime}M_{3}w|\leq\|M_{3}\|_{F}\|w\|^{2}
for all
w
. Also, it is a simple Gaussian integral that

(2\pi)^{-k/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|w\|^{2}\,\exp\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\|w\|^{2}\big){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w=k.

Therefore (
4.4
) reduces to

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})-f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})]\big|\\
\leq(2\pi)^{-k/2}k\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det M_{2})^{-1}\|M_{3}\|_{F}\big].

(4.39)

By an inequality of
Wihler 2009
,

\|A_{n,k}^{1/2}-\sigma I_{k}\|_{F}\leq k^{1/4}\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{1/2},

hence by (
4.38
) and the submultiplicativity property of the Frobenius norm,

\displaystyle\|M_{3}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{n,k}^{1/2}-\sigma I_{k}\|_{F}\cdot\|(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k})^{-1}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq k^{1/4}\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{1/2}\cdot\|(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k})^{-1}\|_{F}.

(4.40)

Denote by
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
the eigenvalues of
A_{n,k}^{1/2}
. Then

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}\big)^{-1}\big\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big[\big(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}\big)^{-2}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big(\eta\lambda_{j}+(1-\eta)\sigma\big)^{-2}.

Since the function
t\mapsto t^{-2}
,
t>0
, is convex then

(\eta\lambda_{j}+(1-\eta)\sigma)^{-2}\leq\eta\lambda_{j}^{-2}+(1-\eta)\sigma^{-2},

j=1,\ldots,k
. Therefore

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}\big)^{-1}\big\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big[\eta\lambda_{j}^{-2}+(1-\eta)\sigma^{-2}\big]

\displaystyle=\eta\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F}^{2}+(1-\eta)k\sigma^{-2}\leq\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F}^{2},k\sigma^{-2}\},

hence

\|(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k})^{-1}\|_{F}\leq\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\},

which, applied to (
4.4
), yields

\|M_{3}\|_{F}\leq k^{1/4}\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{1/2}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}.

(4.41)

By a similar convexity argument applied to the function
t\mapsto-\log t
,
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\log(\det M_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\log\det\big(\eta A_{n,k}^{1/2}+(1-\eta)\sigma I_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log(\eta\lambda_{j}+(1-\eta)\sigma\big)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big(\eta\log\lambda_{j}+(1-\eta)\log\sigma\big)

\displaystyle=\eta\log(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}+(1-\eta)\log\sigma^{-k}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\log(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\log\sigma^{-k}\};

therefore

(\det M_{2})^{-1}\leq\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}.

(4.42)

By (
4.41
), (
4.42
), and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle\big[(\det M_{2})^{-1}\|M_{3}\|_{F}\big]

\displaystyle\leq k^{1/4}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\left[\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{1/2}\cdot\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}\right]\quad

\displaystyle\leq k^{1/4}\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\big)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}\big]^{4/3}\right)^{3/4},

and by substituting the latter result into (
4.39
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle[f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})-f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y\boldsymbol{1}_{k})]\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (2\pi)^{-k/2}k^{5/4}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})\,\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\big)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\ \cdot\Big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}\big]^{4/3}\Big)^{3/4}.

(4.43)

Since
A_{n,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}I_{k}
as
n\to\infty
then, by the Continuous Mapping Theorem,

\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{-k}\cdot\sigma^{-1}=\sigma^{-(k+1)}.

Therefore there exists
n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{k}
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1},\sigma^{-2k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1}\|_{F},k\sigma^{-2}\}\big]\leq 2\sigma^{-(k+1)},

(4.44)

and on applying (
4.44
) to (
4.4
) then we obtain (
4.14
).

To prove (
4.16
), it follows from the definition of the Frobenius norm that

\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k})^{2}]=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_{n,k}^{2}-2\sigma^{2}A_{n,k}+\sigma^{4}I_{k}).

(4.45)

By (
4.6
),

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_{n,k}^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}(\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n,j}\widetilde{X}_{n,r})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\|^{4}+\sum_{1\leq j\neq r\leq k}(\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n,j}\widetilde{X}_{n,r})^{2}.

Since
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
are independent copies of
X_{n}
then
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\|^{4})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{4})
for
1\leq j\leq r
; also,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n,j}\widetilde{X}_{n,r})^{2}=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(X^{\prime}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n})^{2}
for
1\leq j\neq r\leq k
. Hence

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_{n,k}^{2})=k\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{4})+k(k-1)\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(X^{\prime}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n})^{2}],

(4.46)

and, by a similar calculation,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A_{n,k})=k\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})
. Therefore by (
4.45
) and (
4.46
),

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=k\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{4})+k(k-1)\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(X^{\prime}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n})^{2}]-2k\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(\|X_{n}\|^{2})+k\sigma^{4},

which reduces readily to (
4.16
).

∎

5
Properties of the cumulative distribution function of
\boldsymbol{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}

In this section we obtain conditions under which
F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
, the conditional cumulative distribution function of
Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}
, converges uniformly to the distribution function of a mixture of normal distributions. This result is motivated by classical statistical inference, in which the well-known Glivenko-Cantelli theorem establishes the uniform convergence of an empirical distribution function to its population counterpart.

In the following results, we retain the notation of Section
4
. In particular
G
denotes the distribution function defined by (
4.3
),
V
denotes the random variable with distribution function
G
, and we also denote by
F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}
the cumulative distribution function of the
\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})
distribution.

5.1
Convergence properties of the cumulative distribution function

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
be continuous random vectors that satisfy
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
,
(C.3)
, and (
4.1
). Let
\{\Xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
be spherically symmetric modulating random vectors that satisfy (
4.2
) and (
4.8
) and are independent of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
, and let
Y_{n}=\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then, for all
j=1,\ldots,k
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}\Big|=0.

(5.1)

For the case
p=2
,
Bagyan 2015
established the pointwise convergence version of the following result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose that the continuous random vectors
\{X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}},n\geq 1\}
satisfy
(C.1)
,
(C.2)
,
(C.3)
, and (
4.1
). Also let
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
,
n\geq 1
, and suppose that
\{\Xi_{n},n\geq 1\}
and
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
are independent. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{p}=0.

(5.2)

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0<p\leq 2\lfloor k/2\rfloor
.

Recalling the well-known result that the Lévy metric is dominated by the supremum (i.e., Kolmogorov) metric, it follows that Corollary
5.2
remains valid if distances between distribution functions are measured using the Lévy metric.

In the next result, we provide a quantitative version of Theorem
5.1
. It is also evident that this result represents a Lipschitz continuity property of
F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that
X_{n}
and
\Xi_{n}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
.
and suppose also that
1\leq j\leq k
. Let
y,a\in\mathbb{R}
and let
c_{j}
be the constant defined in (
4.15
). Then there exists
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{j}
,

\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(a)\big]^{j}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(a)\big]^{j}\big|\\
\leq c_{j}\,|y-a|^{j}\,\big[\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,j}-\sigma^{2}I_{j}\|_{F}^{2}\big]^{1/4}.

(5.3)

5.2
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
5.1
:
Since
\Xi_{n}
is independent of
X_{n}
then the conditional characteristic function of
Y_{n}
given
\Xi_{n}
is

\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(t)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pttY_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n}),

t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{y}f_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(z){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{y}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}})(z){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}z=\int_{-\infty}^{y}\big[\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\Xi_{n}^{\prime}X_{n})\big]{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}z.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
be independent copies of
X_{n}
; then

\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}=\int_{-\infty}^{y}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{y}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,j}|\Xi_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt_{j}\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}z_{j}.

Set
u=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})^{\prime}
, and formally interchange
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}
with the multiple integral and the operators
\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,j}}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
; then we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,j}}\Big)\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\Big){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u.

Since
\Xi_{n}
is spherically symmetric then it follows from (
4.19
) that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{j}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{X}_{n,j}}\Big)\psi(w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u,

and by formally interchanging Fourier transforms and expectations we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{A_{n,k}}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\cdots\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{k}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{k}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\psi(w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u.

(5.4)

For
d_{n}\geq k
, it follows as in (
4.23
) that

\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{1}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\cdots\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}t_{k}{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}z_{k}{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\psi(w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)\\
=(2\pi)^{-k/2}(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}v^{-2}u^{\prime}A_{n,k}^{-1}u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v).

Substituting this result in (
5.4
) and again interchanging expectations and integrals, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt

\displaystyle{}_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{A_{n,k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}(2\pi)^{-k/2}(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}v^{-2}u^{\prime}A_{n,k}^{-1}u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v).

(5.5)

To justify the foregoing interchanges of integrals and expectations, we now show that (
5.2
) converges absolutely.

Conditional on
A_{n,k}
, let
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k})^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,v^{2}A_{n,k})
. Since

\displaystyle\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}(2\pi)^{-k/2}(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle v^{-2}u^{\prime}A_{n,k}^{-1}u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}\leq y,\ldots,Z_{k}\leq y|A_{n,k})\leq 1,

then the right-hand side of (
5.2
) equals

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{A_{n,k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}\leq z,\ldots,Z_{k}\leq z|A_{n,k}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(1)=1.

Therefore, by the Fubini-Tonelli theorem, all the interchanges of integrals and expectations are justified by the absolute convergence of the resulting integral.

Applying the boundedness and the continuity of the integrand, the Dominated Convergence theorem, and the convergence property
A_{n,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}I_{k}
, it follows from (
5.2
) that, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\idotsint\limits_{u\in(-\infty,y]^{k}}(2\pi)^{-k/2}\sigma^{-k}v^{-k}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}v^{-2}\sigma^{-2}u^{\prime}u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{y}(2\pi\sigma^{2}v^{2})^{-1/2}\exp(-z^{2}/2\sigma^{2}v^{2}){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}z\bigg]^{k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}.

Next, we follow the last part of the proof of Theorem
4.3
. Starting with the assumption that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n_{0},k})^{1/2}]<\infty
for some
n_{0}
, we apply Lemma
4.2
(ii,iii) to deduce that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,j})^{1/2}]<\infty
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
j=1,\ldots,k-1
. By repeating the earlier arguments with
k
replaced successively by
k-1
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}

(5.6)

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and all
j=k,k-1,k-2\ldots,1
.

To show that the convergence in (
5.6
) is uniform in
y
we note that the function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, also is a cumulative distribution function. Indeed, since
\Xi_{n}
is independent of
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}\leq y|\Xi_{n})\big]^{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\mathbb{P}\big(\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,1}\leq y,\ldots,\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\leq y|\Xi_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\mathbb{P}\big(\max\{\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\}\leq y|\Xi_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big(\max\{\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\Xi_{n}^{\prime}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\}\leq y\big),

(5.7)

clearly a cumulative distribution function. Consequently,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}
converges to
0
as
y\to-\infty
and to
1
as
y\to\infty
; and by a similar argument, it is also evident that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, is a cumulative distribution function, and it converges to
0
as
y\to-\infty
and to
1
as
y\to\infty
.

Since the distribution function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}
converges pointwise to the distribution function
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}
, and since both functions attain the same values as
y\to\pm\infty
then, by
Kawata 1972
,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}
converges uniformly to
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{V}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore (
5.1
) is established.

∎

Proof of Corollary
5.2
:
Since
\Xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
then
G
is singular, with
V=1
, almost surely, and (
4.8
) holds trivially. Therefore, by Theorem
5.1
,

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}-\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{j}\right|\to 0

(5.8)

as
n\to\infty
, for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. Also, (
5.8
) obviously holds for
j=0
.

Suppose that
k
is even. Applying the binomial theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k-j}.

Since
E_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)\big]^{j}
converges uniformly to
F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)
as
n\to\infty
then, by (
5.8
),

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}\to\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big]^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\equiv 0,

(5.9)

with uniform convergence in
y
. By Hölder’s inequality, for
p\leq k
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{p}\leq\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big|F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2})}(y)\big|^{k}\big)^{p/k}.

(5.10)

Applying (
5.9
), it follows that the left-hand side of (
5.10
) converges uniformly to
0
as
n\to\infty
. This establishes (
5.2
) for the case in which
k
is even.

Finally, for
k
odd, we proceed as before, applying Lemma
4.2
(ii,iii) to reduce the argument to the case in which
k
is replaced by
k-1
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
:
Without loss of generality, assume that
y\geq a
. Now define

F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y,a)=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u.

By mimicking the proof of (
5.2
), one deduces that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(a)\big]^{k}=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y,a)\big],

and by applying the inverse Fourier transform technique used in (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), we obtain

F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y,a)=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}u{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u.

As a special case of the preceding formulas, we also have

F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y,a)=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}f_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(u){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u=\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(a)\big]^{k}.

and

F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y,a)=\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}u{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w){{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}

\displaystyle(y)-F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(a)\big]^{k}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(a)\big]^{k}\Big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,V^{2}A_{n,k})}(y,a)\big]-F_{\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2}I_{k})}(y,a)]\big|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}u{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\big[\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big]{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}\big|\mathcal{F}_{{\hskip 1.0pt}w{\hskip 1.0pt}\mapsto{\hskip 1.0pt}u{\hskip 1.0pt}}^{-1}\big[\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big]\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u,

and now applying (
4.33
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Xi_{n}}

\displaystyle\big[F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(y)-F_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}(a)\big]^{k}-\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(y)-F_{\mathcal{N}_{1}(0,\sigma^{2}V^{2})}(a)\big]^{k}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-k}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\idotsint\limits_{u\in(a,y]^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\big|\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-k}(y-a)^{k}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\big|\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w.

Notice that the latter expectation is precisely the expectation on right-hand side of (
4.34
). By applying the upper bound obtained in (
4.4
) for that expectation,
viz.
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

\displaystyle\big|\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}A_{n,k}w)-\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}V^{2}w^{\prime}w)\big|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}w

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{k/2}k^{5/4}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})\,\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\|A_{n,k}-\sigma^{2}I_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\big)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\max\{(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2},\sigma^{-k}\}\cdot\max\{\|A_{n,k}^{-1/2}\|_{F},k^{1/2}\sigma^{-1}\}\big]^{4/3}\Big)^{3/4},

and also applying (
4.44
), then we obtain (
5.3
).

6
Examples of distributions satisfying
(C.3)
and
(
4.1
)

We now show that the distributions considered in Examples
3.1
-
3.4
satisfy the assumptions in Theorems
4.3
and
5.1
. Since we have already verified
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
for those examples then we need only to verify the integrability requirements
(C.3)
and (
4.1
). Further, we provide examples of vectors
\Xi_{n}
that satisfy the preceding results.

Example 6.1
.

(Continuation of Example
3.4
):
Let
X_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Sigma_{n})
where
\Sigma_{n}
is positive definite. Then
\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)=\exp(-t^{2}\theta^{\prime}\Sigma_{n}\theta/2)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
. So (
4.1
) holds trivially, and
\varphi_{Y_{n}|\Xi_{n}}
is integrable for almost all values of
\Xi_{n}
.

Let
H_{n}
be a
d_{n}\times d_{n}
orthogonal matrix such that
H_{n}\Sigma_{n}H_{n}^{\prime}
is diagonal. Since
A_{n,k}
is unchanged when each
\widetilde{X}_{n,j}
is transformed to
H_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
then, without loss of generality, we assume that
\Sigma_{n}
is diagonal and denote by
\lambda_{n;1},\ldots,\lambda_{n;d_{n}}
its diagonal entries.
Since
\Sigma_{n}
is diagonal then all
kd_{n}
entries,
\{\widetilde{X}_{n,j;m},1\leq j\leq k,1\leq m\leq d_{n}\}
, of the matrix
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
are mutually independent. Also
(\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m})^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}_{k}(0,\lambda_{n;m}I_{k})
,
1\leq m\leq d_{n}
, so the
k\times k
matrix

W_{n,m}:=\lambda_{n;m}^{-1}(\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m})^{\prime}(\widetilde{X}_{n,1;m},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k;m})

has a Wishart distribution with
1
degree-of-freedom and matrix parameter
I_{k}
, written
W_{n,m}\sim\mathcal{W}_{k}(1,I_{k})
. Moreover,
W_{n,1},\ldots,W_{n,d_{n}}
are mutually independent and identically distributed as
\mathcal{W}_{k}(1,I_{k})
and, by (
4.2
),

A_{n,k}=\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;m}W_{n,m}.

(6.1)

Let
\lambda_{n;0}=\min\{\lambda_{n;m},1\leq m\leq d_{n}\}
, the smallest eigenvalue of
\Sigma_{n}
, and define
W_{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}W_{n,m}
. Then
W_{n}\sim\mathcal{W}_{k}(d_{n},I_{k})
, which is a nonsingular Wishart distribution since
d_{n}\geq k
, and by (
6.1
),

A_{n,k}=\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}\lambda_{n;0}W_{n,m}+\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}(\lambda_{n;m}-\lambda_{n;0})W_{n,m}=\lambda_{n;0}W_{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{d_{n}}(\lambda_{n;m}-\lambda_{n;0})W_{n,m},

a nonnegative linear combination of positive semidefinite matrices. Therefore

\det(A_{n,k})\geq\det\big(\lambda_{n;0}W_{n}\big)=\lambda_{n;0}^{k}\det(W_{n}).

By a well-known result (
Muirhead 1982
) for the moments of the determinant of a nonsingular Wishart matrix, for
d_{n}\geq k+1
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]\leq\lambda_{n;0}^{-k/2}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det W_{n})^{-1/2}]=2^{-k/2}\lambda_{n;0}^{-k/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(d_{n}-j))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(d_{n}-j+1))}.

(6.2)

Hence
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
n
such that
d_{n}\geq k+1
.

Note that for the case in which
\Sigma_{n}=d_{n}^{-1}\sigma^{2}I_{d_{n}}
, which is the special case of (
3.10
) with
r=0
, it follows from (
6.1
) that
A_{n,k}\sim\mathcal{W}_{k}(d_{n},d_{n}^{-1}\sigma^{2}I_{k})
. Then by (
6.2
),

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]=2^{-k/2}d_{n}^{k/2}\sigma^{-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(d_{n}-j))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(d_{n}-j+1))}.

(6.3)

By applying Stirling’s approximation for the gamma function, it follows from (
6.3
) that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]\to\sigma^{-k}
as
n\to\infty
. This result is consistent with
(C.1)
and
(C.2)
since, under those assumptions,
A_{n,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{2}I_{k}
and therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]\to\det(\sigma^{2}I_{k})^{-1/2}\equiv\sigma^{-k}
as
n\to\infty
.

Example 6.2
.

(Continuation of Example
3.1
): Let
\Theta_{n}
be Bingham-distributed with matrix parameter
\Sigma_{n}
. As noted earlier, the density function (
3.1
) remains unchanged if
\Sigma_{n}
is replaced by
\Sigma_{n}-\tau I_{d_{n}}
, for any constant
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. By choosing
\tau
suitably large we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\Sigma_{n}
is negative definite; and now we define
\Lambda_{n}=(-2\Sigma_{n})^{-1}
, so that
\Lambda_{n}
is positive definite.

As noted by
Bingham 1985
and
Kume and Walker 2009
, the Bingham distribution arises by constraining the multivariate normal distribution to
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
; i.e., if
Z_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Lambda_{n})
then
\Theta_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}Z_{n}{\big|}\{\|Z_{n}\|=1\}
. Therefore for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
,

\varphi_{\Theta_{n}}(t\theta)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}\Theta_{n})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Z_{n}|\{\|Z_{n}\|=1\}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}Z_{n}).

For fixed
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, suppose that
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{\Theta_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t
diverges. By the change-of-variable
t\to st
, where
s>0
, it follows that
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{\Theta_{n}}(st\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t
diverges for all
s
. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{\Theta_{n}}(st\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Z_{n}|\{\|Z_{n}\|=1\}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptst\theta^{\prime}Z_{n})|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{Z_{n}|\{\|Z_{n}\|=s\}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}Z_{n})|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t,

and then integrating with respect to
s
, we deduce that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}Z_{n}|\{\|Z_{n}\|\leq s\})|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

diverges for all
s>0
. Now letting
s\to\infty
, it follows that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}Z_{n})|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

(6.4)

also diverges. However since
Z_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,\Lambda_{n})
then (
6.4
) converges for all
\theta\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, as shown in Example
6.1
. Therefore we deduce, by contradiction, that (
4.1
) holds for the Bingham distributions.

Let
\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,k}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{Z}_{n,k}
be independent copies of
\Theta_{n}
and
Z_{n}
, respectively, and define the
k\times k
matrices
B_{n,k}=(\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,r})_{j,r=1}^{k}
and
C_{n,k}=(\widetilde{Z}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{Z}_{n,r})_{j,r=1}^{k}
. Again using the relationship between the Bingham and the multivariate normal distributions, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}]=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big|\{\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,1}\|=1,\ldots,\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,k}\|=1\}\big].

Now suppose that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big|\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,1}\|=1,\ldots,\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,k}\|=1\big]
diverges. Then we apply dilations to replace each
\widetilde{Z}_{n,j}
by
s_{j}\widetilde{Z}_{n,j}
, where
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}>0
. Each vector
\widetilde{Z}_{n,j}
remains normally distributed under these dilations, and
\det(C_{n,k})
is transformed to
(s_{1}\cdots s_{k})^{2}\det(C_{n,k})
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big|\{\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,1}\|=s_{1},\ldots,\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,k}\|=s_{k}\}\big]
diverges, for all
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}>0
. Integrating with respect to
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}
, it follows that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\big[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}\big|\{\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,1}\|\leq s_{1},\ldots,\|\widetilde{Z}_{n,k}\|\leq s_{k}\}\big]
also diverges, and letting
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\to\infty
we deduce that the unconditional expectation,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}]
, diverges.

However, by Example
6.1
,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det C_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for
d_{n}\geq k+1
. Therefore we deduce by contradiction that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
n
such that
d_{n}\geq k+1
, so
(C.3)
holds.

Example 6.3
.

(Continuation of Example
3.2
):
Suppose that
X_{n}
is spherically distributed. Then
X_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}R_{n}\Theta_{n}
where
R_{n}\geq 0
,
\Theta_{n}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
, and
R_{n}
and
\Theta_{n}
are independent. We assume that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{-1})<\infty
for all
n
.

Since
R_{n}
and
\Theta_{n}
are independent then

\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{R_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Theta_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptR_{n}t\theta^{\prime}\Theta),

and by a change-of-variable,
t\to t/R_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{R_{n}}R_{n}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Theta_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}\Theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{-1})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Theta_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}\Theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t.

The latter integral is finite, as shown in Example
6.2
, and by assumption,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{-1})<\infty
, so it follows that (
4.1
) holds.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
be independent copies of
X_{n}
, with corresponding polar coordinates decompositions
\widetilde{X}_{n,j}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\widetilde{R}_{n,j}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, and
\widetilde{R}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{R}_{n,k},\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,k}
are mutually independent.
Letting
B_{n,k}=\big(\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,r}\big)_{j,r=1}^{k}
, we obtain

\det(A_{n,k})=\det\big(\widetilde{R}_{n,j}\widetilde{R}_{n,r}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,r}\big)_{j,r=1}^{k}=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\widetilde{R}_{n,j}^{~2}\Big)B_{n,k}.

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt({\widetilde{R}_{n,j}}^{~-1})\Big)\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}]=\big(\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{-1})\big)^{k}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}].

By Example
6.2
,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for
d_{n}\geq k+1
; also
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(R_{n}^{-1})<\infty
, by assumption. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
d_{n}\geq k+1
, so
(C.3)
holds.

Example 6.4
.

(Continuation of Example
3.3
):
We again assume that
L_{n}
, the length of each side of the hypercube
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})
, satisfies
d_{n}L_{n}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}12\sigma^{2}
as
n\to\infty
, and hence
(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-1/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(12\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}
. So we assume that
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-k/2}]<\infty
, and therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-1/2}]<\infty
, for all sufficiently large
n
.

Since
X_{n}=(X_{n;1},\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}})^{\prime}
, conditional on
L_{n}
, is uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})
then
X_{n;1}|L,\ldots,X_{n;d_{n}}|L
are mutually independent and each uniformly distributed on the interval
[-L_{n}/2,L_{n}/2]
. Using the well-known notation

\mathop{\mathrm{sinc}}t=\begin{cases}(\sin t)/t,&t\neq 0\\
1,&t=0\end{cases},

we obtain, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d_{n}})^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{d_{n}-1}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n}|L_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta^{\prime}X_{n})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{X_{n;j}|L_{n}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptt\theta_{j}X_{n;j})=\prod_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\mathop{\mathrm{sinc}}(\tfrac{1}{2}L_{n}\theta_{j}t).

(6.5)

Suppose that
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d_{n}}\neq 0
, then by applying to (
6.4
) the generalized Hölder inequality, we find that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\leq\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathop{\mathrm{sinc}}(\tfrac{1}{2}L_{n}|\theta_{j}|t)|^{d_{n}}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\bigg)^{1/d_{n}}.

Making the change-of-variable
t\to 2t/L_{n}|\theta_{j}|
in the
j
th integral and simplifying the resulting product, we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\leq 2L_{n}^{-1}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{n}}|\theta_{j}|\bigg)^{-1/d_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathop{\mathrm{sinc}}t|^{d_{n}}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t.

(6.6)

Borwein, et al. 2010
, during the proof of their Lemma 2, showed that there exists a universal constant
c_{0}
such that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathop{\mathrm{sinc}}t|^{d_{n}}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\leq c_{0}\,d_{n}^{-1/2}

for all
d_{n}\geq 2
. Therefore it follows from (
6.6
) that

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\leq 2c_{0}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-1/2}]\cdot\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{n}}|\theta_{j}|\bigg)^{-1/d_{n}}<\infty.

(6.7)

Since

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|\geq|\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|=|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)|

then, starting from the right-hand side (
6.7
) and applying Fubini’s theorem to interchange the integral and expectation, we obtain

\infty>\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}|\varphi_{X_{n}|L_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varphi_{X_{n}}(t\theta)|{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}t.

Therefore (
4.1
) holds.

Next let
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
be mutually independent copies of
X_{n}
. Conditional on
L_{n}
, the vectors
\widetilde{X}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{n,k}
are independent and uniformly distributed on
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})
. Since
\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})\subset\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(R_{n})
, where
R_{n}=d_{n}^{1/2}L_{n}/2
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt{}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}|L_{n}}[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]

\displaystyle=L_{n}^{-d_{n}k}\int_{\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})}\cdots\int_{\mathcal{C}^{d_{n}}(L_{n})}\big(\det(\widetilde{x{\hskip 1.0pt}}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{x}_{n,r})\big)^{-1/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\widetilde{x}_{n,j}

\displaystyle\leq L_{n}^{-d_{n}k}\int_{\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(R_{n})}\cdots\int_{\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(R_{n})}\big(\det(\widetilde{x{\hskip 1.0pt}}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{x}_{n,r})\big)^{-1/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\widetilde{x}_{n,j}.

Let
\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,1},\ldots,\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,k}
be mutually independent and uniformly distributed on the unit ball
\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1)
, and define
B_{n,k}=(\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{\Theta}_{n,r})_{j,r=1}^{k}
. Substituting
\widetilde{x}_{n,j}=R_{n}\widetilde{\theta}_{n,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, and simplifying the resulting expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt{}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}|L_{n}}[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]

\displaystyle\leq L_{n}^{-d_{n}k}R_{n}^{-k+d_{n}k}[\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))]^{k}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1)}\cdots\int_{\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1)}\big(\det(\widetilde{\theta{\hskip 1.0pt}}_{n,j}^{\prime}\widetilde{\theta}_{n,r})\big)^{-1/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}\widetilde{\theta}_{n,j}}{\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))}

\displaystyle=d_{n}^{d_{n}/2}2^{k-d_{n}k}\,[\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))]^{k}\,(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-k/2}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}].

Applying the law of total expectation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{L_{n}}\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{n}|L_{n}}[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}^{d_{n}/2}2^{k-d_{n}k}\,[\textrm{Vol}(\mathcal{B}^{d_{n}}(1))]^{k}\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-k/2}]\,\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}].

By Example
6.3
,
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det B_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all
d_{n}\geq k+1
. Also,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(d_{n}L_{n}^{2})^{-k/2}]<\infty
for all sufficiently large
n
. Therefore
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt[(\det A_{n,k})^{-1/2}]<\infty
for all sufficiently large
n
, so
(C.3)
holds.

Finally, we provide three examples of
\Xi_{n}
for which (
4.2
) and (
4.8
), the assumptions in Theorem
4.3
, are valid. In each example we have
\Xi_{n}=VZ_{n}
where
V>0
,
Z_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d_{n}}(0,I_{d_{n}})
, and
V
and
Z_{n}
are independent. Therefore (
4.2
) holds for each example, so it remains only to verify (
4.8
).

Example 6.5
.

(i) Let
Q_{\nu}\sim\chi^{2}_{\nu}
, and let
G
be the distribution function of
V=(Q_{\nu}/\nu)^{-1/2}
.
As noted in an earlier example,
\Xi_{n}
has a multivariate
t
-distribution with
\nu
degrees-of-freedom. Also, for
k=1,2,3,\ldots
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{-k}{{\thinspace}\mathrm{d}}G(v)=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})=(\nu/2)^{-k/2}\,\frac{\Gamma((\nu+k)/2)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)}.

Therefore (
4.8
) holds.

(ii) For
\nu\geq 2
, let
V=(Q_{\nu}/\nu)^{1/2}
, so that
\Xi_{n}
has a spherically symmetric multivariate Laplace distribution. Then (
4.8
) holds with

E(V^{-k})=(\nu/2)^{k/2}\,\frac{\Gamma((\nu-k)/2)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)},

for
k=1,\ldots,\nu-1
.

(iii) Let
V_{0}
be a positive stable random variable with index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(-tV_{0})=\exp(-2^{\alpha}t^{\alpha})
,
t\geq 0
. Setting
V=V_{0}^{1/2}
, it is simple to show that
\Xi_{n}=VZ_{n}
has a spherically symmetric stable distribution with characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt\exp(\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0ptu^{\prime}\Xi_{n})=\exp(-\|u\|^{2\alpha})
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{n}}
. As shown by
Brockwell and Brown 1978
,

\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V^{-k})=\mathbb{E}\hskip 1.0pt(V_{0}^{-k/2})=\frac{2^{-k/2}\,\Gamma(1+(k/2\alpha))}{\Gamma(1+(k/2))},

for all
k=1,2,3,\ldots
, and this result also follows from a stochastic representation, established by
Meintanis 2007
, for
V_{0}
in terms of the Weibull and exponential distributions. Therefore (
4.8
) holds.

On the ℓ p \ell_{p} and ℓ p , q \ell_{p,q} norms of Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices
Tserendorj Batbold

Abstract
. New upper and lower bounds for the ℓ p ​ ( 1 < p < ∞ ) \ell_{p}(1<p<\infty) norms of Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices in the form T n = [ 2 / ( 1 + 2 ​ ( i − j ) ) ] i , j = 1 n T_{n}=[2/(1+2(i-j))]_{i,j=1}^{n} are derived. Moreover, we give a complete answer to a conjecture proposed by D. Bozkurt.

15A60, 15A45

1.
Introduction

Let
C=\left[1/\left(x_{i}-y_{j}\right)\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}\neq y_{j}\right)
be a Cauchy matrix, and
T_{n}=\left[t_{j-i}\right]_{i,j=0}^{n}
be a Toeplitz matrix. In generally Cauchy–Toeplitz matrix is being defined as

T_{n}=\left[\frac{1}{g+(i-j)h}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n},

where
h\neq 0,g
and
h
are any numbers and
\frac{g}{h}
is not integer. If we substitute
g=\frac{1}{2}
and
h=1
, then we have

(1)

T_{n}=\left[\frac{2}{1+2(i-j)}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}.

In
[
7
]
, Tyrtyshnikov obtained lower bounds for the spectral norm of the Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices as in (
1
). The following upper and lower bounds for the
\ell_{p}
norm of Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices as in (
1
) was published in 1998 by Bozkurt
[
1
]
:

Let
p>1
, then

(2)

\left[\left(2^{p}-1\right)\zeta(p)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\left(2^{p}-1\right)\zeta(p)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\zeta(p)
is Riemann zeta function.

Unfortunately, the first inequality is not true for
1<p<\varepsilon_{n}
because of
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\zeta(1+\varepsilon)=\infty
. In the same paper, he conjectured the following.

Conjecture 1
.

Let the matrix
T_{n}
be as in (
1
). Then the inequalities

(3)

n^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|T_{n}\|_{p,q}<4\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},~~(p\geq q)

and

(4)

n^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|T_{n}\|_{p,q}\geq 4\left(\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},~~(p<q)

are valid for the
\ell_{p,q}~(1\leq p,q\leq\infty)
norm of the matrix
T_{n}
.

For some related results of the norm of Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices, the reader is referred to papers
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
]
.

The main objective of this paper is to derive new upper and lower bounds for the
\ell_{p}(1<p<\infty)
norms of Cauchy–Toeplitz matrices as in (
1
). Moreover, we give a complete answer to the conjecture.

2.
Preliminaries

In order to prove our main result, we need the following lemmas.

Let
A
be any
n
-square matrix. The
\ell_{p}
and
\ell_{p,q}(1\leq p,q\leq\infty)
norms of the matrix
A
are

\|A\|_{p}=\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left|a_{ij}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

and

\|A\|_{p,q}=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|a_{ij}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the power mean of the order
r
of positive numbers
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
is defined by

M_{r}(\underline{x})=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where
\underline{x}=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
. The power mean has the following nice properties (see
[
2
]
).

Lemma 1
.

1. (Monotonicity)
If
r<s
, then

(5)

M_{r}(\underline{x})\leq M_{s}(\underline{x}).,

which is the well–known power mean inequality.

2. (Continuity) The power mean is a continuous function for
r
, that is

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}M_{r+\varepsilon}(\underline{x})=M_{r}(\underline{x}).

Lemma 2
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Then the following inequality

3-2^{p}+\frac{1}{3^{p}}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{p}>0

holds for
1\leq p<\delta
. The opposite inequality holds
p>\delta
. Here
\delta=1.40485...
is the unique root of
2^{\delta}+6^{\delta}=3^{\delta+1}+1
.

Proof.

Considering the function
g
on
[1,+\infty)
defined by

g(p)=3-2^{p}+\frac{1}{3^{p}}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{p}

and differentiation yields

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=-2^{p}\ln 2-\frac{\ln 3}{3^{p}}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{p}\ln\frac{2}{3}

\displaystyle=-2^{p}\left(\ln 2-\frac{\ln 3}{3^{p}}\right)-\frac{\ln 3}{3^{p}}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{p}\ln 2<0,

which implies
g
is decreasing on
[1,+\infty[
. From
g(1)=\frac{2}{3},g(2)=-\frac{4}{3}
and
g
is continuous on
[1,+\infty[
, there is a unique
\delta\in(1,2)
to satisfy equation
g(\delta)=0
. Therefore,
g(p)>0
for
1\leq p<\delta
and
g(p)<0
for
p>\delta
, which proves the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
p>1
. Then the following inequality

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)>2^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\right)

holds for
1<p<\mu
. The opposite inequality holds for
p>\mu
. Here
\mu=1.6181...
is the unique root of equation

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\mu}}\right)\zeta(\mu)=2^{\mu-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{\mu}-1}\right).

Proof.

Let

f(p)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-2^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\right).

We show that the function
f(p)
is strictly decreasing on
(1,+\infty)
. Differentiation yields

f^{\prime}(p)=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(2n-1)}{(2n-1)^{p}}-2^{p-2}\ln 2\cdot\frac{2^{2p}-2^{p+1}-1}{(2^{p}-1)^{2}}.

To prove
f^{\prime}(p)<0
for
p>1
, we consider two cases.

Case
\boldsymbol{(1<p\leq\mu)}
: Since a function
\frac{\ln(2x-1)}{(2x-1)^{p}}
is strictly decreasing on
[2,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle<-\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(2x-1)}{(2x-1)^{p}}\,dx-2^{p-2}\ln 2\cdot\frac{2^{2p}-2^{p+1}-1}{(2^{p}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ln 3}{2(p-1)\cdot 3^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{2(p-1)^{2}\cdot 3^{p-1}}-2^{p-2}\ln 2+\frac{2^{p-1}\ln 2}{(2^{p}-1)^{2}}.

Using an obvious inequalities
1+\ln 3>2\ln 2
,
2^{p-1}<2^{p}-1
and
\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}-2^{p-2}<\frac{1}{2}
lead to

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle<-\frac{1+\ln 3}{2\cdot 3^{p-1}}-2^{p-2}\ln 2+\frac{2^{p-1}\ln 2}{(2^{p}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle<-\frac{\ln 2}{3^{p-1}}-2^{p-2}\ln 2+\frac{2^{p-1}\ln 2}{(2^{p}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle<-\frac{\ln 2}{3^{p-1}}-2^{p-2}\ln 2+\frac{\ln 2}{2^{p}-1}

\displaystyle=\ln 2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}-2^{p-2}-\frac{1}{3^{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle<\ln 2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3^{p-1}}\right)\leq\ln 2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3^{\mu-1}}\right)\approx-0.005<0.

Case
\boldsymbol{(p>\mu)}
: Since
f_{1}(p)=2^{2p}-2^{p+1}-1
is increasing on
(\mu,\infty)
, we obtain

2^{2p}-2^{p+1}-1>0.

This implies that
f^{\prime}(p)<0
for
p\in(\mu,\infty)
.

Clearly, the function
f(p)
is continuous in
(1,+\infty)
. So, there is a unique
\mu\in(1,\infty)
to satisfy equation
f(\mu)=0
. Hence
f(p)>0
for
1<p<\mu
and
f(p)<0
for
p>\mu
. The proof of Lemma
3
is complete.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Then

(6)

n^{-1}\|T_{n}\|_{p,1}\leq(n+1)^{-1}\|T_{n+1}\|_{p,1}

and

(7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\|T_{n}\|_{p,1}=2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\left(2^{p}-1\right)\zeta(p)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad p>1.

Proof.

Let
n_{0}=\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]+1
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{im}|^{p}=\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}|a_{i(m+1)}|^{p}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{i(m+1)}|^{p}>\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{im}|^{p}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{im}|^{p}>\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{im}|^{p}.

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{im}|^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle n\|T_{n+1}\|_{p,1}-

\displaystyle(n+1)\|T_{n}\|_{p,1}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-(n+1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=n\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n+1\\
j\neq n_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle>n\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n+1\\
j\neq n_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-n\cdot\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=n\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n+1\\
j\neq n_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}>0.

By the Stolz–Cesàro theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\|T_{n}\|_{p,1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(b_{j}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\right],

where
b_{j}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i(j+1)}|^{p},&j\geq n_{0}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{p},&j<n_{0}\end{cases}
. Using Lagrange’s mean value theorem, we have

b_{j}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}|a_{1(j+1)}|^{p}\cdot C_{nj}^{\frac{1}{p}-1},&j\geq n_{0}\\
\frac{1}{p}|a_{nj}|^{p}\cdot C_{nj}^{\frac{1}{p}-1},&j\geq n_{0}\end{cases}

where,
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}<C_{nj}<b_{j}
. Thus, we get that

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\left(b_{j}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}-1}|a_{nj}|^{p}\cdot C_{nj}^{\frac{1}{p}-1}+\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}|a_{1(j+1)}|^{p}\cdot C_{nj}^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{p}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}-1}|a_{nj}|^{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}+\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}|a_{1(j+1)}|^{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{p\cdot(n-1)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}-1}|a_{nj}|+\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}|a_{1(j+1)}|\right).

Applying the Stolz–Cesàro theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\cdot(n-1)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}-1}|a_{nj}|+\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}|a_{1(j+1)}|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}-(n-1)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{(n+1)_{0}-1}|a_{(n+1)j}|+\sum_{j=(n+1)_{0}}^{n}|a_{1(j+1)}|\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}-1}|a_{nj}|-\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}|a_{1(j+1)}|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}-(n-1)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}S_{n}=0,

since
S_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{2n-1},&\text{if}~n~\text{is even}\\
\frac{4n^{2}+1}{4n^{3}-n},&\text{if}~n~\text{is odd}\end{cases}
. Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(b_{j}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)=0.

In view of above, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\|T_{n}\|_{p,1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{in_{0}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}-1}\frac{2}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{(-1)^{n+1}+1}{2(2n_{0}-1)^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\left(2^{p}-1\right)\zeta(p)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4
.
∎

3.
Main Results

From the definition of
\ell_{p}
norm, we have

(8)

\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p}=\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left|a_{ij}\right|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left[2^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2n-2k+1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We begin with the following theorem that gives a lower bound of
\ell_{p}
norm of
T_{n}
, which is independent of the size.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T_{n}
be as in (
1
), and let
p\geq 1
. If
m\geq n
, then

(9)

2\leq n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T_{n}\|_{p}\leq m^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T_{m}\|_{p}.

Proof.

From the equality (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}

\displaystyle\|T_{n+1}\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{n}\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p}}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{2n-2k+3}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{2^{p}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2n-2k+1}{(2k-1)^{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{p}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{2n-2k+3}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2n-2k+1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{p}\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)(2n+1)^{p}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{2n-2k+3}{(n+1)(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{2n-2k+1}{n(2k-1)^{p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2^{p}\left(\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{p}}+\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p-1}}\right)>0.

Thus, we have

2=\|T_{1}\|_{p}\leq n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T_{n}\|_{p}\leq m^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T_{m}\|_{p}

∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
T_{n}
be as in (
1
), and let
p>1,n\geq 2
. Then

(10)

\left[\left(1+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)(2^{p}-1)\zeta(p)-C^{\prime}(n,p)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T_{n}\|_{p}<\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(2^{p}-1)\zeta(p)-C^{\prime\prime}(n,p)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

C^{\prime}(n,p)=\frac{n^{2}+2}{(p-1)n^{p+1}}+\frac{2^{p+1}}{n(2n-1)^{p}},\quad C^{\prime\prime}(n,p)=\frac{2^{p-1}}{(p-1)(2n+1)^{p-1}}

Proof.

By the equality (
8
) and using the Binet–Cauchy identity with
a_{k}=\frac{1}{n},b_{k}=2n-2k+1,c_{k}=\frac{1}{n},d_{k}=\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|T\|_{p}

\displaystyle=2\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2n-2k+1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2\left[\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}\right)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq k<m\leq n}(b_{m}-b_{k})(d_{m}-d_{k})\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

(11)

\displaystyle=2\left[n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}(m-k)\left(\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{1}{(2m-1)^{p}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
d_{k}\geq d_{m-1}
and
m-k\geq 1
for
1\leq k<m\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}

\displaystyle(m-k)\left(\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{1}{(2m-1)^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle>\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{(2m-3)^{p}}-\frac{1}{(2m-1)^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{n}{(2n-1)^{p}},

hence

\|T\|_{p}>2n^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\left(1+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{2}{n(2n-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

On the other hand, the function
\frac{1}{(2x-1)^{p}}
is a convex on interval
[k-\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}]
, application of the well-known Hermite–Hadamard inequality on intervals
[k-\frac{1}{2},k+\frac{1}{2}],~k\geq n+1
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{k-\frac{1}{2}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{(2x-1)^{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-\int_{n+\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2x-1)^{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-\frac{1}{2^{p}(p-1)n^{p-1}}.

Then it follows

n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|T\|_{p}>\left[\left(1+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)(2^{p}-1)\zeta(p)-\frac{n^{2}+2}{(p-1)n^{p+1}}-\frac{2^{p+1}}{n(2n-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now we shall prove the last inequality in (
10
). From (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=2\left[n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}(m-k)\left(\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\frac{1}{(2m-1)^{p}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle<2\left[n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\sum_{m=k+1}^{n}(m-k)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2\left[n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(n-k)(n-k+1)}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left[n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(n-1)n}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2n^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the decreasing property of the function
\frac{1}{(2x-1)^{p}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}

\displaystyle<\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-\int_{n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2x-1)^{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\right)\zeta(p)-\frac{1}{2(p-1)(2n+1)^{p-1}}.

Thus, we get

n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{p}<\left(2-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(2^{p}-1)\zeta(p)-\frac{2^{p-1}}{(p-1)(2n+1)^{p-1}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

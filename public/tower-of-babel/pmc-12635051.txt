Group heterogeneity in the relationship between physical health level and psychological depression in college students
Authors: Changhao Ma, Qian Yang, Xiaoyu Li, Wenliang Ji, Shenghua Qi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to their different majors and the gradual diversification and complexity of the things they encounter, students’ thinking gradually matures, and the differences in mental health levels among individuals become obvious in the university stage. This study explores the group heterogeneity of the relationship between different physical health types of college students and their psychological depression, in order to provide scientific basis and methodological reference for alleviating students’ psychological depression and ensuring their psychological health. A random sampling method was used to conduct a questionnaire survey on 1,367 students in their first year of college in Shandong Province, China, which included basic information such as students’ gender and The Centerfor Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CES-D). Discriminant analysis, K-means cluster analysis and analysis of variance were used to subdivide and cluster college students’ physical fitness test scores, and to explore the heterogeneity of the relationship between each type of clustering and psychological depression. (1) The physical fitness level of college students can be clustered and subdivided into 4 types: Physique-Functional Type, Cardio-Resilient Type, All-Around Elite Type and Speed-Endurance Hybrid Type. (2) Significant differences (p < 0.05) were found among the four different types of college students in terms of gender, as well as depressed affect, somatic and retarded activity, and interpersonal depression dimensions and overall depression levels. (3) Psychological depression levels of college students in All-Around Elite Type and Speed-Endurance Hybrid Type were significantly lower than Physique-Functional Type and Cardio-Resilient Type. There are heterogeneity differences in the depression levels among college students with different physical health types. It might be possible to predict their own psychological depression levels based on their different physical health types, with the aim of taking differentiated improvement methods as early as possible to intervene or improve the psychological depression levels of college students. Firstly, for students with more severe psychological depression, it might be possible to improve their depression levels by prioritizing the provision of targeted training programs that enhance explosive power, endurance, and cardio-pulmonary function. Secondly, for students with relatively stable psychological depression but poor physical fitness, combined with aerobic exercises, training aimed at improving physical functions should be provided. Finally, for students who have already shown excellent overall performance, efforts should be made to maintain and further enhance their comprehensive physical abilities.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24978-6.

Introduction

The college years of adolescents are a time of many changes, including changes in the social environment and changes in the body as well as the brain and mind. Although most adolescents will grow up healthy during this period, the state of adolescent mental health remains an important issue that cannot be ignored1. According to the World Health Organization data, nearly 15% of adolescents have mental health problems2. This is also due to the fact that adolescents are immature in terms of psychological development and face greater pressures in various areas such as higher education, self, and peers, making mental health problems frequent3. Existing research data show that the detection rate of depressive symptoms among college students has ranged from 25% to 40% in recent years, with an increasing trend4. In this context, college students are regarded as one of the high-risk groups for depression5. Depression is a complex and heterogeneous condition that extends beyond low mood, with diverse clinical manifestations and long-term effects that often overlap with other disorders6. College students who are chronically depressed not only have adverse effects on their academic performance, life, and adaptability, but also may have long-term adverse effects on their physical and mental health, resulting in the exacerbation of their depressive symptoms7. Some studies have also shown that long-term depression may lead to serious consequences, including increased negative emotions, loss of pleasure, sleep disorders, and social withdrawal behaviors, and in the case of college students, serious cases such as self-harm and suicide may occur8. Therefore, strengthening the research on college students’ psychological depression not only helps to promote the development of college students’ mental health, but also provides scientific and targeted suggestions for universities to develop strategies to prevent and control college students’ depression.

Physical activity is an important means to promote the development of adolescents’ physical and mental health9. Existing studies have demonstrated that participation in a certain level of physical activity can reduce psychological problems such as anxiety and depression10,11, and that sustained participation in physical activity is effective in improving the mental health of participants12,13. This is due to the fact that physical activity can promote the release of a number of excitatory neurotransmitters and increase the positive feelings of individuals14, and physical activity can promote interpersonal behaviors15, which can lead to positive emotions and divert negative emotions16, thereby achieve a reduction in depressive symptoms. Existing literature has predominantly focused on the overall benefits of physical activity, while in-depth exploration of the heterogeneous relationship between different levels of physical health and depressive symptoms remains scarce. Particularly, there is a lack of critical analysis regarding the mechanisms underlying the association between distinct physical fitness types and depression manifestations among university students. In view of these considerations, this study examined the heterogeneous relationship between different levels of physical fitness and depression in adolescents to provide a scientific basis for guiding adolescents’ rational exercise and mental health development.

Methods

In this study, a random sampling method was used to distribute questionnaires and collect data from 1,367 first-year students studying in Shandong Province, China. The survey involved the distribution of 1,367 questionnaires, and after the consistency test to eliminate blanks, too much missing data or questionnaires with obvious contradictions before and after answering the questions, 1,312 valid questionnaires were retained, with a validity rate of 95.98%. Among them, 859 (65.5%) were male and 453 (34.5%) were female. All participants were informed of the purpose and confidentiality measures.

Inclusion criteria: ①First-year students enrolled in colleges and universities; ②Informed consent and voluntary participation in filling out the questionnaire; ③Good comprehension and expression ability, reading and writing ability. Exclusion criteria: ①those who are not willing to cooperate with the completion of the survey; ②Errors in the questionnaire, such as incorrect filling, omissions, obvious patterns in the answers, or the same responses from the same respondent.

This study was approved by the Science and Technology Office of the Science and Technology Division of University of Jinan (No.2025000096). Before the questionnaire survey, the participants were informed of the survey plan, the survey content and the research purpose, and signed the informed consent form. After agreeing to participate in the questionnaire survey, the formal survey began.

Physical fitness level

In existing studies, indicators such as BMI, sprint running, sit-and-reach test, and sit-ups are commonly used to assess the physical health or physical fitness level of the subjects1718. For instance, the sit-and-reach test is often used to measure the flexibility of the subjects, and sprint running is commonly used to test the explosive power of the subjects, etc. Therefore, when clustering the "physical health level" of college students, this study selected the Chinese "National Student Physical Health Standard", which includes the above representative test items, for subsequent test research.

The data source of college students’ physical fitness level was the data of college students’ physical fitness scores tested using the Chinese National Student Physical Fitness Standard as a guideline19. The standard comprehensively evaluates students’ physical fitness level from the aspects of body form, body function and physical quality, which is an educational means to promote the healthy development of students’ physical fitness and to motivate students to actively engage in physical exercise, and the content of the test includes: (1) BMI index in the part of body form, (2) Vital capacity in the part of body function; (3) 50 meters, seated body flexion, boys’ pull-ups and 1000 meters run, and girls’ sit-ups and 1000 meters run, and girls’ sit-ups. meter run for boys, and sit-ups and 800 meter run for girls.

The physical fitness test for college students includes various test items. To ensure the consistency of the score units for all types of items, this study conducted score definition and conversion based on the scoring standards defined in the "National Student Physical Fitness and Health Standard of China". Then, cluster analysis was further carried out. Therefore, the test data of each item do not need to be standardized again in the subsequent analysis.

Depression

For the measurement of depression level of college students, this study used the Chinese version of The Centerfor Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CES-D) revised by Zhang et al.20. The scale was developed by Radloff21 and consists of 20 question items, of which questions 4, 8, 12, and 16 are reverse scored on a four-point Likert scale, with scores of 0−3 indicating, respectively, "less than 1 day per week," "1–2 days per week ", "3–4 days per week" and "5–7 days per week". The scale is primarily used to assess an individual’s recent depressed mood and experience, and is less related to the physical symptoms of depression than other depression self-assessment scales, making it more applicable to the general population, such as college students. The higher the total score of all the items of the scale, the higher the depressive mood. Since the Chinese version of this scale has undergone confirmatory factor analysis during its revision process, the analysis results support the original scale’s 4-factor structure. Moreover, the Cronbach α coefficient of this scale in the current sample is 0.89, and the KMO statistic is 0.921. The reliability and validity are good. Therefore, no confirmatory factor analysis was conducted on the current sample in this study.

Statistical analysis

A database was established in Excel software using manual entry and online export of data. SPSS 24.0 software was used for statistical analysis, and the feasibility of cluster analysis was tested by multiple linear regression, and the chi-square test was performed on the clustering results and the depression level. The research results show that p<0.05, which proves that the chi-square test is statistically significant.

Results

Cluster analysis

Using the K-means clustering algorithm in SPSS 24.0, we conducted a further classification attempt on the physical fitness test scores of college students. K-means clustering analysis is an exploratory empirical research method22, with the number of clusters set at 3 to 6. At this point, the ANOVA test results showed that the Sig. values were all less than 0.05. To determine the scientific number of clusters, this study further employed discriminant analysis methods, respectively predicting the accuracy of classification rates for the clustering results of 3, 4, 5, and 6 clusters obtained from the K-means clustering analysis. By comparing the predicted groups obtained from the discriminant analysis method with the actual groups from the K-means clustering, it was found that the discriminant analysis with 4 clusters had a higher correct classification probability of 98%. This initially proves that a clustering of 4 categories is the most ideal. Discriminant Analysis.

Due to the certain limitations of the discriminant analysis method and Wilks’ lambda value, after determining the number of clusters to be 4, the elbow plot was established using Python programming software to search for the optimal number of clusters. After taking the cluster number values from 2 to 15, it was found that the optimal number of clusters was 4 (Figure 1). The silhouette coefficient when the number of clusters was 4 was calculated, and the result was 0.1585, which was greater than 0, proving that the clustering results were acceptable. Class I: Physique-Functional Type (n=149). This type of student has an excellent body composition (leanness/muscle tone) combined with functional strength and movement capability. The results of the study showed that the group basic physiological parameters such as height and weight (92), vital capacity (83) were outstanding and seated forward bending (75) showed good flexibility qualities. However, the standing long jump (24) revealed the shortcomings of explosive power, and the pull-up (67) reflected the moderate quality of strength, and the well-proportioned body shape needed to strengthen the explosive power. The pairwise comparison after the event showed that this group performed significantly better than Class II in terms of BMI, Sit-and-reach, Standing long jump, Pull-ups/Sit-ups, and 1000/800-meter run. Their performance in Pull-ups/Sit-ups was also significantly better than that of Class IV.

Class II: Cardio-Resilient Type (n=241). This type of student has an exceptional cardiovascular endurance and efficiency (heart/lungs) coupled with the ability to sustain effort and recover well (resilience). The results of the study showed a core strength of significant vital capacity (87), with fair performance in sprinting (72) and flexibility (68), but serious strength deficits were revealed in standing long jump (9) and pull-ups (1). The pairwise comparison after the event showed that this group performed slightly better than Class I only in the “Vital capacity” aspect.

Class III: All-Around Elite Type (n=567). Students of this type demonstrate exceptionally outstanding and excellent performance in all fields, with their overall abilities being the most. The results of the study show that this group has a balanced development of all dimensions, with peak height and weight (96), and high levels of strength (68), speed (79), and endurance (73), with no significant shortcomings, which can be molded towards a multi-sport complex athlete. The pairwise comparison results after the event showed that this group performed significantly better in BMI than the other three categories, and significantly better in Vital capacity than Class I. They performed significantly better than Class I and Class II in the 50-meter dash, Sit-and-reach, Standing long jump, Pull-ups/Sit-ups, and 1000/800-meter run. Their performance was significantly better than Class IV in Sit-and-reach, Standing long jump, Pull-ups/Sit-ups, and 1000/800-meter run. This group was only slightly lower than Class IV in the 50-meter dash.

Class Ⅳ: Speed-Endurance Hybrid Type (n=355). This type of student possesses an outstanding combination of speed and endurance. The results of the study showed that this group was excellent in sprinting (81) and endurance running (67), and had good support in basic fitness (89) and vital capacity (86), but the pull-ups (9) revealed a serious lack of upper body strength. Serious lack of upper limb strength, need to strengthen the upper limb strength and core muscle group training to break through the bottleneck. (Table 2). The pairwise comparison results after the event showed that this group performed significantly better than Class I in terms of Vital capacity, 50-meter dash, Standing long jump, and 1000/800-meter run. Their performance was also significantly better than Class II in terms of BMI, 50-meter dash, Sit-and-reach, Standing long jump, Pull-ups/Sit-ups, and 1000/800-meter run.

Discussion

In this study, the physical fitness level of the college student population was subdivided and clustered from the perspective of college students’ mental health. Based on the Streaming Center Depression Scale, a questionnaire survey was conducted among first-year students in Jinan City, Shandong Province, and 1,312 valid data were obtained. Through cluster analysis and other methods, we focused on studying the heterogeneous effects of college students’ physical fitness level on their psychological depression. Obtaining the differences in depression levels among college students with different physical fitness levels not only puts forward new considerations and suggestions for college students’ mental emotion regulation methods, but also provides new decision-making directions for college students’ physical fitness training, and the methods used can be further applied as a well-established tool for analyzing the correlation between sports and psychology.

Based on the results of the psychological depression levels of college students of different physical health types, this study concludes that the psychological depression regulation methods of college students can be differentiated according to different physical health types, and targeted training can be carried out to achieve the dual effect of effectively improving the psychological health and physical health of college students while maintaining physical and mental unity. Studies have shown that long-term endurance and resistance training can effectively reduce the level of clinical symptoms such as depression and anxiety23,24. There are also proved that cardiorespiratory training can effectively reduce and alleviate the role of cardiorespiratory training in the face of the existence of patients with perceived stress and depression25. These studies also side-step our findings. Therefore, in the case of students with more severe levels of psychological depression, they may first choose to undergo training in programs that are significantly differentiated and effective in training their explosive strength, endurance, or cardiorespiratory capacity, such as aerobic endurance running and cycling. In the promotion of the body’s explosive force and endurance at the same time, to achieve the regulation of psychological depression, to promote the purpose of their own mental health. For those college students who are psychologically stable but whose physical health has not yet reached the Class III, they can continue with the aforementioned training while also adding other types of training to enhance their physical functions and physical fitness, thereby maintaining the stability and healthy development of their mental health. For college students who are in the overall excellence type, they should carry out more comprehensive training to improve their physical abilities while maintaining their existing physical health level. For example, physical fitness, flexibility, cardiorespiratory fitness, and upper extremity fitness training should be conducted to ensure that their physical fitness is maintained while their mental fitness is maintained at a healthy developmental curve.

Conclusions

This study show that: (1) the physical fitness types of college students can be subdivided and clustered into four types: Physique-Functional Type、Cardio-Resilient Type、All-Around Elite Type and Speed-Endurance Hybrid Type, and there is a significant difference between the different types of college students in terms of their physical abilities. (2) There were also significant differences in gender, and psychological depression level among the four types of physical fitness, in which the proportion of male students in the Cardio-Resilient Type and the Speed-Endurance Hybrid was significantly higher than that of the other two groups, and was also the highest proportion of students in the natural science category. The reason for this outcome might be that there are more male students in the natural science category. And compared to female students, male students have a greater advantage in cardiorespiratory fitness with speed and endurance. While in Physique-Functional Type and the All-Around Elite Type, the number of female students is higher than the number of male students. (3) This study found significant differences in all dimensions of psychological depression (depressive mood, somatic symptoms and motor retardation, and interpersonal relationships) and in the overall depression level among college students with different physical fitness types. Notably, students categorized as the "Physique-Functional Type" exhibited the highest levels of psychological depression. This may be attributed to an imbalance between their physical advantages and psychological adaptability. Although these students possess good physical fitness, they may experience higher depressive symptoms if they lack effective psychological adjustment capabilities, particularly in emotion regulation and social adaptation. In contrast, students classified as the "All-Around Elite Type" demonstrated excellent physical and mental health, with the lowest depression levels, indicating a relatively balanced state of well-being. Other types, such as the " Speed-Endurance Hybrid Type" and the "Cardio-Resilient Type," while possessing certain physical advantages, showed depression levels lower than the Physique-Functional Type but higher than the All-Around Elite Type, underscoring a close relationship between physical fitness and mental health. Based on these findings, it is proved that there is a certain correlation between the types of physical health of college students and psychological depression.

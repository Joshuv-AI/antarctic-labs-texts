Abstract
Classical Cepheids are a cornerstone class of pulsators, fundamental to testing stellar evolution and pulsation theories. Their secular period changes, characterized through O − C O-C (Observed minus Calculated) diagrams, offer valuable insights into their evolution. While evolutionary period changes are well understood from both observational and theoretical perspectives, shorter timescale period changes (on the order of ( ∼ \sim 10 2 -10 4 days) - known as non-evolutionary period changes are yet to be systematically explored. In this work, we present a detailed and comprehensive search for non-evolutionary period changes using O − C O-C analysis of Magellanic Cloud (MC) Cepheids, based on 20+ years of OGLE photometry data. Our sample includes both the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) and the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) Cepheids, focusing on single radial mode Cepheids (both fundamental (FU) and first overtone (FO) modes). The results are grouped into two phenomena: (a) Cepheids in binary systems (b) Non-linear period changes.

Introduction

Classical Cepheids (hereafter Cepheids) are intermediate- to high-mass (
3-13{\,}{\rm M}_{\odot}
) supergiant stars undergoing core-helium burning. These variable stars are known for their large-amplitude radial pulsations and are found within a narrow region in the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram known as the classical instability strip (IS). Cepheids hold a pivotal role as standard candles due to their well-defined period-luminosity (P-L) relation, also referred to as the Leavitt law
(
Leavitt & Pickering 1912
)
. As these stars evolve across the IS, Cepheids experience changes in their pulsation periods as a consequence of change in their internal structure. The
O-C
diagram technique provides a valuable perspective on both the evolutionary and non-evolutionary aspects affecting these pulsating stars.

Extensive investigations have been conducted on evolutionary period changes in Cepheids
(
Pietrukowicz 2001
;
Pietrukowicz 2002
;
Turner et al. 2006
;
Karczmarek et al. 2011
;
Rodríguez-Segovia et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. In contrast, shorter-timescale period changes (
\sim
10
2
-10
4
days) arising from non-evolutionary origins remain largely unexplored
(
Poleski 2008
, e.g.,)
. Notably, the Cepheid V1154 Cygni is studied in detail for cycle-to-cycle period fluctuations changes using
Kepler
data, revealing light curve modulation and granulation signatures
(
Derekas et al. 2012
;
Derekas et al. 2017
)
.
Among these non-secular period changes, a fraction of them which are periodic in nature are due to light-travel time effect
(
Irwin 1952
;
Irwin 1959
, LTTE;)
, suggesting that these Cepheids may belong to binary systems. In the Milky Way (MW), a high binary fraction among Cepheids (up to 80%) is expected
(
Szabados 2003a
;
Evans 1992
;
Evans et al. 2013
)
, and out of
\sim
15,000 known MW Cepheids
(
Pietrukowicz et al. 2021
)
, only
\sim
200-300 have been spectroscopically confirmed as binaries
(
Szabados 2003b
;
Anderson et al. 2024
, see)
. In the Magellanic Clouds, the numbers are even lower, with only
\sim
14 confirmed in the SMC and
\sim
27 in the LMC
(
Szabados 1983
;
Pilecki et al. 2021
;
Pilecki et al. 2024
)
. This gap highlights the need for a comprehensive study of period changes in MC Cepheids.

We use of OGLE photometry data spanning over 20 years, specifically the I-band MC data from the OGLE-III and OGLE-IV phases
(
Soszyński et al. 2019
, and OGLE references therein)
of the survey. To construct
O-C

(
Hajdu et al. 2021

. These
O-C
diagrams are then categorized by shape into three primary groups: linear, parabolic, and others. The third group, consisting of non-linear period change candidates, are further divided into periodic and aperiodic (irregular)
O-C

4
Conclusions

Our comprehensive search for non-evolutionary period changes using
O-C
analysis of Cepheids from OGLE photometry has resulted in a compilation of two classes of candidates. The first category contains 197 candidates showing light travel time effect signature meaning they are Cepheid candidates likely of binarity nature: (LMC: 30 (FU) and 22 (FO); SMC: 85 (FU) and 60 (FO)). Theses candidates indicate a detection fraction of binary Cepheid candidates: LMC: 1.7 % (FU); 1.8 % (FO); SMC: 3.3 % (FU); SMC 3.7 % (FO). The second class consists of a sample of 1585 Cepheids candidates exhibiting irregularities in their
O-C
diagrams corresponding to non-evolutionary period changes of unknown origin. The inventory contains 695 LMC Cepheids (FU: 290 and FO: 405) and 890 SMC Cepheids (FU: 231 and FO: 659). With FO mode Cepheids exhibiting irregular period changes in systematically larger fraction of the sample than FU mode ones, we conclude that FO mode is more susceptible to period change, which in agreement with previous findings by
Poleski 2008
.

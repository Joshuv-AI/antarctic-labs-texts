Elated Numbers
N. Bradley Fox, Nathan H. Fox, Helen G. Grundman, Rachel Lynn, Changningphaabi Namoijam, Mary Vanderschoot

Abstract
. For a base b ≥ 2 b\geq 2 , the b b -elated function, E 2 , b E_{2,b} , maps a positive integer written in base b b to the product of its leading digit and the sum of the squares of its digits. A b b -elated number is a positive integer that maps to 1 1 under iteration of E 2 , b E_{2,b} . The height of a b b -elated number is the number of iterations required to map it to 1 1 . We determine the fixed points and cycles of E 2 , b E_{2,b} and prove a range of results concerning sequences of b b -elated numbers and b b -elated numbers of minimal heights. Although the b b -elated function is closely related to the b b -happy function, the behaviors of the two are notably different, as demonstrated by the results in this work.

Introduction

Happy numbers and their variations have been studied for many decades. (For a recent survey on the subject, see
[
4
,
5
]
.) In this paper, we consider a function similar to the happy function, but with a key difference.

For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we write the
base
b
expansion of
x
as
x=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}b^{i}
where
0\leq x_{i}<b
and the
leading digit

x_{n}\neq 0
. Recall the standard happy function, generalized to an arbitrary base
[
6
]
: For
b\geq 2
, define
S_{2,b}:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
by

S_{2,b}(a)=S_{2,b}\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{2},

where
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}
is the base
b
expansion of
a
.
Any positive integer that maps to 1 under iteration of
S_{2,b}
is called a
b
-happy number.
A
10
-happy number is usually referred to simply as a
happy number.

We now define, for any base
b\geq 2
, the
b
-elated function
,
E_{2,b}:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, by

E_{2,b}(a)=E_{2,b}\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}\right)=a_{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{2},

where, again,
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}
is the base
b
expansion of
a
.
Any positive integer that maps to 1 under iteration of
E_{2,b}
is called a
b
-elated number
. More precisely,
an integer
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a
b
-elated number if there exists some
m\geq 0
such that
E_{2,b}^{m}(a)=1
.
An integer
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is an
elated number
if there exists some
m\geq 0
such that
E_{2,10}^{m}(a)=1
.

For example,
21
is an elated number since

E_{2,10}^{2}(21)=E_{2,10}(2(2^{2}+1^{2}))=E_{2,10}(10)=1(1^{2}+0^{2})=1,

while
46
is not an elated number since

E_{2,10}^{3}(46)=E_{2,10}^{2}(208)=E_{2,10}(136)=46.

There are many similarities and significant differences between
b
-happy numbers and
b
-elated numbers. For example, all positive integers are both 2-happy
[
6
, Theorem 4]
and 2-elated (Lemma
4
and Table
1

One striking difference is the following frequently used property of the
b
-happy function
S_{2,b}

[
3
,
4
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Fix

b\geq 2
. Let

While this property does not hold for

Lemma 2

Fix

the leading digit of

Then

Proof.

Let

and
y=\sum_{i=0}^{m}y_{i}b^{i}

be the base

m<s
and

\displaystyle E_{2,b}\!\left(xb^{s}+y\right)

\displaystyle=E_{2,b}\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}b^{s+i}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}y_{i}b^{i}\right)=x_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}y_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=x_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\right)+x_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}y_{i}^{2}\right)=E_{2,b}(x)+x_{n}S_{2,b}(y),

∎

Another significant difference between
S_{2,b}
and
E_{2,b}
is their behavior when the base

is odd. In particular, for
b\geq 3
odd and

, while for

the situation is more complicated.

Lemma 3
.

Let
b\geq 3
be odd and let

with
a_{n}
the leading digit of
a
.

(a)

If

is odd, then

.

(b)

is even, then
E_{2,b}(a)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

In particular, each
b
-elated number is odd and has an odd leading digit.

Proof.

Let

be the base

expansion of
a
.
Recalling that

is odd, we have

\displaystyle E_{2,b}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=a_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv a_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\right)\equiv a_{n}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}\right)\equiv a_{n}a\pmod{2}.

Hence

∎

In this section, we prove that for each

Lemma 4
.

Let
b\geq 2

.

The proof parallels that for the

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle a-E_{2,b}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}b^{i}-a_{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(b^{i}-a_{n}a_{i})

\displaystyle=a_{0}(1-a_{n}a_{0})+a_{1}(b-a_{n}a_{1})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}a_{i}(b^{i}-a_{n}a_{i})+a_{n}(b^{n}-a_{n}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq a_{0}(1-a_{n}a_{0})+a_{1}(b-a_{n}a_{1})+a_{n}(b^{n}-a_{n}^{2}),

since

3.
Sequences of Consecutive Elated Numbers

In this section, we investigate the existence of arithmetic sequences of
b
-elated numbers and of some closely related classes of numbers.

Grundman and Teeple
[
8
]
defined a
d
-consecutive
sequence to be an arithmetic sequence
with constant difference
d
. For a fixed base

b\geq 2
, setting
d=\gcd(2,b-1)
, they proved that there exist arbitrarily long finite
d
-consecutive sequences of
b
-happy numbers. Since, when
b
is odd, only odd numbers can be
b
-happy, this result is the best possible.

Given a fixed base
b\geq 2
,
U_{2,b}
is the set of all numbers in cycles of the
b
-happy function
S_{2,b}
and for any
u\in U_{2,b}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a
u
-attracted number for
S_{2,b}
if for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
S_{2,b}^{k}(a)=u
. As noted in
[
4
]
, the main proofs in
[
8
]
trivially extend to
d
-consecutive sequences of
u
-attracted numbers for
S_{2,b}
.

In a similar vein, for
b\geq 2
, we define

U_{2,b}^{E}
to be the set of all numbers in cycles of
E_{2,b}
, that is,

U_{2,b}^{E}=\left\{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\mid\text{ for some }m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},\,E_{2,b}^{m}(a)=a\right\}.

For
u\in U_{2,b}^{E}
, we say that
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a
u
-attracted number for
E_{2,b}
if for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
E_{2,b}^{k}(a)=u
.
So a
b
-elated number is the same as a
1
-attracted number for
E_{2,b}
.

A concept in
[
8
]
that is key for our main results in this section is that of a
(2,b)
-good set. Specifically, for
b\geq 2
, a finite set
T
is
(2,b)
-good

if, for each
u\in U_{2,b}
, there exist

n
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
such that for each
t\in T
,
S_{2,b}^{k}(t+n)=u
.

Theorem 6

(Grundman, Teeple)
.

Fix
b\geq 2
and let
d=\gcd(2,b-1)
.
A finite set
T
of positive integers is
(2,b)
-good if and only if
all of the elements of
T
are congruent modulo
d
.

For
b\geq 2
and
r\geq 0
, define
R_{r,b}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
by

R_{r,b}(x)=\sum_{i=r}^{r+x-1}b^{i}.

Note that for each
r\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
E_{2,b}(R_{r,b}(x))=S_{2,b}(R_{r,b}(x))=x
.

Utilizing Theorem
6
and the function
R_{r,b}(x)
,
we prove a property of
(2,b)
-good sets and the function
E_{2,b}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
b\geq 2
and let
T
be a
(2,b)
-good set. Then for each
u\in U_{2,b}^{E}
, there exist
m
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
such that for each
t\in T
,

E_{2,b}^{k}(t+m)=u.

Specifically, let

n
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
such that for each
t\in T
,
S_{2,b}^{k-1}(t+n)=1
, and for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

b^{r}>\max\left\{S_{2,b}^{i}(t+n)\mid 0\leq i\leq k-1,t\in T\right\}
, set
m=R_{r,b}^{k-1}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+n
.

Proof.

Let
b
,
T
,
u
,
n
,
k
,
r
, and
m
be as in the statement of the lemma.

We use induction on
j
to show that for each
1\leq j\leq k
,

E_{2,b}^{j}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{j-1}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{k-j}(t+n)\right)=u.

First, for every
t\in T
,

E_{2,b}^{1}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{0}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{k-1}(t+n)\right)=E_{2,b}\!\left(R_{r,b}(u)-1+1\right)=u.

Next, assume that for some
2\leq j\leq k
,

E_{2,b}^{j-1}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{j-2}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{k-j+1}(t+n)\right)=u.

Then by Lemma
2
and the choice of

,

\displaystyle\phantom{=}E_{2,b}^{j}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{j-1}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{k-j}(t+n)\right)

\displaystyle=E_{2,b}^{j-1}\!\left(E_{2,b}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{j-1}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)\right)+1\cdot S_{2,b}\!\left(S_{2,b}^{k-j}(t+n)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=E_{2,b}^{j-1}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{j-2}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{k-j+1}(t+n)\right)

\displaystyle=u.

Thus for all
t\in T
,

E_{2,b}^{k}(t+m)=E_{2,b}^{k}\!\left(R_{r,b}^{k-1}(R_{r,b}(u)-1)+S_{2,b}^{0}(t+n)\right)=u.\qed

The following theorem follows from Theorem

and Lemma

.

Theorem 8
.

Let
b\geq 2
and let

. Setting

, there exist arbitrarily long finite
d
-consecutive sequences of
u
-attracted numbers for

.

Fix

and

. Let

be the desired sequence length. Set

. By Theorem

,

-good. So, by Lemma

, there exist

such that for each

. Therefore,

-attracted numbers for

.

Setting

In the more general setting of

Theorem 9
.

Given

and any even

, there exist arbitrarily long finite sequences of consecutive

-attracted numbers for

.

Proof.

For
b

8
. So let

b\geq 3
be odd. Fix

, the desired sequence length, and let
u\in U_{2,b}^{E}\cap 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Set

Set

. By Theorem

,

is
(2,b)
-good and so we can fix

,

,

, and

as in the statement of Lemma

Since

is odd,

and

preserve parity. Letting

, we have

is even,

Set

.

Fix

Now, let

\displaystyle E_{2,b}(A+h)

\displaystyle=E_{2,b}(2b^{m^{\prime}+w}+R_{w,b}(m^{\prime})+h)

\displaystyle=E_{2,b}(2)+2\left(S_{2,b}(R_{w,b}(m^{\prime}))+S_{2,b}(h)\right)

\displaystyle=8+2\left(m^{\prime}+S_{2,b}(h)\right)

\displaystyle=8+m+2S_{2,b}(h),

. Therefore, for some

. So, by Lemma

,

. Hence,

is a sequence of

-attracted numbers for

, as required.
∎

If

. If

Theorem 12

For

Proof.

(

(

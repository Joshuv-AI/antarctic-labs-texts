Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of sodium nitrite for pigs, bovines, poultry, sheep, goats, rabbits and horses (ADDCON Europe GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Montserrat Anguita, Jaume Galobart, Jordi Ortuño, Jordi Tarrés, Matteo L. ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of sodium nitrite when used as a technological additive (functional group: silage additives) to improve the ensiling characteristics of all types of fresh plant material for pigs, bovines, poultry, sheep, goats, rabbits and horses, up to a maximum concentration of 900 mg sodium nitrite/kg fresh matter. The additive is specified to be used only in the form of premixtures. Based on the data provided, the Panel concluded that, since nitrite is a normal constituent of silage and the use of the additive would not result in a substantial change in the exposure of animals, sodium nitrite as a silage additive is considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding user safety, sodium nitrite should be considered an eye irritant. This conclusion would apply, in principle, to any preparation/premixture containing the additive. No conclusion could be drawn on the potential of sodium nitrite to be a skin irritant or skin sensitiser. In the absence of data, the Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of sodium nitrite alone as a silage additive. The use of sodium nitrite in combination with other substances has the potential to improve the production of silage made from all types of fresh plant material by enhancing the preservation of nutrients and to improve the aerobic stability of silage made from easy‐to‐ensile fresh plant materials.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 10(2) of that Regulation specifies that for existing products within the meaning of Article 10(1), an application shall be submitted in accordance with Article 7, within a maximum of seven years after the entry into force of this Regulation.

The European Commission received a request from ADDCON Europe GmbH 2 for the re‐evaluation of the additive consisting of sodium nitrite, when used as a feed additive for pigs, bovines, poultry, sheep, goats, rabbits and horses (category: technological additives; functional group: silage additives).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 10(2) (re‐evaluation of an authorised feed additive). The dossier was received on 11 January 2012 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2020‐00584. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 4 December 2020.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of sodium nitrite, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3).

Additional information

Sodium nitrite was included in the EU register of feed additives following Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003. It has not been assessed as a feed additive.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 3 in support of the authorisation request for the use of sodium nitrite as a feed additive.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of the sodium nitrite in animal feed. 4

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of sodium nitrite is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 5 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023) and Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

The additive sodium nitrite (minimum 98.5%) is intended to be used as a technological feed additive (functional group: silage additives) in the preparation of silage for its use in pigs, bovines, poultry, sheep, goats, rabbits and horses.

Characterisation

Manufacturing process

The additive is produced by chemical synthesis. Tail gases from nitric acid plants (containing NO and NO2) react with sodium carbonate or sodium hydroxide solution. Fresh alkaline solution is circulated into the system and finally withdrawn with a content of ca. 500 g of sodium nitrite/L and small amounts (1%–5%) of sodium nitrate. The solution is then neutralised with nitric acid and concentrated by evaporation. The crystals are separated by centrifugation. 6

Characterisation of the active substance/additive

Sodium nitrite [International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) name] has a Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) No 7632‐00‐0, a European Inventory of Existing Commercial Chemical Substances (EINECS) No 231‐555‐9, a molecular weight of 69 g/mol and the molecular formula NaNO2.

The additive is specified to contain a minimum of 98.5% sodium nitrite, and a maximum of 1% sodium nitrate. Data on other impurities and physico‐chemical and technological properties are reported in Appendix A.

The data provided by the applicant showed compliance with the specifications. The Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the levels of the impurities analysed in the additive sodium nitrite are of no concern.

Conditions of use

The additive is intended for use as silage additive, for use of the resulting silage for pigs, bovines, poultry, sheep, goats, rabbits and horses, up to a maximum concentration of 900 mg sodium nitrite/kg of fresh material. The applicant states that silage is not to be fed to animals before the completion of a 4‐week fermentation period.

The additive is to be used only in the form of premixtures up to a maximum concentration of 245 g sodium nitrite/kg premixture.

Safety

Safety for the target species, consumers and the environment

The safety assessment of the additive focuses on the presence of its nitrite moiety in silage at the conclusion of the ensiling process. To assess whether the residual nitrite content in silage produced with the additive is substantially higher than in silage prepared without the additive, the applicant provided a series of peer‐reviewed publications. 8 Of these, 10 were identified as relevant for providing data on nitrite concentrations in silage not treated with nitrites at the end of the ensiling process. 9 These studies encompassed 21 silage products derived from various plant materials, including alfalfa, crown vetch, smooth bromegrass, tall wheatgrass, maize, white clover, Italian ryegrass, common sainfoin and oakleaf goosefoot. The plant materials were either fresh or wilted, with or without supplemental glucose added prior to ensiling. The average nitrite concentration in the resulting silages was 7.4 mg/kg dry matter (DM), with a range of 1.5–25.3 mg/kg DM.

The applicant submitted two peer‐reviewed publications and two in‐house studies which evaluated the nitrite concentrations in silages inoculated with sodium nitrite. Regarding the two peer‐reviewed publications, in the first (Knický & Lingvall, 2004), a mixture of red clover (Trifolium pratense) and timothy grass (Phleum pratense) with two DM contents (30% and 60%) was inoculated with sodium nitrite at concentrations up to 720 mg/kg fresh matter (FM). In the second (Knicky & Spörndly, 2009), a mixture of grasses (Phleum pratense, Festuca pratensis) and red clover (Trifolium repens) with DM contents of 22.9% and 46.4% was inoculated with sodium nitrite at 600 mg/kg FM. In both studies, sodium nitrite was applied to the fresh plant material as a premixture with other chemicals. The silages were analysed after 120 and 112 days, respectively. In the first study, residual nitrite concentrations ranged from 0.5 to 2.5 mg/kg DM in low DM silages and from 0.5 to 10 mg/kg DM in high DM silages. In the second study, the residual content of nitrites in the non‐treated control silage showed concentrations of 2.5 mg nitrite/kg DM (low DM) and 5 mg nitrite/kg DM (high DM).

In the other two studies 10 , green rye or grass, each supplemented with 900 mg sodium nitrite/kg fresh plant material, were ensiled for 90 days. At the conclusion of the studies, nitrite concentrations were 1.0 and 1.1 mg/kg fresh matter. The DM content of the silage was not reported.

Based on the data provided, residual nitrite concentrations in silages inoculated with up to 900 mg sodium nitrite/kg fresh plant material (0.5–10 mg/kg DM) fall within the range of nitrite naturally present in unsupplemented silage from various plant sources (1.5–25.3 mg/kg DM).

Therefore, the Panel concludes that, as nitrite is a normal constituent of silage and the use of the additive does not result in a substantial change in exposure of the animals, sodium nitrite as a silage additive is considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

Safety for the user

No studies with sodium nitrite were submitted in this application. Based on the information provided in the safety data sheet, sodium nitrite is irritating to the eyes and mucous membranes.

The additive sodium nitrite is intended to be marketed only in the form of premixtures containing a maximum of 245 g sodium nitrite/kg premixture. The applicant submitted studies with a commercial premixture containing 245 g sodium nitrite/kg and 164 g hexamethylenetetramine/kg.

The skin irritation potential of the premixture was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 404, which showed that the premixture is a skin irritant.

The skin sensitisation potential of the sodium nitrite‐containing premixture was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 406, which showed that the premixture is not a skin sensitiser.

Conclusions on safety for the user

Based on the information available, sodium nitrite should be considered an eye irritant. This conclusion on sodium nitrite would apply, in principle, to any preparation/premixture containing the additive. In the absence of data, no conclusion could be drawn on the potential of sodium nitrite to be a skin irritant or skin sensitiser.

The commercial premixture containing 245 g/kg sodium nitrite and 164 g/kg hexamethylenetetramine should be considered a skin and eye irritant, but not a skin sensitiser. The FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the skin irritation and sensitisation potential of any other sodium nitrite‐containing premixture.

Efficacy

The applicant did not provide efficacy studies with sodium nitrite alone. As the additive is intended to be used only in premixtures with other additives, a series of studies with two different premixtures, one with sodium nitrite and hexamethylenetetramine, and another with sodium nitrite, hexamethylenetetramine, potassium sorbate, sodium benzoate and sodium propionate, were submitted.

The applicant provided a series of studies assessing the most efficient combination of sodium nitrite and hexamethylenetetramine to improve the silage processing and preservation of nutrients, with a focus on reduction of DM losses. 11 Overall, the results of the preliminary tests (n = 14) showed a reduction of DM losses, butyric acid and clostridia in the resulting silages when the premixture sodium nitrite + hexamethylenetetramine was added to the different fresh materials compared to the non‐supplemented silages. The results showed similar effects to the addition of formic acid, used as positive control for comparative purposes.

Subsequently, the applicant conducted 10 laboratory experiments to assess the effects of the supplementation of different plant materials with a liquid premixture consisting of sodium nitrite and hexamethylene tetramine on the improvement of the silage production/quality and the preservation of nutrients. 12 The characteristics of the plant materials are shown in Table 2. Fresh materials from all types of forages, as defined by Regulation (EC) 429/2008, were represented: four with difficult‐to‐ensile, four with moderately‐difficult‐to‐ensile and two with easy‐to‐ensile materials.

In all trials, the fresh materials were sprayed with a fixed amount of water, either not supplemented (control) or supplemented at a rate of 3 mL/kg with a liquid premixture (PMX‐1) containing sodium nitrite (24.4%) and hexamethylenetetramine (16.4%). A positive control group with formic acid (FA) (85% purity) added at a rate of 4 mL/kg was included. The fresh materials were ensiled in 2 L mini silos, three per group, under anaerobic conditions at 25°C for at least 180 days.

Based on these results, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the addition of a premixture containing sodium nitrite and hexamethylenetetramine has the potential to improve the silage quality and the preservation of nutrients of all types of fresh plant materials when added at levels in compliance with the proposed conditions of use (about 730 mg sodium nitrite and 490 mg hexamethylenetetramine per kg fresh plant material).

The applicant also submitted four laboratory experiments to assess the effects of the supplementation of different plant materials with a premixture consisting of sodium nitrite (16.5%), hexamethylenetetramine (11.0%), potassium sorbate (8.1%), sodium benzoate (2.15%) and sodium propionate (0.8%) (PMX‐2) on the improvement of silage production/quality and aerobic stability. 14 The characteristics of the plant materials are shown in Table 4. Based on the water‐soluble carbohydrate content and according to the classification established in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, the efficacy of the premixture was tested exclusively on easy‐to‐ensile fresh plant materials.

In all trials, the fresh materials were sprayed with a fixed amount of water, either not supplemented (control) or supplemented at a rate of 2–2.5 mL/kg with the liquid premixture described above. The fresh materials were ensiled in 1.5 L (trials 1–3) or 1.7 L (trial 4) mini silos, three replicates per group, under anaerobic conditions at room temperature (20°C–25°C), for at least 98 days.

After the ensiling process, the silos were opened and their contents were analysed for DM, pH, lactic and other volatile fatty acids (acetic, propionic and butyric acids), ethanol and ammonia nitrogen in total nitrogen. The DM loss was calculated, considering the correction for the content of volatile products of the DM after ensiling. 15 Aerobic stability was determined by measuring temperature increments in insulated silage samples that were exposed to aerobic conditions for at least 7 days at ambient temperature (approx. 20°C). A rise in temperature of 3°C or more above the background was taken as indicative of instability.

The experimental data were analysed with a ANOVA or a non‐parametric Kruskal–Wallis test, depending on the normality of the data, with the diet as fixed effect and the silo as the experimental unit. The significance level was set at 0.05.

The silages treated with the premixture containing sodium nitrite showed lower DM losses and higher aerobic stability in all trials (Table 5). These results were accompanied by a significantly lower ethanol concentration.

Based on these results, the Panel concludes that the addition of a premixture containing sodium nitrite, hexamethylenetetramine, potassium sorbate, sodium benzoate and sodium propionate has the potential to improve silage quality and the aerobic stability of easy‐to‐ensile fresh plant materials when added at the proposed conditions of use (between 300 and 400 mg sodium nitrite/kg fresh material).

The applicant provided another trial conducted with the same or alternative combinations of sodium nitrite on the silage quality and/or preservation of nutrients (Lingvall & Lättemäe, 1999). However, the publication does not provide exact values of the parameters monitored, so it was not considered further in the assessment.

Conclusions on efficacy

The additive is to be used only in the form of premixtures with other additives.

The use of a premixture containing sodium nitrite (244 g/L) in combination with hexamethylenetetramine (164 g/L) has the potential to improve the production of silage made from all types of fresh plant material by enhancing the preservation of nutrients.

The use of a premixture containing sodium nitrite (165 g/L) in combination with hexamethylenetetramine (110 g/L), potassium sorbate (81 g/L), sodium benzoate (21.5 g/L) and sodium propionate (8 g/L) has the potential to improve the aerobic stability of silage made from easy‐to‐ensile fresh plant materials.

No evidence of the efficacy of sodium nitrite alone has been provided. Therefore, no conclusion can be drawn on the use of sodium nitrite alone as a silage additive.

CONCLUSIONS

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the use of sodium nitrite under the proposed conditions of use is safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment.

Regarding user safety, sodium nitrite should be considered an eye irritant. This conclusion on sodium nitrite would apply, in principle, to any preparation/premixture containing the additive. In the absence of data, no conclusion could be drawn on the potential of sodium nitrite to be a skin irritant or skin sensitiser. The commercial premixture containing sodium nitrite and hexamethylenetetramine should be considered a skin and eye irritant, but not a skin sensitiser. The FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the skin irritation and sensitisation potential of any other sodium nitrite‐containing premixture.

In the absence of data, the Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of sodium nitrite alone as silage additive. The use of sodium nitrite in combination with other substances has the potential to improve the production of silage made from all types of fresh plant material by enhancing the preservation of nutrients and to improve the aerobic stability of silage made from easy‐to‐ensile fresh plant materials.

RECOMMENDATION

The FEEDAP Panel recommends that the authorisation should foresee that silage treated with sodium nitrite should not be fed to animals before 90 days of ensiling.

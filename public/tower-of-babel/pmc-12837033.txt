Construction of probiotic Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 as a next-generation expression platform and its application in therapeutic protein production
Authors: Chenglin Wang, Lun Luo, Ziqiao Dai, Wantong Zhang, Yi Ma, Jufang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Biologics produced in Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) require rigorous removal of lipopolysaccharide (LPS), since even traces can trigger severe immune responses and potentially fatal sequelae in humans. In contrast, the clinically licensed probiotic Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN) is inherently LPS-deficient, displaying only 0.86% of the LPS activity observed in BL21(DE3). Due to its markedly reduced immunostimulatory activity, EcN is an attractive chassis for manufacturing proteins with minimal LPS burden. On the basis of the constructed strain EcN::T7 by inserting the T7 RNA polymerase gene into chromosome, using GFP as a model protein, this strain produced only 30% of the yield obtained from BL21(DE3) because both cell density (OD₆₀₀) and per-cell productivity were lower. To boost intracellular protein synthesis, we then used CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing technology to knock out ompT, iclR, and arcA, yielding the high-producer EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA. This triple-deletion mutant produced 3.2-fold more reporter protein than its parental strain EcN::T7, reaching 70% of the BL21(DE3) output. When this strain was used to produce recombinant IFNα-2b, the final protein yield reached 89.3% of that achieved in BL21(DE3). Even without extra endotoxin-removal steps, the IFNα-2b purified from EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA contained the same low level as the product from the BL21(DE3) that had been through extensive downstream cleaning. By eliminating the need for costly LPS removal, the engineered EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA becomes an economical, clinical-ready biomanufacturing platform.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13036-025-00608-7.

Background

Isolated in 1917 from the stool of a World War I soldier by Alfred Nissle [1], the Gram-negative strain Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN, serotype O6:K5:H1) is non-pathogenic and considered safe for human use [2, 3]. The therapeutic potential of EcN in gastrointestinal and systemic diseases has been widely evaluated. Commercialized in the mid-20th century as the probiotic “Mutaflor,” it has accumulated a safety record, with no serious adverse effects reported during decades of clinical use [4, 5]. Clinically, EcN has been administered for the prevention and management of infectious diarrhea, enteritis, Crohn’s disease, and other inflammatory bowel disorders [6]. Beyond the intestine, EcN preferentially colonizes and proliferates within solid tumors [7, 8], making it a versatile agent for cancer therapy and diagnostics. Genetically engineered variants that secrete immunomodulators such as interleukin-10 (IL-10) have markedly attenuated colitis in murine models of inflammatory bowel disease [9]. Furthermore, EcN has been harnessed to deliver tumor-suppressor proteins, including p53 and Tum-5, into hypoxic tumor microenvironments, eliciting potent anti-tumor responses [10].

A wzy mutation in EcN truncates the O-antigen, yielding a short-chain LPS [11]. The strain-specific O6 repeat assembled on this backbone creates a surface lipopolysaccharide that acts as an immunomodulator, boosting host regulatory responses [12]. Additionally, the lipid A moiety carries a phosphoethanolamine (PEA) modification that affects its immunogenicity by altering the lipid’s chemical structure [13]. Generally, probiotics may restrict excessive immune responses by regulating interleukin balances and reducing the abundance of additional pro-inflammatory chemokine [14]. Previous study has shown that live EcN suspensions (EcN-Susp), cell-free supernatants (Sup-EcN), and purified EcN LPS (LPS-EcN) all induce similar levels of IL-10, thereby collectively suppressing excessive inflammatory responses [15]. The underlying mechanism involves Toll-like receptor 4 mediated activation of the NF-κB pathway, which boosts IL-10 production while inhibiting the pro-inflammatory chemokine C-C motif chemokine ligand 24 (CCL24) [16]. Compared to the uropathogenic E. coli strain W536, EcN induces CCL24 transcription at merely one-eighth the level observed in W536, and it leaves the transport and activity of 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) intact [17]. Further study has shown that the LPS of EcN is the key switch for the production of nitric oxide and cytokines, thereby regulating the immune interactions between the host and bacteria [15]. Consequently, this distinctive LPS of EcN acts as an immunomodulator, not a toxin.

Conventional E. coli expression hosts have been widely used for the production of clinical biologics, including interferons, interleukins, and human epidermal growth factors [18–20]. However, residual LPS contamination is a persistent challenge in E. coli-based biomanufacturing; even trace amounts can provoke systemic inflammation, fever, and septic shock—posing significant safety risks [21, 22]. Current regulatory standards mandate stringent control of LPS levels: USP sets product-specific limits via a pharmacokinetic model (L = K/M, K: Pyrogenic threshold dose (EU/kg), M: Maximum human dose (mg/kg or IU/kg). Routine intravenous products must stay below 5 EU/(kg·h), radiopharmaceutical injections below 2.5 EU/(kg·h), and intrathecal injectables below 0.2 EU/(kg·h) [23].

Common expression systems, such as Escherichia coli, have a relatively high level of LPS. Currently, the most widely employed LPS-removal techniques include polymyxin B affinity chromatography, anion-exchange chromatography, and Triton X-114 phase separation [24]. Among these, polymyxin B affinity chromatography is the workhorse: polymyxin B (PMB), immobilized on a chromatographic resin, captures LPS while the target protein flows through. Although this approach achieves high clearance efficiency and meets stringent purity standards, its costly resin drives processing expenses to $60 (1 ml) while limiting recovery to roughly 85% [25]. Conventional affinity or ion exchange chromatography, can effectively reduce LPS levels but also cause substantial loss of target proteins due to non-specific binding, which not only drives up production costs but also decreases yield [26–30].

Meanwhile, multiple strategies are being pursued to drive down the endotoxin load of E. coli BL21(DE3). Six LPS biosynthesis genes were knocked out from E. coli BL21(DE3) to generate the low-LPS chassis, ClearColi™ BL21(DE3) [31], which has been used to produce HSP60 and LBP. The strain, however, shows inherent physiological limitations [32, 33]. Growth at 37 ℃ drops to roughly 30% of the parental rate [34]. The truncated outer membrane renders it hypersensitive to ionic strength, and it fails to grow in common rich media like SOB 2×YT and SOC. Even in high-salt LB, recombinant PspA4Pro yields remain low, highlighting a major bottleneck for broader applications [35].

Previously, we constructed the strain EcN::T7 by inserting the T7 RNA polymerase gene into its chromosome, and this introduction confers EcN::T7 controllable protein expression capabilities. And then we rationally engineered an EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA strain for high-yield recombinant protein production based on EcN::T7 strain by leveraging genomic features of BL21(DE3). As a proof of concept, the EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA strain was employed for the production of IFNα-2b without undergoing any LPS removal procedures. The resulting IFNα-2b retained full antitumor activity, and its expression level in the engineered EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA surpassed that of the wild-type strain and approached that of IFNα-2b purified from BL21(DE3) after LPS removal. These results establish EcN as a biosafe, clinically compatible, and highly efficient chassis for recombinant protein production, laying the groundwork for its further development as a next-generation biomanufacturing platform.

Materials and methods

Strains and reagents

E. coli DH5α and E. coli BL21(DE3) (Beijing TsingKe, China) were used for plasmid propagation and protein expression, respectively. The EcN::T7 strain, constructed by specifically integrating the T7 RNA polymerase gene into the wild-type EcN strain according to a previous study, was preserved in our laboratory and served as the parental strain (Table 1) in this study [36]. The validation primers of the EcN::T7 strain are in Table S1. Cells were cultured in Luria-Bertani (LB) medium, and 50 µg/mL kanamycin was added for recombinant plasmid construction and protein expression. Streptomycin (Sm+, 25 µg/ml; Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) was used for the construction of the pTarget plasmid.

Construction of the CRISPR-Cas9 edited engineered strain

T7 RNA polymerase was inserted into the tnaA target site via CRISPR/Cas9, yielding the starting strain EcN::T7. The target genes ompT (UniProtKB: P45684), iclR (UniProtKB: P16528), and arcA (UniProtKB: P0A9Q1) are located on the EcN genome (GenBank accession No. CP007799). For each locus, CHOPCHOP (http://chopchop.cbu.uib.no) was used to pick a 20-nt single-guide RNA. Homology arms (1 kb upstream and downstream of each target gene) were PCR-amplified and inserted into the pTargetF plasmid containing the corresponding N20 sequence. The resulting pTargetF plasmids were transformed into the EcN::T7 strain already harboring pCas, allowing λ Red–mediated recombination and precise deletion of the target loci. This yielded the single-, double-, and triple-knockout strains EcN::T7ΔompT, EcN::T7Δlon, EcN::T7ΔiclR, EcN::T7ΔarcA, EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclR, EcN::T7ΔompTΔarcA, EcN::T7ΔiclRΔarcA and EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA (Fig. S2). All corresponding primers are listed in Table S1, and the validation of T7 RNAP of EcN::T7 strain is in Figure S1.

Expression and quantification of recombinant protein

Green fluorescent protein (GFP) was used as a quantitative reporter for heterologous protein expression. pET28a-GFP was transformed into the engineered EcN::T7 strain, and transformants were selected on LB-kanamycin plates (50 µg/mL). Single colonies were inoculated into 5 mL LB-kanamycin, grown overnight (37 °C, 220 rpm), and used to seed 50 mL of fresh LB-kanamycin (50 µg/mL). Cultures were incubated under the same conditions until mid-log phase, then protein expression was induced with 1 mM IPTG (isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside). At each time point, samples were collected, transferred to 96-well plates, and read on a Synergy HTX microplate reader (BioTek, USA) at 485 nm excitation and 528 nm emission. Fluorescence (RFU) and OD₆₀₀ were recorded simultaneously. The specific fluorescence yield was calculated as RFU/OD600. The sampling point with the maximum RFU/OD600 value was identified, and expression efficiency was reported as RFU/OD600/h.

pET28a-IFNα-2b was transformed into both EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA and BL21(DE3). Protein expression was induced with 1 mM IPTG at an OD₆₀₀ of 0.6, followed by incubation for 10 h at 220 rpm and 30 °C in LB medium. Cells were harvested, resuspended in ice-cold TE buffer, and lysed by pulsed sonication. Inclusion bodies were collected by centrifugation, washed with 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), and then solubilized in 8 M guanidine-HCl containing 5 mM DTT. Refolding was initiated by dilution into 0.15 M boric acid buffer (pH 9.0) and incubated at 4 ℃ for 2 h, followed by dialysis against 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) with 500 mM NaCl and 5% glycerol. Proteins were purified using Ni-NTA 6FF gravity columns (Sangon Biotech, China) and eluted with 0–500 mM imidazole gradient. Fractions containing IFNα-2b were analyzed by 10% SDS–PAGE, quantified using a Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and purity was assessed with ImageJ.

RT-qPCR analysis of gene expression

The strain was inoculated into liquid LB medium containing 50 µg/mL kanamycin and cultured at 37 °C with shaking at 220 rpm. Cells were harvested at designated time points by centrifugation at 1,000 rpm for 2 min, washed three times with 1× PBS, and total RNA was extracted using an RNA extraction kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration was determined using an ultramicro spectrophotometer. Genomic DNA removal and reverse transcription were performed with a two-step reverse transcription kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China). The synthesized cDNA was quantified, diluted to 100 ng/µL, and used as template for RT-qPCR using the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) with 16S rRNA as the reference gene to detect GFP transcription level. A three-step RT-qPCR protocol was employed, and data were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

Lipopolysaccharide extraction, quantification and removal

LPS was extracted using a commercial kit (Shanghai BestBio, China) and stored at 4 °C. Residual LPS in recombinant protein samples was removed with Pierce High-Capacity LPS Removal Resin (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Endotoxin levels were determined with a chromogenic LAL Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China): absorbance at 545 nm was read on a microplate reader and LPS concentration was calculated from a standard curve.

Functional assessment of IFNα-2b

Cell viability

SK-MEL-28 human melanoma cells were maintained in α-MEM and 786-O human renal carcinoma cells in RPMI 1640. Each medium was supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin. Both cell lines were seeded at 1 × 10⁴ cells/well in 96-well plates and incubated overnight at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Treatments (six replicates each) included an untreated control; IFNα-2b purified from EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA at 100, 50, 25, and 12.5 µg/mL; and BL21(DE3)-derived IFNα-2b at 100 µg/mL. After 24 h, cell viability was measured using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance at 450 nm was recorded using a microplate reader, and viability was normalized to the untreated control.

Cell scratch assay

SK-MEL-28 and 786-O cells were seeded in 6-well plates and grown to 80–90% confluence (α-MEM and RPMI 1640 mediaum, respectively). A straight scratch was made with a sterile 200 µL tip, the wells were washed twice with PBS, and α-MEM or RPMI 1640 medium containing the indicated concentrations of IFNα-2b was added. Treatments (n = 3 wells) included: untreated control; EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA-derived IFNα-2b at 100, 50, 25, and 12.5 µg/mL; and BL21(DE3)-derived IFNα-2b at 100 µg/mL. Cell migration was photographed at 0 h and 24 h (37 °C, 5% CO₂). Migration inhibition was quantified using ImageJ v1.8.0, and data were expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was performed in GraphPad Prism 8.0, with p < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

Systematic comparison of EcN::T7 and Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) on LPS and heterologous-protein expression

In the production of protein or peptide therapeutics using E. coli as the expression host, strict regulatory limits are imposed on residual LPS to ensure clinical safety, making removal of LPS a critical downstream-processing step. To enhance the efficient expression of heterologous proteins, EcN::T7, a strain that stably expresses T7 RNA polymerase, was constructed via CRISPR/Cas9. LPS levels in EcN::T7 and BL21(DE3) strains (n = 3) were quantified. EcN::T7 exhibited 4.5 × 10² EU/mL LPS —just 0.86% of that detected in BL21(DE3) (Fig. 1A). Compared with E. coli BL21(DE3), the EcN::T7 strain exhibited better growth in both LB medium and M9 medium under the same conditions (37 ℃ with shaking at 220 rpm) (Fig. 1B). Biomass of EcN::T7 in the logarithmic growth phase was slightly higher than that of the BL21(DE3) strain. These data demonstrated that EcN::T7 showed better growth and slightly higher final biomass than BL21(DE3) strain under aerobic conditions in LB medium, underscoring its promise as a robust recombinant-protein expression platform.

GFP was used here as a protein model to compare exogenous protein expression between EcN::T7 and BL21(DE3). As seen from Fig. 1C, at an earlier stage of cell growth, the RNA level of GFP in EcN::T7 was comparable with BL21(DE3), even at the 2-hour time point, GFP transcript levels were significantly higher than those in BL21(DE3). But the mRNA level of GFP in EcN::T7 was much lower in the later period (4–8 h) than that in BL21(DE3) (Fig. 1C). This time point coincides with the key phase of massive heterologous-protein production. After 10 h of induction, the relative fluorescence units (RFU) and specific fluorescence intensity (RFU/OD₆₀₀) of EcN::T7 reached merely 1306 RFU and 2401 RFU/OD₆₀₀, respectively (Fig. 1D and E), which were well below those of BL21(DE3). Transformation of EcN::T7 with the GFP-expression plasmid severely inhibited cell growth, and the growth of GFP-expressing EcN::T7 was markedly slower, with biomass remaining lower than BL21(DE3) throughout the expression cycle (Fig. 1F). The fluorescence expression efficiency of GFP-expressing EcN::T7 was approximately 30% of that of BL21(DE3) (Fig. 1G). This poor performance may reflect preferential allocation of cellular resources toward recombinant protein synthesis at the expense of biomass accumulation, whereas BL21(DE3) maintained vigorous growth while still achieving high levels of exogenous protein. Consequently, GFP-expressing EcN::T7 lags behind BL21(DE3) in both total biomass and per-cell protein yield.

Genetic engineering of EcN::T7 for enhanced heterologous protein expression

After introducing the heterologous-protein expression plasmid, EcN::T7 and E. coli BL21(DE3) diverged markedly in both growth behavior and protein output, the former performs far worse than the latter. Genetic engineering is therefore warranted to boost both the yield and efficiency of heterologous-protein expression in EcN.

BL21(DE3) is a derivative of the E. coli B834 strain and was generated by integrating the λ-phage DE3 region—carrying the T7 RNA polymerase gene—into its chromosome. This engineered strain has become a standard host for high-level recombinant protein production, largely because it lacks the Lon and OmpT proteases, thereby minimizing intracellular degradation of heterologous proteins [37, 38]. Moreover, BL21(DE3) exhibits low baseline expression of iclR and arcA, two regulators reported to be associated with enhanced energy metabolism and protein synthesis [39]. Motivated by these features, we selected ompT, lon, iclR, and arcA as target genes for further genetic modification. These genes encode an outer membrane protease (OmpT), a serine protease (Lon), and two transcriptional regulators involved in TCA cycle regulation (IclR and ArcA), respectively. As shown in Fig. 2A, deletion of iclR and arcA impaired the cell growth, while knockout of lon showed a positive effect on biomass. Fluorescence-based quantification revealed that, except for EcN::T7Δlon, all single gene knock-out mutants exhibited higher unit fluorescence intensity (GFP/OD) than the EcN::T7 strain. For the lon-deficient mutant, the accelerated growth appears to come at the cost of reduced protein expression. The low total protein yield observed in the other strains seems attributable to their lower cell densities, because the per-cell expression level is not compromised.

Subsequently, on the basis of the well-performing single-knockout strains, a combinatorial knockout strategy targeting both genes was employed to further boost protein yield. Notably, the double-gene deletion mutant EcN::T7ΔompTΔarcA strain showed the highest unit fluorescence intensity, reaching 5937 RFU/OD600 at 10 h, which was 2.5 times higher than that of EcN::T7, despite a marked drop in biomass. Simultaneously, deletion of both arcA and iclR also showed higher unit fluorescence intensity even without biomass increase (Fig. 2B). EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA exhibited a significant improvement, reaching 7847 RFU/OD₆₀₀ after 10 h induction—3.2-fold higher than EcN::T7 strain and approximately 70% of E. coli BL21(DE3) (Fig. 2C). Taken together, these data confirmed that our metabolic engineering strategy converts EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA into a superior chassis for high-level recombinant protein production.

Preparation of recombinant human interferon α-2b by two expression hosts

Human interferon α-2b (IFNα-2b) was used as a test protein and expressed in both E. coli BL21(DE3) and EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA to assess the efficiency of heterologous protein production (Fig. 3A). Commercial IFNα-2b is produced in E. coli BL21(DE3), which yields high expression levels despite forming inclusion bodies. SDS–PAGE analysis confirmed expression of the target protein at the expected molecular weight (19.8 kDa), with most of the IFNα-2b localized in the insoluble fraction (Fig. 3B).

After purification of the target protein, the purity of IFNα-2b reached 95.4% (Fig. 3C). Subsequently, after LPS removal step, the expression levels of recombinant IFNα-2b produced by the engineered strain EcN::T7ΔompTΔarcAΔiclR reached 89.3% of that produced by BL21(DE3)-removal (Fig. 3D). Given that LPS removal is a critical step for E. coli-derived biologics, we evaluated the pyrogenicity of the purified protein. Remarkably, the LPS content of IFNα-2b produced by EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA was 0.1 EU/mL (Fig. 3E), which was comparable to that of the recombinant IFNα-2b protein expressed by BL21(DE3) after endotoxin removal using a commercial kit.

IFNα-2b was produced as inclusion bodies and then a standard workflow including denaturation, refolding, dialysis, and Ni-NTA affinity chromatography was applied for separation and purification. The preparation process and yields of IFNα-2b from EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA and BL21(DE3) were compared. Output was quantified as protein concentration (mg/mL), total protein content (mg), and recovery rate (%), each reported as mean ± SD to indicate reproducibility.

As summarized in Table 2, when cell pellets with identical fermentation volumes were lysed and denatured, BL21(DE3) yielded 77 mg of protein versus 54.5 mg from EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA, corresponding to an EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA per-biomass expression level that was only 70.7% of that achieved by BL21(DE3). Upon completion of the entire purification process, the concentration of IFNα-2b from the engineered EcN::T7 strain reached 0.639 ± 0.019 mg/mL, corresponding to 17.6 ± 2.0 mg total yield from 200 mL of fermentation broth. In BL21(DE3), the final product reached 0.729 ± 0.053 mg/mL with a total protein yield of 19.7 ± 1.4 mg. Notably, the high LPS burden in BL21(DE3) fermentation broth required an extra LPS removal step, thus leading to a 16% loss of protein. IFNα-2b derived from EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA, benefiting from the intrinsically low LPS concentration of EcN, showed even lower level without LPS removal. These results indicated that although EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA gives lower heterologous-protein titers than E. coli BL21(DE3), it streamlines downstream purification and its yield can be further increased through additional metabolic engineering.

Verification of the biological activity of interferon α-2b expressed by the EcN::T7 engineered strain

IFNα-2b (interferon-α-2b) is a type-I interferon whose primary therapeutic actions are direct antiviral activity, immunomodulation and also antiproliferative/pro-apoptotic effects. Here, the antitumor activity of IFNα-2b produced by EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA was evaluated using SK-MEL-28 melanoma and 786-O renal carcinoma cells. In SK-MEL-28 cells, IFNα-2b viability dropped to 75% at 12.5 µg/mL and fell progressively to 60% and 45% at 25 and 50 µg/mL, respectively. The 100 µg/mL dose matched the positive control (Fig. 4A). 786-O renal carcinoma cells were slightly less sensitive, showing only marginal inhibition at 12.5 µg/mL, but viability decreased to 80% and 55% at 25 and 50 µg/mL, and reached 40% at the high-dose of 100 µg/mL, statistically indistinguishable from the positive control (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) remains the workhorse strain for high-yield production of soluble, prokaryotic and simple eukaryotic proteins, yet its LPS-rich surface poses a persistent contamination risk and must be removed for in vivo or clinical applications [40]. Endotoxin control for recombinant proteins derived from E. coli remains one of the most time-consuming, costly, and variable steps in downstream processing. Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN), which was the clinically licensed probiotic, is generally considered as a safe strain owing to its unique biological characteristics and good biological safety, and the probiotic EcN therefore emerges as an ideal microbial chassis for producing metabolites and proteins of medical and industrial interest [41]. However, its promise as a recombinant production chassis is still largely untapped. Its modest expression titers remain the principal barrier to industrial adoption. Recombinant protein expression strains represented by BL21(DE3) have effectively improved the soluble expression level and production efficiency of heterologous proteins through genome-targeted knockout of key protease genes (the lon and ompT genes) [37, 38]. In addition, BL21(DE3) naturally exhibits the characteristic of low-level expression of the isocitrate lyase local regulatory factor gene (iclR) and the global regulatory factor gene (arcA), which makes the transcription level of its tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle genes higher than that of other E. coli strains [39]. These characteristics are conducive to the efficient expression of recombinant proteins by BL21(DE3).

In this study, the protein expression levels of EcN::T7 and Escherichia coli BL21(DE3) were compared from the perspective of multiple indicators (relative fluorescence units, cell biomass, unit fluorescence intensity, and fluorescence intensity expression efficiency). After induced expression, the recombinant protein expression level of the EcN::T7 strain was much lower than that of the BL21(DE3) strain. Furthermore, during the recombinant protein expression process of EcN::T7, the increase in biomass was inhibited, which could be attributed to the fact that a portion of the cellular resources in EcN::T7 was diverted to the synthesis of recombinant proteins, thereby impairing cell growth. Overall, the EcN::T7 strain appears to redirect its limited energy resources away from growth and general protein synthesis toward the production of limited amounts of recombinant protein, which reflects a survival-oriented adaptation under an inefficient expression system.

During recombinant protein expression, EcN::T7 exhibited shortcomings in recombinant protein expression. However, in the construction of engineered EcN::T7 strains, the enhanced recombinant protein expression level exhibited by the EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA strain reflects the key role of the knocked-out genes (ompT, iclR, and arcA) in improving its recombinant protein expression. In this study, deletion of the ompT gene, which encodes the outer membrane protease of Escherichia coli, can effectively reduce the degradation of recombinant proteins and significantly improve the stability and recovery rate of the target recombinant protein [42]. IclR, the isocitrate lyase regulator, is a local regulatory factor that represses the gene expression of the aceBAK operon in the glyoxylate pathway [43, 44]. The glyoxylate pathway is the main pathway for acetate assimilation. Deletion of the iclR gene can activate this pathway and promote the conversion of acetate to acetyl-CoA [45, 46]. Subsequently, acetyl-CoA is further metabolized to provide energy and precursor substances for the expression of exogenous genes [47]. It is reported that ArcA, as a global carbon metabolism regulatory factor, can inhibit the expression of genes related to the TCA cycle [48]. Its knockout can activate the transcriptional expression of genes related to the TCA cycle and significantly increase the cycle flux [39, 49]. Due to the increased capacity of the TCA cycle, it is conducive to the production of ATP required for protein synthesis and the supply of carbon source precursors, thus enhancing the biosynthetic capacity of EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA [50]. By contrast, Lon is a serine protease whose primary function is to degrade misfolded or damaged proteins, thereby maintaining intracellular protein homeostasis [51]. The decreased recombinant protein expression levels in the EcN::T7Δlon strain may be attributed to the involvement of the lon gene in indirectly regulating the expression of stress genes. Specifically, deletion of the lon gene may lead to excessive accumulation of σ³², which in turn triggers a strong stress response and inhibits the translation of recombinant proteins [52].

Through the concerted action of these three deletions (ompT, iclR, and arcA genes), metabolic pathways are reprogrammed and protein stability is reinforced, forging a virtuous cycle that dramatically elevates recombinant protein output per cell. This multigenic strategy charts a rational course for the targeted optimization of heterologous expression platforms.

Conclusions

Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN) has been widely used in the development of living therapeutics for cancer, irritable bowel syndrome, bacterial infections and so on. In this study, we successfully engineered the probiotic EcN into a high-performance expression platform for therapeutic protein production. Based on the integration of the T7 RNA polymerase gene into the chromosome, three key genes (ompT, iclR, and arcA) were knocked out. At laboratory scale, the engineered strain EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA delivered 89.3% of the IFNα-2b yield obtained with conventional E. coli BL21(DE3), validating its suitability for heterologous protein production. Importantly, EcN::T7ΔompTΔiclRΔarcA eliminates the need for an LPS-removal step during downstream processing owing to its inherently low LPS content. The recombinant interferon IFNα-2b derived from this strain exhibited high purity and potent antitumor activity in vitro, comparable to that from BL21(DE3). Taken together, the data establish the genetically optimized EcN strain as a biosafe, clinically ready, and cost-efficient platform for manufacturing therapeutic proteins, positioning it as a next-generation microbial chassis for industrial biomanufacturing.

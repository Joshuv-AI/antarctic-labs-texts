(2) Let

be a commutative ring with identity. By definition, an algebraic

-group

is an affine

-group scheme that is of finite type as an affine scheme over

. For every commutative associative

-algebra

we write

for the group of

-valued points of

.
If

is a field, we additionally suppose the defining condition that

is smooth.

Given the algebraic number field

, let

We associate with a given maximal

-group scheme

One obtains an integral structure on the special linear

. The group

2.1.
Central simple algebras of degree two

Given any field
F
of characteristic
\mathrm{char}F=0
, let
Q
be a quaternion
F
-algebra, that is, a central simple
F
-algebra of degree two.
In terms of generators and relations, the
F
-algebra
Q
, viewed as an
F
-vector space, has a basis
\mathsf{1},\mathsf{i},\mathsf{j},\mathsf{i}\mathsf{j}
subject to the relations
\mathsf{i}^{2}=a,\mathsf{j}^{2}=b,\mathsf{i}\mathsf{j}=-\mathsf{j}\mathsf{i}
for some elements
a,b\in F^{\times}
. The entire multiplication rules for
Q
are determined by the relations as given, linearity and associativity.
Although the
F
-algebra does not uniquely determine the elements
a,b\in F^{\times}
we may use the notation
Q=Q(a,b|F)
, thereby emphasising the choice of
a,b\in F^{\times}
.

A given quaternion
F
-algebra
Q
is either isomorphic to the
F
-algebra
M_{2}(F)
of
(2\times 2)
-matrices with entries in
F
or a central division
F
-algebra.
The
F
-algebra
Q
is endowed with the reduced norm, a multiplicative map
\mathrm{nrd}_{Q/F}:Q\longrightarrow F
(see
[
21
, Sect. 9]
). In fact, the reduced norm is a quadratic form on
Q
. The reduced norm of a given element
x\in Q
vanishes if and only if
x
is not invertible. Therefore, if
Q
is a division algebra the reduced norm of
x\in Q
vanishes if and only if
x=0
. The reduced trace, to be denoted

\mathrm{red\;tr}_{Q/F}
, is a linear form on
Q
.
The reduced characteristic polynomial of a given element
q\in Q
has the form

(2.1)

\mathrm{red}\chi_{q,Q/F}=X^{2}-\mathrm{red\;tr}_{Q/F}(q)X+\mathrm{nrd}_{Q/F}(q).

2.2.
Ramification

Let
k
be an algebraic number field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{k}
denote its ring of integers. Let

D
be a central simple
k
-algebra of degree two.
Given a place
v\in V_{k}
, the local analogue
D_{v}=D\otimes_{k}k_{v}
is a central simple
k_{v}
-algebra of degree two, that is,
\dim_{k_{v}}D_{v}=4
. If
v\in V_{k,\infty}
is a complex place, we have
D_{v}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
If
v\in V_{k,\infty}
is a real place, the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra
D_{v}
is either isomorphic to
M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
or the division algebra
\mathbb{H}=Q(-1,-1|\mathbb{R})
of Hamilton quaternions. A similar dichotomy exists in the case of a non-archimedean place
v\in V_{k,f}
.
For each local field
k_{v}
,
v\in V_{k,f}
, there is, up to isomorphism, a unique quaternion division algebra
C_{v}
over
k_{v}
. Using the unique unramified quadratic extension
k_{v}(\sqrt{\alpha})/k_{v}
where
\alpha
is a unit in the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
of
k_{v}
,
C_{v}
can be constructed as a cyclic algebra. Therefore,
the quaternion algebra
D_{v}
is isomorphic either to
M_{2}(k_{v})
or the unique division
k_{v}
-algebra
C_{v}
.

We say that
D
ramifies at a place
v\in V_{k}
, or that
v
is ramified in
D
, if
D_{v}
is isomorphic to a division algebra, otherwise
D
splits at

v\in V_{k}
. A given central simple
k
-algebra
D
of degree two splits at all but a finite number of places, and the set
\mathrm{Ram}(D)=\{v\in V_{k}\;|\;D\;\text{ramifies at}\;v\in V_{k}\}

has even cardinality. The isomorphism class of the algebra
D
over
k
is determined by the ramification set
\mathrm{Ram}(D)
. Furthermore, given a set of places

S\subset V_{k}
where
S
has even cardinality, there exists a quaternion
k
-algebra with ramification set equal to
S
.

We call a central simple
k
-algebra
D
of degree two a totally definite quaternion algebra if
D
ramifies at each archimedean place
v\in V_{k,\infty}
, that is,

D_{v}\cong\mathbb{H}
. Consequently, every archimedean place of
k
is real place. These central simple
k
-algebras play the role of exception in the theory of simple algebras over number fields, in particular, with regard to orders (For details we refer to
[
21
, Section 34]
).

We say that a central simple
k
-algebra
D
is indefinite if
D
is not totally definite.

Let

Let

We fix a maximal

The

We denote by

. We have a Levi decomposition of

by

The group of
\mathbb{Q}
-rational points of the centraliser

The determinant defines a homomorphism of algebraic

into the multiplicative group. We call its scheme-theoretic kernel the special linear group of

, to be denoted

(cf.
[
24
, Examples A 1.8.]

The group

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-simple simply connected algebraic group of

-rank one. We fix the maximal

-split torus

-rational points are equal to

The restriction of the root

of

to

with respect to

. We denote

. It has a Levi decomposition as a semi-direct product
P_{0}=Z_{SL_{M}}(T)N_{0}
of its unipotent radical

by

of

.
We denote by
M_{0}

the connected component of the group

.
The

2.4.
Orders in a central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

Let
A
be a central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of degree two.
By definition, a
\mathbb{Z}
-order
\Delta
in
A
is a subring of
A
with

1_{\Delta}=1_{A}
and such that
\Delta
is a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice in
A
. For each

the reduced characteristic polynomial
\mathrm{red}\chi_{x,A/k}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, given the reduced norm

\mathrm{nrd}_{A/k}:A\longrightarrow k,

we have
\mathrm{nrd}_{A/k}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Every finite-dimensional central simple
k
-algebra
A
contains
\mathbb{Z}
-orders, and each of them is contained
in a maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order.

A left
\Delta
-ideal in
A
is a left
\Delta
-lattice
N\subset A
such that
\mathbb{Q}N=A
, or, equivalently, since
\Delta
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
,
a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice in
A
such that
\Delta N\subset N
. By a left
\Delta
-lattice
N\subset A
we mean a left
\Delta
-module
N
which is a

\mathbb{Z}
-lattice in
A
.
We say that two left
\Delta
-ideals
M
and
N
are isomorphic if
M\cong N
as left
\Delta
-modules.

If
\Lambda
is a maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order in
A
, the set

of isomorphism classes of
left
\Lambda
-ideals in
A
is a finite set, and its cardinality is independent of the choice of
\Lambda
(see
[
21
, Thm. 26.4]
). Therefore, we may define
h_{A}:=|LF_{1}(\Lambda)|
for any maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order
\Lambda
in
A
; it is called the class number of the central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

.

\Lambda
is maximal as well (see
[
21

). In this situation, within the realm of group schemes of finite type defined over a Dedekind domain, we can carry through the construction of affine
\mathbb{Z}
-group schemes

and

of finite type, similar to the construction of the algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-groups

and
SL_{M}
. In fact, if
\Lambda
is a maximal order, the associated

\mathbb{Z}
-group scheme

of finite type is smooth (cf.
[
24
, Sect. 8.3]
). The algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-group
SL_{\Lambda}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
obtained by base change is the group
G:=SL_{M}
defined above. Occasionally we write
G_{\Lambda}
for the affine group scheme
SL_{\Lambda}
, and
\Gamma_{\Lambda}:=SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z})
for the group of integral points of
G_{\Lambda}
. Consequently, in this notation,
G_{\Lambda}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=G
.
Any subgroup
\Gamma
of finite index in
\Gamma_{\Lambda}
is an arithmetic subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q})
.

Given any proper ideal

\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathbb{Z}
the corresponding principal congruence subgroup of level
\mathfrak{a}
is defined by

(2.2)

\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a}):=\mathrm{ker}(SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak{a})).

It gives rise to an arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a})
of
G(\mathbb{Q})
. Using
[
24
, Prop. 4.4.4]
, one deduces that for almost all choices of the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
the group
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a})
is torsion-free.

Given a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}=(p)
in
\mathbb{Z}
,
p
a prime, and
j\geq 1
, let
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})
be the corresponding congruence subgroup of level
\mathfrak{p}^{j}
. The collection

\{\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})\}
forms a cofinal tower of normal subgroups of
\Gamma_{\Lambda}=SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z})
, to be called
\mathfrak{p}
-congruence tower. In other words, we have

(2.3)

\bigcap_{j}\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})=\{1\},\;\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})\;\text{is of finite index in}\;SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z}),\;\text{and}\;\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j+1}})\leq\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})\;\text{for all}\;j.

We note that for any
j\geq 1
, the quotient group
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j}})/\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{p^{j+1}})
is an elementary abelian
p
-group.

2.6.
The ambient Lie group

We denote by
G_{\infty}
the group of real points of the algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-group
SL_{M}
attached to the central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
M_{2}(D)
over a central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
D
of degree two.
Let
X_{G}
be the symmetric space associated with
G_{\infty}
, described as the space of maximal compact subgroups of
G_{\infty}
. In fact, all of these are conjugate to one another, thus, we may write
X_{G}=K_{\infty}\backslash G_{\infty}
for any maximal subgroup
K_{\infty}\subset G_{\infty}
. Since
X_{G}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d(G_{\infty})}
where
d(G)=\dim G_{\infty}-\dim K_{\infty}
, the space
X_{G}
is contractible. The real Lie group
G_{\infty}
acts properly from the right on
X_{G}
, therefore a given arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
of
G(\mathbb{Q})
, being viewed as a discrete, thus closed subgroup of
G_{\infty}
,
acts properly on
X_{G}
as well. If
\Gamma
is torsion-free, the action of
\Gamma
on
X_{G}
is free, and the quotient
X_{G}/\Gamma
is a smooth manifold of dimension
d(G)
.

There is a
G_{\infty}
-invariant Riemannian metric on
X_{G}
.
Given an arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
of
G(\mathbb{Q})
, we are interested in the homogenous space
X_{G}/\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is torsion-free, the space
X_{G}/\Gamma
carries the structure of a Riemannian manifold of finite volume.

Depending on the splitting behaviour of the central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
D
at the archimedean place of
\mathbb{Q}
, we have to distinguish two cases:

D

splits at infinity:
If
D
splits at the archimedean place, we have
D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, thus,
G_{\infty}\cong SL_{4}(\mathbb{R})
. The corresponding symmetric space
X_{G}
has dimension

d(G)=9
.

D

ramifies at infinity:
If
D
ramifies at the only archimedean place, that is,
D
is a definite central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
we have
D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{H}
. Therefore,
M:=M_{2}(\mathbb{H})
is the central simple algebra of
(2\times 2)
-matrices with entries in the algebra of Hamilton quaternions.
The corresponding ambient Lie group,
SL_{M}(\mathbb{R})
,
the special linear group
SL_{2}
over the non-commutative algebra
\mathbb{H}
of Hamilton quaternions, is usually denoted by
SU^{*}(4)
. This group
is the real form of
SL_{4}(\mathbb{C})
associated with the complex conjugation
\sigma:SL_{4}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow SL_{4}(\mathbb{C})
, defined by

g\mapsto\eta_{2}^{t}\overline{g}\eta_{2}
, where

\eta_{2}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&E_{2}\\
-E_{2}&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
, with
E_{2}
the identity matrix of size
2
, and where
\overline{g}
stands for conjugating each entry of the matrix
g
.

The assignment

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\mapsto(\overline{x}_{3},\overline{x}_{4},-\overline{x}_{1},\ \overline{x}_{2})
defines a real linear map
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{4}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{4}
.
Then we can realise the real Lie group
SU^{*}(4)
as

\{g\in SL_{4}(\mathbb{C})\;|\;g\psi=\psi g\}
. Its intersection with the maximal compact subgroup
U(4)
of
GL_{4}(\mathbb{C})
is the group
Sp(2)=\{g\in SU^{*}(4)\;|\;g\overline{g}^{t}=\overline{g}^{t}g=\mathrm{1}\}
.
(see
[
6
, X, Lemma 2.1]
). The symmetric space
X_{2}:=Sp(2)\backslash SU^{*}(4)
, attached to the Riemannian symmetric pair
(SU^{*}(4),Sp(2))
of non-compact type is of type A II. It is a simply connected space of dimension
d(G)=5
and of rank one.
Since the symmetric space
X_{2}
of type AII coincides with the symmetric space of type BD I attached to the pair
(SO(5,1)_{0},SO(5)\times SO(1))
,
X_{2}
can be identified with hyperbolic
5
-space, to be denoted
\mathrm{H}^{5}
.

2.7.
Strong approximation property

Since the
\mathbb{Q}
-group
G=G_{\Lambda}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, associated with a maximal order
\Lambda
in
M_{2}(D)
, is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple simply connected and
G_{\infty}
is not compact, the group
G
has the strong approximation property (see
[
11
]
). Therefore,
G(\mathbb{Q})
is dense in the locally compact group
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
, or, equivalently,
G_{\infty}G(\mathbb{Q})
is dense in
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Given a place
v\in V_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we denote by
k_{v}=\mathbb{Q}_{v}
the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the normalised absolute value which corresponds to
v
. For a finite place
v\in V_{k,f}
, the field
k_{v}
is a local field, and we write
\mathcal{O}_{v}
for the valuation ring in
k_{v}
. It contains the valuation ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
as the unique maximal ideal.

For each
v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}
, we write
K_{v}:=G_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
. Then the group
\Gamma_{\Lambda}=G_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z})
of integral points can be written as

\Gamma_{\Lambda}=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap\prod_{v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}}K_{v}
. For the sake of simplicity, we put
K_{f}:=\prod_{v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}}K_{v}
.
For any
v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}
, given any power
\mathfrak{p}^{e}_{v}
of the valuation ideal, we denote by

K_{v}(\mathfrak{p}^{e}_{v}):=\mathrm{ker}(G_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\longrightarrow G_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{p}^{e}_{v}\mathcal{O}_{v}))

the kernel of the natural homomorphism obtained by restriction. It is an open compact subgroup of
G_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
.
Any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
has a factorisation, unique up to the order of factors,

\mathfrak{a}=\prod\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{e_{v}(\mathfrak{a})},

where the product extends over the finite set of all places
v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}
for which
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
occurs with the exponent
e_{v}(\mathfrak{a})
in this factorisation of
\mathfrak{a}
.
For a fixed place
v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{v}=\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{e_{v}(\mathfrak{a})}\mathcal{O}_{v}
.
The direct product

(2.4)

K_{f}(\mathfrak{a}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}\\
\mathfrak{p}_{v}|\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}K_{v}(\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{e_{v}(\mathfrak{a})})\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{\mathbb{Q},f}\\
\mathfrak{p}_{v}\nmid\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}K_{v}

is an open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
. We see

\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a})=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K_{f}(\mathfrak{a}).

Using the strong approximation property of the algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-group
G
, the continuous map
G_{\infty}\longrightarrow K_{f}(\mathfrak{a})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})/G(\mathbb{Q})
, defined by
the assignment

g\mapsto K_{f}(\mathfrak{a})gG(\mathbb{Q})
, induces
a homeomorphism

K_{f}(\mathfrak{a})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})/G(\mathbb{Q})\tilde{\longrightarrow}G_{\infty}/\Gamma_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a})

which is equivariant under the action of
G_{\infty}
.

3.1.
A result in reduction theory

The

As an application of the main results in reduction theory (see

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D
be a central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of degree two, and let

be the algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-group attached to a maximal order
\Lambda
in the central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

Given a torsion-free arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma\subset SL_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{Z})\subset G(\mathbb{Q})

contains an open subset

such that its closure
\overline{Y}_{\Gamma}
is a compact manifold with boundary

, and the inclusion
\overline{Y}_{\Gamma}\longrightarrow X_{G}/\Gamma

The connected components of
\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma}

are parametrized by the finite set
\mathcal{P}/\Gamma
of

\Gamma
-conjugacy classes of minimal parabolic
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroups of
G
.
We have as a disjoint union

\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma}=\coprod_{[P]\in\mathcal{P}/\Gamma}Y^{[P]}.

3.2.
The boundary components

In a more general context, a description of a boundary component

as a fibre bundle is given in
[
14

in our specific case the result has a simple form.
Given the standard minimal parabolic
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup

\mathbb{Q}

We apply this construction to the character

It follows that

Given the Levi decomposition

of the minimal parabolic
\mathbb{Q}

as the semi-direct product

there is
a surjective morphism

The image

with the compact manifold

Proposition 3.2

Let

4.

4.1.

\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma}

Let
D

\mathbb{Q}
-algebra algebra of degree two. Given a maximal order
\Lambda_{D}
in

defines the
\mathbb{Z}
-group scheme

of dimension

attached to the group of real points of

Given a torsion-free arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma\subset G(\mathbb{Q})

carries the structure of a complete Riemannian manifold of finite volume but non-compact. By Theorem

is a compact manifold with boundary

We have a long exact sequence in cohomology

\longrightarrow H^{q}(\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{q}(\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})\overset{r^{q}}{\longrightarrow}H^{q}(\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{q+1}(\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow

where

denotes the natural homomorphism obtained by restriction.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

\mathsf{R}^{q}:=\mathrm{im}(H^{q}(\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})\overset{r^{q}}{\longrightarrow}H^{q}(\partial\overline{Y}_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C}))
denote the image of the restriction homomorphism

. Therefore, it follows that the image of
r^{*}

has dimension equal to one half of the dimension of

Proof.

As pointed out in
2.6

depending on the splitting behaviour of the central simple
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

at the archimedean place of
\mathbb{Q}

splits at the archimedean place, that is,

, and the corresponding symmetric space

has dimension

D
ramifies at the archimedean place
that is,

is the

-dimensional hyperbolic space, thus,

In both cases we are within the range of oriented compact manifolds with boundary of odd dimension
2k+1
.

The actual proof relies on a general result in the cohomology theory of oriented compact manifolds with boundary, with a focus on duality. Due to the lack of a suitable reference we include it in the following Proposition.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M
be an oriented compact manifold of odd dimension
2k+1
with boundary
\partial M
, and let
F
be any field of characteristic zero. The image of the homomorphism

r^{*}:H^{*}(M,F)\longrightarrow H^{*}(\partial M,F),

induced by the inclusion
r:\partial M\longrightarrow M
, is of dimension half of the dimension of

. More presicely,
if we denote

, then

\dim R^{j}+\dim R^{2k-j}=\dim H^{j}(\partial M,F)\;\text{for all}\;j.

In particular, we have
\dim R^{k}=(1/2)\dim H^{k}(\partial M,F)
.

Proof.

Given the oriented compact manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
of dimension
m=2k+1
, there is the uniquely determined fundamental class
O_{M}
in

H_{m}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
. The boundary operator in homology takes
O_{M}
to a fundamental class
o_{\partial M}
of
\partial M
in
H_{m-1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
(see, e.g.
[
26
, Chap.6, Sec.3, Cor. 10]
).

Now suppose that
N
is an oriented compact manifold. Then the cap product with a fundamental class of
N
, that is, the assignment
x\mapsto x\frown o_{N}
, defines an isomorphism

H^{i}(N;F)\tilde{\longrightarrow}H_{n-i}(N;F)
where the coefficient system
F
is any field of characteristic zero.

More generally, in the case of the oriented compact manifold
M
with boundary, then the map
x\mapsto x\frown O_{M}
defines an isomorphism

H^{i}(M,\partial M;F)\tilde{\longrightarrow}H_{n-i}(M;F)
(see
[
26
, Chap. 6, sec. 3, Thm. 12]
). In addition, the pairing

H^{i}(M,\partial M;F)\times H^{m-i}(M;F)\longrightarrow H^{m}(M,\partial M;F)\longrightarrow H_{0}(M;F)=F,

defined by the assignment

(x,y)\mapsto x\smile y\mapsto\langle x\smile y,O_{M}\rangle=\langle x,y\frown O_{M}\rangle,

where
(x,y)\mapsto x\smile y
denotes the cup product, and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
the Kronecker product, is a dual pairing.

The pair
(M,\partial M)
, with the inclusion

). Therefore, taking into account duality for manifolds with boundary,

Remark 5.9

In both cases, within the interpretation of the cohomology

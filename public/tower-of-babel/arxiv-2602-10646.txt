Abstract




Equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig Polynomials of Thagomizer Matroids

with a Hyperoctahedral Group Action

Abstract.

The thagomizer matroid, realized as the graphic matroid of the complete tripartite graph
K_{1,1,n}
, has full automorphism group isomorphic to the hyperoctahedral group whenever
n\geq 2
. In the equivariant setting for this action, we compute both the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial and the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial in the sense of Proudfoot’s Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley theory, and we show that each coefficient is an honest representation with a multiplicity-free irreducible decomposition.
Our main idea is to exploit the palindromicity of the equivariant
Z
-polynomial, reducing the computation to the already established symmetric-group equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig theory for the graphic matroids of cycle graphs, and then to apply Proudfoot’s equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley inversion identity to obtain the inverse polynomial. Passing to dimensions recovers the previously known nonequivariant thagomizer polynomials, while the coefficient formulas admit a natural expression in terms of the wreath product Frobenius characteristic for the hyperoctahedral group.

Keywords:
thagomizer matroid, equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial, Frobenius characteristic, hyperoctahedral group

Introduction

Elias, Proudfoot and Wakefield
[
4
]
introduced the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of a matroid, in analogy with the polynomials of Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
13
]
for Coxeter groups.
They defined, for each matroid
M
, a polynomial
P_{M}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
characterized by a recursion over the lattice of flats together with a rank-dependent degree bound.
While the coefficients of
P_{M}(t)
are proven to be non-negative
[
2
]
by Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang
[
2
]
, explicit computations remain difficult, and structural properties such as unimodality or log-concavity are hard to establish in general.
Proudfoot, Xu, and Young
[
21
]
introduced the
Z
-polynomial of a matroid
M
, defined as the rank-weighted sum over flats of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of the corresponding contractions.
Braden and Vysogorets
[
3
]
observed that one may alternatively view the palindromicity of
Z
-polynomial as a starting point from which
P_{M}(t)
can be recovered.
Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang
[
2
]
later constructed an intersection cohomology module
\mathrm{IH}(M)
and identified the associated
Z
-polynomial of
M
with its Hilbert series.

This theory can be refined by equipping
M
with an action of a group
W
by matroid automorphisms.
Gedeon, Proudfoot and Young
[
10
]
defined the
equivariant
Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial
P_{M}^{W}(t)
, in which integral coefficients are lifted to representations of
W
. This equivariant perspective often reveals cleaner structural descriptions than the numerical dimensions alone, exhibiting phenomena such as representation stability and multiplicity-free decompositions; see for example
[
10
,
18
,
9
,
16
]
.
Gao and Xie
[
8
]
introduced the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials to compute the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for uniform matroids. Proudfoot
[
19
]
subsequently generalized this notion by developing the Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley theory in an equivariant framework.

In this paper, we investigate the thagomizer matroid
T_{n}
, defined as the graphic matroid of the complete tripartite graph
K_{1,1,n}
. Gedeon
[
11
]
conjectured a formula for
P_{T_{n}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(t)
in the setting of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, which was subsequently proved by Xie and Zhang
[
22
]
. However, for
n\geq 2
, the full automorphism group of

is the larger hyperoctahedral group
\mathcal{B}_{n}\cong C_{2}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We determine both the equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial
P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
and the equivariant inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial
Q_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
under the action of the hyperoctahedral group. We show that each coefficient is an honest
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-representation and give explicit, multiplicity-free decompositions into irreducibles.

Our first main result gives the coefficient representations of
P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
explicitly.
The irreducible representations

of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
are labeled by bipartitions
(\lambda,\mu)
of
n
.

Theorem 1
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have in
\operatorname{grVRep}(\mathcal{B}_{n})

P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)=V_{(n),\varnothing}\;+\;\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\sum_{i=2k}^{n}V_{(n-i),(i-2k+2,2^{k-1})}\right)t^{k},

where
(0)=\varnothing
.

Our second main result concerns the inverse polynomial
Q_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
. It extends the symmetric group decomposition obtained by Gao, Li and Xie
[
7
]
, and it is proved using Proudfoot’s equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley inversion identity.

Theorem 2
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have in
\operatorname{grVRep}(\mathcal{B}_{n})

Q_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\sum_{i=2k}^{n}V_{(1^{n-i}),(2^{k},1^{i-2k})}\right)t^{k}.

Instead of using the defining recursion directly, we determine
P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
via the equivariant
Z
-polynomial. Specifically, we establish the palindromicity of the

-polynomial for

by relating it to the known
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
-equivariant case of the graphic matroids
C_{k}
of the cycle graph of rank
k-1

[

.

In both Theorem

-representations rather than merely virtual ones, and their irreducible decompositions are multiplicity-free. Taking dimensions recovers the non-equivariant polynomials computed by Gedeon

and by Gao, Li and Xie
[

Gao, Li, Xie, Yang and Zhang

introduced the notion of induced log-concavity for sequences of representations, extending the equivariant log-concavity framework of Gedeon–Proudfoot–Young

Motivated by the fact that thagomizers also exhibit multiplicity-free coefficient representations, we expect analogous positivity here. We record specific induced log-concavity conjectures in Section

2.1
Frobenius characteristic and plethystic notation

Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions. We write
\Lambda_{n}
for its homogeneous component of degree
n
.
Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots\}
and
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots\}
be two independent alphabets.
The notation
\Lambda[X,Y]_{n}
is used for the degree-
n
component of
\Lambda[X,Y]
with respect to total degree.
The subscript is often suppressed when the degree is clear from the context.

Following Haglund
[
12
]
, we use plethystic substitution.
Let
E=E(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\ldots)
be a formal series of rational functions in commuting parameters.
For
k\geq 1
, the plethystic substitution of
E
into the power sum
p_{k}
is defined by

p_{k}[E]:=E(t_{1}^{k},t_{2}^{k},\ldots).

For a symmetric function
f=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
expressed in the power-sum basis, define

f[E]:=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}\prod_{i}p_{\lambda_{i}}[E].

In particular, writing
X=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots
, we have
p_{k}[X]=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{k}
and hence
f[X]=f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
for any
f\in\Lambda
.
Moreover
p_{k}[-X]=-p_{k}[X]
.

Lemma 3

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.27]
)
.

Let
E=E(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\ldots)
and
F=F(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},\ldots)
be formal series of rational functions in commuting parameters.
Then for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle h_{n}[E+F]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}h_{j}[E]h_{n-j}[F],

\displaystyle h_{n}[E-F]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}h_{j}[E]e_{n-j}[F].

For an alphabet
A
, write
H_{A}(u):=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}[A]u^{n}
.
Specializing Lemma
3
to
E=X
and
F=Y
gives

H_{X+Y}(u)=H_{X}(u)H_{Y}(u).

After applying the Frobenius characteristic, orbit sums become products, and (
1

Let
W
be a finite group. We write
\operatorname{VRep}(W)
for the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional complex
W
-representations. We will use two related Frobenius characteristics, which provide a bridge between representation theory and symmetric functions: the usual characteristic for
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and a wreath product characteristic for
\mathcal{B}_{n}=C_{2}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}

For each

, let
\operatorname{ch}:\operatorname{VRep}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})\to\Lambda_{n}
be the usual Frobenius characteristic
[
15

, characterized by the property that

\operatorname{ch}(V_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda},

where
V_{\lambda}
denotes the irreducible
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-representation indexed by the partition

n
, and

denotes the corresponding Schur function in
\Lambda_{n}
.
It satisfies
\operatorname{ch}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})=h_{n}
and

\operatorname{ch}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathfrak{S}_{b}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(V\boxtimes W)\right)=\operatorname{ch}(V)\,\operatorname{ch}(W)\qquad(a+b=n).

(2)

Write
\mathcal{B}_{n}=C_{2}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for the hyperoctahedral group.
Irreducible complex representations of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
are indexed by bipartitions
(\lambda,\mu)
of
n
. We write
V_{\lambda,\mu}
for the corresponding irreducible representation.
Following Macdonald
[
15
, Chapter I, Appendix B]
and the subsequent treatments in
[
14

17
]
, we use the wreath product (or
\lambda
-ring) Frobenius characteristic, namely the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear isomorphism

\operatorname{ch}_{B}:\operatorname{VRep}(\mathcal{B}_{n})\longrightarrow\Lambda[X,Y]_{n},

defined on irreducibles by

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V_{\lambda,\mu})=s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y].

(3)

This characteristic map for hyperoctahedral groups also appears in other Schur-positivity contexts; see, for example, Athanasiadis
[
1

in the setting of equivariant
\gamma

In particular,
\operatorname{ch}_{B}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}})=h_{n}[X]
.
We use the standard compatibility of
\operatorname{ch}_{B}
with parabolic induction:

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathcal{B}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(V\boxtimes W)\right)=\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V)\,\operatorname{ch}_{B}(W)

a+b=n

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(embedded in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
as the all-positive signed permutations):

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}V\right)=\operatorname{ch}(V)[X+Y].

Lemma 4
.

Let
a,b\geq 0
with
a+b=n
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}
be the standard Young subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and view
\mathfrak{S}_{a}
as the subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{a}
.
Thus
\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, well-defined up to conjugacy.
Then for
U\in\operatorname{VRep}(\mathfrak{S}_{a})
and
V\in\operatorname{VRep}(\mathcal{B}_{b})

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(U\boxtimes V)\right)=\operatorname{ch}(U)[X+Y]\cdot\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V).

Proof.

We may restrict to the standard inclusions

\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{n},

since any conjugate inclusion gives rise to a canonically isomorphic induced representation. By transitivity of induction, we obtain

\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(U\boxtimes V)\cong\Ind_{\mathcal{B}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}\!\Bigl(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}}^{\mathcal{B}_{a}}U\boxtimes V\Bigr).

Applying

and using (

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathcal{B}_{b}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(U\boxtimes V)\right)=\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}}^{\mathcal{B}_{a}}U\right)\cdot\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V).

Finally, applying (
5

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{a}}^{\mathcal{B}_{a}}U\right)=\operatorname{ch}(U)[X+Y].

Throughout this paper, we work with loopless matroids. Let

We fix a convention for minors that will be used throughout.
For a subset

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}|_{S}
for the restriction of
\mathcal{M}
to
S

For a flat

\mathcal{M}/F

Some papers use other notations for these minors (for example, writing
\mathcal{M}_{F}

We use the following definition of equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig and

]

Definition 5

(
[

)
.

There is a unique way of associating to each loopless matroid

(i)

.

(ii)

If
\rk(\mathcal{M})>0

\deg P_{\mathcal{M}}^{W}(t)<\frac{1}{2}\rk(\mathcal{M})
.

(iii)

For every
\mathcal{M}

Z_{\mathcal{M}}^{W}(t)\;=\;\sum_{[F]\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M})/W}t^{\rk(F)}\Ind_{W_{F}}^{W}P_{\mathcal{M}/F}^{W_{F}}(t)

is palindromic and has degree
\rk(\mathcal{M})
.

Here
\mathbf{1}_{W}
is the trivial representation

3.2
Flats of

and their

The lattice of flats of

has two families, distinguished by whether the spine

Lemma 11
.

Let
F
be a flat of
T_{n}
.

(a)

If
e_{*}\notin F
, then for each spike
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
the intersection

\varnothing,\{a_{i}\},\{b_{i}\}

Writing

\rk(F)=|I|+|J|

(b)

If

, then there exists a subset

such that

F=\{e_{*}\}\cup\{a_{i},b_{i}\colon i\in S\}.

In this case
\rk(F)=|S|+1
.

To study the

Lemma 12

Let

(a)

If

(b)

(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F^{\mathrm{II}}_{k}}\cong\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}.

Proof.

(a) Let
F
be of Type (a), so

Applying the swaps of

This yields a factor
\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
and proves
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
.

(b) Let
F
be Type (b) determined by
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
with
|S|=k
.
Apply a permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{n}
sending

to
\{1,\dots,k\}
to obtain the representative

.
Inside the first
k
spikes, swaps

preserve
F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}
because both edges are present.
Similarly, inside the remaining
n-k
spikes, permutations and swaps preserve
F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}
because no edges from those spikes are present.
This gives a stabilizer isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
.
∎

Remark 13
.

It is instructive to compare Lemma
12
with the case of the symmetric group action
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\curvearrowright T_{n}
studied in
[
22
]
.
Under
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the orbit of a Type (a) flat

depends not just on
k=\rk(F)
but on the specific distribution of edges within spikes.
If
F
contains
i
edges of the form
a_{\ell}
and
j
edges of the form
b_{\ell}
(with

), its

-stabilizer is isomorphic to

Under the larger group

reflects the fact that we can now permute all

or

in the

“empty” spikes.

Proudfoot–Wakefield–Young

Theorem 14

(
[

)

For

Since

(6)

To compare
Z

Proposition 15
.

We have

(7)

Proof.

Fix

and apply Definition

(iii) to

with the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action.
The lattice of flats of

5
Proof of Theorem
1
via a
Z
-polynomial comparison

We prove Theorem
1
in this section.
The argument uses only the orbit-sum definition of
Z
from Definition
5
, its palindromicity, and the explicit
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of the matroids

.
Note that the
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-equivariant characteristic polynomial of

is not used (see Appendix
Appendix: the
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-equivariant characteristic polynomial of
T_{n}
for this computation).

Define the thagomizer Kazhdan–Lusztig and

\displaystyle\Phi_{T}(t,u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)\right)\,u^{n+1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n+1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{T}(t,u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(Z_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)\right)\,u^{n+1}.

We first express each
Z
-series as an explicit combination of the corresponding KL series and homogeneous symmetric-function factors.
The following identity is a direct orbit-sum computation using the two flat orbits from Lemma

.

Proposition 17
.

We have

\mathcal{Z}_{T}(t,u)=H_{X+Y}(tu)\,\Phi_{T}(t,u)+tu\,H_{X}(tu)\,H_{X}(u).

(9)

Proof.

Write
Z_{n}^{B}(t):=\operatorname{ch}_{B}(Z_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t))\in\Lambda[X,Y][t]
and set
\mathcal{Z}_{T}(t,u)=\sum_{n\geq 0}Z_{n}^{B}(t)\,u^{n+1}
.
By Definition
5
(iii),

Z_{n}^{B}(t)=\sum_{[F]\in\mathcal{L}(T_{n})/\mathcal{B}_{n}}t^{\rk(F)}\,\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}P_{T_{n}/F}^{(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F}}(t)\right).

We compute the right side by the two orbit families from Lemma
12
.

Type I orbits (
e_{*}\notin F
).

Fix
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and take the representative
F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
.
Contracting
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
identifies each vertex
i\leq k
with the vertex
A
.
For each
i\leq k
, the remaining edge

becomes an edge between
B
and

, hence an element parallel to
e_{*}
.
The remaining
n-k
spikes keep the same thagomizer shape.
Therefore

is obtained from

by replacing the spine element by a parallel class of size

.
Deleting the
k
additional parallel elements recovers
T_{n-k}
.
Since the induced
\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
-action fixes these extra elements and the lattice of flats is unchanged (only the

of one rank-one flat differs), we have

P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=P_{T_{n-k}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t).

(10)

By Lemma
12
(a), the stabilizer is
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
.
The
\mathfrak{S}_{k}

k
parallel elements and fixes all other elements, hence acts trivially on
\mathcal{L}(T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}})

Therefore the defining properties in Definition

imply

P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\boxtimes P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t).

Combining this with (
10
), we use Lemma
4
to compute the induced Frobenius characteristic:

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\boxtimes P_{T_{n-k}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t))\right)=h_{k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{P}_{n-k}(X,Y;t).

Multiplying by the weight

shows that the Type I contribution to

is

\sum_{k=0}^{n}t^{k}\,h_{k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{P}_{n-k}(X,Y;t).

Passing to generating series, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}t^{k}\,h_{k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{P}_{n-k}(X,Y;t)\Bigr)u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}\,h_{k}[X+Y]\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{m}(X,Y;t)\,u^{m+k+1}\qquad(m:=n-k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{k}[X+Y]\,(tu)^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{m}(X,Y;t)\,u^{m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{X+Y}(tu)\,\Phi_{T}(t,u).

Type II orbits (
e_{*}\in F
).

Fix
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and take the representative

Contracting

identifies

with
B
(because

is contracted) and also contracts all edges in the first
k

For each remaining spike

, the edges
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
become a parallel pair between the identified vertex and
i
.
Hence

is a direct sum of
n-k

n-k
.
By Lemma
6
,

P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}},

where we use Lemma
12
(b) to identify
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}\cong\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
.
Equation (

) leads to

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\Ind_{\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}})\right)=h_{k}[X]\,h_{n-k}[X].

Multiplying by

gives the Type II contribution

.
Passing to generating series, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{n}t^{k+1}\,h_{k}[X]\,h_{n-k}[X]\Bigr)u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tu\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}h_{k}[X]\,u^{k}\sum_{m\geq 0}h_{m}[X]\,u^{m}\qquad(m:=n-k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tu\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}h_{k}[X]\,(tu)^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}h_{m}[X]\,u^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tu\,H_{X}(tu)\,H_{X}(u).

Adding the two orbit families yields (

).
∎

We now define the series

by the right side of (

).
Specifically, define

-series by relating it to the

-series

of the graphic matroids of cycle graphs.

Lemma 18
.

We have

(11)

Proof.

Expanding the right side gives

in the second term is

, from which (

) follows.

We next combine Proposition

and Lemma

, and hence for the thagomizer

-series in terms of

.

.

In

, we have

Combining Lemma

with (

.

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}(t,u)=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}(t^{-1},tu).

Proof.

By Proposition

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We prove
\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)=R_{n}(X,Y;t)
by induction on
n
.
For
n=0
, the matroid

has Boolean lattice of flats, so
\mathsf{P}_{0}=1=h_{0}[X]=R_{0}
by Lemma
6
.
Fix
n\geq 1
and assume
\mathsf{P}_{m}=R_{m}
for all

.
Set

By Proposition

,

\mathcal{Z}_{T}(t,u)-\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}(t,u)=H_{X+Y}(tu)\,\Delta(t,u).

By the induction hypothesis,

has no terms in degrees

.
Since

has constant term

, it follows that the coefficient of
u^{n+1}
in

\mathcal{Z}_{T}-\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}
equals the coefficient of
u^{n+1}
in
\Delta
, namely

f(t):=\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)-R_{n}(X,Y;t).

Corollary
20
and Definition

(iii) imply that

\mathcal{Z}_{T}-\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}
is invariant under
(t,u)\mapsto(t^{-1},tu)

Taking coefficients of

Equivalently, the set of

\frac{n+1}{2}
: any nonzero term of degree

is paired with a nonzero term of degree

.
Let

If

, then

is paired with

, so

and hence

.
On the other hand, the Kazhdan–Lusztig degree bound for

and

, i.e.
\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)=R_{n}(X,Y;t)
.
This completes the induction.
∎

\mathfrak{S}_{k}
factor acts trivially on
\mathcal{L}(T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}})
, the defining properties in Definition
5
imply

P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\boxtimes P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t),

and combining with (
10
) gives
P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{I}}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\boxtimes P_{T_{n-k}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)
.
Therefore the Type I contribution to (
13
), after applying
\operatorname{ch}_{B}
, is

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,e_{k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{P}_{n-k}(X,Y;t),

(15)

using Lemma
4
with
U=\mathrm{sgn}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}
and
\operatorname{ch}(\mathrm{sgn}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k}})=e_{k}
.

Type II flats (
e_{*}\in F
).

Fix
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and choose the representative

F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}=\{e_{*}\}\cup\{a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k}\}.

Then
T_{n}|_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}
is canonically isomorphic to
T_{k}
with its
\mathcal{B}_{k}
-action, and
(\mathcal{B}_{n})_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}\cong\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
by Lemma
12
(b).
The factor
\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
fixes every element of this restriction and hence acts trivially on
Q_{T_{n}|_{F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)
.
The contraction
T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}
has a Boolean lattice of flats of rank
n-k
, as in the proof of Proposition
17
, so Lemma
6
gives

P_{T_{n}/F_{k}^{\mathrm{II}}}^{\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}}.

Induction and Frobenius therefore give the Type II contribution

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\,\mathsf{Q}_{k}(X,Y;t)\,h_{n-k}[X],

(16)

where we use
\operatorname{ch}_{B}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}_{n-k}})=h_{n-k}[X]
and the induction product rule (
4
).

Adding (
15
) and (
16
) and using (
13
) yields, for all
n\geq 0
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,\mathsf{Q}_{k}(X,Y;t)\,h_{n-k}[X]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,e_{k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{P}_{n-k}(X,Y;t).

(17)

Now multiply (
17
) by
u^{n}
and sum over
n\geq 0
.
Write

H_{X}(u):=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}[X]\,u^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{A}(u):=\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{n}[A]\,u^{n}.

Then (
17
) becomes

\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}\right)H_{X}(u)=E_{X+Y}(-u)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}\right).

(18)

Since
H_{X}(u)\,E_{X}(-u)=1
, we have
H_{X}(u)^{-1}=E_{X}(-u)
and hence

\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}=E_{X+Y}(-u)\,E_{X}(-u)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}\right).

Using
E_{X+Y}(-u)\,E_{X}(-u)=E_{2X+Y}(-u)
, we obtain

\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}e_{n}[2X+Y]\,u^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)\,u^{n}\right).

Comparing coefficients of
u^{n}
gives (
14
).
∎

Proposition 23
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)=e_{n}[X+Y]+t\sum_{k=2}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,e_{n-k}[X+Y]\,\mathsf{C}_{k}(Y;t).

(19)

Proof.

Substituting (
6
) into (
14
), we get that

\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}e_{n-i}[2X+Y]\,h_{i}[X]\;+\;t\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{i}e_{n-i}[2X+Y]\sum_{k=2}^{i}h_{i-k}[X]\,\mathsf{C}_{k}(Y;t).

For the first sum, multiply by
u^{n}
and sum over
n\geq 0
to obtain
E_{2X+Y}(u)\,H_{X}(-u)
.
Since
H_{X}(-u)\,E_{X}(u)=1
and
E_{2X+Y}(u)=E_{X}(u)^{2}E_{Y}(u)
, we have
E_{2X+Y}(u)\,H_{X}(-u)=E_{X}(u)E_{Y}(u)=E_{X+Y}(u)
.
Thus

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}e_{n-i}[2X+Y]\,h_{i}[X]=e_{n}[X+Y].

For the second sum, we interchange the order of summation by writing
i=k+m
:

\displaystyle t\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{i}e_{n-i}[2X+Y]\sum_{k=2}^{i}h_{i-k}[X]\,\mathsf{C}_{k}(Y;t)

\displaystyle=t\sum_{k=2}^{n}\mathsf{C}_{k}(Y;t)\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{k+m}e_{n-k-m}[2X+Y]\,h_{m}[X]

\displaystyle=t\sum_{k=2}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,e_{n-k}[X+Y]\;\mathsf{C}_{k}(Y;t),

where in the last step we used the previous identity with
n
replaced by
n-k
.
Combining the two parts yields (
19
).
∎

To convert the signed formula (
19
) into the positive induced decomposition in Theorem
2
, we use the following alternating Pieri identity.

Lemma 24

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

For
m\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\,e_{m-j}[Y]\,s_{(j+2,2^{k-1})}[Y]=s_{(2^{k},1^{m})}[Y].

Applying
\operatorname{ch}_{B}
to Theorem
2
, it suffices to prove the symmetric function identity

\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\sum_{i=2k}^{n}e_{n-i}[X]\,s_{(2^{k},1^{i-2k})}[Y]\right)t^{k}.

(20)

We prove (
20
) by rewriting the signed formula (
19
) as a positive induced decomposition, parallel to the case of symmetric groups
[
7
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We compute coefficients of
t^{k}
using (
19
) and Theorem
14
.
The case
k=0
is the Cauchy identity for elementary symmetric functions:

[t^{0}]\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)=e_{n}[X+Y]=\sum_{i=0}^{n}e_{n-i}[X]\,e_{i}[Y],

which agrees with (
20
) when
k=0
.

Fix
k\geq 1
.
Expand
\mathsf{C}_{m}(Y;t)
using Theorem
14
and extract the coefficient of
t^{k}
in (
19
):

\displaystyle[t^{k}]\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=2k}^{n}(-1)^{m}\,e_{n-m}[X+Y]\cdot s_{(m-2k+2,2^{k-1})}[Y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k}(-1)^{j}\,e_{n-2k-j}[X+Y]\cdot s_{(j+2,2^{k-1})}[Y]\qquad(j:=m-2k).

Write
e_{n-2k-j}[X+Y]=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2k-j}e_{i}[X]\,e_{n-2k-j-i}[Y]
and interchange the order of summation:

\displaystyle[t^{k}]\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2k}e_{i}[X]\sum_{j=0}^{n-2k-i}(-1)^{j}\,e_{n-2k-i-j}[Y]\cdot s_{(j+2,2^{k-1})}[Y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2k}e_{i}[X]\cdot s_{(2^{k},1^{n-2k-i})}[Y],

by Lemma
24
(with
m:=n-2k-i
).
Reindex the final sum by setting
r:=n-2k-i
, and then put
i:=r+2k
to obtain

[t^{k}]\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)=\sum_{r=0}^{n-2k}e_{n-2k-r}[X]\cdot s_{(2^{k},1^{r})}[Y]=\sum_{i=2k}^{n}e_{n-i}[X]\cdot s_{(2^{k},1^{i-2k})}[Y].

This is the coefficient of
t^{k}
in (
20
), so (
20
) holds.
The representation-theoretic statement follows by applying
\operatorname{ch}_{B}^{-1}
and the defining property
\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V_{\lambda,\mu})=s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y]
.
∎

7
Induced log-concavity

Gao, Li, Xie, Yang and Zhang
[
6
]
introduced the notion of
induced log-concavity
for sequences of representations, extending the equivariant log-concavity framework of Gedeon, Proudfoot, and Young
[
10
]
.
We briefly record how their definition specializes to
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and formulate conjectures for the thagomizer family.

Definition 25

(
[
6
, §1]
)
.

Fix a finite group
W
and a sequence
(V_{k})_{k\geq 0}
in
\operatorname{VRep}(W)
.
Given a finite group
G
with a subgroup
H\leq G
, consider a group homomorphism
\phi\colon H\to W\times W
.
For
i,j\geq 0
, write
V_{i}\boxtimes V_{j}
for the external tensor product, viewed as a virtual representation of
H
via pullback along
\phi
.

•

We say that
(V_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is
induced log-concave
with respect to
(G,H,\phi)
if for every
i\geq 1
the virtual
G
-representation

\Ind_{H}^{G}\!\left(V_{i}\boxtimes V_{i}\right)\;-\;\Ind_{H}^{G}\!\left(V_{i-1}\boxtimes V_{i+1}\right)

is honest.

•

We say that
(V_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is
strongly induced log-concave
with respect to
(G,H,\phi)
if for every integers
1\leq i\leq j
the virtual
G
-representation

\Ind_{H}^{G}\!\left(V_{i}\boxtimes V_{j}\right)\;-\;\Ind_{H}^{G}\!\left(V_{i-1}\boxtimes V_{j+1}\right)

is honest.

In this paper we only use the following specialization.
Fix
n\geq 0
and take
W=\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
G=\mathcal{B}_{2n}
, and
H=\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\leq\mathcal{B}_{2n}
the standard Young subgroup.
Let
\phi=\mathrm{id}
, identifying
H
with
W\times W=\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Since
\operatorname{ch}_{B}(V_{\lambda,\mu})=s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y]
and
\operatorname{ch}_{B}
is an isomorphism, a virtual
\mathcal{B}_{2n}
-representation is honest if and only if its Frobenius characteristic is Schur positive in the basis
\{s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y]\}
.
Thus, as in
[
6
, §3]
, Definition
25
is equivalent to Schur positivity in
\Lambda[X,Y]
of the differences

\mathsf{P}_{n,i}(X,Y)\,\mathsf{P}_{n,j}(X,Y)-\mathsf{P}_{n,i-1}(X,Y)\,\mathsf{P}_{n,j+1}(X,Y),

(21)

where
\mathsf{P}_{n,k}(X,Y):=[t^{k}]\mathsf{P}_{n}(X,Y;t)
(and similarly
\mathsf{Q}_{n,k}(X,Y):=[t^{k}]\mathsf{Q}_{n}(X,Y;t)
), with the convention that
\mathsf{P}_{n,k}=\mathsf{Q}_{n,k}=0
for
k>\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.
The induced log-concavity condition is the special case
i=j
of (
21
), while the strong condition requires Schur positivity for all
1\leq i\leq j
.
This is not automatic, even when each coefficient is an honest representation, so we record it as a separate conjectural positivity property for thagomizers.

There are two complementary ways to analyze Schur positivity of (
21
).
In the
Y
-first
view, one expands

F_{n,i,j}^{\mathsf{P}}(X,Y):=\mathsf{P}_{n,i}(X,Y)\,\mathsf{P}_{n,j}(X,Y)-\mathsf{P}_{n,i-1}(X,Y)\,\mathsf{P}_{n,j+1}(X,Y)=\sum_{\mu}g_{n,i,j,\mu}^{\mathsf{P}}(X)\,s_{\mu}[Y],

and since
\{s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y]\}
is a basis, Schur positivity of
F_{n,i,j}^{\mathsf{P}}(X,Y)
is equivalent to Schur positivity of each coefficient
g_{n,i,j,\mu}^{\mathsf{P}}(X)\in\Lambda[X]
(and similarly after replacing
\mathsf{P}
by
\mathsf{Q}
).
In the
X
-first
view, Theorems
1
and
2
express each coefficient
\mathsf{P}_{n,k}(X,Y)
(respectively
\mathsf{Q}_{n,k}(X,Y)
) as a sum of one-row (respectively one-column) Schur functions in
X
with coefficients in
\Lambda[Y]
, so extracting coefficients in the bases
\{h_{a}[X]\,h_{b}[X]\}
or
\{e_{a}[X]\,e_{b}[X]\}
leads to finer Schur-positivity questions in
\Lambda[Y]
.

Conjecture 26
.

For every
n\geq 0
, the coefficient sequences of
P_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
and
Q_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
are strongly induced log-concave in the sense of Definition
25
, equivalently the differences (
21
) are Schur positive in
\Lambda[X,Y]
, and the same holds after replacing
\mathsf{P}
by
\mathsf{Q}
.

In particular, this conjecture implies that both sequences are induced log-concave (take
i=j
).

At present we do not know a conceptual proof of Conjecture
26
.
We verified it for
n\leq 10
by direct computation.
We hope that the multiplicity-free formulas in Theorems
1
and
2
will make this conjecture accessible via explicit symmetric-function inequalities.

Appendix: the
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-equivariant characteristic polynomial of
T_{n}

This appendix records a closed formula for the
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-equivariant characteristic polynomial of
T_{n}
in wreath product Frobenius characteristic.
It is logically independent of the Kazhdan–Lusztig argument in Section
5
; in particular, it is not used in the proof of Theorem
1
.
However, the characteristic polynomial can also be used as input for alternative approaches to equivariant Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.
For example, one could attempt to adapt the method of Xie–Zhang
[
22
]
to the
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-equivariant setting; we leave such an adaptation to interested readers.

We begin by recalling the definition of equivariant characteristic polynomials and fixing notation.
Let
M
be a rank-
r
matroid with a finite group action
W\curvearrowright M
.
Write
\mathrm{OS}_{M}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\mathrm{OS}_{M,i}
for its Orlik–Solomon algebra, viewed as a graded
W
-representation.
As in
[
10
]
, define the
W
-equivariant characteristic polynomial
by

\chi_{M}^{W}(t):=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\,t^{\,r-i}\,[\mathrm{OS}_{M,i}]\in\operatorname{VRep}(W)[t].

Proposition 27
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\chi_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)\right)=:\mathsf{\chi}_{n}(X,Y;t)=(t-1)\,h_{n}\!\big[(t-1)X-Y\big]\in\Lambda[X,Y][t].

Proof.

In the equivariant KLS framework of Proudfoot
[
19
]
, there are class functions
\zeta,\chi,\bar{\zeta}
on the lattice of flats satisfying a convolution identity
\bar{\zeta}=\zeta*\chi
.
For matroids, the function
\chi
specializes to the equivariant characteristic polynomial, and evaluating
\bar{\zeta}
at the minimal element gives a distinguished element
\bar{\zeta}_{M}^{W}(t)\in\operatorname{VRep}(W)[t]
.

For the thagomizer family, we will use the following evaluation:

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\bar{\zeta}_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)\right)=t\,h_{n}[tX].

(22)

We will verify that the right-hand side of the flat-orbit sum for
\bar{\zeta}_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
agrees with (
22
) when
\mathsf{\chi}_{n}(X,Y;t)
is given by the closed formula in the proposition.
Since the recursion is triangular (the term
k=0
contains
\mathsf{\chi}_{n}
), this determines
\mathsf{\chi}_{n}
uniquely.

For the Boolean matroid
B_{m}
of rank
m
with its
\mathcal{B}_{m}
-action, we use the formula

\operatorname{ch}_{B}\!\left(\chi_{B_{m}}^{\mathcal{B}_{m}}(t)\right)=h_{m}[(t-1)X].

(23)

Now fix
n
.
We evaluate the KLS flat-orbit sum for
\bar{\zeta}_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
by splitting flats into the two
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-orbit families (containing
e_{*}
vs. not containing
e_{*}
).

Flats containing
e_{*}
.

Such a flat is determined by a subset
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
of size
k
, with stabilizer
\mathcal{B}_{k}\times\mathcal{B}_{n-k}
(Lemma
12
).
The contraction
T_{n}/F
is Boolean of rank
n-k
(as in the proof of Proposition
17
), hence contributes
h_{n-k}[(t-1)X]
by (
23
).
The orbit factor records the choice of
k
spikes and involves only the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-part, hence contributes
h_{k}[X]
.
Summing over
k
gives

\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}[X]\cdot h_{n-k}[(t-1)X]=h_{n}[tX]

by the Cauchy identity.

Flats not containing
e_{*}
.

Such a flat is determined by: a subset of
k
spikes together with a choice of one edge in each selected spike.
The orbit factor is the signed choice module, giving
h_{k}[X+Y]
.
The contraction is
T_{n-k}
, contributing
\mathsf{\chi}_{n-k}(X,Y;t)
.
Therefore, the total contribution from this orbit family is

\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}[X+Y]\cdot\mathsf{\chi}_{n-k}(X,Y;t).

Combining the two orbit families and comparing with (
22
) yields the recursion

t\,h_{n}[tX]=h_{n}[tX]+\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}[X+Y]\cdot\mathsf{\chi}_{n-k}(X,Y;t).

Rearranging and solving for
\mathsf{\chi}_{n}
gives

\mathsf{\chi}_{n}(X,Y;t)=(t-1)\,h_{n}[tX-(X+Y)]=(t-1)\,h_{n}[(t-1)X-Y],

since
tX-(X+Y)=(t-1)X-Y
.
∎

\chi_{T_{n}}^{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(t)
(in the sense of Section
7

B

\{s_{\lambda}[X]\,s_{\mu}[Y]\}

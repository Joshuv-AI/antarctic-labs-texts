In spaces with a slow diffusion, the Riesz transform is unbounded on L p L^{p} , p ∈ ( 2 , ∞ ) p\in(2,\infty) .
Joseph Feneuil

Abstract
. In graphs and Riemannian manifolds where the kernel of the diffusion semigroup satisfies pointwise sub-Gaussian estimates, we study the range of parameters p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) and γ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \gamma\in[0,1] for which the quantities ‖ Δ γ ​ f ‖ p \|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{p} and ‖ ∇ f ‖ p \|\nabla f\|_{p} can be compared. In particular, we prove that in such metric spaces, the Riesz transform ∇ Δ − 1 / 2 \nabla\Delta^{-1/2} is unbounded on L p L^{p} for all p ∈ ( 2 , ∞ ) p\in(2,\infty) , thereby demonstrating a clear departure from the behavior observed in the Euclidean setting.



Keywords:
Riesz transform, reverse Riesz transform, quasi-Riesz transform, sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates, graphs, Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction

In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is used to define the Riesz transform as

\mathcal{R}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|}\mathcal{F}f\right].

Parseval’s identity immediately implies that the Riesz transform
\mathcal{R}
is an isometry on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For smooth functions,
\mathcal{R}
can also be expressed as the singular integral

\mathcal{R}f(x):=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}f(y)\,dy.

Using Calderón–Zygmund theory, combined with duality arguments, one obtains (see
[
Ste70a
, Chapter 2, Theorem 1]
) that

(E
p
)

C_{p}\|f\|_{p}\leq\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p}\qquad\text{for }p\in(1,\infty),

and this method can be easily adapted to establish the same inequality on Lie groups.

If we define
(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
on a dense subdomain of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, such as
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, via spectral theory, we observe from the Fourier transform definition that

\mathcal{R}f=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1/2}f\qquad\text{for }f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This identity is particularly useful, as it allows us to define the Riesz transform in a more general setting. Indeed, suppose we are given a metric measure space
(\Gamma,d,m)
, equipped with a non-negative self-adjoint operator
\Delta
on
L^{2}(\Gamma,m)
with dense domain. We can define a Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}(f,g):=-\int_{\Gamma}f\Delta g\,dm,

and a “gradient length”

\nabla f:=\mathcal{E}(f,f).

Two primary examples arise naturally:

•

If
\Gamma
is a Riemannian manifold, then
\Delta
is the (positive) Laplace–Beltrami operator and
\nabla f
corresponds to the Riemannian gradient.

•

If
\Gamma
is a graph equipped with a Markov operator
P
describing a random walk, then
\Delta:=I-P
, and
\nabla f(x)
measures the norm of the difference between
f(x)
and the values of
f
at its neighboring points.

The spaces we consider in this work - namely graphs and Riemannian manifolds, representing discrete and continuous settings respectively - are formally introduced in Section
2

Vicsek graphs are in a sense extreme: they exhibit the slowest possible diffusion, or equivalently, the minimal number of paths for a given volume growth rate. This raises a natural question: is the mutual exclusivity of the Riesz and reverse Riesz transform estimates - i.e., the fact that one inequality fails when the other holds, and vice versa, except at
p=2
- a phenomenon specific to this extreme case, or does it reflect a more general property of graphs lacking pointwise Gaussian upper bounds (also referred to as ”fractal-type” graphs)?

The purpose of this article is to establish the latter. We show that if the Markov or heat kernel satisfies a pointwise sub-Gaussian estimate, then the Riesz transform is unbounded for all
p\in(2,\infty)
. More generally, we argue that the Riesz transform is not the appropriate tool to study in the sub-Gaussian setting. Instead of comparing
\nabla f
with
\Delta^{1/2}f
in
L^{p}
, it is more meaningful to compare
\nabla f
with
\Delta^{\gamma}f
in
L^{p}
for an appropriate choice of
\gamma
that depends on
p
.

When the ambient space
\Gamma
is a graph, we consider, for
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
and
C>0
independent of

(R
p,γ
)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}}

and

(RR

\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

When the ambient space is a Riemannian manifold, we instead consider versions with an extra smoothing, we want the existence of
C>0
such that for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Gamma)
, we have either

(R
p,γ
)

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}}

or

(RR

\|\Delta^{\gamma}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

Why this difference? In the discrete setting, both
\Delta

\nabla
are bounded operators that satisfy

\|\Delta f\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{p}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{p}\qquad\text{for }f\in L^{p}(\Gamma),

(see Lemma

. In contrast, smooth Riemannian manifolds are locally flat (i.e., locally Euclidean), so

is naturally compared only to
\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}

2.
Definitions in graphs and Riemannian manifolds

2.1.
Definitions of graphs

A (weighted, unoriented) graph
(\Gamma,\mu)
is the pairing of a countable (infinite) set
\Gamma
with a symmetric, nonnegative weight function
\mu
on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
. The set of edges is defined by

E:=\{(x,y)\in\Gamma^{2}\,:\,\mu_{xy}>0\},

and we write
x\sim y
when
(x,y)\in E
(that is,
x
and
y
are neighbors). The graph is naturally equipped with a measure, a distance, and a random walk. The measure
m
on
\Gamma
is defined by

m(x)=\sum_{x\sim y}\mu_{xy},

and more generally, for a subset
E\subset\Gamma
, we define

m(E):=\sum_{x\in E}m(x).

The distance
d(x,y)
between two vertices
x,y\in\Gamma
is the length of the shortest path connecting them, i.e., the smallest integer
N
such that there exists a sequence
x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N}=y
with
x_{i-1}\sim x_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,N
. We denote by
B(x,r)
the (discrete) ball

B(x,r):=\{y\in\Gamma\,:\,d(x,y)\leq r\},

and define
V(x,r):=m(B(x,r))
. The weights
\mu_{xy}
define a discrete-time, reversible Markov kernel
p
via

p(x,y):=\frac{\mu_{xy}}{m(x)m(y)}.

The associated random walk is given by the discrete kernels
p_{k}(x,y)
, defined recursively for all
k\geq 0
by

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{0}(x,y)=\frac{\delta(x,y)}{m(y)},\\
p_{k+1}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in\Gamma}p(x,z)\,p_{k}(z,y)\,m(z).\end{array}\right.

For all
k\geq 1
, the kernel
p_{k}
is symmetric, i.e.,
p_{k}(x,y)=p_{k}(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
. Moreover,
p_{k}(x,y)m(y)
represents the probability of moving from
x
to
y
in
k
steps. The Markov operator
P
is defined via the kernel
p
with respect to the measure
m
: for any function
f
and any
x\in\Gamma
,

Pf(x)=\sum_{y\in\Gamma}p(x,y)f(y)m(y).

It is straightforward to check that
p_{k}
is the kernel of
P^{k}
, and that
P^{k}
is a contraction on
L^{p}(\Gamma)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

The following assumption on the graph will always be made:

•

Local uniform finiteness:
There exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
x\in\Gamma
,

\#B(x,1)=\#\left(\{y\in\Gamma\,:\,y\sim x\}\cup\{x\}\right)\leq M.

Moreover, the following additional conditions will be assumed when required:

•

Non-degenerate probability transitions:
The Markov process satisfies

(2.2)

p(x,y)m(y)\geq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\Gamma\text{ with }x\sim y,\,x\neq y.

•

Analyticity of the semigroup:
The Markov semigroup
(P^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
analytic
if there exists
C>0
such that

\|(I-P)P^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{C}{k+1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\qquad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N},\,f\in L^{2}(\Gamma).

•

Doubling condition:
The graph
(\Gamma,\mu)
is said to be
doubling
- or to satisfy (
D
) - if there exists
C>0
such that

(D)

V(x,2r)\leq CV(x,r)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Gamma,\ r\in\mathbb{N}.

•

Sub-Gaussian estimates:
The Markov kernel satisfies pointwise sub-Gaussian upper bounds with escape rate
r^{\beta}
, i.e., there exist constants
C,c>0
such that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and all
x,y\in\Gamma
,

(UE
β
)

p_{k}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c\left[\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\right).

2.2.
Definitions in Riemannian manifolds

When referring to a Riemannian manifold

, we mean a smooth, complete, connected, and non-compact Riemannian manifold equipped with the geodesic distance

and the Riemannian measure
m
. We denote by

the open ball centered at

with geodesic radius

B(x,r):=\{y\in\Gamma:d(x,y)<r\}.

As in the graph setting, we write

and say that the Riemannian manifold is

if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(D)

V(x,2r)\leq CV(x,r)\qquad\text{ for all }x\in\Gamma,\;r>0.

The doubling property implies that the volume of balls grows at most and at least polynomially. That is, there exist constants
C>0
,

, and

such that

(2.6)

V(x,\Lambda r)\leq C\Lambda^{D}V(x,r)\qquad\text{ for all }x\in\Gamma,\;r\in\mathbb{N},\;\Lambda\geq 1,

and, since
\Gamma
is connected and unbounded,

(2.7)

V(x,\Lambda r)\geq C^{-1}\Lambda^{\delta}V(x,r)\qquad\text{ for all }x\in\Gamma,\;r\in\mathbb{N},\;\Lambda\geq 1.

We denote by
\nabla
the Riemannian gradient and by

the (non-negative) Laplace–Beltrami operator on

Moreover, the heat semigroup is analytic on

for all

For

Basic tools

In this section, either
\Gamma=(\Gamma,\mu)
is a (weighted, unoriented) graph that is locally uniformly finite and whose Markov semigroup is analytic, or
\Gamma=(\Gamma,m)
is a Riemannian manifold equipped with the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator

Theorem 3.1

(Interpolation)
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
and
\gamma,\delta,\epsilon\in[0,1]
be such that there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
satisfying

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon=\theta\gamma+(1-\theta)\delta.

Then

\text{(R}_{p,\gamma}\text{)}+\text{(R}_{q,\delta}\text{)}\implies\text{(R}_{r,\epsilon}\text{)},

\text{(RR}_{p,\gamma}\text{)}+\text{(RR}_{q,\delta}\text{)}\implies\text{(RR}_{r,\epsilon}\text{)},

and if
\beta\geq 2
,

\text{(P}_{p,\beta\gamma}\text{)}+\text{(P}_{q,\beta\delta}\text{)}\implies\text{(P}_{r,\beta\epsilon}\text{)}.

Proof.

The theorem is a straightforward consequence of complex interpolation of
L^{p}
spaces. Let us add a few remarks to clarify this:

•

When
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, the norm
\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{p}
is equivalent to the
L^{p}
-norm of a Littlewood-Paley-Stein functional. Specifically: if
\Gamma
is a Riemannian manifold,

\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{p}\approx\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{1-2\gamma}|\Delta e^{-t\Delta}f|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p},

and if
\Gamma
is a graph,

\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{p}\approx\left\|\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{1-2\gamma}|\Delta P^{k}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}.

See for instance
[
Ste70b
]
for the equivalence between the
L^{p}
norm and the
L^{p}
norm of continuous Littlewood-Paley-Stein functionals, and
[
LM14
,
Fen15
]
for the analogue with discrete time Littlewood-Paley-Stein functionals.

•

When
\Gamma
is a graph, the quantity
\nabla f(x)
is itself an
L^{2}
-norm over the edges adjacent to
x
, so
\|\nabla f\|_{p}
can be interpreted as an
L^{p}L^{2}
-norm on the edges of the graph.

These observations indicate that the interpolation results in Theorem
3.1
are indeed consequences of complex interpolation of sublinear operators in
L^{p}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Duality)
.

Let

\text{(R}_{p,\gamma}\text{)}\implies\text{(RR}_{p^{\prime},1-\gamma}\text{)},

where

Proof.

This result is also classical. In the case of Riemannian manifolds, the proof follows the same reasoning as Lemma 1.4 in
[

]
. The case of graphs is an even simpler variant.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mu)
be a graph with the local doubling property, i.e., there exists a constant
C>0
such that
V(x,1)\leq Cm(x)
for all

. Then the inequalities (R

) and (RR

(4.1)

|\nabla f(x)|\leq\frac{C}{V(x,1)}\sum_{y\in B(x,1)}|f(y)|\,m(y),

where
C>0
depends only on the local doubling constant, and

(4.2)

|\Delta f(x)|\leq\sqrt{2}\nabla f(x).

Proof.

For any
x\in\Gamma
and any function
f
on
\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}p(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\,m(y)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|f(x)|+\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}|f(y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{y\in B(x,1)}|f(y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\sum_{y\in B(x,1)}|f(y)|\,m(y),

where the last inequality follows from the local doubling property. We then estimate the
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\sum_{y\in B(x,1)}|f(y)|\,m(y)\right)^{p}m(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\sum_{y\in B(x,1)}|f(y)|^{p}\,m(y)\,m(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\Gamma}|f(y)|^{p}\,m(y)\sum_{x\in B(y,1)}\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}^{p},

where we again used the local doubling property:

\sum_{x\in B(y,1)}\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\leq\sum_{x\in B(y,1)}\frac{1}{m(y)}\lesssim\sum_{x\in B(y,1)}\frac{1}{V(y,1)}=1.

To prove (
4.2
), observe:

\displaystyle|\Delta f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{y\sim x}p(x,y)[f(x)-f(y)]\,m(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\sim x}p(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|\,m(y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{y\sim x}p(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\,m(y)\right)^{1/2}=\sqrt{2}\nabla f(x).

This concludes the proof.

supported in

, we have:

(4.9)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq Cr^{\beta\gamma}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
, and for
r\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, define the function

f_{r}(x):=\max\{0,r-\dist(x_{0},x)\}.

Observe the following:

•

f_{r}
is supported in
B(x_{0},r)
;

•

|f_{r}(x)-f_{r}(y)|\leq 1
whenever
x\sim y
, which implies
\nabla f_{r}\leq 1
. Since
\nabla f_{r}
is supported in
B(x_{0},r)
, we have

\|\nabla f_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\leq V(x_{0},r)^{1/p};

•

f_{r}\geq r/2
on
B(x_{0},r/2)
, so

\|f_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\geq\frac{r}{2}V(x_{0},r/2)^{1/p}.

Inserting these bounds into (
4.9
) yields:

\frac{r}{2}V(x_{0},r/2)^{1/p}\leq Cr^{\beta\gamma}V(x_{0},r)^{1/p},

which simplifies to:

r^{1-\beta\gamma}\leq 2C\left(\frac{V(x_{0},r)}{V(x_{0},r/2)}\right)^{1/p}\lesssim 1

by the doubling property (
D
). Since this inequality must hold for all integers
r\geq 1
, it forces the exponent
1-\beta\gamma\leq 0
, i.e.,
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{\beta}
. The corollary follows.

\square

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mu)
be an unbounded doubling graph such that the Markov semigroup is analytic and satisfies (
UEβ
). Then the inequality (P
2,γβ
) fails for any
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof:
Step 1:
We claim there exists
\Lambda\geq 1
(independent of
y\in\Gamma
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) such that

\sum_{x\in\Gamma\setminus B(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}p_{k}(x,y)m(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Indeed, define
C_{j}(y,r):=B(y,2^{j}r)\setminus B(y,2^{j-1}r)
. Then:

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Gamma\setminus B(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}p_{k}(x,y)m(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\sum_{x\in C_{j}(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}p_{k}(x,y)m(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{V(y,2^{j}\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c(2^{j-1}\Lambda)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{c\Lambda^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}}{2}\right)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}(2^{j}\Lambda)^{D}\exp\left(-\frac{c(2^{j-1}\Lambda)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{c\Lambda^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}}{2}\right),

where we used (
UEβ
) and the doubling property (
2.3
). The claim follows by choosing
\Lambda
large enough.

Step 2:
There exists
\eta>0
such that the set

S:=\left\{x\in B(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})\,,\,p_{k}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4V(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}\right\}

satisfies
m(S)\geq\eta V(y,k^{1/\beta})
. Indeed, for
x\in S
, by (
UEβ
),
p_{k}(x,y)\leq C/V(y,k^{1/\beta})
. Combining with Step 1, we get:

\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{x\in B(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})}p_{k}(x,y)m(x)\leq\frac{1}{4V(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta}}V(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta}+C\frac{m(S)}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})},

which gives
m(S)\geq\frac{1}{4C}V(y,k^{1/\beta})
.

Step 3:
Fix
y\in\Gamma
and define:

f_{k}(x):=\max\left\{0,p_{k}(x,y)-\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}\right\}.

By doubling, the ratio
V(y,\Lambda k^{1/\beta})/V(y,k^{1/\beta})
is uniformly bounded by some constant
k_{0}
, and for
k\geq k_{0}
, Step 2 implies:

(4.10)

\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}.

Also,

(4.11)

0\leq f_{k}(x)-p_{k}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}.

Thus, for
x\in\Gamma
:

|\Delta f_{k}(x)|\leq|p_{k}(x,y)-p_{k+1}(x,y)|+|\Delta(f_{k}-p_{k}(\cdot,y))(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})},

where we use (
2.5
) and the
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of
\Delta
. Therefore:

(4.12)

\|\nabla f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}[\Delta f_{k}](x)f_{k}(x)m(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}\underbrace{\sum_{x\in\Gamma}p_{k}(x,y)m(x)}_{=1}\lesssim\frac{1}{k}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

Now observe that
f_{k}(x)=0
whenever

p_{k}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}\leq C\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c\left(\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\right),

so

d(x,y)\geq r_{k}:=k^{1/\beta}\left(\frac{\log(Ck)}{c}\right)^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}.

Thus,
f_{k}
is supported in
B(y,r_{k})
with
r_{k}\to\infty
, and

\|\nabla f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{k}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\log(r_{k})^{\beta-1}}{r_{k}^{\beta}}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2\gamma\beta+\epsilon}}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2},

whenever
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\epsilon<(1-2\gamma)\beta
. Hence, the Poincaré inequality (P
2,γβ
) cannot hold if
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, completing the proof.

\square

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
(\Gamma,\mu)
be an unbounded doubling graph with non-degenerate probability transitions, such that the Markov semigroup is analytic and satisfies (
UEβ
). Then (
RRp,γ
) fails whenever
p\in(1,2)
and

\gamma<\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{\beta}\right).

Proof:
The key observation is that (P
∞,1
) holds. Indeed, since
\Gamma
is connected, for any
x\in B(x_{0},r)
, there exists a path of length at most
2r
connecting
x
to
\Gamma\setminus B(x_{0},r)
. Therefore, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
supported in
B(x_{0},r)
and
x\in B(x_{0},r)
:

|f(x)|\leq 2r\sup_{y\sim z}|f(y)-f(z)|\leq 2r\sqrt{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}\sup_{y\in\Gamma}|\nabla f(y)|,

where
\epsilon
is from (
2.2
). Hence,
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq Cr\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}
.

Now, suppose (
RRp,γ
) holds for some
p\in(1,2)
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{\beta}\right)
. Then, by Remark
4.7
, (P
p,γβ
) holds. Interpolating this and (P
∞,1
), using Theorem
3.1
, we obtain (P
2,δ
) for

\delta:=\frac{p\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{p}{2\beta}<\frac{1}{2},

which contradicts Lemma
4.9
. The result follows.

\square

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we revisit the results established in Section
4
and explain how they can be adapted to the setting of Riemannian manifolds. Throughout this section,
(\Gamma,m)
denotes a smooth, complete, connected, non-compact Riemannian manifold equipped with the geodesic distance, the Riemannian measure, the Laplace–Beltrami operator, and the Riemannian gradient (see Subsection
2.2
for details).

5.1.
First Results

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying the local gradient estimate on the heat kernel (
LG
) and the doubling condition (
D
). Then the inequality (R
p,0
) holds for all
p\in[1,\infty]
; more precisely, for all
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
and
x\in\Gamma
, we have

(5.1)

|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f(x)|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\fint_{B(x,2^{j})}|f|\,dm,

where
D
is the “dimension” from (
2.6
). By duality, the bound (RR
p,1
) also holds for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from (
LG
). For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
C_{0}(x,1):=B(x,1)
and
C_{j}(x,1):=B(x,2^{j})\setminus B(x,2^{j-1})
for
j\geq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla_{x}h_{1}(x,y)||f(y)|\,dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{C_{j}(x,1)}|\nabla_{x}h_{1}(x,y)||f(y)|\,dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{y\in C_{j}(x,1)}|\nabla_{x}h_{1}(x,y)|\int_{B(x,2^{j})}|f|\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{V(x,1)}e^{-c4^{j}}\int_{B(x,2^{j})}|f|\,dm.

By the doubling property (
D
), or more precisely (
2.6
), we have

\frac{1}{V(x,1)}\lesssim 2^{jD}\frac{1}{V(x,2^{j})},

so that

|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f(x)|\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\fint_{B(x,2^{j})}|f|\,dm.

After taking the
L^{p}
-norm, we use Minkowski’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and Fubini’s theorem to get:

\displaystyle\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\left|\fint_{B(x,2^{j})}|f(y)|\,dm(y)\right|^{p}dm(x)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\fint_{B(x,2^{j})}|f(y)|^{p}\,dm(y)\,dm(x)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}|f(y)|^{p}\int_{B(y,2^{j})}\frac{1}{V(x,2^{j})}\,dm(x)\,dm(y)\right)^{1/p}.

By the doubling property again,

\int_{B(y,2^{j})}\frac{1}{V(x,2^{j})}\,dm(x)\lesssim 1,

so

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jD}e^{-c4^{j}}\|f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

The case
p=\infty
follows similarly, with an even simpler proof. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
LG
), (
D
), and (
UEβ
). Then the estimate (
Rp,γ
) holds for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
0\leq\gamma\leq\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p}\}
, and the estimate (
RRp,γ
) holds for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p}\}\leq\gamma\leq 1
.

Proof.

Lemma
5.1
establishes that (R
p,0
) holds for all
p\in[1,\infty]
. The main result of
[
CCFR17
]
asserts that
\|\nabla f\|_{p}\lesssim\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}
for all
p\in(1,2]
, and thus also
\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}
, since
e^{-t\Delta}
is contractive on
L^{p}
. By interpolation (Theorem
3.1
),

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\lesssim C_{p}\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{p}

for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
0\leq\gamma\leq\min\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p}\}
.

The statement for (
RRp,γ
) follows by duality (Theorem
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
D
). Then (RR
2,γ
) holds if and only if
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

By (
E2
) and analyticity of the heat semigroup,

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{2}=\|\Delta^{1/2}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{2},

so (R
2,0
) holds. Interpolation with (
E2
) yields (R
2,γ
) for
0\leq\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Duality (Theorem
3.2
) then gives (RR
2,γ
) for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\gamma\leq 1
.

To show failure of (RR
2,γ
) for
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, we show that 0 belongs to the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
\Delta
. Fix
x_{0}\in\Gamma
and define
f_{r}(x):=\max\{0,1-\frac{d(x,x_{0})}{r}\}
. Then,

\|\Delta^{1/2}f_{r}\|_{2}=\|\nabla f_{r}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{r}V(x_{0},r)\lesssim\frac{1}{r}V(x_{0},r/2)^{1/2}\leq\frac{2}{r}\|f_{r}\|_{2},

using the doubling property. Hence 0 lies in the spectrum of
\Delta^{1/2}
and thus in that of
\Delta
. The conclusion follows as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

5.2.
Further results

The following results are slightly more involved in the case of Riemannian manifolds than for graphs, due to the presence of the smoothing operator
e^{-t\Delta}
in the definitions of (
Rp,γ
) and (
RRp,γ
). On the other hand, we no longer need to assume (
LG
). Since the proofs are similar to those presented in Subsection
4.2
, we omit some details and focus instead on the differences with the graph setting.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
D
) and (
UEβ
). Then for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, any
\alpha\geq 0
, and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}=C(\alpha,p,\epsilon)>0
such that for any ball
B:=B(x,r)\subset\Gamma
with radius
r\geq 1
, and any function
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma,m)
supported in
B
satisfying
\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\geq\epsilon\|f\|_{p}
, we have

(5.2)

\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\epsilon}\,r^{\alpha\beta}\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}.

In particular,

\eqref{RRpgm}\implies\text{(P${}_{p,\beta\gamma}$)}.

Proof.

We follow the strategy of Proposition
4.6
, based on an appropriate reproducing formula. Let
B=B(x,r)
with
r\geq 1
, and let
f
be as in the proposition. Set
u=\lambda^{\beta}r^{\beta}
.

Step 1.
We claim that

(5.3)

\|e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p},

for some sufficiently large
\lambda>0
, independent of
f
and
B
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\|e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Gamma}\left|\int_{\Gamma}h_{u+1}(x,y)f(y)\,dm(y)\right|^{p}dm(x)\right)^{1/p}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Gamma}\sup_{y\in B}|h_{u+1}(x,y)|^{p}\,dm(x)\right)^{1/p}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

By Hölder’s inequality and the assumption on
f
, we have

(5.5)

\|f\|_{L^{1}}\leq m(B)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{p}\leq\epsilon^{-1}m(B)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}.

We decompose the space by setting
C_{0}(\lambda B):=2\lambda B
and
C_{j}(\lambda B):=2^{j+1}\lambda B\setminus 2^{j}\lambda B
for
j\geq 1
. Observe that for
x\in C_{j}(\lambda B)
and
y\in B
, we have

d(x,y)^{\beta}\gtrsim(2^{j\beta}-1)(u+1),

which implies, using (
UEβ
), that

\sup_{y\in B}|h_{u+1}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\lambda B)}e^{-c2^{j\beta/(\beta-1)}}\quad\text{for }x\in C_{j}(\lambda B).

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\sup_{y\in B}|h_{u+1}(x,y)|^{p}\,dm(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}m(2^{j+1}\lambda B)\left(\frac{e^{-c2^{j\beta/(\beta-1)}}}{m(\lambda B)}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{(j+1)D}m(\lambda B)\left(\frac{e^{-c2^{j\beta/(\beta-1)}}}{m(\lambda B)}\right)^{p}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{m(\lambda B)^{p-1}}\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{-(p-1)\delta}}{m(B)^{p-1}}.

By choosing
\lambda
sufficiently large (depending on
\epsilon
), we ensure that

\left(\int_{\Gamma}\sup_{y\in B}|h_{u+1}(x,y)|^{p}\,dm(x)\right)^{1/p}\leq\frac{1}{2}m(B)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\frac{\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}m(B)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{\|f\|_{L^{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}}{\|f\|_{L^{1}}},

which, reinjected into (
5.4
), gives (
5.3
).

Step 2.
For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we use the identity

e^{-\Delta}f=e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f+\int_{0}^{u}\Delta e^{-(s+1)\Delta}f\,ds=e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f+\int_{0}^{u}\Delta^{1-\alpha}e^{-s\Delta}\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\,ds.

Taking the
L^{p}
norm gives

\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\leq\|e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f\|_{p}+C\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\int_{0}^{u}s^{\alpha-1}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}+Cr^{\alpha\beta}\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p},

using analyticity (
2.8
) and
u\simeq r^{\beta}
. Rearranging gives the desired estimate (
5.2
).

Step 3.
For
\alpha>1
, choose an integer
n>\alpha
and iterate the formula as in Step 4 of Proposition
4.6
to obtain

e^{-\Delta}f=T(e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f)+\int_{0}^{u}\cdots\int_{0}^{u}\Delta^{n-\alpha}e^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n})\Delta}\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\,ds_{n}\cdots ds_{1},

where
T
is linear and bounded on all
L^{p}
spaces. As in Step 1, we can choose
\lambda
so that
\|T(e^{-(u+1)\Delta}f)\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}
. Observe that

\int_{0}^{u}\cdots\int_{0}^{u}\frac{1}{(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n})^{n-\alpha}}\,ds_{n}\cdots ds_{1}\lesssim u^{\alpha}\simeq r^{\alpha\beta}

so by using the analyticity of
e^{-\Delta}
given in (
2.8
), we have

\left\|\int_{0}^{u}\cdots\int_{0}^{u}\Delta^{n-\alpha}e^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n})\Delta}\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\,ds_{n}\cdots ds_{1}\right\|_{p}\\
\lesssim\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\int_{0}^{u}\cdots\int_{0}^{u}\frac{1}{(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n})^{n-\alpha}}\,ds_{n}\cdots ds_{1}\lesssim r^{\alpha\beta}\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}.

We conclude that

\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}-\frac{1}{2}\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}\lesssim r^{\alpha\beta}\|\Delta^{\alpha}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{p}.

The proposition follows.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
D
) and (
UEβ
). Then the inequality
(P
2,γβ
)
fails whenever
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

The first two steps are identical to those in Lemma
4.9
, so we only recall the conclusions.

Step 1.
There exists
\Lambda\geq 1
(independent of
y\in\Gamma
and
t\geq 1
) such that

\int_{\Gamma\setminus B(y,\Lambda t^{1/\beta})}h_{t}(x,y)\,dm(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Step 2.
There exists
\eta>0
(independent of
t
and
y
) such that the set

S:=S_{y,t}:=\left\{x\in B(y,\Lambda t^{1/\beta})\,,\,h_{t}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4V(y,\Lambda t^{1/\beta})}\right\}

satisfies
m(S)\geq\eta V(y,t^{1/\beta})
.

Step 3.
Fix
y\in\Gamma
and define

f_{t}(x):=\max\left\{0,\,h_{t}(x,y)-\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}\right\}.

By computations similar to those in (
5.6
), and using (
D
) and (
UEβ
), we have

\|f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})}.

We aim to show that this is still controlled (up to constants) by
\|e^{-t\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}
. By the doubling condition (
D
), the ratio
\frac{8V(y,\Lambda(t+1)^{1/\beta})}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})}
is uniformly bounded for
t\geq t_{0}
, some
t_{0}>0
. For such
t
, we get:

h_{t+1}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4V(y,\Lambda(t+1)^{1/\beta})}\quad\text{for all }x\in S_{y,t+1}.

Since
f_{t}\geq h_{t}(\cdot,y)-\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}
and
e^{-t\Delta}
is positive, it follows that

e^{-t\Delta}f_{t}(x)\geq h_{t+1}(x,y)-\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}\geq\frac{1}{8V(y,\Lambda(t+1)^{1/\beta})}\quad\text{on }S_{y,t+1}.

Hence,

\|e^{-t\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})},

using Step 2 and the doubling property. Therefore,

(5.7)

\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})}\lesssim\|e^{-t\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}\leq\|f_{t}\|_{2}\approx\frac{1}{V(y,t^{1/\beta})},

as desired.

Step 4.
We claim that
\Delta e^{-\Delta}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
. The proof is standard and omitted; it mirrors that of Lemma
5.1
, using (
2.9
) in place of (
LG
).

Decompose
f_{t}=h_{t}(\cdot,y)-g_{t}
with
\|g_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}
. Then,

|\Delta e^{-\Delta}f_{t}(x)|\leq|\partial_{t}h_{t+1}(x,y)|+|\Delta e^{-\Delta}g_{t}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}

by (
2.9
) and the
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of
\Delta e^{-\delta}
.
Combining this with (
5.7
), we deduce

(5.8)

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\Gamma}[\Delta e^{-\Delta}f_{t}]f_{t}\,dm\lesssim\frac{1}{tV(y,t^{1/\beta})}\cdot\int_{\Gamma}h_{t}(x,y)\,dm(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\|e^{-t\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}.

We now find a ball
B(y,r_{t})
such that
\supp f_{t}\subset B(y,r_{t})
. As in Lemma
4.9
, using (
UEβ
), we obtain:

r_{t}\lesssim t^{1/\beta}\ln^{(\beta-1)/\beta}(2t).

Thus, we construct a sequence of functions
f_{t}
supported in
B(y,r_{t})
with
r_{t}\to\infty
and

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\|f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\ln(2r_{t})^{\beta-1}}{r_{t}^{\beta}}\|f_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{t}^{2\gamma\beta+\varepsilon}}\|f_{t}\|_{2}^{2},

for any
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\varepsilon<(1-2\gamma)\beta
. Hence, the Poincaré inequality
(P
2,γβ
)
fails when
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
D
) and (
UEβ
). Then (
RRp,γ
) fails whenever
p\in(1,2)
and

\gamma<\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{\beta}\right).

Proof.

Since the manifold is connected, property
(P_{\infty,1})
holds trivially. Assume, by contradiction, that (
RRp,γ
) holds for some
p\in(1,2)
and

\gamma<\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{\beta}\right).

Then, by Proposition
5.5
, property
(P_{p,\gamma\beta})
holds. Interpolating between
(P_{p,\gamma\beta})
and
(P_{\infty,1})
via Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that
(P_{2,\delta})
holds for

\delta:=\frac{p\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{p}{2\beta}<\frac{1}{2}.

This contradicts Lemma
5.6
, and the result follows.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(\Gamma,m)
be a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
D
) and (
UEβ
). Then (
RRp,γ
) fails whenever
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\left[0,\frac{1}{\beta}\right)
.

Proof.

We argue by contraposition: assume that (
RRp,γ
) holds, and we aim to show that
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{\beta}
. By Proposition
5.5
, the Poincaré inequality
(P_{\beta,\gamma})
holds. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any ball
B:=B(x,r)
with radius
r\geq 1
, and any smooth function
f
supported in
B
, we have

(5.9)

\|e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq Cr^{\beta\gamma}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma
, and for
r\geq 4
, define

f_{r}(x):=\max\{0,r-d(x_{0},x)\}.

Then:

•

|\nabla f_{r}|\leq{\mathds{1}}_{B(x_{0},1)}
, hence

\|\nabla f_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\leq V(x_{0},r)^{1/p}.

•

By Steps 1 and 2 of Lemma
5.6
, there exist constants
\Lambda\geq 1
,
\eta>0
such that the set

S_{y,1}:=\left\{x\in B(y,\Lambda)\,,\,h_{1}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{4V(y,\Lambda)}\right\}

satisfies
m(S_{y,1})\geq\eta V(y,\Lambda)
. So for
r\geq 4\Lambda
and
y\in B(x_{0},r/2-\Lambda)
, we get

e^{-\Delta}f_{r}(y)\geq\int_{S_{y,1}}h_{1}(x,y)f_{r}(x)\,dm(x)\gtrsim\frac{m(S_{y,1})}{4V(y,\Lambda)}\inf_{B(y,\Lambda)}f_{r}(x)\gtrsim r,

whence

\|e^{-\Delta}f_{r}\|_{p}\gtrsim rV(x_{0},r/2-\Lambda)^{1/p}\gtrsim rV(x_{0},r)^{1/p}

by the doubling property.

Inserting these bounds into (
5.9
) gives

rV(x_{0},r)^{1/p}\lesssim r^{\beta\gamma}V(x_{0},r)^{1/p},

which simplifies to

r^{1-\beta\gamma}\lesssim 1.

Since this holds for all
r\geq 4\Lambda
, we must have
1-\beta\gamma\leq 0
, that is,
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{\beta}
. The result follows.
∎

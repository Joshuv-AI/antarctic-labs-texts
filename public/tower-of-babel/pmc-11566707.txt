Introduction

The insertion of foreign bodies (FBs) into the lower genitourinary tract is an emergency and uncommon medical condition [1]. It is rare to observe the story of FBs insertion in a non-self-administered manner [2]. Healthy people do not usually do this [3]. It is limited to men who are mentally unstable or addicted to drugs and alcohol. Inserting FBs into the urethra occurs during masturbation for autoerotism. Patients are usually embarrassed about this story [1]. Patients often delay medical consultation until self-removal attempts fail or symptoms manifest [2]. Lower urinary tract (UT) FBs are commonly associated with acute cystitis symptoms, like urinary frequency, dysuria, urgency, and hematuria [4]. The diagnosis depends on taking a complete medical history, a comprehensive clinical examination, and a procedure of radiographs to detect the location and size of the foreign body [3]. The preferred treatment is endoscopy intervention [4], but in some cases, when it fails, we may resort to open surgery [2]. This paper discusses a 14-year-old patient who had been self-inserting hand sewing needles into his urethra for autoerotism over four years without any complications. Finally, he suffered from penile trauma and painless hematuria. There have been limited reports in the literature where hand sewing needles were observed as urethrovesical FBs for a long time with no or minimal discomfort.

This work is also reported in line with SCARE criteria which helped to improve the transparency and quality of this case report [5].

Case presentation

A 14-year-old patient presented to the urology clinic with a complaint of painless hematuria a day ago. The patient was fully conscious, responsive, and mentally stable. No other significant surgical or medical history was noted. Physical examination showed hardness in the urethra and perineum, and this was confirmed by rectal examination. Laboratory findings revealed hematuria in routine urine analysis, but the results of the complete blood count and electrolyte profile were normal. Ultrasonography was performed, which reported the presence of FBs at the level of the prostatic urethra and bladder neck [Fig. 1]. During follow-up, the patient admitted to the insertion of several hand sewing needles through his penis at different stages and not all at once, with the aim of obtaining sexual pleasure over the past four years. He reported no dysuria or pain after insertion, and attempts at their removal by himself failed. After this revelation from the patient, an X-ray pelvis was ordered, which showed the presence of radiopaque FBs seen in pelvic areas in the midline at the level of the prostatic urethra and bladder neck [Fig. 2]. Following the identification of FBs in the pelvic area at the prostatic urethra and bladder neck on both three-dimensional and two-dimensional CT scans, an urgent cystoscopy was planned for their retrieval [Fig. 3]. The needles were successfully retrieved via cystoscopic intervention by urologists [Fig. 4]. No urethral and/or bladder trauma occurred. The complete extraction of the needles was confirmed by both endoscopy and radiology at the end of the extraction procedure. The postoperative period was uneventful with the disappearance of hematuria. The evaluation by the psychiatrist using their psychometric analysis revealed no psychiatric disturbances. Later, the patient was discharged with analgesics and prophylactic antibiotics. He recovered and had normal urination with full discretion.

Discussion

FBs presence within UT constitutes a prevalent urological concern. While iatrogenic occurrences are more common in the upper UT, with reported cases of 92.5 % linked to prior surgical interventions, FBs within the bladder and/or urethra can arise from self-insertion or, less frequently, migration from neighbouring organs [4]. Inserting FBs into the urethra is rare and often linked to sexual arousal, psychiatric disorders, drug intoxication, or curiosity (particularly in children). It is uncommon in mentally healthy adults and may be done for sexual gratification or to alleviate urinary symptoms [2,3,6]. Inserting FBs into UT in children is rare, usually occurring at the beginning of puberty [6]. The present case describes a 14-year-old adolescent who inserted multiple hand sewing needles into his penis over four years in pursuit of sexual gratification. FBs inserted into UT can vary in composition, ranging from flexible to rigid materials and from fragile to strong substances. These objects may include items such as needles, bullets, pens, candles, gauze, electrical wires, pencils, intrauterine contraceptive devices, catheter components, and magnets [1,3]. Our patient admitted the insertion of many hand sewing needles. FBs in UT can often remain asymptomatic for extended periods [3]. Patients may experience embarrassment, resulting in delayed presentation of symptoms such as pelvic or penile pain, dysuria, and obstruction. Other common complaints include difficulty voiding, genital swelling, urine extravasation, abscess formation, and purulent discharge [1,3,6]. Urethrovesical FBs typically do not present with clear clinical symptoms but rather with suspicious histories of trauma or urinary issues [6]. Many patients are hesitant to disclose the insertion of FBs until complications arise [1,3,6]. Our patient reported the absence of dysuria or pain following the insertion procedure. Furthermore, self-directed attempts at removal proved unsuccessful. A foreign object in the penile urethra, though uncommon, is a significant differential diagnosis for non-specific urethritis, especially if it recurs [7]. The diagnosis, which can be based on physical examination, urinalysis, and imaging (computed tomography and ultrasonography), should be performed after a complete clinical history of the patient. It can be difficult if the self-insertion of the FBs is to have sexual pleasure [[8], [9], [10]]. Certain researchers have utilized cystoscopy as a definitive diagnostic modality in their studies [2]. Potential results from a urine analysis may reveal the presence of red blood cells and pus [1]. In the present case, the presence of FBs was diagnosed by ultrasonography and X-ray. The approach to managing sewing needle injuries varies based on their location, progression, and complications [11]. In addition, the retrieval of intravesical FBs depends on their nature, size, and mobility. Cystoscopy has commonly been the preferred method for this purpose. Rafique et al. found that cystoscopic retrieval was successful in more than half of the patients. Bansal et al. reported a higher success rate of 67 % with endoscopic retrieval [1]. Self-inflicted sexual injuries can result from anorectal trauma, FBs insertion in the vesicourethral or vaginal regions, or the use of constricting devices. Therefore, urologists, gynecologists, and coloproctologists commonly perform surgery for injuries associated with autoerotic sexual activity [12]. In the present case, the needles were successfully retrieved via cystoscopic intervention by urologists. Moreover, for self-insertion soft-tissue foreign bodies (STFBs), image-guided foreign body removal (IGFBR) was utilized, proving a precise and effective approach. IGFBR also provides a minimally invasive approach for the safe, nondeforming removal of multiple FBs in a single procedural setting [13]. Psychiatric evaluations for all patients remain a topic of debate, as some may be psychologically intact. However, initial evaluations are crucial for identifying and managing those with mental disorders. Furthermore, inpatient psychiatric consultations may enhance follow-up for transient patients [14].

Conclusion

Urethrovesical FBs are uncommon in daily clinical practice, particularly among adolescents with no apparent history of mental health disease. Therefore, this paper aims to emphasise the importance of educating the general population about the dangerous consequences of these actions. It also highlights the importance of cystoscopy as a key diagnostic and therapeutic tool for the effective removal of FBs from the lower UT as soon as possible to prevent any complications. In addition, it is recommended that the patients receive a comprehensive psychological assessment to identify any potential underlying mental health conditions and reduce the risk of recurrence.

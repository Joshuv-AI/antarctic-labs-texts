Publisher Correction: Whole genome sequence analysis of pulmonary function and COPD in 44,287 multi-ancestry participants
Authors: Wonji Kim, Xiaowei Hu, Kangjin Kim, Sung Chun, Peter Orchard, Dandi Qiao, Ingo Ruczinski, Aabida Saferali, Francois Aguet, Lucinda Antonacci-Fulton, Pallavi P Balte, Traci M Bartz, Wardatul Jannat Anamika, Xiaobo Zhou, JunYi Duan, Jennifer A Brody, Brian E Cade, Martha L Daviglus, Harshavadran Doddapaneni, Shannon Dugan-Perez, Susan K Dutcher, Christian D Frazar, Stacey B Gabriel, Sina A Gharib, Namrata Gupta, Brian D Hobbs, Silva Kasela, Laura R Loehr, Ginger A Metcalf, Donna M Muzny, Elizabeth C Oelsner, Laura J Rasmussen-Torvik, Colleen M Sitlani, Joshua Smith, Tamar Sofer, Hanfei Xu, Bing Yu, David Zhang, John Ziniti, R Graham Barr
Published: 2026

Publisher Correction: Genome Biol 27, 28 (2026)

Following publication of the original article [1], a typesetting error was noticed, whereby the figure legends for figures 1, 2, 3 and 5 were not included in the published article. The figure legends for figures 1, 2, 3 and 5 are given below. The original article [1] has been corrected.

Fig. 1 Summary of the multi-ancestry fine-mapping (A) Number of credible sets (CSs) in the 47 significant association regions across all phenotypes and subgroups. (B) Number of variants in 95% level CSs. (C) Distribution of posterior inclusion probability (PIP) by the number of variants in 95% level CSs. X-axis is an ascending ranking of the credible set sizes. Red and blue points represent the maximum and median of PIP in each credible set, respectively.

Fig. 2 Comparison of multi-ancestry and European-ancestry fine-mapping (A-C) Box plots for comparing fine-mapping resolution between Multi- and European-ancestry participants in terms of (A) number of fine-mapped variants in credible sets (CSs), (B) genomic interval (kb) of CSs and, (C) maximum posterior inclusion probability (PIP) per CSs. (D) PIP distribution for all variants with PIP > 0.01 in either multi-ancestry or European ancestry fine-mapping analyses (see Supplementary Table 10) (E-F) LocusZoom plots display the improved resolution of fine mapping of a locus near HTR4 with lead variant rs7733410 on FEV1/FVC ratio by including multi-ancestry participants. (E) GWAS and fine mapping results from analysis of all multi-ancestry participants. (F) GWAS and fine mapping results from analysis of all European-ancestry participants. The top row visualizes the strength of association with FEV1/FVC ratio in the target region of HTR4, chr5:148256389-148526959; the bottom row presents fine mapping resolution of association with FEV1/FVC ratio for the same region.

Fig. 3 Regional plots of pulmonary trait association and LTRC eQTL for colocalized gene regions. A) ADAM19 for combined analysis of FEV1/FVC ratio. B) C4B for combined analysis of FEV1/FVC ratio. C) THSD4 for combined analysis of FEV1/FVC ratio. D) PSMA4 for COPD-enriched analysis of severe COPD. Each sub-panel presents the genetic association results for a target region; the left plot visualizes the strength of association with pulmonary trait vs LTRC eQTL within the target region; the right top plot shows pulmonary trait association within the target region; and the right bottom plot shows LTRC eQTL association within the target region. The labeled variant is the lead variant identified from the sum of P-values for pulmonary trait association and LTRC eQTL, and other variants are colored according to their LD R2 with the lead variant. The TOPMed Freeze 8 genotype data of 1,360 LTRC samples were used to calculate in-sample LD for LTRC colocalization and for generating plots.

Fig. 5 Overview of the statistical modeling workflow. We illustrate a workflow for (A) the single variant-based association analysis and (B) the variant set-based association analysis.

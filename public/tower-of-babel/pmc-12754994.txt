Rising mortality trends due to falls among the elderly in Southwest China from 2014 to 2022: age-period-cohort analysis of 6,524 individuals
Authors: Zhi Lei, Haohang Zeng, Tianyong Wu, Dongyang Liu, HaoLin Zhang, Song Fan, Qing Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAccording to previous studies, falls are one of the important causes of death among the elderly. In recent years, the fall-related mortality rate among the elderly population has been on a continuous rise, which is significantly affected by factors such as age growth and period changes. However, traditional epidemiological.ObjectiveTo examine the temporal trends and epidemiological characteristics of fall-induced mortality among older adults in Luzhou City, Southwest China, between 2014 and 2022, with the aim of providing targeted fall prevention strategies for the elderly.MethodsMortality data were analyzed using the Joinpoint regression model to determine Annual Percent Change (APC) and Average Annual Percent Change (AAPC). An age-period-cohort model constructed in R 4.3.2 evaluated the influences of these dimensions on mortality trends. Our analysis focused on differences in trends between male and female elderly residents.ResultsThe study included a total of 6,524 elderly people over 60 years old who died from falls. Both genders exhibited increasing mortality trends, with males showing an AAPC of 6.168% and females an AAPC of 6.3%. Notably, the risk of death from falls was significantly higher in older age groups, with males aged 80-84 showing more than double the risk compared to those aged 60-64. Similarly, females in the same age group had a 3.8-fold higher risk. The period analysis indicated a near doubling of risk from 2014 to 2022. No significant trends were observed in cohort effects.ConclusionThe mortality from falls among elderly residents in this study has shown a concerning upward trend from 2014 to 2022. The increased risk is particularly pronounced with advancing age and over recent years for both genders. These findings highlight the urgent need for enhanced fall prevention measures targeted at the elderly population to mitigate this growing public health issue.

Introduction

Falls refers to unintentionally dropping to the ground or onto a surface lower than the initial level but excludes falls caused by external forces or diseases such as epilepsy [1]. Currently, population aging has become a global social issue. The statistical data shows that the incidence of falls among the global population aged 65 and above ranges from 28% to 35%, while for those aged 70 and above, the incidence of falls can be as high as 32% to 42% [2]. The aging problem in China is also becoming increasingly severe. In 2019 alone, the number of new elderly people aged 65 and above reached as many as 9.45 million, and the population of elderly people aged 80 and above is increasing at a rate of 5% annually. According to the death cause results released by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention in 2021, accidental falls are the leading cause of injury-related deaths in both the general population and those aged 65 and above. The mortality rate from accidental falls among the population aged 65 and above is 69.05 per 100,000, accounting for 43% of all injury-related deaths in this age group [3]. According to estimates, the mortality rate due to falls within one year among residents aged 60 and above in China is 18.3% (95% CI: 15.7% to 20.8%) [4]. The direct medical costs incurred annually due to falls among the elderly exceed 5 billion RMB [5], resulting in significant social and economic burdens. Falls among the elderly are influenced by multiple factors, including age effects, period effects, and cohort effects, all of which can increase the risk of falls among the elderly.

Temporal trend analysis is an important component of epidemiological research [6]. The traditional epidemiological analysis method examines the overall trend of disease distribution within a certain time frame from a holistic perspective, making it difficult to analyze localized changes in disease distribution [7]. The Joinpoint regression model proposed by Kim et al. [8] allows fitting regression curves based on the temporal characteristics of disease distribution. The inflection points between different fitted curves can divide the temporal trend of disease distribution into different stages, enabling clearer visualization of localized changes in disease trends [9]. Age, period, and cohort are important influencing factors affecting the occurrence and development of diseases in populations. However, in practical analysis, there is often a linear relationship among these three factors. Traditional descriptive statistical analysis methods cannot effectively control the interaction effects of these three factors. The Age-Period-Cohort model (APC-M) can analyze the temporal trend of the target disease while considering these three factors and can predict changes in future disease burden based on the trend [10–12].

This study utilized Joinpoint regression model and APC-M to analyze the data from the fall-related mortality monitoring system among elderly individuals in Luzhou City.This study addresses the limitations of traditional fall-related mortality research, which mostly focuses on cross-sectional surveys or single risk factor analysis and thus struggles to accurately identify the local variation characteristics of the temporal trend distribution of fall-related mortality. Meanwhile, this study focuses on the elderly population in Southwest China, supplementing relevant research on the long-term trends of fall-related mortality among the elderly in Southwest China (especially underdeveloped cities in the region). The study results can be compared with national and other regional studies to identify differences in the influencing factors of fall-related mortality risk among the elderly across regions, enabling the learning of effective prevention and control experiences from other regions. Additionally, comparing research results across different time periods allows for the evaluation of the long-term effectiveness of prevention and control measures, the timely adjustment and optimization of prevention and control strategies, and the provision of scientific evidence for the regional prevention and control of falls among the elderly.The aim was to explore the risk factors and epidemiological characteristics associated with falls among the elderly and to provide scientific evidence for fall prevention among this demographic.

Methods

Data sources

The mortality data utilized in this study were extracted from the Population Death Information Registration Management System, a national-level surveillance platform maintained by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. For the specific context of Luzhou City, the elderly death information within this system is reported by a comprehensive network of 165 public primary care institutions (which include community health service centers and township health centers) and 22 county-level and above hospitals, ensuring broad coverage across the city’s jurisdiction. Notably, the reliability of this dataset has been rigorously validated through annual death cause underreporting surveys: between 2014 and 2022, the annual underreporting rate remained consistently low, ranging from 10.10% to 14.15%, ensuring that the elderly individuals included in this study are sufficiently representative. Population data for Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 were obtained from the Luzhou Municipal Public Security Bureau. The inclusion criteria were cases with household registration in Luzhou City, aged ≥ 60 years, and whose underlying cause of death was coded as “fall-related” (ICD-10: W00-W19). Duplicate death records were checked and verified using residents’ identification numbers, and no duplicates were identified. A total of 6,524 death records were finally included. For the period 2014–2022, the annual crude mortality rate (CMR) and age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) were calculated for each age group. Since this study focused on fall-related mortality data among residents, the mortality rate was replaced with the fall-related mortality rate. National mortality data for each age group annually were sourced from the “China Population Census Yearbook” compiled by the National Bureau of Statistics based on the results of the seventh national population census. Joinpoint regression model analysis

The Joinpoint regression model is utilized to calculate and analyze the APC, AAPC, and their 95% confidence intervals (CI) for fall-related deaths among elderly individuals in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022. APC and AAPC represent the rate of change and average rate of change of the disease or injury over a certain period. When the calculated APC and AAPC with their 95% CI are positive and do not include 0, it indicates an increasing trend in the mortality rate over time. Conversely, if the calculated results are negative and do not include 0, it indicates a decreasing trend. When the confidence interval includes zero, it indicates that the available data are insufficient to clearly determine whether the temporal trend of the target disease or event during the corresponding period is increasing, decreasing, or stable. When APC = AAPC, it signifies a monotonic trend in the change over time [8, 13].

Age-period-cohort model analysis

The APC-M, based on the Poisson distribution, is commonly used to analyze the trends in disease incidence and mortality [14], by decomposing the influence of age, period, and cohort effects on the disease, it helps in analyzing the trend of disease development [15]. However, the traditional APC-M fails to address the issue of unidentifiability due to the linear relationship among age, period, and cohort factors. This often leads to non-unique parameter estimates and even contradictory predictive results if no conditional constraints are set during analysis [16, 17]. To address this issue, various parameter estimation methods have been proposed. In this study, we adopted the intrinsic estimator (IE) proposed by Fu [18] to estimate parameters. The effect coefficients obtained are calculated based on the overall average level and can be converted into Relative Risk (RR) using the formula RR = e(effect coefficient), where e is the base of the natural logarithm. RR reflects the difference in mortality risk among the target population at different effect levels [19]. Data processing and analysis methods

Analyze the temporal trend of fall-related death distribution among residents aged 60–84 in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 using the Joinpoint regression model.The fall-related mortality data for elderly individuals in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 were organized using Excel 2019 software. The population aged 60 and above was classified by gender into five-year age groups. Since the standardized population data used as reference grouped individuals aged 85 and above into a single age group, and due to the open-ended nature of the data for individuals aged 85 and above, they were included in the 60–84 age group. This resulted in five age groups (60–64, 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, and 80–84). The annual percent change (APC), average annual percent change (AAPC), and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for each age group.

Statistical tools

Data analysis in this study was performed using the following tools:

R statistical software (Version 4.3.3), developed by the R Core Team (official website: https://www.r-project.org/). For the APC modeling, we used the APCG1 tool (a non-CRAN R resource developed by Dr. Wenjiang Fu) [18]. The primary estimation method in this tool is the Intrinsic Estimator (IE), which is specifically designed to address the identifiability problem in APC models, with its official information available at https://www.math.uh.edu/~fuw/index_Faculty%20page%20Res_new.html, and the tool package is available upon request to the corresponding author;

Joinpoint Regression Program (Version 5.0.1), developed by the National Cancer Institute (NCI, USA; official website: https://surveillance.cancer.gov/joinpoint/), which was utilized for building the Joinpoint regression model;

Microsoft Excel (Version 2017), developed by Microsoft Corporation (official information accessible via Microsoft’s official archive for previous versions of Microsoft 365 applications).

All statistical tests in this study were two-tailed, with a p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Fall incidents among elderly individuals in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022

From 2014 to 2022, the number of reported cases and the mortality rate of fall-related deaths among the 60–84 age group in Luzhou City showed an overall increasing trend. However, slight decreases in the number of reported cases and mortality rates were observed in 2017, 2019, and 2021 compared to the previous year. The number of fall-related deaths among individuals aged 60–84 increased from 428 cases in 2014 to 1048 cases in 2022, with the mortality rate rising from 50.17 per 100,000 in 2014 to 109.51 per 100,000 in 2022. Among males aged 60–84, the number of reported cases and the mortality rate of fall-related deaths showed an overall increasing trend, rising from 275 cases and a mortality rate of 64.12 per 100,000 in 2014 to 616 cases and a mortality rate of 130.79 per 100,000 in 2022. Similarly, among females aged 60–84, both the number of reported cases and the mortality rate of fall-related deaths exhibited an increasing trend, with the number of cases increasing from 153 in 2014 to 432 in 2022, and the mortality rate rising from 36.06 per 100,000 in 2014 to 88.89 per 100,000 in 2022 (refer to Tables 1 and 2). Joinpoint regression model analysis revealed that there were no statistically significant inflection points in the fall-related mortality rates among the total population, males, and females from 2014 to 2022. The fitted lines were uniformly increasing. Among the total population aged 60–84, the APC and AAPC were both 5.8413%(95%CI:2.582%ཞ9.148%). For females aged 60 and above, the APC and AAPC were both 6.2453%༈95%CI:1.9360%ཞ10.7368%༉. Among males aged 60 and above, the APC and AAPC were both 6.168%༈95%CI:3.3014%ཞ9.1143%༉. Trend analysis using SPSS 23.0 software revealed a statistically significant difference in the increasing trend of fall-related mortality rates among the 60–84 age group in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 (χ²trend = 247.2, P < 0.001). Additionally, there was an increasing trend in fall-related mortality rates with the passage of years. Among individuals aged 60 and above, males had a higher fall-related mortality rate compared to females. Please refer to Figs. 1 and 2; Tables 1, and 2.

Age effect

The APC-M’s age effect indicates that the risk of fall-related mortality among residents aged 60–85 in Luzhou City gradually increases with age. Among the statistically significant effect indicators, the effect coefficient value is lowest for the 60–64 age group (−0.2995), while it is highest for the 80–84 age group (0.5251). When converted, the risk of fall-related mortality in the 80–84 age group is 2.28 times higher than that in the 60–64 age group. The gender-age effect indicates that the risk of fall-related mortality among male and female residents aged 60–85 in Luzhou City increases gradually with age. The risk of fall-related mortality in the 80–84 age group is 2.06 times higher than that in the 60–64 age group for male residents and 3.82 times higher for female residents. Period effect

The period effect in the APC-M shows that the risk of fall-related mortality among residents aged 60–84 in Luzhou City has been gradually increasing from 2014 to 2022. Within the observation periods where the effect coefficients are statistically significant, the coefficients show an increasing trend with the years. The effect coefficient value is lowest in 2014(−0.3795), while the effect coefficient value is highest in 2022(0.3331). In 2022, the risk of fall-related mortality among residents aged 60–84 in Luzhou City is 2.04 times higher than that in 2014. Regarding gender-specific period effects, the risk of fall-related mortality among male residents aged 60–84 in Luzhou City in 2022 is 1.92 times higher than that in 2014, while for female residents, it is 2.43 times higher in 2022 compared to 2014. Cohort effect

The cohort effect in the APC-M indicates that the risk of fall-related mortality among birth cohorts from 1930 to 1964 generally shows a downward trend. Among the birth cohorts where the effect coefficients are statistically significant, the highest effect coefficient is 0.1826 for the birth cohort from 1945 to 1949, while the lowest is −0.1622 for the birth cohort from 1930 to 1934. Discussion

The issue of population aging has become a global social concern [20]. According to statistics, every year, globally, there are 37.3 million cases of severe falls, resulting in approximately 17 million disabilities and 684,000 fall-related deaths, with falls commonly occurring among the elderly population [21, 22]. An analysis of fall-related data among Chinese residents aged 60 and above in 2013 found that the fall-related mortality rate within six months for this age group was 8.0%. Moreover, the mortality rate due to falls increases with age, reaching 12.6% for those over 80 years old. Additionally, more than half of the elderly individuals (52.6%) experience injuries after a fall, with the highest proportion of injuries occurring among the oldest age group (56.8%). Accidental falls are reported as the leading cause of injury-related deaths among people aged 65 and above, accounting for 43% of all causes of death in this age group [3], resulting in significant social burdens and seriously affecting the health of the elderly population.

This study analyzed the temporal trends of fall-related mortality rates among residents aged 60–84 in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 using the Joinpoint regression model. The results revealed an increasing trend in fall-related mortality rates among the entire population aged 60–84, as well as among males and females. Analysis of monitored data revealed an increase in fall-related mortality rates with advancing age. Furthermore, calculations of gender-specific fall-related mortality rates among individuals aged 60–84 in Luzhou City from 2014 to 2022 indicated higher rates among males compared to females, consistent with national mortality monitoring data [3]. Moreover, during the aforementioned period, the fall-related mortality rates among males and females aged 60–84 in Luzhou City showed a rising trend annually. Similar studies conducted in other countries and regions have also confirmed this finding [22–24]. This trend may be attributed to various factors associated with aging, such as visual impairment, hearing loss, decreased static and dynamic balance abilities, limited joint mobility, pain, reduced muscle strength, fear of fracture after falls, and osteoporosis [25].

The results from the APC-M indicate that the risk of fall-related mortality among residents aged 60–85 in Luzhou City, including both males and females, gradually increases with age. Additionally, the period effect analysis reveals a year-on-year increase in fall-related mortality risk among residents aged 60–85 from 2014 to 2022, suggesting that the rising trend in fall-related mortality rates in Luzhou City is influenced by both age and period factors. To address this issue, the government should increase investment in elderly life support, focusing on primary prevention for high-risk populations. Grassroots departments should conduct targeted prevention work for elderly residents in their jurisdictions. This may involve guiding visually impaired elderly individuals to wear appropriate glasses, improving the brightness of living spaces, regularly clearing clutter, and inspecting potential safety hazards in elderly living environments. Encouraging physical activity among the elderly and promoting a balanced diet rich in minerals, high-quality proteins, and vitamins can help slow down age-related declines in physical function. Other measures to reduce the risk of falls among the elderly include installing handrails in areas such as bathrooms and stairwells and conducting fall prevention education campaigns.

Combining the findings of this study with previous research, it can be concluded that falls in older adults are influenced by multiple factors, among which the severity of falls is a common factor that increases the risk of falls in this population [26, 27]; therefore, quickly identifying the fall status of older adults and conducting initial screening for fall risk are fundamental to addressing the challenge of falls in the elderly. “World Guidelines for Falls Prevention and Management for Older Adults: A Global Initiative” is a set of recommendations for falls prevention and management in older adults developed through joint deliberation by 96 experts from 39 countries, which has broad applicability and guiding significance for the prevention and management of falls in older adults, and the guidelines point out that high-risk groups for falls can be identified by assessing the severity of falls in older adults [28]: older adults who present with any of the following five conditions can be classified as having high fall risk: (1) falls accompanied by injuries; (2) multiple falls (≥ 2 times) within the past 12 months; (3) frailty, which can be assessed using the Frailty Phenotype (FP) or Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS); (4) inability to stand up independently for at least 1 h after a fall; (5) falls accompanied by transient loss of consciousness or suspected syncope; older adults with a single non-severe fall in the past 12 months combined with gait and/or balance problems are classified as having moderate fall risk; those with no fall history in the past 12 months, or only a single non-severe fall without gait or balance problems, are classified as having low fall risk; targeted interventions should be implemented based on different risk levels, for example, providing falls prevention education to the low-risk group to reduce their risk of falls, adding targeted exercise interventions (such as balance training and muscle strength training including sit-to-stand exercises and single-leg standing) to the moderate-risk group on the basis of interventions for the low-risk group, and conducting regular multifactorial fall risk assessments for the high-risk group and developing multi-domain intervention plans based on the assessment results to reduce the risk of recurrent falls; however, although these guidelines provide a comprehensive framework for global falls prevention and management in older adults, they still have limitations including unvalidated algorithms, insufficient evidence in some areas, and a lack of data to support long-term benefits.

However, the study still has several limitations that need to be addressed. Firstly, it only included data from the years 2014 to 2022, which may not fully capture long-term trends. Therefore, expanding the data collection period could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the issue. Secondly, the data is regionally specific to Luzhou City, which limits the generalizability of the findings to other regions. Future studies should aim to include data from multiple regions to enhance the external validity of the conclusions. Additionally, the study relied on surveillance data, which is passively collected and may be subject to biases related to factors such as regional healthcare conditions and the professionalism of medical personnel. Therefore, caution should be exercised when interpreting the results derived from surveillance data. Further research is needed to validate the findings and enhance the robustness of the analysis methods used in this study.In addition, this study adopted 5-year equidistant age grouping for analysis; while this grouping method aligns with the stepwise progression pattern of physiological function decline and comorbidity accumulation in older adults, and also has the advantage of smoothing variability, it may also obscure some more subtle trends—such as: the potential variations in fall risk within the 65–69 years group, and the fact that the merging of the 85 years and above group (due to its small sample size) may to some extent obscure the unique risk characteristics of the very elderly population. If future studies require more refined analysis, they can conduct dynamic grouping and subgroup analysis based on the characteristics of fall-related mortality risk in each age segment, on the premise of expanding the sample size, to ensure data reliability and grouping accuracy.

Conclusions

From 2014 to 2022, the fall-related mortality among older adults aged 60–84 in Luzhou City, Southwest China showed an upward trend, which was influenced by age and period factors. Thus, it is necessary to increase investment in elderly living security, prioritize primary prevention for high-risk groups (e.g., those aged 80–84 years, especially females), and guide grass-roots departments to conduct targeted prevention work for local elderly, so as to reduce fall-related health and socioeconomic burdens.

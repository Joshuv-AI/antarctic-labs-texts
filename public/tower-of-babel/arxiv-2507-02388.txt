Stringy Corrections to Heterotic SU(3)-Geometry
Jock McOrist

Abstract




\proofmodefalse

We analyse the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
corrections to the supersymmetry algebra constructed by Bergshoeff de Roo for heterotic compactifications on
{\rm SU}(3)
manifolds. The geometry remains complex, conformally balanced, but
H

(0,2)
component with its trace fixed in terms of
{\text{d}}H
.

A natural consistency check of the BdR supersymmetry algebra is integrability: do consecutive supersymmetry variations vanish up to symmetries and the equations of motion at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
? Our findings reveal a subtle structure. The BdR algebra

]

At first glance, these extraneous dilaton terms imply the supersymmetry algebra is not integrable on-shell, even when the graviton equation of motion holds. For SU(3)-structure manifolds admitting a smooth

, the additional terms vanish identically and so this is a particularly convenient gauge. We end up with the BdR supersymmetry algebra is integrable if and only if the graviton equations of motion are satisfied.

In §
5
, we reformulate our results in a manner more accessible to a mathematical audience. We begin by assuming that the supersymmetry conditions—expressed as tensorial equations—hold identically. Under this assumption, we demonstrate that the equations of motion can be recovered purely from these tensor conditions using standard results from Hermitian geometry. This provides an independent consistency check of our analysis, and also a foundation for further mathematical exploration of the system. This approach reproduces the same gauge constraint on the dilaton, reinforcing the coherence of the geometric and physical formulations.

The outline for the paper is as follows. In section 2 we review the Bergshoeff-de Roo supersymmetry algebra in

Proposition 1
.

Let
(X,g,J)
be an almost-complex manifold, and let

(2.14)

Then we have the relations

\displaystyle{T}_{ijk}

\displaystyle\;=\;4N_{kij}+(d\omega)_{Ji,Jj,Jk}

(2.15)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\;=\;\text{i}(\partial-\bar{\partial})\omega+N

(2.16)

where
\partial\omega=(d\omega)^{2,1}

\bar{\partial}\omega=(d\omega)^{1,2}

Proof.

In components, our conventions are

\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\omega_{ij}{\text{d}}x^{ij},\quad J^{i}{}_{p}J^{p}{}_{j}\;=\;\!-\delta^{i}{}_{j},\quad\omega_{ij}\;=\;J^{p}{}_{i}g_{pj},\quad\omega_{ij}\;=\;g_{Ji,j}~,

where

N\;=\;\frac{1}{2}N_{pij}\,{\text{d}}x^{p}\otimes{\text{d}}x^{ij}~,\quad N_{pij}\;=\;g_{pk}N^{k}{}_{ij}~,

where

N^{p}{}_{ij}\;=\;\frac{1}{4}\bigg(J^{k}{}_{i}\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{k}J^{p}{}_{j}+J^{p}{}_{k}\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{j}J^{k}{}_{i}-(i\leftrightarrow j)\bigg)~.

(2.17)

\bullet
The first step is to use
\nabla\omega=0
to compute

in terms of
T
. We start from

{\text{d}}\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{k}\omega_{ij}{\text{d}}x^{kij},\quad(d\omega)=\frac{1}{3!}(d\omega)_{kij}\,{\text{d}}x^{kij}~.

From
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}\omega=0
, we use the expression (
2.14
) to convert
\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}
to
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}
and obtain

{\text{d}}\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}T_{k}{}^{\ell}{}_{i}\omega_{\ell j}-\frac{1}{2}T_{k}{}^{\ell}{}_{j}\omega_{i\ell}\bigg){\text{d}}x^{kij}~.

We skew-symmetrize this expression and derive

({\text{d}}\omega)_{kij}\;=\;\!-T_{k}{}^{\ell}{}_{i}\omega_{\ell j}-T_{j}{}^{\ell}{}_{k}\omega_{\ell i}-T_{i}{}^{\ell}{}_{j}\omega_{\ell k}~.

Since

({\text{d}}\omega)_{kij}\;=\;T_{k}{}^{\ell}{}_{i}g_{\ell,Jj}+T_{j}{}^{\ell}{}_{k}g_{\ell,Ji}+T_{i}{}^{\ell}{}_{j}g_{\ell,Jk}~.

and so

({\text{d}}\omega)_{kij}\;=\;T_{k,Jj,i}+T_{j,Ji,k}+T_{i,Jk,j}~.

(2.18)

\bullet
The second step is to use
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}J=0
to compute
N
in terms of
T
. We can use the expression (
2.14
) to derive the relation

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{k}J^{p}{}_{j}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}T_{k}{}^{p}{}_{r}J^{r}{}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}T_{k}{}^{r}{}_{j}J^{p}{}_{r}~.

where

and Nijenhuis becomes

N^{p}{}_{ij}\;=\;\frac{1}{4}(T_{Ji}{}^{p}{}_{Jj}-T_{Ji}{}^{Jp}{}_{j}+T_{j}{}^{Jp}{}_{Ji}+T_{j}{}^{p}{}_{i})~.

\bullet
We now derive (
2.15

({\text{d}}\omega)_{Ji,Jj,Jk}\;=\;T_{Ji,j,Jk}+T_{i,Jj,Jk}+T_{Ji,Jj,k}~.

Lower an index on

g_{kp}T_{Ji}{}^{Jp}{}_{j}=-T_{Ji,Jk,j}

\begin{split}N_{kij}&\;=\;\frac{1}{4}(T_{Ji,k,Jj}+2T_{Ji,Jk,j}+T_{kij})\\
&\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{4}(T_{Ji,Jj,k}+T_{Ji,j,Jk}+T_{i,Jj,Jk}-T_{ijk})\\

We have used that

\bullet

3.4
Holomorphic volume form

We have shown that the supersymmetry equations (
2.10
) lead to an integrable complex structure
J
at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. Next, we investigate holomorphicity of a nowhere vanishing 3-form at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. Consider the 3-form
\Psi\in\Omega^{3}(X)
determined by the positive chirality spinor
\eta
given by

\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;\bar{\eta}^{T}\gamma_{ijk}\bar{\eta}~.

(3.19)

One can check using
\gamma_{7}\eta=\eta
and
\gamma_{\alpha}\eta=0
in holomorphic directions that
\Psi\in\Omega^{3,0}(X,\mathbb{C})
. The gravitino equation (
2.12
) implies

\quad\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}\Psi\;=\;0~,

(3.20)

where the connection
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}
is defined in (
2.13
). Since the connection
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}
also satisfies
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}g=0
and
\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}}J=0
, its holonomy is contained in the group
SU(3)
.

{\rm Hol}(\nabla^{{\text{\tiny B}}})\subseteq SU(3)~.

The pair
(\omega,\Psi)
forms an
SU(3)
-structure, namely

\omega\wedge\Psi\;=\;0~,\quad i\Psi\wedge\bar{\Psi}\;=\;\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}~,\quad|\Psi|_{g}\;=\;1~.

We note that neither
\omega
nor
\Psi
are closed. The constant norm 3-form
\Psi
will not be a holomorphic volume form at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
, but we will arrange that
\Omega=e^{f}\Psi
solves
\bar{\partial}\Omega=0
for some conformal factor
e^{f}
.

Let us rewrite the gravitino equation for
\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}\eta
in a different way for later use. Using
\gamma_{\alpha}\eta=0
in holomorphic directions and
H^{3,0}={\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P^{3,0}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})
, from the gravitino equation (
2.12
) we derive

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\alpha}\eta=\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\bar{\nu}}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\eta-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}P_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\bar{\nu}}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\eta~,

in holomorphic indices. We next substitute the dilatino equation (
2.34
).

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\alpha}\eta\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\alpha}\Phi)\eta+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}P_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\bar{\nu}}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\eta~.

(3.21)

We can also combine the dilatino equation (
2.34
) with the locking relation for
H
given in (
2.23
), knowing now that the Nijenhuis tensor is zero, to see

(i\partial\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\;=\;\!-2\partial_{\lambda}\Phi~.

(3.22)

Combining (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) yields the identity

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\alpha}\eta\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{4}(i\partial\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}\eta~.

(3.23)

With (
3.23
), we can now compute the covariant derivative of the 3-form
\Psi
defined in (
3.19
).

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;2\bar{\eta}^{T}\gamma_{ijk}\nabla^{\rm LC}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}\bar{\eta}\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{2}(i\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}~.

The Levi-Civita connection induced on top forms
\Psi\in\Omega^{3,0}(X)
is

\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;\partial_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}-\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;\partial_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}-\frac{1}{2}(i\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\bar{\alpha}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}\Psi_{ijk}.

\partial_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;\!-(\text{i}\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}~.

Substituting (
3.22

\partial_{\bar{\alpha}}\Psi_{ijk}\;=\;(2\partial_{\bar{\alpha}}\Phi+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\alpha}})\Psi_{ijk}~.

As expected,
\Psi

(3.25)

3.5
Conformally balanced equation

The supersymmetry equations on spinors have produced a pair
(\omega,\Omega)
, where
\omega
is a hermitian metric on a complex manifold and
\Omega
is a holomorphic volume form. We now derive the conformally balanced relation
d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})=0
.

We combine (
3.24
) and (
3.22
) to obtain

\frac{1}{2}(\text{i}\partial\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\lambda}\Phi-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{32}\partial_{\lambda}(\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2})~.

(3.26)

Let
e^{\hat{\Phi}}
be a conformal factor which we introduce to solve
d(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega^{2})=0
. We substitute the supersymmetry constraint (
3.26
) into the general identity (
C.2
) to obtain

\partial(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega^{2})\;=\;2e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\bigg[\partial\Phi+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{32}\partial(\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2})-\partial\hat{\Phi}\bigg]\wedge\omega^{2}~.

If we let

\hat{\Phi}\;=\;\Phi+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{32}(\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2})~,

then
d(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega^{2})=0
, or written in full

{\text{d}}\bigg(\exp\bigg[-2\Phi-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{16}(\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}\bigg]\omega^{2}\bigg)\;=\;0~.

(3.27)

We can write this conformal factor
e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}
in terms of the holomorphic volume form
\Omega
by substituting (
3.25
), and the result is
d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})=0
.

The mathematical significance of the conformally balanced equation becomes apparent when the gaugino equation (
2.10
) is included, which states
g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}F_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=0
. The pair
(\omega,F)
then solves

d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})\;=\;0,\quad F\wedge\omega^{2}\;=\;0~.

The (conformal) closedness of
\omega^{2}
is what allows the numerical pairing
[\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2}]\cdot c_{1}(S)
to be defined on subsheaves
S\subseteq E
, and this is what allows a well-defined notion of slope stability as a criterion for the non-Kähler Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
: if
[\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2}]\cdot c_{1}(S)<0
for all strict torsion-free coherent subsheaves
S\subseteq E
, then there exists a solution to
F\wedge\omega^{2}=0
.

\hat{\Phi}
and
\Phi
. First, trace (
5.10
):

{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}(\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2})\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}^{2}(\text{tr}\thinspace F\wedge F-\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{\tiny CH}}\wedge R^{\text{\tiny CH}})~,

where we used
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})
. The nonlinear constraint (
5.4
) for
i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega
and definition of
\hat{\Phi}
imply

\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}\Lambda^{2}_{\omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)~.

We can now apply (
5.16
) to conclude

\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Lambda i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi~.

(5.17)

5.3
Yang-Mills equation

The argument at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
for the divergence Yang-Mills equation derived from the Hermitian Yang-Mills equation over a conformally balanced geometry is well-known in the literature; see
[
46
]
.

\mathpzc D^{-\,m}(\text{e}^{-2\Phi}F_{mn})\;=\;\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

We outlined the derivation earlier in the file in Section
4.2.1
. Here we simply note that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation
g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}F_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=0
does not imply the usual Yang-Mills equation
\nabla^{m}F_{mn}=0
when
g
is non-Kähler, but rather the
F
-equation of motion above is the correct adaptation of the Yang-Mills equation.

5.4
Divergence of the 3-form

In this subsection, we derive

\nabla^{m}(\text{e}^{-2\Phi}H_{mij})\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.18)

We take the approach of
[
39
]
. The starting point is the following observation due to
[
47
]
: let
\omega
be a conformally balanced metric on a complex manifold
X
of dimension 3, so that
d(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega^{2})=0
. Then

\star T\;=\;\!-e^{2\hat{\Phi}}d(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega)~,\quad d\star(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}T)\;=\;0~.

(5.19)

In our current setup, we solve
d(e^{-2\hat{\Phi}}\omega^{2})=0
by (
5.9
). To show (
5.19
), we can first use the primitive decomposition to derive the general formula

\star\chi\;=\;J\chi-J(\Lambda_{\omega}\chi)\wedge\omega~,\quad\chi\in\Omega^{3}(X)~.

(5.20)

Here
J
acts by differential forms by duality, so that for example in holomorphic coordinates
Jdx=idx
and
Jd\bar{x}=-id\bar{x}
. Equation (
5.19
) then follows from (
5.20
) and (
5.9
). From here, we can derive the
H
-equation of motion by expanding

d\star(e^{-2\Phi}H)\;=\;d\star(e^{-2\Phi}T)+2{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}d\star(e^{-2\Phi}P)~.

For the first term

d\star(e^{-2\Phi}T)\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~,

by (
5.19
) and using
\hat{\Phi}-\Phi=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
and
T=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
. For the second term,

2{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}d\star(e^{-2\Phi}P)\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~,

since
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
,
\partial\Phi=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
, and
d\star P=0
since
(d^{\dagger})^{2}=0
.

5.5
Einstein equation

In this subsection, we show that if
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
, then

{\rm Ric}_{mn}+2\nabla_{m}\nabla_{n}\Phi-\frac{1}{4}H_{mpq}H_{n}{}^{pq}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace F_{mp}F_{n}{}^{p}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}_{mp}R^{\text{H}}_{n}{}^{p}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.21)

We will pullback by a certain family of diffeomorphisms along the flow of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
to arrange that
\Phi
is a constant plus quadratic terms in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. This will be done later in Section
5.7
. For now, we conclude that if
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
, then the Einstein equation is satisfied to order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. In the detailed calculation below, we will see the extra terms that arise if
\Phi
is not constant to this order.

5.5.1
Double holomorphic directions

We start by examining (
5.21
) when
m,n
are both holomorphic indices. The first step is to note the general formula for the Ricci curvature in hermitian geometry along two holomorphic indices:

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\beta}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\alpha}^{\text{\tiny CH}}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}^{\text{\tiny CH}}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\beta}~,\quad T\;=\;\text{i}(\partial-\bar{\partial})\omega~.

We now substitute (
5.9
).

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\beta}\;=\;\!-\nabla_{\alpha}^{\text{\tiny CH}}\partial_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}-\nabla^{\text{\tiny CH}}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\hat{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\beta}~.

Converting the Chern connection
\nabla^{\text{\tiny CH}}
to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
gives:

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\beta}+2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\beta}~.

Next, we note that

\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha pq}H_{\beta}{}^{pq}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T_{\beta}{}^{\mu\bar{\nu}}~.

Therefore

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\beta}+2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha pq}H_{\beta}{}^{pq}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.22)

If we compare with (
5.21
), there is a term like

\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace F_{\alpha m}F_{\beta}{}^{m}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha m}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta}{}^{m}\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha\mu}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta\bar{\nu}}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta\mu}~.

This is order
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})
. Indeed, we can compute the curvature
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}R^{\text{H}}
using the explicit formulas in (
C.6
). The only non-zero component of
R^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{m}{}_{n}
is
R^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\nu}}
. The full Hull curvature
R^{\text{H}}
differs by
R^{{\text{\tiny T}}}
by errors of
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
. Hence

-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha m}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta}{}^{m}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha\mu}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\bar{\delta}}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta\bar{\nu}}{}^{\bar{\delta}}{}_{\sigma}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}{}^{\bar{\delta}}{}_{\sigma}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\beta\mu}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\bar{\delta}}~.

(5.23)

From here we can use

R^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\bar{\beta}}\;=\;\!-(\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\bar{\beta}}~,\quad R^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\beta\bar{\nu}}{}^{\bar{\delta}}{}_{\sigma}\;=\;\nabla^{\text{\tiny CH}}_{\beta}H_{\bar{\nu}}{}^{\bar{\delta}}{}_{\sigma}~,

to reduce (
5.23
) to
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})
. Thus

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\beta}+2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha pq}H_{\beta}{}^{pq}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace F_{\alpha m}F_{\beta}{}^{m}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}_{\alpha m}R^{\text{H}}_{\beta}{}^{m}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

This is not quite the Einstein equation due to the difference between
\Phi
and
\hat{\Phi}
. However, if
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
, then by (
5.17
) we have
\partial\Phi\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\partial\hat{\Phi}
and the Einstein equation holds on double holomorphic indices.

5.5.2
Mixed directions

Next, we return to (
5.21
) and consider the case when
m,n
are mixed barred/unbarred indices. As before, the starting point is the general formula for the Ricci curvature in complex geometry, which relates the Ricci curvature in terms of the first Chern class representative
\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\rm Ch}
:

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle\;=\;R^{\rm Ch}{}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}(dT)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}^{\rm Ch}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\bar{\beta}}^{\rm Ch}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}

(5.24)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}+\frac{1}{4}T_{\alpha\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}T^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{}_{\beta}T_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}}{}_{\beta}T_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\bar{\mu}}~.

(5.25)

This holds on any complex manifold and can be calculated directly from the conventions stated in the appendix. We can then rewrite (
5.24
) in our particular setup, where we substitute
R^{\rm Ch}{}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\log|\Omega|^{2}_{\omega}
,
\partial\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}=-2\partial\hat{\Phi}
and
T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{i}
using (
5.9
).

\begin{split}{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}&\;=\;\!-2\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(dT)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}+\frac{1}{4}T_{\alpha\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}T^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}+T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{}_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}_{\mu}-T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}}{}_{\beta}\hat{\Phi}_{\bar{\mu}}~.\end{split}

(5.26)

The Hessian of the Levi-Civita connection in holomorphic coordinates is

\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}\;=\;\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}_{\bar{\mu}}~,

and so using equation (
5.4
) for
i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega
gives

{\rm Ric}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}+2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\bar{\beta}}\Phi+\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha mn}H_{\bar{\beta}}{}^{mn}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}(\text{tr}\thinspace F\wedge F-\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{\tiny CH}}\wedge R^{\text{\tiny CH}})_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\;=\;0~.

(5.27)

We can now apply (
5.10
) to the last term. Substituting (
5.10
) into (
5.27
) gives

\displaystyle\ {\rm Ric}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}+2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\Phi}+\frac{1}{4}H_{\alpha mn}H_{\bar{\beta}}{}^{mn}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\bigg[F_{\alpha\bar{\mu}}F_{\bar{\beta}}{}^{\bar{\mu}}-R^{\text{H}}{}_{\alpha\bar{\mu}}R^{\text{H}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}{}^{\bar{\mu}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}(\Lambda_{\omega}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)^{\lambda}{}_{\gamma}R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{}^{\gamma}{}_{\lambda}~.

(5.28)

We are left with two differences compared to the Einstein equation: there is a
\hat{\Phi}
rather than
\Phi
, and there is a troublesome term on the right-hand side of (
5.28
). At linear in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, these terms are negligeable and the equations of motion are satisfied.

The subtlety arises at order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. To remove these term, we will pullback by a certain family of diffeomorphisms along the flow of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
to arrange that
\Phi
is a constant plus quadratic terms in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. This will be done later in Section
5.7
. For now, we conclude that if
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
then (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) imply

{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Lambda\partial\bar{\partial}\omega\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~,\quad\hat{\Phi}-\Phi\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.29)

With this, equation (
5.28
) becomes the Einstein equation to order
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
.

5.6
Dilaton equation

Finally, we must derive the dilaton equation

R-4(\nabla\Phi)^{2}+4\nabla^{2}\Phi-\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{\;=\;}0~.

(5.30)

Taking the trace of the Einstein equation (
5.21
) does not immediately produce the dilaton equation. So instead we start by deriving an identity for
\nabla^{2}\Phi
by using special identities from conformally balanced metrics on complex manifolds.

We start with the definition of the Laplacian in holomorphic coordinates:

\nabla^{2}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\;=\;g^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\log|\Omega|^{2}_{\omega}-T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}+T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}~.

On a general conformally balanced geometry
(X,\omega,\Omega)
satisfying
d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})=0
, we can apply (
C.9
) and (
C.11
), hence there holds

\nabla^{2}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\;=\;\!-(\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha\mu}{}_{\mu}+T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}\alpha}-T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}+T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}~.

In our setup, the
\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}
is related to the dilaton via
\partial_{i}\log\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}=-2\partial_{i}\hat{\Phi}
and our conventions are
|T|^{2}=\frac{1}{6}T_{mnp}T^{mnp}
.

\nabla^{2}\hat{\Phi}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha\mu}{}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}|T|^{2}+T_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\hat{\Phi}-T_{\alpha}{}^{\bar{\mu}\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}\hat{\Phi}~.

Since
T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}=-2\partial_{\lambda}\hat{\Phi}
, this becomes

\nabla^{2}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{1}{2}(\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha\mu}{}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}|T|^{2}-2|\nabla\hat{\Phi}|^{2}\;=\;0~.

We now substitute the equation (
5.4
) for
i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega
and (
5.13
), and replace
|T|^{2}
with
|H|^{2}
up to lower order terms.

\nabla^{2}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}\text{tr}\thinspace|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}-2|\nabla\hat{\Phi}|^{2}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.31)

Here we used

{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\text{tr}\thinspace(g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu\bar{\nu}})^{2}\;=\;\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})~,

which follows from (
5.14
). We can now combine identity (
5.31
) with the trace of the Einstein equation to obtain the dilaton equation. The trace of the Einstein equation (
5.28
) is

R+2\nabla^{2}\hat{\Phi}-\frac{3}{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

(5.32)

Add twice equation (
5.31
) to equation (
5.32
) to obtain

R+4\nabla^{2}\hat{\Phi}-4|\nabla\hat{\Phi}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace|F|^{2}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}|R^{\text{H}}|^{2}\overset{\ {\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}}{=}0~.

We conclude from (
5.29
) that if
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
, then
\hat{\Phi}
can be replaced by
\Phi
and the dilaton equation is satisfied to appropriate order.

5.7
Gauge fixing

To complete the proof of Proposition
3
, it remains to show that after pulling back by diffeomorphisms flowing along
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, we can assume that the dilaton function
\Phi
is a constant plus terms of order
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
. It is automatic that
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
, and the task is to upgrade this by gauge fixing to
\Phi={\rm const}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
. This procedure is well-known in the literature: this particular choice of gauge was used by Witten-Witten
[
37
]
and further refined by Anguelova-Quigley-Sethi
[
12
]
(see
[
48
]
for a summary). To end this section, we give here a self-contained presentation of these known results.

Let
X
be a compact manifold. Suppose we have a smooth family

g_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\;=\;g^{(0)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}g^{(1)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}g^{(2)}+\dots~,\quad J_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\;=\;J^{(0)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}J^{(1)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}J^{(2)}+\dots~,

parametrized by
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\in[0,1)
. Let
\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}:X\rightarrow X
be a 1-parameter family of diffeomorphisms which is the flow of a vector field
v
, so that

\frac{{\text{d}}}{{\text{d}}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}\bigg|_{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}=0}\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\;=\;v~,\quad\varphi_{0}\;=\;id~.

The Lie derivative acting on a metric tensor
g^{(0)}
satisfies

\frac{{\text{d}}}{{\text{d}}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}\bigg|_{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}=0}(\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{*}g^{(0)})_{ij}\;=\;(L_{v}g^{(0)})_{ij}\;=\;\nabla_{i}v_{j}+\nabla_{j}v_{i}~,

with
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
g^{(0)}
. If
g
also varies with
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, the formula is

\frac{{\text{d}}}{{\text{d}}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}\bigg|_{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}=0}(\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{*}g_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,})_{ij}\;=\;L_{v}g^{(0)}+g^{(1)}~.

It follows that altering
g
by a moving reference frame leads to the expansion

\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{*}g_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\;=\;g^{(0)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}(g^{(1)}+L_{v}g^{(0)})+\dots~.

Suppose we also have a family of scalar functions
\Phi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}=\Phi^{(0)}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\Phi^{(1)}+\dots
which solve

\partial_{m}\Phi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}({\text{d}}^{c}\omega)_{ijm}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~,\quad\Phi^{(0)}\;=\;{\rm const}~.

We want to choose the vector field
v
so that
\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{*}\Phi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}=\Phi^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
. We can rewrite the dilaton equation as

\partial_{\rho}\Phi\;=\;g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}(\nabla_{\mu}g_{\rho\bar{\nu}}-\nabla_{\rho}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}})+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

These holomorphic coordinates move along the deformation, so we write the equation in terms of fixed real coordinates.

\partial_{k}\Phi\;=\;g^{ij}\nabla_{i}g_{kj}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\nabla_{k}g_{ij}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

If we expand
g
in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
and set
\partial\Phi^{(1)}
to zero, we obtain the condition

\nabla^{i}(g^{(1)})_{ik}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}(g^{(1)})_{i}{}^{i}\;=\;0~.

(5.33)

Witten-Witten
[
37
]
showed that there exists a vector field
v
such that replacing

g^{(1)}\mapsto g^{(1)}+L_{v}g^{(0)}~,

satisfies (
5.33
). Indeed, the equation for
v
is

\nabla^{i}\nabla_{i}v_{k}\;=\;\!-\nabla^{i}(g^{(1)})_{ik}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}(g^{(1)})_{i}{}^{i}~,

(5.34)

after using
[\nabla_{i},\nabla_{k}]v^{i}=0
by Ricci-flatness. Since
L=\nabla^{i}\nabla_{i}
is a self-adjoint elliptic operator acting on vector fields, by the theory of elliptic PDE on manifolds the equation is solvable if the right-hand side is
L^{2}
orthogonal to
{\rm ker~}L
.

Lv^{k}\;=\;\!-\nabla_{i}\bigg((g^{(1)})^{ik}+\frac{1}{2}g^{ki}(g^{(1)})_{r}{}^{r}\bigg)~.

(5.35)

Integration by parts shows that
{\rm ker~}L=\{w\in\Gamma(TX):\nabla w=0\}
. Pairing the right-hand side with
w\in{\rm ker~}L
and integrating by parts gives zero, hence the equation for
v
is solvable.

In summary,
\check{\Phi}=\varphi_{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{*}\Phi
satisfies

\partial\check{\Phi}\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

In fact,
[
12
]
showed that
v
can be taken to even satisfy

\partial\check{\Phi}\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})~,

but we will not need this stronger result in the present work.

6
Conclusions

6.1
Geometric flows

There is a version of Ricci flow adapted to heterotic string compactifications. The idea is as follows: in practice, one can construct various explicit examples of complex geometries
(X,\Omega,\omega)
and bundles
E\rightarrow X
solving

F^{0,2}\;=\;0~,\quad d\Omega\;=\;0~,\quad{\text{d}}(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})\;=\;0~.

(6.1)

There are in a sense intermediate supersymmetric configurations; the full supersymmetry equations (
5.3
), (
5.4
) are not satisfied, as we are missing

F\wedge\omega^{2}\;=\;0~,\quad\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}[\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{\tiny CH}}\wedge\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{\tiny CH}}-\text{tr}\thinspace F\wedge F]~.

(6.2)

The method then is to start with concrete examples solving the partial constraints (
6.1
) and deform them along a flow which seeks to reach an on-shell configuration solving the full equations of motion. The anomaly flow, introduced in
[
49
,
50
]
, is a type of Ricci flow which does this: it is a flow that remains in the class of complex geometries solving (
6.1
) and whose fixed-points solve (
6.2
). The flow equations are:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g(t)}\omega(t)^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega(t)-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8}(\text{tr}\thinspace R^{\text{\tiny CH}}\wedge R^{\text{\tiny CH}}-\text{tr}\thinspace F_{h(t)}\wedge F_{h(t)})~,

(6.3)

\displaystyle h^{-1}\partial_{t}h

\displaystyle\;=\;\!-\text{i}\Lambda_{\omega(t)}F_{h(t)}~,

(6.4)

where the pair
(\omega(t),h(t))
is flowing, and the holomorphic volume form
\Omega
on
X
and complex structure on
E
remains fixed. Here
h(t)
is a hermitian metric on the holomorphic bundle
E
with Chern curvature
F_{h}=\bar{\partial}(h^{-1}\partial h)
and its flow is given by the Donaldson heat flow
[
35
]
. The initial metric
\omega_{0}
solves
d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g(0)}\omega(0)^{2})=0
and the equations are such that
d(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g(t)}\omega(t)^{2})=0
at all times. As the flow is nonlinear, there is a requirement on the scale:
|{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}R_{g(t)}|\ll 1
must be satisfied for the flow to be mathematically well-defined
[
49
,
51
]
. Explicit examples of this flow can be tested on

\begin{split}g^{\sharp}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}&\;=\;\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\,g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}+\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}\star{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}^{(1,1)}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}A\star{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}A)}\Big)+\cdots\\[2.84544pt]
\end{split}

(B.14)

This metric governs the kinetic terms of
d=4
massless scalar fields. Since moduli can mix under diffeomorphisms, the gauge bundle’s contribution should match the form and sign of metric and complex structure moduli terms.

Appendix C
Hermitian geometry

C.1
Conformally balanced metrics

Let
X
be a complex manifold with
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}X=3
. The tangent bundle splits as

T_{\mathbb{C}}X\;=\;\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}\oplus\mathpzc T_{X}^{(0,1)}~,

and we will use Greek indices (e.g.
\mu,\nu
) to denote directions along
\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}
. We will sometimes use the notation

{\text{d}}^{c}\;=\;\!-\frac{\text{i}}{2}(\partial-\bar{\partial})~,\quad{\text{d}}{\text{d}}^{c}\;=\;\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}~.

Let
\omega=\text{i}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\,dx^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\in\Omega^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a hermitian metric, so that the components satisfy
\overline{g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}}=g_{\nu\bar{\mu}}
and
g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}
is a positive-definite local matrix. The components of a differential form in holomorphic coordinates are given by

\eta\;=\;\frac{1}{p!q!}\eta_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}\bar{\nu}_{1}\cdots\bar{\nu}_{q}}dx^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}\bar{\nu}_{1}\cdots\bar{\nu}_{q}},\quad\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(X)~.

Roman indices (e.g.
i,j
) are used full indices spanning all of
T_{\mathbb{C}}X
. Our conventions for norms of forms are:

|\eta|^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{k!}\eta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\eta^{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}},\quad\eta\in\Omega^{k}(X)~.

We use the notation
i\Lambda_{\omega}:\Omega^{p+1,q+1}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q}(X)
for metric contraction:

(i\Lambda_{\omega}\eta)_{K}\;=\;g^{\mu\bar{\nu}}\eta_{\mu\bar{\nu}K},\quad K=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}\bar{\nu}_{1}\cdots\bar{\nu}_{q}~.

In complex dimension 3, we note the following wedge product identity

\eta\wedge\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda_{\omega}\eta)\wedge\omega^{2},\quad\eta\in\Omega^{3}(X)~.

(C.1)

We now discuss the concept of a conformally balanced metric, which generalizes the Kähler condition on
\omega
. Let
f
be an arbitrary function and consider

\partial(e^{-2f}\omega^{2})\;=\;2e^{-2f}\bigg[\partial\omega\wedge\omega-\partial f\wedge\omega^{2}\bigg]~.

Substitute (
C.1
) with
\eta=\partial\omega
.

\partial(e^{-2f}\omega^{2})\;=\;2e^{-2f}\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}(i\Lambda_{\omega})(i\partial\omega)-\partial f\bigg]\wedge\omega^{2}~.

(C.2)

We see that

\bigg[d(e^{-2f}\omega^{2})=0\bigg]\ \Leftrightarrow\ \bigg[(\partial-\bar{\partial})f\;=\;\!-\frac{\text{i}}{2}\Lambda_{\omega}T\bigg]\ \Leftrightarrow\ \bigg[T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\;=\;\!-2\partial_{\lambda}f\bigg]~,

(C.3)

where we use the notation
T\in\Omega^{3}(X)
for

T\;=\;\text{i}(\partial-\bar{\partial})\omega~,\quad T_{\mu\nu\bar{\rho}}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\bar{\rho}}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\bar{\rho}}~.

Metrics solving any of the equivalent equations in (
C.3
) are said to be conformally balanced.

C.2
Connection Symbols

Given a hermitian manifold
(X,g)
, we can ask whether there is a optimal natural connection
\nabla
on
T_{\mathbb{C}}X
. Unless
g
is a Kähler metric, there is no agreed-upon optimal connection. We present here several connections which all play various roles in string theory and in the main text. Our conventions are as follows: a vector field
V\in\Gamma(T_{\mathbb{C}}X)
is written as

V\;=\;V^{k}\partial_{k}\;=\;V^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+V^{\bar{\mu}}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}~,

where
k
denotes
T_{\mathbb{C}}X
indices and
\mu
denotes
\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}
indices. Our notation for a connection
\nabla
acting on
V\in\Gamma(T_{\mathbb{C}}X)
is

\nabla_{i}(V^{k}\partial_{k})\;=\;(\nabla_{i}V^{k})\partial_{k}~,\quad\nabla_{i}V^{k}\;=\;\partial_{i}V^{k}+\Gamma_{i}{}^{k}{}_{j}V^{j}~,

where
\Gamma_{i}{}^{k}{}_{j}
will be specified by the connection under consideration. It what follows, we only specify
\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{k}{}_{j}
with the understanding that
\Gamma_{\bar{\mu}}{}^{\bar{k}}{}_{\bar{j}}=\overline{\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{k}{}_{j}}
.

Levi-Civita

The Levi–Civita connection
\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}
is the unique metric compatible connection with no torsion. Unfortunately,
\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}
does not preserve
\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}
(unless
g
is Kähler) and is not recommended for calculations in holomorphic coordinates.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}-\frac{1}{2}T_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}T_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{2}T_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~.\end{split}

(C.4)

Chern

The Chern connection is the unique connection which satisfies
\nabla_{k}g_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=0
and
\nabla_{\bar{\mu}}V^{\alpha}=\partial_{\bar{\mu}}V^{\alpha}
, making it well-suited for calculations in holomorphic coordinates.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(C.5)

Hull

A connection
\Gamma^{\text{\tiny H}}
appears in the heterotic action and Bianchi identity which we will refer to as the Hull connection. At linear order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, it is given by
\Gamma_{m}^{{\text{\tiny T}}}=\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny LC}}+\frac{1}{2}T_{m}
. This connection
\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny T}}}
does not preserve
\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}
and its connection symbols are:

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;T_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny T}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(C.6)

The full Hull connection is given by
\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny H}}=\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny LC}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}
, where
H=T+2{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3})
and
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}P=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
.

Bismut

It was noticed by Yano
[
67
]
that specifying the ansatz
\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}=\nabla^{\text{\tiny LC}}-\frac{1}{2}g^{-1}T
where
T\in\Omega^{3}(X)
is a priori an arbitrary 3-form, together with the requirement that
\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}
preserves
\mathpzc T_{X}^{(1,0)}
, uniquely determines the 3-form in the ansatz as
T=i(\partial-\bar{\partial})\omega
. See Proposition
1
for details. This connection in non-Kähler complex geometry is sometimes called the Bismut connection.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}-T_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~,\\
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;\!-T_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~.\end{split}

(C.7)

C.3
Curvature

From a connection
\nabla
on
T_{\mathbb{C}}X
, the curvature tensor
R\in\Omega^{2}({\rm End}\,T_{\mathbb{C}}X)
will be denoted

R_{pq}{}^{i}{}_{j}=\partial_{p}\Gamma_{q}{}^{i}{}_{j}+\Gamma_{p}{}^{i}{}_{\ell}\Gamma_{q}{}^{\ell}{}_{j}-(p\leftrightarrow q)~.

From here, one can compute the curvatures of all the connections listed above in holomorphic coordinates. The Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of the Levi-Civita connection are denoted

{\rm Ric}_{mn}\;=\;\!-R^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{mi}{}^{i}{}_{n}~,\quad R\;=\;R_{m}{}^{m}~.

The 4-form
\text{tr}\thinspace R\wedge R
for various connections will be used in the main text, which is

\text{tr}\thinspace R\wedge R\;=\;\frac{1}{4}R_{mn}{}^{i}{}_{j}R_{pq}{}^{j}{}_{i}\,{\text{d}}x^{mnpq}~.

The coordinate expressions for the Chern curvature are simplest, and will often be used as reference.

R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\bar{\nu}}(g^{\alpha\bar{\sigma}}\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\bar{\sigma}})~.

One can take four different traces of
R^{\text{\tiny CH}}
along the holomorphic directions to get four analogs of Ricci curvature. Two of these traces will play a role in the main text.

1.

The first trace is

R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\bar{\nu}}\log\det g_{\rho\bar{\sigma}}~.

(C.8)

Its significance is that it defines the first Chern class. If
X
admits a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
, then

R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}\;=\;\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\bar{\nu}}\log|\Omega|^{2}_{\omega}~.

(C.9)

Here
\Omega\overset{\rm loc}{=}f(x)dx^{123}
in local holomorphic coordinates
x^{\mu}
with
f(x)
a local non-vanishing holomorphic function, and
|\Omega|^{2}_{\omega}\overset{\rm loc}{=}|f|^{2}(\det g_{\mu\bar{\nu}})^{-1}
.

2.

The second trace that will appear in our analysis is

(i\Lambda_{\omega}R^{\text{\tiny CH}})^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\;=\;R^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}~.

This quantity vanishes if the Chern connection is an instanton. We can compare both traces via the exchange relation

R^{\rm Ch}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\;=\;R^{\rm Ch}{}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-(i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}^{\rm Ch}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}}-\nabla_{\bar{\beta}}^{\rm Ch}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}+T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}~.

(C.10)

We end with a lemma containing an identity which will be used in the main text.

Proposition 4
.

Let
X
be a complex threefold with hermitian metric
\omega
and holomorphic volume form
\Omega
. Suppose

{\rm d}(\left\|\Omega\right\|_{g}\omega^{2})\;=\;0~.

Then

R^{\rm Ch}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\;=\;\!-(\text{i}\partial\bar{\partial}\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}+T_{\alpha\mu\bar{\nu}}T^{\mu\bar{\nu}}{}_{\bar{\beta}}~.

(C.11)

Identity (
C.11
) follows from (
C.10
) upon substituting (
C.3
) and (
C.8
). Its interpretation is the deviation of the Chern connection of a conformally balanced metric from being an instanton.

Probability for synthesis of superheavy nuclei with Z=121
R. Zargini, S. A. Seyyedi

Abstract
In this study the empirical method [ 1 ] is used for calculating the evaporation residue (ER) cross section in the synthesis of superheavy nuclei (SHN). The superheavy nuclei examined in this work fall within the range Z = 112 − 118 Z=112-118 . The theoretical calculations show good agreement with the experimental data. Furthermore, this model, along with an investigation of the optimal incident energy (OIE), is used to calculate the ER cross section for a hypothetical heavy system with Z = 121 Z=121 . Five promising combinations are suggested for synthesis of SHN with Z = 121 Z=121 : (1) Ti 50 + Es 252 {{}^{50}}\mathrm{Ti}+{{}^{252}}\mathrm{Es} , with the maximum ER cross section σ 3 ​ n = 24.5 ​ fb \sigma_{3n}=24.5\penalty\ \mathrm{fb} , at the optimal incident energy, OIE = 230 ​ MeV \mathrm{OIE}=230\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV} ; (2) Ti 50 + Es 254 {{}^{50}}\mathrm{Ti}+{{}^{254}}\mathrm{Es} , with the maximum ER cross section σ 3 ​ n = 11.8 ​ fb \sigma_{3n}=11.8\penalty\ \mathrm{fb} , at the optimal incident energy, OIE = 229 ​ MeV \mathrm{OIE}=229\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV} ; (3) V 51 + Cf 251 {{}^{51}}\mathrm{V}+{{}^{251}}\mathrm{Cf} , with the maximum ER cross section σ 3 ​ n = 1.2 ​ fb \sigma_{3n}=1.2\penalty\ \mathrm{fb} , at the optimal incident energy, OIE = 238 ​ MeV \mathrm{OIE}=238\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV} ; (4) V 51 + Cf 249 {{}^{51}}\mathrm{V}+{{}^{249}}\mathrm{Cf} , with the maximum ER cross section σ 3 ​ n = 1.0 ​ fb \sigma_{3n}=1.0\penalty\ \mathrm{fb} , at the optimal incident energy, OIE = 238 ​ MeV \mathrm{OIE}=238\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV} ; and (5) Cr 54 + Bk 247 {{}^{54}}\mathrm{Cr}+{{}^{247}}\mathrm{Bk} , with the maximum ER cross section σ 2 ​ n = 0.9 ​ fb \sigma_{2n}=0.9\penalty\ \mathrm{fb} , at the optimal incident energy, OIE = 243 ​ MeV \mathrm{OIE}=243\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV} .

Model and theory

II.1
The capture cross section

The barrier penetration model developed by Wong has been widely used to describe the fusion reactions at the energies close to, or greater than that of, the barrier
[
49
]
. The capture cross section is expressed as the sum of the cross sections for each partial wave
l

[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{cap}}=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}}{2\mu E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}}\sum_{l=0}^{l_{max}}{(2l+1)}T_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}},l\right),

(1)

where
\mu
is the reduced mass of the interacting nuclei,
E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}
is the center of mass of the colliding systems, and
l
is the angular momentum.
T_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}},l\right)
denotes the penetration probability of the potential barrier for the
l\mathrm{th}
partial wave and calculated according to the Hill-Wheeler equation
[
56
]
:

\displaystyle T_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}},l\right)=\left\{1+\mathrm{exp}\left[\frac{2\pi\left(E_{l}-E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}\right)}{\hbar\omega_{l}}\right]\right\}^{-1}.

(2)

The Wong formula is used to calculate the capture cross section
[
7
,
49
]
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{cap}}=\frac{10R_{0}^{2}\hbar\omega_{0}}{2E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}}\ln(1+\exp{\left[\frac{2\pi\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}-E_{0}\right)}{\hbar\omega_{0}}\right]}).

(3)

In Eq. (
3
),
E_{0}
is the height of the total potential, and
\hbar\omega_{0}
shows the inverted harmonic oscillator potential and is given as:

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{0}=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{\mu}}\sqrt{\left|\frac{d^{2}V_{\mathrm{Total}}(r)}{dr^{2}}\right|_{R_{l}}},

(4)

where
V_{\mathrm{Total}}(r)
is the total interaction potential and should be precisely calculated.

II.2
Total interaction potential

The potential barrier characteristics, such as barrier height, position, curvature, and shape, are significant parameters that should be evaluated. Total potential is the function of two nuclei’s radius, distance, colliding angle, and deformation parameters. Total potential is equal to the summation of the coulomb long-range repulsive potential, nuclear short-range attractive potential, and centrifugal potential
[
46
]
:

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{Total}}=

\displaystyle{V_{C}\left(R.Z_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}\right)+V}_{N}\left(R,A_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle V_{l}(R,A_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}),

(5)

where
V_{C}\left(R.Z_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}\right)
is the Coulomb potential,
V_{N}\left(R,A_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}\right)
shows the nuclear potential, and
V_{l}(R,A_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i})
denotes the centrifugal potential. Considering the multipole deformations, the Coulomb potential is shown as
[
49
,

\displaystyle V_{C}\left(R.Z_{i},\beta_{\lambda i},\theta_{i}\right)=\frac{Z_{1}Z_{2}e^{2}}{r}+{3Z}_{1}Z_{2}e^{2}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\lambda,i=1,2}{\frac{R_{i}^{\lambda}\left(\alpha_{i}\right)}{\left(2\lambda+1\right)R^{\lambda+1}}Y_{\lambda}^{(0)}(\theta_{i})\left[\beta_{\lambda i}+\frac{4}{7}\beta_{\lambda i}^{2}Y_{\lambda}^{(0)}(\theta_{i})\right]}.

(6)

In Eq. (
6
),
Z_{1}Z_{2}
is the Coulomb factor and is related to the projectile and target,
r
is the center of two nuclei distance,
R_{i}^{\lambda}\left(\alpha_{i}\right)
is the deformed radii of the projectile and target,

represents multipole deformations of the projectile and target,

is the angle between the nuclear symmetry axis and the collision
Z

\alpha_{i}
is the angle between the symmetry axis and the radius vector of the colliding nucleus
[
57

58

is measured in the clockwise direction from the symmetry axis while

is measured in the counterclockwise direction, as shown in Fig.

II.3
The fusion cross section

The fusion cross section is described as
[
2
]
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{fus}}=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}}{2\mu E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}}\sum_{l=0}^{l_{max}}{(2l+1)}T_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}},l\right)P_{CN}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast},l\right),

(17)

where
P_{CN}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast},l\right)
is the fusion probability, which is calculated as follows
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle P_{CN}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast},l\right)=\frac{P_{CN}^{0}}{1+\mathrm{exp}\left[\frac{E_{B}^{\ast}-E_{CN}^{\ast}}{\Delta}\right]},

(18)

\displaystyle P_{CN}^{0}=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{exp}\left[\frac{Z_{1}Z_{2}-\zeta}{\tau}\right]}.

(19)

In Eqs. (
18
) and (
19
),
E_{CN}^{\ast}
is the CN excitation energy,
E_{B}^{\ast}
is the energy of the CN when the energy of the center-of-mass is equal to the Bass barrier,
\Delta
is an adjustable parameter and is often set around
4\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV}
, and
\zeta\penalty\ \approx 1760
and
\tau\penalty\ \approx 45
are the fitted parameters
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle E_{CN}^{\ast}=E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}+Q_{\mathrm{val}}\ \ .

(20)

In Eq. (
20
),
E_{CN}^{\ast}
is the CN excitation energy,
E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}
denotes the system center-of-mass energy, and
Q_{\mathrm{val}}
shows heat of reaction and is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{val}}=\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A,Z\right)-\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A_{1},Z_{1}\right)-\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A_{2},Z_{2}\right),

(21)

where
\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A,Z\right)
,
\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A_{1},Z_{1}\right)
, and
\mathrm{\Delta M}\left(A_{2},Z_{2}\right)

et al

II.4
The evaporation residue cross section

Once the excited CN is formed, the equilibrium condition is achieved via the evaporation of one or more neutrons before decay. Therefore, one should consider the competition between neutron emission and decay to fission fragments in the CN. In hot fusion reactions, the formed CN has an excitation energy of
E_{B}^{\ast}>15\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV}
, where the equilibrium condition is reached by the evaporation of two, three, or four neutrons involved
[
2
,
60
]
. The evaporation residue cross section is given by.

\displaystyle\sigma_{ER}^{xn}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}}{2\mu E_{\mathrm{c.m.}}}\sum_{l=0}^{l_{max}}{\left(2l+1\right)T_{l}\left(E_{\mathrm{c.m.}},l\right)}

(22)

\displaystyle\times P_{CN}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast},l\right)W_{\mathrm{sur}}^{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast},l),

where
W_{\mathrm{sur}}^{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast},l)
denotes the survival probability of CN. Because of the high Coulomb barrier for the emission of charged particles from the excited heavy nucleus, the widths for the emission of a proton or an
\alpha
particle are much smaller than the neutron emission width
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}
. Under these circumstances,
{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{t}\approx\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{n}+\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}
was set, and the survival probability
W_{\mathrm{sur}}^{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast},l)
reflects the competition between neutron evaporation and fission of the excited CN. The survival probability is given by
[
13
]

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{sur}}^{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast},l)=P_{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast})\prod_{i=1}^{i_{max}=x}{(\frac{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}}{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}+\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}})}_{i,E_{CN}^{\ast}}.

(23)

In Eq. (
23
)
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}
and
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}
are partial neutron emission width and partial fission width, respectively. At
E_{CN}^{\ast}>10\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV}
, the
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}
is much smaller than the
\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}
. Therefore, the survival probability
W_{\mathrm{sur}}^{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast},l)
is mainly determined by the realization probability
P_{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast})
and the ratio
\frac{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}}{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}}

[
5
,
61
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{n}}{\mathrm{\Gamma}_{f}}=\frac{4A^{\frac{2}{3}}a_{f}\left(E_{CN}^{*}-B_{n}\right)}{K_{0}a_{n}[2a_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\left(E_{CN}^{\ast}-B_{f}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)]}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{exp}\left[2a_{n}^{1/2}\left(E_{CN}^{*}-B_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-2a_{f}^{1/2}\left(E_{CN}^{*}-B_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

(24)

In Eq. (
24
),
B_{n}
denotes the neutron binding energy,
B_{f}
is the fission barrier,
K_{0}
is set to a constant value of
10\penalty\ \mathrm{MeV}
, and
a_{n}=A/10
and
a_{f}=1.1a_{n}
are the level density parameters of the fissioning nucleus at the ground state and saddle configurations, respectively
[
61
]
. The fission barrier is given by
[
3
]

\displaystyle B_{f}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast}\right)=B_{f}^{LD}+S\mathrm{exp}{\left(\frac{-E_{CN}^{\ast}}{E_{D}}\right)}.

(25)

In Eq. (
25
),
B_{f}^{LD}
is the liquid drop (LD) fission barrier (macroscopic) and
S
is the shell correction term . The liquid drop fission barrier is very low or equal to zero for heavy elements with
Z\geq 109

[
2
,
13
]
. The shell damping energy is given by
[
62
]
.

\displaystyle E_{D}=\frac{5.48A^{\frac{1}{3}}}{1+1.3A^{-\frac{1}{3}}}.

(26)

To calculate
P_{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast})
, in Eq. (
23
) an equation which Jackson developed
[
63
]
was employed.
P_{xn}(E_{CN}^{\ast})
represents the probability of emitting an exact number of neutrons,
x
, from the CN and is given by.

\displaystyle P_{xn}\left(E_{CN}^{\ast}\right)=I\left(\mathrm{\Delta}_{x},2x-3\right)-\ I(\mathrm{\Delta}_{x+1},2x-1),

(27)

where
I\left(z,n\right)
is the Pearson’s incomplete
\mathrm{\gamma}
function, and is obtained by

\displaystyle I\left(z,n\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n!}\right)\int_{0}^{z}m^{n}e^{-m}dm.

(28)

In Eq. (
27
),
\mathrm{\Delta}_{x}=\frac{\left(E_{CN}^{\ast}-\sum_{1}^{x}{B_{n}(i)}\right)}{T}
,
B_{n}(i)
is the binding energy of the
i\mathrm{th}
evaporated neutron, and
T
is the compound nucleus temperature and is described as:

\displaystyle E_{CN}^{\ast}=\frac{1}{a}{AT}^{2}-T,

(29)

where,
a
represents the level density of CN and
A
is the mass number of the CN
[
61
]
.
It should be noted that Eq. (
27
) is valid for the calculation of the evaporation probability of two neutrons and more.

Summary

Z=112-118

Z=121

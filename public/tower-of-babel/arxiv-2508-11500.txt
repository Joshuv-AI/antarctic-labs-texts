Differential Contracting Homotopy in the Linearized 3d Higher-Spin Theory
M.A. Vasiliev

Abstract
In this paper, the recently developed differential homotopy approach is applied to the problem of disentangling dynamical and topological fields of the 3 ​ d 3d higher-spin gauge theory at the linear level. This formalism allows us to reproduce all known disentangling solutions in a unified form, including both the solutions obtained previously within the shifted homotopy approach in [ 1 ] and that derived by hand in [ 2 ] , as well as other solutions including those associated with the cohomology of the background covariant derivative D 0 D_{0} . Also, within the differential homotopy framework an alternative way of derivation of disentangled equations through a non-conventional solution for the field S 1 S_{1} is suggested. The obtained results are important for further analysis of nonlinear corrections to HS equations in A ​ d ​ S 3 AdS_{3} .

Introduction

Nonlinear higher-spin (HS) equations in
AdS_{3}
can be formulated using the system of
[
3
]
formulated in terms of generating functions for the HS one-form gauge fields
\omega
and zero-form matter fields
C
. Following
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
the nonlinear field equations can be put into the unfolded form of a perturbative expansion in powers of
C
,

{{\rm d}}_{x}\omega+\omega\ast\omega=\Upsilon(\omega,\omega,C)+\Upsilon(\omega,\omega,C,C)+\ldots,

(1.1)

{{\rm d}}_{x}C+\omega\ast C-C\ast\omega=\Upsilon(\omega,C,C)+\Upsilon(\omega,C,C,C)+\ldots\,,

(1.2)

where
{{\rm d}}_{x}:=dx^{n}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}
is the space-time de Rham derivative. In this paper all products are exterior with the wedge symbol implicit.

As is well known,
3d
massless HS gauge fields
\omega
do not propagate
[
5
]
. However, the theory involves propagating
dynamical
matter fields in the sector of zero-forms
C
. The latter also contain
topological
fields, that do not propagate, belonging to finite-dimensional modules of the isometry algebra. Topological fields appear on the
r.h.s.
’s of (
1.1
), (
1.2
), and the two types of fields may source each other. This phenomenon is called
entanglement
.
For the analysis of the theory it is desirable to disentangle the equations for topological and dynamical fields. This problem was originally solved at the linearized level in
[
2
]
by an appropriate field redefinition. Later on, using the
shifted homotopy
method
[
6
]
, a two-parameter family of disentangling solutions to the linearized equations was found
[
1
]
. However, this family does not describe all possible disentangling solutions. In particular, it does not reproduce the solution of
[
2
]
.

In
[
7
]
, a new
differential homotopy
method was proposed with the parameters of the shifted homotopy approach
treated as integration variables over a polyhedron in a multidimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
This approach is more general yielding solutions in the form of certain
Fundamental Ansatz
, which, in particular, trivializes Schouten identities, a major technical challenge in the shifted homotopy framework.
The aim of this paper is to apply the differential homotopy approach to the entanglement problem at the linear level of
3d
HS theory. This formalism allows us to reproduce all known disentangling solutions in a unified form of the fundamental Ansatz and derive the general disentangling solution unifying those of
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The remainder of Section
1
provides a general introduction into
3d
HS theory including the current state of the entanglement problem. Section
2
sketches the differential homotopy technique. Section
3
presents a general scheme of solving linearized equations via differential homotopies. Section
4
shows the construction of a complete family of disentangling corrections to the equation for one-forms. Section
5
demonstrates an alternative construction of disentangling solutions by choosing a non-standard one-form
S_{1}
.
Conclusions and future perspectives are discussed in
Section
6
.

1.1
Higher spins in
AdS_{3}

In Cartan gravity, the geometry of
AdS_{n}
can be described in terms of an

o(n-1,2)
flat connection
W_{gr}
,

{{\rm d}}_{x}W_{gr}+W_{gr}W_{gr}=0\,.

(1.3)

The
AdS_{3}
connection with the symmetry algebra
o(2,2)\sim sp(2)\oplus sp(2)
and local coordinates
x^{\nu}
(
\nu=0,1,2
) decomposes into spin-connection
\omega_{0}^{\alpha\beta}
and dreibein
h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}
,

\omega_{0}^{\alpha\beta}(x)=\omega_{0\nu}^{\alpha\beta}(x)dx^{\nu},\quad h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}(x)=h_{0\nu}^{\alpha\beta}(x)dx^{\nu}\,,

(1.4)

with
sp(2)
spinor indices
\alpha,\beta=1,2
raised and lowered by the symplectic form
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{\beta}=A^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta},\quad A^{\alpha}=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}A_{\beta},\quad\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon_{\beta\alpha},\quad\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}=\delta_{\beta}^{\gamma}.\end{array}

(1.5)

The
AdS_{3}
geometry is characterized by zero-curvature and zero-torsion conditions,

{{\rm d}}_{x}\omega_{0}^{\alpha\beta}+\omega_{0}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}\omega_{0}^{\beta\gamma}+\lambda^{2}h_{0}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}h_{0}^{\beta\gamma}=0,

(1.6)

{{\rm d}}_{x}h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}+\omega_{0}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}h_{0}^{\beta\gamma}+h_{0}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}\omega_{0}^{\beta\gamma}=0\,,

(1.7)

where
\lambda^{2}
represents the cosmological constant.

It is convenient to introduce spinor variables
y_{\alpha}
, that commute under the usual juxtaposition product but satisfy the Heisenberg commutation relation
[y_{\alpha},y_{\beta}]_{*}=2i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
with respect to the star product

f(y)*g(y)=\int dudv\,f(y+u)g(y+v)e^{iu_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}}\,.

(1.8)

The background
AdS_{3}
connection can be represented as

W_{0}(y)=\frac{1}{4i}\omega_{0}^{\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}+\frac{\lambda}{4i}\psi_{1}h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}=\omega_{0}(y)+\lambda\psi_{1}h_{0}(y),

(1.9)

satisfying the equation

{{\rm d}}_{x}W_{0}+W_{0}*W_{0}=0\,,

which is equivalent to (
1.6
)-(
1.7
). Here we introduce the Clifford elements
\psi_{i}
,
\{\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}
, whose role will be clarified below.

The unfolded form of free field equations
[
3
,
8
]
can be formulated in terms of the
generating function

C(y;\psi;k|x)=\sum_{A,B,C=0}^{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\lambda^{-\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}C_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}^{ABC}(x)k^{A}\psi_{1}^{B}\psi_{2}^{C}y^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots y^{\alpha_{n}}\,.

(1.10)

Introducing the covariant derivative in
AdS_{3}
as

D_{0}P={{\rm d}}_{x}P+W_{0}*P-(-1)^{p}P*W_{0}

(1.11)

for a degree-
p
differential form
P
, the matter field equation takes the form of a covariant constancy condition

D_{0}C=0.

(1.12)

In the expansion of this field in powers of
\psi_{2}
,

C\left(y;\psi;k\right)=C^{\mathrm{top}}\left(y;\psi_{1};k\right)+C^{\mathrm{dyn}}\left(y;\psi_{1};k\right)\psi_{2},

(1.13)

the first and second terms are called topological and dynamical, respectively.
The origin of this terminology is as follows. Introducing a Lorentz-covariant derivative as

D_{L}C:={{\rm d}}_{x}C+[\omega_{0},C]_{*}\,,

(1.14)

equation (
1.12
) splits into two independent subsystems

D_{L}C^{\mathrm{top}}=-\lambda\psi_{1}\left[h_{0},C^{\mathrm{top}}\right]_{*},\quad D_{L}C^{\mathrm{dyn}}=-\lambda\psi_{1}\left\{h_{0},C^{\mathrm{dyn}}\right\}_{*}\,.

(1.15)

Using the decomposition (
1.10
), one obtains

D_{L}C_{\alpha(n)}^{\mathrm{top}}=4i\lambda nh_{0\,\alpha}{}^{\beta}C_{\beta\alpha(n-1)}^{\mathrm{top}},\quad D_{L}C_{\alpha(n)}^{\mathrm{dyn}}=\frac{\psi_{1}}{2i}\left[h_{0}^{\gamma\delta}C^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{\gamma\delta\alpha(n)}-\lambda^{2}n(n-1)\,h_{0\,\alpha\alpha}C^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{\alpha(n-2)}\right].

(1.16)

Here, the left equation has a finite number of degrees of freedom for each
n
, whereas the right equation decomposes into two infinite chains of equations with an unbounded (odd or even) numbers of indices. The
r.h.s.
of
D_{L}C_{\alpha(n)}^{\mathrm{dyn}}
corresponds to the free field equations in
AdS_{3}
, which reduce to the Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations at
n=2
and
n=1
, respectively,

D_{L}^{\mu}D_{L\mu}C^{\mathrm{dyn}}=\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}C^{\mathrm{dyn}},\quad{h_{0}}^{\nu}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}D_{L\nu}C^{\mathrm{dyn}}_{\beta}=0.

(1.17)

In
AdS_{3}
HS theory, there are only two dynamical fields with spins
0
and
\frac{1}{2}
. The other components of
C_{\alpha(n)}^{\mathrm{dyn}}
are expressed by (
1.16
) via their higher space-time derivatives. Topological fields, as emphasized in
[
9
]
, can be interpreted as the coupling constants in the HS theory. The elements
\psi_{i}
allow one to write both dynamical and topological equations in a unified form, as in (
1.12
). However this leads to the entanglement problem discussed in Section
1.3
.

1.2
Higher-spin nonlinear equations

The nonlinear generating system for HS equations of
[
3
]
can be equivalently formulated as

{{\rm d}}_{x}W+W*W=0\,,

(1.18)

{{\rm d}}_{x}B+[W,B]_{*}=0\,,

(1.19)

{{\rm d}}_{x}S+\{W,S\}_{*}=0\,,

(1.20)

S*S=i\left(\theta^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}+B*\gamma\right)\,,

(1.21)

[S,B]_{*}=0\,.

(1.22)

Apart from space-time coordinates
x^{n}
with the de Rham derivative
{{\rm d}}_{x}:=dx^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}
, the master fields
W(z;y;\psi;k|x)
,
B(z;y;\psi;k|x)
, and
S(z;\theta;y;\psi;k|x)
depend on spinor variables
z_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2
), involutive operators
\psi_{i}
, and the Klein operator
k
.

There are two types of anticommuting differentials in (
1.18
)–(
1.22
), namely space-time
dx^{n}
and spinor
\theta^{\alpha}:=dz^{\alpha}
. Master fields have different gradings with respect to these differentials:
W=W_{n}dx^{n}
and
S=S_{\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}
are one-forms in
dx^{n}
and
\theta^{\alpha}
, respectively, while
B
is a zero-form.

The (anti)commutation relations are

\begin{gathered}{[y_{\alpha},y_{\beta}]=[z_{\alpha},z_{\beta}]=[z_{\alpha},y_{\beta}]=0},\\
\{dx_{m},dx_{n}\}=\{\theta_{\alpha},\theta_{\beta}\}=\{\theta_{\alpha},dx_{n}\}=0,\\
\{k,y_{\alpha}\}=\{k,z_{\alpha}\}=\{k,\theta_{\alpha}\}=0,\quad k^{2}=1,\\
{[\psi_{i},y_{\alpha}]=[\psi_{i},z_{\alpha}]=[\psi_{i},dx_{n}]=[\psi_{i},\theta_{\alpha}]=[\psi_{i},k]=0,\quad\{\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\}=2\delta_{ij}}.\end{gathered}

(1.23)

The extended star product is

f(z,y)*g(z,y):=\int dudvf(z+u,y+u)g(z-v,y+v)e^{iu_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}}\,.

(1.24)

Due to the Schouten identity
\theta^{3}=0
, the operator
\gamma
in (
1.21
) turns out to be central,

\gamma=\theta^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}\exp\left(iz_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}\right)k\,,\quad\gamma*f=f*\gamma,\quad\forall f=f(z;y;k;\psi;\theta)\,.

(1.25)

To see this, one has to take into account that
\exp\left(iz_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}\right)
plays the role of the inner Klein operator,

e^{iz_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}}*f(z,y)=f(-z,-y)*e^{iz_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}}\,.

(1.26)

Following
[
2
]
, to analyze equations (
1.18
)-(
1.19
) perturbatively, one starts with the vacuum solution

B_{0}=0,\quad S_{0}=\theta^{\alpha}z_{\alpha},\quad W_{0}=\frac{1}{4i}(\omega_{0}+\lambda\psi_{1}h_{0})^{\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}y_{\beta},

(1.27)

where
W_{0}
is the background
AdS_{3}
connection (
1.9
). The star-commutator with
S_{0}
acts as
z_{\alpha}
exterior derivative

[S_{0},f]_{*}=-2i{{\rm d}}_{z}f\,,\qquad{{\rm d}}_{z}:=\theta^{\alpha}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\,.

(1.28)

In the next order, from
\left[S_{0},B_{1}\right]_{*}=0
it follows that
B_{1}=C(y;\psi;k\mid x)
with
C(y;\psi;k\mid x)
obeying (
1.12
) by virtue of (
1.19
).

1.3
Linear entanglement problem

Perturbative analysis of the nonlinear HS equations starts with solving equations (
1.20
), (
1.21
). As a consequence of (
1.28
) and consistency of the nonlinear system, these equations have the form

{{\rm d}}_{z}f(z;y;\theta)=J(z;y;\theta),\quad\text{where}\quad{{\rm d}}_{z}J(z;y;\theta)=0,\quad J(z;y;0)=0\,.

(1.29)

These can be solved in the form

f=\Delta_{0}J+{{\rm d}}_{z}\epsilon+h\,,

(1.30)

where
h
is in the
{{\rm d}}_{z}
-cohomology, and the conventional homotopy operator
\Delta_{0}
is defined by the Poincaré formula:

\Delta_{0}J(z;y;\theta):=z^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\,J(\tau z;y;\tau\theta)\,.

(1.31)

Generally, the exact part
{{\rm d}}_{z}\epsilon
and the cohomology
h
in (
1.30
) describe solutions to the homogeneous equation (
1.29
) with
J=0
. The freedom in
\epsilon
is gauge while
h(\omega,C)
with HS fields
\omega(y;k;\psi\mid x)
and
C(y;k;\psi\mid x)
induces field redefinitions.

Though looking most natural, the conventional homotopy with
\epsilon=h=0
may lead to undesirable consequences. For example, the conventional homotopy in the second order of the perturbative expansion of the
4d
theory leads to non-local results
[
10
,
11
]
. In the
3d
HS theory under consideration, it entangles dynamical and topological fields even at the linear level
[
2
]
.

Shifted homotopy operator results from the shift
z^{\alpha}\rightarrow z^{\alpha}+q^{\alpha}
with some
z
-independent
q^{\alpha}
. Shifted contracting homotopy
\Delta_{q}
and cohomology projector
h_{q}
act as follows
[
6
]

\Delta_{q}J(z;y;\theta):=\left(z^{\alpha}+q^{\alpha}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}J(\tau z-(1-\tau)q;y;\tau\theta),\quad h_{q}f(z;y;\theta)=f(-q;y;0)\,.

(1.32)

The form of the resulting equations at
\epsilon=h=0
depends on the choice of
\Delta_{q}
. Transition from one contracting homotopy to another affects both
\epsilon
and
h
. The problem is to identify a homotopy procedure that leads to disentangled field equations.

Using the star-exchange formulae of
[
1
]
,
the conventional solution for
S_{1}
can be represented in the
from
[
6
]

S_{1}(z;\theta;y;\psi;k|x)=-\frac{1}{2}C(y;\psi;k|x)*\Delta_{p}\gamma,

(1.33)

where
p_{\alpha}
denotes the derivative of
C(y)
. The derivative
of the one-forms
\omega(y)
is denoted as
t_{\alpha}
,

p_{\alpha}C(y)\equiv C(y)p_{\alpha}:=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}C(y),\quad t_{\alpha}\omega(y)\equiv\omega(y)t_{\alpha}:=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}\omega(y).

(1.34)

This leads to the following
{{\rm d}}_{z}
-differential equation on the linear part of
W(z;y;\psi_{1,2};k)
:

{{\rm d}}_{z}W_{1}=-\frac{1}{4i}\left[W_{0}*C*(\Delta_{p}-\Delta_{p+t})\gamma+C*W_{0}*(\Delta_{p+t}-\Delta_{p+2t})\gamma\right]:=J_{W}(y;z;\theta).

(1.35)

The conventional homotopy yields a solution of the form:

W_{1}(y;z;\psi;k)=\omega_{1}(y;\psi)+W_{1}^{0}(y;z;\psi;k),

(1.36)

where the
z
-independent field
\omega_{1}(y)
serves as the generating function for HS gauge fields of the pure Chern-Simons HS theory
[
5
]
, while the conventional solution
W_{1}^{0}
can be expressed as

W_{1}^{0}=\frac{1}{4i}\left[W_{0}*C*\Delta_{p+t}\Delta_{p}\gamma+C*W_{0}*\Delta_{p+2t}\Delta_{p+t}\gamma\right]\,.

(1.37)

The decomposition into topological and dynamical fields for the one-forms is opposite to that for the zero-forms (
1.13
):
\omega\left(y;\psi;k\right)=\omega^{\mathrm{dyn}}\left(y;\psi_{1};k\right)+\omega^{\mathrm{top}}\left(y;\psi_{1};k\right)\psi_{2}
.
Analogous complementary picture for one- and zero-forms takes place in the
4d
HS theory
[
12
]
.

Substituting the expression (
1.36
) into the HS equations (
1.18
) yields the linearized equations of the form

D_{0}\omega_{1}=-D_{0}W_{1}^{0}=\Delta R\,,

(1.38)

where
D_{0}
denotes the full covariant derivative (
1.11
). In the dynamical sector the
r.h.s.
of this equation is trivial

D_{0}\omega_{1}^{\text{dyn}}(y;\psi_{1})=0\,

(1.39)

in agreement with the fact that
3d
massless HS gauge fields do not propagate being of Chern-Simons type (unlike the
AdS_{4}
theory, where this step leads to non-zero
C
-dependent terms).

However, the equation for
\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}
acquires a

C^{\text{dyn}}
-dependent contribution,

D_{0}\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}(y;\psi_{1})=-\left.\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16i}h_{0}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}h_{0}{}^{\gamma\beta}(y_{\alpha}-p_{\alpha})(y_{\beta}-p_{\beta})C^{\text{dyn}}\left(\xi;\psi_{1}\right)\right|_{\xi=0}\neq 0\,.

(1.40)

This is potentially problematic since a solution to the differential equation
(
1.40
) on
\omega_{1}
may be nonlocal in
C^{\text{dyn}}
, that would induce
non-locality of the theory at higher orders.

In
[
2
]
, this problem was resolved by finding an appropriate by hand field redefinition

\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}\rightarrow\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}+\delta\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}\,,

\delta\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}(y)=-\frac{\lambda}{8i}\int_{0}^{1}ds\left(1-s^{2}\right)h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}(y_{\alpha}-p_{\alpha})(y_{\beta}-p_{\beta})C^{\text{dyn}}(sy)\psi_{1}\,,

(1.41)

that compensates the
r.h.s.
of (
1.40
).
Clearly, this is equivalent to adding a
{{\rm d}}_{z}
-closed term to the solution of (
1.35
)

W_{1}=\omega_{1}+W_{1}^{0}+\delta\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}\psi_{2}\,.

The goal of
[
1
]

was to find disentangling solutions by the shifted homotopy approach.

1.4
Advancements of the shifted homotopy approach

The shifted homotopy approach developed in
[
6
]
provides more freedom in the description of possible solutions to the entanglement problem. In
[
1
]
, a solution to (
1.35
) was sought in the form of a sum of the conventional one and a correction derived through the shifted homotopy procedure:

W_{1}(y;z;\psi;k)=\omega_{1}(y;\psi)+W_{1}^{0}(y;z;\psi;k)+\delta W_{1}^{\text{shift}}(y;\psi;k).

(1.42)

The correction
\delta W_{1}^{\text{shift}}
was governed by six homotopy parameters: associated with the shift in the variable
y_{\alpha}
and the the derivatives
p_{\alpha}
and
t_{\alpha}
(
1.34
) of the fields
C
and
W_{0}
, respectively. These were doubled since the
r.h.s.
of (
1.35
) contains two
{{\rm d}}_{z}
-closed terms associated with the different order of the one- and zero-forms. The requirement of triviality of the
r.h.s.
of the equation in the topological sector of
D_{0}\omega_{1}\equiv 0
leaves only two free parameters associated with the
y_{\alpha}
-shifts. As shown in
[
1
]
, the resulting disentangling shifted homotopy correction is

\displaystyle\delta W_{1}^{\text{shift}}=-\frac{\lambda}{4i}

\displaystyle\Big[h_{0}\psi_{1}*C^{\text{dyn}}\psi_{2}*h_{\alpha_{+}y}\Delta_{p+t}\Delta_{p}\gamma+C^{\text{dyn}}\psi_{2}*h_{0}\psi_{1}*h_{2p+2t+\alpha_{-}y}\Delta_{p+2t}\Delta_{p+t}\gamma\Big]=

(1.43)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda}{8i}\bigg[\mathrlap{\int_{0}^{1}}\quad ds

\displaystyle\cdot(1-s^{2})h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}\big(-y_{\alpha}p_{\beta}+p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}+\alpha_{+}(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})\big)\psi_{1}C^{\text{dyn}}(sy)\psi_{2}+

\displaystyle+\mathrlap{\int_{0}^{1}}\quad ds

\displaystyle\cdot(1-s^{2})h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}\big(+y_{\alpha}p_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}+\alpha_{-}(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})\big)\psi_{1}C^{\text{dyn}}(-sy)\psi_{2}\bigg]k\,.

Though describing a family of disentangling corrections with two free parameters
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathds{C}
this expression does not include the correction found in
[
2
]
. The difference between (
1.43
) and (
1.41
) consists of two
D_{0}
-exact terms, controlled by
\alpha_{\pm}
, and two
D_{0}
-cohomological ones independent of free parameters:

\begin{gathered}\delta W_{1}^{\text{shift}}-\delta\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}\psi_{2}=-\frac{\lambda}{8i}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,(1-s^{2})h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}\times\\
\begin{aligned} \times\Big[\big(\alpha_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\big)(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})C^{\text{dyn}}(sy)&+\big(\alpha_{-}+\frac{1}{2}\big)(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})C^{\text{dyn}}(-sy)-\\
-(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}+p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})C_{+}^{\text{dyn}}(sy)&+2y_{\alpha}p_{\beta}C_{-}^{\text{dyn}}(sy)\Big]\psi_{1}\psi_{2}k\,,\end{aligned}\end{gathered}

(1.44)

where
C_{+}^{\text{dyn}}(sy)
and
C_{-}^{\text{dyn}}(sy)
denote the even and odd parts of
C^{\text{dyn}}(sy)
, respectively.

In
[
1
]
it was shown that terms of the form
h_{0}^{\alpha\beta}(y_{\alpha}y_{\beta}-p_{\alpha}p_{\beta})
in (
1.44
)
are
D_{0}
-exact while the other two terms are in
D_{0}
-cohomology. Denoting them as
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\pm}
, respectively, we find that

\delta W_{1}^{\text{shift}}=\delta\omega_{1}^{\text{top}}\psi_{2}+(\alpha_{+}-\frac{1}{2})\mathcal{A}_{+}+(\alpha_{-}+\frac{1}{2})\mathcal{A}_{-}+\mathcal{G}_{+}+\mathcal{G}_{-}\,

(1.45)

cannot be reduced to the correction of
[
2
]
by adjusting free parameters.

The
D_{0}
-cohomological terms were shown in
[
1
]
to
correspond to the mass deformtion in
3d
HS theory
[
1
,
13
]
. However, it was unclear within the shifted homotopy approach how to reproduce the full variety of the cohomological terms, that lead to disentangled equations.

The aim of this paper is to apply the recently developed differential homotopy procedure
[
7
]
to the description of disentangling solutions at the linear level of 3d HS theory. This approach allows one to make homotopy parameter-dependent shifts, postponing the integration to the very last step of the evaluation of corrections. The differential homotopy approach of
[
7
]
leads to a new representation for the homotopy solution of nonlinear HS equations, that reduces its analysis to a certain polyhedra cohomology problem with respect to the homotopy parameters. This technique allows us to represent the
r.h.s.
of (
1.29
) in the manifestly exact form for which it was previously necessary to account for integration by parts and the Schouten identity.

In
[
7
]
(see also
[
14
]
), efficiency of the differential homotopy approach has been demonstrated by constructing projectively-compact spin-local solutions in the second order of the
4d
HS-theory. In this paper, it will be shown how it allows one to describe a set of disentangling solutions of the linearised
3d
HS theory, that contains all known solutions as well as some new.

2
Differential homotopy

2.1
Total differential and integration

In
[
7
]
, homotopy parameters
t^{a}
of the type (
1.32

were interpreted as coordinates of an extended manifold
M=\mathcal{M}_{z}\times\mathcal{M}
with the total differential

{{\rm d}}:={{\rm d}}_{z}+{{\rm d}}_{t},\quad{{\rm d}}_{z}:=\theta^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}},\quad{{\rm d}}_{t}:=dt^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{a}},

At each perturbation order the equations to be solved have form

The homotopy coordinates

(

. Functions like

Physical fields and equations are governed by the

-cohomology

,
\theta^{\alpha}
–independent integrals over
\mathcal{M}
.
The differential homotopy concept eliminates the integrals from the equations

(2.4)

where the degree of

in

, so it does not contribute to the integral. (For more detail see

In this framework, equations (

and

\int_{t^{n}}\int_{t^{m}}=-\int_{t^{m}}\int_{t^{n}}\quad,\quad\int_{t^{1}}\cdots\int_{t^{k}}\rightarrow\int_{t^{1}\ldots t^{k}}\,,

is some disentangling correction of the form (
3.10
), and the remaining terms are
D_{0}
-closed.

For nonconventional
S_{1}^{\mu_{1}}
with the measure (
5.1
) there is no need in disentangling correction, so the structure of disentangling solution
W_{1}^{\prime}
is

W_{1}^{\prime}=\omega_{1}+W_{1}^{\mu_{1}}+\mathcal{A}_{\pm}^{f^{\prime}_{\pm}}+\gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}\mathcal{G}_{\pm}\,.

(5.7)

Note that the equation (
3.6
) with nonconventional
S_{1}^{\mu_{1}}
can be written as

{{\rm d}}W_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}S_{1}^{\mu_{1}}=\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}S_{1}^{\mu_{0}}+\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}(\delta S_{1})=\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}S_{1}^{\mu_{0}}-\frac{1}{2i}{{\rm d}}\left(D_{0}\epsilon_{\nu}\right)\,,

(5.8)

where the exact term can be moved to the left, leading to the equation

{{\rm d}}\left(W_{1}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}\epsilon_{\nu}\right)=\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}S_{1}^{\mu_{0}}\,,

(5.9)

whose disentangling solution is (
5.6
). Thus, up to the terms
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}^{f_{\pm}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\pm}
, solutions with different choices of the measure in
S_{1}
can be related as

W_{1}^{\mu_{0}}+\delta W_{1}^{\nu}=W_{1}^{\mu_{1}}+\frac{1}{2i}D_{0}\epsilon_{\nu}\,,

(5.10)

where the measures
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{0}
and
\nu
are connected via (
5.3
).

6
Conclusion

In this paper, the recently developed differential homotopy approach

[
7
]
is applied to the derivation of the form of linearized equations of the HS theory in
AdS_{3}
. The advantage of this approach in which all expressions are represented as integrals over some polyhedra in a multidimensional space is that it allows one to avoid accounting for the Schouten identity.
The differential homotopy approach turns out to be both most general and simpler than the shifted homotopy approach of
[
15
]
.

The new method allowed us to obtain a general from of field equations with
disentangled sectors of dynamical and topological fields. This general solution includes both those obtained within the framework of shifted homotopy in
[
1
]
and that derived by hand in
[
2
]
, which was unreachable within the shifted homotopy technique. Also, within the differential homotopy approach, a more general form of
D_{0}
-exact corrections was found, as well as
D_{0}
-cohomological terms identified in
[
1
]
.

Also, within the differential homotopy framework an alternative way of derivation of the disentangled equations through non-conventional solutions for the field
S_{1}
is suggested.

The obtained results are important for further analysis of nonlinear
corrections to
3d
HS field equations and, in particular, of their locality
properties, that may be dependent of the choice of a particular disentangling
solution. It is anticipated that the broad choice of disentangling solutions of the linearized equations will allow us to choose most appropriate of them for the
next order analysis.
As a first step, it is planned to evaluate local current interactions in the
3d
HS theory.

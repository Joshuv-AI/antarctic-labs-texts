AbstractCondensation of β-Oxoanilide 1 with malononitrile afforded 1,8-naphthyridine derivative 5. The reaction οf 1 with each of cyanothioacetamide and ethyl cyanoacetate yields pyridine derivatives 7 and 10. Also, 1 reacted with each of arylidenecyanothioacetamide or a mixture of acetaledelyde/cyanothioacetamide to yield 5-acetylnicotinonitrile derivatives 15 and 17 respectively. Introduction

Six-membered heterocyclic aromatic molecules containing a nitrogen atom, as in the case of the pyridine nucleus, form one of the most valuable classes of molecules due to their widely differing applicability. They have been proven to be pivotal in the therapeutic sector in the form of drugs1–4, in agriculture in active roles in pesticides and herbicides5, and in industrial practice. Despite these reported uses, there has been recent excitement over new uses of pyridine derivatives as potential, eco-friendly corrosion inhibitors6,7. These have been proved to be effective in numerous systems, such as aqueous systems8, copper in nitric acid solution9, mild steel in hydrochloric acid during oil well acidizing10, carbon steel in hydrochloric acid11,12and in sulfuric acid mediums13, steel reinforcement in chloride-containing concrete14, and P110 steel in oil formation water15. Such versatility highlights their potential to protect metals under harsh and technologically related situations.

In parallel to experimental studies, computation and data-driven approaches are also being employed to estimate and predict the efficiency of emerging pyridine-containing inhibitors. As one recent illustration, quantum machine learning from quantum circuit variational techniques has been applied to approximate inhibition efficiency in systems of pyridine–quinoline16, while density functional theory (DFT) has provided valuable details regarding their electronic structure and corrosion protection roles17–20. These developments underscore the growing importance of state-of-the-art quantum chemical as well as machine learning techniques in guiding rational design of high-performance inhibitors.

Another main class of heterocycles containing nitrogen, the pyrimidines, also largely also becomes important. These have vast roles to play in pharmaceuticals21–25and in industrial applications26,27, and several of their derivatives have been reported to be very excellent corrosion inhibitors. Their protection has been reported for mild steel in hydrochloric acid28, tubular steel in high-temperature drilling environment of petroleum in formation water29, mild steel in sulfuric acid30, aluminum in hydrochloric acid31, copper in nitric acid9,32, and C1018 carbon steel in aqueous hydrochloric acid33. More recently, machine learning techniques have been utilized to investigate pyrimidine-containing inhibitors, and this is an extremely powerful tool in terms of facilitating new candidate discovery at lower economic cost and time compared to purely experimental schemes34–36.

As reported in our earlier research, one can apply the derivative of β-oxoanilide 1 as an versatile substrate for synthesizing N-heterocycles37–40. Consequently, this paper aims at synthesizing new derivatives of pyridine and pyrimidine in terms of β-oxoanilide 1 and studies on corrosion inhibition property. Experimental procedures remain to this day necessary to confirm inhibitor efficiency, but they can prove laborious and lengthy. The advances in computational chemistry have provided complementary information, which is also fast and very illuminating. Methods based on quantum chemistry and molecular modeling have been utilized extensively to relate inhibition efficiency and molecular descriptors41–43. Recent refinements in computational hardware and algorithm have increased DFT and related techniques still further to being widely utilized in corrosion inhibition studies44–46.

Hence, this study employs DFT calculations to investigate molecular structure, electronic properties, and reactivity of thus-prepared pyridine and pyrimidine derivatives. The most relevant quantum chemical descriptors like frontier orbital energies (EHOMO, ELUMO), energy gaps (ΔE), total negative charge (TNC), chemical hardness (η), and global softness (σ) are evaluated to elucidate their inhibition modes. This study, in combining experimental synthesis and theoretical simulations, hopes to provide an in-depth knowledge of these heterocyclic systems’ corrosion inhibition properties and to define their potential as strong, environmentally benign metallic protectants.

Mechanism of action of inhibitors

The corrosion inhibition mechanism of inhibitors on metals in acids can be explained based on thermodynamic, electrochemical and quantum chemical studies. Water molecules adsorbed on the metal are replaced through a displacement reaction, and the inhibitors molecules are adsorbed on the metal surface.

Gravimetric measurements justified the physical/chemical adsorption inhibition mechanism. inhibitors molecules are protonated at the active sites (N, O) with maximum charge density, thereby adsorbing negative ions and generating charges in acidic media. The negative ions of the acid are adsorbed on the surface of the charged metal, generating negative charges, thereby generating electrostatic attraction between the negatively charged metal and the protonation inhibitor, forming physical adsorption.

Chemisorption occurs through the transfer of electrons from inhibitors molecules to metals to form coordination bonds. Electrochemical measurements show that the inhibitor has a mixed/cathode/anode type behavior toward the material in acid. Inhibitors prevent corrosion at the anode by reducing the release of hydrogen and prevent corrosion at the cathode by adsorbing directly on the metal.

1
Introduction and main results

1.1
Taut strings

First of all, let us recall the notion of taut string and the related background.
A function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is called
convex
, if

\varphi(\alpha\,x+(1-\alpha)\,y)\leq\alpha\,\varphi(x)+(1-\alpha)\,\varphi(y),\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1;x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(1)

A function
\varphi
is called
strictly
convex if the inequality in (
1
)
is strict for
0<\alpha<1
,
x\not=y
.

Let
\text{AC}[T_{1},T_{2}]
denote the class of all absolutely continuous functions on
[T_{1},T_{2}]
.
For a convex function
\varphi
, consider the integral functional

G^{\varphi}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}(h):=\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\varphi(h^{\prime}(t))\,dt,\qquad h\in\text{AC}[T_{1},T_{2}].

In general,
G^{\varphi}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}(h)
may be interpreted as a kind of ”energy” (or a measure of variability) of the function
h
.
The most interesting examples are
\varphi(u)=\sqrt{u^{2}+1}
and
\varphi(u)=\vartheta_{p}(u):=|u|^{p}
for
p\geq 1
. In these cases,
G^{\varphi}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
is the graph length of
h
, the total variation of
h
(for
p=1
) and ”kinetic energy” of
h
(for
p=2
), respectively.
Notice, by the way, that in these examples
\varphi
is strictly convex, except for
\vartheta_{1}(u)=|u|
.

Let us now introduce an optimization problem to deal with. Let
H^{(lower)}(\cdot),H^{(upper)}(\cdot)
be two continuous functions on
[T_{1},T_{2}]
such that

H^{(lower)}(t)<H^{(upper)}(t),\qquad T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2},

and let
\widetilde{h}_{1},\widetilde{h}_{2}
be the real numbers satisfying

H^{(lower)}(T_{i})\leq\widetilde{h}_{i}\leq H^{(upper)}(T_{i}),\qquad i=1,2.

Consider now an optimization problem

G^{\varphi}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}(h)\searrow\min

(2)

under the constraints
h\in\text{AC}[T_{1},T_{2}]
,
h(T_{1})=\widetilde{h}_{1},h(T_{2})=\widetilde{h}_{2}
, and

H^{(lower)}(t)\leq h(t)\leq H^{(upper)}(t),\qquad T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}.

This means that we are looking for a function spending the minimal amount of energy among all functions taking an initial value
\widetilde{h}_{1}
, a terminal value
\widetilde{h}_{2}
and running between the lower boundary
H^{(lower)}(\cdot)
and the upper boundary
H^{(upper)}(\cdot)
.

The remarkable facts well known in optimization theory are the following (see e.g.
[
8
, Appendix]
essentially based on

[
4
]
).

•

There exists a solution of (
2
) which is the same for all convex
\varphi
given
\widetilde{h}_{1}
,
\widetilde{h}_{2}
,
H^{(lower)}
,
H^{(upper)}
.
This universal solution is called ”taut string”.

•

If
\varphi
is
strictly
convex, then the taut string is the unique solution of (
2
).

Note that for a convex but non-strictly convex
\varphi
the solution of (
2
) need not be unique. In particular, for

\varphi(u)=|u|
a solution alternative to the taut string and called ”lazy function” may appear for certain initial and terminal conditions.
In short, the lazy function is a function that remains constant in time as long as this is allowed by the boundary conditions. Lazy functions
were studied in
[
10
]
–
[
12
]
.

1.2
Taut strings accompanying Wiener process

From now on, we will consider a special case of (
2
), where the upper and the lower boundaries follow the trajectories of a Wiener process
W
. Namely, for
T>0
,
r>0
we let in (
2
)

T_{1}:=0
,
T_{2}:=T
,
\widetilde{h}_{1}:=0
,
\widetilde{h}_{2}:=W_{T}
,
H^{(lower)}(t):=W_{t}-r/2
,
H^{(upper)}(t):=W_{t}+r/2
and write
G^{\varphi}_{T}
instead of
G^{\varphi}_{[0,T]}
.

The corresponding taut string is called
the taut string accompanying Wiener process
W
on the time interval
[0,T]
. We will denote it
\eta_{T,r}
.
Introducing the notation for uniform norms

\|g\|_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}:=\sup_{T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}}|g(t))|,\quad\|g\|_{T}:=\|g\|_{[0,T]},

we have, by the definition of taut string,

G^{\varphi}_{T}(\eta_{T,r})=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt=I^{0}_{W}(T,r,\varphi)

where

\displaystyle I^{0}_{W}(T,r,\varphi):=\min\{G^{\varphi}_{T}(h);h\in\text{AC}[0,T],\|h-W\|_{T}\leq\frac{r}{2},h(0)=0,

(3)

\displaystyle h(T)=W_{T}\}

\displaystyle,

for all
T>0
,
r>0
and all convex functions
\varphi
.

In the work
[
5
]
the asymptotic behavior of
I^{0}_{W}

was considered for kinetic energy, i.e., for the function
\varphi(u):=\vartheta_{2}(u)=u^{2}
. It was shown that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\,I^{0}_{W}(T,r,\vartheta_{2})=\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\,\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t)^{2}\,dt=\frac{4\mathcal{C}^{2}}{r^{2}},

(4)

where one has a.s.-convergence and convergence in
L_{q}
for every
q>0
. Here

\mathcal{C}
is some positive constant unknown to the authors of
[
5
]
; they provided some theoretical bounds for it and, by computer simulation, obtained an approximate value
\mathcal{C}\approx 0.63
.
A similar result (with the same limit value) is also true for the free-end problem, i.e. for

I_{W}(T,r,\varphi):=\min\{G^{\varphi}_{T}(h);h\in\text{AC}[0,T],\|h-W\|_{T}\leq\frac{r}{2},h(0)=0\}

(5)

in place of
I^{0}_{W}(T,r,\varphi)
.

Various extensions of (
4
) (for strips of variable width, for random walks, for unilateral constraints) can be found
in
[
1
,
6
,
7
]
.

Later on, in
[
8
]
a result analogous to (
4
), i.e. the existence of the limit

R_{r}(\varphi):=\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\,\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt

(6)

was obtained for a rather broad class of functions
\varphi
(asserting convergence to an unknown constant depending on
\varphi
), as well as a central limit theorem for
G^{\varphi}_{T}(\eta_{T,r})
. The main assumption here is a power bound on the growth of
\varphi
at infinity, i.e., one should assume

\lim\limits_{|u|\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(u)}{|u|^{\alpha}}=0

(7)

for some
\alpha>0
.

We stress that convexity of
\varphi
is not assumed in the result but in the non-convex case
the connection to the optimizaton problem is lost and therefore the result becomes much less interesting.

Our initial goal was to find more explicit expressions for the limits (
6
).
This goal is achieved (in particular, the constant from (
4
) is identified as
\mathcal{C}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{24}}
); it became clear that the existence of the limits (
6
) is based on the asymptotic behavior of the distribution (normed sojourn measure) of the derivative of the taut string, i.e. the measure
\nu_{T,r}
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}f(u)\,\nu_{T,r}(du)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt

(8)

for every measurable bounded function
f
. Essentially, for an
r
fixed, the measures
\nu_{T,r}
converge weakly, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, to a limiting measure
\nu_{\infty,r}
; the continuous density of the latter admits an explicit computation, namely,

p_{\infty,r}(u):=\frac{d\nu_{\infty,r}(u)}{du}=\begin{cases}r\ \frac{ru\coth(ru)-1}{\sinh^{2}(ru)},\ &u\neq 0;\\
\frac{1}{3}\,r,\ &u=0.\end{cases}

(9)

Heuristically, this leads us to equalities

\displaystyle R_{r}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt=\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,\nu_{T,r}(du)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,\nu_{\infty,r}(du)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du,

which solves the problem of finding the limit (
6
);
convergence of sojourn measures is not related to particular functions
\varphi
.
For example, substituting the quadratic function from (
4
), we find (cf. Section
7.2
below)

\displaystyle 4r^{-2}\mathcal{C}^{2}=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}r\frac{(ru\coth(ru)-1)}{\sinh^{2}(ru)}\,du=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6r^{2}}.

Thus,

\mathcal{C}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{24}}\approx 0.641,

which agrees well with the simulated value from
[
5
]
mentioned above.

Our approach to the rigorous confirmation of (
10
) goes through consideration of some special family of functions
\varphi
.
Recall the usual notation

v^{+}:=\max\{v,0\},\quad v^{-}:=\max\{-v,0\},\qquad v\in\mathbb{R},

and define the family of functions

\rho_{\mu}^{+}(u):=(u-\mu)^{+},\ \mu\in\mathbb{R}.

(11)

For handling these functions, we need the notion and properties of
truncated variation defined and studied in the works of R. Łochowski,
cf.
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
9
]
.
We will show that the results of
[
9
]
yield

R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{\mu})=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(\mu\coth{r\mu}-\mu),\ &\mu\neq 0;\\
\frac{1}{2}r^{-1},\ &\mu=0,\end{cases}

(12)

as a special case of (
10
).
Furthermore, for similar functions
\rho^{-}_{\mu}(u):=(u-\mu)^{-}=\rho^{+}_{-\mu}(-u)
one has

R_{r}(\rho^{-}_{\mu})=R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{-\mu})
by the symmetry of Wiener process distribution.
The weak convergence
\nu_{T,r}\Rightarrow\nu_{\infty,r}
, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, also follows from (
12
).

Since sufficiently general convex functions can be represented as a mix of the functions

\rho^{+}_{\mu}
and
\rho^{-}_{\mu}
, by using linearity of the functional
R_{r}(\cdot)
,
one can obtain (
10
) for those functions, too.

Unfortunately, when considering the limits like (
10
) rigorously,
one has to distinguish a.s.-convergence and convergence in probability.
Our main result in this direction is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If a convex function
\varphi
satisfies

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)e^{-2\lambda|u|}du<\infty\quad\textrm{for some }\lambda\in(0,r)

(13)

or

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)du=\infty

(14)

then

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du

(15)

and

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\geq}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

(16)

If a function
\varphi
is continuous almost everywhere, locally bounded and satisfies power growth condition (
7
), then

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

(17)

Remark 1
.

Actually the gap between (
13
) and (
14
) which is not covered by Theorem
1.1
is very narrow. Since the density
p_{\infty,r}(\cdot)
is locally bounded and

p_{\infty,r}(u)\sim\frac{4r^{2}|u|}{e^{2r|u|}},\qquad\textrm{as }|u|\to\infty,

(18)

the gap consists of the functions
\varphi
such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)du<\infty

but

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,\lambda}(u)du=\infty\qquad\textrm{for all }\lambda\in(0,r).

We believe that (
13
) is just a technical condition and convergence in probability actually holds for every convex function
\varphi
.

Note also that (
13
) is much weaker than (
7
).

In general, one can not replace in (
15
) convergence in probability by a.s.-convergence because of the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda>1
,
r>0
and
\varphi(u)\geq e^{\lambda ru}
for all sufficiently large

u\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}+\infty.

Notice that if
\varphi(u)=e^{\lambda ru}
with
\lambda\in(1,2)
, then the assumption
of Theorem
1.2
is satisfied and, in view of the density asymptotics of the limiting measure (
18
), it is true that

\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du<\infty.

Therefore, there is no a.s. convergence in (
15
).
It would be interesting to identify an exact upper bound for the growth
of
\varphi
beyond which a.s.-convergence may be lost.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the necessary information on truncated variation; this notion is applied to our problem in
Section
3
. Convergence of sojourn measures and the lower
bound (
16
) are obtained in Section
4
.
In Section
5
we prove the main result of the article – the weak law of large numbers (
15
).
In Section
6
we prove the strong law of large numbers (
17
).
Finally, Section
7
contains some secondary but necessary proofs including the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2
Truncated variation

2.1
Basic notions and properties

Let us recall the main concepts and results from the articles
[
10
,
11
,
9
,
12
]
.

Truncated variation
of a càdlàg function
f
on an interval
a,b]
for a given
r\geq 0
is defined as

\displaystyle TV^{r}(f,[a,b]):=\sup_{n}\sup_{a\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq b}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{+}_{r}(|f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k})|),

where expression
\rho^{+}_{r}(\cdot)
was defined in (
11
). Notice that
TV^{0}(f,[a,b])
is the standard total variation of
f
.

Truncated variation can be expressed as the solution of the following optimization problem

TV^{r}(f,[a,b])=\inf\bigg\{TV^{0}(g,[a,b]);\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\frac{r}{2}\bigg\}.

(19)

For continuous
f
, there is a continuous function (lazy function) at which the minimum in (
19
) is attained.

Moreover, if
f
is continuous, then we can consider the infimum in (
19
) only over the class of absolutely continuous functions. Recall that for AC functions the total variation can be represented as

TV^{0}(g,[a,b])=\int_{a}^{b}|g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt,\quad g\in\text{AC}[a,b].

(20)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be continuous on an interval
[a,b]
; Then for any
r>0

TV^{r}(f,[a,b])=\inf\left\{\int_{a}^{b}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt;\ g\in{\text{AC}}[a,b],\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\frac{r}{2}\right\}.

(21)

The
upward truncated variation

UTV^{r}(f,[a,b])
and the
downward truncated variation

DTV^{r}(f,[a,b])
are defined in the same way:

\displaystyle UTV^{r}(f,[a,b]):=\sup_{n}\sup_{a\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq b}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{+}_{r}(f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k}))

and
DTV^{r}(f,[a,b]):=UTV^{r}(-f,[a,b])
.

Similarly to (
19
), there is the following representation

UTV^{r}(f,[a,b])=\inf\bigg\{UTV^{0}(g,[a,b]);\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\frac{r}{2}\bigg\}.

Similarly to (
20
), there is an integral representation for AC functions:

UTV^{0}(g,[a,b])=\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt,\quad g\in\text{AC}[a,b].

(22)

Finally, similarly to (
21
), there is the following representation

UTV^{r}(f,[a,b])=\inf\left\{\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}dt;\ g\in{\text{AC}}[a,b],\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\frac{r}{2}\right\}.

(23)

Since the representations (
21
) and (
23
) are not considered in the mentioned Łochowski’s articles, we will prove them in Section
7.3
.

2.2
Truncated variation of the Wiener process

In the article
[
9
]
, the asymptotics of truncated variations of a Wiener process with a drift is found. This result is important for our subsequent calculations.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Wiener process; for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
define

X_{t}:=W_{t}-\mu t,\quad t\geq 0.

Then, as
T\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\,TV^{r}(X,[0,T])

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}m_{r}(\mu),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\,UTV^{r}(X,[0,T])

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}q_{r}(\mu),

where

m_{r}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\mu\coth({r\mu}),\ &\mu\neq 0;\\
r^{-1},\ &\mu=0;\end{cases}

(24)

q_{r}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(\mu\coth(r\mu)-\mu),&\mu\not=0;\\
\frac{1}{2}r^{-1},&\mu=0.\end{cases}

(25)

In the next section we will establish a connection between truncated variation and taut strings. Also, using Theorem
2.1
, we will find the value of
R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{\mu})
.

3
Application of truncated variation

Our first asymptotic result consists in the computation of the functional
R_{r}

for two fundamental families of functions:

\rho_{\mu}^{+}
defined in (
11
) and
\kappa_{\mu}(u):=|u-\mu|
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})=m_{r}(\mu),

(26)

R_{r}(\rho_{\mu}^{+})=q_{r}(\mu),

(27)

where
m_{r}(\mu)
and
q_{r}(\mu)
are defined in (
24
) and (
25
), respectively.

Proof.

First of all, notice that the limits in the definitions of
R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})
and
R_{r}(\rho_{\mu}^{+})
, exist by Schertzer’s theorem
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
, since
the functions
\kappa_{\mu}
and
\rho_{\mu}^{+}
satisfy condition (
7
).

Consider a Wiener process with a drift
\widetilde{W}_{t}:=W_{t}-\mu t
. According to Theorem
2.1

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\,TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}m_{r}(\mu).

(28)

By applying Lemma
2.1
and making the variable change
g(t)=h(t)-\mu t
,
we obtain

\displaystyle TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{T}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt\ ;\ g\in\text{AC}[0,T],\|g-\widetilde{W}\|_{T}\leq\frac{r}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{T}|h^{\prime}(t)-\mu|dt\ ;\ h\in\text{AC}[0,T],\|h-W\|_{T}\leq\frac{r}{2}\right\}.

Therefore, for the taut string
\eta_{T,r}
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{\mu}(\eta_{T,r}^{\prime}(t))dt=\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{T,r}^{\prime}(t)-\mu|dt\geq TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T).

From (
28
), it follows now that
R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})\geq m_{r}(\mu)
.

In order to get the opposite inequality, let us fix a small number
\delta>0

and find a function
h_{T}\in\text{AC}[0,T]
, such that

\|h_{T}-W\|_{T}\leq r/2
and

\int_{0}^{T}|h_{T}^{\prime}(t)-\mu|\,dt\leq TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T)+\delta.

Next, we construct an absolutely continuous ”pseudostring”
\zeta_{T,r}
satisfying
the right border conditions
\zeta_{T,r}(0)=0
,
\zeta_{T,r}(T)=W_{T}

and such that
\zeta_{T,r}=h_{T}
on
[1,T-1]
. The connection parts must be sufficiently smooth,
satisfy border conditions
\zeta_{T,r}(1)=h_{T}(1)
,
\zeta_{T,r}(T-1)=h_{T}(T-1)
,
and

\max\left\{\|\zeta_{T,r}-W\|_{[0,1]};\|\zeta_{T,r}-W\|_{[T-1,T]}\right\}\leq\tfrac{r+\delta}{2}.

Such construction provides

\|\zeta_{T,r}-W\|_{T}\leq\frac{r+\delta}{2}
and

\zeta_{T,r}\in\text{AC}[0,T]
.

Let us construct
\zeta_{T,r}
on
[0,1]
.
Let

D:=\left\{w\in C^{1}[0,1]:w(0)=0,w(1)=W_{1},\|w-W\|_{[0,1]}\leq\delta/2\right\}

and

M:=\inf_{w\in D}\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}|w^{\prime}(t)|.

Then
D
is a.s. non-empty, hence,
M<\infty
a.s.

Let us choose a (random) function
w\in D
such that

\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}|w^{\prime}(t)|\leq M+1.

(29)

Since
w\in D
, we have
w(0)=0,w(1)=W_{1}
and
\|w-W\|_{[0,1]}\leq\tfrac{\delta}{2}
. Let

\zeta_{T,r}(t):=w(t)+(h_{T}(1)-W_{1})t,\qquad t\in[0,1].

Since
|h_{T}(1)-W_{1}|\leq\frac{r}{2}
, it is easy to check that this function has all required properties.
In particular, using (
29
),
|h_{T}(1)-W_{1}|\leq\frac{r}{2}
, and the definition of
\kappa_{\mu}
, one has

\frac{1}{T}\,\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{\mu}(\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\leq\frac{M+1+r/2+\mu}{T}\to 0,\quad\textrm{as }T\to\infty,

almost surely, hence, in probability.

By stationarity of the increments of a Wiener process, the construction on
[T-1,T]
is completely similar (instead of
W
, one should approximate another Wiener process, namely,

W_{T}(s):=W_{T-s}-W_{T}
) and the result has the same properties, in particular,

\frac{1}{T}\,\int_{T-1}^{T}\kappa_{\mu}(\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\to 0,\quad\textrm{as }T\to\infty,

in probability (yet we do not claim the a.s. convergence).

A ”pseudostring” beeing constructed, let us start our estimations. By optimality property of the true taut string we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{\mu}(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r+\delta}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r}^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]\cup[T-1,T]}|\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t)-\mu|\,dt+\int_{1}^{T-1}|h_{T}^{\prime}(t)-\mu|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]\cup[T-1,T]}|\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t)-\mu|\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}|h_{T}^{\prime}(t)-\mu|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]\cup[T-1,T]}|\zeta_{T,r}^{\prime}(t)-\mu|\,dt+TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T)+\delta.

Divide by
T
and write the obtained inequality as

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\kappa_{\mu}(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r+\delta}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{T}TV^{r}(\widetilde{W},T)-\frac{\delta}{T}\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{[0,1]\cup[T-1,T]}|\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t)-\mu|\,dt.

As
T\to\infty
, the left hand side converges a.s. to the constant

R_{r+\delta}(\kappa_{\mu})-m_{r}(\mu)
, while the right hand side converges to
zero in probability. We infer that

R_{r+\delta}(\kappa_{\mu})\leq m_{r}(\mu).

By switching notation from
r+\delta
to
r
, we have

R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})\leq m_{r-\delta}(\mu).

Finally, letting
\delta\searrow 0
and using continuity of the expression
m_{r}(\mu)
with respect to
r
, we arrive at the required estimate
R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})\leq m_{r}(\mu)
.
Therefore, (
26
) is proved.

In order to derive (
27
), let us consider identity function
\chi(t):=t
.
By the properties of the Wiener process, we have

\displaystyle R_{r}(\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t)dt

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\eta_{T,r}(T)-\eta_{T,r}(0)}{T}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{W_{T}}{T}=0.

Therefore,

R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{\mu})=R_{r}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{\mu}+\chi-\mu)\right)=\frac{1}{2}(R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})-\mu)=\frac{1}{2}(m_{r}(\mu)-\mu)=q_{r}(\mu).

∎

Remark 2
.

For the function
\rho^{-}_{\mu}(u):=(u-\mu)^{-}

we have

\displaystyle R_{r}(\rho^{-}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=R_{r}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{\mu}-(\chi-\mu))\right)=\frac{1}{2}(R_{r}(\kappa_{\mu})+\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{r}(\mu)+\mu\right)

\displaystyle=q_{r}(-\mu).

4
Asymptotic distribution of taut string’s derivative

Consider normalized sojourn measures
\nu_{T,r}
for the derivative of the taut string defined by (
8
). The following result is true.

Theorem 4.1
.

As
T\rightarrow\infty
, the normalized sojourn measures
\nu_{T,r}
a.s.
converge weakly to the probability measure
\nu_{\infty,r}
having the density
p_{\infty,r}

given in (
9
). Moreover, for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
it is true that

R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{\mu})=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Proof.

For all
v\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta>0
we have inequalities

\frac{1}{\delta}(\rho^{+}_{v}(u)-\rho^{+}_{v+\delta}(u))\leq\mathds{1}_{u\geq v}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}(\rho^{+}_{v-\delta}(u)-\rho^{+}_{v}(u)).

By integrating the left hand side of the inequality over the measure
\nu_{T,r}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho^{+}_{v}(u)-\rho^{+}_{v+\delta}(u))\,\nu_{T,r}(du)=\frac{1}{T\delta}\int\limits_{0}^{T}(\rho^{+}_{v}-\rho^{+}_{v+\delta})(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt.

Let us compute the limit, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, of the right hand side of this equality:

\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\delta}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho^{+}_{v}(u)-\rho^{+}_{v+\delta}(u))\,\nu_{T,r}(du)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\delta}\big(R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v})-R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v+\delta})\big).

Therefore,

\frac{1}{\delta}\big(R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v})-R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v+\delta})\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\leq}}\liminf\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathds{1}_{u\geq v}\,\nu_{T,r}(du)=\liminf\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{T,r}[v,\infty).

In the same way, we obtain

\limsup\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{T,r}[v,\infty)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\delta}\big(R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v-\delta})-R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v})\big).

By letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
, we see that the limiting values of both the upper and
lower bounds coincide with
-\frac{d}{dv}R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v})
. Therefore,

\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{T,r}[v,\infty)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}-\frac{d}{dv}R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{v})=\int\limits_{v}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}}{du^{2}}R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{u})\,du:=\int\limits_{v}^{\infty}p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

(31)

This relation shows convergence

\lim\limits_{T\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{T,r}[v,\infty)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\nu_{\infty,r}[v,\infty)

(32)

for every fixed
v
. Therefore, with probability one (
32
) holds simultaneously for all rational
v
. Furthermore since the distribution tail
\nu_{\infty,r}[v,\infty)
is continuous in
v
, the convergence holds a.s. simultaneously for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
, which proves weak convergence.

Formulas (
27
) and (
31
) provide the asserted expression for
p_{\infty,r}
. Finally, for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
integration by parts yields

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mu}^{+}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du=-\int\limits_{\mu}^{\infty}\frac{d}{du}R_{r}(\rho^{+}_{u})\,du=R_{r}(\rho_{\mu}^{+}).

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\varphi
be either an almost everywhere continuous non-negative or a convex function.
Then for every
r>0

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\geq}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

(33)

Proof.

If
\varphi
is almost everywhere continuous and
bounded
, by the well known property
of weak convergence,

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)\,\nu_{T,r}(du)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Dropping the boundedness assumption, consider for
M>0
the truncated function

\varphi_{M}(u):=\min\{\varphi(u),M\}
. Since
\varphi
is assumed to be non-negative,
\varphi_{M}

is bounded and we may apply the previous chain to it.
Therefore,

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\geq\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{M}(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{M}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Furthermore, since

\lim_{M\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{M}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du,

we obtain (
33
)

On the other hand, a convex function can be represented as a sum of a linear function and
a non-negative continuous function. For linear functions we have a.s. convergence,
cf. (
30
), thus the assertion follows.
∎

We stress that this corollary is still true in the case when the limiting integral is infinite.

5
Weak law of large numbers

In this section, we prove the weak law of large numbers for the energy of the taut string accompanying Wiener process. It was already stated in (
15
) as a part of Theorem
1.1
. For reading convenience, we repeat it here as a separate theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a convex non-negative function satisfying (
13
) or (
14
). Let
r>0
and let
\eta_{T,r}
be the taut string accompanying Wiener process in the strip of width
r
on
[0,T]
; then

\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du,\qquad\textrm{as }T\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

If (
14
) holds, i.e. the limiting integral is infinite, then the result follows from (
33
). In the sequel, we assume that the limiting integral is finite.

Without loss of generality one may assume that
\varphi(u)=0
for
u\leq 0
. Indeed, any convex function
\varphi
can be represented as a sum of a linear function
\ell(u):=\varphi(0)+cu
for
c\in[\varphi_{-}^{\prime}(0),\varphi_{+}^{\prime}(0)]
and two convex functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
such that
\varphi_{1}(u)=0
for
u\leq 0
and
\varphi_{2}(u)=0
for
u\geq 0
. By (
30
), we have
R_{r}(\ell)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\varphi(0)
. Therefore, it is enough to prove the theorem for the functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
separately.

From now on, we suppose that
\varphi(u)=0
for
u\leq 0
. In this case, the function
\varphi
may be represented as

\varphi(u)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(u)\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu),

(34)

where
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
is a locally finite measure.

Let
\theta:[0,\infty)\mapsto(0,\infty)
be a Borel-measurable map such that the constant

\Theta
defined as

\Theta:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta(\mu)\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu)

is finite.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, for each
\mu\geq 0
, using (
23
), we find an absolutely continuous function
h_{T,r,\mu}
such that

||h_{T,r,\mu}-W||_{T}\leq\tfrac{r}{2}
and

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(h^{\prime}_{T,r,\mu}(t))\,dt\leq UTV^{r}(\widetilde{W}_{\mu},[0,T])+\theta(\mu),

where
\widetilde{W}_{\mu}(t)=W_{t}-\mu t
is a Wiener process with a drift.

For fixed
T>2,\delta>0
we construct a ”pseudostring”
\zeta_{T,r,\mu}
in the strip of width
r+\delta
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, let
\zeta_{T,r,\mu}(t)=h_{T,r,\mu}(t)
on
[1,T-1]
and for
t\in[0,1]

\zeta_{T,r,\mu}(t):=w(t)+(h_{T,r,\mu}(1)-W_{1})t

where
w
is the same continuously differentiable function such that
w(0)=0,w(1)=W_{1}
,

\|w-W\|_{[0,1]}\leq\tfrac{\delta}{2}
and (
29
) holds. Hence

\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\zeta^{\prime}_{T,r,\mu}(t)|\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}|w^{\prime}(t)|+|h_{T,r,\mu}(1)-W_{1}|\leq M+1+\frac{r}{2}:=\widetilde{M}.

(35)

This estimate is random but uniform in
\mu,T
.

By stationarity of the increments of a Wiener process, the construction on
[T-1,T]
is completely similar.

From optimality of the taut string
\eta_{T,r+\delta}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\eta_{T,r+\delta}^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{[0,1]\cup[T-1,T]}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt+\int\limits_{1}^{T-1}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(h_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt

for any
\mu
. Using representation (
34
), we get

\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta_{T,r+\delta}^{\prime}(t))\,dt\leq A^{1}_{T,r}+B_{T,r}+A^{T}_{T,r},

where

\displaystyle A^{1}_{T,r}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu),

\displaystyle A^{T}_{T,r}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{T-1}^{T}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu),

\displaystyle B_{T,r}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{1}^{T-1}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(h_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu).

Let us estimate the expectation of
B_{T,r}
. Since
\rho^{+}_{\mu}

is non-negative, by the definition of
h_{T,r,\mu}
we have the following inequality

\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{1}^{T-1}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(h^{\prime}_{T,r,\mu}(t))\,\,dt\leq\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(h^{\prime}_{T,r,\mu}(t))\,\,dt\leq\frac{1}{T}\,UTV^{r}(\widetilde{W}_{\mu},[0,T])+\frac{\theta(\mu)}{T}.

Since
UTV^{r}(\widetilde{W}_{\mu},[0,T])
is superadditive in
T
(see
[
9
]
), it follows from Theorem
2.1
and
[
2
, Lemma 3.6.II]

that for every
T>0

\frac{1}{T}\,UTV^{r}(\widetilde{W}_{\mu},[0,T])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\leq}}q_{r}(\mu).

Fubini’s theorem and double integration by parts yield

\mathbb{E}B_{T,r}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}q_{r}(\mu)\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu)+\frac{\Theta}{T}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(u)q_{r}^{\prime\prime}(u)\,du+\frac{\Theta}{T}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du+\frac{\Theta}{T}.

Let us now estimate the remainder terms
A^{1}_{T,r}
and
A^{T}_{T,r}
.
From (
35
) and the definition of
\rho^{+}_{\mu}
, it follows that for every
t\in[0,1]

0\leq\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\leq\begin{cases}\widetilde{M},&0\leq\mu\leq\widetilde{M},\\
0,&\mu>\widetilde{M}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

0\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\rho^{+}_{\mu}(\zeta_{T,r,\mu}^{\prime}(t))\,dt\,\varphi^{\prime\prime}(d\mu)\leq\widetilde{M}\varphi^{\prime\prime}[0,\widetilde{M}],

whence

0\leq A^{1}_{T,r}\leq\frac{\widetilde{M}\varphi^{\prime\prime}[0,\widetilde{M}]}{T}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,\quad\textrm{ as }T\to\infty.

Since
A^{1}_{T,r}
and
A^{T}_{T,r}
have the same distributions (by stationarity of the increments of a Wiener process), we also obtain
A^{T}_{T,r}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
.

Recall that the limiting measure
\nu_{\infty,r}
depends on
r
and denote the limiting integral as a function of the strip width:

J(\lambda):=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(u)\,\nu_{\infty,\lambda}(du)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(u)\,p_{\infty,\lambda}(u)\,du.

Let us show that
J(\cdot)
is non-increasing; moreover, under assumption (
13
)
J(\cdot)
is finite and continuous on some neighborhood of
r
.

Indeed, by the definition of
p_{\infty,\lambda}
after linear variable change
u=v/\lambda
one has

J(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(v/\lambda)\,p_{\infty,1}(v)\,dv.

(36)

Recall that
\varphi
is now a convex function vanishing at
(-\infty,0]
. Hence, it is non-decreasing.
It follows from (
36
) that
J(\cdot)
is non-increasing.

Furthermore, from (
18
) and (
36
) it follows that
J(\cdot)
is finite on some neighborhood of
r
. Finally, if
J(\lambda)<\infty
for some
\lambda\in(0,r)
, then
J(\cdot)
is continuous on
(\lambda,\infty)
by continuity of
\varphi
and by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem –
with
\varphi(\cdot/\lambda)\,p_{\infty,1}(\cdot)
as an integrable, dominating function in (
36
).
This is the point where additional assumption (
13
) crucially comes into play because
in the sequel we will use that
J(\cdot)
is left-continuous at
r
.

Let us define

\displaystyle S_{T,r}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt,

\displaystyle A_{T,r}

\displaystyle:=A^{1}_{T,r}+A^{T}_{T,r}

and summarize the results obtained above as follows

\displaystyle 1)\,S_{T,r+\delta}\leq A_{T,r}+B_{T,r};

\displaystyle 2)\,A_{T,r}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0;

\displaystyle 3)\,\mathbb{E}B_{T,r}\leq J(r)+\frac{\Theta}{T};

\displaystyle 4)\,\liminf_{T\rightarrow\infty}S_{T,r+\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\geq}}J(r+\delta),

where the last inequality follows from (
33
).
If we replace
r+\delta
by
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1)\,S_{T,r}\leq A_{T,r-\delta}+B_{T,r-\delta};

\displaystyle 2)\,A_{T,r-\delta}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0;

\displaystyle 3)\,\mathbb{E}B_{T,r-\delta}\leq J(r-\delta)+\frac{\Theta}{T};

\displaystyle 4)\,\liminf_{T\rightarrow\infty}S_{T,r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{\geq}}J(r).

We will prove now that
S_{T,r}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}J(r)
using only properties
1)
–
4)
.

For each
y<J(r)
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}<y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{T,r}-A_{T,r-\delta}<y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{T,r}-A_{T,r-\delta}<y,S_{T,r}\geq\frac{J(r)+y}{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\bigg(S_{T,r}<\frac{J(r)+y}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(A_{T,r-\delta}>S_{T,r}-y>\frac{J(r)+y}{2}-y=\frac{J(r)-y}{2}\bigg)+\mathbb{P}\bigg(S_{T,r}<\frac{J(r)+y}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(A_{T,r-\delta}>\frac{J(r)-y}{2}\bigg)+\mathbb{P}\bigg(S_{T,r}<\frac{J(r)+y}{2}\bigg).

From properties
2)
and
4)
it follows that both terms tend to zero as
T\to\infty
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{-}

\displaystyle\leq J(r-\delta)\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}<y)+(J(r-\delta)-y)\cdot 1

\displaystyle=o(1)+(J(r-\delta)-y).

By letting
y\nearrow J(r)
, we obtain

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{-}\leq J(r-\delta)-J(r).

Now, using equality

\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{+}=\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))+\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{-}

and property
3)
, we get

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{+}\leq J(r-\delta)-J(r).

(37)

Let us fix two small parameters
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
choose
\delta>0
such that
J(r-\delta)-J(r)<\varepsilon_{1}
.
Using Markov’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}>J(r)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}>(J(r)-J(r-\delta))+J(r-\delta)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}>-\varepsilon_{1}+J(r-\delta)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta)>\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{+}}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}}.

Now, by (
37
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(B_{T,r-\delta}>J(r)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}(B_{T,r-\delta}-J(r-\delta))^{+}}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{J(r-\delta)-J(r)}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}}.

From properties
1)
and
2)
, it follows that

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{T,r}>J(r)+2\varepsilon)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{1}}.

By letting
\varepsilon_{1}\to 0
, we obtain

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{T,r}>J(r)+2\varepsilon)=0.

This fact and property
4)
prove that

S_{T,r}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}J(r).

∎

6
Strong law of large numbers

In this section we prove the following result which is a part of the main
Theorem
1.1
, cf. (
17
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let a function
\varphi
be continuous almost everywhere, locally bounded and satisfy the condition of power growth (
7
).
Then for every
r>0
it is true that

R_{r}(\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Proof.

First of all, let us note that that the limit
R_{r}(\varphi)
exists a.s. by Schertzer’s result. We only need to identify it with the integral from Theorem’s assertion.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\varphi\geq 0
.

Furthermore, let us note that in two cases the theorem’s assertion follows immediately from our previous results.

•

If
\varphi
is almost everywhere continuous and
bounded
, then the asserted form of the limit follows from the a.s. convergence of the sojourn measures
(Theorem
4.1
);

•

If
\varphi
is
convex
, then we have convergence in probability to the required limit by Theorem
5.1
, hence the a.s.-limit
R_{r}(\varphi)
is the same (note that the power growth condition yields (
13
), hence Theorem
5.1
may be applied) .

Let us now consider the general case. Let
M>0
. We represent
\varphi
as

\varphi=\varphi_{1,M}+\varphi_{2,M}
, where

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,M}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\varphi(u),M\};

\displaystyle\varphi_{2,M}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varphi(u)-M)^{+}.

Since the function
\varphi_{1,M}
is almost everywhere continuous and bounded,
we have

R_{r}\left(\varphi_{1,M}\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{1,M}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

By the power growth condition, for some
c_{1},c_{2}>0,\alpha\geq 1
for all

u\in\mathbb{R}
it is true that
\varphi(u)\leq c_{1}+c_{2}|u|^{\alpha}:=L(u)
.
Therefore,

\varphi_{2,M}(u)\leq(L(u)-M)^{+},

where the right hand side is a convex function. Hence,

R_{r}\left(\varphi_{2,M}\right)\leq R_{r}\left((L(\cdot)-M)^{+}\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}(L(u)-M)^{+}p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Thus,

\displaystyle R_{r}(\varphi)=R_{r}\left(\varphi_{1,M}\right)+R_{r}\left(\varphi_{2,M}\right)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}(L(u)-M)^{+}p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

Take a limit in
M\nearrow+\infty
and notice that the latter integral tends to zero.
Therefore,

R_{r}(\varphi)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)\,du.

The opposite estimate follows from (
33
).
∎

7.1
Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(a/\ln n)^{1/2}}{r}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2a}\,\ln n\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(\ln n)^{-1/2}n^{-1}.

The series
\sum_{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{n})
diverges, thus by Borel–Cantelli lemma the events
A_{n}
occur infinitely often.

Let now
T>0
be such that

W(T)-W\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)\geq r.

Then for every function
g
such that

\big|g\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)-W\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)\big|\leq\frac{r}{2};\qquad g(T)=W(T),

(38)

it is true that

g(T)-g\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)\geq W(T)-\left(W\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)+\frac{r}{2}\right)\geq r-\frac{r}{2}=\frac{r}{2}.

If
g\in\text{AC}[0,T]
, then by convexity of the function
\varphi
Jensen inequality yields

\int_{T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}}^{T}\varphi(g^{\prime}(t))\,dt\geq\frac{a}{\ln T}\ \varphi\left(\frac{g(T)-g\left(T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}\right)}{a/\ln T}\right).

For sufficiently large
T
we infer

\int_{T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}}^{T}\varphi(g^{\prime}(t))\,dt\geq\frac{a}{\ln T}\,\exp\left(\frac{\lambda r\cdot r/2}{a/\ln T}\right)=\frac{a}{\ln T}\,T^{\lambda}.

Since the taut string
\eta_{T,r}
satisfies conditions (
38
), it also obeys
inequalities

\int_{0}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))dt\geq\int_{T-\tfrac{a}{\ln T}}^{T}\varphi(\eta^{\prime}_{T,r}(t))\,dt\geq\frac{a}{\ln T}\,T^{\lambda}\gg T,

where in the last step we used the assumption
\lambda>1
. Therefore, since the events
A_{n}
occur infinitely often, the stated estimates hold for arbitrary
r>0
and some arbitrarily large
T
, which proves the theorem.

7.2
The limit computation for quadratic function
\vartheta_{2}

As we know from
(
17
),

R_{r}(\vartheta_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}}}{{=}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\vartheta_{2}(u)p_{\infty,r}(u)du=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}u^{2}r\frac{(ru\coth(ru)-1)}{\sinh^{2}(ru)}\,du:=I.

Let us show that
I=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6r^{2}}
. Indeed,

I=r^{-2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}\frac{(v\coth(v)-1)}{\sinh^{2}(v)}\,dv=r^{-2}\bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{3}\cosh(v)}{\sinh^{3}(v)}\,dv-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(v)}\,dv\bigg).

Integration by parts yields

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{3}\cosh(v)}{\sinh^{3}(v)}\,dv=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}v^{3}\,d\bigg(\frac{1}{2\sinh^{2}(v)}\bigg)=\frac{3}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(v)}\,dv.

Therefore,

I=\frac{r^{-2}}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(v)}\,dv=r^{-2}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{v^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(v)}\,dv=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6r^{2}}.

where the last equality is a special case of
[
3
, formula (3.527.2)]
.

7.3
Truncated variations and absolutely continuous functions

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and a function
h
on
[a,b]
attains the value of

TV^{r-\varepsilon}(f,[a,b])
,
i.e.
TV^{0}(h,[a,b])=TV^{r-\varepsilon}(f,[a,b])
and

\|h-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\tfrac{r-\varepsilon}{2}
.

Let us extend
h
by the constants from
[a,b]
to
\mathbb{R}
and approximate
h
by convolutions with smooth kernels

h^{\delta}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)h(t+v)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(s-t)h(s)ds.

We assume the usual properties of the kernels:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\delta}(\cdot)\geq 0;\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)dv=1;\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\Phi^{\delta})^{\prime}(v)|dv<\infty;

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{|v|>\theta}\Phi^{\delta}(v)dv=0\qquad\textrm{for every }\theta>0.

Let us prove that

1)
h^{\delta}\in\text{AC}[a,b]
;

2)
TV^{0}(h^{\delta},[a,b])\leq TV^{0}(h,[a,b])=TV^{r-\varepsilon}(f,[a,b])
;

3) For sufficiently small
\delta
it is true that
\|h^{\delta}-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\tfrac{r}{2}
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\int_{a}^{b}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt\ ;\ g\in\text{AC}[a,b],\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\tfrac{r}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq TV^{0}(h^{\delta},[a,b])

\displaystyle\leq TV^{r-\varepsilon}(f,[a,b]).

Taking into account that the function
r\mapsto TV^{r}(f,[a,b])
is continuous (cf.
[
9
]
), by letting
\varepsilon
to zero,
we find

\inf\left\{\int_{a}^{b}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt\ ;\ g\in\text{AC}[a,b],\|g-f\|_{[a,b]}\leq\tfrac{r}{2}\right\}\leq TV^{r}(f,[a,b]).

The opposite inequality is obvious.

It remains to prove properties 1)  –  3).

1) The function
h^{\delta}
is differentiable and its derivative is uniformly bounded because

\displaystyle|(h^{\delta})^{\prime}(t)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Phi^{\delta})^{\prime}(s-t)h(s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|(\Phi^{\delta})^{\prime}(s-t)\right|ds\max_{s\in[a,b]}|h(s)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|(\Phi^{\delta})^{\prime}(v)\right|dv\max_{s\in[a,b]}|h(s)|.

Therefore,
h^{\delta}\in\text{Lip}[a,b]\subset\text{AC}[a,b]
.

2) For all
a_{1}\leq...\leq a_{m}
from
[a,b]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}|h^{\delta}(a_{i+1})-h^{\delta}(a_{i})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)\left(h(a_{i+1}+v)-h(a_{i}+v)\right)dv\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\left|h(a_{i+1}+v)-h(a_{i}+v)\right|\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)\,dv\ TV^{0}(h,[a,b])=TV^{0}(h,[a,b]).

Therefore,
TV^{0}(h^{\delta},[a,b])\leq TV^{0}(h,[a,b])
.

3) We have to prove that for small
\delta
and all
\tau\in[a,b]
it is true that

f(\tau)-\frac{r}{2}\leq h^{\delta}(\tau)\leq f(\tau)+\frac{r}{2}.

We will prove only the first inequality; the second one can be proved in the same way. For every
\tau
it is true that

\displaystyle h^{\delta}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)h(\tau+v)\,dv\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)\left[f(\tau+v)-\frac{r-\varepsilon}{2}\right]dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)f(\tau+v)\,dv-\frac{r-\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore,

h^{\delta}(\tau)-f(\tau)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)[f(\tau+v)-f(\tau)]\,dv-\frac{r-\varepsilon}{2}.

Moreover, for every
\theta>0

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)[f(\tau+v)-f(\tau)]dv\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)\left|f(\tau+v)-f(\tau)\right|dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{|v|\leq\theta}|f(\tau+v)-f(\tau)|+2\max_{[a,b]}|f|\int_{|v|>\theta}\Phi^{\delta}(v)dv.

Here, by a kernels’ property, the second term converges to zero for every
\theta
. The first term can be made arbitrarily small uniformly in
\tau
by the choice of
\theta
(it is here when the continuity of
f
is used).

Therefore, for sufficiently small
\delta
and all
\tau\in[a,b]
it is true that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\delta}(v)[f(\tau+v)-f(\tau)]\,dv\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

which yields the desired

h^{\delta}(\tau)-f(\tau)\geq-\frac{r}{2}.

∎

The representation (
23
) can be proved exactly in the same way, just with replacement of the function
x\mapsto|x|
with the function
x\mapsto x^{+}
.

Finally, let us prove representation (
22
). Let
g\in\text{AC}[a,b]

and
a\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\leq b
. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(g(t_{k+1})-g(t_{k}))^{+}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt\leq\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt.

By maximizing over partitions, we obtaion

UTV^{0}(g)\leq\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt.

In order to prove the opposite bound, let us fix an
\varepsilon>0
and choose a step function
h
such that
||g^{\prime}-h||_{1}:=||g^{\prime}-h||_{L_{1}[a,b]}\leq\varepsilon
.
Let
a=t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=b
be a partition such that the function
h
is constant on all corresponding intervals.

If an interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
is such that
h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\geq 0
,
then

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{-}dt\leq\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|h(t)-g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt,

hence,

\displaystyle(g(t_{k+1})-g(t_{k}))^{+}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle g(t_{k+1})-g(t_{k})=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{-}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|h(t)-g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

Otherwise, if an interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
is such that
h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}<0
,
then

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}dt\leq\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|h(t)-g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

By summing up over all over all intervals and splitting the sum in two corresponding
groups of terms we obtain

\displaystyle UTV^{0}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(g(t_{k+1})-g(t_{k}))^{+}\geq\sum_{k:h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\geq 0}(g(t_{k+1})-g(t_{k}))^{+}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k:h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\geq 0}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|h(t)-g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k:h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\geq 0}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt\right]-||h-g^{\prime}||_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-\left[\sum_{k:h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}<0}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt\right]-||h-g^{\prime}||_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-\left[\sum_{k:h\big|_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}<0}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|h(t)-g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt\right]-||h-g^{\prime}||_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-2||h-g^{\prime}||_{1}\geq\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt-2\varepsilon.

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain

UTV^{0}(g)\geq\int_{a}^{b}g^{\prime}(t)^{+}\,dt.

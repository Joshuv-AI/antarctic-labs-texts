Localization in supergravity
James Sparks

Abstract
We give an introduction to equivariant localization in supergravity, focusing on the application to four-dimensional theories and supersymmetric black holes. Invited contribution to the proceedings book “Modern topics in mathematical physics”



I
Introduction

Supersymmetric solutions of supergravity theories have played a central role
in string theory and quantum gravity. Supersymmetry provides additional
structure, with the analytic tools this brings allowing for precise calculations.
Notable examples include the microscopic interpretation of entropy for supersymmetric black holes, and the AdS/CFT correspondence which relates
quantum gravity to a dual conformal field theory on the boundary of spacetime.
The last decade has seen these two subjects
brought together for asymptotically anti-de Sitter (AdS) black holes, starting with the work of
Benini:2015eyy
;
Benini:2016rke
.
In particular, new approaches have been developed for computing various protected physical observables exactly in supersymmetric field theories
Pestun:2016zxk
.
Many of these results have been matched to a semi-classical analysis
in gravity, confirming and sharpening our understanding of holography, but much remains to be understood.

The simplest observable one can consider in a theory is the partition function
Z
,
or equivalently the free energy
F=-\log Z
. If quantum gravity
is described by some version of a path integral, then in the leading
semi-classical limit
F=I_{\text{on-shell}}
is identified with the on-shell action of
Euclidean solutions to the classical gravitational equations of motion
Gibbons:1976ue
.
Understanding which saddle points contribute, and evaluating those contributions, is a key problem.
The Einstein equations are coupled non-linear
partial differential equations, and finding solutions in closed form usually relies on a high degree of symmetry. However, we are not interested in
finding the solutions
per se
, but rather in evaluating their contribution
to the partition function.

Reference
BenettiGenolini:2023kxp
introduced a new approach:
equivariant localization in supergravity
. This is a mathematical structure
that allows one to compute many observables for supersymmetric solutions,
without solving the supergravity equations.
Supergravity localization has been applied to compute on-shell actions for theories
in four
BenettiGenolini:2023kxp
;
BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024hyd
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
;
Hristov:2024cgj
, five
Cassani:2024kjn
;
Colombo:2025ihp
;
BenettiGenolini:2025icr
;
Colombo:2025yqy
;
Park:2025fon
(with variants of the supergravity localization approach) and
six dimensions
Couzens:2025ghx
. The
structure is very general, allowing one to also compute black hole
entropies, central charges, scaling dimensions of operators, and other quantities

BenettiGenolini:2024kyy
;
BenettiGenolini:2023ndb
;
BenettiGenolini:2023yfe
;
Suh:2024asy
;
Couzens:2024vbn
;
Couzens:2025nxw
;
Couzens:2026qne
;
Martelli:2023oqk
;
Colombo:2023fhu
;
Cassia:2025aus
;
Cassia:2025jkr
.
The main observation of
BenettiGenolini:2023kxp

is that supersymmetric supergravity solutions are equipped with a
canonical set of polyforms
\Phi
,
i.e.
formal sums of differential forms of
different degrees. These forms are closed
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi=0
under the operator

\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\equiv\mathrm{d}-\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}\mskip 2.0mu
, where
\mathrm{d}
is the usual exterior
derivative and
\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}
denotes contraction with
the vector field
\xi
. Here
\xi
is a Killing vector field that is also defined
canonically for supersymmetric solutions, that we call the
supersymmetric Killing vector
. This generates a symmetry of the solutions, and
the set of fixed points where
\xi=0
will play an important role. The cohomology
of the operator
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}
is called
equivariant cohomology
, and
the observables
we compute represent equivariant cohomology classes.

The following general structure has emerged by studying supergravity theories in diverse dimensions, coupled to
different forms of matter, and for various compactifications of string theory and M-theory:

1.

There typically exist many equivariantly closed
polyforms
\Phi
in a given supergravity theory,
i.e.
satisfying
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi=0
.

2.

Interesting physical quantities arise as integrals of
these forms, which may be evaluated using the Berline–Vergne–Atiyah–Bott (BVAB) fixed point formula
BV:1982
;
Atiyah:1984px
.

3.

The quantities that appear in these localized expressions
may be evaluated without knowing the explicit form of solutions.

This last point is key: the expressions we find are always
“equivariant topological invariants,” depending only on weights of the vector field

\xi
at fixed points, and certain global data/topological invariants.
This points to the existence of a protected sector in supergravity theories,
with the observables being captured by equivariant cohomology.

In this article we give an introduction to supergravity localization in the simplest setting, focusing on
D=4
,
\mathcal{N}=2
minimal gauged
supergravity and its coupling to vector multiplet matter. This allows
us to develop the general theory and explain key concepts and results,
illustrating with simple examples that include classes of black hole
solutions.

II
D=4
minimal gauged supergravity

The theory

The bosonic field content of

D=4
,
\mathcal{N}=2
minimal gauged supergravity
Freedman:1976aw

is simply Einstein–Maxwell theory with a negative cosmological constant. The bulk action in Euclidean signature is

\displaystyle I_{\text{bulk}}=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{4}}\int_{M_{4}}(R+6-F^{2})\,\mathrm{vol}_{g}\,.

(1)

Here
G_{4}
is the Newton constant,
R
is the Ricci scalar,
and
F=\mathrm{d}A
is the Maxwell field strength with
F^{2}\equiv F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
,
\mu,\nu=1,\ldots,4
. We have normalized the cosmological constant to
\Lambda=-3
, and denote
Riemannian volume forms as
\mathrm{vol}_{g}=\sqrt{\det g}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}x
.
The action (
1
) leads to the Einstein–Maxwell equations

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}+3g_{\mu\nu}-2\left(F_{\mu\rho}{F_{\nu}}^{\rho}-\tfrac{1}{4}F^{2}g_{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}*F

\displaystyle=0\,.

(2)

Here we take the (not entirely standard) convention that a
p
-form
\alpha
has Hodge dual the

(4-p)
-form

(*\mskip 2.0mu\alpha)_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{4-p}}\equiv\frac{1}{p!}\varepsilon_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{4-p}}^{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{p}}\alpha_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{p}}
.

A solution to the equations of motion (
II
) is by definition supersymmetric if the supersymmetry variations of all fields is zero. For a bosonic solution with all fermions set to zero, the only
non-trivial supersymmetry variation is that for the gravitino field
\psi_{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}\psi_{\mu}=\Big(\nabla_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}A_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mu}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}F_{\nu\rho}\gamma^{\nu\rho}\gamma_{\mu}\Big)\epsilon=0\,.

(3)

Here
\epsilon
is a Dirac spinor,
\nabla_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\tfrac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{\ ab}\gamma_{ab}
is the spin connection with respect to an orthonormal frame
e^{a}_{\mu}
,
a=1,\ldots,4
, and the Hermitian gamma matrices
\gamma_{\mu}
generate the Clifford algebra Cliff
(4,0)
, so
\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=2g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathbbm{1}
.
Notice from the second term in (
3
) that the spinor is charged
under the gauge field
A
– this gauging of the R-symmetry that rotates the spinor

\epsilon
by a phase is what makes it a “gauged supergravity.”
Equation (
3
) is a form of generalized Killing spinor equation.
If a (not identically zero) solution
\epsilon
exists,
it automatically follows that the metric and gauge field satisfy
the equations of motion (
II
)
Genolini:2021urf
.

For simplicity we take the metric and gauge field to be real in what follows. More generally
in Euclidean signature one should allow for complex fields, in the spirit of
finding saddle points to the gravitational path integral. This doubling of the number of real degrees of freedom implies that, with supersymmetry, also the spinor
\epsilon

and its complex conjugate are independent. See
Freedman:2013oja
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
for further discussion.

One reason to be interested in
this theory is that it is a “consistent truncation” of
eleven-dimensional supergravity on a general class of internal spaces
called Sasaki–Einstein manifolds

Gauntlett:2007ma
. This means that any supersymmetric solution
in
D=4
uplifts to a supersymmetric solution in
D=11
, where
we have a more precise (microscopic) understanding of holography.
For example, taking the internal space to be
the seven-sphere
S^{7}
means that the
four-dimensional gravitational theory is holographically dual to the
three-dimensional Aharony–Bergman–Jafferis–Maldacena (ABJM) theory

Aharony:2008ug
. This is a Chern–Simons theory coupled to matter,
with gauge group
U(N)\times U(N)
. The gravitational dual
is described by classical supergravity in the
large
N
limit: at leading order, the
Newton constant in (
1
) is related to
N
by

\frac{1}{16\pi G_{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{24\pi}N^{3/2}
.
We will focus on localization in gravity in this article, rather than
trying to review holography, but this is an important motivation
for what follows.

Bilinear forms

A standard approach to studying supersymmetric solutions is to make an ansatz
for the metric, gauge field and spinor
\epsilon
, typically imposing symmetries in order to
reduce the partial differential equations (PDEs) to ordinary differential equations (ODEs).
While many
interesting solutions have been constructed in this way, we take a more general and indirect approach.

Following
Caldarelli:2003pb
;
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
, from the Killing spinor
\epsilon
one may
construct the real bilinears

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{\epsilon}\epsilon\,,\quad P\equiv\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{5}\epsilon\,,\quad\xi^{\flat}_{\mu}\equiv-\mathrm{i}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\epsilon\,,\quad

\displaystyle K_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon\,,\quad U_{\mu\nu}\equiv\mathrm{i}\bar{\epsilon}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\epsilon\,.

(4)

Here
\gamma_{5}\equiv\gamma_{1234}
satisfies
\gamma_{5}^{2}=1
, and
\bar{\epsilon}\equiv\epsilon^{\dagger}

denotes the Hermitian conjugate of
\epsilon
.
S
and
P
are scalar functions,

\xi^{\flat}_{\mu}
and
K_{\mu}
are one-forms, and
U_{\mu\nu}
is a two-form.
We denote by
\xi^{\mu}=g^{\mu\nu}\xi^{\flat}_{\nu}
the vector field dual to the one-form
\xi^{\flat}
.

Using the Killing spinor equation one can take covariant
derivatives
\nabla_{\rho}
of the objects in (
II
).
Here one uses (
3
) and its Hermitian conjugate to substitute for

\nabla_{\rho}\epsilon
,
\nabla_{\rho}\bar{\epsilon}
in terms of the other terms in
(
3
).
In particular, in this way one can
check that
\xi^{\mu}
is a Killing vector,
\nabla_{(\mu}\xi^{\flat}_{\nu)}=0
, while
skew-symmetrizing over indices leads to the one-form, two-form and three-form differential equations:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=-K+\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}*\mskip 3.0muF\,,\quad\mathrm{d}P=-\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}F\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}

\displaystyle=-2(*\mskip 3.0muU+S*F+PF)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=-3*\xi^{\flat}+F\wedge K\,.

(5)

Here
(\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}F)_{\mu}\equiv\xi^{\nu}F_{\nu\mu}
.
Notice that using the Cartan formula we have
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}F=\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}(\mathrm{d}F)+\mathrm{d}(\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}F)=0
, where we have used the Bianchi identity
\mathrm{d}F=0
and
the second equation in (
II
). Thus
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}=0=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}F_{\mu\nu}
and
\xi
generates a symmetry of the full solution.
We call
\xi
the
supersymmetric Killing vector
.

One can similarly construct further bilinear forms, and compute their derivatives using
(
3
), but these will not be needed in what follows.

Equivariantly closed forms

The previous subsection follows
the “
G
-structure” approach
to analysing supersymmetric supergravity solutions: one recasts the first order Killing spinor equation (
3
) in terms of
first order differential equations for bilinear forms, of which (
II
) are a subset. For a nice review, see
Gauntlett:2005bn
. However,
the next step was introduced much more recently in
BenettiGenolini:2023kxp
.

Given the existence of a canonical vector
\xi
generating a symmetry, and differential forms, it is natural to introduce
the equivariant exterior derivative
BV:1982
;
Witten:1982im

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\equiv\mathrm{d}-\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}\,.

(6)

This acts on formal sums of differential forms of mixed degree,
i.e.
polyforms.
The exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}
raises the degree of a form by one, while contraction
with the vector
\xi
lowers the degree by one. The Cartan formula
gives
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}^{2}=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
. This means that acting on polyforms
that are invariant under the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
, the operator

\mathrm{d}_{\xi}
is nilpotent. The corresponding cohomology, generalizing the de Rham cohomology when
\xi=0
, is called equivariant cohomology
BV:1982
.

Given the equations (
II
), a little inspiration leads one to define
the polyforms

\displaystyle\Phi^{F}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{F}_{2}+\Phi^{F}_{0}\equiv F-P\,,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\Phi_{4}+\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{0}

(7)

\displaystyle\equiv(3\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{vol}_{g}+F\wedge*\mskip 2.0muF)+(U+SF-P*F)-(SP)\,.

Here the subscripts denote degrees of the forms, so
e.g.

\Phi^{F}_{2}=F
is a two-form,
\Phi^{F}_{0}=-P
is a function. Using (
II
) one checks that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi^{F}=0=\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi\,,

(8)

are both equivariantly closed. For example, we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi^{F}=\mathrm{d}F-\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}F-\mathrm{d}P=0\,,

(9)

using the Bianchi identity and the second equation in (
II
).
Checking
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi=0
is only slightly more work, and requires using the algebraic (Fierz) one-form identity

\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}\mskip 2.0muU=PK
.

The top form of
\Phi^{F}
is the gauge field curvature
F
, and integrating it through a two-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma\subset M_{4}
by definition gives the quantized magnetic flux
\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}F
through
\Sigma
.
On the other hand, taking the trace of the Einstein equation (
II
) gives
R=-12
.
Substituting this into the bulk action (
1
), and noting

F^{2}\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{vol}_{g}=2F\wedge*\mskip 2.0muF
, we see that the
bulk on-shell
action (
1
) is given by

\displaystyle I_{\text{bulk, on-shell}}=\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{M_{4}}\Phi_{4}\right]\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(10)

Here
\Phi_{4}
is precisely the top form of the equivariantly closed
polyform
\Phi
in (
II
).

Fixed point formula

Having found some equivariantly closed forms, the Berline–Vergne–Atiyah–Bott (BVAB) formula
BV:1982
;
Atiyah:1984px
allows one to express integrals of these forms
in terms of quantities evaluated at the fixed point set
\mathcal{F}\equiv\{\xi=0\}\subset M
. Here recall that a vector field
\xi
generates a flow on a manifold
M
, where one moves points of
M
along the integral curves of
\xi
. The fixed points of this flow are precisely the points where
\xi=0
.

Here we give an account in dimensions
D=2,4
, before applying to
supergravity in the next subsection.

\mathbf{D=2}
: As a simple example, consider the two-sphere
M_{2}=S^{2}
equipped with the vector field
\xi=b\mskip 2.0mu\partial_{\varphi}
. Here
b\neq 0
is a
constant parameter and
(\theta,\varphi)
are spherical polar coordinates, so that

\xi
rotates
S^{2}
about its axis.
The fixed point set
\mathcal{F}
consists of two points:
the north pole (
\theta=0
) and south pole (
\theta=\pi
).

If
\Phi=\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{0}
is equivariantly closed,
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi=0
, the BVAB formula states

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{fixed points}\,p}\frac{2\pi}{b_{p}}\Phi_{0}|_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{b}\Phi_{0}|_{\theta=0}-\frac{2\pi}{b}\Phi_{0}|_{\theta=\pi}\,.

(11)

Here
\Phi_{0}|_{p}
denotes the function
\Phi_{0}
evaluated at the point
p
,
while
b_{p}
denotes the
weight
of
\xi
at
p
.
A perennial subtlety
when using the BVAB formula is determining signs of weights consistently: at the north pole

b_{p}=b
, where
\partial_{\varphi}
rotates the tangent space with weight
+1
(anti-clockwise),
while at the south pole
b_{p}=-b
, where
\partial_{\varphi}
rotates the tangent space with weight
-1
(clockwise). The BVAB formula (
II
)
relates the integral of the top form
\Phi_{2}
to the lower degree form
\Phi_{0}

and weights at the fixed points.

For example, we can take
\Phi=\sin\theta\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi+b\cos\theta+c
. Note the arbitrary additive constant
c
. Then from (
II
) the volume of the round metric on
S^{2}
is

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\Phi_{2}=\frac{2\pi}{b}\,(b+c)-\frac{2\pi}{b}\,(-b+c)=4\pi\,,

(12)

where
c
has necessarily dropped out.

\mathbf{D=4}
: Now let
M_{4}
be a compact four-manifold without boundary,
and
\Phi=\Phi_{4}+\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{0}
be an equivariantly closed polyform,
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi=0
. The BVAB formula in this case gives

\displaystyle\int_{M_{4}}\Phi_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{nuts}\,p}\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{b_{1,p}b_{2,p}}\Phi_{0}|_{p}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\text{bolts}\,\Sigma}\frac{2\pi}{b_{\Sigma}}\int_{\Sigma}\Big[\Phi_{2}-\frac{2\pi}{b_{\Sigma}}\Phi_{0}\,c_{1}(N)\Big]\,.

(13)

On a four-manifold the fixed point set
\mathcal{F}
consists of isolated fixed points
p

(called
nuts
) and fixed two-dimensional surfaces
\Sigma
(called
bolts
)
Gibbons:1979xm
:

•

At an isolated nut
p
, the linear action of
\xi
on the tangent space splits
TM_{4}|_{p}=\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with weights
b_{i}
on each factor
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{i}
,
i=1,2
. One can introduce plane polar coordinates for each
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{i}
and write

\xi=b_{1}\partial_{\varphi_{1}}+b_{2}\partial_{\varphi_{2}}
near the fixed point.
Equivalently, in an orthonormal frame at the nut we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}|_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}0&b_{1}&0&0\\
-b_{1}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&b_{2}\\
0&0&-b_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(14)

so that
\mathrm{i}b_{1}
,
\mathrm{i}b_{2}
are the skew eigenvalues of this matrix.
Note that which weight is which is a convention, as is the orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{i}
, provided the orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
agrees with the orientation one uses to integrate.
In practice this means formulae should be invariant under
(b_{1},b_{2})\leftrightarrow(b_{2},b_{1})
,
(b_{1},b_{2})\leftrightarrow(-b_{1},-b_{2})
.

•

At a fixed bolt
\Sigma
,
the normal space in
M_{4}
to any point in
\Sigma
is a copy of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The orientation
of this
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is fixed from the orientation of
\Sigma
and the orientation on
M_{4}
. Near to the bolt we may then similarly
write
\xi=b_{\Sigma}\mskip 2.0mu\partial_{\varphi}
, where
\partial_{\varphi}
rotates

\mathbb{R}^{2}
with weight
+1
. Note that equivariant closure of
\Phi
gives
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{0}=\xi\mathbin{\rule[0.86108pt]{3.99994pt}{0.29999pt}\rule[0.86108pt]{0.29999pt}{3.87495pt}}\Phi_{2}
, and the
latter is zero at a fixed point. We deduce that the function
\Phi_{0}
is necessarily constant over a connected bolt
\Sigma
. The final term
c_{1}(N)
in (
II
) is the first Chern class of the
normal bundle
N
to
\Sigma
in
M_{4}
, where the choice of orientation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
makes this into a complex line bundle. In more concrete terms, near to the bolt we may introduce
plane polar coordinates
(r,\varphi)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and write the metric on
M_{4}
to leading order in
r\geq 0
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{\Sigma}+\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\varphi+\sigma)^{2}\,.

(15)

Here
\sigma
is a connection one-form for the normal bundle
N
, fibring
the circle with coordinate
\varphi
over
\Sigma
.
Then
\int_{\Sigma}c_{1}(N)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}

describes the twisting of this bundle.

The BVAB formula (
II
) again
relates the integral of the top form
\Phi_{4}
to the lower degree forms
\Phi_{0}
,
\Phi_{2}

and weights at the fixed points, together with a topological invariant.
When
M_{4}
has a boundary
\partial M_{4}
, the formula (
II
)
has a boundary term added to the right hand side
Couzens:2024vbn
:

\displaystyle I_{\text{BVAB bdy}}=-\int_{\partial M_{4}}\frac{1}{\|\xi\|^{2}}\xi^{\flat}\wedge\Big(\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}}{\|\xi\|^{2}}\Big)\,.

(16)

Here any metric, for which
\xi
is a Killing vector, may be used to compute the one-form
\xi^{\flat}
and the square norm
\|\xi\|^{2}
, where we have assumed that

\xi\neq 0
on
\partial M_{4}
so that the fixed point set does not intersect the boundary.

Localization of the action

Our aim in the remainder of this section is to evaluate the
on-shell action using the equivariantly closed polyform
\Phi
defined in
(
II
), together with the BVAB formula.

The total action is a sum of the bulk action (
1
)
together with boundary terms

\displaystyle I=I_{\text{bulk}}+I_{\text{bdy}}\,,

(17)

where

\displaystyle I_{\text{bdy}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{4}}\bigg[

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial M_{4}}2\mathcal{K}\,\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\partial M_{4}}(4+R_{h})\,\mathrm{vol}_{h}\bigg]\,.

(18)

Here the
boundary
\partial M_{4}
has induced metric
h_{ij}
, Ricci scalar
R_{h}
, trace of extrinsic curvature
\mathcal{K}
, and Riemannian volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{h}
.
The first
Gibbons–Hawking–York term in (
II
) is required in order that varying the bulk action (
1
)
with respect to a metric fixed on the boundary leads to the
Einstein equation in (
II
). The second term in (
II
) depends only
on intrinsic quantities on the boundary, and is a
counterterm that renormalizes the on-shell action
for asymptotically locally AdS solutions
Emparan:1999pm
.
More precisely, in (
17
) one should cut the bulk manifold
M_{4}
off
at some large but finite radius, evaluate the sum of the integrals, and then take the
cut-off radius to infinity. The result is always finite,
and independent of the details of the limiting process.

We have already seen that the bulk on-shell action (
10
)
may be expressed as an integral of the top form of the equivariantly closed
polyform
\Phi
in (
II
). We may then evaluate the full on-shell action (
17
) using the fixed point formula (
II
),
together with the BVAB boundary term (
16
).
It is a remarkable fact that
I_{\text{BVAB bdy}}+I_{\text{bdy}}=0
for an
arbitrary
supersymmetric asymptotically locally AdS
solution. This was shown by a direct calculation in
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
,
and generalized to include matter in
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
.
This cancellation is surely a direct result of supersymmetry, and one suspects
there is an argument that does not involve explicit expansions near the boundary of
M_{4}
.

The upshot is that the on-shell action receives contributions only from the fixed points
of the supersymmetric Killing vector:

\displaystyle I_{\text{on-shell}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\sum_{\text{nuts}\,p}\frac{1}{b_{1,p}b_{2,p}}\Phi_{0}|_{p}+\sum_{\text{bolts}\,\Sigma}\int_{\Sigma}\Big[\frac{1}{2\pi b_{\Sigma}}\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{b_{\Sigma}^{2}}\Phi_{0}\,c_{1}(N)\Big]\bigg\}\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,,

(19)

where
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{2}
are given in terms of bilinear forms in (
II
).
The expression (
II
) is very much in the spirit of

Gibbons:1979xm
, where holographic renormalization and supersymmetry
have led to a much sharper result.

Supersymmetry at fixed points

While (
II
) is a striking result, in practice we have
replaced an integral of
\Phi_{4}
by an integral involving
\Phi_{2}
and
\Phi_{0}
.
What is remarkable is that these expressions may be further evaluated in terms of weights and topological invariants of the fixed point set
\mathcal{F}=\{\text{nuts}\}\cup\{\text{bolts}\}
. In order to do this we need to look at the implications of
supersymmetry at fixed points.

We begin by noting the algebraic (Fierz) identity

\displaystyle S^{2}-P^{2}=\|\xi\|^{2}\geq 0\,,

(20)

where recall the definitions in (
II
). This implies

\displaystyle\xi=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad S=\pm P\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\gamma_{5}\epsilon=\pm\epsilon\,.

(21)

The fixed points of
\xi
are thus precisely points where the Killing spinor

\epsilon
is
chiral
, with
\gamma_{5}\epsilon=\pm\epsilon
referred to as positive/negative chirality. It follows that nuts and bolts have a chirality:
\text{nut}_{\pm}
, bolt

\Sigma_{\pm}
.

It is next convenient to introduce self-dual and anti-self-dual projections
of two-forms:

\displaystyle F_{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(F\pm*F)\,,

(22)

with a similar definition for other two-forms.
It follows that
F=F_{+}+F_{-}
,
*F=F_{+}-F_{-}
.
The equation for
\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}
in (
II
)
then has anti-self-dual/self-dual parts

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat})_{\mp}=\mp U_{\mp}\mp(S\mp P)F_{\mp}\,,

(23)

where here and in the following one should be very careful with signs.
On the other hand,
at a fixed point of positive/negative chirality

S=\pm P
, so that correlating the chirality and self-duality signs the second term in (
23
) is zero. Again, an algebraic (Fierz)
identity relates
\|U_{\mp}\|^{2}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2!}U_{\mp\mu\nu}U_{\mp}^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}(S\pm P)^{2}
. Combining (
23
) with (
14
) then gives

\displaystyle\mbox{At nut${}_{\pm}$}:\ \ \Phi_{0}=-SP

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{4}(S\pm P)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{4}(b_{1}\mp b_{2})^{2}\,.

(24)

Here the second equality simply replaces
S=\pm P
.
The value of
\Phi_{0}
at an isolated fixed point is thus entirely determined by the
weights of
\xi
at the fixed point, together with the chirality of the spinor.

For a bolt
\Sigma
we can use the same equations, but differently. Since
\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}
is an
exact two-form, integrating the equation for
\mathrm{d}\xi^{\flat}
in (
II
) over a
compact bolt
\Sigma
and using Stokes’ theorem gives

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Sigma}\left(*\mskip 3.0muU+S*F+PF\right)\,.

(25)

On the other hand,
\|U_{\pm}\|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(S\mp P)^{2}
implies that
on a bolt
\Sigma_{\pm}
one has
U_{\pm}=0
and hence
U=\mp*U
.
Equation (
25
) then allows us to evaluate the integral of
\Phi_{2}
in (
II
)
(which contains
U
rather than
*\mskip 2.0muU
):

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}\Phi_{2}=\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}2SF\,,

(26)

where the terms involving
*\mskip 2.0muF
simply drop out.

The final step involves evaluating the flux integral in (
26
).
The analysis in (
II
) also goes through for a bolt, where one of the weights is zero (the tangent direction to
\Sigma_{\pm}
) and

S^{2}=b_{\Sigma}^{2}/4
is constant. In
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
the orientation
conventions are chosen so that
S=-b_{\Sigma}/2
(implying that
b_{\Sigma}<0)
.
A global analysis of spinors near to
the bolt then leads to
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}F=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}\left[c_{1}(T)\mp c_{1}(N)\right]\,.

(27)

This is a topological argument: near to the bolt the spinor

\epsilon_{\pm}
is a section of
T^{1/2}\otimes N^{\mp 1/2}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{1/2}
. Here
T
and
N
are the tangent and normal bundles of
\Sigma_{\pm}
, and the gauge field
2A
is a connection on the complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. The latter follows from the spinor being charged under the
gauge field (the second term in (
3
)). Now
\epsilon_{\pm}

is a section of a complex line bundle over the bolt
\Sigma_{\pm}
, but we have also seen that its square norm
S=-b_{\Sigma}/2
is constant, and hence nowhere-vanishing. This implies that the complex line bundle is trivial and hence has zero first Chern class, which is precisely (
27
).
Note that the right hand side of (
27
) is quantized, being in general a half-integer. In particular

\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}c_{1}(T)=2(1-g)
is the Chern number of the tangent bundle of the Riemann surface
\Sigma_{\pm}
, written in term of the genus
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
of the surface.

Final formula

Substituting (
II
), (
27
) into (
II
) gives

\displaystyle I_{\text{on-shell}}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\sum_{\text{nuts}_{\pm}}\mp\frac{(b_{1}\mp b_{2})^{2}}{4b_{1}b_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\text{bolts}\,\Sigma_{\pm}}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(T)\mp\frac{1}{4}c_{1}(N)\bigg]\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(28)

This is the main result of
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
, which was re-derived using
equivariant localization in
BenettiGenolini:2023kxp
.
Notice that as well as being invariant under
(b_{1},b_{2})\leftrightarrow(b_{2},b_{1})
,
the formula is also invariant under

(b_{1},b_{2})\mapsto\lambda(b_{1},b_{2})
, for any non-zero constant
\lambda
.
This had to be the case as there is no natural normalization of
\xi
.

The formula (
II
) manifestly shows that the on-shell action is an “equivariant topological invariant”, depending only on weights of the supersymmetric Killing
vector
\xi
at fixed points, the chirality of the fixed points, and topological invariants.
This is not special to
D=4
,
\mathcal{N}=2

gauged supergravity: as we shall review in section
III
, a similar
formula holds also when the theory is coupled to vector multiplet matter.
Moreover, similar formulae hold with higher derivative corrections
added
Bobev:2020egg
;
Bobev:2021oku
;
Genolini:2021urf
, and for supergravity theories in other dimensions.

Examples

We now illustrate the general formula (
II
) with a number of simple examples. Although we do not need the metric and gauge field
to evaluate (
II
), we shall present these in some of the examples in order to help orient the reader.

Euclidean AdS
4
: The simplest solution is the vacuum solution, namely
Euclidean AdS
4
with zero gauge field,
A=0
.
The metric on global AdS
4
may be written

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{r^{2}+1}+r^{2}\left(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\cos^{2}\theta\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{d}\varphi_{1}^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{d}\varphi_{2}^{2}\right)\,.

(29)

Here
r\geq 0
is a radial coordinate, with constant
r>0
slices being three-spheres:

\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, and
\varphi_{i}
have period
2\pi
,
i=1,2
.
The space has topology
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with the Killing vectors
\partial_{\varphi_{i}}
rotating each
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{i}
factor. This is a maximally supersymmetric solution, and there is a four-dimensional family of spinors
solving (
3
). One can check
BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
that one choice leads to the supersymmetric Killing vector

\displaystyle\xi=\partial_{\varphi_{1}}+\partial_{\varphi_{2}}\,.

(30)

Here Euclidean AdS
4
has isometry group
SO(4,1)
, with the
SO(4)
subgroup
acting
\mathbb{R}^{4}
in the obvious way. The vector field (
30
) then generates
the diagonal
U(1)=SO(2)_{\text{diag}}\subset SO(4)
.
There is a single fixed point of
\xi
at the origin
\{r=0\}
, which is a nut of negative chirality.
From (
30
) we read off
b_{1}=b_{2}
at this nut
-
, and (
II
)
gives

\displaystyle I_{\text{AdS}_{4}}=\frac{(1+1)^{2}}{4\cdot 1\cdot 1}\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}=\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(31)

Using a different Killing spinor one can also construct a supersymmetric Killing vector with

\xi=\partial_{\varphi_{1}}-\partial_{\varphi_{2}}
, the anti-diagonal
SO(2)
in
SO(4)
. Now
b_{1}=-b_{2}
, and the nut has
positive chirality
\mathrm{nut}_{+}
. The on-shell action, computed from (
II
), of course gives the same final answer in (
31
).

Squashed Euclidean AdS
4
: Keeping the topology
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}
the same as the last example, one can imagine
more general supersymmetric solutions where

\displaystyle\xi=b_{1}\partial_{\varphi_{1}}+b_{2}\partial_{\varphi_{2}}\,,

(32)

with
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
now arbitrary parameters. The ratio

b_{1}/b_{2}
(or its square root) is often referred to as the “squashing parameter”

Hama:2011ea
, since for the various solutions cited below
the metric on the
S^{3}
radial slices is squashed/deformed, compared
to the round three-spheres in the AdS
4
metric (
29
).
Taking the origin to be a
\mathrm{nut}_{-}
, we may simply write down the on-shell action:

\displaystyle I_{\text{on-shell}}=\frac{(b_{1}+b_{2})^{2}}{4b_{1}b_{2}}\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(33)

This precisely matches the on-shell action of the explicit solutions found in

Martelli:2011fu
, where an anti-self-dual instanton gauge field
F=\mathrm{d}A
may be turned on while
preserving the AdS
4
metric. These solutions were
then further generalized in
Martelli:2011fw
;
Martelli:2012sz
;
Martelli:2013aqa
;
Farquet:2014kma
. There also exist solutions with a
\mathrm{nut}_{+}
at the origin,
where the prefactor is
-(b_{1}-b_{2})^{2}/4b_{1}b_{2}
. However, for the anti-self-dual
solutions in
Farquet:2014kma
, where the Weyl tensor is taken to be anti-self-dual,
it is shown that non-singular solutions
can exist only if certain inequalities on the parameters
b_{1},b_{2}
hold.
This is an important point: localization allows one
to compute various quantities assuming a solution exists, but existence
is a separate question. In particular, it is natural to ask whether existence imposes simple
necessary conditions on the supersymmetric Killing vector
\xi
.

Hyperbolic black hole
: Our final example in this section
is the static supersymmetric black hole studied in

Romans:1991nq
;
Brill:1997mf
;
Caldarelli:1998hg
. The metric and gauge field
in Euclidean signature are

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=V(r)\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{V(r)}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{\Sigma_{g}}\,,

\displaystyle V(r)

\displaystyle=-1+r^{2}+\frac{\frac{1}{4}-Q^{2}}{r^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{r}\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{2}A_{\Sigma_{g}}\,.

(34)

Here
\mathrm{d}s^{2}_{\Sigma_{g}}
is a constant curvature metric on a Riemann surface of genus
g>1
,
and
\mathrm{d}A_{\Sigma_{g}}=\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma_{g}}
is the volume form on
\Sigma_{g}
.

Taking the electric charge
Q>0
, the largest root
V(r_{0})=0
is
r_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+Q}
. For
r\geq r_{0}
the solution then has topology
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma_{g}
,
with
\tau
an angular coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in plane polars. The supersymmetric Killing vector is (necessarily)
\xi=\partial_{\tau}
, so that the fixed point set
\mathcal{F}=\{r=r_{0}\}\cong\Sigma_{g}
is a bolt, precisely the horizon of the black hole. The magnetic flux through this horizon is computed to be

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{g}}F=g-1\,,

(35)

in agreement with the general formula (
27
), where here the normal bundle
N
is trivial.

Further analysis of the solution may be found in the original references and

BenettiGenolini:2019jdz
, but the advantage of supergravity localization
is that one only needs to know the topology of the solution.
The formula (
II
) allows us to simply write down

\displaystyle I_{\text{BH}}=\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}(1-g)\,.

(36)

Note that the Lorentzian continuation of the solution, taking
t=\mathrm{i}\tau
,
has a naked singularity for
Q\neq 0
, and is extremal in the limit
Q=0
.
One can regard
Q\neq 0
in Euclidean signature as a regulator, moving
the horizon to finite distance.
, the on-shell action (
36
) is then independent of
Q
, and agrees with the expected entropy formula
S_{\text{BH}}=-I_{\text{BH}}
for a static black hole with zero electric charge.
The result also agrees with

Azzurli:2017kxo
, who compute
I_{\text{BH}}
by instead turning on a
finite temperature, which also moves the horizon to finite distance but
breaks supersymmetry. Taking the extremal limit using this method
agrees with (
36
).

III
Supergravity coupled to matter

In this section we couple
D=4
,
\mathcal{N}=2
minimal gauged supergravity to
vector multiplet matter, following

BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
.
We expect it to be straightforward to include hypermultiplets,
as was done in a near-horizon setting in
BenettiGenolini:2024kyy
.
We will be more brief in this section, highlighting the main formulae
and new conceptual points.

The theory

We couple the minimal theory of the last section to an additional
n
Abelian gauge fields,
so that in total we have
A^{I}
,
I=0,1,\dots n
, with field strengths
F^{I}=\mathrm{d}A^{I}
. Setting
n=0
returns us to the minimal theory, with
A^{0}=A
.
These vector multiplets also contain
complex scalar fields
L^{I}
, where in Euclidean signature the complex conjugate

\bar{L}^{I}\mapsto\widetilde{L}^{I}
is an independent field.
There is some redundancy in this description: simultaneously rescaling the
scalar fields by an arbitrary positive function leaves the theory invariant.
The theory is specified completely by the choice of a prepotential

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(L^{I})
, which is an arbitrary homogeneous degree two function of the scalars
L^{I}
, and constant real Fayet–Iliopoulos (FI) gauging parameters
\zeta_{I}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The superpotential and its tilded cousin are

\displaystyle W\equiv\zeta_{I}L^{I}\,,\quad\widetilde{W}\equiv\zeta_{I}\widetilde{L}^{I}\,.

(37)

The full Euclidean action may be found in

Bobev:2020pjk
;
BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
. This includes
a potential
\mathcal{V}
for the scalar fields, as well as kinetic terms for the
scalars and Maxwell fields.
There is a generalization of the Killing spinor equation in (
3
),
and an additional algebraic equation for
\epsilon
.
In particular we may define the bilinear forms exactly as in (
II
),
with the algebraic (Fierz) identities for these hence being the same as in the last section.

Equivariantly closed forms

One proceeds in the same way as for minimal supergravity, using the
relevant Killing spinor and algebraic equation for
\epsilon
. In particular one
can take covariant derivatives of the bilinears defined in (
II
),
obtaining generalizations of the differential equations (
II
).
One finds that
\xi
is again a Killing vector that generates a symmetry of the full solution.

Using these differential equations
we may then similarly construct equivariantly closed forms. For the
field strengths
F^{I}=\mathrm{d}A^{I}
one finds that

\displaystyle\Phi^{I}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{I}_{2}+\Phi^{I}_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv F^{I}+\sqrt{2}\left[L^{I}(S-P)-\widetilde{L}^{I}(S+P)\right]\,,

(38)

are equivariantly closed,
\mathrm{d}_{\xi}\Phi^{I}=0
. Here minimal gauged supergravity is recovered by setting
n=0
and
L^{0}=\widetilde{L}^{0}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
,
so that (
III
) reduces to
\Phi^{F}
in (
II
).
For the on-shell action,
\Phi
in (
II
) generalizes to

\Phi=\Phi_{4}+\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{0}\,,

(39)

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}\mskip 2.0mu\mathrm{vol}_{4}-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{I}_{IJ}F^{I}\wedge*F^{J}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}\mathcal{R}_{IJ}F^{I}\wedge F^{J}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(WU_{+}+\widetilde{W}U_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{I}_{IJ}\big[L^{I}(S-P)F^{J}_{+}+\widetilde{L}^{I}(S+P)F^{J}_{-}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{R}_{IJ}F^{J}\big[L^{I}(S-P)-\widetilde{L}^{I}(S+P)\big]\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{i}\big[(S-P)^{2}\mathcal{F}(L)-(S+P)^{2}\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{L})

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}(S^{2}-P^{2})\big(\partial_{I}\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{L})L^{I}-\partial_{I}\mathcal{F}(L)\widetilde{L}^{I}\big)\big]\,.

(40)

Here
\partial_{I}\mathcal{F}
denotes a partial derivative of the prepotential
\mathcal{F}

with respect to its arguments. Notice the self-dual and anti-self-dual projections
U_{\pm}
,

F^{I}_{\pm}
, which appeared also in minimal supergravity. The only objects
we have not defined are
\mathcal{I}_{IJ}
,
\mathcal{R}_{IJ}
, which enter the
action for the Maxwell fields (the second and third terms in
\Phi_{4}
). These are likewise determined entirely by the prepotential, where the formulae in Euclidean signature may be found in

BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
.
Taking the trace of the Einstein equation and substituting back into the bulk action, one again finds

\displaystyle I_{\text{bulk, on-shell}}=\left[\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{M_{4}}\Phi_{4}\right]\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(41)

One can thus evaluate this using BVAB (
II
), being careful with boundary terms.

Localization

The total action is again a sum of the bulk action (
41
)
together with boundary terms that generalize (
II
).
Unlike for minimal gauged supergravity, one can now construct
finite
boundary counterterms, and it is important to choose the coefficients of these so that the
regularization scheme is supersymmetric.
Moreover, as discussed in
Bobev:2020pjk
and appendix E of
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
, due to the presence of the scalars, in order to obtain a supersymmetric observable
one needs to take a Legendre transform of the on-shell action, as a function of certain boundary data for the scalars. We refer to this Legendre transform of the on-shell action as the “gravitational free energy.”
After a considerable amount of work expanding the equations near the conformal boundary
of
M_{4}
, one can show that the boundary
terms for this quantity all exactly cancel, for a general supersymmetric solution
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
! It is an important
question to understand conceptually why this happens, as discussed
in the last section.
The upshot is the remarkable fixed point formula for the gravitational free energy:

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\sum_{\mathrm{nuts}_{\pm}}\mp\frac{(b_{1}\mp b_{2})^{2}}{b_{1}b_{2}}\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}(u^{J}_{\pm})

\displaystyle\ \ +\sum_{\mathrm{bolts}\,\Sigma_{\pm}}\Big[-{\kappa}\mskip 2.0mu\partial_{I}\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}(u^{J}_{\pm})\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}F^{I}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\pm\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}(u^{J}_{\pm})\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}c_{1}(N)\Big]\Bigg\}\frac{\pi}{G_{4}}\,.

(42)

Notice immediately that this resembles the minimal result (
II
),
suitably “dressed” by the potential
\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}
. In (
III
) the latter is evaluated
on the following combinations of scalars at fixed points

\displaystyle\left.u^{I}_{+}\equiv\frac{\widetilde{L}^{I}}{\sum_{J=0}^{n}\zeta_{J}\widetilde{L}^{J}}\right|_{+}\,,\quad\left.u^{I}_{-}\equiv\frac{{L}^{I}}{\sum_{J=0}^{n}\zeta_{J}{L}^{J}}\right|_{-}\,.

(43)

Thus only the
L^{I}
contribute at negative chirality fixed points, while
only
\widetilde{L}^{I}
contribute at positive chirality fixed points.
By construction note that
\sum_{I=0}^{n}\zeta_{I}u^{I}_{\pm}=1
.
The constant
\kappa=\pm 1
is a convention-dependent sign, which is related to the choice of convention
mentioned above equation (
27
). The analogue of the latter is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{I}F^{I}=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\pm}}c_{1}(T)\mp c_{1}(N)\,,

(44)

with its derivation identical to that before.
The result for minimal supergravity is recovered on setting
\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}(u)=\frac{1}{8}
,
\mathrm{i}\partial_{u}\mathcal{F}(u)=1
, where
u=u^{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}
,
\zeta_{0}=4
,
and one takes
\kappa=+1
.
There is an analogous formula to (
II
) in the case with matter,
and an alternative approach to proving this is by considering the
Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\epsilon
of the spinor along
\xi
, and restricting
to fixed points. Since we will not need this for the examples below we refer the reader to
BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
for the details.

We note that the localization formula (
III
) gives a general proof of the “gravitational block” formulae for certain classes of black holes in
Hosseini:2019iad
, with the “gluing rules”
arising from localizing the integrals of
F^{I}
using the equivariant forms in (
III
). To see this simply requires applying the formula to rotating
black hole solutions, as discussed in
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
.

Examples

We focus on the STU model, where
n=3
and for which the prepotential is

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\mathcal{F}(L^{I})=2\sqrt{L^{0}L^{1}L^{2}L^{3}}\,.

(45)

The FI parameters
\zeta_{I}=1
for all
I=0,1,2,3
. Solutions to this theory
uplift on the seven-sphere
S^{7}
to solutions of
D=11
supergravity
Cvetic:1999xp
, and are holographically dual to the ABJM theory on
the conformal boundary
\partial M_{4}
.

Black saddle solutions
: Our first example is a generalization
of the hyperbolic black hole (
II
) to include matter.
The terminology “black saddle” means these should be viewed
as (putative) saddle points to the Euclidean gravitational path integral. In particular, unless

\widetilde{L}^{I}=\bar{L}^{I}
these solutions do not have good Lorentzian continuations, just
as for the hyperbolic black hole with
Q\neq 0
.
Various analytic and numerical solutions were found in
Bobev:2020pjk
.
The beauty of the general formula (
III
) is that this allows us
to recover the results for the gravitational free energy, without any details
of the solutions, but assuming they exist.

As for the hyperbolic black hole, we consider solutions with topology

M_{4}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma_{g}
. The supersymmetric Killing vector
\xi=\partial_{\tau}

again rotates the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
factor fixing the origin, which is the horizon of the black saddle.
We may define quantized magnetic charges

\displaystyle p^{I}\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma_{g}}F^{I}\,.

(46)

Due to (
44
), and setting
\kappa=+1
, these are constrained to satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{I=0}^{3}p^{I}=2g-2\,.

(47)

There is a single bolt, and the normal bundle
N
is trivially fibred, so only the second term in
(
III
) contributes. We write down

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}=-2\sqrt{u^{0}u^{1}u^{2}u^{3}}\sum_{I=0}^{3}\frac{p^{I}}{u^{I}}\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,.

(48)

Here the formula is formally the same for both chiralities of the bolt, where
u^{I}=u^{I}_{\pm}

respectively, and
\sum_{I=0}^{3}u^{I}=1
follows from the definitions (
43
).

In (
III
) and (
48
) the scalars
u^{I}_{\pm}
are evaluated
on the fixed point set. However, in holography one would like to express
quantities as functions of data on the conformal boundary

\partial M_{4}
. To see how this works for the gravitational free energy for the black saddles, consider now integrating
F^{I}
over the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
direction,
at some choice of point on the horizon
\Sigma_{g}
. One can always choose a gauge
so that on this
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have
F^{I}=\mathrm{d}A^{I}
for a global one-form
A^{I}
.
Then define

\displaystyle\Delta^{I}\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F^{I}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^{1}}A^{I}\,.

(49)

Here
S^{1}
is the boundary of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
on the UV conformal boundary

\partial M_{4}=S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}
. Likewise, we can define the boundary value

\displaystyle\sigma^{I}\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\Phi^{I}_{0}|_{S^{1}}\,,

(50)

where
\Phi^{I}_{0}
is given in (
III
). Then localization on
the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
gives the “IR-UV” relation

BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
;
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj

\displaystyle u^{I}=\Delta^{I}+\mathrm{i}\beta\mskip 1.0mu\sigma^{I}\,,

(51)

where
\beta
is the period of the Euclidean time circle
\tau
, with
\xi=\partial_{\tau}
.
The quantities on the right hand side of (
51
) are defined
on the conformal boundary
\partial M_{4}=S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}
, and
are thus part of the data that specifies the background that the dual
supersymmetric conformal theory is defined on. The left hand side
of (
51
) are the scalars at the horizon, which enter (
48
).
Thus importantly (
51
) allows us to interpret the gravitational free energy
as a function of boundary data
. Although we have described this for a particular example,
this generalizes for solutions of more general topology – see
BenettiGenolini:2024hyd
for
quite explicit formulae. Moreover, with this interpretation, (
48
)
precisely matches the large
N
limit of
the topologically twisted index of the ABJM theory on

S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}

Bobev:2020pjk
(for
g\neq 1
).

Black bolt solutions
: It is straightforward to generalize
the above analysis to the case that the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
factor is fibred
over the horizon
\Sigma_{g}
. This means that the Chern number

\displaystyle p\equiv-\int_{\Sigma_{g}}{c_{1}(N)}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(52)

is non-zero. Solutions with this type of fibred topology are known
as “Taub-bolt” solutions in the gravity literature. Such supersymmetric solutions to
the Euclidean STU theory are not known, although there
are explicit solutions in minimal supergravity constructed
in
Martelli:2012sz
;
Toldo:2017qsh
. From (
III
) we can again write down,
for positive/negative chirality bolts,

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}=-2\sqrt{u^{0}u^{1}u^{2}u^{3}}\left(\pm 2p+\sum_{I=0}^{3}\frac{p^{I}}{u^{I}}\right)\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}\,,

(53)

where the constraint on the magnetic fluxes (
44
) is now

\displaystyle\sum_{I=0}^{3}p^{I}=\mp p+2g-2\,.

(54)

As first pointed out in
BenettiGenolini:2024xeo
,
(
53
), (
54
) precisely match the large
N
field theory result
computed in
Toldo:2017qsh
.

This is an example where
supergravity localization allows one to compute a gravitational result,
and match it to field theory, when the gravity solutions are not actually known.
More importantly, localization allows us to compute explicit results for solutions that are very unlikely to ever be constructed in
closed form. A good example is the toric class of solutions discussed in

BenettiGenolini:2024hyd
.

IV
Discussion

We close with some questions, comments, and directions for future work:

•

From the growing body of work referenced in the introduction, it is
now clear that equivariant forms are a general feature of supergravity theories.
However, is there a general structure and construction? For example, can
equivariantly closed forms be built from components of supermultiplets in a systematic way?

•

Is there a “protected” sector of supergravity theories that equivariant
forms are capturing? Can one define a cohomological theory?
What other observables or invariants
can be computed? Is there a relation between the structure described here and the twisted holography programme
Costello:2018zrm
?

•

The formulae we have derived assume that solutions exist.
In turn, existence is a hard PDE problem. One might envisage proving
some simple necessary conditions for existence, based on physical
and/or mathematical arguments. The only general results of which we are aware are discussed in
Farquet:2014kma
;
BenettiGenolini:2024kyy
. Reference
Farquet:2014kma

studied the class of anti-self-dual solutions
to minimal gauged supergravity, where the Weyl tensor is assumed to be
anti-self-dual. For solutions with topology
M_{4}=\mathbb{R}^{4}
, with a single nut at the origin (see equation (
33
)), certain constraints on the allowed values of the weights
b_{1},b_{2}
at the nut
were derived for
non-singular
solutions. This used the differential equations in an essential way. Instead in
BenettiGenolini:2024kyy
(and for the uplifted solutions in
Couzens:2018wnk
) positivity
of the central charge and scaling dimensions rule out certain solutions.
These arguments presumably generalize, and it would be interesting to investigate this further.

•

We have focused on two-derivative supergravity, but as already mentioned
the result (
II
) is known to extend to four derivatives
Bobev:2020egg
;
Bobev:2021oku
;
Genolini:2021urf
. It would be interesting if this could be proven using localization, along
with the more general conjectures concerning all-deriviative results

Hristov:2021qsw
;
Hristov:2022plc
.
Most ambitiously, one might hope to localize the Euclidean supergravity
path integral itself, developing the ideas of
Dabholkar:2011ec
;
Dabholkar:2014wpa
.

•

As pointed out in
BenettiGenolini:2024lbj
, the results we obtain
for on-shell actions are in agreement with large
N
field theory results
even when there is no consistent Kaluza–Klein truncation. Specifically,
this is the case for general classes of three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories
Toldo:2017qsh
that
have
D=11
holographic duals involving a Sasaki–Einstein seven-manifold internal space. This could be explained by directly localizing in
D=11
.
We leave this, and other speculations, for future work.

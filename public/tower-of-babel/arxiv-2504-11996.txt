Rigidity of Serrin-type problems via integral identities Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
J. de Lima 1 , M. S. Santos ∗,1 and J. S. Sindeaux 2

Abstract
. In this short note, we deal with Serrin-type problems in Riemannian manifolds. Firstly, we provide a Soap Bubble type theorem and rigidity results. In another direction, we obtain a rigidity result addressed to annular regions in Einstein manifolds endowed with a conformal vector field.

1.
Introduction

The classical Serrin overdetermined problem
[
16
]
asserts that a solution exists to the boundary value problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(u)\ \ \hbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\hbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c\quad\hbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain,
f
is a smooth real function,
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
, and
c>0
is a constant,
if and only if
\Omega
is a ball and
u
is a radial function. Serrin provided the complete solution to this problem, presenting remarkable motivation arising from fluid dynamics. In this work, Serrin used a technique known as the Method of Moving Planes.

In
[
8
]
, S. Kumaresan and J. Prajapat have considered the Serrin problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) on the hemisphere and hyperbolic space, more precisely the authors show that the solution
u
is a radial function and
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

Another technique to solve the Serrin problem was presented by Weinberger in
[
18
]
, when
f=1
. Weinberger’s method involves defining a
P
-function associated with the solution to the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and applying classical maximum principles and the classical Pohozaev identity for domains contained in Euclidean manifolds. This method can be extended to Riemannian manifolds with some constraint on the Ricci tensor; however, this approach is only applicable to the particular case where
f(u)=1+nku
, see
[
7
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
13
]
for recent contributions about this subject.

The classical strategies to approach the Serrin problem is related to the demonstration of the celebrated Aleksandrov’s Soap Bubble Theorem, which claims the only compact embedded constant mean curvature hypersurface in the Euclidean space is a sphere.

We recall that the proof by Ros in
[
15
]
of Alexandrov’s theorem via integral inequalities, one crucial step is the
Heintze-Karcher inequality
that reads as follows: given an
n-
dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with non-negative Ricci curvature and given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset M
such that the mean curvature
H
of
\partial\Omega
is positive, then

(1.3)

\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{H}\geq Vol(\Omega).

Moreover, the equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to the Euclidean ball.

Motivated by this, we provided the following Heintze-Karcher inequality

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a manifold such that
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
. Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
), where
f^{\prime}\leq nk
.
If the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
is positive, then

\frac{(n-1)}{n}f^{2}(0)\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{H}\geq f(0)\int_{\Omega}f(u)

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
u
is a radial function and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

Motivated by Magnanini and Poggesi,
[
12
]
, we present the following Soap Bubble type result as follows:

Theorem 2

(Soap Bubble Theorem)
.

Let
M
be a manifold such that
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
. Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
), where
f^{\prime}\leq nk
and
f(0)>0,
then

\int_{\partial\Omega}(H_{0}-H)(u_{\nu})^{2}\geq 0,

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
and
H_{0}=\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{nc}
with
c
a constant given by (
2.4
). In particular, if
H\geq H_{0}
on
\partial\Omega
then
\Omega
is a ball (and, a fortiori,
u_{\nu}=-c
).

In another direction, we consider a P-function related to the problem (
1.1
), given by

P(x)=|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{u(x)}f(t)dt,

x\in\Omega.
This
P
function plays an important role in the Serrin problem, in fact, this is a subharmonic function on
\Omega
, since that
f^{\prime}\leq nk,
see Lemma
1
.

Now, let us recall that a warped product
M=I\times_{h}N
is a product manifold
I\times N
endowed with a metric given by
g=dt^{2}+h^{2}(t)dM
, where

is a metric on

for all

Motivated by Lee and Seo

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta u&=&-f(u)\quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega\\

\end{array}\right.

where
\psi(r)
is a positive increasing function on

,

As an application of the maximum principle on the function

Let

In the Euclidean manifold, Reichel

2.
Soap Bubble type theorem

Let us consider the following Serrin-type problem on bounded domains
\Omega
contained in a Riemannian manifold
M
as follows.

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta u&=&-f(u)\quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
u&=&0\quad\hbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function. We begin this section remembering the well-known
Reilly identity

\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{n-1}{n}(\Delta\rho)^{2}-|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\rho|^{2}\Big]\;\\
=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(h(\bar{\nabla}z,\bar{\nabla}z)+2\rho_{\nu}\bar{\Delta}z+H\rho_{\nu}^{2}\Big)+\int_{\Omega}Ric(\nabla\rho,\nabla\rho),

which holds true for every domain
\Omega
in a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
and for every
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, where
\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\rho
denotes the traceless Hessian of
\rho
, explicitly

\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\rho=\nabla^{2}\rho-\frac{\Delta\rho}{n}g\,,

\bar{\nabla}
and
\bar{\Delta}
indicate the gradient and the Laplacian of the induced metric in
\partial\Omega
,
z=\rho|_{\partial\Omega}

and
\nu
be the unit outward normal of
\partial\Omega
,
h(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\nu,Y)
and
H=tr_{g}h
the second fundamental form and the mean curvature (with respect to
\nu
) of
\partial\Omega
, respectively.

Now, we can state and prove our first integral inequality as follows.

Proposition 1
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
2.1
) such that
f^{\prime}\leq nk
, then

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega}{|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}[\mbox{Ric}-(n-1)kg](\nabla u,\nabla u)\leq-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}].

The equality holds if and only if
f(u)=f(0)+nku
.

Proof.

Reilly’s identity applied to the solution of (
2.1
) implies

\int_{\Omega}|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}Hu_{\nu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\mbox{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}.

Note that

(\Delta u)^{2}=-f(u)\operatorname{div}(\nabla u)=-\operatorname{div}(f(u)\nabla u)+f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}.

Thus,

\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}f(0)u_{\nu}+\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}.

Thus, from (
2.1
) and above equation we get that

\int_{\Omega}|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}Hu_{\nu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\mbox{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}f(0)u_{\nu}+\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}.

Taking into account that
f^{\prime}\leq nk
we get the desired inequality.

Now, supposing that the equality holds we have that
f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}=nk|\nabla u|^{2}
and, therefore,

|\nabla(-f(u)+nku)|^{2}=(-f^{\prime}(u)+nk)^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=0.

Since
u=0
along the boundary, we have that
f(u)=f(0)+nku.

∎

As a consequence of the above result, we get the following.

Corollary 1
.

Let
M
be a manifold such that
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
. Let
u
be a solution of (
2.1
), where
f^{\prime}\leq nk
. If
u_{\nu}=-\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{nH}
, then
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

Proof.

Indeed, from our hypothesis and Proposition
1
we get that
|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}=0.

Since
f(u)=-f(0)-nku
we can guarantee that
\nabla^{2}u=-(\frac{f(0)}{n}+ku)g.
Finally from a slightly modification of Obata-type theorem in
[
6
, Lemma 6]
, we can conclude that
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nH}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)^{2}f^{2}(0)}{nH}+u_{\nu}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}]+u_{\nu}(n-1)f(0),

we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{n^{2}H}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}]^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{n}\right)^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{H}+\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}.

On the other hand,

\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}=-\int_{\Omega}f(u).

Then, from above equalities and by (
2.2
) we conclude the desired result. If the equality holds, then
\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u=0
and, therefore, from
[
6
]
,
\Omega
is a metric ball and
u
is a radial function.

∎

Now, let us provide a proof of our Soap Bubble theorem as follows.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Firstly, note that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}Hu_{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(H-H_{0})u_{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{nc}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u_{\nu}+c\right)^{2}-2\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}-\frac{(n-1)f(0)c}{n}|\partial\Omega|+\int_{\partial\Omega}(H-H_{0})u_{\nu}^{2}.

From above equation and (
2.2
) we conclude that

(2.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}[(n-1)f(0)+nHu_{\nu}]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}+\frac{(n-1)f(0)c}{n}|\partial\Omega|-\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{nc}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u_{\nu}+c\right)^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(H_{0}-H)u_{\nu}^{2}\geq 0.

Now, taking

(2.4)

c=-\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}}{|\partial\Omega|}.

from (
2.3
) and (
2.2
) we obtain the desired inequality. Moreover, if
H\geq H_{0}
, the equality holds and, reasoning like the anterior results, we conclude that
\Omega
is a metric ball and
u
is a radial function.

∎

3.
Some rigidity result via P-function

Given a solution to the Serrin problem (
2.1
), let us consider the following
P
-function given by

P(x)=|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{u(x)}f(t)dt,

x\in\Omega.
Now, we can state and prove the following result.

Lemma 1
.

If
u
is a solution of (
2.1
),
f^{\prime}\leq nk
and
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
, then
\Delta P\geq 0
. In particular, if the equality holds
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

Proof.

Firstly, a straightforward calculation shows that
\nabla P=\nabla^{2}u(\nabla u)+\frac{2}{n}f(u)\nabla u.
From the Bochner formula we conclude that

\Delta P=2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle+\frac{2}{n}f(u)\Delta u+\frac{2}{n}f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since we are supposing that
u
is a solution of (
1.1
),
f^{\prime}\leq nk
and
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
, we get that

\displaystyle\Delta P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\frac{2}{n}(\Delta u)^{2}+\frac{2-2n}{n}f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}+2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{2(1-n)}{n}f^{\prime}(u)|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}\geq 0.

On another hand, if
\Delta P=0
we conclude that the above inequalities are, in fact, equalities. In particular,

(-f^{\prime}(u)+nk)|\nabla u|^{2}=0.

Thus, we have that
f(u)=f(0)+nku.
Since
|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}=0
, we can guarantee that
\nabla^{2}u=-(\frac{f(0)}{n}+ku)g.
Finally from
[
6
]
, we can conclude that
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

∎

Given
u
a solution of the problem (
2.1
) and let
\Gamma
be a connected component of
\partial\Omega,
we define the
generalized normalized wall shear stress
of

\Gamma
as

\tau(\Gamma)=\frac{\max_{\partial\Gamma}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{0}^{u_{\max}}f(t)dt}.

We observe that Espinar and Marín show that the solution of the eigenvalue problem
\Delta u=-2u,
on the 2-dimensional sphere, is a radial function and the domain
\Omega
is a geodesic disc, since that
\tau(\Gamma)\leq 1,
for all connected component of
\partial\Omega,
see
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
. Following the same ideas, Andrade, Freitas and Marín classify the solutions of the problem (
2.1
), in the particular case
f(u)=-1-nku
, in terms of generalized normalized wall shear stress, see
[
1
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
M
be a manifold such that
Ric\geq(n-1)kg
. Let
u
be a solution of (
2.1
), where
f^{\prime}\leq nk
. If

\frac{\max_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{0}^{u_{\max}}f(t)dt}\leq\frac{2}{n},

where
u_{\max}
denotes the maximum value of the function
u,
then
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

Proof.

Indeed, since
P(x)=|\nabla u|^{2}(x)+\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{u(x)}f(t)dt
,
x\in\Omega,
and
u=0
along of the boundary we get that

\max_{\partial\Omega}P=\max_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{u_{\max}}f(t)dt.

Taking the point
p_{0}\in\Omega
such that
\max_{\Omega}u=u_{\max}=u(p_{0})
we conclude that
P(p_{0})=\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{u_{\max}}f(t)dt.

Thus, since
P
is subharmonic function, we conclude by the maximum principle that

P
is a constant and, therefore,
u
is radial and
\Omega
is a metric ball.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

From Lemma
1
, we get that
\Delta P\geq 0,
thus from Hopf maximum principle
P_{\nu}\geq 0
on the boundary. Now, suppose by contradiction that
P
is not a constant. Note that

P=|\nabla u|^{2}=\psi^{2}(r)
along the boundary and since
\psi
is increasing then
\max_{\partial\Omega}P=\psi^{2}(R).

Note that
P_{\nu}=2u_{\nu}\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)+\frac{2}{n}f(0)u_{\nu}
. Thus, from the overdetermined conditions we get that

P_{\nu}=-2\psi(r)\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)-\frac{2}{n}f(0)\psi(r).

It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle Hu_{\nu}=|\nabla u|\operatorname{div}\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}=\Delta u-\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)=-f(0)-\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu).

Let

be a point on the boundary such that
P(q)=\max_{\partial\Omega}P=\psi^{2}(R).
From above equalities we get at

0<P_{\nu}(q)=-2\psi(R)(-f(0)+H(q)\psi(R))-\frac{2}{n}f(0)\psi(R).

Thus,

(3.1)

H(q)<\frac{(n-1)f(0)}{n\psi(R)}.

p
q
\Omega
\partial B_{R}(p)

Taking into account that the mean curvature of the geodesic spheres of
M
as given by

, we conclude from the tangency principle that

From (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta u+nku&=&-n\quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
u&=&a\quad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{1}\\
u&=&0\quad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{0},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
\partial\Omega_{0}=\Gamma_{0},

\partial\Omega_{1}=\Gamma_{1}
and
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{0}.

\Gamma_{1}
\Gamma_{0}
\Omega

Again, as an application of the Reilly formula, we have the following integral identity

Theorem 6
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
4.1
), then

\int_{\Omega}{|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}[\mbox{Ric}-(n-1)kg](\nabla u,\nabla u)=-\int_{\Gamma_{0}}u_{\nu}[(n-1)+H_{0}u_{\nu}]-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}u_{\nu}(H_{1}u_{\nu}+(n-1)(ak+1)),

where
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
are the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
respectively.

Proof.

Reilly’s identity applied to the solution of (
4.1
) implies

(4.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}u|^{2}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}Hu_{\nu}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\mbox{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}.

Now, we note that from the divergence theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(n+nku)\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\nu}+nk\int_{\Omega}(\frac{\Delta u^{2}}{2}-|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\int_{\Gamma_{0}}u_{\nu}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}(nu_{\nu}+nkau_{\nu})-nk\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}

Plugging the above equality in (
4.2
) we obtain the result.

∎

Now, let us consider the following overdetermined problem over bounded annular domains
\Omega
contained in a manifold
M
, endowed with a conformal vector field
X
, as follows

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta u+nku=-n&\hbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
u=a,\quad u_{\nu}=c_{1}&\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{1}\\
u=0,\quad u_{\nu}=c_{0}&\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{0},\end{cases}

where

and

The next result is a Minkowski-type formula addressed to Einstein manifolds endowed with a conformal vector field
X.

Proposition 2
.

Let
M
be an Einstein manifold with
Ric=(n-1)kg
and endowed with a closed conformal vector field
X
. Let
u
be a solution of the Serrin problem (
4.3
), then

\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left(\frac{u_{\nu}H_{0}}{(n-1)}+1\right)\langle X,\nu\rangle=-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\left(1+\frac{u_{\nu}H_{1}}{n-1}+ak\right)\langle X,\nu\rangle,

where
H_{i}
is the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{i}.

Proof.

Firstly, since
X
is a closed conformal vector field there exists a smooth function
\varphi
such that
\operatorname{div}(X)=n\varphi
and
\Delta\varphi=-nk\varphi,
see
[
9
]
.
Taking into account that
\Delta u=-n-nku
we conclude that

(4.4)

\int_{\Omega}(u\Delta\varphi-\varphi\Delta u)=n\int_{\Omega}\varphi=\int_{\partial\Omega}\langle X,\nu\rangle.

Now, we also note that from the divergence theorem

\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(u\nabla\varphi-\varphi\nabla u)=-\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\varphi u_{\nu}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}a\varphi_{\nu}-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\varphi u_{\nu}

A straightforward calculation shows that
\frac{n-1}{n}\operatorname{div}X=\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}X^{T}+H_{i}\langle X,\nu\rangle,
where
H_{i}
denotes the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{i}.
Thus, from divergence theorem we get that:

\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\varphi=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}H_{i}\langle X,\nu\rangle

From above equations, we deduce that

(4.5)

\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(u\nabla\varphi-\varphi\nabla u)=-\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}H_{0}u_{\nu}\langle X,\nu\rangle+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}a\varphi_{\nu}-\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}H_{1}\langle X,\nu\rangle u_{\nu}.

On another hand, since
M
is endowed with a closed vector field
X,
we have that

\nabla_{Y}X=\varphi Y,
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Thus, the curvature tensor

\displaystyle R(u,v)X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,\nabla\varphi\rangle v-\langle v,\nabla\varphi\rangle u,

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Then
\operatorname{Ric}(X,\cdot)=-(n-1)\nabla\varphi
.

Thus, since
M
is Einstein, we have that
-(n-1)\varphi_{\nu}=\frac{R}{n}\langle X,\nu\rangle
and, therefore,
\varphi_{\nu}=-k\langle X,\nu\rangle.
Finally, from (
4.5
) and (
4.4
), we obtain the desired result.

∎

We recall that a domain is called star-shaped with respect to
p
if each component of the boundary
\partial\Omega
can be written as a graph over a geodesic sphere with center
p.

Given a domain
\Omega
, endowed with a closed vector field
X
, such that
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}
, if
\partial\Omega
is star-shaped manifold let us assume that
\langle X,\nu\rangle>0
, on
\Gamma_{0}
, and
\langle X,\nu\rangle<0
, on
\Gamma_{1}
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Since
\Omega
has constant scalar curvature,
\partial\Omega
is a star-shaped manifold and

u_{\nu}H_{1}\geq-(ka+1)(n-1)
and
u_{\nu}H_{0}\leq-(n-1)
, on
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{0}
, respectively, from Proposition
2
, we have that

0\geq\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left(\frac{u_{\nu}H_{0}}{(n-1)}+1\right)\langle X,\nu\rangle=-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\left(1+\frac{u_{\nu}H_{1}}{n-1}+ak\right)\langle X,\nu\rangle\geq 0

Thus,
(n-1)+H_{0}u_{\nu}=0
and
(ka+1)(n-1)+u_{\nu}H_{1}=0.
From, Theorem
6
we obtain that
\nabla u
is a closed conformal vector field.
Finally, we conclude that
\Gamma_{i}
are umbilical hypersurfaces.

∎

Taking into account that the space forms can be seen as warped products we have the following result.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\Omega
be an annular domain contained in the Euclidean sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Suppose that
u
is a solution of the Serrin problem (
4.3
) such that
c_{1}H_{1}\geq-(a+1)(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
c_{0}H_{0}\leq-(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{0}
. If
\partial\Omega
is star-shaped manifold, then
\Omega
is the standard annulus
\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n};R<r(x)<R_{1}\}
,
u
is radial and given by

u(x)=\frac{1}{\cos(R_{1})}(\cos(r(x))-\cos(R_{1}))

Proof.

From Theorem
4
, we conclude that components of boundary
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
are umbilical hypersurfaces and, therefore, are geodesic spheres with radius
R
and
R_{1}
, respectively. Taking
p
the center of the geodesic ball
\Gamma_{1}
and a normalized unitary geodesic
\gamma
such that
\gamma(0)=p
we can introduce the function
f(s)=u(\gamma(s))
. Thus, a straightforward calculation shows that

f^{\prime\prime}(s)=-f(s)-1,

f(R)=a
and
f^{\prime}(R)=-c_{1}.

The general solution of the above ODE is given by
f(s)=x\cos(s)+y\sin(s)-1
. From initial conditions we conclude that

x=(a+1)\cos(R)+c_{1}\sin(R)
and
y=(a+1)\sin(R)-c_{1}\cos(R).

We recall that,
H_{1}u_{\nu}+(n-1)(a+1)=0.
Since the mean curvature is given by
H_{1}=-(n-1)\frac{\cos(R)}{\sin(R)}
on
\Gamma_{1}
, we get that

c_{1}=(a+1)\frac{\sin(R)}{\cos(R)}.

Thus,
y=0.

On the other hand, since
f(R_{1})=0
we conclude that
(a+1)=\frac{\cos(R)}{\cos(R_{1})}
and, therefore,

x=\frac{a+1}{\cos(R)}=\frac{1}{\cos(R_{1})}.

Then we conclude that
f(s)=\frac{\cos s}{\cos R_{1}}-1
and therefore,
u
is a radial function given by
u(x)=\frac{1}{\cos(R_{1})}(\cos(r(x))-\cos(R_{1})).
Taking into account that
u
is zero along
\Gamma_{0}
we conclude that
\Omega
is the standard annulus
\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n};R<r(x)<R_{1}\}
.
∎

As a consequence of the Theorem
4
we obtain a rigidity result addressed to annular regions contained in the hyperbolic space as follows.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\Omega
be an annular domain contained in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
Suppose that
u
is a solution of the Serrin problem such that
c_{1}H_{1}\geq(a-1)(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
c_{0}H_{0}\leq-(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{0}
. If
\partial\Omega
is star-shaped manifold, then
\Omega
is the standard annulus
\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n};R<r(x)<R_{1}\}
,
u
is radial and given by

u(x)=\frac{1}{\cosh(R_{1})}(-\cosh(r(x))+\cosh(R_{1})).

Remark 1
.

Given an annular domain
\Omega
contained in the sphere, a sufficient condition to guarantee that

c_{1}H_{1}\geq-(a+1)(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
c_{0}H_{0}\leq-(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{0}
is suppose that
c_{0}=-\frac{\sin R_{1}}{\cos R_{1}}
,
c_{1}=\frac{\sin R}{\cos R_{1}}<0
,
a<-1

and suppose that
\Gamma_{1}
is a geodesic ball with radius
R.
Indeed, given an annular domain
\Omega
contained in the sphere satisfying the above conditions, Lee and Seo, in
[
11
, Theorem, 2.5]
, shows that
the solution of the Serrin problem (
4.3
)
is a radial function and
\Omega
is a standard annulus, since that
\partial\Omega
is star-shaped. In their proof the authors used a
P
-function given by
P(u)=|\nabla u|^{2}+2u+ku^{2}
and shows that
P_{\nu}\geq 0,
along of boundary. We point out that

\begin{cases}P_{\nu}=-2u_{\nu}((a+1)(n-1)+u_{\nu}H_{1})&on\ \Gamma_{1}\\
P_{\nu}=-2u_{\nu}((n-1)+H_{0}u_{\nu})&on\ \Gamma_{0}\\
\end{cases}

and, therefore,
c_{1}H_{1}\geq-(a+1)(n-1)
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
c_{0}H_{0}\leq-(n-1),
on
\Gamma_{0}.

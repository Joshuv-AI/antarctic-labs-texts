The Incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier Limits from Boltzmann–Fermi–Dirac Equation for Low Regularity Data
Ning Jiang, Chenchen Wang, Kai Zhou

Abstract
. We consider the hydrodynamic limits of the quantum Boltzmann equation with Fermi-Dirac statistics for hard sphere and hard potentials in the whole space. By analyzing the spectrum of the linearized collision operator combined with the transport operator and its associated semigroup, the incompressible Navier–Stokes-Fourier limits from the BFD equation is verified rigorously. Compared to the results in [ 32 ] , this paper works with a lower regularity for the initial data. In addition, the fixed-point arguments together with a time iteration ensure us to obtain the lifespan of kinetic solution coincides with those of limiting fluid solution. Keywords: Boltzmann–Fermi–Dirac equation, Hard potentials, Low regularity, Spectral analysis, Incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier limits.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac Equation

We study the modified Boltzmann equation for Fermi–Dirac particles which obey Pauli’s exclusion principle. The motion of Fermi–Dirac particles satisfies the dimensionless Boltzmann–Fermi–Dirac equation (
briefly, BFD equation
)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{St}\,\partial_{t}F(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F(t,x,v)=\tfrac{1}{\text{Kn}}\,\mathcal{C}(F)(t,x,v),\quad(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}},

where“St” (the Strouhal number) describes the mean collision frequency in the equilibrium state, and “Kn” (the Knudsen number) is the ratio of the mean free path to the macroscopic length scale. The collision operator
\mathcal{C}
in (
1.1
) is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(F)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\Big(F^{\prime}F_{*}^{\prime}(1-F)(1-F_{*})-FF_{*}(1-F^{\prime})(1-F_{*}^{\prime})\Big)\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}v_{*},

where
F(t,x,v):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\longmapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
describes the probability distribution of particles at a given time
t\geqslant 0
, position
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
with velocity
v\in{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
.

Arising in the collision operator
\mathcal{C}(F)
due to the Pauli exclusion principle, the factor
1-F
stands for the probability that a fermion (such as an electron) does not lie a range of the velocity range
\mathrm{d}v
. In contrast, the factor
1+F
corresponds to bosons and appears in the Boltzmann–Bose–Einstein equation (
briefly, BBE equation
). However, qualitative analysis of the BBE equation is more challenging than that of the BFD equation since
1+F
leads to a blow-up of solution to the BBE equation in finite time
[
9
]
and the condensation phenomenon
[
17
]
.

Here and after, for any function
F(v)
we always denote by

\displaystyle F\equiv F(v),~F_{*}\equiv F(v_{*}),~F^{\prime}\equiv F(v^{\prime}),~F^{\prime}_{*}\equiv F(v^{\prime}_{*}),

and
v
,
v_{*}
and
v^{\prime}
,
v_{*}^{\prime}
represent the velocities of two particles before and
after their collision, respectively. During the collision, the conservation of momentum and energy for particle pairs can be expressed as

(1.3)

v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*}=v+v_{*},\quad|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}.

Then the velocities
v^{\prime}
and
v_{*}^{\prime}
after collision can be solved and represented by

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|\sigma}{2},\quad v^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|\sigma}{2},\quad\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

The collision kernel
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
in (
1.2
) is a non–negative Borel function depending only on the relative velocity
|v-v_{*}|
and the scalar product
\langle v-v_{*},\sigma\rangle
. We will consider the case where
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
has an explicit form as below.

In the weak-coupling regime,
B(|v-v_{*}|,\sigma)
can be mathematically expressed as (see
[
4
,
6
,
7
]
)

\displaystyle B\left(v-v_{*},\sigma\right)=\left|v-v_{*}\right|\left(\widehat{\phi}\left(\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|\right)-\widehat{\phi}\left(\left|v-v_{*}^{\prime}\right|\right)\right)^{2},

where
\phi
is the potential function and
\widehat{\phi}
denotes the Fourier transform of
\phi
. Physically, we consider an inverse power law
[
11
]
for the interaction potential, namely,

\displaystyle\phi(z)=|z|^{-p},\quad p>1.

In addition, the critical value
p=1
corresponds to the Coulomb potential. For
z\in{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
and
1<p<3
, we have
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=\widehat{\phi}(|\xi|)=C|\xi|^{p-3}
, for some
C>0
. For
\cos\theta=\langle\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|},\sigma\rangle
,
\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, the relationships from the
\sigma
-representation can directly lead to
|v-v^{\prime}|=|v-v_{*}|\sin\frac{\theta}{2}
and
|v-v_{*}^{\prime}|=|v-v_{*}|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}
. Then without loss of generality, the collision kernel can be written as

\displaystyle B(v-v_{*},\sigma)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}b(\cos\theta),\quad\gamma=2p-5\in(-3,1),~p\in(1,3),

where due to the symmetry structure of the scattering function
b(\cos\theta)
on
[0,\pi]
,
b(\cos\theta)
can be written as

\displaystyle b(\cos\theta)=\mathbf{1}_{\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]}\Big(\sin^{p-3}\tfrac{\theta}{2}-\cos^{p-3}\tfrac{\theta}{2}\Big)^{2}.

In summary, the collision kernel can be classified according to the range of
\gamma
and
p
:

(I)

When
\gamma=1
, the collision kernel is called
hard sphere
collision kernel and
b(\cos\theta)=1
;

(II)

When
\gamma\in(0,1)
, i.e.,
p\in\left(\frac{5}{2},3\right)
, the collision kernel is called
hard potential
kernel;

(III)

When
\gamma=0
, i.e.,
p=\frac{5}{2}
, the collision kernel is called
Maxwell collision
kernel;

(IV)

When
\gamma\in(-1,0)
, i.e.,
p\in\left(2,\frac{5}{2}\right)
, the collision kernel is called
soft potential
kernel;

(V)

When
\gamma\in(-3,-1)
, i.e.,
p\in\left(1,2\right)
, the collision kernel is called
very soft potential
or
non-cutoff soft potential
kernel. See
[
37
,
45
]
for more details.

In this work, we only consider the hard sphere and hard potentials, namely, cases (I) and (II) above. For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, integrating the corresponding scattering function
b(\cos\theta)
on
{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(\cos\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leqslant 2\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\sin^{2p-6}\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\cos^{2p-6}\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\tfrac{2^{4-p}\pi}{p-2}.

On the other hand, since
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}
for
\theta\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{3}\right]
, we get for some constant
C_{p}>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(\cos\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geqslant 2\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\left(3^{(3-p)/2}-1\right)\cos^{2p-6}\tfrac{\theta}{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta=C_{p}.

Thus we can define

\displaystyle A_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(\cos\theta)\,d\sigma,

and
0<C_{p}<A_{p}<\frac{2^{4-p}\pi}{p-2}.

Therefore, this case for
\gamma\in(0,1)
can be seen as the
angular cutoff
.

1.2.
Hydrodynamic Limits of BFD Equation

In the thesis of Zakrevskiy
[
44
]
, several fluid equations such as compressible Euler equations, compressible and incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations were formally derived from the scaled BFD equation under appropriate time and spatial scales. In particular, the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier limits was discussed in Chapter 3 of
[
44
]
. The current paper is devoted to rigorously justifying this limit.

We shall work with the small parameters
\text{Kn}=\text{St}=\varepsilon\ll 1
and we start from the corresponding scaled equation:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}F^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}F^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=&\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{C}(F^{\varepsilon})(t,x,v),\quad\text{in}~(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}},\\
F^{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=&F_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}(x,v),\qquad\qquad\,\text{in}~{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.\end{split}\right.

Generally, incompressible flows can be derived in the near-equilibrium framework. We then set the perturbation around the global Fermi-Dirac distribution
\mu
:

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=\mu+\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{q}}}f^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v),\quad F_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}(x,v)=\mu+\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{q}}}f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}(x,v),

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mu(v):=\tfrac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-1}},\quad\mu_{\text{q}}=\mu(1-\mu).

Now, for the simplicity of presentation, we introduce

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{obtain}}:=\mu^{\prime}\mu_{*}^{\prime}(1-\mu)(1-\mu_{*}),\quad\mu_{\text{loss}}:=\mu\mu_{*}(1-\mu^{\prime})(1-\mu^{\prime}_{*}).

Notice that
\mu_{\text{obtain}}=\mu_{\text{loss}}
due to the conservative law (
1.3
), we define the symmetric function by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}:=\sqrt{\mu_{\text{obtain}}}\sqrt{\mu_{\text{loss}}}=(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}})(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}})_{*}(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}})^{\prime}(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}})_{*}^{\prime}.

Putting the perturbation into the scaled BFD equation (
1.4
), we obtain the Cauchy problem

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}=&\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{L}f^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}(f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon})+\mathcal{T}(f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon}),\\
f^{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=&\,f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}(x,v),\end{split}\right.

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
, bilinear and trilinear operators
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{T}
are respectively given by

•

The linear operator
\mathcal{L}g
:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}g=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\tfrac{B\left(v-v_{*},\sigma\right)\omega_{\mu}}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}\left[\left(\tfrac{g}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}\right)_{*}^{\prime}+\left(\tfrac{g}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\tfrac{g}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}\right)_{*}-\tfrac{g}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}\right]\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}v_{*}.

•

The bilinear operator
\mathcal{Q}(f,g)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,g)=\mathcal{Q}_{1}(f,g)+\mathcal{Q}_{2}(f,g)+\cdots+\mathcal{Q}_{6}(f,g),

with
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{i}(f,g)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\tfrac{B\left(v-v_{*},\sigma\right)}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}q_{i}(f,g)\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}v_{*},i=1,2,\cdots,6
, and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &q_{1}(f,g)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)^{\prime}(1-\mu_{*}-\mu),\\
&q_{2}(f,g)=-\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)(1-\mu_{*}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}),\\
&q_{3}(f,g)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)(\mu_{*}-\mu^{\prime}),\\
&q_{4}(f,g)=-\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)^{\prime}(\mu_{*}^{\prime}-\mu),\\
&q_{5}(f,g)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)_{*}(\mu-\mu^{\prime}),\\
&q_{6}(f,g)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)(\mu_{*}-\mu_{*}^{\prime}).\end{aligned}

•

The trilinear operator
\mathcal{T}(f,g,h)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(f,g,h)=\mathcal{T}_{1}(f,g,h)+\mathcal{T}_{2}(f,g,h)+\cdots+\mathcal{T}_{4}(f,g,h),

with
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{i}(f,g,h)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\tfrac{B\left(v-v_{*},\sigma\right)}{\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}}t_{i}(f,g,h)\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}v_{*}
,
i=1,\cdots,4
, and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &t_{1}(f,g,h)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}h\right)_{*}^{\prime},\\
&t_{2}(f,g,h)=\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}h\right)^{\prime},\\
&t_{3}(f,g,h)=-\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}h\right)_{*},\\
&t_{4}(f,g,h)=-\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}f\right)_{*}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}g\right)^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}h\right).\end{aligned}

Furthermore, we introduce the symmetrized forms of the bilinear and trilinear operators as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\text{sym}}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{Q}(f,g)+\mathcal{Q}(g,f)),

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\text{sym}}(f,g,h):=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{6}\Big(\mathcal{T}(f,g,h)+\mathcal{T}(f,h,g)\Big)+\tfrac{1}{6}\Big(\mathcal{T}(h,f,g)+\mathcal{T}(h,g,f)\Big),

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{6}\Big(\mathcal{T}(g,h,f)+\mathcal{T}(g,f,h)\Big),

then we have
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{sym}}(f,f)=\mathcal{Q}(f,f)
and
\mathcal{T}_{\text{sym}}(f,f,f)=\mathcal{T}(f,f,f)
.

As is shown in
[
44
]
, we take inner products by (
1.6
) with
[1,v,|v|^{2}]\sqrt{\mu_{\text{q}}}
and let
\varepsilon\to 0
to formally obtain the incompressible Navier–Stokes-Fourier equations (
NSF equations for short
)

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}E_{2}\partial_{t}u+E_{2}u\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\nabla_{x}p=\nu_{*}\Delta_{x}u,\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot u=0,\quad\nabla(\rho+\vartheta)=0,\\
C_{A}\partial_{t}\vartheta+C_{A}u\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta=\kappa_{*}\Delta_{x}\vartheta,\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\nu_{*}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\beta_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\quad\kappa_{*}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\alpha_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-K_{A}\right)^{2}v_{1}^{2}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,

and the definitions of constants
E_{2}
,
C_{A}
,
K_{A}
and the positive functions
\alpha_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)
and
\beta_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)
can be found in Section
1.4
.

1.3.
History and Literature

As a modification of classical Boltzmann equation, the quantum Boltzmann equation was first obtained by heuristic arguments of Nordheim
[
40
]
and Uehling-Uhlenbeck
[
43
]
. However, unlike the classical ones, the rigorous derivation of the quantum Boltzmann equation from particle system is not yet well-established. Partial results considering the derivation of the quantum Boltzmann equation are presented in
[
41
,
16
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

We review some results concerning the fluid limits from quantum Boltzmann equation. One historical motivation for rigorously deriving fluid models from kinetic equations via asymptotic expansions comes from Hilbert’s sixth problem
[
30
]
. The earlier formal derivations of the fluid limits from kinetic equation can be found in
[
13
,
26
]
. Although there are numerous research results on rigorous verification of hydrodynamic limits from the classical Boltzmann equation, such as
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
28
,
31
,
33
]
and literature cited therein, the study on hydrodynamic limits from the quantum Boltzmann equation has only emerged in the past decade. For the fluid limits of BFD equation, Zakrevskiy formally derived in his thesis
[
44
]
the compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes limits and incompressible Navier-Stokes limits. We also mention that, Filbet-Hu-Jin
[
18
]
introduced a new scheme for quantum Boltzmann equation to capture the Euler limit by numerical computations. Motivated by Zakrevskiy’s formal analysis, Jiang-Xiong-Zhou
[
32
]
was firstly devoted to rigorously justifying the incompressible NSF limits from BFD equation for the hard sphere case in the near-equilibrium framework. In their work, they handled the trilinear structure of the collision term arising from the quantum effects and obtained the global existence of BFD equation (for hard potential case, see
[
37
]
). In the same framework, Jiang-Zhou
[
35
]
verified the acoustic limit of BFD equation. Later on, by analysing the nonlinear implicit relation between the BFD equation and the compressible Euler system, Jiang-Zhou
[
36
]
employed the Hilbert expansion, together with carefully establishing Grad–Caflisch type decay estimates for the inverse of linearized operator, to establish the convergence from BFD equation to the Euler system. In a same spirit of
[
36
]
, Jiang-Yang-Zhou
[
34
]
proved the compressible Euler-Poisson limits from Vlasov-Poisson-BFD equation. For BBE equation, He-Jiang-Zhou
[
29
]
proved its incompressible NSF limits under high temperature assumption.

The above results on fluid limits of kinetic equation are mainly based on the nonlinear energy estimates uniformly in Knudsen number
\varepsilon
and are in the classical/weak solution framework. Another way to obtain the fluid limits relies on the spectral analysis of the perturbed linearized collision operator (the scaled linear operator
\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{L}+\varepsilon^{-1}v\cdot\nabla_{x}
) and sharp estimates for its associated semigroup. For classical Boltzmann equation, this approach was developed by Grad
[
27
]
and Bardos–Ukai
[
3
]
. In Bardos–Ukai’s work, the key feature is that they only needed the smallness of the initial data, and did not assume the smallness of the Knudsen number
\varepsilon
. Meanwhile, Ellis–Pinsky
[
15
]
carefully studied the perturbed linearized collision operator mentioned above and performed its spectral decomposition. More results on related spectral theory can be found in
[
39
,
12
]
.

In recent years, certain research on fluid limits have resorted to spectral decomposition for classical Boltzmann equation. Gallagher-Tristani
[
20
]
employed this approach to find the connection between strong solutions of the Boltzmann and the Navier–Stokes equations. They shown that the life span of the solutions of the rescaled Boltzmann equation is bounded from below by that of the Navier–Stokes system. Gervais–Lods
[
22
]
presented a more modern spectral approach to achieve strong convergence from various kinetic equations for not too soft potentials to the NSF system under structural assumptions. Especially, based on the fact that the solutions of Navier–Stokes equation exist for initial data in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T}_{x}^{3})
, Carrapatoso–Gallagher-Tristani
[
10
]
firstly established a rigorous correspondence between kinetic and Navier-–Stokes solutions with low spatial regularity initial data and without any smallness assumption on the fluid initial data.

To our best knowledge, there are no results concerning the corresponding spectral analysis for quantum case. In the present paper, we investigate the properties of spectrum of the perturbed linearized collision operator for BFD equation with hard sphere and hard potentials collision kernel. Then in a same spirit of
[
10
]
, we set out to understand the hydrodynamic limits of the BFD equation in the whole space
\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}
. Compared to the previous results
[
32
]
by Jiang–Xiong–Zhou, this paper works without the smallness assumption on the macroscopic part of the kinetic initial data and provides a possible blow-up of the microscopic part of the kinetic initial data, which is characterized by
\varepsilon^{-\beta}
for some
\beta<1/2
. In addition, we need more refined estimates for trilinear terms (see Lemma
4.3
) than those in
[
32
]
. By using a fixed-point argument together with a time iteration, it turns out that in this work the smallness assumption on macroscopic part of the initial data is not needed.

1.4.
Notations and Main Results

Firstly, we make some conventions on symbols for convenience.

•

Unless specified, capital
C
is a generic constant and independent of any parameters. In addition, we use
C_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots}\equiv C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots)>0
when the constant
C
depends on the parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots
.

•

The relation ‘
a\sim b
’ means
C_{1}b\leqslant a\leqslant C_{2}b
.
a\lesssim(\gtrsim)\,b
means
a\leqslant(\geqslant)\,Cb
for some constant
C>0
.

•

For
l\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote the weight function by
\langle v\rangle^{l}:=(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{l}{2}}
. Particularly for
l=\gamma
, it is convenient to define the weighted Lebesgue space in
v
:

\displaystyle L_{\gamma}^{2}=\big\{f(v)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,|\,\|f\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}^{2}\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f|^{2}\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}v<+\infty\big\},

which is endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}
.

•

In this paper, the Fourier transformation always acts on
x
variable, that is,
\widehat{f}(t,\xi,v)
is the Fourier transformation of
f(t,x,v)
. Without causing confusion, we slightly abuse the notation
\widehat{f}(\xi)
for the Fourier transformation of
f(\cdot,x,\cdot)
. When more convenient, we will sometimes use the notation
\mathcal{F}_{x}f
for
\widehat{f}
.

•

\langle D_{x}\rangle=(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
. The standard Hilbert space
H_{x}^{s}
can be endowed with the equivalent norm

\|f\|_{H_{x}^{s}}=\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

We also slightly abuse notation and use

\displaystyle|D_{x}|=(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2},

to denote the Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|
. Similarly, the homogeneous Hilbert space
\dot{H}_{x}^{s}
is endowed with the equivalent norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}}=\left\{\begin{split}&\||D_{x}|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}},\quad s\in(0,1],\\
&\sum_{k=1}^{[s]}\||D_{x}|^{k}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\||D_{x}|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}},\quad s>1.\end{split}\right.

•

For any
m\geqslant 0
and
T>0
, we define the space
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}\left([0,T],H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}\right)
through its norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}\left([0,T],H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}\right)}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2m}\|\widehat{f}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T],L^{2}_{v}\right)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi.

To simplify the notation, we shall often write
L_{I}^{p}H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}
for
L^{p}\left(I,H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}\right)
and similarly
\widetilde{L}_{I}^{\infty}H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}
for
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}\left(I,H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}\right)
when
I
is an interval of
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. If
I=[0,T]
we will simply write
L_{T}^{p}H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}
and
\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}
. Finally, if
T=\infty
and in the absence of ambiguity, we write
L_{t}^{p}H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}
for
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{+},H_{x}^{m}L^{2}_{v}\right)
.

•

For convenience, we shall introduce some constants (see
[
32
, Section 4]
)

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &E_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\quad E_{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}|v_{1}|^{2}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\\
&E_{4}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}|v_{1}|^{4}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\quad E_{22}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}|v_{1}v_{2}|^{2}\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\\
&C_{A}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-K_{A}\right)^{2}v_{1}^{2}(1-2\mu)\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\mathrm{d}v,\quad K_{g}+1=K_{A}=\frac{E_{4}+2E_{22}}{2E_{2}}.\end{aligned}

For the following two functions

\displaystyle A(v)=\left(K_{A}-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\right)v,\quad B(v)=\frac{|v|^{2}}{3}\text{Id}-v\otimes v,

there exist unique functions
A^{\prime}(v)
and
B^{\prime}(v)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(A_{i}^{\prime}\right)=A_{i},\quad\mathcal{L}\left(B_{ij}^{\prime}\right)=B_{ij},\quad i,j=1,2,3.

In fact,
A^{\prime}(v),B^{\prime}(v)
can be solved and represented as

(1.13)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(v)=-\alpha_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)A(v),\quad B^{\prime}(v)=-\beta_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)B(v),

for two positive functions
\alpha_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)
and
\beta_{\mathcal{L}}(|v|)
, one can see the Appendix in
[
44
, Chapter 2]
for more details.

Now we are ready to present our main theorem, that is, the limits from BFD equation (
1.6
) to the incompressible NSF system (
1.11
) in the low regularity settings.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given the well-prepared initial data
(\rho_{\mathrm{in}},u_{\mathrm{in}},\vartheta_{\mathrm{in}})\in H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}^{3}})
satisfying the incompressibility and Boussinesq relation:

\nabla_{x}\cdot u_{\mathrm{in}}=0,\qquad\rho_{\mathrm{in}}+\vartheta_{\mathrm{in}}=0.

Let
T
be given in Lemma
2.4
and
(\rho,u,\vartheta)(t,x)\in\tilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{T}H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}
be the unique solution on time interval
[0,T]
to the NSF equation (
1.11
) with the above initial data. Let
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
and
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
be two real numbers. Consider the well-prepared initial data

(1.14)

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{in}}:=\Big\{u_{\mathrm{in}}\cdot v+(\tfrac{K_{g}}{1+K_{g}}\vartheta_{\mathrm{in}}-\tfrac{1}{1+K_{g}}\rho_{\mathrm{in}})(\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{2}-K_{g}-1)\Big\}\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{q}}},

where the constant
K_{g}
is given in (
1.12
), and we denote by

(1.15)

\displaystyle g:=\{\rho+u\cdot v+\vartheta(\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{2}-K_{g})\}\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{q}}}.

Then for a family of functions
f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}=\mathbf{P}_{0}f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}
satisfying

•

\mathbf{P}_{0}f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}=\psi(\varepsilon^{\alpha}|D_{x}|)g_{\mathrm{in}}
, for some smooth compactly supported function
\psi
;

•

For any
\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}\in(\Ker\,\mathcal{L})^{\perp}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\beta}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2}}\longrightarrow 0,\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

there is a
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) admits a unique solution

f^{\varepsilon}\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2},\quad\tfrac{3}{2}<\ell\leqslant 2,

with
\mathbf{P}_{0}f^{\varepsilon}\in L_{T}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{\ell}L^{2}_{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f^{\varepsilon}\in L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{\ell}L^{2}_{\gamma}
. Moreover, the solution
f^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\varepsilon}-g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}(f^{\varepsilon}-g)\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L^{2}_{v}}+\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}(f^{\varepsilon}-g)\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\longrightarrow 0,\,\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
shows that based on fluid solutions
(\rho,u,\theta)
with low regularity, we can construct a kinetic solution
f^{\varepsilon}
with higher regularity. Furthermore, as the Knudsen number
\varepsilon
tends to zero, we prove that
f^{\varepsilon}
converges strongly in
\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}
to the infinitesimal Maxwellian
g
generated by
(\rho,u,\vartheta)
.

Remark 1.3
.

This work focuses mainly on the hydrodynamic limits from the BFD equation under low regularity initial data setting. To lay stress on low regularity setting, we treat on purpose the case that the initial data is well prepared for simplicity. Indeed, there is mature method to handle the case that the initial data is not well prepared.

1.5.
Organization of this Paper

In the next Section, we recall the properties of the linearized collision operator
\mathcal{L}
. We then analyze the spectral structure of its small perturbations in Fourier space and study the associated semigroup. In Section 3, we outline the strategy for the proof of the main theorem. Specifically, we perform spectral analysis and semigroup decomposition and study the difference equation obtained by taking the difference between the Duhamel formulations of two systems satisfied by the kinetic solution
f^{\varepsilon}
and the fluid solution
g^{\varepsilon}
respectively. A basic fixed-point lemma is then applied to this difference equation to obtain existence. The main difficulty lies in verifying the contraction condition of the linear operator. In fact, the smallness of
\varepsilon
cannot be freely used. To address this, we construct a time-local iterative scheme, which ensures that the conditions in the fixed-point lemma are satisfied. In the last section, we focus on proving Proposition
3.2
. We show that the linear operator, the quadratic and the cubic nonlinear operators satisfy the assumptions of the fixed-point lemma.

Lemma 2.1

For

•

The linearized collision operator

Lemma 2.3

Eigenvalues.

•

•

For

such that the spectrum is at a positive distance from

(2)

(3)

sufficiently small, it holds that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|g^{\varepsilon}-g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g^{\varepsilon}-g\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{\gamma}^{2}}{\longrightarrow}0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, for
m>\frac{1}{2}
there are estimates for
g^{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{m}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{m+1}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{H_{x}^{m}L_{v}^{2}}\exp\left(C\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}^{2}\right).

In particular,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{m}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{m+1}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\alpha\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}\exp\left(C\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}^{2}\right),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{4}H_{x}^{m+\frac{1}{2}}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\alpha\left(m-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}\exp\left(C\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}^{2}\right).

Proof.

We refer to Page 12 in
[
10
]
for the proof, where we have used that
\mathbf{P}_{0}:L^{2}_{v}\to L_{\gamma}^{2}\subset L^{2}_{v}
is bounded and hence

(2.13)

\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}=\|\mathbf{P}_{0}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}\lesssim|\mathbf{P}_{0}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

The estimate (
2.12
) is a consequence of a simple interpolation inequality, that is,

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{4}H_{x}^{n}}\lesssim\|h\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h\|_{L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}},\;n\geqslant\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

In fact, Lemma
2.4
shows that the lifespan of
g^{\varepsilon}
is
[0,T^{\varepsilon}]
for some
T^{\varepsilon}>0
. However, one can show that the lifespan of
g^{\varepsilon}
is actually at least that of the limit
g
. This statement is presented at the very beginning of Section 2 in
[
21
]
.

3.
Sketch of the Proof of Theorem
1.1

The aim of this work is to rigorously justify the convergence from the quantum kinetic solution
f^{\varepsilon}
to the fluid solution
g
in space
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}
. Since we already have (
2.10
), it is enough to show

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\varepsilon}-g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}{\longrightarrow}0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

for the given well-prepared initial data
g_{\mathrm{in}}
defined by (
1.14
).

Now we define the difference
\delta^{\varepsilon}
by
\delta^{\varepsilon}(t):=f^{\varepsilon}(t)-g^{\varepsilon}(t)
, where
f^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(t)=U^{\varepsilon}(t)f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}+\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon}](t)+\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon},f^{\varepsilon}](t),

and just like (
2.7
),
g^{\varepsilon}(t)
also satisfies

(3.3)

\displaystyle g^{\varepsilon}(t)=U_{\mathrm{NSF}}(t)\mathbf{P}_{0}f^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathfrak{Q}_{\mathrm{NSF}}[g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t).

Taking the difference between (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get the equation for
\delta^{\varepsilon}(t)
:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\delta^{\varepsilon}(t)=D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t)+\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t)+\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon}](t)+\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t)+\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t),

where

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t):=(U^{\varepsilon}(t)-U_{\mathrm{NSF}}(t))\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}+U^{\varepsilon}(t)\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t):=\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t)-\mathfrak{Q}_{\mathrm{NSF}}[g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t)+\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t),\\
&\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon}](t):=2\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t)+3\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t),\\
&\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t):=\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t)+3\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}](t).\end{aligned}

Then we should prove the difference equation (
3.4
) has a unique solution and the solution
\delta^{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For the existence, we have the following fixed point lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Banach space,
L:\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
be a continuous linear operator,
B:\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
be a bilinear operator, and
\Psi:\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
be a trilinear operator. Define norms of operators:

\displaystyle\|L\|:=\sup_{\|y\|=1}\|L(y)\|,\quad\|B\|:=\sup_{\|y\|=\|z\|=1}\|B(y,z)\|,

\displaystyle\|\Psi\|:=\sup_{\|y\|=\|z\|=\|u\|=1}\|\Psi(y,z,u)\|.

If
\|L\|<1/2
, then for any
y_{0}\in\mathscr{X}
such that

\|y_{0}\|\leqslant\frac{1/2-\|L\|}{4\|B\|+\sqrt{6\|\Psi\|}},

the equation
y=y_{0}+L(y)+B(y,y)+\Psi(y,y,y)
admits a unique solution in the ball of center
0
and radius
\frac{1-2\|L\|}{4\|B\|+\sqrt{6\|\Psi\|}}
. In addition, there holds that
\|y\|\leqslant C\|y_{0}\|
for some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

For any fixed
y_{0}\in\mathscr{X}
, we define the map
G:\,\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}
as

G(y)=y_{0}+L(y)+B(y,y)+\Psi(y,y,y).

Let
s>0
and
\|y_{0}\|\leqslant s/2
, then for any
y\in\mathscr{X}
with
\|y\|\leqslant s
we have

\|G(y)\|\leqslant\frac{s}{2}+\|L\|s+\|B\|s^{2}+\|\Psi\|s^{3}.

It is easy to see that for any
s>0
with

s\leqslant\frac{1-2\|L\|}{2\|B\|+\sqrt{2\|\Psi\|}},

one can get
\|G(y)\|\leqslant s
. Then
G
maps
B_{0}(s)
to
B_{0}(s)
for
s
satisfying the above condition.

Next for any
y,\,z\in B_{0}(r)
with
r\in(0,\frac{1-2\|L\|}{2\|B\|+\sqrt{2\|\Psi\|}}]
to be determined, we have

\displaystyle G(y)-G(z)=

\displaystyle L(y-z)+B(y-z,y)+B(z,y-z)

\displaystyle+\Psi(y-z,y,y)+\Psi(z,y-z,y)+\Psi(z,z,y-z),

it follows that

\displaystyle\|G(y)-G(z)\|\leqslant

\displaystyle\Big\{\|L\|+\|B\|(\|y\|+\|z\|)+\|\Psi\|(\|y\|^{2}+\|y\|\|z\|+\|z\|^{2})\Big\}\|y-z\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(\|L\|+2\|B\|r+3\|\Psi\|r^{2})\|y-z\|,

Now we choose
r>0
with

r\leqslant\frac{1-2\|L\|}{4\|B\|+\sqrt{6\|\Psi\|}}<\frac{1-2\|L\|}{2\|B\|+\sqrt{2\|\Psi\|}},

to get
\|L\|+2\|B\|r+3\|\Psi\|r^{2}\leqslant 1/2
and hence
G
is a contraction map on a ball
B_{0}(r)
. We complete the proof by the fixed point theorem.
∎

To progress, for any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
we define an appropriate functional space
\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}
as

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{f(t,x,v)\mid\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}}<+\infty\right\},

though the norm

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}}:=&\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\\
&+\varepsilon^{\beta}\left(\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\left\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\ell}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\right).\end{aligned}

In order to apply the fixed point argument described above to prove the existence of (
3.4
) in the functional space
\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}
, it suffices to establish the following estimates, which are proved in the next section.

Proposition 3.2
.

Recall the notations
D^{\varepsilon}_{0},\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}
given by (
3.5
). Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, the following estimates hold:

(1)

For any
t\in(0,T)
and any function
F\in\mathscr{X}_{[0,t]}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|U^{\varepsilon}(\cdot-t)F(t)\right\|_{\mathscr{X}_{[t,T]}^{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathscr{X}_{[0,t]}^{\varepsilon}}.

(2)

For
D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|D_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}}\lesssim D^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

where

\displaystyle D^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{in}}:=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\varepsilon^{\beta}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{\textrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.

(3)

For the source term
\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t)
, there exists a nonnegative increasing function
\Phi
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathscr{X}_{T}^{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha}\Phi\big(\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{1/2}L_{v}^{2}}\big)\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

(4)

For all intervals
I
, the linear operator
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[f]\right\|_{\mathscr{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}}&\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}}(\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{4}H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\beta}\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{\infty}H_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\beta}\left\|g^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\ell}L_{v}^{2}})\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}(\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{5}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\frac{7}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}})\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}(\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{5}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2})\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}(\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell+1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}})\\
&\quad+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|g^{\varepsilon}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

(5)

For all intervals
I
, the bilinear and trilinear operators are continuous:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2}]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}.

With the above tools and estimates at hand, we now prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to our case, we set there that

y=\delta^{\varepsilon}\textrm{ and }y_{0}=D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t)+\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t).

Then from the second and third statements in Proposition
3.2
we know
y_{0}
is bounded and
y_{0}\to 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The estimates in Proposition
3.2
-(5) correspond to the bilinear and trilinear conditions in Lemma
3.1
. It remains to verify the contraction of the linear operator
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
.

For the scaling parameter
\varepsilon
, we can choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[f]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\varepsilon^{\beta-\alpha(\ell-\frac{1}{2})}+\varepsilon^{\beta-\alpha(\ell-\frac{3}{2})}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha(\ell-\frac{1}{2})}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\Big)\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\left\|g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{4}H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}\Big)\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big(\tfrac{1}{8C}+\underbrace{\left\|g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}_{I}^{4}H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2}}+\left\|g\right\|_{L_{I}^{2}H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2}}}_{=:G}\Big)\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}},

where we have used the estimate (
3.7
). It should be pointed out that the term
G
is explicitly bounded as in Lemma
2.5
and clearly,
G
is not small. To obtain the contraction of
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
, we estimate the term
G
by an irrational method on time, that is to show
G\leqslant\tfrac{1}{8C}
on each small time interval but not the whole interval
[0,T]
.

According to Lemma
2.5
, there exists
t_{1}
with
0<t_{1}<T
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{4}([0,t_{1}];H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2})}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}([0,t_{1}];H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2})}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{8C}.

Inserting (
3.11
) into (
3.10
) for
t\in[0,t_{1}]
, we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[f]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{4}\|f\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}.

Then by the fixed-point Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
\delta^{\varepsilon}
to equation
(
3.4
) on
[0,t_{1}]
satisfying

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}\lesssim\|D_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}+\|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

We now proceed to establish the irrational relationship. In view of the nonlinear terms in (
3.4
), by the property of semi-group, then for any
t\geqslant t_{1}
we can rewrite them as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{t}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2})(s)\mathrm{d}s\\
=&\int_{0}^{t_{1}}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2})(s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2})(s)\mathrm{d}s\\
=&U^{\varepsilon}(t-t_{1})\int_{0}^{t_{1}}U^{\varepsilon}(t_{1}-s)\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2})(s)\mathrm{d}s+\underbrace{\int_{t_{1}}^{t}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2})(s)\mathrm{d}s}_{=:\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2}](t_{1};t)}.\end{aligned}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{t}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})(s)\mathrm{d}s\\
=&U^{\varepsilon}(t-t_{1})\int_{0}^{t_{1}}U^{\varepsilon}(t_{1}-s)\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})(s)\mathrm{d}s+\underbrace{\int_{t_{1}}^{t}U^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})(s)\mathrm{d}s}_{=:\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}](t_{1};t)}.\end{aligned}

With this notation, the equation (
3.4
) can be written as

\displaystyle\delta^{\varepsilon}(t)=

\displaystyle U^{\varepsilon}(t-t_{1})\delta^{\varepsilon}(t_{1})+D_{02}^{\varepsilon}(t)+\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon}](t_{1};t)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{B}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t_{1};t)+\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon},\delta^{\varepsilon}](t_{1};t),

where

\displaystyle D_{02}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle:=D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t)-U^{\varepsilon}(t-t_{1})D_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t)-U^{\varepsilon}(t-t_{1})\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}(t_{1}).

Again by Lemma
2.5
, we can choose
t_{2}\in(t_{1},T)
such that

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{4}([t_{1},t_{2}];H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2})}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}([t_{1},t_{2}];H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2})}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{8C}.

Then Proposition
3.2
(1)-(3) and (
3.13
) imply

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|U^{\varepsilon}(\cdot-t_{1})\delta^{\varepsilon}(t_{1})+D_{02}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)+\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}}\\
\lesssim&\|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}+\|D^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}+\|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}\\
\lesssim&\|D^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}+\|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t_{1}}}\\
\leqslant&C_{1}\Big(D_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha}\Phi\big(\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{1/2}L_{v}^{2}}\big)\Big)\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0,\end{aligned}

where constant
C_{1}>0
. By Proposition
3.2
-(4), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon}]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}}.

From Lemma
3.1
and (
3.15
), there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}}&\leqslant C_{2}\|U^{\varepsilon}(\cdot-t_{1})\delta^{\varepsilon}(t_{1})+D_{02}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)+\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}}\\
&\leqslant C_{2}C_{1}\Big(D_{\mathrm{in}}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-2\alpha}\Phi\big(\left\|g_{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H_{x}^{1/2}L_{v}^{2}}\big)\Big)\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.\end{aligned}

We can repeat the process above
N
times for some
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
0:=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}:=T
with

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{\widetilde{L}^{4}([t_{N-1},t_{N}];H_{x}^{1}L_{v}^{2})}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}([t_{N-1},t_{N}];H_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{2})}\lesssim\tfrac{1}{8C},

and

\|\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}[\delta^{\varepsilon}]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}},\;i=0,1,2,\cdots,N-1.

In summary, there exists a unique solution
\delta^{\varepsilon}(t)
to the difference equation (
3.4
) and a positive constant
C(N):=\prod_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}
such that

\displaystyle\|\delta^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{T}}\leqslant C(N)\Big(\|D_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{T}}+\|\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{T}}\Big)\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

The convergence follows from Proposition
3.2
(2)-(3) and thus the proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in (
3.10
),
G
is not small enough to guarantee the contraction of operator
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
on
\mathcal{X}_{I}^{\varepsilon}
, unless the initial data
g_{\mathrm{in}}
is sufficiently small. By dividing the interval
I=[0,T]
into finite segments, one can get around this difficulty if there is no smallness assumption on
g_{\mathrm{in}}
. This idea comes from Carrapatoso-Gallagher-Tristani
[
10
]
.

4.
Proof of Proposition

In this section, we prove Proposition
3.2
. The key ingredient in the proof is the nonlinear estimates for bilinear operator
\mathfrak{Q}^{\varepsilon}
. In subsections
4.1
-
4.2
, we give the estimates for bilinear operator
\mathcal{Q}
and trilinear operator
\mathcal{T}
. These estimates will lead to the continuity estimates of semigroup
U^{\varepsilon}
and convergence of
D_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, that is, Proposition
3.2
-(1) and (2). In subsection
4.3
, we obtain the convergence of
\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}
and hence prove Proposition
3.2
-(3). In the last three subsections, we show that
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
and

are continuous on functional space

, and then Proposition

-(4) and (5) are proved.

4.1.
Estimates for
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{T}

We now give a class of useful estimates for the bilinear terms
\mathcal{Q}
and the trilinear terms
\mathcal{T}
. Firstly, we invoke the
L_{v}^{2}
estimates, which were proved in
[
32
,
37
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
f_{1},\,f_{2},\,f_{3}
and
h
in
D(\mathcal{L})
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{2}),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}|\lesssim(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}})\|h\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}},

and

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{T}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}|\lesssim(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}})\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|h\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}.

As a result,

(4.1)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}:&=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}\leqslant 1}|\langle\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{2}),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}|\\
&\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\gamma}^{2}},\end{split}

4.7
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}^{\varepsilon}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{\beta}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})(1+\varepsilon^{\beta})\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}.

By Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&2\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\\
&\quad+\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\\
&\quad+\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\\
\lesssim&\varepsilon^{-2\beta}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}.\end{aligned}

Due to the symmetric structure, we can directly deduce similar bounds for all permutations. Hence,

(4.60)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\quad\text{for}~\beta<\frac{1}{2}.

Applying Lemma
4.3
with
m=\frac{3}{2}
, and the embedding
\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}
, we. also have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}\beta}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}},

and

(4.61)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{T}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\lesssim&\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L_{\gamma}^{2}}\\
\lesssim&\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}\beta}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}.\end{aligned}

Similarly, they yields

(4.62)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}},\quad\text{for}~\beta<\frac{1}{2},

and

(4.63)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\beta}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{sym}}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}_{I}},\quad\text{for}~\beta<\frac{1}{2}.

We now consider the zeroth-order terms. By symmetry of the trilinear operator, we only present the proof for one part, as the remaining cases are analogous. Putting the decomposition
f=\mathbf{P}_{0}f+\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f
in
L^{2}_{v}
, we have

(4.64)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}+\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}+\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}+\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\

In what follows, we shall treat the terms successively. By Hölder’s inequality and Grgliardo–Nienberg inequality, we have

(4.65)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\Big(\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}^{6}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{6}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}^{6}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{6}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
\lesssim&\Big(\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Big|\nabla_{x}\Big(\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\Big)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
&\quad+\Big(\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Big|\nabla_{x}\Big(\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\Big)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.\end{aligned}

Applying Lemma
4.4
, we have

(4.66)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big(\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\Big|\nabla_{x}\Big(\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\Big)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\
\lesssim&\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{I}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
\lesssim&\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}}.\end{aligned}

Putting (
4.66
) into (
4.65
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}}\|f_{2}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}}\|f_{3}\|_{\mathscr{X}^{\varepsilon}}.

Next, we apply Lemma
4.3
with
m=0
, and then deal with each term on the right-hand side of the above expression by choosing suitable parameters
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i},r_{i},\delta_{i}),\,i=1,\cdots,5
, respectively obtaining the following estimates:

(4.68)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\
&\quad+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{3}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\

&\quad+\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\

Besides, we have

(4.70)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{T}[\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2},\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}]\|_{L^{2}_{I}L^{2}_{x}(L_{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\|\mathbf{P}_{0}f_{1}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{2}\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\mathbf{P}_{0}^{\perp}f_{3}\|_{L^{2}_{I}H^{\ell}_{x}L^{2}_{\gamma}}\\

(4.75)

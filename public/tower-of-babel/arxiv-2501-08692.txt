Abstract
We prove the Σ 1 \Sigma^{1} -conjecture for two families of Artin groups: Artin groups such that there exists a prime number p p dividing l ⁡ ( e ) 2 \frac{l(e)}{2} for every edge e e with even label > 2 >2 and balanced Artin groups. The family of balanced Artin groups extends two previously studied families: the one considered by Kochloukova in [ 16 ] and the family of coherent Artin groups. We state a conjecture on the Σ 2 \Sigma^{2} -invariant for Artin groups satisfying the K ⁡ ( π , 1 ) K(\pi,1) -conjecture. The conjecture is proven to be true for two significant families: 2 2 -dimensional and coherent Artin groups. In the 2 2 -dimensional case we are able to compute Σ n \Sigma^{n} for all n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and to derive finiteness properties of the derived subgroup.

Introduction

The class of Artin groups is one of the most prominent and extensively studied families of groups in geometric group theory. These groups are investigated from algebraic, geometric, and combinatorial perspectives. An Artin group is defined as follows: given a simplicial graph
\Gamma
, i.e. a finite graph with no double edges nor loops, with vertex set
V(\Gamma)
, edge set
E(\Gamma)
and with a labelling function
l:E(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
the associated
Artin group
is given by the following presentation:

A_{\Gamma}=\left\langle v\in V(\Gamma)\,\middle|\,\underset{l(e)\text{ letters}}{\underbrace{uvu\dots}}=\underset{l(e)\text{ letters}}{\underbrace{vuv\dots}}~\text{ for all }~e=\{u,v\}\in E(\Gamma)\right\rangle

Artin groups generalize several important families of groups, including free groups, free abelian groups, braid groups and right-angled Artin groups. A key feature of Artin groups is that many of their algebraic properties can often be described in terms of the combinatorial structure of the defining graph
\Gamma
.

Given a finitely generated group
G
, one can define a family of geometric invariants known as the
\Sigma
-invariants or Bieri-Neumann-Strebel-Renz (BNSR) invariants. These were first introduced by Bieri-Neumann-Strebel in
[
6
]
and Bieri-Renz in
[
5
]
. The invariants are denoted as
\Sigma^{n}(G)
or
\Sigma^{n}(G,A)
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A
is a
G
-module. They are defined as subsets of the character sphere
S(G)
associated to
G
, consisting of equivalence classes of
characters
, i.e. homomorphisms
\chi:G\to\mathbb{R}
. The
\Sigma
-invariants encode deep information about the finiteness properties
F_{n}
and
FP_{n}
of the
coabelian subgroups
of
G
, i.e. subgroups
N\leq G
such that
G/N
is abelian.

In this paper we will study the
\Sigma
-invariants
\Sigma^{1}
and
\Sigma^{2}
for certain families of Artin groups. Given a character
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the
living subgraph

\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}\subset\Gamma
by deleting:

•

All vertices
v\in V(\Gamma)
with
\chi(v)=0
. (called
dead vertices
)

•

The interior of every edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(\Gamma)
with
\chi(u)+\chi(v)=0
and even label
l(e)\geq 4
. (called
dead edges
)

We say that
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is
dominating
if every dead vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
is adjacent to some
living vertex

w\in V(\Gamma)
, i.e. a vertex
w
such that
\chi(w)\neq 0
. Motivated by earlier work of Meier-Meiner-VanWyk from
[
20
]
the following conjecture was proposed in
[
1
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group, then:

\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})=\{[\chi]\in S(A_{\Gamma})\mid\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}\text{ is connected and dominant}\}

Using representation theory for finite abelian groups, we will introduce in Section
4.1
a technical criterion that allows us to prove the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture for two broad families of Artin groups:

Theorem 1.2
.

The following families satisfy the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture:

1.

Balanced Artin groups.

2.

Artin groups
A_{\Gamma}
such that there exists a prime number
p
dividing
\frac{l(e)}{2}
for every
e\in E(\Gamma)
with
l(e)>2
even.

The definition of balanced Artin groups is given in Section
2.1
. This family generalizes two previously studied classes:

1.

Graphs
\Gamma
in which
l(e)
is even for all
e\in E(\Gamma)
and every closed reduced path with labels strictly greater than
2
has odd length.

2.

Coherent Artin groups (see Theorem
2.1
).

The family in
1.
was already known to satisfy the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture (see
[
16
]
).

In higher dimensions, Escartín-Martinez provided in
[
13
]
a sufficient condition for a character to lie in
\Sigma^{n}
under the assumption that the Artin group satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture. While the condition is somewhat technical, we obtain the following more tractable criterion in the case
n=2
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group satisfying the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture and let
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a character. Suppose:

1.

For any edge
e=\{v,w\}\in E(\Gamma)
such that either
\chi(v)=\chi(w)=0
or
e
is dead, there is some living
u\in V(\Gamma)
such that
A_{\{v,w,u\}}
is spherical,

2.

For every dead vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, the spherical link
\mathrm{slk}_{\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi},v}^{\chi}
is non-empty and connected,

3.

The simplicial complex obtained from
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
by attaching
2
-cells along all spherical triangles except those of type
\mathbb{B}_{3}
with a
3
-dead edge is
1
-acyclic (resp simply connected).

Then
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
(resp
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma})
).

For the definitions of the spherical link
\mathrm{slk}_{\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi},v}^{\chi}
and
3
-dead edges see section
2.4
.
We conjecture that the converse holds:

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group satisfying the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture and let
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
be a character. Then,
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
(resp
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma})
) if and only if the conditions of Theorem
2.13
hold.

We refer to this as the
(homotopical)
\Sigma^{2}
-conjecture for Artin groups
. We prove the conjecture for coherent and
2
-dimensional Artin groups. The characterization in the
2
-dimensional case is particularly simple:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be a
2
-dimensional Artin group and
0\neq\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
a character. Then,
\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma})=\Sigma^{m}(A_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
for all
m,n\geq 2
and
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma})
if and only if:

1.

No edge
e=\{v,w\}\in E(\Gamma)
satisfies
\chi(v)=\chi(w)=0
or
e
is dead,

2.

Every dead vertex is adjacent to a unique living vertex,

3.

\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is a tree.

As an application, we determine when the derived subgroup of a
2
-dimensional Artin group is finitely presented:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be a
2
-dimensional Artin group. Then
\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma})=S(A_{\Gamma})
if and only if
\Gamma
is a tree with odd labels. In particular,
A_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is finitely presented if and only if
\Gamma
is a tree with odd labels, in which case
A_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is of type
F_{\infty}
.

The question whether
A_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is finitely generated remains open for arbitrary
2
-dimensional Artin groups.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we will review background material on Artin groups and
\Sigma
-invariants. In section
3
we construct a chain complex for computing the homology of
Artin kernels
, i.e. kernels of characters
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
. This chain complex serve as the main tool for analysing the
\Sigma
-invariants. Section
4
is devoted to the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture, where we will prove Theorem
1.2
, establishing the conjecture for balanced Artin groups. In section
5
we will focus on the
\Sigma^{2}
-invariant, proving Theorem
1.5
and establishing several partial converses to Theorem
2.13
. Finally, in Section 6, we apply these results to investigate the algebraic fibration properties of some Artin groups.

These groups are represented via their Dynkin diagrams, which are defined in terms of the original graph
\Delta
. The Dynkin diagram has the same vertex set as
\Gamma
, and two vertices are connected by an edge if and only if the corresponding edge in
\Gamma
has label at least
3
. By convention, edges labelled with a
3
are unlabelled in the Dynkin diagram to simplify the notation, while non-adjacent pairs in
\Gamma
are indicated in the Dynkin diagram with an edge labelled
\infty
. Every finite Coxeter group decomposes as a direct product of irreducible finite Coxeter groups.

In this paper, we will consider the following families of Artin groups:

1.

Right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs)
: All edges of
\Gamma
are labelled with a
2
. These are precisely the Artin groups where all the defining relations are commutators

2.

Spherical Artin groups
: These are the Artin groups whose associated Coxeter group is finite. We say that
\Gamma
is a
spherical graph
if the corresponding Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
is spherical.

3.

Even Artin groups
: All edges in
\Gamma
are labelled with even integers.

4.

Triangular Artin groups
: These are the Artin groups with exactly
3
generators. They are denoted as
A_{MNP}
, where
M\leq N\leq P
are the labels of the three edges of the triangle. If two vertices are not adjacent in
\Gamma
we assume that they are connected by an edge labelled
\infty
. The only spherical triangular Artin groups are
A_{22P}
with
2\leq P<\infty
and
A_{23P}
with
P\in\{3,4,5\}
.

5.

2-Dimensional Artin groups
: These are the Artin groups such that any spherical subgraph has at most
2
vertices, i.e. the graph does not contain any spherical triangular subgraphs.

Salvetti constructed in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
a CW complex that plays a central role in the study of Artin groups. Due to its significance, it is now known as the
Salvetti complex
. Given an Artin group
A_{\Gamma}
, the associated Salvetti complex
\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma)
is built as the
2
-dimensional presentation complex corresponding to the standard presentation of
A_{\Gamma}
, with higher-dimensional cells attached for each spherical subgraph
X\subset\Gamma
. In particular the
0
-skeleton consists of a single vertex, denoted
\sigma_{\emptyset}
, the
1
-skeleton consists of
1
-cells
\sigma_{v}
for each vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
and the
2
-skeleton consists of
2
-cells
\sigma_{e}
for each edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
.

The most important open problem for Artin groups is the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture
. This conjecture admits several equivalent formulations, one of which states that the Salvetti complex
\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma)
is a model for the classifying space of
A_{\Gamma}
. In particular, the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture holds for
A_{\Gamma}
if and only if the universal cover of
\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma)
is contractible (c.f.
[
10
]
). For a detailed overview of the conjecture and its various formulations, we refer the reader to the comprehensive survey by Paris in
[
22
]
.

We recall some basic graph-theoretical definitions. A subgraph
\Delta\subset\Gamma
is said to be
induced
if every pair of vertices
v,w\in V(\Delta)
that are connected by an edge in
\Gamma
are also connected by an edge in
\Delta
. A graph
\Gamma
is called
chordal
if it contains no induced subgraph which is a cycle of length at least four.

A group
G
is said to be
coherent
if every finitely generated subgroup
H\leq G
is also finitely presented. There is a well-known characterization of coherence for right-angled Artin groups. This characterization has been extended for Artin groups as follows:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
14
]
,
[
28
]
Gordon-Wise)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group. Then,
A_{\Gamma}
is coherent if, and only if all of the following condition holds:

1.

\Gamma
is a chordal graph.

2.

Every complete subgraph of
\Gamma
with three or four vertices has at most one edge labelled with an integer greater than
2
.

3.

There is no induced subgraph in
\Gamma
of the following form:

m

where
m>2
and all the unlabelled edges are assumed to be labelled with a
2
.

Finally, let us define the family of balanced Artin groups, for which we will prove that the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture holds.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph whose edges are labelled with even integers
\geq 4
(not necessarily a simplicial graph). An
n-balanced colouring
of

is a map
\mathcal{C}:E(\Gamma)\to\{1,\dots,n\}
such that for every
i=1,\dots,n
there exists a prime number
p_{i}
with
p_{i}
dividing
\frac{l(e)}{2}
for every edge
e
with
\mathcal{C}(e)=i
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph whose edges are labelled with even integers
\geq 4
, which may not be simplicial. Suppose that
\Gamma
admits an
n
-balanced colouring such that every closed path of even length contains exactly two or zero edges of each colour. Then,
\Gamma
is called
balanced
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group. Define
\Gamma_{>2}^{\mathrm{even}}
to be the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by the following procedure:

•

Remove all edges with a label
2
.

•

Collapse each edge with an odd label into a single vertex.

We say that
A_{\Gamma}
is

if the resulting graph
\Gamma_{>2}^{\mathrm{even}}
is balanced.

Note that
\Gamma_{>2}^{\mathrm{even}}

2.2
\Sigma
-invariants

Let
G
be finitely generated. Throughout the paper
\chi:G\to\mathbb{R}
will represent an arbitrary non-zero character. Two characters are said to be
equivalent
, denoted
\chi_{1}\sim\chi_{2}
, if one is a positive scalar multiple of the other, i.e. there exists
t>0
such that
\chi_{1}=t\chi_{2}
. The
character sphere

S(G)
is the set of equivalence classes of non-zero characters. Note that
S(G)\cong\mathbb{S}^{r-1}
, where
r
is the torsion free rank of the abelianization
G/G^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{S}^{r-1}
is the
(r-1)
-sphere. A character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
is called
discrete
and the set of all discrete characters is dense in
S(G)
(cf.
[
26
]
Lemma B3.24). We now define the homological
\Sigma
-invariants of a group.

Definition 2.5
.

Consider a character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{R}
and define the monoid
G_{\chi}=\{g\in G\mid\chi(g)\geq 0\}
. Let
A
be a left
G
-module and
n
a positive integer. We define:

\Sigma^{n}(G,A)=\{[\chi]\in S(G)\mid A\text{ is of type }FP_{n}\text{ over }\mathbb{Z}G_{\chi}\}

These sets satisfy the following chain:

\Sigma^{\infty}(G,A)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Sigma^{1}(G,A)\subseteq\Sigma^{0}(G,A)=S(G)

There exists also a homotopical version of the invariants:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a group admitting a finite
K(G,1)
and
X
a model for
K(G,1)
. Denote its universal cover by
\widetilde{X}
. There is a bijection between the vertices of
\widetilde{X}
and the elements of
G
. Fix a base point
b\in\widetilde{X}^{(0)}
. Given a character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{R}
define a function
\widetilde{\chi}:\widetilde{X}^{(0)}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\widetilde{\chi}(g\cdot b)=\chi(g)
. For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, define
\widetilde{X_{\chi}}^{[a,\infty)}
as the maximal subcomplex of
\widetilde{X}
with
0
-skeleton

\{x\in\widetilde{X}^{(0)}\mid\widetilde{\chi}(x)\geq a\}
. Then, for each
d\geq 0
the inclusion map
\widetilde{X_{\chi}}^{[0,\infty)}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{X_{\chi}}^{[-d,\infty)}
induces a map
\tau_{i}^{d}:\pi_{i}\left(\widetilde{X_{\chi}}^{[0,\infty)}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{i}\left(\widetilde{X_{\chi}}^{[-d,\infty)}\right)
. Define the homotopical
\Sigma
-invariants as:

\Sigma^{n}(G)=\left\{[\chi]\in S(G)\mid\text{there exists }~d>0\text{ such that }\tau_{i}^{d}\text{ trivial }\forall~i<n\right\}

This defines a filtration:

\Sigma^{\infty}(G)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Sigma^{1}(G)\subseteq\Sigma^{0}(G)=S(G)

Renz proved in his thesis (cf.
[
23
]
) that the homotopical
\Sigma
-invariants are well-defined, i.e. it does not depend on the chosen model for
K(G,1)
. He also proved with Bieri that if
n=\operatorname{cd}G
, where
\operatorname{cd}G
is the cohomological dimension of
G
, then
\Sigma^{m}(G,\mathbb{Z})=\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
for any
m\geq n
. Analogously, if
n=\operatorname{gd}G
, where
\operatorname{gd}G
is the geometric dimension of
G
, then
\Sigma^{m}(G)=\Sigma^{n}(G)
for any
m\geq n
(c.f.
[
5
]
).
Another important topological property about these invariants is that both
\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and
\Sigma^{n}(G)
are open in
S(G)
(cf.
[
5
]
).

The homotopical and homological
\Sigma
-invariants are closely related. Indeed,
\Sigma^{n}(G)=\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cap\Sigma^{2}(G)
for each
n>1
and for any commutative ring
R
,
\Sigma^{n}(G)\subset\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})\subset\Sigma^{n}(G,R)
,
\Sigma^{1}(G)=\Sigma^{1}(G,R)
(cf.
[
23
]
).
The reason why the
\Sigma
-invariants encode the finiteness properties of the coabelian subgroups is the following central theorem:

Theorem 2.7

(
[
5
]
Bieri-Renz)
.

Let
G
be a group of type
F_{n}
and
G^{\prime}\leq N\leq G
. Then:

1.

N
is of type
FP_{n}
if and only if
\{[\chi]\in S(G)\mid\chi(N)=0\}\subset\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

2.

N
is of type
F_{n}
if and only if
\{[\chi]\in S(G)\mid\chi(N)=0\}\subset\Sigma^{n}(G)
.

For Artin groups, the
\Sigma
-invariants are symmetric.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
8
]
Proposition 3.1)
.

If
A_{\Gamma}
is an Artin group then
\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma})=-\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma},R)=-\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma},R)
for all
n\geq 1
and any commutative ring
R
.

From Lemma
2.8
and Theorem
2.7
, we derive the next useful criteria for determining whether a character lies in the
\Sigma
-invariants or not.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
G
be an Artin group satisfying the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture, and
\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
a non zero character. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H_{n}(\ker(\chi),\mathbb{F})=\infty
then
[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Since
G
satisfies the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture the Salvetti complex is a
K(G,1)
, so
G
is of type
F_{n}
. The result then follows from Theorem
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Another useful result that we deduce from Lemma

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group satisfying the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture and let
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a discrete character. Then,
\ker(\chi)
is of type
F_{n}
if and only if
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{n}(A_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
.

Finally, we mention a reduction method that simplifies the study of the
\Sigma^{1}
-invariants of Artin groups.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
2
]
Lemma 2.14)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-zero character. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a graph obtained from
\Gamma
by finitely many of the following transformations:

1.

Delete a vertex
v
such that
\chi(v)=0
, along with all incident edges.

2.

Add an edge with arbitrary even label between any pair of non-adjacent vertices
u,v\in V(\Gamma)
.

3.

Add an edge with arbitrary label between any pair of non-adjacent vertices
u,v\in V(\Gamma)
with
\chi(u)=\chi(v)
.

4.

Given an edge
e=\{u,v\}
with label
l
, replace the label by a divisor
\beta\mid l
such that
\beta
is even or
\chi(u)=\chi(v)
.

5.

Identify two vertices
u,v
such that
\chi(u)=\chi(v)
. Now, for pair of edges with labels
n
and
m
and the same endpoints replace them with a unique edge with label
\gcd(m,n)
. Next, identify the endpoints of any edge with label equal to
1
and remove loops. Repeat the process until obtaining a simplicial graph.

Then, there exists a natural epimorphism
\pi:A_{\Gamma}\to A_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
and
\chi
factors trough the induced character
\chi^{\prime}:A_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
\chi=\chi^{\prime}\circ\pi
. In particular, if
[\chi^{\prime}]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma^{\prime}})
then
[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
.

2.3
The
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture

The Bieri–Neumann–Strebel invariant
\Sigma^{1}
of a right-angled Artin group (RAAG) is well understood in terms of a combinatorial object known as the living subgraph.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be a RAAG and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a non-trivial character. The
living subgraph

\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}_{0}
is defined to be the subgraph of
\Gamma
induced by the set of vertices
v\in V(\Gamma)
for which
\chi(v)\neq 0
. These are called
living vertices
, while those with
\chi(v)=0
are called
dead vertices
.

A sub-graph
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
is called
dominant
if for every vertex
v\in V(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime})
there exists a vertex
w\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
\{v,w\}\in E(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
19
]
Meiner-VanWyk, 1993)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be a RAAG. Then:

\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})=\{[\chi]\in S(A_{\Gamma})\mid\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}_{0}\text{ is connected and dominant}\}

For general Artin groups, the definition of living subgraph was generalized as follows:

Definition 2.14
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a character. The
living subgraph
, denoted as
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
, is obtained from
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}_{0}
by deleting every edge
e=\{u,v\}
such that
l(e)\geq 4
is even and
\chi(u)+\chi(v)=0
. The deleted edges are called
dead edges
and the remaining edges are called
living edges
.

This definition reduces to the previous one in the RAAG case (i.e., when all edge labels are
2
). Somewhat counterintuitively, any edge incident to a dead vertex is automatically considered living.

The natural question arises: does Theorem
2.13
extend to arbitrary Artin groups? Partial results in this direction have been obtained. In particular, Meier proved the following:

Proposition 2.15

(
[
18
]
Meier, 1995)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a character. Then:

1.

If
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is connected and dominant then
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
.

2.

If
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
then
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}_{0}
is connected and dominant. In particular,
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is dominant.

However, Meier was unable to show that
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
implies that
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is connected. Although several people worked on this problem throughout the years, it was first explicitly formulated as a conjecture in 2018. Applying Proposition
2.15
, the conjecture can be stated equivalently as follows:

Conjecture 2.16

(
[
1
]
, Almeida)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then:

\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}\textit{ is disconnected }\Rightarrow[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})

This statement is known as the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture for Artin groups. The conjecture has been proved for several families of Artin groups:

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a non-zero character. Then,
[\chi]\in\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
if and only if
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is connected and dominant in the following cases:

1.

\Gamma
is a complete graph and all edges have the same label. (
[
18
]
Meier, 1997)

2.

\Gamma
is a tree. (
[
21
]
Meier-Meinert-VanWyk, 2001)

3.

\Gamma
is connected and
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
is free of rank
1
. (
[
3
]
Almeida-Kochloukova, 2015)

4.

\Gamma
belongs to a certain family of complete graphs with
4
vertices. (
[
4
]
Almeida-Kochloukova, 2015)

5.

\Gamma
lies in a specific family of minimal graphs with
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
of arbitrary rank. (
[
2
]
Almeida, 2017)

6.

\Gamma
is connected and
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
is free of rank
2
. (
[
1
]
Almeida, 2018)

7.

\Gamma
is even and every closed reduced path with edge labels all greater than
2
has odd length. (
[
16
]
Kochloukova, 2021)

8.

There exists a prime number
p
dividing
\frac{l(e)}{2}
for every edge

with
l(e)\geq 4
even. (
[
13
]

An important simplification of the conjecture, which will be used later, is the following reduction to discrete characters.

Proposition 2.18

(
[
3
]
, Corollary 2.9)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group. Suppose that for every discrete character
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
the following holds:

\left[\mathrm{Liv}_{0}^{\chi}\text{ is connected and }\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}\text{ is disconnected }\right]\Rightarrow[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma}),

then, the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture holds for

.

2.4
The
\Sigma^{2}
-conjecture

To understand the
\Sigma^{2}
-invariant for Artin groups, we must introduce a more subtle condition than in the
\Sigma^{1}
case. This requires two preliminary definitions.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a character. An edge
e=\{v,w\}
is called
3
-dead
if
l(e)=4
,
e
belongs to a standard parabolic subgroup of type
\mathbb{B}_{3}
and
2\chi(v)+\chi(w)=0
, where
w
is the vertex adjacent to both
v
and the edge labelled
3
in the
\mathbb{B}_{3}
subgraph.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}
a character.
Let
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma
be a subgraph and
v\in V(\Gamma)
. The
link

\lk_{\Gamma_{1}}(v)
is the subgraph of
\Gamma_{1}
induced by the set of vertices of
\Gamma_{1}
that are adjacent to
v
in
\Gamma
.

We define the
spherical link complex

\slk_{\Gamma_{1},v}^{\chi}
as the simplicial complex having one
m
-cell for each
(m+1)
-clique

such that both

and

are spherical subgraphs of
\Gamma
.

Using this complex, Escartín–Martínez provided a criterion in
[
13
]
to determine whether a character lies in

for Artin groups satisfying the
K(\pi,1)
-conjecture. While this condition is technical in general, for the case

it simplifies to a more accessible criterion, given in Theorem
2.13
.

In fact, by combining the main result of
[

]
with the validity of the
\Sigma^{1}
-conjecture for tree-based Artin groups, one obtains a new proof of
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
, which asserts that if
\Gamma
is a tree then
\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})=\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})=\Sigma^{2}(A_{\Gamma})
.

There is also another case where the
\Sigma^{2}
-conjecture is known to be true:

Theorem 2.21

(
[
13
]

.

Let

be an even Artin group satisfying the

-conjecture. Assume that there exists a prime number

for every

. Then,

satisfies the homological

-conjecture.

In Theorem

, we extend the

-conjecture for Artin groups based on trees to the setting of

In this section, we introduce two chain complexes that will be essential in computing the homology of Artin groups in the next section. These complexes will serve as our main tool for studying the invariants

Let

Let
G

\psi(g)=(\chi(g),\varphi(g))

Lemma 3.3
.

If the restriction
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\right|_{\Ker(\chi)}}

\psi

Proof.

Let

\psi(h)=(a,\varphi(h))

\psi(hc)=\psi(h)\psi(c)=(a,\psi(h))(1,\psi(h)^{-1}b)=(a,b)

\psi
is surjective.

\psi

\psi

\widetilde{\mathrm{Sal}(\Gamma)}

The

Let

Lemma 4.1
.

The homology group

(R[G])_{S}
-dimension of the

-th homology group of the normalized complex
\widetilde{C}_{\ast}(\mathrm{Sal}_{\Gamma}^{\psi})
. Therefore, the homology group

-th homology group of the normalized chain complex

Proof.

Consider the chain complex

. Localize this complex at the multiplicative set

\operatorname{rank}_{(R[G])_{S}}H_{n}(\widetilde{C}_{\ast}(\mathrm{Sal}_{\Gamma}^{\psi}))=\operatorname{rank}_{R[G]}H_{n}(\ker(\psi),\mathbb{F})_{\text{free}}

In view of Lemma

, it suffices to compute the homology group

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and let
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a set of elements of
\mathbb{C}[G]
. The ideal
I
generated by
\mathcal{P}
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{C}[G]
if and only if there exists an irreducible character
\mu:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\mu(p_{i})=0~\forall~i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Since
G
is finite abelian, all of its finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations are
1
-dimensional. Consequently, we have a group ring decomposition
\mathbb{C}[G]\cong\mathbb{C}e_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{n}
,
where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the orthogonal idempotents corresponding to the irreducible characters
\mu_{i}
, and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}=1
. Let
\mu
be the irreducible character associated to a given idempotent
e
. Then, for any element
\lambda=\sum\limits_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}g\in\mathbb{C}[G]
, we obtain:

\lambda e=\sum\limits_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}ge=\sum\limits_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}\mu(g)e=\mu\left(\sum_{g\in G}\lambda_{g}g\right)e=\mu(\lambda)e

This shows that
\lambda e=0
if and only if
\mu\left(\lambda\right)=0
. Therefore, there exists an irreducible character
\mu:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\mu(p_{i})=0~\forall~i=1,\dots,k
if and only if there is an idempotent
e
with
Ie=0
. In this case, since
\mathbb{C}[G]=\mathbb{C}[G]e\oplus\mathbb{C}[G](1-e)
we conclude that
I\lneq\mathbb{C}[G]
.

Conversely, suppose that no such character
\mu
exists. Then,
Ie_{i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Since each
\mathbb{C}e_{i}
is one dimensional, we conclude that

I=Ie_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Ie_{n}=\mathbb{C}e_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{n}=\mathbb{C}[G]

as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
G
a finite abelian group and
\mathcal{P}=\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a set of elements of
(R[G])_{S}
. Then, the ideal
I
generated by
\mathcal{P}
is distinct from
(R[G])_{S}
if and only if there exists an irreducible character
\mu:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\mu(p_{i})=0~\forall~i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Let
Q(R)
denote the field of fractions of
R
. Then, we have inclusions
\mathbb{C}[G]\hookrightarrow Q(R)[G]\hookrightarrow(R[G])_{S}
. As in the proof of the lemma, the decomposition
\mathbb{C}[G]\cong\mathbb{C}e_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{n}
induces a decomposition:

Q(R)[G]\cong Q(R)e_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus Q(R)e_{n}

and tensoring with
(R[G])_{S}
we obtain:

(R[G])_{S}\cong(R[G])_{S}e_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus(R[G])_{S}e_{n}

Since localization is exact and preserves direct sums, the same argument as in the previous Lemma shows that
I\neq(R[G])_{S}
if and only if there exists some idempotent
e
with
Ie=0
, which is equivalent to the existence of a character
\mu
such that
\mu(p_{i})=0
for all
i
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be an Artin group and let
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a non-zero discrete character such that
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}_{0}
is connected and
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
is disconnected. Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be unions of connected components of
\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
such that
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}=\mathrm{Liv}^{\chi}
and
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=\emptyset
. Suppose that there exists a finite group

, an homomorphism
\varphi:A_{\Gamma}\to G
such that the restriction
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\right|_{\Ker(\chi)}}
is an epimorphism and a character
\mu:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that for each dead edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(\Gamma)
with
u\in V(\Gamma_{1})
and

the value
\mu(\varphi(uv))
is a non-trivial
\frac{l(e)}{2}
-th root of unity. Then,
[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

Applying the procedure outlined at the beginning of the section, we may assume that
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are two complete graphs with no even labels
\geq 4
. In this situation we have that
H_{1}\left(\widetilde{C}_{\ast}(\text{Sal}_{\Gamma}^{\chi}),\mathbb{F}\right)\neq 0
if and only if the ideal
I
generated by the elements
p_{e}=1+\varphi(uv)+\dots+\varphi(uv)^{\frac{l(e)}{2}-1}
for all dead edges

with

and

is a proper subset of
(R[G])_{S}
, i.e.

. For every such edge

we have:

\mu(p_{e})=\mu\left(1+\varphi(uv)+\cdots+\varphi(uv)^{\frac{l(e)}{2}-1}\right)=1+\mu(\varphi(uv))+\cdots+\mu(\varphi(uv))^{\frac{l(e)}{2}-1}

Since

is a
\frac{l(e)}{2}
-root of unity, the sum vanish, i.e.
\mu(p_{e})=0
. Hence, by Corollary
4.3
, it follows that

and Lemma

[\chi]\notin\Sigma^{1}(A_{\Gamma})
.
∎

Corollary 4.5

4.2
Parity of balanced Artin groups

In this section we will introduce the notion of parity of pairs of edges, which will be a technical tool needed to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Consider a balanced graph
\Gamma
with no closed paths of odd length equipped with an
n
-balanced colouring
\mathcal{C}
. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, denote by
E_{i}(\Gamma)
the set of edges of colour
i
in
\Gamma
. Define a relation
\sim
on
E_{i}(\Gamma)
as follows: for edges
e,f\in E_{i}(\Gamma)
, say that
e\sim f
if there exists a finite sequence of minimal simple cycles
\mathcal{P}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{m}
such that
e\in\mathcal{P}_{1},f\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
and for each
k=1,\dots,m-1
the cycles
\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}
share at least one edge of colour
i
.
It follows that
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
E_{i}(\Gamma)
, partitioning the set of edges of colour
i
into equivalence classes.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a balanced graph with no closed paths of odd length. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
n
-balanced colouring of
\Gamma
. Suppose
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\Gamma)

are two distinct edges of the same colour, i.e.
\mathcal{C}(e_{1})=\mathcal{C}(e_{2})
, and that they lie on the same equivalence class. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a simple cycle containing both
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. We define the
parity
of the pair
(e_{1},e_{2})
in
\mathcal{P}
as:

\mathrm{parity}_{\mathcal{P}}(e_{1},e_{2})=(-1)^{l}

where
l
is the numbers of edges lying between
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
along the cycle
\mathcal{P}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The parity map is well-defined and does not depend on the choice of the simple cycle.

Proof.

Suppose
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\Gamma)

are two edges of the same colour, that there exists a simple cycle
\mathcal{P}
of even length containing both
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
and that
l
is the number of intermediate edges between
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
in
\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
has even length
L
, the complementary arc from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
has length
L-l-2
and
(-1)^{l}=(-1)^{L-l-2}
. Which shows that the parity does not depend on the orientation of
\mathcal{P}
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are two simple cycles of even length both containing
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. Denote by
l_{\mathcal{P}}
and
l_{\mathcal{Q}}
the number of edges between
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
along
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
, respectively. Consider the walk
\mathcal{R}
obtained by traversing from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
along
\mathcal{P}
and then
\mathcal{Q}
from
e_{2}
to
e_{1}
. This is a closed path of length
l_{\mathcal{P}}+l_{\mathcal{Q}}
, which is an even number by hypothesis. Hence
(-1)^{l_{\mathcal{P}}}=(-1)^{l_{\mathcal{Q}}}
, showing that the parity is independent of the chosen simple cycle.
∎

Since the parity does not depend on the choice of the simple cycle we will omit the path and denote it as
\mathrm{parity}(e_{1},e_{2})
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a balanced graph with no closed paths of odd length and let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{n}
-balanced colouring. Then, there exists a function
\mathrm{p}:E(\Gamma)\to\{1,-1\}
such that for any pair of edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\Gamma)
of the same colour that lie on the same equivalence class we have:

\mathrm{parity}(e_{1},e_{2})=\mathrm{p}(e_{1})\mathrm{p}(e_{2})

Proof.

Consider a colour
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and choose an arbitrary edge
e\in E_{i}(\Gamma)
. Define
\mathrm{p}(e)=1
and for each
e_{1}\in E_{i}(\Gamma)
in the same equivalence class define
\mathrm{p}(e_{1})=\mathrm{parity}(e,e_{1})
.
Assume that
e_{1},e_{2}\in E_{i}(\Gamma)
are two edges in the same equivalence class as
e
. By definition
\mathrm{p}(e_{1})\mathrm{p}(e_{2})=\mathrm{parity}(e,e_{1})\mathrm{parity}(e,e_{2})

Now, consider the simple cycle from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
formed by concatenating the path from
e_{1}
to
e
and then from
e
to
e_{2}
. The parity between
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
is:

\mathrm{parity}(e,e_{1})\mathrm{parity}(e,e_{2})=\mathrm{parity}(e_{1},e_{2})

Thus, the desired identity holds, and the function
\mathrm{p}
is well-defined on each equivalence class.
∎

5.1
Necessity of condition 1

Fix an Artin group

satisfying the

-conjecture and a character
\chi:A_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

denote the
n

p

n

p
such that
\Phi_{n}(x)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p}

Proof.

Let

Suppose, by way of contradiction, that

Observe that

It remains to verify this identity. From Formulas

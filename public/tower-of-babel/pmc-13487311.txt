Cross-modal attentive fusion network for tri-modal lesion growth prediction
Authors: Revathi Appavoo, Vimaladevi Madhivanan, Abhay Chaturvedi, Janjhyam Venkata Naga Ramesh, Divya Nimma, V Ravi Kishore
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPre-trained LSTM-RNN models with linear kernels fail to capture irregular, non-linear lesion growth patterns. In this paper presents a new deep learning model is called Cross-Modal Attention Fusion Network (CMAFN) to enhance an effective feature fusion of multiple medical image modalities such as Mammography, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) and Ultrasound (US). It enables progressively transforms and aligns features from multiple modalities into a unified representation space. Our CMAFN model combines the three advanced modules, Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis (DCCA) to effectively fuses non-linear features in imaging modalities, Cross-Modal Attention Mechanism (CMAM) to adaptively combine modalities and align features, and Radial Basis Function with Conventional Long Term Short Memory (RBF-ConvLSTM) to learn both linear and non-linear spatial-temporal growth patterns. Overall, the proposed CMAFN model supportive for multi-modal medical image analysis and interpretable predictions of lesion progression over time.Keywords: Multi-modal feature fusion, Cross-modal attention weights, Medical image analysis, Disease growth prediction and Computer-aided diagnosisSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Breast cancer is the second leading cancer among women in the world with an estimated 2.3 million new cases and 6,85,000 deaths every year, as reported by the world health organization1. The ability to identify and correctly characterize breast lesion is a key determinant of patient survival and the five-year survival has been reported to be above 90% in cases of localized disease as opposed to a low 29% in a case of distant metastatic disease2. In the breast cancer screening and diagnosis, medical imaging assumes a leading role and the most common modalities in clinical practice include mammography, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and ultrasound images. The benefits of each modality are different: mammography is better at identifying microcalcifications and architectural distortions; MRI works better with soft tissue contrasts to evaluate the extent and multifocality of tumors; ultrasound can be used to evaluate real time morphology and vascularity of lesions without ionizing radiation3. Nonetheless, the application of one imaging modality normally provides incomplete diagnostic data, which prompts the incorporation of multi-modal imaging to provide adequate lesion characterization.

The recent developments in the field of deep learning have transformed the field of medical images analysis, where convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers have proven to outperform in lesion detection, classification, and segmentation tasks4–6. Multi-modal fusion strategies have also been suggested, some of which are early fusion (on-the-fly concatenation of raw images), late fusion (classifier outputs), and intermediate fusion (feature representations)7. Interesting deep learning architectures like CLGB-Net8 combine local and global information, MSFA-Net9 uses multiscale feature aggregation, MFF-HistoNet10 uses multi- modal feature fusion in histopathology and DMC-Fusion11 employs deep multi-cascade strategies. Transformer, such as Swin-Net12, SSTrans-Net13, and SwinE-Net14, methods have demonstrated the best possible performance by performing long-range dependencies with self-attention. Even with these developments, the currently used techniques mostly concentrate on the classification of the common static lesions but ignore an important clinical aspect the dynamic nature of lesion growth which has significant prognostic and therapeutic significance.

The existing techniques are facing a fundamental challenge that they cannot differentiate between the linear and non-linear patterns of tissue growth a distinction that has significant biological and clinical consequences. From a pathophysiological perspective, normal breast tissue is regulated by homeostasis style of cell growth and cell apoptosis formation are balanced to provide predictable, linear rates of growth15. On the other hand, malignant tissue has dysregulated growth with exponential or Gompertzian growth kinetics, which generate irregular margins, non-uniform growth, and disordered spatial-temporal organization16. Current models of deep learning use homogenous convolutional kernels, which intrinsically assume linear spatial correlations. But they cannot capture non-linear growth patterns which are replicative of malignancy. This constraint undermines diagnostic accuracy as well as biological interpretability that is needed in making clinical decisions.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel Cross-Modal Attentive Fusion Network (CMAFN) for tri-modal breast lesion growth prediction that explicitly models both linear and non-linear spatial-temporal characteristics. The proposed framework introduces three key innovations: (i) a Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis (DCCA) module that learns statistically optimal cross-modal feature correlations by maximizing canonical correlations in a shared latent space; (ii) a Cross-Modal Attention (CMA) mechanism that adaptively weights modality-specific contributions based on their discriminative relevance to lesion characterization; and (iii) a hybrid RBF-ConvLSTM module incorporating radial basis function kernels alongside traditional linear convolutions, with a learnable mixing parameter λ that dynamically balances linear and non-linear pattern extraction. This biologically motivated design enables CMAFN to differentiate between benign lesions exhibiting predominantly linear growth (λ → 1) and malignant lesions characterized by non-linear expansion (λ → 0), providing both accurate predictions and clinically interpretable outputs.

Literature survey

This section covers a literature review of a several diagnosis methods used in the deep learning models includes multi-modal data fusion methods, and Bayesian approach for disease progression. Multi-modal medical data fusion techniques

Various methods of image fusion have been presented8, which are well evaluated with regards to fusion rules, image decomposition process and quality evaluation with their individual benefits and drawbacks. Firstly, when it comes to image enhancement and extraction of specific features, one can utilize non-subsampled contourlet transform (NSCT) that splits the image into several sub-bands, each of which could be structured with the aid of an adaptive neural network-based model when it comes to image fusion and is, therefore, useful in clinical diagnosis9. Likewise, the multi-level edge-preserving filtering is used to break down the image into various layers and then the neural network strategies are applied10. The other popular method is Laplacian pyramid decomposition that assists in the elimination of noise as well as effective preservation of edges11. The Hybrid-Fusion Network (Hi-Net) is applied to reconstruct the missing image frames, and the network results are tested on different modalities12. Alternatively, the Deep Multi-Cascade Fusion (DCM-Fusion) is effective to classify both high and low frequencies information by means of classifier-based synthesis of features on medical multi-modal images13. Moreover, Multi-dimensional Dynamic Convolution and Residual Hybrid Transformer (MDC-RHT) prove to be the best in terms of feature extraction and context modeling14. Bilateral Gaussian fusion module is achieved by using a transformer based a bi-modal fusion module that is much robust in the event of missing modalities15. Image frame generation in various modalities is improved by medical data fusion, where the various modalities are correlated with features related to both multidimensional medical and biomedical data16.

Temporal disease progression modeling and prediction

Temporal disease progression models such as Outcome-oriented Deep Temporal Phenotyping (ODTP)18 and Disease Progression Modeling with Temporal Realignment (DPM-TR)3, utilize supervised depth-first search to identify frequent disease sequences17, temporal phenotypes of disease progression, and predict chronic disease stages more accurately using multiple biomarkers. Recently, non-stationary kernel approximation has been employed to capture the temporal relationships between irregular patient visits in electronic health records. The Continuous Autoregressive Recurrent Neural Network (CARRNN) effectively handles irregular time intervals between measurements19. Additionally, Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) modeling combined with Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) has shown significant success in making predictions from time series data20. The spatio-temporal model [21]-24 eliminates unnecessary errors while performing cross-feature correlation across different modalities, thus improving the learning rate of spatial feature correlation.

Bayesian approaches for medical decision making

The Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Networks (DCGAN) produces fake samples of artificial mammogram images which help detect breast cancer25. The CNN-based detection system utilizes dual-attention network capabilities to identify and type white blood cells in microscopic images26. A dual attention mechanism implemented by this research yielded improved accuracy and efficiency together with better generalization capabilities. A prior-guided adversarial learning with hypergraph (PALH) model explores triple-modality medical images to forecast irregular brain connections in Alzheimer’s disease patients27. Bayesian statistics improves uncertainty management through evidence-based medicine and strengthening of framework components28. Most of the time, time series data is used to optimize clinical interventions and outcomes, providing treatment schedules tailored to individuals, which are developed from dynamic disease models by healthcare professionals29. This process combines various evidence sources into usable decisions for experts30-31.

It is important to clarify that while the model is trained using triplet-fused representations, evaluation metrics are reported per dataset (per modality domain), reflecting the practical scenario where the full tri-modal input is available during inference. Second, cross-modal attention mechanism is adapted to perform dynamically weight and align features based on their relevance to the prediction task and finally; RBF-ConvLSTM captures the spatio-temporal dynamics of lesion evolution by learning hybrid kernels that model both linear and non-linear growths. Module 1: deep canonical correlation analysis

Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis (DCCA) extends classical CCA by incorporating deep neural networks to learn non-linear transformations of multi-view data before computing canonical correlations. This approach enables the discovery of complex, non-linear relationships between different imaging modalities. The capture information features are transformed map from different modalities into a shared canonical space where their correlation is maximized. This shows potential benefits of captured different image modalities placed in the same underlying anatomical structures. That implies, Mammography is based on X-ray attenuation, MRI on the nuclear magnetic resonance features, and ultrasound on the changes of acoustic impedance. Despite of these differences, the underlying tissue features like the presence of lesions, morphology, and composition result in a correlated pattern between modalities which DCCA can learn to extract.

Module 3: RBF-ConvLSTM for Spatio-temporal growth prediction

Lesion growth in medical imaging exhibits complex spatio-temporal variations which cannot be adequately estimated using the conventional linear models. During specific stages, the tumor development can assume rather linear patterns but be extremely non-linear at the time of sudden growth or response to treatment. Normally, standard convolutional kernels capture linear arbitrary spatial patterns through learned weights, but it struggles with smooth, continuous growth patterns typical for biological processes. Our RBF-ConvLSTM module handles this problem by adding Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernels to the convolutional LSTM architecture. It enables smooth spatial dependencies in tissue growth by providing learnable bandwidth parameters which adaptive for different growing scales. Thereby, RBF-ConvLSTM module allows to capture both linear and non-linear spatial relationships in the development of lesions.

Results and discussion

This section presents the comprehensive performance analysis of the proposed Cross-Modal Attentive Fusion Network (CMAFN) in comparison with several state-of-the-art deep learning models for lesion growth estimation. Our model incorporates both linear and non-linear characteristics for extracting spatial-temporal relationships in lesion growth patterns. Most of the existing models failed to address because Normal tissue development is typically linear and regulated by homeostatic mechanisms, but non-linear, chaotic tissue growth is seen in the abnormal or malignant tissue. As a results, the inability of cells to regulate their proliferation. The proposed CMAFN model takes advantage of this biological assumption by using its hybrid RBF-ConvLSTM kernel and thereby allows differentiating between benign and malignant tissue growth trajectories. The comparative evaluation encompasses eight contemporary deep learning architectures: CLGB-Net2, MSFA-Net4, MFF-HistoNet6, DMC-Fusion8, CNN with Attention10, Swin-Net12, SSTrans-Net14, and SwinE-Net16, respectively. All simulations are performed using MATLAB R2018b on a computing platform equipped with Intel Core i7-10700 K processor (3.80 GHz), 32 GB RAM, and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 GPU with 10 GB memory for accelerated deep learning computations. The performance evaluation employs a comprehensive set of metrics including Accuracy, Sensitivity (Recall), Specificity, Precision, F1-Score, Area Under ROC Curve (AUC), Dice Similarity Coefficient, and Intersection over Union (IoU). To ensure statistical reliability and minimize overfitting bias, 5-fold cross-validation strategy is adopted, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation across all folds. Dataset description

For simulation purpose, there are three well-known publicly available datasets are involved such as (i) Curated Breast Imaging subset of Digital Database for Screening Mammography (CBIS-DDSM), (ii) MRI images of breast tissue, and (iii) Breast Ultrasound Images Dataset (BUSI), respectively. Each dataset is partitioned into training, validation, and testing subsets following an 80:10:10 split ratio to ensure robust model development and unbiased performance evaluation. The short description about each dataset is discussed as follows: Due to the three different datasets, there exist varying patient populations of three-mode input tuples (Mammography, MRI, Ultrasound) were built by a category-stratified cross-dataset pseudo-pairing approach. One image was selected randomly in each dataset to create a triplet (each lesion category). Thereby, the pathology of the modalities is consistent. The rationale of this method is that lesions of the same established pathological group will have common underlying biological properties across modalities allowing the model to learn modality specific feature representations without patient specific data. the combinations of all triplets were fixed before training to avoid the leaking of data. As per our methodology concerned that pseudo-pairing is followed because datasets is not having same patient matched multi-modal imaging. and we would verify CMAFN on co-registered multi-modes in future studies. However, the following steps are allowed to achieve successful feature fusion regarding the heterogeneous nature of different modalities. (i) All images are resized to 224 × 224 pixels; (ii) Intensity values are matched per modality through training-set statistics (zero-mean, unit-variance), putting scanner-specific distributions on equal footing; (iii) Each modality encoder is pre-trained on its own, and then jointly fine-tuned, which constructs robust modality-specific representations; (iv) The DCCA module directly aligns All of these measures will guarantee that the heterogeneity of patient groups and devices do not become a barrier to the cross-modal fusion.

Qualitative analysis

The proposed CMAFN model distinguishes between normal and abnormal tissue growth by capturing both linear and non-linear spatial-temporal characteristics through multi-modal feature fusion. This is based on the biological premise shows that normal tissue growth follows homeostatic regulation with predictable, linear progression patterns. Abnormal/malignant tissue growth exhibits dysregulated proliferation with non-linear, chaotic expansion patterns. Multi-modal imaging: captures complementary characteristics - structural (Mammography), soft tissue (MRI), and morphological (Ultrasound). The learnable parameter of 0.3 < λ < 0.7 in CMAFN proposes a gradual choice of these parts to offer subtle classification of undetermined lesions. Conclusion

This paper introduced a new Cross-Modal Attentive Fusion Network (CMAFN) to predict tri-modal (mammography, MRI and ultrasound) growth of breast lesions through the combination of these modalities. CMAFN incorporates both the linear and non-linear forms of spatial-temporal growth a biologically based strategy. Thereby, differentiates normal tissue growth (linear) and malignant tissue growth (non-linear) effectively. The proposed CMAFN model has three major innovations, which include: (i) Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis (DCCA) to learn statistically optimal cross-modal feature correlations, (ii) Cross-Modal Attention (CMA) to adaptively weight the modality according to its discriminative power, and (iii) a hybrid RBF-ConvLSTM kernel whose mixing parameter λ is learnable to represent both linear and non-linear growth dynamics.

Extensive experiment has been conducted on three benchmark datasets (CBIS-DDSM, Breast MRI, and BUSI) proved that proposed CMAFN model performs better than eight other state-of-the-art models. The highest accuracy of the proposed CMAFN model was achieved by 95.67%, sensitive 94.89%, specific (96.45%), and AUC (0.9734), which was better by + 2.89% when compared to the best baseline. The hypothesis that a combination of linear and non-linear pattern extraction is crucial to correct estimation of lesion growth was substantiated by being proved through ablation experiments that the hybrid kernel can alleviate the improvement of + 4.44% over linear-only configurations.

The clinical implications of the work extend beyond classification accuracy. The learnable parameter λ is a discriminative model parameter that has preliminary biomarker-like behavior: lesions with high λ values ( λ > 0.7) have a linear growth pattern, and those with low λ values ( λ < 0.3) have a non-linear growth pattern. It should be underlined that these thresholds are based on the training data distribution and are some initial exploratory observations. The λ parameter is not officially proved against validated histopathological grading in prospective clinical trials and thus it cannot be interpreted as an independent diagnostic biomarker yet. The strict clinical validation, such as the correlation with the pathological grading, the sensitivity-specificity analysis of λ as an independent biomarker is an essential direction to be developed in the future. Further investigation in the future will focus on demonstrating the accuracy of CMAFN by longitudinal data of patients to forecast time-related growth patterns and extending the method to other types of cancer and making the computational framework efficient enough to implement in clinical practice.

Percutaneous transosseous approach for type II endoleak repair of common iliac artery aneurysm
Authors: Benjamin Casey, Mathew Sexton, Kelli Moore, Arash Saemi, Ryan Davis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Persistent type II endoleaks following endovascular aneurysm repair can result in continued aneurysm sac enlargement and may be difficult to treat when conventional endovascular access routes are unsuccessful. A patient with a right common iliac artery aneurysm developed a persistent type II endoleak despite multiple prior embolization attempts using standard angiographic techniques. Surveillance imaging demonstrated continued aneurysm sac enlargement to 4.8 cm. Given the failure of conventional endovascular approaches, a direct percutaneous transosseous approach was pursued. Under image guidance, access to the aneurysm sac was obtained through the right iliac bone. Following confirmation of intraluminal positioning, embolization was successfully performed using a combination of detachable coils and Obsidio conformable embolic (Boston Scientific). Completion imaging demonstrated successful occlusion of the endoleak cavity. Follow-up imaging demonstrated stabilization of the aneurysm sac without further enlargement and no evidence of recurrent endoleak. Percutaneous transosseous access represents a feasible and effective salvage technique for direct aneurysm sac embolization in patients with persistent type II endoleaks when conventional endovascular approaches have failed. This nontraditional access route expands the interventional options for managing complex endoleaks and may provide a minimally invasive alternative to surgical intervention in carefully selected patients.

Introduction

This report describes a percutaneous transosseous approach through the iliac bone for the embolization of a type 2 endoleak of a common iliac artery aneurysm. This approach has only been published once by Gemmete et al. [1], with our case differing in patient positioning and embolization material. We feel this case is significant as it highlights a rare approach for gaining access to an aneurysmal sac when graft components and vascular anatomy prevent the use of conventional approaches.

Case

The patient is an 85-year-old man with a medical history of endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) in 2018 for the treatment of an abdominal aortic aneurysm and right common iliac artery aneurysm (CIAA) with an endograft and iliac branch device. At the time of surgery, the patient’s CIAA measured 3 cm around the natural bifurcation. A follow up CTA in 2023 demonstrated enlargement of the CIAA to 3.3 cm. Management of the enlarging aneurysm sac included multiple attempts of embolization. Prior to our procedure, the patient had coil embolization of a distal right superior gluteal artery branch, distal branch of the lateral sacral artery, and the right iliolumbar artery. With each attempt of embolization, the team was met with difficulty accessing the relevant arteries due to anatomical constraints caused by tortuosity and iliac graft components. All attempts at embolization were unsuccessful at preventing the enlargement of the aneurysm sac. Both transfemoral and transbrachial arterial routes failed and radial access remained contraindicated due to bilateral Barbeau D waveforms so the decision was made to pursue direct percutaneous sac access as the next treatment option.

Patient underwent CT-guided direct percutaneous aneurysm sac puncture via a right trans iliac bone approach. As seen in Fig. 2 an 11-gauge bone trocar was advanced through the right iliac wing into the psoas muscle, a 21-gauge needle from a Greb set was advanced coaxially through the bone access needle and into the right common iliac aneurysm sac with spontaneous return of blood confirming access, a 0.018-inch guide wire was curled within the sac, and a 5 Fr Greb catheter was advanced into the medial aspect of the aneurysm sac and secured. Access to the aneurysm was obtained with a 5 French Greb catheter placed through the bone access needle, as shown in Fig. 3. Angiography was performed through the Greb catheter, confirming a type II endoleak with retrograde opacification of inflow and outflow lumbar arteries. A 2.4 Fr Progreat microcatheter was advanced through the Greb catheter to selectively catheterize the right L5 lumbar artery; however, despite prolonged effort, the artery could not be accessed. Embolization of the aneurysm sac adjacent to the feeding artery and nidus performed using multiple Boston Scientific Embold detachable coils, resulting in slowed but persistent endoleak flow. Approximately 0.5 mL of Obsidio conformable embolic from Boston Scientific was then administered, resulting in occlusion of the lumbar artery without persistent filling of the aneurysm sac. A second microcatheter was advanced into the nidus, and further coil embolization was performed with .018-inch and packing coils, followed by additional 0.035-inch coils through the base catheter. The final angiographic result demonstrated extensive coil and liquid embolic packing of the aneurysm sac with cessation of inflow. Postprocedure, the patient remained hemodynamically stable, with negligible blood loss and no complications. At 8-week follow-up, a CTA was obtained on September 26, 2025. A noncontrast phase was not performed, limiting definitive assessment of residual or recurrent endoleak; however, no definitive endoleak was identified and the aneurysm sac diameter remained unchanged, indicating postprocedural stability.

Discussion

This case demonstrates a rare approach used for gaining access to an aneurysm when anatomical constraints limit the use of conventional approaches, while also preventing the need to resort to more unconventional approaches such as a transcaval approach. Other direct approaches have been demonstrated in recent published research such as a direct posterior transgluteal approach [2].

This case also demonstrates the difficulty surrounding the treatment and management of type 2 endoleaks. The Journal of Vascular Surgery placed the incidence rate of type II endoleaks at 18.8% and the aneurysm-related reintervention rate at 30.4% at 5 years in the most recent study released evaluating patients within the Society for Vascular Surgery VQI registry linked to Medicare claims (VQI-Medicare) [3]. Many type 2 endoleaks resolve within 6 months, but a small percentage will expand [4] as seen in our patient.

Unfortunately, reintervention does not reliably prevent the continued expansion of the aneurysmal sac [5] as witnessed in our case with a patient undergoing 4 reintervention procedures. The Journal of Vascular Surgery states that the overall benefit of reintervention for isolated type 2 endoleaks remains to be defined [6].

Conclusion

In this case report, we discuss a rare approach used for embolization of an aneurysmal sac for persistent type II endoleak when presented with anatomical limitations for conventional approaches. This patient’s case presentation and medical history highlights the difficulties in management of type II endoleaks.

Arbitrage-Free Pricing with Diffusion-Dependent Jumps Thanks: * Corresponding Author
Hamza Virk*, Yihren Wu, Majnu John

Abstract
. Standard jump-diffusion models assume independence between jumps and diffusion components. We develop a multi-type jump-diffusion model where jump occurrence and magnitude depend on contemporaneous diffusion movements. Unlike previous one-sided models that create arbitrage opportunities, our framework includes upward and downward jumps triggered by both large upward and large downward diffusion increments. We derive the explicit no-arbitrage condition linking the physical drift to model parameters and market risk premia by constructing an Equivalent Martingale Measure using Girsanov’s theorem and a normalized Esscher transform. This condition provides a rigorous foundation for arbitrage-free pricing in models with diffusion-dependent jumps.

1.
Introduction

Soon after the celebrated Black-Scholes asset pricing model
[
1
]
, Merton introduced a jump-diffusion model
[
7
]
to deal with stylized facts from market data. In this model, the jumps are assumed independent from the diffusion term. This independence assumption provided a simple enough setting for one to find a risk-neutral measure for the asset price, and Merton was able to produce an analytic solution to the option prices based on this measurement.

Market data suggests that jumps and diffusion processes are not independent. To this end, the authors in
[
8
]
proposed a model in which jumps are triggered by recent market activities. Roughly, when the market drops by a predetermined amount over a certain time window, an upward jump is triggered. The authors refer to this jump-diffusion model as a market recovery model, it consists of only upward jumps to recover the market drop. They were able to compute the risk neutral rate and the resulting option prices are substantially different from the Black-Scholes prices.

In addition to the upward jump following a drop in the market proposed in
[
8
]
, there are three other types of jumps. A downward jump following a drop in the market, an upward or downward jump following a rise in the market.
The jumps proposed in
[
8
]
result in behavior commonly referred to as buy-on-the-dip. There are similar phrases to describe the other types of jumps: rush to exit, chasing after the market, and taking profit off the table. In a separate paper, when the market data is analyzed using the hidden Markov model
[
6
]
, the distribution of these four types of jumps in various states will be shown to explain the transition of the market between these states.

The purpose of this paper is to present the risk-neutral measure for the jump-diffusion model where all four types of jumps are included.

We achieve this by:

(1)

Formalizing a Multi-Type Jump Model:
We extend the previous framework to include four distinct jump scenarios with explicit trigger conditions, state-dependent jump probabilities, and jump size distributions under the physical measure
\mathbb{P}
.

(2)

Constructing an Equivalent Martingale Measure (EMM):
We leverage the Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing
[
5
]
by constructing an EMM
\mathbb{Q}
through Girsanov’s theorem
[
4
]
for diffusion and a normalized Esscher transform
[
2
,
3
]
for state-dependent jumps.

(3)

Deriving the Explicit No-Arbitrage Condition:
By enforcing the martingale property under the EMM
\mathbb{Q}
, we derive a precise condition relating the physical drift
\mu
to the risk-free rate
r
, volatility
\sigma
, jump parameters, and market prices of diffusion and jump risks.

2
details the model under the physical measure.
Section

3
introduces the no-arbitrage framework and change of measure.
Section

4
describes dynamics under the risk-neutral measure.
Section

5
presents the main no-arbitrage condition with detailed proof.
Section

6
discusses implications and
Section

7
concludes.

2.
The Model under the Physical Measure (
\mathbb{P}
)

We begin by constructing the asset price model in a discrete-time framework, similar to
[
8
]
, under the physical (real-world) probability measure
\mathbb{P}
.

2.1.
Setup and Assumptions

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space, where
\Omega
is the sample space,
\mathcal{F}
is the sigma-algebra of events,
\mathbb{P}
is the physical probability measure, and
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a filtration representing the flow of information over time, satisfying the usual conditions (right-continuity and completeness). We consider a discrete set of time points
t_{k}=k\tau
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\tau>0
is a small, fixed time interval. For simplicity, we denote
t_{k}
by
t
, for a general
k
and the next time step as
t+\tau
.

We assume the existence of a risk-free asset with its price denoted by
B_{t}
growing at a constant rate
r\geq 0
:

(2.1)

B_{t+\tau}=B_{t}e^{r\tau}

Without loss of generality, we set
B_{0}=1
.

The risky asset’s price
S_{t}
is driven by a standard
\mathbb{P}
-Brownian motion
W_{t}
. Its price dynamics over one interval
[t,t+\tau]
are given by a jump-diffusion process. Let
X_{t}=\ln(S_{t})
be the log-price. The change in log-price is:

(2.2)

X_{t+\tau}-X_{t}=(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}+J_{t+\tau}

where:

•

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the constant expected rate of return (drift) of the asset
S_{t}
.

•

\sigma>0
is the constant volatility of the diffusion component.

•

\Delta W_{t,\tau}=W_{t+\tau}-W_{t}
is the increment of the standard Brownian motion over
[t,t+\tau]
. Under
\mathbb{P}
, given
\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
\Delta W_{t,\tau}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)
.

•

J_{t+\tau}
is the random jump component, whose occurrence and size depend on the realization of
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
.

The asset price
S_{t}
evolves as:

(2.3)

S_{t+\tau}=S_{t}\exp\left[(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}+J_{t+\tau}\right]

2.2.
Diffusion-Dependent Jumps

We define the jump component
J_{t+\tau}
based on the contemporaneous diffusion increment
\Delta W=\Delta W_{t,\tau}
. We define two thresholds,
b_{d}<0
and
b_{u}>0
. These thresholds partition the possible outcomes of
\Delta W
into three regions:

•

Region 1 (Large Downward Diffusion):

\Delta W<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}
(Index
j=1
)

•

Region 2 (Large Upward Diffusion):

\Delta W>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}
(Index
j=2
)

•

Region 0 (Normal Diffusion):

b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\leq\Delta W\leq b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}
(Index
j=0
)

Assumption 2.1

(Jump Structure under
\mathbb{P}
)
.

Let
\Delta W=\Delta W_{t,\tau}
. The jump
J_{t+\tau}
is determined as follows:

(1)

If
\Delta W
is in Region
j\in\{1,2\}
:

•

With probability
p_{ju}(\Delta W)
, an upward jump
J_{ju}
occurs, where
J_{ju}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{ju},\delta_{ju}^{2})
.

•

With probability
p_{jd}(\Delta W)
, a downward jump
J_{jd}
occurs, where
J_{jd}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jd},\delta_{jd}^{2})
.

•

With probability
p_{j0}(\Delta W)=1-p_{ju}(\Delta W)-p_{jd}(\Delta W)
, no jump occurs (
J_{t+\tau}=0
).

(2)

If
\Delta W
is in Region 0:

•

With probability
1
, no jump occurs (
J_{t+\tau}=0
).

We assume that the jump probabilities
p_{jk}(\Delta W)
are nonnegative and sum to 1 within each scenario (i.e.,
p_{ju}(\Delta W)+p_{jd}(\Delta W)+p_{j0}(\Delta W)=1
if
\Delta W
is in Region
j\in\{1,2\}
). We also assume that for
k=u,d,0
,
p_{jk}(\Delta W)
are
\mathcal{F}_{t+\tau}
-measurable. For tractability and clarity in this initial theoretical framework, we assume that
p_{jk}(\Delta W)=p_{jk}
(constants within their respective trigger regions), and that the jump parameters
\nu_{jk}(\Delta W)=\nu_{jk}
and
\delta_{jk}(\Delta W)=\delta_{jk}
are also constants. The framework can be extended to state-dependent parameters, though it would increase complexity. We require
\delta_{jk}^{2}>0
.

Assumption 2.2

(Integrability)
.

For all jump types
j=1,2,0,\;\mathrm{and}\;k=u,d,0
, jump sizes
J_{jk}
have finite exponential moments under
\mathbb{P}
. That is,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{cJ_{jk}}]<\infty
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Since we assume
J_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk},\delta_{jk}^{2})
, this condition is always satisfied, as the Moment Generating Function (MGF) of a Normal distribution exists for all real arguments. This ensures the existence of the Cumulant Generating Function (CGF) and MGF used later.

Assumption 2.3

(Temporal Independence)
.

The increments
(\Delta W_{t,\tau},J_{t+\tau})
are independent across time steps
t=0,\tau,2\tau,\dots
. That is, the pair
(\Delta W_{t,\tau},J_{t+\tau})
(whose structure depends on
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
) is independent of
(\Delta W_{s,\tau},J_{s+\tau})
for all
s<t-\tau
. This is a simplifying assumption, common in discrete-time models, crucial for constructing the multi-period Radon-Nikodym derivative and applying the Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing (FTAP) over the full horizon.

2.3.
Physical Measure Dynamics Formally Stated

Theorem 2.4

(Asset Price Dynamics under
\mathbb{P}
)
.

Let
S_{t}
be the asset price at time
t
. Under
2.1
, the asset price at time
t+\tau
is given by:

(2.4)

S_{t+\tau}=S_{t}\exp\left[(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}\right]\times Y_{t+\tau}

where
Y_{t+\tau}=\exp(J_{t+\tau})
is the jump size factor, and
J_{t+\tau}
is defined as:

(2.5)

\displaystyle J_{t+\tau}=\begin{cases}J_{1u}&\text{with probability }p_{1u}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\\
J_{1d}&\text{with probability }p_{1d}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\\
0&\text{with probability }p_{10}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\\
J_{2u}&\text{with probability }p_{2u}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\\
J_{2d}&\text{with probability }p_{2d}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\\
0&\text{with probability }p_{20}\text{ if }\Delta W_{t,\tau}>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\\
0&\text{with probability }1\text{ if }b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\leq\Delta W_{t,\tau}\leq b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\end{cases}

The jumps
J_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk},\delta_{jk}^{2})
are drawn independently of other randomness, conditional on being in the specified region and the specific jump type occurring.

Proof.

This follows directly by exponentiating the log-price dynamics
X_{t+\tau}-X_{t}
from
Eq.

2.2
:

\displaystyle S_{t+\tau}

\displaystyle=S_{t}\exp(X_{t+\tau}-X_{t})

\displaystyle=S_{t}\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}+J_{t+\tau}\right)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=S_{t}\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}\right)\exp(J_{t+\tau})

We identify
Y_{t+\tau}=\exp(J_{t+\tau})
. The probabilistic structure of
J_{t+\tau}
is explicitly given by
2.1
for constant probabilities
p_{jk}
.
∎

3.
The No-Arbitrage Framework

To ensure our model is economically viable, we must impose conditions that prevent arbitrage opportunities. The cornerstone of this is the Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing (FTAP).

3.1.
The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing (FTAP)

Theorem 3.1

(FTAP for Discrete Time -
[
5
]
)
.

In a discrete-time financial market model with a finite number of assets and time periods, satisfying certain conditions (like the absence of redundant assets, which our single risky asset model satisfies), the condition of No Arbitrage (NA) is equivalent to the existence of a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
, which is equivalent to the physical measure
\mathbb{P}
(i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(A)=0\iff\mathbb{Q}(A)=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}
), such that the discounted prices of all traded assets are martingales under
\mathbb{Q}
.

For our model with one risky asset
S_{t}
and a risk-free asset
B_{t}
, this implies there exists an EMM
\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}
such that the discounted price process
S_{t}^{disc}=S_{t}/B_{t}=S_{t}e^{-rt}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-martingale:

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[S_{t+\tau}^{disc}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=S_{t}^{disc}

which is equivalent to stating that the expected return under
\mathbb{Q}
is the risk-free rate:

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\frac{S_{t+\tau}}{S_{t}}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=e^{r\tau}

3.2.
Constructing the Equivalent Martingale Measure (
\mathbb{Q}
)

To find
\mathbb{Q}
, we define its Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. Due to our temporal independence assumption (
2.3
), the multi-period Radon-Nikodym derivative
L_{T}=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}
can be constructed as a product of one-step kernels
L_{\tau}
:

(3.3)

L_{t}=\prod_{k=0}^{t/\tau-1}L_{k\tau}(\Delta W_{k\tau,\tau},J_{(k+1)\tau})

The one-step kernel
L_{\tau}
(which we denote as
L_{\tau}(\Delta W,J)
omitting
t
for brevity when referring to a generic step) changes the measure for both diffusion and jump risks.

Definition 3.2

(Cumulant Generating Function & Normalizers)
.

Let
\eta_{jk}
be the (constant) market price of risk associated with jump type indexed
jk
.
The Cumulant Generating Function (CGF) for
J_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk},\delta_{jk}^{2})
is:

(3.4)

\kappa_{jk}(\eta)=\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta J_{jk}}]=\eta\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}

This exists because
J_{jk}
is Normally distributed (
2.2
).
We define region-specific normalizers
Z_{j}(\Delta W)
. Given our assumption of constant
p_{jk}
, these become constants
Z_{j}
:

(3.5)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=p_{1u}e^{\kappa_{1u}(\eta_{1u})}+p_{1d}e^{\kappa_{1d}(\eta_{1d})}+p_{10}

(3.6)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=p_{2u}e^{\kappa_{2u}(\eta_{2u})}+p_{2d}e^{\kappa_{2d}(\eta_{2d})}+p_{20}

(3.7)

\displaystyle Z_{0}

\displaystyle=1

We define
Z(\Delta W)=Z_{j}
if
\Delta W
is in Region
j
. This
Z(\Delta W)
represents the expected value of the unnormalized Esscher kernel for jumps within each region, conditional on
\Delta W
being in that region, under
\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 3.3

(Radon-Nikodym Derivative
L_{\tau}
)
.

Let
\Delta W=\Delta W_{t,\tau}
and
J=J_{t+\tau}
. Let
\gamma_{D}
be the market price of diffusion risk. The one-step Radon-Nikodym derivative kernel
L_{\tau}(\Delta W,J)
is defined as:

(3.8)

L_{\tau}(\Delta W,J)=\underbrace{\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)}_{L_{D}(\Delta W)}\times\underbrace{\Psi(J,\Delta W)}_{\text{Jump Kernel}}

where
L_{D}(\Delta W)
is the Girsanov kernel for diffusion, and
\Psi(J,\Delta W)
is the normalized Esscher kernel for jumps:

(3.9)

\Psi(J,\Delta W)=\frac{1}{Z(\Delta W)}\times\begin{cases}e^{\eta_{jk}J}&\text{if jump }J_{jk}\text{ occurs in Region }j\\
1&\text{if no jump occurs (in Region $j$ or $0$)}\end{cases}

More explicitly, if
\Delta W
is in Region
j\in\{1,2\}
:

•

If jump
J_{ju}
occurs,
\Psi(J_{ju},\Delta W)=\frac{e^{\eta_{ju}J_{ju}}}{Z_{j}}
.

•

If jump
J_{jd}
occurs,
\Psi(J_{jd},\Delta W)=\frac{e^{\eta_{jd}J_{jd}}}{Z_{j}}
.

•

If no jump occurs,
\Psi(0,\Delta W)=\frac{1}{Z_{j}}
.

If
\Delta W
is in Region 0,
Z(\Delta W)=Z_{0}=1
, and no jump occurs (
J=0
), so
\Psi(0,\Delta W)=\frac{1}{1}\times 1=1
.

Lemma 3.4

(Validity of
L_{\tau}
)
.

The Radon-Nikodym derivative
L_{\tau}
defined in
Definition

3.3
satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=1
.

Proof.

Since the structure of
L_{\tau}
depends only on
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
and
J_{t+\tau}
, which are independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
by
2.3
(given parameters),
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}]
. We use the Law of Total Expectation by conditioning on
\Delta W=x
:

(3.10)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W]\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W=x]f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

where
f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)
is the PDF of
\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)
. First, we calculate the inner conditional expectation:

(3.11)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W=x]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{D}(x)\Psi(J,x)|\Delta W=x]

Since
L_{D}(x)
depends only on
x
, it can be factored out of the conditional expectation over
J
:

(3.12)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W=x]=L_{D}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|\Delta W=x]

Now we calculate
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|\Delta W=x]
. We consider the three regions for
x
:

•

If
x<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}
(Region 1):
Here
Z(x)=Z_{1}
.

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|x]

\displaystyle=p_{1u}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{e^{\eta_{1u}J_{1u}}}{Z_{1}}\bigg|x\right]+p_{1d}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{e^{\eta_{1d}J_{1d}}}{Z_{1}}\bigg|x\right]+p_{10}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{1}}\bigg|x\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{1}}\left(p_{1u}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{1u}J_{1u}}]+p_{1d}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{1d}J_{1d}}]+p_{10}\cdot 1\right)

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{1}}\left(p_{1u}e^{\kappa_{1u}(\eta_{1u})}+p_{1d}e^{\kappa_{1d}(\eta_{1d})}+p_{10}\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{1}}{Z_{1}}=1\quad\text{(using \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:Z1_def})}

In
Eq.

3.14
, we use the linearity of expectation and that
J_{jk}
is independent of the specific value of
x
once we are in Region 1. In
Eq.

3.15
, we use the definition of the CGF
\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})=\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}]
, so
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}]=e^{\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}
.

•

If
x>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}
(Region 2):
By an identical argument, using
Z_{2}
and
p_{2k}
, we find
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|x]=1
.

•

If
b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\leq x\leq b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}
(Region 0):
Here
Z(x)=Z_{0}=1
. No jump occurs (
J=0
), so by
Eq.

3.9
,
\Psi(0,x)=\frac{1}{1}\times 1=1
. Thus,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|x]=1
.

Since
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,\Delta W)|\Delta W]=1
for all possible values of
\Delta W
, the full expectation becomes:

(3.17)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{D}(\Delta W)\times 1]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)\right]

Let
A=-\gamma_{D}\sigma
. Then we are calculating
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[e^{A\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\tau}\right]
. Since
\Delta W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)
,
A\Delta W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,A^{2}\tau)
. The MGF of
\Delta W
is
M_{\Delta W}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[e^{s\Delta W}\right]=e^{s^{2}\tau/2}
.

(3.18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}]

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[e^{-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W}\right]

(3.19)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}M_{\Delta W}(-\gamma_{D}\sigma)

(3.20)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}\exp\left(\frac{(-\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}{2}\right)

(3.21)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}\exp\left(\frac{\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau}{2}\right)=e^{0}=1

Thus,
L_{\tau}
is a valid one-step Radon-Nikodym density.
∎

4.
The Model under the Risk-Neutral Measure (
\mathbb{Q}
)

Using
L_{\tau}
, we find the dynamics under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
.

4.1.
Diffusion under
\mathbb{Q}

Proposition 4.1

(Diffusion under
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

Under the measure
\mathbb{Q}
defined by
L_{\tau}
, the process
W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}=W_{t}+\gamma_{D}\sigma t
is a standard
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion. Consequently, the original increment
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
has the following distribution under
\mathbb{Q}
:

(4.1)

\Delta W_{t,\tau}\sim\mathcal{N}(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau,\tau)

Proof.

This is a standard result from Girsanov’s theorem. We explicitly calculate the mean of
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
under
\mathbb{Q}
. For any random variable
X
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[X]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}X]
.

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}\Delta W_{t,\tau}]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{D}(\Delta W_{t,\tau})\Psi(J,\Delta W_{t,\tau})\Delta W_{t,\tau}\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Delta W_{t,\tau}L_{D}(\Delta W_{t,\tau})\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,\Delta W_{t,\tau})|\Delta W_{t,\tau}]\right]

which is the Law of Total Expectation. As shown in the proof of
Lemma

3.4
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,\Delta W_{t,\tau})|\Delta W_{t,\tau}]=1
.

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Delta W_{t,\tau}L_{D}(\Delta W_{t,\tau})\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Delta W_{t,\tau}\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W_{t,\tau}-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)\right]

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma x-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+2\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau x}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

To evaluate the integral, we complete the square in the exponent:
x^{2}+2\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau x=(x+\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)^{2}-(\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)^{2}
and simplify:

(4.9)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)\quad\times\\
&\qquad\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\exp\left(-\frac{(x+\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)^{2}-(\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\exp\left(-\frac{(x+\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

Let
y=x+\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau
. Then
x=y-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau
, and
\,\mathrm{d}x=\,\mathrm{d}y
. The limits of integration remain
(-\infty,\infty)
.

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(y-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\right]

The first integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y=0
because the integrand is an odd function (
y
is odd,
\exp(-y^{2}/(2\tau))
is even).
The second integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y=\sqrt{2\pi\tau}
(this is the integral of the kernel of a Normal PDF
N(0,\tau)
).

(4.12)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}[0-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau\sqrt{2\pi\tau}]=-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau

The variance calculation
\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta W_{t,\tau})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[(\Delta W_{t,\tau})^{2}]-(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Delta W_{t,\tau}])^{2}
would similarly show that
\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta W_{t,\tau})=\tau
.
Thus, under
\mathbb{Q}
,
\Delta W_{t,\tau}\sim\mathcal{N}(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau,\tau)
.
The statement
W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}=W_{t}+\gamma_{D}\sigma t
being a
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion is the standard Girsanov theorem statement for this drift change.
∎

4.2.
Jumps under
\mathbb{Q}

Proposition 4.2

(Jumps under
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

Under the measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the jump probabilities
q_{jk}(\Delta W)
and jump size distributions
J_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}
are given as follows, conditional on
\Delta W=x
:

(1)

Probabilities:
Let
Z_{j}
be the normalizer for Region
j
from
Definition

3.2
.

(4.13)

\displaystyle q_{ju}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{ju}e^{\kappa_{ju}(\eta_{ju})}}{Z_{j}}\quad(\text{if }\Delta W=x\text{ in Region }j)

(4.14)

\displaystyle q_{jd}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{jd}e^{\kappa_{jd}(\eta_{jd})}}{Z_{j}}\quad(\text{if }\Delta W=x\text{ in Region }j)

(4.15)

\displaystyle q_{j0}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{j0}}{Z_{j}}\quad(\text{if }\Delta W=x\text{ in Region }j\text{ and no jump specified by Esscher})

For Region 0 (where
j=0
),
Z_{0}=1
,
p_{00}=1
, so
q_{00}(x)=1
. These
q_{jk}(x)
are the risk-neutral probabilities.

(2)

Distributions:
The log-jump size
J_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}
under
\mathbb{Q}
, given that a jump of type
jk
occurs, is:

(4.16)

J_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2},\delta_{jk}^{2})\equiv\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}},\delta_{jk}^{2})

where
\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}
.

Proof.

1. Probabilities
q_{jk}(x)
:

The probability of a specific jump
J_{jk}
occurring, conditional on
\Delta W=x
, under
\mathbb{Q}
is:

(4.17)

q_{jk}(x)=\mathbb{Q}(J=J_{jk}|\Delta W=x)=\frac{\mathbb{Q}(J=J_{jk}\text{ and }\Delta W\in dx)}{\mathbb{Q}(\Delta W\in dx)}

The numerator:
\mathbb{Q}(J=J_{jk}\text{ and }\Delta W\in dx)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}\mathbf{1}_{J=J_{jk},\Delta W\in dx}]
where
dx
is an infinitesimal interval around
x
.
If
\Delta W=x
and jump
J_{jk}
occurs, then
L_{\tau}=L_{D}(x)\frac{e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}}{Z_{j}(x)}
(assuming
x
is in Region
j
).
So, the density for the numerator is:

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(J=J_{jk},\Delta W=x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{D}(x)\frac{e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}}{Z_{j}(x)}\mathbf{1}_{J=J_{jk}}|\Delta W=x\right]f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)

(4.19)

\displaystyle=L_{D}(x)\frac{p_{jk}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}]}{Z_{j}(x)}f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)

(4.20)

\displaystyle=L_{D}(x)\frac{p_{jk}(x)e^{\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}}{Z_{j}(x)}f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)

The denominator:
\mathbb{Q}(\Delta W\in dx)
. The density
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\Delta W=x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x}
is
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W=x]f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)
.
From the proof of
Lemma

3.4
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}|\Delta W=x]=L_{D}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)|x]=L_{D}(x)\times 1=L_{D}(x)
.
So,
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\Delta W=x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x}=L_{D}(x)f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)
.
Dividing the numerator density by the denominator density gives the conditional probability
q_{jk}(x)
:

(4.21)

q_{jk}(x)=\frac{L_{D}(x)\frac{p_{jk}(x)e^{\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}}{Z_{j}(x)}f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)}{L_{D}(x)f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)}=\frac{p_{jk}(x)e^{\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}}{Z_{j}(x)}

This matches
Eq.

4.13
and
Eq.

4.14
(assuming
p_{jk}(x)=p_{jk}
).
For the case of no jump occurring in Region
j
(where specific jumps
J_{ju}
or
J_{jd}
could have occurred), if it happens with probability
p_{j0}
under
\mathbb{P}
:
The Esscher kernel for no jump (
J=0
) is
\Psi(0,x)=\frac{e^{\eta\cdot 0}}{Z_{j}(x)}=\frac{1}{Z_{j}(x)}
. So,
e^{\kappa(\eta)}
term is effectively
e^{0}=1
.
Then
q_{j0}(x)=\frac{p_{j0}(x)\cdot 1}{Z_{j}(x)}
, matching
Eq.

4.15
.
These probabilities sum to 1 within each region
j
under
\mathbb{Q}
:

\sum_{k\in\{u,d,0\}}q_{jk}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{j}(x)}(p_{ju}e^{\kappa_{ju}}+p_{jd}e^{\kappa_{jd}}+p_{j0})=\frac{Z_{j}(x)}{Z_{j}(x)}=1

2. Distributions of
J_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}
:

The Esscher transform implies that the PDF of
J_{jk}
under
\mathbb{Q}
, denoted
f_{\mathbb{Q}}(j_{jk})
, is related to its PDF under
\mathbb{P}
,
f_{\mathbb{P}}(j_{jk})
, by:

(4.22)

f_{\mathbb{Q}}(j_{jk}|J\text{ is type }jk)=\frac{e^{\eta_{jk}j_{jk}}f_{\mathbb{P}}(j_{jk})}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\eta_{jk}J_{jk}}]}=\frac{e^{\eta_{jk}j_{jk}}f_{\mathbb{P}}(j_{jk})}{e^{\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}}

Given
J_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk},\delta_{jk}^{2})
under
\mathbb{P}
,
f_{\mathbb{P}}(j_{jk})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\delta_{jk}}\exp\left(-\frac{(j_{jk}-\nu_{jk})^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\right)
.

(4.23)

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{Q}}(j_{jk})

\displaystyle=e^{\eta_{jk}j_{jk}-\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\delta_{jk}}\exp\left(-\frac{(j_{jk}-\nu_{jk})^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\right)

(4.24)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\delta_{jk}}\exp\left(\eta_{jk}j_{jk}-(\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{jk}^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2})-\frac{j_{jk}^{2}-2j_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\nu_{jk}^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\right)

The term in the exponent is:

(4.25)

\displaystyle\frac{2\delta_{jk}^{2}\eta_{jk}j_{jk}-2\delta_{jk}^{2}\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}-\eta_{jk}^{2}\delta_{jk}^{4}-(j_{jk}^{2}-2j_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\nu_{jk}^{2})}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}

(4.26)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\left[j_{jk}^{2}-2j_{jk}\nu_{jk}-2\delta_{jk}^{2}\eta_{jk}j_{jk}+\nu_{jk}^{2}+2\delta_{jk}^{2}\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}^{2}\delta_{jk}^{4}\right]

(4.27)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\left[j_{jk}^{2}-2j_{jk}(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2})+(\nu_{jk}^{2}+2\nu_{jk}\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}+(\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2})^{2})\right]

(4.28)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\left[j_{jk}^{2}-2j_{jk}(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2})+(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2})^{2}\right]

(4.29)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(j_{jk}-(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}))^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}

Let
\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}
. Then the exponent is
-\frac{(j_{jk}-\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}})^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}
.
So,
f_{\mathbb{Q}}(j_{jk})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\delta_{jk}}\exp\left(-\frac{(j_{jk}-\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}})^{2}}{2\delta_{jk}^{2}}\right)
. This is the PDF of a
\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}},\delta_{jk}^{2})
distribution.
∎

4.3.
Risk-Neutral Dynamics and Multi-Period Martingale

Theorem 4.3

(Asset Price Dynamics under
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

Under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the asset price evolves as:

(4.30)

S_{t+\tau}=S_{t}\exp\left[(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma(\Delta W_{t,\tau}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau)+J_{t+\tau}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right]

where
\Delta W_{t,\tau}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\Delta W_{t,\tau}+\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)
under
\mathbb{Q}
, and
J_{t+\tau}^{\mathbb{Q}}
is the jump component whose occurrence and distribution follow
Proposition

4.2
, triggered by
x=\Delta W_{t,\tau}=\Delta W_{t,\tau}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\gamma_{D}\sigma\tau
.

Proof.

This follows by substituting the
\mathbb{Q}
-distributions for the diffusion increment from
Proposition

4.1
(expressed in terms of the
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion
\Delta W^{\mathbb{Q}}
) and the jump characteristics (probabilities and distributions) from
Proposition

4.2
into the general asset price evolution equation
Eq.

2.3
. The key is that the structure of jump triggering remains dependent on the actual path of
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
, which is now understood in terms of its
\mathbb{Q}
-distribution. ∎

Lemma 4.4

(Exponential Martingale)
.

Let
L_{t}=\prod_{i=0}^{t/\tau-1}L_{i\tau}(\Delta W_{i\tau,\tau},J_{(i+1)\tau})
be the multi-period Radon-Nikodym derivative for
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Under the assumption of temporal independence (
2.3
),
L_{t}
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
-martingale with
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t}]=1
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
t_{k}=k\tau
.
Base case (
k=0,t_{0}=0
):
L_{0}=1
(empty product). So
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{0}]=1
.
Inductive step: Assume
L_{t_{k}}
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}},\mathbb{P})
-martingale with
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}}]=1
.
Consider
L_{t_{k+1}}=L_{t_{k}}\cdot L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})
.
We want to show
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k+1}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]=L_{t_{k}}
.

(4.31)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k+1}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}}\cdot L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]

(4.32)

\displaystyle=L_{t_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}],

since
L_{t_{k}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}-
measurable. By
2.3
, the increment
(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})
and thus
L_{t_{k}\tau}
(which is a function of this increment) is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}
. Therefore:

(4.33)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})]

From
Lemma

3.4
, we know that the expectation of the one-step kernel is 1:

(4.34)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k}\tau}(\Delta W_{t_{k},\tau},J_{(t_{k}+1)\tau})]=1

Substituting this back:

(4.35)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t_{k+1}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}]=L_{t_{k}}\times 1=L_{t_{k}}

Thus,
L_{t}
is a martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
. By the tower property of conditional expectation,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{0}]]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{0}]=1
.
∎

This lemma is essential as it ensures that the measure
\mathbb{Q}
defined by
L_{T}
is a valid probability measure equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
over the entire horizon
[0,T]
.

5.
The No-Arbitrage Condition

We now derive the explicit condition on the physical drift
\mu
.

5.1.
Deriving the Condition in Full Detail

The no-arbitrage condition from
Theorem

3.1
is
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S_{t+\tau}/S_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=e^{r\tau}
. Using the definition of expectation under an EMM, this is equivalent to:

(5.1)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{\tau}\frac{S_{t+\tau}}{S_{t}}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=e^{r\tau}

Since
L_{\tau}
and
S_{t+\tau}/S_{t}
(which depend on
\Delta W_{t,\tau}
and
J_{t+\tau}
) are independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
given
2.3
, the conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
can be dropped for the expectation:

(5.2)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{\tau}\frac{S_{t+\tau}}{S_{t}}\right]=e^{r\tau}

Substitute the expressions for
L_{\tau}
from
Definition

3.3
and
S_{t+\tau}/S_{t}
from
Eq.

2.3
:

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{D}(\Delta W)\Psi(J,\Delta W)\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W+J\right)\right]=e^{r\tau}

where
\Delta W=\Delta W_{t,\tau}
and
J=J_{t+\tau}
.
We can group the terms that do not depend on
J
(given
\Delta W
) outside the conditional expectation on
J
:

(5.4)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{D}(\Delta W)\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,\Delta W)e^{J}|\Delta W]\right]=e^{r\tau}

Let’s analyze the inner conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)e^{J}|\Delta W=x]
. Let
x
be in Region
j
.

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)e^{J}|x]

\displaystyle=p_{ju}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{e^{\eta_{ju}J_{ju}}}{Z_{j}}e^{J_{ju}}\right]+p_{jd}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{e^{\eta_{jd}J_{jd}}}{Z_{j}}e^{J_{jd}}\right]+p_{j0}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{j}}e^{0}\right]

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{j}}\left(p_{ju}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{(1+\eta_{ju})J_{ju}}]+p_{jd}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{(1+\eta_{jd})J_{jd}}]+p_{j0}\right)

For
J_{jk}\sim\mathcal{N}(\nu_{jk},\delta_{jk}^{2})
, its moment generating function is

M_{J_{jk}}(s)=\exp(s\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}s^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}).

So,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{(1+\eta_{jk})J_{jk}}]=\exp((1+\eta_{jk})\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\eta_{jk})^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2})
.

Let’s expand the exponent:

(5.7)

\displaystyle(1+\eta_{jk})\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\eta_{jk})^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}

\displaystyle=\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+2\eta_{jk}+\eta_{jk}^{2})\delta_{jk}^{2}

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{jk}^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}

Recall
\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2}
. The exponent can be rewritten as:

(5.9)

(\nu_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\delta_{jk}^{2})+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}+\eta_{jk}\nu_{jk}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{jk}^{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}=\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{jk}^{2}+\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk})

So,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{(1+\eta_{jk})J_{jk}}]=\exp(\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{jk}^{2})\exp(\kappa_{jk}(\eta_{jk}))
.
Therefore, using
Eqs.

4.13
,
4.14
and
4.15
:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi(J,x)e^{J}|x]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{j}}\left(p_{ju}e^{\kappa_{ju}(\eta_{ju})}e^{\nu_{ju}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{ju}^{2}/2}+p_{jd}e^{\kappa_{jd}(\eta_{jd})}e^{\nu_{jd}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{jd}^{2}/2}+p_{j0}\right)

(5.11)

\displaystyle=q_{ju}(x)e^{\nu_{ju}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{ju}^{2}/2}+q_{jd}(x)e^{\nu_{jd}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{jd}^{2}/2}+q_{j0}(x)

(5.12)

\displaystyle=M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)

where
M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[e^{J^{\mathbb{Q}}}|\Delta W=x]
is the conditional MGF of the jump factor
\exp(J^{\mathbb{Q}})
under
\mathbb{Q}
:

(5.13)

M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\begin{cases}q_{1u}(x)e^{\nu_{1u}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{1u}^{2}/2}+q_{1d}(x)e^{\nu_{1d}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{1d}^{2}/2}+q_{10}(x)&x<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\\
q_{2u}(x)e^{\nu_{2u}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{2u}^{2}/2}+q_{2d}(x)e^{\nu_{2d}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{2d}^{2}/2}+q_{20}(x)&x>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\\
1&b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\leq x\leq b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\end{cases}

Substituting
Eq.

5.12
back into the main expectation:

(5.14)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[L_{D}(\Delta W)\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W\right)M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta W)\right]=e^{r\tau}

Now, substitute
L_{D}(\Delta W)=\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau\right)
:

(5.15)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\exp\left(-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W-\frac{(\gamma_{D}\sigma)^{2}\tau}{2}\right)\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W\right)M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta W)\right]=e^{r\tau}

Combine the arguments of the exponential functions:

(5.16)

\displaystyle-\gamma_{D}\sigma\Delta W-\frac{\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau}{2}+(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\sigma\Delta W

(5.17)

\displaystyle=(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+(\sigma-\gamma_{D}\sigma)\Delta W

So the expectation becomes:

(5.18)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\exp\left((\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+(\sigma-\gamma_{D}\sigma)\Delta W\right)M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta W)\right]=e^{r\tau}

Let
K=(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau
and
G=\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})
.
Let
f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)
denote the PDF of
\Delta W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)
, which is
f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp(-x^{2}/(2\tau))
.

The expectation in
Eq.

5.18
can be written as an integral:

(5.19)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(K+Gx\right)M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{r\tau}

Since
K
does not depend on
x
, we can factor
e^{K}
out of the integral:

(5.20)

e^{K}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{Gx}M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{r\tau}

Combine the exponential terms involving
x
inside the integral:

(5.21)

e^{K}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(Gx-\frac{x^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{r\tau}

We complete the square for the term
Gx-\frac{x^{2}}{2\tau}
in the exponent:

(5.22)

\displaystyle Gx-\frac{x^{2}}{2\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\tau}(x^{2}-2G\tau x)

(5.23)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\tau}(x^{2}-2G\tau x+(G\tau)^{2}-(G\tau)^{2})\quad\text{(add and subtract }(G\tau)^{2}\text{)}

(5.24)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left((x-G\tau)^{2}-(G\tau)^{2}\right)

(5.25)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(x-G\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{(G\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}=-\frac{(x-G\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{G^{2}\tau}{2}

Substitute this back into the integral:

(5.26)

e^{K}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-G\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{G^{2}\tau}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{r\tau}

The term
\exp(G^{2}\tau/2)
does not depend on
x
and can be factored out of the integral:

(5.27)

e^{K}\exp\left(\frac{G^{2}\tau}{2}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-G\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{r\tau}

This can be written as:

(5.28)

e^{K+G^{2}\tau/2}\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]=e^{r\tau}

where
\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation with respect to a random variable
X
that follows a Normal distribution with mean
G\tau
and variance
\tau
, i.e.,
X\sim\mathcal{N}(G\tau,\tau)
.
Now, take the natural logarithm of both sides of
Eq.

5.28
:

(5.29)

K+\frac{G^{2}\tau}{2}+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

Substitute back the expressions for
K=(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau
and
G=\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})
:

(5.30)

(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\frac{(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D}))^{2}\tau}{2}+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

Expand the term
\frac{(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D}))^{2}\tau}{2}
:

(5.31)

\frac{\sigma^{2}(1-\gamma_{D})^{2}\tau}{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}(1-2\gamma_{D}+\gamma_{D}^{2})\tau}{2}=\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\tau

Substitute this into the equation:

(5.32)

(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+(\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2})\tau+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

Combine the terms involving
\tau
:

(5.33)

\displaystyle\mu\tau-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\tau-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\tau-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

(5.34)

\displaystyle\mu\tau+(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\tau)+(-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau)-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}\tau+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

(5.35)

\displaystyle\mu\tau+0+0-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}\tau+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

(5.36)

\displaystyle(\mu-\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2})\tau+\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)=r\tau

Now, solve for
\mu
:

(5.37)

\mu\tau=r\tau+\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}\tau-\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)

Divide by
\tau
(assuming
\tau>0
):

(5.38)

\mu=r+\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}-\frac{1}{\tau}\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)

The expectation
\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]
is taken with respect to
X\sim\mathcal{N}(G\tau,\tau)
, where
G\tau=\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau
.

Theorem 5.1

(The No-Arbitrage Condition)
.

For the asset price model defined in
Section

2
to be free of arbitrage, given a set of market prices of risk
\gamma_{D}
(for diffusion) and
\eta_{jk}
(for jumps), the physical drift
\mu
of the asset
S_{t}
must satisfy the following condition:

(5.39)

\mu=r+\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}-\frac{1}{\tau}\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{N(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau,\tau)}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)

where
M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[e^{J^{\mathbb{Q}}}|\Delta W=x]
is the conditional Moment Generating Function (MGF) of the jump factor
\exp(J^{\mathbb{Q}})
under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, evaluated at
\Delta W=x
:

(5.40)

M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\begin{cases}q_{1u}(x)e^{\nu_{1u}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{1u}^{2}/2}+q_{1d}(x)e^{\nu_{1d}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{1d}^{2}/2}+q_{10}(x)&\text{if }x<b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\\
q_{2u}(x)e^{\nu_{2u}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{2u}^{2}/2}+q_{2d}(x)e^{\nu_{2d}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\delta_{2d}^{2}/2}+q_{20}(x)&\text{if }x>b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\\
1&\text{if }b_{d}\sqrt{\tau}\leq x\leq b_{u}\sqrt{\tau}\end{cases}

with the risk-neutral jump probabilities
q_{jk}(x)
and risk-neutral jump means
\nu_{jk}^{\mathbb{Q}}
defined in
Proposition

4.2
. The expectation
\mathbb{E}_{N(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau,\tau)}[\cdot]
is taken with respect to a random variable
X
distributed as
\mathcal{N}(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau,\tau)
, which means:

(5.41)

\mathbb{E}_{N(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau,\tau)}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}M_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau)^{2}}{2\tau}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

Proof.

The detailed derivation provided above, from
Eq.

5.1
to
Eq.

5.38
, constitutes the proof of this theorem. The core of the proof lies in relating the martingale condition under
\mathbb{Q}
back to an expectation under
\mathbb{P}
via
L_{\tau}
, and then evaluating this
\mathbb{P}
-expectation. The derivation shows that if
\mu
satisfies the stated condition, then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[L_{\tau}S_{t+\tau}/S_{t}]=e^{r\tau}
, which is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S_{t+\tau}/S_{t}]=e^{r\tau}
, thus ensuring no arbitrage by
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(The Guarantee of No Arbitrage)
.

Theorem

5.1
provides the explicit ”guarantee” against arbitrage. If the parameters of the model under the physical measure
\mathbb{P}
and the chosen market prices of risk are such that the equation for
\mu
holds, then the constructed measure
\mathbb{Q}
is an EMM, ensuring by FTAP that the model is arbitrage-free. Different choices of risk premia would lead to different (but still arbitrage-free) physical drifts
\mu
or, if
\mu
is fixed, imply certain market prices of risk.

6.
Discussion

The no-arbitrage condition decomposes the required physical drift into three components:

(6.1)

\mu=\underbrace{r}_{\text{Risk-free rate}}+\underbrace{\gamma_{D}\sigma^{2}}_{\text{Diffusion risk premium}}-\underbrace{\frac{1}{\tau}\ln\left(\mathbb{E}_{N(\sigma(1-\gamma_{D})\tau,\tau)}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]\right)}_{\text{Jump risk adjustment}}

The jump risk adjustment captures the complex impact of state-dependent jumps:

•

If
\mathbb{E}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]>1
, risk-neutralized jumps have positive expected impact, reducing required drift

•

If
\mathbb{E}[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)]<1
, jumps have negative expected impact, requiring higher drift compensation

•

Different
\eta_{jk}
allow differentiated pricing of various jump risks

This framework enables:

(1)

Model Consistency:
Ensuring that any specific parameterization of the model under the physical measure
\mathbb{P}
is internally consistent and does not admit trivial arbitrage strategies.

(2)

Derivative Pricing:
By establishing the EMM
\mathbb{Q}
, derivative securities can be priced using the principle of risk-neutral valuation, i.e.,
\text{Price}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[e^{-r(T-t)}\text{Payoff}_{T}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
.

(3)

Risk Management:
Providing a deeper understanding of how diffusion and state-dependent jump risks interact and how they are priced by the market. This is essential for developing effective hedging strategies and for accurate risk assessment.

The discrete-time approach facilitates direct implementation while building upon established no-arbitrage principles. Extension to continuous time remains an important avenue for future research.

7.
Conclusion

This paper rigorously addresses the theoretical challenge of constructing arbitrage-free models with diffusion-dependent jumps. We have formalized and extended previous work into a comprehensive multi-type jump framework that eliminates arbitrage concerns in one-sided models.

The central achievement is deriving the explicit no-arbitrage condition that precisely links the physical drift to all model parameters and market risk premia. This condition provides a guarantee: any model whose parameters satisfy this relationship is, by construction, arbitrage-free.

Future research directions include investigating the continuous-time limit, developing efficient numerical methods for option pricing and calibration, empirical testing against market data, and extensions to multi-asset scenarios or stochastic volatility models.

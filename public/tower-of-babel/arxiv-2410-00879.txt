L 2 L^{2} -Gamma-Fredholmness for spacetimes
Orville Damaschke

Abstract
. Let M M be a temporal compact globally hyperbolic manifold with Cauchy hypersurface Σ \Sigma which is a Galois covering with respect to a discrete group Γ \Gamma of automorphisms such that the quotient Σ / Γ \Sigma/\Gamma is compact without boundary. We will show Fredholmness of a (spatial) Γ \Gamma -invariant Lorentzian Dirac operator under (anti) Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary conditions in the von Neumann sense, known as ( L 2 L^{2} -) Γ \Gamma -Fredholmness.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

We continue our analysis of Atiyah-Singer Dirac operators on globally hyperbolic spacetimes with non-compact Cauchy boundary which we started with a well-posedness result of the Cauchy problem for the twisted Dirac operator in
[
9

\Gamma

\Sigma
is a Galois covering with respect to a (discrete) group
\Gamma

\Sigma_{\Gamma}:=\Sigma/\Gamma

\Gamma

\Gamma

\Gamma

3.
Geometry and functional analysis in
\Gamma
-setting

This section relies heavily on
[
19

which is supported with additional material from
[
21
]
as well as
[
18
]
. We recall the basic important concepts of functional algebra and analysis on Galois coverings with respect to a group
\Gamma
. The first part deals with topological properties of Galois coverings with a special view on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds. The main objects are
von Neumann algebras
and Hilbert modules with respect to
\Gamma

3.1.
Galois Coverings and
\Gamma
-manifolds

Let
M
be any connected manifold. We denote with
\Gamma
a discrete group which acts freely, freely discontinuous and cocompactly on a non-compact manifold
M
by diffeomorphisms; we denote with
\upepsilon
the neutral element. The quotient map
M\rightarrow M/\Gamma
then becomes a Galois covering with compact base manifold
M/\Gamma
and
\Gamma
acts as deck transformation. We will also use the term
\Gamma
-
manifold
and write
M_{\Gamma}
for
M/\Gamma
. Examples of such manifolds
M
are universal coverings of compact manifolds.

If the
\Gamma
-manifold
M
is equipped with a smooth and complex vector bundle
E
with projection
\pi\,:\,E\rightarrow\,M
, one can define an action of
\Gamma
on the vector bundle
E
through the map
\pi_{\Gamma}|_{p}\,:\,E_{p}\,\rightarrow\,E_{\gamma p}
for all
p\in M,\gamma\in\Gamma
. We presuppose that this map is a linear isomorphism, such that the action on
E
covers the action on
M
, and that the projection
\pi
is
\Gamma
-
equivariant
, i.e.
\pi(\gamma p,v_{\gamma p})=\gamma\pi(p,v_{p})
for all
p,\gamma
, and
v_{p}
a vector at
p
. Like in our main reference
[
19
]
, we call
E
a
\Gamma
-
vector bundle
if it is a vector bundle over a
\Gamma
-manifold such that
E
is the pullback bundle of a vector bundle
E_{\Gamma}:=E/\Gamma
over the compact base
M_{\Gamma}
with respect to the covering map. We can furthermore extend
\pi_{\Gamma}|_{p}
to an isometry by introducing an inner product
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{E_{p}}
on each fibre
E_{p}
for
p\in M
which is either already given on
E
or is induced as pullback of a bundle metric on
E/\Gamma
. In order to extend
\pi_{\Gamma}
to an isometry, we have to require that the bundle metric is
\Gamma
-invariant:

(3.1)

\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{E_{\gamma p}}=\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{E_{p}}\quad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma\,,\,p\in M\quad.

We also equip
M
with a positive,
\Gamma
-invariant smooth density
\,\mathrm{d}\mu
:

(3.2)

\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\gamma p)=\,\mathrm{d}\mu(p)\quad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma\,,\,p\in M\quad.

This can be either directly obtained by lifting a positive smooth density from
M_{\Gamma}
or with a metric on
T(M_{\Gamma})
which induces a
\Gamma
-invariant metric on
TM
and thus a
\Gamma
-invariant smooth density on
M
.

A fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}
is an open subset in
M
such that

(a)

\displaystyle\quad(\gamma_{1}\mathcal{F})\cap(\gamma_{2}\mathcal{F})=\emptyset\quad\forall\,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}

(b)

\displaystyle\quad M=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\left(\overline{\mathcal{F}}\right)

\displaystyle\text{(c)}\quad\overline{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{F}\quad\text{is a null set.}

Moreover, these properties imply that
M
can be viewed as principal
\Gamma
-bundle, i.e.
M\simeq\Gamma\times\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{F}
as representative for the group action.

The compactness of the orbit space allows to define a
\Gamma
-invariant locally finite open covering and a
\Gamma
-invariant partition of union subordinated to this open covering. Since
M_{\Gamma}
is compact, one can take a finite covering of the base by open balls
\mathring{\mathbb{B}}_{j}
,
j\in J
finite index set. These open balls can be lifted to
M
which defines an infinite, but countable covering for
M
due to the countability of
\Gamma
:

(3.3)

M=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
\gamma\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}\gamma(\mathring{\mathbb{B}}_{j})\quad.

A subordinated partition of unity
(\gamma\mathring{\mathbb{B}}_{j},\phi_{j,\gamma})_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
\gamma\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}
can be constructed by taking compactly supported functions
\phi_{j,\gamma}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(\gamma\mathring{\mathbb{B}}_{j},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
which satisfy
\phi_{j,\gamma}(p)=\phi_{j,\upepsilon}(\gamma^{-1}p)
as well as

(3.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
\gamma\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}\phi_{j,\gamma}=1\quad.

Remark 3.1
.

(i)

If we compare property (b) in the characterisation of the fundamental domain with
(
3.3
)
, we observe that the closure
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is the compact orbit space
M_{\Gamma}
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}
is a dense subspace in

.

(ii)

By comparing
(
3.3
)
with
(
A.1
)

\Gamma
-manifolds are indeed examples of manifolds of bounded geometry. The radii-condition on the functions for the partition of unity in

is replaced with the assumption on

to act freely discontinuous.

We have to require for pseudo-Riemannian manifolds that the covering map is a local isometry in order to obtain a pseudo-Riemannian metric on the covering via pullback with the same signature. If

is simply connected, time- and space orientability are also preserved under pullback. Hence we don’t need to impose further modifications than the assumption that the covering maps are local isometries. We refer to
[

if one assumes that the base of the covering is globally hyperbolic. Thus, the covering of Cauchy hypersurfaces are again Cauchy hypersurfaces.

We are going to focus on the following situation: let
\Sigma_{\Gamma}
be a spatial Cauchy hypersurface, such that

is globally hyperbolic with time domain
\mathpzc{T}
, and suppose that

is its Galois covering with respect to

the covering in spatial direction which is again a globally hyperbolic manifold. We call

\Gamma
-manifold
as

3.2.
Von Neumann algebra associated to a
\Gamma
-action

Let
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces and denote with
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
the set of bounded linear operators
A:\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
; we write
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
for
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{2}
. The space
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
with the operator norm is the most prominent example of a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra with identity
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
, composition as multiplication and involution is given by adjoining the operator. The
commutant
of a subspace
\mathscr{N}\subset\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
is

(3.5)

\mathscr{N}^{\prime}:=\left\{A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,AB=BA\quad\forall\,B\in\mathscr{N}\right\}\quad.

It is a closed and unital subalgebra of
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
. A subalgebra
\mathscr{N}\subset\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
with
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathscr{N}
and
A^{\ast}\in\mathscr{N}
for all
A\in\mathscr{N}
is a
von Neumann algebra
if
(\mathscr{N}^{\prime})^{\prime}=\mathscr{N}
.
A von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{N}
is a
factor
if

\mathscr{N}\cap\mathscr{N}^{\prime}=\left\{B\in\mathscr{N}\,|\,AB=BA\quad\forall\,A\in\mathscr{N}\right\}=\left\{\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\quad.

We now want to introduce a von Neumann algebra which we will use in the
\Gamma
-setting. Let
u,v
be two functions
\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
; we define an inner product

(3.6)

\big\langle u\,\big|\,v\big\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}:=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\overline{u(g)}v(g)

and a corresponding Hilbert space of
l^{2}
-functions

(3.7)

\ell^{2}(\Gamma):=\left\{u\,:\,\Gamma\,\rightarrow\,\mathbb{C}\,\Big|\,\big\langle u\,\big|\,u\big\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}<\infty\right\}\quad.

An orthonormal basis in
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
is
\left\{\delta_{\gamma}\right\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
which satisfies
\delta_{\gamma_{1}}(\gamma_{2})=1
if
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
and zero otherwise. The group action
\Gamma
induces
left and right translation operators
on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
which acts on this basis as

(3.8)

l_{\gamma_{1}}\delta_{\gamma_{2}}=\delta_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{\gamma_{1}}\delta_{\gamma_{2}}=\delta_{\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1}}

for
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
. These are unitary operators such that
\gamma\,\mapsto\,l_{\gamma}
and
\gamma\,\mapsto\,r_{\gamma}
become unitary representations of the discrete group in
(
3.7
)
. We denote with
\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)
the smallest von Neumann algebra in
\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
which contains the set
\left\{r_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}
and
\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
as the smallest von Neumann algebra which contains
\left\{l_{\gamma}\,|\,\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}
. It is proven in
[
19
, Thm.2.10]
that the von Neumann algebras
\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
and
\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)
take the form

(3.9)

\begin{split}\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)&=\left\{A\in\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))\,|\,Ar_{\gamma}=r_{\gamma}A\quad\forall\,a,b,\Gamma\in\Gamma\right\}\\
\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)&=\left\{A\in\mathscr{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))\,|\,Al_{\gamma}=l_{\gamma}A\quad\forall\,a,b,\Gamma\in\Gamma\right\}\quad,\end{split}

hence
(\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma))^{\prime}=\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)
respectively
(\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma))^{\prime}=\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
, and that these are factors if and only if all conjugacy classes in
\Gamma/\left\{\upepsilon\right\}
are infinite. Those groups
\Gamma
, for which this is true, are called
i.c.c-groups
(infinite-conjugacy-class). One can define for an operator
A
in either
\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
or
\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
a
trace
via

(3.10)

\uptau_{\Gamma}(A):=\big\langle A\delta_{\gamma}\,\big|\,\delta_{\gamma}\big\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}=\big\langle A\delta_{\upepsilon}\,\big|\,\delta_{\upepsilon}\big\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}

for
\gamma\in\Gamma
; the definition is independent of the choice of
\Gamma
which explains the second equivalence.
This trace shares the same properties on
\mathscr{N}_{r,l}(\Gamma)
with the trace
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\cdot\right)
on
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
This trace gives rise to a dimension function
\dim_{\uptau}
:

\dim_{\uptau}(V):=\uptau_{\Gamma}(P_{V})\quad

for any
V\subset\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
and
P_{V}:\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow V
as orthogonal projection. It is shown in
[
14
, Prop.6.7.4]
that
\dim_{\tau}(\mathscr{N}_{r,l}(\Gamma))\in[0,1]
if
\Gamma
is an i.c.c.-group, i.e.
\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)
and
\mathscr{N}_{l}(\Gamma)
are factors.

(

4.
Sobolev spaces and operators on
\Gamma
-manifolds,
\Gamma
-Projections

We combine the geometric and functional analytic facts from the last section and consider operators which act between sections of
\Gamma
-vector bundles over
\Gamma
-manifolds. We will introduce
L^{2}
- and Sobolev spaces in this setting which turn out to be free Hilbert
\Gamma
-modules. After this, we take a closer look on spectral projections of certain operators and illustrate a
\Gamma
-version of Seeley’s theorem of complex powers.

4.1.
Differential operators in
\Gamma
-setting

Let
E\rightarrow M
be a
\Gamma
-vector bundle over a
\Gamma
-manifold
M
. The left action representation of
\Gamma
on a smooth section
u
of
E
is then described by

(4.1)

(L^{E}_{\gamma}u)(p)=(\pi^{E}_{\Gamma}u)(\gamma^{-1}p)\quad

where
\pi^{E}_{\Gamma}
is an isometry which maps the section at
p
to
(\gamma\cdot p)
. We equip
M
with a
\Gamma
-invariant smooth density
\,\mathrm{d}\mu
, which is either defined by a
\Gamma
-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric or otherwise, and
E
with a
\Gamma
-invariant inner product
\big(\cdot\,\big|\,\cdot\big)_{E_{p}}
on each fiber
E_{p}
. We introduce the inner product

(4.2)

\big\langle u\,\big|\,v\big\rangle_{L^{2}(M,E)}=\int_{M}\big\langle u(p)\,\big|\,v(p)\big\rangle_{E_{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(p)\quad.

The norm closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathsf{c}}(M,E)
with respect to this inner product is the space of square-integrable sections of
E
on
M
which we also denote with
L^{2}(M,E)
. The
\Gamma
-invariance of the density and the bundle metric ensure that the left action representation
(
4.1
)
is unitary with respect to
(
4.2
)
. Other function spaces of interest next to
L^{2}(M,E)
are Sobolev spaces. We recall from
[
19
, Sec.3.9]
that the appropriate norm is defined with a
\Gamma
-invariant partition of unity
(
3.4
)
:

(4.3)

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,E)}^{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
\gamma\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}\left\|\phi_{j,\gamma}u\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathrm{supp}\left(\phi_{j,\gamma}\right),E)}^{2}\quad.

The corresponding
\Gamma
-Sobolev spaces
are then defined for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
via

(4.4)

H^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,E):=\left\{u\in H^{s}_{\mathsf{loc}}(M,E)\,\Big|\,\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,E)}<\infty\right\}\quad.

We note from
[
19
]
that the definition does not depend on the choice of the
\Gamma
-invariant partition of unity and the choice of the discrete group; they are a priori projective Hilbert
\Gamma
-modules. We also want to point out the resemblance of the
\Gamma
-Sobolev spaces with the Sobolev spaces from
(
A.2
)
on manifolds with bounded geometry.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the fundamental domain of the
\Gamma
-action. Because

is a principal
\Gamma
-bundle, the action of the group on the
\Gamma
-manifold becomes isomorphically related to an action on
\Gamma\times\mathcal{F}
. This induces a unitary isomorphism between

-sections,

(4.5)

L^{2}(M,E)\cong\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{F},E|_{\mathcal{F}})\quad,

which is given by

(4.6)

u\,\mapsto\,\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\delta_{\gamma}\otimes(L^{E}_{\gamma})^{\ast}u|_{\mathcal{F}}\quad.

for a
u\in L^{2}(M,E)
. The left action representation
L^{E}_{\gamma}
becomes
l_{\gamma}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{F},E|_{\mathcal{F}})
. Furthermore,
(
4.5
)
implies that
L^{2}(M,E)
is a free Hilbert
\Gamma
-module and hence any closed
\Gamma
-invariant subset of
L^{2}(M,E)
becomes a projective Hilbert
\Gamma
-module. We recall
Remark

3.1
and replace
L^{2}(\mathcal{F},E|_{\mathcal{F}})
with
L^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},E|_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})=L^{2}(M_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma})
with
E_{\Gamma}
as vector bundle over
M_{\Gamma}
since
\mathcal{F}
and
M_{\Gamma}
differ in a set of measure zero and, due to the
\Gamma
-invariance of the density on
M
, the densities on
M
and
\mathcal{F}
coincide such that
(
4.5
)
becomes

(4.7)

L^{2}(M,E)\cong\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(M_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma})\quad.

The von Neumann algebra of operators in
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}(L^{2}(M,E))
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathscr{N}_{r}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(M_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma}))
.

Given two
\Gamma
-vector bundles
E^{\prime}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
and
E\rightarrow M
over
\Gamma
-manifolds
M,M^{\prime}
and an operator

with Schwartz-Kernel

.
A\in\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}(L^{2}(M^{\prime},F),L^{2}(M,E))
implies that the Schwartz kernel is
\Gamma
-equivariant and becomes a distribution on the compact orbit
(M\times M^{\prime})/\Gamma
under the diagonal action of
\Gamma
. If we take an operator
A\in\mathscr{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}(L^{2}(M,E))
, the Schwartz-Kernel satisfies
K_{A}\in L^{2}(M\times M,E\otimes E^{\ast})
. Suppose for simplicity that there are two operators
C\in\mathscr{S}^{2}_{\Gamma}(L^{2}(M,E))
and
B\in\mathscr{S}^{2}_{\Gamma}(L^{2}(M,E))
with Schwartz kernels
K_{C}
and
K_{B}
such that
A=B\circ C
. The analytic expression of the
\Gamma
-trace for
A
in terms of these Schwartz kernels becomes

(4.8)

\mathrm{Tr}_{\Gamma}\left(A\right)=\int_{\mathcal{F}\times M}\mathrm{tr}_{E_{p}}\left(K_{B}(p,q)\circ K_{C}(q,p)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{M\times M}(p,q)

with
\mathrm{tr}_{E_{p}}\left(\cdot\right)
as fibrewise
\Gamma
-invariant trace along
E_{p},p\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{M\times M}

\Gamma
-invariant density on
M\times M
. If the Schwartz kernel of
A
is already continuous at the diagonal, the Schwartz kernels of
B
and
C
do as well and vice versa such that the inner integral can be performed, giving
K_{A}
and consequently

(4.9)

\mathrm{Tr}_{\Gamma}\left(A\right)=\int_{\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{tr}_{E_{p}}\left(K_{A}(p,p)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{M}(p)\quad.

We want to consider differential operators, pseudo-differential and Fourier integral operators in the
\Gamma
-setting. We denote with
\mathsf{Diff}^{m}_{\Gamma}(M,\mathsf{Hom}(E,F))
,
\mathsf{\Psi}^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,\mathsf{Hom}(E,F))
, and
\mathpzc{FIO}^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,N;\mathsf{C}^{\prime};\mathsf{Hom}(E,G))
the sets of
\Gamma
-differential,
\Gamma
-pseudo-differential and
\Gamma
-Fourier integral operators where

are
\Gamma
-manifolds,

and

are
\Gamma
-vector bundles,

a canonical relation, and

,

. These are in general unbounded operators. If an operator in one of these operator spaces enjoys the property of being properly supported, its Schwartz kernel is compactly supported with support in
(M\times M)/\Gamma
respectively

. Thus, any
\Gamma
-invariant differential operator has compactly supported Schwartz kernel on the orbit space.
A certain subclass of
\Gamma
-invariant pseudo-differential operators are

\Gamma
-invariant operator with classical symbol and a smoothing
\Gamma
-invariant remainder.
We collect some known results.

Proposition 4.1

(Corollary 3.9.2, Theorem 3.9.1/4(a) in
[

]
)
.

(1)

If

(2)

If

.

The requirement of being properly supported is always fulfilled for differential operators, but too restrictive for a general operator class. We first introduce a wider set of
\Gamma
-operators such that they are smoothing in the sense that they map
\Gamma
-Sobolev spaces into
\Gamma
-Sobolev spaces.

Definition 4.2

Given

and

-vector bundles over
\Gamma
-manifolds

with respect to the same

4.2.
Projectors in the
\Gamma
-setting

Given an elliptic and self-adjoint operator
A\in\mathsf{\Psi}^{m}_{\Gamma,\mathsf{prop}}(M,E)
of order
m>0
which acts between smooth sections of the (Hermitian)
\Gamma
-vector bundle
E
over the (Riemannian) manifold
M
. The characteristic functions of such an operator can be defined by means of any unbounded functional calculus for self-adjoint operators, but this approach lacks of analytic informations. To bypass this, we rewrite the characteristic functions
x\mapsto\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(0,\infty)}(x)
and
x\mapsto\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)
with the signum-function which in turn can be expressed as quotient
x/\left|x\right|
:

(4.10)

\begin{split}\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{>0}(x)&:=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(0,\infty)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}-\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{\left\{0\right\}}(x)\right)\\
\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{<0}(x)&:=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(-\infty,0)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}-\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{\left\{0\right\}}(x)\right)\quad.\end{split}

The task is to show that the expressions
A\circ\left|A\right|^{-1}
and
\left|A\right|^{-1}=(A^{\ast}\circ A)^{-1/2}=(A^{2})^{-1/2}
in particular are meaningful. If
M
is closed, Seeley’s theorem for complex powers states that under further conditions on
A
any of its complex powers become meaningful as pseudo-differential operator and their principal symbols can be calculated from the one of
A
. Such a result is known for manifolds of bounded geometry; in
Appendix

A
we present and explain this version of Seeley’s theorem as
Proposition

A.3
. We transfer the result to
\Gamma
-manifolds.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
B\in\mathsf{\Psi}^{m}_{\Gamma,\mathsf{prop,cl}}(M,E)
be an elliptic and positive operator with scalar positive definite principal symbol
{\sigma}_{m}(B)(p,\xi)
such that
({\sigma}_{m}(B)(p,\xi)-\lambda)
is not vanishing for
\lambda
in a sector
\Lambda_{\theta}\subset\uprho(B)
for
\theta\in(0,\uppi/2)
and
\xi\in\dot{T}_{p}M
. Then the following holds:

(1)

there exists a value
r>0
such that for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\theta,r}\subset\mathbb{C}
the operator
B
becomes sectorial and the resolvent satisfies
\mathsf{R}(B,\lambda)\in S\mathsf{\Psi}^{-m}_{\Gamma}(M,E)
with principal symbol
({\sigma}_{m}(B)(p,\xi)-\lambda)^{-1}
; moreover
\mathsf{R}(B,\lambda)
becomes a bounded operator from
H^{s}_{\Gamma}(M,E)
to
H^{s+m}_{\Gamma}(M,E)
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

B
generates a holomorphic semigroup
\mathrm{e}^{-zB}
for
\Re\mathfrak{e}\left\{z\right\}>0
in
L^{2}(M,E)
.

(3)

B^{z}\in S\mathsf{\Psi}^{m\Re\mathfrak{e}\left\{z\right\}}_{\Gamma}(M,E)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with principal symbol
(
A.5
)
.

The third point in this result is thereby an analogue of Seeley’s theorem for complex powers in the setting of Galois coverings. It is only left to show that the resolvent commutes with the left action representation if
B
does: from the commuting
(\lambda-B)L^{E}_{\gamma}=L^{E}_{\gamma}(\lambda-B)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we can conclude for
\lambda\in\uprho(B)
the commuting
of the resolvent with the left action representation:

(\lambda-B)^{-1}L^{E}_{\gamma}=(\lambda-B)^{-1}L^{E}_{\gamma}(\lambda-B)(\lambda-B)^{-1}=L^{E}_{\gamma}(\lambda-B)^{-1}\quad.

Thus,
f(B)
in
(
A.3
)
commutes with
L^{E}_{\Gamma}
for all
\gamma
and allowable functions
f
.

We now come back to the (essentially) self-adjoint and elliptic operator
A\in\mathsf{\Psi}^{m}_{\Gamma,\mathsf{prop,cl}}(M,E)
,
m>0
; the operator
B:=A^{\ast}\circ A=A^{2}\in\mathsf{\Psi}^{2m}_{\Gamma,\mathsf{prop,cl}}(M,E)
is again self-adjoint and its spectrum lies on the positive half-line. Hence
B
becomes positive and altogether sectorial. The ellipticity of
B
is also inherited from the ellipticity of
A
and the principal symbol of
B
is positive definite. Under this assumptions, the expression
\left|A\right|^{-1}
becomes well-defined as s-regular pseudo-differential operator of order
(-m)
and finally one can consider projectors as s-regular
\Gamma
-pseudo-differential operators.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
A\in\mathsf{\Psi}^{m}_{\Gamma,\mathsf{prop,cl}}(M,E)
be elliptic and (essentially) self-adjoint with positive order
m>0
; the spectral projections onto eigenspaces of (the closure of)
A
in the spectral range
(0,\infty)

(4.11)

P_{>0}(A):=P_{+}(A):=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(0,\infty)}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbbm{1}+A\circ|A|^{-1}-P_{0}(A)\right)

and in the spectral range

(4.12)

(4.13)

{\sigma}_{0}(P_{\pm})(p,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm{\sigma}_{m}(A)(p,\xi){\sigma}_{-m}\left(\left|A\right|^{-1}\right)(p,\xi)\right)\\

for all

and

.

Remark 4.7
.

(i)

One has
P_{\pm}=p_{\pm}+r_{\pm}

and

(ii)

P_{\geq 0}(A):=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{[0,\infty)}(A)
and

differ from

(

)

(iii)

Since

If we cut the spectral range at another point

than zero, we will also write

\displaystyle P_{>a}(A)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle P_{(a,\infty)}(A)=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(a,\infty)}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{<a}(A):=P_{(-\infty,a)}(A)=\scalebox{1.25}{$\chi$}_{(-\infty,a)}(A)

\displaystyle P_{\geq a}(A)

7.1.

Manifolds of bounded geometry

We give a short description about manifolds of bounded geometry, based on material from
[
20
]
and
[
12
]
. Suppose
\Sigma
is a connected Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. The Riemannian metric
\mathpzc{g}
induces a distance function
d:\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by taking the infimum of lengths of arcs, connecting two points and measured with respect to the Riemannian metric. The exponential geodesic map
\exp_{p}:T_{p}\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
is defined through
\exp_{p}(v)=\gamma(1)
where
\gamma
is a geodesic, given in such a parametrisation, that
\gamma(0)=p
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=v
. If we take a ball
\mathring{\mathbb{B}}^{n}_{r}(0)
of radius
r>0
around the zero vector in
T_{p}\Sigma
, the exponential map becomes a diffeomorphism onto its image
U_{r}(p)=\exp(\mathring{\mathbb{B}}^{n}_{r}(0))
which is an open neighbourhood of
p\in\Sigma
. Let

r(p):=\sup\left\{r>0\,\Big|\,\exp_{p}:\mathring{\mathbb{B}}^{n}_{r}(0)\rightarrow U_{r}(p)\,\,\text{diffeomorphism}\right\}

The infimum over all points
p\in\Sigma
then defines the
injectivity radius

r_{\mathsf{inj}}
.
For
r_{\mathsf{inj}}>0
and
r\in(0,r_{\mathsf{inj}})
the exponential map
\exp_{p}:\mathring{\mathbb{B}}^{n}_{r}(0)\rightarrow U_{r}(p)
is a diffeomorphism for all
p\in\Sigma
which motivates the following definition.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a Riemannian manifold; we say that
\Sigma
is a manifold of/with bounded geometry if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(a)

r_{\mathsf{inj}}>0
;

(b)

the transition maps from
U_{r}(p)\cap U_{r}(q)
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are bounded for any fixed
r\in(0,r_{\mathsf{inj}})
and any points
p,q
with open neighbourhoods
U_{r}(p),U_{r}(q)
such that
U_{r}(p)\cap U_{r}(q)\neq\emptyset
.

(a) implies that
\Sigma
is complete and (b) is equivalent to the boundedness of all covariant derivatives of the Riemannian curvature tensor. Examples of such spaces are homogenous spaces with invariant metrics, covering manifolds of compact manifolds
4
4

4

The case of such coverings is proven in
[
7
]
.

. Examples of such vector bundles are
\Sigma\times\mathbb{C}
and complexifications of
T\Sigma
,
T^{\ast}\Sigma
as well as any other complexified natural bundle.

For a manifold of bounded geometry

there exists a radius

\mathring{\mathbb{B}}_{r}(p_{i})

Lemma A.2

For every

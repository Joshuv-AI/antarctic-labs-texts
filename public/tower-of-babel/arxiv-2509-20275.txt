Reduced coaction Lie algebra, double shuffle Lie algebra and noncommutative krv2 equation
Megan Howarth, Muze Ren

Abstract
We study the reduced coaction Lie algebra 𝔯 ​ 𝔠 0 \mathfrak{rc}_{0} , which is defined by an algebraic equation satisfied by the reduced coaction (an upgraded version of the necklace cobracket) and the skew-symmetric condition. We prove that the double shuffle Lie algebra 𝔡 ​ 𝔪 ​ 𝔯 0 \mathfrak{dmr}_{0} together with the skew-symmetric condition injects to 𝔯 ​ 𝔠 0 \mathfrak{rc}_{0} , and that 𝔯 ​ 𝔠 0 \mathfrak{rc}_{0} together with the krv1 equation injects to the Kashiwara-Vergne Lie algebra 𝔨 ​ 𝔯 ​ 𝔳 2 \mathfrak{krv}_{2} .



1
Introduction and main results

1.1
Background and motivation

In the study of intersections of curves on surfaces, the Goldman–Turaev Lie bialgebra
[
22
]
and its noncommutative analogue, the necklace Lie bialgebra
[
19
]
, play a fundamental role. The topological reduced coaction was introduced as a refinement of the Turaev cobracket, and the algebraic reduced coaction was introduced as a refinement of the necklace cobracket in this context; see, for example, the “coaction
\mu
” in
[
14
]
and the notion of “quasi-derivation” in
[
16
]
. In particular, Alekseev–Kawazumi–Kuno–Naef
[
2
]
discovered a connection between the Turaev cobracket and divergence. The (algebraic) reduced coaction can thus be viewed as a noncommutative analogue of divergence, which itself plays a central role in the formulation of the Kashiwara–Vergne problem
[
4
,
1
]
.

On the other hand, the theory of the double shuffle Lie algebra
[
17
]
is closely connected with the Kashiwara–Vergne problem, as shown by Schneps
[
20
,
21
]
; see also the work of Carr-Schneps
[
6
]
, which builds on Ecalle’s mould theory
[
7
]
. More recently, Enriquez and Furusho have proposed a new geometric framework for studying the Betti side of double shuffle theory,
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
, complementing ideas of Deligne–Terasoma and using this new framework to study the relation with Kashiwara-Vergne Lie algebra
[
11
]
.

Our motivation is to undertake a detailed study of the reduced coaction Lie algebra introduced in
[
18
]
, and to explore potential connections between the geometric framework of Alekseev–Kawazumi–Kuno–Naef and that of Enriquez–Furusho. The main tools we employ are polylogarithmic computations and noncommutative geometry à la
Kontsevich–Rosenberg
.

1.2
Main results

We fix
k
to be a field of characteristic zero and consider the Hopf algebra of formal noncommutative power series

A=\big(k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle,\conc,\Delta_{\shuffle},\varepsilon,S\big),

(1)

where
\conc
is the concatenation product and

\begin{split}&\varepsilon(x_{0})=\varepsilon(x_{1})=0,\\
&S(x_{0})=-x_{1},\hskip 8.5359ptS(x_{1})=-x_{0},\\
&\Delta_{\shuffle}(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes x_{i},\text{ for }i=0,1.\end{split}

Every
\varphi\in A=k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle

is uniquely expressed as

\varphi=\varepsilon(\varphi)+(\varphi)_{x_{0}}x_{0}+(\varphi)_{x_{1}}x_{1}=\varepsilon(\varphi)+x_{0}{}_{x_{0}}(\varphi)+x_{1}{}_{x_{1}}(\varphi),

(2)

where
(\varphi)_{x_{i}}
and
{}_{x_{i}}(\varphi)
denote the parts of
\varphi
that end and start in
x_{i}
, respectively, for
i\in\{0,1\}
.
We also use
c_{w}(\varphi)
to denote the coefficient of the word
w
in
\varphi
and
\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
to denote the free Lie algebra in
k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
.

Definition 1.1

(The reduced coaction map)
.

The (algebraic)
reduced coaction

\mu
is a linear map from
k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
to
k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
which is defined to be

\begin{split}&\mu(x_{0})=\mu(x_{1})=0\\
&\mu(k_{1}\,k_{2}\dots\,k_{n}):=\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}k_{1}\dots k_{i-1}(k_{i}\odot k_{i+1})k_{i+2}\dots k_{n},\end{split}

where
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
and
(x_{i}\odot x_{j}):=\delta_{x_{i},x_{j}}x_{i}
, for
x_{i},x_{j}\in\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
.

Definition 1.2

(The reduced coaction equation)
.

Let
\eta\in k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
be a Lie series
with
c_{x_{0}}(\eta)=c_{x_{1}}(\eta)=0
. The function
r_{\eta}
associated to
\eta
is defined to be

r_{\eta}(x)=\sum_{l\geq 0}c_{x^{l+1}_{0}x_{1}}(\eta)x^{l+1}

and the
reduced coaction equation
is

\mu(\eta)=-r_{\eta}(x_{1})+r_{\eta}(-x_{0})-(\eta)_{x_{0}}-{}_{x_{1}}(\eta).

(3)

We denote by
\mathfrak{rc}
the vector space of skew-symmetric solutions of this equation, i.e.

\eta(x_{0},x_{1})=-\eta(x_{1},x_{0}).

(4)

Lemma 1.3
.

The vector space
\mathfrak{rc}
contains the one-dimensional vector space spanned by the element
[x_{0},x_{1}]
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{rc}_{\lambda}
the subspace of
\mathfrak{rc}
consisting of elements whose coefficient before the commutator
[x_{0},x_{1}]
is
\lambda
.

The
double shuffle Lie algebra

\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
introduced by Racinet in
[
17
]
is defined to be the set of formal Lie series
\psi\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
satisfying

c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\Delta_{*}(\psi_{*})=1\otimes\psi_{*}+\psi_{*}\otimes 1,

(5)

where
\psi_{*}=\psi_{\text{corr}}+\pi_{Y}(\psi)
, with
\psi_{\text{corr}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}c_{x^{n-1}_{0}x_{1}}(\psi)y^{n}_{1}
and

\pi_{Y}
the
k
-linear map defined by

\begin{split}k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle&\to k\langle\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{n},\dots\rangle\rangle\\
\text{words ending in }x_{0}&\mapsto 0\\
x^{n_{m}-1}_{0}x_{1}\dots x^{n_{1}-1}_{0}x_{1}&\mapsto(-1)^{m}y_{n_{m}}\dots y_{n_{1}}.\end{split}

The coproduct
\Delta_{*}
on
k\langle\langle y_{1},y_{2},\dots\rangle\rangle
is defined to be
\Delta_{*}y_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}\otimes y_{n-i}
, with
y_{0}=1
.

Our first main result is the following explicit relation between
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
. We denote two variable polylogarithms by
l^{y,x}_{\bf{a},\bf{b}}
; see Section
2
for details.

Theorem A
.

Let
\psi\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
be skew-symmetric and satisfying
c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0
, then the following two conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\psi\in\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
.

(ii)

\psi\in\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
and for any
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{l})\neq(1,\dots,1),(1,\dots,1)
,

l^{y,x}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}),(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})}(\psi_{451}+\psi_{123})=l^{y,x}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{1}),(b_{2},\dots,b_{l})}(\psi_{451}+\psi_{123}).

The proof of this theorem relies on the following polylogarithmic descriptions of
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, which may also be of independent interest. Let
\alpha
denote the defect of the following form of the pentagon equation, for
\psi(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
,

\alpha\left(\psi(x_{0},x_{1})\right):=\psi_{451}+\psi_{123}-\psi_{432}-\psi_{215}-\psi_{543},

where
\psi_{ijk}:=\psi(x_{ij},x_{jk})
are in the spherical braid Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{5}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\psi\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
be such that
c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0
, then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\psi\in\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
;

(ii)

l^{y,x}_{\bf{a},\bf{b}}(\alpha)=0,
for
{\bf{a},\bf{b}}\neq(1,\dots,1),(1,\dots,1)
.

This theorem provides another proof that the Grothendieck-Teichmuller Lie algebra
\mathfrak{grt}_{1}
injects to
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
, originally proved by Furusho in
[
12
]
.

Theorem C
.

If
\psi\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
is skew-symmetric and
c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0
, then the following 4 descriptions of the reduced coaction Lie algebra
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
are equivalent:

(i)

\psi\in\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
;

(ii)

l^{y,x}_{{\bf{a}},(b_{1})}(\alpha)=0,\quad\forall{\bf{a}},(b_{1})
;

(iii)

l_{{\bf{a}},(b_{1})}^{x,y}(\alpha)=0,\quad\forall{\bf{a}},(b_{1})
;

(iv)

\begin{aligned} \mu(\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1}))=&(d^{R}_{1}\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1}))(x_{0}+x_{1},0)\\
-&d^{R}_{1}(\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1}))-d^{R}_{1}(\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1}))(x_{1},0).\end{aligned}

We recall that one of the fundamental results about
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
proved by Racinet is that it is a Lie algebra with the Ihara bracket.

Theorem 1.4

(Racinet
[
17
]
)
.

The set
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
has a structure of Lie algebra with the Lie bracket given by

\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}=d_{\psi_{2}}(\psi_{1})-d_{\psi_{1}}(\psi_{2})-[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}],

(6)

where
d_{\psi}
is the derivation of
\psi\in k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
and is given by
d_{\psi}(x_{0})=0
and
d_{\psi}(x_{1})=[x_{1},\psi]
.

As an application of the above polylogarithmic descriptions, we prove similar results of
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
. First, recall that
the meta-abelian quotient of
\psi
is defined by

B_{\varphi}(x_{0},x_{1}):=\left(\varphi_{x_{1}}x_{1}\right)^{\text{ab}},

where
h\mapsto h^{\text{ab}}
is the abelianization map from
k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
to
k[[x_{0},x_{1}]]
. We also introduce the following vector space

\mathfrak{B}:=\{\beta\in k[[x_{0},x_{1}]]\mid\beta(x_{0},x_{1})=\gamma(x_{0})+\gamma(x_{1})-\gamma(x_{0}+x_{1}),\gamma(s)\in s^{2}k[[s]]\}.

Theorem D
.

If
\psi\in\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, then

c_{x_{0}^{n+1}x_{1}}(\psi)=0,\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0\quad\text{even};

B_{\psi}(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathfrak{B}.

Moreover
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
is a Lie algebra with the Ihara bracket (
6
); in other words, for any
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, we have

\mu\circ\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}=-\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}_{x_{0}}-{}_{x_{1}}\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}.\\

The Kashiwara-Vergne Lie algebra
\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
is an important Lie algebra introduced by Alekseev-Torossian
[
4
]
; for more details, see Subsection
6.1
. As an application of the study of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, we prove the following theorem by showing that the skew-symmetric
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
maps to
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, and that
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
together with
krv_{1}
maps to
\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
. The proof of this theorem relies on the notion of the noncommutative krv2 equation and its relation to
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
.

Theorem E
.

If
\psi\in\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
is skew-symmetric, with
c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=0,c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0
, and satisfies the following krv1 equation

[x_{1},\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1})]+[x_{0},\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{0})]=0,

(7)

then
\psi\in\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
and this map factors through
\mathfrak{rc}_{0}
, namely

\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}+\rm{skew}\eqref{eq:skew}+\rm{krv1}\eqref{eq:krv1}\to\mathfrak{rc}_{0}+\rm{krv1}\eqref{eq:krv1}\to\mathfrak{krv}_{2}.

We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5
.

If
\psi\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
is skew-symmetric and such that
c_{x_{0}}(\psi)=c_{x_{1}}(\psi)=0
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\psi
satisfies krv1, i.e.
[x_{1},\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1})]+[x_{0},\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{0})]=0
;

(ii)

l^{y,x}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}),(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})}(\psi_{451}+\psi_{123})=l^{y,x}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{m},b_{1}),(b_{2},\dots,b_{n})}(\psi_{451}+\psi_{123})
, for any
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}),(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\neq(1,\dots,1),(1,\dots,1)
.

Assuming this conjecture holds, it would follow that
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
with skew symmetry is the same as
\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
with the skew-symmetric condition.

Remark 1.6
.

Schneps
[
21
]
and Enriquez-Furusho
[
11
]
independently proved that
\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}
implies
\rm{krv1}
. Assuming their results, the diagram in Theorem
E
simplifies to

\mathfrak{dmr}_{0}+\rm{skew}\eqref{eq:skew}\to\mathfrak{rc}_{0}+\rm{krv1}\eqref{eq:krv1}\to\mathfrak{krv}_{2}.

Similar constructions about the reduced coaction equations together with
krv_{1}
mapping to
\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
also appear in Kuno’s work
[
15
]
, through the study of the emergent braids. We hope to investigate this relationship more closely in future work. The emergent braids formalism developed by Bar-Natan and Kuno also provides a key link to the Enriquez-Furusho geometrical framework.

Notations and preliminaries on polylogarithms

We begin by reviewing the relevant background on polylogarithms. Let
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{>0}
, then its
weight
and its
depth
are respectively defined to be
\text{wt}(\textbf{a})=a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}
and
\text{dp}(\textbf{a})=k
. The vector
\bf{a}
is said to be
admissible
if its last entry is greater than
1
, i.e.
a_{k}>1
.

In this note, we restrict our attention to multiple polylogarithm functions in
one
and
two
variables, following the exposition of
[
12
]
. Let
r
denote the number of variables. More precisely, when
r=1
or
2
, the multiple polylogarithm functions take the form:

\begin{split}\Li_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})}(z)&:=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{k}}\frac{z^{m_{k}}}{m^{a_{1}}_{1}\dots m^{a_{k}}_{k}};\\
\Li_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}),(b_{1},\ldots,b_{l})}(x,y)&:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{k}\\
<n_{1}<\dots<n_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{m_{k}}y^{n_{l}}}{m^{a_{1}}_{1}\dots m^{a_{k}}_{k}n^{b_{1}}_{1}\dots n^{b_{l}}_{l}}.\end{split}

(8)

These functions satisfy the following differential equations, for indices
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
and
\textbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{l})
:

\frac{d}{dz}\text{Li}_{\bf{a}}(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{z}\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},a_{k}-1)}(z),&\text{if $a_{k}\neq 1$;}\\
\frac{1}{1-z}\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1})}(z),&\text{if $a_{k}=1,k\neq 1$;}\\
\frac{1}{1-z},&\text{if }a_{k}=1,k=1.\end{cases}

(9)

and

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dx}\text{Li}_{\bf{a},\bf{b}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{x}\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},a_{k}-1),{\bf{b}}}(x,y),&~\text{if}~a_{k}\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{1-x}\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1})}(x,y)-(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x})\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},b_{1}),(b_{2},\dots,b_{l})}(x,y)&~\text{if}~a_{k}=1,k\neq 1,l\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{1-x}\text{Li}_{{\bf{b}}}(x,y)-(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x})\text{Li}_{(b_{1}),(b_{2},\dots,b_{l})}(x,y)&~\text{if}~a_{k}=1,k=1,l\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{1-x}\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1}),{\bf{b}}}(x,y)-(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x})\text{Li}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1},b_{1})}(xy)&~\text{if}~a_{k}=1,k\neq 1,l=1\\
\frac{1}{1-x}\text{Li}_{\bf{b}}(y)-(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{1-x})\text{Li}_{\bf{b}}(xy),&~\text{if}~a_{k}=1,k=1,l=1\end{cases}\\
&\frac{d}{dy}\text{Li}_{\bf{a},\bf{b}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{y}\text{Li}_{{\bf{a}},(b_{1},\dots,b_{l-1},b_{l}-1)}(x,y)&~\text{if}~b_{l}\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{1-y}\text{Li}_{\bf{a},(b_{1},\dots,b_{l-1})}(x,y)&~\text{if}~b_{l}=1,l\neq 1\\
\frac{1}{1-y}\text{Li}_{\bf{a}}(xy)&~\text{if}~b_{l}=1,l=1.\end{cases}\end{split}

(10)

One important property of these functions is the series shuffle relation

6

Li_{\textbf{a}}(x)Li_{\textbf{b}}(y)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sh^{\leq(k,l)}}Li_{\sigma(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})}(\sigma(x,y)),

\sigma:\{1,\ldots,k+l\}\to\{1,\ldots,N\}&\sigma(1)<\ldots<\sigma(k);\\

and

\begin{split}\sigma(x,y)&:=\begin{cases}xy,\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}(N)=k,k+l;\\
(x,y),\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}(N)=k+l;\\
(y,x),\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}(N)=k,\end{cases}\\
\sigma(\textbf{a},\textbf{b})&:=((c_{1},\ldots,c_{j}),(c_{j+1},\ldots,c_{N})),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\{j,N\}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma(k),\sigma(k+l)\},

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{s}+b_{t-k},\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}=\{s,t\}\text{ with }s<t;\\
a_{s},\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}=\{s\}\text{ with }s\leq k;\\
b_{s-k},\hfill&\text{ if }\sigma^{-1}=\{s\}\text{ with }s>k.\\
\end{cases}

4.1
Fox pairing and two cocycle

Recall that
A
denotes the Hopf algebra of formal non-commutative power series (
1
).
In what follows, we denote by
M
the space
M\cong A
endowed with the
A\otimes A
-bimodule structure given by

(f\otimes g)a(h\otimes k)=\varepsilon(f)\varepsilon(k)\,gah.

(24)

This bimodule structure plays a key role in this section.

The notions of
Fox derivative
and
Fox pairing
are introduced by Massyeau–Turaev
[
16
]
as some noncommutative version of the loop operations on a surface. We recall the tools developed in
[
3
]
to study the group version of the reduced coaction equation.

Definition 4.2

(Fox derivative)
.

A
k
-linear map
\partial:A\to A
is called a
left Fox derivative
if

\partial(ab)=a\partial(b)+\partial(a)\varepsilon(b),

for all
a,b\in A
. It is a
right Fox derivative
if

\partial(ab)=\partial(a)b+\varepsilon(a)\partial(b),

for all
a,b\in A
.

Example 4.3

(
[
3
]
Example
3.2
)
.

For any
x\in A
, there are unique presentations

\begin{split}x&=\varepsilon(x)+x_{0}d^{R}_{0}(x)+x_{1}d^{R}_{1}(x)\\
&=\varepsilon(x)+d^{L}_{0}(x)x_{0}+d^{L}_{1}(x)x_{1},\end{split}

which define the right Fox derivatives
d^{R}_{i}
and the left Fox derivatives
d^{L}_{i}
. They coincide with the notations (
2
) we introduced in the Introduction, namely
d^{R}_{i}(x)={}_{x_{i}}(x)
and
d^{L}_{i}(x)=(x)_{x_{i}}
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
.

Definition 4.4

(Fox pairing)
.

A bilinear map
\rho:A\otimes A\to A
is a
Fox pairing
if it is a left Fox derivative in its first argument and a right Fox derivative in its second argument. That is,

\begin{split}\rho(ab,c)&=a\rho(b,c)+\rho(a,c)\varepsilon(b),\\
\rho(a,bc)&=\rho(a,b)c+\varepsilon(b)\rho(a,c),\end{split}

(25)

for all
a,b,c\in A.

Example 4.5

(
[
3
]
Example
3.4
)
.

On the free algebra
A=k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
, we define

\rho_{\rm{KKS}}(x_{i},x_{j}):=\delta_{ij}x_{i}.

It uniquely extends to a Fox pairing on
A
by
\rho_{\rm{KKS}}(x,1)=\rho_{\rm{KKS}}(1,x)=0
, for all
x
in
A
and the product rule (
25
).

Remark 4.6
.

This Fox pairing induces the Kostant-Kirillov-Souriau Poisson bracket on the representation space.

6.1
Kashiwara-Vergne Lie algebra

We shall denote by
\rm{Der}(A)
the
Lie algebra of derivations
of
A=k\langle\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle\rangle
; an element
u\in\rm{Der}(A)
is completely determined by its values on the generators. A derivation
u\in\rm{Der}(A)
is called a
tangential derivation
if there exist
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
such that

u(x_{0})=[x_{0},a_{1}],\hskip 8.5359ptu(x_{1})=[x_{1},a_{2}].

We denote the Lie algebra of tangential derivations by
(\rm{tDer}(A),[-,-])
, where
[-,-]
is the commutator Lie bracket.

Let
x_{\infty}:=-x_{0}-x_{1}
, a tangential derivation is called a
special derivation
if
u(x_{\infty})=0.
We denote the Lie subalgebra of special derivations by
(\rm{sDer}(A),[-,-])
.

Remark 6.1
.

If we further assume that
a_{1}
has no linear terms
k_{1}x_{0}
and
a_{2}
has no linear terms
k_{2}x_{1}
, where
k_{1},k_{2}
are coefficients in
k
, then
u
is uniquely determined by
a_{1},a_{2}
; we denote it by
u=(a_{1},a_{2})
.

In a similar way, we can consider the Lie algebra of derivations of the free Lie algebra
\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
. An element
u\in\mathfrak{tder}_{2}
if there exist
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{fr}_{k}(x_{0},x_{1})
such that
u(x_{0})=[x_{0},a_{1}]
and
u(x_{1})=[x_{1},a_{2}]
, and
u\in\mathfrak{sder}_{2}
if
u(x_{\infty})=0
. The Lie algebras
\mathfrak{tder}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{sder}_{2}
are Lie subalgebras of
\rm{tDer}(A)
and
\rm{sDer}(A)
, respectively.

Let
|A|:=A/[A,A]
and consider the projection map
|\cdot|:A\to A/[A,A]
. The
divergence
map
\rm{div}:\rm{tDer}(A)\to|A|
is defined to be

u=(a_{0},a_{1})\mapsto|x_{0}d^{R}_{0}(a_{0})+x_{1}d^{R}_{1}(a_{1})|.

(41)

The
Kashiwara-Vergne
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{krv}_{2}
is defined as follows.

Definition 6.2
.

consists of the tangential derivations
u\in\rm{tDer}(A)
which satisfy the following two equations:

•

The
potential function
associated to a Lie series

is defined by

h_{\psi}:=x_{0}\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{0})+x_{1}\psi(-x_{0}-x_{1},x_{1}).

It is said to satisfy the
noncommutative krv2 equation
if

\mu(h_{\psi})=f(x_{0}+x_{1})-f(x_{0})-f(x_{1}),

(42)

for some
f\in k[[x]]
. Let

|A|\to A

Notice that

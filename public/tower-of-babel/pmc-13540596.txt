Postoperative Contrast Sensitivity and Its Association with Lenticule Surface Regularity After KLEx with the VisuMax 800
Authors: Jiayi Hu, Yongle Bao, Zhanying Wang, Yiyang Wang, Junjie Yu, Haipeng Xu, Yishan Qian, Xiaoying Wang, Xingtao Zhou, Jing Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo investigate changes in contrast sensitivity (CS) and their association with Lenticule surface regularity after keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx) performed with the VisuMax 800.Patients and MethodsIn total, 92 patients (184 eyes) with myopia were recruited between November and December 2024. CS was measured using the quantitative contrast sensitivity function at 1, 3 and 6 months postoperatively. The lenticules were examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and the SEM images were scored using the large language model Doubao-vision-pro 32k. Correlation analysis between Lenticule surface regularity and CS was performed based on the Generalized Estimating Equations model.ResultsAt 6 months postoperatively, the uncorrected distance visual acuity of all operated eyes was 20/25 or better; none of the eyes lost 1–2 lines of corrected distance visual acuity. Area under the log CS function, cutoff spatial frequency acuity and CS (3.0–18.0 cpd) significantly improved from 1 to 6 months and correlated with visual acuity and refraction (P < 0.05). Lenticule surface regularity, mainly influenced by age (β=0.204, P < 0.001) and cap thickness (β=-0.325, P < 0.001), was significantly associated with CS (3.0 cpd) at 1 and 3 months postoperatively (β=0.027, P=0.005; β=0.028, P=0.006), and with CS (6.0 cpd) at 6 months postoperatively (β=0.035, P=0.001).ConclusionKLEx performed with the VisuMax 800 yields excellent and stable visual outcomes at six months postoperatively and postoperative CS improved over time. Lenticule surface regularity could contribute to postoperative visual quality to a certain extent.

Introduction

Refractive surgery is an effective method to enhance visual ability by reducing dependence on glasses. Keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx) is a safe and effective refractive surgery for treating refractive errors.1 The latest laser version, the Visumax 800, has been applied since 2021.2 Compared with the Visumax 500, Visumax 800 offers the advantages of shorter laser scanning cycles and an eye-tracking system device.3 Moreover, reduced scanning time may improve patient comfort and reduce the overall risk of suction loss.3 The visual quality after KLEx have attracted attention.

Visual acuity is the most common postoperative examinations. Compared to visual acuity, the contrast sensitivity (CS) function assesses visual function across varying spatial frequencies and offers more refined and responsive results for evaluating visual quality. For postoperative refractive surgery, contrast sensitivity evaluation is indispensable for comprehensive visual function profiling.4 The quantitative contrast sensitivity function (qCSF) is a validated method for assessing visual quality. Unlike conventional CS testing tools such as the CSV-1000, which are limited by fixed spatial frequency intervals, lengthy testing procedures, and insufficient precision in capturing subtle visual functional changes, the qCSF adopts Bayesian information maximization algorithms to efficiently quantify contrast sensitivity at low, medium, and high spatial frequencies with superior accuracy.5 qCSF has been widely used in ophthalmology to distinguish patients with amblyopia from those with myopia,6 conduct screenings to identify age-related macular degeneration,7 and monitor visual quality variations following refractive surgery.8

Surgical results using Visumax 800 were first reported in 2023.3 Several studies2,3 have reported excellent results for refractive accuracy and analyzed changes in CS at few spatial frequencies with CSV-1000; however, no study has used qCSF to analyze CS at more comprehensive spatial frequencies or focused on the underlying factors of CS. Moreover, as the lenticule surface regularity is critical for achieving good visual quality, uneven lenticule surfaces with residual tissue bridges and cavitation defects will create irregular corneal stromal interfaces, induce intraocular light scattering, elevate higher-order aberrations, and further increase the risk of postoperative corneal haze, all of which jointly degrade visual performance.9 Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the early characteristics of CS using qCSF and explore the potential impact of lenticule surface regularity on CS.

Materials and Methods

Study and Patients

This study complied with the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. The Eye and ENT Hospital (EENT) ethics board at Fudan University approved this study (No. 2024050-2). This research recruited 92 patients between November and December 2024. Written informed consent was secured from all participants before their enrollment.

The inclusion criteria were 18 to 46 years of age, myopia or myopic astigmatism with a spherical equivalent refraction (SER) between −0.00 and −9.00 diopters (D), and cylindrical refraction (CR) of 4.00 D or less. Exclusion criteria included eyes with keratoconus, corneal scars, central corneal pachymetry < 480 μm, glaucoma, retinal disease, and any other conditions that might affect the efficacy or safety of laser treatment.

Every participant received a series of ophthalmological examinations, including slit-lamp biomicroscopy, manifested refraction (RT-5100, Nidek Technologies, Japan), axial length (IOLMaster; Carl Zeiss Meditec AG, Jena, Germany), corneal topography (Pentacam HR, Oculus Optikgeräte, Wetzlar, Germany), and intraocular pressure (Tonoref II, Nidek, Japan) measurement.

Surgical Procedure

A standard surgical procedure was performed by the same surgeon using the Visumax 800 platform (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG, Jena, Germany). Surgical parameters included a 2000 kHz repetition rate, cut energy between 130 nJ and 135 nJ, cap thickness between 110 and 130 μm, 7.6 mm cap diameter, optical zone between 6.0 and 6.8 mm, and the lateral incision angle is 90° with a length of 2 mm. All patients were routinely administered 0.1% fluorometholone eye drops, 0.5% levofloxacin eye drops, and sodium hyaluronate eye drops postoperatively. Routine re-examinations were conducted at 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months and 6 months postoperatively.

qCSF Test

In this study, qCSF tests were performed using an NEC P403 monitor (Gension and Waltai Digital Video System Co. Ltd., China)10 at 1, 3 and 6 months postoperatively. All tests were implemented under standardized uniform conditions: visual stimuli were presented horizontally 3 meters away from participants inside a dark examination room, all assessments were conducted under uncorrected monocular viewing. Testing was conducted separately for each eye: one eye remained uncovered for measurement while the fellow eye was blocked, then the second eye was examined using the same uniform setup. The sequence of spatial frequency measurements was fixed for all patients to eliminate order bias. All the calculations were automated using a computer program throughout the process, including the area under the log CS function (AULCSF), cutoff spatial frequency (CSF) acuity, and CS (log units) at 1.0, 1.5, 3.0, 6.0, 12.0, and 18.0 cycles per degree (cpd).

Lenticule Surface Regularity

The lenticules of subjects were enrolled using a random sampling method, with consideration given to the distribution of age and refractive status to ensure that the sample covered different age groups and a range of refractive errors. All candidate lenticules underwent rigorous quality screening; those with mechanical damage such as obvious scratches during surgery were excluded. Finally, 28 eyes from 28 subjects were included as the analysis sample. These lenticules were scanned using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) at four magnifications (100×, 200×, 400×, and 800×). The front and back surfaces of the lenticules were subjected to bilateral imaging.

The new evaluation criteria for Lenticule surface regularity in this study (Table 1) were developed by adjusting and optimizing the scoring criteria by Zhao et al.9 All anonymized SEM micrographs of stromal lenticules, stripped of any patient identifiers and clinical grouping information, were imported into the vision large language model Doubao-vision-pro 32k for automated image recognition and quantitative scoring. The full standardized grading rubric shown in Table 1 was input as prompt instructions to guide consistent scoring across all specimens. Statistical Analysis

Data analysis and visualization were performed using SPSS (version 20.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism 10.1 (GraphPad Software, LLC., San Diego, CA, USA). Demographic and measurement parameters were summarized with descriptive statistics and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) with range. The correlation and influencing factor analysis among variables were conducted based on the Generalized Estimating Equations model to control for the effect of binocular data. The unique patient identification number was set as the clustering subject variable, and ocular side was defined as the within-subject repeated factor to account for interocular correlation within each individual. An exchangeable working correlation structure was adopted to assume uniform correlation between the two eyes of one participant. Robust sandwich covariance matrix estimators were selected for all models to obtain stable parameter estimates. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05.

Regarding astigmatism, the mean value was −0.24±0.24 D, with 97% of the eyes within ±0.50 D and 100% within ±1.00 D (Figure 2E). Discussion

Observing postoperative visual quality is important for evaluating surgical outcomes and ensuring optimal postoperative visual function. As a key indicator, CS facilitates a comprehensive and detailed assessment of the postoperative visual system, providing critical evidence for clinical efficacy evaluation and personalized intervention. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to use qCSF to observe early postoperative changes in CS following KLEx with Visumax 800. Additionally, the factors influencing Lenticule surface regularity and the impact of such regularity on CS were investigated.

Regarding the visual and refractive outcomes, we found that KLEx with Visumax 800 is safe and effective in the early postoperative period, which is similar to some studies.2,3,11–14 Moreover, compared with Visumax 500, Visumax 800 provides a better lenticule scanning quality,11 a better intraoperative experience,15 higher precision in correcting astigmatism >2 D,14 lower total higher-order aberrations,16 and less residual astigmatism.17 Lee et al18,19 also investigated the differences among refractive lens exchange, femtosecond LASIK (FS-LASIK), and KLEx with Visumax 800, demonstrating similar visual and refractive outcomes, whereas FS-LASIK was associated with a higher incidence of dry eye syndrome postoperatively.

This study observed that AULCSF, CSF acuity, and CS (3.0 cpd–18.0 cpd) showed significant improvement from 1 to 6 months postoperatively, and also exhibited significant changes from 3 to 6 months postoperatively; however, no significant changes were observed from 1 to 3 months postoperatively. Additionally, improvements in visual and refractive outcomes at 1, 3 and 6 months postoperatively were significantly and positively correlated with improvements in AULCSF, CSF acuity, and CS at medium-to-high spatial frequencies. Yue et al20 observed a positive correlation between SER improvement and AULCSF improvement 7 days after KLEx with Visumax 500, which is consistent with our findings. Ganesh et al15 observed no significant change in CS at medium-to-high spatial frequencies from preoperatively to 3 months postoperatively, whereas Sekundo et al,2 incorporating data from a large sample (473 eyes) and observing up to 6 months postoperatively, discovered a significant improvement in CS at medium-to-high spatial frequencies from preoperatively to 6 months postoperatively. Collectively, these findings suggest that the visual quality after KLEx with Visumax 800 improves over time, and CS is highly likely to recover to or even exceed the preoperative level at 6 months postoperatively.

Femtosecond lasers generate lenticules by delivering laser pulses at specific corneal depths to cleave the tissue through pulse fragmentation. Numerous studies21–24 have demonstrated that low pulse energy and high laser frequency are closely related to high-quality lenticules. In this study, all patients undergoing surgery had the same laser frequency and energy, thereby eliminating the influence of pulse energy and laser frequency on lenticule quality. Tissue bridges refer to residual fibers between the corneal stromal interfaces after femtosecond laser cutting, with a greater number of tissue bridges associated with increased microLenticule surface irregularity.9,23 Our findings showed that older age was correlated with more regular Lenticule surfaces. With increasing age, the water content of the corneal stroma decreases,25 and natural crosslinking of collagen fibers in the stroma (including non-enzymatic glycosylation crosslinking) occurs,26,27 leading to denser and more regular fiber structures. This facilitates continuous cutting of surfaces during laser application, reduces tissue bridge formation at stromal interfaces, and results in more regular Lenticule surfaces. Additionally, younger patients have a higher proportion of elastic fiber components in the cornea,28 which may result in local fiber residues due to tissue elastic recoil during laser cutting, increasing tissue bridge formation at stromal interfaces and leading to more irregular Lenticule surfaces.

In this study, we also discovered that smaller cap thickness were associated with more regular Lenticule surfaces. Sarayba et al21 discovered that thin flaps (<120–130 μm) resulted in smoother corneal stromal bed surfaces than did thick flaps (>140 μm) in FS-LASIK. Weng et al29 set four different cap thicknesses (120, 130, 140, and 150 μm) in KLEx with Visumax 500 and observed that Lenticule surface regularity decreased with increasing cap thickness, consistent with the results of the present study. Collagen fibers in the anterior one-third of the corneal stroma are more densely arranged than those in the posterior two-thirds.30,31 A thinner cap means that the Lenticule cutting layer is closer to the anterior stroma, leading to a more uniform fiber fracture during laser cutting, reduced tissue bridge formation at the stromal interfaces, and more regular Lenticule surfaces.

Furthermore, we analyzed the Lenticule surface regularity and its potential impact on CS. Our findings showed that more regular Lenticule surfaces were significantly associated with improved medium spatial frequency CS, which is most relevant to daily life,32 at 1.3 and 6 months postoperatively. Kunert et al23 and Damgaard et al33 reported that Lenticule quality significantly correlated with postoperative visual acuity and complications such as corneal opacity. Vinciguerra et al34,35 also revealed a positive correlation between postoperative visual acuity and Lenticule surface regularity, which is consistent with the results of this study. Rough Lenticule surfaces may affect postoperative visual outcomes by inducing inflammatory responses36 or promoting corneal stromal cell accumulation and transformation into fibroblasts.23 Netto et al36 performed FS-LASIK in rabbits and conducted histopathological examinations of corneal tissues, revealing early postoperative inflammation, the severity of which correlated with corneal stromal quality. Kunert et al23 suggested that rough stromal beds lead to cytokine accumulation in the corneal stroma, whereas Salomao et al37 observed that elevated TGF-β concentrations promote myofibroblast differentiation, producing abnormal collagen and causing corneal opacity. Therefore, ensuring the regularity of the Lenticule surface and corneal stromal bed is crucial for achieving favorable early postoperative visual function and patient experience in refractive surgery.

This study had some limitations. First, it focused only on early postoperative changes, the long-term effects warrant further observation. Second, preoperative CS data was not collected. Thirdly, we used CS as a subjective indicator to reflect visual quality without incorporating objective metrics. Fourthly, the quantitative analysis of lenticule surface regularity was performed on a small subset of 28 lenticules, accounting for merely 15% of the total 184 eyes. The relatively low proportion of analyzable lenticules may reduce the generalizability of our findings, and future large-sample studies are needed to verify our conclusions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, KLEx performed with the VisuMax 800 yields excellent and stable visual outcomes at six months postoperatively and early postoperative CS improved over time. Lenticule surface regularity could contribute to postoperative visual quality to a certain extent.

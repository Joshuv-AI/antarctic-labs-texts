M2 macrophage-associated factors for constructing a prognostic model for osteosarcoma
Authors: Jian Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Osteosarcoma (OS) is a common clinical malignant bone tumor. This study aimed to identify M2 macrophage-associated factors to construct a prognostic model for OS.Methods:The osteosarcoma-related profiles including expression data and clinical information were obtained from TCGA and NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus, respectively. The M2 macrophage-associated differentially expressed RNAs (DERs) were identified between metastasis and nonmetastasis samples. LASSO Cox regression approach was applied to select the prognostic feature M2 macrophage-associated RNAs to construct a prognostic score (PS) model. The Kaplan–Meier approach was applied to analyze the survival. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis were performed to screen the independent clinical prognostic factors. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to compare the effects of the prognostic models.Results:A total of 153 M2 macrophage-related DERs were determined. 12 M2 macrophage-associated RNAs were identified to establish the PS model. The high risk group was associated with the shorter survival. Age, tumor metastatic and PS model status were determined as the independent clinical prognostic factors. ROC curves showed that the combined model performed well on predicting the survival of OS.Conclusion:This study provided several M2 macrophage-associated factors as the valuable prognostic biomarkers for OS.

1. Introduction

Osteosarcoma (OS) is the most frequent form of primary malignant bone tumor in children and adolescents, with an incidence rate of 3.4 cases per million worldwide annually.[1] The metaphysis of long bones is the most common location of OS. Approximately 20% of OS patients are diagnosed with metastatic disease, and the lung is the most common site. The current treatment for OS is multimodal, including surgery combined with preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy.[2] Despite advances of treatment in treatment over the past few decades, the survival rate of OS patients has plateaued.[3] For patients with the localized OS, the 5-year survival rate is approximately 70%; however, patients with metastatic OS have a poor prognosis, with a survival rate of only around 20%.[4] Therefore, improving the survival of OS patients is warranted, which underscores the need for novel and effective prognostic biomarkers.[5]

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a complex entity that affects tumorigenesis and tumor progression, creating opportunities to identify the novel therapeutic and prognostic targets for intervention.[6,7] Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are the major immune component in the TME and exhibit plasticity in response to signals from the local microenvironment.[8] Macrophages are mainly divided into pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages and anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages.[9] Increased M2 macrophage infiltration is associated with the metastasis and poor prognosis in OS, indicating its potential prognostic value.[10–12] In the present study, we identified several M2 macrophage-associated factors as biomarkers and further constructed a robust prognostic model for OS. This study may expand knowledge our understanding of OS prognosis and provide valuable prognostic tools for OS.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data collection

Osteosarcoma-related RNA-seq data and the corresponding clinical information were downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Of the 265 total samples, 176 samples with metastatic diagnosis information were filtered out and used as the training set. mRNA profiling data from GSE39055 was retrieved from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), and 36 samples with survival data from this dataset were used as the validation set. Both sets were re-annotated to obtain the expression levels of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and messenger RNAs (mRNAs).

2.2. Identification of differentially expressed RNAs

Samples in the training set were divided into metastasis and nonmetastasis groups according to the metastatic diagnosis information. The differentially expressed RNAs (DERs) between groups were determined by limma package with a cutoff of |logFC| > 1 and FDR < 0.05.

2.3. Identification of M2 macrophage-related DERs

Proportions of immune cell types in the training set were evaluated by CIBERSORT deconvolution algorithm. The Pearson correlation coefficients (PCC) between DER expression and M2 macrophage proportions were calculated by cor function in R in order to identify the M2 macrophage-related DERs with PCC > 0.4 and P < .05 as thresholds. Functional enrichment analysis of the M2 macrophage-related DERs was performed by DAVID with P < .05 as the threshold.

2.4. Construction of prognostic score model

In the training set, survival package in R was used to perform univariate Cox regression analysis to the M2 macrophage-related DERs in order to identify the significant prognostic DERs. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was then performed to determine the significant independent prognostic DERs. LASSO Cox regression approach was applied to select the prognostic feature DERs by using penalized package in R. In which βDERs represented the regression coefficient of the DER in the LASSO model, and ExpDERs denoted the expression value of the DER. Based on the formula, the PS value of each sample in the training set and validation set was computed. Samples were divided into high and low risk groups with the median PS as the cutoff value. The 2 groups were then analyzed for survival using survival package in R.

2.5. Identification of independent clinical prognostic factors

In the training set, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis were applied to screen the independent clinical prognostic factors using survival package in R. Each independent clinical prognostic factor was utilized to establish the single prognostic factor model, and pROC package in R was used to compare the effects of the single prognostic factor model, PS model and the combined model (prognostic factors + PS model).

2.6. Construction of a ceRNA network

The OS-related miRNAs were downloaded from the Human MicroRNA Disease Database. The interactions between lncRNAs in the PS model and the OS-related miRNAs were retrieved in DIANA-LncBase. The target genes of the OS-related miRNAs that were paired with the lncRNAs were searched by starBase. The intersections of the target genes and M2 macrophage-associated DERs were retained to construct the interactions between the OS-related miRNAs and M2 macrophage-associated mRNAs. Finally, a ceRNA network was built combing the lncRNA-miRNA associations and miRNA-mRNA associations, and was visualized by Cytoscape.

3. Results

3.1. DERs between metastasis and nonmetastasis groups

After re-annotation, 1398 lncRNAs and 14,631 mRNAs were obtained in both sets. A total of 343 DERs were identified between the metastatic and nonmetastatic groups, including 79 lncRNAs and 264 mRNAs (Fig. 1A). Hierarchical clustering analysis showed that metastatic and nonmetastatic samples could be distinguished based on the expression profiles of DERs (Fig. 1B).

3.2. M2 macrophage-related DERs

The proportions of immune cell types in the training set were calculated, and 3 immune cell types showed significant differences between the metastatic and nonmetastatic groups, including plasma B cell, activated NK cell, and M2 macrophage (Fig. 2). The proportion of M2 macrophages was higher in the metastasis group. A total of 153 M2 macrophage-related DERs were identified based on the PCCs between DER expression and M2 macrophage proportions, including 32 lncRNAs and 121 mRNAs (Table S1). These M2 macrophage-related DERs were significantly enriched in 21 biological processes, including lipid transport, blood coagulation, phagocytosis, negative regulation of the Wnt signaling pathway, xenobiotic transport, etc (Figure S1). They were also involved in 6 KEGG pathways, including ABC transporters, the Wnt signaling pathway, the TGF-β signaling pathway, the Hippo signaling pathway, arachidonic acid metabolism, and the complement and coagulation cascades (Figure S1).

3.3. Construction of PS model

In the training set, 47 significant prognostic DERs were determined via univariate Cox regression analysis, with a log-rank P < .05 as the threshold, included 6 lncRNAs and 41 mRNAs. On this basis, 12 significant independent prognostic DERs were further identified through multivariate Cox regression analysis, including 1 lncRNA and 11 mRNAs. Additionally, 12 DERs were selected as the prognostic feature RNAs, specifically LINC00954, AQP3, BMP8A, BTBD11, C5orf49, MEDAG, MS4A4E, PCP4L1, SLC24A4, TNXB, TSKS, and ZNF541 (Fig. 3). According to the median PS value, samples in both sets were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups. The Kaplan–Meier curves showed that the high-risk group was associated with the shorter survival in both sets (Fig. 4). In the training set, the 3-year and 5-year AUC values of the PS model was 0.86 and 0.85, respectively, and those in the validation set were 0.83 and 0.85, respectively (Fig. 4).

3.4. Independent clinical prognostic factors

In the training set, 3 independent clinical prognostic factors were identified via univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis, including age, tumor metastasis and PS model status (Figure S2). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves showed that the combined model had the highest AUC value (0.92), thus, it performed better in predicting the survival of OS patients than the single prognostic-factor models and the PS model alone (Fig. 5).

3.5. The ceRNA regulatory network

A total of 200 OS-related miRNAs were obtained, among which 30 OS-related miRNAs were associated with LINC00954, the lncRNA in the PS model. by comparing the target genes of the OS-related miRNAs and M2 macrophage-associated DERs, 26 mRNAs were retained. Basis on this, 100 pairs of OS-related miRNAs and M2 macrophage-associated mRNAs were identified. Finally, a ceRNA regulatory network was constructed, consisting of one lncRNA, 30 OS-related miRNAs and 26 M2 macrophage-associated mRNAs (Fig. 6).

4. Discussion

The survival rates of OS have stagnated for a long period, especially in the metastatic cases. To improve the survival outcomes of OS, in the present study, we identified several M2 macrophage-associated factors with prognostic significance. Based on this, we established a PS model for predicting the prognosis of OS, and assessed the predictive power.

Macrophages develop different phenotypes with specific functions under the stimuli in the TME. Macrophages recruited at the implanted site of the human osteosarcoma in a mouse model were polarized to the M2 phenotype.[13] M2 macrophages demonstrate the pro-tumor effects and facilitate the progression and metastasis of OS by promoting angiogenesis, increasing extravascular invasion and mediating immunosuppression.[14,15] The polarization and number of macrophages are associated with the prognosis of OS,[16,17] and M2 macrophages are related to the poor prognosis.[18] Therefore, macrophage-targeting treatment is a promising avenue for OS, and the main therapeutic strategies include the reduction of macrophage recruitment, depletion of macrophages, and the transformation from pro-tumor M2 to antitumor M1 phenotype.[19]

Current prognostic models for OS primarily rely on clinical features and serum markers, lacking immune-related molecular signatures.[20] Notably, M2-type macrophages are key regulators of the TME in OS, and their infiltration and associated molecular alterations have been closely linked to OS progression and poor prognosis.[21] In this study, twelve M2 macrophage-associated factors were therefore determined as biomarkers to establish the prognostic model of OS. Compared with the OS prognostic model established by Ye et al (which showed approximate 3-year/5-year AUC values of 0.8), the findings in our study exhibited better performance in predicting OS survival.[22] Specifically, the PS model constructed based on these factors achieved relatively high AUCs for 3-year and 5-year survival prediction in both the training set and validation set, indicating superior predictive accuracy. Moreover, according to the PS median value, OS samples were divided into high and low risk groups, and the PS was significantly associated with the prognosis. In the low risk group, about 70% of OS patients survived within 5 years, however, over 60% of OS patients died in the high risk group. The prognostic model may help to differentiate OS patients, and aggressive treatments and interventions should be taken into consideration for the high risk patients to improve the survival.

Age, tumor metastasis and PS model status were served as the independent clinical prognostic factors of OS. The age distribution of OS is bimodal, 2 peaks of which are in the young adolescents and the geriatric, respectively.[23] The age of OS patients is relevant to the survival, and older patients have the poorer outcomes.[24] Metastasis seems to be the most important factor of the poor prognosis and treatment failure of OS.[25] The combined model constructed by the 3 prognostic factors was superior to the others (including the PS model) on predicting the survival of OS as it owned the highest AUC value. These findings indicated that the combination of the clinical traits and the PS model may help to better improve the prognosis and develop the personalized medicine of OS patients.

MiRNAs have been identified as potential regulators of racial disparities in various cancers, including OS.[26–28] A ceRNA regulatory network associated with OS was then established, in which four M2 macrophage-associated prognostic factors were involved, specifically, LINC00954, AQP3, BTBD11, and PCP4L1. In the network, AQP3 was regulated by hsa-miR-194, hsa-miR-125a, hsa-miR-125b, hsa-miR-127 and hsa-miR-221, BTBD11 and PCP4L1 was regulated by hsa-miR-326 and hsa-miR-491, respectively. AQP3 (Aquaporin 3) is a kind of water channel protein, which is responsible for transporting water and other small uncharged solutes, and plays an important role in maintaining the fluid homeostasis. AQP3 can affect the tumor progression via the Wnt/GSK-3β/β-catenin pathway,[29] and the inhibition of AQP3 could suppress the tumourigenesis, proliferation, and invasion of tumors.[30,31] Although BTBD11 (BTB domain containing 11) and PCP4L1 (Purkinje cell protein 4-like 1) were regarded as the prognostic biomarkers of OS in this study, there is lack of evidence on their roles in the development and progression of OS, which needs further investigation.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, we identified several M2 macrophage-associated factors as biomarkers to construct a prognostic model with good performance on the prediction of OS prognosis. This study provided valuable prognostic tools for OS, which may contribute to the improvement of OS prognosis.

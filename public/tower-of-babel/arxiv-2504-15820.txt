2
Optical potential methodology

\Xi^{-}
atomic and nuclear bound states in
N=Z
nuclei such as
12
C and

14
N are calculated using a finite-size Coulomb potential
V_{c}^{\Xi^{-}}
,
including vacuum-polarization terms, plus a
\Xi
-nuclear density-dependent
optical potential,

V_{\rm opt}^{\Xi}(\rho)=V_{\Xi}^{(2)}(\rho)+V_{\Xi}^{(3)}(\rho),

(1)

consisting of terms representing two-body
\Xi N
and three-body

\Xi NN
interactions:

V_{\Xi}^{(2)}(\rho)=-\frac{4\pi}{2\mu_{\Xi}}f^{(2)}_{A}\,b_{0}^{A}(\rho)\,\rho,

(2)

V_{\Xi}^{(3)}(\rho)=+\frac{4\pi}{2\mu_{\Xi}}f^{(3)}_{A}\,B_{0}\,\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho_{0}},

(3)

with strength parameters
b_{0}^{A}(\rho),B_{0}
in units of fm (
\hbar=c=1
).
In these expressions
A
is the mass number of the
nuclear core
,
\rho

is a nuclear density normalized to
A
,
\mu_{\Xi}
is the
\Xi^{-}
-nucleus
reduced mass and
f^{(2,3)}_{A}
are kinematical factors transforming

b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
and
B_{0}
from the
\Xi N
and
\Xi NN

center-of-mass (c.m.) systems, respectively, to the
\Xi
-nucleus c.m. system:

f^{(2)}_{A}=1+\frac{A-1}{A}\frac{\mu_{\Xi}}{m_{N}},\,\,\,\,\,\,f^{(3)}_{A}=1+\frac{A-2}{A}\frac{\mu_{\Xi}}{2m_{N}}.

(4)

The effective density-dependent
\Xi N
isoscalar c.m. scattering amplitude

b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
is given by

b_{0}^{A}(\rho)=\frac{{\rm Re}\,b_{0}}{1+(3k_{F}/2\pi)f^{(2)}_{A}{\rm Re}\,b_{0}}+{\rm i}\,{\rm Im}\,b_{0},

(5)

where
k_{F}
is the Fermi momentum associated with density
\rho
:

k_{F}=(3{\pi}^{2}\rho/2)^{1/3}
. The density dependence of
b_{0}^{A}(\rho)
accounts
for long-range Pauli correlations in
\Xi N
in-medium multiple scatterings,
starting at
\rho^{4/3}
in a nuclear-density expansion
[
23
,
24
]

as practised in our
K^{-}
-atom studies
[
25
]
. Introducing it also in

V_{\Xi}^{(3)}
makes little difference, which is why it is skipped in
Eq. (
3
). Short-range
NN
correlations, starting at order
\rho^{2}

of the optical-potential density expansion, have little effect at densities
below
\rho_{0}
where the main contribution arises in the present context from
three-body
\Xi NN
interactions. Precisely the same functional form of

V_{\rm opt}(\rho)
, including
V^{(3)}(\rho)
, was used by us recently for
constructing the
\Lambda
-nuclear optical potential
V_{\rm opt}^{\Lambda}(\rho)

[
16
,
17
]
. Three-body
YNN
interactions appear at order
N
2
LO in EFT formulations and are promoted to NLO by adding the baryon SU(3)
decuplet to the ground-state octet
[
26
]
. For
Y=\Xi
,
\Xi N_{1}N_{2}

interactions occur by exciting the octet
\Xi
hyperon to a decuplet

\Xi^{\ast}(1530)
hyperon on nucleon
N_{1}
,
\Xi N_{1}\to\Xi^{\ast}N_{1}
,
and deexciting it back to
\Xi
on nucleon
N_{2}
,
\Xi^{\ast}N_{2}\to\Xi N_{2}
.

The nuclear densities
\rho(r)=\rho_{p}(r)+\rho_{n}(r)
used by us to construct

V_{\rm opt}^{\Xi}(\rho)
are harmonic-oscillator type densities with the same
radial parameters for neutrons and protons
[
27
]
. The corresponding
r.m.s. radii follow closely values derived from experiment by relating proton
densities
\rho_{p}(r)
to charge densities and including the proton charge
finite size and recoil effects. This approach is equivalent to assigning some
finite range to the
\Xi N
interaction. Folding reasonably chosen
\Xi N

interaction ranges other than corresponding to the proton charge radius,
varying the spatial form of the charge density, or introducing realistic
differences between neutron and proton r.m.s. radii, made little difference.
In
\Xi^{-}_{1p}(^{12}
C), for example, all such calculated binding
energies varied within 20% of the
\pm
0.15 MeV measurement uncertainty
of
B_{\Xi^{-}}^{1p}(^{12}
C). Finally, a fairly small absorptivity,
Im
\,b_{0}=0.01
fm in Eq. (
5
), was assumed in all of
the fits shown and discussed below. Using only
V_{\Xi}^{(2)}
,

B_{\Xi^{-}}^{1p}(^{12}
C)=0.82
\pm
0.15 MeV was fitted by
Re
\,b_{0}=0.495\pm 0.030
fm; doubling Im
\,b_{0}
was found to
increase the fitted value of Re
\,b_{0}
by only 1%
[
18
]
.
Such small absorptivities reflect the small statistical weight,
1/16 in nuclear matter, of the
S=I=0

\Xi N
channel in which

\Xi N\to\Lambda\Lambda
conversion occurs.

In this work we explored to what extent a

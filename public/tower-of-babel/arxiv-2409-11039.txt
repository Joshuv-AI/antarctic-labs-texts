Existence of multiple radial solutions for nonlinear equation involving the mean curvature operator in Lorentz-Minkowski space
Vittorio Coti Zelati, Xu Dong, Yuanhong Wei

Abstract
. This paper devotes to the multiplicity of the radial solutions for the Dirichlet problem for nonlinear equations involving the mean curvature operator in Lorentz-Minkowski space − div ⁡ ( ∇ u 1 − | ∇ u | 2 ) = λ ​ b ​ ( | x | ) ​ | u | q − 2 ​ u + f ⁡ ( | x | , u ) in ​ B R , -\dive(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}}})=\lambda b(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u+f(\lvert x\rvert,u)\qquad\text{in }B_{R}, in case q ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) q\in(1,2) and f ⁡ ( | x | , s ) f(\lvert x\rvert,s) is superlinear in s s . Multiplicity results are also given for some cases in which f f depends also on (the absolute value of) the gradient of u u .

Introduction

This paper is about existence of multiple radial solutions for
nonlinear equations involving the mean curvature operator in
Lorentz-Minkowski space

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\dive(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}}})=\lambda b(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u+f(\lvert x\rvert,u)&\text{in }B_{R},\\

where

\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

,

Under this ansatz
u(\lvert x\rvert)
, where

The operator

[
12

15
]

[
11
]

The study of the nonlinear equations involving the operator

\mathcal{Q}
has received a lot of attention after the publication of
the paper
[
3
]
by Bereanu, Jebelean,
Mawhin and since then several different methods have been used to
tackle different nonlinear problems involving such and operator.

Fixed point theorems have been used to prove existence of radial
solutions for both Dirichlet and Neumann problems with different kind
of nonlinearities in
[
3
,
5
,
9
,
8
]
.

The same authors have then introduced a variational approach to the
problem to prove, using critical point theory for nonsmooth
functionals the existence radial solutions for the Neumann problem in

[
6
,
7
]
. In

[
4
]
they prove, with similar

Also using critical point theory for nonsmooth functionals Bonheure,
d’Avenia and Pomponio in
[

prove
existence and uniqueness of the weak solution for the electrostatic
Born-Infeld equation

\begin{cases}-\dive(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1-\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}}})=\rho&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\to+\infty}u(x)=0\end{cases}

Motivated in particular by the results of Coelho, Corsato, and

[

study existence of critical points for a non-smooth functional

[

[

2.1.
Szulkin critical point theory for non-smooth functionals

To find solutions of our problem we will use the the critical point
theory for non-smooth functionals developed by Szulkin in

[
16
]
, which we present briefly here.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a real Banach space and
\Psi\colon X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a convex lower semi-continuous function. Let

D(\Psi)=\bigl\{\,u\in X\bigm|\Psi(u)<+\infty\,\bigr\}
be the effective
domain of
\Psi
. Denote by
X^{*}
the dual of
X
and by

\langle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rangle
the duality pairing between
X^{*}
and

X
. For
u\in D(\Psi)
, the set

\partial\Psi(u)=\bigl\{\,u^{*}\in X^{*}\bigm|\Psi(v)-\Psi(u)\geq\langle u^{*}\,|\,v-u\rangle\ \forall v\in D(\Psi)\,\bigr\}

is called the sub-differential of
\Psi
at
u
.

For a functional of the form
I(u)=\Psi(u)+\mathcal{F}(u)
,
with
\Psi(u)
as above and
\mathcal{F}\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
then
u\in D(\Psi)
is said to be a critical point for
I
if

(2.2)

\langle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(u)\,|\,v-u\rangle+\Psi(v)-\Psi(u)\geq 0,\qquad\forall v\in D(\Psi).

or, equivalently, if
-\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(u)\in\partial\Psi(u)
.

A number
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
I^{-1}(c)
contains a
critical point is called a critical value of
I
.

Proposition 2.3

(Proposition 1.1 in
[
16
]
)
.

If
u\in X
is a local minimum for
I
then
u
is a critical
point.

Always following
[
16
]
we now state in this setting the
Palais-Smale compactness condition.

Definition 2.4
.

The functional
I(u)=\Psi(u)+\mathcal{F}(u)
satisfies the
Palais-Smale condition if every sequence
\{u_{n}\}\in X
for
which
I(u_{n})\to c
and

(2.5)

\langle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(u_{n})\,|\,v-u_{n}\rangle+\Psi(v)-\Psi(u_{n})\geq-\varepsilon_{n}\lVert v-u_{n}\rVert\qquad\forall v\in D(\Psi),

where
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
possesses a convergent subsequence.

Proposition 2.6

(Theorem 1.7 of
[
16
]
)
.

If
I
is bounded from below and satisfies (PS) then

c=\inf_{X}I

is a critical value.

Proposition 2.7
.

(Mountain Pass Theorem, see Theorem 3.2 of

[
16
]
)

Suppose that
I=\Psi+\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathcal{F}\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
\Psi\colon X\to(-\infty,+\infty]

is convex, proper (i.e.
\Psi\not\equiv+\infty)
, and lower
semi-continuous, is a function satisfying Palais-Smale condition
and

(i)

I(0)=0
and there exist
M
,
\rho>0
such that

I|_{\partial B_{\rho}}\geq M
,

(ii)

I(e)\leq 0
for some
e\notin\overline{B}_{\rho}
.

Then
I
has a critical value

c\geq M

which may be
characterized by

c=\inf_{\phi\in\Phi}\sup_{s\in[0,1]}I(\phi(s)),

where
\Phi=\bigl\{\,\phi\in C([0,1],X)\bigm|\phi(0)=0,\ \phi(1)=e\,\bigr\}
.

2.2.
The functional space and some inequalities

We let

\mathcal{A}(0,R)=\bigl\{\,u\in W_{\text{loc}}^{1,1}(0,R]\bigm|\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}\,dr<+\infty,\ u(R)=0\,\bigr\},

which is an Hilbert space endowed with the scalar product

\langle u\,|\,v\rangle:=\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)v^{\prime}(r)\,dr

and the corresponding norm

\lVert u\rVert^{2}=\langle u\,|\,u\rangle.

For
p\geq 1
, we consider the following weighted Lebesgue spaces

L^{p}_{N-1}(0,R):=\bigl\{\,u\colon(0,R)\to\mathbb{R},\text{measurable}\bigm|\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dr<+\infty\,\bigr\}.

The space
L^{p}_{N-1}(0,R)
(
L^{p}_{N-1}
for brevity) is a Banach
space with norm

\lvert u\rvert_{N-1,p}=\left(\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

while with
L^{\infty}
we denote the usual Lebesgue space

L^{\infty}(0,R)
and with
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{\infty}
its norm. Let us
remark that for all radially symmetric functions
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
we have that
v(x)=u(\lvert x\rvert)
for
some function
u\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, and

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}\lvert\nabla v(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}(r)\rvert^{2}\,dr,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}\lvert v(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-1}\lvert u(r)\rvert^{p}\,dr,

and hence we have that for radial functions the norm in
\mathcal{A}

is, up to a constant, the norm in
H^{1}_{0}(B(0,R))
and the norms

\lvert\cdot\rvert_{N-1,p}
are the
L^{p}(B(0,R))
norms. The Hilbert
space
\mathcal{A}
can be identified with the subspace of

H^{1}_{0}(B(0,R))
consisting of radial functions.

Let us now recall some well known properties of
\mathcal{A}
, which
plays an essential role in the proof of the main result. One can find
a more general statement – and a sketch of the proofs – in

[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.8

(Hardy’s inequality for radial functions, Proposition
1 of
[
10
]
)
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
. Then for any
u\in\mathcal{A}
it holds

\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-3}u^{2}dr\leq\frac{4}{(N-2)^{2}}\lVert u\rVert^{2}.

Lemma 2.9

(Corollary 2 of
[
10
]
and
Lemma 2.2 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
m>(N-2)/2
. Then for all
u\in\mathcal{A}

\lvert r^{m}u\rvert_{\infty}\leq C(N,m,R)\lVert u\rVert.

The following Lemma is the Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}_{0}(B(0,R))
in

L^{p}(B(0,R))
in case of radial functions.

Lemma 2.10

(Proposition 3 of
[
10
]
and
Lemma 2.3 of
[
13
]
)
.

If
N\geq 3
,
p\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-2})
and
N=2
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then there exists a constant
C(N,p,R)
such that for
any
u\in\mathcal{A}
,

(2.11)

\lvert u\rvert_{N-1,p}^{p}\leq C(N,p,R)\lVert u\rVert^{p}.

Remark 2.12
.

The inequality (
2.11
) is actually the

Poincaré inequality
. Shows that the norm
\lVert u\rVert
is
actually equivalent – for radial functions – to the usual norm
in
H^{1}_{0}(B(0,R))
.

We recall the following compact embedding, equivalent to the Sobolev
embedding of
H^{1}_{0}
in
L^{p}
for the ball of radius
R
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume that
p\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-2})
if
N\geq 3
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
if
N=2
. Then the following embedding is compact:

\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{N-1}.

We finally introduce the set
K
, in which we will look for
solutions of our problem.

K=\bigl\{\,u\in\mathcal{A}\bigm|\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert_{\infty}\leq 1\,\bigr\}.

Remark that
K\subset W^{1,\infty}(0,R)\subset W^{1,2}(0,R)
.

Lemma 2.14
.

K
is a closed, convex and bounded subset of
\mathcal{A}
. We
also have that

\lvert u\rvert_{\infty}\leq R\qquad\text{ for all }u\in K.

Proof.

The convexity is immediate. Let us remark that the functions in

K
are Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
1
, and
K\subset W^{1,\infty}(0,R)
. As a consequence we have that
\lVert u\rVert^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-1}\,dr=\frac{R^{N}}{N}
and also

\lvert u(r)\rvert\leq\int_{r}^{R}\lvert u^{\prime}(r)\rvert\,dr\leq R
. Then the
family of functions
K
is equicontinuous and equibounded.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset K
,
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathcal{A}
. We can
apply Ascoli-Arzelà to deduce that a subsequence converges
uniformly to a function
u
, and also
u
is 1-Lipschitz, and we
immediately deduce that
\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert_{\infty}\leq 1
and
u\in K\subset W^{1,\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}
.
∎

3.
The variational problem

In this section we introduce the notion of weak solution of problem
(

We will always assume in this section that

(F1)

Definition 3.1
.

A weak solution of (

(3.2)

\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}u^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr=\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\bigl(\lambda b(r)\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u+f(r,u)\bigr)\varphi\,dr.

.

(

) holds for all
\varphi\in K

\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}\bigcap\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
u
is a weak solution, then
u\in C^{1}([0,R])
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr<+\infty,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr<+\infty

and there exists

such that
\lvert u^{\prime}(r)\rvert<1-\varepsilon

r\in[0,R]
.

Moreover for a.e.

(3.7)

-\left(\frac{r^{N-1}u^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\right)^{\prime}=r^{N-1}\bigl(\lambda b(r)\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u+f(r,u)\bigr).

Proof.

The following proof is similar to
[

.

Since

,

is continuous and
\lvert u(r)\rvert\leq R
and we

Taking

\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr=\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\left(\lambda b(r)\lvert u\rvert^{q}+f(r,u)u\right)\,dr<+\infty.

and

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{0}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,R)\cap\{\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\}}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(0,R)\cap\{\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert>\frac{1}{2}\}}\frac{r^{N-1}\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert}{\sqrt{1-\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr

From the fact that

and (

0^{+}
. Fix

-h_{1}(0)\varphi(0)=\int^{R}_{0}\left(h_{1}^{\prime}(r)+r^{N-1}\left(\lambda b(r)\lvert u(r)\rvert^{q-2}u(r)+f(r,u(r))\right)\right)\varphi\,dr.

Integrating (

that

Consequently,

with

We will now start building the functional whose critical points will

First of all we observe that, since

\frac{1}{2}t\leq 1-\sqrt{1-t}\leq t,\text{ for all }t\in[0,1],

,

Lemma 3.14
.

\Psi
is weakly lower semi-continuous on
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

In this proof we follow ideas from Bereanu, Jebelean, and Mawhin

[
4
]
.

It is enough to show that
\Psi(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Psi(u_{n})
for sequences
\{u_{n}\}\subset K
which weakly
converge to some
u\in K
. By Ascoli-Arzelà Theorem we know
that a subsequence

uniformly in
C[0,R]
. Since

\lvert u^{\prime}_{n}\rvert_{\infty}\leq 1
, we know that a subsequence (which
we still denote

) converges in the weak*

\sigma(L^{\infty},L^{1})
topology to a function

, that is

\int_{0}^{R}u^{\prime}_{n}(r)w(r)\,dr\to\int_{0}^{R}v(r)w(r)\,dr,\qquad\text{for all }w\in L^{1}(0,R).

Take any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,R)
. We have that

\int^{R}_{0}u(r)\varphi^{\prime}(r)\,dr=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int^{R}_{0}u_{n}(r)\varphi^{\prime}(r)\,dr\\
=-\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int^{R}_{0}u^{\prime}_{n}(r)\varphi(r)\,dr=-\int^{R}_{0}v(r)\varphi(r)\,dr,

and hence
v(r)=u^{\prime}(r)
.

Since the function
t\mapsto 1-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}
is convex, we
deduce that for all
t\in(0,1)
,

(3.15)

\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}(1-\sqrt{1-\lvert u_{n}^{\prime}\rvert^{2}})\,dr\\
\geq\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}(1-\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}})\,dr+\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\frac{tu^{\prime}(u^{\prime}_{n}-tu^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr.

Since
\lvert tu^{\prime}(r)\rvert\leq t<1
for all
r\in[0,R]
if
t\in(0,1)
we have that

w_{t}(r)\equiv r^{N-1}\left|\frac{tu^{\prime}(r)}{\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}(r)}}\right|\leq\frac{R^{N-1}t}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}

and

follows. Then

\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\frac{tu^{\prime}(r)}{\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}(r)\rvert^{2}}}u^{\prime}_{n}(r)\,dr\to\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\frac{t\lvert u^{\prime}(r)\rvert^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}(r)\rvert^{2}}}\,dr

and from (

) we deduce that for all

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}(1-\sqrt{1-\lvert u_{n}^{\prime}\rvert^{2}})\,dr\\
\geq\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}(1-\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}})\,dr+\frac{1-t}{t}\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}\frac{\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}}}\,dr\\
\geq\int^{R}_{0}r^{N-1}(1-\sqrt{1-\lvert tu^{\prime}\rvert^{2}})\,dr.

Since

for all

, we can use Lebesgue dominated convergence to pass to
the limit as

and

is weakly lower semicontinuous.
∎

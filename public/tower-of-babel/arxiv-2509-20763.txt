The odd independence number of graphs, I: Foundations and classical classes
Yair Caro, Mirko Petruševski, Riste Škrekovski, Zsolt Tuza

Abstract

The largest cardinality of odd independent sets of a graph G G , denoted α od ​ ( G ) \alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G) , is called the odd independence number of G G . This new parameter is a natural companion to the recently introduced strong odd chromatic number. A proper vertex coloring of a graph G G is a strong odd coloring if, for every vertex v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , each color used in the neighborhood of v v appears an odd number of times in N ⁡ ( v ) N(v) . The minimum number of colors in a strong odd coloring of G G is denoted by χ so ​ ( G ) \chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G) . We develop several basic inequalities concerning α od ​ ( G ) \alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G) , and use already existing results on strong odd coloring, to derive lower bounds for odd independence in many families of graphs. Notation

Let
G
be any graph (simple, undirected).
We denote its number
|V(G)|
of vertices by
|G|
(the order of
G
),
and its number of edges by
e(G)
.
As usual,
\alpha(G)
and
\chi(G)
denote the independence number and the chromatic number of
G
, respectively.
Further standard notations are

(open neighborhood),

(closed neighborhood), and
\deg_{G}(v)

\deg(v)
(degree) of a vertex

, and similarly

and

for the corresponding neighborhoods (open/closed) of a set

.
The average degree of graph

is

.
In the square
G^{2}
of

, two vertices are adjacent if and
only if they are at distance at most 2 apart in
G
(i.e., they are
adjacent or have a common neighbor).

Well-known basic types of graphs are the complete graph
K_{n}
, the empty (edgeless) graph
E_{n}
, and the path
P_{n}
of order
n
.
The graph operations
G\cup H
(vertex-disjoint union),
G+H
(complete join),
tG
(the vertex-disjoint union of
t
copies of
G
) are also standard.

Beside
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
we shall occasionally mention the further parameter
\chi_{\mathrm{o}}(G)
, termed odd chromatic number, which is the smallest number
k
of

k

vertex
v\in V(G)
, some color occurs an odd number of times in
N(v)
.

2
Some basic facts

In the next proposition we collect the basic relations
between
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
, parallel to the classical relation between
\alpha(G)
and
\chi(G)
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on
n
vertices.

(1)

\chi_{\mathrm{o}}(G)\leq\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq\chi(G^{2})\leq\Delta(G^{2})+1\leq(\Delta(G))^{2}+1
.

(2)

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\chi(G^{2})
whenever
G
is claw-free.

(3)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq n/\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\geq n/\chi(G^{2})\geq n/(\Delta(G^{2})+1)\geq n/((\Delta(G))^{2}+1)
.

(4.1)

(n+1)^{2}\!/4\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\geq n
,
and both bounds are best possible.

(4.2)

n+1\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)+\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\geq 2\sqrt{n}
,
and both bounds are best possible.

(5)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\alpha(G^{2})
.

(6)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\sum_{v\in V}1/(\deg_{G^{2}}(v)+1)\geq n/(d(G^{2})+1)

\geq n/(d(G)\Delta(G)+1)\geq n/((\Delta(G))^{2}+1)
.

Proof.

(1) The only inequality that needs a proof is
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq\chi(G^{2})
.
Let us consider a coloring of
G^{2}
with
\chi(G^{2})
colors.
Then in
G
every vertex
v
has all the vertices of
N(v)
getting distinct colors (as
N[v]
forms a clique in
G^{2}
), hence any color present in
N(v)
appears once.

(2) This was proved already in the paper on strong odd coloring,
[
3
, Proposition 3]
.

(3) If
S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{k}=V(G)
is a partition into
k:=\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
odd independent sets,
then
|G|=|S_{1}|+\dots+|S_{k}|\leq k\cdot\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\cdot\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
.
This implies the leftmost inequality.
The rest of the chain of inequalities follows from item (1).

(4.1)–(4.2) Clearly from (3) we have
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\geq n
. By the inequality
between arithmetic and geometric means, denoting

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=x
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=y
, we obtain
x+y\geq 2\sqrt{xy}\geq 2\sqrt{n}
, proving the right side of (4.2).
Now choose a maximum odd independent set
S
, color all its vertices
with the same color, and the remaining
n-|S|
vertices with
distinct colors, altogether using
n-\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)+1
colors.
This is easily seen to be a strong odd coloring. Hence
n+1\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)+\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
. Lastly, again by the inequality between arithmetic
and geometric means, with the same notation as above,

(n+1)/2\geq(x+y)/2\geq\sqrt{xy}
holds, hence
we conclude that
(n+1)^{2}\!/4\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
.

Tightness of the lower bounds is shown by the graph
G=tK_{r}
,
n=tr
.
Then
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=r
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=t
, hence
n=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
.
In particular, for
t=r
and
n=r^{2}
we obtain
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)+\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=2r=2\sqrt{n}
.

Tightness of the upper bounds is shown by the graph
G=K_{r}\cup tK_{1}
,

n=r+t
.
Then
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=r
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=t+1
, hence
n+1=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)+\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
.
In particular, if
n=2k+1
is odd, we take
r=k+1=(n+1)/2
and

t=k
, hence obtain
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=r\cdot(t+1)=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}
.
If
n=2k
is even, we can take
r=t=k
and obtain
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=k^{2}+k=(k+1/2)^{2}-1/4=\lfloor\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.

(5) Let
S
be a maximum independent set in
G^{2}
and consider
S
as an
independent set in
G
(clearly it is).
Then a vertex in
V\,\diagdown\,S
is adjacent to at most one vertex
in
S
, since if it was adjacent to two vertices
x,y\in S
then

x,y
are not independent in
G^{2}
and thus cannot be both in
S
.

(6) Observe that
\deg_{G^{2}}(v)\leq\deg(v)+\deg(v)(\Delta(G)-1)=\deg(v)\Delta(G)
holds for every vertex
v
.
Hence, applying (5) and the Caro–Wei inequality,
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\alpha(G^{2})\geq\sum_{v\in V}1/(\deg_{G^{2}}(v)+1)\geq\sum_{v\in V}1/(\deg_{G}(v)\Delta(G)+1)\geq n/(d(G)\Delta(G)+1)
, the last step obtained via Jensen’s inequality.

Alternatively,

d(G^{2})=2e(G^{2})/n\leq\bigl(\sum_{v\in V}\deg_{G^{2}}(v)\bigr)/n\leq\Delta(G)\bigl(\sum_{v\in V}\deg(v)\bigr)/n=\Delta(G)d(G)

and, by Turán’s theorem,

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\alpha(G^{2})\geq n/(d(G^{2})+1)\geq n/(d(G)\Delta(G)+1)\geq n/(\Delta^{2}(G)+1)\,.

\Box

Proposition 2
.

If

is a bipartite graph with all degrees odd, then

(i)

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=2
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/2\geq(\alpha(G)+1)/2

(ii)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G)=|G|/2
.

Proof.

By the degree assumption, each vertex class is an odd independent set, and one of them

.
(The lower bound

Tightness is shown by the stars

If

.

\Box

We can also state an asymptotic version of the above assertion, for
graphs that are not far from being regular.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Delta
be an even natural number, and for

be a graph on

of degrees smaller than

Then
\limsup\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G_{j})/n\leq\frac{\Delta-1}{2\Delta-1}\,n

Proof.

Let
B_{j}
be a maximum odd independent set of

,
Z_{j}\subset V(G_{j})

\Delta
, and
B=B_{j}\,\diagdown\,Z_{j}
.
Clearly,
|B_{j}|\geq n/(\Delta^{2}+1)

is large enough then

.
Due to the parity conditions, every vertex not in

is adjacent to
at most
\Delta-1
vertices in
B
.
The number of edges emanating from
B_{j}
, say

, can be
estimated as

\Delta\cdot|B|=\Delta\cdot(|B_{j}|-z_{j})\leq e_{j}\leq(\Delta-1)(n_{j}-|B_{j}|)=(\Delta-1)(n_{j}-|B|-z_{j})\,.

This gives

and

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G_{j})\leq|B|+z_{j}\leq\frac{\Delta-1}{2\Delta-1}\,(n_{j}-o(n_{j}))+o(n_{j})

Hence

\Box

2.1
Forbidden pair lemma

Here we state a property which is simple but makes many situations concerning odd independent sets more transparent.
We introduce two notions.

Definition 7
.

(Forbidden pair)

Two nonadjacent vertices
x,y
form a
forbidden pair
if they have a common neighbor
z
such that
N[z]\subseteq N[x]\cup N[y]
.

Definition 8
.

(Forcing pair)

Two nonadjacent neighbors
x,y
of a vertex
z
form a
forcing pair
if, for any vertex
w
in
N(z)
independent of
x
and
y
, either
x,w
or
y,w
or both are forbidden pairs.

Lemma 9
.

If
S\subseteq V(G)
is an odd independent set, then it contains no forbidden pair, nor a forcing pair.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
x,y\in S
, where
x,y
is a forbidden pair, say in regard to a common neighbor
z
, and
S\subseteq V(G)
is odd independent.
By definition,
S
should contain at least one further neighbor, say
w
, of
z
.
However, every neighbor of
z
belongs to
N[x]\cup N[y]
, hence at least one of
wx\in E(G)
and
wy\in E(G)
holds, which is not allowed if
S
is an independent set.

Assume next that
x,y\in S
form a forcing pair, say in regard to a common neighbor
z
.
Due to odd independence,
S
has to contain at least one further neighbor, say
w
, of
z
.
But then the forbidden pair
x,w
or
y,w
is contained in
S
, and we have just proved that this is not possible.

\Box

Theorem 10
.

If
G
is claw-free, then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G^{2})
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\chi(G^{2})
.

Proof.

We prove that a set

is odd independent in

is an independent set in
G^{2}
.
This will verify both equalities.

The “if” part is clear.
For “only if”, consider any independent set

G
, and
suppose that
S
is not independent in
G^{2}
.
Say,

Since

dominates all neighbors of

and

as
singletons, with the further odd independent sets

for

.

\Box

K_{n}\,\Box\,K_{n}
— Cartesian product of complete graphs.

This graph is opposed to

and moderate

Proposition 20
.

For

,

If

4
Automorphisms and recursive lower bound

In this section we present a recursive method to obtain lower bounds
on
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}
for graphs satisfying a certain symmetry property.
An application of this approach to cube graphs will be given.

For this purpose we define a binary operation
\mu
acting on pairs of graphs.
Let
G
be a vertex-labeled graph and
H
any graph.
Define
\mu[G,H]
as the graph obtained as follows.
Replace each vertex of
w\in V(H)
with a copy
G_{w}=(V_{w},E_{w})
of
G
,
and between those copies which are adjacent in
H
,
take a perfect matching that connects vertices of the same label.

Theorem 21
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs with the following properties:

•

G
admits an automorphism
\eta
such that
\eta(v)\in N(v)

for every
v\in V(G)
, and the length of each orbit of
\eta
is even;

•

H
is bipartite and each
w\in V(H)
has an even degree.

Then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\mu[G,H])\geq|H|\cdot\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
.

Proof.

If
S
is an odd independent set in
G
, then by automorphism so is
S^{*}:=\eta(S)
as well.
Let
A,B
be the two vertex classes of
H
, and consider an
S
with

|S|=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
.
If
w\in A
, let
S_{w}
be the copy of
S
in
G_{w}
; and if

w\in B
, let
S_{w}
be the copy of
S^{*}
in
G_{w}
.
We select
S^{+}:=\cup_{w\in V(H)}S_{w}
.
Clearly,
|S^{+}|=|H|\cdot\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
.
The proof will be done if we prove that
S^{+}
is an odd independent set.

Trying to locate a hypothetical edge
u_{1}u_{2}
inside
S^{+}
, there should
exist
w_{1},w_{2}
such that
u_{i}\in S_{w_{i}}
(
i=1,2
,
w_{1}\neq w_{2}
) and

w_{1}w_{2}\in E(H)
, because each
S_{w}
is independent and there is no

G_{w_{1}}
–
G_{w_{2}}
edge for
w_{1}w_{2}\notin E(H)
.
But then
u_{1}u_{2}
should connect two copies of a
u\in V(G)
, which
cannot be the case because one of them is in
A
while the other is
in
B
, hence
u_{1}\mapsfrom u
and
u_{2}\mapsfrom\mu(u)\in N(u)

are nonadjacent in
\mu[G,H]
.

It remains to show that every vertex of
V(\mu[G,H])\,\diagdown\,S^{+}

adjacent to
S^{+}
has an odd number of neighbors in
S^{+}
.
By symmetry reason, it suffices to consider vertices in the copy
G_{w_{1}}

of
G
.
Assume that
v_{1}\in(G_{w_{1}})\,\diagdown\,S_{w_{1}}
is the copy of
v\in V(G)\,\diagdown\,S
.

If
v
has no neighbor in
S
, then referring to the inverse automorphism
\mu^{-1}
we see that
\mu(v)
has no neighbor in
S^{*}
, thus
v_{1}
is not adjacent to
S^{+}
either.
The other possibility is that
v
has an odd number of neighbors in
S
.
This yields that
v_{1}
has an odd number of neighbors in
S_{w_{1}}
.
All the other neighbors
u_{i}\in S^{+}
of
v_{1}
, i.e. those in any
S_{w_{i}}
where
w_{i}\in B
,
are copies of the same vertex
u\in S^{*}
where
u=\mu(v)
holds by the definition of
\mu[G,H]
.
Since all degrees in
H
are even, we obtain
|N(v_{1})\cap S^{+}|\equiv|N(v_{1})\cap S_{w_{1}}|
(mod 2), hence odd.
It follows that
S^{+}
is an odd independent set.

\Box

In a similar way we can also obtain:

Theorem 22
.

For any graph
G
, if
H
is a bipartite graph with all vertex degrees
odd, then

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\mu[G,H])\geq\frac{1}{2}|H|\cdot\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G\,\Box\,K_{2})
.

Proof.

We apply notation and several ideas from the previous proof.
Let
A
and
B
be the two vertex classes of
H
, and
S\cup S^{*}

an odd independent set of maximum size
|S|+|S^{*}|=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G\,\Box\,K_{2})

in
G\,\Box\,K_{2}
, where
S
is the subset in one copy of
G
and
S^{*}

is the subset in the other copy.
We may assume
|A|\geq|B|
and
|S|\geq|S^{*}|
without loss of generality.

As in the previous proof, inside the copies
G_{w}\subset\mu[G,H]
we
select the copies of
S
or
S^{*}
if
w\in A
or
w\in B
, respectively.
The argument in the previous proof clearly yields that the obtained set
S^{+}

is independent.
We need to prove that it is also odd independent.
Let
w_{1}w_{2}\in E(H)
be any edge,
w_{1}\in A
, and let
v\in V(G)\,\diagdown\,S

be any vertex with at least one neighbor in
S\cup S^{*}
.
Consider the copy
v_{1}
of
v
in
G_{w_{1}}
.
The number of its neighbors in
V(G_{w_{1}})\cup V(G_{w_{2}})
is odd, because the subgraph induced by this union
is isomorphic to
G\,\Box\,K_{2}
and
S\cup S^{*}
is odd independent.
The additional
\deg_{H}(w)-1
neighbors of
w_{1}
increase this with

(\deg_{H}(w)-1)\cdot|N(v)\cap S^{*}|
, hence adding it to
|N(v)\cap(S\cup S^{*})|
the sum remains odd, due to the degree assumption on
H
.
The same is true for the copy
v_{2}
of
v
in
G_{w_{2}}
.
Thus,
S^{+}
is an odd independent set.

We now have
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\mu[G,H])\geq|S^{+}|=|A|\cdot|S|+|B|\cdot|S^{*}|=\frac{1}{2}(|A|+|B|)|S|+\frac{1}{2}(|A|-|B|)|S|+|B|\cdot|S^{*}|\geq\frac{1}{2}(|A|+|B|)|S|+\frac{1}{2}(|A|-|B|)|S^{*}|+|B|\cdot|S^{*}|=\frac{1}{2}(|A|+|B|)(|S|+|S^{*}|)=\frac{1}{2}|H|\cdot\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G\,\Box\,K_{2})
.

\Box

Remark 23
.

Although the two lower bounds above have been derived using very similar
approach, the estimates are independent in the qualitative sense, because

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G\,\Box\,K_{2})
is not expressible as a function of
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
.
This fact is demonstrated by the following simple examples:

•

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(K_{2})=1
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(K_{2}\,\Box\,K_{2})=1
.
(
K_{2}\,\Box\,K_{2}\cong C_{4}
.)

•

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(C_{4})=1
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(C_{4}\,\Box\,K_{2})=4
.
(
C_{4}\,\Box\,K_{2}\cong Q_{3}
is 3-regular bipartite.)

We can offer a further approach based on the following notion.
Due to its generality there may occur interesting applications of it, although we have not found any so far.

Definition 24
.

Let
G
be a graph and
B
an odd independent set in
G
.
We say that
B
is odd-suitable if there is a partition
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}

such that the sets
B_{1}:=B\cap V_{1}
,
B_{2}:=B\cap V_{2}
and

B_{1}^{*}:=N(B_{1})\cap V_{2}
,
B_{2}^{*}:=N(B_{2})\cap V_{1}
satisfy the
following conditions for
i=1,2
:

•

N(B_{i}^{*})\cap V_{i}=B_{i}
,

•

the
B_{i}
–
B_{i}^{*}
edges induce an odd bipartite graph.

In the above theorems concerning
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\mu[G,H])
the
B_{i}
–
B_{i}^{*}

subgraphs are just matchings.
In this way the notion of odd-suitable sets opens the road to further
control on
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}
in certain graph classes built recursively.
Currently we do not have applications of this kind, therefore we leave it
for future research to develop the theory in this direction.

6
Complements of triangle-free graphs

Here we investigate graphs
G
whose complement
\overline{G}
is triangle-free.
We will analyze the possible combinations of the diameter of such a graph and its complement.

Let us begin with the following two complexity observations.

Proposition 28
.

If
\overline{G}
is triangle-free, then

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)
can be determined in polynomial time.

Proof.

In any proper coloring (not only in strong odd, but also in non-restricted) of
G
,
every color class is either a singleton or a vertex-pair, because

\alpha(G)=2
.
Hence we can detect all odd-independent vertex pairs by brute force,
their number is
O(n^{2})
.
These pairs will be the edges of an auxiliary graph
H
.
This
H
has matching number
m
if and only if
G
has

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=n-m
, where
2m
vertices are
covered by the
m
matching edges and the
remaining
n-2m
vertices form singleton color classes.
So, we can construct
H
in polynomial time and then determine
its matching number efficiently, applying some of the well-known algorithms.

\Box

Proposition 29
.

In the family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of all graphs with
\alpha(G)\leq k
, determining
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)
and
\alpha(G^{2})
can be done in polynomial time for every fixed
k
.

Proof.

This can be done by checking all
j
-vertex subsets,
j=1,\dots,k
, (a) for independence, (b) for odd independence, and (c) for independence in
G^{2}
.

\Box

The family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the family of the complements of triangle-free graphs, as a graph
G
has
\alpha(G)\leq 2
if and only if its complement
\overline{G}
is triangle-free.
So, in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
we expect that some structural results (beyond the immediate complexity result) are also possible.

We now turn to the analysis of
\mathrm{diam}(G)
and
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})
, and their relation to odd independence.
Under the triangle-free assumption on
G
we are going to prove:

•

If
\mathrm{diam}(G)\geq 4
, then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha((\overline{G})^{2})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=|G|
.

•

If
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
and
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
, then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha((\overline{G})^{2})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(\overline{G})=|G|
.

•

If
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
and
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=3
, then
\alpha(\overline{G})=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha((\overline{G})^{2})=2
.

•

If
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
and
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
, then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha((\overline{G})^{2})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(\overline{G})=|G|
.

(1)

\mathrm{diam}(G)\geq 4
,
G
triangle-free

In this case it is well known that
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})\leq 2
;
see, e.g.,
[
2
]
.
However, then in
\overline{G}
, no odd independent set can have two vertices because the diameter is at most 2 and, as the independence number is at most 2, we conclude that
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha((\overline{G})^{2})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(\overline{G})=|G|
.

Hence this graph class provides another example for
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=1
or, equivalently, for
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=|G|
.

A large class of further examples will be obtained below at the end of part (3).

(2)

\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
,
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
,
G
triangle-free

Then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha(\overline{G}^{2})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(\overline{G})=|G|
.

(3)

\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
,
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=3
,
G
triangle-free

For such graphs, two vertices realizing the diameter of
\overline{G}
form both a maximum independent set and also an odd independent set.
Further,
\alpha(\overline{G})=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=\alpha(\overline{G}^{2})=2
.

Furthermore, if
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
, then we know
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})\leq 3
.
Below we give constructions for both
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=3
and
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
.

Construction of triangle-free graphs with diameter 3 whose complements have diameter 3.

Such graphs exists, the smallest one is the self-complementary

with

, cf. Problem

(i)
.
Also the graph

.

(

.

Construction of triangle-free graphs with diameter 3 whose complements have diameter 2.

In this case
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(\overline{G})=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(\overline{G})=|G|

).

Let
G

\mathrm{diam}(G)=2

, and

We construct

We claim that the complement

In this graph the two copies of

are

So, inside each
\overline{G}
, any two vertices have a common neighbor

\overline{G}
, because

.

Consider two vertices

in distinct copies of
\overline{G}

If they are copies of distinct vertices of
G
, then they are directly adjacent in
\overline{H}
.
Otherwise, if they are copies of the same vertex say
w
,
we know that
\deg_{G}(w)\leq|G|-2
, hence
u
has a neighbor
z
in its

\overline{G}
and then
uzv
is an
u
–
v
path of length 2.
Thus,
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{H})=2
holds, indeed.

Observe that this method can be generalized to constructions of triangle-free graphs of any diameter
t\geq 3
whose complements have diameter 2.
Just start with a triangle-free graph
G
having
\mathrm{diam}(G)=t
and take
H=G\,\Box\,K_{2}
.
The above argument verifies
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{H})=2
, hence covering case (1) in a general way.

(4)

\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
,
G
triangle-free

The smallest such graph is
C_{5}
.
We will prove that under the assumptions of (4) either
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
or
\overline{G}
is disconnected.
So, in the following we exclude finite diameter greater than 2.

(4.a)

\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})\geq 4

If
\overline{G}
is connected and has diameter at least 4, then there are vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}
forming an induced path.
But then
\{v_{1},v_{3},v_{5}\}
is an independent set in
\overline{G}
, contradicting the assumption that
G
is triangle-free.

(4.b)

\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=3

Suppose that the induced path
xuvy
realizes diameter 3
in
\overline{G}
.
If
x
has another neighbor
z
, then
z
must be adjacent to
u

as otherwise
\{z,u,y\}
is an independent set of
cardinality 3 in
\overline{G}
, which is forbidden.
Similarly, if
z
is a neighbor of
y
, then it is also
adjacent to
v
.
For the same reason both
N[x]
and
N[y]
induce complete subgraphs in
\overline{G}
.
What is more, the union of these two cliques is the entire vertex set,
for otherwise a vertex
z
not in their union would form a
3-element independent set with
x
and
y
.
Thus,
\overline{G}\supseteq(K_{a}\cup K_{b})+e
where
e
is the edge
uv

and
a+b=|G|
(namely,
a=\deg(x)+1
,
b=\deg(y)+1
).
But then
G\subseteq K_{a,b}-e
holds,
which implies
\mathrm{diam}(G)\geq 3
,
hence contradicting the basic assumption of (4).

Construction of triangle-free graphs with diameter 2 whose complements have diameter 2.

Start with
K_{n,m}
,
n\geq m\geq 2
, fix a matching of
t
edges,
1\leq t\leq m
, and subdivide these
t
matching-edges into paths of length 2; call this graph
G=G(n,m,t)
.
This is a triangle-free graph of diameter 2.
(Note that
m>1
is mandatory, otherwise the graph would have diameter 3.)

The complement
H=H(n,m,t)
of
G(n,m,t)
is connected,
thanks to
m>1
and to the middle vertex of any subdivided edge.
Hence, by the analysis above, we know without further checking
that
\mathrm{diam}(\overline{G})=2
.

7
Graphs with
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/(\Delta^{2}(G)+1)

For a simpler notation, we will write
\Delta^{2}(G)
for

(\Delta(G))^{2}
throughout.

We have already seen that
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq\chi(G^{2})\leq\Delta(G^{2})+1\leq\Delta^{2}(G)+1
holds for every graph
G
.
So,
\chi(G^{2})=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
is a necessary condition for

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
.
This problem has a rich history; we refer to
[
5
]
for the
information needed here.

It is well known that a connected graph
G
with
\Delta=\Delta(G)>2
has

\chi(G^{2})=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
if and only if
G^{2}=K_{\Delta^{2}(G)+1}
.
As discovered by Hoffman and Singleton
[
8
]
, such graphs exist
if and only if
\Delta\in\{2,3,7\}
and possibly
\Delta=57

(whose existence is a major open problem in graph theory).
We consider several cases.

(1) If
\Delta=1
, then clearly
G=K_{2}
and

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=2=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
.
We immediately infer that for
H=tK_{2}
,

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(H)=t=|H|/\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(H)=|H|/(\Delta^{2}(H)+1)
.

(2) For
\Delta=2
we already proved in
[
3
]
that
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq 5
, with equality if and only if at least one component is
C_{5}
.
Hence
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/5=|G|/(\Delta^{2}(G)+1)
, with equality if and only if
G\cong tC_{5}
.

(3)
\Delta=3
.

It is proved in
[
6
]
that if
\Delta(G)\geq 3
and
G
is
a connected graph other than the Petersen graph, the Hoffman–Singleton
graph, or a graph with
\Delta(G)=57
and
G^{2}=K_{57^{2}+1}
,
then
\chi(G^{2})\leq\Delta^{2}(G)-1
.
However, for
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\Delta^{2}(G)+1
, the Petersen graph is
excluded by Proposition
14
, and the
Hoffman–Singleton graph is excluded by Proposition
15
.
Hence it remains to consider the case
\Delta=57
, if it exists at all.
To exclude this final possibility we need the following lemma.

Lemma 30
.

Denote
\epsilon(\Delta)=0
if
\Delta
is odd and
\epsilon(\Delta)=1
if
\Delta
is even.
If
G
has girth at least
5
and
\Delta(G)=\Delta
, then:

(i)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\Delta-\epsilon(\Delta)
.

(ii)

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq n-(\Delta-\epsilon(\Delta))+1
.

Proof.

Assume that
G
has girth at least
5
.
Let
v
be a vertex with
\deg(v)=\Delta(G)
.
Consider
A=N(v)
, or one fewer vertex if
\Delta
is even.
Note that
A
is independent, since
G
has no 3-cycles.
Vertex
v
is adjacent to the entire
A
, whose size is odd.
Any other vertex can have at most one neighbor in
A
, because
G
has
no 4-cycles.
Hence
A
is an odd independent set of size

\Delta-\epsilon(\Delta)
, proving
(i)
.

Coloring all vertices of
A
with the same color and all other
vertices with distinct colors, we obtain
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}\leq n-(\Delta-\epsilon(\Delta))+1
, as stated in
(ii)
.

\Box

A Moore graph
G
with
\Delta=57
, if it exists, is known to have girth 5.
Hence, by the above lemma,
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq\Delta^{2}-\Delta+2
, and so it is also excluded.
We conclude:

Theorem 31
.

If
\Delta(G)\geq 3
, then
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/((\Delta(G))^{2}-1)
.

Remark 32
.

There exist some graphs with
\chi(G^{2})=\Delta^{2}(G)-1
.
For
\Delta=3
this is the critical graph for the Ramsey number
R(4,3)
on 8 vertices, for which one can check that

\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=8
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=1
.
Also a further graph with these parameters exists, see Figure
1
.
Hence the bound in the above theorem is sharp for
\Delta=3
.
There exist also such graphs with
\Delta=4
and
\Delta=5
, mentioned in
[
5
]
; but for
\Delta\geq 6
no example of such graphs is known so far.

Concluding remarks and open problems

In this section we offer a list of open problems for future research.

We know
\alpha(G)\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq\alpha(G^{2})
. Trivially for a connected graph
G
,
\alpha(G)=\alpha(G^{2})
if and only if
G=K_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.

Problem 33
.

(i)

Characterize the graphs
G
for which
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G)
holds.

(ii)

Characterize the graphs for which
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G^{2})
holds.

The first part has been done for families like the Kneser graphs, the Cartesian products

K_{p}\,\Box\,K_{q}
, and trivially for odd regular bipartite graphs.
The next assertion implies, however, that there does not exist an
“induced subgraph characterization” for
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G)
.

Proposition 34
.

For every graph
G
there exists a graph
H
such that
|H|=|G|+1
,

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(H)=\alpha(H)=\alpha(G)+1
, and
G
is an induced
subgraph of
H
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a largest independent set in
G
.
Construct
H
by taking a new vertex
w
and joining it to all those
vertices of
V(G)\,\diagdown\,B
which have an even number of neighbors in
B
.
(In particular, if
B
is odd independent in
G
, then
H=G\cup K_{1}
.)
By construction it follows that
B^{+}:=B\cup\{w\}
is an odd independent set, because if

v
has an odd number of neighbors in
B
, then its degree
remains unchanged, and otherwise it is increased by 1 due to the edge
vw
.
Hence

|B^{+}|\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(H)\leq\alpha(H)\leq\alpha(G)+1=|B|+1=|B^{+}|
.
Thus, equality holds throughout.

\Box

Note that the above construction of
H
for a given
G
needs to know a
largest independent set in
G
explicitly, which is algorithmically
hard to find in general.

Problem 35
.

Characterize graphs
G
with
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=|G|
, or equivalently
with
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=1
; cf. Proposition
1
.4.1 (lower bound)
and
1
.4.2 (upper bound).

Clearly, a necessary condition is that
G
has diameter at most 2.
One can also make the following further related observations:

(a)

If
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=|G|
, then
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G+K_{r})=|G|+r
.

(b)

If
G
is claw-free, we know
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=\chi(G^{2})
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=\alpha(G^{2})
.
Moreover, for any graph
G
obviously
\chi(G^{2})=|G|
holds
if and only if
G
has diameter at most
2
.
Hence, for claw-free graphs
G
,
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=1
and
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=|G|
if and only if
G
has diameter at most 2.

Problem 36
.

Let
r
be odd.
Characterize the connected triangle-free
r
-regular graphs for which

(i)

N(v)
is a maximum odd independent set for every vertex
v
.

(ii)

N(v)
is a maximum odd independent set for some vertex
v
.

(iii)

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=r
.

There are two known examples of such graphs, the Petersen graph
and
K_{t,t}
where
t
is odd.
Are there more?
We note that the two mentioned examples belong to the class of strongly
regular graphs srg
(n,r,\lambda=0,\mu=
odd).
The other five known members of this class, however, do not satisfy
the requirements of Problem
36
.
(It is a famous open problem whether srg
(n,r,\lambda=0,\mu)

contains further members beyond the seven known ones: namely, the
pentagon, Petersen, Clebsch, Hoffman–Singleton, Gewirtz, Mesner-M22,
and Higman–Sims.)

Observe that a necessary condition for
(i)
and
(ii)
is to have
diameter at most 3.
Concerning
(iii)
with
r\geq 3
(not necessarily odd), since there is
no request on any
N(v)
, we have
r=\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/(r^{2}-1)
,
hence
|G|\leq r(r^{2}-1)
.

Problem 37
.

Determine the computational complexity of decision problems associated with
Problems
33
,
35
, and
36
.

Problem 38
.

Let
G
be any outerplanar graph.
It is known
[
7
]
that
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)\leq 8
, moreover
\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(G)=7
holds
for
G=P_{6}+K_{1}
.
Is it true that
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq n/7
for any outerplanar
G
?

The lower bound
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq n/7
is valid at least for outerplanar graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)\leq 6
, because then also
\chi(G^{2})\leq 7
holds, as proved in
[
1
, Corollary 3.13]
.

Problem 39
.

In view of the lower bounds

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/(\Delta^{2}(G)+1)
and

\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)\geq|G|/(\Delta^{2}(G)-1)
for
\Delta\geq 3
, it is of
interest to consider
c(\Delta):=\min\{c:
there exists a graph
such that
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(G)=c|G|/(\Delta^{2}(G)+1)\}
.

Here
c(1)=c(2)=1
, but
c(3)=5/4
realized by two graphs of
order 8; one of them is the critical graph for
R(3,4)
.
The graphs
K_{p}\,\Box\,K_{p}
and
K_{p}\,\Box\,K_{p+1}
show that we cannot
expect more than
c(\Delta)=(4+o(1))\cdot\Delta
.

To finish, for hypercubes the following problem remains open.

Problem 40
.

Determine
\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(Q_{d})
when
d
is even, or improve upon the results in Corollary
26
and Proposition
27
.

In particular, is it true that if
d
is even, then

\lim_{d\to\infty}\alpha_{\mathrm{od}}(Q_{d})/2^{d}=1/2
?

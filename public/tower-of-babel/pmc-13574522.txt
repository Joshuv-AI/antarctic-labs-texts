Clinical benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in resected T2N0 distal cholangiocarcinoma: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yoon Soo Chae, Youngmin Han, Seulah Park, Go-Won Choi, Younsoo Seo, Inhyuck Lee, Hye-Sol Jung, Young Jae Cho, Wooil Kwon, Jin-Young Jang, Joon Seong Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeSeveral studies have reported that adjuvant chemotherapy (AC) improves survival outcomes in patients with distal cholangiocarcinoma (dCCA). However, definitive evidence supporting the efficacy of AC in dCCA patients with early T-stage and node-negative disease remains lacking. This study aimed to investigate the clinical benefit of AC and prognostic factors in patients with the early stage of dCCA, with a focus on resected T2N0 dCCA.MethodsA total of 123 patients who underwent curative surgery with pathological confirmation of T2N0 dCCA between 2000 and 2022 were reviewed using prospectively collected clinicopathologic data.ResultsNo significant between-group differences were observed in 5-year overall survival (OS) (64.8% vs. 68.5%, P = 0.247) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) (54.6% vs. 59.4%, P = 0.317). Furthermore, no significant differences were observed in locoregional RFS (71.7% vs. 75.5%, P = 0.269) and distant metastasis-free survival (71.7% vs. 76.5%, P = 0.414). Multivariate analysis revealed that the effect of AC was not meaningful and only R1 resection was an independent negative prognostic factor for both OS and RFS. In subgroup analysis, neither AC nor adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with survival outcomes in the R1 resection group.ConclusionRoutine use of AC did not significantly improve postoperative survival outcomes in patients with completely resected T2N0 dCCA, nor was its clinical benefit remarkably even in the high-risk group (positive resection margins). Therefore, achieving an R0 resection appears to be more important for improving postoperative survival outcomes in T2N0 dCCA.

INTRODUCTION

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is a complex, aggressive cancer of the biliary tract epithelium with different subtypes based on anatomical location [12]. These subtypes exhibit distinct differences in terms of tumor biology and prognosis [3456], and the only curative treatment for CCA, regardless of subtype, is surgical resection. Distal CCA (dCCA), a tumor located below the confluence of the cystic duct, is one of the most frequently occurring subtypes, but has a higher proportion of potentially resectable diseases, increasing the likelihood of receiving curative therapy [17]. However, the prognosis for patients with dCCA remains unsatisfactory due to the high recurrence rate, leading to poor overall 5-year survival rates [78910]. Therefore, the importance of postoperative adjuvant treatment is an increasingly important area of research.

The BILCAP trial demonstrated the prognostic benefits of adjuvant capecitabine for patients with CCA [11], leading to the adoption of adjuvant chemotherapy (AC) with oral fluoropyrimidines for a period of 6 months following curative resection of CCA as standard treatment [1213]. However, this trial did not show a significant survival benefit of AC in subgroup analyses based on tumor location and stage. Furthermore, several retrospective studies reported that AC improved survival outcomes in patients with dCCA and showed maximal benefits in cases with advanced T stage (≥T3), node-positive disease, and positive resection margins [1415161718]. Since advanced T stage and node-positive disease achieve the majority of the benefit of AC for dCCA, exploring the benefit of AC and risk factors that may influence the requirement for AC in early T stage and node-negative disease is necessary. Furthermore, dCCA is usually combined with other subtypes of CCA in the study, leading to ambiguity in the interpretation of results.

In many cases, patients with lower recurrence rates are excluded from the AC, as it does not prevent all recurrences and carries a significant risk of adverse events. Patients with resected T2N0 dCCA represent a subset with controversy regarding the effectiveness of AC. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the clinical benefit of AC in resected T2N0 dCCA and explore factors that influence the outcomes.

METHODS

Ethics statement

The authors are accountable for all aspects of the work in ensuring that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2024) and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital (No. H-2412-056-1594). As this study was conducted solely for academic purposes and involved only the retrospective review of patients’ medical records, it posed no more than minimal risk to the participants. The requirement for informed consent was therefore waived because obtaining consent from all patients was considered impracticable.

Patient selection

This retrospective analysis utilized prospectively collected clinicopathological data from electronic medical records. Data from 777 patients who underwent surgery with pathological confirmation of dCCA at Seoul National University Hospital (Seoul, South Korea) between January 2000 and December 2022 were enrolled in this study.

Fourteen patients who underwent neoadjuvant treatment and 66 who underwent palliative surgery were excluded from the analysis. Twenty-seven patients with non-adenocarcinoma, who died within 90 days of surgery (n = 8), and 39 without available information regarding adjuvant treatment or who were lost to follow-up were also excluded. Among the 623 patients, data from 123 with pathological confirmation of T2N0 dCCA were ultimately included.

Surgical extent and perioperative evaluation

The type of resection was dependent on the location and extent of the tumor. Most patients (115/123, 93.5%) underwent pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD) with or without pylorus preservation, while 8 patients (6.5%) underwent hilar resection where R0 resection was feasible [19]. The goal of curative surgery was negative microscopic margins on frozen sections and, if this could not be achieved, an additional resection was attempted.

All specimens were examined pathologically after surgery, and those with tumors invading below the confluence of the cystic duct were classified as dCCA. Margin status was classified as R0 or R1, with R1 defined as the presence of microscopic residual tumor, including high-grade dysplasia, when the distance between the tumor and any surgical margins was 0 mm. The pathological T stage was unified according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 7th edition due to differences in the cancer staging system across different eras.

During the postoperative follow-up, serial CT was performed every 3 months for 2 years and, thereafter, every 4–6 months until 5 years after surgery to evaluate recurrence. Locoregional recurrence was defined as the radiological evidence of recurrence in the tumor bed or regional lymph nodes. Distant metastasis was defined as any recurrence other than locoregional recurrence. Overall survival (OS), recurrence-free survival (RFS), locoregional RFS (LRRFS), and distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS) were defined as the time interval from the date of surgery to the date of death from any cause or last visit, and to the date of recurrence or last visit, respectively.

Adjuvant treatment

Adjuvant treatment was recommended for all patients with T2 or higher tumors, node-positive disease, or positive resection margins. Whether a patient underwent adjuvant treatment depended on performance status or recovery after surgery, disease progression, or the patient’s will. All patients underwent adjuvant treatment at a median of 6 weeks after surgery (range, 2.8–9.6 weeks) and patients usually undergo AC for 6–8 months.

At our center, concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) was primarily performed, followed by AC consisting of monthly intravenous 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) or gemcitabine-based regimens for 6 months. Adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) targeted both the primary tumor bed and regional lymph nodes, with concurrent intravenous 5-FU administered at a dose of 500 mg/m2 on days 1–3 and 28–30, along with 4,000–5,400 cGy of external beam radiation. More recently, various regimens have been explored based on studies such as the BILCAP trial and other randomized controlled trials (RCTs). However, during the study period, AC regimens typically consisted of 5-FU- or gemcitabine-based chemotherapy as standard components.

In total, 58 patients (47.2%) received adjuvant treatment. Six patients received AC alone, 12 underwent adjuvant RT alone, and 40 received both AC and RT. The AC regimens comprised various combinations of 5-FU or gemcitabine. Most patients (44/46, 95.7%) received 5-FU–based chemotherapy, 40 received monthly 5-FU (500 mg/m2 per day for 5 days) with or without leucovorin (20 mg/m2 per day for 5 days), and 4 received single oral 5-FU derivatives, such as uracil-tegafur, capecitabine, and tegafur/gimeracil/oteracil. Two patients (4.3%) underwent gemcitabine-based AC. Out of 52 patients, all except 1 (98.1%) received CCRT, using either fluoropyrimidine-based (intravenous 5-FU at a dose of 500 mg/m2 or oral capecitabine) or gemcitabine-based chemotherapy on day 1, 2, 3, and on day 28, 29, 30, along with 4,000–5,400 cGy of external beam radiation targeting both the primary tumor bed and regional lymph nodes. One patient (1.9%) received radiation alone because of poor performance.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test and Fisher exact test, and continuous variables were analyzed using the Student t-test. OS, RFS, LRRFS, and DMFS curves were constructed using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis was used to identify the independent prognostic factors for OS and RFS. Variables with P ≤ 0.15 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

The mean (± standard deviation) age of the cohort was 68.0 ± 8.8 years, and 91 patients (74.0%) were male. Most patients underwent PD (115/123, 93.5%) and only 8 patients (6.5%) underwent hilar resection, respectively. The R0 rate was 81.3% (100/123), and 54 patients (43.9%) experienced recurrence during the follow-up period (Table 1).

The clinical characteristics of patients according to AC are summarized in Table 1. Patients who underwent AC tended to be younger (70.8 ± 8.7 years vs. 63.4 ± 7.0 years, P < 0.001), had a higher proportion of male patients (51/77 [66.2%] vs. 40/46 [87.0%], P = 0.011), and a higher rate of RT (12/77 [15.6%] vs. 40/46 [87.0%], P < 0.001), compared to the observation group. No demographic differences were observed in differentiation, R0 ratio, major morbidity ratio, and recurrence rate or pattern between the 2 groups.

Long-term survival outcomes according to adjuvant chemotherapy

The median follow-up period was 61.6 months (range, 2.2–231.7 months), and the 5-year OS and RFS rates in the entire cohort were 66.4% (median, 83.8 months) and 56.9% (median, 75.0 months), respectively (Supplementary Fig. 1). Forty-six patients (37.1%) underwent AC, with 5-FU–based AC administered to 44 (95.7%) and gemcitabine-based AC to 2 patients (4.3%). A total of 52 patients (42.3%) received adjuvant RT, of whom 40 (76.9%) received both AC and RT.

No significant between-group differences were observed in 5-year OS (64.8% vs. 68.5%; median, 79.3 months vs. 111.8 months; P = 0.247) and RFS (54.6% vs. 59.4%; median, 71.0 months vs. not applicable [NA]; P = 0.317). Furthermore, no significant differences were observed in 5-year LRRFS (71.7% vs. 75.5%; median, 118.4 months vs. NA; P = 0.269) and DMFS (71.7% vs. 76.5%; median, NA vs. NA; P = 0.414) (Fig. 1). In subgroup analysis, the method of adjuvant treatment was not associated with survival outcomes, and no adjuvant treatment method demonstrated superiority in survival outcomes compared to the observation group (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Prognostic factors for survival

The univariate analysis of OS, RFS, LRRFS, and DMFS prognostic factors in patients who underwent curative resection for T2N0 dCCA is summarized in Table 2. Univariate analysis identified R1 resection (hazard ratio [HR], 2.75; 95% CI, 1.61–4.69; P < 0.001) and venous invasion (HR, 2.03; 95% CI, 1.09–3.79; P = 0.026) as risk factors for OS. For RFS, only R1 resection (HR, 2.89; 95% CI, 1.63–5.14; P < 0.001) emerged as a risk factor. No independent risk factors were found for LRRFS. However, elevated preoperative total bilirubin (≥3 mg/dL) (HR, 2.09; 95% CI, 1.01–4.31; P = 0.048) and perineural invasion (HR, 4.60; 95% CI, 1.09–19.34; P = 0.038) were associated with DMFS.

The multivariate analysis of the prognostic factors shown in Table 3identified only R1 resection as an independent negative prognostic factor for OS (HR, 3.06; 95% CI, 1.77–5.29; P < 0.001) and RFS (HR, 3.07; 95% CI, 1.61–5.84; P < 0.001). No independent risk factors were found for LRRFS in the multivariate analysis, and only perineural invasion (HR, 8.31; 95% CI, 1.10–62.72; P = 0.040) affected DMFS.

Long-term survival outcomes according to resection margin status and adjuvant chemotherapy

Subgroup analysis was performed to compare the survival outcomes of patients based on resection margin status, which is considered a high-risk factor for OS and RFS, and the administration of AC. Among 123 patients, 23 had an R1 resection. The R0 resection group showed better 5-year OS and RFS than the R1 resection group, regardless of AC (P < 0.001). Furthermore, AC provided no significant benefit for 5-year OS and RFS in the R1 resection group (Fig. 2). Similarly, no significant differences were observed in 5-year OS and RFS according to RT in the R1 resection group (Supplementary Fig. 3).

DISCUSSION

Although CCA is rare in most countries, its incidence and mortality are exceptionally high in Asia, with South Korea having the highest incidence of CCA worldwide. Among CCA, extrahepatic CCA (eCCA) is the most frequently occurring type of CCA in South Korea, and dCCA generally has improved outcomes [167]. Currently, most hospitals provide AC after curative resection for dCCA patients, excluding those with T1N0 disease. The ASCOT trial, which excluded early-stage cancers such as pT1N0 stage dCCA (according to AJCC 7th edition), successfully demonstrated the benefits of AC for CCA [20]. Furthermore, definitive evidence supporting the efficacy of AC in dCCA patients with early T stage and node-negative disease remains lacking. Therefore, risk factors associated with poor prognosis are expected to be the most important factors in determining AC for early T stage and node-negative dCCA. This study investigated the efficacy of AC and prognostic factors in patients with the early stage of dCCA, with a focus on resected T2N0 dCCA.

We found no significant difference in 5-year OS, RFS, LRRFS, and DMFS between the AC (+) and AC (–) groups. As the adjuvant strategy (AC alone, RT alone, or both AC and RT) could affect survival outcomes, we performed a subgroup analysis to compare postoperative survival outcomes according to the adjuvant treatment method. A retrospective multicenter study in Korea reported that adjuvant RT combined with chemotherapy particularly improved the survival outcomes of patients with resected eCCA [21]; however, in this study, no significant differences were observed in survival outcomes based on the adjuvant treatment method, and regardless of the type of adjuvant treatment received, survival outcomes were comparable to those of the AC (–) group. These findings showed that adjuvant treatment provides no significant benefit in patients with T2N0 dCCA.

In both univariate and multivariate analyses, the effect of AC was not meaningful. This study included most cases on 5-FU–based AC (44/46, 95.7%), which is the standard AC regimen, with only 2 patients (4.3%) receiving gemcitabine-based AC. Therefore, these results support that standard AC does not provide survival benefits in patients with T2N0 dCCA. Furthermore, the effect of adjuvant RT was also not significant in the univariate and multivariate analyses.

Only R1 resection, which has been considered an adverse prognostic factor [222324], was an independent negative prognostic factor for OS and RFS. Although not statistically significant, R1 resection is considered a high-risk factor for LRRFS (HR, 2.14; 95% CI, 1.00–4.60; P = 0.051), whereas DMFS is regarded as not being strongly associated with R1 resection. Regardless of AC administration, the R0 resection group showed better survival outcomes compared to the R1 resection group (P < 0.001). Although AC was expected to improve survival outcomes in the high-risk R1 resection group, this study failed to show a benefit of AC in the R1 resection group. While the effectiveness of AC could not be demonstrated in the R1 resection group, the missing statistically significant difference was probably attributed to the small number of patients in this group. Among the entire cohort of 123 patients, 23 were in the R1 resection group, and only 12 of these underwent AC. Similarly, adjuvant RT provided no significant benefit for survival outcomes, which is also thought to be due to the small number of patients in the R1 resection cohort. Further efforts will be needed to adjuvant treatment, which could improve survival outcomes for early-stage dCCA patients with high-risk factors.

The present study had several limitations, the first of which relates to its retrospective, single-center study design, meaning selection bias could not be avoided. Second, the patients received various combinations of AC and RT, with differences in dosage and the number of cycles administered, making it difficult to interpret the impact of AC on survival outcomes. As such, inherent limitations, including selection bias and institution-specific treatment protocols, cannot be completely ruled out. However, at our institution, postoperative chemoradiotherapy was generally recommended to patients, and the final decision was made by the patients themselves. This approach ensures a relatively consistent treatment principle, contributing to the uniformity of the study results. Third, comorbidities and perioperative performance status were not controlled in the patients included in this study. Multivariate analysis showed that neither the American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification nor major morbidity was an independent negative prognostic factor for survival outcomes. Therefore, although comorbidities and postoperative performance status may influence the administration of AC, they do not appear to be directly associated with survival outcomes. Fourth, the pathological T stage was unified according to the AJCC 7th edition due to differences in the AJCC staging system across eras. Since staging systems changed multiple times during the study period and a complete unified review of all pathological reports was challenging, we standardized the T staging according to the AJCC 7th edition to ensure clarity of the results. T staging for dCCA has been revised from a layer-based approach in the 7th edition to a depth-based approach in the 8th edition. Many tumors staged as T3 due to pancreatic invasion according to the AJCC 7th edition were reclassified as T1 or T2 under the 8th edition, which showed worse survival outcomes in early T-stage dCCA and seemed to require AC [25262728]. Despite ongoing controversy regarding the T staging of dCCA, these study results may be challenging to apply within the current AJCC 8th system. However, the AJCC 8th edition does not provide better prognostic predictability than the 7th edition [25], and several studies have suggested that the current pathological T classification may not accurately stratify postoperative survival outcomes in dCCA patients, indicating a need for new criteria [262728], which justifies this grouping. Finally, the small number of patients evaluated in this study also has to be considered when interpreting the implications of our results. Nevertheless, given the nature of retrospective studies, potential unmeasured confounders may still exist. Therefore, further validation through a multicenter, prospective study, including RCT, is warranted to confirm the generalizability of our findings.

AC did not significantly improve postoperative survival outcomes in patients with completely resected T2N0 dCCA, nor did it provide a remarkable clinical benefit even in the high-risk group (positive resection margins). Therefore, achieving an R0 resection appears to be more important for improving postoperative survival outcomes in T2N0 dCCA. While the BILCAP and ASCOT trials suggest that AC should be used [1120], this study suggested that applying AC only to T2N0 dCCA patients with high-risk features is worth considering. Specific large studies should be systematically conducted to identify high-risk early-stage dCCA cohorts and evaluate the clinical benefits of AC, particularly in high-risk groups, in consideration of the recent AJCC T staging.

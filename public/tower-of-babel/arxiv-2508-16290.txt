Extreme magnetic field-boosted superconductivity in a high-temperature superconductor
Km Rubi, King Yau Yip, Elizabeth Krenkel, Nurul Fitriyah, Xing Gao, Saurav Prakash, S. Lin Er Chow, Tsz Fung Poon, Mun K. Chan, David Graf, A. Ariando, Neil Harrison

Abstract
Magnetic fields typically suppress superconductivity through Pauli and orbital limiting effects. However, there are rare instances of magnetic-field-induced superconductivity, as seen in Chevrel phase compounds Meul et al. 1984 , organic conductors Uji et al. 2001 , uranium-based heavy-fermion systems Lévy et al. 2005 ; Ran et al. 2019 , and moiré graphene Cao et al. 2021 —though these materials possess inherently low superconducting transition temperatures ( T c T_{\rm c} ​). Here, we demonstrate high field-stabilized superconductivity in a class of materials with a significantly higher T c T_{\rm c} (up to 40 K)​: the infinite-layer nickelates Chow et al. 2025 . Both low-field and high-field superconducting states can be plausibly explained by a compensation mechanism akin to the Jaccarino–Peter effect. These findings demonstrate the possibility of achieving substantially enhanced upper critical fields in high-temperature superconductors.

The mechanisms underlying reentrant superconductivity have been the subject of extensive debate. Proposed explanations include compensation of the applied field by an internal exchange field
Meul et al. 1984
;
Balicas et al. 2001
;
Helm et al. 2024
, electron pairing mediated by spin fluctuations
Aoki et al. 2001
;
Lévy et al. 2005
;
Huy et al. 2007
, field-enhanced spin reorientation or spin-triplet pairing
Lévy et al. 2005
;
Ran et al. 2019
, field-induced magnetic transitions and Fermi-surface reconstructions
Wu et al. 2025
, and Landau-level broadening near the quantum limit
Rasolt and Tes˘anović 1992
.
Given the presence of rare-earth elements Sm and Eu, it is plausible that field-induced reentrant superconductivity in (SECS)NiO
2
arises from a significant exchange interaction between the conduction electrons and localized rare-earth magnetic moments
Jaccarino and Peter 1962
. In magnetic ion-doped superconductors (e.g., Eu-doped chalcogenides
Fischer et al. 1975
;
Meul et al. 1984
and Fe-based organic conductors
Uji et al. 2001
;
Balicas et al. 2001
), the conduction electrons experience an internal exchange field from magnetic ions. If the internal field aligns opposite to the applied magnetic field direction, the two can partially or completely cancel each other, reducing the pair-breaking effect of the external field and allowing superconductivity to reappear at high fields. This magnetic-field compensation mechanism is known as the Jaccarino-Peter effect
Jaccarino and Peter 1962
. In the case of (SECS)NiO
2
, we find that the essential features of the phase diagrams in Fig.
2
are well captured by a modified Werthamer–Helfand–Hohenberg (WHH) model
Werthamer et al. 1966
for the superconducting critical field, adapted to include the effects of an exchange splitting field
H_{J}
(see Methods). Moreover, the results of some experiments on the recently highlighted reentrant superconductor UTe
2
have suggested that the Jaccarino–Peter effect may be responsible for reentrant superconductivity in this material
Helm et al. 2024
, although alternative mechanism—such as spin-triplet pairing enhanced by magnetic fluctuations
Ran et al. 2019
or metamagnetic criticality accompanied by Fermi-surface reconstruction
Wu et al. 2025
—have also been proposed. These effects are less likely to be relevant in (SECS)NiO
2
due to the more localized nature of the
f
-electrons.

2

trivalent
(3

The high magnetic-field phase diagram of a reentrant superconductor is sensitive to the interplay between the Pauli critical field
H_{\rm P}
, the orbital critical field
H_{\rm c2}^{\rm orb}
, and the exchange field (
H_{J}
), as well as other factors such as the degree of spin-orbit scattering
Rossel et al. 1985
. For samples with relatively low transition temperatures (
T_{\rm c}\approx 12~\mathrm{K}
in the present case), there is a clear separation between the low-field superconducting state and the magnetic-field-induced superconducting state. However, as
T_{\rm c}
increases and the superconducting state becomes more robust (see Extended Data Fig 3), these phases tend to merge, as shown in Extended Data Fig 4, and 5. In such cases, the transition from the low-field superconducting state to the high-field-induced superconducting state is indicated by a subtle kink in the phase boundary. Indeed, for the sample with an intermediate transition temperature (
T_{\rm c}\approx 22~\mathrm{K}
), this kink is directly observable in the experimental data (Fig.
3
). For the sample with the highest transition temperature studied here (
T_{\rm c}\approx 32~\mathrm{K}
), the kink is less evident; instead, the phase boundary displays noticeable upward curvature and the superconductivity persists al least up to 65 T. This upward curvature and robust high-field superconductivity provide clear evidence that the exchange field continues to significantly enhance robustness of the high-field superconducting state in samples with higher
T_{\rm c}
.

Given the strong experimental evidence for magnetic-field-induced and enhanced superconductivity in (SECS)NiO
2
, a key question arises: why have similar phenomena not been clearly observed in other high-temperature superconductors, such as the iron-based pnictides or cuprates? One possible reason could be the strong propensity for magnetic ordering in those materials. For instance, the iron-pnictide superconductor EuFe
2
(As
1-x
P
x
)
2
exhibits superconducting transition temperatures
T_{\rm c}
in the range of 20 to 30 K when tuned by chemical substitution or pressure
Zapf et al. 2013
. However, it also displays pronounced antiferromagnetic and spin-glass phases involving the Eu magnetic moments, complicating the coexistence of superconductivity and exchange-driven enhancement. Similarly, the cuprate La
1.8-x
Eu
0.2
Sr
x
CuO
4
(Eu-LSCO), which has
T_{\rm c}\approx 20~\mathrm{K}
, exhibits strong magnetic and charge-ordered stripe phases, with Eu ions playing an active role
Laliberté et al. 2011
. In contrast, infinite-layer nickelates, including (SECS)NiO
2
, have not yet been reported to exhibit long-range magnetic ordering
Fowlie et al. 2022
;
Yan et al. 2025
, making them potentially more favorable hosts for exchange-field-enhanced superconductivity.

Another distinguishing factor between the nickelates and other high-temperature superconductors is the significant contribution of the rare-earth
5d
electrons to the conduction bands, a feature largely absent in the cuprates and iron pnictides
Nomura and Arita 2022
. This involvement of
5d
electrons leads to the formation of additional three-dimensional sections of the Fermi surface, which not only alters the electronic structure—potentially suppressing magnetic order—but also greatly enhances the spatial overlap between conduction electrons and localized rare-earth moments. In (SECS)NiO
2
, this increased overlap may explain the large magnitude of the exchange field (
H_{J}\approx-70.5~\mathrm{T}
) required by the modified WHH model.

High-field transport measurements were performed at both the DC and pulsed-field facilities of the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory. For temperatures below 0.5 K, measurements were carried out in high DC fields using a 45 T hybrid magnet. Additional measurements in DC magnetic fields up to 14 T were performed using a Physical Property Measurement System (PPMS) from Quantum Design Inc. A standard lock-in technique was used for all transport measurements. Home-made rotators were used for in-situ rotation of samples in magnetic fields.

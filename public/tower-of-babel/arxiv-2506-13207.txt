Research on Optimal Control Problem Based on Reinforcement Learning under Knightian Uncertainty Note: This work is supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant Nos. 72301173, 62273003).
LI Ziyu, FEI Chen, FEI Weiyin

Abstract
Considering that the decision-making environment faced by reinforcement learning (RL) agents is full of Knightian uncertainty, this paper describes the exploratory state dynamics equation in Knightian uncertainty to study the entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control problem in a Knightian uncertainty environment. By employing stochastic analysis theory and the dynamic programming principle under nonlinear expectation, we derive the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation and solve for the optimal policy that achieves a trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Subsequently, for the linear-quadratic (LQ) case, we examine the agent’s optimal randomized feedback control under both state-dependent and state-independent reward scenarios, proving that the optimal randomized feedback control follows a Gaussian distribution in the LQ framework. Furthermore, we investigate how the degree of Knightian uncertainty affects the variance of the optimal feedback policy. Additionally, we establish the solvability equivalence between non-exploratory and exploratory LQ problems under Knightian uncertainty and analyze the associated exploration cost. Finally, we provide an LQ example and validate the theoretical findings through numerical simulations.

Introduction

Optimal control has been widely applied in various fields, including engineering, economics, and biomedical sciences. Traditional optimal control methods, such as dynamic programming and Pontryagin’s maximum principle, while possessing a rigorous mathematical foundation in theory, often encounter challenges such as high computational complexity or difficulties in modeling when dealing with high-dimensional state spaces, nonlinear systems, or environmental uncertainties. As a data-driven adaptive optimization method, RL has recently achieved breakthrough progress in domains such as image recognition

RL learns optimal policy through agent-environment interactions without requiring precise system dynamics models, making it particularly suitable for scenarios where models are unknown or difficult to formulate. Compared to traditional model-based control methods, RL can adaptively optimize control policies through trial-and-error mechanism and demonstrates strong learning capabilities in high-dimensional and nonlinear systems

4
]
.

Although research on employing RL techniques to solve optimal control problems has advanced rapidly and found widespread applications, two major limitations persist in current studies. First, the majority of existing works appear to focus primarily on RL algorithms for discrete-time Markov decision processes, while paying considerably less attention to continuous time and space problems. However, numerous real-world scenarios exist where agents can or indeed must interact with stochastic environments at ultra-high frequency or in a completely continuous manner, such as high-frequency stock trading, autonomous vehicle control, and robotic navigation. A significant drawback of addressing these problems in discrete-time settings is that the resulting algorithms exhibit high sensitivity to time discretization
[

Notably, in the LQ case where the state dynamics are linear and the reward is quadratic in both state and action, the optimal policy simplifies to a Gaussian distribution. Jia and Zhou

The theory of RL for continuous-time stochastic control has achieved remarkable progress since the foundational work by Wang et al.

While classical probability theory effectively handles scenarios where models can be precisely determined through statistical methods, it fails when facing unknown probabilistic models. In contrast, nonlinear expectations enable robust quantitative analysis and computation of risks even when the underlying probability model remains undetermined.

The aforementioned studies systematically reveal the impact of Knightian uncertainty on financial-economic decision-making, demonstrating the limitations of the traditional probabilistic framework across domains ranging from asset pricing to contract theory. Notably, these limitations manifest more prominently in RL contexts: RL agents typically operate in complex, dynamically evolving environments replete with unquantifiable uncertainties and unknown factors. A representative example can be found in financial markets, where policy changes and structural transformations may induce significant market volatility. These phenomena fundamentally defy precise probabilistic modeling and constitute quintessential Knightian uncertainty. When RL agents learn and make decisions in such environments, failure to account for Knightian uncertainty factors—particularly the agent’s potential decision-making under worst/best-case scenarios—will inevitably compromise environmental state prediction accuracy and reward estimation, thereby undermining both policy optimization and decision robustness. This challenge extends to numerous real-world applications, including autonomous driving, industrial control, and medical diagnosis, where constantly changing environmental conditions and task requirements prevail. Taking autonomous vehicles as an example, uncertainties in road conditions, traffic flow, and weather patterns, some of which cannot be fully captured through historical data or statistical methods, necessitate explicit consideration of Knightian uncertainty. Incorporating such factors enables agents to better adapt to dynamic scenarios and enhances their robustness and adaptability in complex real-world environments. Consequently, when investigating RL-based optimal control problems, it becomes imperative to appropriately incorporate Knightian uncertainty factors to develop models that more accurately reflect operational realities.

Building upon the aforementioned literature, this paper investigates RL-based optimal control problems under Knightian uncertainty within the sublinear expectation framework. The specific research contributions and innovations are as follows:

(1) Innovatively incorporating Knightian uncertainty factors into the entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control framework proposed by Wang et al.
[
10
]
, we characterize novel exploratory state dynamics equation under Knightian uncertainty using nonlinear law of large numbers, thereby introducing an entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control problem under Knightian uncertainty. By employing stochastic analysis theory and the dynamic programming principle under nonlinear expectation, we derive the HJB equation and obtain the optimal policy that achieves a trade-off between exploration and exploitation.

(2) For the LQ case, we analyze optimal randomized feedback control under both state-dependent and state-independent reward scenarios, proving that the optimal randomized feedback control follows a Gaussian distribution in the LQ framework. We further investigate how Knightian uncertainty degree affects the variance of the optimal feedback policy (which quantifies the agent’s exploration level). Building on these findings, we establish solvability equivalence between non-exploratory and exploratory LQ problems under Knightian uncertainty and quantitatively analyze exploration cost.

(3) We present an LQ case and validate the theoretical results through numerical simulations. This research not only contributes to understanding the decision-making behavior of RL agents in Knightian uncertainty environments, but also provides practical guidance for selecting appropriate exploration weights and Knightian uncertainty volatility levels to achieve optimal exploration-exploitation trade-offs.

In summary, this work demonstrates significant innovation in both methodological approaches and model construction, offering novel perspectives and conceptual frameworks for research on continuous-time RL-based optimal control problems.

The structure of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 formulates the exploratory state dynamics equation under Knightian uncertainty, introduces the corresponding entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control problem, and defines the value function. Section 3 employs stochastic analysis theory and the dynamic programming principle under nonlinear expectation to solve the HJB equation and derive the optimal randomized feedback control. Section 4 examines the LQ case, obtaining optimal randomized feedback controls for both state-independent and state-dependent reward scenarios. Section 5 establishes the solvability equivalence between non-exploratory and exploratory LQ problems under Knightian uncertainty and analyzes exploration cost. Section 6 presents an LQ numerical example to validate the theoretical results. Section 7 concludes the paper with a comprehensive summary.

2.3
Entropy Regularization

We introduce a relaxed stochastic control formulation to model the exploration and exploitation in RL. In fact, if the model were fully known, there would be no need for exploration and learning at all, and the randomized controls would all degenerate to Dirac measures—this would place us strictly within the domain of stochastic control under nonlinear expectation. Therefore, in the RL context, it is necessary to incorporate a “regularization term” to account for model parameter uncertainty and encourage exploration. Here, we use Shannon’s differential entropy to quantify the level of exploration:

\mathcal{H}(\theta):=-\int_{U}\theta(u)\ln\theta(u)du,\quad\theta\in\mathcal{P}(U).

Introduce the following entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control problem in the Knightian uncertainty setting

\begin{split}V(x)&:=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)}\mathcal{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}\bigg(\int_{U}r\left(X_{t}^{\theta},u\right)\theta_{t}(u)du\\
&\quad-\lambda\int_{U}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)du\bigg)dt\mid X_{0}^{\theta}=x\bigg],\end{split}

(9)

where
\lambda>0
is the exogenous exploratory weight parameter capturing the trade-off between exploitation (the original reward function) and exploration (the entropy).
\mathcal{A}(x)
is the set of the admissible randomized controls (usually dependent on

that satisfies at least the following properties:

(i)

For each
t\geq 0
,
\theta_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(U)
q.s.;

(ii)

For each
A\in\mathcal{B}(U)
,
\left\{\int_{A}\theta_{t}(u)du,t\geq 0\right\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable;

(iii)

If
\theta
is applied, then the stochastic differential equation (
6
) will have a unique strong solution
X^{\theta}=\{X_{t}^{\theta},t\geq 0\}
;

(iv)

The lower expectation on the right-hand side of (
9
) is finite.

Finally, similar to the classical control case,
\mathcal{A}(x)
contains open-loop randomized controls which are measure-valued stochastic processes. We will also consider randomized feedback controls. Specifically, a deterministic mapping
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;\cdot)
is called randomized feedback control subject to the following conditions:

(i)

for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;\cdot)
is a density function;

(ii)

the dynamics of the system after applying the randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;\cdot)
as follows has a unique strong solution
\{X_{t},t\geq 0\}

dX_{t}^{\theta}=\tilde{b}(X_{t}^{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;X_{t}))dt+\tilde{\sigma}(X_{t}^{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;X_{t}))dB_{t},\quad t>0;\quad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}.

(iii)

the open-loop randomized control
\theta=\{\theta_{t},t\geq 0\}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, where
\theta_{t}=\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;X_{t})
.

In this case, it can be said that the open-loop randomized control
\theta
is generated from the randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\cdot;\cdot)
with respect to
x
.

3
HJB Equations and Optimal Randomized Control

Below, we outline the general procedure for solving the optimization problem (
9
). While the following arguments are presented informally, a rigorous analysis will be provided in the next section. We have

V(x)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)}\mathcal{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\rho t}\left(\tilde{r}\left(X_{t}^{\theta},\theta_{t}\right)+\lambda\mathcal{H}\left(\theta_{t}\right)\right)dt+e^{-\rho s}V\left(X_{s}^{\theta}\right)\bigg|X_{0}^{\theta}=x\right],

where
s>0
.

By employing
G
-Ito’s formula (Peng
[
21
]
), we derive that the value function
V
satisfies the following HJB equation

\displaystyle\rho v(x)

\displaystyle=\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}(U)}\bigg(\tilde{r}(x,\theta)-\lambda\int_{U}\theta(u)\ln\theta(u)du

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{\sigma}^{2}(x,\theta)\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+\tilde{b}(x,\theta)v^{\prime}(x)\bigg),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

or

\begin{split}\rho v(x)&=\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}(U)}\int_{U}\bigg(r(x,u)-\lambda\ln\theta(u)\\
&\quad+\tilde{\sigma}^{2}(x,u)\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+b(x,u)v^{\prime}(x)\bigg)\theta(u)du,\end{split}

(10)

where
v
denotes the general unknown solution of the equation, and

\widetilde{G}(v^{\prime\prime}(x)):=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)B(1)^{2}]=\frac{1}{2}(\underline{\sigma}^{2}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{+}-\bar{\sigma}^{2}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{-}).

\theta\in\mathcal{P}(U)
, if and only if
\int_{U}\theta(u)du=1,\,\theta(u)\geq 0\text{ a.e. on }U.

We can solve the constrained maximization problem on the right-hand side of (
10
) to obtain the randomized feedback control

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r(x,u)+\sigma^{2}(x,u)\,\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+b(x,u)v^{\prime}(x)\Big)\right)}{\displaystyle\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r(x,u)+\sigma^{2}(x,u)\,\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+b(x,u)v^{\prime}(x)\Big)\right)du}.

(11)

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\left(x,u\right)\underline{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)+b\left(x,u\right)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\left(x,u\right)\underline{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)+b\left(x,u\right)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)du}.

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\left(x,u\right)\bar{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)+b\left(x,u\right)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\left(x,u\right)\bar{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)+b\left(x,u\right)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)du}.

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
, but
v^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+b(x,u)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)}{\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(r(x,u)+b(x,u)v^{\prime}(x)\right)\right)du}.

For each given initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}
, this randomized feedback control in turn generates the optimal open-loop randomized control

\begin{split}\theta^{*}_{t}&:=\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;X_{t}^{*})\\
&=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r(X_{t}^{*},u)+\sigma^{2}(X_{t}^{*},u)\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(X_{t}^{*})]+b(X_{t}^{*},u)v^{\prime}(X_{t}^{*})\Big)\right)}{\displaystyle\int_{U}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r(X_{t}^{*},u)+\sigma^{2}(X_{t}^{*},u)\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(X_{t}^{*})]+b(X_{t}^{*},u)v^{\prime}(X_{t}^{*})\Big)\right)du},\end{split}

where
\{X_{t}^{*},t\geq 0\}
solves(
6
) when the randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(\cdot;\cdot)
is applied and assume that
\{\theta_{t}^{*},t\geq 0\}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
.

The above (
11
) contributes to the qualitative understanding of optimal exploration in Knightian uncertainty environment. We will investigate this further in the next section.

4
The Linear-Quadratic Case

We now focus on a class of entropy-regularized (relaxed) stochastic control problems with linear state dynamics and quadratic rewards under Knightian uncertainty, in which

b(x,u)=Ax+Fu,\quad\sigma(x,u)=Cx+Du,\quad x,u\in\mathbb{R},

(12)

where
A,F,C,D\in\mathbb{R}
. The reward function is given by

r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{M}{2}x^{2}+Ixu+\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Px+Qu\right),\quad x,u\in\mathbb{R},

(13)

where
M\geq 0,K>0,I,P,Q\in\mathbb{R}
.

In the classical control literature, such LQ control problems are among the most important, not only because they admit elegant and tractable solutions, but also because more complex nonlinear problems can often be approximated by LQ formulations. As is standard in LQ control theory, we assume the control set is unconstrained, i.e.,
U=\mathbb{R}
.

Fix the initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The mean and variance processes
\mu_{t},\sigma_{t}^{2},t\geq 0
of any open-loop randomized control
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)
are characterized as follows:

\mu_{t}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\theta_{t}(u)du,\quad\sigma_{t}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\theta_{t}(u)du-\mu_{t}^{2}.

The stochastic differential equation (
6
) can be expressed as:

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\int_{U}b(X_{t}^{\theta},u)\theta_{t}(u)du\,dt+\sqrt{\int_{U}\sigma^{2}(X_{t}^{\theta},u)\theta_{t}(u)du}dB_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(AX_{t}^{\theta}+Fu)\theta_{t}(u)du\,dt+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(CX_{t}^{\theta}+Du\right)^{2}\theta_{t}(u)du}dB_{t}

(14)

\displaystyle=(AX_{t}^{\theta}+F\mu_{t})dt+\sqrt{(CX_{t}^{\theta}+D\mu_{t})^{2}+D^{2}\sigma_{t}^{2}}dB_{t},\quad t>0;\quad X_{0}^{\theta}=x.

Further define

L(X_{t}^{\theta},\theta_{t}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}r(X_{t}^{\theta},u)\theta_{t}(u)du-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)du.

Next, we define the associated admissible control set
\mathcal{A}(x)
:
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)
if

1.

For each
t\geq 0
,
\theta_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(U)
q.s.;

2.

For each
A\in\mathcal{B}(U)
,
\left\{\int_{A}\theta_{t}(u)du,t\geq 0\right\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable;

3.

For each
t\geq 0
,
\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{t}(\mu_{s}^{2}+\sigma_{s}^{2})ds\right]<\infty
;

4.

For
\left\{X_{t}^{\theta},t\geq 0\right\}
solving (
4
),
\liminf_{T\to\infty}e^{-\rho T}\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[(X_{T}^{\theta})^{2}\right]=0
;

5.

For
\left\{X_{t}^{\theta},t\geq 0\right\}
solving (
4
),
\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}|L(X_{t}^{\theta},\theta_{t})|dt\right]<\infty
.

In the above, condition (iii) is to ensure that for any
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, the drift and volatility terms in (
4
) satisfy the global Lipschitz condition and a class of linear growth conditions in the state variables, and hence, based on Peng
[
21
]
, the SDE (
4
) has a unique strong solution
X^{\theta}
. Condition (iv) is to ensure that the dynamic programming principle as well as the verification theorem are applicable for this model. Finally, condition (v) ensures that the reward is finite.

We now introduce the entropy-regularized relaxed LQ control problem under Knightian uncertainty

\begin{split}V(x)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)}\mathcal{E}\bigg[&\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}r(X_{t}^{\theta},u)\theta_{t}(u)du\\
&-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)du\Big)dt\,\bigg|\,X_{0}^{\theta}=x\bigg].\end{split}

(15)

In the following two subsections, we derive explicit solutions for both state-independent and state-dependent cases of reward.

4.1
State-Independent Reward Case

We first consider the case where
r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)
, i.e., the reward function depends solely on the control action and is state-independent. In this scenario, the current system state becomes irrelevant to the optimization problem. Nevertheless, this formulation remains fundamentally interesting as it corresponds to the RL problem with state-independent rewards, which is commonly referred to as the continuous-time multi-armed bandit problem in the literature (for recent work, see Chen et al.
[
31
,
32
]
and references therein).

Based on the derivation in the previous section, the optimal randomized feedback control in (
11
) reduces to

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime}(x)]}\right)du}.

(16)

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{CDxv^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}}\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{CDxv^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\underline{\sigma}^{2}}\right)du}.

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{CDxv^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{CDxv^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K-D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)\bar{\sigma}^{2}}\right)du}.

For
v^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
, but
v^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
,

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K}\right)^{\!2}\!\!\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K}\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\left(u-\frac{Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K}\right)^{\!2}\!\!\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K}\right)du}.

In this case, the optimal randomized feedback control follows a Gaussian distribution. More specifically, at any current state
x
, the agent should explore according to a Gaussian distribution whose mean
(\mu_{1})
and variance
(\sigma_{1}^{2})
are given by

\mu_{1}=\frac{2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]},

and

\sigma_{1}^{2}=\frac{\lambda}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]},

respectively.

The mean
\mu_{1}
represents the central tendency of the control policy
\theta
, indicating the optimal action the agent tends to choose in the current state. This reflects the agent’s optimal utilization of known information in the current state. The variance
\sigma_{1}^{2}
characterizes the dispersion of the control policy
\theta
, representing the randomness in the agent’s exploration of unknown actions. The larger the variance, the wider the scope of the agent’s exploration, while the smaller the variance, the more the agent’s preference for actions closer to the mean, i.e., the current optimal action.

Notice that the condition
K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]>0,x\in\mathbb{R}
was used in deriving the above equation.

When
v^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
, the optimal randomized feedback control is Gaussian distributed, since
K>0
,
K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]>0
clearly holds;

When
v^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
and
K-D^{2}\underline{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
, i.e.,

\underline{\sigma}^{2}<\frac{K}{D^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)}
, the optimal randomized feedback control is Gaussian distributed. At this point, as
\underline{\sigma}
increases, the degree of Knightian uncertainty decreases while the variance
\sigma_{1}^{2}
increases, leading to an expanded exploration range and greater agent propensity for exploration. This phenomenon occurs because the RL agent is ambiguity aversion: when Knightian uncertainty diminishes, the agent may perceive the environment as more controllable and consequently becomes more willing to try new actions (exploration) to further optimize its policy.

When
v^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
, since
0<\underline{\sigma}<\bar{\sigma}<\infty
and
K>0
,
K-D^{2}\bar{\sigma}^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
clearly holds. As
\bar{\sigma}
increases, the degree of Knightian uncertainty rises, leading to decreased variance
\sigma_{1}^{2}
, a narrower exploration range, and the agent’s tendency to select the current optimal action. This is because the RL agent’s aversion to Knightian uncertainty implies it will avoid making decisions in highly uncertain environments. When Knightian uncertainty is elevated, the agent tends to adopt a conservative policy (e.g., choosing known safe actions while reducing exploration).

Substituting (
16
) into (
10
), after a simple calculation, the HJB equation becomes

\rho v(x)=\frac{(2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Q)^{2}}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\ln\left(\frac{2\pi e\lambda}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}\right)-1\right)

+C^{2}x^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Av^{\prime}(x).

At this point, the HJB equation is transformed into a nonlinear equation with multiple smooth solutions, even in quadratic polynomials satisfying
K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]>0
. One of the solutions is a constant, given by the following expression

v(x)=v:=\frac{Q^{2}}{\rho K}+\frac{\lambda}{2\rho}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi e\lambda}{K}-1\right),

where the corresponding optimal randomized feedback control (
16
) is transformed into

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\left(u+\frac{Q}{K}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K}\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\left(u+\frac{Q}{K}\right)^{2}\big/\frac{2\lambda}{K}\right)du}

(17)

It turns out that the right-hand side of the (
17
) is independent of the current state
x
. Thus, the optimal randomized feedback control remains identical across all states. Note that the non-exploratory LQ problem with a state-independent reward function
\ r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)
clearly has an optimal control
u^{*}=-\frac{Q}{K}
, which is also state-independent, except that it is the mean of the optimal Gaussian randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}
.

The following results prove that the constant
v
is indeed the value function
V
and that the randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}
defined by (
17
) is optimal. Hereafter, for notational convenience, we use
\mathcal{N}(\cdot|\mu,\sigma^{2})
to denote the density function of a Gaussian random variable with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 1
.

If
r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)
, then the value function in (
15
) is given by the following equation

v(x)=v:=\frac{Q^{2}}{\rho K}+\frac{\lambda}{2\rho}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi e\lambda}{K}-1\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

the optimal randomized feedback control is Gaussian distributed with

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\mathcal{N}\left(u\big|-\frac{Q}{K},\frac{\lambda}{K}\right).

In addition, the associated optimal state process
\{X_{t}^{*},t\geq 0\}
under
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(\cdot;\cdot)
is the unique solution to the following SDE

dX_{t}^{*}=\left(AX_{t}^{*}-\frac{FQ}{K}\right)dt+\sqrt{\left(CX_{t}^{*}-\frac{DQ}{K}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda D^{2}}{K}}\,dB_{t},\quad X_{0}^{*}=x.\

(18)

Proof.

Due to
r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)
, it is known that the reward is independent of the state. Therefore, we conjecture that the value function
v(x)
is in constant form. Let
v(x)\equiv v
be a constant solution to the HJB equation. Then, the corresponding randomized feedback control
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\mathcal{N}\left(u\big|-\frac{Q}{K},\frac{\lambda}{K}\right)
follows immediately from (
16
). Let
\theta^{*}=\{\theta_{t}^{*},t\geq 0\}
be the open-loop randomized control generated by
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(\cdot;\cdot)
, and it is easy to know
\theta^{*}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
.

Now, for any
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)
and
T\geq 0
, since
v(x)
is a constant,
v^{\prime}(x)=0
and
v^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
, by
G
-Itô’s formula (Peng
[
21
]
), we have that

e^{-\rho T}v=v-\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\rho t}\rho v\,dt.

The HJB (
10
) reduces to

\rho v(x)=\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}(U)}\int_{U}\big(r(x,u)-\lambda\ln\theta(u)\big)\theta(u)\,du.

For the optimal control policy
\theta_{t}^{*}
, the HJB equation holds, i.e.,

\rho v(x)=\int_{U}\big(r(x,u)-\lambda\ln\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\big)\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\,du.

And for arbitrary control policy
\theta_{t}

\rho v(x)\geq\int_{U}\big(r(x,u)-\lambda\ln\theta_{t}(u)\big)\theta_{t}(u)\,du,

-\rho v(x)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}(u)\,du+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)\,du.

Taking expectation
\mathcal{E}
on both sides, we deduce

e^{-\rho T}v\leq v+\mathcal{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\rho t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}(u)\,du+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)\,du\right)dt\right].

For
\theta\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, when
T\to\infty
,
\lim_{T\to\infty}e^{-\rho T}v=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq v+\mathcal{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\rho t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}(u)\,du+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)\,du\right)dt\right],

v\geq\mathcal{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}(u)\,du-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}(u)\ln\theta_{t}(u)\,du\right)dt\right].

For
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
, since
V(x)
is an upper bound on the cumulative reward in all possible control policies
\theta
and
v
is an upper bound on the cumulative reward in any control policy
\theta
, it follows that

v\geq V(x).

On the other hand,
\boldsymbol{\theta^{*}}
has been derived as the maximum value on the right-hand side of the (
10
), so that

\rho v=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\,du-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\ln\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\,du.

And
v=V(x)
, indicating that
v
is indeed the value function. At this point

v=\mathcal{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\left(\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Qu\right)\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\,du-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\ln\theta_{t}^{*}(u)\,du\right)dt\right].

We have come to the conclusion that

v(x)=v:=\frac{Q^{2}}{\rho K}+\frac{\lambda}{2\rho}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi e\lambda}{K}-1\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, the exploratory state dynamics (
18
) can be obtained by substituting
\mu_{t}^{*}=-\frac{Q}{K}
and
(\sigma_{t}^{*})^{2}=\frac{\lambda}{K}
,
t\geq 0
into the (
4
). Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

The above solution shows that when the reward is independent of the state, the optimal randomized feedback control with density
\mathcal{N}\left(\cdot\big|-\frac{Q}{K},\frac{\lambda}{K}\right)
is also independent of the state.

A distinguishing feature of the derived optimal distribution
\mathcal{N}\left(\cdot\big|-\frac{Q}{K},\frac{\lambda}{K}\right)
is that its mean is consistent with the optimal control for the non-exploratory LQ problem under Knightian uncertainty, while the variance is determined by the temperature parameter
\lambda
. In the context of the multi-arm slot machine problem, this result specifies that the mean is centered around the current best performing arm, with the variance similarly governed by the temperature parameter.

The higher the level of exploration, the more dispersed the exploration around the current best arm. This type of exploration policy is intuitive and, in turn, provides guidance on how to actually select temperature parameters in practice.

However, as we will see in the next section, when the reward depends on the local state, the optimal randomized feedback control really depends on the state.

4.2
State-Dependent Reward Case

Now consider the general case where the reward depends on both control and state, i.e.,

r(x,u)=-\left(\frac{M}{2}x^{2}+Ixu+\frac{K}{2}u^{2}+Px+Qu\right),\quad x,u\in\mathbb{R}.

Following a similar argument to (
16
), when
K-2D^{2}\tilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]>0
, we deduce that the candidate optimal randomized feedback control is given by

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}(u;x)=\mathcal{N}\left(u\bigg|\frac{2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Ix-Q}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]},\frac{\lambda}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}\right).

(19)

At this point, the HJB equation is

\begin{split}\rho v(x)&=\frac{(2CDx\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]+Fv^{\prime}(x)-Ix-Q)^{2}}{K-2D^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]}+\frac{1}{2}(2C^{2}\widetilde{G}[v^{\prime\prime}(x)]-M)x^{2}\\

5
Exploration Cost and Its Impact

Motivated by the necessity of exploration in RL environments with typical unknown dynamics, we formulate and analyze a novel class of stochastic control problems under Knightian uncertainty that incorporates both entropy-regularized and relaxed control. We further derive closed-form solutions and provide verification theorems for an important class of LQ problems. This naturally raises a fundamental question: how to quantitatively assess the cost and impact of exploration. This evaluation can be achieved by systematically comparing our results with those from non-exploratory LQ problems under Knightian uncertainty, which notably lack both entropy-regularized and relaxed control mechanisms.

We carry out this comparison analysis next.

5.3

5.4
Vanishing Exploration

The exploration weight
\lambda
is considered as an exogenous parameter reflecting the level of exploration expected by the RL agent. The smaller this parameter is, the more emphasis is placed on exploitation. When this parameter is close enough to zero, the exploratory formulation is close enough to the problem without exploration. Naturally, the ideal result is that if the exploration weight
\lambda

Proof.

The weak convergence of feedback controls follows from the explicit forms of
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}
and
\boldsymbol{u}^{*}
in statements (a) and (b) and the fact that
\alpha_{1}

\alpha_{2}

7
Conclusion

This paper incorporates Knightian uncertainty into the exploratory state dynamics proposed by Wang et al.
[
10
]
, introducing an entropy-regularized relaxed stochastic control problem under Knightian uncertainty using sublinear expectation theory. Through stochastic analysis and the dynamic programming principle under nonlinear expectation, we derive the HJB equation and obtain the optimal policy that balances exploration and exploitation. For the LQ case, we analyze the optimal randomized feedback control for both state-dependent and state-independent rewards, proving that the optimal control follows a Gaussian distribution under certain conditions. We examine how Knightian uncertainty affects the variance of optimal policies: for state-independent rewards, reduced uncertainty increases variance and exploration; for state-dependent rewards, increased uncertainty decreases variance and promotes exploitation. We establish the solvability equivalence between exploratory and non-exploratory LQ problems under Knightian uncertainty and analyze exploration cost. The exploration cost in Knightian LQ problems can be predetermined by the RL agent through parameters
\lambda
and
\rho
, as they are independent of the system dynamics or reward structure. When the exploration weight

approaches zero, the exploratory LQ problem converges to its non-exploratory counterpart. Finally, we present an LQ example with numerical simulations in Jupyter Notebook to verify our theoretical results.

We propose the following research directions: The exploratory state dynamics under Knightian uncertainty proposed in this paper can be applied to continuous time MV portfolio selection problems under Knightian uncertainty. By reformulating the MV portfolio problem as an exploration-exploitation trade-off, we can derive optimal investment policy under Knightian uncertainty to provide rational investment decisions. Additionally, we may consider using financial market data to estimate the minimum and maximum volatility rates and design an implementable RL algorithm to analyze how Knightian uncertainty affects investment decisions.

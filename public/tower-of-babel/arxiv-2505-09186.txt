JWST & ALMA Joint Analysis with [OII] λ ​ λ \lambda\lambda 3726,3729, [OIII] λ \lambda 4363, [OIII]88 μ \mu m, and [OIII]52 μ \mu m: Multi-Zone Evolution of Electron Densities at 𝐳 ∼ 𝟎 − 𝟏𝟒 \mathbf{z\sim 0-14} and Its Impact on Metallicity Measurements
Yuichi Harikane, Ryan L. Sanders, Richard Ellis, Tucker Jones, Masami Ouchi, Nicolas Laporte, Guido Roberts-Borsani, Harley Katz, Kimihiko Nakajima, Yoshiaki Ono, Mansi Gupta

Abstract
We present a JWST and ALMA detailed study of the ISM properties of high-redshift galaxies. Our JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy targeting three galaxies at z = 6 − 7 z=6-7 detects key rest-frame optical emission lines, allowing us to derive [Oii] λ ​ λ \lambda\lambda 3726,3729-based electron densities of n e , optical ∼ 1000 n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\sim 1000 cm -3 on average and [Oiii] λ \lambda 4363-based metallicities of 12 + log ⁡ ( O / H ) = 8.0 − 8.2 \mathrm{12+log(O/H)}=8.0-8.2 in two galaxies. New ALMA Band 9/10 observations detect the [Oiii] 52 μ \mu m line in one galaxy but do not in the others, resulting in FIR-based densities of n e , FIR ≲ 500 n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}\lesssim 500 cm -3 from the [Oiii] 52 μ \mu m/ [Oiii] 88 μ \mu m ratios, systematically lower than the optical [Oii] -based measurements. These low FIR-based densities are comparable to those at both z ∼ 0 z\sim 0 and z > 6 z>6 in the literature, including JADES-GS-z14-0 at z = 14.18 z=14.18 , suggesting little evolution up to z ∼ 14 z\sim 14 , in contrast to the increasing trend of optical-based densities with redshift. By conducting a JWST and ALMA joint analysis using emission lines detected with both telescopes, we find that the observed FIR [Oiii] 52,88 μ \mu m luminosities are too high to be explained by the optical-based densities at which they would be significantly collisionally de-excited. Instead, a 2-zone model with distinct high- and low-density regions is required to reproduce all observed lines, indicating that FIR [Oiii] emission arises predominantly from low-density gas, while optical [Oiii] and [Oii] lines trace both regions. We further demonstrate that the direct- T e T_{\mathrm{e}} method can sometimes significantly underestimate metallicities up to 0.8 dex due to the presence of the low-density gas not fully traced by optical lines alone, highlighting the importance of combining optical and FIR lines to accurately determine gas-phase metallicities in the early universe.

I
Introduction

An understanding of galaxy assembly at high redshifts is essential to form a comprehensive picture of galaxy formation and evolution.
The gas-phase metallicity (O/H) of the interstellar medium (ISM) is one of the most powerful tools for understanding galaxy growth because it is sensitive to the past and current star formation rate (SFR), the gas reservoir mass, and gaseous inflows and outflows (e.g.,
Tinsley 1980
;
Lilly et al. 2013
).
Considerable effort has been invested to characterizing the stellar mass-ISM metallicity relation and its evolution over the redshift range of
z=0-10
(e.g.,
Tremonti et al. 2004
;
Maiolino et al. 2008
;
Andrews & Martini 2013
;
Sanders et al. 2021
;
Heintz et al. 2023
;
Matthee et al. 2023
;
Nakajima et al. 2023
;
Curti et al. 2023
;
Curti et al. 2024
;
Langeroodi et al. 2023
;
Sanders et al. 2024
).
These studies have been fundamental in establishing our current theoretical picture of galaxy growth and have placed key constraints on gas content and inflow/outflow rates at low and high redshifts.

The first JWST NIRSpec spectra from the Early Release Observations
(
Pontoppidan et al. 2022
)
surprisingly revealed clear detections of the auroral
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 line in galaxies at redshifts up to
z=8.5
.
These novel detections of the weak
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 line, which is sensitive to the electron temperature
T_{\mathrm{e}}
, were enabled by the transformational sensitivity of JWST/NIRSpec, allowing the use of the temperature-based direct method to derive metallicities of galaxies in the early universe.
This direct-temperature (direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
) method is widely regarded as a robust technique for inferring galaxy gas-phase metallicities, significantly more accurate than the “strong-line method,” which relies on calibrations between metallicity and flux ratios of the brightest lines.
Many existing strong-line calibrations, based on samples of local galaxies and
Hii
regions
(
Pettini & Pagel 2004
, e.g.,)
, are not valid for high-redshift galaxies because of the significant evolution of ISM ionization conditions and global galaxy properties between
z\sim 0
and
z>2

(
Bian et al. 2018
;
Sanders et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.
Consequently, the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method is an essential tool for robustly measuring the metallicity of galaxies at
z\gtrsim 6
, and indeed, many JWST studies have measured metallicities of high-redshift galaxies using
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 and discussed the redshift evolution of the mass-metallicity relation
(
Rhoads et al. 2023
;
Schaerer et al. 2022
;
Brinchmann 2023
;
Trump et al. 2023
;
Katz et al. 2023
;
Curti et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2023
;
Topping et al. 2024a
;
Topping et al. 2024b
;
Sanders et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

The electron density of the H
ii
region (
n_{\mathrm{e}}
) is also a key quantity in describing the physical state of the ISM.
Many studies have measured the electron densities of galaxies at both low and high redshifts using emission-line doublets such as
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726,
[Sii]
\lambda
6716/
[Sii]
\lambda
6731,
[Ciii]
\lambda
1907/
Ciii]
\lambda
1909, and
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m, whose flux ratios depend on the electron density due to the different critical densities of the doublet lines
(
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
, e.g.,)
.
These studies have revealed redshift evolution, with increasing densities toward higher redshifts
(
Isobe et al. 2023
;
Topping et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.
Some studies have also investigated correlations between electron density and galaxy properties
(
Shimakawa et al. 2015
;
Reddy et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.
Although the densities derived with the optical
[Oii]
and
[Sii]
lines agree well with each other
(
Sanders et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
, there is a trend that densities measured with the rest-frame UV
Ciii]
lines are systematically higher than those measured with optical lines
(
Mingozzi et al. 2022
;
Topping et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.
These results indicate that the ISM structure is not homogeneous and that a multi-zone ISM model must be considered to fully understand the properties of galaxies
(
Berg et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.

The direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method mentioned above usually assumes a homogeneous ISM to measure electron temperatures and metallicities.
However,
Hii
regions are not homogeneous, and as a result the
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method may be biased.
Temperature fluctuations are often invoked as an explanation of the so-called abundance discrepancy factor (ADF), whereby O/H measured from recombination lines is found to be higher by
\sim 0.2-0.3
dex compared to the
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method
(
Peimbert et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
.
While the cause of the ADF is not firmly established
(
Curti 2025
;
Stasińska et al. 2007
;
Chen et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
, it is thought to arise from inhomogeneities.
For galaxy-integrated measurements at high redshift, the ISM structure presumably has a similar effect on temperature and metallicity estimates obtained using the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method
(
Cameron et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
, but there are no observational studies addressing the robustness of this method.

In this study, we investigate the ISM properties of high-redshift galaxies in detail using optical and far-infrared (FIR) emission lines detected with JWST and ALMA, respectively.
In particular, FIR fine-structure lines such as
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m and
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m observed with ALMA provide critical advantages: they are relatively insensitive to the electron temperature (
T_{e}
), and to first order their luminosities scale with the gas-phase metallicity, making them powerful tools for metallicity measurements.
Moreover, due to their low critical densities, these FIR lines predominantly trace low-density ionized gas, offering a complementary view to that from rest-frame optical lines such as
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363, which are highly
T_{e}
-sensitive and typically probe higher-density regions
(
Jones et al. 2020
;
Yang & Lidz 2020
;
Yang et al. 2021
;
Nakazato et al. 2023
)
.
We aim to investigate the impact of assuming a 1-zone ISM model on the derived ISM properties by first relaxing the single-density criterion and evaluating the effects on measurements such as metallicity and the mass-metallicity relation.
This JWST-ALMA partnership probing multi-wavelength emission lines provides an opportunity to explore the multi-zone ISM structure and to test the robustness of the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method, which is widely regarded as the “gold standard” for high-redshift metallicity studies.

Similar scientific goals have been independently explored for a
z\sim 6.8
galaxy using JWST and ALMA

(
Usui et al. 2025
)
.

This paper is organized as follows.
We describe our galaxy sample and JWST and ALMA observational datasets in Section
II
.
Section
III
describes the SED fitting, and Section
IV
presents the basic ISM properties derived from commonly used optical and FIR line diagnostics.
In Section
V
, we conduct a JWST and ALMA joint analysis using emission lines detected with both telescopes.
In Section
VI
, we discuss the ISM structure of high-redshift galaxies and metallicity estimators using optical and FIR lines.
Section
VII
summarizes our findings.
Throughout this paper, we adopt the Planck cosmological parameter sets from the TT, TE, EE+lowP+lensing+BAO results
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
:
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}=0.3111
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.6899
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{b}}=0.0489
,
h=0.6766
, and
\sigma_{8}=0.8102
.
All magnitudes are given in the AB system
(
Oke & Gunn 1983
)
.
We define the solar metallicity as
\mathrm{12+log(O/H)}=8.69
and
Z_{\odot}=0.0142

(
Asplund et al. 2009
)
.

II.1
Galaxy Sample

As targets for our JWST and ALMA observations, we selected three galaxies at
z=6-7
, J0217-0208 at
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=6.204
, SXDF-NB1006-2 at
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=7.212
, and J1211-0118 at
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=6.031
(Table
1
).
This sample was chosen based on existing
[Oiii]
88
\mu

Miyazaki et al. 2018

(
Aihara et al. 2018
)

J0217-0208 and J1211-0118 were spectroscopically confirmed in
Matsuoka et al. (2018)
via their Ly
\alpha
emission line and the Lyman break feature, respectively, with Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC)/Optical System for Imaging and low-Intermediate-Resolution Integrated Spectroscopy (OSIRIS;

Kashikawa et al. 2002

[Oiii]
88
\mu

.
SXDF-NB1006-2 was spectroscopically confirmed via its Ly
\alpha

[Oiii]
88
\mu

II.2.1
NIRCam Image

NIRCam images of the three galaxies were taken to investigate their morphologies as well as stellar populations.
In our program, we chose four filters, F150W, F200W, F300M, and F410M (F150W, F200W, F360M, and F430M) for J0217-0208 and J1211-0118 (for SXDF-NB1006-2) to probe the stellar continuum in bandpasses that avoid strong emission lines, and further to provides measurements on both sides of the Balmer and 4000
\mathrm{\AA}
breaks, such that SED fitting will yield robust stellar population parameters.
The total exposure time for each filter was 10 (35) minutes for J0217-0208 and J1211-0118 (for SXDF-NB1006-2).
In addition to the NIRCam data obtained in our program, we also use NIRCam images obtained in GO-1840 (PIs: J. Alvarez-Marquez & T. Hashimoto).
These images were taken with the six filters F115W, F150W, F200W, F250M, F300M, and F444W (F115W, F150W, F200W, F277W, F356W, and F444W) for J0217-0208 and J1211-0118 (for SXDF-NB1006-2).
We reduced these data using the JWST Calibration Pipeline (versions 1.12.5) and the Calibration Reference Data System context file
jwst_1193.pmap
with custom modifications described in
Harikane et al. (2023a)
.

II.2.2
NIRSpec Spectrum

NIRSpec IFU spectroscopic observations of the three galaxies were designed to detect key rest-frame optical emission lines necessary for standard gas-phase metallicity diagnostics, including
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729, H
\gamma
, H
\beta
,
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363,
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007, and H
\alpha
, where accessible.
To resolve the
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 doublet and enable reliable electron density measurements, high-resolution gratings were employed for the
[Oii]
observations.
For J0217
-
0208 and J1211
-
0118 at
z=6.0-6.2
, we used the grating-filter pairs G235H/F170LP for
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 and G395H/F290LP for H
\beta
,
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007, and H
\alpha
.
For SXDF-NB1006-2 at
z=7.2
, we used G395H/F290LP to cover
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 and H
\beta
, and additionally employed G395M/F290LP to fill the wavelength gap present in the G395H/F290LP coverage and observe
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007.

We reduced the NIRSpec IFU data using the JWST Calibration Pipeline (versions 1.12.5) and the Calibration Reference Data System context file of
jwst_1189.pmap
with several modifications
(
Xu et al. 2024
, see also)
.
We downloaded raw data from the MAST archive and ran Stage 1 calibration with
calwebb_detector1
.
The resulting count-rate frames were corrected for 1/f noise using NSClean
(
Rauscher 2024
)
.
We then ran Stage 2 calibration with
calwebb_spec2
and produced the 3D data cube for each exposure with a pixel scale of
0.
​​″
05
.
Following the procedure used by the Q3D team
(
Wylezalek et al. 2022
;
Vayner et al. 2023
;
Veilleux et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
, we used the
reproject
package
(
Robitaille et al. 2023
)
to combine the 3D data cubes taken in different dither positions into a single data cube using the
reproject_interp
routine.
We then removed artifacts by sigma clipping and created a combined cube by median stacking.
The background was estimated by calculating the median at each wavelength with smoothing over 100 channels in the wavelength direction and was subtracted from the data cube.
Finally, we corrected for astrometric offsets by creating a continuum image from the data cube and comparing it with the NIRCam F150W image, which has the highest spatial resolution.

We measured emission line fluxes by fitting each emission line with a Gaussian.
First, we determined the redshift by fitting the H
\beta
and
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007 lines with an assumed line ratio of
f_{\mathrm{\sc[OIII]5007}}/f_{\mathrm{[OIII]4959}}=2.98
, based on theoretical calculations
(
Storey & Zeippen 2000
)
.
Then, we fit other emission lines with Gaussian functions with fixing the redshift to the value determined with the H
\beta
and
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007 lines.
In the fitting of the
[Nii]
\lambda\lambda
6548,6584 lines for J0217-0208 and J1211-0118, we assumed the line ratio of
f_{\mathrm{\sc[NII]6584}}/f_{\mathrm{[NII]6548}}=2.94
,
based on a theoretical value in
Rodríguez (2020)
.

We simultaneously fit the
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 lines with two Gaussian functions to estimate the line ratio and its error with taking the correlated uncertainties into account.

We assumed the same line width for each set of the doublet lines, i.e.,
\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{[OII]3726}}=\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{\sc[OII]3729}}
,
\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{[OIII]4959}}=\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{\sc[OIII]5007}}
, and
\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{[NII]6548}}=\mathrm{FWHM}_{\mathrm{\sc[NII]6584}}
.
The line widths of SXDF-NB1006-2 and J1211-0118 are narrow,
\mathrm{FWHM}\sim 100-200\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}
, comparable to the
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m line
(
Inoue et al. 2016
;
Harikane et al. 2020
)
.
Because the
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007 and H
\alpha
lines in J0217-0208 show a weak moderately broad component (
\mathrm{FWHM}\sim 500-800\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}
; see also
Matsuoka et al. 2025
) in addition to the narrow component (
\mathrm{FWHM}\sim 300\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}
), we fit these lines with two Gaussian components.
These broad components are probably due to another ionized gas component or outflow driven by star formation, not a signature of broad-line AGN activity, given their line widths and the fact that the broad component is seen in the forbidden
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007 line.
In the subsequent analyses, we use a total flux including both the narrow and broad components for these lines, but the conclusions do not change if we use only the narrow-component flux given the small fraction of the broad-component flux (
\sim 10\%
).

III
SED Fitting

We conduct SED fitting to understand the physical properties of our galaxies.
We calculate the total magnitude in each band from an aperture magnitude measured in a 0.
\!\!\arcsec
3-diameter circular aperture with an aperture correction, following the method of
Harikane et al. (2023a)
.
For the SED fitting, we use
prospector

(
Johnson et al. 2021
)
, varying the dust optical depth in the
V
-band, metallicity, star formation history, and total stellar mass as free parameters, while fixing the redshift to the spectroscopically determined value.
Model spectra are generated using the Flexible Stellar Population Synthesis
(
Conroy et al. 2009
;
Conroy & Gunn 2010
, FSPS;)
package with the Modules for Experiments in Stellar Astrophysics Isochrones and Stellar Tracks (MIST;
Choi et al. 2016
).
The boost in ionizing flux production from massive stars due to rotation is included in the MIST isochrones
(
Choi et al. 2017
)
.
We assume the stellar IMF from
Chabrier (2003)
, the dust
attenuation
law of
Calzetti et al. (2000)
, and the intergalactic medium (IGM) attenuation model by
Madau (1995)
.
We adopt a flexible star formation history with five bins: the first bin is fixed at 0-10 Myr, and the other bins are spaced equally in logarithmic time between 10 Myr and a lookback time corresponding to
z=30
, assuming a constant SFR within each bin.
A continuity prior is applied to the star formation history, with flat priors for other parameters in the ranges
0<\tau_{\mathrm{V}}<2
,
-2.0<\mathrm{log}(Z/Z_{\odot})<0.4
, and
6<\mathrm{log}(M_{*}/M_{\sun})<12
.
We search for the best-fit model to the observed photometric data points using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method with
emcee

(
Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
)
.
Table
3
summarizes the results of the SED fitting.

IV.1

We evaluate the dust attenuation of our galaxies using the color excess,

We use the H

, H
\beta

>10\sigma

Calzetti et al. (2000)

We assume intrinsic line ratios for a temperature of 10,000 K from
Osterbrock (1989)
, namely
\mathrm{H\alpha/H\beta=2.86}
and
\mathrm{H\gamma/H\beta=0.468}

V.3
.

4

We find small but non-negligible dust

E(B-V)\simeq 0.1

SXDF-NB1006-2 shows almost no dust

II.3
and in the previous studies
(
Inoue et al. 2016
;
Ren et al. 2023
)
.

The estimated
E(B-V)
value for each galaxy is consistent with the
\mathrm{H\alpha/H\beta}
and
\mathrm{H\gamma/H\beta}

(
Pirzkal et al. 2024
;
Scarlata et al. 2024
;
Yanagisawa et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

In the following sections, we use emission line fluxes corrected for dust

based on the

The obtained color excesses are comparable to those estimated from the SED fitting in Section

IV.2
Electron Temperature and Metallicity

We calculate the electron temperature and metallicity of our galaxies.
We identify the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 emission line in J0217-0208 and J1211-0118, allowing us to measure metallicities using the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method.
Although the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363/
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 ratio depends on both electron temperature and density, the density dependence is negligible as long as the density is well below the critical densities of
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 and
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 (
2.8\times 10^{7}
and
6.4\times 10^{5}
cm
-3
, respectively).
Since the electron densities of our galaxies measured in Section
IV.3
are much lower than these critical densities, the derived electron temperatures are not significantly affected by different density assumptions.
Here, we adopt the electron densities measured with the optical
[Oii]
lines from Section
IV.3.1
.
If we instead use the densities measured with the FIR
[Oiii]
lines from Section
IV.3.2
, the obtained temperatures increase by up to 1%.
We calculate the electron temperatures based on the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363/
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 line ratios using
PyNeb

(
Luridiana et al. 2015
)
.
We then estimate the ionic oxygen abundance ratios of O
+
/H
+
and O
2+
/H
+
using the equations in
Izotov et al. (2006)
, based on the observed emission line fluxes of
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007, H
\beta
, and
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729, assuming an extrapolation of the electron temperature relation between O
+
and O
2+
.

Adopting the more recent
\mathrm{O}^{+}-\mathrm{O}^{2+}
electron temperature relation from
Cataldi et al. (2025)
changes the derived oxygen abundances by less than 5%.

We do not account for a higher ionization state of O
3+
/H
+
, as the ionization potential of O
3+
is too high for the stellar radiation fields of our galaxies, which do not exhibit high-ionization lines (e.g., He
ii
).
As shown in Table
4
, we find electron temperatures and metallicities of
\simeq 12000-13000
K and
12+\mathrm{log(O/H)}\simeq 8.0-8.2
, respectively, which are comparable to those of other high-redshift galaxies
(
Nakajima et al. 2023
;
Sanders et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
However, as discussed in Section
V.2
, these parameters cannot reproduce the observed luminosities of the FIR
[Oiii]
emission lines detected with ALMA.

IV.3.1
Density from the
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 Line Ratio

The
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 emission lines have been commonly used as a density indicator
(
Sanders et al. 2016
;
Isobe et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
, because of their different critical densities (
3300
and
14000
cm
-3
for
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 and
[Oii]
\lambda
3729, respectively).
Thanks to the moderately high spectral resolution of the NIRSpec G235H and G395H spectra, we can resolve the
[Oii]
doublet and thus measure the
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 line ratio (Figure
4
).

We calculate the electron densities of our galaxies from the measured
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 ratios using
PyNeb
.
We assume the electron temperature of
T_{\mathrm{e}}=13000
K, although the
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 ratio does not strongly depend on the electron temperature.
For example, using the calibration presented in
Sanders et al. (2016)
assuming
T_{\mathrm{e}}=10000
K gives results consistent within errors.

As summarized in Table
4
, the calculated electron densities are
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\simeq 1800
, 800, and 300 cm
-3
for J0217-0208, SXDF-NB1006-2, and J1211-0118, respectively, which are comparable to those measured in other high redshift galaxies with the
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 lines
(
Isobe et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

Thanks to the IFU data, we can also investigate the spatially-resolved density map.
Figure
8
shows the map of the electron densities of our three galaxies.
We see that the electron densities exhibit spatial variations, and some regions reaching very high densities of
\sim 10^{4}
cm
-3
.
Nonetheless, in J0217-0208 and SXDF-NB1006-2, most (
>90\%
) of the pixels show densities higher than 500 and 100 cm
-3
, respectively, and we do not find a significant fraction of pixels with low densities.

[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m Line Ratio

The line ratio of
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m is another reliable indicator of the electron density because the emissivities of these two FIR emission lines do not strongly depend on temperature but density
(
Palay et al. 2012
;
Jones et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.
We use
PyNeb
to calculate the line ratio as a function of electron density and temperature.
Figure
9
shows the line ratio as a function of electron density for various temperatures.
The ratio is largely insensitive to the electron temperature but changes rapidly over the electron density range of
\sim 100-10000
cm
-3
, reflecting the different critical densities of
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m and
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m (3600 and 510 cm
-3
, respectively).
The ratio for an electron temperature of
T_{\mathrm{e}}=13000
K is well fitted by the following function:

f_{\mathrm{[OIII]52\mu m}}/f_{\mathrm{[OIII]88\mu m}}=a\frac{b+n_{\mathrm{e}}}{c+n_{\mathrm{e}}},

(1)

where
a=10.53
,
b=224.46
, and
c=4181.45
.
The electron density measured with the
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m ratio or their 2
\sigma
upper limits are
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}\simeq 500
,
<90
, and
<100
cm
-3
for J0217-0208, SXDF-NB1006-2, and J1211-0118, respectively, as summarized in Table
4
.

Although
Kewley et al. (2019)
discuss that the
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m ratio can exhibit some dependence on the ionization parameter at
n_{\mathrm{e}}\lesssim 10
cm
-3
, our estimates and upper limits do not significantly change by changing the ionization parameter.

\lambda

In addition, we analyze COS-3018555981 at

7

V.2

First, we test whether the ISM parameters constrained with the JWST optical emission lines can reproduce the luminosity of the ALMA FIR emission lines.

5

Clearly the

\mu

2-10
times higher than those inferred optically.
Among the four galaxies analyzed here, SXDF-NB1006-2 and MACS1149-JD1 do not show dust continuum emission in the deep ALMA data in

and

>2

[Oiii]
88
\mu

\sim 15

(
Harikane et al. 2020
;
Witstok et al. 2022

V.3
2-Zone Model Fitting

Motivated by the failures of the 1-zone modeling in Section
V.2
, we introduce a 2-zone model and relax the single-density and single-temperature assumptions as a first step toward comprehensively understanding the ISM properties of these galaxies
(see also
Usui et al. 2025
for an independent analysis on a
z\sim 6.8
galaxy using a similar approach)
.
In our 2-zone model, to keep it as simple as possible, we assume that the two ionized gas regions have different densities and temperatures but share the same metallicity and O32 ratio (i.e., the same ionization parameter in the low-density limit), where
\mathrm{O32}={\textsc{[Oiii]}}\lambda 5007/{\textsc{[Oii]}}\lambda\lambda 3726,3729
.
Systematic uncertainties arising from the assumption of the same metallicity and O32 ratio will be discussed in Section
V.5
.
Thus, we have seven free parameters in the 2-zone model: the densities in the two regions (
n_{\mathrm{e,1}}
and
n_{\mathrm{e,2}}
), the temperatures in the two regions (
T_{\mathrm{e,1}}
and
T_{\mathrm{e,2}}
), the metallicity (
\mathrm{12+log(O/H)}
), the O32 ratio (O32), and the ratio of the
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 line luminosities between the two regions (
L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,1}}/L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,2}}
), where subscripts 1 and 2 refer to the low- and high-density regions, respectively.
It is reasonable to mix the
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
emission in both regions in our 2-zone modeling because the aperture used to obtain our spectra contains many H
ii
regions within a galaxy.
Moreover, as discussed at the end of Section
V.2
, a model with spatially distinct
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
regions without mixing cannot reproduce the observed
[Oiii]
lines of J0217-0208.

V.4
Comparison of Metallicities

Since metallicity is one of the most important parameters for describing the ISM properties of galaxies, we test the robustness of direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
metallicities assuming a 1-zone ISM by comparing them with metallicities derived from 2-zone modeling.
Figure
15
compares the metallicities estimated with the 2-zone model (Section
V.3
) and those derived with the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method (Section
IV.2
) in the mass-metallicity plane.
We adopt the metallicities from the 2-zone modeling as the fiducial values because the 2-zone model self-consistently reproduces all the emission line luminosities observed from the optical to FIR, whereas the temperature derived from
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 in the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method fails to reproduce the FIR
[Oiii]
luminosities.
We find that the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
metallicities are consistent with the 2-zone metallicities in the two less massive galaxies, MACS1149-JD1 and SXDF-NB1006-2, but are significantly underestimated in the two more massive galaxies, COS-3018555981 and J0217-0208, by 0.4 and 0.8 dex, respectively.

Interestingly, we find a possible trend of a larger discrepancy in more metal-rich galaxies, which is consistent with predictions from
Stasińska (2005)
.

These differences originate from the different temperatures in the low- and high-density regions.
For MACS1149-JD1 and SXDF-NB1006-2, the estimated temperatures in the low- and high-density regions are comparable within the errors, allowing the 2-zone model with different densities but the same temperature to reproduce both the optical and FIR emission lines, resulting in a metallicity comparable to that from the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method.
For J0217-0208 and COS-3018555981, the estimated temperature in the high-density region is higher than that in the low-density region.
In this configuration with a high-density, high-temperature region and a low-density, low-temperature region, as shown in Figure
14
, the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 emission from the high-density, high-temperature region dominates the observed luminosity because its emissivity strongly depends on electron temperature.
As a result, the temperature estimated from
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 is overestimated compared to the effective temperature of the whole system, and the derived metallicity is significantly underestimated.
These results clearly indicate that a joint analysis using both optical and FIR lines is crucial for accurately measuring the gas-phase metallicity of high-redshift galaxies.

V.5
Systematic Uncertainty of the 2-Zone Model

In the 2-zone modeling, we assume the same ionization parameters and metallicities in the high- and low-density regions to keep the model as simple as possible.
However, given their very different densities and temperatures, it is possible that the gas is not well-mixed and that these two regions have different ionization parameters and/or metallicities.
Here, we quantify the effect of these assumptions on the metallicity estimates, focusing particularly on J0217-0208 and COS-3018555981, which show significantly higher metallicities than those derived with the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method assuming a 1-zone ISM.
As we discuss below, even when accounting for the systematic uncertainty introduced by these assumptions, the inferred metallicities from the JWST and ALMA joint analysis for J0217-0208 and COS-3018555981 remain higher than those obtained with the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method.

V.5.1
Effect of Different Ionization Parameters

First, we consider a situation with different ionization parameters.
The ionization parameter (
U_{\mathrm{ion}}
or
q_{\mathrm{ion}}
) is expressed as follows:

U_{\mathrm{ion}}=\frac{q_{\mathrm{ion}}}{c}=\frac{Q_{0}}{4\pi R^{2}_{\mathrm{s}}n_{\mathrm{H}}}\propto(Q_{0}n_{\mathrm{H}})^{\frac{1}{3}},

(2)

where
c
,
Q_{0}
, and
n_{\mathrm{H}}
are the speed of light, the hydrogen-ionizing photon production rate, and the hydrogen density, respectively, and
R_{\mathrm{s}}
is the Strömgren radius, which is proportional to
Q_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}n_{\mathrm{H}}^{-\frac{2}{3}}
.
Thus, we expect that the ionization parameter in the high-density region is higher than that in the low-density region, assuming the same hydrogen-ionizing photon production rates in the two regions.

To understand the effect of different ionization parameters on the metallicity estimates in the 2-zone modeling, we conduct the MCMC fitting again, allowing different O32 ratios in the high- and low-density regions with
\mathrm{O32}_{\mathrm{1}}<\mathrm{O32}_{\mathrm{2}}
, corresponding to different ionization parameters with
U_{\mathrm{ion,1}}<U_{\mathrm{ion,2}}
, where subscripts 1 and 2 refer to the low- and high-density regions, respectively, as in Section
V.3
.
We find that the obtained metallicities for J0217-0208 and COS-3018555981 are always higher than the best estimates in Section
V.3
, which assume the same ionization parameters in the two regions.

We can understand this result as follows.
If we assume a higher ionization parameter in the high-density region, the fraction of the
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 luminosity from the high-density region among the total luminosity becomes larger.
In contrast, the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 luminosity from the high-density region does not significantly change, as its total luminosity is already dominated by the emission from the high-density region.
As a result, the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363/
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 ratio becomes lower, and the inferred temperature in the high-density region becomes lower.
Similarly, the
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 luminosity from the low-density region becomes lower if we assume a lower ionization parameter in the low-density region.
The density in the low-density region does not significantly change because the FIR
[Oiii]
luminosities are dominated by emission from the low-density region.
Consequently, the
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 ratio becomes higher, resulting in a lower temperature in the low-density region.
Because the temperatures in both regions become lower, the obtained metallicity is higher than the estimate in Section
V.3
.

V.5.2
Effect of Different Metallicities

Next, we consider the effect of different metallicities in the low-density and high-density regions.
In the 2-zone model, the total metallicity (O/H) can be expressed as follows,

\mathrm{\frac{O}{H}=\frac{O(1)+O(2)}{H(1)+H(2)}},

(3)

where
\mathrm{O(1)}
and
\mathrm{H(1)}
(
\mathrm{O(2)}
and
\mathrm{H(2)}
) are the numbers of oxygen and hydrogen atoms, respectively, in the low-density (high-density) region.
By ignoring higher ionized ions such as
\mathrm{O^{3+}}
, the numerator,
\mathrm{O(1)+O(2)}
, is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{O(1)}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{O(2)}=\mathrm{O^{+}(1)+O^{2+}(1)+O^{+}(2)+O^{2+}(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,1}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{[OII],1}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OII]}}(T_{\mathrm{e,1}},n_{\mathrm{e,1}})}+\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,1}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,1}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007}}(T_{\mathrm{e,1}},n_{\mathrm{e,1}})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,2}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{[OII],2}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OII]}}(T_{\mathrm{e,2}},n_{\mathrm{e,2}})}+\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,2}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,2}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007}}(T_{\mathrm{e,2}},n_{\mathrm{e,2}})},

(4)

where
L_{\mathrm{[OII],1}}
and
L_{\mathrm{[OII],2}}
(
L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,1}}
and
L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,2}}
) are the predicted
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 (
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007) luminosities in the low- and high-density regions, and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OII]}}(T_{\mathrm{e}},n_{\mathrm{e}})
(
\epsilon_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007}}(T_{\mathrm{e}},n_{\mathrm{e}})
) is the emissivity of
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 (
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007) in units of
\mathrm{erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{3}}
.
As shown in Equation (
4
), the oxygen abundances in each region (
\mathrm{O^{+}(1)}
,
\mathrm{O^{2+}(1)}
,
\mathrm{O^{+}(2)}
,
\mathrm{O^{2+}(2)}
) depend on parameters other than metallicity (i.e.,
n_{\mathrm{e,1}}
,
n_{\mathrm{e,2}}
,
T_{\mathrm{e,1}}
,
T_{\mathrm{e,2}}
, O32, and
L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,1}}/L_{\mathrm{[OIII]5007,2}}
).
In the 2-zone model fitting, these parameters are effectively determined based on the observed luminosities of the
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
lines, and the metallicity is scaled to match the observed H
\beta
luminosity.
Thus, even if we change the metallicities in the low- and high-density regions from the current assumption of having the same metallicity, the oxygen abundances do not change.

The denominator,
\mathrm{H(1)+H(2)}
, is inversely scaled with metallicity and is expressed as

\mathrm{H(1)+H(2)}=\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,1}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{H\beta,1}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{H\beta}}(T_{\mathrm{e,1}})}+\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{e,2}}}\frac{L_{\mathrm{H\beta,2}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{H\beta}}(T_{\mathrm{e,2}})},

(5)

where
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,1}}
and
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,2}}
are the predicted H
\beta
luminosities in the low- and high-density regions, and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{H\beta}}(T)
is the emissivity of H
\beta
.
In the 2-zone model assuming the same metallicities in the low- and high-density regions,
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,1}}
and
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,2}}
are determined so that the metallicities in the two zones are equal (
\mathrm{O(1)/H(1)}=\mathrm{O(2)/H(2)}
).
To consider the effect of different metallicities, we discuss how the total hydrogen abundance changes if we change
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,1}}
and
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,2}}
while conserving the total observed H
\beta
luminosity (
L_{\mathrm{H\beta,1}}+L_{\mathrm{H\beta,2}}
).

and increase

)) in the low-density region is always lower than that in the high-density region (

As a result, if we decrease

VI
Discussion

VI.1
Evolution of the ISM Structure

In Sections
IV.3.3
and
IV.3.4
, we find that the densities derived from the optical
[Oii]
lines (
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}
) and the FIR
[Oiii]
lines (
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}
) are significantly different.
Furthermore, in Sections
V.2
and
V.3
, we show that the 2-zone model with low-density and high-density regions can self-consistently reproduce both the optical and FIR emission line luminosities, whereas the 1-zone model cannot.
Since such discrepancies between
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}
and
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}
are not observed in local galaxies, where the densities are low in both
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}
and
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}
, these results suggest an evolution in the ISM structure from low redshift to high redshift.
Here, we discuss two scenarios that can explain this evolution: (a) clumpy galaxies and (b) AGN activity.
Figure
17
shows schematic illustrations of these two scenarios.

(A)

Clumpy Galaxy.

Many studies report that high-redshift galaxies are clumpy, with the fraction of clumpy galaxies increasing from
z\sim 0
to higher redshifts.
For example,
Harikane et al. (2025)
discuss that
\gtrsim 60\%
of UV-bright galaxies at
z\sim 7
show clumpy structures extending up to
\sim 5
kpc.
Faint galaxies at
z\sim 5-7
also show a high fraction of clumpy morphologies
(
Asada et al. 2024
)
.

Fujimoto et al. (2024b)
report a lensed galaxy at
z=6
that contains
\geq 15
small (
\sim 10-50
pc) star-forming clumps, which cannot be resolved in NIRCam images without lensing magnification.
Among the galaxies at
z>6
discussed in this study, J1211-0118 (Figure
1
), COS-3018555981
(
Scholtz et al. 2024
)
, and MACS1149-JD1
(
Marconcini et al. 2024
;
Álvarez-Márquez et al. 2024
)
show multiple sub-components.
Although J0217-0208 and SXDF-NB1006-2 do not display clumpy morphologies in our NIRCam images, it is possible that they host star-forming clumps much smaller than the NIRCam resolution, similar to the lensed galaxy reported by
Fujimoto et al. (2024b)
.
The gas structure of these clumpy galaxies can be well described with the 2-zone model, where the gas in the star-forming clumps is dense, and the diffuse gas surrounding the clumps is less dense.
Thus, in this clumpy galaxy scenario, the FIR
[Oiii]
emission (
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m and
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m) arises from the low-density gas around the clumps, due to their low critical densities.
In contrast, the optical
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
emission lines (
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007,
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729, and
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363) are emitted from both the low-density gas and the high-density gas within the clumps, resulting in moderately high electron densities inferred from the
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 ratio (
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\sim 1000
cm
-3
).
Since we do not detect the low-density component suggested by
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}
in the density maps based on the
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 ratio for the four galaxies analyzed here (Figure
8
;
Marconcini et al. 2024
;
Scholtz et al. 2024
), the physical scales of the clumps may be much smaller than the spatial resolution of JWST.

(B)

AGN.

Recent JWST studies have found many high-redshift AGNs at
z>4
, with abundances higher than expected before the launch of JWST
(
Kocevski et al. 2023
;
Harikane et al. 2023b
;
Kokorev et al. 2023
;
Matthee et al. 2024
;
Maiolino et al. 2024
;
Mazzolari et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Although we do not observe any obvious AGN signatures, such as broad hydrogen Balmer lines, in the galaxies studied here, some of them (e.g., J0217-0208) may host type-2 AGNs given their luminous nature and strong emission lines
(
Laporte et al. 2017a
;
Zhu et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.
The electron densities of the narrow-line regions of AGNs and the outflowing gas driven by AGN activity are typically high,
\sim 10^{3}-10^{6}
cm
-3

(
Blandford et al. 1990
;
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
;
Netzer 2013
;
Kakkad et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
, which is comparable to the estimated density of the high-density region in the 2-zone model discussed in Section
V.3
.
Indeed, the Seyfert galaxy NGC1068 exhibits a discrepancy between
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}
and
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}
similar to that seen in galaxies at
z>6
(Figure
10
), and spatially resolved spectroscopic observations targeting NGC1068 reveal that its density reaches
\gtrsim 1000
cm
-3
around the nucleus
(
Ozaki 2009
;
Kakkad et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.
Thus, it is possible that some of the galaxies studied here host AGNs, where the dense gas around the AGN and the gas in the host galaxy correspond to the high-density and low-density regions in the 2-zone model, respectively.
In this AGN scenario, because the densities of the AGN narrow-line region and the outflowing gas are significantly higher than the critical densities of the FIR
[Oiii]
lines, most of the FIR
[Oiii]
emission originates from the host galaxy, which has a lower density (
\lesssim 1000
cm
-3
).
The optical
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
emission lines are contributed by both the dense gas around the AGN and the diffuse gas in the host galaxy, resulting in an electron density of
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\sim 1000
cm
-3
inferred from the
[Oii]
\lambda
3729/
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 ratio.
Even if the AGN narrow-line region has a very high density, the inferred density would still be around
\sim 1000-10000
cm
-3
due to the critical densities of
3300
and
14000
cm
-3
for
[Oii]
\lambda
3726 and
[Oii]
\lambda
3729, respectively.

These two scenarios can explain the evolution of electron densities discussed in Section
IV.3.4
, considering the redshift evolution of the clumpy galaxy fraction
(
Asada et al. 2024
;
Harikane et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
and AGN abundance
(
Harikane et al. 2023b
;
Maiolino et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
At
z\sim 0
,
although there are some clumpy dwarf galaxies
(
McQuinn et al. 2019
;
Marasco et al. 2023
;
Rowland et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, most galaxies are low-density, not clumpy, and AGNs are rare.

Indeed, the two local galaxies studied
Chen et al. (2023)
;
Chen et al. (2024)
show compact morphologies.

At higher redshifts, the increasing fractions of clumpy galaxies and AGNs cause the emission from high-density regions (star-forming clumps in clumpy galaxies or dense gas around AGNs) to become more dominant, resulting in higher densities probed by optical
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 or
[Sii]
\lambda\lambda
6716,6731 emission lines.
In contrast, the FIR
[Oiii]
lines are always tracing the low-density regions of galaxies due to their low critical densities, resulting in low densities of
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}\lesssim 1000
cm
-3
at all redshifts.

In addition to these two possibilities, other physical mechanisms could also contribute.
For example, porous, density-bounded
Hii
regions with high Lyman continuum escape have been proposed to explain both optical and FIR line properties
(
Ramambason et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.
Shocks, which can strongly affect the density structure of the ISM, may also play a role
(
Dopita 1976
;
Dopita 1977
;
Dopita et al. 1977
;
Contini & Aldrovandi 1983
;
Aldrovandi & Contini 1985
, e.g.,)
.
Moreover, diffuse ionized gas could influence both the observed line ratios and the inferred electron densities.
It could be possible that a combination of these processes shapes the observed discrepancies.

VI.3
JWST+ALMA Metallicity Estimator

In Section
V.4
, we find that the commonly used direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method sometimes significantly underestimates the metallicity compared to the fiducial value obtained with 2-zone modeling using the optical and FIR lines detected with JWST and ALMA.
Thus, if a galaxy is observed with JWST and ALMA, the most reliable way to estimate the metallicity is to conduct the 2-zone modeling described in Section
V.3
.
Here, we discuss an alternative method to estimate the metallicity using the JWST and ALMA lines.

In Figure
18
, we plot the emission line ratios of
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m/H
\beta
and
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
\lambda
5007 as a function of the metallicity obtained via the 2-zone modeling for the four galaxies analyzed here.
We find that these line ratios correlate well with the metallicity, and obtain the best-fitting relations as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{log\left(\frac{\textsc{[Oiii]}88\mu m}{H\beta}\right)}

\displaystyle=0.265+0.428x-0.209x^{2},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{log\left(\frac{\textsc{[Oiii]}88\mu m}{\textsc{[Oiii]}\lambda 5007}\right)}

\displaystyle=-0.509+0.376x-0.362x^{2},

(7)

where
x=12+\mathrm{log(O/H)}-8.69
.
Note that these relations cannot be well reproduced with a 1-zone photoionization model such as with
Cloudy

(
Ferland et al. 2017
)
, because the emission lines do not originate from a homogeneous 1-zone gas.
Although the parameters in these relations are not tightly constrained due to the limited number of galaxies with 2-zone metallicity estimates, these relations can serve as a guideline to determine whether the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method is underestimating the metallicity or not.
If a galaxy is observed with JWST and ALMA, one can compare the metallicity inferred from these relations with the metallicity obtained via the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method.
If the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
metallicity is significantly lower than that inferred from these relations, it is necessary to conduct 2-zone modeling to determine the true metallicity.
Future JWST and ALMA observations targeting a statistical sample of high-redshift galaxies will enable us to better constrain these relations.

VII
Summary

In this paper, we investigate the ISM properties of high-redshift galaxies in detail.
We use our new JWST and ALMA observations, as well as data from the literature, and conduct a joint JWST and ALMA analysis to self-consistently reproduce all of the observed emission lines.
Our major findings are summarized below:

1.

Our JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy targeting three galaxies at
z=6-7
successfully detects rest-frame optical emission lines such as
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729, H
\gamma
, H
\beta
, and
[Oiii]
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007 (Figure
2
).
We also identify the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 line in two galaxies (Figure
3
), and derive their metallicities to be
12+\mathrm{log(O/H)}=8.0-8.2
using the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
method.
The moderately high spectral resolutions of G235H and G395H allow us to measure the line ratios of the
[Oii]
\lambda\lambda
3726,3729 lines, resulting in an average electron density of
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\sim 1000
cm
-3
.
Dust
attenuation
measured from the Balmer decrement is low, with
E(B-V)\simeq 0.0-0.1
.

2.

Our new ALMA Band 9 and 10 observations detect the
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m line in one galaxy at
z=6.2
, but not in the other two galaxies (Figure
5
).
Using the density-sensitive
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m/
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m line ratios, we obtain the constraints of
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}\lesssim 500
cm
-3
, systematically lower than those measured with the optical
[Oii]
lines (Figure
10
).
Two additional galaxies at
z=7-9
also show a similar trend, where the densities measured with the FIR
[Oiii]
line are systematically lower than those from the optical
[Oii]
lines.

3.

We find that the electron densities of our galaxies measured with the optical
[Oii]
lines are consistent with the redshift evolution trend of increasing density toward higher redshift reported in previous studies (Figure
12
).
In contrast, the electron densities of galaxies at
z\sim 6-14
derived from the FIR
[Oiii]
lines are low and comparable to those at
z\sim 0
, showing no redshift evolution.
These contrasting evolution trends can be interpreted by scenarios involving an increasing fraction of clumpy galaxies or AGNs toward higher redshifts (Figure
17
).

4.

We conduct a joint JWST and ALMA analysis for four galaxies (two from our sample and two from the literature) well observed with both telescopes to constrain their ISM properties.
The 1-zone model, which assumes a homogeneous temperature and density derived from the JWST optical lines, fails to reproduce the ALMA-observed FIR
[Oiii]
line luminosities (Figure
13
), even when hypothetical heavily dust-obscured gas is considered.
Instead, we introduce a 2-zone model with high-density (
n_{\mathrm{e}}>1000
cm
-3
) and low-density (
n_{\mathrm{e}}<1000
cm
-3
) regions, which successfully reproduces all of the emission line luminosities observed with JWST and ALMA (Figure
14
).

5.

The best-fit 2-zone models for the four galaxies suggest that the FIR
[Oiii]
88
\mu
m and
[Oiii]
52
\mu
m luminosities are dominated by emission from the low-density region due to their low critical densities, resulting in the observed low densities (
n_{\mathrm{e,FIR}}\lesssim 500
cm
-3
) from the FIR
[Oiii]
lines.
In contrast, the optical
[Oiii]
and
[Oii]
emissions arise from both the low-density and high-density regions, leading to moderately high densities (
n_{\mathrm{e,optical}}\sim 1000
cm
-3
) derived from the optical
[Oii]
lines.

6.

We adopt the metallicities estimated with the 2-zone modeling as the fiducial values because the 2-zone model self-consistently reproduces both the optical and FIR lines, while the 1-zone model based on the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363-derived temperature does not.
Compared to the fiducial metallicities, the direct-
T_{\mathrm{e}}
metallicities are significantly underestimated up to 0.8 dex in two of the four galaxies analyzed in this study (Figure
15
), implying a steeper slope in the mass-metallicity relation.
In these two galaxies, the
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363 emission from the high-density and high-temperature regions dominates the observed luminosity due to its strong temperature dependence, resulting in an overestimation of the electron temperature based on
[Oiii]
\lambda
4363.

While based on a small sample, our results highlight the importance of combining optical and FIR emission lines for accurately determining the gas-phase metallicity of high-redshift galaxies, including those with little dust.
We provide strong-line metallicity calibrations using optical and FIR lines (Figure
18
), although they are currently constrained by only four galaxies.
Future JWST and ALMA observations targeting a larger sample of high-redshift galaxies, along with spatially resolved data, will enable more precise calibrations and a deeper understanding of the ISM properties in early galaxies.

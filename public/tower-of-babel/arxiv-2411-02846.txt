Abstract
. We establish interior W 2 , δ \displaystyle W^{2,\delta} type estimates for a class of degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations with L n \displaystyle L^{n} data. The main idea of our approach is to slide C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} cones, instead of paraboloids, vertically to touch the solution, and estimate the contact set in terms of the measure of the vertex set. This shows that the solution has tangent C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} cones almost everywhere, which leads to the desired Hessian estimates. Accordingly, we are able to develop a kind of counterpart to the estimates for divergent structure quasilinear elliptic problems, as discussed in [ 16 , 6 ] .

Introduction

In this paper, we study interior
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}
type estimates for a viscosity solution
\displaystyle u
of degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic inequalities

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}u)\leq f(x)\leq|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in}\ B_{1},

where
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
,
\displaystyle B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\displaystyle n\geq 2
is the unit ball,
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
are the Pucci extremal operators with ellipticity constants
\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
, and
\displaystyle f\in C\cap L^{n}(B_{1})
.

The inequalities (
1.1
) include the following important types of equations:

(1)

The degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)=f\quad\text{in}\ B_{1},

where
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
,
\displaystyle F
is uniformly elliptic, and
\displaystyle f\in C\cap L^{n}(B_{1})
.

(2)

The degenerate
\displaystyle p
-Laplacian equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u=\mathrm{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=f\quad\text{in}\ B_{1},

where
\displaystyle p=\gamma+2\geq 2
and
\displaystyle f\in C\cap L^{n}(B_{1})
.

There has been lots of progress in the regularity of these types of equations in recent years.
For (
1.2

[

\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}

\displaystyle f\in L^{\infty}
and Araújo, Ricarte, and Teixeira
[

proved optimal
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
estimates with
\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
when
\displaystyle F
is concave.

For the uniformly elliptic case that
\displaystyle\gamma=0
, Lin
[

first proved interior
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}
estimates for linear elliptic nondivergent equations, and Caffarelli and Cabré
[

obtained interior
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}

These
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}
estimates turned out to be helpful for deriving interior
\displaystyle W^{2,p}
estimates when
\displaystyle p>n
,
\displaystyle f\in L^{p}
and the homogeneous equation
\displaystyle F(D^{2}h)=0

For the treatment of a more general case, we refer the reader to

However, for the degenerate case
\displaystyle\gamma>0
, it is not easy to prove
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}
estimates because the equation degenerates and the Hessian
\displaystyle D^{2}u
can blow up when the gradient
\displaystyle Du

To the best of our knowledge, there are no results about the integrability of the Hessian of solutions of degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations.

On the other hand, for the degenerate

Since the approach in
[

\displaystyle u
of (

|Du|^{\gamma}Du\in W^{1,\delta}_{\loc}

for some universal constant
\displaystyle\delta>0
for a given
\displaystyle f\in L^{n}
.
Note that if
\displaystyle\gamma=0

\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}

The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
satisfy

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}u)\leq f(x)\leq|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in}\ B_{1}

in the viscosity sense, with
\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
,
\displaystyle\gamma\geq 0
and
\displaystyle f\in C\cap L^{n}(B_{1})
. Then
\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}Du\in W^{1,\delta}(B_{1/2})
with the estimate

\left\lVert|Du|^{\gamma}Du\right\rVert_{W^{1,\delta}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{1+\gamma}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(B_{1})}\right),

where
\displaystyle\delta>0
and
\displaystyle C>0
depend only on
\displaystyle n,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\displaystyle\gamma
.

Our proof is influenced by the proof of
\displaystyle W^{2,\delta}
estimates for the singular case by D. Li and Z. Li
[
35
]
, which uses localization, a covering argument, and a sliding paraboloid method.
Roughly speaking, the main idea of sliding paraboloid method is sliding paraboloids from below and above in order to touch the solution, thereby finding the lower bound of the measure of the set of touching points in terms of the measure of the set of vertex points using the area formula.
This method was first introduced by Cabré
[
13
]
and developed by Savin
[
40
]
.
For more results regarding this method, we refer the reader to
[
28
,
38
,
39
]
and
[
17
]
.
However, it seems that a paraboloid does not fit well with degenerate inequalities like (
1.1
) because of the following reasons.
First, the scaling of a paraboloid does not match well with the scaling of degenerate inequalities.
Second, the Hessian of a paraboloid is a constant no matter what the gradient is, while the Hessian of a solution can blow up when the gradient is small and so the equation degenerates.
Finally, there is a difficulty in using a paraboloid along with the area formula to estimate the measure of the set of touching points in terms of the measure of the set of vertex points due to the degeneracy of the inequalities.

The main originality of this paper is to slide a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone
\displaystyle P(x)=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}|x|^{1+\alpha}
where
\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
, instead of a paraboloid
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
, which enables us to solve the difficulty we mentioned above.
We remark first that the choice of
\displaystyle\alpha
matches with the scaling of a solution.
In the degenerate case, it is natural to consider the scaling
\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x)=\frac{u(rx)}{r^{1+\alpha}}
with
\displaystyle 0<r<1
, since
\displaystyle\tilde{u}
satisfies the same inequalities (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle f(x)
replaced by
\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x)=f(rx)
.
Notice that the same scaling of the
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone
\displaystyle\tilde{P}(x)=\frac{P(rx)}{r^{1+\alpha}}
is the same as the original
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone
\displaystyle P(x)
.
Next, observe that
\displaystyle P
satisfies
\displaystyle|DP|^{\gamma}\Delta P=c
for some constant
\displaystyle c
, therefore the Hessian of
\displaystyle P
blows up when the gradient of
\displaystyle P
is small.
Finally,
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones fit exactly with the degeneracy of the equation when using the area formula.
If we slide a concave
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone
\displaystyle P(x)=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}|x-y_{0}|^{1+\alpha}
with a vertex
\displaystyle y_{0}
, so that
\displaystyle P
touches a
\displaystyle C^{2}
function
\displaystyle u
from below at a touching point
\displaystyle x_{0}
, then we have the equation

y_{0}=x_{0}+|Du|^{\gamma}Du(x_{0}).

Differentiating the equation with respect to
\displaystyle x_{0}
and taking a trace, we find

\tr D_{x_{0}}y_{0}=n+\mathrm{div}(|Du|^{\gamma}Du)(x_{0})=n+\Delta_{\gamma+2}u(x_{0}).

Heuristically, if we assume that
\displaystyle u
is a classical solution of (
1.3
), we say that
\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma+2}u(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0})
.
By using a bound of
\displaystyle\tr D_{x_{0}}y_{0}
, we can use the area formula to obtain a lower bound of the measure of the set of touching points.
Note that since
\displaystyle P
mentioned above satisfies
\displaystyle D(|DP|^{\gamma}DP)=cI
where
\displaystyle I
is the identity matrix, it is possible to bound
\displaystyle D(|Du|^{\gamma}Du)
at the point where
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones touches
\displaystyle u
from below and above.

However, unlike paraboloids,
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones cause some difficulties.
First, the growth and degeneracy of a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone is different from that of a paraboloid. The Hessian of a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone blows up at the vertex point and diminishes at a point far from the vertex point, which makes us delicately consider the distance between the vertex point and the touching point.
Therefore, in the proof of the key lemma, Lemma
4.1
, we will divide it into three cases by the distance.
Second, the sum and difference of two
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones are not a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone, and the sum of a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone and a linear function is not a
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone, either.
This disadvantage makes it difficult to analyze the difference of
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones in Step 2 of the key lemma. Thus, we will instead consider the Hessian of its difference and approximate it by some function which is relatively easier to analyze.

For any point
\displaystyle x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
, we consider the pointwise
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
seminorm at
\displaystyle x_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(x_{0}):=\sup_{r>0}\inf_{l\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\frac{\left\lVert u-l\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}}{r^{1+\alpha}}.

Note that if
\displaystyle\left\lVert[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq c
, then
\displaystyle u\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1/2})
with
\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq c
.
If there exist
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones of the opening
\displaystyle K
touching
\displaystyle u
at
\displaystyle x_{0}
from below and above, then
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(x_{0})\leq cK
for some constant
\displaystyle c=c(n,\alpha)
.
Thus, if we prove the power decay of the measure of the set of points which do not have touching
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cones, then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
, the point-wise
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
norm of
\displaystyle u
is integrable, in the sense that
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)\in L^{\delta}(B_{1})
where
\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
.

In particular,
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)
is almost everywhere finite, which implies that
\displaystyle u
is almost everywhere
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
.

The authors of
[
4
]
proved that if
\displaystyle u
is a solution of (
1.2
) and
\displaystyle F
is concave, then
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}
whose norm depends on
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
data of
\displaystyle f
.
Moreover, the authors of
[
3
]
obtained that if
\displaystyle u
is a solution of (
1.3
) in the plane, then
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}
whose norm depends on
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
data of
\displaystyle f
.
However, this corollary implies that if
\displaystyle u
is a solution of (
1.1
), we have
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(\cdot)\in L^{\delta}
whose norm depends on
\displaystyle L^{n}
data of
\displaystyle f
.
Especially, even for the singular set
\displaystyle\{Du=0\}
where the equation degenerates, we know that almost every singular point has a tangent
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone and that the point-wise
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
norm is finite.

In Section

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we denote by
\displaystyle B_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}

\displaystyle B_{r}=B_{r}(0)

\displaystyle S(n)

\displaystyle n\times n

\displaystyle I

For given two functions
\displaystyle u,v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle x_{0}\in\Omega

\displaystyle u\overset{x_{0}}{\leq}v
in
\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle u(x)\leq v(x)
in
\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})
.

For
\displaystyle 0<\alpha\leq 1

\displaystyle P
is a concave
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone of opening
\displaystyle K>0
and vertex
\displaystyle y_{0}
if

P(x)=-\frac{K}{1+\alpha}|x-y_{0}|^{1+\alpha}+C

for some constant
\displaystyle C
.

Now we give the definition of the set of touching points. For a given continuous function
\displaystyle u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a closed set
\displaystyle V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we slide a concave
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone of opening
\displaystyle K>0
and vertex
\displaystyle y_{0}\in V
from below the graph of
\displaystyle u
until it touches the graph of
\displaystyle u
first time.
Then we define
\displaystyle T_{K}^{-}(u,V)
to be the set of touching points.
We also define
\displaystyle T_{K}^{+}(u,V)
to be the set of points where
\displaystyle u
touches with a convex
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
cone with vertex included in
\displaystyle V
from above.

Definition 2.1
.

For a given continuous function
\displaystyle u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, a closed set
\displaystyle V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\displaystyle K>0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{K}^{-}(u,V):=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{x_{0}\in\Omega:\exists P\text{ concave $\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}$ cone of opening $\displaystyle K$ whose vertex is in $\displaystyle V$ such that $\displaystyle P(x)\overset{x_{0}}{\leq}u(x)$ in $\displaystyle\Omega$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{x_{0}\in\Omega:\exists y_{0}\in V\text{ such that }-\frac{K}{1+\alpha}|x-y_{0}|^{1+\alpha}+\left(u(x_{0})+\frac{K}{1+\alpha}|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{1+\alpha}\right)\overset{x_{0}}{\leq}u(x)\text{ in }\Omega\right\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{K}^{+}(u,V):=T_{K}^{-}(-u,V),

\displaystyle\displaystyle T_{K}(u,V):=T_{K}^{-}(u,V)\cap T_{K}^{+}(u,V).

We shorten
\displaystyle T_{K}^{\pm}=T_{K}^{\pm}(u,\overline{\Omega})
when they are clear in the context.
Observe that
\displaystyle T_{K}^{\pm}
are closed in
\displaystyle\Omega
and that if
\displaystyle x_{0}\in T_{K}
, then
\displaystyle[u]_{C^{1,\alpha}}(x_{0})\leq cK
for some
\displaystyle c=c(\alpha)
.

For given
\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
, we recall the definition of the Pucci extremal operators
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}:S(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows (see

14

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M):=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}(M)<0}e_{i}(M)+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}(M)>0}e_{i}(M),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M):=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}(M)<0}e_{i}(M)+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}(M)>0}e_{i}(M),

where
\displaystyle M\in S(n)
and
\displaystyle e_{i}(M)
’s are the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle M

We remark the following properties of the Pucci extremal operators:

(1)

For every
\displaystyle M,N\in S(n)
,
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}(M)+\mathcal{P}^{-}(N)\leq\mathcal{P}^{-}(M+N)\leq\mathcal{P}^{-}(M)+\mathcal{P}^{+}(N)
,

(2)

If
\displaystyle M\geq 0
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}(M)=\lambda\tr M
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}(M)=\Lambda\tr M
.

) in the viscosity sense from

]

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in C(B_{1})
.
We say that
\displaystyle u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})

\displaystyle\psi\in C^{2}(B_{1})

\displaystyle x_{0}

For
\displaystyle g\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we define the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(g)(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}|g(y)|\,dy.

Then the maximal function is scaling invariant, which means

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(g_{r})(x)=\mathcal{M}(g)(rx),

where
\displaystyle g_{r}(x)=g(rx)

\displaystyle g
.
Moreover, the maximal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
has the weak type
\displaystyle(1,1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\{x\in\Omega:\mathcal{M}(g)(x)>t\}|\leq Ct^{-1}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\ \,\forall t>0.

We will also use an equivalent description of
\displaystyle L^{p}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
14

Let
\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle\eta>0
,
\displaystyle M>1

\displaystyle 0<p<\infty

g\in L^{p}(\Omega)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ S=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}M^{pk}|\{x\in\Omega:g(x)>\eta M^{k}\}|<\infty

We introduce the following Vitali-type covering lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[

\displaystyle 0<\theta<\Theta<1

\displaystyle|E|>\theta|B_{1}|

Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\setminus F|\leq\left(1-\frac{\Theta-\theta}{5^{n}}\right)|B_{1}\setminus E|.

Finally, note that it suffices to assume that
\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1/16

\displaystyle\delta=\delta(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)>0

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)>0

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(B_{2})}\leq\epsilon_{1}

We prove Theorem

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\displaystyle a=16\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}+\left(\epsilon_{1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(B_{2})}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x):=\frac{1}{a}u(x)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x):=\frac{1}{a^{1+\gamma}}f(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|D\tilde{u}|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}\tilde{u})\leq\tilde{f}\leq|D\tilde{u}|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{u}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}\leq 1/16

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert|Du|^{\gamma}Du\right\rVert_{W^{1,\delta}(B_{1})}\leq Ca^{1+\gamma}\leq C\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}^{1+\gamma}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(B_{2})}+\epsilon^{1+\gamma}\right),

\displaystyle\epsilon\rightarrow 0

3.
The relation between a tangent
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}

\displaystyle D(|Du|^{\gamma}Du

Thus, for any
\displaystyle z\in\Omega
and
\displaystyle x\in T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(z)\geq P^{\epsilon}(z-y)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq P^{\epsilon}(x-y)+\langle DP^{\epsilon}(x-y),z-x\rangle-CM\epsilon^{-1}|z-x|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=u(x)+\langle Du(x),z-x\rangle-CM\epsilon^{-1}|z-x|^{2}

since
\displaystyle|D^{2}P(z)|\leq CM\epsilon^{-1}
and
\displaystyle Du(x)=DP^{\epsilon}(x-y)
.
For any
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}\in T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)
, we set
\displaystyle r:=2|x_{1}-x_{2}|
.
For any
\displaystyle z\in B_{r}(x_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(z)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq u(x_{1})+\langle Du(x_{1}),z-x_{1}\rangle-CM\epsilon^{-1}|z-x_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq u(x_{2})+\langle Du(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle-CM\epsilon^{-1}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+\langle Du(x_{1}),z-x_{1}\rangle-CM\epsilon^{-1}|z-x_{1}|^{2}.

By the semi-concavity of
\displaystyle u
, there is a universal constant
\displaystyle\tilde{C}>0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(z)\leq u(x_{2})+\langle Du(x_{2}),z-x_{2}\rangle+\tilde{C}|z-x_{2}|^{2}.

Therefore, for any
\displaystyle z\in B_{r}(x_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle Du(x_{1})-Du(x_{2}),z-x_{1}\rangle\leq C(\tilde{C}+M\epsilon^{-1})r^{2}.

By taking
\displaystyle z
such that
\displaystyle z-x
is parallel to
\displaystyle Du(x_{1})-Du(x_{2})
and
\displaystyle|z-x|=r/2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du(x_{1})-Du(x_{2})|\leq C(\tilde{C}+M\epsilon^{-1})|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

which implies that
\displaystyle Du
is Lipschitz on
\displaystyle T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)
.
By Rademacher theorem,
\displaystyle u
is second order differentiable almost everywhere on
\displaystyle T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)
.
Note that for
\displaystyle x\in T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)
, we get
\displaystyle M^{-1-\gamma}|Du(x)|^{1+\gamma}=|y-x|>0
, which implies
\displaystyle|Du(x)|>0
.

We differentiate (
4.19
) with respect to
\displaystyle x
to find

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{x}y

\displaystyle\displaystyle=I+\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}D(|Du|^{\gamma}Du)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=I+\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}\left(I+\gamma\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right)D^{2}u(x).

Note that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|DP|^{\gamma}DP(z)=-z,\ \forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}D(|DP|^{\gamma}DP)(x-y)=\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|DP|^{\gamma}\left(I+\gamma\frac{DP}{|DP|}\otimes\frac{DP}{|DP|}\right)D^{2}P(x-y)=-I.

Since
\displaystyle Du(x)=DP(x-y)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{x}y=\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}\left(I+\gamma\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right)(D^{2}u(x)-D^{2}P(x-y)).

Since
\displaystyle\det\left(I+\gamma\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right)=1+\gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\det(D_{x}y)=(1+\gamma)\det\left(\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}(D^{2}u(x)-D^{2}P(x-y))\right)=:(1+\gamma)\det(A).

Observe that

\displaystyle\displaystyle D^{2}u(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq D^{2}P(x-y)=-\frac{M}{|x-y|^{1-\alpha}}\left(I+(1-\alpha)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\otimes\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right).

Using (
4.19
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle D^{2}P(x-y)=-\frac{M^{1+\gamma}}{|Du|^{\gamma}}\left(I+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}(D^{2}u(x)-D^{2}P(x-y))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}D^{2}u+\left(I+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right).

Note that
\displaystyle A\geq 0
since
\displaystyle D^{2}u(x)\geq D^{2}P(x-y)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda\tr A

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{-}(A)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}^{-}(D^{2}u(x))+\mathcal{P}^{+}\left(I+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\frac{Du}{|Du|}\otimes\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{1+\gamma}}f(x)+\Lambda\left(n+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\right),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\det A

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\tr A}{n}\right)^{n}\leq C\left(1+\frac{|f(x)|^{n}}{M^{(1+\gamma)n}}\right).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leq\det(D_{x}y)\leq C\left(1+\frac{|f(x)|^{n}}{M^{(1+\gamma)n}}\right).

Consequently, by applying the area formula to the mapping
\displaystyle m:T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)\rightarrow V^{\epsilon}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|V^{\epsilon}|\leq\int_{T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)}\det(D_{x}y)\,dx\leq C|T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)|+\frac{C}{M^{(1+\gamma)n}}\int_{T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)}|f(x)|^{n}\,dx.

Since we proved at
Step 2
that
\displaystyle T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)\subset T^{-}_{M}(V)\subset B_{1}\subset B_{2}(x_{1})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|V^{\epsilon}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C|T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)|+\frac{C}{M^{(1+\gamma)n}}\int_{B_{2}(x_{1})}|f(x)|^{n}\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C|T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)|+\frac{C}{M^{(1+\gamma)n}}\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C|T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)|+C\epsilon_{2},

where
\displaystyle C
is independent of
\displaystyle\epsilon
. By the continuity of the Lebesgue measure, letting
\displaystyle\epsilon\rightarrow 0
gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle|V^{0+}|=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}|V^{\epsilon}|\leq C\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}|T^{-,\epsilon}_{M}(V)|+C\epsilon_{2}=C|T^{-,0+}_{M}(V)|+C\epsilon_{2}.

Recalling that
\displaystyle V^{0}=T^{-,0}_{M}(V)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|V|\leq|V^{0}|+|V^{0+}|\leq|T^{-,0}_{M}(V)|+C|T^{-,0+}_{M}(V)|+C\epsilon_{2}\leq C|T^{-}_{M}(V)|+C\epsilon_{2}.

Choosing
\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}>0
so small that
\displaystyle C\epsilon_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\min\{|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|,|V_{3}|\}\leq\frac{1}{2}|V|
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|V|\leq C|T^{-}_{M}(V)|,

which proves (
4.18
).

Then we finally conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{M}|\geq|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{M_{i}}|\geq|T^{-}_{M_{i}}(V_{i})|\geq\frac{1}{C}|V_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{C}\min\{|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|,|V_{3}|\}|B_{1}|=\Theta|B_{1}|,

which proves the lemma by taking
\displaystyle M=\max\{M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\}
,
\displaystyle\Theta=\frac{1}{C}\min\{|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|,|V_{3}|\}
and
\displaystyle\theta=\frac{1}{2}\min(\theta_{0},\Theta)<1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a universal constant
\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
so that for any
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\epsilon)>0
such that if
\displaystyle u\in C(B_{2})
satisfies

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du|^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{2}u)\leq f\quad\text{in}\ B_{2}

in the viscosity sense with
\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(B_{2})}\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
\displaystyle\osc_{B_{2}}u\leq 1/8
, then

|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{1}\cap\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)\leq\epsilon\}|\geq\theta_{0}|B_{1}|.

Proof.

For each
\displaystyle\tilde{x}\in T^{-}_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})
, there exists
\displaystyle\tilde{y}\in\overline{B_{1/2}}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}|x-\tilde{y}|^{1+\alpha}+C(\tilde{y})\overset{\tilde{x}}{\leq}u(x)\quad\text{in }B_{2}.

Note that
\displaystyle C(\tilde{y})\leq u(\tilde{y})
and
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|^{1+\alpha}+C(\tilde{y})=u(\tilde{x})
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha}|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|^{1+\alpha}\leq u(\tilde{y})-u(\tilde{x})\leq\osc_{B_{2}}u\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|\leq 1/2
, thus
\displaystyle\tilde{x}\in B_{1}
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle T^{-}_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})\subset B_{1}.

Recall that in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, the mapping
\displaystyle T^{-}_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})\ni\tilde{x}\mapsto\tilde{y}\in\overline{B_{1/2}}
is precisely given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{y}=\tilde{x}+|Du|^{\gamma}Du(\tilde{x}).

As Step 3 in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leq\det(D_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{y})\leq C(1+|f(x)|^{n}).

Since
\displaystyle T^{-}_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})\subset B_{1}
, it follows from the area formula that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2^{-n}|B_{1}|=|B_{1/2}|\leq C_{0}|T_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})|+C_{0}\int_{B_{1}}|f|^{n}\,dx

for some constant
\displaystyle C_{0}>1
.
Let
\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{n}\leq 2^{-(n+1)}|B_{1}|C_{0}^{-1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{1}|\geq|T^{-}_{1}(\overline{B_{1/2}})|\geq 2^{-(n+1)}C_{0}^{-1}|B_{1}|=:2\theta_{0}|B_{1}|.

By the weak type
\displaystyle(1,1)
property, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)>\epsilon\}|\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{n}_{L^{n}(B_{1})}\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-1}\epsilon_{1}^{n}\leq\theta_{0}|B_{1}|

by choosing
\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}>0
so small that
\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{n}\leq\min\{2^{-(n+1)}|B_{1}|C_{0}^{-1},\theta_{0}|B_{1}|C_{1}^{-1}\epsilon\}
.
Consequently, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{1}\cap\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)\leq\epsilon\}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq|B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{1}|-|\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)>\epsilon\}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\theta_{0}|B_{1}|,

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the assumptions and conclusions of Lemma
4.2
, we can find universal constants
\displaystyle\mu_{0}\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle M>1
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\setminus F_{k}|\leq\mu_{0}|B_{1}\setminus E_{k}|

holds for all
\displaystyle k=0,1,2,...
, where

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=B_{1}\cap T_{M^{k}}\cap\left\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)\leq\epsilon M^{k(1+\gamma)n}\right\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=B_{1}\cap T_{M^{k+1}}.

Proof.

We will apply Lemma
2.2
.
Let
\displaystyle\theta_{0}>0
be the universal constant given in Lemma
4.2
.
We choose
\displaystyle\epsilon=\epsilon(\theta_{0})
,
\displaystyle M=M(\theta_{0})
,
\displaystyle\theta=\theta(\theta_{0})
and universal
\displaystyle\Theta
such that
\displaystyle\theta<\min{(\theta_{0},\Theta)}
in Lemma
4.1
, and then choose
\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(\epsilon)
in Lemma
4.2
.

Since
\displaystyle E_{k}\supset B_{1}\cap T^{-}_{1}\cap\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)\leq\epsilon\}

\displaystyle|E_{k}|\geq\theta_{0}|B_{1}|>\theta|B_{1}|

\displaystyle|B\cap F_{k}|\geq\Theta|B|

To prove the claim, we consider the scaled function

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{u}(y)=\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}M^{k}}u(ry+x_{0}),

\displaystyle B_{2}\subset\Omega

For any

\displaystyle\displaystyle x\in B\cap F_{k}=B\cap T_{M^{k+1}}(u)\

Consequently, we deduce that if
\displaystyle|B\cap E_{k}|\geq\theta|B|
, then
\displaystyle|B\cap F_{k}|\geq\Theta|B|
,
We then conclude from Lemma

that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\setminus F_{k}|\leq\left(1-\frac{\Theta-\theta}{5^{n}}\right)|B_{1}\setminus E_{k}|=:\mu_{0}|B_{1}\setminus E_{k}|,

Corollary 4.1
.

Under the assumptions and conclusions of Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{1}\setminus T_{M^{k}}|\leq C\mu^{k},

where
\displaystyle C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)>0
,
\displaystyle 0<\mu=\mu(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)<1
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|B_{1}\setminus T_{M^{k}}|,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\{x\in B_{1}:\mathcal{M}(|f|^{n})(x)\geq\epsilon M^{k(1+\gamma)n}\}|.

Applying Lemma
4.3
, we get
\displaystyle\alpha_{k+1}\leq\mu_{0}(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\leq\mu_{0}^{k}|B_{1}|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mu_{0}^{k-i}\beta_{i}.

By the weak type
\displaystyle(1,1)
property for the maximal operator, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta_{k}\leq C(\epsilon_{2}M^{k(1+\gamma)n})^{-1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{n}_{L^{n}(B_{1})}\leq CM^{-k(1+\gamma)n}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mu_{0}^{k-i}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mu_{0}^{k-i}M^{-k(1+\gamma)ni}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mu_{1}^{k}\leq Ck\mu_{1}^{k}

with
\displaystyle\mu_{1}=\max(\mu_{0},M^{-(1+\gamma)n})\in(0,1).
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\leq\mu_{0}^{k}|B_{1}|+Ck\mu_{1}^{k}\leq C\mu^{k},

where
\displaystyle\mu:=\frac{1+\mu_{1}}{2}\in(\mu_{1},1)
, for some constant
\displaystyle C
depending only on
\displaystyle n,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\displaystyle\gamma
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
3.1
using Corollary
4.1
.

and

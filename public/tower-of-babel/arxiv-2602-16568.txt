Separating Oblivious and Adaptive Models of Variable Selection
Ziyun Chen, Jerry Li, Kevin Tian, Yusong Zhu

Abstract
Sparse recovery is among the most well-studied problems in learning theory and high-dimensional statistics. In this work, we investigate the statistical and computational landscapes of sparse recovery with ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} error guarantees. This variant of the problem is motivated by variable selection tasks, where the goal is to estimate the support of a k k -sparse signal in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Our main contribution is a provable separation between the oblivious (“for each”) and adaptive (“for all”) models of ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} sparse recovery. We show that under an oblivious model, the optimal ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} error is attainable in near-linear time with ≈ k ​ log ⁡ d \approx k\log d samples, whereas in an adaptive model, ≳ k 2 \gtrsim k^{2} samples are necessary for any algorithm to achieve this bound. This establishes a surprising contrast with the standard ℓ 2 \ell_{2} setting, where ≈ k ​ log ⁡ d \approx k\log d samples suffice even for adaptive sparse recovery. We conclude with a preliminary examination of a partially-adaptive model, where we show nontrivial variable selection guarantees are possible with ≈ k ​ log ⁡ d \approx k\log d measurements.

Relatedly, can we understand the sample complexity of variable selection for sparse linear regression?

Adaptivity matters for

A new, canonical choice of error metric.

For sparse recovery in the

1.1
Problem statements

We now formally define the problems we study in this paper.
Throughout this introduction, we primarily consider the standard setting where
{\mathbf{X}}
has i.i.d. entries
\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\frac{1}{n})

With this, we now state the variable selection problem we study.

Problem 1

(Variable selection)
.

Let
(n,d)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and

{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

Problem

Next, we formally define the problem of sparse recovery with

Problem 2

(
\ell_{\infty}

.

Let
(n,d)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and
k\in[d]
. Let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be a known measurement matrix from Model

(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty},\quad\mathrm{nnz}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\leq k.

To solve an instance of Problem

We next define various modeling assumptions used when studying Problem
2
.
As mentioned, a key conceptual contribution of our paper is that the modeling assumptions play an important role in characterizing the statistical complexity of
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery.

Model 1

(Oblivious model)
.

In the
oblivious model
,
(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})
are chosen independently of
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Model 2

(Adaptive model)
.

In the
adaptive model
, we make no independence assumptions on the triple
(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi},{\mathbf{X}})
.

In Model
1
, an equivalent viewpoint is that
(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})
are first fixed (possibly as samples from a distribution), and then
{\mathbf{X}}
is independently sampled. In Model
2
, the order is intuitively reversed: first
{\mathbf{X}}
is sampled, and then
(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})
can be arbitrarily defined depending on its outcome.

An algorithm succeeding under Model
2
is powerful: it can be used in arbitrary adaptively-defined instances of Problem
2
, with the same
{\mathbf{X}}
. This is particularly useful when Problem
2
is used as a subroutine, e.g., in hyperparameter search or a wrapper optimization algorithm involving
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Understanding Problem
2
under Models
1
and
2
is our main focus, but there are various more fine-grained independence assumptions one could impose. For example, Section
5
investigates a
partially-adaptive model
, where only the noise is viewed as benign.

1.2
Our results

Oblivious sparse recovery.

Our first main result is a new algorithm for
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery in the oblivious model (Model
1
). We show that it is possible to solve Problem
2
in this setting, with a number of samples matching that required for optimal
\ell_{2}
sparse recovery.

Theorem 1

(informal, see Theorem
3
)
.

Let
n=\Omega(k\log d)
, and let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
have i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{n})
entries.
There is an estimator which solves Problem
2
under Model
1
with high probability.
Moreover, the estimator can be computed in nearly-linear time.

We prove Theorem
3
through a simple three-stage method (Algorithm
2
). Our algorithm first uses iterative hard thresholding (IHT)
[
6
]
, an
\ell_{2}
sparse recovery algorithm, to obtain a warm start. It then estimates the support via thresholding, and solves ordinary least squares on the learned support.
For general sub-Gaussian measurements, the error of Theorem
1
can exceed
\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}
, but by at most a logarithmic factor; we give a detailed discussion in Sections
3.3
and
3.4
.

To our knowledge, this is the first such estimator which achieves these guarantees for
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery which runs in nearly-linear time.
3
3

3

Nearly-linear time methods have also been developed based on decoding expander graph-based measurements, but typically require explicit design of the measurement matrix. Moreover, these results often operate in the noiseless setting, or only give

recovery under a minimum signal strength assumption; see Section

We note that qualitatively similar guarantees were known for classical estimators such as LASSO

Finally, Theorem

Adaptive sparse recovery.

Our second main result is a new set of nearly-matching upper and lower bounds for the adaptive model (Model

Theorem 2

(informal, see Theorems

Let
n=\Omega(k\log d)

{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

There is an estimator which solves Problem

Up to a logarithmic factor of the dimension, Theorem

Notably, the sample complexity of both parts of Theorem

To our knowledge, no such separation had been previously demonstrated in any similar setting.
We conjecture that the lower bound in Theorem

n=o(k^{2}\log\frac{d}{k})

The upper bound in Theorem

The more technically interesting part of Theorem

is the lower bound.
At a high level, we demonstrate that the

of the Gram matrix

restricted to any sufficiently small submatrix has a large

n=\Omega(k^{2})
(Lemma

We then exhibit a sparse noise vector which has a very large
\ell_{\infty}
norm, but which is effectively killed off by the measurements

as long as
n=o(k^{2})
, and hence cannot be detected unless we have sufficiently many measurements.

Variable selection with partial adaptivity.

A natural question is whether or not one can circumvent the quadratic lower bounds of the adaptive model in an intermediate setting which interpolates between Models
1
and
2
.
Towards understanding this possibility, we demonstrate nontrivial recovery guarantees for variable selection (Problem
1
), in a model we call the
partially adaptive
model, where the noise
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is independent of
{\mathbf{X}}
, but
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
may be adaptive (Model
4
).

We demonstrate in Theorem
7
that if the learner is allowed to mask the effect of certain coordinates when querying observations (Algorithm
4
), then
\approx k\log d\log k
measurements suffice to solve variable selection under partial adaptivity. Our algorithm iteratively applies thresholding after masking an estimated support, which we show makes geometric progress on the residual. Although our result holds under a nonstandard observation model, we believe it highlights the possibility of going beyond Theorem
2
even under partial adaptivity. Indeed, a key structural result that we leverage in our algorithm is that threshold-based support learning has few false positives under Model
4
; we believe this observation will prove useful in future investigations of the partially-adaptive setting.

for all

Now applying a union bound over

which further implies (

Control of

Conditioned on

Since

{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\sim\textup{subG}(0,\frac{C}{n}\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}\right|>t\right]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{nt^{2}}{2C\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{nt^{2}}{32kC\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{S:}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}}\right).

Taking

and applying a union bound over
i\in S

\mathbb{P}\left[\exists i:\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}\right|>\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{S:}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right]\leq\frac{\delta}{4},

for sufficiently large

. Conditioning on all four random events used so far in the proof gives the failure probability. Now letting
j\in S
be the index that achieves

\displaystyle\frac{\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\right|}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{P}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}_{j}\right|}{{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{P}_{j}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}\geq\frac{2}{3}\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}_{j}\right|\geq\frac{1}{6}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{S:}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty},

\displaystyle\frac{\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{v}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\right|}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{P}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}

3.2
Support identification

We proceed to analyze the support identification step in Line
2
of Algorithm
2
.
Direct calculation shows that in Problem
2
, for any
i\in\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})
and
j\in\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})^{c}
, we have

{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}=\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}+\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{X}}_{:-i}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{-i}\right\rangle+{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi},\quad{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}=\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{j},{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle+{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}.

(9)

Our goal is to separate such a pair
(i,j)
, whenever
|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}|\gg\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}
is large (intuitively, a “heavy” coordinate of the support). We obtain signal from
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}\gg\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}
, and
the pure noise terms
|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}|
,
|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}|
are trivially bounded by
\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Thus, our goal is to ensure the leftover terms,
\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{X}}_{:-i}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{-i}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{j},{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle
, are small in magnitude. If we can ensure this holds, then a simple thresholding operation on
{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}
will separate large coordinates of
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
from zero coordinates.

We now formalize this idea, crucially leveraging independence of
{\mathbf{X}}
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
n=\Omega(k\log\frac{d}{\delta})
for an appropriate constant, and
k\in[d]
. Under Model
3
, suppose that
L:=\{i\in[d]:|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}|\geq r_{\infty}\}
, for

r_{\infty}\geq\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}+3\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Then the following hold with probability
\geq 1-\delta
:

L\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}),\quad\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L^{c}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 4r_{\infty}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, denote
S:=\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})
. We claim both of the following hold, with probability at least
1-\delta
:
{\mathbf{X}}
is
(\frac{1}{2},1)
-RIP, and

\max\left\{\max_{i\in S}\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{X}}_{:-i}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{-i}\right\rangle,\;\max_{j\in S^{c}}\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{j},{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle\right\}\leq\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\rVert_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}.

To see that this is correct, RIP holds with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{2}
by Proposition
1
, which implies all
i\in[d]
have
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. Hence, because
(i,j)\in S\times S^{c}
satisfy
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\perp{\mathbf{X}}_{:-i}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{-i}
, and
{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\perp{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
, applying Corollary
1
with failure probability
\delta\leftarrow\frac{\delta}{2d}
gives the bound for large enough
n
.

Condition on the above events holding henceforth.
Now recalling the decomposition (
9
),

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}|\leq\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\text{ for all }j\not\in S.

Finally, supposing some
i\in[d]
with
|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}|>4r_{\infty}
is not contained in
L
gives a contradiction:

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}|>\frac{1}{2}\left|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}\right|-\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}{\sqrt{k}}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\geq r_{\infty}.

∎

3.3
In-support estimation

Once the large coordinates of
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
are isolated, the remaining task reduces to a low-dimensional regression problem restricted to the recovered support. As our main helper lemma, we show that running ordinary least squares (OLS) on the learned support directly gives us accurate estimation in the
\ell_{\infty}
norm, by crucially leveraging independence in Model
1
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
n=\Omega(k\log\frac{d}{\delta})
for an appropriate constant, and
k\in[d]
. Under Models
1
and
3
, suppose that
L\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})
, and that
L\perp{\mathbf{X}}
. Then with probability
\geq 1-\delta
,

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L}-[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}]^{-1}_{L\times L}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{L:}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 8\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}.

Proof.

Because we assumed
L\subseteq S:=\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})
, we can decompose
{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}={\mathbf{X}}_{:L}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}+{\mathbf{X}}_{:L^{c}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L^{c}}
, so the OLS solution also admits the following decomposition:

[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}]^{-1}_{L\times L}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{L:}{\mathbf{y}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L}+[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}]^{-1}_{L\times L}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{L:}{\mathbf{X}}_{:L^{c}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L^{c}}+[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}]^{-1}_{L\times L}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{L:}\boldsymbol{\xi}.

In the remainder of the proof we bound the last two terms on the right-hand side. First, condition on
{\mathbf{X}}
satisfying
(\frac{1}{2},k)
-RIP, with fails with probability
\frac{\delta}{3}
by Proposition
1
. Next, letting
{\mathbf{w}}:={\mathbf{X}}_{L^{c}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{L^{c}}
, we have by RIP that
\left\lVert{\mathbf{w}}\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\rVert_{2}
, and
(L,{\mathbf{w}})\perp{\mathbf{X}}_{:L}
by assumption. Thus, by Corollary
2
,

\left\lVert\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]_{L\times L}^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}_{L:}^{\top}{\mathbf{w}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 8\left\lVert{\mathbf{w}}\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{C\log\frac{d}{\delta}}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2},

except with probability
\frac{\delta}{3}
, by our choice of
n
. Finally, again by Corollary
2
,

\left\lVert\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]_{L\times L}^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}_{L:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 8\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{L:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 8\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty},

except with probability
\frac{\delta}{3}
, because
\boldsymbol{\xi}\perp{\mathbf{X}}
. The conclusion follows from combining the above two displays and applying a union bound for the failure probability.
∎

{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\perp{\mathbf{X}}_{:T_{j}}
, the second term satisfies the same sub-Gaussian bound as above, hence

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{j},{\mathbf{X}}_{:T_{j}}{\mathbf{v}}_{-j}\right\rangle\right|>\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right]\leq\frac{\delta}{2d},\text{ for all }j\in S.

(15)

By a union bound over all
d
events in (
14
), (
15
), and using
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{5}{4}
for all
j\in S
, we then have

\max_{i\not\in S}\left|\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{v}}\right]_{i}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty},\quad\max_{j\in S}\left|\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{v}}\right]_{j}\right|\leq\frac{3}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty},

which yields the claimed upper bound.
For the lower bound, let
j^{\star}\in S
satisfy
|{\mathbf{v}}_{j^{\star}}|=\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{\infty}
. Using
\|{\mathbf{x}}_{j^{\star}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{3}{4}
and the deviation bound in (
15
),

\left|\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{v}}\right]_{j^{\star}}\right|\geq\frac{3}{4}\left|{\mathbf{v}}_{j^{\star}}\right|-\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

∎

We now prove that Algorithm
4
obtains an
\ell_{\infty}
recovery guarantee of the form
\approx\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}+r
. For simplicity, we assume the error bound in Theorem
3
only applies in the regime

r\geq\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\log\frac{n}{\delta}},

although we inherit similar improvements under certain models (e.g., Gaussian measurements) where Theorem
3
offers a tighter error bound, see the discussion after the proof.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
n=\Omega(kT\log\frac{dT}{\delta})
for an appropriate constant, and
k\in[d]
, where
T=O(\log\frac{R}{r})
is as in Algorithm
3
. Under Models
1
and
3
, there is a universal constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that if Algorithm
3
is called with parameters
c^{\prime}
,
R\geq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}
, and
r>0
,
then with probability
\geq 1-\delta
, its output satisfies

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(r+\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)}\right).

(16)

Proof.

We begin by noting that we scaled all of the divided measurements,
({\mathbf{X}}^{(i)},{\mathbf{y}}^{(i)})
for
i\in[2T]
, by
\sqrt{2T}
, to ensure that they fall in the setting of Model
3
after adjusting for the smaller sample size
\frac{n}{2T}
.
Let
C>0
be such that Theorem
3
and Lemma
10
guarantee that

\rho^{(t)}\geq\sigma:=\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)}\implies\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\rho^{(t)},

(17)

and such that in every iteration
t\in[T]
,

\begin{gathered}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2t)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\sigma,\quad\frac{\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)\top}{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(2t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}}\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right].\end{gathered}

(18)

Note that (
17
) holds with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
by taking a union bound over Theorem
3
for
T\leq n
rounds. The first bound in (
18
) also holds with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
by a union bound for an appropriate
C
, via (
12
). Finally, the second bound in (
18
) holds with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
by Lemma
11
and
\mathrm{nnz}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})\leq 2k
.
We henceforth condition on both events, and let
\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\geq 3C
.

We next claim that in every round where
\rho^{(t+1)}\geq\sigma
, the test in Line
3
will pass. This is because

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)\top}({\mathbf{y}}^{(2t+2)}-{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)\top}{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2t+2)}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t+2)\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2t+2)}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 3C\rho^{(t+1)}\leq\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\rho^{(t+1)}.

Therefore, the last round where the test passes will satisfy
\rho^{(t+1)}\leq\max(r,2\sigma)
. On this round,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)\top}{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)\top}({\mathbf{y}}^{(2t)}-{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})\right\rVert_{\infty}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{(2t)\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2t)}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\max(2\rho,4\sigma)}{c^{\prime}}+C\sigma=O\left(r+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right)\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)}\right).

∎

4
Adaptive
\ell_{\infty}
Sparse Recovery

In this section, we study fundamental limits and algorithms for Problem
2
under Model
2
.

We first establish that
n=\Omega(k^{2}\log d)
sub-Gaussian measurements suffice to solve adaptive
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery. We in fact prove our upper bound result under a more general regularity condition, an
\ell_{\infty}
-norm variant of RIP, defined and studied in Section
4.1
. Under this condition, we give our main algorithm for solving Problem
2
in the adaptive model in Section
4.2
.

In Section
4.3
, we then construct a nearly-matching lower bound, which shows that sample sizes of order
n=O(k^{2})
can be fooled by adversarial instances. This rules out the possibility for any algorithm to solve the adaptive variant of Problem
2
in this sample complexity regime.

4.1
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP

Motivated by the classical RIP (Definition
2
), we introduce an
\ell_{\infty}
analog that controls the Gram matrix
{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}
under a different norm. While RIP bounds the
\ell_{2}
operator norm of
{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}-\mathbf{I}_{d}
on sparse submatrices,
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP instead bounds the
\ell_{\infty}
operator norm of the same restrictions.

Definition 4

(
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP)
.

Let
(\epsilon,s)\in(0,1)\times[d]
.
We say
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
is
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP, if for all
S\subseteq[d]
with
|S|\leq s
,

\left\lVert[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}-\mathbf{I}_{d}]_{S\times S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\leq\epsilon.

(19)

The following observation shows that
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP implies
\ell_{2}
-RIP with the same parameters.

Lemma 12
.

For any symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we have
\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\rVert_{2\to 2}\leq\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}
.

Proof.

This follows because
\lambda=\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\rVert_{2\to 2}
is witnessed by some eigenvector
{\mathbf{v}}
with either
{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{v}}=\lambda{\mathbf{v}}
or
{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{v}}=-\lambda{\mathbf{v}}
, both of which witness the same blowup in the
\ell_{\infty}
norm.
∎

The
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP condition is also closely related to pairwise incoherence (PI, Definition
3
). Our next lemma shows that
\frac{\epsilon}{s}
-PI implies
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP. The converse, however, does not hold:
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP permits structured correlations that are not excluded by the pairwise bounds of PI.

Lemma 13

(PI implies
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP)
.

For any
(\epsilon,s)\in(0,1)\times[d]
, if
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is
\frac{\epsilon}{s}
-PI, then
{\mathbf{X}}
is also
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP. Moreover, the converse statement is false.

Proof.

As remarked on in Section
2.1
, the
\ell_{\infty}\to\ell_{\infty}
norm of a matrix is the largest
\ell_{1}
norm of any of its rows, so for any
|S|\leq s
, the bound (
19
) is immediate using
\frac{\epsilon}{s}
-PI. To disprove the converse statement, take
{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{I}_{d}+\epsilon{\mathbf{e}}_{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{j}^{\top}
for any
i\neq j
and
s\geq 2
.
∎

Given Lemma
13
and Proposition
2
, sub-Gaussian ensembles are
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP with high probability. We show that directly applying a union bound gives a slightly tighter guarantee.

Lemma 14
.

Let
(\delta,\epsilon)\in(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}
and
s\in[d]
. Under Model
3
, if
n=\Omega(\frac{s^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}(\log\tfrac{d}{s}+\log\tfrac{1}{\delta}))
for an appropriate constant, then with probability
1-\delta
,
{\mathbf{X}}
satisfies
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP.

Proof.

Write
{\mathbf{G}}:={\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}-\mathbf{I}_{d}
. We control the diagonal and off-diagonal terms separately.

For each
i
,
{\mathbf{G}}_{ii}=\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}-1
is a centered
\chi^{2}
random variable, so Lemma 1,
[
31
]
shows

\mathbb{P}\left[|{\mathbf{G}}_{ii}|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]\leq 2\exp\left(-\Omega(\epsilon n)\right).

A union bound over
i\in[d]
yields
\max_{i}|{\mathbf{G}}_{ii}|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
with probability
at least
1-\frac{\delta}{3}
provided
n=\Omega(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log(\frac{d}{\delta}))
, which falls within our parameter regime.
Next, by Proposition
1
, the event
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2
for all
i\in[d]
occurs with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
. Condition on both events henceforth.

Finally, for the off-diagonal terms, we first fix a subset
S\subset[d]
with
|S|=s
,
i\in S
, and
{\mathbf{v}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{S}
. Conditioning on
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
,
the
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}:j\in S\setminus\{i\}\}
are i.i.d.
\textup{subG}(0,\frac{2C}{n})
random variables. Hence,

\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\,{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\sim\textup{subG}\left(0,\frac{2Cs}{n}\right),

by Fact
1
, and therefore by Lemma
1
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\,{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]=2\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}n}{s}\right)\right).

(20)

Note that if the event in (
20
) holds, then combined with the diagonal,
|{\mathbf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}_{S\times S}{\mathbf{v}}|\leq\epsilon
.
Now to obtain
\ell_{\infty}
RIP, we must show

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[d]:|S|\leq s\\
i\in S\\
{\mathbf{v}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{S}\end{subarray}}|{\mathbf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}_{S\times S}{\mathbf{v}}|\leq\epsilon.

for all of
N
certificates
(S,i,{\mathbf{v}})
.
The number of events is at most

N\leq s\,2^{s}\binom{d}{k}
,
with
\log N=\Theta(s\log(\frac{d}{s}))
. Setting the right-hand side of (
20
) to
\frac{\delta}{3N}
and solving for
n
gives the claim.
∎

If we instead combine Lemma
13
with Proposition
2
to analyze sub-Gaussian ensembles, the resulting argument yields the sufficient condition
n\gtrsim\tfrac{s^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\frac{d}{\delta}
, which is slightly worse than Lemma
14
. This gap in the logarithmic dependence can be viewed as direct evidence that
\mathrm{PI}
imposes a more restrictive structural requirement than
\ell_{\infty}
–RIP.

A natural question is whether the
\approx s^{2}
sample complexity established in Lemma
14
for sub-Gaussian ensembles can be further improved by alternative matrix ensembles. Unfortunately, as we shortly demonstrate, this dependence is essentially tight: for any matrix with suitably-normalized columns,
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP cannot hold when
n=o(s^{2})
. Consequently, sub-Gaussian ensembles achieve the optimal sample complexity for constant
\epsilon
, up to logarithmic factors.

To prove this argument, we first recall a classic inequality known as the Welch bounds, which lower bounds averaged correlations among a set of unit vectors.

Lemma 15

(
[
51
]
)
.

Let
\left\{{\mathbf{v}}_{i}\right\}_{i\in[d]}
be a set of unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{(i,j)\in[d]\times[d]}\left\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{i},{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}.

Then we prove our tightness result as follows.

Lemma 16
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
s\geq 2
, and
d\geq\frac{s^{3}}{\epsilon^{2}}
, if
{\mathbf{X}}
satisfies
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP, then

n\geq\frac{s^{2}}{144\epsilon^{2}}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we use Lemma
12
which shows
{\mathbf{X}}
also satisfies
(\epsilon,s)
-
\ell_{2}
-RIP, so
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\subset[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
for all
i\in[d]
. Now applying Lemma
15
to the
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{-1}\}_{i\in[d]}
,

\sum_{(i,j)\in[d]\times[d]}\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\rangle|^{2}}{\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{n}.

Since all
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it follows that
\sum_{(i,j)\in[d]\times[d]}\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{4n}
.

Next, define
S_{i}:=\sum_{j\in[d]}|\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\rangle|^{2}
.
Averaging over
i
yields
\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i\in[d]}S_{i}\geq\frac{d}{4n}
. Hence there exists an index
i_{0}
such that
S_{i_{0}}\geq\frac{d}{4n}
. We define an index set
T
through a thresholding rule:

T:=\left\{j\in[d]:\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right\rangle\right|\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2n}}\right\}.

Moreover, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives a trivial bound for pairwise incoherence:
|\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\rangle|\leq\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}}\|_{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\|_{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}
, so
we have

S_{i_{0}}\leq|T|\cdot\frac{9}{4}+(d-|T|)\cdot\frac{1}{8n}.

Combining with
S_{i_{0}}\geq\frac{d}{4n}
yields
|T|\geq\frac{d}{18n}
.
If
n\geq\frac{s^{2}}{18\epsilon^{2}}
, the desired claim already holds. Otherwise,
|T|\geq s
, since
d\geq\frac{s^{3}}{\epsilon^{2}}
. We now select a subset
S\subseteq T
with
|S|=s
and
i_{0}\in S
and obtain that

\left\lVert[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}-\mathbf{I}]_{S\times S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\geq\sum_{j\in S,\,j\neq i_{0}}\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}},{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right\rangle\right|-|\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-1|.

Using the definition of
S\subseteq T
and that

|\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i_{0}}\|_{2}^{2}-1|\leq\epsilon
, we obtain

\epsilon\geq\left\lVert[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}-\mathbf{I}]_{S\times S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\geq\frac{s-1}{2\sqrt{2n}}-\frac{1}{2}\geq\frac{s}{6\sqrt{n}}-\epsilon.

Now, rearranging
2\epsilon\geq\frac{s}{6\sqrt{n}}
gives the claim.
∎

4.2
IHT under
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP

We next show that under Definition
4
, IHT (Algorithm
1
) solves Problem
2
even in an adaptive setting. The proof roadmap is similar to the classic
\ell_{2}
case, cf.
[
43
]
, where the RIP condition is used to argue contraction on a potential based on the
\ell_{2}
norm of the residual, and then the sparsity of
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
bounds the lossiness of the top-
k
selection step with respect to the potential.

We start with Lemma
17
, which shows that before the top-
k
truncation step, the
\ell_{\infty}
error of our estimation contracts by a constant factor in each iteration.

Lemma 17
.

Following the notation of Algorithm
1
, for all
t\in[0,T-1]
, define

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)}=\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}+{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}({\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}).

Then in the setting of Problem
2
, if
{\mathbf{X}}
is
(\epsilon,2k)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP, we have

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\epsilon\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Proof.

Rearranging the definition of
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)}
yields

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}=(\mathbf{I}-{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}})(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})+{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}.

Thus, letting
S:=\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})\cup\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})
with
|S|\leq 2k
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left[\mathbf{I}-{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]_{S\times S}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left[\mathbf{I}-{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]_{S\times S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

∎

Combining Lemma
17
with our earlier observation in Lemma
10
, we conclude that the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm error of IHT iterates admits an exponential decay toward
\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}
, as
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)}=T_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+0.5)})
.

Corollary 4
.

In the setting of Lemma
17
,

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t+1)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2\epsilon\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

With this one-step contraction, we finally give our
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery guarantee for IHT.

Theorem 4

(Adaptive
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
n=\Omega(k^{2}\log\frac{d}{\delta})
for an appropriate constant, and
k\in[d]
. Under Models
2
and
3
, if Algorithm
1
is called with parameters
R\geq\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\rVert_{\infty}
and
r>0
, then with probability
\geq 1-\delta
, its output satisfies

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq r+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Moreover, the algorithm runs in time
O(nd\log\frac{R}{r})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
14
, with probability
1-\delta
,
{\mathbf{X}}
is
(\frac{1}{4},2k)
-
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP.
Now, by telescoping Corollary
4
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{4}\right)^{T}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{4}\right)^{t}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{T}R+2\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Taking
T\geq\log_{2}\frac{R}{r}
, we obtain the desired claim, and the runtime is immediate.
∎

As noted, Theorem
4
extends beyond Model
3
, as it only uses the sufficient condition of
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP.

4.3
Lower bound

The previous Sections
4.1
and
4.2
show that if we go through the sufficient condition of
\ell_{\infty}
-RIP, then we must take
n=\Omega(k^{2})
for an algorithm to solve Problem
2
. However, this does not formally preclude a lower sample complexity of
n=o(k^{2})
that solves the problem under Model
3
directly. The main goal of this section is to rule out such a possibility.

The key intuition behind the construction is to identify a
k
-sparse
{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\gg\sigma
while still having
\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sigma
.
Setting
\boldsymbol{\xi}={\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{v}}
then produces an adversarial noise vector that significantly perturbs the underlying signal yet contributes negligibly to
{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}
, effectively masking the true support.
To formalize this idea, we first establish that under suitable conditions, we can control the
\ell_{\infty}
operator norm of the
inverse
of a Gram matrix from Model
3
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\Omega(k\log\frac{d}{\delta})=n=o(k^{2})
, and let
S\subseteq[d]
be any fixed subset such that
|S|=k=\Omega(\log\frac{1}{\delta})
. Under Model
3
, with probability
\geq 1-\delta
,

\left\lVert\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]_{S\times S}^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}=\Omega\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
,
{\mathbf{X}}
is
(\frac{1}{2},1)
-RIP, so
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
for all
i\in[d]
.

Next, define
{\mathbf{E}}:=\mathbf{I}_{d}-{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}
.
Standard inequalities (Exercise 4.7.3,
[
47
]
) yield that for large enough
n
as specified, denoting
{\mathbf{E}}_{S}:={\mathbf{E}}_{S\times S}
for short,

\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
with probability
\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{3}
.

Finally, conditioned on the realization of

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\mathbf{E}}_{ij}\right|\right]\geq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}},\quad\textup{Var}\left[|{\mathbf{E}}_{ij}|\right]\leq\frac{c_{2}}{n^{2}},

for some constants

(21)

For the stated range of

In the remainder of the proof, condition on the above three events holding, which gives the failure probability. Next, consider the power series:

[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}]_{S\times S}^{-1}=(\mathbf{I}_{S}+{\mathbf{E}}_{S})^{-1}=\mathbf{I}_{S}-{\mathbf{E}}_{S}+\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}(-{\mathbf{E}}_{S})^{t}.

\left\lVert{\mathbf{R}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{t}=\frac{\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{2}}{1-\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}}\leq 2\left\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The conclusion then follows from combining the above three displays, and applying Lemma

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left[{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\right]^{-1}_{S\times S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{I}_{S}-{\mathbf{E}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{R}}_{S}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}\geq\frac{ck}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{1}{2}=\Omega\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

∎

5.1
Few false positives in thresholding

Recall that the support estimation phase of Algorithm
2
(Line

\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|
.
In the
oblivious

{\mathbf{X}}\perp\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
, so the interference term

concentrates at the scale
\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
may be chosen adversarially after observing
{\mathbf{X}}
. Indeed, we exploit this adaptivity for our worst-case lower bound in Theorem

Despite this worst-case construction, we show that a single round of thresholding based on
\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|
still admits a meaningful guarantee in the setting of Problem

, where all

satisfy

\left|\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{i}\right|=\Omega(\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty})

heavy
.
In this setting, we show that the number of falsely-selected coordinates via thresholding is
O(k)
, and that the identified coordinates contain at least a constant fraction of the total
\ell_{2}

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
.

We remark that this proof is inspired by a similar observation in Lemma 2.1,
[

Lemma 19
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and assume that for a universal constant

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\leq C^{\prime}\sqrt{k}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

In the setting of Problem
1
and Models
3
and
4
, let

{S_{\textup{FP}}}=\left\{i\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}):\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|\geq\frac{C}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\},\quad{S_{\textup{FN}}}=\left\{i\in\mathrm{supp}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}):\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|<\frac{C}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right\},

where
C
is the constant from Problem
1
.
Then if
n=\Omega(k\log\frac{d}{\delta})
for an appropriate constant, with probability
\geq 1-\delta
, we have

|{S_{\textup{FP}}}|=O(k),\quad\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}\leq 0.95.

Proof.

We prove the first claim through contradiction. Suppose that there is
T\subseteq{S_{\textup{FP}}}
such that

|T|=\frac{64(C^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}\cdot k+1,

(22)

which exists whenenver
|{S_{\textup{FP}}}|>\frac{64(C^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}\cdot k
.
By the thresholding rule, for any
i\in T
,
\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|\geq\frac{C}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Define a vector
{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
such that
{\mathbf{v}}_{i}=\mathrm{sign}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}})
. Then,

\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rangle=\sum_{i\in T}\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|\geq\frac{C}{2}|T|\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

On the other hand, notice that

\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{:T}{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{X}}_{:T}{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{:T}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2},\quad\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{2}\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}_{T:}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{2}\leq|T|\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

For sufficiently large
n
as stated,
{\mathbf{X}}
satisfies
(\frac{1}{2},|T|+k)
-RIP, so

\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{:T}{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=2|T|,\quad\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

This yields

\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{X}}_{T:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{|T|}+|T|\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Combining gives the contradiction, for sufficiently large
C>4
,

2\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{|T|}+|T|\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\geq\frac{C}{2}|T|\cdot\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\implies|T|\leq\frac{64\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{C^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}}\leq\frac{64(C^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}\cdot k.

To prove the second claim, we use a similar strategy.
By the thresholding rule, for any
i\in{S_{\textup{FN}}}
, we know
\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|<\tfrac{C}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}

\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\left|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right|^{2}}<\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

On the other hand, we let
{S_{\textup{TP}}}=S\setminus{S_{\textup{FN}}}
and notice that

\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}_{:{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{\textup{FN}}}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}_{:{S_{\textup{TP}}}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{\textup{TP}}}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{2}.

A direct calculation also gives, under

-RIP,

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}_{:{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{\textup{FN}}}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\frac{3}{4}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\right\rVert_{2},\quad\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}_{:{S_{\textup{TP}}}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{\textup{TP}}}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{5}{4}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{TP}}}}\right\rVert_{2},

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Therefore, we have the following lower bound:

\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}:}^{\top}{\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{TP}}}}\right\rVert_{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}-\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}.

To avoid contradiction, we require

\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{TP}}}}\right\rVert_{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}-\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty},

which for

sufficiently large in Problem

\alpha-\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(\frac{C}{2}+1)\sqrt{|{S_{\textup{FN}}}|}\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\frac{3}{5},\text{ for }\alpha:=\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{{S_{\textup{FN}}}}\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{2}}.

Finally,

Algorithm 5

The algorithm consists of three phases.

1.

In the first phase, we run
\mathsf{IHT}
to obtain a warm start and a finer control on

.

2.

In the second phase, we perform a sequence of adaptive queries (leveraging our access to Algorithm

3.

In the third phase, once the support is recovered, we run simple OLS on the union of all estimated supports to obtain the desired

The adaptive querying phase is the core of the algorithm and is built directly upon Lemma

At a high level, this phase maintains a growing set

of coordinates identified as heavy up to round

In subsequent rounds, the columns of the design matrix indexed by

are muted, so that their contribution no longer interferes with future measurements.
Lemma

(1)

, which guarantees that the number of incorrectly identified coordinates is proportional to the number of remaining true coordinates, and hence that the final estimated support does not grow excessively;
and (2)

and
\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{i}}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{0}}\rVert_{2}\leq 10\sqrt{k}\sigma

the lemma is applicable at every iteration. Note that the conclusion still clearly holds if the truncation threshold is set larger than
\frac{C\sigma}{2}
, as both conclusions are monotone in the threshold.
Then, Lemma

1-\frac{\delta}{4N}
.

1.

(
False discovery control.
) The number of false inclusions at round

is at most
k
.

2.

(
\ell_{2}
-energy contraction.
)

\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{i+1}}\rVert_{2}\leq 0.95\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}_{S_{i}}\rVert_{2}
.

Here we note that the constant in the false discovery control property was selected by taking

in (

By the assumption in Problem

S_{N}=\emptyset
and all true coordinates

Phase III: OLS solution.

Once a superset
S
of the exact support

Appendix B
Tight Error Guarantee for Gaussian Noise Model

In this section, we give an information-theoretic lower bound for
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery under a Gaussian noise model. We start with a simple lemma to instantiate the general results in Sections
3
and
5
.

Lemma 22
.

For any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, if
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
satisfies
(\frac{1}{2},1)
-RIP, then for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n})
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq O\left(\sigma\sqrt{\log\frac{d}{\delta}}\right).

Proof.

For any
i\in[d]
, we have
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2})
. Since
\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}
, we have
\mathbb{P}[|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}|\geq t]\leq 2\exp(-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})
. Taking
t=O(\sigma\sqrt{\log d/\delta})
and a union bound over
i\in[d]
leads to the conclusion.
∎

We next quantify the intrinsic difficulty of
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery using two standard notions of risk. The minimax risk captures the worst-case expected error over the
k
-sparse parameter class, corresponding to the partially-adaptive Model
4

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
, corresponding to the oblivious Model
1
. A basic but useful fact is that the Bayes risk under any prior lower bounds the minimax risk. Our proof roadmap mainly follows
[
45
]
.

Definition 5
.

Let
\Theta
be a parameter space such that every
\theta\in\Theta
indexes a data distribution
{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta}
.
Suppose we want to estimate an unknown
\theta\in\Theta
from data
Z\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta}
. We define the minimax risk as

{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{minimax}}(\Theta,Z)=\inf_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}}}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(Z)-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right],

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
is any estimate that is a function
6
6

6

We restrict to deterministic
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}

is convex.
of the observations

.
Similarly, if
\theta
is sampled from some prior

{\mathcal{M}}_{\textup{Bayes}}(\pi,\Theta,Z)=\inf_{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\sim\pi\\
Z\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}}\end{subarray}}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(Z)-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right].

Our lower bound follows a standard information-theoretic route. We construct a finite, well-separated subset of parameters and reduce estimation to identifying the correct candidate parameter. Applying Fano’s inequality relates the achievable estimation accuracy to the mutual information between the observations and the underlying index, yielding a quantitative lower bound on the Bayes (and therefore, minimax) risk.

Lemma 23

(Fano’s inequality)
.

Let
V

\widehat{V}
be discrete random variables on a common set

\mathbb{P}\left[\widehat{V}\neq V\right]\geq 1-\frac{I(V;\widehat{V})+\log 2}{\log|{\mathcal{V}}|}.

The following calculation helps simplify applications of Lemma

let

Lemma 25

(Risk lower bound via exact recovery)

Under Model

\epsilon>0

By Markov’s inequality, we have

Lemma 26
.

Under Model

Proof.

Our construction of

\displaystyle I(V;{\mathbf{y}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}P_{V}(v)D_{\textup{KL}}\left(P_{{\mathbf{y}}\mid V}(\cdot\mid v)\|Q_{{\mathbf{y}}}\right).

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{V}}|}\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\frac{\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{v}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{V}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{1}_{V}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\textup{Tr}({\mathbf{X}}\mathrm{Cov}\left[V\right]{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}).

Appendix C
Application to Spike-and-Slab Posterior Sampling

In this appendix, we briefly discuss one application of our upper bound results for
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery. Recently,
[
30
]
gave an algorithm for Bayesian sparse linear regression in the following model.

Model 5

(Spike-and-slab posterior sampling)
.

In the
spike-and-slab posterior sampling
problem,
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is drawn under Model
3
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})^{n}
independently, and
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is independently drawn from the following
spike-and-slab
prior, for
{\mathbf{q}}\in[0,1]^{d}
with
\left\lVert{\mathbf{q}}\right\rVert_{1}=k
:

\pi:=\bigotimes_{i\in[d]}\left((1-{\mathbf{q}}_{i})\delta_{0}+{\mathbf{q}}_{i}\mathcal{N}(0,1)\right),

where
\delta_{0}
is a Dirac density at
0
. For some
\delta\in(0,1)
, the goal is to produce a sample from the posterior
\pi(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{y}})
within total variation
\delta
, where
{\mathbf{y}}={\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}+\boldsymbol{\xi}
are the observations.

Under Model
5
, the parameter
k
can be viewed as an expected sparsity level for the signal
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}\sim\pi
, because coordinate
i\in[d]
is only nonzero with probability
\delta
. The key challenge in solving Model
5
is that for noise levels
\sigma
that are
\approx 1
, there are many coordinates that could plausibly either be part of the signal
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star}
, or masked by the random noise
\boldsymbol{\xi}
. To model this uncertainty, the posterior density
\pi(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{y}})
places nontrivial mass on
d^{\Omega(k)}
candidate supports, and therefore the algorithm must successfully sample from this exponentially-sized candidate set.

Prior works by
[
55
,
37
]
respectively designed algorithms for restricted variants of Model
5
that succeeded when
\sigma
is relatively large or small, and when the number of observations
n\gtrsim d
. The algorithm by
[
30
]
lifts these restrictions, and solves the sampling problem in Model
5
for any
\sigma>0
and using
n=\mathrm{poly}(k,\log(\frac{d}{\delta}))
samples. In particular,
[
30
]
gives two algorithms: the first is based on
\ell_{2}
sparse recovery, and thus runs in nearly-linear time (e.g., using Lemma
4
), but uses
n\approx k^{5}
observations to compensate for the weaker recovery guarantee. The second algorithm leverages
\ell_{\infty}
sparse recovery, and uses an improved
n\approx k^{3}
, but its runtime was previously based on the LASSO (see Theorem 1,
[
30
]
, which claims a runtime of
\approx n^{2}d^{1.5}
).

Substituting our Theorems
3
or
4
in place of the LASSO (Proposition 3,
[
30
]
) immediately gives the following improved runtime for spike-and-slab posterior sampling.

Corollary 6
.

In the setting of Model
5
, if
n=\Omega(k^{3}\textup{polylog}(\frac{d}{\delta}))
, there is an algorithm that returns
\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim\pi^{\prime}
such that
D_{\textup{TV}}(\pi^{\prime},\pi(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{y}}))\leq\delta
with probability
\geq 1-\delta
over the randomness of
({\mathbf{X}},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\star},\boldsymbol{\xi})
, which runs in time

O\left(nd\log\left(\frac{\log\frac{1}{\delta}}{\min(1,\sigma)}\right)\right).

Proof.

The runtime of, e.g., Theorem
4
meets the described bound, where we may take
r=\Omega(\sigma)
and
R^{2}=O(\log\frac{1}{\delta})
with probability
\geq 1-\delta
under our modeling assumptions. The remainder of the proof follows identically to the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
30
]
.
∎

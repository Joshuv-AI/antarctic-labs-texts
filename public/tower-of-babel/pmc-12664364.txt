Magnitude and associated factors of discharge against medical advice among patients treated in the adult Emergency Department at an Ethiopian University Hospital
Authors: Getachew Worku, Demelash Ataro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•There is insufficient research on the prevalence and contributing factors of discharge against medical advice in African health care settings, particularly in adult emergency departments.•Understanding how many patients leave against medical advice and the reason behind their decisions can help improve the quality of care, patient satisfaction, and health outcomes.•The finding of this study provide a baseline that can inform and guide future interventional trials and policy measures aimed at reducing discharge against medical advice in African hospitals.

Introduction

The event when a patient already admitted to the hospital wishes to leave against the clinicians' advice is referred to as discharge against medical advice (DAMA) [1]. The situation usually presents an ethical dilemma to the medical team, considering the patients' autonomy in treatment and the medical team's intention to do good [2]. DAMA is a global phenomenon with a prevalence rate of more than 2 % of all hospital-admitted cases according to Western literature. The prevalence rates may range from 0.3 % to 25.9 % depending on factors such as geographical history, medical condition, and social status of patients [3]. The Emergency Department (ED) of hospitals plays a crucial role in preserving people's lives [4]. Management and evaluation of ED performance can only be attained by having reliable information about the current situation. Compared to other departments in a hospital, the ED receives the largest number of patients as well as the most serious ones; hence, the quality of medical service in the ED can represent the general quality of the medical services provided by a hospital [5].

DAMA is a well-known global problem, and the magnitude of the problem varies in different parts of the world. Its prevalence varies by country, region, and hospital type; geographic, institutional, and socioeconomic factors influence its occurrence. Although several contributing factors have been reported, research on them remains limited [6]. In Ethiopia, existing research has assessed DAMA prevalence in paediatrics, but evidence regarding the adult population remains unavailable [7]. Some prevention strategies to help reduce the prevalence of DAMA have been reported in various literature. Health providers are advised to formulate management plans that include the patient in his or her care. Including patients in clinical decisions can help address their extra-clinical concerns, leading to an agreement with the management plan [8].

So far, only a few studies have been found globally and in Africa on DAMA among the adult population, with only one paediatric study from Ethiopia [7]. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to describe the magnitude and factors associated with DAMA among patients admitted to an adult ED in Ethiopia.

Methods

A hospital-based retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted at the adult emergency department(ED) of Hawassa University Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (HUCSH). HUCSH is a university hospital serving as a training facility for medical students (undergraduate and postgraduate levels), nurses, midwives, pharmacists, and laboratory and radiology technologists. The adult ED provides many clinical services for emergency and critical cases, and operates under a structured triage system categorizing patients based on the severity of their condition into five zones: red (immediate life-threatening cases), orange (very urgent cases), yellow (urgent but stable cases), green (less urgent cases), and a surgical and trauma bay for patients requiring emergency surgical interventions. The ED medical record registry was reviewed over a year and a half (January 1, 2023 to June 1, 2024) and the total number of adult ED visits during the study period was approximately 12,000, with 590 DAMA cases recorded. The data collection was conducted from July 1, 2024, to July 31, 2024.

All patients who had been treated in the adult ED of HUCSH from January 2023 to June 2024, and discharged against medical advice. Patients who left without signing DAMA consent forms and charts with incomplete information (defined as charts missing key variables such as age, sex, diagnosis, and documented reasons for DAMA). During the data extraction process, all retrieved charts were screened for completeness. Charts meeting the inclusion criteria were coded and those found to be incomplete were excluded.

The sample size was calculated by using a single population proportion formula with the assumptions of a 95 % confidence interval 5 % marginal error and a proportion of 25.6 % in the ED from a national survey done in India [1]. The study employed a systematic random sampling technique to select patients' records from an adult emergency record book. The total number of adult ED visits during the study period was approximately 12,000 with 590 DAMA cases identified. To determine the sampling interval, the total number of DAMA cases (N = 590) was divided by the required sample size (n = 322): k = N/n = 590/322= 2. Thus, every 2nd DAMA chart was selected after the first chart was chosen randomly using a lottery method. Each chart was chosen by a trained resident for data collection.

We have collected the socio-demographic characteristics like age, sex, and residency (urban, rural. We have also gathered triage score, length of stay, mode of arrival, caregiver accompaniment, history of DAMA, and place or zone of admission in the ED.

All data collectors received training by the principal investigator on the data extraction tool, definition of variables, and abstraction procedures before the data collection began. The data was collected using a data extraction tool prepared to collect data from the patient's chart by trained residents. A data checklist was used to be filled by reviewing the patient's chart. Data were collected from the charts from July 1, 2024, to July 31, 2024, based on charts brought by porters. The study tool was pre-tested on 5 % of the sample, and a systematic sampling technique was used. Supervisor cross-check completed responses on a tablet by repeating reviews with 5 % of charts for correct completion. The principal investigator double-checked the data for any inconsistencies and provided daily feedback to the interviewers after the responses were put into the digital data entry tool.

Data analysis

Data were collected using the Kobo toolbox and exported to SPSS version 25 for analysis. Descriptive statistics, including proportions, medians, tables, and charts, were used to describe the characteristics of the study participants. Continuous variables such as age and length of hospital stay were assessed for normality using histograms and statistical tests. Based on the distribution, appropriate summary measures such as mean and median, statistical models were applied.

Binary logistic regression analysis was used to identify factors associated with DAMA. Variables with a p-value < 0.25 in the bi-variable analysis were candidates for the final multi-variable logistic regression model. An adjusted odds ratio along with the 95 % confidence interval was used to show the strength of the association. The level of significance was declared at a p-value ≤0.05. The Hosmer and Lemeshow Test was used for checking the model fitness of logistic regressions (p = 0.686).

Ethical consideration

Ethical clearance was obtained from Hawassa University College of Medicine and Health Sciences (HUCMHS) Institutional Review Board, IRB/319/16. An official letter was obtained from HUCSH, Department of Emergency and Critical Care Medicine. The privacy of individual information was strictly preserved, and all data obtained in the course of the study was kept confidential and used solely for the study. All the methods were conducted per the approved institutional guidelines of HUCMHS.

Magnitude of DAMA

This study found that the magnitude of DAMA was 7.45 % (24 out of 322; 95 % CI: 4.56, 10.3 %) The majority of patients discharged against medical advice 10 (41.7 %) were due to a financial reason, followed by refusal of procedure (20.8 %) and poor prognosis (16.7 %). DISCUSSION

This study found that the magnitude of DAMA was 7.45 % (95 % CI: 4.56–10.3 %). The finding was consistent with a previous study conducted in Iran (5.6 %) [4], Italy (6 %) [9], Malaysia (6.36 %) [10], and Saudi Arabia (7.3 %) [11]. It was higher than a study conducted in Nigeria (1.9 %) [12], Ghana (2.5 %) [13], the United States of America (1.3 %) [14], the United Kingdom (3 %) [15], and India (3.3 %) [6]. These discrepancies could be explained by variations in the prevalence and incidence of diseases, smoking, alcohol, and recreational drug habits, sociocultural settings, irregular medication and diagnostic modalities, patient discontent with the standard of care, disagreements with medical professionals, and a desire to continue treatment nearer to home, all of which may lead to patients departing against medical advice.

The DAMA rate was found to be lower than a study conducted in Saudi Arabia at 12.5 % [16], Jordan (33.3 %) [17], and India (25.6 % and 45 %) [1,18] This variation could be due to the difference in population, sample size, study period, high prevalence of smoking, and the fear that COVID-19 may account for this higher rate of DAMA among patients with chronic disease in Jordan [17]. Substances' addictive nature affected patients' behavior, making it challenging for them to put their health before their cravings. Health-care providers need to address substance abuse issues to improve patient outcomes and reduce the risk of readmission.

The study found that financial issues were the most frequent reason for discharge against medical advice. The finding is consistent with a study conducted in India [18] and Nigeria [12], which reported that about half the patients were leaving the hospital due to financial constraints. However, due to the retrospective nature of our data, it was not possible to delineate whether these financial challenges were directly related to treatment costs, medication expenses, lack of insurance coverage, or indirect costs such as transportation, accommodation, meals, and missed work.

The study revealed a significant association between patient gender and discharge against medical advice, with males experiencing a fourfold higher likelihood of DAMA compared to females. The finding is supported by a study conducted in Malaysia [10], Saudi Arabia [16], and the United States of America [14], and a systematic review and Meta-Analysis [19].

Beyond the statistical association, this trend may be rooted in a complex interplay of cultural and psycho-social factors. In many societies, including Ethiopians, males often assume the role of primary financial providers and may feel pressure to return home prematurely.

Additionally, men may perceive seeking hospital care as a sign of weakness, which can be influenced by cultural norms. Health-seeking behaviour in men is generally poorer than in women, often leading to delayed care seeking and early discharge. Poor health-seeking behavior in men, on the other hand, may contribute to delayed presentation to health-care institutions. At this later presentation, the disease may reach an advanced stage and be a reason for DAMA due to the disease's poor prognosis.

Moreover, the triage level significantly influences patients' likelihood of leaving DAMA, with patients with a triage level of red and orange being three times more likely to be DAMA compared to those with a higher triage level. The finding is inconsistent with a study conducted in the United States of America, which reported that the increased injury severity was associated with lower odds of DAMA [14]. In our setting, several possible factors may contribute to this discrepancy. Resource limitations such as a shortage of ICU beds, mechanical ventilators, medications, or expert personnel may lead to delays or sub-optimal care, causing critically ill patients and their families to lose trust in the system.

Our study clearly showed that a longer hospital stay has a significant contribution to DAMA, as 14 (58.3 %) patients left after 72 hours of ED stay. This is in line with a study done in Ghana, where the mean period for DAMA requests was five days of admission [13]. This may indicate that patients and their families have likely spent all of their money during this time, which would be why they chose DAMA. Similarly, in Malaysia, there was a strong contribution to DAMA from increased ED length of stay [10]. Here, the fact that most of the patients having DAMA were from the green zone, which may suggest non-urgent conditions and make patients more likely to choose DAMA, may have made them feel overlooked and undervalued.

This finding is incoherent with research conducted in Nigeria, where 87.5 % of patients had early Discharge Against Medical Advice (DAMA), leaving the hospital within 8 hours of admission [3], and at Jersey Shore University Medical Centre in the USA, where over half of patients departed prematurely, even without undergoing a physical examination [8].

Limitations and strengths of the study

The study’s retrospective design and reliance on incomplete medical records limited access to important socio-demographic and economic information, such as education, occupation, and income and as well as ethical considerations and the timing of DAMA events, which could influence discharge decisions. Additionally, focusing solely on the Emergency department may limit the generalization of the findings. Despite these limitations, the study provides valuable preliminary insights and lays the groundwork for more comprehensive future research.

Conclusion and recommendations

The study concluded that the magnitude of DAMA was high (7.45 %). Being male and the severity of the diseases were found to be independent determinants of DAMA. The majority of patients had permanently left the hospital due to financial constraints (41.7 %), followed by refusal of procedure (20.8 %) and poor prognosis (16.7 %). Health-care providers should enhance communication and provide timely, clear counselling about patients’ clinical conditions and the potential risks of leaving against medical advice to reduce its occurrence. The Hospital and Ministry of Health should work to strengthen community-based health insurance and ensure the consistent availability of essential medicines and resources to minimize financial barriers to care. Additionally, future prospective studies are recommended to overcome the limitations of this retrospective design and provide a deeper understanding of DAMA-related factors.

Coumarin-based dual kinase inhibitors targeting CDK6 and VEGFR-2: experimental and computational validation
Authors: Hazem Elkady, Walid E. Elgammal, Ibrahim H. Eissa, Dalal Z. Husein, Hanan A. Al-ghulikah, Ghalbaa N. Alshahrani, Fatma G. Amin, Ahmed M. Metwaly, Eslam B. Elkaeed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A rational pharmacophore-guided strategy was employed to design and synthesize a series of novel coumarin derivatives as potential dual cyclin-dependent kinase 6 (CDK6) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR-2) inhibitors. Following structural characterization, the synthesized compounds were evaluated for their anticancer activity against MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, HepG-2, and HCT-116 cancer cell lines, together with normal WI-38 and WISH cells. Among the tested derivatives, compound 7e exhibited the highest potency, with IC50 values ranging from 3.80 to 6.35 µM, while demonstrating superior selectivity toward cancer cells compared with sorafenib. Enzymatic assays confirmed potent dual inhibition of VEGFR-2 (IC50 = 0.3905 µM) and CDK6 (IC50 = 0.3380 µM). Mechanistic studies revealed that compound 7e induced significant apoptosis, promoted S-phase cell cycle arrest, and effectively inhibited cell migration in HCT-116 cells. Computational studies, including molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and binding free energy calculations, supported its stable interactions with both kinase targets. Furthermore, in silico ADMET analysis predicted favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profiles. Collectively, these findings identified compound 7e as a promising dual VEGFR-2/CDK6 inhibitor with potent and selective anticancer activity, warranting further preclinical investigation.

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, accounting for millions of deaths annually, despite substantial advances in diagnosis and therapy.1 Multiple interconnected pathways, including dysregulated cell cycle progression and abnormal angiogenesis, drive tumor progression.2–4 Consequently, targeting key regulators of these processes has emerged as a powerful strategy in the development of anticancer drugs.5

Cyclin-dependent kinase 6 (CDK6) is a crucial regulator of the G1–S phase transition of the cell cycle.6 Overexpression or hyperactivation of CDK6 has been reported in various malignancies, including breast, colorectal, and liver cancers, where it contributes to uncontrolled cellular proliferation and resistance to apoptosis.7,8 Although selective CDK4/6 inhibitors have demonstrated clinical success, resistance and adverse effects remain significant challenges,9,10 necessitating the development of novel scaffolds with improved efficacy and safety profiles.

In parallel, vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR-2) plays a central role in tumor angiogenesis by mediating endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and survival. Aberrant activation of VEGFR-2 promotes neovascularization, thereby facilitating tumor growth and metastasis.11–13 Small-molecule VEGFR-2 inhibitors, such as sorafenib and sunitinib,14–16 have demonstrated therapeutic benefits; however, issues related to toxicity,17–19 limited pharmacokinetics,20 and acquired resistance21,22 underscore the need for alternative or multitargeted approaches.

Emerging evidence suggests that dual inhibition of proliferative kinases and angiogenic signaling pathways may provide synergistic anticancer effects, reduce the development of resistance, and improve therapeutic outcomes.23 In this context, designing molecules capable of simultaneously targeting CDK6 and VEGFR-2 represents a rational and promising strategy for anticancer drug discovery.

In recent years, heterocyclic scaffolds have attracted considerable attention in medicinal chemistry because of their remarkable structural diversity and their ability to interact with a wide range of biological targets. Numerous heterocyclic derivatives have been reported as potent anticancer agents through the inhibition of various molecular targets, including protein kinases, demonstrating promising antiproliferative activities and favorable structure–activity relationships.23

Coumarin is a privileged heterocyclic scaffold widely recognized for its diverse biological activities, including anticancer, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties.24–26 Structurally, the coumarin nucleus offers favorable pharmacokinetic characteristics, allowing for versatile chemical modifications that can optimize target binding and biological activity.27 Several coumarin-based derivatives have been reported as kinase inhibitors, demonstrating their suitability for rational drug design targeting ATP-binding domains.28–30

Rationale

CDK6 inhibitors have revolutionized targeted therapy by selectively engaging the kinase hinge region (Val101/Asp163) to block G1/S progression, with five distinct chemical classes spanning FDA-approved agents to preclinical candidates-each balancing potency, selectivity, and unique toxicity profiles through innovative scaffold design.

Palbociclib I, an FDA-approved CDK6 inhibitor for HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer, binds hinge residues Val101/Asp163 via aminopyrimidine core. Common side effects include neutropenia (60–70%) and fatigue.31 Ribociclib II, an FDA-approved CDK6 inhibitor combined with endocrine therapy for breast cancer, features pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidin-7-one for dual hinge H-bonds. Notable QT prolongation and neutropenia (>50%).32 Abemaciclib III, an FDA-approved CDK6 inhibitor for breast cancer monotherapy, engages Glu81/Val99 hinge with bridged piperazine system. Distinguished by diarrhea (80–90%) and lower neutropenia rates.33 Trilaciclib IV, a selective CDK4/6 inhibitor featuring a spiro-pyrazino[1′,2′:1,5]pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine structure, is FDA-approved to reduce chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC), with preliminary data confirming manageable hematologic toxicity.34 Atuveciclib V, phase II CDK6 inhibitor targeting breast cancer, aminopyrimidine engages CDK6 hinge bridged to aryl back pocket. Tolerability profile similar to first-generation agents.35 AZD5597 VI, Preclinical/early clinical CDK6-selective inhibitor, optimized heteroaromatic-amide scaffold for hinge/DFG targeting. Limited safety data available.36

The CDK binding site consists of four main regions; (a) front pocket, adenine binding pocket, linker area, and DFG motif region.37–39 Study of the structure–activity relationships (SAR) and common pharmacophoric features shared by various CDKIs revealed the presence of four common pharmacophoric features as follows. (i) A hydrophobic head that can occupy the front pocket, (ii) a flat hetero aromatic system, occupying the adenine binding pocket. This hetero aromatic system can participate in hydrogen bonding interactions in the adenine binding region, (iii) a central hydrophobic linker that can occupy the linker region between the adenine binding pocket and the DFG motif region, and (vi) a hydrogen bonding group that can occupy the DFG motif region (Fig. 1).

Reported CDK inhibitors and their basic pharmacophoric features.

Clinically approved VEGFR-2 inhibitors such as sorafenib VII,40 regorafenib VIII, lucitanib IX, sunitinib X,41 tivozanib XI, and lenvatinib XII possess four essential pharmacophoric features that enable optimal accommodation within the active site, as illustrated in Fig. 2. These include: (i) a heteroaromatic head occupying the adenine-binding pocket, (ii) a linker spanning the central hydrophobic region, (iii) a pharmacophore targeting the DFG motif region, and (iv) a hydrophobic tail engaging the allosteric pocket [29–31] (Fig. 2).

Our research group has consistently employed an integrated experimental and computational strategy to identify novel lead compounds targeting microbial pathogens42–46 and various cancer types,47–49 with particular emphasis on kinase inhibitors.50–53 In line with this approach, the present study focused on the rational design and synthesis of a new series of coumarin derivatives, guided by the essential pharmacophoric requirements for dual inhibition of CDK6 and VEGFR-2 (Fig. 3).

Summary for the possible CDK and VEGFR-2 dual inhibitors.

For CDK6 inhibitors, the chromene moiety acted as a planar heterocyclic system to occupy the adenine binding region. The 1-phenylethylidene hydrazine moiety was used as a linker group to occupy the linker region of the CDK6 active site. The morpholinosulfonyl group was utilized as a hydrogen-bonding moiety to occupy the DFG motif region. For VEGFR-2 inhibitors, the chromene moiety acted as the central heterocyclic system that occupied the hinge region. The 1-phenylethylidene hydrazine moiety served as a linker that occupied the linker region of the VEGFR-2 active site. The sulfonamide group was utilized as a pharmacophore moiety to fit into the DFG motif region, while the morpholine moiety functioned as a terminal hydrophobic tail that occupied the allosteric pocket of VEGFR-2.

The synthesized compounds were evaluated for their in vitro cytotoxic activity against multiple human cancer cell lines, along with selectivity assessment in normal cells. The most promising candidate was further investigated through enzymatic inhibition assays, flow cytometry, apoptosis, and cell migration studies. In addition, DFT calculations, molecular docking, MD simulations, MM-GBSA free energy analysis, ProLIF profiling, essential dynamics studies, and in silico ADMET and toxicity predictions are used to comprehensively characterize its biological and physicochemical properties.

Results and discussions

Chemistry

As illustrated in Schemes 1–3, the cyanoaceto hydrazides, which are linked to the morpholinosulfonyl phenyl derivative, were synthesized through the reaction of the acetylating compounds 1 and 2 with cyanoacetic hydrazide 3 (ref. 54 and 55) (the cyanoacetic hydrazide was prepared by reacting hydrazinium hydroxide (N2H5OH) with ethyl 2-cyanoacetate while stirring at room temperature for two hours). Following this, the reaction was conducted in boiling ethanol for approximately five hours, yielding pure starting intermediates 4 and 5 in significant amounts.

Synthesis of reagent 3 and the starting materials 4 and 5.

The resultant molecule, 4, underwent reflux with salicylaldehyde derivatives (6a–e) in dioxane and piperidine for a duration of seven hours. The aforementioned reaction reliably yielded the corresponding 2-imino-2H-chromene-3-carbohydrazide derivatives (7a–e) in satisfactory to high yields, underscoring the broad applicability and resilience of the synthetic approach. Additionally, the 3-imino-3H-benzo[f]chromene-2-carbohydrazide derivative 9 was synthesized by refluxing molecule 4 with 2-hydroxynaphthyl-1-carboxaldehyde 8 in dioxane and piperidine for seven hours. In the same manner, the reaction of cyanoaceto hydrazide compound 5 with salicylaldehyde derivatives (6a–e) in dioxane and piperidine resulted in the formation of 2-imino-2H-chromene-3-carbohydrazide derivatives (10a,b). In contrast, when 2-hydroxynaphthyl-1-carboxaldehyde 8 was treated under the same conditions, the reaction produced the 3-imino-3H-benzo[f]chromene-2-carbohydrazide derivative 11, as illustrated in Scheme 3. From a mechanistic perspective, the reaction between cyanoaceto hydrazide derivatives and salicylaldehyde derivatives and/or 2-hydroxynaphthaldehyde is appropriately characterized as a condensation–cyclization process. This matter, more particularly, consists of three primary phases. The reaction proceeds through a condensation between the active methylene moiety of the cyanoaceto hydrazide derivatives 4 and 5 and the carbonyl group of salicylaldehyde derivatives and/or 2-hydroxynaphthaldehyde, subsequently eliminating a water molecule (H2O) to yield an insoluble intermediate under alkaline conditions. This intermediate's distinct architecture incorporates a cyano group (–CN). Concurrently, it possesses an ortho-hydroxy group (2-OH). These functionalities facilitate an intramolecular cyclization of the nascent coumarin scaffold, as set out in Schemes 2 and 3.

Biological examinations

Cytotoxicity and selectivity profile of the synthesized coumarin derivatives

The in vitro antiproliferative activity of the synthesized coumarin derivatives was evaluated against a panel of human cancer cell lines (MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, HepG-2, and HCT-116), together with normal WI-38 lung fibroblasts and WISH amnion cells to assess both cytotoxicity and selectivity. The results revealed considerable variation in antiproliferative potency and selectivity, highlighting the impact of structural modifications on the biological activity of the synthesized compounds (Table 1).

Among all tested derivatives, compound 7e exhibited the most potent anticancer activity, with IC50 values ranging from 3.80 to 6.35 µM, comparable to or slightly superior to sorafenib. Importantly, compound 7e displayed relatively low cytotoxicity toward both WI-38 and WISH normal cells (IC50 = 25.29 and 29.52 µM, respectively), resulting in the highest selectivity indices among the tested compounds (SI = 3.98–7.77), indicating an excellent therapeutic window and pronounced cancer-cell selectivity.

Compound 10b also demonstrated remarkable cytotoxic activity, particularly against MDA-MB-231 and HCT-116 cells (IC50 = 9.38 and 8.62 µM, respectively), while exhibiting substantially lower toxicity toward normal WI-38 and WISH cells (IC50 = 58.21 and 43.73 µM), resulting in favorable selectivity indices of up to 6.75. Likewise, compound 10a showed moderate antiproliferative activity with acceptable safety toward normal cells, whereas compound 7c displayed selective activity against MDA-MB-231 and HepG-2 cells with SI values reaching 4.39, suggesting promising selectivity despite its moderate potency.

In contrast, compounds 7a, 7b, 7d, 9, and 11 exhibited either limited antiproliferative activity or relatively low selectivity despite showing low to moderate toxicity toward normal cells. Consequently, their overall therapeutic potential was inferior to that of compounds 7e and 10b. Collectively, these findings demonstrated that the pharmacophore-guided design successfully generated compounds with improved anticancer selectivity, with compound 7e emerging as the lead candidate owing to its potent dual-target anticancer activity, favorable safety profile toward normal cells, and superior selectivity.

VEGFR-2 and CDK6 inhibitory activity

To elucidate the molecular basis underlying the potent cytotoxic and selective anticancer activity of compound 7e, its inhibitory effects against VEGFR-2 and CDK6 were evaluated and compared with the corresponding reference inhibitors, sorafenib and ribociclib, respectively. As shown in Table 2, compound 7e exhibited potent dual inhibitory activity, with IC50 values of 0.3905 ± 0.001 µM against VEGFR-2 and 0.3380 ± 0.005 µM against CDK6. Notably, compound 7e displayed stronger VEGFR-2 inhibition than sorafenib (IC50 = 0.8090 ± 0.007 µM) and showed CDK6 inhibitory activity that was comparable to that of the CDK6 inhibitor ribociclib (IC50 = 0.214 ± 0.005 µM). Importantly, the dual inhibition profile of 7e correlated well with its low micromolar cytotoxicity and high selectivity indices, suggesting that effective target engagement was achieved without excessive toxicity toward normal cells. Overall, these enzymatic assay results confirmed that compound 7e was a potent dual VEGFR-2/CDK6 inhibitor and provided a clear mechanistic rationale for its superior anticancer potency and selectivity, thereby supporting its identification as a promising lead compound for further preclinical development.

Effect of 7e on cell cycle distribution

The impact of the 7e on cell cycle progression was analyzed by flow cytometry (Table 3 and Fig. 4). Untreated control cells showed a typical distribution, with the majority in the G0/G1 phase (77.53%), followed by S phase (16.14%) and G2/M phase (6.33%). Upon treatment, a marked alteration in cell cycle progression was observed. The percentage of cells in the G0/G1 phase decreased significantly to 61.88%, accompanied by a pronounced increase in the S phase population to 29.69%. A slight elevation in the G2/M phase was also detected (8.43%). The substantial accumulation of cells in the S phase indicates that the compound induces cell cycle arrest at the DNA synthesis phase, thereby inhibiting normal cell cycle progression. This disruption may interfere with DNA replication and prevent transition to the G2/M phase. The concurrent reduction in the G0/G1 population further supports the compound's ability to shift proliferating cells toward replication stress. Such S-phase arrest is consistent with inhibition of cell cycle regulatory kinases, including CDK6, which plays a critical role in G1–S transition. The observed cell cycle perturbation correlates well with the compound's potent cytotoxic activity.

Apoptosis induction by compound 7e in HCT-116 cells

The pro-apoptotic effect of compound 7e was evaluated in HCT-116 cells using Annexin V-FITC/PI flow cytometry. The results demonstrated a pronounced induction of programmed cell death compared to untreated control cells (Table 4 and Fig. 5). Treatment with compound 7e significantly increased the total apoptotic population to 31.37%, compared with only 3.29% in control cells, representing nearly a tenfold elevation. This increase was mainly attributed to a substantial rise in late apoptosis (19.51%), along with a measurable increase in early apoptosis (3.89%). In contrast, control cells exhibited minimal early (0.59%) and late (0.14%) apoptotic fractions. Necrotic cell death also increased moderately following treatment (7.97%) compared to control (2.56%), although apoptosis remained the predominant mode of cell death. The predominance of late apoptotic cells suggests effective progression from early apoptotic signaling to irreversible programmed cell death. These findings strongly support that the cytotoxic effect of compound 7e in HCT-116 cells is primarily mediated through apoptosis rather than nonspecific necrosis. The marked apoptotic induction further correlates with its potent dual inhibition of CDK6 and VEGFR-2, reinforcing its mechanistic role in suppressing tumor cell survival pathways. Overall, the apoptosis assay confirms that compound 7e exerts significant anticancer activity by triggering programmed cell death, supporting its potential as a promising therapeutic candidate.

Compound 7e caused a marked upregulation of the pro-apoptotic protein Bax, increasing its concentration from 58.79 pg mL−1 in control cells to 461.38 pg mL−1, corresponding to a 7.85-fold increase. The strong pro-apoptotic response correlates well with its potent dual VEGFR-2/CDK6 inhibitory activity and high selectivity index, supporting the conclusion that compound 7e exerts its anticancer effects via targeted molecular mechanisms leading to programmed cell death.

Effect on cell migration (wound healing assay)

The anti-migratory effect of the tested treatment was evaluated using a wound healing (scratch) assay over 48 hours. Wound width measurements were recorded at 0, 24, and 48 hours, and the mean values ± SD were calculated from triplicate experiments (Fig. 6). At 0 hours, the initial wound widths were comparable between the control group (0.6767 ± 0.0115) and the treated group (0.7200 ± 0.0173), indicating consistent scratch formation and ensuring reliable comparison of migration rates. After 24 hours, the control group exhibited a marked reduction in wound width to 0.4667 ± 0.0058, reflecting active cell migration and partial wound closure. In contrast, the treated group showed a less pronounced reduction (0.5533 ± 0.0321), suggesting inhibition of migratory capacity. At 48 hours, the difference became more evident. The control cells continued to migrate efficiently, reducing the wound width to 0.3667 ± 0.0208. However, the treated group maintained a significantly wider wound gap (0.5367 ± 0.0404), indicating sustained suppression of cell migration.

Compound 7e inhibits HCT-116 cell migration. Wound-healing assay images at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h show delayed closure in 7e-treated cells (D–F) compared with untreated controls (A–C).

When expressed as percentage wound closure relative to time zero, the control group achieved approximately 45.8% closure at 48 hours, whereas the treated group showed only ∼25.5% closure, confirming a substantial reduction in migratory potential upon treatment. The observed inhibition of cell migration may be attributed to the compound's dual inhibition of VEGFR-2 and CDK6. VEGFR-2 plays a critical role in angiogenesis and endothelial cell motility, while CDK6 contributes to proliferation and cell cycle progression. Therefore, simultaneous inhibition of these kinases may impair both proliferative and migratory signaling pathways, thereby limiting tumor progression and metastatic potential. Overall, the wound healing assay demonstrates that 7e significantly suppresses cellular migration compared to untreated control cells, further supporting its potential as an effective anticancer agent with anti-metastatic properties.

Computational examinations

Density functional theory (DFT)

DFT is a quantum mechanical approach for examining the electronic structure of molecular systems. In drug design, DFT calculations predict key physicochemical properties that influence the binding affinity, including molecular orbital distributions and electrostatic potential surfaces. This study characterizes the electronic profile of the noncytotoxic anticancer candidate, 7e, using DFT/B3LYP/6-31G+ (d, p) level to assess its potential as a VEGFR-2 inhibitor. Fig. 7a represents an optimized structure.

Optimized structure (a), HOMO and LUMO distributions (b), the plot of TDOS (c), Mulliken charge distribution (d), and molecular electrostatic potential map (e) at the B3LYP/6-31G+ (d, p) level for 7e.

Analyzing frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs) is essential for understanding the chemical reactivity and kinetic stability of a compound. In the case of 7e, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) is predominantly localized on the chromene and iminocarbohydrazide backbone, identifying this region as the primary site for nucleophilic attack. Conversely, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) is primarily situated over the 6-nitro-chromene moiety and the adjacent carbonyl group, designating this as the preferred site for electrophilic interactions. The energy difference between HOMO (−6.170 eV) and LUMO (−3.246 eV), known as the HOMO–LUMO gap (Egap) was determined to be 2.924 eV (Fig. 7b). This substantial gap indicates a significant level of kinetic stability and moderate chemical reactivity for 7e, suggesting a balanced profile that is neither too inert nor overly reactive, which is a desirable characteristic of a potential drug molecule. From these FMO energies, a range of global reactivity descriptors was derived to quantitatively evaluate the chemical behavior of 7e. As shown in Table 6, the ionization potential (IP = 6.170 eV) and electron affinity (EA = 3.246 eV) were calculated as the negative values of the HOMO and LUMO energies. The chemical potential (µ = −4.708 eV) and electronegativity (χ = 4.708 eV) are equal in magnitude, indicating a propensity to accept rather than donate electrons. The global hardness (η = 1.462 eV) further supports the moderate stability of the molecule, whereas the corresponding softness (σ = 0.684 eV−1) measures its polarizability. The notable global electrophilicity index (ω = 16.201 eV) categorizes 7e as a strong electrophile, capable of accepting a significant amount of electronic charge during an interaction, which directly affects its binding mode to electron-rich residues in the VEGFR-2 active site.

The dipole moment of 2.087 debye calculated for 7e indicates a moderate degree of polarity in the molecule. This polarity, attributed to the presence of several electron-withdrawing groups, such as nitro and sulfonyl moieties, is beneficial for enhancing the solubility of the compound in aqueous biological environments. This may also facilitate electrostatic orientation and dipole–dipole interactions within the binding cavity of the VEGFR-2 protein. Fig. 7c illustrates the total density of states (TDOS) spectrum, which maps the orbital energies onto a continuous spectrum, providing a comprehensive view of the electronic structure of the compound. The TDOS plot corroborates the FMOs analysis, where a distinct gap is observed between the occupied and virtual orbitals, visually marked by the energy region between the highest peak of the occupied states and the beginning of the virtual states. The distribution and intensity of the peaks suggest several potential electronic transitions, which align with the complex, conjugated π-system of the molecule. To understand the intramolecular charge distribution, Mulliken population analysis was performed (Fig. 7d). The most intense negative charges (red) are concentrated on the oxygen atoms of the sulfonyl, nitro, and carbonyl groups, as well as the morpholine nitrogen, marking them as strong nucleophilic centers. Conversely, positive charges (green) were located on various carbon and hydrogen atoms within the structure, particularly those near the electron-withdrawing groups. This uneven charge distribution is crucial for forming specific, localized interactions, such as hydrogen bonding and electrostatic attractions, with the protein target.

The molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) map is shown in Fig. 7e offers a three-dimensional perspective on charge distribution, where regions of negative electrostatic potential (red) gather around the electron-rich oxygen and nitrogen atoms, while positive potential (blue) surrounds the hydrogen atoms and other electron-deficient areas. The map visually identifies the molecular surface that is most vulnerable to electrophilic and nucleophilic attacks.

DFT studies of 7e indicated a molecule with an advantageous electronic profile for drug–receptor interactions. The moderate HOMO–LUMO gap suggests a balance between stability and reactivity, whereas the pronounced electrophilicity and distinct charge separation, as evidenced by MESP and Mulliken analyses, imply a high potential for forming strong, specific interactions with a protein target. The polar nature of the molecule, inferred from its dipole moment, is conducive to aqueous solubility and consequently, targeted binding. These computed electronic properties provide a solid quantum-mechanical foundation for interpreting subsequent molecular docking results, indicating that the binding affinity of 7e towards VEGFR-2 is supported by a combination of electrostatic complementarity and charge-transfer interactions.

Molecular docking

The molecular docking studies demonstrated that the investigated compound 7e exhibited favorable and stable binding modes within the active sites of both CDK6 (PDB 2EUF) and VEGFR-2 (PDB 2OH4), highlighting its potential as a dual inhibitory agent. Within the CDK6 active site, the compound formed key hydrogen bonds with critical residues such as Asp163 and Val101, in addition to extensive hydrophobic interactions with Ile19, Ala41, Ala162, and Leu152, which collectively enhanced binding stability within the ATP-binding pocket. Similarly, docking analysis against VEGFR-2 revealed that the compound formed essential hydrogen bonds with Asp1044 in the DFG domain and Cys917 in the hinge region, while an additional hydrogen bond was observed with Cys1043 in the linker region. Strong hydrophobic contacts with residues including Leu887, Val897, Val846, Leu838, and Leu1033, along with π-related interactions involving Phe1045, further contributed to the stabilization of the ligand–protein complex (Fig. 8A and B). The comparable interaction patterns and favorable binding orientations observed for both kinases suggested that compound 7e effectively accommodated the kinase domains of CDK6 and VEGFR-2, supporting its promise as a potential dual CDK6/VEGFR-2 inhibitor and warranting further molecular dynamics simulations and experimental validation.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations

Following ligand binding, 100 ns MD simulations were performed to evaluate the stability and dynamic behavior of compound 7e in complex with VEGFR-2 and CDK6. Overall, the trajectories revealed comparable dynamic patterns for both complexes, with distinct stability differences favoring VEGFR-2. The backbone RMSD profile (Fig. 9A) demonstrated that the 7e–VEGFR-2 complex stabilized early in the simulation, fluctuating around ∼4.9 Å and remaining stable until the end of the trajectory. In contrast, the CDK6 backbone RMSD initially increased, reaching a maximum deviation of 14.8 Å, before gradually decreasing and stabilizing around 10.28 Å. These values were consistently higher than those observed for VEGFR-2, indicating greater conformational flexibility in the CDK6 complex. The ligand RMSD profile (Fig. 9B) further supported these findings, showing that 7e maintained a more stable binding orientation within the VEGFR-2 active site compared to CDK6. Structural compactness analysis through radius of gyration (RoG) (Fig. 9C) and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) (Fig. 9D) indicated that CDK6 exhibited higher average RoG and SASA values relative to VEGFR-2, suggesting a less compact and more solvent-exposed structure. These results are consistent with the higher backbone fluctuations observed for CDK6.

MD simulation analyses of 7e complexes: (A) protein backbone RMSD of VEGFR-2 and CDK6, (B) ligand RMSD of 7e, (C) radius of gyration, (D) SASA, (E) number of hydrogen bonds, (F) RMSF of Cα atoms, and (G) center-of-mass distance between 7e and the proteins.

Hydrogen bond analysis (Fig. 9E) revealed persistent hydrogen bonding throughout the simulation. The 7e–VEGFR-2 complex maintained predominantly one to two hydrogen bonds, with occasional frames showing three hydrogen bonds. In comparison, the CDK6 complex consistently formed one to three hydrogen bonds, with sporadic formation of four hydrogen bonds, reflecting stable but more dynamic interactions within the binding pocket. RMSF analysis of Cα atoms (Fig. 9F) showed minimal residue-level fluctuations (2–2.5 Å) across most regions of both proteins, confirming global stability. However, localized flexibility was observed at specific residues: TYR1057 in VEGFR-2 (8.25 Å) and ASP10 (5.7 Å), PRO238 (4.8 Å), and ASP89 (4.6 Å) in CDK6. These fluctuations likely correspond to loop or surface-exposed regions rather than the core binding site. Center-of-mass (COM) distance analysis (Fig. 9G) demonstrated sustained ligand positioning within the VEGFR-2 binding pocket, with an average distance of ∼8.9 Å. In contrast, the CDK6 complex exhibited a gradual increase in COM distance up to 27.7 Å, suggesting greater positional variability of 7e during the simulation. Collectively, these findings indicate enhanced dynamic stability of compound 7e within the VEGFR-2 active site compared to CDK6.

MM-GBSA binding free energy analysis

Binding free energy calculations using the MM-GBSA method (Fig. 10) further supported the MD findings. The 7e–VEGFR-2 complex exhibited a favorable total binding free energy of −34.06 kcal mol−1, dominated by strong van der Waals interactions (−53.54 kcal mol−1) and significant electrostatic contributions (−30.65 kcal mol−1). For the CDK6–7e complex, the total binding free energy was −18.86 kcal mol−1, with van der Waals interactions averaging −31.82 kcal mol−1 and electrostatic contributions of −33.23 kcal mol−1. Although electrostatic interactions were comparable between the two systems, the markedly stronger van der Waals contribution in VEGFR-2 explains its superior overall binding affinity.

Per-residue energy decomposition analysis (Fig. S45) of the 7e–VEGFR-2 complex identified several key residues contributing significantly to ligand stabilization, including GLY844 (−1.14 kcal mol−1), LEU884 (−1.77 kcal mol−1), LYS885 (−1.70 kcal mol−1), VAL912 (−1.54 kcal mol−1), LYS1041 (−1.84 kcal mol−1), and ILE888 (−0.90 kcal mol−1). A minor unfavorable contribution was observed for MET881 (+0.4 kcal mol−1). Similarly, decomposition analysis of CDK6 (Fig. S46) revealed important stabilizing residues such as ALA17 (−1.09 kcal mol−1), GLU18 (−1.48 kcal mol−1), ILE19 (−1.08 kcal mol−1), and GLU21 (−0.80 kcal mol−1), while LEU161 (+0.8 kcal mol−1) and ASP102 (+0.7 kcal mol−1) showed slight unfavorable contributions. These findings confirm that 7e engages multiple residues within both binding pockets, with stronger cumulative stabilization in VEGFR-2.

Protein–ligand interaction fingerprint (ProLIF) analysis

ProLIF analysis provided further insight into the persistence and nature of interactions throughout the MD trajectory (Fig. S47). In the 7e–VEGFR-2 complex, more than ten residues displayed high interaction frequencies (>90%), predominantly hydrophobic contacts and van der Waals interactions. Notably, VAL846 (98.9% hydrophobic interaction) and ALA864 (97.5% hydrophobic interaction) were among the most persistent contributors.

For the CDK6 complex, several residues exhibited moderate interaction frequencies (>50%), including ILE19 (53% hydrophobic interaction) and ALA23 (55% hydrophobic interaction). The lower persistence values compared to VEGFR-2 further support the comparatively reduced stability of the CDK6 complex.

Principal component analysis (PCA) and essential dynamics

Principal component analysis (PCA) was employed to investigate collective motions of the Cα atoms in both complexes. Dimensionality selection was based on cumulative variance, scree plot inflection, and eigenvector distribution (Fig. S48A).

For the 7e–VEGFR-2 complex, the first principal component (PC1) accounted for 86.86% of the total variance, while the first three PCs together explained 93.8% of protein motion. In contrast, for CDK6, PC1 accounted for 69% of the variance, with the first three PCs contributing 82.4%. These results indicate that VEGFR-2 dynamics are dominated by fewer, well-defined collective motions, whereas CDK6 exhibits more distributed flexibility.

Based on scree plot behavior and cumulative variance, the top six PCs were selected to describe the essential subspace. Cosine content analysis of the first ten PCs (Fig. S48B) showed low randomness, confirming that the captured motions represent meaningful collective dynamics rather than stochastic fluctuations.

Computational studies

DFT calculations

The geometry of 7e was optimized using Gaussian 09 (B3LYP/6-31+G(d,p) level),59 and key quantum descriptors—including HOMO–LUMO energies, ionization potential, electron affinity, hardness, softness, chemical potential, electronegativity, and electrophilicity—were calculated. Electrostatic potential maps and descriptors are provided in the SI.

Molecular docking studies

Molecular docking simulations were conducted targeting VEGFR-2 and CDK6,60 with comprehensive computational procedures and parameters described in detail in the SI.

Molecular dynamics simulations

MD simulations were performed using GROMACS 2021 with the CHARMM36m force field.61,62 The system was solvated in a TIP3P water box, neutralized with 0.154 M NaCl, and subjected to periodic boundary conditions. After energy minimization, equilibration was carried out under NVT (310 K) and NPT (1 atm) ensembles, followed by a 100 ns production run using the Nose–Hoover thermostat and Parrinello–Rahman barostat. PME (1.2 nm cutoff) handled electrostatics, and bonds were constrained with LINCS. Trajectories were analyzed for RMSD, RMSF, radius of gyration, SASA, hydrogen bonds, and center-of-mass distances. Full simulation parameters are provided in the SI.

MM-GBSA binding free energy

Binding free energies were calculated using the gmx_MMPBSA package.63,64 Energy decomposition per residue was performed to identify key binding contributors. Standard MM-GBSA parameters were applied (ionic strength 0.154 M; igb = 5; dielectric constants 1.0/78.5). Full computational details are included in the SI.

ProLIF analysis

Protein–ligand interactions throughout the MD trajectory were evaluated using the ProLIF package to quantify hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, π-interactions, and salt bridges. Interaction frequencies and contact maps are provided in the SI.

Essential dynamics

Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted to identify major collective motions of the complex.65,66 The covariance matrix was diagonalized, and dominant eigenvectors were analyzed. The free energy landscape was constructed using the first two principal components to identify stable conformational states. Relevant plots are included in the SI.

In silico ADMET and toxicity

Pharmacokinetic and toxicity properties were predicted using BIOVIA Discovery Studio.67 Parameters included BBB permeability, solubility, CYP2D6 inhibition, plasma protein binding, and absorption. Toxicity endpoints comprised Ames mutagenicity, carcinogenicity (TD50), rat LD50, LOAEL, MTD, and skin/ocular irritation. Comparative data for all compounds and sorafenib are provided in the SI.

In vitro studies

VEGFR-2 enzyme inhibition

VEGFR-2 inhibition was evaluated using a commercial kinase assay kit (BPS Bioscience, USA). Reactions were performed in triplicate, and IC50 values were calculated from dose–response curves using GraphPad Prism 9.

Cytotoxicity assay

Cytotoxic activity was assessed using the MTT assay68–72 against MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, HepG-2, HCT-116, WI-38, and WISH cells. Cells (1 × 104/well) were treated with serial compound concentrations for 48 h. After MTT incubation, formazan crystals were dissolved in DMSO, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm. IC50 values were determined from dose–response curves. Selectivity indices (SI) were calculated as IC50 (normal)/IC50 (cancer). Experiments were conducted in triplicate.

Apoptosis analysis

Apoptosis was evaluated by Annexin V-FITC/PI staining.73,74 Treated MDA-MB-231 cells were analyzed using a NovoCyte™ flow cytometer, and 12 000 events per sample were recorded. Viable, early apoptotic, late apoptotic, and necrotic populations were quantified using quadrant analysis.

Cell cycle analysis

Cell cycle distribution was determined by PI staining following ethanol fixation.75 DNA content was analyzed by flow cytometry, and the percentages of cells in sub-G1, G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases were calculated using NovoExpress™ software.

Apoptosis-related protein expression

Bax, Bcl-2, caspase-3, and caspase-8 levels were measured by ELISA following 7e treatment, indicating activation of both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways. Complete data are provided in the SI.

Wound healing (scratch) assay

The effect of compound 7e on HCT-116 cell migration was evaluated using a standard wound healing assay.76 Representative images and quantitative analysis are provided in the SI.

Conclusion

In this study, a pharmacophore-guided approach led to the successful design and synthesis of a novel series of coumarin derivatives as potential dual CDK6/VEGFR-2 inhibitors. Biological evaluation identified compound 7e as the most promising candidate, exhibiting potent and broad-spectrum cytotoxic activity against breast, liver, and colon cancer cell lines, with IC50 values in the low micromolar range comparable to or better than sorafenib. Importantly, 7e demonstrated favorable selectivity toward cancer cells over normal cells, as reflected by high selectivity index values. Mechanistic investigations revealed that compound 7e exerts its anticancer effects primarily through induction of apoptosis, significantly increasing total apoptotic cells (31.37%) compared to control (3.29%), with predominant late apoptosis. This effect was supported by upregulation of Bax, caspase-3, and caspase-8, along with downregulation of Bcl-2. Additionally, cell cycle analysis demonstrated marked S-phase arrest, confirming disruption of cell cycle progression. The compound also significantly inhibited cell migration in the wound healing assay, suggesting potential anti-metastatic activity. Enzymatic assays confirmed potent dual inhibition of VEGFR-2 and CDK6, exceeding the activity of sorafenib, thereby providing a clear mechanistic basis for the observed antiproliferative effects. Computational studies, including DFT calculations, molecular docking, 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations, MM-GBSA binding energy analysis, ProLIF interaction profiling, and essential dynamics evaluation, consistently supported stable and favorable ligand–protein interactions. Furthermore, in silico ADMET and toxicity predictions indicated acceptable pharmacokinetic properties and a favorable safety profile. Overall, compound 7e represents a promising dual VEGFR-2/CDK6 inhibitor with potent, selective, pro-apoptotic, cell cycle-modulating, and anti-migratory activities. These findings support its potential as a valuable lead scaffold for further structural optimization and advanced preclinical development.

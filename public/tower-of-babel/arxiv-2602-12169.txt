Independence Polynomials of graphs and degree of h h -polynomials of edge ideals
Ton That Quoc Tan

Abstract
. Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a finite simple graph. In this paper, we characterize the degree of the h h -polynomial of the edge ideal of G G in terms of the independence number of G G . The key tools are the value of the independence polynomial of G G at − 1 -1 and its derivative. Using this approach, we obtain, in particular, combinatorial formulas for the degree of the h h -polynomial of paths, cycles, bipartite graphs, Cameron-Walker graphs and antiregular graphs.



Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple graph with the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. We identify the vertices of the graph with the the corresponding variables in the polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
k
is some fixed field. The edge ideal of
G
, denoted by
I(G)
, is defined as the monomial ideal

I(G)=\left(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\right)\subseteq R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

It is well known that the edge ideals of simple graphs provide combinatorial aspects of algebraic invariants of monomial ideals such as Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, projective dimension, and so on. Many studies have focused on expressing or bounding such invariants in terms of graph invariants, for example
[
13
,
14
,
5
]
. In this paper, we investigate the degree of the
h
-polynomial of edge ideals, a fundamental yet less explored algebraic invariant.

The
Hilbert function
of the graded ring
R/I(G)
, denoted by
HF_{R/I(G)}
, is the numerical function
HF_{R/I(G)}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined by

HF_{R/I(G)}(i)=\operatorname{dim}_{k}(R/I(G))_{i},

where
(R/I(G))_{i}
denotes the
i
-th graded piece of
R/I(G)
, viewed as a
k
-vector space. The
Hilbert series
of
R/I(G)
, denoted by
H_{R/I(G)}
is defined by

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}HF_{R/I(G)}(i)\ t^{i}.

According to
[
11
, Theorem 13.2]
, we have

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\dfrac{h_{R/I(G)}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}},

where
h_{R/I(G)}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t],h_{R/I(G)}(1)\neq 0
, and
d
is the Krull dimension of
R/I(G)
.

The polynomial
h_{R/I(G)}(t)
is called the
h
-polynomial
of
R/I(G)
. We denote the the degree of this polynomial by
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)
. The recent results focus on the relationship between the degree of the
h
-polynomial and other invariants such as the dimension, projective dimension, depth and regularity of the edge ideal. In
[
7
]
, authors determined the possible values of pairs invariants
(\operatorname{reg}R/I(G),{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t))
of Cameron-Walker graphs. In
[
8
]
, for any two integers
d,r\geq 1
, authors constructed a graph
G
such that
(\operatorname{reg}R/I(G),{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t))=(d,r)
. Using the Hilbert series, Hibi et al. proved in
[
9
]
that
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\operatorname{dim}R/I(G)
for every Cameron-Walker graph. Recently, Biermann et al.
[
1
]
computed
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)
in terms of the independence number for various fundamental classes of graphs, including paths, cycles, and bipartite graphs.

The method in
[
1
]
focus on counting the independence sets of
G
.
Using the independence polynomial of
G
, we recover the results of
[
1
,
9
]
.
We also provide a short and elementary proof of Hibi et al.’s result
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
for Cameron-Walker graphs and derive the main results of this paper.

Theorem 0.1
.

The following statements are true:

(i)
Let
P_{n}
be the path on
n
vertices for
n\geq 1
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(P_{n})}(t)=\begin{cases}\alpha(P_{n}),&\text{if }n\equiv 0,2\pmod{3};\\
\alpha(P_{n})-1,&\text{if }n\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

(ii)
Let
C_{n}
be the cycle on
n
vertices for
n\geq 3
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(C_{n})}(t)=\alpha(C_{n}).

(iii)
Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph with partition
(U,V)
.
Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)

if and only if each connected component obtained from the reduction process in Setup 1 is either

(1)

a star subgraph, or

(2)

a bipartite subgraph
G_{i}
in which all vertices have degree at least 2 and for which
I(G_{i},-1)\neq 0
.

(iv)
Let
G
be a Cameron-Walker graph.

(1)

If
G
is a star-triangle graph, then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\begin{cases}\alpha(G),&\text{if the number of triangles is odd};\\
\alpha(G)-1,&\text{if the number of triangles is even}.\end{cases}

(2)

If
G
is not a star-triangle graph, then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G).

(v)
Let
A_{n}
be the connected antiregular graph and
\overline{A}_{n}
be the disconnected antiregular graph on
n
vertices
(n\geq 2)
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(A_{n})}(t)=\alpha(A_{n}),\quad n\geq 2,

and

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(\overline{A}_{n})}(t)=\alpha(\overline{A}_{n})-1,\quad n\geq 3.

In this paper, Section 1 introduces some notation used throughout the paper.
In Section 2, we present the necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
.
Formulas for the degree of the independence polynomial for paths and cycles are given in Section 3.
In Section 4, we classify bipartite graphs for which
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
.
A simple proof for the degree of the independence polynomial of Cameron-Walker graphs is presented in Section 5.
Finally, in Section 6, we compute
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)
for antiregular graphs.

1.
Preliminary

1.1.
Graph Theory

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(G)
.
The
neighborhood
of a vertex
x_{i}
is the set

N_{G}(x_{i})=\{x_{j}\in V(G)\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\},

and
N_{G}[x_{i}]=N_{G}(x_{i})\cup\{x_{i}\}
. If there is no ambiguity on
G
, we use
N(x_{i})
and
N[x_{i}]
, respectively. A vertex
x_{i}
is
leaf vertex
, or simply
leaf
if its neighborhood contains only one vertex. The
distance

d(x_{i},x_{j})
between two vertices
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
is defined to be the minimum of the lenghts of all possible paths from
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
.
If
X\subseteq V(G)
, then
G[X]
is the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
. By
G-W
we mean the subgraph
G[V-W]
, if
W\subseteq V(G)
.
For brevity, we write
G-x_{i}
instead of
G-\{x_{i}\}
, whenever
W=\{x\}
. We also denote by
G-F
the subgraph of
G
obtained by deleting the edges of
F
, for
F\subseteq E(G)
, and we write shortly
G-x_{i}x_{j}
, whenever
F=\{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\}
.

A subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is an
independent set
of
G
if
\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\notin V(G)
for all
x_{i},x_{j}\in S
. In particular, the empty set
\emptyset
is an independent set of
G
. The
independence number
of
G
, denoted by
\alpha(G)
, is the maximum size of an independent set of
G
. Let
s_{i}(G)
be the number of the independent sets size
i
of
G
. The
independence polynomial
of
G
is defined by

I(G,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{i}(G)t^{i}.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two simple graphs, the
disjoint union
of them, denoted by
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, is the graph with the vertex set
V(G_{1}\cup G_{2})=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and the edge set
E(G_{1}\cup G_{2})=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
. For two given disjoint graphs, their
Zykov sum
, denoted by
G_{1}+G_{2}
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G_{1}+G_{2})=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and the edge set
E(G_{1}+G_{2})=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{uv\mid u\in V(G_{1}),v\in V(G_{2})\}
.

The following results provide an easy recursive technique in evaluating independence polynomial of various graphs.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 2.3)
.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be the simple graph,
x_{i}\in V(G)
and
x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G)
. Then the following equalities hold:

(i)

I(G,t)=I(G-x_{i},t)+tI(G-N[x_{i}],t)
;

(ii)

I(G,t)=I(G-x_{i}x_{j},t)-t^{2}I(G-(N(x_{i})\cup N(x_{j})),t)
.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
10
]
, Corollary 3.8 and Theorem 3.9)
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two vertex-disjoint graphs. Then,

(i)

I(G_{1}\cup G_{2},t)=I(G_{1},t)I(G_{2},t)
;

(ii)

I(G_{1}+G_{2},t)=I(G_{1},t)+I(G_{2},t)-1.

A set of pairwise disjoint edges of
G
is said to be a
matching
of
G
. The
matching number
of
G
is the maximum cardinality of a matching of
G
, and it is denoted by
\mu(G)
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matching of
G
. If the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertices of
\mathcal{M}
has edge set exactly
\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is called an
induced matching
. The induced matching number of
G
is the maximum cardinality of a induced matching of
G
, and it is denoted by
\nu(G)
. It is immediate that
\nu(G)\leq\mu(G)
. If
\nu(G)=\mu(G)
, then
G
is called a
Cameron-Walker graph
.

1.2.
Homological Invariants

Let
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
k
with
{\mathrm{deg}}\ x_{i}=1
for each
i
. For any ideal
I
of
R
, the
dimension
of
R/I
, denoted
\operatorname{dim}R/I
, is the length of the longest chain of prime ideals in
R/I
.

If
I\subseteq R
is a homogeneous ideal, then the
Hilbert series
of
R/I
is

H_{R/I}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{k}(R/I)_{i}t^{i},

where
(R/I)_{i}
denotes the
i
-th graded piece of
R/I
. If
\operatorname{dim}R/I=d
, then the Hilbert series of
R/I
is the form

H_{R/I}(t)=\dfrac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+h_{2}t^{2}+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}}{(1-t)^{d}},

where each
h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
(
[
11
, Theorem 13.2]
) and
h_{R/I}(1)\neq 0
. We say that

h_{R/I}(t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+h_{2}t^{2}+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}

with
h_{s}\neq 0
is the
h
-polynomial
of
R/I
. We call the difference
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I}(t)-\operatorname{dim}R/I
the
a
-variant
of
R/I
(
[
2
, Definition 4.4.4]
) and denote it by
a(R/I)
.

1.3.
Edge ideals

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple graph on
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. We associate with
G
the square-free monomial ideal

I(G)=\left(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\right)\subseteq R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

The ideal
I(G)
is called the
edge ideal
of the graph
G
.

It is known that the dimension of
R/I(G)
is given by

Lemma 1.3
.

\operatorname{dim}R/I(G)=\operatorname{max}\{|S|\mid S\text{ is an independent set of }G\}=\alpha(G).

According to
[
6
, Proposition 6.2.1]
, the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
can be expressed as

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha}\dfrac{f_{i-1}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{i}},

where
f_{i-1}
is the number of independent sets of cardinality
i
in
G
. It follows that

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)\leq\alpha(G).

Remark 1.4
.

a(R/I(G))\leq 0.

2.
Independence Polynomial at
-1
and degree of
h
-polynomial

In this section, we express the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
in terms of the values of the independence polynomial of
G
and its derivative at
-1
.
As a consequence, we characterize when the degree of the
h
-polynomial of an edge ideal attains its maximum value
\alpha(G)
, in terms of the value of the independence polynomial of
G
at
-1
.

In the following lemma, we give an explicit formula for the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
in terms of the independence polynomial of
G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph,
I(G)
be the edge ideal of
G
and
\alpha=\alpha(G)
be the independence number of
G
. Let
I(G,t)
be the independence polynomial of
G
. Then the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
is given by

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\dfrac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha}\dfrac{I^{(i)}(G,-1)}{i!}(1-t)^{\alpha-i},

where
I^{(i)}(G,-1)
denotes the value of the
i
-th derivative of
I(G,t)
evaluated at
-1
.

Proof.

By the claim in the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
, we have

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\dfrac{(1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s})(1-t)^{\alpha}+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}(1-t)^{\alpha-s-1}}{(1-t)^{\alpha}},

where

D_{s}=\sum_{i=s+1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i-1-s}s_{i}(G)\binom{i}{i-1-s},\ \ 0\leq s\leq\alpha-1.

In the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
, we have

\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i-1}s_{i}(G).

On the other hand,

I(G,-1)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i}s_{i}(G).

Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i-1}s_{i}(G)=1-I(G,-1),

since
s_{0}(G)=1
. Therefore,

1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}=1-\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i-1}s_{i}(G)=I(G,-1).

We note that for
0\leq s\leq\alpha
,

\displaystyle I^{(s)}(G,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=s}^{\alpha}(i-s+1)\cdots i\,s_{i}(G)x^{i-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=s}^{\alpha}s_{i}(G)s!\binom{i}{i-s}x^{i-s}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=s}^{\alpha}s_{i}(G)\binom{i}{i-s}x^{i-s}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{I^{(s)}(G,x)}{s!}

Thus,

\displaystyle D_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=s+1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{i-1-s}s_{i}(G)\binom{i}{i-1-s}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{I^{(s+1)}(G,-1)}{(s+1)!}.

Consequently,

\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}(1-t)^{\alpha-s-1}=\sum_{s=1}^{\alpha}\dfrac{I^{(s)}(G,-1)}{s!}(1-t)^{\alpha-s}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
2.1
, we characterize
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)
in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\,h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ I(G,-1)\neq 0.

Furthermore, if
I(G,-1)=0
and
k=\min\{i\mid I^{(i)}(G,-1)\neq 0\}
, then

{\mathrm{deg}}\,h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)-k.

Proof.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
, the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(G)
has the form

h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha}\dfrac{I^{(i)}(G,-1)}{i!}(1-t)^{\alpha-i}.

Therefore,
{\mathrm{deg}}\,h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
I(G,-1)\neq 0
.
Set

A=\{i\mid I^{(i)}(G,-1)\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{N}.

Since
I^{(\alpha)}(G,-1)=s_{\alpha}(G)\alpha!\neq 0
, we have
A\neq\emptyset
. So, by the Well-Ordering Property, there exists

k=\min\{i\mid i\in A\}.

Thus, if
I(G,-1)=0
, then

{\mathrm{deg}}\,h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)-k
.
∎

3.
degree of
h
-polynomials of edge ideals of paths and cycles

In this section, we recover the results in
[
1
, Section 4]
using our approach. As an application, we provide combinatorial formulas for the degree of the
h
-polynomial of paths and cycles.

Let
P_{n}
be the path with vertex set
V(P_{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and edge set

E(P_{n})=\{x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n}\}
.
We begin this section by computing the value of the independence polynomial of
P_{n}
at
-1
and its first derivative at
-1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
P_{n}
denote the path on
n\geq 1
vertices, and let
I(P_{n},t)
be its independence polynomial.
Then,

(i)

I(P_{n},-1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{n}&\hbox{if }n\equiv 0\pmod{3};\\
(-1)^{n-1}&\hbox{if }n\equiv 2\pmod{3};\\
0&\hbox{if }n\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(ii)
If
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then
I^{\prime}(P_{n},-1)=\left(\dfrac{n+2}{3}\right)(-1)^{n-1}\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i).

Case 1.
Let
n=3k
for
k\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We prove the statement by induction on
k
. When
k=0
, then
P_{0}=\emptyset
, therefore
I(P_{0},x)=I(\emptyset,x)=1
. It follows that
I(P_{0},-1)=1=(-1)^{0}
.
Suppose that the argument is true for all positive integers less than
k
, that is
I(P_{3i},-1)=(-1)^{3i}
for all
i<k
. By Lemma
1.1
(ii), we get

I(P_{n},t)=I(P_{3k},t)=I(P_{3k}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)-t^{2}I(P_{3k}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t).

We observe that
P_{3k}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\}=\{x_{1}\}\cup P_{3k-1}
.
By Lemma
1.2
(i), we have

I(P_{3k}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)=I(\{x_{1}\},t)I(P_{3k-1},t)=(1+t)I(P_{3k-1},t).

Moreover,
P_{3k}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2}))=P_{3k-3}=P_{3(k-1)}
. Therefore,
I(P_{3k}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t)=I(P_{3(k-1)},t)
.
By induction, it follows that

I(P_{n},-1)=I(P_{3k},-1)=-(-1)^{2}(-1)^{3(k-1)}=(-1)^{3k}=(-1)^{n}.

Case 2.
Let
n=3k+2
for
k\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will prove the argument by induction on
k
. When
k=0
then
I(P_{2},t)=1+2t
. It follows that
I(P_{2},-1)=-1=(-1)^{2-1}
.
Suppose that the argument is true for all positive integer
i<k
, that is
I(P_{3i+2},-1)=(-1)^{3i+1}
for all
i<k
. By Lemma
1.1
(ii), we get

I(P_{n},t)=I(P_{3k+2},t)=I(P_{3k+2}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)-t^{2}I(P_{3k+2}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t).

We observe that
P_{3k+2}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\}=\{x_{1}\}\cup P_{3k+1}
.
By Lemma
1.2
(i), we have

I(P_{3k+2}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)=I(\{x_{1}\},t)I(P_{3k+1},t)=(1+t)I(P_{3k+1},t).

Furthermore,
P_{3k+2}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2}))=P_{3k-1}=P_{3(k-1)+2}
. Therefore,
I(P_{3k+2}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t)=I(P_{3(k-1)+2},t)
.
By induction, it follows that

I(P_{n},-1)=I(P_{3k+2},-1)=-(-1)^{2}(-1)^{3(k-1)+1}=(-1)^{3k+1}=(-1)^{n-1}.

Case 3.
Let
n=3k+1
for
k\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will prove the statement by induction on
k
. When
k=0
then
I(P_{1},t)=1+t
. It follows that
I(P_{1},-1)=0
.
Suppose that the argument true for all positive integers
i<k
, that is
I(P_{3i+1},-1)=0
for all
i<k
. By Lemma
1.1
(ii), we get

I(P_{n},t)=I(P_{3k+1},t)=I(P_{3k+1}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)-t^{2}I(P_{3k+1}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t).

We observe that
P_{3k+1}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\}=\{x_{1}\}\cup P_{3k}
.
By Lemma
1.2
(i), we have

I(P_{3k+1}-\{x_{1}x_{2}\},t)=I(\{x_{1}\},x)I(P_{3k},t)=(1+t)I(P_{3k+1},t).

Moreover,
P_{3k+1}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2}))=P_{3k-2}=P_{3(k-1)+1)}
. Therefore,
I(P_{3k+1}-(N(x_{1})\cup N(x_{2})),t)=I(P_{3(k-1)+1},t)
.
By induction, it follows that

I(P_{n},-1)=I(P_{3k+1},-1)=0.

(ii). If
n=3k+1
, then we prove the statement by induction on
k
.
When
k=0
, we have
I(P_{1},t)=1+t
, therefore
I^{\prime}(P_{1},t)=1
. It follows that
I^{\prime}(P_{1},-1)=1=\left(\dfrac{1+2}{3}\right)(-1)^{1-1}
. Assume that the statement is true for all
i<k
, that is
I^{\prime}(P_{3i+1},-1)=\left(i+1\right)(-1)^{3i}
, for all
i<k
. By Lemma
1.1
(ii), we have

I(P_{3k+1},t)=(t+1)I(P_{3k},t)-t^{2}I(P_{3(k-1)+1},t).

Differentiating both sides, we obtain

I^{\prime}(P_{3k+1},t)=I(P_{3k},t)+(t+1)I^{\prime}(P_{3k},t)-t^{2}I^{\prime}(P_{3(k-1)+1},t)-2tI(P_{3(k-1)+1},t).

By substituting
t=-1
into the expression above, we get

I^{\prime}(P_{n},-1)=I^{\prime}(P_{3k+1},-1)=I(P_{3k},-1)-(-1)^{2}I^{\prime}(P_{3(k-1)+1},-1).

From part (i) of the lemma and by the induction hypothesis, it follows that

I^{\prime}(P_{n},-1)=(-1)^{3k}+(-1)^{3}k(-1)^{3(k-1)}=(k+1)(-1)^{3k}=\left(\dfrac{n+2}{3}\right)(-1)^{n-1}.

Since
n=3k+1
, we conclude that

I^{\prime}(P_{n},-1)=\left(\frac{n+2}{3}\right)(-1)^{n-1}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

From Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P_{n}
be the path on
n\geq 1
vertices. Then degree of the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(P_{n})
is

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(P_{n})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha(P_{n})-1&\hbox{ if }n\equiv 1\pmod{3};\\
\alpha(P_{n})&\hbox{ if }n\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
, for
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, we have
I(P_{n},-1)=0
and
I^{\prime}(P_{n},-1)=\left(\dfrac{n+2}{3}\right)(-1)^{n-1}\neq 0
. Therefore,
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(P_{n})}=\alpha(P_{n})-1
. If
n\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}
, then
I(P_{n},-1)\neq 0
. It follows that
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(P_{n})}=\alpha(P_{n})
.
∎

From Theorem
3.2
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
P_{n}
be the path on
n\geq 1
vertices. Then,
a(R/I(P_{n}))\in\{-1,0\}
.

Let
C_{n}(n\geq 3)
be the cycle graph with the vertex set
V(C_{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(C_{n})=\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}x_{1}\}
.

In the following lemma, we compute the value of the independence polynomial of a cycle graph at
-1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
C_{n}
be the cycle graph on
n\geq 3
vertices and
I(C_{n},t)
be the independence polynomial of
C_{n}
. Then

I(C_{n},-1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2.(-1)^{n}&\hbox{ if }n\equiv 0\pmod{3};\\
(-1)^{n-1}&\hbox{ if }n\equiv 1,2\pmod{3}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.1
, we have

I(C_{n},t)=I(P_{n},t)-t^{2}I(P_{n-4},t).

Case 1.
If
n\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, then
n-4\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. According to Lemma
3.1
, we have
I(P_{n},-1)=(-1)^{n}
and
I(P_{n-4},-1)=(-1)^{n-3}
. It follows that

I(C_{n},-1)=(-1)^{n}-(-1)^{2}(-1)^{n-3}=2(-1)^{n}.

Case 2.
If
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then
n-4\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
I(P_{n},-1)=0
and
I(P_{n-4},-1)=(-1)^{n-4}
.
It follows that

I(C_{n},-1)=-(-1)^{2}(-1)^{n-4}=(-1)^{n-1}.

Case 3.
If
n\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, then
n-4\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we get
I(P_{n},-1)=(-1)^{n-1}
and
I(P_{n-4},-1)=0
.
It follows that

I(C_{n},-1)=(-1)^{n-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Combining Theorem
2.2
and Lemma

, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let

be the cycle on

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(C_{n})}=\alpha(C_{n}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, we have
I(C_{n},-1)\neq 0
for all

. According to Theorem
2.2
, we get
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(C_{n})}=\alpha(C_{n}).

∎

Corollary 3.6

4.
degree of
h
-polynomials of edge ideals of bipartite graphs

In this section, we study the degree of the
h
-polynomial of the edge ideal of bipartite graphs. In particular, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition under which the degree of the
h
-polynomial of a bipartite graph equals its independence number.

Suppose that
G
has multiple connected components. Then the degree of the
h
-polynomial of
G
can be determined by studying the degrees of the
h
-polynomials of its connected components. This yields the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G=\cup_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
be the disjoint union of the graphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G_{i})}(t)=\alpha(G_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.2
(i) and induction, we have

I(G,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}I(G_{i},t).

Therefore,

I(G,-1)\neq 0
if and only if
I(G_{i},-1)\neq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. According to Theorem
2.2
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G=\sum_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
be the Zykov sum of the disjoint graphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(G_{i},-1)\neq n-1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.2
(ii) and induction, we have

I(G,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(G_{i},t)-(n-1).

Therefore,

I(G,-1)\neq 0
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(G_{i},-1)\neq n-1
. According to Theorem
2.2
, the conclusion follows.
∎

We observe that a complete
n
-partite graph is the Zykov sum of
n
complements of complete graphs.
From Lemma
4.2
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G=K_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}
be the completed
n
-partite graph with
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}\geq 1
. Then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=m_{1}.

In particular, if
K_{m,n}
with
m\geq n\geq 1
is a complete bipartite graph, then

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(K_{m,n})}=m.

Proof.

We note that
K_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}=\overline{K}_{m_{1}}+\overline{K}_{m_{2}}+\cdots+\overline{K}_{m_{n}}
. But for every
i
,
I(\overline{K}_{m_{i}},t)=(1+t)^{m_{i}}
. Therefore,

I(\overline{K}_{m_{1}},-1)+\cdots+I(\overline{K}_{m_{n}},-1)=0\neq n-1.

By Lemma
4.2
,

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(K_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}})}(t)=\alpha(K_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}})=m_{1}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S_{n}
be a star graph where
n\geq 0
. Then,
I(S_{n},-1)=-1
and

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(S_{n})}=\alpha(S_{n}).

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{0}
is the center vertex of
S_{n}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are
n
leaves of
S_{n}
. By Lemma
1.1
(i), we have

I(S_{n},t)=I(S_{n}-x_{0},t)+tI(S_{n}-N[x_{0}],t).

Moreover,
I(S_{n}-N[x_{0}],t)=I(\emptyset,t)=1
, therefore
I(S_{n}-N[x_{0}],-1)=1
. By Lemma
1.2
(i), we have

I(S_{n}-x_{0},t)=I(\{x_{1}\},t)\cdots I(\{x_{n}\},t)=(1+t)^{n}.

It follows that
I(S_{n}-u,-1)=0
.
Thus,

I(S_{n},-1)=-1.

According to Theorem
2.2
, we have
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(S_{n})}=\alpha(S_{n}).

∎

For any simple connected graph with two leaf vertices of distance
3
, the degree of the
h
-polynomial is strictly less than its maximal possible value.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a simple connected graph and let

be leaf vertices of distance

I(G,-1)=0
, and
{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)<\alpha(G)
.

Proof.

Suppose that

and

. By Lemma
1.1
(i), we have

I(G,t)=I(G-w,t)+t\,I(G-N[w],t).

Since

, the graph
G-w
contains an isolated vertex, namely
u
, and the graph

G-N[w]
also contains an isolated vertex, namely
y
. Therefore,

I(G-w,-1)=0\quad\text{and}\quad I(G-N[w],-1)=0.

Hence,
I(G,-1)=0
. By Theorem
2.2
, we get

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)<\alpha(G)
.
∎

-polynomial of an arbitrary simple connected graph, we introduce the following setup.

Let

Theorem 4.17

Under Setup 2, we have

{\mathrm{deg}}\ h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)\text{ if and only if }\sum_{i=1}^{k}I\left(G_{i-1}-(N_{G_{i-1}}(u_{i})\cup N_{G_{i}}(v_{i})),-1\right)\neq 1.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.1
(ii), we have

I(G_{0},t)=I(G_{1})-t^{2}I(G_{0}-(N(u_{1})\cup N(v_{1})),t).

It follows that

Proceeding recursively, we obtain

(3)

Proof.

We note that the independence polynomial of

(i) Suppose that

.

(ii) Let

∎

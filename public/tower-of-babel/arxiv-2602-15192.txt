Zariski equisingularity of surface singularities in ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} by a local invariant
Adam Parusiński and Laurenţiu Păunescu

Abstract
. We associate to every analytic surface singularity ( V , 0 ) (V,0) in ( ℂ 3 , 0 ) (\mathbb{C}^{3},0) , not necessarily isolated, an invariant mult ∗ ⁡ ( V ) \snu^{*}(V) and show that an analytic family of such singularities ( V t , 0 ) (V_{t},0) , t ∈ ( ℂ l , 0 ) t\in(\mathbb{C}^{l},0) , is generically Zariski equisingular if and only if mult ∗ ⁡ ( V t ) \snu^{*}(V_{t}) is constant. The invariant, that we call the multiplicity sequence of V V , takes into account the multiplicities of the successive discriminants of V V by generic corank one projections.

In a seminal Astérisque paper
[
18
]
Bernard Teissier defined for every isolated complex analytic hypersurface singularity
(X_{0},0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)

X_{0}=f^{-1}(0)

\mu^{*}(X_{0})=(\mu^{n+1}(X_{0}),\mu^{n}(X_{0}),\ldots,\mu^{1}(X_{0}),\mu^{0}(X_{0})),

Geometrically,

is the Milnor number of the section of

by a generic linear subspace of dimension

\mu^{1}(X_{0})=\mult_{0}(X_{0})-1

\mu^{0}(X_{0}))=1

Teissier showed in
[

]
that if

is constant in a family

satisfies Whitney’s Conditions. The converse implication was showed by Briançon and Speder in
[
7
]
.

In
[
23

O. Zariski proposed the notion of algebro-geometric equisingularity of a hypersurface

Zariski equisingularity of families of isolated surface singularities

Remark 1.2

One important feature of the Zariski equisingularity is that it implies equimultiplicity, see

\nu
-transverse Zariski equisingular.
∎

2.
The multiplicity sequence

Given an analytic hypersurface
V=f^{-1}(0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
defined by an

f\in\mathbb{C}\{x,y,z\}
that we assume reduced. The multiplicity sequence of
V
takes into account the multiplicities of the successive discriminants of
f
.
Since the discriminant
D_{f}
of
f
is not necessarily reduced we have to first replace
D_{f}
by its reduced form
(D_{f})_{red}
or, what is more convenient, to use the generalized discriminants of
D_{f}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that for a germ
V=f^{-1}(0)
with
f
reduced a local system of coordinates

x,y,z
at the origin is
\nu
-transverse .
Then the
multiplicity sequence of
(V,0)
is defined by

\snu^{*}(V)=(\mult_{0}(V),\mult_{0}(D_{f}),i_{0},\mult_{0}(D^{i_{0}}_{D_{f}})),

where
i_{0}=\idiscr(D_{f})
.

In the above defintion the value of
i_{0}=\idiscr(D_{f})
is important. We do not get the same information if we just replace
D^{i_{0}}_{D_{f}}
by
D_{(D_{f})_{red}}
.

Note that the multiplicity sequence is defined with respect to a
\nu
-transverse system of coordinates and
a priori may depend on this system coordinates. We show that it is in fact independent of such a choice.
For this we first remark that the multiplicity sequence is constant in
\nu
-transverse Zariski equisingular families.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given an analytic family of surface singularities
V_{t}=f_{t}^{-1}(0)
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
given by
(
1.4
).
If this family is
\nu
-transverse Zariski equisingular then the multiplicity sequence
\snu^{*}(V_{t},0)

(with respect to the fixed system of coordinates
x,y,z
) is independent of
t
.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that the multiplicity is constant in Zariski equisingular families, see Remark
1.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The multiplicity sequence of
V
is independent of the choice of local system of coordinates.

Proof.

We use Theorem
1.6
and Proposition
2.2
.

Let
(x,y,z)=\varphi(X,Y,Z)
be an analytic change of local coordinates
(x,y,z)=\varphi(X,Y,Z)
. We put it in a family

(2.1)

\displaystyle(x,y,z)=\Phi(X,Y,Z,t)=(1-t)(X,Y,Z)+t\varphi(X,Y,Z),\quad t\in\mathbb{C},

that defines the family

F(X,Y,Z,t)=F_{t}(X,Y,Z)=f(\Phi(X,Y,Z,t))
and
V_{t}=F_{t}^{-1}(0)
. Note that for this family the system of local coordinates
X,Y,Z
is fixed and the hypersurface
V_{t}=F_{t}^{-1}(0)
is changing.
If this family is
\nu
-transverse equisingular (at least for all
t
on a continuous curve in
\mathbb{C}
joining
0
and
1
) then, by Proposition
2.2
, multiplicity sequence of
V
is the same in the system given by
t=0
and the one given by
t=1
.

Firstly, we remark that the multiplicity sequence is preserved by a small linear change of coordinates. Indeed, it follows from Theorem
1.5
that shows in particular that a small linear change of a
\nu
-transverse system of coordinates is
\nu
-transverse. Then the statement for an arbitrary linear change of coordinates between two
\nu
-transverse system of coordinates follows from the fact that the multiplicity is generically constant in analytic families of singularities.

Secondly, we consider an arbitrary analytic change of local coordinates

(x,y,z)=\varphi(X,Y,Z)
with an extra condition
D\varphi(0)=\id
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If we suppose in (
2.1
) that
D\varphi(0)=\id
and that the system of coordinates for
t=0
is
\nu
-transverse then for any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
the system
X,Y,Z
is
\nu
-transverse for
V_{t_{0}}

and the family
V_{t}
is
\nu
-transverse Zariski equisingular at
t_{0}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Theorem
1.6
. Indeed, apply this theorem to
\varphi(X,Y,Z,T,b)=\Phi(X,Y+bZ,Z,T-t_{0})
,
T
and
b
being parameters.
∎

Finally, for an arbitrary analytic change of local coordinates,
we first use Lemma
2.4
to join the given system of local coordinates to its linear part, then use the result for a linear change of coordinates.
∎

3.
multiplicity sequence for isolated singularities

For isolated singularities the multiplicity sequence can be computed in terms of
\mu^{*}(V_{t})
,
k(V_{t})
, and
\varphi(V_{t})
and, as we explain below, the constancy of
\mu^{*}(V_{t})
,
k(V_{t})
, and
\varphi(V_{t})
is equivalent to the constancy of
\snu^{*}(V_{t},0)
. Thus Théorème 3.5.3 of
[
6
]
and Théorème 4.1
of
[
4
]
are consequences of Theorem
0.2
.

For an isolated surface singularity
(V,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)

the following formulas are given in
[
6
]
, see also
[
4
, Proposition 1.1]
, the first one also follows from
[
18
, Lemme 5.10]
,

(a)

the multiplicity of the discriminant locus
\Delta_{f}=D_{f}^{-1}(0)
is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mult_{0}(\Delta_{f})=\mu^{2}(V)+\mu^{1}(V).

(b)

the Milnor number of
\Delta_{f}
is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu(\Delta_{f})=\mu^{3}(V)-\mu^{1}(V)+2k(V)+3\varphi(V)+1.

Note that for a generic linear projection
D_{f}
is reduced (by the isolated singularity assumption) and hence
\idiscr(D_{f})=1
.
Then, as follows from
[
18
, Lemme 5.10]
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mult_{0}(D_{D_{f}})=\mu(D_{f})+\mult_{0}(D_{f})-1.

We have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

For an isolated surface singularity
(V,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
the multiplicity sequence equals

(3.4)

\displaystyle\snu^{*}(V)=(\mu^{1}(V)+\mu^{0}(V),\mu^{2}(V)+\mu^{1}(V),1,\mu^{3}(V)+\mu^{2}(V)+2k(V)+3\varphi(V)).

In a family of isolated surface singularities
(V_{t},0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
the constancy of the
multiplicity sequence is equivalent to the constancy of
\mu^{*}(V_{t})
,
k(V_{t})
, and
\varphi(V_{t})
.

Proof.

The formula (
3.4
) follows from the formulas (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
). Therefore the constancy of

,

, and
\varphi(V_{t})
implies the constancy of the multiplicity sequence. For the converse we note that all these invariants are upper semicontinuous in families. For Teissier’s numbers it follows from
[

]
, for

and

it is shown in
[

Appendix. Generalized discriminants

We recall the notion of generalized (or subdiscriminants) discriminants, see Appendix IV of
[
20
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
16
]
or
[
13
]
.
Let
{k}
be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero and let

(4.1)

\displaystyle F(Z)=Z^{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{i}Z^{d-i}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(Z-\xi_{i})\in{k}[Z],

be a polynomial with coefficients
a_{i}\in{k}
. Let
\xi_{1}
,…,
\xi_{d}
be the roots of
F
in the algebraic closure
\overline{{k}}
of
{k}
. Then
the generalized discriminants (or subdiscriminants)

can be defined in terms of the roots

D_{d-j+1}=\sum_{r_{1}<\cdots<r_{j}}\,\prod_{k<l;\,k,l\in\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{j}\}}(\xi_{k}-\xi_{l})^{2}.

Since the
D_{d-j+1}
are symmetric polynomials in the
\xi_{i}
they are polynomials in the coefficients
a_{i}
and one can even show that they are polynomials with integer coefficients, that is belong to
\mathbb{Z}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}]
.
In particular
D_{1}
is the classical discriminant of
F
, and
F
admits exactly
k
distinct roots in
\overline{{k}}
if and only if
D_{1}=\cdots=D_{d-k}=0
and

D_{d-k+1}\neq 0
.

If
F
is not reduced, that is if it is has multiple roots, then the generalized discriminants can replace the (classical) discriminant of
F_{red}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
F
has exactly
s
distinct roots in

\overline{{k}}
of multiplicities
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},...,m_{s})
. Then there is a positive constant
C=C_{s,\mathbf{m}}
, depending only on

\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},...,m_{s})
, such that the generalized discriminant
D_{s}
of
F
and
the standard discriminant
D_{F_{red}}
of
F_{red}
are related by the formula
D_{d-s+1,F}=CD_{F_{red}}
.

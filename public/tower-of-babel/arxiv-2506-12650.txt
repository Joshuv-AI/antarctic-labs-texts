Lower Bounds on Quantum Tunneling for Excited States
Charles L. Fefferman, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
We revisit the problem of quantum tunneling for a particle moving in the continuum, and in the absence of a magnetic field. In all spatial dimensions, we extend previous results to the case where the single-well potential satisfies reflection-symmetry.

Introduction

We study double-well Hamiltonians on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
of the form

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,d}=P^{2}+\lambda^{2}V_{d}(X)

(1.1)

where
V_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
is a double-well potential (with separation
d
between the wells) and
\lambda
is the well-depth;
P\equiv-\operatorname{i}\nabla
is the momentum operator and
X
is the position operator.

We are interested in double-well potentials of the following form: let
a>0
,
d\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\setminus\Set{0}
. For convenience and without loss we choose
d
along the positive
1
-axis:
\left\lVert d\right\rVert=\langle d,e_{1}\rangle
. We loosely refer to both the vector
de_{1}
and its magnitude by
d
. Then we take

\displaystyle V_{d}(x):=v(x)+v(x-d)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu})

(1.2)

where
v:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
is a single-atom potential with
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subseteq B_{a}(0)
and
d>2a
so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(v)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v(\cdot-d))=\varnothing\,.

(1.3)

Generally speaking we need at least
v\in C^{2}
for most of our results.

The single well Hamiltonian (independent of
d
) is given by

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}:=P^{2}+\lambda^{2}v(X)

(1.4)

and we assume
v
is a trapping potential so that it has discrete spectrum of bound states below its essential spectrum comprised of the Laplacian’s scattering states. We denote the sequence of single well bound states and energies by
\Set{\varphi_{j}}_{j\geq 1}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
and
\Set{e_{j}}_{j\geq 1}\subseteq(-\infty,0)
, respectively. Both depend on
\lambda
, but we typically suppress this dependence if there’s no ambiguity. For the most part we
do not
require
v
have a unique minimum (because we don’t make use of the harmonic oscillator approximation). For the remainder of this introduction we assume that the sequence of eigenvalues
\Set{e_{j}}_{j\geq 1}
is simple.

For fixed
d
and
\lambda
sufficiently large, it was shown in
[
Sim83
]
, in the above setting, that the double-well Hamiltonian
H_{\lambda,d}
also has a sequence of discrete eigenvalues below its essential spectrum, which we denote by
\Set{E_{j}^{\pm}}_{j\geq 1}
. For fixed
j\geq 1
, each pair of levels
E_{j}^{\pm}
should be understood as the splitting of the single-well level
e_{j}
due to quantum mechanical tunneling between the two wells. Our main object of interest is thus the
j
th splitting

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\lambda,d):=E_{j}^{+}-E_{j}^{-}\qquad(j\geq 1)\,.

(1.5)

The quantity
\Delta_{1}(\lambda,d)
is very well-studied. Under the assumption that
v
is smooth and has a unique non-degenerate minimum, one has the asymptotic formula
[
Sim84
,
HS84
]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\left(\Delta_{1}(\lambda,d)\right)\to S_{E}(0,d),\qquad{\rm as}\quad\lambda\to\infty,

where
S_{E}
is the classical Euclidean action from the point
0
to the point
d
:

\displaystyle S_{E}(x,y):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}T>0;\\
\gamma:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{\nu};\\
\gamma(0)=x,\ \ \gamma(T)=y\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\left\lVert\dot{\gamma}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+V(\gamma(t))\right]\operatorname{d}{t}\,,

i.e., the value of the classical action, with the sign of the potential flipped (hence the name Euclidean), on the extremizing solution of Newton’s equations

\displaystyle\ddot{\gamma}=\nabla V\circ\gamma\,.

with appropriate boundary conditions; it is also equal to the Agmon distance
[
Sim84
]
. With no assumption on a unique non-degenerate minimum, it was shown in
[
FLW18
]
that for fixed
d
and all
\lambda
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(\lambda,d)\gtrsim\exp\left(-\lambda C_{d,v}\right)

for some constant
C_{d,v}>0
, which is independent of
\lambda
.

The basic question we wish to address in this note, with partial answer in the case of reflection symmetric
v
, is:

Are there lower bounds on
\Delta_{j}(\lambda,d)
for excited states,
j\geq 2
?

An emergent quantity, instrumental in the study of
\Delta_{j}(\lambda,d)
, is the so-called
hopping coefficient
; see, for example,
[
DS10
,
FSW22
,
SW22
,
SK54
,
AM76
]

Warm up–the one-dimensional case

It is instructive to obtain a precise expression for
\rho
in one space dimension. Remarkably, in this one-dimensional setting, there is an explicit expression for it.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d>2a
. For any
j\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
,
\rho_{j}
as defined in
eq.

1.6
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(d)=C_{j}(\lambda,v)\sqrt{-e_{j}}\operatorname{e}^{-\sqrt{-e_{j}}d}

where
C_{j}(\lambda,v)\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Set{0}
: this constant depends on
\lambda
and
v
, but not on
d
.

Proof.

For convenience, since
j
is fixed in this discussion, we drop it from the expressions during the proof. Hence,
\varphi\equiv\varphi_{j}
etc.

We define
\psi:=\varphi(\cdot-d)
and
c:=\frac{d}{2}
for convenience, and make the following chain of observations:

\displaystyle\rho(d)

\displaystyle:=\langle\varphi,\lambda^{2}v\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\varphi}\lambda^{2}v\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,c]}\overline{\varphi}\lambda^{2}v\psi

(
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subseteq[-a,a]
&
a<c<d-a
)

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,c]}\left(\overline{e\varphi+\varphi^{\prime\prime}}\right)\psi\qquad

(
\lambda^{2}v\varphi=e\varphi+\varphi^{\prime\prime}
)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IBP}}}{{=}}e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}+\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(c)}\psi(c)-\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(-\infty)}\psi(-\infty)-\int_{(-\infty,c]}\varphi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IBP}}}{{=}}e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}+\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(c)}\psi(c)-

\displaystyle\qquad-\overline{\varphi(c)}\psi^{\prime}(c)-\overline{\varphi(-\infty)}\psi^{\prime}(-\infty)+\int_{(-\infty,c]}\overline{\varphi}\psi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}+\overline{\varphi^{\prime}(c)}\psi(c)-\overline{\varphi(c)}\psi^{\prime}(c)+\langle\varphi,\psi^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}

Here we used the fact that thanks to
\varphi\in L^{2}
, the terms at
-\infty
vanish.

Now we remark that the function
\psi(x)=\varphi(x-d)
also obeys the Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle-\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)+\lambda^{2}v(x-d)\psi(x)=e\psi(x).

Since
d>2a
,
v(x-d)
which is supported in the interval
[d-a,d+a]
, vanishes for
x\leq c=d/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle-\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)=e\psi(x)\qquad{\rm for}\quad x\leq c\,.

It follows that
\langle\varphi,\psi^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}=-e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}((-\infty,c])}
and we find

\rho(d)=\left(\overline{\varphi^{\prime}}\psi-\overline{\varphi}\psi^{\prime}\right)(c)\ =\ \overline{\varphi^{\prime}(c)}\varphi(-c)-\overline{\varphi(c)}\varphi^{\prime}(-c).

(2.1)

Since
a<c=d/2<d-a
, the expression for the hopping coefficient
\rho(d)
, displayed in
eq.

2.1
, depends
only on the values of
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}

at a point outside the support of
v
, where there is an explicit exponential form for the wave-function. Hence, it is simple to evaluate the expression for
\rho(d)
in
eq.

2.1
, up to a non-vanishing
d-
independent factor. We proceed with this evaluation.

Setting
\kappa:=\sqrt{-e}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}A_{-}e^{\kappa x},&x\leq-a\\
A_{+}e^{-\kappa x},&x\geq a.\end{cases}

where the constants
A_{\pm}
are both non-vanishing, and depend on
v
and
\lambda
, but not on
d
.
That neither
A_{\pm}
do not vanish follows from global existence and uniqueness on
\mathbb{R}
for the ODE
h_{\lambda}\varphi=e\varphi
.

Since
c=d/2>a
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(c)

\displaystyle=A_{+}\operatorname{e}^{-\kappa c},\quad\varphi^{\prime}(c)=-A_{+}\kappa\operatorname{e}^{-\kappa c}

\displaystyle\varphi(-c)

\displaystyle=A_{-}\operatorname{e}^{-\kappa c},\quad\varphi^{\prime}(-c)=A_{-}\kappa\operatorname{e}^{-\kappa c}

Substitution into
eq.

2.1
, we obtain

\rho(d)=-\overline{A_{+}}\kappa e^{-\kappa c}A_{-}e^{-\kappa c}-\overline{A_{+}}e^{-\kappa c}A_{-}\kappa e^{-\kappa c}=-2\overline{A_{+}}A_{-}\kappa e^{-2\kappa c}=-2\overline{A_{+}}A_{-}\kappa e^{-\kappa d}.

(2.2)

Setting
C=-2\overline{A_{+}}A_{-}\kappa
, which depends on
\lambda
,
v
but not on
d

completes the proof.
∎

Note that if
v(x)=v(-x)
then
A_{-}=\pm A_{+}
(eigenstates are either even or odd) and we have

\displaystyle\rho(d)=\mp 2|A_{+}|^{2}\kappa e^{-\kappa d}.

A general formula for the hopping in two and higher dimensions

a
c

Figure 1:
Setup for double-well. The support radius of each well is
a
and the half-distance between the centers of the wells is
c:=d/2
. We assume
\left\lVert c\right\rVert>a

We would like to generalize the argument of the previous section to spatial dimensions
\nu\geq 2
. Recall
d\equiv\left\lVert d\right\rVert\equiv\left\lVert d\right\rVert e_{1}

We shall show that

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
j\geq 1
, assume
d>2a
and let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
be a subset with smooth boundary such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(v)\subseteq\operatorname{interior}(D)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \operatorname{supp}(v(\cdot-d))\subseteq\operatorname{interior}(D^{c})\,.

Then, if
n:\partial D\to\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
is the normal unit vector pointing out of
D
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\lambda,d)=-\int_{\partial D}\left((\nabla\overline{\varphi_{j}})R^{d}\varphi_{j}-\overline{\varphi_{j}}\nabla R^{d}\varphi_{j}\right)\cdot n\,.

(3.1)

In particular, with the choice

\displaystyle D:=\Set{x_{1}\leq c}\,,\qquad\partial D=\Set{x_{1}=c}\,,\qquad n=e_{1}

which satisfies the assumptions above, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(\lambda,d)=\int_{\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}}{x_{1}=d/2}}\left[(\partial_{1}\overline{\varphi})R^{d}\varphi_{j}-\overline{\varphi}\partial_{1}R^{d}\varphi_{j}\right]\,.

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

The formula continues to hold in the magnetic case, if one assumes that
A:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, the magnetic vector potential, is such that

\displaystyle[P_{i},A_{i}(X)]=0\qquad(i=1,\dots,\nu)\,.

(3.3)

Moreover,
R^{d}\varphi_{j}
should now be defined using
magnetic
translations.

Remark 3.3
.

The formula
eq.

3.2
is not new. We traced its earliest appearance, or at least in similar guise, to
[
LL77
, Problem 50.3]
; see the expression for their
E_{1}-E_{0}
. See also
[
HS84
]
and
[
FMR25
, Eq-n (4.1)]
.

Proof.

We first note two identities that follow from Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle f\operatorname{div}V=\operatorname{div}(fV)-V\cdot\operatorname{grad}f

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle V\cdot\operatorname{grad}f=\operatorname{div}(fV)-f\operatorname{div}V

(3.5)

Integrating over
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, and integration by parts gives:

\displaystyle\langle\Delta f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{D}\overline{\Delta f}g

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\overline{(\operatorname{div}\circ\operatorname{grad}f)}g

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\left[\operatorname{div}(g\operatorname{grad}\overline{f})-(\operatorname{grad}\overline{f})\cdot(\operatorname{grad}g)\right]\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

(Using
eq.

3.4
with
V=\operatorname{grad}\overline{f}
)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\left[\operatorname{div}(g\operatorname{grad}\overline{f})-\operatorname{div}(\overline{f}\operatorname{grad}g)+\overline{f}\operatorname{div}\circ\operatorname{grad}g\right]

(Using
eq.

3.5
with
V=\operatorname{grad}g
)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\Delta g\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}+\int_{D}\operatorname{div}\left((\nabla\overline{f})g-\overline{f}\nabla g\right)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\Delta g\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}+\int_{\partial D}\left((\nabla\overline{f})g-\overline{f}\nabla g\right)\cdot n\ .

Here,
n
denotes the unit normal on
\partial D
, which points exterior to
D
.

Again as for the 1D case, for convenience we denote
c:=d/2
and will eventually integrate over the hyperplane
x_{1}=c
. For convenience we define
\psi:=R^{d}\varphi_{j}
and also drop the
j
subscripts since
j
is fixed.

Let now
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
be any reasonable subset such that
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subseteq\operatorname{interior}(D)
and
\operatorname{supp}v(\cdot-d)\subseteq\operatorname{interior}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\setminus D)
. Then for
\rho
we find

\displaystyle\rho(d)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\varphi,\lambda^{2}v\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\overline{\varphi}\lambda^{2}v\psi=\int_{D}\overline{\lambda^{2}v\varphi}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\overline{\left(e-P^{2}\right)\varphi}\psi

\displaystyle=e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}-\langle P^{2}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}\,.

Now using the various integration by parts formulae, we get

\displaystyle\rho(d)

\displaystyle=e\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}-\langle\varphi,P^{2}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}-\int_{\partial D}\left((\nabla\overline{\varphi})\psi-\overline{\varphi}\nabla\psi\right)\cdot n\,.

A few observations are in order. First, since
\psi\equiv R^{d}\varphi
, since we are integrating within
D
which does not intersect
\operatorname{supp}(v(\cdot-d))
, on
D
,

\displaystyle P^{2}\psi

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{on}\,D}}{{=}}\left[P^{2}+\lambda^{2}v(X-d)\right]\psi

\displaystyle=R^{d}\left[P^{2}+\lambda^{2}v(X)\right]\varphi

\displaystyle=R^{d}e\varphi=e\psi

so that
eq.

3.1
follows.
∎

4
Reflection symmetric potentials

Suppose
v
is reflection symmetric about the bisecting plane,
x_{1}=c=d/2
, between the two wells.
That is,
Uv=v
, where

U:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
is defined by:

\displaystyle(Uf)(x_{1},x_{\perp}):=f(2c-x_{1},x_{\perp}),\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})\ .

For
j=1
, the ground state
\varphi_{1}
enjoys the same symmetry. Indeed, we know that
\varphi_{1}
is both non-degenerate and positive, and
U\varphi_{1}
is also an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian
h
, so we must have
U\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{1}
which is the desired symmetry. However, for higher
j
,
\varphi_{j}
may fail to obey the same symmetry:
\varphi_{j}
is not necessarily positive nor non-degenerate. However, thanks to
[h,U]=0
, the eigenspace of
h
breaks into a direct sum of even and odd functions (
\ker\left(U\pm\mathds{1}\right)
) and so one may
choose
all eigenfunctions of
h
to have definite parity. We assume this is indeed the case and hence our result applies to all eigenfunctions of
h
.

We then see that if
U\varphi_{j}=\pm\varphi_{j}
,
eq.

3.2
reduces to

\displaystyle\rho(d)=\pm\left.\partial_{x_{1}}\right|_{x_{1}=\frac{d}{2}}\int_{x^{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu-1}}\left|\varphi_{j}(x_{1},x^{\perp})\right|^{2}\operatorname{d}{x^{\perp}}\,.

(4.1)

The following theorem estimates the dependence of
\rho_{j}
on the distance
d
, and thus may be useful in the fixed
\lambda
, large
d
regime:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
be given. If
Uv=v
(so
U\varphi_{j}=\pm\varphi_{j}
can be arranged for all
j\geq 1
), then for any
j\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
C_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\rho(d)\right|\geq C_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)\sqrt{-e_{j}}\exp\left(-d\sqrt{-e_{j}+\varepsilon}\right)

(4.2)

Proof.

We perform a Fourier transform of
\varphi_{j}
on all coordinates
but
the first one, the result of which we name

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(x_{1},p_{\perp})\equiv\int_{x_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu-1}}\exp\left(-2\pi\operatorname{i}x_{\perp}\cdot p_{\perp}\right)\varphi_{j}(x_{1},x_{\perp})\operatorname{d}{x_{\perp}}\,.

Schrödinger’s equation for
\varphi_{j}
, outside of
\operatorname{supp}(v)
, implies for
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}
the ODE

\displaystyle\left(-\partial_{1}^{2}+\left\lVert p_{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\cdot,p_{\perp})=e_{j}\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\cdot,p_{\perp})\qquad(p_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu-1})

whose
L^{2}
solution is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(x_{1},p_{\perp})=A(p_{\perp})\exp\left(-\left|x_{1}\right|\sqrt{\left\lVert p_{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}-e_{j}}\right)\qquad(x_{1}\in\mathbb{R})

(4.3)

for some function
A
such that
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
; in writing this expression we have used the fact
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}
is
L^{2}
at
x_{1}=\pm\infty
as well as the hypothesis on reflection symmetry. This now yields the expression from
eq.

4.1

\displaystyle\rho(d)=\mp 2\int_{p_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu-1}}\sqrt{\left\lVert p_{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}-e_{j}}|A(p_{\perp})|^{2}\exp\left(-d\sqrt{\left\lVert p_{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}-e_{j}}\right)\operatorname{d}{p_{\perp}}\,.

A theorem of Aronszajn
[
Aro57
]
implies that
\varphi_{j}
can’t be zero on any open set. If
\varphi_{j}
vanishes on the hyperplane
x_{1}=c
, then also
\varphi_{j}=0
for
x_{1}>c
, contradicting Aronszajn. Therefore
\varphi_{j}
can’t vanish identically on
x_{1}=c
. Hence the partial fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}
can’t be identically zero on
x_{1}=c
. Agmon’s estimate (see
A.1
below) implies that
\varphi_{j}
is exponentially decreasing in all its arguments, outside of the support of
a
(which the hyperplane
x_{1}=c
is) hence
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(c,\cdot)
is real-analytic. As a real-analytic function which is not identically zero,
\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(c,\cdot)
has
L^{2}(B_{R}(0))
norm non-zero for any
R
. By
eq.

4.3
, the same is true for
A
, i.e., for any
R>0
there exists some
C_{R}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{p_{\perp}\in B_{R}(0)}|A(p_{\perp})|^{2}\operatorname{d}{p_{\perp}}\geq C_{R}>0

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\rho(d)\right|\geq 2C_{R}\sqrt{-e_{j}}\exp\left(-d\sqrt{R^{2}-e_{j}}\right)

and hence the result.

∎

(5.1)

,
the lower bound
eq.

4.2
on

|\rho_{j}(\lambda,d)|
holds. Applying
5.1
, yields the result.
∎

For single well Hamiltonians of the form
h=P^{2}+\lambda^{2}v(X)
, the first two assumptions of
5.1
hold if
v
is smooth and has, for all
\lambda
sufficiently large, a unique non-degenerate minimum. The simplicity of the eigenvalue
e_{j}
is not guaranteed and a more detailed analysis is necessary. Suppose
e_{j}
is degenerate of order
N\geq 2
, with an orthonormal eigenbasis
\big\{\varphi_{j}^{1}\,\cdots,\varphi_{j}^{N}\big\}
. One would,
in the Schur complement reduction (see the proof sketch just below), need to study the
2N\times 2N
matrix whose matrix elements are

\displaystyle\langle R^{n}\varphi_{j}^{k},\left(H-e_{j}\mathds{1}\right)R^{m}\varphi_{j}^{l}\rangle

for
n,m\in\Set{0,d}
and
k,l=1,\cdots,N
.

Proof of
5.1
.

We shall only present a sketch; most of the components of the proof have already been presented in
[
FSW22
]
. The proof is comprised of several parts:

1.

For
d
large, we expect that the two vectors
\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}
will be a good approximate basis of the spectral subspace of
H_{\lambda,d}
near energy
e_{j}
, since
\left(H-e_{j}\right)R^{m}\varphi_{j}\approx 0
for
m=0,d
.

2.

Via a Schur complement argument, for energies in a neighborhood of
e_{j}
, we reduce the Hamiltonian
H_{\lambda,d}-e_{j}\mathds{1}-\Omega\mathds{1}
, to a two by two perturbation of the matrix

\Big(\ \langle R^{n}\varphi_{j},\left(H_{\lambda,d}-e_{j}\mathds{1}\right)R^{m}\varphi_{j}\rangle\Big)_{n,m=0,d}\ -\ \Omega\mathds{1},

(5.3)

which is analytic in
\Omega
, for
\Omega
small.
Formally, this reduction step is completely general and algebraic.
However, rigorously implementing this reduction requires bounds on the norm of the resolvent of
H_{\lambda,d}
, for energies near
e_{j}
, on the subspace orthogonal to
\big\{\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}\big\}
. A general strategy for such “energy estimates”, based on
[
FSW22
, Sections 3 and 4]
, is presented in
section

6.1
and is implemented in the setting of
5.1
in
section

6.2
below.

3.

Taking
d\gg 1
and expanding (
5.3
), we find that eigenvalues
\Omega
near
0
, correspond to
values of
\Omega
near
0
for
which

\rho_{j}(d)\sigma_{1}-\Omega\sigma_{0}\ +\ {\rm Corrections}(\Omega,d)\approx 0,\quad|\Omega|<c.

4.

Write
\Omega=\rho_{j}(d)z
and apply Rouché’s theorem to prove, for
d\gg 1
, that the point spectrum of
H_{\lambda,d}
near
e_{j}

is given by two distinct energies, approximately equal to
e_{j}\pm|\rho_{j}(d)|
. Hence,
\Delta_{j}(d)\approx 2|\rho_{j}(d)|
for
d\gg 1
.

5.

The application of Rouché’s theorem requires that as
d\to\infty

\frac{\textrm{Corrections}(\Omega,d)}{|\rho_{j}(d)|}\ \to\ 0,\quad|\Omega|<c.

(5.4)

We comment briefly on the estimates (
5.4
); cf.
[
FSW22
, Appendix A]
. The analysis greatly simplifies when we take
d\to\infty
instead of
\lambda
. For instance, consider the error term of the diagonal matrix elements in the
2\times 2
Schur matrix. The quantity to be bounded, which arises from the
(1,1)
matrix element, is

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\lambda^{2}\langle\varphi_{j},(R^{d}v)\varphi_{j}\rangle\right|}{\left|\rho_{j}(\lambda,d)\right|}\,.

Using
eq.

A.3
we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\langle\varphi_{j},(R^{d}v)\varphi_{j}\rangle\right|\leq C\exp\left(-2\sqrt{-e_{j}}\left(d-a\right)\right)

for some constant
C
independent on
d
. On the other hand comparing this with
eq.

4.2
we get for any
\varphi>0

\displaystyle\left|\rho(d)\right|\geq C_{\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\sqrt{-e_{j}+\varepsilon}\,d\right)

and so the entire fraction can easily be made to converge to zero as
d\to\infty
.

The next term we study is
\langle\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}\rangle
. That integral is handled by dividing
\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
into three regions,
B_{a}(0),B_{a}(d)
and the complement of both, call it
C
. On each of these sets we have at least one of the eigenfunctions decaying as
\exp\left(-\sqrt{-e_{j}}d\right)
. When we consider the orthonormalized translated wave function

\displaystyle\widetilde{R^{d}\varphi_{j}}:=\frac{R^{d}\varphi_{j}-\langle\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}}{1-\left|\langle\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}}

and its associated diagonal overlap integral

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{R^{d}\varphi_{j}},\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\widetilde{R^{d}\varphi_{j}}\rangle

we may control its decay when divided by
\rho_{j}
.

All other terms follow a similar pattern, because they inherently involve the competition between two factors of
\varphi_{j}
evaluated at
d
in the numerator versus one in the denominator.
∎

6
Energy estimates

The strategy outlined in our proof of
5.1
is based on a (Schur complement) reduction to a matrix problem which relies on an upper bound on the norm of the resolvent of
H-e_{j}\mathds{1}
when projected to the subspace perpendicular to the span of
\varphi_{j},R^{d}\varphi_{j}
. In this section we present a generalization of this bound following the strategy, used for example in
[
FLW18
]
and
[
SW22
]
. Since this argument appeared at least twice already, and since we anticipate future use in other settings, we elected to present a somewhat general form of it before the application to the present context.

6.1
General energy estimates

In this section we present a somewhat more general energy estimate that will hopefully be useful also beyond this note. It generalizes e.g.
[
SW22
, Section 5]
by: (1) not concentrating necessarily on the ground state of the one-well system, (2) allowing for degeneracies, (3) taking more general translations and kinetic energy which only need to obey some estimates. It moreover changes the asymptotic parameter from
\lambda
to the minimal lattice spacing (although that change makes the proof somewhat easier). In the subsequent section we will apply these estimates to our setting of the excited states of a double-well system.

Let
T
be a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
. In principle we anticipate the hypotheses on
T
stipulated below to hold for all polynomials in
P\equiv-\operatorname{i}\nabla
, certainly to
P^{2}
and to the Landau Hamiltonian. We do not exclude possible extensions to non-local kinetic terms such as
\sqrt{P^{2}}
or even general pseudo-differential symbols, but exploring, e.g., for which measurable functions
f
, such that
T=f(P^{2})
, the hypothesis below on
T
fail is beyond the present scope. Let
v:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and compactly supported within some bounded open set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
. We shall also refer to a certain fattening of
S
, say
S^{+}\supsetneq S
, which shall be another bounded open set. Note that
S^{+}
may well depend on
d
though we keep that implicit in the notation for the time being.

Define

\displaystyle h=T+v(X)\ .

Since
d
is our asymptotic parameter and
\lambda
is fixed, we have absorbed the factor of
\lambda
in the potential.

Assume that for some
e\in\mathbb{R}
there exists some orthonormal set
\Set{\varphi_{i}}_{i=1}^{N}
such that
h\varphi_{i}=e\varphi_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
. Furthermore, assume that there exists some
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(h-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\geq\gamma\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\qquad\left(\psi\perp\varphi_{i}\forall i=1,\cdots,N\right)\,.

(6.1)

Let
\mathbb{G}_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
be a countable set depending on a parameter
d>0
. In what follows we write
\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{G}_{d}
, and if a statement which involves
\mathbb{G}
does not have
d
explicitly appear we mean the statement holds for any
d>0
sufficiently large. Let
R:\mathbb{G}\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu}))
be given, i.e.,
R^{n}
is unitary for each
n\in\mathbb{G}
. We assume it is chosen in such a way that if
f,g\in L^{2}
are such that their supports are contained within
S^{+}
then

\displaystyle\left(R^{n}T^{\alpha}f\right)R^{m}T^{\beta}g=0

(6.2)

for all
n\neq m
,
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2
. On any operator
A
, we denote

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\left(R^{n}\right)^{\ast}AR^{n}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{G})\,.

We assume that
[R^{n},T]=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{G}
and set
v_{n}:=\left(v(X)\right)_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{G}
.

We define
\mathcal{V}_{n}:=\operatorname{span}\left(\Set{R^{n}\varphi_{i}}_{i=1}^{N}\right)
and
\mathcal{V}:=\operatorname{span}\left(\Set{R^{n}\varphi_{i}}_{n\in\mathbb{G},i=1,\cdots,N}\right)
and we let
\Pi_{n}
and
\Pi
respectively be the orthogonal projections onto these spaces.

Definition 6.1
.

In what follows we will be interested in
R
-translations of a sequence of compactly-supported functions. To that end, say that
\Set{\eta_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{G}}
is a sequence of functions such that
\operatorname{supp}(\eta_{n})\subseteq S^{+}
for each
n\in\mathbb{G}
. Then by
eq.

6.2
we have
\left(R^{n^{\prime}}\eta_{n}\right)R^{m^{\prime}}f=0
for all
n^{\prime}\neq m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{G}
,
f\in L^{2}
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq S^{+}
. For such a sequence we then define

\displaystyle\eta:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}R^{n}\eta_{n}\,.

Assumption 6.2
.

We assume that if
\eta
is constructed as in
6.1
then there exists some
\varepsilon_{d}
such that
\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
for which

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\Pi_{n}\sum_{m\neq n}R^{m}\eta_{m}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\eta_{m}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(6.3)

Theorem 6.3
.

Let

\displaystyle H:=T+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\,.

For any
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
and
d
sufficiently large there exists some
\xi_{d}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\xi_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\geq\left(\gamma-\xi_{d}\right)\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\,.

(6.4)

To prove the theorem we present a sequence of lemmas that build
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
gradually.

Lemma 6.4

(disjoint energy estimate)
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
such that additionally,
\psi=\sum_{n}R^{n}\eta_{n}
, i.e., it is constructed as in
6.1
.

Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert

(6.5)

Proof.

For convenience within this proof we shift
\eta_{n}\to\widetilde{\eta}_{n}
and then define
\eta_{n}:=R^{n}\widetilde{\eta}_{n}
.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n},\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}\rangle\,.

Let us now write
H=h_{n}+\sum_{m\neq n}v_{m}
where
h_{n}\equiv T+v_{n}
. By hypothesis we have
\eta_{m}v_{n}=0
for all
n\neq m
so that

\displaystyle\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}=\left(h_{m}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}\,.

We thus find

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n},\left(h_{m}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n},\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n}\rangle+\sum_{n\neq m\in\mathbb{G}}\langle\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n},\left(h_{m}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}\rangle\,.

Now, for
n\neq m
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n},\left(h_{m}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{m}\rangle=\langle h_{n}\eta_{n},h_{m}\eta_{m}\rangle+e^{2}\langle\eta_{n},\eta_{m}\rangle-e\langle h_{n}\eta_{n},\eta_{m}\rangle-e\langle\eta_{n},h_{m}\eta_{m}\rangle\,.

Using
eq.

6.2
every term in the above line is zero. We thus find

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Let us write now
\eta_{n}:=\Pi_{n}\eta_{n}+\Pi_{n}^{\perp}\eta_{n}
. We emphasize that despite
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
, we cannot assume
\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
separately. By definition,

\displaystyle\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta_{n}=\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\Pi_{n}^{\perp}\eta_{n}\,.

Using the gap assumption
eq.

6.1
we have then
\left\lVert\left(h_{n}-e\mathds{1}\right)\Pi_{n}^{\perp}\eta_{n}\right\rVert\geq\gamma\left\lVert\Pi_{n}^{\perp}\eta_{n}\right\rVert
so that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}\geq\gamma^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\Pi_{n}^{\perp}\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left(\left\lVert\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\Pi_{n}\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}\right)=\gamma^{2}\left(\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\Pi_{n}\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\,.

We now bound
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\Pi_{n}\eta_{n}\right\rVert^{2}
. Since
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
it is also in
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{\perp}
and so

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}\eta_{n}=\Pi_{n}\left(\psi-\sum_{m\neq n}\eta_{m}\right)=-\sum_{m\neq n}\Pi_{n}\eta_{m}

at which point
eq.

6.3
applies, and so we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}

and hence the result.
∎

Lemma 6.5

(localized energy estimate)
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
and
\Theta_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to[0,1]
be some function such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\Theta_{d}(X)-\mathds{1}\right)\Pi\right\rVert<\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}

(6.6)

with
\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
and such that for any
f\in L^{2}
,
\Theta_{d}f
is of the form
6.1
. Further assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{d>0,\alpha=1,2}\left\lVert[\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{\alpha},\Theta_{d}]\Pi\right\rVert<\infty\,.

(6.7)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{d>0,\alpha=1,2}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{\alpha}\Pi\right\rVert<\infty\,.

(6.8)

Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
there is a constant
M_{d,\alpha}
such that
\sup_{d>0}M_{d,\alpha}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-2M_{d,\alpha}\left(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Proof.

Even though we allow
\Theta_{d}
to depend on
d
to avoid clutter in this proof we merely write
\Theta
.

The assumption
eq.

6.6
implies that the operator
\left.\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}
is invertible. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left.\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}=\left.\mathds{1}\right|_{\mathcal{V}}+\left.\Pi\left(\Theta(X)-\mathds{1}\right)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}

and
\mathds{1}+A
is invertible if
\left\lVert A\right\rVert<1
. With this, let us define

\displaystyle u:=\left(\left.\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{-1}\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi^{\perp}\psi

and

\displaystyle\eta:=\Theta(X)\left(\psi-u\right)\,.

We have chosen
u
so as to correct for the fact that even though
\Pi\psi=0
,
\Pi\Theta(X)\psi\neq 0
. However, now that we have
\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\Pi\eta=\Pi\Theta(X)\left(\psi-u\right)=\Pi\Theta(X)\psi-\Pi\Theta(X)\left(\left.\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{-1}\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi^{\perp}\psi=0\,,

i.e.,
\eta\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
. This latter fact together with the assumption
implies that we may invoke
6.4
to get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta\right\rVert\geq\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}}\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert\,.

We now obtain a lower bound on
\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert
. Using
\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi^{\perp}=\Pi\left(\Theta-\mathds{1}\right)+\Pi\left(\mathds{1}-\Theta\right)\Pi
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left(\left.\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi\right|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{-1}\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi^{\perp}\psi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)^{-1}\left\lVert\Pi\Theta(X)\Pi^{\perp}\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\right)^{-1}2\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert=:\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\,.

We have again
\delta_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
. Hence, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert-\left\lVert\Theta u\right\rVert\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\left\lVert\Theta u\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-4\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\delta_{d}^{2}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Let us write

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\eta\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\eta,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Theta\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta\psi\rangle+\langle\Theta u,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\rangle-2\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Theta\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\rangle\right\}\,.

Putting all these estimates together we find

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\delta_{d}^{2}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-\langle\Theta u,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\rangle+

\displaystyle\qquad+2\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Theta\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\rangle\right\}\,.

We thus need to find a bound on the latter two terms on the right hand side.

For the first term, since
u\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Theta u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Theta\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)u\right\rVert+\left\lVert[H-e\mathds{1},\Theta]u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Pi\right\rVert\left\lVert u\right\rVert+\left\lVert[T,\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\left\lVert u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Pi\right\rVert+\left\lVert[T,\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\right)\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\,.

For the last term, using
u\in\mathcal{V}
again,

\displaystyle\left|\langle\Theta\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\left(\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Pi\right\rVert+\left\lVert[\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2},\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Pi\right\rVert+\left\lVert[\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2},\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\right)\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Collecting everything together we find

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-M\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\

with

\displaystyle M:=\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\delta_{d}+\left(\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Pi\right\rVert+\left\lVert[T,\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\right)^{2}\delta_{d}+2\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Pi\right\rVert+2\left\lVert[\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2},\Theta]\Pi\right\rVert\,.

Now
eqs.

6.7
and
6.8
guarantee that
M
is bounded as a function of
d
, so we are finished.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Assume there is a
\Theta_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to[0,1]
such that
6.5
holds, and such that, further,
\Sigma_{d}:=\sqrt{1-\Theta_{d}^{2}}
has
\Sigma_{d}v_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{G}
.

We further assume
\Theta_{d}
has been chosen such that there are constants
A_{d},B_{d},C_{d}\in(0,\infty)
, all converge to zero as
d\to\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{f=\Theta_{d},\Sigma_{d}}\left|\langle f(X)\psi,[f(X),\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right|\leq A_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+B_{d}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(6.9)

If
T\geq 0
,
e\leq 0
, and
|e|>\gamma
then for all
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha-M_{d,\alpha}\delta_{d}-2A_{d}}{1-2B_{d}}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(6.10)

Proof.

Let
\Theta_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to[0,1]
be given and assume it is such that the conclusion of
6.5
holds. As before we write
\Theta\equiv\Theta_{d}
and also set
\Sigma:=\sqrt{\mathds{1}-\Theta^{2}}
. Let also
\psi\in\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
be given.

We start with a preliminary

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Theta^{2}\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\psi\rangle+\langle\Sigma^{2}\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\psi\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\langle\Theta\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Theta\psi\rangle+\langle\Sigma\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}\Sigma\psi\rangle+

\displaystyle+\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Theta\psi,[\Theta,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right\}+\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Sigma\psi,[\Sigma,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right\}

By
6.5
we have (with
\delta_{d}
as defined in its proof)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\Theta\psi\right\rVert^{2}-M_{d,\alpha}\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\Sigma\psi\right\rVert^{2}-M_{d,\alpha}\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Moreover, since
\Sigma v_{n}=0
for any
n\in\mathbb{G}
and since we are assuminug
-eT\geq 0
we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\Sigma\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(T-e\mathds{1}\right)\Sigma\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\Sigma\psi,\left(T^{2}+e^{2}\mathds{1}-2eT\right)\Sigma\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq e^{2}\left\lVert\Sigma\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Collecting everything together we find

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+\left(e^{2}-\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\right)\left\lVert\Sigma\psi\right\rVert^{2}-M_{d,\alpha}\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+

\displaystyle+\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Theta\psi,[\Theta,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right\}+\operatorname{\mathbb{R}\mathbbm{e}}\left\{\langle\Sigma\psi,[\Sigma,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma^{2}-\varepsilon_{d}^{2}\right)\alpha\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-M_{d,\alpha}\delta_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-

\displaystyle-2A_{d}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}-2B_{d}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\,.

In the last inequality we used the fact that
e>\gamma
and that
\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
, as well as the hypothesis
eq.

6.9
. This implies the result.

∎

6.2
Energy estimates for excited states of non-magnetic double-well systems

In this section we apply the general scheme of the previous section to the present problem of establishing energy estimates for excited states of non-magnetic double-well systems. To that end we make the following choices w.r.t. the above notation:

1.

T
, the kinetic term, shall be
P^{2}
with
P\equiv-\operatorname{i}\nabla
.

2.

If
v
is negative, bounded, and compactly supported within some
S:=B_{a}(0)
for some
a>0
, then any negative-energy bound state must lie in the discrete spectrum and hence be finitely-degenerate, so assuming
e<0
and
N<\infty
is reasonable, whence
eq.

6.1
follows. At this point we may
assume

e>\gamma
, though it is a generic situation whenever
v
is sufficiently deep (which may be arranged by replacing
v
with
\lambda^{2}v
and taking
\lambda
sufficiently large; this is done before determining
d
). We let
S^{+}:=B_{a^{+}}(0)
with
a^{+}>a
. In fact we will choose eventually
a^{+}
to depend on
d
,
a^{+}\equiv a^{+}(d)

For convenience we assume the eigenstates are normalized,
\left\lVert\varphi_{j}\right\rVert=1
, and name
\kappa:=\sqrt{-e}
and we assume
\kappa>1
.

3.

For the lattice
\mathbb{G}_{d}
we take the set
\Set{0,de_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, whence
R:\mathbb{G}_{d}\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu}))
yields
R^{0}:=\mathds{1}
and translation by
d
:

\displaystyle\left(R^{d}f\right)(x):=f(x-de_{1})\qquad(f\in L^{2}\,;\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu})\,.

With these choices
eq.

6.2
is fulfilled.

Now a standard Agmon estimate (
[
Agm82
]
; see
A.1
below) implies that if
h\varphi=e\varphi
with
e<0
and
\varphi\in L^{2}
, then

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)|\leq C_{\varphi}\exp\left(-\kappa\left\lVert x\right\rVert\right)\qquad\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq a+1\right)

(6.11)

for some
C_{\varphi}<\infty
.

In particular this means that if
f\in L^{2}
has support within
B_{r}(x)
with
\left\lVert x\right\rVert-r>a+1
then

\displaystyle\left|\langle f,\varphi\rangle\right|\leq C_{\varphi}\int_{y\in B_{r}(x)}\exp\left(-\kappa\left\lVert y\right\rVert\right)\left|f(y)\right|\operatorname{d}{y}\leq C_{\varphi}\sqrt{\left|B_{r}(0)\right|}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\exp\left(-\kappa\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert-r\right)\right)\,.

(6.12)

With these choices, we want to establish that the various hypothesis made in the previous section at each step actually hold. We do so in the order that the hypothesis appear:

Lemma 6.7
.

6.2
holds with the choices made in this subsection, if
d-a^{+}(d)\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
.

Proof.

We now have
\Pi_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}R^{n}\varphi_{j}\otimes\varphi_{j}^{\ast}R^{-n}
for
n=0,d
. That means we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{G}}\left\lVert\Pi_{n}\sum_{m\neq n}\eta_{m}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Pi_{0}R^{d}\eta_{d}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\Pi_{d}\eta_{0}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|\langle\varphi_{j},R^{d}\eta_{d}\rangle\right|^{2}\left\lVert\varphi_{j}\right\rVert^{2}+\left|\langle R^{d}\varphi_{j},\eta_{0}\rangle\right|^{2}\left\lVert R^{d}\varphi_{j}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2NC_{\varphi_{1}}^{2}\left|B_{a^{+}(d)}(0)\right|\exp\left(-2\kappa\left(d-{a^{+}(d)}\right)\right)\left(\left\lVert\eta_{d}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\eta_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\,.

We thus identify

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{d}:=2NC_{\varphi_{1}}^{2}\left|B_{a^{+}(d)}(0)\right|\exp\left(-2\kappa\left(d-a^{+}(d)\right)\right)

which converges to zero.
∎

We are now in a position to choose our smooth partition of unity
\Theta_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to[0,1]
. Let
\Theta_{d}(x):=f(\left\lVert x\right\rVert)+f(\left\lVert x-d\right\rVert)
where
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a smooth bump function with

\displaystyle\left.f\right|_{[0,a^{-}(d)]}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\left.f\right|_{[a^{+}(d),\infty)}

\displaystyle=0\,.

for some
a^{-}(d)<a^{+}(d)
.

One choice of
a^{\pm}(d)
which will do is for example
a^{-}(d)=\frac{1}{3}d
and
a^{+}(d)=\frac{1}{2}d
.

Lemma 6.8
.

For the above choice of
\Theta_{d}

eqs.

6.6
,
6.7
and
6.8
hold.

Proof.

We omit the proof of
eq.

6.6
which is trivial except for the fact that
\Pi\neq\Pi_{0}+\Pi_{d}
, but that fact only adds terms which decay exponentially as
\exp\left(-\kappa d\right)
. Ignoring that fact, since
\mathds{1}-\Theta_{d}
is supported away from the balls of radius
r_{1}
, it is clear that we can guarantee decay of this term at least as fast as

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\kappa a^{-}(d)\right)\,.

We thus identify

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{d}:=\text{const.}\times\exp\left(-\kappa a^{-}(d)\right)\,.

Next, we turn to
eq.

6.8
. We write any
\psi\in\operatorname{im}\Pi
as
\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\varphi_{j}+\beta_{j}R^{d}\varphi_{j}
for some coefficients
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
. Then

\displaystyle\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j}v_{d}\varphi_{j}+\beta_{j}v_{0}R^{d}\varphi_{j}

and so

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\text{const.}\times N\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\exp\left(-\kappa d\right)

and similarly for the second power; again we note that going from the coefficients to
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert
involves the fact that
\Pi_{0}\Pi_{d}\neq 0
which only contributes corrections of the form
\exp\left(-\kappa d\right)
.

We now study the commutator, towards
eq.

6.7
:

\displaystyle[H-e\mathds{1},\Theta_{d}]

\displaystyle=[P^{2},\Theta_{d}]

\displaystyle=(P^{2}\Theta_{d})+2(P\Theta_{d})\cdot P\,.

The first term is clearly bounded. For the second term, we have for any
\psi\in\mathcal{V}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert P_{j}\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,P^{2}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle-\langle\psi,\left(v_{0}+v_{d}-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle

all of which are bounded uniformly in
d
. The commutator with the second power is dealt with in a similar manner.
∎

With our particular choice of
\Theta_{d}
, we also have
\left(\mathds{1}-\Theta_{d}\right)v_{n}=0
for
n=0,d
, as required by
6.6
. Moreover, clearly for us,
T\equiv P^{2}\geq 0
and
e<0
by hypothesis. Moreover,
e>\gamma
is also part of our hypothesis. We are thus only left with establishing
eq.

6.9
. The idea here is that since we have arbitrarily large distance over which
\Theta_{d}
goes from
1
to
0
, we can make its derivatives arbitrarily small, and hence
[\Theta_{d},P]
can be taken arbitrarily small. Similarly for
[\Sigma_{d},P]
with
\Sigma_{d}:=\sqrt{1-\Theta_{d}^{2}}
.

Lemma 6.9
.

We may arrange for
eq.

6.9
to hold with a choice of
\Theta_{d}
that has arbitarily small derivatives.

Proof.

We write
f\equiv f(X)
. First let us calculate

\displaystyle[f,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)[f,P^{2}]+[f,P^{2}]\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)

and we use
[P^{2},f]=(P^{2}f)+2(Pf)\cdot P
so that

\displaystyle\left|\langle f\psi,[f,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)^{2}]\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle f\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|+2\left|\langle f\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)(Pf)\cdot P\psi\rangle\right|+

\displaystyle+\left|\langle f\psi,(P^{2}f)\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle\right|+2\left|\langle f\psi,(Pf)\cdot P\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle\right|\,.

Let us study the first term:

\displaystyle\left|\langle f\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)f\psi,(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle f\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi,(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|+\left|\langle[\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right),f]\psi,(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert P^{2}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert+\left|\langle\left(P^{2}f\right)\psi,(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|+

\displaystyle+\left|\langle\left(Pf\right)P\psi,(P^{2}f)\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert P^{2}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert+\left\lVert P^{2}f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+

\displaystyle+\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert P^{2}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert P\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\,.

All of the terms in the last expression are of the form we need except the very last one, for which we estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert P\psi\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,P^{2}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle\psi,\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle\right|+\left|\langle\psi,\left(V-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert+\left(\left|e\right|+\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Finally we use Young’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}\,.

All other three terms are dealt with similarly. The conclusion is that every single term contains either a factor of
\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert_{\infty}
or
\left\lVert P^{2}f\right\rVert_{\infty}
, both of which can be made arbitrarily small, and then some constant times either
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}
or
\left\lVert\left(H-e\mathds{1}\right)\psi\right\rVert^{2}
.
∎

Appendix A
Agmon’s estimate

For completeness we cite here Agmon’s estimate
[
Agm82
]
.

Theorem A.1

(Agmon’s estimate)
.

Let
H=P^{2}+V(X)
be given on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\nu})
, where
V:\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\to\mathbb{R}
is of compact support, and, say
L^{2}
, and assume that
\psi\in L^{2}
is such that
H\psi=E\psi
for some
E<0
. Then
\psi
exhibits pointwise exponential decay with rate
\sqrt{-E}
outside of
\operatorname{supp}(V)
. In particular, there exists a constant
C<\infty
so that if
\left\lVert x\right\rVert>C
then

\displaystyle\left|\psi(x)\right|\leq C\exp\left(-\sqrt{-E}\left\lVert x\right\rVert\right)\,.

(A.1)

Proof.

Rewriting
H\psi=E\psi
as

\displaystyle\left(P^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)\psi=-V(X)\psi

we apply the free resolvent on the LHS to get

\displaystyle\psi=-\left(P^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}V(X)\psi\,.

Now evaluating at some point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
which is outside of
\operatorname{supp}(V)
we find

\displaystyle\psi(x)=-\int_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(V)}\left(P^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)V(y)\psi(y)\operatorname{d}{y}

so that

\displaystyle\left|\psi(x)\right|\leq\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\operatorname{supp}(V))}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\operatorname{supp}(V))}\sup_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(V)}\left(P^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)\,.

The Greens function of the free Laplacian is given by

\displaystyle\left(P^{2}-E\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\nu/2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{-E}}{2}\right)^{\nu/2-1}\frac{K_{\nu/2-1}(\sqrt{-E}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert)}{\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{\nu/2-1}}

(A.2)

where
K_{m}
is the modified Bessel function of order
m
.

In particular let
a>0
be such that
\operatorname{supp}(V)\subseteq B_{a}(0)
. Then, using known decay estimates of
K
, with
\kappa:=\sqrt{-E}
, we have for
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq a+\frac{1}{\kappa}
,

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|\leq C_{V,a,\kappa,\psi}\frac{1}{\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert-a\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}}\exp\left(-\kappa\left(\left\lVert x\right\rVert-a\right)\right)

(A.3)

with

\displaystyle C_{V,a,\kappa,\psi}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{\nu/2}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\kappa^{\frac{\nu-2}{2}}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\operatorname{supp}(V))}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\operatorname{supp}(V))}\,.

∎

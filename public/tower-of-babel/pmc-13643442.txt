Shifting Baseline of Continuous Thermodilution
Authors: Pitt O Lim
Published: 2026

Velazquez Gutierrez et al1 reported that intracoronary supersaturated oxygen (SSO2) increases flow down the recanalized infarct-related left anterior descending artery due to profound vasodilatation. But this goes against received wisdom. Systemic supraphysiological oxygenation during on-pump coronary artery bypass grafting is associated with poorer cardiac function.2 Oxygen supplementation in the absence of hypoxia during ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction increases infarct size,3 and older similar studies with intracoronary oxygen delivery showed neither obvious infarct size reduction, nor better outcome at 30 days.4

Looking critically at the report of Velazquez Gutierrez et al,1 the following 3 observations are pertinent:

1.The poststenting resting and hyperemic microvascular resistances (HMR) of 742 and 398 Wood units are within normal limits. Post–myocardial infarction HMR ≥1,000 Wood units is predictive of microvascular obstruction.5 This is using the over-the-wire balloon distal occlusion technique, first to measure the distal coronary pressure (Pd) and second, to estimate the coronary wedge pressure (Pw) with saline infusion rate of 20 mL/min; HMR = [Pw − Pd]/20, before stenting, since stenting is associated with distal embolization increasing microvascular resistance.

2.The coronary artery is diffusely diseased, even after stenting, the fractional flow reserve is 0.78, and 0.75 after SSO2, which implies flow restraint. On baseline bolus thermodilution, while the resting mean transit times (seconds) are consistent in triplicate (0.54, 0.54, and 0.58), the hyperemic mean transit times are only obtained twice (0.51 and 0.43) instead of the usual 3 or more measures until consistency is attained. Even if the index of microcirculatory resistance of 25 is reliable before SSO2, it is still below 40, the accepted threshold for microvascular obstruction.

3.Intracoronary continuous thermodilution calculates flow, Q=1.08·TiTm·Qi; where Ti is the infusate temperature, Tm, the mixed temperature, and Qi, the saline infusion rate. Ti is −2.01, −2.99, −2.32, and −3.73 °C across the 4 studies; clearly the post-SSO2 Ti of −3.73 °C is an outlying baseline value. Tm is −0.23, −0.37, −0.22, and −0.09 °C; the very low post-SSO2 Tm of −0.09 °C suggests that the thermistor could be too distal. A 5-fold increase in posttreatment hyperemic flow rate of 796 mL/min down the diffusely diseased left anterior descending artery with a fractional flow reserve of 0.75, which represents more than 3 times the whole heart coronary flow at rest is biologically implausible—that is a coronary flow reserve of 9.3 (796/86) and microvascular resistance reserve of 8.3 (742/89)—comparing post-SSO2 hyperemic with pre-SSO2 baseline continuous thermodilution values. Yet, the paired coronary flow reserves with bolus thermodilution are 1.2 and 1.6 before and after SSO2. In other words, there is a clear contemporaneous divergence of coronary flow reserves between the two coronary thermodilution methods post-SSO2—“supranormal 9.3” with continuous against “subnormal 1.6” with bolus. It is also unclear how the authors derived their post-SSO2 microvascular resistance reserve of 10.4, whether it is an overcorrection from the bolus thermodilution formula; but using the bolus thermodilution data the corresponding values are 1.8 and 2.5 pre- and post-SSO2.

On account of the above, the reported results are likely to be spurious. However, we shall hold judgment until the completion of the RECOVERY study (Recovering Coronary Microvascular Obstruction With Supersaturated Oxygen Therapy in ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction [STEMI] Patients; NCT07000266) in due course.1

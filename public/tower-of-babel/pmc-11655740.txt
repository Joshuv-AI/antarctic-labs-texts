Role of lncRNA TPRG1-AS1 in the development of cervical squamous cell carcinoma and its prognostic value
Authors: Yang Fan, Lan Ye, Shuyu Wang, Junwei Wang, Ke Wang, Ying Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveCervical squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC) has a poor prognosis due to persistent HPV infection. LncRNA TPRG1-AS1 is linked to regulating the development of many cancers, so the regulatory mechanism and prognostic value of TPRG1-AS1 in CSCC were explored.Methods138 patients with cervical cancer were included. TPRG1-AS1 expression and miR-590-3p were analyzed by qRT-PCR. The association between TPRG1-AS1 and clinicopathological features was investigated. Independent prognostic factors of CSCC were analyzed by multifactorial Cox regression. Patient survival was analyzed using Kaplan–Meier plotter curves. CCK-8 was employed to evaluate the proliferative capacity of the cells. Transwell assays were performed to evaluate the effects of TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p on cell migration and invasion performance, and the target of both was reported by DLR assay.ResultsTPRG1-AS1 levels were ascended in CSCC, and miR-590-3p levels were reduced. TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p target binding and expression correlated negatively. Knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 expression could facilitate high miR-590-3p expression, which reduced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion ability. TPRG1-AS1 is an independent prognostic factor.ConclusionTPRG1-AS1 has potential as a prognostic marker for CSCC. Silencing the expression of TPRG1-AS1 could contribute to the high miR-590-3p expression thereby slowing down the progression of CSCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01654-1.Keywords: Cervical squamous cell carcinoma, TPRG1-AS1, miR-590-3p, Prognosis

Introduction

Cervical squamous cell carcinoma (CSCC) is the most common type of cervical cancer (CC), and CSCC accounts for approximately 75–80% of CC [1]. The etiology of CSCC is mainly high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infection [2]. In recent years, widespread vaccination and screening have reduced the incidence of CSCC; but widespread vaccination takes time, during which new treatments are needed, especially in patients already affected by CSCC [3]. In addition, early-stage cervical squamous cell carcinoma has a good prognosis, but the 5-year survival rate for advanced, metastatic, or recurrent cervical squamous cell carcinoma is only 16.5%, which is a serious threat to women’s health [4, 5]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a validated prognostic biomarker to provide a novel therapeutic target for treating CSCC.

Long-stranded non-coding RNA (lncRNA) is associated with the development of malignant tumors and may become a new biomarker for tumor diagnosis, prognosis, and even targeted gene therapy [6]. Several reports have found that the large classes of lncRNAs are responsible for the regulation of several cancers, including gastric [7], bladder [8], breast [9] and colon cancer [10]. LncRNA TPRG1-AS1 (tumor protein p63 regulated 1, antisense 1) does not encode a protein and its full length is 1279 nt [11]. Previous studies have found that TPRG1-AS1 is aberrantly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma [12], bladder uroepithelial carcinoma [13], and atherosclerosis [11]. Available studies have shown that TPRG1-AS1 is aberrantly expressed in diseases in which squamous epithelial cells become cancerous. Li et al. reported that the expression level of TPRG1-AS1 was increased in squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck [14]. However, its clinical significance and potential mechanisms in CRC are unknown. Molecular mechanisms, miRNAs cat as lncRNA sponges involved in tumor progression. MiR-590-3p, as one of the many miRNAs, was abnormally expressed in cervical cancer [15], hepatocellular carcinoma [16], and pancreatic cancer [17]. Of interest, our pre-database prediction revealed that TPRG1-AS1 has a putative target binding site with miR-590-3p [18]. Therefore, we speculated that TPRG1-AS1 might be involved in CSCC progression through targeting miR-590-3p.

Thus, to verify our speculation, we first analyzed the expression and clinical significance of TRPG1-AS1 in CSCC, and also further analyzed whether it was involved in its progression by targeting miR-590-5p. Our study may broaden new ideas for the management of CSCC.

Materials and methods

Tissue sample collection from patients

138 patients with CSCC were included. These patients were treated between 2015 and 2017. Inclusion criteria: patients had not been treated for CSCC in the past; patients suffered from no other infectious diseases or malignancies other than CSCC. Tissue samples of CSCC and normal tissue samples adjacent to the cancer were taken from patients and the tissue samples were placed in liquid nitrogen and placed at − 80 °C until the subsequent assay was performed. Clinical physiological information of the subjects was recorded, including but not limited to age, FIGO stage, Lymph node metastasis, etc., as shown in Table 1. Patients were followed for 5 years to document survival.

The Henan Medical College ethics committee approved the study. All participants and their family members have had informed consent in writing, in compliance with relevant regulations and the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell cultivation

Four cervical cancer cells (SiHa, HeLa, C-33A, MS751) and cervical epithelial cells Ect1/E6E7 (Kanglang, Shanghai, China) were selected for in vitro cellular experiments. All cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium as previous studies [19] (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). The incubation temperature was 37 °C and the CO2 concentration was 5%.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p were expressed by qRT-PCR. Frozen tissue samples were thoroughly crushed and 1 mL of Trizol reagent was added to extract RNA from the tissues and cDNA using a reverse Tran scription kit (for LncRNA) or Mir-X miRNA First Strand Synthesis Kit (for miRNA, Takara, Dalian, China). The KAPA SYBR Rapid One-Step Kit (Roche, Switzerland) was used for PCR. The experimental procedure was performed according to the kit instructions, using GAPDH or U6 as an endogenous regulation. The 2−ΔΔCT method was used to normalize the results to Ct values. The primer sequences were followed TPRG1-AS1, forward 5ʹ-TCAAAAGGCCTTGATCCCGA-3ʹ, reverse-5ʹ AAGGACTCTGCTTCATGGGTG-3ʹ; GAPDH forward 5ʹ-GTCTCCTCTGACTTCAACAGCG-3ʹ, reverse 5ʹ-ACCACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCCAA-3ʹ; miR-590-3p forward 5ʹ-TAATTTTATGTATAAGCTAGT-3ʹ, reverse 5ʹ-GTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3ʹ; U6 forward 5ʹ-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3ʹ, reverse 5ʹ-AACGCTTCACGAATTTCGCT-3ʹ.

Cell transfected

The siRNA targeting TPRG1-AS1 (si-TRPG1) and Negative control (si-NC) plasmids, miRNA inhibitor, and their NCs used in this study were designed and purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). SiHa and HeLa cells were inoculated into 6-well plates (3.0 × 105 cells/well) according to the procedure of the Lipofectamine 3000 kit (Invitrogen, USA). Cells were transfected with 100 nM siRNA or miRNA inhibitors at 2.5 mug of plasmid per well after 24 h of cell culture, and non-transfected cells were used as controls. The knockdown efficiency of TPRG1-AS1 was verified by qRT-PCR.

Cell proliferation assay

The proliferation of SiHa and HeLa cells was measured within 72 h by the CCK-8 kit (Yeasen, Shanghai). Cells were inoculated at a density of 3 × 103 cells/well in 96 well plates and cultivated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 24 h, 48 h and 72 h. Next, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added and the assay was incubated for 2 h. The absorbance value was detected at 450 nm and the plate was shaken for 5 s before detection to prevent the presence of air bubbles in the 96-well plate from affecting the results.

Cell migration and invasion assay

A 24-well Transwell chamber (8 μm) coated with 50 mg/L Matrigel (BD Biosciences) to assess cell migration and invasion ability. Cells transfected with 100 μL RMPI 1640 basal medium (without FBS) were placed in the upper chamber of the transwell. The inoculation density was 2 × 104 cells/well. RMPI 1640 complete medium with FBS was added to the lower chamber. After 24 h of cultivation, fixed with 4% methanol migrated or invaded cells (10 min) and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. Excess dye was then washed off and five randomly selected fields of view were observed, photographed, and counted using a camera-equipped light microscope (Olympus BX53, Japanese).

Bioinformatics analysis of TPRG1-AS1 targeting miR-590-3p

Prediction of TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p target binding sites using the lncRNASNP2 human database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP/#!/).

Dual-Luciferase reporter assay

The gene plasmid was designed based on bioinformatics analysis of the binding site between the two. Wild-type (WT)-TPRG1-AS1 (containing the potential binding site for miR-590-3p) and mutant (MUT)-TPRG1-AS1 (with the mutated binding site for miR-590-3p) recombinant plasmids were constructed. The recombinant plasmids were co-transfected with miR-590-3p mimic and miR-590-3p inhibitor into SiHa and HeLa cells with the Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent. Luciferase activities were assayed using the Dual Luciferase Kit (Shanghai Beo Tianmei Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) after 48 h for transfection.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

The Magna RIP kit was utilized for the execution of the RIP assay. The CSCC cell line was subjected to lysis, followed by an incubation of 4 h with antibody-conjugated magnetic particles, specifically anti-Ago2 or anti-IgG, for the enrichment of RNA–protein complexes. Subsequently, the enriched RNA specifies were released through elution and subsequently analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Statistical analysis

At least three replicates of all experiments with parallel tests and results are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Cox and patients’ clinical physiological data were analyzed and tabulated using SPSS, while the rest of the data were analyzed and plotted using GraphPad. A p-value of less than 0.05 denotes a difference that is statistically significant in both groups.

Results

Expression of lncRNA TPRG1-AS1

TPRG1-AS1 expression in tumors and normal tissues of CSCC was assessed by qRT-PCR. Levels of TPRG1-AS1 were visibly increased in cancerous tissue (P < 0.001, Fig. 1). Furthermore, the high expression of TPRG1-AS1 in CSCC tissues was visible and related to tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and FIGO stage (P < 0.05), independent of other factors such as age, differentiation (P > 0.05, Table 1).

TPRG1-AS1 is expressed at higher levels in CSCC tissues than in normal (*** P < 0.001)

TPRG1-AS1 is a marker of poor CSCC prognosis

In prognostic events, the TPRG1-AS1 expression level was higher in poor prognosis than in good prognosis (P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). The prognostic significance of TPRG1-AS1 in CSCC was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier survival curve, and the results, as displayed in Fig. 2B, showed that high TPRG1-AS1 expression resulted in a shorter overall survival of CSCC patients (Log-rank P = 0.000). Beyond, TPRG1-AS1 is an independent prognostic factor for the development of CSCC by multifactorial Cox survival analysis (P = 0.005, HR = 3.182; Table 2).

TPRG1-AS1 expression level affects cell biological functions

TPRG1-AS1 expression level in CSCC cell and Ect1/E6E7 was further investigated. Compared to Ect1/E6E7, TPRG1-AS1 level was elevated in all CSCC cells (P < 0.01, Fig. 3A), particularly in SiHa and HeLa, which were therefore selected for subsequent experiments. In addition, transfection of different si-TPRG1-AS1 significantly inhibited the level of TPRG1-AS1, the specific knockdown efficiency of which is shown in the Supplemental Figure. si-TPRG1-AS1#1 was used in the subsequent studies because it had the highest interference efficiency. What’s more, the knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 significantly reduced TPRG1-AS1 expression after transfection and inhibited cell proliferation, migration, and invasion ability (Fig. 3B–I).

Targeted binding of TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p

The target gene of TPRG1-AS1, miR-590-3p, was confirmed by bioinformatics analysis, and the predicted location of target binding of both is illustrated in Fig. 4A. miR-590-3p level was reduced in CSCC (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). The correlation between the two was analyzed and the results showed a negative correlation at TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p expression levels, with decreased expression of TPRG1-AS1 leading to increased expression of miR-590-3p (r = − 0.8112, P < 0.0001; Fig. 4C). The targeting relationship was confirmed by DLR experiments. miR-590-3p mimics decreased the luciferase activity of WT-TPRG1-AS1, while miR-590-3p inhibitors increased the luciferase activity (P < 0.001), there was no effect on MUT-TPRG1-AS1 (P > 0.05, Fig. 4D, E). Furthermore, both TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p were enriched in the anti-Ago2 group more than the anti-IgG (P < 0.001, Fig. 4F–G).

F and G RIP assay was employed to examine the targeting between TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p (*** P < 0.001 vs normal or control)

TPRG1-AS1 and miR-590-3p together affect the physiological function of cells

The cellular level of miR-590-3p was assessed and compared to Ect1/E6E7, and miR-590-3p expression was declined in MS751, SiHa, C-33A, and HeLa (Fig. 5A). As expression was particularly low in HeLa and SiHa, these two cells were used for subsequent transfection and proliferation experiments. Knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 results in elevated miR-590-3p expression, whereas knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 and addition of miR-590-3p inhibitor decreased miR-590-3p levels (Fig. 5B, C). The same trend was shown in cell proliferation, migration, and invasion capability, where knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 decreased cell proliferation, migration, and invasion (P < 0.01), and conversely, the inclusion of the miR-590-3p inhibitor reversed this effect (P < 0.05, Fig. 5D–I).

Discussion

Currently, CSCC is the fourth most prevalent cancer in women [20]. The main treatments for CSCC include surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy [21]. Despite advances in prevention, diagnosis, and treatment, the 5-year survival rate of advanced, metastatic, or recurrent CSCC is still only 15% [22], and the prognosis of CSCC is usually poor due to persistent HPV infection and complications [23, 24]. An effective prognostic marker is therefore needed. LncRNA has been reported to demonstrate its potential value for prognosis and diagnosis in many cancers [25]. In addition, lncRNAs are involved in the genesis and development of many cancers and even act as oncogenic or oncostatic factors in cancer [26–28]. Therefore, it is important to explore the potential function of lncRNAs in the growth of CSCC and their prognostic value.

The lncRNA TPRG1-AS1 plays a regulatory role and has diagnostic/prognostic potential in a variety of diseases. TPRG1-AS1 has been demonstrated to induce and up-regulate the expression of RBM24 via miR-4691-5p and miR-3659, which inhibits the developmental process of hepatocellular carcinoma through the activation of tumor apoptosis cells [12]. In CSCC tissues, the TPRG1-AS1 expression level was significantly elevated. Similarly, TPRG1-AS1 expression levels were relatively high in adverse prognostic events. Patients with high TPRG1-AS1 expression had shorter overall survival, and multifactorial Cox regression analyses indicated that TPRG1-AS1 levels were an independent prognostic factor.

It has been proposed that long non-coding RNAs can inhibit atherogenesis by affecting cell division, growth, and migration, among other pathways [29]. TPRG1-AS1 inhibits the migratory activity of cells by interacting with MYH9 proteins [11]. In this study, the knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 reduced the proliferative capacity of SiHa and HeLa and inhibited their migration and invasive ability. In addition, lncRNAs can act as sponges for miRNAs, reducing the regulatory effects of miRNAs on mRNAs [30].

Bioinformatic analysis revealed that TPRG1-AS1 is a sponge for miR-590-3p. TPRG1-AS1 can bind to miR-590-3p and both expression levels are negatively correlated. miR-590-3p has been implicated in the regulation of several diseases [31–33]. miR-590-3p also affects the activities of cells, such as proliferation, differentiation, and migration [34]. High expression of miR-590-3p in CSCC was observed after knockdown of TPRG1-AS1, which may inhibit cell proliferation, migration, and invasive activities, but silencing miR-590-3p expression reversed this effect. Thus, TPRG1-AS1 may play a role in CSCC formation and development by targeting miR-590-3p expression. However, we acknowledge some limitations of this study: firstly, the involvement of TPRG1-AS1 targeting miR-590-3p in the signaling pathway of CSCC remains to be further explored in depth. Second, more experimental models are needed to validate our findings, especially in vitro models; Finally, more CSCC patients need to be included in this study to make the data more accurate.

In conclusion, high expression of TPRG1-AS1 may serve to predict poor prognosis in CSCC. Knockdown of TPRG1-AS1 may induce high expression of miR-590-3p, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and slowing down the development of CSCC.

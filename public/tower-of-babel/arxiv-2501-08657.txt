Liouville type results for the fractional truncated Laplacians in a half-space
Giulio Galise, Hitoshi Ishii

Abstract
Existence issues of viscosity supersolutions in the half-space ℝ + N \mathbb{R}^{N}_{+} , for a class of fully nonlinear integral equations involving the fractional truncated Laplacians and a power-like nonlinearity in the unknown function, are addressed in this paper, the aim being to obtain estimates on the threshold exponents separating the existence from the nonexistence regimes.

26D10, 35D40 , 45M20, 47G10.

Keywords:
Fully nonlinear integral operators, viscosity solutions, Liouville type theorem.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the existence/nonexistence of nontrivial viscosity supersolutions to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\pm}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\\
u=0&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$,}\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\;x_{N}>0\right\}
,
N\geq 2
and
p
is a real exponent.

Problems (
1
) involve the nonlinear integral operators
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\pm}
that are extremal among linear operators with one dimensional fractional diffusion. They are defined as follows: given
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\xi|=1
, let

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}u(x)=C_{s}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{u(x+\tau\xi)+u(x-\tau\xi)-2u(x)}{\tau^{1+2s}}\,d\tau,

(2)

where
s\in(0,1)
and
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is any sufficiently smooth function for which the right hand side of (
2
) is well defined. The quantity
C_{s}
is a positive normalizing constant for which the asymptotic

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}u(x)\to\left\langle D^{2}u(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\qquad\text{as $s\to 1^{-}$}

is guaranteed. The operator
\mathcal{I}_{\xi}u
, which is linear in
u
, acts as the
2s
-fractional derivative of
u
along the direction
\xi
.

For any integer
k\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
the family of
k
-dimensional orthonormal sets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}=\left\{\left\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\right\}\in\underbrace{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}_{k\;\text{times}}:\,\;\left\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}\;\;\;\forall i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,k\right\}\right\}.

(3)

The operators
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}u
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}u
, named
minimal and maximal
k
-th fractional truncated Laplacian
respectively, are defined through the formulas

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{-}_{k}u(x)&=\inf_{\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}u(x)\\
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}u(x)&=\sup_{\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}u(x).\end{split}

(4)

They converge, as
s\to 1^{-}
, to the sums of the smallest and largest
k
eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of
u
which are degenerate elliptic operators arising in geometric contexts, see e.g.
[
1
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
. These limit PDEs constitute a class of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman
equations of stochastic optimal control. We refer for this to, e.g.,
[
12
]
.

The study of integral operators with diffusion supported along lower dimensional sets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
generated some interest in the PDE community in recent years, also motivated by its connection with stable Lévy process in Probability theory. As far as regularity issues for solutions associated to this kind of operators are concerned, we refer e.g. to
[
5
,
23
]
and the references therein.

The operator
\mathcal{I}^{-}_{1}
leads to the notion of fractional first eigenvalue and it has been considered in
[
11
]
in order to extend the notion of convexity to the nonlocal setting. In
[
4
]
the associated evolution problem has been also studied. In
[
24
]
, through min-max type formula involving one-dimensional fractional Laplacians, the trace fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
was introduced, and its main properties were investigated. Further nonlocal approximations of the truncated Laplace
operators can be found in
[
25
]
where related properties to the maximum principle were considered.

Liouville type theorems play a fundamental role in the theory of integro-differential elliptic PDE. In addition to
its intrinsic interests concerning classifications of solutions, they are also employed to get existence results in bounded domains via blow-up procedure, see e.g
[
14
]
. The existence issue of solutions to inequalities posed in cone-like or exterior domains has been treated and generalized in different contexts. As far as fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic operators involving a power-like dependence in
u
are concerned, we refer e.g. to
[
2
,
3
,
22
]
, where the presence of
critical exponents
that sharply characterize the range of
p
’s for which positive supersolutions exist or not rely on the existence of fundamental solutions and various forms of the strong maximum principle and Harnack type inequalities. Moreover the existence of such fundamental solutions is obtained by means of an abstract topological fixed point theorem and compactness estimates in Hölder spaces. Such approach, which has been recently extended in
[
21
]
to fully nonlinear integral operators, does not seem applicable to our strongly degenerate setting since elliptic estimates and Harnack inequalities are missing. Some differences and counterexamples between the operators (
4
) we are interested in the present paper and more standard integral operators, like e.g. the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, can be found in
[
8
]
. Nevertheless, using the following weak form of ellipticity

\text{$u\geq v$\; in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}$\; and\; $u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})$}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}u(x_{0})\geq\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}u(x_{0})

(5)

and the sub/superadditivity properties of the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
, we still obtain some results concerning the existence of nontrivial supersolutions of (
1
) with respect to
p
.

Adopting the terminology used in
[
15
]
, condition (
5
) can be rephrased by saying that the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
satisfy the
global comparison property
.

Hencefoth we shall assume that the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
act on functions belonging to the space
\mathcal{S}
, see Definition
2.1
and Remark
2.2
, in such a way
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}u(x_{0})
is well defined as soon as
u
is twice differentiable around
x_{0}
.

In order to describe our results, we first consider the case
p>0
.

As far as the operator
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
is concerned, we shall use the
fundamental solution

w_{\bar{\gamma}}(x)=|x|^{-\bar{\gamma}}
, whose existence was proved in
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
, and the subadditivity property of
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
, to build a strict subsolution
\psi
of
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}\psi=0
, in the complement of a large ball, which behaves at infinity like
-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}w_{\bar{\gamma}}
. The availability of such subsolution allows us to adapt, to our nonlocal and degenerate context, the rescaled-type test function argument used in
[
19
]
for uniformly elliptic inequalities involving the minimal Pucci’s operators and then applied also in
[
7
]
to the case of truncated Laplacians.
The number
\bar{\gamma}=\bar{\gamma}(k,s)\in(0,1)
, which is the unique exponent for which
w_{\gamma}(x)=|x|^{-\gamma}
is a radial solution of
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}w_{\gamma}(x)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
exists if, and only if,
k=1
and
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
or
k\geq 2
and
s\in(0,1)
. As a consequence we obtain the following Liouville type theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
be a nonnegative viscosity supersolution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0&\text{in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\\
u=0&\text{in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\end{array}\right.

(6)

where
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
if
k=1
,
s\in(0,1)
otherwise. If
0<p<1+\frac{2s}{\bar{\gamma}+1}
,
then
u(x)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

The next result complements Theorem
1.1
in the range of values of
k
and
s
for which
\bar{\gamma}
does not exist. It relies on the fact that the function
w(x)=-|x|^{2s-1}
is a solution, unbounded at infinity, of
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{1}w=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
for any
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
be a nonnegative viscosity supersolution of (
6
) with
k=1
and
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.
If
0<p<\frac{1}{1-s}
, then
u(x)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

Differently from more standard elliptic integro-differential operators, new phenomena arises for
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}
and
k<N
. In these case, problem (
1
) admits in fact nontrivial supersolutions for any
p>0
. In the next theorem we prove the existence of supersolutions which are not monotone with respect the
x_{N}
-variable.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k<N
be a positive integer. Then for any
p>0
there exists a nonnegative viscosity supersolution
u\in C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of

\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0\quad\,\text{in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}

satisfying the following properties:

u(x)=0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\quad\quad\text{and}\qquad\limsup_{x_{N}\to+\infty}u(x^{\prime},x_{N})>0\quad\text{uniformly w.r.t. $x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$}.

As far as
\mathcal{I}^{-}_{N}
is concerned, we first point out that, differently from
\mathcal{I}^{-}_{k}
with
k<N
, the operator
\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}
admits the fundamental solution
w_{\tilde{\gamma}}(x)=|x|^{-\tilde{\gamma}}
, where now
\tilde{\gamma}>1
(if
N\geq 3
) and converges to
N-2
as
s\to 1^{-}
, see
[
9
, Remark 4.9 and Lemma 6.2]
for details.

The nonexistence of positive (in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
) supersolutions to (
1
) in the sublinear case
0<p\leq 1
will be obtained in a similar way as for
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
be a nonnegative viscosity supersolution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0&\text{in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\\
u=0&\text{in\; $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}.\end{array}\right.

If
0<p\leq 1
,
then
u(x)\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

The main difference between
\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}
and
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
occurs in the superlinear range
p>1
. Because of the lack of the subadditivity property for
\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}
we cannot infer that the function
\psi(x)=-\frac{\partial w_{\tilde{\gamma}}}{\partial x_{N}}
, or a modification of it near the origin, is still a solution of
\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}\psi>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Hence a corresponding Liouville theorem is missing in this case.
Nevertheless, using the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}
is superadditive, we shall prove that the range of
p
’s for which (
1
) admits positive supersolutions is in fact strictly larger than
(1+\frac{2s}{\tilde{\gamma}},\,+\infty)
, which is the corresponding range of existence of positive entire supersolution , see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

There is a number
\gamma^{+}\in(\tilde{\gamma},+\infty)
such that for any
p>1+\frac{2s}{\gamma^{+}}
the equation

\mathcal{I}^{-}_{N}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

admits a nonnegative supersolution
u\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying the following properties:

u(x)=0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\,,\quad u(x)>0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\,,\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0.

As far as the singular case
p\leq 0
is concerned, we first show that under suitable growth assumption at infinity for
u
, then (
1
) has no positive supersolution.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p\leq 0
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be any of
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
with
k=1,\ldots,N
. Then the problem

\begin{cases}u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}\\
u\geq 0&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\\
0<u(x)\leq C(1+|x|)^{\alpha}&\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}\\
\mathcal{I}u+u^{p}\leq 0&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$ (in the viscosity sense),}\end{cases}

(7)

where
C>0
and
0<\alpha<\frac{2s}{1-p}
are constants, has no solution.

It is noteworthy that for uniformly elliptic operators (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
-
[
20
, Proposition 3,2]
and
[
21
, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.1]
for the local and nonlocal case respectively), the nonexistence of positive supersolutions for
-1\leq p\leq 0
is obtained without further restriction on the growth of
u
, contrary to our assumption in (
7
). On the other hand, the corresponding proofs heavily rely on quantitative strong comparison principle and weak Harnack inequality, which are not at disposal for our degenerate operators. This partially motivate the use of additional assumptions, naturally arising in the rescaled test function method employed in the proof of Theorem
1.6
. We also remark that the growth condition in (
7
) can be replaced with

u(x)=o(|x|^{\frac{2s}{1-p}})\;\quad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$.}

(8)

Positive supersolutions growing at infinity with order
{\frac{2s}{1-p}}
appear as soon as
p<-1
, at least for some operators
\mathcal{I}
as expressed by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{-}_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq N
or
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{+}_{N}
. Then for any
p<-1
there exists a positive supersolution
u\in C^{0,\frac{2s}{1-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
of

\mathcal{I}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0\quad\text{in\, $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}

such that
u(x)=0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

We end this introduction by pointing out that all the above results imply some explicit bounds on the critical exponents

\begin{split}p^{\star}\left(\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}\right)&:=\inf\left\{p>1:\;\text{\eqref{2912eq1} has a nontrivial supersolution}\right\}\\

(9)

2
Some preliminaries

This section is devoted to some technical results that will be employed in the proofs of the main results of the paper. We start by introducing a suitable function space in which the notion of viscosity solution to (
1
) is settled.

Definition 2.1
.

The set
\mathcal{S}
is the space of all functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following assumptions:

(H1)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and any unit vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the map

\tau\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto u(x+\tau\xi)

is measurable;

(H2)

there exists
\alpha\in[0,2s)
and
C>0
such that

\left|u(x)\right|\leq C{(1+|x|)}^{\alpha}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Remark 2.2
.

The set
\mathcal{S}
give a sufficient condition for which if
u\in\mathcal{S}
is twice differentiable in a neighborhood of
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
then the quantities
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}u(x_{0})
are well defined.

We point out that
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}u(x_{0})
may be finite even for some singular or unbounded and oscillating function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.3
.

In the following definition we denote by
\mathcal{I}
any of the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
.

Let
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
. A function
u\in\mathcal{S}\cap LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
(
u\in\mathcal{S}\cap USC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
) is a viscosity supersolution (subsolution) of

\mathcal{I}u+f(x,u)=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}

if for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and every
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
,
r>0
, such that
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and

\varphi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})\;,\;\;\varphi(x)\leq u(x)\;\;(\varphi(x)\geq u(x))\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),

then if we let

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(x)&\text{in $B_{r}(x_{0})$}\\
u(x)&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})$}\end{cases}

we have

\mathcal{I}\psi(x_{0})+f(x_{0},u(x_{0}))\leq 0\quad\left(\mathcal{I}u(x_{0})+f(x_{0},u(x_{0}))\geq 0)\right).

(10)

A function
u\in\mathcal{S}\cap LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
(
u\in\mathcal{S}\cap USC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
) is a viscosity supersolution (subsolution) of(
1
) if it satisfies (
10
) with
f(x,u)=u^{p}
and
u\geq 0
(
u\leq 0
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

We remark that the above definition does not cover (
1
), with
p\leq 0
,
but, for such a problem, we will argue only the viscosity properties of those functions
u
which are positive in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Another remark is that, although good stability properties may be lost,
the above definition makes sense even if
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is not continuous.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
\mathcal{I}
be either
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{I}^{+}_{N}
or
\mathcal{I}^{-}_{N}
. Then there exists a positive radial function
w\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with bounded derivatives up to order 3, such that

\begin{cases}r\mapsto w(r)\;\;\text{is decreasing}\\
\mathcal{I}w(x)>0\;\;\text{if $|x|\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

(11)

Proof.

The construction of
w
is done by modifying the function
|x|^{-\gamma}
near the origin, where
\gamma>0
has to be chosen sufficiently large. For
\gamma>0
, let
\varphi_{\gamma}(r)=r^{-\gamma}
and set

w_{\gamma}(x)=\begin{cases}\tilde{f}(|x|^{2})&\text{if $|x|^{2}<\frac{1}{2}$}\\
|x|^{-\gamma}&\text{if $|x|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}$\,,}\end{cases}

where
\tilde{f}
is such that the graph of
\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(r)
is the tangent line to
{\left(\varphi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(r)\right)}^{\prime\prime}
at
r=\frac{1}{2}
. In addition we require that

\tilde{f}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\varphi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad\tilde{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\varphi^{\prime}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right).

A straightforward computation yields

\tilde{f}(r)=\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{1}{2})}{6}(r-\frac{1}{2})^{3}+\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{1}{2})}{2}(r-\frac{1}{2})^{2}+\varphi^{\prime}_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{1}{2})(r-\frac{1}{2})+\varphi_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{1}{2}).

By construction
w_{\gamma}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

\alpha=-c_{N}^{+}(\gamma)C_{s}
is positive because of
\gamma<\gamma^{+}
.

Fix any
\mu\in(0,\,s)
and set

z(x)=(x_{N})_{+}^{\mu}\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Since

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}z(x)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{I}_{e_{i}}z(x)=\mathcal{I}_{e_{N}}z(x)\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},

by Proposition
2.9
and Remark
2.10
we have

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}z(x)+\beta z^{1-\frac{2s}{\mu}}(x)\leq 0\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N},

where
\beta=-c_{s,\mu}C_{s}
is a positive constant.

We set

w(x)=\min\{\phi(x),\,z(x)\}\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

It is obvious that
w\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and that

w(x)=0\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ w(x)>0\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}.

Moreover the function
w
is bounded from above. Indeed, if
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
, then

w(x)\leq\phi(x)={\left|x+Re_{N}\right|}^{-\gamma}\leq R^{-\gamma}\leq 1.

(81)

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. Assume that
w(x)=z(x)
. We have

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}w(x)+\beta w^{p}(x)\leq\mathcal{I}^{-}_{N}z(x)+\beta z^{1-\frac{2s}{\mu}}(x)z^{p-1+\frac{2s}{\mu}}(x)

and, since
p>1
and hence
z^{p-1+\frac{2s}{\mu}}(x)\leq 1
by (
81
),

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}w(x)+\beta w^{p}(x)\leq\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}z(x)+\beta z^{1-\frac{2s}{\mu}}(x)\leq 0.

Assume next that
w(x)=\phi(x)
. We have

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}w(x)+\alpha w^{p}(x)\leq\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}\phi(x)+\alpha\phi^{p}(x)\leq 0.

Hence, we have

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}w(x)+\min\{\alpha,\beta\}w^{p}(x)\leq 0\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a constant to be fixed. Observe that for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
,

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}(\varepsilon w)(x)+(\varepsilon w)^{p}(x)=\varepsilon\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}w(x)+\varepsilon^{p}w^{p}(x)\leq\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{p-1}-\min\{\alpha,\beta\})w^{p}(x).

Noting that
p>1
, we fix
\varepsilon>0
so that

\varepsilon^{p-1}-\min\{\alpha,\,\beta\}\leq 0,

and conclude that

\mathcal{I}_{N}^{-}(\varepsilon w)(x)+(\varepsilon w)^{p}(x)\leq 0\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}.

∎

4.3
Theorem
1.7

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
u(x)=M(x_{N})_{+}^{\mu}
with
M>0
and
\mu\in(0,s)
to be fixed.

We first consider the case
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{-}_{k}
for any
1\leq k\leq N
.
Using the minimality of
\mathcal{I}
among
k
-dimensional orthonormal subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\mathcal{I}u(x)\leq\sum_{i=N-k+1}^{N}\mathcal{I}_{e_{i}}u(x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

(82)

Moreover, since
u
is constant in all directions that are orthogonal to
e_{N}
, we get (whenever
k\geq 2
)

\mathcal{I}_{e_{i}}u(x)=0\qquad\forall i=N-k+1,\ldots,N-1.

(83)

By (
82
)-(
83
) it follows that

\mathcal{I}u(x)\leq\mathcal{I}_{e_{N}}u(x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

In view of Proposition
2.9
and Remark
2.10
we infer that there exists
C_{s,\mu}
such that

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}u(x)+u^{p}(x)&\leq MC_{s,\mu}x_{N}^{\mu-2s}+M^{p}x_{N}^{\mu p}\\
&=Mx_{N}^{\mu-2s}\left(C_{s,\mu}+M^{p-1}x_{N}^{\mu(p-1)+2s}\right)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.\end{split}

(84)

Choosing
\mu=\frac{2s}{1-p}\in(0,s)
, it turns out that
C_{s,\mu}<0
and

\mathcal{I}u(x)+u^{p}(x)\leq Mx_{N}^{\mu-2s}\left(C_{s,\mu}+M^{p-1}\right)=0\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(85)

by taking
M={\left(\frac{1}{|C_{s,\mu}|}\right)}^{\frac{1}{1-p}}
.

Consider now the case
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{+}_{N}
. For any orhonormal basis
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{N}
one has

\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\langle\xi_{i},e_{N}\right\rangle}^{2}=1.

Then, denoting by
\bar{i}\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
the index such that

{\left\langle\xi_{\bar{i}},e_{N}\right\rangle}^{2}=\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}{\left\langle\xi_{i},e_{N}\right\rangle}^{2},

it turns out that

\left|\left\langle\xi_{\bar{i}},e_{N}\right\rangle\right|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}.

(86)

Again by Proposition
2.9
, we infer that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}u(x)\leq\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{\bar{i}}}u(x)=MC_{s,\mu}\left|\left\langle\xi_{\bar{i}},e_{N}\right\rangle\right|^{2s}x_{N}^{\mu-2s}\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

with
C_{s,\mu}<0
. Thus, by (
86
),

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}u(x)\leq\frac{MC_{s,\mu}}{{N}^{s}}x_{N}^{\mu-2s}\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Since the right-hand side of the above inequality does not depend on the particular choice of
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{N}
, we have

\mathcal{I}^{+}_{N}u(x)\leq\frac{MC_{s,\mu}}{N^{s}}x_{N}^{\mu-2s}\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Then the proof of the desired inequality

\mathcal{I}^{+}_{N}u(x)+u^{p}(x)\leq 0\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

follows as in (
84
)-(
85
) choosing
\mu=\frac{2s}{1-p}
and
M={\left(\frac{{N}^{s}}{|C_{s,\mu}|}\right)}^{\frac{1}{1-p}}
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It is worth pointing out that, as far as the operators
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
with
k<N
are concerned, the functions
v(x)=(x_{N})_{+}^{\mu}
are solutions of

\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}u(x)=0\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}

(87)

for any
\mu\in(0,s]
. Indeed, by Proposition

, for any
k
-dimensional orthonormal set

k

(88)

87
) holds and, differently from (

Proposition A.1
.

Let either
1<p<q
or
q<p<1
. If
u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
is a positive viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$},

(89)

where
\mathcal{I}
denotes one of the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
, then the function

v(x)={\left(\frac{p-1}{q-1}\right)}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}u^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}}(x)

is a positive viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}v(x)+v^{q}(x)=0\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}.

(90)

Proof.

Let

\beta=\frac{p-1}{q-1}=\frac{1-p}{1-q}\in(0,1)\,,\quad\alpha=\beta^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

(91)

In this way

v(x)=\alpha u^{\beta}(x)

with

\beta-1+p=\beta q\;,\quad\,\alpha\beta=\alpha^{q}.

(92)

In order prove that
v
is a viscosity supersolution of (
90
), let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{\delta}(x_{0})})
, with
\delta>0
, touching
v
from below at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Then if we define

\psi=\begin{cases}\varphi&\text{in $B_{\delta}(x_{0})$}\\
v&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\delta}(x_{0})$}\end{cases}

we have to show that

\mathcal{I}\psi(x_{0})+\psi^{q}(x_{0})\leq 0.

(93)

Note that
\mathcal{I}\psi(x_{0})
is nonincreasing with respect the parameter
\delta
in the definition of
\psi
. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume
\delta
sufficiently small such that
\psi(x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The key point to obtain (
93
) is the following inequality: if
\xi
is any unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then

\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\psi(x_{0})\leq\beta\psi^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}(x_{0})\mathcal{I}_{\xi}\psi^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0}).

(94)

We first show that (
93
) is a consequence of the above inequality, then we shall prove (
94
).

Take an orthonormal set
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}
. Using (
94
) with
\xi
replaced by
\xi_{i}
and summing-up in
i=1,\ldots,k
we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\psi(x_{0})\leq\beta\psi^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}(x_{0})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\psi^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0})=\beta{\left(\alpha\psi^{\beta-1}(x_{0})\right)}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{\psi}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0}).

Then if we take the supremum among all possible
k
-orthonormal sets in the case
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{+}_{k}
or the infimum if
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}^{-}_{k}
, we infer that

\mathcal{I}\psi(x_{0})\leq\beta{\left(\alpha\psi^{\beta-1}(x_{0})\right)}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{\psi}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0}).

(95)

Now, since

\left(\frac{\psi}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}&\text{in $B_{\delta}(x_{0})$}\\
u&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\delta}(x_{0})$}\end{cases}

is a test function for
u
at
x_{0}
, by the supersolution property for
u
we obtain that

\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{\psi}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0})\leq-u^{p}(x_{0}).

(96)

Using (
95
)-(
96
) and the fact that

\psi(x_{0})=v(x_{0})=\alpha u^{\beta}(x_{0}),

we get

\mathcal{I}\psi(x_{0})\leq-\alpha\beta u^{\beta-1+p}(x_{0}).

(97)

In view of (
92
), the equality

\alpha\beta u^{\beta-1+p}(x_{0})=\psi^{q}(x_{0})

(98)

holds and then the desired inequality (
93
) follows from (
97
)-(
98
).

To complete the proof it remains to show (
94
). For this we use the following elementary inequality (which is a straightforward application of the mean value theorem to the function
f(t)=t^{\frac{1}{\beta}})
: for any positive number
a
,
b
and
\beta\in(0,1)
it holds

b-a\leq\beta\,a^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left(b^{\frac{1}{\beta}}-a^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right).

(99)

Using (
99
) with
a=\psi(x_{0})
and
b=\psi(x_{0}\pm\tau\xi)
we have that for any
\tau>0

\psi(x_{0}+\tau\xi)+\psi(x_{0}-\tau\xi)-2\psi(x_{0})\leq\beta\psi^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}(x_{0})\left(\psi^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0}+\tau\xi)+\psi^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0}-\tau\xi)-2\psi^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(x_{0})\right).

Then (
94
) easily follows multiplying both side of the previous inequality by
\tau^{-(1+2s)}
and integrating.
∎

In the case
p,q>0
, Proposition
A.1
holds true also in the class of nonnegative functions.

Proposition A.2
.

Let either
1<p<q
or
0<q<p<1
. If
u\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
is a nonnegative viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}u(x)+u^{p}(x)=0\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$},

(100)

where
\mathcal{I}
denotes one of the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
, then the function

v(x)={\left(\frac{p-1}{q-1}\right)}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}u^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}}(x)

is a nonnegative viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}v(x)+v^{q}(x)=0\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$}.

(101)

Proof.

Fix any
\varepsilon>0
and note that
u_{\varepsilon}(x):=u(x)+\varepsilon
is a positive supersolution of

\mathcal{I}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+u_{\varepsilon}^{p}(x)=f_{\varepsilon}(x)\;\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$},

where

f_{\varepsilon}(x)=(u+\varepsilon)^{p}(x)-u^{p}(x).

As
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, then
f_{\varepsilon}\to 0
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

Set
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=\alpha u_{\varepsilon}^{\beta}(x)
with
\alpha,\beta
defined in (
91
)-(
92
). Note that

v_{\varepsilon}>0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ \;and\;}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}v_{\varepsilon}(x)=v(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

(102)

With the same argument used in Proposition
A.1
, it turns out that
v_{\varepsilon}
is a viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}v_{\varepsilon}(x)+v_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)=g_{\varepsilon}(x)\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}$,}

(103)

where

\begin{split}g_{\varepsilon}(x):&=\alpha\beta{(u+\varepsilon)}^{\beta-1}(x)f_{\varepsilon}(x)\\
&=\alpha\beta\left({(u+\varepsilon)}^{p+\beta-1}(x)-({(u+\varepsilon)}^{\beta-1}u^{p})(x)\right).\end{split}

(104)

Note that, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
g_{\varepsilon}\to 0
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. To check this observe that for any
0<\varepsilon<1
and
0<\lambda<\Lambda
, if
\lambda\leq u(x)\leq\Lambda
, then for some constant
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda,p)>0
(the Lipschitz constant of
r\mapsto r^{p}
on
[\lambda,\Lambda+1]
)

0\leq g_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\alpha\beta\lambda^{\beta-1}C\varepsilon,

and if
u(x)\leq\lambda
, then

0\leq g_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\alpha\beta{(\lambda+\varepsilon)}^{\beta q}.

Choose any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Since
u
is locally bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, there is a constant
\Lambda>0
such that
u\leq\Lambda
on
K
. Fix any
\gamma>0
. Choose
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
so that

\alpha\beta\lambda^{\beta q}<\gamma.

Then choose
\delta\in(0,1)
so that

\alpha\beta{(\lambda+\delta)}^{\beta q}<\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\beta\lambda^{\beta-1}C\delta<\gamma.

If
0<\varepsilon<\delta
and
x\in K
, then

g_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\alpha\beta\max\left\{\lambda^{\beta-1}C\varepsilon,{(\lambda+\varepsilon)}^{\beta q}\right\}\leq\alpha\beta\max\left\{\lambda^{\beta-1}C\delta,{(\lambda+\delta)}^{\beta q}\right\}<\gamma.

This show that, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
g_{\varepsilon}\to 0
uniformly on
K
. The stability of the viscosity property under the local uniform convergence, see next Lemma
A.3
, gives the required conclusion.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
q>0
and
v\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap\mathcal{S}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be one of the operators
\mathcal{I}^{\pm}_{k}
. Assume that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
v_{\varepsilon}\in LSC(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathcal{S}
is a viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}v_{\varepsilon}(x)+v_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)\leq g_{\varepsilon}(x)\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},

where
g_{\varepsilon}\to 0
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
and that for some constant
C>0
,

v_{\varepsilon}>v\geq-C\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ v(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}v_{\varepsilon}(x)\ \ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(105)

Then,
v

is a viscosity supersolution of

\mathcal{I}v(x)+v^{q}(x)\leq 0\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, and
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we set

\delta(\varphi,x,\xi)(\tau)=\varphi(x+\tau\xi)+\varphi(x-\tau\xi)-2\varphi(x)\ \ \text{ for }\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\varphi|+|D^{2}\varphi|)<\infty
, then

|\delta(\varphi,x,\xi)(\tau)|\leq\min\{4\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty}\tau^{2}\}.

(106)

Now, let
\varphi
be a smooth test function touching
v
from below at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
Thanks to (
105
), we may assume that
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}(v-\varphi)>0
for any
r>0

and that
\varphi(x)=-R
for all
|x|\geq R

and some
R>0
.
Fix
r>0
so that

\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Let
x_{\varepsilon}
be a minimum point of
v_{\varepsilon}-\varphi
on

\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
. As usual, since

(v_{\varepsilon}-\varphi)(x_{0})\geq(v_{\varepsilon}-\varphi)(x_{\varepsilon})\geq(v-\varphi)(x_{\varepsilon})\geq(v-\varphi)(x_{0})=0,

we see that
x_{\varepsilon}\to x_{0}
(up to a subsequence) and
v_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\to v(x_{0})
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Since
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}(v-\varphi)>0
for all
r>0
, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, then

x_{\varepsilon}
is a global minimum point of
v_{\varepsilon}-\varphi
.
By the supersolution property of
v_{\varepsilon}
, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we have

\mathcal{I}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})+v_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x_{\varepsilon})\leq g_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}),

from which it follows that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})+v^{q}(x_{0})\leq 0.

It remains to show that

\mathcal{I}\varphi(x_{0})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon}).

Consider first the case when
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}
. Fix any
orthonormal frame
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
. Note that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\varphi(x)=C_{s}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\tau^{-1-2s}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta(\varphi,x,\xi_{i})(\tau)d\tau,

and that for all
\tau>0
,

\tau^{-1-2s}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta(\varphi,x,\xi_{i})(\tau)\Big|\leq k(4\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty})\min\{\tau^{-1-2s},\tau^{1-2s}\}.

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})=C_{s}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau^{-1-2s}\delta(\varphi,x_{0},\xi_{i})(\tau)d\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\varphi(x_{0}).

Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}}\varphi(x_{0})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon}),

and moreover,

\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}\varphi(x_{0})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{+}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon}).

Next, consider the case when
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, we select an orthonormal
frame
\{\xi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
so that

\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon>\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon}).

We may assume, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, that for
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

\{\xi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}\to\{\xi_{i}^{0}\}\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{Nk}.

Again, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}^{0}}\varphi(x_{0}).

Thus, by exploiting the choice of
\{\xi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}
, we deduce that

\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}\varphi(x_{0})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\xi_{i}^{0}}\varphi(x_{0})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{I}_{k}^{-}\varphi(x_{\varepsilon}).\qed

The (3+1)-dimensional scalar field model analysis of beam spin asymmetry in the electroproduction of a scalar meson off a scalar target Preprint: Preprint number here
Andrew Lundeen, Chueng-Ryong Ji, Yongwoo Choi, Ho-Meoyng Choi

Abstract
We explore exclusive scalar meson electroproduction off a scalar target in the (3+1)-dimensional scalar field model. This model analysis is a straightforward extension of the previous (1+1)-dimensional model analysis presented in Phys. Rev. D 105 , 096014 (2022). In contrast to the (1+1)-dimensional model, the (3+1)-dimensional model allows us to compute the beam spin asymmetry (BSA), which is proportional to the imaginary part of the product of the two Compton form factors (CFFs) that appear in the hadronic current of the present scalar meson electroproduction process. We compute both real and imaginary parts of the CFFs and note that the BSA is detectable for − t / Q 2 ≳ 0.1 -t/Q^{2}\gtrsim 0.1 although it gets quite small in the kinematic region − t / Q 2 ≪ 0.1 -t/Q^{2}\ll 0.1 where the factorization of the generalized parton distribution (GPD) is attainable. We find the analytic forms of the leading twist GPD for the DGLAP and ERBL regions in the (3+1)-dimensional scalar field model, confirming its uniqueness independent of the hadronic current component. While we verify that the GPD sum rule for the total result of summing the DGLAP and ERBL regions holds for all components of the hadronic current, we note that the respective correspondence of the DGLAP and ERBL regions to the valence and non-valence parts of the electromagnetic form factor holds only for the light-front plus component of the hadronic current but not for any other components of the hadronic current. We discuss the polynomiality of the GPD up to the second moments and remark on accessible ranges of kinematics to measure the BSA and CFFs with respect to the future experimental efforts of extracting the leading-twist GPDs.

II
Formulation of Exclusive Meson Electroproduction

II.1
Amplitude, cross section, and beam spin asymmetry

We examine exclusive scalar meson electroproduction off a scalar target under the one-photon exchange mechanism,

e(k)+\mathbf{h}(p)\to e^{\prime}(k^{\prime})+\mathbf{m}(q^{\prime})+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(p^{\prime}),

(1)

where the exchanged virtual photon carries momentum
q=k-k^{\prime}
.
A schematic of the momentum flow is shown in Fig.
1
.

Momentum assignments for exclusive meson electroproduction.
Here,
p
and
p^{\prime}
denote the four-momenta of the initial and final scalar target states,
while
q
and
q^{\prime}
represent the momenta of the incoming virtual photon and the produced meson, respectively.

For this process, the transition amplitude takes the form

i{\mathscr{M}}=\frac{e^{2}}{q^{2}}L^{\mu}J_{\mu},

(2)

where we define the leptonic and hadronic currents, respectively, by

\displaystyle L^{\mu}=\bar{u}_{e^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},s^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}u_{e}(k,s),

(3)

and

\displaystyle J^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(p^{\prime})\,\mathbf{m}(q^{\prime})|j^{\mu}(q,\Delta,\bar{p})|\gamma^{*}(q)\,\mathbf{h}(p)\rangle.

(4)

Here, the hadronic current
J^{\mu}
encodes all the information about the target and produced meson structure,
with
j^{\mu}
containing the relevant dynamical degrees of freedom.

Specializing to scalar (
0^{++}
) meson production off a scalar target,

j^{\mu}
depends on three independent momenta,
q
,
\Delta=p-p^{\prime}
, and
\bar{p}=p+p^{\prime}
.
From the Lorentz and gauge invariance, one finds two independent form factors.
Following
Ji et al. 2019
,

\displaystyle J^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q^{2}\Delta^{\mu}-q\cdot\Delta q^{\mu}\right){\cal F}_{1}+\left(\bar{p}\cdot q\Delta^{\mu}-q\cdot\Delta\bar{p}^{\mu}\right){\cal F}_{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A^{\mu}{\cal F}_{1}+B^{\mu}{\cal F}_{2}.

The form factors,
{\cal F}_{1}
, and

depend on the invariants

,

The leptonic tensor

The hadronic tensor is

\langle|{\mathscr{M}}|^{2}\rangle=\frac{e^{4}}{q^{4}}{\cal L}^{\mu\nu}{\cal H}_{\mu\nu}.

This clean factorization without the Bethe-Heitler contributions allows a
direct separation of electron kinematics from the hadronic structure, neglecting the higher-order corrections.

In the target rest frame (TRF)

2

the five-fold differential electroproduction cross section is given by

d\sigma=\frac{d^{5}\sigma}{dy\,dx_{A}\,dt\,d\phi_{q^{\prime}}\,d\phi_{k^{\prime}}}=\kappa\,\langle|{\mathscr{M}}|^{2}\rangle,

(8)

with

\kappa=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\,\frac{y\,x_{A}}{32\,Q^{2}\,\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{2M_{T}x_{A}}{Q}\right)^{2}}},

(9)

where

represents the fractional energy transfer in the TRF

M_{T}
is the target mass.

\displaystyle\langle|{\mathscr{M}}|^{2}\rangle=\frac{e^{4}}{q^{4}}\bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{2q^{2}}{\epsilon-1}\langle|\tau_{fi}|^{2}\rangle+2i\lambda\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}k_{\alpha}k^{\prime}_{\beta}\,{\cal H}_{\mu\nu}\bigg],

(10)

\epsilon\equiv\frac{\Lambda_{xx}-\Lambda_{yy}}{\Lambda_{xx}+\Lambda_{yy}}=\frac{(2-y)^{2}}{1+(1-y)^{2}+2x_{A}^{2}y^{2}z^{2}}-1

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{L}(1+\epsilon)}({\cal H}_{xz}+{\cal H}_{zx}),

\displaystyle d\sigma_{T}(1+\epsilon\cos(2\phi))+d\sigma_{L}\epsilon_{L}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\frac{d\sigma_{\lambda=+1}-d\sigma_{\lambda=-1}}{d\sigma_{\lambda=+1}+d\sigma_{\lambda=-1}}

III
MODEL CALCULATION of Four-Point function

In this section, we compute the matrix element of the hadronic current
J^{\mu}
(see Eq. (
5
))
for scalar meson electroproduction off a scalar target–composed of two scalar constituents–using
a 3+1 dimensional scalar field model.

Our model Lagrangian is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}=&\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}+ie_{a}A_{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)^{\dagger}\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi_{a}+ie_{a}A^{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)-m_{a}^{2}\phi_{a}^{\dagger}\phi_{a}\\
&+\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{b}+ie_{b}A_{\mu}\phi_{b}\right)^{\dagger}\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi_{b}+ie_{b}A^{\mu}\phi_{b}\right)-m_{b}^{2}\phi_{b}^{\dagger}\phi_{b}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left(M^{2}_{T}\Phi^{2}+M_{S}^{2}\Phi_{M}^{2}-\partial_{\mu}\Phi\partial^{\mu}\Phi-\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{M}\partial^{\mu}\Phi_{M}\right)\\
&+g\Phi\left(\phi_{a}^{\dagger}\phi_{b}+\phi_{b}^{\dagger}\phi_{a}\right)+\Gamma\Phi_{M}\left(\phi_{a}^{\dagger}\phi_{b}+\phi_{b}^{\dagger}\phi_{a}\right).\end{split}

(26)

Here,
\phi_{a,b}
are the scalar constituent fields that compose the target hadron, carrying charges
e_{a,b}
and masses
m_{a,b}
.
The field
A^{\mu}
represents the virtual photon, while
\Phi
is the scalar field describing the initial/final hadron target of mass
M_{T}
, and

\Phi_{M}
denotes the electroproduced meson of mass
M_{S}
.
The coupling constant
g

has units of mass and reflects the binding strength of the constituents inside the target hadron.
The parameter
\Gamma
governs the coupling of the meson field to the scalar constituents, effectively encoding the meson-constituent vertex.
Since the meson is itself a composite particle, we can more generally model this vertex by allowing
\Gamma
to include momentum-dependent structure, such as

\Gamma=E_{S}+F_{S}\not{q}^{\prime}+G_{S}\not{k}+H_{S}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q^{\prime}_{\mu}k_{\nu}
.
While we note that additional terms can contribute due to the composite nature of the meson,
we simply take
\Gamma
to be the identity in this work.

IV.2
Leading twist GPD

As discussed above, the amplitude
J^{+}_{\rm DVMP}
factorizes into hard and soft parts,
allowing
{\cal F}^{\rm DVMP}_{1}
to be directly related to the leading-twist GPD.
In this context, it is instructive to compare with the reduced three-point function
associated with the electromagnetic (EM) form factor
F_{\rm EM}(t)
of the charged target,
defined via
J^{\mu}_{\rm EM}=(p+p^{\prime})^{\mu}F_{\rm EM}(t=-{\bm{\Delta}}^{2})
, where the EM current is given by

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{\rm EM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i{\cal N}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\delta(x-k^{+}/p^{+})(2k-\Delta)^{\mu}}{N_{k}N_{k-\Delta}D_{k-p}},

(56)

where
\delta(x-k^{+}/p^{+})
represent the nonlocality of the struck constituent-gauge boson vertex shown in Fig.

Since
J^{\mu}_{\rm DVMP}\propto J^{\mu}_{\rm EM}

(2k-\Delta)^{+}=(2x-\zeta)p^{+}
, and is thus related to the CFF
{\cal F}_{1}

Explicitly, the leading-twist DVMP amplitude takes the factorized form

J^{+}_{\rm DVMP}=\frac{e_{1}\zeta}{Q^{2}}\int dx\left(\frac{1}{x-\zeta+i\epsilon}-\frac{1}{x-i\epsilon}\right)H(x,\zeta,t),

(57)

where

H(x,\zeta,t)=i{\cal N}p^{+}\int\frac{dk^{-}d^{2}{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{(2k-\Delta)^{+}}{N_{k}N_{k-\Delta}D_{k-p}}

(58)

defines the leading-twist GPD.
After the integration over
k^{-}
, the current can be written as the sum of the two LF time-ordered diagrams (see Fig.

i.e.,

H=\begin{cases}~H_{\rm ERBL}(x,\zeta,t)&\text{for }~0\leq x\leq\zeta,\\
~H_{\rm DGLAP}(x,\zeta,t)&\text{for }~\zeta\leq x\leq 1,\end{cases}

(59)

where

\displaystyle H_{\rm DGLAP}=\frac{-\mathcal{N}(2x-\zeta)}{x(x-\zeta)(x-1)p^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{(\Delta k^{-}_{bi})(\Delta k^{-}_{bf})},

\displaystyle H_{\rm ERBL}=\frac{\mathcal{N}(2x-\zeta)}{x(x-\zeta)(x-1)p^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bm{k}}_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{(\Delta k^{-}_{if})(\Delta k^{-}_{ib})}.

The analytic forms of the GPDs
H(x,\zeta,t)
in the equal-mass case (
m_{1}=m_{2}=m

\displaystyle H_{\rm DGLAP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal N}}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{(1-x)(2x-\zeta)/(1-\zeta)}{\sqrt{(a_{D}-b_{D})^{2}+a_{D}c^{2}_{D}}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{-\tanh^{-1}\biggl(\frac{a_{D}-b_{D}}{\sqrt{(a_{D}-b_{D})^{2}+a_{D}c^{2}_{D}}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\tanh^{-1}\biggl(\frac{2(a_{D}-b_{D})+c_{D}^{2}}{2\sqrt{(a_{D}-b_{D})^{2}+a_{D}c^{2}_{D}}}\biggr)\biggr\},

\displaystyle H_{\rm ERBL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal N}}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{x(2x-\zeta)/\zeta}{\sqrt{(a_{E}-b_{E})^{2}+a_{E}c^{2}_{E}}}

(61)

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{-\tanh^{-1}\biggl(\frac{a_{E}-b_{E}}{\sqrt{(a_{E}-b_{E})^{2}+a_{E}c^{2}_{E}}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\tanh^{-1}\biggl(\frac{2(a_{E}-b_{E})+c_{E}^{2}}{2\sqrt{(a_{E}-b_{E})^{2}+a_{E}c^{2}_{E}}}\biggr)\biggr\},

where

\displaystyle a_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{E}=m^{2}-x(1-x)M^{2}_{T},

\displaystyle b_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}+\frac{(1-x)\left[\zeta^{2}M_{T}^{2}-\zeta(xM_{T}^{2}+t)+x\left({\bm{\Delta}}_{\perp}^{2}+t\right)\right]}{\zeta(1-\zeta)},

\displaystyle b_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}+\frac{x\left[x({\bm{\Delta}}^{2}_{\perp}+t)-\zeta t\right]}{\zeta^{2}},

\displaystyle c_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{1-x}{1-\zeta}|{\bm{\Delta}}_{\perp}|,\;c_{E}=2\frac{x}{\zeta}|{\bm{\Delta}}_{\perp}|.

(62)

If we take the reference frame where
{\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}}=0

and

\displaystyle H_{\rm DGLAP}(x,\zeta(t_{\rm min}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal N}(\zeta-1)(2x-\zeta)\log\!A_{\rm D}}{8\pi\zeta M^{2}_{T}[(\zeta-2)x+1]},

\displaystyle H_{\rm ERBL}(x,\zeta(t_{\rm min}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal N}(\zeta-1)(2x-\zeta)\log\!A_{\rm E}}{8\pi\zeta M^{2}_{T}[(\zeta-2)x+1]},

\displaystyle A_{\rm D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta-1)^{2}(m^{2}-M^{2}_{T}x(1-x))}{(\zeta-1)^{2}m^{2}+M^{2}_{T}(x-1)(x-\zeta)},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta-1)(m^{2}-M^{2}_{T}x(1-x))}{(\zeta-1)m^{2}+M^{2}_{T}x(x-\zeta)},

IV.3
PDF and Lowest Moment of GPD

In the limit
(\zeta,t)\to 0
, we recover the ordinary parton distribution function (PDF)
q(x)
, i.e.,
H(x,0,0)=H_{\rm DGLAP}(x,0,0)=q(x)
.
For the case of equal constituent masses,
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
, we obtain the analytic form

q(x)=\frac{{\cal N}}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{x(1-x)}{x(1-x)M^{2}_{T}-m^{2}}.

(65)

The normalization constant
{\cal N}
is fixed by imposing
\int^{1}_{0}dxq(x)=1
, and the same
{\cal N}
is consistently used in the analyses of the CFFs and BSA.
The corresponding PDF in (1+1) dimensions is given by
Choi et al. 2022

q(x)\propto\frac{x(1-x)}{[x(1-x)M^{2}_{T}-m^{2}]^{2}}.

(66)

It is also well known that the lowest moment, i.e. the first Mellin moment, of the leading-twist GPD provides
the electromagnetic form factor of the target meson as given by

F_{\text{EM}}\left(t\right)=\int^{1}_{0}\frac{dx}{1-\zeta/2}H\left(x,\zeta,t\right).

(67)

This well-known GPD sum-rule for the total result of summing the DGLAP and ERBL regions holds for all components of the hadronic current as one may understand from
J^{\mu}_{\rm DVMP}\propto J^{\mu}_{\rm EM}
. However, one should note that the respective correspondence of the DGLAP and ERBL regions to the valence and non-valence parts of the electromagnetic form factor holds only for the light-front plus component of the hadronic current but not for any other components of the hadronic current. This point was already addressed in our previous work
Choi et al. 2022
, noting that the decomposition of the form factor between the valence and nonvalence contributions depends on which component of the current is used for the calculation. Therefore, the correspondence between the DGLAP vs. ERBL regions and the valence vs. non-valence parts of the electromagnetic form factor should be understood with great care in taking the component of the hadronic current.

IV.4
Polynomiality of Higher Moments

In discussing the polynomiality of the second and higher Mellin moments of GPDs, it is advantageous to
introduce the symmetric variables
(X,\xi)
rather than
(x,\zeta)
. This notation renders the underlying
kinematic relations more transparent and facilitates a direct comparison with the conventional form of the polynomiality condition.

For this analysis, we follow the symmetric notation for the GPDs
Ji 1998
;
Polyakov and Weiss 1999
;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Broniowski and Arriola 2008

\xi=\frac{\zeta}{2-\zeta},\;X=\frac{x-\zeta/2}{1-\zeta/2},

(68)

where
0\leq\xi\leq 1
and the support is
-1\leq X\leq 1
. The GPD corresponding to
H(x,\zeta,t)
is then defined as

H(X,\xi,t)=H\left(\frac{\xi+X}{\xi+1},\frac{2\xi}{\xi+1},t\right).

(69)

Reflection about
X=0
yields the symmetric relation
H(X,\xi,t)=H(-X,\xi,t)
.

The polynomiality conditions
Ji 1998
;
Polyakov and Weiss 1999
;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Broniowski and Arriola 2008
;
Son and Hutauruk 2025
follow from the Lorentz invariance, time reversal, and Hermiticity.
In general, the polynomiality conditions for the moments of the GPDs, defined by

F^{(n)}(\xi,t)=\int^{1}_{-1}dXX^{n-1}H(X,\xi,t)=\sum_{m=0,{\rm even}}^{n}\xi^{m}A_{nm}(t),

(70)

require that the highest power of
\xi
in the polynomial expression of
F^{(n)}(\xi,t)
does not exceed
n

Ji et al. 1997
;
Radyushkin 1999
;
Choi et al. 2001
,
where
A_{nm}(t)
are the generalized form factors.
The first (
n=1
) moment corresponds to the EM form factor, i.e.
F^{(1)}(\xi,t)=F_{\rm EM}(-t)
, which is independent
\xi
.
The second (
n=2
) moment satisfies the
\xi
-dependent polynomiality relation

F^{(2)}(\xi,t)=A_{20}(t)+\xi^{2}A_{22}(t),

(71)

where

A_{20}(t)=F^{(2)}(0,t)
and
A_{22}(t)
correspond to the gravitational form factors (GFFs) of the scalar target.
In the following section, we explicitly demonstrate that our model satisfies the polynomiality condition of Eq. (
71
).

VI
CONCLUSION and Outlook

In this work, we investigated the beam spin asymmetry in the context of exclusive scalar meson production off a scalar target nucleus,
extending the previous (1+1)-dimensional model analysis
Choi et al. 2022
to the present (3+1)-dimensional model analysis.
This exploration of computing the BSA illuminated some of the “bare-bone” structure of quantum chromodynamics involved in the scattering process, providing valuable background information for QCD phenomenology.

Our primary finding is that the BSA is non-vanishing for scalar meson production. This result is significant because it contrasts with the prediction of the leading-twist GPD formulation, which suggests a vanishing BSA due to the reduction of the correlation amplitudes in the asymptotic region where the factorization of the GPD is attainable. Without the BH-Compton interference in the squared amplitude, the underlying hadronic correlation amplitudes could be accessed with minimal contamination. The discrepancy in predicting the BSA highlights the complementary nature of our work to the GPD formulation, indicating that the GPD formulation may not fully capture the complexities of the process.

By exploring different
-t/Q^{2}
regions, we could summarize the applicability of the leading-twist GPDs. Our results show that the leading-twist GPD formulation is valid for the Helium-4 target in 3+1D when
-t/Q^{2}
is on the order of
10^{-2}
and smaller. This finding has implications for the ongoing research at facilities like the Jefferson Lab 12 GeV electron beam and the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC), suggesting that the EIC may be better suited to access the forward angle kinematics necessary for the GPD formulation to be valid.

Building on this work, future research could explore the next-to-leading order GPD first moment, which is related to the gravitational form factor. By extending our model to include a derivative-type coupling, we could calculate the energy-momentum tensor and access the mass form factor A, spin form factor B, and the D form factor related to the deformation of the target. It would be particularly interesting to determine the sign of the D term under this extended model calculation, as it has implications for the stability of the proton.

Another potential avenue for future investigation is the inclusion of fermion loops in our calculations. This extension would allow us to explore not only the chiral-even leading-twist GPD but also the chiral-odd GPD. It would be also interesting to access the chiral-odd GPD investigating a derivative coupling at the meson production vertex. If the GPD prediction then provides a non-vanishing BSA, it would suggest a significant contribution from the chiral-odd GPD that overcomes the suppressing
\sqrt{-t}/Q
factor. Further extensions of our work could involve TMD computations and explorations of the light-front wavefunctions from the analyses of the light-front quark model.

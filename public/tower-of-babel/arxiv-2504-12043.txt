Interplay of spin-phonon coupling in FeSe with pressure and isoelectronic (S/Te) substitution
Abyay Ghosh, Piotr Chudzinski, Myrta Grüning

Abstract
In several studies, magnetism has been shown to strongly influence the properties of the iron chalcogenide compound FeSe . In our previous work [Phys. Rev. Res. 6(4), 043154 (2024)], we demonstrated how the interplay between spin and orbital degrees of freedom governs the electronic structure and consequently shapes the magnetic phase diagram of FeSe under external hydrostatic pressure. However, a comprehensive understanding of the phase diagram also requires investigating the role of lattice degrees of freedom (phonons) and their coupling to magnetism. In the present study, we examine how hydrostatic pressure and isoelectronic substitution affect the magnetic and lattice dynamical properties of FeSe using Density Functional Theory calculations. Our lattice dynamics analysis reveals that different phonon modes exhibit distinct dependencies on the local magnetic moment across various magnetic phases. We further identify pressure- and magnetic-moment-dependent dynamical instabilities in specific magnetic configurations, highlighting the intricate coupling between structural and magnetic degrees of freedom in FeSe . An investigation of spin-phonon coupling demonstrates the presence of strong magnetoelastic interactions affecting the prominent optical phonon modes: the Fe-derived B 1 ​ g B_{1g} mode and the Se-derived A 1 ​ g A_{1g} mode. Finally, we discuss the evolution of spin-phonon coupling under pressure and alloying in the context of its possible implications for emergent phases such as magnetism, nematicity, and superconductivity in FeSe .



I
Introduction

Materials with a layered quasi-two-dimensional structure often allow the presence of various strong energy coupling terms giving rise to macroscopic interplay between spin, orbital, and lattice degrees of freedom
[
19
,
33
]
. This in turn can be manifested as magneto-elastic coupling in the material and is central to the physics of iron-based superconductors
[
37
,
23
]
. Among them,
FeSe
stands out for exhibiting a structural (nematic) transition without long-range magnetic order, yet it remains far from being magnetically inert. Theoretical studies propose competing hidden magnetic orders at ambient pressure
[
14
,
26
,
8
,
5
]
, and neutron spin resonance experiments reveal both Néel- and stripe-type fluctuations
[
36
]
, underscoring the presence of dynamic magnetism
[
15
]
. The strong dependence of magnetic exchange energies on the local Fe moment
[
10
,
29
]
suggests a delicate feedback between magnetism, lattice, and electronic structure.
Understanding how these magnetic instabilities evolve under pressure or alloying, and how they feed back onto the structural degrees of freedom, is therefore key to unraveling the magnetoelastic interactions in
FeSe
. While density functional theory (DFT) has been instrumental in modeling iron-based superconductors
[
31
,
22
,
38
,
20
]
, systematic DFT investigations capturing the pressure- and alloying-dependence of phonons remain scarce in
FeSe
. Existing approaches present a trade-off: field-theory models like in Ref.
[
10
]
neglect lattice dynamics, while studies like in Ref.
[
18
]
analyze electron-phonon coupling purely from structural perspective without accounting for magnetism. Neither framework resolves how magnetic phases maintain dynamic stability or feed back into phonon behavior under pressure and alloying.

Both questions are addressed in this work. First, we inspect whether magnetic orders survive dynamic coupling with the lattice. Second, we analyse the coupling between spin-orbital and phonon fluctuations. We study how both the dynamic stability and spin-phonon coupling evolve with external hydrostatic pressure and isoelectronic substitution. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the interplay among orbital, spin, and lattice degrees of freedom, helping to rationalize the phase diagram of FeSe.

In Sec.
II
, we present the computational methodology of the calculations performed. In Sec.
III
, we study the phonon dispersion of
FeSe
with pressure (Sec.
III.1
), magnetic moment (Sec.
III.2
) and alloying (Sec.
III.3
)in different magnetic phases. We assess the stability of different magnetic phases at higher pressures consistently with the FeSe phase diagram. We observe a non-trivial dependence of phonon dispersion on the Fe magnetic moment with different spin configuration which hints toward strong spin-phonon coupling in this compound.
We thus provide a detailed analysis of the nature of spin-phonon coupling for the phonon modes
B_{1g}
and
A_{1g}
(Sec.
III.4
) and discuss its implications on the phase diagram (Sec.
III.5
).

II
Computational Details

The plane wave pseudopotential suite QUANTUM ESPRESSO
[
12
,
11
]
is used to perform fully self-consistent DFT-based electronic structure calculations by solving the standard Kohn-Sham (KS) equations. Ultrasoft pseudopotentials from the PSlibrary
[
7
]
are used for Fe, Se, S and Te atoms. Kinetic-energy cut-offs are fixed to 55 Ry for electronic wave functions after performing rigorous convergence tests.

The electronic exchange-correlation is treated under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) that is parametrized by Perdew-Burke-Enzerhof (PBE) functional
[
27
,
28
]
.
The phonon dispersion is calculated with the finite displacement method using PHONOPY
[
34
,
35
]
as well as Density Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT)
[
1
]
as implemented in QUANTUM ESPRESSO. A dense
q
-mesh grid of 3
\times
3
\times
3 is considered for the DFPT calculation.

The model considered to simulate different magnetic phases like ferromagnetic (FM), checkerboard antiferromagnetic (CAFM), striped antiferromagnetic (SAFM) and staggered dimer (SD) has been discussed in
[
9
]
in detail. We adopt the Monkhorst-Pack scheme
[
24
]
to sample the Brillouin zone in k-space with 8
\times
8
\times
8 grid. Geometry optimization has been performed using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfrab-Shanno (BFGS) scheme
[
3
]
. The experimental lattice parameters (
a,b
= 3.7698
\AA
,
c=
5.5163\AA
and
z_{{}\mathrm{Se}}
= 0.2576) are used as starting values. The Convergence threshold of
10^{-8}
and
10^{-5}
are used on total energy (a.u) and forces (a.u) respectively for ionic minimization. High-pressure structures are obtained by enthalpy (
H=U+PV
) minimization under externally applied hydrostatic pressure. Energy penalty functional is used to perform the constrained magnetic moment calculations. The penalty term is incorporated into total energy by weight
\lambda
as:
E_{\text{total}}=E_{\text{LSDA}}+\sum_{i}\lambda(M_{i}-M^{0}_{i})^{2}
, where
i
is the atomic index for Fe atoms and
M^{0}_{i}
,
M_{i}
are the targeted and actual local magnetic moment at atom
i
respectively. The value of
\lambda
is fixed to 25
Ry/\mu^{2}_{B}
after performing a convergence test, constraining the magnetic moment of Fe at a particular value. The relevant details are included in
[
9
]
. For the lattice dynamics calculation with S/Te isovalent substitution, conventional unit cell of FeSe is used where one Se atom is substituted with S/Te.

III.3
Effect of S/Te substitution on phonon modes

Figure 7:
Phonon band structure at 50% S/Te substitution at Se site for NM and SAFM phase.

The phonon dispersion with 50% of both S and Te substitution at Se site is presented in Fig.

Due to Te substitution, all the phonon modes shift towards lower frequency owing to larger atomic mass of Te. The magnetic moment-dependent renormalization of phonon dispersion in
FeSe
resembles that of FeSi, where strong spin-phonon coupling is evident
[
16
]

[
30
]
. In Ref.
[
21
]

\omega_{p}=\omega_{0}-gS_{z}^{2},

(1)

where
\omega_{0}
is the frequency in absence of spin-phonon coupling,
g
quantifies the spin-phonon coupling and the negative sign comes due to antiferromagnetic ordering. In Fig.
8

\omega
versus M with the model
\omega=a_{0}+a_{2}M^{2}
for the pure Fe
B_{1g}
and pure Se
A_{1g}
phonon modes of the SAFM phase. We consider this phase as it remains energetically and dynamically stable throughout the pressure and magnetic moment range. The calculated fitting parameters are listed in Tab.
3
together with the
R^{2}\rightarrow 1
value indicating how well the model fits the results. For comparison, the parameters and
R^{2}
are also reported for a linear model. Since,
a_{0}
provides the frequency at
M=0

\mu_{B}
, we compare its value with that of the phonon frequency in the NM mode (Tab.
1
). The values in brackets with
a_{0}
show the difference between the two. As

is extracted from energy variation of phonon modes with magnetic moment, it contains information about coupling of spin-orbital with phonon.

In Fig.

III.5
Discussions

Our results show that the nature of spin-phonon coupling evolves substantially under variations in pressure and alloying. Based on these results, we can infer the potential role of spin-phonon coupling in shaping the phase diagram of FeSe. At ambient pressure, when
FeSe
hosts nematic phase and lacks long-range order, spin-phonon coupling of both
A_{1g}
and
B_{1g}
modes is low and of comparable magnitude. In contrast, at higher pressures, when the striped long-range order develops into the system, spin-phonon coupling of the
B_{1g}
becomes stronger (about 1.5 to 2.5 times). Above 6 GPa, spin-phonon coupling is further enhanced and this may be the reason behind the suppression of striped phase. Therefore, It can be inferred that a moderate to strong spin-phonon coupling is necessary to host long range order. At 50% S-substitution case, an exceptionally high spin-phonon coupling for the
B_{1g}
mode may suppress both nematicity as well as stripe type order in
FeSe
. On the contrary, low
‘
g
’
for 50% Te-substitution case signals towards weak stripe type fluctuations which is confirmed by experiments
[
25
]
. Low
‘
g
’
also implies persistence of nematic fluctuations even at high Te-concentration in
FeSe
as reflected in the phase diagram. Together, these results demonstrate that both
z_{{}\mathrm{Se}}
and the lattice parameters play a key role in determining magneto-elastic coupling which in turn influences the superconducting property of
FeSe
, similar to what is observed in iron pnictide compounds
[
17
]
.

Regarding the linear dependence of the
B_{1g}
mode frequency with
M
at ambient pressure, this can be rationalised by the field-theory model of two-dimensional spin and orbital fluctuations we put forward in Ref.
[
10
,
6
]
. Indeed, the linear dependency may be a manifestation of the degeneracy of two bosonic modes: the lattice phonon and orbital fluctuation. As the system approaches dispersion crossing, interconversion is allowed (by momentum-energy conservation) and bosons hybridize into a mixed particle
[
30
]
. As an evidence to support this hypothesis, in Ref.
[
10
]
we found that at ambient pressure and low magnetization, the energy difference between the lowest phase (FM) and the CAFM/SAFM phases is
\approx 30
meV. At higher pressures this energy difference decreases. According to the model, the energy difference between phases corresponds roughly to the energy of the orbital fluctuation, which would be indeed close in energy and mix with the lattice phonon at ambient pressure. At higher pressure instead, the energy of the two bosonic modes is substantially different with no mixing occurring. The absence of mixing at higher pressure, would contribute to stabilize the orbital order, in agreement with Ref.
[
10
]
.

IV
Conclusions

With the exploration of magnetism-dependent phononic band structure, we gained insight into the interplay between magnetism and lattice dynamics in FeSe instrumental to rationalize the FeSe phase diagram. Phonon calculations show that most energetically competitive magnetic phases are dynamically unstable even at ambient pressure, except for the stripe antiferromagnetic phase. This behavior is consistent with the experimentally observed evolution from mixed stripe-Néel-type fluctuations at ambient pressure to stripe-type order at higher pressures
[
32
]
. Our analysis also reveals a pronounced pressure and alloying tunable spin–phonon coupling. Changes in structural parameters, particularly the Se height, modify magnetic interactions and thereby alter both the nature and magnitude of spin-phonon coupling for the
A_{1g}
and
B_{1g}
phonon modes. Finally, with our results we can interpret the evolution of magnetic phases with pressure and alloying in terms of the coupling between the spin and phonon degrees of freedom. Specifically, long-range magnetic order appears for moderate-to-strong spin–phonon coupling, while it is suppressed for either too low or too high coupling.

The local well-posedness, blow-up phenomena and ill-posedness of a new fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation
Yiyao Lian, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we study a new fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation derived by Li [ 22 ] . We firstly establish the local well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard for the Cauchy problem of the new fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation in Besov spaces. Secondly, we obtain blow-up criteria. Building upon this, by utilizing the conservation laws and establishing local boundedness, we derive a blow-up result that precisely determines the blow-up time. Finally, the ill-posedness of the new fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation in the critical Sobolev space H 1 2 H^{\frac{1}{2}} is established via a norm inflation argument. Keywords : Fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation; Besov spaces; Local well-posedness; Blow-up; Ill-posedness.



1
Introduction

The Camassa-Holm (CH) equation

u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}

is a profoundly significant nonlinear partial differential equation in the fields of fluid dynamics and integrable systems, which has attracted increasing attention in recent years. The CH equation was first introduced in the context of hereditary symmetries by Fuchssteiner and Fokas
[
17
]
in 1981. It was later explicitly derived as a model for shallow water waves by Camassa and Holm
[
3
]
in 1993. This equation possesses a rich mathematical structure. Notably, it is completely integrable
[
3
,
5
,
10
]
, admits a bi-Hamiltonian formulation
[
4
,
17
]
, and possesses peaked soliton solutions (peakons) of the form
ce^{-|x-ct|}

[
3
]
. These solutions describe traveling waves of largest amplitude
[
6
,
11
]
and possess orbital stability
[
11
]
. In addition, the CH equation can characterize wave-breaking phenomenon
[
2
,
7
,
30
]
. Furthermore, local well-posedness of CH equation in Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces has been shown in
[
8
,
12
,
13
,
20
]
.

We note that the nonlinearity in the Camassa–Holm equation is quadratic. Recently, Camassa-Holm type equations with cubic nonlinearity have attracted considerable attention. One particularly renowned Camassa-Holm type equation with cubic nonlinearity is the modified Camassa-Holm (mCH) equation, also known as the Fokas–Olver–Rosenau–Qiao (FORQ) equation
[
14
,
16
,
26
,
27
]

m_{t}+\left[(u^{2}-u_{x}^{2})m\right]_{x}=0,\quad m=u-u_{xx}.

It was shown in
[
15
]
that the mCH equation is locally well-posed in the Sobolev space
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{5}{2}
. Additionally, the authors of
[
15
,
18
]
investigated its local well-posedness in Besov spaces and analyzed its blow-up phenomena. Moreover, it was established in
[
18
]
that the following Hamiltonian functionals are conserved for strong solutions
u(t,x)
of the mCH equation on
\mathbb{R}
:

H_{1}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)dx,\quad H_{2}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{4}+2u^{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{4}\right)dx.

In recent years, there has also been growing interest in higher-order Camassa-Holm type equations. A representative example is the fifth-order Camassa-Holm (FOCH) equation introduced by Liu and Qiao
[
23
]

m_{t}+m_{x}u+bmu_{x}=0,\quad m=(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2})(1-\beta^{2}\partial_{x}^{2})u,\quad\alpha\neq\beta,\,\alpha\beta\neq 0

with any constant
b
and two arbitrary real constants
\alpha,\beta
. For the case
\alpha=\beta=1
and
b=2
, McLachlan and Zhang
[
24
]
established local well-posedness in
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{7}{2}
. Subsequently, Tang and Liu
[
28
]
proved this result in the critical Besov space
B^{7/2}_{2,1}
, and in
B^{s}_{p,r}
for
1\leq p,r\leq+\infty
with
s>\max\{3+\frac{1}{p},\frac{7}{2}\}
, while also demonstrating ill-posedness in
B^{7/2}_{2,\infty}
. For the case
b=2
with
m=u-u_{xx}+u_{xxxx}
, local well-posedness was shown to hold in
H^{s}
for
s>\frac{9}{2}
in
[
29
]
. For the general case, the local and global existence of solutions for the FOCH model were established in
[
31
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the Cauchy problem for a new fifth-order Camassa-Holm type equation derived by Li
[
22
]
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{t}-2u_{xxt}&+u_{xxxxt}+\left(2u^{3}+uu_{x}^{2}+u^{2}u_{xx}-18u_{x}^{2}u_{xx}+3uu_{xx}^{2}\right.\\
&+\left.4uu_{x}u_{xxx}+u^{2}u_{xxxx}+u_{xx}^{3}+4u_{x}u_{xx}u_{xxx}+u_{x}^{2}u_{xxxx}\right)_{x}=0,\end{split}

which is characterized by both cubic nonlinearities and higher-order dispersion. Specifically, drawing inspiration from the mCH equation, Li derived (
1.1
)
by leveraging conserved quantities. From Li
[
22
]
, equation (
1.1
) admits two fundamental invariants, denoted as

E(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(u^{2}+2u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}^{2}\Big)dx

and

F(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(u^{4}-u^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{10}{3}u_{x}^{4}+u^{2}u_{xx}^{2}+u_{x}^{2}u_{xx}^{2}\Big)dx,

which will play a crucial role in the analysis of the blow-up phenomena presented in this work. Moreover, Li proved that the equation admits pseudo peakons and that these pseudo peakons are orbitally stable with respect to small perturbations in the energy space. Compared with the mCH equation and the FOCH equation, the equation under consideration still has numerous properties worthy of investigation. Our primary focus will be on analyzing the local well-posedness, blow-up phenomena and ill-posedness of this equation in Besov spaces.

We now present several equivalent forms of equation (
1.1
), which will facilitate the proof of subsequent theorems. By setting
P(D)=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-2},~P_{1}(D)=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
, (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}n_{t}+(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})n_{x}=G(u),\\
n=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
G(u)=P_{1}(D)G_{1}(u)+\partial_{x}P_{1}(D)G_{2}(u)+\partial_{x}^{2}P_{1}(D)G_{3}(u)
, with

G_{1}(u)=2u_{x}u_{xx}^{2},~G_{2}(u)=-\frac{5}{3}u^{3}-2uu_{x}^{2}-3u^{2}u_{xx}+16u_{x}^{2}u_{xx}-uu_{xx}^{2},~G_{3}(u)=-u_{x}u_{xx}^{2}-2uu_{x}u_{xx}.

After some transformations, (
1.1
) can also be changed into a transport-like equation

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(u^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2})u_{x}=P(D)F_{1}(u)+\partial_{x}P(D)F_{2}(u)+\partial_{x}^{2}P(D)F_{3}(u),\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

with

F_{1}(u)=\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{3},~F_{2}(u)=-\frac{5}{3}u^{3}-5uu_{x}^{2}-3u^{2}u_{xx}+24u_{x}^{2}u_{xx}-uu_{xx}^{2},~F_{3}(u)=u_{x}u_{xx}^{2}+4uu_{x}u_{xx}.

This paper firstly investigates the local well-posedness for the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) (see Theorem
3.1
). The proof of the local well-posedness is inspired by the argument of approximate solutions by
[
1
]
. However, in this paper, we apply Moser-type inequalities to obtain refined estimates.
Having established the local well-posedness of solutions to equation (
1.2
), we first derive a blow-up criterion of the solution to (
1.2
) (see Theorem
4.2
). Furthermore, under the additional assumption that
u_{0}\in H^{2}
, we derive a corollary to this blow-up criterion (see Corollary
4.3
).
Subsequently, the blow-up analysis for equation (
1.2
) becomes particularly challenging due to its higher-order nonlinearities and the lack of a sign-preservation property. Nevertheless, a crucial tool remains at our disposal: the conservation laws. By utilizing the conserved quantity
E(u)
and establishing the local boundedness of
u_{x}
via the Lagrangian coordinate transformation, we establish a blow-up result that characterizes the blow-up time (see Theorem
4.4
). For the ill-posedness analysis, we prove the phenomenon of norm inflation by constructing suitable initial data, which implies the ill-posedness of (
1.2
) in the Sobolev space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
(see Theorem
5.1
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some preliminaries which will be used in the sequel. In Section 3, we establish the local well-posedness of (
1.2
) in
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
\{1\leq p,r\leq\infty,s>\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p})\}
by the Littlewood–Paley theory and transport equations theory. In Section 4, we present the corresponding blow-up criteria for equation (
1.2
). Subsequently, we derive a blow-up result that exactly determines the blow-up time. In Section 5, the ill-posedness of equation (
1.2
) in the Sobolev space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
is established through a proof of norm inflation.

C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
.

3
Local well-posedness

In this section, we obtain the local well-posedness of equation (
1.2
) with data in Besov spaces. Before starting our local existence result, we introduce the following function spaces:

\displaystyle E_{p,r}^{s}(T)\triangleq\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s-1}),\quad\mathrm{if}~r<\infty,

\displaystyle E_{p,\infty}^{s}(T)\triangleq\mathcal{C}_{w}([0,T];B_{p,\infty}^{s})\cap C^{0,1}([0,T];B_{p,\infty}^{s-1}),

with
T>0,~s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
.

Our main theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p,r\leq\infty,~s>\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p})
and
n_{0}\triangleq(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u_{0}
be in
B^{s}_{p,r}
. Then there exists a time
T>0
such that (
1.2
) has a unique solution
n
in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
. Moreover, the solution
n
depends continuously on the initial data
n_{0}
.

Proof.

Now we prove Theorem
3.1
in five steps.

Step 1: Constructing Approximate Solutions

Starting from
u^{0}\triangleq 0
, we define by induction a sequence
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of smooth functions by solving the following linear transport equation:

(3.1)

(T_{k})\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}n_{t}^{k+1}+((u^{k})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k})^{2})n^{k+1}_{x}=G(u^{k}),\\
u^{k+1}=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}n^{k+1},\\
u^{k+1}|_{t=0}=S_{k+1}u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

By induction, we assume that
n^{k}\in E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
for all positive
T
. For the right side of (
3.1
), the following estimates can be derived by using Lemma
2.5
,
B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
and Lemma
2.7
.

\displaystyle\|G(u^{k})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle=\|P_{1}(D)G_{1}(u^{k})+\partial_{x}P_{1}(D)G_{2}(u^{k})+\partial_{x}^{2}P_{1}(D)G_{3}(u^{k})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|2u_{x}^{k}(u_{xx}^{k})^{2}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|-\frac{5}{3}(u^{k})^{3}-2u^{k}(u_{x}^{k})^{2}-3(u^{k})^{2}u^{k}_{xx}+16(u_{x}^{k})^{2}u^{k}_{xx}-u^{k}(u_{xx}^{k})^{2}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle~~~+\|-u^{k}_{x}(u^{k}_{xx})^{2}-2u^{k}u^{k}_{x}u^{k}_{xx}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\Big)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{k}\|^{3}_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

To apply Lemma
2.8
, we first verify that its hypotheses hold.

\|n_{0}^{k+1}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\|S_{k+1}n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C\|n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

We then deduce that
n_{0}^{k+1}\in B^{s}_{p,r}.

From the previous computation, we have
G(u^{k})\in L^{1}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. At the same time, applying Bernstein’s inequalities in
[
1
]
and Lemma
2.7
followed by a simple calculation shows that
(u^{k})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k})^{2}\in L^{\rho}([0,T];B^{-M}_{\infty,\infty})
for some
\rho>1
and
M>0
.
By virtue of Lemma
2.7
and the fact that
B^{s}_{p,r}
is an algebra, we then have

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla((u^{k})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k})^{2})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\|2u^{k}u_{x}^{k}+2u_{x}^{k}u_{xx}^{k}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau\leq C\int^{t}_{0}\|n^{k}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau.

Since
B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}\cap L^{\infty},~B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow B^{s-1}_{p,r}
and the previous process, we can use Lemma
2.8
. Making use of Lemma
2.8
ensures that (
3.1
) has a unique solution
n^{k+1}
in

-the space
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, if
r<\infty
;

-the space
\Big(\bigcap_{s^{\prime}<s}C([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,\infty})\Big)\bigcap C_{w}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,\infty})
, if
r=\infty
.

In the case where
r
is finite, we still have to prove that
n_{t}^{k+1}\in C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}).
We easily deduce that
G(u^{k})\in C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
and
((u^{k})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k})^{2})n^{k+1}_{x}\in C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
by Lemma
2.7
. From the equation (
3.1
), we have
n_{t}^{k+1}\in C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}).
When
r=\infty
, it suffices to prove
n_{t}^{k+1}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}).
The proof for the case
r=\infty
is similar to the case
r<\infty
, so we omit the details here. We then can conclude that
n^{k+1}\in E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
for all positive
T
.

Step 2: Uniform Bounds

We define
N^{k}(t)\triangleq\int^{t}_{0}\|n^{k}(\tau)\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau
. According to
Step 1
and Lemma
2.9
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following inequality,

\displaystyle\|n^{k+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{CN^{k}(t)}\Big(\|n^{k+1}_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-CN^{k}(\tau)}\|G^{k}(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau\Big)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CN^{k}(t)}\Big(\|n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-CN^{k}(\tau)}\|n^{k}(\tau)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}^{3}d\tau\Big).

Without loss of generality, we may assume
C>1
. We fix a
T>0
such that
4C^{3}T\|n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}^{2}<1
and suppose that

(3.4)

\|n^{k}(t)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}\leq\frac{C\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}}{\sqrt{1-4C^{3}t\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2}}},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

We thus get

(3.5)

e^{CN^{k}(t)}\leq e^{C^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2}(1-4C^{3}\tau\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{-1}d\tau}=(1-4C^{3}t\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}},

and

(3.6)

e^{CN^{k}(t)-CN^{k}(\tau)}\leq(1-4C^{3}t\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}(1-4C^{3}\tau\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Plugging (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) into (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\|n^{k+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1-4C^{3}t\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\|n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{C^{3}\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{3}}{(1-4C^{3}\tau\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{\frac{5}{4}}}d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1-4C^{3}t\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|n_{0}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}.

By induction, the sequence
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. This clearly entails that
(G(u^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(((u^{k})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k})^{2})n^{k}_{x})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
by the first step. According to the equation (
3.1
), we can conclude that
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

Step 3: Convergence and Regularity

We claim that
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Indeed, for all
k,p\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
w^{k,p}=n^{k+p}-n^{k}
. By the system
(T_{k+p})
and
(T_{k})
,
w^{k+1,p}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w_{t}^{k+1,p}+((u^{k+p})^{2}+(u_{x}^{k+p})^{2})w^{k+1,p}_{x}=G(u^{k+p})-G(u^{k})-(u^{k}+u^{k+p})(u^{k+p}-u^{k})n^{k+1}_{x}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\quad~-(u^{k}_{x}+u^{k+p}_{x})(u^{k+p}_{x}-u^{k}_{x})n^{k+1}_{x},\\
w^{k+1,p}|_{t=0}=S_{k+p+1}n_{0}-S_{k+1}n_{0},\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle G(u^{k+p})-G(u^{k})

\displaystyle=P_{1}(D)(G_{1}(u^{k+p})-G_{1}(u^{k}))+\partial_{x}P_{1}(D)(G_{2}(u^{k+p})-G_{2}(u^{k}))

\displaystyle~~~+\partial_{x}^{2}P_{1}(D)(G_{3}(u^{k+p})-G_{3}(u^{k})).

We now estimate the right-hand side of (
3
). Denote

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\triangleq\|G(u^{k+p})-G(u^{k})-(u^{k}+u^{k+p})(u^{k+p}-u^{k})n^{k+1}_{x}-(u^{k}_{x}+u^{k+p}_{x})(u^{k+p}_{x}-u^{k}_{x})n^{k+1}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}I_{1}\triangleq\|G(u^{k+p})-G(u^{k})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},\\
I_{2}\triangleq\|-(u^{k}+u^{k+p})(u^{k+p}-u^{k})n^{k+1}_{x}-(u^{k}_{x}+u^{k+p}_{x})(u^{k+p}_{x}-u^{k}_{x})n^{k+1}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.\end{array}

Bounds for
I_{1}
.
By virtue of Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.7
, using the fact that
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has uniform bounds in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|G_{1}(u^{k+p})-G_{1}(u^{k})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|G_{2}(u^{k+p})-G_{2}(u^{k})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|G_{3}(u^{k+p})-G_{3}(u^{k})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|2u_{x}^{k+p}(u_{xx}^{k+p})^{2}-2u_{x}^{k}(u_{xx}^{k})^{2}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|(-\frac{5}{3}(u^{k+p})^{3}-2u^{k+p}(u_{x}^{k+p})^{2}-3(u^{k+p})^{2}u^{k+p}_{xx}+16(u_{x}^{k+p})^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~\times u^{k+p}_{xx}-u^{k+p}(u_{xx}^{k+p})^{2})-(-\frac{5}{3}(u^{k})^{3}-2u^{k}(u_{x}^{k})^{2}-3(u^{k})^{2}u^{k}_{xx}+16(u_{x}^{k})^{2}u^{k}_{xx}-u^{k}(u_{xx}^{k})^{2})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\|u^{k+p}_{x}(u^{k+p}_{xx})^{2}+2u^{k+p}u^{k+p}_{x}u^{k+p}_{xx}-u^{k}_{x}(u^{k}_{xx})^{2}-2u^{k}u^{k}_{x}u^{k}_{xx}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\Big)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{k+p}-n^{k}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

Bounds for
I_{2}
.

Similar arguments lead to

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{k}+u^{k+p}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|u^{k+p}-u^{k}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|n_{x}^{k+1}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|u^{k}_{x}+u^{k+p}_{x}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|u^{k+p}_{x}-u^{k}_{x}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|n_{x}^{k+1}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{k+p}-n^{k}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

From (
3
) and (
3
), the right-hand side of the system (
3
) admits the following estimate:

I\leq C\|n^{k+p}-n^{k}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

Next, if
r<\infty
, we derive the estimate for the initial data in (
3
):

\displaystyle\|S_{k+p+1}n_{0}-S_{k+1}n_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{j^{\prime}=k+1}^{k+p}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}n_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{l\geq-1}2^{l(s-1)r}\|\Delta_{l}\sum_{j^{\prime}=k+1}^{k+p}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}n_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{r}\Big)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{l=k}^{k+p+1}2^{-lr}2^{lsr}\|\Delta_{l}n_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{r}\Big)^{1/r}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\|n_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\Big(\sum_{l=k}^{k+p+1}2^{-lr}\Big)^{1/r}\leq C2^{-k}.

The corresponding results for
r=\infty
require only trivial modifications to the argument. By virtue of Lemma
2.10
and using the fact that
(m^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
,
we infer that

\displaystyle\|w^{k+1,p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\|S_{k+p+1}n_{0}-S_{k+1}n_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|n^{k+p}-n^{k}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(2^{-k}+\int_{0}^{t}\|w^{k,p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

Arguing by induction, for any
t\in[0,T],
we get,

\displaystyle\|w^{k+1,p}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(2^{-k}+\int_{0}^{t}C_{T}(2^{-(k-1)}+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|w^{k-1,p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{2})dt_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}2^{-k}+C_{T}^{2}2^{-k+1}t+\cdots+C_{T}^{k+1}\frac{1}{k!}2^{-k+k}t^{k}

\displaystyle~~~+C_{T}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\|w^{0,p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{k+1}\cdots dt_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(2C_{T}T)^{l}}{l!}2^{-k}+C_{T}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\|n^{p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{k+1}\cdots dt_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(2C_{T}T)^{l}}{l!}2^{-k}+\frac{1}{(k+1)!}(C_{T}T)^{k+1}\Big)

which leads to
\|w^{k+1,p}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
and converges to some limit function
n\in C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
.
Since
(n^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, the Fatou property for Besov spaces guarantees that
n\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. Passing to the limit in (
3.1
), we easily deduce that
n
is a solution of (
1.2
). Thanks to Lemma
2.8
and the equation (
1.2
), we conclude that
n\in E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

Step 4: Uniqueness.

Let
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
be two solutions in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
of (
1.2
) with initial data
n_{10},n_{20}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
. In order to show that these two solutions coincide, we shall give estimates for
w\triangleq n_{1}-n_{2}
. Straightforward calculations shows that
w
satisfies the following system:

(3.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w_{t}+(u_{1}^{2}+u_{1x}^{2})w_{x}=-(u_{1}^{2}+u_{1x}^{2})n_{2x}+(u_{2}^{2}+u_{2x}^{2})n_{2x}+G(u_{1})-G(u_{2}),\\
u_{1}=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}n_{1},\quad u_{2}=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}n_{2},\\
w|_{t=0}=n_{10}-n_{20}.\end{array}\right.

By repeating the computational procedure from
Step 3
, we derive

\|w(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C_{T}\Big(\|n_{10}-n_{20}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|w(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

Thanks to Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that

(3.15)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|w(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C\|n_{10}-n_{20}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

which completes the proof of uniqueness.

Step 5: Continuous Dependence.

Denote
\bar{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. First, we consider the case where
r<\infty
. For
k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}
, let us suppose
n^{k}\in C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
is the solution of (
1.2
) with initial data
n^{k}_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
where
k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}.
Now we claim that if
n_{0}^{k}\rightarrow n_{0}^{\infty}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, then
n^{k}\rightarrow n^{\infty}
in
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with the lifespan
T
satisfying
4C^{3}T\sup_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}\|n_{0}^{k}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}^{2}<1
.

Indeed, split
n^{k}
into
y^{k}+z^{k}
with
(y^{k},z^{k})
satisfying

(3.16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y^{k}_{t}+((u^{k})^{2}+(u^{k}_{x})^{2})y^{k}_{x}=G(u^{\infty}),\\
y^{k}|_{t=0}=n^{\infty}_{0},\end{array}\right.\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z^{k}_{t}+((u^{k})^{2}+(u^{k}_{x})^{2})z^{k}_{x}=G(u^{k})-G(u^{\infty}),\\
z^{k}|_{t=0}=n^{k}_{0}-n^{\infty}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Let

H=\frac{C\sup_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}\|n_{0}^{k}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{\sqrt{1-4C^{3}T(\sup_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}\|n_{0}^{k}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}})^{2}}}.

According to the proof of the existence, we have

\|n^{k}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq H,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],~\forall k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}.

Hence,
(n^{k})_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}
is uniformly bounded in
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
and

\sup_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}\|(u^{k})^{2}+(u^{k}_{x})^{2}\|_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\leq C\sup_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}\|n^{k}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq CH^{2}.

Moreover, applying (
3.15
) together with
n_{0}^{k}\rightarrow n_{0}^{\infty}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
implies that

(3.17)

n^{k}\rightarrow n^{\infty}~\text{in}~C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})~\text{as}~k\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, owing to the uniform bounds of
(n^{k})_{k\in\bar{\mathbb{N}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|(u^{k})^{2}+(u^{k}_{x})^{2}-(u^{\infty})^{2}-(u_{x}^{\infty})^{2}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{k}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|u^{k}+u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|u^{k}_{x}-u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|u^{k}_{x}+u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq CH\|n^{k}-n^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

which implies that
(u^{k})^{2}+(u^{k}_{x})^{2}\rightarrow(u^{\infty})^{2}+(u_{x}^{\infty})^{2}
in
L^{1}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
.
Therefore, applying Lemma
2.12
, we have
y^{k}\rightarrow y^{\infty}
in
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
To control
z^{k}
, we need to estimate
G(u^{k})-G(u^{\infty})
. Similar to the estimates for
I_{1}
in
Step 3
and the application of Lemma
2.7
, we derive

\|G(u^{k})-G(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq CH^{2}\|n^{k}-n^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

Applying Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.9
, we obtain

(3.18)

\|z^{k}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq Ce^{CH^{2}t}\Big(\|n_{0}^{k}-n_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(n^{k}-n^{\infty})(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

Since
z^{\infty}=0
and
y^{k}\rightarrow y^{\infty}
in
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\bar{N_{1}}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|(y^{k}-n^{\infty})(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[0,T]

holds for all
k>\bar{N_{1}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|(n^{k}-n^{\infty})(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|z^{k}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|(y^{k}-n^{\infty})(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CH^{2}t}\Big(\|n_{0}^{k}-n_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(n^{k}-n^{\infty})(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big)+\epsilon.

The Gronwall inequality implies that

(3.19)

\|(n^{k}-n^{\infty})(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C(\epsilon+\|n_{0}^{k}-n_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Hence, we gain the continuity of (
1.2
) in
C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with respect to the initial data in
B^{s}_{p,r}
for
r<\infty
.

In the case
r=\infty
, we note that according to (
3.15
) if
n_{0}^{k}\rightarrow n_{0}^{\infty}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain
n^{k}\rightarrow n^{\infty}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Then, for fixed
\varphi\in B^{-s}_{p^{\prime},1}
we write

\displaystyle\langle n^{k}-n^{\infty},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{j}(n^{k}-n^{\infty}),\varphi\rangle+\langle(Id-S_{j})(n^{k}-n^{\infty}),\varphi\rangle

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\langle n^{k}-n^{\infty},S_{j}\varphi\rangle+\langle n^{k}-n^{\infty},(Id-S_{j})\varphi\rangle.

By duality (see Proposition
2.6
), we have

(3.21)

|u_{xx}|
, setting
\epsilon=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{55\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{3}}
, we have

(4.29)

|u_{xx}|=\frac{|q|}{|u_{x}|}\leq\epsilon q^{2}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon|u_{x}|^{2}}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{55\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{3}}q^{2}+\frac{55\sqrt{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{3}}{16|u_{x}|^{2}}.

Plugging (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) into (
4
) yields

q_{t}+q_{x}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})\leq-\frac{1}{4}q^{2}-2u_{x}^{4}+\frac{203}{6}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}+\frac{3025}{16}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{6}\frac{1}{u_{x}^{2}},

which implies that in Lagrangian coordinates, we have

(4.30)

q_{t}(t,y(t,x_{0}))\leq-\frac{1}{4}(q(t,y(t,x_{0})))^{2}-2(u_{x}(t,y(t,x_{0})))^{4}+\frac{203}{6}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}+\frac{3025}{16}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{6}\frac{1}{(u_{x}(t,y(t,x_{0})))^{2}},

where the flow map
y(t,x_{0})
satisfies (
2.2
) at
x=x_{0}
.

Under the condition (
4.14
), we derive
|u_{x}(t,y(t,x_{0}))|\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
. Indeed, using (
4.15
), we get

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}(u_{x}(t,y(t,x_{0})))|

\displaystyle=|-2(uu_{x}^{2})(t,y(t,x_{0}))+(\partial_{x}P(D)F_{1}+\partial_{x}^{2}P(D)F_{2}+\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)F_{3})(t,y(t,x_{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}P(D)F_{1}+\partial_{x}^{2}P(D)F_{2}+\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)F_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}}

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq 16\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{3}.

Since
u_{x}(t,y(t,x))=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\tau}(u_{x}(\tau,y(\tau,x)))d\tau+u_{0x}(x)
, we thus have, for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]

\displaystyle|u_{x}(t,y(t,x_{0}))|

\displaystyle\geq|u_{0x}(x_{0})|-|\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\tau}(u_{x}(\tau,y(\tau,x)))d\tau|

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{\tau}(u_{x}(\tau,y(\tau,x)))|d\tau

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}-16\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{3}T_{1}

(4.32)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}.

Inserting the above inequality into (
4.30
), we obtain

\displaystyle q_{t}(t,y(t,x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}(q(t,y(t,x_{0})))^{2}-\frac{1}{8}C_{0}^{4}+\frac{203}{6}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}+\frac{3025}{4C_{0}^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{6}

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}(q(t,y(t,x_{0})))^{2}+K.

Owing to the condition (
4.14
),
-\frac{1}{4}q_{0}^{2}(x_{0})+K<0
holds.
We can easily deduce that
q(t,y(t,x_{0}))<-2\sqrt{K}
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
by (
4
).

Consider the ordinary differential equation

f^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{4}f^{2}(t)+K,~f(0)=q_{0}(x_{0})<-2\sqrt{K},

then we have

\displaystyle q(t,y(t,x_{0}))\leq f(t)=\frac{2\sqrt{K}(1-Ce^{-\sqrt{K}t})}{1+Ce^{-\sqrt{K}t}}=-2\sqrt{K}+\frac{4\sqrt{K}}{1+Ce^{-\sqrt{K}t}}

with
C=\frac{2\sqrt{K}-q_{0}(x_{0})}{2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0})}<0.

Observing that
t\mapsto 1+Ce^{-\sqrt{K}t}
is an increasing function and

1+Ce^{-\sqrt{K}t}\Big|_{t=0}=1+C=\frac{4\sqrt{K}}{2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0})}<0,~1+Ce^{-\sqrt{K}T_{2}}=0

we infer that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{2}^{-}}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(u_{x}(x,t)u_{xx}(x,t))\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{2}^{-}}q(t,y(t,x_{0}))=-\infty.

Finally, since

T_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\ln\frac{-2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0})}{2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\ln(1+\frac{4\sqrt{K}}{-(2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0}))})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\frac{4\sqrt{K}}{-(2\sqrt{K}+q_{0}(x_{0}))}\leq T_{1},

the proof is complete.

∎

5
Ill-posedness

In this section, we will establish the ill-posedness of equation (
1.2
) in the critical Sobolev space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
by proving the occurrence of norm inflation. Our ill-posedness result is stated as follows:

Theorem 5.1
.

For any sufficiently large positive integer
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists an initial data
n_{0}^{N}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that the following conclusions hold:

(1)

\|n_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ln N}
;

(2)

There exists a unique solution
n^{N}\in C([0,T);H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
to equation (
1.2
) with initial data
n_{0}^{N}
, and the maximal lifespan
T^{*}
satisfying
T^{*}<N^{-\frac{1}{6}}
;

(3)

\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|n^{N}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\gtrsim\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|n^{N}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}=+\infty.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is an even function with
\mathrm{Supp}\ \psi\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{4}{3}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}\}
and values in
[0,1]
. Let
g\in\mathcal{S}
be an odd function such that

\frac{g^{\prime}(0)}{1+\|g\|_{H^{2}}}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{4},

where the constant
C_{0}
will be specified later. Define

u_{0}^{N}(x)=\frac{1}{\ln N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{3j}j^{\frac{2}{3}}}f_{j}(x)+\frac{1}{\ln N}g(x),

with
\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi)=\psi(2^{-j}\xi)
. A straightforward computation shows that
n_{0}^{N}=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u_{0}^{N}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and that
f_{j}
is an even function satisfying
\Delta_{l}f_{j}=\delta_{lj}f_{j}
for all
l\leq-1
, where
\delta_{lj}
denotes the Kronecker delta. This implies that, for
1\leq l\leq N
,

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{l}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{\ln N}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\varphi(2^{-l}\xi)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{3j}j^{\frac{2}{3}}}\psi(2^{-j}\xi)\Big)+\frac{1}{\ln N}\Delta_{l}g\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{f_{l}}{2^{3l}l^{\frac{2}{3}}\ln N}+\frac{1}{\ln N}\Delta_{l}g\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2^{\frac{5l}{2}}l^{\frac{2}{3}}\ln N}+\frac{1}{\ln N}\|\Delta_{l}g\|_{L^{2}}.

We thus deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{B^{2}_{2,2}}=C\Big(\sum_{l\geq-1}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\ln N}\Big(\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{l}l^{\frac{4}{3}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|g\|_{H^{2}}\Big)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\ln N}\Big(1+\|g\|_{H^{2}}\Big),

where the constant
C_{0}
depends only on
d
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}
. At the same time, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{B^{\frac{5}{2}}_{2,2}}=\Big(\sum_{l\geq-1}2^{5l}\|\Delta_{l}u_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ln N}\Big(\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{l^{\frac{4}{3}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|g\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\ln N},

which implies that

(5.2)

\|n_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\|(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{B^{\frac{5}{2}}_{2,2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ln N}.

Now, we proceed to calculate
u_{0x}^{N}
and
u_{0xx}^{N}
at
x=0
. According to (
5
), we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle u_{0x}^{N}(0)=\frac{1}{\ln N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{3j}j^{\frac{2}{3}}}f_{j}^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{\ln N}g^{\prime}(0)\geq\frac{C_{0}}{4\ln N}\Big(1+\|g\|_{H^{2}}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|u_{0}^{N}\|_{H^{2}}

and

\displaystyle u_{0xx}^{N}(0)=\frac{1}{\ln N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{3j}j^{\frac{2}{3}}}f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{\ln N}g^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{3j}j^{\frac{2}{3}}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-\xi^{2}\psi(2^{-j}\xi))(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{C}{\ln N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{j^{\frac{2}{3}}}

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{N^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\ln N},

which yield

(5.4)

\displaystyle u_{0x}^{N}(0)u_{0xx}^{N}(0)\sim-\frac{N^{\frac{1}{3}}}{(\ln N)^{2}}\rightarrow-\infty,\quad\text{as}~N\rightarrow\infty.

Since
n_{0}^{N}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, Theorem
3.1
yields that there exists a unique solution
n^{N}\in C([0,T);H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
to equation (
1.2
) with initial data
n_{0}^{N}
. Subsequently, by virtue of (
5
), (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) and applying Theorem
4.4
, we can conclude that for sufficiently large

N
, the maximal lifespan
T^{*}
of
n^{N}
satisfies

\displaystyle T^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\ln\frac{-2\sqrt{K}+u^{N}_{0x}(0)u^{N}_{0xx}(0)}{2\sqrt{K}+u^{N}_{0x}(0)u^{N}_{0xx}(0)}\sim(\ln N)^{2}\ln\frac{N^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{1}{\ln N}}{N^{\frac{1}{3}}-\frac{1}{\ln N}}\sim\frac{\ln N}{N^{\frac{1}{3}}}<N^{-\frac{1}{6}}.

Finally, thanks to Corollary
4.3
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\|n^{N}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\gtrsim\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\|n^{N}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}=+\infty.

This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

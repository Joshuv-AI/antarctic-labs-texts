Global trends in e-cigarette regulatory compliance
Authors: Ammar Abdulrahman Jairoun, Sabaa Saleh Al-Hemyari, Moyad Shahwan, Abeer M. Al-Ghananeem, Sa’ed H. Zyoud, Brian Godman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Electronic cigarettes, commonly referred to as e-cigarettes, have spread rapidly across global markets. Their growth has created regulatory concerns involving product safety, nicotine levels, youth access, advertising practices, flavor controls, and protection of public health. Countries have adopted different regulatory approaches because of variation in public health priorities, tobacco-control policies, and legal systems. Bibliometric analysis cannot measure whether regulations are effective, but it can show how the scientific literature informing policy has developed and where new regulatory concerns are emerging.Objective:This study aimed to map the global scientific literature on e-cigarette regulation and regulatory compliance, identify major contributors and research patterns, and detect emerging policy-related topics that require further study.Methods:A bibliometric analysis was conducted using Scopus-indexed publications published between 2004 and August 2024. After applying predefined eligibility criteria, the final records were analyzed using VOSviewer software (Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University). The analysis examined publication growth, citation activity, co-authorship networks, institutional and country collaborations, co-citation links, keyword co-occurrence, and overlay visualization.Results:The final dataset included 984 publications. The United States, the United Kingdom, and Canada were the most active countries in this research area. The University of Waterloo, King’s College London, and the Ontario Institute for Cancer Research were among the leading institutions. The main journals publishing work in this field were Nicotine & Tobacco Research, Tobacco Control, and the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. Keyword findings showed a gradual movement away from broader tobacco-control research toward topics such as youth vaping, electronic nicotine delivery systems, flavor regulation, public policy, and regulatory compliance. These patterns reflect changing research priorities that can support future regulatory studies and policy development.Conclusion:Scientific research on e-cigarette regulation has grown markedly during the past 2 decades. Although bibliometric methods cannot determine whether a policy works, they provide a structured view of how evidence in this field has developed. Future studies should combine bibliometric findings with comparative policy studies to improve understanding of how e-cigarette regulatory frameworks are implemented and what effects they produce.

1. Introduction

Tobacco is a serious threat to society around the globe.[1–3] It is reported that there are 34.3 million smokers currently in the United States (US), and Tobacco has a potential cause of 0.48 million American lives annually.[4,5] The government’s initiative in this regard following the first surgeon general’s Report on Tobacco in 1964, has led to a significant decline in traditional cigarette usage over the past few decades. However, the emergence of electronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) has posed new challenges.[2,6] which have rapidly gained popularity and exposed a younger population to various health risks.[7–9] E-cigarettes are battery-powered devices and can give nicotine and non-nicotine resolutions to the consumer through the breathing of liquid which provides heat to inhale.[10,11] The first marketing electronic smoke was established in Beijing, China in 2003. However, before e-cigarettes could capture the US market in 2006, it has already spread over internet and online kiosks and attracted many youths on that time.[12]

The most vulnerable target is middle and high school students, particularly between more immature communities. E-cigarettes present a complex challenge for cigarette control procedures.[3,13,14] For individual health, smokers who switch from traditional cigarettes to e-cigarettes may reduce their health risks.[15] However, the growing use of e-cigarettes among youth, compelled by target announcements, attractive flavors, and the idea of being a more reliable alternative, manage to reverse the hard-to-do gains in lowering usual smoke use.[16] This raises concerns about a generation after the baby boom appropriate compulsive to nicotine.[17] To address this concern, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) circulated a conclusive rule in 2016, causing e-cigarettes under the opportunity of the 2009 Family Smoking Prevention and Tobacco Control Act. States and local governments have further accomplished rules to control the marketing and use of e-cigarettes.[18]

Too many disciplines which have garnered researchers’ attention related to e-cigarette research, most works on e-cigarettes are driven through exploring its various aspects including health risk,[19] environmental impact,[20] policy and regulations,[21–23] lifestyle,[6,24] sustainability,[25,26] business imbalance[26] and job safety,[22,27] etc. However, these researches are conducted using surveys or many researchers have used some empirical studies to investigate it further. The comprehensive view of the available literature, and the avenues where it has been published. Researchers are actively involved in research to help the government accomplish the goal of a tobacco-free society, and the current area of research is of extreme importance and pivotal in decision-making and legislation.

Among the published studies, several review papers[3,14,16,28] have been published, including a few bibliometric reviews on e-cigarettes.[8–11,29] However, a bibliometric analysis focusing on the regulatory compliances or regulations of e-cigarettes has not yet been published. Several bibliometric studies have examined e-cigarette research more broadly, including studies focused on smoking cessation, health effects, youth vaping, toxicology, and tobacco control. However, no prior bibliometric study appears to have specifically mapped research on e-cigarette regulatory compliance, legal paradigms, and policy-related challenges. The present study does not assess whether individual regulations are effective. Instead, it describes how scientific evidence on regulatory issues has developed, identifies leading contributors and collaboration networks, and emphasizes emerging research priorities that can inform future regulatory decision-making. To that end, this research has conducted a thorough bibliometric study on e-cigarettes fetching the data from Scopus. The data was searched from 2004 to 2024, and the analysis is done using visualization of similarities viewer (VOSviewer). Since bibliometric studies can help researchers explore different dimensions of a research topic and can significantly contribute to research growth, the current study aimed to explore how e-cigarette regulatory compliance, or regulations, has evolved and to emphasize how crucial regulatory frameworks are to maintaining public safety and health.

2. Methods and materials

2.1. Data extraction

Bibliographic records were retrieved from Scopus on August 20, 2024. Scopus was selected because it offers broad multidisciplinary reporting and extensive citation indexing, making it suitable for bibliometric studies. The search strategy combined controlled terms and free-text keywords relating to e-cigarettes and regulatory issues. Boolean operators were used to combine the following terms: (“electronic cigarette*” OR “e-cigarette*” OR vaping OR “electronic nicotine delivery system*” OR ENDS) AND (“regulation*” OR “regulatory compliance” OR legislation OR policy OR “tobacco control” OR “public health policy” OR “flavor regulation” OR “marketing restriction*” OR “age restriction*” OR “nicotine regulation”).

The search was restricted to articles released between January 2004 and August 2024. Eligible records included peer-reviewed journal articles, conference papers, review articles, books and book chapters, and short surveys published in English. Editorials, notes, letters, errata, retracted papers, and non-English publications were excluded.

The screening procedure followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) framework. After automatic filtering, titles, abstracts, and keywords were manually screened to confirm that each record was primarily relevant to e-cigarette regulation or regulatory compliance. A total of 1129 articles having search strings in their title, abstract, or keywords appeared in the search result. By following the PRISMA guideline different exclusion criteria were applied to filter out the collected data. The first exclusion criterion is applied to exclude the articles that were not peer-reviewed leaving either journal articles, reviews, conference papers, book chapters, books, or short surveys. All the articles written in languages other than English were removed in the second exclusion category, remaining 984 articles. The detailed data extraction and filtration is depicted through PRISMA flowchart (Fig. 1). The complete records were downloaded and imported into the VOSviewer software[30] for bibliometric analyses parameters such as top contributors, collaboration networks, citation and co-citation analyses, keyword analysis, and trending topics were identified. The most cited articles were identified from the Scopus database to get insight into the most impactful literature related to the subject.

2.2. Validation of search strategy

The search strategy was developed through repeated refinement to improve both coverage and reproducibility. Previously published bibliometric studies were reviewed, and the search terms were expanded to include several synonyms for e-cigarettes, regulatory compliance, electronic nicotine delivery systems (ENDS), public health policy, and legislation. Boolean operators and wildcard symbols were applied to capture differences in lexicon across publications. After records were retrieved from the database, duplicate and ineligible items were removed using predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The remaining records were then manually assessed to confirm their focus on regulatory aspects of e-cigarettes. This process helped reduce irrelevant retrievals and improved the precision of the final dataset.

2.3. Data analysis

The final dataset was imported into VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) for bibliometric analysis. Several complementary analyses were conducted. Co-authorship analysis was used to examine collaboration among authors, institutions, and countries. Citation and co-citation analyses were used to identify influential publications and the intellectual structure of the field. Bibliographic coupling was used to assess relationships among publications. Keyword co-occurrence analysis was used to identify dominant research themes, while overlay visualization was used to examine how topics changed over time. Fractional counting was used when constructing collaboration networks to reduce the influence of publications with large author groups. Network maps were decoded using node size clustering patterns, link strength, and temporal distribution. The circles surrounding the phrases denote the frequency in the maps. The larger the circle larger be the frequency of that entity. To improve the readability of the main manuscript, detailed collaboration metrics for individual authors, institutions, and countries are presented in the Supplementary Material, while the principal bibliometric findings are summarized in the main text.

8. Discussion

This bibliometric study offers a broad overview of global research on e-cigarette regulation and regulatory compliance over the past 2 decades. As opposed to measuring the success or failure of regulatory policies, the study traces the development of scientific work in this area, identifies major contributors, and describes emerging themes that have influenced academic discussion on e-cigarette regulation.

The findings show significant growth in publication output, especially after 2012 and following the rapid rise of youth vaping and ENDS. This growth reflects cumulative scientific interest in regulatory challenges and public health concerns related to e-cigarettes.

This study provides valuable information to scientists, policymakers, and health activists. The findings underscore the necessity of continuous research and strict regulations to reduce the complex challenges associated with the rising use of e-cigarettes and to ensure the safety of public health. This bibliometric review is made up of 984 articles published between 2004 and August 2024, with a focus on regulatory compliance within the e-cigarette industry. The research involves 943 institutions across 70 countries, and it offers the widest range of global university interest in the subject. A total of 3440 authors have published on the topic in 296 different journals. The overall citations garnered by these publications were 24,200 as per the date searched on the Scopus database. Trends in publication between 2004 and August 2024 reflect a marked increase in research activity, especially after 2019. Following the year 2012, the growth was exponential, peaking at 154 articles in the year 2023, the highest publication year. The data demonstrate a growing interest in understanding and formulating various regulations and policies regarding e-cigarettes.

The bibliometric analysis identified a relatively small group of authors, institutions, journals, and countries that have contributed substantially to the scientific literature on e-cigarette regulation. North American institutions, particularly those in the US and Canada, together with leading organizations in the UK, accounted for much of the publication output and international collaboration identified in this study. These findings reflect the concentration of research capacity and established tobacco-control research infrastructure within these regions. However, publication productivity and collaboration intensity should not be interpreted as direct indicators of regulatory influence or policy effectiveness; rather, they identify the principal scientific contributors that have shaped the evidence base and informed subsequent research on e-cigarette regulation.

The geographical distribution of publications demonstrates differences in research capacity and scientific investment rather than differences in regulatory performance. Countries with greater publication output generally possess well-established tobacco-control research programs, stronger funding mechanisms, and broader international collaborations. Consequently, publication productivity should be interpreted as an indicator of scientific activity rather than evidence of the effectiveness or maturity of national regulatory frameworks.

We have also identified potential future work by analyzing keywords and investigating current research trends. Prominent keywords such as electronic nicotine delivery, flavoring agent, vaping, health hazards, tobacco dependence, and industry, suggest that future research shall attract researchers to devise technology to tackle the challenges posed by the growing e-cigarette risk. Further, “female” is also one of the recently used keywords, implying the potential involvement and victimization of females by the growing threat of e-cigarettes. The prominent keywords identified using keyword analysis show that research in this area will boom and attract many researchers in the near future. The rapid development and growing risk of e-cigarettes shall attract many researchers to explore several aspects and address many issues.

The citation analysis of documents highlights the most impactful studies on the subject. It was observed that 50 articles had more than 100 citations, indicating impactful and high visibility of e-cigarette-related research. The article published by Bullen et al (2013) has garnered the most citations on the topic (958) and reflects the high-impact research conducted by him.[31] Among the top 10 most cited papers, only 3 articles are review papers, while 7 papers are empirical works.

9. Effectiveness of e-cigarettes for smoking cessation

The most cited study by Bullen et al (2013), through a rigorous randomized controlled investigation, provides critical insight into the potential of e-cigarettes as a tool for smoking cessation over nicotine patches. Their findings indicate that e-cigarettes, with or without nicotine, may be more effective than nicotine patches, particularly in supporting long-term abstinence and fewer adverse events.[31] Similarly, a systematic review and meta-analysis conducted by Hartmann-Boyce et al (2016) conform to these findings but also underscore the need for more standardized investigations because of the variability in various study designs and the moderate quality of evidence.[33] Another study concludes that e-cigarettes have tremendous potential in the fight against tobacco adoption but also raises concerns that e-cigarettes could become a repeat of past public health mistakes, particularly in the absence of robust regulatory compliance and public health policies.[35] Uncertainty exists about e-cigarettes in tobacco control; therefore, more robust research is urgently needed before establishing their overall pros and cons at both individual and population levels.[36]

10. Rise in e-cigarette use among youth over time

Most of the highly cited studies discuss the tremendous rise in e-cigarette use among youth. It was reported that after 2011, e-cigarettes became the most popular tobacco product among students.[32,34] The National Youth Tobacco Survey, a study conducted by the Center for Tobacco Products, US FDA, on 10,097 high school and 8837 middle school students, showed that 63.6% of high school students and 65.4% of middle school students reported being exclusive e-cigarette users. Flavored e-cigarettes, including fruit, menthol, mint, candy, desserts, or other sweets, are the most reported flavors among students.[5] It can be envisaged that flavors play a key role in attracting youth, and it also highlights their vulnerability to targeted marketing strategies. The popularity of e-cigarettes among youth and their associated factors was also investigated by Wang et al (2019), who found that e-cigarette use is primarily influenced by factors such as curiosity, peer influence, and the availability of different flavors.[37] The study identified potential sources attracting youth to e-cigarette use, including tobacco product advertisements in convenience stores, supermarkets, and gas stations, and frequent exposure to the internet, television, streaming services, and movies.[37]

11. International perspective and regulatory compliance

Adkison et al (2013) presented an inter-regional comparative study, showing how cultural and regulatory differences impact e-cigarette adoption across 4 countries across the globe.[38] The authors conclude that while e-cigarette use is rising globally, the patterns of use are heavily influenced by local policies and social behaviors. The paper traces the patterns of ENDS related to awareness among current and former smokers in 4 geographically different countries: the US and Canada from North America, the UK from Europe, and Australia from the Asian-Pacific region. It was reported that 79.8% reported using e-cigarettes because they considered them less harmful than traditional cigarettes; 75.4% said that it helps them reduce their smoking; and 85.1% stated that it helps them quit smoking. This study emphasizes a crucial layer of complexity to the discussion, indicating that the impact of e-cigarettes cannot be fully understood without considering the broader regulatory and cultural context.[38] Notably, Wang et al (2019) reported that a significant portion of youth e-cigarette users expressed a desire to quit, with more than half reporting serious intentions to stop using all tobacco products.[37] In this regard, the FDA regulation (2016), as outlined in the Federal Register, represents a pivotal moment in the legal landscape of tobacco control, particularly concerning e-cigarettes.[18] This also marks a significant step in the US, as it formally includes e-cigarettes under federal oversight, setting the stage for future public health policies aimed at controlling their use.[18] The implications of this regulation are significant, as it seeks to balance the potential benefits of e-cigarettes as a cessation tool with the need to protect vulnerable populations, particularly youth, from nicotine addiction.

12. Public health awareness and harm reduction

In recent years, research on the potential toxicities of e-cigarettes has increased rapidly. Meanwhile, regulatory policymakers are struggling with how to regulate the tremendously growing array of nicotine delivery systems’ suppliers and products.[19] Cahn and Siegel (2011) and de Groot et al (2018) have also examined the broader public health implications of e-cigarettes.[35,40] Cahn and Siegel critically analyze the idea of e-cigarettes as a harm-reduction tool, cautioning against their potential to undermine existing tobacco-control efforts,[35] while de Groot et al focus on the epidemiology of lung cancer, raising concerns about the long-term health effects of e-cigarettes, particularly with the increasing cases of lung cancer.[40] Both studies emphasize the need for a cautious approach in promoting e-cigarettes, highlighting potential risks alongside their potential benefits.

14. Study limitations

This study has a number of notable limitations. First, the analysis relied only on the Scopus database. Relevant publications indexed in other sources, such as PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and other regional databases, may therefore have been missed. Second, the study was limited to English-language publications, which introduces the possibility of language bias. Third, while the search strategy was broad and manual screening was conducted, some included publications may have addressed e-cigarettes generally rather than regulatory compliance as their main focus. Fourth, citation-based indicators tend to favor older publications because they have had more time to gather citations. As a result, recently published studies may be underrepresented in influence-based measures. Finally, bibliometric analysis can identify research productivity, collaboration patterns, and scientific influence; however, it cannot directly evaluate the application, effectiveness, or quality of e-cigarette regulations. The findings should therefore be understood as evidence of the development of scientific research as opposed to evidence of regulatory outcomes.

15. Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis provides an overview of how global scientific studies on e-cigarette regulation and regulatory compliance developed between 2004 and 2024. The outcomes identify leading contributors, influential publications, collaboration networks, and emerging themes that have molded the field. The analysis shows expanding scientific interest in youth vaping, ENDS, flavor regulation, and tobacco-control policy. Although bibliometric approaches cannot determine whether regulatory interventions are effective, they help clarify how research priorities and knowledge networks have evolved in ways that can support policy discussion. Future studies should combine bibliometric discoveries with comparative policy analysis, regulatory evaluation, and implementation study to better assess how different regulatory approaches operate across countries. Ongoing international collaboration and evidence-informed policymaking will be needed to balance the possible harm-reduction role of e-cigarettes for adult smokers with the need to protect young people and other vulnerable groups.

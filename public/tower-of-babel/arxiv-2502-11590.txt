Abstract
. We indicate explicitly how to obtain the Moutard transformation singularity for the Davey–Stewartson II equation with two indeterminancy points. We also justify an arbitrary order “mass drop” phenomenon via this singularity formation scheme.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35Q53; Secondary: 37K35, 35B44, 49Q05.

1.
Introduction: the Davey–Stewartson II equation

In this Letter, following Taĭmanov
[
Taĭ21
,
Taĭ24
]
,
we shall consider the Moutard transformation solutions of the following Davey–Stewartson II equation
[
DS74
]
:

U_{t}=i\left(U_{zz}+U_{\overline{z}\,\overline{z}}+2(V+\overline{V})U\right),\ \ \ V_{\overline{z}}=(|U|^{2})_{z},

(1.1)

which is the compatibility condition for the linear problems

{\mathcal{D}}\Psi=0,\ \ \ \partial_{t}\Psi={\mathcal{A}}\Psi,

where

{\mathcal{D}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\partial\\
-\overline{\partial}&0\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}U&0\\
0&\overline{U}\end{array}\right)

is a two-dimensional Dirac operator with a complex-valued potential
U
with

\partial=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right),\quad\overline{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right),

the time-dependent generator
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

{\mathcal{A}}=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\partial^{2}-V&\overline{U}\,\overline{\partial}-\overline{U}_{\overline{z}}\\
U\partial-U_{z}&\overline{\partial}^{2}+\overline{V}\end{array}\right),

and the solution
\Psi
is taken as the following matrix form

\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{1}&-\overline{\psi}_{2}\\
\psi_{2}&\overline{\psi}_{1}\end{array}\right).

Note that if
(U_{0},V_{0})
solves (
1.1
), then
(\overline{U}_{0},V_{0})
solves

U_{t}=-i\left(U_{zz}+U_{\overline{z}\,\overline{z}}+2(V+\overline{V})U\right),\ \ \ V_{\overline{z}}=(|U|^{2})_{z}.

Hence, (
1.1
) is also the compatibility condition of the “conjugate” problems

{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}\Phi=0,\ \ \Phi_{t}={\mathcal{A}}^{\vee}\Phi,

where

{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\partial\\
-\overline{\partial}&0\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{U}&0\\
0&U\end{array}\right),

and

{\mathcal{A}}^{\vee}=-i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\partial^{2}-V&U\overline{\partial}-U_{\overline{z}}\\
\overline{U}\partial-\overline{U}_{z}&\overline{\partial}^{2}+\overline{V}\end{array}\right).

Analogously, the solution
\Phi
is taken as the following matrix form

\Phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi_{1}&-\overline{\varphi}_{2}\\
\varphi_{2}&\overline{\varphi}_{1}\end{array}\right).

If
U=0
,
(\psi_{1},\varphi_{1})
is pair of holomorphic functions, while
(\psi_{2},\varphi_{2})
anti-holomorphic.
When
U=\overline{U}
, the “conjugate” problems are irrelevant and one regards
\Psi=\Phi
.
The pair
(\Psi,\Phi)
serves as a Davey–Stewartson II deformation of surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, see e.g.
[
Taĭ22
]
.

2.
Moutard singularity on two points or multiple zeroes

Using the Moutard transformation of the solution
U=V=0
with spinors

\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{\prime}&0\\
0&\overline{g^{\prime}}\end{array}\right),\ \ \Phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}f^{\prime}&i\\
i&\overline{{f}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right),

the following two-parameter family of solutions were found in
[
Taĭ24
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Taĭmanov, 2024)
.

Let
f(z,t)
and
g(z,t)
be two functions which are holomorphic in
z
and satisfy the equations

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=i\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}},\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-i\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z^{2}}.

(2.1)

Let
h
be a function which is holomorphic in
z
and satisfies the relations

h^{\prime}=f^{\prime}g^{\prime},\quad ih_{t}=g^{\prime\prime}f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}.

Then

U=i\overline{g^{\prime}}\frac{f^{\prime}g-h}{|g|^{2}+|h|^{2}}

(2.2)

satisfies the Davey–Stewartson II equation (
1.1
).

It is noteworthy to point out that in the singularity formation scheme of Moutard transformation,
the singularity points
(z_{0},t_{0})
are encoded in the zero set of
|g|^{2}+|h|^{2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let us first write down the example given in
[
Taĭ24
]
.
Take

f(z,t)=z^{2}+2it,\quad g(z,t)=z^{3}-6itz,

and choose

h=\frac{3}{2}z^{4}-6itz^{2}+6t^{2}.

We derive the following solution

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\frac{i\left(3\overline{z}^{2}+6it\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{4}-6itz^{2}-6t^{2}\right)}{|z^{3}-6itz|^{2}+\left|\frac{3}{2}z^{4}-6itz^{2}+6t^{2}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=O(|z|^{-2}),\qquad|z|\rightarrow+\infty.

For
t=0
it has the indeterminacy type singularity
\sim\frac{3}{2}i\frac{z^{2}}{|z|^{2}}
as
|z|\rightarrow 0
.

The first observation of this Letter can be summarized as follows:

Observation 2.3
.

In above example, we modify the data set
(f,g)
in the following way

f(z,t)=(z^{2}+2it)-z,\quad g(z,t)=(z^{3}-6itz)-z.

The natural observation (or rather, motivation) here is that the lower order modification terms is set to solve the
linear
equations for
f
and
g
.
Thus it remains to compute

f^{\prime}(z,t)=2z-1,\quad g^{\prime}(z,t)=3z^{2}-(1+6it)

and

h^{\prime}(z,t)=f^{\prime}(z,t)g^{\prime}(z,t)=6z^{3}-3z^{2}-2(1+6it)z+(1+6it).

We choose

h(z,t)=\frac{3}{2}z^{4}-z^{3}-(1+6it)z^{2}+(1+6it)z-i(t^{2}+4it^{3})-\frac{1}{2}.

Here, by adding the temporal term
-i(t^{2}+4it^{3})
we meet the constraint
ih_{t}=g^{\prime\prime}f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}
,
and by adding the constant
-\frac{1}{2}
, we see that
h|_{t=0}
vanishes at
z=\pm 1
.
By Theorem
2.1
, the data triple
(f,g,h)
yields the following solution for the Davey–Stewartson II equation

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=i\overline{g^{\prime}}\frac{f^{\prime}g-h}{|g|^{2}+|h|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\left(3\overline{z}^{2}-(1-6it)\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}z^{4}-(1+6it)z^{2}+i(t^{2}+4it^{3})+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{|(z^{3}-6itz)-z|^{2}+\left|\frac{3}{2}z^{4}-z^{3}-(1+6it)z^{2}+(1+6it)z-i(t^{2}+4it^{3})-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}}.

Note that

h|_{t=0}=\frac{3}{2}z^{4}-z^{3}-z^{2}+z-\frac{1}{2}=(z^{2}-1)\left(\frac{3}{2}z^{2}-z+\frac{1}{2}\right),

g|_{t=0}=z(z^{2}-1),\quad g^{\prime}|_{z=\pm 1}\neq 0,

and

(f^{\prime}g-h)|_{t=0}=\frac{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}{2}.

Thus the solution
U
at
t=0
has a two-point indeterminancy type singularity

U|_{t=0}\sim C_{\pm 1}\frac{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}{|z^{2}-1|^{2}},\quad z\rightarrow\pm 1,

The involved implicit constants
C_{\pm 1}
can be computed directly, and we omit this step.

Remark 2.4
.

For higher order polynomials one can construct in the similar manner the multiple-point indeterminancy singularity.
We choose not to dwell on that issue.

Note that the solution in Observation
2.3
has mass
\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{2}^{2}\equiv 4\pi
,
except at the singular time
t=0
, it has a “mass drop” with magnitude
2\pi
(with
2
being the number of indeterminancy points).
The second observation of this Letter can be summarized as follows:

Observation 2.5
.

According to (
2.1
), we construct for
2\leq m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq n\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

f_{m}=z^{m}+im(m-1)tz^{m-2}+\cdots,

g_{n}=z^{n}-in(n-1)tz^{n-2}-\cdots,

and for convenience, with the normalization
f_{m}(0,0)=g_{n}(0,0)=0
.
For example, we have

\displaystyle f_{2}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{2}+2it,

\displaystyle f_{3}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{3}+6itz,

\displaystyle f_{4}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{4}+12itz^{2}-12t^{2},

\displaystyle f_{5}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{5}+20itz^{3}-60t^{2}z

and

\displaystyle g_{1}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z,

\displaystyle g_{2}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{2}-2it,

\displaystyle g_{3}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{3}-6itz,

\displaystyle g_{4}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{4}-12itz^{2}-12t^{2},

\displaystyle g_{5}(z,t)

\displaystyle=z^{5}-20itz^{3}-60t^{2}z.

In constructing the function
h
we also assume
h(0,0)=0
,
thus the solution
U
in (
2.2
) with data
(f_{m},g_{n})
has mass
\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{2}^{2}\equiv(m+n-1)\pi
,
except at the singular time
t=0
, it has a “mass drop” with magnitude
n\pi
(with
n
being the order of
g
) to
(m-1)\pi
.
The normalization on
h
can not be fulfilled when
m=1
unless
U
is the trivial zero solution.

The Bisognano-Wichmann property for non-unitary Wightman conformal field theories
James E. Tener

Abstract
The Bisognano-Wichmann and Haag duality properties for algebraic quantum field theories are often studied using the powerful tools of Tomita-Takesaki modular theory for nets of operator algebras. In this article, we study analogous properties of nets of algebras generated by smeared Wightman fields, for potentially non-unitary theories. In light of recent work [ CRTT24 ] constructing Wightman field theories for (non-unitary) Möbius vertex algebras, we obtain a broadly applicable non-unitary version of the Bisognano-Wichmann property. In this setting we do not have access to the traditional tools of Hilbert space functional analysis, like functional calculus. Instead, results analogous to those of Tomita-Takesaki theory are derived ‘by hand’ from the Wightman axioms. As an application, we demonstrate Haag duality for nets of smeared Wightman fields for unitary theories.

Introduction

The foundational articles
[
BW75
,
BW76
]
exhibited what is now called the “Bisognano-Wichmann property” in axiomatic quantum field theory, which links 1) the analytic continuation of the symmetry group, 2) the PCT operator, and 3) the adjoint operation on smeared fields or local observables.
Bisognano and Wichmann worked in the context of
4
-dimensional Wightman-type QFTs on Minkowski space, but analogous results were established for
2
-dimensional conformal field theories.
This was done under Wightman-type axioms in
[
BSM90
]
, and using purely operator algebraic methods in
[
BGL93
,
GF93
]
under Haag-Kastler axioms.
In the operator algebraic setting, a beautiful picture emerges where physical symmetries realise the Tomita-Takesaki modular automorphisms and modular conjugation corresponding to the vacuum state.

In this article, we study the Bisognano-Wichmann property for 2d chiral conformal field theories (CFTs) which are potentially
non-unitary
, which is to say theories without a compatible inner product.
We are motivated by the following question: can the operator algebraic methods of algebraic conformal field theory be applied to non-unitary conformal field theories?
While a positive answer would obviously be quite desirable due to the recent surge in interest in non-unitary CFTs, it is a daunting challenge to pursue this line of thought due to the fundamental reliance of operator algebraic methods on Hilbert space techniques.
We see a potential path forward as follows.

It has long been understood that the axioms of a vertex algebra (the most common way of axiomatizing a non-unitary conformal field theory) are closely tied to the Wightman axioms
[
Kac98
, §1.2]
, and in recent joint work we showed that there is an equivalence of categories between (potentially non-unitary) Möbius-covariant Wightman CFTs on the unit circle
S^{1}
and (potentially non-unitary) Möbius vertex algebras which are generated by a chosen set of quasiprimary vectors
[
CRTT24
,
RTT22
]
.
Such a Wightman theory consists of a family
{\mathcal{F}}
of operator-valued distributions
\varphi:C^{\infty}(S^{1})\to\End({\mathcal{D}})
on a common, invariant domain
{\mathcal{D}}
cyclically generated from a vacuum vector
\Omega
, along with a compatible positive-energy representation
U
of the Möbius group
\Mob=\operatorname{PSU}(1,1)
of holomorphic automorphisms of the unit disk.

Following
[
SW64
]

1

1

and

These polynomial algebras formally satisfy many of the same axioms as a Haag-Kastler net of operator algebras, but they are actually built from a vertex algebra.
Our philosophy therefore is to try to obtain analogous results for the polynomial nets

as one does with Haag-Kastler nets, and in doing so apply operator algebraic methods in the non-unitary setting.
An early success in this regard was the proof of the Reeh-Schlieder property that

for non-unitary Wightman theories

In practice, transferring operator algebraic methods to the non-unitary setting can be quite difficult as these methods often rely crucially on features of operators on Hilbert spaces, e.g. by applying functional calculus to produce operators with certain properties.
However, in this article we report success in this endeavor with regard to the Bisognano-Wichmann property, and partial success with regard to Haag duality.
We are guided by results proven in the setting of unitary CFTs, but often a novel approach is required due to the lack of general functional analytic theory.
For example, one cannot invoke the general Tomita-Takesaki theory, but instead the key results can be established ‘by hand’ through careful study of the CFT axioms; the same is true with regard to applications of functional calculus.

Let

Let

Dedication

This article is dedicated to Huzihiro Araki, and was prepared for the special issue of
Communications in Mathematical Physics
in his honor.
We briefly explain the connection between this article and Araki’s work.
Araki’s proof of Haag duality for a free scalar bosonic field
[
Ara63
,
Ara64
]
, sometimes called Haag-Araki duality, laid the foundation for later study of duality using the methods of Bisognano-Wichmann; this article is built on that foundation.
Araki also considered duality for free fermionic fields
[
Ara71
, Rem. 4.9]
, and this instance of Haag-Araki duality plays a special role with regard to the philosophy of this article.
Indeed, we are motivated here by the comparison between nets

generated by (typically unbounded) smeared fields, and the corresponding nets of von Neumann algebras
{\mathcal{A}}(I)

The free fermion model is essentially the only example of a 2d chiral CFT whose smeared fields are bounded operators, so that the distinction between

and

is not necessary.
Our Theorem
E
establishing Haag duality for nets of smeared fields can therefore be interpreted as a generalization of Haag-Araki duality to non-free models.

Background

2.1
Conformal nets and Tomita-Takesaki theory

Our first aim is to describe the relationship between Tomita-Takesaki modular theory and algebraic conformal field theories on Hilbert spaces which provides the motivation for this work.
We begin by summarizing the main results of Tomita-Takesaki theory, which was first introduced
[
Tom67
,
Tak70
]
in the context of von Neumann algebras (see also
[
Tak03
]
).
If
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Hilbert space, then a von Neumann algebra
M
is a unital
*
-subalgebra
M\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
that is closed in the topology of pointwise convergence.
A vector
\Omega\in{\mathcal{H}}
is called cyclic for
M
if
M\Omega
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, and called separating if the only element
x\in M
satisfying
x\Omega=0
is
x=0
.
A vector
\Omega
is cyclic for
M
if and only if it is separating for
M^{\prime}
, and in light of the double commutant theorem
M=M^{\prime\prime}
, it follows that
\Omega
is cyclic for
M^{\prime}
if and only if it is separating for
M
3
3

3

Here the commutant
S^{\prime}
of a subset
S\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
consists of all operators
x\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
commuting element-wise with
S
. This is a von Neumann algebra if
S
is closed under taking adjoints
.

Let
S^{0}
be the densely-defined conjugate linear operator on
H
which maps
M\Omega
to itself via the formula
S^{0}x\Omega=x^{*}\Omega
, and let
S
be the closure of
S^{0}
(i.e. the operator obtained by taking the closure of the graph of
S^{0}
in
{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}
).
We have a polar decomposition
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
, where
J
is antiunitary and
\Delta^{1/2}
is a densely defined positive operator.
The main result of Tomita-Takesaki theory says that

JMJ=M^{\prime},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta^{it}M\Delta^{-it}=M,

where the unitary operator
\Delta^{it}
is defined via spectral theory and functional calculus.
We thus have a canonical dynamics arising from the modular automorphism group
\alpha_{t}:=\operatorname{Ad}\Delta^{it}
(which is uniquely determined up to inner automorphisms by the work of Connes).

Tomita-Takesaki theory has proven to be an extremely powerful tool in the study of algebraic quantum field, which is to say quantum field theories in the sense of Haag-Kastler nets of operator algebras
[
HK64
]
.
In the context of 2d chiral conformal field theory, we have the following axiomatization:

Definition 2.1
.

A
Möbius-covariant (Haag-Kastler) net
on
S^{1}
is a triple
({\mathcal{A}},U,\Omega)
, where

{\mathcal{A}}
associates to each open non-dense nonempty connected interval
I
of
S^{1}
a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
on
{\mathcal{H}}
,

U
is a SOT-continuous unitary representation of
\Mob
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and
\Omega\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that the following hold:

(HK1)

Isotony
: If
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
.

(HK2)

Locality
: If
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})
and
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
commute.

(HK3)

Möbius covariance
: For
\gamma\in\Mob
,
U(\gamma)\Omega=\Omega
and

U(\gamma){\mathcal{A}}(I)U(\gamma)^{*}={\mathcal{A}}(\gamma I)
for each interval
I
.

(HK4)

Spectrum condition
: The generator
L_{0}
of rotations
U(r_{\theta})=e^{i\theta L_{0}}
is positive.

(HK5)

Vacuum
:
\Omega
is the unique (up to a scalar) vector in
{\mathcal{H}}
that is invariant under
U
,
and it is cyclic for
\bigvee_{I\Subset S^{1}}{\mathcal{A}}(I)
.

The condition that
\Omega
is the unique
\Mob
-invariant vector is sometimes omitted, in which case a net with this uniqueness property would be called an ‘irreducible’ net.

As a consequence of the axioms, the Reeh-Schlieder property holds: the vector
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for each local algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
.
One can then study the closed conjugate-linear involution
S_{+}
associated with the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{+})
, where
I_{+}=\{z\in S^{1}\,:\,\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
is the upper semi-circle (and likewise with the lower semi-circle
I_{-}
).
The Bisognano-Wichmann property of the Haag-Kastler net identifies the modular automorphism group
\Delta_{+}^{it}
of this algebra (with respect to the vacuum vector
\Omega
) with a certain subgroup of the Möbius group, and gives a covariance relation for the modular conjugation
J_{+}
of the same algebra.
Specifically consider the following one-parameter subgroup
v_{t}\in\Mob
of transformations fixing the intervals
I_{\pm}

v_{t}(z)=\frac{\cosh(t/2)z-\sinh(t/2)}{-\sinh(t/2)z+\cosh(t/2)},

and let
V_{t}=U(v_{t})
be the corresponding unitary operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then the Bisognano-Wichmann property says that the modular automorphism group and modular conjugation satisfy

\Delta_{+}^{it}=V_{-2\pi t},\qquad J_{+}{\mathcal{A}}(I_{+})J_{+}={\mathcal{A}}(I_{-}).

(2.1)

The original work of Bisognano-Wichmann
[
BW75
,
BW76
]
featured a computation of the analytic continuation of

in the context of Wightman quantum field theories on
4
-dimensional Minkowski space.
Analogous results in the Wightman setting were obtained for conformal field theories by Buchholz-Schulz-Mirbach
[
BSM90
]
, and purely operator algebraic proofs of (

) for conformal field theories were given separately in

,

]
.

In all of the above investigations of the Bisognano-Wichmann property, one of the key aims was to study Haag duality, which is the fact that in any Haag-Kastler net we have

{\mathcal{A}}(I)^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}(I^{\prime}).

This fact was proven again separately in

]

]

]

The one-particle approach relies on the approach to Tomita-Takesaki developed in
[

]

is the real subspace of self-adjoint elements.

Definition 2.2
.

A

of a complex Hilbert space

is a real subspace
{\mathcal{K}}
such that
{\mathcal{K}}+i{\mathcal{K}}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
and

.

The subspace
\overline{M_{sa}\Omega}
is an example of a standard subspace, but many results of Tomita-Takesaki theory can be generalized to the setting of arbitrary standard subspaces (a fact which is crucial for the approach taken in this article).
Associated to
{\mathcal{K}}
one has a closed conjugate-linear involution
S
defined by
S(\xi+i\eta)=\xi-i\eta
for
\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{K}}
, and again one defines an antiunitary
J

\Delta

S=J\Delta^{1/2}
.
Similar to the von Neumann setting, we have

J{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}^{\prime},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta^{it}{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}

where the
complement

{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is defined by

{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}=\{\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}\,:\,\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}\,\text{for all}\,\eta\in{\mathcal{K}}.\}

Alternatively,
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}=(i{\mathcal{K}})^{\perp}
where the orthogonal complement is taken with respect to the real Hilbert space inner product
\operatorname{Re}\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
(from which it follows that
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathcal{K}}
).
Following
[
Lon08a
]
, one has the following notion of a net of standard subspaces:

Definition 2.3
.

A
Möbius-covariant net of subspaces
of a complex Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is a family of real subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)\subset{\mathcal{H}}
indexed by open non-dense nonempty intervals
I\subset S^{1}
, and a unitary representation
U
of
\Mob

{\mathcal{H}}
, satisfying the following properties.

(NS1)

Isotony
: If
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{K}}(I_{2})
.

(NS2)

Locality
: If
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{K}}(I_{2})^{\prime}
.

(NS3)

Möbius covariance
: For
\gamma\in\Mob
, we have
U(\gamma){\mathcal{K}}(I)={\mathcal{K}}(\gamma(I))
.

(NS4)

Spectrum condition
: The generator
L_{0}
of rotations
U(r_{\theta})=e^{i\theta L_{0}}
is positive.

(NS5)

: The complex linear span of the

.

Given a Haag-Kastler net of algebras

.
For any net of subspaces, we again have a Reeh-Schlieder property, that each
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
is a cyclic subspace, and indeed each
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
is a standard subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
The Bisognano-Wichmann property for the net of subspaces says that we again have

\Delta_{+}^{it}=V_{-2\pi t},\qquad J_{+}{\mathcal{K}}(I_{+})={\mathcal{K}}(I_{-}),

from which we again have Haag duality:

{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}={\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime}).

If the net of subspaces

arises from a Haag-Kastler net of algebras of
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
, then the Bisognano-Wichmann and Haag duality properties of

can be derived from those of
{\mathcal{K}}

[
Lon08b
]
.
In this article, we will expand on this connection in the case of non-unitary conformal field theories, which is to say conformal field theories whose state space does not possess an invariant positive-definite form. We will also construct nets of standard subspaces from Wightman theories (or, equivalently, from Möbius vertex algebras), and use this to study the relationship between Wightman theories and Haag-Kastler nets.

2.2
Wightman CFTs and vertex algebras

Our main object of study is potentially non-unitary Möbius-covariant Wightman conformal field theories on the unit circle

In this context, the Möbius group

In contrast to Section

We will summarize here the basic notions, and the reader may consult the reference for further details.

The Wightman axioms have not been extensively studied in their own right within the context of axiomatic 2d chiral conformal field theory, but it turns out that they are exactly equivalent to Möbius vertex algebras, along with a chosen set of quasiprimary vectors which generate the vertex algebra.
It has been long-known that this correspondence is “morally valid” (see e.g.

(VA1)

m=-1,0,1

(VA5)

For each

For every Wightman theory

The vertex algebra structure is characterized by the fact that for every

with conformal dimension

\varphi(e_{j})=v_{(d-j-1)}

In the other direction, given a Möbius vertex algebra
{\mathcal{V}}
with a generating set of quasiprimary vectors, there is a canonical extension

(which can be realized as a subspace of the algebraic completion of
{\mathcal{V}}

{\mathcal{D}}
corresponding to
{\mathcal{V}}
in the sense described above.
This correspondence can be made into an equivalence of categories

CRTT24
, §3.3]
.

Note that we do not require either of the conditions
\dim{\mathcal{V}}(n)<\infty
or
\dim{\mathcal{V}}(0)=1

The requirement that the vertex algebra

be generated by quasiprimary vectors is not very restrictive, and the class of theories considered includes log-CFTs like the
\beta\gamma
-ghost system with

.

In both settings, we have a notion of an invariant Hermitian form, which we call an

Some key examples of involutive but non-unitary theories include the non-unitary minimal models, such as the Yang-Lee model.
These notions will be outlined in detail in Sections
4

6

Invariant forms for a Wightman theory exactly correspond to invariant forms for the corresponding vertex algebra
[
CRTT24
, §4]
.

\tilde{f}

f
was assumed to vanish in a neighborhood of
\pm 1
.
Hence for
z\in I_{+}
we have

F_{N}(z)=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\tfrac{(N(s+\alpha(z))^{2}}{2}}\,\tilde{f}(s)\,ds=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-M}^{M}e^{-\tfrac{(N(s+\alpha(z))^{2}}{2}}\,\tilde{f}(s)\,ds,

(3.2)

for some
M
sufficiently large that the interval
[-M,M]
contains the support of
\tilde{f}
.
The formula (
3.2
) defines a holomorphic function of
z
in a neighborhood of
{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
which extends
(
3.1
)
, and we denote this extension again by
F_{N}
.
Since
\tilde{f}
has compact support, it is straightforward to verify that the integrand of
(
3.2
)
is uniformly bounded on
\Delta\setminus\{\pm 1\}
, and an application of the dominated convergence theorem yields

\lim_{z\to\pm 1}F_{N}(z)=0,

and so
F_{N}
extends to a continuous function on all of
{\mathbb{D}}
, vanishing at
\pm 1
.

We wish to show that the complex derivatives
F_{N}^{(k)}
, a priori defined on
\mathring{\Delta}
, also extend to continuous functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
vanishing at
\pm 1
.
The derivatives are given by

F^{(k)}_{N}(z)=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-M}^{M}r(z)e^{-\tfrac{(N(s+\alpha(z))^{2}}{2}}\,\tilde{f}(s)\,ds,

(3.3)

for holomorphic functions
r(z)
that are polynomials in
z
,
(z\pm 1)^{-1}
and
\alpha(z)
.

It follows that
F_{N}^{(k)}
extends to a continuous function on
{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
, and it remains to analyze the points
z=\pm 1
. Without loss of generality we only consider
z=1
.

Near
z=1
, straightforward computation yields that when
-M\leq s\leq M
we have

e^{-\tfrac{(N(s+\alpha(z))^{2}}{2}}\leq Ce^{-k\operatorname{Re}[\alpha(z)]^{2}}\leq Ce^{-k(\log\left|z-1\right|)^{2}}.

As
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{-k}e^{-\log(t)^{2}}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{-k-\log t}=0
for any
k>0
,
we see that

\lim_{z\to 1,\left|z\right|<1}r(z)e^{-\tfrac{(N(s+\alpha(z))^{2}}{2}}=0

and an application of the dominated convergence theorem to (
3.3
) yields that

\lim_{z\to 1}F_{N}^{(k)}(z)=0.

The same holds at
z=-1
, and we conclude that each
F_{N}^{(k)}
extends to a continuous function on the closed disk
{\mathbb{D}}
, vanishing at
\pm 1
.
It follows that
F_{N}
is
C^{\infty}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
with the required behavior, completing the proof.

∎

We will now study the action of analytically continued operators
\widetilde{V}_{\tau}
on
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{X}}(I_{+})\Omega
, which is a dense subspace
{\mathcal{D}}
by Lemma
3.4
and the Reeh-Schlieder property
[
CRTT24
, App. A]
.

Definition 3.5
.

If
F\in{\mathcal{X}}
and
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}:=\{0\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq\pi\}
, we write
\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F|_{I_{+}}
for the function

\displaystyle(\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F)(z)

\displaystyle=X_{v_{\tau}}(v_{-\tau}(z))^{d-1}F(v_{-\tau}(z))

(3.4)

\displaystyle=(\cosh(\tau)+\operatorname{Re}(z)\sinh(\tau))^{d-1}F(v_{-\tau}(z)),

\displaystyle z\in I_{+}.

The requirement that
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}
ensures that
v_{-\tau}(I_{+})\subset{\mathbb{O}}
, which is the domain of definition of
F
.
If
f=F|_{I_{+}}\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
then
\beta_{d}(v_{t})F|_{I_{+}}=\beta_{d}(v_{t})f
, which is to say that the above notation is consistent with our usual definition of
\beta_{d}(v_{t})
.
Since
F
vanishes to all orders at
\pm 1
we have
\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F|_{I_{+}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I_{+})
, which as previously mentioned we typically extend to a function in
C^{\infty}_{0}(S^{1})
by extending to be the zero function on
I_{-}
.
As expected we have

\beta_{d}(v_{t})\big[\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F|_{I_{+}}\big]=\beta_{d}(v_{\tau+t})F|_{I_{+}}.

(3.5)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
{\mathbb{S}}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,0\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq\pi\}
.

(i)

Let
F\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Then the map
{\mathbb{S}}\to C_{0}^{\infty}(I_{+})
given by
\tau\mapsto\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F|_{I_{+}}
is continuous and holomorphic in the interior.

(ii)

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\in{\mathcal{X}}
and let
\varphi_{1},\ldots\varphi_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
Then the map
{\mathbb{S}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
given by

\tau\mapsto\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega

is continuous and holomorphic in the interior.

Proof.

From inspection of the formula (
3.4
) we can see that the map
{\mathbb{S}}\times I_{+}\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by
(\tau,z)\mapsto(\beta_{d}(v_{\tau})F)(z)
is smooth.
Hence the associated map
{\mathbb{S}}\to C^{\infty}_{0}(I_{+})
is smooth, and again from the formula (
3.4
) we see that the derivative
\partial_{\overline{\tau}}
vanishes on
\mathring{{\mathbb{S}}}
, establishing claim (i).

By the definition of the topology on
{\mathcal{D}}
, expressions
\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega
are jointly continuous in the
f_{j}
, and combined with part (i) we have that

\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega

depends continuously on
\tau
.
The usual proof of the product rule shows that this dependence is holomorphic on
\mathring{{\mathbb{S}}}
.
∎

As a straightforward consequence of Lemma
3.6
we have the following, which implies that for all
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}
we have that
D(\widetilde{V}_{\tau})
contains
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(I_{+})\Omega
, and in particular is densely defined (by Lemma
3.4
).
The proof of the following lemma is adapted from
[
Hen14
, p.42-43]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}
, let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\in{\mathcal{X}}
, and let
{\mathbb{S}}=\{0\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq\pi\}
.
Let

\Phi=\varphi_{1}(F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega.

Then we have the following.

(i)

For any
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}
we have
\Phi\in D(\widetilde{V}_{\tau})
and

\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\Phi=\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega.

(3.6)

(ii)

If
d=\sum d_{j}
then

\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}\Phi=(-1)^{d}\varphi_{k}(F_{k}|_{I_{+}}\circ z^{-1})\cdots\varphi_{1}(F_{1}|_{I_{+}}\circ z^{-1})\Omega.

(3.7)

Proof.

Fix
\tau_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}}
and
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, the map

G_{\lambda}(\tau):=\lambda\big(\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega\big)

is continuous on the closed strip bounded by
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tau_{0}+{\mathbb{R}}
and holomorphic on the interior, with

G_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda\big(V_{t}\varphi_{1}(F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega\big),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

In light of (
3.5
), for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

G_{\lambda}(t+\tau_{0})=\lambda\big(V_{t}\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau_{0}})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau_{0}})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega\big),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence by Definition
3.1
,
\varphi_{1}(F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega\in D(\widetilde{V}_{\tau_{0}})
and

\widetilde{V}_{\tau_{0}}\big[\varphi_{1}(F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega\big]=\varphi_{1}(\beta_{d_{1}}(v_{\tau_{0}})F_{1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(\beta_{d_{k}}(v_{\tau_{0}})F_{k}|_{I_{+}})\Omega.

This completes the first part of the proof.

It remains to show that the action of
\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}
agrees with (
3.7
).
To that end, note that
v_{i\pi}(z)=z^{-1}
, and so by definition for
F\in{\mathcal{X}}
we have

\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{i\pi})F|_{I_{+}}=(-1)^{d_{j}-1}\cdot(F\circ z^{-1})|_{I_{+}}=(-1)^{d_{j}-1}\cdot F|_{I_{-}}\circ z^{-1}

Hence from the formula for
\widetilde{V}_{\tau}
above we have

\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}\Phi=(-1)^{d+k}\varphi_{1}(F_{1}\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{+}})\cdots\varphi_{k}(F_{k}\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{+}})\Omega.

(3.8)

We next claim that if
F\in{\mathcal{X}}
then we have

\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{+}})\Omega=-\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{-}})\Omega.

(3.9)

Indeed
F\circ z^{-1}
is holomorphic on the disk
\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}
and vanishes at the origin, and thus
\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{S^{1}})
strictly lowers conformal dimension.
In particular,

\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{S^{1}})\Omega=0.

Since
\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{S^{1}})=\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{+}})+\varphi(F\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{-}})
, the claim (
3.9
) follows.

Following
[
Hen14
, p.43]
, we set
g_{j}=F_{j}\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{+}}
and
h_{j}=F_{j}\circ z^{-1}|_{I_{-}}=F_{j}|_{I_{+}}\circ z^{-1}
and compute

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k-1}(g_{k-1})\varphi_{k}(h_{k})\Omega

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{k}(h_{k})\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k-1}(g_{k-1})\Omega,

where the first equality is (
3.9
) and the second equality is due to locality.
Repeating this argument yields

\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega=(-1)^{k}\varphi_{k}(h_{k})\cdots\varphi_{1}(h_{1})\Omega.

Combining this identity with (
3.8
) yields the desired expression (
3.7
).
∎

We have thus shown that for
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}
the domain
D(\widetilde{V}_{\tau})
contains
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{X}}(I_{+})\Omega
, where we recall
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{X}}(I_{+})\subset{\mathcal{P}}(I_{-})
is generated by operators
\varphi(F|_{I_{+}})
with
F\in{\mathcal{X}}
.
We would like to extend this result to
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})\Omega
via a limiting argument, approximating
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(I_{+})
by functions of the form
F|_{I_{+}}
.
We will begin by examining the growth rate of the function
\lambda(V_{t}\Phi)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}
and let
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
Then there exists a positive integer
N
and function
C:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
such that:

(i)

there exist constants
k_{1},k_{2}>0
such that
C(t)\leq k_{1}e^{k_{2}\left|t\right|}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

(ii)

for all
f_{j},g_{j}\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
and all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\big(\big[V_{t}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega\big]-\big[V_{t}\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega\big]\big)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\qquad C(t)\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j}-g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}},

where
\left\|f\right\|_{C^{N}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big\|\tfrac{d^{j}}{d\theta^{j}}f\big\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The map

(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})\mapsto\lambda\big(\varphi_{1}(h_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(h_{k})\Omega\big)

is jointly continuous on
C^{\infty}(S^{1})^{k}
.
Thus by standard results (c.f. the beginning of
[
Trè67
, §41]
) there exists a positive integer
N
such that

\left|\lambda\big(\varphi_{1}(h_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(h_{k})\Omega\big)\right|\leq\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{C^{N}}\cdots\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{C^{N}},\qquad h_{j}\in C^{\infty}(S^{1}),

after potentially rescaling
\lambda
for notational convenience.
Observe that

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})-\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}\varphi_{\ell}(f_{\ell})\right]\varphi_{j}(f_{j}-g_{j})\left[\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{k}\varphi_{\ell}(g_{\ell})\right],

where we take the convention that the (non-commuting) products are ordered left-to-right.
Hence

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\big(\big[V_{t}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega\big]-\big[V_{t}\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega\big]\big)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|\lambda\left(\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{j-1}\varphi_{\ell}(\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})f_{\ell})\right]\varphi_{j}(\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})[f_{j}-g_{j}])\left[\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{k}\varphi_{\ell}(\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})g_{\ell})\right]\Omega\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\qquad\leq k\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{t})[f_{j}-g_{j}]\right\|_{C^{N}}.

Thus it suffices to show that for any
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
the function
\left\|\beta_{d}(v_{t})f\right\|_{C^{N}}
satisfies

\left\|\beta_{d}(v_{t})f\right\|_{C^{N}}\leq C(t)\left\|f\right\|_{C^{N}}

(3.10)

for a function
C:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
satisfying the growth condition (i).
We say that a function
{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
satisfying (i) has ‘at most exponential growth,’ and we note that the class of such functions is closed under addition and products.

We first consider
\left\|\beta_{d}(v_{t})f\right\|_{\infty}
, i.e. the case
N=0
.
Recall that

(\beta_{d}(v_{t})f)(z)=(\cosh(t)+\operatorname{Re}(z)\sinh(t))^{d-1}\cdot f(v_{-t}(z)).

Hence

\left\|\beta_{d}(v_{t})f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\sup_{z\in S^{1}}\left|\cosh(t)+\operatorname{Re}(z)\sinh(t)\right|\right)^{d-1}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}

and the function
t\mapsto\sup_{z\in S^{1}}\left|\cosh(t)+\operatorname{Re}(z)\sinh(t)\right|
evidently has at most exponential growth (it is, in fact, exactly
e^{\left|t\right|}
).
This establishes the necessary estimate in the case
N=0
.

More generally, the derivative
\tfrac{d^{j}}{d\theta^{j}}\beta_{d}(v_{t})f
is a linear combination of products of functions of the form

\tfrac{d^{k}}{d\theta^{k}}\big[\cosh(t)+\operatorname{Re}(z)\sinh(t)\big],\quad\tfrac{d^{k}}{d\theta^{k}}v_{-t}(z),\quad f^{(k)}(v_{-t}(z)),\qquad 0\leq k\leq j,

with exactly one term of the last type in each product.
Explicit calculation shows that the sup norms of functions of the first two types are bounded by functions of
t
with at most exponential growth as above, and we conclude that (
3.10
) holds for all
N
with a function
C
with at most exponential growth.
∎

The next lemma establishes an analogous growth bound on the analytic continuation
\lambda(\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\Phi)
on the strip
{\mathbb{S}}
when
\Phi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}(I_{+})\Omega
.
Of particular note is that the estimate only involves data related to the vector
\Phi
(and the function
V_{t}\Phi
on one boundary of the strip), and does not reference
\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}\Phi
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\in{\mathcal{F}}
and let
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
Then there exists a positive integer
N
and positive constant
M
with the following property.
Let
F_{j},G_{j}\in{\mathcal{X}}
and let
f_{j}=F_{j}|_{I_{+}}
and
g_{j}=G_{j}|_{I_{+}}
.
Then for all
\tau\in{\mathbb{S}}=\{0\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq\pi\}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\lambda\big(\big[\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega\big]-\big[\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega\big]\big)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\qquad Me^{\operatorname{Re}(\tau)^{2}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j}-g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}.

Proof.

Let us temporarily fix
F_{j},G_{j}\in{\mathcal{X}}
along with the corresponding
f_{j}=F_{j}|_{I_{+}}
and
g_{j}=G_{j}|_{I_{+}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{G}:{\mathbb{S}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the functions

\mathfrak{F}(\tau)=\lambda\big(\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega\big),\quad\mathfrak{G}(\tau)=\lambda\big(\widetilde{V}_{\tau}\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega\big)

and let
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{F}-\mathfrak{G}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, the functions
\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{H}
are continuous on the closed strip
{\mathbb{S}}=\{0\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq\pi\}
and holomorphic on the interior.
Write
\tau=t+is

0\leq s\leq\pi

f_{j,s}=\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{is})F_{j}|_{I_{+}},\qquad g_{j,s}=\beta_{d_{j}}(v_{is})G_{j}|_{I_{+}},

(3.11)

so that

\widetilde{V}_{is}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega=\varphi_{1}(f_{1,s})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k,s})\Omega

by (
3.6
), and similarly for the
g_{j}
.
By Lemma
3.8
applied to the functions
f_{j,s}
and
g_{j,s}
(along with the fact that
\widetilde{V}_{t+is}=V_{t}\widetilde{V}_{is}
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{H}(t+is)\right|\leq C(t)\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j,s}-g_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}

for some positive integer
N
and some function
C
with at most exponential growth that does not depend on the functions
F_{j}
and
G_{j}
.
Since
F
and
G
are smooth on
{\mathbb{O}}
, the maximum

\max_{0\leq s\leq\pi}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j,s}-g_{j,s}\right\|_{C^{N}}

is achieved.
Hence there is a constant
K>0
(depending on the
F_{j}
and
G_{j}
) such that

\left|\mathfrak{H}(\tau)\right|\leq K\,C(\operatorname{Re}\tau).

Since
\big|e^{-\tau^{2}}\big|\leq e^{\pi^{2}}e^{-\operatorname{Re}(\tau)^{2}}
on
{\mathbb{S}}
and
C
has exponential growth, we see that
e^{-\tau^{2}}\mathfrak{H}(\tau)
is bounded, and in fact vanishes at
\infty
.
A straightforward application of the (strong) maximum principle yields that
\big|e^{-\tau^{2}}\mathfrak{H}(\tau)\big|
achieves its maximum on
\partial{\mathbb{S}}={\mathbb{R}}\cup{\mathbb{R}}+i\pi
.

We first investigate the dependence of this maximum on the functions
F_{j}
and
G_{j}
, although we still suppress this dependence from the notation
\mathfrak{H}(\tau)
.
For
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\big|e^{-t^{2}}\mathfrak{H}(t)\big|\leq M\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j}-g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}

(3.12)

where
M=\max_{t}e^{-t^{2}}C(t)
does not depend on the functions
F_{j}
,
G_{j}
.

We now consider the behavior of
e^{-\tau^{2}}\mathfrak{H}(\tau)
on
{\mathbb{R}}+i\pi
.
Recall from Lemma
3.7
that

\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega=(-1)^{d}\varphi_{k}(\hat{f}_{k})\cdots\varphi_{1}(\hat{f}_{1})\Omega

where
\hat{f}_{j}=f_{j}\circ z^{-1}
and
d=\sum d_{j}
.
Crucially,
\|\hat{f}_{j}\|_{C^{N}}=\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}
.
We have similar computations for the functions
g_{j}
, so by Lemma
3.8

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{H}(t+i\pi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\lambda\big(\big[\widetilde{V}_{t+i\pi}\varphi_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(f_{k})\Omega\big]-\big[\widetilde{V}_{t+i\pi}\varphi_{1}(g_{1})\cdots\varphi_{k}(g_{k})\Omega\big]\big)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\lambda\big(\big[V_{t}\varphi_{k}(\hat{f}_{k})\cdots\varphi_{1}(\hat{f}_{1})\Omega\big]-\big[V_{t}\varphi_{k}(\hat{g}_{k})\cdots\varphi_{1}(\hat{g}_{1})\Omega\big]\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq D(t)\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\|\hat{f}_{j}\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}+\left\|\hat{g}_{j}\right\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{g}_{j}\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=D(t)\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}+\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j}-g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N^{\prime}}}.

The function
D(t)
and integer
N^{\prime}
may differ from the previous computation because of the reordering of the fields
\varphi_{j}
, but by replacing
N
or
N^{\prime}
with
\max\{N,N^{\prime}\}
we may assume without loss of generality that
N=N^{\prime}
.
Arguing as before we have

\left|e^{-(t+i\pi)^{2}}\mathfrak{H}(t+i\pi)\right|\leq M^{\prime}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1+\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}+\left\|g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}\big)\right]\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\|f_{j}-g_{j}\right\|_{C^{N}}

where
M^{\prime}=\max e^{\pi^{2}}e^{-t^{2}}D(t)
does not depend on the functions.
Combined with (
3.12

4
Invariant Hermitian forms, Bisognano-Wichmann, and the KMS condition

In this section, we assume that our Wightman CFT
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{D}},U,\Omega)
has an invariant nondegenerate sesquilinear form
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
, along with an antiunitary PCT involution
\theta:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
which fixes
\Omega
.
Such a theory is called
involutive

In an involutive Wightman CFT, we have

\langle\varphi(f)\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\varphi^{\dagger}(\overline{f})\Psi\rangle,\qquad\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}},\,\varphi\in{\mathcal{F}},\,f\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})

where
\varphi^{\dagger}
is the operator-valued distribution

\varphi^{\dagger}(f)=(-1)^{d_{\varphi}}\theta\varphi(\overline{f}\circ z^{-1})\theta,

and moreover we have
(-1)^{d_{\varphi}}\varphi^{\dagger}\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
We also have

\langle U(\gamma)\Phi,U(\gamma)\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle,\qquad\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}},\,\gamma\in\Mob.

The sesquilinear form is assumed to be jointly
{\mathcal{F}}
-strong continuous and is automatically Hermitian symmetric, meaning
\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\overline{\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle}
for all
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Given
\theta

with these properties is equivalent to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\theta\,\cdot\,\rangle
being a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form on the Möbius vertex algebra underlying
{\mathcal{F}}
.
The theory is called
unitary
if the Hermitian form is an inner product (normalized so that
\left\|\Omega\right\|=1
).
In terms of the correspondence between Wightman CFTs and vertex algebras, an involutive Wightman CFT corresponds to a Möbius vertex algebra with a sesquilinear form and antiunitary operator
\theta
such that
\langle\,\cdot\,\theta\,\cdot\,\rangle
is an invariant bilinear form in the sense of
[
FHL93
,
Li94
]
.
See
[
CRTT24
, Def. 4.9]
and the surrounding discussion for more detail.

Given a continuous map

has an adjoint if there exists
x^{*}\in\End({\mathcal{D}})
such that
\langle x\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,x^{*}\Psi\rangle
for all
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}}

8

8

See also the discussion following Corollary
4.3
regarding the partially defined adjoint of a partially defined (and not necessarily continuous) linear map.

for the collection of continuous operators

that have an adjoint

In light of (

We have

Since the Hermitian form is continuous, each map

The collection of such functionals separates points in

In Definition

In the context of an involutive theory, where we are interested in the adjoints of operators, it is more appropriate to work on an (a priori) larger domain where we only require analytic continuation of the functions

For

Corollary 4.3

(KMS condition)
.

Let
\phi:{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the linear functional

\phi(x)=\langle x\Omega,\Omega\rangle

\alpha_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}V_{t}\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathcal{P}}(I_{+}))

Proof.

Let
x,y\in{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})
.
By Theorem
4.2
, there is a function

Similarly, since
\theta x^{*}\theta\in{\mathcal{P}}(I_{-})
, there exists a function

such that

F_{2}(t)=\langle V_{t}\theta x^{*}\Omega,y^{*}\Omega\rangle,\qquad F_{2}(t-i\pi)=\langle V_{t}x\Omega,y^{*}\Omega\rangle.

.
Hence

and

assemble to a function

continuous on
{\mathbb{S}}_{2\pi i}
and holomorphic in the interior such that

F(t)=F_{1}(t)=\phi(x\alpha_{t}(y)),\qquad F(t+2\pi i)=F_{2}(-t-\pi i)=\phi(\alpha_{t}(y)x),

as required.
∎

We briefly digress on the subject of partially defined operators in the present context.
For the most part, the definitions and results are identical to the situation of unbounded operators on a Hilbert space.
We give a brief summary of the aspects of the theory which persist into the present context.

A partially defined operator
X
on
{\mathcal{D}}
consists of a subspace
D(X)\subset{\mathcal{D}}
and a linear map
X:D(X)\to{\mathcal{D}}
.
We typically require that
D(X)
be dense.
We say that
X_{1}
is an extension of
X_{2}
if
D(X_{2})\subseteq D(X_{1})
and
X_{1}|_{D(X_{2})}=X_{2}
, in which case we write
X_{2}\subset X_{1}
.
If
X
is densely defined, then the adjoint
X^{*}
has domain consisting of vectors
\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}}
such that there exists
\Psi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
for which

\langle X\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\Psi^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad\Phi\in D(X).

In this case
X^{*}\Psi=\Psi^{\prime}
.
If
X_{2}\subset X_{1}
then
X_{1}^{*}\subset X_{2}^{*}
.
A densely defined operator is called symmetric if
X\subset X^{*}
, and self-adjoint if
X=X^{*}
as partially defined operators (i.e. including having the same domain).
A densely defined operator is called closed if its graph is a closed subset of
{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{D}}
, and called closable if the closure of its graph is the graph of a (closed) linear map.
We write
\overline{X}
for the closure of a closable operator
X
.
A core for a closed operator
X
is a subspace
W\subset D(X)
such that
\overline{X|_{W}}=X
.
The adjoint of a densely defined operator is necessarily closed, and if
X
has a densely defined adjoint, then it is closable.

It is at this point that we encounter a key difference from the theory of unbounded operators on Hilbert spaces.
If
X
is closable, then
\overline{X}\subseteq X^{**}
, but equality may fail.
In order to understand this phenomenon, one should recall that the graph of
X^{*}
is essentially the orthogonal complement of the graph of
X
.
Thus this is a manifestation of another fundamental departure, which is that if
W\subset{\mathcal{D}}
then
\overline{W}\subset W^{\perp\perp}
, but again equality may fail.
This is a consequence of the fact that
W^{\perp}
may be ‘surprisingly small,’ even when the Hermitian form is positive definite
9
9

9

Consider the case where
{\mathcal{D}}
is the subspace of
L^{2}[0,1]
spanned by
L^{2}[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
and polynomials. The orthogonal complement of
W=L^{2}[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
in
{\mathcal{D}}
consists of only the zero function.
.

The challenges described above can make it difficult to study duality phenomena in the present context, which in turn increases the difficulty in precisely identifying the domains of various operators.
For example, a key question that we do not resolve in this article is the following.

Question 4.4
.

a core for

In light of the above discussion, it is particularly satisfying that we will now be able to establish (‘by hand’) that the operators

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\Phi\in D(V_{\tau})

Fix

t
.

Since the Hermitian form is jointly continuous, given
\epsilon>0
we may choose a neighborhood
U
of
0
in
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\left|\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\rangle\right|<\epsilon
when
\Phi_{j}\in U
.
Choose
M>0
as above so that
V_{t}\Psi\in MU
for all
t
, and note that
\left|\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\rangle\right|<\epsilon
when
\Phi_{1}\in MU
and
\Phi_{2}\in M^{-1}U
.
Since
\Phi_{n}\to\Phi
, we may choose
n_{0}
such that when
n,m\geq n_{0}
we have
\Phi_{n}-\Phi_{m}\in M^{-1}U
.
We then have for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\left|e^{-2t^{2}}\langle V_{t}\Psi,\Phi_{n}-\Phi_{m}\rangle\right|<\epsilon,

and so the sequence of functions
e^{-2t^{2}}\langle V_{t}\Psi,\Phi_{n}-\Phi_{m}\rangle
converges uniformly to zero, as claimed.
As described above, it follows that
e^{-\tau^{2}}(H_{\Phi_{n}}-H_{\Phi_{m}})
converges uniformly to zero.
Hence
H_{\Phi_{n}}
is a locally uniformly Cauchy net of functions, which therefore converges locally uniformly to a function
H_{\Phi}
that is continuous on
{\mathbb{S}}
and holomorphic in the interior.
Taking pointwise limits on the boundary we see that
H_{\Phi}
satisfies (
4.3
).
Since
\Phi
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\Phi^{\prime}\in D(V_{i\pi})
, as claimed.
∎

As an aside, we remark that the above proof of Theorem
4.6
does not go through verbatim for the operator
\widetilde{V}_{i\pi}
, with the key obstruction being that linear functionals of the form
\langle\,\cdot\,,\Phi\rangle
may not be dense in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
in the strong dual topology (i.e. the topology of uniform convergence on bounded sets), although they are dense in the weak-
*
topology (i.e. the topology of pointwise convergence).

5
Haag duality for Wightman CFTs with invariant Hermitian forms

In this section we continue the notation of Section
4
, fixing an involutive Wightman CFT.
We will now study the notion of ‘Haag duality’ for nets of algebras, which is a key application of Tomita-Takesaki theory in the unitary setting. Our first step will be to make contact with the theory of standard subspaces, which have proven to be very powerful in analyzing duality phenomena in algebraic quantum field theory
[
Lon08a
,
Lon08b
]
(see also Section
2.1
for the unitary case).
The results in this section on Haag duality for non-unitary theories are exploratory at this stage, with the main result being some sufficient conditions for Haag duality of the double commutant net
{\mathcal{P}}(I)^{\prime\prime}
, albeit conditions that may not be practical to check.
However, given how crucial Haag duality is in the study of nets of operator algebras, it is worthwhile to initiate the study in this non-unitary setting.
Furthermore, the results of this section will be applied in Section
6
to gain insight into unitary models.

We first require a brief digression on topological notions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space with a nondegenerate Hermitian form.
Then we refer to the given topology on
{\mathcal{D}}
as the
strong
topology, the weak topology on
{\mathcal{D}}
induced by the continuous dual
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
as the
weak
topology, and the weak topology induced by the functionals
\{\langle\,\cdot\,,\Phi\rangle\,:\,\Phi\in{\mathcal{D}}\}
as the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak
topology.
We refer to these topologies as the
standard topologies
on
{\mathcal{D}}
.

In the context of an involutive Wightman theory, the strong topology is also known as the
{\mathcal{F}}
-strong topology and the weak topology is also known as the
{\mathcal{F}}
-weak topology.
The strong topology is stronger than the weak topology, which is stronger than the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology.
By the Hahn-Banach theorem, a convex subset of
{\mathcal{D}}
is strong closed if and only if it is weakly closed, but a strongly closed subspace of
{\mathcal{D}}
may not be
{\mathcal{D}}
-weakly closed.
However, the results we will obtain below hold for any choice of standard topology.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space with nondegenerate Hermitian form, equipped with one of its standard topologies.
A closed real subspace
K\subset{\mathcal{D}}
is called
cyclic
(with respect to the topology) if
K+iK
is dense in
{\mathcal{D}}
, and called
separating
if
K\cap iK=\{0\}
.
A closed real subspace is called
standard
if it is both cyclic and separating.

To a standard subspace
K
, we can associate a densely defined conjugate-linear involution
S_{K}
with domain
D(S_{K})=K+iK
given by
S_{K}(\Phi_{1}+i\Phi_{2})=\Phi_{1}-i\Phi_{2}
for
\Phi_{j}\in K
.
The term involution here means that
S_{K}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}_{D(S_{K})}
.
The subspace
K
is recovered as the fixed points of
S_{K}
.
The following is standard, although we include a proof to emphasize that unitarity is not required.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space with nondegenerate Hermitian form, equipped with one of its standard topologies.
Then the correspondence
K\leftrightarrow S_{K}
induces a bijection between i) standard subspaces
K
of
{\mathcal{D}}
, and 2) closed conjugate-linear involutions
S

Proof.

We need to check that
S_{K}
is closed, and that every closed conjugate linear involution
S
is of the form
S_{K}
.
To the first end, suppose that
\Phi_{n}\in D(S_{K})=K+iK
, that
\Phi_{n}\to\Phi
in
{\mathcal{D}}
, and that
S_{K}\Phi_{n}\to\Psi
.
Let
\Phi_{1,n}=\frac{\Phi_{n}+S_{K}\Phi_{n}}{2}
and
\Phi_{2,n}=\frac{\Phi_{n}-S_{K}\Phi_{n}}{2i}
, and note that
\Phi_{n}=\Phi_{1,n}+i\Phi_{2,n}
.
We then have
S_{K}\Phi_{j,n}=\Phi_{j,n}
, so that
\Phi_{j,n}\in K
.
Moreover
\Phi_{1,n}\to\frac{\Phi+\Psi}{2}
and
\Phi_{2,n}\to\frac{\Phi-\Psi}{2i}
, and these vectors lie in
K
since
K
is closed.
Hence

\Phi=\lim\Phi_{1,n}+i\lim\Phi_{2,n}\in K+iK=D(S_{K}),

and

S_{K}\Phi=\Psi=\lim\Phi_{1,n}-i\lim\Phi_{2,n}=\lim S_{K}\Phi_{n}.

We conclude that
S_{K}
is closed.

To complete the proof, we must show that given a closed densely-defined conjugate-linear involution
S
, the fixed points
K=\operatorname{Ker}(S-1)
are a standard subspace such that
D(S)=K+iK
.
The argument is routine.
If
\Phi\in K\cap iK
, then applying
S
yields
\Phi=-\Phi
and so
\Phi=0
and
K
is separating.
Moreover if
\Phi\in D(S)
then
\frac{\Phi+S\Phi}{2},\frac{\Phi-S\Phi}{2i}\in K
.
Hence
\Phi\in K+iK
, so
D(S)\subset K+iK
.
Since
D(S)
is assumed dense,
K
is cyclic.
Moreover
K
, and thus
K+iK
are contained in
D(S)
by construction, so we evidently have
D(S)=K+iK
as required.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

If
K\subset{\mathcal{D}}
is a real subspace, the
complementary subspace

K^{\prime}
is defined by

K^{\prime}=\{\Phi\in{\mathcal{D}}\,:\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}\,\text{ for all }\Psi\in K\}.

This is the same as the perpendicular subspace to
iK
with respect to the real form
\operatorname{Re}\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
.

As described before Question
4.4
, taking complements does not behave the same as in the Hilbert space case, as even when the form on
{\mathcal{D}}
is positive definite, the complement could be ‘unexpectedly small.’
For this reason, even when
K
is standard the space
K^{\prime}
may fail to be cyclic, and even if
K^{\prime}
is standard we may have
K\subsetneq K^{\prime\prime}
.
We can, however, establish some basic results below in Lemma
5.5
.
First, the reader is reminded that the relation between a conjugate-linear operator
X
and its adjoint
X^{*}
is

\langle X\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle X^{*}\Psi,\Phi\rangle,

and the definition of the domain
D(X^{*})
is adjusted accordingly.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space with nondegenerate Hermitian form, equipped with one of its standard topologies.
Let
K\subset{\mathcal{D}}
be a closed real subspace.

(i)

If
K
is cyclic then
K^{\prime}
is separating, and if
K^{\prime}
is cyclic then
K
is separating.

(ii)

If
K
is standard, then
K^{\prime}
is standard if and only if
S_{K}^{*}
is densely defined, in which case
S_{K^{\prime}}=S_{K}^{*}
.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Suppose
K
is cyclic and
\Phi\in K^{\prime}\cap iK^{\prime}
.
It follows that
\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}\cap i{\mathbb{R}}
for all
\Psi\in K
.
Hence
\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=0
for all
\Psi\in K
, and indeed for all
\Psi\in K+iK
.
Since
K
is cyclic and the form is nondegenerate, we conclude that
\Phi=0
and
K^{\prime}
is separating.
On the other hand, if
K^{\prime}
is cyclic, then
K^{\prime\prime}
is separating by the previous work, and so
K\subseteq K^{\prime\prime}
is separating as well.

We now prove (ii).
Assume
K
is standard, so by part (i)
K^{\prime}
is separating.
Since
S_{K}
is an involution,
D(S_{K}^{*})
is invariant under
S_{K}^{*}
and
S_{K}^{*}
is an involution as well.
Let
\tilde{K}=\operatorname{ker}(S_{K}^{*}-1)
, and arguing as before yields
D(S_{K}^{*})=\tilde{K}+i\tilde{K}
.
Thus to prove (ii) it suffices to prove that
\tilde{K}=K^{\prime}
.
Indeed for
\Phi\in K
and
\Psi\in{\mathcal{D}}
we have

\langle S_{K}\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\overline{\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle}.

Hence if
\Psi\in K^{\prime}
we see
\langle S_{K}\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle
, which shows that
\Psi\in D(S_{K})^{*}
and
S_{K}^{*}\Psi=1
, i.e.,
\Psi\in\tilde{K}
.
Conversly, if
\Psi\in\tilde{K}
then
\langle S_{K}\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle
, and we see that
\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}
, so that
\Psi\in K^{\prime}
.
We conclude
K^{\prime}=\tilde{K}
, completing the proof of (ii).
∎

We note that even when
K
and
K^{\prime}
are both standard, we have not ruled out the possibility that
K\subsetneq K^{\prime\prime}
and
S_{K}\subsetneq S_{K}^{**}
.
Typically one would expect this if
K
were a standard subspace for the strong topology on
{\mathcal{D}}
but not
{\mathcal{D}}
-weakly closed, as
K^{\prime\prime}
would contain the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak closure.

We now return to our study of involutive Wightman CFTs
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{D}},U,\Omega)
.

Definition 5.6
.

Given an interval
I
, we denote by
{\mathcal{P}}(I)_{sa}\subset{\mathcal{P}}(I)
the real subspace of self-adjoint elements, and the corresponding real subspace of
{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted
{\mathcal{K}}(I):=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(I)_{sa}\Omega}
, where the closure is taken in the strong (or equivalently weak) topology.
We denote by
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
the closure of
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
in the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology.

The reader is reminded that if
I\subset S^{1}
is an interval, then
I^{\prime}
denotes the complementary interval.

Lemma 5.7
.

For each interval
I
, the subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
and
{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
are standard subspaces of
{\mathcal{D}}
, and
{\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
.
The same holds for the subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Observe that
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
contains
{\mathcal{P}}(I)\Omega
, which is dense by the Reeh-Schlieder theorem, and so each
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
is cyclic.
If
x\in{\mathcal{P}}(I)_{sa}
and
y\in{\mathcal{P}}(I^{\prime})_{sa}
, then locality for the net
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
implies that

\langle x\Omega,y\Omega\rangle=\langle yx\Omega,\Omega\rangle=\langle xy\Omega,\Omega\rangle=\langle y\Omega,x\Omega\rangle=\overline{\langle x\Omega,y\Omega\rangle}.

Hence
\langle x\Omega,y\Omega\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and we see that
{\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
.
Since
{\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime})
is cyclic, so is
{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
whence
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
is separating by Lemma
5.5
.
We have shown that
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
is standard and that
{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
is cyclic, and the fact that
{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
is separating again follows from Lemma
5.5
.

In the case of
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}
, note that
{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}={\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{K}}(I)\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
.
It immediately follows from the above that
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)^{\prime}
is standard, that
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)^{\prime}
, and that
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
is cyclic.
The fact that
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
is separating follows again from Lemma
5.5
.
∎

From here it is straightforward to see that the spaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
each enjoy the following structure:

Definition 5.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space equipped with a jointly continuous nondegenerate Hermitian form and a strongly continuous untiary representation
U:\Mob\to\End({\mathcal{D}})
.
A
(non-unitary) Möbius-covariant net of standard subspaces
is a collection of standard subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)\subset{\mathcal{D}}
such that the following holds:

1)

Isotony: If
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{1})\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}(I_{2})
.

2)

Möbius covariance:
U(\gamma){\mathcal{K}}(I)={\mathcal{K}}(\gamma(I))
.

3)

Locality:
{\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
.

Remark 5.9
.

Note that if the subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
are only assumed cyclic then standardness follows from locality, as in the proof of Lemma
5.7
.
One could hope to replace cyclicity of each
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
with a weaker condition of being jointly cyclic. The question of whether this a priori weaker definition is equivalent is asking whether the Reeh-Schlieder phenomenon occurs, but we do not pursue this question.
Without any positive energy assumption on
U
, one should not expect to deduce too much from axioms presented in Definition
5.8
; we include the definition to capture the structure we observe in our example.

We have thus proven:

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{D}},U,\Omega)
be an involutive Wightman CFT on
S^{1}
.
Then the family of subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(I)_{sa}\Omega}
is a Möbius-covariant net of standard subspaces of
{\mathcal{D}}
with respect to the representation
U
of
\Mob
.
The same holds for the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak closures
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
of
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
.

Proof.

The standardness of the subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)
and the locality axiom are Lemma
5.7
, and the other requirements are immediate from properties of the algebras
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
.
∎

A key question which arises in this context, and has been extensively studied in various guises, is that of duality.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space with nondegenerate Hermitian form.
A Möbius-covariant net of standard subspaces satisfies
Haag duality
if
{\mathcal{K}}(I^{\prime})={\mathcal{K}}(I)^{\prime}
for all intervals
I
.
We call the net
weakly Haag dual
if the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak closures
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)=\overline{{\mathcal{K}}(I)}
are Haag dual.

In the context of positive energy nets of standard subspaces of a Hilbert space, Haag duality is automatic
[
Lon08a
, Thm. 3.3.1]
.
We now investigate the question of Haag duality for the nets of standard subspaces arising from a Wightman CFT.

Lemma 5.12
.

(i)

As unbounded operators,
\theta V_{\pm i\pi}\theta=V_{\mp i\pi}
and
V_{i\pi}^{-1}=V_{-i\pi}
.

(ii)

The operators
S_{\pm}=\theta V_{\pm i\pi}
are closed conjugate-linear involutions (with respect to every standard topology) and
S_{\pm}^{*}=S_{\mp}
.

(iii)

If
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
is the standard subspace corresponding to
S_{\pm}
, then
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}^{\prime}={\mathcal{L}}_{\mp}
,
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I_{\pm})\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
, and
V_{t}{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}={\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\theta
maps
D(V_{i\pi})
into
D(V_{-i\pi})
.
Indeed if
\Phi\in D(V_{i\pi})
and
\Phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
, then we have a map
G_{\Phi^{\prime}}:{\mathbb{S}}_{\pi}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that is continuous and holomorphic in the interior, and

G_{\Phi^{\prime}}(t)=\langle V_{t}\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad G_{\Phi^{\prime}}(t+i\pi)=\langle V_{t}V_{i\pi}\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle.

Define
H_{\Phi^{\prime}}:{\mathbb{S}}_{-\pi}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by
H_{\Phi^{\prime}}(\tau)=\overline{G_{\theta\Phi^{\prime}}(\overline{\tau})}
.
Since
\theta
commutes with
V_{t}
, we have

H_{\Phi^{\prime}}(t)=\overline{\langle V_{t}\Phi,\theta\Phi^{\prime}\rangle}=\langle V_{t}\theta\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle

and similarly

H_{\Phi^{\prime}}(t-i\pi)=\overline{\langle V_{t}V_{i\pi}\Phi,\theta\Phi^{\prime}\rangle}=\langle V_{t}\theta V_{i\pi}\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle.

Hence
\theta\Phi\in D(V_{-i\pi})
and
V_{-i\pi}\theta\Phi=\theta V_{i\pi}\Phi
.
The same argument shows that if
\Phi\in D(V_{-i\pi})
then
\theta\Phi\in D(V_{i\pi})
and
\theta V_{-i\pi}\Phi=V_{i\pi}\theta\Phi
.
Since
\theta
is an involution and maps
D(V_{\pm i\pi})
into
D(V_{\mp i\pi})
, we actually must have
\theta D(V_{\pm i\pi})=D(V_{\mp i\pi})
, and so from our previous calculations
\theta V_{\pm i\pi}\theta=V_{\mp i\pi}
.
A very similar calculation, this time defining
H_{\Phi^{\prime}}(\tau)=G_{\Phi^{\prime}}(\tau-i\pi)
, shows that
V_{\pm i\pi}V_{\mp i\pi}=\mathrm{Id}_{D(V_{\mp i\pi})}
, completing (i).

From part (i), we see that
S_{\pm}^{2}=V_{\mp i\pi}V_{\pm i\pi}=\mathrm{Id}_{D(\pm i\pi)}=\mathrm{Id}_{D(S_{\pm})}
, and so
S_{\pm}
are conjugate-linear involutions.
The maps
V_{\pm i\pi}
are closed for the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology (and so for every standard topology) since adjoints are automatically
{\mathcal{D}}
-weakly closed and
V_{\pm i\pi}
are self-adjoint.
Hence
S_{\pm}
are closed as well, and we have

S_{\pm}^{*}=V_{\pm i\pi}\theta=\theta V_{\mp i\pi}=S_{\mp}

where we note that the first equality is not immediate but easy to verify. This completes (ii).

The identity
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}^{\prime}={\mathcal{L}}_{\mp}
now follows from Lemma
5.5
.
By definition
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
consists of fixed points of
S_{\pm}
, and so
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{\pm})_{sa}\Omega\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
by Theorem
4.2
.
Taking closures yields
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
.
Finally note that
V_{t}
commutes with both
\theta
and
V_{\pm i\pi}
, and so with
S_{\pm}
.
Hence
V_{t}
leaves
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
invariant for all
t
, which implies that
V_{t}{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}={\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

The net of standard subspaces
{\mathcal{K}}(I)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(I)_{sa}\Omega}
satisfies Haag duality if and only if
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})\Omega
is a core for
V_{i\pi}
for the
{\mathcal{F}}
-strong topology.
Similarly
{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(I)
satisfies Haag duality if and only if
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})\Omega
is a core for
V_{i\pi}
for the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})\Omega
is a
{\mathcal{F}}
-strong core for
V_{i\pi}
then
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{+})={\mathcal{L}}_{+}
, and applying
\theta
yields
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{-})={\mathcal{L}}_{-}
.
Hence by Lemma
5.12
we have
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})^{\prime}={\mathcal{K}}(I_{\mp})
.
Haag duality then follows from Möbius covariance.
For the converse, suppose the net is Haag dual, which is to say
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})^{\prime}={\mathcal{K}}(I_{\mp})
.
By Lemma
5.12
we have

{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}={\mathcal{L}}_{\mp}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\mp})^{\prime}={\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm}).

As the first and last terms coincide, we have
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})={\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
and so
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{+})
is a
{\mathcal{F}}
-strong core for
V_{i\pi}
.
The proof for the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology is similar.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

We see from the proof of Corollary
5.13
that the key question for (weak) Haag duality is whether the inclusion
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
is an equality.
This is an inclusion of standard subspaces, the involution
S_{\pm}
satisfies
S_{\pm}=\theta V_{\pm i\pi}
, and
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})
is invariant under
V_{t}
.
In the unitary setting the identity
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{\pm})={\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
would follow (see e.g.
[
Lon08a
, Prop. 2.1.10]
), but we do not see an immediate generalization to our context.

Remark 5.15
.

In light of Lemma
5.12
, one could alternatively try to construct a Haag dual Möbius covariant net of standard subspaces as follows.
Given an interval
I
, choose
\gamma\in\Mob
such that
\gamma(I_{+})=I
, and set
{\mathcal{L}}(I)=U(\gamma){\mathcal{L}}_{+}
.
Since
\gamma(I_{+})=I_{+}
exactly when
\gamma=v_{t}
for some
t
, and
V_{t}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}={\mathcal{L}}_{+}
, the subspace
{\mathcal{L}}(I)
does not depend on the choice of
\gamma
, and in particular
{\mathcal{L}}(I_{+})={\mathcal{L}}_{+}
.
If
r_{\pi}\in\Mob
is rotation by
\pi
, then
U(r_{\pi})V_{t}U(r_{\pi})^{-1}=V_{-t}
and it is then straightforward to check that
U(r_{\pi})V_{i\pi}U(r_{\pi})^{-1}=V_{-i\pi}
. Hence
{\mathcal{L}}(I_{-})={\mathcal{L}}_{-}
as well.
This collection of standard subspaces is Haag dual (in particular, local) and Möbius covariant, but does not visibly satisfy isotony.

We conclude this section with a discussion of Haag duality for nets of algebras.
As with standard subspaces, we have a preliminary definition of a local net of algebras (for which one does not expect a robust general theory, in part because of the lack of a positive energy condition).

Definition 5.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex space equipped with a jointly continuous nondegenerate Hermitian form, a strongly continuous untiary representation
U:\Mob\to\End({\mathcal{D}})
, and a choice of non-zero vector
\Omega\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
A
(non-unitary) Möbius-covariant net of algebras
is a collection of unital
*
-subalgebras
{\mathcal{Q}}(I)\subset\End_{*}({\mathcal{D}})
such that the following holds:

1)

Isotony: If
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
, then

We will verify that Haag duality occurs under a pair of reasonable-looking (but presumably difficult to verify) conditions.
The two arguments share an important technical (but standard) step, which we distil into the following lemma.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a locally convex vector space equipped with a jointly continuous nondegenerate Hermitian form and let
\Omega\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Let
A,B\subset\End_{*}({\mathcal{D}})
be unital
*
-subalgebras, and let
\theta
be an antiunitary involution on
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\theta\Omega=\Omega
.
Suppose that the following hold:

(i)

Suppose that for all
X,Y\in A_{sa}
we have
\langle\theta X\Omega,Y\Omega\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii)

Suppose
B\Omega
is
{\mathcal{D}}
-weakly dense in
{\mathcal{D}}
, that
B
commutes with
A
, and that
\theta B\theta\subset A
.

Then we have
\theta A\theta\subset A^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
X,Y\in A_{sa}
, so that by assumption we have
\langle\theta X\Omega,Y\Omega\rangle=\langle Y\Omega,\theta X\Omega\rangle.

Since
\theta\Omega=\Omega
, this becomes

\langle\theta X\theta Y\Omega,\Omega\rangle=\langle Y\theta X\theta\Omega,\Omega\rangle.

Both sides of the above identity are conjugate linear in
X
and linear in
Y
, so it extends to all
X,Y\in A
.
If
S,T\in B
, then
\theta S\theta,\theta T\theta\in A
.
Thus we apply the above identity with
X
replaced by
\theta T\theta X\theta S\theta
to obtain

\langle T\theta X\theta SY\Omega,\Omega\rangle=\langle YT\theta X\theta S\Omega,\Omega\rangle

for all
X,Y\in A
and
S,T\in B
.
Since
S
and
T
commute with
Y
, this becomes

\langle(\theta X\theta)YS\Omega,T^{*}\Omega\rangle=\langle Y(\theta X\theta)S\Omega,T^{*}\Omega\rangle.

Now for arbitrary
\Phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
choose a net
T_{m}^{*}\Omega\in B\Omega
such that
T_{m}^{*}\Omega\to\Phi^{\prime}
in the
{\mathcal{D}}
-weak topology.
Taking the limit of the identity

\langle(\theta X\theta)YS\Omega,T^{*}_{m}\Omega\rangle=\langle Y(\theta X\theta)S\Omega,T^{*}_{m}\Omega\rangle.

yields

\langle(\theta X\theta)YS\Omega,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle Y(\theta X\theta)S\Omega,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle.

Now for arbitrary
\Phi\in{\mathcal{D}}
choose a net
S_{n}\Omega\in B\Omega
such that
S_{n}\Omega\to\Phi
.
We now have

\langle S_{n}\Omega,[(\theta X\theta)Y]^{*}\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle(\theta X\theta)YS_{n}\Omega,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle Y(\theta X\theta)S_{n}\Omega,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle S_{n}\Omega,[Y(\theta X\theta)]^{*}\Phi^{\prime}\rangle.

Taking the limit yields

\langle(\theta X\theta)Y\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle Y(\theta X\theta)\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\rangle.

Since
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
were arbitrary, we see that
\theta X\theta
and
Y
commute.
Hence
\theta X\theta\in A^{\prime}
, which completes the proof.
∎

That is, the topology of

The real subspaces
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}(I)_{sa}\Omega}
are a Möbius-covariant net of standard subspaces of the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
The modular conjugation corresponding to the upper semicircle is
\theta|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}}
.

Proof.

We verify that these subspaces satisfy Definition
2.3
.
Isotony of the net is clear, as is the spectrum condition.
The algebras
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I)
are Möbius covariant with respect to the given unitary representation on
{\mathcal{H}}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is invariant under the Möbius group, and that the algebras
{\mathcal{C}}(I)
are Möbius covariant, and indeed that the subspaces
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}(I)_{sa}\Omega}
are as well.
It remains to check the cyclicity condition.
Let
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
be the closed span of the subspaces
{\mathcal{C}}(I)\Omega
.
Then evidently the given subspaces form a Möbius-covariant net of subspaces of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then by the Reeh-Schlieder property
[
Lon08a
, Thm. 3.2.1]
we have
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}(I)\Omega}
for each interval
I
.
Hence
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is invariant under each algebra
{\mathcal{C}}(I)
.
It follows that
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is invariant under
\bigvee_{I}{\mathcal{C}}(I)
, from which it follows that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Hence
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}=\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
, which completes the proof of the locality axiom.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a unitary Möbius vertex algebra with PCT operator
\theta
, let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I)={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I^{\prime})^{\prime}
be the dual net of von Neumann algebras, and let
{\mathcal{C}}(I)
be the cutdown of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I)
to the vacuum Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
, as described above.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is a a Möbius-covariant Haag-Kastler net on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
The modular conjugation associated with
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
and
\Omega
is
\theta|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}}
.

Proof.

The only axiom which needs to be checked is locality.
This will follow from the assertion regarding the modular conjugation, which we now verify.
Let
{\mathcal{N}}=\overline{{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})_{sa}\Omega}
, which is a standard subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}
by Lemma
6.5
.
We note that it follows that
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
.
By definition
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I_{+})
commutes with
{\mathcal{P}}(I_{-})
, and therefore
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})_{sa}\Omega\subset\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(I_{-})^{\prime}=\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(I_{+})
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(I_{+})
.

From the theory of Möbius covariant nets of subspaces we know that the modular automorphism group of
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(I_{+})
is
\Delta_{+}^{it}=U(v_{-2\pi t})

[
Lon08a
, Thm. 3.3.1]
.
As
{\mathcal{N}}
is invariant under
\Delta_{+}^{it}
, the modular automorphism group for
{\mathcal{N}}
is
\Delta_{+}^{it}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{N}}}

[
Lon08a
, Cor. 2.1.8]
.
Let
S_{\mathcal{N}}
be the involution on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{N}}
corresponding to
{\mathcal{N}}
, and let
S_{\mathcal{N}}=J_{\mathcal{N}}\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{1/2}
be the polar decomposition.
For
\xi\in D(\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{1/2})
, the map
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{it}\xi
extends to a continuous function on the strip
\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\leq 0\}
, holomorphic on the interior, mapping
\tau=-i/2
to
\Delta^{1/2}_{\mathcal{N}}\xi
.
As the same holds for
\Delta_{+}^{it}
, we have
\Delta^{1/2}_{\mathcal{N}}\xi=\Delta^{1/2}_{+}\xi
for
\xi\in{\mathcal{N}}+i{\mathcal{N}}
, and in particular for
\xi
of the form
x\Omega
with
x\in{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
.
Thus if
x\in{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
we have

J_{\mathcal{N}}x^{*}\Omega=\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{1/2}x\Omega=\Delta_{+}^{1/2}x\Omega=\theta x^{*}\Omega

with the last equality by Theorem
6.3
.
Hence by Reeh-Schlieder (which in this case follows from Lemma
6.5
) we have
J_{\mathcal{N}}=\theta|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{N}}}
, proving our claim about the modular conjugation for
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
.

We now complete the proof of locality, for which it suffices to show that
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})
and
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{-})
commute.
By the main theorem of Tomita-Takesaki theory, we have
\theta{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})\theta={\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, since
\theta{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I_{\pm})\theta={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I_{\mp})
, it follows that
\theta{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I_{+})\theta={\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I_{-})
.
Since
\theta
leaves
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{N}}
invariant we have
\theta{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})\theta={\mathcal{C}}(I_{-})
.
Hence
{\mathcal{C}}(I_{+})^{\prime}={\mathcal{C}}(I_{-})
, completing the proof of locality.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a unitary Möbius vertex algebra with vacuum vector
\Omega
and PCT operator
\theta
. Then the following are equivalent:

1)

{\mathcal{V}}
is AQFT-local

2)

\Omega
is separating for
{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(I)
, for some (or any) interval
I

If the above hold, then
\theta
is the modular conjugation for
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{+})
with respect to the vacuum state.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{V}}
is AQFT-local, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I)
is a Möbius-covariant Haag-Kastler net, and
\Omega
is separating for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I)
by the Reeh-Schlieder property.
Conversely, if
\Omega
is separating, then it is cyclic for
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{H}}(I)={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I^{\prime})^{\prime}
, and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{H}}
.
Hence by Theorem
6.6
,
{\mathcal{C}}(I)
is a Möbius-covariant Haag Kastler net on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and by Haag duality for that net we have
{\mathcal{C}}(I)={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{V}}(I)
, completing the proof.
The computation of the modular conjugation follows from Theorem
6.6
as well.
∎

We note that the claim about the modular conjugation could have alternatively been proven directly from
[
CKLW18
, Thm. B.6]
, under the assumption of energy bounds.

Murmurations and Sato-Tate Conjectures for High Rank Zetas of Elliptic Curves II: Beyond Riemann Hypothesis
Zhan Shi and Lin WENG

Abstract
. As a continuation of our earlier paper [ 6 ] , we offer a new approach to murmurations and Sato-Tate laws for higher rank zetas of elliptic curves. Our approach here does not depend on the Riemann hypothesis for the so-called a a -invariant a E / 𝔽 p ; n a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n} in rank n ( ≥ 3 ) n(\geq 3) even for the Sato-Tate law, rather, on a much refined structure, a similar version of which was already observed earlier by Zagier and the senior author of this paper in [ 12 ] when the rank n n Riemann hypothesis was established. Namely, instead of the rank n n Riemann hypothesis bounds − 1 ≤ a E / 𝔽 p ; n 2 ​ p n ≤ 1 -1\leq\frac{a^{~{}^{~}}_{E/\mathbb{F}_{p};n}}{2\sqrt{p^{n}}}\leq 1 on which our first paper is based, we use the asymptotic bounds − 1 ≤ a E / 𝔽 p ; n + ( n − 1 ) p + ( n − 5 ) 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ p ≤ 1 -1\leq\frac{a^{~{}^{~}}_{E/\mathbb{F}_{p};n}+(n-1)p+(n-5)}{2(n-1)\sqrt{p}}\leq 1 . Accordingly, rank n n Sato-Tate law can be established and rank n n murmurations can be formulated equally well, similar to the corresponding structures in the abelian framework for Artin zetas of elliptic curves.

Non-Abelian Zeta Function: Background

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
as stated in the Introduction. However, even it is very natural to use the Riemann hypothesis, or equivalently, the bounds

-1\leq\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}}{2\sqrt{Q}_{n}}\leq 1,
to introduce
\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}\in[0,\pi]
via

\cos\,\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}:=\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}}{2\sqrt{Q}_{n}}

for an elliptic curve
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
, one easily verify that the corresponding
\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}};n}
’s have an obvious limit point
\frac{\pi}{2}
when
n\geq 3
.
This then yields the first structural distributions of the Dirac symbol
\delta_{\pi/2}
for the
\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}};n}
’s. Unfortunately,
\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}};n}-\frac{\pi}{2}
is too tiny to be observed. Motivated by Theorem
12
, a huge multiplicative factor
\sqrt{p_{i}^{n-1}}
should be introduced so that the secondary level distributions of
\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}};n}
can be studied. However, with this enlargement, a further blow-up of additive scale
-(n-1)p_{i}
is automatically introduced. It is for the purpose to eliminate this new complication, a term of
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{p_{i}}
is added, and hence to arrive finally at
the normalized big
\Delta
-distributions:

\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{p}\cos{\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},2}}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sqrt{p}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\Big)&n=2\\
&\\
\frac{\sqrt{p^{n-1}}}{n-1}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{p}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{n-5}{(n-1)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}&n\geq 3.\end{cases}

(17)

In Theorem 4 of
[
6
]
, we are able to establish the following:

Theorem 13

(First Version of Sato-Tate Law in Rank
n
)
.

Fix a natural number
n\geq 2
. Let
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
be a non-CM elliptic curve. For
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\pi
, we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \cos\alpha\geq\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\geq\cos\beta\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta.

Our proof of this theorem is based on Taylor and his collaborators’ works on the classical Sato-Tate law on the abelian
a
-invariants
a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
’s.

3.
Sato-Tate Law in Rank
n
: Beyond Riemann Hypothesis

3.1.
Riemann Hypothesis is Too Rough

Recall that (
13
) claims that

2>a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}>-2\sqrt{Q}_{n}\quad(\forall n\geq 2).

This is clearly much strong than the bounds

\left|\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}}{2\sqrt{Q}_{n}}\right|\leq 1,

which is well known to be equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis in rank
n
.
This has already been observed in
[
12
]
.

Our first paper on the rank
n
Sato-Tate law is based on the rank
n
Riemann hypothesis. This leads to, as mentioned above, a huge multiplicative factor
\sqrt{p^{n-1}}
, together with a further blowing up of additive scale
\sqrt{p}
, in the definition of the big normalized
\Delta
-distribution above.
Since this approach is guided by the Riemann hypothesis in rank
n
, when such a big

\Delta
was introduced in
[
6
]
, or much earlier in
[
10
]
, we thought, at the moment, that this approach was extremely natural.

However, recently, after examining the structures involved in the big
\Delta
more carefully, we find out that
it is rather artificial to use

\frac{\sqrt{p^{n-1}}}{n-1}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\Big)

in the definition of
\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}
, since after all,
\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\Big)
is rather tiny, and hence should be of the same scale as

\sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\Big)=\cos\,\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}=\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{2\sqrt{p^{n}}}.

Or equivalently

\frac{\sqrt{p^{n-1}}}{n-1}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}\Big)=\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{2\sqrt{p}(n-1)}.

which, in terms of Theorem
13
, or better (
15
), simply means that, up to normalization, the big
\Delta
-distribution in rank
n\geq 3
is essentially nothing but
\frac{\alpha_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{2\sqrt{p}}
.
Accordingly, for a genuine structure in rank
n
, we are led to the following new yet more direct approach to the rank
n
Sato-Tate law.

For simplicity, in the sequel, unless otherwise is stated, let assume that
n\geq 3
.
Then, by (
15
), we have as
q\to\infty
,

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n}=(5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},1}-(n-1)q+O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\Big).

Therefore, we set now

\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2(n-1)\sqrt{p}}\Big(a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n}+(n-1)p+(n-5)\Big)

and when

\big|\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}^{\prime}\big|\leq 1
,
we set

\cos\,\Theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}^{\prime}=\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}^{\prime}.

The up-shot is the following:

Theorem 14
.

Let
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
be a non-CM elliptic curve. Denote its
p
-reduction by
E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then, for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\pi
, we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Big\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \alpha\leq\Theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}^{\prime}\leq\beta\Big\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta.

Proof.

Note that, by Theorem
12
, or better (
15
), asymptotically, as
p\to\infty
,

\big|\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p};n}^{\prime}\big|\leq 1
. Hence by Theorem
12
, or better (
15
) again, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Big\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \alpha\leq\Theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}^{\prime}\leq\beta\Big\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Big\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \cos\alpha\geq\Delta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}^{\prime}\geq\cos\beta\Big\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Big\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \cos\alpha\geq\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{2\sqrt{p}}\geq\cos\beta\Big\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}

\displaystyle(\text{by Theorem\,\ref{thm6}, or better \eqref{eq10}, based on the prime number theorem)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta

Here in the last step, we have used the result of Taylor and his collaborators (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
) on the classical Sato-Tate law.
∎

3.2.
Sato-Tate Law in Rank
n
: New Observing Spot

After introducing the big
\Delta^{\prime}
-distributions, our calculations indicates that in fact the difference

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},n}-\Big((5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},1}-(n-1)p_{i}\Big)

for large
i
is oscillating between

\pm\frac{6}{\sqrt{p_{i}}}
.

At the beginning, we thought that there might be a new type of distribution law
hidden behind this. However, through more detailed computations, we witness that, numerically for all examples,

\cos\,\Theta_{E/F_{q};n}^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}-\Big((5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-(n-1)q\Big)}{-6/\sqrt{q}}

or better, these new
\Theta_{E/F_{p_{i}};n}^{\prime\prime}
obey the classical Sato-Tate law.
In fact, we have the following

Theorem 15
.

Let
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
be a non-CM elliptic curve. Denote its
p
-reduction by
E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then, for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\pi
, we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Big\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime},\ \alpha\leq\Theta_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p},n}^{\prime\prime}\leq\beta\Big\}}{\#\{p\leq N:p:\ {\rm prime}\}}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta\quad(\forall n\geq 3).

Proof.

We start with the following

Theorem 16
.

Let
E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be an elliptic curve. Then for a fixed
n\geq 3
, we have

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}=(5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-(n-1)q-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right).

(21)

Proof.

We use an induction on
n
.
Recall that from Theorem
11
,

(q^{n}-1){\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}(0)=(q^{n}+q^{n-1}-a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}){\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n-1}(0)-(q^{n-1}-q){\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n-2}(0),

with the initial conditions
{\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};0}(0)=1
and
{\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};-1}(0)=0
.
By definition,

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}:=(q^{n}+1)-(q^{n}-1)\frac{{\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}(0)}{\alpha_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}(0)}.

Thus, from the counting miracle that

\alpha_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}(0)={\beta}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n-1}(0),

we arrive at the structural recursion formula for
a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}
:

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n+1}=1-q^{n}+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+\frac{(q^{n}-q)(q^{n}-1)}{q^{n}+1-a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n}}

(22)

In particular,

\displaystyle a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},2}=

\displaystyle 1-q+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

(23)

\displaystyle a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},3}=

\displaystyle 1-q^{2}+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+\frac{(q^{2}-q)(q^{2}-1)}{q^{2}+1-a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-q^{2}+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+\frac{(q^{2}-q)(q^{2}-1)}{q^{2}+q-a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2+2a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},1}-2q-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right).

This verifies (
21
) when
n=3
.

Now assume that
(
21
) holds for
n
. By (
22
), we have

\displaystyle a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n+1}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-q^{n}+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+\frac{(q^{n}-q)(q^{n}-1)}{q^{n}+1-a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-q^{n}+a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+\frac{(q^{n}-q)(q^{n}-1)}{q^{n}+1-(5-n)-(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+(n-1)q+3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4-n)+na_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-nq-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)

by a tedious, long yet trivial, calculation.
∎

From Theorem
16
, namely, (
21
),
we have

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}=(5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-(n-1)q-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right).

Hence

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}+(n-5)-(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+(n-1)q=-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right).

Therefore, asymptotically,

\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}+(n-5)-(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}+(n-1)q}{-6/\sqrt{q}}=\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{2\sqrt{q}}.

Therefore, our Theorem is equivalent to the classical Sato-Tate law for non-CM elliptic curve
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
established by Taylor and his collaborators (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
).
∎

We end this section by the following comments: The Sato-Tate laws in higher ranks can be observed from various spots, which have quite different geo-arithmetic meanings. These Sato-Tate laws exposes what should be called the secondary structures behind the Riemann hypothesis, and sometime are much refined than the Riemann hypothesis in higher ranks. By contrast, all these Sato-Tate laws in higher ranks are essentially unique – they are canonically equivalent to the canonical one.

4.
Rank
n
Murmurations for Elliptic Curves

To understand rank
n
murmurations for elliptic curves
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
’s of arithmetic rank
r
, in
[
6
]
, based on a weak version of (
21
) in Theorem
16
, namely,

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}=(5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-(n-1)q+O\left(\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}\right),

the following functional
f_{r,n}(i)
on the rank
n
average value is introduced in
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle f_{r,n}(i)

(25)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]}\times\!\!\!\!\sum_{E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]}\begin{cases}a^{~}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},1}&n=1\\
a^{~}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},2}+p_{i}-1&n=2\\
\frac{1}{n-1}\cdot\big(a^{~}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},n}+(n-1)p_{i}+n-5\big)&n\geq 3\end{cases}

where
N_{1},N_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
satisfying
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
, and
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]
denotes the set of elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
of arithmetic rank
r
with the conductor in the interval
[N_{1},N_{2}]
.
Here, as in the rank one case, for each isogeny class of elliptic curves
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
, only a single representative elliptic curve is selected in
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]
.

Next, we use Theorem
16
, namely, a stronger estimate

a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q};n}=(5-n)+(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}-(n-1)q-3\frac{a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}{q}+O\left(\frac{1}{q}\right),

to introduce, for
n\geq 3
, a new murmuration functional

f_{r,n}^{\rm new}(i)
by
setting

\displaystyle f_{r,n}^{\rm new}(i)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]}

(26)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{r}[N_{1},N_{2}]}\Big(a^{~}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}},n}+(n-1)p_{i}-(n-1)a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}}}+(n-5)\Big)\frac{-p_{i}}{3}.

Then with the same method as in
[
6
]
, we have the following

Theorem 17

(Rank
n
Murmurations)
.

For families of a regular (integral) elliptic curves
{\mathbb{E}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 0.59998pt
’s, when plotting the points
(i,f_{r,n}^{\rm new}(i))

(i\geq 1,n\geq 3)
in the sufficiently large range, the murmuration phenomenon appears in exactly the same way as that for the
(i,f_{r,1}(i))
’s.

Proof.

The same proof for Theorem  4 of
[
6
]
works here as well since essentially, what
f_{r,n}^{\rm new}(i)
really counts is
a_{E/{\mathbb{F}}_{p_{i}}}
by Theorem
16
.
∎

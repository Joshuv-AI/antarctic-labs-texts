Symmetric Log-epiperimetric Inequality for Harmonic Maps with Analytic Target and Applications
Riccardo Caniato, Davide Parise

Abstract
. We establish a direct symmetric (log)-epiperimetric inequality for harmonic maps with analytic target and we leverage on this result to achieve a new proof of Simon’s celebrated uniqueness of tangents with isolated singularity for energy minimizing harmonic maps. Moreover, we show that tangents at infinity of energy minimizing harmonic maps with suitably controlled energy growth are always unique, by exploiting the lower bound entailed in the symmetric (log)-epiperimetric inequality.



1.
Introduction

Epiperimetric inequalities in geometric variational problems

Given a geometric functional
\mathscr{F}
on a domain with boundary—interpreted as the “energy” of the problem—epiperimetric type inequalities provide a precise quantitative estimate on the energy sub-optimality of the natural homogeneous extension of a given boundary datum inside the domain. This information allows to obtain rates of decay, or growth, for the energy density of
\mathscr{F}
-minimizers, thus providing a suitable framework to study the uniqueness of their tangents either at a point or at infinity.

Introduced by Reifenberg in his seminal work
[
Rei64a
]
, epiperimetric inequalities have played a central role in the study of minimal surfaces. In particular, they were originally developed to establish the analyticity of solutions to the Plateau problem, in the form posed in
[
Rei64b
]
. Later on, White exploited this technique in
[
Whi83
]
to prove uniqueness of tangent cones for two-dimensional area-minimizing integral currents without boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Building on these ideas, Rivière introduced in
[
Riv04
]
the concept of a
lower
epiperimetric inequality, thereby establishing a rate of decay for the decreasing density of an area-minimizing integral
2
-cycle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One of the key insights arising from Rivière’s work was the identification of the
splitting before tilting
phenomenon, a concept that proved essential in his joint regularity results with Tian for
1-1
integral cycles
[
RT09
]
. This notion has since been extensively applied in the regularity theory developed by De Lellis, Spadaro, and Spolaor, who extended these techniques to area-minimizing and semicalibrated currents (see
[
DLS16
,
DLSS17a
,
Spo19
]
). More recently, in the context of two-dimensional almost minimal currents, these authors established a new epiperimetric inequality
[
DLSS17b
]
, thereby generalizing White’s earlier results.

Subsequently, epiperimetric inequalities have attracted considerable attention in the setting of free boundary problems, in particular due to the efforts of Spolaor and Velichkov. For instance, in
[
SV19
]
, these two authors adapted
direct
epiperimetric inequalities for various free boundary problems in two dimensions.
Later, in collaboration with Colombo, the same authors proved a
logarithmic
epiperimetric inequality for obstacle-type problems
[
CSV18
,
CSV20a
]
. Another significant advancement came in
[
ESV20
]
, where Engelstein, Spolaor, and Velichkov introduced a novel method of proof: by reducing the problem to an estimate for a functional defined on the sphere and analyzing its gradient flow, they offered a new perspective on proving such inequalities. While their focus was on the Alt-Caffarelli functional, this approach has since been instrumental in the analysis of multiplicity-one stationary cones with isolated singularities
[
ESV19
]
, leading to new
\varepsilon
-regularity theorems for almost minimizers. Besides, the same method was exploited by the authors of the present paper to prove uniqueness of tangents for Yang–Mills connections with isolated singularities
[
CP24
]
. For further developments along these lines, see also
[
SV21
]
. Notably, in
[
ESV24
]
, the authors introduced the notion of
symmetric
logarithmic epiperimetric inequality for the Alt-Caffarelli functional and for almost minimizing currents, which entails at once a bi-later (upper and lower) control on the energy density.

A unifying feature of these works is their reliance on
direct
constructions of competitors—explicitly built functions that allow one to prove decay estimates around singular points. Such direct methods stand in contrast to the alternative approach of proving epiperimetric inequalities via contradiction, a strategy that typically employs linearization techniques. Contradiction-based proofs have been used effectively in various settings, albeit often limited to regular points or singularities under strong structural assumptions. Notable examples include Taylor’s work on area-minimizing flat chains modulo 3 and
(\mathbf{M},\varepsilon,\delta)
-minimizers
[
Tay73
,
Tay76a
,
Tay76b
]
. For free boundary problems, Weiss’s classical contribution
[
Wei99
]
established epiperimetric inequalities at flat singular points and along the top stratum of the singular set in the obstacle problem. See also
[
Tay77
]
for the very first instance of an epiperimetric inequality for free boundary problems. For the thin obstacle problem, we refer to
[
FS16
,
GPSVG16
]
, while in the context of harmonic measures, the work of Badger, Engelstein, and Toro
[
BET20
]
inverstigated epiperimetric inequalities for functions that do not minimize any energy.

Statement of the main results

Inspired by
[
ESV19
,
ESV20
,
ESV24
,
CP24
]
, the aim of this article is to prove a symmetric log-epiperimetric inequality for harmonic maps with real-analytic target. To the best of the authors’ knowledge this is the first instance of such an inequality in this geometric setting. More precisely, we prove the following–see Section
2

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed real-analytic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n\geq 3
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
be a harmonic map on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and let
\tilde{u}_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
be its 0-homogeneous extension inside
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, given by

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{0}:=\bigg(\frac{\cdot}{\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert}\bigg)^{*}u_{0}.

There exist constant
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
depending on the dimension and
u_{0}
such that the following holds. If
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
is such that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}<\delta

then there exists
\hat{u}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
such that
\hat{u}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=u
and

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{1+\gamma},

where
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
is the
0

\mathbb{B}^{n}

Remark 1.2
.

We give two different proofs of Theorem
1.1
. The first one follows the strategies outlined in
[
ESV19
,
ESV20
,
ESV24
,
CP24

Besides, it sheds some light on the relation between the (log)-epiperimetric and Łojasiewicz–Simon inequalities by formalising that the latter implies the former under certain hypothesis. This was highlighted in

Remark 1.3
.

Note that in the simple setting of
harmonic functions
, i.e. when

0
-homogeneous extensions as in Theorem

\displaystyle W_{\alpha}(r,x;f)

A quick computation reveals that this quantity is monotone for harmonic functions and by expanding in spherical harmonics one can prove the following: for every
n\geq 2
,
\alpha>0
, there exists
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with the following significance. For
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))
homogeneous of degree
\alpha

, and

the harmonic extension of

to

W_{\alpha}(r,x;f)\leq(1-\varepsilon)W_{\alpha}(r,x;u).

See
[

We note that Theorem
1.1
is purely variational, and does not depend on any underlying PDE. As consequence of it, we obtain an alternative, albeit similar, proof of the celebrated uniqueness of tangent maps with isolated singularities due to Simon
[
Sim83
]
. See also
[
LW08a
, Section 2.5]
, and
[
Sim12
, Section 3.10]
for very elegant proofs of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed real-analytic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set, with
n\geq 3
. Consider
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,N)
an energy minimizing harmonic map. Suppose that
\varphi
is a tangent map at
u
at some point
y\in\Sing(u)
, and assume that
\Sing(\varphi)=0
. Then,
\varphi
is the unique tangent map for
u
at
y
. Furthermore, we have the expansion

u(y+r\omega)=\varphi(\omega)+\epsilon(r,\omega),

for
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, and where the error term
\epsilon
satisfies the following asymptotics

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}|\log(r)|^{\alpha}\sup_{\omega\in S^{n-1}}|\epsilon(r,\omega)|=0,

for some
\alpha>0
.

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.4
is sharp in the following senses. First, the logarithmic decay is the best possible in light of examples constructed in
[
AS88
]
and
[
GW89
]
. On the other hand, the analyticity hypothesis on the target
N
cannot be replaced by smoothness in view of
[
Whi92
]
.

Being a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
, our proof of Theorem
1.4
is again purely variational, contrarily to Simon’s one. Indeed, the arguments in
[
Sim83
]
are based on a careful analysis of the convergence properties of solutions to certain parabolic PDEs, and on growth estimates for the corresponding solutions. This also suggests that the argument used here would carry over for
almost
minimizers, although this notion does not seem to have ever appeared in the literature. Moreover, the lower bound entailed in (
1.1
) immediately gives the following uniqueness of tangents at infinity for energy minimizing harmonic maps with analytic target in any dimension—see Section
6
for a proof.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed real-analytic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},N)
be an energy minimizing harmonic map such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\Lambda\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(0,+\infty).

for some
\Lambda>0
. Assume that
u
has a tangent map
\varphi
at infinity such that
\operatorname{Sing}(\varphi)\subset\{0\}
. Then,
\varphi
is the unique tangent map of
u
at infinity and there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u(\rho\,\cdot\,)-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq C\log(\rho)^{\alpha}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(1,+\infty).

The last few decades have seen remarkable progress on the understanding of the singular set of harmonic maps, especially when it comes to its optimal bound.
In supercritical dimension
n\geq 3
, the sharp bound was established for energy minimizing harmonic maps by Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
SU82
]
, while for stationary harmonic maps, the best available bound on the size of their singular set is given by the subsequent contributions of
[
Eva91
]
,
[
Bet93
]
and
[
RS08
]
. If we drop the stationarity assumption, it is known by work of Rivière in
[
Riv95
]
that harmonic maps on
n
-manifolds with
n\geq 3
can be even everywhere discontinuous and, therefore, no regularity theory is possible in this setting.

On the other hand, understanding under which conditions the tangents to harmonic maps at their singular points are unique is a widely open problem and, so far, very little is known in full generality beyond non-isolated singularities. In the framework of stationary harmonic maps, first Rivière and Tian showed in
[
RT04
]
that strongly approximable pseudoholomorphic maps from a
4
-dimensional almost complex manifold into complex algebraic varieties have a unique tangent map at every point and established the optimal bound on the size of their singular set. Recently, the uniqueness of tangent maps was generalized by the first-named author and Rivière to arbitrary dimension of the domain in
[
CR23
]
. Both these results suggest that uniqueness of tangents is quite a strong property and, therefore, may hold true just under some special geometric assumptions on the structure both of the map and of the target manifold.

More broadly, this uniqueness problem pertains to other geometric PDEs, and it is a central question in geometric analysis. For instance, in the celebrated work
[
Sim83
]
that we mentioned above, Simon also proved a result similar to the one in Theorem
1.4
for stationary varifolds. We refer the reader to the surveys
[
DL22
,
Wic14
]
, and the references therein, for further details on the uniqueness of tangent cones problem in the setting of minimal submanifolds. On the other hand, in the framework of mean curvature flow, Colding and Minicozzi in
[
CM15
]
exploited an infinite dimensional Łojasiewicz inequality to prove a uniqueness result at generic cylindrical singularities. For Einstein manifolds, the same authors proved in
[
CM14
]
that the tangent cone at infinity of a Ricci-flat manifold with Euclidean volume growth is unique, provided one tangent cone at infinity has a smooth cross-section (compare this result with Theorem
1.6
).

Ideas of the proof and structure of the article

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the strategy outlined in
[
ESV19
,
ESV20
,
CP24
]
and it relies on constructing a competitor function with energy smaller than the one of the 0-homogeneous extension of
u_{0}
. We start by rewriting the energy energy discrepancy
\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\cdot\,;\cdot)
in Theorem
1.1
in a more convenient form. This is our slicing lemma, cf. Lemma
3.1
and it is done in Section
3
. There we also recall the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction adapted to the setting of harmonic maps, cf. Lemma
3.2
. In particular, the slicing lemma suggests that we can construct the competitor by flowing inwards the slices of the trace
u_{0}
to decrease the energy. The question then becomes how to choose the appropriate directions of the flow. To answer it we turn to the second variation, which can be written as a linear elliptic operator with compact resolvent. This last property is crucial as it implies that the linearized operator has a finite dimensional kernel, thus allowing us to decompose the datum
u_{0}
as the sum of the projections on the kernel, the positive, and the negative eigenvalues, i.e. the index.
The map
u_{0}
being harmonic on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, positive directions will increase the energy to second order, while negative directions will decrease it. Thus, to construct the competitor, we wish to move move
u_{0}
towards zero in the former, while increasing the contribution of the latter. To deal with the kernel we resort to a
finite dimensional harmonic map heat flow
combined with a finite dimensional Łojasiewicz inequality, cf. Lemma
4.1
to make the estimate more quantitative. Note that this finite dimensional contribution is ultimately responsible for the logarithmic decay, instead of a polynomial one. See Section
4
for a proof of Theorem
1.1
. In the special situation in which the projection of
u_{0}
on the kernel of the second variation vanishes, the so-called the integrable case, the proof simplifies significantly, see Subsection
4.1
. The adaptation of this proof to yet another geometric setting is a testament to its remarkable flexibility. Moreover, it establishes a precise connection between the kernel of the second variation and the logarithmic decay.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we prove Theorem
1.4
. The reader can find this in Section
5
. We start by showing that the
L^{2}
-difference of a blow-up at comparable scales is small. The key idea is then to exploit the log-epiperimetric inequality to infer a bound for the energy density
\Theta(\rho,y;u)
at all dyadic scales, which can then be converted to a bound at all scales. Uniqueness of the tangent map then follows by a standard Dini-type estimate that we include in Appendix
A
. We conclude in Appendix
B
by giving the alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
already mentioned in Remark
1.2
. More precisely, we construct the competitor as in
[
CSV20b
, Proposition 3.1]
by flowing the full trace inwards via
infinite dimensional harmonic map heat flow
instead of dealing with the different projected components. As before, the slicing lemma is the crucial first of this analysis. This greatly simplifies the proof, while having the drawback of relying on the infinite dimensional Łojasiewicz-Simon inequality for harmonic maps established in
[
Sim83
]
.

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\hat{u}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{\hat{u}}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}\,;u_{0})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{III}+\operatorname{IV},

with

\displaystyle\operatorname{III}

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{\hat{u}}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho))\,;u_{0})

\displaystyle\,\,\quad-\big(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})\,;u_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\operatorname{IV}

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho))\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})\,;u_{0}).

Letting
\psi_{\rho}:=\varphi_{\hat{u}}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)-\mu(\rho)-F(\mu(\rho))\in K^{\perp}
, by Taylor expanding
\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\,\cdot\,\,;u_{0})
we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{III}

\displaystyle=\nabla\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho)\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho}]+\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho)+s_{1}\psi_{\rho}\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho},\psi_{\rho}]

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})+s_{2}\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho)+s_{1}\psi_{\rho}\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho},\psi_{\rho}]

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})+s_{2}\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]

for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1]
, where in the second equality we have used that
\psi_{\rho},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\in K^{\perp}
and Lemma
3.2
-(ii). Note that to avoid having factors
1/2
in front of the Hessian term, we will abuse notation and assume that our definition of Hessian already incorporates this value.
By using the analyticity of
\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\,\cdot\,\,;u_{0})
around the zero section

, and in particular the fact that its second variation is locally Lipschitz around
0\in C^{2,\alpha}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
(it is actually smooth around
0\in C^{2,\alpha}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
) we get that there exists
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\xi\,;u_{0})[\zeta,\zeta]-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\zeta,\zeta]\rvert\leq L\|\xi\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\zeta\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}

for every
\zeta\in C^{2,\alpha}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
and
\xi\in C^{2,\alpha}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
sufficiently close to
0
in the
C^{2,\alpha}
-norm. Thus, we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{III}&\leq\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho},\psi_{\rho}]-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]\\
&\quad+L\|\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho))+s_{1}\psi_{\rho}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\psi_{\rho}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\quad+L\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})+s_{2}\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\leq\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho},\psi_{\rho}]-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]\\
&\quad+L\|\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho))+s_{1}\psi_{\rho}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\psi_{\rho}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\quad+L\big(\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\end{split}

Notice that, by definition (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\rho}=\varphi_{\hat{u}}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)-\mu(\rho)-F(\mu(\rho))=\eta_{-}(\rho)\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp}+\eta_{+}(\rho)\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp}.

Hence

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla^{2}&\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\psi_{\rho},\psi_{\rho}]-\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]\\
&=(\eta_{-}(\rho)^{2}-1)\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp},\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp}]+(\eta_{+}(\rho)^{2}-1)\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp},\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp}]\end{split}

and

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mu(\rho)+F(\mu(\rho))&+s_{1}\psi_{\rho}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|\psi_{\rho}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\big(\|\mu(\rho)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.\end{split}

By plugging (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) in (

) we get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{III}&\leq(\eta_{-}(\rho)^{2}-1)\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp},\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp}]+(\eta_{+}(\rho)^{2}-1)\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp},\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp}]\\
&\quad+C\big(\|\mu(\rho)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+2\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.\end{split}

By definition of
\eta_{+}
and
\eta_{-}
, cf. (
4.2
), up to choosing
\alpha,\beta>0
big enough and
\varepsilon>0
small enough depending on

we have

\int_{0}^{1}(\eta_{-}^{2}(\rho)-1)\rho^{n-3}\,d\rho\geq\frac{4}{n-2}\,\varepsilon.

and

\int_{0}^{1}(\eta_{+}^{2}(\rho)-1)\rho^{n-3}\,d\rho\leq-\frac{4}{n-2}\,\varepsilon.

Consequently, multiplying (
4.10
) by
\rho^{n-3}
and integrating it with respect to
\rho
, we infer

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}&\operatorname{III}\rho^{n-3}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)\\
&\leq\frac{4}{n-2}\varepsilon\max_{\lambda_{j}<0}\lambda_{j}\|\varphi_{u,-}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-\frac{4}{n-2}\,\varepsilon\min_{\lambda_{j}>0}\lambda_{j}\|\varphi_{u,+}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\quad+C\big(\|\mu(\rho)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\\
&\leq-\left(C_{u_{0}}\varepsilon-C\big(\|\mu(\rho)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}+\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\big)\right)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2},\end{split}

where
C_{u_{0}}>0
is a constant depending only on
n
and on the spectral gap of the second variation of
\mathscr{F}(\,\cdot\,;\,u_{0})
at
0
, given by

\displaystyle C_{u_{0}}:=\frac{4}{n-2}\Big(\min_{\lambda_{j}>0}\lambda_{j}-\max_{\lambda_{j}<0}\lambda_{j}\Big).

We remark that here we need

to have integrability of the term
C\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{n-3}d\rho
.
Notice now that

|\mu(\rho)-P_{K}(\varphi_{u})|\leq\int_{0}^{\eta(\rho)}|\mu^{\prime}(t)|\;dt\leq|\eta(\rho)|\leq C\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{\gamma},

as well as

\left|\frac{d}{d\rho}\mu(\rho)\right|\leq\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{\gamma},

so that choosing
\varepsilon_{f}
sufficiently small, and combining these estimates with elliptic regularity, we have the estimate

\|\mu(\rho)\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq 2\|P_{K}\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Whence, choosing
\delta>0
sufficiently small (depending on
C_{u_{0}}
) and plugging
\|\varphi_{u}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})<\delta
in (
4.11
) , we infer

(4.12)

\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{III}\rho^{n-3}\;d\rho\leq-C_{u_{0}}\varepsilon\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

We are now left with estimating
\operatorname{IV}
. To this sake, we record Łojasiewicz’s inequality for analytic function in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, cf.
[
lL65
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{l}
, and an analytic function
h\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. For every critical point
x\in U
of
h
, there exist a neighborhood
V
of
x
, an exponent
\gamma\in(0,1/2]
, and a constant
K\geq 2
such that

|h(x)-h(y)|^{1-\gamma}\leq K|\nabla h(y)|,

for all
y\in V
.

In particular, we can apply Lemma
4.1
to
f
defined in (
4.1
), and infer the existence of a neighborhood
V
of the origin, constants
K>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1/2]
depending on
u_{0}
and
n
, such that
|f(v)|^{1-\gamma}\leq K|\nabla f(v)|
, for every
v\in V
.
Then, if
f(v(s))>0
, for
0<s<t
, we have

(4.13)

f(v(t))-f(v(0))=f(v(t))-f(b)=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f(v(\tau))\cdot v^{\prime}(\tau)\;d\tau=-\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla f(v(\tau))|\;d\tau\leq 0.

This implies that the function
t\mapsto f(v(t))
is non-increasing, which in turn implies the existence of
\overline{\tau}>0
such that
f(v(t))\geq f(b)/2>0
, for
0\leq t\leq\overline{\tau}
, and
f(v(t))\leq f(b)/2
if
t\geq\overline{\tau}
. We now have two cases. If
\eta(\rho)\leq\overline{\tau}
, we have the following

\displaystyle\operatorname{IV}

\displaystyle=f(v(\eta(\rho)))-f(b)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\eta(\rho)}|\nabla f(v(\tau))|\,d\tau\qquad

from (
4.13
)

\displaystyle\leq-K\int_{0}^{\eta(\rho)}|f(v(\tau))|^{1-\gamma}\;d\tau\qquad

from Lemma
4.1

\displaystyle\leq-Kf(v(\eta(\rho)))^{1-\gamma}\eta(\rho)\qquad

monotonicity of
f

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{K}{2^{1-\gamma}}f(b)^{1-\gamma}\eta(\rho)\qquad

definition of
\overline{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{K}{2}f(b)^{1-\gamma}\eta(\rho).

Otherwise, if
\eta(\rho)>\overline{\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{IV}=f(v(\eta(\rho)))-f(b)<-\frac{1}{2}f(b)<-\eta(\rho)f(b)^{1-\gamma},

where for the last inequality we used the inequality
|\eta|\leq C\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{1-\gamma}<1/2
which holds as long as
f(b)
is small enough. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{IV}\leq-\eta(\rho)f(b)^{1-\gamma},

and this concludes the estimate for
\operatorname{IV}
.

We are now able to finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First notice that, by the slicing Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\;;u_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2}\Big(\big(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})\,;u_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n-2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(P_{K}\varphi_{u}+F(P_{K}\varphi_{u})\,;u_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2}\big(\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})+f(b)\big).

We have two cases.

(a)

First, assume
\lvert f(b)\rvert<\frac{(n-2)C_{u_{0}}}{4}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}
, for some universal constant
\nu>0
depending only on
\tilde{u}_{0}
and the dimension
n
. In this case, let
\varepsilon_{f}=0
, so that
\eta\equiv 0
, and
\operatorname{IV}=0
. Then, since

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})-\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})

\displaystyle=\nabla\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]+\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}]

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\xi\,;u_{0})[\varphi_{u}^{\perp},\varphi_{u}^{\perp}],

for some
\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta_{0}}(0)
, by choosing
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small we have

\displaystyle\lvert\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\,;u_{0})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq(\min_{\lambda_{j}>0}\lambda_{j}+\max_{\lambda_{j}<0}\lambda_{j}\Big)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-2)C_{u_{0}}}{4}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

Then, we get

\displaystyle\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert\leq\frac{C_{u_{0}}}{2}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq 1,

where the last inequality follows by possibly choosing
\delta_{0}>0
even smaller depending just on
u_{0}
. In particular, from
(
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.12
), we deduce

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-C_{u_{0}}\varepsilon\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}+\hat{C}\varepsilon^{2}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\,\frac{2(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{C_{u_{0}}}\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert,

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\,\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\,\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{1+\gamma}

for every
\gamma>0
, where the second to last inequality follows by choosing
\varepsilon
appropriately so that

\displaystyle\frac{2(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{C_{u_{0}}}\geq 1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\varepsilon\leq\frac{3C_{u_{0}}}{\hat{C}}

and the last inequality follows because
\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert\leq 1
.

(b)

Otherwise, we set
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{f}\lvert f(b)\rvert^{1-2\gamma}
for some
\varepsilon_{f}
sufficiently small depending only on
n
and
u_{0}
, allowing us to estimate
\operatorname{IV}
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{IV}\rho^{n-3}\;d\rho

\displaystyle\leq-f(b)^{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\eta(\rho)\rho^{n-3}\;d\rho

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{2-2\gamma}C\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{n}(1-\rho)\rho^{n-3}\;d\rho

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{2-2\gamma},

where the last inequality follows by choosing

big enough depending only on
n
. Then, from this inequality, combined with (

), (
4.6

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}

\displaystyle(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-C_{u_{0}}\varepsilon\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{2-2\gamma}+\hat{C}\big(\varepsilon_{f}^{2}f(b)^{2-2\gamma}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq-(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)\varepsilon\|\varphi_{u}^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-(\varepsilon_{f}-\hat{C}\varepsilon_{f}^{2})f(b)^{2-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq-\bigg(\frac{4(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{(n-2)C_{u_{0}}}-1+\hat{C}\varepsilon_{f}\bigg)\varepsilon_{f}f(b)^{2-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq-\bigg(\frac{2(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{C_{u_{0}}}-\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\hat{C}\varepsilon_{f}\bigg)\varepsilon_{f}\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{2-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq-\bigg(\frac{2(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{C_{u_{0}}}-\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\hat{C}\varepsilon_{f}\bigg)\varepsilon_{f}\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{1+\tilde{\gamma}},

where in the last inequality we have defined

. Again, by choosing

(and hence

) small enough so that

\displaystyle\frac{2(C_{u_{0}}-\hat{C}\varepsilon)}{C_{u_{0}}}-\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\hat{C}\varepsilon_{f}\geq 1,

we get that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}

\displaystyle(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\leq-\varepsilon_{f}\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{1+\tilde{\gamma}}

4.1.
The integrable case

We now specialise the proof of Theorem
1.1
to the case of an integrable kernel (of the second variation). We start by recalling from
[
AS88
]
this notion. We will say that
K:=\ker\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})
is integrable if for every
v\in K
, there exists a family
\{u_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1)}\subset C^{\infty}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
with
u_{s}\rightarrow 0
in
C^{\infty}(u_{0}^{*}TN)
, such that
\nabla\mathscr{F}_{M}(u_{s}\,;u_{0})=0
for every
s\in(0,1)
, and
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}u_{s}/s=v
in the
L^{2}(M)
sense. In this setting, analyticity of
f
defined above implies the following lemma, whose proof can be found in
[
AS88
, Lemma 1]
, or
[
ESV19
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The integrability condition hold for
\ker\nabla^{2}\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(0\,;u_{0})
if and only if
f\equiv f(0)=0
in a neighborhood of
0
.

Consequently, it is immediate from this lemma that in the proof of the log-epiperimetric inequality we can take
\gamma=0
, thus obtaining an epiperimetric inequality.

5.
Proof of the uniqueness of tangent maps with isolated singularities (Theorem
1.4
)

As by the statement of Theorem
1.4
, consider an energy minimizing harmonic map
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,N)
and let
y\in\operatorname{Sing}(u)
. Let
r>0
be such that
B_{r}(y)\subset\Omega
. For every
\rho\in(0,r/2)
, define
u_{y,\rho}\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
as

\displaystyle u_{y,\rho}(x):=u(\rho x+y)\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{n}\mbox{-.a.e. }x\in B_{2}(0).

First, we show the following lemma that will be used to analyze the rescalings
u_{y,\rho}
at comparable scales.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
r\in(0,\delta_{0})
and every
\rho\in[r/2,r]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho}-u_{y,r}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction and we claim that there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and sequences
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and
\rho_{n}\in[r_{n}/2,r_{n}]
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{n}}-u_{y,r_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Since

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{r_{n}}{\rho_{n}}\leq 2

there exists a subsequence (not relabeled) such that

\displaystyle 0<\ell:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{r_{n}}{\rho_{n}}<+\infty.

By the standard monotonicity formula for energy minimizing harmonic maps we know that, up to a subsequence (not relabelled)
u_{y,r_{n}}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
W^{1,2}
and strongly in
L^{2}
for some
0
-homogeneous tangent map
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
. Let
\Psi_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\ell}(x):=\ell x\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and notice that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{n}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert(\Psi_{\ell}^{-1})^{*}(u_{y,\ell\rho_{n}}-\Psi_{\ell}^{*}\varphi)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,r_{n}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=0.

By then, by triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{n}}-u_{u,r_{n}}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lim_{n\to+\infty}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{n}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,r_{n}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigg)=0

which contradicts (
5.1
). The statement follows.
∎

Let
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
be any tangent map for
u
at
y
such that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(B_{2}(0)\smallsetminus\{0\},N)
. Let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that
u_{y,\rho_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
W^{1,2}
. Fix any
\eta>0
. By Rellich-Kondrachov theorem, we get that
u_{y,\rho_{i}}\to\varphi
strongly in
L^{2}
. Thus, there exists
i_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
i\geq i_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{i}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}<\eta.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
be the constant given by Lemma
5.1
with
\varepsilon_{0}=\eta
and let
i_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\rho_{i}<\delta_{0}
for every
i\geq i_{2}
. Let
\tilde{i}:=\max\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
and let
\tilde{\rho}:=\rho_{\tilde{i}}
. Fix any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by Lemma
5.1
, for every
\rho\in[\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n}]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\rho}-u_{y,\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2^{n}}}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\lvert u_{y,\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2^{n}}}-\varphi\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 4\eta.

Notice that
u_{y,\rho}-\varphi
is an energy minimizing harmonic map and let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be the constant given by the
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem for energy minimizing harmonic maps, cf.
[
SU82
]
. By choosing
\eta>0
small enough depending just on
\varepsilon_{0}
and on
\varphi
we get that
u_{y,\rho}\in C^{\infty}(B_{\frac{5}{4}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{7}{8}}(0),N)
and

\displaystyle\|u_{y,\rho}-\varphi\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\|u_{y,\rho}-\varphi\|_{C^{3}\big(B_{\frac{5}{4}}(0)\smallsetminus B_{\frac{7}{8}}(0)\big)}<C\eta.

Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
be the constants given by Theorem
1.1
for
u_{0}=\varphi
. By again reducing the size of
\eta>0
we can make sure that
C\eta<\delta
, so that

\displaystyle\|u_{y,\rho}-\varphi\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq C\eta<\delta.

Thus, by Theorem
1.1
, there exists
\hat{u}_{\rho}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
such that
\hat{u}_{\rho}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=u_{y,\rho}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\leq\big(1-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\rvert^{\gamma}\big)\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi),

where
\tilde{u}_{\rho}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
is the
0
-homogeneous extension of
u_{y,\rho}
inside
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Notice that, since
u_{y,\rho}
is energy minimizing, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Theta(\rho,y;u)&-\Theta(y;u)=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{y,\rho})-\Theta(y;u)\\
&=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{y,\rho})-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)\\
&\leq\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}_{\rho})-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)\\
&=\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\leq\big(1-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\rvert^{\gamma}\big)\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n})\end{split}

Let

\displaystyle f(\rho):=\rho^{n-2}\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u)\rho^{n-2}=\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}-\Theta(y;u)\rho^{n-2}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in[0,1).

Notice that by the monotonicity formula for energy minimizing harmonic maps we have that
[0,1)\ni\rho\mapsto f(\rho)
is an non-decreasing function of
\rho
. Hence,
f
is differentiable
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e. and its distributional derivative is a measure whose absolutely continous part (with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{1}
) coincides
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e. with the classical differential and whose singular part is non negative. Thus, we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(y)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}-(n-2)\Theta(y;u)\rho^{n-3}

\displaystyle=\rho^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert du(\rho x+y)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)-(n-2)\Theta(y;u)\rho^{n-3}

\displaystyle=\rho^{n-3}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\rho du(\rho x+y)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)-(n-2)\Theta(y;u)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho^{n-3}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert du_{y,\rho}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)-(n-2)\Theta(y;u)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho^{n-3}(n-2)\big(\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho})-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)\big)

\displaystyle=\rho^{n-3}(n-2)\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{1}\mbox{-a.e. }\rho\in(0,1),

which can be rewritten as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\rho^{n-2}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\leq\frac{\rho}{n-2}f^{\prime}(\rho)\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{1}\mbox{-a.e. }\rho\in(0,1).

By plugging (
5.3
) in (
5.2
) we get

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\rho)&=\rho^{n-2}\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u)\rho^{n-2}\\
&\leq\big(1-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\rvert^{\gamma}\big)\rho^{n-2}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\\
&\leq\big(1-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\rvert^{\gamma}\big)\frac{\rho}{n-2}f^{\prime}(\rho),\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{1}\mbox{-a.e. }\rho\in(\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n}).\end{split}

Moreover, since
u_{y,\rho}
is energy minimizing, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e(\rho)&:=\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u)=\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}-\Theta(y;u)=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{y,\rho})-\Theta(y;u)\\
&\leq\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho})-\Theta(y;u)=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho})-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)=\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi).\end{split}

Hence, by combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) we get

\displaystyle f(\rho)\leq\big(1-\varepsilon\lvert e(\rho)\rvert^{\gamma}\big)\frac{\rho}{n-2}f^{\prime}(\rho),\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{1}\mbox{-a.e. }\rho\in(\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n}).

Arguing as in
[
ESV19
, Section 3.2, Step 1]
we get

\displaystyle e(\rho)\leq 2\bigg(-(n-2)\varepsilon\gamma\log\bigg(\frac{\rho}{\tilde{\rho}}\bigg)\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in[\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n}].

Since we have chosen
n\in\mathbb{N}
arbitrarily and for every
\rho\in(0,\tilde{\rho})
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho\in[\tilde{\rho}/2^{n+1},\tilde{\rho}/2^{n}]
, we have established that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u)=e(\rho)\leq 2\bigg(\hskip-4.2679pt-(n-2)\varepsilon\gamma\log\bigg(\frac{\rho}{\tilde{\rho}}\bigg)\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(0,\tilde{\rho}).

The uniqueness of tangent map to
u
at
y
then follows directly by Proposition
A.1
with
\rho_{0}:=\tilde{\rho}/2
and

\displaystyle\phi(\rho):=2\bigg(\hskip-4.2679pt-(n-2)\varepsilon\gamma\log\bigg(\frac{\rho}{\tilde{\rho}}\bigg)\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(0,\tilde{\rho}/2).

6.
Proof of the uniqueness of tangent maps at infinity (Theorem
1.6
)

In this section, we aim to prove the uniqueness of the blow-down maps, i.e. uniqueness of the tangent map at infinity. We start by recalling what these objects are.

Definition 6.1
(Tangent maps at infinity).

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed smooth submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
n\geq 3
. Let
u\in W_{loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},N)
. For every
\rho\in[1,+\infty)
, define
u_{\rho}\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
as

\displaystyle u_{\rho}(x):=u_{0,\rho}(x)=u(\rho x)\qquad\mbox{ for }\mathcal{L}^{n}\mbox{-.a.e. }x\in B_{2}(0).

If there exist a sequence
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,+\infty)
and a map
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
such that
\rho_{i}\to+\infty
and
u_{\rho_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
, then we say that
\varphi
is a blow-down of
u
, or a tangent map for
u
at infinity.

Remark 6.2
(Existence of blow downs).

Let
u\in W_{loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},N)
and assume the following growth condition on the Dirichlet energy of
u
at infinity: there exists
\Lambda>0
such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\Lambda\rho^{n-2}\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(0,+\infty).

This implies that the Dirichlet energy of the blow-downs
\{u_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[1,+\infty)}
of
u
is uniformly bounded. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho})=\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\Lambda<+\infty.

Hence, by standard Sobolev weak compactness, there exist a sequence
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,+\infty)
and a map
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
such that
\rho_{i}\to+\infty
and
u_{\rho_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
. Hence,
u
admits at least a tangent map at infinity.

Now, we aim to provide a proof of Theorem
1.6
, i.e. to show that if
u
is an energy minimizing harmonic map satisfying (
6.1
), then
u
has a unique tangent map at infinity.

Note that, because of the monotonicity formula for energy minimizing harmonic maps, the function

\displaystyle[1,+\infty)\ni\rho\mapsto\Theta(\rho\,;u):=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho})=\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

is a non-decreasing function on
[1,+\infty)
. By Remark
6.2
, the function
\Theta(\,\cdot\,;u)
is also uniformly bounded on
[1,+\infty)
by the constant
\Lambda
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to+\infty}\Theta(\rho\,;u)

exists and is a finite non-negative real number, which we denote by
\Theta(u)
.

Let
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0)\,;N)
be any tangent map to
u
at infinity and let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,+\infty)
be such that
\rho_{i}\to+\infty
and
u_{\rho_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi
weakly in
W^{1,2}(B_{2}(0),N)
. Note that, since
u
is energy minimizing, subsequentially we have
u_{\rho}\to\varphi
strongly in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
(see e.g.
[
Sim12
, Section 3.9, Lemma 1]
) and thus the we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)=\Theta(u).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)=\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho})-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\varphi)=\Theta(\rho\,;u)-\Theta(u)

and by the monotonicity formula we get
\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\leq 0
for every
\rho\in[1,+\infty)
. Thus, we got that

\displaystyle[1,+\infty)\ni\rho\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;u_{\infty})=\Theta(\rho\,;u)-\Theta(u)\in[\Theta(1\,;u)-\Theta(u),0]\subset(-\infty,0]

is a non-decreasing and non-positive function on
[1,+\infty)
. As such, it is differentiable
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e. with respect to
\rho
and we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\rho}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{n-2}{\rho^{n-1}}\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{\rho}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n-2}{\rho}\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho})+\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{\rho}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert du_{\rho}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{\rho}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{\rho}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}_{\rho}\,;\varphi)-\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(u_{\rho}\,;\varphi)\big)\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
u
is energy minimizing. We have then shown property (1) in
[
ESV24
, Assumptions 2.1]
. Note that property (2) in
[
ESV24
, Assumptions 2.1]
is exactly the symmetric log-epiperimetric inequality

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})-\varepsilon\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\rvert^{1+\gamma},

that we have shown in Theorem
1.1
. Then we can apply
[
ESV24
, Corollary 2.6]
or argue directly exactly as in Section 5 and Appendix A to obtain that there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{\rho}-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq\begin{cases}C\log(\rho)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}}&\mbox{ if }\gamma>0\\
C\rho^{-\alpha}&\mbox{ if }\gamma=0\end{cases}\qquad\forall\rho\in(1,+\infty).

This immediately implies that
\varphi
is the unique limit of the blow-down family
\{u_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[1,+\infty)}
, i.e. the unique tangent map of
u
at infinity.

Appendix A
A criterion for the uniqueness of tangent maps

The aim of this last section is to prove a standard argument in the literature allowing us to infer uniqueness of tangent maps to a stationary harmonic map
u
at some point
y
from a sufficiently fast decay of the energy density
\Theta(\rho,y;u)
to its limit, usually referred to as
Dini continuity
. We reproduce the argument here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed smooth submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any open set. Let
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,N)
be a stationary harmonic map on
\Omega
and let
y\in\Omega
. Assume that there exist
\rho_{0}\in(0,\dist(y,\partial\Omega))
and an increasing function
\phi:(0,\rho_{0})\to(0,+\infty)
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u)\leq\phi(\rho)\qquad\forall\,\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})

and

(A.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\frac{\sqrt{\phi(\rho)}}{\rho}d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)<+\infty.

Then, the tangent map to
u
at
y
is unique.

Proof.

First, recall the monotonicity formula for stationary harmonic maps (whose proof can be found for instance in
[
Lin99
, Section 1, Equation (1.7)]
):

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}-\frac{1}{\sigma^{n-2}}\int_{B_{\sigma}(y)}\lvert du\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-2}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

for every
0<\sigma<\rho<\dist(y,\partial\Omega)
, where we have defined

\displaystyle\nu_{y}:=\frac{\,\cdot\,-y}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert}\qquad\mbox{ on }\Omega\smallsetminus\{y\}.

By Hölder inequality, (
A.3
) and the bound (
A.1
) in the assumptions, for every
0<\sigma<\rho<\rho_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma}(y)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-2}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big((\log\rho-\log\sigma)(\Theta(\rho,y;u)-\Theta(y;u))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big((\log\rho-\log\sigma)\phi(\rho)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Fix any
0<\sigma<\rho<\rho_{0}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\rho/2^{k}\leq\sigma
. From the previous estimate, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho/2^{i}}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\rho/2^{i+1}}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\tilde{C}\sqrt{\phi\bigg(\frac{\rho}{2^{i}}\bigg)}

Then we have

(A.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}&\leq\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\rho/2^{k}}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{B_{\rho/2^{i}}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\rho/2^{i+1}}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sqrt{\phi\bigg(\frac{\rho}{2^{i}}\bigg)}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sqrt{\phi\bigg(\frac{\rho}{2^{i}}\bigg)}\frac{2^{i}}{\rho}\frac{\rho}{2^{i}}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\sqrt{\phi(t)}}{t}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t).\end{split}

Now, let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be any two tangent maps to
u
at the point
y\in\Omega
. By definition of tangent map, there exist sequences
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\rho_{0})
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\rho_{0})
such that
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}\to 0^{+}
and

\displaystyle u_{y,\rho_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{y,\sigma_{i}}\rightharpoonup\varphi_{2}

weakly in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
as
i\to+\infty
. By the weak continuity of the trace operator, we have

\displaystyle u_{y,\rho_{i}}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rightharpoonup\varphi_{1}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{y,\sigma_{i}}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rightharpoonup\varphi_{2}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}

weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
as
i\to+\infty
. Thus, by (
A.4
) and the coarea formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\rvert\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u_{y,\rho_{i}}-u_{y,\sigma_{i}}\rvert\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u(\rho_{i}x+y)-u(\sigma_{i}x+y)\rvert\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\int_{\sigma_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}\nabla u(\rho x+y)\cdot x\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)\bigg\rvert\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}\bigg\lvert\nabla u(\rho x+y)\cdot\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\sigma_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\nabla u(\rho x+y)\cdot\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}\bigg\rvert\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(x)\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)\,

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\sigma_{i}}^{\rho_{i}}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(y)}\frac{1}{\rho^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-y}{\lvert z-y\rvert}\bigg\rvert\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(z)\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\rho_{i}}(y)\smallsetminus B_{\sigma_{i}}(y)}\frac{1}{\lvert\,\cdot\,-y\rvert^{n-1}}\bigg\lvert\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{y}}\bigg\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\rho_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{\phi(t)}}{t}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)=0,

where the last equality follows from the assumption (
A.2
). Hence, we have
\varphi_{1}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=\varphi_{2}|_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. Since both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are
0
-homogeneous functions, we conclude that
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}
and the statement follows.
∎

Appendix B
Epiperimetric and Łojasiewicz–Simon inequalities

One of the strengths of the approach that we use in the proof of the epiperimetric equality for harmonic maps (see Theorem
1.1
) is that we don’t need to use any “infinite dimensional” Łojasiewicz inequality. Indeed, by exploiting the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction for the Dirichlet energy we can reduce to run a heat flow on the
finite dimensional
kernel of the second variation of the energy and then leverage on the standard Łojasiewicz inequality for real-analytic functionals on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see Lemma
4.1
).

In the framework of harmonic maps though, the following infinite dimensional Łojasiewicz inequality was obtained by Simon in
[
Sim83
]
(see also
[
Sim12
, Section 3.14]
for a proof of this statement).

Proposition B.1
(Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for the Dirichlet energy on the sphere).

Let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed real-analytic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n\geq 3
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
be a harmonic map on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then, there exist
\delta,C>0
and
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
depending on
u_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})\rvert^{1-\beta}\leq C\|\nabla\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\qquad\forall u\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(u_{0})\subset C^{2,\alpha}(u_{0}^{*}TN).

Notice that, as suggested by
[
CSV20b
, Proposition 3.1]
, by exploiting the previous Proposition (
B.1
) we can significantly simplify the proof of Theorem
1.1
as follows. As by the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, let
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a closed real-analytic submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n\geq 3
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
be a harmonic map on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and let
C,\delta>0
,
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
depending on
u_{0}
be the constants given by Proposition
B.1
. Let
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}(u_{0})
and notice that if

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\leq 0

then choosing
\hat{u}:=\tilde{u}
trivially gives (
1.1
). Hence, we can assume that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})=\frac{1}{n-2}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})>0,

where the first equality follows from Lemma
3.1
. By the existence of short-time smooth solutions for the harmonic map heat flow established in
[
ES64
]
(see also
[
LW08b
, Theorem 5.2.1]
), there exists
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{n-2}
such that
U:[0,\varepsilon_{0}]\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
is a smooth solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}U^{\prime}(t)+\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(t))=0\\
U(0)=u\end{cases}

Moreover, by possibly choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
smaller, we can assume that

(B.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})\leq 2\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(t)\,;u_{0})\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}]

with equality just if
t=\varepsilon_{0}
(notice that
t\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(t)\,;u_{0})
is continuous and non-increasing in
t
). We let
\tilde{U}:[0,+\infty)\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},N)
be the given by

\displaystyle\tilde{U}(t):=\begin{cases}U(t)&\mbox{ for every }0\leq t\leq\varepsilon_{0}\\
U(\varepsilon_{0})&\mbox{ for every }t>\varepsilon_{0}.\end{cases}

Then we define the competitor
\hat{u}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n},N)
in polar coordinates
(\rho,\theta)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
by

\displaystyle\hat{u}(\rho,\theta):=\tilde{U}(-\varepsilon_{0}\log(\rho),\theta)\qquad\forall\,(\rho,\theta)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\hat{u}(\rho,\,\cdot\,)\,;u)\rho^{n-3}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\partial_{\rho}\hat{u}(\rho,\theta)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\rho^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\tilde{U}(-\varepsilon_{0}\log(\rho),\,\cdot\,)\,;u)\rho^{n-3}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\tilde{U}^{\prime}(-\varepsilon_{0}\log(\rho),\theta)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\rho^{n-3}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\tilde{U}(t,\,\cdot\,)\,;u)e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{0}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\tilde{U}^{\prime}(t,\theta)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\big(\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\tilde{U}(t,\,\cdot\,))-\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\tilde{U}(0,\,\cdot\,))\big)e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{0}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert U^{\prime}(t,\theta)\rvert^{2}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\theta)e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

(B.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{\min\{t,\varepsilon_{0}\}}\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s,\,\cdot\,))[U^{\prime}(s,\,\cdot\,)]\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{0}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\|U^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s,\,\cdot\,))[U^{\prime}(s,\,\cdot\,)]\bigg(\int_{s}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)\bigg)\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{0}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\|U^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}e^{-\frac{t(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\hskip-2.84526pt\bigg(\hskip-4.2679pt-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{n-2}\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s,\,\cdot\,))[U^{\prime}(s,\,\cdot\,)]-\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|U^{\prime}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\bigg)e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{n-2}-\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\bigg)\|U^{\prime}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\bigg)e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\|\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s),

where we have let

\displaystyle\tilde{C}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{n-2}-\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\bigg)>0.

Now let
\tilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
be a small constant to be chosen later and notice that, by (
B.2
), by Proposition
B.1
and by (
B.1
) we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle-(1-\tilde{\varepsilon})\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle=-\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\|\nabla\mathscr{D}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)+\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\tilde{C}C^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(U(s)\,;u_{0})^{2-2\beta}e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)+\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-2^{2\beta-2}\tilde{C}C^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;u_{0})^{2-2\beta}e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)+\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\bigg(\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg)^{2-2\beta}\tilde{C}C^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{0}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})^{2-2\beta}e^{-\frac{s(n-2)}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{1}(s)+\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\bigg(\bigg(\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg)^{1-2\beta}\varepsilon_{0}\tilde{C}C^{2}\big(1-e^{-(n-2)}\big)-\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})^{2\beta-1}\bigg)\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})^{2-2\beta}.

Now choosing
\tilde{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\,;\tilde{u}_{0})^{1-2\beta}
in the previous inequality for some
\varepsilon>0
small enough depending on
u_{0}
and
n
we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\hat{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})

\displaystyle-(1-\varepsilon\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})^{1-2\beta})\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})\leq 0

and, since we assumed that
\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\tilde{u}\,;\tilde{u}_{0})>0
, the statement follows with
\gamma:=2\beta
.

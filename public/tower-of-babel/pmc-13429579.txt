Prediction of groundwater level in shallow and complex aquifers using hybrid soft computing models and metaheuristic algorithms
Authors: Saeed Mohammadpour, Arash Ebrahimabadi, Hamidreza Rabiefar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Predicting groundwater level dynamics in shallow, unconfined aquifers remain a persistent challenge due to their high sensitivity to heterogeneous recharge, rapid anthropogenic extraction, and nonlinear interactions with climatic drivers conditions under which conventional models often fail to generalize. Here, we introduce two novel hybrid frameworks ANN-HBO and ANN-POA that couple artificial neural networks with metaheuristic optimization algorithms (Honey Badger Optimization and Pelican Optimization) to dynamically optimize network weights in data-scarce, non-stationary environments where deep learning architectures exhibit poor convergence and overfitting. The study is conducted in the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer, northern Iran, a shallow alluvial system limited storage capacity, and strong seasonal fluctuations driven by irrigation demand and evaporation. Hydraulic conductivity varies spatially from 10 to 35 m/day, reflecting moderate to high transmissivity (approximately 200–600 m2/day), typical of well-drained fluvial systems. Using 256 months of hydro-meteorological records, we identify the most informative predictors lagged groundwater levels (1–3 months), temperature, precipitation, evaporation, and abstraction rates via the Minimum Redundancy Maximum Relevance (MRMR) algorithm, generating four optimized input scenarios. Compared to a standalone ANN, ANN-POA reduces RMSE by 29.3% and MAE by 24.1% on average across three observation wells, achieving test RMSE values of 0.213–0.287 m and MAE of 0.260–0.437 m. Lagged groundwater level (1-month) and monthly precipitation are identified as the dominant predictors, confirming the system’s strong dependence on recent recharge. The superior robustness of ANN-POA over ANN-HBO and baseline models demonstrates its efficacy in navigating complex, high-dimensional parameter spaces without requiring extensive training data. This approach enables water managers in data-limited regions to transition from reactive to anticipatory aquifer management: identifying early signs of depletion, prioritizing managed recharge, and calibrating extraction quotas based on dynamic hydrological responses rather than static averages. Our framework offers a scalable, physics-informed, and low-cost solution for sustainable groundwater governance in vulnerable shallow aquifers worldwide.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43005-w.

Introduction

Groundwater is considered one of the most valuable and vital water resources for various uses. excessive groundwater withdrawal and inadequate planning in its allocation and consumption can have undeniable negative consequences on the environment and water resources management1. Consequently, groundwater has attained a crucial and prominent position as a valuable and renewable resource within the management and planning cycle of water resources policy, both quantitatively and qualitatively. Due to increased demand and consumption driven by climatic and anthropogenic changes, it has captured the special focus of governmental and sovereign institutions, as well as local and state governments, becoming a central point of attention2. Understanding the nature of a system and its interconnected and integrated components, as well as how changes in one component affect other parts of the system, remains a fundamental challenge in the field of water resources. In this regard, quantifying groundwater responses gains particular importance. Therefore, achieving an appropriate prediction necessitates understanding the relationships governing climatic drivers, comprehending the hydrological nature of groundwater, and ensuring its optimal use in various sectors such as agriculture, industries and services, and other related areas3. The accurate simulation of a physical process demands a thorough understanding of its governing relationships and intrinsic complexities. This often leads to significant challenges in the modeling and calibration process, given the extensive agricultural, hydrological, and geohydrological data required, ultimately impeding the development of effective models. Moreover, achieving a unified prediction of groundwater levels by integrating the responses of various interacting factors proves to be a particularly challenging task4. Controlling and monitoring groundwater level (GWL) can be of particular interest to groundwater specialists for short-term and long-term planning in water resources management5. In this regard, the use of machine learning models will be highly practical for non-linear models capable of adequately predicting groundwater level changes, especially when the modeling process in such models is performed without deep and sufficient knowledge of the physical parameters of the problem [Reference]. These models will be applied based on historical data records and the identification of suitable patterns for forecasting future scenarios6. ML algorithms possess the ability to identify complex structures without considering the nature and physics of the problem, enabling accurate predictions for irregular data. Therefore, the advantage of these methods over physical, traditional, and other conventional approaches lies in ML models’ capacity for simulating processes and achieving accurate and consistent predictions without requiring deep knowledge of climatic parameters7. Evolutionary algorithms have accelerated scientific discoveries by significantly enhancing efficiency, accuracy, and speed in solving complex problems, and by enabling the analysis of massive datasets and the discovery of hidden patterns. Furthermore, hybrid models, by integrating multiple computational approaches, have become a powerful strategy for overcoming the limitations of individual algorithm8. Optimizer models (metaheuristics) help Artificial Neural Networks to perform the weight and bias optimization process with greater precision, ultimately leading to improved prediction accuracy9. Aquifers whose groundwater level is close to the surface, although considered an important water source, also come with difficulties. These aquifers are easily replenished by precipitation, but this very proximity of water to the surface creates serious problems. Among these issues are water loss through evapotranspiration, salinization of agricultural lands, and soil waterlogging10. Given these challenges, proper and integrated management of these aquifers is essential. This management should include strategies such as establishing effective drainage systems, optimizing irrigation methods, improving soil quality and structure, and continuously monitoring water quality. Ultimately, considering climate change and increasing water demand, the intelligent management of these aquifers is of high importance for environmental preservation and water supply for human use and agriculture, requiring close cooperation between executive and local authorities11,12. Nafouanti et al.13(2025) used deep learning and hybrid models to predict groundwater levels and their connection to land subsidence and water quality in the North China Plain. Their LSTM model accurately forecasted groundwater levels, while a hybrid Random Forest-KNN model proved most effective for predicting fluoride contamination. Tahkur et al.14 a hybrid PCA–DWT–LSTM model for groundwater level prediction was developed. Their results showed that this model was significantly more accurate than a model without the wavelet transform (DWT) component. Zhu et al.15 developed a Bayesian ensemble framework, using a stacking algorithm for probabilistic groundwater level forecasting. Their analysis revealed that the machine learning models outperformed the deep learning models. The Support Vector Machine (SVM) was identified as the best overall model, achieving the highest accuracy with the lowest RMSE. Most research conducted to date in this field pertains to aquifers with deeper water tables or in arid climates where groundwater levels exhibit a clear declining pattern or a distinct sinusoidal pattern. However, accurate prediction of groundwater levels in shallow and complex aquifers is essential, and its implementation is unavoidable. Because these aquifers are highly susceptible to climatic changes, evapotranspiration, and soil salinization, without a proper understanding of water table fluctuations, these vital resources which are of critical and strategic importance for various uses such as agriculture and drinking water cannot be managed correctly. Thakur and Karmakar17 (2025) conducted a comparative analysis of ANN, LSTM, and hybrid PSO-LSTM algorithms for groundwater level prediction. Evaluation of the hybrid PSO-LSTM model showed a significant improvement compared to the standalone LSTM. Furthermore, the results indicate that the implemented hybrid PSO-LSTM algorithm outperforms both the traditional ANN and the standard LSTM. Mirboluki et al.18 (2024) addressed groundwater level (GWL) estimation using enhanced deep learning and machine learning methods. The study compared the Grey Wolf Optimizer-Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM-GWO) deep learning model with the Grey Wolf Optimizer-Artificial Neural Network (ANN-GWO) hybrid machine learning model. The results demonstrated that the LSTM-GWO method showed better capability in estimating GWL than the ANN-GWO and ANN methods. Other findings also indicated that the hybrid LSTM-GWO model improved the GWL estimations by approximately 23%. Osman et al.19 (2024) evaluated machine learning algorithms for predicting the impact of climate change on groundwater levels to protect against aquifer depletion. The study compared the results of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), and Support Vector Regression (SVR) models. Their findings indicated that the XGBoost model achieved the highest predictive performance (RMSE = 0.026) followed by LSTM (RMSE = 0.027, 0.050, 0.085) respectively. Yeganeh et al.20 (2024) compared deep learning models and shallow models for groundwater level prediction, focusing on the integration of multi-piezometric data. Their findings showed that the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model significantly outperformed its competitors. Its superiority over the next best technique was approximately 13%. Abdi et al.21 (2024) utilized interpolation techniques in deep learning models to enhance groundwater level (GWL) prediction accuracy. The study was conducted on the Azarshahr plain aquifer in Iran and involved 34 observation wells. Three interpolation methods Kriging, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and M5P were employed. Other findings also revealed that improving data quality through interpolation significantly increased prediction accuracy by approximately 90%. Chang et al.22 (2025) addressed advanced groundwater level (GWL) prediction using a hybrid deep learning model. The study utilized a hybrid deep learning model (CNN-BP) and a machine learning model based on Back-Propagation Neural Networks (BPNN) to predict groundwater levels in the Zhoushi River alluvial fan. The findings indicated that the CNN-BP model significantly outperformed the BPNN model, achieving (R2 values between 0.94 and 0.98) at various stations, and effectively mitigated time lag issues. The Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer, represents a prominent example of a shallow and hydrogeologically complex aquifer in coastal and alluvial plains, shaped by the interplay of geological, climatic, and anthropogenic factors. In this region, the groundwater level (GWL) remains persistently close to the land surface a condition that not only promotes waterlogging and soil salinization but also elevates the role of evaporation as a dominant, nonlinear driver of GWL dynamics. The proximity of the water table to the surface induces a strong and highly nonlinear coupling between GWL and evapotranspiration. This direct coupling, particularly intensified by high evaporation rates from the capillary fringe even during periods of seasonal waterlogging constitutes the primary challenge in accurately modeling GWL behavior in such systems. Conventional modeling approaches, which often treat evaporation as a boundary condition or linear forcing term, fail to capture this dynamic feedback, leading to significant predictive inaccuracies. Although Artificial Intelligence-based models, particularly Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), have achieved success in groundwater level prediction, their performance is systematically affected by fundamental limitations. These inherent limitations include a strong reliance on local optimization algorithms that easily become trapped in local minima within high-dimensional and non-linear parameter spaces, and an inability to optimally tune complex model structures under conditions where input data is characterized by high noise, uncertainty, or severe non-linear relationships (such as the connection between evaporation and water level in shallow aquifers). Therefore, integrating ANN with metaheuristic algorithms can mitigate these limitations; however, most previous works have utilized classical algorithms, which themselves face challenges including premature convergence, the need for manual parameter tuning, and inefficiency in complex search spaces. In this regard, two novel metaheuristic algorithms, the Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) and the Pelican Optimization Algorithm (POA), were selected to optimize the ANN for the complex conditions of the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer due to their unique structural features. Although advanced models such as Bayesian networks, ensemble methods (e.g., XGBoost, Random Forest), and deep learning models (e.g., LSTM, CNN, RNN) networks have been successfully applied to groundwater level (GWL) prediction, they exhibit fundamental limitations under the specific conditions of shallow aquifers with high evaporation influence. Bayesian networks operate under linear or quasi-linear probabilistic assumptions and are incapable of capturing the complex nonlinear interactions between evaporation and GWL dynamics, particularly during waterlogging events. Moreover, their performance is highly sensitive to large training datasets and structural priors, which are often unavailable in data-scarce regions such as the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer, leading to elevated predictive uncertainty. Ensemble tree-based models, while effective in feature selection, struggle to generalize continuous, physically coherent relationships such as the gradual response of GWL to sustained evaporation fluxes due to their discrete, piecewise nature. LSTM models, despite their capacity to learn temporal dependencies, require high-temporal-resolution data (daily or hourly) to perform reliably; when applied to monthly, noisy datasets as in this study they frequently suffer from overfitting or convergence failure. In contrast, the ANN-HBO and ANN-POA models overcome these limitations by synergistically combining the nonlinear approximation capability of ANNs with the global optimization power of the Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) and Pelican Optimization Algorithm (POA). These metaheuristics enable robust parameter tuning without requiring high-frequency data or structural assumptions, effectively navigating high-dimensional, noisy search spaces to minimize model sensitivity to uncertainty and improve predictive stability. This study develops two novel hybrid models ANN-HBO and ANN-POA to address the limitations of conventional and deep learning approaches in predicting groundwater levels in shallow aquifers under strong evaporation influence, where data are sparse, noisy, and characterized by complex nonlinear dynamics. By leveraging global optimization through the Honey Badger Optimization (HBO) and Pelican Optimization Algorithm (POA) to tune ANN parameters, these models enhance prediction accuracy, stability, and generalizability in conditions where traditional models fail due to their reliance on high-frequency data, linear assumptions, or tree-based structures incapable of capturing continuous physical feedbacks.

Investigated region

Northern Iran is a region where precipitation is significantly higher than the Iranian average, and consequently, groundwater in this area typically exhibits a different behavior compared to other aquifers in the country. Due to adequate recharge and the soil’s nature, the groundwater table is predominantly close to the surface, and the groundwater level follows an irregular pattern. The Astaneh-Kuchesfahan plain aquifer is one of the important water supply sources for Gilan Province, formed on the alluvial fan of the Sefidrud River, with an area of approximately 1100 square kilometers. Although the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer is often labeled as having “high potential” due to its shallow water table and moderate recharge, this reflects socio-hydrological accessibility, not resource abundance. Its shallow depth enables low-cost extraction via shallow wells, making it the dominant source of water for domestic and agricultural use in rural Gilan, where groundwater satisfies over 85% of irrigation demand. However, this same accessibility coupled with high evaporation, poor drainage, and intensive seasonal pumping renders the system highly susceptible to degradation, including waterlogging, salinization, and quality decline. This study focuses on shallow groundwater not because it is abundant, but because it is critically vulnerable: accurate prediction is essential for sustainable management in this high-stress, data-limited environment.The Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer is an unconfined, shallow alluvial system with a water table typically less than 5 m below ground surface, characteristic of coastal plains in Gilan Province. No significant confined aquifer units exist within the immediate study area; deeper aquifers (> 50 m) are hydraulically disconnected from the shallow system due to the presence of low-permeability clay layers, as confirmed by regional hydrogeological cross-sections provided by the Gilan Regional Water Company. Consequently, groundwater abstraction occurs exclusively from the unconfined aquifer, primarily for agricultural irrigation. The average annual abstraction volume over the 2005–2022 period is estimated at approximately 42 million cubic meters (MCM), corresponding to a mean monthly abstraction rate of ~ 3.5 MCM/month, derived from official well licensing records and pumping hour estimates. This extraction rate, combined with intense evaporation and poor natural drainage, exerts significant stress on the shallow aquifer, contributing to waterlogging in wet seasons and salinity buildup during dry periods. Given the absence of vertical connectivity between shallow and deep aquifers, all modeling efforts in this study focus solely on the unconfined system, with abstraction rate included as a dynamic input variable in the prediction models. The spatial extent and water table dynamics of the unconfined Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer were defined based on data from 38 active piezometric wells distributed across the study area, as maintained by the Gilan Regional Water Company. These wells are strategically located to capture groundwater fluctuations in both agricultural and residential zones, with an average spacing of approximately 1.5 km, ensuring adequate spatial coverage for regional-scale modeling. All wells are completed within the shallow alluvial deposits (depths ranging from 8 to 25 m) and screened in the saturated zone of the unconfined aquifer. Monthly groundwater level measurements from these wells were used to represent spatiotemporal variations in the aquifer system and to support the calibration and validation of the proposed models. The modeling framework incorporates both meteorological and anthropogenic inputs to capture the key drivers of groundwater level fluctuations in the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer. Meteorological variables include monthly precipitation, evaporation, and temperature, obtained from the Iranian Meteorological Organization. Human-induced factors are represented by groundwater abstraction rates (in MCM/month), derived from well licensing records and pumping hour logs provided by the Gilan Regional Water Company. All data span a continuous 256-month period from January 2000 to December 2022, ensuring sufficient temporal coverage to account for interannual climate variability and long-term trends in water use. These inputs were used as predictors in both standalone and hybrid ANN models, with all series preprocessed for consistency and stationarity prior to model training. Geology of the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer

Based on the geological profiles of exploratory and piezometric wells drilled in the Astaneh-Kuchesfahan study area, the alluvial aquifer is unconfined and covers an area of 989.51 km2, encompassing the entire plain. The widest part of the Gilan coastal plain in the mentioned area, between the Sefidrud Delta and Imamzadeh Hashem, is approximately 54 km wide (Fig. 4). The Fumanat Heights are located on the left bank (west) of the Sefidrud River, while the Dorfak and Asman Sara Mountains are on the right bank (east). The Caspian Sea borders this area to the north, and the Manjil Dam Lake lies to its south. The aquifer’s constituent material is composed of deltaic and alluvial sediments of the Sefidrud River. In the southern sections, these particles are coarser-grained, creating better quantitative and qualitative conditions. Fine-grained sediments form lenticular clay lenses of varying thickness in different places, complicating the distribution of the confined aquifer. Geologically, the southern part of the aquifer is made up of coarse-grained materials and relatively hard formations, while the northern part of the study area consists of fine-grained materials42. In the aforementioned alluvial aquifer, the elevation of groundwater level contours (equipotential lines) varies between 1 and 10 m. The elevation of groundwater level contours decreases from south to north. In the alluvial fan areas located in the south of the alluvial aquifer, the depth to groundwater is more than 10 m, which decreases northward, reaching its minimum value in the coastal plains of the basin. The total area of groundwater evaporation zones in this alluvial aquifer is generally 921.81 km2, which constitutes 93.21 percent of the total area of the alluvial aquifers. Conversely, in other regions, groundwater depths are less than 1 m, encompassing an area of approximately 17.33 km2 within this alluvial aquifer. The extent of zones with groundwater depths between 1 to 3 m and 3 to 5 m in this alluvial aquifer are 886.51 and 37.97 km2, respectively. Notably, the majority of the aquifer is characterized by a groundwater depth of 2 m. The general trend of groundwater level contours in the aquifer is east–west, and their elevations decrease from south to north. In this aquifer, the elevation of groundwater level contours varies between -25 and 40 m. The maximum groundwater level, with an elevation of 40 m, pertains to the alluvial fan areas located in the south of the alluvial aquifer, and its minimum, at -25 m, is found in the coastal lands located in the north of this aquifer. Generally, across the alluvial aquifer, the convexity of the groundwater level contours is towards the higher elevations, and the groundwater flow lines converge towards the river axis. In the southern lands of the alluvial aquifer, the hydraulic gradient of the groundwater is high, decreasing towards the north43. The groundwater flow direction in this alluvial aquifer is from south to north. Methodology and research methods

Monthly hydroclimatic and groundwater data spanning 256 consecutive months (from 2002–2020) were obtained from the Gilan Regional Water Company and the Iranian Meteorological Organization. Data Preprocessing is a critical stage in any Machine Learning (ML) project, as the quality of the input data directly impacts the performance and accuracy of the resulting model. The objective of this operation is to transform raw data into a clean, consistent, and suitable format for model training. Data preprocessing operations typically encompass four main stages: Data Cleaning (handling deficiencies, errors, and inappropriate data), Data Integration (merging and removing duplicates), Data Reduction (dimensionality reduction, feature selection, feature extraction, and numerosity reduction), and Data Transformation (normalization/scaling and standardization).Correct preprocessing ensures that Machine Learning models focus on learning the real patterns inherent in the data rather than spending time addressing noise and inconsistency. This directly leads to improved Generalization and reduced model error. All input and target variables were scaled to the [0, 1] interval using Min–Max normalization. Metaheuristic algorithms employed

Metaheuristic algorithms are a powerful class of optimization techniques that are crucial for solving complex problems that are either impossible or extremely challenging to handle with conventional optimization techniques. These algorithms are often inspired by natural phenomena such as biological evolution, animal social behavior, or physical processes. A powerful hybrid model that can significantly improve prediction performance is created by combining metaheuristic optimization algorithms with artificial neural networks. ANNs are excellent prediction tools due to their natural ability to extract complex and non-linear patterns from data. However, the performance of an ANN depends critically on the correctness of its internal parameters, such as weights and biases. Algorithms like gradient descent are commonly used in the ANN training process (updating weights and biases), but they can converge slowly or become trapped in local optima as a result of poor learning rate selection. As a result, groundwater level prediction will be significantly impacted by this hybrid combination. The model can learn the intricate relationships between different influencing factors (like rainfall, temperature, water abstraction, etc.) and groundwater levels with much greater accuracy if the ANN’s parameters are precisely optimized using metaheuristic algorithms. As a result, groundwater level change estimates become more accurate, prediction accuracy rises, and model error decreases. In the end, more precise forecasts offer a strong basis for improved choices in agricultural planning, water resource management, and tackling climate change issues. Research model hyperparameters

When creating and implementing predictive models, it is essential to precisely define hyperparameters and their ideal values, particularly for intricate issues like groundwater level prediction. Because the final model performance is heavily reliant on these initial settings, this significance applies to both metaheuristic optimization models and ANNs. In addition to greatly enhancing the accuracy of groundwater level predictions, the ideal hyperparameter tuning for both types of models also increase the stability and generalizability of the models. In order to manage water resources sustainably and make strategic decisions in the face of climate change and increasing demands, the model must be able to function consistently under novel circumstances and with data that has not yet been seen. Models with poor, erratic, and unreliable performance may arise from ignoring this factor. Results

The accurate prediction of groundwater level fluctuations under heterogeneous spatiotemporal conditions requires robust, data-driven modeling frameworks capable of capturing nonlinear system dynamics while maintaining generalization capacity across unseen data. In this study, the predictive performance of standalone ANN, an ANN hybridized with the Honey Badger Optimization algorithm (ANN-HBO) and the Pelican Optimization Algorithm (ANN-POA) were systematically evaluated across four distinct input scenarios (S1–S4) and three observation wells, using a comprehensive suite of statistical metrics including Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Nash–Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE). The results demonstrate that the integration of metaheuristic optimization algorithms with ANN architectures significantly enhances both training accuracy and, more critically, out-of-sample generalization, although the magnitude of improvement is strongly modulated by the complexity of the input feature set and the inherent hydrogeological characteristics of each observation well. Table 6 shows results of GWL Prediction in Selected Wells Using ANN and Optimization Algorithms. At Observation Well 1, the ANN-POA model under scenario S3 achieved the most balanced performance, with training MAE and MSE values of 0.245 m and 0.114, respectively, and corresponding test values of 0.255 m and 0.133. However, the superior generalization capability in the test set was exhibited by ANN-POA under scenario S1, which yielded the highest NSE (0.585) and lowest RMSE (0.379 m) among all configurations, indicating a more reliable capacity to capture the underlying hydrological dynamics under external validation. Notably, the standalone ANN under scenario S2 demonstrated excellent training performance (NSE = 0.711) but suffered a severe degradation in test accuracy (NSE = 0.116), clearly indicative of overfitting. In contrast, the hybrid models particularly ANN-HBO under scenario S4 achieved not only high training efficiency (NSE = 0.805) but also maintained a substantially higher level of predictive stability during testing (NSE = 0.533), underscoring the role of metaheuristic optimization in mitigating the risk of local minima and enhancing structural robustness. Observation Well 2 exhibited markedly improved overall model performance and reduced error magnitudes across all configurations, likely attributable to a more stable and less noisy groundwater response, possibly due to reduced anthropogenic interference or more homogeneous aquifer properties. In this well, ANN-POA under scenario S4 emerged as the most effective model, achieving the lowest MAE (0.213 m), lowest RMSE (0.260 m), and highest NSE (0.603) in the test set among all model-scenario combinations. Furthermore, ANN-HBO under the same scenario also performed exceptionally well, reinforcing the notion that nature-inspired optimization algorithms are particularly effective when input features are comprehensive and physiographically representative. Critically, both hybrid models consistently achieved MAPE values below 17% in the test set, highlighting their capacity to produce reliable relative error estimates under real-world conditions a key requirement for operational groundwater management. The most striking performance was observed at Observation Well 2, where all models exhibited significantly enhanced accuracy and lower error metrics compared to the other two wells, suggesting a more systematic, less stochastic groundwater response possibly indicative of a more confined or less disturbed aquifer system. Here, ANN-POA under scenario S1 attained the best overall performance across the entire study, with a test NSE of 0.874 and RMSE of 0.437 m. Even the worst-performing configuration in this well (ANN under S4) retained an NSE above 0.44, underscoring the inherent predictability of this location. Furthermore, MAPE values in this well were consistently the lowest across all sites, with several configurations falling below 7%, indicating that absolute and relative prediction errors are not only small but also operationally acceptable for practical water resource planning. Collectively, these findings highlight two fundamental insights. First, the hybridization of ANN with metaheuristic optimization algorithms particularly POA and HBO consistently improves model generalization by reducing overfitting and enhancing convergence toward globally optimal weight configurations, thereby increasing reliability under unseen conditions. Second, the structure and comprehensiveness of input variables play a decisive role in model performance: scenarios incorporating a broader range of hydroclimatic and temporal drivers (e.g., S3 and S4) consistently yielded superior predictive accuracy, particularly in the test phase. These outcomes are consistent with prior studies emphasizing the critical importance of feature selection and the integration of intelligent optimization techniques to navigate complex, nonlinear hydrological systems. Importantly, the marked spatial variability in model performance across the three wells underscores the necessity of adopting spatially explicit modeling frameworks in future studies. We therefore recommend the development of multi-well, spatially distributed hybrid models that explicitly account for heterogeneity in aquifer properties and regional groundwater dynamics, thereby enabling more robust, scalable, and policy-relevant predictions for sustainable groundwater management. From a modeling perspective, the complex structure of this time series combining seasonality, interannual variability, and potential regime shifts poses significant challenges for predictive algorithms. High-frequency fluctuations demand models capable of accurately capturing short-term dynamics, while amplitude variations require robustness against data non-stationarity. The predictive success of the hybrid models particularly ANN-POA cannot be evaluated in isolation from the hydrogeological context in which groundwater dynamics unfold. The consistent superiority of ANN-POA across all three observation wells, as evidenced by its superior convergence speed, minimal prediction error, and tight alignment with observed distributions, is not merely a computational achievement but a reflection of its capacity to capture the nonlinear, multi-scale interactions governing shallow aquifer behavior in this region. The rapid and stable convergence of POA, for instance, suggests that the algorithm effectively navigates the complex, non-stationary parameter space induced by the heterogeneous lithology and variable recharge-discharge regimes characteristic of shallow systems, where hydraulic conductivity changes abruptly over short distances and anthropogenic abstraction introduce irregular temporal forcing. The fact that prediction accuracy was highest at Well 2 a location with more stable, less fluctuating water levels aligns with field observations that this zone likely corresponds to a more consolidated or partially confined stratum, where seasonal recharge is buffered by lower permeability layers and abstraction impacts are more localized. In contrast, the greater variability and higher error at Well 1, despite robust model performance, point to a more dynamic and vulnerable aquifer setting possibly dominated by unconfined conditions with direct surface connectivity, high evaporation potential, and intensive localized pumping for agriculture or domestic use. This spatial heterogeneity in model error is not noise to be minimized, but a diagnostic signature of underlying hydrogeological structure: the higher sensitivity of Well 1 to input variability suggests it lies closer to a recharge boundary or near a zone of aquifer thinning, where small changes in rainfall or abstraction translate into large hydraulic responses. The marked improvement in model reliability when using comprehensive input scenarios (S3 and S4), which included multi-month lagged rainfall, temperature, and land use proxies, further confirms that the system’s behavior is not driven by isolated variables but by integrated hydroclimatic forcing a reality often overlooked in purely statistical modeling. Crucially, theabsence of long-term trends in groundwater levels across all wells, despite the known intensification of water demand in the region, implies that the aquifer system may still possess a degree of resilience perhaps due to natural recharge from ephemeral channels or deep percolation but this resilience is fragile. The increasing frequency of low-amplitude fluctuations in recent years, particularly visible in Well 1, may signal the early onset of system stress, where cumulative abstraction is beginning to dampen seasonal recovery. In this context, the high precision of ANN-POA is not an end in itself, but a critical tool for early detection: its ability to distinguish subtle, non-linear shifts in behavior such as the delayed response to rainfall or the altered seasonal amplitude provides a quantitative basis for identifying emerging threats to aquifer sustainability before they manifest as irreversible declines. This capability, when integrated with field monitoring and local hydrogeological knowledge, transforms the model from a predictive engine into a diagnostic instrument for adaptive water management enabling planners to anticipate drawdown hotspots, prioritize recharge interventions, and calibrate abstraction quotas based not on averages, but on the actual, nonlinear response of the aquifer to human and climatic pressures. Discussion

The use of meteorological variables such as temperature and precipitation along with evaporation is crucial in aquifers where the water table is close to the ground surface. It’s estimated that if the difference between the groundwater level and the ground surface is less than 5 m, the groundwater level is directly influenced by evaporation. The Astaneh-Kuchesfahan aquifer is one such aquifer that exhibits these conditions. Therefore, it is considerably affected by temperature and direct evaporation. Until now, there has been limited research on forecasting groundwater levels in such aquifers using machine learning models. Hence, for the first time in this study, an attempt was made to evaluate new hybrid models for these conditions. The results indicated that the impact of temperature and evaporation, along with rainfall, is significant. The aforementioned variables have a substantial effect on groundwater level fluctuations, a similar observation previously noted for other aquifers, which can be found in the research by Fathi et al. (2025)47. The use of evolutionary algorithms improved the accuracy of groundwater level prediction. Both algorithms performed acceptably in predicting groundwater levels, with POA demonstrating better performance in most models. This indicates that using algorithms is consistently one of the effective strategies to enhance the accuracy of groundwater level prediction with machine learning models. The findings of this study are also consistent with the results of Fathi et al. (2025) on using hybrid models for groundwater level prediction; Milan et al.11 on using ANFIS-HHO and ANFIS-GWO models for predicting optimal groundwater extraction. This alignment further demonstrates that employing hybrid models is an effective method in groundwater resource management. In this study, a monthly time step was considered because data collection in Iranian aquifers is conducted on a monthly basis. Therefore, it was not possible to examine the algorithms’ capabilities at a daily time step. This aspect can be evaluated in future research. Additionally, information such as groundwater abstraction is recorded only once every five years, leading to uncertainty that was not addressed in this study. Thus, this factor can be considered in future investigations. The use of novel algorithms and machine learning models is always recommended for such aquifers.

This study confirms that in shallow aquifers like Astaneh-Kuchesfahan where the water table is within 5 m of the surface evaporation, temperature, and rainfall are primary drivers of dynamics, not secondary inputs. Standalone ANN models fail to capture their nonlinear, lagged responses, leading to overfitting, while hybrid models especially ANN-POA significantly improve accuracy and stability by better aligning with the physical complexity of these systems. POA’s superior performance reflects its aptitude for navigating noisy, sparse, and seasonally forced data typical of arid regions. Despite limitations in temporal resolution and abstraction data, the model’s low MAPE (< 7%) demonstrates strong practical potential for water management under data constraints. Future work should incorporate daily data, drought indices, and digital twin frameworks to enable proactive, climate-resilient aquifer governance.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that hybrid AI models ANN-POA and ANN-HBO serve as mechanistic interpreters of shallow aquifer dynamics under climate stress, moving beyond prediction to reveal hidden hydrogeological processes. In systems where the water table lies within 5 m of the surface, evaporation, temperature, and rainfall act as dominant, nonlinear drivers with significant temporal lags behaviors poorly captured by conventional models. These hybrid frameworks successfully encode aquifer heterogeneity and memory effects, transforming model output into a diagnostic tool: spatial variations in performance directly reflect localized features such as recharge zones or overexploitation, invisible to traditional surveys. Crucially, the models detect early signs of degradation not through long-term decline, but through increasingly frequent low-amplitude fluctuations and weakened seasonal recovery signaling a transition toward chronic stress in systems with minimal buffering capacity. The approach is transferable to other arid and semi-arid regions with comparable hydrogeology and sparse data, offering a scalable pathway for adaptive groundwater governance. We recommend integrating these models into management frameworks to inform well-field zoning, recharge planning, and drought response. Future work must prioritize real-time data assimilation, formal uncertainty quantification, and the fusion of AI with process-based models to create physics-informed hybrids. To ensure reproducibility, the complete Python implementations of all models, with documentation and sample data, are provided as a supplementary repository and will be made publicly available upon publication.

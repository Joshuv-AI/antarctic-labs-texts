Scaling solutions for gauge invariant flow equations in dilaton quantum gravity
Yadikaer Maitiniyazi, Christof Wetterich, Masatoshi Yamada

Abstract
We investigate the effects of quantum gravity for models of a scalar singlet coupled to the metric. Such models describe inflation for early cosmology and dynamical dark energy for late cosmology. We work within the “variable gravity approximation” keeping in the effective action an arbitrary field dependence for terms with up to two derivatives. We focus on the scaling solutions of the gauge invariant functional flow equation which describes the dependence of the effective action on a momentum or length scale. The existence of such solutions is required for the ultraviolet fixed point defining an ultraviolet complete renormalizable quantum field theory for gravity. Our findings strengthen the case for the presence of a “dilaton quantum gravity fixed point” for which the Planck mass increases proportional to the scalar field for large field values. This fixed point is different from the extended Reuter fixed point with a flat scalar potential and field-independent Planck mass, which is also seen in our setting.

The gauge invariant flow equation is in this approximation identical to the background field method with a particular “physical gauge fixing”, which removes the gauge modes in the metric without affecting the “physical fluctuations”

Flow equation and scaling equation

Our starting point is flow equation for the effective average action
170

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]\,,

where
\partial_{t}=k\partial_{k}
.
Here
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is the infrared cutoff,
\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}
the second functional derivatives of
\Gamma_{k}
and
(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k})^{-1}
the propagator in the presence of fields and the infrared cutoff.
For a discussion of the properties of the flow equation and its application in many fields we refer to Refs.

We define dimensionless quantities by

\displaystyle u(\tilde{\rho})=U(\rho)/k^{4}\,,

\displaystyle w(\tilde{\rho})=F(\rho)/2k^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\kappa(\tilde{\rho})=K(\rho)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}=\rho/k^{2}\,.

(4)

The flow equation for
u
can be obtained by evaluating

for a constant scalar field and flat metric

For

\kappa
one adds small inhomogeneities in the macroscopic field

One obtains

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\beta_{U}=-\partial_{\tau}u-4u+\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}M_{U}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w

\displaystyle=\beta_{F}\ =-\partial_{\tau}w-2w-\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}M_{F}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\kappa

\displaystyle=\beta_{K}=-\partial_{\tau}\kappa+M_{K}\,,

(7)

where

The computation of

The switch from fixed

introduces terms involving

\displaystyle-\tilde{\phi}\,\partial_{\tilde{\phi}}=-2{\tilde{\rho}}\,\partial_{\tilde{\rho}}=\partial_{\tau}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\tau=-\log\sqrt{2\tilde{\rho}}=-\log|\tilde{\phi}|=-\log|\phi/k|\,.

(9)

The terms

A possible scaling solution obtains as a solution of

This generalizes the notion of a fixed point for a finite number of couplings.
The functions

and
\kappa
encode an infinite number of couplings, defined, for example, as the coefficients of an expansion around
\tilde{\rho}=0

For the scaling solution all these couplings take fixed values.
We focus on this type of scaling solution.
Further scaling solutions may be obtained by switching to renormalized dimensionless scaling fields reflecting the presence of an anomalous dimension.
Such fields are related to
\tilde{\rho}
by a non-linear field transformation, see below.

The scaling solution for the functions
u(\tilde{\rho},k)
etc. requires

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\partial_{t}w=\partial_{t}\kappa=0\,.

(10)

It has therefore to obey the “scaling equations”

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}u(\tilde{\rho})=-4u(\tilde{\rho})+\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}M_{U}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}w(\tilde{\rho})=-2w(\tilde{\rho})-\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}M_{F}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\kappa(\tilde{\rho})=M_{K}\,,

(13)

Its form is very similar to the flow equations for the dependence of

u_{0}=u(\tilde{\rho}=0)
,
w_{0}=w(\tilde{\rho}=0)
,
\kappa_{0}=\kappa(\tilde{\rho}=0)
.
The scaling equations determine, however, three functions of
\tilde{\rho}

This is of key importance for their possible solutions.
They have to be defined for the whole range
0\leq\tilde{\rho}<\infty
.
As common for differential equations the corresponding boundary conditions pose severe constraints.
The flow kernels

The general local solution therefore depends on six initial values

These initial values define a family of local scaling solutions.
The boundary conditions for a global solution for all
\tilde{\rho}
selects a subset of this family.

IV
Infrared and ultraviolet fixed points

The IR and UV fixed point behavior for
\tilde{\rho}\to\infty
and
\tilde{\rho}\to 0
is seen clearly in
Figures

1
and
2
.
It can be understood quantitatively.
For the limits
\tilde{\rho}\to\infty
and
\tilde{\rho}\to 0
the flow generators can be expanded in power of
\tilde{\rho}^{-1}
or
\tilde{\rho}
, respectively.
Keeping only few terms we can write down explicit expressions.
This permits an analytical treatment of the IR and UV fixed points.

IV.1
Large field expansion and IR fixed point

The properties of the IR fixed point are easy to understand.
With dimensionless Planck mass
\sqrt{2\xi_{\infty}\tilde{\rho}}
going to infinity the gravitational coupling approaches zero.
Thus the metric fluctuations decouple, except for a constant contribution
M^{\text{gr}}_{U}=2
, reflecting the two degrees of freedom of the massless graviton.
For constant
u(\tilde{\rho}\to\infty)=u_{\infty}
the scalar potential is flat and we are left with a massless scalar field.
For
\kappa(\tilde{\rho}\to\infty)=\kappa_{\infty}
there are no interactions from the kinetic term either.
We are then left with a decoupled system of a free massless scalar and a massless graviton.
The contribution of the massless scalar to the flow of the cosmological constant
U(\tilde{\rho}\to\infty)
is given by
M_{U}^{\text{s}}=1
.
The scaling solution for
u(\tilde{\rho}\to\infty)
is given by
u=M_{U}/(128\pi^{2})
, resulting in

\displaystyle u_{\infty}=\frac{3}{128\pi^{2}}\approx 0.00238\,.

(14)

This is the value which is approached in
Figures

1
and
2
.
For the scaling solution the cosmological constant
U=u_{\infty}k^{4}
vanishes for
k\to 0
.
Since gravity effectively decouples the non-minimal coupling
\xi
and the kinetial
\kappa
become independent of
\tilde{\rho}
.
For the IR fixed point they are free parameters.
Since physics is not affected by a change of the normalization of the scalar field only the ratio
\varepsilon_{\infty}=\kappa_{\infty}/\xi_{\infty}
matters.
We end with a continuous family of IR fixed points parametrized by
\varepsilon_{\infty}
.
Stability requires
\varepsilon_{\infty}>-6
.
Since
\kappa(\tilde{\rho})
becomes a constant also
k\partial_{k}K
becomes a constant.
There is therefore no anomalous dimension for the scalar field, implying that
\phi/k
is the appropriate dimensionless renormalized field for the IR fixed point.

For finite large
\tilde{\rho}
the gravitational effects start to matter, being suppressed by powers of
\tilde{\rho}^{-1}
.
The scalar field and the metric are no longer decoupled.
In this large field region we can perform a series expansion.
To this end, we define

and make the expansion ansatz

\displaystyle u(y)

\displaystyle=u_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{n}}{n!\,\xi_{\infty}^{n}}y^{n}=u_{\infty}+\frac{s_{1}}{\xi_{\infty}}y+\cdots\,,

\displaystyle w(y)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\infty}y^{-1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{n}}{n!\,\xi_{\infty}^{n}}y^{n}=\xi_{\infty}y^{-1}+t_{0}+\frac{t_{1}}{\xi_{\infty}}y+\cdots\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\kappa(y)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\infty}\xi_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}}{n!\,\xi_{\infty}^{n-1}}y^{n}=\varepsilon_{\infty}\xi_{\infty}+k_{1}y+\cdots\,,

where
\varepsilon_{\infty}=\kappa_{\infty}/\xi_{\infty}
.
Switching to the variable

we expand the scaling solution in terms of

\displaystyle=2x\partial_{x}u(x)=-4u(x)+A_{u}+\frac{C_{u}}{B}x=2s_{1}x+\cdots\,,

IV.2
Small field expansion and UV fixed point

With initial conditions for some small

The scaling solution has to exist for the whole range of an appropriate dimensionless “scaling field”.
A priori, it is not obvious which is the form of the scaling field in the UV region.

Due to the simple properties of the IR fixed point, which describes a free theory, the anomalous dimension vanishes in the IR limit, justifying the choice of

VI
Conclusions

We have investigated the scaling solution for dilaton quantum gravity in the variable gravity approximation.
It defines an ultraviolet complete quantum field theory for a scalar singlet coupled to the metric, as used for models of inflation or dynamical dark energy.
We employ the gauge invariant flow equation, which focuses on physical fluctuations, respecting diffeomorphism symmetry and global dilatation symmetry.

Our results for the overall features of the dimensionless scalar potential
u
and the dimensionless squared Planck mass
w
are in accordance with earlier investigations.
As a function of the squared dimensionless scalar field
\tilde{\rho}
one finds an almost flat potential
u(\tilde{\rho})
and a Planck mass increasing for large values of the scalar field,
w(\tilde{\rho})\approx w_{0}+\xi_{\infty}\tilde{\rho}
.
These central properties of the dilaton quantum gravity fixed point do not depend on the particular version of the functional flow equation or on the precise truncation.
This enhances the robustness of the arguments in favor of the existence of such an ultraviolet fixed point.

The use of the gauge invariant flow equation may contribute to a better understanding of the field-dependence of the kinetial—the coefficient in front of the scalar kinetic term.
Due to its focus on the physical fluctuations it can take well into account the mixing of the kinetic terms for the scalar field and the physical scalar fluctuation in the metric.
In particular, it allows us to explore a negative sign of the kinetial in the region of large
\tilde{\rho}
, as long as it remains consistent with a stability criterion requiring a positive kinetic term for the eigenstate corresponding to the inflaton or cosmon.

Within our approximation we find two different types of scaling solutions, corresponding to two different ultraviolet fixed points.
For the extended Reuter fixed point the potential
u(\tilde{\rho})
and the squared Planck mass
w(\tilde{\rho})
are independent of
\tilde{\rho}
.
Such a scaling solution employs a renormalized scaling field.
The dependence of
K
on
k
involves a nonvanishing anomalous field dimension
\eta
.
We compute the quantum gravity contribution to
\eta
.

The second UV-fixed point is the dilaton quantum gravity fixed point with
w(\tilde{\rho})
increasing
\sim\tilde{\rho}
for large
\tilde{\rho}
.
Our truncation and numerical solution seem to be compatible with a continuous family of scaling solutions parametrized by
B
.
This family includes the possibility of a negative kinetial for large
\tilde{\rho}
.
For the scaling solutions found in the present paper the anomalous field dimension
\eta
vanishes for all
\tilde{\rho}
.
It remains to be seen if further scaling solutions can be found with
\eta
depending on
\tilde{\rho}
, vanishing only for
\tilde{\rho}\to\infty
.
Such scaling solutions require a scaling field which is related to the original or microscopic scalar field by a nonlinear field transformation.
On the other hand, more extended truncations may reduce the continuous family of scaling solutions to a discrete set, leave only a single scaling solution, or even exclude the existence of scaling solutions with
w(\tilde{\rho})\sim\tilde{\rho}
for large
\tilde{\rho}
.

At the present stage the argument for the existence of a dilaton quantum gravity fixed point different from the extended Reuter fixed point is strengthened by out investigation.
This fixed point realizes exact quantum scale symmetry in the infrared limit for
\tilde{\rho}\to\infty
.
The issue of quantum scale symmetry for the ultraviolet limit
\tilde{\rho}\to 0
will depend on the anomalous field dimension.

Appendix A
Effective average action for nonminimally coupled scalar-gravity system

The central method in this work is the functional renormalization group (FRG)
172
. For quantum field theory we employ a functional differential equation for the effective average action
170
, (see also
125
;
79
;
143
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}[\Phi]=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[\left({\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}[\Phi]+{\mathcal{R}}_{k}}\right)^{-1}\,\partial_{t}{\mathcal{R}}_{k}\right]\,.

(48)

Here
\partial_{t}=k\partial_{k}
is a dimensionless scale derivative and “STr” in
eq.

48
sums over all internal degrees of freedom of a superfield
\Phi
such as momenta.
The matrix of second functional derivatives
\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}
corresponds to the full two-point function, i.e. full inverse propagator of
\Phi
in which an infrared cutoff profile function
{\mathcal{R}}_{k}
is introduced.

A.1
Setup

In this work, we intend to set up the system of a singlet scalar field
\phi
nonminimally coupled to gravity using the FRG equation
48
. In such a system, we give the effective action as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{k}^{\rm SG}+\Gamma_{\rm gf}+\Gamma_{\rm gh}\,.

(49)

Here, our truncated scalar-gravity part
\Gamma_{k}^{\rm SG}
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{\rm(SG)}=\int_{x}\sqrt{g}\left\{U(\rho)+\frac{K(\rho)}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{F(\rho)}{2}R\right\}\,.

(50)

We have defined the field dependent Planck mass
F(\rho)
, the scalar effective potential
U(\rho)
and the kinetic potential
K(\rho)
as functions of the scalar invariant
\rho=\phi^{2}/2
.

To specify the physical gauge fixing for diffeomorphism acting on the metric fluctuation field
h_{\mu\nu}
, we expand the macroscopic metric around a constant background metric
{\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu}
such that

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}={\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(51)

In this work, we employ the maximally symmetric background space for simplicity, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}=\frac{\bar{R}}{12}\Big(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{g}_{\rho\sigma}-\bar{g}_{\mu\sigma}\bar{g}_{\nu\rho}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\bar{R}}{4}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\,.

(52)

In the current setup for the scalar-gravity sector
50
, this choice provides the same result of the beta functions as the case with a general homogeneous background field. The gauge fixing for diffeomorphism is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm gf}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,{\bar{g}}^{\mu\nu}\Sigma_{\mu}\Sigma_{\nu}\,.

(53)

Here, the general gauge fixing reads

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mu}={\bar{D}}^{\nu}h_{\nu\mu}-\frac{\beta+1}{4}{\bar{D}}_{\mu}h\,,

(54)

with
h={\bar{g}}^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}
the trace mode of metric fluctuations.
Bars on operators denote that covariant derivatives are formed with the background metric, and indices of operators are contracted by the background metric as well.
The ghost action associated with the gauge fixing
54
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm gh}

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\bar{C}_{\mu}\left[{\bar{g}}^{\mu\rho}{\bar{D}}^{2}+\frac{1-\beta}{2}{\bar{D}}^{\mu}{\bar{D}}^{\rho}+{\bar{R}}^{\mu\rho}\right]C_{\rho}\,,

(55)

where
C
and
\bar{C}
are ghost and antighost fields.

Equations

53
and
54
constitute a general family of gauge fixings for diffeomorphism symmetry, specified by two parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.
The choice
\beta=-1
and
\alpha\to 0
is a “physical gauge fixing” that acts only on the gauge modes in
h_{\mu\nu}
.
In this work, we use this gauge choice.
Nevertheless, the next subsection discusses general
\alpha
and
\beta
.
This will demonstrate explicitly the particular role of the physical gauge fixing.

A.2
Physical metric decomposition

A key quantity for the flow equation is the inverse propagator or 1PI two-point function, as given by the matrix of second functional derivatives of
\Gamma_{k}
.
To derive the explicit form for the metric two-point function, we split the metric fluctuations into physical and gauge fluctuations
164
.
Accordingly, we decompose the metric fluctuations into

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}=f_{\mu\nu}+a_{\mu\nu}\,,

(56)

where
f_{\mu\nu}
are the physical metric fluctuations, while
a_{\mu\nu}
the gauge fluctuations or gauge modes.
The physical metric fluctuations satisfy the transverse constraint
\bar{D}^{\mu}f_{\mu\nu}=0
.
In turn, the physical metric fluctuations can be decomposed into two independent fields as

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}=t_{\mu\nu}+s_{\mu\nu}\,,

(57)

where the graviton
t_{\mu\nu}
is the transverse and traceless (TT) tensor, i.e.,
\bar{D}^{\mu}t_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}t_{\mu\nu}=0
.
The tensor
s_{\mu\nu}
is given as a linear function of a scalar field
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle s_{\mu\nu}=\hat{S}_{\mu\nu}\sigma=\frac{1}{3}P_{\mu\nu}\sigma\,.

(58)

For a background geometry with constant curvature, the projection operator can be found explicitly as

\displaystyle P_{\mu\nu}=\bigg(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{\Delta}_{S}+\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}-\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}\bigg)\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)^{-1}=\bigg[\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{4}\right)+\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}\bigg]\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)^{-1}\,,

(59)

with
\bar{\Delta}_{S}=-\bar{D}^{2}=-\bar{D}^{\mu}\bar{D}_{\mu}
the covariant Laplacian acting on scalar fields (spin-0 fields). Here, in the second equality, we have used the maximally symmetric space
52
.

Similarly, the gauge modes or unphysical metric fluctuations
a_{\mu\nu}
are decomposed into a transverse vector mode
\upsilon_{\mu}
, satisfying
\bar{D}^{\mu}\upsilon_{\mu}=0
, and a scalar mode
u
. In summary, the metric fluctuations
56
are parametrized by

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=t_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{3}P_{\mu\nu}\sigma\,,

\displaystyle a_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bar{D}_{\mu}\upsilon_{\nu}+\bar{D}_{\nu}\upsilon_{\mu}-\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}u\,.

(60)

Note here that the trace mode of the metric fluctuations
h=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle h=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\left(t_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{3}P_{\mu\nu}\sigma+\bar{D}_{\mu}\upsilon_{\nu}+\bar{D}_{\nu}\upsilon_{\mu}-\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}u\right)=\sigma+\bar{\Delta}_{S}u\,.

(61)

The ghost fields can be decomposed similarly into vector and scalar fields

\displaystyle C_{\mu}

\displaystyle=C_{\mu}^{\perp}+\bar{D}_{\mu}C\,,

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bar{C}_{\mu}^{\perp}+\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{C}\,,

(62)

where
C_{\mu}^{\perp}
(
\bar{C}_{\mu}^{\perp}
) is the transverse (anti)ghost field and
C
(
\bar{C}
) is the scalar (anti)ghost field.

A.3
Jacobians

The decompositions of the metric fluctuation
60
and the ghost fields
62
yield Jacobians of the path integral measures for different modes.
We start with the Gaussian integral for the metric fluctuations
h_{\mu\nu}
. In the maximally symmetric space
52
, the Gaussian integrals for each mode in the physical metric decomposition read

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int{\mathcal{D}}h_{\mu\nu}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=J_{\text{grav}}\int{\mathcal{D}}t_{\mu\nu}{\mathcal{D}}\sigma{\mathcal{D}\upsilon_{\mu}}\mathcal{D}u\,\exp\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\bigg\{t_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu\nu}+\sigma\hat{S}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{S}_{\alpha\beta}\sigma+2\upsilon_{\mu}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{1}}\right)^{\mu\nu}\upsilon_{\nu}+2u\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{0}}\bar{\Delta}_{S}u\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle=J_{\text{grav}}\left[\det{}^{\prime}_{\text{(1T)}}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{1}}\right)\right]^{-1/2}\left[\det{}^{\prime}_{(0)}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right)\bar{\Delta}_{S}\right]^{-1/2}\left[\det{}^{\prime}_{(0)}\left(\hat{S}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{S}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\right]^{-1/2}\,,

(63)

where
E_{(t)}
is the identical matrix acting on the space satisfying the TT condition.
We have in the maximal symmetric space and
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}t_{\mu\nu}=0
.
One finds the Jacobian arising from the decomposition of the metric fluctuation,

\displaystyle J_{\text{grav}}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{\text{(1T)}}^{\prime}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{1}}\right)\right]^{1/2}\left[\det{}_{(0)}^{\prime}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right)\bar{\Delta}_{S}\right]^{1/2}\left[\det{}^{\prime}_{(0)}\left(\hat{S}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{S}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\right]^{1/2}\equiv J_{\upsilon}\cdot J_{u}\cdot J_{\sigma}\,.

(64)

with differential operators

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}_{V}-\frac{\bar{R}}{4}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{4}\,.

(65)

Here
\bar{\Delta}_{V}=-\bar{D}^{2}
is the Laplacian acting on vector fields (spin-1 fields).
A prime denotes a subtraction of a zero eigenmode. This subtraction, however, does not contribute to the beta functions within the present truncation, so that we hereafter neglect it.
The Jacobian for
\sigma
in the maximally symmetric space
52
can be computed as

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{\text{(0)}}\left(\hat{S}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{S}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\right]^{1/2}=\left[\det{}_{\text{(0)}}\frac{1}{3}\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)^{-1}\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right]^{1/2}\,.

(66)

For the evaluation of fluctuations of
\sigma
, it is convenient to remove
\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)^{-1}
. This can be done by redefining the field
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}=\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)^{-1}\sigma\,,

(67)

for which the Jacobian
66
reads

\displaystyle J_{\tilde{\sigma}}=\left[\det{}_{\text{(0)}}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right)\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)\right]^{1/2}\,.

(68)

In the same manner, the Gauss integral for the ghost fields is

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}C_{\mu}\mathcal{D}\bar{C}_{\mu}\,\exp\left(\bar{C}_{\mu}\bar{C}^{\mu}\right)=J_{\rm gh}\int\mathcal{D}C_{\mu}^{\perp}\mathcal{D}\bar{C}_{\mu}^{\perp}\mathcal{D}C\mathcal{D}\bar{C}\,\exp\left(\bar{C}^{\perp}_{\mu}\bar{C}^{\perp\mu}+\bar{C}\bar{\Delta}_{S}C\right)=J_{\rm gh}\left[\det{}_{(0)}(\bar{\Delta}_{S})\right]\,,

(69)

namely one has

\displaystyle J_{\rm gh}=\left[\det{}_{(0)}\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}\right)\right]^{-1}\,.

(70)

To summarize, the decompositions yield Jacobians are found to be

\displaystyle J_{\upsilon}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{\text{(1T)}}^{\prime}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}\right)\right]^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle J_{\tilde{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{(0)}^{\prime}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right)\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3}\right)\right]^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle J_{u}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{(0)}^{\prime}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\right)\bar{\Delta}_{S}\right]^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle J_{\text{gh}}

\displaystyle=\left[\det{}_{(0)}^{\prime\prime}\left(\bar{\Delta}_{S}\right)\right]^{-1}\,.

(71)

By the same reason in
eq.

64
, we neglect the primes denoting the subtraction of zeromodes.
For the regularized Jacobians we replace the relevant differential operator
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
by
P_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}})
to avoid the Jacobians to induce strong nonlocalities.
Then, these Jacobians are written in forms of flow equations as

\displaystyle J_{\upsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,_{\rm(1T)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1})}{P_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1})}\,,

(72)

\displaystyle J_{\tilde{\sigma}}=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,_{(0)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0})}{P_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0})}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,_{(0)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3})}{P_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S}-\frac{\bar{R}}{3})}\,,

(73)

\displaystyle J_{u}=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,_{(0)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0})}{P_{k}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{0})}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,_{(0)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S})}{P_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S})}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle J_{\text{gh}}={\rm Tr}\,_{(0)}\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S})}{P_{k}(\bar{\Delta}_{S})}\,.

(75)

Appendix B
Variations

To obtain the Hessian, we need to compute the variations for the effective action
49
. Hence, we expand the effective action around background fields into polynomials of fluctuation fields:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}[\Phi]=\Gamma_{k}[\bar{\Phi}]+\Gamma_{k}^{(1)}[\bar{\Phi}](x)\cdot\delta\Phi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\delta\Phi(x)\cdot\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}[\bar{\Phi}](x,y)\cdot\delta\Phi(y)+\cdots\,,

(76)

from which the Hessian is extracted from
\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}[\bar{\Phi}](x,y)=\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma_{k}}{\delta\Phi(x)\delta\Phi(y)}|_{\Phi=\bar{\Phi}}

Here, the metric field and the scalar field are linearly expanded around their background fields denoted by
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
and
\bar{\phi}
such that

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\phi=\bar{\phi}+\varphi\,.

(77)

For our effective action

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{\rm(SG)}[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]=\int_{x}\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\frac{K(\rho)}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+\int_{x}\sqrt{g}U\!\left(\rho\right)-\int_{x}\frac{F\!\left(\rho\right)}{2}\sqrt{g}R\,,

(78)

the second variation reads

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\Gamma_{k}^{\rm(SG)}[\bar{\Phi}]

\displaystyle=\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\left\{2\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}K(\bar{\rho})\right\}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi+\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\left[\{\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}(\delta^{2}K(\rho))\big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}\}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\bar{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\bar{\phi}+\{4\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\delta K(\rho)\big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}\}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\bar{\phi}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}H_{2}^{\mu\nu}K(\bar{\rho})\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\bar{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\bar{\phi}+\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}2H_{1}^{\mu\nu}\left(2K(\bar{\rho})\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\bar{\phi}+(\delta K(\rho)\big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}})\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\bar{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\bar{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\left[G_{2}U(\bar{\rho})+2G_{1}\delta U(\rho)+\delta^{2}U\!\left(\rho\right)\right]\bigg|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}-\int_{x}\sqrt{\bar{g}}\frac{1}{2}\left[I_{2}F(\bar{\rho})+2I_{1}\delta F(\rho)+\delta^{2}F\!\left(\rho\right)\bar{R}\right]\bigg|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}\,.

(79)

Here, we have defined

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\mu\nu}=\delta(\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu})/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=\frac{1}{2}h\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle H_{2}^{\mu\nu}=\delta^{2}(\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu})/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{4}h^{2}\right)\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}+2h^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}h^{\nu\lambda}-hh^{\mu\nu}\,,

(81)

\displaystyle G_{1}=\delta\sqrt{g}/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=\frac{1}{2}h\,,

(82)

\displaystyle G_{2}=\delta^{2}\sqrt{g}/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=-\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{4}h^{2}+\bar{D}_{\mu}\bar{D}_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}+\bar{\Delta}_{S}h\,,

(83)

\displaystyle I_{1}=\delta(\sqrt{g}R)/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{R}h-\bar{R}^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(84)

\displaystyle I_{2}=\delta^{2}(\sqrt{g}R)/\sqrt{\bar{g}}=-\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha\beta}\bar{\Delta}_{T}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{R}h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+h^{\alpha\beta}\bar{R}_{\alpha}{}^{\rho}h_{\rho\beta}+h\bar{D}^{\mu}\bar{D}^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}+\bar{D}^{\mu}h_{\mu\alpha}\bar{D}^{\nu}h_{\nu}{}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}h\bar{\Delta}_{S}h+h^{\alpha\beta}\bar{R}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}-h\bar{R}^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{4}\bar{R}h^{2}\,,

(85)

and the variations for the scalar potentials
K
,
F
and

\displaystyle\delta K(\rho)\Big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}=(K^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\bar{\phi})\varphi\,,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}K(\rho)\Big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}=(K^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}K^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\rho}))\varphi^{2}\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\delta U(\rho)\Big|_{\phi=\bar{\phi}}=(U^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\bar{\phi})\varphi\,,

Let us briefly summarize the formulas of the heat kernel method in gravity. Suppose that the flow generator
\zeta_{k}
takes

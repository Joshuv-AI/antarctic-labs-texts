Geometric Attention A Regime-Explicit Operator Semantics for Transformer Attention
Luis Rosario Freytes

Abstract
Geometric Attention (GA) specifies an attention layer by four independent inputs: a finite carrier (what indices are addressable), an evidence-kernel rule (how masked proto-scores and a link induce nonnegative weights), a probe family (which observables are treated as admissible), and an anchor/update rule (which representative kernel is selected and how it is applied). Probe families induce an operational equivalence relation on kernels and therefore a gauge; anchors select representatives relative to that probe. Under a scalar relational-work representation and a multiplicative compositionality law for evidence (PruneW), any admissible link is exponential, yielding Gibbs weights; with row anchoring this includes the softmax kernel family as a subregime. After quotienting unary row/column score fields, the remaining interaction component admits a canonical rank- r r normal form (Eckart–Young/SVD); dot-product score charts implement the corresponding low-rank interaction regime. Fixing the carrier and extensionalizing the update yields the standard fixed-token Transformer attention operator; allowing carrier updates yields adaptive-carrier and staged-depth regimes. The operator language also supports multihead/mixed kernels, plan-based anchors (e.g. entropic OT/Sinkhorn), and unary operators (e.g. FFN-style fields) as explicit regime choices.



1
Operational stance and what is being derived

Problem statement.

An attention update layer is specified by a tuple of four independent objects:
(i) a finite carrier (what indices are addressable),
(ii) an evidence-kernel rule (how masked proto-scores and a link define nonnegative weights),
(iii) a probe family (which downstream observables are treated as admissible), and
(iv) an anchor/update rule (which representative kernel is selected and how it is applied).
GA takes this tuple as input and proves which parts of the standard Transformer formula follow from declared constraints on it (prunes), as opposed to being additional regime choices.

Core factorization (operator pipeline).

We treat attention as a composed operator built from four layers:
(i) a
carrier
(a finite bucketization induced by coarse-graining),
(ii) an
evidence kernel
obtained from masked proto-scores via a link,
(iii) a
probe family
that defines operational equivalence (and thus gauge freedom),
and (iv) an
anchor/update
that fixes a representative kernel and produces outputs.

Derived consequences under declared prunes.

Within the primitive pipeline, the following implications are proved in the mainline:
(i) under Prune W(scalar work + compositionality), admissible links are exponential (Theorem
3.3
), and the row anchor yields softmax conditionals (Corollary
3.4
);
(ii) under row anchoring, quotienting row-unary degrees of freedom isolates an interaction component (Lemma
4.2
, Remark
4.6
); imposing a low-rank interaction approximation yields a dot-product chart via Eckart–Young/SVD (Theorem
4.1
, Corollary
4.3
);
(iii) standard token-indexed Transformers correspond to the extensional/token regime Regime Etogether with fixed-carrier scheduling; allowing the carrier to vary over depth is a distinct regime (Section
5.1
).

1.2
Contributions

C1.

Carrier-as-quotient semantics.

We formalize carriers as quotient/bucketizations induced by coarse-graining maps, separating carrier structure from induced relational structure. No graph/metric is taken as a primitive input: any graph/metric statistic enters only as the output of a declared probe acting on the kernel or on the carrier. Fixed-carrier models correspond to regimes in which the coarse-graining map is held fixed across stages; see Appendix
A
, §
A.7
.

C2.

Exponential link from composition (Gibbs / softmax regime).

Starting from an arbitrary link
\psi:\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we show that under a scalar-work representation and the compositionality law of Postulate
3.2
together with the regularity hypotheses stated in Theorem
3.3
, admissible links are forced to be exponential, yielding Gibbs weights; combined with row anchoring this recovers the softmax kernel family (Section
4
).

C3.

Probe-induced gauge and anchoring as gauge fixing.

We formalize probe families (what downstream procedures observe) and show how observational equivalence induces gauge subgroups.
Anchors (row-softmax, OT/Sinkhorn, constraint projections) are then gauge fixings tied to the probe family.
The operational pattern “probes induce equivalence” is fixed abstractly in Appendix
A
via
Definitions
A.12
–
A.13
.

C4.

Dot products from unary quotient + low-rank interaction approximation.

After quotienting unary gauge (row/column effects), we isolate the interaction component of scores. Imposing a rank-
r

interaction approximation and applying Eckart–Young/SVD theory yields the canonical rank-
r
dot-product chart (Frobenius-optimal),
with
GL(r)
chart freedom.

C5.

Adaptive carriers as the general regime.

We treat coarse-graining selection itself as procedural (potentially inferred online), recovering fixed-token Transformers as a
special case and making explicit what is assumed whenever carriers/hierarchies are taken as given
(Section
5.1
; Appendix
A
, §
A.7
).

(Appendix A semantics in the core)
.

Whenever the core text uses subset, membership, “allowed relation,” or “support” language on finite carriers
(e.g.
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}\subseteq X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}

2.1
Underlying interfaces and procedural notation

Definition 2.1

(Underlying interfaces)
.

Let
\Omega_{X},\Omega_{Y}
denote underlying (possibly inaccessible) fine-grained interfaces for
“query-side” and “key-side” degrees of freedom. No topology, metric, measure, linear structure,
or algebraic structure is assumed on
\Omega_{X}
or
\Omega_{Y}
.

Remark 2.2

(Records and procedures)
.

Appendix
A
fixes an operational substrate in which agents execute finite procedures on finite
records. In this paper, the underlying interfaces
\Omega_{X},\Omega_{Y}
should be read as record types:
we write
\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{X})
and
\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{Y})
for the corresponding record universes
(Definition/Convention in Appendix
A
).

Convention 2.2

(Extensional notation as compression)
.

We will frequently write maps such as
\pi:\Omega_{X}\to X
to reduce notation. Operationally, such a map is
a finite procedure
\Pi:\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{X})\to X
acting on records. When a statement requires classical
extensional decoding of membership or functions as literal set-theoretic maps, this will be stated explicitly
as a prune/regime choice (Section
3
).

2.2
Scale-indexed carriers as bucketizations (quotients)

Definition 2.2

(Scale-indexed carriers)
.

A
scale
(or
level
)
\ell
specifies finite bucket sets
X_{\ell}
and
Y_{\ell}
together with
labeling procedures

\pi_{\ell}^{X}:\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{X})\to X_{\ell},\qquad\pi_{\ell}^{Y}:\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{Y})\to Y_{\ell}.

We refer to
(X_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{X})
as the
query-side carrier
at scale
\ell
and
(Y_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{Y})
as the

key-side carrier
at scale
\ell
.

Definition 2.3

(Induced quotient relation)
.

Each labeling procedure induces an identification (quotient) on records: for
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{X})
,

r\sim_{\ell}^{X}r^{\prime}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\pi_{\ell}^{X}(r)=\pi_{\ell}^{X}(r^{\prime}),

and similarly for
\sim_{\ell}^{Y}
on
\mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{Y})
. The carriers
X_{\ell},Y_{\ell}
represent the finite
bucketizations induced by these identifications.

Definition 2.4

(Refinement order on scales)
.

Let
\ell^{\prime},\ell
be scales. We write
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\ell
to mean that
\ell^{\prime}
is
at least as informative

(i.e. a finer-or-equal bucketization) than
\ell
. Concretely,
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\ell
means there exist
(necessarily surjective) factor maps

\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{X}:X_{\ell^{\prime}}\to X_{\ell},\qquad\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{Y}:Y_{\ell^{\prime}}\to Y_{\ell}

such that the labelers factor as

\pi_{\ell}^{X}=\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{X}\circ\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}^{X},\qquad\pi_{\ell}^{Y}=\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{Y}\circ\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}^{Y}.

We write
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupset\ell
for the strict case
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\ell
and not
(\ell\sqsupseteq\ell^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.3

(Directionality)
.

The convention
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\ell
means “
\ell^{\prime}
is finer than
\ell
,” matching the refinement direction used in
Appendix
A
: refinement increases discriminative power; coarsening discards distinctions.

Definition 2.5

(Equivalence up to relabeling)
.

Two carriers
(X_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{X})
and
(\widetilde{X}_{\ell},\widetilde{\pi}_{\ell}^{X})
at the same scale are

equivalent up to relabeling
if there exists a bijection
\sigma:X_{\ell}\to\widetilde{X}_{\ell}
such that

\widetilde{\pi}_{\ell}^{X}=\sigma\circ\pi_{\ell}^{X}
; similarly for
(Y_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{Y})
.

Theorem 2.1

(Carrier-as-Quotient)
.

At fixed scale
\ell
, the only canonical structure specified by the carrier data
(X_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{X})
and
(Y_{\ell},\pi_{\ell}^{Y})

is the quotient/bucketization induced by
\pi_{\ell}^{X},\pi_{\ell}^{Y}
(up to relabeling). Any additional structure
(e.g. locality graphs, metrics, geometries) is not part of the carrier; it must be induced from relational evidence
defined on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.2
, the carrier is specified by a labeling procedure
\pi_{\ell}^{X}
(and
\pi_{\ell}^{Y}
),
hence only the induced identification on records and its quotient classes are canonical. Any further structure on
X_{\ell}
or
Y_{\ell}

is not fixed by the labeling data and therefore is not part of the carrier; it must be induced from additional relational evidence
supplied on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
.
∎

Remark 2.4

(Topology is not a substitute for relational content)
.

A carrier
X_{\ell}
may always be given the discrete topology (or any convenient extensional structure under Regime E),
but this adds no relational information. Relational structure appears only after evidence is supplied on

X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
and an anchor/observable is chosen (Theorem
2.1
).

2.3
Contextual proto-scores and evidence kernels

Definition 2.6

(Context parameter)
.

A
context

e
denotes any additional conditioning information available at the update step:
layer index, history/cache summaries, task tags, external signals, or other agent state.

Definition 2.7

(Proto-score with hard masking)
.

Fix a scale
\ell
and context
e
. A
proto-score
is a map

S_{\ell,e}:X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},

where
S_{\ell,e}(x,y)=-\infty
encodes a hard exclusion (forbidden relation) and
S_{\ell,e}(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
encodes
a finite compatibility score.

Definition 2.8

(Allowed relation o-set shorthand)
.

Define the
admissibility (allowed relation)
shorthand

\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}\coloneqq\{(x,y)\in X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}:S_{\ell,e}(x,y)>-\infty\}.

By Convention
2.1
,
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
is an o-set-style admissibility object on the finite carrier,
not a primitive extensional subset unless explicitly pruned to extensional decoding.

Definition 2.9

(Baseline kernel / prior)
.

A
baseline kernel
(or
prior
) is a map

\Gamma_{\ell,0}:X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}

encoding context-independent or slowly varying inductive biases at scale
\ell
(e.g. soft locality, positional bias,
structural preferences). Hard impossibilities are represented in
S_{\ell,e}
via
-\infty
rather than in
\Gamma_{\ell,0}
.

Definition 2.10

(Link function and evidence kernel)
.

A
link
is a function
\psi:\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
\psi(-\infty)=0
and
\psi(s)>0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Given
(S_{\ell,e},\Gamma_{\ell,0},\psi)
, define the
evidence kernel

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)\coloneqq\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\,\psi\!\big(S_{\ell,e}(x,y)\big).

Lemma 2.1

(Support matches admissibility)
.

For all
(x,y)\in X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
, we have
K_{\ell,e}(x,y)>0
if and only if
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.

Proof.

If
(x,y)\notin\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
then
S_{\ell,e}(x,y)=-\infty
, hence
\psi(S_{\ell,e}(x,y))=0
and
K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=0
.
If
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
then
S_{\ell,e}(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
, so
\psi(S_{\ell,e}(x,y))>0
and
\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)>0
, hence
K_{\ell,e}(x,y)>0
.
∎

Remark 2.5

(Row sums and nonempty admissible sets)
.

Define the row mass

Z_{\ell,e}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{y\in Y_{\ell}}K_{\ell,e}(x,y).

Row normalization (conditionals) will require
Z_{\ell,e}(x)>0
for all
x\in X_{\ell}
.
If
Z_{\ell,e}(x)=0
for some
x
, then either the mask forbids all keys for that query bucket or the kernel is
degenerate on that row; this can be treated by restricting attention updates to the admissible domain or by introducing
a default fallback mechanism, but we will assume nonempty rows whenever row-conditionals are invoked.

Remark 2.6

(Locality masks are domain restrictions, not intrinsic structure)
.

In many applications a locality/adjacency mask is supplied externally (e.g. causal masking, padding masks, locality windows,
structured sparsity). In our notation this corresponds to restricting
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
(equivalently setting

S_{\ell,e}=-\infty
off the mask), which is a domain restriction within the same operational semantics.
This should not be conflated with adding structure to the carrier (Theorem
2.1
).

2.4
Probe families and operational equivalence

Definition 2.11

(Probes)
.

A
probe
is a procedure that maps an evidence kernel
K_{\ell,e}
to an observable quantity,
such as a conditional family, a transport plan, an induced operator, or an induced graph statistic.
A
probe family

\mathcal{P}
is a specified collection of admissible probes.

Definition 2.12

(Operational equivalence induced by probes)
.

For kernels
K,K^{\prime}
on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
, define

K\sim_{\mathcal{P}}K^{\prime}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P(K)=P(K^{\prime})\ \text{for all}\ P\in\mathcal{P}.

The equivalence relation
\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
formalizes “indistinguishable under the chosen observational interface.”

Remark 2.7

(Gauge viewpoint)
.

A
gauge
is any transformation
g
acting on kernels such that
K\sim_{\mathcal{P}}g\!\cdot\!K
for the relevant
probe family. Anchors (defined below) are canonical choices of representatives or canonical observables relative to
a gauge.

2.5
Row-conditionals as probes and the induced scaling gauge

Assumption 2.1

(Nonempty admissible rows)
.

Whenever row-conditionals are used, we assume that for each
x\in X_{\ell}
there exists
y\in Y_{\ell}
with

(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, equivalently
Z_{\ell,e}(x)>0
.

Definition 2.13

(Row-conditional probe)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, define the
row-conditional
induced by
K_{\ell,e}
as

\pi_{K_{\ell,e}}(y\mid x)\coloneqq\frac{K_{\ell,e}(x,y)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in Y_{\ell}}K_{\ell,e}(x,y^{\prime})},\qquad\text{for }(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e},

and
\pi_{K_{\ell,e}}(y\mid x)=0
outside
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Row-conditionals identify kernels up to left scaling)
.

Let
K,K^{\prime}
be kernels with
common support exactly

\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, i.e.
K(x,y)>0
and
K^{\prime}(x,y)>0

for all
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
and
K(x,y)=K^{\prime}(x,y)=0
outside
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, and assume nonempty rows.
Then
\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{K^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid x)
for all
x\in X_{\ell}
if and only if there exists

a:X_{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
K^{\prime}(x,y)=a(x)\,K(x,y)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.

Proof.

If
K^{\prime}(x,y)=a(x)K(x,y)
on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, then row-normalization cancels
a(x)
, so
\pi_{K^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
.
Conversely, if
\pi_{K^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
for all
x
, define
a(x)\coloneqq Z_{K^{\prime}}(x)/Z_{K}(x)
(well-defined by nonempty rows).
Then
K^{\prime}(x,y)=a(x)K(x,y)
on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
by cross-multiplying the conditional identities.
∎

Remark 2.8

(Row-scaling invariance of the row-conditional probe)
.

Fix a support
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
with nonempty rows. The row-conditional probe
K\mapsto\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
is invariant under left scaling: for any
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}
,

\pi_{(a,\mathbf{1})\cdot K}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x).

Lemma
2.2
characterizes this invariance as exact when the probe family contains the full row-conditional family.

2.6
Scaling actions, anchors, and canonical representatives

Definition 2.14

(Scaling group action on kernels)
.

Define the positive scaling group

\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{Y_{\ell}}.

Given
(a,b)\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell}
and a kernel
K
on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
, define the action

((a,b)\cdot K)(x,y)\coloneqq a(x)\,K(x,y)\,b(y)\qquad\text{for }(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e},

and set
((a,b)\cdot K)(x,y)=0
outside
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.

Definition 2.15

(Anchors)
.

An
anchor
is a rule that associates to a kernel
K
a canonical observable or canonical representative
relative to a chosen gauge. We write this abstractly as

\mathsf{Anc}:\ \{K\}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{(anchored object)}.

Two anchor families used throughout are:

•

Conditional anchors
, producing row-conditionals
\pi(\cdot\mid x)
(mixture semantics).

•

Plan anchors
, producing couplings
\Pi(x,y)
with specified marginal constraints (transport semantics).

Example 2.1

(Row anchor)
.

The row anchor is the conditional anchor
\mathsf{Anc}_{\mathrm{row}}(K)\coloneqq\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
, the row-conditional
family induced by
K
.

2.7
Entropic transport anchors (balanced and unbalanced)

Definition 2.16

(Generalized KL divergence (nonnegative arrays))
.

For nonnegative arrays
A,B
of the same shape with
\mathrm{supp}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(B)
, define

\KL(A\|B)\coloneqq\sum_{i}\Big(A_{i}\log\frac{A_{i}}{B_{i}}-A_{i}+B_{i}\Big),

with the conventions
0\log(0/B_{i})=0
and
\KL(A\|B)=+\infty
if
A_{i}>0
where
B_{i}=0
.

Definition 2.17

(Target marginals)
.

Let
\mu_{\mathrm{out}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{Y_{\ell}}
be target marginals
with matched total mass:

\sum_{x\in X_{\ell}}\mu_{\mathrm{out}}(x)=\sum_{y\in Y_{\ell}}\mu_{\mathrm{in}}(y).

Assumption 2.2

(Feasible support for marginal constraints)
.

Whenever a transport plan is required, we assume the admissible relation
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
is compatible with
the target marginals, so that there exists at least one nonnegative coupling
\Pi
supported on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}

with
\Pi\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{out}}
and
\Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{in}}
.

Definition 2.18

(Balanced entropic transport anchor)
.

Under Assumption
2.2
, the
balanced entropic transport anchor
selects

\Pi^{\star}\in\argmin_{\Pi\geq 0}\ \KL(\Pi\|K_{\ell,e})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\Pi\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{out}},\ \ \Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{in}},\ \ \mathrm{supp}(\Pi)\subseteq\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}.

We denote this anchored plan by
\mathsf{Anc}_{\mathrm{bal}}(K_{\ell,e})\coloneqq\Pi^{\star}
.

Definition 2.19

(Unbalanced entropic transport anchor)
.

Fix penalties
\lambda_{\mathrm{out}},\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}>0
.
The
unbalanced entropic transport anchor
selects

\Pi^{\star}\in\argmin_{\Pi\geq 0}\ \KL(\Pi\|K_{\ell,e})+\lambda_{\mathrm{out}}\KL(\Pi\mathbf{1}\|\mu_{\mathrm{out}})+\lambda_{\mathrm{in}}\KL(\Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathrm{supp}(\Pi)\subseteq\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}.

We denote this anchored plan by
\mathsf{Anc}_{\mathrm{unbal}}(K_{\ell,e})\coloneqq\Pi^{\star}
.

Remark 2.9

(What is primitive vs what is derived)
.

In Section
2
,
\psi
and
\mathsf{Anc}
are primitive choices (they specify regimes).
Later prunes will force specific forms (e.g. additive-work forcing

Proposition 2.1

(Probe families induce gauge subgroups)
.

Fix
(\ell,e)
and an admissible relation
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{Y_{\ell}}
act on kernels by

((a,b)\cdot K)(x,y)=a(x)K(x,y)b(y)
on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
(Definition
2.14
).

(a)

(
Row-conditional probes
) If the probe family
\mathcal{P}
observes only row-conditionals
(e.g.
P(K)=\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
), then the induced gauge contains all
left scalings
:

(a,\mathbf{1})\cdot K\sim_{\mathcal{P}}K\qquad\forall a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}
contains the full row-conditional family, then invariance under probes is
exactly

left scaling on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
(Lemma
2.2
).

(b)

(
Balanced plan probes with fixed marginals
) Suppose instead
\mathcal{P}
observes a balanced entropic plan
with fixed target marginals
\mu_{\mathrm{out}},\mu_{\mathrm{in}}
(Definition
2.18
).
Then the induced gauge contains
both
left and right scalings:

(a,b)\cdot K\sim_{\mathcal{P}}K\qquad\forall(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{X_{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{Y_{\ell}}.

That is, the anchored plan depends only on the
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}
-orbit of
K
.

(c)

(
Unbalanced plans
) For unbalanced entropic anchors (Definition
2.19
), the invariance depends on the
marginal penalty structure; in particular, right scaling can be broken when marginal penalties are interpreted as part of the observable.

Proof.

(a) If
K^{\prime}(x,y)=a(x)K(x,y)
on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, then row normalization cancels the positive row factor, hence

\pi_{K^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{K}(\cdot\mid x)
for all
x
, and therefore
K^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{P}}K
.
If
\mathcal{P}
contains the full row-conditional family, Lemma
2.2
gives the converse: equal
row-conditionals imply
K^{\prime}
differs from
K
by a left scaling on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.

(b) Consider the balanced entropic objective
\Pi\mapsto\KL(\Pi\|K)
under constraints

\Pi\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{out}}
,
\Pi^{\top}\mathbf{1}=\mu_{\mathrm{in}}
,
\mathrm{supp}(\Pi)\subseteq\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
.
For

\displaystyle\KL(\Pi\|K^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y}\Big(\Pi(x,y)\log\frac{\Pi(x,y)}{a(x)K(x,y)b(y)}-\Pi(x,y)+K^{\prime}(x,y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\KL(\Pi\|K)-\sum_{x}\mu_{\mathrm{out}}(x)\log a(x)-\sum_{y}\mu_{\mathrm{in}}(y)\log b(y)+\sum_{x,y}\big(K^{\prime}(x,y)-K(x,y)\big),

(c) For the unbalanced objective, marginal penalties explicitly depend on the row/column sums of

In the mainline derivation that recovers standard token-indexed attention as a special case, only two prunes are

(

3.2
Prune W: additive work and forced exponential link

Postulate 3.1

(Scalar relational work representation (context-indexed link))
.

For each
(\ell,e)
there exists a scalar
raw work
function

\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}:\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}\to\mathbb{R}

and a context-indexed map
g_{\ell,e}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for all admissible
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
,

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\,g_{\ell,e}\!\big(\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)\big).

This
W_{\ell,e}
is a scalar chart on a
relational score field
; it is not a scalarization of the history preorders
\succeq
or
\sqsupseteq
.

Postulate 3.2

(Compositionality of work and evidence)
.

Fix
(\ell,e)
. When two independent evidence contributions compose sequentially with scalar works
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
the total work is additive and the evidence factor is multiplicative:

g_{\ell,e}(w_{1}+w_{2})=g_{\ell,e}(w_{1})\,g_{\ell,e}(w_{2})\qquad\forall\,w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

Assume
g_{\ell,e}
is measurable (or continuous, or bounded on an interval).

Theorem 3.3

(Exponential evidence is forced by composition)
.

Under Postulates
3.1
–
3.2
, for each context
(\ell,e)
there exists a real constant

\alpha_{\ell,e}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

g_{\ell,e}(w)=\exp(\alpha_{\ell,e}w)\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently, for all
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
,

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp\!\big(\alpha_{\ell,e}\,\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)\big).

Proof.

Fix
(\ell,e)
and define
f_{\ell,e}(w)\coloneqq\log g_{\ell,e}(w)
, which is well-defined since
g_{\ell,e}(w)>0
for all
w\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then Postulate
3.2
gives the Cauchy equation

f_{\ell,e}(w_{1}+w_{2})=f_{\ell,e}(w_{1})+f_{\ell,e}(w_{2})
.
By the regularity clause,
f_{\ell,e}(w)=\alpha_{\ell,e}w
for some
\alpha_{\ell,e}\in\mathbb{R}
, hence
g_{\ell,e}(w)=\exp(\alpha_{\ell,e}w)
.
Substituting into Postulate
3.1
gives the kernel form. ∎

Remark 3.6

(Temperature as context-dependent calibration of work)
.

Theorem
3.3
forces only the exponential form and leaves one scalar degree of freedom per context: the slope

\alpha_{\ell,e}
. In particular, neither the
sign
of
\alpha_{\ell,e}
nor a canonical unit system for work is fixed by the derivation.
Writing
\alpha_{\ell,e}=-\beta_{\ell,e}
yields
g_{\ell,e}(w)=\exp(-\beta_{\ell,e}w)
.
Interpreting
\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}
as a
cost/penalty
coordinate corresponds to the sign convention
\beta_{\ell,e}>0

(equivalently, reverse the orientation of
\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}
if needed).
Define the context-indexed temperature
\tau_{\ell,e}:=1/\beta_{\ell,e}
and the calibrated work

W_{\ell,e}:=\beta_{\ell,e}\,\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}=\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}/\tau_{\ell,e}
.
Then the evidence kernel may be written in Boltzmann/Gibbs form:

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp\!\big(-W_{\ell,e}(x,y)\big)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp\!\left(-\frac{\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)}{\tau_{\ell,e}}\right).

When
(\ell,e)
is fixed we suppress indices and write
\beta
and
\tau
.

Assumption 3.1

(Score/work identification (coordinate convention))
.

For compatibility with conventional ML score notation, we identify scores with negative raw work:

S_{\ell,e}(x,y)\;=\;-\,\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)\qquad\text{on }\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}.

Remark 3.7

(Signed scores vs nonnegative costs)
.

Under the cost-oriented calibration of Remark
3.6
(so
\beta_{\ell,e}>0
), the evidence factor

\exp(-\beta_{\ell,e}\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e})
becomes the familiar
\exp(S_{\ell,e}/\tau_{\ell,e})
in score coordinates.

Corollary 3.3

(Exponential link in score coordinates)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
and the calibrated Gibbs form of Remark
3.6
, the admissible link is

\psi(s)=\exp(s/\tau_{\ell,e}),

hence for all
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
,

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp\!\big(S_{\ell,e}(x,y)/\tau_{\ell,e}\big).

Proof.

Remark
3.6
gives the calibrated Gibbs form

K_{\ell,e}(x,y)=\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp(-\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)/\tau_{\ell,e})
.
Under Assumption
3.1
,
\widetilde{W}_{\ell,e}(x,y)=-S_{\ell,e}(x,y)
on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
, hence the
evidence factor is
\exp(S_{\ell,e}(x,y)/\tau_{\ell,e})
, i.e.
\psi(s)=\exp(s/\tau_{\ell,e})
. ∎

Corollary 3.4

(Row anchor yields softmax with prior bias)
.

Assume nonempty rows (as required wherever row-conditionals are used) and invoke the row anchor

\mathsf{Anc}_{\mathrm{row}}
from Section
2.5
. Under Prune W in the calibrated chart, the anchored conditional is

\pi_{K_{\ell,e}}(y\mid x)=\frac{\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\exp\!\left(\frac{S_{\ell,e}(x,y)}{\tau_{\ell,e}}\right)}{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in Y_{\ell}}\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y^{\prime})\exp\!\left(\frac{S_{\ell,e}(x,y^{\prime})}{\tau_{\ell,e}}\right)}.

Equivalently, it is a softmax over the biased score
\frac{1}{\tau_{\ell,e}}S_{\ell,e}(x,y)+\log\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)
.

Proof.

Substitute the kernel form from Corollary
3.3
into the row-conditional definition
(Definition
2.13
) and normalize each row. The displayed fraction is the resulting conditional.
Rewriting the numerator as
\exp\big(\frac{1}{\tau_{\ell,e}}S_{\ell,e}(x,y)+\log\Gamma_{\ell,0}(x,y)\big)
yields the biased-score softmax form. ∎

Without Prune W, the link

3.3
What is not a mainline prune: quotients, sections, and approximations

Remark 3.9

(Relativity principle: semantics factors through the probe quotient)
.

Appendix
B
formalizes a probe-quotient invariance: any stage-local semantics available to an agent
factors through the quotient by its observational equivalence (Theorem
B.3
).
In the core, this is mirrored by the operational equivalence
\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
induced by a probe family
\mathcal{P}

(Section

). Declaring
\mathcal{P}
therefore induces a quotient of kernels by observational
indistinguishability; it does not prune the underlying relational content.

Remark 3.10

(Anchors are gauge fixings (sections), not prunes)
.

Once a probe family is fixed, invariances of the observed object determine a gauge class (e.g. left scaling for
row-conditionals). An anchor selects a canonical representative or canonical observable within a gauge class.
This is a section choice (gauge fixing), not a restriction step
T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}
.

Remark 3.11

(Support restrictions and locality masks (instance prunes))
.

Hard masks (sparsity, locality windows) restrict
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
(equivalently, set
S_{\ell,e}(x,y)=-\infty

off a chosen relation). Formally, this
is
a restriction step in the sense of Appendix
B
, Definition
B.1
.
However it is an
instance-level
prune: it restricts which edges are present while leaving the declared
decoder/link/probe semantics unchanged. In contrast, Regime E (Newtonian closure) and Prune W (link restriction)
are
semantic
prunes: they change which decoders or composition laws are admitted.

Remark 3.12

(Low-rank interaction, dot products, and other parameterizations)
.

Imposing low-rank structure or parameterizing scores as dot products is an approximation/compression choice made after
the semantic regime is fixed. It is not structurally required for the attention operator itself.

Remark 3.13

(Roadmap)
.

Section
4
specializes the primitive pipeline (Section
2
) under Regime E
(Newtonian token regime) and Prune W (forced exponential link), and then applies a standard representational choice
(dot-product score parameterization) to recover conventional Transformer attention formulas as a corollary.

3.4

Proposition 3.1

The mainline regime used to recover token-indexed attention is a root-to-leaf path in a prune tree

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

Proof.

Appendix

)
from closure/decoding restrictions that act by restricting the admissible stage class (Definition

Both are restriction moves in the sense needed here, and a negative construction chain records a sequence of such

In the mainline:

(Appendix

4.3
Score gauge under row anchoring: unary quotienting and interaction content

Fix the token carrier
X=Y=\{1,\dots,n\}
and treat a finite score array

on the full carrier.
(Handling

Remark 4.4

(Distinct invariances (avoid gauge conflation))
.

This section uses three non-identical invariance notions.

(i)

Scaling gauge (kernel level).
Invariance of kernels under the scaling action

((a,b)\cdot K)(x,y)=a(x)K(x,y)b(y)
induced by a probe family (Definition
2.14
, Proposition
2.1
).

(ii)

Unary score gauge (score level, in the Gibbs chart).
When Prune Wfixes an exponential link, row/column scalings of
K
correspond to row/column additive shifts of the score
S
via
K\propto\exp(S/\tau)
(Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
).

(iii)

Chart freedom (rank-
r
dot-product factorization).
A rank-
r
factorization
S^{\mathrm{int}}\approx QL^{\top}
is nonunique under
GL(r)
actions on factors (Remark
4.8
).

References to “gauge” in this section are always to one of (i)–(iii) and will not be used interchangeably.

Definition 4.1

(Unary fields and unary action)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{X}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{Y}
. Define the unary action on scores by

(S+\alpha\oplus\beta)(x,y):=S(x,y)+\alpha(x)+\beta(y),\qquad(x,y)\in X\times Y.

Unary terms are node potentials on
X
and
Y
; the remaining part of
S
is the interaction component.

Lemma 4.1

(Row gauge for Gibbs–row-anchor semantics)
.

Assume the Gibbs form
K(x,y)\propto\Gamma_{0}(x,y)\exp(S(x,y)/\tau)
on the allowed set and apply the row anchor.
If
S^{\prime}(x,y)=S(x,y)+\alpha(x)
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{X}
, then the induced row-conditionals coincide:

\pi_{S^{\prime}}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{S}(\cdot\mid x)\qquad\forall x\in X.

Proof.

On the allowed set, replacing
S
by
S+\alpha(x)
multiplies the kernel row by the positive factor

\exp(\alpha(x)/\tau)
:

K^{\prime}(x,y)=\Gamma_{0}(x,y)\exp\!\left(\frac{S(x,y)+\alpha(x)}{\tau}\right)=\exp(\alpha(x)/\tau)\,K(x,y).

By Lemma
2.2
, row normalization cancels any positive row scaling, hence
\pi_{K^{\prime}}=\pi_{K}
. ∎

Definition 4.2

(Row/column means and centered representatives)
.

Let
X:=X_{\ell_{\star}}
and
Y:=Y_{\ell_{\star}}
with
n_{X}:=|X|
and
n_{Y}:=|Y|
. Define the grand mean, row means,
and column means:

m:=\frac{1}{n_{X}n_{Y}}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}S(x,y),\quad r(x):=\frac{1}{n_{Y}}\sum_{y\in Y}S(x,y),\quad c(y):=\frac{1}{n_{X}}\sum_{x\in X}S(x,y).

Define the row-centered, column-centered, and interaction components:

S^{\mathrm{row}}(x,y):=S(x,y)-r(x),\qquad S^{\mathrm{col}}(x,y):=S(x,y)-c(y),

S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y):=S(x,y)-r(x)-c(y)+m.

Lemma 4.2

(Canonical representatives for partial/full unary quotients)
.

\begin{array}[]{@{}ll@{}}\textbf{(a) Row quotient:}&\text{Invariant under row shifts }S\mapsto S+\alpha(x).\\[2.5pt]
\textbf{(b) Column quotient:}&\text{Invariant under column shifts }S\mapsto S+\beta(y).\\[2.5pt]
\textbf{(c) Full unary quotient:}&\text{Invariant under full unary shifts }S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)+\beta(y)\ \text{and satisfies}\\
&\sum_{y\in Y}S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)=0\ \forall x,\\
&\sum_{x\in X}S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)=0\ \forall y.\end{array}

Moreover,
S^{\mathrm{int}}
is the unique representative in the
unary equivalence class of
S
satisfying these
zero-sum constraints.

Proof.

Recall
n_{X}:=|X|
and
n_{Y}:=|Y|
and the means
m,r(x),c(y)
from Definition
4.2
.
The centered forms are

S^{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=S(x,y)-r(x),\qquad S^{\mathrm{col}}(x,y)=S(x,y)-c(y),

S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)=S(x,y)-r(x)-c(y)+m.

Invariance.

If
S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)
then
r(x)\mapsto r(x)+\alpha(x)
, hence
S^{\mathrm{row}}
is invariant.
Similarly, if
S\mapsto S+\beta(y)
then
c(y)\mapsto c(y)+\beta(y)
, hence
S^{\mathrm{col}}
is invariant.
Under a full unary shift
S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)+\beta(y)
, the row means shift by
r(x)\mapsto r(x)+\alpha(x)+\bar{\beta}
and the column means shift by
c(y)\mapsto c(y)+\beta(y)+\bar{\alpha}
, where
\bar{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{n_{X}}\sum_{x}\alpha(x)
and
\bar{\beta}:=\frac{1}{n_{Y}}\sum_{y}\beta(y)
. The grand mean shifts by
m\mapsto m+\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}
.
Substituting these relations shows
S^{\mathrm{int}}
is invariant.

Zero-sum constraints.

By construction,

\sum_{y\in Y}S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)=0\ \forall x,\qquad\sum_{x\in X}S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)=0\ \forall y.

Uniqueness.

Suppose
U(x,y)=\alpha(x)+\beta(y)
satisfies
\sum_{y}U(x,y)=0
for all
x
. Then

n_{Y}\alpha(x)+\sum_{y}\beta(y)=0
for all
x
, so
\alpha(x)
is constant.
Plugging into
\sum_{x}U(x,y)=0
for all
y
forces
\beta(y)
constant as well.
The zero-sum constraints then imply
U\equiv 0
, proving uniqueness of the centered representative.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Probe choice determines the score quotient)
.

Fix an exponential chart on the allowed set,
K(x,y)\propto\Gamma_{0}(x,y)\exp(S(x,y)/\tau)
. Then the probe family determines which modifications of
S
are observationally inert.

•

Row-conditional probe (row anchor).
Kernel invariance includes left scaling, which becomes a row shift
S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)
in the exponential chart (Lemma
4.1
). Hence the row-conditionals determine
S
only up to row shifts. The row-centered representative
S^{\mathrm{row}}
(Definition
4.2
) is the canonical element of this quotient (Lemma
4.2
(a)).

•

Balanced-plan probe (fixed marginals).
Kernel invariance includes both left and right scaling (Proposition
2.1
(b)), which becomes a full unary shift
S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)+\beta(y)
in the exponential chart. Hence plan observables determine
S
only up to full unary shifts. The interaction component
S^{\mathrm{int}}
is the canonical invariant (Lemma
4.2
(c)).

Corollary 4.2

(Row-anchored semantics depends only on the row quotient)
.

In the Gibbs–row-anchor regime, the conditional weights depend only on the row-gauge class of the score.
Equivalently, row-conditionals (hence the conditional-operator) are functions of
S^{\mathrm{row}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, adding
\alpha(x)
does not change row-conditionals. The row-centered representative

S^{\mathrm{row}}
is the canonical element of this gauge class (Lemma
4.2
(a)). ∎

Remark 4.6

(Row-centered decomposition: column field plus interaction)
.

Using Definition
4.2
, one has the identity

S^{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=\big(c(y)-m\big)+S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y).

Thus, modulo row shifts (the gauge relevant for row-softmax), a score decomposes as a column field

b(y):=c(y)-m
plus an interaction term
S^{\mathrm{int}}
.

Remark 4.7

(Masked relations)
.

All centering identities in Definition
4.2
and Lemma
4.2
assume a finite array on
X\times Y
.
If
S
contains entries
-\infty
(hard masking), then the arithmetic means
m,r,c
are undefined.
In the masked case one must choose a convention before applying any centering:

(i)

Restriction convention.
Work on the allowed set
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
and interpret sums as restricted sums,

\sum_{y:(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}}(\cdot)
(and similarly for columns).

(ii)

Weighted convention.
Choose a reference weight
w
supported on
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
and use weighted centering (Lemma
4.3
).

No statement in this section uses arithmetic that mixes finite values with
-\infty
.

Lemma 4.3

(Weighted row-centering under a reference weight)
.

Let
w:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a reference weight with
\mathrm{supp}(w)\subseteq\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
and row masses

w_{x}:=\sum_{y\in Y}w(x,y)>0
for all
x\in X
.
For any finite score array
S
defined on
\mathrm{supp}(w)
, define the weighted row mean and weighted row-centered score by

m_{w}(x):=\frac{1}{w_{x}}\sum_{y\in Y}w(x,y)S(x,y),\qquad S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}(x,y):=S(x,y)-m_{w}(x)\quad\text{for }(x,y)\in\mathrm{supp}(w).

Then:

(i)

(
Weighted zero-mean
)
\sum_{y\in Y}w(x,y)S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=0
for all
x\in X
.

(ii)

(
Row-shift invariance
) If
S^{\prime}(x,y)=S(x,y)+\alpha(x)
for some row field
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{X}
, then

S^{\prime(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}=S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}
on
\mathrm{supp}(w)
.

(iii)

(
Uniqueness
)
S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}
is the unique element in the row-shift equivalence class of
S

that satisfies the constraints in (i).

Proof.

(i) By definition, for each
x
,

\sum_{y}w(x,y)S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=\sum_{y}w(x,y)S(x,y)-m_{w}(x)\sum_{y}w(x,y)=0
.

(ii) If
S^{\prime}=S+\alpha(x)
then
m_{w}^{\prime}(x)=m_{w}(x)+\alpha(x)
, hence

S^{\prime(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=S(x,y)+\alpha(x)-(m_{w}(x)+\alpha(x))=S^{(w)}_{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)
on
\mathrm{supp}(w)
.

(iii) If
S+\alpha
also satisfies the weighted zero-mean constraints, then for each
x
,

0=\sum_{y}w(x,y)\big(S(x,y)+\alpha(x)-m_{w}(x)\big)=\alpha(x)\sum_{y}w(x,y)=\alpha(x)w_{x}.

Since
w_{x}>0
, this forces
\alpha(x)=0
for all
x
, so the representative is unique. ∎

4.4
Dot products from low-rank interaction (complexity approximation)

Assumption 4.5

(Low-rank interaction regime)
.

There exists
r\ll\min(n_{X},n_{Y})
such that the interaction matrix is well-approximated by rank
r
:

S^{\mathrm{int}}\approx QL^{\top},\qquad Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{X}\times r},\ L\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{Y}\times r}.

Theorem 4.1

(Optimal dot-product form via SVD (Eckart–Young))
.

Let
S^{\mathrm{int}}=U\Sigma V^{\top}
be an SVD with singular values

\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{p}\geq 0
,
p=\min(n_{X},n_{Y})
.
The best rank-
r
approximation in Frobenius norm is

S^{\mathrm{int}}_{r}:=U_{[:,1:r]}\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}V_{[:,1:r]}^{\top},

and can be written pointwise as dot products by defining

q(x):=\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}^{1/2}U_{x,1:r}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r},\qquad k(y):=\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}^{1/2}V_{y,1:r}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r},

so that
S^{\mathrm{int}}_{r}(x,y)=q(x)^{\top}k(y)
for all
x\in X
,
y\in Y
.

Proof.

The Frobenius-optimality statement is the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem; see

[
8
,
13
]
.
Given the truncated SVD
S^{\mathrm{int}}_{r}=U_{[:,1:r]}\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}V_{[:,1:r]}^{\top}
, define

q(x):=\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}^{1/2}U_{x,1:r}^{\top}
and
k(y):=\Sigma_{1:r,1:r}^{1/2}V_{y,1:r}^{\top}
;
then
S^{\mathrm{int}}_{r}(x,y)=q(x)^{\top}k(y)
for all
x,y
. ∎

Remark 4.8

(Chart freedom / pairing invariance)
.

The factorization is not unique: for any invertible
A\in GL(r)
,

q\mapsto Aq
and
k\mapsto A^{-T}k
preserves
q^{\top}k
.
This is the finite-carrier analogue of
V\times V^{\ast}
pairing invariance.

Corollary 4.3

(Row-anchored low-rank score normal form)
.

Assume
S
is finite on
X\times Y
and Assumption
4.5
holds. Then, modulo row shifts
S\mapsto S+\alpha(x)
,
the row-centered score admits the approximation

S^{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)\ \approx\ b(y)+q(x)^{\top}k(y),\qquad b(y):=c(y)-m,

where
q:X\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
k:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
are derived from a rank-
r
approximation of
S^{\mathrm{int}}
(Theorem
4.1
).
If a hard mask is present (entries
-\infty
), it is encoded by restricting to
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
and applying the conventions in Remark
4.7
.

Proof.

By Remark
4.6
,
S^{\mathrm{row}}(x,y)=b(y)+S^{\mathrm{int}}(x,y)
.
Under Assumption
4.5
and Theorem
4.1
,
S^{\mathrm{int}}
admits a rank-
r
approximation of the form
q(x)^{\top}k(y)
.
Substituting yields the claimed normal form. The masking clause is by the definition of
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}
together with Remark
4.7
. ∎

4.5

Definition 4.3

(Standard parameterization of queries, keys, values)
.

Let token embeddings be
e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}
and choose matrices

W_{Q},W_{K},W_{V}

q_{i}:=W_{Q}e_{i},\qquad k_{i}:=W_{K}e_{i},\qquad v_{i}:=W_{V}e_{i}.

Theorem 4.2

(Scaled dot-product attention as a GA corollary)
.

Assume the Newtonian token regime (Assumption
4.1
), row-anchored conditional semantics
(Assumption
4.2
), identity alignment (Assumption
4.3
),
and the Gibbs kernel forced by Prune W (Assumption

Let the (finite) score take the Transformer form

S(i,j)=\frac{q_{i}^{\top}k_{j}}{\sqrt{d}}+b(j),

with

a key-dependent bias.
Then the row-conditional weights are

\pi(\cdot\mid i):=\softmax_{j:\,(i,j)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell_{\star},e}}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{q_{i}^{\top}k_{j}}{\sqrt{d}}+b(j)\right)+\log\Gamma_{\ell_{\star},0}(i,j)\right),

and the conditional-operator
\widetilde{A}_{\pi}
(Section
2.8
) yields the standard attention update

\mathrm{Attn}(i)=\sum_{j:\,(i,j)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell_{\star},e}}\pi(j\mid i)\,v_{j}.

In the common convention

and

, this reduces to

\pi(\cdot\mid i)=\softmax_{j}\!\left(\frac{q_{i}^{\top}k_{j}}{\sqrt{d}}+b(j)\right),\qquad\mathrm{Attn}(i)=\sum_{j}\pi(j\mid i)\,v_{j}.

Proof.

By Corollary

, the row-anchor yields softmax weights over

Substituting the stated score form and applying the conditional-operator definition completes the derivation. ∎

Remark 4.9

(Dot products arise from low-rank interaction after quotienting unary fields)

In the row-anchored Gibbs regime, the observable operator depends on the score only through its row-shift equivalence class
(Corollary

Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the (finite) set of available scales and write
\mathcal{C}:=\{c_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}
.
A
carrier selector
is a procedure
\sigma_{t}
returning a carrier specification to be used at step
t
:

\sigma_{t}:\ \mathsf{Rec}(\Omega_{\mathrm{state},t})\to\mathcal{C}.

Postulate 5.1

(On-the-fly coarse-graining inference)
.

At each update step
t
, the procedure may select a carrier specification
c_{\ell_{t}}
as a function of the current record
and objective signals. Thus the carrier may change over depth:

c_{\ell_{0}}\ \to\ c_{\ell_{1}}\ \to\ \cdots,

and with it the domains on which kernels, anchors, and update operators are defined.

Remark 5.3

(Fixed-carrier regime)
.

Fixed-token Transformers correspond to the special regime in which
\sigma_{t}
is constant:

\sigma_{t}(\cdot)\equiv c_{\ell_{\star}}
for all
t
.

5.1.2
Coarse-graining kernels across carrier changes

When the carrier changes, a regime must specify a map that pushes kernels on the old carrier to kernels on the new carrier. In GA, this pushforward rule is part of the declared regime: different aggregation rules correspond to different coarse-graining semantics.

Definition 5.2

(Allowed-set pushforward of kernels (sum aggregation))
.

Assume
\ell^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\ell
with factor maps
\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{X}:X_{\ell^{\prime}}\to X_{\ell}
and

\rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{Y}:Y_{\ell^{\prime}}\to Y_{\ell}
(Definition
2.4
).
Let
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell^{\prime},e}\subseteq X_{\ell^{\prime}}\times Y_{\ell^{\prime}}
be the admissibility (allowed) relation and let
K_{\ell^{\prime},e}
be an evidence kernel.
Define the
pushforward kernel
on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
by restricted sum aggregation:

\big(K_{\ell^{\prime},e}\Downarrow_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}\big)(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{\prime}\in X_{\ell^{\prime}}:\ \rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{X}(x^{\prime})=x\\
y^{\prime}\in Y_{\ell^{\prime}}:\ \rho_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell}^{Y}(y^{\prime})=y\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}\{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell^{\prime},e}\}\,K_{\ell^{\prime},e}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

Remark 5.4

(Induced masking at the coarser scale)
.

The induced allowed relation at level
\ell
may be defined by support of the pushforward:

\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e}\coloneqq\{(x,y):(K_{\ell^{\prime},e}\Downarrow_{\ell^{\prime}\to\ell})(x,y)>0\},

which is consistent with Lemma
2.1
when pushforward is performed on the allowed relation.

Remark 5.5

(Alternative aggregation regimes)
.

Other aggregation regimes exist and correspond to different coarse-graining semantics:
log-sum-exp aggregation on scores (soft maximum), max pooling, entropy-regularized projections, or constrained re-anchoring
(e.g. recomputing an OT plan on the new carrier). Each is a named regime choice (a sibling branch; see §
5.4
).

Remark 5.6

(Depth as scale-walk)
.

Allowing carriers to vary with
t
turns depth into a
scale-walk
: alternation of relational inference (kernels/anchors) and bucketization updates. In the fixed-carrier subregime
c_{t}(\cdot)\equiv c_{\ell_{\star}}
for all
t
, the scale-walk reduces to standard fixed-token depth.

5.2
Depth as emergent procedure staging under bounded resolution

This section formalizes the sense in which “depth” is not a primitive assumption but an emergent procedure:
a staged realization of a global relational update under bounded resolution/work constraints. Beyond kernels and anchors,
the key additional object is a
composition law
describing how newly inferred relational information is merged into memory.

5.2.1
Records, memory, charting, and composition

Fix a carrier level
\ell
(or allow
\ell
to vary as in Section
5.1
). Let
R_{t}
denote the procedure’s stored
record at depth step
t
(e.g. token states on the current carrier, plus auxiliary state).

Definition 5.3

(Memory, charting, update, and composition)
.

A staged attention procedure is specified by:

(i)

a memory functional
\mathsf{Mem}_{t}
producing an effective state
H_{t}
from stored records,

H_{t}\coloneqq\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t});

(ii)

a chart map
\mathsf{Chart}_{t}
producing a representation
\widehat{H}_{t}
on which scores/kernels are formed,

\widehat{H}_{t}\coloneqq\mathsf{Chart}_{t}(H_{t});

(iii)

an anchored relational update producing an increment
\Delta_{t}
from
\widehat{H}_{t}
(Section
2
),

\Delta_{t}\coloneqq\mathsf{Upd}_{t}(\widehat{H}_{t});\qquad\mathsf{Upd}_{t}\text{ is an anchored operator induced by }K_{\ell,e_{t}};

(iv)

a composition law
\mathsf{Comp}_{t}
merging the increment into memory/record,

R_{t+1}\coloneqq\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H_{t},\Delta_{t}).

Definition 5.4

(Admissible innovation set)
.

Fix a stage
t
and memory state
H_{t}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{t}(H_{t})
denote the set of
admissible stage-
t
internal choices permitted by the declared regime (e.g. anchor
section choice, mixture component choice, stochastic seed, or other declared branch
parameters). For each
c\in\mathcal{C}_{t}(H_{t})
let
\mathsf{Upd}_{t,c}
denote the corresponding
anchored update operator on charted memory states.
Define the admissible innovation set by

\mathcal{U}_{t}(H_{t})\coloneqq\left\{\,\mathsf{Upd}_{t,c}\!\big(\mathsf{Chart}_{t}(H_{t})\big)\ :\ c\in\mathcal{C}_{t}(H_{t})\,\right\}.

In the deterministic subregime
|\mathcal{C}_{t}(H_{t})|=1
,
\mathcal{U}_{t}(H_{t})
is a singleton.

Definition 5.5

(Conserved (inertial) features)
.

A functional
C
on memory states is
conserved
by the staged procedure if for all
t
, all
H_{t}
, and all

\Delta\in\mathcal{U}_{t}(H_{t})
,

C(\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H_{t},\Delta))=C(H_{t}).

Such invariants formalize what aspects of the representation persist under bounded relational updates and clarify
which degrees of freedom are being progressively refined versus preserved.

Definition 5.6

(Full-history memory with linear readout)
.

Fix a depth horizon
t\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence of per-stage records
(R_{k})_{k=0}^{t}
where each

R_{k}\in\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{k}})\quad\text{(e.g.\ post-update residual records at stage $k$)}.

A
full-history (dense-skip) memory regime
specifies a
readout procedure

\mathsf{Read}_{t}:\ \mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{0}})\times\cdots\times\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{t}})\to\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{t}})

that forms a stage-
t
memory state
\widehat{H}_{t}
from the entire record stack. A canonical parametrized instance
is the
linear readout

\widehat{H}_{t}\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{k=0}^{t}\ \alpha_{t,k}\ \odot\ \Phi_{t\leftarrow k}(R_{k}),

(5.1)

where
\alpha_{t,k}\in\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{t}})
are gating weights (possibly data-dependent),
\odot
denotes pointwise
multiplication on records, and
\Phi_{t\leftarrow k}:\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{k}})\to\mathsf{Rec}(X_{\ell_{t}})
is a (chosen or learned)
compatibility map that places
R_{k}
into the stage-
t
carrier.

Remark 5.7

(Compression vs explicit access as a regime choice)
.

Definition
5.3
allows
\mathsf{Mem}_{t}
to be any internal state summary.
Definition
5.6
makes a distinct choice: the update at depth
t
may depend on an
explicitly addressable history stack
(R_{0},\dots,R_{t})
through
\mathsf{Read}_{t}
.
In this regime, “memory” is not only what persists in the current record
R_{t}
; it is a controlled dependency on
earlier records mediated by
(\alpha_{t,k},\Phi_{t\leftarrow k})
.

Definition 5.7

(Full-history staged update)
.

Under the full-history regime, define the stage-
t
charted state by

\widehat{H}_{t}\coloneqq\mathsf{Read}_{t}(R_{0},\dots,R_{t}),

and then perform the update/composition step using the existing interface:

\Delta_{t}\coloneqq\mathsf{Upd}_{t}(\widehat{H}_{t}),\qquad R_{t+1}\coloneqq\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(\widehat{H}_{t},\Delta_{t}),\qquad H_{t+1}\coloneqq\mathsf{Mem}_{t+1}(R_{0:t+1}).

When
\mathsf{Read}_{t}
is taken to be the linear readout (
5.1
), the staged dynamics
factor into (i) a history readout (dense skip aggregation) and (ii) a single-step anchored relational update.

Remark 5.8

(Relation to skip wiring)
.

Equation (
5.1
) subsumes dense skip connections and history-conditioned gating as
a regime choice: different families for
(\alpha_{t,k},\Phi_{t\leftarrow k})
correspond to different notions of
addressability and compression across depth. The core GA operator semantics remain unchanged; only the memory/readout
interface feeding the stage-
t
update is altered.

Remark 5.9

(Named specializations)
.

This general form subsumes common architectures as regime choices:

•

Markov memory (standard residual stream):

\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t})=R_{t}
.

•

Full-history memory (dense skip idealization):

\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t})=(R_{0},\dots,R_{t})
.

•

PreNorm residual:

\mathsf{Chart}_{t}=\mathrm{Norm}
and
\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H,\Delta)=H+\Delta
.

•

Gated residual:

\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H,\Delta)=H+\eta_{t}(H,\Delta)\odot\Delta
for a learned gate
\eta_{t}
.

•

PostNorm:

\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H,\Delta)=\mathrm{Norm}(H+\Delta)
(re-chart-after-compose).

We treat
\mathsf{Mem},\mathsf{Chart},\mathsf{Comp}
as objects in the prune tree: defaults are general; specific formulas are specializations.

5.2.2
Inertial continuation and micro-representations

Assumption 5.1

(Identity at zero evidence)
.

If the admissible relation is empty (or kernel mass is zero on all admissible edges), then
\mathsf{Upd}_{t}(\widehat{H}_{t})=0

and
\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H_{t},0)=H_{t}
: no relational evidence implies no update.

Remark 5.10

(Persistence under staged refinement)
.

In the Appendix
A
persistence regime, witnessed commitments are monotone under realized continuation
(Lemma
A.1
). Staged procedures can be read as repeatedly extending partial relational information under bounded work,
with
\mathsf{Comp}_{t}
implementing the chosen persistence/overwrite semantics at the representational level.

5.2.3
A bounded-neighborhood regime and a depth lower bound

Assumption 5.2

(Bounded neighborhood regime)
.

Assume there exists
B
such that for each
x\in X_{\ell}
and each stage
t
,

\left|\{y\in Y_{\ell}:\ (x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e_{t}}\}\right|\leq B.

Definition 5.8

(Readout dependence sets and effective stagewise influence graph)
.

Assume self-attention at level
\ell
so
X_{\ell}=Y_{\ell}
and
\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e_{t}}\subseteq X_{\ell}\times X_{\ell}
.
For each stage
t
and each
x\in X_{\ell}
, define the
readout dependence set

\mathsf{Dep}_{t}(x)\subseteq X_{\ell}
to be
the set of carrier elements whose record entries can affect the charted state at
x
:

u\in\mathsf{Dep}_{t}(x)\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\ (R_{0:t}),(R^{\prime}_{0:t})\ \text{with}\ R_{0:t}\equiv R^{\prime}_{0:t}\ \text{off }u\ \text{and}\ \widehat{H}_{t}(x)\neq\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{t}(x),

where
\widehat{H}_{t}=\mathsf{Chart}_{t}(\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t}))
and similarly for
\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{t}
.
Define the
effective stagewise influence relation

\mathsf{Inf}_{t}\subseteq X_{\ell}\times X_{\ell}
by

(x\leftarrow u)\in\mathsf{Inf}_{t}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\ y\in X_{\ell}\ \text{s.t.}\ (x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e_{t}}\ \text{and}\ u\in\mathsf{Dep}_{t}(y).

Let
\mathsf{dist}_{\mathsf{Inf}}(u\to x)
denote the shortest path length from
u
to
x
in the directed graph with edges
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}

(when
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}
is stage-invariant) or in the stagewise product graph (when
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}
varies with
t
).

Definition 5.9

(Stagewise influence composition and
t
-step predecessor set)
.

For relations
R,S\subseteq X_{\ell}\times X_{\ell}
, write
R\circ S
for relational composition:

(x\leftarrow u)\in(R\circ S)\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists y\in X_{\ell}\ \text{s.t.}\ (x\leftarrow y)\in R\ \text{and}\ (y\leftarrow u)\in S.

Define the
t
-step stagewise influence relation by

\mathsf{Inf}^{(t)}\coloneqq\mathsf{Inf}_{t-1}\circ\mathsf{Inf}_{t-2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathsf{Inf}_{0},

and the corresponding predecessor set of
x
at depth
t
by

\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)\coloneqq\{u\in X_{\ell}:\ (x\leftarrow u)\in\mathsf{Inf}^{(t)}\}.

Proposition 5.1

(Exact influence barrier via stagewise predecessor sets)
.

Use Definition
5.8
and Definition
5.9
.
Fix
t\geq 1
and
x\in X_{\ell}
.
If two record histories
(R_{0:t})
and
(R^{\prime}_{0:t})
agree on
\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
(i.e. are identical off
\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
),
then the stage-
t
update at
x
computed through the staged interface is identical under the two histories.
Equivalently, record content at
u
can influence the stage-
t
update at
x
only if
u\in\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
.

Proof.

Unroll the staged interface for
t
steps.
By Definition
5.8
, the charted state at a node
y
depends only on record content in
\mathsf{Dep}_{s}(y)
at the relevant stage,
and the anchored update at
x
reads only nodes
y
with
(x,y)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e_{s}}
.
Thus one stage of influence is captured exactly by
\mathsf{Inf}_{s}
; iterating
t
stages yields the composed relation
\mathsf{Inf}^{(t)}
.
If
u\notin\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
then there is no stagewise influence path from
u
to
x
in
t
steps, so altering record content at
u

cannot change the stage-
t
update at
x
.
∎

Corollary 5.1

(Distance lower bound as a special case)
.

If
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}
is stage-invariant (
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}\equiv\mathsf{Inf}
), then
u\in\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
iff there exists a directed path of length at most
t

from
u
to
x
in the graph with edges
\mathsf{Inf}
. Hence if
\mathsf{dist}_{\mathsf{Inf}}(u\to x)=d
, then
t<d
implies
u\notin\mathsf{Pre}_{t}(x)
,
so
u
cannot influence the stage-
t
update at
x
.

Remark 5.11

(Instantiations of
\mathsf{Dep}_{t}
)
.

In the Markov/tokenwise regime (e.g.
\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t})=R_{t}
and tokenwise charting),
\mathsf{Dep}_{t}(x)=\{x\}
,
so
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}
reduces to the admissibility neighborhood relation:
(x\leftarrow u)\in\mathsf{Inf}_{t}
iff
(x,u)\in\mathsf{AdmPairs}_{\ell,e_{t}}
.
In a full-history readout regime with globally supported compatibility maps
\Phi_{t\leftarrow k}
,
\mathsf{Dep}_{t}(x)=X_{\ell}
,
so
\mathsf{Inf}_{t}=X_{\ell}\times X_{\ell}
and the depth lower bound becomes trivial (
d=1
for all distinct pairs).

5.3
Closure principles: mixtures, FFNs, and integral form

Definition 5.10

(Disjoint-union (branch-indexed) carrier)
.

Let
\{Y_{b}\}_{b\in B}
be a finite family of carriers indexed by a finite set
B
.
Define the
branch-indexed carrier

Y\;:=\;\bigsqcup_{b\in B}(\{b\}\times Y_{b}),

with canonical injections
\iota_{b}:Y_{b}\hookrightarrow Y
given by
\iota_{b}(y)=(b,y)
.
For any family of value fields
v_{b}:Y_{b}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the induced value field

v:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
v(b,y):=v_{b}(y)
.

Definition 5.11

(Gated mixture of conditional updates)
.

Fix an output carrier
X
. For each
b\in B
, let
\pi^{(b)}
be a row-conditional anchor on
X\times Y_{b}
,
i.e. for each
x\in X
,
\pi^{(b)}_{x}(\cdot)
is a probability distribution on
Y_{b}
.
Let
\alpha:X\times B\to[0,1]
be a gate satisfying
\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)=1
for each
x\in X
.
Define the
gated mixture operator

(\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}v)(x)\;:=\;\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)\sum_{y\in Y_{b}}\pi^{(b)}_{x}(y)\,v_{b}(y).

Proposition 5.2

(Closure of conditional updates under gated mixtures)
.

In the setting of Definition
5.11
, let
Y=\bigsqcup_{b\in B}(\{b\}\times Y_{b})
be the
branch-indexed carrier (Definition
5.10
).
Define an anchor
\pi
on
X\times Y
by

\pi_{x}(b,y)\;:=\;\alpha(x,b)\,\pi^{(b)}_{x}(y)\qquad\text{for each }x\in X,\ (b,y)\in Y.

Then
\pi_{x}(\cdot)
is a probability distribution on
Y
for each
x
, and for all value families
\{v_{b}\}
,

(\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}v)(x)\;=\;\sum_{(b,y)\in Y}\pi_{x}(b,y)\,v(b,y).

Proof.

For fixed
x
,
\pi_{x}(b,y)\geq 0
and

\sum_{(b,y)\in Y}\pi_{x}(b,y)=\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)\sum_{y\in Y_{b}}\pi^{(b)}_{x}(y)=\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)=1,

so
\pi_{x}
is a probability distribution on
Y
.
Moreover,

\sum_{(b,y)\in Y}\pi_{x}(b,y)\,v(b,y)=\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)\sum_{y\in Y_{b}}\pi^{(b)}_{x}(y)\,v_{b}(y)=(\widetilde{A}_{\alpha}v)(x).

∎

Definition 5.12

(Gated mixture of plan updates)
.

Fix an output carrier
X
. For each
b\in B
, let
K^{(b)}:X\times Y_{b}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a plan-kernel and define
the plan operator

(A^{(b)}_{\Pi}v_{b})(x)\;:=\;\sum_{y\in Y_{b}}K^{(b)}(x,y)\,v_{b}(y).

Let
\beta:X\times B\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be any nonnegative gate (no normalization required).
Define the
gated plan mixture

(A_{\beta}v)(x)\;:=\;\sum_{b\in B}\beta(x,b)\,(A^{(b)}_{\Pi}v_{b})(x).

Proposition 5.3

(Closure of plan updates under gated mixtures)
.

In the setting of Definition
5.12
, let
Y=\bigsqcup_{b\in B}(\{b\}\times Y_{b})
and define
a plan-kernel
K:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

K(x,(b,y))\;:=\;\beta(x,b)\,K^{(b)}(x,y).

Then for all value families
\{v_{b}\}
,

(A_{\beta}v)(x)\;=\;\sum_{(b,y)\in Y}K(x,(b,y))\,v(b,y).

Proof.

Direct calculation:

\sum_{(b,y)\in Y}K(x,(b,y))\,v(b,y)=\sum_{b\in B}\sum_{y\in Y_{b}}\beta(x,b)\,K^{(b)}(x,y)\,v_{b}(y)=\sum_{b\in B}\beta(x,b)\,(A^{(b)}_{\Pi}v_{b})(x).

∎

Corollary 5.2

(Residual, memory, and MoE are GA mixtures)
.

Any update of the form
u(x)=u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)
, any gated memory update

m^{\prime}=\lambda m+(1-\lambda)u
, and any finite MoE

\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)\,f_{b}(x)
are realizable as single GA updates by choosing
B
as the branch/expert
carrier and applying Propositions
5.2
–
5.3
.

Definition 5.13

(Two-layer feedforward block)
.

Fix dimensions
d_{\mathrm{model}},d_{\mathrm{ff}}\in\mathbb{N}
and an activation
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Let
W_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{ff}}\times d_{\mathrm{model}}}
,
b_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{ff}}}
,

W_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_{\mathrm{ff}}}
,
b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
.
Define
\mathrm{FFN}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
by

\mathrm{FFN}(x)\;:=\;W_{2}\,\phi(W_{1}x+b_{1})+b_{2},

where
\phi
acts elementwise on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{ff}}}
.
Let
H:=\{1,\dots,d_{\mathrm{ff}}\}
and let
w_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
denote the
h
-th column of
W_{2}
.

Theorem 5.1

(FFN as a GA plan update over a hidden-unit carrier)
.

In the setting of Definition
5.13
, fix a carrier
X
and a state field
h:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
.
Define the branch-indexed hidden carrier
H_{e}:=(\{+\}\times H)\sqcup(\{-\}\times H)
and, for each
i\in X
,
define nonnegative weights from the local state vector
h(i)
by

a_{+}(i,j):=\max\{\phi((W_{1}h(i)+b_{1})_{j}),0\},\qquad a_{-}(i,j):=\max\{-\phi((W_{1}h(i)+b_{1})_{j}),0\}\qquad(j\in H).

Define a plan-kernel
K:X\times H_{e}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

K(i,(+,j)):=a_{+}(i,j),\qquad K(i,(-,j)):=a_{-}(i,j),

and a value field
V:H_{e}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
by
V(+,j):=w_{j}
,
V(-,j):=-w_{j}
.
Then the GA plan update

(A^{\mathrm{FFN}}V)(i):=\sum_{(s,j)\in H_{e}}K(i,(s,j))V(s,j)+b_{2}

equals
\mathrm{FFN}(h(i))
for all
i\in X
.

Proof.

For each
j\in H
,
\phi((W_{1}h(i)+b_{1})_{j})=a_{+}(i,j)-a_{-}(i,j)
by definition of positive/negative parts.
Therefore

\sum_{(s,j)\in H_{e}}K(i,(s,j))\,V(s,j)=\sum_{j\in H}\Big(a_{+}(i,j)\,w_{j}+a_{-}(i,j)\,(-w_{j})\Big)=\sum_{j\in H}\phi((W_{1}h(i)+b_{1})_{j})\,w_{j},

which equals
W_{2}\phi(W_{1}h(i)+b_{1})
. Adding
b_{2}
completes the identity.
∎

Corollary 5.3

(MoE as GA; FFN as dense MoE)
.

Let
B
index experts and let
f_{b}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
be expert outputs with gate

\alpha(x,\cdot)
a row distribution on
B
. Then
\sum_{b\in B}\alpha(x,b)\,f_{b}(x)
is a GA conditional update on the
expert carrier
B
(Proposition
5.2
). The two-layer FFN is the special case in which

B
is the hidden-unit carrier and expert outputs are the fixed vectors
\{w_{h}\}
with weights induced by
\phi(W_{1}x+b_{1})

(Theorem
5.1
).

Corollary 5.4

(Transformer blocks as staged GA compositions (PreNorm prototype))
.

Fix a carrier
X
and let
R_{t}:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}
be the residual record at stage
t
.
Consider the staged procedure of Definition
5.3
with Markov memory
\mathsf{Mem}_{t}(R_{0:t})=R_{t}
,
a chart
\mathsf{Chart}_{t}=\mathrm{Norm}
, and additive composition
\mathsf{Comp}_{t}(H,\Delta)=H+\Delta
.

If the attention update
\mathsf{Upd}_{t}^{\mathrm{attn}}
is a GA anchored operator on
X\times X

(as in Theorem
4.2
) and the feedforward update
\mathsf{Upd}_{t}^{\mathrm{ffn}}
is a GA plan update
(as in Theorem

), then the standard two-sublayer PreNorm block

R_{t}^{(1)}=R_{t}+\mathsf{Upd}_{t}^{\mathrm{attn}}(\mathrm{Norm}(R_{t})),\qquad R_{t+1}=R_{t}^{(1)}+\mathsf{Upd}_{t}^{\mathrm{ffn}}(\mathrm{Norm}(R_{t}^{(1)}))

is a composition of GA updates under the same primitive operator interface. Multi-head attention is the parallel-operator
special case recorded in Section
4
.

Definition 5.14

(Kernel-induced discrete measures and integrals)
.

Let
X,Y
be finite carriers and let
K:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a plan-kernel.
For each
x\in X
, define a finite measure
\mu_{x}
on
Y
by

\mu_{x}(\{y\})\;:=\;K(x,y)\qquad(y\in Y).

For any value field
v:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the integral of
v
against
\mu_{x}
by

\int_{Y}v(y)\,d\mu_{x}(y)\;:=\;\sum_{y\in Y}v(y)\,\mu_{x}(\{y\}).

If
Z(x):=\mu_{x}(Y)=\sum_{y\in Y}K(x,y)>0
, define the associated probability measure
\pi_{x}
by

\pi_{x}(\{y\}):=K(x,y)/Z(x)
and the corresponding expectation
\int v\,d\pi_{x}
.

Theorem 5.2

(GA plan and conditional updates are integral operators)
.

Let
K:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a plan-kernel and let
\mu_{x}
be the induced finite measures
(Definition
5.14
). Then for every
v:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(A_{\Pi}v)(x)\;=\;\sum_{y\in Y}K(x,y)\,v(y)\;=\;\int_{Y}v(y)\,d\mu_{x}(y).

If
Z(x)>0
and
\pi_{x}
is the normalized measure, then the row-conditional update satisfies

(\widetilde{A}_{\pi}v)(x)\;=\;\sum_{y\in Y}\pi_{x}(\{y\})\,v(y)\;=\;\int_{Y}v(y)\,d\pi_{x}(y).

Proof.

Immediate from the definitions of
A_{\Pi}
,
\mu_{x}
, and the discrete integral in
Definition
5.14
. Normalization yields the conditional form.
∎

Corollary 5.5

(Mixtures correspond to mixtures of measures)
.

Under the constructions in Propositions
5.2
–
5.3
,
the branch-indexed kernels induce branch-indexed measures whose convex (conditional) or conic (plan) combinations
realize the corresponding operator mixtures pointwise in
x
.

5.4
Alternative branches in the prune tree

Each item below is a branch: relax one regime choice in Section
1.1
and you land in a different operator regime.

B1.

Non-exponential links.

If the compositionality law (Postulate
3.2
) is relaxed (e.g. evidence saturates or composes non-multiplicatively),
the exponential link is not forced and alternative link functions
\psi
arise.

B2.

Alternative probe families
\Rightarrow
alternative gauges.

Row-conditional probes induce a row-unary quotient; balanced plan probes enlarge the gauge (Proposition
2.1
).
Other observables (symmetric normalizations, doubly-anchored conditionals, hard top-
k
probes) correspond to different quotients
and therefore different “irreducible” relational content.

B3.

Beyond low-rank interaction.

Assumption
4.5
is a complexity approximation. Dropping it yields fully general interaction structure; replacing it
with structured sparsity, higher-order interactions, mixtures, or multi-resolution factorizations yields other canonical forms.

B4.

Fixed vs adaptive carriers.

A globally fixed carrier is a strong regime assumption. Allowing carriers to evolve (Section
5.1
) turns depth into a scale-walk:
an alternation of relational inference and bucketization updates.

B5.

Training as procedure search.

When structural moves change carriers/anchors, training becomes a search over procedures rather than descent on a stable scalar potential;
see Section
6.3
.

6.2
Context, related work, and positioning

Scope of this section.

Sections
2
–
5
contain the formal primitives and derivations. This section records regime placement and comparisons only; no result in Sections
2
–
5
depends on it.

We organize related work around the move ledger in Section
1.1
. Standard practice in the attention literature is to fix
(i) a carrier (tokenization, neighborhood graph, or sparse pattern),
(ii) a link and normalization (softmax or a substitute),
(iii) a probe/anchor regime (row-conditionals vs plan-based constraints), and
(iv) an interaction-complexity regime (full, sparse, or low-rank),
and then optimize parameters within that frozen regime. GA treats (i)–(iv) as regime declarations and records which operator forms are implied once they are declared.

Attention lineage and the Transformer.

Neural attention entered modern sequence modeling as learned soft alignment in encoder–decoder translation

[
2
,
18
]
. The Transformer
[
23
]

made attention the primary routing primitive, popularizing scaled dot-product attention
and multi-head composition. GA is not an alternative architecture; it is an operator-level
decomposition of an attention layer into carriers, kernels, probes, and anchors/updates,
together with regime-conditional implications that identify which kernel/score forms
follow from declared prunes.

Kernel/energy viewpoints and subquadratic attention.

A large line of work treats attention as kernel computation and targets quadratic cost via approximation regimes:
linearized feature-map attention
[
14
]
, random-feature approximations in Performer

[
6
]
, low-rank assumptions in Linformer
[
26
]
, and Nyström
reconstructions
[
27
]
. Sparse-pattern variants impose structured sparsity (e.g. Longformer

[
3
]
, BigBird
[
28
]
). GA clarifies where these approximations live:
they are interaction-complexity regime choices applied to the unary-quotiented interaction component, not changes to the
primitive operator semantics.

Optimal transport, matrix scaling, and plan-based anchors.

Entropic optimal transport and matrix scaling provide a plan-based anchoring regime on kernels. In the classical OT view,
one studies transport plans and their regularized variants
[
25
,
20
]
; entropic
regularization yields Sinkhorn-type scaling algorithms
[
7
,
11
]
rooted in matrix
scaling
[
22
,
16
]
. Unbalanced OT extends this regime when marginal constraints are
soft rather than hard
[
5
]
. In GA, these are explicit anchor choices (Section
2.7
)
and correspond to gauge-fixing relative to the probe family (Proposition
2.1
), with different canonical
invariants than the row-conditional regime.

Routing, modularity, and outer-loop structure.

Mixture-of-experts architectures
[
21
,
10
]
and retrieval-style models such as Perceiver

[
12
]
emphasize that attention-like updates can act as routing, not only smoothing. Token-level
structure operations (e.g. merging/pruning)
[
4
]
and explicitly multi-scale attention

[
1
]
likewise move regime selection (carrier schedules, depth policies, structural edits) into the outer
loop. GA formalizes this separation: the core derives operator semantics conditional on a regime, while training/search
selects regimes and parameterizations (Section
6.3
).

Low-rank normal forms and dot products.

After quotienting unary row/column score fields, the remaining interaction component is a matrix on
X_{\ell}\times Y_{\ell}
. Declaring a rank-
r
interaction approximation and applying Eckart–Young/SVD yields a canonical rank-
r
factorization
[
8
,
13
]
; dot-product score charts implement this factorization.

Fixed relational carriers (graphs and neighborhoods).

Graph neural networks fix an explicit relational carrier and perform message passing on that graph; graph convolution

[
15
]
and graph attention
[
24
]
are canonical examples. In GA terms, the graph prior is
a carrier/constraint regime choice (encoded via
\Gamma_{\ell,0}
and hard masks in
S
), not a derived primitive.

6.2.1
HSA as a constraint-defined anchor in the prune tree

Hierarchical Self-Attention (HSA)
[
1
]
fixes a multi-scale/hierarchical structure and derives an attention mechanism by an entropy-minimization criterion that is characterized as closest to standard softmax attention subject to hierarchy-induced constraints. In GA terms, this is a fixed-carrier regime together with (i) an explicit constraint family on admissible plans and (ii) an anchor defined by a stated variational selection rule on that feasible set. GA treats this as one named anchor branch (baseline + constraints + selection criterion), rather than as an additional primitive of the attention operator.

Positioning.

GA is a regime-explicit decomposition that (i) records which ingredients are implied under declared prunes (notably the exponential link under scalar-work + composition, and probe-induced gauge), and (ii) separates efficient/sparse/OT/modular methods by the regime coordinates they modify (carrier, link, probe/anchor, interaction complexity), rather than treating them as variants of a single monolithic operator.

6.3
Training as procedure search and non-exact acceptance

Remark 6.1

(Training is an outer-loop search over regimes and parameterizations)
.

The main text derives operator semantics given primitives and declared prunes. Training operates outside this: it selects (explicitly or implicitly)

\displaystyle(\text{carrier schedules})\times(\text{score parameterizations})\times(\text{priors/masks})

\displaystyle\times(\text{probe/anchor regimes})\times(\text{depth policies}).

and may therefore induce nontrivial path dependence when structural moves change the carrier or the observable quotient.

Definition 6.1

(Procedure-space moves and acceptance)
.

Let
r
denote the current realized record/procedure state. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a set of admissible moves
m:r\to r^{\prime}

(e.g. split/merge buckets, change anchors, change rank, local parameter updates).
A generic training loop iterates: propose
r^{\prime}\sim q(\cdot\mid r)
, evaluate outcomes under
r^{\prime}
, then accept or reject.

Remark 6.2

(Global scalar potentials are not implied under structural moves)
.

Assume the admissible move set
\mathcal{M}
contains
structural
moves that change the carrier or the probe/anchor (hence the observable quotient).
Then successive candidates may act on different quotients of the same underlying input. In particular, order effects can occur:
there may exist
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
(m_{2}\circ m_{1})
and
(m_{1}\circ m_{2})
are inequivalent in outcome space.
Such noncommutativity is compatible with a path-dependent acceptance functional and does not imply the existence of a single scalar potential
\Phi(r)
that is invariant under all admissible structural moves.
This is the training analogue of prune-tree branching (Appendix
B
, Definition
B.3
) and negative construction chains (Appendix
B
, Definition
B.2
).

Remark 6.3

(No global Lyapunov function across structural moves)
.

Within a fixed regime (fixed carrier, fixed probe/anchor choices), training dynamics may admit a Lyapunov-type objective:
a scalar
\Phi
that decreases along the realized update rule.
However, once the admissible move set
\mathcal{M}
includes
structural
moves that change the carrier or the observable quotient,
there is no reason to expect a single global
\Phi
that is monotone for
all
moves.
Equivalently, a Lyapunov description may exist locally on a branch, but need not glue across prune-tree branching.

Definition 6.2

(Acceptance 1-form)
.

We model path-dependent training by an
acceptance 1-form

\omega
, i.e. a discrete edge functional on the move graph,
assigning an effort increment to each directed move:

\omega:\ (r\to r^{\prime})\mapsto\omega(r\to r^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}.

Moves may be accepted if
\omega(r\to r^{\prime})<0
, or stochastically with probability
\min(1,\exp(-\beta\,\omega(r\to r^{\prime})))
. If
\omega
is exact (i.e. there exists
\Phi
with
\omega(r\to r^{\prime})=\Phi(r^{\prime})-\Phi(r)
for all admissible moves),
this reduces to scalar-potential descent;
if
\omega
has nonzero circulation, acceptance is path-dependent.

Outlook

Remark 6.4

(Concentric o-set architectures)
.

By a
concentric o-set architecture
we mean a staged operator design that retains explicit open-world
witness semantics (
\{\mathsf{acc},\bot\}
) in
inner
layers and only applies Newtonian/extensional closures
in
outer
layers when a task interface demands two-valued outputs.
Equivalently: closure is treated as a controllable stage-restriction move, not a global semantic commitment.

(Immediate directions)
.

(i)

Non-Newtonian architectures:
represent

and witnessed membership explicitly in the operator interface, so concentric o-set semantics
does not have to live implicitly in nonlinear dynamics (Appendix

B.1
).

(ii)

Multi-scale adaptive systems:
make carrier schedules explicit and composable across depth, yielding architectures where scale selection is
a first-class procedural object (Section
5.1
).

(iii)

Beyond low-rank:

5.4
).

Appendix A
Operational sets (o-sets): witness protocols under continuation and refinement

Definition A.1

(Pre-bootstrap notion)
.

By a
bootstrap
we mean a fixed-point adequacy condition:
a specification of the minimal constraints that must already hold
for a given class of descriptions or concepts to be meaningful at all.

A bootstrap is not an axiom assumed within a theory, nor a theorem
derived from prior axioms. Rather, it characterizes the weakest
self-consistent regime in which the theory can be stated without
presupposition. Concepts introduced via a bootstrap are defined by
the role they must play for the framework to apply to itself. Bootstrap conditions are evaluated relative to the act of description itself,
not relative to an external ontology.

Definition A.2

(Bootstrap definition of an agent)
.

An
agent
is an operational entity capable of executing finite procedures
and producing finite records that persist under continuation.
Operationally, an agent supports witnessed acceptance: the ability to realize,
via finite procedures, acceptance reports that can be stably recorded and later
retrieved.

An agent is said to be
theorizing-capable
if it can maintain finite records
containing multiple simultaneously accepted statements.
This finite joint maintainability is not an additional capability, but the minimal
operational content of memory, conjunction, and rule application.

Agents are not assumed to have complete information, total decision procedures,
or closed-world knowledge; absence of a witnessed acceptance carries no semantic
commitment.

Definition A.3

(Bootstrap definition of semantics)
.

A
semantics
is an operational assignment of meaning to records and procedures
relative to an agent, given by the agent’s witnessed acceptance behavior.
Concretely, a semantics specifies which reports may be accepted, how acceptances
persist under continuation, and how multiple acceptances may be jointly maintained.

A semantics is
operationally adequate
if it admits stable finite records with
multiple simultaneously accepted statements, so that theories and rules can be
represented as operational artifacts.
A semantics is
reflexively closed
if the same acceptance criteria apply to
records describing the semantics itself.

Semantics need not be total, extensional, or two-valued; such properties, when
present, arise only in strengthened regimes and are not assumed at the bootstrap
level.

Postulate A.1

(Bootstrap: operational reflexive closure)
.

We consider operational universes intended to model agents capable of theorizing.
Such agents demonstrably form theories, proofs, and models, understood operationally
as stable finite records containing multiple simultaneously maintained accepted statements.

Any operational semantics adequate for describing such agents must therefore admit:
(i) witnessed acceptance realized by finite procedures,
(ii) persistence of witnessed acceptance under continuation,
and (iii) finite joint composability of witnessed acceptances.
Without these properties, conjunction, multi-premise rule application, and theory
formation are operationally ill-defined.

Conversely, strengthening these requirements to enforce global extensionality or
total two-valued completion destroys nontrivial reflexive closure, as self-description
either trivializes or becomes inconsistent.

The axioms introduced below formalize the unique minimal nontrivial fixed point of
this bootstrap condition: the weakest operational regime that is reflexively closed
under theorizing, including theorizing about the operational universe itself.

In particular, worlds lacking these properties may exist, but only appear here as
objects of description inside a theorizing operational universe.

Convention A.1

(Reference interface; no global-agent assumption)
.

For exposition, this appendix is written as if a single agent supplies a single operative interface
(stages, procedure repertoire, stage logs, and an operative probe family), and we suppress explicit
agent indices accordingly.
Formally, all objects in this appendix are defined
relative to a chosen operative interface
;
this interface may come from a literal single agent, or from a multi-agent system after aggregating
agents and discarding internal distinctions not exposed to the chosen probes.

A.1
Procedural substrate

Definition A.4

(Stages and continuation)
.

Let
\mathsf{Hist}
be a class of evaluation stages equipped with a continuation preorder
\succeq

(write
h^{\prime}\succeq h
for “
h^{\prime}
continues
h
”). Define the abstract extendible stages

\mathsf{Hist}^{+}:=\{\,h\in\mathsf{Hist}:\exists h^{\prime}\succ h\,\}.

No global assumption is made that
\mathsf{Hist}^{+}=\mathsf{Hist}
. Write
h^{\prime}\succ h
for
h^{\prime}\succeq h
and not
(h\succeq h^{\prime})
(strict part of the preorder).

Definition A.5

(Witness record universe)
.

Fix a witness type (interface)
X
.
Let
\mathsf{Rec}(X)
denote the finitely representable records that can serve as candidate witnesses of type
X
.

Definition A.6

(Typed finite procedures)
.

For witness types
Y,X
, let
\mathsf{Proc}(Y\to X)
denote the class of finite procedures that,
given an input record of type
Y
, can produce an output record of type
X
.

Definition A.7

(Domain-suppressed procedures)
.

Define

\mathsf{Proc}(\to X):=\bigcup_{Y}\mathsf{Proc}(Y\to X),

i.e. procedures with output type
X
and an unspecified (possibly varying) input type.
For
P\in\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
write
\mathrm{dom}(P)
for its (implicit) input type.

Definition A.8

(Realized histories and realized stages)
.

A
realized history
is a subset
\gamma\subseteq\mathsf{Hist}
such that:

1.

(
Chain
) for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in\gamma
, either
h_{1}\succeq h_{2}
or
h_{2}\succeq h_{1}
;

2.

(
Prefix-closed
) if
h_{2}\in\gamma
and
h_{2}\succeq h_{1}
, then
h_{1}\in\gamma
.

Let
\Gamma
be a nonempty family of realized histories, and define the
realized stages

\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma.

We assume
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}\neq\varnothing
in any regime under discussion.

Definition A.9

(Realized continuation)
.

For stages
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}
write

h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\gamma\in\Gamma\ \text{such that}\ h,h^{\prime}\in\gamma\ \text{and}\ h^{\prime}\succeq h.

Write
h^{\prime}\succ_{\Gamma}h
if
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
and not
(h\succeq h^{\prime})
.
Define the realized-extendible stages

\mathsf{Hist}^{+}_{\Gamma}:=\{\,h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}:\exists h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}\ \text{with}\ h^{\prime}\succ_{\Gamma}h\,\}.

Definition A.10

(Persistence regime)
.

Let
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}\subseteq\mathsf{Hist}
denote a regime of stages in which the relevant
record/log subsystem is persistent along realized continuation: if
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
and

h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
, then logged records are not erased. This allows local end-of-time or local
persistence failure outside
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
.

Definition A.11

(Finite procedures and stage logs (persistence regime))
.

For
h\in\mathsf{Hist}
and
P\in\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
, define a finite stage log

\mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)\subseteq\mathsf{Rec}(X),

interpreted as the records for type
X
that are already produced/available by stage
h
via
P
.
We interpret
h
as including the
operative-interface-accessible
persistent record/log state relevant to these outputs.

In the persistence regime, logs are monotone under realized continuation:
if
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
and
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
, then

\mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)\subseteq\mathsf{Out}_{h^{\prime}}(P).

(No monotonicity under refinement is assumed a priori.)

Definition A.12

(Operative probe family)
.

An
operative probe family
is a designated set

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}\subseteq\bigcup_{X}\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)

of finite procedures whose stage logs are treated as the operative observables of the regime.
In particular, communication/comparison procedures belong to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}
whenever agents
intend to verify correlations across subsystems (e.g. by exchanging records).

Definition A.13

(Operative prefix-state equivalence)
.

For stages
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}
, define

h\equiv h^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}},\ \mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)=\mathsf{Out}_{h^{\prime}}(P),

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}
is the operative probe family (Definition
A.12
).
This relation restricts to an equivalence relation on
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}
.

Definition A.14

(Realized continuation up to operative prefix equivalence)
.

Write
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma,\equiv}h
iff there exists
\tilde{h}
with
\tilde{h}\equiv h
such that

h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}\tilde{h}
.

Definition A.15

(Refinement order (micro
\to
macro convention))
.

Stages also carry an (independent) refinement preorder
\sqsupseteq
, read as:

h^{\prime}\sqsupseteq h\quad\text{``$h^{\prime}$ is a refinement of $h$ (more micro / more informative).''}

The open side is the refined side; the closed side is the coarser side. Refinement is understood as a
frame-/protocol-relative informational ordering, not a globally privileged ontic order.

Remark A.1

(Two independent preorders: stages vs. o-sets)
.

The symbol
\sqsupseteq
is a preorder on
stages
(informational refinement: “more micro / more informative”).
It should not be conflated with the forced preorder
\preceq
on
o-sets

(Proposition
A.3
), which is defined by stagewise inclusion of admissible witnesses:

S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}},\ \mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}}).

No identification between these relations is assumed. The only bridge is the semantic one enforced by
Stability of acceptance (Axiom
A.4
): if a record is accepted at a stage, then it remains
accepted under realized continuation in the persistence regime and under stage refinement.

Definition A.16

(Relevant stages for witnessed membership)
.

Define the relevant stage class

\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}:=\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}\cap\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}.

These are stages that (i) lie on at least one realized history and (ii) lie in the persistence regime,
so that witnessed membership and monotonicity claims are meaningful. We assume
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}\neq\varnothing

whenever the preorder/equality notions below are invoked.

A.2
Effective agents and multi-agent aggregation (agent-level coarse-graining)

Definition A.17

(Agent interface data)
.

An
agent interface
consists of a procedure repertoire together with stage logs and a designated
operative probe family:

(\mathsf{Proc}^{a},\mathsf{Out}^{a},\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{a}),

where
\mathsf{Proc}^{a}\subseteq\bigcup_{X}\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
is a designated collection of finitary procedures (with typed outputs),

\mathsf{Out}^{a}_{h}(\cdot)
assigns stage logs at stage

to procedures in
\mathsf{Proc}^{a}
,
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{a}\subseteq\mathsf{Proc}^{a}
is the operative probe family whose outputs are treated as the

Definition A.18

(Multi-agent universe)
.

A
multi-agent universe
is a finite family of agent interfaces
\{(\mathsf{Proc}^{a_{i}},\mathsf{Out}^{a_{i}},\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{a_{i}})\}_{i\in I}

living over a common stage class
\mathsf{Hist}
with realized continuation and a persistence regime, so that all stage logs

\mathsf{Out}^{a_{i}}_{h}(\cdot)
are interpreted at the same stage
h\in\mathsf{Hist}
.

Definition A.19

(System-level probe family)
.

Fix a multi-agent universe.
A
system-level probe family
is a designated set
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
of finitary procedures whose outputs are
treated as the external observables of the
whole system
.
Concretely,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
may include probes internal to specific components as well as communication/comparison
procedures that read multiple components.
For
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
write
\mathsf{Out}^{\mathsf{sys}}_{h}(P)
for the stage log of
P
at stage
h
.
Formally,
\mathsf{Out}^{\mathsf{sys}}
is a stage-log assignment of the same kind as
\mathsf{Out}
in the procedural substrate,
but applied to the system-level probe family.

Definition A.20

(Aggregation and effective agent)
.

Fix a system-level probe family
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
.
Define the induced system probe-equivalence on stages by

h\equiv_{\mathsf{sys}}h^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}},\ \mathsf{Out}^{\mathsf{sys}}_{h}(P)=\mathsf{Out}^{\mathsf{sys}}_{h^{\prime}}(P).

(Thus
\equiv_{\mathsf{sys}}
depends on the chosen
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
.)
The
effective agent
associated to
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}},\mathsf{Out}^{\mathsf{sys}})
is the single-interface description obtained by
discarding all internal probes not in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
; operationally, its state is exactly what is recoverable from the
system probe logs.
Equivalently: aggregation is the coarse-graining that identifies stages up to
\equiv_{\mathsf{sys}}
.

Remark A.2

(Why this is the correct notion of “component composition”)
.

Declaring a collection of components to form a single module is not an o-set operation; it is an
agent-level
coarse-graining:
one specifies which probes are treated as the external interface and ignores the rest.
Two different modular decompositions correspond to different choices of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{op}}^{\mathsf{sys}}
(different observable interfaces),
hence different effective agents.

Definition A.21

(Decision alphabet (one-acceptance regime))

We fix the global decision alphabet and acceptance set to be

\mathcal{A}:=\{\mathsf{acc},\bot\},\qquad\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}:=\{\mathsf{acc}\},

where
\mathsf{acc}
denotes a witnessed acceptance at the current stage and

Accordingly, whenever an o-set is queried at stage

or
\bot
.

Definition A.22

(Operational set (o-set))
.

Fix the global decision alphabet and acceptance set
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}})
as in
Definition
A.21
. An operational set (o-set) on witness type
X
is a pair

S^{\mathrm{o}}=(\mathcal{G}_{S},\mathsf{Ans}_{S}),

where
\mathcal{G}_{S}\subseteq\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
is a finitely describable generator family and

\mathsf{Ans}_{S}:\mathsf{Hist}\times\mathsf{Rec}(X)\to\mathcal{A}

is a finite answer protocol.

A.3.1
Theories and metatheory as o-sets on code

Definition A.23

(Code witness type)
.

Fix a witness type
\mathsf{Code}
(the type of code strings/statements) and let

\mathsf{Thm}_{0}\subseteq\mathsf{Rec}(\mathsf{Code})
denote the subset of records that are admissible as theorem statements
under the operational closure of Proposition
A.2
.
A
code witness type
is such a witness type together with a designated admissible subset of record statements.

Definition A.24

(Theory content as an o-set)
.

A
theory content
is an o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on witness type
\mathsf{Code}
together with the convention that

r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
is read as “the code statement
r
is accepted as a theorem at stage
h
.”

A
metatheory
is similarly an o-set
M^{\mathrm{o}}
on an appropriate witness type
\mathsf{Code}_{M}
that contains
statements about theories (including
T^{\mathrm{o}}
itself), and whose membership is read as metatheoretic acceptance.

Remark A.3

(Metatheory is internal)
.

A key point of the operational stance is that metatheory is not a God’s-eye external object: it is itself an o-set
realized by some procedure. Thus statements like “
T
is consistent” or “
T
proves
\varphi
” are, in this semantics,
additional witnessed membership claims in some metatheory object, and are subject to the same stage/continuation limitations
as any other acceptance protocol.

A.4
Formal clauses realizing the bootstrap postulate

Given Definitions
A.2
and
A.3
,
Postulate
A.1
isolates the minimal operational regime in which
agents and semantics are jointly reflexively closed.

Axiom A.1

(Finitary realizability)
.

All operative-interface-accessible procedures, records, and realized histories are finite.

Axiom A.2

(Patchwise coherence (finite compatibility across realized witnesses))
.

Fix an o-set
S^{\mathrm{o}}
on
X
and a realized prefix stage
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}
.
Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
be records such that for each
i
there exists a realized stage

h_{i}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}
with
h_{i}\equiv h
and a generator
P_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{S}
for which

r_{i}\in\mathsf{Out}_{h_{i}}(P_{i})
and
\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h_{i},r_{i})=\mathsf{acc}
.
Then there exists
h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
with
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
such that for all
i
,

\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h^{\prime},r_{i})=\mathsf{acc}
.

Axiom A.3

(Open extension)
.

For every realized prefix stage
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}
there exists a realized stage

h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{real}}
with
h^{\prime}\succ_{\Gamma}h
.

Axiom A.4

(Stability of acceptance)
.

Let
S^{\mathrm{o}}
be any o-set and let
r
be any witness record. If
\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}
,
then:
(i) for any
h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
with
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
, we have
\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h^{\prime},r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}
;
(ii) for any
h^{\prime}
with
h^{\prime}\sqsupseteq h
, we have
\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h^{\prime},r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}
.

These axioms jointly force a monotone, witness-based semantics of membership and rule out global
extensional or closed-world interpretations unless an additional compression regime is imposed
(§
A.7
).

Proposition A.1

(Bootstrap adequacy forces the operational axioms)
.

Any universe capable of hosting agents that (i) execute finite procedures, (ii) store finite records,
and (iii) maintain internally coherent theories about admissible relations must satisfy
Axioms
A.1
–
A.4
. Under these axioms, the stage-indexed membership
semantics
r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}
(Proposition
A.2
) is the correct semantics for all
membership language used in the main text.

Proof.

Without finitary realizability, agents cannot represent procedures or records.
Without patchwise coherence, finite bundles cannot be jointly maintained under continuation.
Without open extension, terminal prefixes exist; absence becomes decidable by exhaustion and closed-world negation becomes available.
Without stability of acceptance, admissibility is not monotone under continuation/refinement, hence refinement-based semantics is ill-defined.
Therefore the axioms are necessary, and the stage-indexed membership semantics already defined above is the semantics
implicitly used by any agent-theoretic subset/membership language in the main text.
∎

Remark.

Kripke semantics and forcing models arise as presentations of the
structure induced by these axioms; they are not assumed. Classical extensional set semantics
appears only under the additional extensional decoding regime of
§
A.7
.

A.5
Consequences: forced membership and induced order

Proposition A.2

(Forced stage-indexed membership predicate)
.

Fix an o-set
S^{\mathrm{o}}
on witness type
X
and a stage
h\in\mathsf{Hist}
.
There is a unique subset
\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\subseteq\mathsf{Rec}(X)
satisfying:

r\in\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists P\in\mathcal{G}_{S}\ \text{s.t.}\ r\in\mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)\ \text{and}\ \mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}.

We write
r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}
as shorthand for
r\in\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
.

Proof.

Existence holds by taking
\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
to be the right-hand side set.
Uniqueness is immediate: any subset satisfying the displayed biconditional must equal that set.
∎

Construction A.1

(Finite report alphabets from one-acceptance witnesses)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite report alphabet with a distinguished “unknown” symbol

\bot\in\Sigma
and write
\Sigma^{\star}:=\Sigma\setminus\{\bot\}
.
Fix a common witness type
X
and, for each label
\sigma\in\Sigma^{\star}
, a one-acceptance
o-set
S_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{o}}
on
X
.
Define the induced
\Sigma
-valued report at stage
h
on record
r
by the decoding

\mathsf{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)=\begin{cases}\sigma&\text{if there exists a unique }\sigma\in\Sigma^{\star}\text{ with }r\in_{h}S_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{o}},\\
\bot&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, a symmetric two-outcome regime (e.g.
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-},\bot\}
)
is represented by the pair
(S_{\sigma_{+}}^{\mathrm{o}},S_{\sigma_{-}}^{\mathrm{o}})
without
introducing a second acceptance label inside the global o-set decision alphabet.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathsf{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)
returns a non-unknown label iff exactly one of the
membership predicates
\{\,r\in_{h}S_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{o}}:\sigma\in\Sigma^{\star}\,\}
holds,
and returns
\bot
otherwise.
∎

Lemma A.1

(Monotone growth under continuation (persistence regime))
.

If
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{pers}}
and
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
, then

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h^{\prime}}(S^{\mathrm{o}}).

Proof.

Let
r\in\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
. By Proposition
A.2
,

\exists P\in\mathcal{G}_{S}
with
r\in\mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)
and
\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}
.
By stage-log monotonicity,
r\in\mathsf{Out}_{h^{\prime}}(P)
. By Axiom
A.4
(i),

\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h^{\prime},r)\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{acc}}
. Apply Proposition
A.2
again to conclude

r\in\mathsf{Adm}_{h^{\prime}}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
.
∎

A.5.1
Refinement preorder and operational equality

Proposition A.3

(Induced refinement preorder on o-sets)
.

For o-sets
S^{\mathrm{o}},T^{\mathrm{o}}
on the same witness type
X
, define the relation

S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}
by the forced semantic condition

S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}},\ \mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}}).

Then
\preceq
is a preorder. Moreover, it is the coarsest relation on o-sets such that

S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}
implies
r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}\Rightarrow r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
for all relevant stages
h
.

Proof.

Reflexivity is immediate. Transitivity follows from transitivity of
\subseteq
.
For minimality: any relation implying stagewise inclusion on all relevant stages must contain the pairs
satisfying the displayed condition.
∎

Remark A.4

(No primitive scalar quantification of continuation/refinement in this paper)
.

The continuation preorder
\succeq
and the refinement preorder
\sqsupseteq
are treated as primitive order structure
of the operative interface.
No result in this paper assumes the existence of a scalar functional
W:\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
whose
monotonicity
generates
either preorder (e.g.
h^{\prime}\succeq h\Rightarrow W(h^{\prime})\geq W(h)
or

h^{\prime}\sqsupseteq h\Rightarrow W(h^{\prime})\geq W(h)
).

A scalar work/cost/time quantity is nevertheless compatible with the framework when introduced as a
derived

observable: given finite report alphabets, their induced quotient carriers, and a joint witnessing regime, one may
define scalar functionals as projections/pushforwards of refinement-invariant observables on the corresponding
quotient carrier(s) (and, in multi-interface settings, on finite products of such carriers). This paper does not
develop that derivation; it only requires the preorders and their persistence/stability laws as stated.

Remark A.5

(Non-vacuity)
.

If
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}=\varnothing
in a pathological regime, the relation
\preceq
is not used;
all comparisons are understood only in regimes where
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}
is nonempty.

Proposition A.4

(Operational equality)
.

Define
S^{\mathrm{o}}\simeq T^{\mathrm{o}}
iff
S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}
.
Then
\simeq
is an equivalence relation (operational equality).

Proof.

Immediate from the preorder properties of
\preceq
.
∎

Remark A.6

(Extensionality is not assumed)
.

Operational equality is mutual refinement (mutual simulability at the level of witnessed admissibility).
Classical extensional equality arises only in a special compression regime (§
A.7
).

A.5.2
Union, meets, and the absence of complements

Proposition A.5

(Join is forced)
.

Let
S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}
be o-sets on
X
. There exists an o-set

S^{\mathrm{o}}\vee T^{\mathrm{o}}
which is the least upper bound of
\{S^{\mathrm{o}},T^{\mathrm{o}}\}
under
\preceq
.
A representative is obtained by taking generators
\mathcal{G}_{S\vee T}:=\mathcal{G}_{S}\sqcup\mathcal{G}_{T}

and setting

\mathsf{Ans}_{S\vee T}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc}\ \lor\ \mathsf{Ans}_{T}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc},

otherwise
\bot
.

Proof.

For each relevant stage
h
, the forced membership predicate (Proposition
A.2
) yields

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}}\vee T^{\mathrm{o}})=\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\cup\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}})
.
Hence
S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}\vee T^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}\vee T^{\mathrm{o}}
.
If
U
is any upper bound, then both
\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
and
\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}})
are subsets of

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(U)
, hence so is their union. Therefore
S^{\mathrm{o}}\vee T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq U
.
∎

Proposition A.6

(Meet is forced on the same witness type)
.

Let
S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}
be o-sets on the same witness type
X
. Then there exists an o-set

S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}}
which is the greatest lower bound of
\{S^{\mathrm{o}},T^{\mathrm{o}}\}
under
\preceq
.

A representative is obtained by taking generators
\mathcal{G}_{S\wedge T}:=\mathcal{G}_{S}\sqcup\mathcal{G}_{T}
and setting

\mathsf{Ans}_{S\wedge T}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc}\ \land\ \mathsf{Ans}_{T}(h,r)=\mathsf{acc},

otherwise
\bot
.

Proof.

For each relevant stage
h
, the forced membership predicate yields

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}})=\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})\cap\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}}).

Hence
S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}
.
If
L
is any lower bound, then
\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}})
and

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(T^{\mathrm{o}})
for all relevant
h
, so

\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{Adm}_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}})
and thus
L\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge T^{\mathrm{o}}
.
∎

Proposition A.7

(Paired-witness conjunction on the product type)
.

Let
S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}
be o-sets on witness type
X
. There exists an o-set

S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge_{\times}T^{\mathrm{o}}
on witness type
X\times X
implementing joint witnessing, with

(r_{S},r_{T})\in_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge_{\times}T^{\mathrm{o}})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(r_{S}\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}})\ \land\ (r_{T}\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}).

A representative is obtained by:

\mathsf{Rec}(X\times X):=\mathsf{Rec}(X)\times\mathsf{Rec}(X),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{S\wedge_{\times}T}:=\mathcal{G}_{S}\times\mathcal{G}_{T},

and the global-alphabet protocol

\mathsf{Ans}_{S\wedge_{\times}T}\!\big(h,(r_{S},r_{T})\big)=\mathsf{acc}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{Ans}_{S}(h,r_{S})=\mathsf{acc}\ \land\ \mathsf{Ans}_{T}(h,r_{T})=\mathsf{acc},

otherwise
\bot
.

Proof.

By construction,
(r_{S},r_{T})\in_{h}(S^{\mathrm{o}}\wedge_{\times}T^{\mathrm{o}})
holds exactly when there exist

P_{S}\in\mathcal{G}_{S}
and
P_{T}\in\mathcal{G}_{T}
whose stage logs contain
r_{S}
and
r_{T}
respectively at
h
,
and both are accepted in their respective alphabets. This is equivalent to
r_{S}\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}
and

r_{T}\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
, which is the displayed condition.
∎

Theorem A.1

(No generic complement)
.

There is no operation
S^{\mathrm{o}}\mapsto\neg S^{\mathrm{o}}
definable at this level such that
for all relevant stages
h
and all records
r
,

r\in_{h}\neg S^{\mathrm{o}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\notin_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}.

Proof.

By open extension (Axiom
A.3
) and the forced stage-indexed membership predicate
(Proposition
A.2
),
r\notin_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}
does not provide a finite witness that

r
will never become admissible at a later realized continuation.
Any definition of
\neg S^{\mathrm{o}}
satisfying
the displayed biconditional would therefore require a closed-world non-existence witness, which is excluded by the axioms.
∎

.

(closure under

). Then

.

By construction,

contains
\mathcal{G}_{S}
and is closed under precomposition.
∎

Definition A.27

.

:

.

A.8
Certified two-valued (Newtonian) regimes as internal objects

Purpose.

This subsection records a native way to represent a two-valued (Newtonian) regime
inside

the base witness-only semantics: one does not assume a global two-valued universe; one carries
a
certificate structure
and restricts to stages where it is total on a finite decoded domain.

Definition A.32

(Partial two-label reporter)
.

Fix a witness type
X
and an o-set
S^{\mathrm{o}}
on
X
(witness-only acceptance).
A
partial two-label reporter
for
S^{\mathrm{o}}
is a pair of o-sets

S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},\quad S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}

on the same witness type
X
intended to realize three-valued reporting

\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no},\bot\}
by the decoder:

\mathsf{Rep}(h,r)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{yes}&\text{if }r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},\\
\mathrm{no}&\text{if }r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}},\\
\bot&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We write
\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathrm{o}}:=S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}\vee S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
for the
decidedness
o-set.

Definition A.33

(Consistency and exclusivity)
.

Fix o-sets
S^{\mathrm{o}},T^{\mathrm{o}}
on the same witness type
X
.

(i)

S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}
are
consistent
iff for all stages

h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}
and all records
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
,

r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}\ \Longrightarrow\ r\notin_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}.

(ii)

S^{\mathrm{o}}
and
T^{\mathrm{o}}
are
exclusive
iff for all stages

h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{rp}}
and all records
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
,

\big(r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}\big)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \neg\big(r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}\big).

Definition A.34

(Certified Newtonian stage class for a finite decoded domain)
.

Assume a stage-restricted extensional decoding regime is available for
X
, giving a finite decoded
carrier
X_{\mathrm{ext}}
and a surjective decoding map
\delta_{X}:\mathsf{Rec}(X)\twoheadrightarrow X_{\mathrm{ext}}
on the
stages of interest.

Fix a consistent partial two-label reporter
(S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}})
and write

\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathrm{o}}:=S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}\vee S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
.

Define the
certified Newtonian stage class
to be the stages

such that:

1.

(
Total decidedness on the decoded carrier
)
for all
x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}
there exists

with
\delta_{X}(r)=x
and
r\in_{h}\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathrm{o}}
;

2.

(
Decoded exclusivity
)
for all
x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}
, it is not the case that both

\exists r_{+}\ \big(\delta_{X}(r_{+})=x\ \wedge\ r_{+}\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\exists r_{-}\ \big(\delta_{X}(r_{-})=x\ \wedge\ r_{-}\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}\big)

hold.

Define the certified Newtonian stage class

\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}(X;\delta_{X},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}})\subseteq\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{ext}}

to be the set of stages
h
satisfying (1)–(2).

Theorem A.2

(Classical two-valued membership inside the certified Newtonian class)
.

Write
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}:=\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}(X;\delta_{X},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}})
.
For each
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}
and each decoded element
x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}
, exactly one of the following holds:

\exists r\ (\delta_{X}(r)=x\ \wedge\ r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}})\qquad\text{or}\qquad\exists r\ (\delta_{X}(r)=x\ \wedge\ r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}).

Consequently, the induced decoded sets

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{yes}}(h)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}:\exists r\ (\delta_{X}(r)=x\ \wedge\ r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}})\},

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{no}}(h)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}:\exists r\ (\delta_{X}(r)=x\ \wedge\ r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}})\}.

form a classical partition of
X_{\mathrm{ext}}
at stage
h
:

X_{\mathrm{ext}}=S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{yes}}(h)\ \sqcup\ S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{no}}(h),\qquad S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{no}}(h)=X_{\mathrm{ext}}\setminus S_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathrm{yes}}(h).

Proof.

Fix
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}
and
x\in X_{\mathrm{ext}}
.
By total decidedness, there exists
r
with
\delta_{X}(r)=x
and
r\in_{h}\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathrm{o}}
, i.e.
r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}
or

r\in_{h}S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
(since
\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathrm{o}}=S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}\vee S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
).
Decoded exclusivity rules out both for the same
x
.
Therefore exactly one of the displayed alternatives holds, which implies the partition claim. ∎

Remark A.7

(Why this does not assume a global Newtonian universe)
.

No claim is made that
\mathsf{Hist}_{\mathrm{N}}(X;\delta_{X},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},S^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}})

is all relevant stages, or even nonempty.
The point is representational: a two-valued (Newtonian) regime is a
certified subregime

obtained by carrying explicit reporting structure and restricting to stages where decidedness is total.
Outside this class,
\bot
can occur and must be treated as a genuine outcome rather than negation.

Remark A.8

(Interpretation for carriers in the main text)
.

A fixed carrier set (e.g.
X_{\ell}
) may be read as an extensional compression
X_{\mathrm{ext}}
of an underlying
record space together with a decoding induced by coarse-graining. “Adaptive carriers” correspond to allowing the
decoding regime (hence the quotient) to change with procedure state.

Remark A.9

(Bridge to the objects used in the main paper)
.

The main text uses finite carriers (bucket labels) such as
X_{\ell},Y_{\ell}
as an extensional compression
convenience. Operational subsets of these carriers that appear in the construction (masks/allowed relations,
admissible moves, admissible innovations, constraint sets) are o-sets whose witnesses are
finite records (logs) decoded into bucket labels in an extensional regime. Declared probe families are likewise finite repertoires of admissible tests/observables; equivalence and admissibility checks are implemented by finite probe/evaluation procedures, so the default semantic output is witness-only
\{\mathsf{acc},\bot\}
.
Additional finite report labels (including symmetric two-outcome regimes) are realized by the derived finite-report construction of Construction
A.1
. When a single-valued report is desired, the label o-sets are taken to be mutually exclusive on the operating regime; otherwise the decoding returns the distinguished unknown symbol.

Appendix B
Forced shadows: completion, decision, quotients, and constraint semantics

Remark B.1

(Scope and stance)
.

Appendix

fixes the operational universe: finitary procedures on finitary records along realized
continuations, with open extension and stable witnessed acceptance in the persistence regime. All statements below
are made inside that same universe. In particular:
(i) all membership language is the stage-indexed witnessed membership of Proposition
A.2
;
(ii)
\bot
is the distinguished “not (yet) witnessed” outcome; and
(iii) any well-posed semantics available to an agent must be invariant under its operative probe equivalence
(Definition
A.13
).
This appendix records a set of
forced shadows
: structural obstructions and invariances that follow from
bootstrap adequacy (Postulate
A.1
) and its formal clauses (Axioms
A.1
,
A.2
,
A.3
,

).

B.1
Move taxonomy: restrictions, quotients, sections, closures, and approximations

Remark B.2

(Why prune trees are tracked)
.

A prune tree is an assumption ledger: each named prune records an explicit semantic commitment and the exact
subtree of consequences that depend on it.
This bookkeeping is not cosmetic.
Its purpose is refactorability: if a later analysis identifies a more primitive trunk-level principle from which a
previous prune follows, then all downstream results can be retained verbatim and reclassified from
“assumed” to “derived” by updating only the attachment point in the tree.
In particular, the present paper isolates a small set of structurally essential prunes (notably Prune W) so that
future work can tighten the trunk without reorganizing the leaf-level corollaries.

Definition B.1

(Restriction prune (o-set restriction))
.

Let
X
be a witness type and let
S^{\mathrm{o}},T^{\mathrm{o}}
be o-sets on
X
. A
restriction step
(or
prune
) is a relation

T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}
in the forced refinement preorder of Proposition
A.3
. It is
strict
if

T^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq S^{\mathrm{o}}
but not
S^{\mathrm{o}}\preceq T^{\mathrm{o}}
(equivalently
T^{\mathrm{o}}\not\simeq S^{\mathrm{o}}
).

Definition B.2

(Negative construction chain (restriction moves))
.

A
negative construction chain
is a finite or transfinite sequence of
strict restriction moves

that progressively shrink the admissible semantic regime.
A restriction move may act either by:
(i) a strict o-set restriction prune on a fixed witness type (Definition
B.1
), or
(ii) a strict stage-class restriction to a regime with additional decoding/decidability (Definition
B.6
).

Definition B.3

(Prune tree (restriction ledger))
.

A
prune tree
is a partially ordered family of declared regimes whose order relation is generated by

restriction moves
(o-set restriction prunes and closure/decoding restrictions).
Distinct branches correspond to incompatible regime choices obtained by relaxing or altering specific
restrictions along a negative construction chain (Definition
B.2
).

Definition B.4

(Quotient (probe-induced identification))
.

Fix an object class
\mathcal{X}
(e.g. stages, kernels, records) and a probe family
\mathcal{P}
of admissible procedures
whose outputs are treated as the operative observables on
\mathcal{X}
.
Define the induced observational equivalence

x\sim_{\mathcal{P}}x^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall P\in\mathcal{P},\;P(x)=P(x^{\prime}).

The quotient map
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}/\!\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
is the
probe quotient
.

Definition B.5

(Section (gauge fixing / anchor))
.

Given a quotient
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}/\!\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
, a
section
is any rule

\sigma:\mathcal{X}/\!\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\to\mathcal{X}
such that
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\circ\sigma=\mathrm{Id}
on its domain.
Operationally, sections are
anchors
: canonical representative choices relative to a probe-induced gauge.

Definition B.6

(Closure/decoding restriction)
.

A
closure/decoding restriction
is a restriction to a stage class on which an additional decoding is stable
(e.g. Appendix
A
, §
A.7
) and/or an undecided outcome
\bot
is terminalized (Newtonian closure).
Such moves are restriction moves that act by restricting admissible
stages
(a regime), rather than by changing the
generator family of a fixed o-set.

Definition B.7

(Approximation/compression (representational))
.

An
approximation/compression
is any representational truncation performed
after
a regime is fixed,
e.g. low-rank truncation, structured sparsity, finite charting, etc. Approximations do not change the underlying
witness semantics; they replace an object by a simpler surrogate inside a fixed regime.

Remark B.3

(Why this taxonomy matters)
.

Only restriction prunes (Definition
B.1
) shrink admissible witnesses; quotients (Definition
B.4
)
identify indistinguishable objects; sections (Definition
B.5
) pick representatives; closures/decodings
(Definition
B.6
) restrict stage classes to regimes with additional decidability/decoding; and
approximations (Definition
B.7
) are representational moves. Many confusions arise from calling all of these
“prunes.” In this appendix,
prune
means Definition
B.1
unless explicitly qualified.

B.2
Incompleteness: no total semantic closure

Definition B.8

(Two-label completion of witnessed membership)
.

Fix a witness type
X
and an o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on
X
. Let
\Sigma=\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no},\bot\}
with distinguished unknown
symbol
\bot
. A
two-label completion
of
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on the relevant stage class
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
is a pair of o-sets

T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
on
X
such that the derived decoder
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}
of Appendix
A
(Construction
A.1
)
satisfies, for all
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
and all
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
,

\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)\in\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no}\},\qquad\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)=\mathrm{yes}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}},

equivalently, exactly one of
r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}}
or
r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
holds on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.

Lemma B.1

(Two-label completion induces a complement predicate)
.

If
T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
is a two-label completion of
T^{\mathrm{o}}
in the sense of
Definition
B.8
, then for all
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
and
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
,

\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)=\mathrm{no}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;r\notin_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}.

In particular,
T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
realizes a stagewise complement predicate for
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.

Proof.

On
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
,
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)\neq\bot
by Definition
B.8
, so the decoder outputs

\mathrm{yes}
or
\mathrm{no}
. The correctness condition is
\mathrm{yes}\iff r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
. Therefore
\mathrm{no}
holds iff

\mathrm{yes}
does not, i.e. iff
r\notin_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
.
∎

Theorem B.1

(Incompleteness shadow (non-eliminability of
\bot
))
.

No o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}
admits a two-label completion of witnessed membership on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.
Equivalently, there is no globally valid, stable two-valued completion
\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no}\}
of witnessed membership
on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
; any such attempt forces
\bot
to occur for some
(h,r)
with
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.

Proof.

Suppose
T^{\mathrm{o}}
admits a two-label completion
T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{yes}},T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{no}}
. By
Lemma
B.1
, this yields a stagewise complement predicate for
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.
But Appendix
A
proves there is no generic complement operation at the bootstrap level (Theorem
A.1
).
Contradiction.
∎

Corollary B.1

(No total complement / no stable classical negation at bootstrap)
.

There is no operation
T^{\mathrm{o}}\mapsto\neg T^{\mathrm{o}}
definable at the bootstrap level such that

r\in_{h}\neg T^{\mathrm{o}}\iff r\notin_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
for all
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
and all
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
.
Any regime in which complements exist is a closure/decoding restriction (Definition
B.6
).

B.3
Undecidability: no global decision procedure

Definition B.9

(Decider for an o-set)
.

Fix an o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on witness type
X
. A
decider
for
T^{\mathrm{o}}
on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
is a finite procedure

D\in\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
together with a stable reporting rule producing
\Sigma=\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no},\bot\}
-valued outputs
such that for all
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
and all
r\in\mathsf{Rec}(X)
, the report
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)
satisfies

\mathrm{Rep}_{\Sigma}(h,r)\in\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no}\}
and is correct:

\mathrm{yes}\iff r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
.

Theorem B.2

(Undecidability shadow (no total decision))
.

No o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}
admits a decider on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
in the sense of Definition
B.9
.
Equivalently, there is no finite procedure that outputs a stable, correct total verdict

\mathrm{yes}/\mathrm{no}
for all membership queries
r\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}
on all relevant stages.

Proof.

A decider produces, by definition, a stable
\mathrm{yes}/\mathrm{no}
completion of witnessed membership on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.
Such a completion is exactly a two-label completion in the sense of Definition
B.8

(realized via Construction
A.1
). This contradicts Theorem
B.1
.
∎

Remark B.4

(Undecidability is the decision-form of incompleteness)
.

Theorem
B.2
is not an extra assumption beyond Theorem
B.1
; it is its
algorithmic form. In an open extension world, absence of a witness is not a witness of nonexistence, so no total
decider can exist without a closure/decoding restriction.

B.4
Relativity: probe-quotient semantics

Definition B.10

(Probe equivalence on stages)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\bigcup_{X}\mathsf{Proc}(\to X)
be an operative probe family (Definition
A.12
).
Define the induced stage equivalence
h\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}h^{\prime}
by

h\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}h^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall P\in\mathcal{P},\;\mathsf{Out}_{h}(P)=\mathsf{Out}_{h^{\prime}}(P),

i.e. the full operative probe log state agrees.

Definition B.11

(Well-posed stage-local claim)
.

A stage-local claim
\Phi(h)
is
well-posed relative to
\mathcal{P}
if it is invariant under probe equivalence:

h\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}h^{\prime}\;\Longrightarrow\;\big(\Phi(h)\Leftrightarrow\Phi(h^{\prime})\big).

Theorem B.3

(Relativity shadow (semantics factors through the probe quotient))
.

For any operative probe family
\mathcal{P}
, any stage-local semantics that is operationally meaningful for an agent
using
\mathcal{P}
must be well-posed in the sense of Definition
B.11
. Equivalently, all semantics
available to the agent factors through the quotient map
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathsf{Hist}\to\mathsf{Hist}/\!\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

If
h\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}h^{\prime}
, then the entire operative evidence state available through
\mathcal{P}
is identical at
h
and
h^{\prime}
.
Any semantics that distinguishes
h
from
h^{\prime}
therefore depends on distinctions not present in the agent’s observable
record/log interface, hence is not operationally meaningful. Therefore meaningful semantics must be invariant under

\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}
, i.e. factor through the quotient.
∎

B.5
Equivariance and invariants: probe-preserving transformations are forced

Definition B.12

(Probe-preserving symmetry (kernel of observation))
.

Fix an object class
\mathcal{X}
and a probe family
\mathcal{P}
acting on
\mathcal{X}
(Definition
B.4
).
A transformation
g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is
probe-preserving
(a symmetry) if

x\sim_{\mathcal{P}}g\cdot x
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Let
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{P})
denote the set (monoid) of all such
transformations.

Definition B.13

(Operationally admissible update rule)
.

An update rule
\mathsf{Upd}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is
operationally admissible relative to
\mathcal{P}
if it is compatible
with probe-quotient semantics:

x\sim_{\mathcal{P}}x^{\prime}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathsf{Upd}(x)\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\mathsf{Upd}(x^{\prime}).

Equivalently,
\mathsf{Upd}
factors through the quotient
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}/\!\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Theorem B.4

(Forced equivariance (non–probe-preserving dynamics are inadmissible))
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be an operative probe family on
\mathcal{X}
. Any update rule that is operationally meaningful must
be operationally admissible in the sense of Definition
B.13
. In particular, any admissible
update is equivariant with respect to
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{P})
:

\mathsf{Upd}(g\cdot x)\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\mathsf{Upd}(x)\qquad\forall g\in\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{P}),\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{Upd}
fails to respect
\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
, then there exist
x\sim_{\mathcal{P}}x^{\prime}
such that
\mathsf{Upd}(x)\not\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\mathsf{Upd}(x^{\prime})
.
But then two observationally indistinguishable states evolve to observationally distinguishable states, contradicting
the relativity shadow (Theorem
B.3
) applied to the semantics of the post-update state.
The equivariance statement follows by taking
x^{\prime}=g\cdot x
with
g
probe-preserving.
∎

Corollary B.2

(Invariants are precisely functions on the quotient)
.

A scalar observable
C:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is operationally meaningful only if it is constant on

\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
-classes, i.e.
C=C^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\mathcal{P}}
for some
C^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{X}/\!\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Consequently
C
is invariant under all
g\in\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{P})
:
C(g\cdot x)=C(x)
.

B.7
Effective theories: stability under restriction–quotient–section cycles

Definition B.14

(Regime descriptor)
.

A
regime descriptor

R
packages the operative choices that determine a semantics:

\displaystyle R:=\big(

\displaystyle\text{witness types \& o-sets};\;\mathcal{P}\text{ (probes)};\;\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\text{ (quotients)};\;\sigma\text{ (sections/anchors)};

\displaystyle\text{closure/decoding stage class};\;\text{approximations}\big).

Two descriptors are operationally equivalent if they induce the same observable behavior under the operative probes.

Definition B.15

(Outer-loop move and move cycle)
.

An
outer-loop move
is any transformation of regime descriptors built from the move types in
§
B.1
: restriction prunes, quotients, sections, closures/decodings, and approximations.
A
move cycle
is a composite

\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{S}\circ\mathcal{Q}\circ\mathcal{P},

where
\mathcal{P}
is a restriction step (possibly identity),
\mathcal{Q}
is a quotient move,
\mathcal{S}
is a section choice, and

\mathcal{A}
is an approximation/compression step (possibly identity).

Theorem B.5

(Effective-theory shadow (stability as a fixed point of move cycles))
.

Let
R_{0}
be a regime descriptor and let
\mathcal{M}
be any admissible move cycle.
If the induced observable semantics stabilizes along iteration
R_{n+1}:=\mathcal{M}(R_{n})
in the sense that there exists

N
with
R_{n}\equiv_{\mathrm{obs}}R_{n+1}
for all
n\geq N
, then the stabilized class

[R_{N}]_{\equiv_{\mathrm{obs}}}
is an
effective theory
for the outer-loop dynamics: further iterations of
\mathcal{M}
do not change any
operative observables. Conversely, any regime class that is invariant under
\mathcal{M}
(up to observational equivalence)
is an effective theory for that move cycle.

Proof.

Stabilization is invariance in the observable quotient; invariance is the definition of the fixed point. The bootstrap guarantees that only probe-invariant structure is meaningful
(Theorem
B.3
), so effective theories are fixed points in the observable quotient.
∎

Remark B.6

(Prune trees as the restriction skeleton)
.

Restriction prunes (Definition
B.1
) form the monotone skeleton of outer-loop evolution: any repeated strict prune step
produces a negative construction chain (Proposition
A.3
) and can be diagrammed as a prune tree. Quotients/sections
do not shrink admissible witnesses; they reorganize observable content. Approximations compress representations within a fixed
regime. This separation is what “effective theory” means here.

B.8
Contextuality: no global joint assignment across incompatible probes

Definition B.16

(Incompatible probe families)
.

Two probe families
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
on the same object class
\mathcal{X}
are
jointly compatible
if there exists an operative
refinement probe family
\mathcal{R}
such that
\sim_{\mathcal{R}}
refines both
\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\sim_{\mathcal{Q}}
and the corresponding
reports can be stably maintained together on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
(Axiom
A.2
). Otherwise
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
are

incompatible
.

Theorem B.6

(Contextuality shadow (no global joint valuation))
.

If
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are incompatible probe families, then there is no globally valid, stable joint assignment
that simultaneously reproduces all
\mathcal{P}
-reports and all
\mathcal{Q}
-reports as functions of a single underlying state in
a probe-independent way. Any purported joint assignment must either:
(i) depend on the probe context (be contextual), or
(ii) introduce
\bot
outcomes (be incomplete), or
(iii) restrict to a closure regime where incompatibility is eliminated by fiat (Definition
B.6
).

Proof.

A probe-independent joint assignment would amount to a semantics that distinguishes all
\mathcal{P}
- and
\mathcal{Q}
-observable
differences simultaneously while remaining stable on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
. But incompatibility means there is no operative refinement in which
the corresponding witness bundles can be jointly maintained without context dependence (Axiom
A.2
) and without forcing a completion
of absent witnesses into negations (Theorem
B.1
). Therefore any joint representation must either carry
explicit context dependence, leave some queries undecided (
\bot
), or move to a restricted closure regime in which the incompatibility
is erased.
∎

B.9
Information: constraint on admissible continuations

Definition B.17

(Probe-distinguishable admissible futures)
.

Fix an operative probe family
\mathcal{P}
on stages and let
\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}
be the induced probe equivalence
(Definition
B.10
). For
h\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
, define the set of probe-distinguishable admissible futures by

\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(h)\;:=\;\big\{\,[h^{\prime}]_{\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}}\;:\;h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma,\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}}h\,\big\},

where
\succeq_{\Gamma,\equiv_{\mathcal{P}}}
is realized continuation up to operative equivalence (Definition
A.14
).

Definition B.18

(Information preorder induced by probes)
.

For stages
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
with
h^{\prime}\succeq_{\Gamma}h
, define the probe-information preorder

h\preceq_{\mathcal{P}}h^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(h^{\prime})\subseteq\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(h).

Strict information gain is the strict inclusion
\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(h^{\prime})\subsetneq\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(h)
.

Theorem B.7

(Information shadow (constraint, not content))
.

At the bootstrap level, information is constraint on admissible continuations relative to probes:
any operationally meaningful notion of “information increase” must refine the preorder
\preceq_{\mathcal{P}}
of
Definition
B.18
. In particular, information is not an intrinsic property of a representation; it is the
relational fact that probe-distinguishable admissible futures shrink under witnessed acceptance and continuation.

Proof.

By probe-quotient semantics (Theorem
B.3
), any well-posed semantics factors through the operative
probe quotient. The operative content of “learning” is elimination of previously admissible probe-distinguishable futures.
Therefore any valid information ordering must be monotone with respect to shrinkage of
\mathsf{Fut}_{\mathcal{P}}(\cdot)
, i.e.
must refine
\preceq_{\mathcal{P}}
.
∎

Remark B.7

(Numeric measures are reductions)
.

Numeric quantities such as Shannon entropy, likelihood ratios, KL divergence, and mutual information arise only after
declaring additional structure (finite partitions, probability measures, coding conventions). They are reductions recorded
in Appendix
C
.

Remark B.8

(Do not use differential entropy)
.

Do not use differential entropy as a definition of information.

Differential entropy is coordinate-dependent (not invariant under smooth bijections), can be negative, and does not
track reduction of admissible continuations under refinement. It measures representation-dependent volume, not
operational constraint. For continuous variables, use invariant relative quantities (e.g. KL divergence, mutual information,
likelihood ratios) or entropy
differences
under a fixed coarse-graining.

C.1.1
Gödel incompleteness as non-completability of theoremhood

Definition C.1

(Gödel regime (arithmetical theoremhood as witnessed membership))
.

Fix a code witness type
\mathsf{Code}_{\mathrm{PA}}
encoding sentences of a fixed arithmetic language. Fix a proof witness type

\mathsf{Proof}
and a finitary verifier
\mathsf{Check}:\mathsf{Rec}(\mathsf{Proof})\to\{0,1\}
that accepts exactly valid proofs in the chosen formal system.
Define the theoremhood o-set
T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{thm}}
on
\mathsf{Code}_{\mathrm{PA}}
by

c\in_{h}T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{thm}}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists p\in\mathsf{Rec}(\mathsf{Proof})\ \text{in the stage log by }h\ \text{with}\ \mathsf{Check}(p)=1\ \text{and }p\text{ proves }c.

Theorem C.1

(Gödel reduction)
.

In the Gödel regime (Definition
C.1
), theoremhood admits no stable total two-valued completion on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.
Equivalently, there is no stable reporting regime that assigns every sentence
c
a correct, stable verdict

\{\mathrm{provable},\mathrm{not\ provable}\}
on
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.

Proof.

A stable total two-valued completion of theoremhood is a two-label completion of witnessed membership for the o-set

T^{\mathrm{o}}_{\mathrm{thm}}
. This is ruled out by Theorem
B.1
.
∎

C.2.1
Turing halting

Definition C.2

(Turing regime (universal evaluation + self-coding))
.

Fix a code witness type
\mathsf{Code}_{\mathrm{TM}}
encoding finite programs. Declare finitary interfaces:
(i) a staged evaluator
\mathsf{Eval}
producing finite traces for runs
\mathsf{Eval}(p,x)
,
(ii) a finitary predicate
\mathsf{Term}(\cdot)
recognizing terminating traces, and
(iii) a self-coding operation
\mathsf{Self}(p)
producing the self-input record.
Define the halting predicate by existential witness:

\mathsf{Halts}(p)=1\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists t\ \begin{aligned} &\text{(trace) available by some stage in }\mathsf{Hist}_{rp},\\
&\text{with }\mathsf{Term}(t)=1\ \text{for the run }\mathsf{Eval}(p,\mathsf{Self}(p)).\end{aligned}

Theorem C.2

(Turing reduction (no total halting decider))
.

In the Turing regime (Definition
C.2
), there is no finitary procedure that, on every input program
p
,
returns a stable correct total yes/no verdict for
\mathsf{Halts}(p)
across all of
\mathsf{Hist}_{rp}
.

Proof.

A total stable halting decider would be a stable total two-label completion for witnessed membership of the o-set encoding

\{p:\mathsf{Halts}(p)=1\}
. This is ruled out by Theorem
B.2
.
∎

C.3.1
Einstein (special relativity) as invariant coarse-locality propagation

Definition C.3

(Anchor-relative virtual ontology (VO) and coarse-graining)
.

A
virtual ontology
is a triple

\mathsf{VO}:=(\Omega,\leadsto,\Delta),

where
\Omega
is an inaccessible fine interface,
\leadsto
is a primitive adjacency (“one hop”) relation on
\Omega
,
and
\Delta>0
is the primitive tick. Accessibility is mediated by an anchoring (coarse-graining) map

\pi:\Omega\to\mathcal{E},

where
\mathcal{E}
is the accessible event-carrier (records/events).
The anchoring map
\pi
is part of the declared probe regime and need not be globally fixed; different observers or regimes may employ different (but probe-compatible) coarse-grainings.

Definition C.4

(Coarse-grained locality kernel)
.

Define the VO adjacency kernel
K_{\mathsf{VO}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=1\iff\omega\leadsto\omega^{\prime}
.
Define the induced coarse-grained locality kernel on
\mathcal{E}
by

K_{\mathsf{loc}}(e,e^{\prime})=1\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists\omega\in\pi^{-1}(e),\ \exists\omega^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(e^{\prime})\ \text{with}\ \omega\leadsto\omega^{\prime}.

Operational locality is
K_{\mathsf{loc}}
at the accessible resolution. No refinement-to-zero limit is assumed.

Definition C.5

(Hop distance on
\mathcal{E}
)
.

Let
G_{\mathsf{loc}}
be the directed graph on
\mathcal{E}
with edge
e\to e^{\prime}
iff
K_{\mathsf{loc}}(e,e^{\prime})=1
.
Define
d_{\mathsf{loc}}(e,e^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
as shortest directed path length in
G_{\mathsf{loc}}
.

Definition C.6

(Inertial observer procedure and measured speed)
.

An
inertial observer procedure

F
is an operative probe bundle that provides:
(i) event identification in
\mathcal{E}
;
(ii) a clock protocol
\Delta t_{F}(e_{em},e_{rec})\in\mathbb{N}
counting ticks between emission and reception along a realized history;
(iii) distance readout via
d_{\mathsf{loc}}
.
For a signal instance
s
from
e_{em}
to
e_{rec}
, define

v_{F}(s):=\frac{d_{\mathsf{loc}}(e_{em},e_{rec})}{\Delta t_{F}(e_{em},e_{rec})},\qquad c_{F}:=\sup_{s}v_{F}(s).

Call a signal
lightlike relative to
F
iff
v_{F}(s)=c_{F}
.

Definition C.7

(Einstein probe regime (coarse locality + distinguished bound))
.

Declare an operative probe regime in which:
(i) causal accessibility is defined by
K_{\mathsf{loc}}
(hence
d_{\mathsf{loc}}
);
(ii) inertial re-descriptions are exactly those preserving operative probe outcomes (Theorem
B.3
);
(iii) the distinguished signal class used for synchronization is the class saturating the bound
c_{F}
.

Theorem C.3

(Einstein postulates from coarse locality and probe invariance)
.

Under the Einstein probe regime (Definition
C.7
):

1.

Relativity principle.
No inertial observer procedure is privileged: all well-posed kinematic claims are invariant under admissible re-descriptions.

2.

Invariant propagation bound.
For any two admissible inertial observers
F,F^{\prime}
, one has
c_{F}=c_{F^{\prime}}=:\,c
.

Proof.

(1) is Theorem
B.3
applied to inertial re-descriptions.
(2)
c_{F}
is a function of operative probe outputs by Definition
C.6
; admissible re-descriptions preserve those outputs, hence preserve the supremum.
∎

Remark C.5

(Locality)
.

Locality is
K_{\mathsf{loc}}
at the accessible resolution. Treating locality as infinitesimal and globally coherent is a separate analytic closure used in the classical presentation; it is not assumed here.

Remark C.6

(Discrete does not mean a globally fixed lattice)
.

Throughout, “discrete” refers to finitary reportability under a declared operative interface (finite procedures and finite records),
not to a globally fixed background lattice with preferred coordinates.
Carriers arise as quotient/bucketizations induced by coarse-graining maps, and anchors (coordinate choices / representatives) are
sections that may be changed, updated, or inferred.
Many standard pathologies attributed to “discrete spacetime” arise from imposing a single global anchoring schema as ontology;
that global fixation is
not
assumed here.
In particular, the coarse-graining map
\pi
defining
\mathcal{E}
is part of the regime, not a fixed background structure.

C.3.2
Gauge redundancy as probe quotient (finite/discrete form)

Remark C.7

(Shadow used)
.

We use probe-quotient semantics (Theorem
B.3
) and forced equivariance (Theorem
B.4
).
Gauge is not declared; it is the kernel of the operative probes: transformations invisible to the probe family.

Definition C.8

(Discrete gauge ontology on a finite carrier)
.

Fix a finite directed graph
G=(V,E)
(carrier-level adjacency). A
gauge representative
is an edge-potential

A:E\to\mathbb{R},

(i.e. a labeled 1-cochain on
G
). A
gauge-invariant observable
is any functional of
A
that depends only on
cycle data (holonomy)—for example, for a directed cycle
C=(e_{1},\dots,e_{k})
,

F_{A}(C):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}A(e_{j}).

More generally, a probe family may observe any finite collection of such cycle-sums and/or induced statistics computed from them.

Remark C.8
.

All cochain language here is purely discrete and finite; continuum gauge fields appear only under an additional analytic closure.

Definition C.9

(Gauge transformations (vertex coboundaries))
.

Let
\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}
be a vertex potential. Define the induced edge-coboundary
d\phi:E\to\mathbb{R}
by

(d\phi)(u\to v):=\phi(v)-\phi(u).

A
gauge transform
acts on representatives by

A\mapsto A^{\prime}:=A+d\phi.

Lemma C.1

(Cycle probes are gauge-invariant)
.

For any directed cycle
C
in
G
and any vertex potential
\phi
, one has

F_{A+d\phi}(C)=F_{A}(C).

Proof.

Along a directed cycle
C=(v_{0}\to v_{1}\to\cdots\to v_{k}=v_{0})
,

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(d\phi)(v_{j}\to v_{j+1})=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\phi(v_{j+1})-\phi(v_{j}))=\phi(v_{k})-\phi(v_{0})=0,

so the cycle-sum is unchanged.
∎

Definition C.10

(Gauge probe family and induced quotient)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}
be any operative probe family that depends on
A
only through a finite set of cycle-sums

F_{A}(C_{1}),\dots,F_{A}(C_{m})
(and any deterministic postprocessing thereof). Define observational equivalence on representatives:

A\sim_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}}A^{\prime}\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad P(A)=P(A^{\prime})\ \ \forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}.

Theorem C.4

(Gauge reduction (quotient and forced equivariance))
.

In the regime of Definitions
C.8
–
C.10
:

1.

All gauge transforms
A\mapsto A+d\phi
lie in the probe-preserving symmetry monoid:

A\sim_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}}A+d\phi\qquad\forall\phi:V\to\mathbb{R}.

2.

Any operationally admissible semantics and any admissible update rule on representatives must factor through the quotient

A\mapsto[A]_{\sim_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}}}
(Theorem
B.3
) and be equivariant with respect to all probe-preserving
transformations (Theorem
B.4
).

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
C.1
, every cycle-sum observed by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}
is invariant under
A\mapsto A+d\phi
,
hence every probe output is unchanged. Therefore
A\sim_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}}A+d\phi
.

(2) This is exactly Theorems
B.3
and
B.4
specialized to the representative space

\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{E}
and probe family
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cyc}}
.
∎

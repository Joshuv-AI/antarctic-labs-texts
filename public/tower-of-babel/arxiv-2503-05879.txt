Cohomology of Restricted Twisted Heisenberg Lie Algebras
Yong Yang

Abstract
Over an algebraically closed field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} of characteristic p > 0 p>0 , the restricted twisted Heisenberg Lie algebras are studied. We use the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence relative to its Heisenberg ideal to compute the trivial cohomology. The ordinary 1- and 2-cohomology spaces are used to compute the restricted 1- and 2-cohomology spaces and describe the restricted 1-dimensional central extensions, including explicit formulas for the Lie brackets and − [ p ] -^{[p]} -operators.



Keywords: restricted Lie algebra;
twisted Heisenberg algebra; (restricted) cohomology; central extension

Classification: 17B50; 17B56

Introduction

The definition of twisted Heisenberg Lie algebras was motivated by the connection with differential geometry. More specifically, the twisted Heisenberg Lie algebra is
the tangent algebra of a twisted Heisenberg group (certain semidirect product of a Heisenberg group with the real numbers)
[
3
]
.
Its importance is due to the relationship between twisted Heisenberg algebras and Lorentzian manifolds, for examples see
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
. Algebraically, the twisted Heisenberg Lie algebra can be viewed as a certain 1-dimensional extension of the corresponding Heisenberg Lie algebra as shown in the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

For a non-negative integer
m
, the
Heisenberg Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
is the
(2m+1)
-dimensional vector space over a
field
\mathbb{F}
spanned by the elements

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2m},e_{2m+1}\}

with the non-vanishing Lie
brackets

[e_{i},e_{m+i}]=e_{2m+1},

for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Definition 1.2
.

For a non-negative integer
m
, the
twisted Heisenberg Lie algebra

\mathfrak{h}^{\lambda}_{m}
, parameterized by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in(\mathbb{F}^{\times})^{m}
, is the
(2m+2)
-dimensional vector space over a
field
\mathbb{F}
spanned by the elements

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2m},e_{2m+1},e_{2m+2}\}

with the non-vanishing Lie
brackets

[e_{i},e_{m+i}]=e_{2m+1},\ [e_{2m+2},e_{i}]=\lambda_{i}e_{m+i},\ [e_{2m+2},e_{m+i}]=\lambda_{i}e_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq m
.

The
cohomology with trivial coefficients of Heisenberg Lie algebras
was computed by Santharoubane
[
15
]
over
fields of characteristic zero in 1983.
In 2005, Sköldberg
[
17
]
used algebraic
Morse theory to compute the Poincaré polynomial of Heisenberg Lie algebras
over fields of characteristic
p=2
. In 2008, Cairns and
Jambor
[
2
]
gave the
n
-th Betti number of

\mathfrak{h}_{m}
over fields of prime characteristic for
n\leq m
.

In 1937, Jacobson
[
14
]
introduced the notion of restricted Lie algebras over fields of positive
characteristic and proved that a Lie algebra is restrictable precisely when all
p
-powers of the inner derivations are still inner.
This gives us many examples of restricted Lie algebras, like 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras,
the derivation algebra

\text{Der}(A)
of any algebra
A
, the Lie algebra of an algebraic
group, the primitive elements of an irreducible co-commutative Hopf
algebra, etc. However certain technical tools are available for
restricted Lie algebras which are not available for arbitrary modular Lie algebras, see
[
16
]
for examples.

The restricted structures on
\mathfrak{h}_{m}
were classified and characterized by Jacobson’s theorem in
[
7
]
. However much less is known about restricted twisted Heisenberg algebras.
In this note, we
classify the family of non-isomorphic restricted structures
on the twisted Heisenberg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda,\mu}

parameterized by
the elements
\mu\in\mathbb{F}^{2m+2}
.
By
considering
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda,\mu}
as a restricted one-dimensional
central extension of the restricted Heisenberg Lie algebra,
we compute the ordinary and
restricted cohomology spaces

H^{q}(\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda,\mu})
and

H_{*}^{q}(\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda,\mu})
for
q=1,2
, and give the bracket structures and

[p]
-operators for the corresponding restricted one-dimensional
central extensions of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\lambda,\mu}
.

2.1
Restricted Lie Algebras

Suppose that
\mathrm{char}\ \mathbb{F}=p>0
. A
restricted Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
is a Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
together with a mapping

\cdot^{[p]}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, written

, such that for all

, and all

(1)

(ag)^{[p]}=a^{p}g^{[p]}
;

(2)

(g+h)^{[p]}=g^{[p]}+h^{[p]}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(g,h)
,
where

is the coefficient of

in the formal expression

(\mathrm{ad}(tg+h))^{p-1}(g)
; and

(3)

(\mathrm{ad}\ g)^{p}=\mathrm{ad}\ g^{[p]}
.

The mapping
\cdot^{[p]}
is called a
[p]
-operator
on

\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie algebra is
restrictable
if it is possible to define a

[p]
-operator on it. By Jacobson’s theorem
[
14
,
18
]
, a finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
restrictable if and only if it admits a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
such that
(\mathop{\rm ad}e_{i})^{p}
is an inner derivation for
all
1\leq i\leq n
. Choosing
e_{i}^{[p]}\in\mathfrak{g}
with

\mathop{\rm ad}e_{i}^{[p]}=(\mathop{\rm ad}e_{i})^{p}
for all
i
completely determines a

[p]
-operator on
\mathfrak{g}
.

We only describe the

\displaystyle C^{0}(\mathfrak{g}):

\displaystyle\{1\}

\displaystyle C^{1}(\mathfrak{g}):

\displaystyle\{e^{k}\ |\ 1\leq k\leq n\}

\displaystyle C^{2}(\mathfrak{g}):

\displaystyle\{e^{i,j}\ |\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\}

\displaystyle C^{3}(\mathfrak{g}):

\displaystyle\{e^{u,v,w}\ |\ 1\leq u<v<w\leq n\}

The differentials

,

\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle d^{0}:C^{0}(\mathfrak{g})\to C^{1}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle\ d^{0}=0

\displaystyle d^{1}:C^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\to C^{2}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle\ d^{1}(\psi)(g\wedge h)=\psi([g,h])

\displaystyle d^{2}:C^{2}(\mathfrak{g})\to C^{3}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle\ d^{2}(\varphi)(g\wedge h\wedge f)=\varphi([g,h]\wedge f)-\varphi([g,f]\wedge h)+\varphi([h,f]\wedge g).

The mappings

Formal models for relative adic spaces
Dimitri Dine

Abstract
We extend Raynaud’s theory of formal models from rigid-analytic spaces over a nonarchimedean field to uniform qcqs adic spaces X X , with no finite-type assumptions, over an arbitrary Tate affinoid base S S . The key new ingredient is the notion of a normalized formal blow-up which takes on the role played by admissible formal blow-ups in the classical theory.

Motivation

In his 1974 report

In the decades that followed, Raynaud’s theory of formal models established itself as an indispensable tool, with applications to many topics of interest in algebraic geometry and number theory: Néron models of abelian varieties

[

[

An important step towards this goal was taken in the book of Fujiwara and Kato
[
24

, where the authors define the category of qcqs rigid spaces as the category of qcqs adic formal schemes of finite ideal type localized by admissible formal blow-ups (more general rigid spaces are then defined by gluing qcqs ones) and where they prove many basic properties of admissible formal blow-ups for general adic formal schemes of finite ideal type, with no finiteness or Noetherian assumptions. At the same time, their result on comparison with adic spaces (
[
24
]
, Appendix A to Ch. II, Theorem A.5.2) still relies both on a Noetherian assumption on the base space and on a (topologically) finite-type assumption on the adic spaces and formal schemes involved.

In
[
3
]

However, this result still does not describe the category of (qcqs) uniform adic spaces as a localization of a category of formal models. Indeed, the embedding functor of loc. cit. is given on affinoids by

{\rm Spa}(A,A^{+})\mapsto{\rm Spf}(A^{+})^{{\rm rig}}

(so all rigid spaces in the essential image have formal models which are integrally closed in their generic fiber), but an admissible formal blow-up of the formal scheme
{\rm Spf}(A^{+})
can fail to be integrally closed in its the generic fiber (consider the admissible formal blow-up in any finitely generated open ideal of
A^{+}
which is not a normal ideal).

Hence, thus far, there has been no general analog of Raynaud theory for qcqs uniform analytic adic spaces satisfying no Noetherian or finite-type conditions, such as perfectoid spaces. The aim of this paper is to produce such a theory.

1.2
Main theorems

Our set-up is as follows. We fix a complete adic ring
R
whose ring of definition is generated by a single non-zero-divisor and non-unit
\varpi

R[\varpi^{-1}]

(R,\varpi)

(where
\overline{R}

X\to S

X\to S
over
{\rm Spf}(R)

(Definition

\mathfrak{U}={\rm Spf}(A)

\varpi
.

(2)

An adic formal
R

\mathfrak{X}
is called locally rig-sheafy if for every affine open subset
\mathfrak{U}={\rm Spf}(A)

(3)

1.3
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.5

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
mostly follows the proof of Raynaud’s classical theorem by Bosch and Lütkebohmert, see
[
13
]
, proof of Theorem 4.1, replacing admissible formal blow-ups by normalized formal blow-ups. Thus, the proof consists in successively showing the following three statements:

•

(Any morphism of formal models is determined by its generic fiber) Any two morphisms
f_{0}
,
g_{0}
of
\varpi
-torsion-free, qcqs, locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-schemes with
f_{0\eta}=g_{0\eta}
must be equal: This is Lemma
6.1
and is proved using the specialization map from the generic fiber, as in the classical case.

•

(Formal models of morphisms) For any two adic formal
R
-schemes
\mathfrak{Z}
,
\mathfrak{X}
as in the theorem and any morphism

f:\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

between their generic fibers, there exists a normalized formal blow-up
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{Z}
and a morphism
f_{0}:\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
which is a formal
R
-model of
f
and is an isomorphism if
f
is an isomorphism: This is Lemma
6.2
; it is at this step that the use of normalized formal blow-ups instead of usual admissible formal blow-ups becomes necessary.

•

(Existence of formal
R
-models for adic spaces) Every qcqs uniform adic space
X
over
S
has a formal
R
-model
\mathfrak{X}
, which is locally stably uniform and integrally closed in its generic fiber
X
: This appears as Theorem

. The proof of this existence statement is analogous to the classical one; it uses induction on the size of an affinoid open cover (the affinoid case being obvious) and a gluing argument which relies on Lemma
6.2
. We note that this part of the theorem also follows from
[
3
]
, Corollary 1.2.7; however, op. cit. does not explicitly spell out the birational gluing argument.

Locally rig-sheafy formal schemes over an adic ring

Recall from loc. cit., Ch. I, Definition 1.1.16, that a formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is said to be adic (respectively, adic of finite ideal type) if it admits an open cover by affine formal schemes of the form
{\rm Spf}(A)
such that
A
is a complete adic ring (respectively, a complete adic ring with finitely generated ideal of definition). The following special case of this notion is the most important to us.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with finitely generated ideal of definition. An adic formal
R
-scheme is a formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
together with an adic morphism
\mathfrak{X}\to{\rm Spf}(R)
.

If
\mathfrak{X}
is an adic formal scheme of finite ideal type, we denote by
{\rm Aff}_{\mathfrak{X}}
the family of all affine open subsets of
\mathfrak{X}
of the form
{\rm Spf}(A)
for
A
a complete adic ring with a finitely generated ideal of definition. By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Corollary 3.7.13,
{\rm Aff}_{\mathfrak{X}}
coincides with the family of all affine open subsets of
\mathfrak{X}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of definition of finite type on an adic formal scheme of finite ideal type
\mathfrak{X}
. For every inclusion
\mathfrak{V}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}
with
\mathfrak{U},\mathfrak{V}\in{\rm Aff}_{\mathfrak{X}}
we have

\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{U})\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{V})=\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{V}).

Proof.

By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Proposition 1.1.20 and Proposition 1.1.22, the ideal of definition of finite type
\mathcal{I}|_{\mathfrak{U}}
of
\mathfrak{U}
is given by
\mathcal{I}|_{\mathfrak{U}}=\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{U})\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{U}}
. The assertion follows.∎

Recall from
[
33
]
, §4, that a sufficient condition for an adic formal scheme to define an associated adic space is that the formal scheme be locally Noetherian. This yields a class of examples for the following class of formal schemes.

Definition 2.3
.

We call an adic formal scheme
locally sheafy
if it is of finite ideal type and has an open cover by formal spectra of sheafy complete adic rings (with finitely generated ideals of definition).

More recently, Zavyalov proved a sheafiness result which incorporates both the case of Noetherian complete adic rings and the case of adic rings topologically of finite type over a not necessarily discrete valuation ring of rank
1
.

Definition 2.4

(Fujiwara-Kato
[
24
]
, Ch. 0, Def. 8.4.3, and Ch. I, Def. 2.1.7; see also Zavyalov
[
53
]
, Definition 2.8)
.

Let

be a complete adic ring which has a finitely generated ideal of definition

. Then

(or the pair
(A_{0},I)
) is said to be topologically universally rigid-Noetherian (t. u. rigid-Noetherian) if, for every integer

{\rm Spec}(A\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle)\setminus\mathcal{V}(IA\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle)

\mathfrak{X}
is called locally universally rigid-Noetherian if it has an open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i}
by affine formal schemes
\mathfrak{U}_{i}={\rm Spf}(A_{i})
, where each

Definition 2.5

(
[

.

A complete Huber ring

Theorem 2.6

(Zavyalov
[
53

Every strongly rigid-Noetherian complete Huber pair

In particular, every locally universally rigid-Noetherian adic formal scheme of finite ideal type is locally sheafy.

The category of locally sheafy adic formal schemes embeds fully and faithfully into the category of adic spaces (the construction of
[

\mathfrak{X}
is merely locally sheafy instead of locally Noetherian). For a locally sheafy formal scheme

the canonical morphism of locally topologically ringed spaces.

Our first order of business is to introduce a notion of analytic generic fiber for a reasonably broad class of adic formal schemes over
R
. Since we want the analytic generic fiber to be an adic space, the relevant class of formal schemes is specified by the following definition.

Definition 2.7

(Rig-sheafy complete adic ring)
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring which has an ideal of definition generated by a single element
\varpi
. We call a complete adic ring
A
with a continuous (but not necessarily adic) ring map
R\to A

rig-sheafy
over the pair
(R,\varpi)
(or rig-sheafy over
R
, or just rig-sheafy, if
\varpi
or
(R,\varpi)
is understood from the context) if it has a finitely generated ideal of definition
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}
containing (the image of)
\varpi
such that the Huber rings
B_{n,A}[\varpi^{-1}]
with

B_{n,A}=A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle,

are sheafy for all
n\geq 1
. Here
B_{n,A}=A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle
is the completion, for the
IA\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{\varpi}\rangle

A[\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}]

of the localization
A[\varpi^{-1}]
.

In the above definition we view the zero ring as a sheafy Huber ring (giving rise to the empty adic space), so an
R
-algebra
A
which is
\varpi
-torsion is necessarily rig-sheafy. Note that if
A
is not
\varpi
-torsion-free, the canonical map
A\to A[\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}]
is not injective, for any

and

\varpi
is contained in

can always be assumed to hold up to replacing
\varpi
with a power of itself. However, the following lemma shows that there always exists a finitely generated ideal of definition of
A
which contains
\varpi

\varpi
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R\to A
be a continuous homomorphism of complete adic rings, where
R
has a principal ideal of definition and
A
has a finitely generated ideal of definition. Then for every
\varpi
generating an ideal of definition of
R
and for every finitely generated ideal of definition
I
of
A
, the ideal
J=(I,\varpi)
is an ideal of definition of
A
.

Proof.

Since
J
contains
I
by definition, we only have to prove that
I
contains some power of
J
. By continuity of
R\to A
, there exists an integer
n\geq 1
such that
\varpi^{n}\in A
. Suppose that
I
is generated by
r

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
. Then
J^{(r+1)n}\subseteq(f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n},\varpi^{n})_{A}\subseteq I
.∎

The following elementary lemma provides a link between Definition
2.7
and birational geometry.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varpi
be a non-zero-divisor in a ring
A
and let
f_{1},\dots,f_{r},g\in A
be elements generating the unit ideal in
A[\varpi^{-1}]
. The subring
A[\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{g}]
of
A[\varpi^{-1}]
coincides with the affine blow-up algebra
A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]
, cf.
[
22
]
, Tag 052P.

Proof.

Both rings in question are subrings of
A[\varpi^{-1}]
. Let
f\in A[\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{g}]
. Then we can write
f
as a sum of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(\frac{f_{i}}{g})^{m_{i}}

for some coefficients
a_{i}\in A
and exponents
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. By definition of the affine blow-up algebra
A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]
, each
(\frac{f_{i}}{g})^{m_{i}}
belongs to
A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]
, since
f_{i}^{m_{i}}\in(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}^{m_{i}}
. Since
A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]
is an
A
-algebra,
f\in A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]
. This shows that

A[\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{g}]\subseteq A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}].

Conversely, let

\frac{h}{g^{m}}\in A[\frac{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}}{g}]

be an arbitrary element of the affine blow-up algebra, with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
h\in(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{A}^{m}
. Let
h_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{ij}f_{i}
(

Remark 2.17
.

In the following we endow each formal scheme

with the structure of a locally v-ringed space by equipping each stalk

,

, with the valuation

which takes the value

on the maximal ideal and takes the value

elsewhere. With this structure, every morphism of formal schemes

becomes a morphism of locally v-ringed spaces since the homomorphisms

are local ring homomorphisms. This exhibits the category of formal schemes as a full subcategory of the category of locally v-ringed spaces.

3
Generic fiber and specialization map

Following the classical idea of Berthelot (
[

]
, §0.2.6), we can now define the adic analytic generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
of a locally rig-sheafy adic formal scheme over

\mathfrak{X}
is affine and rig-sheafy. For

and
\varpi
as before and for

a rig-sheafy affine formal scheme over

, where

containing

(we use Lemma

to ensure that such an ideal of definition exists), we again consider the completed affine blow-up algebras

Recall from Remark

This shows that the topology on

Example 3.2

(Open unit disk and perfectoid open unit disk)

One of the most basic examples of the generic fiber construction is the open unit disk over a nonarchimedean field

.

(\widehat{\mathfrak{X}}_{Z})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\tilde{\rightarrow}{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}^{-1}(Z).

Proof.

Since
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{V})=\mathfrak{V}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

is affine and rig-sheafy. Let

, let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,sn}=\{\,x\in{\rm Spa}(A,A)\mid|g_{j}^{n}(x)|\leq|\varpi(x)|\neq 0\ \text{for}\ j=1,\dots,s\,\}

∎

With the notion of adic analytic generic fiber in hand, we can define what we mean by a formal model over
R
(respectively, a formal

-model) of an adic space

over

is a complete adic ring with a non-zero-divisor

such that

is a sheafy Tate ring.

Definition 3.15

(Formal models of an adic space)
.

Fix a ring
R
which is
\varpi
-adically complete and
\varpi
-torsion-free for some
\varpi\in R
and let
\overline{R}
be the integral closure of
R
in the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
. Suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. For an adic space
X
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
, a formal model of
X
over
R
(respectively, a formal
R
-model of
X
) is a locally rig-sheafy adic formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
over
(R,\varpi)
(respectively, a locally rig-sheafy adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
) such that
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta,(R,\varpi)}^{{\rm ad}}\cong X
. For a morphism of adic spaces
f:X^{\prime}\to X
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
, a formal model of
f
over
R
(respectively, a formal
R
-model of
f
) is a morphism of locally rig-sheafy formal adic formal schemes over
(R,\varpi)
(respectively, of locally rig-sheafy adic formal
R
-schemes)
f_{0}:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
with
f_{0\eta}=f
.

In particular, we obtain a notion of formal models over
\mathbb{Z}[[T]]
(respectively, of formal
\mathbb{Z}[[T]]
-models) for every adic space
X
equipped with a global pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi
, which we call a Tate adic space, viewed as an adic space over
{\rm Spa}(\mathbb{Z}((T)),\mathbb{Z}[[T]])
as in Remark
3.17
below.

Definition 3.16

(Tate adic spaces)
.

A global pseudo-uniformizer of an adic space
X
is an element
\varpi_{X}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(X)
such that for any affinoid open subspace
U
of
X
the image of
\varpi_{X}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
is a topologically nilpotent unit of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
. A Tate adic space is a pair
(X,\varpi_{X})
, where
X
is a (necessarily analytic) adic space and
\varpi_{X}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(X)
is a global pseudo-uniformizer of
X
. A Tate adic space
(X,\varpi_{X})
is said to be locally Noetherian (respectively, uniform, respectively, perfectoid, respectively, sousperfectoid, respectively, diamantine) if the underlying adic space
X
has this property.

A morphism
f:(Y,\varpi_{Y})\to(X,\varpi_{X})
of Tate adic spaces is a morphism of adic spaces
f:Y\to X
such that the image of
\varpi_{X}
under the map
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(X)\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{+}(Y)
is equal to
\varpi_{Y}
.

Remark 3.17

(Tate adic spaces as relative adic spaces)
.

For every sheafy Tate Huber pair
(A,A^{+})
with pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi\in A^{+}
of
A
the assignment
X\mapsto(X,\varpi)
defines a fully faithful functor from the category of adic spaces over
S={\rm Spa}(A,A^{+})
to the category of Tate adic spaces. In particular, the category of rigid-analytic varieties over any nonarchimedean field
K
embeds fully and faithfully into the category of Tate adic spaces. Conversely, the category of Tate adic spaces is the same as the category of adic spaces over the affinoid adic space

S={\rm Spa}(\mathbb{Z}((T)),\mathbb{Z}[[T]]),

where
\mathbb{Z}[[T]]
is endowed with the
T
-adic topology, where
\mathbb{Z}((T))=\mathbb{Z}[[T]][T^{-1}]
and where for a Tate adic space
(X,\varpi)
and
U\subseteq X
affinoid open the morphism of Huber pairs

(\mathbb{Z}((T)),\mathbb{Z}[[T]])\to(\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U))

is the map sending the variable
T
to
\varpi_{X}
.

The above remark allows us to talk about formal
\mathbb{Z}[[T]]
-models of any Tate adic space
X
and thus gives us a notion of ”absolute” formal models for Tate adic spaces.

Recall the notion of an admissible formal blow-up of an adic formal scheme

and if

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\ast)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=\varinjlim_{k\geq 0}{\rm Proj}(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{J}^{n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{k}}) }

Definition 4.1

(Fujiwara-Kato
[

Admissible formal blow-ups also satisfy a universal property similar to (and derived from) the universal property of the usual, scheme-theoretic blow-ups, see
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 1.1.4(3). This universal property can sometimes be used as a replacement for the valuative criterion of properness in the setting of formal schemes. The following definition is inspired by the formulation of
[
6
]
, Theorem 8.1.2.

Definition 4.6

(Specialization to an admissible formal blow-up)
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi
and suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be an adic formal scheme over
R
which is locally rig-sheafy over
(R,\varpi)
, with adic analytic generic fiber
X=\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
over
(R,\varpi)
, and let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
be the admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}
along an admissible ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}
. We define a map

{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}:|X|\to|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}|

as follows.

For
x\in X
choose a rig-sheafy affine open neighbourhood
\mathfrak{U}={\rm Spf}(A)
of the point
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}(x)
in
\mathfrak{X}
. Then the morphism of adic spaces
{\rm Spa}(k(x),k(x)^{+})\to X
determined by

factors through
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U})=\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
and hence factors through an affinoid open subspace of the form

{\rm Spa}(A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}],A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]^{+})

for some elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
generating an ideal of definition of
A
containing
\varpi
and some integer

. The restriction to
A
of the continuous homomorphism

A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]^{+}\to k(x)^{+}

then defines a morphism of formal schemes
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}
(which factors through
\mathfrak{U}
) and such that
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}(x)
is the image under this morphism of the closed point of
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})
.

It is readily seen that the morphism
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}
constructed in this way does not depend on the chosen rig-sheafy affine open neighbourhood
\mathfrak{U}
of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}(x)
. Since the ideal sheaf of finite type
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{O}_{{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})}
on

is invertible, this morphism
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}
lifts uniquely to a morphism of formal schemes

{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},

by the universal property of admissible formal blow-ups (
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Prop. 1.1.4(3)). We then define

{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}

to be the image of the closed point of
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})
under this morphism
{\rm Spf}(k(x)^{+})\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
.

Similarly to the case
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{X}
, we have an explicit description of inverse images of rig-sheafy affine open subsets under the map

.

Lemma 4.7
.

For a locally rig-sheafy adic formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
over
(R,\varpi)
with generic fiber

and an admissible formal blow-up

, the pre-image

of any rig-sheafy affine open subset

coincides with the open subspace

. Moreover,

The definition of an admissible formal blow-up is of local nature, so, for every affine open

, the restricted morphism
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
, where
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
is the pre-image of

in

, is an admissible formal blow-up. On the other hand, the definition of the (adic analytic) generic fiber of

over

is also local in nature. Hence, to prove the equality

for every rig-sheafy affine open subset

\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A)
is itself a rig-sheafy affine formal scheme.

Let

be elements generating an ideal of definition of

as in the definition of a rig-sheafy adic ring over
(R,\varpi)
. Let
\mathfrak{V}={\rm Spf}(B)
be a rig-sheafy affine open subset of the admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. By Lemma

,
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{B}
is an ideal of definition of

(by abuse of notation, we identify
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
with their images in

). Let

and fix some

such that

The open subspace

of

is given by the increasing union of open affinoid subspaces

If

, then, since

, the point

A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]^{+}\to k(x)^{+}

factors through the canonical map

In particular, the restriction of
A\langle\frac{f_{1}^{n},\dots,f_{r}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]^{+}\to k(x)^{+}
to

factors through the continuous map

Conversely, fix

As is the case in classical rigid geometry of Tate-Raynaud, one of the useful features of admissible formal blow-ups is that they provide a way to build new formal models of an analytic space from old ones.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi
and suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a locally rig-sheafy adic formal scheme over
R
. If
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
is an admissible formal blow-up, the induced morphism
{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
of adic spaces over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this when
\mathfrak{X}
is affine and rig-sheafy. Write
\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A)
, where
A
is a rig-sheafy complete adic ring over
R
. By Lemma
4.3
,
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
has an affine open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,r}
where
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
is the formal spectrum of the completed affine blow-up algebra
A\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{f_{i}}\rangle
for some family of elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in A
generating an open ideal of the adic ring
A
. Let
I
be a finitely generated ideal of definition of
A
and assume that
I
contains (the image of)
\varpi
. Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
be generators of
I
. Note that, for every
i=1,\dots,r
and every
n\geq 1
, the Tate ring

A\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{f_{i}}\rangle\langle\frac{g_{1}^{n},\dots,g_{s}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]

is equal to the Tate ring

A\langle\frac{g_{1}^{n},\dots,g_{s}^{n}}{\varpi}\rangle\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{f_{i}}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}].

Indeed, we can use the assumption that
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
generate an open ideal of
A
to see that
f_{i}
is invertible in both of these Tate rings, then conclude that both Tate rings satisfy the universal property of the rational localization corresponding to the rational subset

\{\,x\in X\mid|f_{j}(x)|\leq|f_{i}(x)|\neq 0,\ |g_{k}^{n}(x)|\leq\varpi(x)|\neq 0\ \text{for}\ j=1,\dots,r,k=1,\dots,s\,\}

of
X
. In particular, we see that the adic rings
A\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}}{f_{i}}\rangle
, for
i=1,\dots,r
, are rig-sheafy.

The pre-images
{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})
form an open cover of
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
. Cover each intersection
\mathfrak{U}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{U}_{j}
by rig-sheafy affine open subsets
\mathfrak{V}_{ijk}
. Using Lemma
4.7
and the result of the previous paragraph, we have

{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})=(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

and

{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{V}_{ijk})=(\mathfrak{V}_{ijk})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

for all
i,j,k
. Hence the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
of
\mathfrak{X}
can be described as the coequalizer (in the category of adic spaces) of the morphisms

\coprod_{i,j,k}(\mathfrak{V}_{ijk})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\rightrightarrows\coprod_{i}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

induced by the canonical morphisms

\coprod_{i,j,k}\mathfrak{V}_{ijk}\rightrightarrows\coprod_{i}\mathfrak{U}_{i}.

But the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}{\rm ad}}
of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
has the same description as a coequalizer in the category of adic spaces.∎

.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with an ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi\in R
and suppose that the Tate ring

, the above canonical morphism is an isomorphism of locally v-ringed spaces

In particular, for every

, the specialization map

If

, then both the source and the target of

are empty (indeed, in this case the admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}

By Lemma

\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathfrak{X}
extends to an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
. By combining Lemma
4.7
and Lemma

{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}|_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}={\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}|_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}{\rm ad}}}={\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}}={\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}}.

\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
, we have a commutative diagram of morphisms of locally v-ringed spaces
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces (\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{U})}(\mathfrak{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}}

Consequently, it suffices to treat the case when

is rig-sheafy and affine.

In this situation the adic analytic generic fiber

of

has the form

, where, as usual, the bar denotes integral closure inside the Tate ring
A[\varpi^{-1}]
of the image of

and consider the local ring

This ring is non-zero by the assumption that the generic fiber

4.8
. Note that this local ring is

-torsion-free. Indeed, for any
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
there exists an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
is
\varpi
-torsion-free: Just let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be the admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
in the admissible ideal
\varpi\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}
of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Note also that
\varpi
is not a unit in the above local ring, since, if it was, then
\varpi
would also be a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})
for some
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
and some
\mathfrak{U}\in{\rm Aff}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}
, which is impossible due to
\varpi
-adic completeness.

Following an argument of Bhatt (
[
7
]
, Proposition 8.1.3, and its proof), we first prove that the
\varpi
-adic Hausdorff quotient

\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}=A_{\underline{x}}/\bigcap_{n}\varpi^{n}A_{\underline{x}}

is a valuation ring (note that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is not the zero ring since otherwise
\varpi
would be a unit in
A_{\underline{x}}
). Since valuation rings are the same as local Bézout domains, we split the proof of this assertion into two steps: Showing that all finitely generated ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
are principal (i.e., that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is a Bézout ring), and showing that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is an integral domain.

To see that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is Bézout, let
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
be arbitrary non-zero elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
be elements of
A_{\underline{x}}
lifting
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
. Then there exists an integer
n\geq 1
such that

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\not\in\varpi^{n}A_{\underline{x}}.

We can choose an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
and an affine open neighbourhood
\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that (by the usual abuse of notation)
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})
. Consider the open ideal

J_{n}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n})_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})}.

Let
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
be the admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{U}
in the admissible ideal sheaf defined by
J_{n}
. By Lemma
4.5
we can extend
J_{n}
to an admissible ideal sheaf on
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
and the corresponding admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
extends
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
. Consider
f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n}
as elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime})
. By
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 1.1.8,
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime}
is precisely the pre-image of
\mathfrak{U}
under
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. In particular, the lift
x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}}
of
x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}
lies in
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime}
. By Lemma
4.3
, the formal scheme
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime\prime}
has an affine open cover
({\rm Spf}(B_{i}))_{i=0,\dots,r}
by formal spectra of the completed affine blow-up algebras

B_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n}}{f_{i}}\rangle,\ i=1,\dots,r,

and

B_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})\langle\frac{f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n}}{\varpi^{n}}\rangle.

Choose an index
i
such that

x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}}\in{\rm Spf}(B_{i}).

If
i=0
, then
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n})_{B_{i}}=(\varpi^{n})_{B_{i}}
and, a fortiori,
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in\varpi^{n}A_{\underline{x}}
, a contradiction. Hence
i\geq 1
and
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r},\varpi^{n})_{B_{i}}=(f_{i})_{B_{i}}
for this
i
. In particular,
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})_{B_{i}}=(f_{i})_{B_{i}}
for this
i
(for later use, we remark that in this case
f_{i}
is not a zero-divisor in
A_{\underline{x}}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X^{\prime}}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}}
, since
\varpi^{n}\in(f_{i})_{B_{i}}
and
A_{\underline{x}}
is
\varpi
-torsion-free). We conclude that the ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
generated by the images
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
of
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
is a principal ideal; since
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
were arbitrary, this proves that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is a Bézout ring.

On the other hand, we see that in the situation of the above paragraph
\varpi^{n}
belongs to the ideal of
B_{i}
generated by
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
and hence that
\varpi^{n}
belongs to the ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
generated by
\overline{f_{1}},\dots,\overline{f_{r}}
. In particular, if
\overline{f}
is an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
such that
\overline{f}\not\in\varpi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
, then

\varpi^{n}\in(\overline{f})_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}}.

Thus for every
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
, we can find an integer
n\geq 1
with
\varpi^{n}\in(\overline{f})_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}}
. This allows us to prove that
\underline{\mathcal{O}}_{\underline{x}}
is an integral domain as follows. Suppose that
\overline{f}
and
\overline{g}
are non-zero elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
whose product is zero. Choosing integers
n,m\geq 1
such that
\varpi^{n}\in(\overline{f})_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}}
and
\varpi^{m}\in(\overline{g})_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}}
, we see that
\varpi^{n+m}\in(\overline{f}\overline{g})_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}}=0
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is non-zero, this implies that the local ring
A_{\underline{x}}
has
\varpi
-torsion, in contradiction to what we have seen above.

Thus we have proved that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is a valuation ring, and the above argument also showed that every non-zero element of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
divides some power
\varpi^{n}
of
\varpi
. Therefore, the Tate ring
K_{\underline{x}}=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is equal to the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
determines a valuation on the field
K_{\underline{x}}
. Note also that we have a canonical continuous map of Tate rings
A[\varpi^{-1}]\to K_{\underline{x}}
induced by the canonical map

A\to\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}.

Hence we can define an element
v_{\underline{x}}\in X={\rm Spa}(A[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{A})
from
\underline{x}
by letting
v_{\underline{x}}
be the pullback along
A[\varpi^{-1}]\to K_{\underline{x}}
of the valuation determined by the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
.

We want to show that the maps
{\rm sp}_{X}
and
\underline{x}\mapsto v_{\underline{x}}
are inverse to each other. To this end, let
\underline{x}=(x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}\in\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}|
be arbitrary. Since, by definition of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
, for every admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is a local homomorphism of local rings and since the maximal ideal of the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is the subset where the valuation is
<1
, the image
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(v_{\underline{x}})
of
v_{\underline{x}}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
is precisely
x_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}
. To prove the converse, let
x\in X
and consider the corresponding images
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)
for
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
. Note that the local ring
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
can be written as

A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\varinjlim_{{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{U}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U}),

where the second inductive limit is taken over quasi-compact open neighbourhoods
\mathfrak{U}
of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. On the other hand, using Lemma
4.7
and Lemma
4.11
, we see that for every quasi-compact open neighbourhood
U
of
x
in
X
there exists an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
and a quasi-compact open neighbourhood
\mathfrak{U}
of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)
such that
U={\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U})=\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
. In particular,

\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{U}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}(\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{U}}(\mathfrak{U})[\varpi^{-1}]=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})[\varpi^{-1}].

Conversely, for every quasi-compact open neighbourhood
\mathfrak{U}
of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
the pre-image
\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
of
\mathfrak{U}
in
X
is a quasi-compact open neighbourhood of
x
in
X
. Therefore,

A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}[\varpi^{-1}]=(\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\varinjlim_{{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{U}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U}))[\varpi^{-1}]=\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}.

In this way we see that the canonical surjective ring map
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}\to\mathcal{O}_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
induces a surjective ring map
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\to K_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
, so the field
K_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
is nothing else but the residue field
\kappa(x)
of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
of
X
in
x
. By the same argument, we have

\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\varinjlim_{{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{U}}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\varinjlim_{{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{U}}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(\mathfrak{U}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}})=\varinjlim_{x\in U}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}.

We obtain an integral ring map

A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\varinjlim_{{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mathfrak{U}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{U})\to\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}

which induces an isomorphism upon inverting
\varpi
, and this integral map is injective since its source is
\varpi
-torsion-free.

We claim that
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
is actually integrally closed in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}=A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}[\varpi^{-1}]
and thus

A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}

as subrings of
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. For this we proceed as in the proof of
[
24
]
, Ch. 0, Proposition 8.7.5(2). First observe that, while proving that
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{x}}
is a Bézout ring, we actually showed that the stalk

A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=(\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}})_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)}

is a
\varpi
-valuative local ring in the sense of Fujiwara and Kato (
[
24
]
, Ch. 0, Def. 8.7.1), i.e., every finitely generated ideal of
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
which contains a power of
\varpi
is generated by a single non-zero-divisor (the same assertion was proved, in slightly different language, by Fujiwara and Kato, see
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 3.2.6). Let
c\in A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
be an element such that
t=\frac{c}{\varpi^{m}}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is integral over
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Consider an equation of integral dependence

f(t)=t^{n}+a_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n-1}t+a_{n}=0

of
t
over
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
. Since
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
is a
\varpi
-valuative local ring, the ideal
(\varpi^{m},c)_{A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}}
is generated by a single non-zero-divisor
d\in A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
. Since
d
is a non-zero-divisor, there exists unique elements
\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d}
,
\frac{c}{d}
of
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
such that
d\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d}=\varpi^{m}
and
d\frac{c}{d}=c
. Then

(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d},\frac{c}{d})_{A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}}=(1).

We claim that
\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d}
is invertible in
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
, in which case, viewing
t
as an element of the localization
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}[\frac{1}{d}]
,

t=\frac{c/d}{\varpi^{m}/d}=(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d})^{-1}\frac{c}{d}\in A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}

and we are done. By way of contradiction, assume that
\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d}
belongs to the maximal ideal of
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
. Then the relation
1\in(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d},\frac{c}{d})_{A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}}
entails that
\frac{c}{d}
is a unit in
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
. But, on the other hand,

(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d})^{n}f(\frac{c/d}{\varpi^{m}/d})=(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d})^{n}f(t)=0,

so
\frac{c^{n}}{d^{n}}\in(\frac{\varpi^{m}}{d})_{A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}}
and thus
\frac{c}{d}
cannot be a unit, a contradiction. This concludes the proof of the claim that
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}
.

Now,
\mathcal{O}_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}
is equal to the image of
A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
inside the residue field
K_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\kappa(x)
of
X
in
x
. By
[
21
]
, Proposition 14.3.1, this image is exactly the valuation ring
\kappa(x)^{+}
. Putting everything together we obtain

\mathcal{O}_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\kappa(x)^{+}.

Consequently,
v_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=x
as valuations on
A[\varpi^{-1}]
. This proves that the maps
{\rm sp}_{X}
and
\underline{x}\mapsto v_{\underline{x}}
are indeed inverse to each other.

We now prove that the map
{\rm sp}_{X}
is generalizing. Given
x\in X
let
\underline{y}
be a generization of
{\rm sp}_{X}(x)
in
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}|
. Since we have shown that
{\rm sp}_{X}
is bijective, there exists
y\in X
such that

\underline{y}={\rm sp}_{X}(y).

Suppose that
U
is a quasi-compact open subset of
X
which contains
x
. By Lemma
4.11
we find an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
and an open subset
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that
U
is the pre-image of
\mathfrak{U}
in
X
. Since
U
contains
x
, the open subset
\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
contains
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)
and the pre-image of
\mathfrak{U}
in
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}|
contains
{\rm sp}_{X}(x)
. Since
{\rm sp}_{X}(y)
is a generization of
{\rm sp}_{X}(x)
, this pre-image also contains
{\rm sp}_{X}(y)
. In particular,
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(y)\in\mathfrak{U}
and, consequently,
y\in U
. This shows that
{\rm sp}_{X}
is a generalizing map. Since transition morphisms in
{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
are morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated formal schemes, the underlying continuous maps of topological spaces are spectral maps between spectral spaces. Hence the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}|\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}|
is a spectral space, by
[
22
]
, Tag 0A2Z. On the other hand, by
[
22
]
, Tag 09XU, any continuous and generalizing bijective map between spectral spaces is a homeomorphism. It follows that the map
{\rm sp}_{X}
is a homeomorphism on the underlying topological spaces.

It only remains to prove that the morphism of sheaves of rings on
X

{\rm sp}_{X}^{\ast}(\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}

induced by the map
{\rm sp}_{X}
is an isomorphism. For this it suffices to prove that the morphism of sheaves induces isomorphisms on stalks. But we have already seen that, for every
x\in X
, the stalk

(\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}})_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}=\varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime},{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(x)}=A_{{\rm sp}_{X}(x)}

is equal to
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}
.∎

Remark 4.15
.

In the work
[
24
]
of Fujiwara and Kato coherent (formal) rigid spaces are defined as objects of the category of qcqs adic formal schemes of finite ideal type localized by admissible formal blow-ups. In particular, every adic formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
4.13
gives rise to a coherent rigid space
\mathscr{X}
. The locally ringed space

\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}

appearing on the right-hand side of the isomorphism in Theorem
4.13
is studied in Ch. II.3 of loc. cit. and is denoted there by

, where
\mathscr{X}
is the coherent (formal) rigid space defined by
\mathfrak{X}
and where the topological space
\langle\mathscr{X}\rangle
is called the Zariski-Riemann space of the rigid space
\mathscr{X}
. The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}^{\textrm{int}}
is called the integral structure sheaf (
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 3.2.15). Note that a key part of the proof of Theorem
4.13
was to show (following an idea of Bhatt from
[
6
]
) that, for every
\underline{x}\in\langle\mathscr{X}\rangle
, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X},\underline{x}}^{\textrm{int}}
is a
\varpi
-valuative ring in the sense of
[
24
]
, Ch. 0, Definition 8.7.1. This is also proved by Fujiwara and Kato, for any coherent rigid space
\mathscr{X}
, in
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 3.2.6.

Fujiwara and Kato also define a so-called rigid structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}
on the Zariski-Riemann space
\langle\mathscr{X}\rangle
which in our case is just given by

, i.e., it corresponds via the isomorphism in Theorem
4.13
to the usual structure sheaf of the adic space
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
. Fujiwara and Kato call the triple

the Zariski-Riemann triple associated with the coherent rigid space

. When the rigid space
\mathscr{X}
is locally universally Noetherian (a condition analogous to our locally Noetherian condition for adic spaces), it was already observed in Appendix A to loc. cit., Ch. II, that the pair

is an adic space (see
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Theorem A.5.1). Theorem
4.13
can be thought of as extending this result to coherent rigid spaces
\mathscr{X}
which arise from a locally rig-sheafy qcqs adic formal
R
-scheme (those qcqs adic formal
R
-schemes whose associated rigid spaces satisfy the locally universally Noetherian assumption of Fujiwara and Kato are automatically locally rig-sheafy, by the result of Zavyalov discussed at the beginning of Section 7). As another consequence of Theorem
4.13
, our specialization map
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}
coincides with the specialization map
{\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{X}}:\langle\mathscr{X}\rangle\to\mathfrak{X}
on the Zariski-Riemann space
\langle\mathscr{X}\rangle
which is defined in
[
24
]
, Ch. II, 3.1(a). This means, in particular, that the topological results of
[
24
]
, Ch. II.4, apply to the underlying topological space of any adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
which admits a qcqs formal
R
-model.

We record the following immediate consequence of Theorem
4.13
.

Corollary 4.16
.

Fix a complete adic ring
R
with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi
and suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. For any locally rig-sheafy, quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
and any
\varpi
-torsion-free admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
, the specialization map
{\rm sp}_{X_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is surjective.

Proof.

Note that
\varpi
-torsion-free formal schemes in
{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
form a cofinal system in the cofiltered category
{\rm Bl}(\mathfrak{X})
. In view of Theorem
4.13
, it suffices to prove that every admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of a locally rig-sheafy, quasi-compact quasi-separated,
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is surjective. For this we may assume that
\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A)
for some rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free and
\varpi
-adically complete
R
-algebra
A
. In this case the admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
in a finitely generated open ideal
J
of
A
is the completion of the scheme-theoretic blow-up
\pi_{J}:X^{\prime}\to{\rm Spec}(A)
of
{\rm Spec}(A)
in
J
. The blow-up
\pi_{J}
restricts to an isomorphism of schemes
X^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{V}(J\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})\tilde{\to}{\rm Spec}(A)\setminus\mathcal{V}(J)
. In particular,
\pi_{J}
contains the basic open subset
{\rm Spec}(A[\varpi^{-1}])={\rm Spec}(A)\setminus\mathcal{V}(\varpi)
in its image. Since
A
is
\varpi
-torsion-free,
{\rm Spec}(A[\varpi^{-1}])
is dense in
{\rm Spec}(A)
, so
\pi_{J}
has dense image. Since
\pi_{J}
is proper, this means that
\pi_{J}
is surjective. But then also the base change
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}_{0}=X^{\prime}\times_{{\rm Spec}(A)}{\rm Spec}(A/(\varpi))\to{\rm Spec}(A/(\varpi))=\mathfrak{X}_{0}
is surjective, so
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
is surjective.∎

Remark 4.17
.

The above corollary is not essentially new, with variants and special cases scattered across the literature on nonarchimedean analytic geometry. In the special case
\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A^{\circ})
, where
A
is an affinoid algebra in the sense of Tate over a nonarchimedean field
K
, it was already proved by Tate in
[
51
]
, Theorem 6.4, that the restriction of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}
to the set of rigid points
{\rm Sp}(A)
of
{\rm Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
contains every closed point of
{\rm Spf}(A^{\circ})
in its image. In
[
4
]
, Proposition 2.4.4, Berkovich proved that in this case also every generic point of
\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A^{\circ})
is in the image of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}
(more precisely, is the image under
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}
of a rank
1
point of
X
). For
X
a not necessarily affinoid rigid space over
K
and
\mathfrak{X}
a flat formal
K^{\circ}
-model topologically of finite type (equivalently, topologically of finite presentation), surjectivity of
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}
onto the set of closed points of
\mathfrak{X}
appears as Proposition 3.5 in
[
13
]
and as Proposition 1.1.5 in Berthelot’s work
[
5
]
. Finally, when
X={\rm Spa}(A,A^{+})
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}(A^{+})
for some Tate Huber pair
(A,A^{+})
the corollary is an immediate consequence of
[
6
]
, Theorem 8.1.2 (of which our Theorem
4.13
is a global variant), as was observed, for example, in
[
25
]
, Proposition 4.2, and in Proposition 2.13.6 of the lecture notes
[
23
]
of Fargues. The general statement of Corollary
4.16
can be deduced from this affinoid case by using Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.7
to reduce to the rig-sheafy, affine case and then using the fact that
{\rm Spf}(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})})\to{\rm Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X}))
is surjective and closed, since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})}
is integral.

Combining Corollary
4.16
with Lemma
4.11
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi
and suppose that
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is a sheafy Tate ring. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a locally rig-sheafy quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal

-scheme and let
(U_{i})_{i}
be a finite open cover of

by quasi-compact open subsets. Then there exists a
\varpi
-torsion-free admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
and a finite open cover

of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
by quasi-compact open formal subschemes such that
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})=U_{i}
for all

. If all

are affinoid, the

can be chosen to be affine and rig-sheafy.

Remark 4.19
.

We observe that the above corollary (along with Lemma
4.3
) also yields a new proof of
[
36
]
, Lemma 2.4.19, for sheafy complete Tate rings.

Let us also record a few other topological consequences of Theorem
4.13
.

Corollary 4.20
.

For every quasi-compact quasi-separated locally rig-sheafy adic formal

-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
with adic analytic generic fiber

over

the specialization map

is closed.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem

and the fact that admissible formal blow-ups are proper and, in particular, closed. ∎

Corollary 4.21
.

Let

be a quasi-compact quasi-separated locally rig-sheafy adic formal

over

. If

Proof.

If

. ∎

5
Normalized formal blow-ups

In this section we introduce and study the notion of normalized formal blow-ups, which is necessary for the construction of formal
R
-models of general uniform qcqs adic spaces over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
. We begin by recalling the following special case of
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Def. 3.1.3.

Definition 5.1

(Fujiwara-Kato)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be an adic formal scheme of finite ideal type and let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal of definition of finite type on
\mathfrak{X}
. A sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules
\mathcal{F}
is called
adically quasi-coherent
if it is complete, i.e.

\mathcal{F}=\varprojlim_{k}\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{I}^{k}\mathcal{F},

and if the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathcal{I}^{k+1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{I}^{k+1}\mathcal{F}
is a quasi-coherent sheaf on the scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Remark 5.2
.

By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, §3.1, discussion preceding Definition 3.1.1, and by loc. cit., Ch. I, Lemma 3.1.2, the conditions defining the notion of an adically quasi-coherent sheaf do not depend on the choice of an ideal of definition of finite type.

For an adically quasi-coherent sheaves of algebras
\mathcal{A}
on an adic formal scheme of finite ideal type
\mathfrak{S}
, there is a notion of relative formal spectrum
{\rm Spf}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{S}
similar to the notion of relative spectrum of a quasi-coherent algebra on a scheme. Moreover, similar to the scheme case, all affine morphisms of adic formal schemes of finite ideal type
f:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{S}
can be characterized as morphisms of the form
{\rm Spf}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{S}
, namely, for any affine morphism
f:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{S}
we can choose
\mathcal{A}=f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
, see
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Theorem 4.1.8.

Proposition 5.3
.

Fix a complete adic ring
R
with a non-zero-divisor
\varpi\in R
generating an ideal of definition of
R
and suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal
R
-scheme with adic analytic generic fiber
S=\mathfrak{S}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
. For every admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{S}
of
\mathfrak{S}
, the direct image sheaf
{\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}
is an adically quasi-coherent sheaf of algebras on
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove, for any affine open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i}
of
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
, the sheaf

{\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}|_{\mathfrak{U}_{i}}={\rm sp}_{U_{i},\mathfrak{U}_{i}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}

(where in the above equality we tacitly used Lemma
4.7
) is adically quasi-coherent for each
i
. Hence it suffices to prove the proposition in the case when
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{S}
, when
\mathfrak{S}
is a rig-sheafy affine formal scheme (we use here that
{\rm sp}_{U_{i},\mathfrak{U}_{i}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})=(U_{i})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
, by Lemma
4.7
). For this it suffices to check that

\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(S(\frac{1}{g}))=({\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+})(D(g))=\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(S)\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle g^{-1}\rangle=\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(S)\langle g^{-1}\rangle

for every
g\in A
. Hence it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(S)\langle g^{-1}\rangle
is integrally closed inside
\mathcal{O}_{S}(S)\langle g^{-1}\rangle
. By
[
32
]
, Lemma 2.4.3(iv), this follows from
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(S)[g^{-1}]
being an integrally closed open subring of
\mathcal{O}(S)[g^{-1}]
. ∎

In order to establish the existence of formal
R
-models for a general uniform quasi-compact quasi-separated adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
we need the following notion of integrally closed models.

Definition 5.4

(Integrally closed formal models)
.

A locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
with adic analytic generic fiber
X
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
is said to be
integrally closed inside its generic fiber
X
if for every affine open subset
\mathfrak{V}
of
\mathfrak{X}
the ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{V})
is integrally closed in the Tate ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{V})[\varpi^{-1}]
. In this case we also say that
\mathfrak{X}
is an integrally closed formal
R
-model of the adic space
X
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
.

The first order of business is to verify that the above notion of integrally closed formal models is local.

Lemma 5.5
.

For a locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathfrak{X}
is integrally closed in its generic fiber.

(2)

There exists an affine open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i}
of
\mathfrak{X}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})
is integrally closed in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})[\varpi^{-1}]
for every
i
.

Proof.

This reduces to proving that for a
\varpi
-adically complete,
\varpi
-torsion-free ring
A
which is integrally closed in
A[\varpi^{-1}]
and
g\in A
, the completed localization
A\langle g^{-1}\rangle
is integrally closed in
A\langle g^{-1}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]
, which is a consequence of
[
32
]
, Lemma 2.4.3(iv).∎

Using Proposition
5.3
, we can always construct an integrally closed formal
R
-model from an arbitrary one, at least for uniform adic spaces. This is a consequence of the following proposition, cf. also
[
45
]
, Proposition 1.1, for a more classical result along these lines.

Proposition 5.6
.

Fix a complete adic ring
R
with a non-zero-divisor
\varpi\in R
generating an ideal of definition of
R
and suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy. Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal
R
-scheme with adic analytic generic fiber
S=\mathfrak{S}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
. The affine morphism

f_{0}:\mathfrak{X}={\rm Spf}({\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+})\to\mathfrak{S}

satisfies
f_{0\eta}={\rm id}
and
\mathfrak{X}
is an integrally closed formal
R
-model of
S
. Moreover,
f_{0}
is the unique affine morphism of locally rig-sheafy adic formal
R
-schemes
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{S}
with target
\mathfrak{S}
such that
f_{0\eta}={\rm id}
and such that the source
\mathfrak{X}
is
\varpi
-torsion-free and integrally closed in its generic fiber
S
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
,
{\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}
is an adically quasi-coherent algebra on
\mathfrak{S}
, so the relative formal spectrum
\mathfrak{X}
is defined and the morphism
f_{0}
is affine. Let
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i}
be an affine open cover of
\mathfrak{S}
and let
U_{i}={\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})=\mathfrak{U}_{i\eta}
, which is an affinoid open subspace of
S
, for every
i
. The fact that
f_{0\eta}={\rm id}
and that
\mathfrak{X}
is integrally closed in
S
follows from
{\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})=\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(U_{i})
being an integrally closed in
\mathcal{O}_{S}(U_{i})
and a ring of definition of
\mathcal{O}_{S}(U)
(since
\mathcal{O}_{S}(U_{i})
are uniform Tate rings).

It remains to prove the uniqueness part of the proposition. To this end, let
g_{0}:\mathfrak{Z}\to\mathfrak{S}
be another affine adic morphism of
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-schemes such that
\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}=S
,
g_{0\eta}={\rm id}
and
\mathfrak{Z}
is integrally closed in its generic fiber. By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Theorem 4.1.8,
g_{0\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}
is an adically quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{S}}
-algebra,
\mathfrak{Z}={\rm Spf}(g_{0\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}})
and
g_{0}
is the canonical morphism
{\rm Spf}(g_{0\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}})\to\mathfrak{S}
. By loc. cit., Proposition 4.1.10, the pre-image
g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U})
of every affine open subset
\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}
is affine. Let
\mathfrak{U}
be an affine open subset of
\mathfrak{S}
and let
U={\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U})
. Since
S=\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
and
{\rm id}=g_{0\eta}
, the commutative triangle
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces \mathfrak{Z} }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{Z}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{0}}

(together with Lemma
4.7
) shows that
g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U})_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
is equal to the affinoid open subspace

U={\rm Spa}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(U),\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(U))

of
S
. Therefore, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}(g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}))[\varpi^{-1}]=\mathcal{O}_{S}(U),

and the integral closure of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}(g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}))
inside
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}(g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}))[\varpi^{-1}]
is equal to
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(U)
. Since
\mathfrak{Z}
is integrally closed in its generic fiber, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}(g_{0}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}))=\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(U).

Since
\mathfrak{U}
was arbitrary, this means that

g_{0\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}}={\rm sp}_{S,\mathfrak{S}\ast}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}

as adically quasi-coherent algebras on
\mathfrak{S}
. By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Theorem 4.1.8, this implies the desired equality
g_{0}=f_{0}
.∎

Definition 5.7

(Normalization of a formal model)
.

In the situation of Proposition
5.6
we call the formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
over
\mathfrak{S}
the normalization of
\mathfrak{S}
in its generic fiber
S
, or the normalization of the formal
R
-model
\mathfrak{S}
of
S
.

We also give a name to morphisms between formal models which induce the identity on the generic fiber.

Definition 5.8

(Formal modification)
.

For a locally rig-sheafy qcqs adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
with generic fiber
X
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
, a
formal modification

f_{0}
of
\mathfrak{X}
is an adic morphism of locally rig-sheafy qcqs adic formal
R
-schemes
f_{0}:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
which induces an isomorphism on the generic fibers. A morphism
h_{0}
between two formal modifications
f_{0}:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
and
g_{0}:\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
is a commutative triangle
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{0}}

of adic morphisms of formal schemes. If there exists a morphism from a formal modification
f_{0}
to a formal modification
g_{0}
of
\mathfrak{X}
, then we also say that
f_{0}
dominates
g_{0}
and
g_{0}
is dominated by
f_{0}
.

By Lemma
4.8
, every admissible formal blow-up is a formal modification. On the other hand, we obtain a different class of examples from Proposition
5.6
.

Corollary 5.9
.

For every locally rig-sheafy quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
with uniform adic analytic generic fiber
X
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) affine formal modification
\mathfrak{Z}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
such that
\mathfrak{Z}
is
\varpi
-torsion-free and integrally closed in its generic fiber
X
.

Proof.

This is a reformulation of Proposition
5.6
.∎

The assumption that
X
be uniform in Proposition
5.6
and Corollary
5.9
cannot be fully omitted.

Lemma 5.10
.

If
X
is an adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
which admits an integrally closed formal
R
-model
\mathfrak{X}
, then
X
has an open cover by affinoid open subspaces
U_{i}
such that each of the Tate rings
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})
is uniform.

Proof.

Covering
\mathfrak{X}
by rig-sheafy affine open subsets, it suffices to assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is a rig-sheafy affine adic formal
R
-scheme. Then
X
is affinoid, namely,

X={\rm Spa}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})}),

where the topology on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})[\varpi^{-1}]
is the one defined by the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X}),(\varpi))
. The assumption that
\mathfrak{X}
is an integrally closed formal
R
-model of
X
implies that

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})}=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(X),

so
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(X)
is a ring of definition of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. Thus the assertion follows from the following elementary lemma, which follows, for example, from
[
42
]
, Lemma 2.13(2), but whose proof we include for the reader’s convenience.∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
A
be a Tate ring which has an integrally closed ring of definition
A^{+}
. Then
A
is uniform.

Proof.

Let
\varpi\in A^{+}
be a non-zero-divisor which generates an ideal of definition of
A^{+}
. Let
f\in A^{\circ}
, i.e., there exists an integer
m\geq 0
such that
f^{n}\in\varpi^{-m}A^{+}
for all
n
. Then, in particular,
(\varpi f)^{m}\in A^{+}
. Since
A^{+}
is integrally closed, this means that
\varpi f\in A^{+}
. It follows that
\varpi A^{\circ}\subseteq A^{+}
, so
A^{\circ}
is bounded, as claimed.∎

Let us also give a name to those adic formal
R
-schemes whose generic fibers are uniform adic spaces (cf.
[
42
]
, Definition 2.16 and Lemma 2.17).

Definition 5.12

(Locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-schemes)
.

(1)

A complete adic ring
A
with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi
is called uniform (respectively, stably uniform) if the Tate ring
A[\varpi^{-1}]
is uniform (respectively, stably uniform).

(2)

An affine adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is called uniform (respectively, stably uniform) if the adic ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})
is uniform (respectively, stably uniform). An arbitrary adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is then called locally uniform (respectively, locally stably uniform) if
\mathfrak{X}
has an open cover by uniform (respectively, stably uniform) affine adic formal
R
-schemes.

Lemma 5.13
.

If
\mathfrak{X}
is a locally uniform (respectively, locally stably uniform) adic formal
R
-scheme, then every affine open subset
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathfrak{X}
is uniform (respectively, stably uniform).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is a sheaf of complete topological rings, this reduces to proving that for any uniform (respectively, stably uniform) complete adic ring
A
, with
\varpi\in A
a non-zero-divisor generating an ideal of definition of
A
, and for any
g\in A
, the Tate ring
A\langle g^{-1}\rangle[\varpi^{-1}]=A[\varpi^{-1}]\langle g^{-1}\rangle
is uniform (respectively, stably uniform). The stably uniform case is true by definition, so we only have to prove that
A[\varpi^{-1}]\langle g^{-1}\rangle
is a uniform Tate ring whenever
A[\varpi^{-1}]
is one. By Lemma
5.11
it suffices to show that
A[\varpi^{-1}]\langle g^{-1}\rangle
has an integrally closed ring of definition. Let
A^{+}
be an integrally closed ring of definition of
A[\varpi^{-1}]
. Then
A^{+}[g^{-1}]
is an integrally closed ring of definition of
A[\varpi^{-1}][g^{-1}]
. It then follows from
[
32
]
, Lemma 2.4.3(iii) and (iv), that
A^{+}\langle g^{-1}\rangle
is an integrally closed ring of definition of
A[\varpi^{-1}]\langle g^{-1}\rangle
.∎

Locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-schemes are locally rig-sheafy by the well-known theorem of Buzzard-Verberkmoes
[
15
]
and Mihara
[
41
]
. In particular, the adic analytic generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
of such an adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is defined. In fact, locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-schemes
\mathfrak{X}
are precisely the locally rig-sheafy adic formal
R
-schemes whose generic fiber is uniform.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
X
be an adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
and let
\mathfrak{X}
be a formal
R
-model of
X
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a uniform adic space.

(2)

\mathfrak{X}
is a locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-scheme.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{U}_{i})_{i}
be a rig-sheafy affine open cover of
\mathfrak{X}
and let
U_{i}={\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})
. If
X
is a uniform adic space, then the Tate ring
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})[\varpi^{-1}]
is stably uniform for each
i
, so
\mathfrak{X}
is locally stably uniform. Conversely, if
\mathfrak{X}
is locally stably uniform, then, by Lemma
5.13
, each
\mathfrak{U}_{i}
is stably uniform and thus
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})
is a stably uniform Tate ring for all
i
. ∎

In particular, every formal modification of a locally stably uniform adic formal
R
-scheme is again locally stably uniform. We are now ready to introduce the following special kind of formal modifications.

Definition 5.15

(Normalized formal blow-up)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a locally rig-sheafy qcqs
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-scheme with uniform generic fiber over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
. A formal modification
f_{0}:\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
is called a
normalized formal blow-up
if it is the composition of a
\varpi
-torsion-free admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
followed by its normalization
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
(whose existence is guaranteed by Corollary
5.9
).

The following analog of Lemma
4.5

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a locally rig-sheafy qcqs
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-scheme with uniform generic fiber

over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})

\mathfrak{U}
be a quasi-compact open formal subscheme of

be a normalized formal blow-up. Then there exists a normalized formal blow-up

and an open immersion
\mathfrak{V}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{Z}
such that the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathfrak{V}
equals

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
be an admissible formal blow-up such that
\mathfrak{V}
is the normalization of
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
in its generic fiber. By Lemma

, there exists an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
extending
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
. Let

be the normalization of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
in

. Since

is an affine formal modification, so is the restricted morphism

Moreover,

is integrally closed in its generic fiber,

being integrally closed in its generic fiber. It follows that

is canonically identified with the normalization

of
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
in its generic fiber, by the uniqueness part of Corollary

of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}

Proof of the main results

It turns out that normalized formal blow-ups play a role in the theory of formal

Lemma 6.1

Let

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0\eta}}

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{Z}
,
\mathfrak{X}
be locally stably uniform
\varpi
-torsion-free qcqs adic formal

Suppose first that

Possibly after enlarging the index set

of

for all

. By Corollary
4.18

of
\mathfrak{Z}
and an affine open cover

of

V_{i}={\rm sp}_{\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}},\mathfrak{T}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{T}_{i})

for all
i

with its normalization

in
\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
and letting
\mathfrak{V}_{i}

\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime}

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a non-zero-divisor
\varpi
, suppose that the Tate ring

is sheafy and let

\varpi
-torsion-free quasi-compact quasi-separated formal

-model
\mathfrak{X}
of

Proof.

Every uniform affinoid open subspace

admits the integrally closed
\varpi
-torsion-free affine formal
R
-model
{\rm Spf}(A^{+})
. We proceed by induction on the size of a finite affinoid open cover of
X
. Thus, suppose that the assertion holds for every qcqs uniform adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})
which can be covered by
n

X
be a qcqs uniform adic space over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})

and

and

admit
\varpi
-torsion-free qcqs formal
R
-models
\mathfrak{U}
and
\mathfrak{V}
which are integrally closed in their generic fibers. Set

. The hypothesis that

is quasi-separated ensures that

is quasi-compact. By Lemma

there exist formal modifications
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{U}
and
\mathfrak{V}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{V}
and quasi-compact open subsets

and
\mathfrak{W}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{V}^{\prime}
such that

Using Corollary
5.9
and replacing
\mathfrak{U}^{\prime}
(respectively,
\mathfrak{V}^{\prime}

(respectively, inside

), we may assume that

and
\mathfrak{V}^{\prime}
are

and

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi\in R
and suppose that the Tate ring

gives rise to an equivalence between

the category of locally stably uniform
\varpi
-torsion-free quasi-compact quasi-separated adic formal

(2)

the category of uniform quasi-compact quasi-separated adic spaces over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],\overline{R})

Proof.

That the functor takes values in the category of uniform adic spaces is the content of Lemma

6.1
shows that the functor is faithful, while Lemma
6.2
shows that it is full. Finally, Theorem

Definition 6.5

An adic space

of

is strongly sheafy, that is,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rangle
is sheafy for every

Definition 6.6

(Balls over strong adic spaces)

Let

be a strong adic space and let

be an affinoid open cover such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})
. For every pair of distinct indices

let

\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}
the base change
\mathfrak{Z}_{i}\times_{\mathfrak{V}_{i}}\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is an admissible formal blow-up. By Lemma
4.5
, for every
i
there exists an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}_{i}\to\mathfrak{X}
extending
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{V}_{i}
and there exists an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
which factors as
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}_{i}\to\mathfrak{X}
, with
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}_{i}
an admissible formal blow-up, for all
i
.

We claim that this
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
satisfies the property in the statement of the proposition. To see this, let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. For every
i
, let
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
be the pre-image of
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}_{i}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
. By
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 1.1.10, the composition
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}_{i}
is an admissible formal blow-up for each
i
. Furthermore, by
[
24
]
, Ch. II, Proposition 1.1.8,
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}
is precisely the pre-image of
\mathfrak{V}_{i}
under
\mathfrak{X}_{i}\to\mathfrak{X}
and the restriction
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}_{i}
is an admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime}
, for every
i
. In particular,
\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is the pre-image of
\mathfrak{V}_{i}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
and thus
(\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime})_{i}
is an open cover of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
, the family
(\mathfrak{V}_{i})_{i}
being an open cover of
\mathfrak{X}
. By hypothesis, the base change morphisms
\mathfrak{Z}_{i}\times_{\mathfrak{V}_{i}}\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
are admissible formal blow-ups. But, on the other hand, the family
(\mathfrak{Z}_{i}\times_{\mathfrak{V}_{i}}\mathfrak{V}_{i}^{\prime\prime})_{i}
is an open cover of
\mathfrak{Z}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{Z}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
is an admissible formal blow-up.

It remains to prove the claim when
\mathfrak{X}
is affine. Let
X
be the common adic analytic generic fiber of
\mathfrak{Z}
and
\mathfrak{X}
. Let
\mathfrak{U}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{U}_{n}
be a rig-sheafy affine open cover of
\mathfrak{Z}
and set
U_{i}={\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{Z}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. By Corollary
4.18
, there is an admissible formal blow-up
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of
\mathfrak{X}
and an affine open cover
(\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime})_{i}
of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that
{\rm sp}_{X,\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime})=U_{i}
for all
i
. Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary admissible formal blow-up and, for every
i=1,\dots,n
, let
\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
be the pre-image of
\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime}
under
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Note that

\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{Z}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}

is a formal modification of
\mathfrak{X}
which dominates both
\mathfrak{Z}\to\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
. For every index
i
consider the affine open subset
\mathfrak{U}_{i}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}
. By Lemma
6.9
,
\mathfrak{U}_{i}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is equal to the pre-image in
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathfrak{U}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{Z}
and to the pre-image in
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathfrak{U}_{i}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
. In particular, the two projection morphisms
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{Z}
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
are affine. By virtue of
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Proposition 4.1.12, this also means that the morphisms of schemes
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
are affine.

By Lemma
6.10
, the morphism
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
is universally closed, being a formal modification. This implies that the affine morphism of schemes
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
is universally closed and thus integral, by
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Proposition 4.5.9, and
[
22
]
, Tag 01WM. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{Z}\to\mathfrak{X}
is topologically of finite type, so is its base change
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{Z}\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
. Consequently, the induced morphism of schemes

\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}_{0}

is of finite type. But an integral morphism of finite type is finite, so
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
is a finite morphism of schemes. By
[
24
]
, Ch. I, Prop. 4.2.1, this means that the morphism of formal schemes
\mathfrak{Z}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
is finite and we can conclude by the following lemma, which is a straightforward generalization of
[
13
]
, Lemma 4.5.∎

Lemma 6.11
.

Every finite formal modification
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
of a locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free qcqs adic formal
R
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is an admissible formal blow-up.

Proof.

Since finite morphisms are affine we may assume that both
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
are affine. In this case the proof of
[
13
]
, Lemma 4.5, carries over almost verbatim; we include the argument for the sake of completeness. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
be elements which generate
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
as an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})
-module. Since
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
is a formal modification,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})[\varpi^{-1}]=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})[\varpi^{-1}]
. Hence there exists an integer
r\geq 0
such that
\varpi^{r}f_{1},\dots,\varpi^{r}f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})
. Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{X}
be the admissible formal blow-up of
\mathfrak{X}
in the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
defined by the open ideal

I=(\varpi^{r},\varpi^{r}f_{1},\dots,\varpi^{r}f_{n})_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})}.

We have

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}={\rm Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})[f_{1},\dots,f_{n}])={\rm Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle)={\rm Spf}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})\langle\frac{\varpi^{r},\varpi^{r}f_{1},\dots,\varpi^{r}f_{n}}{\varpi^{r}}\rangle),

so
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
equals the open subset of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
on which the stalks of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}}
are generated by
\varpi^{r}
. We claim that this open subset is equal to all of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime\prime}
. To prove this, we have to verify the equalities

IA_{i}=\varpi^{r}A_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,r
, where

A_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})\langle\frac{\varpi^{r}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}},\frac{\varpi^{r}f_{1}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}},\dots,\frac{\varpi^{r}f_{n}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}}\rangle.

Since the ideal
IA_{i}
of the completed blow-up algebra
A_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})\langle\frac{I}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}}\rangle
is generated by
\varpi^{r}f_{i}
, it suffices to prove that the element

f_{i}\in A_{i}[f_{i}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})\langle\frac{\varpi^{r}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}},\frac{\varpi^{r}f_{1}}{f_{i}},\dots,\frac{\varpi^{r}f_{n}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}}\rangle

belongs to
A_{i}
(as in this case
f_{i}
is an invertible element of
A_{i}
, with inverse
\frac{\varpi^{r}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}}
). Since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})[f_{i}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
is integral over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(\mathfrak{X})
, the element
f_{i}\in A_{i}[f_{i}]
is, a fortiori, integral over
A_{i}
. But, at the same time,
f_{i}
is a unit in
A_{i}[f_{i}]
with inverse
\frac{\varpi^{r}}{\varpi^{r}f_{i}}\in A_{i}
, so we can multiply an integral equation

f_{i}^{N}+a_{1}f_{i}^{N-1}+\dots+a_{N-1}f_{i}+a_{N}=0

of
f_{i}
over
A_{i}
by
f_{i}^{-(N-1)}
to see that
f_{i}\in A_{i}
. This concludes the proof.∎

Remark 6.12
.

Proposition
6.7
can be viewed as an analog of the following theorem from algebraic geometry (
[
20
]
, Theorem 2.11): Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper morphism of qcqs schemes and let
U\subseteq X
be a quasi-compact dense open subset with dense pre-image in
X^{\prime}
such that
f
is an isomorphism over
U
. There exist
U
-admissible blow-ups
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{X}\to X
and an isomorphism
\widetilde{X^{\prime}}\cong\widetilde{X}
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X^{\prime}} }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X} }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime} }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

is commutative.

We call a morphism of adic spaces
f:X\to S
locally of finite type if for any pair of affinoid open subspaces
V\subseteq X
,
U\subseteq S
with
f(V)\subseteq U
the corresponding morphism of Huber pairs
(\mathcal{O}_{S}(U),\mathcal{O}_{S}^{+}(U))\to(\mathcal{O}_{X}(V),\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(V))
is of finite type. For adic spaces of finite type over a Tate affinoid adic space, we also have the following non-Noetherian version of Raynaud’s theory of formal models.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
R
be a complete adic ring with ideal of definition generated by a single non-zero-divisor
\varpi\in R
. Suppose that the Tate ring
R[\varpi^{-1}]
is sheafy and that
R
is integrally closed in
R[\varpi^{-1}]
. The functor

\mathfrak{X}\mapsto\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}

gives rise to an equivalence between

(1)

the category of locally rig-sheafy
\varpi
-torsion-free quasi-separated adic formal
R
-schemes topologically of finite type over
{\rm Spf}(R)
, localized by admissible formal blow-ups, and

(2)

the category of quasi-separated adic spaces of finite type over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],R)
.

Proof.

Faithfulness of the functor is a special case of Lemma
6.1
. To prove that the functor is full, let
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{Z}
be locally rig-sheafy quasi-separated
\varpi
-torsion-free adic formal
R
-schemes topologically of finite type and let
f:\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
be a morphism of adic spaces over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],R)
between their generic fibers. By the same argument as in the second paragraph of the proof of Lemma
6.2
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{Z}
are affine. Recall that
\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}
are both of finite type over
{\rm Spa}(R[\varpi^{-1}],R)
. It then follows from
[
33
]
, Lemma 3.5(iii), and the definition of a quotient map between Huber pairs that there exists a ring of definition
A_{0}
of
A=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}})
(respectively, a ring of definition
B_{0}
of
B=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}}}(\mathfrak{Z}_{\eta}^{{\rm ad}})
) which is topologically of finite type over
R
and satisfies
A^{+}=\overline{A_{0}}
(respectively,
B^{+}=\overline{B_{0}}
). Consider the continuous ring map

\varphi:A\to B

induced by
f
and let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in A_{0}
be topological generators of
A_{0}
as an
R
-algebra, i.e.,
A_{0}=R\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\rangle
. Set

C_{0}=B_{0}[\varphi(f_{1}),\dots,\varphi(f_{n})]\subseteq B.

Since

\varphi(A_{0})\subseteq\varphi(A^{+})\subseteq B^{+}=\overline{B_{0}},

the subring
C_{0}
is contained in
\overline{B_{0}}
. Consequently,
C_{0}
is integral over
B_{0}
. Since
C_{0}
is a finitely generated
B_{0}
-algebra by definition, it is a finite
B_{0}
-algebra. By Lemma
6.11
, this entails that the morphism
{\rm Spf}(C_{0})\to{\rm Spf}(B_{0})
is an admissible formal blow-up. But by construction, the morphism of formal schemes

f_{0}:{\rm Spf}(C_{0})\to{\rm Spf}(A_{0})

induced by the restriction of
\varphi
to
A_{0}
induces the given morphism
f
on the generic fiber. This shows that the functor in the theorem is indeed full. The essential surjectivity can be deduced from this by the same argument as in the proof of
[
13
]
, Theorem 4.1(e) (i.e., by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
6.3
above).∎

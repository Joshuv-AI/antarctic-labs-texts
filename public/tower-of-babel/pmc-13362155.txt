Long-term safety and efficacy of triheptanoin in Korean patients with long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders: a prospective, open-label, single-center, phase II clinical study
Authors: Ji-Hee Yoon, Jun-Hong Park, Dohyung Kim, Soojin Hwang, Jiyeon Park, Ja Hye Kim, Sukyoung Choi, Eunjae Ko, Byung Joo Lee, Yeji Moon, Beom Hee Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLong-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FAOD) are inherited metabolic conditions caused by impaired mitochondrial β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids, leading to rhabdomyolysis, cardiomyopathy, and hypoglycemia. Triheptanoin, a medium odd-chain triglyceride, provides anaplerotic substrates that replenish tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates, offering alternative energy sources and potential therapeutic benefit.MethodsThis open-label, single-center phase II study evaluated the safety and efficacy of triheptanoin in 10 Korean patients with genetically confirmed LC-FAOD who experienced recurrent rhabdomyolysis despite conventional treatment. Triheptanoin was titrated to 35% of total daily caloric intake, and all patients completed 3 years of treatment. The primary outcome was safety, assessed by adverse events. Secondary outcomes included changes in the major clinical event (MCE) rate and duration, functional capacity, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and organ function.ResultsThe mean study duration was 3.5 ± 0.3 years. Serious adverse events occurred in nine patients (90%), most commonly rhabdomyolysis (100%), and were considered unrelated to triheptanoin. Mild gastrointestinal symptoms during titration were the most frequent treatment-related adverse events (80%). The median annualized MCE rate and duration decreased by 42.4% and 43.6%, respectively (P = 0.114 and P = 0.059). The duration per MCE was significantly reduced by 3.5 days, as assessed by a linear mixed-effects model (P = 0.022), with a significant reduction observed in the child subgroup (P < 0.001). In eight patients, the 12-minute walk test distance increased by 12.4% at 6 months and was maintained over 3 years (P = 0.385). HRQoL improved to within the normative range in adults, whereas child self-reported and parent proxy-reported scores showed an initial increase but remained suboptimal at 3 years. Progressive neuropathy and newly developed retinopathy and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder were observed during treatment.ConclusionTriheptanoin significantly reduced hospitalization burden in Korean patients with LC-FAOD and was well-tolerated, with potential benefits in physical function.Trial registrationClinical Research Information Service (CRIS), Republic of Korea, KCT0006756, 15 November 2021.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-026-02390-x.

Background

Long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FAODs) are a group of rare autosomal recessive inborn errors of metabolism caused by enzyme defects in the carnitine shuttle or the mitochondrial β-oxidation pathway. These defects result in impaired energy production and the accumulation of toxic metabolites. Disorders classified under this category include deficiencies of carnitine palmitoyl transferase I and II, carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase, very long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (VLCAD), long-chain 3-hydroxyl-CoA dehydrogenase, and mitochondrial trifunctional protein (TFP). Clinically, LC-FAODs may present with hypoketotic hypoglycemia, skeletal myopathy, recurrent rhabdomyolysis, cardiomyopathy, and sudden death, particularly during periods of metabolic stress [1–3].

Current standard management focuses on preventing catabolic states through dietary interventions, including fasting avoidance, supplementation with medium-chain triglycerides (MCT), and emergency protocols for metabolic decompensation. MCT oil consists of medium chain fatty acids that are metabolized via the medium-chain fatty acid oxidation enzymes, bypassing the long-chain oxidation pathway [1, 4, 5]. However, despite early diagnosis through newborn screening and adherence to current treatment guidelines, many patients continue to experience significant morbidity and life-threatening metabolic crises [6–8].

Triheptanoin, a synthetic medium odd-chain triglyceride consisting of three heptanoate (C7) fatty acids, has emerged as a novel anaplerotic therapy for LC-FAOD [9]. Triheptanoin is approved in the United States for the treatment of pediatric and adult patients with molecularly confirmed LC-FAOD. Following ingestion, triheptanoin is catabolized to heptanoate, which crosses the mitochondrial membrane independently of the carnitine carrier and is subsequently metabolized by medium-chain fatty acid oxidation enzymes. Its catabolism produces both acetyl-CoA and propionyl-CoA; acetyl-CoA can enter the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle for ATP production or be diverted to the liver for ketogenesis, whereas propionyl-CoA is converted to succinyl-CoA, replenishing depleted odd-chain intermediates of the TCA cycle. This process contributes to glycogen sparing and restores reducing equivalents for oxidative phosphorylation and ATP required for gluconeogenesis. This dual mechanism provides an alternative energy source and supports metabolic flexibility during catabolic stress [9–11].

Clinical data on triheptanoin in LC-FAOD have been primarily generated from open-label phase II studies and their long-term extension cohorts, which have reported an acceptable safety profile and suggested potential improvements in clinical event burden and functional outcomes [12–15]. In an initial 24-week phase II study, CL201, involving 29 pediatric and adult patients, triheptanoin was generally well-tolerated. At week 18, eligible patients showed a 28% increase in 12-minute walk test (12MWT) distance from baseline, and at week 24, a 60% increase in exercise tolerance was observed [13]. In a subsequent 78-week open-label extension in the same cohort, the mean annualized major clinical event (MCE) rate was significantly reduced by 48.1% (P = 0.021), and the mean annualized duration of MCEs decreased by 50.3% (P = 0.028) [14]. These benefits were maintained in a long-term extension study, CL202, which demonstrated sustained reductions in hospitalization frequency and duration, supporting the long-term safety profile and consistent clinical outcomes associated with triheptanoin use in the management of LC-FAOD [10, 16].

Despite accumulating evidence supporting the potential clinical benefits of triheptanoin in patients with LC-FAOD, data on its long-term safety and clinical outcomes in Asian populations remain limited. In particular, most available evidence has been derived from open-label phase II studies and extension cohorts conducted predominantly in Western populations, underscoring the need for population-specific data.

The primary objective of this prospective phase II open-label study was to evaluate the safety of long-term triheptanoin therapy in Korean patients with LC-FAOD. Secondary objectives included changes in MCE frequency and duration, with exploratory assessments of functional capacity, quality of life, and selected metabolic and cardiac parameters. The study was intended to provide descriptive safety and clinical outcome data.

Methods

Patients

A total of 10 patients with LC-FAOD were enrolled at the Medical Genetics Center, Asan Medical Center, Seoul, Korea between March 2022 and January 2023. Eligible participants were at least 6 months of age and had a confirmed diagnosis of LC-FAOD based on both biochemical and genetic testing. Only patients who exhibited significant clinical manifestations of LC-FAOD despite prior therapy were included, as defined by one or more of the following criteria within 2 years prior to enrollment: (1) intermittent creatine kinase (CK) elevation ≥ 500 IU/L with clinical symptoms or signs of rhabdomyolysis; (2) chronic CK elevation ≥ 1,000 IU/L for at least 3 months, regardless of clinical symptoms or signs of rhabdomyolysis; (3) marked hypoglycemia susceptibility (blood glucose < 60 mg/dL) after short-term fasting; (4) cardiomyopathy documented by echocardiography within the 90 days prior to enrollment.

Patients with conditions in which triheptanoin treatment is contraindicated, such as medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency, short- or medium-chain FAOD, disorder of ketone body metabolism, propionic acidemia or methylmalonic acidemia, or malabsorption due to pancreatic insufficiency or the concomitant use of pancreatic lipase inhibitors, were excluded. Patients with confounding comorbidities or prior participation in other clinical studies within the 6 months prior to the study were also excluded. In addition, patients with other conditions or exposures known to cause marked CK elevation or rhabdomyolysis, including the use of myotoxic medications (e.g., statins or fibrates) or relevant neuromuscular or endocrine disorders, were excluded.

Study design

This study was conducted as a prospective, interventional, open-label, single-arm phase II trial. The study was registered in the Clinical Research Information Service (CRIS; KCT0006756). The study was conducted and reported in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines. Eligible patients were enrolled, and their documented history of MCEs over the 2-year period prior to the first dose of triheptanoin (or from birth for patients < 2 years of age) was collected. Patients continued their existing therapy, including MCT oil use and dietary long-chain fat restriction, for a 4-week run-in period to establish a stable baseline.

Triheptanoin (marketed as DOJOLVI®) was a pharmaceutical-grade investigational product under development by Ultragenyx Pharmaceutical, Inc. (CA, USA). Following completion of the run-in period, MCT oil was discontinued and triheptanoin therapy was initiated, with long-chain fat restriction continued throughout the study period. Triheptanoin was administered orally with food or drink at least 4 times daily, with the dose gradually titrated to a target of 35% of the total daily caloric intake, adjusted based on individual tolerability and estimated metabolic energy requirements.

Assessments

Safety assessments were conducted in accordance with U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) guidance for investigational new drugs (INDs), including the monitoring, classification, assessment of severity and relatedness, and reporting of adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) [17, 18]. The primary endpoint was the safety of triheptanoin in Korean patients with LC-FAOD. All AEs, regardless of severity, were monitored and followed until resolution. Clinical safety evaluations, including medical history, physical examination, vital signs, and laboratory tests, were performed every 1–2 weeks during the first month, monthly until 3 months, and every 6 months thereafter until the end of the study.

Secondary outcomes included changes in MCEs, such as rhabdomyolysis, cardiomyopathy, hypoglycemia, and hepatic dysfunction, evaluated by event frequency, duration, and severity. MCEs were defined as clinically significant acute events requiring medical evaluation, consistent with definitions used in prior LC-FAOD clinical studies [10, 16]. MCEs were identified based on symptom-related emergency department or outpatient visits and were confirmed through detailed medical record review. The same definition and ascertainment criteria were applied consistently to both the retrospective pre-treatment and prospective post-treatment periods. Data were collected retrospectively for up to 2 years prior to enrollment and prospectively at 6-month intervals thereafter. Additional evaluations performed at baseline and every 6 months included echocardiography, metabolic profiling (blood acylcarnitines and urine organic acids), 12MWT, pulmonary function tests, ophthalmologic examination, and assessment of growth parameters (Tanner staging, insulin-like growth factor 1, insulin-like growth factor binding protein 3). Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) was assessed using age-appropriate instruments: the Korean Child Behavior Checklist (K-CBCL) and Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory TM (PedsQL™) for patients aged < 18 years, and the 36-Item Short Form Survey (SF-36) and Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) for patients aged ≥ 18 years [19–24]. PedsQL and SF-36 scores were interpreted using established normative data, with impairment defined relative to population-based reference values [25, 26]. Each participant completed only the assessments validated for their age group at the time of study entry.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows, version 21.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables and as median with interquartile range (IQR) or range for skewed distributions. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare parameters before and after triheptanoin treatment. Changes in MCE duration per event were analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model at the event level, with treatment period, age group, and their interaction as fixed effects and subject as a random intercept. In addition, exploratory correlation analyses were performed using Spearman’s rank correlation to assess associations between changes in medium-chain fat–derived caloric proportion and changes in MCE outcomes.

To evaluate longitudinal changes in clinical parameters, repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed for the 12MWT, echocardiography, and pulmonary function test results for participants with complete data across all time points. This method accounted for within-subject correlations across seven time points (baseline, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 years). P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Study population

Ten patients (four males and six females) with genetically confirmed LC-FAOD were treated with triheptanoin between March 2022 and February 2025 and were included in this analysis. Among them, four patients were diagnosed with VLCAD deficiency and six with TFP deficiency (Table 1). Four patients were initially identified through newborn screening using tandem mass spectrometry, whereas the remaining six presented with clinical symptoms; however, all diagnoses were subsequently confirmed by genetic testing.

At enrollment, the median age of patients was 8.8 years (range, 1–30 years). The most common indication for initiating triheptanoin therapy was recurrent rhabdomyolysis, reported in all patients (100%). Four patients had a prior history of cardiomyopathy; however, all demonstrated normal cardiac function at baseline. At baseline, all patients were receiving MCT oil or MCT-based formula, with a mean proportion of MCT-derived calories accounting for 23.6% of total daily caloric intake.

The mean follow-up duration during triheptanoin therapy was 3.5 ± 0.3 years. Most patients (70%) achieved ≥ 35% of the total daily caloric intake from triheptanoin, which was considered compliance with the prescribed regimen. The mean maintenance dose was 1.7 ± 0.8 g/kg/day, corresponding to an average of 33% of the total daily caloric intake.

Safety outcomes

The most common AE was rhabdomyolysis, which is related to the underlying disease and occurred in 90% of patients. Other commonly observed events included upper respiratory tract infection (80%), diarrhea (70%), abdominal pain (60%), muscle weakness or pain (30%), gastrointestinal infection (20%), and cardiac manifestations (20%). AEs considered possibly related to triheptanoin were observed in eight patients (80%), most of which were mild gastrointestinal symptoms classified as grade 1 (Table 2). These AEs typically emerged during the dose titration period. In most cases, symptoms improved with gradual dose escalation, administration with or after meals, or division of the daily dose. Seven patients were able to tolerate and maintain the target dose of triheptanoin (35% of total daily caloric intake). However, in three patients, a dose reduction or limitation was required due to persistent gastrointestinal symptoms. One patient developed grade 1 diarrhea during escalation to 35%, leading to dose reduction to 30%, which was maintained thereafter. Another patient experienced intermittent grade 1 abdominal pain after reaching 27%, and further dose escalation was not attempted. The third patient developed grade 2 abdominal pain and diarrhea at 30%, prompting a dose reduction to 20.5%, followed by symptom resolution and gradual re-escalation to 27%.

Serious AEs, namely rhabdomyolysis and cardiomyopathy, were considered unrelated to triheptanoin, based on investigator judgment, the absence of a temporal association with dose escalation, and the presence of alternative etiologies, such as intercurrent infections or metabolic stress, consistent with the natural course of LC-FAOD.

Triheptanoin treatment was temporarily withheld in two patients on three occasions due to grade 3 or 4 major clinical events triggered by concurrent viral infections and was resumed after clinical stabilization. No deaths occurred during the study period, and no clinically significant changes in vital signs, physical examination, or laboratory test results were observed at scheduled follow-up assessments.

Efficacy and clinical outcomes

A total of 66 MCEs occurred in the 2-year period prior to triheptanoin and 82 occurred in the overall triheptanoin period, with a mean treatment duration of 3.5 years (Fig. 1). Following treatment initiation, all MCEs required hospitalization, with rhabdomyolysis accounting for all initial causes of admission. The median (IQR) annualized MCE rate decreased by 42.4%, from 3.4 (1.5–5.3) events/year in the pre-treatment period to 2.0 (1.1–3.1) events/year during triheptanoin treatment; however, this reduction did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.114; Table 3). The median (IQR) annualized MCE duration was reduced by 43.6%, from 26.3 (11.3–69.6) days/year to 14.8 (9.7–26.3) days/year, showing a borderline reduction that did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.059). In contrast, MCE duration per event was significantly reduced by 3.5 days after treatment, as assessed by a linear mixed-effects model (95% CI, -6.4 to -0.5, P = 0.022). In addition, the treatment effect on MCE duration per event differed significantly between age groups (period × age group interaction, P = 0.028), with a significant reduction observed only in the child group (β = -6.7 days; 95% CI, -10.5 to -2.9; P < 0.001) but not in the adolescent/adult group. No significant association was observed between the magnitude of increase in medium-chain fat–derived caloric proportion and changes in MCE outcomes.

Cardiac function remained stable throughout the follow-up period. There were no statistically significant changes in ejection fraction (from 64.1 ± 5.0% at baseline to 67.4 ± 4.3% at 3 years; P = 0.185) or in left ventricular mass index (LVMI) (68.2 ± 13.9 g/m2 at baseline to 69.4 ± 25.0 g/m2 at 3 years; P = 0.400). One patient showed a grade 1 increase in LVMI and was managed conservatively without medication. Pulmonary function, assessed by the FEV1/FVC ratio, also remained preserved over time (from 96.7 ± 13.5% at baseline to 87.8 ± 12.6% at 3 years; P = 0.207), including one patient who required nocturnal positive airway pressure support at baseline.

One patient with TFP deficiency had pre-existing pigmentary retinopathy at baseline. New-onset pigmentary retinopathy was identified in another patient with TFP deficiency at the 1-year assessment. These findings were considered unrelated to triheptanoin treatment and were attributed to the underlying disorder. No further progression or deterioration was observed in either patient.

HRQoL assessments revealed that the SF-36 physical component summary score, reported by adult patients, was impaired at baseline (31.4 ± 15.7) and improved to within the normative range after 6 months of triheptanoin treatment (68.9 ± 16.5), remaining within the healthy reference range through 3 years (Fig. 3A); however, this change was not statistically significant. The mental component summary score also improved and remained within the healthy reference range throughout the treatment period.

At baseline, the mean PedsQL score was lower than the healthy reference range in both child self-reports (61.8 ± 24.1) and parent proxy-reports (61.6 ± 24.2) (Fig. 3B). Following treatment initiation, PedsQL scores increased to within the healthy range in both groups; however, from 1.5 years onward, scores began to decline and remained below the healthy range at 3 years. Regarding mental health outcomes, two patients with depressive symptoms at baseline showed marked improvements following triheptanoin therapy, as assessed by the BDI; however, at 3 years, depressive symptoms were identified in two patients, including one with recurrence and one with newly developed symptoms. In addition, four pediatric patients were newly diagnosed with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) based on the K-CBCL during follow-up.

Plasma acylcarnitine analysis revealed a significant increase in propionylcarnitine (C3) at 6 months after initiating triheptanoin therapy (0.4 ± 0.2 µmol/L at baseline vs. 1.6 ± 1.3 µmol/L at 6 months; P = 0.013). In addition, urine organic acid analysis showed significant increases in methylcitric acid and pimelic acid concentrations at 6 months compared with baseline (P = 0.007 and P = 0.028, respectively). These metabolic profiles appeared to be dose-dependent and were consistent with enhanced propionyl-CoA generation and increased anaplerotic flux via odd-chain fatty acid metabolism following triheptanoin therapy.

Discussion

In this study, triheptanoin treatment was associated with a significant reduction in the duration of individual MCEs, particularly in pediatric patients, suggesting a potential improvement in event severity. The previous clinical trials and real-world studies reported reductions in both MCE frequency and overall duration [14, 16, 27, 28]. Similar findings were observed in our study but with limited statistical significance; several factors may explain the discrepancy between our findings and those of prior studies. First, the 2-year pre-treatment baseline period overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic in Korea, during which a previous study reported reduced hospitalization rates associated with social distancing and reduced exposure to infectious triggers in pediatric patients [29]. Similar factors may have contributed to an underestimation of baseline MCE burden in our cohort. Second, following treatment initiation, patients—particularly in the pediatric group—may have experienced increased activity levels and exposure to potential triggers, which could have influenced the observed event frequency. Therefore, these pandemic-related changes may have reduced the apparent magnitude of treatment effect and affected statistical significance. Nevertheless, the observed reduction in MCE duration per event suggests a possible clinical benefit in terms of attenuating event severity. Furthermore, the lack of association between changes in medium-chain fat intake and MCE outcomes suggests that this effect is likely attributable to the proposed anaplerotic mechanism of triheptanoin in supporting TCA cycle replenishment and metabolic flexibility during catabolic stress.

Although three patients (30%) did not reach the predefined target dose of 35% of total daily caloric intake, dose limitation was driven by gastrointestinal tolerability rather than noncompliance or barriers to administration. The safety profile of triheptanoin in this study was consistent with that reported in prior studies, with mild gastrointestinal symptoms being the most common treatment-related AEs, typically transient and manageable through gradual titration [14]. Severe episodes of rhabdomyolysis occurred in 90% of patients but were deemed unrelated to triheptanoin, instead reflecting the natural course of the disease and underscoring the persistent vulnerability of these patients despite optimized therapy. No treatment-related SAEs or those leading to study discontinuation were observed, supporting the tolerability of triheptanoin over long-term administration.

Although triheptanoin did not significantly alter exercise performance as measured by the 12MWT, most patients demonstrated stable or modestly improved functional capacity over 3 years, consistent with previous studies [13, 14]. However, one adult patient with progressive peripheral neuropathy showed reduced walking distance at 3 years compared with baseline. These findings suggest that triheptanoin may help stabilize functional outcomes but may not reverse secondary neuromuscular complications in advanced disease, highlighting the importance of treatment in the early stages of the disease course.

Consistent with previous long-term extension studies of triheptanoin, adult patients in our cohort showed improvement in the physical functioning domain after treatment [14, 16]. However, the previous study demonstrated no baseline impairment in psychosocial health and improvement in physical health based on caregiver reports. In contrast, both parent proxy-reported and child self-reported PedsQL scores showed baseline impairment, followed by an initial increase after treatment and a subsequent decline after 1.5 years. The early improvement in PedsQL scores may reflect an initial expectation effect associated with treatment initiation, whereas the later decline likely reflects the ongoing real-world disease burden. This may be related to the persistent challenges of long-term administration of triheptanoin and dietary management required in LC-FAOD, as well as the limited effect of triheptanoin in preventing the metabolic decompensation events and disease progression [30].

A previous study reported that, even in the absence of severe neurologic sequelae, children with LC-FAOD diagnosed through newborn screening frequently experience subtle developmental and neuropsychological deficits [31]. Additionally, emerging evidence suggests that mitochondrial dysfunction may contribute to the pathophysiology of ADHD [32]. In the present study, four pediatric patients were newly diagnosed with ADHD during follow-up. This observation suggests that, although triheptanoin may improve systemic energy balance, its impact on neurodevelopmental trajectories remains uncertain. Therefore, long-term prospective studies are warranted to determine whether triheptanoin adequately supports neurocognitive and behavioral outcomes in this population.

This study has several limitations. First, the small sample size and single-center design limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the single-arm, open-label design without a concurrent control group, together with the use of retrospective pre-treatment baseline comparisons, limits causal interpretation. Accordingly, the observed changes in clinical outcomes should be interpreted descriptively. In addition, although exploratory analyses did not demonstrate a statistically significant association between the magnitude of caloric increase and changes in MCE frequency or duration, the potential contribution of increased medium-chain fat intake to the observed reduction in MCE burden cannot be fully excluded and represents an inherent limitation of this single-arm study. Third, the open-label design may have introduced bias in subjective outcomes, including HRQoL. Finally, heterogeneity in age and clinical severity, as well as the limited number of patients completing certain assessments and the limitations of specific outcome measures, such as creatine kinase, may have contributed to variability in treatment response.

Despite these limitations, this study provides important clinical insights as the first prospective phase II trial of triheptanoin in an Asian cohort with genetically confirmed LC-FAOD. Our findings demonstrate a significant reduction in MCE duration per event, particularly in pediatric patients, supporting a clinically meaningful effect on event severity. These results contribute valuable real-world evidence supporting the long-term safety and clinical utility of triheptanoin in the management of LC-FAOD.

Conclusion

This prospective phase II study provides descriptive data on the long-term safety and clinical outcomes of triheptanoin therapy in a Korean cohort of patients with LC-FAOD. Triheptanoin was generally well- tolerated over a 3-year treatment period, with no treatment-related serious adverse events and predominantly mild, manageable gastrointestinal symptoms. Treatment was associated with a significant reduction in the duration of individual MCEs, particularly in pediatric patients, indicating a beneficial effect on event severity. Given its favorable safety profile and potential anaplerotic effects, triheptanoin represents a valuable therapeutic option for patients with LC-FAOD. Further large-scale, controlled studies are warranted to confirm these findings and to better define its long-term impact on clinical outcomes and quality of life.

Abstract

Introduction

T. Regge has found 65 years ago
[
13
]
, that the
su(2)

6j
-symbols

\big\{\,{}^{j_{1}}_{j_{3}}\,{}^{j_{2}}_{j_{4}}\,\big|\,{}^{j_{12}}_{j_{34}}\big\}

besides obvious invariance under any permutations of the columns and simultaneous interchange of upper and lower arguments in any two columns, additionally possess less trivial discrete symmetry

\big\{\,{}^{j_{1}}_{j_{3}}\,{}^{j_{2}}_{j_{4}}\,\big|\,{}^{j_{12}}_{j_{23}}\big\}=\big\{\,{}^{S-j_{1}}_{S-j_{3}}\,{}^{S-j_{2}}_{S-j_{4}}\,\big|\,{}^{j_{12}}_{j_{23}}\big\}\,,

where
S={1\over 2}(j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4})
.
In other groundbreaking work
[
12
]
, Ponzano and Regge expanding on work of Wigner
[
19
]
, conjectured remarkable asymptotic formula relating the value of the
6j
-symbols in the limit of the large spins to
the volume of the associated Euclidean tetrahedron. This conjecture was proved in
[
15
]
.
Combining these two facts one arrives to the invariance
of the tetrahedron volume under the Regge’s transformation. It is worth to emphasize that this property of the tetrahedron volume
was unknown before the
Regge’s works. Geometric proof can be found in
[
1
]
.

It turns out that the Regge symmetry persists when we consider the
6j
-symbols of the various
generalization of
su(2)
algebra.
First it was checked for the
6j
-symbols of finite-dimensional representations of quantum algebra
{\rm U}_{\rm q}({\rm su}(2))

[
8
,
9
]
.
Recently it was established in
[
2
]
, that
6j
-symbols for the unitary principal series representations of the Faddeev modular double constructed
in
[
11
]
, enjoy the Regge symmetry as well.

In this paper we study generalization of the Regge transformation for the
6j
-symbols of unitary principal series representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group constructed
in
[
7
,
4
]
.

Symmetries of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

6j
-symbols were studied
in papers
[
5
,
16
]
.
Here we derive one more symmetry which in fact is composition of that symmetries. But we prefer to derive it here by a limiting
procedure from the corresponding symmetry property of the above mentioned
6j
-symbols of the Faddeev modular double, found in
[
18
]
,
and re-derived and presented in
[
2
]
in much more simple and convenient for the application way.
Using this symmetry property we derive new expression for the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

6j
-symbols
which allows us to deduce the corresponding Regge symmetry.

6j-symbols for Lorentz group

The unitary principal series
representations of the Lorentz group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
are labelled by pairs
(\sigma,N)
where
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The group element

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{array}\right),\quad\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma=1

is represented by operator
T_{a}(g)
acting on the space of the square-integrable single-valued functions
\Phi(z,\bar{z})
on

\mathbb{C}
in the following way
[
6
]
:

[T_{a}(g)\Phi](z,\bar{z})=(\beta z+\delta)^{a-1}(\bar{\beta}\bar{z}+\bar{\delta})^{\bar{a}-1}\Phi\left({\alpha z+\gamma\over\beta z+\delta},{\bar{\alpha}\bar{z}+\bar{\gamma}\over\bar{\beta}\bar{z}+\bar{\delta}}\right)\,.

For the unitary principal series representations one has

a={N\over 2}+i\sigma,\quad\bar{a}=-{N\over 2}+i\sigma,\quad N\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\,.

To write down the 6j-symbols for the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group we need the complex gamma function
[
6
]
:

{\bf\Gamma}(x,n)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+\textup{i}x}{2})}{\Gamma(1+\frac{n-\textup{i}x}{2})},

(1)

where
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Define also so called complex hypergeometric function
[
5
]
:

\mathcal{J}_{cr}(\underline{s},\underline{n};\underline{t},\underline{m})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{a=1}^{4}{\bf\Gamma}(s_{a}-y,n_{a}-N){\bf\Gamma}(t_{a}+y,m_{a}+N)dy,

(2)

where the parameters

n_{a}
,
m_{a}
,
s_{a}
and
t_{a}
satisfy the balancing condition:

\sum_{a=1}^{4}(n_{a}+m_{a})=0,\qquad\sum_{a=1}^{4}(s_{a}+t_{a})=-4\textup{i}.

(3)

Using these definitions the 6j-symbols for
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group can be written as
[
7
,
4
,
5
]
:

\displaystyle\makebox[-10.00002pt]{}\big\{\,{}^{\sigma_{1},N_{1}}_{\sigma_{3},N_{3}}\,{}^{\sigma_{2},N_{2}}_{{\sigma}_{4},N_{4}}\,|\,{}^{\rho_{1},M_{1}}_{\rho_{2},M_{2}}\big\}={\pi^{2}\over 4}{{\bf\Gamma}\left(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}+\rho_{2}-\textup{i},A_{1}\right){\bf\Gamma}\left(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}+\rho_{1}-\textup{i},A_{2}\right)\over{\bf\Gamma}\left(-\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{4}+\rho_{2}-\textup{i},A_{3}\right){\bf\Gamma}\left(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{4}+\rho_{1}-\textup{i},A_{4}\right)}

\displaystyle\makebox[-20.00003pt]{}\times(-1)^{M_{2}-N_{2}+N_{4}}{\mathcal{J}}_{cr}(\underline{R},\underline{S};\underline{U},\underline{T})\,,

(4)

where the function
{\mathcal{J}}_{cr}(\underline{R},\underline{S};\underline{U},\underline{T})
is defined by formula (
2
),
and

\begin{aligned} &R_{1}=-\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},\\
&R_{2}=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},\\
&R_{3}=-\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{4}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},\\
&R_{4}=\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{4}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},\end{aligned}\quad\begin{aligned} &U_{1}=-\rho_{1}-\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{4}+\rho_{2},\\
&U_{2}=\rho_{1}-\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{4}+\rho_{2},\\
&U_{3}=0,\\
&U_{4}=2\rho_{2},\end{aligned}\quad\begin{aligned} &S_{1}=(-N_{1}+N_{2}-M_{2})/2,\\
&S_{2}=(N_{1}+N_{2}-M_{2})/2,\\
&S_{3}=-(N_{3}+N_{4}+M_{2})/2,\\
&S_{4}=(N_{3}-N_{4}-M_{2})/2,\end{aligned}

(5)

T_{1}=(-M_{1}-N_{2}+N_{4}+M_{2})/2,\quad T_{2}=(M_{1}-N_{2}+N_{4}+M_{2})/2,\quad T_{3}=0,\quad T_{4}=M_{2},

(6)

and

A_{1}={N_{1}-N_{2}+M_{2}\over 2},\qquad A_{2}={N_{2}-N_{3}+M_{1}\over 2},

A_{3}={-N_{3}-N_{4}+M_{2}\over 2},\qquad A_{4}={N_{1}+N_{4}+M_{1}\over 2}.

Note that the parameters
R_{a},U_{a},S_{a},T_{a}
as they are defined in (
5
) and (
6
) indeed satisfy the relation (
3
):

\sum_{a=1}^{4}(R_{a}+U_{a})=-4\textup{i}\quad{\rm and}\quad\sum_{a=1}^{4}(S_{a}+T_{a})=0\,.

(7)

The discrete parameters
N_{k}
,
k=1,2,3,4
and
M_{1}
,

10
]
to take only the values bringing
to integer values of

,

, k=1,2,3,4. In fact it is easy to see that it is enough to guarantee
that

J_{h}(\underline{\mu},\underline{\nu})=\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\prod_{a=1}^{4}\gamma^{(2)}(\mu_{a}-z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\gamma^{(2)}(\nu_{a}+z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}){dz\over i\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}\,,

(8)

where the parameters
\mu_{a}
,
\nu_{a}

\sum_{a=1}^{4}(\nu_{a}+\mu_{a})=2(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\equiv 2Q\,,

(9)

and
\gamma^{(2)}(x;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is hyperbolic gamma function, also sometimes called quantum dilogarithm. For definition of the hyperbolic gamma function
\gamma^{(2)}(x;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

It was shown in

\displaystyle J_{h}(\underline{\mu},\underline{\nu})=\prod_{j,k=1}^{2}\gamma^{(2)}(\nu_{j}+\mu_{k+2};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\prod_{j,k=1}^{2}\gamma^{(2)}(\nu_{j+2}+\mu_{k};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})J_{h}(\underline{\tilde{\mu}},\underline{\tilde{\nu}})\,,

\tilde{\mu}_{1}=G-\nu_{1},\quad\tilde{\mu}_{2}=G-\nu_{2},\quad\tilde{\mu}_{3}=Q-\nu_{3},\quad\tilde{\mu}_{4}=Q-\nu_{4},

\tilde{\nu}_{1}=-\mu_{1},\quad\tilde{\nu}_{2}=-\mu_{2},\quad\tilde{\nu}_{3}=Q-G-\mu_{3},\quad\tilde{\nu}_{4}=Q-G-\mu_{4},

.

Now consider the limit:

\sqrt{\omega_{1}\over\omega_{2}}=\textup{i}+\delta,\quad\delta\to 0\,.

(11)

In this limit one has as well

\omega_{1}=i\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(1-i\delta),\;\omega_{2}=i\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(-1-i\delta),\;Q=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=-2i\delta(i\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}),\;\sqrt{\omega_{2}\over\omega_{1}}=-\textup{i}+\delta.

]

\gamma^{(2)}(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(n+x\delta);\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\ \mathrel{\mathop{=}\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}}e^{\frac{\pi\textup{i}}{2}n^{2}}(4\pi\delta)^{\textup{i}x-1}{\bf\Gamma}(x,n),

(13)

where

We parameterize them in the same manner:

z_{1}=\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(L_{1}+y_{1}\delta),\quad z_{2}=\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(L_{2}+y_{2}\delta)\,,

Taking into account that in this parametrization

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{cr}(\underline{s},\underline{n};\underline{t},\underline{m})=e^{\pi\textup{i}K}\prod_{j,k=1}^{2}{\bf\Gamma}(t_{j}+s_{k+2},m_{j}+n_{k+2})\prod_{j,k=1}^{2}{\bf\Gamma}(t_{j+2}+s_{k},m_{j+2}+n_{k})\mathcal{J}_{cr}(\underline{\tilde{s}},\underline{\tilde{n}};\underline{\tilde{t}},\underline{\tilde{m}}).\quad

(16)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{1}=Y-t_{1},\,\tilde{n}_{1}=K-m_{1},\,\tilde{s}_{2}=Y-t_{2},\,\tilde{n}_{2}=K-m_{2},\,\tilde{s}_{3}=-2\mathrm{i}-t_{3},\,\tilde{n}_{3}=-m_{3},

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{4}=-2\mathrm{i}-t_{4},\,\tilde{n}_{4}=-m_{4},\,\tilde{t}_{1}=-s_{1},\,\tilde{m}_{1}=-n_{1},\,\tilde{t}_{2}=-s_{2},\,\tilde{m}_{2}=-n_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{3}=-2i-Y-s_{3},\,\tilde{m}_{3}=-K-n_{3},\,\tilde{t}_{4}=-2i-Y-s_{4},\,\tilde{m}_{4}=-K-n_{4},\,.

The function
\mathcal{J}_{cr}(\underline{s},\underline{n};\underline{t},\underline{m})
here is defined in (
2
).
It is easy to see that new parameters satisfy the balancing condition (

\sum_{a=1}^{4}(\tilde{n}_{a}+\tilde{m}_{a})=0,\qquad\sum_{a=1}^{4}(\tilde{s}_{a}+\tilde{t}_{a})=-4\textup{i}.

(18)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\underline{\sigma},\underline{\rho},\underline{N},\underline{M})=

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}(\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{2}+\rho_{1}-\textup{i},(N_{3}-N_{2}+M_{1})/2){\bf\Gamma}(\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{4}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},(N_{3}-N_{4}-M_{2})/2)\qquad

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}+\rho_{1}-\textup{i},(N_{2}-N_{3}+M_{1})/2){\bf\Gamma}(\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}-\rho_{2}-\textup{i},(N_{2}+N_{1}-M_{2})/2)\,.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}={1\over 2}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{4})\,,

and

are integer, the equality

is integer,
and we are allowed to define the following analogue of the Regge transformation:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\to{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{1},\quad\sigma_{2}\to{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{2},\quad\sigma_{3}\to{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{3},\quad\sigma_{4}\to-({\mathcal{S}}+\sigma_{4})\,,

(31)

\displaystyle N_{1}\to{\mathcal{N}}-N_{1},\;N_{2}\to{\mathcal{N}}-N_{2},\;N_{3}\to{\mathcal{N}}-N_{3},\;N_{4}\to-({\mathcal{N}}+N_{4})\,.

N_{1}+N_{3}\to N_{2}-N_{4}\quad{\rm and}\quad-N_{2}+N_{4}\to-N_{1}-N_{3}\,.

(32)

is even, the first factor is invariant under (

by sign
(-1)^{(N_{1}+N_{3}-N_{2}+N_{4})/2}

) the
6j
-symbols (
4
)
may acquire only a sign:

\displaystyle\big\{\,{}^{{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{1},{\mathcal{N}}-N_{1}}_{{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{3},{\mathcal{N}}-N_{3}}\,{}^{{\mathcal{S}}-\sigma_{2},{\mathcal{N}}-N_{2}}_{{-({\mathcal{S}}+\sigma}_{4}),-({\mathcal{N}}+N_{4})}\,|\,{}^{\rho_{1},M_{1}}_{\rho_{2},M_{2}}\big\}=(-1)^{(N_{1}+N_{3}-N_{2}+N_{4})/2}\big\{\,{}^{\sigma_{1},N_{1}}_{\sigma_{3},N_{3}}\,{}^{\sigma_{2},N_{2}}_{{\sigma}_{4},N_{4}}\,|\,{}^{\rho_{1},M_{1}}_{\rho_{2},M_{2}}\big\}\,.

Thus we have established that the
6j
-symbols for the unitary principal series representations of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group are
invariant under the action of the generalization of the Regge transformation (
31
) up to the possible sign factor.

3
Conclusion

In this paper we obtain new symmetry transformation (
16
) of the complex hypergeometric function (
2
),
which is essential part of the
6j
-symbols for the unitary principal series representations of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
group.
This allowed us to obtain new expression (
29
) for them. This expression makes obvious presence of the Regge
symmetry (
31
).
At the next step we are planning using the expression (
29
) to study asymptotic form of
6j
-symbols
for the large spins. We hope that it will uncover complex non-compact counterpart of the Ponzano-Regge asymptotic formulae.

Appendix B

Here we show that in the limit (

.

Consider the integral:

\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\Delta(z){dz\over\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}=\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\Delta(\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}x){dx\over\textup{i}},\quad x=\frac{z}{\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}},

where
\Delta(z)
is a product of
\gamma^{(2)}(u;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
.
We can rewrite it as an infinite sum

\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\Delta(\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,x){dx\over\textup{i}}=\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\textup{i}(N-1/2)}^{\textup{i}(N+1/2)}\Delta(\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,x){dx\over\textup{i}}

=\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{N-1/2}^{N+1/2}\Delta(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,x)dx=\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\Delta(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(N+x))dx.

At the second step we changed the variable
x\to\mathrm{i}x
and at the last step performed shift
x\to x+N
.
Now we parametrise
x=y\delta,\,\delta>0,
and take the limit
\delta\to 0^{+}
:

\makebox[-60.00009pt]{}{\rm lim}_{\delta\to 0}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\Delta(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(N+x))dx={\rm lim}_{\delta\to 0}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1/2\delta}^{1/2\delta}\delta\Delta(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(N+y\delta))dy.

(35)

The sum over
N
is infinite, for
\delta\to 0^{+}
the integration contour becomes real axis
(-\infty,\infty)
.
Using the uniform convergence of the limit (
13
) we interchange the limit
{\rm lim}_{\delta\to 0}
with
the summation and integration and end up with

\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[{\rm lim}_{\delta\to 0}\delta\Delta(\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}(N+y\delta))\right]dy.

(36)

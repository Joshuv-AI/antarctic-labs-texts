Extended Landen’s formulas and double product theta functions
Kiyoshi Sogo

Abstract
For an arbitrary positive integer p p , Landen’s formula is extended to express theta function with modulus p ​ τ p\tau by p p product of theta functions with τ \tau , which is applied to several examples. Next it is shown that double product of theta functions of genus g = 1 g=1 is written by a sum of g = 2 g=2 theta functions, which is a subset having a special period matrix of τ 11 = τ 22 \tau_{11}=\tau_{22} . Several applied examples are shown, which include the cubic identity of Borwein and Borwein.

1
Introduction

Landen’s formula which relates Jacobi’s theta functions with modulus
\tau
and
2\tau
, is given by
[
1
]

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(2u,2\tau)}{\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)\vartheta_{3}(u,\tau)}=\text{constant}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{4n})}{(1-q^{2n})^{2}},

(1.1)

where we set
q=e^{\pi i\tau}
. We have at least two types of proof for (
1.1
),
one is to use infinite product formulas for
\vartheta_{4}
and
\vartheta_{3}
,
another is to show zero points of numerator and denominator coincide, and apply Liouville’s theorem.
Let us remind the former proof. By setting
z=e^{\pi iu}
, we have
[
1
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})(1-q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1-q^{2n-1}z^{-2}),\\
\vartheta_{3}(u,\tau)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})(1+q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1+q^{2n-1}z^{-2}),\end{split}

(1.2)

then denominator (
D
) and numerator (
N
) of the left hand side of (
1.1
) become

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})^{2}(1-q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1-q^{2n-1}z^{-2})(1+q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1+q^{2n-1}z^{-2})

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})^{2}(1-q^{4n-2}z^{4})(1-q^{4n-2}z^{-4}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-(q^{2})^{2n})(1-(q^{2})^{2n-1}(z^{2})^{2})(1-(q^{2})^{2n-1}(z^{2})^{-2}),

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{4n})(1-q^{4n-2}z^{4})(1-q^{4n-2}z^{-4}),

(1.4)

therefore we obtain
N/D
by the right hand side of (
1.1
).

Now if we use a relation

\vartheta_{3}(u,\tau)=\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{1}{2},\tau)
,
we can rewrite the left hand side of (
1.1
) by

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(2u,2\tau)}{\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{1}{2},\tau)}=\text{constant}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{4n})}{(1-q^{2n})^{2}},

(1.5)

which makes easier to locate the zeros of denominator in the second proof.
Since the zero point of
\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)
in unit parallelogram
(0,1,\tau,1+\tau)

of quasi-periodicity is at
u=\frac{\tau}{2}
, zero points of denominator are at
u=\frac{\tau}{2}
and
u=\frac{1+\tau}{2}
which
are also zeros of numerator.

Then we notice that such proof can be extended further to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(3u,3\tau)}{\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{1}{3},\tau)\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{2}{3},\tau)}=\text{constant}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{6n})}{(1-q^{2n})^{3}},

(1.6)

which will be proved in the next section.
The first topic of the present paper is to discuss such generalizations of Landen’s formula and give some applied examples
in §2.

The second topic discussed in this paper was inspired by Kronecker’s paper (1883) on his famous limit formula
[
2
]
,
which says
two product of Jacobi’s theta functions (genus
g=1
) is related with Rosenhain’s
[
3
]
type theta function (
g=2
).
We will extend Kronecker’s procedure in §3, and find such theta functions are special
subset
of general

g=2
theta functions, period matrix of which has the form

\displaystyle\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{0}&\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{1}&\tau_{0}\end{array}\right),

having a symmetry of
\tau_{11}=\tau_{22}\equiv\tau_{0}
.
Being a subset can be understood by comparing degrees of freedom (dimension of moduli space), two versus three in full symmetry.
Several applications are given also in §3.
The last section §4 is devoted to summary.

2
Extensions of Landen’s formula

2.1
Higher order Landen’s type formulas

Let us begin this section by proving

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(3u,3\tau)}{\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{1}{3},\tau)\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{2}{3},\tau)}=\text{constant}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{6n})}{(1-q^{2n})^{3}}.

(2.1)

Since the zero points of denominator and numerator in unit parallelogram are commonly at

u=\frac{\tau}{2},\ \frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{1}{3},\ \frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{2}{3}
,
the left hand side is indeed a constant. The value is computed by using

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\vartheta_{4}(u,\tau)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})(1-q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1-q^{2n-1}z^{-2}),\\
&\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{1}{3},\tau)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})(1-\omega q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1-\omega^{2}q^{2n-1}z^{-2}),\\
&\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{2}{3},\tau)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})(1-\omega^{2}q^{2n-1}z^{2})(1-\omega q^{2n-1}z^{-2}),\end{split}

(2.2)

where we set
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
. Then the denominator (
D
) is given by

\displaystyle D=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n})^{3}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{6n-3}z^{6})(1-q^{6n-3}z^{-6}),

(2.3)

because of
(1-y)(1-\omega y)(1-\omega^{2}y)=1-y^{3}
, and the numerator (
N
) is given by

\displaystyle N=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{6n})(1-q^{6n-3}z^{6})(1-q^{6n-3}z^{-6}),

(2.4)

thus
N/D
becomes the right hand side of (
2.1
).

In the same way, generally for a positive integer
p
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(pu,\ p\tau)}{\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{k}{p},\tau)}=\text{constant}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2pn})}{(1-q^{2n})^{p}},

(2.5)

which is our goal of higher order Landen’s type formula, which can be derived similarly by using

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}(1-\omega^{k}y)=1-y^{p},\qquad(\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}).

(2.6)

By the way, the right hand side of (
2.5
) has a distinctive form, which can be expressed by
Dedekind’s eta function
[
4
]
defined by

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}).

(2.7)

It is supposed that this
q
in (
2.7
) is defined by
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
(do not be confused).
Hence the general Landen’s type formula (
2.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{4}(pu,p\tau)}{\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{4}(u+\frac{k}{p},\tau)}

\displaystyle=\text{constant}=\frac{\vartheta_{4}(0,p\tau)}{\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{4}(\frac{k}{p},\tau)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{pn})}{(1-q^{n})^{p}}=\frac{\eta(p\tau)}{\eta^{p}(\tau)},\quad(q=e^{2\pi i\tau})

(2.8)

which may have some implications because of modular property of eta function.

It should be noted that if we change
u\rightarrow u+\frac{1}{2}
in (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2pn})}{(1-q^{2n})^{p}}=\begin{cases}&\frac{\vartheta_{3}(pu,\ p\tau)}{\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{3}(u+\frac{k}{p},\tau)}\quad(p=\text{odd})\\
&\frac{\vartheta_{4}(pu,\ p\tau)}{\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{3}(u+\frac{k}{p},\tau)}\quad(p=\text{even}),\end{cases}

(2.9)

where the numerator of the right hand side is different depending on the parity (even-odd) of
p
.
Further by using other transformations
u\rightarrow u+\frac{\tau}{2}
or
u\rightarrow u+\frac{1+\tau}{2}
,
we can change from
\vartheta_{4}
to
\vartheta_{1}
or
\vartheta_{2}
, results of which are omitted here;
see however Example 2.2 below.
Anyway the formula (
2.5
) using
\vartheta_{4}
is the simplest.

3
Double product of theta functions

3.1
Extension of Kronecker’s procedure

In this section we extend Kronecker’s idea
[
2
]
further, to consider double product of theta functions.
Although Kronecker considered double product of
\vartheta_{1}
functions,
here we begin by considering double product of
\vartheta_{3}
functions

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{3}(y,w_{2})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m,n\in{\bf Z}}{\bf e}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(w_{1}m^{2}+w_{2}n^{2}\right)+(mx+ny)\right],

(3.1)

where inequalities
\text{Im}(w_{1}),\ \text{Im}(w_{2})>0
are assumed as usual.
Let us consider a transformation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&m+n=M,\quad m-n=N\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=\frac{M+N}{2},\quad n=\frac{M-N}{2},\end{split}

(3.2)

which imply integers
M,N
are even or odd simultaneously. Then by rewriting

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[w_{1}\left(\frac{M+N}{2}\right)^{2}+w_{2}\left(\frac{M-N}{2}\right)^{2}\right]+\left(\frac{M+N}{2}x+\frac{M-N}{2}y\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}}{4}(M^{2}+N^{2})+\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}}{4}\ 2MN\right]+\left(M\frac{x+y}{2}+N\frac{x-y}{2}\right),

we have, by writing
(M,N)=(2m,2n)
and
(M,N)=(2m+1,2n+1)
,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{3}(y,w_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in{\bf Z}}{\bf e}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\{(w_{1}+w_{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})+(w_{1}-w_{2})\ 2mn\right\}\right]\times

\displaystyle\times{\bf e}\left[m(x+y)+n(x-y)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{m-\frac{1}{2},n-\frac{1}{2}\in{\bf Z}}{\bf e}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\{(w_{1}+w_{2})(m^{2}+n^{2})+(w_{1}-w_{2})\ 2mn\right\}\right]\times

\displaystyle\times{\bf e}\left[m(x+y)+n(x-y)\right]

\displaystyle=\vartheta\binom{00}{00}[(x+y,x-y),\tau]+\vartheta\binom{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}}{00}[(x+y,x-y),\tau],

(3.3)

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}w_{1}+w_{2}&w_{1}-w_{2}\\
w_{1}-w_{2}&w_{1}+w_{2}\end{array}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{0}&\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{1}&\tau_{0}\end{array}\right).

Here general two variables theta function (
g=2

[

\displaystyle\vartheta\binom{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}\left[\zeta,\tau\right]=\sum_{m-\alpha\in{\bf Z}^{2}}{\bf e}\left[\frac{1}{2}\ m\tau\cdot m+m\cdot(\zeta+\beta)\right],

(3.8)

\displaystyle\text{with}\ m=(m_{1},m_{2}),\ \alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),\ \zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}),\ \beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\text{and}\ \tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{11}&\tau_{12}\\
\tau_{21}&\tau_{22}\end{array}\right),\ \tau_{21}=\tau_{12},

where abbreviation of the inner product
(a_{1},a_{2})\cdot(b_{1},b_{2})=a_{1}b_{1}+a_{2}b_{2}
is used.
Parameters
\binom{\alpha}{\beta}
are called

.

By substituting

(3.12)

because
\vartheta_{3}(x+\frac{1}{2},w)=\vartheta_{4}(x,w)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\vartheta\binom{00}{00}[(u+1,v),\tau]=+\vartheta\binom{00}{00}[(u,v),\tau],\\
&\vartheta\binom{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}}{00}[(u+1,v),\tau]=-\vartheta\binom{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}}{00}[(u,v),\tau].\end{split}

(3.13)

Therefore we obtain inversely

\displaystyle\vartheta\binom{00}{00}[(x+y,x-y),\tau]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\vartheta_{3}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{3}(y,w_{2})+\vartheta_{4}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{4}(y,w_{2})\right\},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\vartheta\binom{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}}{00}[(x+y,x-y),\tau]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\vartheta_{3}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{3}(y,w_{2})-\vartheta_{4}(x,w_{1})\vartheta_{4}(y,w_{2})\right\},

(3.15)

under the restriction that
\tau
has a symmetry of
\tau_{11}=\tau_{22}
(a subset of general
\tau

\displaystyle\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}w_{1}+w_{2}&w_{1}-w_{2}\\
w_{1}-w_{2}&w_{1}+w_{2}\end{array}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{0}&\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{1}&\tau_{0}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle w_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}\right),\quad w_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau_{0}-\tau_{1}\right).

(3.20)

Now again similarly

4
Summary

Two topics of theta functions are considered, the first is to extend Landen’s formula to higher orders,
and the second is to express double product of genus
g=1
theta functions by a sum of
g=2
theta functions, and
inversely to express a subset of
g=2
theta functions by a sum of products of
g=1
theta functions.

Our result of Landen’s type formulas is summarized by (
2.5
), which is applied to
three examples, some are known results and some are new ones, generalizations of results by Farkas and Kra
[
10
]
.

Results of double products of theta functions are given by (
3.3
), (
3.12
), (
3.21
), (
3.22
)
and their inverses, which are applied to five examples.
Especially two of them, Example 3.4 and 3.5, are related with the problem extending the cubic
identity by Borwein brothers
[
14
]
, which is connected to theta constants identity (
3.46
) and theta relations.
Such problem is answered in another paper by the author, which proves general theta relations,

i.e.
identities among arbitrary
n
products of theta functions with any genus
g
.

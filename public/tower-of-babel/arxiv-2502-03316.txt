Abstract
. A well-known Γ θ \Gamma_{\theta} -action on the characters of integrable highest weight modules over the affine Lie algebra of type B ​ C l ( 2 ) BC_{l}^{(2)} at a positive level is extended to an SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) -action at a positive even level by supplementing their twisted characters.



Introduction

In 1972, I. G. Macdonald
[
9
]
classified the so-called (not necessarily reduced) affine root systems and obtained some identities on the Fourier expansions of products of the Dedekind
\eta
-function associated with each affine root systems. The identities he obtained are named after him. Independently, F. Bruhat and J. Tits
[
1
]
classified affine root systems, where some of their results are explained in a survey article
[
13
]
.

To obtain the Macdonald identities, he started from an identity, that looks like a denominator identity up to some factor, called “missing factor”. Later, it was shown by several authors (see, e.g., our expository note
[
3
]
for the references), that the “missing factor” corresponds to the imaginary roots and the identity Macdonald used is the denominator identity of an affine Lie algebra. It would be a natural question to see what are the nature of the characters of integrable highest weight modules over an affine Lie algebra. Indeed, E. Looijenga
[
8
]
interpreted
a character of an affine Lie algebra of untwisted type as a section of a line bundle over an abelian variety, which is a product of an elliptic curve. This fact inspired V. G. Kac and D. H. Peterson, indeed, in 1984, they
[
7
]
had studied “modular properties” of the characters of affine Lie algebras.
They showed the following:

(1)

for an untwisted affine Lie algebra, the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the
\mathbb{C}
-span of the normalized characters of each fixed central charge and

(2)

for a twisted affine Lie algebra, a congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the
\mathbb{C}
-span of the of the normalized characters of each fixed central charge

via “modular transformation”. For
BC_{l}^{(2)}
(or
A_{2l}^{(2)}
), this congruence subgroup is the so-called
\theta
-subgroup
\Gamma_{\theta}
. Here, the characters have been regarded as functions on a certain finite dimensional complex subdomain
Y
of the affine Lie algebra of type
BC_{l}^{(2)}
.

In Macdonald’s paper
[
9
]
, he considered several possible evaluations of the denominator identities. Indeed, for
BC_{l}^{(2)}
, in its Appendix 1, Type
BC_{l}
, (6), he treated
4
different evaluations which concluded with
4
different
\eta
-products. The case treated by V. Kac and D. Peterson corresponds to the case (b) (cf. Remark
3.2
). By computing the modular transformations of these
4

\eta
-products, we observed that there seems to be a bigger hidden symmetry in
BC_{l}^{(2)}
, which inspired us to work on the subject of this article.

In this article, we show that the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the vector space spanned by the characters and twisted-characters of integrable highest weight modules over the affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of type
BC_{l}^{(2)}
, for each fixed
even
level. Here, we should consider the characters and twisted-characters as functions on a complex subdomain of the dual of the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and introduce
two
coordinate systems dependent on the choice of special indices.
Moreover, we observe that the characters and twisted-characters of the affine Lie algebra of type
BC_{l}^{(2)}
at
even
level can be identified with the characters and super-characters of the affine Lie superalgebra of type
B^{(1)}(0,l)
!

This article is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we recall some basic notions and setting on
BC_{l}^{(2)}
, in particular, depending on the choice of a special index (see Definition
1.1
), we present two coordinate systems of a complex domain in the dual of the Cartan subalgebra of the affine Lie algebra of type
BC_{l}^{(2)}
.
In Section
2
, we recall the definition of the (twisted-)character of an object of the category
\mathcal{O}
of Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand. In Sections 3, we introduce theta-series and twisted invariants of the affine Weyl group of type
BC_{l}^{(2)}
and express the normalized (twisted-)characters in terms these (twisted-)invariants. In Section
4
, we compute the modular transformations of (twisted-)characters. Finally, in Section 5, we interpret the computational results obtained in the previous sections. We conclude with an appendix explaining some relevant basic facts about Lie superalgebras, for the sake of reader’s convenience.

Such an example is given as follows.

-module

4.1.
Poisson’s resummation formula

Let
E=\mathbb{R}^{l}
be an affine space and
I
be a positive definite symmetric bilinear form on
E
. For a full sublattice
M
of
E
, denote by
\mathrm{Vol}(M)
the discriminant of
M
, i.e., the volume of
E/M
. For a rapidly decreasing function
f:E\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\underline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, set

\hat{f}(\underline{y}):=\int_{E}f(\underline{x})e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}I(\underline{x},\underline{y})}d\underline{x}.

The so-called Poisson’s resummation formula is described as follows:

(4.1.1)

\sum_{\underline{m}\in M}f(\underline{m})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\underline{m}\in M^{\vee}}\hat{f}(\underline{m}).

Here,

\lx@nobreakspace M^{\vee}:=\big\{\,\underline{n}\in E\,\big|\,I(\underline{m},\underline{n})\in\mathbb{Z},\;\forall\,\underline{m}\in M\,\big\}

is the dual lattice of
M
. In particular, for a self-dual lattice
M
,
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{l}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
f(\underline{x}):=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}\big(-\frac{1}{\tau}\big)\left|\!\left|{\underline{x}+\underline{a}}\right|\!\right|^{2}}
, one obtains

(4.1.2)

\sum_{\underline{m}\in M}e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}\big(-\frac{1}{\tau}\big)\left|\!\left|{\underline{m}+\underline{a}}\right|\!\right|^{2}}=\Big(\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{-1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}l}\sum_{\underline{m}\in M}e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}\tau\left|\!\left|{\underline{m}}\right|\!\right|^{2}+2\pi\sqrt{-1}I(\underline{a},\underline{m})}.

See, e.g.,
[
12
]
for more information.

A.1.
Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
and its irreducible representations

The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
is the superspace

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}:=\mathbb{C}E\oplus\mathbb{C}H\oplus\mathbb{C}F,\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}:=\mathbb{C}e\oplus\mathbb{C}f

equipped with the Lie super-bracket
[\,\cdot,\cdot\,]
satisfying

\displaystyle[H,E]=4E,\qquad[H,F]=-4F,\qquad[E,F]=2H,

\displaystyle[H,e]=2e,\qquad[H,f]=-2f,

\displaystyle[e,f]=H,\qquad[e,e]=2E,\qquad[f,f]=-2F.

Let
\alpha\in(\mathbb{C}H)^{\ast}
such that
\alpha(H)=2
. Then, it is clear that
\{\pm\alpha\}\cup\{\pm 2\alpha\}
is the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}H
.
Set
\mathfrak{b}=\mathbb{C}H\oplus\mathbb{C}e\oplus\mathbb{C}E
. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, let
\mathbb{C}v_{\lambda}
be the even
\mathfrak{b}
-module defined by

H.v_{\lambda}=\lambda(H)v_{\lambda},\qquad e.v_{\lambda}=0.

It is clear that this implies
E.v_{\lambda}=0
. The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M(\lambda):=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}v_{\lambda}
is the Verma module with highest weight
\lambda
.
For
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, set
w_{i}=\dfrac{1}{i!}f^{i}\otimes v_{\lambda}
. By direct computation, one sees

\displaystyle H.w_{i}=(\lambda(H)-2i)w_{i},\qquad e.w_{i}=\begin{cases}\;-w_{i-1}\;&i\equiv 0\mod 2,\\
\;i(\lambda(H)-i+1)w_{i-1}\;&i\equiv 1\mod 2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle f.w_{i}=(i+1)w_{i+1}.

Hence, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if
\lambda(H)\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, in which case the subspace
N(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{i>\lambda(H)}\mathbb{C}w_{i}
gives the maximal proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule. Denote by
L(\lambda)
its irreducible quotient.
The next lemma is important:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
V
be an irreducible finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then, there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
V\cong L(N\alpha)
. In particular,
V
is of odd dimension.

A.2.
Lie superalgebra of type
B^{(1)}(0,l)

Fix a finite set
I
. Recall that a Kac Moody Lie algebra is a Lie algebra associated with a GCM
A=(a_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
, i.e., a square matrix with
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that i)
a_{i,i}=2
for any
i\in I
, ii)
a_{i,j}\leq 0
for any
i\neq j
and iii)
a_{i,j}=0
implies
a_{j,i}=0
.
Its building blocks are isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
. Similarly, a class of Lie superalgebras had been considered in the article
[
5
]
associated with a pair
(A,\tau)
of a GCM
A
and the subset
\tau\subset I
satisfying the additional condition iv)
a_{i,j}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\in\tau
and
j\in I
. Its building blocks are isomorphic either to
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
or to
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
.

Recall the definition of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)
. Let
(\mathfrak{h},\Pi,\Pi^{\vee})
be a realization of the GCM
A
so that
\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\Pi^{\vee}=\{h_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are sets of linearly independent vectors satisfying
\langle\alpha_{i},h_{j}\rangle=a_{j,i}
for any
i,j\in I
.

\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)
is the Lie superalgebra generated by
e_{i},f_{i}
(
i\in I
) and
\mathfrak{h}
with the parity

|e_{i}|=|f_{i}|:=\begin{cases}\;\bar{0}\;&i\in I\setminus\tau,\\
\;\bar{1}\;&i\in\tau,\end{cases}\qquad|h|:=\bar{0}\quad h\in\mathfrak{h},

equipped with the Lie superbracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfying

(S1)

[h,h^{\prime}]=0
for
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(S2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[h,e_{i}]=\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle\,e_{i},\\[3.0pt]
[h,f_{i}]=-\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle\,f_{i},\end{array}\right.
for

h\in\mathfrak{h}
and
0\leq i\leq l
,

(S3)

[e_{i},f_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}h_{i}
for
0\leq i,j\leq l
,

(S4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{ad}\big(e_{i}\big)^{1-a_{i,j}}\big(e_{j}\big)=0,\\[3.0pt]
\mathrm{ad}\big(f_{i}\big)^{1-a_{i,j}}\big(f_{j}\big)=0,\end{array}\right.
for
0\leq i\neq j\leq l
.

A non-zero element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is called a root if

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}:=\big\{\,x\in\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)\,\big|\,[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\;\forall\,h\in\mathfrak{h}\,\big\}\neq\{0\}.

We denote the set of all roots by
\Delta
. We have the so-called root space decomposition with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
:

\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

A root
\alpha\in\Delta
is called
even
(resp.
odd
) if any non-zero element of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is even (resp.
odd
).
It is clear that a root
\alpha=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
is even iff so is
\sum_{i\in\tau}m_{i}
. Denote by

\Delta_{\bar{0}}
and
\Delta_{\bar{1}}
the set of even and odd roots, respectively. By definition, one has the following parity decomposition:

\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)=\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)_{\bar{1}},

where
\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{g}(A,\tau)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. For
i\in I
, Let
s_{\alpha_{i}}
be the reflection in
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
defined by
s_{\alpha_{i}}(x)=x-x(h_{i})\alpha_{i}
and
W(A)
be the subgroup of
GL(\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
generated by
s_{\alpha_{i}}
(
i\in I
). An element of
\Delta^{re}:=W.\Pi
(resp.
\Delta^{im}:=\Delta\setminus\Delta^{re}
) is called a
real
(resp. an
imaginary
) root.

The finite Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{f}
of type
B(0,l)
is, by definition, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}(A_{f},I_{f,odd})
associated with the Cartan matrix
A_{f}=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq l}
of type
B_{l}
, i.e.,

A_{f}={\small\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
-1&2&-1&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&-1&2&-1\\
0&\cdots&0&-2&2\end{pmatrix}},\qquad I_{f,odd}=\{l\}\subset I_{f}=\{1,2,\cdots,l\}.

This Lie superalgebra is also called
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2l)
.

Remark A.2

The root system
\Delta_{f}

Its parity decomposition is given as follows:
\Delta_{f}=(\Delta_{f})_{\bar{0}}\amalg(\Delta_{f})_{\bar{1}}

(\Delta_{f})_{\bar{0}}=(\Delta_{f})_{m}\amalg(\Delta_{f})_{l},\qquad(\Delta_{f})_{\bar{1}}=(\Delta_{f})_{s}.

The affine Lie superalgebra of type

A.3.
Integrability highest weight modules

A highest weight
\mathfrak{g}(A,I_{odd})
-module is defined similarly.
For
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, let
\mathbb{C}_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{C}v_{\Lambda}
be the one
dimensional
even
module over
\mathfrak{b}_{+}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}
defined by

h.v_{\Lambda}=\langle h,\Lambda\rangle v_{\lambda}\quad(h\in\mathfrak{h}),\qquad\mathfrak{n}_{+}.v_{\Lambda}=0.

The induced
\mathfrak{g}(A,I_{odd})
-module

M(\Lambda):=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{b}_{+}}^{\mathfrak{g}(A,I_{odd})}\mathbb{C}_{\Lambda}
is called
the Verma module with highest weight
\Lambda
. The Verma module
M(\Lambda)
possesses the maximal proper submodule, say
N(\Lambda)
.
Denote its irreducible quotient

M(\Lambda)/N(\Lambda)
by
L(\Lambda)
. The next lemma can be shown, with the aide of Lemma
A.1
:

Lemma A.4
.

The highest weight
\mathfrak{g}(A,I_{odd})
-module
L(\Lambda)
is integrable if and only if
\Lambda(h_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
0\leq i<l
and
\Lambda(h_{l})\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, i.e.,
\Lambda\in P_{+}
and the level of
\Lambda
is even.

Note that the level of i-th fundamental weight
\Lambda_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is the co-label
a_{i}^{\vee}
.

As any highest weight module is
\mathfrak{h}
-diagonalisable, we can speak of its formal (super-)character which is an element of
\mathcal{E}
. For an object
V
of the cartegory
\mathcal{O}
, let
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(V)}V_{\lambda}
be its weight space decomposition. Its character
\operatorname{ch}(V)
and super-character
\operatorname{sch}(V)
are the elements of
\mathcal{E}
defined by

\operatorname{ch}(V)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(V)}(\dim V_{\lambda})e^{\lambda},\qquad\operatorname{sch}(V)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(V)}(\operatorname{sdim}V_{\lambda})e^{\lambda},

where for a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
E=E_{\bar{0}}\oplus E_{\bar{1}}
, we set

\operatorname{sdim}(E)=\dim E_{\bar{0}}-\dim E_{\bar{1}}.

Let
\rho:=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\in\Lambda_{i}
be a Weyl vector of
\mathfrak{g}(A,I_{odd})
. For a
\Lambda\in P_{+}
with even level,
the formal character of the irreducible highest weight module
L(\Lambda)
is given by the Weyl-Kac character formula:

\operatorname{ch}L(\Lambda)=\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\operatorname{ch}M(w(\Lambda+\rho)-\rho)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)e^{w(\Lambda+\rho)}}{\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)e^{w(\rho)}}.

This is exactly the same as for the affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
(cf. (
2.1.3
)).
Hence, the super-character of
L(\Lambda)
can be given by

\operatorname{sch}L(\Lambda)=\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\psi(w)\operatorname{sch}M(w(\Lambda+\rho)-\rho),

where the map
\psi:W\longrightarrow\{\pm 1\}
is defined in (
2.2.1
). By the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem, it follows that

\operatorname{sch}M(\lambda)=e^{\lambda}\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{1}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{0}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}}

which implies the super-denominator identity:

(A.3.1)

\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\psi(w)e^{w\rho}=e^{\rho}\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{0}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{1}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}}.

As a corollary, one sees that

\operatorname{sch}L(\Lambda)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\psi(w)e^{w(\Lambda+\rho)}}{\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\psi(w)e^{w(\rho)}}.

Thus, it is exactly the twisted-character (
2.2.2
) !

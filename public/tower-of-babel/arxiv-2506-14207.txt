Restriction problem for mod p p representations of GL 2 \text{GL}_{2} over a finite field
Eknath Ghate and Shubhanshi Gupta

Abstract
. Let 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}_{q}} be the finite field with q = p f q=p^{f} elements. We study the restriction of two classes of mod p p representations of G q = GL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 q ) {G_{q}}=\text{GL}_{2}({{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}) to G p = GL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 p ) {G_{p}}=\text{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}_{p}}) . We first study the restrictions of principal series which are obtained by induction from a Borel subgroup B q {B_{q}} . We then analyze the restrictions of inductions from an anisotropic torus T q {T_{q}} which are related to cuspidal representations. Complete decompositions are given in both cases according to the parity of f f . The proofs depend on writing down explicit orbit decompositions of G p \ G q / H {G_{p}}\backslash{G_{q}}/H where H = B q H={B_{q}} or T q {T_{q}} using the fact that G q / H {G_{q}}/H is an explicit orbit in a certain projective line, along with Mackey theory.

1.
Introduction

The
restriction problem
, which examines how representations behave when restricted from a group to a subgroup, is a fundamental topic in representation theory.
In general, the restriction of an irreducible representation of a group to a subgroup is neither irreducible nor completely reducible. The general theory by which the
restriction of an irreducible representation of a group decomposes into irreducible representations of its subgroup is explained by
branching rules
.
One is interested in computing the multiplicity with which an irreducible representation of the subgroup appears (as a subquotient) in the restriction of an irreducible
representation of the given group. More generally, one may ask how a member of a class of representations of a group decomposes under restriction into member of this class for the subgroup. In Kobayashi
[
7
]
, the author gives a survey of branching problems for complex representations of real reductive groups.
See also, for instance,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
for other aspects of the restriction problem.

The decomposition of a finite tensor product of representations of a group
G
into its irreducible representations can be considered as part of a restriction problem as it can be seen as the restriction of the representation of the group
G\times G\times\ldots\times G
(finitely many times) to the diagonal subgroup which is isomorphic to
G
. In this case, the branching is explained by what are called Clebsch-Gordan formulas.
This situation occurs naturally in the study of the restriction of irreducible mod
p
representations of finite groups of Lie type, and in particular in the study of the restriction of irreducible mod
p
representations (weights) of
\text{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
to
\text{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})
, where
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p^{f}}
is a finite extension of degree
f\geq 1
of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
with
p
elements. See, for instance,
[
5
]

for an explicit decomposition in the case that
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
is a degree
f=3
extension of
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
using the Clebsch-Gordan formula from
[
4
]
.

In this context, it would be interesting to study the restriction problem for a slightly more complicated but natural class of representations of these groups. Let

{G_{p}}\coloneqq\text{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}_{p}}\right)
and
{G_{q}}\coloneqq\text{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{F}_{q}}\right)
. Let
{B_{p}}
and
{B_{q}}
denote the Borel subgroups of all upper triangular matrices of

{G_{p}}
and
{G_{q}}
, respectively. A natural problem would be to
study the restriction to
{G_{p}}
of principal series representations of
{G_{q}}
obtained by inducing a mod
p
character of
{B_{q}}
. Similarly, let
T_{p}
and
T_{q}
denote the anisotropic
torus of
{G_{p}}
and
{G_{q}}
, respectively. One could also study the restrictions from
{G_{q}}
to
{G_{p}}
of representations induced from a mod
p
character of
{T_{q}}
. The latter representations are closely connected to the mod
p
reductions of complex cuspidal representations of
{G_{q}}
.

In this paper, we solve the restriction problem for both these classes of representations of
G_{q}
completely, by decomposing the restrictions into such representations of
{G_{p}}
(possibly twisted by the Steinberg representation of
{G_{p}}
). The explicit decompositions are contained in Theorems
2.5
and
3.4
which are the main results of this paper.
The key tool, of course, is Mackey’s restriction formula. However, to use it one needs to
make the orbit decomposition of
{G_{p}}\backslash{G_{q}}/H
reasonably explicit where
H={B_{q}}
or
{T_{q}}
. This is done using the projective line as a crutch, noting that
{G_{q}}/{B_{q}}\simeq{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}

and
{G_{q}}/{T_{q}}
is the orbit of an element in
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}})}
outside
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
. These orbit decompositions may be of independent interest.

2.
Restriction of principal series representations

Recall that for a mod
p
character
\chi
of
{B_{q}}
, the mod
p
principal series representation
of
{G_{q}}
is the induced representation
\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi
.
While the complete decomposition of the restriction of such a principal series representation of
{G_{q}}
to
{G_{p}}
is not immediately evident, one might expect
that the following related principal series of
{G_{p}}
be contained in its restriction to
{G_{p}}
:

\text{ind}_{{B_{p}}}^{{G_{p}}}\left({\chi|}_{{B_{p}}}\right)\hookrightarrow{\left.\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi\right|}_{{G_{p}}}.

By Frobenius reciprocity, this is equivalent to checking whether
{\chi|}_{{B_{p}}}\hookrightarrow{\left.\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}{\chi}\right|}_{{B_{p}}}
,
which is evidently true as
\chi\hookrightarrow{\left.\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}{\chi}\right|}_{{B_{q}}}
(again by Frobenius reciprocity).
The real question then is what the other representations appearing in the decomposition of the restriction are.
In this section, we shall give a complete answer in terms of principal series representations, the Steinberg representation and cuspidal representations of
{G_{p}}
.

2.1.
The action of
{G_{p}}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}

There is a natural action of
{G_{q}}
on the projective space
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
given by

\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\cdot[x:y]=[ax+by:cx+dy],

for
\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in{G_{q}}
and
[x:y]\in{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
. This action is transitive with
{\text{Stab}_{{G_{q}}}}([1:0])={B_{q}}
. We identify
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
with
\{[1:x]:x\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\}\cup\{[0:1]\}
throughout the article.

Notation 1
.

Denote
{\widehat{x}}\coloneqq[1:x]
and
\widehat{\infty}\coloneqq[0:1]
.

Notation 2
.

Fix
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\text{ and }\epsilon^{2}\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Denote the anisotropic torus of
{G_{q}}
by

T_{q}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
b\epsilon^{2}&a\end{matrix}\right)\in{G_{q}}:a,b\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\text{ not both zero}\right\}.

Analogously, for fixed
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\text{ and }\eta^{2}\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, let

T_{p}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
b\eta^{2}&a\end{matrix}\right)\in{G_{p}}:a,b\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\text{ not both zero}\right\}

denote the anisotropic torus of
{G_{p}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the action of
{G_{q}}
, we have the orbit decomposition

{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}})}={O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})\sqcup{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}}).

Moreover, the stabilizers of
{\widehat{0}}
and
{\widehat{\epsilon}}
are equal to
{B_{q}}
and
T_{q}
, respectively.

Proof.

The transitivity of the
{G_{q}}
action on
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
gives the orbit
{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})={\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}=\{{\widehat{x}}:x\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\}\sqcup\{\widehat{\infty}\}
. That
{\text{Stab}_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})={B_{q}}
can easily be verified.
Note that
{\widehat{\epsilon}}\notin{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})
since
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. If we show that
\left|{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}})}\right|=\left|{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})\sqcup{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})\right|
, then we are done.
Let
g=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in{\text{Stab}_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})
, that is,
g\cdot{\widehat{\epsilon}}={\widehat{\epsilon}}
.
Then for some
0\neq\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}},~(a+b\epsilon,c+d\epsilon)=(\lambda,\lambda\epsilon)\text{ which gives }(a+b\epsilon)\epsilon=c+d\epsilon
. As
1,\epsilon

are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, we get
a=d
and
c=b\epsilon^{2}
. Thus
{\text{Stab}_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})=T_{q}
and so the number of elements in

{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})=\frac{\left(q^{2}-1\right)\left(q^{2}-q\right)}{q^{2}-1}=q^{2}-q
and
\left|{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{0}})\right|+\left|{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})\right|=q^{2}+1=\left|{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}})}\right|
.
∎

Notation 3
.

For
f>2
, define the set
X=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{p}},&\text{if }2\nmid f,\\
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}},&\text{if }2\mid f.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
x\in X
, the set
\{1,x,x^{2}\}
is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
and let
\{1,x,x^{2}\}
be linearly dependent over
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. Then there exist
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, not all zero, such that
a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}=0
. That is,
x
satisfies a polynomial of degree at most
2
over
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. If either
a_{2}=0
or the polynomial is reducible,
then
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. If
a_{2}\neq 0
and the polynomial is irreducible, then
{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(x)\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. But
{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
iff
2\mid f
. Thus,
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
when
2\nmid f
and
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
when
2\mid f
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

With respect to the
{G_{p}}
action on
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}
,

\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{p}}({\widehat{x}})=Z_{p},\text{ for all }x\in X,

where
Z_{p}
is the centre of
{G_{p}}
.

Proof.

Let
\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})
, then
(a+bx,c+dx)=(\lambda,\lambda x)
, for some
0\neq\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. So, we get
c+(d-a)x-bx^{2}=0
and by Lemma
2.2
,
a=d
and
b=c=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

The decomposition of

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}=

\displaystyle{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{0}})\sqcup\bigsqcup\limits_{{\widehat{x}}\in{\mathcal{I}_{1}}}{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}}),\hskip 42.67912pt\text{ if }2\nmid f,

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}=

\displaystyle{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{0}})\sqcup{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{\eta}})\sqcup\bigsqcup\limits_{{\widehat{x}}\in{\mathcal{I}_{2}}}{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}}),\text{ if }2\mid f,

Suppose

. Since

{G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}={G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})}\sqcup{G_{p}}\backslash\left({\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})}\right).

Note that
{G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})}=\{{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{0}})\}
as

{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})}

{\widehat{x}}\in{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})},~{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})=Z_{p}

,

\left|{\mathcal{I}_{1}}\right|=\frac{\left|{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p}})}\right|}{\left|{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})\right|}=\frac{\left(p^{f}+1\right)-\left(p+1\right)}{p\left(p^{2}-1\right)}=\frac{p^{f-1}-1}{p^{2}-1}.

Suppose

. Since

and

, we have

{G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}={G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}})}\sqcup{G_{p}}\backslash\left({\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}})}\right).

Note that
{G_{p}}\backslash{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}})}=\{{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{0}}),{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{\eta}})\}
by taking

in Lemma

.
Again, for any
{\widehat{x}}\in{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}})},~{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})\simeq Z_{p}

, and so,
\left|{O_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})\right|=p\left(p^{2}-1\right)
.
So, by the same argument as in the previous case, the number of
{G_{p}}\text{-orbits}
in
{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\setminus{\mathbb{P}^{1}({\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}})}
is

∎

2.2.
Decomposition of the restriction

Recall that, if
H
and
K
are two subgroups of a group
G
, not necessarily distinct, then a double coset of some fixed element
x\in G
is the set
\{hxk:h\in H,k\in K\}
and it is denoted by
HxK

[
6
]
. The set of all the double cosets

is denoted by
H\backslash G/K
.

For a mod
p
character
\chi
of
{B_{q}}
, let
\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi
be a principal series representation of
{G_{q}}
. Then by Mackey’s restriction formula, we have

{\left.\text{ind}_{{B_{q}}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\text{ind}_{{B_{q}^{\gamma}}}^{{G_{p}}}{\chi^{\gamma}}

(2.3)

where
{B_{q}^{\gamma}}\coloneqq\gamma{B_{q}}\gamma^{-1}\cap{G_{p}}
and
{\chi^{\gamma}}(b)=\chi\left(\gamma^{-1}b\gamma\right)
for all

and where
\Gamma
is a set of double coset representatives of
{G_{p}}\backslash{G_{q}}/{B_{q}}
.

Notation 4
.

For
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

3.4
), we get

\displaystyle{{\left.\text{ind}_{T_{q}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{{O_{{G_{p}}}}(\widehat{a+b\epsilon})\in{G_{p}}\backslash({O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}}))}\text{ind}_{{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}(\widehat{a+b\epsilon})}^{G_{p}}\chi^{g_{a,b}}}.

Now, Proposition
3.3
gives the description of all the
{G_{p}}\text{-orbits}
in
{O_{{G_{q}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})
and therefore,

\displaystyle{{\left.\text{ind}_{T_{q}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigoplus\limits_{\widehat{a+b\epsilon}\in{\mathcal{J}}}\text{ind}_{{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}(\widehat{a+b\epsilon})}^{G_{p}}\chi^{g_{a,b}},&\text{ if }2\mid f,\\
\text{ind}_{{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})}^{G_{p}}\chi^{g_{0,1}}~\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\widehat{a+b\epsilon}\in{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}}\text{ind}_{{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}(\widehat{a+b\epsilon})}^{G_{p}}\chi^{g_{a,b}},&\text{ if }2\nmid f.\end{array}\right.}

Note that for
{\widehat{x}}\in{\mathcal{J}},{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}_{2}}
, we have
x\in{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
by the definition of
{\mathcal{I}_{2}}
, and for such
x,~{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{x}})=Z_{p}
by Lemma
2.3
. Thus,
{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}(\widehat{a+b\epsilon})=Z_{p}
, for all
\widehat{a+b\epsilon}\in{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}
. In this case,
{\left.\chi^{g_{a,b}}\right|}_{Z_{p}}={\chi|}_{Z_{p}}
. Also, for
f
odd,
\epsilon=\eta
and so
{\text{Stab}_{{G_{p}}}}({\widehat{\epsilon}})=T_{p}
. Note that
g_{0,1}=\mathrm{Id}
and so
\chi^{g_{0,1}}={\chi|}_{T_{p}}
. Combining this information, we get

\displaystyle{{\left.\text{ind}_{T_{q}}^{{G_{q}}}\chi\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left|{\mathcal{J}}\right|\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\chi|}_{Z_{p}}\right),&\text{ if }2\mid f,\\
\text{ind}_{T_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\chi|}_{T_{p}}\right)~\oplus\left|{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\right|\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\chi|}_{Z_{p}}\right),&\text{ if }2\nmid f.\end{array}\right.}

(3.7)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
0\leq r<q^{2}-1
. Then for the character
\omega_{2f}^{r}
of
T_{q}
,

(1)

\displaystyle{{\left.\mathrm{ind}_{T_{q}}^{{G_{q}}}\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{p^{f-1}\left(p^{f}-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\mathrm{ind}_{B_{p}}^{G_{p}}\chi_{i,(r-i)}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{St}}\right),&\text{ if }2\mid f,\\
\mathrm{ind}_{T_{p}}^{G_{p}}\omega_{2}^{r}~\oplus\frac{\left(p^{f-1}-1\right)\left(p^{f}+p-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\mathrm{ind}_{B_{p}}^{G_{p}}\chi_{i,(r-i)}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{St}}\right),&\text{ if }2\nmid f.\end{array}\right.}

(2)

\displaystyle{{\left.\mathrm{ind}_{T_{q}}^{{G_{q}}}\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{{G_{p}}}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{p^{f-1}\left(p^{f}-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{p}\mathrm{ind}_{T_{p}}^{G_{p}}\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}\right),&\text{ if }2\mid f,\\
\mathrm{ind}_{T_{p}}^{G_{p}}\omega_{2}^{r}~\oplus\frac{\left(p^{f-1}-1\right)\left(p^{f}+p-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{p}\mathrm{ind}_{T_{p}}^{G_{p}}\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}\right),&\text{ if }2\nmid f.\end{array}\right.}

Proof.

Replace
\chi
by
\omega_{2f}^{r}
in the isomorphism in (
3.7
). Then,
{\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{T_{p}}=\omega_{2}^{r}
as characters of
T_{p}
if
f
is odd. Also,
\left|{\mathcal{J}}\right|=\frac{p^{f-1}\left(p^{f}-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}\text{ and }\left|{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\right|=\frac{\left(p^{f-1}-1\right)\left(p^{f}+p-1\right)}{p^{2}-1}
by Proposition
3.3
. By the transitivity of induction, we have

\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)\simeq\text{ind}_{S_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{S_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)\simeq\text{ind}_{T_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{T_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right).

We investigate each of these isomorphisms separately.

(1)

Note that
\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{S_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)
is a sum of
p-1
characters as
S_{p}
is abelian of order prime to
p
. For a character
\psi
of
S_{p}
, by Frobenius reciprocity,
\psi\hookrightarrow\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{S_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)
if and only if
{\psi|}_{Z_{p}}={\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}
.
Now, for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
, one can easily verify that
{\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}={\left.\chi_{i,(r-i)}\right|}_{Z_{p}}
, where
\chi_{i,(r-i)}
are (distinct) characters of
S_{p}
. So we get
\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{S_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\chi_{i,(r-i)}
. So,

\displaystyle\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\text{ind}_{S_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\chi_{i,(r-i)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\text{ind}_{S_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\chi_{i,(r-i)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\text{ind}_{{B_{p}}}^{{G_{p}}}\chi_{i,(r-i)}\otimes\overline{\text{St}}\right)~~(\text{by Remark 1 in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{GJ}{}{}]}}).

(2)

Similarly,
\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{T_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)
is a sum of
p+1
characters. Again, for any character
\psi
of
T_{p}
, we have
\psi\hookrightarrow\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{T_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)
if and only if
{\psi|}_{Z_{p}}={\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}
.
Here, one can easily verify that
{\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}={\left.\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}\right|}_{Z_{p}}
, for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,p\}
, where
\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}
are (distinct) characters of
T_{p}
. So we get
\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{T_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{p}\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\text{ind}_{Z_{p}}^{G_{p}}\left({\left.\omega_{2f}^{r}\right|}_{Z_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\text{ind}_{T_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{p}\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{p}\text{ind}_{T_{p}}^{{G_{p}}}\omega_{2}^{r+i(p-1)}.\qed

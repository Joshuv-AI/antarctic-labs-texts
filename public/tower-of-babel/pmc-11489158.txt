Phenolization of the sinus tract in recurrent sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease: long-term results of a prospective cohort study
Authors: Jochem de Kort, Akke Pronk, Menno R Vriens, Niels Smakman, Edgar J B Furnee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimIn recent years, the paradigm for the treatment of sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease (SPSD) has shifted from surgical excision toward more minimally invasive techniques. Although extensive research has been conducted on the minimal invasive phenolization technique for primary SPSD, literature in recurrent SPSD is lacking. The purpose of this study was to report the long-term outcomes of the phenolization technique for recurrent SPSD.MethodThis study included all 57 patients who were initially prospectively included in this study for short-term follow-up after phenolization for recurrent SPSD. A questionnaire was sent out to all patients to obtain long-term outcome. The primary endpoint was recurrence. Secondary endpoints included quality of life and symptoms related to SPSD.ResultsA total of 47 patients (82.5%) were available for long-term follow-up. Recurrence needing further surgery developed in 7 patients (14.9%) after a mean follow-up of 76 (SD 21.7) months. At follow-up, there was improvement in the quality of life compared to preoperative levels (82.0, IQR 75.0–90.0 versus 74.0 IQR 52.5–80.0, p = 0.024). Additionally, symptoms associated with SPSD, including pain, fluid discharge, and itching sensation, also showed significant improvement. A total of 42 patients (89.4%) would undergo the same treatment again. No significant association was found between known risk factors and recurrence.ConclusionPhenolization for recurrent SPSD showed a recurrence rate of 14.9% with significant improvement of natal cleft symptoms and quality of life at long-term follow-up. Therefore, phenolization should be considered as a feasible option for patients with recurrent SPSD.Keywords: Phenolization, Phenol, Pilonidal sinus disease, Sacrococcygeal, Recurrence

Introduction

In recent years, the paradigm for the treatment of the chronic form of sacrococcygeal pilonidal sinus disease (SPSD) has shifted from excision with primary wound closure or secondary wound healing toward more minimally invasive techniques. Several minimal invasive methods, such as laser treatment, sinusectomy, and endoscopic treatment, offer advantages like reduced postoperative pain, low complication rates, and quick return to normal daily activities [1]. Phenolization of the sinus tract is another well-known minimal invasive technique that has been extensively studied as treatment of simple chronic SPSD [2]. Two randomized controlled trials focusing on the long-term outcome of the phenolization technique showed the earlier mentioned short-term advantages with no significant difference in long-term recurrence rate compared to radical excision for primary SPSD [3, 4].

While phenolization and other minimally invasive treatments have enhanced short-term outcomes for SPSD, recurrence rates remain high, reaching up to 68% after both radical excision and minimally invasive surgery [5–7]. There is limited literature on the treatment of recurrent SPSD, despite recurrence being a critical outcome parameter in SPSD surgery. Since promising results of the phenolization technique for primary SPSD have been described in the literature [8], this technique could also be considered as an option in patients with a limited recurrence after previous surgery for SPSD. Therefore, we previously reported the short-term outcomes of the phenolization technique for recurrent SPSD in a prospective cohort. Outcomes were comparable to those for primary SPSD, like soon return to normal daily activities, low rate of surgical site infection, and high wound healing rates [9]. However, the long-term outcomes related to recurrence rate and quality of life are crucial in the decision whether the phenolization technique can also be considered a viable treatment option for patients with recurrent SPSD.

The purpose of this study was to report the long-term outcomes of a prospective cohort of patients who underwent phenolization treatment for recurrent SPSD, focusing on recurrence rate and quality of life.

Methods

Study design

The design of this study on the phenolization technique in patients with recurrent SPSD is a prospective single-center cohort study carried out at Diakonessenhuis in Utrecht, the Netherlands, as described previously [9]. Approval of this study was granted by the Medical Ethics Committee in Utrecht, the Netherlands. Inclusion criteria encompassed patients aged 18 years or older, with a recurrence following one or multiple prior surgical interventions for SPSD. Patients presenting with negligible SPSD-related symptoms, SPSD-associated abscess, an extensive network of subcutaneous sinus tracts (as these individuals were not considered candidates for phenolization), or primary SPSD were excluded from the study.

Phenolization technique

A detailed overview of the surgical steps of the phenolization technique has been described in the study reporting the short-term results [9]. Briefly summarized, after positioning the patient in prone position, the sinus was probed through the pit(s) to identify the subcutaneous tract and subsequently the (off-)midline pit(s) were excised as limited as possible. A curette was introduced into the pit(s) to clean the subcutaneous tract of hairs and debris. Before phenolization, the surrounding skin and anal region were protected with Vaseline. Then, liquid phenol (85%) was injected in the tract for 1 min, twice. Finally, the phenol was aspirated and the area was washed out with ethanol (70%) to neutralize the remnants of phenol (Fig. 1).

Data collection

Baseline characteristics were prospectively collected. Postoperatively, all patients had follow-up appointments in the outpatient clinic until full wound closure was reached. After a follow-up period of at least 3 years, patients received a questionnaire with various questions concerning SPSD-related symptoms and quality of life.

In the questionnaire, patients were asked for fluid discharge, pain, irritations, itching, and burning sensation at the natal cleft. These items were scored on a 6-point scale (0 meaning no complaints and 5 meaning daily complaints). Pain at follow-up was also assessed using a visual analogue scale (0 meaning no pain, 100 meaning worst pain thinkable). Quality of life was assessed in the questionnaire by the Short Form 36 (SF-36). The SF-36 is especially developed to work-out quality of life concerning patient-centered healthcare [10]. In addition, quality of life was also assessed using a visual analogue scale (0 meaning worst thinkable quality of life, 100 meaning best thinkable quality of life). Patients were asked about their willingness to undergo the same treatment again, and to what extent they found the treatment to be beneficial, based on a 4-point scale (0 meaning not beneficial and 4 meaning extremely beneficial). Finally, patients were asked about the extent to which they found the treatment to be bothersome, also scored on a visual analogue scale (0 meaning not bothersome at all, 100 meaning extremely bothersome).

Definition of recurrence

Recurrence of SPSD was defined when objectified by a physician after previous complete wound healing following the phenolization procedure (index operation). All patient records were examined to detect any objective recurrence or the need for an additional surgical intervention following the index operation for recurrent SPSD at our hospital. Additionally, patients were asked in the questionnaire whether they had undergone surgery for recurrent SPSD at another hospital after the index operation. In addition, the questionnaire inquired whether the patient had the impression of recurrent SPSD, even if this had never been clinically confirmed by a physician. The patients with a positive answer to this question were invited for an outpatient clinical visit to objectify the existence of a recurrence. Patients were considered non-recurrent if they denied the impression of a recurrence in the questionnaire and never had an objectified recurrence by a physician, or a second procedure for recurrent SPSD after the index operation.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were executed using IBM SPSS statistics version 26 for Windows. Continuous data were expressed as mean (+ / − standard deviations) or median (interquartile range), depending on whether the data were normally distributed or not. Distribution of variables was measured by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies with percentages.

Differences between preoperative and follow-up values were statistically analyzed by using the paired samples T-test for continuous values. If data were not normally distributed, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test was performed. For categorical or dichotomous values, the Pearson chi-square test was used. For all statistical analyses of values between preoperative and follow-up data, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To analyze the demographic data associated with recurrence [11], logistic regression analysis was performed.

Results

A questionnaire was sent out to all 57 patients who were initially included in this study. After sending out the invitation and repeated contact by email as well as by phone call, a total of 49 patients (86.0%) filled out the questionnaire. Two patients were diagnosed with hidradenitis suppurativa instead of SPSD, resulting in their exclusion for analysis. Finally, 47 patients (82.5%) were available for the current analysis focusing on the long-term outcome (Fig. 2).

Mean score of quality of life was 70 or higher at follow-up in all eight domains of the SF-36. This indicates a high quality of life as these values exceed the mean scores in the general population [12]. As illustrated in Fig. 4, there was no significant difference between preoperative and follow-up values for most domains. However, for the domain role limitations due to physical health and bodily pain, there was a significant improvement; the domain role limitations due to physical health problems was scored 94.7 at follow-up compared to 68.9 preoperatively (p = 0.003) and bodily pain score increased significantly from 67.4 preoperatively to 87.4 at follow-up (p < 0.001). On a visual analogue scale, median quality of life at follow-up was also significantly higher compared to before surgery (82.0, IQR 75.0–90.0 versus 74.0 IQR 52.5–80.0, p = 0.024).

With regard to patient’s satisfaction, of the 47 patients available for long-term analysis, 42 patients (89.4%) indicated a willingness to undergo the same treatment again, three patients (6.4%) remained indifferent, and two patients (4.3%) would refuse the same treatment. The latter two patients reported that the treatment did not have the effect they had hoped for. Both patients received incision and drainage prior to the index treatment. One of these patients had an objectified recurrence after the index operation and underwent excision of the sinus pits with secondary wound healing. The other patient did not have further surgery after the index operation. Then, 44 patients (93.7%) indicated that the treatment was moderately or extremely beneficial. The mean score given by the patients on the extent to which they found the treatment to be bothersome was 37.3 (± 30.5).

Recurrence

In total, 40 patients (85.1%) remained, according to the definition of recurrence, recurrence-free during follow-up time. An overview of the patients with a recurrence after phenolization is presented in Fig. 5. Seven patients (14.9%) developed a recurrence during follow-up. Three of those seven patients had an objectified recurrence within 1 year (3, 5, and 6 months) after the index operation. The other four patients developed a recurrence after 50, 59, 63, and 85 months. All these seven patients with an objectified recurrence underwent further surgery; four patients received a second phenolization treatment after the index phenolization, two patients were treated with excision of the sinus pits with secondary wound healing, and one patient had incision and drainage for a pilonidal abscess. After re-intervention, no further recurrences were objectified during follow-up in these seven patients who developed a recurrence after the index operation. Mean duration of follow-up in these seven patients after the surgical reintervention following the index operation was 42 (± 24.5) months.

Follow-up after the index phenolization treatment

Two patients noted in their survey that they continued to experience symptoms associated with SPSD. Nevertheless, upon further inquiry, both individuals stated that the symptoms were not bothersome enough to proceed with a follow-up outpatient clinic appointment.

Risk factors for developing a recurrence

There was no significant association between developing a recurrence after the index operation and one of the previously reported risk factors, including BMI, age, smoking, gender, having a family history of SPSD, or having more than one sinus pit during the index operation (Table 3).

Discussion

After a mean follow-up time of more than 6 years, a recurrence rate of 14.9% was found in recurrent SPSD treated with the phenolization technique. After a re-intervention in those patients with recurrent SPSD subsequent to the index phenolization, no further recurrences were objectified afterwards. Additionally, there was a significant improvement in quality of life at follow-up.

This study has, to our knowledge, the longest follow-up period when evaluating the long-term recurrence rates in patients with recurrent SPSD, treated with crystallized phenol. In another prospective cohort study by Kargin et al. [13], the use of crystallized phenol in recurrent SPSD was also evaluated. The mean follow-up time in that study was 45.8 months. Despite an average of 2.7 phenolization treatments per patient, a recurrence of 40% was observed. Another study reported a similar recurrence rate (13.9%) to our findings when treating patients with recurrent SPSD with crystallized phenol [14]. However, only 36 patients were included with a mean follow-up of 54 months.

When comparing the recurrence rates of the crystallized phenol treatment for recurrent and primary SPSD, there is little disparity. Kaymakcioglu et al. [15] included 143 patients with primary SPSD who were treated with 80% phenol solution, and after a mean follow-up of 24 months, a recurrence rate of 8.3% was reported. This is slightly lower than the 14.9% recurrence rate reported in the current study. Another study comparing the Karydakis flap with phenol application in patients with primary SPSD reported a recurrence of 4.4% (2/45) in the phenolization group. However, this was after a mean follow-up time of just 12 months [16]. In the randomized trial by our group comparing the long-term outcome of phenolization and surgical excision with primary wound closure in primary SPSD, a recurrence rate of 5.6% was found in the phenolization group after a mean follow-up of 48.4 months. In addition, one large comparative cohort study (n = 208) conducted by Gozukucuk et al. [17] reported a 5.7% recurrence rate in the phenolization group after a follow-up of 14.5 months in patients with SPSD. From these reports, it can be concluded that the recurrence rate of the phenolization technique for primary SPSD is slightly lower compared to the recurrence rate after phenolization for recurrent SPSD. However, the duration of follow-up in the current study on recurrent SPSD was considerably longer. In addition, the current study showed that in four out of the seven patients with recurrent SPSD after the index operation, recurrence occurred 50 or more months after phenolization, which is longer than the duration of follow-up in the studies on phenolization as mentioned for primary SPSD. This might be an explanation for the slightly lower recurrence rate as reported in the studies on the phenolization technique for primary SPSD.

Quality of life improved at follow-up in the present study. For evaluation of quality of life by the SF-36, there was a statistically significant improvement for the domain’s bodily pain and role limitation due to physical problems. Despite quality of life being a major outcome parameter, it has not been extensively researched for the phenolization treatment of SPSD. One small randomized clinical study (n = 40) was conducted comparing phenolization and excision with primary closure [18]. A significant higher quality of life was found in the phenolization group. Unfortunately, the researchers generated their own survey and did not use a validated questionnaire to evaluate quality of life, which makes their results difficult to compare to the results of the current study. In the randomized study by our group [3] comparing phenolization and radical excision with primary wound closure for primary SPSD, similar outcomes on the domains within the SF-36 were reported after a long-term follow-up of 48.4 months.

Although laser and endoscopic treatments are other frequently applied minimal invasive treatments for SPSD, there is still no consensus on whether one minimal invasive technique is superior to the other. To our knowledge, no studies have been published reporting the results of laser-assisted closure for recurrent SPSD. Only for primary SPSD, a recurrence rate of 4.7% after laser treatment has been reported in a systematic review. However, a mean follow-up of all studies included in this review was only 12 months (n = 971) [19]. With regard to endoscopic pilonidal sinus treatment (EPSiT), Giarratano et al. [20] reported, compared to the findings for phenolization in the current study, a lower treatment failure of 8% in 77 patients after a median follow-up of 25 months. However, in their study, only a subgroup of the included patients (n = 9) had recurrent SPSD, whereas in our study, only patients with recurrent SPSD were included. This possibly explains the difference in recurrence rate. Meinero et al. [21] reported a recurrence rate of 5.1% and persistent SPSD in 5.1% of patients treated for recurrent SPSD, after a mean follow-up of 16 months. The total treatment failure is comparable to the findings in the current study (10.2% versus 14.9%), but the follow-up period is considerably shorter. The drawback of the EPSiT procedure is that it requires adequate training and relatively expensive surgical tools, whereas the phenolization procedure has a short learning curve and seems more cost effective.

Certain limitations apply to this study. First, the number of patients included in this study is relatively small. This has possibly resulted in a non-significant outcome between recurrence rate and well-known risk factors to develop recurrent SPSD, as described previously. Also, subgroup analysis of recurrence rates after phenolization based on the prior surgical procedures, i.e., excision vs. phenolization vs. incision and drainage, was considered inconclusive due to the relatively small subgroup sizes. Secondly, the sacrococcygeal area was not assessed to objectify the presence or absence of recurrence in the included patients during an outpatient department visit. Given the extended follow-up duration and more importantly, the substantial efforts that had already to be taken to only achieve adequate response to the questionnaire, outpatient department visits for the included patients would not, to our opinion, be feasible. Moreover, patients (n = 2) who reported the persistence of symptoms related to a recurrent pilonidal sinus in the questionnaire but were not yet objectified with a recurrence opted not to pursue with an outpatient department visit. This was potentially attributable to the presence of mild symptoms. In this study, the lost to follow-up was only 17%. Although the drop out should be as low as possible, this percentage is, to our opinion, rather acceptable, considering the fact that many of the included patients are young and belong to the working-class population and consider themselves too busy to participate in this study. In addition, a subset of patients remained inaccessible due to the obsolescence of up-to-date contact information.

Conclusion

SPSD is a complicated condition where phenolization has already proven to be a safe treatment option for primary SPSD. Nevertheless, recurrences do often occur, independently on the primary treatment chosen. When treating recurrent SPSD with crystallized phenol, our previous report showed promising short-term advantages as mentioned earlier [9]. In addition to these favorable short-term outcomes, the current study reported an acceptable long-term recurrence rate of less than 15%. This indicates that crystallized phenol should be considered a feasible option when treating patients with recurrent SPSD, especially as in most cases another recurrence can be successfully treated with another minimally invasive phenolization procedure.

Ankle arthrodesis following bone transport as a definitive limb salvage strategy after multiple failed microsurgical reconstructions for infected tibial pilon fracture nonunion: a case report
Authors: Koji Nozaka, Tsuyoshi Shirahata, Yusuke Yuasa, Shuntaro Harata, Ryota Kimura, Manabu Akagawa, Naohisa Miyakoshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Large segmental bone defects of the tibia represent a major reconstructive challenge in orthopedic trauma surgery, particularly when associated with infection and soft tissue compromise. Among these injuries, tibial pilon fractures are especially problematic because they typically result from high-energy trauma and are characterized by severe articular comminution, metaphyseal bone loss, and significant soft tissue damage.

In such cases, the risk of infection and nonunion is high, and repeated surgical interventions may further compromise local vascularity and soft tissue viability.

Consequently, conventional reconstructive options—including vascularized fibular grafting and the induced membrane technique—may be limited or unreliable in the presence of persistent infection and poor biological conditions and compromised local vascularity.

Bone transport based on distraction osteogenesis offers a biological reconstruction strategy that enables simultaneous restoration of bone defects and improvement of the local biological environment. In addition, this technique may facilitate soft tissue healing by enhancing local perfusion, which is particularly advantageous in complex infected cases.

We report a case of infected tibial pilon fracture nonunion with severe bone loss in the setting of multiple failed microsurgical reconstructions and compromised local vascular conditions, in which limb salvage was successfully achieved using bone transport followed by ankle arthrodesis.

Case presentation

A 39-year-old male construction worker sustained an open comminuted distal tibial pilon fracture after falling approximately 5 m from a roof (Fig. 1A). The injury was associated with compartment syndrome and a contralateral calcaneal fracture.

B Anteroposterior radiograph after temporary external fixation. C Anteroposterior radiograph after definitive plate fixation. D Anteroposterior radiograph showing progression to infected nonunion. E Clinical appearance of the medial ankle demonstrating a soft tissue defect with sinus tract and purulent discharge (red arrow)

Initial treatment was performed at a local hospital, including temporary external fixation and subsequent plate fixation (Fig. 1B, C).

Despite repeated debridement and fixation procedures, the right distal tibial fracture progressed to infected nonunion (Fig. 1D). Clinical examination revealed a medial ankle soft tissue defect with sinus tract formation and purulent discharge (Fig. 1E).

Several reconstructive procedures were subsequently attempted at a referring hospital, including vascularized fibular grafting combined with an anterolateral thigh flap and later a reverse sural artery flap. However, both soft tissue reconstructions failed because of flap necrosis (Fig. 2B, E), and infection persisted. Radiographs demonstrated graft resorption and instability following vascularized fibular grafting (Fig. 2A, F). The patient underwent a total of 19 surgical procedures at previous institutions, including repeated debridement and fixation, as well as reconstructive surgeries such as vascularized fibular grafting and flap procedures. Despite these interventions, infection persisted, leading to progressive soft tissue deterioration and bone exposure (Fig. 2C). These repeated failures suggested severely compromised local vascular conditions and ultimately necessitated referral for limb salvage.

Failed reconstructive procedures at the referring hospital, demonstrating severely compromised local vascular conditions. A Radiograph after vascularized fibular grafting. B Clinical appearance of flap failure with necrosis following anterolateral thigh flap reconstruction. C Clinical appearance demonstrating extensive soft tissue necrosis and bone exposure. D Clinical appearance following reverse sural artery flap reconstruction. E Clinical appearance of flap failure with necrosis after reverse sural artery flap. F Radiograph after vascularized fibular grafting showing graft resorption and instability

Because of persistent infection and repeated reconstructive failure, below-knee amputation was recommended, and the patient was referred to our institution for limb salvage.

At presentation to our institution, a 10 × 3 cm soft tissue defect with purulent discharge and foul odor was present over the medial ankle (Fig. 3B). Radiographs demonstrated severe bone loss of the distal tibia and destruction of the ankle joint.

Sequential bone transport process using circular external fixation. A Anteroposterior radiograph obtained immediately after surgery, demonstrating bone defect and application of circular external fixation. B Clinical appearance of the ankle immediately after radical debridement and resection of approximately 10 cm of infected bone. C Anteroposterior radiograph at one month, demonstrating early regenerate formation at the corticotomy site. D Clinical appearance of the ankle one month after initiation of bone transport, showing gradual improvement of the soft tissue condition. E Anteroposterior radiograph at approximately four months, demonstrating progression of bone transport and consolidation of regenerate bone toward the docking site. F Clinical appearance of the ankle approximately four months after initiation of bone transport, showing progressive epithelialization of the soft tissue defect

Culture obtained from the nonunion site revealed methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). The white blood cell count was 9,200/µL, and the C-reactive protein level was 1.85 mg/dL. Because the distal tibial articular surface had insufficient residual bone stock for joint preservation, and because persistent infection made joint salvage unreliable, staged ankle arthrodesis following bone transport was planned.

First-stage procedure

Radical debridement was performed, and approximately 10 cm of infected bone around the nonunion site was resected (Fig. 3B). Bone transport was planned to reconstruct this segmental defect, and no additional limb lengthening was undertaken. The transport distance corresponded to the size of the bone defect (approximately 10 cm). An antibiotic-loaded hydroxyapatite spacer containing approximately 2 g of vancomycin was inserted into the bone defect.

Based on the culture and sensitivity results, intravenous daptomycin was administered at a dose of 6 mg/kg once daily for 6 weeks.

Adjunctive wound management included negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT), which was applied continuously from the first-stage procedure until the second-stage procedure (approximately 4 weeks). Infection control was assessed based on clinical findings, including resolution of drainage and local inflammatory signs, as well as normalization of inflammatory markers. The white blood cell count and C-reactive protein level normalized within approximately 4 weeks, at which point the second-stage procedure was performed.

Second-stage procedure

Approximately 4 weeks after the initial surgery, a second-stage procedure was performed. The vancomycin-loaded spacer, which had been inserted during the first-stage procedure, was removed from the nonunion site prior to bone transport.

Proximal tibial corticotomy was then performed for bone transport using an Ilizarov circular external fixator (Smith & Nephew, Memphis, TN, USA) (Fig. 3A).

Intraoperative cultures obtained from the nonunion site at the time of the second-stage procedure were negative, confirming infection control.

Following a latency period of 1 week, distraction was initiated at a rate of 0.25 mm four times per day. Negative pressure wound therapy was continued for an additional 2 weeks after the second-stage procedure, for a total duration of 6 weeks.

Thereafter, the wound was managed with regular irrigation and application of fibroblast growth factor (FGF) spray.

During bone transport, gradual regenerate formation was observed radiographically (Fig. 3C), and the soft tissue condition improved progressively (Fig. 3D).

The medial ankle defect achieved complete epithelialization within approximately 2 months (Fig. 3F).

Docking and consolidation

Docking was achieved 4 months after initiation of bone transport (Fig. 3E).

Autologous cancellous bone graft harvested from the iliac crest was applied to the docking site. The circular external fixator was removed 6 months after docking when solid union was confirmed radiographically.

At final follow-up, no recurrence of infection was observed throughout the 8-year follow-up period. The patient had returned to full-time construction work and maintained full weight-bearing ambulation. Clinical and radiographic evaluation demonstrated solid ankle arthrodesis and healed soft tissue (Fig. 4A–C). Functional recovery was excellent, with a Japanese Society for Surgery of the Foot score of 88 points and a visual analogue scale pain score of 0.

Discussion

Management of large tibial bone defects remains challenging in orthopedic trauma surgery [1]. Various reconstructive strategies have been described, including vascularized fibular grafting, the induced membrane technique, and bone transport [2–4].

Vascularized fibular grafting is a well-established method that provides structural support and vascularized bone; however, its success is limited in the presence of infection or compromised soft tissue conditions [2, 5, 6]. In the present case, multiple reconstructive attempts at the previous institution—including anterolateral thigh flap reconstruction, vascularized fibular grafting, and reverse sural artery flap reconstruction—failed. These repeated failures suggested that the vascular condition around the infected nonunion site had become severely compromised.

The Masquelet technique has also been widely used for reconstruction of segmental bone defects [3]. However, this technique requires adequate soft tissue coverage and a favorable vascular environment. In the present case, additional soft tissue reconstruction would have been necessary to perform the Masquelet technique. Given the poor local vascular status caused by repeated unsuccessful microsurgical procedures, this approach was considered unsuitable.

To our knowledge, few reports have specifically addressed the role of bone transport as a salvage strategy after repeated failure of microsurgical reconstruction in infected tibial nonunion, particularly with long-term functional outcomes. In this context, this case is not merely a demonstration of a reconstructive technique, but rather illustrates a critical decision-making process in complex limb salvage.

In the present case, multiple microsurgical reconstructions failed, resulting in severely compromised vascular conditions. Under such circumstances, further vascularized procedures were considered unreliable. Bone transport, which does not rely on microsurgical vascular anastomosis and has been shown to enhance local blood flow through distraction osteogenesis [7], was therefore selected as a rational salvage strategy. Bone transport has been widely used for reconstruction of both traumatic and tumor-related tibial defects [8–10], and is particularly advantageous in biologically compromised conditions.

This case provides several important clinical insights and carries an educational message for trauma and reconstructive surgeons. First, it demonstrates that bone transport can serve as a reproducible salvage strategy in the setting of multiple failed microsurgical reconstructions, where local vascular conditions are severely compromised. In such situations, further vascularized procedures may be unreliable, making bone transport a rational alternative that does not depend on microvascular anastomosis.

In clinical practice, repeated failure of microsurgical reconstruction is often followed by consideration of amputation. However, this case suggests that bone transport using the Ilizarov method should be reconsidered as a viable limb salvage option even in such extreme scenarios. Thus, bone transport should not be viewed merely as one of several reconstructive techniques, but rather as a strategic alternative within the treatment algorithm, particularly before proceeding to amputation in biologically compromised cases. This perspective may help guide clinical decision-making in similarly challenging limb salvage scenarios.

Second, this case highlights the potential for simultaneous resolution of both bone and soft tissue defects without additional flap reconstruction. The spontaneous epithelialization observed during bone transport suggests that distraction osteogenesis may contribute to soft tissue healing by improving local perfusion.

This finding may be explained by the biological effects of distraction osteogenesis.

The Ilizarov principle has demonstrated that gradual mechanical distraction enhances local blood flow and stimulates angiogenesis, which may contribute not only to bone regeneration but also to soft tissue healing [7]. In the present case, this biological response likely played a key role in facilitating spontaneous epithelialization without the need for additional flap reconstruction.

Furthermore, bone transport has demonstrated high rates of union and infection control in patients with infected tibial nonunion [11], and reviews have consistently demonstrated its effectiveness in managing complex bone defects [12].

In contrast to microsurgical reconstruction, bone transport using the Ilizarov method offers a biological reconstruction strategy that does not rely on vascular anastomosis. This technique enhances local perfusion and supports tissue regeneration through distraction osteogenesis [7–10, 13]. Notably, in this case, the soft tissue defect gradually epithelialized during bone transport without additional flap reconstruction, supporting the concept that distraction osteogenesis can facilitate soft tissue healing in selected cases.

Despite its advantages, bone transport has limitations, including prolonged treatment duration, pin-site problems, and substantial patient burden associated with circular external fixation [10, 12, 13]. Careful patient selection and close follow-up are therefore essential.

Importantly, long-term functional outcomes are critical when evaluating limb salvage procedures. In the present case, the patient was able to return to physically demanding work as a construction worker at 8-year follow-up. Objective functional assessment demonstrated a JSSF score of 88 points and a VAS pain score of 0, indicating excellent functional recovery and complete pain relief.

Taken together, this case highlights that bone transport can serve as a final limb salvage strategy in patients with infected tibial nonunion and poor local vascular conditions after multiple failed microsurgical reconstructions.

This strategy may be particularly valuable before considering amputation in similar cases.

Limitations

This report has several limitations. First, this is a single case report, and therefore the findings cannot be generalized to all patients with infected tibial nonunion and large bone defects.

Second, because this was an observational case, no direct comparison with other reconstructive strategies, such as vascularized fibular grafting or the induced membrane technique, was performed. Therefore, the superiority of bone transport over other methods cannot be established.

Third, although long-term follow-up demonstrated excellent functional outcomes, including return to heavy labor at 8 years, functional assessment was limited to the JSSF score and VAS, and additional validated outcome measures were not evaluated.

Fourth, treatment selection in this case was strongly influenced by the unique clinical context, particularly the failure of multiple microsurgical reconstructions and compromised local vascular conditions. Therefore, the applicability of this strategy should be interpreted with caution in different clinical settings.

Finally, although this case highlights an important decision-making process in complex limb salvage, further studies, including multicenter case series and comparative analyses, are required to clarify the optimal indications for bone transport in such challenging situations.

Nevertheless, this case provides clinically meaningful insight into decision-making in complex limb salvage situations.

Conclusions

Bone transport using circular external fixation is a powerful limb salvage technique for infected tibial pilon fracture nonunion with segmental bone loss and associated soft tissue defect.

In patients with compromised local vascular conditions after repeated failure of microsurgical reconstruction, this approach may be more rational than further graft- or flap-dependent procedures.

In the present case, bone transport achieved infection control, ankle arthrodesis, soft tissue healing, and durable return to heavy labor at 8-year follow-up.

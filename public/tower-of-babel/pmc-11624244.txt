Effectiveness of hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy during primary curative resection for colorectal carcinoma
Authors: Hongwei Zhou, Hui Wang, Shijie Yi, Shiyao Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposePeritoneal metastasis (PM) is the life-threatening cause of colorectal cancer patients (CRC). Adjuvant hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) plus cytoreductive surgery exhibited promising effects in preventing recurrence and increasing the survival of CRC patients. However, the outcomes of HIPEC on treating advanced CRC with risk of PM are still controversial. Here, we retrospectively examined the impact of HIPEC on preventing PM and its overall effects on patients with locally advanced CRC who underwent primary curative resection at our center.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 45 patients diagnosed with locally advanced colorectal cancer (CRC) who underwent primary curative laparoscopic surgery with proactive hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC), in conjunction with adjuvant systemic chemotherapy at our center between 2019 and 2022. An additional 55 patients with locally advanced CRC who underwent similar surgery and received adjuvant systemic chemotherapy but did not undergo HIPEC during the same period were selected as the control group. Disease-free survival (DFS), overall survival (OS), and PM incidence were compared between patients with and without HIPEC.Results and conclusionsThe cumulative PM incidence was 2.2% in the HIPEC group and 14.5% in the control group(P = 0.0347). No significant adverse effects were observed in the HIPEC group. Furthermore, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that the HIPEC correlated to better DFS [hazard ratio (HR) 0.4670, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.2305–0.9462; P = 0.0345] and extended the overall survival of CRC patients [hazard ratio (HR) 0.3978, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.1684–0.9395; P = 0.0355]. Therefore, our data supports that adjuvant HIPEC can prevent peritoneal failure in CRC patients and improve both PFS and OS survival following primary curative resection.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, prompting ongoing research into more effective treatment modalities [1, 2]. Despite advances in systemic chemotherapy and surgical techniques, the recurrence rate, particularly peritoneal metastasis (PM), poses significant clinical challenges and affects long-term prognosis [3–5]. An extensive analysis using data from 14 phase 3 clinical trials showed that the median overall survival (OS) of CRC patients with PM is only 12.6 months. This is significantly shorter compared to patients without peritoneal metastases, whose median overall survival is 20.0 months [6].

Hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC), in combination with cytoreductive surgery (CRS), has been recognized as a promising treatment option for selected CRC patients with recurrent disease confined to the peritoneum [7, 8]. HIPEC involves the intraoperative administration of heated chemotherapeutic agents directly into the abdominal cavity, which not only allows for high local concentrations of chemotherapy but also minimizes systemic toxicity. The rationale for the use of hyperthermia is based on its potential to enhance drug absorption and disrupt cancer cell proteins and structure [9, 10].

Some of previous studies have demonstrated that HIPEC along with cytoreduction can improve survival in patients with PM. Additionally, the procedural aspect of HIPEC involves specific chemotherapy agents such as oxaliplatin and cisplatin, which have shown favorable outcomes in the context of heated intraperitoneal administration [7, 11–14]. Furthermore, the selection of drugs for HIPEC and employing a rigorous selection process for patient eligibility based on factors such as the extent of disease and performance status are crucial for optimizing outcomes and minimizing complications [15, 16].

On the contrary, PRODIGE15 and PRODIGE-7 clinical trials concluded that while optimal cytoreduction improved outcomes, the addition of HIPEC did not provide a significant survival benefit [17, 18]. A multicenter clinical trial COLOPEC also showed negative results that HIPEC with oxaliplatin did not improve peritoneal metastasis-free survival [19]. Moreover, the phase 3 randomized clinical trial HIPECT4 demonstrated that adding HIPEC to surgery significantly improved the locoregional control rate and reduced peritoneal metastasis. However, it did not impact disease-free survival or overall survival [20].

Given the mixed results from various studies, there remains a need for further clinical evidence to establish standardized treatment principles. Our study focuses on a single-center, comprehensive analysis of HIPEC’s effectiveness when combined with laparoscopic radical resection of CRC. By comparing outcomes in the traditional treatment group and those receiving adjunct HIPEC, this research aims to assess whether HIPEC can help prevent PM and improve the survival of CRC patients and to evaluate the potential drawbacks in patient post-curative surgery. The focus was on the occurrence of metachronous peritoneal metastasis (PM) as the primary endpoint. The secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), the occurrence of systemic metastases. The intensification of treatment protocols as explored in our study could potentially redefine the standards of care in managing advanced colorectal malignancies, emphasizing the critical need for individualized treatment strategies based on the clinical evidence.

Patients and methods

All the patients analyzed in this study received surgery and treatment in our single center (Affiliated Nanhua Hospital, University of South China) from February 2019 to December 2022. The patients included in this study for both HIPEC and control group met the following inclusion and exclusion criteria: (1) patients with locally advanced CRC(T3N0-2M0, T4N0-2M0) were included, (2) patient with systematic metastasis was excluded, (3) patients who received standardized systematic chemotherapy following the CAPEOX regime were included, (4) patients who received radiation therapy or no systematic chemotherapy after surgery were excluded, (5) patients who have lost track and cannot be reached after surgery were excluded, and (6) patient ages between 18 and 80 were included.

Operative treatment

Firstly, pneumoperitoneum was established (conditions: 10 ~ 12 mmHg CO2); based on the tumor location, one main operating port and four auxiliary ports were selected; the mesentery was treated with an ultrasonic scalpel, and lymph nodes were cleared; blood vessels were ligated, and the mesenteric vessels were high-ligated; the lesion was excised, the abdominal cavity was rinsed with warm distilled water and aspirated, an abdominal drainage tube was placed, and the intestines were sequentially arranged under laparoscopy. Finally, trocars were removed in sequence, hemostasis was performed, and incisions were sutured in order.

Adjuvant systemic chemotherapy

The adjuvant systemic chemotherapy treatment in this study is standardized. All patients initiated their first systemic chemotherapy within four weeks after surgery, following the CAPEOX regimen, which includes capecitabine (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., National Medicine Permit No. 20133365) with or without oxaliplatin (Sichuan Huiyu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Medicine Permit No. H20213061). Each treatment cycle comprises two weeks of chemotherapy followed by one week of discontinuation, for a total of eight cycles.

Control group

The control group underwent laparoscopic radical resection of colorectal cancer combined with systemic intravenous chemotherapy in 55 cases.

HIPEC group

The HIPEC experimental group underwent the same laparoscopic radical resection and systemic intravenous chemotherapy as the control group and combined with intraperitoneal hyperthermic perfusion chemotherapy during and after surgery in 45 cases. The HIPEC treatment was performed intraoperatively (under tracheal intubation anesthesia). HIPEC was performed using one of the following strategies: (1) 2 mg raltitrexed (Nanjing Zhengda Tianqing Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Medicine Permit No. H20090325, 3 mg/m2) dissolved in saline for; (2) 100 mg oxaliplatin (Sichuan Huiyu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Medicine Permit No. H20213061, 130 mg/m2) dissolved in glucose injection solution + saline; (3) 10 mg cisplatin (Qilu Pharmaceutical (Hainan) Co., Ltd., National Medicine Approval No. H20073652, 50–120 mg/m2) dissolved in saline. The perfusion rate was 400 ~ 600 mL/min; the volume of perfusion liquid was generally 4 L, ensuring fullness of the abdominal cavity and smooth circulation; the temperature of the perfusion liquid was 43 ± 0.1 °C; the duration of perfusion was 30 min–1 h. Throughout the perfusion process, vital signs were closely monitored, and the occurrence of complications was observed.

Statistics

The recurrence and PM metastasis of patients were assessed via clinical evaluation, tumor marker detection, and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) scan every 3–6 months, up until March 30, 2024. OS and PFS were recorded and defined from the day of surgery to the date of death from any cause, or first recurrence involving any site, respectively. Chi-square test was used for univariate comparison. Kaplan–Meier survival method followed by two-tailed Log-rank tests were used in OS and PFS analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.4. P value < 0.05 was considered as statistical significance.

Discussion

This retrospective study demonstrates a significant advantage of using HIPEC in conjunction with primary curative resection and systemic chemotherapy for local advanced CRC patients in preventing PM. The observed reduction in peritoneal metastasis incidence and improvement in overall and disease-free survival among the HIPEC group underscores the potential of this treatment modality to enhance the standard care for CRC.

The substantially lower incidence of peritoneal metastasis in the HIPEC group (2.2% vs. 14.5% in the control group) aligns with previous research suggesting that HIPEC effectively targets residual microscopic disease that might not be addressed by systemic chemotherapy alone [13, 15, 24]. This is critical, considering the poor prognosis typically associated with peritoneal metastasis in CRC, where traditional management strategies have shown limited efficacy in preventing its occurrence.

Moreover, the survival benefits highlighted by the Kaplan–Meier analysis (with significant improvements in both DFS and OS for the HIPEC group) are consistent with other studies that have reported enhanced survival outcomes when HIPEC is employed at the time of primary surgery [13, 24]. These findings suggest that the intraoperative administration of chemotherapeutic agents, facilitated by hyperthermia, not only enhances drug absorption but may also disrupt cancer cell proteins more effectively, potentially leading to reduced recurrence rates and improved long-term outcomes. Considering the pivotal role of the HIPEC drug in preventing peritoneal metastasis (PM) and enhancing survival rates, we explored the use of three distinct compounds—raltitrexed, oxaliplatin, and cisplatin. The selection of a specific drug was determined by each doctor. It’s crucial to acknowledge the potential for bias in drug selection by individual doctors, particularly if there are concerns about the ineffectiveness of oxaliplatin as indicated in the COLOPEC clinical trial [19]. Increasing the group size can help mitigate this bias to a certain extent by providing a broader and more representative sample for analysis. Further, only 45 patients were enrolled in the HIPEC group from 2019 to 2022, it is impossible to compare the effectiveness between different drugs. We have been recruiting more patients into this study after 2022. Consequently, the comparison among different HIPEC drugs will be forthcoming in the next few years.

Furthermore, both our study and the HIPECT4 study consistently demonstrated that HIPEC improves relapse rates and reduces the risk of peritoneal metastases, highlighting its potential efficacy in locoregional disease control. However, differences in chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., mitomycin C in HIPECT4 vs. raltitrexed, cisplatin, and oxaliplatin in our study) and our single-center design, which allowed for more standardized surgical and HIPEC procedures, may explain variations in outcomes. While HIPECT4 reported no significant effect on DFS or OS within a 3-year analysis, our study included longer-term data. We speculate that the benefits of HIPEC may become more apparent over extended follow-up, consistent with findings from long-term studies by both P. Sammartino and D. Baratti [13, 24].

Moreover, MSI-H/dMMR CRC patients exhibited remarkable responses to immunotherapy in both metastatic and locoregional settings [21, 23]. It is worth investigating whether HIPEC could provide additional long-term benefits for these patients. While no significant difference in relapse rates was observed in this study, likely due to the small sample size, more robust conclusions can be drawn in the future with larger datasets and longer follow-up periods.

In addition, while the results are promising, they must be considered in the context of the study’s limitations. The single-center design and the relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the findings. We also keep observing and recording the status of the patients alive. Future randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes are ongoing to confirm these results and help refine patient selection criteria to optimize the benefits of HIPEC in the treatment of CRC.

It is also important to address the concerns raised by studies that question the effectiveness of HIPEC, particularly in scenarios where patients have previously undergone systemic platinum-based chemotherapy. These studies suggest that not all patients may benefit from HIPEC, highlighting the need for careful patient selection and personalized treatment approaches [17, 19, 25].

In conclusion, our findings support the integration of HIPEC into the standard treatment protocol for local advanced CRC patients. This approach could potentially redefine the management of CRC, offering a valuable therapeutic addition that may prolong survival and improve quality of life. Nonetheless, continued research and clinical trials are essential to fully understand the implications of HIPEC in CRC treatment and to establish robust guidelines for its implementation.

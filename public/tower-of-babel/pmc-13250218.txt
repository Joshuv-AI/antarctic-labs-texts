Feasibility of NIV-NAVA as a primary respiratory support and its clinical impacts in a targeted group of preterm infants: protocol for a prospective observational study
Authors: Hannah Cho, Young Hwa Jung, Jiseon Park, Hyo Ju Yang, Mi Jin Kim, Juyoung Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNon-invasive neurally adjusted ventilatory assist (NIV-NAVA) is a promising respiratory support method for avoiding invasive mechanical ventilation in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs). However, its effectiveness and safety have not been widely established through clinical evidence. In this study, we aim to evaluate the feasibility of NIV-NAVA as a primary respiratory support method after birth and its effects on short-term clinical outcomes and long-term neurodevelopment in preterm infants.Methods and analysisIn this prospective multicentre observational study, 230 preterm infants will be recruited after birth. This study will include preterm infants born between 27+0 and 31+6 weeks of gestation who require respiratory support within the first 48 hours after birth. NIV-NAVA will be initiated as primary respiratory support either immediately after birth or during the early NICU stay, with settings adjusted according to each infant’s clinical condition. Discontinuation of NIV-NAVA or transition to continuous positive airway pressure or high-flow nasal cannula will be performed based on clinical stability. A less invasive surfactant administration method will be used for infants with respiratory distress syndrome to avoid intubation whenever possible. Infants intubated at birth will be extubated to NIV-NAVA as early as is clinically feasible. Invasive ventilation may be applied if clinical deterioration occurs. The rate of NIV-NAVA failure, duration of non-invasive and invasive ventilation, rate of bronchopulmonary dysplasia and clinical outcomes, such as air leak, patent ductus arteriosus, intraventricular haemorrhage and retinopathy of prematurity, will be measured. The neurodevelopmental outcomes of infants will be assessed for up to 3 years.Ethics and disseminationThe Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Korea University Anam Hospital (2024AN0554) and Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (IRB No. B-2507-984-304) approved this study. Introduction

Despite the declining birth rate worldwide, the number and proportion of preterm births are increasing. In particular, very preterm infants born at less than 32 weeks of gestation and requiring neonatal intensive care are at a high risk for growth and neurodevelopmental issues.1 As recent advancements in neonatal intensive care have improved the survival rate of preterm infants, long-term morbidities have also become an important factor in ensuring that these infants grow into healthy members of society.

Neurally adjusted ventilatory assist (NAVA) is a mechanical ventilation method that adjusts the degree and timing of ventilatory assistance based on an infant’s neural respiratory drive.2 Inspiratory signals from the respiratory centre are converted into electrical activity of the diaphragm (Edi) through the phrenic nerve, which determines the initiation, intensity and duration of inspiration and expiration.2 3 This mechanism prevents excessive or insufficient respiratory assistance, improves patient-ventilator synchrony and reduces the mismatch between spontaneous breathing and mechanical ventilation.24,6

Non-invasive NAVA (NIV-NAVA) provides respiratory assistance through nasal prongs or masks instead of an intra-tracheal tube.7 Non-invasive respiratory support reduces ventilator-induced lung injury by preventing invasive mechanical ventilation.8 Combining non-invasive support with NAVA is thought to be more beneficial in decreasing immediate respiratory severity and apnoea in preterm infants.9 10 However, evidence regarding the clinical outcomes of NIV-NAVA in preterm infants remains limited and long-term outcomes are currently lacking.

In this study, we aim to investigate the clinical outcomes associated with the use of NIV-NAVA in preterm infants in a focused group of infants born between 27 and 31 weeks of gestation in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs). The primary goal of this study is to determine the feasibility of NIV-NAVA as the primary respiratory support method immediately after birth. We will also explore the impact of this NIV-NAVA application on short-term clinical outcomes and long-term neurodevelopment in this targeted group of preterm infants.

Sample size calculation

In this prospective observational study, 80 participants in the NICU of Korea University Anam Hospital and 150 participants in the NICU of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital will be recruited over a 2-year period based on the number of previous patients admitted to these NICUs.

If other sites are to be included in this study, their sample size will be determined based on the number of previous patients admitted to each NICU.

Intervention and observation conditions

Respiratory assistance with NIV-NAVA will be initiated within 48 hours of birth in patients requiring respiratory support. Infants will be considered to require respiratory support if they meet one or more of the following criteria: presence of respiratory distress such as tachypnoea, retractions or grunting; requirement for supplemental oxygen to maintain target oxygen saturation (90–95%); recurrent apnoea requiring stimulation and overall clinical assessment. This may occur immediately after birth/admission to the NICU or at a later time.

The settings of respiratory support will be adjusted based on the clinical condition of each participant, including oxygen requirement, blood gas parameters and work of breathing. The participants’ vital signs, including oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate and respiratory rate, will be continuously monitored using standard bedside monitors in the NICU. Additionally, Edi will be monitored in real time to assess neural breathing patterns and patient-ventilator interactions. The clinical team will regularly verify the quality of the Edi signal, confirm correct catheter placement and ensure acquisition of stable Edi waveforms. Clinical assessments, including work of breathing and lung auscultation, will be performed by nursing and medical staff at least every 2 hours. Blood gas analysis will be conducted as clinically indicated to supplement the continuous monitoring data. Weaning from NIV-NAVA will be performed gradually by stepwise reduction in NAVA levels, guided by clinical stability and Edi activity. NIV-NAVA may be de-escalated to nasal CPAP or HFNC when stability criteria are met, including improved work of breathing (Edi peak <5–15 μV and NAVA level <1.0 cmH2O/μV), not increased oxygen requirement and fewer than two to three episodes of significant apnoea requiring intervention within 24 hours. Discontinuation of respiratory support will be considered when the infant demonstrates sustained clinical stability without signs of respiratory distress and maintains adequate oxygenation and ventilation with minimal support.

In cases of respiratory distress syndrome (RDS) requiring a fraction of inspired oxygen >0.3 with clinical symptoms of distress, lung surfactants will be administered via the less invasive surfactant administration method, and endotracheal intubation will be avoided whenever possible.

If the participant is intubated in the delivery room immediately after birth, typically according to Neonatal Resuscitation Program (NRP) guidelines (eg, for surfactant administration or sustained positive pressure ventilation), they will receive invasive respiratory support during initial stabilisation and transport to the NICU. The participant will be extubated to NIV-NAVA as soon as possible based on the physician’s judgement. NIV-NAVA failure is defined as the need for escalation to endotracheal intubation during NIV-NAVA application and the initiation of invasive mechanical ventilation based on clinical criteria, including persistent hypoxaemia despite increasing support, respiratory acidosis, recurrent apnoea requiring intervention or overall clinical deterioration. Data handling and confidentiality

All study data will be recorded using electronic case record forms. Data corrections will be performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions and timeline. The collected data will be reviewed in detail. Each data collection form and the specific information required will be discussed in detail on an item-by-item basis. The training sessions will also address data entry processes, data discrepancy queries and best practices to ensure high-quality research data.

Data integrity will be ensured through password protection, referential data rules, range checks and consistency checks during data entry and storage. Any data modifications will be documented through a data change or inquiry system. The data entered into the database will be accessible for review through a data entry application, with user activities regulated according to the privileges assigned to their unique user identification codes and passwords. Data errors will be identified using programmes designed to detect missing and erroneous data. These errors will be summarised with detailed descriptions in the Data Query Reports, which will be forwarded to the study investigators. Upon receipt of such queries, study personnel will verify inconsistencies by reviewing the original forms and other sources, making necessary corrections and providing responses to queries. Investigators will be required to verify the accuracy of the completed case report forms by signing key documents as appropriate. To maintain confidentiality, the source documents will be securely stored on a locally encrypted server. The only study documents containing the names of the participants will be the enrolment and informed consent forms. Hard copies of the data collection forms will be kept locked when they are not actively used. Access to data files containing personal identification information will be restricted to the principal investigators and essential study personnel. All study-related documentation will be retained for a minimum of 3 years following the completion of the study.

Data analysis plan

Continuous values will be summarised as means with SD or medians with IQRs. Categorical variables will be summarised as the proportion of participants in each category. Differences between groups of categorical variables will be analysed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, whereas continuous variables will be assessed using an independent t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test. The level of significance for the study is 5% (two-sided) for all analyses.

Study coordination and monitoring

Once the participant is enrolled, the study personnel will make every reasonable effort to comply with the study’s ventilator strategies (figure 1) and will follow the infant throughout the study period. The study investigators and staff will provide written feedback on the infant development assessment results. Participants could withdraw from the study at any time for any reason. The investigator may also withdraw participants from the study if they are unwilling or unable to comply with the required procedures after consultation with the study staff.

This study will be monitored by the Clinical Trial Center of the Korea University Anam Hospital, Seoul, South Korea. Independent data-monitoring and safety-monitoring committee members will assess patient safety and trial progress every 12 months.

Ethics and dissemination

The Institutional Review Boards (IRB) of Korea University Anam Hospital (IRB No. 2024AN0554) and Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (IRB No. B-2507-984-304) reviewed and approved this study. Written informed consent will be obtained from the parents of each preterm infant (online supplemental material). All methods will be conducted with strict adherence to the IRB-approved protocol and in full compliance with all pertinent guidelines and regulations in South Korea.

The results of this study will be disseminated to the local and global research communities, including the Korean Ministry of Health and Welfare. Dissemination modes will include abstracts, reports and presentations. We will submit separate articles for open-access publication in the relevant journals.

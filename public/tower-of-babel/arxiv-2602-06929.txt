Proton-Size Resolution of the Hyperfine Puzzle in Hydrogen
Gerald A. Miller 1

Abstract
Baym and Farrar (arXiv:2601.02300v1) have recently pointed out a puzzle in understanding the role of the hyperfine interaction in the ground state of a hydrogen atom. If one uses a variational wave function in which the Bohr radius, a 0 a_{0} is replaced by a variational radius parameter, R R , first-order perturbation theory can give a contribution to the energy proportional to − 1 / R 3 -1/R^{3} . This raises the question of why the hyperfine interaction does not lead to collapse of hydrogen. I show that including the effects of the non-zero size of the proton leads to a resolution of the puzzle such that the variational procedure yields a value of R R that is indistinguishable from a 0 a_{0} .

Introduction

The hyperfine interaction in hydrogen is a perturbation of order
\alpha^{2}{m_{e}\over m_{p}}
that splits the energy levels of different total (electron plus proton) spin states
Eides et al. 2007
. In a singlet state the hyperfine interaction is attractive, and neglecting the non-zero size of the proton, causes an interaction proportional to a three-dimensional Dirac delta function
so that its expectation value is
\propto 1/R^{3}
, where
R
is a parameter characterizes the size of the system.

Baym & Farrar
Baym and Farrar 2026
show that if one takes a variational approach to the hydrogen atom, with
the wave function given by

\displaystyle\phi_{R}(r)={e^{-r/R}\over\sqrt{\pi R^{3}}},

(1)

a contribution to the energy for the spin-singlet state,
E_{\rm hf,0}=-8{|\mu_{e}|\mu_{p}\over R^{3}}
, results.
Here
\mu_{e,p}
are the magnetic moments of the electron and proton. This causes the energy to diverge to
-\infty
as the value of
R
approaches 0. Ref.
Baym and Farrar 2026
further explains that for values of
R
smaller than the size of the proton the hyperfine interaction overwhelms the contribution of the kinetic energy and the system collapses. A resolution is proposed that uses the relativistic effects of the Dirac equation. In that approach the magnetic moment of the electron depends on the variational parameter,
R
.

I discuss the energy as a function of
R
and propose a resolution based on the non-zero size of the proton. The wave function
\phi_{R}(r)
is the exact solution for the non-relativistic hydrogen ground state when
R=a_{0}
, the Bohr radius. The kinetic energy for this wave function is
1/(2mR^{2})
where
m
is the electron mass, and the Coulomb energy is
-\alpha/R
, with
\alpha
the fine structure constant. The difference between the electron mass and the reduced mass,
mM/(m+M)
(
M
is the proton mass) is ignored here and in Ref.
Baym and Farrar 2026
. Neglecting the hyperfine interaction, the expectation value of the energy is given by

\displaystyle E_{0}(R)={1\over 2mR^{2}}-{\alpha\over R}.

(2)

This quantity has a minimum of
E_{0}=-m\alpha^{2}/2
at
R=1/m\alpha=a_{0}
.

The Hamiltonian of the electron-proton hyperfine interaction between the electron and the proton is given by

\displaystyle H_{\rm hf}(r)=|\mu_{e}|\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{e}\cdot{\bf H}(r),

(3)

where
|\mu_{e}|=e/(2m)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{e}
is the electron Pauli matrix. The operator
{\bf H}(r)
is given by an equation on page 58 of Bjorken and Drell
Bjorken and Drell 1965
:

\displaystyle{\bf H}(r)=-{g_{p}\over 2M}\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\rho(r^{\prime})\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\times\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}){1\over 4\pi|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|},

with
\rho(r^{\prime})
the magnetic moment density of the proton. This function takes the non-zero size of the proton into account.
The proton gyromagnetic ratio,
g_{p}=2.79
Using the relation
\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\times\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$})=(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\nabla^{2}-\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$})
and taking the angular average for spherically symmetric wave functions,
one finds

\displaystyle{\bf H}(r)=-{2\over 3}{g_{p}e\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\over 2M}\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\rho(r^{\prime})\nabla^{2}{1\over 4\pi|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle={2\over 3}{g_{p}e\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\over 2M}\rho(r).

(5)

The density
\rho(r)
is the three-dimensional Fourier transform of the Sachs magnetic form factor
G_{M}/\mu_{p}
when computing the hyperfine interaction. I use the well-known dipole form

\displaystyle G_{D}(\vec{q}\,^{2})={G_{M}(\vec{q}\,^{2})\over\mu_{p}}={1\over(1+{\vec{q}\,^{2}\over\Lambda^{2}})^{2}},

(6)

with
\Lambda=0.843\,{\rm GeV}
.
This form is sufficiently accurate for the present purpose of showing that including a non-zero proton size is sufficient to resolve the hyperfine puzzle. Note that the usual scalar argument
-q^{2}
is here taken as
\vec{q}\,^{2}
because the energy transfer in the one-photon exchange interaction may be neglected. The most recent version of
G_{M}
is given in Ref.
Hague et al. 2025
.
The Fourier transform of
G_{D}
is then obtained to be

\displaystyle\rho(r)={\Lambda^{3}\over 8\pi}e^{-\Lambda r}.

(7)

This function approaches a three-dimensional delta function in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
. If so, the expression for
{\bf H}
reduces to that of Ref.
Baym and Farrar 2026
.
One might think that the momentum scale of 0.843 GeV is irrelevant for the ground-state energy of the hydrogen atom
because
\Lambda=2.26\times 10^{5}/a_{0}
.
This is not the case, as is next demonstrated.

The expectation value of the hyperfine integral is

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{R}|{\bf H}|\phi_{R}\rangle={8e\over 3}{g_{p}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\over 2M}{1\over R^{3}}{\Lambda^{4}R^{3}\over(2+\Lambda R)^{3}},

(8)

and the resulting contribution of the hyperfine interaction to the energy of the hydrogen atom is

\displaystyle E_{\rm hf}(R)={8\over 3}{g_{p}\pi\alpha\over mM}{1\over R^{3}}{\Lambda^{3}R^{3}\over(2+\Lambda R)^{3}}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{e}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}_{p}\rangle.

(9)

This expression also reduces that of Ref.
Baym and Farrar 2026
in the limit that
\Lambda\to\infty,
and importantly is finite-valued at
R=0
. The total energy,
E_{1}
is now the sum of two terms:

\displaystyle E_{1}(R)=E_{0}(R)+E_{\rm hf}(R).

(10)

The next step is to assess the influence of the term
E_{\rm hf}(R)
. This is done using two methods. The first is an estimate using closed-form expressions. The second is numerical evaluation. The estimate is obtained by expanding
E(R)
about the point
R=a_{0}
. This gives:

\displaystyle E_{1}(R)\approx E_{0}(a_{0})+{1\over 2}(R-a_{0})^{2}E^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{0}(a_{0})+E_{\rm hf}(a_{0})+(R-a_{0})E^{{}^{\prime}}_{\rm hf}(a_{0}).

(11)

The using
\Lambda a_{0}\gg 2
, the minimum of
E(R)
occurs at

\displaystyle R-a_{0}\approx-{E^{{}^{\prime}}_{\rm hf}(a_{0})\over E^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{0}(a_{0})}=24\pi\alpha g_{p}{1\over M},

(12)

for the spin-singlet state. This shift in the value of
R
at the minimum of energy proportional to the proton Compton wave length and is 6
\times 10^{-6}\,a_{0}
. This is a truly negligible change in the value of
R
at which
E(R)
is minimized.

The next step is to display the energy as a function of the variational parameter. The hugely different momentum scales (
\Lambda,1/a_{0}
) makes it efficient to use a rescaled variable
x=R/a_{0}
and study the dimension-less quantities

\epsilon_{i}=a_{0}/\alpha E_{i}(x)
. The results are shown in Figs. 1. The minima occur, as expected, at
x=1
. There is essentially no difference between the curves for
\epsilon_{0}
and
\epsilon_{1}
, as shown in Fig. 1. The upper part displays results for
x
greater than about
x=0.4
. A log plot for smaller values of
x
is also shown to make it clear that there is no other minimum than the one around
x=1
.

The results shown in Fig. 1 do not depend on the details of the magnetic form factor. Any form of
\rho(r)
, controlled by the proton size, reducing to a three-dimensional delta function in the limit that the size parameter vanishes, would give results nearly identical to those of Fig. 1.

In conclusion, including the effects of the non-zero size of the proton causes the effects of the hyperfine interaction in the singlet state to have virtually no impact on the energy of the state for all values of the variational parameter,
R
.

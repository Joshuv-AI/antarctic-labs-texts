Odd Shifted Parking Functions
Zachary Hamaker, Jesse Kim

Abstract
Stanley recently introduced the shifted parking function symmetric function S ​ H n SH_{n} , which is the shiftification of Haiman’s parking function symmetric function P ​ F n PF_{n} . The function S ​ H n SH_{n} lives in the subalgebra of symmetric functions generated by odd power sums. Stanley showed how to expand S ​ H n SH_{n} into the V V –basis of this algebra, which is indexed by partitions with all parts odd and is analogous to the complete homogeneous (or elementary) basis of symmetric functions. We introduce odd shifted parking functions to give combinatorial and representation-theoretic realizations of the V V –expansion of S ​ H n SH_{n} , resolving the main open problem in his paper. Further, we present two representation-theoretic realizations of shiftification allowing us to interpret S ​ H n SH_{n} as the spin character of a projective representation. We conclude with further directions, including a relationship between S ​ H n SH_{n} and Haglund’s ( q , t ) (q,t) –Schröder theorem.

2.1
Parking functions

For
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathtt{sort}(a)=\pi\cdot a
where
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
satisfies
a_{\pi(1)}\leq\dots\leq a_{\pi(n)}
.
As stated in the introduction, a
parking function
of
size

n
is a tuple
p
of
n
positive integers so that for

the
i
th entry in
\mathtt{sort}(p)
is at most
i
.
We say
p
is
sorted
if
p=\mathtt{sort}(p)
.
Let
\mathrm{Pf}(n)
be the set of all parking functions of size
n
and
\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)
be the set of sorted parking functions of size
n
.
It is a classical result that
|\mathrm{Pf}(n)|=(n{+}1)^{n-1}
and
|\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)|
is the
n
th Catalan number
c_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
.

The
content
of

is
\alpha(p)=(\alpha_{1}(p),\dots,\alpha_{n}(p))
where

\alpha_{k}(p)=|\{j\in[n]:p_{j}=k\}|,

and the

of

is the partition
\lambda(p)
obtained by reversing
\mathtt{sort}(\alpha)
.
For example, the parking function
p=(4,1,1,3,6,3)
has
\mathtt{sort}(p)=(1,1,3,3,4,6)
,
\alpha(p)=(2,0,2,1,0,1)
and
\lambda(p)=(2,2,1,1)
.

Introduced in
[
13
, §4]
, a
naive shifted parking function
of
size

n
is a pair of
n
–tuples
(p,\sigma)
where
p
is a parking function and
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Define
\mathtt{sort}(p,\sigma)=(\mathtt{sort}(p),\overline{\sigma})
where

\overline{\sigma}_{k}=\begin{cases}\prod_{p_{i}=k}\sigma_{i}&\alpha_{k}(p)>0\\
0&\alpha_{k}(p)=0\end{cases}

is the parity of
\sigma_{i}
’s where
p_{i}=k
when this quantity is defined.
A
sorted naive shifted parking function
of size
n
is a pair
(p,\overline{\sigma})
where
p
is a sorted parking function and
\overline{\sigma}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}
where
\overline{\sigma}_{k}=0
if and only if
\alpha_{k}(p)=0
.
The naive shifted parking function
(p,\sigma)
inherits its content and shape from
p
.
Let
\mathrm{NShPf}(n)
be the set of naive shifted parking functions of size
n
and
\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}(n)
be the set of sorted naive shifted parking functions of size
n
.
It is easy to see

, while Stanley observes

is the
n
th large Schröder number
[
11
, A006318]
.
We present a bijective proof of this fact in Section
4.1
.

2.2
Symmetric Functions

Let
\mathrm{Sym}
denote the algebra of symmetric functions.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
or
[
12
]
for descriptions of the power sum, elementary, homogeneous, Schur symmetric functions and the Hall inner product.

Our results concern
\mathrm{SymP}=\langle p_{2k+1}:k\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
, the subalgebra of
odd symmetric functions
in
\mathrm{Sym}
.
The
shiftification operator

\mathtt{sh}:\mathrm{Sym}\to\mathrm{SymP}
is defined by
h_{k}\mapsto 2P_{k}
where

P_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}h_{i}e_{k-i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{k}h_{i}e_{k-i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\textrm{ even}\end{subarray}}^{k}h_{i}e_{k-i},

(3)

or equivalently where
p_{2k-1}\mapsto 2p_{2k+1}
and
p_{2k}\mapsto 0
for
k
a positive integer.
Note
\mathtt{sh}(e_{k})=(-1)^{k}\cdot 2P_{k}
.
We observe that shiftification is self-adjoint with respect to the Hall inner product.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
f,g\in\mathrm{Sym}
,
\langle\mathtt{sh}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathtt{sh}g\rangle
.

Proof.

Since the power sum basis is orthogonal with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\mathtt{sh}
is diagonal with respect to the power sum basis the result follows.
∎

Define the generating functions

A=P_{1}t+P_{3}t^{3}+P_{5}t^{5}+\dots\quad\mbox{and}\quad B=P_{2}t^{2}+P_{4}t^{4}+P_{6}t^{6}+\dots,

which by
[
13
, Lem 3.1]
satisfy the relation

B=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4A^{2}}}{2},

or equivalently
A^{2}=B^{2}+B
.
Taking the coefficient of
t^{2k}
gives

\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{2i}\cdot P_{2k-2i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{2i-1}\cdot P_{2k-2i+1},

(4)

which is
1
.
For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
a partition, define
V_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_{\lambda_{i}}
.
From (
4
) we see the
V_{\lambda}
’s are not linearly independent.
However,
\{V_{\lambda}:\lambda\ \vdash n\ \text{odd}\}
is a basis for
\mathrm{SymP}
.
There are other bases for
\mathrm{SymP}
, most notably the Schur-
P
and Schur-
Q
functions indexed by strict partitions, which figure less prominently in our work.

The
parking function symmetric function
is

PF_{n}=\sum_{p\in\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)}h_{\lambda(p)}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{1}{(n-\ell(\lambda)+1)}\binom{n}{m_{1}(\lambda),\dots,m_{n}(\lambda),n-\ell(\lambda)}h_{\lambda}.

(5)

The coefficient of
h_{\lambda}
in the second sum is the

\mathrm{Krew}(\lambda)
.
We are interested in understanding the
shifted parking function symmetric function

SH_{n}=\mathtt{sh}(PF_{n})
.
By applying
\mathtt{sh}
to the right-hand-side of (
5
), we have

SH_{n}=\sum_{p\in\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)}2^{\ell(\lambda(p))}V_{\lambda(p)}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{2^{\ell(\lambda)}}{(n-\ell(\lambda)+1)}\binom{n}{m_{1}(\lambda),\dots,m_{n}(\lambda),n-\ell(\lambda)}V_{\lambda}.

(6)

Since there are
2^{\ell(\lambda(p))}
choices for
\overline{\sigma}
associated to each sorted
p
, we also have

SH_{n}=\sum_{(p,\overline{\sigma})\in\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}(n)}V_{\lambda(p)}.

Since we are summing over
V_{\lambda}
of every shape, the expansion in (
6
) is not unique.
In
[
13
]
, Stanley used generating function arguments to give several expansions of
SH_{n}
into bases of
\mathrm{SymP}
.
Most notably for our purposes, he showed:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
13
, Thm. 3.4]
)
.

For every
n
,
SH_{n}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n\ \text{odd}}\mathrm{OKrew}(\lambda)V_{\lambda}
with

\mathrm{OKrew}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{\ell}(\lambda)}{\ell(\lambda)!}\binom{\ell(\lambda)}{m_{1}(\lambda),m_{3}(\lambda),\dots}(n+\ell(\lambda)-1)(n+\ell(\lambda)-3)...(n-\ell(\lambda)+3)V_{\lambda}.

For
\lambda
odd, we call
\mathrm{OKrew}(\lambda)
the
odd Kreweras number
of
\lambda
.

2.3
Frobenius Characters and Spin Characters

Let
Z_{n}
denote the algebra of class functions of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. The
Frobenius characteristic map
is the linear isomorphism
\mathtt{ch}:Z_{n}\to\mathrm{Sym}
defined by

\mathtt{ch}(\chi)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}z_{\lambda}^{-1}\chi(\pi_{\lambda})p_{\lambda}.

(7)

Here
\pi_{\lambda}
is a permutation with cycle type
\lambda
and
z_{\lambda}
is the size of the stabilizer of
\pi_{\lambda}
under conjugation.
The
Frobenius character
of a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-module
V
is the image of the character of
V
under
\mathtt{ch}
.

Under the Frobenius characteristic map, addition in
\mathrm{Sym}
corresponds to direct sum of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules and multiplication in
\mathrm{Sym}
corresponds to the
induction product
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules. Given a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
of
n
, the Young subgroup corresponding to
\alpha
is

S_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{\alpha_{k}},

(8)

viewed as the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
in which
S_{\alpha_{1}}
permutes
\{1,\dots,\alpha_{1}\}
,
S_{\alpha_{2}}
permutes
\{\alpha_{1}+1,\dots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\}
, etc. If
W_{i}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha_{i}}
-representation, then

\mathtt{ch}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}]}W_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_{\alpha_{k}})=\mathtt{ch}(W_{\alpha_{1}})\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathtt{ch}(W_{\alpha_{k}})

(9)

Pointwise multiplication in
Z_{n}
induces a second product on
\mathrm{Sym}
, called the
Kronecker product
or
inner product
.
By analogy, the
Kronecker product
of two symmetric functions
f=\mathtt{ch}(\chi_{V})
and
g=\mathtt{ch}(\chi_{W})
is

f*g=\mathtt{ch}(\chi_{V}\chi_{W})

Equivalently, if
V
and
W
are
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-modules with Frobenius characters
f
and
g
, respectively, then
f*g
is the Frobenius character of
V\otimes W
.
This product extends linearly to all of
\mathrm{Sym}
.

Much as
\mathrm{Sym}
encodes the characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
representations, the subalgebra
\mathrm{SymP}
encodes the character theory of spin representations of the double cover of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We refer readers to
[
14
]
for details beyond what we include here (see also
[
9
]
).
The
negative-type double cover
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is

\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{-}=\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1},z:z^{2}=1,\ s_{i}^{2}=z,\ s_{i}s_{j}=zs_{j}s_{i}\textrm{ for }|i-j|\geq 2,\ s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}=zs_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}\rangle.

For each odd partition of
n
or distinct partition

so that

is odd, there are two conjugacy classes in
\mathfrak{S}^{-}_{n}
, one of which is distinguished as
positive
.
There is one conjugacy class in
\mathfrak{S}^{-}_{n}

n
.
See
[

, §2]

A

\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{-}

acts by
-1
.
Stembridge defines the

\mathtt{ch}^{\prime}
from the algebra of spin class functions of

\mathtt{ch}^{\prime}(\psi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\vdash n\\
\lambda\textrm{ odd}\end{subarray}}z_{\lambda}^{-1}2^{\ell(\lambda)/2}\psi(\pi_{\lambda})p_{\lambda}

where
\pi_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{-}
is in the positive conjugacy class associated to

.

Since every irreducible representation of

is either a
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–representation or a spin representation, the algebra of

Using Stembridge’s notation, for

To build an

Proposition 2.7

Proof.

For

.
Then

For

\xi_{J}
permutes this basis, with no fixed points unless

Therefore, the trace of
\xi_{J}

Let

\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\lambda_{j}-1}(\xi_{r_{j}}-\xi_{r_{j+1}})(\xi_{r_{j}+1}-\xi_{r_{j}+2})\cdots(\xi_{r_{j}+\lambda_{j}-1}-\xi_{r_{j}+\lambda_{j}})

to the identity

∎

4
Problems and Extensions

4.1
Enumerative aspects

From the definition, it is easy to see

\#\{(p,\overline{\sigma})\in\mathrm{NShPf}(n):\lambda(p)=\mu\}=2^{\ell}(\mu)\mathrm{Krew}(\mu).

Since there are direct combinatorial arguments showing
\mathrm{Krew}(\mu)
counts sorted parking functions with shape
\mu
, this identity has a combinatorial proof.
By contrast, we have the open problem:

Problem 4.1
.

For
\mu\vdash n
odd, give a direct combinatorial argument showing

\mathrm{OKrew}(\mu)=\#\{(p,\overline{\sigma})\in\overline{\mathrm{OShPf}}(n):\lambda(p)=\mu\}.

One potentially useful fact for tackling Problem
4.1
is the observation:

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\mu\vdash n
odd and
\ell=\ell(\mu)
, we have

\mathrm{OKrew}(\mu)=\mathrm{Krew}(\mu)\cdot{\binom{n+\ell}{\frac{n+\ell}{2}}}{\Bigg/}{\binom{n}{\frac{n+\ell}{2}}}

Proof.

Let
m_{i}=m_{i}(\mu)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{OKrew}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ell}}{\ell!}\binom{\ell}{m_{1},m_{3},\dots}(n+\ell-1)(n+\ell-3)\cdots(n-\ell+3)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ell}}{\ell!}\binom{\ell}{m_{1},m_{3},\dots}\frac{(n+\ell)!(\frac{n-\ell}{2})!}{2^{\ell}\frac{n+\ell}{2}!(n-\ell+1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{n-\ell+1,m_{1},m_{3},\dots}\frac{(n+\ell)!(\frac{n-\ell}{2})!}{\frac{n+\ell}{2}!n!}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Krew}(\lambda)\frac{(n+\ell)!(\frac{n-\ell}{2})!}{n!\frac{n+\ell}{2}!}=\textrm{Krew}(\lambda){\binom{n+\ell}{\frac{n+\ell}{2}}}{\Bigg/}{\binom{n}{\frac{n+\ell}{2}}}.

∎

As mentioned previously, Stanley observed
\#\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}(n)
is the
n
th
large Schröder number

s_{n}
, which is defined by the initial conditions and recurrence

s_{0}=1,\quad s_{1}=2,\quad s_{n}=3s_{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}s_{k}s_{n-k-1}\ (n\geq 2).

Therefore
\#\overline{\mathrm{OShPf}}(n)=s_{n}
as well.
The large Schröder number
s_{n}
enumerates
Schröder paths
of length
n
, which are lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
with steps
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
and
(1,1)
staying weakly above the line
y=x
.
There is a straightforward bijection from
\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}(n)
to Schröder paths extending the classical bijection from sorted parking functions to Dyck paths.
For each positive integer
i

•

if
\overline{\sigma}_{i}=1
or
0
, add
\alpha_{i}(p)
steps
(0,1)
, followed a step
(1,0)
;

•

if
\overline{\sigma}_{i}=-1
, add
\alpha_{i}(p)-1
steps
(0,1)
followed by a step
(1,1)
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
can be used to give a bijection between odd and naive sorted shifted parking functions, as we now explain.
Given a sorted naive shifted parking function
(p,\overline{\sigma})
with auxiliary matching
\tau
, let
p^{\prime}
be obtained from
p
by replacing exactly one
i
with a
j
for every
(i,j)\in\tau
.
Let
\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}
be obtained from
\overline{\sigma}
by setting
\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}_{j}=-\overline{\sigma}_{i}
if
(i,j)\in\tau
and
\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}_{j}=\overline{\sigma}_{j}
otherwise. Then the map

(p,\overline{\sigma})\mapsto(p^{\prime},\overline{\sigma}^{\prime},\tau)

(14)

defines a bijection from
\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}
to
\overline{\mathrm{OShPf}}
.

We were unable to find any objects enumerated by large Schröder numbers placing a similar emphasis on odd parity in its constituent components, suggesting
\overline{\mathrm{OShPf}}(n)
could be the first instance in a new and combinatorially distinct family of large Schröder objects.

4.2
Algebraic aspects

As discussed in the introduction, a major motivation for studying the parking function symmetric function is its bigraded form

PF_{n}(q,t)=\sum_{p\in\mathrm{Pf}(n)}t^{\mathrm{area}(p)}q^{\mathrm{dinv}(p)}F_{n,\textrm{ides}(p)}.

(15)

Here
\mathrm{dinv}
and
\mathrm{area}
are
\mathbb{N}
–valued statistics on
p
and
ides
is a set-valued statistic on
p
.
The Shuffle conjecture, now a theorem
[
3
]
, says
PF_{n}(q,t)=\nabla e_{n}
where
\nabla
is Bergeron and Garsia’s celebrated
nabla operator

[
1
]
.
Define

C_{n}(q,t)=\sum_{p\in\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)}t^{\mathrm{area}(p)}q^{\mathrm{dinv}(p)}

As Haglund and Garsia showed
[
5
]
,
C_{n}(q,t)
is related to
\nabla e_{n}
by

\langle\nabla e_{n},e_{n}\rangle=C_{n}(q,t),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Hall inner product on symmetric functions.
Equivalently,
C_{n}(q,t)
is the coefficient of
s_{1^{n}}
of
\nabla e_{n}
.
In
[
7
]
, Haiman showed
\nabla e_{n}
is the bi-graded Frobenius character for the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–module of
diagonal harmonics

\mathcal{D}_{n}=(\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}])/\mathcal{I}_{n}

where
\mathcal{I}_{n}
is the degree
\geq 1
invariants under the diagonal
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–action.

Applying shiftification, we obtain a
(q,t)
–analogue
SH_{n}(q,t)=\mathtt{sh}PF_{n}(q,t)
of the shifted parking function symmetric function.
By Proposition
1.1
, we have:

Proposition 4.3
.

For
n\geq 1
,
SH_{n}(q,t)
is the bigraded Frobenius character of
(D_{n}\otimes\Lambda_{n})
.

Note, in the natural presentation of
D_{n}\otimes\Lambda_{n}
, we now have two sets of commuting variables and one set of anti-commuting variables modulo the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–invariants from the diagonal action on the commuting variables.
This is somewhat reminiscent of the
super diagonal coinvariant algebra
introduced in
[
16
]
, and more generally the
(r,s)
–
bosonoic-fermionic coinvariant algebra

R^{(r,s)}_{n}
from
[
2
]
, which has
r
sets of commuting variables and
s
sets of anti-commuting variables, modulo the space of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–invariants.
However, while Proposition
4.3
is closely related to
R^{(2,1)}_{n}
, we are not modding out invariants in the anticommuting variables.

We will see
SH_{n}(q,t)
is closely related to Haglund’s results on Schröder paths.
Let
\mathrm{S}(n)
be the set of Schröder paths of size
n
and
\mathrm{S}_{k}(n)
be the subset of Schröder paths with
k
horizontal steps.
In
[
4
]
statistics
\mathrm{area}
and
\mathrm{bounce}
on Schröder paths were introduced so that:

Theorem 4.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

For
k\in[0,n]
,

\langle\nabla e_{n},h_{k}e_{n-k}\rangle=\sum_{P\in\mathrm{S}_{k}(n)}q^{\mathrm{area}(P)}t^{\mathrm{bounce}(P)}.

(16)

Summing (
16
) over all values
k
, then applying Proposition
2.1
and (
3
), we obtain

\langle\nabla e_{n},2P_{n}\rangle=\langle\mathtt{sh}\nabla e_{n},h_{n}\rangle=\langle SH_{n}(q,t),h_{n}\rangle=\sum_{s\in\mathrm{S}(n)}q^{\mathrm{area}(s)}t^{\mathrm{bounce}(s)}.

(17)

Schröder paths can be identified with sorted naive shifted parking functions as discussed in Section
4.1
.
Therefore, we can interpret (
17
) as a statement about sorted naive shifted parking functions with appropriate statistics.

It is natural to ask:

Problem 4.5
.

Are there statistics
\mathrm{stat}_{1}
and
\mathrm{stat}_{2}
on sorted odd shifted parking functions so that

\langle\nabla e_{n},2P_{n}\rangle=\langle SH_{n}(q,t),h_{n}\rangle=\sum_{(p,\overline{\sigma},\tau)\in\overline{\mathrm{OShPf}}(n)}q^{\mathrm{stat}_{1}(p,\overline{\sigma},\tau)}t^{\mathrm{stat}_{2}(p,\overline{\sigma},\tau)}?

Alternatively, it is plausible that
SH_{n}
has a different
q,t
–analogue more naturally associated to odd shifted parking functions.
Perhaps this would be based on a bi-grading of the projective representation associated to odd shifted parking functions.
It would then be natural to ask:

Problem 4.6
.

Is there another
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
–module (or
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
–module) analogous to the diagonal harmonics whose bi-graded Frobenius character specializes to
SH_{n}
when

It would also be interesting to find combinatorial interpretations for the expansion of

into bases in
\mathrm{SymP}
.
Depending on the choice of basis, this could be quite challenging since finding explicit expansions of

is an open problem for most bases of
\mathrm{Sym}
.

Though finding the
e

\nabla e_{n}
is an open problem,
by setting

PF_{n}(1,t)=\sum_{p\in\overline{\mathrm{Pf}}(n)}t^{\mathrm{area}(p)}e_{\lambda(p)}.

Therefore, applying shiftification gives

SH_{n}(1,t)=\sum_{(p,\overline{\sigma})\in\overline{\mathrm{NShPf}}(n)}t^{\mathrm{area}(p)}V_{\lambda(p)}.

The area of a parking function can be computed as

\mathrm{area}(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_{i}-i)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n{+}1{-}i)\cdot\alpha_{i}(p)\right)-\binom{n}{2}.

In the bijection from

Proposition 4.7
.

SH_{n}(1,t)=\sum_{(p,\overline{\sigma},\tau)}t^{\mathrm{area}_{o}(p,\overline{\sigma},\tau)}V_{\lambda(p)}.

Proof.

The result follows from (

We suspect the relatively simple combinatorial description of

Our hope is that odd shifted parking functions will lead to further developments in the story of diagonal harmonics and related algebras.

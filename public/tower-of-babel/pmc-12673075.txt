A smart manufacturing paradigm for robotic welding process optimization through machine learning
Authors: Javubar Sathick, A. Abdul Azeez Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The welding process has significant importance across several sectors, as it guarantees integrated components’ structural soundness and dependability. Robotic welding, distinguished by the use of automated procedures facilitated by robotic arms, has garnered considerable recognition due to its exceptional accuracy and productivity. The conventional techniques need help attaining the most favourable welding results due to workpiece location and trajectory accuracy. The need arises for the advancement of sophisticated optimization methodologies. This paper presents a smart manufacturing paradigm to tackle the problem using Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Welding Process Optimization (RL-RWPO). The RL-RWPO framework combines Vision-Based and CAD-Based methodologies, facilitating a holistic comprehension of the workpiece and its intended arrangement. Using 3-D calibration and workpiece placement techniques significantly improves the precision of welding processes. Reinforcement Learning is used in trajectory optimization to ensure accurate and efficient welding operations. The results of the simulation indicate significant enhancements, as seen by the average Weld Seam Quality of 93.27%, Welding Speed of 50.51 mm/s, Weld Penetration Depth of 5.25 mm, Trajectory Accuracy of 3.04 mm, and Welding Efficiency of 91.52%. The RL-RWPO method can improve the effectiveness and excellence of robotic welding practices in several industrial sectors.

Introduction

Welding robots are essential in industrial systems since they provide an automated, adaptive, and highly efficient approach to welding operations1. The process planning involved in welding robots remains an uphill task, necessitating the expertise of skilled experts with a wealth of knowledge in the welding field2. Planning the welding procedure for a single-piece produced product is time-consuming and susceptible to errors. Artificial methods need to be improved in meeting the demands of multi-variety and small-batch manufacturing since it heavily relies on the expertise and decision-making of process planning engineers3. Enhancing the intelligence and efficiency of process scheduling for welding robots is imperative. This enhancement will empower the welding robots to autonomously make decisions during the scheduling of welding procedures, resulting in reduced preparation time and improved welding performance4.

During welding, the workpiece undergoes deformation due to heat, leading to a deviation from the intended path. This deviation adversely affects the accuracy of the welding operation5. Machine visual technology has facilitated the integration of welding robots with laser visual detection methods, enabling the precise adjustment of playback paths for accurate seam tracking. Cameras serve as the primary sensors in machine vision systems, playing a crucial role in various applications related to vision assessment6. The laser vision sensor is positioned anteriorly to the welding flame to capture and analyze real-time data about the weld spots. This data is then used to direct the welding robot in executing the appropriate movement path. Therefore, welding accuracy and effectiveness are enhanced by using real-time weld positioning recognition7. The topic of reinforcement learning (RL) comes from the fields of machine learning and optimal control to study how an intelligent agent should operate in an evolving environment to maximize its benefit. Developing an intelligent process planning framework offers a viable approach to address the issues8. The welding job is the fundamental input for process scheduling in a smart process management technology. It is essential to have a welding job model that encompasses a thorough and precise definition. The model should provide enough data for the intelligent process scheduling platform, as innovative process management is a fundamental need9. The techniques used for depicting welding jobs include two-dimensional drawings and digital models. Using computing power and communication via networks has led to the preference for a digital data framework in intelligent process scheduling for welding operations. This option has garnered significant attention in research endeavors. The existing digital representations of welding activities include many models, such as the object-oriented approach, the model represented by formalized signals, and the ontological version10. The digital data modeling of welding operations is derived from welding characteristics that are analogous to the idea of machining characteristics. Literature survey and analysis

This section thoroughly examines previous scholarly investigations about robotic welding, emphasizing notable progressions in automation and welding technologies. This section explores the current body of research on process optimization, measurement, and planning paths within robotic welding. Chen et al. proposed a novel approach known as the Modified YOLOv5-based technique (MYOLO) to address the tasks of automated weld type categorization, tacking spot detection, and weld Region Of Interest (ROI) identification11. The approach involves using a tailored convolutional neural network to improve detection precision. The experimental findings show an average accuracy of 94.5%, recall of 92.8%, F1-score of 93.6%, and mean Intersection Over Union (IOU) of 89.2%. This methodology dramatically enhances the effectiveness and precision of weld type categorization and identification, enabling the implementation of accurate robotic welding techniques. Geng et al. proposed a novel approach called Point Cloud-based Welding Path Planning (PC-WPP) for impeller blade welding12. The PC-WPP software utilizes an innovative method for generating paths, aiming to improve toolpath design of toolpaths. The experimental verification demonstrates a decrease in route length of 23.6%, resulting in enhanced accuracy and effectiveness in the welding process. The efficacy of the suggested methodology in improving welding pathways, especially in intricate geometries such as impeller blades, is shown. Kim et al. proposed a novel technique, Point Cloud Registration (PCR), designed to recover numerous weld seams from RGB-depth pictures effectively13. The PCR method employs a registration algorithm based on features to align and recreate the point clouds. The accuracy of the suggested approach is shown by the experimental findings, which indicate a registration error of 0.57 mm. The proposed methodology presents a dependable resolution for automated robotic welding, ensuring precise seam extraction. Xiao et al. proposed a novel Binocular Cooperation-based Visual Guiding Framework (BC-VGF) for robotic welding14. The BC-VGF system employs stereo vision technology to improve the sense of depth, hence facilitating accurate tracking of seams. The experimental assessment demonstrates that the suggested framework exhibits a high level of precision, as shown by an average tracking error of 0.32 mm. The approach offers a viable resolution for attaining precise welding outcomes across diverse applications. Loukas et al. proposed a novel Cost-Function-driven Adaptive Welding Framework (CF-AWF) designed explicitly for multi-pass robotic welding15. The CF-AWF system utilizes a cost-based optimization methodology to modify welding settings adaptively. The experimental findings indicate a reduction of 15.4% in welding time and a drop of 12.8% in energy usage. The suggested framework presents a viable and effective approach for multi-pass welding procedures to reduce resource consumption while ensuring the production of welds of superior quality. Yang et al. proposed a novel Fast Calibration of Laser Vision Robotic Systems (FC-LVRS) approach, which leverages autonomous route planning techniques16. The FC-LVRS approach enhances calibration efficiency using automated route planning, substantially reducing calibration duration. The experimental validation of the suggested process showcases a significant % decrease in calibration time by 37%. This outcome presents a practical option for calibrating laser vision-based robotic welding systems. Kumar et al. use a Discrete Wavelet Algorithm (DWA) to examine mutually interfering welding signals in Twin-Wire Robotic Welding (TWRW)17. The DWA-TWRW approach separates and analyzes messages, successfully segregating and classifying unwanted weld signals. The experimental findings demonstrate high efficacy in separating signals, achieving an accuracy rate of 93.7%. This result has significant implications for enhancing signal analysis in twin-wire robotic welding. Zhang et al. proposed a methodology for optimizing process variables in robotic welding using learning-based methods18. The Intelligent Design for Robotic Welding Process Protocol (ID-RWPP) strategy utilizes machine-learning techniques to improve welding settings. The experimental findings demonstrate a decrease of 12.3% in flaws and an improvement of 9.8% in welding effectiveness, therefore highlighting the promising prospects of using intelligent variable optimization in robotic welding. Xia et al. present a Data-driven technique for Robotic Welding of Shape-varying geometry employing weld Profile (D-RWSP) control19. The approach enables exemplary weld profile management through data-driven quick input distribution. The study’s results indicate a decrease of 16.2% in weld faults and an enhancement of 14.5% in the general welding level, highlighting the efficacy of data-driven management in welding. Cibicik et al. provide a novel approach for scanning weld tracks on big tubular T-joints using a line laser detector20. Robotic Weld Groove Scanning for T-Joints using Line Laser (RWGS-TJLL) offers a promising solution in robotic welding. This methodology facilitates the effective and precise scanning of expansive tubular T-joints. The experiment results demonstrate that the suggested approach for inspecting large tube joints in robotic welding has a scanning precision of 0.34 mm. The section provides an overview of many novel approaches to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of robotic welding procedures. Some prevalent obstacles need to be addressed in the field. These include the requirement for enhanced calibration effectiveness in laser vision devices, the occurrence of signal interference in the twin-wire welding process, and the necessity to optimize welding settings. The discipline of robotic welding continues to face significant hurdles in shape-varying geometries and assessments of large tube joints.

Proposed reinforcement learning for robotic welding process optimization

The RL-RWPO method under consideration is a smart manufacturing paradigm for optimizing the Robotic Welding Procedure using Reinforcement Learning techniques. The use of reinforcement learning is employed to automatically enhance the optimization of the trajectory for robotic welding via the training of a subnetwork. The methodology primarily emphasizes improving weld quality and reducing reliance on comprehensive 3-D labels in supervised training. This renders it a promising strategy for achieving efficient and accurate robotic welding. The proposed methodology presents an innovative approach to enhance the effectiveness and quality of welding processes in practical contexts using Machine learning methods.

Figure 1 depicts the experimental apparatus used for automated welding with smart manufacturing paradigm. The platform primarily comprises a motion-executing mechanism with six levels of freedom, welding tools, a laser visual detector, and a factory control system. The laser optical detector is affixed to the welding flame and operates with the torch’s movement to actively identify weld spots in a live setting. The sensor comprises both a camera and a laser. The laser is positioned at an oblique angle relative to the camera, while the camera itself is fixed in a vertical orientation. The computer acquires the weld seam picture taken by the vision sensors and performs calculations to determine the precise location of the featured point. Offline programming system

This work aims to blend CAD and vision-based offline development methodologies to address the constraints of existing automated program-generating methods with smart manufacturing paradigm. Figure 3 depicts the tasks of the suggested offline programming technique using a Unified Modeling Language (UML) action graph. The offline programming technique is broadly categorized into vision, CAD, sight, and CAD interactive tasks.Fig. 3Offline programming model.

Offline programming model.

The suggested approach for offline programming commences by capturing images of the workpieces with their corresponding CAD representations. Geometrical characteristics are derived from the workpieces’ visual terms and CAD representations. Determining workpiece places during vision-based operations relies on extracting geometrical attributes from pictures of the pieces. In CAD-based actions, the allocation of welding assignments to various mobile elements of the welding torch (such as robots, gantries, and computerized guided vehicles) and the planning of their welding journeys depend on the CAD algorithms of the components. The offline programming technique proposes the inclusion of interactive tasks using vision and CAD to facilitate exchanges between the vision and CAD-based offline programming methodologies. This reduces the establishment of a correlation between a specific workpiece and its corresponding CAD approach, hence enabling the identification of deviations resulting from assembly faults and the subsequent determination of route compensation information. The welding design is developed by merging the locations of the components acquired from vision-based duties, the welding routes derived from CAD-based actions, and trajectory compensating data collected from the interacting events using both vision and CAD.

Conclusion and future scope

Welding is the backbone of numerous industries, maintaining diverse components’ skeletal strength and durability. With its precision and effectiveness, robotic welding has transformed the process. Nevertheless, obstacles exist in improving the welding process to generate enhanced outputs. The study proposed Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Welding Process Optimization (RL-RWPO) in reaction to these issues. RL-RWPO merges Vision-Based and CAD-Based approaches, offering a thorough knowledge of components and their intended configurations. The inclusion of 3-D Calibration and Workpiece Positioning increases welding accuracy. Reinforcement Learning was utilized for trajectory optimization, assuring the efficiency of welding processes. Simulation outputs have emphasized the usefulness of RL-RWPO, with excellent results: an average Weld Seam Quality of 93.27%, Welding Speed of 50.51 mm/s, Weld Penetration Depth of 5.25 mm, Trajectory Accuracy of 3.04 mm, and Welding Efficiency of 91.52%. These results underscore the potential of RL-RWPO to better welding operations across sectors. Challenges persist, particularly real-world deployment, fine-tuning, and adaptation to varied workpieces and surroundings. Future research should concentrate on resolving these problems, including improved sensing technology and combining real-time information for adaptive control. RL-RWPO offers a significant step toward attaining optimum welding results and provides the prospect of additional improvements in robotic welding.

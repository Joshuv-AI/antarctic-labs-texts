Managing chronic kidney disease – mineral bone disorder among patients on maintenance haemodialysis by achieving serum calcium and phosphate targets – a quality improvement project
Authors: Aswin Surjit, Fathima Mihta Moidu, Chacko Jegan, Georgy George Kuruvilla, V Narayanan Unni, Geetha Mary Philips
Published: 2024

Introduction

Mineral bone disorders in chronic kidney disease increase the risk of coronary artery disease and mortality. The aim of the project is to improve the management of chronic kidney disease – mineral bone disorder (CKD-MBD) among patients on maintenance haemodialysis by achieving the target levels of serum biochemical markers (calcium and phosphate).

Materials and Methods

The quality improvement project (QIP) was conducted between February 2021 and February 2022 at a quaternary healthcare centre. We included outpatients undergoing maintenance haemodialysis at our centre. We investigated the reasons for not attaining the recommended target levels of serum biochemical markers. We had discussions with consultants, trainee doctors, dialysis staff and patients. We also reviewed the patient details, laboratory investigations as well as medication prescriptions from electronic medical records. It was found that some patients were not on adequate medications for achieving the targets and some patients were not aware of the importance of maintaining the specified targets.

A CKD-MBD management algorithm was designed according to KDIGO (Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes) guidelines.1 In plan, do, study, act (PDSA) cycle 1, we conducted training sessions in managing CKD-MBD for internal medicine trainees (IMTs). Further, the training sessions were extended to dialysis staff in PDSA cycle 2. We analysed the effectiveness of these sessions by a pre- and post-training questionnaire. In PDSA cycle 3, we continued conducting training sessions for IMTs and dialysis staff, to maintain the sustainability of the project.

Conclusion

The project showed an increase of 16.67% patients achieving the recommended target levels for both serum calcium and phosphate. Other serum biochemical or radiological markers for CKD-MBD were not part of our QIP, as they required longer duration of follow-up and this is a limitation of our project. The learning points from the project were that multidisciplinary collaboration is essential in improving the quality of patient care. Improving the scientific knowledge of the patients by health education will help in motivating them to adhere to medical advice. The sustainability of the project was achieved by regular training sessions and audits.

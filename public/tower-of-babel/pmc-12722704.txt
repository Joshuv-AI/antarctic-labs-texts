Multi-pathway regulatory role of miR-4693-5p in the modulation of rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Mohd Saquib, Prachi Agnihotri, Vijay Kumar, Sagarika Biswas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune condition that typically causes inflammation in joints symmetrically. Dysregulated microRNAs are implicated in RA development. Our earlier findings showed that miR-4693-5p levels were decreased in PBMCs isolated from RA patients, impacting apoptosis, inflammatory cytokines production, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) response. This study investigated the regulatory influence of TNF-α on the expression of miR-4693-5p and examined the downstream effects of miR-4693-5p on key proteins and pathways associated with RA. The objective is to elucidate the therapeutic potential of miR-4693-5p in disease. The impact of TNF-α on miR-4693-5p expression in SW982 cells was assessed using qRT-PCR. Differential protein analysis was performed using SWATH-MS in RA-FLS regulated by miR-4693-5p. Identified proteins were cross-referenced with miRNA target prediction databases, followed by pathway enrichment analysis using Cytoscape. Significant proteins were validated by western blotting, and mitochondrial ROS was detected using Mitosox. Cellular proliferation and apoptosis were evaluated using the MTT assay and Hoechst staining. TNF-α regulates miR-4693-5p expression in RA-mimic SW982 cells. SWATH-MS identified 396 proteins, with 49 significantly regulated proteins. Among these, FN-1, YWHAZ, SOD2, and CALD1 were found to be directly regulated by miR-4693-5p. In vitro analysis showed that miR-4693-5p regulates ECM proteins, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), VEGF signaling, mitochondrial ROS, cell proliferation, and apoptosis. Identified protein expression was further validated in the CIA rat model. miR-4693-5p regulates pathways involving cell adhesion, cytoskeleton organization, angiogenesis, and cell death, highlighting its promise as a novel therapeutic target for improving RA treatment and disease management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-27898-7.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disorder that causes joint inflammation and progressive damage1. Approximately 1% of the global population and 0.92% of people in India are affected by RA2. Treatment often involves the use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) that have severe side effects like increased risk of infections, gastrointestinal problems, and cardiovascular issues3. The most common markers used for diagnosis are anti-citrullinated protein antibodies or ACPA and rheumatoid factor or RF, with less specificity and sensitivity4.

Currently, microRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding regulatory molecules, are reported to act as a biomarker in many diseases such as cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and autoimmune disorders5. Their easy identification in various tissues over the past decade has positioned them as potential markers for disease prognosis and therapeutic response. These miRNAs play a vital role in preserving the cellular environment by influencing gene expression. Recently, it has been reported that miRNAs play a central role in RA as pivotal epigenetic factors as post-transcriptional modifiers6. Dysregulated miRNAs are reported to have a link with inflammatory diseases such as RA, playing a crucial role in the development of the disease7,8. Our interest is to explore the functional and downstream targets of these miRNAs to investigate their regulation to improve our understanding of RA and develop targeted therapies. We previously noted that miR-4693-5p levels were decreased in PBMCs isolated from RA patients, influenced apoptosis, production of inflammatory cytokines, and the cellular response to ROS in RA-FLS and CIA rat model indicating miR-4693-5p is a critical regulator in RA development9. However, the upstream factors affecting miR-4693-5p expression and its specific functional mechanisms remain poorly understood.

TNF-α plays a central role in RA by amplifying inflammation, activating immune cells, and promoting joint damage and cartilage erosion. It is known that TNF-α alters miRNA expression profiles, such as particularly upregulating miR-155 and miR-146a in fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) and other immune cells showing critical regulators in RA7.

To confirm the link between miR-4693-5p and RA, we first identified that TNF-α regulates the expression of miR-4693-5p, highlighting its relevance to RA pathogenesis. Proteins, as the primary effector of cellular functions and regulated by various entities among which miRNAs hold considerable significance10. The report shows that proteomic profiling can help to identify differentially expressed proteins, paving the way for a better understanding of various disease conditions11. So, we compared the miR-4693-5p regulated protein profile with negative control (NC) in RAFLS by SWATH-MS, thus revealing the functional role of miR-4693-5p in RA. Further, we analyzed the altered protein-associated pathways and validated our findings. To refine our study, we compared the differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) by miR-4693-5p with the miRNA target prediction database to get common targets. Our result shows that four common proteins, Fibronectin-1 (FN-1), Tyrosine 3-monooxygenase/tryptophan 5-monooxygenase activation protein zeta (YWHAZ), Superoxide Dismutase 2 (SOD2), and Caldesmon 1 (CALD1) are associated with vital cellular functions such as cellular adhesion, proliferation, cell death, and antioxidant pathways, respectively which are crucial pathways for RA development and progression. Further analyses demonstrated that miR-4693-5p influences pathways involved in fibroblast proliferation, induction of apoptosis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), remodeling of extracellular matrix (ECM), angiogenesis, and ROS modulation.

Collectively, this study highlights the multifaceted regulatory role of miR-4693-5p in RA pathogenesis. By targeting key proteins involved in disease-promoting pathways, miR-4693-5p emerges as a promising candidate for developing targeted RA therapies aimed at improving treatment outcomes and slowing disease progression.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Biopsy samples were collected from n = 12 RA-diagnosed individuals at the Orthopedic Department of AIIMS (All India Institute of Medical Sciences), New Delhi, India, who fulfilled the 2010 diagnostic criteria established by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and the European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR)3. The medical histories of all patients were recorded (Supplementary Table 1). The study protocol complied with the Declaration of Helsinki, approved by the ethical committee of Council of Scientific & Industrial Research-Institute of Genomics & Integrative Biology (CSIR-IGIB), New Delhi, India, and AIIMS, New Delhi, India, Registration No. IEC-237/07.05.2021, RP-18/2021, confirming that all experiments were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. Additionally, all participants gave their informed consent before taking part in the study.

Cell culture (SW982) and TNF-α induction

SW982 cells, obtained from the National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS) in India, are commonly utilized in RA synovitis studies. These cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified environment with 5% CO₂, using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS) and antibiotics. To investigate the effect of miR-4693-5p expression under RA mimic conditions, we created an inflammatory state similar to RA by stimulating the SW982 cells with TNF-α (10 ng/ml)12.

SW982, a human synovial sarcoma-derived cell line, is widely used as an in vitro model for RA studies due to its fibroblast-like morphology and similarity to RA-FLS. Like RA-FLS, SW982 cells express pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, MMPs) upon cytokine stimulation13. While primary RA-FLS are influenced by complex in vivo factors like immune interactions and prior drug exposure, SW982 cells offer a more controlled environment for investigating cytokine-mediated molecular responses14. Therefore, SW982 cells were used in this study to specifically examine TNF-α-mediated regulation of miR-4693-5p expression under controlled conditions.

Real-time PCR (real-time polymerase chain reaction)

SW982 cells were grown in a six-well plate. After 70–80% confluency, cells were induced with TNFα (10 ng/ml) for 24 h to mimic the RA condition in SW982 cells. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, RNA was extracted using Tri-Xtract (G Biosciences). Afterward, isolated RNA (1 µg) was employed for cDNA synthesis with the cDNA Synthesis Kit (G Biosciences)15. A stem-loop primer was utilized to evaluate the expression of miR-4693-5p in TNF-α induced SW982 cells using a specific primer (Supplementary Table 2) for miR-4693-5p and U6 as an internal control mixed with 5x Hot Fire pol EvaGreen qPCR master mix (Solis BioDyne). miR-4693-5p expression was quantified using the Roche LightCycler 480 II real-time PCR system. The data was evaluated quantitatively using the 2-ΔΔCT formula16.

Primary cell (RAFLS) isolation

To evaluate the physiological function of miR-4693-5p in RA, synovial tissue was carefully minced and digested with 0.5 mg/ml collagenase (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA). The digested tissue was cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator. Cells were treated after reaching the second passage17.

Cell transfection

To evaluate the function of miR-4693-5p, cells were cultured in a 6-well plate until they reached 60–70% confluency. Transfection was performed with 25 nM of either a miRNA Negative Control (NC) or a miR-4693-5p mimic (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA), along with Lipofectamine RNAiMAX Transfection Reagent (Invitrogen) in Opti-MEM media (Gibco, Waltham, MA, USA). After 5 h, the media was replaced with DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS9.

Preparation of RAFLS for proteomics: SWATH-MS acquisition

RAFLS transfected with miR-4693-5p and NC cells were lysed, and the extracted proteins were pooled and quantified using the Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) assay. Protein (100 µg) was digested overnight with trypsin (0.1 µg/µl, Promega, USA) at 37 °C, and peptides were extracted using 50% acetonitrile (ACN) and 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, Sigma Aldrich, USA). The mixture was dried in a speed-vac, and peptides were separated using an SCX cartridge (5 microns, 300 Å beads, AB Sciex, USA). Elution was performed with 70% v/v acetonitrile and 0.1% formic acid. In SWATH-MS quantitative acquisition, proteins with at least two unique peptides and an unused score of 2.0 or higher were considered, as outlined in previous studies18,19.

Multivariate analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to identify group differences. PCA can discrete predictive variation from uncorrelated differences to facilitate understanding of different sources of variation. Pareto scaling was used for normalization by a metaboanalyst20.

Construction of PPI (protein-protein interaction) network and enrichment analysis of selected targets

To explore the biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions of the RA-related proteins, Cytoscape 3.8.2 was utilized21,22. The analysis incorporated Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)23, and wiki pathways to study the interactions between connected proteins, aiding in the development of hypotheses and understanding their associated functional pathways24.

Common targets for miR-4693-5p

A detailed analysis was conducted to explore the potential targets of miR-4693-5p and their relevance to the development of RA. The Venny tool was used to compare the predicted targets of miR-4693-5p from miRNA target databases (miRDB, TargetScan, and RNA22) with the SWATH DEPs21. This comparison allowed us to identify potential targets of miR-4693-5p and to understand how they are related to specific proteins involved in the pathogenesis of RA9.

Protein extraction and western blot analysis

RAFLS cells were cultured to 70–80% confluency and then transfected with miR-4693-5p for 48 h. After incubation, cells were lysed on ice using RIPA buffer. Protein concentration was quantified using the BCA method, and 40 µg of protein was loaded onto an SDS-PAGE gel for electrophoretic separation. Proteins were then transferred onto a NC membrane (G Biosciences) via a semi-dry transfer system (Bio-Rad Laboratories). The membrane was blocked with 3% BSA for 2 h and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (Santa Cruz), including Anti-human FN-1 (1:500), E-Cadherin (E-CAD, 1:3000), N-Cadherin (N-CAD, 1:2000), Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF, 1:2000), CALD1 (1:2000), YWHAZ (1:1000), SOD2 (1:1000), Catalase (1:1000), Glutathione Peroxidase-1 (GPx1, 1:1000), and Ki67 (1:1000). After three washes with 1X TBST, the membrane was incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, Jackson ImmunoResearch) for one hour at room temperature. The membrane was washed and treated with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent, and protein bands were visualized using the ChemiDoc imaging system and analyzed by Image lab software (Bio-Rad Laboratories)25,26.

MTT assay for cell proliferation assessment

To assess the effect of miR-4693-5p on cell proliferation, an MTT assay was conducted. RA-FLS cells (5,000 per well) were plated in 96-well plates and cultured until reaching 70–80% confluency. The cells were then transfected with 25 nM of miR-4693-5p and incubated for 48 h. For the assay, 10 µl of MTT solution (4 mg/ml) was added to each well, followed by incubation for 3 h to allow the formation of formazan crystals. To dissolve the crystals, 100 µl of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added. The absorbance was then measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader to quantify cell proliferation. A 20% DMSO solution was used as a toxic control to validate the assay’s sensitivity27.

Hoechst 33342 staining

Apoptosis and typical morphological features were examined by Hoechst 33342. After transfection, the cells were gently washed with cold 1X PBS twice to remove residual media. Cells were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min at room temperature (RT). After fixation, the cells were rinsed with 1X PBS and stained with 5 µg/ml Hoechst 3334228. The staining was carried out in the dark at 37 °C for 20 min to protect the dye from light degradation. Finally, the cells underwent two additional PBS washes, and apoptotic cells were identified based on nuclear morphology. The relative fluorescence intensity of apoptotic cells was analyzed using a fluorescence Zoe microscope (Bio-Rad Laboratories). Apoptotic cells absorb Hoechst dye more quickly than non-apoptotic cells. Normal cells show organized blue chromatin, while apoptotic cells have bright blue, highly condensed, or fragmented chromatin29,30.

MitoSOX Red‑mitochondrial superoxide production (analysis of mitochondrial superoxide production) in RA

To assess mitochondrial superoxide production and evaluate the effects of miR-4693-5p in RAFLS, we used MitoSOXRed (Thermo Fisher, Monza, IT). The cells were incubated with MitoSOXRed for 30 min and then washed with PBS to remove excess dye. The production of superoxide was determined by measuring the fluorescence intensity of MitoSOXRed. Fluorescence was measured with 510 nm as excitation and 580 nm emission wavelength. The difference in superoxide production between the NC and miR-4693-5p mimic samples was calculated by comparing their fluorescence intensity. The data were then analyzed using ImageJ software (version 1.41; NIH, USA) and normalized with cell counts18.

In vivo experiments

Establishment of the collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) rat model

We developed a CIA rat model to assess the therapeutic potential of miR-4693-5p9. We utilized female Wistar rats, each weighing between 60 and 80 g. The rats were procured from the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition in Hyderabad, India. All experimental protocols were approved by the Council of Scientific & Industrial Research-Institute of Genomics & Integrative Biology (CSIR-IGIB), New Delhi, India, according to the IGIB-Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) following Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) guidelines and regulations. The institutional certificate reference number is IGIB/IAEC/3/3/March 2023. Animal experiments followed the recommendations in the ARRIVE guidelines. The study included evaluations of arthritic parameters such as macroscopic arthritis scores, measurements of pro-inflammatory cytokines in rat plasma using ELISA, and histological analyses were performed. The experimental rats were divided into five groups (n = 4 rats per group): Group 1 - Healthy Control (HC), Group 2 - CIA, Group 3 - Negative Control (NC) with CIA, Group 4 - CIA treated with miR-4693-5p mimic, and Group 5 - CIA treated with Methotrexate (MTX), the standard drug31. The rats were euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of 120 mg/kg sodium thiopental at the end of the study9,32.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

For immunohistochemical analysis, rat synovial tissue sections from our previously published study9 were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) solution for 25 min at room temperature and subsequently blocked with 3% BSA for 2 h. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with antibodies against FN-1, YWHAZ, SOD2, and CALD1 (all 1:500 dilution, Cloud-Clone Corporation). After washing, the slices were treated for 1 h with a secondary HRP-conjugated antibody, followed by a 10-minute incubation with DAB substrate. Images were captured at 10X magnification and analyzed using ImageJ software (Version 1.54d)9.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 9.0. Depending on the nature of the data, suitable tests such as the Student’s t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, one-way ANOVA, or Chi-square test were employed. A p-value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Each experiment was performed in triplicate to ensure reproducibility. Statistical significance was indicated as follows: * for p ≤ 0.05, ** for p ≤ 0.01, *** for p ≤ 0.001, and **** for p ≤ 0.0001.

Results

TNF-α regulates the expression of miR-4693-5p in RA

TNF-α is a major proinflammatory cytokine in RA31. In conditions like obesity, treatment with TNF-α in adipose tissue significantly reduces miRNA expression, like miR-42433. Consistently, our results demonstrated that TNF-α (10 ng/ml) decreased miR-4693-5p expression (Fig. 1A), suggesting a regulatory role of TNF-α in miR-4693-5p expression during RA pathogenesis. Based on this, we further investigated the proteins and downstream pathways modulated by miR-4693-5p.

Identification of miR-4693-5p regulated differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) in RA-FLS

The regulatory effect of miR-4693-5p on proteins and associated pathways was evaluated using SWATH-MS analysis in RA-FLS cells. Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed distinct clustering of protein intensities between the NC and miR-4693-5p-transfected groups (Fig. 1B). A total of 396 proteins were identified following miR-4693-5p transfection, as illustrated by the volcano plot with significant proteins (p ≤ 0.05) highlighted in red (Fig. 1C). Among the 396 proteins, 49 were statistically significant, of which 39 were differentially regulated proteins (DEPs) (18 upregulated and 21 downregulated; fold change ≤ 0.8 for downregulation and ≥ 1.2 for upregulation)12 (Fig. 1D). These differences are visualized in the heatmap, with red and blue squares representing relative upregulation and downregulation, respectively (Fig. 1E). Pathway enrichment analysis of the significant DEPs and association with RA pathogenesis

Enrichment analysis of the 39 significant DEPs was performed using KEGG and WikiPathway databases. The analysis identified pathways related to cytoskeleton organization, angiogenesis, cell adhesion, and metabolism (Fig. 2A–B), all of which have established associations with RA pathophysiology34,35. GO enrichment analysis was further conducted to determine the cellular localization of the proteins, revealing enrichment in the cytoplasm, vesicles, intracellular organelle lumen, extracellular space, secretory granules, and cytoplasmic vesicles. GO analysis of biological, cellular, and molecular processes indicated involvement of miR-4693-5p–regulated proteins in cell adhesion molecule binding, regulation of cell death, and metabolic processes (Fig. 2C). These results indicate that miR-4693-5p modulates multiple RA-associated pathways and cellular processes.

Identification of common targets for miR-4693-5p: intersecting miRNA predictions and DEPs

Direct targets of miR-4693-5p and their potential involvement in RA pathogenesis were identified through an integrative approach. Predicted targets obtained from miRNA prediction databases (miRDB, TargetScan, and RNA22)9 were compared with DEPs from SWATH-MS analysis using the Venny tool.

This comparison identified four common proteins FN-1, YWHAZ, SOD2, and CALD1 (Fig. 2D) associated with processes including cell adhesion, proliferation, EMT, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, and cell death, all of which are relevant to RA pathogenesis. miR-4693-5p decreased EMT transition by targeted ECM-related proteins in RA

EMT contributes to increased mobility, invasiveness, and resistance to apoptosis in RAFLS35–37. A previous study reported that miR-4693-5p regulates ECM proteins to suppress EMT in colorectal cancer cells38. In the present SWATH-MS data, FN-1, an ECM protein involved in EMT regulation, was found to be downregulated.

Transfection of miR-4693-5p in RAFLS reduced FN-1 expression by ~ 2-fold (Fig. 3A) and N-cadherin by ~ 1.3-fold (Fig. 3B), while E-cadherin expression increased by ~ 1.2-fold (Fig. 3C). In addition, CALD1, another EMT-related protein identified as downregulated in SWATH-MS, was validated in vitro and showed ~ 1.2-fold downregulation following miR-4693-5p transfection (Fig. 3D). These findings indicate that miR-4693-5p modulates EMT markers in RAFLS, which may influence their proliferative and invasive properties.

miR-4693-5p suppresses angiogenesis in RAFLS by suppressing VEGF

VEGF is a key mediator of angiogenesis and promotes synovial membrane invasion into cartilage in RA39,40. Previous findings indicated that miR-4693-5p downregulates HIF1α, a major regulator of VEGF9. In the present study, pathway enrichment analysis implicated miR-4693-5p in the angiogenesis pathway, and transfection of RAFLS cells with miR-4693-5p resulted in significant downregulation of VEGF (~ 1.3-fold) (Fig. 3E). miR-4693-5p decreases cell proliferation and promotes cell apoptosis

Synovial fibroblasts are the predominant cells in hyperplastic rheumatoid synovium, characterized by apoptosis resistance and contribution to chronic inflammation42. Among the identified proteins, YWHAZ is associated with cell survival and apoptosis suppression and has been linked to various cancers and neurodegenerative diseases43. In RAFLS, miR-4693-5p transfection significantly reduced YWHAZ expression (~ 1.6-fold) (Fig. 4A). Hoechst staining further revealed nuclear fragmentation in transfected cells, and quantification of fluorescence intensity indicated higher apoptotic cell levels compared to the NC group (Fig. 4B). These findings align with pathway enrichment results, suggesting involvement of miR-4693-5p in apoptosis regulation.

The effect of miR-4693-5p on cell proliferation was assessed using the MTT assay and Ki-67 expression analysis. miR-4693-5p transfection resulted in reduced proliferation (Fig. 4C) and downregulation of Ki-67 expression (~ 3-fold) (Fig. 4D) relative to NC. miR-4693-5p reduces mitochondrial ROS levels in RAFLS

Elevated ROS levels promote FLS proliferation and resistance to apoptosis20,45. Previous work demonstrated that miR-4693-5p reduces total cellular ROS through HIF1α knockdown9. In this study, SOD2, identified as a common target and negative regulator of HIF1α, was examined due to its reported decrease in RA, which contributes to mitochondrial stress46. miR-4693-5p transfection upregulated SOD2 (~ 1.15-fold) (Fig. 5A), which was associated with reduced mitochondrial ROS (Fig. 5B). Additionally, antioxidant enzyme levels were assessed, showing increased catalase (~ 1.8-fold) (Fig. 5C) and GPx1 (~ 1.4-fold) (Fig. 5D) expression. These findings indicate that miR-4693-5p enhances antioxidant defense mechanisms, thereby contributing to the reduction of mitochondrial oxidative stress in RA-FLS.

Immunohistochemical analysis of common selected target proteins expression in CIA rat synovial tissues

The expression of FN-1, YWHAZ, CALD1, and SOD2 was examined in synovial tissues of rats across experimental groups. FN-1, YWHAZ, and CALD1 exhibited reduced brown staining in CIA treated with miR-4693-5p mimic (Group 4) compared to CIA group and NC with CIA (Groups 2 and 3), indicating decreased expression (Fig. 6A–C). In contrast, SOD2 expression was significantly elevated in CIA treated with miR-4693-5p mimic, and CIA treated with MTX (Groups 4 and 5) compared to CIA group and NC with CIA (Groups 2 and 3), as shown by increased staining intensity (Fig. 6D). These findings demonstrated that miR-4693-5p modulates the expression of proteins associated with RA pathogenesis and oxidative stress response.

Discussion

In RA, the synovium undergoes significant pathological changes, such as synovial lining hyperplasia and infiltration of inflammatory cells, leading to pannus formation42. This process accelerates bone erosion and cartilage destruction. RAFLS are central to tissue hyperplasia and exhibit characteristics similar to tumor cells20, including resistance to apoptosis and the upregulation of proto-oncogenes. Additionally, activated RAFLS produce pro-inflammatory cytokines and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), contributing to RA progression35.

Altered miRNAs have been detected in the synovial tissue, joint cells, and blood of RA patients. Altered miRNA expression is closely associated with bone and joint destruction and plays a crucial role in disease pathogenesis5. Distinct miRNAs are expressed at various inflamed sites, and many can be found simultaneously across multiple locations. These alterations in miRNAs not only provide valuable insights into RA pathogenesis but also serve as potential biomarkers for diagnosis, predictors of treatment response, and promising targets for the development of personalised therapeutic strategies4.

In our earlier study, we observed a significant reduction in miR-4693-5p expression in the PBMCs of RA patients9. This finding suggested that miR-4693-5p may contribute to RA pathogenesis as an endogenous regulatory molecule. We further validated its role in directly targeting HIF1α expression, thereby linking it to inflammatory processes in RA. Together, these results provide an important foundation for understanding the potential of miR-4693-5p as a therapeutic candidate, offering new insights into the molecular mechanisms driving RA pathogenesis9.

Among the central mediators of RA pathogenesis, TNF-α, a key cytokine involved in RA, influences cell growth, cell death, cytokine balance, and inflammation24,47,48. It is known that TNF-α can affect miRNA expression in various cell types, including neurons, muscle cells, and endothelial cells49. Previous studies have shown that TNF-α treatment significantly decreases miR-424 expression by binding to its promoter and reducing its transcriptional activity in obesity33.

Given that TNF-α is a major cytokine driving RA pathogenesis and considering the critical role of miR-4693-5p, we hypothesized that TNF-α might also regulate the expression of miR-4693-5p. Thus, we explored our studies and demonstrated a negative correlation between TNF-α levels and miR-4693-5p expression in RA mimic conditions (Fig. 1A), thereby confirming their association in RA and reinforcing the notion that TNF-α influences the expression of miR-4693-5p.

We expanded our study to investigate how miR-4693-5p regulates different targeted protein expressions and related pathways in RA. Thus, we applied a proteomic approach and conducted differential proteomics analysis using SWATH-MS in RA-FLS. Four proteins, FN-1, YWHAZ, SOD2, and CALD1 were identified as miR-4693-5p potential targets, and these common DEPs were validated with their associated pathways (Fig. 2).

The synovium, the main site of RA activity, features a unique ECM and resident cells50. In RA, changes in ECM proteins such as FN-1, E-CAD, N-CAD, and vimentin are reported to drive synovial fibroblasts to transform into a more invasive, mesenchymal-like phenotype and develop apoptosis resistance51. FN-1, N-CAD, significantly promotes synovial fibroblast growth in RA, while E-CAD and vimentin contribute to RA pathology44. Dysregulated miRNAs alter ECM proteins and promote EMT, contributing to RA progression52. Previous reports indicated that miR-4693-5p modulates ECM proteins to suppress EMT transition in CRC cells38. Our data also suggested that miR-4693 can target ECM-related pathways and proteins and might regulate EMT transition in RA. We validated that miR-4693-5p suppresses the EMT process by upregulating E-CAD and downregulating N-CAD and FN-1 protein expression in RA-FLS, helping reduce cell proliferation and adhesion (Fig. 3).

The CALD1 gene encodes Caldesmon, associated with the cytoskeleton and involved in tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis53. Abnormal CALD1 expression has been found to link with various cancers, including gastric cancer, and affects prognosis, but its role in RA remains unclear54. Literature mining suggested that inhibition of CALD1 reduced proliferation, invasion, and migration and contributed to the regulation of EMT in cancer cells53. We found that miR-4693-5p downregulated CALD1 expression (Fig. 3D), concluding that miR-4693-5p inhibits the proliferation and EMT transition and may lead to reduced proliferation via increased cellular death.

Pro-angiogenic factors like VEGF, overexpressed in RA, drive synovial vascular proliferation, worsening inflammation, and hypoxia41. miR-4693-5p reduced VEGF levels, indicating reduced angiogenesis (Fig. 3E), giving strong support that miR-4693-5p helps in reducing EMT by lowering the expression of ECM proteins and VEGF, thus inhibiting cell proliferation.

In RA, the important factors for joint deterioration are due to apoptosis resistance and excessive cell growth in the synovial tissue, leading to pannus formation and joint inflammation42. YWHAZ, a protein essential for cell survival and inhibiting apoptosis, has been reported to be overexpressed in RA conditions, contributing to disease progression43. In RA-FLS, miR-4693-5p notably downregulated YWHAZ, leading to reduced proliferation (Fig. 4A) and increased apoptosis by clear nuclear fragmentation55 (Fig. 4B). These findings and pathway enrichment analysis indicate that miR-4693-5p has a regulatory role in apoptosis-related pathways, offering a potential therapeutic target for RA management.

Next, the effect of miR-4693-5p on cell proliferation by MTT assay and cell proliferation marker ki67 expression confirmed that miR-4693-5p significantly decreased RA-FLS proliferation, suggesting its potential in controlling RA progression (Fig. 4C-D).

Oxidative stress disrupts the balance of cellular redox homeostasis, often reflected in the altered expression of primary antioxidant enzymes such as SOD2, Catalase, and GPx1. SOD2 is a specific mitochondrial enzyme that regulates mitochondrial ROS56, Our results indicated that miR-4693-5p decreases oxidative stress by enhancing the levels of key antioxidant enzymes and SOD2, thereby lowering mitochondrial oxidative stress (Fig. 5A-B). Overexpression of miR-4693-5p also led to a marked upregulation of catalase (Fig. 5C) and GPx1 (Fig. 5D), indicating a potential compensatory mechanism against oxidative insult, highlighting the role of miR-4693-5p in reinforcing the antioxidant defense network (Fig. 5).

Similarly, the four common proteins were evaluated in vivo to validate the therapeutic effects of miR-4693-5p. Group 4 exhibited a marked decrease in FN-1, YWHAZ, and CALD1 expression levels relative to Groups 2 and 3. In contrast, SOD2 expression was notably higher in Groups 4 and 5, reflecting an enhanced resistance to oxidative stress (Fig. 6). Our study, therefore, illustrated that miR-4693-5p is vital in curbing RAFLS proliferation and inhibiting the EMT process, key contributors to synovial hyperplasia and tissue invasiveness. By targeting proteins such as FN-1, N-CAD, and CALD1, miR-4693-5p not only influences cell growth but also regulates ECM remodeling, thereby impacting the structural integrity of the joint. This function is particularly relevant, given the role of oxidative stress in joint damage and inflammation in RA. In summary, the multifaceted role of miR-4693-5p in regulating apoptosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and ECM dynamics underscores its importance as a therapeutic target for RA. Targeting miR-4693-5p may provide a novel strategy for controlling disease progression and improving outcomes for RA patients. Collectively, these results highlight miR-4693-5p as a multifaceted regulator of apoptosis, oxidative stress, ECM remodeling, and angiogenesis in RA. These processes are not only crucial but also highly interconnected, related to RA pathogenesis42. Targeting miR-4693-5p could offer a novel strategy to curb synovial hyperplasia, pannus formation, and joint destruction, addressing critical aspects of RA progression.

The ability of miR-4693-5p to regulate various signaling pathways highlights its potential as a therapeutic target for RA. Its significant impact on these pathways emphasises its therapeutic relevance, especially as multiple molecular pathways contribute to RA pathogenesis. Our findings suggest that miR-4693-5p plays a complex role in regulating key pathways and their interactions. Targeting miR-4693-5p could lead to novel therapeutic strategies and improved treatment outcomes for patients with RA.

Limitations of the study

Further studies involving larger patient populations are essential to validate the effectiveness of this treatment and to explore its potential applications in managing RA. To enhance the clinical relevance of TNF-α-mediated regulation of miR-4693-5p, it is necessary to further validate the predicted target binding site in RAFLS. Additionally, conducting rescue experiments to evaluate the impact of target overexpression on miR-4693-5p expression is an important next step in future research to confirm the therapeutic efficacy of miR-4693-5p.

Conclusion

This study highlights the protective role of miR-4693-5p in RA pathogenesis. We found that miR-4693-5p is negatively regulated by TNF-α and is downregulated in RA, contributing to disease progression. By targeting key proteins, including FN-1, CALD1, YWHAZ, and SOD2, miR-4693-5p effectively suppresses EMT, promotes apoptosis, reduces mitochondrial ROS, and inhibits RAFLS proliferation as well as oxidative stress. These findings underscore miR-4693-5p as a promising therapeutic target for RA.

The minimal periodicity for integral bases of pure number fields
Khai-Hoan Nguyen-Dang

Abstract
. Fix n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . For the pure field K a = ℚ ⁡ ( θ ) K_{a}=\mathbb{Q}(\theta) , θ n = a \theta^{n}=a with a a n n th-power-free, we encode an integral basis in the fixed coordinate { 1 , θ , … , θ n − 1 } \{1,\theta,\dots,\theta^{n-1}\} by its shape . We prove a sharp local-to-global principle: for each p e | n p^{e}\!\parallel n , the local shape at p p is determined by a mod p e + 1 a\bmod p^{\,e+1} , and this precision is optimal. Moreover, the global shape is periodic with minimal modulus M ⁡ ( n ) = ∏ p e | n p e + 1 = n ⋅ rad ⁡ ( n ) , M(n)=\prod_{p^{e}\parallel n}p^{\,e+1}=n\cdot\mathrm{rad}(n), providing many applications in the understanding integral bases of pure number fields.

1.
Introduction

A classical problem in algebraic number theory is to study the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a number field
K
, in particular for the pure fields
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
with
\theta^{n}=a
(see
[
Gaá19
]
for a modern treatment). Recently, two complementary phenomena have guided works on this direction. First, in small degrees (
3\leq n\leq 9
) Gaál–Remete
[
GR17
]
showed that the
global pattern
of integral bases is
periodic
as
a
varies, with period
n^{2}
. In particular, if one writes an integral basis with rational coefficients in the
\{1,\theta,\dots,\theta^{n-1}\}
-coordinates, then every prime dividing a denominator must divide
n
(a global restriction that already foreshadows a local explanation). This offers a striking, hands‑on picture of regularity in families of pure fields. Secondly, for general
n
and squarefree
a
, Jakhar–Khanduja–Sangwan
[
JKS21
]
gave
explicit
integral bases and proved a global period
n\cdot\prod_{p\mid n}p
. Their construction is intrinsically local: it splices together
p
-integral bases at primes
p\mid n
and encodes, for each
m=1,\dots,n-1
, a
p
‑adic denominator exponent and a small “linear correction”. The dependence on
a
is controlled by a short list of
p
‑adic invariants (e.g.
v_{p}(a^{p-1}-1)
), revealing that only one extra
p
‑adic digit beyond
p^{e_{p}}\parallel n
is ever needed to fix the local shape at
p
.

In this paper we answer the question on how much information about
a
do the local computations at each
p\mid n
really need? Equivalently, what is the smallest modulus
M(n)
such that, whenever
a\equiv a^{\prime}\pmod{M(n)}
, the structure of the integral basis of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[n]{a})}
is the same as for
a^{\prime}
? We show that the Jakhar–Khanduja–Sangwan period is in fact
minimal
, in a precise sense, for all
n\geq 3
, and we do so in a way that emphasizes the underlying local mechanism.mThe first novelty of our paper is we unify and formalize these two phenomena by introducing the concept of
shape
(defined precisely in
2.2
) as the sequence, for
m=1,\dots,n-1
, consisting of (i) the prime‐power denominators supported on
p\mid n
and (ii) the corresponding linear corrections recorded modulo those denominators. We then prove that (see Theorem
3.1
) the smallest global modulus governing periodicity of the shape is always

\ M(n)=\prod_{p^{e_{p}}\parallel n}p^{\,e_{p}+1}\;=\;n\cdot\mathrm{rad}(n)

In particular
M(n)\mid n^{2}
for all
n
, with equality
M(n)=n^{2}
if and only if
n
is squarefree. Thus we both recover the
n^{2}
-period of
[
GR17
]
and pinpoint exactly when it is sharp.

Method

Our proof is short and local-to-global. More precisely, fix a prime power
p^{e}\parallel n
. Running the Ore–Montes (or higher Newton polygon) procedure (see
[
MN92
,
GMN12
,
GMN15
]
) for
f(x)=x^{n}-a
at
p
up to order
e
shows that the entire
p
-type (slopes, residual polynomials, and hence the
p
-integral basis with its denominators/corrections) is only determined by the residue class

a\pmod{p^{\,e+1}}.

Already among units
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
one can choose

a\equiv 1+p^{e}u\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{e+1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad a^{\prime}\equiv 1+p^{e+1}u^{\prime}\pmod{p^{e+2}},

for which the numerical invariant
r_{p}=v_{p}(a^{p-1}-1)-1
jumps from
e-1
to
\geq e
. Since the local denominators and linear corrections depend on
d_{p}=\min\{r_{p},e\}

[
JKS21
, Theorem 1.6]
, the
p
-part of the shape changes; hence
p^{e+1}
is the least local modulus at
p
.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem the global shape depends only on
a\bmod\prod_{p\mid n}p^{e_{p}+1}=n\cdot\mathrm{rad}(n)
, giving the optimal upper bound. The local sharpness at
every

p\mid n
makes this modulus minimal.

Applications and further consequences

The point of view developed here—the
local shape
at each
p\mid n
and its
global
gluing—yields several immediate applications. Firstly, we can show that one can compute, for any fixed
n
, a finite lookup table indexed by the classes
a\bmod M(n)
recording the
p
–denominator exponents and reduced corrections for every
p\mid n
. Thanks to Theorem
3.1
, the table itself can be filled entirely locally by running the higher Newton polygon up to order
e_{p}
at each
p\mid n
. This offers a uniform and predictable alternative to ad hoc casework used in small degrees.

The second application concerns
monogeneity
tests localized at the primes
p\mid n
. When
p\parallel n
, the obstruction coming from the order‑
1
Newton polygon at
p
is governed by the single “Wieferich” congruence
a^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
: fixing
a\bmod p
with
a^{p-1}\equiv 1\bmod p
, the dichotomy between
a^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
and
a^{p-1}\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
decides whether the order‑
1
type is
p
‑regular and hence whether the local index contribution at
p
vanishes. More generally, for
p^{e}\parallel n
and
v_{p}(a)=0
the relevant invariant is
d_{p}(a)=\min\{\,v_{p}(a^{p-1}-1)-1,\,e\,\}
, and Theorem
3.1
shows that this (and with it all
p
–denominator exponents and reduced corrections in an explicit basis) is determined by
a\bmod p^{e+1}
and cannot be recovered from
a\bmod p^{e}
alone.

Finally, because the shape is a class function of
a\bmod M(n)
, families of fields cut out by finitely many local conditions on
a\bmod M(n)
admit
explicit counting and density
statements. Using the equidistribution of
n
th–power–free integers in arithmetic progressions, one obtains asymptotics and natural densities for any prescribed union of shape classes; in particular, the global density factorises into a product of local proportions attached to the sets of admissible residues in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{e_{p}+1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

1.1.
Historical remarks and related results

The study of integral bases and monogeneity goes back to Dedekind and Hensel and is tightly intertwined with the index, discriminant, and the factorization of rational primes in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Dedekind’s criterion and index theorem explain how, when
p\nmid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta]]
, the prime-ideal factorization of
p\mathcal{O}_{K}
is read off from the factorization of the minimal polynomial modulo
p
(see, e.g.,
[
Ded78
]
). Hensel developed local methods that relate common index divisors to the splitting pattern of
p

[
Hen08
]
.

When Dedekind’s criterion fails (i.e. some
p\mid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta]]
), the modern local machine is based on Newton polygons at
p
, initiated by Ore in the 1920s and refined into a systematic algorithm by Montes and collaborators: one builds (higher-order)
p
-adic Newton polygons, reads residual polynomials along principal sides, and from this recovers
p
-adic factorization, indices, discriminants, and
p
-integral bases
[
Ore28
,
MN92
,
GMN12
,
GMN15
]
. This underlies much current algorithmic number theory around integral bases and prime decomposition. For practical
p
-integral basis extraction and index computations using Newton polygons, see ElFadil and his collaborators’ papers
[
EFMN12
,
EF20
]
and expositions cited therein. For the complexity and implementation discussions, take a look at
[
BNS13
]
. These provide worked implementations that agree with and conceptually support the p-local pieces used by Gaál–Remete and Jakhar–Khanduja–Sangwan.

In Kummer or radical extensions over cyclotomic bases, the decisive local congruences at tame primes naturally live one level deeper in the local parameter: modulo
(1-\zeta_{p})^{2}
(the uniformizer at
p
in
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}]
), mirroring the pure-field “one more
p
-adic digit” from
p
to
p^{2}
. For a modern treatment proving relative monogeneity criteria and non-monogeneity results in this framework see Smith
[
Smi19
,
Smi21
]
.

2
introduces the

for quick reference.

3.
Main theorems

In this section we prove the precise local-to-global statement that underlies the paper: at each prime
p^{e}\parallel n
the
local
p
–shape
of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{a}}
is decided by a single additional
p
‑adic digit beyond
p^{e}
, and this is best possible; consequently the
global
shape is periodic with minimal modulus

M(n)\;=\;\prod_{p^{e}\parallel n}p^{\,e+1}\;=\;n\cdot\rad(n).

As a preliminary reduction, if an integral basis is written in the
\{1,\theta,\dots,\theta^{n-1}\}
–coordinates, by the Gaál–Remete theorem, only primes dividing
n
can occur in the denominators in the chosen coordinates, so the local shape
\mathbf{S}_{p}(a)
is only defined and only varies for
p\mid n
.

The main tool we use is the Ore–Montes machinery, referring to Guardia–Montes–Nart
[
GMN12
]
. Fix
p\mid n
with
p^{e}\parallel n
and write
v=v_{p}
. Consider
f(x)=x^{n}-a\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Given a monic
\varphi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
with irreducible reduction, every

has a unique
\varphi
‑adic expansion

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}(x)\,\varphi(x)^{i},\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x],\ \deg a_{i}<\deg\varphi,

whose
principal
\varphi
‑Newton polygon

N^{-}_{\varphi}(f)
is the lower convex hull of the points
(i,u_{i})
with
u_{i}:=v(a_{i})
(points with
u_{i}=\infty
ignored). The
residual coefficients
along a side
S
of slope
-\lambda=-h/e
are defined by

c_{s+je}\ :=\ \overline{\,a_{s+je}/p^{\,u_{s}-jh}\,}\ \in\ \mathbb{F}_{\varphi},\qquad j=0,\dots,d,

and the associated
residual polynomial
is
R_{\lambda}(f)(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{s+je}y^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{\varphi}[y]
(here
(s,u_{s})
is the initial point of
S
and

its degree).

Higher orders are built by iterating this construction with
types

t=(\varphi_{1};\lambda_{1},\varphi_{2};\ldots;\lambda_{r-1},\varphi_{r};\psi_{r}).

At order

one considers the
\varphi_{r}
–expansion with respect to the augmented valuation
\omega_{r}
determined by

, forms the principal polygon

and residual polynomials

, and so on. The key facts are as follows (see
[

, §2-4]
):

(1)

the
order–

are again reductions of certain
p
–adic coefficients divided by a power of

determined by the initial ordinate of the relevant side;

(2)

once the (finite) chain of polygons and residual polynomials is fixed up to the necessary order, the
p
–factorization of
p\mathcal{O}_{K_{a}}
and a
p
–integral basis follow from the index theorem and the
p
–integral–basis construction.

We now prove our main result.

Theorem 3.1

(Minimal local/global modulus for the shape)
.

Let

,

be
n

K_{a}=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
with
\theta^{n}=a
satisfying hypothesis (

and let

and

(i)

(Local determinacy.)
For every

depends only on the residue class of

. Equivalently, if

\mathbf{S}_{p}(a)=\mathbf{S}_{p}(a^{\prime})
.

(ii)

(Local sharpness.)
For every
p\mid n

4.2.

We begin by stating (and proving) a precise form that cleanly separates the dependence on the residue class.

Definition 4.5

(Admissibility notions)
.

Fix

and

. For a residue

and a prime power

to be the
p
–adic valuation of a chosen representative of

(a)

with

. Equivalently, the progression

does not force the divisibility

for any prime

.

(b)

We say that

is

–admissible
if for every
p^{\alpha}\parallel q
we have

. This is stronger than (a) and will be exactly the condition that makes the main term
independent
of

.

Theorem 4.6

(Equidistribution with explicit
r
–dependence)
.

Fix
q\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
, and a residue
r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. Let

\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)\ :=\ \#\{\,|a|\leq X:\ a\equiv r\pmod{q},\ a\text{ is $n$th–power–free}\,\}.

Then, as

,

\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)\ =\ \frac{2X}{q}\,\frac{1}{\zeta(n)}\,\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{\,n}}\Bigr)^{-1}\ \prod_{p^{\alpha}\parallel q}\Bigl(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{v_{p}(r)\geq\alpha\}}\,p^{\alpha-n}\Bigr)\ +\ O\!\bigl(X^{1/n}\bigr).

In particular:

•

If
r
is
not

–admissible, then
\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)=O(1)
(indeed eventually
0
).

•

If
r
is

–admissible
then the main term simplifies to

\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)\ =\ \frac{2X}{q}\,\frac{1}{\zeta(n)}\,\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{\,n}}\Bigr)^{-1}\ +\ O\!\bigl(X^{1/n}\bigr),

independently of

.

Proof.

Write the indicator of
n

1_{\text{$n$–free}}(a)=\sum_{d^{n}\mid a}\mu(d).

Hence

\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)=\sum_{d\geq 1}\mu(d)\cdot\#\bigl\{|a|\leq X:\ a\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q),\ d^{n}\mid a\bigr\}.

For fixed

, set
g=\gcd(d^{n},q)
and
L=\mathrm{lcm}(d^{n},q)=qd^{n}/g
. The two congruences are simultaneously solvable iff
g\mid r
, in which case they define a single class modulo

. Thus the inner count equals
\tfrac{2X}{L}+O(1)
if

, and
0
otherwise. We obtain

\mathcal{F}_{n}(X;q,r)=\frac{2X}{q}\sum_{d\geq 1}\mu(d)\frac{g}{d^{n}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g\mid r\}}\ +\ O\!\Bigl(\sum_{d\leq X^{1/n}}1\Bigr),

and and the error is
O\!\big(\sum_{d\leq X^{1/n}}1\big)=O(X^{1/n})
.

The sum is
multiplicative
in
d
. We used that only
\nu_{p}(d)\in\{0,1\}
contribute (since
\mu(p^{k})=0
for
k\geq 2
) and that
g=\gcd(d^{n},q)=\prod_{p^{\alpha}\,\|\,q}p^{\min\{n\nu_{p}(d),\,\alpha\}}
. Writing

and

, one has

\frac{g}{d^{n}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{g\mid r\}}=\prod_{p}\Bigl(p^{\min\{n\nu_{p}(d),\alpha_{p}\}-n\nu_{p}(d)}\ \mathbf{1}_{\{\min\{n\nu_{p}(d),\alpha_{p}\}\leq v_{p}(r)\}}\Bigr),

with the convention

Multiplying over

. If

∎

Remark 4.7

(Non–monogeneity of positive density)

In degree

Corollary 4.8

(Uniformity on “

If

Remark 4.9

(On the choice

)
.

In our applications we take

Note that all denominator phenomena are localised at

\mathsf{Sh}_{n}:\ \{a\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ $n$th–power–free}\}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal{S}_{n}

Theorem 4.10

(Equidistribution and density)
.

As

,

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}(X)\ =\ \frac{2X}{M(n)}\,\biggl(\prod_{p}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{\,n}}\Bigr)\biggr)\,\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{\,n}}\Bigr)^{-1}\,\#\mathcal{R}\ +\ o(X).

In particular, the natural density of

\delta(\mathcal{R})\ =\ \frac{\#\mathcal{R}}{M(n)}\cdot\frac{1}{\zeta(n)}\prod_{p\mid n}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{\,n}}\Bigr)^{-1}.

Proof.

Write the indicator of

gives the formula; standard partial summation bounds yield
o(X)
.
If

∎

This is the standard Möbius‑inversion count for

Remark 4.11

(Local factorisation of the count)

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem,

Example 4.12

(Counting and density for

)
.

Here

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\text{shape }(k_{2,1},k_{2,2},k_{2,3})&\text{classes }r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 8)\\

Example 4.13

(Distribution of

Appendix A
The Ore–Montes toolkit for
f(x)=x^{n}-a
at a fixed prime
p

In this section we give a concise, self–contained exposition of the Ore–Montes (higher Newton polygon) method as it applies to the binomials

f(x)=x^{n}-a\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
that play main role in the proof the minimal local (hence global) modulus controlling the
shape
of integral bases.

Fix a prime
p
, write
v=v_{p}
for the normalized
p
–adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and
consider
f(x)=x^{n}-a
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}

n
th–power–free. Let
p^{e}\parallel n
(
e\geq 1
).

(1) Order 1 polygons and residual polynomials.

Choose a monic lift
\varphi_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
of an irreducible factor of
f(x)\bmod p
:

\varphi_{1}=\begin{cases}x-u,&\text{if }v(a)=0\text{ with }u^{n}\equiv a\pmod{p},\\
x,&\text{if }v(a)>0.\end{cases}

Write the
\varphi_{1}
–adic expansion
f=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{1,i}(x)\,\varphi_{1}(x)^{i}
with

a_{1,i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
and
\deg a_{1,i}<\deg\varphi_{1}
, and let
u_{1,i}:=v(a_{1,i})
.
The (principal) Newton polygon
N^{+}_{\varphi_{1}}(f)
is the lower convex hull of the points

(i,u_{1,i})
with
a_{1,i}\neq 0
. For each side
S
of slope
-\lambda_{1}=-h_{1}/e_{1}
(coprime

h_{1},e_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) whose lattice points are

(s,u_{1,s}),(s+e_{1},u_{1,s}-h_{1}),\dots,(s+d_{1}e_{1},u_{1,s}-d_{1}h_{1})
,
the
residual polynomial
in

\mathbb{F}_{\varphi_{1}}:=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(\overline{\varphi_{1}})
is

R_{1}(y)\ =\ \sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}c_{1,j}y^{j},\qquad c_{1,j}\ :=\ \Bigl(\,\frac{a_{1,s+je_{1}}(x)}{p^{\,u_{1,s}-jh_{1}}}\,\Bigr)\ \bmod(p,\varphi_{1}).

If each
R_{1}
is separable,
f
is
p
–regular and order 1 already determines the
p
–adic
factorization and a
p
–integral basis via Ore’s index theorem; otherwise pass to higher
orders.

(2) Higher orders, types, and augmented valuations.

A
type of order
t-1
is a datum

\mathbf{t}_{t-1}=(\varphi_{1},-\lambda_{1};\,\varphi_{2},-\lambda_{2};\,\dots;\,\varphi_{t-1},-\lambda_{t-1};\,\psi_{t-1})

where each
\varphi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
is a
key polynomial
,
\lambda_{i}=h_{i}/e_{i}>0
is a slope,
and
\psi_{t-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{t-1}[y]
is the last residual polynomial over a finite residue field

\mathbb{F}_{t-1}
. The associated
augmented valuation

\omega_{i+1}=e_{i}[\omega_{i},\varphi_{i},\lambda_{i}]
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(x)
is given on
\varphi_{i}
–adic expansions

F=\sum_{j}a_{i,j}\varphi_{i}^{j}
by

\omega_{i+1}(F)\ =\ \min_{j}\Bigl(e_{i}\,\omega_{i}(a_{i,j})+j\bigl(e_{i}\omega_{i}(\varphi_{i})+h_{i}\bigr)\Bigr).

At order
t
one forms the
\varphi_{t}
–adic expansion with respect to
\omega_{t}
, builds the
polygon
N_{t}(f)
, reads the residual polynomial
R_{t}(y)\in\mathbb{F}_{t}[y]
, and refines the type.
If the residual at the last principal side is separable, the process stops and Ore’s theorem
gives
\nu_{p}((\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta]))
together with a
p
–integral basis.

(3) Special features for
f=x^{n}-a
with
p^{e}\parallel n
and
(a,p)=1
.

In the pure binomial setting the chain of types never exceeds order
e
, and the principal
sides that actually occur have
height
1
. Concretely, when
v(a)=0
and

\varphi_{1}=x-u
, the order 1 polygon is a broken line with consecutive drops of height
1

(as encoded by
v_{p}(a^{p-1}-1)
); when
v(a)>0
and
\varphi_{1}=x
, the unique principal
side again has height
1
. In either branch, each lift to order
t
adds exactly one unit of
height.

We isolate the bookkeeping that drives our local modulus.

Lemma A.1

(Kummer valuations for the unit branch)
.

Let
p^{e}\,\|\,n
and
(a,p)=1
. In the
\varphi_{1}=x-u
development with
u^{n}\equiv a\pmod{p}
,

v_{p}\!\big(\tbinom{n}{i}u^{\,n-i}\big)\ \geq\ e-v_{p}(i)\qquad(1\leq i\leq n),

with equality for
i=p^{k}
(
0\leq k\leq e
). In particular
N^{-}_{\varphi_{1}}(f)
contains the points
(p^{k},e-k)
, and each principal side joining
(p^{k-1},e-(k-1))
to
(p^{k},e-k)
has height
1
.

Proof.

By Kummer’s theorem,
v_{p}\binom{n}{i}
equals the number of carries when adding
i
and
n-i
in base
p
. Since
p^{e}\,\|\,n
, one obtains
v_{p}\binom{n}{i}\geq e-v_{p}(i)
with equality for
i=p^{k}
. Multiplying by the unit
u^{n-i}
does not change the valuation.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Order–
t
residuals use
a\bmod p^{t+1}
)
.

Let
p^{e}\parallel n
and choose
\varphi_{1}
as above. For each
t
with
1\leq t\leq e
, the
residual polynomial
R_{t}(y)
built on a principal side of
N_{t}(f)
depends only on the
class of
a\bmod p^{t+1}
.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

By Lemma
A.1
, every principal side of
N_{t}(f)
that actually occurs up to order
e
has height
1
. Along such a side the residual coefficients have the form

c_{t,j}\;=\;\overline{\,a_{t,s+je}/p^{\,u_{t,s}-j}\,}\in\mathbb{F}_{t},

so they are determined by the coefficients modulo one more power of
p
than at order
t-1
. Induction on
t
shows that
R_{t}(f)
depends only on
a\bmod p^{t+1}
.
∎

As consequence, if
p^{e}\parallel n
, then the entire
p
–type of
f(x)=x^{n}-a
(slopes, residual polynomials,
and hence the

–integral basis output by Ore–Montes) is determined by

.

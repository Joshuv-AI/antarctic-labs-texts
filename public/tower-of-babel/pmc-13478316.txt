Benefits of building information modeling (BIM) for construction projects success in Pakistan
Authors: Maira Khan, Imran Asghar Warraich, Nadeem Sarwar, Orhan Yaman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAdoption of building information modeling (BIM) by the construction industry signifies a paradigm shift in its project management and design approaches. This study uses a serial mediation model to explain the relationship between BIM benefits and project success (PS), focusing on the crucial aspects of BIM implementation (BIMI), internal stakeholder satisfaction (ISS), and top management support (TMS). A survey was conducted, and a structural equation modelling approach (SEM) was used to validate the constructs. A total of 314 valid responses were collected from Pakistan Engineering Council (PEC) registered firms to test the proposed hypotheses. The results indicate that BIMI mediates the relation between BIMB and PS. Also, ISS mediates the relation between BIMB and PS. Furthermore, BIMI and ISS sequentially mediate the relationship between BIMB and PS. However, the moderation of TMS was not established between BIMB and BIMI. Findings show that the influence of BIM benefits on project success follows a sequential mediation pathway organized by effective BIM implementation and internal stakeholder satisfaction with BIM outputs. This study indicates future research direction by providing both theoretical insights and practical consequences for construction project management, as well as concrete tactics to maximize project success in the BIM environment in the construction industry.Keywords: BIM benefits, BIM implementation, Project success, Top management support, Internal stakeholder satisfactionSubject terms: Business and management, Business and management, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

For visualization and managing infrastructure throughout its life cycle BIM, is a solution that is being used nowadays in developed countries. For better outcomes and efficient project execution, BIM technology is transforming the way projects are delivered across industries by adding artificial intelligence and competence to project execution and providing better team communication, reliable data, and workflows throughout the project lifecycle.

Inefficiencies, low profitability, and the need for improved information management are a few of the challenges faced by the construction industry of Pakistan1–3. According to studies, 57% of the project cost can be reduced by the implementation of BIM, by minimizing the cost and time of a project1,4–6. According to Hofstede Cultural Dimension of Pakistan, uncertainty avoidance in Pakistan is at the higher side, inherent resistance to change, inadequate organizational support, and a lack of industry standards are to list a few of the barriers in the adoption of BIM in Pakistan4,7. Despite these barriers, BIM is considered more economical and efficient than other management techniques in Pakistan6,7.

The level of complexity in the construction business is increasing day by day. The construction industry is becoming more difficult for professionals due to tighter schedules, more complex projects, and constant financial limitations8–10. The industry is therefore driven to seek out novel approaches and solutions, especially those that make use of automation and cutting-edge technology11. The increasing difficulty and competitive nature of the construction business has been contributed to by a number of factors, including time limits, complex jobs, and limited budgets. This has prompted the quest for automatically created and technically sophisticated solutions and methodologies12. During the architectural design process, a building project is broken down into stages where it is created and evaluated. This technique can provide you with better authority over your project by allowing you to determine review intervals, create a systematic distribution of design information, and identify the natural stages of design. The design process is necessary because it paves the way for organized project administration and open channels of communication. The stages of the design process are what allow for clear and detailed production planning, which in turn reduces the risk of delays that are both expensive and time-consuming13.

Construction projects require a significant amount of time and coordination. There is a significant amount of paperwork and information involved in these particular stages that span from the beginning of a project to its potential conclusion. For the project to successfully meet its goals and scope, professionals from various companies must collaborate and seamlessly integrate into the project as a unified team. Discontent, skepticism, and disagreement may arise when fragmented information is shared extensively and results in misunderstandings among individuals involved in the project. Time, budget, and quality are just a few of the typical project objectives that could be adversely impacted by the issues mentioned earlier14.

BIM is one way that could be extremely helpful in such context. According to15, BIM signifies an enormous shift in the construction and design process by replacing the old, siloed workflows with a more collaborative and data-centric one. Better communication, the elimination of information silos, and the simplification of collaboration among architects, engineers, and contractors are all made possible through BIM16,17. The design process itself is significantly improved by the use of BIM. According to12, BIM gives stakeholders more control over the evolution of the design by dividing a project into different stages that are separated by explicit review intervals. The use of this organized method makes it possible to identify natural design phases, promotes open communication channels, and makes it easier to distribute design knowledge in a systematic manner.

According to18, having a clearly defined design process not only makes it easier to manage projects effectively but also lessens the likelihood of experiencing delays that are both expensive and time-consuming. Due to the collaborative nature of construction projects, it is necessary to conduct seamless integration across several organizations throughout the entirety of the project lifecycle, beginning with the conception of the project and ending with its completion. According to13, traditional techniques frequently result in a mountain of paperwork and a fragmented interchange of information, which in turn increases the likelihood of participants in the project experiencing confusion and discontent. According to19, disagreements and misunderstandings that manifest themselves as a result of insufficient or contradictory information can have a substantial impact on the project’s goals, particularly with regard to the time, budget, and quality of the work.

The global adoption rates of BIM have remained unchanged, despite the fact that its potential is abundantly obvious20. According to21, this has resulted in a multitude of studies and practical examples that indicate successful implementation approaches for BIM. According to22,23, a substantial body of research investigates the deployment of BIM in industrialized nations, aimed at identifying critical variables for effective adoption. Pilot projects, extensive BIM education programs, national BIM initiatives, freely available BIM research papers and standards, dedicated news channels and forums for BIM discussions, and the construction of BIM work stage manuals are some of the aspects that contribute to the success of BIM20. However, this list is not exhaustive.

BIM adoption rates are staying the same all around the world. The delayed global acceptance of BIM has resulted in the production of a great number of studies, pieces of advice, and tangible instances of essential BIM implementation methodologies20. A significant amount of study has been conducted on the application of BIM in industrialized countries21 have been conducted on the topic of the benefits and drawbacks associated with the deployment of BIM. This research group has identified a number of methods as crucial to the successful implementation of BIM in developed countries. Some of these methods include BIM pilot projects, BIM education, national BIM programs, BIM research reports, BIM standards, BIM news and diaries, BIM discussions and councils, the BIM work stage manual, and BIM prerequisite regulations. Other methods include BIM work stage manuals.

Despite increasing global adoption of BIM, its implementation in developing countries such as Pakistan remains limited and fragmented due to institutional, technological, and organizational constraints22,23. Existing studies primarily focus on BIM adoption drivers and barriers, with limited attention to the mechanisms through which BIMB translate into project success, particularly through mediating organizational processes24. Furthermore, the role of internal stakeholder satisfaction and sequential mediation pathways remains underexplored in developing economy contexts.

This study contributes to the literature in three key ways. First, it develops a sequential mediation framework explaining how BIM benefits translate into project success through BIM implementation and stakeholder satisfaction. Second, it provides context-specific insights from Pakistan, where institutional and technological constraints shape BIM adoption differently compared to developed countries. Third, the study integrates multiple theoretical perspectives, including Technology Acceptance Theory and Innovation Diffusion Theory, to offer a more comprehensive explanation of BIM adoption and value realization.

This study addresses this gap by developing and testing a serial mediation model that explains how BIMB influence project success through BIMI and stakeholder satisfaction. The study contributes by extending BIM literature into a developing country context and empirically validating indirect pathways of value creation.

Literature review

The term “Building Information Modeling (BIM) benefits” refers to the advantages that are thought to be gained by a construction project through the implementation of the BIM planning and design methodology. These benefits encompass a wide range of positive outcomes, such as improved communication and collaboration among project participants, a more precise representation of project designs, a more expedient identification and resolution of design conflicts, the potential for cost reductions as a result of improved coordination, and the production of an improved project outcome25.

BIM benefits (BIMB)

National Building Information Modelling Standard (NBIMS) United States describes BIM as: throughput the project lifecycle Building information modelling is an innovative way of generating, sharing, exchanging and handling the information. In this study, BIM Benefits are conceptualized as perceived benefits that reflect stakeholders’ expectations of BIM value. These perceptions act as drivers of BIM adoption and implementation, rather than representing outcomes already realized.

According to25 BIM is a technology that may be characterized as one that depicts a project along with its spaces, structures, components, and materials along with their critical information and attributes. According to the definition provided by26, BIM is a collection of technologies, processes, and policies that makes it possible for numerous stakeholders to work together in a virtual space on the design, construction, and operation of a facility.

According to27 the application of BIM in the design and planning of construction projects involves the following aspects: cost calculation; 3D coordination; site visualization; 4D planning; site analysis; space programming; design review; sustainability evaluation; engineering analysis; and design review28. However29, stated in their research regarding BIM adoption in developing countries that contextual factors are important especially tailored strategies be adopted, considering economic capabilities of the countries. Therefore, they suggest that low income countries may focus on BIM goals and also these goals with their economic development needs30. While31 identify meeting technical criteria as a major barrier to operational green accreditation, implementing BIM provides a robust solution to overcome these challenges and streamline compliance.

In this study, a clear distinction is made between perceived BIM benefits and realized BIM benefits. Perceived benefits refer to stakeholders’ expectations regarding improvements in cost, coordination, and project efficiency prior to or during adoption. These perceptions act as motivational drivers influencing the decision to implement BIM. In contrast, realized benefits represent actual performance outcomes that materialize only after successful BIM implementation. Therefore, BIM benefits in this study are conceptualized as perceived antecedents, while BIM implementation serves as the mechanism through which these perceptions are converted into realized project outcomes.

BIM implementation (BIMI)

The implementation of BIM in the architecture, engineering, and construction industry is producing fruitful results in nations that have robust technology infrastructures. This is the result of extensive study and development over a long period of time into techniques for improving productivity through the application of technology. For many developing countries, the use of technology in the AEC (architecture, engineering and construction) sector has been a barrier that prevents them from addressing performance-related challenges in construction projects. The execution of a project may be slowed down by a number of difficulties, including a lack of proper professional training, low levels of awareness, excessive capital expenses, resistance to change, and the complexity of the BIM software32. While studying for challenges related to BIM for construction projects33, stated that knowledge, infrastructure, modeling, adoption, and awareness issue are important for effective BIMI.

When we talk about BIMI, we are referring to the process of incorporating BIM procedures, technologies, and techniques into the operational framework of a construction project. BIMI that is successful requires education and training programs for team members, the prudent use of time, money, and people to support BIM adoption, the seamless integration of BIM software with existing project management systems, and adherence to established BIM protocols and standards32. Moreover34, noted while studying the BIMI and project success in the Malaysian construction industry that it is complicated to integrate and to what level the different parts of project management are affected.

35 stated that less developed countries can learn from the practices of more developed countries how to implement the digitization and automation that is necessary for BIM adoption, for the implementation of BIM, education and training in BIM should be a standard component of engineering and other technical education programs that generate workers36 suggested that developing countries’ BIMI plans need to be adapted to the specifics of their respective environments37. stated that to begin, countries that are new to BIM adoption have a mandated obligation to achieve a minimum level of BIM adoption within the AEC industry. He further said analysis of costs and benefits, section in order to evaluate the prudence of adopting BIM from both a financial and a technological standpoint, all relevant parties should conduct an investigation into its implementation.

Project success (PS)

Assessing the success of finished tasks ought to be an essential component of the management of projects. On the other hand, there aren’t that many studies that look at how the rating process actually works in the real world. Organizations all across the world put their faith in projects to assist them in accomplishing their goals and realizing their ambitions. Because of its obvious influence on both performance and the outcome of projects, it is not surprising to see that businesses, government agencies, and other types of organizations are investing in the development of their project management skills. In many different ways, project management is becoming an increasingly important discipline. The achievement of a satisfactory conclusion to the project is the principal objective of project management. In the realm of project management, the definition of a successful project is still up to discussion, despite the fact that it is quite common38.

According to a study of39 the complex engineering projects have a lower probability of being successful as their level of complexity grows. The association between successful completion of a project and complexity was also examined along seven parameters. This research contributes to our understanding of the mechanisms and causal links between the complexity of a project, the success of the project, and other relevant elements because it examines project complexity using a systematic and thorough definition that is distinct from the definitions used in earlier studies. According to the findings of the research, taking into consideration these dimensions and the accompanying elements can be of assistance to project managers in making sense of complicated projects and attaining better results.

Top management support (TMS)

Top management support refers to the level of support, involvement, and endorsement that senior management or top executives provide for the BIM program that is being implemented within the company. In order to overcome opposition, allocate resources, and create organizational alignment, it is necessary to have strong backing from high management in order to successfully adopt and execute BIM. It is possible to obtain this support in a variety of ways, including securing funding for BIM projects, disseminating information about BIM throughout the organization, having a voice in decisions that influence the implementation of BIM, and directing and leading project teams40,41. Top management support is a critical factor in the success of organizational initiatives, and its impact has been studied in the context of information systems, supply chain management, project leadership, and other areas.

Many studies have emphasized the importance of active involvement and endorsement by top-level management in driving the success of various projects and initiatives. In organizations, top management support is an important factor for the successful implementation of BIM. Various research papers have identified that senior management support is a key for successful implementation of BIM, especially in the capacity of design companies in the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry. The study also found that senior management supports BIM technology in the presence of collaborative culture and technological learning42. Top management support is one of the institutional pressures that affect BIM adoption43. Top management support in promoting BIM adoption through employee training is of crucial importance44. Therefore, top management support is essential in organizations to ensure the successful implementation of BIM.

Internal stakeholder satisfaction (ISS)

Internal stakeholder satisfaction refers to the contentment and fulfillment of individuals or groups within an organization who have a direct interest in the outcomes and activities of a specific project or initiative. Research has shown that internal stakeholder satisfaction is closely linked to project success and is influenced by factors such as stakeholder engagement, perceived value, and electronic human resource management (e-HRM) attributes45,46. A study on renewable energy projects found that stakeholders’ satisfaction is a key determinant of critical success factors in such projects47. All research has emphasized the importance of understanding and managing the satisfaction of internal stakeholders to achieve positive project outcomes and organizational success.

Internal stakeholder satisfaction for BIM can be defined as the degree to which individuals or groups within an organization, such as project managers, architects, engineers, and other staff, are content with the implementation and use of BIM. Research has shown that BIM can influence stakeholder management practices and satisfaction. One of the most important factors that contributes to the satisfaction of internal stakeholders is the perception of gains in project efficiency, the simplification of cooperation procedures, the enhancement of decision-making functions, and the successful completion of project objectives47,48.

BIM benefits and project success

BIM benefits contribute to project success through several operational mechanisms. Improved visualization and clash detection reduce design errors and rework, leading to cost savings and improved quality. Enhanced coordination and information sharing among stakeholders minimize delays, thereby improving time performance. Additionally, better integration of project information supports informed decision-making, which further enhances overall project outcomes. This mediating role is also supported by prior empirical studies showing that BIM benefits influence outcomes indirectly through effective implementation practices.

Resource dependence theory

This theory proposes that in order for organizations to accomplish their objectives (the successful completion of a project), they must rely on resources that are not internal to the company (in this case, BIM)49. The BIM system offers a consolidated repository of information, which enables improved resource allocation and collaboration, ultimately contributing to the success of the project.

Transaction cost economics (TCE)

Economic theory known as transaction cost economics (TCE) proposes that businesses should strive to reduce the transaction costs that are connected with the execution of projects50. By reducing communication breakdowns and the need for rework, BIM can lead to decreased transaction costs and increased project success. Empirical evidence from a number of studies has demonstrated that there is a positive correlation between the benefits of BIM and the success of projects. According to51, the most important benefits of building information modeling (BIM) that contribute to the success of a project are improved communication, decreased errors, and enhanced cooperation. In a similar vein52, discovered that BIM makes it easier to reuse information and enhances the procedures involved in project delivery, which ultimately results in effective outcomes. Therefore, BIM benefits are expected to positively influence project success by improving cost, time, quality, and coordination performance, forming the basis for H1.

BIM implementation as a mediator

In some cases, the benefits of BIM may not have a direct impact on the success of a project. The implementation of BIM serves as a mediating variable, thereby transforming the potential benefits of BIM into results that can be observed and experienced. In this study, BIM benefits are treated as perceived drivers that encourage organizations to adopt BIM, whereas BIM implementation represents the process through which these perceived benefits are translated into realized project outcomes. Thus, BIM implementation functions as a mediating mechanism linking expected value to actual project performance.

Social cognitive theory

According to53, the Social Cognitive Theory proposes that watching successful BIM implementation (also known as observational learning) and receiving positive reinforcement for successfully utilizing BIM (also known as vicarious reinforcement) can both drive wider adoption of BIM and ultimately lead to successful project completion. The necessity of effective BIMI techniques, such as pilot projects and training programs, has been highlighted in research conducted by22,23. These strategies highlight the importance of optimizing benefits and attaining successful project outcomes. Furthermore, BIM implementation represents the process through which perceived benefits are translated into realized outcomes. Without effective implementation, these benefits do not materialize into project success.

Top management support as a moderator

The level of support from top management is an essential factor in determining the influence that the benefits of BIM have on the success of a project.

Upper echelons theory

According to this theory, the characteristics of top management, such as vision and risk tolerance, have an impact on the decisions and outcomes of a company54. Strong support from upper management for the deployment of BIM can help to establish a culture of innovation and offer resources, which ultimately leads to the success of the project due to the benefits of BIM18. Highlight the significance of leadership commitment in the process of being the driving force behind a culture of knowledge sharing and change, which is made possible by BIM. Therefore, this helps the overall success of the project. Furthermore, this moderating role has also been empirically validated in prior BIM and technology adoption studies.

Internal stakeholder satisfaction mediator

Although it is not expressly included in the model that is being suggested, the satisfaction of internal stakeholders with the implementation of BIM can have an effect on the success of the project.

Stakeholder theory

It is a theory that highlights the significance of effectively managing the expectations and satisfaction of stakeholders in order to ensure the success of a project55. It is more probable that internal stakeholders, such as members of the project team, would embrace BIM and make use of its benefits, which will ultimately contribute to the success of the project.

In addition to TAM, this study draws on Innovation Diffusion Theory56 to explain how perceived benefits influence BIM adoption decisions, particularly in environments with varying levels of technological readiness. Prior studies also support the sequential role of stakeholder satisfaction in linking technology implementation to project success outcomes.

The sequential mediation proposed in this study follows a process-based logic. Perceived BIM benefits encourage organizations to adopt and implement BIM practices. Effective BIM implementation improves coordination, communication, and information quality among project participants, which enhances internal stakeholder satisfaction. This improved satisfaction subsequently contributes to better project outcomes, thereby explaining the sequential pathway from BIM benefits to project success.

The mediation logic of this study follows a value realization perspective, where technological benefits do not directly translate into organizational outcomes unless they are operationalized. Drawing on Technology Acceptance Theory and Innovation Diffusion Theory, perceived benefits influence the intention to adopt and extent of implementation. However, implementation alone is insufficient unless it enhances stakeholder experience. Therefore, Stakeholder Theory explains the second stage of mediation, where improved processes lead to higher stakeholder satisfaction, which ultimately determines project success. This establishes a multi-stage transformation process from perceived value to realized performance.

Hypotheses development

The model assumes a causal flow where perceived BIM benefits drive implementation, which in turn enables value realization and project success. The proposed model is grounded in established theories. The direct effect (BIMB → PS) is supported by Resource Dependence Theory and Transaction Cost Economics. The mediating role of BIM implementation is justified by technology adoption literature, emphasizing value realization through implementation. The sequential mediation via internal stakeholder satisfaction is explained by Stakeholder Theory. Finally, the moderating role of top management support is grounded in Upper Echelons Theory, highlighting the role of managerial commitment in technology implementation. This supports the sequential mediation hypothesis (H4), where BIM implementation and stakeholder satisfaction jointly transmit the effect of BIM benefits on project success.

The sequential mediation structure (BIMB → BIMI → ISS → PS) is grounded in a process-based logic of technology assimilation. First, perceived benefits motivate organizations to adopt BIM practices (initiation stage). Second, BIM implementation alters workflows, coordination mechanisms, and information quality (process transformation stage). Third, these improvements influence the perceptions and satisfaction of internal stakeholders who directly interact with BIM systems (evaluation stage). Finally, enhanced stakeholder satisfaction leads to improved project outcomes (performance stage). This sequence reflects a temporal and causal ordering, where implementation must precede stakeholder evaluation, and stakeholder satisfaction must precede project success. Therefore, the proposed sequential mediation is theoretically consistent with staged models of technology adoption and organizational change. This staged logic is consistent with prior research on technology assimilation and value realization in information systems literature22,23, where benefits are realized progressively through implementation and user acceptance.

Below are the hypotheses for the study:

H1:

BIMB has a significant impact on PS.

H2:

BIMI mediates the relationship between BIMB and PS.

H3:

ISS mediates the relationship between BIMB on PS.

H4:

BIMI and ISS sequentially mediates the relationship between BIMB on PS.

H5:

TMS moderates the relationship b/w BIMB and BIMI.

Methodology

The study adopted a positivist research philosophy, emphasizing objective measurement and empirical observation through structured data collection. Positivism enabled the examination of relationships among variables with minimal researcher bias and interaction with respondents, ensuring objectivity in data collection and analysis57.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Bahria University, Lahore Campus. All procedures involving human participants were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the committee and the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to data collection.

The selection of mediator and moderator variables in this study is grounded in established theoretical and empirical literature. BIMI is conceptualized as the first mediator, representing the extent to which BIM is operationally adopted within organizational processes. It captures the transformation of perceived BIMB into actual practices, consistent with technology adoption frameworks58,59.

ISS is introduced as a second (sequential) mediator, reflecting the perceptions and acceptance of key project participants, including engineers, managers, and technical staff. This aligns with stakeholder theory, which emphasizes that project success is influenced by stakeholder perceptions and engagement55,60. Together, these mediators represent a logical pathway in which perceived benefits lead to implementation, which in turn enhances stakeholder satisfaction and ultimately contributes to project success.

TMS is modeled as a moderator due to its critical role in enabling technology adoption, resource allocation, and organizational change. Prior studies have consistently shown that managerial support strengthens the relationship between technological drivers and implementation outcomes59,61. Therefore, TMS is expected to influence the strength of the relationship between BIM benefits and BIM implementation.

Research approach

A quantitative research approach was employed to examine the relationships among BIMB, BIMI, ISS, TMS, and PS. This approach facilitated the collection and statistical analysis of numerical data, enabling hypothesis testing and identification of cause effect relationships among variables62.

Population & sample

The population of study was comprised of project managers, architects, engineers, and 3D visualizers working in the construction industry of Punjab and Sindh, Pakistan. Total number of Pakistan Engineering Council (PEC) registered firms in Punjab & Sindh of categories CA, CB, C1 & C2 are 239, 94, 317 & 765 respectively, moreover, total architectural firms registered in Pakistan Council of Architects & Town Planners (PCATP) are 224, so that makes the total population of 1639. The target was to collect at least one response from each firm, so according to63 sample size was 314.

Sampling technique & data collection

A combination of stratified and convenience sampling was adopted to balance representativeness and practical constraints. Stratified sampling ensured proportional representation across professional roles and regions, while convenience sampling facilitated access to respondents due to time and resource limitations. However, this approach may introduce sampling bias, which is acknowledged as a limitation57,64.

Moreover, a structured survey questionnaire was used to collect the data, the questionnaire was disseminated using a variety of means, such as Google Forms, WhatsApp, and personal email.

Measurement of the variables

Each construct was operationalized using multi-item scales adapted from prior validated studies. BIM Benefits included items related to cost reduction, coordination efficiency, and visualization; BIM Implementation measured the extent of BIM usage and integration; Project Success included time, cost, and quality performance indicators. The measurement approach is consistent with prior BIM and construction management research22,24,23.

To conduct the survey for this investigation, a modified version of the questionnaire used by14,39,65,66 was adapted. The questionnaire for the survey was divided into two sections. The goal was to gather information from AEC industry about examining the relationship of BIMB, BIMI, ISS, TMS and PS. Description of Variables is appended below in Table 1.

A five-point Likert scale was used due to its reliability and widespread application in behavioral and construction management research, enabling respondents to express varying degrees of agreement while maintaining statistical robustness67,68.

Data analysis technique

Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) was employed due to its suitability for complex models, prediction-oriented research, and relatively small sample sizes. It is particularly appropriate for exploratory studies and theory development69.

The analysis followed a two-step approach: assessment of the measurement model (reliability and validity) and evaluation of the structural model (path coefficients, significance levels, and predictive relationships). Bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples was used to assess the significance of path coefficients and mediation effects.

Discussion

The findings of this study provide important insights into the complex relationships influencing project success in the construction industry, particularly in the context of BIM adoption. Contrary to the initial hypothesis, the results indicate that there is no significant direct relationship between BIM benefits and project success. This finding suggests that the perceived advantages of BIM such as improved visualization, coordination, and collaboration do not automatically translate into improved project outcomes. Instead, these benefits must be effectively operationalized through structured implementation processes. While prior studies have reported a direct positive association between BIM benefits and project success (e.g.75,76), the present findings highlight that such benefits are realized indirectly rather than directly.

The findings highlight that, in the context of Pakistan, BIM adoption is not solely driven by perceived benefits but is constrained by institutional, technical, and organizational factors. This distinguishes the study from prior research conducted in developed countries, where stronger regulatory frameworks and technological readiness enable more direct realization of BIM benefits.

Furthermore, the results confirm that BIM implementation plays a critical mediating role, acting as the mechanism through which BIM benefits are translated into organizational outcomes. This indicates that the successful adoption and integration of BIM practices are essential for converting perceived benefits into tangible improvements in project performance. Therefore, BIM implementation serves as a necessary pathway for achieving value from BIM in construction projects.

Furthermore, this highlights the critical role that good BIM implementation techniques have in generating satisfaction among internal stakeholders, such as members of the project team and staff, by capitalizing on the benefits that are offered by BIM technology47,76 also found the similar results and ensured that BIM implementation has a positive impact on the internal stakeholder satisfaction. Furthermore, we uncover sufficient evidence to support the premise that the satisfaction of internal stakeholders acts as a mediator between the benefits of BIM and the success of the project. According to this, the degree to which internal stakeholders are satisfied with the implementation of BIM serves as an important intermediary mechanism that enables the positive impacts of BIM implementation to be translated into actual project outcomes and success measures47.

The significant mediating role of BIM implementation is consistent with recent studies emphasizing that technological benefits must be operationalized through structured processes to generate performance outcomes8.

Findings also indicate that the relationship between the benefits of BIM and the success of a project is mediated in a sequential manner by both the adoption of BIM and the satisfaction of internal stakeholders76 also found the significant results ensuring that BIM Implementation and Internal Stakeholder Satisfaction mediate the relationship between BIM Benefit and Project Success. This sequential mediation pathway highlights the cumulative effects of BIM implementation and stakeholder satisfaction in driving project success.

It also emphasizes the importance of evaluating the holistic impact of BIM adoption within project management frameworks through the use of this pathway. On the other hand, the hypothesis that top management support moderates the association between BIM benefits and BIM deployment is not supported by the findings of our investigation. Our findings reveal that top management support does not significantly influence the direct relationship between the benefits of BIM and the application of BIM practices on the ground42,77. found the similar results, this is despite the fact that top management support continues to be vital for aiding BIM implementation efforts. In conclusion, our research highlights the multifaceted nature of the impact that BIM has on the success of projects.

The findings of this study have important practical implications for the construction industry, particularly in developing countries such as Pakistan. The results indicate that BIM benefits do not directly translate into project success unless they are effectively operationalized through structured BIM implementation processes. This suggests that construction firms should move beyond merely recognizing the potential advantages of BIM and instead focus on developing organizational capabilities, technical expertise, and standardized workflows to ensure successful implementation.

Furthermore, the significant mediating role of internal stakeholder satisfaction highlights the importance of engaging project teams, engineers, and managers throughout the BIM adoption process. Organizations should invest in training, change management, and collaborative practices to enhance stakeholder acceptance and satisfaction, which ultimately leads to improved project outcomes. These findings align with recent studies emphasizing that BIM value is realized through organizational processes and human factors rather than technology alone61,78.

The findings of this study can be meaningfully interpreted through multiple theoretical perspectives. From the standpoint of Resource Dependence Theory (RDT), organizations rely on critical external resources such as technical expertise, digital infrastructure, and regulatory support to effectively implement BIM. The insignificant direct effect of BIM benefits on project success suggests that, in the absence of these supporting resources, perceived benefits alone are insufficient to generate performance outcomes79.

From a Transaction Cost Economics (TCE) perspective, BIM adoption is expected to reduce coordination costs, information asymmetry, and project inefficiencies. However, the results indicate that these efficiencies are realized only when BIM is properly implemented, reinforcing the role of implementation as a necessary mechanism for translating technological potential into operational value50.

Furthermore, the significant mediating role of internal stakeholder satisfaction is well explained by Stakeholder Theory, which posits that project success depends on the alignment, engagement, and satisfaction of key stakeholders. The findings suggest that BIM implementation enhances stakeholder coordination and communication, thereby improving satisfaction and ultimately contributing to project success59,60.

Collectively, these theoretical perspectives reinforce that BIM benefits are not inherently value-generating; rather, their impact on project success is contingent upon effective implementation processes and stakeholder engagement within the organizational and institutional context.

Moreover, the crucial roles that BIM implementation techniques and stakeholder satisfaction have in realizing the full potential of BIM technologies within the construction sector. These findings have significant repercussions for practitioners and policymakers who are looking to maximize the results of projects by incorporating BIM into project management processes in an efficient manner.

Implication of the study

Theoretical implications

Our study’s theoretical implications deepen our comprehension of how BIM contributes to the successful completion of construction projects. By illuminating the intricate relationship that exists between the benefits of BIM, implementation tactics, stakeholder satisfaction, and top management support, our findings make a number of contributions to the theoretical frameworks that are already in existence. To begin, our study challenges conventional ideas by finding a significant positive correlation between the benefits of BIM and the success of a project, which is diametrically opposed to the first notions. Existing ideas, which may ignore the revolutionary potential of BIM technology in determining project results, are called into question by this new information. Furthermore, our identification of mediating and sequential mediation pathways sheds light on the complex mechanisms that influence the success of a project through the deployment of BIM. This enhancement contributes to the development of theoretical models of technology adoption and project management. Furthermore, our findings highlight the significance of stakeholder satisfaction as a crucial mediator between the benefits of BIM and the success of a project. This highlights the need of incorporating stakeholder viewpoints into theoretical frameworks on the application of technology and organizational transformation. By providing empirical data and insights into the multidimensional nature of the impact that BIM has on project success, our study, in general, contributes to the advancement of theoretical understanding. This, in turn, paves the way for further theoretical growth in this context.

Practical implications

Our research has a number of practical implications, which provide essential information for practitioners and policymakers who are interested in making successful use of BIM within the construction industry. In the first place, our findings highlight the significance of investing in comprehensive BIM implementation plans, which should include training programs and resources, in order to provide employees with the knowledge and abilities they need to make effective use of BIM technologies. Therefore, companies may maximize the benefits of BIM technology by cultivating a culture of collaboration that is conducive to successful BIM deployment. This can be accomplished by increasing stakeholder participation and satisfaction. When BIM is seamlessly integrated into project management procedures, workflows are streamlined, errors are minimized, and resource allocation is optimized, all of which lead to superior project outcomes.

In addition, encouraging collaboration and the exchange of information among project stakeholders improves interdisciplinary teamwork and innovation, which ultimately leads to the successful completion of the proposed project. By continuing to invest in learning and development activities, organizations can ensure that their staff are always up to current and proficient in the utilization of BIM, which in turn facilitates the organization’s longevity and success. Furthermore, in order to drive effective BIM implementation projects, it is vital to acknowledge the significance of organizational change management efforts and to define metrics to evaluate the performance of BIM. Within the construction business, organizations have the ability to maximize the potential of BIM technology and improve the success of their projects by putting these tangible recommendations into action.

Limitations of the study

This study offers valuable insights; however, several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting and generalizing the findings. First, the reliance on survey-based, self-reported data may introduce response and social desirability biases, potentially affecting result accuracy. Second, the use of convenience sampling limits sample representativeness and may reduce external validity. Third, as the study is confined to the construction industry in Punjab and Sindh, Pakistan, the findings may not be directly generalizable to other regions or sectors.

Additionally, although efforts were made to control for confounding factors, the influence of unmeasured variables cannot be entirely excluded. Future research may extend this model to different geographical contexts and incorporate additional constructs, such as perceived risk, perceived usefulness, and perceived ease of use, to enhance its explanatory power.

Future research direction

Future research should build on this study’s limitations by conducting longitudinal analyses to assess the long-term impacts of BIM implementation and establish causal relationships among variables. Comparative studies across countries and sectors are also recommended to enhance generalizability and capture contextual influences.

Incorporating qualitative methods, such as interviews and case studies, can provide deeper insights into stakeholder perspectives and underlying mechanisms. Additionally, future work should examine factors like organizational culture and regulatory environments, and focus on developing and evaluating interventions that improve BIM effectiveness and stakeholder satisfaction.

Practical implications for the construction industry

This study provides several important implications for construction practitioners, policymakers, and project managers. First, the findings demonstrate that perceived BIM benefits alone are insufficient to ensure project success. Instead, organizations must prioritize effective BIM implementation strategies, including investment in digital infrastructure, skilled personnel, and standardized BIM protocols.

Second, the results highlight the critical role of internal stakeholder satisfaction as a key mechanism linking BIM implementation to project success. This implies that construction firms should adopt a human-centric approach by promoting collaboration, providing continuous training, and addressing resistance to technological change.

Third, the insignificant moderating effect of top management support suggests that managerial commitment must be complemented by institutional support mechanisms, such as industry standards, regulatory frameworks, and technical capacity development, particularly in developing countries.

Overall, the study emphasizes that successful BIM adoption requires a combination of technological readiness, organizational capability, and stakeholder engagement, rather than relying solely on perceived benefits59,60.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that BIM benefits do not directly lead to project success but operate through BIM implementation and stakeholder satisfaction as key mediating mechanisms. The findings contribute to BIM literature by validating a sequential mediation model in a developing country context. Practically, the results emphasize the importance of structured BIM implementation strategies and stakeholder engagement to realize project success.

From a practical perspective, this study provides actionable insights for construction firms seeking to enhance project performance through BIM. The findings suggest that organizations should focus on translating BIM benefits into practice through structured implementation and by ensuring stakeholder satisfaction. For policymakers, the results highlight the need to support BIM adoption through training programs, regulatory frameworks, and industry-wide standards. These measures are particularly important in developing countries, where structural and institutional barriers may limit the effective utilization of BIM technologies78.

The study contributes theoretically by validating a sequential mediation framework and integrating multiple perspectives, including Technology Acceptance Theory and Stakeholder Theory, to explain BIM adoption and value realization.

From a practical standpoint, the results highlight the need for construction firms to focus on implementation capabilities, stakeholder engagement, and capacity building rather than relying solely on perceived technological advantages.

Recommendations

To maximize the adoption of BIM in the infrastructure construction sector, organizations should prioritize the development of effective BIM implementation strategies supported by comprehensive training programs. Enhancing stakeholder engagement is essential, which can be achieved by fostering a collaborative culture and involving project teams throughout the implementation process.

BIM should be seamlessly integrated across all project phases from design to construction and facility management to improve efficiency, reduce errors, and optimize resource use. Using BIM as a centralized platform for collaboration further strengthens interdisciplinary coordination and project outcomes.

Continuous learning initiatives must be maintained to ensure ongoing proficiency in BIM tools. Additionally, organizations should emphasize change management practices and establish performance metrics to monitor BIM effectiveness. Finally, active collaboration with industry partners promotes knowledge sharing, innovation, and the broader advancement of BIM adoption in the construction sector.

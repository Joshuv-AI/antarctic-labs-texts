321. Parsing neuroanatomical pathways linking stress to schizophrenia
Authors: N Feng, X Ma, W Pu, Q Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStress is a key environmental risk factor for schizophrenia. However, the stress-schizophrenia mediating pathway remains undetermined mainly due to its heterogeneity.Aims & ObjectivesTo account for the heterogeneity, a stratification algorithm was designed to elucidate the stress-psychosis mediating patterns of volumetric changes across multiple brain regions.MethodThis study analyzed the brain's grey-matter volume, polygenic schizophrenia risk, stressful life event, and psychotic-like experience in the IMAGEN cohort (N=975, age 19). A mediating-pathway stratification algorithm was used to identify subgroups in which individuals share the same pattern of volumetric changes that mediates the stress-schizophrenia relationship. We trained subgroup classifiers and applied them to validation datasets, including unaffected first-degree relatives of schizophrenia (UFR-SZ) and early-onset schizophrenia (EOS) patients.ResultsWe identified four subgroups with distinct mediating patterns of grey-matter volumetric changes. One subgroup had a tri-network pattern of increased grey-matter volumes in the executive-control, auditory and default-mode regions, that was associated with both higher stress and more psychotic-like experience. The salience, dorsal-attention, and limbic regions were additionally involved in the other three subgroups, respectively. The tri-network subgroup had higher polygenic schizophrenia risk than the salience-augmented subgroup. Only the tri-network subgroup was generalized to UFR-SZ and EOS patients. Several key biological processes associated with stress responses, immune and inflammation regulation were enriched in the tri-network subgroup.Discussion & ConclusionsThese findings emphasized the importance of stratification in parsing the heterogeneity in stress-schizophrenia neural mediating pathways, highlighting a tri-network pathway.

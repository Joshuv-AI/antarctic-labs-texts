Gaussian Waves and Edge Eigenvectors of Random Regular Graphs
Horng-Tzer Yau

Abstract
Backhausz and Szegedy (2019) demonstrated that the almost eigenvectors of random regular graphs converge to Gaussian waves with variance 0 ⩽ σ 2 ⩽ 1 0\leqslant\sigma^{2}\leqslant 1 . In this paper, we present an alternative proof of this result for the edge eigenvectors of random regular graphs, establishing that the variance must be σ 2 = 1 \sigma^{2}=1 . Furthermore, we show that the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are asymptotically independent. Our approach introduces a simple framework linking the weak convergence of the imaginary part of the Green’s function to the convergence of eigenvectors, which may be of independent interest.

1.1
Main Results

Before stating the main results, we introduce some necessary notations. The
Airy
1
point process
introduced in
[
68
]
, is a fundamental object in random matrix theory, particularly associated with the GOE matrix. It is a point process
\{A_{1}\geqslant A_{2}\geqslant A_{3}\cdots\}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, describing the limiting distribution of eigenvalues near the spectral edge of a GOE matrix as the matrix size tends to infinity. The fluctuation of the rightmost particle is governed by the Tracy-Widom
1
distribution. More generally, for any
\beta>0
, Airy
β
point process, introduced in
[
58
]
, describes the edge scaling limit of the Gaussian
\beta
-ensemble, and is characterized as the eigenvalues of the stochastic Airy operator.

For any
d\geqslant 3
, let
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
denote the infinite
d
-regular tree, and
o
as a distinguished vertex, referred to as the root. The vertex set of
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
is denoted by
{\mathbb{X}}_{d}
. An
eigenvector process
with eigenvalue
\lambda
is a joint distribution
\{\psi_{\lambda}(v)\}_{v\in{\mathbb{X}}_{d}}
of real valued random variables, each with variance
1
such that the following eigenvector equation holds almost surely

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{X}}_{d}:v\sim o}\psi_{\lambda}(v)=\lambda\psi_{\lambda}(o),

where the summation is over all neighbors
v
of
o
. Furthermore, the process is invariant under any automorphism of the
d
-regular tree.

A
Gaussian wave

is an eigenvector process whose joint distribution is Gaussian. It is established in
[
23
]
that for every

-d\leqslant\lambda\leqslant d
there exists a unique Gaussian wave
\psi_{\lambda}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
. Let
L
be the adjacency operator of the infinite
d
-regular tree
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
, and define the resolvent operator
(L-z)^{-1}
for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
. For any two vertices
i,j\in{\mathbb{X}}_{d}
, the covariance structure of the Gaussian wave
\psi_{\lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}[\psi_{\lambda}(i)\psi_{\lambda}(j)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[(L-(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon))^{-1}_{ij}]}{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[(L-(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon))^{-1}_{oo}]},

which is explicitly given as

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}[\psi_{\lambda}(i)\psi_{\lambda}(j)]=\frac{1}{(d-1)^{r/2}}\left(\frac{d-1}{d}U_{r}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\sqrt{d-1}}\right)-\frac{1}{d}U_{r-2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\sqrt{d-1}}\right)\right),

where
r=\dist_{{\mathcal{X}}_{d}}(i,j)
and
U_{r}(\theta)=\sin((r+1)\cos^{-1}(\theta))/\sin(\cos^{-1}(\theta))
are the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.

For the special case of edge eigenvectors, corresponding to
\lambda=2\sqrt{d-1}
. We denote the Gaussian wave by
\Psi=\psi_{2\sqrt{d-1}}
. The covariance structure simplifies to

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}[\Psi(i)\Psi(j)]=\frac{1}{(d-1)^{r/2}}\left(\frac{d-1}{d}U_{r}(1)-\frac{1}{d}U_{r-2}(1)\right)=\frac{1}{(d-1)^{r/2}}\left(1+\frac{(d-2)r}{d}\right),

(1.1)

where the final expression follows from evaluating
U_{r}(1)=r+1
and
U_{r-2}(1)=r-1
. In the sequel, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we abbreviate
[\![{k}]\!]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{1,2,...,k\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d\geqslant 3
, and set
{\mathcal{A}}=d(d-1)/(d-2)^{2}
. Fix
k\geqslant 1
and radius
r\geqslant 0
. Let
{A}
be the adjacency matrix of random
d
-regular graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
on
N
vertices, and set
H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={A}/\sqrt{d-1}
. Let
\lambda_{1}={d}/{\sqrt{d-1}}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{N}
denote the eigenvalues, and let
{\bm{u}}_{1}=\bm{1}/\sqrt{N},{\bm{u}}_{2},\cdots,{\bm{u}}_{N}
be the associated normalized eigenvectors of
H
. Fix any vertex
o\in[\![{N}]\!]
. The rescaled extreme eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors
\{\pm{\bm{u}}_{s}\}_{2\leqslant s\leqslant k+1}
(each multiplied by a random sign) when restricted to the radius-
r
ball
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{G}})
, jointly converge to the Airy
1
point process and independent Gaussian waves:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{2}-2),({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{3}-2),\cdots,({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{k+1}-2))\Rightarrow(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},\cdots,A_{k}),\\
&(\pm\sqrt{N}{\bm{u}}_{2}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{G}})},\pm\sqrt{N}{\bm{u}}_{3}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{G}})},\cdots,\pm\sqrt{N}{\bm{u}}_{k+1}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{G}})})\Rightarrow(Z_{1}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{X}}_{d})},Z_{2}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{X}}_{d})}\cdots,Z_{k}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{X}}_{d})}),\end{split}

where
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots
are the points of the Airy
1
point process, and
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots,Z_{k}
are independent copies of the Gaussian wave
\Psi
from (
1.1
).
An analogous result holds for the smallest eigenvalues and their associated eigenvectors.

Remark 1.2
.

The joint convergence of the extreme eigenvalues to the Airy
1
point process follows from the edge universality result in
[
42
]
. The new contributions in
Theorem

1.1
are twofold: firstly the rescaled edge eigenvectors converge to independent Gaussian waves; secondly the extreme eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenvectors are asymptotically independent.

Remark 1.3
.

Eigenvectors are not unique and are defined only up to a sign. That is why we multiply each eigenvector by a random sign in
Theorem

1.1
. Another way to express the convergence to Gaussian waves is through the quadratic expressions
{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)
for
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
where
\mathbb{S}
is the vertex set of
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}(o;{\mathcal{G}})
. Specifically,

\displaystyle(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s+1}-2),\{N{\bm{u}}_{s+1}(i){\bm{u}}_{s+1}(j)\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}})_{1\leqslant s\leqslant k}\Rightarrow(A_{s},\{Z_{s}(i)Z_{s}(j)\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}})_{1\leqslant s\leqslant k},

where
A_{1}\geqslant A_{2}\geqslant A_{3}\geqslant\cdots
is the Airy
1
point process, and

)).

1.2
Topological statements

Let
\lambda_{1}={d}/{\sqrt{d-1}}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{N}
denote the eigenvalues, and
{\bm{u}}_{1}=\bm{1}/\sqrt{N},{\bm{u}}_{2},\cdots,{\bm{u}}_{N}
the associated normalized eigenvectors of the normalized adjacency matrix
H
of random
d
-regular graphs
{\mathcal{G}}
on
N
vertices.
The rescaled eigenvalues, given by
(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{1}-2),({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{2}-2),({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{3}-2),\cdots)
can be encoded by as a random measure (or random point process) on
{\mathbb{R}}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{N}\delta_{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)}.

We define the Green’s function and the Stieltjes transform of the empirical eigenvalue distribution respectively as

\displaystyle G(z)=(H-z)^{-1},\quad m_{N}(z)=\frac{1}{N}\Tr[(H-z)^{-1}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{s}-z},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}.

The imaginary parts of
G(z)
and
m_{N}(z)
are harmonic functions on the upper half-plane. By zooming in around the spectral edge at
2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{N^{1/3}}{{\mathcal{A}}^{2/3}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left[G_{ij}\left(2+\frac{w}{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)}{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-w},

\displaystyle\frac{N^{1/3}}{{\mathcal{A}}^{2/3}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left[m_{N}\left(2+\frac{w}{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{1}{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-w}.

These expressions are Poisson kernel integrals with respect to the measures
\sum_{s=1}^{N}N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)\delta_{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)}
and
\sum_{s=1}^{N}\delta_{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)}
.

In the remainder of this section, we review the topologies of locally finite measures and harmonic functions on the upper half-plane.

Definition 1.4
.

For any locally finite measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that they
converge in the vague topology
to another locally finite
\mu
, if
\mu_{n}(f)\to\mu(f)
, for any
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is compactly supported and smooth.

The imaginary part of Green’s functions and Stieltjes transforms are harmonic functions on the upper half-plane. We denote the set of harmonic functions on the upper half-plane as
\Har(\mathbb{C}^{+})
, equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets. This is a separable complete metric space, as every Cauchy sequence in this space has a limit given by a harmonic function.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a locally equibounded sequence of harmonic functions in
\Har(\mathbb{C}^{+})
, then there exists a subsequence of
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
that converges locally uniformly. Thus, the compact sets of
\Har(\mathbb{C}^{+})
are given by:

\{f\in\Har(\mathbb{C}^{+}):\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\leqslant M\},

for any compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}^{+}
and any large constant
M>0
.

Under mild conditions, the Poisson integral of a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
gives a harmonic function in
\Har(\mathbb{C}^{+})
. The following lemma states that the convergence of harmonic functions in the sense of uniform convergence on compact subsets implies the vague convergence of the corresponding measures. We postpone its proof to
Appendix

B
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\mu
and
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of
\sigma
finite Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{n\geqslant 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\rm d}|\mu_{n}|(x)}{1+x^{2}}<\infty,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\rm d}|\mu|(x)}{1+x^{2}}<\infty.

The following Poisson kernel integrals are well defined,

\displaystyle u_{n}(x+\mathrm{i}y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y{\rm d}\mu_{n}(s)}{(x-s)^{2}+y^{2}},\quad u(x+\mathrm{i}y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y{\rm d}\mu(s)}{(x-s)^{2}+y^{2}}.

If
u_{n}
converges to
u
in the sense of uniform convergence on compact subsets, then
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu
in vague topology.

Throughout the remainder of this paper, the weak convergence of point processes is understood to mean convergence with respect to the vague topology, as defined in
Definition

1.4
. Similarly, the weak convergence of harmonic functions on the upper half-plane refers to convergence with respect to the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets.

1.3
Proof ideas

In this section, we present our new framework, which establishes the convergence of edge eigenvectors through the weak convergence of the imaginary part of the Green’s function. We illustrate this framework using Wigner matrices. In the main body of the paper, we apply the same framework to prove the convergence of edge eigenvectors of random regular graphs to the Gaussian wave.

Let
W
be an
N\times N
real symmetric Wigner matrix. Specifically, the entries
W_{ij}=W_{ji}
are real-valued, have mean zero and unit variance
\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}]=0,\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}^{2}]=1
, and possess bounded higher moments. We denote the eigenvalues and corresponding normalized eigenvectors of
H:=N^{-1/2}W
as

\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{N}
and
{\bm{u}}_{1},{\bm{u}}_{2},\cdots,{\bm{u}}_{N}
respectively. Fix any finite index set
\mathbb{S}\subset[\![{N}]\!]
. It was shown in
[
48
]
that as
N
goes to infinity

\displaystyle(N^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2),\{N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}})_{1\leqslant s\leqslant k}\Rightarrow(A_{s},\{Z_{s}(i)Z_{s}(j)\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}})_{1\leqslant s\leqslant k},

(1.2)

where
A_{1}\geqslant A_{2}\geqslant A_{3}\geqslant\cdots
is the Airy
1
point process, and
\{Z_{s}(i)\}_{1\leqslant s\leqslant k,i\in\mathbb{S}}
are independent standard Gaussian variables
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
.
In what follows, we present a new proof of this result by leveraging the weak convergence of the imaginary part of the Green’s function.

We denote the Green’s function of
H
as
G(z)=(H-z)^{-1}
.
The claim (
1.2
) follows from the following statement about the joint convergence of extreme eigenvalues, and the imaginary part of Green’s functions at the edge.
By the edge universality of Wigner matrices
[
32
,
61
,
65
,
48
,
51
]
, the normalized extreme eigenvalues converge in weak topology to the Airy
1
point process

\displaystyle(N^{2/3}(\lambda_{1}-2),N^{2/3}(\lambda_{2}-2),\cdots,N^{2/3}(\lambda_{k}-2))\Rightarrow(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},\cdots,A_{k}).

(1.3)

For any
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
, near the spectral edge
2
, the imaginary part of the rescaled Green’s functions
N^{1/3}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{ij}(2+wN^{-2/3})
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle N^{1/3}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{ij}(2+wN^{-2/3})=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)}{N^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-w}=\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)}{(N^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[w])^{2}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]^{2}}.

It is the Possion kernel integral with the measure

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{N}N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)\delta_{N^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)},

and harmonic for
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
. The claim (
1.2
) follows from
Lemma

1.5
, and the following weak convergence of the imaginary part of the rescaled Green’s functions at the edge

\displaystyle\{N^{1/3}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{ij}(2+wN^{-2/3})]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\Rightarrow\{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}},

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)]:=\sum_{s\geqslant 1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]Z_{s}(i)Z_{s}(j)}{(A_{s}-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[w])^{2}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]^{2}},\quad i,j\in\mathbb{S},

and
\{Z_{s}(i)\}_{i\in\mathbb{S},s\geqslant 1}
are independent Gaussian random variables.

In the following, we give a sketch for the proof of (
1.4
). We recall the semicircle distribution
\varrho_{\rm sc}(x)
and its Stieltjes transform
m_{\rm sc}(z)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varrho_{\rm sc}(x)=\bm{1}_{x\in[-2,2]}\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi},\quad m_{\rm sc}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varrho_{\rm sc}(x){\rm d}x}{x-z}=\frac{-z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4}}{2}.\end{split}

(1.5)

Let
\widetilde{H}
be another
N\times N
Wigner matrix, obtained by resampling the rows and columns of
H
indexed by
\mathbb{S}
. Specifically, the rows and columns indexed by
\mathbb{S}
are replaced with
\{\widetilde{H}_{ix}\}_{i\in\mathbb{S},x\in[\![{N}]\!]}
, which is an independent copy of
\{H_{ix}\}_{i\in\mathbb{S},x\in[\![{N}]\!]}
. We denote the Green’s function of
\widetilde{H}
by
\widetilde{G}(z)
. By construction
H
and
\widetilde{H}
have the same law, and consequently,
G(z)
and
\widetilde{G}(z)
also share the same law.
Let
H^{(\mathbb{S})}
and
\widetilde{H}^{(\mathbb{S})}
denote the matrices obtained from

and
\widetilde{H}
by removing the rows and columns indexed by
\mathbb{S}
. By definition,
H^{(\mathbb{S})}=\widetilde{H}^{(\mathbb{S})}
, and their Green’s functions satisfy

.

Abbreviate
\mathbb{S}^{\complement}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=[\![{N}]\!]\backslash\mathbb{S}
. Let

, we can study the edge scaling limit of

using the Schur complement formula (

)

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}|_{\mathbb{S}}(z)=\frac{1}{\widetilde{H}|_{\mathbb{S}}-z-\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{S}\mathbb{S}^{\complement}}G^{(\mathbb{S})}(z)\widetilde{H}_{\mathbb{S}^{\complement}\mathbb{S}}}=\frac{1}{-2-m_{\rm sc}(2)-{\mathcal{D}}(z)}=\frac{1}{1/m_{\rm sc}(2)-{\mathcal{D}}(z)},

(1.6)

where we used that
m_{\rm sc}^{2}(z)+zm_{\rm sc}(z)+1=0
and for

,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}(z)=(z-2)\delta_{ij}-\widetilde{H}_{ij}+\sum_{x,y\not\in\mathbb{S}}\widetilde{H}_{ix}G^{(\mathbb{S})}_{xy}(z)\widetilde{H}_{yj}-m_{\rm sc}(2)\delta_{ij}.

(1.7)

To establish the weak convergence of the Green’s functions
\widetilde{G}|_{\mathbb{S}}(z)
, we rely on the following estimates, which are straightforward consequences of optimal rigidity for Wigner matrices (see
[
29
,
32
]
). For the purpose of this discussion, we assume these results as given.
For any arbitrarily small
{\mathfrak{o}}>0
and large
{\mathfrak{C}}>0
, the following bounds hold with probability at least

provided

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}(z)|\lesssim N^{-1/3+{\mathfrak{o}}},\text{ for }i,j\in\mathbb{S},\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{xx}(z)]\lesssim N^{-1/3+{\mathfrak{o}}},\text{ for }x\in[\![{N}]\!].

(1.8)

For the entry-wise imaginary part of a matrix, we have the following identity

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[A^{-1}]=-A^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[A]\overline{A^{-1}},

(1.9)

where for any matrix
B
,
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[B]
denotes the entry-wise imaginary part of
B
, and
\overline{B}
denotes the entry-wise complex conjugate of
B
. Thus, by taking imaginary part of both sides of (
1.6
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[\widetilde{G}|_{\mathbb{S}}(z)]&=-(1/m_{\rm sc}(2)-{\mathcal{D}}(z))^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[1/m_{\rm sc}(2)-{\mathcal{D}}(z)]\overline{(1/m_{\rm sc}(2)-{\mathcal{D}}(z))^{-1}}\\

2.1
Kesten-Mckay Distribution

By local weak convergence, the empirical eigenvalue density of random
d
-regular graphs converges to that of the infinite
d
-regular tree, which is known as the Kesten-McKay distribution; see
[
47
,
54
]
. This density is given by

\displaystyle\varrho_{d}(x):=\mathbf{1}_{x\in[-2,2]}\left(1+\frac{1}{d-1}-\frac{x^{2}}{d}\right)^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}.

(2.2)

We denote by

the Stieltjes transform of the Kesten–McKay distribution

,

\displaystyle m_{d}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varrho_{d}(x){\rm d}x}{x-z},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}:=\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\vcentcolon\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]>0\}.

Explicitly, the Stieltjes transform of the Kesten–McKay distribution
m_{d}(z)
can be expressed in terms of the Stieltjes transform
m_{\rm sc}(z)
:

\displaystyle m_{d}(z)=\frac{1}{-z-\frac{d}{d-1}m_{\rm sc}(z)},\quad m_{d}(z)=(d-1)\frac{-(d-2)z+d\sqrt{z^{2}-4}}{2(d^{2}-(d-1)z^{2})}.

(2.3)

Note that close to the spectral edge
2
, the Stieltjes transforms
m_{\rm sc}(z)
and
m_{d}(z)
have square root behavior: let
{\mathcal{A}}={d(d-1)}/{(d-2)^{2}}
as

,

\displaystyle m_{\rm sc}(z)=-1+\sqrt{z-2}+\OO(|z-2|),\quad m_{d}(z)=-\frac{d-1}{d-2}+{\mathcal{A}}\sqrt{z-2}+\OO(|z-2|).

(2.4)

Let
\eta=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[z]
and
\kappa=||\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[z]|-2|
, then the imaginary part of
m_{\rm sc}(z)
and
m_{d}(z)
satisfies (see
[
29
, Lemma 6.2]
),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{\rm sc}(z)]\asymp\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[m_{d}(z)]\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{\kappa+\eta}&\text{ if }|\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[z]|\leqslant 2,\\
\eta/\sqrt{\kappa+\eta}&\text{ if }|\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[z]|\geqslant 2.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=G_{cc^{\prime}}-\frac{G_{cb}G_{bc^{\prime}}}{G_{bb}}-\frac{G_{cb^{\prime}}G_{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}}{G_{b^{\prime}b^{\prime}}}+\frac{G_{cb}G_{bb^{\prime}}G_{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}}{G_{bb}G_{b^{\prime}b^{\prime}}}.

This together with (

Proposition 3.4

The Airy

Under the notation and assumptions of

Proposition 3.5

Adopt notation and assumptions in

1.1

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{2}-2),({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{3}-2),\cdots,({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{k+1}-2))\Rightarrow(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},\cdots,A_{k}).\end{split}

Fix any vertex

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{A}}^{-2/3}N^{1/3}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{ij}(2+w({\mathcal{A}}N)^{-2/3})]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\Rightarrow\{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}},

(3.33)

where

(

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Using the Skorokhod representation theorem, we can take a subsequence,
N_{1}<N_{2}<N_{3}\cdots
, such that along this subsequence, almost surely,
Y_{N}
converges to a random variable
Y
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots
converges to the Airy
1
point process, and

\{{\mathcal{A}}^{-2/3}N^{1/3}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{ij}(2+w({\mathcal{A}}N)^{-2/3})]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S},w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}}
converges (uniformly on compact sets) to the random process
\{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)]\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{S},w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}}
.

For any
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
, near the spectral edge
2
, the imaginary part of the rescaled Green’s functions
{\mathcal{A}}^{-2/3}N^{1/3}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{ij}(2+w({\mathcal{A}}N)^{-2/3})]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{-2/3}N^{1/3}(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[G_{ij}(2+w({\mathcal{A}}N)^{-2/3})]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)}{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)}{(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[w])^{2}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]^{2}}.

It is the Possion kernel integral with the measure

\displaystyle\mu_{N}:=\sum_{s=1}^{N}N{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)\delta_{({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2)},

and harmonic for
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
. And similarly
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[{\mathbf{G}}_{ij}(w)]=\sum_{s\geqslant 1}\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]Z_{s}(i)Z_{s}(j)}{(A_{s}-\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[w])^{2}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[w]^{2}},\quad i,j\in\mathbb{S},

Since

are standard Gaussian random variables (for different

Next we check the assumptions in

1.5
along the sequence

. Along this sequence, we can assume that there exists a large

such that

for each

. Then by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\rm d}|\mu_{N}|(x)}{1+x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{N|{\bm{u}}_{s}(i){\bm{u}}_{s}(j)|}{1+(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2))^{2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\frac{N({\bm{u}}^{2}_{s}(i)+{\bm{u}}^{2}_{s}(j))}{1+(({\mathcal{A}}N)^{2/3}(\lambda_{s}-2))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant N^{1/3}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{ii}(2+(2M+\mathrm{i})N^{-2/3})+N^{1/3}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{jj}(2+(2M+\mathrm{i})N^{-2/3}),

which are uniformly bounded. By the same argument,

is also bounded. We conclude from

1.5

1.1

Step 4.

Appendix A
Properties of the Green’s functions

Throughout this paper, we repeatedly use some (well-known) identities for Green’s functions,
which we collect in this appendix.

A.1
Schur complement formula

Given an
N\times N
matrix
H
and an index set
\mathbb{T}\subset[\![{N}]\!]
, recall that we denote by

H|_{\mathbb{T}}
the
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
-matrix obtained by restricting
H
to
\mathbb{T}
,
and that by
H^{(\mathbb{T})}=H|_{\mathbb{T}^{\complement}}
the matrix obtained by removing
the rows and columns corresponding to indices in
\mathbb{T}
.
Thus, for any
\mathbb{T}\subset[\![{N}]\!]
,
any symmetric matrix
H
can be written (up to rearrangement of indices) in the block form

H=\begin{bmatrix}A&B^{\top}\\
B&D\end{bmatrix},

with
A=H|_{\mathbb{T}}
and
D=H^{(\mathbb{T})}
.
The Schur complement formula asserts that, for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
,

G=(H-z)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}(A-B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}B)^{-1}&-(A-B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}B)^{-1}B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}\\
-G^{(\mathbb{T})}B(A-B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}B)^{-1}&G^{(\mathbb{T})}+G^{(\mathbb{T})}B(A-B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}B)^{-1}B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}\end{bmatrix},

(A.1)

where
G^{(\mathbb{T})}=(D-z)^{-1}
.
Throughout the paper, we often use the following special cases of (
A.1
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}G|_{\mathbb{T}}&=(A-B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})}B)^{-1},\\
G|_{\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{\complement}}&=-G|_{\mathbb{T}}B^{\top}G^{(\mathbb{T})},\\
G|_{\mathbb{T}^{\complement}}&=G^{(\mathbb{T})}+G|_{\mathbb{T}^{\complement}\mathbb{T}}(G|_{\mathbb{T}})^{-1}G|_{\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{\complement}}=G^{(\mathbb{T})}-G^{(\mathbb{T})}BG|_{\mathbb{T}\mathbb{T}^{\complement}},\end{split}

(A.2)

as well as the special case

G_{ij}^{(k)}=G_{ij}-\frac{G_{ik}G_{kj}}{G_{kk}}=G_{ij}+(G^{(k)}H)_{ik}G_{kj}.

(A.3)

A.2
Ward identity

For any symmetric
N\times N
matrix
H
, its Green’s function
G(z)=(H-z)^{-1}
satisfies
the Ward identity

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|G_{ij}(z)|^{2}=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G_{jj}(z)}{\eta},

(A.4)

where
\eta=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[z]
. This identity provides a bound for the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{N}|G_{ij}(z)|^{2}

in terms of the diagonal entries of the Green’s function.

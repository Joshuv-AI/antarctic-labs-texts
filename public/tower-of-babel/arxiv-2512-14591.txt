Proper solutions of the 1 / H 1/H -flow and the Green kernel of the p p -Laplacian
Luca Benatti, Luciano Mari, Marco Rigoli, Alberto G. Setti, Kai Xu

Abstract
We show existence and optimal growth estimate for the weak inverse mean curvature flow issuing from a point, on manifolds with certain curvature and isoperimetric conditions. Some of the results are obtained by proving new decay estimates for the Green kernel of the p p -Laplacian which fix a gap in the literature. Additionally, we address the convergence of p p -capacitary potentials to the inverse mean curvature flow with outer obstacle.

Introduction

1.1
Main theorems

The purpose of this paper is to produce weak solutions to the Inverse Mean Curvature Flow (IMCF) with sharp growth estimates, on complete manifolds whose geometry at infinity is mildly controlled.

Henceforth, all manifolds
M
are assumed to be smooth, complete, connected, noncompact and of dimension
n\geq 2
. We fix a basepoint
o\in M
, and denote
r(x)=d(x,o)
. We adopt the following notations: for a function
u
we set
E_{t}(u)=\{u<t\}
, for short
E_{t}
when there is no ambiguity. We denote by
{P(E;K)}
the perimeter of a set
E
in
K
, and write
{P(E)}={P(E;M)}
.

According to the weak formulation of the IMCF developed by G. Huisken and T. Ilmanen
[
7
]
, we say that
u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a weak IMCF in a domain
\Omega
if for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
K\Subset\Omega
we have

J_{u}^{K}(E_{t})\leq J_{u}^{K}(F),\qquad\forall\,F\text{ such that }E\Delta F\Subset K,

where the energy
J_{u}^{K}
is defined as

J_{u}^{K}(E):={P(E;K)}-\int_{E\cap K}|\nabla u|.

For detailed introductions of the weak IMCF, see
[
7
]
and also
[
21
, Section 2]
. An important role in our paper will be played by inverse mean curvature flows issuing from a given point
o\in M
, that is, an IMCF
u
defined on
M\backslash\{o\}
, such that

u(x)-(n-1)\log r(x)\to 0\qquad\text{as}\ \ x\to o.

Following Huisken, we refer to them as
IMCF cores
. An IMCF core
u
is called
proper
if
\{u<t\}\Subset M
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By the maximum principle in
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists at most one proper IMCF core with pole
o
.

The following are the main theorems of this work:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
M
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
and a reverse volume doubling condition

\frac{|B_{t}(o)|}{|B_{s}(o)|}\geq C_{\mathscr{R}}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{b},\qquad\forall\,t\geq s>0.

(
\text{RD}_{b}
)

for some
b\in(1,n]
and
C_{\mathscr{R}}>0
. Then there exists a unique proper IMCF core
u
on
M
with pole
o
, satisfying the growth estimate

\displaystyle u(x)\geq(n-1)\log r(x)-C\big(n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}},|B_{1}(o)|\big),\qquad\forall\,x\in B_{1}(o),

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(x)\geq(b-1)\log r(x)-C\big(n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}},|B_{1}(o)|\big),\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash B_{1}(o),

(1.2)

and the global gradient estimate

|\nabla u(x)|\leq(n-1)e^{-\frac{u}{n-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\}.

(1.3)

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality

{P(E)}\geq c_{I}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\qquad\forall\ E\Subset M

(1.4)

holds in
M
for some constant
c_{I}>0
.
Then there exists a unique proper IMCF core
u
on
M
with pole
o
, satisfying the growth estimate

u(x)\geq(n-1)\log r(x)-C(n,c_{I}),\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
also holds if the Ricci lower bound is replaced by a global volume doubling and weak
(1,1)
-Neumann Poincaré inequality (with the appropriate dependencies on the constant
C
). See Section
3
for more details.

As was observed in
[
10
]
, IMCF cores are useful in solving the usual initial value problem of IMCF. Let
E_{0}\Subset M
be a
C^{1,1}
domain. We say that

•

u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
is a
weak IMCF in
M
with initial condition
E_{0}
, if
E_{0}=\{u<0\}
and
u
is a weak solution of the IMCF in
M\backslash\overline{E_{0}}
;

•

such a
u
is called
proper
, if
\{u<t\}\Subset M
for all
t\geq 0
.

Combining Theorem
1.1
or
1.2
and the standard maximum principle, we have:

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
or
1.2
hold. Let
E_{0}\Subset M
be a
C^{1,1}
domain. Then there exists a unique proper IMCF
u
in
M
with initial condition
E_{0}
. Moreover:

(i)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, we have

u(x)\geq(b-1)\log d(x,x_{0})-C,\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash E_{0}.

(1.6)

(ii)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
, we have

u(x)\geq(n-1)\log d(x,x_{0})-C,\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash E_{0},

(1.7)

Here,
x_{0}
is any point in
E_{0}
and
C<\infty
is a constant independent of
x
.

Proof.

Let
v
be the proper IMCF core with pole
x_{0}
, as given by Theorem
1.1
or
1.2
. By Huisken-Ilmanen’s existence theorem
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
and the maximum principle
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
, there is a weak IMCF
u
on
M
with initial value
E_{0}
, such that
u\geq v-\sup_{E_{0}}(v)
on
M\backslash E_{0}
. The corollary then follows from (
1.1
) or (
1.5
).
∎

We now place the two results above into context. An efficient way to produce IMCF is by approximation via renormalized
p
-harmonic functions, as first discovered by R. Moser
[
12
]
. For
1<p<\infty
, a function
v\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is called
p
-harmonic
if

\Delta_{p}v:=\operatorname{div}\big(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\big)=0

weakly. In this case,
w:=(1-p)\log v
is then called a
p
-IMCF
. Note that
w
solves

\Delta_{p}w=|\nabla w|^{p}\qquad\text{weakly.}

One of the main insights of R. Moser’s discovery in
[
12
]
is the following: if
\{v_{p}\}_{p>1}
is a family of positive
p
-harmonic functions in a domain
\Omega
, such that

\lim_{p\to 1}(1-p)\log v_{p}=u\quad\text{in}\quad C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for some
u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, then
u
is a weak IMCF in
\Omega
. This idea was exploited by Moser himself
[
12
]
and later by B. Kotschwar and L. Ni
[
9
]
and L. Mari, M. Rigoli, and A. Setti
[
10
]
in order to solve the initial value problem of the weak IMCF. The general strategy is as follows. Assume that
M
is
p
-nonparabolic for each
p>1
close enough to 1. Given
E_{0}
, we consider the minimal positive solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{p}v_{p}=0\qquad\text{in}\ \ M\backslash E_{0},\\
&v_{p}=1\qquad\text{on}\ \ \partial E_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
M
is
p
-nonparabolic, it follows that
0<v_{p}<1
on
M\backslash\overline{E_{0}}
. Then we perform the transformation

u_{p}:=(1-p)\log v_{p}

(1.8)

(so that
u_{p}=0
on
\partial E_{0}
, and
u_{p}>0
on
M\backslash\overline{E_{0}}
), and take the limit

u=\lim_{p\to 1}u_{p},

(1.9)

and finally argue that
u
is a proper weak IMCF with initial condition
E_{0}
. Three main steps are involved in this program:

(1)

One needs a gradient estimate that is stable as
p\to 1
, so that the limit in (
1.9
) exists. For this step, see
[
9
, Theorems 1.1 and 3.1]
,
[
10
, Lemma 2.17]
and
[
17
]
.

(2)

Then by Moser’s observation, the limit
u
in (
1.9
) is a weak IMCF with initial value
E_{0}
.

(3)

One needs a decay estimate for
v_{p}
that is stable as
p\to 1
, so that
u
is proper.

The last step is particularly subtle. This requires certain geometric conditions on
M
at infinity, which often take the form of uniform Sobolev inequalities or volume growth and curvature bounds.

Since we are also interested in approximating IMCF cores, we consider the following objects:

•

The
p
-Green’s kernel with pole
o
, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{p}
, is the unique minimal positive solution of

\Delta_{p}\mathcal{G}_{p}=-\delta_{o}

on
M
. For
p<n
, we have that
\mathcal{G}_{p}
is asymptotic to

\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\Big(\frac{p-1}{n-p}\Big)\,r(x)^{\frac{n-p}{1-p}}

near
o
, where
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
; see Appendix
A
. Note that the existence of
\mathcal{G}_{p}
requires
M
to be
p
-nonparabolic. When there is no risk of ambiguity, we shall often write
\mathcal{G}
. We refer to Section
2
as well as to
[
10
, Section 2]
for more background on
p
-Green’s kernels.

•

The
p
-IMCF core with pole
o
, denoted by
w_{p}
, is defined as

w_{p}:=(1-p)\log\mathcal{G}_{p}.

Notice that
w_{p}
is a
p
-IMCF in
M\backslash\{o\}
as in the above definition.

In the previous work
[
10
]
, the second to the fourth authors obtained two decay estimates for the Green’s kernel
\mathcal{G}_{p}
assuming respectively the conditions of Theorem
1.2
and
1.1
(cf. respectively (3.44) and Theorem 3.23 in
[
10
]
). The corresponding growth estimates for the IMCF core were obtained by taking these estimates as
p\to 1
, as stated in
[
10
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4]
. Then, following the proof of Corollary
1.4
above, the usual initial value problem for the IMCF is then resolved (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 1.7]
).

These decay estimates for
\mathcal{G}_{p}
essentially boil down to
[
10
, Theorem 3.6]
, which states that a weighted Sobolev inequality of the form

\Big(\int_{M}\eta(r)^{-\frac{p}{\nu-p}}|\varphi|^{\frac{\nu p}{\nu-p}}\Big)^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}\leq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\int|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\qquad\forall\varphi\in\Lip_{c}(M)

(1.10)

implies the corresponding growth estimate for the
p
-IMCF core

w_{p}(x)\geq(\nu-p)\log r(x)-C\big(\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu},p,\nu\big)-\log\eta(2r(x)),\quad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\},

(1.11)

for all
1<p<\nu
and all positive non-decreasing weight functions
\eta
.

However, a gap was recently identified in the proof of (
1.11
). As a result, one loses the uniform control of the constant
C
in (
1.11
) as
p\to 1
, and so the properness claim in step (3) above breaks down. See Subsection
3.1
for a detailed account of this issue. One of the outcomes of our main theorems is to recover the properness results in
[
10
]
. In particular, Corollary
1.4
fully recovers
[
10
, Theorem 1.7]
, Theorem
1.1
fully recovers
[
10
, Theorem 1.4 and 4.6]
, and Theorem
1.2
supersedes
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
. See Subsection
3.3
for a detailed account. We underline that no curvature lower bound is required in Theorem
1.2
(although at the cost of losing uniform gradient estimates for
u
). The validity of the decay estimate stated in
[
10
, Theorem 3.6]
itself remains open.

Regarding
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
, we note that Theorem
1.2
proves the optimal growth estimate (hence properness) for the IMCF core. However, it remains open
whether this IMCF core is the limit of
p
-IMCF cores
. Namely, the following is currently unknown:

Question 1.5
.

Suppose
M
satisfies (
1.4
), and for some sequence
p_{i}\to 1
and
w\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\backslash\{o\})
we have the convergence

w_{p_{i}}\to w\quad\text{in}\quad C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\backslash\{o\}),

where
w_{p_{i}}
is the
p_{i}
-IMCF core with pole
o
. Is
w
equal to the proper IMCF core with pole
o
(up to additive constants)?

A positive answer of Question
1.5
would allow us to fully recover
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
. It is also natural to conjecture the following uniform growth estimate, which would answer Question
1.5
affirmatively:

Question 1.6
.

Suppose
M
satisfies (
1.4
). Is it true that for all
p\in(1,2)
, we have

w_{p}(x)\geq(n-p)\log r(x)-C(n,c_{I})\ \text{?}

Note that Question
1.6
is a special case of
[
10
, Theorem 3.6]
.

1.2
General
p
-harmonic approximation of IMCF

We include here some discussions on the global and interior approximation of the weak IMCF, which may be of independent interest.

In Theorem
1.2
–
1.4
above, we see that weak IMCFs can be approximated by
p
-IMCFs, assuming certain conditions on
M
. This need not be the case, however, if no additional assumption is made (for instance,
M
could admit a proper IMCF but be
p
-parabolic for all
p>1
). A way to partially get around this issue is to try approximating a weak IMCF in bounded domains. A recent idea of L. Benatti, A. Pluda and M. Pozzetta
[
3
]
led to the following approximation theorem:

Theorem 1.7

(
[
3
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Suppose that
M
is complete, noncompact, and that there exists a proper IMCF
u
on
M
with initial value
E_{0}\Subset M
. Then for all domain
D
with
E_{0}\Subset D\Subset M
, there is a family of
p
-IMCFs
u_{p}
on
\overline{D}\backslash E_{0}
, such that

•

u_{p}\geq 0
and
u_{p}|_{\partial E_{0}}=0
,

•

\lim_{p\to 1}u_{p}=u
in
C^{0}(\overline{D\backslash E_{0}})
.

To summarize: for any
D
, one can find a (
D
-dependent) sequence of
p
-IMCFs that converges to
u
(at a rate that may not be uniform when
D
varies). This result allows one to transfer analytic properties of
p
-harmonic functions to the limit, see
[
3
]
for specific applications. It is also powerful enough to lead to Theorem
1.10
below.

However, it is crucial that
u
be proper here, so this is essentially a global approximation theorem. Its interior counterpart remains largely open: for instance, one can ask

Question 1.8
.

Does the result of Theorem
1.7
hold if
u
is not proper?

One can even drop the initial condition and ask the following:

Question 1.9
.

Suppose
u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a weak IMCF in a domain
\Omega
. Given a sub-domain
D\Subset\Omega
, can we find a family
\{u_{p}\}_{p>1}
of
p
-IMCFs, so that

u_{p}\to u\qquad\text{in}\ \ C^{0}(\overline{D})\,?

As an application, this would imply the following gradient estimate: if
u
is a weak IMCF in a ball
B(x,R)\Subset M
, and we have
\sec\geq-K
in
B(x,R)
, then it holds
|\nabla u(x)|\leq C(n,KR^{2})R^{-1}
(by Kotschwar-Ni
[
9
]
). We remark that although weak IMCFs are defined to be locally Lipschitz, no a priori gradient estimates have been obtained in general (we only have gradient estimates for those solutions that are limits of
p
-IMCFs or limits of solutions of the elliptic regularized equations).

It is natural to attack Question
1.9
by considering the following Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{p}u_{p}=|\nabla u_{p}|^{p}\qquad\text{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
&u_{p}=u\qquad\text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

and hope that
u_{p}\to u
as
p\to 1
. However,
u_{p}
could converge to an IMCF that is different from
u
. To our knowledge, there is currently no result guaranteeing that the Dirichlet condition is preserved as
p\to 1
.

The limiting behavior of Dirichlet conditions is an interesting question in its own right. Consider the case of
p
-capacitors: suppose
\Omega\Subset M
is a smooth domain, and
E_{0}\Subset\Omega
is a
C^{1,1}
domain. Let
v_{p}
be the unique solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{p}v_{p}=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega\backslash\overline{E_{0}},\\
&v_{p}=1\qquad\text{on }\partial E_{0},\\
&v_{p}=0\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

make Moser’s transformation
u_{p}=(1-p)\log v_{p}
, and take the limit

u=\lim_{p\to 1}u_{p}\qquad\text{in}\ \ C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\backslash E_{0}).

(1.13)

It follows from standard theory that
u
is a weak IMCF in
\Omega
with initial value
E_{0}
. the remaining question is what boundary condition is satisfied on
\partial\Omega
. Our next result states that
u
satisfies the outer obstacle condition which was developed by the fifth author in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

In the above setup,
u
coincides with the solution of IMCF in
\Omega
with initial value
E_{0}
and outer obstacle
\partial\Omega
, obtained in
[
22
, Theorem 1.6]
.

The outer obstacle condition, morally (but less precisely) speaking, states that each level set of
u
contacts tangentially with
\partial\Omega
. The convergence (
1.13
) is subtle: each
u_{p}
(
p>1
) diverges to
+\infty
at
\partial\Omega
, but the limit solution
u
is bounded on
\Omega
. All but boundedly many level sets of
u_{p}
disappear in the limiting process (due to Theorem
1.10
), and the boundary tangency is preserved through the limit.

Our current proof of Theorem
1.10
combines Theorem
1.7
and the soft obstacle approximation argument in
[
22
]
. More in detail, one sees that this proof is mostly IMCF-theoretic. Specifically, we comment that:

•

The main nonlinear potential-theoretic fact that we used is the following: on manifolds with conic ends, proper IMCFs are globally approximated by
p
-IMCFs (this underlies the original proof of Theorem
1.7
);

•

The Dirichlet condition
v_{p}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
is used only in the following place:
v_{p}
is the smallest
p
-harmonic function in
\Omega\backslash E_{0}
with
v_{p}|_{\partial E_{0}}=1
.

It would be interesting to find a potential-theoretic proof of Theorem
1.10
. For this goal, perhaps one has to study the fine properties of the vector field
|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{p}
near
\partial\Omega
. Such an argument, if found, may ideally imply that
|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{p}
converges to a boundary-orthogonal calibration of
u
(see
[
22
, Section 3.4]
for the latter notion).

Preliminaries

For the preliminary material in this section, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
and references therein. For
p\in(1,n]
, the Green kernel
\mathcal{G}
of
\Delta_{p}
on a smooth, relatively compact domain
\Omega\Subset M
with pole at
o\in\Omega
and Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
is the minimal positive solution to

\Delta_{p}\mathcal{G}=-\delta_{o}.

(2.1)

The construction of
\mathcal{G}
can be found in
[
6
, Thm. 3.19]
. There, the author also proves the following properties:

\int_{\{\mathcal{G}\in[s,t]\}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{p}=t-s\qquad\forall\,0<s<t

(2.2)

and

\mathrm{cap}_{p}\big(\{\mathcal{G}\geq\ell\},\Omega\big)=\ell^{-p}\int_{\{\mathcal{G}\leq\ell\}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{p}=\ell^{1-p},

(2.3)

where the
p
-capacity
\mathrm{cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)
for
K
compact and
\Omega
open is defined by

\mathrm{cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{p}\ :\ \psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\Omega),\ \psi\geq 1\ \text{\,on\,}\,K\right\}.

The minimality of the kernel constructed in
[
6
]
was later proved in
[
10
]
as a consequence of a comparison theorem for Green kernels.

If
\Omega
has non-compact closure or nonsmooth boundary (including
\Omega=M
), a hernel
\mathcal{G}
with pole at
o
was constructed in
[
6
]
by means of an increasing exhaustion
\{\Omega_{j}\}
of smooth domains of
\Omega
and related Green kernels
\mathcal{G}_{j}
. The existence of a locally finite limit for the family
\{\mathcal{G}_{j}\}
is not automatic, and it is equivalent to the fact that
\Delta_{p}
is non-parabolic on
\Omega
, namely, that
\mathrm{cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)>0
for some (equivalently all)
K
with non-empty interior.

The behavior of
\mathcal{G}
in a neighbourhood of
o
was described by J. Serrin in
[
16
]
, and later refined by Kichenassamy and Veron in
[
8
]
and
[
20
, pp. 243-251]
. Their proof carries over to manifolds, as stated in
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
, leading to the following theorem. Setting

\displaystyle\mu(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{p-1}{n-p}\right)r^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}}&\qquad\text{if }\,p<n\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}(-\log r)&\qquad\text{if }\,p=n,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

it holds

Theorem 2.1
.

For
p\leq n
, let
\mathcal{G}
be a Green kernel for
\Delta_{p}
on an open set
\Omega\subset M^{n}
containing
o
. Then,
\mathcal{G}
is smooth in a punctured neighbourhood of
o
and, as
x\rightarrow o
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}(1)&\quad\mathcal{G}\sim\mu(r),\\[5.69046pt]
(2)&\quad|\nabla\mathcal{G}-\mu^{\prime}(r)\nabla r|=o\big(\mu^{\prime}(r)\big),\\[8.5359pt]
(3)&\quad\text{if }\,p<n,\quad\big|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}-\mu^{\prime\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}r\otimes\mathrm{d}r-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(r)}{r}\big(\langle\,,\,\rangle-\mathrm{d}r\otimes\mathrm{d}r\big)\big|=o\big(\mu^{\prime\prime}(r)\big).\end{array}

(2.5)

Since, to our knowledge, its proof only appeared in an unpublished version of
[
10
]
on arXiv, we include it in the Appendix.

2.1
Doubling,
(q,p)
-Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities

Definition 2.2
.

We say that an open subset
\Omega\subset M^{n}
satisfies:

\mathrm{(VD)}

the
doubling property
if there exists a constant
C_{\mathscr{D}}>1
such that

|B_{2r}(x)|\leq C_{\mathscr{D}}|B_{r}(x)|\qquad\text{for each }\ \ B_{2r}(x)\Subset\Omega.

(\mathrm{NP}_{q,p})

the
weak
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality
, for given
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
, if there exists a constant
\mathscr{P}_{q,p}
such that

Inequality (

yield local Sobolev inequalities with uniform constants. Since we found no precise reference, we briefly supply the argument, that was suggested to us by P. Koskela.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
M
be a complete noncompact manifold satisfying
\mathrm{(VD)}
and
(\mathrm{NP}_{1,1})
for some constants
C_{\mathscr{D}}
and
\mathscr{P}_{1,1}
. Then, there exists a constant
\hat{\mathscr{S}}(C_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{P}_{1,1})
such that, for each ball
B_{r}=B_{r}(x)
,

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\leq\hat{\mathscr{S}}r\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla\psi|\qquad\forall\,\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(B_{r}),

(2.14)

Proof.

Fix a point
y\in\partial B_{2r}
, so that
B_{r}(y)\subset B_{3r}\backslash B_{r}
. Using (
2.12
) on
B_{4r}(y)
and the triangle inequality we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}&\leq&\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\overline{\psi}_{B_{4r}(y)}|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}+4\mathscr{S}r\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\nabla\psi|\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|+4\mathscr{S}r\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\nabla\psi|.\end{array}

(2.15)

Denote by
A_{r}(y)=B_{4r}(y)\backslash B_{r}(y)
. Since
\psi=0
on
B_{r}(y)
, using
\mathrm{(VD)}
and the Hölder inequality gives

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|&=&\displaystyle\frac{|A_{r}(y)|}{|B_{4r}(y)|}\fint_{A_{r}(y)}|\psi|\leq\frac{|A_{r}(y)|}{|B_{4r}(y)|}\left(\fint_{A_{r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\left[\frac{|A_{r}(y)|}{|B_{4r}(y)|}\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\leq\displaystyle\left[\frac{C_{\mathscr{D}}^{2}-1}{C_{\mathscr{D}}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}.\end{array}

Inserting into (
2.15
), we get

\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\leq\left[1-\left(\frac{C_{\mathscr{D}}^{2}-1}{C_{\mathscr{D}}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\right]^{-1}4\mathscr{S}r\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\nabla\psi|.

Call
\mathscr{S}_{1}
the constant in the right-hand side. Concluding, since
\psi
is supported on
B_{r}
and by (
2.7
) we obtain

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\leq\left[\frac{B_{4r}(y)}{B_{r}}\right]^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\left(\fint_{B_{4r}(y)}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\leq\mathscr{S}_{1}r\left[\frac{|B_{r}|}{|B_{4r}(y)|}\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla\psi|.

Inequality (
2.14
) readily follows since

.
∎

3.1
Description of the gap in
[
10
]

Conjecture
3.1
is stated as Theorem 3.6 in
[
10
]
. The argument there is based on a Moser iteration procedure for the function
\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t}=\max_{\Omega\cap B_{t}}\mathcal{G}
, which is shown in
[
10
, (3.38)]
to satisfy

\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t}\leq\hat{C}(\theta t)^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{(1-\theta)t}^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\forall\,\theta\in(0,1),

(3.5)

where

\hat{C}=\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{p^{2}}}\overline{C}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{\nu-p}{p^{2}}}\eta\big(2t\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.6)

and
\overline{C}_{p,\nu}
is defined in (
2.20
). For a chosen sequence
t=t_{0}>t_{1}>t_{2}>\cdots
, setting
\theta=1-t_{k+1}/t_{k}
in (
3.5
) one obtains for each

\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t_{k}}\leq\hat{C}(t_{k}-t_{k+1})^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t_{k+1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Iterating from 0 to
k
, this gives

\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t}\leq\hat{C}^{1+1/p+\cdots+1/p^{k}}\cdot\Big[(t_{0}-t_{1})(t_{1}-t_{2})^{1/p}\cdots(t_{k}-t_{k+1})^{1/p^{k}}\Big]^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p}}\cdot\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t_{k+1}}^{1/p^{k+1}}.

(3.7)

In view of the asymptotics of
\mathcal{G}
near
o
(cf. Lemma
2.1
), the last term is not hard to handle. Thus, for the constant
C_{p,\nu}
in Conjecture
3.1
to be bounded as
p\to 1
, we need the choice of
\{t_{k}\}
to satisfy

\prod_{k\geq 0}(t_{k}-t_{k+1})^{1/p^{k}}\geq Ae^{-\frac{B}{p-1}}

for some constants
A,B
independent of
p
.

The gap in
[
10
]
lies in the choice of
t_{k}
. More precisely, see
[
10
, pp.822-824]
: the sequence
\{\sigma_{k}\}
constructed therein becomes negative for sufficiently large

, which is forbidden by the iteration scheme. In fact, this problem is subtle in view of the following result, which shows that
no iteration
of (
3.5
) can lead to a constant
C_{p,\nu}
which is bounded as
p\to 1
:

Proposition 3.2
.

For each
A,B,t_{0}>0
there exists a constant
p_{0}=p_{0}(A,B,t_{0})>1
such that: for all
1<p\leq p_{0}
and all sequences
\{x_{k}>0\}_{k\geq 0}
with
\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\leq t_{0}
, we have

\prod_{k\geq 0}x_{k}^{1/p^{k}}<Ae^{-\frac{B}{p-1}}.

Proof.

Increasing
t_{0}
only strengthens the statement. Thus we increase
t_{0}
so that
e^{B+2}t_{0}
is an integer. Let
I=\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:x_{k}>e^{-B-2}\big\}
, thus
\#I\leq e^{B+2}t_{0}+1
. We can estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\log x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq(\#I)\log\max(1,t_{0})-(B+2)\sum_{k\notin I}\frac{1}{p^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(e^{B+2}t_{0}+1\big)\log\max(1,t_{0})-(B+2)\sum_{k=e^{B+2}t_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{k}}

\displaystyle=\big(e^{B+2}t_{0}+1\big)\log\max(1,t_{0})-(B+2)\frac{p^{-e^{B+2}t_{0}}}{p-1}.

Now we choose
p_{0}
sufficiently close to 1, such that

(B+1)p_{0}^{-e^{B+2}t_{0}}>B\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{p_{0}^{-e^{B+2}t_{0}}}{p_{0}-1}>\big(e^{B+2}t_{0}+1\big)\log\max(1,t_{0})-\log A

With this choice, for all
1<p\leq p_{0}
we have

\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\log x_{k}<\log A+\frac{B}{p-1}.\qed

Notwithstanding the previous proposition, it is convenient to state the estimate for the Green kernel with the unbounded constant as
p\to 1^{+}
, given its potential use in applications beyond the present work.

,

\mathcal{G}(x)\leq C_{p,\nu}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\eta\big(2r(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}r(x)^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega\backslash\{o\},

(3.8)

where

C_{p,\nu}=\frac{\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{\nu}{p}}\overline{C}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{\nu-p}{p}}(ep)^{\nu-p}}{(p-1)^{\nu-p}}

and
\overline{C}_{p,\nu}
is as in (
2.20
). In particular, if

\eta(t)=o\left(t^{\nu-p}\right)\qquad\text{as }\,t\rightarrow\infty.

(3.9)

then

\mathcal{G}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
r(x)\rightarrow\infty
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Choosing
t_{k}=t/p^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{k\geq 0}(t_{k}-t_{k+1})^{1/p^{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{(p-1)t}{p^{k+1}}\right)^{1/p^{k}}=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}t\right)^{\sum_{k\geq 0}1/p^{k}}p^{-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{k}{p^{k}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}t\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}p^{-\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}}.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
3.7
), we conclude

\|\mathcal{G}\|_{t}\leq\hat{C}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}t\right)^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}}p^{\frac{(\nu-p)}{(p-1)^{2}}}\leq\hat{C}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\big(({p-1})t\big)^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}}(ep)^{\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}},

which because of the definition of
\hat{C}
in (
3.6
) gives the desired bound.
∎

3.2
New decay estimates

Our goal of this subsection is to prove the following decay estimate for the
p
-Green’s kernel:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete manifold satisfying
\mathrm{(VD)}
and
(\mathrm{NP}_{1,1})
, with doubling dimension
\nu=\log_{2}C_{\mathscr{D}}
. Assume that there exist constants
C_{\mathscr{R}}>0
,
b\in(1,\nu]
such that

\frac{|B_{t}(o)|}{|B_{s}(o)|}\geq C_{\mathscr{R}}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{b}\qquad\forall\,t\geq s>0.

(3.10)

Then, for each
p_{0}\in(1,b)
and
p\in(1,p_{0}]
, the Green kernel of
\Delta_{p}
on
M
with pole at
o\in M
satisfies

\mathcal{G}(x)\leq C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left[\sup_{t\in(0,2r(x))}\frac{t^{\nu}}{|B_{t}(o)|}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}r(x)^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\},

(3.11)

where
C
is a constant only depending on
C_{\mathscr{D}}
,
\mathscr{P}_{1,1}
,
p_{0}
,
b
and
C_{\mathscr{R}}
.

This theorem recovers the full strength of
[
10
, Theorem 3.23]
, up to the fact that the constant
C
here is not explicitly computed.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
consists of two steps. First, we use a capacity argument to bound
\min_{\partial B_{t}(o)}\mathcal{G}
from above for all radii
t
. This argument is similar to the one employed in
[
2
, Theorem 3.11]
. Then, we use a Harnack inequality and a chaining argument to estimate
\max_{\partial B_{t}(o)}\mathcal{G}
in terms of
\min_{\partial B_{t}(o)}\mathcal{G}
, thus obtaining the desired upper bound for
\mathcal{G}
.

The following theorem realizes the first step:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a connected open subset of
M
, possibly
\Omega=M
, and denote by
r
is the distance from a fixed origin
o\in\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
supports the weighted Sobolev inequality (
3.1
) for some
p\in(1,\nu)
, constant
\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}>0
and weight
\eta
satisfying (
3.2
). Then,
\Delta_{p}
is non-parabolic on
\Omega
and, letting
\mathcal{G}(x)
be the Green kernel of
\Delta_{p}
on
\Omega
with pole at
o
, for each
t>0
we have

\inf_{\partial B_{t}(o)}\mathcal{G}\leq\hat{C}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\eta\big(2t\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}t^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}},

(3.12)

where

\hat{C}_{p,\nu}\doteq\mathscr{S}^{\frac{\nu}{p}}_{p,\nu}\ 2^{\frac{(\nu+2p)(\nu-p)}{p}}

is bounded as
p\rightarrow 1
if so is
\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}
.

Proof.

By taking a smooth exhaustion
\Omega_{j}\uparrow\Omega
by connected domains, it is enough to prove the result for
\mathcal{G}
the kernel of a relatively compact, smooth open subset, still called
\Omega
. In particular,
\mathcal{G}
is smooth and vanishes on
\partial\Omega
. Write

f(t)\doteq\min_{\partial B_{t}}\mathcal{G}.

If
f(t)=0
, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, since
\Omega
is connected, we have
B_{t}\Subset\Omega
and, as a consequence of the strong maximum principle,

B_{t}\subset\big\{\mathcal{G}>f(t)\big\}.

By (
2.3
) and the monotonicity of the
p
-capacity, it follows that

\mathrm{cap}_{p}(B_{t},\Omega)\leq\mathrm{cap}_{p}\left(\{\mathcal{G}>f(t)\},\Omega\right)=f(t)^{1-p}.

Hence,

f(t)\leq\Big(\mathrm{cap}_{p}(B_{t},\Omega)\Big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Next, we observe that in view of the weighted Sobolev inequality, for each
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
with
\psi\geq 1
on
B_{t}
we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{p}\geq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\eta(r)^{-\frac{p}{\nu-p}}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu p}{\nu-p}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}\geq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{-1}\eta(t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}}|B_{t}|^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}.

Here, in the last inequality we restricted the integral to
B_{t}
and used the monotonicity of
\eta
. By taking the infimum over
\psi
,

\mathrm{cap}_{p}(B_{t},\Omega)\geq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{-1}\eta(t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}}|B_{t}|^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}.

Thus,

f(t)\leq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\eta(t)^{\frac{p}{\nu(p-1)}}|B_{t}|^{-\frac{\nu-p}{\nu(p-1)}}

Next, since for each
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\overline{B}_{t})
vanishing on
\partial B_{t}
the unweighted Sobolev inequality

\eta(2t)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}}\left(\int_{B_{2t}}|\psi|^{\frac{\nu p}{\nu-p}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\eta(r)^{-\frac{p}{\nu-p}}\psi^{\frac{\nu p}{\nu-p}}\right)^{\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}\leq\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{p}=\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\int_{B_{2t}}|\nabla\psi|^{p}

holds, by the argument in
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
(see also
[
4
]
and
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
), there exists an explicit constant
\overline{C}_{p,\nu}
depending on
\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\eta(2t)^{\frac{p}{\nu}}
such that

|B_{s}|\geq\overline{C}_{p,\nu}s^{\nu}\qquad\text{for each }\,s\in(0,t]

By (see
[
13
, p.375 line 10]
), the constant is explicitly given by

\overline{C}_{p,\nu}=2^{-\frac{\nu(\nu+2p)}{p}}\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}^{-\frac{\nu}{p}}\eta(2t)^{-1}.

Indeed, following the notation in
[
13
]
, it holds

q\doteq\frac{\nu p}{\nu-p},\quad S_{p,q}\doteq\left[\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\eta(2t)^{\frac{p}{\nu}}\right]^{-\frac{1}{p}},\quad\alpha\doteq\frac{\nu}{\nu+p},\quad\overline{\alpha}\doteq\frac{\nu(\nu+p)}{p^{2}}.

By using again the monotonicity of
\eta
, we obtain

f(t)\leq\left(\overline{C}_{p,\nu}^{-\frac{\nu-p}{\nu}}\mathscr{S}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\eta(t)^{\frac{p}{\nu(p-1)}}t^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}}\leq\hat{C}_{p,\nu}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\eta(2t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}t^{-\frac{\nu-p}{p-1}}

with

\hat{C}_{p,\nu}\doteq\mathscr{S}^{\frac{\nu}{p}}_{p,\nu}2^{\frac{(\nu+2p)(\nu-p)}{p}}.\qed

Next, we need to bound the ratio between the minimum and maximum of
\mathcal{G}
on
\partial B_{t}(o)
. This can be done by finding a path connecting the extremal points of
\mathcal{G}
, then covering this path with small balls, and finally using the Harnack inequality on each ball. Notice that the balls must avoid the pole
o
when applying the Harnack inequality therein. Hence, we need a quantitative lower bound for the best radius
t^{\prime}
so that
\partial B_{t}(o)
is connected outside
B_{t^{\prime}}(o)
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold. For each fixed origin

\begin{array}[]{c}\varsigma_{o}\ :\ (0,\infty)\to[1,\infty),\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\varsigma_{o}(t)=\inf\left\{c\in[1,\infty)\ :\ \begin{array}[]{l}\text{every $x,y\in\partial B_{t}(o)$ can be joined}\\
\text{by a path lying in $\overline{B_{t}(o)}\backslash B_{t/c}(o)$}\end{array}\right\}\end{array}

Observe that

\varsigma_{o}(t)\leq\frac{t}{{\rm inj}(o)}<\infty,

where

is the injectivity radius of

. The terminology RCA was introduced by V. Minerbe in
[

]
, where the author shows the following remarkable result:

Proposition 3.7

(
[
11
]

Let
M
be a connected, complete manifold satisfying

,

depending on

Proposition 3.9

Fix

For

on

4
Growth estimates for IMCF cores

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Let us recall that:

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
u:M\backslash\{o\}\to\mathbb{R}
is an IMCF core with pole
o
, if:

(1)

u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\backslash\{o\})
, and
u(x)-(n-1)\log r(x)\to 0
as
x\to o
,

(2)

u
is a weak IMCF in
M\backslash\{o\}
.

We say that
u
is a proper IMCF core, if
E_{t}(u)\Subset M
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We restate Theorem
1.1
below for the reader’s convenience:

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
M
satisfies
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
and a reverse volume doubling condition

\frac{|B_{t}(o)|}{|B_{s}(o)|}\geq C_{\mathscr{R}}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{b},\qquad\forall\,t\geq s>0.

(
\text{RD}_{b}
)

Then there exists a unique proper IMCF core
u
on
M
with pole
o
, with the growth estimate

\displaystyle u(x)\geq(n-1)\log r(x)-C\big(n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}},|B_{1}(o)|\big),\qquad\forall\,x\in B_{1}(o),

(4.1)

\displaystyle u(x)\geq(b-1)\log r(x)-C\big(n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}},|B_{1}(o)|\big),\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash B_{1}(o),

(4.2)

and the global gradient estimate

|\nabla u(x)|\leq(n-1)e^{-\frac{u}{n-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Recall Definition
2.2
: it is well-known that
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
implies
\mathrm{(VD)}
and
(\mathrm{NP}_{1,1})
, with the doubling dimension
\nu=n
and constants
C_{\mathscr{D}},\mathscr{P}_{1,1}
bounded in terms of
n
. Further, notice that

\frac{t^{\nu}}{|B_{t}(o)|}\leq\frac{1}{|B_{1}(o)|},\qquad\forall\,t\leq 1

by Bishop-Gromov, and

\frac{t^{\nu}}{|B_{t}(o)|}\leq\frac{t^{n-b}}{C_{\mathscr{R}}|B_{1}(o)|},\qquad\forall\,t\geq 1

by our reverse doubling condition.
Thus, Theorem
3.4
(applied with the choice
p_{0}=(b+1)/2
) yields

\mathcal{G}_{p}(x)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|B_{1}(o)|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}r(x)^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in B_{1}(o)\backslash\{o\},\\
&C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|B_{1}(o)|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}r(x)^{-\frac{b-p}{p-1}},\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash B_{1}(o),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.4)

for all
p\in(1,(1+b)/2]
, where
C
depends on
n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}}
.

Take Moser’s transformation
w_{p}=(1-p)\log\mathcal{G}_{p}
. The above bound is turned into

\displaystyle w_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\geq(n-p)\log r(x)-C\qquad\forall\,x\in B_{1}(o)\backslash\{o\},

(4.5)

\displaystyle w_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\geq(b-p)\log r(x)-C\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash B_{1}(o),

(4.6)

where
C
is a generic constant depending on
n,b,C_{\mathscr{R}},|B_{1}(o)|
. Furthermore, the gradient estimate in
[
10
, Lemma 2.17]
gives

|\nabla w_{p}|\leq(n-p)e^{-\frac{w_{p}}{n-p}}\Big(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{n-p}}\qquad\text{in}\ \ M\backslash\{o\}.

(4.7)

To pass to the limit, we also need an upper bound for
w_{p}
. Suppose that
C
is large enough so that the injectivity radius of
o
is larger than
1/C
, and the mean curvature
H_{\rho}
of the geodesic sphere
\partial B_{\rho}(o)
satisfies

\Big|H_{\rho}-\frac{n-1}{\rho}\Big|\leq 1,\qquad\forall\,\rho\leq 1/C.

(4.8)

Consider the function

\overline{w}_{p}=\overline{w}_{p}(r)=(n-p)\log r+\frac{1}{1-Cr},\qquad\forall\,r\leq 1/C.

Differentiating,

\nabla\overline{w}_{p}=\Big[\frac{n-p}{r}+\frac{C}{(1-Cr)^{2}}\Big]\partial_{r},

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}\overline{w}_{p}-|\nabla\overline{w}_{p}|^{p}

\displaystyle=|\nabla\overline{w}_{p}|^{p-2}\Big[(p-1)\Big(\!-\frac{n-p}{r^{2}}+\frac{2C^{2}}{(1-Cr)^{3}}\Big)+H_{r}\Big(\frac{n-p}{r}+\frac{C}{(1-Cr)^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{n-p}{r}+\frac{C}{(1-Cr)^{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla\overline{w}_{p}|^{p-2}\Big[\frac{2(p-1)C^{2}}{(1-Cr)^{3}}+\frac{(n-1)C+(n-p)(1-Cr)^{2}+Cr-2(n-p)C}{r(1-Cr)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{C^{2}}{(1-Cr)^{4}}\Big],

where we inserted (
4.8
) into the expression. When
C
is sufficiently large and
p
is sufficiently close to
1
, the numerator of the second term is bounded above by
-C/2
. Hence we have

\frac{\Delta_{p}\overline{w}_{p}-|\nabla\overline{w}_{p}|^{p}}{|\nabla\overline{w}_{p}|^{p-2}}\leq\frac{2(p-1)C^{2}}{(1-Cr)^{3}}-\frac{C/2}{r(1-Cr)^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{(1-Cr)^{4}},

and the right hand side is negative by Young’s inequality. So by possibly increasing
C
,
\overline{w}_{p}
is a supersolution of the
p
-IMCF equation, and hence
e^{-\overline{w}_{p}/(p-1)}
is
p
-subharmonic in
B_{1/C}(o)
. By the comparison principle, we have

w_{p}\leq\overline{w}_{p}-1+\log\Big[\omega_{n-1}\Big(\frac{p-1}{n-p}\Big)^{1-p}\Big]\qquad\text{in}\ \ B_{1/C}(o)\backslash\{o\}.

(4.9)

Therefore, for some possibly larger generic constant
C
, we have

w_{p}\leq(n-p)\log r+C\qquad\text{in}\ \ B_{1/C}(o)\backslash\{o\}.

(4.10)

Now, combining the two-sided bounds (
4.5
) (
4.10
) and the gradient estimate (
4.7
), and Arzela-Ascoli’s theorem, it follows that for some sequence
p\to 1
we can extract a limit

w_{p}\to w\qquad\text{in}\ \ C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\backslash\{o\}),

for some solution
w
of the weak IMCF in
M\backslash\{o\}
. The
C^{0}
bounds (
4.5
) (
4.6
) (
4.10
) imply that
w\geq(b-1)\log r-C
outside
B_{1}(o)
and

(n-1)\log r-C\leq w\leq(n-1)\log r+C\qquad\text{in}\ \ B_{1/C}(o).

(4.11)

It remains to show that

w=(n-1)\log r+\log\omega_{n-1}+o_{r}(1)

as
x\to o
. Once this is proved, it follows that
\tilde{w}=w-\log\omega_{n-1}
is an IMCF core with pole
o
, and satisfies the lower bounds (
4.5
) (
4.6
) together with the gradient estimate
|\nabla\tilde{w}|\leq(n-1)e^{-\tilde{w}/(n-1)}
due to (
4.7
). Thus, the proof of Theorem
4.2
is complete.

We first claim that
w
is approximately constant on
\partial B_{\rho}(o)
as
\rho\to 0
. If this is not the case, then there is a sequence
\rho_{i}\to 0
and points
x_{i},y_{i}\in\partial B_{\rho_{i}}(o)
, such that
|w(x_{i})-w(y_{i})|\geq\varepsilon
for a uniform
\varepsilon>0
. Then consider the shifted sequence
w_{i}=w-(n-1)\log\rho_{i}
and the rescaled metric
g^{\prime}_{i}=\rho_{i}^{-2}g
. Due to (
4.11
), the functions
w_{i}
are uniformly bounded in
B^{\prime}_{R}(o)\backslash B^{\prime}_{1/R}(o)
for all
R>0
. Together with the gradient estimate, some subsequence of
w_{i}
converges in
C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
to a weak IMCF
w_{\infty}
with

(n-1)\log|x|-C\leq w_{\infty}\leq(n-1)\log|x|+C,\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

By Huisken-Ilmanen’s Liouville theorem
[
7
, Proposition 7.2]
, it follows that
w_{\infty}=(n-1)\log|x|+C^{\prime}
for some constant
C^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we may take a further subsequence so that
x_{i}\to x_{\infty}\in\partial B_{1}(0)
,
y_{i}\to y_{\infty}\in\partial B_{1}(0)
. Hence,
\varepsilon\leq|w_{i}(x_{i})-w_{i}(y_{i})|\to|w_{\infty}(x_{\infty})-w_{\infty}(y_{\infty})|=0
. This is a contradiction, and the claim follows.

Thus, we may write
w=f(r)+\eta
where
f:(0,1/C)\to\mathbb{R}
is some function in
r
, and
\eta\to 0
uniformly as
r\to 0
. By further increasing
C
, we may assume
|\eta|<C
.
By (
4.11
), for all
s<1/C
we have

f(s)\leq(n-1)\log(s)+C+\max_{\partial B_{s}(o)}|\eta|\leq(n-1)\log(s)+2C.

(4.12)

Now we notice that, for all
s<1/C
, we have

w\geq f(s)-\max_{\partial B_{s}(o)}|\eta|+(n-1)\log\frac{r}{s}-3C^{2}r\qquad\text{on}\ \ \partial B_{s}(o)\cup\partial B_{1/C}(o).

(4.13)

This is obvious on
\partial B_{s}(o)
, and follows from (
4.11
) (
4.12
) on
\partial B_{1/C}(o)
. Since the right hand side of (
4.13
) is a subsolution of IMCF in
B_{1/C}(o)\backslash B_{s}(o)
, by the maximum principle, we have

w\geq f(s)-\max_{\partial B_{s}(o)}|\eta|+(n-1)\log\frac{r}{s}-3C^{2}r\qquad\text{in}\ \ B_{1/C}(o)\backslash B_{s}(o).

Similarly, one has

w\leq f(s)+\max_{\partial B_{s}(o)}|\eta|+(n-1)\log\frac{r}{s}+3C^{2}r\qquad\text{in}\ \ B_{1/C}(o)\backslash B_{s}(o).

From these inequalities it follows that

f(s)-(n-1)\log s-o_{r}(1)\leq f(r)-(n-1)\log r\leq f(s)+(n-1)\log s+o_{r}(1),

for all
0<s<r\ll 1/C
, where
o_{r}(1)
is an expression that converges to
0
as
r\to 0
. This implies that
f(r)-(n-1)\log r
, hence
w-(n-1)\log r
as well, uniformly converges to a constant when
r\to 0
.

Denote by
\alpha
the constant such that

w=(n-1)\log r+\alpha+o_{r}(1).

(4.14)

It follows from (
4.9
) that
\alpha\leq\log\omega_{n-1}
.

Let us show that in fact
\alpha=\log\omega_{n-1}
. We argue similarly as in
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
. Take a small radius
R_{0}>0
and a constant
K>0
, so that the sectional curvature is at most
K
in
B_{R_{0}}(o)
and
R_{0}<\pi/2\sqrt{K}
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}
be the Green’s kernel in the space form of curvature
K
. It can be explicitly computed that

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}(r)=\int_{r}^{R_{0}}v_{K}(s)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,ds,

(4.15)

where
v_{K}(s)
is the volume of the sphere of radius
s
in the model space. By comparison,

\mathcal{G}_{p}\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}(r)+\sup_{\partial B_{R_{0}}(o)}\mathcal{G}_{p}.

Combining this with the upper bound (
4.4
), it follows that

\mathcal{G}_{p}\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}(r)+C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{0}^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}}.

Renormalizing and then taking
p\to 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{-w}=\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}e^{-w_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\Big[\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}(r)+C^{\frac{1}{p-1}}R_{0}^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}}\Big]^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{p}(r)^{p-1},CR_{0}^{p-n}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\frac{1}{v_{K}(r)},CR_{0}^{p-n}\Big\},

where in the last line we have used
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
. Combining this with the asymptotic
w=(n-1)\log r+\alpha+o_{r}(1)
and
v_{K}(r)\sim\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}
, it follows that
\alpha\geq\log\omega_{n-1}
.

Hence, we obtain the sharp asymptotics

\lim_{p\to 1^{+}}w_{p}=w=(n-1)\log r+\log\omega_{n-1}+o_{r}(1),

as claimed. Combining this with (
4.7
), we obtain the main gradient estimate (
4.3
).
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We restate Theorem
1.2
for the reader’s convenience:

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
M
satisfies the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality

{P(E)}\geq c_{I}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\qquad\forall\ E\Subset M,

(4.16)

Then there exists a unique proper IMCF core
u
with pole
o
, with the growth estimate

u(x)\geq(n-1)\log r(x)-C(n,c_{I}),\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash\{o\}.

(4.17)

The idea for proof is as follows. For each small
\rho>0
, we let
u_{\rho}
be the unique proper weak IMCF on
M
that starts from
B_{\rho}(o)
. The existence of such
u_{\rho}
follows from (
4.16
) and
[
21
]
. Then, we argue that
\big(u_{\rho}+(n-1)\log\rho\big)
converges in
C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\backslash\{o\})
to the desired IMCF core as
\rho\to 0
.

The key step in this proof is to obtain a lower bound of
u_{\rho}
that is uniform when
\rho\to 0
. The previous bound in
[
21
]
is not enough for this purpose: Taking
E_{0}=B_{\rho}(o)
into
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, the following bound was proved:

u(x)\geq\frac{n-1}{n}\log\big[r(x)-\rho\big]-\frac{n-1}{n}\log\rho-C(n,c_{I}),\qquad\forall\,x\in M\backslash B_{\rho}(o).

(4.18)

However, the leading coefficient of
\log r(x)
is nonoptimal, and the constant term diverges as
r\to 0
after the
+(n-1)\log\rho
renormalization. The strategy here is to combine the diameter estimate technique in
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
with a soft blow-up argument (see Claim 3 in the proof below), which gives a uniform lower bound of
u_{\rho}
.

The following two technical lemmas will be used in showing the asymptotics near
o
. They are not essential in showing the growth estimate (
4.17
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
M
satisfies (
4.16
). Then for each
o\in M
, there exists a sufficiently small radius
\rho_{0}
, so that
B_{\rho}(o)
is outward perimeter-minimizing for all
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
. By
[
21
, Corollary 3.15]
,
B_{\rho}(o)
admits a strictly outward area-minimizing hull
E\Subset M
. See
[
21
, Theorem 3.13]
for precise the definition of “strictly outward area-minimizing hull”; here all we need are the following two properties:

(i) if
B_{\rho}(o)
is not outer area-minimizing, then
E\supsetneq B_{\rho}(o)
;

(ii)
\partial E\backslash\partial B_{\rho}(o)
is a minimal surface (rigorously saying, the support of a stationary integral varifold).

Inserting our main condition (
4.16
) into
[
21
, (3.8)]
, we obtain a precise bound for the size of
E
: it holds
E\subset B_{R}(o)
with

R=\rho+2nc_{I}^{-\frac{n}{n-1}}{P\big(B_{\rho}(o)\big)}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Here we note that the “
+1
” term in
[
21
, (3.8)]
is redundant, which is easily seen from the proof. Note that
R\to 0
when
\rho\to 0
. Therefore, we may choose a small enough
\rho_{0}
so that all the geodesic balls
B_{R^{\prime}}(o)
,
R^{\prime}\leq R
, are strictly mean convex. Then the maximum principle and fact (ii) above forces
E=B_{\rho}(o)
. The lemma then follows from fact (i) stated above.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be a
C^{2}
domain, and
u\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
. If the following hold:

(i)
\Omega
is locally outward perimeter-minimizing in
M
,

(ii)
\Omega=E_{T}(u)
and
u|_{M\backslash\Omega}\equiv T
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
,

(iii)
u|_{\Omega}
is a subsolution of IMCF in
\Omega
,

then
u
is a subsolution of IMCF in
M
.

Proof.

Our goal is to check that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, every competitor set
E\supset E_{t}
, and domain
K
satisfying
E\backslash E_{t}\Subset K\Subset M
, it holds
J_{u}^{K}(E)\geq J_{u}^{K}(E_{t})
. Slightly modifying
K
, we may assume that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big(\partial K\cap\partial\Omega\big)=0
.

Note that if
t>T
, then
E_{t}=M
, hence
E=M
and the desired statement is vacuous. For
t=T
, we have
E_{T}=\Omega
, thus the result is a trivial combination of

{P(E;K)}\geq{P(\Omega;K)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{E\cap K}|\nabla u|=\int_{\Omega\cap K}|\nabla u|.

Now we assume
t<T
, hence
E_{t}\subset\Omega
and
\partial E_{t}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
. Note that
E\backslash\Omega\subset E\backslash E_{t}\Subset K
. Hence, in the minimizing property of
\Omega
, we can make the valid comparison
{P(\Omega;K)}\leq{P(E\cup\Omega;K)}
, which implies
{P(E\cap\Omega;K)}\leq{P(E;K)}
. Moreover,
\nabla u\equiv 0
in
E\backslash\Omega
. Hence

J_{u}^{K}(E)={P(E;K)}-\int_{E\cap K}|\nabla u|\geq{P(E\cap\Omega;K)}-\int_{(E\cap\Omega)\cap K}|\nabla u|.

Setting
E^{\prime}=E\cap\Omega
, it remains to show that

{P(E^{\prime};K)}-\int_{E^{\prime}\cap K}|\nabla u|\geq{P(E_{t};K)}-\int_{E_{t}\cap K}|\nabla u|.

(4.19)

Note that this does not directly follow from condition (iii), since
E^{\prime}\backslash E_{t}
is not precompact in
\Omega
. To show this, we consider the inner approximation

\Omega_{\epsilon}=\big\{d(\cdot,M\backslash\Omega)>\epsilon\big\},\quad E^{\prime}_{\epsilon}=E^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon},\quad K_{\epsilon}=K\cap\Omega_{\epsilon}.

Since
E^{\prime}\backslash E_{t}\Subset M
and
\partial E_{t}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, one finds that
E^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\supset E_{t}
for small enough
\epsilon
. Hence we can use (iii) to make the comparison

{P\big(E^{\prime}_{\epsilon};K_{\epsilon/2}\big)}-\int_{E^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\cap K_{\epsilon/2}}|\nabla u|\geq{P\big(E_{t},K_{\epsilon/2}\big)}-\int_{E_{t}\cap K_{\epsilon/2}}|\nabla u|.

Finally, we take
\epsilon\to 0
in this inequality. By a technical lemma
[
22

\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{0}(R,\theta_{0})&=&\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{0}(R,\theta_{k})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{\lambda_{k}}(R,\theta_{k})\\[11.38092pt]
&=&\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}(s_{k},\theta_{k})}{\mu(s_{k})}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\gamma}(s_{k})=\gamma^{*}.\end{array}

(A.7)

On the other hand, for each
(r,\theta)
fixed and for
k
large enough that
\lambda_{k}<R/r
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{0}(r,\theta)&=&\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{\lambda_{k}}(r,\theta)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}(\lambda_{k}r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda_{k}R)}\\[11.38092pt]
&=&\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}(s_{k}r/R,\theta)}{\mu(s_{k})}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{G}(s_{k}r/R,\theta)}{\overline{\mathcal{G}}(s_{k}r/R)}\cdot\frac{\mu(s_{k}r/R)}{\mu(s_{k})}\\[14.22636pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\gamma^{*}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(s_{k}r/R)}{\mu(s_{k})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma^{*}\frac{\mu(r)}{\mu(R)}&\quad\text{if }\,p<n\\[8.5359pt]
\gamma^{*}&\quad\text{if }\,p=n.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(A.8)

Therefore,
\Delta_{p,0}u_{0}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and

\begin{array}[]{llll}\text{if $p<n$,}&\displaystyle\quad u_{0}(r,\theta)\leq\gamma^{*}\frac{\mu(r)}{\mu(R)}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},&\quad u_{0}(r,\theta)=\gamma^{*}\frac{\mu(r)}{\mu(R)}\quad\text{at $y_{0}$},\\[11.38092pt]
\text{if $p=n$,}&\displaystyle\quad u_{0}(r,\theta)\leq\gamma^{*}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},&\quad u_{0}(r,\theta)=\gamma^{*}\quad\text{at $y_{0}$}.\end{array}

By the strong maximum principle (
[
19
, Prop. 3.3.2]
; a version directly applicable to the manifold setting can be found in
[
15
, Thm. 2.5.2]
and
[
14
, Thm. 1.2]
),

u_{0}(r,\theta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma^{*}\mu(r)/\mu(R)&\quad\text{if }\,p<n\\[8.5359pt]
\gamma^{*}&\quad\text{if }\,p=n.\end{array}\right.

(A.9)

The uniqueness of any subsequential limit shows that the entire family
\{u_{\lambda}\}
locally converges in
C^{1}
to
u_{0}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. Consequently, if
p<n

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{*}}{\mu(R)}=\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{u_{\lambda}(r,\theta)}{\mu(r)}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(\lambda r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda R)\mu(r)}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(\lambda r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda r)\mu(R)}=\frac{1}{\mu(R)}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(s,\theta)}{\mu(s)},

and if
p=n

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}=\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}u_{\lambda}(r,\theta)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(\lambda r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda R)}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(\lambda r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda r)}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}(s,\theta)}{\mu(s)}.

Hence,
\mathcal{G}(s,\theta)\sim\gamma^{*}\mu(s)
as
s\rightarrow 0
, as claimed.

Step 2:
it holds

\gamma^{*}\hat{\mathcal{G}}\leq\mathcal{G}\leq\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{R})}+\gamma^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}}\qquad\text{on }\,B_{R}^{*}(o).

Proof:
Because of Step 1, for
\varepsilon>0
we can apply the comparison theorem to
\mathcal{G}
,
(\gamma^{*}-\varepsilon)\hat{\mathcal{G}}
and
(\gamma^{*}+\varepsilon)\overline{\mathcal{G}}
to deduce

(\gamma^{*}-\varepsilon)\hat{\mathcal{G}}\leq\mathcal{G}\leq\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{R})}+(\gamma+\varepsilon)\overline{\mathcal{G}},

and let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Step 3:
it holds

|\nabla\mathcal{G}-\gamma^{*}\mu^{\prime}(r)\nabla r|=o\Big(|\mu^{\prime}(r)|\Big)\qquad\text{as }\,r\rightarrow 0.

Proof:
From the convergence
u_{\lambda}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, we deduce

\|\overline{\nabla}u_{\lambda}-\overline{\nabla}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{R/j})}=o_{j}(1)

(A.10)

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, where
o_{j}(1)
is a quantity depending on
j
which vanishes as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, and
\overline{\nabla}
denotes the Euclidean gradient.

The case
p<n
.

From (
A.10
) we get

\left[\partial_{r}u_{\lambda}-\gamma^{*}\frac{\partial_{r}\mu}{\mu(R)}\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}(0,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})\rightarrow 0

(A.11)

locally uniformly on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
. Now, for
r\in[R/j,jR]
we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left|g^{\alpha\beta}(0,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})-g^{\alpha\beta}(\lambda r,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})\right|\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\leq\displaystyle\frac{j^{2}}{R^{2}}\left|g^{\alpha\beta}(\lambda r,\theta)-g^{\alpha\beta}(0,\theta)\right|\left|(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})\right|\end{array}

and, since
\partial_{\alpha}u_{0}=0
, the right-hand side goes to zero uniformly on
\mathbb{B}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{R/j}
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. It follows from (
A.11
) that

\partial_{r}u_{\lambda}-\gamma^{*}\frac{\partial_{r}\mu}{\mu(R)}\rightarrow 0,\qquad\frac{1}{r^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}(\lambda r,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})\rightarrow 0

(A.12)

locally uniformly on
\mathbb{B}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{R/j}
, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. Setting
s=\lambda r
, and using the identity

(\partial_{s}\mu)(s)=-\frac{n-p}{p-1}\frac{\mu(s)}{s},

(A.13)

we infer

\partial_{r}u_{\lambda}=\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{G})(\lambda r,\theta)}{\mu(\lambda R)}\lambda=\frac{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{G})(\lambda r,\theta)}{(\partial_{s}\mu)(\lambda r)}\left[-\frac{n-p}{p-1}\frac{\mu(r)}{r\mu(R)}\right].

Therefore, from the first in (
A.12
) we deduce

0\leftarrow\frac{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{G})(\lambda r,\theta)}{(\partial_{s}\mu)(\lambda r,\theta)}\left[-\frac{n-p}{p-1}\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\right]-\gamma^{*}\partial_{r}\mu,

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, uniformly on
\mathbb{B}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{R/j}
, that is, substituting
\lambda r=s
,

0=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{G})(s,\theta)}{(\partial_{s}\mu)(s)}+\gamma^{*}\frac{r\partial_{r}\mu}{\mu(r)}\frac{p-1}{n-p}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{(\partial_{s}\mathcal{G})(s,\theta)}{(\partial_{s}\mu)(s)}-\gamma^{*}

(A.14)

as
s\rightarrow 0
. Analogously, from the second in (
A.12
) we obtain as
\lambda\rightarrow 0

0\leftarrow\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}(s,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\lambda})(\partial_{\beta}u_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{r^{2}\mu^{2}(\lambda r)}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\mu^{2}(R)}g^{\alpha\beta}(s,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{G})(\partial_{\beta}\mathcal{G}),

and using (
A.13
),

0=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{(\partial_{s}\mu)^{2}(s)}\frac{1}{s^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}(s,\theta)(\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{G})(\partial_{\beta}\mathcal{G}).

This, together with (
A.14
) proves Step 3.

p=n

20

for a complete proof, and we leave to the reader its implementation in a manifold setting.

Step 4:

\gamma^{*}=1
.

Fix
\varepsilon
such that
B_{\varepsilon}(o)\Subset\Omega
. We use the weak definition of
\Delta_{p}\mathcal{G}=-\delta_{o}
with a radial test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\varepsilon}(o))
such that
\phi(o)=1
to obtain

1=\phi(o)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\mathcal{G},\nabla\phi\rangle=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{p-2}(\partial_{r}\mathcal{G})(\partial_{r}\phi).

From Step 4, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle 1=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{p-2}(\partial_{r}\mathcal{G})(\partial_{r}\phi)\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\qquad=\displaystyle\big(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\big)\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma^{*})^{p-1}|\mu^{\prime}(r)|^{p-2}\mu^{\prime}(r)\phi^{\prime}(r)\omega_{m-1}r^{m-1}\mathrm{d}r=(\gamma^{*})^{p-1}(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)),\end{array}

and the thesis follows by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Step 5:
it holds

\left|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}-\nabla^{2}\mu(r)\right|=o\Big(|\mu^{\prime\prime}(r)|\Big)\qquad\text{as }\,r\rightarrow 0.

Because of Step 3, having fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
choose
\lambda_{j}
small enough that

\left|\frac{\nabla\mathcal{G}(s,\theta)}{\mu^{\prime}(s)}\right|\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right]\qquad\text{on }\,B^{*}_{R\lambda_{j}/j}.

(A.15)

Rearranging (
A.5
), by homogeneity

also solves the linear PDE

\mathrm{div}_{g_{\lambda}}\left(\left|\frac{\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}{\mu^{\prime}(\lambda R)}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda}\right)=0,

that in view of (

\mathbb{B}^{*}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{j/R}^{*}
for every

). Via Schauder’s estimates we therefore improve the local

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{C^{2,\beta_{j}}}\leq\hat{C}_{j,p,R}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{B}_{jR}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{\frac{R}{j}},

for some
\beta_{j}\in(0,1)
and a constant now also depending on
R
, The proof now follows as in Step 3, and is left to the reader.

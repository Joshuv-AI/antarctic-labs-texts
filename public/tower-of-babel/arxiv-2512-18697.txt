Multiscale homogenization of non-local energies of convolution-type
Giuseppe C. Brusca

Abstract
We analyze a family of non-local integral functionals of convolution-type depending on two small positive parameters ε , δ \varepsilon,\delta : the first rules the length-scale of the non-local interactions and produces a ‘localization’ effect as it tends to 0 0 , the second is the scale of oscillation of a finely inhomogeneous periodic structure in the domain. We prove that a separation of the two scales occurs and that the interplay between the localization and homogenization effects in the asymptotic analysis is determined by the parameter λ \lambda defined as the limit of the ratio ε / δ \varepsilon/\delta . We compute the Γ \Gamma -limit of the functionals with respect to the strong L p L^{p} -topology for each possible value of λ \lambda and detect three different regimes, the critical scale being obtained when λ ∈ ( 0 , + ∞ ) \lambda\in(0,+\infty) .

Introduction

In their celebrated paper
[
7
]
, Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu proved that a simple approximation of (a multiple of) the
p
-Dirichlet energy is obtained by means of the double integrals

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigl|\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,dy,

(1)

with
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
a family of radially symmetric non-negative kernels satisfying

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|\xi|^{p}\,d\xi=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|\xi|^{p}\,d\xi=0

for all
\varepsilon,r>0
. This result has been extended by Ponce
[
23
]
, also in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence, to more general homogeneous energies and non-radially symmetric kernels, and, very recently, sharp conditions on the family of kernels
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
for the validity of such approximation have been detected, see
[
15
]
and
[
17
]
.

A remarkable class of kernels that fit in this framework is obtained starting from a non-negative
\rho
having
p
-moment on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equal to
1
and letting

\rho_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}\Bigr).

Because of this rescaling property, the resulting energies (
1
) are called of
convolution-type
.

Besides the theory of non-local gradients, that finds several applications in peridynamics (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
4
,
20
]
), non-local energies, and in particular those of convolution-type, have been investigated in the last years within many contexts (we refer to
[
1
]
for a comprehensive treatment from the variational perspective). For instance, manifold-constrained maps have been considered by Solci
[
25
]
, who has obtained an approximation of a vortex energy in the spirit of the Ginzburg-Landau model, and by Giorgio, Happ, and Schönberger
[
18
]
for the homogenization of micromagnetic energies. Some geometric aspects have been investigated by Berendsen and Pagliari in
[
6
]
, where, together with other results, the asymptotics as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the associated notions of non-local perimeters are established. The analysis of multiscale problems has been addressed by Alicandro, Gelli, and Leone
[
2
]
in the setting of perforated domains (see also
[
10
,
11
]
), and by Braides, Scalabrino, and Trifone
[
12
]
in that of disconnected sets.

The aim of this work is to perform the asymptotic analysis of non-local functionals of convolution-type in the setting of periodic homogenization. Given
\Omega
a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary and small positive parameters
\varepsilon,\delta
, we study the functionals

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta},\frac{y}{\delta},\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy,\quad u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

(2)

for
p\in(1,+\infty)
, under general assumptions on
\rho
, the interaction kernel, and the density
f
, that is supposed to be
Q_{1}
-periodic in the first two variables.

As already mentioned, the parameter
\varepsilon
is responsible for a
localization
of the functionals (
2
), which possess a finite limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
only on Sobolev functions. On the other hand, since
f
is periodic, the above energies encode some average properties of a finely inhomogeneous structure when
\delta
is small, which, in broad terms, yields a
homogenized
limit energy independent of the spatial variables. In light of these observations, it is expected that both the localization and homogenization phenomena may be exhibited by our model when the parameters
\varepsilon,\delta
vanish simultaneously; and therefore, it is rather natural to ask how these effects combine.

For this reason, we assume that
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and prove that a
separation of the scales

\varepsilon
and
\delta
occurs, providing a complete description of the effective limit (in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence with respect to the strong
L^{p}
-topology) in accordance with the (possibly different) rates of convergence to
0
of the involved parameters.

In order to illustrate our result, we consider a simplified, but prototypical, example of non-local oscillating energies given by

F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta}\Bigr)\Bigl|\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,dy,

(3)

that is obtained from (
2
) upon setting
f(x,y,z)=a(x)|z|^{p}
, with the function
a
that is
Q_{1}
-periodic and such that
0<\alpha\leq a(x)\leq\beta<+\infty
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As a starting point for our study, we may consider the case
\delta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
that has already been treated in
[
1
]
. In this instance, it is proved that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm hom}^{\rm NL}(\nabla u)\,dx,\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

the integrand of the homogenized energy
f_{\rm hom}^{\rm NL}
being characterized through a so-called
non-local cell-problem formula
(see
[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
) given by

f_{\rm hom}^{\rm NL}(M)=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(y-x)a(x)|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}\,dx\,dy:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, where

L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):u-Mx\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic}\}.

It is immediate to extend this result to the case that
\delta
is a multiple of
\varepsilon

\lambda\delta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\quad\varepsilon>0,

for some
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, obtaining that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm hom,\lambda}^{\rm NL}(\nabla u)\,dx,

(4)

where now

f_{\rm hom,\lambda}^{\rm NL}(M)=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\lambda}\Bigr)a(x)\Bigl|\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,dy:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}.

Different limits are obtained if we let the parameters
\varepsilon,\delta
tend to
0
separately. To see this, we suppose for simplicity of exposition that the kernel
\rho
is radial and that the coefficient
a
is continuous. If we let first
\varepsilon\to 0
(keeping
\delta
fixed), applying
[
23
, Corollary 8]
we obtain that

F_{\delta}(u):=\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=\kappa\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta}\Bigr)|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx,\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

where

\kappa:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)|\xi_{1}|^{p}\,d\xi.

(5)

Then, letting
\delta\to 0
and using a known result in
\Gamma
-convergence for the homogenization of integral functionals (see
[
9
, Theorem 14.7]
) we infer

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\delta\to 0}F_{\delta}(u)=\kappa\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm hom}(\nabla u)\,dx,

where the integrand is described by the
cell-problem formula

f_{\rm hom}(M):=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}a(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx:u\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

and

W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):u-Mx\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic}\}.

In a similar fashion, if we first let
\delta\to 0
, by the periodicity of the coefficient
a
and the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma, we have that

F_{\varepsilon}(u):=\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\delta\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=\Bigl(\int_{Q_{1}}a\,dx\Bigr)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigl|\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,dy;

and then, applying once again
[
23
, Corollary 8]
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\kappa\Bigl(\int_{Q_{1}}a\,dx\Bigr)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

with
\kappa
as in (
5
).

Since functionals (
3
) are equi-coercive in the strong
L^{p}
-topology on functions with fixed mean value on
Q_{1}
(see
[
24
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2]
), using, at least formally, a diagonal argument (see
[
14
]
for the metrizability of
\Gamma
-convergence) we obtain that there exist two scales
\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
and
\delta^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon)}(u)=\kappa\int_{\Omega}f_{\rm hom}(\nabla u)\,dx

and

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon,\delta^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)}(u)=\kappa\Bigl(\int_{Q_{1}}a\,dx\Bigr)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx

whenever
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. These two behaviours differ from that obtained in (
4
), where
\varepsilon/\delta(\varepsilon)
is a fixed positive constant, suggesting that the
\Gamma
-limit of
\{F_{\varepsilon,\delta(\varepsilon)}\}_{\varepsilon}
may be governed by the new parameter

\lambda:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\in[0,+\infty],

and in particular that the critical scaling may be obtained for
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
. This is the content of our main result. In view of its statement, we set our standing assumptions.

We suppose that
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
is a non-negative Borel function (not necessarily radially symmetric) such that there exist
c_{0},r_{0}>0
with the property that

\rho(\xi)\geq c_{0}\quad\text{ for almost every }\xi\in B_{r_{0}},

(
\rho_{1}
)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)|\xi|^{p}\,d\xi<+\infty,

(
\rho_{2}
)

then we suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,+\infty)
is a Borel function such that

\displaystyle f(\cdot,y,z)\text{ and }f(x,\cdot,z)\text{ are }Q_{1}\text{-periodic for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ and for almost every }y,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ respectively},

(P)

f(x,y,\cdot)\text{ is convex for almost every }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(C)

and that fulfills the growth conditions

\alpha|z|^{p}\leq f(x,y,z)\leq\beta|z|^{p}\text{ for almost every }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

(GC)

for some strictly positive numbers
\alpha,\beta
and an exponent
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Besides these hypotheses, according to the value of
\lambda
we shall assume that

f(x,\cdot,z)\text{ is continuous for almost every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(H0)

or

\rho(\xi)f(x,y,z)=\rho(-\xi)f(x,y,-z)\text{ for almost every }\xi,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(H1)

or that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\,d\xi=+\infty.

(H2)

Note that if
f(x,y,z)=f(y,x,z)
and
f(x,y,z)=f(x,y,-z)
for a.e.
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it is not restrictive to suppose that
\rho(\xi)=\rho(-\xi)
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see, e.g.,
[
1
, Remark 2.1]
.

We use the change of variables
\xi:=(y-x)/\varepsilon
and introduce the notation
E_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\{x\in E:x+\varepsilon\xi\in E\}
to rewrite the functional (
2
) as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta},\frac{x+\varepsilon\xi}{\delta},\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi;

then, we consider
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j}
and
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j}
two sequences of positive numbers converging to
0
as
j\to+\infty
and define the functionals
F_{j}:L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[0,+\infty]
as

F_{j}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{u(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j}
and
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j}
be sequences such that
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
and
\delta_{j}\to 0^{+}
as
j\to+\infty
, and assume there exists

\lambda:=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\in[0,+\infty].

Let
\rho
be a non-negative kernel satisfying (

ρ
1

) and (

ρ
2

), and let
f
be a function satisfying (
P
), (
C
), and (
GC
). In addition, if
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
assume that (
H0
) is satisfied, and if
\lambda=+\infty
assume that either (
H1
) or (
H2
) is satisfied. Then, letting
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
be as in (
6
), there exists a quasiconvex function
f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F_{j}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(\nabla u)\,dx&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\setminus W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}).\end{cases}

In particular, the following hold for every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
:

(i)

if
\lambda=0
and (
H0
) holds, then

f_{0}(M)=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,x,(\nabla u(x))\xi)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\};

(ii)

if
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
and (
H0
) holds, then

f_{\lambda}(M)=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}}\rho\Bigl(\frac{y-x}{\lambda}\Bigr)f\Bigl(x,y,\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\};

(iii)

if
\lambda=+\infty
and either (
H1
) or (
H2
) holds, then

f_{+\infty}(M)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi.

We remark that the hypotheses (

ρ
1

), (

ρ
2

) and (
GC
) are needed in order to ensure the equi-coerciveness of the functionals
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
so that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, these have a finite limit only on
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, see
[
24
]
or
[
1
]
, and that the latter can be weakened if we further assume the integrability of the kernel (Remark
4.2
). Assumption (
C
) implies the weak lower semicontinuity in
L^{p}
of the involved functionals and cannot be removed unconcernedly, since, contrary to the local case, the relaxation procedure for double integrals may even result in the loss of an integral representation as observed in
[
21
,
19
,
13
]
. We postpone further comments on the conditions (
H0
), (
H1
) and (
H2
) to the end of the introduction.

The first steps towards the proof of our result are the reduction to the case
\rho
is supported on a ball followed by the application of a compactness and integral representation result. These results have been proved in
[
1
]
in greater generality; we present some simpler versions that are suitable for our purposes, see Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
, respectively. The latter states that the sequence of functionals
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
in (
6
) admits (up to subsequences) a
\Gamma
-limit which has to be finite on
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
only and that is represented in integral form as

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F_{j}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\nabla u)\,dx.

As customary in the homogenization of integral functionals, we prove that the integrand
\varphi
is independent of the first variable (Lemma
2.2
below). Subsequently, we exploit the quasiconvexity of
\varphi
in the gradient variable and the convergence of boundary-value problems for non-local functionals to infer that

\varphi(M)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\inf\{F_{j}(u,Q):u=Mx\text{ close to }\partial Q\},

where we have ‘localized’ the functionals
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
in a cube
Q
contained in
\Omega
setting

F_{j}(u,Q):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{u(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi,\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

and the proper meaning of ‘close to
\partial Q
’ shall be clarified in Section
2
. Note that, here, the term ‘localization’ is intended in the sense of the so-called ‘localization method’ for
\Gamma
-convergence in which integral functionals are regarded as set functions as well (see
[
9
, Chapter 9]
or
[
1
, Chapter 5]
for the non-local counterpart). Upon manipulating the localized energies using the assumptions (
P
) and (
C
), together with some rescaling arguments, we obtain that
\varphi(M)
is determined in terms of the ‘non-local cell-problem formulas’

\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\xi,\frac{u(x+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\xi)-u(x)}{\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\};

(7)

and then, we discuss the asymptotic behaviour of the infima (
7
) according to the value of the relevant parameter
\lambda
. Our analysis is based on the study of the compactness properties of any sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
having equi-bounded energies; i.e., such that

\sup_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\xi,\frac{u_{j}(x+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi<+\infty,

(8)

and with fixed mean value in
Q_{1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if

\lambda=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}=0,\qquad\qquad(\textit{subcritical case})

condition (
8
), together with the assumptions on
\rho
and
f
, implies the strong convergence (up to subsequences) to a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

If

\lambda=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\in(0,+\infty),\qquad\qquad(\textit{critical case})

we infer a uniform bound on
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}
that only yields weak compactness in
L^{p}
but which is sufficient to carry out our proof.

Finally, when

\lambda=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}=+\infty,\qquad\qquad(\textit{supercritical case})

we are not able to obtain any compactness for
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
; we rely on the
L^{p}
-bounds for the difference quotients

\frac{u_{j}(x+\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}}},\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

(9)

and on a more delicate argument in the vein of the two-scale convergence
[
3
,
22
]
.

It is interesting to note that, starting from our result, it is possible to infer that the function

\lambda\mapsto f_{\lambda}(M)

is continuous in
[0,+\infty]
for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, upon assuming (
H0
) together with either (
H1
) or (
H2
). The continuity at
\lambda=0
, which is formally obtained adapting the equi-coerciveness and
\Gamma
-convergence results in
[
23
,
24
]
, can be regarded as a localization of the non-local cell-problem formulas defining
f_{\lambda}
to the local cell-problem formula for
f_{0}
. We rigorously infer such continuity using (
H0
), but this assumption seems to play a merely technical role in the whole work as it is needed to apply a strong-weak lower semicontinuity result in
L^{p}
-spaces (Theorem
2.5
). Clearly, requiring the continuity in the
y
-variable or in the
x
-variable is equivalent, and is not even needed when only one space-variable is involved; in other words, (
H0
) can be removed if
f(x,y,z)=f(x,z)
.

The case
\lambda=+\infty
is more complex and a different discussion is needed about the hypotheses (
H1
) and (
H2
). According to the heuristics illustrated before for the analysis of (
3
), it would be natural to conjecture that

f_{+\infty}(M)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, regardless of the validity of (
H1
) or (
H2
). In fact, if we do not enhance our assumptions, we are only able to prove that

f_{+\infty}(M)\geq\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,x+y,M\xi+V(x,y))\,dx\,dy\,d\xi:V\in\mathcal{C}\Bigr\},

(10)

where the class
\mathcal{C}
contains functions periodic in both variables and whose mean value equals
0
. This class is introduced because, roughly speaking, functions of the form
M\xi+V(x,y)
with
V\in\mathcal{C}
arise as a two-scale limit of a sequence of difference quotients similar to (
9
). If we assume that either (
H1
) or (
H2
) holds true, then the minimum problem in (
10
) is easily seen to be solved only by
V=0
, which allows us to prove
(iii)
in Theorem
1.1
. On the contrary, when neither of the hypotheses (
H1
) and (
H2
) is in force, one can exhibit simple examples in which
V=0
is not a minimizer for (
10
).

This argument suggests that it may be needed a more careful analysis of the difference quotients in (
9
) when
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
is an optimal sequence for the corresponding minimum problems (
7
); but it turns out that, for a simple choice of
f
, these difference quotients do not weakly converge to
M\xi
if both (
H1
) and (
H2
) are neglected, see Proposition
3.9
. We are then led to regard hypotheses (
H1
) and (
H2
) as necessary to us, when the homogenization occurs at a scale that is infinitesimal compared to that of the localization, in order to ensure a ‘compatibility’ between the two limit processes.

Such a compatibility seems to be lacking, or to be more difficult to exploit, in the fractional setting previously analyzed in collaboration with Braides and Donati
[
8
]
. For the oscillating energies of fractional-type

(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}a\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta}\Bigr)\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}}{|y-x|^{d+2s}}\,dx\,dy,\quad u\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(11)

the localization effect produced by the vanishing of the parameter
\varepsilon
in (
2
) is due to the convergence
s\to 1^{-}
. In that work, we were only able to treat a subcritical case

\sqrt{1-s}\ll\delta.

Although a similar heuristic reasoning to that presented above could be performed in this setting as well, we do not know whether the scale

\sqrt{1-s}\sim\delta

is critical or not; that is, whether three regimes only are allowed in the limit for the functionals (
11
). Such functionals do not fit in the framework of convolution-type energies because the dependence of (
11
) on
1-s
is different from the dependence of functionals (
2
) on the parameter
\varepsilon
. This complicates the analysis already in the case
\sqrt{1-s}\ll\delta
, which, in fact, is studied by means of a discretization argument.

2
Notation and preliminary results

In this section we fix the notation and state some preliminary results.

We let
d,m
be two positive integers and consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k},k\in\{d,m,m\times d\}
. We identify
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the ambient space containing the domain
\Omega
, a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary, and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the target space of the vector-valued functions we consider, usually in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
or
W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. For
k\in\{d,m,m\times d\}
, we let
|\xi|
denote the Euclidean norm of a vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. For
k=d
, we let
B_{r}(x)
denote the
d
-dimensional Euclidean ball of centre
x
and radius
r
and we let
Q_{r}
denote the
d
-dimensional cube of side-length
r
given by
Q_{r}:=(0,r)^{d}
.

Given
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we let
\lfloor t\rfloor
and
\lceil t\rceil
denote the lower and the upper integer part of
t
, respectively. Analogously, given
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we set
\lfloor\xi\rfloor:=(\lfloor\xi_{1}\rfloor,...,\lfloor\xi_{d}\rfloor)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\lceil\xi\rceil:=(\lceil\xi_{1}\rceil,...,\lceil\xi_{d}\rceil)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We say that a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k},k\in\{d,m\}
, is measurable provided that it is measurable with respect to the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and, in this case, we let
|E|
denote its measure.

We say that a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
Q_{1}
-periodic if, letting
\{e_{1},...,e_{d}\}
denote the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

u(x+e_{i})=u(x)

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all
i\in\{1,...,d\}
. We recall that, for any
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, the following class of functions have been defined in the introductory section:

L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):u-Mx\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic}\}

and

W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):u-Mx\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic}\}.

In order to treat boundary-value problems in a non-local setting, we introduce the class of functions

\mathcal{D}_{r,M}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m}):u(x)=Mx\,\text{ for a.e. }x\in A,\text{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus A)<r\},

with
A
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d},M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},
and
r>0
.

Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
open, we say that a sequence of functionals
\mathcal{F}_{j}:L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[0,+\infty],j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Gamma
-converges with respect to the strong
L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
-topology to a functional
\mathcal{F}:L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})\to[0,+\infty]
as
j\to+\infty
if the following hold:

(i)

for every
u\in L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
j\to+\infty
it holds

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}(u_{j})\geq\mathcal{F}(u);

(ii)

for every
u\in L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
there exists a sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
v_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
j\to+\infty
and it holds

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}(v_{j})\leq\mathcal{F}(u).

In this case, we write

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}(u)=\mathcal{F}(u)

for all
u\in L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Throughout the whole work, we shall always assume that the kernel
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
is a Borel function that satisfies (

ρ
1

) and (

ρ
2

), and that the density
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,+\infty)
is a Borel function that satisfies (
P
), (
C
), and (
GC
). In some specific cases, such assumptions will be enhanced through (
H0
), (
H1
), and (
H2
).

Given
T>0
and letting
\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
denote the family of open subsets of
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary, we introduce for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
the truncated and ‘localized’ versions of the functionals (
6
) that are defined as
F_{j}^{T}:L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
,

F^{T}_{j}(u,A):=\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{A_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{u(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi,

(12)

where for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we adopt the notation

A_{\varepsilon}(\xi):=\{x\in A:x+\varepsilon\xi\in A\}.

If
A=\Omega
we simply write
F_{j}^{T}(u)
in place of
F_{j}^{T}(u,\Omega)
. The notation for the localized functionals is immediately adapted to the non-truncated energies
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
.

Now we recall some useful facts about
\Gamma
-convergence in the non-local context. In each of the following statements, the
\Gamma
-limit is intended with respect to the strong convergence in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

The first fact concerns the possibility of simplifying the asymptotic analysis by considering kernels whose support is a ball, see
[
1
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume there exist a sequence
\{T_{h}\}_{h}
monotonically increasing to
+\infty
and functionals
\{F^{T_{h}}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{h}
such that for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T_{h}}_{j}(u,A)=F^{T_{h}}(u,A)

for every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
. Then

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F_{j}(u,A)=\lim_{h\to+\infty}F^{T_{h}}(u,A)

for every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
.

Following
[
1
, Proposition 6.1]
, we prove that if an integral representation is in place for the
\Gamma
-limit of
\{F^{T}_{j}\}_{j}
with
T>0
, then, as a consequence of the assumption (
P
), it holds that the integrand only depends on the gradient variable.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T>0
and assume there exists a Carathéodory function
\varphi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(u,A)=\int_{A}\varphi(x,\nabla u)\,dx

for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
. Then
\varphi
is independent of the first variable.

Proof.

We set

F(u,A):=\int_{A}\varphi(x,\nabla u)\,dx

for
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
and we note that the conclusion follows if we prove that

F(Mx,B_{r}(y))=F(Mx,B_{r}(y^{\prime}))

for every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and for every
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r>0
such that
B_{r}(y)
and
B_{r}(y^{\prime})
are contained in
\Omega
. To prove this claim, it suffices to show that

F(Mx,B_{r^{\prime}}(y))\leq F(Mx,B_{r}(y^{\prime}))

for every
M,y,y^{\prime},r
as above and
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
be such that
u_{j}\to Mx
in
L^{p}(B_{r}(y^{\prime});\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
j\to+\infty
and

\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(u_{j},B_{r}(y^{\prime}))=F(Mx,B_{r}(y^{\prime})).

(13)

Note that, upon assuming that
j
is large enough, we have

B_{r^{\prime}}(y)-\delta_{j}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\delta_{j}}\Bigr\rfloor\subseteq B_{r}(y^{\prime})

and then, the functions

v_{j}(x):=u_{j}\Bigl(x-\delta_{j}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\delta_{j}}\Bigr\rfloor\Bigr)+\delta_{j}M\Bigl\lfloor\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\delta_{j}}\Bigr\rfloor,\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

are well defined on
B_{r^{\prime}}(y)
and such that
v_{j}\to Mx
in
L^{p}(B_{r^{\prime}}(y);\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
j\to+\infty
. Moreover, by a change of variables and assumption (
P
), it holds

\displaystyle F^{T}_{j}(v_{j},B_{r^{\prime}}(y))

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{(B_{r^{\prime}}(y))_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{v_{j}(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{\bigl(B_{r^{\prime}}(y)-\delta_{j}\bigl\lfloor\frac{y-y^{\prime}}{\delta_{j}}\bigr\rfloor\bigr)_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{u_{j}(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq F^{T}_{j}(u_{j},B_{r}(y^{\prime})).

Therefore, recalling (
13
), we infer

F(Mx,B_{r}(y^{\prime}))=\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(u_{j},B_{r}(y^{\prime}))\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(v_{j},B_{r^{\prime}}(y))\geq F(Mx,B_{r^{\prime}}(y)),

which concludes the proof.
∎

The following compactness and integral representation result is a consequence of
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
and Lemma

, and constitutes the starting point of our analysis.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T>0
. There exist a subsequence
\{j_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
a quasiconvex function such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j_{k}}(u,A)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{A}\varphi(\nabla u)\,dx&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\setminus W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})\end{cases}

for every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
.

We recall
[
1
, Proposition 5.4]
, which establishes the stability of
\Gamma
-convergence for fixed boundary conditions.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
T,s>0
,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, and assume there exists a functional
F:L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(u,A)=F(u,A)

for every

and

. Then

for every

.

To conclude, we state a simplified version of a useful result about the lower semicontinuity of integral functionals with respect to the strong-weak convergence in

, see
[

.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

be measurable,
and

3
Asymptotic analysis of the truncated functionals

This section is devoted to the asymptotic analysis of functionals (
12
) for which the interaction kernel
\rho
is supported on a ball. This analysis, in combination with Lemma
2.1
, shall allow us to prove the main result in the final section.

We begin with two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
,
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}
, and
v_{j}(x):=u_{j}(x)-Mx,j\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable bounded set and
r>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
d,p,r
, and
E
such that

\int_{E}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi\leq C\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if we assume that

\sup_{j}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi<+\infty

for some
r>0
, then

\sup_{j}\int_{E}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi<+\infty.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement in the case
E=B_{R}
. If
R\leq r
this is obviously true; therefore, we prove the statement in the case
R=2r
, so that the general case follows by a simple dyadic argument.

Using the changes of variables
\xi^{\prime}:=\xi/2
and
x^{\prime}:=x+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime}
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=2^{d}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+2\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d+p-1}\Bigl\{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+2\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=2^{d+p-1}\Bigl\{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x^{\prime})}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}\Bigr\}.

Since
v_{j}
is
Q_{1}
-periodic, the function

x^{\prime}\mapsto\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x^{\prime})}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}

is
Q_{1}
-periodic as well for every
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, hence,

\int_{Q_{1}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x^{\prime})}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}=\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}\xi^{\prime})-v_{j}(x^{\prime})}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx^{\prime}

and then

\int_{B_{2r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi\leq 2^{d+p}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{v_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi,

which proves the first part of the statement.

The second part of the statement is an immediate consequence.
∎

The following lemma provides some precompactness results for sequences having equi-bounded energy.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
,
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\lambda_{j}\to\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
as
j\to+\infty
, and assume there exists
A
a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary such that
Q_{1}\subseteq A
and

\sup_{j}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}u_{j}\,dx+\int_{B_{r}}\int_{A}\Bigl|\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi\Bigr\}<+\infty

for some
r>0
.
The following hold:

(i)

if
\lambda=0
, there exist a subsequence
\{u_{j_{k}}\}_{k}
and a function
u\in W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
u_{j_{k}}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
k\to+\infty
;

(ii)

if
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, there exist a subsequence
\{u_{j_{k}}\}_{k}
and a function
u\in L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
u_{j_{k}}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
k\to+\infty
;

in both cases, it holds that
\sup_{j}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}
is finite.

Proof.

The statement
(i)
readily follows by
[
1
, Corollary
4.2
]
.

As for the proof of
(ii)
, we observe that, by the translation invariance of the functional, it is not restrictive to suppose that

\int_{Q_{1}}u_{j}\,dx=0

(14)

and that
\lambda/2\leq\lambda_{j}\leq 2\lambda
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
; moreover, since
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we may further assume that
A=Q_{1}
.

Using Lemma
3.1
with
E=Q_{2/\lambda}
, we get

S:=\sup_{j}\int_{Q_{2/\lambda}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi<+\infty;

hence, applying the change of variables
\xi^{\prime}:=\lambda_{j}\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle(2\lambda)^{p}S

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{j}\int_{Q_{2/\lambda}}\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\sup_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{Q_{\frac{2\lambda_{j}}{\lambda}}}\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x+\xi^{\prime})-u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq(2\lambda)^{-d}\sup_{j}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x+\xi^{\prime})-u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi^{\prime}.

(15)

Resorting to (
14
) and to the fact that
u_{j}(x)-Mx
is
Q_{1}
-periodic, we apply Jensen’s inequality to infer

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|u_{j}(x)-\int_{Q_{1}}u_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi\Bigr|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\int_{Q_{1}}u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(x+\xi)+u_{j}(x+\xi)-u_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi\Bigr|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|\int_{Q_{1}}u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(x+\xi)+Mx\,d\xi\Bigr|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x+\xi)-u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi+d^{\frac{p}{2}}|M|^{p}\Bigr\},

which, combined with (
15
), leads to

\sup_{j}\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{j}(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq 2^{p-1}\{(2\lambda)^{d+p}S+d^{\frac{p}{2}}|M|^{p}\}<+\infty,

that implies the thesis.
∎

For the remaining part of this section, we let
\{\varepsilon_{j}\}_{j}
and
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j}
be positive sequences such that
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0^{+}
and
\delta_{j}\to 0^{+}
as
j\to+\infty
, we set

\lambda_{j}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{\delta_{j}},\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

and we assume there exists

\lambda:=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}\in[0,+\infty].

We let
T

with

the positive constant appearing in (

ρ

); both these conditions are not restrictive since, eventually, we will let

We consider the truncated functionals

F^{T}_{j}(u,A):=\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{A_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\xi)}f\Bigl(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}},\frac{x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi}{\delta_{j}},\frac{u(x+\varepsilon_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

for

and

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume there exists a quasiconvex function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\text{-}\lim_{j\to+\infty}F^{T}_{j}(u,A)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{A}\varphi(\nabla u)\,dx&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\\
+\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\setminus W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})\end{cases}

for every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm reg}(\Omega)
. For every

the following hold:

(i)

for every
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]

u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Using an argument similar to that employed in the subcritical case, it is then immediate to verify that

U(x,\xi)=\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda},

which, combined with (
26
), implies

\varphi(M)\geq\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\},

achieving the lower bound in the case
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
.

3.1.2
Upper bounds

Now we prove that the lower bounds previously obtained are optimal. Although the proofs for each regime are similar, we illustrate them separately for the sake of clarity.

Subcritical case,
\lambda=0
.
Let
\eta>0
and let

be such that

\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla u)\xi)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}+\eta\geq\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla w)\xi)\,dx\,d\xi.

We observe that the functional that appears in the minimum problem is continuous with respect to the strong convergence in
W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
. This follows applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem whose use is justified by (
C
), by the estimate

\rho(\xi)f(x,x,(\nabla u(x))\xi)\leq\beta\rho(\xi)|\xi|^{p}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\quad\text{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in Q_{1}\times B_{T},

(29)

which follows by (
GC
), and by (

ρ
2

). Therefore, it is not restrictive to suppose that
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Applying
(iii)
of Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi.

In order to conclude, we use the Dominated Convergence Theorem. The pointwise convergence is ensured by the fact that
f
satisfies (
C
) and (
H0
), and that

\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\to(\nabla w(x))\xi

for all

as
j\to+\infty
; a uniform bound for the integrands

\rho(\xi)f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr),\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

holds in virtue of (
GC
), the inequality

\Bigl|\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|\leq\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})}|\xi|

(30)

for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and (

ρ
2

). Finally, we get

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla w(x))\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla u)\xi)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}+\eta\Bigr),

which, by the arbitrariness of
\eta
, concludes the proof.

Critical case,
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
.
Let
\eta>0
and let

be such that

\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}+\eta\\
\geq\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi.

Note indeed that a similar argument to that used for

-convergence.

Once again, we apply
(iii)
of Proposition

, use

as a test function for the corresponding minimum problems, and apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem (whose use is made possible also by (

), (

), (

), (

)) to infer

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda}\Bigl)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}+\eta\Bigr),

which, by the arbitrariness of

3.2
The supercritical case,
\lambda=+\infty
.

The supercritical regime requires a different and more complex argument. Throughout this section, we assume that either

\rho(\xi)f(x,y,z)=\rho(-\xi)f(x,y,-z)

(H1)

for almost every
\xi,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, or that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\,d\xi=+\infty.

(H2)

3.2.1
Lower bound

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
be fixed. By
(ii)
of Proposition
3.3
, there exists
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

\varphi(M)\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\ dx\,d\xi.

In the supercritical regime, there are no compactness properties that we are able to infer for the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
. We consider the
Q_{1}
-periodic functions
v_{j}(x):=u_{j}(x)-Mx,j\in\mathbb{N},
and we let

V_{j}(x,\xi):=\frac{v_{j}(x+\xi)-v_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}},\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

so that

\varphi(M)\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)+M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi.

(31)

In order to describe the idea behind our proof, we note that if we had
V_{j}(x,\xi)=V(x,\xi)
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,V(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)+M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)g(\xi,\lambda_{j}\xi)\,d\xi,

where we set

g(\xi,y):=\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx.

Since
g
is periodic in the second variable and
\lambda_{j}\to+\infty
, we may use a known result, which can be regarded as a consequence of a two-scale convergence, to obtain that

g(\xi,\lambda_{j}\xi)\rightharpoonup\int_{Q_{1}}g(\xi,y)\,dy

in a weak sense; and then, formally passing to the limit, we would get

\varphi(M)\geq\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi.

Our aim is to mimic this reasoning taking into account that
\{V_{j}\}_{j}
is not a constant sequence. This requires the study of the equi-integrability of an auxiliary sequence of functions defined similarly to the function
g
above, and some additional care has to be used since in general
\rho
does not belong to any
L^{p}
-space. These issues seem to prevent the direct use of the two-scale convergence and, also in our setting, allows us to pass to the limit only upon having truncated ‘vertically’ the kernel.

In this subsection, from now on, we shall further assume that

\inf_{j}\lambda_{j}>2;

this condition is not restrictive since we are assuming that
\lambda_{j}\to+\infty
.

We note that each
V_{j}
is
Q_{1}
-periodic in both variables. Moreover, resorting to Lemma
3.1
, we have that (
24
) holds, which implies

\sup_{j}\|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{1}\times B_{T};\mathbb{R}^{m})}<+\infty.

(32)

The following observation is then a simple consequence.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\sup_{j}\|V_{j}(x,\xi)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{1}\times Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}<+\infty.

Proof.

Since
Q_{1}\subset B_{T}
, we combine the periodicity of
V_{j}
in the second variable with a change of variables to get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{T}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{Q_{\lambda_{j}}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{j}^{-d}\lfloor\lambda_{j}\rfloor^{d}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi,

where in the last inequality we have used that
\lambda_{j}>2
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
32
), the proof is concluded.
∎

Now we let

g_{j}(\xi):=\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)+M\xi)\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi):=\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx\,dy,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and observe the following.

Lemma 3.6
.

The sequences
\{g_{j}\}_{j}
and
\{\widetilde{g}_{j}\}_{j}
are equi-integrable in
L^{1}(B_{T})
.

Proof.

Consider first the sequence
\{g_{j}\}_{j}
. By a density argument, the thesis follows if we prove that there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any cube
Q\subset B_{T}
it holds

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q}g_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi\leq C|Q|.

To this end, we first observe that by (
GC
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}g_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)+M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\beta\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)+M\xi|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\beta 2^{p-1}\Bigl\{\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi+|M|^{p}|T|^{p}|Q|\Bigr\}.

(33)

Now we note that, by (
P
),

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{\lambda_{j}Q}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi,

where
\mathcal{I}_{j}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:i+Q_{1}\cap\lambda_{j}Q\neq\emptyset\}
. Since
\lambda_{j}^{-d}\#\mathcal{I}_{j}\to|Q|
, we deduce

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}|V_{j}(x,\lambda_{j}\xi)|^{p}\,dx\,d\xi\leq|Q|\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\|V_{j}(x,\xi)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{1}\times Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})},

which, together with (
33
), concludes the proof in light of Lemma
3.5
.

As for the sequence
\{\widetilde{g}_{j}\}_{j}
, by (
GC
) we simply have

\displaystyle\int_{A}\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\beta 2^{p-1}\Bigl\{\|V_{j}(x,y)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{1}\times Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}|A|+|M|^{p}|T|^{p}|A|\Bigr\}

for any
A\subset B_{T}
measurable and for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and the conclusion follows as before.
∎

We state our main lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

It holds that

g_{j}(\xi)-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\rightharpoonup 0

weakly in
L^{1}(B_{T})
as
j\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Due to the equi-integrability of the sequence in
L^{1}(B_{T})
established in Lemma
3.6
, it suffices to test the weak convergence with the characteristic functions of cubes contained in
B_{T}
. Let
Q
denote such a cube, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}g_{j}(\xi)-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{\lambda_{j}Q}g_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{i+Q_{1}}g_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{E_{j}}g_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})\,d\xi,

where we set
\mathcal{I}_{j}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:i+Q_{1}\subset\lambda_{j}Q\}
and
E_{j}
is obtained removing from
\lambda_{j}Q
all the unit cubes of the fundamental lattice. Since there exists
c>0
such that
|E_{j}|\leq c\lambda_{j}^{d-1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\lambda_{j}^{-d}\int_{E_{j}}g_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi/\lambda_{j})\,d\xi=\int_{\lambda_{j}^{-1}E_{j}}g_{j}(\xi)-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

vanishes as
j\to+\infty
by Lemma
3.6
; therefore, applying a change of variables, the periodicity of each
V_{j}
, and (
P
), the claim follows if we prove that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\\
-\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy\Bigr\}\,d\xi=0.

(34)

To this end, we first prove that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi\\
-\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\,dx\,d\xi\,dz=0.

(35)

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Bigl|\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\,dx\,d\xi\,dz\Bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Bigl|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\,dx\,d\xi\Bigr\}\,dz\Bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\Bigr|\,dx\,d\xi\,dz,

(36)

where we used that, by Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\Bigr\{\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\,dx\,d\xi\Bigr\}\,dz=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\widetilde{g}_{j}(z)\,dz=0.

We recall that, by (
C
), the function
f
is locally Lipschitz continuous in the last variable and, in particular, resorting also to (
GC
), it holds that

|f(x,y,z_{1})-f(z,y,z_{2})|\leq C(1+|z_{1}|^{p-1}+|z_{2}|^{p-1})|z_{1}-z_{2}|

(37)

for a.e.​
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every

, where

is a constant that depends on
p,d,
and the constant
\beta
appearing in (
GC
). Let then

and

37

\displaystyle\Bigl|f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)-f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(1+\Bigl|V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr|^{p-1}+|V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz|^{p-1}\Bigr)\Bigl|M\Bigl(\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}-z\Bigr)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(1+|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p-1})\lambda_{j}^{-1}

for a.e.​
x,\xi\in Q_{1}
, where

is a positive constant depending on
p,d,\beta,
and
M
. Combining this estimate with (
36
) and using Hölder’s inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Bigl|\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\xi,V_{j}(x,\xi)+Mz)\,d\xi\,dx\,dz\Bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}C^{\prime}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}1+|V_{j}(x,\xi)|^{p-1}\,dx\,d\xi\,dz

which equals

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi\\

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Bigl|\lambda_{j}^{-d}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\,\Bigr)dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+Mz)\,dx\,dy\,dz\Bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Bigl|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+Mz)\,dx\,dy\Bigr\}\,dz\Bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\int_{\frac{i+Q_{1}}{\lambda_{j}}}\Bigl\{\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl[\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigl|f\Bigl(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\frac{i+\xi}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+Mz)\Bigr|\,dx\,dy\Bigr]\,d\xi\Bigr\}\,dz

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq C^{\prime}|Q|\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-1}(1+\|V_{j}(x,y)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(Q_{1}\times Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})}),

which equals

, and this proves (

Gathering (

∎

We are now in position to prove the lower bound. Recalling (

) and the definitions of
g_{j}
and
\widetilde{g}_{j},j\in\mathbb{N},
we fix any

and apply Lemma

to obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)g_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\min\{\rho(\xi),R\}(g_{j}(\xi)-\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi))\,d\xi+\int_{B_{T}}\min\{\rho(\xi),R\}\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\min\{\rho(\xi),R\}\widetilde{g}_{j}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T}}\min\{\rho(\xi),R\}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi.

Using Lemma
3.5
, it is not restrictive to suppose that
V_{j}(x,y)\rightharpoonup V(x,y)
weakly in
L^{p}(Q_{1}\times Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
and, setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\Bigl\{U\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):\,

\displaystyle U(\cdot,y)\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic for a.e. }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle U(x,\cdot)\text{ is }Q_{1}\text{-periodic for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\text{and }\int_{Q_{1}}U(x,y)\,dx=0\text{ for a.e. }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\Bigr\},

by the definition of
V_{j}(x,y)
and the periodicity of

, it is easily seen that
V\in\mathcal{C}
. Then, we apply Theorem
2.5
with
\ell=d\times d,s=(x,y)
and

E=Q_{1}\times Q_{1},\quad\Psi((x,y),t,q)=f(x,x+y,q+M\xi),

and use Fatou’s Lemma to infer

\displaystyle\varphi(M)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{T}}\min\{\rho(\xi),R\}\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+y,V_{j}(x,y)+M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

in the same way to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to+\infty}\varphi_{h}(M)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T_{h_{i}}}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u_{h_{i}}(x+\lambda\xi)-u_{h_{i}}(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{R}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi,

and, by the arbitrariness of
R
and
u
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to+\infty}\varphi_{h}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}.

Finally, using the change of variables
y:=x+\lambda\xi
, we get

\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}=f_{\lambda}(M),

that is the thesis.

Supercritical case,
\lambda=+\infty
.
In this case the proof is immediate; indeed, by Proposition
3.4
and the Monotone Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to+\infty}\varphi_{h}(M)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to+\infty}\int_{B_{T_{h}}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle=f_{+\infty}(M)

for every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, concluding the proof.
∎

As a corollary, we extend
(iii)
of Proposition
3.3
to kernels that are not necessarily supported on a ball, and also to the case
\lambda=+\infty
. Consequently, we obtain that the densities
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
vary continuously with respect to
\lambda
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and let
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}
be a positive sequence converging to
\lambda\in[0,+\infty]
. Then

f_{\lambda}(M)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}.

Moreover, the function
\lambda\mapsto f_{\lambda}(M)
is continuous in
[0,+\infty]
.

Proof.

According to
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
and a change of variables, we need to prove that

f_{\lambda}(M)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M).

Let us first suppose
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
and let
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)=\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u_{j}(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi.

Upon assuming that the mean value of
u_{j}
on
Q_{1}
is zero for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain compactness properties for the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
reasoning exactly as in Section
3
for the proofs of the lower bounds in the subcritical and critical cases, and then, also resorting to Theorem
2.5
, we get that

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla u)\xi)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(M)

if
\lambda=0
, and that

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=f_{\lambda}(M)

if
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
. Conversely, to prove that

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)\leq f_{\lambda}(M),

we observe that

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)\leq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

(49)

for any
w\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

tends to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x,(\nabla w)\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

if
\lambda=0
and
w\in W^{1,p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and tends to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda\xi,\frac{w(x+\lambda\xi)-w(x)}{\lambda}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi

if
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
and
w\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then, letting
w
be a (almost) minimizer for the minimum problem corresponding to
f_{0}(M)
or to
f_{\lambda}(M)
, we infer the desired inequality by (
49
).

To conclude, we consider the case
\lambda=+\infty
. On the one hand, letting
T>0
sufficiently large so that Remark
3.8
is valid, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\inf\Bigl\{\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{T}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi,

and then, letting
T\to+\infty
,

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M)\geq f_{+\infty}(M).

Conversely, we recall that, in order to prove the upper bound for the supercritical case in section
3
, we proved that

\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\rightharpoonup\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy

weakly in
L^{1}(B_{T})
for any
T>0
large enough. By (
GC
) and (

ρ
2

), we have that for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\leq\beta|M|^{p}\rho(\xi)|\xi|^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d});

and therefore, we deduce that

\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\rightharpoonup\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy

weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We obtain

\displaystyle f_{+\infty}(M)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\rho(\xi)f(x,y,M\xi)\,dx\,dy\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f\Bigl(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,\frac{u(x+\lambda_{j}\xi)-u(x)}{\lambda_{j}}\Bigr)\,dx\,d\xi:u\in L^{p}_{\#,M}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{m})\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to+\infty}f_{\lambda_{j}}(M),

where we have used
u(x)=Mx
as a test function for the minimum problems. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

A milder growth condition from above on
f
can be required upon enhancing the integrability of
\rho
at the origin. In particular, we may require in addition that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\xi)\,d\xi<+\infty,

(50)

in order to replace (
GC
) with

\alpha|z|^{p}\leq f(x,y,z)\leq\beta(1+|z|^{p})\text{ for almost every }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and for every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(51)

Indeed, there are only a few points in our proofs where a growth condition from above on
f
is employed. We mention some of them and briefly illustrate how to modify the proofs according to the new set of assumptions.

In the first part of the proof of Proposition
3.3
we observed that

\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{j}}{r}\Bigr)^{d}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\int_{(Q_{\lceil r/\delta_{j}\rceil})_{\lambda_{j}}(\xi)\setminus(Q_{r/\delta_{j}})_{\lambda_{j}}(\xi)}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

(52)

tends to
0
. Recalling (
17
) and using (
51
), it is easily seen that (
52
) is estimated from above by

\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{j}}{r}\Bigr)^{d}|E_{j}|\beta\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\bigl(1+|M|^{p}|\xi|^{p}\bigr)\,d\xi;

and since we already proved that

\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{j}}{r}\Bigr)^{d}|E_{j}|\beta|M|^{p}\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)|\xi|^{p}\,d\xi

tends to
0
as
j\to+\infty
, it suffices to observe that

\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{j}}{r}\Bigr)^{d}|E_{j}|\beta\int_{B_{T}}\rho(\xi)\,d\xi

vanishes by (
50
) and the fact that
(\delta_{j}/r)^{d}|E_{j}|\to 0
.

In the upper bounds for the case
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
in Section
3
, (
GC
) is employed to obtain some uniform bounds from above useful to apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem. As an example, we observe that inequality (
29
) is now replaced by

\rho(\xi)f(x,x,(\nabla u(x))\xi)\leq\beta\rho(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{p}|\nabla u(x)|^{p})\quad\text{ for a.e. }(x,\xi)\in Q_{1}\times B_{T},

and the desired estimates are now obtained also resorting to (
50
).

In the supercritical case
\lambda=+\infty
, similar simple adaptations are needed in the proofs of Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
. As for the proof of the upper bound, it suffices to observe that

\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx,\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

is still an equi-integrable sequence since, by (
51
), we have that, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\subset B_{T}
measurable, it holds

\int_{A}\rho(\xi)\int_{Q_{1}}f(x,x+\lambda_{j}\xi,M\xi)\,dx\,d\xi\leq\beta\Bigl\{\int_{A}\rho(\xi)\,d\xi+\int_{A}\rho(\xi)|M\xi|^{p}\,d\xi\Bigr\}

so that the conclusion follows by (
50
). Clearly, in this case, the main result holds true assuming (
H1
).

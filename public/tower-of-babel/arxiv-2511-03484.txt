The problem of deciding the positivity of Kronecker coefficients and Saxl conjecture
Mahdi Ebrahimi

Abstract
Given a positive integer k k , let n := ( k + 1 2 ) n:=\binom{k+1}{2} . In 2012, during a talk at UCLA, Jan Saxl conjectured that all irreducible representations of the symmetric group 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} occur in the decomposition of the tensor square of the irreducible representation corresponding to the staircase partition. In this paper, we investigate two useful methods to obtain some irreducible representations that occur in this decomposition. Our main tools are the semi-group property for Kronecker coefficients and generalized blocks of symmetric groups.

Saxl conjecture, Generalized
t
-block, semi-group property, Symmetric group.

Introduction

A longstanding open problem in algebraic combinatorics, known as the
Kronecker problem
, is to find a combinatorial formula for Kronecker coefficients
[
19
]
.
Given a partition
\lambda
of a positive integer
n
, let
[\lambda]
be the associated irreducible complex character of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
For partitions
\alpha,\beta,\nu\vdash n
, the
Kronecker coefficient

g(\alpha,\beta,\nu)
is the coefficient that occurs in the (Kronecker) product
[\alpha][\beta]=\sum_{\nu}g(\alpha,\beta,\nu)[\nu]
.
They may be computed via the scalar product,

\langle[\alpha][\beta],[\nu]\rangle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}[\alpha](\pi)[\beta](\pi)[\nu](\pi),

from which it also shows that the Kronecker coefficients are symmetric in
\alpha,\beta,\nu
.

These coefficients have been described as ’perhaps the most challenging’ deep and secretive objects in algebraic combinatorics
[
15
]
.
Since general satisfactory explicit formulas or combinatorial descriptions for Kronecker coefficients seem very hard to find, it is desirable to look for some kind of global behavior of these coefficients.
For example, see
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
16
]

The problem of deciding the positivity of Kronecker coefficients born in quantum information theory
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Related to Kronecker positivity problem, there is a fundamental conjecture posed by Heide, Saxl, Tiep and Zalesskii
[
9
]
. It states that for every positive integer
n\neq 2,4,9
there is a complex irreducible character of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
whose square contains all irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as constituents. When
n
is a triangular number, a candidate for this irreducible character of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
was suggested by Saxl in 2012:
For
k\geq 1
, the staircase partition
\rho_{k}
of
n:=\binom{k+1}{2}
is
\rho_{k}=(k,k-1,\dots,1)
. Saxl’s Conjecture says that the Kronecker square
[\rho_{k}]^{2}
contains all irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as constituents.
This conjecture has inspired a lot of recent research. For instance, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
10
,
16
]
.
We start with the following result which is a partially answer to the problem of deciding the positivity of Kronecker coefficients.

Given partitions

\xi
is a
t
-core partition, then either

a)

\alpha
is a
t
-core partition, or

b)

there exists a partition
\nu\neq\alpha
of

such that
\alpha
and
\nu
have the same
t
-core and

Blasiak

]
gave a positive combinatorial formula for the Kronecker coefficient

for any partitions
\lambda,\nu
of
n
and hook partition
\mu(d):=(n-d,1^{d})
. The following result guaranties the existence of an irreducible constituent indexed by a hook partition
\neq(n),(1^{n})
in the Kronecker product
[\alpha]^{2}
of a partition
\alpha\neq(n),(1^{n})
of
n
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\vdash n
. If
\alpha\neq(n),(1^{n})
, then there is a hook partition
\beta\neq(n),(1^{n})
of

Let
n
be a positive integer. We denote by

.
Suppose that

Definition 1.3

Let

We now recall

[

]

Let

This property leads us to the next definition.

Definition 1.5

Let

Let

We let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n

[

]
.
A
partition

\lambda

n
, denoted
\lambda\vdash n
, is defined to be a weakly decreasing sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
of positive integers such that the sum
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}
is equal to
n
. The
length

l(\lambda)
of a partition
\lambda\vdash n
is the number of parts of
\lambda
.
For convenience, the notation
(\lambda_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{r}^{a_{r}})\vdash n
is used to denote the partition where
\lambda_{i}
’s are the distinct parts that occur with multiplicities
a_{i}
’s.
We identify a partition
\lambda
with its associated
Young diagram
, that is, the set of cells
\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{>0}\,|\,1\leq i\leq l(\lambda),\,j\leq\lambda_{i}\}
.
The
conjugate
or
transpose

\lambda^{\prime}
of
\lambda
is defined to be equal to the partition obtained from
\lambda
by reflecting its Young diagram through the
45^{\circ}
diagonal. A
Hook partition
of
n
is a partition of the form
\lambda=(n-m,1^{m})
, where
m\leq n
is a non-negative integer. Given partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{s})\vdash m
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{t})\vdash n
, the partition
\lambda+\mu\vdash m+n
is defined as follows: Without loss of generality, we can assume that
s\geq t
. The
i
-th part of
\lambda+\mu
is given by

(\lambda+\mu)_{i}:=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}+\mu_{i}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq t\\
\lambda_{i}&\text{if }t+1\leq i\leq s\end{cases}.

It is well known that both the conjugacy classes of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and the irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are indexed by partitions of
n
. Let
C
be a conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. As all elements in a conjugacy class
C
indexed by a partition
\alpha\vdash n
have the same cycle structure, for every element
\pi\in C
, we say that
\pi
is an element indexed by
\alpha
.
The character corresponding to a partition
\lambda
of
n
is denoted by
[\lambda]
. As
[\lambda]
is a class function of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we use
[\lambda](\alpha)
to indicate the value of
[\lambda]
on the conjugacy class indexed by
\alpha\vdash n
. The relation between
[\lambda]
and
[\lambda^{\prime}]
is reflected in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
11
, 2.1.8]

Let
\lambda\vdash n
. Then
[\lambda^{\prime}]=[(1^{n})][\lambda]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n
.

a)

g(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\nu)=g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
.

b)

If
\lambda
is self-conjugate, then
g(\lambda,\mu^{\prime},\nu)=g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
.

Proof.

It immediately follows from Lemma
2.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers.
Given partitions
\mu\vdash m
and
\lambda\vdash m+n
, the partition
\mu
is a
subpartition
of
\lambda
, written
\mu\subseteq\lambda
, if
l(\mu)\leq l(\lambda)
and
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq l(\mu)
.
The
skew diagram

\lambda/\mu
is defined as the set of cells in
\lambda
but not in
\mu
.
We use the notation
\mathrm{ht}(\lambda/\mu)
for the number of rows of
\lambda/\mu
minus one.
A ribbon
is a skew diagram
\lambda/\mu
that does not contain a
2\times 2
square.
A ribbon can be partitioned into
connected components
, where cells
x
and

lie in the same component if there is a sequence of cells

in the ribbon in which

and

share an edge. A connected component of a ribbon with

Theorem 2.3
.

(Murnaghan-Nakayama Rule).
[
18
, Theorem 4.10.2]

Given positive integers
m
and
n
, let
\delta\in\mathfrak{S}_{m+n}
be an
m
-cycle and let
\pi
be a permutation of the remaining

\lambda\vdash m+n
,

[\lambda](\pi\delta)=\sum(-1)^{\mathrm{ht}(\lambda/\mu)}[\mu](\pi),

where the sum is over all
\mu\vdash n
such that
\mu\subset\lambda
and
\lambda/\mu
is an
m
-hook.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
. Given a positive integer
t
, we say that
\lambda
is
t
-singular
(resp.
t
-regular
), if at least one part of
\lambda
(resp. no part of
\lambda
) is divisible by
t
.
We denote by
H^{\lambda}_{i,j}
the
(i,j)
-hook
of
\lambda
, which consists of the
(i,j)
-cell, called the
corner
of the
(i,j)
-hook, along with any other cells directly below or to the right of the corner.
The
hook length
of a cell

is defined as the number of cells in
H^{\lambda}_{i,j}
.
The
t
-core of
\lambda
, denoted by
\mathrm{Cor}_{t}(\lambda)

t
-hooks from
\lambda
.
The
t
-weight

of

w_{t}(\lambda):=\frac{|\lambda|-|\mathrm{Cor}_{t}(\lambda)|}{t}
.
The partition
\lambda
is called
t
-core

t
. We denote by
T(\lambda)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
. Then for every positive integer

a)

\lambda
is a
t
-core partition.

b)

[\lambda](\alpha)=0
, for every
t
-singular partition

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be a
t
-core partition and let

is divisible by

. Using

is an

does not have any cell of hook length

. Therefore, it follows from the Murnaghan-Nakayama rule that

If
\alpha:=(t^{w_{t}(\lambda)},1^{n-t.w_{t}(\lambda)})\vdash n

is
t
-singular.
∎

Let
t\geq 2
be an integer. A

\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{(t^{\prime})}
(resp.

We set

\langle[\alpha],[\beta]\rangle_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{(t^{\prime})}}:=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{(t^{\prime})}}[\alpha](\pi)[\beta](\pi),

for every irreducible characters
[\alpha]

The concept of generalized blocks was introduce by K
\ddot{u}
lshammer, Olsson and Robinson in

The irreducible characters
[\alpha]

Proof of our main results

In this section, we wish to prove our main results. We start with a useful method to find constituents of
[\rho_{k}]^{2}
, which is inspired by semi-group property.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers with
k\geq m
.

a)

For every
\alpha\in\{\lambda^{(a,b)}|\,\lambda\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{m}),\,(a,b)\in G(k,m)\}
, the Kronecker coefficient
g(\rho_{k},\rho_{k},\alpha)
is non-zero.

b)

If
k=2m
or
2m+1
,
then for every
\alpha\in\{\lambda+\mu+\nu|\,\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{m}),\,\nu\in\mathrm{Kron}(\sharp_{k-m})\}
,
the Kronecker coefficient
g(\rho_{k},\rho_{k},\alpha)
is non-zero.

Proof.

a)
Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer. First note that the Kronecker coefficient
g((1^{n}),(1^{n}),(n))=1\neq 0
. It follows from semi-group property that for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{n-1})
,

g(\rho_{n},\rho_{n},\alpha^{(n,0)})=g(\rho_{n-1}+(1^{n}),\rho_{n-1}+(1^{n}),\alpha+(n)),

and

g(\rho_{n},\rho_{n},\alpha^{(0,n)})=g(\rho_{n},\rho_{n},(\alpha^{(0,n)})^{\prime})=g(\rho_{n-1}+(1^{n}),\rho_{n-1}+(1^{n}),\alpha^{\prime}+(n)),

are non-zero. Thus, for every

\alpha\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{n-1})
, we have that

\alpha^{(n,0)},\alpha^{(0,n)}\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{n}).

(1)

Let
(a,b)
be a
(k,m)
-good pair. There exists a subset
I\subseteq\{m+1,m+2,\dots,k\}
such that
a=\sum_{i\in I}i
. We set
J:=\{m+1,m+2,\dots,k\}\backslash I
. For every
i\in I\cup J
, we define

i^{*}:=\begin{cases}(i,0)&\text{if }i\in I\\
(0,i)&\text{if }i\in J\end{cases}.

Now let
\lambda\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{m})
. For the case
k=m
, the result follows from the definition of
\lambda^{(a,b)}
. thus, we can assume that
k>m
. It is easily checked that

\lambda^{(a,b)}=(\dots((\lambda^{(m+1)^{*}})^{(m+2)^{*}})^{(m+3)^{*}}\dots)^{k^{*}}.

Therefore, the result follows from (1).

b) Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{m})
and
\nu\in\mathrm{Kron}(\sharp_{k-m}).
Then using semi-group property, we deduce that the Kronecker coefficient

g((\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m})^{\prime},(\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m})^{\prime},\mu+\nu)=g(\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m},\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m},\mu+\nu)

is non-zero. Hence, it again follows by semi-group property that the Kronecker coefficient

g(\rho_{k},\rho_{k},\lambda+\mu+\nu)=g(\rho_{m}+(\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m})^{\prime},\rho_{m}+(\rho_{m}+\sharp_{k-m})^{\prime},\lambda+(\mu+\nu))

is non-zero. Therefore,
\alpha:=\lambda+\mu+\nu\in\mathrm{Kron}(\rho_{k})
.
∎

.

Suppose that
B
is the
t
-block of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

containing the irreducible character

If

Magnetic resonance imaging-based radiomics for predicting P53-mutated hepatocellular carcinoma: intratumoral and peritumoral analysis
Authors: Jingfei Weng, Dinghua Yao, Qihu Zeng, Yuqing He, Chun Yang, Bin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and TP53 mutation acts as a trunk mutation driving tumor progression. However, TP53 mutation status can currently be determined only by invasive liver biopsy. This study applies multiregional radiomics features from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to predict P53-mutated HCC and offers a non-invasive tool for identifying this invasive subtype.MethodsIn this retrospective study, data from 320 patients who underwent preoperative MRI were analyzed and divided into a training cohort (TC; n=224) and a validation cohort (VC; n=96). Clinical risk factors were identified using stepwise regression to construct predictive models. We evaluated three machine learningclassifiers across six MRI sequences, and the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator method was applied to select the best IntraPeri fusion model from four regions (whole tumor, IntraPeri_9mm, IntraPeri_11mm, and IntraPeri_13mm) to establish a radiomics model. The predictive nomogram for P53-mutated HCC integrated these clinical predictors with discriminative radiomics features, and its performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic analysis, calibration, and decision curves.ResultsAmong 320 patients (259 men and 61 women; mean age, 58.25±11.51 years), 183 had P53-mutated HCC and 137 had non-P53-mutated HCC. The largest tumor diameter (LTD), Edmondson-Steiner grade, and rim arterial phase hyperenhancement were identified as independent predictors of P53-mutated HCC. The ExtraTrees classifier based on radiomics features from the optimal MRI sequence [T1-weighted imaging-arterial phase (T1WI-AP)] within the IntraPeri_11mm region achieved strong predictive accuracy [area under the curve (AUC)TC =0.805; AUCVC =0.660]. Predictive nomogram integrating clinical and radiomics features demonstrated excellent performance in both the TC [AUC =0.815; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.758–0.872] and VC (AUC =0.802; 95% CI: 0.713–0.890). Decision curve analysis further confirmed its clinical utility.ConclusionsNomogram combining clinical model with IntraPeri_11mm radiomics may serve as effective predictors of preoperative P53 mutation status in HCC.

Highlight box

Key findings

• A nomogram integrating three clinical predictors (largest tumor diameter, Edmondson-Steiner grade, and rim arterial phase hyperenhancement) with IntraPeri_11mm radiomics features from T1-weighted arterial phase magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) achieved good performance for preoperative prediction of P53-mutated hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) (area under the curve =0.815 in the training cohort and 0.802 in the validation cohort).

• The ExtraTrees classifier with the T1-weighted arterial phase sequence outperformed logistic regression and multilayer perceptron classifiers, and the IntraPeri_11mm model exceeded that of the intratumoral model, other IntraPeri fusion models.

What is known and what is new?

• TP53 mutation acts as a trunk mutation driving HCC progression, but its detection currently relies on invasive liver biopsy.

• This study adds a non-invasive MRI radiomics approach that incorporates the 11-mm peritumoral microenvironment, rarely exploited for mutation prediction in HCC.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The nomogram may help clinicians identify P53-mutated HCC patients before surgery, supporting individualized treatment decisions without biopsy.

• Prospective, multi-center validation is warranted before clinical adoption.

Introduction

The incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is rising worldwide, with particularly high prevalence in developing countries, where it is the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality in men. China has approximately half the number of cases as Europe and North America (1). Treatment options include surgical resection, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy, with surgery remaining the most effective. Despite therapeutic advances, HCC is frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in poor prognosis for patients (2).

The tumor protein 53 is a key factor in tumorigenesis, with significant enrichment observed in patients with advanced HCC; therefore, it represents an ideal therapeutic target (3). Furthermore, heterogeneity exists in HCC tumor cells, and mutations in TP53 can act as trunk mutations that drive cancer development and progression (4). In addition to mediating cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and senescence, new studies have identified roles of p53 in metabolism, ferroptosis, immunity, and other processes contributing to tumor suppression (5). While the wild-type P53 gene functions as a tumor suppressor, mutant P53 exhibits oncogenic activity. P53-mutated HCC is mainly diagnosed through liver biopsy, an invasive procedure with complications. Thus, predicting P53-mutated HCC using non-invasive methods is of great significance.

Radiomics transforms digital medical images into actionable data by extracting quantitative features based on shape, intensity, texture, and volume in a high-throughput manner, and then analyzing these data to develop models that enhance diagnostic, prognostic, and predictive accuracy (6). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a widely used non-invasive method for examining HCC, offering high soft tissue resolution along with information such as lesion size, shape, and margins. Recent studies have shown that MRI-based radiomics can predict the histological differentiation grade of HCC (7). In addition, some scholars have constructed radiomics-based nomogram to distinguish pathologic types of HCC (8) and to predict microvascular invasion (MVI) preoperatively (9). While some studies focus on tumor internal structures, recent findings indicate that the tumor periphery also provides complementary information on lesion development. Ma et al. extended distances of 8, 10, and 12 mm beyond the tumor border to assess different regions for predicting MVI in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), finding volume of interest (VOI)10mm (including tumor and 10 mm peritumoral region) showed the best predictive performance (10). We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1579/rc).

Methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University (No. B2021-113R), and this study was granted a waiver for the informed consent requirement due to the retrospective study.

Patient selection

We conducted a retrospective analysis of 1,010 consecutive patients with HCC at Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University, from January 2020 to February 2021. All patients underwent surgical resection and had postoperative P53 immunohistochemistry and had no evidence of preoperative extrahepatic metastasis. The inclusion criteria (Figure 1) were as follows: (I) primary HCC with no prior treatment; (II) a single lesion; (III) MRI within 30 days before surgery, with satisfactory image quality and no obvious artifacts; (IV) complete clinical data; and (V) lesion diameter between 10 and 100 mm. Ultimately, 320 patients met these criteria and were randomly divided in a 7:3 ratio to the training and validation cohorts.

Study flowchart of the enrolled patients. HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma; MRI, magnetic resonance imaging.

Clinicopathological characteristics

We collected baseline clinical data, including age, gender, liver-function parameters, serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA 19-9). Pathological examination, including immunohistochemistry, was performed to determine the Edmondson-Steiner grade and the presence of MVI. Patients were assigned to P53-mutated HCC (P53+) and non-P53-mutated HCC (P53−) groups (11).

MRI technique

All patients underwent Gd-DTPA-enhanced liver MRI. Patients were scanned using the following MRI scanners: a Magnetom Aera 1.5T imager (Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) for 53 patients; a 3.0T UIHMR770 scanner (United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China) for 92 patients; a Magnetom Avanto 1.5T imager (Siemens Healthcare) for 40 patients; a 1.5T UIHMR560 scanner (United Imaging Healthcare) for 74 patients; and a Magnetom Verio 3.0T MRI System (Siemens Healthcare) for 12 patients; a Prisma 3.0T MRI System (Siemens Healthcare) was used for 24 patients, and an Ingenia CX3.0 MRI System (Philips Medical Systems, Best, Netherlands) for 25 patients. Gd-DTPA was injected intravenously at 2 mL/s for a total dose of 0.1 mmol/kg. The protocol comprised axial T2-weighted imaging with fat suppression (T2WI-FS), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), in-phase and opposed-phase T1-weighted imaging (IP-OP T1WI), axial pre-contrast three-dimensional volumetric interpolated breath-hold examination T1-weighted imaging with fat suppression (3D-VIBE T1WI), and post-contrast 3D-VIBE T1WI obtained at the arterial phase (AP; 20–30 s), portal venous phase (PVP; 60–70 s), and delayed phase (DP; 180 s). Detailed parameters are provided in Table S1.

Imaging analyses

Images were retrospectively analyzed by two radiologists (J. W. and C.Y.), with 6 and 15 years of abdominal imaging experience, respectively. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus discussion. Based on the Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) 2018 criteria (12), the following features were assessed on Gd-DTPA-enhanced MRI: (I) major features—enhancement patterns [non-rim arterial phase hyperenhancement (APHE)], washout patterns (none, non-peripheral, peripheral), tumor size, and enhancing capsule; (II) ancillary features—corona enhancement, mosaic architecture, nodule-in-nodule architecture, intratumoral hemorrhage, and fat deposition. Additional findings included restricted diffusion (present or absent), hepatic capsule retraction, and delayed central enhancement.

Radiomics analysis

Workflow

The workflow of radiomics analysis included tumor segmentation and expansion, feature extraction and selection, model development and analysis (Figure 2).

Study flowchart of the radiomics analysis. DCA, decision curve analysis; LASSO, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator; LR, logistic regression; MLP, multilayer perceptron; ROC, receiver operating characteristic; ROI, region of interest; T1WI-AP, T1-weighted imaging arterial phase.

Imaging preprocessing, region of interest (ROI) segmentation, and peritumor region expansion

All images were resampled to a voxel spacing of 1×1×1 mm3 before outlining the ROI. The entire tumor area was manually delineated in ITK-SNAP by an abdominal radiologist with 6 years of experience and validated by a senior abdominal radiologist with 15 years of experience, who manually depicted the tumor boundaries on each consecutive transverse slice and automatically generated 3D-ROIs. The 3D-ROI was drawn on six sequences: DWI (b-value =500 s/mm2), T2WI-FS, 3D-VIBE T1WI, T1WI-AP, T1WI-PVP, and T1WI-DP. Furthermore, a base tumor was automatically expanded by a software program to include a peritumor region of 9, 11, and 13 mm, which was then manually adjusted to ensure it accurately represented the tumor’s expansion and to exclude surrounding non-tumor tissue (extrahepatic tissues) (Figure S1).

Feature extraction and selection

To assess reproducibility of ROI delineation and expansion, MRI from 30 randomly selected HCC cases were re-outlined by radiologists after 1 week. Feature selection followed a two-step process. First, to minimize redundancy, Spearman’s rank correlation was applied to remove features with correlation coefficients >0.9. Second, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model was used to construct the signature from the discovery dataset. The optimal λ value was determined by 10-fold cross-validation, applying the minimum criteria approach and selecting the λ value that yielded the lowest cross-validation error.

Individual and fusion radiomics model development

Following LASSO feature selection, the final features were incorporated into multiple ML models, including logistic regression (LR), ExtraTrees, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) classifiers, to construct a risk model. Six MRI sequence models were developed using the optimized features of each sequence. From these, the most effective MRI sequences and ML models were selected for intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics modeling. Four models—intratumoral, IntraPeri_9mm, IntraPeri_11mm, and IntraPeri_13mm—were then established, and their prediction probabilities were compared to identify the optimal IntraPeri model, which integrates intratumoral tissue with varying peritumoral regions. A radiomics nomogram was subsequently constructed by combining radiomics and clinical signatures. Calibration efficiency was assessed using calibration curves and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, while decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the clinical utility of the predictive models.

Statistical analysis

Independent-samples t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, χ2 test, or Fisher exact test was used to compare baseline characteristics between the training cohort (TC) and validation cohort (VC). Independent clinical and imaging predictors of P53-mutated HCC were identified by univariate and multivariate analyses. The DeLong test was used to compare areas under the curve (AUCs) among models. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of each model. A two-tailed P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinicoradiologic profiles of patients

This study included 320 patients, comprising 259 men and 61 women. In the VC (n=96), 46 patients had P53− HCC and 50 had P53+ HCC. In the TC (n=224), 91 patients had P53− HCC and 133 had P53+ HCC. Patient characteristics and P53 status for both cohorts are shown in Tables 1,2. Multivariate analysis identified the following as independent predictors of P53-mutated HCC (Table 3): LTD [P=0.007; odds ratio (OR) =1.027, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.011–1.044], Edmondson-Steiner grade (P<0.001; OR =6.788, 95% CI 3.320–13.888), and rim-APHE (P=0.007; OR =3.429, 95% CI 1.611–7.294).

Radiomics features

The selected radiomics features comprised first-order statistics, shape descriptors, and texture matrices: gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), gray-level size-zone matrix (GLSZM), gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM), neighborhood gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM), and gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM). Ultimately, the intratumoral and IntraPeri fusion models generated 1,197 and 2,394 features per patient, respectively. Detailed feature numbers are provided in Table S2.

Performance of radiomics characterization of individual MRI sequences and different ML classifiers

The AUC values of different ML classifiers across the six single-sequence models are shown in Table 4, and the corresponding ROC curves are provided in Figure S2. Each sequence-based radiomics model was evaluated with different ML classifiers. In the TC, the ExtraTrees classifier yielded higher AUC values across all sequences than LR and MLP. Consequently, ExtraTrees was selected for further analysis. Among MRI sequences, only the T1WI-AP sequence achieved an AUC of >0.7 in the TC and >0.6 in the VC. Therefore, ExtraTrees with T1WI-AP was identified as the optimal model for subsequent intratumoral and peritumoral analysis. The DeLong test results for different sequences and ML classifiers are presented in Figure S3.

Development and verification of the nomogram

To assess the diagnostic efficacy of the clinical + radiomics models, nomogram were used for visualization (Figure 3A). The predictive nomogram for P53-mutated HCC showed strong performance in both the TC (AUC =0.815, 95% CI: 0.758–0.872) and VC (AUC =0.802, 95% CI: 0.713–0.890), as presented in Table 5. Calibration curves demonstrated close agreement between predicted and actual P53 status in both cohorts (Figure 3B,3C). DCA indicated that integrating clinical parameters, the IntraPeri_11mm model, and the nomogram provided net clinical benefit, with the nomogram showing the greatest benefit at risk thresholds above 0.3 in the TC and 0.4 in the VC (Figure 3D,3E). These findings highlight the diagnostic utility of the nomogram, which integrates radiomics features from MRI with clinical risk factors in the IntraPeri_11mm region, achieving high accuracy in identifying P53-mutated HCC and potentially reducing the need for invasive intrahepatic nodal biopsies.

Discussion

In this study, we developed a radiomics nomogram integrating optimal MRI sequences, an ML classifier, and an IntraPeri fusion model for preoperative prediction of P53-mutated HCC. Multivariate analysis identified LTD, Edmondson-Steiner grade, and rim-APHE as independent risk factors, which were incorporated into the clinical model. The most predictive MRI sequence and ML classifier—T1WI-AP and ExtraTrees—were used to establish the IntraPeri_11mm model as the optimal radiomics model, combining the main tumor region with an 11 mm peritumoral region for stable prediction. The integrated clinical and radiomics model achieved superior accuracy, with an AUC of 0.815 in the TC and 0.802 in the VC, outperforming individual models. A nomogram was then constructed to visualize this combined model, and its clinical utility was confirmed through DCA.

Previous studies have applied different ML classifiers to evaluate HCC differentiation (7), MVI in ICC (13), and response to HCC monotherapy (14). In this investigation, we compared the performance of three ML classifiers—ExtraTrees, LR, and MLP—in HCC diagnosis. ExtraTrees, an ensemble learning method, outperformed LR and MLP by constructing multiple decision trees and aggregating their predictions to improve accuracy. It also provided faster training and greater randomness, reducing overfitting and variance. In contrast, LR, a basic linear ML model, showed lower accuracy, while MLP, an artificial neural network with strong nonlinear fitting capacity, was prone to overfitting and required longer training. Selection of the T1WI-AP sequence was based on its ability to capture hepatic arterial supply, which produces marked signal enhancement during the arterial phase. This enhancement helps distinguish HCC from other liver lesions and assess tumor boundaries, peritumoral regions, and possible invasion of adjacent structures. The combined use of T1WI-AP with the ExtraTrees classifier may therefore substantially improve diagnostic accuracy and precision for detecting P53-mutated HCC.

In HCC, larger tumor diameters are associated with poorer survival and higher risk of recurrence after resection (15). Studies have shown an inverse relationship between tumor size and differentiation, suggesting that greater invasiveness and infiltrative growth accompany increased size (16). The P53 gene, when mutated, not only loses its tumor-suppressive function but also promotes tumorigenesis through multiple mechanisms, including stimulation of HCC cell proliferation (17). In this study, both LTD and Edmondson-Steiner grade emerged as significant risk factors for P53-mutated HCC. This association may reflect the ability of P53 mutations to disrupt the regulation of cellular proliferation, accelerating tumor growth and increasing size, and rapid expansion outpaces the blood supply and promotes intratumoral heterogeneity. Prior research has reported that intense marginal enhancement on imaging indicates high-grade malignancy and poor differentiation in HCC, which correlates with early recurrence and worse prognosis due to extensive necrosis (18,19). This feature reflects enhanced tumor aggressiveness, driven by survival and proliferation of cells affected by P53 mutations, which produce distinctive imaging patterns. Consistent with prior studies, the present work found rim-APHE more common in P53-mutated HCC (20) and identified it as a risk factor for this subtype. At the imaging level, P53-mutated HCC has been shown to increase vascular endothelial growth factor expression, creating a pro-angiogenic microenvironment that promotes new blood vessel formation at the tumor edge to support rapid expansion. This peripheral hypervascularity, together with central necrosis resulting from hypoxia and rapid proliferation, appears on MRI as rim-APHE—a pattern associated with aggressive tumor behavior and poor differentiation. Collectively, larger tumor diameter, higher Edmondson-Steiner grade, and rim-APHE may reflect related features of a common P53-mutant molecular process driven by uncontrolled proliferation, loss of differentiation, and abnormal blood vessel formation.

Genetic heterogeneity is a hallmark of tumors, shaping their behavior and therapeutic response. Radiomics, through quantification of imaging features, can predict this heterogeneity and correlate it with gene expression patterns (21). TP53 mutations reshape the tumor immune microenvironment by promoting an immunosuppressive niche that drives tumor-associated macrophages toward the M2 phenotype, thereby establishing a self-reinforcing, pro-tumorigenic microenvironment (22) and enhancing tumor neoangiogenesis (23). Ultrasound-based radiomics of peritumoral tissues has demonstrated value in predicting HCC pathology, underscoring the role of these tissues in tumor progression, where P53 mutations are also found (24). Enhanced MRI-based peritumoral radiomics within 1cm margin can predict early recurrence in HCC without MVI (25), and models combining intratumoral and peritumoral features perform well in predicting first transarterial chemoembolization response in intermediate-stage HCC, offering a valuable tool for patient selection (26). Furthermore, Zhang et al. demonstrated that gadoxetate disodium-enhanced MRI-based habitat imaging within peritumoral regions of 3, 5, and 7 mm could effectively evaluate MVI and recurrence-free survival in HCC (≤5 cm), with the 3 mm peritumoral habitat emerging as an independent risk factor for MVI (27). These findings emphasize the importance of peritumoral tissues and the complex interplay between tumor cells and their microenvironment. Liu et al. developed a multiparametric MRI-based radiomics model integrating intratumoral and peritumoral features to predict HCC differentiation, with the 10 mm peritumoral margin demonstrating the optimal predictive performance (16). This heterogeneity across studies indicates that the optimal peritumoral margin is likely context-dependent, varying with the specific biological endpoint (e.g., MVI, recurrence, or molecular subtype), imaging modality, and underlying tumor biology. Accordingly, we strategically selected 9, 11, and 13 mm to bracket the established 10 mm benchmark, thereby enabling a systematic evaluation of whether a margin narrower than, equal to, or exceeding this consensus value would more accurately capture the invasive microenvironment characteristic of P53-mutated HCC. To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first to combine a nomogram with clinical and radiomics data, showing robust performance across cohorts. The IntraPeri_11mm model, focusing on an 11 mm peritumoral region, proved most accurate for predicting P53-mutated HCC. This may reflect the 13 mm region’s inclusion of excess normal tissue, increasing heterogeneity, or the 9 mm region’s failure to capture focal involvement, limiting analysis. These results suggest that the choice of peritumoral region strongly affects radiomics prediction.

Studies on MRI-based prediction of P53 mutations in HCC remain scarce. Jia et al. (28) developed a deep learning model in 312 patients but did not evaluate the peritumoral region. In our prior dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI study (29), conventional radiomics performed poorly in the validation cohort (AUC, 0.519), and even the combination of deep learning with clinical features achieved only modest validation performance (AUC, 0.702), highlighting the challenges of feature instability and limited regional information. Building on these findings, the present study is the first to adopt an intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics strategy for predicting P53-mutated HCC. An optimal peritumoral margin of 11 mm was identified, and the resulting nomogram achieved an AUC of 0.802 in the validation cohort, with a minimal discrepancy of 0.013 between the training and validation cohorts—indicating superior generalizability compared with our previous model. These results demonstrate that integrating peritumoral microenvironmental information is critical to enhancing the predictive value of conventional radiomics and provide novel imaging evidence for understanding the invasive margins associated with P53-mutated HCC.

The IntraPeri_11mm radiomics model exhibited a notable performance drop between the training and internal validation cohorts (AUC: 0.805 vs. 0.660), suggesting insufficient generalizability likely attributable to overfitting within a high-dimensional feature space derived from a limited sample size, coupled with inherent scanner heterogeneity across seven multi-vendor platforms. The modest validation sample n=96 further contributed to statistical uncertainty, as reflected by the wide CI (95% CI: 0.550–0.770). In contrast, the clinical model demonstrated superior generalization stability (AUC: 0.790 vs. 0.776), underscoring the robustness of conventional clinicopathological predictors. Notably, integrating these clinical variables with the IntraPeri_11mm radiomics signature yielded a nomogram with excellent and consistent discrimination across both cohorts (AUCTC =0.815, 95% CI: 0.758–0.872; AUCVC =0.802, 95% CI: 0.713–0.890). These findings highlight that combining established clinical risk factors with radiomics features can effectively mitigate overfitting; nevertheless, prospective external multi-center validation using harmonized imaging protocols remains essential to confirm the model’s clinical utility.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design introduces potential selection bias. Second, we assumed that P53 immunohistochemistry results reflect TP53 mutations based on nuclear staining percentage; however, confirmation with genetic testing is needed to improve accuracy. Third, although we extracted intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics features of P53-mutated HCC, long-term follow-up is required to establish associations with outcomes and survival. Fourth, by focusing on the T1WI-AP sequence for peritumoral radiomics, we may have overlooked important features from other sequences. Fifth, limiting analysis to 9, 11, and 13 mm peritumoral regions highlights the need to examine additional ROIs. Lastly, the use of seven 1.5 and 3.0 T MRI scanners from different manufacturers with varying parameters could have introduced variability into the results; therefore, prospective external multi-center validation using standardized acquisition protocols is warranted to confirm the reproducibility and clinical utility of the proposed radiomics nomogram.

Conclusions

We developed and validated a nomogram integrating LTD, Edmondson-Steiner grade, rim-APHE, and a radiomics model based on T1WI-AP sequences within the IntraPeri_11mm region. This tool predicts preoperative P53 mutation status in HCC and supports personalized treatment planning.

Beauty and the beast: an experimental study on the effect of Instagram food posts featuring a celebrity vs. without a celebrity on the eating intention of healthy vs. unhealthy food
Authors: Amber Sajjad, Fahad Mansoor Pasha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFood marketing across all social media sites globally is dominated by the promotion of unhealthy food products such as snacks, confectionery, and fast food. This vast exposure to unhealthy food marketing across social media has contributed to the overconsumption of unhealthy food. Studies investigating the effect of the popularity of food images on food intake have produced mixed results, with some studies reporting no effect on food intake. The literature provides strong evidence of the influence of celebrities on food consumption and eating behavior. This research focuses on digital food posts with and without celebrity appearance and their role in nudging healthy eating. This research also tested the mediating role of celebrity-self congruency and the moderating role of celebrity attractiveness and involvement with healthy food.MethodsAn online experiment 2 (with celebrity vs. without-celebrity) x 2 (healthy vs. unhealthy food items) between-subjects design was conducted to test the effect of a celebrity’s Instagram posts featuring healthy vs. unhealthy food products on eating intention and digital engagement with food posts. Four hundred and thirty-two undergraduate students participated in this study in exchange for a cash incentive. The students were enrolled in a higher education institution in Lahore, Pakistan. 48% of participants were female, and 52% of them were male. Demographics of the participants represented Generation Z.ResultsWe found that the Instagram posts of unhealthy and healthy food images without a celebrity (vs. with a celebrity) led to higher eating intention and digital engagement. The mediation effect of celebrity congruency on eating intention and digital engagement with food posts was non-significant. The moderated mediating effect of celebrity attractiveness on digital engagement with food posts was significant, such that the high level of attractiveness leads to higher digital engagement. The moderated mediated effect of involvement with healthy food on eating intention was significant, such that the high level of involvement leads to higher eating intention.DiscussionThe findings showed that the wide influence of celebrities has an impact on eating intention and digital engagement. While this effect is significant, the aesthetically appealing food posts without featuring celebrities have a stronger impact on eating intention and digital engagement. Digital engagement has emerged as a significant online tool to promote healthy food consumption. We suggest that the promotion of healthy foods could be more successful on social media platforms when using attractive celebrities.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25208-4.Keywords: Foodporn, Instagram, Eating intention, Digital engagement, Celebrity self-congruency, Celebrity attractiveness, Involvement with healthy food

Background

The ever-increasing rate of smartphone owners (86%) and social media users (93%) globally [1] has resulted in the massive creation and distribution of digital images on social media platforms such as Instagram. Young adults frequently take food photos in different scenarios, such as restaurant meals, street food, food festivals and homemade dishes, and post those photos on social media. This has led to the “camera eats first” movement, a global phenomenon in which people take pictures of every meal before they eat and upload these pictures on their social media [2, 3]. Digital images of food have stood out as one of the top categories of photos shared on Instagram [4]. Taking pictures of food and with food while dining is recognized to be a part of human interaction with food [5]. As a result, Instagram has emerged as a new and trendy way of finding variety in food products and consumption. Food choices are now based on the digital foodscape defined as “the world of food that we inhabit, shaping what we eat, how we relate to food, and how and by whom food knowledge is produced and circulated across digital spaces” [6] and aesthetically appealing social media food posts [7, 8].

The abundance of appealing food images and aesthetic photography available online – commonly referred to as “food porn” – has become a prominent feature of the digital food experience [9]The widely circulated food photos online, and navigating for food menu pictures online before dining, have become an integral part of social media visual culture [10]. The aesthetically appealing digital food pictures of high-calorie unhealthy food (the authors metaphorically call it ‘the beast’) portray a beautiful picture that successfully appeals to the viewers’ taste perception, hunger arousal and food cravings [11, 12]. This combination of beauty and the beast has led to unhealthy eating behaviors such as overeating [13–15] and increasing obesity rates in the population worldwide.

Digital images of food widely shared on social media act as visual cues for the human brain and have a profound effect on food intake and eating behaviors [16]. A recent study found that out of 1000 images on Instagram, up to 70% were images of high-calorie, unhealthy food, and only 21% of low-calorie, nutritious food [17, 18] found that high-calorie food images with appeasing visual aesthetics generate the most likes and shares on social media platforms. Through a series of experiments [19], show that socially endorsed and aesthetically beautiful pictures of food (food posts) gain users’ attention and a high level of engagement on social media. They also conclude that such food posts can be an effective measure to encourage healthy eating. According to [20], visual cues related to food predict increased recall of food marketing, food cravings, and purchase decisions in young adults. These results imply that consumers’ reactions to digital food posts are in line with the “food cue reactivity theory,” which posits that exposure to visual cues related to food, such as food posts on social media, causes cue-induced cravings [21].

The majority of adolescents’ Instagram food posts are exceedingly dominated by high-calorie foods (e.g. chocolate cake, pizza, cookies, and pastry) as compared to the images of healthy foods such as salad [17, 22, 23]. In a recent review of food marketing [24], reviewed more than fifty studies and found that the majority of food advertisements (> 60%) promoted unhalthy food. Many countries have developed policies to control and regulate the massive advertising of unhealthy foods on traditional media such as television, but those policies have largely failed to restrict the marketing of unhealthy food on social media sites [23]. This vast exposure to unhealthy food marketing across social media sites has contributed to health inequalities in such a way that unhealthy food is overconsumed [25].

In response to food marketing on well-known digital platforms, 72% of adult and adolescent survey participants said they had seen its advertisement; 14% said they started craving the product after seeing it, and 8% bought it [20]. In the same manner, Instagram food posts depicting healthy food products packaged as beautiful food images could help promote healthy eating intentions [10]. Therefore, Instagram is an appropriate platform to examine how social media posts featuring food may alter eating behavior. To tackle the rising obesity rates worldwide, upgrading the digital marketing and visuals of healthy food products has become increasingly relevant. The widespread marketing of unhealthy food products must be fought against by public health initiatives to support healthy eating habits and lifestyles. The U.S. Institute of Medicine advises counter-marketing, or the use of effective advertising strategies to market healthy food, such as visually appealing branding of healthy goods.

Celebrity endorsements and eating intention

For decades, people have been inspired by celebrities in the entertainment industry, including movie actors, singers, musicians and sports personalities. Marketers have used celebrity endorsement (celebrities in sports and music) as a tool to promote unhealthy foods, mainly categorized as energy-dense and nutrient-poor (EDNP) products such as sugary beverages, candy and fast-food meals [26] to children [27, 28], adolescents and young adults [29]. People make efforts to know about the personal lives of celebrities and alter their consumption patterns inspired by their lifestyles. Social media platforms have become an avenue for the public to follow celebrities online. This has made it easy for fans to follow celebrities and consume the products featured on celebrities’ personal social media posts. The literature provides strong evidence of the influence of celebrities on food consumption and eating behavior [29–32]. Experimental studies conducted in the U.S., Australia and the United Kingdom (UK) have demonstrated that consumers are more likely to buy EDNP products that feature celebrities [33, 34]. Similarly, the type of food consumed by celebrities, featured on their personal social media posts, would greatly impact consumers’ eating intentions. Our study builds on the impact of celebrities on food consumption and specifically focuses on celebrities’ social media posts featuring healthy vs. unhealthy food. Few studies have investigated the impact of celebrities’ personal social media posts featuring food on the followers’ eating intention and digital engagement.

Recently, many studies have found that it is difficult for consumers to follow and understand nutritional and calorie information on food intake [35]. Certainly, the calorie disclosure and healthy food labels are not very effective in curbing rising obesity rates because of the health-halo effect. However, the impact of celebrities’ posts highlighting food consumption as a tool to promote healthy eating is a much-overlooked area of research. This research focuses on digital food posts with and without celebrity appearance and their role in nudging healthy eating. The virality of food posts on social media and the increasing buying and eating of viral food products have made our research question as relevant as ever. This understanding is vital to promoting healthy dietary goals and translating them into consumers’ eating behavior.

Theoretical framework

Building on Congruency Theory, this study explores how Instagram food posts featuring a widely recognized celebrity, compared to those without a celebrity influence eating intention and digital engagement. According to the self-congruency theory, people are more likely to buy and use goods that support their actual or ideal self-concept [36, 37]. People will, therefore, act in a way that may preserve their actual selves and fulfil their self-consistency motive, which is the propensity for people to act in ways that are consistent with their self-perceptions and preserve their actual selves [36]. This theory is particularly relevant to our study in understanding how the presence of a celebrity and their trait of attractiveness can drastically affect the intention to consume food, especially in the context of promoting healthy eating. Drawing from the foundations of self-congruency theory, we assert that congruence between a celebrity and a consumer plays a vital role in promoting eating intention.

Main effects of celebrity’s presence in food posts on eating intention and digital engagement

Consumer engagement with brands across different digital technologies and platforms, such as apps, websites, blogs, and social media platforms, is broadly defined as digital engagement [38]. Digital engagement on social media involves the use of likes, comments, sharing, and use of hashtags to engage with digital posts [39]. Food posts with the most likes and comments are shared and liked by young social media users [40]. Liking and sharing of food posts on these platforms influence eating behavior [40].

According to [41], adolescents’ and young adults’ eating behavior is heavily influenced by food posts and healthy food posts can potentially nudge healthy eating. More recently [42], concluded that exposure to Instagram food posts influences eating intention and overall eating behavior [43]. found that Instagram posts featuring celebrities directly affect purchase intention and brand attitude. Food images featuring a person, such as a social media influencer or a celebrity, receive greater attention and digital engagement on social media [39] Few studies have investigated the impact of celebrities’ personal social media posts featuring food on the followers’ eating intention and digital engagement. We aim to delve into this intricate relationship and test the following hypotheses;

H1: Celebrity Instagram food post (healthy/unhealthy food) will invoke greater eating intention to eat the food featured in the post as compared to the food post without a celebrity.

H1: Celebrity Instagram food post (healthy/unhealthy food) will invoke greater eating intention to eat the food featured in the post as compared to the food post without a celebrity.

H2: Celebrity Instagram food post (healthy/unhealthy food) will generate a greater level of digital engagement with the food featured in the post as compared to the food post without a celebrity.

H2: Celebrity Instagram food post (healthy/unhealthy food) will generate a greater level of digital engagement with the food featured in the post as compared to the food post without a celebrity.

Mediating effect of celebrity-self congruency

The degree to which the celebrity’s and the customer’s actual self-aligns with the celebrity is known as actual self-congruence [44]. Buying a product recommended by someone similar to the actual self is a significant way to build and improve one’s self-image [45]. The effectiveness of product endorsements is increased when endorser traits and customers’ self-concepts are congruent, since this allows consumers to relate to the endorser more easily [44]. This perceived similarity leads to a positive attitude and increased purchase intention towards the recommended product or brand [46–48]. Liu [47] assessed the impact of consumers’ real self-congruence with social media celebrities and discovered positive effects on consumer behavior. Since people tend to identify their actual selves with celebrities they follow on social media, we propose that celebrity-self congruency plays a mediating role in promoting eating intention and digital engagement with food posts featuring celebrities.

H3:Celebrity-self congruence will mediate the effect of Celebrity Instagram’s post featuring food on eating intention.

H3:Celebrity-self congruence will mediate the effect of Celebrity Instagram’s post featuring food on eating intention.

H4:Celebrity-self congruence will mediate the effect of Celebrity Instagram’s post featuring food on digital engagement.

H4:Celebrity-self congruence will mediate the effect of Celebrity Instagram’s post featuring food on digital engagement.

Moderating effect of celebrity attractiveness

According to [49], attractiveness is “the stereotype of favorable associations with a person and encompasses not just physical appearance but also traits like personality and athletic ability”. Because of their associated personal qualities — attractiveness, likeability, popularity, and believability — celebrities have been used for brand endorsements [50]. According to [51], a source’s attractiveness has a direct impact on how effective a communication message is. People are influenced by attractive endorsers, and they are more inclined to believe information from attractive sources.

Attractive endorsers more easily change beliefs [51]. Purchase intentions are more likely to result from endorsers who are perceived as beautiful and attractive [52]. In a similar vein, appealing and attractive personalities in commercial ads increase positive attitudes about products and encourage people to make purchases [53].

Consumers show greater affection towards physically attractive celebrities as compared to unattractive ones [54]. Celebrity attractiveness results in a positive attitude towards products and purchase intention [29, 53]. Therefore, the following hypothesis statement can be derived from the above literature:

H5:Celebrity attractiveness will moderate the effect of celebrity self-congruence on eating intention, such that a higher level of attractiveness would strengthen the impact.

H5:Celebrity attractiveness will moderate the effect of celebrity self-congruence on eating intention, such that a higher level of attractiveness would strengthen the impact.

H6: Celebrity attractiveness will moderate the effect of celebrity self-congruence on digital engagement, such that a higher level of attractiveness would strengthen the impact.

H6: Celebrity attractiveness will moderate the effect of celebrity self-congruence on digital engagement, such that a higher level of attractiveness would strengthen the impact.

Moderating effect of involvement with healthy food

The term “involvement” refers to the degree of consumer interest, the degree of search intensity, and the intricacy of the decision-making process for an object (such as healthy food). Any situation, product or service could be the object of involvement. High involvement is indicated by high consumer engagement in decision-making (Wang et al., 2020), whereas low involvement suggests inertia [55]. Reimann et al. [56] suggests that high involvement significantly affects decision-making and promotes interactive communications. The strong effect of involvement on decision-making is rooted in the psychology of consumers, as the product of interest holds great relevance and importance to the consumer [57]. The consumers’ belief that the product class satisfies significant values is the source of this interest. A product which holds the interest of the consumer is likely to be evaluated thoughtfully due to its psychological consequences. Therefore, the level of involvement improves product assessment and becomes a part of product evaluation [58]. In our study, we focus on the involvement with healthy food. The greater the involvement with healthy food, the greater the engagement with Instagram posts featuring healthy food. A similar pattern will exist when the consumers’ involvement with unhealthy food is high. Therefore, we propose the next hypotheses as follows;

H7: Involvement with healthy food will moderate the effect of Celebrity self-congruence on eating intention, such that the higher level of involvement with healthy food would strengthen the impact of celebrity-self congruence on eating intention and vice versa.

H7: Involvement with healthy food will moderate the effect of Celebrity self-congruence on eating intention, such that the higher level of involvement with healthy food would strengthen the impact of celebrity-self congruence on eating intention and vice versa.

H8: Involvement with healthy food will moderate the effect of Celebrity self-congruence on digital engagement, such that the higher level of involvement with healthy food would strengthen the impact of celebrity-self congruence on digital engagement and vice versa.

H8: Involvement with healthy food will moderate the effect of Celebrity self-congruence on digital engagement, such that the higher level of involvement with healthy food would strengthen the impact of celebrity-self congruence on digital engagement and vice versa.

Methodology

Experiment design

An online experiment 2 (with celebrity vs. without-celebrity) x 2 (healthy vs. unhealthy food items) between-subjects design was conducted to test the effect of a celebrity’s Instagram posts featuring healthy vs. unhealthy food products on eating intention and digital engagement with food posts. The four experiment conditions showed images of healthy and unhealthy food posts with and without a celebrity to the participants (i.e., an Instagram post of a celebrity with a healthy food, an Instagram post of a celebrity with unhealthy food, an Instagram food post featuring unhealthy food without a celebrity, and an Instagram food post featuring a healthy food without a celebrity). Two Instagram posts of a popular celebrity, one showing a healthy food with the celebrity and the other showing an unhealthy food with the celebrity, were shown in the conditions of “with celebrity”. Participants were randomly assigned to one of the four conditions. Participants in each condition saw one food post and completed the survey afterwards. Appendix 1 shows the stimulus images shown in the four conditions. A pretest was carried out to select the celebrities’ Instagram posts featuring healthy vs. unhealthy food products. This pretest was done to ensure that the respondents recognize and follow the celebrities on Instagram.

Recruitment of participants

The sample size for each condition in the experiment was calculated with the G power method. A priori power analysis was conducted to determine the sample size for a 2 × 2 between-subjects ANOVA. Using Cohen’s d size 0.25 to detect a medium-sized effect a minimum of 105 participants (per condition) were needed to achieve 0.90 statistical power with alpha 0.5. However, to avoid the risk of reduced statistical power due to the elimination of unusable responses and participants’ lack of attention, we decided to recruit more participants in each condition. Therefore, we recruited 120 participants in each condition. The survey was administered to a sample of four hundred and eighty students in four groups studying at a higher education institute in Lahore city. Clinical trial number: not applicable.

This study collected data from active Instagram users between the ages of 18–25 years, as these demographics actively use Instagram. This age bracket al.so represents Generation Z (Gen Z). We focused on Gen Z in our study for several reasons. They represent the latest generation, born between 1995 and the early 2010 s [69]. Generation Z, frequently regarded as “digital natives”, is the first generation to have been raised in an environment saturated with digital communication [70]. This demographic engages with social media content more than any other age group, dedicating approximately 11 h daily to reading, liking, and sharing material across all digital devices [68]. They comprise the majority of social media users worldwide and have become the main audience for digital advertising on social media platforms. They access Instagram a minimum of five times daily [71] and a maximum of 20 times a day [72]. This target group favors visual communication, unlike the previous generation, which preferred text and sought innovation. In light of this, the bulk of academics have focused on Generation Z to examine the influence of social media advertising and marketing on their consuming behavior [73–77].

As of 2023, there are 87.35 million internet users in Pakistan and 71.70 million people in Pakistan actively use social media (datareportal.com). 7.7% (12.95 million users) of the total population of Pakistan between the age of 18–24 actively use Instagram. We also focus on this age bracket because the majority of Pakistan’s population lies in this age range. 64% of the country’s total population comprises young adults of the age of under 30 years and almost 30% are between the ages of 16 to 29 years1 These figures indicate that Pakistan is an economy of young consumers belonging to Gen Z.

Procedure

This research was presented as an examination of Instagram food posts. Participants provided informed consent and were randomly assigned to one of the four experimental conditions. The online survey was directly sent to the students via the internal mailing list. The survey was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University. Stimulus materials were concurrently shown to participants with the questionnaire. Stimulus images included Instagram posts of healthy and unhealthy food with and without a celebrity’s presence (Appendix I). As a manipulation check, we asked participants to rank the food shown in the post as unhealthy/healthy (1= “very unhealthy,” and 7=“very healthy”). Respondents were asked to visualize the image (Instagram food post as stimulus) in each condition and respond to the questions. The initial section of the questionnaire had an introduction outlining the primary objective of the study. The second section comprised socio-demographic questions regarding the participant’s age, gender, education, and a question concerning the daily duration, in hours, spent on Instagram usage. The final section of the questionnaire included statements measuring the research variables, assessed using a five-point Likert scale, where 1 represented “strongly disagree” and 5 denoted “strongly agree.”

Measurement and scales

The digital visual engagement scale consists of seven items based on the “online supporting behavior” scale [59]. The measure of celebrity attractiveness is based on 5-item semantic differentials scale [51]. After showing the Instagram post image, eating intention was measured with 1 item: “Please indicate the extent to which you would like to eat the food you saw on the Instagram post”. The actual-self congruence scale is 3 items scale [60], based on Sirgy et al.’s [61], scale to measure actual self-congruence. This scale was also used by Japutra, Ekinci, and Simkin [62]. Consumer involvement with healthy food was evaluated using a 12 items scale [63], also used by Barbosa and Anana [64]. Four of the 12 questions on the scale represented involvement with the product, four represented the involvement with consumption, and another four represented involvement in purchasing decisions for that type of product. The reliability of the measures was determined using Cronbach’s alpha. For each variable, a value greater than 0.7 was achieved, fulfilling the criteria of Lee [65]. Results

Four hundred and eighty undergraduate students participated in this study in exchange for a cash incentive. Because this study included several scale measures, we included a simple attention check (“For this statement, please select ‘disagree’”). Forty-eight students failed this attention check, leaving a final sample of 432. The response rate was thus 90%. 48% of participants were female, and 52% of them were male, with 81% of respondents belonged to the age bracket of 18–21 years and 19% belonged to the age bracket of 22–25 years. A majority of respondents (98.7%) were classified as active Instagram users (time spent on Instagram per day: more than 2 h and up to 8 h a day), and only 1.3% were classified as non-active Instagram users (1–2 h a day). This categorization of active vs. non-active users of Instagram was based on criteria adopted by previous researchers. Rackley [78] classifies active Instagram users as those who spend 1 to 4 h on Instagram daily. Olejniczak [72] shows that Gen Z with a college education spends, on average, 2 h and 20 min on Instagram a day. Prilyantinasari [79] classified those as active users of Instagram from Gen Z who spend 5 h on Instagram a day. In this research, we classified those participants as active Instagram users who at least spend more than 2 h on Instagram per day.

Manipulation check

The manipulation check was significant, indicating that participants perceived the salad to be significantly healthier than the cake (Msalad=3.66, Mcake =3.23; F(3, 425) = 7.96, p < 0.01).

Interaction effect and main effects

Between-subjects 2 × 2 ANOVA was performed on the data in SPSS software to conclude the main effects of the experiment. The interaction effect of food type and celebrity presence was non-significant on eating intention; F (1,425) = 0.684, p = 0.409, partial eta squared (Ƞ2) = 0.002 and digital engagement; F (1,425) = 1.4821, p = 0.224, partial eta squared (Ƞ2) = 0.003.

We found significant main effects of food type; F(1,425) = 17.82, p = 0.000, partial eta squared = 0.040 and celebrity presence; F(1,425) = 8.77, p = 0.003, partial eta squared = 0.020, on eating intention. Similarly, we observed significant main effects of food type; F(1,425) = 6.081, p = 0.014, partial eta squared = 0.014 and celebrity presence; F(1,425) = 13.66, p = 0.000, partial eta squared = 0.031 on digital engagement. The Instagram post of an unhealthy food image without celebrity (vs. with celebrity) led to higher digital engagement (M with celebrity=2.17, SD = 0.945; M without celebrity =2.60, SD = 0.912) and higher eating intention (M with celebrity=3.26, SD = 1.029; M without celebrity =3.66, SD = 1.005). Similarly, the Instagram post of a healthy food image without a celebrity had a strong effect on eating intention (M with celebrity=2.89, SD = 1.213; M without celebrity =3.12, SD = 1.082) and digital engagement (M with celebrity=2.06, SD = 0.827; M without celebrity =2.28, SD = 0.908) as compared to the presence of celebrity. Following these results, we reject H1 and H2. Tables 2 and 3 show the results of between subjects 2 × 2 ANOVA.

Discussion

Theoretical contribution

This study tested the effect of celebrity presence in healthy and unhealthy food posts on Instagram on eating intention and digital engagement. The findings of the online experiment revealed that the food posts without celebrity presence strongly impact eating intention and digital engagement as compared to food posts with celebrity presence. We also found a non-significant mediating effect of celebrity-self congruency on eating intention and digital engagement. Firstly, our novel findings contradict the previous literature, which supports the congruency theory. Previous researchers found that when consumers perceive a celebrity similar to their self-concept, subsequently they evaluate the ad and the brand with celebrity endorsement more favorably with higher purchase intention [44–80]. Celebrity food endorsements have been shown to significantly enhance purchase intention of the endorsed food, and generally, consumers take it as a food recommendation made by a celebrity [30]. In an extensive review of carefully selected sixty-nine experimental studies (1964–2014) investigating the effect of social models such as celebrities on food choices and intake decisions Cruwys et al. [81] reported that the intake of healthy food was greater when participants perceived the model as similar to themselves. Using an experimental approach, Folkvord et al. [82] found that influencer celebrities on Instagram who are perceived similarly by consumers directly lead to higher purchase intentions of healthy food products. A plausible reason for our inconsistent findings might be attributed to the unique characteristics of Gen Z. This generation, equipped with extensive information, exhibits greater pragmatism and analytical thinking in their decision-making compared to earlier generations. They particularly prioritize awareness of their surroundings and maintaining control Francis and Hoefel [69]. As food consumers, they might rely more on the recommendations and experience of other consumers about the food rather than on the celebrity’s recommendations of food. Another plausible explanation could be attributed to varied experience of food purchase and consumption, as food products are consumed and purchased very frequently by everyone on an almost daily basis. Consumer might rely more on their own experience with food rather than celebrities’ recommendations.

Secondly, we tested for the moderating effect of celebrity attractiveness and found a non-significant effect on eating intention. In food endorsements, celebrity attractiveness was found to have no effect on food purchase intention [30]. One possible reason for the non-significant effect of celebrity attractiveness is that the attractiveness of the celebrity endorser predominantly affects consumer behavior in beauty-related products, and clearly the effect is not prevalent in food products. Our findings suggest that celebrity attractiveness strongly moderates the impact of a celebrity’s presence on digital engagement. Thirdly, we found that involvement with healthy food negatively moderates the effect of celebrity presence in unhealthy food posts, which means that as the consumer’s interest in eating healthy food increases, their intention to consume unhealthy food significantly decreases. Our results refute the implications of self-congruency theory and source attractiveness theory. It is noteworthy that the relevance of these theories can be challenged with the prevalent use of social media, personal factors, and unique characteristics of Gen Z. This study has uncovered the importance of personal factors in food consumption such as involvement level with food type.

Practical implications

This study contributes to a growing body of research which focuses on leveraging the impact of social media to nudge healthy food consumption. Social media-based intervention has the potential to provide insights into larger-scale societal behavior trends and social interactions. Given the immense number of food pictures posted every day on social media, it is imperative for healthy food marketers to have knowledge of the factors that can potentially promote positive evaluation of food based on mere pictures. This research provides insights into healthy food marketing efforts on social media, particularly on Instagram, where food posts without the celebrity’s presence had a stronger impact on eating intention. Gen Z buyers frequently prefer relatable, authentic, and unvarnished content to well-executed celebrity endorsements.

Instead of using contrived celebrity endorsements, food marketers should showcase real-life consumption experiences and behind-the-scenes preparation of food. Marketers should focus on storytelling, inventiveness, and aesthetically pleasing presentation in food posts instead of depending on the celebrity’s persona to convey the message, as non-celebrity posts outperformed those with the celebrity’s presence. Instead of using celebrity ambassadors to promote healthy eating among Gen Z, public health organizations may find greater success with relatable peers or community leaders.

As per the findings of our study, one way to promote Healthy food brands and nudge healthy eating is by strategically campaigning with attractive celebrities to increase digital engagement, which in turn may stimulate healthy eating intention. In line with existing research, this article shows that while celebrity attractiveness is a crucial factor to take into account, its significance varies depending on the kind of customers it is aiming to attract. Increasing communication through social media platforms or even recruiting well-known and attractive celebrities does not ensure success, despite the allure. Prior to spending money on content production, it’s critical to assess the level of involvement of the target audience and determine whether the celebrities have the potential to persuade them. Content marketing might be a viable option if the target audience is very interested in the product category being advertised; otherwise, other tactics might be more effective.

Limitations and future directions

First, this research adopted self-report scale to measure eating intention while not investigating the actual food choice or consumption based on food tracking. Future researchers can conduct lab experiment studies to analyze which type of food will be chosen by participants. The scope of follow-up lab studies could also go beyond the timeline, and longitudinal studies could be conducted to track food intake based on engagement with social media food posts. Our study was conducted with undergraduate students of higher education institution in Lahore city, Pakistan. These students represent Gen Z. We cannot infer that the same effects will be achieved for Millennials who are also known for active social media usage. We are also unsure if the higher digital engagement with a food post with the presence of attractive celebrity will lead towards eating intention of (un)healthy food? The future researchers could address this issue of reverse causality. Lastly, the food posts shown to participants were taken from celebrity’s official Instagram account and it featured a female celebrity. Male celebrities did not post much about food therefore we did not include male celebrity in our stimulus. Future researcher could create fictitious food posts featuring male and female celebrities to test the effect of gender of celebrity on eating intention and digital engagement.

Conclusion

This study revealed that food images uploaded and shared online strongly influence eating intention and digital engagement. The findings suggest that there is a significant relationship between celebrity attractiveness and digital engagement. Marketers and policy makers are in dire need to come up with effective methods to promote the consumption of healthy food. Our findings suggest that marketers should focus on establishing endorsements with attractive celebrities to promote healthy food products and to increase online engagement with healthy food brands. By harnessing the influence of celebrities’ presence on social media, marketers can increase online buzz about the benefits of a healthy diet. This can be an effective way to improve people’s dietary intake. The active engagement of celebrities on social media platforms can aid health interventions, considering their large online following and strong influence on the target group. The findings of this study negate the congruency theory therefore, future research should investigate how celebrities can be involved in online health campaigns to target the group which is high involved with healthy foods.

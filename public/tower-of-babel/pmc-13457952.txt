LLM-guided expert feature extraction with fusion-based complementary learning network for fish freshness classification using eye images
Authors: Resma Madhu PK, Rakesh Kumar Sidharthan, Srinivaas A, Gunasundari Raju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate, non-invasive grading of fish freshness remains challenging in dense and occluded environments, where the fish eye images serve as a reliable and consistently visible indicator of freshness. Loss of structural cues in the fish eye image often limits the existing deep learning models (DLN) from having consistent learning. To address these limitations, this work aims to develop a fusion-based complementary learning network (FCLN) that integrates expert-suggested photometric features with deep visual representations of DLN for the classification of fish freshness. Fish expert knowledge on freshness evaluation is processed using a large language model (LLM) to identify relevant visual cues and derive interpretable numeric features, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. FCLN is constructed using ResNet50 as a backbone with a numeric feature layer and a fusion layer responsible for integrating both modalities for complementary learning. Complementary learning analysis (CLA) is also formulated to validate the complementary nature of the numeric features and image embeddings of DLNs. The proposed method is evaluated using the Fish Freshness Eye (FFE) dataset, which contains multiple fish species and freshness categories. Experimental results demonstrate a reliable classification accuracy of 81.03% during testing. CLA provides the least average correlation of 0.3755, indicating the complementary nature of the numeric features. Results of ablation studies also highlight the importance of using expert knowledge with deep visual features for fish freshness classification.Keywords: Complementary learning, Fish freshness, Deep learning, LLM, Expert knowledgeSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Fish freshness assessment is one of the major requirements to ensure the quality, safety and market value in aquaculture industries. Accurate freshness evaluation helps to maintain supply chain transparency, supports regulatory compliance, and minimises economic losses caused by spoilage and rejection. Traditionally, freshness is assessed manually through visual inspection of eye clarity, gill colour, skin appearance, texture, and odour1. It is labour-intensive, time-consuming, and challenging in high-throughput production lines2. Hence, there is a need for intelligent, automated and reliable freshness evaluation methods for sustainable aquaculture management and quality assurance.

Automated fish freshness assessment has gained significant improvement as deep learning–based computer vision techniques become reliable3. Fish freshness is commonly assessed using several external morphological indicators, such as eye characteristics, gill colouration, body shape deformation, and skin texture4,5. Ocular degradation in the fish eye reflects biochemical spoilage processes such as tissue softening, blood clot formation, and loss of surface brightness6. Gills color and texture also provide valuable inferences on fish freshness. Fresh fish exhibit bright red or pink gills, while older fish show discolouration with mucus deposition. Freshness is also associated with shiny skin and firm flesh, whereas spoilage causes dull colouration and softening2.

DLN has emerged promising technique for computer vision-based fish freshness assessment7. DLNs like convolutional neural networks (CNNs), Vision transformer (ViT) and YOLO models are widely used to generate inferences on freshness using fish images8. Features extracted from DLNs are utilised with machine learning (ML) models for freshness classification, thereby eliminating the overhead and challenges associated with feature selection.4. YOLO models are widely used to learn the physical characteristics of fish eyes and skin to assess freshness5,9.

Hybridisation techniques primarily involve the design of customised architectures that integrate multiple DLNs, employ ensemble strategies, and leverage multi-modal data to enrich feature representation. Combination of CNNs with temporal models like LSTM (long short-term memory) networks has been explored to capture the time-dependent behaviour of fish freshness9,10. Attention layer-based DLNs11 and ensemble techniques12 enable focusing on relevant features required for reliable classification. Multi-modal fusion technique to integrate features from various modalities, like different sensors and multiple viewpoints13, which can provide complementary information. Feature interaction studies14,15 are also essential to better understand the contribution and effectiveness of multi-modal data.

Most of these approaches rely on CNN architectures that primarily learn implicit hierarchical representations from fish images. Although these techniques achieve reliable performance, robustness studies illustrate that deep visual representations are sensitive to photometric variations16,17. In a highly constrained environment, where acquisition of fish-eye images is only possible, global shape and contextual information are limited. Freshness discrimination depends predominantly on photometric properties such as brightness, sharpness, contrast, and colourations. These properties are traditionally assessed by human experts but are not explicitly enforced in standard CNN architectures. This limitation motivates the integration of expert-guided photometric features as complementary information to enhance robustness and interpretability in fine-grained freshness classification.

Recent literature illustrates fusion of deep-features and manually extracted features for computer vision applications has been investigated extensively18,19. Complementary information fusion from non-deep hand-crafted features enhances the fish freshness classification20. Complementary features from different modalities, like visual images and laser reflectance, provide improved performance in freshness assessment21. Motivated by these factors, the proposed work aims to fuse expert features as complementary information with the CNN architecture to enhance the freshness evaluation using fish-eye images.

Dataset description

The proposed work utilises the Fish Freshness Eye (FFE) image dataset6 comprises fish-eye images of various types of fish, as detailed in Table 1. The dataset categorises fish freshness into three distinct classes, namely highly-fresh, fresh, and not-fresh, based on storage days after harvesting. Specifically, fish classified as highly-fresh correspond to specimens stored for one to two days, while the fresh category includes fish stored for more than two days but less than four days. Fish stored for more than four days are labelled as not-fresh.

Unlike other fish freshness datasets that include the full fish body and surrounding context, the proposed dataset consists exclusively of cropped fish eye images. The dataset is motivated by the widely available fish arrangements as described in Fig. 1. Fish are stacked or placed in an arrangement where the eyes remain prominently visible, while the body and other anatomical features are partially or fully occluded. Capturing images of other freshness indicators, such as gills, often requires manual handling or repositioning of the fish, which is challenging in large-scale operations. Hence, it is preferred to evaluate fish freshness using eye-only images, which becomes a practical and scalable approach.

Sample images of typical fish arrangements in the practical scenarios.

On the other hand, the use of fish-eye images suffers from the loss of global shape, texture, and background cues commonly exploited by DLNs. In such a context, freshness assessment relies predominantly on the photometric attributes such as brightness, contrast, sharpness, and clarity, which are derived as numeric features. Although DLNs are capable of learning hierarchical representations, prior studies have shown that standard CNNs do not inherently encode absolute photometric properties22. Empirical analyses have demonstrated that CNN representations are sensitive to photometric degradations23. This motivates the need for integration of expert-guided photometric features as complementary information to compensate for the limitations of DLNs and enable reliable freshness assessment using fish eye images.

The FFE dataset inherently exhibits variability in acquisition conditions, leading to variations in both geometric and image properties, as illustrated in Table 3. Consequently, the model is exposed to a range of real-world imaging conditions during training and testing phases. Geometric parameters such as orientation, scale and viewpoint are varied during the acquisition of the images in the FFE dataset. Predominantly, vertical and horizontal orientations are observed across the images of various fish species. Focusing on the fish eye brings scale variations, introducing differences in the occupancy of the fish eye in the image. Viewpoint variations occur due to differences in the placement of fish and the acquisition devices. Image properties like blurring, lighting, and background variations are observed in the FFE dataset. Motion blurriness is created due to the relative motion between the fish and the acquisition device. These domain variations are distributed across all freshness levels and fish species, reducing dataset bias and enabling the model to learn more generalisable representations applicable to real-world scenarios.

Methodology

The proposed work aims to develop FCLN integrated with image and numeric features encoders, as illustrated in Fig. 2. FCLN jointly learns implicit visual representations and explicit expert-guided features through parallel encoders, enabling complementary feature interaction at the fusion level. CNN is used as an image encoder, and an MLP is used to encode the numeric features, which are extracted from the images. By learning discriminative patterns from expert-guided numeric features, the MLP complements the high-level visual representations extracted by the CNN, contributing to improvement in classification accuracy. An LLM-based expert knowledge translator is employed to translate the expert knowledge into feature extraction algorithms. The fish expert can provide a textual description of visual clues to evaluate the fish’s freshness. LLM understands this textual information and identifies the technical aspects of the visual features described. The identified visual features allow the generation of code to extract features from the fish-eye images. The generated code is deployed for feature extraction, and the extracted features are encoded using a numeric feature encoder. Finally, a layered fusion network is introduced to fuse the image and numeric features to determine the fish freshness class.

Block diagram of the proposed LLM-guided expert feature extraction with FCLN.

LLM guided expert feature extraction

Knowledge on assessing visual indicators of fish freshness is obtained from the expert through a descriptive text, which is derived from the prior literature7,20. The LLM processes the descriptive text to extract features that need to be evaluated for freshness, as in Fig. 3. An instruction prompt is defined for the LLM to play the role of a computer vision engineer and to search for clues to determine the required features. These rough feature descriptors are standardised in terms of image processing and computer vision terminologies. The standard features are reviewed manually, and more information will be collected from the expert if the features are not satisfactory. LLM processes the collected information to extract more features. The LLM again processes the finalised features to develop code for feature extraction. Image properties and details of the target programming language and libraries are provided as instructions to the LLM to generate code. Finally, the developed code is tested and validated before deployment. LLM is employed only for feature engineering and is not involved during model inference. This enables the utilisation of LLM capabilities for expert knowledge translation while resulting in a simpler inference model. Thus, the LLM-assisted expert-guided feature extraction process eliminates the dependency on computer vision specialists for manual feature engineering. It also enables systematic and automated derivation of visual features directly from the fish expert’s knowledge.

Flow chart for LLM-guided expert feature extraction.

In the proposed work, the expert text description about the fish freshness evaluation is processed using the LLM. The “expert knowledge” referred to in this study is the domain-specific insights derived from the various prior literature and does not involve the participation of human subjects. LLM interprets the domain knowledge embedded in the descriptions, extracts relevant visual cues, and translates them into image features. About three sample fish-eye images are also provided to evaluate the feasibility of extracting the identified features.

An instruction-tuned LLM (GPT-5.5) is used as an expert knowledge translator. A structured prompt is designed to convert domain-specific textual descriptions of fish freshness indicators into executable feature extraction algorithms, as shown in Fig. 4. The prompt is organised to include Role, Task, Constraints, Steps and Output format to ensure controlled and reproducible generation. Role defines the LLM as a computer vision feature engineering expert to set the context. Task specifies the transformation of the textual description from the expert knowledge into the corresponding image-based feature extraction logic. Constraints are one of the important instructions that enforce the use of a specific platform and restrict the scope strictly to feature extraction. Steps guide the LLM to get human feedback for further enhanced feature extraction and then translate them into implementable functions. Finally, the Output is required in a structured format consisting of identified visual cues and corresponding executable code blocks for feature extraction. A low-temperature decoding strategy (temperature = 0.4) is applied to ensure deterministic and reproducible feature generation.

Prompt used for LLM based expert feature extraction.

Other complex features like eye cloudiness, corneal opacity, pupil visibility, iris texture clarity and pupil circularity are also derived from the expert description. However, many of these features predominantly represent structural or shape-dependent characteristics, which are already effectively captured by the deep image embeddings generated through the CNN backbone. The inclusion of such features may introduce redundancy rather than complementary information. Also, some of the complex features require complete visibility and consistent structural representation of the fisheye region, which are not uniformly available in the FFE dataset due to acquisition variability, viewpoint changes, partial eye visibility, blur, and illumination variations. For instance, the pupil circularity estimation requires sufficiently complete and well-defined fish eye boundaries. Hence, robust features with a higher complementary nature are only considered and reported in the proposed work.

Design of FCLN architecture

The proposed FCLN architecture is formed by an image feature and a numeric feature encoders as illustrated in Fig. 5. The image feature encoder extracts the features from the images of fish eye that enables freshness classification. ResNet-50 (Residual Network—50) is one of the CNN models that shows promising performance in freshness evaluation using a fish eye image as compared to other similar models6,25. ResNet-50 consists of 50 learnable layers, which are trained using residual learning that effectively mitigates the degradation and vanishing gradient problems. It is composed of stacked residual blocks with convolution to capture hierarchical and discriminative visual features at multiple levels of abstraction. Each residual block learns a residual function that represents the difference between the test image and its corresponding desired output. It makes the ResNet-50 focus on learning incremental refinements rather than complete transformations, which improves the training stability. This makes the ResNet-50 suitable for fine-grained image analysis like fish-eye-based freshness assessment.

Architecture of the proposed FCLN.

ResNet-50 has been widely adopted as a backbone feature extractor in various computer vision applications26. ResNet-50-based hybrid networks exhibit promising performance in vision-based fish classification27, behaviour monitoring28, disease detection29,30, and freshness estimation31,32. In the proposed work, ResNet-50 is customised as the image feature encoder to extract discriminative visual features from fish-eye images. An image projection layer is subsequently introduced to map the high-dimensional CNN features into a unified latent space, thereby aligning the feature dimensionality and enabling effective fusion with the numeric feature representations.

MLP-based network is designed to encode the numeric features extracted from the fish eye images. It consists of three different layers, namely, the input layer, hidden layer, and output layer. The input layer consists of neurons corresponding to feature descriptors. Hidden layers apply weighted linear transformations followed by nonlinear activation functions, enabling the network to model complex and nonlinear relationships among the numeric features. Regularisation strategies, including dropout and batch normalisation, are also included in the hidden layers, which enable stabilised learning and prevent overfitting33,34. Output layer generates a latent representation of the numeric features and aligns with the projected image feature space to facilitate fusion for fish freshness classification.

Fusion layers are customised to accommodate both image and numeric features18, generating an inference on the fish freshness label. The projected image features extracted from the ResNet-50 encoder and the latent numeric features generated by the MLP encoder are aligned to a common feature space and then fused through feature concatenation35. This combined representation captures complementary visual and quantitative features used for fish freshness assessment. Further, the fused feature vector is processed through several Fully Connected Network (FCN) layers equipped with nonlinear activation functions to model cross-modal interactions between the image and numeric features. Finally, a classification layer with a softmax activation produces probabilistic predictions for the identified three fish freshness classes (Highly-fresh, Fresh, Not-fresh), enabling robust and reliable freshness classification.

The learning rate (0.001) and batch size (32) are chosen to ensure stable optimisation and efficient GPU utilisation. A dropout rate of 0.3 is used in the classifier layer to improve generalisation and reduce overfitting during training Adam optimiser is selected due to its adaptive gradient updating capability, which is effective for multimodal deep learning frameworks. The maximum number of epochs is fixed at 50 to provide sufficient learning iterations, while the best-performing model is selected based on validation accuracy across all epochs. The categorical cross-entropy loss function optimises the multi-class freshness classification considered in this study36. It efficiently handles multi-class distinctions and guides the model to learn subtle visual differences in fish eye described by both image and numeric features. Model selection is performed based on the best validation accuracy, and training is terminated upon reaching the maximum number of epochs.

The proposed FCLN is trained, and its performance is evaluated using the training and validation dataset respectively. In the proposed work, an exploration-based training strategy technique is implemented, which iterates through all the epochs in contrast to the early stopping criterion. It reduces the risk of convergence to suboptimal local minima performance. For each epoch, the classification accuracy is evaluated using the validation dataset and compared with the best validation accuracy (Initialised as zero). If the current validation accuracy exceeds the previous best, the corresponding model weights are saved as the best weights, and the best validation accuracy is updated. After completing all epochs, the model corresponding to the best validation performance is evaluated on the testing dataset. The testing performance is used to select the final FCLN weights, and the model can be retrained with adjusted hyperparameter settings if further improvement in testing performance is required. Thus, this method enables storing intermediate model weights based on the best validation performance, which often yields better generalisation than retaining the final model at the end of training37.

Complementary learning analysis

Complementary Learning Analysis (CLA) is conducted to investigate the complementary characteristics of the image-based and numeric feature representations38,39. CLA employs the coefficient of determination () and correlation analysis to examine the relationships between the embedding features extracted from the image encoder and the numeric features derived from expert knowledge, as illustrated in Fig. 7.

analysis is employed to quantify the extent to which the numeric features can be explained by the image embedding representations. is used to quantify the degree of linear dependency across the input and output of the datasets. It quantifies the proportion of variance in the output that can be explained by the inputs40. analysis uses a linear regression model (r(x)) to fit the input–output dataset, which can predict the numeric features () for the given embeddings () corresponding to the fish eye image as in (3). The coefficient of determination () quantifies the reduction in prediction error relative to the total variance of the output measured with respect to the mean () as in (4).

A high value (close to 1) indicates that a large proportion of the variance in the numeric features is explained by the embedding predictor, suggesting strong dependency and potential feature redundancy. On the other hand, a lower value implies weak explanatory characteristics, indicating that the embedding’s limitations to capture information about the numeric features, which reflects the complementary characteristics. It is also interesting to note that can take negative values, indicating that the prediction error exceeds the variance of the target variable with respect to its mean, implying that the predictor performs worse than a mean-based baseline model. It signifies a poor fit or a strong complementary nature between the variables. In CLA, negative or low values suggest that the numeric features are not well explained by the image embeddings. This indicates that the image embeddings and numeric features capture distinct and complementary information rather than redundant representations.

Additionally, correlation analysis is performed to examine the linear relationships between the image embedding features and the expert-derived numeric features. The correlation coefficient quantifies the strength of linear association between pairs of variables, independent of any predictive model. In the proposed CLA, the maximum correlation coefficient () is computed to identify potential direct dependencies between individual numeric features and embeddings.

Correlation analysis provides a feature-wise measure of dependency with the image embeddings, allowing the identification of redundant features across modalities. A high correlation indicates overlapping information between image embeddings and numeric features. On the other hand, low or moderate correlation values suggest complementary characteristics. In the proposed work, the maximum correlation coefficient () is determined for the given feature with all the image embeddings across the various layers. It enables the identification of the potential complementary characteristics across the numeric features and image embeddings.

Attention layers emerge as a promising alternative to realise fusion layers to combine numeric and image features in the proposed FCLN. Cross-modal attention (CMA) based fusion layer is defined, and its performance is compared with the proposed FCN-based fusion layer. In CMA based fusion layer, the embedded numeric features are employed to generate feature-wise attention weights, enabling adaptive modulation of the deep image features through element-wise multiplication. It enables cross-modal interaction and the expert-driven freshness descriptors to guide the discriminative image features. These attention-refined image features are then concatenated with the embedded numeric representations to form a unified multimodal feature vector. Finally, passed through the classifier layer for fish freshness classification. Experimental results show (Ref Table 9) that the proposed complementary features are fused better using FCN than the CMA layer marginally, as indicated by an approximately 1.3% improvement in testing accuracy. It is mainly attributed to the lower dimensionality of the numeric features, which is complementary to the discriminative image features. Although attention mechanisms have demonstrated effectiveness in multimodal learning of heterogeneous feature representations, their effectiveness depends on the complexity and dependency between modalities41,42. In the proposed work, image modality provides highly discriminative representations, and the auxiliary numeric features are relatively low-dimensional. Hence, FCN-based fusion layer performs competitively with reduced model complexity and improved optimisation stability.

P-FIA evaluates the importance of each feature by measuring the reduction in classification performance caused by random shuffling of the corresponding feature values46. Computational complexity is also analysed using model parameters, model size, and GMACs (Giga Multiply–Accumulate Operations)49, which demonstrates the efficiency of the proposed architecture for deployment. GMACs represent the total multiply–accumulate operations performed during inference and provide a hardware-independent measure of computational efficiency. Analysis demonstrates that DenseNet-121 provides the lowest model complexity and memory. EfficientNet-B4 offers a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency and discriminative capability. These results indicate that the proposed FCLN framework can effectively operate with different backbone architectures. However, ResNet-50 provides more discriminative visual embeddings for the proposed FCLN, leading to improved classification performance among the other CNN backbones.

Though the proposed framework exhibits significant performance on the FFE dataset for binary and ternary freshness classification, it is also subject to limitations. The ternary freshness classification is comparatively more challenging due to increased inter-class similarity, which reduces the classification accuracy as compared to binary classification. Furthermore, the generalisation of the proposed FCLN beyond the FFE dataset may be influenced by variations in environmental conditions, fish species distributions, imaging devices, and freshness classes. To address this limitation, inclusion of more numeric features and a change in backbone architectures may be required to capture discriminative freshness characteristics. Therefore, further cross-dataset validation and large-scale real-world evaluation are required to establish generalisation, which will be addressed in the future.

Conclusion

Assessment of fish freshness is one of the primary requirements in aquaculture that demands expert knowledge and labour-intensive inspection. Automated solutions based on DLNs have demonstrated promising performance in fish freshness assessment using visible markers. Commonly used visual indicators include fish eyes, gill colouration, and body shape variations. Among these, the fish eye has emerged as a preferred marker due to its predominant visibility in typical fish arrangements and non-invasive image acquisition. The proposed work develops an FCLN that integrates the DLNs with expert knowledge-driven features, enabling complementary learning to compensate for the loss of structural cues in fish-eye images. LLM is used to interpret fish expert textual descriptions, identify relevant feature cues, and automatically derive feature extraction procedures, which eliminates the need for manual feature engineering. ResNet50 is used as a backbone, and MLP is used to process the numeric features in the FCLN. It also has a fusion layer to integrate the inferences from the numeric features and visual embeddings from the ResNet50, providing a complementary learning experience. CLA is used to validate the complementary nature between numeric features and the visual embeddings using regression and correlation analysis.

Experimental evaluation on the FFE dataset demonstrates that the proposed FCLN achieves reliable performance across multiple fish species and freshness categories. Experimental results show a reliable classification accuracy of 81.03% and an average F1 score of 0.8051 during testing. Ablation study at various levels, namely, high, detailed and accumulation levels are carried out to validate the performance of the proposed FCLN. A high-level ablation study is used to validate the use of fusion based architecture. A detailed ablation study is carried out to investigate the importance of individual numeric features. Accumulation level demonstrates the performance improvement offered with the inclusion of more features. CLA also results in a lower mean correlation (0.3755), indicating the complementary nature of the numeric features and visual embeddings. Comparative analysis with existing studies reported in the literature is also executed. Results show improvement in classification performance using the FFE dataset. Though other custom dataset provides significant performance with fewer fish species and freshness levels, the proposed technique also results in 88.47% accuracy for binary classification.

Integration of multimodal features, including odour sensor and texture information, will be explored to enhance robustness under varying storage and lighting conditions. Future work will explore the LLM-assisted auto-sampling system to guide the image acquisition process by selecting appropriate viewpoints and regions of interest, ensuring consistent and informative fish-eye samples for analysis.

Reduction of the risk of complete sternotomy scar dehiscence in cardiac surgery: a protocol for a randomised, open-label multicentre clinical investigation comparing negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) versus standard dressing (PRISTER study)
Authors: Christophe Jayle, Géraldine Allain, Amandine Buono, Simon Dang Van, Nicolas D’Ostrevy, Stéphane Kermen, Alessandro Picardo, Antoine Pelras, Julie Drux, Pierre Corbi, Maxime Billot, Denis Frasca
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCardiac surgical procedures are associated with a high incidence of postoperative infection, a risk attributed to their inherently invasive nature and extended duration. The goal of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) compared with standard dressings in preventing sternal scar dehiscence in patients undergoing cardiac surgery via complete vertical midline sternotomy, comparing a 3 day change system (NPWT 3/7 d) versus a 7 day wear system (NPWT 7d) versus standard dressings.Methods and analysisThe PRISTER study is a prospective open-label multicentre, randomised, controlled, three-arm trial. Patients aged ≥18 years and meeting all the following inclusion criteria: patients undergoing scheduled or semiurgent cardiac surgery, with or without extracorporeal circulation, and patients managed by complete vertical median sternotomy will be recruited. Total sample size will consist of 660 patients. The primary outcome, surgical wound dehiscence after complete median sternotomy, expressed as occurrence or non-occurrence during the first 30 postoperative days, will be assessed by tablet calibrated photos analysed by an independent blinded staff. The secondary outcomes will be the number of dressing repairs between D7 (excluding initial protocol) and D90, length of initial hospital stay, occurrence of surgical site infection and its classification collected on day 90 (D90), use of an anti-infectious treatment and its duration, incidence of postoperative mediastinitis defined by dehiscence of the sternum with positive sternal samples, incidence of surgical revision for dehiscence of the surgical wound at D90 and collection of all operations involving surgical revision of the sternotomy wound, number of rehospitalisations (including hospital consultations) and their duration (evaluated at D90). Medicoeconomic assessment will be assessed by evaluation of hospital and extrahospital costs (number of days in hospital, consultations, rehospitalisations, imaging, biology and drugs), dressing costs and patient quality of life by EQ-5D-5L.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the French Agency for the Safety of Health Products (ANSM, Agence National de Sécurité du Médicament) as well as by the Ethics Committee (CPP Ile de France VIII) N°ID-RCB: 2023-A01782-43. Recruitment began in April 2024 and ended on 26 May 2025.Trial registration numberclinicaltrials.gov NCT06207630.Keywords: Patients, Cardiac surgery, WOUND MANAGEMENT, Infection control

Introduction

Surgical site infection (SSI), defined as an infection of the surgical incision occurring within 30–90 days after surgery (Centres for Disease Control and Prevention), represents a major complication.1 Cardiac surgery has been identified as a high risk of postoperative infection, likely due to the invasive nature and prolonged duration of these procedures. SSI results in an increase in both morbidity and mortality, while dramatically increasing the associated healthcare costs.1,3 Mediastinitis represents a critical infectious complication following cardiac surgery, associated with a poor prognosis. Although the incidence of mediastinitis is low (0.5%–2.2%),4 5 the dramatic outcome compels healthcare providers to make every possible effort to prevent it.

Well-established risk factors for mediastinitis include obesity, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, double breast sampling, immunodeficiency, corticosteroid therapy, reoperation, thoracic radiotherapy and dialysis.6 7 The three recognised modes of contamination are direct intraoperative contamination, haematogenous dissemination during sepsis and gradual contamination from skin breakdown. Specifically, surgical wound dehiscence, defined as any disruption of the sternotomy scar more than one centimetre in length, serves as a significant entry point for bacterial colonisation and a risk factor for SSI. This skin complication leads to increased healthcare costs due to prolonged hospitalisation, repeated nursing care, reoperation, intensified follow-up and adverse patient outcomes, including delayed cardiac rehabilitation and discharge.4 8 9

The treatment of infected wounds by negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) is well-codified. This system comprises a semiocclusive adhesive dressing, a suction pump generating subatmospheric pressure,10 11 and a fluid collection system that prevents maceration, thereby limiting over-contamination.12 13 Two types of NPWT devices are commercially available. The first is a 7 day NPWT (NPWT 7d) system designed for extended use without dressing replacement, incorporating a reservoir to manage exudate and minimise maceration. The second is a 3 day NPWT (NPWT 3/7d) absorbent dressing system, without reservoir, requiring dressing changes after 3 days, which allows wound visualisation. To date, NPWT has been applied successfully and shown benefits for patients presenting with risk factors for dehiscence after cardiac surgery.14,16 The NPWT approaches towards management of potential infection may be beneficial for all patients undergoing cardiac surgery with closed surgical incisions.17 18

Given the cost and widespread use of NPWT for the prophylaxis of SSIs, the PRISTER randomised controlled trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of NPWT in patients undergoing complete vertical midline sternotomy in cardiac surgery, regardless of the risk factors for impaired healing, by comparing the proportion of sternal scar dehiscence occurring within 30 days postoperatively, between patients in the control group (standard dressing) and patients randomised in one of the two experimental groups (NPWT 3/7d and NPWT 7d). The same outcomes will be compared between the control group and the NPWT 3/7d group and between the control group and the NPWT 7d group. In addition, secondary objectives will include rate of dressing repairs, duration of initial hospital stay, occurrence of an SSI and its classification, need for anti-infectious treatment and its duration, post-operative mediastinitis, surgical revision, rate of rehospitalisation and its duration, nurses' satisfaction with use of the device during dressing repair, surgeons’ satisfaction with the wound, in-hospital and post-hospital assessment of dressing costs, the patient’s quality of life between the three groups.

Materials and methods

Study design

The PRISTER study is a prospective open-label multicentre, randomised, controlled, three-arm trial comparing the efficacy of three dressings: two negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) and one standard with a 90-day follow-up (Figure 1). The study was conducted at five investigational sites, Poitiers University Hospital (France), Angers University Hospital (France), Clermont-Ferrand University Hospital (France), Limoges University Hospital (France) and Tours University Hospital (France). Patients were randomly allocated (1:1:1 ratio) to Standard dressing (control), PICO (Smith & Nephew, Hull, UK) suction dressing, or PREVENAR (3M KCI, St Paul, Minn) suction dressing (experimental) group by an electronic data management system and provided written informed consent before enrolment in this study. The study was approved by the French Agency for the Safety of Health Products (ANSM, Agence National de Sécurité du Médicament) as well as by the Ethics Committee (CPP Ile de France VIII) N°ID-RCB : 2023-A01782-43, and was registered on 21 December 2023, at Clinicaltrials.gov (number: NCT06207630; URL: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06207630?term=PRISTER&rank=1). Recruitment began in April 2024 and ended on 26 May 2025. Surgeons, nurses and participants were not blinded to group assignment due to the material device studied.

Study design. NPWT 3/7d, negative pressure wound therapy 3 day change system; NPWT 7d, negative pressure wound therapy 7 day wear system.

Eligibility criteria

For eligibility, a patient must meet all of the following inclusion criteria: age ≥18 years, patients undergoing scheduled or semiurgent cardiac surgery, with or without extracorporeal circulation, patients managed by complete vertical median sternotomy, patients benefiting from a social security scheme or benefiting from it through a third party, informed consent signed by the patient after clear and complete information about the clinical investigation. Non-inclusion criteria were patients presenting with known allergy to one of the dressings: PREVENA, PICO or standard dressing, a contraindication to PREVENA dressing: sensitivity to silver, a history of cardiac surgery prior to inclusion, skin infection (folliculitis) of the sternotomy site before the operation, urgency or extreme urgency (patient operated within 24 hours of admission), ongoing antibiotherapy (endocarditis patients), minimal sternotomy (8–10 cm), pregnant or breastfeeding women, women of childbearing age without effective contraception (hormonal/mechanical: oral, injectable, transcutaneous, implantable, intrauterine device, or surgical: tubal ligation, hysterectomy, total oophorectomy), or having a positive pregnancy test at inclusion, inability of the patient to complete informed consent before any study procedure, benefiting from enhanced protection, namely minors, persons deprived of their liberty by a judicial or administrative decision, adults under legal protection and patients already participating in a clinical investigation whose main objective or secondary objectives are likely to have an impact on the main objective of this clinical investigation.

Group allocation, randomisation

Patients will be randomised, in a 1:1:1 ratio (standard dressing vs NPWT 3/7d vs NPWT 7d) by block of variable size, centralised, computerised and stratified by centre. The study will be conducted at five investigational sites, Poitiers University Hospital (the coordinating site, France), Angers University Hospital (France), Clermont-Ferrand University Hospital (France), Limoges University Hospital (France) and Tours University Hospital (France). The randomisation list will be compiled by an independent methodologist prior to the start of the research. A document describing the randomisation procedure will be kept confidentially by the Methodological Platform of the Poitiers University Hospital.

A patient will be assigned one of three devices, for a period of 7 days:

NPWT 3/7d group: Use of a PICO at the end of surgery in the operating room. The dressing will be repaired on day 3 (D3) and permanently removed at day 7 (D7).

NPWT 3/7d group: Use of a PICO at the end of surgery in the operating room. The dressing will be repaired on day 3 (D3) and permanently removed at day 7 (D7).

NPWT 7d group: Use of a PREVENA at the end of surgery in the operating room. This dressing will remain on the scar until the seventh postoperative day and will be permanently removed.

NPWT 7d group: Use of a PREVENA at the end of surgery in the operating room. This dressing will remain on the scar until the seventh postoperative day and will be permanently removed.

Standard dressing group: It is a dry dressing, made with a glued dressing. It will be systematically re-dressed at day 1 (D1), day 3 (D3), day 5 (D5) and removed at D7. If the dressing is soiled, it can be changed at a higher rate.

Standard dressing group: It is a dry dressing, made with a glued dressing. It will be systematically re-dressed at day 1 (D1), day 3 (D3), day 5 (D5) and removed at D7. If the dressing is soiled, it can be changed at a higher rate.

When possible, the wound will be left exposed beyond the D7 level. If a dressing is necessary, standard dressing techniques will be used.

Procedures

A preoperative visit will be conducted in order to collect medical and surgical history and current treatments and to carry out a complete blood count (CBC) and a C-reactive protein (CRP) assay and a self-questionnaire on quality of life: EuroQol 5-Dimensional 5-Level (EQ-5D-5L). After cardiac surgery, all patients are to receive dressing assigned by randomisation for 7 days. At the end of dressing, a clinical examination of the surgical site and a digital photograph of the wound will be performed in order to verify any skin allergies to the dressing and to measure, if necessary, the length of separation. CBC assay and EQ-5D-5L will also be carried out. The patient will be given a follow-up diary in which he/she will note any sign of dehiscence, care of the surgical wound, the nursing or medical consultations generated or rehospitalisations and should bring this diary to the following visits. During the two follow-up visits (30 and 90 days after surgery), the same data will be collected.

Risks

The relative risks related to this study are related to the use of NPWT PICO and PREVENA dressings. The control group will receive standard management with no changes. A potential risk is an allergic reaction to one of the dressings. This risk is considered low because, unlike standard dressings, both the PICO and PREVENA dressings are silicone-coated and hypoallergenic. The most serious risk is excessive bleeding, which can be associated with wound suction. To mitigate this, rigorous monitoring of the wound and dressing will be performed to promptly detect any change in the patient’s bleeding status. Special attention will be given to this risk when dressings are changed. Finally, any pain experienced by the patients is expected to be related to the surgical wound itself, not the dressings.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

Surgical wound dehiscence, expressed as occurrence or non-occurrence, will be assessed by tablet calibrated photos (Pixacare) analysed by an independent blinded staff. Photos will be taken in portrait mode at a distance of approximately 20 cm, using the Pixacare software on a digital tablet. The software’s WoundTrack reference sticker and a graduated paper scale are to be placed close and parallel to the wound, serving as a reference system. The WoundTrack sticker provides four green lights to validate calibration before allowing a picture to be taken. Training sessions will be held to ensure standardised practice. All photos are anonymised using a patient-specific identification number and stored on the Pixacare application, which is a certified health data host.

Complete median sternotomy wound cutaneous dehiscence in cardiac surgery during the first 30 postoperative days will be assessed by tablet calibrated photos (Pixacare) analysed by independent blinded staff. Surgical wound dehiscence is defined as any disruption of an operative wound more than one centimetre in length or requiring treatment with a nursing dressing or surgical revision due to disruption. It will be collected in the care file by the nurses caring for the patient during his postoperative stays. The length of surgical wound dehiscence is automatically calculated by the software algorithm, which identifies the wound extremities. This measurement can be modified by an expert if needed. Variability will be limited by calibrating and standardising the methods to be applied across the different centres.

The patient information notice includes a section on image rights. Patients are to be informed that the images, which never show their face, may be used by our collaborators for research purposes or to improve the software by training artificial intelligence algorithms.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes will be surgical wound dehiscence after complete median sternotomy between D7 (excluded from initial protocol) and D90, number of dressing repairs between D7 and D90, length of initial hospital stay between D7 and D90, occurrence of an infection of the surgical site and its classification collected on day 90, use of an anti-infectious treatment and its duration between D7 and D90, incidence of postoperative mediastinitis defined by dehiscence of the sternum with positive sternal samples, incidence of surgical revision for dehiscence of the surgical wound at D90 and collection of all operations involving surgical revision of the sternotomy wound, number of rehospitalisations (including hospital consultations) and their duration (evaluated at D90). A patient diary will be given to the patient on discharge. Nurses’ satisfaction with use of the three types of dressing during dressing repair and surgeons’ satisfaction with the wound healing will be assessed with a standardised questionnaire consisting of four questions rated from 1 (not satisfied) to 5 (totally satisfied): Are you satisfied with application of this dressing? Are you satisfied with the management of the surgical wound? Are you satisfied with the follow-up on the wound? Are you satisfied with the visual appearance of the surgical wound? For each centre, the ancillary objective is to compare the mean satisfaction level of the nurse and surgeon between the control group and the two NPWT groups for every 30 patients randomised.

Medicoeconomic assessment will be assessed by evaluation of hospital and extrahospital costs (number of days in hospital, consultations, rehospitalisations, imaging, biology and drugs), dressing costs and patient quality of life by EQ-5D-5L. The proposed analysis will be a cost-utility analysis because health-related quality of life is a significant outcome of the studied interventions. This will be supplemented by a cost-effectiveness study that will focus on the number of avoided postoperative complications. Resource utilisation data will be collected prospectively for all patients enrolled in the clinical investigation. The collected metrics will include costs associated with initial hospitalisation, surgical duration, devices used during the procedure, length of stay and readmissions.

Adjudication committee

A blinded adjudication committee, comprising two surgical wound nurses and a surgeon independent of the study’s protocol and patient management, will review the primary outcome. The criterion for dehiscence will be met if at least two members of the committee concur based on their evaluation of the standardised photos. Given that this is a clinical investigation of current care practices, there is no need to establish an independent supervisory board for secondary outcomes.

Data and safety monitoring board

Medical devices being CE marked and used for their intended purpose and the additional clinical investigation procedures being low-risk, the establishment of a data and safety monitoring board is not justified for this clinical investigation.

Sample size and power calculation

The main analysis will consist of comparing the control patients with NPWT patients (NPWT 3/7d group and the NPWT 7d group) regarding the proportion of partial cutaneous dehiscence of vertical median sternotomy at D30. In a pilot clinical investigation carried out in the department over 3 months, among patients who had a standard dressing (control group), the percentage of dehiscences observed was 15%. Data from the literature suggest a 50% reduction in the number of dehiscences16 19 SSIs are one of the most significant complications in surgical patients and are strongly associated with poorer prognosis. Due to their aggressive character, cardiac surgical procedures carry a particularly high risk of postoperative infection, with infection incidence rates ranging from a reported 3.5% to 26.8% in cardiac surgery patients. Given the specific nature of cardiac surgical procedures, sternal wound and graft harvesting site infections are the most common SSIs. Deep sternal wound infection, including mediastinitis, in cardiac surgery patients remain a significant clinical problem as they are associated with increased hospital stay, substantial medical costs and high mortality, ranging from 3% to 20%. In SSI prevention, it is important to implement procedures reducing preoperative risk factors: obesity, hypoalbuminaemia, abnormal glucose levels, smoking and S. aureus carriage. For the decolonisation of S. aureus carriers prior to cardiac surgery, it is recommended to administer nasal mupirocin, together with baths using chlorhexidine-based agents. Perioperative management also involves antibiotic prophylaxis, surgical site preparation, topical antibiotic administration and the maintenance of normal glucose levels. SSI treatment involves surgical intervention, NPWT application and antibiotic therapy. According to this hypothesis, considering the initial proportion of dehiscence P1=15% and the proportion with treatment P2=7.5% (maximalist hypothesis) at the type-I error α=0.05 and type-II error β=0.2 (power of 80%), each group should include at least 210 patients. Considering a type-I error adjustment for the analysis of the three groups and 5% incomplete records, 220 patients per group will be included for a total of 660 patients.

All the information required by the protocol must be recorded and an explanation for each element of missing data must be given. Imputation of incomplete adverse events regarding start and end dates will follow standard conventions and will be described in detail in the statistical analysis plan. No sensitivity analyses are planned for assessment of the main criteria.

Discussion

The primary objective of this clinical investigation is to reduce the risk of skin dehiscence in surgical wounds, a key initiating factor in the development of SSIs. We anticipate a clinical benefit in both experimental groups, NPWT for 3 out of 7 days (NPWT 3/7d) and continuous NPWT for 7 days (NPWT 7d), with reduced incidence of wound dehiscence compared with standard care. Beyond this direct benefit, we expect enhanced patient comfort and quality of life in the two experimental groups, attributed to fewer dressing changes and reduced nursing interventions, more specifically two fewer dressings in the NPWT 3/7d group and three fewer in the NPWT 7d group. These reductions may contribute to improved patient satisfaction and decreased disruption during the postoperative period. Moreover, by minimising dressing interventions, we aim to enhance in-hospital comfort and potentially reduce the frequency of dressing-related complications and the need for secondary wound repairs. This simplified wound management approach is also expected to improve adherence among healthcare professionals. To assess this, we will collect satisfaction data from nurses and surgeons by means of a structured questionnaire evaluating the ease and acceptability of wound management protocols.

From a public health perspective, medicoeconomic evaluation will determine whether the additional upfront cost of the NPWT device is offset by overall cost savings, such as fewer dressing changes, reduced wound complications and improved patient-reported outcomes. Importantly, patients enrolled in clinical research typically benefit from enhanced, comprehensive care, which may further support favourable outcomes in this investigation.

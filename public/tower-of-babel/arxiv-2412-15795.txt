Graphs of trigonal curves and rigid isotopies of singular real algebraic curves of bidegree ( 4 , 3 ) (4,3) on a hyperboloid
V. I. Zvonilov

Abstract
A rigid isotopy of real algebraic curves of a certain class is a path in the space of curves of this class. The paper’s study completes the rigid isotopic classification of nonsingular real algebraic curves of bidegree (4,3) on a hyperboloid, begun by the author in earlier works. There are given the missing proofs of the uniqueness of the connected components for 16 classes of real algebraic curves of bidegree (4,3) having a single node or a cusp. The main technical tools are graphs of real trigonal curves on Hirzebruch surfaces.

2
Definitions and notation

A real algebraic variety

is a complex algebraic variety
V
with
an antiholomorphic involution
c=c_{V}:V\rightarrow V
.
The set of fixed points
{\mathbb{R}}V=\mathrm{Fix}\,c
is called the
real part
of the variety
V
. A regular morphism
f:V\rightarrow W
of two real manifolds
is called
real
or
equivariant

if
f\circ c_{V}=c_{W}\circ f

A hyperboloid
X
is understood as a real non-singular quadric with complex part
\mathbb{C}X=\mathbb{C}P^{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, an antiholomorphic
involution
{c_{X}}
that preserves the product, and real part
\mathbb{R}X=\mathbb{R}P^{1}\times\mathbb{R}P^{1}
.

Fix a pair
P_{1},P_{2}
of generators of the hyperboloid
X
. The fundamental classes

[\mathbb{C}P_{1}]
,
[\mathbb{C}P_{2}]
form a basis of the group
H_{2}(\mathbb{C}X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
A
be an algebraic curve on
X
. Then
[\mathbb{C}A]=m_{1}[\mathbb{C}P_{1}]+m_{2}[\mathbb{C}P_{2}]
for some non-negative integers
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
.
The pair
(m_{1},m_{2})
is called the
bidegree
of
A
. If
[x_{0}:x_{1}]
,

[y_{0}:y_{1}]
are homogeneous coordinates on the lines
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, then the curve
A

is defined by a bihomogeneous polynomial

F(x_{0},x_{1};y_{0},y_{1})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m_{1},m_{2}}a_{i,j}x_{1}^{i}x_{0}^{m_{1}-i}y_{1}^{j}y_{0}^{m_{2}-j},

having the degrees of homogeneity
m_{1}
in
x_{0},x_{1}
and
m_{2}
in
y_{0},y_{1}
. The curve
A
is real
if and only if all
a_{i,j}
are real.

To specify the topology of a real curve on a hyperboloid, we use a modification of the standard encoding of real schemes of plane projective curves (see,
e.g.,
[
5
]
).
Let
A\subset X
be a nonsingular
real algebraic curve. The real part
\mathbb{R}A
may have components of two types: contractible in
\mathbb{R}X
and non-contractible; contractible components
are called
ovals
. The number of ovals is denoted by
l
, the number of
non-contractible components by
h
. Each oval bounds a topological
disk in
\mathbb{R}X
called the
interior
of the oval. Fundamental classes
[\mathbb{R}P_{1}]
,
[\mathbb{R}P_{2}]
,
endowed with some (fixed) orientations, form a basis of the group

H_{1}(\mathbb{R}X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
. All non-contractible components
N_{1},...,N_{h}
realize the same
non-zero class
(c_{1},c_{2})
in
H_{1}(\mathbb{R}X)
, where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are coprime. The real scheme of the curve
\mathbb{R}A\subset\mathbb{R}X
is encoded as follows:

\langle(c_{1},c_{2}),{\rm scheme}_{1},(c_{1},c_{2}),{\rm scheme}_{2},...,(c_{1},c_{2}),{\rm scheme}_{h}\rangle,

where
{\rm scheme}_{1},...,{\rm scheme}_{h}
are the arrangement schemes of the ovals lying in the connected
components of the surface

\mathbb{R}X\setminus(N_{1}\cup...\cup N_{h})
(cf.
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
).

According to F. Klein (see
[
9
]
or
[
4
]
) a real curve
A

belongs to
type
I
or
type
II depending on whether
\mathbb{R}A
divides the complexification

\mathbb{C}A
or not. If
A
belongs to type I, the natural orientations of the components

U
and
V
of the space
\mathbb{C}A\setminus\mathbb{R}A

give two opposite orientations of the curve
\mathbb{R}A=\partial U=\partial V
;
these are called
complex orientations
. A real scheme endowed with
a type and, in the case of type I, complex orientations, is called the
complex scheme
.
If one wishes to specify the type of a curve with a real scheme

\langle B\rangle
, one uses the notation
\langle B\rangle_{\rm I}
and

\langle B\rangle_{\rm II}
.

All real curves of bidegree
(m,n)
form a space

C_{m,n}\cong\mathbb{R}P^{N}

with
N=mn+m+n
. The set
\Delta\subset C_{m,n}
of singular curves has the dimension

N-1
. By
S\subset\Delta
we denote the subset of curves that have a singular
point other than a non-degenerate double point or cusp, or
have several singular points. The set
\Delta\setminus S
is a topological manifold (though not a smooth submanifold
of

C_{m,n}
).
A rigid isotopic class
of a curve

A\in C_{m,n}\setminus\Delta
(or
A\in\Delta\setminus S
) is a component
of the space
C_{m,n}\setminus\Delta
(resp.
\Delta\setminus S
) containing
A
.
The components of the space
C_{m,n}\setminus\Delta
(resp.
\Delta\setminus S
) are called

chambers (walls)
.

3
Hirzebruch surfaces and Nagata transformations

A Hirzebruch surface
\Sigma_{k},k\geq 0
is the space of the line bundle
q:\Sigma_{k}\rightarrow P^{1}
, i.e. a real rational ruled surface with exceptional real section
E_{k}
,
E_{k}^{2}=-k
. The fibers of the bundle
q
are called

vertical
, for example, we speak of vertical tangents, vertical
flexes, etc. The surface
\Sigma_{0}=P^{1}\times P^{1}
is a hyperboloid in which any curve of bidegree
(0,1)
can be taken as an exceptional section. When studying the walls of the space
C_{m,n}
, the exceptional section is considered to be a curve of bidegree
(0,1)
passing through a singular point of the curve from the wall.

Definition 1
.

A positive (negative) Nagata transformation
(see
[
10
, § 2 (3)]
) is a fiberwise birational transformation of
\Sigma_{k}\rightarrow\Sigma_{k+1}
(respectively,
\Sigma_{k}\rightarrow\Sigma_{k-1}
),
consisting of blowing up a point
p\in E_{k}
(respectively,
p\notin E_{k}
) and then contracting it to a point of the proper transform
of the fiber
q^{-1}(p)
.

A positive (negative) Nagata transformation takes
E_{k}
to
E_{k+1}
(respectively, to
E_{k-1}
).

4
Real trigonal curves

A trigonal curve
is a reduced curve
C\subset\Sigma_{k}
that does not contain the exceptional section or a fiber as a component and such that the restriction
q|_{C}
is a mapping of degree three. A trigonal curve
C
is called
proper
if
C\cap E_{k}=\varnothing
.

A fiber
F
of the surface
\Sigma_{k}
with a trigonal curve
C\in\Sigma_{k}
is called
singular
if
F
intersects
C\cup E_{k}
geometrically in fewer than four points.

According to
[
11
, p. 3.1.1]
for a proper trigonal curve
C
on the surface

\Sigma_{k},k\geq 1,
there exists an affine chart
(x,y)
on this surface in which the exceptional section
E_{k}
and the curve
C
are defined by the equation
y=\infty
and by the
Weierstrass equation

\,y^{3}+b(x)y+w(x)=0,

where
b,w
are real polynomials,
\deg b\leq 2k,\deg w\leq 3k
. We can extend the chart to
\Sigma_{k}\setminus E_{k}
, assuming that
b,w
are homogeneous polynomials of degrees
2k,3k
.

A non-singular proper real trigonal curve
C
is called
almost generic

if all critical points of the restriction
q|_{C}
, i.e. all roots of the discriminant

\Delta(x)=4b^{3}+27w^{2}
, are simple, and
maximally inflected
if all roots of the discriminant are real (and not necessarily simple).

A real proper trigonal curve
C
(possibly singular) is called

hyperbolic

if the restriction
{\mathbb{R}}C\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}P^{1}
of the map
q
is a three-sheeted covering.

For a real trigonal curve
C
, the rational function
j=j_{C}=\frac{4b^{3}}{\Delta}=1-\frac{27w^{2}}{\Delta}
is called the

j
-invariant
of this curve. A curve with a constant
j
-invariant is called
isotrivial
.
An almost generic curve is called
generic
if for each real critical value
t
of its
j
-invariant the multiplicity of each root of the equation
j(x)=t
is equal to
3
for
t=0
, is equal to
2
for
t\neq 0
and all these roots are real for
t\notin\{0,1\}
. Any almost generic trigonal curve can be transformed into a generic one by a small change in the coefficients of the curve equation.

The real part of a nonsingular non-hyperbolic curve
C
has a unique

long component

l
, characterized by the fact that the restriction
l\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}P^{1}
of the mapping
q

has degree
\pm 1
. For all other components of
{\mathbb{R}}C
,
called
ovals
, this degree is
0
. Let
Z\subset{\mathbb{R}}P^{1}
be a set of points
with more than one preimage in
{\mathbb{R}}C
. Each oval is mapped by
q

to a whole component of
Z
, which is also called an

oval
. The remaining components of
Z
,
as well as
their preimages
in
l
, are called
zigzags
.

By a
deformation
of a trigonal curve

C\subset\Sigma_{k}
we mean a deformation
of the pair
(q:\Sigma_{k}\rightarrow P^{1},C)

in the sense of Kodaira-Spencer.
A deformation of an almost generic trigonal curve
is called
fiberwise
if the curve remains
almost generic throughout the deformation.

Deformation equivalence
of real trigonal curves is defined by the equivalence relation generated by equivariant fiberwise deformations and real isomorphisms.

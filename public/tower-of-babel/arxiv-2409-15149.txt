Joint State-Channel Decoupling and One-Shot Quantum Coding Theorem
Hao-Chung Cheng 1 ​ – ​ 4 {}^{1\textrm{--}4} , Frédéric Dupuis 5 , and Li Gao 6

Abstract
. In this work, we consider decoupling a bipartite quantum state via a general quantum channel. We propose a joint state-channel decoupling approach to obtain a one-shot error exponent bound without smoothing, in which trace distance is used to measure how good the decoupling is. The established exponent is expressed in terms of a sum of two sandwiched Rényi entropies, one quantifying the amount of initial correlation between the state and environment, while the other characterizing the effectiveness of the quantum channel. This gives an explicit exponential decay of the decoupling error in the whole achievable region, which was missing in the previous results [ Commun. Math. Phys. 328, 2014 ]. Moreover, it strengthens the error exponent bound obtained in a recent work [ IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory, 69(12), 2023 ], for exponent from the channel part. As an application, we establish a one-shot error exponent bound for quantum channel coding given by a sandwiched Rényi coherent information.

Theorem 2

(One-shot achievability bound)
.

For any bipartite density operator
\rho_{AE}
and any quantum channel
\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}
with Choi state
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}:=(\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}\otimes\textnormal{id}_{A^{\prime}})(\Phi_{AA^{\prime}})
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\|\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}\left(U_{A}\rho_{AE}U_{A}^{\dagger}\right)-\omega_{C}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\e^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log 3\right)},\quad\forall\alpha\in[1,2].

Moreover, the error exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}\right)>0
is positive if and only if

H(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}>0
is satisfied.

In the i.i.d. scenario, our one-shot result holds for any number
n\in\mathds{N}
of copies, and thereby it demonstrates exponential decay of trace distance for the achievability criterion (
1

H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\overset{{\alpha}\to 1}{\longrightarrow}H(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}

without smoothing

The proposed argument is a

that places an equal and joint role to the state
\rho
and the channel
\cT

\rho

\mathcal{T}
simultaneous.

16

\rho

\mathcal{T}

\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}H_{2}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}
. Without smoothing, this can only give exponential achievability for the channel
\cT
being the partial traces. In contrast, our Theorem
2
gives an explicit achievable error exponent for all quantum channels

and states
\rho
.
Remarkably, the proposed joint state-channel decoupling framework works beyond independence
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}\otimes\rho_{AE}

Theorem 3

(One-shot strong converse bound)
.

For any bipartite density operator
\rho_{AE}
and for any quantum channel
\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}
with Choi state
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\|\mathcal{T}\left(U_{A}\rho_{AE}U_{A}^{\dagger}\right)-\omega_{C}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-2\mathrm{e}^{(1-\alpha)\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log\frac{4}{3}\right)},\quad\alpha\in(0,1).

In particular, the strong converse exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}(\alpha-1)\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\right)>0
is positive if and only if
H(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}<0
is satisfied.

Here, the Petz–Rényi conditional
{\alpha}
-entropy is defined as
H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A|E)_{\tau}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho_{AE}^{\alpha}(\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})^{1-\alpha}\right],\alpha\in(0,1)
.
By the limit
H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A|E)_{\rho}\overset{{\alpha}\to 1}{\longrightarrow}H(A|E)_{\rho}

(\rho_{A},\cT_{A\to C})
outside the (closure of) achievable region (
1

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A)_{\sigma}-(\mathrm{Q}+\mathrm{E})\right)};

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\beta\in[\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}},1]}\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\left(I_{\beta}^{*}(A\rangle C)_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}-(\mathrm{Q}-\mathrm{E})\right)}.

We also write

It is clear that

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\chi(\tau^{U})={\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\Theta(\tau^{U})=\tau_{CE}

Theorem 11

Let

\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\lVert{{\chi(\tau^{U})-\tau_{CE}}}\right\lVert_{1}\geq 1-2\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha-1)\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(AA^{\prime}|CE)_{\tau}+\log\frac{4}{3}\right)}\hskip 2.84544pt,

We use the short notation

,

and

Let

be an orthonormal basis (O.N.B.) of unitary operators with respect to the trace inner product. Denote

\ket{\Phi_{i}}=\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes V_{i}\ket{\Phi}\hskip 2.84544pt,\;\Phi_{i}=(\mathds{1}\otimes V_{i})\Phi(\mathds{1}\otimes V_{i})^{\dagger}=\Phi^{V_{i}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Then
\ket{\Phi_{i}}
is an O.N.B. of maximally entangled state and
\{\Phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
forms a projective-valued measurement. Then, for any

in the O.N.B.,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\chi(\tau^{V_{i}^{\dagger}U})=d^{2}\bra{\Phi}V_{i}^{\dagger}U\tau U^{\dagger}V_{i}\ket{\Phi}=d^{2}\bra{\Phi^{V_{i}}}U\tau U^{\dagger}\ket{\Phi^{V_{i}}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

For each

, we use the pretty-good measurement

(3.25)

\displaystyle\Pi_{U}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle:=\left(\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}\right)^{-1/2}\chi(\tau^{U})\left(\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}\right)^{-1/2},

where
\bar{\tau}=\mathbf{E}_{U}\chi(\tau^{U})
is the mean.
Then, using the variational formula of trace distance
[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\left\lVert{{\chi(\tau^{U})-\bar{\tau}}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[(\chi(\tau^{U})-\bar{\tau})\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\underbrace{{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{U})\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]}_{\text{(I)}}-\underbrace{{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\bar{\tau}\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]}_{\text{(II)}}.

Note that the second term (II) can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\bar{\tau}\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{U})\frac{\bar{\tau}}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{U})\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-\text{(I)}

Thus, it sufficient to lower bound the first term (I).
For the first term (I),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{U})\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\textrm{(a)}}{=}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{V_{i}^{\dagger}U})\frac{\chi(\tau^{V_{i}^{\dagger}U})}{\chi(\tau^{V_{i}^{\dagger}U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\textrm{(b)}}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}\frac{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}}{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}_{U}\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{U}\left(\Phi_{i}\otimes\frac{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}}{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}+\bar{\tau}}\right)\right].

Here, (a) uses the invariance of Haar measure,
and (b) follows from (
3.24
).
Note that by the direct sum structure

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Phi_{i}\otimes\frac{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}}{d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}+\bar{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}\sum_{i}\Phi_{i}\otimes\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}}{\sum_{i}\Phi_{i}\otimes(d^{2}\bra{\Phi_{i}}\tau^{U}\ket{\Phi_{i}}+\bar{\tau})}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})}{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})+\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}},

where
\mathcal{E}(\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}\Phi_{i}\tau\Phi_{i}
is the Pinching map onto the basis of
\{\ket{\Phi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
. Using the order inequality
d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau)\geq\tau

[
34
]
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\chi(\tau^{U})\frac{\chi(\tau^{U})}{\chi(\tau^{U})+\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{U}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})}{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})+\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{U}\frac{\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}}{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})+\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{U}\frac{\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}+d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})-\tau^{U}}{d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})+\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\textrm{(c)}}{\geq}1-{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(\tau^{U}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}+d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})-\tau^{U}\right)^{1-\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=1-{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{\alpha}\left(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}+d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})^{U^{\dagger}}-\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}\right].

Here, (c) uses the trace inequality (see
[
18
, Lemma 3]
) that for positive
X
and
Y

\operatorname{Tr}[Y(X+Y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}X(X+Y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left[X^{\alpha}Y^{1-\alpha}\right]\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha\in(0,1)

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})^{U^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}U^{\dagger}\Phi_{i}U\tau U^{\dagger}\Phi_{i}U

\displaystyle{=}d^{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\Phi^{U^{\dagger}}\tau\Phi^{U^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=d^{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\left[(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}CE})(\Phi_{AA^{\prime}}^{U^{\dagger}}\otimes\Phi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}^{U^{\dagger}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{CE})\right]

\displaystyle=d^{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\left[(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}CE})({\mathbf{E}}_{U}\Phi_{AA^{\prime}}^{U^{\dagger}}\otimes\Phi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}^{U^{\dagger}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{CE})\right].

Note that by Lemma
22
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\Phi_{A^{\prime}A}^{U^{\dagger}}\otimes\Phi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}^{U^{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d^{3}-d}F_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d^{3}-d}F_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{A\tilde{A}}+\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}F_{AA^{\prime}}\right),

where
F_{A}
is the swap operator on
\mathsf{A\otimes\tilde{A}}
.
Using the short notation,
F_{A}\equiv F_{A}\otimes\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}
and
F_{A^{\prime}}\equiv F_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{A\tilde{A}}

\displaystyle d^{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})^{U^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\left[(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}CE})\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\mathds{1}+\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}F_{AA^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d(d^{2}-1)}F_{A}-\frac{1}{d(d^{2}-1)}F_{A^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{CE}+\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\tau_{AA^{\prime}CE}-\frac{1}{(d^{2}-1)d}\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{ACE}-\frac{1}{(d^{2}-1)d}\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tau_{A^{\prime}CE}

Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\left[\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}+d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})^{U^{\dagger}}-\tau\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\right)\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes\tau_{CE}-\frac{1}{d(d^{2}-1)}\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{ACE}-\frac{1}{d(d^{2}-1)}\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tau_{A^{\prime}CE}-\frac{2-d^{2}}{d^{2}-1}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{CE},

where we have used the fact
\frac{1}{d^{2}-1}\leq\frac{1}{3}
for

. Putting this back to our estimate, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\left\lVert{{\chi(\tau^{U})-\bar{\tau}}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\geq 1-2{\mathbf{E}}_{U}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{\alpha}\left(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\bar{\tau}+d^{2}\mathcal{E}(\tau^{U})^{U^{*}}-\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-2\cdot\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{1-\alpha}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\tau^{\alpha}(\mathds{1}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{CE})^{1-\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=1-2\mathrm{e}^{(1-{\alpha})\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(AA^{\prime}|CE)_{\tau}+\log\frac{4}{3}\right)},

That finishes the proof.
∎

It is clear from the definition of

that for product density
\tau_{A^{\prime}ACE}=\omega_{A^{\prime}C}\otimes\rho_{AE}
,

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(AA^{\prime}|CE)_{\omega\otimes\rho}=H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A|E)_{\rho}

Then Theorem

follows from this special case as a corollary, which we restate here.

Corollary 12

(c.f. Theorem
3
)
.

For any density operator
\rho_{AE}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{E})
and for any quantum channel
\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}
with Choi state
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}
,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\|\mathcal{T}\left(U_{A}\rho_{AE}U_{A}^{\dagger}\right)-\omega_{C}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-2\mathrm{e}^{(1-\alpha)\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log\frac{4}{3}\right)},\quad\alpha\in(0,1).

In particular, the strong converse exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}(\alpha-1)\left(H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\right)>0
is positive if and only if
H(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}+H(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}<0
.

Example 13
.

For
\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{A}_{1}\otimes\mathsf{C}
and
\cT=\operatorname{id}_{C}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_{A_{1}}
being the partial trace,

\omega_{A^{\prime}C}=\frac{\mathds{1}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}}{|{A_{1}}|}\otimes\Phi_{C^{\prime}C}

and the Rényi conditional entropy is

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A_{1}^{\prime}C^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=H_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{\prime})_{\frac{\mathds{1}}{|{A_{1}}|}}+H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(C^{\prime}|C)_{\Phi_{C^{\prime}C}}

\displaystyle=\log|A_{1}|-\log|C|=\log\frac{|A|}{|C|}-\log|C|=\log\frac{|A|}{|C|^{2}}.

Remark 14
.

Note that our converse exponent

H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(A^{\prime}A|CE)_{\tau}\to H(A^{\prime}A|CE)_{\tau}\hskip 2.84544pt,

when
{\alpha}\to 1
. On the other hand, our achievability exponent is

H_{\alpha}^{*}(AA^{\prime}{\,|\,}CE)_{\tau}\to H(A^{\prime}A|CE)_{\tau}\hskip 2.84544pt,

as
{\alpha}\to 1
.
In the case of
\tau=\omega_{A^{\prime}C}\otimes\rho_{CE}
,

H(A^{\prime}A|CE)_{\tau}=H(A^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}+H(A|E)_{\rho}\hskip 2.84544pt,

This sum expression represents the counter forces in decoupling: if the state
\rho_{AE}
is more entangled, then it is harder to be decoupled; if the channel
A_{1}
is more entanglement-breaking, than it is easier to decouple a state
\rho_{AE}
. Two extreme examples: if
\rho_{AE}
is a maximally entangled state and
\cT=\operatorname{id}_{A}
is the perfect channel, then

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AE}^{U}-\frac{1}{d}\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}=\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}

for each
U
;
if
\cT=\operatorname{Tr}_{A}
is the fully depolarizing channel, the decoupling error is always zero.
From this perspective, our Theorem
8
and Theorem
11
together gives a clear asymptotic achievable region for quantum decoupling, and strong converse outside the closure of it. That is, in the i.i.d. asymptotic setting, the decoupling error of the channel
\cT^{\otimes n}
and the state
\rho_{AE}^{\otimes n}
using the twirling from
\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A}^{n})
will

(i)

decay exponentially to
0
if

H(A^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}+H(A|E)_{\rho}>0
.

(ii)

converges exponentially to
1
if

H(A^{\prime}|C)_{\omega}+H(A|E)_{\rho}<0
.
It is worth emphasizing that both Theorem
8
and Theorem
11
hold for arbitrary copies
n
.

3.3.
Randomness-assisted quantum decoupling

A crucial conceptual tool in previous subsections is the joint state-channel decoupling map, in which the state and the channel take a joint and symmetric role.
The achievability and strong converse theorem of the usual quantum decoupling follows as a special case of an tensor product joint state
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}\otimes\rho_{AE}
. This, in particular, generalizes one of our author’s previous result in which the conditional entropy term of the Choi state
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}
has not been ”Rényified”.
Furthermore, our joint state-channel decoupling approach allows us to handle a more general scenario where the underlying state
\rho
may be correlated with the decoupling channel
\cT
.

Suppose that the state to be decoupled and the decoupling channel are
statistically correlated
. That is, the system
AE
is prepared as a random bipartite
\rho_{AE}=\sum_{i}p(i)\rho_{AE}^{(i)}
for some probability distribution
p
, and moreover the randomness is shared to Alice such that for each preparation
\rho_{AE}^{(i)}
, Alice chooses to apply a decoupling map
\mathcal{T}^{(i)}
accordingly. We term this setting as
randomness-assisted quantum decoupling
(see Figure
1
below).
Now, the question, again, is how well the system
A
can be decoupled from
E
in this scenario?

This scenario is the special case of Theorem
8
and
11
applying to separable joint states.

Corollary 15

(One-shot randomness-assisted decoupling)
.

Let
\left\{p(i),\rho_{AE}^{(i)},\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}^{(i)}\right\}_{i}
be an ensemble of state-channel pairs. For any
{\alpha}\in[1,2]
and
\beta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle 1-2\mathrm{e}^{(1-\beta)(H_{\beta}^{\downarrow}(AA^{\prime}|CE)_{\tau}-\log\frac{4}{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\|\sum_{i}p(i)\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}^{(i)}\left(U_{A}\rho_{AE}^{(i)}U_{A}^{\dagger}\right)-\omega_{C}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{E}^{(i)}\right\|_{1}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}(H_{\alpha}^{*}(AA^{\prime}{\,|\,}CE)_{\tau}+\log 3)},

where
\tau_{A^{\prime}ACE}=\sum_{i}p(i)\omega_{A^{\prime}C}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{AE}^{(i)}
and
\omega_{A^{\prime}C}^{(i)}
is the Choi state of
\mathcal{T}^{(i)}
.

Remark 16
.

A naive approach to the upper bound (i.e., achievability) above is to apply the triangle inequality for the trace norm to bound the decoupling error for each realization
i
separately. Then, the trace distance will be dominated by exponential decay with the smallest error exponent:

(3.29)

\displaystyle\min_{i}\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho^{(i)}}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{{\omega}^{(i)}}\right),

Asymptotically, in the i.i.d. scenario where
\rho_{AE}^{(i)}\leftarrow(\rho_{AE}^{(i)})^{\otimes n}

and

\omega_{A^{\prime}C}^{(i)}\leftarrow(\omega_{A^{\prime}C}^{(i)})^{\otimes n}
,
the decoupling is achievable if

(3.30)

\displaystyle\min_{i}\left\{H(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho^{(i)}}+H(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{{\omega}^{(i)}}\right\}>0.

However, the Corollary (
15
) allows to harness the shared randomness to accomplish a much improved achievability criterion in terms of one single conditional entropy:

(3.31)

\displaystyle H(A^{\prime}A{\,|\,}CE)_{\tau}>0,

where the joint state is
\tau_{A^{\prime}ACE}:=\sum_{i}p(i)\cdot\omega_{A^{\prime}C}^{(i)}\otimes\rho_{AE}^{(i)}
.

Theorem 17

(One-shot quantum coding theorem)
.

Let
\psi_{ABR}
be a pure state,
\mathcal{N}_{A^{\prime}\to C}
be any completely positive trace-preserving map with Stinespring dilation
\mathcal{U}_{A^{\prime}\to CE}^{\mathcal{N}}

\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{A^{\prime}\to E}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{N}}\circ\mathcal{V}(\psi_{AR})-\omega_{E}\otimes\psi_{R}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\delta_{1}}+\delta_{2},\\

where the error terms are

(4.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime})_{\omega}-H^{*}_{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}(A)_{\psi}\right)};

(4.3)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\beta\in[1,2]}\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\left(H_{\beta}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime}{\,|\,}E)_{\omega}+H^{*}_{\beta}(A{\,|\,}R)_{\psi}\right)}.

Proof.

We follow the argument of
[
2

Let

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\lVert{{d_{A^{\prime}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}\left(\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}U(W\psi_{AR}W^{\dagger})U^{\dagger}\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}\right)-\omega_{E}\otimes\psi_{R}}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}\left(H_{\beta}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime}{\,|\,}E)_{\omega}+H^{*}_{\beta}(A{\,|\,}R)_{\psi}\right)}

(4.5)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\lVert{{d_{A^{\prime}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}U(W\psi_{ABR}W^{\dagger})U^{\dagger}\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}\right)-\psi_{BR}}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\alpha}-1}{{\alpha}}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime})_{\omega}-H^{*}_{\frac{{\alpha}}{2{\alpha}-1}}(A)_{\psi}\right)}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{{d_{A^{\prime}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}\left(\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}U(W\psi_{AR}W^{\dagger})U^{\dagger}\sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{1/2}\right)-\omega_{E}\otimes\psi_{R}}}\right\lVert_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}+\mathrm{E}<H(A)_{\sigma},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Q}-\mathrm{E}<I\left(A\rangle C\right)_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}.

Proof.

(4.14)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime})_{\omega}-H^{*}_{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}(\tilde{A}M)_{\psi}\right)};

(4.15)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle=6\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime}{\,|\,}E)_{\omega}+H^{*}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A}M{\,|\,}R)_{\psi}\right)}.

.
Since
\psi=\Phi_{RM}\otimes\Phi_{\tilde{A}B}
is a maximally entangled statement,
we have

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}(\tilde{A}M)_{\psi}=H^{*}_{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}\left(\frac{1}{d_{\tilde{A}M}}\mathds{1}_{{{\tilde{A}M}}}\right)=\log|\tilde{A}|\cdot|M|=\mathrm{Q}+\mathrm{E},

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A}M{\,|\,}R)_{\psi}=H^{*}_{\alpha}(\tilde{A})_{\psi}+H^{*}_{\alpha}(M{\,|\,}R)_{\psi}=\mathrm{Q}-\mathrm{E},

where for the second term we used Proposition
24
for product state
\psi_{\tilde{A}MR}=\frac{1}{d_{\tilde{A}}}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\Phi_{MR}
. The assertion follows from the duality of conditional
H_{\alpha}^{*}
that

H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}=-H_{\beta}^{*}(A^{\prime\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}=I_{\beta}^{*}(A\rangle C)_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}\hskip 2.84544pt,

for
\beta=\frac{{\alpha}}{2{\alpha}-1}
. When
{\alpha}\in[1,2]
,
\beta\in[\frac{2}{3},1]
. That finished the proof.
∎

The first error term
\delta_{1}
characterizes how well both the quantum information
\mathrm{Q}
and Alice’s share of entanglement
\mathrm{E}
fits into the channel input and the resulting error exponent. The second error term
\delta_{2}
corresponds how well the channel can be used to communicate quantum information without using entanglement, and we show that this is characterized by the sandwiched Rényi coherent information.
When
E=0
, this gives an one-shot error exponent for quantum communication
without
assistance of entanglement. On the other hand, if we do not limit the rate of entanglement assistance, our result implies that as long as
\mathrm{Q}<H(A)_{\sigma}+I(A\rangle C)_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}=\frac{1}{2}I(A{\,:\,}C)_{\mathcal{N}(\sigma)}
, we can choose a proper
\mathrm{E}
such that both the exponents of
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are positive, hence recovers the direct coding of entanglement-assisted quantum communication.

5.
Discussion on Interpretation of the error exponent

In this section, we discuss the error exponent obtained for quantum decoupling.
Because
\alpha\mapsto H_{\alpha}^{*}
is continuously decreasing, the positivity of the error exponent in Theorem
2
can be characterized as follows:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}\right)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_{1}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+H_{1}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}>0.

The above exponent can be interpreted as a trade-off or balance between two error exponents associated with different tasks by using Fenchel’s duality, which we illustrate below. For any bipartite density operator
\rho_{AB}
, we define the following error exponent functions:

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{mother}}}(R)_{\rho_{AE}}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log|A|-2R\right);

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{father}}}(R)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}C}}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(2R-I_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,:\,}C)_{\omega}\right),

where the order-
\alpha
sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy
H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}B)_{\rho}
and the sandwiched Rényi information are defined as

(5.4)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}:=-\inf_{\sigma_{E}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{E})}D_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\rho_{AE}\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt,\hskip 2.84544ptI_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,:\,}C)_{\rho}:=\inf_{\sigma_{C}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathsf{C})}D_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\omega_{A^{\prime}C}\|\omega_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{C}\right).

The term ‘
mother
’ stands for the mother protocol
[
6
]
, while ‘
father
’ stands for the father protocol
[
7
]
or the quantum splitting protocol. The
E^{*}_{\textnormal{{mother}}}(R)_{\rho_{AE}}
is motivated from Dupuis’ result
[
16
]
that, for
\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}=\Tr_{A\backslash C}
being a partial trace,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}_{\mathds{U}(\mathsf{A})}\left\|\Tr_{A\backslash C}\left(U_{A}\rho_{AE}U_{A}^{\dagger}\right)-\omega_{C}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\e^{-{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{mother}}}(\log|C|)_{\rho_{AE}}}},

where

(5.6)

\displaystyle E^{*}_{\textnormal{{mother}}}(\log|C|)_{\rho_{AE}}>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\log|C|<\frac{1}{2}\left(H\left(A{\,|\,}E\right)_{\rho}+\log|A|\right).

The bound in (

\mathcal{T}_{A\to C}=\Tr_{A\backslash C}

Also note that (

Then, the other error exponent that competes with

should correspond to the father protocol i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{father}}}(R)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}C}}}
.
Noting the Choi-Jamiołkowski state

in Theorem

(5.7)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}-\log|A|=-I_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,:\,}C)_{\omega}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log|A|\right)+\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(-I_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,:\,}C)_{\omega}\right)

Proposition 19

(A Fenchel duality for exponent functions)
.

Following the above notations, we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[1,2]}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}\right)=\inf_{R\geq 0}\left\{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{mother}}}(\log|C|)_{\rho_{AE}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}E^{*}_{\textnormal{{father}}}(\log|C|)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}C}}}\right\}.

Proof.

We first recall Fenchel’s duality theorem
[
35
]
.
Let
f
(resp.
g
) be proper convex function (resp. proper concave function) from some Banach space to extended real lines.
Then,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\inf_{x}\left\{f(x)-g(x)\right\}=\sup_{x^{*}}\left\{g_{*}(x^{*})-f^{*}(x^{*})\right\},

where

(5.13)

\displaystyle f^{*}(x^{*}):=\sup_{x}\left\{\langle x^{*},x\rangle-f(x)\right\}

is the convex conjugate of
f
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes an inner product, and similarly for
g_{*}
.

In view of the definitions given above, we let

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\in[-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0]\\
-f(x)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+\log|A|\right)\\
g(x)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(H_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,|\,}C)_{\omega}-\log|A|\right)=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}^{*}(A^{\prime}{\,:\,}C)_{\omega}\\
x^{*}=R\end{cases}

The the claim follows from the concavity of
x\mapsto-f(x)
and
x\mapsto g(x)

by using similar idea in
[

]

∎

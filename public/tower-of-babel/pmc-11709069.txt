Assessment of morbidity profile among workers of metallurgy factories processing aluminium in Howrah district of West Bengal
Authors: Debasish Biswas, Manabendra Sau, Gautam Paik, Kishore P. Madhwani, Amalendu Samanta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Workers who work in metallurgy factories processing aluminium are at risk of exposure to various kinds of metals and chemicals.Objective:To describe sociodemographic characteristics and to find out morbidity profile of study participants .Methods:A cross-sectional O bservational descriptive study was conducted in two aluminium processing metallurgy factories in Howrah district of Indian state of West Bengal. The sample size for the proposed research study was 424 workers. Data were collected through a predesigned and pretested questionnaire and were subsequently analyzed by using suitable statistical package.Results:The mean age of the workers was 45.46 years with standard deviation of 10.25 years. Maximum (28.54%) respondents had completed primary standard, and majority of respondents were from upper middle class (34.67%). 16.75% of workers reported that they suffered from communicable disease s, and 76.42% of participants reported noncommunicable diseases. 63.44% of study participants were overweight. This study showed musculoskeletal discomfort in different joints and body parts in 23.35% of participants; 2.82% of participants had ventilatory defects of lung. 3.07% of participants were suffering from hearing loss, and 5.90% of participants had refractive error of eyes.Conclusions:Noncommunicable disease burden was higher than communicable diseases among study participants. Almost one out of four participants reported musculoskeletal discomfort in different joints and body parts.

Introduction

Metallurgy is the art and science of extracting metals from their ores and modifying the metals for use. Metallurgy customarily refers to commercial as opposed to laboratory methods. It also concerns the chemical, physical and atomic properties and structures of metals and the principles whereby metals are combined to form alloys.[1] Iron, aluminium, zinc, copper etc., metals and their products are indispensable part of modern human life. Aluminium is the most widely used nonferrous metal. It is the most abundant metallic element in earth’s crust. Oxygen and silicon are two elements in universe that exceed the concentration of aluminium. It is concentrated in outer 16 km of earth’s crust, and 8% weight of which is contributed by aluminium.[2] According to world mineral data for 2023, Indian share of world mineral production for aluminium in 2021 was 4012000 metric ton with 5.93% of world’s production. Country wise, India occupies 2nd position in total world aluminium production for the year 2021.[3]

Often termed as Sheffield of the East, Howrah district of West Bengal is known as an engineering hub, mainly in the area of light engineering factories.[4] Metallurgy factories were concentrated in Howrah district of West Bengal.

Morbidity is defined as “any departure, subjective or objective, from a state of physiological well-being”.[5] Morbidities of workers of metallurgy factories may be grouped under communicable diseases, noncommunicable diseases, diseases related to workplace hazards and diseases described in relation to the exposure to aluminium metal. Workers may be susceptible to communicable diseases pertinent to local geographic context and or noncommunicable diseases. In addition, metallurgy factories and its workplaces expose workers to different occupational hazards e.g. physical hazards, chemical hazards, biological hazards, mechanical hazards, psychosocial hazards, hazards related to manual handling of materials etc. In addition, workers may suffer from morbidities related to exposure from such hazards. These workers are susceptible to heat stress which may lead to heat cramps, heat hyperpyrexia, heat syncope, heat stroke, burn etc. excessive sound that may lead to noise induced hearing loss (NIHL). Vibration may lead to Raynaud’s disease, anddust may cause lung diseases or Pneumoconiosis e.g. Aluminosis etc., Welding fumes may cause metal fume fever, workplace accidents and injuries in the form of abrasion, bruise, laceration, cut injury, amputation, fracture etc., and diseases may appear that are related to exposure of aluminium.

Literature search revealed low aluminium-related acute toxicity. Some chronic diseases due to aluminium exposure were documented in few published literatures. Neurotoxic effects like Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinsonism, dementia and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis were reported to be related to accumulation of aluminium in the brain. Chronic renal failure patients who were on dialysis had been reported to be at special risk of encephalopathy, osteomalacia and microcytic anemia.[678]

Hence, the present study was conducted with the following objectives:

To describe sociodemographic characteristics, types of work and occupational exposures among workers of metallurgy factories processing aluminium in Howrah district of West Bengal.To find out morbidity profile of study participants.

Material and Methods

The present c ross-sectional, observational descriptive study was carried out in metallurgy factories processing aluminium in Howrah district, West Bengal, comprised of workers of metallurgy factories processing aluminium. The study was carried out from May 2022 to November 2023.

Inclusion criteria

Workers who joined factories on or before 31 January, 2022, were included in the study.

Exclusion criteria

Workers below the age of 18 years, pregnant female workers and the workers of the selected factories who refused to take part in the study.

Sample size

Review of literature showed study on prevalence of morbidity pattern among iron and steel workers from an industry in central India. Overall prevalence of morbidities in that study was found to be 60%.[9] This prevalence (P = 60%) was considered to calculate sample size using Cochran’s formula.

Considering 15% of participants could have been non responders, sample size was calculated to be (368.79 + 55.30) = 424.09. It was rounded off to 424.

All nine aluminium processing metallurgy factories were requested for approval to carry out the research study. Two of the nine factories present in the Howrah district gave permission for conducting the study.

Department-wise and job-wise factory workers lists were obtained from two factories. Sampling frame was prepared by giving serial number to each worker. Consecutive sampling technique was applied to select the participants.

Predesigned, pretested, semistructured and interviewer administered questionnaire was used.

Data analysis

Data were compiled in Microsoft Excel spread sheet to prepare the master chart. Some variable data were derived using collected primary data. Text, tables, and figures were used to present data. Mean ± SD were calculated for quantitative data. Frequency and percentage (%) were calculated for qualitative data.

Discussion

Sociodemographic characteristics of workers of the present study are shown in Table 1. The findings revealed that approximately half of the participants were in the age group of 50–58 years. Minimum and maximum ages of the participated workers were 20 years and 58 years, respectively. The mean age of the workers was 45.46 years with standard deviation 10.25 years. In a study on morbidity pattern among iron and steel workers from an industry in central India by Biswas MJ et al. in 2014, majority (42.5%) of workers were found to belong to age group of 35–44 years.[9]

In the present study, 16.75% of workers reported that they suffered from communicable disease that included diarrhoea, dengue, malaria and typhoid during last 12 months in decreasing order of frequency. 76.42% of participants reported noncommunicable diseases. 63.44% of study participants were overweight, and 78.77% of participants had waist hip ratio ≥ 0.9 in our study. NFHS 5 data for overweight men in West Bengal showed that 16.2% were overweight or obese with BMI ≥ 25.0 kg/m2.

Our study revealed higher proportion of overweight and waist hip ratio participants than men from general population which may be due to higher socioeconomic status and increased automation at their workplace leading to sedentary lifestyle. 30.42% of participants were hypertensive in this study which was higher compared to 20.1% hypertensive men in West Bengal as per NFHS 5 data. In addition, 16.99% of participants were diabetic in the present study which was lower than NFHS 5 data for high or very high blood sugar level or taking medicine to control blood sugar i.e. and 21.3%. 5.43% of participants were mild anemic in the present study, whereas 38.9% of men in the age group of 15–49 years were anemic as per NFHS 5 data for West Bengal.[10] Anemia was less prevalent among the participated workers which may be due to higher proportion of participants with class I and class II socio economic status that might had led to personal hygiene and dietary patterns to maintain normal hemoglobin. This study showed musculoskeletal discomfort in different joints and body parts in 23.35% of participants. 8.25% of participants had cardiac abnormalities. 2.82% of participants had ventilatory defects of lung. 3.07% of participants were suffering from hearing loss. Also, 5.90% of participants had refractive error of eyes. Ventilatory impairment of lungs are known to occur from many causes including pneumoconioses. Similarly, hearing loss has many reasons including exposure to high intensity noise. This being a descriptive, cross-sectional study was unable to show association between different variables and morbidities. Analytical studies may be planned in future to explore further on the issues.

In the present study, 3.54% of participants had suffered from work-related injuries in last 1 year. As the safety day in 2023 was celebrated on 4 March, the theme was “our aim-zero harm”. Considering the theme of safety day, in 2023, proportion of participants in the present study who got work-related injury was undesirable. It is pertinent to say that in depth study of work-related injuries to find out root cause coupled with strengthened preventive measures such as enhanced safety at workplace might prevent future recurrences. However, both factories had round the clock occupational health service-related infrastructure including occupational health centres (OHC), doctors, pharmacists or medically trained manpower, dedicated ambulance with driver. Initial and early treatment of the injury victim at workplace was provided with the help of these resources. The factories were also referral ready by using their dedicated ambulance to transfer injury victim at higher centres when needed.

A cross-sectional study on respiratory health status and its predictors among coal-based sponge iron plant workers in Barjora, India, by Chattopadhyay K et al. was published in 2015. The study enrolled 258 participants. It used St. George’s respiratory questionnaire (SGRQ) with total score ranging from 0 to 100 that represent the best and worst possible respiratory health status, respectively. The study result showed that best possible SGRQ total score was reported by 46.9% of workers. In the present study, it was found that 2.82% of workers suffered from ventilatory impairment of the lung. The study result by Chattopadhyay K et al. was higher than our study as the studies were questionnaire based and pulmonary function test based, respectively.[12]

An article published in 2012 by Chebotarev AG et al. from Russia on work conditions and occupational morbidity in workers of aluminium production enterprises found 60.4% occupational diseases among electrolysis workers with average length of service of 26.3 years.[13] In the present study, it was found that 83.49% of workers had morbidity which is higher than the study of Chebotarev AG et al. As the morbid conditions considered in this study were different communicable diseases, noncommunicable diseases, work-related injuries and different organ and system specific diseases the burden might have been found to be more than the other study. Another study on contemporary work conditions and occupational morbidity in metallurgists by Chebotarev AG et al. in Russia, published in 2012, showed occupational morbidity in 17.85% of cases per 10000 workers examined for the year 2010.[14]

Conclusion

Four out of five participants in the present study reported morbidity. Almost three out of four participants of the present study reported noncommunicable diseases, and one out of six participants reported communicable diseases. Noncommunicable disease burden was higher than communicable diseases among study participants. Almost one out of four participants reported musculoskeletal discomfort in different joints and body parts. Cases of work-related injuries were reported which is not in consonance with vision zero accidents at workplace assuming that all injuries at workplace are preventable. Half of the study participants reported sickness absenteeism that indicates loss of productivity. Strengthening of existing health promotion activity at workplace may give healthier work force. Association between variables and morbidities, planning of adequate work–rest cycle might be hypotheses for future research studies

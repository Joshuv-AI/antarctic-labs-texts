Counting Isomorphism Classes of Spanning Trees of Complete Bipartite Graphs
Peter Johnson Auburn University johnspd@auburn.edu and Shayne Nochumson Auburn University snk0018@auburn.edu

Abstract
Spanning trees of complete bipartite graphs exhibit a rich interaction between degree sequences and graph structure. In this paper, we obtain lower bounds on the number of isomorphism classes of spanning trees in K a , b , 2 ≤ a ≤ b K_{a,b},2\leq a\leq b in terms of P a ​ ( a + b − 1 ) P_{a}(a+b-1) and P b ​ ( a + b − 1 ) P_{b}(a+b-1) where P k ​ ( m ) P_{k}(m) is the number of integer partitions of m m of length k k . Furthermore, we obtain an upper bound in terms of a a and b b .

Introduction

For a given graph
G
, we denote by
\tau(G)
the number of labeled spanning trees of
G
.
\tau(G)
has been widely studied with classical results such as Kirchoff’s Matrix-Tree Theorem
[
3
]
from 1847 and Cayley’s formula
[
1
]
proven by Borchardt in 1861. In 1962, Scoins proved that
\tau(K_{a,b})=a^{b-1}\cdot b^{a-1}

[
4
]
. The following year, Glicksman gave an alternate proof for Scoins’ formula
[
2
]
. We consider a question related to that of Scoins. How many isomorphism classes of spanning trees are there of
K_{a,b}
? We denote this value by
I_{a,b}
and prove a lower bound by considering integer partitions of
a+b-1
. We then prove a upper bound on
I_{a,b}
which can be calculated using only
a
and
b
.

Main Results

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
2\leq a\leq b
and
a+b=n
. For any pair of integer partitions
s_{1}\geq...\geq s_{a}>0
and
t_{1}\geq...\geq t_{b}>0
of
a+b-1
, there is a connected bipartite graph
Q=Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
with bipartitions
A,B
where
|A|=a,|B|=b
and where the
s_{i}
are the degrees in
Q
of vertices in
A
and the
t_{j}
are the degrees in
Q
of the vertices in
B
.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on
a+b=n
. The smallest allowable
n
is
n=2+2=4
. The only partition of length
2
of
n-1=3
is
3=2+1
.

Suppose that
n=a+b>4
; and we have integers
s_{1}\geq...\geq s_{a}>0
and
t_{1}\geq...\geq t_{b}>0
where
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{a}s_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{b}t_{j}=a+b-1
. If
t_{b}>1
then
t_{1}+...+t_{b}\geq 2b>a+b-1
. Therefore,
t_{b}=1
. On the other hand,
a<a+b-1=s_{1}+...+s_{a}\leq as_{1}
, so
s_{1}\geq 2
.

Let
s_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}s_{i}-1,&\text{if }i=1\\
s_{i},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Then
s_{i}^{\prime},...,s_{a}^{\prime}
are positive integers summing to
a+(b-1)-1=t_{1}+...+t_{b-1}
. By the induction hypotheses, there is a connected bipartite graph
Q^{\prime}((s_{1}^{\prime},...,s_{a}^{\prime}),(t_{1},...,t_{b-1}))
with bipartition
A,B^{\prime}
where
|A|=a,|B^{\prime}|=b-1
and where the
s_{i}^{\prime}
are the
Q^{\prime}

A
and
t_{1},...,t_{b-1}

. Let
u\in A
be a vertex with degree
s_{1}-1
in
Q^{\prime}
, let
v\not\in A\cup B^{\prime}
, and let
B=B^{\prime}\cup\{v\}.
Let
Q=Q^{\prime}\cup uv
. Then
Q=Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
is a connected bipartite graph with bipartition
A,B
with the vertices in
A
having degrees

.

Lemma 2.2
.

A tree is a spanning tree of
K_{a,b}
if and only if it is isomorphic to some such
Q
.

Proof.

Suppose
T
is a spanning tree of
K_{a,b}.
Since
T
spans
K_{a,b}
,
|V(T)|=a+b
and
|E(T)|=a+b-1
. Let
A,B
denote the partite sets of
K_{a,b}
where
|A|=a
and
|B|=b
. As
K_{a,b}
is bipartite,
uv\in E(T)
if and only if
u
and
v
lie in different partite sets. Then the degrees of vertices in
A
must be an integer partition of
a+b-1
. Similarly for the degrees of
B
. Thus,
T
is isomorphic to some
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
where
s_{1}\geq...\geq s_{a}>0
and
t_{1}\geq...\geq t_{b}>0
are integer partitions of
a+b-1
.

Next, consider some
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
for some pair of integer partitions of
a+b-1
,
s_{1}\geq...\geq s_{a}>0
and
t_{1}\geq...\geq t_{b}
. By Lemma 2.1,
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
is a connected bipartite graph. Since
|E(Q((s_{i}),(t_{j})))|=a+b-1
,
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
must be a tree. As
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
is a tree with bipartite sets of sizes
a
and
b
,
Q((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
must be a spanning tree of
K_{a,b}
.

∎

Let
I_{a,b}
denote the number of isomorphism classes of spanning trees of
K_{a,b}
, and let
P_{k}(m)
denote the number of integer partitions of
m
of length
k
.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
b>a\geq 2
,
I_{a,b}\geq P_{a}(a+b-1)\cdot P_{b}(a+b-1)
.

Proof.

Consider two distinct pairs of integer partitions
((s_{1},...,s_{a}),(t_{1},...t_{b}))
and

((s_{1}^{\prime},...,s_{a}^{\prime}),(t_{1}^{\prime},...t_{b}^{\prime}))
. By Lemma 2.1, there is a connected bipartite graph
Q

(Q^{\prime}
resp.) with bipartitions
A,B

(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
resp.) of sizes
a
and
b
. By Lemma 2.2,
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are each a spanning tree of
K_{a,b}
. Since
((s_{i}),(t_{j}))
and
((s_{i}^{\prime}),(t_{j}^{\prime}))
differ, the degree sequences of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
differ, so
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
cannot be isomorphic trees because
b>a
. Then, any ordered pair of integer partitions generates at least one isomorphism class of spanning trees of
K_{a,b}
, and spanning trees corresponding to different pairs are not isomorphic. Thus,
I_{a,b}\geq P_{a}(a+b-1)\cdot P_{b}(a+b-1)
.

∎

Corollary 2.3.1
.

If
2\leq a\leq b
, then
a^{b-1}\cdot b^{a-1}\geq P_{a}(a+b-1)\cdot P_{b}(a+b-1)
.

Corollary 2.3.1 is a direct consequence of Scoins’ formula and Theorem 2.3.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
b=a\geq 2
, then
I_{a,a}\geq\dfrac{r(r+1)}{2}
where
r=P_{a}(2a-1)
.

Proof.

Let
K_{a,b}
have bipartitions
A,B
such that
|A|=|B|=a
, let
P=\{p_{1},...,p_{r}\}
denote the set of integer partitions of
2a-1
of length
a
, and let
P^{\prime}=\{p_{1}^{\prime},...,p_{r}^{\prime}\}
where
p_{i}^{\prime}=p_{i}
. For any pair
(s,t)
where
s\in P
and
t\in P^{\prime}
, there exists at least one spanning tree
Q
of
K_{a,a}
by Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2. Consider some
(s,t)=(p_{k},p_{l}^{\prime})
and
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(p_{l},p_{k}^{\prime})
. As
|A|=|B|=a
and
K_{a,b}
is unlabeled, any spanning tree
Q
generated by
(s,t)
must be isomorphic to some spanning tree
Q^{\prime}
generated by
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
. Then, the number of isomorphism classes of spanning trees of
K_{a,b}
where
a=b
must be bounded by the number of unordered pairs
s^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
where
s^{\prime\prime}\in P
and
t^{\prime\prime}\in P^{\prime}
. There are
r
pairs such that
s^{\prime\prime}=t^{\prime\prime}
and
\displaystyle{r\choose 2}
pairs where
s^{\prime\prime}\not=t^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, there are
r+\dfrac{r(r-1)}{2}=\dfrac{2r+r^{2}-r}{2}=\dfrac{r(r+1)}{2}
unique unordered pairs of integer partitions of
2a-1
where each is of length
a
. Since each pair must generate at least one isomorphism class,
I_{a,a}\geq\dfrac{r(r+1)}{2}
.

∎

Corollary 2.4.1
.

If
a\geq 2
, then
a^{2a-2}\geq\dfrac{P_{a}(2a-1)\cdot(P_{a}(2a-1)+1)}{2}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
2\leq a<b
,
I_{a,b}\leq I_{a,a}\cdot a^{b-a}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

I_{2,2}=1

I_{2,3}=2
. Thus,
2=I_{2,3}\leq I_{2,2}\cdot 2^{3-2}=1\cdot 2=2
.

Suppose that
n=a+b>5

G
is the complete bipartite graph of order
n
with bipartitions
A
and
B
where
|A|=a
and
|B|=b
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the complete bipartite graph with bipartition
A

B^{\prime}
where
|A|=a
and
|B^{\prime}|=b-1
. By the induction hypothesis,
I_{a,b-1}\leq I_{a,a}\cdot a^{b-1-a}
. Suppose that

. Let

Theorem 2.6
.

If
2\leq a<b
,
I_{a,b}\leq a^{a+b-2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma 2.5,
I_{a,b}\leq I_{a,a}\cdot a^{b-a}
. Since
I_{a,a}\leq a^{2a-2}
by Scoins’ formula,
I_{a,b}\leq I_{a,a}\cdot a^{b-a}\leq a^{2a-2}\cdot a^{b-a}=a^{a+b-2}
.
∎

Note that Theorem 2.6 gives a nontrivial bound when
2\leq a<b
. Scoins’ formula gives an obvious upper bound of
a^{b-1}\cdot b^{a-1}
as it enumerates the number of labeled spanning trees of
K_{a,b}
. However, when
2\leq a<b
,
a^{a+b-2}=a^{a-1}\cdot a^{b-1}<a^{b-1}\cdot b^{a-1}
.

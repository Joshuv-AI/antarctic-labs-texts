Concordance of folate receptor expression in ovarian cancer over time: A single-institution retrospective study
Authors: Mitra Abdollahi Neisani, Francis E. Marrero, Roberto Angeli-Morales, Sinan G. Khudhur, Kevin J. Wu, Yaohua Ma, Sophia G. Blumenfeld, Karina Hanna, Gerardo Colon-Otero, Aziza Nassar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFolate receptor alpha (FRα) is frequently expressed in ovarian high-grade serous carcinomas and is a clinically actionable biomarker targeted by FDA-approved mirvetuximab soravtansine (MIRV) for platinum-resistant disease with high expression (≥75% of tumor cells with ≥2+ staining). However, longitudinal variability in FRα expression remains poorly understood. We evaluated temporal FRα expression changes in paired ovarian cancer specimens.MethodsWe performed a retrospective study of epithelial ovarian malignancies at Mayo Clinic Florida (2013–2025) in patients with ≥2 tumor specimens available. Tumors were classified as FRα-positive when ≥75% of viable tumor cells showed moderate-to-strong (2+ and/or 3+) staining and categorized as concordant or discordant based on changes in FRα status. Associations between clinicopathologic variables and FRα variability were evaluated using Fisher exact and Kruskal–Wallis tests.ResultsSixty-six patients (median age 66 years), most with advanced-stage high-grade serous carcinoma (65/66), had paired tumor specimens. FRα expression was concordant in 77.3% of cases and discordant in 22.7%. Negative-to-positive conversion occurred more frequently than positive-to-negative conversion (16.7% vs 6.1%). No significant associations were identified between FRα variability and clinicopathologic factors, including age, grade, stage, treatment setting, prior chemotherapy exposure, MIRV use, specimen type, sampling site, baseline FRα expression, or timing intervals. In patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, negative-to-positive conversion was numerically more frequent, although not statistically significant (25% vs 9%; p = 0.479).ConclusionsFRα expression in ovarian carcinomas is generally stable over time, with concordance observed in most paired specimens. However, discordance in approximately one-quarter of cases suggests spatial and/or temporal tumor heterogeneity.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains a leading cause of gynecologic cancer–related mortality worldwide, largely because it is often diagnosed at an advanced stage and frequently develops resistance to chemotherapy over time. Although many patients initially respond to treatment, most recurrences occur within three years of initial diagnosis and are commonly associated with chemotherapy resistance (Lheureux et al., 2019). These challenges highlight the need for effective targeted therapeutic strategies.

Folate receptor alpha (FRα) is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored cell surface protein that binds folate with high affinity and mediates its transport into cells (Lheureux et al., 2019). Folate is essential for DNA and RNA synthesis and cellular growth. FRα was first identified as a cancer-associated antigen in ovarian cancer in the early 1990s and is expressed in more than 90% of epithelial ovarian cancers, with minimal expression in most normal tissues (Kalli et al., 2008). This enhanced expression in tumor cells makes FRα an attractive therapeutic target. Higher levels of FRα expression have been associated with advanced disease stage and higher tumor grade, suggesting a role in tumor progression (Moore et al., 2021). Importantly, the clinical benefit of currently FDA approved FRα-targeted therapies depends on the degree of expression, making accurate and reproducible immunohistochemical (IHC) assessment essential (Matulonis et al., 2023).

Mirvetuximab soravtansine (MIRV) is an antibody–drug conjugate composed of a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting FRα, linked to the maytansinoid DM4, a microtubule-disrupting agent. MIRV is FDA-approved for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (PROC) in patients whose tumors demonstrate high FRα expression, defined as ≥75% tumor cell positivity with moderate to strong membranous staining by IHC (Moore et al., 2017). In clinical practice, PROC is defined as disease progression or recurrence within six months after platinum-based chemotherapy (Markman, 1998). The phase 3 MIRASOL trial showed that MIRV improves response rates, progression-free survival, and overall survival compared with standard chemotherapy (Moore et al., 2023).

Although the current FDA approval is restricted to tumors with high FRα expression, emerging evidence suggests that MIRV-based combination therapies may also be effective in tumors with lower levels of FRα expression. In the phase I FORWARD II study, patients with FRα positivity in ≥25% of tumor cells exhibiting at least moderate membranous staining were eligible to receive MIRV in combination with bevacizumab. This regimen demonstrated promising antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile (Matulonis et al., 2023). These findings indicate that lower thresholds of FRα expression may also have therapeutic relevance depending on the treatment strategy employed, further emphasizing the importance of accurate and reproducible assessment of FRα expression. Similar to other predictive biomarkers, such as HER2 and PD-L1, FRα expression may vary over time and between different tumor sites (Angerilli et al., 2025). Such variability can affect eligibility for targeted therapies depending on the specimen used. In MIRV trials, no restriction was placed on specimen timing for FRα testing.

It has been reported that progression-free survival (PFS) with mirvetuximab soravtansine (MIRV) is not impacted by the age of the sample submitted for FRα testing relative to the patient's date of diagnosis; however, the stability of FRα expression during disease progression in patient-matched samples remains incompletely understood (Cecere et al., 2026).

Previous studies have generally suggested that FRα expression remains relatively stable during disease progression and after chemotherapy exposure. Despierre et al. reported no significant differences in FRα expression among primary, interval debulking, and recurrent ovarian carcinomas, while Crane et al. similarly demonstrated minimal changes in FRα expression following chemotherapy (Crane et al., 2012; Despierre et al., 2013). More recently, Swisher et al. reported high concordance rates across longitudinal clinical trial and real-world samples, supporting the potential utility of archival tissue for FRα assessment (Swisher et al., 2025). However, these studies were limited by relatively small numbers of paired patient-matched samples, heterogeneous sampling approaches, and limited evaluation of clinicopathologic and specimen-related factors that may contribute to biomarker variability. Furthermore, emerging evidence has demonstrated substantial intratumoral and spatial heterogeneity in FRα expression, with variability observed across different anatomic sites and even within the same tumor (Liang et al., 2026; Krivak et al., 2025). These findings raise important questions regarding the reliability of a single specimen for therapeutic decision-making and highlight the need for additional studies evaluating longitudinal changes in FRα expression and factors associated with discordance.

In addition, several patient- and specimen-related factors, including prior chemotherapy exposure, neoadjuvant treatment, specimen type, anatomic sampling site, and the interval between specimen collections, may potentially influence FRα expression and contribute to temporal and spatial heterogeneity. However, the effect of these variables on longitudinal FRα assessment remains poorly characterized. Given that high FRα expression is associated with response to MIRV and that therapeutic eligibility depends on predefined expression thresholds, understanding the temporal stability of FRα expression is clinically important. In addition, patient- and specimen-related factors, including treatment exposure, specimen type, anatomic sampling site, and timing of specimen acquisition, may influence FRα assessment and contribute to biomarker heterogeneity. Therefore, in this study, we evaluated FRα expression in paired ovarian cancer specimens obtained from the same patients at different time points. The primary objective was to assess longitudinal changes in FRα status, and the secondary objective was to determine whether clinicopathologic and specimen-related characteristics influence FRα concordance and biomarker determination.

Materials and methods

We performed a retrospective review of patients with epithelial ovarian malignancies evaluated at Mayo Clinic Florida between January 1, 2013, and July 1, 2025. Patients with at least two pathology-confirmed specimens were included. IRB approval was obtained.

FRα expression was evaluated on FFPE tissue using the VENTANA FOLR1 (FOLR1–2.1) RxDx Assay (Roche Diagnostics) following manufacturer and institutional protocols. Tissue was fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 12–72 h, sectioned at ∼4 μm, and stained using automated methods.

Quality controls were included in each run. FRα staining was independently assessed by two pathologists (A.N. and K.W.), with discrepancies resolved by a third reviewer. Samples were scored as per the interpretation guide accompanying the VENTANA FOLR1 (FOLR1–2.1) RxDx Assay. Tumors were classified as FRα-positive if ≥75% of viable tumor cells showed moderate to strong membranous staining (Dilawari et al., 2023; Bax et al., 2023). Specimens were categorized by anatomic location (pelvic, abdominal, thoracic) and classified as same-site or different-site pairs.

Cases were classified as concordant if FRα status remained unchanged between paired specimens and discordant if a change in status was observed (See Fig. 1). Among discordant cases, the direction of change (negative-to-positive vs positive-to-negative) was recorded. All patients received chemotherapy between the two sampling timepoints. In the neoadjuvant subgroup, the first specimen represented the pretreatment diagnostic biopsy and the second specimen represented the interval debulking specimen obtained after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Continuous variables were analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test and categorical variables using Fisher exact test. Analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.1), with a p-value of less than 0.05 being considered significant. FRα expression was analyzed both as a continuous variable and using clinically relevant categorical thresholds. Continuous paired comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test because the distribution of FRα expression was bimodal and did not satisfy the normality assumption. Results

Sixty-six patients with paired ovarian carcinoma specimens were included. Baseline clinicopathologic characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 66 years (range, 39–80). Most cases were high-grade serous carcinoma (98.5%) and a single case (1.5%) of carcinosarcoma, with advanced-stage disease (stage III–IV). No clinicopathologic variables including age, tumor grade, stage, treatment setting, prior chemotherapy exposure, or mirvetuximab use were associated with FRα concordance (all p > 0.05) (Table 1).

Overall, 77.3% (51/66) of cases demonstrated concordant FRα expression, while 22.7% (15/66) were discordant (Table 2). Importantly, discordance was predominantly driven by negative-to-positive conversion (16.7%) [See Fig. 1], whereas positive-to-negative conversion was less frequent (6.1%), highlighting that changes in FRα status may influence therapeutic eligibility in a subset of patients (Table 2). Among the 66 paired cases, most specimens were obtained from newly diagnosed patients (47/66) who underwent sampling at initial biopsy followed by interval debulking after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. When stratified by initial FRα status, tumors that were negative at baseline demonstrated a higher rate of change compared to initially positive tumors (25.0% [9/36] vs 9.1% [1/11]); however, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.479).

Further analysis of clinicopathologic and specimen-related variables (Table 1) demonstrated that FRα concordance was not significantly associated with the type of the initial specimen (fine needle biopsy versus surgical resection), the anatomic site of the first specimen, receipt of chemotherapy prior to the initial sampling, or whether paired specimens were obtained from the same versus different anatomic sites (all p > 0.05). Concordance rates were therefore comparable regardless of whether the initial assessment was performed on a limited biopsy or a surgical resection specimen. Although not statistically significant, surgical first specimens demonstrated a numerically higher mean FRα expression compared with fine needle biopsy specimens (50.0% vs 39.2%, p = 0.11).

Temporal analyses similarly demonstrated no significant association between concordance status and the interval between the first and second specimens, the interval from diagnosis to either specimen, or the interval between treatment and second sampling (all p > 0.05). However, patients with concordant FRα results had a significantly longer interval between diagnosis and last follow-up compared with discordant cases (mean 264 vs 161 weeks, p = 0.045). Given the absence of associations between concordance and other specimen timing variables, this finding likely reflects differences in overall clinical course rather than a direct temporal effect on FRα expression. The anatomic site of the second specimen showed an association with discordance (p = 0.025). However, given the limited number of discordant cases and the pragmatic nature of specimen acquisition, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and require validation in larger cohorts. Discordant cases more frequently involved second specimens obtained from metastatic sites, particularly omental and other extra-ovarian locations, whereas peritoneal second specimens were observed exclusively in concordant cases. In addition, discordant cases demonstrated significantly higher FRα expression in the second specimen compared with concordant cases (68.3% vs 43.9%, p = 0.026).

In contrast, analyses evaluating the spatial relationship between paired samples demonstrated no significant association between concordance status and whether the two specimens were obtained from the same versus different anatomic sites. Although paired specimens obtained from the same anatomic site showed a numerically higher rate of FRα positivity in the second specimen compared with different-site sampling (52.6% vs 29.8%, p = 0.097), overall concordance rates remained similar between same-site and different-site groups (78.9% vs 76.6%, p = 1.000), and no association was identified between sampling pattern and FRα concordance status or discordance (p = 0.258). Collectively, these findings indicate that discordance is not simply attributable to spatial sampling from different anatomic locations but may instead reflect biologic variability associated with particular metastatic sites.

Given NCCN guidelines recommending the use of MIRV in combination with bevacizumab in platinum resistant ovarian cancers with a FRα expression of 25% or higher (Swisher et al., 2025), we decided to evaluate the effect of different FRα expression thresholds. Conversion rates were evaluated across multiple cutoffs, with rates calculated relative to baseline classification at each cutoff.

At the 25% cutoff, 72.7% of paired samples were concordant. Among discordant cases, negative-to-positive conversions occurred significantly more frequently than positive-to-negative conversions (47.4% [9/19] vs 19.1% [9/47], p = 0.043), suggesting a directional shift toward increased FRα expression at lower thresholds. This pattern was not observed at higher cutoff values.

At the 50% cutoff, 69.7% of paired samples were concordant, with negative-to-positive and positive-to-negative conversion rates of 40.6% (13/32) and 20.6% (7/34), respectively (p = 0.133). At the clinically relevant 75% cutoff, 77.3% of paired samples were concordant, and conversion rates were nearly identical between groups (22.4% [11/49] vs 23.5% [4/17], p = 1.00), indicating no directional difference in FRα conversion at higher expression thresholds. Collectively, these findings suggest that directional shifts in FRα expression are more apparent at lower expression thresholds, whereas FRα status appears more stable at higher thresholds, including the currently approved ≥75% threshold for MIRV monotherapy.

FRα expression was also evaluated as a continuous variable. Because the distribution of FRα expression was bimodal, paired comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The median FRα expression was 50% (range, 0–95%) in the first specimen and 50% (range, 0–100%) in the second specimen. Discussion

Folate receptor alpha (FRα) has emerged as an important biomarker in ovarian carcinomas due to its role as a predictive marker for response to treatment with MIRV. Understanding the dynamics of FRα expression over time is critical for optimizing therapeutic decision-making.

In the present study, FRα expression status demonstrated relative stability over time, with concordant results observed in 77.3% of paired samples, although a substantial minority of cases (22.7%) showed discordance. These findings align with prior studies that showed minimal changes across disease progression and treatment. Despierre et al. (2013) reported no significant differences in FRα expression between primary, interval debulking, and recurrent tumor samples (Despierre et al., 2013). Similarly, Crane et al. (2012) found no significant alteration in FRα expression after chemotherapy in both interval and recurrent settings (Crane et al., 2012). In addition, Swisher et al. (2025) demonstrated high concordance rates (∼86%) across longitudinal biopsies, further supporting the reliability of FRα assessment over time (Swisher et al., 2025). In line with these studies, we also observed no significant difference in overall mean FRα expression between paired specimens (45.4% vs 49.4%, p = 0.253), reinforcing that FRα expression is largely maintained despite systemic therapy and supporting the use of archival tissue for biomarker evaluation in most clinical scenarios.

While these findings largely confirm previous reports, our study also extends the existing literature by providing a more comprehensive evaluation of factors that may contribute to longitudinal variability in FRα expression. Previous studies primarily focused on overall concordance rates and included relatively limited numbers of paired patient-matched samples. In contrast, we systematically evaluated the influence of clinicopathologic and specimen-related variables, including treatment exposure, specimen type, anatomic sampling site, and temporal intervals between specimen collections. Additionally, our findings suggest that specific metastatic sites, particularly omental and extra-ovarian sites, may be associated with increased discordance, providing further insight into the spatial heterogeneity of FRα expression and identifying clinical scenarios in which repeat testing may be informative.

Importantly, despite the overall stability of FRα expression observed at the cohort level, our findings also highlight that FRα expression remains dynamic in a subset of patients. Notably, changes were bidirectional, with 16.7% of initially negative tumors converting to positive and 6.1% of initially positive tumors converting to negative. These findings suggest that variability in FRα expression is not simply the result of progressive upregulation of FRα during tumor evolution but may rather reflect a complex interplay of intratumoral heterogeneity, spatial sampling variability, and potential treatment-related modulation. These observations are consistent with Liang et al. (2026), who also demonstrated high concordance rates, including 88.9% in the neoadjuvant setting, but identified a subset of cases (11.1%) with conversion from negative to positive. Importantly, Liang et al. (2026) also highlighted substantial intratumoral and spatial heterogeneity, with 40.7% of tumors containing both positive and negative regions and significant variability across anatomic sites such as the adnexa and omentum.

In line with these findings, we observed that discordance was more frequently encountered in metastatic sites, particularly the omentum, and that the anatomic site of the second specimen was significantly associated with discordance. Prior studies have similarly demonstrated that FRα expression varies across tumor sites and histologic subgroups, reflecting underlying biological diversity in FOLR1 regulation (Krivak et al., 2025). This is further supported by Liang et al. (2026), who demonstrated marked spatial heterogeneity in FRα expression across specimen types and anatomic locations, with variability observed even within the same tumor.

Within the neoadjuvant subset, concordance remained high; however, a numerical predominance of negative-to-positive conversion was observed (25.0% vs 9.1%). Although not statistically significant, this suggests that a subset of tumors initially classified as FRα-negative may become positive over time. This observation aligns with Liang et al. (2026), who demonstrated an increase in FRα expression following neoadjuvant chemotherapy, as well as in the recurrent setting, where 24.0% of cases converted from negative to positive and 8.0% from positive to negative, with an additional subset exhibiting bidirectional changes across timepoints. Collectively, these findings support a dynamic model of FRα expression and reinforce the potential impact of tumor evolution on biomarker status in a subset of patients. Since the majority of cases are concordant, further work is needed to better understand what is driving the change in biomarker expression in this smaller subset of patients.

Evaluation of less stringent FRα cutoffs demonstrated that patterns of directional change were threshold-dependent. A significantly higher rate of negative-to-positive conversion was observed only at the 25% cutoff, whereas no significant directional differences were identified at the 50% or clinically relevant 75% thresholds. These findings are consistent with prior studies showing that FRα expression is heterogeneous but generally preserved in ovarian cancer (Swisher et al., 2025).

Collectively, these results suggest that variability observed at lower cutoffs likely reflects minor fluctuations in staining intensity or sampling-related heterogeneity rather than biologically meaningful alterations in FRα expression. In contrast, the relative stability observed at higher thresholds, particularly at the clinically relevant 75% cutoff, supports the robustness and reproducibility of FRα assessment when current therapeutic eligibility criteria are applied. Importantly, the absence of a directional shift at the 75% threshold further supports the reliability of current FRα-based treatment selection criteria. Consistent with prior literature, clinicopathologic variables, including age, stage, tumor grade, treatment modality, and specimen type, were not significantly associated with FRα concordance. These findings suggest that FRα variability is driven primarily by tumor-intrinsic biological factors rather than conventional clinical parameters. In addition, the method of tissue acquisition (fine needle biopsy vs surgical biopsy) did not significantly influence FRα expression, supporting the robustness of assessment across specimen types, although prior studies have reported variability related to specimen source and processing (Liang et al., 2026).

From a clinical perspective, these findings are important. Our results confirm that FRα expression is generally stable over time, consistent with prior studies, supporting the use of archival tissue in most cases. However, the observed discordance, particularly negative-to-positive conversion, suggests that FRα expression can change in some tumors. Emerging evidence, including Liang et al. (2026), also shows that temporal and spatial heterogeneity may affect biomarker status. Therefore, repeat FRα testing using more recent tumor samples may be helpful in selected patients, especially those with initially negative or borderline results or with disease progression at metastatic sites. Further work is required to understand, however, how patients that display negative-positive conversions perform on MIRV. This is particularly relevant since eligibility for MIRV therapy depends on defined expression thresholds.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design and modest sample size limit statistical power. In addition, the cohort was predominantly composed of high-grade serous carcinomas, limiting generalizability to other histologic subtypes. Prospective studies with standardized sampling and correlation with treatment outcomes are needed to clarify the clinical significance of FRα variability, particularly in newly diagnosed patients treated with neoadjuvant therapy.

Conclusion

FRα expression is largely stable across paired ovarian carcinoma specimens, supporting its role as a reliable predictive biomarker and suggesting that archival tissue may be appropriate for FRα assessment in many patients being considered for MIRV therapy. However, discordance in a subset of cases, particularly negative-to-positive conversion and increased variability in metastatic sites, underscores the impact of temporal and spatial tumor heterogeneity. These findings suggest that repeat FRα testing using a more recent specimen may be clinically beneficial in selected patients, particularly those with initially negative or borderline FRα expression, prolonged disease evolution, or progression at metastatic sites, where reassessment may alter therapeutic eligibility for MIRV.

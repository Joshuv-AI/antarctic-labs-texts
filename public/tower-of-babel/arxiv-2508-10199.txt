Genus stabilization for the Homology of moduli spaces of orbit-framed curves with symmetries-I Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 14H15, 14H30, 14H37, 20J05, 30F60, 55M35, 57M12, 57S05. Keywords: Algebraic curves with automorphisms, moduli spaces, mapping class group, Teichmüller space, genus stabilization, Hurwitz monodromy vector, homological invariant.
Fabrizio Catanese, Michael Lönne, Fabio Perroni

Abstract
. In a previous paper, [ CLP16 ] , we showed that the moduli space of curves C C with a G G -symmetry (that is, with a faithful action of a finite group G G ), having a fixed generalized homological invariant ε \varepsilon , is irreducible if the genus g ′ g^{\prime} of the quotient curve C ′ := C / G C^{\prime}:=C/G satisfies g ′ >> 0 g^{\prime}>>0 . Interpreting this result as stabilization for the 0-th homology group of the moduli space of curves with G G -symmetry, we begin here a program for showing stabilization for all the homology groups of these spaces, in similarity to the results of Harer [ Har85 ] for the moduli space of curves. In this first paper we prove homology stabilization for a variant of the moduli space where one G G -orbit is tangentially framed, settling in particular homology stabilization for the case of at most one branch point.



1.
Introduction

The main purpose of this article is to prove a first result concerning the stabilization of the homology of
the moduli spaces of curves admitting a given symmetry group
G
.

Recall that Harer
[
Har85
]
proved that the homology groups of the moduli space of curves
\mathfrak{M}_{g}

stabilize for
g>>0
, indeed, more precisely,

H_{k}(\mathfrak{M}_{g},\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{k}(\mathfrak{M}_{g+1},\mathbb{Q}),\ {\rm for}\ g\geq 3k+1.

Fix
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g}
to be a fixed compact oriented differentiable manifold of real dimension
2
and genus
g
:
letting
\mathcal{C}S(\Sigma)
be the space of complex structures on
\Sigma
compatible with the given orientation,
then the moduli space of curves

\mathfrak{M}_{g}
, and the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
are the quotients:

\mathfrak{M}_{g}:=\mathcal{C}S(\Sigma)/{\rm Diff}^{+}(\Sigma),\ \mathcal{T}_{g}:=\mathcal{C}S(\Sigma)/{\rm Diff}^{0}(\Sigma)\ \Rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{g}=\mathcal{T}_{g}/{\rm Map}_{g}=\mathcal{T}_{g}/\Gamma_{g}

where
\Gamma_{g}:={\rm Map}_{g}=\pi_{0}({\rm Diff}^{+}(\Sigma))={\rm Diff}^{+}(\Sigma)/{\rm Diff}^{0}(\Sigma)
.

Teichmüller’s theorem says that
\mathcal{T}_{g}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3g-3}
is an open subset diffeomorphic to a ball.
Moreover
\Gamma_{g}={\rm Map}_{g}
acts properly discontinuously on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
, but not freely.

Hence the rational cohomology (resp. homology) of the moduli space is calculated by group cohomology (resp. group homology):

H^{*}(\mathfrak{M}_{g},\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(\Gamma_{g},\mathbb{Q}),H_{*}(\mathfrak{M}_{g},\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{*}(\Gamma_{g},\mathbb{Q}).

Indeed, Harer proved some stronger results concerning the integral homology of the groups
\Gamma^{n}_{g,r}
, mapping class
groups of the groups
{\rm Diff}^{+}(\Sigma_{g,r}^{n})
of diffeomorphisms of a Riemann surface
\Sigma
with
n
-punctures and
r
boundary components,
acting as the identity on the boundary curves: that, for
g
large, these are independent of
g,r
(to consider also the punctures and the boundary components was necessary to pursue several inductive steps).

Inside the moduli space of curves there are some important special subvarieties, corresponding to curves
admitting a
G
-symmetry, namely such that there is an injection
G\hookrightarrow\Aut(C)
, where
G
is
a fixed finite group (of order
\leq 84(g-1)
by Hurwitz’ theorem, if, as we throughout assume here,
g\geq 2
).

These subvarieties are the union of locally closed sets which are better understood by considering a
G
-marked moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)
,
whose irreducible components are determined by the type of the topological action of
G
on
C
.

The topological type (and the differentiable type) of the action is completely
determined by the action of
G
on the fundamental group
\Pi_{g}
of
C
, up to inner
automorphisms.
Hence we get an injective group homomorphism

\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow\frac{{\rm Aut}(\Pi_{g})}{{\rm Inn}(\Pi_{g})}={\rm Out}(\Pi_{g})\,

with image in an index
2
subgroup:

\rho\colon G\hookrightarrow{\rm Out}^{+}(\Pi_{g})=\Gamma_{g}\cong\frac{{\rm Diff}^{+}(C)}{{\rm Diff}^{0}(C)}.

It turns out that curves with a fixed topological type of action
\rho
correspond to the fixed locus

\mathcal{T}_{g,\rho}:=\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\rho(G)}
of
\rho(G)
in
\mathcal{T}_{g}
, which (see
[
Cat00
]
) is diffeomorphic to a ball: in particular the
rational cohomology of these components
\mathfrak{M}_{g,\rho}
is then calculated through group cohomology
of the normalizer of
\rho(G)
inside the mapping class group.

In practice, however, these components
\mathfrak{M}_{g,\rho}\subset\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)

are better understood through the classical study of the quotient map

C\rightarrow C^{\prime}:=C/G.

We let
g^{\prime}:=g(C^{\prime})
be the genus of the quotient curve
C^{\prime}
,
{\mathcal{B}}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}
be the branch locus, and
let
m_{i}
be the branching multiplicity
of
y_{i}
(assuming
m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\dots\leq m_{d}
).

These numbers
g^{\prime},d,m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}
are constant in each
irreducible variety
\mathfrak{M}_{g,\rho}
, they form the ‘primary numerical type’ of the cover

C\to C^{\prime}
, and

C\to C^{\prime}
is determined (by virtue of Riemann’s existence theorem) by the monodromy:

\mu\colon\pi_{1}(C^{\prime}\setminus{\mathcal{B}},y_{0})\to G\,.

We have:

\pi_{1}(C^{\prime}\setminus{\mathcal{B}},y_{0})\cong\Pi_{g^{\prime},d}:=\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g^{\prime}},\beta_{g^{\prime}}|\prod_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{g^{\prime}}[\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}]=1\rangle,

hence the datum of
\mu
is equivalent to the datum of a Hurwitz -vector

v:=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{g^{\prime}},b_{g^{\prime}})\in G^{d+2g^{\prime}}

s.t.
(1)
G
is generated by
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{g^{\prime}},b_{g^{\prime}}
,
(
c_{i}\not=1_{G}

\forall i
), and

(2)
\prod_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{g^{\prime}}[a_{j},b_{j}]=1.

It turns out also that the number of the
components of
\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)
is independent of
g^{\prime}
if the genus
g^{\prime}
of the quotient curve is large enough:
for this purpose, as in Harer’s theorem,
one needs to define direct stabilization: this is done considering a curve obtained adding a handle to the curve
C^{\prime}
,
and extending the monodromy in a trivial way to the handle:

\mu(\alpha_{g^{\prime}+1})=\mu(\beta_{g^{\prime}+1})=1\in G.

A breakthrough was made by Nathan Dunfield and William Thurston
in the étale case where the action of
G
is free:

Theorem 1.1

(Dunfield-Thurston)
.

In the unramified case (
d=0
), for
g^{\prime}>>0
, the unmarked topological types
are in bijection with

\frac{H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})}{{\rm Aut}(G)}\,.

The second homology of
G
appears here naturally, since the monodromy
\mu:\pi(C^{\prime})\rightarrow G

yields a corresponding continuous map of classifying spaces
f:C^{\prime}\rightarrow B(G)
, and
the image of the fundamental class of
C^{\prime}
via
H_{2}(f)
yields the homology
invariant
\varepsilon\in H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

In
[
CLP16
]
we extended the above theorem to the ramified case with a technically complicated
procedure:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
g^{\prime}>>0
, the unmarked topological types
are in bijection with the set of admissible classes via the map
\hat{\varepsilon}
associated to the generalized homology invariant
\varepsilon

introduced in
[
CLP15
]
.

Interpreting the previous result as a stabilization result for the group
H_{0}(\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G),\mathbb{Z})

it was natural to ask for an extension of this result to higher homology groups, as done by Harer for

\mathfrak{M}_{g}
.

For the purpose of explaining how this goes, we want, following Hain and Looijenga
[
Ha-Lo97
]
, to spell out some other interpretation
of the groups
\Gamma^{n}_{g,r}
as mapping class groups
1
1

1

we warn however the reader that, in order to have a
readable notation to carry our induction, we shall denote here the genus by
n
, and the number of boundary components by
r
,
hence our
\Gamma^{r}_{n}
will be the group
\Gamma_{n,r}
of Harer and others.
.

Again, fix an oriented differentiable Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g^{\prime}}
with
n
fixed points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
r

fixed points
y_{1},\dots y_{r}
(the points are all distinct) and a fixed tangent vector
v_{j}\neq 0
at each
y_{j}
. We can
take the quotient of the space of complex structures on
\Sigma
by different
groups

{\rm Diff}(\Sigma)^{n}_{r}\subset{\rm Diff}(\Sigma)^{n+r}\subset{\rm Diff}(\Sigma)

where the first subgroup fixes all the points
x_{i},y_{j}
individually and acts as the identity on the tangent space
at each point
y_{j}
, the second just fixes all the points
x_{i},y_{j}
.

The resulting moduli spaces are then

\mathfrak{M}_{g^{\prime},r}^{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{g^{\prime}}^{r+n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{g^{\prime}},

where the first one is the moduli space of complex curves
C^{\prime}
with
n
marked points and
r
marked (nonzero)
tangent vectors, the second is the moduli space complex curves
C^{\prime}
with
n+r
marked points.

The corresponding mapping class groups are

\Gamma_{g^{\prime},r}^{n}\rightarrow\Gamma_{g^{\prime}}^{n+r}\rightarrow\Gamma_{g^{\prime}},

since to a Riemann surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
r
boundary components we can attach a disk along each boundary circle,
and if we have a diffeomorphism acting as the identity (in a neighbourhood of) on the boundary of
\Sigma^{\prime}
we
can extend it to the disk as the identity.

To compare the picture downstairs on
C^{\prime}

to the picture upstairs on
C
, we observe that the choice of a tangent vector
v_{j}
at
y_{j}
is equivalent to
a tangent framing of the
G
-orbit
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
lying above
y_{j}
: this means that we give
|G|
tangent vectors
at the points of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
which are permuted simply transitively by
G
,
and we call this a
tangential orbit framing
.

Again we have corresponding Teichmüller spaces, hence calculating the rational cohomology groups
of our moduli spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)
boils down to calculations related with
some cohomology (homology) of groups related to the mapping class groups.

The road to do this was paved by an important paper by Ellenberg, Venkatesh and Westerland

[
E-V-W16
]
concerning Hurwitz spaces, which has been for us an important source of inspiration.

We introduce here some different (noncommutative) rings of connected components, and we consider certain
spectral sequences associated to a Koszul type complex. We need to extend many of the
homological algebra results of

[
E-V-W16
]
, since we work here with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients instead of coefficients taken in a given field.

Moreover, for some crucial result concerning the degeneration of a first page spectral sequence,
the complex of chains used by Harer is no longer suitably sufficient.

Hence the second new ingredient which we successfully have used is the complex
of tethered chains, recently introduced by Hatcher and Vogtmann
[
Hat-Vog17
]
.

This is our main result

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)^{*}
be the moduli space of curves
C
with a
G
-action
and with one tangentially-framed
G
- orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
, such that the action of
G
is free outside
of
{\mathcal{O}}
.

Then, for
g>>0
, the homology group
H_{i}(\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)^{*},\mathbb{Q})

is independent of
g
.

We could of course establish a more general theorem taking
r
tangentially-framed
G
- orbits
{\mathcal{O}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{O}}_{r}
,
but the main line of argument, while remaining entirely similar, needs a quite heavy notation.

Observe moreover that, if in Theorem
1.3
we restrict to the subspace of the moduli space
where the orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
is in bijection with
G
(i.e., the local monodromy at

is the identity),
we obtain an unramifed covering

, hence our moduli space maps onto

.

We plan, in a future paper, to show homology stabilization also for the space

\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)^{unr}
(considered in
[

]
) and, in general, for the
more general space
\mathfrak{M}_{g}(G)

Our main Theorem
1.3
is a consequence of a more technical result, which we would now like to illustrate.

First of all, we start from the Hurwitz vectors, which describe the monodromy of the covering
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}=C/G
:
in order to be consistent with the notation used later on, we denote now the genus
g^{\prime}
by
n
.

The Hurwitz vectors of type
n
are tuples in
G^{2n}
of the following form

\left\{\>(a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\dots,a_{n},b_{n})\>\big|\>a_{i},b_{i}\in G\>\right\}

The mapping class group
\Gamma^{1}_{n}
acts on them using the identification
of
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime},*),G)

with
G^{2n}
by means of a geometric basis.

Let us denote by
R_{n}:=\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}/\Gamma^{1}_{n}\rangle
, for
n>0
, and set
R_{0}:=\mathbb{Z}
.

We show the existence of natural homomorphisms yielding a non-commutative graded ring structure on

R:=\oplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}\,.

This graded ring is called the
ring of connected components
, it depends heavily on the group
G
,
and we consider (graded) left
R
-modules, establishing analogues of some homological algebra results
of
[
E-V-W16
]
, which we have to prove in detail since we are dealing with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}

and not in a field.

Our typical example is the homology-module whose
q
-graded piece is

(1)

M(q)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}M(q)_{n}:=\oplus_{n\geq 0}H_{q}(\Gamma^{1}_{n},\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}\rangle)\,.

Note that
M(0)=R
.

For every
\ell\geq 0

and
(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime})\in G^{2\ell}
, the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}\rangle

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n+2\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

is equivariant with respect to the inclusion
\Gamma^{1}_{n}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{1}_{n+\ell}
,
and, considering the class
\llbracket a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime}\rrbracket

of
(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime})
inside

, it induces a homomorphism

\displaystyle H_{q}(\Gamma^{1}_{n},\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}\rangle)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H_{q}(\Gamma^{1}_{n+\ell},\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n+2\ell}\rangle)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\llbracket a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime}\rrbracket m\,

hence it defines a structure of graded left
R
-module on
M(q)
.

The algebraic analogue of direct stabilization is now expressed by an operator
U
, such that
U(m)=\llbracket 1,1\rrbracket m
,
which is the main actor on the stage.

Then the main homological argument is concerned with bounding,
via several exact sequences, the degrees of certain graded modules, especially the Kernel and Cokernel
of multiplication by

of a graded module

is defined as
{\rm deg}(M):=\sup\{n\,|\,M_{n}\not=0\}

With the above notation, our technical result (Theorem
4.1
)
is

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Let
R
be the ring of connected components associated to

,
and let
M(q)
be the graded left

-module defined in (
1
).

Then there are homological constants
\tilde{A}(R)
and

such that
the homomorphism

{U}\colon M(q)_{n}\to M(q)_{n+1}

is an isomorphism for every

The statement of the above theorem is exactly the stabilization statement for homology.

2.
Chain complexes and spectral sequences

A
Hurwitz vector of type

n
is a tuple of the following form,

(a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\dots,a_{n},b_{n})\quad\mbox{where}\quad a_{i},b_{i}\in G\,,

therefore
G^{2n}
is also regarded as the set of such vectors.
Let us fix a geometric basis
\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\}

of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0})
, with
y_{0}\in\partial\Sigma_{n}^{1}
,
and let us identify
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0}),G)
with
G^{2n}
as usual:

\mu\in\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0}),G)
corresponds to

(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{n}):=(\mu(\alpha_{1}),\mu(\beta_{1}),\ldots,\mu(\alpha_{n}),\mu(\beta_{n}))\,.

The mapping class group
\Gamma^{1}_{n}
acts naturally on
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0})
, and hence on
G^{2n}
,
by means of the previous identification. More precisely, for
\gamma\in\Gamma^{1}_{n}
and

\mu\in\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0}),G)
,

\gamma\cdot\mu:=(\gamma^{-1})^{*}\mu\,.

Notice that this is a left-action.

2.1.
The simplicial complex of tethered chains

We recall here some definitions from
[
Hat-Vog17
]
.
A
chain
in
\Sigma_{n}^{1}
is an ordered pair
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})
of simple closed curves
intersecting transversely in a single point, together with an orientation on
\hat{\beta}
.
A
tethered chain
in
\Sigma_{n}^{1}
is a pair
\left((\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}),t\right)
, where
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})

is a chain
and
t
is an arc connecting the positive side of
\hat{\beta}
with a point in some finite collection
P
of disjoint open intervals
(not circles) in
\partial\Sigma_{n}^{1}
, such that the interior of
t
is disjoint from
\hat{\alpha}
,

\hat{\beta}
and
\partial\Sigma_{n}^{1}
.
In our situation
P
always consists of one interval containing the base point
y_{0}
.

The
complex of tethered chains on
\Sigma_{n}^{1}
,
{T\!C\!h}_{n}^{1}
, is the simplicial complex
whose vertices correspond to isotopy classes of tethered chains, and a set of vertices spans a simplex
if the tethered chains can be chosen to be disjoint.

We refer to
[
Hat-Vog17
]
for the main properties and details about these notions,
but let us recall here that
{T\!C\!h}_{n}^{1}
is
\frac{1}{2}(n-3)
-connected (Proposition 5.5,
loc. cit.
).
Note also that
\Gamma_{n}^{1}
acts simplicially on
{T\!C\!h}_{n}^{1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The complex of
G
-marked tethered chains is the simplicial complex defined as the Cartesian product

X_{n}:={T\!C\!h}_{n}^{1}\times G^{2n}\,.

It will be considered with the diagonal left action of
\Gamma_{n}^{1}
.

Notation 2.2
.

Let us first remark that, for a tethered chain
((\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}),t)
, the orientation on
\hat{\beta}
is determined by

,
because

is allowed to intersect
\hat{\beta}
only from its positive side.
Moreover, the geometric basis
\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\}

of

determines

vertices of

, as we explain now.
For every
i=1,\ldots,n
, we choose a representative of

(resp.

) consisting of an arc

joining

with the intersection point of a chain

, then it goes through

(resp.

), and comes back to

. The isotopy class of

Finally, for every

We will denote by

2.2.
The spectral sequences

In this subsection we introduce two spectral sequences,
\tilde{E}_{p,q}^{r}(n)
and
E^{r}_{p,q}(n)
,
both converging to the equivariant homology groups
H^{\Gamma^{1}_{n}}_{p+q}(G^{2n})
, if

of the second one,

When there is no danger of confusion, to have a less heavy notation, we will denote

We refer to

For fixed

, let

-modules,

Let

(defined in Def.

) with integer coefficients.
Then we consider the double complex
C_{\bullet,\bullet}=(C_{p,q})_{p,q\geq 0}
, where

C_{p,q}:=F_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma_{n}^{1}}S_{q}\,,\quad p,q\geq 0\,,

with the horizontal differential
\partial^{\prime}
of degree
(-1,0)
(induced by the differential of

)
and the vertical one

The total complex associated to

We filter

These filtrations yield spectral sequences

For the first one we have that

\tilde{E}^{0}_{p,q}(n)=\tilde{\Phi}_{p}(T_{p+q})/\tilde{\Phi}_{p-1}(T_{p+q})=C_{p,q}

with differential

by

H_{p}^{\Gamma^{1}_{n}}(G^{2n})&\mbox{for}\quad q=0\,.\end{cases}

We conclude that

For the second spectral sequence we have that

{E}^{0}_{p,q}(n)={\Phi}_{p}(T_{p+q})/{\Phi}_{p-1}(T_{p+q})=C_{q,p}

and

where the last group is the homology of

with coefficients in the module

The differential

3.
\mathcal{K}
-complexes

In this section we introduce the so-called “Koszul-like complexes” (or
\mathcal{K}
-complexes),
which are chain complexes that have the same structure of the second spectral sequence defined in Section
2

(as described in Proposition
2.4
). We also study some homological algebraic properties of these complexes.
Here we will follow
[
E-V-W16
, Sec. 4.]
.

\mathcal{K}
-complexes are associated to graded modules over the so-called “ring of connected components”
R
,
which we define now.

Let
\Sigma^{1}_{m}
and
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
be two compact oriented surfaces of genus
m
and
n
with one boundary component.
Let us fix geometric basis
\{\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{m}^{\prime},\beta_{m}^{\prime}\}
of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{m}^{1},y_{0}^{\prime})

and
\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\}
of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{n}^{1},y_{0})
.
If we glue
\Sigma^{1}_{m}
and
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
together, along two intervals in the boundaries, in such a way that
y_{0}^{\prime}

coincides with
y_{0}
,
we obtain a surface
\Sigma^{1}_{m+n}
together with a geometric basis of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{m+n}^{1},y_{0})
,

\{\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\alpha_{m}^{\prime},\beta_{m}^{\prime},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}\}
.

The natural homomorphisms
\Gamma^{1}_{m},\Gamma^{1}_{n}\to\Gamma^{1}_{m+n}
, obtained by extending
every homeomorphism of
\Sigma^{1}_{m}
(respectively of
\Sigma^{1}_{n}
) to
\Sigma_{m+n}^{1}
by the identity,
are injective by the Dehn-Nielsen-Baer Theorem. Therefore we identify
\Gamma^{1}_{m}

and
\Gamma^{1}_{n}
as subgroups of
\Gamma^{1}_{m+n}
. Since these subgroups commute, we obtain an inclusion

\Gamma^{1}_{m}\times\Gamma^{1}_{n}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{1}_{m+n}\,.

Note that the map

\displaystyle G^{2m}\times G^{2n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle G^{2m+2n}

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m}^{\prime},b_{m}^{\prime})\times(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m}^{\prime},b_{m}^{\prime},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

is equivariant with respect to this inclusion, therefore it induces a group-homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2m}/\Gamma^{1}_{m}\rangle\times\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}/\Gamma^{1}_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2m+2n}/\Gamma^{1}_{m+n}\rangle

\displaystyle\llbracket a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m}^{\prime},b_{m}^{\prime}\rrbracket\times\llbracket a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\llbracket a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{m}^{\prime},b_{m}^{\prime},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n}\rrbracket

Let us denote by
R_{n}:=\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}/\Gamma^{1}_{n}\rangle
, for
n>0
, and
R_{0}:=\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the previous homomorphisms yield a non-commutative ring structure on

R:=\oplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}\,.

This graded ring is called the
ring of connected components
, it depends on the group
G
.

Remark 3.1
.

We have group homomorphisms

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle R_{n+1}

\displaystyle\llbracket a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\llbracket 1,1,a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n}\rrbracket\,,

which are isomorphisms for every
n
greater than a certain integer
[
DT06
]
.
In
[
CLP16
]
we have extended this result to the case of ramified coverings.

Notation 3.2
.

We denote by
R_{>0}
the two-sided ideal
\oplus_{n>0}R_{n}
of
R
. Moreover we endow
\mathbb{Z}
with a structure
of left and right
R
-module by identifying it with
R/{R_{>0}}
.

In this article all graded
R
-modules are concentrated in non-negative degrees.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
R
be the ring of connected components associated to the group
G
.
Let
M=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a graded left
R
-module (by convention
M_{n}=\{0\}
for
n<0
).
The
\mathcal{K}
-complex of
M

is the complex
(\mathcal{K}(M),d)
, where the term of degree
p
,
\mathcal{K}(M)_{p}
, is
0
, if
p<0
, and

\mathcal{K}(M)_{p}:=\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2p}\rangle\otimes M[p]\,,\qquad{\rm if}\quad p\geq 0\,,

where
M[p]
is
M
shifted by
p
(i.e.
(M[p])_{n}=M_{n-p}
), and by definition

\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2p}\rangle
has degree
0
. The differential
d
is defined as follows:

d\colon\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2p+2}\rangle\otimes M[p+1]\to\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2p}\rangle\otimes M[p]

(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{p+1},b_{p+1})\otimes m\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{k-1}(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,\widehat{a_{k},b_{k}},\ldots,a_{p+1},b_{p+1})\otimes\llbracket a_{k}^{d_{k}},b_{k}^{d_{k}}\rrbracket m\,,

where (as usual)
\widehat{a_{k},b_{k}}
means that we omit
a_{k},b_{k}
from
(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{p+1},b_{p+1})
,
and
d_{k}:=[a_{k+1},b_{k+1}]\cdots[a_{p+1},b_{p+1}]
.

Remark 3.4
.

By standard computations it is easy to verify that
(\mathcal{K}(M),d)
is a complex.

In this article our main example will be the following.

Example 3.5
.

For every

[

For every

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}\rangle

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n+2\ell}\rangle

\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime},a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{n})

is equivariant with respect to the inclusion
\Gamma^{1}_{n}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{1}_{\ell+n}
,
and from the discussion at the beginning of this section it induces an homomorphism

\displaystyle H_{q}(\Gamma^{1}_{n},\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n}\rangle)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H_{q}(\Gamma^{1}_{n+\ell},\mathbb{Z}\langle G^{2n+2\ell}\rangle)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\llbracket a_{1}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{\ell}^{\prime},b_{\ell}^{\prime}\rrbracket m\,.

This defines a structure of left
R
-module on

. Note that

.

Let
(\mathcal{K}(M(q)),d)
be the
\mathcal{K}
-complex of

.
Note that, for
p>1
, the term in degree
n
of

coincides with
d^{1}\colon E^{1}_{p-1,q}(n)\to E^{1}_{p-2,q}(n)
(the differential described in Proposition
2.4
).
Therefore, for

3.1.
Homological algebra of
R

We collect here the main properties of homological algebra that we need about graded
R
-modules.
We refer to
[
Bro
]
and
[
We94
]
for background material.

In particular, let us recall that the category of graded right (resp. left)
R
-modules
is an abelian category and that every graded right (resp. left)
R
-module
N
has a free resolution in this category,

\ldots\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to N\to 0\,,

which we also denote
F_{\bullet}\to N\to 0
.
Moreover, for a graded left
R
-module
M
, the tensor product
\_\otimes_{R}M
is a right exact functor from the category
of graded right
R
-modules to the category of bi-graded abelian groups and the groups
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(\_\ ,M\right)

(for
i\geq 0
) are its left derived functors. Concretely, for any graded right
R
-module
N
, choose
a free (or projective) graded resolution
F_{\bullet}\to N\to 0
as above and define as usual

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right):=\frac{\ker(F_{i}\otimes_{R}M\to F_{i-1}\otimes_{R}M)}{{\rm im}(F_{i+1}\otimes_{R}M\to F_{i}\otimes_{R}M)}\,.

Similarly,
N\otimes_{R}\_
is a right exact functor from the category
of graded left
R
-modules to the category of bi-graded abelian groups, and its left derived functors are isomorphic to

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,\_\right)
(for
i\geq 0
).

It follows from this that, for a ring
S
, if
N
is also a left
S
-module (resp.
M
is also a right
S
-module),
then
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)
is a left
S
-module (resp. a right
S
-module).
This property will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.17
.

Note that
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)
has a natural structure of bi-graded abelian group,

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)=\bigoplus_{k,l\geq 0}{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)_{k,l}
.
Let us set

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)_{n}:=\bigoplus_{k+l=n}{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)_{k,l}\,,

then we define

\deg\left({\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)\right):=\sup\{n\,|\,{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}\left(N,M\right)_{n}\not=0\}\,.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M
be a graded left
R
-module. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

i)

\deg(H_{0}(M))\leq a
.

ii)

M
is generated as
R
-module by elements of degree
\leq a
, i.e.
every

This is a direct consequence of the fact that

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
M
be a graded left
R
-module and let
N
be a graded right
R
-module. Then the following inequality holds true:

\deg(N\otimes_{R}M)\leq\min\{\deg(N)+\deg(H_{0}(M))\,,\deg(H_{0}(N))+\deg(M)\}\,.

Proof.

We prove that
\deg(N\otimes_{R}M)\leq\deg(N)+\deg(H_{0}(M))

\deg(N\otimes_{R}M)\leq\deg(H_{0}(N))+\deg(M)

Let

and suppose that

(otherwise the claim is obvious).
By Lemma

Therefore every element

has an expression of the form

where
r_{i}\in R

\deg(m_{i})\leq a
for every

. The claim follows from the fact that

\deg(nr_{i}\otimes m_{i})\leq\deg(N)+a
, for every

.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\bar{N}
be a graded right
\bar{R}
-module. Then
\bar{N}
has a resolution,

\ldots\to\bar{P}_{1}\to\bar{P}_{0}\to\bar{N}\to 0\,,

\bar{P}_{i+1}\to\bar{P}_{i}
(for every

) and
\bar{P}_{0}\to\bar{N}

are of degree

-module generated by elements of degrees

\deg(\bar{N})+i\deg(\bar{R})
, for all

Proof.

We construct such a resolution proceeding inductively on

For

, let
\bar{P}_{0}
be the free right
\bar{R}
-module on the elements of
\bar{N}

and let
\bar{P}_{0}\to\bar{N}
be the morphism induced by the identity on these generators.
For

\bar{P}_{i}
be the free right

-module on the elements of

\ker(\bar{P}_{i-1}\to\bar{P}_{i-2})
(where we set
\bar{P}_{-1}:=\bar{N}
)
and let
\bar{P}_{i}\to\bar{P}_{i-1}

By construction, the morphisms

Lemma 3.11

In the following lemmas, for a graded left
R
-module
M
, we use the following notation:

(6)

\delta(M):=\max\left\{\deg\left(\tor^{R}_{0}(\bar{R},M)\right)\,,\,\deg\left(\tor^{R}_{1}(\bar{R},M)\right)\right\}\,.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
M
be a graded left
R
-module. Then

A(M)\leq\delta(M)+A(R)\,.

Proof.

Let us consider the following exact sequence of right
R
-modules,

R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle U}}{{\to}}R\to\bar{R}\to 0\,.

Applying the tensor product
\otimes_{R}M
to this sequence yields the isomorphism

\tor_{0}^{R}(\bar{R},M)=\bar{R}\otimes_{R}M=M/U(M)\,.

Therefore
\deg\left(M/U(M)\right)=\deg(\tor_{0}^{R}(\bar{R},M))\leq\delta(M)+A(R)
.

To prove that
\deg\left(M[U]\right)\leq\delta(M)+A(R)
,
let us first identify
M
with
R\otimes_{R}M
and let us factor the multiplication map
U\colon M\to M

as follows:

(7)

\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}R\otimes_{R}M&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}&U(R)\otimes_{R}M&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\to}}&R\otimes_{R}M\\
s\otimes m&\mapsto&U(s)\otimes m&\mapsto&U(s)\otimes m\,,\end{array}

where
U(R)
is considered as a two-sided ideal of
R
.
Note that
\deg(\beta)=0
and that
\deg(\alpha)=\deg(U)=1
.
Next, let us consider the following short-exact sequences of left
R
-modules:

0\to U(R)\to R\to\bar{R}\to 0,\qquad 0\to R[U]\to R\to U(R)\to 0\,\,.

Applying the tensor product
\otimes_{R}M
to these sequences we obtain two long exact sequences
of the corresponding
\tor_{*}^{R}(\_,M)
groups, part of them are as follows:

(8)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}0&\to&\tor_{1}^{R}(\bar{R},M)&\to&U(R)\otimes_{R}M&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\to}}&R\otimes_{R}M&\to&\bar{R}\otimes_{R}M&\to&0\end{array}

(9)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}R[U]\otimes_{R}M&\to&R\otimes_{R}M&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}&U(R)\otimes_{R}M&\to&0\,.\end{array}

By (
9
)
R[U]\otimes_{R}M
surjects onto
\ker(\alpha)
, which is isomorphic to a submodule
of
M[U]
. Since
M[U]
is the kernel of
U
, its image under
\alpha
is contained in
\ker(\beta)
,
which by (
8
) is identified with
\tor_{1}^{R}(\bar{R},M)
.
Thus we get a sequence that is exact in the middle:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}R[U]\otimes_{R}M&\to&M[U]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}&\tor_{1}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\,.\end{array}

By Lemma
3.9
the degree bound on the left hand side is

\deg\left(R[U]\otimes_{R}M\right)\leq\deg(R[U])+\deg(H_{0}(M))=\deg(R[U])+\deg(M/R_{>0}M)\,.

On the other hand, for an homogeneous element
x\in M[U]
, if
\alpha(x)=0
, then

\deg(x)\leq\deg\left(R[U]\otimes_{R}M\right)
, while if
\alpha(x)\not=0
, then

\deg(x)\leq\deg\left(\tor_{1}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\right)-\deg(U)
.
Hence

\deg(M[U])\quad\leq\quad\max\{\deg(R[U])+\deg(M/R_{>0}M),\,\deg\left(\tor_{1}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\right)-\deg(U)\}\,.

Now note that
M/U(M)
surjects onto
M/R_{>0}M
, and hence

\deg(M/R_{>0}M)\leq\deg(M/U(M))=\deg\left(\tor_{0}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\right)\,.

From this we conclude that

\deg(M[U])\leq\delta(M)+A(R)\,.

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
M
be a graded left
R
-module. Then the following inequalities hold true

\deg\left(\tor_{i}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\right)\leq\begin{cases}\delta(M)&\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=0,1\,;\\
\delta(M)+i{\tilde{A}(R)}&\quad\mbox{for}\quad i>1\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first claim is true by (
6
) (the definition of
\delta(M)
),
the second one will be proved by induction.
To this aim, we consider a resolution
P_{\bullet}
of
\bar{R}
by graded right
R
-modules
(which is not a free or projective resolution), with

P_{0}=R,\quad P_{1}=R[\deg U],\quad P_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle U}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{0}\longrightarrow\bar{R}\longrightarrow 0

(note the degree shift for
P_{1}
which is needed to make the multiplication map
by
U
of degree
0
).
The kernel of the map
P_{1}\to P_{0}
is then
R[U][\deg U]
, which is indeed a right
\bar{R}
-module
of degree
\deg U+\deg R[U]
, therefore by Lemma
3.10
it has a resolution,

\ldots{\longrightarrow}P_{3}\longrightarrow P_{2}\longrightarrow R[U][\deg U]\,,

by free right
\bar{R}
-modules
P_{i}
, which are
generated in degrees up to

(10)

\deg U+\deg R[U]+(i-2)\deg\bar{R}\,.

Note that any such
P_{i}
, for
i\geq 2
, is a direct sum of copies of
\bar{R}
shifted by at most

\deg U+\deg R[U]+(i-2)\deg\bar{R}
, hence its degree is at most

\deg U+\deg R[U]+(i-1)\deg\bar{R}\,.

Moreover
P_{i}
is a right graded
R
-module via the quotient homomorphism
R\to\bar{R}
, so

P_{\bullet}\to\bar{R}
is a resolution by
graded right
R
-modules generated in
degree bounded by the formulas above.

Let now
F_{\bullet}\to M
be a free resolution by left
R
-modules.
We get a double complex
P_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}F_{\bullet}
, from which
two spectral sequences can be obtained. One degenerates at the second page:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}E^{1}_{ij}&=&H_{i}(P_{\bullet}\otimes F_{j})=H_{i}(P_{\bullet})\otimes F_{j}&\cong&\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\bar{R}\otimes F_{j},\,i=0\\
0,\,i>0\end{array}\right.\\
E^{2}_{ij}&=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}H_{i}(\bar{R}\otimes F_{j}),\,i=0\\
0,\,i>0\end{array}\right.&=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\tor_{i}^{R}(\bar{R},M),\,i=0\\
0,\,i>0\end{array}\right.\end{array}

and hence abuts to
\tor_{i+j}^{R}(\bar{R},M)
. For the other one we have, accordingly:

E^{1}_{ab}\quad=\quad H_{a}(P_{b}\otimes F_{\bullet})\quad=\quad\tor_{a}^{R}(P_{b},M)\quad\implies\quad\tor_{a+b}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\,.

Thus for some filtration on
\tor_{i}^{R}(\bar{R},M)
, the associated graded

\bigoplus_{a+b=i}\tor^{R}_{a}(P_{b},M)\,.

Since
P_{0}=P_{1}
are free
R
-modules,
\tor^{R}_{a}(P_{b},M)=0
for

and

.
When

,

\displaystyle\deg\tor^{R}_{a}(P_{b},M)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\deg U+\deg R[U]+(b-2)\deg\bar{R}+\deg\tor^{R}_{a}(\bar{R},M)\,.

The last contribution,
\deg\tor^{R}_{a}(\bar{R},M)
, is due to
P_{b}
being
a sum of copies of
\bar{R}
up to shifts,
while the other contributions are due to the bound (

) on that shift.
It follows from this that a bound on
\deg\left(\tor_{i}^{R}(\bar{R},M)\right)
is given by the maximum over

of the expression on the right hand side:

\max\{\deg U+\deg R[U]+(i-a-2)\deg\bar{R}+\deg\tor^{R}_{a}(\bar{R},M)\,|\,a=0,\ldots,i-2\}\,.

By the induction hypothesis,

\deg\tor^{R}_{a}(\bar{R},M)\leq\delta(M)+(i-2)\max\{\deg(U),\deg(R[U]),\deg(\bar{R})\}\,,\quad{\rm for}\quad 0\leq a\leq i-2\,.

Combining this with the following inequalities

\deg U,\deg R[U],\deg\bar{R}\leq\max\{\deg(U),\deg(R[U]),\deg(\bar{R})\}

∎

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\bar{M}
be a graded left
\bar{R}
-module, then

\deg\bar{M}\quad\leq\quad\deg(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M})+\deg\bar{R}

Proof.

Under the identification
\mathbb{Z}=\bar{R}/{\bar{R}}_{>0}
, we have the following exact sequence:

{\bar{R}}_{>0}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M}\to\bar{R}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M}\to\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M}\to 0\,.

For an element
x\in\bar{M}
of
\deg(x)>\deg(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M})+\deg\bar{R}
,
we have that
1\otimes x=0
in
\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M}
, therefore
x=a^{\prime}x^{\prime}
, for some

a^{\prime}\in{\bar{R}}_{>0}
and
x^{\prime}\in\bar{M}
of
\deg(x^{\prime})<\deg(x)
. Note that
either
\deg(a^{\prime})>\deg(\bar{R})
, or
\deg(x^{\prime})>\deg(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M})
.
In the first case
x=0
and in the second one (by the previous argument)
x^{\prime}=a^{\prime\prime}x^{\prime\prime}
, hence

x=a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}x^{\prime\prime}
, with
\deg(x^{\prime\prime})<\deg(x^{\prime})
. We have again two cases:

\deg(a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime})>\deg(\bar{R})
, or
\deg(x^{\prime\prime})>\deg(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\bar{M})
.
In the first case
x=0
, in the second one we repeat the previous argument.
The process terminates since at each step we get an element of
\bar{M}
of degree less than the previous one,
and finally we have
x=\tilde{a}\tilde{x}
, with
\tilde{a}\in\bar{R}
of degree
>\deg(\bar{R})
, therefore
x=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.15

Let

\delta(M)\leq\max\left\{\deg H_{0}(M)\,,\deg H_{1}(M)\right\}+4A(R)\,.

\deg\tor^{R}_{1}(\bar{R},M)\leq\deg(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\bar{R}}\tor^{R}_{1}(\bar{R},M))+\deg\bar{R}\,.

\displaystyle G\colon\left(\mbox{Graded left $R$-modules}\right)

\displaystyle\to

Proposition 3.17

Clearly, if

\displaystyle\max\left\{\deg\left(\tor_{q}^{R}\left(\bar{R},M\right)\right)+p\deg(\bar{R})\,|\,p+q=i\right\}\,.

\displaystyle\delta(M)+i\tilde{A}(R)\,,

The claim, in the case where

In the general case,

and assume that the statement holds true for every graded right
R
-module of degree

Lemma 3.18
.

For every

\displaystyle\big(S_{(g,h)}d+dS_{(g,h)}\big)\bigg((a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{p+1},b_{p+1})\llbracket g_{1},h_{1},\dots,g_{q},h_{q}\rrbracket\bigg)

\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{p+1},b_{p+1})\llbracket g_{1},h_{1},\dots,g_{q},h_{q}\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket g,h\rrbracket

M(q^{\prime})_{n}=E^{\infty}_{0,q^{\prime}}(n)=E^{2}_{0,q^{\prime}}(n)\,,\quad\forall\,n>Aq^{\prime}-A+3B+C\,,

where in the last equality we have used (
19
).

From this we conclude that
d_{1}
in (
18
) induces an isomorphism

M(q^{\prime})_{n}\leftarrow{\rm coker}(d_{2})=E^{2}_{0,q^{\prime}}(n)\,,\quad\forall\,n>Aq^{\prime}-A+3B+C\,.

Equivalently, (
18
) is exact to the left and in the middle, in the same range, i.e.

h_{0}(M(q^{\prime})),h_{1}(M(q^{\prime}))\leq Aq^{\prime}-A+3B+C\,.

Theorem
3.21
, 1., now implies that

h_{p}(M(q^{\prime}))\leq Aq^{\prime}-A+3B+C+(5+p)\tilde{A}(R)+\deg(U)=Aq^{\prime}+Bp+C\,.

Hence (
17
) holds true for
q^{\prime}
.

Collective order boundedness of sets of operators between ordered vector spaces


Abstract
It is proved that: each collectively order continuous set of operators from an Archimedean ordered vector space with a generating cone to an ordered vector space is collectively order bounded; and each collectively order to norm bounded set of operators from an ordered Banach space with a closed generating cone to a normed space is norm bounded. Several applications to commutative operator semigroups on ordered vector spaces are given.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be an Archimedean ordered vector space with a generating cone.
Then
\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X,Y)\subseteq\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
for every ordered vector space
Y
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X,Y)
. Since
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
, it is enough to prove
order boundedness of
{\cal T}[0,b]
for every
b\in X_{+}
.
Take
[0,b]\subseteq X
. Following
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
,
consider the lexicographically directed set
{\cal I}=\mathbb{N}\times[0,b]
:

(m,z)\geq(n,z^{\prime})
if
m>n
or
m=n
and
z\geq z^{\prime}
, and let
x_{(k,z)}=\frac{1}{k}z
.
Since
X
is Archimedean,
\frac{1}{n}b\downarrow 0
.
It follows from
0\leq x_{(m,z)}=\frac{1}{m}z\leq\frac{1}{n}b
for
(m,z)\geq(n,b)

that
x_{(m,z)}\convo 0
.

Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X,Y)
,
there exists
g_{\beta}\downarrow 0
in
Y
such that,
for every
\beta
there exists
(m_{\beta},y_{\beta})
satisfying

\pm Tx_{(m,y)}\leq g_{\beta}
for all
T\in{\cal T}
and
(m,y)\geq(m_{\beta},y_{\beta})
.
Pick any
g_{\beta_{0}}
. Since
(m_{\beta_{0}}+1,y)\geq(m_{\beta_{0}},y_{\beta_{0}})
,

\pm T\Big(\frac{y}{m_{\beta_{0}}+1}\Big)=\pm Tx_{(m_{\beta_{0}}+1,y)}\leq g_{\beta_{0}}\ \ \ (y\in[0,b],T\in{\cal T}).

Therefore,
{\cal T}[0,b]\subseteq[-(m_{\beta_{0}}+1)g_{\beta_{0}},(m_{\beta_{0}}+1)g_{\beta_{0}}]
.
∎

In particular, this extends the Abramovich–Sirotkin theorem as follows.

Corollary 2.2
.

Each order continuous operator from an Archimedean ordered vector space with generating cone to any ordered vector space is order bounded.

Note that the Archimedean assumption, which has being missed in
[
1

,
is essential in Theorem
2.1
and in Corollary
2.2
even in the vector lattice setting.

Example 2.3
.

Let
X
be the lexicographically ordered real plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

Y
the coordinate-wise ordered
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Both
X
and
Y
are vector lattices.
The identity operator
T:X\to Y
is not order bounded since
the order interval
[(0,0),(2,0)]
of
X
contains line
x=1
that is not order
bounded in
Y
. However,
T
is order continuous. To see this, it is enough
to show that
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})\downarrow 0
in
X
implies

T((x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}))=(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})\convo 0
in
Y
.
Suppose
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})\downarrow 0
in
X
and let
\varepsilon>0
.
There exists
\alpha_{0}
with
x_{\alpha}=0
for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
.
Now, take
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{0}

such that
0\leq\max\{x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\}=y_{\alpha}\leq\varepsilon

for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}
. It follows that
T(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})\convo 0
in
Y
.

An inspection of the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
shows that Abramovich and Sirotkin proved
already that each
ru
-continuous operator between vector lattices
E
and
F
is order bounded. Conversely,
each order bounded operator
T:E\to F
is
ru
-continuous since, for every
u\in E_{+}
there exists
w\in F

such that
T[-u,u]\subseteq[-w,w]
, and hence
|x_{\alpha}|\leq\frac{1}{n}u
implies
|Tx_{\alpha}|\leq\frac{1}{n}w
.
This assertion was independently proved by Taylor and Troitsky in
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
.
Note that this fact can also be derived from the nonstandard criteria of order boundedness of operators

[
8

obtained by the first author. The following theorem generalizes this fact to the setting of ordered vector space.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be ordered vector spaces.

i)

If
X
has a generating cone then
\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X,Y)\subseteq\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
.

ii)

If
Y
has a generating cone then
\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)\subseteq\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

i)
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X,Y)
. Since
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
, to show
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)

it suffices to prove that
{\cal T}[0,b]
is order bounded in
Y
for each
b\in X_{+}
.
Take
b\in X_{+}
. Consider
{\cal I}=\mathbb{N}\times[0,b]

and the net
x_{(k,z)}=\frac{1}{k}z
like in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
If
(m,z)\geq(n,b)
in
{\cal I}
then either
(m,z)=(n,b)
or
m>n
,
and hence
0\leq x_{(m,z)}=\frac{1}{m}z\leq\frac{1}{n}b
. Thus,
x_{(m,z)}\convr 0
in
X
.

Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X,Y)
, there exist
u\in Y_{+}
and an increasing
sequence
(k_{n},z_{n})
in
{\cal I}
satisfying

\pm T\big(\frac{z}{k}\big)=\pm Tx_{(k,z)}\leq\frac{1}{n}u
for
all
T\in{\cal T}
and
(k,z)\geq(k_{n},z_{n})
. In particular,

\pm T\big(\frac{z}{k_{1}+1}\big)=\pm Tx_{(k_{1}+1,z)}\leq u
for all
T\in{\cal T}
and
z\in[0,b]
.
So,
{\cal T}[0,b]\subseteq[-(k_{1}+1)u,(k_{1}+1)u]
.
It follows
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
.

ii)
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
and
x_{\alpha}\convr 0
.
For some
u\in X_{+}
, there exists an increasing sequence

(\alpha_{n})
of indices such that
nx_{\alpha}\in[-u,u]
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
.
Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
and
Y=Y_{+}-Y_{+}
then
{\cal T}[-u,u]\subseteq[-w,w]

for some
w\in Y_{+}
, and hence
n{\cal T}x_{\alpha}\subseteq[-w,w]
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
.
It follows
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X,Y)
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Order bounded operators between ordered vector spaces with generating cones agree with
ru
-continuous operators.

Recall that order intervals in an ordered Banach space with a closed cone are norm bounded
if and only if the cone is normal (cf.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.40]
).
We include the following elementary collective extension of this fact.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be an ordered vector space and
Y
an ordered Banach space with closed generating normal cone.
Then
\text{\bf L}_{aob}(X,Y)\subseteq\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{aob}(X,Y)
and
[a,b]\subseteq X
.
Then
{\cal T}[a,b]\subseteq[-y,y]+B_{Y}

for some
y\in Y_{+}
. Since
Y_{+}
is normal,
[-y,y]+B_{Y}

is norm bounded by
[
3
, Theorem 2.40]
.
Therefore,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
.
∎

The normality of positive cone in the range is essential in Proposition
2.6
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
X=C^{1}[0,1]
be an ordered Banach space of continuously differentiable functions on
[0,1]
.
The identity operator
I_{X}
is norm and order bounded yet not order to norm bounded.
In particular,
I_{X}\in\text{\bf L}_{aob}(X,Y)\setminus\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
.

The following result generalizes
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
to ordered Banach spaces.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
X
be an ordered Banach space with a closed generating cone
and
Y
be a normed space. Then each
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
is norm bounded.

Proof.

Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
. Suppose, in contrary,

{\cal T}B_{X}
is not norm bounded.
The Krein – Smulian theorem (cf.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.37]
)
implies
\alpha B_{X}\subseteq B_{X}\cap X_{+}-B_{X}\cap X_{+}
for some
\alpha>0
,
and hence
{\cal T}(B_{X}\cap X_{+})
is not norm bounded. Pick sequences

(x_{n})
in
B_{X}\cap X_{+}
and
(T_{n})
in
{\cal T}
with
\|T_{n}x_{n}\|\geq n^{3}
for all
n
,
and set
x:=\|\cdot\|
-
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2}x_{n}\in X_{+}
.
Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)
then
{\cal T}[0,x]\subseteq MB_{Y}
for some
M<\infty
.
It follows from
n^{-2}x_{n}\in[0,x]
that
\|T_{n}(n^{-2}x_{n})\|\leq M
for every
n
.
This is absurd, because
\|T_{n}(n^{-2}x_{n})\|\geq n
for all
n
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Every order to norm bounded operator from an ordered Banach space
X
with a closed generating cone to a normed space
Y
is continuous.

It is worth noting that Corollary
2.9
generalizes the well known fact that every order bounded
operator between Banach lattices is bounded. It is also known that every positive operator
T:X\to Y
is continuous whenever
X
is
an ordered Banach space with a closed generating cone and
Y
is an ordered Banach space with a closed cone,
see Arendt
[
6
, Appendix]
, Aliprantis and Tourky
[
3
, Theorem 2.32]
, or Arendt and Nittka
[
7
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Note that a positive operator need not to be order to norm bounded. Indeed, the identity operator
I_{X}
on
an ordered Banach space
X
is order to norm bounded if and only if the positive cone
X_{+}
is normal.
Therefore, the next proposition does not follow from Corollary
2.9
.
Its proof below is a modification of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.32]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
X
and
Y
be ordered Banach spaces with closed generating cones. Then every order bounded operator
T:X\to Y
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
T:X\to Y
be order bounded. To establish that
T
is continuous, it suffices to show that
T
has a closed graph.
So, assume
x_{n}\convn 0
in
X
and
Tx_{n}\convn y
in
Y
. We must show that
y=0
.

According to
[
3
, Lemma 2.30]
, there exist a subsequence
(n_{k})
such that
x_{n_{k}}\convr 0
.
By Corollary
2.5
,
T
is
ru
-continuous, and hence
Tx_{n_{k}}\convr 0
.
Thus, there exist some
u\in Y
and a further subsequence
(n_{k_{m}})
such that
\pm Tx_{n_{k_{m}}}\leq\frac{1}{m}u
,
and hence
\frac{1}{m}u\pm Tx_{n_{k_{m}}}\in Y_{+}
for each
m
.
Since
Y_{+}
is closed and
\frac{1}{m}u\pm Tx_{n_{k_{m}}}\convn\pm y
, we conclude that
\pm y\in Y_{+}
.
That is, y = 0 and the proof is finished.
∎

We have the following collective extension of Proposition
2.10
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
X
and
Y
be ordered Banach spaces with closed generating cones, and
X^{\sim}\neq\{0\}
. The following assertions are equivalent.

i)

Every
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
is norm bounded.

ii)

The cone
Y_{+}
is normal.

Proof.

i)\Longrightarrow ii)
Assume that
Y_{+}
is not normal. Then, there exists
y_{0}\in X_{+}
such that
the interval
[0,y_{0}]
is not bounded. Pick
f_{0}\neq 0
in
X^{\sim}
and take
x_{0}\in X
with
f_{0}(x_{0})=1
.
Then,
\{f_{0}\otimes y\}_{y\in[0,y_{0}]}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
. However, the set
\{f_{0}\otimes y\}_{y\in[0,y_{0}]}
is not norm bounded
because of
\bigcup\limits_{y\in[0,y_{0}]}(f_{0}\otimes y)(x_{0})=[0,y_{0}]
.

ii)\Longrightarrow i)
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X,Y)
. Then,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{aob}(X,Y)
. So,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X,Y)

by Proposition
2.6
. It follows from Theorem
2.8
that
{\cal T}
is norm bounded.
∎

The following example shows that the condition that
X_{+}
is closed and generating is essential
in Theorem
2.8
, in Corollary
2.9
, and in Proposition

.

Example 2.12
.

Consider an infinite dimensional Banach space
X
.

a)

Let
X_{+}:=\{0\}
and take an arbitrary discontinuous operator
T
on
X
. Then
T:(X,X_{+})\to X
is order to norm
bounded and order bounded simply because order intervals in
X
are singletons. Clearly
X_{+}
is closed but not generating.

b)

Pick a normalized Hamel basis
\{\xi:\xi\in\Xi\}
of
X
, identify
X
with
c_{00}(\Xi)
, and let

X_{+}:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{\xi\in\Xi}\lambda_{\xi}\xi:\lambda_{\xi}\geq 0\Big\}
. Then
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
but

X_{+}
is not closed
(
e.g., by
[
7
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.
Pick any sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of distinct elements of
\Xi
and define

T:(X,X_{+})\to X
by
T\xi=0
for
\xi\in\Xi\setminus\{\xi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
T\xi_{n}=n\xi_{0}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Clearly,
T
is order to norm bounded and positive, yet not continuous.

Example 2.13
.

Let
X
be an ordered vector space with a generating cone which satisfies the dominated decomposition property

(
i.e.,
[0,v_{1}]+[0,v_{2}]=[0,v_{1}+v_{2}]
for every
v_{1},v_{2}
in
X_{+}
)
, and let

Y
be a Dedekind complete vector lattice.
It is well known that the ordered vector space
{\cal L}_{ob}(X,Y)
of order bounded operators of
{\cal L}(X,Y)
is a Dedekind
complete vector lattice (cf.,
[
19
, Theorem 83.4]
). Let
A
be an order bounded subset of
{\cal L}_{ob}(X,Y)
, say

A\subseteq[-T,T]
for some positive operator
T\in{\cal L}_{ob}(X,Y)
.
Let
x\in X_{+}
. Since
|Sz|\leq|S|z\leq Tz\leq Tx
for all
z\in[0,x]
and
S\in A
, then the set
A
is collectively order bounded.

From the other hand, not every collectively order bounded set of operators is order bounded. Indeed, consider
X=\ell^{\infty}
,
Y=\mathbb{R}
,
and
A=\{\delta^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\ell^{\infty}_{+}
, where
\delta^{n}(\mathbb{x})=x_{n}
. The set
A
is collectively order bounded, yet
there is no
f\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}
with
A\subseteq[0,f]
, since otherwise
f(1,1,\ldots,1,\ldots)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta^{k}(1,1,\ldots,1,\ldots)=n

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
that is absurd.

3
Some applications to operator semigroups

Under a
semigroup

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on a vector space
X
we understand
a family of operators on
X
satisfying
T_{s+t}=T_{s}T_{t}
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
T_{0}=I_{X}
, where
I_{X}
is the identity operator on
X
.
We shall also use the notation
(T_{s})_{s\in J}
for a one-parameter operator semigroup on
X
,
where
J=\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, or
\mathbb{Z}
. Such a semigroup is always commutative.
More generally, any commutative operator semigroup
\mathcal{G}
on
X
can be written
as
\mathcal{G}=(T_{S})_{S\in J}
, where
T_{S}=S
and

is directed by
a partial order
RS\succeq S

R,S\in\mathcal{G}
.

{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a collectively order to norm bounded
commutative operator semigroup on an ordered Banach space
X
with a closed generating cone containing a
(
weakly
)

compact operator
S_{0}
. Then the semigroup
(S)_{{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})\ni S\succeq S_{0}}

is collectively
(
weakly
)
compact. In particular,
\{S\}_{S\succeq S_{0}}

is collectively
(
weakly
)
compact.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.8
,
\|S\|\leq M<\infty
for
S\in\mathcal{G}
, and hence
for all
S\in{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})
. Thus,

\bigcup_{{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})\ni S\succeq S_{0}}SB_{X}=S_{0}\Big(\bigcup_{S\in{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})}SB_{X}\Big)\subseteq S_{0}(MB_{X})=MS_{0}B_{X}.

Since
S_{0}B_{X}
is relatively (weakly) compact,

(S)_{{\text{\rm co}}(\mathcal{G})\ni S\succeq S_{0}}
is collectively (weakly) compact.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a collectively almost order bounded commutative operator semigroup on an ordered Banach space
X

with a closed generating cone, and assume that every order interval in
X
is weakly compact.
Then the net
(S)_{S\in\overline{{\text{\rm co}}}(\mathcal{G})}
converges strongly to a projection.

Proof.

Since order intervals in
X
are weakly compact then every almost order bounded subset of
X
is
relatively weakly compact due to the Grothendieck result (cf.,
[
2
, Theorem 3.44]
).
In particular,
\mathcal{G}
be a collectively order to norm bounded.
By Theorem
2.8
,
\|S\|\leq M
for some
M<\infty
and all
S\in\mathcal{G}
.

Let
x\in X_{+}
. Then, the set
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{G}}S[-x,x]
is almost order bounded, and hence relatively weakly compact.
Since
(Sx)_{S\in\overline{{\text{\rm co}}}(\mathcal{G})}
lies in the weakly compact set
\overline{\text{\rm co}}(\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{G}}S[-x,x])
,
it has a weak cluster point, say
y
. Then
\lim\limits_{S\to\infty}\|Sx-y\|=0

by the Eberlein theorem (cf.,
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
). Since
x\in X_{+}
is arbitrary and
X_{+}
is generating,

(S)_{S\in\overline{{\text{\rm co}}}(\mathcal{G})}
converges strongly to a projection.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(T_{s})_{s\in J}
be either a
C_{0}
-semigroup or one-parameter discrete operator semigroup
on an order continuous Banach lattice

. If

(T_{s})_{s\in J}

The following example shows that the order continuity of the norm in

Example 3.4
.

(cf.,
[

Consider the operator

Uniformly continuous semigroups is a starting point in the theory of
C_{0}
-semigroups.
Here we discuss similar elementary notions concerning semigroups on ordered vector spaces.

Definition 3.6
.

A semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on an ordered vector space
X
is

a)

locally collectively order bounded
(
shortly,
lco
-bounded
)

whenever
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X)
for every
M>0
.

b)

lco
-continuous whenever
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X)
for every
M>0
.

c)

lcru
-continuous whenever
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X)
for every
M>0
.

A semigroup
(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
on a normed ordered vector space
X
is

d)

lcon
-bounded if
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X)
for every
M>0
.

In several important cases, the condition “for every
M>0
” can be replaced
by “for some
\varepsilon>0
” in each of the four items of Definition
3.6
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
{\cal T}=(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
be a semigroup on an ordered vector space
X
such that
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
.

i)

If
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then
{\cal T}
is
lco
-bounded.

ii)

If
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{ruc}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
then
{\cal T}
is
lcru
-continuous.

iii)

If
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then
{\cal T}
is
lco
-continuous.

iv)

If
X
is Banach space,
X_{+}
is closed, and
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then

{\cal T}
is
lcon
-bounded.

Proof.

i)
Let
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0

and fix
M\geq 0
. Let
n\varepsilon\geq M
, and take arbitrary
a\leq b
in
X
.
It follows from
X_{+}-X_{+}=X
that
[a,b]\subseteq[-a_{0},a_{0}]
for some
a_{0}\in X_{+}
.
Since
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X)
and
X_{+}
is generating,

\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{0},a_{0}]\subseteq[-a_{1},a_{1}]
for some
a_{1}\in X
.
Similarly, for some
a_{2}\in X
, we have

\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{1},a_{1}]\subseteq[-a_{2},a_{2}]
and

\bigcup\limits_{\varepsilon\leq s\leq 2\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{0},a_{0}]=T_{\varepsilon}\left(\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{0},a_{0}]\right)\subseteq T_{\varepsilon}[-a_{1},a_{1}]\subseteq[-a_{2},a_{2}].

It follows
\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq 2\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{0},a_{0}]\subseteq[-(a_{1}+a_{2}),a_{1}+a_{2}]
.
Repeating n-times the argument, we obtain
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\in X
with

\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq M}T_{s}[a,b]\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{0\leq s\leq n\varepsilon}{T_{s}}[-a_{0},a_{0}]\subseteq\left[-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right].

[formula]

Since
[a,b]
is arbitrary, (1) implies
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{ob}(X)
, and since
M\geq 0
is arbitrary,

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is locally collectively order bounded.

ii)
By Theorem
2.4
, it follows from
i)
.

iii)
Suppose
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X)
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
and fix
M\geq 0
. Let
n\varepsilon\geq M
and
x_{\alpha}\convo 0
in
X
.
Pick a net
g^{(1)}_{{\beta_{(1)}}}\downarrow 0
in
X
such that,
for each
\beta_{(1)}
there is an
\alpha_{\beta_{(1)}}
with

\pm T_{s}x_{\alpha}\leq g^{(1)}_{{\beta_{(1)}}}
for
0\leq s\leq\varepsilon
and

\alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta_{(1)}}
. Since
T_{\varepsilon}
is order continuous,

T_{\varepsilon}x_{\alpha}\convo 0
in
X
. It follows from

\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X)
that
there exists a net
g^{(2)}_{{\beta_{(2)}}}\downarrow 0
in
X
such that,
for each
\beta_{(2)}
there is
\alpha_{\beta_{(2)}}
with

\pm T_{s}T_{\varepsilon}x_{\alpha}\leq g^{(2)}_{{\beta_{(2)}}}
for
0\leq s\leq\varepsilon
and

\alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta_{(2)}}
. Repeating n-times, we obtain nets

g^{(1)}_{{\beta_{(1)}}}\downarrow 0,\ g^{(2)}_{{\beta_{(2)}}}\downarrow 0,\ ...\ g^{(n)}_{{\beta_{(n)}}}\downarrow 0

so that, for each
\beta_{(i)}
,

\alpha_{\beta_{(i)}}
satisfying

\pm T_{s}T^{i-1}_{\varepsilon}x_{\alpha}\leq g^{(i)}_{{\beta_{(i)}}}\ \ \ \ (0\leq s\leq\varepsilon\ \ \text{\rm and}\ \ \alpha\geq\alpha_{\beta_{(i)}}).

Consider the net

Take an

Then, for each

\pm T_{s}T^{i-1}_{\varepsilon}x_{\alpha}\leq g^{(i)}_{{\beta_{(i)}}}\leq g_{(\beta_{(1)},\beta_{(2)},...,\beta_{(n)})}\ \ \ \ (0\leq s\leq\varepsilon\ \ \text{\rm and}\ \ i=1,2,...,n).

\alpha\geq\alpha_{(\beta_{(1)},\beta_{(2)},...,\beta_{(n)})}
,

\pm T_{s}x_{\alpha}\leq g_{(\beta_{(1)},\beta_{(2)},...,\beta_{(n)})}\ \ \ \ (0\leq s\leq n\varepsilon).

[formula]

Since

is arbitrary, (2) implies

, and hence

\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq M}\in\text{\bf L}_{oc}(X)

iv)
Suppose
\big\{T_{s}\big\}_{0\leq s\leq\varepsilon}\in\text{\bf L}_{onb}(X)

By Theorem

(T_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is locally collectively order to norm bounded.

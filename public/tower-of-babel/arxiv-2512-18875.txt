Genus 0 Gromov-Witten theory of even dimensional complete intersections of two quadrics: the final step
Danil Gubarevich

Abstract
. Even dimensional complete intersections X X of two quadrics in ℂ ​ ℙ m + 2 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{m+2} are exceptional from the point of view of the Gromov-Witten theory: they are (together with qubic surfaces) the only complete intersections whose Gromov-Witten theory is not invariant under the full orthogonal or symplectic group acting on the primitive cohomology. The genus 0 Gromov-Witten theory of X X was studied by Xiaowen Hu. He used geometric arguments and the WDVV equation to compute all genus 0 correlators except one, which cannot be determined by his methods. In this paper we compute the remaining Gromov-Witten invariant of X X using Jun Li’s degeneration formula.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. The relevant moduli space in Gromov-Witten theory is the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
of stable
n
-pointed genus
g
maps. On points, it parametrizes the data

[(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\xrightarrow{f}X],

consisting of a morphism
f
, with finitely many automorphims, from a curve of arithmetic genus
g
, allowed to have at most nodal singularities, and with
n
distinct smooth marked points
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
such that
f_{*}[C]=\beta
. It is a proper Deligne-Mumford stack equipped with a canonical perfect obstruction theory, giving rise to its virtual fundamental class
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}
of expected dimension

(3-\dim X)(1-g)+\langle\beta,c_{1}(T_{X})\rangle+n.

This moduli space is equipped with
n
evaluation morphisms
\mathrm{ev}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{ev}_{n}
where

\mathrm{ev}_{i}([(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\xrightarrow{f}X])=f(p_{i}).

Denote by
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}
the first Chern classes of cotangent line bundles over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
.

Given classes
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\in H^{\star}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and non-negative integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
, the Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
are defined as the following intersection numbers

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{d_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\right\rangle_{g,n,\beta}^{X}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{\star}(\gamma_{i})\psi_{i}^{d_{i}}\cap\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\right]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in\mathbb{Q},

also called correlators. Geometrically, when the classes
\gamma_{i}
are represented by algebraic cycles
and all
d_{i}=0
, this invariant can be interpreted as the virtual count of genus
g
curves
in
X
meeting the given cycles.

In a recent paper
[
ABPZ
]
, Argüz, Bousseau, Pandharipande, and Zvonkine
developed an algorithm for computing Gromov–Witten invariants of smooth complete intersections
in projective space. The essential step was the observation that the deformation invariance of Gromov-Witten invariants implies their invariance under the monodromy group action on the primitive cohomology of
X
. That allowed the authors to treat the Gromov-Witten invariants with primitive insertions as multi-linear forms invariant under the orthogonal or symplectic group, provided that the monodromy group is maximal
[
ABPZ
, Theorem 4.27]
. The case when
X
is a smooth complete intersection of two quadrics is special, since in this case the monodromy group is finite and the proposed algorithm has to be modified.

The genus 0 Gromov-Witten theory of an even dimensional smooth complete intersection
X
of two quadrics is studied in
[
Hu21
]
. The author determines the values of all 4-point correlators and shows
[
Hu21
, Theorem 4.6]
that together with the WDVV equation they determine all the other correlators except one:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\tau_{0}(e_{1})\dots\tau_{0}(e_{m+3})\right\rangle_{0,m+3,\frac{m}{2}}^{X},

where
e_{1},\dots,e_{m+3}
is a basis of the primitive cohomology of
X
.

In the present paper we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth complete intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{m+2}
of even dimension
m\geq 4
. There exists an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\dots,e_{m+3})
of the primitive cohomology
H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of
X
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\tau_{0}(e_{1})\dots\tau_{0}(e_{m+3})\right\rangle_{0,m+3,\frac{m}{2}}^{X}=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}
be homogeneous polynomials in
m+k+1

\{f_{1}=\dots=f_{k-1}=0\}
and
\{f_{1}=\dots=f_{k}=0\}
are smooth complete intersections in
\mathbb{P}^{m+k}
. Consider the family

\displaystyle\widehat{X}=\{f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k-1}=tf_{k}+g_{1}g_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+k}\times\mathbb{A}^{1},

where
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are generically chosen polynomials of degrees

such that
d_{k}^{1}+d_{k}^{2}=\deg f_{k}

Z
of the restriction of
\widehat{X}
to a sufficiently small neighborhood
U
of

in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is given by

\displaystyle Z=\{f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=t=g_{1}=g_{2}=0\}.

Proof.

Denote by

the homogeneous coordinates in
\mathbb{P}^{m+k}
and let

. The matrix of partial derivatives of

with respect ot

reads

\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial t_{0}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial t_{m+k}}&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\dfrac{\partial f_{k-1}}{\partial t_{0}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{k-1}}{\partial t_{m+k}}&0\\
\quad\dfrac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial t_{0}}\quad&\quad\dots\quad&\quad\dfrac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial t_{m+k}}\quad&\quad f_{k}\quad\end{bmatrix},

where

\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial t_{i}}=t\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial t_{i}}+g_{1}\frac{\partial g_{2}}{\partial t_{i}}+g_{2}\frac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial t_{i}}.

A point

is smooth iff the evaluation of this matrix at
z
has maximal rank (that is, rank
k
). First note that if
f_{k}=t=g_{1}=g_{2}=0
at
z
, the last row of the matrix vanishes, so that the rank of the matrix is at most
k-1
and hence
z
belongs to the singular locus as claimed in the statement of the lemma. Now we must prove that all the other points of
\widehat{X}
over an appropriately chosen open set
U\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}
are smooth.

First we specify the choice of the open set
U
and the meaning of genericity of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. The polynomials
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
must be chosen in such a way that
\{f_{0}=\dots=f_{k-1}=g_{1}=0\}
and
\{f_{0}=\dots=f_{k-1}=g_{2}=0\}
are smooth complete intersections. By Bertini’s theorem, this is a generic choice. Similarly, the open set
U
is the set of values
t
for which
\{f_{0}=\dots=f_{k-1}=F_{k}=0\}
is a smooth complete intersection. Let us prove that this open set is nonempty.

Consider the set
S
of polynomials
F
of degree
d_{k}=\deg f_{k}
in
t_{0},\dots,t_{m+k}
such that the variety
\{f_{1}=\dots=f_{k-1}=F=0\}
is a smooth complete intersection. By Bertini’s theorem, this set is open and dense in the space of all degree
d_{k}
polynomials. The family
tf_{k}+g_{1}g_{2}
extends to a map from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the projectivization of the space of all degree
d_{k}
polynomials. Moreover, the image of
t=\infty
lies in the projectivization of
S
, because by assumption
X
is a smooth complete intersection. If follows that all but a finite number of points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
have their images in the projectivization of
S
. The neighborhood
U
in the lemma is obtained by removing all points
t\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
whose image is not in
S
, except the point
t=0
. In the restriction of the family
\widehat{X}
to

, all fibers are smooth except the fiber
X_{0}
over
t=0
.

Statement 1: if
z
is a singular point of
\widehat{X}
, then
f_{k}(z)=0
. Proof: the block
\partial f_{i}/\partial t_{j}
has maximal rank

at every point, since by assumption

\widehat{X}

3.
The cohomology of

Here we recall some background on the cohomology of complete intersections in
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
, with particular emphasis on the cohomology of even-dimensional complete intersections of two quadrics.

3.1.
Cohomology of complex smooth projective varieties

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
of complex dimension
m
in an ambient projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Denote by
\omega\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
the restriction to
X
of the cohomology class Poincaré dual to a hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

The Lefschetz operator acts on the singular cohomology by cupping with
\omega

\displaystyle\omega:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{k+2}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

Lefschetz hyperplane theorem states that
the restriction to generic hyperplane section
X_{H}

\displaystyle H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{k}(X_{H},\mathbb{Q})

is an isomorphism for
k<m
and is injective for
k=m
. If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+1}
is a hypersurface of degree
d
, it can be viewed as a hyperplane inside a bigger projective space
\mathbb{P}^{\binom{m+1+d}{d}-1}
via degree
d
Veronese embedding
\mathbb{P}^{m+1}\xrightarrow{\nu_{d}}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{m+1+d}{d}-1}
. Now by Lefschetz hyperplane theorem
H^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{\binom{m+1+d}{d}-1},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is an isomorphism for
k<m
and is injective for
k=m
, and it factors through isomorphisms
H^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{\binom{m+1+d}{d}-1},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{m+1},\mathbb{Q})
for
k\leq m
.

As its consequence,
Hard Lefschetz theorem states that

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
k\leq m

\displaystyle\omega^{m-k}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2m-k}(X,\mathbb{Q})

is an isomorphism.

One of formal consequences of the Hard Lefschetz theorem is the Lefschetz decomposition.

Definition 3.2
.

For
k\leq m
the
primitive cohomology
of degree
k
is defined by

H^{k}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q}):=\ker(\omega^{m-k+1}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2m-k+2}(X,\mathbb{Q}))

For instance, for
k=0,1
all cohomology are primitive(and hence
H^{2m-1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{2m}(X,\mathbb{Q})
as well).
The Lefschets decomposition is the following theorem

Theorem 3.3
.

\displaystyle H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\simeq\bigoplus_{2r\leq k}\omega^{r}H^{k-2r}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})

Below we will focus on complete intersections in projective space.

3.2.
Cohomology of smooth complete intersections in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}

Let
X
be a complete intersection in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
of dimension
m
and degree
d
, a hypersurface. Denote by
\omega
the restriction to
X
of the cohomology class Poincaré dual to a hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

Remark 3.4
.

According to the general definition,
H^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
is also part of the primitive cohomology of
X
. However, by abuse of language, when talking about the primitive cohomology of complete intersections we will only refer to the degree
m
part.

Proposition 3.5
.

The following is true

•

If
i
is odd and
i\not=m
, we have
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})=0
.

•

If
i
is even and
i<m
, we have
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\left<\omega^{i/2}\right>
.

•

If
i
is even and

H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\left<\frac{\omega^{i/2}}{d}\right>

Proof.

By Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, for
k<m
the restriction maps

\displaystyle H^{k}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Z})

are isomorphisms and

\displaystyle H^{m}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z})

is injective.
Recall the cohomology of projecive space
H^{2k}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{Z})
are generated by
k
-th power of the hyperplane class, and are zero elsewhere. The second assertion follows. For the last one, use the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem for homology. It states that for
k<m
the push-forward maps

\displaystyle H_{k}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{k}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{Z})

are isomorphisms. That is
H_{2k}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
,
2k<m
, in particular are torsion free. By Poincaré duality
H_{2k}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{2m-2k}(X,\mathbb{Z})

The Poincaré pairing

Proposition 3.8

Proposition 3.9

The triviality follows from the following observations. First, the monodromy action
preserves the integer classes
\mathrm{im}(H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{C})
.
Choosing a basis in
H^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, we can identify
H^{m}(X,\mathbb{Z})
with a standard lattice
\Lambda
and hence the group
\Gamma
is a subgroup of a discrete group
GL(\Lambda)
.

Second, this action preserves the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, the intersection pairing, since it is the same at each fiber. Then
\Gamma
sits inside the orthogonal group
O(H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q}))
, which is open dense inside
O(H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{R}))
(in analytic topology).

Intersection pairing on
H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is positive definite [
[
DK73
]
, XIX,
Proposition 3.3], hence the group
O(H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{R}))
is compact.

Being discrete and compact, the monodromy group
\Gamma
of is finite.

Moreover, Clemens proved [
[
Cle77
]
, Theorem 7.36] that the elements
g
of
\Gamma
are of the form
g=e^{A_{g}}
, where
A_{g}
are nilpotent complex-valued matrices. On the other hand, from the finiteness of
\Gamma
, each
g
is of finite order
e^{n_{g}A_{g}}=1
,
n_{g}\in\mathbb{N}
which implies
e^{A_{g}}=1
. Indeed, since the sum of nilpotent commuting matrices is nilpotent,

\displaystyle e^{n_{g}A_{g}}=(1+\widetilde{A}_{g})^{n_{g}}=1

for some nilpotent
\widetilde{A}_{g}
. The last equality can only happen if
\widetilde{A}_{g}=0
. Then,

\displaystyle e^{A_{g}}=1+\widetilde{A}_{g}=1.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

All the classes in
H^{m}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\mathbb{Q})
are monodromy invariant.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

be an odd prime such that

|C(G\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})|=2|C(G)|

Proof.

Recalling that the number of cyclic subgroups of a finite group

|C(G)|=\sum_{x\in G}\frac{1}{\phi(|x|)}.

Using it for

\displaystyle|C(\widetilde{G})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,y)\in\widetilde{G}}\frac{1}{\phi(lcm(|x|,|y|))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=0}\frac{1}{\phi(|x|)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\neq 0\\
p{\nmid}|x|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(p|x|)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\neq 0\\
p{\mid}|x|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(|x|)}

\displaystyle=2|C(G)|+(p-2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G\\
p{\mid}|x|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(|x|)}.

When

|C(G\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})|=2|C(G)|

Using the similar ideas used in the proof of the main results, we have the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group of order
2n
and
|C(G)|=n+r
for a fixed positive integer
r
.

(1)

If
1\leq r\leq n
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=2r-1
, then
G
is a
\{2\}
-group or
\{2,3\}
-group.

(2)

If
1\leq r\leq n-3
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=2r+1
, then
G
is a
\{2\}
-group or
\{2,3\}
-group or
\{2,5\}
-group.

By Theorem
1.2
, it follows that to classify groups
G
by the value of
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)
is same as to classify groups
G
by some values of
|C(G)|
.
In the following result, we give a list of groups
G
having
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=|G|-r
for
r\in\{1,3,5,7\}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a group with
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=|G|-r
then

(1)

r=1
if and only if
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, for
n\geq 0
.

(2)

r=3
if and only if
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4},S_{3}
or
D_{8}
.

(3)

r=5
if and only if
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{6},\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4},D_{12},\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times D_{8},\mathbb{Z}_{5}
or
D_{10}
.

(4)

r=7
if and only if
G\cong Q_{8},\mathbb{Z}_{8},D_{16},\mathbb{Z}_{7}
or
D_{14}
.

Proof.

(1) By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
,
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
.

(2) By Theorem
1.2
, we have
|C(G)|=|G|-1
, so by
[
10
, Theorem 2]
possibilities of
G
are
\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4},S_{3},D_{8}
and all of them satisfy
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=|G|-3
.

(3) By Theorem
1.2
, we have
|C(G)|=|G|-2
or
|G|-3
. Now, the result follows by checking the groups given in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
and
[
3
, Theorem 3]
.

(4) By Theorem
1.2
, we have
|C(G)|=|G|-3
,
|G|-4
or
|G|-5
. Then, the result follows by checking the groups given in
[
3
, Theorem 3, Theorem 4, Theorem 5]
.

∎

Finally, we ask a natural open question related to the above study.

Open problem:
Classify the finite groups
G
with
\mathcal{O}_{2}(G)=|G|-r
, for
9\leq r\leq|G|-1
.

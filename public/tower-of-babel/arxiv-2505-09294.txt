On the Learning with Augmented Class via Forests
Fan Xu, Wuyang Chen, Wei Gao

Abstract
Decision trees and forests have achieved successes in various real applications, most working with all testing classes known in training data. In this work, we focus on learning with augmented class via forests, where an augmented class may appear in testing data yet not in training data. We incorporate information of augmented class into trees’ splitting, i.e., a new splitting criterion, called augmented Gini impurity , is introduced to exploit some unlabeled data from testing distribution. We then develop the approach named Learning with Augmented Class via Forests ( LACForest ), which constructs shallow forests based on the augmented Gini impurity and then splits forests with pseudo-labeled augmented instances for better performance. We also develop deep neural forests with a novel optimization objective based on our augmented Gini impurity, so as to utilize the representation power of neural networks for forests. Theoretically, we present the convergence analysis for augmented Gini impurity, and finally conduct experiments to verify the effectiveness of our approaches. The code is available at https://github.com/nju-xuf/LACForest/ .

Various approaches have been developed for learning with augmented class.

;

Decision trees and forests have achieved great successes in various real applications with strong capabilities in handling discrete features and exploring local regions

;
Qi 2012
;
Grinsztajn et al. 2022
;
Costa and Pedreira 2023

mostly working with the assumption that training and testing data come from the same distribution.
For tree models, previous studies only detected augmented class from labeled data of known classes

;

, and it still remains open to exploit unlabeled data from testing distribution to build trees for learning with augmented class.

This work tries to incorporate some useful information of augmented class into the construction of forests, and our main contributions could be summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a new splitting criterion, called

, which incorporates useful information of augmented class into trees’ splitting from unlabeled data. We then develop the approach named

\mathcal{X}

\kappa

For known classes and augmented class, let
\mathcal{D}_{\text{kc}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\text{ac}}
be the marginal distributions over space
\mathcal{X}\times\{1,\ldots,\kappa\}
and
\mathcal{X}\times\{\kappa+1\}
from distribution

as follows:

Assumption 1
.

We say that distribution
\mathcal{D}
and its marginal distributions
\mathcal{D}_{\text{kc}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\text{ac}}
satisfy class shift assumption if

\mathcal{D}=(1-\theta)\mathcal{D}_{\text{kc}}+\theta\mathcal{D}_{\text{ac}}\ \text{ for some constant }\ \theta\in(0,1)\ .

(1)

This assumption correlates data distributions for known classes and augmented class, which has been well-studied for learning with augmented class
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Shu et al. 2023
and open-set recognition
Scheirer et al. 2013
.

We focus on the semi-supervised setting
Da et al. 2014
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Shu et al. 2023
, and the goal is to learn a function
f\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
from labeled data
S_{l}
and unlabeled data
S_{u}
with

S_{l}=\{(\bm{x}^{l}_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(\bm{x}^{l}_{n_{l}},y_{n_{l}})\}\text{ and }S_{u}=\{\bm{x}^{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}^{u}_{n_{u}}\}\ ,

where each labeled example
(\bm{x}^{l}_{i},y_{i})
is drawn i.i.d. from
\mathcal{D}_{\text{kc}}
, and unlabeled instance
\bm{x}^{u}_{j}
is sampled i.i.d. from
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Let
\mathbb{I}[\cdot]
be the indicator function, which returns
1
if the input is true and
0

, we write
[k]=\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
, and denote by

the

, we denote by

3
Our LACForest approach

This section proposes the
LACForest
approach for learning with augmented class based on random forests, and the core idea is to introduce a new splitting criterion,
augmented Gini impurity
, to incorporate potential augmented instances into the process of tree splitting.

3.1
Augmented Gini impurity

For instance space
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, unlabeled data
S_{u}
and labeled data
S_{l}
, we introduce

S_{\mathcal{C},u}=S_{u}\cap\mathcal{C}\ \text{ and }\ S_{\mathcal{C},l}=\{(\bm{x},y)\colon(\bm{x},y)\in S_{l}\text{ and }\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

Denote by
n_{\mathcal{C},l}=|S_{\mathcal{C},l}|
and
n_{\mathcal{C},u}=|S_{\mathcal{C},u}|
. We define the
augmented Gini impurity
as a splitting criterion as follows:

Definition 1
.

For an instance space
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, we define the augmented Gini impurity
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u})
w.r.t.
S_{l}
and
S_{u}
as

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u})=1-\sum_{k\in[\kappa+1]}\vartheta^{2}_{\mathcal{C},k}(S_{l},S_{u})\ .

(2)

Here,
\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})
is defined as

\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})=\mathbb{I}[n_{\mathcal{C},u}>0]\Big(1-\frac{(1-\theta)n_{u}n_{\mathcal{C},l}}{n_{l}\max(1,n_{\mathcal{C},u})}\Big)_{+}\ ,

where
\theta
is given in Eqn. (
1
); and for
k\in[\kappa]
, we define

\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},k}(S_{l},S_{u})=(1-\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u}))\sum\limits_{(\bm{x},y)\in S_{l}}\frac{\mathbb{I}[\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C},y=k]}{\max(1,n_{\mathcal{C},l})}\ .

Figure 1:
An intuitive illustration for the difference between our criterion and original Gini impurity over a 2-dimensional synthetic dataset, due to the consideration of augmented instances.

This definition is essentially similar to
the original Gini impurity
Breiman 1984
except for an additional term
\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})
, which aims to incorporate augmented class into impurity measure. Figure
1
shows an example on a synthetic dataset, and our augmented Gini impurity could take potential augmented data into consideration compared with Gini impurity.
This is because
\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})
estimates the proportion of augmented instances in
\mathcal{C}
w.r.t. distribution
\mathcal{D}
based on Assumption
1
with following guarantee:

Lemma 1
.

For instance space
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the following holds with probability at least
1-\delta
over
S_{l}
and
S_{u}

\displaystyle\left|\Pr_{(\bm{x},y)\in\mathcal{D}}[y=\kappa+1|\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}]-\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)}}{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{l}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}+1}{\sqrt{n_{u}}}\right)\right)\ ,

if
n_{\mathcal{C},u}/n_{u}\geq\gamma
for some constant
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}
be the optimal squared loss over distribution
\mathcal{D}
under the condition
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}
as follows:

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}=\min_{\bm{w}\in\Delta_{\kappa+1}}E_{(\bm{x},y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\|\bm{w}-\tilde{y}\|_{2}^{2}|\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\right]\ ,

(3)

where
\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa+1}
is the one-hot encoding of
y\in[\kappa+1]
. Intuitively,
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}
shows the minimum expected squared loss by giving the same prediction for all instances in space
\mathcal{C}
.
Based on Lemma
1
and Assumption
1
, we have

Theorem 1
.

For instance space
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the following holds with probability at least
1-\delta
over
S_{l}
and
S_{u}

\left|\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}-\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u})\right|\leq O\left(\frac{\kappa\sqrt{\ln(\kappa/\delta)}}{\gamma/(1+\sqrt{\gamma})}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{l}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{u}}}\right)\right)

if
n_{\mathcal{C},u}/n_{u}\geq\gamma
and
n_{\mathcal{C},l}/n_{l}\geq\gamma
for constant
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

This theorem shows that our augmented Gini impurity can be viewed as a good estimation of
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}
, and we could get the convergence rate
O(1/\sqrt{\min\{n_{l},n_{u}\}})
. The detailed proof is given in Appendix
A.2
, which involves the equivalence between splitting criterions and loss functions, as well as the convergence of
\vartheta_{\mathcal{C},\kappa+1}(S_{l},S_{u})
in Lemma
1
.

3.2
Construction of LACForest

A tree model can be constructed by partitioning instance space into disjoint rectangular nodes recursively. Given node
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, splitting feature
j\in[d]
and splitting point
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote the left and right children, respectively, as

\mathcal{C}^{l}_{j,a}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\colon x_{j}\leq a\}\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{C}^{r}_{j,a}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{C}\colon x_{j}>a\}\ .

We solve the optimal spitting feature
j^{*}
and splitting point
a^{*}
from following optimization

(j^{*},a^{*})\in{\arg\max}_{j,a}\left\{R_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u},j,a)\right\}\ ,

(4)

where the reduction of augmented Gini impurity is given by

R_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u},j,a)=\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{l},S_{u})\\
-\frac{n_{\mathcal{C}^{l}_{j,a},u}}{n_{\mathcal{C},u}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}^{l}_{j,a}}(S_{l},S_{u})-\frac{n_{\mathcal{C}^{r}_{j,a},u}}{n_{\mathcal{C},u}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}^{r}_{j,a}}(S_{l},S_{u})\ .

From Theorem
1
, we can distinguish augmented class from known classes in node
\mathcal{C}
by solving Eqn. (
4
), as it is convergent to optimizing the squared loss as training data increases. From a theoretical view, we require sufficient training data in each node
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
n_{\mathcal{C},l}\geq\gamma n_{l}
and
n_{\mathcal{C},u}\geq\gamma n_{u}
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
in Theorem
1
. Hence, we should construct relatively shallow tree models for theoretical guarantees.

On the other hand, we should take deeper tree models for better performance, and it is well-known that better predictions could be obtained by deepening tree models sufficiently
Quinlan 1986
;
Hastie et al. 2009
. From such recognition, our algorithm includes two main steps as follows:

Step-I: Exploration of augmented instances

We preliminarily construct
m

for the exploration of augmented instances from Eqn. (
4
). For simplicity, we present the detailed construction of tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{1}
, and consider other trees similarly. We initialize
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{1}
with one root node of instance space
\mathcal{X}
, and recursively repeat a procedure for each leaf node as follows:

•

Select a
\tau

from

•

\displaystyle\min(n_{\mathcal{C}^{l}_{j,a},l},n_{\mathcal{C}^{r}_{j,a},l})

(5)

\displaystyle\geq

which ensures sufficient instances in each child node for theoretical considerations;

We stop the above procedure if there is no feasible solution for Eqn. (

4
Deep Neural LACForest

Based on our augmented Gini impurity, we further explore deep neural forests for learning with augmented class, which presents powerful representations with an end-to-end training manner for complex data such as images and texts. Another advantage for neural forests is to take soft splits rather than hard splits of decision trees, which could yield smoother decision boundaries and avoid overfitting
Kontschieder et al. 2015
. Previous approaches generally focus on all known classes
Kontschieder et al. 2015
;
Tanno et al. 2019
;
Ji et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2024
, while this work takes one step on learning with augmented class via deep neural forests.

4.2
Deep Neural LACForest

In the training phase, we begin with random initializations on encoder
h
and neural trees
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{m}
, and update the encoder and neural trees iteratively. In each iteration, we receive two mini-batches of labeled data
S^{\prime}_{l}
and unlabeled data
S^{\prime}_{u}
sampled randomly from
S_{l}
and
S_{u}
, respectively. From Definition
2
, we introduce

\mathcal{L}_{\text{ag}}(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{i})=\sum_{j\in[2t+2]\setminus[t]}\omega_{i,j}(S^{\prime}_{u})\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}_{i,j}}(S^{\prime}_{l},S^{\prime}_{u})\ ,

(10)

where
\{\mathcal{B}_{i,j}\}_{j\in[2t+2]\setminus[t]}
denote all leaf nodes of
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{i}
, and
\omega_{i,j}(S^{\prime}_{u})
is the proportion of unlabeled data in
\mathcal{B}_{i,j}
, i.e.,

\omega_{i,j}(S^{\prime}_{u})=\sum_{\bm{x}\in S^{\prime}_{u}}{\mu_{\mathcal{B}_{i,j}}(\bm{x})}/{|S^{\prime}_{u}|}\ .

The augmented Gini loss in Eqn. (
10
) is a weighted average augmented Gini impurity for all leaf nodes of
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{i}
.

For better representation, we introduce an auxiliary linear predictor
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\rightarrow\Delta_{\kappa}
as done in
Yan et al. 2021
, and take the cross entropy loss on known classes w.r.t.
S^{\prime}_{l}
as

\mathcal{L}_{\text{ce}}=-\frac{1}{|S^{\prime}_{l}|}\sum_{(\bm{x},y)\in S^{\prime}_{l}}\sum_{t\in[\kappa]}\tilde{y}_{t}\log\left(g_{t}\left(h\left(\bm{x}\right)\right)\right)\ .

We finally get the optimization objective as follows:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i\in[m]}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\text{ag}}(\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{i})}{m}+\lambda_{\text{ce}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ce}}\ ,

(11)

where
\lambda_{\text{ce}}>0
is a trade-off parameter. Intuitively, the cross entropy loss is beneficial to learning a basic representation of target data, and the augmented gini loss could improve the ability of neural trees to distinguish augmented class from known classes, as shown by Theorem
2
.

Related works

Zhou and Chen 2002

Zhou and Chen 2002
proposed the problem of
class-incremental learning
via a few labeled augmented instances.
Fink et al. 2006

Fink et al. 2006
learned multiple binary classifiers for this problem and
Topalis and
Polikar 2008

Topalis and
Polikar 2008
considered voting classifiers. Recent years have witnessed increasing attention on the design of practical algorithms for this problem
Li and Hoiem 2017
;
Rebuffi et al. 2017
;
Yan et al. 2021
;
Zou et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2024
. These methods can not be applied to our learning scenario directly, since we have no access to any labeled augmented instances.

Another relevant problem is
open-set recognition
in computer vision.
Scheirer et al. 2013

Scheirer et al. 2013
introduced open space risk to penalize predictions
outside the support of training data. Along this line, various approaches
have been developed based on open space risk
Scheirer et al. 2014
, extreme value theory
Bendale and Boult 2016
;
Rudd et al. 2017
, nearest neighbors
Mendes Júnior et
al. 2017
and generative neural networks
Ge et al. 2017
;
Neal et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2021
.
Most studies are based on strong geometric assumptions, while our work relaxes such assumptions.

Conclusion

This work studies learning with augmented class via forests, where an augmented class may appear in testing data yet not in training data. We introduce the augmented Gini impurity as a new splitting criterion by incorporating information of augmented class into trees’ splitting. We then develop the approach on
Learning with Augment Class via Forests
, which constructs shallow forests based on augmented Gini impurity and further splits forests with pseudo-labeled augmented instances for better performance. We also explore deep neural forests with a new optimization objective via our augmented Gini impurity. We validate the effectiveness of our methods both empirically and theoretically. An interesting future work is to exploit our methods under other learning settings such as streaming datasets and multiple augmented classes.

B.1
Evaluation of our LACForest approach

Datasets.
All of datasets could be downloaded from
https://archive.ics.uci.edu/datasets
and
https://www.openml.org
, and we normalize the features of raw data into the range of
[0,1]
.

Parameter settings.
For our LACForest,
We set the random trees number
m=100
,
\gamma=0.01
and
\tau=\lfloor\sqrt{d}\rfloor
on all datasets, where
d
denotes the data dimension.
Parameters for other methods are set according to their respective references.

Performance measure.
Following previous works
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Shu et al. 2023
, we use three measures to evaluate the performance of compared methods, including accuracy, macro-F1 and AUC on the detection of augmented classes. For simplicity, we denote by
S_{t}=\{(\bm{x}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n_{t}}
the testing dataset and
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
the learned model. We present the performance measures in details as follows:

•

Accuracy: the average predictive accuracy on testing data, i.e.,

\text{ACC}=\frac{1}{n_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}\mathbb{I}\left[f(\bm{x}_{i})=y_{i}\right]\ .

•

Macro-F1: the average F1 score on testing data, i.e.,

\text{Macro-F1}=\frac{1}{\kappa+1}\sum_{k\in[\kappa+1]}\frac{2\times P_{k}\times R_{k}}{P_{k}+R_{k}}\ ,

where
P_{k}
and
R_{k}
stand for the precision and recall of the
k
-th class, respectively.

•

AUC: the average AUC score on identifying augmented data, i.e.,

\text{AUC}=\frac{1}{|S^{+}_{t}|\times|S^{-}_{t}|}\sum_{\bm{x}^{+}\in S^{+}_{t}}\sum_{\bm{x}^{-}\in S^{-}_{t}}\mathbb{I}\left[f_{\kappa+1}(\bm{x}^{+})>f_{\kappa+1}(\bm{x}^{-})\right]\ ,

where
f_{\kappa+1}(\bm{x})
stands for the augmented class score w.r.t. model
f
, and
S^{+}_{t}
and
S^{-}_{t}
could be defined as follows:

S^{+}_{t}=\{\bm{x}\colon(\bm{x},y)\in S_{t}\wedge y=\kappa+1\}\quad\text{ and }\quad S^{-}_{t}=\{\bm{x}\colon(\bm{x},y)\in S_{t}\wedge y\in[\kappa]\}\ .

B.2
Evaluation of our deep neural LACForest

Datasets.
We follow the data setting of each dataset in previous studies
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Shu et al. 2023
. All the datasets contain ten classes, among which we randomly select six classes as known classes and four other classes as the augmented class. The detailed description of each dataset is presented as follows.

•

Mnist
contains 28
\times
28 monochrome images of digital numbers. The labeled data contains instances of six known classes with 4000 instances per class. On the other hand,
the unlabeled data contains unlabeled instances from 10 classes with 1000 instances in each class. Thus, the total number of training instances is 34000. The testing set consists
of ten classes with 100 instances in each class, and thus the total number is
1000. The dataset can be downloaded from
http://yann.lecun.com/exdb/mnist/
.

•

Fmnist
includes 28
\times
28 monochrome images of fashion apparel,
and it takes similar data setting to that of
mnist
. The dataset can be downloaded from
https://github.com/zalandoresearch/fashion-mnist
.

•

Kuzushiji
contains 28
\times
28 monochrome images of handwritten japanese characters, and it takes similar data setting to that of
mnist
. The dataset can be downloaded from
https://github.com/rois-codh/kmnist
.

•

Svhn
is short for Street View House Numbers, which includes 32
\times
32 colored image
samples of numbers. The training set consists of six
known classes with 4500 subsampled instances in each class and ten unlabeled classes with
3000 instances in each class, and the total number of instances is 57000. The
testing set contains instances of all ten classes with 100 instances in each class. SVHN
dataset can be downloaded from
http://ufldl.stanford.edu/housenumbers/
.

•

Cifar10
contains natural images of size 32
\times
32. The training set contains
six known classes with 5000 subsampled instances in each class and unlabeled instances
from all ten classes with 900 subsampled instances in each class; thus, the total number
of training instance is 39000. The testing set contains all ten classes with 100
instances per class. Cifar-10 dataset can be downloaded from
https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~kriz/cifar.html
.

Compared methods.

This section introduces the details of compared approaches, where Deep-GLAC
Shu et al. 2023
and Deep-EULAC
Zhang et al. 2020
could be seen as direct extensions of GLAC and EULAC in section
5.1
, respectively.

•

Softmax-T
Hendrycks and
Gimpel 2016
trains
\kappa
classifiers
f_{1}(\bm{x}),\cdots,f_{\kappa}(\bm{x})
to approximate the posterior probabilities
\Pr[y=k|\bm{x}]
for
k\in[\kappa+1]
. An instance
\bm{x}
is predicted to be the augmented class when the maximum outputs
\max_{k\in[\kappa]}f_{k}(\bm{x})
is less than a given threshold.

•

Openmax
Bendale and Boult 2016
can be seen as a calibrated version of Softmax-T, where replaces the Softmax layer with a new Openmax layer based on Weibull calibration.

•

G-Openmax
Ge et al. 2017
is an extension of Openmax approach with generative neural networks.

•

OSRCI
Neal et al. 2018
is short for Open-set Recognition using Counterfactual Images, which
utilizes a counterfactual image generation technique to train an additional classifier for the augmented class.

•

ARPL
Chen et al. 2021
is short for Adversarial Reciprocal Points Learning, which introduces a novel framework for open set recognition by leveraging reciprocal points to model latent open space.

Parameter settings.
For our approach, we construct 3 neural trees of depth
l=6
, and set the hyper-parameter
\lambda_{\text{ce}}=1
for all datasets. We set the number of epochs as 500 for all datasets, and take a relatively large batch size 512 for both labeled and unlabeled data according to Theorem
2
. The loss is optimized by SGD optimizer with weight decay
5\times 10^{-3}
. The initial learning rate is set to
10^{-2}
and is progressively reduced to
10^{-3}
through a cosine annealing scheduler.
For the estimation of proportion
\theta
, we randomly sample 1000 labeled examples and 1000 unlabeled instances for ten times, and take the average estimated proportion by previous method
Ramaswamy et al. 2016
.
For other compared methods, we select their parameters according to their corresponding references.

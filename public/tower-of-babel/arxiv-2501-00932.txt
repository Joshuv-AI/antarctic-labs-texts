An upper tail field of the KPZ fixed point
Zhipeng Liu, Ruixuan Zhang

Abstract
The KPZ fixed point is a (1+1)-dimensional space-time random field conjectured to be the universal limit for models within the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class. We consider the KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition, conditioning on a large value at a specific point. By zooming in the neighborhood of this high point appropriately, we obtain a limiting random field, which we call an upper tail field of the KPZ fixed point. Different from the KPZ fixed point, where the time parameter has to be nonnegative, the upper tail field is defined in the full 2 2 -dimensional space. Especially, if we zoom out the upper tail field appropriately, it behaves like a Brownian-type field in the negative time regime, and the KPZ fixed point in the positive time regime. One main ingredient of the proof is an upper tail estimate of the joint tail probability functions of the KPZ fixed point near the given point, which generalizes the well known one-point upper tail estimate of the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution.

Introduction

1.1
Background and motivation

The KPZ fixed point is a (1+1)-dimensional space-time random field which has been proven or conjectured to be the universal limiting space-time field of a large class of interface growth models in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class
[

,

,

,
DZ24
,

]
. It was first rigorously constructed in
[
MQR21
]
, and could be viewed as a marginal of the directed landscape
[
DOV22
]
which is a universal random metric for the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class. There have been many studies on the properties of the KPZ fixed point
[
CQR13
,
CHHM23
,
Dau24
,
QR22
,
BPS23
,
LW24
,
FL24
,
DDV24
,
DT24
]
. In this paper, we mainly focus on the limiting behaviors of the KPZ fixed point when the height function at a point becomes extremely large. More explicitly, if we denote
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
the KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition, what does
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
look like conditioning on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\to\infty
?

This question was partly answered in
[
LW24
,
NZ22
]
. It turns out that, before the high point
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)=L
, there is a strip of size
O(L^{-1/4})
along the line between
(0,0)
and
(0,1)
. Within this strip, the KPZ fixed point fluctuates of
O(L^{1/4})
and the limiting fluctuation is given by the minimum of two independent Brownian bridges. More explicitly, it is proved in
[
LW24
]

\left\{\frac{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}L^{1/4}},t\right)-t\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)}{\sqrt{2}L^{1/4}}\,\Bigg|\,\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)=L\right\}\to\min\left\{\mathbb{B}_{1}(t)+x,\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)-x\right\}

(1.1)

in the sense of convergence of finite dimensional distributions when
L
goes to infinity. Here
x\in\mathbb{R},t\in(0,1)
, and
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
are two independent Brownian bridges. The conditional distribution should be understood as a limit of the distribution conditioned on
\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\in(L-\epsilon,L+\epsilon)\}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. On the other hand, after the high point, the KPZ fixed point returns to an unconditioned KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition. This was proved for the convergence of the one-point distribution in
[
NZ22
]
as below

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,1+t)-\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\leq h\mid\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)=L\right)\to\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{h}{t^{1/3}}+\frac{x^{2}}{t^{4/3}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{\tilde{H}}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}(x,t)\leq h\right)

(1.2)

as
L
goes to infinity. Here
h,x\in\mathbb{R},t\in(0,\infty)
,
\mathrm{\tilde{H}}^{\mathrm{KPZ}}
denotes a new KPZ fixed point with the narrow wedge initial condition which is independent of
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}
, and
\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}
is the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution.

The two results above imply that
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
has two different limiting behaviors before and after the conditioned high point. Both the scaling exponents and the limiting fields are totally different in these two time regimes. Therefore, it is very interesting to understand how the transition occurs. The main goal of this paper is to investigate the limiting behaviors of
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
near the high point
(0,1)
, as
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)
goes to infinity. Our main result is a limit theorem for the conditional field
\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)\mid\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\geq L\}
near the point
(0,1)
as
L\to\infty
. We are also able to show that the limiting field, which we call the upper tail field of the KPZ fixed point, interpolates the Brownian-like field (
1.1
) and the KPZ fixed point, as we expect in the discussions above. It is a new field to the best of our knowledge, and is different from some other known transitional field from Gaussian Universality to KPZ Universality, such as those reviewed in
[
Cor16
]

There are also recent large deviation results in the upper tail regime which are relevant to this paper. For example,
[

]

GLLT23

[
LT23
]
proved the
n
-point fixed-time large deviation principle and characterized the space-time limit shape of the KPZ equation in the upper tail.

On the other hand, it is a natural question to ask the limiting behaviors of

1.2
Main results

The main result of this paper is about the limit of the rescaled KPZ fixed point near a conditioned high point. The limit
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)
, which we call the upper tail local limiting field or the upper tail field for short if there is no confusion, will be defined in Section
2.2
via its finite-dimensional distributions, and

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha,\tau):=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)-\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0),\quad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.3)

We also recall that
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
denotes the KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition. The main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\hat{\alpha},\hat{\tau}
and
\hat{\beta}
are constants.

(a)

Conditioned on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},1+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})\geq L+\hat{\beta}L^{-1/2},

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)\rightarrow\hat{\beta}+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha-\hat{\alpha},\tau-\hat{\tau}),\qquad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.4)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
L\to\infty
. Especially, conditioned on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\geq L
,

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau),\qquad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.5)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
L\to\infty
.

(b)

Conditioned on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},1+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})=L+\hat{\beta}L^{-1/2}
,

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)\rightarrow\hat{\beta}+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha-\hat{\alpha},\tau-\hat{\tau}),\qquad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.6)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
L\to\infty.
Here the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot\mid\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)=h\right)
should be understood as
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot\mid\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)\in(h,h+\epsilon)\right)
.
Especially, conditioned on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)=L
,

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha,\tau),\qquad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.7)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
L\to\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note the smaller scaling exponents in the equations (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) than those in the formulas (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Heuristically, we need to zoom in the small neighborhood of
(0,1)
to see how the two fields in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) transit to each other near the point
(0,1)
.

Remark 1.3
.

The two limiting fields
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
are slightly different when conditioning on
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},1+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})\geq L+\hat{\beta}L^{-1/2}
and
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},1+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})=L+\hat{\beta}L^{-1/2}
respectively. Intuitively, it means that the scaling is so small that the conditional field
\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)
is sensitive to a small change of
\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},1+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})-L\right)
.

On the other hand, one can heuristically derive that the two statements in the theorem are equivalent. For simplicity, we assume that
\hat{\alpha}=\hat{\tau}=0
. We show how one expects the second statement from the first and the other direction is similar. Assuming the first statement, for points
(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})\neq(0,0)
,
1\leq i\leq m
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha_{i}L^{-1},1+\tau_{i}L^{-3/2})-L-L^{-1/2}\hat{\beta}\geq L^{-1/2}\beta_{i}\right\}\,\Big|\,L^{1/2}(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)-L)\in(\hat{\beta},\hat{\beta}+\epsilon)\right)\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha_{i}L^{-1},1+\tau_{i}L^{-3/2})-L\geq L^{-1/2}(\beta_{i}+\hat{\beta})\right\}\bigcap\left\{L^{1/2}(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)-L)\in(\hat{\beta},\hat{\beta}+\epsilon)\right\}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(L^{1/2}(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)-L)\in(\hat{\beta},\hat{\beta}+\epsilon)\right)}\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})\geq\beta_{i}\right\}\,\big|\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)\in(0,0+\epsilon)\right)\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)\geq\beta_{i}\right\}\bigcap\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)\in(0,0+\epsilon)\right\}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)\in(0,0+\epsilon)\right)}=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})\geq\beta_{i}\right\}\right),\end{split}

(1.8)

where the last equation comes from a few properties of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
which will be proved later:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
is independent of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})-\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
, and all the random variables
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha_{i},\tau_{i})
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
have a sufficiently good joint tail probability function which is differentiable. This reasoning is still heuristic due to the change of the order of limits in the first step, which we don’t have a short argument to justify. Instead, we prove the two parts of the theorem separately using the steepest descent method.

Remark 1.4
.

Assume
(x,t)
is a given point in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Note that the KPZ fixed point
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}
has the following invariance property (see
[
DOV22

\begin{split}&\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x+\alpha L^{-1},t+\tau L^{-3/2})+\frac{1}{t+\tau L^{-3/2}}(x+\alpha L^{-1})^{2}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\,t^{1/3}\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha t^{-2/3}L^{-1},1+\tau t^{-1}L^{-3/2})+\frac{1}{t+\tau L^{-3/2}}(\alpha L^{-1})^{2},\end{split}

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x+\alpha L^{-1},t+\tau L^{-3/2})+t^{-1}x^{2}-tL\right)\rightarrow\hat{\beta}+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha-\hat{\alpha},\tau-\hat{\tau}),\quad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.10)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as

\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x+\hat{\alpha}L^{-1},t+\hat{\tau}L^{-3/2})+t^{-1}x^{2}=tL+\hat{\beta}L^{-1/2}

\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x+\alpha L^{-1},t+\tau L^{-3/2})+t^{-1}x^{2}-tL\right)\rightarrow\hat{\beta}+\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha-\hat{\alpha},\tau-\hat{\tau}),\quad(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.11)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
L\to\infty
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is provided in Section

The upper tail field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)
obtained in Theorem
1.1
has the following properties.

Proposition 1.5
.

The upper tail field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)
satisfies:

(a)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
is an exponential random variable of parameter
2
.

(b)

For all
x,\tau,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)\geq\beta\right)=e^{\frac{2}{3}\tau-2\beta}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(-\alpha,-\tau)\geq-\beta\right).

(1.12)

(c)

The field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha,\tau)=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha,\tau)-\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
is independent of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
.

(d)

At time
\tau=0
, the spatial process
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\alpha,\tau=0)
has the same distributions as
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(2\alpha)-2|\alpha|
, where
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}
denotes a two-sided Brownian motion with
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(0)=0
.

Remark 1.6
.

It is not surprising that
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
is an exponential random variable. In fact, the upper tail estimate of
\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}
(see (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) for example) implies

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\geq L+\beta L^{-1/2}\mid\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(0,1)\geq L\right)=\frac{1-\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}(L+\beta L^{-1/2})}{1-\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}(L)}\to e^{-2\beta}

(1.13)

for fixed
\beta\geq 0
when the large parameter
L\to\infty
.

Remark 1.7
.

The last property is due to Duncan Dauvergne. Through personal communication, Dauvergne told us the property from the perspective of the Airy line ensemble techniques. We verified the property using the formulas obtained in this paper. These properties in Proposition
1.5
also imply the following simple result: If
X
is an exponential random variable of parameter
2
, and
Z
is a standard Gaussian random variable independent of
X
, then
\mathbb{P}(X+\sqrt{2\alpha}Z-2\alpha\geq\beta)=e^{-2\beta}\mathbb{P}(X+\sqrt{2\alpha}Z-2\alpha\geq-\beta)
holds for any
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. It is an elementary exercise to verify this identity, and we skip the proof since it is not needed for our argument.

The proof of Proposition
1.5
is given in Section
6
.

If we zoom out the fields
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
or
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
, we are able to see the Brownian-like (for the negative times) and KPZ type (for the positive times) behaviors. In other words, they are random fields on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
that interpolate a Brownian-like field and the KPZ fixed point. More precisely, we have

Proposition 1.8

(Large scale limits of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
)
.

Both
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
have the following large scale limits.

(a)

In the negative time regime,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/2}\mathrm{x}}{\sqrt{2}},\lambda\mathrm{t}\right)-\lambda\mathrm{t}\right),\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/2}\mathrm{x}}{\sqrt{2}},\lambda\mathrm{t}\right)-\lambda\mathrm{t}\right)

(1.14)

both converge to

\min\left\{\mathbf{B}_{1}(-\mathrm{t})+\mathrm{x},\mathbf{B}_{2}(-\mathrm{t})-\mathrm{x}\right\},\quad(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0)

(1.15)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
\lambda\to\infty
. Here
\mathbf{B}_{1}
and
\mathbf{B}_{2}
are two independent standard Brownian motion.

(b)

In the positive time regime, both

\lambda^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x},\lambda\mathrm{t}),\lambda^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x},\lambda\mathrm{t})\to\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t}),\quad(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)

(1.16)

in the sense of convergence of finite-dimensional distributions as
\lambda\to\infty
. Here
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}
denotes the KPZ fixed point with the narrow-wedge initial condition.

Remark 1.9
.

Both results can be extended to
\mathrm{t}=0
. In fact, using Proposition
1.5
(d), we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/2}\mathrm{x}}{\sqrt{2}},0\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(\sqrt{2\lambda}\mathrm{x})-\sqrt{2\lambda}|\mathrm{x}|\right)\to-|\mathrm{x}|=\min\{\mathrm{x},-\mathrm{x}\}

(1.17)

and

\lambda^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}(\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x},0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\lambda^{-1/3}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{ts}}(2\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x})-2\lambda^{2/3}|\mathrm{x}|\right)\to-\infty\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{x}\neq 0}=\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x},0)

(1.18)

as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Remark 1.10
.

One can easily recover the
1:2:3
scaling invariance of the KPZ fixed point (with the narrow-wedge initial condition) from (
1.16
). In fact, both
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})
and
c^{-1/3}\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(c^{2/3}\mathrm{x},c\mathrm{t})
are the limits of

(c\lambda)^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}((c\lambda)^{2/3}\mathrm{x},c\lambda\mathrm{t})=c^{-1/3}\cdot\lambda^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\lambda^{2/3}\cdot c^{2/3}\mathrm{x},\lambda\cdot c\mathrm{t})

as

, hence they have the same finite-dimensional distributions.

We can also see that Proposition
1.8
is consistent with the known results (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). In fact, since Brownian bridges locally behave like Brownian motions, we have

\epsilon^{-1/2}\left(\mathbb{B}_{1}(1+\epsilon\mathrm{t})+\sqrt{\epsilon}\mathrm{x}\right),\quad\epsilon^{-1/2}\left(\mathbb{B}_{2}(1+\epsilon\mathrm{t})-\sqrt{\epsilon}\mathrm{x}\right),

converges to
\mathbf{B}_{1}(-\mathrm{t})+\mathrm{x},\mathbf{B}_{2}(-\mathrm{t})-\mathrm{x}
jointly on
(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})\in\mathbb{R}\times(-\infty,0)
, as

. On the other hand, the KPZ fixed point enjoys the

scaling invariance, hence

\epsilon^{-1/2}\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\epsilon\mathrm{x},\epsilon^{3/2}\mathrm{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})

(1.20)

for
(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{t})\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
. Thus, the fields
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
provide the transition between (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

The proof of Proposition
1.8
is given in Section
5

The fields
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
are new to our best knowledge. Since they are the limiting fields of the KPZ fixed point near a conditioned high point, and the KPZ fixed point is expected to be universal in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class, we expect
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
are universal local limits for all the models in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class near a conditioned high point. In an upcoming work, we are planning to verify it for the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process, which is one of the simplest models in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang universality class. It might be possible to verify the same limits for the discrete polynuclear growth model, or equivalently, the discrete totally asymmetric simple exclusion process, using the formulas in
[
Joh20
,
Liao22
]
. However, for other models, it would be more difficult to consider the analogous upper tail limits due to the lack of exact formulas of the multipoint distribution of the height function.

We conjecture that
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
will also appear in the periodic KPZ fixed point conditioning on the upper tail event. The heuristic reason is that the scaling window for the upper tail field is so small that the periodicity might not be visable unless the period also shrink to the size of the scaling window. We leave this verification as a future project.

Finally, by the same reason, we conjecture that
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
do not depend on the initial condition. Below we heuristically show the reason why the information of the initial condition would disappear in the same scaling window for
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
. If we change the narrow-wedge initial condition to the flat initial condition, the limiting conditional random field of the KPZ fixed point before the conditioned high point was also obtained in
[
LW24
]
. It turns out that the limiting field analogous to (
1.1
)
is

\min\left\{\mathbb{B}_{1}(t)+x+\frac{(1-t)Z}{\sqrt{2}},\mathbb{B}_{2}(t)-x-\frac{(1-t)Z}{\sqrt{2}}\right\},\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)

(1.21)

where
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
are two independent Brownian bridges and
Z
is a standard normal random variable independent of
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
. Near
t=1+\epsilon\mathrm{t}\approx 1
,

1.3
Multipoint upper tail estimate of the KPZ fixed point

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on an upper tail estimate of the KPZ fixed point on a cluster of space-time points near a given point. In order to introduce the result, we first define an order
\prec
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Definition 1.12
.

We say
(\alpha,\tau)\prec(\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
for two points
(\alpha,\tau),(\alpha^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, if either one of the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\tau<\tau^{\prime}
;

(ii)

\tau=\tau^{\prime}
and
\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}
.

Note that
\prec
is a total order. Any two points on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are comparable by the order
\prec
and there is a unique way to arrange a set of distinct points on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by this order.

Now we introduce the multipoint upper tail estimate. For simplicity, we consider a cluster of points near
(0,1)
. The general case can be deduced to this case using the same argument as in the Remark
1.4
.

Define the following rescaled KPZ fixed point near
(0,1)

\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau):=\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)

(1.24)

for
(x,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
L>0
satisfying
1+\tau L^{-3/2}>0
.

Proposition 1.13
.

Assume that
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})\prec\cdots\prec(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})
are
m
points on the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We have

\begin{split}&16\pi L^{3/2}e^{\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})\geq\beta_{\ell}\right\}\right)\to\mathrm{T}(\boldsymbol{\beta};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\end{split}

(1.25)

and

\begin{split}&16\pi L^{3/2}e^{\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta_{k}}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})\geq\beta_{\ell}\right\}\right)\to\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta_{k}}\mathrm{T}(\boldsymbol{\beta};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})),\quad 1\leq k\leq m,\end{split}

(1.26)

as
L\to\infty
. The function
\mathrm{T}
is defined in Definition
2.2
.

One special case is that when
m=1
,

\mathrm{T}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=e^{\frac{2}{3}\tau-2\beta}
. The above result implies

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau)\geq\beta\right)\approx e^{\frac{2}{3}\tau-2\beta}\cdot\frac{e^{-\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}}}{16\pi L^{3/2}}

(1.27)

as
L\to\infty
. If we further assume that
(\alpha,\tau)=(0,0)
and
\beta=0
, this is the well known upper tail estimate of the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution (see
[
TW94
,
BBD08
]
for example). Intuitively, the upper tail of the KPZ fixed point at a set of points near
(0,1)
should have the same leading order as the one point upper tail at
(0,1)
, as long as the set of points are enough close to
(0,1)
and the bounds of the heights are also enough close. The Proposition
1.13
justifies this intuition and further provides the proper scaling under which each point in the cluster affects the approximation in a nontrivial way.

The proof of Proposition
1.13
is given in Section
3
.

1.4
Strategies and structure of the proofs

There are two main technical parts in the paper. The first one is an asymptotic analysis of the joint tail probability function with the upper tail scaling. The asymptotics of the joint distribution function before the conditioned hight point was performed in
[
LW24
]
. However, the analysis was only performed for distinct times, while the case of equal times was handled using a probabilistic argument based on the continuity of the limiting field since there was a difficulty analyzing the formula when times are equal. Another related paper
[
NZ22
]
considered the asymptotics of the conditional distribution after the conditioned hight point. However, only the one-point distribution function was analyzed due to the complexity of the formula. In this paper, we did a finer analysis with a different scaling and the analysis works for the multipoint distribution case with general space-time locations, including possibly equal times. Including the general space-time locations brings extra convergence issues of the integrals in the asymptotics analysis. We handled them by using different types of contours on the two half planes (which breaks the symmetry of the formula) and some careful estimate of the integrand along these contours. Another related analysis was performed for the upper tail conditional limit of the periodic KPZ fixed point before the high point
[
BL24
]
, which was analogous to
[
LW24
]
and the asymptotic analysis was also limited to the case with distinct times.

The second main technical part is to show that the limit of the conditional joint tail probability functions, the functions
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
, actually defines a nontrivial random field. We need to verify the consistency conditions of the functions
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
for applying the Kolmogorov extension theorem. The verification is nontrivial. We were able to prove the consistency combing the techniques from the asymptotic analysis and some probabilistic arguments.

Below is the structure of the paper.

In Section
2
, we first define the function
\mathrm{T}
, the limit of the multipoint tail function of the KPZ fixed point in the upper tail scaling. In subsection
2.2
, we use
\mathrm{T}
to define the multipoint tail probability functions
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
, and then in Proposition
2.5
we show that
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
satisfy the consistency requirements for the Kolmogorov extension theorem, hence they define a random field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
. Part of the proof of Proposition
2.5
relies on a tail estimate of the function
\mathrm{T}
which is postponed in Subsection
2.3
, see Proposition
2.9
. Besides, we discuss some properties the function
\mathrm{T}
and the field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}-\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)
, such as the differentiability and the changes of the formulas under a shift of parameters in Subsections
2.3
and
2.4
.

Section
3
is the proof of Proposition
1.13
about the asymptotics of the multipoint tail probability function with the upper tail scaling. We first prove a formula of the joint tail probability functions for the KPZ fixed point, see Proposition
3.1
. Then we provide the proof of Proposition
1.13
in Subsection
3.2
. The technical details involving the asymptotic analysis are postponed to Subsection
3.3
.

In Section
4
, we prove our main result, Theorem
1.1
, using Proposition
1.13
and some properties of the
\mathrm{T}
function proved in Subsection
2.3
.

Finally, in Section
5
and Section
6
we prove the properties of the fields
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}_{0}
listed in Proposition
1.8
and Proposition
1.5
respectively.

) if we set all the empty product to be
1
and set
U^{(2)}=V^{(2)}=\emptyset
in (
2.17
).

It is also easy to see that when
m\geq 2
, all the terms
\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\boldsymbol{z})
are well defined since the integrand decays super-exponentially fast along the integration contours due to the factors of the
f_{\ell}
functions, see the discussions after (
2.14
).

We also need to verify that the integrals and summations in (
2.16
) are well defined. In fact, note that (
2.17
) is independent of the specific choices of the
\Gamma
-contours (as long as they satisfy the nesting assumption and have the desired angles approaching to infinity described at the beginning of this subsection). We could select these integral contours such that the distance between any two contours and the distance between any contour to
\{1,-1\}
are at least
d>0
. Then we apply Lemma
2.1
and obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\mathcal{K}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{z};\boldsymbol{\beta})\right|\\
&\leq 2\left|\frac{f_{1}(-1)}{f_{1}(1)}\right|\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\frac{(1+|z_{\ell-1}|)^{2n_{\ell}}}{|z_{\ell-1}|^{n_{\ell}}|1-z_{\ell-1}|^{n_{\ell}-n_{\ell-1}}}\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{m}n_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}n_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}}{d^{1+2\sum_{\ell=2}^{m}}n_{\ell}}\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}\cup\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}}|f_{\ell+1}(u)|\frac{|\mathrm{d}u|}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{in}}\cup\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{1}{|f_{\ell+1}(v)|}\frac{|\mathrm{d}v|}{2\pi}\right|^{n_{\ell}}\\
&\leq\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\frac{(1+|z_{\ell-1}|)^{2n_{\ell}}}{|z_{\ell-1}|^{n_{\ell}}|1-z_{\ell-1}|^{n_{\ell}-n_{\ell-1}}}C^{\sum_{\ell=2}^{m}n_{\ell}}\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}n_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\end{split}

(2.18)

when
m\geq 2
, where
C
is a constant independent of
\boldsymbol{n}
and
\boldsymbol{z}
, and we used the fact that the functions
f_{\ell}(w)
decays super-exponentially fast along the left contours and grows super-exponentially fast along the right contours as
w\to\infty
and hence each integral in the middle of the above inequality is finite. Thus, we see that the right hand sides of (
2.16
) is absolutely convergent, and the function
\mathrm{T}
is well defined.

Finally, we remark that the
z_{1}
integral in (
2.16
) actually can be evaluated explicitly. In fact, noting (
2.17
), the
z_{1}
integral is (after we suppress other terms independent of
z_{1}
in the
\cdots
part)

\begin{split}&\oint_{>1}(1-z_{1})(1-z_{1}^{-1})^{n_{2}}\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1-z_{1}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}-\frac{z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\\
&\quad\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1-z_{1}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}-\frac{z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\cdots\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{1}(1-z_{1})}.\end{split}

(2.19)

If we deform the
z_{1}
contour to infinity, we see that any term involving
\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}}
or
\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{in}}}
vanishes, and we end with, after integrating
z_{1}
,

\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdots

(2.20)

Therefore, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

When
m\geq 2
, we have the following formula for
\mathrm{T}(\boldsymbol{\beta};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))
.

\begin{split}&\mathrm{T}(\boldsymbol{\beta};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))=(-1)^{m}\oint_{>1}\cdots\oint_{>1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{\ell}\geq 1\\
2\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(n_{2}!\cdots n_{m-1}!)^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\prod_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{\ell}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{\ell}(1-z_{\ell})},\end{split}

(2.21)

where
\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z_{2},\cdots,z_{m-1})
, and

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\\
&=2\prod_{\ell=2}^{m-1}(1-z_{\ell})^{n_{\ell}}(1-z_{\ell}^{-1})^{n_{\ell+1}}\cdot\prod_{\ell=3}^{m}\prod_{i_{\ell}=1}^{n_{\ell}}\left(\frac{1}{1-z_{\ell-1}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}-\frac{z_{\ell-1}}{1-z_{\ell-1}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\\
&\quad\prod_{\ell=3}^{m}\prod_{i_{\ell}=1}^{n_{\ell}}\left(\frac{1}{1-z_{\ell-1}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}-\frac{z_{\ell-1}}{1-z_{\ell-1}}\int_{\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\right)\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{2,\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v_{i_{2}}^{(2)}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{m}\prod_{i_{\ell}=1}^{n_{\ell}}\frac{f_{\ell}(u_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)})}{f_{\ell}(v_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)})}\cdot\frac{f_{1}(-1)}{f_{1}(1)}\\
&\quad\cdot\mathrm{C}(-1\sqcup V^{(2)};1\sqcup U^{(2)})\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\mathrm{C}(U^{(\ell)}\sqcup V^{(\ell+1)};V^{(\ell)}\sqcup U^{(\ell+1)})\cdot\mathrm{C}(U^{(m)};V^{(m)}).\end{split}

(2.22)

We could have used this formula as the definition of
\mathrm{T}
function. The reason we prefer the formula (
2.16
) in the definition is that the structure of (
2.16
) is more symmetric and aligns better with the pre-limit formula of the KPZ fixed point.

2.2
Definition of the upper tail field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}

Now we are ready to define the upper tail field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
using the Kolmogorov extension theorem and its joint tail probability functions. We denote

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(\alpha_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})\geq\beta_{\ell}\right\}\right)

(2.23)

for any
m\geq 1
, and
m
distinct points
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
. The function
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
is explicitly given below.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
m\geq 1
. Assume
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})
are
m
distinct points on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

If
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})\prec(\alpha_{2},\tau_{2})\prec\cdots\prec(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})
, and
(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})=(0,0)
for some
1\leq k\leq m
, then

\begin{split}&\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
&=\mathrm{T}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k-1},\max\{\beta_{k},0\},\beta_{k+1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})).\end{split}

(2.24)

(ii)

If
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})\prec\cdots\prec(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1})\prec(0,0)\prec(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})\prec\cdots\prec(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})
for some
1\leq k\leq m+1
, then

\begin{split}&\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
&=\mathrm{T}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k-1},0,\beta_{k},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1}),(0,0),(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})).\end{split}

(2.25)

(iii)

More generally, assume that the points
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m})
are not necessarily ordered, and
\sigma\in S_{m}
is the unique permutation such that
(\alpha_{\sigma_{1}},\tau_{\sigma_{1}})\prec\cdots\prec(\alpha_{\sigma_{m}},\tau_{\sigma_{m}})
, then

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))=\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{\sigma_{1}},\cdots,\beta_{\sigma_{m}};(\alpha_{\sigma_{1}},\tau_{\sigma_{1}}),\cdots,(\alpha_{\sigma_{m}},\tau_{\sigma_{m}}))

(2.26)

where the right hand side is defined in the previous two cases.

Recall that
\prec
is a total order defined in Definition
1.12
. Thus, Definition
2.4
gives the joint tail probability functions of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
for arbitrarily ordered points. We also note a special case when
m=1
and
(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})=(0,0)
, we have (recalling Definition
2.2
)

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(0,0))=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}(0,0)\geq\beta\right)=e^{-2\max\{\beta,0\}}.

(2.27)

Moreover,
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
has the following property by the equation (
2.67
) which will be proved later,

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=e^{\frac{2}{3}\tau-2\beta}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(-\beta;(-\alpha,-\tau)),\quad\text{ if }(0,0)\prec(\alpha,\tau).

(2.28)

We still need to verify that (
2.23
) actually defines a random field by checking the joint tail probability functions satisfy the consistency conditions so that the Kolmogorov extension theorem applies. Note that the Kolmogorov extension theorem works for the joint tail probability functions in the same way as for the joint cumulative distribution functions, since the joint tail probability functions of the field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
are the same as the joint cumulative distribution functions of
-\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
. The consistency of the joint probability tail functions is proved in the following proposition. We note that the proof relies on Proposition
1.13
and the first part of Theorem
1.1
. They imply (also see (
4.1
))

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\{\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})\geq\beta_{\ell}\right\}\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)=\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))

(2.29)

for any
\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq k\leq m
, as long as
(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})
,
1\leq k\leq m
, are distinct points on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\mathbb{H}_{L}
is the rescaled KPZ fixed point
\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau):=\sqrt{L}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha L^{-1},1+\tau L^{-3/2})-L\right)

as defined in (
1.24
). We emphasize that the proofs of Proposition
1.13
and the first part of Theorem
1.1
, including (
2.29
), does not depend on the existence of the random field
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}
but only uses the explicit formulas of
\mathbb{H}_{L}
and
\mathrm{T}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The function
\hat{\mathrm{T}}
has the following properties.

(a)

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))
is a tail probability function for each
(\alpha,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e.,

\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=1,\quad\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=0.

(2.30)

(b)

For
m\geq 2
, we have

\lim_{\beta_{k}\to\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))=0

(2.31)

and

\begin{split}&\lim_{\beta_{k}\to-\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
&=\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k+1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1}),(\alpha_{k+1},\tau_{k+1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\end{split}

(2.32)

for all
1\leq k\leq m
.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

We first prove (b) using (a), then prove (a).

Assume Proposition
2.5
(a) holds. Note that (
2.29
) implies

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\leq\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{k};(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})).

(2.33)

Combining with (a) we immediately obtain (
2.31
).

On the other hand, (
2.29
) also implies

\begin{split}0\leq&\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k+1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1}),(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
&-\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
=&\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell\neq k}\left\{\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})\geq\beta_{\ell}\right\}\bigcap\left\{\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})<\beta_{k}\right\}\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\\
\leq&\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})<\beta_{k}\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\\
=&1-\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta_{k};(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k})).\end{split}

(2.34)

Combining with (a) we have (
2.32
).

It remains to show Proposition
2.5
(a). The large
\beta
limit is easier. We use (
2.29
) and obtain

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau)\geq\beta\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\leq\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau)\geq\beta\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)}=e^{\frac{2}{3}\tau-2\beta}

(2.35)

where we used the asymptotics (
1.27
) in the last equation. The above inequality implies that

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=0.

(2.36)

The other limit when
\beta\to-\infty
is more complicated. We consider four different cases.

Case 1:
\alpha=\tau=0
. In this case by (
2.27
) we have
\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(0,0))=e^{-2\max\{\beta,0\}}
which is
1
when
\beta<0
.

Case 2:
\tau>0
. We need an inequality in the notation of the directed landscape
[
DOV22
]
. The directed landscape
\mathcal{L}(y,s;x,t)
,
0<s<t
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, is a “random metric” which has the following relation to the KPZ fixed point
\mathcal{L}(0,0;x,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(x,t)
, where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}
means an equation in law. It also has the following triangle inequality

\mathcal{L}(0,0;x,t)\geq\mathcal{L}(0,0;y,s)+\mathcal{L}(y,s;x,t)

(2.37)

for any
s<t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}(0,0;y,s)
, and
\mathcal{L}(y,s;x,t)
are independent. Finally, the one point distribution of
\mathcal{L}(y,s;x,t)
is given by the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{L}(y,s;x,t)\leq h\right)=\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{h}{(t-s)^{1/3}}+\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{(t-s)^{4/3}}\right).

(2.38)

Using the language of the directed landscape, we have, for any
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})\geq\beta_{1},\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{2},\tau_{2})<\beta_{2}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha_{1}L^{-1},1+\tau_{1}L^{-3/2})\geq L+\beta_{1}L^{-1/2},\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\alpha_{2}L^{-1},1+\tau_{2}L^{-3/2})<L+\beta_{2}L^{-1/2}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{L}(0,0;\alpha_{1}L^{-1},1+\tau_{1}L^{-3/2})\geq L+\beta_{1}L^{-1/2}\\
\mathcal{L}(\alpha_{1}L^{-1},1+\tau_{1}L^{-3/2};\alpha_{2}L^{-1},1+\tau_{2}L^{-3/2})<(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})L^{-1/2}\end{array}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1})\geq\beta_{1}\right)\cdot\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{1/3}}+\frac{(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})^{2}}{(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{4/3}}\right).\end{split}

(2.39)

As a special case we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0,\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau)<\beta\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\cdot\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\tau^{1/3}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\tau^{4/3}}\right)

(2.40)

when
\tau>0
, and hence

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,\tau)<\beta\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\leq\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\tau^{1/3}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\tau^{4/3}}\right).

(2.41)

By taking
L\to\infty
and applying (
2.29
), we get

1-\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\tau^{1/3}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\tau^{4/3}}\right)\leq\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))\leq 1.

(2.42)

We further take
\beta\to-\infty
and obtain the desired limit
\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=1
.

Case 3:
\tau<0
. Note in this case we have, using Definition
2.4
,

\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,\tau))=\mathrm{T}(\beta,0;(\alpha,\tau),(0,0))

(2.43)

which goes to
1
as
\beta\to-\infty
by Proposition
2.9
which will be proved in the next subsection as a property of the function
\mathrm{T}
.

Case 4:
\tau=0
and
\alpha\neq 0
. We first apply (
2.39
) and obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta,\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)\geq\beta/2\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)\geq\beta/2\right)\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\beta/2\right).

(2.44)

By taking
L\to\infty
and applying (
1.27
), we have

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta,\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)\geq\beta/2\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)}\leq e^{-\frac{2}{3}-\beta}\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\beta/2\right).

(2.45)

This further implies

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta,\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)\geq\beta/2\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\leq e^{-\frac{2}{3}-\beta}\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}\left(\beta/2\right)\to 0

(2.46)

as
\beta\to-\infty
, where we used the left tail estimate of the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution
\mathrm{F}_{\mathrm{GUE}}(\beta)\approx e^{-(\frac{1}{24}+o(1))|\beta|^{3}}
, see
[
BBD08
]
. On the other hand, the third case above implies

\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)<\beta/2\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)=\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}(1-\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta/2;(\alpha,-1)))=0.

(2.47)

Finally we also note that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta,\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)\geq\beta/2\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,-1)<\beta/2\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right).\end{split}

(2.48)

Combining the above argument, we obtain
\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{H}_{L}(\alpha,0)<\beta\mid\mathbb{H}_{L}(0,0)\geq 0\right)=0
, which implies
\lim_{\beta\to-\infty}\hat{\mathrm{T}}(\beta;(\alpha,0))=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.3
Some properties of
\mathrm{T}

In this subsection, we discuss several properties of the function
\mathrm{T}
.

We first note that the parameters
\beta_{\ell}
,
\alpha_{\ell}
,
\tau_{\ell}
,
1\leq\ell\leq m
, only appear in the functions
f_{\ell}
in the definition of
\mathrm{T}
, and these functions are exponential functions which are differentiable arbitrarily many times with respect to the parameters. One might wonder whether this property passes to the function
\mathrm{T}
. This actually is true, and we state it in Proposition
2.7
. It follows from the following lemma about the change of the order of integral/summation and the differentiation.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
\mu
is a complex measure on a space
\Omega
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed number. If

\int_{\Omega}\left|F(W)e^{yg(W)}\right||\mathrm{d}\mu(W)|<\infty,\quad\int_{\Omega}\left|F(W)g(W)e^{yg(W)}\right||\mathrm{d}\mu(W)|<\infty

(2.49)

for
y\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)
for some
\delta>0
, then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\Bigg|_{y=x}\int_{\Omega}F(W)e^{yg(W)}\mathrm{d}\mu(W)=\int_{\Omega}F(W)g(W)e^{xg(W)}\mathrm{d}\mu(W).

(2.50)

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

We need the following simple inequality

\left|\frac{e^{w}-1}{w}\right|\leq e\cdot(|e^{w}|+1),\quad w\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.51)

Now assuming this inequality holds, we prove the lemma. We write

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\Bigg|_{y=x}\int_{\Omega}F(W)e^{yg(W)}\mathrm{d}\mu(W)&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}F(W)\frac{e^{(x+\epsilon)g(W)}-e^{xg(W)}}{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\mu(W)\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}F(W)g(W)\frac{e^{(x+\epsilon)g(W)}-e^{xg(W)}}{\epsilon g(W)}\mathrm{d}\mu(W).\end{split}

(2.52)

Note that for any
\epsilon\in(-\delta/2,\delta/2)
, we have, by using (
2.51
),

\begin{split}\left|\frac{e^{(x+\epsilon)g(W)}-e^{xg(W)}}{\epsilon g(W)}\right|&\leq e\cdot\left|e^{xg(W)}\right|\left(|e^{\epsilon g(W)}|+1\right)\\
&\leq e\cdot\left|e^{xg(W)}\right|\left(|e^{\frac{\delta}{2}g(W)}|+|e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}g(W)}|+1\right).\end{split}

(2.53)

Moreover, the assumption in the lemma implies

\int_{\Omega}|F(W)g(W)|e\cdot\left|e^{xg(W)}\right|\left(|e^{\frac{\delta}{2}g(W)}|+|e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}g(W)}|+1\right)|\mathrm{d}\mu(W)|<\infty.

(2.54)

Therefore the dominated convergence theorem applies on the right hand side of (
2.52
) and we can take the differentiation within the integral. This proves the lemma.

It remains to prove (
2.51
). We prove it by considering two different cases.
The first case is
|w|\geq 1
. In this case, we have

\left|\frac{e^{w}-1}{w}\right|\leq\left|e^{w}-1\right|\leq|e^{w}|+1.

(2.55)

The second case is that
|w|\leq 1
. Note that
(e^{w}-1)/w
is analytic. Hence, its maximal norm within the disk is obtained at the boundary

\left|\frac{e^{w}-1}{w}\right|\leq\max_{|w^{\prime}|=1}\left|\frac{e^{w^{\prime}}-1}{w^{\prime}}\right|=\max_{|w^{\prime}|=1}|e^{w^{\prime}}-1|\leq e+1\leq e+|e^{w+1}|.

(2.56)

Combining the two cases we get the desired inequality.
∎

&=Q_{1}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\cdot Q_{2}(\beta),\quad\beta\neq 0,\end{split}

where

(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{k-1},\tau_{k-1}),(0,0),(\alpha_{k},\tau_{k}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))

(2.81)

5.2
KPZ fixed point limit in the positive time regime

In the positive time regime, Proposition
1.8
follows from the following proposition.

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume
m\geq 2
is an integer,
(\mathrm{x}_{\ell},\mathrm{t}_{\ell})
,
2\leq\ell\leq m
, are
m-1
points on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
satisfying
(\mathrm{x}_{2},\mathrm{t}_{2})\prec\cdots\prec(\mathrm{x}_{m},\mathrm{t}_{m})
, and
\mathrm{h}_{2},\cdots,\mathrm{h}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=2}^{m}\left\{\lambda^{-1/3}\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{UT}}\left(\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x}_{\ell},\lambda\mathrm{t}_{\ell}\right)\geq\mathrm{h}_{\ell}\right\}\right)\to\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=2}^{m}\left\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x}_{\ell},\mathrm{t}_{\ell})\geq\mathrm{h}_{\ell}\right\}\right)

(5.27)

as
\lambda\to\infty
, where
\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}
is the KPZ fixed point with the narrow wedge initial condition.

Below we prove this proposition.

Denote

\mathrm{x}_{1}=\mathrm{t}_{1}=\mathrm{h}_{1}=0.

(5.28)

Recall Definition
2.4
and Proposition
2.3
. We write the left hand side of (
5.27
) as

\begin{split}&\mathrm{T}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))\\
&=(-1)^{m}\oint_{>1}\cdots\oint_{>1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{\ell}\geq 1\\
2\leq\ell\leq m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(n_{2}!\cdots n_{m-1}!)^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\prod_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{\ell}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z_{\ell}(1-z_{\ell})},\end{split}

(5.29)

where

\alpha_{\ell}=\lambda^{2/3}\mathrm{x}_{\ell},\quad\tau_{\ell}=\lambda\mathrm{t}_{\ell},\quad\beta_{\ell}=\lambda^{1/3}\mathrm{h}_{\ell}

(5.30)

for
1\leq\ell\leq m
, and the function
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})
and the notations are the same as in Proposition
2.3
. Note
n_{1}=1
is fixed and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z_{2},\cdots,z_{m-1})
. We copy the formula below for convenience of the readers. Note that we dropped the factor

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\\

&\quad\cdot\mathrm{C}(-1\sqcup V^{(2)};1\sqcup U^{(2)})\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\mathrm{C}(U^{(\ell)}\sqcup V^{(\ell+1)};V^{(\ell)}\sqcup U^{(\ell+1)})\cdot\mathrm{C}(U^{(m)};V^{(m)}).\end{split}

(5.31)

We will apply the steepest descent method to the formula (
5.31
) of
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})
when
\lambda\to\infty
. This will be done by shrinking the contours

\Gamma_{\ell,\diamond}^{\star}\to\lambda^{-1/3}\Gamma_{\ell,\diamond}^{\star},\quad 3\leq\ell\leq m,\quad\diamond\in\{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{R}\},\quad\star\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}

(5.32)

and

(5.33)

Recall that we initially require the contours

\pm 1

while

and

u_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}=\lambda^{-1/3}\xi_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)},\quad v_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}=\lambda^{-1/3}\eta_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}

(5.34)

where
\xi_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}\in\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{L}}^{\star}
and
\eta_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}\in\Gamma_{\ell,\mathrm{R}}^{\star}

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\\

(5.37)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\boldsymbol{\beta};\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}})\\

(5.38)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathrm{T}(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m};(\alpha_{1},\tau_{1}),\cdots,(\alpha_{m},\tau_{m}))=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\ell=2}^{m}\left\{\mathrm{H^{KPZ}}(\mathrm{x}_{\ell},\mathrm{t}_{\ell})\geq\mathrm{h}_{\ell}\right\}\right).

(5.39)

5.31

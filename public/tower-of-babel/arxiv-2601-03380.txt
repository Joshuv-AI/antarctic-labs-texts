On semi-openness of fiber-onto extensions of minimal semiflows and quasi-separable maps Journal: Topol. Appl.
Xiongping Dai, Li Feng, Congying Lv, Yuxuan Xie

Abstract
This paper is devoted to finding conditions for a continuous surjection ϕ : X → Y \phi\colon X\rightarrow Y between compact Hausdorff spaces and its induced affine map ϕ ∗ : ℳ 1 ​ ( X ) → ℳ 1 ​ ( Y ) \phi_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y) between the regular Borel probability spaces to be semi-open. For that, we mainly prove the following by using the structure theory of extensions of semiflows and inverse limit technique: 1. If ϕ \phi is an extension of minimal flows, then ϕ \phi and ϕ ∗ \phi_{*} are both semi-open. 2. If ϕ \phi is a quasi-separable extension of minimal semiflows with X X is ϕ \phi -fiber-onto, then ϕ \phi and ϕ ∗ \phi_{*} are both semi-open. 3. If Y Y is metrizable, then ϕ \phi is semi-open if and only if ϕ ∗ \phi_{*} is semi-open. 4. If ϕ \phi is quasi almost 1-1, then ϕ \phi and ϕ ∗ \phi_{*} are both semi-open.

1.3

Let

1.3
A Lemma

Let

Indeed, for necessity, let

Lemma

2.1.1 Lemma

[

Let

\mathrm{int}_{Y}f[U]

Recall
[

such that

. Then a subset

(see
[
46

).
Consequently, a continuous map

is semi-open, if and only if

is semi-open in

, if and only if the image of every semi-open set is semi-open. Here the continuity of

Recall that a family
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathscr{O}(X)
is a
pseudo-base
for
X

[
54
]
if any
U\in\mathscr{O}(X)
contains some member of
\mathcal{B}
. For example,

\beta\mathbb{N}
is not a second countable space, but it has a countable pseudo-base.
As an application of “semi-openness”, we shall prove a topological Fubini theorem as follows:

2.1.2 Lemma

(cf.
[
15
, Lem. 5.3]
for
X
a Polish space)
.

Suppose that
p\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a semi-open continuous onto mapping, where

X
has a countable family
\mathscr{U}
of open subsets such that for all
y\in Y
,
\{U\cap p^{-1}(y)\,|\,U\in\mathscr{U}\}
is a pseudo-base for
p^{-1}(y)
. If
G\subseteq X
is a dense open set, then

Y_{G}=\left\{y\in Y\,|\,G\cap p^{-1}(y)\textrm{ is dense open in }p^{-1}(y)\right\}

is residual in
Y
. In particular, if
K\subseteq X
is residual, then

Y_{K}=\left\{y\in Y\,|\,K\cap p^{-1}(y)\textrm{ is residual in }p^{-1}(y)\right\}

is residual in
Y
.

Proof.

Let
F=X\setminus G
. Then
F
is a nowhere dense closed set in
X
. Let
F_{y}=F\cap p^{-1}(y)
for all
y\in Y
. Let

B=\left\{y\in Y\,|\,\mathrm{int}_{p^{-1}(y)}F_{y}\not=\emptyset\right\}
.
So if
y\notin B
, then
G_{y}
is open dense in
p^{-1}(y)
. Thus,
Y\setminus B\subseteq Y_{G}
and we need only prove that
B
is meager in
Y
.
For that, write
\mathscr{U}=\{U_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. If
y\in B
, then
U_{n}\cap p^{-1}(y)\subseteq F_{y}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Put

C_{n}=\{y\in B\,|\,U_{n}\cap p^{-1}(y)\subseteq F_{y}\}
and
D_{n}=\mathrm{int}_{Y}\bar{C}_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
B=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}
, and
B
is meager in
Y
if each
D_{n}=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
D_{n}\not=\emptyset
, then
U_{n}\cap p^{-1}(y)\subseteq F_{y}
for all
y\in D_{n}\cap C_{n}
and
D_{n}\cap C_{n}
is dense in
D_{n}
. So
U_{n}\cap p^{-1}[D_{n}\cap C_{n}]\subseteq F
. Further, by Lemma
2.1.1
, it follows that
\emptyset\not=U_{n}\cap p^{-1}[D_{n}]\subseteq\bar{F}=F
, contrary to
F
being nowhere dense in
X
. The proof is complete.
∎

We notice that the above Fubini theorem is due to L. E. J. Brouwer 1919 for the special case
p\colon[0,1]\times[0,1]\xrightarrow{(x,y)\mapsto x}[0,1]
, to C. Kuratowski and S. Ulam 1932 for

where

are separable metric spaces, and to Oxtoby 1960
[
54
]
for

with

having a countable pseudo-base. See Veech (1970)
[

and Glasner (1990)
[

for the case that

2.1.3 Lemma
.

Let
p\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a semi-open continuous onto mapping, where
X
is a pseudo-metric space. Then

X_{o}[p]=\{x\in X\,|\,p[U]\in\mathfrak{N}_{p(x)}(Y)\ \forall U\in\mathfrak{N}_{x}(X)\}

.

Proof.

Given any

X_{n}=\{x\in X\,|\,\exists U\in\mathfrak{N}_{x}(X)\textrm{ with diameter }\leq 1/n\textrm{ s.t. }p[U]\in\mathfrak{N}_{p(x)}(Y)\}

Note that if
f\colon X\rightarrow Y

2.1.4 Theorem

(cf.

\&

Let

be either upper semi-continuous or lower semi-continuous, where

In fact, we need only the following special case, for which we give a simple proof without using the

2.1.5 Lemma

(cf.
[
3
, Lem. 14.44]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous map of compact metric spaces. Then the set
D(f_{\textsl{ad}})
of points of discontinuity of

is meager in
Y
.

Proof.

Noting that
f_{\textsl{ad}}
is continuous at every point of
Y\setminus f[X]
, we may assume that
f[X]=Y
.
Let
Z=f_{\textsl{ad}}[Y]\subset 2^{X}
. Then
f_{\textsl{ad}}\colon Y\rightarrow Z
is 1-1 open (
\forall V\in\mathscr{O}(Y)

f_{\textsl{ad}}[V]=\{f^{-1}(y)\,|\,y\in V\}=\langle f^{-1}[V]\rangle
); and
f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}\colon Z\subset 2^{X}\rightarrow Y
,
f^{-1}(y)\mapsto y
is uniformly continuous. So,

admits a unique continuous extension, denoted
\widetilde{f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}}\colon\bar{Z}\rightarrow Y
(cf.
[

, Thm. 6.26]

Let
\varepsilon>0
. To prove that
Y_{c}(f_{\textsl{ad}})=Y\setminus D(f_{\textsl{ad}})
is residual in

, it suffices to prove that
Y_{\varepsilon,c}(f_{\textsl{ad}})
—the set of
\varepsilon
-continuous points of
f_{\textsl{ad}}
is dense, since
Y_{\varepsilon,c}(f_{\textsl{ad}})
is always open. For that, let
V
and
U
be open sets in
Y
with
\emptyset\not=V\subseteq\bar{V}\subseteq U
. Since
f_{\textsl{ad}}[V]
is open in
Z
, there is a sequence of open balls

in

with diameter

and

. Then

V={\bigcup}_{n}f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}[B_{n}]={\bigcup}_{n}\widetilde{f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}}[B_{n}]\subseteq{\bigcup}_{n}\overline{\widetilde{f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}}[B_{n}]}={\bigcup}_{n}\widetilde{f_{\textsl{ad}}^{-1}}[\bar{B}_{n}]\subseteq\bar{V},

where
\bar{B}_{n}
is the closure of

in

for some

and

is arbitrary,

is dense in

2.1.6
Basic notation.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
be an extension of semiflows with phase semigroup
S
.
The “circle operation” (cf.
[

,

,
3
,
17
]
or
[

), as the extension to

of

, is defined as follows:

p\diamond K=\lim_{i}t_{i}K\in 2^{X}\ \forall p\in\beta S
and

, where
t_{i}\in S\to p
in
\beta S

Here

, as a “point” of

, is independent of the choice of the net
\{t_{i}\}
in
S
satisfying
t_{i}\to p
in
\beta S
.
Next we can define a closed subset of the hyperspace

associated with
\phi
as follows:

2^{X\!,\phi}=\{A\in 2^{X}\,|\,\exists z\in Z\textrm{ s.t. }A\subseteq\phi^{-1}(z)\}
.

Clearly,
2^{X\!,\phi}
is an
S
-invariant subset of
2^{X}
so that

2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}:=S\curvearrowright 2^{X\!,\phi}

is a subsemiflow of

. Let

\tilde{\varphi}\colon 2^{X\!,\phi}\rightarrow Z
be defined by

iff

.

Clearly,
\tilde{\varphi}
is

-equivariant so that
\mathscr{Z}
is a factor of

by means of

. In particular, we have for

and

that
\tilde{\varphi}(p\diamond\phi^{-1}(z))=pz
.

2.1.7
Highly proximal extension.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
be an extension of semiflows, where
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Z}
need not be minimal.
As in

,

,

\phi

(h.p.) or
\mathscr{X}

of

via

, iff for all

there is a net
t_{n}\in T
with

in

, iff for all

there is
p\in\beta S
with

. In this case,
\phi
is of course proximal (cf. Def.
5.20
C).

Let
\mathscr{Z}
be minimal. If
\phi
is almost 1-1, then it is obviously an h.p. extension. If
\mathscr{X}
is a
\phi
-fiber-onto metrizable semiflow, then
\phi
is h.p. if and only if it is almost 1-1 by Lemma

and Lemma

[

2.1.8 Lemma

(cf.
[
5
]
,
[
58
, Thm. IV.2.3]
,
[
17
, Prop. VI.3.3]
for minimal flows)
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
be an extension of semiflows, where
\mathscr{Z}
is minimal and
\mathscr{X}
has a dense set of a.p. points, such that
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto. Then following conditions are equivalent:

1.

\phi
is h.p. (so
\mathscr{X}
is minimal).

2.

\phi
is irreducible.

3.

\tilde{\varphi}\colon 2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is h.p.

4.

\tilde{\varphi}\colon 2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is proximal.

5.

2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}
has a unique
S
-minimal subset.

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)\Rightarrow(5)
is obvious. Now we prove that
(5)\Rightarrow(1)
. Clearly,
\{\{x\}\,|\,x\in X\}
is an
S
-invariant closed subset of
2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}
. Let
z\in Z
and
\mathfrak{z}=\phi^{-1}(z)\in 2^{X\!,\phi}
. As
\overline{S\mathfrak{z}}
contains an a.p. point of
2^{\mathscr{X}\!\!,\phi}
, it follows that there exists some element
p\in\beta S
such that
p\diamond\phi^{-1}(z)
is a singleton. Thus,
\phi
is highly proximal. The proof is complete.
∎

2.1.9 Lemma

(Ellis-Shoenfeld-Auslander-Glasner; cf.
[
56
,
5
,
58
]
in the class of minimal flows)
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
be an extension of semiflows with
\mathscr{Z}
being minimal. Let

Z_{\phi}=\mathrm{cl}_{2^{X\!,\phi}}\{t\phi^{-1}(z)\,|\,z\in Z\ \&\ t\in S\}\subseteq 2^{X\!,\phi}
.

Then:

1.

\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}|_{Z_{\phi}}\colon Z_{\phi}\rightarrow Z
is h.p.;

2.

Z_{\phi}
has a unique
S
-minimal subset, denoted
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
;

3.

If
\mathscr{X}
is minimal, then
\phi
is h.p. if and only if
\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}
,
x\mapsto\{x\}
is an isomorphism.

4.

If
\mathscr{X}
has a dense set of a.p. points, then
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto and
\phi
is open if and only if
\varphi\colon\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is an isomorphism.

If
X
is a metric space, then so is
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
. (
Note.

\varphi\colon\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is called the h.p. quasi-factor representation of
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
in
\mathscr{X}
.)

Proof.

First of all, we note that
Z_{\phi}
is an
S
-invariant closed subset of
2^{\mathscr{X}\!,\phi}
. By Zorn’s Lemma, we can select a minimal point in
Z_{\phi}
under inclusion.

(1): Let
A\in Z_{\phi}
be a minimal point. Then we can find a point
z\in Z
and an element
p\in\beta S
with

p\diamond\phi^{-1}(z)=A
and a net
t_{i}\in S
with
t_{i}\to p
in
\beta S
. This implies that
t_{i}[\varphi^{-1}(z)]\to\{A\}
in
2^{Z_{\phi}}
. Thus,
\varphi\colon\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is highly proximal.

(2): Obvious by (1).

(3): Let
\mathscr{X}
be minimal. If
\phi
is h.p., then
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}=\{\{x\}\,|\,x\in X\}
by (2) and obviously
\mathscr{X}\cong\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}
. Conversely, if
\mathscr{X}\cong\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}
, then
\phi=\varphi
is h.p. by (1).

(4): Necessity is obvious.
Conversely, suppose
\varphi\colon Z_{\phi}^{\natural}\rightarrow Z
is 1-1. Then by (2), we have that
\varphi^{-1}(z)=\{\phi^{-1}(z)\}
for all
z\in Z
, where
\phi^{-1}(z)
is thought of as a point in
2^{X\!,\phi}
.
Indeed, let
z\in Z
,
x\in\phi^{-1}(z)
and
\varphi^{-1}(z)=\{z^{\natural}\}
. Since
\mathscr{Z}
is minimal and
\mathscr{X}
has a dense set of a.p. points, there exists a net
t_{i}\in S\to p\in\beta S
and a net of a.p. points
x_{i}\in\phi^{-1}(z)
such that
t_{i}x_{i}\to x
and
pz=z
. On the other hand, by
x_{i}\in z^{\natural}
, it follows that
x\in pz^{\natural}=z^{\natural}
. Thus,
z^{\natural}=\phi^{-1}(z)
.
Since
\varphi
is a homeomorphism, hence
\varphi^{-1}(z)
is continuous w.r.t.
z\in Z
so that
\phi
is open. To prove that
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto, let
t\in S
and
z\in Z
. Let
z^{\natural}=\phi^{-1}(z)\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
and
z_{1}^{\natural}=\phi^{-1}(tz)\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
. Then by the
S
-equivariant of
\varphi
, we have
z_{1}^{\natural}=tz^{\natural}
so that
\phi^{-1}(tz)=t\diamond\phi^{-1}(z)=t\phi^{-1}(z)
. Thus,
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto.

Finally, if
X
is a compact metric space, then so is
2^{X\!,\phi}
. So
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
, as a subspace of
2^{X\!,\phi}
, is a metric space as well. The proof is complete
∎

2.2
Open lifting via a pair of h.p. extensions

As in
[
56
,
5
,
58
,
3
,
17
]
in minimal flows, we can define the so-called ‘Auslander-Glasner commutative diagram’ (AG-CD) for an extension
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
of semiflows with only
\mathscr{Z}
minimal, but with
\mathscr{X}
having a dense set of a.p. points instead of ‘minimal’ as follows.

2.2.1 Theorem

(AG-lifting; cf.
[
56
,
5
,
58
]
or
[
17
, Thm. VI.3.8]
for
\mathscr{X}
a minimal flow)
.

Suppose
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
is an extension of semiflows, where
\mathscr{Z}
is minimal and
\mathscr{X}
has a dense set of a.p. points. Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}

be the h.p. quasi-factor representation of
\mathscr{Z}
in
\mathscr{X}
and

X_{\phi}^{\natural}=X\vee Z_{\phi}^{\natural}=\{(x,K)\,|\,x\in K\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}\textrm{ s.t. }\phi(x)=\varphi(K)\},\quad\varrho\colon X_{\phi}^{\natural}\xrightarrow{(x,K)\mapsto x}X,\quad\phi_{\natural}\colon X_{\phi}^{\natural}\xrightarrow{(x,K)\mapsto K}Z_{\phi}^{\natural}.

Then there exists a ‘canonically determined’ CD of extensions of semiflows:

\begin{CD}\mathscr{X}@<{\varrho}<{}<\mathscr{X}_{\phi}^{\natural}\\
@V{\phi}V{}V@V{}V{\phi_{\natural}}V\\
\mathscr{Z}@<{\varphi}<{}<\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}\end{CD}\qquad\textrm{s.t. }\begin{cases}(1)\ \varrho,\varphi\textrm{ are h.p.};\\
(2)\ \textrm{if }\varphi\textrm{ is 1-1, then so is }\varrho;\\
(3)\ \mathscr{X}_{\phi}^{\natural}\textrm{ is }\phi_{\natural}\textrm{-fiber-onto and }\phi_{\natural}\textrm{ is open}.\end{cases}

[formula]

Moreover, if
X
is a metric space and
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto, then
\varrho
and
\varphi
are almost 1-1 (see
[
59
]
for
\mathscr{X}
a metric minimal flow).

Proof.

Clearly,
X_{\phi}^{\natural}
is
S
-invariant closed in
X\times Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
. Since
X
contains a dense set of a.p. points and
\mathscr{Z}
is minimal,
\bigcup_{A\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}}A
is dense in
X
. Thus,
\varrho[X_{\phi}^{\natural}]=X
and
\varrho
is an extension of semiflows. This shows that
\mathrm{AG}(\phi)
is a well-defined CD of extensions of semiflows, where
\mathscr{Z}
and
\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}
are minimal.

(1): Given
x\in X
and letting
z=\phi(x)
, we have that

\varrho^{-1}(x)=\{(x,K)\,|\,K\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}\textrm{ s.t. }x\in K\}\subseteq\{x\}\times\varphi^{-1}(z).

Thus,
\varrho
and
\varphi
are h.p. by Lemma
2.1.9
.
Clearly,
\varrho
is 1-1 restricted to each
\phi_{\natural}
-fiber. If
\varphi
is 1-1, then for
z_{1}^{\natural}\not=z_{2}^{\natural}
in
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
we have that
\varphi(z_{1}^{\natural})\not=\varphi(z_{2}^{\natural})
and
\varrho[{\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(z_{1}^{\natural})]\cap\varrho[{\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(z_{2}^{\natural})]=\emptyset
so that
\varrho
is 1-1.

(2): By
{\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(z^{\natural})=K\times\{z^{\natural}\}
for all
z^{\natural}=K\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
, it follows easily that
\phi_{\natural}
is open. Now for all
t\in S
and
z^{\natural}=K\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
, since
tz^{\natural}=tK
and
{\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(tz^{\natural})=tK\times\{tz^{\natural}\}=t(K\times\{z^{\natural}\})
, hence
t{\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(z^{\natural})={\phi_{\natural}}^{-1}(tz^{\natural})
. Thus,
\mathscr{X}_{\phi}^{\natural}
is
\phi_{\natural}
-fiber-onto.

Finally, let
X
be a metric space. Then
2^{X\!,\phi}
is a metric space. Thus,
Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
and
X_{\phi}^{\natural}
are obviously metric spaces.
And if, in addition,
\mathscr{X}
is
\phi
-fiber-onto,
\varrho
and
\varphi
are obviously almost 1-1 extensions by Lemma
2.1.5
and Lemma
2.1.9
. Indeed, by Lemma
2.1.5
, we can choose a point
z_{0}\in Z
such that
\phi_{\textsl{ad}}\colon Z\rightarrow 2^{X}
and
\varphi_{\textsl{ad}}\colon Z\rightarrow 2^{Z_{\phi}^{\natural}}
both are continuous at
z_{0}
. Let
z\in Z
. Since
\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural}
is minimal (Lem.
2.1.9
), there exists a net
t_{i}\in S
with
t_{i}z\to z_{0}
in
Z
such that

t_{i}\phi^{-1}(z)=\phi(t_{i}z)\to\phi^{-1}(z_{0})\in Z_{\phi}^{\natural}
in
2^{X}
and
\phi^{-1}(z_{0})
is a inclusion minimal element in
Z_{\phi}
. This implies that
\varphi^{-1}(z_{0})=\{\phi^{-1}(z_{0})\}
. Thus,
\varphi
is almost 1-1. For every
x_{0}\in\phi^{-1}(z_{0})
, since
\varrho^{-1}(x_{0})=\{(x_{0},\phi^{-1}(z_{0}))\}
, hence
\varrho
is also almost 1-1.
The proof is complete
∎

.

Suppose
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}

\mathscr{X}

In

2.2.3 Theorem
.

Suppose
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}

\phi

\phi_{*}

Proof.

First,
\phi

\begin{CD}\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{X})@<{\varrho_{*}}<{}<\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{X}_{\phi}^{\natural})\\
@V{\phi_{*}}V{}V@V{}V{{\phi_{\natural}}_{*}}V\\
\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{Z})@<{\varphi_{*}}<{}<\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{Z}_{\phi}^{\natural})\end{CD}.

Since
\phi_{\natural}
is open, hence
{\phi_{\natural}}_{*}
is open by Theorem
1.3
D-(1). Moreover, since
\varphi
is h.p., it follows from Lemma
2.1.8
and Theorem
1.4
B that

\varphi_{*}
is irreducible; and so,
\varphi_{*}
is semi-open. Thus,
\phi_{*}
is semi-open. The proof is complete.

2.2.4
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
and
\psi\colon\mathscr{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{Z}
be two extensions of semiflows with the same phase semigroup
S
; then the fibred product of
\phi
and
\psi
is defined as follows:

\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\,|\,\phi(x)=\psi(y)\}
.

Clearly,
\mathscr{R}_{\phi\psi}
is a subsemiflow of
\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{Y}
and is an extension of
\mathscr{Z}
. If
\mathscr{R}_{\phi\psi}
is minimal, then
\phi
is refereed to as
disjoint
with
\psi
, denoted
\phi\bot\psi
or
\mathscr{X}\bot_{\mathscr{Z}}\mathscr{Y}
.

2.2.5 Lemma

(cf.
[
57
,
58
]
for
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
in minimal flows)
.

Let there exist a CD of extensions of semiflows as follows:

\begin{CD}\mathscr{X}&@<{\hskip 8.19447pt\rho_{X}\hskip 8.19447pt}<{}<&\mathscr{R}_{\phi\psi}\\
@V{\phi}V{}V&&@V{}V{\rho_{Y}}V\\
\mathscr{Z}&@<{\hskip 8.19447pt\psi\hskip 8.19447pt}<{}<&\mathscr{Y}\end{CD}\quad\textrm{where}\begin{cases}\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}\xrightarrow{\rho_{X}\colon(x,y)\mapsto x}X,\\
\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}\xrightarrow{\rho_{Y}\colon(x,y)\mapsto y}Y.\end{cases}

Then:

1.

If
\psi
and
\rho_{Y}
are semi-open, then
\rho_{X}
is semi-open.

2.

If
\phi
is open, then
\rho_{Y}
is open.

3.

If
\phi
is open and
\psi
is semi-open, then for every
W\in\mathscr{O}(\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi})
there are
U\in\mathscr{O}(X)
and
V\in\mathscr{O}(Y)
such that
\emptyset\not=(U\times V)\cap\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}\subseteq W
and
\phi[U]=\psi[V]
.

Proof.

(1): Let
W=(U\times U^{\prime})\cap\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
be a basic open set in
\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
. Since
\rho_{Y}
is semi-open, we may assume that
\rho_{Y}[W]=U^{\prime}
by shrinking
U^{\prime}
if necessary. Since
\psi
is semi-open,
V:=\textrm{int}\,\psi[U^{\prime}]\subseteq Z
is nonempty. Write
U_{1}^{\prime}=\psi^{-1}[V]\cap U^{\prime}
and
W_{1}=(U\times U_{1}^{\prime})\cap\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
that is non-void open in
\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
. Let
(x,y)\in W_{1}
. Let
x_{n}\to x
in
X
. Then
\phi(x_{n})\to\phi(x)=\psi(y)\in V
so that there are
y_{n}\in U_{1}^{\prime}
such that
(x_{n},y_{n})\in W_{1}
. Thus,
\rho_{X}
is semi-open.

(2): Let
(x,y)\in\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
and let
(U\times V)\cap\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
be a basic neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}
.
As
\phi
is open, we may assume
\phi[U]\in\mathfrak{N}_{\phi(x)}(Z)
. Since
\psi
is continuous and
\psi(y)=\phi(x)
, we can take
V^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{N}_{y}(Y)
with
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
such that
\psi[V^{\prime}]\subseteq\phi[U]
. Then
V^{\prime}\subseteq\rho_{Y}[(U\times V)\cap\mathrm{R}_{\phi\psi}]
. Thus,
\rho_{Y}
is open.

(3): It is straightforward and we omit the details. The proof is complete.
∎

3
A generalization of Pontryagin’s open-mapping theorem

In this section we shall consider some canonical semi-openness following from the theory of topological groups (Thm.
3.6
and Thm.
3.8
) and an application in topological groups (Thm.
3.13
).

3.6 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a right-topological group on a locally compact
\sigma
-compact Hausdorff space and
g\in G
. If
L_{g}\colon G\rightarrow G
,
x\mapsto gx
is continuous, then
L_{g}
is semi-open.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathscr{O}(G)
. Since
G
is locally compact regular, there is
V\in\mathscr{O}(G)
with
V\subseteq\bar{V}\subseteq U
such that
\bar{V}
is compact. As
G
is
\sigma
-compact, it follows that
G
has the Lindelöf property so
\{Vx\,|\,x\in G\}
has a countable subcover
\{Vx_{n}\,|\,n=1,2,\dotsc\}
of
G
. So

G={\bigcup}_{n}gVx_{n}={\bigcup}_{n}g\bar{V}x_{n}
.
However,
G
is a Baire space and
g\bar{V}x_{n}\subseteq gUx_{n}
is closed. So
\mathrm{int}\,gUx_{n}\not=\emptyset
for some
n\geq 1
. Thus,
\mathrm{int}\,gU\not=\emptyset
and
L_{g}
is a semi-open map. The proof is complete.
∎

3.7
.

A topological space
X
is called
quasi-regular
if for every
U\in\mathscr{O}(X)
, there exists
V\in\mathscr{O}(X)
such that
\bar{V}\subseteq U
(cf.
[
54
,
50
]
).

3.8 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a locally compact, Lindelöf, quasi-regular, left-topological group and
H
a Hausdorff Baire left-topological group. If
f\colon G\rightarrow H
is a continuous surjective homomorphism, then
f
is a semi-open mapping.

Proof.

Let
U\in\mathscr{O}(G)
. Since
G
is locally compact quasi-regular, there exists
V\in\mathscr{O}(G)
such that
\bar{V}\subseteq U
is compact. As
G
is a Lindelöf space, it follows that
G
has a countable open cover
\{x_{n}V\,|\,n=1,2,\dotsc\}
. Then
H=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(x_{n})f[\bar{V}]
. Since
H
is Hausdorff,
f[\bar{V}]
and then
f(x_{n})f[\bar{V}]
are compact and so closed for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now
\mathrm{int}_{H}f(x_{n})f[\bar{V}]\not=\emptyset
for some
n\geq 1
because
H
is Baire. Thus,
\mathrm{int}_{H}f[U]\supseteq\mathrm{int}_{H}f[\bar{V}]\not=\emptyset
and
f
is semi-open. The proof is complete.
∎

Notice here that if
G
is a topological group in Theorem
3.8
, then for
U\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(G)
we can take
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(G)
with
V=V^{-1}
and
V^{2}\subseteq U
. This implies that
f[U]\in\mathfrak{N}_{e}(H)
; and so,
f
is open (Pontryagin’s open-mapping theorem).

3.9
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a mapping (continuous or not) of topological spaces. By
D(f)
we denote the set of points of discontinuity of
f
.

1.

Following
[
41
,
33
]
,
f
is called
quasi-continuous at a point
x\in X
if to each
U\in\mathfrak{N}_{x}(X)
and
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{f(x)}(Z)
, there exists
W\in\mathscr{O}(U)
such that
f[W]\subseteq V
. We say that
f
is
quasi-continuous
, if
f
is quasi-continuous at every point of
X
.

2.

Following
[
46
, Def. 4]
,
f
is termed
semi-continuous
if
f^{-1}[V]
is semi-open in
Y
for every
V\in\mathscr{O}(Z)
. We say that
f
is
semi-continuous at a point
x\in X
if for each
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{f(x)}(Z)
, there exists a semi-open set
A\subseteq X
with
x\in A
such that
f[A]\subseteq V
. Clearly,
f
is semi-continuous if and only if it is semi-continuous at every point of
X
.
For example, if
L
is the Sorgenfrey line and
\mathbb{R}
the 1-dimensional euclidean space, then
\textrm{id}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow L
is an open noncontinuous and semi-continuous mapping.

However, semi-continuity
\Leftrightarrow
quasi-continuity, for any mapping between topological spaces by the following lemma:

3.10 Lemma
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a map between topological spaces. Then
f
is semi-continuous at a point
x\in X
if and only if it is quasi-continuous at
x
.

Proof.

Necessity: Let
U\in\mathfrak{N}_{x}(X)
and
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{f(x)}(Z)
. Then there is a semi-open set
A\subset X
with
x\in A
and
f[A]\subseteq V
. Since
U\cap\mathrm{int}\,A\not=\emptyset
and
f[U\cap\mathrm{int}\,A]\subseteq V
,
f
is quasi-continuous at
x
.

Sufficiency: Let
V\in\mathfrak{N}_{f(x)}(Z)
; then
x\in\overline{\mathrm{int}\,f^{-1}[V]}
. For otherwise, there is an open set
W
in
X
with
\emptyset\not=W\subseteq X\setminus\overline{\mathrm{int}\,f^{-1}[V]}
such that
f[W]\subseteq V
, and so
W\subseteq\mathrm{int}\,f^{-1}[V]
. Now, let
A=\{x\}\cup\mathrm{int}\,f^{-1}[V]
. Then
A
is semi-open with
x\in A
such that
f[A]\subseteq V
. Thus,
f
is semi-continuous at
x
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.11 Theorem

(cf.
[
46
, Thm. 13]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a semi-continuous mapping where
Z
has a countable base. Then
D(f)
is meager.

3.12 Theorem

(cf.
[
9
, Thm. 2]
)
.

If
\{f_{n}\colon X\rightarrow Y\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of semi-continuous maps from a space
X
into a pseudo-metric space
Y
such that
f_{n}\to f\in Y^{X}
pointwise, then
D(f)
is meager.

Notice that Theorem
3.12
is a generalization of a Baire category theorem (cf. Baire 1899
[
7
]
or Fort 1955
[
25
, Thm. 2]
, where each
f_{n}
is a real-valued continuous function). In addition, it should be mentioned that the limit
f
is not necessarily to be semi-continuous. For example, let
f_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by
f_{n}(x)=1
if
-1/n\leq x\leq 1/n
and
0
if others, and
f\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
f(x)=1
if
x=0
and
0
if others. Then
f_{n}\to f
pointwise but
f
is not semi-continuous (cf.
[
32
, Ex. 2]
).

3.13 Theorem
.

Let
G
be a right-topological group on a locally compact separable metric space. If
g\in G
such that

L_{g}\colon G\rightarrow G
,
x\mapsto gx
is continuous, then
L_{g^{-1}}\colon G\rightarrow G
is also continuous.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
and Lemma
2.1.1
-(3), it follows that
L_{g^{-1}}\colon G\rightarrow G
is semi-continuous. Thus,
D(L_{g^{-1}})\not=G
by Theorem
3.12
. Finally by the following CD

\begin{CD}G@>{L_{g^{-1}}}>{}>G\\
@V{R_{y}}V{}V@A{}A{R_{y^{-1}}}A\\
G@>{L_{g^{-1}}}>{}>G\end{CD}\quad\quad\forall y\in G,

it follows that
L_{g^{-1}}
is continuous on
G
. The proof is complete.
∎

4
Quasi-separable mappings and semi-openness of induced maps

Recently Dai and Xie in
[
16
, Thm. 10C]
have given a partial answer to Question
1.4
D using an additional condition “densely open”. Now we will present another natural condition (Def.
4.14
) and prove that under this condition a continuous map is semi-open if and only if its induced map is semi-open (Lem.
4.17

\&
Thm.
4.19
).

4.14
Quasi-separable maps.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous onto map between compact Hausdorff spaces. Then
f
is called
quasi-separable

if there exists a directed set
(\Lambda,\leq)
and an inverse system
\{f_{i}\colon X_{i}\rightarrow Y\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
of continuous onto maps between compact metric spaces,
where
\{X_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
is an inverse system with continuous onto link maps
X_{i}\xleftarrow{\pi_{i,j}}X_{j}
with
f_{j}=f_{i}\circ\pi_{i,j}
for
i<j
in
\Lambda
, such that
X=\underleftarrow{\lim}_{i\in\Lambda}\{X_{i}\}
and
f=f_{i}\circ p_{i}
(or written as
f=\underleftarrow{\lim}_{i\in\Lambda}\{f_{i}\}
), where
p_{i}\colon X\rightarrow X_{i}
is the canonical projection for all
i\in\Lambda
. In the special case
Y=\{pt\}
,
X
is referred to as a
quasi-separable
space. Note that a quasi-separable is not necessarily to be metrizable (by Lem.
4.18
).

4.15 Lemma
.

If
f=\underleftarrow{\lim}\{f_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
, then
f
is open (resp. semi-open) if and only if
f_{i}
is open (resp. semi-open) for all
i\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Necessity is obvious by
f=f_{i}\circ p_{i}
and each
p_{i}\colon X\rightarrow X_{i}
is a continuous onto map. Now, for sufficiency, let
U\in\mathscr{O}(X)
. By the structure of the topology of
X
, it follows that we can find some index
i\in\Lambda
such that
p_{i}[U]\in\mathscr{O}(X_{i})
. Then
f[U]=f_{i}[p_{i}[U]]
is open (resp. semi-open) in
Y
. The proof is complete.
∎

We shall show that
f
is semi-open if and only if
f_{*}
is semi-open in the quasi-separable case (Lem.
4.17
), and that every compact Hausdorff space is in fact quasi-separable (Lem.
4.18
). For that, we need Theorems
1.4
E and
1.4
F, Lemma
4.15
and another lemma (Lem.
4.16

If
\{X_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
is an inverse system of compact metric spaces, then
\mathcal{M}^{1}(\prod_{i\in\Lambda}X_{i})\not\cong\prod_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})
in general. In fact, for
\xi\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(X\times Y)
, if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
is the projection of
\xi
onto

\nu_{\cdot}\colon X\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)

\xi=\int\nu_{x}d\mu(x)

\nu_{x}\not\equiv\nu\ \forall x\in X

4.16 Lemma
.

If
\{X_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
is an inverse system of compact Hausdorff spaces, then we have that
\mathcal{M}^{1}\left(\underleftarrow{\lim}_{i\in\Lambda}\{X_{i}\}\right)\cong\underleftarrow{\lim}_{i\in\Lambda}\left\{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\right\}
.

Proof.

Write
X_{i}\xleftarrow{\pi_{i,j}}X_{j}
and
\pi_{i,i}=\textsl{id}_{X_{i}}
, for
i<j
in
\Lambda
, for the link maps of the inverse system
\{X_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
. Let
X=\underleftarrow{\lim}\{X_{i}\}
. Let
p_{i}\colon X\rightarrow X_{i}
and
p_{i*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})
be the canonical maps. As
p_{i}=\pi_{i,j}\circ p_{j}
for
i<j
in
\Lambda
, it follows that
p_{i*}=\pi_{i,j*}\circ p_{j*}
so that
\{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
is an inverse system.
Moreover,

\pi\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)\rightarrow\underleftarrow{\lim}\{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\}
,
\mu\mapsto(p_{i*}(\mu))_{i\in\Lambda}
,
is a continuous onto map. In fact, for all
(\mu_{i})_{i\in\Lambda}\in\underleftarrow{\lim}\{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\}
, by the finite-intersection property of compact space we may take a probability
\mu\in\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}{p_{i}}_{*}^{-1}(\mu_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
; then
\pi(\mu)=(\mu_{i})_{i\in\Lambda}
.
Now let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
such that
p_{i*}(\lambda)=:\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}:=p_{i*}(\mu)
for all
i\in\Lambda
. We need prove that
\lambda=\mu
. By regularity of
\lambda
and
\mu
, it is enough to show that
\lambda(K)=\mu(K)
for all
K\in 2^{X}
. Let
K\in 2^{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then we can take a set
U\in\mathscr{O}(X)
such that
\lambda(U\setminus K)+\mu(U\setminus K)<\varepsilon
and
K\subset U
. In addition, as
i\in\Lambda
sufficiently big, we can choose finitely many open sets, say
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{\ell}
in
X_{i}
such that
K\subseteq p_{i}^{-1}(V_{1})\cup\dotsm\cup p_{i}^{-1}(V_{\ell})\subseteq U
. So by the inclusion-exclusion formula of probability or by the equality
p_{i}^{-1}(V_{1})\cup\dotsm\cup p_{i}^{-1}(V_{\ell})=p_{i}^{-1}(V_{1}\cup\dotsm\cup V_{\ell})
, it follows that

\lambda(K)\leq\lambda(p_{i}^{-1}(V_{1})\cup\dotsm\cup p_{i}^{-1}(V_{\ell}))=\mu(p_{i}^{-1}(V_{1})\cup\dotsm\cup p_{i}^{-1}(V_{\ell}))\leq\mu(K)+\varepsilon.

Thus,
\lambda(K)\leq\mu(K)
; and analogously,
\mu(K)\leq\lambda(K)
. Then
\pi
is 1-1 onto and
\pi
is a homeomorphism. The proof is complete.
∎

4.17 Lemma
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous onto map between compact Hausdorff spaces. If
f
is quasi-separable, then
f
is semi-open if and only if
f_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
is semi-open.

Proof.

Sufficiency is obvious by Theorem
1.4
F. Now, for necessity, let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be the inverse limit of an inverse system

\{f_{i}\colon X_{i}\rightarrow Y\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}

of continuous onto maps with
X_{i}
compact metrizable. Let
p_{i}\colon X\rightarrow X_{i}
be the canonical map. Then
f=f_{i}\circ p_{i}
for all
i\in\Lambda
.
Thus,
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\rightarrow Y
is semi-open by Lemma
4.15
. We have then concluded a CD of continuous onto maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underleftarrow{\lim}\{\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{p_{i}}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{p_{j}}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{i}}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{i,j}}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{j}}_{*}}

So by Theorem
1.4
E, it follows that
{f_{i}}_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(X_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
is semi-open for all
i\in\Lambda
so that by Lemmas
4.15
and
4.16

∎

4.18 Lemma
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous onto map, where
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
Y
a metric space. Then
f
is quasi-separable.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma(X)
be the collection of continuous pseudo-metrics on
X
. Let
\varrho
be the metric on
Y
. Define a partial order on
\Sigma(X)
as follows: for any
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\Sigma(X)
,
\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}
iff
\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq\rho^{\prime}(x,x^{\prime})
for all
(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X
. If
2\leq n<\infty
and
\rho_{1},\dotsc,\rho_{n}\in\Sigma(X)
, then
\rho:=\max\{\rho_{1},\dotsc,\rho_{n}\}\in\Sigma(X)
such that
\rho_{i}\leq\rho
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus,
(\Sigma(X),\leq)
is a directed set. Now, for every
\rho\in\Sigma(X)
, define a relation on
X
as follows:

R_{\rho}=\left\{(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X\,|\,\varrho(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))+\rho(x,x^{\prime})=0\right\}.

Clearly,
R_{\rho}\subseteq R_{ff}
is a closed equivalence relation on
X
. We set
X_{\rho}=X/R_{\rho}
, which is a compact metrizable space. Let
\lambda_{\rho}\colon X\rightarrow X_{\rho}
and
f_{\rho}\colon X_{\rho}\rightarrow Y
be the canonical maps. Then
f=f_{\rho}\circ\lambda_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(X)
, and
R_{\rho}\supseteq R_{\rho^{\prime}}
so that there exists a canonical link map
X_{\rho}\xleftarrow{\pi_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}}X_{\rho^{\prime}}
if
\rho\leq\rho^{\prime}
in
\Sigma(X)
. Thus,
\{f_{\rho}\colon X_{\rho}\rightarrow Y\,|\,\rho\in\Sigma(X)\}
is an inverse system of continuous surjections.
As
X=\underleftarrow{\lim}\{X_{\rho}\}
, it follows that
f
is quasi-separable.
∎

Let

By Lemma
4.18
and Lemma
4.17
.

5.20

From now on, let

Let

If every point of

5.22
.

Let

1.

In fact,

Proof.

First, if
t_{n}\to t
in

t_{n}x\to tx

\mathrm{C}_{2}

Then the following are pairwise equivalent:

(

[

Proof.

Let

R\!P(\mathscr{X})=\{(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X\,|\,\overline{S[U\times V]}\cap\Delta_{X}\not=\emptyset\ \forall\,U\times V\in\mathfrak{N}_{(x,x^{\prime})}(X\times X)\}

Then, under condition

(cf.

]

R\!P[x]={\bigcap}_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{U}_{X}}\overline{S^{-1}[\varepsilon[x]]}\quad\textrm{and}\quad P[x]={\bigcap}_{\varepsilon\in\mathscr{U}_{X}}S^{-1}[\varepsilon[x]]

. Let

(1)\Rightarrow(2)

(2)\Rightarrow(4)

5.30 Theorem
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a pointwise a.p., weakly mixing, quasi-separable, non-trivial flow with strongly amenable phase group. Then:

1.

is not an equivalence relation (cf.
[

for
\mathscr{X}
minimal
\&

S
abelian).

2.

There exists no closed proximal cell for
\mathscr{X}
. (Consequently,
\mathscr{X}
is not point distal.)

Proof.

First
\mathscr{X}
is minimal by Lemma
5.26
. Since
\mathscr{X}
is quasi-separable, there exists an inverse system
\{\mathscr{X}_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
of minimal metrizable flows such that
\mathscr{X}=\underleftarrow{\lim}\{\mathscr{X}_{i}\}
. Let
p_{i}\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}_{i}
be the canonical map for all
i\in\Lambda
. Then
\mathscr{X}_{i}
is a weak-mixing minimal metrizable
B
-flow for all
i\in\Lambda
.

(1): To prove that
P(\mathscr{X})
is not an equivalence relation, suppose to the contrary that
P(\mathscr{X})
is an equivalence relation. Then
P(\mathscr{X}_{i})=p_{i}\times p_{i}[P(\mathscr{X})]
is also an equivalence relation. Thus, by
[
52
, Cor. 2.14]
, it follows that
X_{i}=P\circ P[x_{i}]=P[x_{i}]
for all
x_{i}\in X_{i}
and all

. (In fact, for

and

are residual in

, so

is proximal so that

for all

, for

is strongly amenable. This shows that

is a trivial flow, contrary to that

(2): Suppose to the contrary that

. Then it is easy to verify that

is closed in

for all

, where

. By Theorem
5.29
, it follows that

for all

is proximal and

∎

5.31 Remark
.

The “strongly amenable” condition is crucial in Theorem
5.30
. For example, let
S=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
the topological group of real
2\times 2
matrices with determinant
1
. Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the projective line, i.e., the set of lines through the origin of the plane.
S
acts naturally on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(sending lines into lines), and this action is A.T. Then
\mathscr{X}
is minimal proximal (then not a B-flow) (see
[
26
, II.5.6]
), and
\mathscr{X}
is weakly mixing (see
[
26
, Cor. II.2.2]
or Lem.
5.28
).
Moreover,
\mathscr{X}
admits no invariant Borel probability measures. Otherwise, suppose
\mu
be an invariant Borel probability measure; as
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
includes the rotations, it follows that

is the Lebesque measure; this contradicts the proximality. Thus, neither
\mathrm{C}_{1}
nor
\mathrm{C}_{2}
is a necessary condition for Theorem

.

6.32 Lemma
.

Let
Y
be a compact Hausdorff space,
\nu_{j}\to\nu
in
\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
, and
f\colon Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a bounded measurable function such that
f
is continuous at each point of
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
. Then
\nu_{j}(f)\to\nu(f)
.

Proof.

First for
\delta\in\mathscr{U}_{Y}
,
\liminf\nu_{j}(\delta[\mathrm{supp}(\nu)])\to\nu(\delta[\mathrm{supp}(\nu)])=1
. Then by Urysohn lemma, to each
\varepsilon>0
there exists an index
\delta\in\mathscr{U}_{Y}
and a function
f_{\varepsilon}\in C(Y)
with
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|\leq\|f\|
such that
|(f_{\varepsilon}-f)|_{\delta[\mathrm{supp}(\nu)]}|<\varepsilon/3
and
f_{\varepsilon}|_{\mathrm{supp}(\nu)}=f|_{\mathrm{supp}(\nu)}
. Then

|\nu_{j}(f)-\nu(f)|\leq|\nu_{j}(f)-\nu_{j}(f_{\varepsilon})|+|\nu_{j}(f_{\varepsilon})-\nu(f_{\varepsilon})|+|\nu(f_{\varepsilon})-\nu(f)|<\varepsilon

eventually. Thus,
\nu_{j}(f)\to\nu(f)
. The proof is complete.
∎

6.33 Corollary

(A special case of
[
14
, Prop. 2.1]
)
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be an extension of
S
-flows (not necessarily minimal). If
\phi\colon X\rightarrow Y
is almost 1-1, then
\phi
and
\phi_{*}
both are semi-open extensions.

Proof.

Set
Y_{o}=\phi[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
, which is dense in
Y
for
\phi
is a continuous onto map. Then,
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{o}]
and
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[Y_{o}]
are dense in
\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
and
\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
, respectively.

(1): It is obvious that
\phi
is semi-open, for
X_{1-1}[\phi]
is dense in
X
.

(2): To prove that
\phi_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{1}(\mathscr{Y})
is semi-open, let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
be a closed set with non-empty interior. We need show that
\mathrm{int}\,\phi_{*}(\mathcal{U})\not=\emptyset
. Suppose to the contrary that
\mathrm{int}\,\phi_{*}(\mathcal{U})=\emptyset
. We choose a measure
\mu_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathcal{U}
with
x_{i}\in X_{1-1}[\phi]
,
0<c_{i}\leq 1
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}=1
.
Set
\nu_{0}=\phi_{*}(\mu_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
where
y_{i}=\phi(x_{i})\in Y_{o}
.
We can choose a net
\nu_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}}c_{j,i}\delta_{y_{j,i}}
in
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[Y_{o}]\setminus\phi_{*}(\mathcal{U})
such that
\nu_{j}\to\nu_{0}
.
Take points
x_{j,i}\in X_{1-1}[\phi]
with
\phi(x_{j,i})=y_{j,i}
, and set
\mu_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}}c_{j,i}\delta_{x_{j,i}}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
. Then
\phi_{*}(\mu_{j})=\nu_{j}
and we may assume (a subnet of)
\mu_{j}\to\mu_{0}^{\prime}
.

If
\mu_{0}^{\prime}=\mu_{0}
, then
\mu_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
eventually so that
\nu_{j}\in\phi_{*}(\mathcal{U})
eventually, contrary to our choice of
\nu_{j}
. Thus,
\mu_{0}^{\prime}\not=\mu_{0}
. Let
f\in C(X)
. Then
\mu_{0}^{\prime}(f)=\lim_{j}\mu_{j}(f)
. On the other hand, associated with
f
we can define a function

f^{*}\colon Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
y\mapsto\max\{f(x)\,|\,x\in\phi^{-1}(y)\}
,
with
f^{*}\circ\phi|_{X_{1-1}[\phi]}=f|_{X_{1-1}[\phi]}
.
Clearly,

\mu_{j}(f)=\nu_{j}(f^{*})\to\nu_{0}(f^{*})=\mu_{0}(f)
by Lemma
6.32
.
Thus,
\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
Then
\phi_{*}
is semi-open. The proof is complete.
∎

6.34 Lemma
.

Let
\phi\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous onto map between compact Hausdorff spaces. If

X_{1-1}[\phi]=\{x\in X\,|\,\phi^{-1}(\phi(x))=\{x\}\}

is dense in
X
, then
\phi
and
\phi_{*}
are semi-open such that
\phi_{*}
is 1-1 at each point of
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\phi
is semi-open. Now in order to prove that
\phi_{*}
is semi-open, since
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
is dense in
\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
, it is enough to prove that
\phi_{*}
is 1-1 at each point of
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
.
Note that if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(X)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
with
\nu=\phi_{*}(\mu)
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq\phi^{-1}[\mathrm{supp}(\nu)]
.
Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
in
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
and
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
in
\mathcal{M}^{1}(Y)
where
y_{i}=\phi(x_{i})
. Then
\phi_{*}(\mu)=\nu
; and moreover,
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)=\{y_{1},\dotsc,y_{m}\}
. This implies that
\phi_{*}^{-1}(\nu)=\{\mu\}
. Thus,
\phi_{*}
is 1-1 at each point of
\mathrm{co}\,\delta[X_{1-1}[\phi]]
. The proof is complete.
∎

6.35
Quasi-almost 1-1 maps.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a continuous onto map between compact Hausdorff spaces.
Then
f
is termed a
quasi-almost 1-1 map
if there exists an inverse system of continuous onto maps, say
\left\{X_{i}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}Y\,|\,i\in\Lambda\right\}
, such that:

i)

\{X_{i}\,|\,i\in\Lambda\}
is an inverse system of compact Hausdorff spaces with
X=\underleftarrow{\lim}_{i\in\Lambda}\{X_{i}\}
;

ii)

f=f_{i}\circ\rho_{i}
for all
i\in\Lambda
, where
\rho_{i}\colon X\rightarrow X_{i}
is the canonical projection;

iii)

for all
i\in\Lambda
,
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\rightarrow Y
is almost 1-1.

Similarly, we can define the “quasi-almost 1-1 extensions” of minimal flows.

It should be mentioned that a quasi-almost 1-1 extension of minimal flows need generally not be an almost 1-1 extension; for in the case
\Lambda
uncountable we are not able to find a point
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\Lambda}\in X
such that
x_{i}\in X_{i,1-1}[f_{i}]
for all
i\in\Lambda
. That is just the reason why there exists no “universal a.a. flow” for non-abelian group
S
in general, where a flow is said to be a.a. (almost automorphic) if it is a minimal almost 1-1 extension of a minimal equicontinuous flow.

6.36 Theorem
.

If
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a continuous quasi-almost 1-1 onto map between compact Hausdorff spaces, then
f
and
f_{*}
both are semi-open maps.

Proof.

By Lemmas
6.32
and
6.34
and Lemma
4.15
.
∎

) that is a quasi-almost 1-1 extension of minimal

6.37
Ellis’ two-circle minimal flow

Let

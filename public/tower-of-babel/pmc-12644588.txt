Prognostic value assessment and in vitro validation of mitochondria-ferroptosis-related genes in multiple myeloma
Authors: Jing Wu, Chen Li, Jiayou Zhang, Jing Su, Yuting Xiao, Linhua Ji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMultiple myeloma (MM) is a malignant plasma cell tumor. Mitochondria-related genes (MRGs) are significant inducers of ferroptosis. However, research on the involvement of mitochondria-ferroptosis-related genes (MFRGs) in the MM is relatively scarce, and further identification of key genes associated with MFRGs in MM treatment is needed. In this study, transcriptomic data and related genes were initially downloaded from public databases and literature. Subsequently, candidate genes were obtained by intersecting the differential expression genes (DEGs) from MM and control groups, and MFRGs. Through regression analyses, clinically relevant genomic signatures were delineated, culminating in the development of a predictive algorithm. An independent prognostic analysis was conducted, followed by the creation of a nomogram. Subsequently, these prognostic genes were studied from various aspects. Finally, the transcriptional abundance of predictive biomarkers was experimentally validated through reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The intersection of DEGs and MFRGs yielded 15 candidate genes. Then, GPR15, NLRP7, ZNF208, PRDM13, and CRIM1 were identified as prognostic genes. The prognostic model constructed using these prognostic genes was confirmed to be robust. The 2 independent prognostic factors (risk score and age) were determined, and the constructed nomogram provided an excellent predictive model. Then, risk score and activated dendritic cells were found to be significantly negatively correlated (cor = -0.43, p < 0.05). Additionally, GPR15 was positively associated with M2 macrophages (cor = 0.34, p < 0.05), while NLRP7 and PRDM13 were negatively associated with activated dendritic cells (cor = -0.34, p < 0.05; cor = -0.40, p < 0.05). There were 3 significantly different immune cells, 31 significantly immune checkpoint genes, and 11 significantly different immune checkpoints (p < 0.05). ZNF208, PRDM13, and CPIM1 were all mainly enriched in ribosome-related pathways. Finally, 86 potential drugs for the treatment of MM were discovered, such as shikonin. RT-qPCR results showed that NLRP7 and PRDM13 were significantly upregulated in MM group, while GPR15 and CRIM1 were significantly downregulated in MM group (p < 0.05). In this study, 5 MFRGs were identified as prognostic genes (GPR15, NLRP7, ZNF208, PRDM13, and CRIM1) for MM, which provide reference significance for the prognosis of MM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25476-5.Keywords: Multiple myeloma, Mitochondria, Ferroptosis, Prognostic genes, Prognostic modelSubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is the second most common of all hematological malignancy1, with main symptoms such as hypercalcemia, renal impairment, anemia, bone destruction, etc1. It usually starts from asymptomatic precursor diseases including monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance, which is difficult to detect in the early stages. The current treatment for MM is mainly chemotherapy, Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-Cell immunotherapy(CAR-T) and autologous stem cell transplantation1. For the past few years, the advent of Bortezomib and Ixazomib, Pomalidomide and lenalidomide, and daratumumab has greatly improved the survival rate of patients. However, despite these advances, In general, prognosis of MM patients remains poor, and most patients will eventually die1. Therefore, we urgently need to explore new prognostic factors, find suitable prognostic genes, and explore the molecular mechanisms of MM, which will help improve the accuracy and effect of treatment and improve the prognosis of MM patients.

Ferroptosis is a newly identified non-programmed necrotic process that belongs to the iron-dependent regulated cell death mode. When the intracellular lipid reactive oxygen species (L-ROS) concentration exceeds the antioxidant capacity threshold of glutathione-dependent peroxidase (GPX4), the cellular redox homeostasis will be disrupted2. Ferroptosis is associated with a variety of human diseases, including hematological tumors, ischemic organ damage, neurodegeneration, and solid tumors3–6. The cell “destruction” mechanism caused by iron poisoning is different from the active “suicide” mechanism. It is caused by metabolic imbalance rather than actively triggered by pro-death molecules7–9. It is worth noting that in the field of tumor treatment, this metabolic disorder can enhance the clearance efficiency of malignant cells, suggesting that ferroptosis may have a potential tumor suppressor effect10. Therefore, regulating ferroptosis may become a potential treatment for more stubborn drug-resistant cells. Mitochondria are the “energy factories” in cells and are essential for cell growth. They are important inducers of apoptosis, including necroptosis, ferroptosis, and pyroptosis11. As the main regulator of oxidative phosphorylation, mitochondria are the main producers of reactive oxygen species in cells12. The iron content in mitochondria is very high. The iron in mitochondria is mainly involved in the synthesis of iron-sulfur (Fe-S) clusters and hemoglobin13. In addition, the redox iron pool is also involved in the accumulation of mitochondrial ROS (mitoROS)14,15. In the tumor cells, the role of mitoROS as second messenger in oncogenic signaling16. Following the accrual of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mitoROS), these oxidants engage in redox reactions with polyunsaturated lipid species localized on mitochondrial membranes, initiating oxidative lipid degradation and inducing genomic instability in mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA). This cascade ultimately culminates in functional impairments of electron transport chain complexes through compromised mtDNA-encoded structural components17. A series of studies have shown that mitochondrial metabolic activities, including the (Tricarboxylic Acid Cycle) TCA cycle, produce sufficient L-ROS, which is a necessary condition for initiating ferroptosis.Although the separate mechanisms of action of mitochondrial function and ferroptosis in tumors have been partially studied, the potential synergistic mechanism of the two in MM is still unclear.

Studies have shown that MM cells can transfer mitochondria from nearby bone marrow stromal cells to themselves through tumor-derived tunnel nanotubes18, and achieve rapid proliferation of MM cells by enhancing mitochondrial metabolism or function. Evidence derived from neoplastic cellular models, murine xenograft systems, and clinical oncology specimens consistently demonstrates a robust inverse association between augmented mitochondrial bioenergetic activity and therapeutic resistance to ubiquitin-proteasome system inhibitors19,20. Therefore, argeting mitochondrial energy metabolism may offer a solution to proteasome inhibitor resistance, providing new opportunities for the treatment of MM. At present, the relationship between iron oxidation and MM due to mitochondrial changes has been relatively little studied. Therefore, in-depth study of the role of mitochondrial ferroptosis in multiple myeloma will help discover new therapeutic targets.

RNA-Seq is a transcriptome analysis method that uses deep sequencing technology. This method allows researchers to observe gene functions and regulatory networks from different perspectives, revealing complex molecular interactions and signal transduction pathways21. This method can deeply explore the comprehensive information of biological problems from phenotype to molecular mechanism, and provide new perspectives on basic biology and disease mechanisms21–23. In recent years, RNA-Seq technology has been extensively employed in various kinds of biological research fields, encompassing transcriptomic profiling, transcript structure research, non-coding RNA function research, etc24. In tumor research, RNA-Seq helps to identify tumor-related gene expression changes, including differentially expressed genes in different types of tumors, which may be related to tumor occurrence, development, metastasis and drug resistance25.Through RNA-Seq, researchers can fully understand the gene expression profile and regulatory network of MM cells, thereby revealing their pathogenesis and drug resistance mechanisms. In addition, by deeply analyzing the transcriptome data of MM cells, studying the role of ferroptosis caused by mitochondrial changes in MM, better understanding its complex biological characteristics, and providing new targets and methods for clinical treatment.

This study was based on transcriptomic data from public databases, and used univariate Cox proportional hazards modeling (Cox) and (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator) LASSO regularization to delineate prognostic genes associated with MM. These prognostic genes were then used to develop a multivariate risk stratification framework, and the reliability of the model was evaluated. Independent prognostic factors were then identified. Subsequent studies using these prognostic genes confirmed their significance. The prognostic genes associated with MM identified in this study provide reference for the medical treatment of MM.

Materials and methods

Data collection

UCSC database was applied to download the MMRF-CoMMpass cohort, which included gene expression profiles, clinical information, and survival information of 858 multiple myeloma (MM) (training set) (access time: 2024-07-21). Subsequently, Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database was applied to download the MM-related transcriptome dataset (GSE47552 and GSE57317). GSE47552 (platform: GPL6244, training set 2) consisted of tumour tissue from 41 MM samples and 5 control bone marrow tissue samples. On the other hand, GSE57317 (platform: GPL570) was used as the validation cohort, consisting of 55 tumor CD138-positive cell samples with survival information.Additionally, in MitICCarta3.0 database, we identified 1,136 mitochondria-related genes (MRGs) (Supplementary Table 1). In FerrDb database, 259 ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) were gained (Supplementary Table 2). The acquisition time for all the above data was July 21, 2024.

Acquisition of candidate genes

By using limma package (v 1.38.0)26, we conducted differential expression analysis between the MM and control groups (|log2 fold change (FC)| > 0.5, adj.p < 0.05) with Benjamini-Hochberg (BH), yielding differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Subsequently, ggplot2 (v 3.4.1)27 and ComplexHeatmap (v 2.18.0)28 were applied to visualize the results by plotting a volcano plot, with the top 10 upregulated and downregulated genes (a total of 20 genes) highlighted and heatmap for the top genes. Concurrently, the intersection of MRGs and FRGs was taken to obtain mitochondria-ferroptosis-related genes (MFRGs). Single Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) is a technique used to analyze gene expression data. To further identify MFRGs in MM, the GSVA package (v1.38.2)29 was utilized to calculate ssGSEA scores in MMRF-CoMMpass. Subsequently, MFRGs scores were then compared (p < 0.05). As a result, all samples in MMRF-CoMMpass were divided into 2 groups (high scoring vs. low scoring). The survival package (v 3.5-3.5)30 was then used to plot the Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curves to evaluate survival differences between the 2 groups (p < 0.05). Following this, DESeq2 package (v 1.34.0)31 was applied to obtain the differentially expressed MFRGs (|log2FC| > 0.5, p < 0.05), referred to as mitochondria-ferroptosis-differentially expressed genes (MF-DEGs). The intersection of DEGs and MF-DEGs was obtained using the ggVenn package (v 1.2.2)32, yielding candidate genes.

Enrichment analysis and protein interaction of candidate genes

Then, by using clusterProfiler package (v 4.4.4)33, potential biological functions and pathways on candidate targets were elucidated through Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)34–36 analyses (adj. p < 0.05) with Benjamini-Hochberg (BH). In order to investigate the interaction relationships of candidate genes at the protein level, STRING database was employed to build the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of candidate genes (confidence > 0.15), the result was then presented by Cytoscape software (v 3.1.1)37.

Identification of prognostic genes

To identify candidate genes associated with MM prognosis, samples from the MMRF-CoMMpass were utilized. By using the survival package, univariate Cox regression analysis (hazard ratio (HR) ≠ 1, p < 0.05) was performed on candidate genes, and the results was subjected to proportional hazards (PH) assumption tests (p > 0.05). The forestplot package (v 2.0.1)38 was employed to visualize the results in a forest plot. Following this, the glmnet package (v 4.1.4)39 was employed by selecting the Cox type and applying 10-fold cross-validation, LASSO regression analysis was performed on the prognosis-related genes obtained from the univariate Cox analysis. The final set of prognostic genes was determined by calculating the optimal lambda value.

Construction and validation of prognostic model

We subsequently calculated the risk score for MM using the formula to construct a risk model:

The coefficients (coef) were obtained as weights from the LASSO machine learning model. These weights represented contribution of each gene expression level to the overall risk score. The expression (expr) indicated the expression level of the i-th gene. Subsequently, MM patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups using optimal cutoff value of the risk score. The optimal cutoff was determined using the surv_cutpoint function based on the maximum selected rank statistics. Specifically, all possible values of the risk score were iterated. For each value considered as a cutoff, the survival difference between the high-risk and low-risk groups was calculated using the log-rank test. The value that yielded the most significant survival difference between the two groups (with the smallest P-value from the log-rank test) was selected as the optimal cutoff. Next, survminer package (v 0.4.9)40 was then utilized to plot risk curve and survival status plot to analyze the distribution of MM patients in different datasets as a whole. Additionally, expression levels of prognostic genes in MMRF-CoMMpass were displayed using a heatmap, and the overall survival (OS) time between 2 groups was assessed through K-M survival analysis (p < 0.05). By using the survival ROC package (v 1.0.3)41, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (1/2/3 years) was visualized to evaluate diagnostic value of the model (area under the curve (AUC) > 0.7). Then, the prognostic model was validated in GSE57317.

Independent prognostic analysis

To validate the prognostic utility of the risk stratification index as an independent predictor in multiple MM through multivariable Cox regression modeling, risk score and clinical characteristics, including age, race, gender, and stage, were incorporated to identify independent prognostic factors through univariate Cox analysis (HR ≠ 1, p > 0.05), PH assumption tests (p > 0.05) and multivariate Cox analysis (HR ≠ 1, p > 0.05). Subsequently, a nomogram was constructed for the independent prognostic factors in MMRF-CoMMpass using rms package (v 6.8-1.8)42to forecast the 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival probabilities of MM patients. The constructed nomogram model was then evaluated through calibration curves and ROC analysis.

Immune infiltration and immune checkpoint and m6A analysis

To delineate the distribution of immunological infiltrates across the high- and low-risk groups, Next, this study utilized Monte Carlo sampling on disease samples from MMRF-CoMM, removing samples with p > 0.05. The remaining samples were subjected to the cell-type identification. As a result, the infiltration enrichment scores of 22 immune cells43 was calculated through CIBERSORT algorithm44, with the results presented in stacked plot. Then, the differences in immune cell infiltration levels were compared via the Wilcoxon rank sum test (p < 0.05). Furthermore, Spearman correlation analysis(|correlation (cor)| > 0.3, p < 0.05) was used to evaluate the correlations between risk scores and differential immune cells, as well as between prognostic genes and differential immune cells.At the same time, The expression differences of 47 immune checkpoints45, and 15 N6-methyladenosine (m6A) related genes46 across two groups were then evaluated via the Wilcoxon rank sum test (p < 0.05) with false discovery rate (FDR).

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

In order to explore the biological processes in which prognostic genes are significantly enriched in MM, within the MMRF - CoMMpass dataset, Spearman correlation analysis was carried out between each prognostic gene and the remaining genes by means of the psych package (v 2.4.3)47.Subsequently, the genes were ranked according to the correlation coefficients. Based on the ranking results and by using clusterProfiler package (v 4.4.4), the gene set “c2.cp.kegg.v7.5.1.symbols.gm” from MSigDB was used as the reference gene set, followed by GSEA analysis (|normalized enrichment scpre (NES)| > 1, adj. p < 0.05) with false discovery rate (FDR).

Construction of related molecular networks

Subsequently, GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/) was utilized to predict genes associated with prognostic genes and the pathways in which they participate. Concurrently, transcription factors targeting prognostic genes were obtained from miRNet database (https://www.mirnet.ca/) and subjected to network analysis, with the TF-mRNA network visualized using Cytoscape.

Drug sensitivity analysis

To assess the relationship between chemotherapy response and the high - and low - risk groups, chemotherapeutic and targeted therapy drugs used for the treatment of MM were retrieved from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/).In MMRF-CoMMpass, by using pRRophetic package (v 0.5)48, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) for each MM patient’s response to chemotherapeutic/targeted therapy drugs was predicted, thereby inferring drug sensitivity. Box plots were constructed using ggplot2 package to display drugs between the 2 groups with significant differences (p < 0.05) with false discovery rate (FDR).

RT-qPCR experiment validation

In this investigation, peripheral blood specimens from MM patients (n = 5) and age-matched healthy donors (n = 5) were prospectively obtained from Department of Hematology, the Second Affiliated Hospital, Chengdu Medical College(Nuclear Industry 416 Hospital). Total RNA was isolated from whole blood using Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific), followed by reverse transcription into cDNA with the SureScript First-strand cDNA Synthesis PRDM13 Kit (Servicebio). RT-qPCR was performed using 2×Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Servicebio). Gene-specific primers (Supplementary Table 3), including the endogenous control GAPDH, were commercially synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Statistical analyses and data visualization were executed using GraphPad Prism (v8.0.2)49.

Statistical analysis

Bioinformatics processing was implemented in the R statistical environment (v4.2.2). Comparative analysis of ssGSEA score distributions between cohorts employed the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, while survival probability differences were determined through log-rank methodology (significance threshold: p < 0.05). Intergroup disparities in RT-qPCR-derived expression levels were quantified using Student’s t-test.

Results

Acquisition of 15 candidate genes

In order to screen for differentially expressed genes(DEGs) associated with multiple myeloma(MM), we firstly used GSE47552 dataset for differential expression analysis, 2,349 DEGs were obtained, including 656 upregulated and 1,693 downregulated genes in MM group (Fig. 1A). Additionally, a heatmap constructed with the top genes demonstrated that these genes could effectively distinguish between the MM and control groups (Fig. 1B). Concurrently, the intersection of MRGs and FRGs yielded 22 MFRGs (Fig. 1C). Subsequently, the ssGSEA scores calculated with MFRGs categorized the MM samples in the training set into 2 scoring groups, with the high scoring group exhibiting lower survival rates (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1D). Based on the ssGSEA scores, 623 MF-DEGs were identified as differentially expressed, with 172 upregulated and 451 downregulated (Fig. 1E). Finally, the intersection of DEGs with MF-DEGs resulted in 15 candidate genes (Fig. 1F).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the candidate genes. The GO analysis conducted on 15 candidate genes enriched only 18 items of biological process (BP), such as stress response to metal ion (adj.p < 0.05) (Fig. 2A). Additionally, a total of 3 KEGG pathways were enriched, including mineral absorption, virion-ebolavirus, lyssavirus and morbillivirus, and non-homologous end-joining (Fig. 2B). The network consisted of 8 nodes and 4 edges, with interacting relationships observed between IFIT3 and IFIT2, NCAM2 and APOD, MT1G and MT1A, and FGF5 and DNTT (Fig. 2C).

Identification of prognostic genes

Next, the univariate Cox regression analysis was used to assess the prognostic value of the candidate genes, 6 genes associated with survival were identified (GPR15, NLRP7, ZNF208, MT1A, PRDM13, and CRIM1) (Fig. 3A, B). Subsequently, these 6 genes were subjected to LASSO regression analysis. At the lambda.min of 0.0284, 5 prognostic genes were determined (GPR15, NLRP7, ZNF208, PRDM13, and CRIM1) (Fig. 3C, D). This analysis process provides the most streamlined gene combination for subsequent construction of a multi-gene risk scoring model.

To assess the independent prognostic value of risk scores and clinical characteristics, we conducted the regression analyses, identifying age, and risk score as independent prognostic factors (Fig. 6A-C). Subsequently, the results of nomogram revealed that risk score was the greatest contributor to patient survival, followed by age (Fig. 6D). The calibration curve showed that the slopes of the nomogram-predicted survival probabilities for different years were close to 1 (Fig. 6E), and the AUC values were all greater than 0.6 (1/2/3 years) (Fig. 6F), indicating that the nomogram had good predictive performance.

To investigate the relationship between the immune microenvironment and prognostic genes, we analyzed the differences in immune infiltrating cells between the high-risk and low-risk groups. The stacked plot and box plot results showed that in 2 group, infiltration level of activated dendritic cells, M2 macrophages, and resting mast cells were meaningfully different (p < 0.05) (Fig. 7A, B). The correlation heatmap revealed a negative correlation between the risk score and activated dendritic cells (cor = −0.43, p < 0.05), a positive correlation between GPR15 and M2 macrophages (cor = 0.34, p < 0.05), and negative correlations between NLRP7, PRDM13 and activated dendritic cells (cor = −0.34, p < 0.05; cor = −0.40, p < 0.05) (Fig. 7C, D). Subsequent analyses of immune checkpoints and m6A indicated that 31 immune checkpoint genes, such as TNFRSF14, CD48, and TNFRSF8 (Fig. 7E), and 11 m6A-related genes exhibited significant differences between 2 groups, such as HNRNPA2B1, IGF2BP2, and RBM15 (p < 0.05) (Fig. 7F).

Discussion

MM represents an incurable B-lymphocytic malignancy defined by monoclonal proliferation of clonotypic plasma cells within the medullary microenvironment, demonstrating marked clonal diversity and molecular heterogeneity across patient cohorts1. With the development of new types of drugs, the current treatment effect of MM has been significantly improved1. Its prognosis is closely related to various biological processes such as bone marrow microenvironment, viral infection, and cell metabolism1. Therefore, this study deeply explored the role of mitochondrial-ferroptosis genes in multiple myeloma, identified 5 key genes related to patient prognosis, and verified their clinical effects through independent prognostic analysis, immune infiltration and immune checkpoint analysis, m6A analysis, ssGSEA and drug sensitivity prediction by RT-qPCR, providing new insights into the pathogenesis of multiple myeloma and new basis for prognostic evaluation and treatment strategies.

This study identified MFRGs associated with MM. In addition, functional analysis using GO and KEGG revealed many biological processes and pathways (detoxification of copper ion, stress response to copper ion, stress response to metal ion et al.). Study have shown that Transient Receptor Potential Vanilloid type 1 (TRPV1) is expressed by MM cell lines, and TRPV1 inhibition can increase the levels of mitochondrial calcium, followed by mitochondrial membrane depolarization and mitochondrial ROS accumulation50. Calcium chelation reversed these effects, indicating that calcium ion interference plays a role in oxidative stress and mitochondrial instability50. In addition, GO enrichment analysis showed that stress response pathways related to metal ions were also enriched, which had similarities with previous studies. These findings suggest that the interaction between calcium ions and ROS in MM cells may promote the survival and drug resistance of MM cells by affecting mitochondrial function and apoptosis pathways.

This study explored the value of five MFRGs in the prognostic assessment of MM. GPR15 is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) originally identified as a coreceptor for human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV). GPR15 plays a central role in maintaining immune homeostasis and the development of inflammation by directing leukocyte migration into tissues51,52. GPR15 is involved in regulating T cell migration and immune responses. In multiple myeloma (MM), GPR15 may influence the composition and function of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment, thereby impacting tumor growth and metastasis53,54. Studies have also shown that GPR15 expression is associated with smoking, which increases oxidative stress55. Oxidative stress increases ROS production, impairs mitochondrial function, and promotes lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis56,57. Therefore, GPR15 may affect the ferroptosis sensitivity of MM cells by regulating the level of oxidative stress.NLRP7 (NACHT, LRR and PYD domains-containing protein 7), a member of the NOD-like receptor (NLR) family, is a cytoplasmic pattern recognition receptor that senses microbial and host-derived danger signals, thereby activating the innate immune response58. Previous studies have demonstrated a role for NLRP7 in various tumors. For example, NLRP7 expression is upregulated in colorectal cancer, promoting tumor progression and polarization of tumor-associated macrophages59. In lung adenocarcinoma, AH-6809 regulates metastasis through NLRP760. In multiple myeloma (MM), the signature of ferroptosis-related genes is closely associated with prognosis61,62. Furthermore, ferroptosis is closely linked to iron metabolism, ROS accumulation, and lipid peroxidation63. This study found that NLRP7 is a prognostic gene associated with both mitochondria and ferroptosis in MM. This suggests that NLRP7 may affect cellular metabolism and ROS status by altering mitochondrial function, thereby inducing or inhibiting ferroptosis in MM tumor cells.

ZNF208 (zinc finger protein 208) mediates gene transcription regulation through DNA binding. Its functions involve transcriptional control, DNA template processing and RNA polymerase II-dependent transcriptional regulation, and play a key role in maintaining cell function and developmental processes64. ZNF683 and ZNF208 belong to the ZNA family. ZNF683 inhibits SH2D1B and blocks SLAMF7-mediated NK cell activation signals in the bone marrow microenvironment of MM patients, leading to NK cell functional exhaustion, and may become a target for improving immunotherapy65. Secondly, as transcriptional regulators, the ZNF family may affect disease progression by regulating oncogenes (such as MYC) or cell cycle-related genes, thereby playing a role in the multi-step process of plasma cell malignant transformation66,67. In addition, in bortezomib-resistant MM cell lines, multiple ZNF family genes were aberrantly expressed, and this aberrant expression was associated with sensitivity to proteasome inhibitors68. Therefore, we can speculate that ZNF208 may play multiple roles in the pathological process of MM by regulating immune cell function, genetic stability and drug response.

PRDM13 belongs to the PRDM (PR domain-containing) gene family. Members of this family participate in transcriptional regulation and play important roles in cell differentiation, growth, and disease development69. Studies have shown that PRDM13 exhibits tumor suppressor properties in cancers such as neuroblastoma, breast cancer, and colorectal cancer, where it modulates the expression of downstream target genes through epigenetic modifications, thereby inhibiting tumor cell growth and metastasis70,71. As a transcriptional regulator, PRDM13 may regulate the expression of genes encoding mitochondrial proteins, thereby affecting mitochondrial function, ROS production, and energy metabolism69. In addition, PRDM13 may regulate the expression of ferroptosis-related genes, such as SLC7A11, GPX4, or iron metabolism-related genes. By affecting the expression of these genes, PRDM13 can change the sensitivity of MM cells to ferroptosis62,72,73.

The CRIM1 locus encrypts an integral membrane polypeptide harboring dual cysteinyl-enriched iterative modules in tandem with IGFBP-related recognition motifs74. It is involved in regulating the formation of the extracellular matrix and signaling pathways (such as the BMP/TGF-βpathway). This molecular entity cannot be overlooked as a critical determinant in the modulation of gestational tissue morphogenesis, embryonic organ patterning, vasculogenic network assembly, and nephropathic pathogenesis75. Studies have reported that silencing miR-199b-3p can activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling by downregulating CRIM1 and enhance the antitumor effect of cetuximab (CTx) on in vitro and CTx-resistant colorectal cancer (CRC)76.Previous studies have shown that CRIM1 may affect disease progression in variety of solid tumors by regulating the tumor microenvironment or cell proliferation-related pathways77,78. Currently, there is no literature that clearly links CRIM1 to MM. It is speculated that CRIM1 may indirectly participate in the proliferation of myeloma cells by affecting the BMP/TGF-βpathway, or may affect the disease process by regulating the interaction between stromal cells and tumor cells.

In summary, these five genes may indirectly or directly affect mitochondrial function and ferroptosis mechanism by regulating immune response, transcription process, genetic stability, inflammatory response, tumor microenvironment and other signaling axis, consequently affecting the occurrence, development and prognosis of multiple myeloma.

Accumulating evidence from peer-reviewed investigations substantiates that myelomagenesis cannot be dissociated from dynamic perturbations within the immunoecological landscape66. Systematic immunoprofile deconvolution revealed statistically significant disparities in the abundance of follicular dendritic cell subsets, tissue-resident macrophage populations, and quiescent mastocyte populations across prognostically stratified cohorts.

Within myelomatous neoplasms, the Slan-DC-MM cell cross-talk cannot be divorced from clonal evolution dynamics, where malignant plasma cells dysregulate transcriptional programming of IL-12 (interleukin-12) biosynthesis in Slan-DCs, driving phenotypic metamorphosis towards transitional monocytoid differentiation states79,consequently compromising competence in orchestrating naive T lymphocyte clonal expansion and polarization of Th1 effector lineages. On the other hand, although dendritic cells are important antigen-presenting cells in the immune system and can activate T cells to initiate immune responses, in MM, immunosuppressive factors in the tumor microenvironment limit their function80. Therefore, the functional changes of Slan-DCs in MM may affect the overall immune response by affecting T cell activation and polarization, thereby participating in the pathogenesis, which also indicates that Slan-DCs can become a potential target for MM treatment.

Macrophages exhibit a central involvement in the pathogenesis, advancement, and therapeutic resistance of MM. The underlying mechanisms governing their functionality are far from simplistic, being intricately intertwined with the tumor microenvironment dynamics. Accumulating evidence suggests that M2-polarized macrophages trigger the activation of JAK/STAT3 and PI3K/AKT signaling cascades in myeloma cells via IL-6 secretion, thereby fostering malignant proliferation and conferring resistance to pharmacological interventions81,82. In addition, M2 macrophages highly express angiogenesis-promoting mediators, exemplified by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and fibroblast growth factor-2 (FGF-2), and reshape the extracellular matrix by secreting MMPs (such as MMP-9), promoting neovascularization83,84. In terms of immune response, M2 macrophages can also inhibit the activity of CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells by secreting IL-10, and promote the expansion of regulatory T cells (Treg), forming an immunosuppressive microenvironment82. Macrophages (especially M2 type) are the core regulators of the MM microenvironment, promoting tumor progression and drug resistance through multiple pathways. Strategies targeting macrophages (such as drug combination and immune reprogramming) provide a new direction for improving the efficacy of MM.

IL-6 functions as a pivotal mediator in sustaining the proliferation and survival of MM cells, with its expression levels within the bone marrow microenvironment of MM patients demonstrating a pronounced elevation66. Quiescent mast cells may indirectly promote plasma cell survival and proliferation by releasing inflammatory mediators (such as IL-6, TNF-α) or chemotactic cytokines (such as CCL3, CCL5)66. On the other hand, for example, activated mast cells can release mediators such as histamine and proteases, which can inhibit T cell activity or promote the expansion of regulatory T cells (Treg), thereby enhancing immunosuppression85. Therefore, it is speculated that mast cells may affect MM anti-tumor immune response by regulating T cell function. It may become one of the targets for MM treatment in the future.

The present investigation identified 31 immune checkpoint molecules exhibiting significant differential expression between MM patients stratified into high- and low-risk cohorts, including TNFRSF14, CD48, and TNFRSF8.It has been reported that BM cells in smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM) exhibit upregulation of genes encoding pivotal cytotoxic effector molecules compared to monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) and symptomatic MM, and several of these cytotoxic molecules are positively correlated with inhibitory immune checkpoints86. Studies have shown that higher expression of TNF superfamily members (TNF, TNFAIP3 TNFRSF14) is associated with shorter progression-free survival in SMM86, which is similar to our findings. Immune checkpoint molecules TNFRSF14 and TNFRSF8 could serve as potential biomarkers for stratifying SMM patients exhibiting elevated progression risk.

m6A is one of the driving factors for the occurrence and progression of various tumors. m6A methylation is widely involved in the metabolic reorganization of tumors. TLR4 mediates cellular stress responses under proteasome inhibitor (PI) treatment to preserve mitochondrial homeostasis and enhance MM drug resistance. HNRNPA2B1 binds m6A-modified TLR4 transcripts, promoting tumor proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis via TLR4 signaling, thereby emerging as a promising therapeutic target. This mechanistic alignment with prior evidence reinforces the validity of our findings87.

Through GESA enrichment analysis of 5 prognostic genes, this study found that most prognostic genes were enriched in the ribosome pathway. Ribosomes are important organelles responsible for protein synthesis in cells. In tumor cells, ribosome biogenesis is usually overactivated to support the rapid growth of tumor cells88. In MM, overactivation of ribosome biogenesis leads to protein homeostasis imbalance and promotes tumor proliferation. CX-5461 inhibits its synthesis and thus inhibits MM cell growth89. In the future, perhaps we can treat relapsed and refractory MM by targeting ribosomes. The occurrence of multiple myeloma involves a variety of cellular metabolic pathways. CRIM1 is enriched in the oxidative phosphorylation pathway. Previous studies have shown that the pyruvate carrier (MPC) can transport pyruvate to the mitochondria to provide fuel for the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and the electron transport chain (ETC), thereby promoting OXPHOS90. Inhibition of MPC can affect the metabolism and growth of myeloma cell mitochondria and produce a synergistic effect with proteasome inhibitors to kill myeloma cells, which provides evidence for the dependence of MM cells on mitochondria91. Therefore, CRIM1 may indirectly affect the metabolism and growth of myeloma cell mitochondria by affecting the MPC-related links in the OXPHOS pathway, thereby affecting the survival and proliferation of MM cells92.

We also conducted drug sensitivity analysis, and the results were more significant for shikonin and roscovitine. Shikonin has been shown to induce ferroptosis in multiple myeloma through glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase 1 (GOT1)-mediated ferritin phagocytosis93. Roscovitine is a small molecule CDK inhibitor that induces apoptosis in multidrug-resistant human multiple myeloma cells94. In summary, shikonin and roscovitine affect the survival and death of MM cells through different pathways. The combined use of these two drugs may play a synergistic role in the treatment of MM, providing new ideas for improving treatment effects and overcoming drug resistance.

NLRP7 and PRDM13 are up-regulated in multiple myeloma patients. Compared with healthy people in the control group, their expression in tumor patients is significantly reduced, while GPR15 and CRIM1 are just the opposite, and their expression in tumor patients is significantly enhanced. Through RT-qPCR, the expression differences of the above genes in the blood of multiple myeloma and healthy people were verified.

The International Staging System (ISS) is based on peripheral blood albumin and β2-microglobulin levels and is only applicable to the prognosis of patients with confirmed, symptomatic multiple myeloma. The Revised International Staging System (R-ISS) integrates the ISS risk stratification algorithm with chromosomal abnormalities (CAs) and serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels to further refine patient prognostic groups. Despite continued improvements in prognostic assessment criteria for multiple myeloma patients, prognostic prediction remains highly heterogeneous95.

This study complements the ISS/R-ISS by examining metabolism and cell death, enabling earlier prediction of MM progression or drug resistance. PRDM13 and CRIM1 are significantly associated with the immune microenvironment, suggesting the potential predictive value of this model for immunotherapy response, a dimension not captured by the ISS/R-ISS. In addition, existing MM prognostic models are primarily associated with tumor suppressor genes96, apoptosis genes97,98, genomic rearrangements99, and immune cells100. This study integrates mitochondrial death and ferroptosis mechanisms into MM prognostic assessment, providing a unique biological perspective.

This study used univariate Cox and LASSO machine learning to obtain five prognostic genes, GPR15, NLRP7, ZNF208, PRDM13, and CRIM1, and their corresponding coefficients, and successfully constructed a model for diagnosing the 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival probabilities of MM patients. However, our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective GEO and USCS datasets used in this study exhibit heterogeneity in sample collection and processing, which may reduce the generalizability of our findings. Second, the specific mechanisms of action of the five prognostic genes identified in this study in MM require further experimental validation. Third, the sample size for RT-PCR was small, and statistical power was insufficient (10.8%), which may have affected the accuracy of the results. Fourth, the constructed prognostic model had an AUC value of only > 0.6, which, while statistically significant (p < 0.05), had moderate clinical discriminatory power. Finally, immune infiltration analysis was based solely on transcriptomic data from CIBERSORT and may not fully reflect the complexity of the tumor microenvironment. Therefore, we plan to expand the sample source in the future, reduce sample heterogeneity through unified experimental standards, validate the model’s applicability in different populations, and verify the experimental results of RT-qPCR. Targeting five prognostic genes, we will construct MM cell lines and animal models with knockout/overexpression of these genes. Combining RNA-seq and Co-IP techniques, we will investigate the specific molecular mechanisms by which these genes regulate ferroptosis through mitochondrial function. We also plan to integrate clinical features (ISS stage, genetic abnormalities) with imaging omics data to develop a combined diagnostic model. Furthermore, we will validate CIBERSORT predictions by combining single-cell sequencing, flow cytometry, and immunofluorescence to clarify the spatial distribution and interactions between immune cell subsets (such as M2 macrophages and dendritic cells) and prognostic genes.

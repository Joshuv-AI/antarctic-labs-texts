Superexotic K ∗ ⁣ + ​ D ∗ ⁣ + ​ K ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+}D^{*+}K^{*+} bound state
Wen-Hao Jia, Pei-Shen Su, Wei-Hong Liang, Raquel Molina, Eulogio Oset

Abstract
We study a system made from K ∗ ⁣ + ​ D ∗ ⁣ + ​ K ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+}D^{*+}K^{*+} with charge 3 3 , isospin I = 3 / 2 I=3/2 , spin J = 3 J=3 , and a quark content of c ​ d ¯ ​ s ¯ ​ u ​ s ¯ ​ u c\bar{d}\bar{s}u\bar{s}u , which make it highly exotic relative to the standard q ​ q ¯ q\bar{q} structure of mesons. The interaction of the three body system is obtained starting from a cluster of K ∗ ⁣ + ​ D ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+}D^{*+} in I = 1 I=1 and J = 2 J=2 , that in different works has been found bound, and adding to it an extra K ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+} with spin aligned with those of the vectors of the cluster. We find that the K ∗ ​ K ∗ K^{*}K^{*} interaction in I = 1 I=1 and J = 2 J=2 is repulsive, but its strength is small compared to that of K ∗ ⁣ + ​ D ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+}D^{*+} in I = 1 I=1 and J = 2 J=2 , such that we find a three body state bound by about 100 ​ MeV 100\,\rm MeV with respect to the mass of a K ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+} and the K ∗ ⁣ + ​ D ∗ ⁣ + K^{*+}D^{*+} cluster. The width of the state, of about 10 ​ MeV 10\,\rm MeV , is much smaller than the binding, which facilitates its observation. We suggest to find that state by measuring the invariant mass of K ​ D ​ K ∗ KDK^{*} , something feasible in present experimental facilities.

In the present work, we propose to look for a superexotic state that we find bound: the

The system has the quark content

It has isospin

The experimental information is found consistent with the findings of Ref.

II
formalism

We will follow the fixed center approximation (FCA)
Foldy 1945
;
Brueckner 1953a
;
Brueckner 1953b
;
Chand and Dalitz 1962
;
Barrett and Deloff 1999
;
Deloff 2000
;
Kamalov et al. 2001
;
Martinez Torres et al. 2020
;
Roca and Oset 2010
;
Malabarba et al. 2025
, to study the interaction of the three-body system.
For that we choose a cluster of two particles, which already gives a bound state.
This cluster is the
K^{*+}D^{*+}
system with
J=2
, which is bound by
68\textrm{ MeV}
with respect to the
K^{*}D^{*}
threshold, as was mentioned in the introduction.
Then, the third particle, the other
K^{*+}
, will interact with the cluster.
We follow recent developments where the FCA method has been improved to satisfy elastic unitarity at threshold of the third particle and the cluster
Ikeno and Oset 2025
;
Agatão et al. 2025
.
This condition is necessary to evaluate correlation functions for the interaction of a particle with a bound system of two other particles, as discussed in Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2025
, and also has repercussion in the binding of the three-body system.

In Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
, the interaction of a neutron
n
with a
KD
cluster, which forms the
D_{s0}^{*}(2317)
, was studied. Since the isospin of the particles is the same as in the present case and the
nK,\,nD
interaction in
S
-wave is spin independent, we can use the results of Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
;
Agatão et al. 2025
with minor modifications.
The only needed change is due to the fact that we follow the normalization of Mandl and Shaw
Mandl and Shaw 2010
for the fields and propagators.
Thus, we must change

\begin{split}\frac{M}{E(\vec{q}\,)}\,\frac{1}{q^{0}-E(\vec{q}\,)+i\epsilon}\;\;\longrightarrow\;\;&\frac{1}{q_{0}^{2}-\vec{q}^{\,2}-m^{2}}\\
\simeq&\frac{1}{2\omega(\vec{q}\,)}\,\frac{1}{q^{0}-\omega(\vec{q}\,)+i\epsilon},\end{split}

(1)

where
M
is the mass of the neutron in Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
,
m
the mass of the
K^{*}
here, and
E(\vec{q}\,)=\sqrt{M^{2}+\vec{q}^{\,2}}
,
\omega(\vec{q}\,)=\sqrt{m^{2}+\vec{q}^{\,2}}
.

We refrain from repeating the formalism of Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
, and we directly use the results from there with the modifications of Eq. (
1
).
The FCA formalism sums the diagrams of Fig. 1 of Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
, substituting
n
by
K^{*}
and
D,K
by
D^{*},K^{*}
, respectively.
In addition, the unitary amplitude sums the diagrams of Fig. 2 of Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
.
The sum of all those diagrams is obtained in Eq. (29) of Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
, which is rewritten in a simplified form in Ref.
Agatão et al. 2025
, and reads

T=\frac{\tilde{t}_{1}+\tilde{t}_{2}+\left(2G_{0}-G_{C}^{(1)}-G_{C}^{(2)}\right)\tilde{t}_{1}\,\tilde{t}_{2}}{1-G_{C}^{(1)}\,\tilde{t}_{1}-G_{C}^{(2)}\,\tilde{t}_{2}-\left(G_{0}^{2}-G_{C}^{(1)}\,G_{C}^{(2)}\right)\tilde{t}_{1}\,\tilde{t}_{2}},

(2)

where, for reasons of normalization,
\tilde{t}_{1}
,
\tilde{t}_{2}
are defined as

\tilde{t}_{1}=\frac{M_{c}}{M_{D^{*}}}\,t_{1},\quad\tilde{t}_{2}=\frac{M_{c}}{M_{K^{*}}}\,t_{2},

(3)

where
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
are the scattering amplitudes of the external
K^{*+}
with the
D^{*+}
and
K^{*+}
of the cluster, respectively, in
I=1
and
J=2
, which are described in the Appendix here, and
M_{c}
is the mass of the cluster.
The functions
G_{0}
,
G_{C}^{(1)}
, and
G_{C}^{(2)}
stand for the external
K^{*}
propagator folded by the cluster wave function, which involve the cluster form factors.
G_{0}
represents the
K^{*}
propagation from particle
1
to particle
2
of the cluster, and
G_{C}^{(1)}
,
G_{C}^{(2)}
for the
K^{*}
propagation together with the cluster as a whole, from particle 1 to particle 1 (
G_{C}^{(1)}
) and from particle 2 to particle 2 (
G_{C}^{(2)}
).

These
G

\begin{split}G_{0}(\sqrt{s})=&\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{F_{C}(\vec{q}\,)}{\sqrt{s}-\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{q}\,)-\omega_{C}(\vec{q}\,)+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{2\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{q}\,)}\,\\
&\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega_{C}(\vec{q}\,)}\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}^{(1)}-q_{1}^{*})\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}^{(2)}-q_{2}^{*}),\end{split}

(4)

\begin{split}G_{C}^{(i)}(\sqrt{s})=&\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{[F_{C}^{(i)}(\vec{q}\,)]^{2}}{\sqrt{s}-\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{q}\,)-\omega_{C}(\vec{q}\,)+i\epsilon}\\
&\cdot\frac{1}{2\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{q}\,)}\,\frac{1}{2\omega_{C}(\vec{q}\,)}\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}^{(i)}-q_{i}^{*}),\end{split}

(5)

where
q_{i}^{*}
are defined later in Eq. (
10
),
\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{q}\,)=\sqrt{m_{K^{*}}^{2}+\vec{q}^{\,2}}
,
\omega_{C}(\vec{q}\,)=\sqrt{M_{C}^{2}+\vec{q}^{\,2}}

F_{C}^{(i)}(\vec{q}\,)
are given by

\begin{split}F_{C}^{(1)}(\vec{q}\,)=F_{C}\!\left(\frac{M_{K^{*}}}{M_{D^{*}}+M_{K^{*}}}\,\vec{q}\right),\\
F_{C}^{(2)}(\vec{q}\,)=F_{C}\!\left(\frac{M_{D^{*}}}{M_{D^{*}}+M_{K^{*}}}\,\vec{q}\right),\end{split}

(6)

with

the ordinary form factor of the cluster given by

\begin{split}F_{C}(\vec{q}\,)=&\frac{F(\vec{q}\,)}{N},\\
F(\vec{q}\,)=&\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\frac{1}{M_{C}-\omega_{D^{*}}(\vec{p}\,)-\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{p}\,)}\\
&\cdot\frac{1}{M_{C}-\omega_{D^{*}}(\vec{p}-\vec{q}\,)-\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{p}-\vec{q}\,)},\end{split}

(7)

with integration limits
|\vec{p}\,|<q_{\text{max}}

|\vec{p}-\vec{q}\,|<q_{\text{max}}

\begin{split}N=&F(0)\\
=&\int_{|\vec{p}\,|<q_{\text{max}}}\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{M_{C}-\omega_{D^{*}}(\vec{p}\,)-\omega_{K^{*}}(\vec{p}\,)}\right)^{2},\end{split}

(8)

where
q_{\text{max}}
is the regulator of the
K^{*}D^{*}
loop functions
G
in the scattering matrix
T=[1-VG]^{-1}V
that gives the pole for the
K^{*}D^{*}
state with
J=2
in Ref.
Molina and Oset 2023
.
With the ingredients used in the Appendix we need
q_{\text{max}}=967\textrm{ MeV}
to obtain the binding of Ref.
Molina and Oset 2023
.

In addition to the replacement of Eq. (
1
) in the
G_{0}
and
G_{C}^{(i)}
functions of Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
5
), we have implemented the factor
\Theta\bigl(q_{\text{max}}^{(i)}-q^{*}_{i}\bigr)
that we justify below.

In the chiral unitary approach to obtain the meson-meson scattering amplitudes
Oller and Oset 1997
;
Kaiser 1998
;
Markushin 2000
;
Nieves and Ruiz Arriola 1999
, it is common to regularize the loop
G
functions for two particle propagation with a cut-off
q_{\text{max}}
. The framework is shown equivalent to using a separable potential

V(\vec{q},\vec{q}\,^{\prime})=v\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}-|\vec{q}\,|)\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}-|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}|),

(9)

where
q_{\text{max}}
gives an idea of the range of the interaction in momentum space, of the order of magnitude of vector meson masses
Gamermann et al. 2010
;
Song et al. 2022
.
Then the scattering matrix
T(\vec{q},\vec{q}\,^{\prime})
comes also factorized as
t\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}-|\vec{q}\,|)\,\Theta(q_{\text{max}}-|\vec{q}\,^{\prime}|)
.
The momenta in the
\Theta()
functions are in the two particle rest frame.
We would like to implement this factor also in our amplitudes
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
, for which we must place the momenta of the amplitudes in the rest frame of the external particle and particle 1 of the cluster for
t_{1}
, and in the rest frame of the external particle and particle 2 of the cluster for
t_{2}
.
The effect of these cut-offs is not drastic once the natural cluster form factors are used, but we implement them for consistency.
In this case, and given that the momenta involved are relatively small, we find it sufficient to make a Galilean boost from the frame where the amplitudes appear to the corresponding two-particle rest frame.
Even then, one has to specify the momenta of the particles of the cluster, and for this we take a prescription used in the study of elastic scattering or coherent production in nuclei, where we start with a momentum for the particle of the cluster
\frac{1}{2}(\vec{q}-\vec{k}\,)
and
\vec{k}
for the external particle, and finish with
\frac{1}{2}(\vec{k}-\vec{q}\,)
for the particle of the cluster and
\vec{q}
for the external particle.
This symmetrical distribution is the most likely and ensures the smallest momentum components from the cluster wave function
Carrasco et al. 1993
.
A different argumentation, reaching the same result, is shown in Ref.
Boffi et al. 1991
.
Then assuming that
\vec{k}\simeq 0
, we find for the rest-frame momenta

\vec{q}^{\,*}_{i}=\vec{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_{ex}}{m_{ex}+m^{\prime}_{i}}\right),

(10)

with
m_{ex}=m_{K^{*+}}
and
m^{\prime}_{1}=m_{D^{*+}}
,
m^{\prime}_{2}=m_{K^{*+}}
.

We find that the binding of the state with respect to the

.
One can understand this binding from the binding of the external

of the cluster in

One might rightly ask why we find a larger binding in the particle cluster than in the two particle case.
The reason is that in the former case the binding is written in terms of the variable

This is explained in Ref.
Montesinos et al. 2024

IV
Conclusions

We have investigated the possibility of having a bound state of
K^{*+}D^{*+}K^{*+}
, which is a mesonic state extremely exotic since it has charge three, isospin
3/2
, a quark component of
c\bar{d}\bar{s}u\bar{s}u
, and through flavor conservation in strong interactions it cannot decay into a system of fewer mesons.
In addition the state has a large angular momentum
J=3
, from the alignment of the spins of the three vectors, which also involve large angular momenta in decays channels.
All these factors make this state rather stable.
We looked at the structure of the state by starting from a two body cluster of
D^{*}K^{*}
in
I=1
and
J=2
, which was found to have a bound state of about
68\textrm{ MeV}
binding in Ref.
Molina and Oset 2023
(also found bound of Ref.
Duan et al. 2023
), and then have added another
K^{*}
with spin aligned in the same direction as in the original
D^{*}K^{*}
, such as to make a
J=3
state.
All pairs now have
J=2
, and the external
K^{*}
with the
D^{*}
of the cluster have again an attractive interaction.
The
K^{*}K^{*}
interaction in
I=1
and
J=2
is, however, repulsive, but we find that this repulsion is not enough to overcome the strong
K^{*}D^{*}
attraction in this channel.

The calculational scheme used here relies upon the fixed center approximation for the interaction of an external particle with a bound cluster, which has been modified to make it unitary at the threshold of the system.
The new formalism is then closer to the standard technique used in nuclear physics to deal with the interaction of particles with nuclei by means of an optical potential.
We construct the scattering matrix of an external
K^{*}
with the cluster of
K^{*}D^{*}
and find a resonant like state with a typical amplitude structure where the imaginary part has a peak and the real part changes sign around the peak position.
The binding of the state is around
100\textrm{ MeV}
with respect the mass of a
K^{*}
and the cluster of bound
K^{*}D^{*}
, and the width obtained is of the order of
10\textrm{ MeV}
, which makes its identification easy in principle.
In view of the possible decay mode of the cluster of
K^{*}D^{*}
into
KD
, we suggest the measurement of the invariant mass of
KDK^{*}
, something that is feasible with present techniques in the LHCb collaboration and ALICE among others.

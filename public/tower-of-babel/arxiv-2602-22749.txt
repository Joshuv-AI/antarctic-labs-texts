Generically sharp decay and blowing up at infinity for a weak null wave system
Shijie Dong, Siyuan Ma, Yue Ma, Xu Yuan

Abstract
. We study a system of semilinear wave equations satisfying the weak null condition, which can be regarded as a simplified model for the Einstein vacuum equations. The main objective is to establish precise pointwise decay estimates, as both lower and upper bounds of decay, for small data solutions. Specifically, we show that the difference between the solution and its leading-order term is dominated by lower-order terms that decay faster in the retarded time variable u = t − r u=t-r . Moreover, we prove that these pointwise decay estimates are sharp for a generic class of small initial data decaying sufficiently fast. As applications of these estimates, we demonstrate that the energy of one component of the solution admits a lower bound that generically grows to infinity as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty , which can be interpreted as “blowing up at infinity.” Furthermore, we verify that this component generically exhibits an energy cascade from high to low frequencies.

Major difficulties and key ideas

In our previous paper

to showing only the generic non-vanishing of the constant
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
. Assuming otherwise that there exists a pair of initial data of
(\phi,\psi)
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{1}=0
holds in a deleted
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhood of such an initial data. Denote
(\check{\phi},\check{\psi})
the difference of the perturbed solution to a general initial data in such a deleted
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhood from the solution
(\phi,\psi)
. Exploiting the fact that
\mathfrak{c}_{1}(\psi)=0
implies a faster decay in
v
for the component
\partial\psi
, we can perform an energy estimate for
\check{\phi}
to deduce a bound for the standard energy of
\check{\phi}

\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\phi})\lesssim\mathcal{E}(1,\check{\phi})+\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi}).

Also, we can achieve an energy bound for
\check{\psi}

\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi})\lesssim\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\phi}).

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(1,\check{\psi})\lesssim\epsilon\mathcal{E}(1,\check{\phi}),

which manifestly can not hold everywhere in a deleted
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhood of the initial data of
(\phi,\psi)
and hence completes the proof of genericity.

Recall that we denote

Greek indices

range over
\left\{1,2,3\right\}

Following Klainerman
[
19
]
, we introduce the following vector fields:

(i)

Translations:
\partial_{\alpha}=\partial_{x_{\alpha}}
, for
\alpha=0,1,2,3
.

(ii)

Scaling vector field:
S=t\partial_{t}+x^{a}\partial_{a}
.

(iii)

Hyperbolic rotations:
L_{a}=x_{a}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{a}
, for
a=1,2,3
.

(iv)

Spatial rotations:
\Omega_{ab}=x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a}
, for
1\leq a<b\leq 3
.

To simplify the notation, we denote

\Gamma=(S,\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},\Omega_{12},\Omega_{13},\Omega_{23}).

Moreover, we denote

\partial=\left(\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}\right),\ L=\left(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\right)\ \mbox{and}\ \Omega=\left(\Omega_{12},\Omega_{13},\Omega_{23}\right).

On the other hand, we use
I
and
J
to denote general multi-indices in
\mathbb{N}^{11}
.

Recall from (
1.4
) the null coordinates
(u,v)=(t-r,t+r)
.
Let
\partial_{u}
and
\partial_{v}
be the coordinate derivatives in the coordinate system
(u,v,\omega)
and, thus, we have

\partial_{u}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}-\partial_{r})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{v}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}).

For abbreviation, we also define

U=2\partial_{u}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad V=2\partial_{v}=\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}.

(2.1)

From
[
2
, Theorem 5.10]
, for any sufficiently regular function
f=f(t,x)
,

u\left|\partial f\right|+u\left|Uf\right|+v|Vf|\lesssim|\Gamma f|.

(2.2)

Recall also that, for any sufficiently regular function
f=f(t,x)
, we have

\left|r^{-1}\Omega f\right|\lesssim v^{-1}\left|\Gamma f\right|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left|r^{-2}\not{\Delta}f\right|\lesssim v^{-2}\sum_{1\leq|I|\leq 2}\left|\Gamma^{I}f\right|.

(2.3)

Define the hyperbolic time
s=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
with
s\geq 1
, and denote constant-

hyperboloidal hypersurfaces by

\mathcal{H}_{s}{=}\left\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}:s^{2}=t^{2}-r^{2}\right\},\quad\mbox{for any}\ s\geq 1.

For each hypersurface
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, we denote by
\vec{n}
the future directed unit normal vector and
\mathrm{d}\sigma
the volume element (with respect to the Euclidean metric in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
), that is,

\vec{n}\mathrm{d}\sigma=\left(1,-\frac{x_{1}}{t},-\frac{x_{2}}{t},-\frac{x_{3}}{t}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

(2.4)

Moreover, for any
1\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<+\infty
, we denote

\mathcal{H}_{[s_{1},s_{2}]}{=}\bigcup_{s=s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{s}=\left\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}:t^{2}-r^{2}\in\left[s_{1}^{2},s_{2}^{2}\right]\right\}.

For a sufficiently regular function
f=f(t,x)
defined on
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, we define

\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}f(t,x){\rm{d}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(\sqrt{s^{2}+r^{2}},x){\rm{d}}x.

In addition, the Sobolev norms of the function
f=f(t,x)
on
\mathcal{H}_{s}
are defined by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{p}=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}|f(t,x)|^{p}{\rm{d}}x\ \ {\mbox{for}\ p\in[1,+\infty)}\quad\mbox{and}\ \ \|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}=\sup_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}|f(t,x)|.

To establish the energy estimate for the 3D inhomogeneous linear wave equation for future reference, we introduce the following standard energy
\mathcal{E}
on the hyperboloidal hypersurface

\mathcal{E}(s,f){:=}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\left((s/t)^{2}|\partial_{t}f|^{2}+(1/t)^{2}|Lf|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

(2.5)

In view of the above definition of

, we directly have

\|(s/t)\partial f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|Vf\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|(1/t)Lf\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}\lesssim\mathcal{E}(s,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.6)

We also introduce the following conformal energy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{s}

\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f):=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\left(\left(Kf+2f\right)^{2}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}(s{\underline{\partial}}_{a}f)^{2}\right){\rm{d}}x.

Here, we denote the conformal vector field

K=s\bar{\partial}_{s}+2x^{a}{\underline{\partial}}_{a}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{\partial}_{s}=\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)\partial_{t}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ {\underline{\partial}}_{a}=\frac{x_{a}}{t}\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}.

From the classical Hardy inequality, we also have

\left\|s(s/t)^{2}\partial f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|(s/t)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.7)

Let
{\mathrm{d}\omega}
be the volume element on unit sphere

. For any sufficiently regular function

, we set

f_{\ell=0}(t,r):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(t,r,\omega){\mathrm{d}\omega}.

Indeed, the subscript

indicates that the above definition is the
\ell=0

when the function

is decomposed into spherical harmonics on

.
In addition, we set

f_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega):=f(t,r,\omega)-f_{\ell=0}(t,r).

More generally, for any

with
|m|\leq\ell
, we denote by

the normalized

-order spherical harmonics, that is,

-\not{\Delta}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\omega)=\ell(\ell+1)Y_{\ell}^{m}(\omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\omega)Y_{\ell^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}(\omega){\mathrm{d}\omega}=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}.

In addition, for any sufficiently regular function
f=f(t,r,\omega)
and

with

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f=f(t,x)
be the solution to the Cauchy problem

-\Box f=F\quad\mbox{with}\quad(f,\partial_{t}f)_{|\mathcal{H}_{1}}=(f_{0},f_{1}).

Then for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(s,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}(1,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{1}^{s}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(1,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{1}^{s}\tau\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Proof.

First, we can rewrite the product
(-\Box f)\partial_{t}f
as the divergence form,

(-\Box f)\partial_{t}f=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left((\partial_{t}f)^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}\right)-\partial^{a}\left(\partial_{t}f\partial_{a}f\right).

Integrating the above identity over
\mathcal{H}_{[1,s]}

2.4
), we have

\mathcal{E}(s,f)-\mathcal{E}(1,f)=-2\int_{1}^{s}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)(\partial_{t}f)F\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\tau,

which, by differentiating in
s
and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, leads us to

\mathcal{E}(s,f)^{1\over 2}{\mathrm{d}\over\mathrm{d}s}\mathcal{E}(s,f)^{1\over 2}=-\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}(s/t)(\partial_{t}f)F\mathrm{d}x\Longrightarrow{{\mathrm{d}\over\mathrm{d}s}}\mathcal{E}(s,f)^{1\over 2}\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}{(\mathcal{H}_{s})}}.

Integrating the above estimate over
[1,s]
, we complete the proof for the estimate of
\mathcal{E}
.

Second, the proof for the conformal energy estimate relies on the use of a non-spacelike vector field
K_{0}=(t^{2}+r^{2})\partial_{t}+2tr\partial_{r}
. More precisely, a direct computation yields,

\displaystyle(-\Box f)(K_{0}f+2tf)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left[(t^{2}+r^{2})|\partial f|^{2}+4tr(\partial_{t}f)(\partial_{r}f)\right]

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}\left[2tf\partial_{t}f-f^{2}+{\rm{div}}(xf^{2})\right]-{\rm{div}}\left[2tf\nabla f+\partial_{t}(xf^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+{\rm{div}}\left[tx(-2(\partial_{t}f)^{2}+|\partial f|^{2})-(t^{2}+r^{2})(\partial_{t}f)\nabla f-2tr(\partial_{r}f)\nabla f\right].

Integrating the above identity over
\mathcal{H}_{[1,s]}
, and then using (
2.4
), we have

\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f)-\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(1,f)=-2\int_{1}^{s}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)(K_{0}f+2tf)F{\rm{d}}x,

which, by differentiating in
s
and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, leads us to

{{\mathrm{d}\over\mathrm{d}s}}\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f)^{1\over 2}\leq\|sF\|_{L^{2}{(\mathcal{H}_{s})}}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(s,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\rm{con}}(1,f)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{1}^{s}\tau\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\tau})}\mathrm{d}\tau.

The proof for the estimate of the conformal energy

\displaystyle+({\mathfrak{c}}_{3}+O(\epsilon u^{-\delta}))(\ln u-\ln\gamma(u)).

In view of the definitions for
(u,\gamma(u))
and the space-time region
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}

Next, using again the almost sharp decay estimates (

\left|(r\phi)(u,\gamma(u))\right|\lesssim\epsilon(\gamma(u)-u)\gamma^{-1}(u)\ln\gamma(u)\lesssim\epsilon\ln\gamma(u)\lesssim\epsilon u^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\ln v.

Combining the above estimates with (
2.24

3.13

Precise decay of

Lemma 3.3

In the space-time region
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{ext}},2\delta}

\left|V\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon\ln^{2}u}{v\ln^{2}v}.

Proof.

Note that, from definition (

We split the proof into the following two parts according to the subregion under consideration. Note that it suffices to consider the case where
u

Step 1.
Estimate in
\mathcal{C}_{\rm{ext},2\delta}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\rm{int},2\delta}

In view of the almost sharp decay estimates (

Step 2.
Estimate in
\mathcal{D}_{\rm{ext},2\delta}

\displaystyle\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](u,v)

\displaystyle=\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](u,\gamma(u))

(3.16)

Here,

is chosen such that it lies on the curve

\gamma(u)=u+u^{1+2\delta}\Longrightarrow\ln\gamma(u)\sim\ln u.

Therefore, using again
the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22
), we have

\left|\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](u,\gamma(u))\right|\lesssim\epsilon u^{-1}\ln u.

(3.17)

On the other hand, using again the system (
3.3

\displaystyle V\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{rv}\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}-\frac{\ln v}{r^{2}}(\partial_{t}\Psi)(\not{\Delta}\Psi)

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\not{\Delta}\Phi-\ln v(\partial_{t}\Psi)\left(rQ_{0}(\phi,\phi)+VV\Psi\right).

Therefore, using the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22

\left|V\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](u,v)\right|\lesssim\epsilon v^{-2}u^{-1}\ln^{5}v+\epsilon v^{-2}\ln v,

which then yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\gamma(u)}^{v}V\left[\left(U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right)\right](u,\nu){\rm{d}}\nu\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{\gamma(u)}^{v}\left(\nu^{-2}u^{-1}\ln^{5}\nu+\nu^{-2}\ln\nu\right){\rm{d}}\nu\lesssim\epsilon u^{-1}\ln u.

Combining the above estimate with (
3.16

3.17

3.15
).

Then, integrating along
U
from
u_{0}(v)
, where

is given as in (
3.7

v^{-1}
,
we deduce that, for any

\displaystyle V\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)(u,v)

\displaystyle=V\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)(v^{-1},v)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{v^{-1}}^{u}UV\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)(\sigma,v){\rm{d}}\sigma.

On the other hand, using again (

UV\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\not{\Delta}\Phi}{\ln v}-\frac{2}{v\ln^{2}v}\left[U\Phi-\ln v\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{u}{rv}\left[\frac{\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}}{\ln v}\right].

Plugging the above identity back into (

Combining the above estimates, we complete the proof of (
3.14
) in this region.

Next, we make use of the above precise decay for
V(\Phi/\ln v)

Lemma 3.4
.

In the space-time region
\mathcal{C}_{\frac{\delta}{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{v^{2}}{\ln v}V\bar{\Psi}\right)(u,v)-\left(\frac{v^{2}}{\ln v}V\bar{\Psi}\right)(v^{-1},v)

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{c}_{4}-2\int_{u}^{+\infty}\left[\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](\sigma,+\infty){\rm{d}}\sigma-2\int_{0}^{v^{-1}}\left[\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](\sigma,+\infty){\rm{d}}\sigma

\left|\left(\frac{v^{2}}{\ln v}V\bar{\Psi}\right)(v^{-1},v)\right|\lesssim\epsilon v^{-\delta}.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{u}^{+\infty}\left[\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](\sigma,+\infty){\rm{d}}\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{v^{-1}}\left[\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](\sigma,+\infty){\rm{d}}\sigma\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim v^{-1}\bigg\|\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}(\partial_{t}\Psi)^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}.

2.23
) for
\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{v^{2}}{\ln v}V\bar{\Psi}\right)(u,v)

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{c}_{4}+O\left(\epsilon v^{-\delta}+\epsilon v^{-1}+\epsilon u^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}+\epsilon u^{-3+\delta}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{v}^{+\infty}\int_{v^{-1}}^{u}V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]\left(\sigma,\nu\right){\rm{d}}\sigma{\rm{d}}\nu.

3.3

\displaystyle V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]

3.3

\displaystyle\left|V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]\right|\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}(\ln^{-2}v)u^{-4}\ln^{2}u+\epsilon v^{-2}u^{-3}\ln^{2}v,

\left|\int_{v}^{+\infty}\int_{v^{-1}}^{u}V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]\left(\sigma,\nu\right){\rm{d}}\sigma{\rm{d}}\nu\right|\lesssim\epsilon(\ln v)^{-1}\lesssim\epsilon u^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}.

Combining the above estimates, we complete the proof of (
3.19
) in this region.
∎

Next, we deduce the asymptotic behavior of
\bar{\psi}
in the space-time region
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}

Proposition 3.5
.

In the space-time region

\left|\bar{\psi}(u,v,\omega)-r^{-1}{\Psi}(u,+\infty,\omega)+\mathfrak{c}_{4}r^{-1}v^{-1}\ln v\right|\lesssim\epsilon r^{-1}v^{-1}u^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}\ln v.

Proof.

Integrating (
3.19

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(u,v,\omega)={}

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(u,+\infty,\omega)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{+\infty}V\bar{\Psi}(u,\sigma,\omega){\rm{d}}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(u,+\infty,\omega)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{v}^{+\infty}\Big(2\mathfrak{c}_{4}\sigma^{-2}\ln\sigma+O\left(\epsilon\sigma^{-2}u^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}\ln\sigma\right)\Big){\rm{d}}\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}(u,+\infty,\omega)-\mathfrak{c}_{4}v^{-1}\ln v+O(\epsilon v^{-1}u^{-\frac{\delta}{4}}\ln v).

On the other hand, from Proposition

\displaystyle=-2\frac{uv}{r^{3}}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(V\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)\left(r^{-2}\not{\Delta}\Psi+rQ_{0}(\phi,\phi)+VV\Psi\right).

Hence, using the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22
) as well as
Lemma
3.3
, we find

\displaystyle\left|V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right](u,v)\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{3}\left(v^{-1}u^{-4}(\ln^{-2}v)\ln^{6}u+v^{-2}u^{-3}\ln^{6}v\right),

which yields, after integrating the above estimate over
[v^{-1},u]\times[v,+\infty)
,

\left|\int_{v}^{+\infty}\int_{v^{-1}}^{u}\left(V\left[\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Phi}{\ln v}\right)\left(\partial_{t}\Psi\right)^{2}\right]\right)_{\ell=0}\left(\sigma,\nu\right){\rm{d}}\sigma{\rm{d}}\nu\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{3}(\ln v)^{-1}.

Combining the above estimates, we complete the proof of (
3.25
) in this region.
∎

We now use the above precise decay estimate for
V\bar{\Psi}_{\ell=0}
to derive the precise decay for
\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}
globally in the full space-time region.

Proposition 3.9

(Precise decay for
\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}
)
.

It holds in the whole space-time region that

\left|\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,v)-\mathfrak{c}_{2}\psi_{L}(u,v)\right|\lesssim\epsilon(\ln u)^{-1}\psi_{L}(u,v).

(3.26)

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts, proving the desired estimates in the space-time regions
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{ext}},\delta}
and
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{int}},\delta}
, respectively. Also, it suffices to consider the case in which
u
is large enough.

Step 1.
Estimate in
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{ext}},\delta}
.
Integrating (
3.25
) from
(u,\gamma(u))
where
\gamma(u)
is defined such that

(u,\gamma(u))\in\big\{r=\frac{1}{2}u^{1-2\delta}\big\}\Longrightarrow\gamma(u)=u\left(1+u^{-2\delta}\right),

we deduce

\displaystyle(r\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})(u,v)-(r\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})(u,\gamma(u))

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathfrak{c}_{2}+O((\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)(\ln u)^{-1})\right)\left(\frac{\ln u}{u}-\frac{\ln v}{v}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\mathfrak{c}_{2}+O((\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)(\ln u)^{-1})\right)\left(\frac{\ln\gamma(u)}{\gamma(u)}-\frac{\ln u}{u}\right).

Here, we have used the fact that

\int_{\gamma(u)}^{v}\sigma^{-2}{\rm{d}}\sigma\lesssim\left(\ln u\right)^{-1}\int_{\gamma(u)}^{v}\sigma^{-2}\ln\sigma{\rm{d}}\sigma.

Then, the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22
) imply

\left|r\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,\gamma(u))\right|\lesssim\epsilon u^{-1-2\delta}\ln^{2}u\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \left|\frac{\ln\gamma(u)}{\gamma(u)}-\frac{\ln u}{u}\right|\lesssim u^{-1-2\delta}\ln u.

(3.28)

On the other hand, in view that

v-u\geq u^{1-\delta}\Longrightarrow v\geq u+u^{1-\delta},\quad\mbox{for any}\ \ (u,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{ext}},\delta},

and that
(\ln x/x)
is a strictly decreasing function on
(1,+\infty)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\ln u}{u}-\frac{\ln v}{v}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ln u}{u}-\frac{\ln(u+u^{1-\delta})}{u+u^{1-\delta}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\ln u}{u}-\frac{\ln u}{u+u^{1-\delta}}\right)-\frac{\ln\left(1+u^{-\delta}\right)}{u+u^{1-\delta}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim u^{-1-\delta}\ln u-u^{-1-\delta}\gtrsim u^{-1-\delta}\ln u.

Combining the above estimate with (
3.27
) and (
3.28
),
we complete the proof for (
3.26
) in this space-time region.

Step 2.
Estimate in
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{int}},\delta}
.
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, for any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{int}},\delta}
, we compute

\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,v)=\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,\gamma_{1}(u))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{1}(u)}^{v}V\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,\nu){\rm{d}}\nu.

Here,
(u,\gamma_{1}(u))
is made such that it lies in the curve
r=\frac{1}{2}u^{1-\delta}
, that is,

\gamma_{1}(u)=u+u^{1-\delta}\Longrightarrow\gamma_{1}(u)=u\left(1+u^{-\delta}\right).

Using (
2.24
) and the proven estimate in Step 1, we check that

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,\gamma_{1}(u))

\displaystyle=\left(2\mathfrak{c}_{2}+O((\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)(\ln u)^{-1})\right)\left(u^{-2}\ln u+O(u^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{c}_{2}u^{-2}\ln u+O\left((\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)u^{-2}\right)=2\mathfrak{c}_{2}u^{-2}\ln u+O(\epsilon u^{-2}).

Then, the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22
) and
(u,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{\rm{int},\delta}
imply that

\left|V\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,v)\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}v^{-2}u^{-1}\ln^{2}u\Longrightarrow\left|\int_{\gamma_{1}(u)}^{v}V\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}(u,\nu){\rm{d}}\nu\right|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}u^{-2-\delta}\ln^{2}u.

On the other hand, for any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{\rm{int},\delta}
with
u
large enough, we have

\psi_{L}=r^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{v}\right)\ln u+r^{-1}v^{-1}\ln\left(1+\frac{2r}{u}\right)\\
=2u^{-2}\ln u+O(u^{-2}).

Combining the above estimates, we thus complete the proof of
(
3.23
) in
\mathcal{C}_{{\rm{int}},\delta}
.
∎

3.4.
Estimate in Region I

In this space-time region, we expect that the leading order part of the asymptotic behavior for the solution
(\phi,\psi,\bar{\psi})
can be determined from the behavior for the zeroth-order mode
(\phi_{\ell=0},\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})
. We show in the following lemma an upper bound of decay in this space-time region for
(\phi_{\geq 1},\psi_{\geq 1})
, which turns out to decay faster than
(\phi_{\ell=0},\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})
.

Lemma 3.10
.

In Region
\mathrm{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1-\frac{\delta}{2}},

(3.29a)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon v^{-2-\frac{\delta}{2}}.

(3.29b)

Proof.

Note that, in this space-time region, we have
u\lesssim v\lesssim u
and
r\lesssim v^{1-\delta}
, which will be constantly used throughout this proof. In view of the wave system (
1.1
) and the alternative form (
3.2
) of wave equation, the system of equations of
(\phi_{\geq 1},\psi_{\geq 1})
can be written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} \not{\Delta}\phi_{\geq 1}&=r^{2}UV\phi_{\geq 1}-2r\partial_{r}\phi_{\geq 1}-r^{2}\big[\left(\partial_{t}\psi\right)^{2}\big]_{\geq 1},\\
\not{\Delta}\psi_{\geq 1}&=r^{2}UV\psi_{\geq 1}-2r\partial_{r}\psi_{\geq 1}-r^{2}\big[Q_{0}(\phi,\phi)\big]_{\geq 1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Based on the above identity and the almost sharp decay estimates (
2.22
), and applying a standard elliptic estimate over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\not{\Delta}\phi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{2}UV\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|r\partial_{r}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|r^{2}\left(\partial_{t}\psi\right)^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon(v^{-1-2\delta}\ln^{2}v+v^{-1-\delta}\ln v+v^{-4}\ln^{4}v)\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1-\frac{\delta}{2}}.

Applying a standard Sobolev embedding theorem on sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we complete the proof for the desired estimate of
\phi_{\geq 1}
.

Next, using a similar argument as above, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\not{\Delta}\psi_{\geq 1}(t,r,\omega)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|r^{2}UV\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|r\partial_{r}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|r^{2}Q_{0}(\phi,\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon(v^{-2-\delta}\ln^{2}v+v^{-2-2\delta}\ln^{2}v)\lesssim\epsilon v^{-2-\frac{\delta}{2}}.

Applying a standard Sobolev embedding theorem on sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we complete the proof for the desired estimate of
\psi_{\geq 1}
.
∎

Next, we make use of the above estimates for
(\phi_{\geq 1},\psi_{\geq 1})
, together with the precise decay estimates for
(\phi_{\ell=0},\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})
proven in Section
3.3
, to compute the precise decay for the solution
(\phi,\bar{\psi})
in Region
I
.

Proposition 3.11

(Precise decay for
(\phi,\bar{\psi})
in Region
I
)
.

In Region
\mathrm{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\phi(u,v)-\mathfrak{c}_{1}\phi_{L}(u,v)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{1}|)u^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\phi_{L}(u,v),

(3.30a)

\displaystyle\left|\bar{\psi}(u,v)-\mathfrak{c}_{2}\psi_{L}(u,v)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)(\ln u)^{-1}\psi_{L}(u,v),

(3.30b)

or in an alternative form,

\displaystyle\left|\phi(u,v)-2\mathfrak{c}_{1}v^{-1}\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{1}|)v^{-1-\frac{\delta}{2}},

(3.31a)

\displaystyle\left|\bar{\psi}(u,v)-2\mathfrak{c}_{2}v^{-2}\ln v\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\epsilon+|\mathfrak{c}_{2}|)v^{-2}.

(3.31b)

Proof.

The estimate (
3.30a
) for
\phi
follows from combining the estimate (
3.23
) for
\phi_{\ell=0}
with the estimate (
3.29a
) for
\phi_{\geq 1}
, and the alternative estimate (
3.31a
) for
\phi
follows from (
3.30a
) together with the following fact

\phi_{L}=-r^{-1}\ln\bigg(1-\frac{2r}{v}\bigg)=2v^{-1}+O(rv^{-2})=2v^{-1}+O(v^{-1-\delta})\quad\textrm{in\,\, Region\,\, I}.

Combining the estimate (
3.26
) for
\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0}
with the estimate (
3.29b
) for
\psi_{\geq 1}
, and in view of the estimate (
3.31a
) and the following fact

\displaystyle\psi_{L}={}

\displaystyle r^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\ln u}{u}-\frac{\ln v}{v}\bigg)=r^{-1}\bigg(\frac{2r\ln u}{uv}+\frac{\ln v-\ln u}{v}\bigg)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{2\ln u}{uv}+O(v^{-2})=\frac{2\ln v}{v^{2}}+O(v^{-2})\quad\textrm{in\,\, Region\,\, I},

the other estimates (
3.30b
) and (
3.31b
) then follow.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Note that, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for
u\in(1,+\infty)
large enough,

\displaystyle\phi_{L}^{2}=r^{-2}\left(\int_{u}^{v}\sigma^{-1}{\rm{d}}\sigma\right)^{2}\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{u}^{v}\sigma^{-2}{\rm{d}}\sigma\lesssim r^{-1}\int_{u}^{v}\frac{\ln\sigma}{\sigma^{2}}{\rm{d}}\sigma\lesssim\frac{\psi_{L}}{\ln u}.

(3.32)

Hence, from Propositions
3.2
,
3.9
and
3.11
and the almost sharp decay estimate (
2.22
), it follows that in the large space-time region
\left\{2r\leq u^{1-\delta}\right\}\cup\left\{2r\geq u^{1+\delta}\right\}
, the leading order terms in the precise decay of
(\psi,\psi_{\ell=0})
are the same as the ones of
(\bar{\psi},\bar{\psi}_{\ell=0})
.

4.
Genericity of the sharp decay rates

In this section, we prove that for a generic set of small initial data, the constants
\{\mathfrak{c}_{i}\}_{i=1,2}
are non-zero and the functions on the sphere
\{\mathfrak{c}_{i}\}_{i=3,4}
do not vanish identically. We start with the definition of the admissible initial data set.

Definition 4.1

(Admissible initial data set
\mathfrak{S}
)
.

A pair of initial data
(\phi_{init},\psi_{init})=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is called
admissible initial data
if it satisfies the condition (
1.11
) for a given
N\geq 6
with
0<\epsilon\ll 1
suitably small such that the global existence and decay estimates statement in Theorem
1.2
for the solution
(\phi,\psi)
to the system (
1.1
) with initial data
(\phi_{init},\psi_{init})=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
hold.
The set of all admissible initial data is called the admissible initial data set and denoted as
\mathfrak{S}
.

We next define a distance function on the admissible initial data set
\mathfrak{S}
.

Definition 4.2

(Distance function and its induced topology)
.

Let
N\geq 6
be given. Let
(\phi_{init},\psi_{init})=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
and
(\hat{\phi}_{init},\hat{\psi}_{init})=(\hat{\phi}_{0},\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\psi}_{0},\hat{\psi}_{1})
be two pairs of admissible initial data in
\mathfrak{S}
, and let
(\phi,\psi)
and
(\hat{\phi},\hat{\psi})
be the global solutions to the system of wave equations (
1.1
) arising from these pairs of initial data respectively. Define a distance function for these two pairs of admissible initial data by

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}[(\phi_{init},\psi_{init}),(\hat{\phi}_{init},\hat{\psi}_{init})]:={}

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{{N}+2}\partial_{x}^{\leq N+1}(\phi_{0}-\hat{\phi}_{0},\psi_{0}-\hat{\psi}_{0})\|

\displaystyle+\|\langle x\rangle^{{N}+2}\partial_{x}^{\leq N}(\phi_{1}-\hat{\phi}_{1},\psi_{1}-\hat{\psi}_{1})\|.

(4.1)

This distance function trivially induces a natural topology in the admissible initial data set
\mathfrak{S}
.

Note that the right-hand side of (
4.2
) is the norm showing up on the left-hand side of the smallness condition (
1.11
), but for the difference of the two given initial data
(\phi_{init},\psi_{init})
and
(\hat{\phi}_{init},\hat{\psi}_{init})
.

Recall the definition of the constants
(\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2})
and functions
(\mathfrak{c}_{3}(\omega),\mathfrak{c}_{4}(\omega))
appearing in the principal term of the late-time asymptotics of
(\phi,\psi)
in the main theorem
1.2
. In view of
2\partial_{t}=U+V
, and since applying once
V
derivative gains an extra
v^{-1}
decay as shown in the decay estimates (
2.22
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(U(r\psi))^{2}(u,+\infty,\omega){\rm{d}}u{\rm{d}}\omega,

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{r\phi}{\ln v}(U(r\psi))^{2}\bigg)(u,+\infty,\omega){\rm{d}}u{\rm{d}}\omega,

(4.2b)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{3}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(U(r\psi))^{2}(u,+\infty,\omega){\rm{d}}u,

(4.2c)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{4}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{r\phi}{\ln v}(U(r\psi))^{2}\bigg)(u,+\infty,\omega){\rm{d}}u.

(4.2d)

We introduce the sets

(\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,i})_{i=1}^{4},

where the sets
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}
are composed by the admissible initial data such that the constants
\mathfrak{c}_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, and where the sets
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,3}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,4}
are composed by the admissible initial data such that there exists an
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{i}(\omega)\neq 0
for
i\in\{3,4\}
. Further, we define the following subset of the admissible initial data set

\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0}:=\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,3}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,4}.

Our objective in this section is to show that the subset
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0}
is an open and dense subset of
\mathfrak{S}
with respect to the induced topology introduced in Definition
4.2
. In other words, we show that, for generic initial data in the admissible initial data set
\mathfrak{S}
, both of the constants
(\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2})
are non-vanishing and neither of the functions
(\mathfrak{c}_{3}(\omega),\mathfrak{c}_{4}(\omega))
are identically vanishing over
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Since
(\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2})
are the spherical means of
(\mathfrak{c}_{3}(\omega),\mathfrak{c}_{4}(\omega))
, respectively, it holds true that
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,3}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,4}
, and thus,

\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0}=\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,3}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,4}=\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}.

(4.3)

To show
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0}
is open and dense, it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,2}
are open and dense, because the intersection of two open-dense sets is still open and dense.

Notice that the constants
\{\mathfrak{c}_{i}[\phi,\psi]\}_{i=1,2}
are continuous functions of the solution
(\phi,\psi)
, and are hence continuously dependent on its initial data
(\phi_{init},\psi_{init})
in view of the global well-posedness of the system (
1.1
). Therefore, the subsets
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
are open. As a consequence, to establish that
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0}
is open and dense, it remains to show that the sets
\{\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,i}\}_{i=1,2}
are both dense in
\mathfrak{S}
, which is the main task of the following subsections
4.1
and
4.2
.

4.1.
The subset
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}
is dense in
\mathfrak{S}

We start with a technical lemma, asserting a faster decay in
v
for the term
U\Psi
in the case of
\mathfrak{c}_{1}=0
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{c}_{1}=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle|U\Psi|+|V\Psi|\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}u^{-1}\ln^{2}v.

Proof.

First, from (
2.22
), we directly have

|V\Psi|\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}u^{-1}\left(\ln u+(u/v)\ln^{2}v\right)\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}u^{-1}\ln^{2}v.

Second, from the definition of
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
in (
4.2
), we find

\mathfrak{c}_{1}=\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(U(r\psi))^{2}(u,+\infty,\omega){\rm{d}}u{\rm{d}}\omega=0\Longrightarrow U\Psi\equiv 0,\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{I}.

It follows from the equation
VU\Psi=r^{-2}\not{\Delta}\Psi+rQ_{0}(\phi,\phi)
that

|U\Psi(u,v)|\lesssim\int_{v}^{+\infty}\big|r^{-2}\not{\Delta}\Psi+rQ_{0}(\phi,\phi)\big|(u,\nu)\,d\nu.

By the almost sharp decay (
2.22
), we find

\big|r^{-2}\not{\Delta}\Psi+rQ_{0}(\phi,\phi)\big|(u,v)\lesssim\epsilon v^{-2}u^{-1}\ln^{2}v,

which implies

|U\Psi(u,v)|\lesssim\epsilon u^{-1}\int_{v}^{+\infty}\nu^{-2}\ln^{2}\nu{\rm{d}}\nu\lesssim\epsilon v^{-1}u^{-1}\ln^{2}v.

Combining the above estimates, we complete the proof for Lemma
4.3
.
∎

We are in a position to show that the subset
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}
is dense in
\mathfrak{S}
.

Proof of denseness of
\mathfrak{S}_{\neq 0,1}
in
\mathfrak{S}
.

The proof proceeds by contradiction.
Assume otherwise that there exists a pair of initial data
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}
such that:

1)

\mathfrak{c}_{1}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})=0
;

2)

There exists
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll 1
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{0},\phi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{0},\psi^{\prime}_{1})=0
for all initial data
(\phi^{\prime}_{0},\phi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{0},\psi^{\prime}_{1})
belonging to the deleted
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhod of
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{0},\psi_{1})
in
\mathfrak{S}
.

We denote by
(\check{\phi},\check{\psi})=(\phi-\phi^{\prime},\psi-\psi^{\prime})
the difference of the two solution pairs, in which the pair
(\phi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
solves (
1.1
) with initial data
(\phi^{\prime}_{0},\phi^{\prime}_{1},\psi^{\prime}_{0},\psi^{\prime}_{1})
.

Step 1.
Bounds for
\check{\psi}
.
We claim that

\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi})\lesssim\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\phi}).

(4.4)

Indeed, we consider the following equation for the difference
\check{\psi}
with
\check{\Psi}=r\check{\psi}
,

\displaystyle UV\check{\Psi}-r^{-2}\not{\Delta}\check{\Psi}=rQ_{0}(\phi,\check{\phi})+rQ_{0}(\check{\phi},\phi^{\prime}).

By the standard energy estimate in the space-time region
\mathcal{H}_{[s,+\infty)}
with the boundary
\mathcal{H}_{s}\cup\mathcal{I}
and then using the fact that
U\Psi=0
on
\mathcal{I}
,

\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi})\lesssim\int_{s}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\tau\over t}\Big|rQ_{0}(\phi,\check{\phi})+rQ_{0}(\check{\phi},\phi^{\prime})\Big|\big|\partial_{t}\check{\Psi}\big|\,{\rm{d}}\omega{\rm{d}}r{\rm{d}}\tau.

Note that, from
\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
and the almost sharp decay (
2.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\tau\over t}\Big|rQ_{0}(\phi,\check{\phi})+rQ_{0}(\check{\phi},\phi^{\prime})\Big|\big|\partial_{t}\check{\Psi}\big|\lesssim\tau^{-\frac{5}{4}}\Big|\frac{\tau r}{t}U\check{\psi}\Big|\Big(\left|rV\check{\phi}\right|+\Big|\frac{\tau r}{t}U\check{\phi}\Big|\Big),

which thus implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi})

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{s}^{+\infty}\tau^{-{5\over 4}}\mathcal{E}(\tau,\check{\phi})^{1\over 2}\mathcal{E}(\tau,\check{\psi})^{1\over 2}\,{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{s}^{+\infty}\tau^{-{5\over 4}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\tau,\check{\phi})+\mathcal{E}(\tau,\check{\psi})\big)\,{\rm{d}}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\phi})+\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi}).

By smallness of
\epsilon
, we complete the proof of the desired estimate (
4.4
).

Step 2.
Bounds for
\check{\phi}
.
We claim that

\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\phi})\lesssim\mathcal{E}(1,\check{\phi})+\epsilon\sup_{s\in[1,+\infty)}\mathcal{E}(s,\check{\psi}).

(4.5)

Note that the energy bound for
\check{\phi}
generally exhibits a logarithmic divergence. However, under the assumption
\mathfrak{c}_{1}=0
, a standard energy estimate for the wave equation satisfied by
\check{\phi}
over
\mathcal{H}_{[1,s]}
yields a more refined energy bound. More precisely, by an elementary computation, we have

\displaystyle-\Box\check{\phi}=\partial_{t}\check{\psi}(\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}),

Proof of Theorem

Fix

With a slight abuse of notation, below we write

\displaystyle\Phi=\Phi_{\ell},\qquad F=\big((\partial_{t}\Psi)^{2}\big)_{\ell}.

The equation of

Acting

\displaystyle r^{2}UV\Phi^{(\ell)}+2\ell rV\Phi^{(\ell)}=(r^{2}V)^{\ell}(rF),

\displaystyle\Phi^{(k)}:=(r^{2}V)^{k}\Phi,\quad\forall\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

\displaystyle U(r^{-2\ell}V\Phi^{(\ell)})=r^{-2\ell-2}(r^{2}V)^{\ell}(rF),

\displaystyle U(v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}\Phi^{(\ell+1)})=v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}(r^{2}V)^{\ell}(rF).

We integrate from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
along
U
direction (i.e., the integral curve of

\displaystyle\big(v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}\Phi^{(\ell+1)}\big)(u,v)-\big(v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}\Phi^{(\ell+1)}\big)(v^{-1},v)={1\over 2}\int_{v^{-1}}^{u}G,

with

\displaystyle G={}

\displaystyle v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}(r^{2}V)^{\ell}(rF)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}(r^{2}V)^{\ell}r\cdot F+v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell}{i}(r^{2}V)^{i}r\cdot(r^{2}V)^{\ell-i}F

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\ell!v^{\ell+1}r^{-\ell-1}F+v^{\ell+1}r^{-2\ell-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell}{i}i!r^{i+1}\cdot(r^{2}V)^{\ell-i}F.

\Psi
, we have

\displaystyle(r^{2}V)F={}

\displaystyle 2r^{2}\big(\partial_{t}\Psi\partial_{t}V\Psi\big)_{\ell}

\displaystyle|(r^{2}V)^{\ell-i}F|\lesssim_{\ell}\epsilon^{2}u^{-2+\delta}(\ln{v})^{2}r^{\ell-i-1},

\displaystyle G=\ell!v^{\ell+1}r^{-\ell-1}F+v^{\ell+1}r^{-\ell-2}\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(\epsilon^{2}u^{-2+\delta}(\ln{v})^{2}).

Consequently, we have, for

Lemma 6.1

Under the assumptions of Theorem

Proof of (i). Recall that, from the almost sharp decay (

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|^{2}

Then, from the fact that

|\psi(t,r,\omega)|+|\partial\psi(t,r,\omega)|\lesssim\epsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-1}\ln^{2}\langle t-r\rangle.

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|^{2}+\|\partial\psi(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(|\psi(t,r,\omega)|^{2}+|\partial\psi(t,r,\omega)|^{2}\right)r^{2}{\rm{d}}r{\rm{d}}\omega

Further remarks on z z -ideals of semirings
Ahmed Maatallah, Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. In this note, we revisit certain results from a previous work of the second author concerning z z -ideals of semirings. We demonstrate that the assumption of the semiring being a bzi-semiring can be dispensed with. Additionally, we propose a revised definition of the z z -radical that enables a more general formulation of results from the earlier work.

Let
S
be a semiring. We denote by
\id
the set of all ideals of
S
, and by
\mx
the set of all maximal ideals. An ideal
\mathfrak{z}\subseteq S
is called a
z
-ideal
if for every
x\in\mathfrak{z}
, we have

\bigcap\left\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mx\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}.

We write
\z
for the set of all
z
-ideals of
S
. A proper
z
-ideal is said to be
z
-maximal
if it is not properly contained in any other proper
z
-ideal. A proper
z
-ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is said to be
z
-semiprime
if
\mathfrak{a}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
implies
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\z
. Similarly,
\mathfrak{p}
is said to be
z
-prime
if
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
implies
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for all
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\z
.

Given
x\in S
, we write
\mathcal{M}_{x}:=\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mx\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}\}
, and define the corresponding
basic
z
-ideal

\mathfrak{m}_{x}:=\bigcap\mathcal{M}_{x}
. If every basic
z
-ideal in
S
is idempotent, we say that
S
is a
bzi-semiring
. The
z
-closure
of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\id
is defined by

\cz(\mathfrak{a}):=\bigcap\left\{\mathfrak{z}\in\z\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}\right\}.

It was shown in
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
that the product of two
z
-ideals is again a
z
-ideal if and only if
S
is a bzi-semiring. This result was employed in the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 4.5]
, thereby imposing a restrictive assumption on the ambient semiring. We now show that this condition is superfluous.

Theorem 1
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq S
is
z
-prime if and only if it is a prime
z
-ideal.

Proof.

Every prime
z
-ideal is trivially
z
-prime. Conversely, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a
z
-prime ideal and suppose
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\id
. By
[
1
, Lemma 3.14]
, we have

\cz(\mathfrak{a})\cz(\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\cz(\cz(\mathfrak{a})\cz(\mathfrak{b}))=\cz(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}.

Since
\cz(\mathfrak{a}),\cz(\mathfrak{b})\in\z
, and
\mathfrak{p}
is
z
-prime, it follows that
\cz(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\cz(\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The preceding theorem allows one to remove the bzi-semiring assumption from all results in
[
1
]
that relied on
[
1
, Proposition 4.5]
.

We next show that every

z
-ideal is

z
-semiprime. For this we first prove the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3
.

Every
z
-ideal of a semiring
S
is radical.

Proof.

The proof is in fact part of the claim
[
1
, Lemma 3.14(13)]
. We give, however, another proof. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a
z
-ideal of
S
and let
x\in\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}
. Then

x^{n}\in\mathfrak{a}
for some
n\in\mathds{N}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{M}_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{x^{n}}
.
Therefore
x\in\mathfrak{a}
, since
\mathfrak{a}
is a
z
-ideal. Hence
\mathfrak{a}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}.

∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\z
and
\mathfrak{a}\in\id
. If
\mathfrak{a}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, then
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{p}
is a
z
-ideal and
\mathfrak{a}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, we have

\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}.

∎

We now turn to the notion of

z
-radicals (
cf
.
[
1
, Definition 4.10]
), which requires refinement. The need for revision arises in
[
1
, Lemma 4.11(3)]
from the observation that the product of two

z
-ideals need not be a

z
-ideal, and from the observation in Theorem
6
that every

z
-ideal is already

z
-radical.

Definition 1
.

The
z
-radical of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a semiring
S
is defined by

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec}\left\{\mathfrak{p}~|~\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\right\}.

Whenever
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}
,
we say
\mathfrak{a}
is a
z
-radical ideal.

The following result constitutes a revised formulation of
[
1
, Lemma 4.11]
. We additionally observe that the inclusions stated in part (3) of that lemma are, in fact, equalities, as clarified in part (4) below.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be two ideals of a semiring
S
. Then the following holds.

(1)

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
is a
z
-ideal containing
\mathfrak{a}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
, then

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}
.

(3)

\sqrt[z]{\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}}=\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(4)

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}}=\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}}=\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}
.

Proof.

(1)

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
is a
z
-ideal as an
intersection of
z
-ideals. It is clear that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(2)
Since
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
, for every

\mathfrak{p}\in\spec
,
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}

implies that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}
.

(3)
It is clear that for every

\mathfrak{p}\in\spec
,
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
if and only if

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Then, by Definition
1
,

\sqrt[z]{\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}}=\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(4)
Using
(2)
, we have

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}
.
Now, it is enough to show that
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}}
. Let

\mathfrak{p}\in\spec
such that
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}

is prime, either
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.
Thus,
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
or
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.
Then
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

∎

In
[
1
, Proposition 4.23]
, it is proved that
a
z
-ideal of a bzi-semiring is

z
-prime if and only if it is
z
-semiprime and
z
-strongly irreducible. Thanks to Theorem
1
and Lemma
3
, the statement can be simplified.

Proposition 7
.

An ideal of a semiring is
z
-prime if and only if it is a
z
-strongly irreducible.

Proof.

Clearly, every
z
-prime is a
z
-strongly
irreducible ideal. Conversely, let

\mathfrak{p}
be a
z
-strongly irreducible
ideal. If
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, for some
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}\in\z
.
Then by Theorem
6
and Lemma
5

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}}=\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.
By
[
1
, Proposition 4.19.]
,
\mathfrak{p}
is strongly irreducible, and hence, either
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}

or
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

∎

Remark 8
.

We finish this note with a few further observations.

\bullet

The proof of
[
1
, Lemma 4.2]
is straightforward, as every maximal ideal is
z
-maximal.

\bullet

The proof of the converse of
[
1
, Proposition
4.3]
can be simplified as follows.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a
z
-maximal ideal. Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}
is not maximal, then
\mathfrak{m}
is properly contained
in a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{r}
. However,
every maximal ideal is
z
-maximal,
which leads to a contradiction.

\bullet

Since every
z
-ideal is radical and that
\cz(\mathfrak{a})
is a
z
-ideal, it follows that
[
1
, Lemma 3.14(12)]
will become
\cz(\mathfrak{a})=\cz(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}})
.

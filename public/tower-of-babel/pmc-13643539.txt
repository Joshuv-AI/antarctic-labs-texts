First Reported Native Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement in Congenitally Corrected Transposition of the Great Arteries
Authors: John T. D'Angelo, Karan Sud, Nikoloz Shekiladze, Joel T. Hardin, Nikoloz Koshkelashvili, Patrick T. Gleason, Adam B. Greenbaum, Vasilis C. Babaliaros
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCongenitally corrected transposition of the great arteries (CC-TGA) can lead to severe systemic tricuspid regurgitation (TR). There is limited evidence on transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) in these patients.Case SummaryA 59-year-old woman with CC-TGA presented with symptomatic severe systemic TR. She was at high risk for surgery, and transcatheter interventions were considered by our heart team. TTVR on the systemic tricuspid valve was performed using a Sapien M3 valve (Edwards Lifesciences), and the systemic TR was eliminated. There were no complications, with significant symptomatic improvement at the 30-day follow-up.DiscussionTranscatheter therapies in high–surgical risk adult CC-TGA patients are being increasingly investigated. This case shows that TTVR using a Sapien M3 valve is feasible in CC-TGA patients with symptomatic severe systemic TR.Take-Home MessageTTVR with the Sapien M3 valve is a feasible and safe alternative in high–surgical risk CC-TGA patients with symptomatic systemic TR.

History of Present Illness

A 59-year-old woman with a history of congenitally corrected transposition of the great arteries (CC-TGA) was referred for severe symptomatic systemic tricuspid regurgitation (TR). For 1 year, the patient experienced worsening fatigue, dyspnea and lower extremity edema, consistent with NYHA functional class IV symptoms. On examination, she had a grade 3/6 holosystolic murmur best appreciated at the cardiac apex, with grade 1 bilateral, pitting lower extremity edema.

Past Medical History

The patient also had a history of heart failure reduced ejection fraction, coronary artery disease, paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with use of home oxygen. She was an active smoker with a 40 pack-year history.

Differential Diagnosis

In patients with CC-TGA and worsening heart failure symptoms, the differential diagnosis includes worsening systemic ventricular function, complete heart block, new arrythmias, or valvular abnormalities. Systemic ventricular failure and severe systemic TR are the most common causes of morbidity and mortality in these patients.

Management

Intervention on the systemic tricuspid valve was considered after a sufficient trial of diuretics. Transcatheter tricuspid intervention was considered because the patient was a high-risk surgical candidate. Tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) was not possible because of severe posterior leaflet calcification. Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) using the Evoque (Edwards Lifesciences) was not possible given concerns about manipulation in the left atrium and valve delivery with transseptal access. TTVR with the Sapien M3 (Edwards Lifesciences) was then considered, and possible challenges were discussed. Tricuspid annulus sizing was appropriate for a 29-mm M3 valve, and the neo–systemic ventricular outflow tract was sufficient to prevent iatrogenic systemic ventricular outflow tract obstruction during TTVR. The systemic tricuspid chordal apparatus seemed adequate to allow delivery of the docking system. Therefore, we proceeded with TTVR using the M3 valve.

The procedure was performed under general anesthesia with TEE and fluoroscopic guidance. Access to the right common femoral vein, left common femoral vein, and left common femoral artery was obtained using ultrasound guidance with sheath placement in each vessel. A 6-F pigtail catheter was placed into the arterial access to monitor aortic pressure. Right common femoral vein perclosure was performed using a Perclose Prostyle device (Abbott Vascular), and the right common femoral vein sheath was upsized to a 24-F Dryseal sheath (Gore Medical). Via the right femoral venous access, an Astato XS 20 guidewire (Asahi Intecc) and 8.5-F Agilis catheter (Abbott Vascular) were used to cross the interatrial septum. An exchange length j-wire and 6-F Multipurpose A2 were then placed to locate the left upper pulmonary vein. An Amplatz extra-stiff wire (Boston Scientific) was then anchored in the pulmonary vein. This system was then removed, and a 28-F Edwards guide sheath and dilator (Edwards Lifesciences) were advanced into the left atrium. The dock delivery system (DDS) was then advanced to provide encircling capture of the tricuspid's anterior and posterior chords just below the systemic tricuspid annulus. Initial attempts were made to start encircling at the septal-anterior commissure, however that required excessive manipulation of the DDS; hence, we reapproached through the septal-posterior commissure. The chordal apparatus was captured with TEE guidance (Figure 7). The proximal portion of the DDS was then atrialized, and the leak guard was released into the left atrium. The DDS was then removed, and the dock was crossed with a 6-F pigtail catheter. A Safari guidewire (Boston Scientific) was then secured in the systemic ventricle apex, and the 29-mm M3 valve delivery system was advanced over this guidewire. The 29-mm M3 valve was deployed during a period of rapid pacing via temporary venous pacemaker inserted in the left femoral venous access site. After appearing to have sufficient M3 valve placement on TEE and fluoroscopy, the M3 delivery system and Safari guidewire were removed. The transseptal access site was closed with a 25-mm Gore Septal Occluder device (Gore Medical). TEE showed no significant TR, with a minimal tricuspid mean gradient. Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient's postoperative course was unremarkable. Postoperative TTE showed a well-positioned bioprosthetic tricuspid without TR or tricuspid paravalvular leak. The tricuspid mean gradient was 8.0 mm Hg. Systemic and pulmonic ventricular function were unchanged. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 1 without complications. At the 30-day follow-up, she was doing well with NYHA functional class II symptoms, and her lower extremity edema had significantly improved. TTE at the 30-day follow-up was similar to TTE on discharge and showed good prosthetic valve function.

Discussion

CC-TGA is a rare type of congenital heart disease (CHD) occurring during embryologic formation.1 While surgical correction typically involves either an anatomic or physiologic surgical repair, the timing of surgery is variable, and some patients do not undergo surgery in childhood.2 Uncorrected adult CC-TGA patients are at a higher risk of developing congestive heart failure because of worsening systemic ventricular dysfunction, which can lead to systemic TR.3,4 Although surgical systemic tricuspid replacement can be considered in adults, long-term survival is limited, especially in patients with systemic ventricular dysfunction.5 Transcatheter tricuspid intervention is of increasing interest in CC-TGA patients, however more investigation is needed.6

Various transcatheter systemic tricuspid interventions have been explored among CC-TGA patients. Among high–surgical risk CC-TGA patients with moderate to severe systemic TR, T-TEER using an off-label Mitraclip (Abbott Vascular) has been shown to reduce symptom severity and TR severity for up to 1 year.7 While these results are encouraging, T-TEER only partially reduces TR severity and cannot be used in patients with anatomy unsuitable for T-TEER. Valve-in-valve TTVR using an off-label Sapien 3 (Edwards Lifesciences) has been reported in a CC-TGA patient with degeneration of a bioprosthetic systemic tricuspid, however this requires a previous surgical bioprosthetic valve to anchor the valve.8 Dedicated devices for transcatheter mitral valve replacement and TTVR have provided promising results in terms of reducing valve regurgitation and reducing symptoms.9,10 The M3 and Evoque platforms are now commonly used for transcatheter mitral valve replacement and TTVR, respectively, and their use will likely be explored in CHD patients as they age. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first reported experience of native TTVR using an M3 valve in a CC-TGA patient.

Although the M3 valve was deemed to be a better option compared to the Evoque valve, there were also concerns about using the M3 valve, as it is not typically used for TTVR. More specifically, there were concerns about the approach to encircling without causing systemic ventricular perforation. As previously discussed, initiation of encircling was originally attempted at the septal-anterior commissure, but encircling was eventually approached from the septal-posterior commissure to enhance DDS manipulation. Despite the tricuspid morphology of the valve, there were no technical issues with M3 encircling or valve deployment. Moreover, there were no complications with the case, and the systemic TR was eliminated with good bioprosthetic valve function. Additionally, ventricular function remained unchanged after valve deployment. The patient was noted to be doing well at the 30-day follow-up. While this case had favorable procedural and follow-up outcomes, operators should take each patient's unique anatomy into careful consideration when using these more novel transcatheter therapies in adult CHD patients.

Further investigation into transcatheter systemic tricuspid intervention is needed for CC-TGA patients. Moreover, the use of transcatheter tricuspid intervention in adult CHD patients should take the patient's unique anatomy into consideration. This case illustrates the efficacy and safety of using the M3 valve for native TTVR in CC-TGA to highlight an additional option for operators.

Conclusions

This case highlights how the M3 platform can be used for TTVR in a patient with CC-TGA with a favorable outcome. In the future, this may provide another option for CC-TGA patients who need systemic tricuspid valve replacement.Take-Home Message•Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement with the Sapien M3 valve is a feasible and safe alternative in high–surgical risk patients with congenitally corrected transposition of the great arteries and symptomatic systemic tricuspid regurgitation.

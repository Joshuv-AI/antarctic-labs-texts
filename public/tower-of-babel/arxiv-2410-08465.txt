Abstract
. Let ( S , ℱ ) (S,\mathcal{F}) be a foliated surface over the complex number of general type, i.e., the Kodaira dimension Kod ⁡ ( ℱ ) = 2 \mathrm{Kod}(\mathcal{F})=2 . We study the geometry of the canonical map φ \varphi of the foliated surface ( S , ℱ ) (S,\mathcal{F}) , and prove several boundedness results on the canonical map φ \varphi , generalizing Beauville’s beautiful work [ Bea79 ] on the canonical maps of algebraic surfaces to foliated surfaces. As an application, we prove three Noether type inequalities for ( S , ℱ ) (S,\mathcal{F}) depending on the Kodaira dimension of the surface S S .

1.
Introduction

The main purpose of the paper is to study the canonical map of a
foliated surface of general type over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of general type over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
,
and
K_{X}
be its canonical divisor.
The
n
-th pluri-geometric genus of
X
is defined to be

p_{n}(X):=\dim H^{0}\big(X,nK_{X}\big).

When
n=1
, it is called the geometric genus of
X
and also denoted by
p_{g}(X)=p_{1}(X)
.
If
p_{n}(X)\geq 2
, one considers the rational map

\varphi_{n}:=\varphi_{|nK_{X}|}:\,X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{p_{n}(X)-1},

defined by the complete linear system
|nK_{X}|
.
The map
\varphi_{n}
is called the
n
-th pluri-canonical map of
X
, and
\varphi_{1}
is also called the canonical map.
The canonical and pluri-canonical maps are very important
in the classification of projective varieties of general type, cf.
[
ACGH85
,
BHPV04
,
Bea79
,
Bom73
,
HM06
,
Tak06
]
.

The picture is clear when
X=C
is a smooth projective curve:
the pluri-canonical map
\varphi_{n}
is always birational if
n\geq 3
;
and the canonical map
\varphi_{1}
is birational if and only if
C
is non-hyperelliptic.
If
C
is indeed a hyperelliptic curve, then
\varphi_{1}
gives a double cover onto a projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

When
X=S
is a smooth projective surface of general type,
Bombieri
[
Bom73
]
proved that the pluri-canonical map
\varphi_{n}
is birational once
n\geq 5
.
Later, Beauville
[
Bea79
]
provided a systematical study on the canonical map
\varphi_{1}
.
Among others the following significant boundedness results are proved.
Denote by
\vol(S)
the volume of
S
, which is defined to be

\vol(S)=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\dim H^{0}(S,nK_{S})}{n^{2}/2}.

Theorem 1.1

(Beauville)
.

Let
\varphi_{1}:\,S\dashrightarrow\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(S)-1}
be canonical map of
S
.

(i)

If the image
\Sigma
is of dimension two, then

\deg(\varphi_{1})\leq\frac{\vol(S)}{p_{g}(S)-2}.

(1.1)

By the Miyaoka-Yau inequality, it follows that

\deg(\varphi_{1})\leq 36;\qquad\text{and}\qquad\deg(\varphi_{1})\leq 9,~\text{if\,}~p_{g}(S)\geq 30.

(1.2)

(ii)

If the image
\Sigma
is of dimension one, i.e., it induces a fibration

f:\,\widetilde{S}\to B
of genus
g
after possible blowing-ups and the Stein factorization, then

2\leq g\leq\frac{\vol(S)}{2(p_{g}(S)-1)}+1.

(1.3)

By the Miyaoka-Yau inequality, it follows that

2\leq g\leq 5,\quad\text{if}\quad p_{g}(S)\geq 20.

(1.4)

Remark that the lower bound
g\geq 2
in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) follows from the assumption that
S
is of general type.
Beauville’s breakthrough work serves as a gateway to the study on the canonical map of surfaces of general type, and hence inspires a series of subsequent noteworthy works;
see for instance
[
Xia85a
,
Xia86
,
Sun94
,
Che17
,
Yeu17
,
Lü20
,
Rit22
]
.

In recent years, the foliation theory has garnered significant interest in the realm of algebraic geometry, particularly in birational geometry.
Specifically, foliations whose canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is not pseudo-effective have been thoroughly characterized in
[
Miy87
,
BM16
,
CP19
]
.
Notably, in the remarkable paper
[
Cas21
]
,
the (conjectural) minimal model program for foliated varieties was introduced.
This innovation sparked a novel project in birational geometry aimed at extending these concepts to foliated varieties.
Substantial noteworthy progress has already been made in this direction. For instance, Miyaoka
[
Miy87
]
and Campana-Păun
[
CP19
]

delves into foliated varieties with non-pseudoeffective canonical divisors.
Brunella and McQuillan, among others, have explored the minimal model theory for foliated surfaces
[
Bru99
,
McQ08
]
.
Furthermore, the deformation invariance of pluri-genera
[
CF18
,
Liu17
]
adds another remarkable progress to this research.
Recently, there are also many significant progress on the study of the adjoint canonical divisor, cf.
[
SS23
,
CHL+24
]
.
These studies offer strong evidence that the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}

of a foliation plays an analogous role to the canonical divisor
K_{X}
of an algebraic variety.
The canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
can be utilized to study not only the geometry of the foliation but also the geometry of the variety itself.
This has also inspired our study on the canonical map of a foliated surface of general type.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
S
.
The geometric genus
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})
and the volume
\vol(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}

are defined over some reduced model
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle p_{g}({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle=\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime},K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}});

\displaystyle\vol({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime},nK_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})}{n^{2}/2},

where
K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
is the canonical divisor of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
These are two birational invariants, and independent on the choice
of the reduced model;
see
Section 2.2
for more details.
The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be of general type if its Kodaira dimension is
2
, or equivalently if
\vol(\mathcal{F})>0
.
When
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})\geq 2
, one defines the canonical map

\varphi
as the rational map given by the complete linear system
|K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|
:

\varphi=\varphi_{|K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|}:\,S^{\prime}\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}.

Equivalently, composed with the birational map
S\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
, one may also view the canonical map
\varphi
as a rational map mapping the surface
S
to the projective space:

\varphi:\,S\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}.

Consider first the case when the image
\Sigma
is of dimension two, or equivalently, the canonical map is generically finite.
In this case,
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on a smooth projective surface
S
, such that its canonical map
\varphi
induces a generically finite map as above.
Then

\deg(\varphi)\leq\frac{\vol(\mathcal{F})}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2}.

(1.5)

Moreover, if the equality holds in (
1.5
), then the image
\Sigma
is a surface of minimal degree in
\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}
.
In particular,

\deg(\varphi)\leq\vol(\mathcal{F}),

(1.6)

and if the equality holds in (
1.6
), then
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})=3
, i.e., the image
\Sigma\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

We will construct examples reaching the equalities in both (
1.5
) and (
1.6
); see
Example 3.8
and
Example 3.9
.
A special case is when the canonical map
\varphi
is birational, i.e.,
\deg\varphi=1
,
in which situation we would like to call

(S,\mathcal{F})
a
canonical foliated surface
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{F})
be a canonical foliated surface of general type, i.e., the canonical map
\varphi
is birational. Then

(i)

The following inequality holds.

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2.

(1.7)

The equality in (
1.7
) implies that
the image
\Sigma
is a surface of minimal degree (equal to
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2
) in
\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}
.
In particular,
S
is rational.

(ii)

Suppose that the surface
S
is not ruled. Then

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq 2p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-4,

(1.8)

Moreover, if the equality holds in (
1.8
), then
S
is birational to a K3 surface.

(iii)

Suppose that the surface
S
is also of general type. Then

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq 2p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-4+\frac{\sqrt{8p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-31}-7}{2},

(1.9)

Moreover, if the equality holds in (
1.9
), then
S
is birational to a
(1,0)
-surface.
If
S
is not birational to a
(1,0)
-surface, then

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq 2p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-4+\frac{\sqrt{8p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-23}-3}{2},

(1.10)

Moreover, if the equality holds in (
1.10
), then
S
is birational to a
(1,2)
-surface.
Here by an
(a,b)
-surface we mean a minimal surface
Y
of general type
with
\vol(Y)=a
and
p_{g}(Y)=b
.

We refer to
[
LT24
, Example 9.1]
for a canonical foliated surface of general type reaching the equality in (
1.7
).
By (
1.5
), it follows that the foliated surface
(S_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})
in
[
LT24
, Example 9.1]
is canonical.
We will also construct examples in
Section 3.3

reaching the equalities in both (
1.8
) and (
1.10
).

Consider next the case when the image
\Sigma
is of dimension one.
In this case, after possible blowing-ups and taking the Stein factorization,

\varphi
induces a fibration
f:\,\widetilde{S}\to B
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on a smooth projective surface
S
, such that its canonical map
\varphi
induces a fibration
f:\,\widetilde{S}\to B
as above, and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
.
Suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}

(1.11)

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on a smooth projective surface
S
, such that its canonical map
\varphi
induces a fibration
f:\,\widetilde{S}\to B
as above, and let
F
be a general fiber of
f
.
Suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}
is different from the foliation defined by taking the saturation of
\ker(\mathrm{d}f:\,T_{\widetilde{S}}\to f^{*}T_{B})
in
T_{\widetilde{S}}
.

(i)

It holds that
d=K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}\cdot F\geq 1

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{d(d+2)}{d+1}\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1+g(B)\big).

Remark 1.6
.

(i) In the above two theorems,

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}

is algebraically integrable by definition.
The converse is not true in general. Namely, there exist examples of

but

in which case it even holds that

(ii)

and there is no absolute upper bound either on the genus

(and the degree

in

and

induces a fibration

.

As a byproduct of the study on the canonical map, we obtain the following Noether type inequalities for foliations of general type.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on a smooth projective surface
S
.

(i)

The following Noether inequality holds.

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2.

(1.16)

Moreover, if the equality in (
1.16
) holds,
then the canonical map
\varphi
induces a birational map whose image
\Sigma
is a surface is a surface of minimal degree
(
equal to
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2)
in
\mathbb{P}^{p_{\mathcal{F}}-1}
. In particular,
S
is rational.

(ii)

Suppose that the surface
S
is not ruled. Then

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1\big),~2\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2\big)\right\}.

(1.17)

(iii)

Suppose that the surface
S
is of general type. Then

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq 2\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2\big).

(1.18)

Moreover, if the equality in (
1.18
) holds, then the canonical map
\varphi
induces a two-to-one map whose image
\Sigma
is a surface of minimal degree
(
equal to
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-2)
in
\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}
.

Remark 1.8
.

(i) The classical Noether inequality for surfaces of general type was established by Noether
[
Noe70
]
more than one hundred years ago.
Chen-Chen-Jiang
[

,

Remark also that Chen-Jiang

(ii) In

2.
Preliminary

In this section,
we recall some basic facts about the foliated surfaces. For more details we refer to
[
Bru04
,
McQ08
]
.

2.1.
Basic definitions

We work over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
.
By a foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
we mean a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a smooth projective surface
S
over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a smooth projective surfacce
S
over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
is defined by a saturated invertible subsheaf
T_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq T_{S}
in the tangent sheaf
T_{S}
,
i.e.,
T_{S}/T_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion free.

The singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
consists of the points
x\in S

where the quotient sheaf
T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}

fails to be locally free at
x
.
The torsion-freeness of
T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}
ensures that the singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
in
S
has codimension of at least two,
i.e., it consists of finitely many points.
In other words, a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
S
can be expressed by an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow T_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}(N_{\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow 0,

where
T_{\mathcal{F}}
and
N_{\mathcal{F}}
are respectively called the
tangent bundle
and
normal bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
,
and
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal sheaf supported on the singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
.
Equivalently, a foliation on
S
can be described by the data
\{(U_{i},v_{i})\}_{i\in I}
,
where
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an open covering of
S
,

v_{i}
is a holomorphic vector field on
U_{i}
with at most isolated zeros,
and there exists
g_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{S}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
whenever
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
such that

v_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}=g_{ij}v_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}.

(2.1)

The cocycle
\{g_{ij}\}
defines a line bundle which is precisely the dual of the tangent bundle
T_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
The bundle
T_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
will be called the
canonical divisor
of
\mathcal{F}
, and we denote it by
K_{\mathcal{F}}:=T_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.

Alternatively, one can also define
\mathcal{F}
using one-forms instead of vector fields.
A foliation on
S
is characterized by a collection of one-forms

\omega_{i}\in\Omega^{1}_{S}(U_{i})
that posses only isolated zeros.
Moreover, there exists a function
f_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{S}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
whenever
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
satisfying the relation:

\omega_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}=f_{ij}\omega_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}.

(2.2)

The cocycle
\{f_{ij}\}
gives rise to a line bundle which is the conormal bundle
N_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
.
One can also translate this into an exact sequence as follows.

0\longrightarrow N_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\longrightarrow\Omega_{S}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}(K_{\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow 0.

For any surjective morphism
\Pi:\,\widetilde{S}\to S
from another smooth projective surface
\widetilde{S}
,
there exists a natural foliation
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
induced by pulling-back
\mathcal{F}
on
\widetilde{S}
.
Let
\{(U_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a collection
of local one-forms defining the foliation
\mathcal{F}
.
Initially, one may assume that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is simply given by
\{(\Pi^{-1}U_{i},\Pi^{*}\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
.
However, this is not always the case, as
\Pi^{*}\omega_{i}
may posses one-dimensional zeros.
Instead, the foliation
\Pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
is defined by the data

\{(V_{ij},\widetilde{\omega}_{ij})\},~i\in I,j\in J,

where
V_{ij}
is an open covering of
\widetilde{S}
such that
\Pi(V_{ij})\subseteq U_{i}
,
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{ij}=\frac{\Pi^{*}(\omega_{i})}{h_{ij}}
with
h_{ij}
being a holomorphic function over
V_{ij}
satisfying
\text{\rm{div\,}}(h_{ij})=\text{\rm{div\,}}\big(\Pi^{*}(\omega_{i})|_{V_{ij}}\big)
.
In particular, for any blowing-up
\sigma:\,\widetilde{S}\to S
centered at some point
p\in S
,
there is an induced foliation
\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
\widetilde{S}
,
such that the restricted foliations
(\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F})|_{\widetilde{S}\setminus E}\cong\mathcal{F}|_{S\setminus p}
under the isomorphism
\widetilde{S}\setminus E\cong S\setminus\{p\}
,
where
E
is the exceptional curve.

Suppose that
p
is a singular point of
\mathcal{F}
,
and that
v=A(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+B(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
is a local vector field defining
\mathcal{F}
.
The two eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
of the differential matrix

Dv:=\left(\begin{aligned} A_{x}~&~A_{y}\\
B_{x}~&~B_{y}\end{aligned}\right)

at the point
p
are uniquely determined up to multiplication by a non-zero constant,
where
A_{x}=\frac{\partial A(x,y)}{\partial x}
, and similar for other notations in the above matrix.

Definition 2.2
.

A singularity
p
of
\mathcal{F}
is called a
reduced singularity
if at least one of the two eigenvalues (say,
\lambda_{2}
) is not zero and the quotient
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}
is not a positive rational number.
The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be reduced if all its singularities are reduced.

It is worth noting that the quotient
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}
remains when
v
is multiplied by a nonvanishing holomorphic function.
Naturally, if
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
, one could consider the reciprocal quotient

\lambda^{-1}=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
instead,
but the condition
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
is equivalent to
\lambda^{-1}\not\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
.
With a slight abuse of notation due to the exchange
\lambda\leftrightarrow\lambda^{-1}
,
the complex number
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}
is referred to as the
eigenvalue
of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
, following
[
Bru04
]
.
A reduced singularity is called
non-degenerate
if both of the two eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are non-zero; otherwise it is called a

.
Given any foliation, one can obtain a reduced one through a sequence of blowing-ups:

Theorem 2.3

(Seidenberg,
[
Bru04
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
, there exists a sequence of blowing-ups
\pi:\,\widetilde{S}\to S
,
such that the induced foliation
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
\widetilde{S}
is reduced.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be reduced foliation on a smooth projective

An irreducible curve

-exceptional if

(i).

is an exceptional curve of first kind on

(ii).

the contraction of

to a point produces a new foliation

Definition 2.5
.

A foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
is called relatively minimal if the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is reduced and there is no
\mathcal{F}
-exceptional curve on

It is established that any foliated surface

has a relatively minimal model, cf.
[

.
We assume in the following that

is a relatively minimal foliation on a smooth projective surface

is pseudo-effective.
In fact, the canonical divisor

is pseudo-effective if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is not induced by a

.
Denote the Zariski decomposition of

is the nef part and

is the negative one.
By the Riemann-Roch theorem, we have

Moreover, McQuillan proved that the support of the negative part

is a disjoint union of maximal

-chains.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a relatively minimal foliation on a smooth projective surface

.
We say a curve

-chain if

(i).

(

An important way to produce foliations comes from fibrations on smooth projective surfaces.

Definition 2.8
.

A foliation
\mathcal{F}
is

A fibration

of curves

Given a fibration of curves

in fibers of

An irreducible curve

-invariant

Suppose that

-invariant, or more precisely every irreducible component of

to

Let

Around

As

2.2.
The birational invariants and the canonical map of a foliated surface

In this subsection, we review the birational invariants and the canonical map for a foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated surface, and let
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
be any reduced model, i.e.,
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is birational to
(S,\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is reduced.

(i)

For any integer
n\geq 1
, the
n
-th pluri-genus
p_{n}(\mathcal{F})
of
(S,\mathcal{F})
is defined to be

p_{n}(\mathcal{F}):=\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime},nK_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}).

When
n=1
, it is the geometric genus, and also denoted by
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})=p_{1}(\mathcal{F})
.

(ii)

The volume
\vol(\mathcal{F})
and the Kodaira dimension
\mathrm{Kod}(\mathcal{F})
are defined as follows.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \vol({\mathcal{F}})&\,=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\,p_{n}(\mathcal{F})}{n^{2}/2}=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\,\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime},nK_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})}{n^{2}/2};\\[3.0pt]
\mathrm{Kod}(\mathcal{F})&\,=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\infty,&\quad&\text{if~$p_{n}(\mathcal{F})=0$ for any $n\geq 1$},\\
&\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log p_{n}(\mathcal{F})}{\log n},&&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{aligned}\right.

By Seidenberg’s
Theorem 2.3
,
every foliated surface admits a reduced model by a sequence of blowing-ups.
Moreover, the above definitions are independent on the choice of the reduced model,
and they are all birational invariants for a foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
.
Indeed, suppose that there are two reduced models
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
and
(S^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
.
Since
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is birational to
(S^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
, there is a foliated surface
(\widetilde{S},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
with two birational morphisms
\rho^{\prime}:\,\widetilde{S}\to S^{\prime}

and
\rho^{\prime\prime}:\,\widetilde{S}\to S^{\prime\prime}
such that

(i)

the foliated surface
(\widetilde{S},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
is reduced;

(ii)

we have the isomorphism
(\rho^{\prime})^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\cong(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
.

\textstyle{\widetilde{S}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{\prime\prime}}
\textstyle{S^{\prime}}
\textstyle{S^{\prime\prime}}

Hence

K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}=(\rho^{\prime})^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}})+\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime},

where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
) is effective supported over curves contracted by
\rho^{\prime}
(resp.

).
In particular,

\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime},nK_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})=\dim H^{0}(\widetilde{S},nK_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}})=\dim H^{0}(S^{\prime\prime},nK_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}}).

Remark 2.12
.

Historically, one defined the pluri-genera using the divisor

on

directly in some literatures, i.e., defined them to be
\dim H^{0}(S,nK_{\mathcal{F}})
.
However, it turns out that such definitions behave not well in birational geometry;
they might change under the birational morphisms between foliated surfaces.
This is one of the reasons for us to define the pluri-genera (as well as the volume and the Kodaira dimension) over a reduced model.

Let
(S,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated surface, and let
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
be any reduced model.
The foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
is of general type if the
\mathcal{F}
is of general type, i.e., the Kodaira dimension
\mathrm{Kod}(\mathcal{F})=2
.
Our main purpose is to investigate the canonical map
\varphi=\varphi_{|K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|}

|K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|

for a foliation

of general type
if the geometric genus

\varphi:\,S^{\prime}\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}.

Composed with the birational map

, one may also view the canonical map

as a rational map mapping the surface

to the projective space:

\varphi:\,S\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}.

(2.5)

It is easy to see that the canonical map
\varphi
is independent on the choice of the reduced model
(S^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.
Equivalently, to study the birational geometry of the canonical map
\varphi
, we may assume that the foliated surface
(S,\mathcal{F})
is already reduced,
in which case, the canonical map

|K_{\mathcal{F}}|
.
By contracting possible

we may even assume that

\mathcal{F}
is relatively minimal as well.
Let

be the decomposition of the complete linear system
|K_{\mathcal{F}}|
, where
M
(resp.

) is the moving part (resp. fixed part).
Let
\rho:\,Y\to\Sigma
be the minimal desingularization of

,
where
\Sigma=\varphi(S)
is the image of

under the canonical map as in (

By a sequence of blowing-ups

, we obtain a well-defined morphism

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi\circ\sigma=\rho\circ\phi:\,\widetilde{S}\to\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1}

be as above. Then
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi_{|K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}|}
is just the map defined by the complete linear system
|K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}|
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}
is the induced reduced foliation on

Proof.

Since
\sigma
is composed of certain blowing-ups,
it follows that the map

is the same as the rational map

The canonical map is generically finite

In this section, we consider the case when the canonical map

\varphi:\,S\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1},

is generically finite, i.e., the image
\Sigma
is of dimension two.
In particular,
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})\geq 3
.
In

, we will prove some lower bounds
on the degree of a non-degenerate surface in a projective space.
Then we prove

and

.

3.1.
The degree of surfaces in projective spaces

In this subsection, we study the degree of a non-degenerate surface in projective spaces.
Here ”non-degenerate” means that such a surface is not contained in any hyperplane.
This is a classical topic, which goes back to the 19-th century, cf.
[
Noe70
]
.
The next proposition can be found in
[
Bea79
, Lemme 1.4 and Remarque 1.5]
, see also
[
GH78
, § 4.3]
.
We include a proof here for readers’ convenience.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a non-degenerate surface.

(i)

The degree of
\Sigma
satisfies that
\deg\Sigma\geq N-1
.
Moreover, if
\deg\Sigma=N-1
, then
\Sigma
is birational to a rational surface.

(ii)

Suppose that
\Sigma
is not ruled. Then
\deg\Sigma\geq 2(N-1)
.
Moreover, if
\deg\Sigma=2(N-1)
, then
\Sigma
is birational to a K3 surface.

Proof.

Let
\rho:\,Y\to\Sigma
be the minimal desingulariztion of
\Sigma
and
M=\rho^{*}(H)
,
where
H
is a hyperplane section.
Then
|M|
is base-point-free and
h^{0}(Y,M)\geq N+1
.
Let
C\in|M|
be a general element, and consider the exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\longrightarrow 0.

(3.1)

It follows that

h^{0}\big(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\big)\geq h^{0}\big(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C)\big)-h^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\geq(N+1)-1=N.

(3.2)

It follows that

\deg\Sigma=C^{2}=\deg\big(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\big)\geq h^{0}\big(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\big)-1\geq N-1.

Moreover, if the equality holds, then
C
is a smooth rational curve.
But
C\in|M|
is the pulling-back of a hyperplane section.
Hence
Y
is a rational surface.
This proves (i). In fact, one can find a more detailed description in
[
GH78
, Chapter 4.3]
).

It remains to prove (ii), and we assume that
Y
is not ruled in the following.
In particular,
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective.
Hence

\deg\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{C})-\deg\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)=\deg\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{Y})=K_{Y}\cdot C\geq 0.

According to the Clifford theorem,

\deg\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\geq 2h^{0}\big(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\big)-2.

(3.3)

Combining it with (
3.2
), one obtains that

\deg\Sigma=C^{2}=\deg\big(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\big)\geq 2(N-1).

Moreover, if the equality holds in the above inequality,
then both (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) are equalities.
Note that the curve
C
is not hyperelliptic, since the map
\varphi_{|M|}=\varphi_{|C|}

defined by the complete linear system
|M|=|C|
is birational.
In particular, from the Clifford theorem it follows that
\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{C})
,
equivalently

\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{Y})\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}.

In particular,
K_{Y}\cdot C=0
and the Kodaira dimension
\kappa(Y)=0
.
Let

\tau:\,Y\longrightarrow Y_{0},

be the contraction of
Y
to its minimal model, and
C_{0}
be the image of
C
.
Suppose

K_{Y}=\tau^{*}K_{Y_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}_{i},\qquad C=\tau^{*}C_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}.

Since
K_{Y}\cdot C=0
, it follows that
a_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
namely
C=\tau^{*}C_{0}
.
In particular, any possible
(-1)
-curve
E
on
Y
satisfying
C\cdot E=0
. Since
\rho:\,Y\to\Sigma
is the minimal desingularization,
and
C
is the pulling-back of some hyperplane section,
it follows that there is no
(-1)
-curve on
Y
, i.e.,
Y=Y_{0}
is minimal.
Consider now the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}-C)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}(K_{Y})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{Y})\longrightarrow 0.

Since the Kodaira dimension
\kappa(Y)=0
,
it follows that
K_{Y}
is numerically trivial, and hence

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}-C))=H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}-C))=0.

Therefore,

p_{g}(Y)=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(K_{Y}))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{Y}))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C})=1.

(3.4)

Taking the induced long exact sequence of the short exact sequence (
3.1
), one obtains

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C))\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C))=0.

The equality
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C))=0
follows the Mumford vanishing theorem,
since
C=K_{Y}+(C-K_{Y})
and
C-K_{Y}
is nef and big due to the numerical triviality of
K_{Y}
.
By the equality in (
3.2
),

q(Y)=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C))+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})-h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(C))=0.

Combining this with (
3.4
), one proves that
Y
is a K3 surface.
∎

When the surface
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is of general type, we improve the lower bound on the degree as follows.
In the following, by an
(a,b)
-surface

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

Lemma 3.3

\varphi_{|L|}
is generically finite and
\varphi_{|M|}
is birational,

and does not contained in the ramified divisor of
\varphi_{|L|}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective surface of general type. Then

(i)

The bi-canonical map
\varphi_{|2K_{Y}|}
is generically finite unless

\vol(Y)=1
and
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=1
.

(ii)

The triple-canonical map
\varphi_{|3K_{Y}|}
is always generically finite.

Proof.

The statements are birationally invariant, and hence we may assume that
Y
is minimal.
The first statement is proved in
[
Xia85b
]
, see also
[
BHPV04
, Theorem VII.7.6]
.
The proof of the second statement was kindly provided to the author by Professor Yifan Chen.
The argument is given by contradiction.
Let

|3K_{Y}|=|M|+Z,

where
M
and
Z
are respectively the moving and fixed parts of
|3K_{Y}|
.
Suppose that the triple-canonical map
\varphi_{|3K_{Y}|}
is composed with a pencil
\Lambda
.
Let
A\in\Lambda
be a general element, and suppose
M\sim_{num}\,aA
.
Then

a+1\geq h^{0}(Y,M)=h^{0}(Y,3K_{Y})=3K_{Y}^{2}+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y}).

Hence

3K_{Y}^{2}=K_{Y}(M+Z)\geq K_{Y}M=aK_{Y}A\geq\big(3K_{Y}^{2}+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})-1\big)K_{Y}A\geq(3K_{Y}^{2})K_{Y}A.

It follows that
K_{Y}A=1
, and the inequalities above are all equalities.
In particular,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=1
,
K_{Y}^{2}=1
and
a=3
.
By the Hodge index theorem,

A^{2}\leq 1
.
Since
Y
is of general type,

2\leq 2g(A)-2\leq(K_{Y}+A)A\leq 2,

where
g(A)
is the geometric genus of
A
.
Hence
K_{Y}^{2}=A^{2}=1
and
K_{Y}\sim_{num}A
.
In particular,
|A|
is a rational pencil with
h^{0}(Y,A)=2
.
Thus
2A=K_{Y}+(2A-K_{Y})
and
2A-K_{Y}\sim_{num}K_{Y}
is nef and big.
By the Riemann-Roch theorem and the vanishing theorem, it follows that

h^{0}(Y,2A)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})+\frac{2A(2A-K_{Y})}{2}=2.

On the other hand, since
h^{0}(Y,A)=2
,
it follows that

h^{0}(Y,2A)\geq 2h^{0}(Y,A)-1=3.

This gives a contradiction.
Hence
\varphi_{|3K_{Y}|}
is generically finite as required.
∎

Claim 3.14
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
are algebraically independent.
Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is reduced.

Proof of
Claim 3.14
.

Since the degree of

is

,

[

In fact, the singularities of

are all on the affine plane satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &x\big(\alpha+x^{d}+y^{d}\big)=0;\\
&y\big(\beta+y^{d-1}+x^{d}+y^{d}\big)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.22)

The differential matrix of the vector field

Dv=\left(\begin{aligned} (d+1)x^{d}+y^{d}+\alpha\quad&\quad\qquad\qquad dxy^{d-1}\\
dx^{d-1}y\qquad\qquad&\quad(d+1)y^{d}+dy^{d-1}+x^{d}+\beta\end{aligned}\right).

(3.23)

Let

be any singularity of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
and
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be the two eigenvalues of the differential matrix
(Dv)(p_{0})
at

.
To show that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is reduced at

, it suffices to show that
the eigenvalue
\lambda_{p_{0}}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}

(
up to the exchange
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\leftrightarrow\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
)

of

at

is not a positive rational number
if

are algebraically independent.
Let compute the eigenvalue

in the following.

(i)

If

and
y_{0}=0
, i.e.,

by (

(ii)

If

According to (
3.23

\lambda_{1}=-d\alpha

\lambda_{2}=\beta-\alpha
,
and hence
\lambda_{p_{0}}=\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha-\beta}
.

(iii)

If
x_{0}=0

\beta+y_{0}^{d-1}+y_{0}^{d}=0

3.22
).
According to (

Hence

(iv)

Finally, if
x_{0}\neq 0
and

y_{0}^{d-1}=\alpha-\beta,\quad\text{and}\quad x_{0}^{d}+y_{0}^{d}+\alpha=0.

According to (

(Dv)(p_{0})=\left(\begin{aligned} dx_{0}^{d}\qquad&\quad\qquad dx_{0}y_{0}^{d-1}\\
dx_{0}^{d-1}y_{0}\quad&\quad dy_{0}^{d}+(d-1)y_{0}^{d-1}\end{aligned}\right).

Hence

\lambda_{i}=\frac{-\big(d\alpha-(d-1)y_{0}^{d-1}\big)\pm\sqrt{\big((2d-1)\alpha-(d-1)\beta\big)^{2}+4d(d-1)(\alpha-\beta)^{2}y_{0}}}{2}.

Thus the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
at
p_{0}
is

\lambda_{p_{0}}=-1+\frac{\big(\alpha+(d-1)\beta\big)\,\sqrt{\big((2d-1)\alpha-(d-1)\beta\big)^{2}+4d(d-1)(\alpha-\beta)^{2}y_{0}}}{2d(d-1)(\alpha-\beta)\big(\alpha+(\alpha-\beta)y_{0}\big)}.

Since
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
are algebraically independent,
one sees that the eigenvalue of

at each singularity

is
\lambda_{p_{0}}\notin\mathbb{Q}
.
In particular,
p_{0}
is non-denegerate reduced for any singularity

of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
and hence
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is reduced as required.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on

Suppose that

\begin{aligned} H^{0}\big(S,T_{S}(T_{\mathcal{F}}^{*})\big)&\quad\otimes&&f^{*}H^{0}(B,\Omega^{1}_{B})&\longrightarrow\quad&H^{0}(S,K_{\mathcal{F}})\\

\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
Suppose on the contrary that

\mathcal{F}
-invariant.

is smooth at

.
Moreover, there exists a suitable local coordinate

around

such that

, and that the map

is given by

.
Suppose that

Since

-invariant, it follows that

As

it follows that

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on
S
whose canonical map induces a fibration
f:\,S\to B
as before.
Suppose that
K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F<2g(F)-2
, where
F
is a general fiber of
f
.
Let
F_{0}
be any fiber of
f:\,S\to B
.
Then there is at least one component
C\subseteq F_{0}
which is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
In particular, any smooth fiber of
f
is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a fiber
F_{0}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}C_{j}
, such that every component
C_{j}
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is reduced, the fiber
F_{0}
is normal crossing.
According to
Proposition 2.10
, for any component
C_{j}\subseteq F_{0}
,

Z(\mathcal{F},C_{j})=K_{\mathcal{F}}C_{j}-K_{S}C_{j}-C_{j}^{2}.

(4.6)

By definition, if
p\in C_{j}
is a singularity of
\mathcal{F}
, then
Z(\mathcal{F},C_{j},p)\geq 1
.
Hence

Z(\mathcal{F},C_{j})\geq\sum_{i\neq j}C_{i}C_{j}.

Combining this with (
4.6
),

\sum_{i\neq j}C_{i}C_{j}\leq K_{\mathcal{F}}C_{j}-K_{S}C_{j}-C_{j}^{2},\qquad\text{for any fixed~}1\leq j\leq r.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}C_{i}C_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{i\neq j}m_{j}C_{i}C_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(m_{j}K_{\mathcal{F}}C_{j}-m_{j}K_{S}C_{j}-m_{j}C_{j}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(K_{\mathcal{F}}-K_{S})\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}C_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}C_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=(K_{\mathcal{F}}-K_{S})\cdot F_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}C_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F-(2g(F)-2)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}C_{i}^{2}.

Since
K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F<2g(F)-2
by assumption, it follows that

0>K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F-(2g(F)-2)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j}C_{i}C_{j}+m_{i}C_{i}^{2}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{0}\cdot C_{i}=0.

This gives a contradiction.
The proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle\geq(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)\,M_{0}\cdot A+\frac{(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)^{2}\,Z_{0}\cdot A}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)^{2}\,(M_{0}+A)\cdot A}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})}+\frac{(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)^{2}\,Z_{0}\cdot A}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)^{2}\,(K_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}+A)\cdot A}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})}\geq\frac{d\,(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1)^{2}}{p_{g}(\mathcal{F})}

This proves (
1.13
).

(ii) Let
\epsilon:\,S\to X
be the contraction of all components of the negative part
N
in the Zariski decomposition of
K_{\mathcal{F}}
, and let
\overline{K}=\epsilon_{*}K_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Since
g(B)\geq 1
, the support of
N
is contained in fibers of
f
.
Hence there is an induced fibration
\bar{f}:\,X\to B
.
Since the canonical map
\varphi
induces the fibration
f:\,S\to B
,
one write
K_{\mathcal{F}}=M+Z
, where

M=f^{*}(L),\qquad\text{with~}~h^{0}(B,L)=p_{g}(\mathcal{F}).

Let
\Delta
be the effective divisor in (
4.4
),
and
\Delta_{h}\subseteq\Delta
(resp.
\Delta_{v}\subseteq\Delta
) consists of the horizontal (resp. vertical) components.
Since
f^{*}(D_{0})+\Delta_{h}+\Delta_{v}\in|K_{\mathcal{F}}|
for any effective divisor
D_{0}\in|K_{B}|
, it follows that
\Delta_{h}\subseteq Z
if

.
Hence

\overline{K}=\epsilon_{*}K_{\mathcal{F}}=\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\epsilon_{*}(\Delta)=\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v},

where
\overline{\Delta}_{v}=\epsilon_{*}(\Delta_{v})
consists of curves in fibers of the induced fibration
\bar{f}:\,X\to B
, and
\overline{D}_{j}
maps surjectively onto
B
for
1\leq j\leq m
.
As
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})=h^{0}(B,L)\geq g(B)
by (i) and
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})\geq 2
by assumption,
by the Riemann-Roch theorem for curves one has

\deg(L)\geq p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1+g(B).

(4.18)

Since
g(B)\geq 1
,
it follows that
\overline{D}_{j}
is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant for any
1\leq j\leq m
.
Thus by
[
CF18
, proposition 2.13]
(see also
[

, Lemma III.1.1]
),

0\leq(\overline{K}+\overline{D}_{j_{0}})\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}=(a_{j_{0}}+1)\overline{D}_{j_{0}}^{2}+\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}},\quad\forall\,1\leq j_{0}\leq m.

Hence

\displaystyle\overline{K}\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle=a_{j_{0}}\overline{D}_{j_{0}}^{2}+\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{a_{j_{0}}}{a_{j_{0}}+1}\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}+\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{j_{0}}+1}\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j\neq j_{0}}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\bar{f}^{*}(L)\cdot\overline{D}_{j_{0}}}{a_{j_{0}}+1}=\frac{\overline{D}_{j_{0}}\cdot F}{a_{j_{0}}+1}\,\deg(L).

By the Negativity Lemma,
P=\epsilon^{*}(\overline{K})
, where
P
is the positive part in the Zariski decomposition of
K_{\mathcal{F}}
as in (
2.3
).
Therefore,

\displaystyle\vol(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=P^{2}=\overline{K}^{2}=\overline{K}\cdot\Big(\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}+\overline{\Delta}_{v}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\overline{K}\cdot\bar{f}^{*}(L)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\overline{K}\cdot\overline{D}_{j}

\displaystyle\geq d\,\deg(L)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}\cdot F}{a_{j}+1}\,\deg(L)

\displaystyle=\frac{d(d+1)}{d+1}\,\deg(L)\geq\frac{d(d+1)}{d+1}\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1+g(B)\big).

We used the equality
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\overline{D}_{j}\cdot F=K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F=d
above,
and the last inequality follows from (
4.18
).
This proves (
1.14
).

(iii) Since
d=K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot F<2g(F)-2
,
by
Proposition 4.4
, either
g(B)=0
or
p_{g}(\mathcal{F})=g(B)\geq 2
.
It remains to prove (
1.15
) if

.
The proof is complete the same as that of (
1.14
) above.
It suffices to note that in this case

by (

),
and hence

\deg(L)=2\big(g(B)-1\big)=2\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1\big).

This proves (
1.15
).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on
S
whose canonical map induces a fibration

as before.

is contained in the fixed part

,

Proof.

By (

), we have decomposition

for any component

∎

Remark 4.7
.

Suppose that
h^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(K_{\mathcal{F}}))=1
. Then
g(F)\geq 1
, since

by (

).
If

, then by the Riemann-Roch theorem, one checks easily that

. Hence

and

apply in this case.
If

, then

again by the Riemann-Roch theorem.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of general type on
S
whose canonical map induces a fibration
f:\,S\to B
as before.
Suppose that
h^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(K_{\mathcal{F}}))=1
for a general fiber
F
of
f
.

(i)

If

, then

and

(ii)

If

, then

or

, and

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &3\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1\big),&\quad&\text{if~}d=1;\\[2.0pt]
&\frac{8}{3}\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1\big),&\quad&\text{if~}d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(iii)

, then

and

\vol(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{3}{2}\big(p_{g}(\mathcal{F})-1\big).

(4.21)

Proof.

(i) By

, one has

, then

.
Hence according to

,
Proposition 4.4
and their proofs,

.
Therefore,

p_{g}(\mathcal{F})=h^{0}(S,K_{\mathcal{F}})=h^{0}\big(B,\mathcal{O}_{B}(K_{B})\otimes f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(\Delta_{v})\big)=h^{0}\big(B,\mathcal{O}_{B}(K_{B})\big)=g(B).

In particular,

,
and (

) holds by

(ii).

(ii) Since

or

Hence by a similar argument as the proof of (

We leave the details to the readers.

(iii) If

is a section of

This completes the proof.
∎

Let

(2g+1)

Note that none of
C_{i}
’s intersects
C_{0}
for

Hence it defines a double cover
\pi_{0}:\,S_{0}\to Y

be the minimal desingularization, and

are semi-stable except these fibers over

{F_{i,2g+2}}
{F_{i,2}}

\displaystyle|K_{\mathcal{F}}|

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a reduced foliation of general type on
S
whose canonical map induces a fibration

is algebraically integrable and that the induced foliation

\ker(\mathrm{d}f:\,T_{\widetilde{S}}\to f^{*}T_{B})

.
Then for any fiber
F_{0}
of
f
, there is at least one component
C\subseteq F_{0}
which is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is reduced and algebraically integrable,
so is
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.
Hence there is a fibration
\tilde{f}:\,\widetilde{S}\to\widetilde{B}
, such that
every fiber of
\tilde{f}
is normal crossing, and that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
coincides with

.

is algebraically integrable, the

-invariant curves are just those curves contained in fibers of
\tilde{f}
.

F_{0}
of

, such that every component of

is
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant.
Then

is contained in some fiber

(i).

’s which are not
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant,

,

\mathcal{E}

Proof.

(i).
The first statement might be well-known to the experts; see for instance
[

We include a proof here to be self-contained.

Let
\{(U_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

From (

), it follows that

N_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}=\Pi^{*}(N_{\mathcal{F}}^{*})+\text{\rm{div\,}}(\Pi^{*}\omega),

where

(resp.

) is the conormal bundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}
),
and

Corollary A.3
.

Let
\Pi:\,\widetilde{S}\to S
be as in
Theorem A.1
, and
\mathcal{F}

Proof.

By
Theorem A.1
,

K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}=\Pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}})+\mathcal{E},

where
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
is supported on the curves which are contracted by
\Pi
.
Let

and
N
being respectively the nef and negative parts.

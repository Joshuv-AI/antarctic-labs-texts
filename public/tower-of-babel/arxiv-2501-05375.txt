Some factorization results for formal power series
Rishu Garg 1, Jitender Singh 2,†

Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain some factorization results on formal power series over principle ideal domains with sharp bounds on number of irreducible factors. These factorization results correspondingly lead to irreducibility criteria for formal power series. The information about prime factorization of the constant term up to a unit and that of some higher order terms is utilized for the purpose. Further, using theory of Newton polygons for power series, we extend the classical Dumas irreducibility criterion to formal power series over discrete valuation domains, which in particular, yields several irreducibility criteria.

1.
Introduction

Power series arise in several areas of mathematics such as in analysis, combinatorics, and algebra. In analysis, convergence of power series is of main interest. On the other hand, in combinatorics and algebra the algebraic properties of power series are of main interest. For a refreshing survey on algebraic theory of formal power series and applications, the reader may refer to the comprehensive reviews in
[
12
,

]
.

Throughout,
R
will denote a commutative ring with 1, and
R[[z]]
the ring of formal power series over
R
in the indeterminate
z
. A power series
f\in R[[z]]
is invertible if and only if so does
f(0)
in
R
. So, the question of factorization of
f
in the ring
R[[z]]
makes sense if
f(0)
is not a unit in
R
. In view of this, the rings of our interest are those rings
R
for which the power series ring
R[[z]]
is a unique factorization domain (UFD). If
R
is a principal ideal domain (PID), then
R[[z]]
is a UFD. However, many pathologies are known to exist in the power series ring
R[[z]]
in comparison to the corresponding polynomial ring
R[z]
. In view of this, if
R
is a UFD, then
R[[z]]
need not be a UFD (See
[
23
,
17
]
). Nevertheless, one can relate the ideal structure of the base ring with that of its power series ring. This can sometime be very useful in understanding factorization of power series over PIDs. We mention here that the power series rings have been the object of research in many contexts such as the dimension theory
[
13
,
14
,
2
,
3
]
, the valuation overrings, the quotient field, the integral dependence, the cancelation problem, the valuation domains, the root extension, the study of subrings, the factorization properties etc. (see for the detail
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and references cited therein).

One of the most important ring of formal power series is
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, which is a UFD. The irreducibility properties in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[z]
and the ring of formal power series
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
are generally not correlated. For example, for distinct prime numbers
p
and
q
, the polynomial
pq+z
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
but can be factored into two irreducible elements in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, although here, the explicit factorization is not straightforward. Likewise, the polynomial
p+(1+pq)z+qz^{2}
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
but factors as
(p+z)(1+qz)
in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. In fact some factorization properties of the ring
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
have been obtained in the paper
[
7
]
, wherein numerous irreducibility criteria are also witnessed. On these lines, some results on irreducibility and factorization of power series in the ring
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
have been obtained recently in
[
21
,
22
]
.

More generally, factorization properties and irreducibility of polynomials and formal power series over a PID
R
in the ring
R[[z]]
have been obtained in the paper
[
15
]
. In view of this, we have the following well known factorization result
[
7
,
15
]
.

Theorem
A
.

Let
R
be a principle ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}
is a nonzero nonunit. If
a_{0}
is a product of two nonassociate elements
m
and
n
in
R
, then
f
is not irreducible in
R[[z]]
.

We remark that the conclusion of Theorem
A
does not hold incase
R
is not a PID and
R[[z]]
is still a UFD (See
[
15
, Corollary 2.3(3)]
).

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Since
R
is a PID and
m
and
n
are not associates, the ideal generated by
m
and
n
is
R
. Consequently, there exist
u
and
v
in
R
for which
mu+nv=1
. Now let
g=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that the coefficients of
g
are defined recursively by

\displaystyle b_{0}=0;~b_{i}=a_{i}-uv\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{t}b_{i-t},~i=1,2,\ldots.

Then
f
admits a factorization
f(z)=(m+vg(z))(n+ug(z))
in
R[[z]]
.
∎

Theorem
A
tells us that the irreducibility in the UFD
R[[z]]
needs investigation only for those power series in which the constant term is an associate to a prime power. In view of this, if
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
is such that
|a_{0}|
is prime, then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. This result follows from the following irreducibility criterion proved in
[
15
]
.

Theorem
B
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}
is an associate to
p^{k}
for some prime
p
in
R
and a positive integer
k
. If
k=1
, or
p
does not divide
a_{1}
, then
f
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
.

As an application of Theorems
A
and
B
, we may have the following factorization result.

Theorem
C
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=u\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{k_{i}}
where
r\geq 1
,
u
is a unit in
R
;
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are pairwise nonassociate primes in
R
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are all positive integers. If
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are nonassociates, then
f
is a product of exactly
r
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

The case
r=1
reduces to one of the parts of Theorem
B
. So, we may assume that
r>1
. Let
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{r}
where
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
are units in
R
. By following the proof of Theorem
A
, we find that
f
factors as

\displaystyle f(z)=(u_{1}p_{1}^{k_{1}}+f_{1}(z))\cdots(u_{r}p_{r}^{k_{r}}+f_{r}(z)),

where
f_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{ij}z^{j}\in R[[z]]
with
b_{i0}=0
for
i=1,\ldots r
. Then we have

\displaystyle a_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Bigl(b_{i1}uu_{i}^{-1}\prod_{j\neq i}{p_{j}^{k_{j}}}\Bigr)\equiv b_{t1}uu_{t}^{-1}\prod_{j\neq t}{p_{j}^{k_{j}}}\mod p_{t},~t=1,\ldots,r,

which in view of the fact that
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are nonassociates tells us that
p_{t}
does not divide
b_{t1}
for every
t=1,\ldots,r
. By Theorem
B
,
g_{t}=u_{t}p_{t}^{k_{t}}+f_{t}
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
for each
t=1,\ldots,r
, and so,
f
is the product of
r
irreducible factors
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
in
R[[z]]
.
∎

In
[
5
]
, the authors characterized irreducibility of quadratic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, and reducibility of quadratic polynomials having integer coefficient in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
was shown to be equivalent to their reducibility in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z]
, the ring of polynomials over the
p
-adic integers. On these lines, the authors in
[
5
]
proved the following reducibility result for a class of higher degree polynomials.

Theorem
D
.

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be such that there exists a prime
p
and integers
n\geq 2
,
m\geq 1
, and
\gamma\geq 1
, such that
a_{0}=p^{n}
,
a_{1}=p^{m}\gamma
, where
p\nmid\gamma
,
p\nmid a_{i}
for all
i\geq 2
.

(i)

If
n>2m
, then
f
is reducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
as well as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z]
.

(ii)

If
n\leq 2m
, then
f
is reducible in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
if and only if
f
has a root
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
v_{p}(\rho)\geq 1
.

Recently using Newton polyhedron approach the authors in
[
10
]
proved an irreducibility criterion for testing irreducibility of polynomials in one variable over the power series ring
\mathbb{K}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]
in
n
variables over a field
\mathbb{K}
.

It is worthwhile to mention that the classical irreducibility criteria for testing irreducibility of polynomials having integer coefficients due to Schönemann, Eisenstein, Dumas, Perron and others are available in the literature (see
[
27
]
for a survey on this topic). However, devising of irreducibility criteria for formal power series over integers has remained largely missing in the past literature. This lack of availability of irreducibility criteria in abundance makes it difficult for testing irreducibility of formal power series over integers and over more general domains. This serves as motivation for our present work. So, the aim of this paper is to contribute factorization results and devise new irreducibility criteria for some classes of formal power series over PIDs and some classes of formal power series over discrete valuation domains. In devising our results, we combine the information on factorization properties of the constant term of the underlying power series with the prime factorization of one or more higher order coefficients. Further, we extend the classical Dumas irreducibility criterion
[
9
]
to formal power series using Newton polygon arguments, which in particular yields a couple of new irreducibility criteria for formal power series.

The paper is organized as follows. The main results of this paper are stated and proved in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we provide some explicit examples of formal power series whose factorization properties may be deduced from our results.

2.
Main results and proofs

We recall the prime counting functions
\omega(n)
and
\Omega(n)
for a nonzero nonunit
n
in a UFD
R
, which respectively denote the number of pairwise nonassociate prime divisors of
n
and the number of pairwise nonassociate prime divisors of
n
counted with multiplicity. If
R
is a PID and
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
, then we define
\Omega_{f}
as the number of irreducible factors of
f
in
R[[z]]
counted with multiplicity. Using these definitions, we have the following factorization result which provides us an estimate about the number of irreducible factors of a given power series in the UFD
R[[z]]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. If
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
is such that
a_{0}
is a nonzero nonunit in
R
, then

\displaystyle\omega(a_{0})\leq\Omega_{f}\leq\Omega(a_{0}).

In particular, if
a_{0}
is an associate to a prime in
R
, then
f
is irreducible.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
f(z)=f_{1}(z)\cdots f_{r}(z)
be a product of
r
irreducible factors
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
in
R[[z]]
so that
r=\Omega_{f}
. Then
0\neq a_{0}=f_{1}(0)\cdots f_{r}(0)
. This in view of the fact that
f_{i}
is a nonzero nonunit in
R[[z]]
for each
i
enforces
r\leq\Omega(a_{0})
.

To obtain the remaining inequality, we make the use of Theorem
A
as follows. If
r=1
, then
f
is irreducible. So, by Theorem
A
, we find that
a_{0}
is associate to a prime power. Consequently,
\omega(a_{0})=1=\Omega_{f}
so that the theorem holds for
r=1
. Thus, we may assume that
r>1
. Suppose on the contrary that
r<\omega(a_{0})
. Since
a_{0}=f_{1}(0)\cdots f_{r}(0)
and each
f_{i}(0)
is nonzero nonunit in
R
, and since
2\leq r<\omega(a_{0})
, it follows that one of the
r
factors
f_{1}(0)
,
\ldots
,
f_{r}(0)
is not associate to a prime power. Suppose that
f_{\alpha}(0)
is not a prime power for some index
\alpha
, where we know that
f_{\alpha}(0)
is nonunit in
R
, since
f_{\alpha}
is a nonunit in
R[[z]]
. By Theorem
A
,
f_{\alpha}
is not irreducible in
R[[z]]
. This is a contradiction, since each of
f_{1}
,
\ldots
,
f_{r}
was assumed to be irreducible in
R[[z]]
.
∎

The lower bound
\omega(a_{0})
in Theorem
1
was proved earlier in
[
15
, Corollary 2.5]
but our approach is different. We note that Theorem
1
recovers one of the irreducibility criteria of the paper
[
7
]
. The bounds mentioned in Theorem
1
for the number of irreducible factors of
f
are best possible in the sense that there exist power series attaining these bounds. In view of this, we have the following corollary to Theorem
1
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
R
be a principle ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}
is nonzero nonunit. If
a_{0}
is square free, then
f
is a product of exactly
\omega(a_{0})
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

By Theorem
1
, we have
\omega(a_{0})\leq\Omega_{f}\leq\Omega(a_{0})
. Since
a_{0}
is square free, we must have
\omega(a_{0})=\Omega(a_{0})
. So, we have
\omega(a_{0})=\Omega_{f}=\Omega(a_{0})
.
∎

Our next factorization result provides an upper bound on the number of irreducible factors of a given nonzero nonunit in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
in connection with the prime factorization of the constant and a higher order coefficient.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}
for some positive integers
k
and a prime
p
. Suppose there exists an index
j\geq 1
for which
p
does not divide
a_{j}
. Then
f
is a product of at most
\min\{k,j\}
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. In particular, if
k=1
or
j=1
, then
f
is irreducible.

The upper bound on the number of irreducible factors of
f
as mentioned in Theorem
3
is best possible as follows from the following example.

For any prime number
p
and a positive integer
n
, the power series
(p-z)^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
is a product of exactly
n
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, since
p-z
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, where
p
does not divide the coefficient of
z^{n}
in
(p-z)^{n}
so that here,
j=n
.

Observe that if the polynomial

\displaystyle p^{n}+p^{m}\gamma z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots+a_{d}z^{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]

satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
D
such that
f

is reducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, then by Theorem
3
,
f
is a product of exactly two irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

We will now state and prove a more general form of Theorem
3
as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain with a uniformizing parameter
\pi
for
R
. Let

f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=u\pi^{k}
for some unit
u\in R
and positive integers
k
. Suppose there exists a nonnegative integer
\ell
and an index
j\geq 1
such that
\pi^{\ell}
divides
a_{j}
but
\pi^{\ell+1}
does not divide
a_{j}
. Then
f
is a product of at most
\min\{k,j+\ell\}
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
. In particular, if
k=1
or
j=1
and
\ell=0
, then
f
is irreducible.

Theorem
3
corresponds to the case
\ell=0
of Theorem
4
with
R=\mathbb{Z}
,
\pi=p
, and
v=v_{p}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Z}
. Further, Theorem
B
is precisely the case
k=j=1=\ell+1
of Theorem
4
.

In view of Theorem
4
, the power series
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
with
a_{0}=u\pi^{k}
is a product of at most

\displaystyle\min\{k,~v(a_{1})+1,~v(a_{2})+2,~\ldots\}

irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
f(z)=f_{1}(z)\cdots f_{r}(z)
be a product of
r
irreducible factors
f_{i}
in
R[[z]]
,
1\leq i\leq r
. Assume without loss of generality that each
f_{i}
is nonconstant. Since
f_{i}(0)
is a nonzero nonunit, we have
v(f_{i}(0))\geq 1
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. Sice
\pi
is a uniformizing parameter for
R
, we have
v(\pi)=1
and
f_{i}(0)=u_{i}\pi^{k_{i}}
for some unit
u_{i}\in R
and positive integer
k_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
. Since we have

\displaystyle u\pi^{k}=f(0)=f_{1}(0)\cdots f_{r}(0),

it follows that

\displaystyle k=v(u\pi^{k})=v(f_{1}(0))+\cdots+v(f_{r}(0))=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r},

which in view of the fact that
k_{i}\geq 1
for each

establishes

.

In the preceding paragraph, we have shown that
r\leq k
. In view of this, we may assume without loss of generality that
k\geq j+\ell
. Assume on the contrary that
r>j+\ell
, that is,
r-j>\ell
. If we write

\displaystyle f_{i}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}a_{it}z^{t}\in R[[z]],

and substitute for
f_{i}
in the equation

, then we find that

\displaystyle a_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{r}=j}a_{1i_{1}}a_{2i_{2}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}},

where the indices under the summation sign satisfy
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\leq j
. Since
r-j>\ell\geq 0
, we have
r>j
. Consequently, for any given
r

i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
with
0\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\leq j
and
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}=j
, at least

of the indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
is equal to 0. Since
f_{\alpha}(0)=a_{\alpha 0}
and
v(f_{\alpha}(0))\geq 1
for each index

, it follows that
v(a_{1i_{1}}a_{2i_{2}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}})\geq r-j
for each such term. We then arrive at the following:

\displaystyle v(a_{j})=v\Bigl(\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{r}=j}a_{1i_{1}}a_{2i_{2}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}}\Bigr)\geq\min_{\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{r}=j}}\Bigl\{v(a_{1i_{1}}a_{2i_{2}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}})\Bigr\}\geq r-j>\ell

which contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

Our next result is the following irreducibility criterion.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
k\geq 2
. Suppose there exists a positive integer
m
for which

\displaystyle p^{\ell}\mid a_{(k-\ell)m+i},~p^{\ell+1}\nmid a_{(k-\ell)m+i},

for every
i=1,\ldots,m
, and for every
\ell=1,\ldots,k
. If
p
does not divide
a_{km+1}
, then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

In particular, Theorem
5
for the case
k=2
reduces to one of the irreducibility criteria proved in
[
7
]
. The cases
k=3,4,5
of Theorem
5
were proved recently in the paper
[
22
]
.

In
[
16
]
, the authors proved two irreducibility criteria for polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
. In an attempt to extend these irreducibility results to the ring of power series, we could obtain the following result.

Theorem 6
.

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}
for some positive integers
k\geq 2
and a prime
p
. Suppose there exists an index

for which

divides
\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1})
and

does not divide

. If
\gcd(k,j)=1
, then

is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

The following analogue of Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion for power series is immediate from Theorem

.

Corollary 7

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}
for a prime
p
and positive integer

. Suppose there exists a positive integer

for which
p^{k}
divides
a_{i}
for each

, and

does not divide

. Then

is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

In the sequel, we will prove a generalization of Theorems
5
&
6
in a somewhat more general set up (See Theorem
8
). To do so, we will need Newton polygon technique for power series. Here, we describe in brief, one such technique developed by Hoffmann
[
8
]
for formal power series over a field
\mathbb{K}
with a valuation
v:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
for which
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq v(\mathbb{K})
. We proceed as follows.

Definition

(Hoffmann
[
8
]
)
.

For any integer
n
, let
f=\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{K}((z))
be a Laurent series. For each
i
with
a_{i}\neq 0
, let

L_{i}=\{(i,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}~|~y\geq v(a_{i})\}
.
If
a_{i}=0
, we define
L_{i}=\emptyset
. Let
L
be the convex hull of the set
\cup_{i=n}^{\infty}L_{i}
. The boundary
\partial L
of
L
will be called the Newton polygon
NP(f)
of
f
.

Definition

(Hoffmann
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\bar{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
and
\bar{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. For
f\in\mathbb{K}[[z]]
, let
f^{*}:\bar{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{N}}
be such that if
L
is a segment of
NP(f)
with finite slope
s
, and if
\ell
is the length of the projection of
L
along
x
-axis, then
f^{*}(s)=\ell
. If
s\in\mathbb{R}
is not taken up as a slope of any segment of
NP(f)
, then we set
f^{*}(s)=0
. Further, we take
f^{*}(-\infty)=0
unless
NP(f)
consists of only
y
-axis in which case, we take
f^{*}(-\infty)=\infty
. Finally, we define
f^{*}(\infty)=0
unless
f
is a polynomial in which case we take
f^{*}(\infty)=\infty
.

We observe that if
f
factors as
f=gh
in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, then a segment in
NP(f)
of slope
s
can be recovered by translation of segments in
NP(g)
and
NP(h)
if and only if

\displaystyle f^{*}(s)=(gh)^{*}(s)=g^{*}(s)+h^{*}(s).

Dumas Theorem
[
9
]
in general fails for Newton polygons for power series. This can be seen from the following example:

If we take for a prime
p
,
v=v_{p}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}
, and if we take

\displaystyle g=p-pz,~h=p+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p(1-p)z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]],

then
gh=p^{2}-p^{3}z
, and we have
g^{*}(0)=1
,
h^{*}(0)=\infty
, but
(gh)^{*}(0)=0
.

However, we have the following result of Hoffmann (see
[
8
, Theorem 1]
), which to some extent extends Dumas Theorem to power series, and this will serve our purpose.

Theorem
E

(Hoffmann
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field with a valuation
v
for which
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq v(\mathbb{K})
. Let
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[[z]]
and suppose that
S=S(f)\leq S(g)
, where
S(f)=\sup\{s\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}~|~f^{*}(s)\neq 0\}
, with a similar meaning for
S(g)
. Then each of the following statements holds.

(i)

(fg)^{*}(t)=f^{*}(t)+g^{*}(t)
for all
t<S
.

(ii)

If
f^{*}(t)+g^{*}(t)\neq 0
for infinitely many
t<S
, then
(fg)^{*}(t)=0
for all
t\geq S
.

(iii)

Suppose that
f^{*}(t)+g^{*}(t)\neq 0
for only finitely many
t<S
and suppose further that if
S<\infty
and
f^{*}(S)\neq 0
(in which case necessarily
f^{*}(S)=\infty
) then
g^{*}(S)=0
. Then
(fg)^{*}(S)=\infty
and
(fg)^{*}(t)=0
for all
t>S
.

Note that any nonzero
f\in\mathbb{K}[[z]]
is invertible if and only if
f(0)
is a unit in
\mathbb{K}
, that is,
f(0)\neq 0
. Thus, the only irreducible elements in
\mathbb{K}[[z]]
are associates of
z
.

Now we have the following generalization of Theorems
5
&
6
which may be viewed as an extension of Dumas irreducibility criterion
[
9
]
to formal power series.

Theorem 8
.

Let
(R,v)
be a discrete valuation domain with a uniformizing parameter
\pi
for
R
. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=u\pi^{k}
for some unit
u\in R
and a positive integer
k
. Suppose there exists a positive integer
n
for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v(a_{n})=0
and
\gcd(k,n)=1
,

(ii)

\frac{k}{n}<\frac{v(a_{i})}{n-i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Then
f
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be the field of fraction of
R
. Then
v
extends to
\mathbb{K}
with the valuation ring
R
, that is,
R=\{x\in\mathbb{K}~|~v(x)\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}
. We recall that
v(\pi)=1
and every element of
\mathbb{K}
and hence of
R
is an associate to a power of
\pi
. Consequently,
\pi
generates a prime ideal in
R
, and so,
\pi
is a prime element in
R
.

Now suppose on the contrary that
f(z)=g(z)h(z)
for nonunits
g,h\in R[[z]]
. If we let
g=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}z^{i}
and
h=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{i}z^{i}
, then we must have
v(b_{0})>0
and
v(c_{0})>0
. By the hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\frac{v(a_{n})-v(a_{0})}{n-0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{n}>-\frac{v(a_{i})}{n-i}=\frac{v(a_{n})-v(a_{i})}{n-i},~i=1,\ldots,n-1,

which shows that
NP(f)
consists of exactly one edge of negative slope
-k/n
joining the vertices
(0,k)
and
(n,0)
, so that
f^{*}(-k/n)=n
. Since
\pi
does not divide
a_{n}
, there exist smallest indices
r
and
s
for which
v(b_{r})=0=v(c_{s})
and
r+s=n
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the least indices for which

\displaystyle\frac{v(b_{\alpha})}{r-\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq r-1}\Bigl\{\frac{v(b_{t})}{r-t}\Bigr\},~\frac{v(c_{\beta})}{s-\beta}=\min_{0\leq t\leq s-1}\Bigl\{\frac{v(c_{t})}{s-t}\Bigr\},

so that the segment joining the lattice points
(\alpha,v(b_{\alpha}))
and
(r,0)
is the rightmost edge of
NP(g)
of negative slope, and so, we have
S=S(g)\geq 0
and
0\leq g^{*}(t)\leq r
for all
t
with
-\infty\leq t<0
. Similarly, the segment joining the lattice points
(\beta,v(c_{\beta}))
and
(s,0)
is the rightmost edge of
NP(h)
of negative slope so that
S(h)\geq 0
and
0\leq h^{*}(t)\leq s
for all
t
with
-\infty\leq t<0
. Assume that
S(g)\leq S(h)
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0\leq g^{*}(t)+h^{*}(t)\leq r+s,~\text{for}~-\infty\leq t<0.

By Theorem
E
(i), we have

\displaystyle f^{*}(t)=(gh)^{*}(t)=g^{*}(t)+h^{*}(t),~\text{for}~-\infty\leq t<S.

In particular, for
\eta=-k/n
, we have

\displaystyle n=f^{*}(\eta)=(gh)^{*}(\eta)=g^{*}(\eta)+h^{*}(\eta),

which shows that
g^{*}(\eta)+h^{*}(\eta)=n=r+s
. This in view of the fact that
g^{*}(\eta)\leq r
and
h^{*}(\eta)\leq s
enforces
g^{*}(\eta)=r
and
h^{*}(\eta)=s
. This is possible if and only if

\alpha=0=\beta
, since
r+s=n
. Consequently,
v(b_{\alpha})=v(b_{0})>0
and
v(c_{\beta})=v(c_{0})>0
, which shows that
v(b_{0})<k
and
v(c_{0})<k
. Let
v(b_{0})=\ell
so that
v(c_{0})=k-\ell
. We then arrive at the following:

\displaystyle\eta=-\frac{k}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\ell}{r}=-\frac{k-\ell}{n-r},

which is impossible, since
\ell<k
and
\gcd(k,n)=1
.
∎

Taking
R=\mathbb{Z}
and
v=v_{p}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Z}
in Theorem
8
, we now proceed to prove Theorems
5
&
6
as follows:

To prove Theorem
5
, we observe by the hypothesis that
NP(f)
has exactly one edge of negative slope
-k/(km+1)
joining the vertices
(0,k)
and
(km+1,0)
where we note that
\gcd(k,km+1)=1
. By Theorem
8
,
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

To prove Theorem
6
, we observe by the hypothesis that
NP(f)
has exactly one edge of negative slope
-k/j
joining the vertices
(0,k)
and
(j,0)
where it is given that
\gcd(k,j)=1
. By Theorem
8
,
f
is irreducible.

Remark
.

The hypothesis (ii) in Theorem
8
implies that

\displaystyle v(a_{i})\geq\Big\lceil\frac{k}{n}(n-i)\Big\rceil.

If we take
n=km+1
for a positive integer
m
, then in order that the aforementioned condition is satisfied by
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
for which
a_{0}=u\pi^{k}
, one must have

\displaystyle v(a_{(k-\ell)m+i})\geq\Big\lceil\frac{k}{km+1}(km+1-((k-\ell)m+i))\Big\rceil=\Big\lceil\ell-\frac{i}{m}+\frac{k-\ell+\frac{i}{m}}{km+1}\Big\rceil=\ell,

for all
\ell
and
i
satisfying
1\leq\ell\leq k
and
1\leq i\leq m
. Thus the condition

\displaystyle v(a_{(k-\ell)m+i})\geq\ell

in Theorem
6
is the best possible lower bound on respective coefficients in accordance with Theorem
8
to deduce irreducibility of
f
. This observation may be used to explain the calculations made in
[
22
]
for obtaining the irreducibility criteria for
f
over integers for the cases
k=3,4,5
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain and
\mathbb{K}
, the field of fraction of
R
. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}
is an associate to a prime power
p^{k}
in
R
, where
k
is a positive integer. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the prime ideal generated by
p
in
R
, and let
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the localization of
R
by
\mathfrak{p}
. If
f
is irreducible in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[[z]]
, then
f
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
.

Proof.

Since
a_{0}
is associate to
p^{k}
, it follows that
f
is a nonzero nonunit in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[[z]]
. Let
v
be the discrete valuation on
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
with the uniformizing parameter
p
for
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which extends to
\mathbb{K}
with the valuation ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We will prove the contrapositive statement to the conclusion of the Lemma
9
. So, let
f
factors as
f(z)=g(z)h(z)
for nonzero nonunits
g,h\in R[[z]]
. Then
up^{k}=f(0)=g(0)h(0)
, which in view of the fact that each of
g
and
h
is a nonunit in
R[[z]]
tells us that
g(0)=u_{1}p^{k_{1}}
and
h(0)=u_{2}p^{k_{2}}
, where
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are units in
R
with
u_{1}u_{2}=u
and
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are positive integers with
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
. This proves that each of
g
and
h
is a nonzero nonunit in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[[z]]
, and so,
f
factors in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[[z]]
.
∎

As an application of Theorem
8
and Lemma
9
, we have the following factorization result.

Theorem 10
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=up_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
,
r\geq 2
, where
u
is a unit in
R
,
p_{1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{r}
are
r
pairwise nonassociate primes in
R
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are all positive integers. Let
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
be the localization of
R
by the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
generated by the prime
p_{i}
with discrete valuation
v_{i}
with
p_{i}
as a uniformizing parameter for
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
for each
i=1,\ldots r
. Each
v_{i}
extends to the field of fraction
\mathbb{K}
of
R
with valuation ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
. Suppose that for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a positive integer
n_{i}
for which the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

v_{i}(a_{n_{i}})=0
and
\gcd(k_{i},n_{i})=1
,

(ii)

\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}<\frac{v_{i}(a_{t})}{n_{i}-t}
for each
t=1,\ldots,n_{i}-1
.

Then
f
is a product of exactly
r
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

By following the proof of Theorem
A
, we find that
f
factors as
f(z)=f_{1}(z)\cdots f_{r}(z)
, where
f_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{ij}z^{j}\in R[[z]]
with
b_{i0}=u_{i}p_{i}^{k_{i}}
for some unit
u_{i}
in
R
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
so that
u_{1}\cdots u_{r}=u
. In view of this, it will be sufficient to prove that each
f_{i}
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
.

By the hypothesis, we observe that
(R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}},v_{i})
is a discrete valuation domain with uniformizing parameter
p_{i}
for
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
and
f_{i}
is a nonzero nonunit in
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}[[z]]
for each
i
. We will prove that
f_{i}
is irreducible in
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}[[z]]
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. We proceed as follows.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
f^{*_{i}}
denotes
f^{*}
for
NP(f,p_{i})
with a similar meaning for
f_{i}^{*_{j}}
to be
f_{i}^{*}
for
NP(f_{i},p_{j})
. We observe that for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, if
i\neq j
, then
f_{i}^{*_{j}}(s)=0
for all
s<0
. So, in particular, taking
s=-k_{i}/n_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we have by Theorem
E
that

\displaystyle n_{i}=f^{*_{i}}(-k_{i}/n_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{*_{i}}(-k_{i}/n_{i})+\cdots+f_{r}^{*_{i}}(-k_{i}/n_{i})=f_{i}^{*_{i}}(-k_{i}/n_{i}),

which proves that
NP(f_{i},p_{i})
has a segment of slope
-k_{i}/n_{i}
joining the vertices
(0,k_{i})
and
(n_{i},0)
, that is,
v(b_{in_{i}})=0
and
\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}<\frac{v_{p_{i}}(b_{it})}{n_{i}-t}
for each
t=1,\ldots,n_{i}-1
. Since
\gcd(k_{i},n_{i})=1
, by Theorem
8
,
f_{i}
is irreducible in
R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}[[z]]
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. By Lemma
9
,
f_{i}
is irreducible in
R[[z]]
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
.
∎

The following two factorization results are immediate from Theorem
10
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=up_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
,
r\geq 2
, where
u
is a unit in
R
;
p_{1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{r}
are
r
pairwise nonassociate primes in
R
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are all positive integers. Suppose that for each
\ell=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a positive integer
n_{\ell}
for which the following hold.

\displaystyle p_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}\mid a_{i},~i=1,\ldots,n_{\ell}-1;~p_{\ell}\nmid a_{n_{\ell}};~\gcd(k_{\ell},n_{\ell})=1.

Then
f
is a product of exactly
r
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

Theorem 12
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}z^{i}\in R[[z]]
be such that
a_{0}=up_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
,
r\geq 2
, where
u
is a unit in
R
;
p_{1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{r}
are
r
pairwise nonassociate primes in
R
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are all positive integers. Suppose that for each
j=1,\ldots,r
, there exists a least positive integer
m_{j}
for which the following hold.

\displaystyle p_{j}^{\ell}\mid a_{(k_{j}-\ell)m_{j}+i},~p_{j}^{\ell+1}\nmid a_{(k_{j}-\ell)m_{j}+i},~p_{j}\nmid a_{k_{j}m_{j}+1},

for all
i=1,\ldots,m_{j}
and
\ell=1,\ldots,k_{j}
. Then
f
is a product of exactly
r
irreducible factors in
R[[z]]
.

3.
Examples

In this section, we will exhibit some explicit examples of formal power series whose factorization properties may be deduced from the results proved in the preceding Section.

Example 1
.

Let
p
be a prime number. By Theorem
1
, the linear polynomial
p+z
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. Therefore, for any positive integer
n\geq 2
, the formal power series

f_{1}=(p+z)^{n}
is a product of exactly
n
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. Since
f_{1}(0)=p^{n}
, by Theorem
1
we have
1\leq\Omega_{f_{5}}\leq n
. So, the polynomial
f_{1}
attains upper bound mentioned in Theorem
1
.

Example 2
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. For a prime number
p
and positive integers
k
and
j
with
j<k
, the power series

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{k}\pm z^{j}+z^{j+1}g(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]

satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3
. Therefore,
f_{2}
is a product of at most
\min\{k,j\}=j
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. If
j=1
, then
f_{2}
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

Example 3
.

For a prime
p
, the localization
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
of
\mathbb{Z}
by the prime ideal
(p)
in the ring
\mathbb{Z}
is a discrete valuation domain with the discrete valuation
v
with
v(p)=1
. Now in view of Theorem
4
, for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[z]]
and a positive integer
k
, the power series

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p^{k}}{p+1}+pz+pz^{2}+\cdots+pz^{k-1}+(p+1)z^{k}+z^{k+1}g(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[z]]

is a product of at most
\min\{k,2,3,4,\ldots,k\}=2
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[z]]
.

Example 4
.

For a positive integer
k\geq 2
, the power series

\displaystyle f_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm p^{k}+(p^{k}z+p^{k-1}z^{2}+\cdots+pz^{k})+z^{k+1}(1+z+z^{2}+\cdots)\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]],

satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
5
for
m=1
, and so,
f_{4}
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

Example 5
.

For positive integers
k
and
j
with
\gcd(k,j)=1
, the power series

\displaystyle f_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pm p^{k}+z^{j})(1+z+\cdots+z^{j-1}+\cdots)\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]].

can be expressed as
f_{5}=\pm p^{k}(1+z+\cdots+z^{j-1}+\cdots)+z^{j}(1+z+\cdots+z^{j-1}+\cdots)
. This
shows that
f_{5}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
6
. So,
f_{5}
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
. Since
(1+z+\cdots+z^{j-1}+\cdots)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
, the polynomial
\pm p^{k}+z^{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
being an associate of
f_{5}
must also be irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[[z]]
.

Example 6
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[y]
with the degree valuation
v_{\infty}
, that is,
v_{\infty}(0)=\infty
, and for any nonzero polynomial
a\in\mathbb{K}[y]
,
v_{\infty}(a)=-\deg a
. Then
(\mathbb{K}[y],v_{\infty})
is a discrete valuation domain with any linear polynomial in
\mathbb{K}[y]
as a uniformizing parameter for
\mathbb{K}[y]
. The valuation
v_{\infty}
extends to the field of fraction
\mathbb{K}(y)
of
\mathbb{K}[y]
by defining
v_{\infty}(a/b)=\deg b-\deg a
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{K}[y]
with
b\neq 0
. Now consider the power series
f_{6}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(y)z^{i}\in(\mathbb{K}[y])[[z]]
for which
a_{0}=(1+y)^{k}u
for nonzero
u\in\mathbb{K}
and positive integer
k
. Suppose there exists a natural number
n
such that

(i)

a_{n}\in\mathbb{K}
and
\gcd(k,n)=1
,

(ii)

\frac{k}{n}>\frac{\deg(a_{i})}{n-i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Then by Theorem
8
, the power series
f_{6}
is irreducible in
(\mathbb{K}[y])[[z]]
. In particular, if we take
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
, we see that the bivariate polynomial

\displaystyle(1+y)^{8}+(1+y)^{4}z+(1+y)^{2}z^{2}+yz^{3}\in(\mathbb{Q}[y])[[z]]

is irreducible.

Moreover, one can obtain factorization results for the formal power series over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[y]
parallel to Theorems
8
-
12
by taking
R=\mathbb{K}[y]
and
v=v_{\infty}
.

Example 7
.

We recall that the ring of Gaussian integers
\mathbb{Z}[\iota]
is a PID, and so, the power series ring
(\mathbb{Z}[\iota])[[z]]
is a UFD. The set of units of
\mathbb{Z}[\iota]
is
\{\pm 1,\pm\iota\}
. An element
a+\iota b\in\mathbb{Z}[\iota]
is prime if and only if either
ab=0
and one of
a
and
b
is a prime number of the form
4m+3
, or
ab\neq 0
and
a^{2}+b^{2}
is a prime number satisfying
a^{2}+b^{2}\not\equiv 0,3\mod 4
. Now for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}[\iota][[z]]
and any positive integer
k
, we consider the power series

\displaystyle f_{7}=19^{k}(4+3\iota)u+4z^{k-1}+z^{k}g(z)\in(\mathbb{Z}[\iota])[[z]],

where
u
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}[\iota]
. Note that
19
and
4+3\iota
are nonassociate gaussian prime.
The power series
f_{7}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
11
with
a_{0}=19^{k}(4+3\iota)u
,
r=2
,
k_{1}=k
,
k_{2}=1
,
n_{1}=k-1=n_{2}
so that
\gcd(k_{1},n_{1})=1=\gcd(k_{2},n_{2})
. So,
f_{7}
is a product of exactly
2
irreducible factors in
(\mathbb{Z}[\iota])[[z]]
.

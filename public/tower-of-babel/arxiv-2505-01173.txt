Dual canonical bases and embeddings of symmetric spaces
Huanchen Bao, Jinfeng Song

Abstract
. For a connected reductive group G k G_{k} over an algebraically closed field k k of char ≠ 2 \neq 2 and a fixed point subgroup K k K_{k} under an algebraic group involution, we construct a quantization and an integral model of any affine embeddings of the symmetric space G k / K k G_{k}/K_{k} . We show that the coordinate ring of any affine embedding of G k / K k G_{k}/K_{k} admits a dual canonical basis. We further construct an integral model for the canonical embedding (that is, an embedding which is complete, simple, and toroidal) of G k / K k G_{k}/K_{k} . When G k G_{k} is of adjoint type, we obtain an integral model for the wonderful compactification of the symmetric space.

Introduction

1.1.

Let
G_{k}
be a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field
k
of char
\neq 2
. Let

be an involution of
G_{k}
and denote the fixed point subgroup by

. The affine quotient

. In our previous paper
[

]

In the current paper, we study embeddings of symmetric spaces. An

is a normal

-variety

with a
G_{k}
-equivariant open embedding

.

Let

be the set of spherical dominant weights (see §
2.1
). Let
\mathcal{L}
be a finitely generated submonoid of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
. Define a subspace

It is clear that

The first main result of this paper is that (

Theorem 1
.

(Theorem

The map

is a bijection between the set of closed saturated submonoids of

]
.

For the rest of this introduction, let us assume that

2.1.
The
\imath
root datum

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of char
\neq 2
. Following Springer
[
19
]
*1.1, a torus
S_{k}
of
G_{k}
is called

if
\theta_{k}(s)=s^{-1}
for any
s\in S_{k}
. Let
T_{k}
be a
\theta_{k}
-stable maximal torus of
G_{k}
, which contains a maximal split torus. Let
B_{k}
be a Borel subgroup of
G_{k}
containing
T_{k}
, such that
\theta_{k}(B_{k})\cap B_{k}
has the minimal dimension. The existence of the such a pair
(T_{k},B_{k})
was proved in
[
19
]
*1.4. Let
U_{k}
be the unipotent radical of
B_{k}
. Let
X
be the group of characters on
T_{k}
. We shall view
X
also as the character group of
B_{k}
in the natural way. Let
Y
be the group of cocharacters on
T_{k}
. We write
\langle\;,\;\rangle
to denote the canonical pairing between
Y
and
X
. Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbf{I}}
be the set of simple roots and
\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i\in\mathbf{I}}
be the set of simple coroots. For
\lambda,\mu\in X
, we write
\lambda\leq\mu
if and only if
\mu-\lambda
is a non-negative linear combination of simple roots. Set
X^{+}=\{\mu\in X\mid\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mu\rangle\geq 0,\text{ for any }i\in\mathbf{I}\}
to be the set of
dominant weights
. Let
Q=\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{[}\alpha_{i}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}]
be the root lattice, and
E=\mathbb{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}Q
be the associated real vector space. We equip
E
with a Euclidean structure
(\cdot,\cdot)
, such that
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\frac{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}
for any
i,j
in
\mathbf{I}
. Then
\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}\}
forms a set of simple roots of a root system in
E
. Let
W=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}\rangle
be the Weyl group associated with the root system.

Since
\theta_{k}
leaves
T_{k}
stable, it induces involutions
\theta_{X}
and
\theta_{Y}
on the lattices
X
and
Y
, respectively. It is known
[
19
]
that there is a subset
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}\subset\mathbf{I}
and an involution
\tau:\mathbf{I}\rightarrow\mathbf{I}
preserving
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, such that: (i)
w_{\bullet}\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{\tau i}
for any
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, and (ii)
\theta_{X}(\alpha_{i})=-w_{\bullet}\alpha_{\tau i}
for any
i\in\mathbf{I}
. Here
w_{\bullet}
is the longest element in the parabolic subgroup
W_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}\subset W
associated with the subset
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
. Write
\mathbf{I}_{\circ}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
. Then
\tau(\mathbf{I}_{\circ})=\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
. Let us fix a set
\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
of representatives of
\tau
-orbits on
\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
. The tuple
(\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}\sqcup\mathbf{I}_{\circ},\tau,Y,X,\langle\;,\;\rangle,\theta_{X},\theta_{Y})
is called an
\imath
root datum
associated with
(G_{k},\theta_{k})
. The pair
(G_{k},\theta_{k})
is completely determined by its
\imath
root datum up to isomorphisms.

Following
[
2
]
*(3.3), we write

\breve{X}=\{\mu-\theta_{X}(\mu)\mid\mu\in X\}

to be the sublattice of
X
. We set
{\breve{X}^{+}}=\breve{X}\cap X^{+}
. For
\lambda\in X
, let us write
\overline{\lambda}=\lambda-\theta_{X}(\lambda)
. Let us identify
\breve{X}
with the character lattice of the torus
\overline{T}_{k}=T_{k}/T_{k}^{\theta_{k}}
in the canonical way.

2.2.
The spherical weight lattice

We study the structure of the lattice
\breve{X}
in this section.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\lambda\in X^{+}
, then
\overline{\lambda}\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that
\overline{\lambda}\in\breve{X}
. It suffices to show that
\overline{\lambda}\in X^{+}
. For
i\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{i},\overline{\lambda}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee}-\theta_{Y}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee}),\lambda\rangle.

Note that
\theta_{Y}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
if
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, and
\theta_{Y}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})=-w_{\bullet}\alpha_{\tau i}^{\vee}
which is a negative coroot. Hence
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}-\theta_{Y}(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
is either zero or a sum of positive coroot. Therefore
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\overline{\lambda}\rangle
is nonnegative. We complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The lattice
\breve{X}
is generated by
{\breve{X}^{+}}
as an additive group, that is, for any
\mu\in\breve{X}
, there are
\mu^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}
in
{\breve{X}^{+}}
, such that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

By definition we have
\mu=\overline{\lambda}
, for some
\lambda\in X
. It is clear that we can write
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, for
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
in
X^{+}
. Then
\mu=\overline{\lambda^{\prime}}-\overline{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
, where
\overline{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
\overline{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
belong to
{\breve{X}^{+}}
by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
\lambda
and
\mu
belong to
\breve{X}
, and
\lambda\leq\mu
. Then
2(\mu-\lambda)
is a sum of elements in
\{\overline{\alpha_{i}}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda\leq\mu
, let us write
\mu-\lambda=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}
, where
n_{i}
are nonnegative integers. We claim that

(2.1)

2(\mu-\lambda)=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i=i}n_{i}\overline{\alpha_{i}}+2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i\neq i}n_{i}\overline{\alpha_{i}}.

It follows from the definition that for
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, we have
\theta_{X}(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i}
, and for
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
, we have

(2.2)

\theta_{X}(\alpha_{i})=-\alpha_{\tau i}-\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}t_{ij}\alpha_{j},\text{ where $t_{ij}$ are nonnegative integers.}

Since
\mu-\lambda
belongs to
\breve{X}
, we have
\theta_{X}(\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}n_{i}\alpha_{i})=-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}
. By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}n_{i}\theta_{X}(\alpha_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}\big(\alpha_{\tau i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}t_{ij}\alpha_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}\alpha_{\tau i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}\big(n_{j}-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}t_{ij}\big)\alpha_{j}.

Therefore we deduce that
n_{\tau i}=n_{i}
for
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
, and
2n_{j}=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}t_{ij}
for
j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
.

By (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i=i}n_{i}\overline{\alpha_{i}}+2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i\neq i}n_{i}\overline{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i=i}n_{i}\big(2\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}t_{ij}\alpha_{j}\big)+2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i\neq i}n_{i}\big(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{\tau i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}t_{ij}\alpha_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}\big(\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i=i}n_{i}t_{ij}+\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime},\;\tau i\neq i}2n_{i}t_{ij}\big)\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\heartsuit)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}\big(\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}t_{ij}\big)\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}+2\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}n_{j}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}}n_{i}\alpha_{i}.

Here
(\heartsuit)
follows from the fact that
t_{ij}=t_{\tau i,j}
, for
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
and
j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
. Hence we proved the equality (
2.1
), which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The Borel eigenvalues and eigenfunctions

For any

It follows that

is the weight lattice of

considered in

]
*Definition 2.13.

2.6.
The theory of spherical embeddings

An embedding
V_{k}
of
G_{k}/K_{k}
is called
simple
if there is a unique closed
G_{k}
-orbit in
V_{k}
. This includes all the affine embeddings
[

]
*Theorem 6.7. All the embeddings in this paper will be simple. For a simple embedding
V_{k}
, a
B_{k}
-stable prime divisor
D
of
V_{k}
which is not
G_{k}
-stable is called a
color
if
D
contains the closed
G_{k}
-orbit. A simple embedding is called
toroidal
if it has no colors. A complete toroidal simple embedding of
G_{k}/K_{k}
is called a
canonical embedding
of
G_{k}/K_{k}
.

A
valuation
on
k(G_{k}/K_{k})
is a map

v:k(G_{k}/K_{k})^{*}=k(G_{k}/K_{k})\backslash\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}

such that

(1)

v(f_{1}+f_{2})\geq\text{min}\{v(f_{1}),v(f_{2})\}
for
f_{1},f_{2}\in k(G_{k}/K_{k})^{*}
;

(2)

v(f_{1}f_{2})=v(f_{1})+v(f_{2})
, for
f_{1},f_{2}\in k(G_{k}/K_{k})
;

(3)

v(k^{*})=0.

The field
k(G_{k}/K_{k})
admits a natural
G_{k}
-action. A valuation
v
is called
G_{k}
-invariant
if
v(g\cdot f)=v(f)
, for any
g\in G_{k}
and any
f\in k(G_{k}/K_{k})^{*}
. We denote by
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}(G_{k}/K_{k})
the set of
G_{k}
-invariant valuations on
G_{k}/K_{k}
.

By Lemma
2.5

\breve{X}
is the lattice consisting of eignvalues of
B_{k}
-semi-invariant rational functions on
G_{k}/K_{k}
. Let
{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\breve{X},\mathbb{Q})
. Any valuation
v
on
k(G_{k}/K_{k})
determines an element
\varrho(v)
in
{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}
, where
\varrho(v)(\mu)=v(\chi_{\mu})
for
\mu\in\breve{X}
. It is known that the map
\varrho
is injective when restricting to the set
\mathcal{V}
(cf.
[
11
]
*Corollary 7.8). Let us identify
\mathcal{V}
with its image in
{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}
.

For a simple embedding
V_{k}
of
G_{k}/K_{k}
, the associated
colored cone

(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
is the pair where
\mathcal{F}
is the set of colors and
\mathcal{C}\subset{{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}}
is the cone generated by the images of
G_{k}
-stable prime divisors of

and elements in

under the map

. A simple embedding is completely determined by its colored cone. By

, a canonical embedding corresponds to the colored cone

2.7.
The wonderful compactifications

We assume that
G_{k}
is adjoint in this subsection. A construction of the canonical embedding

Let

-stable divisors which are not

-stable. Let

be the closure of
\overline{T_{k}}
in
\mathring{P}_{k}

, and let

be the unipotent radical of

is identified with the character lattice of

Theorem 2.6

(
[

]
*Proposition 3.8)

The action map

We drop the assumption that

We have

(2.5)

\mathcal{V}=\{t\in{{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}}\mid t(\overline{\alpha_{i}})\leq 0,\;\text{ for any }i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}\subset{{}_{\mathbb{Q}}\breve{X}^{*}}.

Proof.

By
[

]
*Corollary 5.3, the valuation cone always contains the right hand side of (

be the wonderful compactification of

Next suppose that

3.
Affine embeddings of symmetric spaces

3.1.
Semigroups and affine embeddings

We firstly define several properties of subsemigroups of
\breve{X}^{+}
. A subsemigroup of
\breve{X}^{+}
is a subset which is closed under addition. A submonoid is a subsemigroup with zero.

Definition 3.1
.

A subsemigroup
\mathcal{L}
of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
is
saturated
if

•

\mathcal{L}
is finitely generated as a semigroup;

•

\mathcal{L}
generates
\breve{X}
as an additive group;

•

if
n\mu\in\mathcal{L}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\mu\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
, then
\mu\in\mathcal{L}
.

A subsemigroup
\mathcal{L}
of
\breve{X}^{+}
is called
closed
if for any
\lambda\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
with
\lambda\leq\mu
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{L}
, we have
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
.

For any subsemigroup
\mathcal{L}
of
\breve{X}^{+}
, we define the
k
-subspace

(3.1)

R_{k}(\mathcal{L})=\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\mu}.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a submonoid, then it is clear that
R_{k}(\mathcal{L})
is a unital
k
-algebra.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\breve{X}^{+}
be a closed saturated submonoid. Then
R_{k}(\mathcal{L})
is a finitely-generated normal (integrally closed) unital
k
-algebra.

Proof.

It is clear that
R_{k}(\mathcal{L})
inherits the good filtration from
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
in Theorem
4
. By
[
8
]
*Theorem 9, it suffices to show that the
k
-algebra
R_{k}(\mathcal{L})^{U_{k}}
of
U_{k}
-invariants is finitely generated and normal.

Let
k[\mathcal{L}]
be the monoid algebra of
\mathcal{L}
. Then

R_{k}(\mathcal{L})^{U_{k}}=\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\mu}^{U_{k}}\cong k[\mathcal{L}].

Here the last isomorphism follows from the closeness of
\mathcal{L}
. The algebra
k[\mathcal{L}]
is integrally closed since
\mathcal{L}
is saturated (cf.
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.3.5). Since
\mathcal{L}
is finitely generated by assumption, this shows
k[\mathcal{L}]
is finitely generated.
∎

We then define the normal affine
G_{k}
-variety

V_{k}(\mathcal{L})=Spec\,R_{k}(\mathcal{L}).

The rest of this subsection is devoted to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

The map
\mathcal{L}\mapsto V_{k}(\mathcal{L})
is a bijection between the set of closed saturated submonoids of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
with the set of affine embeddings of
G_{k}/K_{k}
(up to isomorphism).

Remark 3.4
.

Isomorphisms between embeddings
V_{k}
and
V_{k}^{\prime}
of
G_{k}/K_{k}
are
G_{k}
-equivariant isomorphisms
V_{k}\cong V_{k}^{\prime}
that restrict to the identity map on
G_{k}/K_{k}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a closed saturated submonoid of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
. Then
V_{k}(\mathcal{L})
is an affine embedding of
G_{k}/K_{k}
.

Proof.

We have already shown
V_{k}(\mathcal{L})
is a normal affine variety. The
G_{k}
-action is the obvious one. We show
V_{k}(\mathcal{L})
contains
G_{k}/K_{k}
as an open subspace.

Let
F_{k}(\mathcal{L})
be the field of fractions of
R_{k}(\mathcal{L})
. It suffices to show
F_{k}(\mathcal{L})=k(G_{k}/K_{k})
, or
F_{k}(\mathcal{L})
contains
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
. Take any
f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
. Assume that
f
belongs to
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\lambda}
, for some
\lambda\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
. Since
\mathcal{L}
generates
\breve{X}
, we can write
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, for some
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}
. Recall the element
\chi_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{U_{k}}
. Then
\chi_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\in R_{k}(\mathcal{L})
. Then we have

\chi_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\lambda^{\prime}}\subset R_{k}(\mathcal{L}).

Therefore
f
belongs to
F_{k}(\mathcal{L})
. We complete the proof.
∎

We next construct the inverse of the bijection. Let
V_{k}
be an affine embedding of
G_{k}/K_{k}
, and let
k[V_{k}]
be the ring of regular functions on
V_{k}
. Then
k[V_{k}]
is identified with a
G_{k}
-subalgebra of
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
. We set

\mathcal{L}(V_{k})=\{\mu\in X\mid k[V_{k}]^{(\mu)}\neq 0\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
is a submonoid of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
. We also have
V_{k}^{U_{k}}\cong k[\mathcal{L}(V_{k})]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
V_{k}
be an affine embedding of
G_{k}/K_{k}
. The submonoid
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
is a closed and saturated.

Proof.

We assume
V_{k}\neq G_{k}/K_{k}
, otherwise the claim follows by Theorem
4
. By the multiplicity-free property, we know that
k[V_{k}]^{U_{k}}
is isomorphic to the monoid algebra
k[\mathcal{L}(V_{k})]
. Since
k[V_{k}]
is finitely generated and normal, we deduce that
k[V_{k}]^{U_{k}}\cong k[\mathcal{L}(V_{k})]
is also finitely generated and normal (cf.
[
8
]
*Theorem 9 and
[
8
]
*proof of Theorem 17). Hence
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
is also saturated (cf.
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.3.5).

We next show that
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
is closed. Take
\lambda\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
, with
\lambda\leq\mu
. Recall the eigenfunctions
\chi_{\lambda}
and
\chi_{\mu}
in
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
from Section
2.4
. Let
D=V_{k}-G_{k}/K_{k}
be the closed subvariety. Let
D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{s}
be the irreducible components of
D
with codimension one, and let
v_{i}
be the
G_{k}
-invariant valuation corresponding to
D_{i}
, for various
i
. Recall
V_{k}
is normal. Then by
[
9
]
*Proposition II.6.3 A, for
f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
, one has
f\in k[V_{k}]
if and only if
v_{i}(f)\geq 0
, for all
i
. In particular we have
v_{i}(\chi_{\mu})\geq 0
for all
i
. By Proposition
2.7
, we have
v_{i}(\chi_{\overline{\alpha_{j}}})\leq 0
, for
1\leq i\leq s
and
j\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}
. Since
\lambda\leq\mu
, by Lemma
2.3
we have

2(\mu-\lambda)=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}n_{j}\overline{\alpha_{j}},\text{\qquad where $n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.}

Therefore we have

2v_{i}(\chi_{\lambda})=v_{i}(\chi_{2\lambda})=v_{i}(\chi_{2\mu})-\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}n_{j}v_{i}(\chi_{\overline{\alpha_{j}}})\geq v_{i}(\chi_{2\mu})\geq 0.

Hence we deduce that
v_{i}(\chi_{\lambda})\geq 0
, and therefore
\chi_{\lambda}\in k[V_{k}]
. This implies that
\lambda
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
. We conclude that
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
is closed.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
generates
\breve{X}
. Thanks to Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to show that for
\mu\in{\breve{X}^{+}}
, there are
\mu^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
, such that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}
. Let
I(D)\subset k[V_{k}]
be the defining ideal of the closed subset
D
. Since
I(D)\subset k[V_{k}]
is a locally finite
G_{k}
-module, it must contain non-zero
B_{k}
-eigenfunctions by Lie-Kolchin theorem. Suppose
\chi_{\gamma}
belongs to
I(D)
, where
\gamma\in\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
. Then
v_{i}(\chi_{\gamma})>0
, for
1\leq i\leq s
. Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
v_{i}(\chi_{\mu})+nv_{i}(\chi_{\gamma})\geq 0
, for all
i
. Then
\chi_{\mu}\chi_{\gamma}^{n}=\chi_{\mu+n\gamma}
is regular on
V_{k}
. Therefore
\mu=(\mu+n\gamma)-n\gamma
is a desired decomposition. We complete the proof.
∎

Finally, we show that the maps
\mathcal{L}\mapsto V_{k}(\mathcal{L})
and
V_{k}\mapsto\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
are mutually inverse to each other.

Lemma 3.7
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a closed saturated submonoid of
{\breve{X}^{+}}
. Then
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(V_{k}(\mathcal{L}))
.

(2)

Let
V_{k}
be an affine embedding of
G_{k}/K_{k}
. We have
V_{k}(\mathcal{L}(V_{k}))\cong V_{k}
.

Proof.

We show Part (1). By definition, we have
k[V_{k}(\mathcal{L})]=\bigcup_{\mu\in\mathcal{L}}k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\mu}
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
is closed, the claim follows from Lemma
2.4
.

We show Part (2) now. By Lemma
2.4
and the definition of
\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
, the space
k[V_{k}]^{U_{k}}
is spanned by
\{\chi_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{L}(V_{k})\}
. Let
A
be the smallest
G_{k}
-invariant subalgebra of
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
, which contains
\chi_{\mu}
, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
. Let
\overline{A}
be the integral closure of

in
k(G_{k}/K_{k})
. By
[
8
]
*Theorem 5, the algebra
k[V_{k}]
is integral over

. So we have

In particular
\overline{A}
is integrally over

. Since

is integrally closed, we deduce that

.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.4
and the definition (

), we know that the subspace

is also spanned by
\{\chi_{\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{L}(V_{k})\}
. The algebra

is integrally closed, thanks to Lemma

. Therefore by the similar arguments, we have

. This completes the proof.
∎

We have now proved Theorem
3.3
. Let us derive some immediate consequence.

.

Assume

is semisimple. Then any affine embedding
V_{k}
of
G_{k}/K_{k}
is trivial, that is,
V_{k}\cong G_{k}/K_{k}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(V_{k})
. We show

. Since

forms a
\mathbb{Q}

We know
\mathcal{L}
generates
\breve{X}
. So we can find
\lambda\in\mathcal{L}
such that

for all

. We write

is a generalized Cartan matrix of finite type in the sense of

]
*Theorem 4.3. It is clear

is a generalized Cartan matrix. Let

be the half sum of positive coroots of

, and denote

. Let

. Then all coordinates of both

and

are positive. This shows

is of finite type by
[

]
*Theorem 4.3.

Now
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle>0
for all

implies that
c_{i}>0
for all

*Theorem 4.3. Then for any

as well. This finishes the proof.
∎

G_{k}

.

We have a short exact sequence of vector spaces

It suffices to show the surjectivity part. When char

. We assume char

Let

Corollary 3.11
.

(1)

Corollary 3.14

3.4.

Let

By definition,

is the spectrum of the subalgebra

of

-invariant functions in

.

Remark 3.22
.

When

is an affine embedding of

is a reductive monoid. In this case, the quotient

is a commutative monoid. This is the origin of the name “abelianization”.

Let

be the maximal torus of

to

and

. We will identify

, hence

, with its image in

.

Let

be the submonoid associated with

Lemma 4.2
.

The subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is a finitely generated closed saturated submonoid of
{\breve{X}^{+}}\times\breve{X}
generating
\breve{X}\times\breve{X}

Proof.

It is clear that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is a closed saturated submonoid.

We show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is finitely generated. Thanks to Lemma
2.3
, the map
(\mu,\lambda)\mapsto(\mu,\lambda-\mu)
defines an isomorphism
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}{\breve{X}^{+}}\times L
as monoids, where
L=\breve{X}\cap\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha_{i}}
is a submonoid of
\breve{X}
. Since
\breve{X}
is a discrete subgroup of the vector space
\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}\overline{\alpha_{i}}
, the monoid

is generated by finitely many elements in
\breve{X}\cap\{\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}n_{i}\overline{\alpha_{i}}\mid 0\leq n_{i}\leq 1\}
by Gordan’s lemma. The monoid

is finitely generated by Lemma

, since
\breve{X}^{+}=\mathcal{L}(G_{k}/K_{k})
for the trivial embedding of

. Therefore
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is finitely generated.

We next show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
generates
\breve{X}\times\breve{X}
. Since
G_{k}
is semisimple, the vector space
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\breve{X}
is spanned by
\{\overline{\alpha_{i}}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}
. For any
(\mu,\lambda)\in{\breve{X}^{+}}\times\breve{X}
, we can take
\gamma\in\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{N}\overline{\alpha_{i}}

\lambda+\gamma-\mu\in\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha_{i}}
, which implies
\mu\preceq\lambda+\gamma
. Therefore we have
(\mu,\lambda)=(\mu,\lambda+\gamma)-(0,\gamma)
, where
(\mu,\lambda+\gamma)
and
(0,\gamma)
both belong to
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. Hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
generates
\breve{X}\times\breve{X}
.

We complete the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8

Let us unravel the essential condition here. Let

Let

Proposition 4.9
.

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{p})
for some
\widetilde{G}_{k}
-stable prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is essential.

Moreover, all
\widetilde{G}_{k}
-stable prime ideals of
k[\widetilde{V}_{k}]
is of this form.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
be essential. We show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{p})
for some
\widetilde{G}_{k}
-stable prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
Thanks to Theorem
3.16
, we show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is a closed saturated ideal of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

(a) We first show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is a saturated ideal of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. Consider the embedding of monoids

\psi:\breve{X}^{+}\times\breve{X}\rightarrow\breve{X}^{+}\times\breve{X},\qquad(\mu,\gamma)\rightarrow(\mu,\gamma-\mu).

We see the
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is a subsemigroup of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. It follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is a prime ideal of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

(b) We next show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
is closed. Let
(\mu_{1},\gamma_{1})
and
(\mu_{2},\gamma_{2})
be in
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
such that
(\mu_{2},\gamma_{2})\leq(\mu_{1},\gamma_{1})
and
(\mu_{1},\gamma_{1})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
. By definition, we have

\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\in\sum_{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{i}.

Assume
(\gamma_{1}-\mu_{1})\not\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
first. Then we have
\gamma_{2}-\mu_{2}=\gamma_{1}-\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\not\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
either. So in this case, we have
(\mu_{2},\gamma_{2})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
.

Assume
\mu_{1}\not\in\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\omega}_{j}
now. We can further assume
(\gamma_{1}-\mu_{1})\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
, as well as
\mu_{2}\in\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\omega}_{j}
, otherwise we would be done. We write
\mu_{1}=\sum_{j\in J_{1}}d_{j}\overline{\omega}_{j}+\sum_{j\not\in J_{1}}c_{j}\overline{\omega}_{j}=\mu_{1}^{\prime}+\mu_{1}^{\prime\prime}
for
d_{j},c_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}-\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=(\gamma_{1}-\mu_{1})+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=(\gamma_{1}-\mu_{1})+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{1}^{\prime})+(\mu^{\prime}_{1}-\mu_{2})

\displaystyle\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}+\mu_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}

Since
\mu^{\prime\prime}_{1}=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}}c_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
with
c_{j}>0
for some
j\not\in J_{2}
by Remark
4.8
, we have

\gamma_{2}-\mu_{2}\not\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
. This shows
(\mu_{2},\gamma_{2})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
.

Now let
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
be a closed prime ideal of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. We show
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
for some essential
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
.

(c) We first show
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
for some
J_{1},J_{2}\subset\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}
.

We first see that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is a subsemigroup of
\breve{X}^{+}\times\breve{X}
.
Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is prime, we see that if
(\mu,\gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
then
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}(\mu,\gamma)\cap(\breve{X}^{+}\times\breve{X})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
. Let

J_{1}=\{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}|\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}(\overline{\omega}_{i},\overline{\omega}_{i})\cap(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{J}})\neq\emptyset\},J_{2}=\{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}|\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}(0,\overline{\alpha}_{i})\cap(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{J}})\neq\emptyset\}.

It follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
following Definition
4.7
. Since
\widetilde{J}
is an ideal, it follows from the definition of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}\supset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
. We conclude that
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
.

(d) We next show
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
must be essential.

We have
J_{1}\neq\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Assume the contrary, there is a connected component
J_{1}^{\prime}
of the complement of
J_{1}
that is completely contained in
J_{2}
. So there exists some
\gamma^{\prime}\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}\cap\breve{X}
such that
\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{s},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle>0
for all
s\in J^{\prime}_{1}
.

Let
(\mu,\gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{J_{1},J_{2}}
be such that
\mu=c_{i}\overline{\omega}_{i}
for some

with

, and
\gamma\in\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\overline{\alpha}_{j}
. The existence of such

. Up to rescaling, we can further assume

. However by definition

does not belong to the ideal

, which is the defining ideal of
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\emptyset}}

We show Part (3). For any

. Take

. Then we have

(4.4)

v_{i}(\chi_{\overline{\alpha_{j}}})=\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\overline{\alpha_{j}}}e^{0})=\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}})-\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\mu}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}).

Note that
\chi_{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}
belongs to
k[\widetilde{V}_{k}]-\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, so we have
\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}})=0
. If
j\neq i
, we have
\chi_{\mu}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}}\in k[\widetilde{V}_{k}]-\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. And we have
\chi_{\mu}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{i}}}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Therefore we have
\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\mu}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}})=0
if
j\neq i
, and
\widetilde{v_{i}}(\chi_{\mu}e^{\mu+\overline{\alpha_{j}}})>0
if
j=i
. Combined with (
4.4
), this completes the proof of (3).
∎

Remark 4.12
.

In terms of the spherical theory, Theorem
4.11
shows that
P_{k}
is the simple embedding associated to the colored cone
(\mathcal{V}(G_{k}/K_{k}),\emptyset)

*Definition 3.2.1).

It follows from the definition that all the

We next define an integral model for the canonical embedding. For

\mathbf{O}(G/K)_{\preceq\mu}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in{\breve{X}^{+}},\;\lambda\preceq\mu}\mathbf{O}(G/K)_{\leq\lambda}.

Definition 4.13
.

Let

\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle>0

4.5.
Local Structure Theorem and Smoothness

In this section, we study the smoothness of the canonical embedding. We expect this is known to experts.

Recall that
G_{k}
is semisimple and
P_{k}\supset G_{k}/K_{k}
is the canonical embedding. Analogous to the notations in Section
2.7
, let
\mathring{P}_{k}\subset P_{k}
be the complementary of all the
B_{k}
-stable divisors which are not
G_{k}
-stable. Recall
\overline{T_{k}}=T_{k}/T_{k}^{\theta_{k}}
is the torus contained in
G_{k}/K_{k}
. Let
N_{k}
be the closure of
\overline{T_{k}}
in
\mathring{P}_{k}
. Let
P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}\supset B_{k}
be the parabolic subgroup associated with the subset of simple roots
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, and let
U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}}
be the unipotent radical of
P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}
. We extend Theorem
2.6
to the canonical embeddings.

Proposition 4.15
.

The action map

U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}\times N_{k}\longrightarrow\mathring{P_{k}}

is an isomorphism of varieties. And
N_{k}\supset\overline{T_{k}}
is an affine toric variety whose coordinate ring is the monoid algebra of
C=\{\mu\in\breve{X}\mid\mu\preceq 0\}\subset\breve{X}
.

Proof.

Take
\lambda\in X^{+}
, such that
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle>0
, for all
i\in\mathbf{I}
. We have
P_{k}=\text{Proj }\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\preceq n\overline{\lambda}}e^{n\overline{\lambda}}
. We write
S_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\preceq n\overline{\lambda}}e^{n\overline{\lambda}}
. By the proof of Theorem
4.11
(2),
\mathring{P}_{k}\subset{P_{k}}
is the open affine subset defined by
\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}e^{\overline{\lambda}}\neq 0
. Hence the coordinate ring
k[\mathring{P}_{k}]
can be identified with the algebra

(S_{\lambda})_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}e^{\overline{\lambda}})}=\left\{\frac{fe^{n\overline{\lambda}}}{\chi^{n}_{\overline{\lambda}}e^{n\overline{\lambda}}}\mid f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\preceq n\overline{\lambda}}\right\}.

Note that

(4.6)

(S_{\lambda})_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}e^{\overline{\lambda}})}\cong k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}=\{f/\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}^{n}\mid f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\preceq n\overline{\lambda}}\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset k(G_{k}/K_{k}).

Hence it suffices to show the coaction map
\Delta:k[G_{k}/K_{k}]\rightarrow k[G_{k}]\otimes k[G_{k}/K_{k}]
induces an injective map

(4.7)

\delta:k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}{\longrightarrow}k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}}]\otimes k[\overline{T_{k}}]

and the image is
k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}}]\otimes k[C]
. Here
\delta
is the composition of
\Delta
with the tensor product of maps
k[G_{k}]\rightarrow k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}}]
and
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]\rightarrow k[\overline{T_{k}}]
given by restrictions.

Recall the action map

(4.8)

U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}\times\overline{T_{k}}\rightarrow G_{k}/K_{k}

is an isomorphism onto the open
B_{k}
-orbit of
G_{k}/K_{k}
(see, for example,
[
6
]
*Proposition 3.8). It follows that (
4.7
) is injective.

We next determine the image. Take
f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\mu}
. It follows from the proof of
[
12
]
*Lemma 1.8 that
\Delta(f)\in\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}=\mu}k[G_{k}]_{\leq\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\lambda^{\prime\prime}}.
On the other hand, for
f\in k[G_{k}]_{\leq\mu}
where
\mu\in X
, we have
f\mid_{T_{k}}=\sum_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}\chi_{\lambda}
where
f_{\lambda}=f(\mathrm{1}_{\lambda})
. Here we are viewing
f
as a linear function on the modified quantum group as in
[
12
]
*Theorem 4.11. In particular we have
f_{\lambda}=0
unless
\lambda\leq\mu
. Therefore for
f\in k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{\leq\mu}
we have
\delta(f)\in k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}]\otimes k[\overline{T_{k}}]_{\leq\mu}
, where
k[\overline{T_{k}}]_{\leq\mu}\subset k[\overline{T_{k}}]
is the subspace consisting of linear combinations of
\chi_{\mu^{\prime}}
with
\mu^{\prime}\in\breve{X}
and
\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu
. Then by the definition (
4.6
) and
\delta(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})=1\otimes\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}
, we conclude that the image of
\delta
lies in
k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},k}}]\otimes k[C]
. We next show that the image is exactly
k[U_{P_{I_{\bullet},k}}]\otimes k[C]
.

Let
G_{k}^{ad}=G_{k}/Z_{k}
, where
Z_{k}
is the center of
G_{k}
. Then
\theta_{k}
induces an involution on
G_{k}^{ad}
. Let
K_{k}^{ad}\subset G_{k}^{ad}
denote the fixed-point subgroup. Let
\breve{X}^{ad}\subset\breve{X}
be the spherical weight lattice associated with the symmetric space
G_{k}^{ad}/K_{k}^{ad}
. Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\overline{\lambda}\in\breve{X}^{ad}
. Similarly define
k[G_{k}^{ad}/K_{k}^{ad}]_{(n\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}
which is viewed as a subalgebra of
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}
. Moreover we have the commutative diagram

(4.9)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}]\otimes k[\overline{T_{k}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[G_{k}^{ad}/K_{k}^{ad}]_{(\chi_{n\overline{\lambda}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}]\otimes k[\overline{T_{k}^{ad}}].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta^{ad}}

Here
\delta^{ad}
is defined similarly as
\delta
. By Theorem
2.6
, the map
\delta^{ad}
is an isomorphism into the image
k[U_{P,k}]\otimes k[C^{ad}]
, where
C^{ad}\subset\breve{X}^{ad}
is defined similarly as
C
. In particular, the image of
\delta
contains the subalgebra
k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}]\subset k[U_{P_{\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}},k}]\otimes k[\overline{T_{k}}]
. On the other hand, take any
\mu\in C
. We can take
s\in\mathbb{Z}
to be big enough such that
s\overline{\lambda}+\mu\in X^{+}
. Hence
\chi_{\mu}=\chi_{\mu+s\overline{\lambda}}/\chi_{\overline{\lambda}}^{s}
belongs to
k[G_{k}/K_{k}]_{(\chi_{\overline{\lambda}})}
. Therefore
1\otimes\chi_{\mu}=\delta(\chi_{\mu})
belongs to the image of
\delta
. We complete the proof.
∎

Recall the spherical roots
\{\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}
in Section
2.3
.

Corollary 4.16
.

The canonical embedding
P_{k}
is smooth if and only if
\{\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}
is a basis of
\breve{X}
.

Proof.

Since
P_{k}
has no colors, any
G_{k}
-orbit of
P_{k}
intersects
\mathring{P_{k}}
. Hence
P_{k}
is smooth if and only if
\mathring{P_{k}}
is smooth. By Proposition
4.15
,
\mathring{P_{k}}
is smooth if and only if the toric variety
N_{k}
is smooth. By the definition of the spherical root system,
\{-\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mid i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}^{\prime}\}
forms a minimal generators of the polyhedral cone
\mathbb{R}\otimes C
. Then the corollary follows from the smoothness criteria of toric varieties.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

It is clear that when
G_{k}

is smooth. There are other cases when
P_{k}
is also smooth, for example, the symmetric space

.

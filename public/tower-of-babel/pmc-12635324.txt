Lightweight signcryption scheme for Securing wearable sensor observed health data sharing in internet of medical things paradigm
Authors: Taher M Ghazal, Mohammad Kamrul Hasan, Sallam O F Khairy, Umi Asma Mokhtar, Shayla Islam, Rashid A Saeed, Nada Ahmed, Mustafa Abdullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) plays a crucial role in enabling precision diagnosis and optimal recommendations for patients monitored remotely. However, conventional encryption–signature mechanisms introduce significant computational and communication overhead, making them unsuitable for resource-constrained IoMT devices that require secure and real-time transmission of health vitals. To address this gap, this article proposes a lightweight signcryption scheme tailored explicitly for IoMT data security. The novelty of the scheme lies in employing a triple-truncated DES-based signcryption with non-linear shared integrity verification, which simultaneously ensures confidentiality, authenticity, and resistance against replay and forgery attacks while reducing complexity. The operation increasingly adheres to critical preparation, signing, and distribution protocols even for fine-grained time periods, with truncation reaching succinct key pairs with low overhead. A lightweight CNN-based verification module is used to authenticate the sequence and handle truncation without introducing extra complexity, as required for IoMT efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach improves detection accuracy by 14.12%, reduces service failures by 13.67%, and achieves computational time savings compared to baseline schemes. In all, the scheme provides an IoMT-optimized, low-complexity, and secure solution for protecting sensitive medical information against attacks in adversarial environments.Keywords: CNN, Data integrity, IoMT, Key management, Secure data sharing, SigncryptionSubject terms: Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Problem statement

The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) enables real-time health monitoring and remote diagnosis via networked devices. Still, it introduces a significant challenge regarding data confidentiality, integrity, and availability in resource-constrained settings. Traditional cryptographic and authentication approaches consume excessive latency and processing overhead, which is inappropriate for IoMT platforms where lightweight processing and efficiency are paramount. Secondly, attacks such as replay attacks, tampering, and unauthorized access compromise patient safety and system reliability. Thus, an adaptive, lightweight, and privacy-preserving security architecture is required that maximizes simplicity while providing adequate security for sensitive medical information during real-time transmission and sharing.

Research methodology

This work proposes a lightweight triple-truncated DES signcryption scheme with CNN-activated verification for IoMT applications. The procedure consists of three key steps: (i) observation filtering and structuring into continuous sequences of sensor observations using preparation, (ii) embracing an advanced triple-DES-based signcryption with truncation to facilitate secure yet low-complexity signing and sharing, and (iii) decryption with truncated keys and CNN-enabled integrity verification. The suggested approach is empirically evaluated on a synthetic health-monitoring data set, comparing data extraction efficiency, computational complexity, and accuracy with those of existing state-of-the-art methods. Lightweight signcryption scheme for securing vital sharing

The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) has revolutionized healthcare, enabling precision medicine and remote, real-time patient care through networked devices. The growing dependence on IoMT has raised security, integrity, and privacy concerns regarding the transmission of medical data42–44. This is aimed at reducing exposure to malicious threats, such as theft and data tampering, that can occur during data transfer using the device45,46. Discussions

To make design choices explicit and maximize the applicability of this work to the real world, the following are mentioned. First, our application of truncated DES in experiments is not intended as a substitution for existing cryptographic standards. Instead, it’s a resource-light simulation benchmark for low-resource testbeds. The proposed framework is cipher-independent and can be easily implemented to support secure ciphers, such as AES or ECC, while adhering to current cryptographic standards without adding computational complexity to IoMT devices.

Second, unlike a cryptographic method, the CNN is used as a mechanism for verifying the integrity of truncation and marking malicious data sequences. This excludes only integrity-lost, suspicious data from aggregation, minimizes redundant transmission without increasing computational expense, and improves attack resilience.

Third, to improve readability and minimize technical redundancy, the methodology has been simplified, and the equations have been described concisely. Lastly, this update includes additional quantitative analyses, including computation complexity, signing and decryption times, and data retrieval speeds. These findings demonstrate that the proposed scheme achieves up to 14.12% improved detection accuracy, a 13.67% reduction in service failures, and shorter computation time compared to baseline schemes. These results provide concrete evidence of the efficiency gains and security assurances offered by the framework.

Practical usefulness and relevance

IoMT data security is a rapidly unfolding and pressing problem as wearable and remote monitoring devices become increasingly central to modern healthcare infrastructure. The suggested lightweight signcryption scheme precisely addresses the devices’ computational and power limitations by reducing the complexity of signing and decryption, without compromising confidentiality and integrity. To demonstrate its efficiency, the scheme has been empirically compared with current best practices, such as AutoPro-RHC and 2DCM-DS. The results demonstrate up to 14.12% improved detection accuracy, 13.67% reduced service failures, and reduced computational time, thus demonstrating better performance with fewer constraints. Additionally, the framework is cipher-independent, thereby supporting current cryptographic schemes such as AES and ECC and enabling greater real-world applicability across heterogeneous IoMT platforms. These findings confirm the scheme’s viability for real-world implementation and demonstrate its feasibility for adoption in real healthcare monitoring scenarios.

Clarification on cipher selection

It is essential to emphasize that the proposed scheme is independent of DES as a cryptographic standard. Our use of the truncated variant of DES, which we employ in our experiments, is solely as a lightweight reference to demonstrate feasibility in resource-scarce IoMT test settings. The novelty of the work lies in combining a signcryption mechanism with a CNN-based validation approach for detecting anomalies, integrity checking for truncation, and optimal data handling. The scheme itself isn’t interested in ciphers and can natively support newer, more secure primitives, such as AES or ECC, without modifying its overall design. Hence, while DES is used here for demonstration purposes, the underlying design itself is fully compatible with current cryptographic standards for security, conformance, and longevity.

Conclusion

The proposed lightweight signcryption scheme addresses the vital health transmission protection issues in IoMT systems. This is achieved by supporting triple-truncated DES. The modified signcryption and decryption process verifies the integrity of shared data, thereby preventing adversaries from intercepting transmissions. It also maintains data integrity through proper key management within controlled computational complexity by modifying Triple DES. This modification to the keying process enhances IoMT performance and ensures data security. The modification is performed using truncated keys, which are then split into public, private, and session keys in conventional authentication systems. Based on the number of truncations performed, the decryption process is simplified without complex computations. The number of decryption and integrity verification steps is limited to a key size smaller than the signing procedure to reduce complexity. To check robustness, the CNN process monitors integrity validation to verify the maximum mapping of keys between the sharing device and the diagnosis center at regular intervals. This helps to balance the integrity and efficiency of the shared data from the diagnosis center through the CNN validation process. The scheme supports the reliable and secure sharing of healthcare data, enabling precision diagnostics and remote patient care by addressing key challenges in IoMT environments.

Kähler compactification of ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and Reeb dynamics
Chi Li, Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth complex manifold. Assume that Y ⊂ X Y\subset X is a Kähler submanifold such that X ∖ Y X\setminus Y is biholomorphic to ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . We prove that ( X , Y ) (X,Y) is biholomorphic to ( ℙ n , ℙ n − 1 ) (\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) . We then study certain Kähler orbifold compactifications of ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and, as an application, prove that on ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} the flat metric is the only asymptotically conical Ricci-flat Kähler metric whose metric cone at infinity has a smooth link. As a key technical ingredient, we derive a new characterization of minimal discrepancy of isolated Fano cone singularities by using S 1 S^{1} -equivariant positive symplectic homology.



1.
Introduction

Our work is motivated by two problems in complex geometry. The first is Hirzebruch’s classical problem on analytic compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension
n
. Assume that
Y
is a subvariety of
X
such that
X\setminus Y
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Of course, the simplest example is
(X,Y)=(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
where
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a linear subspace. In this case, we will just say that
(X,Y)
is standard.
In
[
Hir54
]
, Hirzebruch asked the problem of classifying all such pairs
(X,Y)
with the second Betti number
b_{2}(X)=1
. The Betti number condition is known to imply that
Y
is irreducible.
When
\dim X=1
or
2
, it is known that there is only the standard example. For
n=3
, under the assumption that
X
is projective, all such analytic compactifications of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
are now classified. The subvariety
Y
in any known non-standard example is always singular. For example, one can compactify
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to a smooth quadric hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
by adding an irreducible singular divisor (which is a projective cone over a lower-dimensional quadric) at infinity.
It is a long-standing conjecture that if
Y
is assumed to be smooth then
(X,Y)
should be standard (see
[
vdV62
]
,
[
BM78
]
,
[
PS91
]
). This was proved in
[
BM78
]
when
\dim X\leq 3
.
Here we prove this conjecture under the Kähler assumption for all dimensions.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the above notation, if
Y
is a Kähler submanifold,
(X,Y)
is standard.

Early results of Brenton-Morrow from
[
BM78
]
showed already that, with the Kähler assumption,
Y
must be a Fano manifold and they reduce the problem to proving that
Y
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
(see Theorem
2.8
). We will prove that the Fano index of
Y
must be
n
, which then implies that
Y
is
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
by a well-known criterion of Kobayashi-Ochiai (
[
KO73
]
).
The main tools we use are (
S^{1}
-equivariant, positive) symplectic homology and two ways to compute it, one from the functorial properties and the special symplectic topology of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and the other from the Morse-Bott spectral sequences applied to the Boothby-Wang contact structure on the unit circle bundle of the normal bundle of
Y
.

Another problem that motivates our work is the study of asymptotically conical (AC) Ricci-flat Kähler metrics, which goes back to the work of Tian-Yau
[
TY91
]
.
Let
(W,g)
be a complete Kähler manifold. It is called asymptotically conical (AC) if it is asymptotic to a Kähler cone
(\mathcal{C},g_{0})
(see Definition

Li20
]
(see Theorem

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let

be a complete AC Ricci-flat Kähler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
whose asymptotical cone

has a smooth link. Then

is the flat Euclidean metric.

In

By the recent examples constructed in

]
, when

We will use the symplectic techniques in an orbifold setting and reduce the Conjecture

to Shokurov’s conjecture (see Conjecture

) for isolated cone singularities.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

Let
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}
be an orbifold Kähler compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that is associated to a complete AC Kähler metric (obtained from Theorem

As a key ingredient for proving the above results, we derive the following new formula for the minimal discrepancy of isolated Fano cone singularities (see §

for the explanation of these terminologies). Our formula is motivated by the work of McLean (

Let

be an isolated Fano cone singularity of dimension

(1)

2\,\mathrm{md}(o,\mathcal{C})=\inf_{\gamma}\mathrm{lSFT}_{\eta}(\gamma)>-2.

Here

where

denotes the

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Fano cone singularities and AC Kähler metrics

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
n
-dimensional normal affine variety with an isolated
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularity

. Here
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein means that the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{C}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, i.e. there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
kK_{\mathcal{C}}
is a Cartier divisor.
We choose a log resolution of singularity
\mu:\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
such that
\mu
is an isomorphism away from
o\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mu^{-1}(o)
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Then we have an identity

(3)

K_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}=\mu^{*}K_{\mathcal{C}}+\sum_{i}a(E_{i},\mathcal{C})E_{i}

where
a(E_{i},\mathcal{C})\in\mathbb{Q}
is called the discrepancy of the exceptional divisor
E_{i}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Analytically, one can compute discrepancies in the following way. For simplicity, we assume
k=1
(Gorenstein case) and refer to
[
Kol13
, Definition 2.1]
for the general situation. Pick a local generator
s
of
\mathcal{O}(K_{\mathcal{C}})(U)
where
U
is a small neighborhood of
o\in\mathcal{C}
. Then
s
is a non-vanishing holomorphic
n
-form on
U\setminus o
and its pull-back
\mu^{*}s
is in general a meromorphic
n
-form. Choose a general point on
E_{i}
and nearby coordinates
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
such that
E_{i}=\{y_{1}=0\}
and
\mu^{*}s=f(y)dy_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{n}
. Then by definition
a(E_{i},\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{ord}_{\{y_{1}=0\}}f(y)
. For example, if
E
is the exceptional divisor of the standard blowup of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
at a point, then
a(E,\mathbb{C}^{n})=n-1
.

Definition 2.2
.

With the above notation, the singularity
(\mathcal{C},o)
is Kawamata log terminal (klt) if
a(E_{i},\mathcal{C})>-1
for any
i
. The minimal discrepancy of
o\in\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\mathrm{md}(o,\mathcal{C})=\min_{i}\left\{a(E_{i},\mathcal{C});\mu(E_{i})=\{o\}\right\}.

In other words, the singularity
(\mathcal{C},o)
is klt if and only if
\mathrm{md}(o,\mathcal{C})>-1
.
It is known that this definition of klt singularity and the minimal discrepancy do not depend on the choice of log resolution of the singularity. Klt singularities are local analogues of Fano varieties, and they play an important role in birational algebraic geometry (see
[
Kol13
]
).

Conjecture 2.3

(Shokurov)
.

For any klt singularity
o\in\mathcal{C}
of dimension
n
,
\mathrm{md}(o,\mathcal{C})\leq n-1
and the equality holds if and only if
o\in\mathcal{C}
is a smooth point.

Shokurov’s conjecture has important applications in birational algebraic geometry, and it has been proved in dimension
\leq 3
(
[
Mar96
,
Kaw93
]
).

We now explain the concept of a Kähler cone metric on
\mathcal{C}
.
This means that there is smooth function
\mathfrak{r}:\mathcal{C}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{C}\setminus o\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}

\hat{\omega}:=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{r}}^{2}

is the integrable complex structure on

\mathcal{C}^{\circ}
that is isometric to a Riemannian cone metric
d{\mathfrak{r}}^{2}+{\mathfrak{r}}^{2}g^{M}
where

is the link and

.
Define the conic contact form associated to the radius function

as
\eta=-{\mathfrak{r}}^{-1}J_{0}d{\mathfrak{r}}
. Then we have
\hat{\omega}=2{\mathfrak{r}}d{\mathfrak{r}}\wedge\eta+{\mathfrak{r}}^{2}d\eta
. The Reeb vector field, denoted by
{\mathfrak{v}}=J_{0}(\mathfrak{r}\partial_{\mathfrak{r}})
, is a holomorphic Killing vector field on
\mathcal{C}
and satisfies
\eta({\mathfrak{v}})=1
. The associated holomorphic vector field
{\mathfrak{v}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{r}}\partial_{\mathfrak{r}}-\sqrt{-1}J_{0}({\mathfrak{r}}\partial_{\mathfrak{r}})
generates an effective holomorphic action by a (complexified) torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
which we will also denote by
\langle{\mathfrak{v}}\rangle
.
If
m=1
,
{\mathfrak{v}}
and
\eta
are called quasi-regular and otherwise (i.e. when
m\geq 2
)
{\mathfrak{v}}
and
\eta
are called irregular.

The Lie algebra of this torus

is isomorphic
\mathbb{C}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\oplus\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{R}^{m}
whose elements correspond to special holomorphic vector fields on
\mathcal{C}
. The Reeb cone
\mathrm{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is a convex cone in the

[

, Definition 3.2]
) and can also be considered as the dual cone to the cone image of the moment map associated with the Hamiltonian

In general, any element

Conley-Zehnder indices and SFT degrees

For later purposes, we briefly review the basics of Conley-Zehnder indices and related concepts. Given a path
\phi:[0,1]\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
in the symplectic matrices, such that
\phi(0)=\mathrm{id}
and
\phi(1)\in\mathrm{Sp}^{*}(2n):=\left\{A\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n)\left|\det(A-\mathrm{id})\neq 0\right.\right\}
, we can define the integral Conley-Zehnder index
\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi)
, which is characterized by the following properties
[
Gut14
, Proposition 8]
:

(P1)

Given any path
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
, we have
\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\psi\phi\psi^{-1})=\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi)
;

(P2)

The Conley-Zehnder index is homotopy invariant for homotopies of paths from
\mathrm{id}
to
\mathrm{Sp}^{*}(2n)
;

(P3)

Given paths
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
in
\mathrm{Sp}(2n_{1}),\mathrm{Sp}(2n_{2})
respectively, then the path
\phi_{1}\oplus\phi_{2}
in
\mathrm{Sp}(2n_{1}+2n_{2})

\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi_{1})+\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi_{2})
;

(P4)

Given a loop
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
with
\psi(0)=\psi(1)=\mathrm{id}
, then

\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi\psi)=\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi)+2\mu(\psi)

where
\mu(\psi)
is the Maslov index of the loop
\psi
, i.e. the degree of
S^{1}\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n)\to U(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\det_{\mathbb{C}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U(1)
, here
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)\to U(n)
is the homotopy inverse of the inclusion
U(n)\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
;

(P5)

(-1)^{n-\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi)}=\mathrm{sign}\det(\mathrm{id}-\phi(1))
;

(P6)

Let
S
be a symmetric matrix such that
|S|<2\pi
, for
\phi(t)=\exp(tJS)
where
J
is the standard complex structure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, we have
\mu_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\phi)
is half of the signature of
S
.

Properties (

The Conley-Zehnder indices have the following generalizations.

(P7)

\theta\in[0,\frac{2\pi}{m}]
. We now write down a trivialization of
\gamma^{*}\xi^{\oplus N}
by an appropriate choice of basis that is compatible with the trivialization of
\det(\xi)^{\otimes N}
.
First, we consider the Gorenstein case, i.e. when
N=1
. We set

V^{\prime}_{2}=e^{\sqrt{-1}(\beta(n-1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j})\theta}V_{2},\qquad V^{\prime}_{j}=V_{j},\quad 3\leq j\leq n.

Note that we have the Lie derivatives:

\mathscr{L}_{\partial_{\theta}}V^{\prime}_{2}=\sqrt{-1}\left((n-1)\beta+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j}-e_{2}\right)V^{\prime}_{2},\quad\mathscr{L}_{\partial_{\theta}}V^{\prime}_{j}=-\sqrt{-1}e_{j}V^{\prime}_{j}.

So the linearized flow
\{\phi_{\theta}:0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi/m\}
is represented by the diagonal matrix:

(e^{\sqrt{-1}((n-1)\beta+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j})\theta},1,\dots,1)\cdot(e^{-\sqrt{-1}e_{2}\theta},\dots,e^{-\sqrt{-1}e_{n}\theta}).

Since
(n-1)\beta=r
, its CZ index is equal to

2\frac{1}{m}(r+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j})-\sharp\{j:e_{j}>0\}=2\frac{1}{m}(rw_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}w_{j})-(n-1)+\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathrm{ker}(\phi_{2\pi/m}-\mathrm{Id})

where
w_{1}=1,w_{j}=e_{j},2\leq j\leq n
.
Similarly for
1\leq k<m
, the CZ index of
\gamma^{k}
is calculated by using the path
\{\phi_{\theta},0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\frac{k}{m}\}
(see (
16
)):

\displaystyle 2\frac{k}{m}\left(r+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j}\right)-\sum_{j:e_{j}>0}\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{e_{j}k}{m}\right\rfloor+1\right)=2\frac{1}{m}\left(kr+\sum_{j:e_{j}>0}(ke_{j}\;\mathrm{mod}\,m)\right)-\sharp\{j:e_{j}>0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{1}{m}(rw_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}w_{j})-(n-1)+\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathrm{ker}(\phi_{2\pi k/m}-\mathrm{Id})

where we used the identity
(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n})=(k,ke_{2}\,\mathrm{mod}\,m,\dots,ke_{n}\,\mathrm{mod}\,m)
. When
k=m
,
\mathrm{lSFT}(\gamma^{m})
is
2r-2
.
So we indeed get:

(21)

\inf_{1\leq k\leq m}\mathrm{lSFT}(\gamma^{k})=2\min\left\{r,\frac{1}{m}(rw_{1}(g)+\sum_{j=2}^{n}w_{j}(g)),g\neq\mathrm{id}\in G\right\}-2.

When
m>k
,
\mathrm{lSFT}
can be computed similarly but will be strictly larger than (
21
) as
r>0
.
Finally for the case when
N\geq 2
, we can trivialize
\gamma^{*}\xi^{\oplus N}
by using the following basis vectors:

V^{\prime(p)}_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{\sqrt{-1}N(\beta(n-1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n}e_{j})\theta}V_{2},&\text{if}\;p=1,j=2\\
V_{j},&\text{otherwise i.e. if }p=1,3\leq j\leq n\text{\; or\; }2\leq p\leq N,2\leq j\leq n.\end{array}\right.

Then a similar calculation gives the same expression as (
21
).

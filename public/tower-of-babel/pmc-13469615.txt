A quantum fuzzy logic-enhanced evolutionary framework for energy-latency optimization in 6G Edge-IioT
Authors: Siavash Siavashian Rashidi, Ali Broumandnia, Abbas Mirzaei, Ramin Karimi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The transition toward 6G-enabled Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) requires strict Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC), creating a fundamental trade-off with energy consumption. Orchestrating resources in dense, hyper-dynamic edge environments is an NP-hard problem where traditional algorithms often exhibit brittleness and premature convergence. In this paper, we propose a novel multi-objective orchestration framework, QFLN-AMRO, designed to robustly resolve the energy-latency conflict. The architecture integrates a Fractional-Order NSGA-II evolutionary engine with a cognitive Quantum Fuzzy Logic Network (QFLN) to dynamically adjust evolutionary parameters, thereby establishing a superior deterministic Pareto front. To prevent network saturation and queue drift under extreme URLLC stress, we further incorporate a Lyapunov-guided resilience mechanism. The scientific validity and robustness of the proposed framework are rigorously evaluated using a tripartite real-world data setup comprising EUA spatial topologies, Edge-IIoT payloads, and Google Cluster Traces. Empirical results demonstrate that QFLN-AMRO significantly outperforms state-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning models (PPO, GAT) and metaheuristics (GA, PSO, GWO) in minimizing energy expenditure while guaranteeing deterministic latency thresholds. Introduction

The transition from the Fourth Industrial Revolution to the Industry 5.0 paradigm has profoundly reshaped global manufacturing and service landscapes1,2. Fueled by the rapid expansion of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), billions of interconnected, heterogeneous devices now generate vast quantities of critical data3,4. However, centralized cloud architectures, despite their computational power, struggle to meet the stringent low-latency, high-bandwidth, and data privacy requirements of modern industrial applications. Consequently, edge computing has emerged as a key distributed architectural paradigm5,6. By migrating computational resources to the network periphery—in close proximity to data sources—edge architectures significantly reduce transmission latency, alleviate core network congestion, and facilitate real-time, autonomous decision-making in densely connected industrial ecosystems7–9.

A central mechanism in this edge-enabled ecosystem is task offloading. Executing complex, AI-driven, or data-intensive workloads on end-tier IIoT devices is often infeasible due to their inherent limitations, such as constrained battery life and limited processing capacity10. Task offloading addresses this challenge by migrating computationally intensive tasks to more powerful edge servers, thereby extending device longevity and enhancing the overall Quality of Service (QoS)11–13. Nevertheless, orchestrating this offloading process in dense, dynamic environments presents a multifaceted, NP-hard optimization problem5,14.

The complexity of this resource orchestration involves a critical trade-off between algorithmic latency and infrastructure energy consumption. While edge servers reduce the computational strain on IIoT nodes, aggressive workload aggregation at the edge can create thermal hotspots and increase total systemic energy consumption11,15,16. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), data centers and transmission networks accounted for approximately 1.4%–1.7% of global electricity use in 2022. This substantial energy demand increases operational expenditures (OpEx) for industrial entities and conflicts with global mandates for carbon footprint reduction16,17. Conversely, prioritizing energy conservation may require local queueing or delayed execution, which fails to meet the Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) constraints essential for critical operations like industrial robotics and real-time process control18–20.

Although meta-heuristic algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithms (GA) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), are widely used to address this Multi-Objective Optimization Problem (MOOP)5,21,22, existing methods have notable vulnerabilities. Most conventional frameworks are deterministic or single-objective, failing to account for the stochastic nature of modern IIoT workloads, including fluctuating data volumes, dynamic bandwidth, and heterogeneous task lifecycles. Moreover, standard evolutionary algorithms often experience premature convergence when applied to large, highly constrained search spaces, which limits their ability to dynamically balance latency and energy consumption under peak workload stress.

To address this research gap, this paper introduces a novel architecture: the Quantum Fuzzy Logic-Enhanced Adaptive Multi-Objective Resource Orchestrator (QFLN-AMRO). This framework advances beyond static heuristics by integrating a Fractional-Order Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) with an advanced Quantum Fuzzy Logic cognitive layer. This synergy mitigates premature convergence and redefines the Pareto-optimal frontier, enabling a dynamic balance between URLLC latency requirements and green-computing energy objectives. To address this, the QFLN integrates a cognitive layer into the NSGA-II framework, dynamically self-tuning the genetic operators—namely, the crossover and mutation probabilities. This is achieved through a set of fuzzy inference rules that monitor the algorithm’s state, such as population diversity and convergence progress, in real-time. Based on these metrics, the fuzzy membership functions adjust the operators’ rates to strike an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation. For instance, if population diversity decreases, the mutation rate is increased to introduce new genetic material, whereas in later generations, the crossover rate might be prioritized to refine high-quality solutions. Furthermore, the “quantum” aspect of QFLN introduces a significant advantage over classical fuzzy logic by leveraging the principles of quantum mechanics in its cognitive layer. Unlike traditional fuzzy systems that use real-numbered membership values between [0, 1], our QFLN employs qubits (quantum bits). A qubit, existing in a superposition of states, can represent a fuzzy membership value not as a single point, but as a probabilistic distribution. This allows the system to model uncertainty and ambiguity with much higher fidelity. Consequently, the QFLN’s decision-making process for tuning NSGA-II parameters becomes more robust and nuanced, enabling a more intelligent and adaptive search of the complex solution space for energy-latency trade-offs.

Related work and critical literature review

The proliferation of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and Edge Computing has made task offloading a critical mechanism for mitigating the computational constraints of end-devices while optimizing energy and latency. Due to the NP-hard nature of resource orchestration in dense IIoT environments, the literature from 2021 to 2025 has primarily focused on meta-heuristic and learning-based multi-objective optimization strategies. This section provides a critical synthesis of these methodologies, delineating their contributions and identifying the structural limitations that motivate the development of the proposed QFLN-AMRO architecture.

Limitations of conventional meta-heuristic approaches

Nature-inspired meta-heuristics are widely applied to address the Multi-Objective Optimization Problem (MOOP) of balancing energy and latency in IIoT networks. For instance, You and Tang5 employed Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to achieve a more effective energy-latency balance compared to baseline Genetic Algorithms (GA) and Simulated Annealing (SA). Similarly, Wang and Lu14 introduced a hybrid Grey Wolf Optimizer-GA (GWO-GA) that reduced QoS-aware service discovery costs by 30%. Other research has focused on expanding exploration capabilities; Bali et al.22 hybridized the Artificial Bee Colony and Whale Optimization Algorithms (HAWO), reporting significant systemic gains including a 50% reduction in energy and a 25% decrease in latency. Furthermore, Mir et al.21 integrated chaos theory and fuzzy logic into a Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (FLGOA) to extend network lifespan by 16%.

Critical Insight: While effective for baseline optimization, these frameworks are susceptible to premature convergence (stochastic stagnation) when applied to the vast and highly constrained search spaces characteristic of ultra-dense 6G/IIoT environments. Their reliance on standard evolutionary mechanics limits their ability to adapt to high-frequency workload volatility, exposing a critical need for more advanced exploration techniques, such as quantum parallelism, to avoid local optima.

The computational bottleneck of learning-based paradigms

To overcome the static nature of conventional meta-heuristics, recent research has incorporated Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for adaptive offloading. Chen et al.23 modeled the offloading problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and utilized a DDTO algorithm to balance task completion rates with energy consumption. Moghaddasi et al.24 leveraged a Rainbow Deep Q-Network (DQN) in a tiered D2D-Edge-Cloud architecture, improving energy efficiency by 29.8%. For highly dynamic topologies, Li et al.18 combined Graph Neural Networks (GNN) with the Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C) algorithm in aerial Edge-IoT systems. In a similar vein, Deng et al.12 employed Q-learning and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) to reduce latency by up to 30%. Notably, Lin et al.7 pioneered an RL-based algorithm enhanced with quantum parallelism for virtual reality viewport rendering, demonstrating the potential of quantum-inspired approaches.

Critical Insight: Despite their adaptive capabilities, DRL-based architectures are frequently constrained by the “curse of dimensionality” and significant training overheads. In mission-critical industrial settings where Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) is essential, the non-deterministic exploration phases inherent to pure RL models can lead to unacceptable transient latency spikes and dropped tasks.

Environmental sustainability and QoS Trade-offs

The “Green Edge Computing” paradigm has catalyzed architectural designs focused on sustainable IIoT. Alharbi and Aldossary16 showed that edge-fog architectures could reduce carbon emissions by 43%, while Song et al.25 and Chai et al.26 extended orchestration to satellite-based MEC systems for global energy optimization. To ensure strict QoS compliance, particularly in hazardous industrial environments, Lv et al.19 developed a parallel optimization framework that achieved a task success rate above 90%. Other studies have scrutinized resource allocation models; Jiang et al.6 formulated a mixed-integer nonlinear programming problem solved with a binary Fireworks Algorithm, and Hou et al.27 created incentive-based homogeneous allocation models.

Critical Insight: Although these studies address important aspects of architectural sustainability and QoS, they often treat the energy-latency trade-off as a static scalarization problem. Consequently, they typically fail to produce a mathematically rigorous, dynamically adapting Pareto-optimal front that can maintain URLLC standards under extreme network stress, such as massive concurrent task injections.

Recent advancements in Industrial IoT (IIoT) and edge computing environments have increasingly focused on the critical intersection of intelligent resource optimization, trust management, and cybersecurity. On the optimization front, recent studies have introduced advanced meta-learning frameworks for hyperparameter optimization in resource-constrained IoT devices40 and secure, energy-aware high-order clustering algorithms for efficient resource allocation41. Additionally, closed-loop digital twin architectures have been proposed to optimize energy-efficient scheduling in manufacturing systems42. Simultaneously, the inherent vulnerabilities of decentralized Edge-IIoT networks have driven the rapid development of robust security and trust evaluation mechanisms. Notable state-of-the-art solutions include dual-modal physical fingerprints for adaptive device authentication43, dynamic trust evaluation utilizing heterogeneous graph convolutional networks44, and distributed trust-driven intelligence for secure edge-level predictions45. Furthermore, hybrid deep learning architectures—such as CNN-BiLSTM-Lite + + and enhanced XGBoost models—have demonstrated significant potential in multi-class cyber threat and intrusion detection within wireless sensor networks and IIoT environments46,47. While these contemporary approaches substantially advance specific domains of IIoT security and resource management, they often address these challenges in isolation. Our proposed Quantum-Fuzzy NSGA-II framework distinctively bridges this gap by simultaneously tackling multi-objective resource allocation and deterministic system stability under extreme stress conditions, reinforced by Lyapunov optimization.

Datasets and simulation environment

The empirical validation of the QFLN-AMRO framework was conducted within a high-fidelity simulation testbed, meticulously designed to mirror the complex dynamics of a real-world IIoT ecosystem. This environment is architected by integrating three specialized, publicly available datasets, which collectively model the physical network topology and the computational workload. A detailed specification of these datasets is provided in Table 2.

Physical Infrastructure Modeling: The geospatial distribution of the edge infrastructure is modeled using the EUA (Edge Cloud Ecosystem) Dataset. This dataset provides the precise latitude and longitude coordinates for 20 heterogeneous edge servers. The utilization of real-world geographic data is a critical methodological choice, enabling the accurate calculation of great-circle distances between task origins and compute nodes. This, in turn, facilitates a realistic estimation of network propagation delays, moving beyond the oversimplified, and often inaccurate, assumptions common in prior studies.

Computational Workload Synthesis: The computational workload is synthesized from two complementary sources to capture both the specific nature and the operational scale of IIoT demands. The Edge-IIoT Dataset provides the foundational task characteristics, offering granular parameters such as data payload size (input data size) and required computational cycles (task length). These parameters are essential for accurately modeling processing delays and energy expenditure on the edge servers.

To rigorously evaluate the framework’s performance under stress and at scale, these IIoT task profiles are augmented with arrival patterns and intensity levels derived from the Google Cluster Trace. This synthesis results in a demanding and dynamic workload profile, featuring the concurrent injection of 500 heterogeneous computational tasks. This methodology ensures a robust stress test of the proposed orchestration algorithm’s scalability, stability, and resource management efficacy under high-throughput conditions.

Proposed framework: The QFLN-AMRO architecture

To address the inherent limitations of conventional task offloading methods in heterogeneous edge-cloud environments, this paper introduces the QFLN-AMRO framework. Traditional optimization approaches often rely on static scalarization—a technique that aggregates multiple, often conflicting, objectives like latency and energy into a single cost function using predefined weights. This approach suffers from a fundamental limitation: it artificially collapses the multi-dimensional decision space. Consequently, it often yields a single, potentially biased solution that fails to capture the complex, non-linear trade-offs inherent in dynamic network operations, leading to sub-optimal performance.

In stark contrast, the QFLN-AMRO is architected as a native Multi-Objective Optimization Problem (MOOP). It forgoes scalarization entirely, instead focusing on computing the complete Pareto-optimal front. This front comprises a set of non-dominated solutions, where each solution represents an optimal and distinct trade-off between the objectives. The availability of this entire solution set provides a critical strategic advantage: it grants system orchestrators the flexibility to select the most suitable operating point based on dynamic network conditions or shifting Service-Level Agreements (SLAs)—for instance, prioritizing ultra-low latency for critical industrial tasks or emphasizing energy conservation during off-peak hours—without the prohibitive computational cost of re-running the entire optimization.

The framework’s architecture is methodically organized into three sequential layers, ensuring a structured and robust optimization process:

Mathematical Formulation Layer: This foundational layer formally defines the optimization problem. It establishes the precise mathematical models, objective functions for minimizing end-to-end latency and total energy consumption, and the operational constraints of the IIoT ecosystem (e.g., computational capacity of nodes, network bandwidth).Multi-Objective Evolutionary Engine: At the core of the framework lies the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II). We employ this state-of-the-art evolutionary algorithm as the primary optimization engine. NSGA-II is renowned for its efficiency in exploring complex, high-dimensional search spaces and its robust convergence to the true Pareto front, making it exceptionally well-suited for this problem domain.Deterministic Decision Support Layer: Once the Pareto front is generated by the evolutionary engine, this layer provides an autonomous mechanism for selecting the final, optimal solution for deployment. Integration of Lyapunov-Guided resilience mechanism

The QFLN-AMRO framework employs a two-tier optimization strategy to ensure both long-term efficiency and real-time stability, particularly for stringent URLLC requirements. While the QFLN-tuned NSGA-II engine serves as the primary, proactive optimization tool for discovering the Pareto-optimal trade-offs between energy and latency, the Lyapunov-guided mechanism acts as a complementary, reactive control layer. Its integration is crucial for maintaining network stability under unforeseen traffic surges and preventing violations of latency guarantees.

The synergy is realized by incorporating the Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty function directly into the fitness evaluation process of the NSGA-II algorithm. Specifically, we define a Lyapunov function based on the virtual queue lengths of the network nodes. The objective of the Lyapunov optimization is to minimize the “drift” of this function, which corresponds to stabilizing the queues and preventing data backlogs. This drift metric is introduced as a dynamic penalty term into the NSGA-II’s multi-objective fitness function. Consequently, any candidate solution (i.e., a specific resource allocation configuration) that leads to an increase in queue length, thereby threatening URLLC latency bounds, receives a higher penalty score. This forces the evolutionary search to discard unstable solutions and prioritize those that not only reside on the energy-latency Pareto front but also ensure queue stability. In essence, the Lyapunov mechanism acts as a real-time stability-aware constraint, guiding the QFLN-NSGA-II synergy toward robust solutions that guarantee URLLC performance even in high-concurrency scenarios.

Discussion and interpretation

The comprehensive performance evaluation presented in Sect. 4 provides strong empirical evidence for the superiority of the proposed QFLN-AMRO framework. This section moves beyond the empirical results to offer a theoretical interpretation of these findings. We analyze the underlying mechanisms driving the framework’s performance, dissect the dynamics of multi-objective trade-offs, and examine the architectural foundations of its resilience in dense Edge-IIoT environments.

Algorithmic synergy and convergence dynamics

The convergence analysis highlights a fundamental difference between conventional metaheuristics (e.g., GA, PSO) and the QFLN-AMRO architecture. Standard algorithms are susceptible to premature convergence and stochastic stagnation, particularly when navigating the high-dimensional, combinatorial search space typical of large-scale task offloading scenarios (> 500 tasks). While the baseline AMRO employs NSGA-II to preserve population diversity, it lacks a mechanism for adaptive control.

The principal innovation of QFLN-AMRO lies in the synergy between its Fractional-Order Calculus core and its Quantum Fuzzy Logic Network (QFLN) controller. The fractional-order component introduces a long-range memory property, enabling the optimization process to utilize historical gradient information and effectively avoid local optima. Simultaneously, the QFLN functions as an intelligent cognitive controller. It dynamically maps Pareto front topological states, such as hypervolume growth and solution spread, to fuzzy membership functions. These functions, in turn, leverage quantum probability amplitudes to precisely adjust mutation and crossover rates. This synergy facilitates the rapid and steep descent in objective cost observed within the initial 20 generations, ensuring the orchestrator achieves near-optimal solutions with the fast convergence required for real-time 5G/6G Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) services.

Resolution of multi-objective trade-offs

A critical finding of this research is the effective resolution of the inherent conflict between latency (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{f}_{1}$$\end{document}) and energy consumption (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{f}_{2}$$\end{document}). Simple heuristic schedulers like Greedy or Round-Robin may achieve low latency for specific task subsets, but our results show this comes at the cost of significant energy inefficiencies and thermal imbalances (“hotspots”) across the edge infrastructure. In contrast, QFLN-AMRO synthesizes a mathematically robust, non-dominated Pareto front, characterized by a superior Hypervolume (HV) and optimal solution distribution (Spread). This provides network operators with a diverse set of high-quality “knee-point” solutions. The framework integrates spatial awareness, using Haversine distance for propagation modeling, and spectral efficiency awareness through NOMA-inspired protocols. This allows for intelligent load balancing, leading to assignment matrices that reduce total energy consumption by up to 25% compared to GA, while limiting the associated latency increase to a marginal 5%. This demonstrates the framework’s ability to achieve true multi-objective harmony rather than collapsing into a single-objective solution.

Infrastructural resilience via lyapunov-guided admission control

The stress-testing scenarios, which involved scaling the network to 1000 IIoT tasks with only 10 active servers, revealed critical scalability limitations in baseline algorithms. Under such high-density conditions, conventional schedulers suffer from uncontrolled queue growth and cascading latency failures. QFLN-AMRO circumvents these bottlenecks through its integrated Lyapunov-guided optimization strategy. The framework incorporates a proactive admission control mechanism that continuously monitors the system’s drift-plus-penalty trajectory (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\Delta\:}Q\left(t\right)$$\end{document}). Instead of allowing the network to reach saturation, QFLN-AMRO strategically rejects tasks that would violate hard delay constraints under heavy congestion. This load-shedding is not a system failure but a strategic self-preservation mechanism. It guarantees that the core communication backbone remains uncongested, thereby maintaining high spectral efficiency (> 926 bps/Hz) and ensuring equitable resource distribution (confirmed by Jain’s Fairness Index) for all admitted mission-critical payloads.

To ensure that the observed performance gains were not an artifact of stochastic variation, a rigorous statistical validation was performed. For Industry 4.0 systems where operational predictability is paramount, QFLN-AMRO provides a mathematically validated and robust performance envelope.

Conclusion and future work

This paper introduced, formulated, and empirically validated the Quantum Fuzzy Logic-Enhanced Adaptive Multi-Objective Resource Orchestrator (QFLN-AMRO), a novel framework designed to address the NP-hard problem of dynamic task offloading in heterogeneous Edge-IIoT environments. By treating resource allocation as a native Multi-Objective Optimization Problem (MOOP), our approach circumvents the inherent limitations of traditional methods that rely on static scalarization, thereby avoiding premature convergence to sub-optimal solutions. The core of the QFLN-AMRO framework is a synergistic integration of a Quantum Fuzzy Logic Network, which adaptively tunes the operators of a Fractional-Order NSGA-II engine. This architecture is further enhanced by NOMA-aware transmission protocols and a Lyapunov-guided admission control mechanism, which collectively ensure both spectral efficiency and systemic stability.

Rigorous evaluations were conducted within a high-fidelity simulation environment, leveraging a composite of real-world and synthetic datasets (EUA, Edge-IIoT, and Google Cluster Traces) to ensure the clinical relevance of our findings. The results demonstrate that QFLN-AMRO consistently outperforms conventional metaheuristic (e.g., GA, PSO) and heuristic (e.g., Greedy, Round-Robin) baselines. Statistically, the framework’s superiority was confirmed by a highly significant omnibus ANOVA result (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p\ll\:0.001$$\end{document}), showcasing its ability to redefine the achievable Pareto-optimal frontier. Furthermore, QFLN-AMRO exhibited exceptional stability, evidenced by a minimal coefficient of variation ( 0.0105) across 30 independent runs. Under extreme load conditions, its integrated resilience mechanism effectively prevented network saturation, ensuring robust structural survivability and SLA compliance.

In summary, QFLN-AMRO provides a deterministic and computationally efficient solution that simultaneously achieves ultra-low latency and promotes energy-conscious green computing. It establishes a robust and scalable architectural foundation poised to meet the demanding operational requirements of sustainable Industry 4.0 applications.

Hardware-in-the-Loop and Digital Twin Validation: To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment, future work will focus on validating the framework on physical testbeds. This involves implementing QFLN-AMRO in Kubernetes-based containerized environments (e.g., KubeEdge) and integrating it with a Digital Twin (DT). This Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) approach is essential for quantifying performance against practical challenges such as virtualization overhead and thermal throttling.

Migration to Native Quantum Hardware: The success of the quantum-inspired cognitive engine motivates a long-term research goal: mapping the simulated quantum states and superposition mechanics onto physical qubit-based hardware. Executing the orchestration logic on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices could unlock unprecedented computational advantages, potentially achieving true quantum supremacy for real-time edge resource management.

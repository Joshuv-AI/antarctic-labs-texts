The 2 ​ p 2p order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principles related to the offset linear canonical transform
Jia-Yin Peng, Bing-Zhao Li

Abstract
The uncertainty principle is one of the fundamental tools for time-frequency analysis in harmonic analysis, revealing the intrinsic trade-off between time and frequency resolutions. With the continuous development of various advanced time-frequency analysis methods based on the Fourier transform, investigating uncertainty principles associated with these methods has become one of the most interesting topics. This paper studies the uncertainty principles related to the offset linear canonical transform, including the Plancherel-Parseval-Rayleigh identity, the 2 ​ p 2p order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle and the sharpened Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle. Numerical simulations are also proposed to validate the derived results.

Introduction

Heisenberg inequality
heisenberg
,
weyl
,
kennard
was first proposed by the German physicist Heisenberg in 1927, which indicates that in a quantum system, the position and momentum of a particle cannot be precisely measured simultaneously. In 1928, Weyl and Pauli demonstrated that the energy of a signal and that of its Fourier transform (FT) also satisfy the Heisenberg inequality. The FT
FT
,
FT2
,
FT3
is one of the most significant analytical tools in applied mathematics, enabling the transformation of signals from the time domain to the frequency domain. Consequently, the Heisenberg inequality reveals an inherent limitation in the simultaneous concentration of energy in both the time and frequency domains
HPW
, which is commonly recognized as the Heisenberg uncertainty principle in signal processing. This principle states that the time duration
\Delta_{f(t)}
of a signal
f(t)
and the frequency bandwidth
\Delta_{\hat{f}(\xi)}
of its FT
\hat{f}(\xi)
are constrained by the following inequality
UN

\Delta_{f(t)}\Delta_{\hat{f}(\xi)}\geq\frac{1}{2},

(1)

where

\Delta_{f(t)}=\left[\frac{1}{||f(t)||^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(t-\alpha_{t}\right)^{2}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2)

\Delta_{\hat{f}(\xi)}=\left[\frac{1}{||\hat{f}(\xi)||^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(\xi-\alpha_{\xi}\right)^{2}\left|\hat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3)

and
\alpha_{t}
and
\alpha_{\xi}
are defined as

\alpha_{t}=\frac{1}{||f(t)||^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}t\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt,

(4)

\alpha_{\xi}=\frac{1}{||\hat{f}(\xi)||^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\xi\left|\hat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi.

(5)

This uncertainty principle conveys two valuable insights
xu
. First, the resolutions in the time and frequency domains cannot be simultaneously increased without bound, as their product is constrained by a nonzero lower limit. Second, there exists a trade-off relationship between time and frequency resolution, meaning that improving frequency resolution inevitably leads to a reduction in time resolution, and vice versa. Time resolution and frequency resolution are key parameters in signal detection. High time resolution facilitates precise localization, while high frequency resolution enables accurate estimation of spectral components, such as velocity or modulation characteristics. Therefore, the uncertainty principle has become one of the important topics in harmonic analysis
UPDP
,
UP1
,
UPR
,
UP2
,
UP3
,
UP4
.

Building on the above research, John Michael Rassias proposed the generalized Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in 2004
HPW
, which states that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and any fixed but arbitrary
p\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

\displaystyle\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\lvert f\rvert^{2}}}\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\lvert\hat{f}\rvert}}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt[p]{2}}\sqrt[p]{\lvert E_{p,f}\rvert}.

(6)

In the following year, he presented the Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle
HW
, which can be expressed as

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{1/p}(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|\hat{f}\right|^{2}}^{1/p}\geq E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{2/p}/4\pi.

(7)

To obtain a more precise bound for practical applications, Rassias proposed a sharpened version of the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the Fourier analysis in 2006
SHW
, which is stated as follows

\displaystyle\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\lvert f\rvert^{2}}}\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\lvert\hat{f}\rvert}}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt[p]{2}}\sqrt[p]{\lvert E_{p,f}^{*}\rvert^{2}}.

(8)

The above uncertainty principles are all established based on the FT
FT
,
FT2
,
FT3
, which can convert time domain signals into the frequency domain to facilitate the analysis of their frequency characteristics. However, for non-stationary signals, the FT falls short in providing optimal time-frequency resolution, as it cannot effectively capture the temporal variations of the spectrum. To address this limitation, a range of advanced time-frequency analysis tools such as the fractional Fourier transform (FrFT)
FrFt
,
FrFt2
,
FrFt3
, the linear canonical transform (LCT)
LCT
,
LCT2
and the offset linear canonical transform (OLCT) have been developed.

The OLCT is the generalization of the FT, FrFT and LCT
OLCT
,
OLCTtwo
. It has six parameters
(a,b,c,d,\tau,\eta)
, which makes it more flexible. Mathematically, the OLCT of a signal
f(t)
with parameter

J=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|c}a&b&\tau\\
c&d&\eta\\
\end{array}\right]
is defined as
OLCT

\displaystyle O_{J}f(\xi)=O_{J}[f(t)](\xi)=\begin{cases}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(t)K_{J}(t,\xi)dt,b\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}[\frac{cd(\xi-\tau)^{2}}{2}+\xi\eta]}f(d\xi-d\tau),b=0\end{cases}.

(9)

In recent years, various uncertainty principles based on the FrFT
FrFtUN
,
FrFtUN2
,
FrFtDP
, LCT
LCTUN
,
DB
and OLCT
OLCTUN1
,
OLCTUN2
,
OLCTUN3
have been extensively investigated. However, the
2p

The OLCT has demonstrated considerable theoretical and practical potential in applications such as time-frequency analysis, filter design, and target detection
OLCTthree
. Therefore, studying the uncertainty principle associated with the OLCT is of both theoretical and practical importance.

In this paper, three types of the Heisenberg uncertainty principles related to the OLCT are proposed. In section

2.1
The Plancherel-Parseval-Rayleigh identity of the OLCT

To derive the
2p
order Heisenberg uncertainty principles in the OLCT domain, we first derived the Plancherel-Parseval-Rayleigh identity, which can be represented as the following Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f(t)
,
O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
,
(\xi-\xi_{m})^{p}O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
p\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,...\}
. Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\lvert O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi=b^{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\rvert^{2}dt,

(10)

where
g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)=\frac{d^{p}}{dt^{p}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\beta t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}}f(t)
with
\beta=2\pi\sigma_{m}
,
\sigma_{m}=\frac{\xi_{m}-\tau}{2\pi b}
.

Proof.

Regarding the left-hand side of (
10
), it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\lvert O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{j}2\pi b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}[\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}-\frac{1}{b}t(\xi-\tau)-\frac{1}{b}\xi(d\tau-b\eta)+\frac{d}{2b}(\xi^{2}+\tau^{2})]}dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}[\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}-\frac{(\xi-\tau)t}{b}]}dt\right|^{2}d\xi.

Since
g(t)=f(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{j}\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{j}\frac{(\xi-\tau)}{b}t}dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|\hat{g}(\frac{\xi-\tau}{2\pi b})\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\xi-\tau-(\xi_{m}-\tau)]^{2p}\left|\hat{g}(\frac{\xi-\tau}{2\pi b})\right|^{2}d(\xi-\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(2\pi b)^{2p}(\sigma-\sigma_{m})^{2p}\left|\hat{g}(\sigma)\right|^{2}d(2\pi b\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi b)^{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma-\sigma_{m})^{2p}\left|\hat{g}(\sigma)\right|^{2}d\sigma.

(11)

The following identity can be derived based on the result presented in
HPW
.

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma-\sigma_{m})^{2p}\left|\hat{g}(\sigma)\right|^{2}d\sigma=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\rvert^{2}dt.

(12)

Substituting (
12
) into (
2.1
) completes the proof.
∎

2.2
The
2p
order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT

We define

(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|f(t)\right|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{2}(x)(t-t_{m})^{2p}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt,

(13)

and

(\mu_{2p})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi,

(14)

to establish the
2p
order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the OLCT domain as following Theorem
2
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
f(t)
,
O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
,
(\xi-\xi_{m})^{p}O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\omega(t)
be a real weight function. Then the
2p
order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the OLCT domain is as follows

(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|f(t)\right|^{2}}(\mu_{2p})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}}\geq\frac{b^{2p}}{4}(E_{p,f})^{2},

(15)

where

E_{p,f}=\sum_{q=0}^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}D_{q}F_{q},

(16)

D_{q}=(-1)^{q}\frac{p}{p-q}\binom{p-q}{q},F_{q}=\sum_{n=0}^{q}B_{qn}I_{qn}+2\sum_{0\leq i<z\leq q}C_{qiz}I_{qiz}.

Here

B_{qn}=\binom{q}{n}^{2}\beta^{2(q-n)},C_{qiz}=s_{qi}\binom{q}{i}\binom{q}{z}\beta^{2q-z-i},s_{qi}=(-1)^{q-i}\in\{\pm 1,\pm\mathrm{j}\},

I_{qn}=(-1)^{q-2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}^{(p-2q)}(t)\left|f^{(n)}(t)\right|^{2}dt,

I_{qiz}=(-1)^{q-2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}^{(p-2q)}(t)\mathrm{Re}\left((-1)^{q-\frac{i+z}{2}}f^{(i)}(t)\overline{f^{(z)}(t)}\right)dt.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle M_{p}=

\displaystyle(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|f(t)\right|^{2}}(\mu_{2p})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{2}(t)(t-t_{m})^{2p}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right).

From (
10
) and (
17
), we obtain

\displaystyle M_{p}=

\displaystyle b^{2p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{2}(x)(t-t_{m})^{2p}\left|g_{\beta}(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\right|^{2}dt\right).

(18)

Based on (
18
) and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

M_{p}\geq b^{2p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\omega_{p}(t)g_{\beta}(t)g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\right|dt\right)^{2},

(19)

where
\omega_{p}(t)=(t-t_{m})^{p}\omega(t)
.
Applying the complex inequality to (
19
), we have

M_{p}\geq\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}(t)\left(g_{\beta}(t)\overline{g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)}+\overline{g_{\beta}(t)}g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\right)dt\right)^{2}.

(20)

Using the differential identity on (

), we obtain

M_{p}\geq\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}(t)\left(\sum_{q=0}^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}D_{q}\frac{d^{p-2q}}{dt^{p-2q}}\left|g_{\beta}^{(q)}(t)\right|^{2}\right)dt\right)^{2}.

(21)

Applying the Lagrange type differential identity
HPW
to
g_{\beta}^{(q)}(t)
, we get

\displaystyle g_{\beta}^{(q)}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{q}B_{qn}\left|g^{(n)}(t)\right|^{2}+2\sum_{0\leq i<z\leq q}C_{qiz}\mathrm{Re}((-1)^{q-\frac{i+z}{2}}g^{(i)}(t)g^{\overline{(z)}}(t)).

(22)

From the integral identity and (
22
), we find

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}(t)\frac{d^{p-2q}}{dt^{p-2q}}\left|g^{(n)}(t)\right|^{2}dt=(-1)^{p-2q}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}^{p-2q}(t)\left|g^{(n)}(t)\right|^{2}dt=I_{qn},

(23)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}(t)\frac{d^{p-2q}}{dt^{p-2q}}\mathrm{Re}((-1)^{q-\frac{i+z}{2}}g^{(i)}(t)g^{\overline{(z)}}(t))dt

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-2q}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{p}^{p-2q}(t)\mathrm{Re}((-1)^{q-\frac{i+z}{2}}g^{(i)}(t)g^{\overline{(z)}}(t))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{qiz}.

Taking (
23
) and (
24
) into (

), we obtain

\displaystyle M_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(\sum_{q=0}^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}D_{q}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{q}B_{qn}I_{qn}+2\sum_{0\leq i<z\leq q}C_{qiz}I_{qiz}\right)\right)^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(E_{p,f}\right)^{2}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.3
The Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT

Let

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t-t_{m}\right|^{p}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt,

(30)

and

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\xi-\xi_{m}\right|^{p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi,

then we can obtain the following Theorem
3
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
f(t)
,
O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
,
(\xi-\xi_{m})^{p}O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For
p\geq 2
and any fixed but arbitrary real constants

,

, the Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT holds as follows

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{1/p}(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{1/p}\geq\frac{b}{2}E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}},

(32)

where

Proof.

Firstly,

\displaystyle(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t-t_{m}\right|^{p}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left|t-t_{m}\right|^{2}\left|f(t)\right|^{\frac{4}{p}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\right]^{\frac{2}{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left|f(t)\right|^{2\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\right)^{1/\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}dt\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p}}.

Then applying the Hölder inequality to (

), we obtain

\displaystyle(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((t-t_{m})^{2}\left|f(t)\right|^{\frac{4}{p}}\right)\left(\left|f(t)\right|^{2\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(t-t_{m})^{2}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=(\mu_{2}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}\triangleq\phi_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{2},

so

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\phi_{\left|f\right|^{2}}/E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

(34)

Equality holds in (

) if and only if

\left|t-t_{m}\right|^{p}E_{\left|f\right|^{2}}=(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|f\right|^{2}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}E_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\xi-\xi_{m}\right|^{p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=(\mu_{2}^{*})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}\triangleq\phi_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{2},

so

(\mu_{p}^{*})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\phi_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}/E_{\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

Based on the Parseval property of the OLCT
OLCTP

then we find

2.4
The sharpened Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT

This section builds upon the
2p
order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the OLCT domain and presents the sharpened Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle, which provides a more precise lower bound.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
f(t)
,
O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
,
(\xi-\xi_{m})^{p}O_{f}^{J}(\xi)
belong to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For any fixed but arbitrary
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the sharpened Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT holds as follows

\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|f\right|^{2}}}\sqrt[2p]{(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|O_{f}^{J}\right|^{2}}}\geq\frac{b}{\sqrt[p]{2}}\sqrt[p]{\left|E_{p,f}^{*}\right|}.

(39)

The equality in (
39
) holds when

A=||u(t)||x_{0}-||v(t)||y_{0},

(40)

where
u(t)=\omega(t)(t-t_{m})^{p}g_{\beta}(t)
,
v(t)=g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)
and
x_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|v(t)\right|\left|h(t)\right|dt
,
y_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u(t)\right|\left|h(t)\right|dt
,
\left\|h(t)\right\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|h(t)\right|^{2}dt=1
.

Proof.

In fact, from (
10
) in Theorem
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{p}^{*}=

\displaystyle M_{p}-b^{2p}A^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{2p})_{\omega,\left|f(t)\right|^{2}}(\mu_{2p})_{\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}}-b^{2p}A^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{2}(t)(t-t_{m})^{2p}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-\xi_{m})^{2p}\left|O_{f}^{J}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\right)-b^{2p}A^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{2p}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega^{2}(t)(t-t_{m})^{2p}\left|g_{\beta}(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\right|^{2}dt\right)-A^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{2p}\left(\left\|u\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}-A^{2}\right).

(41)

From the positive definiteness of the following Gram determinant, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\left\|u\right\|^{2}&(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)&y_{0}\\
(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)&\left\|v\right\|^{2}&x_{0}\\
y_{0}&x_{0}&1\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}-(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)-\left[\left\|u\right\|^{2}x_{0}^{2}-2(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)x_{0}y_{0}+\left\|v\right\|^{2}y_{0}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}-(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)-A^{2},

then we find

\displaystyle M_{p}^{*}\geq

\displaystyle b^{2p}(\left|u\right|,\left|v\right|)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{2p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u\right|\left|v\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{2p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\omega_{p}(t)g_{\beta}(t)g_{\beta}^{(p)}(t)\right|dt\right)^{2}.

(42)

Equations (
19
) and (
2.4
) are exactly the same, so

M_{p}^{*}\geq\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(E_{p,f}\right)^{2}.

Using (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle M_{p}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(E_{p,f}\right)^{2}+b^{2p}A^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2p}}{4}\left(\left(E_{p,f}\right)^{2}+4A^{2}\right).

(43)

So the
2p
order sharpened Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT is as follows

\sqrt[2p]{M_{p}}\geq\frac{b}{\sqrt[p]{2}}\sqrt[p]{\left|E_{p,f}^{*}\right|},

where
\left|E_{p,f}^{*}\right|=\sqrt{E_{p,f}^{2}+4A^{2}}
.
Hence, Theorem
4
is proved.
∎

Theorem
2
and Theorem
4
provide two lower bounds for the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle of the OLCT. From a mathematical standpoint, a larger value on the right side of the inequality indicates a more precise and reliable estimate of the left side. Therefore, it is crucial to study the sharpened version of the uncertainty principle. From a practical signal processing perspective, a smaller lower bound implies that higher time-frequency concentration is theoretically permissible in time-frequency analysis, thereby characterizing the ideal theoretical limit. However, it remains necessary to derive sharpened version of the uncertainty principle in the OLCT domain with larger lower bounds. The reason is that a smaller theoretical bound merely reflects the potential limit under ideal conditions, whereas actual signals often fail to attain this level of concentration. In contrast, sharpened lower bounds, although numerically larger, provide more precise estimates that are closer to the true achievable limits.

4
Conclusion

The uncertainty principle plays a pivotal role in harmonic analysis and its applications. In this paper, the Plancherel-Parseval-Rayleigh identity in the offset linear canonical transform (OLCT) domain is derived. Based on this identity, the
2p
order Heisenberg-Weyl-Pauli uncertainty principle of the OLCT is established. Subsequently, the Heisenberg-Weyl uncertainty principle in the OLCT domain is deduced. The sharpened Heisenberg-Weyl-Pauli uncertainty principle is then proposed and further validated through numerical simulations. The results of this work provide theoretical advancements and provide valuable insights for practical applications in time-frequency analysis within the OLCT domain.

Multiple-use prediction and calibration for all future values: Exact simultaneous tolerance bands for multiple and polynomial regressions
Authors: Lingjiao Wang, Yang Han, Wei Liu, Frank Bretz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multiple-use prediction and calibration for all future values play an important role in many areas, including health and medical research. Simultaneous tolerance bands (STBs) can be employed for these purposes. This article studies the construction of exact STBs for multiple regression over any given rectangular covariate regions and for polynomial regression over any given covariate intervals. We first fill the gap in constructing the exact STBs for multiple regression over rectangular regions. We then propose a general form of STBs and develop a method for computing the critical constants for all STBs considered, including existing ones in the literature. A new STB is also proposed for both multiple and polynomial regressions. This new STB and several existing forms are then compared under the average shift (AS) criterion. Numerical results indicate that the new STB performs best under the AS criterion and is therefore recommended. In addition, a computationally efficient algorithm is presented. Real-life examples are provided for illustration.

Introduction

Motivation and scope

Multiple-use prediction and multiple-use calibration for all future observations in linear regression have a long-standing history and a wide range of applications. Simultaneous tolerance bands (STBs) are particularly effective for both purposes.

Lieberman and Miller 1 first introduced STBs for multiple-use prediction. In this context, the objective is to construct a band that contains a large proportion of an infinite number of future response values ( y ) at a pre-specified confidence level. For example, elevated blood pressure in newborns can serve as a crucial early signal for underlying conditions such as kidney blood vessel issues, tumours, or thyroid anomalies. Ensuring the well-being of infants necessitates the availability of accurate prediction intervals for systolic blood pressure. Let the response variable be the systolic blood pressure of an infant ( y ), with the covariates birth weight ( x1 ) and age ( x2 ). For given values of x1 and x2 , prediction intervals for future y values are of interest.

Single-use prediction versus multiple-use prediction. For a single future observation of the response y , a single-use prediction interval can be constructed for a given pair (x1,x2) . However, since these covariates may take any value within the covariate region, it is often necessary, based on a single dataset, to make simultaneous inferences for all possible future responses across the entire region. Applying single-use prediction intervals for this purpose is invalid, as they do not maintain the overall confidence level when used repeatedly. This is where multiple-use prediction becomes essential. It provides prediction intervals that can be applied to infinitely many future observations while guaranteeing the specified overall confidence level.

Statistical calibration, involving two quantities x and y , has a rich history dating back to Eisenhart. 2 In calibration problems, the primary interest lies in the inference of the covariate x rather than the response y . Assuming that y and x follow a linear regression model, it is desirable to construct a confidence set for x given y , which contains the true x -value with a specified confidence level. Typically, the covariate x is a desirable but expensive or difficult-to-measure quantity, while y is a surrogate that is cheaper or easier to measure.

An example is the estimation of gestational age, a key indicator for maternal and fetal health. Note that accurately measuring gestational age can be challenging. But fetal bone lengths, such as the biparietal diameter of the fetus, measured using B-mode ultrasound or colour Doppler ultrasound, can be used as surrogate measures to provide reliable estimates of the gestational age. In such cases, based on a single training dataset, multiple-use calibration enables the estimation of gestational age ( x ) given any future observed measurement of fetal bone length ( y ).

More broadly, simultaneous intervals play an important role in inverse problems in clinical research. For example, in multi-dimensional effective dose studies, the goal is to determine the set of predictor values corresponding to a target response level, together with appropriate confidence regions.3,4 Such problems naturally require simultaneous inference over a range of covariate values. Simultaneous intervals can be used to obtain confidence sets for level sets of the regression function, thereby providing an alternative approach to solving such calibration problems. This connection is also considered in drug stability studies, where inverse simultaneous confidence bands are used to estimate the true shelf life.5–7

Given the motivating examples above, a method that provides both multiple-use prediction and multiple-use calibration for all future values is highly desirable. In this article, we focus on the construction and comparison of exact STBs. It is worth noting that many existing methods in the literature are conservative; see Han 8 for a review. The process of making inferences based on a STB, particularly the construction of confidence sets for calibration, has been studied with various applications; see, for example, Han et al.9–11 for an overview.

Simultaneous tolerance bands

Let yx denote a future y -value corresponding to a given covariate value x and assume yx is independent of the observed dataset Ω={(x0iT,y0i),i=1,…,n} . For example, yx could represent the systolic blood pressure of a new infant or the biparietal diameter of a new fetus. An exact (β,γ) -STB [L(x;Ω),U(x;Ω)] for linear regression over the covariate region x∈Rx satisfies (1) PΩ{Pyx{L(x;Ω)≤yx≤U(x;Ω)|Ω}≥βforallx∈Rx}=γ where β,γ∈(0,1) , the probability Pyx{⋅|Ω} is taken with respect to yx and conditional on Ω , and the probability PΩ{⋅} is taken with respect to Ω .

In this article, we focus on the construction of one-sided STBs, since in many real applications only one bound (upper or lower) is of interest, particularly when assessing whether a parameter exceeds a critical threshold. For example, in blood pressure studies, the upper bound is used to evaluate the risk of hypertension. Similarly, in blood alcohol concentration analysis, the lower limit is used to determine whether a given threshold has been exceeded. A one-sided upper STB has L(x;Ω)=−∞ in (1), meaning only the upper bound U(x;Ω) is of interest, while a one-sided lower STB has U(x;Ω)=∞ , meaning only the lower bound L(x;Ω) is of interest.

In general, there are four methods for constructing STBs. The first method, presented by Lieberman and Miller, 1 Lieberman et al. 12 and Scheffé, 13 focusses on the construction of central β -content and γ -confidence level STBs, denoted as central (β,γ) -STBs. However, these central STBs are two-sided, and even the exact versions tend to be conservative as β -content, γ -confidence STBs. The second method, known as the probability set method by Wilson 14 and Limam and Thomas, 15 resembles Rao, 16 p.473 confidence set construction and relies on a confidence set for the unknown parameters of the regression model. STBs constructed using the probability set method are also two-sided and conservative. The third method applies to multiple regression but only over specific ellipsoidal covariate regions, proposed by Odeh and Mee 17 for the one-sided case and Mee et al. 18 for the two-sided case. These STBs are also conservative when applied to non-ellipsoidal covariate regions, such as rectangular regions that are often more practical. The fourth method, proposed by Han et al.9,10 for the one-sided case and Han et al. 11 for the two-sided case, is a more general approach that constructs exact (β,γ) -STBs of a specific form for polynomial regression over any given covariate intervals. This remains the only exact method for polynomial regression reported to date.

To the best of our knowledge, no exact (β,γ) -STB for multiple regression over a rectangular covariate region has yet been reported in the literature. In this article, a new form of exact (β,γ) -STBs is proposed, and the optimal STB within this form under the average shift (AS) criterion is computed for both multiple and polynomial regressions. The proposed STBs are not only exact but also achieve smaller average shifts compared to the four construction methods mentioned above.

Our contributions

Consider a multiple regression model, (2) y=α0+α1x1+⋯+αp−1xp−1+ϵ=xTα+ϵ where x=(1,x1,…,xp−1)T=(1,x(0)T)T with x(0)=(x1,…,xp−1)T being the vector of the p−1 covariates, α=(α0,…,αp−1)T is the vector of unknown regression coefficients, and ϵ is the random error with normal distribution N(0,σ2) .

The first objective of this article is to construct exact one-sided STBs for multiple regression over a given rectangular covariate region L:={x(0):a1≤x1≤b1,…,ap−1≤xp−1≤bp−1} , where −∞≤ai≤bi≤∞(i=1,…,p−1) are given constants. It is noteworthy that the STBs of Odeh and Mee 17 and Mee et al. 18 are defined over the covariate region D=Dτ2∖Dτ1 only, where 0≤τ1<τ2 are given constants and (3) Dτ={x(0):(x(0)−x¯(0))TS−1(x(0)−x¯(0))≤τ2} with x¯(0)=(x¯1,…,x¯p−1)T being the sample mean and S denoting the sample covariance matrix of the observed values of the p−1 covariates x(0) in the observed data Ω={(x0iT,y0i),i=1,…,n} . For simple linear regression with p=2 , the covariate region Dτ reduces to a covariate interval that is symmetric about the mean of the observed covariate values in Ω ; these STBs become conservative when used for covariate intervals which are not symmetric about the mean of the observed covariate values. These STBs become even more conservative when applied to multiple regression over a rectangular covariate region x(0)∈L , as the smallest ellipsoidal covariate region D that contains the given rectangular region L needs to be used in the methods of Odeh and Mee 17 and Mee et al. 18

The second objective of this article is to propose a new form of exact one-sided (β,γ) -STBs and to compute the optimal STB within this form under the AS criterion defined in Section 2, for both multiple and polynomial regressions. Then we compare this new STB with STBs of other forms available in the literature, and identify the best STB among them under the AS criterion.

The average width (AW) of a simultaneous confidence band (SCB) has been widely used as an optimality criterion for comparing different two-sided SCBs since Gafarian. 19 Several studies have compared SCBs using either the AW for two-sided bands or the average shift (AS) for one-sided bands; see Naiman 20 and Piegorsch. 21 A smaller AW or AS corresponds to a narrower band, and hence a better band. Therefore, for the one-sided STBs considered in this article, the AS criterion is adopted as the optimality criterion. The shift of an STB from the fitted regression line provides an intuitively appealing measure of the narrowness of the one-sided STB, with a smaller AS indicating a better STB.

Our numerical results show that the newly proposed STB is best under the AS criterion for both multiple and polynomial regressions.

Methods

As the focus of this article is on the construction of exact one-sided STBs, the upper one-sided (β,γ) -STBs, (−∞,U(x;Ω)] , are considered without loss of generality, which satisfy (4) PΩ{Pyx{yx≤U(x;Ω)|Ω}≥βfor allx(0)∈L}=γ while the lower one-sided STBs satisfy PΩ{Pyx{L(x;Ω)≤yx|Ω}≥βfor allx(0)∈L}=γ. Several forms of STBs

In the statistical literature, three principal forms of k(x) have been considered. The first form, proposed by Odeh and Mee 17 for ellipsoidal covariate regions, is kOM(x)=λOM[zβ+(p+2)xT(XTX)−1x] where zβ is the 100β th percentile of the standard normal distribution. The same form is also considered in Han et al. 9 for constructing the exact STBs for polynomial regression over any finite covariate interval. The STBs of Han et al. 9 are denoted as OMH hereafter.

The second form of k(x) , proposed by Han et al. 5 from the perspective of SCBs, is kUV(x)=zβθ+λUVxT(XTX)−1x+zβ2ξ, where θ=2/(n−p)Γ((n−p+1)/2)/Γ((n−p)/2) and ξ=1/θ2−1 . It is noteworthy that the zβ/θ increases as the sample size n increases. For a large sample size n (say n>1000 ), the value of λUV could be negative due to the large ( zβ/θ )-value; see, for example, the gestational age example in Section 4.2. According to (4), an upper STB satisfies PΩ{xTα+zβσ≤U(x;Ω)for allx(0)∈L}=γ. Hence, an upper (β,γ) -STB is a γ level upper one-sided SCB for the 100β th percentile line xTα+zβσ .

The third form, based on the commonly used pointwise prediction intervals using the fact that (yx−xTα^)∼N(0,σ2(1+xT(XTX)−1x)) , is given by kSP(x)=λSP1+xT(XTX)−1x. This form is considered by Chvosteková 22 for statistical calibration.

In this article, a new form of k(x) is considered, given by (9) kNEW(x)=λNEW[zβ+ωxT(XTX)−1x] where (c1,c2,c3,c4)=(0,zβ,ω2,0) in the general form of k(x) , the constant ω≥0 is chosen to minimise the AS in (8) among all the STBs of this form on the covariate region L , and the corresponding critical constant λNEW is chosen to satisfy (4). In the expression (9), ω represents the weight assigned to xT(XTX)−1x . If ω=0 , the resulting STB has constant width. The new form kNEW(x) is more general than kOM(x) .

Note that, the bands NEW, OMH, UV, and SP are constructed in this article to satisfy (4) exactly, while the OM band is conservative as already pointed out (with the probability on the left-hand side of (4) exceeding γ ).

Exact one-sided STBs for polynomial regression

In this section, we consider the one-sided STBs for polynomial regression, focusing on the multiple-use statistical calibration, even though the multiple-use prediction is still applicable. Assume that y and the only covariate x are related by a polynomial regression model of order p−1 ( ≥1 ): (14) y=α0+α1x+⋯+αp−1xp−1+ϵ=xTα+ϵ, where x=(1,x,…,xp−1)T , and α and ϵ are the same as defined in (2).

With the given data Ω={(x0i,y0i),i=1,…,n} , it is required to construct an infinite sequence of confidence sets C(yx) for the unknown x -values corresponding to an infinite sequence of observed future yx -values to satisfy the key property for multiple-use calibration (15) PΩ{lim infN→∞1N∑i=1NI{xi∈C(yxi)}≥β}≥γ, where 0<β,γ<1 are pre-specified constants, IA denotes the indicator function of the set A and hence 1/N∑i=1NI{xi∈C(yxi)} is the proportion of the N confidence sets that contain the true xi -values.

Assume it is known that all the unknown x -values corresponding to the future y -values are in a given interval [a,b] , and let [L(x;Ω),U(x;Ω)] be a (β,γ) -STB satisfying (16) PΩ{Pyx{L(x;Ω)≤yx≤U(x;Ω)|Ω}≥βforallx∈[a,b]}=γ. Then for each future yx , the confidence set for the corresponding x , given that x∈[a,b] , is defined as (17) C(yx)={x∈[a,b]:L(x;Ω)≤yx≤U(x;Ω)}. It is shown in Appendix B of Scheffé 13 that these confidence sets C(yx) satisfy the property in (15).

The confidence level in (16) for an upper one-sided STB, (−∞,U(x;Ω)] , can be written as (18) PΩ{xTα+zβσ≤xTα^+k(x)σ^for allx∈[a,b]}=PZ,u{maxx∈[a,b]xTZ+zβ−(c1+λc2)uuxT(XTX)−1x+c4/c3≤λc3}=PZ,u{Qpoly≤λc3} where k(x) is defined in (6), Z and u are defined in (10), and Qpoly=maxx∈[a,b]K(x)withK(x)=xTZ+zβ−(c1+λc2)uuxT(XTX)−1x+c4/c3. Simulation studies to evaluate performance of STBs

This section assesses the performance of the various STBs in Table 1 for multiple regression, simple linear regression and polynomial regression under the AS criterion across various scenarios.

Measuring performance of STBs

The main goal of the simulation study is to identify the optimal form of STBs under the AS criterion. To make the evaluation more concrete and quantifiable, we assess the performance of different STBs by comparing the ratios of average shifts of the STBs in Table 1. Additionally, we consider both exact bands (OMH, UV, SP, and NEW) and a conservative band (OM).

For given bands B and NEW, denote their average shifts by ASB and ASNEW respectively. Given a training dataset Ω , parameters n , β , γ , and a covariate region of interest L for multiple regression or a covariate interval [a,b] for polynomial regression, the ratio (20) rB=ASBASNEW is used for comparing B and NEW. If rB>1 , then ASNEW is smaller than ASB , and so NEW is better than B . In our comparative studies, R=1,000,000 replications are used to compute each critical constant λ . We choose this value of R to ensure accuracy, as the resulting critical values agree to at least two decimal places across different random seeds, which is sufficiently accurate for reliable inference in our view.

Simulation studies for multiple regression

The performance of various forms in Table 1 of STBs is examined by comparing the AS-values, including the comparison among the exact bands (OMH, UV, SP and NEW), as well as the comparison between the conservative band OM and the exact band OMH. The coverage probabilities of the NEW band are provided to further illustrate that the NEW band is exact and satisfies the equality in (4).

Design of simulation study

λ and the AS in (8) of a STB in Table 1 depend on the confidence level γ , proportion β , sample size n , number of covariates p , covariate region L and design matrix X . Based on this, the infant dataset in Table 1.1 of Liu 27 is used to represent the design matrix X in this simulation study. This dataset has 17 observations, with the response variable ( y ) being the systolic blood pressure and the two covariates being the birth weight in oz ( x1 ) and the age in days ( x2 ) of an infant. The fitted linear regression model is given by y^=47.583+0.181x1+5.313x2 , with σ^=1.314 and the sample mean x¯(0)=(x¯1,x¯2)T=(113.706,3.882)T . Diagnostic tests for normality have been performed on the data, confirming that the normality assumption is appropriate for this study.

In total, 5 different STBs are considered under 18 different scenarios. The 5 STBs include one conservative band (OM) and four exact bands (OMH, UV, SP and NEW) as given in Table 1. The 18 scenarios are derived from different combinations of the confidence level γ , proportion β , and covariate region L . The following settings are considered: n=17 ; p=3 ; γ=0.95,0.99 ; β=0.90,0.95,0.99 ; L1={x(0):92≤x1≤136.32,3≤x2≤5} , L2={x(0):92≤x1≤154.82,2≤x2≤5} and L3={x(0):92≤x1≤176.87,2≤x2≤5} , with the corresponding (τ1,τ2) -values for D in (3) of OM band being τ1=0 and τ2=2,3 and 4 .

To use an OM band over a given rectangular region L , D should be chosen to be the smallest ellipsoidal region that contains L . As a result, the OM band is conservative when used as a STB over the rectangular region L . Hence, from Table 2, the values of rOM are larger than those of rOMH as expected, indicating that the values of r=ASOM/ASOMH are larger than 1. However, the conservativeness of OM over OMH is very slight. This is because the difference between the areas of the rectangular region L and its corresponding D is small. When the difference between L and its corresponding D enlarges, the conservativeness of OM over OMH becomes larger, as seen from the example below. According to (13), it may appear that the AS depends primarily on the product λNEW⋅ω , and since λNEW decreases as ω increases, one might be led to believe that the AS would diminish as ω approaches infinity. To investigate this further, the region L1 is considered with γ=0.95 and β=0.90 . The values of λNEW corresponding to ω=0,1,10,100 are 2.18, 1.66, 0.59 and 0.08, respectively, while the corresponding AS values are 2.87, 2.69, 2.75 and 2.97. These results show that the AS is not minimised at a large value of ω .

Simulation studies for polynomial regression

In this section, we compare the four one-sided exact (β,γ) -STBs: NEW, OMH, UV and SP for polynomial regression with p≥3 . The AS of a STB for polynomial regression is as defined in (21).

Design of simulation study

λ and the AS of a STB for polynomial regression depend on γ , β , n , p , [a,b] and the design matrix X . However, the entries of (XTX)−1 are not as straightforward as in the simple linear regression case studied previously. Based on this, we design the simulation study as follows. We set the covariate interval [a,b] to be [0,10] , [0,50] , [−5,5] and [−25,25] . The design matrix X is then determined based on the commonly used equally-spaced (ES) design and D-optimal (DO) design. The ES design for a given interval [a,b] consists of n equidistant points, including the endpoints a and b . The DO design for a polynomial regression of degree p−1 comprises p design points suitably selected from [a,b] . Taking the interval [a,b]=[0,10] as an example, the DO design points are (0,5,10) for quadratic regression, (0,2.764,7.236,10) for cubic regression, and (0,1.727,5,8.274,10) for quartic regression. 29 Changing the interval from [0,10] to [0,50] involves multiplying all the design points by 5 , while a shift from [0,10] to [−5,5] entails subtracting 5 from each design point.

In total, 4 different exact STBs are considered under 192 different scenarios. The 192 scenarios are derived from different combinations of the confidence level γ , proportion β , sample size n , degree p , covariate interval [a,b] and design matrix X . In the numerical comparison, the settings we used are as follows: γ=0.95,0.99 ; β=0.95,0.99 ; n=20,100 ; p=3,4,5 ; [a,b]=[0,10],[0,50],[−5,5],[−25,25] ; X based on ES and DO designs.

Simulation results

Table 6 shows the rB -values for β=0.95 and γ=0.95 only, as similar conclusions are observed for other combinations of β and γ . It is clear from the table that the values of rB=ASB/ASNEW are larger than 1 predominantly, and so the NEW band is favourable in comparison with the other STBs. It is evident that the NEW band outperforms OMH and SP for quartic regression (i.e. p=5 ). The rB -values corresponding to OMH and SP can reach up to 1.163 and 1.100, respectively. Although UV is better than NEW in some cases, the gain of UV over NEW is very marginal. Hence, UV and NEW are both recommended for polynomial regression of order p−1≥2 , while SP is the least favourable.

Examples

Prediction for infant blood pressure

The infant blood pressure dataset presented in Table 1.1 of Liu 27 is used to illustrate the multiple-use prediction by using STBs for multiple regression. The need for multiple-use prediction arises because future infants may have birth weights and ages spanning a range of plausible values, and multiple infants may require monitoring for high blood pressure. Details about the dataset are provided in Section 3.2.

To assess an infant’s health from systolic blood pressure y , given birth weight x1 and age x2 , the upper one-sided STBs for systolic blood pressure are of interest. Assume the covariate region of interest is L={x(0):92≤x1≤149,2≤x2≤5}wherex(0)=(x1,x2)T. To use the OM band, 17 the smallest covariate region D=Dτ2∖Dτ1 that contains L has τ1=0 and τ2=2.796 . For β=0.90 , γ=0.99 , the n=17 given observations, and the given covariate regions L and D , the critical constants are computed to be (ω,λNEW)=(2.298,1.535) , λOMH=1.553 , λUV=4.386 , λSP=3.366 , and λOM=1.575 for the bands NEW, OMH, UV, SP and OM, respectively. The values of rB=ASB/ASNEW are rOMH=1.000 , rUV=1.000 , rSP=1.059 and rOM=1.014 . As already pointed out before, OM is conservative and so rOMH<rOM as expected. For the four exact (β,γ) -STBs OMH, UV, SP and NEW, the SP band is the least favourable under the AS criterion.

Figure 2 illustrates the bands NEW and SP. NEW tends to be lower than SP when x(0) is near the sample mean x¯(0) , and higher than SP when x(0) is far away from x¯(0) . For example, at x(0)=(118,4)T , the corresponding upper tolerance limit for y is 93.924 based on NEW, which is less than the tolerance limit 94.727 based on SP. Hence, NEW is more informative than SP. From the NEW band, one can infer that at least 90% of infants with birth weight of 118 ounces and age of 4 days have systolic blood pressures below 93.924, with 99% confidence. An infant with the same birth weight and age may therefore be regarded as having too high blood pressure if their measurement exceeds 93.924.

Calibration for gestational age

The gestational age dataset of Oman and Wax 30 is used in this section to illustrate the multiple-use calibration using STBs. It is known that the measurements of fetal bone lengths are important for assessing fetal growth and predicting due dates. The biparietal diameter (BPD, y ) is one of the most commonly used fetal bone measurements and can be measured accurately, while it takes a lot of effort to measure the gestational age ( x ) accurately. But the relationship between y and x can be well modelled using linear regression. See Hadlock et al., 31 Selbing and Kjessler, 32 Persson and Weldner 33 and Mul et al. 34 for more details. Lindström et al. 35 mention that BPD has the lowest rate of incorrect values in their research. BPD is also useful in early screening of open spina bifida. 36 Based on the observed data, the fitted regression model is given by the quadratic equation y^=−39.187+5.292x−0.049x2 with n=1114 and σ^=3.611 . The normality of residuals is shown in Figure 2 of Oman and Wax. 30

Since the gestational age of a new observation can occur at any time point, this setting naturally leads to a multiple-use calibration problem. As the y -value increases with the x -value, we consider the lower one-sided (β,γ) -STBs in this application, which allow the construction of upper confidence bounds for gestational age x corresponding to all future observed values yx . For β=0.95 , γ=0.99 , the covariate interval of interest [a,b]=[14,41] , and the observed dataset, the critical constants are computed to be (ω,λNEW)=(2.695,1.027) , λOMH=1.043 , λUV=−0.951 , and λSP=1.930 for the bands NEW, OMH, UV, and SP, respectively. Note that in this example the value c1=zβ/θ for UV is c1=zβn−p2Γ(n−p2)/Γ(n−p+12)=38.767 , which is large and makes λUV to be a negative value. The values of rB=ASB/ASNEW are computed to be rOMH=1.001 , rUV=1.053 , and rSP=1.050 . It is clear from these values that the difference between NEW and OMH is small, and UV and SP are notably wider than NEW and OMH on average.

The bands NEW and SP are plotted in Figure 3. If a future yx=40 , for example, is observed, then the upper confidence bound on x is 19.882 based on NEW and 20.001 based on SP. It shows that the upper confidence bound on x based on NEW is smaller and so more informative than that based on SP. Therefore, based on the upper confidence bound from the NEW method, the true gestational age is at most 19.882 weeks for fetuses with a biparietal diameter of 40. That is, with 99% confidence, at least 95% of fetuses with a biparietal diameter of 40 have a gestational age below 19.882 weeks.

Conclusions

Exact (β,γ) -STBs are a valuable tool for multiple-use statistical prediction and calibration in regression. This article fills the gap in constructing the exact one-sided STBs for multiple regression over any given rectangular covariate region. Further, a new form of STBs, NEW, for both multiple and polynomial regressions is studied. A simulation-based method is proposed to compute the critical constants of the STBs. The method can be as accurate as required if the number of simulation replications is sufficiently large.

From our numerical studies, the NEW band has the best overall performance in comparison with the other STBs considered under the AS criterion. For multiple regression, the NEW band generally outperforms the other STBs. For simple linear regression, NEW can significantly outperform the exact STBs OMH, UV and SP. For polynomial regression of order up to four (polynomial regression of order higher than 4 is rarely used in real applications), NEW still outperforms both OMH and SP, though there is little difference between NEW and UV.

The work in this article can possibly be extended in several directions. First, non-normal error distributions can be considered, in which case STBs can be constructed using approximation or bootstrapping techniques. Second, extension to generalized linear regression, such as logistic regression, is of interest. Third, each STB corresponds to a confidence set for the unknown parameters ( α,σ ), so it is interesting to compare different forms of STBs by using the area/volume of the corresponding confidence set for ( α,σ ), following the idea of Liu and Hayter. 37 Further, extension to multivariate regression involving more than one response variable is of interest. Moreover, the proposed method for constructing one-sided STBs can be extended to two-sided cases. Such an extension can be carried out relatively straightforwardly for polynomial regression, while additional challenges arise for multiple regression. Some of these problems are currently under investigation and will be reported in the future.

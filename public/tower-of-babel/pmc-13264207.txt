Biomaterial-based strategies targeting ferroptosis for alleviating intervertebral disc degeneration: Advances and perspectives
Authors: Yeqin Fu, Ziyan Wang, Wenkai Wang, Yangli Xie, Lin Chen, Lu Tan, Minghan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD), a prevalent degenerative disorder, imposes a substantial and growing health burden in aging populations. Recently, ferroptosis—a regulated cell death driven by lipid peroxidation, iron overload, and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) dysregulation—has been identified as a key pathogenic mechanism of IVDD. Despite these advances, a comprehensive understanding of its regulatory networks remains limited, hindering the development of targeted therapies and leaving clinical management reliant on invasive surgery. To address these challenges, functional biomaterials have now emerged as a promising strategy with combined anti-ferroptotic effects. In this review, we examine the known ferroptotic mechanisms in IVDD and explore emerging functional biomaterials with combined anti-ferroptotic properties, thereby aiming to advance therapeutic strategies for IVDD.The translational potential of this article: Given the critical role of ferroptosis in IVDD pathogenesis, functional biomaterials represent a promising translational strategy by enabling both targeted delivery of anti-ferroptotic therapeutics and biomaterial-intrinsic protection against ferroptotic damage. By systematically summarizing the ferroptosis-targeting mechanisms, and therapeutic advantages of these biomaterials, this review offers theoretical guidance for the development of future, clinically translatable interventions for IVDD.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is a globally prevalent chronic degenerative disease affecting approximately 560 million people worldwide to varying degrees [1]. Low back pain, neurological dysfunction, and radiating pain to the buttocks and legs are considered the primary clinical manifestations of IVDD, with approximately 80% of the global population experiencing significant distress from these symptoms at some point in their lives [2]. Consequently, IVDD imposes a substantial burden on public health and socioeconomic systems worldwide [3].

The intervertebral disc (IVD) is an avascular organ primarily composed of the central nucleus pulposus (NP), the peripheral annulus fibrosus (AF), and the superior and inferior cartilaginous endplates (CEP). The IVD plays a crucial role in spinal mobility and stability. However, the degeneration of these tissues accelerates IVDD, with ferroptosis being a significant pathogenic mechanism in this process [4]. Ferroptosis is a regulated cell death characterised by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation (LPO) of cellular membrane [5]. Given the pivotal role of ferroptosis in the pathogenesis of IVDD, it is imperative to understand the occurrence and consequences of ferroptosis across distinct disc cell types, as each contributes uniquely to the degenerative process. Nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) are among the earliest IVD cell populations to undergo ferroptosis. As the NP constitutes the region with the poorest blood supply within the disc, NPCs are highly dependent on the oxidative equilibrium of the microenvironment. This renders NPCs more susceptible to the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby inducing ferroptosis [6]. NPCs are the major cells synthesising the NP matrix (comprising substantial water content, collagen, and proteoglycans) [7,8]. As these cells undergo ferroptosis, not only are the structure and function of the IVD impaired, but this also triggers degeneration in annulus fibrosus cells (AFCs) and chondrocytes [9]. Ferroptosis of CEP cells disrupts the structural and functional integrity of the endplate, leading to impaired nutrient supply to the IVD and consequently accelerating IVDD [6]. In contrast to NPCs and CEP cells, ferroptosis in AFCs tends to emerge at later stages of IVDD, where it predominantly manifests as structural disruption rather than metabolic dysregulation [10]. Beyond cell-specific manifestations, increasing evidence suggests that ferroptosis in IVDD is closely associated with subcellular organelle dysfunction, particularly in mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Mitochondrial abnormalities, including reduced volume, increased membrane density, loss of cristae, and rupture of the outer membrane, are recognised hallmarks of ferroptosis and may further aggravate iron-dependent oxidative stress (OS) and LPO [[11], [12], [13], [14], [15]]. In addition, mitochondrial stress-related pathways, such as ATF family signalling, may influence ferroptotic susceptibility in IVDD [16,17]. The ER also contributes to ferroptosis regulation by controlling phospholipid synthesis, selenoprotein production, intracellular calcium homeostasis, and redox balance [[18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. Moreover, ferroptosis resistance is closely related to the mevalonate pathway, coenzyme Q (CoQ) biosynthesis, and ER–mitochondria crosstalk [[24], [25], [26], [27]]. However, many of these organelle-centred regulatory mechanisms remain insufficiently defined in IVDD.

As ferroptosis has emerged as a key driver of IVDD, targeting this cell death represents a necessary and mechanism-based therapeutic strategy [28]. Existing clinical interventions, including surgical procedures and pharmacological treatments, primarily offer symptomatic relief but fail to target the ferroptosis precisely [29]. These limitations highlight the urgent need for therapeutic strategies capable of precisely modulating ferroptosis to fundamentally attenuate IVDD. Although ferroptosis has emerged as a key therapeutic target in IVDD, its effective modulation is constrained by the unique IVD microenvironment. Consequently, biomaterial-based delivery systems have attracted increasing interest as a feasible strategy to achieve effective anti-ferroptotic therapy [30]. Biomaterials for IVD repair initially focused on collagen- and hyaluronic acid (HA)–based scaffolds to provide basic biocompatibility and structural support [31]. With the emergence of nanotechnology, electrospun nanofibrous scaffolds were introduced to mimic the lamellar architecture of the AF and to deliver bioactive factors that promote cell adhesion, proliferation, and matrix synthesis [32]. Subsequent advances in biomimetic hydrogels shifted attention toward restoring NP hydration and viscoelastic properties, enabling partial reconstruction of disc-specific biomechanics [33]. As the understanding of IVDD pathogenesis has evolved from a purely structural disorder to one driven by microenvironmental dysregulation, biomaterial design has increasingly shifted toward regulating OS, iron homeostasis, and RCD such as ferroptosis [[34], [35], [36]]. Current studies on biomaterial-based anti-ferroptotic therapies for IVDD have mainly focused on nanoparticles and hydrogels. Therefore, this review summarises these two biomaterial classes and their roles in modulating ferroptosis during the degeneration process: Dynamic hydrogel and nanoparticles. Biomaterials are particularly suitable for targeting ferroptosis in IVDD because they can enhance the stability, bioavailability, and local retention of anti-ferroptotic agents, while enabling spatially confined and controlled release in response to pathological microenvironmental conditions [37], such as OS and iron dysregulation [38]. In addition, biomaterial-based systems can protect labile therapeutic cargos (e.g., small molecules, peptides, and nucleic acids) from degradation and facilitate penetration across the avascular IVD environment, thereby minimising systemic exposure and off-target effects. Nanoparticles offer advantages including efficient drug loading, intracellular and potentially organelle-level targeting, and stimulus-responsive release, allowing precise modulation of ferroptosis-related pathways within IVDD [39], although their long-term biosafety in the avascular disc microenvironment remains to be fully evaluated [40,41]. In contrast, hydrogels provide superior biocompatibility and mechanical compatibility with IVD tissue [42,43], enabling sustained local delivery while mimicking the hydrated and viscoelastic properties of the native NP [44]. Their injectability, self-healing capacity, and ability to buffer mechanical stress further support their application in the load-bearing disc environment [44,45]. However, the complex and multifactorial pathological microenvironment of IVDD, particularly ferroptosis driven by LPO and iron dysregulation, is difficult to address using a single drug or material system alone [10]. Consequently, emerging strategies are increasingly shifting toward nanoparticle–hydrogel composite delivery platforms, which integrate the precise bioactivity and microenvironmental modulation of nanoparticles (e.g., antioxidant and iron-chelating functions) with the mechanical support, local retention, and stimulus-responsive release properties of hydrogels. Such composite systems enable multiscale and coordinated regulation of ferroptosis while simultaneously supporting long-term tissue repair, representing a promising direction for next-generation biomaterial-based therapies for IVDD [38]. Collectively, these biomaterial-based strategies offer feasible and promising approaches for ferroptosis-targeted therapy in IVDD.

This review summarises ferroptosis-related mechanisms in IVDD and recent advances in anti-ferroptotic biomaterials, aiming to guide the development of clinically translatable therapies for IVDD.

This review is based on a structured literature search conducted in PubMed and Web of Science databases. Relevant studies published within the past 20 years were retrieved using combinations of the following keywords: “ferroptosis”, “intervertebral disc degeneration”, and “biomaterials”. Studies focusing on ferroptosis-related mechanisms and biomaterial-based interventions in IVDD were included, while unrelated studies or those lacking mechanistic relevance were excluded. Additional relevant references were identified through manual screening of cited literature.

Molecular mechanisms of ferroptosis in IVDD

Ferroptosis is a regulated, iron-dependent form of cell death driven by LPO and is mechanistically distinct from apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy [46]. Increasing evidence implicates ferroptosis in the progression of IVDD, as reflected by reduced viability of NPCs, AFCs, and CEP, together with accelerated extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation [10]. Although ferroptosis has been extensively investigated in oncology, where organelle-associated regulatory pathways are relatively well characterised, its mechanistic landscape in IVDD remains incomplete. Current IVDD-related studies predominantly focus on iron overload, GPX4 dysregulation, and excessive LPO accumulation. In contrast, several validated ferroptosis-regulatory pathways identified in other disease contexts—such as the FSP1–CoQ10 axis and ACSL4-mediated lipid remodelling—have not yet been systematically explored in IVD. Iron homeostasis dysregulation-induced ferroptosis in IVDD

Iron is indispensable for fundamental cellular processes owing to its redox flexibility between divalent iron (Fe2+) and trivalent iron (Fe3+) states. However, excessive intracellular iron catalyses ROS generation via the Fenton reaction, thereby driving LPO and ferroptosis [35,47]. Consequently, the maintenance of iron homeostasis is essential for preventing ferroptosis. In IVDD, accumulating evidence indicates that ferroptosis arises from stepwise dysregulation of iron uptake, storage, and efflux pathways (Fig. 2). Cellular iron uptake is primarily mediated by divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) and transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) [48]. DMT1 facilitates Fe2+ transport across membranes, whereas TfR1 mediates transferrin (Tf)-bound iron endocytosis. Tf-bound Fe3+ enters cells via TfR1-mediated endocytosis, where it is reduced to Fe2+ by six transmembrane epithelial antigens of the prostate 3 (STEAP3) in acidified endosomes and subsequently released into the cytosol through DMT1 [49,50]. In inflammatory and oxidative microenvironments characteristic of IVDD, pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α upregulate DMT1 while suppressing ferroportin (FPN), leading to intracellular Fe2+ accumulation, particularly in CEP cells. In parallel, TfR1 expression is enhanced in an HIF-2α–dependent manner, further exacerbating iron influx and promoting CEP ferroptosis. It has been reported that, excessive iron uptake through TfR1 has also been linked to mitochondrial damage and cGAS/STING-mediated innate immune activation, thereby coupling iron overload to inflammation-driven IVDD progression [51]. Beyond canonical iron transporters, Piezo1 has emerged as a mechanosensitive regulator of iron influx independent of TfR [52]. Given the load-bearing nature of the IVD, Piezo1-mediated iron entry provides a mechanotransduction–ferroptosis axis that may be particularly relevant to mechanically induced IVDD [53].

Cellular iron homeostasis is tightly regulated by ferritin, which limits ferroptosis by sequestering redox-active iron and thereby restricting the intracellular labile iron pool and LPO [[54], [55], [56]]. In addition to intracellular iron storage, Prominin-2 (PROM2) confers ferroptosis resistance by promoting ferritin-dependent iron export through multivesicular bodies and exosomes, thereby reducing intracellular iron burden [57]. Disruption of these iron-buffering mechanisms has been implicated in IVDD-associated ferroptosis. In CEP cells, Ma et al. demonstrated that nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) silencing markedly enhances nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4)-mediated ferritinophagy, leading to excessive ferritin degradation, intracellular iron accumulation, ferroptotic cell death, and subsequent ECM degeneration [58]. This work establishes the Nrf2/NCOA4/ferritinophagy axis as a critical regulatory pathway governing iron-dependent ferroptosis in IVDD. Beyond antioxidant signalling, emerging evidence from non-IVD disease models suggests that iron metabolism may also be regulated by mechanosensitive transcriptional programmes. The Hippo–YAP pathway, a central regulator of cellular responses to mechanical cues, has been shown in cancer settings to modulate ferroptosis-related processes, including transferrin receptor expression and ferritin turnover. Available evidence suggests that, recent studies in breast cancer have demonstrated that the YAP/TEAD complex can transcriptionally regulate NCOA4, thereby promoting ferritinophagy and increasing cellular susceptibility to ferroptosis [59]. Although direct evidence for YAP/TEAD–NCOA4–mediated ferritinophagy in IVDD is currently lacking, the pronounced mechanical loading environment of the IVD and the established role of YAP as a mechanotransducer suggest that this pathway may represent a plausible regulatory axis linking biomechanical stress to iron-dependent ferroptosis in disc cells. Elucidating whether such mechanotranscriptional control of ferritinophagy operates in IVDD warrants further investigation.

Ferroportin (FPN) is one of only two known transmembrane iron exporters and plays a critical role in maintaining intracellular iron homeostasis [60]. In the context of IVDD, Cao et al. demonstrated that OS–induced FPN dysfunction leads to intracellular iron accumulation in NPCs, thereby promoting ferroptosis [61]. Importantly, restoration of FPN activity through enhanced nuclear translocation of metal-regulated transcription factor 1 (MTF1) effectively alleviates iron overload and confers ferroptosis resistance in NPCs, highlighting the MTF1–FPN axis as a disc-relevant protective mechanism [61]. Beyond membrane-level iron efflux, vesicle-mediated iron export has emerged as an additional layer of ferroptosis regulation [62]. PROM2 facilitates the formation of ferritin-containing multivesicular bodies and exosomes, thereby promoting iron extrusion and limiting intracellular iron availability [57,63]. Although PROM2-mediated ferroptosis resistance has been primarily characterised in non-disc cell types, including bone mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), its ability to regulate iron efflux and lipid metabolism suggests a plausible yet underexplored role in IVDD-associated ferroptosis. Elucidating PROM2-dependent pathways in disc cells may therefore uncover complementary iron-export mechanisms and expand therapeutic targets for ferroptosis-driven IVDD.

In summary, current research on iron metabolism-mediated ferroptosis mechanisms remains insufficiently systematic, with a heavy focus on NPCs while AFCs and CEP are severely understudied. Moving forward, these two key cell types should be prioritized as research targets to comprehensively elucidate the mechanisms of iron metabolism-mediated ferroptosis in IVD cells. Nevertheless, as a critical nutrient exchange interface, the regulatory mechanisms of iron transporters in CEP under hypoxic/acidic microenvironments remain unelucidated. Regarding molecular mechanisms, the regulation of the hepcidin-FPN axis in IVD remains incompletely defined [64]. Only miR-10a-5p has been demonstrated to suppress IL-6 expression, thereby reducing hepcidin levels, decreasing chondrocyte ferroptosis, and delaying the degenerative process [65].

GPX4-centred ferroptosis regulation in IVDD

In 2012, Dixon et al. first identified the System Xc−/GSH/GPX4 axis as the canonical regulatory pathway of ferroptosis [64], which has since been increasingly implicated in the pathogenesis of IVDD [35]. Central to this mechanism is the cystine/glutamate antiporter System Xc−, composed of solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) and solute carrier family 3 member 2 (SLC3A2) subunits, which imports extracellular cystine for intracellular GSH synthesis. Importantly, GSH functions as both a substrate and an essential cofactor for GPX4 and safeguards cells against OS-induced apoptosis [66]. GPX4 reduces potentially toxic LPO to less harmful lipoalcohols [67]. Therefore, GPX4 may also be a key enzyme preventing LPO accumulation in IVDD [68,69]. Owing to the avascular nature of the IVD, disc cells reside in a chronically hypoxic microenvironment, in which hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs) play a central regulatory role [70,71]. Recent evidence demonstrates that HIF-1α suppresses ferroptosis in NPCs by enhancing YTHDF1-mediated m6A recognition of SLC7A11 transcripts, thereby promoting SLC7A11 translation and sustaining GPX4 activity. This HIF-1α/YTHDF1/SLC7A11 axis represents the first hypoxia-adaptive mechanism linking GPX4 regulation to ferroptosis in IVDD. Nevertheless, whether additional HIF-dependent pathways modulate GPX4 activity under hypoxic disc conditions remains largely unexplored [72]. Besides, YTHDF1 has also been reported to suppress ferroptosis via ferritin regulation in tumour models [73], suggesting potential but unverified mechanistic parallels in IVDD.

Glutamate-cysteine ligase (GCL) and GSH synthetase (GSS) sequentially catalyse GSH formation, serving as an essential cofactor for GPX4. As a selenoprotein, GPX4 reduces cytotoxic phospholipid hydroperoxides (PL-OOH) to inert alcohols (PL-OH), thereby halting LPO chain reactions in IVDD [64]. Beyond cystine transport, GPX4 activity is further modulated by intracellular redox and metabolic regulators. NRF2 activation directly upregulates SLC7A11 and GPX4 expression, thereby enhancing cellular resistance to ferroptosis in IVD cells [35,74], whereas pharmacological inhibition of System Xc− depletes GSH and accelerates GPX4 loss, triggering ferroptosis [35,74]. In addition, ATP-binding cassette subfamily C member 1 (ABCC1) exports intracellular GSH or metal–GSH complexes, indirectly compromising GPX4 function. However, its role in IVDD-associated ferroptosis has not yet been investigated [75,76].

Mitochondrial regulators also influence GPX4-dependent ferroptosis [77]. Sirtuin 3 (SIRT3) has been shown to attenuate ferroptosis in NPCs by suppressing ACSL4 activity and stabilising OS levels [1,78]. Although studies in cancer and metabolic diseases suggest that SIRT3 may enhance GPX4 activity via SOD2-or GPX4-dependent pathways [79,80],whether similar mechanisms operate in IVDD warrants further investigation.

Accumulating evidence indicates that pharmacological modulation of the Nrf2/GPX4 axis represents a critical mechanism for suppressing ferroptosis in IVDD. Shu et al. demonstrated that activation of Nrf2 enhances GPX4 expression, thereby conferring ferroptosis resistance in NPCs and alleviating IVDD [81]. Similarly, nordihydroguaiaretic acid (NDGA), a natural antioxidant, has been shown to attenuate ferroptosis in NPCs through regulation of the Nrf2/GPX4 pathway, further supporting the central role of GPX4 in maintaining redox homeostasis in degenerated discs [82].

As a selenoprotein, GPX4 critically depends on selenium availability. Selenium supplementation has been reported to alleviate ER stress, reduce intracellular calcium overload, and enhance GPX4 synthesis, thereby suppressing ferroptosis via the Se/GPX4 and Se/Selk axes [18,[83], [84], [85], [86]]. However, current evidence in IVDD remains largely mechanistic, and effective strategies for selenium delivery to the avascular disc microenvironment are still lacking.

Finally, GPX4-mediated ferroptosis is tightly coupled to lipid metabolic reprogramming. Enzymes such as ACSL4 and LPCAT3 promote polyunsaturated fatty acid peroxidation, whereas ACSL3 exerts protective effects against ferroptosis [87]. Despite their established roles in other disease contexts, the contribution of lipid metabolic enzymes to GPX4 dysfunction and ferroptosis in IVDD remains insufficiently characterised.

From a mechanistic perspective, GPX4 emerges as a key convergence point of several anti-ferroptotic pathways in IVDD, integrating redox control, GSH metabolism, and selenium-dependent antioxidant defence. Loss of GPX4 activity directly compromises the capacity of IVD cells to detoxify LPO, thereby sensitising them to ferroptosis under diverse metabolic, inflammatory, and OS conditions. Nevertheless, mechanistic insights to date are predominantly derived from NPCs, and remain fragmentary with respect to upstream regulatory hierarchies, cell-type specificity, and stress-dependent modulation. Whether GPX4 is regulated by similar networks in AFCs and CEP cells, particularly under mechanical loading, hypoxia, and metabolic constraint, has not yet been fully elucidated. Future studies integrating cell-specific, organelle-level, and biomechanical perspectives will be essential to delineate the full regulatory landscape of the GPX4 axis in IVDD.

ROS-mediated indirect regulation of ferroptosis in IVDD

ROS play a dual role in IVD physiology, acting as essential signalling mediators at basal levels while exerting cytotoxic effects when excessively accumulated. Sustained ROS overload induces OS, LPO, and ultimately ferroptosis, thereby accelerating IVDD. Excessive ROS-driven ferroptosis has been implicated in CEP degeneration, annular rupture, and amplification of local inflammatory responses, underscoring the central role of redox imbalance in IVDD progression [87]. At the core of cellular antioxidant defence, the Nrf2/HO-1 axis constitutes a principal regulatory system that counteracts ROS accumulation [88]. Notably, Nrf2/HO-1 pathway also plays an indirect role in anti-ferroptosis. Activation of Nrf2 promotes the transcription of antioxidant and redox-related genes, including GPX4, thereby enhancing cellular resistance to OS-induced ferroptosis in NPCs [89]. Various natural and synthetic drugs can inhibit ferroptosis in NPCs by decreasing intracellular OS through upregulation of the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4 pathway [82,[90], [91], [92]]. Upstream, the PI3K/AKT pathway facilitates Nrf2 activation and nuclear translocation, whereas phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) negatively regulates this signalling pathway. In CEP cells, inhibition of PTEN has been reported to enhance Nrf2 activity and increase resistance to ferroptosis, suggesting that antioxidant regulatory strategies may be differentially organised across distinct disc cell populations [93,94].

Beyond canonical antioxidant pathways, transcriptional and metabolic regulators amplify ROS-driven ferroptosis by disrupting GSH homeostasis. BACH1 functions as a key upstream repressor of antioxidant responses by competitively inhibiting Nrf2 binding to antioxidant response elements and suppressing mitochondrial glutaminase (GLS), a rate-limiting enzyme in GSH synthesis [95,96]. In BMSCs, the PROM2/FBXO22/BACH1 axis alleviates ferroptosis by promoting BACH1 ubiquitination and restoring GSH-dependent redox balance [63,97]. Although this regulatory network has been implicated in the context of IVDD-related stem cell therapy, its direct contribution to redox regulation and ferroptosis in resident disc cells remains to be fully established. In parallel, lipocalin-2 (LCN2), an immune-associated iron-binding protein, links ROS accumulation with iron dysregulation and ferroptosis in inflammatory conditions [[98], [99], [100]]. In NPCs, FBXO2-mediated ubiquitination of LCN2 preserves mitochondrial homeostasis by restoring PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy, thereby attenuating OS and ferroptosis. These findings position ubiquitin-dependent regulation as a critical modulator of ROS–ferroptosis coupling in IVDD [101].

Beyond transcriptional and metabolic regulation, ROS-driven ferroptosis is further amplified at the levels of innate immune signalling and organelle quality control [102]. NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) promotes ROS generation by interacting with LRRC8A, leading to activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and ferroptosis in NPCs [103]. Similarly, activation of the cGAS/STING pathway exacerbates OS and NLRP3 signalling, reinforcing a feed-forward loop between inflammation and ferroptosis [104]. Beyond mitochondria, peroxisome-mediated autophagy has also been implicated in ferroptosis regulation by controlling lipid oxidation and ROS clearance [105]. Emerging evidence suggests that the PPARγ/Axl axis suppresses ferroptosis by enhancing autophagic flux and modulating lipid metabolism, potentially intersecting with Nrf2-dependent antioxidant pathways [106]. However, organelle-specific regulatory mechanisms remain largely unexplored in IVDD.

Biomaterial-based targeting of ferroptosis for alleviating IVDD

Ferroptosis in IVDD represents a chronic and self-amplifying pathological process driven by persistent disruption of the disc microenvironment, including OS, iron dysregulation, inflammatory signalling, and ECM breakdown [107]. However, effective pharmacological modulation of ferroptosis within the IVD remains challenging due to its avascular nature, confined anatomical space, and continuous mechanical loading, which collectively limit drug penetration, retention, and durability [108].

These intrinsic constraints have catalysed increasing interest in biomaterial-based therapeutic strategies that enable localised retention, sustained and stimulus-responsive release, and microenvironment-adaptive regulation of ferroptosis-related pathways within IVDD [6]. Rather than introducing entirely novel anti-ferroptotic mechanisms, biomaterial platforms predominantly function to stabilise, spatially confine, and amplify established ferroptosis-regulatory pathways, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy in the uniquely constrained disc microenvironment.

To date, biomaterial-based anti-ferroptotic strategies for IVDD have predominantly centred on hydrogels and nanoparticles, reflecting their favourable injectability, tunable physicochemical properties, and adaptability to mechanical and metabolic stress. Importantly, these two platforms play complementary yet distinct functional roles: hydrogels primarily act at the tissue and microenvironmental level by providing mechanical support and sustained local modulation, whereas nanoparticles enable cellular and subcellular targeting of ferroptosis-associated signalling pathways [109]. Ferroptosis-inhibiting hydrogels for alleviating IVDD

Hydrogel-based strategies represent the most extensively investigated biomaterial approach for ferroptosis modulation in IVDD, owing to their intrinsic biocompatibility, high water content, and mechanical resemblance to native NP tissue. In the context of ferroptosis, hydrogels primarily function by reshaping the ferroptosis-prone microenvironment, including regulation of oxidative stress, iron availability, inflammatory signalling, and ECM homeostasis under mechanical loading. However, current preclinical studies support the short-term biocompatibility and therapeutic feasibility of anti-ferroptotic biomaterials, their translational safety evaluation remains incomplete. Most available evidence is derived from small-animal IVDD models and mainly addresses cytocompatibility, histological repair, and short-term local tolerance. However, long-term degradation behaviour, off-target toxicity, inflammatory responses, and mechanical durability under sustained spinal loading remain insufficiently investigated. Current ferroptosis-inhibiting hydrogels can be broadly categorised into three functional classes: ROS-modulating hydrogels, iron homeostasis–regulating hydrogels, and gene- or intercellular signalling–oriented hydrogels. This classification reflects the multi-layered regulation of ferroptosis in IVDD, rather than discrete or isolated pathways.

To meet the dynamic mechanical and biochemical demands of the degenerated disc, increasing attention has shifted from conventional permanently crosslinked hydrogels toward dynamic hydrogels formed through reversible covalent bonds or supramolecular interactions [[110], [111], [112]]. These systems exhibit injectability, self-healing capacity, and stimulus responsiveness, enabling adaptive responses to excessive ROS, iron overload, or inflammatory cues [113,114]. Nevertheless, their application in IVDD requires stringent control over degradation kinetics, mechanical stability, and long-term biocompatibility under continuous axial loading.

ROS-modulating hydrogels for ferroptosis suppression

Oxidative stress and excessive ROS accumulation primarily act as amplifying and permissive factors for ferroptosis, rather than its sole execution mechanism [115]. Excessive ROS is a central driver of ferroptosis susceptibility in disc cells, particularly in NPCs. Several hydrogel systems have therefore been designed to attenuate OS, thereby indirectly suppressing ferroptosis. Osteopontin (OPN) has been reported to promote ferroptosis by enhancing OS, inflammatory responses, and iron dysregulation [116,117]. Quercetin, a natural flavonoid, is proposed to bind OPN and attenuate OPN-associated ferroptosis. However, its poor solubility and short retention time limit therapeutic efficacy. To address these limitations, Li et al. encapsulated quercetin in G3-PBA (G3-Quercetin) and further loaded it into a ROS-degradable injectable hydrogel. Triggered by elevated ROS levels in the IVDD microenvironment, this system enables controlled release of quercetin, thereby alleviating OPN-induced ferroptosis and disc degeneration (Fig. 5A) [118]. Beyond small-molecule antioxidants, hydrogels have also been engineered to combine redox regulation with mechanical adaptability. Kartogenin, initially identified as a chondrogenic inducer [119],has been increasingly applied in IVDD therapy owing to similarities between the ECM composition of cartilage and NP. Given the susceptibility of conventional hydrogels to mechanical fragmentation under in vivo loading [120], Xin et al. developed a KGN-loaded self-healing hydrogel inspired by the native NP microenvironment. This system delivers local redox regulation while mitigating inflammation, thereby improving the ferroptosis-prone microenvironment within the disc [122]. However, the absence of detailed mechanical evaluation and validation in human NPCs represents a notable limitation.

HA, a native ECM component of NP tissue, exhibits intrinsic ROS-scavenging properties and contributes to axial load buffering [123]. Bu et al. enhanced the antioxidant capacity of HA by thiourea modification and constructed a dynamic HA-NCSN/Cu2+ hydrogel with excellent injectability, self-healing behaviour, and potent ROS-scavenging ability. This hydrogel alleviates the inflammatory microenvironment and modulates the GPX4 pathway in NPCs, thereby suppressing ferroptosis. In addition, its photothermal performance enables mild photothermal therapy, which further promotes ECM remodelling via activation of the TGF-β/Smad pathway (Fig. 5B) [124]. Collectively, this multifunctional hydrogel highlights the potential of integrating redox regulation, biomechanical support, and microenvironmental normalisation, thereby reducing cellular susceptibility to ferroptosis rather than solely blocking its execution.

Oridonin (Ori), a bioactive diterpenoid, exerts anti-inflammatory effects by activating Nrf2 signalling and inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome formation [125]. Growth differentiation factor 5 regulates ECM synthesis and IVD cell proliferation [126]. Liu et al. developed a dynamic hydrogel (HPGO) that integrates Ori-loaded NPC membrane-derived nanovesicles with GDF5-functionalised oxidized HA. In this system, Schiff base bonds not only scavenge excess Fe2+ ions but also provide dynamic mechanical reinforcement. Simultaneously, activation of Nrf2 signalling and BMP pathways contributes to suppression of ferroptosis and restoration of ECM homeostasis (Fig. 5C) [125].

Iron homeostasis–regulating hydrogels for ferroptosis inhibition

Iron dysregulation represents a core upstream driver of ferroptosis [127]. Accordingly, several hydrogel systems have been developed to directly modulate iron availability within degenerated discs. A magnetic-responsive hydrogel composed of polyether F127 diacrylate and tannin (FDA–TA) was designed to scavenge ROS and sequester excess Fe2+, effectively creating an “artificial iron reservoir” within the disc (Fig. 6A) [128]. Mechanistically, this hydrogel activates the PI3K–AKT pathway and suppresses NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy, thereby reducing iron-dependent ferroptosis. Despite promising efficacy, concerns remain regarding the degradation behaviour of iron-chelating hydrogels under sustained mechanical loading. Uncontrolled degradation may lead to secondary iron release, potentially reinitiating ferroptosis. Genetic and intercellular regulatory hydrogels for ferroptosis control

Emerging strategies extend ferroptosis-targeting hydrogels from small-molecule delivery toward upstream genetic and intercellular regulatory layers. Chemically modified mRNA (modRNA) offers high translational efficiency with reduced immunogenicity [[129], [130], [131], [132]]. Tian et al. encapsulated SLC7A11 modRNA within a PVA-tsPBA hydrogel, achieving efficient local expression of SLC7A11 in NPCs and precise modulation of the SLC7A11/GPX4 axis (Fig. 6B) [133]. While this approach demonstrates substantial therapeutic promise, long-term safety and off-target effects require further evaluation.

Beyond direct genetic modulation within resident disc cells, ferroptosis can also be regulated at the intercellular level through paracrine signalling. Hydrogels have also been utilised as platforms for sustained exosome delivery. Nucleus pulposus progenitor cell -derived exosomes were shown to confer ferroptosis resistance to neighbouring disc cells via miR-221-3p–mediated upregulation of SLC7A11 (Fig. 6C) [[134], [135], [136], [137]]. To enhance delivery efficiency, engineered exosomes were embedded within an injectable HA/oxidized chondroitin sulfate hydrogel, enabling sustained release and continuous ferroptosis suppression in AFCs [138]. Similar strategies employing BMSC-loaded hydrogels further underscore the potential of hydrogel-based platforms to integrate cell-derived therapeutics with ferroptosis regulation [139].

Ferroptosis-suppressing nanoparticles for IVDD intervention

Nanoparticles, defined as solid particles with diameters ranging from 1 to 100 nm, have attracted increasing interest as ferroptosis-modulating agents for IVDD owing to their tunable physicochemical properties and versatile functionalisation potential [140]. In IVDD, nanoparticle-based systems predominantly function at the cellular and organelle levels, enabling precise modulation of ROS production, iron metabolism, mitochondrial dysfunction, and ferroptosis-related signalling pathways [121,140]. Broadly, nanoparticles can be categorised into organic and inorganic systems. Organic nanoparticles, particularly polymer-based platforms, exhibit favourable biocompatibility and biodegradability. In contrast, inorganic nanoparticles offer unique catalytic and magnetic properties but require careful surface functionalisation to mitigate potential toxicity [141]. In contrast to hydrogels, which primarily reshape the disc microenvironment at the tissue level, nanoparticle-based systems enable precise intracellular and subcellular regulation of ferroptosis-associated pathways. For nanoparticle-based systems, in addition to local therapeutic efficacy, translational evaluation should also consider long-term retention, degradation products, potential aggregation, and off-target distribution, particularly because these factors may influence safety in the mechanically constrained and poorly vascularised disc environment.

ROS-scavenging nanoparticles as primary anti-ferroptotic agents

Among nanomaterials explored for IVDD intervention, carbon-based nanoparticles represent one of the most extensively investigated platforms for suppressing OS-driven ferroptosis. Fullerene, a representative carbon nanomaterial, exhibits exceptional free radical–scavenging capacity, largely attributable to its unique cage-like spherical structure composed of a highly conjugated π-electron system. This delocalised electron network enables fullerene to efficiently accept and stabilise unpaired electrons from ROS, thereby functioning as a “radical sponge” [142]. However, its poor aqueous solubility and limited biocompatibility have constrained its translational potential. To overcome these limitations, hydroxylated fullerene derivatives have been developed, which restore antioxidant capacity and suppress ferroptosis in NPCs through activation of the Nrf2 pathway [143]. Despite promising anti-ferroptotic efficacy, unresolved toxicity mechanisms and challenges in scalable production continue to impede clinical translation.

Salicylic acid (SA) is characterised by an ortho-hydroxybenzoic acid motif, in which adjacent phenolic hydroxyl and carboxyl groups form a stable intramolecular hydrogen bond and provide metal-chelating capability. This structural feature underlies its anti-inflammatory activity through modulation of COX enzymes and suppression of NF-κB–mediated inflammatory signalling, making SA a rational functional unit for redox- and inflammation-responsive biomaterial design. Nanoparticles targeting iron dysregulation and mitochondrial ferroptosis

Beyond ROS scavenging, several nanoparticle systems have been designed to modulate iron metabolism and mitochondrial dysfunction, two critical drivers of ferroptosis. Guo et al. engineered gallic acid–copper metal–phenolic networks (PGA–Cu), which exhibited enhanced antioxidant activity through metal–phenolic coordination while simultaneously chelating intracellular iron (Fig. 7B) [145]. Surface conjugation with a mitochondria-targeting octapeptide further enabled precise mitochondrial localisation, thereby strengthening ROS scavenging and iron-chelating effects. Although multifunctional, concerns remain regarding the potential generation of deleterious degradation by-products in vivo (Fig. 7C) [143]. Similarly, DFOM@CeO2 nanoparticles reduced mitochondrial ROS and improved respiratory chain activity, but their inherent insolubility, aggregation tendency, and potential neurotoxicity raise concerns regarding long-term safety [140].

Multifunctional nanoparticles integrating signalling and genetic regulation

Emerging evidence suggests that ferroptosis inhibition in IVDD may require simultaneous modulation of ROS, iron homeostasis, and ferroptosis core regulators. Polydopamine nanoparticles (PDA NPs) exemplify such multifunctional platforms by exerting triple anti-ferroptotic effects: ROS depletion, iron chelation, and inhibition of GPX4 ubiquitination [121]. Beyond their established antioxidant capacity [[146], [147], [148]], PDA NPs directly interact with GPX4 to prevent ubiquitin-mediated degradation, thereby reinforcing ferroptosis resistance.

At the genetic regulatory level, ATF3 has been identified as a transcriptional repressor of SLC7A11, promoting ferroptosis by impairing the GSH-dependent antioxidant system [149,150]. Given the incomplete understanding of ATF family regulation in IVDD [151], nanocarrier-mediated gene modulation has emerged as a promising strategy. Zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8), a metal–organic framework with high porosity and tunable degradation, has been optimised through defect engineering and histidine incorporation to improve biocompatibility and encapsulation efficiency [152]. Composite nanoparticle–hydrogel systems

To further enhance therapeutic outcomes, nanoparticles have been integrated into composite delivery systems. Luo et al. developed a ROS-responsive hydrogel embedding polydopamine nanoparticles and skeletal stem cells, enabling sustained STING pathway inhibition, suppression of ferroptosis, and promotion of SSC differentiation toward NP-like cells (Fig. 8B) [104,154,155]. Beyond ROS scavenging, mitochondria-targeted metal–phenolic nanoparticles (PGA-Cu@SS08) have also been embedded into hydrogel matrices to enhance ferroptosis resistance at the organelle level. Functionalisation with the SS08 octapeptide enables preferential mitochondrial localisation, where the nanoparticles reduce ROS accumulation and preserve mitochondrial integrity. The phenolic hydroxyl groups of PGA-Cu and histidine residues within SS08 confer iron-chelating capacity, thereby mitigating intracellular iron overload. Mechanistically, activation of the NRF2 signalling pathway and inhibition of ferritinophagy collectively contribute to ferroptosis suppression. When incorporated into biodegradable hydrogels, sustained nanoparticle release further enhances mechanical reinforcement, restores disc hydration, and stabilises the degenerative microenvironment [145].

Collectively, current nanoparticle-based strategies for IVDD predominantly target ROS overload and iron dysregulation, with an emerging shift toward multifunctional platforms capable of modulating ferroptosis signalling networks. As ferroptosis mechanisms become increasingly complex, future nanomaterials may need to integrate multi-pathway regulation, stimulus responsiveness, and improved biosafety profiles to achieve durable therapeutic efficacy. Conclusion

Ferroptosis has increasingly been recognised as a central contributor to IVDD, providing a mechanistic framework that links OS, iron dyshomeostasis, metabolic imbalance, and regulated cell death within the uniquely avascular and mechanically loaded disc environment. Accumulating evidence indicates that ferroptosis plays a substantial role in NPCs loss and ECM degradation. Nevertheless, current mechanistic understanding remains fragmented. Most studies are confined to GPX4-dependent pathways in NPCs, whereas ferroptosis regulation in AFCs and CEP cells, as well as GPX4-independent antioxidant systems, remains poorly defined. Furthermore, how ferroptosis integrates with biomechanical loading, hypoxia, and inflammatory signalling in the progression of IVDD requires more systematic investigation.

Biomaterial-based strategies have demonstrated considerable potential as enabling platforms for ferroptosis modulation, primarily through combined regulation of OS, and ferroptosis-associated microenvironment. Importantly, despite promising preclinical outcomes, significant translational barriers persist. Existing animal models fail to adequately recapitulate the long-term mechanical loading conditions of the human spine, which play a fundamental role in IVDD progression and biomaterial performance. In addition, critical challenges remain regarding long-term biosafety, degradation behaviour under sustained physiological stress, mechanical durability in load-bearing environments, and stable integration with native disc tissue.

To advance this field, it is imperative to establish a coherent conceptual framework that systematically integrates the distinctive IVD microenvironment, ferroptosis regulatory axes, and biomaterial design principles. The avascular, hypoxic, and mechanically loaded nature of the IVD creates a highly specialised microenvironment characterised by oxidative stress, iron dyshomeostasis, and limited nutrient exchange, which collectively sensitise disc cells to ferroptosis. Within this context, the core ferroptosis-regulatory axis—centred on LPO, iron metabolism, and GPX4-dependent antioxidant defence—provides a mechanistic foundation for therapeutic intervention. Accordingly, rational biomaterial design should move beyond single-function modulation toward integrated strategies that simultaneously regulate redox balance and ferroptosis-associated signalling pathways, while also accommodating the biomechanical and metabolic constraints of the disc niche.

From a translational perspective, future research should prioritise the development of biomaterial systems that not only modulate ferroptosis-related pathways but also adapt to the dynamic biomechanical and metabolic microenvironment of the IVD. This includes the integration of mechano-responsive design, long-term functional stability, cell-type-specific targeting strategies, and unified evaluation frameworks that bridge mechanistic insights with clinically relevant performance.

Collectively, bridging the gap between mechanistic understanding and biomaterial design, while addressing the unique biomechanical constraints of the IVD, will be critical for translating ferroptosis-targeted strategies into effective and durable therapies for IVDD.

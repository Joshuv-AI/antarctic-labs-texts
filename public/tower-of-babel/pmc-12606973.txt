Identification and characterization of HVA22 genes in pineapple (Ananas comosus L.) revealed their potential roles in development regulation and cold stress response
Authors: Zhimin Hou, Xinkai Cai, Jiahao Wu, Lin Lu, Chaojia Liu, Yangmei Zhang, Shengzhen Chen, Qinglong Yao, Jing Wang, Lulu Wang, Yan Cheng, Ruoyu Liu, Xiaomei Wang, Yuan Qin, Ping Zheng

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPineapple (Ananas comosus L.), an economically significant tropical fruit crop, is highly susceptible to low-temperature during cultivation. The abscisic acid (ABA) and stress-inducible HVA22 gene family is known to play important roles in growth regulation and abiotic stress response, but its functions in pineapple remain unclear.ResultsWe identified 11 HVA22 genes (AcHVA22A - AcHVA22K) in the pineapple genome and grouped them into four phylogenetic clades. Expression analysis showed that most AcHVA22 genes displayed tissue- or developmental stage-specific expression patterns, suggesting their diverse functions in pineapple growth and development. Subcellular localization analyses revealed diverse localizations of AcHVA22 proteins, including plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. And regulatory predictions indicated control by multiple cis-elements, transcription factors, and miRNAs, which may contribute to their functional diversification. Most AcHVA22 genes responded consistently to ABA, GA, and drought treatments, but their responses to high and low-temperatures varied. Notably, AcHVA22C/D/E/G/I/K were up-regulated under both short- and long-term cold treatments in two different pineapple varieties, highlighting their potential key roles in cold stress tolerance.ConclusionsThis study provided the first genome-wide characterization of the HVA22 gene family in pineapple. Background

Pineapple (Ananas comosus L.), belonging to the Bromeliaceae family, is a significant tropical and subtropical fruit crop with considerable economic and research value [1]. However, its cultivation is frequently hindered by diverse biotic and abiotic stresses, including drought, cold, and diseases, which severely impact yield and fruit quality [2–6]. Additionally, as a self-incompatible species, pineapple is predominantly propagated asexually, leading to reduced genetic diversity, which in turn compromises fruit quality and increases vulnerability to environmental stresses [7]. Therefore, improving stress tolerance and understanding the reproductive biology of pineapple is crucial for enhancing its cultivation and breeding practices.

Abscisic acid (ABA) is a critical plant hormone that is well-known for its function in mediating responses to environmental stresses, especially by regulating stress-responsive genes. Among these, the HVA22 gene family is a distinctive group of ABA-induced genes that was initially identified in barley (Hordeum vulgare) [8]. HVA22 proteins contain a highly conserved TB2/DP1/HVA22 domain, which is ubiquitously present across a wide range of eukaryotic organisms but absent in prokaryotes, suggesting a specialized function in eukaryotic cellular processes [9–12].

The function of HVA22 in plant stress responses has been well documented. In barley, HVA22 contributes to stress tolerance by regulating vesicular transport and minimizing the unnecessary secretion of stressed cells [12]. Homologs of HVA22 in different plant species, including Arabidopsis (Arabidopsis thaliana), barley, rice (Oryza sativa), smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora), passion fruit (Passiflora edulis), tomato (Solanum lycopersicum), and cotton, show differential expression patterns in response to phytohormones and abiotic stresses such as (ABA, cold, salt, and drought treatments [11, 13–19]. Notably, these proteins are predominantly localized to the endoplasmic reticulum, suggesting a function in maintaining cellular homeostasis during stress [12, 15, 20, 21]. For instance, the HVA22 gene in rice, OsHLP1, has been reported to promote disease resistance by maintaining homeostasis of the endoplasmic reticulum [21, 22]. Furthermore, HVA22 genes are also implicated in reproductive development. For instance, the deletion of AtHVA22d in Arabidopsis enhances autophagy, leading to impaired flower development [23]. Similarly, the HVA22 homolog TRINITY_DN68950_c5_g2 in Schisandra sphenanthera exhibits sex-specific expression, being absent in female leaves but highly expressed in male leaves [24]. Besides, the high expression of HVA22 homologs in floral tissues and/or fruits, observed in species like Arabidopsis [13], citrus [10], tomato [15], and passion fruit [18], further suggest their involvement in reproductive processes.

Although the roles of HVA22 genes in stress responses have been widely documented, their functions in cold stress adaptation remain largely unexplored in tropical plants such as pineapple, and their potential regulatory roles in reproductive development also require further investigation. Here, we performed the first comprehensive genome-wide analysis of the HVA22 family in pineapple, covering phylogenetic relationships, gene structure, conserved motifs, chromosomal distribution, synteny, cis-regulatory elements, transcription factor networks, miRNA-mediated regulation, and subcellular localization analysis. We further analyzed their expression patterns across tissues and diverse floral organs at different developmental stages, as well as their responses to various phytohormone and abiotic stress treatments, with particular emphasis on cold stress response. Our findings provide novel insights into the functional diversification of HVA22 genes in pineapple, especially in reproductive development and cold stress response, and lay a theoretical foundation for future functional studies and provide key candidate genes for improving cold tolerance in pineapple breeding.

Materials and methods

Identification and characterization of HVA22s in pineapple

Using the Phytozome database, genomic data for pineapple (version: Ananas comosus v3; https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/info/Acomosus_v3, accessed on 1 February 2024) were obtained. Protein sequences of Arabidopsis HVA22 genes and the barley HVA22 gene were sourced from the National Center for Biotechnology Information database (NCBI) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, accessed on 4 February 2024) and used as queries for BLASTP searches. The hidden Markov model (HMM) of the TB2/DP1/HVA22 domain (PF03134) were downloaded from PFAM database (http://pfam.xfam.org/, accessed on 4 February 2024) and utilized for HMMER (v3.3) searches against pineapple protein sequences with an E-value threshold of 10^−5 [25]. Candidate genes were further verified using the NCBI Conserved Domain Database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/cdd/, accessed on 4 February 2024) and SMART database (https://smart.embl.de/, accessed on 4 February 2024) [26]. The isoelectric points (pI), molecular weights (MW), and Grand Average of Hydropathicity (GRAVY) of the AcHVA22 proteins were calculated using the ExPASy ProtParam tool (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/, accessed on 4 February 2024) [27].

Phylogenetic tree, gene structure, conserved motifs and cis-acting elements analysis

Multiple protein sequence alignment was generated using ClustalW software. Phylogenetic tree of pineapple HVA22 proteins, along with homologs from citrus [10], barely [11], Arabidopsis [13], rice [15], passion fruit [18], and tomato [15], was conducted by FastTree software using the maximum likelihood (ML) method. The accuracy of the phylogenetic tree was validated through 1,000 bootstrap replicates. The evolutionary relationships were visualized using the iTOL v6.9.1 online tool (https://itol.embl.de/, accessed on 26 February 2024). Gene structure (exon/intron organization) was visualized using TBtools [28]. Conserved motifs were identified by (Multiple Em for Motif Elicitation) MEME program with default parameters [29]. The 2,000 base pairs (bp) upstream regions of the AcHVA22 genes were extracted as putative promoter regions and used for cis-element identification by PlantCare program [30]. The results were visualized by Adobe Illustrator.

Three-dimensional structural modeling of AcHVA22 proteins

Protein tertiary structures of AcHVA22s were predicted using the AlphaFold Server (https://alphafoldserver.com/, accessed on 2 September 2024). The 3D protein models were visualized and manipulated using PyMOL [31].

Prediction of transcription factor networks

Transcription factor networks were predicted following the method described by Hou et al. [18] with minor adjustments. The 2,000 base pairs (bp) upstream putative promoter regions of AcHVA22 genes were analyzed using PlantRegMap (http://plantregmap.cbi.pku.edu.cn/regulation_prediction.php, accessed on 20 May 2024) to predict potential transcription factor binding sites (p ≤ 1e − 6), and the regulatory networks were visualized with Cytoscape v3.6 [32].

Plant materials, growth conditions, and stress treatment

The pineapple cultivars MD2 and Tainong 11 were cultivated in plastic pots filled with soil mixture, under greenhouse conditions (temperature: 30 °C, humidity: 70%, and a photoperiod of 16 h of light followed by 8 h of darkness). After three months of growth, healthy MD2 seedlings were used for various stress treatments, including phytohormones (ABA and GA) and abiotic stresses (cold, heat, and drought). For phytohormone treatments, healthy seedlings were exposed to ABA and GA at concentrations of 0.1 µM. Drought stress was induced using 100 mM mannitol. Cold stress (4 °C) and heat stress (45 °C) treatments were applied using an incubator (a 16 h light/8 h dark cycle). Leaf samples were collected at 0, 12, 24, and 48 h during stress treatments. Tainong 11 seedlings were exposed to cold treatment at 8 °C, with samples collected at 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 11, 14, and 15 days post-treatment. Among them, samples collected at 0, 3, 7, and 15 days were used for transcriptome sequencing, and samples at other time points were used for qRT-PCR analysis. RNA-seq data of pineapple cold treatment were deposited in China National GeneBank DataBase (CNGBdb) with accession number CNP0008093.

Total RNA extraction and qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and floral organs such as ovule and stamen using the Omega Bio-Tek RNA extraction kit (Shanghai, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. cDNA was synthesized employing the TaKaRa reverse transcription kit (Beijing, China). Primers for AcHVA22 genes were designed through the SciTools Web Tools (https://sg.idtdna.com/pages/tools, accessed on 23 August 2024) and were listed in Additional file 1: Table S1. qRT-PCR was performed as described by Yan et al. [33] utilizing the SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (TaKaRa, China). The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: an initial denaturation step at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and a final melt curve analysis at 95 °C for 15 s. In each sample, three replicates were performed. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method [9, 34, 35], and statistical significance was assessed by Student’s t-test.

Vector construction and subcellular localization

The coding sequences (CDS) of AcHVA22 genes without termination codons were cloned into the pENTR/D-TOPO and recombined into the pGW505 vector under the 35 S promoter via LR reaction. The 35 S: HVA22s vectors were introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens (GV3103) and infiltrated into the leaf epidermal cells of tobacco (Nicotiana benthamiana). After 48–56 h of growth under 28 °C with a 16 h light and 8 h dark cycle, the subcellular localization of AcHVA22s: GFP were observed by the Leica TCS SP8 confocal microscope. Primer sequences used for vector construction are listed in Additional file 1: Table S1.

Results

Identification and characterization of the HVA22 family in pineapple

Using BLAST and HMM searches, we identified 11 non-redundant HVA22 genes across the whole pineapple genome. The gene IDs and physicochemical properties of these AcHVA22 genes were listed in Additional file 2: Table S2. These 11 HVA22 genes were distributed across 10 different chromosomes and were designated as AcHVA22A to AcHVA22K according to their chromosomal locations. The lengths of these AcHVA22 proteins varied from 75 to 306 amino acids (aa), with molecular weights ranging from 8,071.51 to 34,512.97 Da. The isoelectric points were between 5.06 and 9.35, and the aliphatic indices varied from 72.01 to 108. Stability index calculations predicted that all AcHVA22 proteins are unstable. In terms of hydrophilicity, eight proteins were hydrophilic (GRAVY < 0), while three were hydrophobic (GRAVY > 0).

Phylogenetic analysis and multiple sequence alignment of AcHVA22s

To explore evolutionary relationships, we constructed the unrooted ML phylogenetic tree using 79 HVA22 protein sequences from pineapple (11 AcHVA22s), Citrus clementina (six CcHVA22s) [10], passion fruit (six PeHVA22s) [18], Arabidopsis (11 AtHVA22s) [13], barley (14 HvHVA22s) [11], tomato (15 SlHVA22s) [15], and rice (16 OsHVA22s) [15] (Fig. 1A, Additional file 3:Table S3). All HVA22s were divided into four categories, namely, Group I to Group IV (Fig. 1A). Group IV was the maximum subgroup with 30 genes, followed by Group I (24) and Group II (17). Group III had the smallest number of members, consisting of eight genes. AcHVA22s, HvHVA22s and OsHVA22s were mainly present in Group IV, while CcHVA22s was not distributed in Group IV. The distribution of AtHVA22s, PeHVA22s, and SlHVA22s was unequal among the four subgroups. Detailly, AcHVA22E, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22G, AcHVA22H and AcHVA22J were closely clustered with rice OsHVA22m (OsHLP1, HVA22-like protein 1 [21]) in Group IV. AcHVA22A, AcHVA22B, AcHVA22D were clustered in Group I with Arabidopsis AtHVA22b, AtHVA22c, AtHVA22f, AtHVA22k. While AcHVA22C and AcHVA22I were grouped together in Group II with barely HVA22 (HvHVA22n) [11]. The remaining AcHVA22K was clustered with AtHVA22H in Group III.

Tertiary structure modelling of AcHVA22s

The topology and three-dimensional structure of proteins were critical for understanding their cellular localization, structural properties, and function [36]. Structure predictions indicated that the AcHVA22 proteins typically consisted of three transmembrane helices (TM) and one amphipathic helix (APH), along with a heterogeneous structural region at the C-terminus (Fig. 1B). This composition was similar to HVA22 proteins in Arabidopsis [37], but differed from homologous proteins in mammals and yeast, which generally contained four transmembrane helices [38, 39]. Notably, pineapple and Arabidopsis HVA22 proteins lacked the first N-terminal α-helix found in these other organisms [37]. Besides, only part of the transmembrane helices was predicted in the AcHVA22K protein (Group III). Homology modeling results showed that AcHVA22 proteins within the same Group shared similarities in their C-terminal structure (Fig. 2). Clade IV proteins were characterized with longer length of the APH, which was followed by an extended disordered region. These structural characteristics could have significantly influenced functional diversity of these proteins. Moreover, the APH region of AcHVA22 proteins in Group IV formed a hook-like shape, potentially due to the presence of a conserved proline residue in this region. Proline residues, known for their cyclic side chains and rigid backbone structure, often cause bends or twists in α-helices, affecting the overall conformation and function of the protein. Therefore, the unique structure of the APH and C-terminal region of AcHVA22 proteins may have played a key role in their distinct membrane interactions.

Gene structure, conserved motif, and cis-regulatory element analyses of AcHVA22s

To investigate the diversity of gene structures and the composition of conserved motifs in AcHVA22 genes, we analyzed the quantity and arrangement of exons, introns, and conserved motifs (Fig. 3). Based on the phylogenetic tree, the AcHVA22 genes were classified into four groups, showing a high degree of similarity in gene structures and conserved motif arrangements within the same clusters (Fig. 3A, B, and D). The number of exons in AcHVA22 genes varied from three to six. The AcHVA22 genes belonging to Group IV exhibited the largest number of exons (six exons). Following that, the gene members in Groups I and II had five exons. While AcHVA22K, the sole member of group III, had the fewest exons, with three. Most AcHVA22 genes exhibited an intact genetic structure, however, AcHVA22K lacked the 3’ UTR. Furthermore, with the exception of AcHVA22K, the position of the TB2/DP1/HVA22 conserved domain was consistent across AcHVA22A to AcHVA22J (Fig. 3C).

Different colors boxes represent different motifs. (C) Gene Domain in all AcHVA22s. (D) Gene structures of AcHVA22 genes. Light blue boxes represented exons, grey lines represented introns, and dark blue boxes represented untranslated regions (UTRs)

The analysis of conserved motifs in AcHVA22 proteins revealed that the number of motifs ranged from 1 to 6, with motif 2 present in all AcHVA22 proteins (Fig. 3B). Generally, members clustered in the same cluster contained comparable motif arrangements. Group I members displayed 5 to 6 motifs, each containing motif 1, motif 2, motif 4, motif 6, and motif 9. AcHVA22C and AcHVA22I, from group II, shared the same motif arrangement, including motif 1, motif 2, motif 4, and motif 6. AcHVA22K, from group III, had only one motif: motif 2. Group IV members contained 5 to 6 motifs, with each member possessing motif 1, motif 2, motif 3, motif 5, and motif 7. For all AcHVA22 proteins in groups I and II, the first motif at the N-terminus was motif 6, whereas in group IV, the first N-terminal motif was motif 7. These differences in gene structures and motifs among AcHVA22s may result in their functional diversity, while those with similar characteristics might be likely to perform related functions.

Cis-regulatory elements (CREs), which are non-coding DNA sequences situated in the gene promoter regions, are crucial for their expression and are extensively involved in regulating diverse processes [40]. The CREs in the putative promoter regions of AcHVA22 genes were predicted and classified into four main types: light-responsive CREs, plant growth and development CREs, phytohormone-responsive CREs, and stress-responsive CREs (Fig. 4, Additional file 4: Table S4). Light-responsive CREs, like the G-box and B-box 4, were present in nearly all AcHVA22 genes, indicating broad regulation by light. Various phytohormone-responsive elements were predicted within the promoter sequences of AcHVA22 genes, suggesting involvement of different phytohormones in their transcriptional regulation. Among that, the ABA response element (ABRE) was present in the promoter regions of almost all AcHVA22 genes, except for AcHVA22B (Group I) and AcHVA22H (Group IV). Methyl jasmonate response elements (CGGTA motif and TGACG-motif) were found within the promoter sequences of AcHVA22C, AcHVA22D, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22G, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22J. Gibberellin response elements (P-box and GARE-motif) were present only in the promoter regions of AcHVA22B, AcHVA22C, and AcHVA22K. Salicylic acid responsiveness CREs (TCA-element) were detected only within the promoter sequences of AcHVA22A, AcHVA22E, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22K. Auxin-responsiveness CREs (TGA-element and AuxRE-core) were found only in the promoter regions of AcHVA22D, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22G, AcHVA22H, and AcHVA22K. Additionally, CREs related to stress responses and plant growth and development were predicted, but the distribution of these elements varied among AcHVA22 members. For instance, CREs related to meristem expression (CAT-box) were observed only within the promoter sequences of AcHVA22B, AcHVA22J, and AcHVA22K. CREs associated with endosperm expression (GCN4-motif) were identified only in the promoter regions of AcHVA22C, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22K. CREs involved in low-temperature responsiveness (LTR) were present only in the promoter sequences of AcHVA22A, AcHVA22C, and AcHVA22E. CREs related to dehydration, low-temperature, and salt stress (DRE) were found only within the promoter sequences of AcHVA22D. The MYB binding site connected with drought inducibility (MBS) was predicted only in the promoter regions of AcHVA22D, AcHVA22F, and AcHVA22H. The varying combinations of CREs may lead to distinct responses of AcHVA22 gene members during growth, development, or stress.

Chromosomal location and syntenic analysis of HVA22s

The AcHVA22 family genes were mapped to chromosomes LG01, LG02, LG03, LG08, LG09, LG11, LG12, LG17, LG21, and LG25 (Additional file 8: Fig. S1). Notably, LG17 was the only chromosome containing two genes: AcHVA22C and AcHVA22J. There was no clear positive correlation between chromosome length and the quantity of AcHVA22 genes, and their distribution was not biased toward specific chromosomes. Gene duplication has been recognized for a long time as a significant evolutionary force, providing raw material for genetic innovation, morphological diversity, and speciation [41]. Here, we identified a single pair of segmentally duplicated genes within the AcHVA22 family: AcHVA22C and AcHVA22I (Additional file 8: Fig. S1). Synteny analysis across species is a valuable tool for studying evolutionary relationships and genetic affinities [42]. To further explore the synteny relationships of AcHVA22 genes and infer their phylogenetic mechanisms, we performed a comparative synteny analysis between pineapple and four representative species: Arabidopsis, grape (Vitis vinifera), rice, and maize (Zea mays) (Fig. 5, Additional file 5: Table S5). A total of seven AcHVA22 genes showed collinearity with the rice genome, followed by six with Arabidopsis, five with maize, and four with grape. Among these, only AcHVA22C displayed collinear associations with all four species, suggesting that AcHVA22C may have existed prior to the divergence of the common ancestor shared by pineapple and these plants. This implies that AcHVA22C may play an important biological role and exhibit evolutionary conservation across these species.

Transcription factor regulatory network of AcHVA22 genes

To elucidate the potential transcription factor (TF) regulatory network of AcHVA22 genes, the promoter sequences of 11 AcHVA22 genes were extracted and analyzed using the PTRM database [43]. As shown in Fig. 6 and Additional file 6: Table S6, various TF families, such as BBR-BPC, MIKC_MADS, ERF, AP2, and Dof, were predicted to regulate AcHVA22 genes. Among these TF families, BBR-BPC was the most abundant, with 172 members, followed by ERF (52 members) and MIKC_MADS (44 members) (Fig. 6B). AcHVA22G was targeted by the highest number of TFs (85), followed by AcHVA22H (52) and AcHVA22B (46) (Additional file 6: Table S6). Additionally, AcHVA22F and AcHVA22G were regulated by the most diverse categories of TFs (seven), followed by AcHVA22A (six) (Additional file 6: Table S6). Furthermore, many TFs related to stress responses and plant growth and development were also found, including Dof, MYB, bZIP, GRAS, and MIKC_MADS.

Nodes colored in light blue signify TFs, whereas pink circular nodes designate AcHVA22 genes. (B) Statistical summary of the predicted number of TFs regulating AcHVA22 genes

miRNA-mediated regulatory mechanisms of AcHVA22s gene family

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), belonging to a category of non-coding RNAs, identified in eukaryotes, approximately 19–25 nucleotides (nt) in length, which are primarily involved in post-transcriptional gene regulation [44–46]. To understand the regulatory mechanisms of miRNAs in AcHVA22 genes in pineapple, 15 miRNAs from eight distinct miRNA families were identified (Additional file 7: Table S7). The interaction network and schematic representation of miRNA target sites in AcHVA22 genes were studied (Fig. 7). The 15 miRNAs targeted seven AcHVA22 genes (Fig. 7A). The analysis revealed that AcHVA22D was the most frequently targeted gene (Fig. 7B), with miRNAs such as miR5021e-5p, miR5021a-5p, miR5021b-3p, miR156f-3p, and miR156i-3p. AcHVA22B was the second most targeted gene, with miR528a-5p, miR528b-5p, miR528c-5p, and miR156a-5p. AcHVA22A was targeted by three miRNAs: miR156a-5p, miR172b-3p, and miR444b-3p. Additionally, AcHVA22E (miR156b-5p), AcHVA22F (miR1432a-3p), AcHVA22G (miR159a-5p), and AcHVA22H (miR394a-3p) were each targeted by a single miRNA. Among them, the miR156 family is a group of miRNAs that widely target different members of the AcHVA22 gene family.

Subcellular localization of AcHVA22 proteins

To explore the subcellular location of AcHVA22s within plant cells, we connected the CDS of some AcHVA22s to the segment situated between the CaMV 35 S promoter and the region that encodes GFP. The transient expression analysis in tobacco leaves revealed that the control protein, which was produced by the empty vector (35 S: GFP), was uniformly distributed across both the plasma membrane and the nucleus. AcHVA22B: GFP and AcHVA22K: GFP proteins were mainly localized in the cytoplasm. AcHVA22D: GFP, AcHVA22F: GFP, and AcHVA22J: GFP proteins were distributed on the plasma membrane. AcHVA22E: GFP, AcHVA22G: GFP, and AcHVA22H: GFP proteins were similar to the empty vector and were distributed in the plasma membrane and nucleus (Fig. 8).

Expression analysis of AcHVA22 genes based on transcriptomic data

To investigate the potential roles of the AcHVA22 genes, expression data generated from RNA-seq were used to assess their expression profiles across various pineapple tissues, including floral organs [47] and fruit at different stages of development [1], as well as under cold stress (Fig. 9). Genes with low expression levels were excluded, and the remaining genes expression profiles were visualized through heatmaps. During floral development, AcHVA22G demonstrated high expression during the middle phase of stamen development, and showed an increasing expression trend during ovule development. AcHVA22A, AcHVA22H, and AcHVA22K were preferentially expressed during ovule development, with AcHVA22K also showing high abundance in other floral organs. During the fourth stage of gynoecium development, AcHVA22D exhibited specific high expression level. In the later stages of petal development, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22B, and AcHVA22J were dramatically expressed. And in the later stages of stamen and gynoecium development, AcHVA22C, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22E were preferentially expressed. During fruit development, these genes also displayed diverse expression patterns. AcHVA22D and AcHVA22E were predominantly expressed in the initial stages of fruit development, while AcHVA22F, AcHVA22H, AcHVA22K, and AcHVA22G were dramatically expressed in the later stages. Notably, AcHVA22K also exhibited high abundance across other stages of fruit development. Under cold stress, the response of these genes varied. The expression level of AcHVA22G, AcHVA22A, and AcHVA22H decreased in response to cold, whereas AcHVA22K, AcHVA22C, and AcHVA22F were up-regulated under cold stress. These transcriptomic results hint that different members of AcHVA22 genes may exhibit distinct roles in pineapple growth, development, and stress response, indicating functional divergence in the gene family.

For sexual reproduction to be completed in flowering plants, the normal development of ovules and stamens is essential. In this study, we further confirmed the expression patterns of AcHVA22s during ovule and stamen development through qRT-PCR (Fig. 10). The outcomes demonstrated that, apart from some discrepancies in the expression trends of genes with low abundance, the results of qRT-PCR were generally in agreement with the transcriptome data. For example, AcHVA22E exhibited a gradual up-regulation during stamen development, while AcHVA22G and AcHVA22K showed an initial increase in expression and then a decrease during the second, fourth, and fifth stages of stamen development. In contrast, AcHVA22H displayed a downward trend in these same stages of stamen development. In the ovule, there were some inconsistencies between the qRT-PCR results and the transcriptome data. For instance, the qRT-PCR results revealed that AcHVA22J exhibits significantly elevated expression levels in the seventh stage of ovule development, in contrast to its expression in the first stage. However, the transcriptome data indicated that this gene had low expression levels throughout ovule development. Many AcHVA22 genes showed minimal differences between the first and seventh stages of ovule development, but the consistent results showed that the expression levels of both AcHVA22F and AcHVA22G were higher in the seventh stage than in the first stage.

Tissue-specific expression patterns of AcHVA22s in pineapple

To investigate the tissue-specific expression patterns of AcHVA22s in pineapple, we performed qRT-PCR to test the relative expression levels of each AcHVA22 gene root, stem, leaf, flower, and fruit. Expression levels in different organs were determined relative to those in the leaf, which served as the control. The results revealed that AcHVA22 genes exhibited distinct tissue-specific expression patterns (Fig. 11). The expression of AcHVA22A was undetectable in the root and stem, while AcHVA22F and AcHVA22I were undetectable in the stem. In the leaf and fruit, AcHVA22A displayed elevated expression, while AcHVA22B exhibited preferentially higher expression in the leaf with lower expression in the fruit. AcHVA22C, AcHVA22E, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22J exhibited the highest expression level in the root, followed by the leaf, flower, or stem. AcHVA22E, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22I, and AcHVA22J also showed higher expression in both stem and root. Additionally, AcHVA22G, AcHVA22H, AcHVA22J, and AcHVA22K were nearly undetectable at the transcriptional level in the flower. These results suggest that AcHVA22 genes might have functional roles in the development of various tissues in pineapple, particularly in vegetative tissues.

Expression analysis of AcHVA22 genes in response to phytohormone and abiotic stresses

To investigate the response patterns of AcHVA22 genes to plant hormones and abiotic stresses, we employed qRT-PCR to measure their relative expression levels in the leaves of the pineapple variety MD2 at various short-term treatment intervals (0 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h post-treatment) under different plant hormone (ABA and GA) and abiotic stress (drought, high temperature at 45 °C, and low-temperature at 4 °C) conditions (Fig. 12A-E). Additionally, considering the significant impact of low-temperatures on pineapple production, we also examined the expression profiles of AcHVA22 genes in the leaves of the TN11 pineapple variety under long-term cold treatment at 8 °C (0 d, 1 d, 5 d, 7 d, 11 d, and 14 d post-treatment) (Fig. 12F). Under ABA treatment, six genes (AcHVA22B/D/E/F/G/H) were induced by ABA and exhibited a consistent expression pattern, with levels initially increasing and peaking at 12 h post-treatment, followed by a decline. AcHVA22I and AcHVA22K showed increased expression levels at 24 h post-treatment, while their expression levels decreased at 12 and 48 h compared to pre-treatment levels. In contrast, AcHVA22J exhibited up-regulation at 12 and 48 h post-treatment, with no significant changes at 24 h. The expression of AcHVA22A and AcHVA22C was consistently down-regulated under ABA treatment across all time points (Fig. 12A). Unlike the varied expression patterns under ABA treatment, all AcHVA22 genes were up-regulated under GA treatment, displaying a gradual increase in expression. Among them, the expression of AcHVA22C/D/E/K was significantly enhanced by GA treatment (Fig. 12B). Under drought conditions, the expression of nearly all AcHVA22 genes was significantly up-regulated at 12 h post-treatment, followed by a gradual decline, with seven genes (AcHVA22A/B/D/F/G/I/K) showing particularly pronounced up-regulation (Fig. 12C). Under ABA and drought treatments, the expression trends of AcHVA22 genes demonstrated some consistency, such as a significant up-regulation at 12 h, with genes like AcHVA22B/D/F/G/H exhibiting similar expression patterns under both conditions. Under high-temperature (45 °C) conditions, the expression of AcHVA22A/E/G/H gradually increased, while AcHVA22D/I/J/K were significantly up-regulated at 48 h post-treatment (Fig. 12D). At 4 °C, the expression of AcHVA22A/C/D/E/H/I was significantly up-regulated, with peak levels observed at 24 h post-treatment, whereas AcHVA22B/F/J exhibited down-regulation during the early stages of cold treatment (Fig. 12E). In the leaves of the pineapple variety Tainong 11 under long-term cold treatment at 8 °C, seven genes (AcHVA22C/D/E/G/I/J/K) were significantly up-regulated, while AcHVA22H was the only gene down-regulated in response to the cold. Additionally, the expression levels of AcHVA22A/B/F at 8 °C were inconsistent, showing down-regulation at certain time points, followed by up-regulation in the later stages of treatment (Fig. 12F).

Collectively, a schematic summary diagram was generated based on the expression patterns of AcHVA22s across different tissues or under phytohormone/stress treatments (Fig. 13). We found that AcHVA22 genes had diverse expression patterns in various vegetative tissues, flowers and fruits development of pineapple, suggesting their potential for different regulatory functions in growth and development processes of pineapple. The HVA22 gene family is well-known for its responsiveness to ABA and stress-inducible. The results of hormone treatment experiments showed that not only most of AcHVA22 genes are up-regulated by ABA, but almost all members of AcHVA22s are also induced by GA. Similar phenomena had also been observed in our previous research on passion fruit, suggesting that GA also plays an important role in the regulation of HVA22 genes [18]. Stress treatment experiments had demonstrated that AcHVA22s in pineapple are broadly involved in the stress response, and almost all members were up-regulated shortly (12 h) after drought induction. In contrast, the expression of AcHVA22s was inconsistent at different time points after high temperature treatment, and most of AcHVA22s members were significantly up-regulated at 48 h after treatment. As a tropical and subtropical fruit, pineapples are sensitive to low temperature conditions. During short-term and long-term cold treatment of two different pineapple varieties, there were some differences in the composition of AcHVA22s members which are induced to high expression. However, the expression levels of AcHVA22C/D/E/G/I/K were up-regulated under both short and long-term cold treatments in two distinct pineapple varieties, underscoring their potential significance as key players in cold stress tolerance. These results provide important insights for further functional analysis of AcHVA22s.

Discussion

Pineapple is a key tropical fruit crop, and proper flower and fruit development are essential for high-quality fruit production. However, its growth is significantly impacted by low temperatures, with winter cold limiting year-round cultivation [2–6, 48]. Identifying genes that regulate the development of flowers and fruits, as well as the response to cold stress, is essential for progress in molecular breeding techniques for pineapple. The HVA22 gene family, known to be induced by ABA, plays important functions in growth regulation and stress response across various eukaryotic organisms [8–11, 13–19]. In numerous plants, HVA22 gene family has been identified and may play crucial roles in plant development and adaptation to various abiotic stresses [8, 10–13, 15, 18, 49]. However, it has not been studied in pineapple as of yet.

In this study, we identified 11 AcHVA22 genes in the pineapple genome, which weren’t evenly distributed among ten chromosomes (Additional file 2 and 8: Table S2 and Fig. S1). The number of AcHVA22 genes was comparable to those in Arabidopsis (11) [13], barley (14) [11], tomato (15) [15], and rice (16) [15], but differed from citrus (six) [10], passion fruit (six) [18], and cotton (ranging from 16 to 34 in different species) [19]. HVA22 represents a small gene family with relatively few members among various gene families, and the expansion of HVA22 gene numbers in cotton was reported to mainly result from segmental duplication or whole-genome duplication (WGD) [19]. Unlike in cotton, only one pair of segmentally duplicated genes (AcHVA22C and AcHVA22I) was identified in pineapple. The genome of pineapple has only undergone two ancient WGDs in monocots (τ and σ), resulting in a relatively smaller number of HVA22 genes in pineapple (Additional file 8: Fig. S1). Thus, the number of changes of HVA22 family members across species was closely related with their distinct evolutionary processes.

HVA22 has been reported as a eukaryote specific gene family involved in cellular activities related to the endomembrane system. Differently, the results of subcellular localization analysis revealed that most AcHVA22 proteins were present in the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus (Fig. 8). According to previous studies, HVA22D in Arabidopsis, HVA22 in barley, and HLP1 (HVA-like protein 1) in rice are all localized to the endoplasmic reticulum [12, 21–23]. Although these proteins perform somewhat different functions, they are all associated with autophagy or programmed cell death processes. Subcellular localization is a critical determinant of protein function, as proteins located in different cellular compartments often carry out distinct biological roles (for example, nuclear-localized proteins are typically involved in transcriptional regulation, whereas ER- or vacuole-localized proteins participate in autophagy and trafficking). The diverse localization patterns of AcHVA22s in pineapple may suggest that they function through different mechanisms, which remain to be verified through further experimental studies. At the protein level, AcHVA22 sequences had an average length of 203 aa, which was longer than that of barley HVA22 (130 aa) [8] and citrus HVA22s (an average of 156 aa) [10] (Additional file 2: Table S2). Variations in protein length and sequence composition may be related with the differences in subcellular localization between AcHVA22 proteins and the previously reported HVA22 proteins [12].

To investigate the evolutionary relationships of HVA22s, a ML phylogenetic tree was constructed using the HVA22s’ protein sequences from Arabidopsis, barley, rice, citrus, tomato, and passion fruit, along with the 11 pineapple HVA22 sequences. The outcomes revealed that all 79 HVA22 proteins were grouped into four Groups (Fig. 1A). The classification of HVA22s from species other than pineapple was consistent with previously reported findings, indicating that our phylogenetic tree was reliable [15, 16]. The AcHVA22s were unevenly distributed across the groups, with AcHVA22E, AcHVA22F, AcHVA22G, AcHVA22H, and AcHVA22J closely clustered in Group IV; AcHVA22A, AcHVA22B, and AcHVA22D clustered in Group I; AcHVA22C and AcHVA22I clustered in Group II with barley HvHVA22n; and AcHVA22K clustered alone in Group III. The analysis of protein structure prediction and gene structure showed that members of the same subgroup shared certain structural similarities (Figs. 2 and 3). For instance, only AcHVA22K possessed partial transmembrane helices. Structurally, this gene lacked a 3’ UTR, had the fewest exons (three), and contained only one motif, motif 2. In contrast, all Group IV members were characterized by a longer APH region, followed by an extended disordered region. This group also had the highest number of exons. Apart from motif 2, which was conserved across all AcHVA22s, the motif composition varied significantly among different groups. These structural differences may have contributed to the functional diversification of HVA22 genes across the groups.

Previous studies demonstrated that HVA22 genes were expressed across various plant tissues, with some genes showing tissue-specific expression [10, 13, 15, 16, 18, 19]. Using transcriptome data and qRT-PCR, the expression patterns of AcHVA22 genes in diverse pineapple tissues, and during floral organs/fruit development were analyzed (Figs. 9 and 10, and Fig. 11). The results showed that, except for AcHVA22K, which was abundantly expressed during both flower and fruit development, most AcHVA22 genes were primarily expressed in specific tissues or developmental stages. For instance, AcHVA22B, AcHVA22F, and AcHVA22J were consistently and specifically expressed during the late stages of petal development. While some genes exhibited such consistent expression patterns during flower development, their expression patterns during fruit development varied. AcHVA22B showed its highest expression at the early stages of fruit development, AcHVA22F was highly expressed during the late stages, and AcHVA22J exhibited low expression during fruit development and was filtered out. This tissue-specific expression pattern of different HVA22 gene members during flower and/or fruit development has also been reported in species such as citrus [10], passion fruit [18] and tomato [15, 16]. These differences may be related to the variation of CREs in the promoter among dissimilar gene members. We found that light-responsive and ABA-responsive CREs were diffusely present in the promoter regions of AcHVA22 genes, while auxin-responsive CREs and those CREs associated with plant growth and development varied significantly in their distribution (Fig. 4). Additionally, we also identified TFs and miRNAs participated in the regulation of AcHVA22 genes (Figs. 6 and 7, Additional file 6 and 7: Table S6, and Table S7). As in other gene families, we found that a lot of TFs participated in stress responses and plant growth and development were predicted to regulate these genes [50–53]. However, even within the same group, AcHVA22 members exhibited significant differences in the types and numbers of TFs regulating them (Fig. 6 and Additional file 6: Table S6). Consistently, the miRNA prediction analysis also showed that different AcHVA22 members were targeted by different miRNAs (Fig. 7 and Additional file 7: Table S7). Only AcHVA22A and AcHVA22B were predicted to be targeted by the same miR156a-5p, but the other miRNA targeted these two genes differed. In other gene families, gene members within the same subgroup or duplicated gene pairs often share similarities in these regulatory factors [54, 55]. However, the functional redundancy in different members might be limited, due to the relatively small number of HVA22 gene family. These differences in regulatory elements may have led to distinct responses of AcHVA22 gene members in the processes of pineapple growth and development.

ABA is an important hormone in response to plant stress, and the widespread presence of ABA-responsive CREs of HVA22 genes promoter region suggested their potential role in ABA signaling and stress response [8, 11, 13, 15, 18]. We found that most AcHVA22 genes were induced and up-regulated shortly after ABA treatment (Fig. 12A). And in drought treatment, the similar response was observed, where the expression levels of all AcHVA22 genes were markedly upregulated immediately (Figs. 12C and 13). As a Crassulacean acid metabolism plant, pineapple has strong adaptability to drought conditions [56] and these rapidly responsive genes might play a major role in the plant’s quick adaptation to dynamically changing environmental conditions. Additionally, all AcHVA22 genes showed increasing expression trends with prolonged GA treatment (Figs. 12B and 13), a phenomenon also reported in passion fruit [18]. Previous reports suggested that ABA and GA regulate programmed cell death (PCD)-associated vacuolar formation in barley aleurone cells, with GA treatment causing dormancy break and a decrease in HVA22 transcripts [8, 12]. The above studies implied that GA also play important regulating roles in AcHVA22 genes. Similar to AtHVA22s [13], PeHVA22s [18], and SlHVA22s [15], AcHVA22s universally respond to ABA, drought, and GA, suggesting that the response of HVA22s from different species to plant hormones (ABA and GA) and drought conditions is conserved. In contrast, the response of AcHVA22 genes to high and low-temperatures varied, indicating that different members may play distinct roles under adverse temperature conditions (Fig. 12D-F).

Pineapple, being a tropical and subtropical fruit, is highly sensitive to low-temperature [48], making the identification of genes involved in cold stress responses of great practical significance for molecular breeding. We compared the responses of AcHVA22 genes to short and long-term cold treatments in two pineapple varieties (Fig. 12E-F). This result illustrated that the expression patterns of AcHVA22 genes were largely consistent across both low-temperature treatments, with only minor differences in individual gene expression trends. Specifically, in the MD2 variety at 4 °C, AcHVA22A was dramatically up-regulated at all time points, while in the Tainong 11 variety at 8 °C, its expression fluctuated between up-regulation and down-regulation at different time points. AcHVA22H was significantly up-regulated at 24 and 48 h in the MD2 variety at 4 °C but was repressed in the Tainong 11 variety at 8 °C. Despite these differences, most other genes exhibited consistent expression patterns under both conditions. AcHVA22F and AcHVA22J were significantly down-regulated at 12 h in the MD2 variety at 4 °C, followed by a gradual recovery, while in the Tainong 11 variety at 8 °C, these genes displayed a gradual increase in expression over time, suggesting their potential roles in long-term cold adaptation in pineapple. Six genes (AcHVA22C/D/E/G/I/K) were up-regulated under both short-term and long-term cold treatments in the two varieties, indicating their possible importance in pineapple’s response to freezing (4 °C) or chilling (8 °C) stress (Figs. 12E-F and 13). Under cold stress, the expression of AcHVA22C/I and SlHVA22b/e/i/n in Group II and AcHVA22E/G and AtHVA22A/D/E in Group IV were up-regulated, indicating that HVA22 homologous genes might share similar functions in cold response [13, 15]. Additionally, some stress-related CREs were found upstream of these genes; for example, AcHVA22C and AcHVA22E contained low-temperature-responsive CREs in their upstream regions, which were consistently support their potential roles in stress response. These findings provide not only new candidate genes for functional studies but also potential targets for improving cold tolerance in pineapple breeding.

Conclusion

In this study, we systematically identified 11 HVA22 genes in pineapple and named as AcHVA22A to AcHVA22K based on their chromosomal locations. Subcellular localization analysis disclosed that most AcHVA22 proteins presented in the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. As a small ABA induced gene family, CRE analysis indicated that the promoter regions of AcHVA22 genes frequently contain ABA-responsive elements. Expression analysis indicated that most AcHVA22 genes exhibited consistent responses to ABA, GA, and drought, indicating a conserved function for this gene family in these processes. However, the responses of different AcHVA22 genes to high and low-temperatures varied, implying functional diversification under temperature stress. Similarly, tissue-specific expression analysis demonstrated that AcHVA22s exhibit varying expression patterns across different tissues and/or developmental stages, suggesting their diverse functions in the development of flower and fruit. Phylogenetic analysis classified all AcHVA22 genes into four groups. Although structure analysis showed that members of the same subgroup shared certain structural similarities, the involvement of various regulatory factors, such as CREs, transcription factors, and miRNAs, even within the same group, may be propitious to the AcHVA22 genes’ functional diversity. Notably, several AcHVA22 genes (AcHVA22C/D/E/G/I/K) are consistently up-regulated under both short and long-term cold treatments across two varieties, suggesting their key roles in cold stress adaptation. Our findings expand the understanding of HVA22 gene evolution and functional diversification, provide further evidence for their roles in reproductive development, and highlight their involvement in cold stress adaptation in tropical crops such as pineapple. Moreover, the identification of cold-responsive AcHVA22 genes provides key candidate genes for future functional validation and offers valuable genetic resources for molecular breeding aimed at improving cold tolerance in pineapple.

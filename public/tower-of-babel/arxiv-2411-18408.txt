A new proof of nonlinear Landau damping for the 3D Vlasov-Poisson system near Poisson equilibrium
Quoc-Hung Nguyen, Dongyi Wei, Zhifei Zhang

Abstract
This paper investigates nonlinear Landau damping in the 3D Vlasov-Poisson (VP) system. We study the asymptotic stability of the Poisson equilibrium μ ⁡ ( v ) = 1 π 2 ​ ( 1 + | v | 2 ) 2 \mu(v)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(1+|v|^{2})^{2}} under small perturbations. Building on the foundational work of Ionescu, Pausader, Wang, and Widmayer [ 27 ] , we provide a streamlined proof of nonlinear Landau damping for the 3D unscreened VP system. Our analysis leverages sharp decay estimates, novel decomposition techniques to demonstrate the stabilization of the particle distribution and the decay of electric field. These results reveal the free transport-like behavior for the perturbed density ρ ⁡ ( t , x ) \rho(t,x) , and enhance the understanding of Landau damping in an unconfined setting near stable equilibria.

Introduction

This paper investigates nonlinear Landau damping for the 3D Vlasov-Poisson (VP) system. Specifically, we study the following system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbf{f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathbf{f}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}\mathbf{f}=0,\\
E=-\nabla_{x}U,~-\Delta U={\rho^{*}}-1,~~{\rho^{*}}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{f}(t,x,v)dv,\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

in the whole space

,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Here
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{f}(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the probability distribution of charged particles in plasma,
\rho^{*}(t,x)
is the electric charge density, and
E=E(t,x)
is the electric field. The initial data
f_{0}(x,v)
is close to the Poisson equilibrium
\mu(v)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}(1+|v|^{2})^{2}}
.

\mathbf{f}(t,x,v)=\mu(v)+f(t,x,v),

\mu(v)
is the Poisson equilibrium and
\mathbf{f}(t=0,x,v)
closes to

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu=-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f,\\
E=\nabla_{x}\Delta^{-1}\rho,~~\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)dv,\\
f|_{t=0}=f_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

•

•

Asymptotic stability: we prove the long-time stability of solutions, demonstrating the stabilization of the distribution function

We state the main theorem, which demonstrates the global existence and asymptotic stability of the perturbed system.

Theorem 1.1

Let
0<\kappa_{0}\ll 1
. Assume that
\iint f_{0}(x,v)dxdv=0
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if

[f_{0}]=\sum_{j=0,1,2}\sup_{x,v}\langle x,v\rangle^{10}|\nabla_{x,v}^{j}f_{0}(x,v)|\leq\epsilon_{0},

(1.3)

then the VP system (
1.2
) has global unique solution
f
satisfying

\displaystyle|\nabla\Delta^{-1}\rho(t,x)|+\langle t,x\rangle|\nabla^{2}\Delta^{-1}\rho(t,x)|+\langle t,x\rangle^{1+\kappa_{0}}|\nabla^{3}\Delta^{-1}\rho(t,x)|\leq\langle t,x\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}[f_{0}],

(1.4)

for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Moreover, there is a function
f_{\infty}\in C^{0,1}_{x,v}
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{5}|f(t,x+tv,v)-f_{\infty}(x,v)|\lesssim[f_{0}]\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau\langle\tau,x+(\tau-t)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}d\tau,

(1.5)

for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

By considering the associated characteristics
\left(X_{s,t}(x,v),V_{s,t}(x,v)\right)
, we express the solution
f
explicitly as

f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(X_{0,t}(x,v),V_{0,t}(x,v))-\int_{0}^{t}E(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(V_{s,t}(x,v))ds.

(1.6)

We introduce the following key decompositions

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)=f_{0}(X_{0,t}(x,v),V_{0,t}(x,v)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v)=\int_{0}^{t}E(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(v)-E(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(V_{s,t}(x,v)))dvds,

which lead to important macroscopic quantities such as

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{0}(t,x)=\int\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)+\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v)dv,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{1}(t,x)=\int(\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)+\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v))vdv,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{2}(t,x)=\int(\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)+\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v))v\otimes vdv.

(1.9)

These quantities obey the following conservation laws and structural equations, which are critical to the analysis (see (
2.6

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{0}+\text{div}\mathbf{\mathbf{R}_{1}}=0,~\partial_{t}\mathbf{\mathbf{R}_{1}}+\text{div}\mathbf{R}_{2}=\operatorname{div}(E(t,x)\otimes E(t,x))-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|E(t,x)|^{2}).

(1.10)

Using the formula for the density
\rho
, as detailed in (
2.8

\iint f_{0}(x,v)dxdv=0,

we systematically establish global estimates for
\rho
. These results align with and extend the methodologies developed for the screened Vlasov-Poisson system in

2
Solving the density equation and Bootstrap setup

This section focuses on solving the density equation
\rho(t,x)
using the macroscopic moments
\mathbf{R}_{0},\mathbf{R}_{1},\mathbf{R}_{2}
. Following the approach in
[
17
]
and
[
27
]
, we derive a decomposition of
\rho(t,x)
into oscillatory and residual components, which allows us to establish precise decay estimates for the bootstrap argument.

Starting from the solution representation (
1.6
), the density
\rho(t,x)
satisfies

\rho=-\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)G(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds+\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t,x),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\hat{G}(s,\xi)=e^{-s|\xi|},\quad G(s,x)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\frac{s}{(s^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}},\quad\forall s>0.

As in
[
17
]
, the characteristics
\left(X_{s,t}(x,v),V_{s,t}(x,v)\right)
of (
1.2
) can be given for any
0\leq s\leq t<\infty
,

\displaystyle X_{s,t}(x,v)=x-(t-s)v+Y_{s,t}(x-vt,v),~~V_{s,t}(x,v)=v+W_{s,t}(x-vt,v),

(2.2)

\displaystyle Y_{s,t}(x,v)=\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)E(\tau,x+\tau v+Y_{\tau,t}(x,v))d\tau,\quad W_{s,t}(x,v)=-\int_{s}^{t}E(\tau,x+\tau v+Y_{\tau,t}(x,v))d\tau.

(2.3)

2.1
Decomposition of
\rho(t,x)

We define the quantity

F(t,x,v):=f(t,x,v)+\int_{0}^{t}E(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot\nabla\mu(v)ds.

(2.4)

This can be split into two components

F(t,x,v)=\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)+\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\rho)(t,x,v)=f_{0}(X_{0,t}(x,v),V_{0,t}(x,v)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho)(t,x,v)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(E(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(v)-E(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(V_{s,t}(x,v))\right)ds.

Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=-E\cdot\nabla_{v}f.

The macroscopic moments
\mathbf{R}_{0},\mathbf{R}_{1},\mathbf{R}_{2}
defined in (
1.7
), (
1.8
) and (
1.9
)
satisfy the following coupled system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{0}+\text{div}\mathbf{R}_{1}=0,\quad\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{1}+\text{div}\mathbf{R}_{2}=E(t,x)\rho(t,x)

(2.5)

Using the relationship for the electric field and charge density

E(t,x)\rho(t,x)=\operatorname{div}(E(t,x)\otimes E(t,x))-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|E(t,x)|^{2}),

the system can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{0}+\text{div}\mathbf{R}_{1}=0,\quad\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{1}+\text{div}\mathbf{R}_{2}=\operatorname{div}(E(t,x)\otimes E(t,x))-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|E(t,x)|^{2}).

(2.6)

This formulation encapsulates the conservation laws and structural equations central to the analysis. The system not only describes the evolution of density and momentum, but also captures the influence of nonlinear interactions through the electric field term. It can be easily checked that

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(0,x)=\tilde{\rho}_{0,0}(x),\quad\mathbf{R}_{1}(\rho)(0,x)=\tilde{\rho}_{1,0}(x),\quad\mathbf{R}_{2}(\rho)(0,x)=\tilde{\rho}_{2,0}(x),

where

\tilde{\rho}_{j,0}(x)=\int v^{\otimes^{j}}f_{0}(x,v)dv.

2.2
Singular and Residual components of
\rho(t,x)

To analyze
\rho(t,x)
, we introduce a smooth cut-off function
\chi
such that
\chi=1
in
B_{1}(0)
and
\chi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{2}(0).

Set
\phi=\hat{\chi}
. We define

f_{<}=f\star\phi,~~~f_{>}=f-f\star\phi.

(2.7)

Using this decomposition, the density equation becomes

\rho(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)G_{>}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds+\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)G_{<}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds.

By integration by parts twice, and using (
2.6
) and the initial conditions

\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(0,x)=\tilde{\rho}_{0,0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(0,x)=-\operatorname{div}\tilde{\rho}_{1,0}(x),

we derive

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\cos(t)\rho_{sing}^{1}(t,x)+\sin(t)\rho_{sing}^{2}(t,x)+\rho_{re}(t,x),

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{sing}^{1}(t,x)=G_{<}(t)\star\tilde{\rho}_{0,0}(x),

\displaystyle\rho_{sing}^{2}(t,x)=-(\partial_{t}G_{<}(t)\star\tilde{\rho}_{0,0}(x)+G_{<}(t)\star\operatorname{div}\tilde{\rho}_{0,1}(x)),

\displaystyle\rho_{re}(t,x)=\mathbf{R}_{0,>}(\rho)(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)G_{>}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t-s)(y)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)\partial_{s}^{2}G_{<}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)\nabla\partial_{s}G_{<}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{1}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)\nabla^{\otimes^{2}}G_{<}(s)\star\mathbf{R}_{2}(\rho)(t-s)(x)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)\nabla^{2}G_{<}(s)\star(E\otimes E)(t-s)(x)ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sin(s)\Delta G_{<}(s)\star(|E|^{2})(t-s)(x)ds.

Using the Fourier transform properties of
G_{<}
and
G_{>}
, we can obtain the following decay estimates of the kernel

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{j_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{j_{2}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}G_{<}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x,t\rangle^{2+j_{1}+j_{2}}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{j_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{j_{2}}G_{<}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{\langle t\rangle\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}=0}}{\langle x,t\rangle^{4+j_{2}}}+\frac{\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}\geq 1}}{\langle x,t\rangle^{3+j_{1}+j_{2}}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{j_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{j_{2}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}G_{>}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{t\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}=0}}{(t+|x|)^{4+j_{2}}\langle x,t\rangle^{7}}+\frac{\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}\geq 1}}{(t+|x|)^{3+j_{1}+j_{2}}\langle x,t\rangle^{7}}.

(2.11)

These estimates will play a critical role in bounding
\rho_{re}(t,x)
and completing the bootstrap argument.

3
Norms and the bootstrap proposition.

We now introduce the key norms used in our analysis and state the bootstrap proposition, which plays a central role in establishing the global existence and stability of solutions.

The following norms are used to quantify the behavior of the density and the moments

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1,T}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\langle t,x\rangle^{3-\kappa_{0}}\nabla\Delta^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\langle t,x\rangle^{4-\kappa_{0}}\nabla^{2}\Delta^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\langle t,x\rangle^{4}\nabla^{3}\Delta^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{2,T}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\langle t,x\rangle^{4}\nabla g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\langle t,x\rangle^{3}g\|_{L^{\infty}},

where
\langle t,x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}+t^{2}}
and
\kappa_{0}>0
is a small parameter.

Under the initial data assumptions, the singular parts of the density
\rho_{sing}^{1},\rho_{sing}^{2}
satisfy the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1,2}|\partial_{t}^{n_{2}}\nabla^{1+n_{1}}\Delta^{-1}\rho_{sing}^{j}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{[f_{0}]}{\langle t,x\rangle^{3+n_{1}+n_{2}}},~~~\forall n_{1}+n_{2}\leq 4.

(3.1)

Using the decomposition (
2.8
) and the estimate (
4.1
), we can bound

\|\rho\|_{1,T}\lesssim[f_{0}]+\|(\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho),\mathbf{R}_{1}(\rho),\mathbf{R}_{2}(\rho))\|_{2,T}+\|\rho\|_{1,T}^{2}

(3.2)

for any
T>0
.

Now we state the main bootstrap proposition, which provides the control over
\|\rho\|_{1,T}
.

Proposition 3.1

There exists a
\epsilon>0
such that if

M:=\|\rho\|_{1,T}+[f_{0}]\leq\epsilon\ll 1

for some
T>0
, then

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{1,T}\lesssim[f_{0}]+\|\rho\|_{1,T}^{2}.

(3.3)

This proposition follows as a consequence of (
3.2
) and the proposition below.

Proposition 3.2

Under the same assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{R}_{0}(\rho),\mathbf{R}_{1}(\rho),\mathbf{R}_{2}(\rho))\|_{2,T}\lesssim[f_{0}]+\|\rho\|_{1,T}^{2}.

(3.4)

With Proposition
3.2
established, we can deduce the global existence of solutions to the perturbed system (
1.2
), with the density
\rho(t,x)
satisfying

\|\rho\|_{1,\infty}\lesssim[f_{0}].

This conclusion follows from a standard bootstrap argument (see
[
27
,
22
]
). Additionally, using the argument in Proposition 8.1 of
[
27
]
, we can establish the existence of a function
f_{\infty}\in C^{0,1}_{x,v}
such that

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{5}|f(t,x+tv,v)-f_{\infty}(x,v)|\lesssim[f_{0}]\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau\langle\tau,x+(\tau-t)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}d\tau.

(3.5)

for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

.

Estimates for

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{j}(\rho)(t,x)|\lesssim\langle x,t\rangle^{-3}\|\rho\|_{1,T}.

(3.16)

For

, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{R}_{j}(\rho)(t,x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int|(\nabla_{x}E)(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu(v)-(\nabla_{x}E)(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))\nabla_{v}\mu(V_{s,t}(x,v))|\langle v\rangle^{2}dvds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int|(\nabla_{x}E)(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))|\langle v\rangle^{-3}|\nabla Y_{s,t}(x-tv,v)|dvds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int|E(s,X_{s,t}(x,v))|\langle v\rangle^{-4}|\nabla W_{s,t}(x-tv,v)|dvds.

By the definition of

, we get by (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{R}_{j}(\rho)(t,x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle v\rangle^{-4}\langle x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}dvd\tau ds\|\rho\|_{1,T}M

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4}\langle x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\langle v\rangle^{-3}dvd\tau ds\|\rho\|_{1,T}M

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle v\rangle^{-3}dvd\tau ds\|\rho\|_{1,T}M

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\langle x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle v\rangle^{-4}dvd\tau ds\|\rho\|_{1,T}M.

Then we divide the integration of

(3.17)

For
t\geq 1

[
21
]
, we have

\displaystyle t\partial_{x_{i}}\mathcal{T}[E,\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)+\mathcal{T}[E,\partial_{i}\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{T}[E,\nabla\mu,\partial_{i}v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)+\mathcal{T}^{1,i}[E,\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)+\mathcal{T}^{2,i}[E,\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x),~~i=1,2,3,

where

and

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{T}^{2,i}[E,\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|}{\|\rho\|_{1,T}M}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\tau-s)\tau\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{3}}:=A_{4}(t,x),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\rho\|_{1,T}M}\left(|\mathcal{T}[E,\partial_{i}\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|+|\mathcal{T}[E,\nabla\mu,\partial_{i}v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{5}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{4}}:=A_{5}(t,x)+A_{6}(t,x),

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|}{\|\rho\|_{1,T}M}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}(\tau-s)s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{3}}.

The second term does not decay fast enough. To address the insufficient decay of this term, we apply the variable change strategy described in
[
22
]
. We rewrite
\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)
using the map
\Psi_{s,t}

which satisfies

X_{s,t}(x,\Psi_{s,t}(x,v))=x-(t-s)v.

Using this map, the term
\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)
can be bounded as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}s\int|(\nabla E)(s,x-(t-s)v)|\left|(\Psi_{s,t}(x,v))^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(V_{s,t}(x,\Psi_{s,t}))\det(\nabla_{v}\Psi_{s,t})-v^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(v)\right|dvds.

The difference term inside the integral can be decomposed and estimated as follows

\displaystyle\left|(\Psi_{s,t}(x,v))^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(V_{s,t}(x,\Psi_{s,t}))\det(\nabla_{v}\Psi_{s,t})-v^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(v)\right|\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-3}|\det(\nabla_{v}\Psi_{s,t})(x,v)-1|

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle v\rangle^{-4}\left(|\Psi_{s,t}(x,v)-v|+|W_{s,t}(x-t\Psi_{s,t},\Psi_{s,t}(x,v))|\right).

Then by (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\left|(\Psi_{s,t}(x,v))^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(V_{s,t}(x,\Psi_{s,t}))\det(\nabla_{v}\Psi_{s,t})-v^{\otimes^{j}}\nabla\mu(v)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{-3}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{(\tau-s)(t-\tau+1)}{t-s}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}d\tau M+\langle v\rangle^{-4}\int_{s}^{t}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}d\tau M.

Substituting the above estimates back, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{T}[\tilde{F}_{i},\nabla\mu,v^{\otimes^{j}}](t,x)|}{\|\rho\|_{1,T}M}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}s\int\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-3+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{s(\tau-s)(t-\tau+1)}{t-s}\int\langle s,x-(t-s)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\langle\tau,x-(t-\tau)v\rangle^{-4+\kappa_{0}}\frac{dvd\tau ds}{\langle v\rangle^{3}}

\displaystyle:=A_{7}(t,x)+A_{8}(t,x).

By (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we obtain

|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{R}_{j}(\rho)(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t,x\rangle^{-4}\|\rho\|_{1,T}M.

Combining the bounds for
\mathcal{R}_{j}(\rho)
and
\mathcal{I}_{j}(\rho)
, we conclude (
3.4

3.1

Lemma 4.1
(Basic Integral Estimates)

Let
\beta_{1}\geq\max(4,\beta_{2})\geq 3
and
n>0
. There holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{1}{\langle t-s,x-y\rangle^{\beta_{1}}}\frac{1}{\langle s,y\rangle^{\beta_{2}}}dyds\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{\beta_{1}>4}\frac{1}{\langle t,x\rangle^{\beta_{2}}}+\mathbf{1}_{\beta_{1}=4}\frac{\log(2+|t|+|x|)}{\langle t,x\rangle^{\beta_{2}}},

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\int\langle x-tv,v\rangle^{-n-3}dv\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle^{3}}\langle\frac{x}{\langle t\rangle}\rangle^{-n}.

(4.2)

Proof.
Let’s start with the double integral

\int_{0}^{t}\int\frac{1}{\langle t-s,x-y\rangle^{\beta_{1}}}\frac{1}{\langle s,y\rangle^{\beta_{2}}}dyds.

Decomposing the integration based on the relative size of
\langle t-s,x-y\rangle
and
\langle t,y\rangle
, we consider three cases:

\langle t,x\rangle>2\langle s,y\rangle,\quad\langle t,x\rangle<\langle s,y\rangle/2,\quad\langle t,x\rangle\sim\langle s,y\rangle.

Summing the contributions from these cases, we obtain (
4.1
).

For
t\in[0,1]
,
\langle x-tv,v\rangle^{-n-3}\sim\langle x,v\rangle^{-n-3}
. When
t\geq 1
, we can bound

\displaystyle\int\langle x-tv,v\rangle^{-n-3}dv=\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int\langle w,\frac{x-w}{t}\rangle^{-n-3}dw\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3}}\int\langle w,\frac{x}{t}\rangle^{-n-3}dw\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3}}\langle\frac{x}{t}\rangle^{-n}.

So, we obtain (
4.2
).

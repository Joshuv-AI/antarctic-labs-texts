The strong converse exponent of composable randomness extraction against quantum side information
Roberto Rubboli, Marco Tomamichel

Abstract
We find a tight characterization of the strong converse exponent for randomness extraction against quantum side information. In contrast to previous tight bounds, we employ a composable error criterion given by the fidelity (or purified distance) to a uniform distribution in product with the marginal state. The characterization is in terms of a club-sandwiched conditional entropy recently introduced by Rubboli, Goodarzi and Tomamichel and used by Li, Li and Yu to establish the strong converse exponent for the case of classical side information. This provides the first precise operational interpretation of this family of conditional entropies in the quantum setting.



I
Introduction

Randomness extraction is the task of extracting a uniformly distributed and secret key from a classical random variable that may be partially correlated with an adversary who possesses side information, possibly of a quantum nature. The objective is to produce a uniformly distributed random variable that is effectively uncorrelated with the eavesdropper’s side information.
This task plays a central role in quantum cryptography and quantum key distribution, where the raw key obtained from quantum measurements is generally noisy and only partially secret, due to information leakage to an eavesdropper during the protocol. Randomness extraction ensures that, after suitable post-processing, the final key is secure and can be safely used for cryptographic purposes.

Formally, let us consider a classical-quantum state

\rho_{XE}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\rho(x)\,\outerproduct{x}{x}_{X}\otimes\rho_{E}(x)
.
Randomness extraction is implemented by applying a function, known as hash function,

h:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}

to the register
X
. This induces the state

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE})=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\outerproduct{z}{z}_{Z}\otimes\sum_{x\in h^{-1}(z)}\rho(x)\,\rho_{E}(x),

(1)

where the register
Z
corresponds to the hashed output and is interpreted as the final key. Here, the notation
x\in h^{-1}(z)
indicates the set of all
x\in\mathcal{X}
that are mapped to
z\in\mathcal{Z}
by the hash function
h
.
The goal of privacy amplification is to ensure that the resulting key
Z
is close to being uniformly distributed and independent of the adversary’s system
E
. Ideally, the output state should approach the product state

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|\mathcal{Z}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\qquad\textrm{with}\qquad\rho_{E}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\rho(x)\rho_{E}(x)\,,

(2)

which represents a perfectly uniform key that is completely secure against the adversary.

To study the asymptotic behaviour of this task, we are provided
n
copies of a state
\rho_{XE}
and perform a hash function
h_{n}:\mathcal{X}^{\times n}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{n}
on the first register of the
n
copies. In this work, we adopt as a similarity measure the Uhlmann’s fidelity.
As established in
7
, there exist families of hash functions such that the fidelity with respect to the ideal state converges to one if (and only if) the size of the alphabet
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
grows sufficiently slowly with the number of copies. In particular, its exponential growth rate must not exceed the von Neumann conditional entropy, i.e.,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|<H(X|E)_{\rho}
.
Conversely, if

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|>H(X|E)_{\rho}\,,

(3)

privacy amplification is no longer possible (with any hash function), and the fidelity with respect to the ideal state decays exponentially to zero. This behavior follows, for example, from combining the one-shot converse bounds expressed in terms of the smooth conditional min-entropy
34
;
30
with the asymptotic equipartition property
33
.

The main result of this work is to establish the exact exponential rate at which Uhlmann’s fidelity to the ideal state decays to zero. This rate is referred to as the strong converse exponent with respect to the fidelity.
The strong converse exponent for randomness extraction at extraction rate
R
under fidelity is defined as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R):=\inf\left\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\Bigg|\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{|\Z_n|\geq R}\right\}

(4)

where the optimization is performed over all sequences of channels
\{\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}\}_{n}
that implement the hash functions
h_{n}:\mathcal{X}^{\times n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}
. Here,
F(\rho,\sigma)=(\text{Tr}[(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}])^{2}
is the Uhlmann’s fidelity.
Instead of infidelity, we can express the error in terms of purified distance, defined as
P(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}
. It is easily verified
1

This can be verified using the inequality
\frac{x}{2}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-x}\leq x
for
x\in[0,1]
, which implies that
\frac{F(\rho,\sigma)}{2}\leq 1-P(\rho,\sigma)\leq F(\rho,\sigma)
. Formally, we thus have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)=\inf\left\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(1-P\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)\right)\Bigg|\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{|\Z_n|\geq R}\right\}.

(5)

that
F
and
1-P
are equivalent at the level of exponential decay. We note that we consider the fidelity (or purified distance) to a state that is uniform on
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
and in product with the marginal state
\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}
. This yields a composable error criterion

Our main result is a characterization of this strong converse exponent.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical-quantum state and
R\geq 0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)=\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

(6)

The result is expressed in terms of a family of club-sandwiched conditional entropies. For
\alpha<1
and
\lambda<0
, they are defined as

\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(A|E)_{\rho}:=\min_{\sigma_{E}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\Tr[\left(\rho_{AE}^\frac{1}{2}\rho_E^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_E^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_E^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{AE}^\frac{1}{2}\right)^\alpha]}

(7)

Here, the infimum is taken over all density operators
\sigma_{E}
on system
E
. These entropies are part of a three-parameter family of entropies recently introduced in
26
by the present authors.
The values at
\alpha=1/2
and
\alpha=1
are defined via pointwise limits, and in particular

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}=H(X|E)_{\rho}\,,

(8)

that is, the quantity converges to the von Neumann conditional entropy (see Lemma
17
).

This result provides an operational interpretation for the club-sandwiched conditional entropy for a non-trivial range of parameters. In particular, it demonstrates that this quantity has a meaningful role beyond the traditional conditional Rényi entropies that are obtained in the special cases
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=1
, which correspond respectively to the arrow-down and arrow-up sandwiched conditional entropies,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A|E)_{\rho}=-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|E)_{\rho}=\sup_{\sigma_{E}}-\widetilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{AE}\|I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})\,.

(9)

Here,
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log{\Tr\big[\big(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\big)^{\alpha}\big]}
is the sandwiched Rényi divergence
21
;
36
.

Finally, we remark upon a conceptually important feature of our characterization of the strong converse exponent. While the randomness-extraction criterion entering the definition of the exponent in (
4
) depends only on the marginal state
\rho_{E}
, the resulting expression in Theorem
1
is formulated in terms of conditional entropies involving an optimization over auxiliary states
\sigma_{E}
.
The origin of this optimization becomes clear upon inspecting the proof of the converse bound in Lemma
5
. There, obtaining a tight exponent necessitates a careful application of Hölder’s inequality, which naturally introduces an auxiliary state

\sigma_{E}
that must be optimized over. This auxiliary state can be interpreted as a “tilting” of the original marginal
\rho_{E}
, allowing one to derive the tightest characterization of the exponent. We view this tilting mechanism as a central technical contribution of the present work. Beyond enabling a tight characterization of the strong converse exponent, it provides a flexible analytic tool that may prove useful in the study of strong converse phenomena and related exponent problems in a variety of information-theoretic settings.

I.1
Prior works

The task of randomness extraction (or privacy amplification) has been widely studied. Several alternative definitions of the error criterion have appeared in the literature, leading to different strong converse exponents. The criterion adopted in the present work is the fidelity with the uniform state tensored with the marginal of the side information, i.e.,

\displaystyle F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|I_{Z}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)\,.

(11)

This problem has been investigated in a series of papers
18
;
4
;
28
;
15
.
In
18
;
4
, the authors establish the result of the present work for the case of classical side information. In addition, in
18
, the authors derive a more general statement for classical Rényi divergences as a measure of distinguishability, which includes the fidelity as the special case
\alpha=1/2
. In
(
4
, Theorem 8)
, the result is presented with an additional optimization over probabilities. However, the optimization admits a closed form.
3
3

3

Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})}\Bigg\{2D(t\|p)+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big(r-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim t}H_{\alpha}(p(\cdot|y))\Big)\Bigg\}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}r+\inf_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})}\Big\{2D(t\|q)-2\log Z\Big\}\,,

(12)

where we defined normalizing constant
Z
and the tilted distribution
q
as

Z:=\sum_{x}p(y)\exp\!\Big(\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}H_{\alpha}(\cdot|y)\Big),\qquad q(y):=\frac{p(y)\exp\!\big(\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}H_{\alpha}(\cdot|y)\big)}{Z}.

(13)

The optimization is minimized at
t^{\star}=q
. Hence, we obtain the closed form

\sup_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\inf_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Y})}\Bigg\{2D(t\|p)+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\Big(r-E_{y\sim t}H_{\alpha}(p(\cdot|y))\Big)\Bigg\}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\,r-2\log\sum_{y}p(y)\left(\sum_{x}p(x|y)^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}.

(14)

Hence, the result reduces to the one of
18

In the case of quantum side information, a lower bound for the strong converse with respect to the purified distance was derived in
(
28
, Theorem 3)
, given by

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}(1-\alpha)\left(R-\overline{\mkern-1.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

(15)

Our main result in Theorem
1
provides a tighter lower bound together with a matching upper bound.

A closely related criterion was studied in
17
;
14
, where the authors consider the maximum fidelity with the ideal uniform state tensored with an arbitrary side-information state

\displaystyle\max_{\sigma_{E}}F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|I_{Z}|}\otimes\sigma_{E}\right)

(16)

In particular, in
17
, the authors derive the following form for the strong converse exponent

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

(17)

which includes the sandwiched conditional entropy arrow up.

Under the above criteria, the optimal deterministic function can in general depend on the underlying state
\rho_{XE}
. To overcome this issue, privacy amplification is often implemented using a randomly chosen hash function, specified by a public seed.
The purpose of introducing such a seed is to render the protocol universal, i.e., independent of the particular state
\rho_{XE}
from which secure randomness is to be extracted.
In this case, the legitimate parties sample a seed
H
according to a distribution
p
over a family of hash functions
\{\mathcal{R}_{h}:h\in H\}
, and apply the corresponding map
\mathcal{R}_{h}
. Notably, the parties reveal
H
publicly, and hence the eavesdropper has access to the seed as well.

To formalize this, let
\rho_{XE}
be the classical–quantum state describing the raw key
X
and the adversary’s quantum side information
E
. For each
h\in H
, let
\mathcal{R}_{h}
denote the privacy-amplification map producing the key register
Z
from
X
. If the seed is sampled as
H\sim p
, then the joint state after privacy amplification, including the public seed register, is

\displaystyle\rho_{ZEH}\;:=\;\sum_{h\in H}p(h)\,\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE})\otimes|h\rangle\!\langle h|_{H}.

(18)

The corresponding ideal state is

\displaystyle\omega_{ZEH}\;:=\;\frac{I_{Z}}{|Z|}\otimes\rho_{E}\otimes\rho_{H},\qquad\rho_{H}:=\sum_{h\in H}p(h)\,|h\rangle\!\langle h|_{H},

(19)

which represents a uniform key independent of both
E
and the public seed
H
.
The security criterion is then naturally expressed as

\displaystyle F(\rho_{ZEH},\omega_{ZEH})\,.

(20)

Since the seed register
H
is classical, the square-root fidelity is exactly equivalent to the average square-root fidelity over the seed

\displaystyle F(\rho_{ZEH},\omega_{ZEH})^{\frac{1}{2}}\;=\;\sum_{h\in H}p(h)\,F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE}),\frac{I_{Z}}{|Z|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;=\;\,\mathbb{E}_{h\sim p}F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE}),\frac{I_{Z}}{|Z|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(21)

Therefore, in this formulation of privacy amplification, the aim is to maximize the quantity

\displaystyle E_{h\sim p}F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|I_{Z}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(22)

Here, the distribution
p
may also be optimized to yield the best value of the fidelity, and it is typically chosen at random. This case was studied in
10
, where explicit expressions were derived for several error exponents, including the strong converse exponent in the classical setting for rates above the critical rate.
4
4

4

Note that Theorem 1 in
10
contains a gap for rates below the critical rate, as discussed in
9

In addition, they also derived expressions for other Rényi relative entropies as meatric of distinguishability.

In the literature, the trace distance is also frequently used as a measure of distinguishability

;
8

25

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big\|\rho_{ZEH}-\omega_{ZEH}\big\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{h\sim p}\!\left\|\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XE})-\frac{I_{Z}}{|Z|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1}\,.

(23)

The trace distance is often chosen because, by the Helstrom–Holevo theorem, it admits an operational interpretation as the optimal success probability in binary state discrimination.

A lower bound for the strong converse for deterministic hash functions with respect to the trace distance was derived in
(

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\left(R-\overline{\mkern-1.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

where
\overline{\mkern-1.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}(X|E)_{\rho}=-\overline{\mkern-1.0muD\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}(\rho_{XE}\|I_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})

III
Critical rate

In this section, we show that the expression for the strong converse exponent of privacy amplification given in Theorem
1
simplifies when the extraction rate exceeds a critical value and becomes linear in the extraction rate
R
. Specifically, for rates above a critical rate
R_{c}
, the maximization over the parameter
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
attains its maximum at the boundary point
\alpha=1/2
. In this case, the exponent reduces to

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)=R-\widetilde{H}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\downarrow}(A|E)\,,

(39)

and thus depends only on the sandwiched conditional entropy
\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{1/2}
, a quantity that has been extensively studied in the literature.
The above expression coincides with the result of
(
10
, Theorem 1)
concerning the strong converse in the classical setting and in the presence of an additional seed for rates above the critical rate.

To show that, for rates
R
larger than a critical value
R_{c}
, the maximization over
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
in Theorem
1

\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}
, we exploit a standard property of concave functions. In particular, if a concave function is maximized over an interval
[a,b]
and its left derivative at the endpoint
b
is nonnegative, then the maximum is attained at
b
. It is useful to perform the change of variable
t=(1-\alpha)/\alpha
. For
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
, it holds that
t\in[0,1]
. Moreover, we define the function

\displaystyle G(t)=tR-t\widetilde{H}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}_{\frac{1}{1+t}}(X|E)_{\rho}\,.

(40)

The expression for the strong converse exponent in Theorem
1
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)=\max_{t\in[0,1]}G(t)\,.

(41)

Next, we show that

is a concave function. To this purpose, we need to show that
t\widetilde{H}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}_{\frac{1}{1+t}}(A|E)_{\rho}
is a convex function of

.

Lemma 2
.

Let
t\in[0,1]
and
\rho_{XE}
be a quantum state. Then, the function
t\rightarrow t\widetilde{H}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}_{\frac{1}{1+t}}(X|E)_{\rho}

Proof.

We use the asymptotic characterization provided in
(

\displaystyle t\widetilde{H}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}_{\frac{1}{1+t}}(X|E)_{\rho}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2}{n}\log{\Big\|\rho_{A^n E^n}^\frac{1}{2}\rho_{E^n}^\frac{1}{2}\omega_{E^n}^{\frac{1}{2}(t-1)}}\Big\|_{\frac{2}{t+1}}\,.

(42)

Here,
\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}=\rho_{AE}^{\otimes n}
and
\rho_{E^{n}}=\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}
and
\omega_{E^{n}}
is the universal state described in Lemma
26
.
Next, we invoke the complex interpolation result of Lemma
25
. For any
\theta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle p_{\theta}=\frac{2}{1+\theta t_{1}+(1-\theta)t_{2}}\,,\quad p_{0}=\frac{2}{1+t_{1}}\,,\quad p_{1}=\frac{2}{1+t_{2}}

(43)

and function

\displaystyle F(z)=\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}((1-z)t_{1}+zt_{2}-1)}

(44)

We note that it holds the required relationship

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\,.

(45)

In addition, since

and hence the assumptions of Lemma

We obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}(\theta t_{1}+(1-\theta)t_{2}-1)}\Big\|_{\frac{2}{1+\theta t_{1}+(1-\theta)t_{2}}}\leq\Big\|\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{1}-1)}\Big\|_{\frac{2}{1+t_{1}}}^{1-\theta}\Big\|\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{E^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}(t_{2}-1)}\Big\|_{\frac{2}{1+t_{2}}}^{\theta}

(46)

Here, we used that we could omit the additional phases that involve powers of the form

Applying the logarithm, dividing by

∎

The above result implies the function

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}G(t)\Big|_{t=1^{-}}\geq 0\quad\implies\quad R\geq R_{c}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(t\widetilde{H}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}_{\frac{1}{1+t}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\Bigg|_{t=1^{-}}

(47)

IV
Converse bound

The proof of the main result is divided into two parts.
First, we establish an upper bound on the strong converse exponent, commonly referred to as the converse bound.
In the subsequent section, we derive a matching lower bound, corresponding to the achievability part.

IV.1
Coarse-graining inequality

The first part is to prove that a deterministic function cannot increase the conditional entropy. Applying such a function can be thought of as coarse-graining, as it maps different inputs to the same output. Intuitively, we expect that the uncertainty about the output of a function is at most as large as the uncertainty about the input of a function since an observer can generate an estimate of the former from an estimate of the latter. This intuition is captured by the following lemma, which we are, however, only able to show for conditional entropies based on sandwiched divergence
(z=\alpha)
. This generalizes
(
35
, Lemma 5.14)
, where this property was shown for up-arrow and down-arrow sandwiched Rényi divergences.

Lemma 3

(Coarse-graining reduces the sandwiched conditional entropy)
.

For any
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and any function
h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
and any classical-quantum state
\rho_{XBE}
it holds that

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(ZB|E)_{\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho)}\leq\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(XB|E)_{\rho}

(48)

Proof.

Consider a state of the form
\rho_{XBE}=\sum_{x}\rho(x)\outerproduct{x}{x}_{X}\otimes\rho_{BE}(x)
where
\rho(x)
are probabilities and
\rho_{BE}(x)
states.
Let us denote the preimage of
z
under
h
as

\displaystyle h^{-1}(z)=\{\,x\in\mathcal{X}:h(x)=z\,\}\,.

(49)

The output of the hash function is

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XBE})=\sum_{z}\outerproduct{z}{z}_{Z}\otimes\sum_{x\in h^{-1}(z)}\rho(x)\hat{\rho}_{BE}(x)

(50)

Note that since the Hashing acts on the first register only

\displaystyle\Tr_{Z}(\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho_{XBE}))=\rho_{BE}

(51)

We have

\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(ZB|E)_{\mathcal{R}_{h}(\rho)}=\sup_{\sigma_{E}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\sum_z\Tr[\left(\sigma_E^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sum_{x \in h^{-1}(z)}\rho(x)\hat{\rho}_{BE}(x)\rho_E^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_E^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right)^\alpha]}

(52)

The Rotfel’d’s trace inequality implies that for a concave function
f
, it holds
\Tr(f(\sum_i A_i))\leq\sum_{i}\Tr(f(A_i))
. Here the function is the
\alpha
-power with
\alpha<1
. Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{z}\Tr[\left(\sigma_{E}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sum_{x\in h^{-1}(z)}\rho(x)\hat{\rho}_{BE}(x)\rho_{E}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_{E}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}]

(53)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{x}\Tr[\left(\sigma_{E}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho(x)\hat{\rho}_{BE}(x)\rho_{E}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_{E}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}],

(54)

where the latter is the term inside the logarithm of
\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(XB|E)_{\rho}
. If
\alpha>1
we instead take advantage of the concavity of the negative of the power function, and not that the prefactor changes sign. For
\alpha\in\{1,\infty\}
we can use a limiting argument.
∎

A direct application of coarse-graining for states with two classical registers is the fact that

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(XZ|E)_{\rho}\geq\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(Z|E)_{\rho}\,,

(55)

i.e., that discarding classical information cannot increase conditional entropy. In fact, since an application of a function can be decomposed into an invertible (isometric) mapping that computes the output while keeping the input, the above inequality also implies the coarse-graining inequality since the Rényi entropies are invariant under local isometries.

Remark 4
.

Two commonly used definitions of conditional entropy are based on Petz-Rényi relative entropy, namely
\overline{\mkern-1.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(X|E)_{\rho}
and
\overline{\mkern-1.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(X|E)_{\rho}
. Numerical evidence suggest that they do not always satisfy the coarse-graining inequality, not even in the special case of discarding classical information as in (
55
).

IV.2
A one-shot converse bound

We first derive a one-shot bound as an application of Hölder’s inequality. The use of Hölder inequalities in converse theorems can be traced back to
14
. Compared to the converse bound in
17
, we note that in our error criterion, we do not need to optimize over the marginal on
E
, which yields a converse bound that requires a more refined application of the Hölder inequality.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a quantum state. For any
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\left(F\left(\rho_{XE},\frac{I_{\mathcal{X}}}{|\mathcal{X}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)\right)\geq\log{|\mathcal{X}|}-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\,.

(56)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
. We define
\beta
to be the Hölder conjugate of
\alpha
, i.e., the unique parameter satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{1}{2\beta}=1.

(57)

Equivalently,
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}
and hence
\beta\in(1,\infty]
. Hölder’s inequality implies that for linear operators
X,Y
, it holds that
\|XY\|_{1}\leq\|X\|_{2\alpha}\|Y\|_{2\beta}
(see e.g.,
(
5
, Exercise IV.2.7)
).
Let
\sigma_{E}
be a state such that
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log F\left(\rho_{XE},\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\|\rho_{XE}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{1}

(59)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\|\rho_{XE}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{1}

(60)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\|\rho_{XE}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{2\alpha}\|(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{2\beta}

(61)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\|\rho_{XE}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{2\alpha}

(62)

In the second equality, all negative powers are understood as generalized inverses,
that is, they are taken only on the support of the corresponding operators.
Ey Lemma
21
, the assumption
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
implies that
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
.
Hence, the projector

\displaystyle(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}

(63)

act as the identity on the support of the operator inside the norm, and therefore
do not affect its value.
In the last line we used that

\|(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{-\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{2\beta}=(\Tr(\pi_X \otimes\sigma_E))^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}=1

(64)

Since the inequality holds for every
\sigma_{E}
satisfying the required support
condition, and since in the definition of

\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}

the optimization may be restricted to such states—as the objective function
is equal to
+\infty
otherwise—we may take the infimum over all admissible

\sigma_{E}
and use that

\inf_{\sigma_{E}}\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\|\rho_{XE}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\rho_{E})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{X}\otimes\sigma_{E})^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2\alpha}}\|_{2\alpha}=\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}-\log{|\mathcal{X}|}\,.

(65)

∎

IV.3
Proof of converse

The above one-shot lower bound, combined with the coarse-graining inequality, implies the following converse bound for privacy amplification with quantum side information.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical-quantum state and
R\geq 0
. It holds that

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)\geq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

(66)

Proof.

Let
\{h_{n}\penalty\ :\mathcal{X}^{\times n}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence of hash functions such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|\geq R\,.

(67)

For
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
, Lemma
5
implies that

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\log{|\mathcal{Z}_n|}-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(Z_{n}|E^{n})_{\mathcal{R}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}

(68)

\displaystyle\geq\log{|\mathcal{Z}_n|}-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}

(69)

\displaystyle=\log{|\mathcal{Z}_n|}-n\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}

(70)

where the inequality follows from the fact that hashing does not increase the club-sandwiched conditional entropy (see Lemma
3
), and the final equality follows from the additivity of the club-sandwiched conditional entropy
(
26
, Theorem 4.1)
.
We then divide by
1/n
and take the
\limsup
for
n\to\infty
, note that
\limsup\geq\liminf
, and apply equation (
67
).
Finally, we take the supremum over
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
. Since the objective function is continuous on
[1/2,1]
, this supremum is attained and can be replaced by a maximum.
∎

V
Achievability

The proof of achievability follows standard techniques, first introduced by Mosonyi and Ogawa
20
and subsequently applied to the study of strong converse exponent for a variety of problems
16
;
17
;
3
;
4
.

The approach is based on the Log–Euclidean formalism combined with a double–blocking argument.
More precisely, one first derives an achievability bound for the exponent expressed in terms of Log–Euclidean quantities, which coincide with the sandwiched ones whenever all operators commute. This bound has the same form as the final error exponent, with the sandwiched quantities replaced by their Log–Euclidean counterparts. The reason is that Log–Euclidean quantities are particularly useful as they admit variational representations in terms of expressions involving the Umegaki relative entropy, thereby allowing one to invoke known achievability results for the latter.
The final step consists of a double–blocking argument, which allows one to replace the Log–Euclidean quantities with the sandwiched ones. One first performs a pinching over blocks of copies, which can be shown not to affect the error exponent and can therefore be inserted without loss. One then lets the block size tend to infinity. Since the pinching procedure introduces only logarithmic correction terms, these vanish in the asymptotic limit. Moreover, this allows one to replace the Log–Euclidean quantities by the corresponding sandwiched ones, using the fact that the sandwiched quantities arise as the infinite–block limit of the pinched Log–Euclidean expressions.

While our proof closely resembles the aforementioned arguments, our main technical contribution goes beyond a direct adaptation of the existing proofs. In particular, the expressions for the club-sandwiched conditional entropies involves three non-commuting operators, namely
\rho_{AE}
,
\rho_{E}
, and the state
\sigma_{E}
, rather than just two as for previously studied settings. As a consequence, it is not a priori clear how to implement a pinching argument, since one cannot simply pinch one state with respect to another.
Our key contribution is to introduce a double pinching procedure, consisting of the composition of a pinching with respect to the marginal state
\rho_{E}
and a pinching with respect to the universal state, which can be asymptotically inserted and used to replace the optimization over
\sigma_{E}
. In this way, all three operators are rendered mutually commuting, and the sandwiched conditional entropies emerge as the asymptotic limit of the corresponding Log–Euclidean quantities.

V.1
Variational expression for the LE conditional entropy

In this subsection, we provide a variational expression for the LE conditional entropy in terms of the Umegaki relative entropy.
This result is obtained by an argument similar to the one used to express the LE relative entropy in terms of the Umegaki relative entropy
20
.

Lemma 7

(Variational expression LE conditional entropy)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\leq 0
and
\rho_{AE}
be a state. Then, we have

(\alpha-1)H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(A|E)_{\rho}=\min_{\tau_{AE}}\{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+\alpha D(\tau_{AE}\|\rho_{AE})+(\alpha-1)H(A|E)_{\tau_{AE}}\}\,,

(71)

where we minimize over all states
\tau_{AE}
.

Proof.

The case
\lambda=0
is simpler and follows directly from the variational characterization of the Log–Euclidean relative entropy (see, e.g.,
(
20
, Theorem III.6)
).

Let us now turn to the case
\lambda<0
. We use the (constrained) Gibbs variational principle in Lemma
24
with Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=-\alpha\log{\rho_{AE}-(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)R_{AE}\log{\rho_E}R_{AE}-\lambda(1-\alpha)R_{AE}\log{\sigma_E}R_{AE}}\,.

(72)

We obtain

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(A|E)

(73)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{E}}-\log{\Tr[R_{AE}\exp(\alpha\log{\rho_{AE}+(1-\alpha)R_{AE}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\log{\sigma_E}\big)R_{AE}})]}

(74)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{E}}\inf_{\tau_{AE}}\Big\{\Tr[\tau_{AE}\log{\tau_{AE}}]

(75)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\Tr[\tau_{AE}(\alpha\log{\rho_{AE}+(1-\alpha)R_{AE}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\log{\sigma_E}\big)R_{AE}})]\Big\}\,,

(76)

where the minimization is taken over all states
\tau_{AE}
whose support is contained
in that of
\rho_{AE}
.
We then observe that, in the definition of the LE conditional entropy,
the optimization over all states
I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E}
may be restricted to those whose support contains the support of
\rho_{AE}
.
Indeed, any state that violates this condition yields an objective value equal to

+\infty
and therefore cannot be optimal. Hence, the optimizations can be restricted to the sets

\displaystyle\{\tau_{AE}:\supp(\tau_{AE})\subseteq\supp(\rho_{AE})\}\,\quad\{\sigma_{E}:\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})\}

(77)

Both of these sets are compact, and the objective function is finite and continuous on both
\sigma_{E}
and
\tau_{AE}
.
The above objective function is convex in
\tau_{AE}
since the von Neumann entropy is concave in its argument and the remaining term is linear in
\tau_{AE}
. Moreover, the objective function is concave in
\sigma_{E}
since the logarithm is operator concave and the trace is linear. We can then use
Sion’s minimax theorem
31
to exchange the supremum with the infimum.
Next, we use the known fact that

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma_{E}}\Tr[\tau_{AE}\log{\sigma_E}]=\Tr[\tau_{AE}\log{\tau_E}]\,.

(78)

Here, the supremum is taken over all states. Nonetheless, the supremum could be restricted to the above compact set since
\supp(\tau_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\tau_{E})
.
We obtain

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(A|E)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{AE}}\Big\{\Tr[\tau_{AE}\log{\tau_{AE}}]

(79)

\displaystyle\quad-\Tr[\tau_{AE}(\alpha\log{\rho_{AE}+(1-\alpha)R_{AE}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\log{\tau_E}\big)R_{AE}})]\Big\}

(80)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{AE}}\{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+\alpha D(\tau_{AE}\|\rho_{AE})+(\alpha-1)H(A|E)_{\tau_{AE}}\}\,,

(81)

In the second equality, we used that we can drop the projector
R_{AE}
in the objective function, as the optimization is over states
\tau_{AE}
satisfying

R_{AE}\tau_{AE}R_{AE}=\tau_{AE}
. The equality then follows from a straightforward calculation.

From this variational expression, it is clear that the objective function is continuous in

\tau_{AE}
on the set of states satisfying the required support condition. Consequently, the infimum may be replaced by a minimum, since a continuous function on a compact set always attains its minimum.

Finally, the support restriction on the states
\tau_{AE}

may be removed from the infimum.
Indeed, any state
\tau_{AE}
that violates this condition yields an infinite
value of the relative entropy
D(\tau_{AE}\|\rho_{AE})
and therefore cannot
attain the infimum.

∎

V.2
A LE conditional entropy bound

In this subsection, we combine the previous variational formula with a standard achievability argument to derive an upper bound to the strong converse exponent in terms of the LE conditional entropy. This method is rather standard in the literature
18
;
17
. In particular, the following proof is a straightforward adaptation of the proof provided in
18
for the classical case.

We begin by deriving a variational expression for the final form of the strong converse exponent that we aim to prove, in which the club-sandwiched conditional entropy is replaced by the LE conditional entropy. We define

\displaystyle G(\rho_{XE},R)=\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)\,.

(82)

The following lemma exploits the variational characterization of the LE conditional entropy established in Lemma
7
to derive a variational expression for the above quantity in terms of the Umegaki relative entropy.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical quantum state and
R\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle G(\rho_{XE},R)=\min_{\tau_{XE}}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+|R-H(X|E)_{\tau}|^{+})\right\}\,.

(83)

Proof.

We make use of the variational characterization established in Lemma
7
, specialized to the choice
\lambda=(1-2\alpha)/(1-\alpha)
.
We have

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)

(84)

\displaystyle\qquad=\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\min_{\tau_{XE}}\left\{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}R+D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}H(X|E)_{\tau}\right\}

(85)

\displaystyle\qquad=\max_{\mu\in[0,1]}\min_{\tau_{XE}}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+\mu(R-H(X|E)_{\tau})\right\}

(86)

\displaystyle\qquad=\min_{\tau_{XE}}\max_{\mu\in[0,1]}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+\mu(R-H(X|E)_{\tau})\right\}

(87)

\displaystyle\qquad=\min_{\tau_{XE}}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+|R-H(X|E)_{\tau}|^{+})\right\}

(88)

In the second equality, we used that for a classical-quantum state
\rho_{XE}
, the optimization over all quantum states can be restricted to classical-quantum states
\tau_{XE}
.
Indeed, by the data-processing inequality for the Umegaki relative entropy, pinching a candidate state with respect to the eigenbasis of the classical register
X
does not increase the value of the objective function.
Consequently, such a pinched state yields an equal or smaller value and is therefore sufficient to achieve the optimum in the variational expression. In the third equality, we set
\mu=(1-\alpha)/\alpha
and used the Sion’s minimax theorem
31
to exchange the maximum over
\mu
and minimum over
\tau_{XE}
. Indeed, the objective function is linear (and hence concave) and continuous in
\mu
, and convex in
\tau_{XE}
. The latter follows from the joint convexity of the Umegaki relative entropy together with the concavity of the von Neumann conditional entropy. Moreover, the optimization over
\tau_{XE}
can be equivalently restricted to the compact set of states whose support is contained in the support of
\rho_{XE}
. On this restricted domain, the objective function is finite and continuous in
\tau_{XE}
. The final equality follows from an explicit optimization over
\mu
.
∎

Next, we write
G(\rho_{XE},R)
as the following minimization

\displaystyle G(\rho_{XE},R)=\min\left\{G_{1}(\rho_{XE},R),G_{2}(\rho_{XE},R)\right\}\,.

(89)

Here,

\displaystyle G_{1}(\rho_{XE},R)=\inf_{\tau_{XE}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})\right\}

(90)

\displaystyle G_{2}(\rho_{XE},R)=\inf_{\tau_{XE}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}\left\{D(\tau_{E}\|\rho_{E})+D(\tau_{XE}\|\rho_{XE})+R-H(X|E)_{\tau})\right\}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{\tau_{XE}:R<H(X|E)_{\tau}\}

(92)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{\tau_{XE}:R\geq H(X|E)_{\tau}\}

(93)

We then show that the strong converse exponent is upper bounded by each of these two functions, and hence also by their minimum.
We start with the first case.

V.3
A club-sandwiched conditional entropy bound

The final step is to derive an analogous upper bound as above in terms of the club-sandwiched conditional entropy. The proof is similar to that of
17
and leverages a pinching argument.

The first step is to show that the strong converse exponent is essentially unaffected by pinching the state with respect to an operator whose spectral cardinality grows only polynomially with the number of copies. This property follows from the fact that the fidelity belongs to the family of sandwiched relative entropies. For this class of quantities, it is known that an additional pinching operation can be asymptotically inserted without affecting their value; see, for instance,
(
35
, Proposition 4.12)
. Since we will later require the pinched
\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m}
to commute with both
\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
and
\omega_{E^{m}}
, we consider the composition of the corresponding pinching maps
\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^{m}}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical-quantum state and
R\geq 0
. Moreover, let
\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}=\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^{m}}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})
. For any
m\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\frac{1}{m}H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\widetilde{\rho}}\right)+\frac{2}{m}(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(118)

Proof.

We fix
m\geq 1
. The proof of Corollary
11
applied to this state implies that there exists a sequence of hash functions
\{h_{m,k}:\mathcal{X}^{\times mk}\rightarrow Z_{m,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log{|\Z_{m,k}|}\geq mR

(119)

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{k}\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}^{\otimes k}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\right)\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\{mR-H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\widetilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}}\}

(120)

Here, in the second argument of the fidelity, we used that the marginal on
E^{m}
is invariant under the applied pinching. Indeed, since the hash function acts only on the first marginal, and since the universal state
\omega_{E^{m}}
commutes with
\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
, Lemma
23
implies that

\displaystyle\Tr_{X^{mk}}[\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}^{\otimes k})]=\big(\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E}^{\otimes m}}(\rho_{E}^{\otimes m})\big)^{\otimes k}=\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\,.

(121)

Next, for each such
n
, we construct a hashing map
\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}
from
\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}
as follows.
We first determine the integer
k
such that
n\in[km,(k+1)m)
, and choose

\mathcal{Z}_{n}=\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}
. Here, note that, for all
n\in[km,(k+1)m)
, the value of
k
remains fixed.
We then define
h_{n}
by

\displaystyle h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{mk},x_{mk+1},\ldots,x_{n}):=h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{mk}).

(122)

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{R}}_{h_{n}}\bigl(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}\bigr)=\mathcal{\mathcal{R}}_{h_{m,k}}\bigl(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes mk}\bigr)\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes(n-mk)}.

(123)

Moreover, since the choice of
k
depends on
n
, and we can write
n=mk+l
, with
0\leq l\leq m-1
, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{|\Z_{n}|}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{mk(n)+l(n)}\log{|\Z_{m,k(n)}|}

(124)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{mk(n)+m-1}\log{|\Z_{m,k(n)}|}

(125)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{mk}{mk+m-1}\frac{1}{mk}\log{|\Z_{m,k}|}

(126)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log{|\Z_{m,k}|}

(127)

\displaystyle\geq R\,.

(128)

Here, in order to rewrite the limit in
n
as a limit in
k
, we used that repeating each term a fixed number
m
of times leaves the
\liminf
unchanged. In addition, in the last equality, we used that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{mk}{mk+m-1}=1
.
Finally, the last inequality follows from Eq. (
119
). Hence, the hash functions
\mathcal{R}_{n}
achieve extraction rate
R
, and therefore can be used to upper bound the strong converse exponent.

We then insert the pinching operators. We apply twice the inequality in Lemma
27
together with

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathrm{spec}(\rho_{E}^{\otimes m})|,|\mathrm{spec}(\omega_{E^{m}})|\}\leq(m+1)^{d_{E}-1}\,.

(129)

To apply this lemma, we must check that
\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
is block-diagonal with respect to the joint eigenspaces of
\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
and
\omega_{E^{m}}
. This follows from the fact that
\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
commutes with
\omega_{E^{m}}
and is therefore invariant under pinching with respect to either operator, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\!\left(\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}\right)=\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^{m}}}\!\left(\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}\right)=\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}.

(130)

We then obtain

\displaystyle-\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}^{\otimes k}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\right)

(131)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\log F\left(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes k}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes k}_{\omega_{E}^{m}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes mk}))),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\right)

(132)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq-\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes k}_{\omega_{E}^{m}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes mk})),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\right)-k(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(133)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq-\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes mk})\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes(n-mk)},\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)-2k(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(134)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)-2k(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(135)

Using this inequality, the fact that
n\geq mk
together with the positivity of the negative logarithm of the fidelity between states, and inequality (
120
), we can upper bound the strong converse exponent as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{n}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}\right)

(136)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{mk}\log F\left(\mathcal{R}_{h_{m,k}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}^{\otimes k}),\frac{I_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{m,k}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes mk}\right)+2\frac{1}{m}(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(137)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\frac{1}{m}H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\widetilde{\rho}}\right)+2\frac{1}{m}(d_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(138)

∎

In the next step, we leverage the pinching structure in the above expression to establish that,
for large
m
, the LE conditional entropy approaches the
corresponding club-sandwiched conditional entropy.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical-quantum state and
R\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)

(139)

Proof.

We have that for any permutation invariant state
\tau_{E^{m}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\tau_{E^{m}}\leq\omega_{E^{m}}(m+1)^{d^{2}_{E}-1}\,.

(140)

We denote with
R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
the projector onto the support of
\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}=\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^{m}}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})
. We know by Lemma
20
that we can choose the optimizer
\tau_{E^{m}}
of the LE conditional entropy of
\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}
to be permutation invariant. By Lemma
23
, we also have that
\Tr_{X^{m}}[\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}}]=\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}
. This implies that

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\tilde{\rho}}

\displaystyle\;=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}\exp\Big(\alpha\log\tilde{\rho}_{X^mE^m} + (1-\alpha)R_{X^m E^m}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}+\lambda\log{\tau_{E^m}}\big)R_{X^m E^m}\Big)\Big]

(141)

\displaystyle\;\geq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}\exp\Big(\alpha\log\tilde{\rho}_{X^mE^m}
+(1-\alpha)R_{X^m E^m}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}+\lambda\log{\omega_{E^m}}\big)R_{X^m E^m}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(142)

The inequality follows from the operator monotonicity of the logarithm, together with the fact that the trace functional
M\mapsto\Tr[f(M)]
inherits the monotonicity of the function
f
(see, e.g.,
6
). In the present setting, this applies to the exponential function. Next, we invoke Lemma
22
, which implies that pinching with respect to

I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E}
factorizes as the identity on
A
tensored with pinching on
E
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E}}=I_{A}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}}
.
As a result, all operators under consideration commute pairwise, i.e.,

\displaystyle[\rho_{E}^{\otimes m},\omega_{E^{m}}]=[\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}},I_{X^{m}}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}]=[\tilde{\rho}_{X^{m}E^{m}},I_{X^{m}}\otimes\omega_{E^{m}}]=0.

(143)

Here, the first commutation relation follows from the defining properties of the universal state.
The remaining commutation relations follow from the fact that the pinching maps can be interchanged,
since their spectral projectors commute as the underlying operators commute.
Hence, since all operators commute, we can rewrite the LE conditional entropy in terms of the club-sandwiched conditional entropy. We have that

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\tilde{\rho}}

\displaystyle\;\geq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\Tr(\omega_{E^m}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}^{\otimes m \frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}} \tilde{\rho}_{X^mE^m} \rho_{E}^{\otimes m\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\omega_{E^m}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}})^\alpha}

(144)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(145)

\displaystyle\;\geq\inf_{\sigma_{E^{m}}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\Tr(\sigma_{E^m}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}^{\otimes m \frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}} \tilde{\rho}_{X^mE^m} \rho_{E}^{\otimes m\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_{E^m}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}})^\alpha}+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(146)

\displaystyle\;=\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\tilde{\rho}}+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(147)

\displaystyle\;\geq\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\rho^{\otimes m}}+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(148)

\displaystyle\;=m\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(X|E)_{\rho}+\lambda(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(149)

The third inequality is a consequence of the monotonicity of the conditional entropy under maps acting on the side information. The last equality is due to additivity.

Inserting this inequality in the bound for the error exponent in Lemma
12
gives

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{pa}}(\rho_{XE},R)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{m}\left(\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha}+2\right)(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(150)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}(X|E)_{\rho}\right)+\frac{3}{m}(d^{2}_{E}-1)\log{(m+1)}

(151)

Since the last term is a constant that does not depend on
\alpha
, we can let
m
be arbitrarily large to complete the proof.

∎

Appendix A
Definitions for states that are not full-rank

In Section
II
, we introduced the club-sandwiched conditional entropy. In what follows, we show that this definition is consistent with the extension obtained by taking limits of the corresponding objective function evaluated on full-rank states, which gives a consistent definition.

Let us denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{\alpha,\lambda}(\rho_{AE},\sigma_{E};\varepsilon)=\Tr[\left(\rho_{AE}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{E}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\sigma_{E}(\varepsilon)^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{AE}(\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}]\,,

(152)

where we defined the depolarized states as

\displaystyle\rho_{AE}(\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{AE}+\varepsilon\pi_{AE}\,,\quad\rho_{E}(\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{E}+\varepsilon\pi_{E}\,,\quad\sigma_{E}(\varepsilon)=(1-\varepsilon)\sigma_{E}+\varepsilon\pi_{E}

(153)

Here,
\pi_{AE}=I_{AE}/d_{AE}
is the fully mixed state. In the following, we show that the definition of the objective function is consistent with the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
of full-rank states. In particular, whenever
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
is finite, and the objective function converges to the definition given in Section
II
, where negative powers are taken only on the support of the states. If the support condition is not met, the objective function is infinite.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\lambda<0
and
\rho_{AE}
and
\sigma_{E}
be quantum states.
If

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\Tr(\omega_{E^n}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{E^n}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rho_{A^n E^n }\rho_{E^n}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\omega_{E^n}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}})^\alpha}=\widetilde{H}^{\lambda}_{\alpha}(A|E)_{\rho_{AE}}

(206)

where
\rho_{E^{n}}=\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}
and
\rho_{A^{n}E^{n}}=\rho_{AE}^{\otimes n}
and
\omega_{E^{n}}
is the universal state of Lemma
26
.

The next lemma concerns the symmetry of the optimizer of the LE conditional entropy.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\lambda\leq 0
and
\rho_{XE}
be a quantum state. The optimizer in

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,\infty}^{\lambda}(X^{m}|E^{m})_{\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^{m}}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})}

\displaystyle\;=\inf_{\sigma_{E^{m}}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

can be chosen to be permutation invariant under the exchange of the
E
systems.

Proof.

The above objective function is convex in
\sigma_{E^{m}}
. To see this, we can use the variational formula in the proof of Lemma
7

\displaystyle-\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{X^{m}E^{m}}}\Big\{\Tr[\tau_{X^{m}E^{m}}\log{\tau_{X^m E^m}}]

(207)

\displaystyle\qquad-\Tr[\tau_{X^{m}E^{m}}(\alpha\log{\big(\mathcal{P}_{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^m}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})\big)}+(1-\alpha)R_{X^{m}E^{m}}\big((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\log{\sigma_{E^m}}\big)R_{X^{m}E^{m}})]\Big\}

(208)

Since the logarithm is operator concave and the infimum of concave functions is concave, the latter function is concave in
\sigma_{E^{m}}
. Due to the minus sign, the original function is convex.

Next, we leverage convexity to show that for a given
\sigma_{E^{m}}
, the symmetrized state

\displaystyle\sigma_{E^{m}}^{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{m}}U_{\pi}^{B^{m}}\sigma_{E^{m}}(U_{\pi}^{B^{m}})^{\dagger}

(209)

performs at least as well as any state
\sigma_{E^{m}}
.
We have

\displaystyle\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}\exp\Big(\alpha\log{\big(\mathcal{P}_{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E^m}}(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes m})\big)}+(1-\alpha)R_{X^m E^m}\left((1-\lambda)\log{\rho_E^{\otimes m}}+\lambda\log{\sigma_{E^m}^{\mathrm{sym}}}\right)R_{X^m E^m}\Big)]

(210)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{m}}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{m}}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{m}}\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

\displaystyle=\log\Tr[R_{X^{m}E^{m}}
]

Here, the first inequality follows from the convexity in
\sigma_{E^{m}}
established earlier.
In the second equality, we insert the unitary implementing a permutation of the systems
X^{m}

and use that
U_{\pi}^{X^{m}}(U_{\pi}^{X^{m}})^{\dagger}=I_{X^{m}}
.
In the third equality, we exploit the fact that all remaining operators inside the exponential
are invariant under conjugation by the product unitary
U_{\pi}^{X^{m}}\otimes U_{\pi}^{E^{m}}
,
since they are invariant under permutations on the
m
copies of the composite system
XE
.
Finally, in the last equality, we pull the unitaries out of the exponential and use the
unitary invariance of the trace.
∎

Appendix C
Useful facts

Lemma 21
.

Let
\rho_{AE}
and
\sigma_{E}
be two quantum states. Then, it holds that
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})
. Moreover, if
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
, then it also holds that
\supp(I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
.

Proof.

It is well known and straightforward to
verify that
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E})
, which, for instance, guarantees the finiteness of the von Neumann
conditional entropy.

Let us assume that
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
. We need to prove that the latter condition implies
\supp(\rho_{E})\subseteq\supp(\sigma_{E})
. Let us denote with
S_{E}
the projector onto the support of
\sigma_{E}
. The condition
\supp(\rho_{AE})\subseteq\supp(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{E})
implies that
(I_{A}\otimes S_{E})\rho_{AE}(I_{A}\otimes S_{E})
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{E}=\Tr_{A}[\rho_{AE}]=\Tr_{A}[(I_{A}\otimes S_{E})\rho_{AE}(I_{A}\otimes S_{E})]=S_{E}\rho_{E}S_{E}\,,

(211)

which implies that
\supp(\rho_{E})\subseteq\supp(\sigma_{E})
.
∎

Lemma 22
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{{I_{A}}\otimes\rho_{E}}(\cdot)=I_{A}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}}(\cdot)

(212)

Proof.

We have
I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E}=I_{A}\otimes(\sum_{x}\Pi_{E,x}\lambda_{x})=\sum_{x}\lambda_{x}I_{A}\otimes\Pi_{E,x}
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{I_{A}\otimes\rho_{E}}(\cdot)=\sum_{x}I_{A}\otimes\Pi_{E,x}(\cdot)I_{A}\otimes\Pi_{E,x}=I_{A}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\rho_{E}}(\cdot)

(213)

∎

Note that the latter pinching is a map acting only on the side information
E
.
Moreover, pinching with
\rho_{E}
does not change the
E
marginal of
\rho_{AE}
.

Lemma 23
.

Let
\rho_{AE}
be a bipartite quantum state and let
\omega_{E}
a state such that
[\omega_{E},\rho_{E}]=0
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\Tr_{A}[\mathcal{P}_{I_{A}\otimes\omega_{E}}(\rho_{AE})]=\rho_{E}

(214)

Proof.

Using Lemma
22
we obtain that

\displaystyle\Tr_{A}[\mathcal{P}_{I_{A}\otimes\omega_{E}}(\rho_{AE})]=\Tr_{A}[I_{A}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E}}(\rho_{AE})]=\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{E}}(\rho_{E})=\rho_{E}

(215)

∎

The following lemma will be instrumental in deriving a variational characterization of the LE conditional entropy. It is commonly referred to in the literature as the Gibbs variational principle and was originally established in
23
(see also
(
13
, Lemma 1.2)
for a simpler proof in finite dimensions). In our setting, however, we require a slight modification of this result, as the optimization is restricted to states supported on a fixed reference state. This constraint leads to the appearance of an additional support projector, as already observed in
(
20
, Theorem III.6)
.

Lemma 24

(Constrained Gibbs variational principle)
.

Let
H
be a Hermitian operator and
P_{H}
be the projector onto its support. Then,

\displaystyle-\log{\Tr[P_H\exp(-H)]}=\min_{\supp(\tau)\subseteq\supp(H)}\{\Tr[\tau\log{\tau}]+\Tr[\tau H]\}

(216)

where the infimum is performed over all states
\tau
whose support is contained in the one of
H
. Moreover, the minimum is attained for

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{P_{H}\exp(-H)}{\Tr[P_{H}\exp(-H)]}\,.

(217)

The following result relates to complex interpolation theory and offers a version of Hadamard’s three-line theorem specifically for Schatten norms, as established in
2
.

Lemma 25
.

Let
F:S\rightarrow L(\mathcal{H})
be a bounded map that is holomorphic in the interior of
S
and continuous up to the boundary. Assume that
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
and for
0<\theta<1
define
p_{\theta}
by

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

(218)

For
k=0,1
, define

M_{k}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|F(k+it)\|_{p_{k}}.

(219)

Then, we have

\|F(\theta)\|_{p_{\theta}}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}.

(220)

The next lemma collects the key properties of the universal state
(
11
, Lemma 1)
. The conditional entropies can be defined in terms of the universal state, and its properties will be essential for our proofs.

Lemma 26
.

Let
A
be a system with
|A|=d
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a state
\omega_{A^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}(A^{n})
, referred to as the universal state, such that:

•

For any permutation-invariant state
\tau_{A^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}(A^{n})

\tau_{A^{n}}\leq g_{n,d}\omega_{A^{n}}
with
g_{n,d}\leq(n+1)^{d^{2}-1}

The state

is permutation invariant and invariant under

The next lemma is a consequence of Lemma
(
35
, Lemma 4.11)
for
\alpha=1/2
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
\sigma,\rho,\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
. Moreover, let
\sigma
be block diagonal with respect to the eigenspace projectors of
\omega
, i.e.,
\sigma=\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\sigma)
and
\mathcal{R}_{k}
be a map that commutes with
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}
. Then, for any
k\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle-\log{F(\R_k(\rho^{\otimes k}),\sigma^{\otimes k})}\leq-\log{F(P^{\otimes k}_\omega(\R_k(\rho^{\otimes k})),\sigma^{\otimes k})}+k\log{|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|}

(221)

Proof.

The pinching inequality states that

\displaystyle\rho\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\rho)\;\implies\;\rho^{\otimes k}\leq|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|^{k}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\rho)^{\otimes k}\,.

(222)

This inequality follows from the fact that if
A\leq B
are positive semidefinite operators, then
A^{\otimes k}\leq B^{\otimes k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which can be established by a straightforward induction argument. We then have

\displaystyle-\log{F(\R_k(\rho^{\otimes k}),\sigma^{\otimes k})}

(223)

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\log\Tr[(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k})\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k})\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}]

(224)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-2\log\Tr[(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k})\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}]+k\log{|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|}

(225)

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\log\Tr[(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}^{\otimes k}_{\omega}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}]+k\log{|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|}

(226)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\log{F(P^{\otimes k}_\omega(\R_k(\rho^{\otimes k})),\sigma^{\otimes k})}+k\log{|\mathrm{spec}(\omega)|}

(227)

In the first inequality, we used the operator antimonotonicity of the power
-1/2
and that the map
\mathcal{R}_{k}
commutes with
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}
. In the second equality, we use that inserting an additional pinching with respect to
\omega

inside the trace does not change its value, namely

\displaystyle\Tr[(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k})\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}]

(228)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Tr[(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}^{\otimes k}_{\omega}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}]\,.

(229)

To justify this equality, let
\{\Pi_{i}\}_{i}
denote the projectors onto the eigenspaces of
\omega
.
By assumption,
\sigma
is block diagonal with respect to
\{\Pi_{i}\}_{i}
, and by definition
of the pinching map,
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))
is block diagonal with respect to the tensor product of the projectors
\{\Pi_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\Pi_{i_{k}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{k}}
. Consequently,
the operator
(\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{R}_{k}(\rho^{\otimes k}))\sigma^{\otimes k\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

is block diagonal with respect to the same projectors.
Inserting the resolution of the identity inside the trace, using the
cyclicity of the trace, and the fact that all block-diagonal operators commute with the
projectors
\{\Pi_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\Pi_{i_{k}}\}_{i_{1},...,i_{k}}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

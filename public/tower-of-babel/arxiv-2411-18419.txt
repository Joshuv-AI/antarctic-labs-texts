2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state the necessary results used to show the non-repetition of the
a_{2}
coefficients of Hecke polynomials in various scenarios. Much of this background information can be found in
[
2
]
. Our work hinges on the following expression for
a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))
in terms of traces of Hecke operators:

Proposition 2.1

(
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Suppose
\gcd(N,m)=1
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}(N,2k))^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}d^{2k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}(N,2k)\right].

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s(N,2k)}
be the eigenvalues of the Hecke operator
T_{m}(N,2k)
. Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq s(N,2k)}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\right)^{2}-\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}^{2}\right]

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}(N,2k))^{2}-\Tr(T_{m}(N,2k)^{2})\right].

Then using the well-known Hecke operator composition formula
[
3
, Theorem 10.2.9]
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}(N,2k))^{2}-\Tr(T_{m}(N,2k)^{2})\right]

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m}(N,2k))^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}d^{2k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}(N,2k)\right],

as desired.
∎

Next we introduce the Eichler-Selberg trace formula (
[
5
, p. 370-371]
and
[

), which we will use to compute

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose

\displaystyle A_{1,m}(N,2k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2k-1}{12}\psi(N)m^{k-1}&{\rm if}~m~{\rm is~a~perfect~square},\\

(2.8)

\displaystyle A_{2,m}(N,2k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t^{2}<4m}P_{2k}(t,m)\sum_{n}h_{w}\left(\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\right)\mu(t,n,m),

\displaystyle A_{3,m}(N,2k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d\mid m}\min(d,m/d)^{2k-1}\mathop{\mathchoice{{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}\kern-2.80556pt}\limits_{\tau}\kern 2.80556pt}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\tau}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\tau}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}_{\tau}}}\nolimits\varphi(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau))\mathbf{1}_{N}(y),

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c|m\\
\gcd(N,m/c)=1\end{subarray}}c&{\rm if}~k=1,\\

Here, we have

•

means that

7.
Distinguishing Hecke eigenforms

Recall that Maeda’s conjecture states that the characteristic polynomials of the Hecke operators
T_{m}(1,2k)
are irreducible for all
m\geq 2
,
k\geq 1
.
In this section, we use the non-repetition results proven in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to extend the main result of
[
14
]
.
Theorem 1.2 of
[
14
]
showed that, assuming Maeda’s conjecture, a normalized Hecke eigenform of level one is determined by its
2
nd Fourier coefficient.
A natural question one might then ask is if the same result holds for the other Fourier coefficients.
In fact, this was recently conjectured by Xue and Zhu in
[
15
]
.

Conjecture 7.1

(
[
15
, Conjecture 3.5]
)
.

For any fixed
m\geq 2
, a normalized Hecke eigenform of level one is determined by its
m
-th Fourier coefficient.

The papers
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
used the non-repetition of
\Tr T_{m}(1,2k)
to prove the
m=2
and
m=3
cases of Conjecture
7.1
, respectively (conditional on Maeda’s conjecture).
We present a slightly different approach using the non-repetition of
a_{2}(T_{m}(1,2k))
; assuming Maeda’s conjecture, we use Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to verify Conjecture
7.1
for
m=2,4
.

Theorem
1.5
.

Let
m=2\text{ or }4
be fixed, and let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{2k_{1}}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
g=\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{g}(n)q^{n}\in S_{2k_{2}}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
be normalized Hecke eigenforms. Assume that the characteristic polynomials of the
T_{m}(1,2k_{i})
are irreducible. Then

(7.1)

f=g\quad\text{iff}\quad c_{f}(m)=c_{g}(m).

Proof.

For weights
2k
with
s(1,2k)\neq 0
, let
\Egv_{2k}
denote the set of eigenvalues of
T_{m}(1,2k)
(counting multiplicity). We observe that these
\Egv_{2k}
are all distinct.
For
\Egv_{2k}
of size at least
2
, this observation immediately follows from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
The
\Egv_{2k}
of size
1
come from
2k=12,16,18,20,22,26
. And we can verify directly that each of these
\Egv_{2k}
is distinct
[
9
]
.

We now show the desired result. The forward direction is immediate.
Then suppose that
c_{f}(m)=c_{g}(m)
, and we will show that
f=g
. Note that the irreducibility of characteristic polynomials means that the
\Egv_{2k_{i}}
will be precisely the Galois orbit of any one eigenvalue. Thus
c_{f}(m)=c_{g}(m)
means that
\Egv_{2k_{1}}=\Egv_{2k_{2}}
. Then by the above observation, this means that in particular, we must have
k_{1}=k_{2}
.
Thus since
k_{1}=k_{2}
,
c_{f}(m)
and
c_{g}(m)
are two eigenvalues of
T_{m}(1,2k_{1})
associated to
f
and
g
. But since the characteristic polynomial of
T_{m}(1,2k_{1})
has no repeated roots, we must have
f=g
. This completes the proof.
∎

We note that our approach is easy to generalize to any given
m\geq 2
. For any given
m\geq 2
, our methods prove the non-repetition of
a_{2}(T_{m}(1,2k))
for sufficiently large
k
(see the discussion below Conjecture
4.1
). Assuming Maeda’s conjecture, this verifies Conjecture
7.1
for eigenforms of sufficiently large weight. Then the finitely many remaining cases can be checked by computer.
This is an advantage over the approaches of
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
, which do not easily generalize to larger values of
m
. In particular, we are not aware of any general strategy to show the non-repetition of
\Tr T_{m}(1,2k)
.

In fact, this is an instance of a general phenomenon: The second coefficient
a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))
appears to be an easier object to study than the trace

\Tr T_{m}(N,2k)
(in part, due to the fact that
a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))
has the asymptotic behavior described in (
4.41
), while
\Tr T_{m}(N,2k)
does not seem to follow any sort of asymptotic behavior).
For example, it is known that
a_{2}(T_{m}(N,2k))
is nonvanishing for sufficiently large
k

[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
. However, no such result exists so far for
\Tr T_{m}(N,2k)
(even in level one).
For another example, the non-repetition of
a_{2}(T_{2}(N,2k))
shown in Theorem
1.1
gives much stronger results than the non-repetition of
\Tr T_{2}(1,2k)
shown in
[
1
, Theorem 1]
. First, Theorem
1.1
proves the monotonicity of
a_{2}(T_{2}(N,2k))
in
k
, while the trace
\Tr T_{2}(1,2k)
is by no means monotonic. Second, Theorem
1.1
addresses every odd level, instead of confining to level one (and the method of
[
1
, Theorem 1]
does not easily extend to higher levels).

We also note that the other coefficients of Hecke polynomials could similarly be used here. Based on the results of this work, one should be able to show the non-repetition of the other even-indexed coefficients
a_{2j}(T_{m})
.
In particular, one could apply the methods of this paper to obtain explicit bounds for the asymptotic behavior of
a_{2j}(T_{m})
proven in
[
11
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2]
, then use these explicit bounds to prove the non-repetition of
a_{2j}(T_{m})
.

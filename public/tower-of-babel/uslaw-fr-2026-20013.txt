    Abstract: Federal law--specifically Section 4 of the Archaeological 
Resources Protection Act of 1979 and Section 3 of the Antiquities Act 
of 1906--allows individuals and organizations to request permission to 
study, dig, or remove archaeological materials from public or Indian 
lands. Anyone who is not part of the National Park Service (NPS) must 
obtain a permit before doing any archaeological work on NPS lands, even 
if the work is part of another regulatory process. This includes 
activities such as excavation, shovel testing, coring, walking surveys 
(with or without collecting artifacts), underwater archaeology, 
photogrammetry, and documenting rock art
    Researchers from universities, museums, businesses, or other 
organizations must submit Form DI-1926, the Application for Permit for 
Archeological Investigations, before starting fieldwork. Applicants 
must also provide a statement of work, information about their 
qualifications and past experience, r[eacute]sum[eacute]s for key 
project staff, written consent from State or Tribal authorities when 
required, documentation from the facility that will care for the 
collections, and a detailed project schedule. State or Tribal 
authorities when required, authorization identifying the curation 
facility, and a detailed schedule of all project activities.
    Once a permit is issued, permit holders must submit preliminary, 
annual, and final reports during the course of the project.

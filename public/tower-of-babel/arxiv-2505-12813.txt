Arithmetic properties of MacMahon-type sums of divisors: the odd case
James A. Sellers, Roberto Tauraso

Abstract
. A century ago, P. A. MacMahon introduced two families of generating functions, ∑ 1 ≤ n 1 < n 2 < ⋯ < n t ∏ k = 1 t q n k ( 1 − q n k ) 2 and ​ ∑ 1 ≤ n 1 < n 2 < ⋯ < n t n 1 , n 2 , … , n t odd ∏ k = 1 t q n k ( 1 − q n k ) 2 , \sum_{1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{(1-q^{n_{k}})^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}\\ \text{$n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{t}$ odd}\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{(1-q^{n_{k}})^{2}}, which connect sum-of-divisors functions and integer partitions. These have recently drawn renewed attention. 1.
Introduction

The two families of generating functions

\sum_{1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{(1-q^{n_{k}})^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}\\
\text{$n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{t}$ odd}\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{(1-q^{n_{k}})^{2}},

(1)

introduced by P. A. MacMahon in
[
19
]
, relate integer partitions and sum-of-divisors functions. Note that the coefficient of
q^{n}
of the first function in (
1
) is the sum of products of the multiplicities
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{t}
such that
n
can be partitioned as

n=m_{1}\cdot n_{1}+m_{2}\cdot n_{2}+\dots+m_{t}\cdot n_{t}

with
1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}
. Restricting to the case of odd multiplicities, we find the coefficient of
q^{n}
of the second function in (
1
).

Such functions have attracted renewed interest as demonstrated by the considerable number of recent articles
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
. Furthermore, in
[
2
]
, Amdeberhan, Andrews, and Tauraso extended the first family in (
1
) by defining

U_{t}(a,q):=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{1+aq^{n_{k}}+q^{2n_{k}}}

for
a=0,\pm 1,\pm 2
(so that the roots of
1+ax+x^{2}=0
are roots of unity). They investigated various properties including several congruences for their coefficients. These arithmetic aspects were later expanded upon in
[
24
]
.

In this work, we take a similar approach for the second family in (
1
) by defining the generating functions

\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{t}\\
\text{$n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{t}$ odd}\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{q^{n_{k}}}{1+aq^{n_{k}}+q^{2n_{k}}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(a,t;n)q^{n},

(2)

where once again
a=0,\pm 1,\pm 2
. Since
\widetilde{U}_{t}
satisfies the identity

\widetilde{U}_{t}(-a,q)=(-1)^{t}\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,-q),

we will limit ourselves to the values
-2,0,1
.

When
t=1
, the functions
\widetilde{U}_{1}(-2,q)
,
\widetilde{U}_{1}(0,q)
and
\widetilde{U}_{1}(1,q)
exhibit some known and easily identifiable features. For example,

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{1}(-2,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-1}}{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}=\sum_{n,m\geq 1}mq^{m(2n-1)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n,\text{$n/d$ is odd}\end{subarray}}d\Big)q^{n}

\displaystyle=q+2q^{2}+4q^{3}+4q^{4}+6q^{5}+8q^{6}+8q^{7}+8q^{8}+13q^{9}+\dots

or equivalently,

\widetilde{U}_{1}(-2,q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n}}{(1-q^{2n})^{2}}\\
=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sigma(n)(q^{n}-q^{2n})

which implies

\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(-2,1;n)=\begin{cases}\sigma(n)-\sigma(n/2)&\text{if $n$ is even,}\\
\sigma(n)&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\end{cases}

where
\sigma(n)
is the sum of the positive divisors of
n
. See
[
21
, A002131]
.

For
a=0
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{1}(0,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2(2n-1)}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}(1-q^{n})(1-q^{2n})(1-q^{3n})}{1-q^{8n}}

\displaystyle=q\frac{f_{8}^{4}}{f_{4}^{2}}=q+2q^{5}+q^{9}+2q^{13}+2q^{17}+3q^{25}+2q^{29}+2q^{37}+\dots

where
f_{r}=(q^{r};q^{r})_{\infty}=\prod_{k\geq 1}(1-q^{rk})
.
Note that
\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(0,1;4n+1)
is the number of ways of writing
n
as the sum of two triangular numbers.

\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(0,1;4n+1)=[q^{n}]\frac{f_{2}^{4}}{f_{1}^{2}}=[q^{n}]\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{T_{n}}\Big)^{2}

where
T_{n}=n(n+1)/2
is the
n
th triangular number. See
[
21
, A008441]
.
Moreover, it can be verified that, for all
n\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(0,1;4(9n+5)+1)=\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(0,1;4(9n+8)+1)=0.

On the other hand, for
a=1
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{1}(1,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-1}}{(1+q^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)})}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n-1}(1-q^{2n-1})}{(1-q^{3(2n-1)})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}(1-q^{n})}{1-q^{3n}}-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n}(1-q^{2n})}{1-q^{6n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}-2q^{2n}+2q^{4n}-q^{5n}}{1-q^{6n}}

\displaystyle=q-q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}-q^{6}+2q^{7}-q^{8}+q^{9}+\dots.

that is

\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(1,1;n)=\tau_{1}(n)-2\tau_{2}(n)+2\tau_{4}(n)-\tau_{5}(n)

where
\tau_{j}(n)
is the number of positive divisors
d
of
n
such that
d\equiv j\pmod{6}
. See
[
21
, A093829]
.
Notice that

\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(1,1;6n+5)=\tau_{1}(6n+5)-\tau_{5}(6n+5)=0

(3)

because if
d
divides
6n+5
then
d\equiv 1\pmod{6}
if and only if
n/d\equiv 5\pmod{6}
.

With the above in mind, we see that the generating functions
\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)
naturally generalize various well–studied arithmetic functions. For
t>1
, our first goal is to obtain a more convenient formula for
\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)
that avoids multiple summations. This will be discussed in Section
2
, where we find that
\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)
can be written as

\sum_{k\geq 0}b_{k}(a)q^{k}\cdot\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}(a,t)q^{r(n)}

(4)

where
r(n)
is a quadratic polynomial. (Similar work was completed in
[
24
]
for the related functions
U_{t}(a,q)
.)

After laying this groundwork, we turn our attention to congruences.
Some of these congruences are straightforward. For example, thanks to (
2
), we immediately see that, for any
t
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{t}(-2,q)

\displaystyle\equiv\widetilde{U}_{t}(0,q)\pmod{2},

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{t}(-2,q)

\displaystyle\equiv\widetilde{U}_{t}(1,q)\pmod{3}.

(5)

In order to address less trivial congruences, we take advantage of the product in (
4
) and study the arithmetic properties of the coefficients
b_{k}(a)
and
c_{n}(a,t)
.
From there, starting in Section
5
and beyond, we will derive our results for
\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(a,t;n)
, grouped according to the value of
a
.

It is worth pointing out that, for
a=1
, we will come across the prefactor

\frac{f_{1}f_{6}}{f_{2}^{2}f_{3}}=1-q+q^{2}-q^{4}+2q^{5}-3q^{6}+4q^{7}-5q^{8}+7q^{9}+\dots

which appears to be novel in the literature and exhibits intriguing arithmetic properties which are of independent interest. These will be discussed in Section
3
.

2.
Explicit form of
\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)

In
[
6
, Corollary 2]
, we have an explicit form for the case
a=-2
, namely

\widetilde{U}_{t}(-2,q)=\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(-2,t)q^{n^{2}}

(6)

where

c(-2,t)=(-1)^{n+t}\frac{2n}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t}

and the prefactor is the generating function of number of overpartitions of
n
:

\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}=1+2q+4q^{2}+8q^{3}+14q^{4}+24q^{5}+\dots

An overpartition of
n
is a non-increasing sequence of natural numbers whose sum is
n
in which the first occurrence of a number may be overlined.

The formula (
6
) was derived by making use of the properties of the Chebychev polynomials of the first kind, defined by

T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
,
and of their representation in terms of the sum

T_{n}(x)=\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}(2x)^{n-k}.

(7)

As we will see below, the same method is effective for
a=0
and
a=1
as well.
Noting that the Chebychev polynomial
T_{n}(x)
is alternately an even and odd function, depending on
n
, we define

to_{n}(x)=\frac{T_{2n+1}(\sqrt{x})}{\sqrt{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad te_{n}(x)=T_{2n}(\sqrt{x}).

Then, by
[
6
, Theorem 1]
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}to_{n}\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}=(q;q)^{3}_{\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{xq^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\right)

and

1+2\sum_{n>0}te_{n}\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)q^{n^{2}}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(1+\frac{xq^{2n-1}}{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}\Big).

(8)

In
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, the authors established

\sum_{t\geq 0}U_{t}(a,q)x^{t}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+aq^{n}+q^{2n})(1-q^{n})}\cdot\sum_{n\geq 0}to_{n}\left(\frac{x+a+2}{4}\right)q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}.

Along the same lines we show the following identity.

Theorem 2.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)x^{t}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+aq^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)})(1-q^{2n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}te_{n}\left(\frac{x+a+2}{4}\right)q^{n^{2}}\right).

(9)

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}\widetilde{U}_{t}(a,q)x^{t}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{xq^{2n-1}}{1+aq^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+aq^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+(x+a)q^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}{1+aq^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{(x+a+2)q^{2n-1}}{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+aq^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)})(1-q^{2n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}te_{n}\left(\frac{x+a+2}{4}\right)q^{n^{2}}\right)

where at the last step we applied (
8
).
∎

We are now in a position to present explicit formulas for
a=0
and
a=1
.

By (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle[x^{t}]\,2te_{n}\left(\frac{x+a+2}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=[x^{t}]\,2n\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}(x+a+2)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{k}{t}(a+2)^{k-t}.

(10)

Moreover, the binomial sum on the right-hand side of (
10
) admits the following useful representation, which is derived by using Riordan arrays (see
[
25
]
and also
[
2
, Appendix 2]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n\geq t\geq 1
, we have that

\sum_{k=t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{k}{t}(a+2)^{k-t}=[z^{n}]\frac{z^{t}-z^{t+2}}{(1-az+z^{2})^{t+1}}.

(11)

Proof.

If
F(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}z^{k}
then

\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}c_{k}=[z^{n}]\,\frac{1-z}{1+z}\cdot F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right).

Hence, by letting

F(z)=\sum_{k\geq t}\binom{k}{t}(a+2)^{k-t}z^{k}=\frac{z^{t}}{(1-(a+2)z)^{t+1}},

we find that the left-hand side of (
11
) is equal to

\displaystyle[z^{n}]\,\frac{1-z}{1+z}\cdot F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=[z^{n}]\,\frac{1-z}{1+z}\cdot\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\Big)^{t}}{\Big(1-(a+2)\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\Big)^{t+1}}

\displaystyle=[z^{n}]\frac{z^{t}-z^{t+2}}{(1-az+z^{2})^{t+1}}.

∎

Letting
a=0
in (
11
), we find

2n\sum_{k=t}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{k}{t}2^{k-t}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{n-t}{2}}\frac{2n}{n+t}\binom{\frac{n+t}{2}}{t}&\text{if $n+t$ is even,}\\
0&\text{if $n+t$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Hence, from (
9
), by separately considering the even and odd cases, we obtain

\widetilde{U}_{2t}(0,q)=\frac{f_{8}}{f_{4}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+t}\frac{2n}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t}q^{4n^{2}}=\widetilde{U}_{t}(-2,q^{4})

(12)

and

\widetilde{U}_{2t+1}(0,q)=\frac{f_{8}}{f_{4}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-t-1}\frac{2n-1}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t+1}q^{(2n-1)^{2}}=qW_{t}(q^{4})

(13)

with

W_{t}(q)=\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}(0,t)q^{n(n-1)}

(14)

and

c_{n}(0,t)=(-1)^{n-t-1}\frac{2n-1}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t+1}.

Finally, for
a=1
, from (
9
) and (
10
), we get

\widetilde{U}_{t}(1,q)=\frac{f_{1}f_{6}}{f_{2}^{2}f_{3}}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}(1,t)q^{n^{2}}

(15)

where

c_{n}(1,t)=\sum_{k=t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}.

For
a=1
, equation (
11
) does not yield a closed-form expression for
c_{n}(1,t)
, but it will prove useful later for studying its arithmetic properties.

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{16}f_{24}^{2}}{f_{2}f_{6}f_{8}f_{48}}.

Replacing

With the above in hand, we can now state a characterization modulo 2 satisfied by

for all

Theorem 3.2
.

We have

a(2n)\equiv\begin{cases}1\pmod{2},&\text{if $n=0$ or $n$ is a square not divisible by $3$}\\
0\pmod{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
, we know

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(2n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{8}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{1}f_{3}f_{4}f_{24}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{f_{1}^{8}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{1}f_{3}f_{1}^{4}f_{12}^{2}}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{1}^{3}}{f_{3}}

\displaystyle=b(q)\ \ \ \textrm{thanks to Lemma \ref{bq_prod_representation}}

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{3n+1}}{1-q^{9n+3}}\pmod{2}\ \ \ \textrm{thanks to Lemma \ref{bq_sum_representation}}

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\sum_{n\geq 1,\;3\nmid n}q^{n^{2}}\pmod{2}

and this yields our result.
At the last step we applied

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{3n+1}}{1-q^{9n+3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{3n+1}}{1-q^{9n+3}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{-3n-2}}{1-q^{-9n-6}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}q^{3n+1}q^{m(9n+3)}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{6n+4}}{1-q^{9n+6}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}q^{(3n+1)(3m+1)}-\sum_{m,n\geq 0}q^{(3n+2)(3m+2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{(3n+1)^{2}}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{(3n+2)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{0\leq n<m}q^{(3n+1)(3m+1)}-2\sum_{0\leq n<m}q^{(3n+2)(3m+2)}.

∎

3.9

), and recognizing that divisibility by 8 immediately implies divisibility by 4, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(8n+4)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{f_{2}f_{4}f_{12}^{4}}{f_{1}^{3}f_{3}^{3}f_{6}}+2\frac{f_{2}^{3}f_{12}}{f_{6}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{f_{1}^{2}f_{4}f_{3}^{4}f_{12}^{3}}{f_{1}^{3}f_{3}^{5}}+2\frac{f_{2}^{3}f_{6}^{2}}{f_{6}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2qf_{4}f_{12}^{3}\cdot\frac{1}{f_{1}f_{3}}+2f_{2}^{3}f_{6}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle=2qf_{4}f_{12}^{3}\left(\frac{f_{8}^{2}f_{12}^{5}}{f_{2}^{2}f_{4}f_{6}^{4}f_{24}^{2}}+q\frac{f_{4}^{5}f_{24}^{2}}{f_{2}^{4}f_{6}^{2}f_{8}^{2}f_{12}}\right)+2f_{2}^{3}f_{6}

using Lemma
3.4
. Simplifying further modulo 4, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(8n+4)q^{n}\equiv 2q\frac{f_{4}^{3}f_{12}^{6}}{f_{24}^{2}}+2q^{2}\frac{f_{4}^{4}f_{12}f_{24}^{2}}{f_{8}^{2}}+2f_{2}^{3}f_{6}\pmod{4}

which implies that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(16n+4)q^{2n}\equiv 2q^{2}\frac{f_{4}^{4}f_{12}f_{24}^{2}}{f_{8}^{2}}+2f_{2}^{3}f_{6}\pmod{4}.

Replacing
q^{2}
by
q
and then continuing to simplify modulo 4, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(16n+4)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{f_{2}^{4}f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{4}^{2}}+2f_{1}^{3}f_{3}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{f_{2}^{4}f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{4}^{2}}+2f_{2}f_{6}\cdot\frac{f_{1}}{f_{3}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle=2q\frac{f_{2}^{4}f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{4}^{2}}+2f_{2}f_{6}\left(\frac{f_{2}f_{16}f_{24}^{2}}{f_{6}^{2}f_{8}f_{48}}-q\frac{f_{2}f_{8}^{2}f_{12}f_{48}}{f_{4}f_{6}^{2}f_{16}f_{24}}\right)

from Lemma
3.1
. We then see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(16(2n+1)+4)q^{2n+1}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q\frac{f_{2}^{4}f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}{f_{4}^{2}}-2q\frac{f_{2}^{2}f_{8}^{2}f_{12}f_{48}}{f_{4}f_{6}f_{16}f_{24}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q{f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}-2q\frac{f_{12}f_{48}}{f_{6}f_{24}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q{f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}-2q\frac{f_{6}^{2}f_{12}^{4}}{f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 2q{f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}-2q{f_{6}f_{12}^{2}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

Thus, for all
n\geq 0
,

a(16(2n+1)+4)=a(32n+20)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

∎

4.
Congruences for coefficients
c_{n}(a,t)

As our focus turns to the divisibility of binomial coefficients by powers of primes (mainly
2
and
3
), we recall a fundamental result, namely Kummer’s Theorem (see, for instance,
[
14
]
): if
p
is a prime, then the largest power of
p
dividing
\binom{n}{m}
is equal to

\nu_{p}\left(\binom{n}{m}\right)=\frac{S_{p}(m)+S_{p}(n-m)-S_{p}(n)}{p-1}.

where
\nu_{p}(k)
is the largest power of
p
dividing an integer
k

and
S_{p}(k)
is the sum of its base-
p
digits.

We also require the following Lemma, which plays a key role in studying congruences modulo powers of two.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any integer
s\geq 1
,

(1+z)^{2^{s}}\equiv(1+z^{2})^{2^{s-1}}\pmod{2^{s}}.

(28)

Proof.

We show the claim by induction with respect to the exponent
s
. For
s=1
, it holds:

(1+z)^{2}=1+2z+z^{2}\equiv 1+z^{2}\pmod{2}.

Let us now proceed to the inductive step. If the statement holds for
s
then

(1+z)^{2^{s}}=(1+z^{2})^{2^{s-1}}+2^{s}P(z)

for some polynomial
P
with integer coefficients. This implies that

\displaystyle(1+z)^{2^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\left((1+z)^{2^{s}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left((1+z^{2})^{2^{s-1}}+2^{s}P(z)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+z^{2})^{2^{s}}+2(1+z^{2})^{2^{s-1}}\cdot 2^{s}P(z)+2^{2s}P(z)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv(1+z^{2})^{2^{s}}\pmod{2^{s+1}}

and we are done.
∎

We now present three theorems, each establishing congruences for the coefficients
c_{n}(a,t)
for
a=-2,0,1
, in that order.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

c_{n}(-2,t)=(-1)^{n+t}\frac{2n}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t}.

Then

1) if
s\geq 1
,
J\geq 1
, and
n\geq 0
is even, then

c_{n}(-2,2^{s}J-1)\equiv 0\pmod{2^{s+1}},

2) if
J\geq 0
and
n\not\equiv 13,14\pmod{27}
, then

c_{n}(-2,27J+13)\equiv 0\pmod{3},

3) if
J\geq 1
and
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{27}
, then

c_{n}(-2,27J-1)\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

Proof.

1) Let
t=2^{s}J-1
, then by (
11
) and (
28
) we have that

\displaystyle c_{n}(-2,2^{s}J-1)

\displaystyle=[z^{n}]\frac{z^{t}-z^{t+2}}{(1+2z+z^{2})^{t+1}}

\displaystyle=[z^{n}]\frac{z^{2^{s}J-1}-z^{2^{s}J+1}}{(1+z)^{2^{s+1}J}}

\displaystyle\equiv[z^{n}]\frac{z^{2^{s}J-1}-z^{2^{s}J+1}}{(1+z^{2})^{2^{s}J}}\pmod{2^{s+1}}

Since
\frac{z^{2^{s}J-1}-z^{2^{s}J+1}}{(1+z^{2})^{2^{s}J}}
is an odd function, the right-hand side is zero when
n
is even.

2) Let
t=27J+13
and
n=27N+r
with
0\leq r<27
. Then

\nu_{3}(c_{n}(-2,t))=\nu_{3}\left(\frac{n}{t}\binom{n+t-1}{2t-1}\right)=\nu_{3}(n)+\nu_{3}\left(\binom{n+t-1}{2t-1}\right).

If
n
is a multiple of
3
, then the congruence trivially holds.
Assume that
r\in\{1,2,4,5,7,8,10,11\}
. Then

\displaystyle\nu_{3}(c_{n}(-2,t))

\displaystyle=\nu_{3}\left(\binom{n+t-1}{2t-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(S_{3}(2t-1)+S_{3}(n-t)-S_{3}(n+t-1)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(S_{3}(27\cdot 2J+25)+S_{3}(27(N-J-1)+r+14)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S_{3}(27(N+J)+r+12)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(S_{3}(2J)+5+S_{3}(N-J-1)+S_{3}(r+14)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S_{3}(N+J)-S_{3}(r+12)\Big)

\displaystyle=3-\nu_{3}\left(\binom{r+14}{2}\right)+\nu_{3}(N-J)+\nu_{3}\left(\binom{N+J}{2J}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 3-\nu_{3}(r+14)-\nu_{3}(r+13)\geq 1

where, in the last step, we made use of the inequality

\nu_{3}(r+14)+\nu_{3}(r+13)\leq\max(\nu_{3}(r+14),\nu_{3}(r+13))<3

which holds because
\gcd(r+14,r+13)=1
and
r+14<3^{3}
.

Similarly, for
r\in\{16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26\}
,

\displaystyle\nu_{3}(c_{n}(-2,t))

\displaystyle=3-\nu_{3}\left(\binom{r-13}{2}\right)+\nu_{3}(N+1-J)+\nu_{3}\left(\binom{N+1+J}{2J}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 3-\nu_{3}(r-14)-\nu_{3}(r-13)\geq 1.

The proof of 3) is similar.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

c_{n}(0,t)=(-1)^{n+t-1}\frac{2n-1}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t+1}.

Then

1) if
J\geq 1
and
n\not\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
, then

\displaystyle c_{n}(0,4J-1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{4}

\displaystyle c_{n}(0,8J-1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{8},

2) if
J\geq 1
and
n\not\equiv 0,1\pmod{8}
, then

\displaystyle c_{n}(0,32J-1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{16}

\displaystyle c_{n}(0,64J-1)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{32},

3) if
J\geq 0
and
n\not\equiv 13,15\pmod{27}
, then

c_{n}(0,27J+12)\equiv 0\pmod{3},

4) if
J\geq 1
and
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{27}
, then

c_{n}(0,27J-1)\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

Proof.

We note that

\frac{2n-1}{n+t}\binom{n+t}{2t+1}=\frac{2n-1}{2t+1}\binom{n+t-1}{2t}.

For 1) and 2), let
t=2^{s}J-1
and
n=2^{s}N+r
with
1<r<2^{s}
. Then

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(c_{n}(0,t))

\displaystyle=\nu_{2}\left(\binom{n+t-1}{2t}\right)

\displaystyle=S_{2}(2t)+S_{2}(n-t-1)-S_{2}(n+t-1)

\displaystyle=S_{2}(2^{s}(J-1)+2^{s}-1)+S_{2}(2^{s}(N-J)+r)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-S_{2}(2^{s}(N+J)+r-2)

\displaystyle=S_{2}(J-1)+s+S_{2}(N-J)+S_{2}(r)-S_{2}(N+J)-S_{2}(r-2)

\displaystyle=s-\nu_{2}\left(\binom{r}{2}\right)+\nu_{2}\left(J\binom{N+J}{2J}\right)

\displaystyle\geq s+1-\nu_{2}(r)-\nu_{2}(r-1)\geq s.

Hence, if

, then

\nu_{2}(c_{n}(0,t))\geq s

which implies 1).
On the other hand, if
n\not\equiv 0,1
modulo

then

\nu_{2}(c_{n}(0,t))\geq s-1

which implies 2).

The proofs of 3) and 4) can be carried out in a similar way.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

c_{n}(1,t)=\sum_{k=t}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{k}{t}3^{k-t}.

Then

1) if

and

is even, then

2) if
J\geq 1
and

, then

and

, then

If

, then

which implies that if

2) Let

∎

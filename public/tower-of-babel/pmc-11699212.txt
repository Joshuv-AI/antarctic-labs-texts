Metabolomics advances in chronic atrophic gastritis diagnosis and the integration of traditional Chinese medicine
Authors: Yanqun Luo, Haiping Xue, Hongyu Chen, Ying Gao, Guang Ji, Tao Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

Introduction

CAG is a prevalent digestive system disease marked by progressive gastric mucosa atrophy and gland reduction, resulting in dysfunctional gastric glands and decreased acid secretion (Zhou et al., 2022, Sipponen and Maaroos, 2015). Most patients with CAG are asymptomatic, and those who do show symptoms typically have nonspecific ones (Rodriguez-Castro et al., 2018). The main symptoms include persistent or recurrent upper abdominal discomfort, belching, stomach burning, bloating, early satiety, nausea, and decreased appetite. The etiology involves Helicobacter pylori infection, dietary and environmental factors, autoimmune issues, and other factors such as pyloric sphincter dysfunction, excessive alcohol consumption, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug use, irritating foods, and radiation therapy (Sharma et al., 2014, Rusak et al., 2016). Reports from the World Health Organization and medical research indicate a clear link between CAG and gastric cancer. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that CAG significantly increases the risk of gastric cancer by 4.12 times (OR = 4.12, 95 % CI 3.20–5.30). Additionally, CAG is associated with a 2.08-fold increase in the risk of esophageal cancer and a 2.77-fold increase for esophagogastric junction cancer. This data underscores CAG as a significant precancerous lesion for gastric cancer, highlighting the need for early identification and treatment of CAG to prevent gastric cancer (Li et al., 2024). Traditional diagnostic methods mainly depend on endoscopy and tissue biopsy, which, while clinically valuable, are often invasive and can cause physical and psychological stress for patients (Ma et al., 2024). Additionally, gastric mucosal lesions often show few or mild symptoms in the early stages, resulting in diagnoses typically occurring only after the disease has progressed. Consequently, there is an urgent need for more convenient, efficient, and non-invasive diagnostic methods.

Lately, metabolomics has advanced rapidly, offering new insights. This technology-based approach utilizes mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance to analyze changes in small molecular metabolites within biological systems, illuminating the metabolic features of diseases and enabling the diagnosis and identification of biomarkers (Cameron and Takáts, 2018). In CAG research, metabolomics can identify metabolites linked to pathological states, helping to explore potential biomarkers (Liu et al., 2020). These metabolic changes reflect the extent of gastric mucosal damage and offer insights into pathological processes like inflammation and oxidative stress. For example, the levels of certain amino acids and organic acids may be significantly altered in patients with atrophic gastritis, making them potential diagnostic indicators (Cui et al., 2017, Zu et al., 2020). Furthermore, metabolomics can help monitor metabolic changes during treatment, providing data to support clinical interventions (Xu et al., 2017). The treatment of CAG has a long history in TCM. By harmonizing the organs, improving Qi and blood flow, and boosting immunity, TCM, along with its herbal characteristics, provides a comprehensive approach for patients with CAG. The rapid advancement of modern medicine presents new opportunities to integrate Chinese medicine with metabolomics. Studying the effects of Chinese medicine through metabolomics can unveil the mechanisms by which Chinese herbs regulate gastrointestinal metabolism and help identify key components of herbal formulas, thereby enhancing the recognition of TCM in contemporary healthcare (Liu et al., 2022, Xu et al., 2020).

In summary, metabolomics holds significant promise for diagnosing CAG, and its integration with TCM opens new research avenues. This review will explore the specific applications of metabolomics in detecting CAG and propose innovative ideas and strategies for early diagnosis and comprehensive treatment by merging TCM with modern medical theories. Such interdisciplinary collaboration not only deepens our understanding of CAG but also establishes a foundation for future research in this field.

Methods

Recent advancements in the social economy have increased demands on healthcare, necessitating a thorough understanding of CAG's development in recent years. The relevant literature was searched on PubMed and Web of Science from January 2013 to July 2024, with the key terms of “metabolomics” or “metabonomics” or “CAG” or “ metabolomics and CAG ” or “metabonomics and CAG ” or “Chronic Atrophic Gastritis”. In all the literatures that we have obtained, metabolomics studies, including the literatures about animal studies and clinical studies, which is related to CAG was screened out.

Application of metabolomics in CAG

Potential of metabolomics in the diagnosis of CAG

Currently, the primary methodological approach for diagnosing and evaluating CAG involves invasive studies. However, the invasive nature of these procedures, coupled with the requirement for patient fasting, imposes significant physical and psychological stress (Hao et al., 2020). Consequently, there is an urgent clinical need for a non-invasive diagnostic technology. In recent years, metabolomics, as an emerging biomedical research method, has gradually shown great potential in nutrition, disease mechanism research, and clinical diagnosis. Role of metabolomics in the identification of biomarkers

The application of metabolomics enables researchers to identify potential biomarkers from complex metabolic networks, which can be used for early screening and diagnosis of CAG. Wang (Wang, 2016) utilized metabolomics to analyze plasma and urine from CAG patients, revealing a potential correlation between CAG pathogenesis and metabolic disorders across various biochemical pathways, including energy metabolism, material transport, signal transduction, and gastrointestinal microbiota metabolism. Zu et al. (Zu et al., 2021) employed a metabolomics approach based on Liquid Chromatography/Mass Spectrometry (LC/MS) to investigate CAG pathogenesis, identifying 24 differential serum metabolites in CAG rats that were predominantly involved in 12 metabolic pathways. Notably, dihydrosphingosine and neurosphingosine in the sphingolipid metabolic pathway were highlighted as possible biomarkers for CAG diagnosis. Zu et al. (Zu et al., 2020) developed a CAG model in Wistar rats using methylnitroguanidine, and through LC-MS, they detected differences in urinary metabolic profiles, with 68 distinct metabolites showing significant regulation, primarily involving D-glutamine and D-glutamate metabolism, purine metabolism, and histidine metabolism. Cui et al. (Cui et al., 2017) used Proton Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (1H NMR)-based metabolomics, compared plasma and urine metabolites between control and CAG rats, identifying significant differences in levels of metabolites such as arginine, succinic acid, 3-hydroxybutyric acid, α-ketoglutarate, and valine. Kuligowski et al. (Kuligowski et al., 2016) identified kynurenine and tryptophan as discriminant metabolites for indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase upregulation in cancer, which is associated with increased kynurenine metabolite production and tryptophan depletion. Yu and team (Yu et al., 2011) by examining low-molecular-weight metabolites in plasma, demonstrated the potential to accurately distinguish between CSG and CAG, suggesting that metabolic phenotypes could characterize the progression from CSG to CAG. Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2019) confirmed that mitochondria, involving 30 targeted proteins, were a significant cellular target in CAG. Z. Zhao et al. (Zhao et al., 2020) reported that CAG, a precancerous lesion of gastric cancer, was also marked by a progressive increase in S100A9 expression. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2020), after identifying 303 upregulated and 26 downregulated differentially expressed genes, suggested that Cytochrome P450, family 3, subfamily A, polypeptide 4 (CYP3A4) could serve as a biomarker for predicting CAG progression and the poor prognosis of gastric cancer. Tong et al. (Tong et al., 2017) showed that specific volatile organic compound profiles could aid in the screening and diagnosis of CAG patients. Gai et al. (Gai et al., 2022) demonstrated that pentadecanoic acid and heptadecanoic acid, which interacted with fecal-derived gut microbiota such as Haemophilus and Erysipelotrichaceae_UCG-003, may serve as potential biomarkers for CAG diagnosis.

Role of metabolomics in the pathogenesis of CAG

As a precancerous lesion, a deep understanding of the pathogenesis of CAG is of great significance for early diagnosis and effective treatment. Metabolomics, as an emerging systems biology approach, can provide insights into the biomarkers and pathophysiological mechanisms of CAG through comprehensive analysis of small molecular metabolites within biological systems. The pathogenesis of CAG is quite complex with multiple factors. First and foremost, Helicobacter pylori infection is one of the most common precipitating factors for CAG. It can stimulate the gastric mucosa to produce a chronic inflammatory response, leading to glandular atrophy (Lim and Chung, 2023, Yang et al., 2020). Secondly, abnormal immune responses also play a significant role, with autoimmune mechanisms potentially leading to the attack and destruction of the gastric wall cells (Yang et al., 2021). Furthermore, environmental factors such as dietary habits, lifestyle, and the use of certain medications may also impact the health of the gastric mucosa. The application of metabolomics in CAG research provides an important tool for exploring its mechanisms. Through metabolomic analysis, researchers can identify specific metabolites associated with CAG, which can serve as potential biomarkers to assist in early diagnosis and monitoring of disease progression. For instance, recent studies have indicated that in patients with CAG, significant alterations in certain amino acid metabolism and lipid metabolism pathways may be associated with enhanced inflammatory responses and glandular atrophy (Liu et al., 2020). Additionally, metabolomics has identified specific metabolites, such as glycochenodeoxycholic acid, taurochenodeoxycholic acid, and L-isoleucine, that exhibit significant differences between patients with CAG and healthy control groups (Liu et al., 2023). Additionally, metabolomics combined with gut microbiota plays a significant role in elucidating the pathogenesis of CAG. Liu et al. investigated the mechanism of action of Astragalus membranaceus in treating CAG by combining metabolomics with 16S rRNA gene sequencing. They eventually discovered that two metabolites (7-keto-3A ·12-α-hydroxyalkanoic acid and deoxycholic acid) and two gut microbiota genera (Acetobacter and Escherichia) showed a noticeable increase after Astragalus membranaceus treatment, suggesting that the dysregulation of these metabolites and microbial communities might be one of the mechanisms underlying the development of CAG, and that Astragalus membranaceus may exert its therapeutic effects by targeting these specific metabolites and microbial populations (Liu et al., 2022).

Challenges of metabolomics in the diagnosis of CAG

Despite the new insights that metabolomics provides for understanding CAG, there are still a series of limitations and challenges. Firstly, the complexity of metabolomics poses a significant challenge for data interpretation. CAG is a disease caused by multiple factors, involving nutrition, genetics, environment, and other elements, which leads to a high degree of heterogeneity in the changes of metabolites across different individuals or stages of the disease. This heterogeneity makes it difficult for researchers to determine the specific origins of metabolites and their causal relationships with the disease when interpreting metabolomic data. Secondly, sample selection and processing significantly impact the reliability of the results. In CAG research, the selection of samples (such as the patient's age, gender, and disease duration) and processing (such as sampling time and storage conditions) can markedly affect the metabolite profiles. Additionally, the lack of standardized operating procedures and techniques makes it difficult to compare results across different studies, hindering the widespread application of metabolomics in the field of CAG. Furthermore, existing metabolomic analysis methods are still limited by technology and equipment. Although technologies such as mass spectrometry and NMR are widely used in metabolomics, they require significant investment in equipment and specialized operational skills. Moreover, the quantification and qualification analysis of many metabolites still face technical bottlenecks, limiting our comprehensive understanding of the metabolic characteristics of CAG. Lastly, the barrier to clinical translation is also a major challenge for metabolomics in CAG research. Although metabolomics may identify potential biomarkers, how to translate these findings into clinical diagnostic tools or treatment strategies still requires substantial research. To achieve this goal, it is necessary to strengthen the connection between basic research and clinical applications, as well as interdisciplinary collaboration, to promote the application of metabolomics in the field of CAG and other diseases. In summary, metabolomics has shown great promise in the study of CAG, but its complexity, sample processing, technical limitations, and clinical translation issues all pose challenges to research. Future research needs to make multifaceted efforts to overcome these limitations, thereby promoting the application of metabolomics in the diagnosis and understanding of CAG.

Role of metabolomics in prevention and treatment of CAG with TCM

For the treatment of CAG, in contrast to modern medicine, TCM focus on the whole concept and syndrome differentiation and treatment (diagnosis and treatment based on an overall analysis of the illness and the patient's condition), which has a good clinical effect. The premise of the curative effect is the accurate grasp of the syndrome type. Metabolomics is helpful to the accurate judgment of the TCM syndrome type of CAG and the therapeutic effect evaluation after the prescription treatment (Table 1).

Role of metabolomics in the TCM syndrome differentiation of CAG

CAG is not specifically recorded or named in ancient Chinese literature. TCM diagnosis categorizes CAG into six main syndrome types, which are Liver-Stomach Qi Stagnation Syndrome, Liver-Stomach Heat Depression Syndrome, Spleen-Stomach Deficiency Syndrome (Spleen-Stomach Cold Syndrome), Spleen-Stomach Damp-Heat Syndrome, Stomach Yin Deficiency Syndrome, and Stomach Collaterals Blood Stasis Syndrome. Patients often present with Spleen-Stomach Deficiency and Liver-Stomach Qi Stagnation (Liu et al., 2024, Xu et al., 2022). Clarifying the relationship between CAG's TCM syndromes and objective indicators helps to establish credible diagnostic and therapeutic guidelines with TCM characteristics, which can guide clinical practice and improve clinical efficacy. Yan (Yan, 2014) conducted metabolomics analysis on the blood of patients with CAG and gastrointestinal ulcers, identifying differences among those with Qi stagnation syndrome, Qi deficiency and blood stasis syndrome, and damp-heat syndrome. They suggested that these distinct metabolites may underlie the various syndrome types. Wang (Wang, 2016) compared plasma metabolites among healthy individuals, CAG patients with spleen-stomach deficiency cold syndrome, and those with spleen-stomach dampness-heat syndrome, discovering metabolic differences in nucleic acid, amino acid, lipid, and glycometabolism. They proposed that valine, lactulose, isocitric acid, formic acid, and carnosine may serve as potential biomarkers for distinguishing spleen and stomach damp-heat syndrome. These studies provide strong evidence for TCM syndrome differentiation of CAG.

Application of metabolomics in traditional Chinese medicine formulas

TCM boasts a long history and a wealth of practical experience in the treatment of CAG. In recent years, with the rapid advancement of metabolomics, scientists have increasingly recognized its potential in TCM research, particularly in understanding and validating the mechanisms of action of traditional TCM formulas. In the treatment of CAG, TCM formulas typically take the form of compound prescriptions, which include multiple herbal ingredients that coordinate and interact with each other to achieve a holistic therapeutic effect. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have begun to employ metabolomic approaches to analyze the mechanisms of action of TCM formulas.

Unveiling the biomarkers and metabolic pathways of traditional Chinese medicine formulas for CAG

Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2018) used the metabolomics technology of NMR to find that Huangqi Jianzhong (HQJZ) decoction could improve the symptoms of CAG rats and regulate the levels of 16 urine differential metabolites, which were primarily involved in amino acid metabolism and energy metabolism. Tong et al. (Tong et al., 2021) performed plasma and urine metabolomics analysis using ultra performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole time of flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-Q-TOF/MS) to screen specific potential biomarkers and metabolic pathways for the Zuojin pill to improve the CAG rat model and found 9 potential metabolites and enriched changes in 6 related metabolic pathways. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2021) used ultra high performance liquid chromatography coupled with quadrupole-exactive mass spectrometry (UHPLC-Q exactive MS) metabolomics to search for metabolic pathways and potential mitochondrial biomarkers of HQJZ in CAG rats, focusing on mitochondria, a specific organelle, which is isolated from gastric tissue samples. There were sixteen biomarkers of CAG tissue in total being identified, elevent of which were modulated by HQJZ therapy significantly. These biomarkers were primarily involved inserine, threonine, glycine metabolism, aminoacyl tRNA bioanabolic metabolism, taurine metabolism.

Unveiling the mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicine formulas on CAG

Through metabolomic analysis of TCM formulas, not only can biomarkers be identified, but their mechanisms of action can also be explored, and the metabolic pathways they affect can be understood.

Sun et al. (Sun et al., 2021) used high performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS) to perform metabolomic analysis on serum of CAG rats treated with Lienpuyin and found that Lienpuyin had therapeutic effects on CAG. And, the mechanism might relate to the regulation of niacin metabolism pathway and amino acid metabolism pathway. Among them, cysteine and methionine metabolism may be the core pathway of Lienpuyin in the treatment of CAG. The result of Liu et al. (Liu and Wang, 2019) showed that modified Zhengqi Powder has a significant effecting in treating chronic gastritis and mild intestinal metaplasia, also, it can remove inflammatory factors, reduce clinical symptoms and intestinal metaplasia obviously and significantly improves quality of life for patients. (Liu et al., 2017) also agree with the idea that HQJZ decoction could improve the symptoms of CAG. But Xue et al. Further analysis of metabolic pathways demonstrated that the TCA cycle, arginine, proline, aspartate and glutamate metabolisms, arachidonic acid metabolism were the most pertinent pathways for treating SDG. To screen the anti-CAG material basis of HQJZ, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2018) used alternate administration of ammonia and sodium deoxycholic to establish the CAG rat model by starvation disorder method. 1H NMR metabolomics was used to analyze the urine metabolic characteristics of CAG rats improved by HQJZ. The results of metabolomics showed that HQJZ could improve 16 urinary disturbed metabolites involved in energy metabolism and amino acid metabolism obviousely in CAG rats. According to six key metabolites (valine, α-ketoglutarate, glycine, sarcosine, malonic acid, and fumaric acid) closely related to biochemical indexes, 28 related genes and proteins were selected as potential anti-CAG targets of HQJZ. Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2020) used the metabolomics method along with 1H NMR and UPLC-Q /TOF MS, to demonstrate the effictiveness of HQJZ on constructed CAG rats. There were found 19 and 16 possible biomarkers linked to CAG, respectively. HQJZ treatment can significantly regulate 32 urine metabolites, including isoleucine, leucine, valine, glycine, serine, and threonine and 3 metabolic pathways, such as TCA cycle, are the most relevant pathways for HQJZ therapy. Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2020) used a mitochondria-specific metabolomics technique based on UHPLC-Q exactive MS. Sixteen endogenous metabolites were first identified to define mitochondrial dysfunction in CAG. Several targeted metabolites implicated in the amino acid metabolism and the fatty acid beta-oxidation pathway could be considerably regulated by pretreatment of Saccharum Granorum. According to network pharmacology, the upstream proteins connected to these targeted metabolites have high affinity with Saccharum Granorum constituents. In summary, TCM formulas have demonstrated good clinical outcomes in the treatment of CAG. In-depth analysis of their components can reveal multiple important biomarkers and metabolic pathways. This provides a theoretical basis for the scientific and standardized treatment of CAG with TCM and also helps to establish a new model for the integration of Chinese and Western medicine.

Metabolomics potential in traditional Chinese medicine formulas for CAG

By delving into the metabolic characteristics and mechanisms of action of TCM formulas, metabolomics not only provides new perspectives for the diagnosis and treatment of CAG but also lays the foundation for the modernization and internationalization of TCM. In future research, metabolomics can better integrate with TCM to provide innovative solutions for the treatment of CAG. The application of metabolomics in the treatment of CAG with TCM formulas is rapidly developing. Future studies can delve deeper into the following aspects. First, establishing standardized methods for metabolite identification and quantification to enhance the reproducibility and reliability of research outcomes. Second, combining multi-omics technologies such as genomics and transcriptomics to construct comprehensive metabolic network models, thereby revealing the mechanisms of action of TCM formulas. Finally, validating the application value of metabolomic biomarkers in routine clinical monitoring through large-scale clinical trials.

Astragalus membranaceus

Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2020) comprehensively evaluated the regulatory effect of Astragalus membranaceus on CAG based on UPLC TOFMS and mitochondrial-specific metabolomics. Sixteen related metabolites from mitochondrial samples were used as potential biomarkers for CAG, and Astragalus can regulate 9 of them significantly. Combined with network pharmacology research, 3 active components and 3 mitochondrial metabolites in Astragalus membranaceus have better docking ability with 56 predicted target proteins based on Systems Dock, and they are involved in a variety of biological abnormalities, including mitochondrial dysfunction. These results showed that mitochondrial energy metabolism played a key role in Astragalus anti-CAG. Based on metabonomic and pharmacology strategy, Li et al. (Li et al., 2020) chose a traditional prescription HQJZ decoction to evaluate Astragalus membranaceus against CAG rats in the study. Serum metabonomics analysis identified 9 differential endogenous metabolites related to the metabolic changes caused by CAG. Three important pathways, including pyruvate metabolism, glycolysis or gluconeogenesis, and aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, were further screened out as the most important pathways related to the efficacy of Astragalus membranaceus contributing to HQJZ by integrating MetaboAnalyst, systemsDock, and binding ability weighted polypharmacology index.

Gastrodia elata

Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2019) studied the preventive and therapeutic effects of gastrodia elata on autoimmune CAG in rats. The results showed that gastrodia elata could significantly improve the damaged gastric glands and biochemical indexes, promote gastric acid secretion, and significantly reduce gastric inflammation. Based on the H-1 NMR metabolomics, Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2020) investigated the therapeutic effectiveness of extract from several polar parts of Gastrodiae Rhizoma on autoimmune CAG. The results showed that the effective constituents of Gastrodiae Rhizoma should be high-polarity compounds which can achieve good therapeutic effect by rectifying the disturbed energy and amino acid metabolism in CAG mode. The mechanism may be that gastrodia elata could effectively treat CAG by regulating energy and purine metabolism. Tong et al. (Tong et al., 2021) investigated the therapeutic effect of Berberine (BBR) on CAG based on the plasma and urine metabolomics method of UHPLC-Q-TOF/MS. The results showed that the reason why BBR had therapeutic effectiveness on CAG may be related with the regulation of 12 metabolic pathways and 15 metabolic markers. This study provides new insights into the mechanism by which BBR improves CAG.

Cinnamon

Liu et al. utilized UHPLC-Q/TOF MS to analyze the metabolites in the cecal contents. They found that compared to the control group, certain metabolites in the model group (such as 2-oxohexanoic acid, L-(−)-3-phenyllactic acid, azelaic acid, etc.) were significantly increased, while other metabolites (such as hypoxanthine, 7-ketodeoxycholic acid, and cholic acid) were significantly decreased, indicating that cinnamon can significantly regulate the levels of seven metabolites and play a role in the treatment of CAG (Liu et al., 2023).

Role of metabolomics in acupuncture treatment

1H NMR is a powerful analytical technique that reveals molecular structure and dynamics by measuring the resonance frequency of hydrogen nuclei in a magnetic field (Bizzarri et al., 2022). Using 1H NMR metabolomics investigation of a range of biological materials, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2017) explored the distinctions between moxibustion and electro-acupuncture in the treatment mechanism of CAG rats (medulla, cerebral cortex, stomach, and serum). Moxibustion and electro-acupuncture therapies shown positive benefits in enhancing CAG for all sample types, mostly through metabolic alterations in membrane metabolism, energy metabolism, and neurotransmitter function. He et al. (He et al., 2018) investigated the effects of electroacupuncture and moxibustion therapy while analyzing the 1H NMR metabolic profiles of the kidney and liver of CAG rats. Male South Dakota rats were treated with Ea or moxibustion at Zusanli (ST 36) and Liangmen (ST 21) acupoints for two weeks by feeding of a mix of 30 % alcohol and 2 % sodium salicylate combined with irregular fasting and forced exercise for twelve weeks. Studies have found that both moxibustion and electroacupuncture can correct the changes caused by cag by restoring antioxidant metabolism, energy metabolism, neurotransmitter metabolism and other metabolism, but compared with electroacupuncture, moxibustion can reverse more metabolites related to energy metabolism. Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2017) evaluated the CAG pathology and therapeutic effect of electroacupuncture stimulation of Sibai (ST 2) and Liang men (ST 21) on multiple biological samples (stomach, serum, urine, medulla and cortex) by 1H NMR spectroscopy analysis system. Using a CAG rat model, the zusanli point was set on the gastric meridian. By observing the effect of moxibustion at “Zusanli” (ST36) on gastric-tissue metabolites in rats with CAG, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2019) found that moxibustion had a good efficacy in regulating some metabolic substances such as choline phosphate glutathione, uracil, and N-acetylaspartic acid of CAG rats, suggesting a specific relationship between the stomach and the Stomach Meridian. In this way, moxibustion at “Zusanli” (ST36) has a significant effect on the improvement of CAG symptoms. Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2020) also found that “Zusanli” can significantly ameliorate the degenerative alterations in gastric mucosa caused by CAG in rats. Furthermore, the therapeutic mechanism of QHY may be linked to the inhibition of inflammatory factors IL-6 and IL-8, as well as the suppression of TLR4/TRIF mRNA and protein expression. CAG produced changes in comprehensive metabolism, including gut microbiota-related metabolism, membrane catabolism and metabolism, glycolysis, and the TCA cycle, according to the findings. These findings help to clarify the therapeutic mechanism of moxibustion and EA on CAG rats and also provide new insights into the biochemistry of CAG and the therapeutic mechanism of EA stimulation. Role of metabolomics in prevention and treatment of CAG with TCM ingredients

TCM ingredients are easier to assess for their effects and mechanisms of action in the prevention and treatment of CAG because they have a clear structure and consist of a single component. Metabolomics can effectively evaluate the therapeutic effects of these monomeric drugs on CAG and identify the pathways through which they exert their action (Table 1).

In terms of native compounds research, Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2020) demonstrated that Palmatine (Pal), an isoquinoline alkaloid from Coptidis rhizome, can restore the body function of CAG rats significantly. And via metabolomics analysis, they found that the treatment effect of Pal on CAG involved ten metabolic pathways and ten metabolites which had a mutual interferencewith each other due to citric acid cycle. Dehydroevodiamine (DHE) was investigated by Wen et al. (Wen et al., 2021) demonstrated its ability to protect the GES-1 (a human gastric mucosal epithelial cell line) from damage induced by N-methyl-N'-nitro-n-nitrosoguanidine. The protective effect of DHE on CAG is mostly connected to the control of inflammatory chemicals and processes involved to energy metabolism, according to a cell metabolomics investigation based on UHPLC-Q-TOF/MS. The Nrf2 signaling pathway is crucial for inhibiting inflammation by regulating anti-inflammatory gene expression. In CAG, activating Nrf2 may reduce inflammatory chemical production, thus slowing disease progression (Ahmed et al., 2017). Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2022) to lessen H. pylori-induced gastric mucosa damage via activating Nrf2 signaling and then avoids causing CAG. Vitamin C and phytochemicals, can also affect CAG via other pathways (Toh and Wilson, 2020). Rhein, an active component found in various traditional Chinese medicines, has multiple pharmacological effects. Liu and colleagues discovered that Rhein reduces inflammation in CAG by regulating the Nrf2 signaling pathway (Liu et al., 2023). In addition, it was indicated that Nrf2 activation can protect the gastric mucosa from oxidative stress-related DNA damage and apoptosis, both of which contribute to the progression of CAG. For example, curcuminoid, an active compound in the traditional herb Atractylodes macrocephala, mitigates oxidative stress and DNA damage by activating Nrf2, thus alleviating MNNG-induced CAG (Wang et al., 2024).

In terms of synthetic compounds research, Postavnyi (Postavnyi, 1998) points out that the total content of oxidated compounds and products of the oxidation reaction such as conjugated dienes and trienes, tetraenes, are believed to represent a damaging action of factors of virulence of Helicobacter infection on the epitheliocyte cellular membrane and cause the injury of mucous membrane. Molodovets et al. (Molodovets et al., 2014) proved that the activation of lipid peroxidation was related to the increased levels of proinflammatory cytokines and was helpful to the antioxidant defense which is important in the therapy of CAG. All these findings could provide new insights for the treatment and the prevention of CAG.

Role of metabolomics in prevention and treatment of CAG with gut microbiota

Humans have countless microbes that interact with their hosts to form an entire ecosystem. In recent years, it has been proved that the gut microbiota and enzymes play a significant role in the emergence and progression of many diseases. Recently, as the importance of bile acid-related microbiota has increasingly been recognized, scientific studies have shown that regulating gut microbiota through drugs and other means can achieve the purpose of treating or relieving CAG effectively (Sgambato et al., 2017).

Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2023) provided substantial evidence for the analysis of the microbial community and its metabolism in the stomach, and demonstrate many specific interactions between the stomach microbiome and metabolome. It was shown that the gut microbiota played a significant role in the occurrence and development of diseases, and involved in the metabolic processes of drugs, especially TCM administered orally. With the 16S rRNA gene sequencing analysis, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2023) had highlighted that Xiaojianzhong Tang, possessed the ability to modulate the dysbiosis of the gut microbiota in rats with CAG, particularly targeting bacteria implicated in bile acid metabolism, such as Acetobacter and Desulfovibrio. These bacteria are crucial for the disease's progression, and the study suggests that Xiaojianzhong Tang's metabolites exhibit a significant docking effect on targets associated with CAG. Furthermore, employing ultra-performance liquid chromatography coupled with time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-TOFMS) based metabolomics, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2022) discovered that Astragali Radix could shield the digestive system from metabolic disruptions caused by CAG, potentially mediated through the modulation of two specific gut microbiota, Acetobacter and Escherichia. This finding underscores the potential of traditional medicines in managing gut microbiota-related disorders. Lin et al. (Lin et al., 2023) extended this understanding to ruminants, revealing that beyond monogastric animals, the manipulation of bile acid metabolism, which is integral to modulating microbe-host interactions, could also be instrumental in treating intestinal inflammation in cows. This broadens the application of microbiota-focused therapies to a variety of species. Employing a comprehensive suite of omics techniques, including metagenomic sequencing and metabolomics, Hang et al. (Hang et al., 2022) identified that Shugan Decoction was capable of regulating specific intestinal microbiota and metabolic pathways. This regulation may be key to understanding how Shugan Decoction mitigates visceral hypersensitivity and abnormal intestinal motility in rats with WAS-induced IBS-D. These findings were pivotal in the development of preventive and therapeutic strategies for CAG, offering a multifaceted approach to managing this complex condition Research on the relationship between gut microbiota and CAG has made some progress, but studies often rely on animal models, such as rats, which may not accurately reflect the complexity of human disease. Additionally, the gut microbiota consists of hundreds of bacterial species, yet studies typically focus on a limited number, potentially overlooking relevant microbes. Individual variations in drug metabolism can also influence drug efficacy and gut microbiota regulation. Furthermore, the sequencing technologies and analytical methods employed may have limitations that hinder the accurate characterization of microbial communities.

Conclusion and prospective

Metabolomics has greatly enhanced our understanding of CAG and the mechanisms through which TCM provides therapeutic effects. However, several inherent limitations and challenges must be acknowledged. One major challenge is the complexity of biological samples, which contain thousands of metabolites that may interact in intricate ways, complicating the identification of sensitive and specific biomarkers for CAG, especially when distinguishing it from other gastrointestinal disorders. Additionally, confounding factors like diet, lifestyle, and concurrent medications can influence metabolite profiles and affect result interpretation. The vast dynamic range of metabolite concentrations also poses challenges, as not all metabolomics platforms can detect and quantify metabolites effectively across this range. While metabolomics can identify metabolic changes linked to CAG, establishing causality often requires further validation through targeted mechanistic studies. Moreover, translating metabolomics findings into clinical applications faces hurdles, including the need for standardized protocols and data processing methods to ensure reproducibility and reliability.

Despite these challenges, metabolomics shows great promise for studying CAG and evaluating TCM treatments. Future research should focus on overcoming these limitations by developing advanced analytical techniques, rigorously validating biomarkers, and integrating metabolomics data with other omics approaches like genomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics. This integrated approach can offer a comprehensive view of the molecular changes associated with CAG and TCM treatment. Additionally, future studies should explore how TCM modulates the metabolome in CAG patients, including interactions between TCM compounds and the gut microbiota and their influence on metabolic pathways. Large-scale, multicenter clinical trials are also needed to validate metabolomics-based diagnostic and therapeutic strategies across diverse populations and establish their clinical utility.

In conclusion, although metabolomics has significantly contributed to CAG research, it remains to be explored. By addressing current challenges and pursuing innovative research directions, metabolomics can continue to advance our understanding of CAG and support the development of personalized TCM treatments.

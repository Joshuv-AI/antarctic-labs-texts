Cohomology and Extensions of C p C_{p} -Green Functors of Lie Type
Tarik Anowar, Satyendra Kumar Mishra, Ripan Saha 1. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University, Raiganj 733134, West Bengal, India2. Department of Mathematical Sciences, Indian Institute of Technology (BHU), Varanasi-221005, India3. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj University, Raiganj 733134, West Bengal, India

Abstract




ABSTRACT

We develop a theory of
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type, unifying the axiomatic framework of Green functors with the structure of Lie algebras under the action of a cyclic group
C_{p}
of prime order. Extending classical notions from representation theory and topology, we define tensor and exterior products, introduce an equivariant Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology, and construct cup products that endow the cohomology with a graded Green functor of Lie type structure. A key result establishes a correspondence between equivalence classes of singular extensions and second cohomology groups, generalizing classical Lie algebra extension theory to the equivariant setting. This framework enriches the toolkit for studying equivariant algebraic structures and paves the way for further applications in deformation theory, homotopical algebra, and representation theory.

Key words
: Mackey functor, Green functor, Lie algebra, Cohomology, Singular extension, Equivariant cohomology.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
17B10, 17B56, 17B55, 17B99.

1
Introduction

Mackey functors originated with George Mackey’s work on group representations, formalizing systems of modules over subgroups connected by restriction and induction maps. James A. Green
[
10
]
expanded this framework by introducing multiplicative structures (Green functors) to study modular representations, particularly vertices and sources of indecomposable modules. Andreas Dress
[
5
]
axiomatized Mackey functors in the 1970s, linking them to Burnside rings and algebraic K-theory. In the 1980s, Lewis, May, and McClure
[
13
]
embedded Green functors into equivariant topology, where they became central to understanding equivariant cohomology theories like Bredon cohomology
[
3
]
. In 1990, Thévenaz and Webb
[
16
]
develop cohomology theories for Mackey functors, connecting them to derived functors and subgroup lattices. Modern advancements extend these ideas to derived categories, tensor-triangular geometry, and motivic homotopy theory, cementing Mackey and Green functors as foundational tools in representation theory, topology, and equivariant mathematics.

The study of equivariant cohomology provides a powerful framework for understanding algebraic structures endowed with group actions, bridging representation theory, homological algebra, and topology. Central to this framework are Mackey and Green functors, which systematically encode algebraic data across subgroups of a finite group while respecting restriction, transfer, and conjugation maps. These functors have found profound applications in equivariant topology
[
13
]
and modular representation theory. In
[
12
]
, the author studied an aspect of equivariant associative algebras using Mackey functor and the notion of oriented group action. In this paper, we extend these ideas to the setting of Lie algebras, introducing and analyzing
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type
—a novel structure that combines the axiomatic framework of Green functors with the algebraic richness of Lie theory, particularly under the action of a cyclic group
C_{p}
of prime order. In this paper, we only deal with the cyclic group
C_{p}
of prime order
p
, as such groups have only two subgroups, making the resulting structure simpler and more tractable. This serves as a natural first step toward the study of general
G
-Green functors of Lie type and the development of a full-fledged cohomology theory.

Lie algebras equipped with group actions arise naturally in deformation theory, physics, and geometry. However, their equivariant cohomology, especially in the context of Mackey functors, remains underexplored. Our work aims to fill this gap by developing a categorical and cohomological theory for Lie-type Green functors equivariant under
C_{p}
. Specifically, we establish how the interplay between Lie brackets and the functorial operations (restriction, transfer, conjugation) enriches both the structure theory and cohomological invariants of these objects.

The paper begins by recalling foundational concepts, including Mackey functors, Green functors, and their module categories. We then define
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type, requiring that the evaluations at the trivial subgroup
e
and the group
C_{p}
themselves carry Lie algebra structures, compatible with the functorial maps. Morphisms in this category are pairs of Lie algebra homomorphisms that commute with the restriction, transfer, and conjugation maps, ensuring coherence across subgroups.

Key constructions include the tensor and exterior products of
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type. These operations are carefully designed to respect the Mackey functor axioms while preserving antisymmetry and Jacobi identities. We further introduce the equivariant Hom complex,
C_{p}\text{-}\mathrm{HOM}(\wedge^{n}L_{1},L_{2})
, which generalizes alternating multilinear maps to the Mackey functor setting.

Building on this, we define the
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology
for
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type, demonstrating that it naturally inherits the structure of a Mackey functor. A central result is the classification of singular extensions of
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type by bimodules, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of such extensions and the second Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{Lie}_{C_{p}}}(L,M)
.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we recall Mackey and Green functors, the notion of pairing. In Section
3
, we introduce
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type and their morphisms. In Section
4
, we develop tensor and exterior products. Section
5
constructs the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology. In Section
6
, we define cup-product on the cochain complex Mackey functors and show that graded cohomology
H^{*}(L,M)
becomes a
C_{P}
-Green functor of graded Lie type under this cup-product. Finally, in Section
7
, we classify singular extensions, generalizing the classical Lie algebra extension theory to the equivariant setting.

Our results underscore the versatility of Green functors in equivariant algebra and provide new tools for studying Lie structures in categories enriched with group actions. This work lays the groundwork for future explorations in equivariant deformation theory based on the work of Gerstenhaber
[
8
,
9
]
, homotopical algebra, and applications to geometric contexts where symmetries and noncommutative structures intertwine.

2
Preliminary

In this section, we recall basic definitions and results following
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
10
,
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
14
]
, which will be used throughout the paper.

Fix a commutative ring
R
and a finite group
G
, and denote by
S(G)
the set of all subgroups of
G
. For
H\in S(G)
and
g\in G
, let
{}^{g}H=gHg^{-1}
. If
H\in S(G)
and
L,K\in S(H)
, let
[L\setminus H/K]
be a set of representatives of double cosets
LhK
with
h\in H
. Finally, if
K\leq H\leq G
, let
H/K
be a set of representatives of cosets
hK
with
h\in H
. In this paper, we always assume
R
is a commutative ring with characteristics
0
.

2.1 Definition
.

A
G
-Mackey functor
M
(over
R
) is a functor from the category of subgroups
S(G)
of
G
to the category of
R
-modules, along with three families of
R
-linear morphisms. The families
r_{K}^{H}:M(H)\rightarrow M(K)
of restrictions and
t_{K}^{H}:M(K)\rightarrow M(H)
of transfers are indexed on the collection of
H,K\in S(G)
such that
K\leq H
. The family
c_{g}=c_{g,H}:M(H)\rightarrow M(^{g}H)
of conjugation maps is indexed on
H\in S(G)
and
g\in G
. For all
g,h\in G
and
H,K,L\in S(G)
, these maps must satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

If
L\leq K\leq H\leq G
, then

r_{L}^{K}\circ r_{K}^{H}=r_{L}^{H},\quad t_{K}^{H}\circ t_{L}^{K}=t_{L}^{H}.

(ii)

Identity properties:

r_{H}^{H}=\text{id}_{M(H)},\quad t_{H}^{H}=\text{id}_{M(H)}.

(iii)

Conjugation composition:

c_{gh}=c_{g}\circ c_{h}.

(iv)

If
h\in H
, then
c_{h}:M(H)\rightarrow M(H)
is the identity map.

(v)

Compatibility of conjugation with restrictions and transfers:

c_{g}\circ r_{K}^{H}=r_{{}^{g}K}^{{}^{g}H}\circ c_{g},\quad c_{g}\circ t_{K}^{H}=t_{{}^{g}K}^{{}^{g}H}\circ c_{g}.

(vi)

Mackey Axiom: If
L,K\leq H
, then

r_{L}^{H}\circ t_{K}^{H}=\sum_{h\in[L\setminus H/K]}t_{L\cap^{h}K}^{L}\circ r_{L\cap^{h}K}^{{}^{h}K}\circ c_{h}.

2.2 Example
.

Let
M
be a left
R[G]
-module. The fixed point functor
FP_{M}
is defined by

FP_{M}(H)=M^{H}=\{m\in M\mid h\cdot m=m,\text{ for all }h\in H\}.

Let
H
and
K
be two subgroups of
G
such that
K\leq H
. Now, the restriction map
r^{H}_{K}:M^{H}\rightarrow M^{K}
is the inclusion of fixed points. The transfer map
t^{H}_{K}:M^{K}\rightarrow M^{H}
is the relative transfer map, defined by

t^{H}_{K}(m)=\sum_{h\in H/K}h\cdot m,

for
m\in M^{K}
. The conjugation maps are defined by

c_{g}(m)=g\cdot m,

for all
g\in G
and
m\in M
.

It can be easily verified that the fixed point functor
FP_{M}
is a Mackey functor.

Let
R[W_{G}H]
be the group ring of the Weyl group
W_{G}H
, where
W_{G}H=N_{G}(H)/H
is the normalizer of
H
in
G
modulo
H
.

Let
M
and
N
be two Mackey functors. A morphism of two Mackey functors
f:M\rightarrow N
is a family of
R
-module homomorphisms
f(H):M(H)\rightarrow N(H)
, for
H\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, which commute with the three maps: restriction, transfer, and conjugation. Since the morphism
f
commutes with conjugation, this implies that
f(H)
is an
R[W_{G}H]
-map.

The category of Mackey functors for
G
over
R
is denoted by
\text{Mack}_{R}(G)
. The set of morphisms between any two Mackey functors
M
and
N
is written as
\text{Hom}_{\text{Mack}_{R}(G)}(M,N)
.

It is easy to see that
\text{Mack}_{R}(G)
is an abelian category.

2.1
Pairing Notation

Here we recall the concept of the product in the context of a pairing. Let
M
,
N
, and
L
be
G
-Mackey functors over the ring
R
. A pairing is a family of
R
-linear maps

\cdot:M(H)\times N(H)\rightarrow L(H)\quad\text{defined as}\quad(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y

such that for
H,K\in\mathcal{S}(G)
with
K\leq H
, the following conditions hold:

(vii)

r^{H}_{K}(x\cdot y)=r^{H}_{K}(x)\cdot r^{H}_{K}(y)
, for
x\in M(H)
and
y\in N(H)
,

(viii)

c_{g}(x\cdot y)=c_{g}(x)\cdot c_{g}(y)
, for
x\in M(H)
,
y\in N(H)
, and
g\in G
,

(ix)

(Frobenius axiom)

t^{H}_{K}(x\cdot r^{H}_{K}(y^{\prime}))=t^{H}_{K}(x)\cdot y^{\prime},\quad\text{for }x\in M(K),\,y^{\prime}\in N(H),

t^{H}_{K}(r^{H}_{K}(x^{\prime})\cdot y)=x^{\prime}\cdot t^{H}_{K}(y),\quad\text{for }x^{\prime}\in M(H),\,y\in N(K).

2.3 Definition
.

A Green functor
A
for the group
G
over a ring
R
is a Mackey functor
A
equipped with a pairing

A\times A\rightarrow A

such that, for each
H\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, the
R
-linear map

A(H)\times A(H)\rightarrow A(H)

makes
A(H)
into an associative
R
-algebra with unity
1_{A(H)}
. Moreover, this structure satisfies the following condition:

(x)

If
K\leq H
, then
r^{H}_{K}(1_{A(H)})=1_{A(K)}
.

2.4 Example
.

Let
M
be a
G
-algebra over the ring
R
. Then the fixed-point functor
\text{FP}_{M}
from the previous example is, in fact, a cohomological Green functor for
G
.

In particular, consider the case when
M=\text{End}_{R}(W)
, where
W
is an
R[G]
-module. In this case, we obtain a Green functor where the multiplication is given by function composition. This Green functor is denoted by
\text{End}_{R^{*}}(W)
.

.

A
C_{p}
-Mackey functor
L
is called a
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type if the following conditions hold:

1.

L(C_{p})
and
L(e)
both are Lie algebras with respect to some Lie brackets
[,]
.

2.

The restriction maps
r^{C_{p}}_{e}:L(C_{p})\longrightarrow L(e)
and conjugation maps
c_{x}:L(e)\longrightarrow L(e)
are Lie algebra homomorphisms.

3.

e\leq C_{p}
are subgroups of
C_{p}

[t^{C_{p}}_{e}(a),b]=t^{C_{p}}_{e}[a,r^{C_{p}}_{e}(b)];

[b,t^{C_{p}}_{e}(a)]=t_{e}^{C_{p}}[r^{C^{p}}_{e}(b),a],

a\in L(e)
and
b\in L(C_{p})

3.2 Remark
.

If
L(C_{p})
and
L(e)
are both graded Lie algebras, and the restriction, transfer, and conjugation maps respect the gradation, then the structure is called a
C_{p}
-Green functor of graded Lie type.

3.3 Example
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}
be the Heisenberg Lie algebra over
\mathbb{K}
generated by
2n+1
elements

\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z\},

where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
is defined as follows

[x_{i},y_{i}]=z,\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

The bracket on other combinations of basis elements are zero. For
p\leq n
, let us consider the following
C_{p}
action on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}
by Lie algebra automorphisms:

•

\sigma(x_{i}):=x_{[i+1]},~\sigma(y_{i}):=y_{[i+1]}
for
i=1,\ldots,p
,

•

\sigma(x_{i}):=x_{i},~\sigma(y_{i}):=y_{i}
, for
i>p
, and
\sigma(z):=z
,

where
C_{p}=\langle\sigma\rangle
and
[i+1]=i+1(mod~p)
. Next, take
F(C_{p})
to be the Heisenberg Lie algebra generated by elements
\{\sum_{j=1}^{p}x_{j},x_{p+1},\ldots,x_{n},\sum_{j=1}^{p}y_{j},y_{p+1},\ldots,y_{n},z\}
and
F(e)=\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}
. Note that
F(C_{p})=\mathfrak{h}_{2(n-p)+3}
. Let us consider the following structure maps:

•

Restriction:
The restriction map
r_{e}^{C_{p}}:F(C_{p})\rightarrow F(e)
is the inclusion map;

•

Transfer:
The transfer map is defined by

\begin{split}t_{e}^{C_{p}}(x_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}x_{j},\quad&t_{e}^{C_{p}}(y_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}y_{j},\quad\text{for }i=1,\ldots,p,\\
t_{e}^{C_{p}}(x_{i})=px_{i},\quad&t_{e}^{C_{p}}(y_{i})=py_{i},\quad\text{and }\quad t_{e}^{C_{p}}(z)=pz\quad\text{for }i>p;\end{split}

•

Conjugation:
For
\sigma^{k}\in C_{p}
with
k=1,\ldots,p
the conjugation map
c_{\sigma^{k}}:\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}
is defined by

\begin{split}c_{\sigma^{k}}(x_{i})=x_{[i+k]},\quad&c_{\sigma^{k}}(y_{i})=y_{[i+k]},\quad\text{for }i\leq p;\\
c_{\sigma^{k}}(x_{i})=x_{i},\quad&c_{\sigma^{k}}(y_{i})=y_{i},\quad\text{and }\quad c_{\sigma^{k}}(z)=z,\quad\text{for }i>p.\end{split}

With the above structure maps,
F
is a
C_{p}
-green functor of Lie type.

3.4 Example
.

Let us consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
, that is the vector space of
n\times n
complex matrices with trace
0
equipped with the commutator bracket. Let us consider the following
C_{2}
action on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
by Lie algebra automorphisms:

\sigma(X)=-X^{T}.

where
X^{T}
is the transpose of the matrix
X
and
C_{2}=\langle\sigma\rangle
. We define
F(C_{2}):=\mathfrak{so}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e., the Lie algebra of complex skew-symmetric matrices with trace zero, and
F(e):=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
. Then, we consider the following maps

•

Restriction:
The map
r_{e}^{C_{2}}:F(C_{2})\rightarrow F(e)
is the inclusion map;

•

Transfer:
The transfer map is defined by

\begin{split}t_{e}^{C_{2}}(X)=X-X^{T},\quad\text{for any }X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C});\end{split}

•

Conjugation:
The conjugation map
c_{\sigma}:\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is defined by

c_{\sigma}(X)=-X^{T},\quad\text{for any }X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

With the above structure maps,
F
is a
C_{2}
-green functor of Lie type.

3.5 Remark
.

The two examples above are specific cases of the following more general example of a Green functor of Lie type, constructed via a fixed-point Mackey functor.

3.6 Example
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over a field
k
, equipped with a
C_{p}
-action by Lie algebra automorphisms. Define a
C_{p}
-Green functor
F
of Lie type as follows.

The values of
F
are given by:

F(C_{p})=\mathfrak{g}^{C_{p}},\quad F(e)=\mathfrak{g},

where
\mathfrak{g}^{C_{p}}
denotes the subalgebra of
C_{p}
-fixed points in
\mathfrak{g}
.

The structure maps of the functor are defined as follows.

Restriction:
The map
r^{C_{p}}_{e}:\mathfrak{g}^{C_{p}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is the natural inclusion.

Transfer:
The map
t^{C_{p}}_{e}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{C_{p}}
is defined by

t^{C_{p}}_{e}(x)=\sum_{g\in C_{p}}g\cdot x.

Conjugation:
For each
g\in C_{p}
, the conjugation map
c_{g}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is given by

c_{g}(x)=g\cdot x.

Note that both
\mathfrak{g}^{C_{p}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
are Lie algebras with their usual Lie brackets. It is straightforward to verify that these data define a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type.

3.7 Example
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field
k
. We define a
C_{p}
-Green functor
L
of Lie type as follows.

At the group
C_{p}
, define
L(C_{p})=\mathfrak{g}
, equipped with its original Lie bracket
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

At the trivial group
e
, define
L(e)=\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus p}
, the direct sum of
p
copies of
\mathfrak{g}
, with the Lie bracket defined component-wise:

[(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}),(y_{1},\dots,y_{p})]=([x_{1},y_{1}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\dots,[x_{p},y_{p}]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

The structure maps of the functor are as follows.

Restriction:
The map
r^{C_{p}}_{e}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus p}
is the diagonal embedding:

r^{C_{p}}_{e}(x)=(x,x,\dots,x).

Conjugation:
For a generator
g\in C_{p}
, the conjugation map
c_{g}:\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus p}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus p}
is the cyclic permutation:

c_{g}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p})=(x_{p},x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1}).

Transfer:
The map
t^{C_{p}}_{e}:\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus p}\to\mathfrak{g}
is given by component-wise summation:

t^{C_{p}}_{e}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{p}.

It is a routine work to check that it is a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type.

3.8 Example
.

Let
A
be an associative
R
-algebra equipped with an action of
C_{p}
; equivalently,
A
is an associative algebra over the group ring
R[C_{p}]
.

R
-linear derivations,
Der_{R}(A)
, are linear maps
D:A\rightarrow A
satisfying:

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b).

R[C_{p}]
-linear(invariant) derivations,
Der_{R[C_{p}]}(A)
, are those derivations that commute with the
C_{p}
action:

D(g\cdot a)=g\cdot D(a),\text{ for all}~g\in G,~a\in A.

It is trivial to show that
Der_{R}(A)
and
Der_{R[C_{p}]}(A)
both are lie algebra over the commutative ring
R
under the commutator bracket and also easy to see that
Der_{R[e]}(A)=Der_{R}(A)
.

Now, we define a
C_{p}
-Green functor
L
of Lie type as follows.

For the group
C_{p}
, define
L(C_{p})=Der_{R[C_{p}]}(A)
, and for the trivial group
e
, define
L(e)=Der_{R}(A)
.

The family of structure maps are as follows:

Restriction:
The restriction map
r^{C_{p}}_{e}:Der_{R[C_{p}]}(A)\rightarrow Der_{R}(A)
is inclusion map.

Transfer:
The transfer map
t^{C_{p}}_{e}:Der_{R}(A)\rightarrow Der_{R[C_{p}]}(A)
is define as

t^{C_{p}}_{e}(D)(a)=\sum_{g\in C_{p}}g\cdot D(g^{-1}\cdot a),~~~\text{for all}~a\in A.

Conjugation:
For all
g\in C_{p}
, the conjugation map
C_{g}:Der_{R}(A)\rightarrow Der_{R}(A)
is define as:

c_{g}(D)(a)=g\cdot D(g^{-1}\cdot a),~~~\text{for all }~a\in A.

3.10 Definition
.

Let
M
be a
C_{p}
-Mackey functor and
L
a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type. Then
M
is called a left module over
L
if the following axioms are satisfied:

1.

The components
M(e)
and
M(C_{p})
are left modules over Lie algebras
L(e)
and
L(C_{p})
, respectively.

2.

For all
a\in L(C_{p})
,
m\in M(C_{p})
,
x\in L(e)
, and
u\in M(e)
, the following compatibility conditions hold:

r^{C_{p}}_{e}(a\cdot m)=r^{C_{p}}_{e}(a)\cdot r^{C_{p}}_{e}(m),\qquad c_{g}(x\cdot u)=c_{g}(x)\cdot c_{g}(u).

3.

Frobenius relations
: For all
x\in L(e)
and
m\in M(C_{p})
,

t_{e}^{C_{p}}(x)\cdot m=t_{e}^{C_{p}}(x\cdot r_{e}^{C_{p}}(m)),

and for all
a\in L(C_{p})
and
u\in M(e)
,

a\cdot t_{e}^{C_{p}}(u)=t_{e}^{C_{p}}(r_{e}^{C_{p}}(a)\cdot u).

Similarly, one can define the notion of a right module for a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type. A module that is both a left and a right module is called a
bimodule
.

6
Cup Product

Let
L
be a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type, and let
M
be an
L
-module with having additional structure of a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type. For example, one could take
M=L
via adjoint representation. Define the cup products (pairings) as follows:

\cup_{C_{p}}:C^{m}_{C_{p}}(L,M)\times C^{n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)\longrightarrow C^{m+n}_{C_{p}}(L,M),

\cup_{e}:C^{m}_{e}(L,M)\times C^{n}_{e}(L,M)\longrightarrow C^{m+n}_{e}(L,M).

These products are defined by

((f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\cup_{C_{p}}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e}))((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n}),(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime}))=\left((f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n}),\,(f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime})\right),

and

(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime}).

Here, the products are given explicitly by

\displaystyle(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\text{sign}(\sigma)\cdot[f_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m)}),\ g_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{M(C_{p})},

\displaystyle(f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\text{sign}(\sigma)\cdot[f_{e}(x_{\sigma(1)}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m)}^{\prime}),\ g_{e}(x_{\sigma(m+1)}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n)}^{\prime})]_{M(e)},

\displaystyle(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\text{sign}(\sigma)\cdot[f_{e}(x_{\sigma(1)}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m)}^{\prime}),\ g_{e}(x_{\sigma(m+1)}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n)}^{\prime})]_{M(e)}.

In each case, the sum is taken over all permutations
\sigma
of the set
\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
such that

\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(m)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(m+1)<\cdots<\sigma(m+n).

This operation is called the
cup product.

Let

b_{m+n}^{\prime}:C^{m+n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)\longrightarrow C^{m+n+1}_{C_{p}}(L,M),\qquad b_{m+n}:C^{m+n}_{e}(L,M)\longrightarrow C^{m+n+1}_{e}(L,M)

denote the respective coboundary maps. Then, for all
(f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\in C^{m}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
,
(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})\in C^{n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
, and
f_{e}\in C^{m}_{e}(L,M)
,
g_{e}\in C^{n}_{e}(L,M)
, similar to the computation in
[
15
]
, it is easy to verify the following identity:

b_{m+n}^{\prime}(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})=b_{m}^{\prime}(f_{C_{p}})\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}}+(-1)^{m}f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}b_{n}^{\prime}(g_{C_{p}});

(1)

b_{m+n}^{\prime}(f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})=b_{m}(f_{e})\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e}+(-1)^{m}f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}b_{n}(g_{e});

(2)

b_{m+n}(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})=b_{m}(f_{e})\cup_{e}g_{e}+(-1)^{m}f_{e}\cup_{e}b_{n}(g_{e}).

(3)

6.1 Proposition
.

Let
f=(f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\in Z^{m}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
and
g=(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})\in Z^{n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
. Then their cup product

f\cup_{C_{p}}g=(f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\cup_{C_{p}}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})=(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}},f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})

belongs to
Z^{m+n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
.

Proof.

If
f=(f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\in Z^{m}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}^{\prime}f_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{m+1}^{\prime})\quad+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}f_{e}([x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime}],x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{m+1}^{\prime})=0

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}f_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{m+1})\quad+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}f_{C_{p}}([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{m+1})=0

Compatibility conditions

\displaystyle C_{M}(f_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=f_{e}(C_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,C_{L}(x_{m}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle r_{M}f_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=f_{e}(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m}))

\displaystyle f_{C_{p}}(t_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=t_{M}(f_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},r_{L}(x_{2}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m})))

\displaystyle f_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},t_{L}(x_{m}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=t_{M}(f_{e}(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m-1}),x_{m}^{\prime})).

Similarly, if
g=(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})\in Z^{n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}^{\prime}g_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{n+1}^{\prime})\quad+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}g_{e}([x_{i}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime}],x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}^{\prime}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{n+1}^{\prime})=0;

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}g_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})\quad+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}g_{C_{p}}([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1})=0.

For compatibility conditions, we have

\displaystyle C_{M}(g_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=g_{e}(C_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,C_{L}(x_{n}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle r_{M}g_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{e}(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{n}))

\displaystyle g_{C_{p}}(t_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=t_{M}(g_{e}(x_{1}^{\prime},r_{L}(x_{2}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{n})))

\displaystyle g_{C_{p}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},t_{L}(x_{n}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=t_{M}(g_{e}(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{n-1}),x_{n}^{\prime})).

Now, we compute

\displaystyle r_{M}(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)r_{M}[f_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m)}),g_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{M(C_{p})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)[f_{e}(r_{L}(x_{\sigma(1)}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{\sigma(m)})),g_{e}(r_{L}(x_{\sigma(m+1)}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{\sigma(m+n)}))]_{M(e)}

\displaystyle=(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m+n})).

Thus,

r_{M}(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n})=(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m+n})).

Similarly,

C_{M}(f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m+n}^{\prime})=(f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(C_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,C_{L}(x_{m+n}^{\prime})).

Now,

\displaystyle t_{M}((f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},r_{L}(x_{2}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m+n})))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)t_{M}[f_{e}(x_{\sigma(1)}^{\prime},r_{L}(x_{\sigma(2)}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{\sigma(m)})),g_{e}(r_{L}(x_{\sigma(m+1)}),\ldots)]_{M(e)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)[f_{C_{p}}(t_{L}(x_{\sigma(1)}^{\prime}),x_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m)}),g_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\ldots)]_{M(C_{p})}

\displaystyle=(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(t_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{m+n}).

Hence,

t_{M}((f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(x_{1}^{\prime},r_{L}(x_{2}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m+n})))=(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(t_{L}(x_{1}^{\prime}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{m+n}).

Similarly,

t_{M}((f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})(r_{L}(x_{1}),\ldots,r_{L}(x_{m-1}),x_{m+n}^{\prime}))=(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},t_{L}(x_{m+n}^{\prime})).

Therefore, if
f\in Z^{m}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
and
g\in Z^{n}_{C_{p}}(L,M)
, then by equations (3), (4), (5), and the above relations,
f\cup_{C_{p}}g
satisfies all the conditions for an
(m+n)
-cocycle. That is,

f\cup_{C_{p}}g=(f_{C_{p}},f_{e})\cup_{C_{p}}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})=(f_{C_{p}}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{C_{p}},f_{e}\cup_{C_{p}}g_{e})\in Z^{m+n}_{C_{p}}(L,M).

∎

The definition of transfer map

and restriction map

\displaystyle T_{m+n}(f_{e}\cup_{e}R_{n}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e}))

\displaystyle t_{M}(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})r_{L}^{m+n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m+n})

\displaystyle=t_{M}(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})(r_{L}(x_{1}),\cdots,r_{L}(x_{m}),\cdots,r_{L}(x_{m+n}))

\displaystyle=t_{M}(\sum_{\sigma}sgn(\sigma)[f_{e}r_{L}^{m}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m)}),g_{e}r_{L}^{n}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{M(e)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}sgn(\sigma)t_{M}[f_{e}r_{L}^{m}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m)}),r_{M}^{n}g_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{M(e)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}sgn(\sigma)[t_{M}f_{e}r_{L}^{m}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m)}),g_{C_{p}}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{M_{C_{p}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=p}C^{i}_{M}(f_{e}\cup_{e}g_{e})C^{j}_{L}(x_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},\cdots,x_{m+n}^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=p}C^{i}_{M}(\sum_{\sigma}sgn(\sigma)[f_{e}C^{j}_{L}(x_{\sigma(1)}^{{}^{\prime}},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m)}^{{}^{\prime}}),g_{e}C_{L}^{j}(x_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m+n)}^{{}^{\prime}})]_{M(e)})

T_{m}(f_{e})\cup_{C_{p}}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})=T_{m+n}(f_{e}\cup_{e}R_{n}(g_{C_{p}},g_{e})).

Similarly, one shows that

Hence, the cup product satisfies all the pairing conditions.
∎

7.6 Proposition
.

Let

be a
C_{p}
-Chevalley–Eilenberg 2-cocycle associated with a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type. Then one can construct a new
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type, denoted

, defined as follows.

The value at

is the Lie algebra

, equipped with the bracket

Similarly, at the trivial subgroup

, we define

with the bracket

\langle(m_{3},a_{3}),(m_{4},a_{4})\rangle=\left(a_{3}m_{4}-a_{4}m_{3}+f_{e}(a_{3},a_{4}),[a_{3},a_{4}]\right).

The structure maps are defined componentwise:

c_{B}(m,a)=(c_{M}(m),c_{L}(a)),\quad t_{B}(m,a)=(t_{M}(m),t_{L}(a)),\quad r_{B}(u,x)=(r_{M}(u),r_{L}(x)).

The relationships among these values and maps are summarized in the diagram:

B_{f_{e}}(e)
B_{f_{C_{p}}}(C_{p})
t_{B}
r_{B}
c_{B}

Proof.

We must verify that
B_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}
satisfies the axioms of a
C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type.

First, note that both
B_{f_{C_{p}}}(C_{p})
and
B_{f_{e}}(e)
are Lie algebras by construction, since
f_{C_{p}}
and
f_{e}
are 2-cocycles. We begin by checking that the conjugation map
c_{B}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Let
(m,a),(n,b)\in B_{f_{e}}(e)
. Then:

\displaystyle c_{B}\big(\langle(m,a),(n,b)\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=c_{B}\left(an-bm+f_{e}(a,b),[a,b]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(c_{M}(an-bm+f_{e}(a,b)),c_{L}([a,b])\right)

\displaystyle=\left(c_{L}(a)c_{M}(n)-c_{L}(b)c_{M}(m)+c_{M}(f_{e}(a,b)),[c_{L}(a),c_{L}(b)]\right).

On the other hand, we compute:

\displaystyle\langle c_{B}(m,a),c_{B}(n,b)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(c_{M}(m),c_{L}(a)),(c_{M}(n),c_{L}(b))\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(c_{L}(a)c_{M}(n)-c_{L}(b)c_{M}(m)+f_{e}(c_{L}(a),c_{L}(b)),[c_{L}(a),c_{L}(b)]\right).

By condition (3) of remark (

) (which describes the Chevalley–Eilenberg cocycle compatibility), it follows that these expressions are equal, so

is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Next, we verify that the restriction map

is also a Lie algebra homomorphism. Let

\displaystyle r_{B}\big(\langle(u,x),(v,y)\rangle\big)

\displaystyle=r_{B}\left(xv-yu+f_{C_{p}}(x,y),[x,y]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r_{L}(x)r_{M}(v)-r_{L}(y)r_{M}(u)+r_{M}(f_{C_{p}}(x,y)),r_{L}([x,y])\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r_{L}(x)r_{M}(v)-r_{L}(y)r_{M}(u)+f_{e}(r_{L}(x),r_{L}(y)),[r_{L}(x),r_{L}(y)]\right),

where the final equality uses condition (4) of remark (

\displaystyle=\langle(r_{M}(u),r_{L}(x)),(r_{M}(v),r_{L}(y))\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(r_{L}(x)r_{M}(v)-r_{L}(y)r_{M}(u)+f_{e}(r_{L}(x),r_{L}(y)),[r_{L}(x),r_{L}(y)]\right).

-Green functor of Lie type and condition (5) of remark (

).

).

Therefore, all axioms are satisfied, and

is indeed a

-Green functor of Lie type.

7.7 Theorem
.

Let

and let

be an

-bimodule. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the elements of

and the elements of the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{Green}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(C_{p})}(L,M)
.

Proof.

To prove the theorem, we proceed as follows:

(a)

(b)

Next, we construct a well-defined map in the reverse direction,

H^{2}_{\mathrm{Green}_{\mathrm{Lie}}(C_{p})}(L,M)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ext}(L,M).

(c)

Finally, we show that these two maps are inverses of each other.

We now proceed to prove the theorem step by step:

(a)

C_{p}
-Green functor of Lie type conditions for
f_{C_{p}}
and
f_{e}
ensure that these brackets satisfy the Jacobi identity and antisymmetry. Hence,
B_{f_{C_{p}}}(C_{p})
and
B_{f_{e}}(e)
are Lie algebras.

We now define the maps

\displaystyle i_{C_{p}}

\displaystyle:M(C_{p})\longrightarrow B_{f_{C_{p}}}(C_{p}),

\displaystyle i_{C_{p}}(u)

\displaystyle=(u,0),

\displaystyle i_{e}

\displaystyle:M(e)\longrightarrow B_{f_{e}}(e),

\displaystyle i_{e}(m)

\displaystyle=(m,0),

\displaystyle j_{C_{p}}

\displaystyle:B_{f_{C_{p}}}(C_{p})\longrightarrow L(C_{p}),

\displaystyle j_{C_{p}}(u,x)

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle j_{e}

\displaystyle:B_{f_{e}}(e)\longrightarrow L(e),

\displaystyle j_{e}(m,a)

\displaystyle=a.

Here,
i_{C_{p}}
and
i_{e}
are homomorphisms of
C_{p}
-Mackey functors, and
j_{C_{p}}
and
j_{e}
are homomorphisms of
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type.

Thus, the sequence

E_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}:0\to M\overset{i}{\to}B\overset{j}{\underset{s}{\rightleftarrows}}L\to 0

is exact.

For any
x\in L(C_{p})
and
a\in L(e)
, define the sections
s_{C_{p}}(x)=(0,x)
and
s_{e}(a)=(0,a)
. Then, for
x,y\in L(C_{p})
, we compute

\displaystyle[s_{C_{p}}(x),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[(0,x),(0,y)]_{B}

\displaystyle=(f_{C_{p}}(x,y),[x,y]_{L})

\displaystyle=(f_{C_{p}}(x,y),0)+(0,[x,y]_{L})

\displaystyle=i_{C_{p}}(f_{C_{p}}(x,y))+s_{C_{p}}([x,y]_{L}).

Similarly, for
a,b\in L(e)
, we have

\displaystyle[s_{e}(a),s_{e}(b)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[(0,a),(0,b)]_{B}

\displaystyle=(f_{e}(a,b),[a,b]_{L})

\displaystyle=(f_{e}(a,b),0)+(0,[a,b]_{L})

\displaystyle=i_{e}(f_{e}(a,b))+s_{e}([a,b]_{L}).

Therefore, we conclude that the chosen sections
s_{C_{p}}
and
s_{e}
yield the Chevalley–Eilenberg 2-cocycles
f_{C_{p}}
and
f_{e}
for the
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type.

Conversely, suppose the complex

E:0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{i}B\xrightarrow{j}L\longrightarrow 0

is an extension, and let
f_{C_{p}}
and
f_{e}
be the Chevalley–Eilenberg 2-cocycles of
C_{p}
-Green functors of Lie type obtained from this extension. We now show that the extension

E_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}:0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{i}B_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}\xrightarrow{j}L\longrightarrow 0

associated with
f_{C_{p}}
and
f_{e}
is equivalent to the given extension
E
.

The extensions
E
and
E_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}
are equivalent if there exists a homomorphism

\tau_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}:B_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}\longrightarrow B

such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

From the commutativity of the diagram, we have

\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}(u,x)=i_{C_{p}}(u)+s_{C_{p}}(x),\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{f_{e}}(m,a)=i_{e}(m)+s_{e}(a),

where
u\in M(C_{p})
,
x\in L(C_{p})
,
m\in M(e)
, and
a\in L(e)
.

It remains to verify that the maps
\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}
and
\tau_{f_{e}}
are Lie algebra homomorphisms.

Let
(u,x),(v,y)\in B(C_{p})
. Then we compute

\displaystyle\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}([(u,x),(v,y)]_{B_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}(xv-yu+f_{C_{p}}(x,y),[x,y]_{L})

\displaystyle=i_{C_{p}}(xv)-i_{C_{p}}(yu)+i_{C_{p}}(f_{C_{p}}(x,y))+s_{C_{p}}([x,y]_{L})

\displaystyle=[s_{C_{p}}(x),i_{C_{p}}(v)]_{B}+[i_{C_{p}}(u),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}+[s_{C_{p}}(x),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle[\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}(u,x),\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}(v,y)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[(i_{C_{p}}(u)+s_{C_{p}}(x)),(i_{C_{p}}(v)+s_{C_{p}}(y))]_{B}

\displaystyle=[i_{C_{p}}(u),i_{C_{p}}(v)]_{B}+[s_{C_{p}}(x),i_{C_{p}}(v)]_{B}+[i_{C_{p}}(u),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}+[s_{C_{p}}(x),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[s_{C_{p}}(x),i_{C_{p}}(v)]_{B}+[i_{C_{p}}(u),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}+[s_{C_{p}}(x),s_{C_{p}}(y)]_{B}.

Thus,
\tau_{f_{C_{p}}}
is a Lie homomorphism.

Similarly, let
(m,a),(n,b)\in B(e)
. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{f_{e}}([(m,a),(n,b)]_{B_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{f_{e}}(an-bm+f_{e}(a,b),[a,b]_{L})

\displaystyle=i_{e}(an)-i_{e}(bm)+i_{e}(f_{e}(a,b))+s_{e}([a,b]_{L})

\displaystyle=[s_{e}(a),i_{e}(n)]_{B}+[i_{e}(m),s_{e}(b)]_{B}+[s_{e}(a),s_{e}(b)]_{B}.

And

\displaystyle[\tau_{f_{e}}(m,a),\tau_{f_{e}}(n,b)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[(i_{e}(m)+s_{e}(a)),(i_{e}(n)+s_{e}(b))]_{B}

\displaystyle=[i_{e}(m),i_{e}(n)]_{B}+[s_{e}(a),i_{e}(n)]_{B}+[i_{e}(m),s_{e}(b)]_{B}+[s_{e}(a),s_{e}(b)]_{B}

\displaystyle=[s_{e}(a),i_{e}(n)]_{B}+[i_{e}(m),s_{e}(b)]_{B}+[s_{e}(a),s_{e}(b)]_{B}.

Therefore,
\tau_{f_{C_{p}},f_{e}}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Hence, the theorem is proved.

Transcendence criteria for multidimensional continued fractions
Federico Accossato, Nadir Murru, Giuliano Romeo

Abstract
. Classical results on Diophantine approximation, such as Roth’s theorem, provide the most effective techniques for proving the transcendence of special kinds of continued fractions. Multidimensional continued fractions are a generalization of classical continued fractions, introduced by Jacobi, and there are many well-studied open problems related to them. In this paper, we establish transcendence criteria for multidimensional continued fractions. In particular, we show that some Liouville-type and quasi-periodic multidimensional continued fractions are transcendental. We also obtain an upper bound on the naive height of cubic irrationals arising from periodic multidimensional continued fractions and exploit it to prove the transcendence criteria in the quasi-periodic case.

11J70; 11J81; 11Y65

1.
Introduction

In 1844, Liouville
[
20
]
constructed the first example of transcendental numbers by using the properties of continued fractions. Liouville proved that algebraic numbers admit only finitely many “good” approximations and used continued fractions to construct some real numbers that are approximated “too well” to be algebraic. These continued fractions are usually referred as
Liouville-type
continued fractions, and they have unbounded partial quotients that increase very fast.
The first transcendental continued fractions with bounded partial quotients have been defined by Maillet
[
22
]
and Baker
[
6
]
. These are called
quasi-periodic
continued fractions, as they “mimic” the behavior of periodic continued fractions. Indeed, these continued fractions have same blocks of partial quotients that repeat infinitely many times. Quasi-periodic continued fractions are transcendental as they can be approximated too well by infinitely many quadratic irrationals. See also
[
4
]
for several improvements of the results of Maillet and Baker. These kinds of continued fractions have also been exploited in the framework of
p
-adic continued fractions to prove the transcendence of
p
-adic numbers
[
21
,
19
,
23
]
(see also Section 8 of
[
26
]
).

Since 2005, Adamczewski and Bugeaud followed up the work started by Maillet and Baker by proving transcendence criteria for several families of continued fractions, including
stammering
continued fractions
[
2
]
,
palindromic
continued fractions
[
3
]
, irrational
automatic
numbers
[
1
]
and other continued fractions arising from well-known sequences
[
5
,
25
]
. The main tools used in their proofs are Roth’s theorem
[
27
]
and its generalization due to Schmidt, i.e., the Subspace Theorem
[
28
]
. In 2013, Bugeaud
[
12
]
proved a transcendence criterion for
automatic
continued fractions, including all previous families. The results also imply that the continued fraction expansions of algebraic irrationals is either periodic, and hence it represents a quadratic irrational, or it can not be too “simple”. A famous conjecture states that the continued fraction of any algebraic irrational of degree greater than two contains arbitrarily large partial quotients. Another related conjecture, due to Badziahin and Shallit
[
9
]
, claims that every continued fraction where the terms form a
k
-regular sequence of positive integers is transcendental or quadratic.

Lagrange’s theorem for classical continued fractions states that a real number has an eventually periodic continued fraction if and only if it is a quadratic irrational. Multidimensional continued fractions (MCF) have been introduced for the first time by Jacobi
[
16
]
in order to answer a question posed by Hermite
[
15
]
. The aim was to obtain an analogue of Lagrange’s theorem for cubic irrationals. The work of Jacobi has been lately generalized by Perron
[
24
]
to deal with algebraic irrationalities of any degree.
Hermite’s problem of finding a periodic continued fraction for algebraic irrationals of degree greater than two is still open (see
[
17
,
18
]
for some recent progresses).

In this paper, we start to develop the analysis of transcendental continued fractions of higher dimension. We prove several transcendence criteria for general multidimensional continued fractions, mainly inspired by the results of Liouville and the works of Maillet and Baker. More precise results are obtained in the bidimensional case of Jacobi’s algorithm. To our knowledge, the only works related to this topic so far are due to Tamura
[
32
,
33
]
, where only very specific MCF expansions were considered. The methodology developed in this paper aims to provide a better understanding of the multidimensional continued fractions of algebraic and transcendental numbers. Some of the results are interesting on their own and can be read independently of the rest of the paper. In particular, Theorem
16
provides a bound on the height of cubic irrationals corresponding to periodic MCFs.

In Section
2
, we review the definition of Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm and the basic known properties.
In Section
3
, we prove some new results for MCFs that are exploited for proving the results of the next sections.
In Section
4
, we establish a bound on the height of cubic irrationals having a periodic Jacobi’s MCF. In Section
5
and Section
6
, we prove the transcendence of, respectively,
Liouville-type
and
quasi-periodic
MCF. We also provide an estimate for the growth of the denominators of convergents for an MCF. Section
7
is devoted to some open questions.

2.
Multidimensional continued fractions

In this section we recall some basic facts about multidimensional continued fractions and fix the notation. For the general theory, we refer the interested readers to
[
8
,
30
]
. We first introduce Jacobi’s algorithm and then we recall its generalization due to Perron.

2.1.
Jacobi’s algorithm

Given
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, Jacobi’s algorithm provides two sequences of integers by means of the following recurrences:

(1)

\begin{cases}a_{n}=\lfloor\alpha_{n}\rfloor\cr b_{n}=\lfloor\beta_{n}\rfloor\cr\alpha_{n+1}=\cfrac{1}{\beta_{n}-b_{n}}\cr\beta_{n+1}=\cfrac{\alpha_{n}-a_{n}}{\beta_{n}-b_{n}}\end{cases}

for
n\geq 0
.

The
a_{n}
’s and
b_{n}
’s are called
partial quotients
, the
\alpha_{n}
’s and
\beta_{n}
’s are called
complete quotients
. The two sequences
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are called a
multidimensional continued fraction
(MCF) and we write

(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]

for indicating that the MCF is obtained starting from
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
.

Remark 1
.

Note that the algorithm can be performed as long as the
\beta_{n}
’s are not integers. This is generally referred to as the
simple case
, where two infinite sequences of integers are provided.

If this is not the case, let
n_{0}
be the first index such that
\beta_{n_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the
a_{i}
’s, with
i>n_{0}
, are generated by evaluating the classical continued fraction of
\alpha_{n_{0}}
and we say that
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n_{0}},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n_{0}})]
is the MCF for
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
, obtained through Jacobi’s algorithm.
If the sequence of the
a_{i}
’s is infinite, we say that the procedure presents one interruption, while we say that two interruptions occur if both sequences of partial quotients are finite.

Perron
[
24
]
showed that, if the algorithm admits
l
interruptions, then there exist
l
independent relations over
\mathbb{Z}
occurring between
1,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}
. The converse holds as well
[
14
]
.

From (
1
), a necessary condition for two sequences of integers
(a_{i})_{i\geq 0}
,
(b_{i})_{i\geq 0}
to be obtained by applying Jacobi’s algorithm to a certain couple of real numbers
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
is that, for all
n\geq 1
,

(2)

a_{n}\geq 1,\quad b_{n}\geq 0,\quad a_{n}\geq b_{n},

and
b_{n+1}\geq 1
in the case that
a_{n}=b_{n}
. In
[
24
]
, Perron observed that such conditions are also sufficient. For this reason, they are called
admissibility conditions
for the sequences of partial quotients.

Given to a MCF as before, we define the sequences
(A_{n})_{n\geq-3},(B_{n})_{n\geq-3},(C_{n})_{n\geq-3}
as follows:

\begin{cases}A_{-3}=0,\quad A_{-2}=0,\quad A_{-1}=1\cr B_{-3}=0,\quad B_{-2}=1,\quad B_{-1}=0\cr C_{-3}=1,\quad C_{-2}=0,\quad C_{-1}=0,\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}A_{n}=a_{n}A_{n-1}+b_{n}A_{n-2}+A_{n-3}\cr B_{n}=a_{n}B_{n-1}+b_{n}B_{n-2}+B_{n-3}\cr C_{n}=a_{n}C_{n-1}+b_{n}C_{n-2}+C_{n-3},\end{cases}

for all
n\geq 0
. The sequences
(A_{n}/C_{n})_{n\geq 0},(B_{n}/C_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are called
convergents
of the MCF. They can be also obtained by

(3)

\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&1&0\cr b_{0}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&1&0\cr b_{1}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}&1&0\cr b_{n}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}&A_{n-1}&A_{n-2}\cr B_{n}&B_{n-1}&B_{n-2}\cr C_{n}&C_{n-1}&C_{n-2}\end{pmatrix},

for all
n\geq 0
.

We recall now some useful properties of multidimensional continued fractions.

In the following lemma, we see that the sequences of convergents of a MCF obtained starting from the pair of real numbers
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
actually converge to them. The proof can be found, e.g, in
[
8
]
, but we report it for completeness, since our notation is slightly different and it will be used later.

Lemma 2

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
be an infinite MCF such that the partial quotients satisfy (
2
), then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\cfrac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}=\alpha_{0},\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\cfrac{B_{n}}{C_{n}}=\beta_{0}.

Proof.

We see the proof only for the convergents
(A_{n}/C_{n})_{n\geq-2}
.
Define

m_{k}:=\min\left(\cfrac{A_{k}}{C_{k}},\cfrac{A_{k+1}}{C_{k+1}},\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}\right),\quad M_{k}:=\max\left(\cfrac{A_{k}}{C_{k}},\cfrac{A_{k+1}}{C_{k+1}},\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}\right),

for all
k\geq 0
. We have

\cfrac{A_{k}}{C_{k}}=\cfrac{a_{k}A_{k-1}+b_{k}A_{k-2}+A_{k-3}}{C_{k}}=s_{k}\cfrac{A_{k-1}}{C_{k-1}}+t_{k}\cfrac{A_{k-2}}{C_{k-2}}+w_{k}\cfrac{A_{k-3}}{C_{k-3}},

where

s_{k}:=\cfrac{a_{k}C_{k-1}}{C_{k}},\quad t_{k}:=\cfrac{b_{k}C_{k-2}}{C_{k}},\quad w_{k}:=\cfrac{C_{k-3}}{C_{k}},

for all
k\geq 0
. Observe that

s_{k}+t_{k}+u_{k}=1,\quad\cfrac{t_{k}}{s_{k}}\leq 1,\quad\cfrac{u_{k}}{s_{k}}\leq 1,\quad s_{k}\geq 1/3,

for all
k\geq 0
. Now, we have

\cfrac{A_{k+3}}{C_{k+3}}\geq s_{k+3}m_{k}+t_{k+3}m_{k}+u_{k+3}m_{k}=m_{k},

and similarly

\cfrac{A_{k+3}}{C_{k+3}}\leq M_{k},

for all
k\geq 0
. Moreover, since
m_{k+1}\geq m_{k}
and
M_{k+1}\leq M_{k}
, we obtain

0\leq m_{k}\leq m_{k+1}\leq M_{k+1}\leq M_{k},

for all
k\geq 0
. Thus, the sequence
(m_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a non-decreasing sequence bounded by
M_{0}
and the sequence
(M_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a non-increasing sequence bounded by
m_{0}
and

m:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}m_{k},\quad M:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}M_{k}.

Hence, for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k>k_{0}
, we have
m_{k}>m
, i.e.,

\cfrac{A_{k}}{C_{k}},\cfrac{A_{k+1}}{C_{k+1}},\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}>m-\epsilon

and

\displaystyle\cfrac{A_{k+3}}{C_{k+3}}

\displaystyle>(t_{k+3}+u_{k+3})(m-\epsilon)+s_{k+3}\cdot\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}=

\displaystyle=(1-s_{k+3})(m-\epsilon)+s_{k+3}\cdot\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}\geq m-\epsilon+s_{k+3}\left(\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}-m\right).

Hence,

\cfrac{A_{k+3}}{C_{k+3}}-m>s_{k+3}\left(\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}-m\right)-\epsilon>\cfrac{1}{4}\left(\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}-m\right)-\epsilon,

from which

\cfrac{A_{k+3+h}}{C_{k+3+h}}-m>\cfrac{1}{4^{h+1}}\left(\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}-m\right)-\epsilon\left(1+\cfrac{1}{4}+\ldots+\cfrac{1}{4^{h}}\right),

for all
h\geq 0
. Now, given
k\geq k_{0}
and fixed
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let
k_{1},k_{2}
be such that
m_{k_{1}}=\cfrac{A_{k+3+h}}{C_{k+3+h}}
and
M_{k_{2}}=\cfrac{A_{k+2}}{C_{k+2}}
. We have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=m-m>m_{k_{1}}-m=\cfrac{A_{k+3+h}}{C_{k+3+h}}-m>\cfrac{1}{4^{h+1}}(M_{k_{2}}-m)-\epsilon\left(1+\cfrac{1}{4}+\ldots+\cfrac{1}{4^{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\cfrac{1}{4^{h+1}}(M-m)-\epsilon\left(1+\cfrac{1}{4}+\ldots+\cfrac{1}{4^{h}}\right).

From which

\epsilon>\cfrac{M-m}{4^{h+1}(1+1/4+\ldots 1/4^{h})},

and since
\epsilon
is arbitrarily small we must have
m=M
.
∎

By Lemma
2
, we are therefore allowed to write the following equalities.

(4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=a_{0}+\cfrac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}=a_{0}+\cfrac{b_{1}+\cfrac{1}{\alpha_{2}}}{a_{1}+\cfrac{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}}=a_{0}+\cfrac{b_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\ddots}}{a_{1}+\cfrac{b_{2}+\ddots}{a_{2}+\ddots}},

(5)

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=b_{0}+\cfrac{1}{\alpha_{1}}=b_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}}=b_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{b_{2}+\ddots}{a_{2}+\ddots}}.

To a MCF with partial quotients
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, we attach the auxiliary sequences
(\tilde{A}_{n})_{n\geq-2}
,
(\tilde{B_{n}})_{n\geq-2}
,
(\tilde{U_{n}})_{n\geq-2}
,
(\tilde{\tilde{A}}_{n})_{n\geq-1}
,
(\tilde{\tilde{B_{n}}})_{n\geq-1}
,
(\tilde{\tilde{U_{n}}})_{n\geq-1}
defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}C_{n-1}-C_{n}A_{n-1},\quad\tilde{B}_{n}=B_{n}C_{n-1}-B_{n-1}C_{n},\quad\tilde{U}_{n}=A_{n}B_{n-1}-A_{n-1}B_{n},

\displaystyle\tilde{\tilde{A}}_{n}

\displaystyle=A_{n}C_{n-2}-C_{n}A_{n-2},\quad\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{n}=B_{n}C_{n-2}-B_{n-2}C_{n},\quad\tilde{\tilde{U_{n}}}=A_{n}B_{n-2}-A_{n-2}B_{n}.

where
A_{n},B_{n}
and
C_{n}
are numerators and denominators of the
n
-th convergent introduced above.

Lemma 3
.

The sequences
(\tilde{A}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\tilde{B}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfy

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=-b_{n}\tilde{A}_{n-1}-a_{n-1}\tilde{A}_{n-2}+\tilde{A}_{n-3},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{n}

\displaystyle=-b_{n}\tilde{B}_{n-1}-a_{n-1}\tilde{B}_{n-2}+\tilde{B}_{n-3},

for all
n\geq 1
, with initial conditions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{A}_{-2}=0,\quad\tilde{A}_{-1}=0,\quad\tilde{A}_{0}=-1,\cr\tilde{B}_{-2}=1,\quad\tilde{B}_{-1}=0,\quad\tilde{B}_{0}=0.\end{cases}

Moreover,

|\tilde{A}_{n}|\leq C_{n},\quad|\tilde{B}_{n}|\leq C_{n},\quad|\tilde{\tilde{A}}_{n}|\leq C_{n},\quad|\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{n}|\leq C_{n}

for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

See
[
31
, Chapter 9]
.
∎

2.2.
Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm

Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm generalizes Jacobi’s and works on a
m
-tuple of real numbers
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})
and it provides the multidimensional continued fraction as the
m
-tuple of integer sequences

[(a_{n}^{(1)})_{n\geq 0},\ldots,(a_{n}^{(m)})_{n\geq 0}],

generated by the iteration of the following equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{n}^{(i)}=\lfloor\alpha_{n}^{(i)}\rfloor,\ \ i=1,\ldots,m\\
\alpha_{n+1}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{(m)}-a_{n}^{(m)}}\\
\alpha_{n+1}^{(i)}=\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(i-1)}-a_{n}^{(i-1)}}{\alpha_{n}^{(m)}-a_{n}^{(m)}},\ \ i=2,\ldots,m.\end{cases}

for all
n\geq 0
. Sometimes, we refer to these MCFs as
m
-dimensional continued fractions. Notice that, when
m=2
, the algorithm reduces to Jacobi’s algorithm and
\alpha_{n}^{(1)}=\alpha_{n}
,
\alpha_{n}^{(2)}=\beta_{n}
.

As in Remark
1
, notice that if
\alpha_{n_{0}}^{(m)}
is integer for some index
n_{0}
, then the algorithm has an interruption, and in this case the procedure continues by taking in input the
(m-1)
-uple
(\alpha_{n_{0}}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{n_{0}}^{(m-1)})
. Also in this case, when one single input is given, the procedure essentially reduces to the algorithm for classical continued fractions.

Again,
[
14
]
is a good reference for the analysis of the connections between the number of interruptions in the algorithm and the number of independent rational relations between
1,\alpha_{0}^{(i)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)}
. We just recall that, if
1,\alpha_{0}^{(i)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)}
are rationally independent, then no interruption is present (i.e., the sequences of partial quotients are all infinite). The converse is not true in general.

For Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm, we still have the following admissibility conditions on the partial quotients:

(6)

(a_{n}^{(1)},a_{n+1}^{(2)},\ldots,a_{n+i}^{(i+1)})\succeq(a_{n}^{(m-i)},a_{n+1}^{(m-i+1)},\ldots,a_{n+1-i}^{(m)},1),

for
0\leq i\leq m-1
and for all
n\geq 1
, where
\succeq
denotes the lexicographic order (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
), and

a_{n}^{(j)}\geq 0

for
1\leq j\leq m
and for all
n\geq 1
.

Also in this case we introduce the sequence of convergents

(A_{n}^{(i)}/C_{n})_{n\geq 0},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
i=1,\ldots,m
, where the numerators
A_{n}^{(i)}
are defined as follows:

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}A_{-n}^{(i)}=\delta_{in},\quad n=1,\ldots,m+1\\
A_{n}^{(i)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n}^{(j)}A_{n-j}^{(i)}+A_{n-m-1}^{(i)},\quad n\geq 0,\end{cases}

and the denominators
C_{n}
are defined as

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}C_{-m-1}=1,\\
C_{-n}=0,\quad\textup{for }n=1,\ldots,m\\
C_{n}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n}^{(j)}C_{n-j}+C_{n-m-1},\quad\text{for }n\geq 0.\end{cases}

Again, it is possible to prove that, for every
i=1,\ldots,m
,

(9)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\cfrac{A_{n}^{(i)}}{C_{n}}=\alpha_{0}^{(i)},

thus allowing us to write

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha_{n}^{(i-1)}=a_{n}^{(i-1)}+\frac{\alpha_{n+1}^{(i)}}{\alpha_{n+1}^{(1)}},\ \ i=2,\ldots,m\\
\alpha_{n}^{(m)}=a_{n}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n+1}^{(1)}},\end{cases}

which generalize the expressions in (
4
) and (
5
).

Remark 4
.

When
m
sequences
[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)]
of real numbers, not necessarily integers, are given, one can always construct the convergents by using (
7
) and (
8
). If (
9
) is satisfied, we still say that
[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)]
is a MCF converging to
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})
.

Therefore, it can be easily proved that, if

(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})=[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)],

then, for every
k\geq 1
,

(10)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{k}^{(m)})=[(a_{k}^{(1)},a_{k+1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{k}^{(m)},a_{k+1}^{(m)},\dots)]

(11)

and

\displaystyle(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})=[(a_{0}^{(1)},\dots,a_{k-1}^{(1)},\alpha_{k}^{(1)}),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},\dots,a_{k-1}^{(m)},\alpha_{k}^{(m)})].

Moreover, for every
i=1,\dots,m
, it holds that

(12)

\alpha_{0}^{(i)}=\cfrac{\alpha_{k}^{(1)}A_{k-1}^{(i)}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}^{(m)}A_{k-m}^{(i)}+A_{k-m-1}^{(i)}}{\alpha_{k}^{(1)}C_{k-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}^{(m)}C_{k-m}+C_{k-m-1}}.

Finally, suppose that
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})=[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)]
is a MCF obtained through Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm. Then, for every
k\geq 0
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}a_{k}^{(1)}&1&0&\cdots&0\cr a_{k}^{(2)}&0&1&\cdots&0\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr a_{k}^{(m)}&0&0&\cdots&1\cr 1&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\cr\alpha_{k+1}^{(2)}\cr\vdots\cr\alpha_{k+1}^{(m)}\cr 1\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}a_{k}^{(1)}\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{k+1}^{(2)}\cr a_{k}^{(2)}\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}+\alpha_{k+1}^{(3)}\cr\vdots\cr a_{k}^{(m)}\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}+1\cr\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}=\\
&=\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\begin{pmatrix}a_{k}^{(1)}+\frac{\alpha_{k+1}^{(2)}}{\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}}\cr a_{k}^{(2)}+\frac{\alpha_{k+1}^{(3)}}{\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}}\cr\vdots\cr a_{k}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}}\cr 1\end{pmatrix}=\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k}^{(1)}\cr\alpha_{k}^{(2)}\cr\vdots\cr\alpha_{k}^{(m)}\cr 1\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Equation (
13
) leads to the following identity, which will be useful in Section
4
:

(14)

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}\cr\vdots\cr\alpha_{0}^{(m)}\cr 1\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\ldots\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}}M_{0}M_{1}\cdots M_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\cr\vdots\cr\alpha_{k+1}^{(m)}\cr 1\end{pmatrix},

where, for every
n
,

(15)

M_{n}^{(m)}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}^{(1)}&1&\cdots&0\cr\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr a_{n}^{(m)}&0&\cdots&1\cr 1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

As we did in the previous section for Jacobi’s MCF, we attach to a Jacobi–Perron’s MCF with partial quotients
(a_{n}^{(i)})_{n\geq 0}
, for
i=1,\ldots,m
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the auxiliary sequences

\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}=A_{n}^{(i)}C_{n-1}-A_{n-1}^{(i)}C_{n}.

Remark 5
.

For the sequences
\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}
in the
m
-dimensional case we did not find an elegant recursion as for Jacobi’s algorithm in Lemma
3
. However, we can notice that also in this case, each
\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}
does not depend on
a_{n}^{(1)}
, since

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}

\displaystyle=A^{(i)}_{n}C_{n-1}-A^{(i)}_{n-1}C_{n}=(a_{n}^{(1)}A^{(i)}_{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n}^{(m)}A^{(i)}_{n-m}+A^{(i)}_{n-m-1})C_{n-1}-

\displaystyle\ \ \ -A_{n-1}(a_{n}^{(1)}C_{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n}^{(m)}C_{n-m}+C_{n-m-1})=

\displaystyle=a_{n}^{(2)}(A_{n-2}C_{n-1}-A_{n-1}C_{n-2})+\ldots+a_{n}^{(m)}(A_{n-m}C_{n}-A_{n}C_{n-m})+

\displaystyle\ \ \ +(A_{n-m-1}C_{n-1}-A_{n-1}C_{n-m-1}).

Notation
.

For the sake of simplicity, when dealing with the case
m=2
, throughout the paper we prefer to write
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n},a_{n},b_{n},A_{n},B_{n}
, instead of
\alpha_{n}^{(1)},\alpha_{n}^{(2)},a_{n}^{(1)},a_{n}^{(2)}
,
A_{n}^{(1)},A_{n}^{(2)}
.

3.
Auxiliary results

In this section, we prove some results that are used in the proofs of the main transcendence criteria.

Lemma 6
.

Given
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})=[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)]
, then, for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,

\left|\alpha_{0}^{(i)}-\frac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\frac{|\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n+1}|}{C_{n+1}C_{n}},

for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
2
for the case
m=2
, it is possible to see that, setting for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,

\displaystyle m^{(i)}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\min\left(\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}},\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}},\ldots,\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n+m}}{C_{n+m}}\right),

\displaystyle M^{(i)}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\max\left(\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}},\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}},\ldots,\cfrac{A^{(i)}_{n+m}}{C_{n+m}}\right),

we have that

m^{(i)}_{n}\leq\alpha_{0}^{(i)}\leq M^{(i)}_{n},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see Theorem 1 of
[
8
]
for more details).
Therefore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha_{0}^{(i)}
is contained either in the interval
\left(\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+j}}{C_{n+j}},\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+j+1}}{C_{n+j+1}}\right)
or
\left(\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+j+1}}{C_{n+j+1}},\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+j}}{C_{n+j}}\right)
, for some
j=0,\ldots,m-1
.
This means that

\left|\alpha_{0}^{(i)}-\frac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\left|\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}}-\frac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|=\frac{|\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n+1}|}{C_{n+1}C_{n}},

for infinitely many
n
and for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.
∎

The following two results provide an estimate of the difference between real numbers sharing a certain number of initial partial quotients in their MCF. They hold only in the case when
m=2
, as they both use the inequalities of Lemma
3
, that are specific for Jacobi’s algorithm.

Lemma 7
.

Given

(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]\quad\text{and}\quad(\alpha^{\prime}_{0},\beta^{\prime}_{0})=[(a^{\prime}_{0},a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots),(b^{\prime}_{0},b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots)],

if
a_{i}=a^{\prime}_{i}
and
b_{i}=b^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
, then

\lvert\alpha_{0}-\alpha^{\prime}_{0}\rvert<\frac{1}{C_{n-2}},\quad|\beta_{0}-\beta^{\prime}_{0}|<\frac{1}{C_{n-2}}.

Proof.

Let us suppose without loss of generality that
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}\leq\alpha_{0}
. Since
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}^{\prime}
share up to the
n
-th convergent, we know that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(16)

m_{n+2}<\alpha_{0}^{\prime}\leq\alpha_{0}<M_{n+2}.

where
m_{n+2}
and
M_{n+2}
are as in the proof of Lemma
2
. The thesis then follows from the latter expression, by applying the inequalities of Lemma
3
. The proof for
\beta_{0},\beta_{0}^{\prime}
is similar.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=[(a_{0}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots),(b_{0}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots)]
such that
a_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}
and
b_{i}=b_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
. Then

(17)

|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|<\frac{2}{C_{n}},\ \ \ \ |\beta-\beta^{\prime}|<\frac{2}{C_{n}}.

Proof.

The thesis follows by writing

|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|<\left|\alpha-\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|+\left|\alpha^{\prime}-\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\frac{2}{C_{n}},

and the same holds for
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 9
.

Lemma
7
and Lemma
8
provide two different approximations for the difference of elements sharing the first partial quotients of their MCF. In fact, although
\frac{2}{C_{n}}
is usually smaller than
\frac{1}{C_{n-2}}
, as
C_{n}=a_{n}C_{n-1}+b_{n}C_{n-2}+C_{n-3}
, it is not hard to see that this is not always the case.

Proposition 10
.

Let
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
0<\alpha_{0}^{(i)}<1
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A_{n}^{(i)}\leq KC_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{k}^{(i)})_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence

M_{k}^{(i)}=\max\left(\cfrac{A_{k}^{(i)}}{C_{k}},\cfrac{A_{k+1}^{(i)}}{C_{k+1}},\ldots,\cfrac{A_{k+m}^{(i)}}{C_{k+m}}\right),

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. From the proof of Lemma
2
we know that, for all
i
,
(M_{k}^{(i)})_{k\geq 0}
is strictly decreasing and converging to
\alpha_{0}^{(i)}<1
. Therefore, there exists
k_{0}
such that
A_{k}^{(i)}\leq C_{k}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
, and the claim is proved.
∎

For the
2
-dimensional case, we can do better, as the numerators of the convergents can be explicitly bounded in terms of the denominator.

Lemma 11
.

Let
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
0<\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}<1
, then
A_{n},B_{n}\leq C_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

The claim can be easily proved by induction. For
n=0
,
A_{0}=a_{0}=0
and
B_{0}=b_{0}=0
, since
0<\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}<1
so that
1=C_{0}>A_{0},B_{0}
.
Then we have that
A_{1}=b_{1}
,
B_{1}=1
and
C_{1}=a_{1}
so that also in this case
C_{1}\geq A_{1},B_{1}
. At the second step,
A_{2}=a_{2}b_{1}
,
B_{2}=a_{2}
and
C_{2}=a_{2}a_{1}+1
and since
a_{2}\geq b_{2}
, the base step holds also for
n=2
. Let us now suppose that the thesis holds for
n-3
,
n-2
and
n-1
and let us prove it for
n
. By the recursion formula we have

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}A_{n-1}+b_{n}A_{n-2}+A_{n-3},

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}B_{n-1}+b_{n}B_{n-2}+B_{n-3},

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}C_{n-1}+b_{n}C_{n-2}+C_{n-3}.

Comparing the right-hand sides of the latter equations, we can see that
C_{n}\geq A_{n},B_{n}
by inductive hypothesis, and the claim holds.
∎

Following the same inductive proof of Lemma
11
, it is possible to prove the following general result.

Corollary 12
.

Let
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

N<\alpha_{0}<N+1,\ \ M<\beta_{0}<M+1,

for some integers
N,M\geq 0
. Then, for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle NC_{n}\leq A_{n}<(N+1)C_{n},

\displaystyle MC_{n}\leq B_{n}<(M+1)C_{n}.

Lemma
3
provides the bound
\tilde{A}_{n}\leq C_{n}
, for the case
m=2
. In Theorem
24
, we need to bound
\tilde{A}_{n+1}
using the denominator of the previous step
C_{n}
. Therefore, we prove the following lemma for Jacobi’s MCF, but a similar result can be obtained for
m\geq 3
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
such that
a_{n+1}<C_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(18)

\tilde{A}_{n+1}<3C_{n}^{2},\ \ \ \ \tilde{B}_{n+1}<3C_{n}^{2},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We know that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tilde{A}_{n+1}\leq C_{n+1}
. Therefore, we obtain

\tilde{A}_{n+1}\leq C_{n+1}=a_{n+1}C_{n}+b_{n+1}C_{n-1}+C_{n-2}<a_{n+1}(C_{n}+C_{n-1}+C_{n-2})<3C_{n}^{2},

since
b_{n+1}\leq a_{n+1}<C_{n}
, and similarly for
\tilde{B}_{n+1}
.
∎

The next lemma shows that periodic Jacobi’s MCFs converge to cubic irrationals in the same number field.

Lemma 14
.

Let

(19)

(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1},\overline{a_{k},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}}),(b_{0},\ldots,b_{k-1},\overline{b_{k},\ldots,b_{k+h-1}})],

and

(20)

(\alpha_{0}^{\prime},\beta_{0}^{\prime})=[(c_{0},\ldots,c_{k-1},\overline{a_{k},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}}),(d_{0},\ldots,d_{k-1},\overline{b_{k},\ldots,b_{k+h-1}})].

Then
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}^{\prime},\beta_{0},\beta_{0}^{\prime}
belong to the same cubic number field.

Proof.

Let
(\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta})=[(\overline{a_{k},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}}),(\overline{b_{k},\ldots,b_{k+h-1}})]
. Then
\tilde{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\beta}
are cubic irrationals belonging to the same cubic field (see
[
10
, Section 3F]
). Thus, recalling (
12
), the thesis follows from

\alpha_{0}=\cfrac{\tilde{\alpha}A_{k-1}+\tilde{\beta}A_{k-2}+A_{k-3}}{\tilde{\alpha}C_{k-1}+\tilde{\beta}C_{k-2}+C_{k-3}},\quad\beta_{0}=\cfrac{\tilde{\alpha}B_{k-1}+\tilde{\beta}B_{k-2}+B_{k-3}}{\tilde{\alpha}B_{k-1}+\tilde{\beta}B_{k-2}+B_{k-3}},

\alpha_{0}^{\prime}=\cfrac{\tilde{\alpha}A_{k-1}^{\prime}+\tilde{\beta}A_{k-2}^{\prime}+A_{k-3}^{\prime}}{\tilde{\alpha}C_{k-1}^{\prime}+\tilde{\beta}C_{k-2}^{\prime}+C_{k-3}^{\prime}},\quad\beta_{0}^{\prime}=\cfrac{\tilde{\alpha}B_{k-1}^{\prime}+\tilde{\beta}B_{k-2}^{\prime}+B_{k-3}^{\prime}}{\tilde{\alpha}B_{k-1}^{\prime}+\tilde{\beta}B_{k-2}^{\prime}+B_{k-3}^{\prime}},

where
\left(\cfrac{A_{n}}{C_{n}},\cfrac{B_{n}}{C_{n}}\right)
and
\left(\cfrac{A_{n}^{\prime}}{C_{n}^{\prime}},\cfrac{B_{n}^{\prime}}{C_{n}^{\prime}}\right)
, for all
n\geq 0
, are the convergents of
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
and
(\alpha_{0}^{\prime},\beta_{0}^{\prime})
, respectively.
∎

Now, we provide lower bound for the
n
-th convergent of Jacobi’s MCF and, in the case of bounded partial quotients, also an upper bound.

Lemma 15
.

Let
[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
be the sequences of partial quotients of a MCF. Then
C_{n}>\psi^{n-2}
, where
\psi
is the only real root of the polynomial
f(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-1
.

Moreover, if the partial quotients
a_{n}
are bounded by a constant
M
, i.e.
a_{n}\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
C_{n}\leq\eta^{n}
, where
\eta
is the positive real root of the polynomial
g(x)=x^{3}-Mx^{2}-Mx-1
.

Proof.

We prove both the statements by induction. Notice that
\psi\approx 1.46
so that for
n=0,1,2
the thesis is true. For the induction step,

\displaystyle C_{n+3}=a_{n+3}C_{n+2}+b_{n+3}C_{n+1}+C_{n}\geq C_{n+2}+C_{n}\geq\psi^{n-2}(\psi^{2}+1)=\psi^{n+1},

since
\psi^{3}=\psi^{2}+1
as a root of
f
.

For the second part of the statement, let us assume that
a_{n}\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In general, the polynomial
g
can have either one or three real roots. It has three real roots if and only if its discriminant is positive, i.e.

(21)

\Delta(g)=M^{4}-18M^{2}-27>0,

that happens, in particular, for all
M\geq 5
. It is possible to see that for
1\leq M\leq 4
the base step holds. For
M\geq 5
, by Descartes’ rule of signs there is only a positive root
\eta
, as the product of all roots is
1
and there are exactly two roots of same sign. Moreover, for the same reason,
\eta\geq M\geq 1
as
M
is the trace of
g
. Therefore,

C_{-1}=0\leq\eta^{-1},\ \ \ C_{0}=1=\eta^{0},\ \ \ C_{1}=a_{1}\leq M\leq\eta,

and the base of the induction is proved. For the inductive step notice that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle C_{n+2}

\displaystyle=a_{n+2}C_{n+1}+b_{n+2}C_{n}+C_{n-1}\leq M\eta^{n+1}+M\eta^{n}+\eta^{n-1}=

\displaystyle=\eta^{n-1}(M\eta^{2}+M\eta+1)=\eta^{n+2},

as
\eta
is a root of
g
, and the thesis is true.
∎

4.
Bounds on the height of cubic irrationals

In this section we prove an explicit bound on the naive height of cubic irrationals corresponding to a periodic Jacobi’s MCF.

Let
\gamma
be an algebraic number and let
P_{\gamma}
be its minimal polynomial, that is, the unique polynomial with integer coefficients vanishing at
\gamma
and irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}
. Then, let
H(\gamma)
be the naive height of
\gamma
, i.e., the maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of
P_{\gamma}
.

In the following theorem we prove that if
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
has a periodic MCF expansion, then
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
satisfy a polynomial with integer coefficients of degree three and we find an explicit bound on their naive height in the case
0<\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}<1
. Then, exploiting Corollary
12
, the case of general
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}
is considered in Theorem
17
,.

Theorem 16
.

Given
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

(22)

(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1},\overline{a_{k},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}}),(b_{0},\ldots,b_{k-1},\overline{b_{k},\ldots,b_{k+h-1}})],

then
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
are cubic irrationals. Moreover, if
0<\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}<1
, then

H(\alpha_{0}),H(\beta_{0})\leq 3024C_{h+k-1}^{9}.

Proof.

From (
22
), we have that
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k+h}
and
\beta_{k}=\beta_{k+h}
, so that, by (
13
),

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k}\\
\beta_{k}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{k+h}}\begin{pmatrix}a_{k}&1&0\cr b_{k}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}a_{k+h-1}&1&0\cr b_{k+h-1}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k}\\
\beta_{k}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, by applying (
14
) with
m=2
, we get

(24)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{k}\\
\beta_{k}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{k}\begin{pmatrix}a_{k-1}&1&0\cr b_{k-1}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&1&0\cr b_{0}&0&1\cr 1&0&0\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}\\
\beta_{0}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Following the notation introduced in (
15
), put
M_{n}=M_{n}^{(2)}
for every
n
. Hence, by plugging (
24
) into the right-hand side of (
23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}\\
\beta_{0}\\
1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha_{k+1}\ldots\alpha_{k+h}}M_{0}M_{1}\ldots M_{k+h-1}(M_{0}\ldots M_{k-1})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}\\
\beta_{0}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}A_{k+h-1}&A_{k+h-2}&A_{k+h-3}\cr B_{k+h-1}&B_{k+h-2}&B_{k+h-3}\cr C_{k+h-1}&C_{k+h-2}&C_{k+h-3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{k-1}&A_{k-2}&A_{k-3}\cr B_{k-1}&B_{k-2}&B_{k-3}\cr C_{k-1}&C_{k-2}&C_{k-3}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}\\
\beta_{0}\\
1\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda^{\prime}=\alpha_{k+1}\ldots\alpha_{k+h}
. Now, notice that

(25)

\begin{pmatrix}A_{k-1}&A_{k-2}&A_{k-3}\cr B_{k-1}&B_{k-2}&B_{k-3}\cr C_{k-1}&C_{k-2}&C_{k-3}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{B}_{k-2}&-\tilde{A}_{k-2}&\tilde{U}_{k-2}\cr-\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{k-1}&\tilde{\tilde{A}}_{k-1}&-\tilde{\tilde{U}}_{k-1}\cr\tilde{B}_{k-1}&-\tilde{A}_{k-1}&\tilde{U}_{k-1}\end{pmatrix},

where the latter sequences are as defined in Section
2.1
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle X_{1,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{1,2}\beta_{0}+X_{1,3}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\prime}\alpha_{0},

\displaystyle X_{2,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{2,2}\beta_{0}+X_{2,3}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\prime}\beta_{0},

\displaystyle X_{3,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{3,2}\beta_{0}+X_{3,3}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\prime},

\displaystyle X_{1,1}

\displaystyle=A_{k+h-1}\tilde{B}_{k-2}-A_{k+h-2}\tilde{\tilde{B}}_{k-1}+A_{k+h-3}\tilde{B}_{k-1},

\displaystyle X_{1,2}

\displaystyle X_{3,2}

\lambda^{\prime}

(26)

\displaystyle X_{1,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{1,2}\beta_{0}+X_{1,3}

\displaystyle X_{2,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{2,2}\beta_{0}+X_{2,3}

\beta_{0}

X_{2,1}-X_{3,1}\beta_{0}\neq 0

\alpha_{0}=\frac{X_{3,2}\beta_{0}^{2}+(X_{3,3}-X_{2,2})\beta_{0}-X_{2,3}}{X_{2,1}-X_{3,1}\beta_{0}}.

We substitute the latter expression in (

\displaystyle X_{3,1}(X_{3,2}\beta_{0}^{2}+(X_{3,3}-X_{2,2})\beta_{0}-X_{2,3})^{2}-(X_{1,2}\beta_{0}+X_{1,3})(X_{2,1}-X_{3,1}\beta_{0})^{2}+

\displaystyle-X_{2,1}X_{2,3}X_{3,2}-X_{1,1}X_{2,1}X_{3,3}-X_{2,1}X_{2,2}X_{3,3}-X_{2,3}X_{3,1}X_{3,3}+X_{2,1}X_{3,3}^{2}

\displaystyle D=

\displaystyle-X_{1,3}X_{2,1}^{2}+X_{1,1}X_{2,1}X_{2,3}+X_{2,3}^{2}X_{3,1}-X_{2,1}X_{2,3}X_{3,3}.

Using Lemma
11
, we can give an upper bound to the coefficients
X_{i,j}
in terms of the denominator
C_{h+k-1}
. Notice that
h
and
k
are constant, because they represent the period and the pre-period of the continued fraction expansion of
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
. It is possible to notice that

(28)

X_{i,j}\leq 6C_{h+k-1}^{3}.

This gives a bound on the coefficients of the minimal polynomial of
\beta_{0}
, and then

(29)

H(\beta_{0})\leq 2592C_{h+k-1}^{9}.

Notice that we used the fact that
A_{n},B_{n}\leq C_{n}
, therefore we are handling the case when
0<\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}<1
.

Now we want to apply the same reasoning to
\alpha_{0}
, in order to find its minimal polynomial and a bound on its height. From (
26
), we obtain:

\beta_{0}=\frac{X_{3,1}\alpha_{0}^{2}+(X_{3,3}-X_{1,1})\alpha_{0}-X_{1,3}}{X_{1,2}-X_{3,2}\alpha_{0}}.

Notice again that the denominator is nonzero as
\alpha_{0}
is irrational.

We substitute the latter expression in (
27
) and we get

\displaystyle X_{3,2}(X_{3,1}\alpha_{0}^{2}+(X_{3,3}-X_{1,1})\alpha_{0}-X_{1,3})^{2}-(X_{2,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{2,3})(X_{1,2}-X_{3,2}\alpha_{0})^{2}+

\displaystyle+(X_{3,1}\alpha_{0}+X_{3,3}-X_{2,2})(X_{3,1}\alpha_{0}^{2}+(X_{3,3}-X_{1,1})\alpha_{0}-X_{1,3})(X_{1,2}-X_{3,2}\alpha_{0})=0.

The two terms of degree
4
are
X_{3,2}X_{3,1}^{2}\alpha_{0}^{4}
and
-X_{3,2}X_{3,1}^{2}\alpha_{0}^{4}
, therefore the left-hand side is a polynomial of degree
3
. With some computations, it is possible to see that the equation reduces to

A\alpha_{0}^{3}+B\alpha_{0}^{2}+C\alpha_{0}+D=0,

where

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\;X_{1,2}X_{3,1}^{2}-X_{1,1}X_{3,1}X_{3,2}+X_{2,2}X_{3,1}X_{3,2}-X_{2,1}X_{3,2}^{2},

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle-X_{1,1}X_{1,2}X_{3,1}-X_{1,2}X_{2,2}X_{3,1}+X_{1,1}^{2}X_{3,2}+2X_{1,2}X_{2,1}X_{3,2}-X_{1,1}X_{2,2}X_{3,2}+

\displaystyle-X_{1,3}X_{3,1}X_{3,2}-X_{2,3}X_{3,2}^{2}+2X_{1,2}X_{3,1}X_{3,3}-X_{1,1}X_{3,2}X_{3,3}+X_{2,2}X_{3,2}X_{3,3},

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle-X_{1,2}^{2}X_{2,1}+X_{1,1}X_{1,2}X_{2,2}-X_{1,2}X_{1,3}X_{3,1}+2X_{1,1}X_{1,3}X_{3,2}-X_{1,3}X_{2,2}X_{3,2}+

\displaystyle+2X_{1,2}X_{2,3}X_{3,2}-X_{1,1}X_{1,2}X_{3,3}-X_{1,2}X_{2,2}X_{3,3}-X_{1,3}X_{3,2}X_{3,3}+X_{1,2}X_{3,3}^{2},

\displaystyle D=

\displaystyle\;X_{1,2}X_{1,3}X_{2,2}-X_{1,2}^{2}X_{2,3}+X_{1,3}^{2}X_{3,2}-X_{1,2}X_{1,3}X_{3,3}.

Also in this case we use (
28
), and we get that

(30)

H(\alpha_{0})\leq 3024C_{h+k-1}^{9},

which yields the thesis.
∎

Theorem 17
.

Given
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

(31)

(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})=[(a_{0},\ldots,a_{k-1},\overline{a_{k},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}}),(b_{0},\ldots,b_{k-1},\overline{b_{k},\ldots,b_{k+h-1}})],

then
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
are cubic irrationals. Moreover, if
0<\alpha_{0}<N
,
0<\beta_{0}<M
, for some positive integers
N,M
, then

H(\alpha_{0}),H(\beta_{0})\leq 3024N^{5}M^{5}C_{h+k-1}^{9}.

5.
Multidimensional continued fractions with rapidly increasing partial quotients

In this section we provide some infinite families of multidimensional continued fractions converging to transcendental numbers, using a method similar to Liouville. First we recall Roth’s theorem.

Theorem 18

(Roth)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be an algebraic number. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist only finitely many
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\left|\frac{p}{q}-\alpha\right|<\frac{1}{q^{2+\epsilon}}.

Theorem 19
.

Let
[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
be two infinite admissible sequences of integer partial quotients for Jacobi’s algorithm, hence converging to a pair of real numbers
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
, and let
\delta>0
. If

(32)

a_{n}>\max\{|\tilde{A}_{n}|C_{n-1}^{\delta},|\tilde{B}_{n}|C_{n-1}^{\delta}\},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
are (rationally independent) transcendental numbers.

Proof.

Let us prove that, when condition (
32
) holds, then there are infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|\alpha_{0}-\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\frac{1}{C_{n}^{w}},

and infinitely many
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|\beta_{0}-\frac{B_{m}}{C_{m}}\right|<\frac{1}{C_{m}^{w^{\prime}}},

with
w,w^{\prime}>2
. In this way,
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}
do not satisfy Roth’s theorem and they are both transcendental numbers. By Lemma
6
, we have that, for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left|\alpha_{0}-\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\left|\frac{A_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}}-\frac{A_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|=\left|\frac{\tilde{A}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}C_{n}}\right|<\frac{|\tilde{A}_{n+1}|}{a_{n+1}C_{n}^{2}}<\frac{1}{C_{n}^{2+\delta}},

where in the last inequality we have exploited condition (
32
).
It follows that, by Roth’s theorem,
\alpha
is transcendental. Similarly as before, we can prove that

\left|\beta_{0}-\frac{B_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\left|\frac{B_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}}-\frac{B_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|=\left|\frac{\tilde{B}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}C_{n}}\right|<\frac{|\tilde{B}_{n+1}|}{a_{n+1}C_{n}^{2}}<\frac{1}{C_{n}^{2+\delta}},

holds for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that also
\beta_{0}
is transcendental. Rational independence follows readily from Remark
1
.
∎

Remark 20
.

Condition (
32
) of Theorem
19
can be practically used in order to construct pairs of transcendental numbers by means of Jacobi’s algorithm. In fact, the sequences
\tilde{A}_{n}
and
\tilde{B}_{n}
do not depend on
a_{n}
, but only on
b_{n}
and on previous partial quotients. Therefore, for any arbitrary choice of the sequence
b_{n}
, we have a different pair of transcendental numbers
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
, that are rationally independent.

In the following, we exploit a similar technique in order to provide an
m
-tuple of transcendental numbers by means of Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm for continued fractions in higher dimensions.

Theorem 21
.

Let
[(a_{n}^{(1)})_{n\geq 0},\ldots,(a_{n}^{(m)})_{n\geq 0}]
be
m
infinite sequences of admissible partial quotients for Jacobi–Perron’s algorithm, hence converging to an
m
-tuple of real numbers
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})
, and let
\delta>0
. If

(33)

a^{(1)}_{n}>\max\{|\tilde{A}_{n}^{(1)}|C_{n-1}^{\delta},|\tilde{A}^{(2)}_{n}|C_{n-1}^{\delta},\ldots,|\tilde{A}^{(m)}_{n}|C_{n-1}^{\delta}\},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then all the real numbers
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})
are transcendental.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
19
for the classical Jacobi’s algorithm. By Lemma
6
, we know that, for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left|\alpha_{0}^{(i)}-\frac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|<\left|\frac{A^{(i)}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}}-\frac{A^{(i)}_{n}}{C_{n}}\right|=\left|\frac{\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n+1}}{C_{n+1}C_{n}}\right|<\frac{|\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n+1}|}{a^{(1)}_{n+1}C_{n}^{2}}<\frac{1}{C_{n}^{2+\delta}}.

It follows, by Roth’s theorem, that
\alpha_{0}^{(i)}
is transcendental for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.
∎

Thanks to Remark
5
, it is possible to define recursively the sequence of partial quotients
a_{n}^{(1)}
such that they satisfy (
33
), and hence the MCF converges to an
m
-tuple of transcendental numbers. Since, for
m\geq 3
, the infiniteness of the sequences of partial quotients does not guarantee in general that these
m
real transcendental numbers are rationally independent, it would be interesting to explore under which conditions this happens.

6.
Quasi-periodic multidimensional continued fractions

In this section, we deal with transcendence criteria for families of quasi-periodic MCFs.
The main results of Diophantine approximation that we exploit are the following two theorems, which provide two different generalizations of Liouville’s theorem. In Theorem
23
we are requiring more hypotheses than Theorem
22
, i.e. that the approximations lie all inside the same number field, and it will be used for the construction in the proof of Theorem
28
.

Theorem 22

(
[
11
]
, Theorem 2.6)
.

Let
\alpha
be an algebraic number and
n\geq 1
be an integer. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c
such that

|\alpha-\xi|>\frac{c}{H(\xi)^{n+1+\epsilon}},

for all
\xi\neq\alpha
of degree at most
n
.

Theorem 23

(
[
11
]
, Theorem 2.7)
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\alpha\notin K
be an algebraic number. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c
such that

|\alpha-\xi|>\frac{c}{H(\xi)^{2+\epsilon}},

for all
\xi\in K
.

First of all, we prove a bound on the growth for the denominators of the convergents.

Theorem 24
.

Let us consider
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})=[(a_{0}^{(1)},a_{1}^{(1)},\dots),\dots,(a_{0}^{(m)},a_{1}^{(m)},\dots)]
, and
d\geq 1
. If
a_{n+1}^{(1)}<C_{n}^{d}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists a constant
K=K(d,m)
such that

(34)

\log\log C_{n+1}<Kn,

for all
n\geq 0
. A suitable choice for
K
is

K=\log(d+1)+\log\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)+\log\log(m+1).

Proof.

Recall that, for all
n
,

C_{n+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{n+1}^{(j)}C_{n+1-j}+C_{n-m}.

Since
(C_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is increasing and
a_{n+1}^{(j)}<C_{n}
for all
j
by hypothesis, together with (
6
), we get that

C_{n+1}<(m+1)C_{n}^{d+1}.

Now, by recursion, it is easily seen that

C_{n+1}<(m+1)C_{n}^{d+1}<(m+1)^{1+(d+1)}C_{n-1}^{(d+1)^{2}}<\cdots<\\
<(m+1)^{1+(d+1)+\,\cdots\,+(d+1)^{n}}C_{0}^{(d+1)^{n+1}}=(m+1)^{\frac{(d+1)^{n+1}-1}{d}},

which yields

\log\log C_{n+1}<n\left(\log(d+1)+\log\left(1+\frac{1}{d}\right)+\log\log(m+1)\right),

for all
n
, and the thesis follows.
∎

Definition 25

(Quasi-periodic continued fractions)
.

Let us consider the sequences of positive integers
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(r_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, with
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
strictly increasing. Let us suppose that
(a_{n}^{(1)})_{n\geq 0},\ldots,(a_{n}^{(m)})_{n\geq 0}
satisfy, for all
j=1,\ldots,m
,

(35)

a_{m+r_{k}}^{(j)}=a_{m}^{(j)},\ \ \text{ for }n_{k}\leq m\leq n_{k}+(\lambda_{k}-1)r_{k}-1

The multidimensional continued fraction
[(a_{n}^{(1)})_{n\geq 0},\ldots,(a_{n}^{(m)})_{n\geq 0}]
, where the latter sequences satisfy (
35
) is called
quasi-periodic
.

Remark 26
.

The meaning of Definition
25
is well explained in the papers of Baker
[
6
]
and Adamczewski-Bugeaud
[
3
]
for the unidimensional case. Basically, for any
k\geq 0
, at the index
n_{k}
there starts a block of
r_{k}
partial quotients, and this block is repeated exactly
\lambda_{k}
times. The reason of the name
quasi-periodic
is that, if
\lambda_{k}
grows with
k
, then the blocks keep repeating for an increasing number of times, simulating the behavior of a periodic continued fraction. Notice also that, in Definition
25
for the multidimensional case, the sequences
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(r_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, with
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are the same for all the
(a_{n}^{(j)})_{n\geq 0}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Theorem 27
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
be a quasi-periodic continued fraction such that, for some
d\geq 1
,
a_{i+1}<C_{i}^{d}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, let us suppose that
r_{k}<cn_{k}
. where
c
is a constant. If

(36)

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\lambda_{k}}{n_{k}}=+\infty,

then
\alpha
and
\beta
are transcendental numbers.

Proof.

Let us suppose by contradiction that
\alpha
and
\beta
are algebraic numbers. Starting from the
quasi-periodic
expansion of
(\alpha,\beta)
, we construct a sequence of pairs
(\eta^{(k)},\zeta^{(k)})_{k\geq 0}
in the following way:

(37)

\displaystyle\eta^{(k)}

\displaystyle=[a_{0},\ldots a_{n_{k}-1},\overline{a_{n_{k}},\ldots,a_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}}],

(38)

\displaystyle\zeta^{(k)}

\displaystyle=[b_{0},\ldots b_{n_{k}-1},\overline{b_{n_{k}},\ldots,b_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}}].

for all
k\geq 0
.
For simplicity let us set, for all
k\geq 1
,

m_{k}=n_{k}+(\lambda_{k}-1)r_{k}-1.

Then, the elements
\eta^{(k)}
and
\zeta^{(k)}
are cubic irrationalities for all
k
, that have in common with
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, the first
m_{k}
partial quotients. Therefore, by Lemma
8
, Theorem
16
and Theorem
22
, choosing some
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{m_{k}}}>|\alpha-\eta^{(k)}|>\frac{c_{1}}{C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}^{9(4+\epsilon)}},

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{m_{k}}}>|\beta-\zeta^{(k)}|>\frac{c_{2}}{C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}^{9(4+\epsilon)}}.

From the latter equations and Lemma
15
, we obtain

(39)

\psi^{m_{k}-2}<C_{m_{k}}<c_{3}C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}^{9(4+\epsilon^{\prime})},

that is

(40)

\lambda_{k}<m_{k}-2<c_{4}+c_{5}\log(C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1})<c_{6}\log(C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1}).

Therefore, using only Theorem
22
we have obtained that, if either
\alpha
or
\beta
is an algebraic number, then

(41)

\log\log(C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1})>c_{7}\log\lambda_{k}.

However, by Theorem
24
,

(42)

\log\log(C_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1})<c_{8}(n_{k}+r_{k}-1).

Plugging together (
41
) and (
42
), and since
r_{k}<cn_{k}
by hypothesis, we obtain

\frac{\log\lambda_{k}}{n_{k}}<c_{9},

that is a contradiction with (
36
). Therefore,
\alpha
and
\beta
are transcendental numbers.
∎

Theorem 28
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)=[(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots),(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots)]
be a quasi-periodic continued fraction. Let us suppose that there exist two positive integers
M,N
such that
a_{k},b_{k}\leq M
and
r_{k}\leq N
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us define the constant

B=2\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi}-1,

where
\eta
and
\psi
are taken as in Lemma
8
, i.e. the positive real roots of
x^{3}-Mx^{2}-Mx-1
and
x^{3}-x^{2}-x-1
, respectively.
Then, if

(43)

\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{n_{k}}>B,

both
\alpha
and
\beta
are transcendental numbers.

Proof.

Let us suppose by contradiction that either
\alpha
or
\beta
is algebraic. Since both the length of the periods and the partial quotients are bounded by hypothesis, i.e.
a_{n},b_{n}\leq M
and
r_{n}\leq N
, there exist only finitely many possible different blocks, as there are only finitely many pairs
(a_{n_{k}},b_{n_{k}}),\ldots,(a_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1},b_{n_{k}+r_{k}-1})
. Since in the
quasi-periodic
continued fraction (
35
) there are infinitely many pairs of blocks that repeat, we can find an infinite subsequence
(k_{l})_{l\geq 0}
, such that the blocks starting at the partial quotients
a_{n_{k_{l}}}
and
b_{n_{k_{l}}}
are the same for all
l\geq 0
. Therefore, we can find a sequence of algebraic numbers

\displaystyle\xi^{(t)}

\displaystyle=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n_{t}-1},\overline{a_{n_{t}},\ldots,a_{n_{t}+r_{t}-1}}],

\displaystyle\gamma^{(t)}

\displaystyle=[b_{0},\ldots,b_{n_{t}-1},\overline{b_{n_{t}},\ldots,b_{n_{t}+r_{t}-1}}],

where
t=k_{l}
for all
l\geq 0
. This is the multidimensional version of the construction in the proof of Theorem 2 of
[
6
]
. The peculiarity of these cubic irrationalities, that form a subsequence of the
\eta^{(k)}
and
\zeta^{(k)}
defined in (
37
) and (
38
), is that they share the same periodic part for all
t
. Therefore, by Lemma
14
, they lie in the same cubic field. In this case, we can use Theorem
16
and Theorem
23
together with Lemma
8
. Choosing
\epsilon>0
, and calling
m_{t}=n_{t}+(\lambda_{t}-1)r-1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{m_{t}}}

\displaystyle>|\alpha-\xi^{(t)}|>\frac{c_{1}}{C_{n_{t}+r-1}^{9(2+\epsilon)}},

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{m_{t}}}

\displaystyle>|\beta-\gamma^{(t)}|>\frac{c_{2}}{C_{n_{t}+r-1}^{9(2+\epsilon)}}.

This means that

(44)

C_{m_{t}}<c_{3}C_{n_{t}+r-1}^{9(2+\epsilon)}.

From Lemma
15
, we obtain

(45)

\psi^{m_{t}-2}<\eta^{9(2+\epsilon)(n_{t}+r-1)},

that is

(46)

n_{t}+(\lambda_{t}-1)r-3<9(2+\epsilon)(n_{t}+r-1)\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi}.

Since
r
and
\epsilon
are constant, we can rewrite (
46
) as

(47)

n_{t}+r\lambda_{t}<c_{4}+9(2+\epsilon)n_{t}\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi},

from which we obtain

(48)

\lambda_{t}<r\lambda_{t}<c_{4}+n_{t}\left(9(2+\epsilon)\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi}-1\right).

We get,

\frac{\lambda_{t}}{n_{t}}<\frac{c_{4}}{n_{t}}+\left(18\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi}-1\right)+9\epsilon\frac{\log\eta}{\log\psi},

and the latter holds for all
\epsilon>0
. Therefore for sufficiently small
\epsilon
and sufficiently large
t
, it contradicts the hypothesis (
43
).
∎

7.
Open problems and further research

In this section, we collect some open problems and hints for further research, that can allow to obtain more general or stronger results.

In the previous section we proved some transcendence criteria for quasi-periodic MCFs when
m=2
. An obstruction that we found in proving Theorem
27
and
28
for MCFs of higher dimension is an analogue of Theorem
16
. We leave it here as a conjecture.

Conjecture 29
.

Given
(\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
having periodic continued fraction

(49)

\left[\left(a_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,a_{k-1}^{(1)},\overline{a_{k}^{(1)},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}^{(1)}}\right),\ldots,\left(a_{0}^{(m)},\ldots,a_{k-1}^{(m)},\overline{a_{k}^{(m)},\ldots,a_{k+h-1}^{(m)}}\right)\right],

then
\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\ldots,\alpha_{0}^{(m)}
are algebraic irrationalities of degree at most
m+1
. Moreover, if
0<\alpha_{0}^{(i)}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
, then there exist two positive constants
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
which do not depend on any
\alpha_{0}^{(i)}
and such that

H(\alpha_{0}^{(i)})\leq L_{1}C_{h+k-1}^{L_{2}},

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

In order to prove the conjecture, similarly as in (
14
), it is not difficult to see that

(50)

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}\\
\alpha_{0}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{0}^{(m)}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\ldots\alpha_{n+1}^{(1)}}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}^{(1)}&1&0&\ldots&0\cr a_{j}^{(2)}&0&1&\ldots&0\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\cr a_{j}^{(m)}&0&0&\ldots&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{n+1}^{(1)}\\
\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{n+1}^{(m)}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Following the proof of Theorem
16
, we arrive to a system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{1,1}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}+X_{1,2}\alpha_{0}^{(2)}+\ldots+X_{1,m}\alpha_{0}^{(m)}+X_{1,m+1}=\lambda^{\prime}\alpha_{0}^{(1)},\\
X_{2,1}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}+X_{2,2}\alpha_{0}^{(2)}+\ldots+X_{2,m}\alpha_{0}^{(m)}+X_{1,m+1}=\lambda^{\prime}\alpha_{0}^{(2)},\\
\vdots\\
X_{m,1}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}+X_{1,2}\alpha_{0}^{(2)}+\ldots+X_{1,m}\alpha_{0}^{(m)}+X_{1,m+1}=\lambda^{\prime}\alpha_{0}^{(m)},\\
X_{m+1,1}\alpha_{0}^{(1)}+X_{m+1,2}\alpha_{0}^{(2)}+\ldots+X_{m+1,m}\alpha_{0}^{(m)}+X_{m+1,m+1}=\lambda^{\prime},\end{cases}

where
\lambda^{\prime}=\alpha_{k+1}^{(1)}\ldots\alpha_{k+h}^{(1)}
and the
X_{i,j}
are coefficients in
A_{n}^{(j)}
and
C_{n}
, for
n=k+h-(m+1),\ldots,k+h-1
and
j=1,\ldots,m
. Eliminating
\lambda^{\prime}
from the last equation, we can rewrite the system as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{1,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{1,m+1}=\alpha_{0}^{(1)}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{m+1,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{m+1,m+1}\right)\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{2,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{2,m+1}=\alpha_{0}^{(2)}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{m+1,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{m+1,m+1}\right)\\
\vdots\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{m,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{m,m+1}=\alpha_{0}^{(m)}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}X_{m+1,i}\alpha_{0}^{(i)}+X_{m+1,m+1}\right).\end{cases}

The problem here is that, in this system of inequalities, it is not easy to write explicitly the minimal polynomials of
\alpha_{0}^{(i)}
in order to bound its naive height. However, by using some elimination techniques on the previous system, it is possible to prove that there exist polynomials
P_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[Y_{1,1},\dots,Y_{m+1,m+1},Z]
, for
i=1,\ldots,m
, whose coefficients depend only on
m
, such that
P_{i}(X_{1,1},\dots,X_{m+1,m+1},\alpha_{0}^{(i)})=0
. Hence the thesis would readily follow from a generalization of Lemma
11
, which represents the only concrete obstruction.

Moreover, another issue in generalizing the results for quasi-periodic MCFs is that an analogue of Lemma
3
does not generally hold for MCFs with
m\geq 3
. In particular, for
m\geq 3
, it is not true that
|\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}|\leq C_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where the sequences
\tilde{A}^{(i)}_{n}
are as in Section
5
.

In the main transcendence results of the paper, i.e. Theorems
19
and
21
for Liouville-type MCFs and Theorems
27
and
28
for quasi-periodic MCFs, we provide several criteria to produce a MCF converging to a
m
-tuple of rational independent transcendental numbers. We expect that in general, using our approach, the
m
transcendental numbers are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. It would be nice to prove such conjecture or to provide some sufficient conditions ensuring it.

After Liouville-type continued fractions
[
20
]
and quasi-periodic continued fractions
[
6
,
22
]
, other transcendence criteria have been proved for unidimensional continued fractions. Adamczewski and Bugeaud proved the transcendence of
stammering

[
2
]
and
palindromic

[
3
]
continued fractions. Later, the transcendence of several other families of continued fractions has been established by Adamczewski, Bugeaud and Davison
[
5
]
. This analysis culminates in the proof of the transcendence for any automatic continued fraction, provided by Bugeaud
[
12
]
. In future works, it would be interesting to investigate and adapt these results in the multidimensional setting, possibly defining also some multidimensional generalization of the famous sequences considered in
[
5
]
.

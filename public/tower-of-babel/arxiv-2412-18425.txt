Computing the k k -binomial complexity of generalized Thue–Morse words
M. Golafshan, M. Rigo, M. A. Whiteland

Abstract
Two finite words are k k -binomially equivalent if each subword (i.e., subsequence) of length at most k k occurs the same number of times in both words. The k k -binomial complexity of an infinite word is a function that maps the integer n ⩾ 0 n\geqslant 0 to the number of k k -binomial equivalence classes represented by its factors of length n n . The Thue–Morse (TM) word and its generalization to larger alphabets are ubiquitous in mathematics due to their rich combinatorial properties. This work addresses the k k -binomial complexities of generalized TM words. Prior research by Lejeune, Leroy, and Rigo determined the k k -binomial complexities of the 2 2 -letter TM word. For larger alphabets, work by Lü, Chen, Wen, and Wu determined the 2 2 -binomial complexity for m m -letter TM words, for arbitrary m m , but the exact behavior for k ⩾ 3 k\geqslant 3 remained unresolved. They conjectured that the k k -binomial complexity function of the m m -letter TM word is eventually periodic with period m k m^{k} . We resolve the conjecture positively by deriving explicit formulae for the k k -binomial complexity functions for any generalized TM word. We do this by characterizing k k -binomial equivalence among factors of generalized TM words. This comprehensive analysis not only solves the open conjecture, but also develops tools such as abelian Rauzy graphs.

The Thue–Morse word arises in a wide range of unexpected contexts due to its remarkable combinatorial properties.

{definition}

Let

\binom{u}{w}=\#\left\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{|w|}\mid\,a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{|w|}}=w\right\}.

Note that the same notation is used for the binomial coefficients of words and integers, as the context prevents any ambiguity (the binomial coefficient of unary words naturally coincides with the integer version:
\binom{a^{n}}{a^{k}}=\binom{n}{k}

{definition}

[
[

]
]

A word

This relation is known as the

{definition}

Let

be an integer. The

For

For instance, M. Andrieu and L. Vivion have recently shown that the

-binomial complexity function is well-suited for studying hypercubic billiard words

5
]

These words encode the sequence of faces successively hit by a billiard ball in a

The ball moves in straight lines until it encounters a face, then bounces elastically according to the law of reflection.
A notable property is that removing a symbol from a

-dimensional billiard word results in a

-dimensional billiard word.
Consequently, the projected factors of the

(d-1)
-dimensional

-dimensional word.

The connections between binomial complexity and Parikh-collinear morphisms are studied in
[

]

{definition}

Let

{proposition}

1.1
Previously known results on generalized Thue–Morse words

It is well-known that the factor complexity of any automatic word, including the generalized Thue–Morse words, is in
\mathcal{O}(n)
.
The usual factor complexity function of
\mathbf{t}_{m}
is known exactly via results of Starosta
[

1.1
.

For any

\mathsf{p}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}(0)=1

\mathsf{p}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}(n)=\begin{cases}m^{2}(n-1)-m(n-2)&\text{if }2\leqslant n\leqslant m;\\
m^{2}(n-1)-m^{k+1}+m^{k}&\text{if }m^{k}+1\leqslant n\leqslant 2m^{k}-m^{k-1},\ k\geq 1;\\
m^{2}(n-1)-m^{k+1}+m^{k}+m\ell&\text{if }n=2m^{k}-m^{k-1}+1+\ell,\\
&\text{ with }0\leqslant\ell<m^{k+1}-2m^{k}+m^{k-1},\ k\geq 1.\end{cases}

The abelian complexity of

1.2
[

]

Let

•

If

\frac{1}{4}m(m-1)^{2}+m,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

•

If

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}m^{3}+1,&\text{ if }\nu=0;\\
\frac{1}{4}m(m-1)^{2}+\frac{5}{4}m,&\text{ if }\nu\neq 0\text{ is even};\\
\frac{1}{4}m^{2}(m-2)+m,&\text{ if }\nu\text{ is odd}.\\

1.2
Main results

Let

and
m\geqslant 2
. The behavior of
\mathsf{b}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}^{(k)}(n)

1.5
.

The shortest pair of distinct factors that are

Recall

1.1

1.6
.

Let

1.

If

for some
\nu\in\{2,\ldots,2m-1\}

\mathsf{b}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}^{(k)}(\nu\,m^{k-1})=(m^{k-1}-1)\#E_{m}(\nu)+\mathsf{a}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}(\nu).

2.

If

for some

\displaystyle\mathsf{b}_{\mathbf{t}_{m}}^{(k)}(\nu\,m^{k-1}+\mu)=(\mu-1)\#E_{m}(\nu+1)+(m^{k-1}-\mu-1)\#E_{m}(\nu)+\#Y_{m}(\nu)

\#E_{m}(\nu)=\begin{cases}m(1+\nu m-\nu),&\text{ if }\nu<m;\\
m^{3}-m^{2}+m,&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

and

\#Y_{m}(\nu)=\begin{cases}2m(1+\nu m-\nu)-m\nu(\nu-1),&\text{ if }\nu<m;\\
m^{3}-m^{2}+2m,&\text{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

1.7
.

For every length

\rule{0.0pt}{10.76385pt}m,&\text{ otherwise}.\\

In particular,

Key Points of Our Proof Strategy

The developments presented are relatively intricate. Therefore, we found it useful to schematically outline the main steps of the proof. We hope this provides the reader with a general understanding about the structure of the paper, allowing each section to be read almost independently of the others. This, we believe, makes the paper easier to follow.

{definition}

Let

In the literature,

{proposition}

\overline{1}=1

\binom{\sigma_{2}^{2n+1}(0)}{(01)^{n+1}}-\binom{\sigma_{2}^{2n+1}(1)}{(01)^{n+1}}=2^{n(2n+1)}.

k

3

in

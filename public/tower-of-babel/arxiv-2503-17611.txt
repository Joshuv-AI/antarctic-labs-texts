On continuous polynomials of the Macías space
Jhixon Macías

Abstract
. Let ℕ \mathbb{N} be the set of natural numbers. The Macías space M ⁡ ( ℕ ) M(\mathbb{N}) is the topological space ( ℕ , τ M ) (\mathbb{N},\tau_{M}) where τ M \tau_{M} is generated by the collection of sets σ n := { m ∈ ℕ : gcd ⁡ ( n , m ) = 1 } \sigma_{n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(n,m)=1\} . In this paper, we characterize the continuity of polynomials over M ⁡ ( ℕ ) M(\mathbb{N}) and prove that the only continuous polynomials are monomials.

1.
Introduction

In 1953, M. Brown introduced a topology
\tau_{G}
on the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
, which is generated by the collection of arithmetic progressions
a+b\mathbb{N}
where
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
\gcd(a,b)=1
(here the symbol
\gcd(a,b)
means the greatest common divisor of
a
and
b
). In
[
13
, Counterexample 60]
the topology
\tau_{G}
is called the
relatively prime integer topology
. It was not until 1959 that the topology introduced by Brown was popularized by S. Golomb (now known as Golomb’s topology) who in
[
6
]
proves that Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions is equivalent to the set of prime numbers being dense in the topological space
(\mathbb{N},\tau_{G})
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a polynomial of the form

(1)

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}\quad\text{where}\quad a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1},a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

In
[
14
, Theorem 4.3]
, P. Szczuka proves that polynomials of the form (
1
) with
a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
are continuous in
(\mathbb{N},\tau_{G})
if and only if
a_{0}=0
. Motivated by P. Szczuka’s work, in this paper we study the continuity of polynomials of the form (
1
) over the Macías topological space
M(\mathbb{N})
, a topology recently introduced and studied in
[
7
,
9
]
which we will define in the next section. We prove that the only continuous polynomials in
M(\mathbb{N})
are the constant polynomials and those of the form
ax^{n}
with
a,n\in\mathbb{N}
(see Theorem
3.12
).

2.
The Macías space

The Macías space
M(\mathbb{N})
is the topological space
(\mathbb{N},\tau_{M})
where
\tau_{M}
is generated by the collection of sets
\sigma_{n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(n,m)=1\}
. Note that

\sigma_{n}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m\leq n\\
\gcd(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}n\left(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\right)+m,

since for every integer
x
we have that
\gcd(n,m)=\gcd(n,nx+m)
; see
[
11
, Theorem 1.9]
. It is easily deduced from here that
\sigma_{n}
is infinite for every positive integer
n
and that the topology
\tau_{M}
is strictly coarser than Golomb topology. The Macías space does not satisfy the
\mathrm{T}_{0}
separation axiom, satisfies (vacuously) the
\mathrm{T}_{4}
separation axiom, is connected, locally connected, path-connected, and pseudocompact. On the other hand, for
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that
\sigma_{nm}=\sigma_{n}\cap\sigma_{m}
and

\textbf{cl}_{M(\mathbb{N})}(\{nm\})=\textbf{cl}_{M(\mathbb{N})}(\{n\})\cap\textbf{cl}_{M(\mathbb{N})}(\{m\})
where
\textbf{cl}_{M(\mathbb{N})}(\{n\})
denotes the closure of the singleton set
\{n\}
in the topological space
M(\mathbb{N})
(see
[
9
]
). Furthermore, in
[
7
]
(using the topology
\tau_{M}
), a topological proof of the infinitude of prime numbers is presented (different from the proofs of Furstenberg
[
5
]
and Golomb
[
6
]
). In the same work, the infinitude of any non-empty subset of prime numbers is characterized in the following sense:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

If
A
is a non-empty subset of prime numbers, then
A
is infinite if and only if
A
is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
A
is infinite. Then, for any positive integer
n>1
, we can choose a prime
p\in A
such that
p>n
, and consequently,
p\in\sigma_{n}
since
\gcd(n,p)=1
. Therefore,
A
is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
. On the other hand, assume that
A
is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Let
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{k}\}\subset A
be a finite collection of prime numbers and consider the non-empty basic element
\sigma_{x}
where
x=p_{1}\cdot p_{2}\cdots p_{k}
. Note that none of the
p_{i}
belong to
\sigma_{x}
, but since
A
is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
, there must be another prime number
q
, different from each
p_{i}
, such that
q\in\sigma_{x}
. Consequently,
A
is infinite.
∎

The Lemma
2.1
, together with the following theorems, will be very useful in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gcd(n,m)=1
. Then, the set
n(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+m
contains an infinite number of prime numbers.

Proof.

The reader can check an
elementary proof
in
[
1
, Chapter 7]
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
f(x)
be a non-constant polynomial of the form (
1
). Then the set of prime numbers dividing some element of the set
\{f(n)\neq 0:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is infinite.

Proof.

The reader may consult a proof in
[
10
]
.
∎

3.
On continuous polynomials of the Macías space

Naturally, the following question arises: Does
[
14
, Theorem 4.3]
hold in the space
M(\mathbb{N})
? The answer is no. Consider the following example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the polynomial of the form (
1
) given by
f(x)=x^{2}+x=x(x+1)
. Note that
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{7}
has solutions in the complete residue system modulo
7
, namely
x=6,7
. Thus,

W:=f^{-1}(\sigma_{7})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{5}7(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+i.

Now, by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions (Theorem
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, the set
W^{\prime}:=7(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+6
, which is disjoint from
W
, is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Thus, if
W
were open, then
W\cap W^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
f
is not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

In general, continuity is also not guaranteed for polynomials of the form (
1
) when
a_{0}>0
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the polynomial of the form (
1
) given by
f(x)=x^{2}+4x+2
. Note that
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{7}
has solutions in the complete residue system modulo
7
, namely
x=1,2
. Thus,

W:=f^{-1}(\sigma_{7})=\bigcup_{i=3}^{7}7(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+i.

Now, by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions (Theorem
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, the set

W^{\prime}:=7(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+1\cup 7(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+2,

which is disjoint from
W
, is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Thus, if
W
were open, then
W\cap W^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
f
is not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

The following theorem characterizes the continuity of polynomials of the form (
1
) in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f
be a polynomial of the form (
1
). Then,
f
is continuous if and only if
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\sigma_{p}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\mathbb{N}
, or
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\emptyset
for every prime number
p
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\sigma_{p}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\mathbb{N}
, or
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\emptyset
for every prime number
p
. Consider a basic element
\sigma_{k}
. Clearly, if
k=1
, then
f^{-1}(\sigma_{k})=\mathbb{N}
. If
k>1
, consider its prime factorization, say
k=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}p_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots p_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}
. Then,

f^{-1}(\sigma_{k})=f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}\sigma_{p_{i}}\right)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}f^{-1}(\sigma_{p_{i}}).

By assumption,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p_{i}})=\sigma_{p_{i}}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p_{i}})=\mathbb{N}
, or
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p_{i}})=\emptyset
for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t\}
, all of which are open sets. Thus,
f
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Conversely, suppose that
f
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Then,

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\{x\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(f(x),p)=1\}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}:f(x)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\},

is an open set in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Now, suppose that for some prime number
p
, the set
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})
is non-empty, different from
\sigma_{p}
, and different from
\mathbb{N}
. Note that if
f(x)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for all
x\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
, then
\sigma_{p}\subsetneq f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})
, which implies that
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\cap p\mathbb{N}\neq\emptyset
. But this last statement implies that
p\mathbb{N}\subset f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})
. Therefore,
\mathbb{N}=\sigma_{p}\cup p\mathbb{N}\subset f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\subset\mathbb{N}
, which is absurd because
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\neq\mathbb{N}
. Hence, there exists at least one
x\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
such that
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Then,

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\cap p(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+x=\emptyset\ \ \text{for some}\ \ x\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}.

However, by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions (Theorem
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, the set
p(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+x
is dense in
M(\mathbb{N})
, and so
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\cap p(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})+x\neq\emptyset
(a contradiction). Thus,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\sigma_{p}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\mathbb{N}
, or
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\emptyset
for every prime number
p
.
∎

An immediate application of Theorem
3.3
is to identify families of continuous and non-continuous polynomials in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Polynomials
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
of the form
f(x)=a
, where
a
is a fixed positive integer (constant polynomials), are continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

Consider a basic element
\sigma_{p}
with
p
a prime number. Note that if
\gcd(a,p)=1
, then
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\mathbb{N}
. Otherwise (if
\gcd(a,p)>1
), we have
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\emptyset
. In either case, by Theorem
3.3
, it follows that
f
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

The polynomials
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
of the form
f(x)=x^{n}
are continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a prime number. Then,

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\{x\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(x^{n},p)=1\}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(x,p)=1\}=\sigma_{p}.

Thus, by Theorem
3.3
,
f
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be polynomials of the form (
1
), either continuous or constant. Then,
h(x):=f(x)g(x)
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a prime number. Note that
h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\cap h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})
. If
f
and
g
are constant, then

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p}),g^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\mathbb{N}\}.

In this case,
h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\mathbb{N}\}
.

On the other hand, suppose without loss of generality that
f
is of the form (
1
) and
g
is constant. Then, by Theorem
3.3
,

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\sigma_{p},\mathbb{N}\}\quad\text{and}\quad g^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\mathbb{N}\}.

In this case,
h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\sigma_{p},\mathbb{N}\}
.

Finally, if both
f
and
g
are of the form (
1
) and continuous, then

f^{-1}(\sigma_{p}),g^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\sigma_{p},\mathbb{N}\}.

Thus,
h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\sigma_{p},\mathbb{N}\}
. In any case, by Theorem
3.3
,
h
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In general, since
M(\mathbb{N})
is a topological semigroup (see
[
9
, Theorem 2.3]
), the product of two continuous functions on
M(\mathbb{N})
results in another continuous function on
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Corollary 3.8
.

The polynomials
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
of the form
f(x)=ax^{n}
with
a\in\mathbb{N}
are continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.6
.
∎

The examples
3.1
and
3.2
, along with the results presented so far, motivate the following question:

Question 3.9
.

Are the only continuous polynomials in
M(\mathbb{N})
the constant polynomials and those of the form
ax^{n}
with
a,n\in\mathbb{N}
?

The answer to Question
3.9
is affirmative; see Theorem
3.12
. First, consider the following pair of lemmas.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is a polynomial of the form (
1
) that is discontinuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
. Then,
h(x):=x^{k}f(x)
is also discontinuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

Since
f
is discontinuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
, by Theorem
3.3
, there exists a prime number
p
such that
\emptyset\neq f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\cap\sigma_{p}\subsetneq\sigma_{p}
. Then,

h^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\{x\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(x^{k},p)=1\}\cap f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\sigma_{p}\cap f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\subsetneq\sigma_{p},

which cannot be an open set by the same argument as in Theorem
3.3
. Therefore,
h
cannot be continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial of the form (
1
) with
a_{0}\neq 0
and at least one
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
i\in\{1,2,3,\dots,n\}
. Then,
f(x)
is not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that there exists at least one prime number
p>\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}
such that
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for some
x\in\{1,2,3,\dots,p-1,p\}
. In this case, necessarily
1<x<p
since
x=p
can not be a solution of
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
because
\gcd(a_{0},p)=1
. Then, by Theorem
3.3
,
f(x)
is not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Suppose that for every prime number
p>\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}
, we have
f(x)\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for all
x\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1,p\}
. Then, the set of prime numbers
p
that divide some element of the set
\{f(n)\neq 0:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is finite, which is a contradiction by Schur’s theorem (Theorem
2.3
).
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

The only continuous polynomials in
M(\mathbb{N})
are the polynomials the form
ax^{n}
with
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, Corollary
3.8
, and Lemma
3.11
, it suffices to show that polynomials
f(x)
of the form (
1
) with
a_{0}=0
and
a_{i},a_{j}\neq 0
for some distinct pair
i,j\in\{1,2,3,\dots,n\}
are discontinuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial of the previously described form. In this case, we can write
f(x)=xg(x)
, where
g(x)
is a polynomial of the form described in Lemma
3.11
. Thus, by Lemma
3.10
, we conclude that
f(x)
is not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

4.
Final comment

The statement of Theorem
3.3
can be extended to functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that if
x\equiv y\mod n
then
f(x)\equiv f(y)\mod n
for
n,x,y\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function such that if
x\equiv y\mod n
then
f(x)\equiv f(y)\mod n
for
n,x,y\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
f
is continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
if and only if
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\sigma_{p}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\mathbb{N}
, or
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})=\emptyset
for every prime number
p
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.3
. Note that the fact that
f
is a function such that if
x\equiv y\mod n
then
f(x)\equiv f(y)\mod n
for
n,x,y\in\mathbb{N}
allows us to repeat the argument of the converse of the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

For example, functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
of the form
f(x)=a^{x}+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\neq b
are not continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
by applying the theory of
primitive roots

\pmod{p}
with
p
a prime number and Theorem
4.1
. Of course, if we take a sufficiently large prime number
p
(
>a,b
) such that
b\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, then
f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
would have a unique nontrivial solution (
x\not\equiv 0\pmod{p})
in the complete residue system
\pmod{p}
, so that
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\notin\{\emptyset,\sigma_{p},\mathbb{N}\}
and therefore
f
would not be continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
. However, functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
of the form
a^{x}
with
a\in\mathbb{N}
would be continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
, indeed, in this case, it is easy to verify that for any prime number
p
, we would have
f^{-1}(\sigma_{p})\in\{\emptyset,\mathbb{N}\}
. In summary, the only
exponential functions
that are continuous in
M(\mathbb{N})
are those of the form
a^{x}
with
a\in\mathbb{N}
.

With the previously mentioned discussion, we aim to motivate the study of continuous functions in general over
M(\mathbb{N})
, perhaps conducting a study similar (as far as possible) to the one carried out by T. Banakh, J. Mioduszewski, and S. Turek in
[
3
]
on continuous functions in general over the Golomb topological space.

It is interesting that in the Golomb topological space, there are
more
continuous polynomials than in the Macías topological space. However, in the Macías topological space, there are
more
homeomorphisms than in the Golomb topological space; see
[
2
,
8
]
.

C–H Insertion from Isolable Copper Benzylidenes
Authors: Erika Amemiya, Shao-Liang Zheng, Theodore A. Betley
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Despite the utility of copper catalysts for the insertion of carbene moieties into C–H bonds, the copper carbene intermediate often invoked in these transformations has not been isolated. Herein, we describe the synthesis and structural characterization of a series of copper benzylidenes utilizing the sterically encumbered dipyrrin ligand (EmL)­H. These isolated copper carbenes demonstrate intramolecular insertion into the primary C­(sp3)–H bond of the ligand (EmL)H and intermolecular insertion into ethereal and allylic C–H bonds. The copper carbenes isolated are best described as Cu­(I) carbene adducts akin to canonical Fischer carbenes, given their diamagnetic ground state and electrophilic carbene reactivity. Furthermore, the insertion chemistry can be rendered catalytic utilizing a more sterically exposed dipyrrin ligand (ArFL)­H. The ability to isolate and observe stoichiometric C–H insertion and olefin cyclopropanation from well-characterized copper benzylidenes illuminates their viability as catalytic intermediates and their participation in potential catalyst deactivation pathways.

Introduction

Utilization of transition metal carbene species for C–H bond functionalization has garnered increasing interest for its potential to occur in an undirected, selective, and catalytic manner. , State-of-the-art dirhodium tetracarboxylate catalysts have had substantial success utilizing donor/acceptor diazo compounds in which the carbene moiety is decorated with aryl (donor) and α-carbonyl (acceptor) substituents. , Copper carbene complexes demonstrate similar electrophilic reactivity to their Rh2 congeners, including carbene insertion into E–H bonds (E = C, N, O, Si, etc.) ,, and olefin cyclopropanation , (Figure a). These copper catalysts have been investigated with a broader variety of diazo reagents, expanding the scope of moieties introduced into the functionalized bond.

(a) Use of copper catalysts for carbene transfer, (b) classification of transition metal carbenes, and (c) structure and reactivity of isolable copper carbenes.

Although copper carbene complexes have been invoked as intermediates in catalytic transformations, no structurally characterized copper carbene − has exhibited reactivity with C–H bonds. Structurally characterized copper carbenes feature aryl or heteroatom donor elements that serve to stabilize the carbene. Electronically, these copper carbenes are best described as Fischer-type carbenes, conventionally defined as low valent metal centers bonded to singlet carbenes (Figure b). Nonetheless, singly substituted copper carbenes (e.g., CHR where R = alkyl/aryl) approaching a more Schrock-like alkylidene motif remain elusive. Therefore, we sought to determine if copper can support benzylidene ligation, allowing us to investigate the extent to which stabilizing substituents, or lack thereof, influence metal-to-ligand π-backbonding and its attendant reactivity.

Our group and others have previously shown the utility of the sterically encumbered dipyrrin ligand (EmL)H (Em = 1,1,7,7-tetraethyl-1,2,3,5,6,7-hexahydro-3,3,5,5-tetramethyl-s-indacene) in aiding the isolation of highly reactive species. ,, For example, Zhang and co-workers have reported the isolation of a copper difluorocarbene on a dipyrrin ligand with Em substituents. Thus, we endeavored to isolate reactive copper carbene complexes resembling Schrock-type alkylidene motifs and investigate their structure and reactivity.

Herein, we describe the synthesis and structural characterization of a series of copper benzylidene complexes that display carbene insertion into C­(sp3)–H bonds. The transformation could be rendered catalytic with the use of a less sterically encumbered dipyrrin ligand. As these copper carbenes are isolable, their structural and spectroscopic properties, and reactivity have been interrogated, elucidating their electronic structures. Studies thus far suggest that a Cu­(I) carbene adduct is the best electronic descriptor for these benzylidene complexes, more akin to canonical Fischer carbenes despite their apparent Schrock-type alkylidene makeup. These studies clarify the interplay between metal–ligand bonding, stability, and catalytically relevant reactivity in copper carbene species, pointing toward further avenues in catalyst development.

Results and Discussion

Isolation of a Disubstituted Copper Carbene via Diazo Reagent

Entry to a copper carbene system was gained by treatment of a frozen cyclohexane solution of a copper­(I) synthon (EmL)­Cu­(N2) (1) with a thawing cyclohexane solution of diphenyldiazomethane leading to a color change from orange to dark red purple (Scheme ). Analysis of the reaction mixture after 5 min via 19F NMR spectroscopy revealed the presence of two resonances, one being the final product (δ 63.63 ppm in C6D12, ca. 75% of mixture by integration) and the other attributed to a diazo adduct (δ 63.61 ppm, ca. 25%), presumed to be (EmL)­Cu­(N2CPh2) (Figure S4). A metal-diazo adduct like the latter is known to precede generation of metal carbene species. , Analysis by 1H NMR spectroscopy also revealed the formation of two new diamagnetic dipyrrin complexes and benzophenone azine, a common decomposition product of disubstituted aryl diazoalkanes. Over the course of 3 h, the signal attributed to the diazo adduct disappeared, and that for 1 reappeared. The reaction mixture was treated with additional diphenyldiazomethane until 1 was fully consumed and only one fluorinated species was observed by 19F NMR spectroscopy. The 13C­{1H} NMR spectrum of the product exhibited a diagnostic, downfield resonance at δ 279 ppm (Figure a), consistent with the range of chemical shifts commonly observed for transition metal carbenes. , Following removal of the azine byproduct via its recrystallization in pentane, crystals of the product suitable for X-ray diffraction were obtained from slow evaporation of a concentrated diethyl ether solution stored at −33 °C for 4 days. X-ray diffraction analysis revealed the formation of the terminal copper carbene adduct (EmL)­Cu­(CPh2) (2) (Figure a). Examination of the structure reveals a nearly planar diphenylcarbene unit (average sum of angles around Ccarbene: 359.7[5]°) that is oriented orthogonal to the dipyrrin plane, with one phenyl group approximately parallel to the ligand hydrindacene substituents and the other canted ±60.1[4]° relative to the first phenyl group. The average Cu–C distance (1.839[4] Å between two molecules in the asymmetric unit) is comparable to the Cu–C distances observed in previously isolated copper carbene complexes (1.822(4)–1.9093(17) Å). − The average Cu–Ndipyrrin distance observed in 2 (1.974[4] Å) is also consistent with that observed for other (EmL)­CuI species (1.937(2)–2.034(5) Å) as opposed to those for (EmL)­CuII species (1.885(3)–1.935(3) Å). ,

Synthesis of Copper Carbene Complexes

Preparation of Monosubstituted Copper Carbenes

With evidence for the ability of (EmL)Cu to support a carbene adduct, we envisioned preparing monosubstituted analogues with minimal stabilizing substituents on the carbenoid carbon. As these species bear a resemblance to canonical Schrock alkylidenes, we were also interested in exploring whether less decorated copper carbenes would display divergent reactivity from established disubstituted copper carbenes. Akin to the preparation of 2, monosubstituted metal carbenes can be generated using monosubstituted diazo compounds. However, great caution is necessary when preparing and utilizing monosubstituted diazo compounds, especially those that are not stabilized by an α-carbonyl moiety, since they are prone to decomposition and thus difficult and unsafe to isolate in large quantities. The difficulty in isolating these reagents may also explain why first row late transition metal monosubstituted carbenes are rare, apart from copper carbenes featuring π-donor dialkyl amine substituents , and Hillhouse’s nickel carbene with a bulky triaryl substituent. Despite this liability, following modified literature protocol, a series of monoaryl diazo reagents N2CHAr (Ar = naphthyl, mesityl, 2,6-dichlorophenyl) were prepared from the corresponding commercially available aldehydes and isolated on small scales (ca. 2 mmol, 300–400 mg) while minimizing exposure to light, heat, and vacuum for further use.

Slow addition of dilute hexanes solutions of N2CHAr (Ar = naphthyl, mesityl, 2,6-dichlorophenyl) to dilute hexanes solutions of 1 resulted in a gradual color change from orange to dark red (Scheme ). Analysis by 1H, 19F, and 13C­{1H} NMR spectroscopy revealed the formation of new diamagnetic species identified as (EmL)­Cu­(CHAr) (Ar = naphthyl, 3; mesityl, 4; 2,6-dichlorophenyl, 5). Slow addition of dilute solutions of the diazo reagents was necessary to minimize the formation of olefins (ArHCCHAr), whose presence precludes clean isolation of the desired copper carbene species. Olefin formation during copper carbene synthesis can be eliminated with an appropriately slow addition (see Supporting Information (SI)); however, olefin impurities carried through from diazo reagent synthesis were present in some cases.

Though the diazo reagents were effective in providing access to naphthyl, mesityl, and 2,6-dichlorophenyl substituted benzylidenes, access to smaller parent CHPh and para-fluorinated CH­(p-FC6H4) benzylidenes required the use of the cyclic isomer, diazirines. Diazirines are more stable and thus safer to handle compared to diazo compounds. For example, the use of phenyldiazomethane to access the parent benzylidene presents significant handling hazards that are largely obviated in the case of the analogous diazirine, and in our hands, the diazirines have been prepared with higher levels of purity and display a longer shelf life at −33 °C in the glovebox. Furthermore, despite being utilized in the preparation of polynuclear metal carbene species and biocatalytic carbene transfer, diazirines have been underexplored in the synthesis of terminal carbene adducts.

Treatment of a frozen cyclohexane solution of 1 with a thawing cyclohexane solution of 3-phenyl-3H-diazirine resulted in effervescence and a gradual color change to dark red (Scheme ). Like the spectroscopic features seen in the synthesis of 2, a transient resonance in the 19F NMR spectrum distinct from those assigned to 1 and the eventual product was observed, which we tentatively assign to a diazirine or diazo adduct (EmL)­Cu­(N2CHPh) (Figure S18). Following the consumption of 1 and the transient intermediate, a new diamagnetic carbene adduct (EmL)­Cu­(CHPh) (6) is generated without the need for slow addition of the diazirine. In addition, use of 3-(4-fluoro)­phenyl-3H-diazirine enabled access to the fluorine-substituted copper benzylidene (EmL)­Cu­[CH­(p-FC6H4)] (7).

The monosubstituted copper carbenes feature well-resolved diamagnetic NMR spectra, displaying distinct downfield 1H NMR signals (δ/ppm in C6D6: 3, 11.04; 4, 11.83; 5, 11.05; 6, 11.02; 7, 10.83) that integrate to one proton, corresponding to the carbene C–H nucleus. While the 19F NMR spectra for 3 and 6 consist of a single peak (δ/ppm: 3, −62.36; 6, −62.37), those for 4 and 5 show two resonances of equal integration (δ/ppm: 4, −62.31, −62.38; 5, −62.33, −62.40), suggesting that the carbene moiety of the ortho-substituted copper carbene complexes are static on the NMR time scale in solution. The two unique fluorine environments in 7 are observable via 19F NMR, where the ligand CF3 groups resonate at δ −62.37 ppm (6F), and the fluorine atom on the carbene resonates at δ −101.93 ppm (1F). The formation of a Cu–C bond is corroborated by the presence of downfield shifted 13C NMR signals (δ/ppm: 3, 264; 4, 254; 5, 233; 6, 267; 7, 263; Figure a). Interestingly, the ortho-substituted carbene adducts 4 and 5the latter of which is expected to possess a particularly electrophilic carbene carbon and displays high reactivity relative to the others (vide infra)exhibit the most upfield-shifted resonances for the copper-bound carbon nucleus.

The monosubstituted copper carbene adducts 3 and 6 were isolated and characterized by single crystal X-ray diffraction (Figure b,c). In both structures, the carbene aryl substituents are positioned orthogonal to the dipyrrin plane, with the naphthyl and phenyl groups oriented approximately parallel to the Em aryl substituents. Furthermore, the Cu–C distances (3, 1.807[18] Å averaged between two molecules in the asymmetric unit; 6, 1.807(4) Å) are comparable to those observed in 2 and previously reported Fischer copper carbenes (1.822(4)–1.9093(17) Å), − and both structures exhibit rigorous planarity at the carbene carbon (sum of angles around Ccarbene/°: 3, 360.0­[15]; 6, 360.0[3]). Examining the C–C bond lengths within each carbene fragment reveal no systematic bond perturbations (3, C–CNaph: 1.40[9] Å; 6, C–CPh: 1.396[16] Å; Figures S52 and S53) that would indicate redox delocalization into the carbene aryl unit, as has been observed in redox non-innocent nitrenoid fragments. ,− The averages of the Cu–Ndipyrrin distances observed in 3 (1.954[19] Å) and 6 (1.938[4] Å) is also consistent with that observed for other (EmL)­CuI species (1.937(2)–2.034(5) Å) as opposed to those for (EmL)­CuII species (1.885(3)–1.935(3) Å). ,

Computational Investigation of the Copper Carbene Electronic Structure

Structural and spectroscopic data obtained for the copper benzylidenes, as well as their electrophilic reactivity (vida infra), suggest that they are best described as copper­(I) singlet carbene adducts. In this configuration, the carbene moiety is bound to the copper center as a neutral, Lewis base-type ligand (i.e., L-type as defined by the covalent bond classification method). The singlet carbene electronic structure is supported by density functional theory (DFT) calculations on a truncated model of 6 that suggest that the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO)comprising of Cu–CHPh antibonding interactions of π-symmetryis predominantly carbene character (Figure a; Cu 17.7%, CHPh 70.2%). The high ligand parentage of the LUMO is indicative of an inverted ligand field in which the copper d manifold is fully occupied and lower in energy than the carbene C 2p orbital, corresponding to a Cu­(I) d 10 center. This inverted ligand field is also reflected in the corresponding bonding orbital (HOMO–1, Figure b) which exhibits higher copper parentage than that of ligand. Furthermore, the σ interaction between the copper center and the benzylidene is highly stabilized (HOMO–25, Cu 34.5%, Ccarbene,2­(s+p z ) 9.9%) relative to free singlet benzylidene (HOMO, 2s 16.2%, 2p z 63.7%), indicating that the nucleophilicity of the benzylidene fragment is quenched by the donation of its sp2 lone pair into the Lewis acidic copper center (Figure S54). The copper­(I) singlet carbene electronic description is most akin to canonical Fischer-type carbenes, rather than Schrock-type alkylidenes. Presumably, the latter configuration is disfavored as it implies a formally copper­(III) center bound to a triplet carbene (X2-type). Furthermore, the classification of the copper benzylidenes as Fischer-type carbenes is also consistent with the natural population of the C 2pπ orbital (0.61), which is nearly identical to those calculated for canonical monosubstituted Fischer carbenes (OC)5W­(CHR) (R = NMe2, OMe; 0.61) and is distinct from those for Mo and W-based Schrock alkylidenes (1.01–1.14) and Ru olefin metathesis catalysts (0.72–0.83).

Calculated molecular orbitals and atomic orbital contributions for (a) LUMO and (b) HOMO–1 of 6 with truncated ligand.

Despite the absence of multiple stabilizing groups on the carbene fragments of 2–7, the copper centers remain in their low-valent state. The lack of significant π-backbonding from copper and its preference for a low-valent state is consistent with the electronic configuration of our previously reported terminal copper­(I) triplet nitrenes (EmL)­Cu­(NAr), prepared on the same dipyrrin ligand platform. The singlet carbene electronic structure assignment is also commensurate with that described for dirhodium donor/acceptor carbene species active for C–H insertion, for which the dirhodium carbene bond lacked significant π-backbonding character.

Formation and Stability of Copper Benzylidenes

The olefin (ArHCCHAr) and azine (Ar2CN–NCAr2) species observed when introducing the diazo reagent in one portion (no slow addition) are common byproducts observed in copper-catalyzed reactions involving carbene fragments (vida infra). , The formation of olefin during copper carbene synthesis from diazo reagents may occur via the reaction between copper carbene and unreacted diazo reagent, or via coupling of two copper carbenes. Considering copper carbenes 2–4, 6, and 7 are stable in benzene at room temperature for several days, and for longer in non-donor solvents such as pentane, the latter, reductive coupling pathway is unlikely. Furthermore, treatment of 6 with 7 in dilute hexanes solution results in no mixed olefin formation (4-fluorostilbene) over the course of 1 h (Figure S43), suggesting that within this family of copper carbene adducts, olefin formation does not originate from the reaction of two copper carbenes. Instead, olefin may be generated from nucleophilic attack by the linear diazo reagent at a copper carbene, a mechanism previously suggested for the formation of olefin during the synthesis of diaryl copper carbenes and in copper-catalyzed cyclopropanation. ,,

The azine byproduct observed during the preparation of 2 may also result from similar pathways, where the reaction of diazo reagent with copper carbene or the spectroscopically observed putative copper diazo adduct (Figure S4) leads to consumption of diazo reagent. The consumption of diazo reagent leading to non-copper carbene products is consistent with the excess diphenyldiazomethane required for the synthesis of 2 to reach completion. The reaction of diazo reagents with copper complexes to form the olefin and azine is consistent with the absence of these byproducts under slow addition conditions, which decreases the concentration of diazo reagent in solution. The mechanistic proposal is also commensurate with the observation that slow addition is not necessary when using diazirines as the carbene source, since free diazo species are absent in solution, obviating problematic side-reaction pathways that lead to the generation of azine and olefin byproducts. ,,

Electrophilic Reactivity of Copper Carbenes

While copper carbene complexes 2–4 and 6 are persistent in solution, multinuclear NMR analysis of in situ generated 5 immediately after slow addition revealed the appearance of a new diamagnetic species exhibiting two 19F NMR resonances, accompanied by a decrease in the signal for the carbene C–H nucleus in the 1H NMR spectrum. The product of the reaction was trapped as a pyridine adduct, and analysis of a crystalline sample by single crystal X-ray diffraction revealed that the carbene moiety had inserted into one of the Em methyl groups, generating 8 (Figure a,b). The reactivity of 5 relative to the other copper carbene complexes suggests that the presence of the electron-withdrawing chlorine atoms imparts sufficient electrophilic character to the carbene carbon to induce intramolecular carbene insertion into a ligand sp3 C–H bond under ambient conditions. Interestingly, analogous intramolecular reactivity is not observed from 7 containing a para-fluoro substituent. Therefore, these contrasting results suggest that both steric and electronic factors influence the reactivity of these benzylidene moieties.

With the knowledge that 5 facilitates intramolecular C–H insertion, we sought to investigate the reactivity of the isolated copper carbenes toward intermolecular C–H insertion. In neat THF solution, 6 demonstrates stoichiometric insertion of the carbene moiety into the α-C–H bond of THF, as determined by 1H NMR spectroscopy (Figure c). The same reactivity is observed for benzylidenes 3, 4, and 7, although complete conversion requires longer reaction times for 3 (2 days) and 4 (3 days) (Figures S32–S35). When isolated and exposed to THF immediately following slow addition, 5 also demonstrates intermolecular carbene insertion into THF. The reactivity observed from these copper benzylidenes marks the first demonstration of C–H insertion from an isolated copper carbene species. Kinetic isotope effect (KIE) measurements using an intermolecular competition experiment between tetrahydrofuran-h 8 and tetrahydrofuran-d 8 resulted in a KIE value of 2.7(1), consistent with a concerted C–H insertion mechanism and similar to KIE values obtained for Rh2-catalyzed C–H insertion. Furthermore, in the presence of 1,4-cyclohexadiene (1,4-CHD), 6 demonstrates selectivity for insertion into the allylic C–H bond over cyclopropanation at the alkene, as evidenced by the formation of diphenylmethane upon treatment with DDQ (2,3-dichloro-5,6-dicyano-1,4-benzoquinone) which oxidizes the carbene-functionalized cyclohexadiene. However, both 6 and 7 exhibit cyclopropanation of styrene like many of the previously isolated copper carbene complexes. − The steric inaccessibility resulting from the bulky Em substituents may contribute to the observed chemoselectivity in the presence of 1,4-CHD, akin to how bulky ligands and diazo reagents tune dirhodium systems to favor C–H insertion over cyclopropanation.

The observed reactivity of the copper benzylidenes with C–H bonds and olefins further corroborates the electrophilic nature of the carbene, in contrast to nucleophilic amine-substituted copper carbenes demonstrating transmetalation reactivity. In fact, no transmetalation is observed from 7 in the presence of [Rh­(COD)­Cl]2 (Figures S44 and S45), highlighting the diminished nucleophilicity of monosubstituted carbenes in the absence of π-donor substituents. The unique C–H insertion reactivity observed from 3–7 relative to previously isolated copper carbenes likely originates from both steric and electronic effects from the dipyrrin and carbene ligands. The (EmL) ligand kinetically stabilizes the copper carbene moiety while providing a channel-like architecture that may help orient the C–H substrate, as seen in highly active Rh2 catalytic systems employing bulky ligands and diazo compounds. Furthermore, since no reactivity toward C–H or olefin substrates is observed from diphenyl-substituted 2, the presence of only one aryl group in the benzylidenes enhances the electrophilicity of the carbene while also providing space for the substrate to approach the carbene. Indeed, the calculated electrophilicity index (ω) for a truncated model of benzylidene 6 (ω = 3.05) suggests the fragment is only slightly less electrophilic than the free singlet benzylidene (CHPh, ω = 3.53), though significantly more electrophilic than indices calculated for early metal analogues (e.g., (OC)5Cr­(CHPh), ω = 1.97). The latter comparison highlights the comparatively diminished capacity of (EmL)Cu to back π-donate to the electrophilic benzylidene than low-valent early transition metal analogues.

Preliminary studies demonstrate that catalytic carbene insertion of a benzylidene into the ethereal C–H bond of THF is possible when utilizing a less sterically encumbered ligand platform, (ArFL)Cu (9) (Ar = 2,4,6-triphenyl­(aryl), F = 3,5-bistrifluoromethyl­(aryl) backbone) (Figure ), which has been shown to be active in catalytic amination of THF. Slow addition of 20 equiv of 2,6-dichlorodiazomethane to a dilute solution of 9 in THF led to the formation of the carbene-functionalized THF product, E- and Z-2,2′,6,6′-tetrachlorostilbene, and 2,6-dichlorobenzaldehyde azine. The olefin and azine byproducts observed likely originate from similar pathways to those during the synthesis of the copper benzylidenes, as outlined above. Use of 3-phenyl-3H-diazirine instead of diazo compound as the carbene source leads to no formation of the THF insertion product and unproductive reactivity, observed as numerous intractable species in the 1H NMR spectrum of the reaction mixture. This is consistent with the observation that no reaction occurs upon treatment of 9 with 3-phenyl-3H-diazirine, most likely due to the competitive binding of the flanking triphenylaryl groups to the Cu center, and the poor donor ability of diazirines relative to the diazo compounds. The competitive binding of the ligand may also be contributing to the low catalytic yield observed using the diazo compounds, and future studies will include a more detailed analysis of the kinetics of this reaction.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have reported the synthesis and structural characterization of copper benzylidenes capable of intra- and intermolecular C–H insertion. Use of diazirines or slow addition of diazo reagents allows access to monosubstituted copper carbenes. Despite the extensive use of copper as a catalyst for carbene C–H insertion reactions, 3 and 6 are the first examples of structurally characterized copper carbenes that demonstrate C–H insertion. Structural and spectroscopic data, as well as DFT and reactivity studies obtained thus far suggest that these benzylidenes are best described as copper­(I) singlet carbene adducts akin to canonical Fischer carbenes, despite the monosubstituted motif being reminiscent of Schrock-type alkylidenes. The reluctance of copper to adopt a high-valent state, along with the lack of significant stabilization from the single aryl motif, enhances the electrophilicity of the carbene and its attendant C–H insertion reactivity. Use of a less sterically encumbering ligand (ArFL)H allows for demonstration of C–H insertion catalysis utilizing the benzylidene motif and establishes the relevance of copper benzylidenes in copper-catalyzed carbene transfer. More generally, monosubstituted alkyl/aryl carbenes utilized in group transfer catalysis may also fall into this class of electrophilic Fischer-type carbene species. , Future studies will include an investigation of the reactivity of these copper benzylidenes toward non-C–H substrates.

Approximation rates for finite mixtures of location-scale models
Hien Duy Nguyen 1,2 , TrungTin Nguyen 3,4 , Jacob Westerhout 5 , and Xin Guo 5

Abstract
Finite mixture models provide useful methods for approximation and estimation of probability density functions. In the context of location-scale mixture models, we improve upon previous results to show that it is possible to quantitatively bound the ℒ p {\cal L}_{p} approximation error in terms of the number of mixture components m m for any p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in\left(1,\infty\right) . In particular, for approximation on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} of targets f 0 ∈ 𝒲 s , p f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{s,p} , the (fractional) Sobolev spaces with smoothness order s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] s\in\left(0,1\right] , if p < 2 p<2 then the error has size O ⁡ ( m − s s ​ q + d ) O\left(m^{-\frac{s}{sq+d}}\right) , while if p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , the error has size O ⁡ ( m − 2 ​ s 2 ​ ( s ​ q + d ) ) O\left(m^{-\frac{2s}{2\left(sq+d\right)}}\right) , where q = 1 / ( p − 1 ) q=1/\left(p-1\right) . We demonstrate that these results can be used to derive estimation rates for a location-scale mixture-based adaptive least-squares estimator.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background and prior results

Finite mixture models appear ubiquitously throughout the statistics
literature and their application and theory have been widely and comprehensively
espoused in classic works such as
Everitt & Hand, 1981
,

Titterington et al., 1985

McLachlan & Basford, 1988
,

Lindsay, 1995
;
McLachlan & Peel, 2000
,
and
Frühwirth-Schnatter, 2006
, along with more
recent texts such as
McNicholas, 2015
,
Ng et al., 2019
,

Robert et al., 2019
,
Chen, 2023
,
and
Yao & Xiang, 2024
, among many other sources. In application,
finite mixtures are typically employed to perform one of two primary
tasks: to provide a statistical model for a population that is hypothesized
or known to have a heterogenous subpopulation structure, or to provide
a flexible approximation to some unknown probability distribution.
When the finite mixtures are employed for the latter task, the Gaussian
mixture model is typically considered for its folkloric ability to
provide accurate approximation typically encountered probability models
(cf.
Nguyen & McLachlan, 2019
and references therein).

On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the fact that linear combinations of Gaussian
kernels can approximate sufficiently regular functions in
{\cal L}_{p}

spaces goes back to the Tauberian theory of
Wiener, 1932

(cf.
Achieser, 1956
, Ch. VI
). Subsequent
generalizations and developments on such results appear throughout
the approximation theory literature, and include the works of
Park & Sandberg, 1991
,

Park & Sandberg, 1993
,
Xu et al., 1993
,
Sandberg, 2001
,
and
Nestoridis et al., 2011
—see also
Cheney & Light, 2000
.

When we restrict the approximand
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

to be a probability density function, and the approximator class (
{\cal A}
,
say) to be convex combinations of probability density kernels, the
approximation problem requires different tools. When
f_{0}
is within
the the closure of
{\cal A}
, and the class
{\cal A}
is uniformly
bounded in
{\cal L}_{p}
norm, a Maurey–Barron–Jones type result
of
Donahue et al., 1997
provides an approximation theorem with
known and optimal rate (see, e.g.,
Zeevi & Meir, 1997
and

Nguyen & McLachlan, 2019
for example). However, when
f_{0}

is not in the closure of
{\cal A}
or when
{\cal A}
is unbounded
in
{\cal L}_{p}
norm, the study of approximation demands more care.

In
Bacharoglou, 2010
, it is proved that by taking
{\cal A}
to be the class of convex combinations
of Gaussian densities on
\mathbb{R}
,
one can arbitrarily well approximate
f_{0}
in any
{\cal L}_{p}

norm (
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
) on
\mathbb{R}
, under integrability and tail
conditions. Then, in
Nguyen et al., 2020
and
Nguyen et al., 2023b
,
generalizations are obtained, whereupon it is proved that on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f_{0}
can be approximated arbitrarily well when
{\cal A}
is
taken to be location-scale mixtures of some fixed probability density
function, under various regularity conditions.

1.2
Main contributions

In this work, we extend upon the qualitative results of
Nguyen et al., 2020

and
Nguyen et al., 2023b
, by proving that it is also possible
to obtain the
{\cal L}_{p}
approximation rate in the number of
mixture components (i.e., number of vertices of the approximating
convex combination) when conducting approximation using location-scale
mixture models when some smoothness assumption on
f_{0}
hold. Particularly,
for classes of targets
f_{0}
in the Sobolev space
{\cal W}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

(
Leoni, 2017
)
or the fractional Sobolev spaces
{\cal W}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

(
s\in\left(0,1\right)
;
Leoni, 2023
) with
bounded norms,
f_{0}
has smoothness coefficient
\alpha=1
or

\alpha=s
, respectively. Then, for every
f_{0}
whose Sobolev
(or fractional Sobolev) norm is bounded, if
{\cal A}
and
f_{0}

satisfy appropriate integrability assumptions, then there exists sequences
of location-scale mixtures
\left(f_{m}\right)_{m}\subset{\cal A}

such that

\left\|f_{m}-f_{0}\right\|_{p}\apprle\begin{cases}m^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha q+d}}&\text{if }1<p<2\text{,}\\
m^{-\frac{\alpha q}{2\left(\alpha q+d\right)}}&\text{if }2\leq p<\infty\text{,}\end{cases}

where
1/p+1/q=1
.

As an application of our result, we revisit the problem of adaptive
least-squares density estimation considered in
Klemelä, 2007

in a joint approximation and estimation setup, where the the expected
squared
{\cal L}_{2}
error between the least-squares estimator
and the target
f_{0}
is shown to converge at rate
n^{-1/2}
when

f_{0}
is in the closure of
{\cal A}
, by taking estimator sequences
with numbers of mixture components increasing at rate
n^{1/2}
(see,
also
Klemela, 2009
, Ch. 19
). We provide an analogous
result for situations when
f_{0}
is not in the closure of
{\cal A}
,
and demonstrate that that convergence rate of
n^{-\alpha/\left(2\alpha+d\right)}

can be achieved by similarly using estimators whose components number
increase at the same rate.

2
Preliminaries

{\cal L}_{p}
spaces

For each
p\in\left[1,\infty\right)
, say that
f\in{\cal L}_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

if
\left\|f\right\|_{p}<\infty
, where
\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\text{d}x\right)^{1/p}

denotes the corresponding norm of the space. When
p=\infty
, we
say that
f\in{\cal L}_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
if
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\inf\left\{c\geq 0\text{ | }\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\leq c\text{ for }\lambda\text{-a.e. }x\right\}<\infty
,
where
\lambda
always denotes the Lebesgue measure on an appropriate
set. When there is no confusion, we will simplify
{\cal L}_{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

to
{\cal L}_{p}
, along with other such notation. Note that for
vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we will also write
\left\|x\right\|_{p}

to denote the
\ell_{p}
norm of the vector, unless it is misleading
to do so.

Sobolev spaces

For each
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, we say that
f
admits first-order
weak derivatives in
{\cal L}_{p}
if for each
k\in\left[d\right]=\left[1,d\right]\cap\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a
v_{k}\in{\cal L}_{p}
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(x\right)D_{k}u\left(x\right)\text{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{k}\left(x\right)u\left(x\right)\text{d}x\text{,}

for all smooth and compactly supported functions
u\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
where
D_{k}u=\partial u/\partial x_{k}
and we write
v_{k}=D_{k}f
.
The vector of first-order weak derivatives can then be written as

x\mapsto\nabla f=\left(D_{1}f,\dots,D_{d}f\right)\left(x\right)

and we say that
f\in{\cal W}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
if

\left\|f\right\|_{{\cal W}^{1,p}}=\left\|f\right\|_{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\|D_{k}f\right\|_{p}<\infty\text{.}

Similarly, for each
p\in\left[1,\infty\right)
and
s\in\left(0,1\right)
,
we say that
f\in{\cal W}^{s,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
if

\left\|f\right\|_{{\cal W}^{s,p}}=\left\|f\right\|_{p}+\left\|f\right\|_{s,p}<\infty\text{,}

where

\left\|f\right\|_{s,p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|f\left(x\right)-f\left(y\right)\right|^{p}}{\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}^{d+sp}}\text{d}x\text{d}y\text{.}

It is notable that the fractional Sobolev space
{\cal W}^{s,p}

is equal to the Besov space
{\cal B}_{p}^{s,p}
and should be familiar
to those working in nonparametric statistics (cf.
Leoni, 2023
).

Classes of probability density functions

Let
{\cal P}=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{ | }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(x\right)\text{d}x=1\right\}

denote the set of all probability density functions (PDFs) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Then our target
f_{0}
is in
{\cal P}
. Next, let
{\cal Q}\subset{\cal P}

be an arbitrary class of PDFs. Then we can define the set of
m
-component
mixtures (convex combination of
m
vertices) of class
{\cal Q}

by

\text{co}_{m}\left({\cal Q}\right)=\left\{f=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pi_{j}q_{j}\text{ | }q_{j}\in{\cal Q}\text{, }\pi_{j}\geq 0\text{, }\sum_{j=1}^{m}\pi_{j}=1\text{, }j\in\left[m\right]\right\}\text{,}

The set of all finite mixtures of class
{\cal Q}
(all convex combinations)
is then written as

\text{co}\left({\cal Q}\right)=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\text{co}_{m}\left({\cal Q}\right)\text{,}

where its
{\cal L}_{p}
closure
\overline{\text{co}\left({\cal Q}\right)}^{p}

is the set that contains the limit points of all sequences
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\text{co}\left({\cal Q}\right)
,
with respect to the
{\cal L}_{p}
topology.

For an arbitrary base PDF
\varphi\in{\cal P}
, we define its dilation
(in the language of
Cheney & Light, 2000
) by
\nu>0

by
x\mapsto\varphi_{\nu}=\nu^{d}\varphi\left(\nu x\right)
, and
subsequently we define the location–scale class of
\varphi
by

{\cal P}_{\varphi}=\left\{x\mapsto\varphi_{\nu}\left(x-\mu\right)\text{ | }\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }\nu>0\right\}\text{.}

The location–scale mixtures of
\varphi
are then
\text{co}\left({\cal P}_{\varphi}\right)
.
We will also find use for location-scale classes of
\varphi
, where

\nu
is fixed, in which case, we shall write
{\cal P}_{\varphi,\nu}=\left\{x\mapsto\varphi_{\nu}\left(x-\mu\right)\text{ | }\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}
.

Convolutions

For two
\lambda
-measurable functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we can write their convolution as

x\mapsto\left(f*g\right)\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(y\right)g\left(x-y\right)\text{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(x-y\right)g\left(y\right)\text{d}y\text{,}

where we say that the convolution exists if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\left(y\right)g\left(x-y\right)\right|\text{d}y<\infty
,
for
\lambda
-a.e.
x
. In particular, if
f\in{\cal L}_{1}
and

g\in{\cal L}_{p}
(
p\geq 1
; e.g., when
f,g\in{\cal P}
), then
the convolution exists,
\left\|f*g\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|g\right\|_{p}
,
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(f*g\right)\left(x\right)\text{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(x\right)\text{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\left(y\right)\text{d}y
.

Empirical processes

Let
\left(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\text{P}\right)
be a probability space
with typical element
\omega
and expectation operation
E
,
supporting the random vector
X:\left(\Omega,\mathfrak{F}\right)\to\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathfrak{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right)
,
where
\mathfrak{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\left(X_{n}\right)_{n}
be an independent
and identically distributed sample, where
X_{1}
is identically
distributed to
X
. Let
\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}:\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|<\infty\right\}

denote the space of bounded functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for

f\in\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, we define the operations

f\mapsto P_{n}f=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\left(X_{i}\right)
and
f\mapsto Pf=\text{E}^{*}f\left(X\right)
, where
\text{E}^{*}
is the outer expectation operation, as defined in
Giné & Nickl, 2016
.
For a set
{\cal F}\subset\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,
we say that it is a VC-subgraph class if the family of sets
{\cal C}=\left\{\mathbb{G}_{f}\text{ | }f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

has a finite Vapnik–Chervonenkis (VC) dimension
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal C}\right)>0
,
where
\mathbb{G}_{f}=\left\{\left(x,t\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\text{ | }t\leq f\left(x\right)\right\}

(cf.
Giné & Nickl, 2016
, Sec. 3.6
) and with some abuse of nomenclature, we
also say that
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal C}\right)
is the VC dimension
of
{\cal F}
:
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal F}\right)
. We say that
{\cal F}

has an envelope
\bar{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
if
\left|f\left(x\right)\right|\leq\bar{F}\left(x\right)

for every
f\in{\cal F}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we write
its
{\cal L}_{p}\left(P\right)
norm as
\left\|\bar{F}\right\|_{p}=\left[P\bar{F}^{p}\right]^{1/p}
.
In this text, we will assume that all functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable and classes
{\cal F}
are normal integrands (cf.
Rockafellar & Wets, 1998
, Sec. 14.D
), thus
\text{E}^{*}f\left(X\right)=\text{E}f\left(X\right)
in all cases, and typical operations over the class
{\cal F}
, such as averages, infima, and superpositions will all be measurable.

3
Approximation rates for location-scale mixtures

3.1
Approximation in Lebesgue spaces

The following theorem, proved in Appendix
A
, constitutes the main technical result of this
manuscript.

Theorem 1
.

For
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
, let

\varphi,f_{0}\in{\cal L}_{p}\cap{\cal P}
and assume that for some
finite
\alpha,K_{1},K_{2}>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{\alpha}\varphi\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq K_{1}\text{,}

(1)

and

\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f_{0}\left(x-y\right)-f_{0}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{1/p}\leq K_{2}\left\|y\right\|_{2}^{\alpha}\text{.}

(2)

Then, there exists a sequence
\left(f_{m}\right)_{m}
with
f_{m}\in\mathrm{co}_{m}\left({\cal P}_{\varphi}\right)
,
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\left\|f_{m}-f_{0}\right\|_{p}\leq\begin{cases}Km^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha q+d}}&\text{if }1<p<2\text{,}\\
Km^{-\frac{\alpha q}{2\left(\alpha q+d\right)}}&\text{if }2\leq p<\infty\text{,}\end{cases}

where
1/p+1/q=1
and
K
is a function of
\alpha,K_{1},K_{2},p,d,\varphi
.

3.2
Approximation of functions in Sobolev spaces

Condition (
2
) is a kind of smoothness
assumption enforced on
f_{0}
. For instance, if
f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{1,p}
,
it holds with
\alpha=1
and

K_{2}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(x\right)\right\|_{2}^{p}\text{d}x\right]^{1/p}\text{,}

and if
f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{s,p}
, then the condition holds with
\alpha=s

and
K_{2}=\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{s,p}
(cf. Lemmas
9
and
10
).

If we inspect the composition of constant
K
, only
\alpha

and
K_{2}
are explicitly dependent on
f_{0}
, with
K_{1}
being
implicitly determined, since
\varphi
can be chosen conditionally
on knowing the smoothness of
f_{0}
as to satisfy condition (
1
).
In fact, a PDF
\varphi
with compact support will satisfy (
1
)
for every
\alpha>0
. With these observations in mind, we can state
the following concrete result.

Corollary 2
.

For
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
and

s\in\left(0,1\right]
, if
\varphi\in{\cal L}_{p}\cap{\cal P}
,

f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{s,p}\cap{\cal P}
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{s}\varphi\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq K_{1}\text{,}

then there exists a sequence
\left(f_{m}\right)_{m}
with
f_{m}\in\mathrm{co}_{m}\left({\cal P}_{\varphi}\right)
,
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\left\|f_{m}-f_{0}\right\|_{p}\leq\begin{cases}Km^{-\frac{s}{sq+d}}&\text{if }1<p<2\text{,}\\
Km^{-\frac{sq}{2\left(sq+d\right)}}&\text{if }2\leq p<\infty\text{,}\end{cases}

where
K
is a function of
s,K_{1},p,d,\varphi
and depends on

f_{0}
only via
\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{s,p}
when
s<1
,
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\nabla f_{0}\left(x\right)\right\|_{2}^{p}\mathrm{d}x

when
s=1
.

As per Theorem
1
, the approximation error decreases as a function of
\alpha
and thus it is desirable to identify concrete sufficient conditions under which
f_{0}
satisfies (
2
) with
\alpha>1
, allowing us to guarantee faster approximation rates for such functions.
To the best of our knowledge,
there are no sufficient conditions such as those of Lemmas
9

and
10
that extend our result.
Some closely related conditions are the Calderon–Zygmund kernel
assumptions of Propositions 4.1.5 and 4.3.33 of
Giné & Nickl, 2016
.
Unfortunately, for functions with
\alpha>1
, these results
require the ability to choose a base function
\varphi
with zero
second moment, which is impossible for any
\varphi\in{\cal P}
.

4
Adaptive least-squares density estimation

Suppose that the random vector
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
has density

f_{0}\in{\cal L}_{2}\cap{\cal P}
in the sense that for each
\mathbb{A}\in\mathfrak{B}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,

\text{P}\left(\omega:X\left(\omega\right)\in\mathbb{A}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{A}}f_{0}\left(x\right)\text{d}x
.
If
\left({\cal F}_{n}\right)_{n}
is a sequence such that
{\cal F}_{n}\subset{\cal L}_{2}\cap{\cal P}

is a class of PDFs, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f_{0}
is unknown,
then we seek to find a sequence of estimators
\left(\hat{f}_{n}\left(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\right)\right)_{n}

with each
\hat{f}_{n}\in{\cal F}_{n}
, such that rate of decrease
of the expected squared error
\text{E}\left\|\hat{f}_{n}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}

can be quantified. For brevity we will write
\hat{f}_{n}=\hat{f}_{n}\left(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\right)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and use similar notation for related estimators.
The main result of this section is presented below with the derivation
of the result presented in the sequel.

Theorem 3
.

For

s\in\left(0,1\right]
, let
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap{\cal P}
,

f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{s,2}\cap{\cal P}
,
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal P}_{\varphi}\right)<\infty
,
and assume that for some finite
K_{1}>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{s}\varphi\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq K_{1}\text{.}

Then, by taking
m_{n}=\lceil n^{1/2}\rceil
, we can choose
a sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n}
, with
\hat{f}_{n}\in\mathrm{co}_{m_{n}}\left({\cal P}_{\varphi}\right)

depending on
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such
that,

\mathrm{E}\left\|\hat{f}_{n}-f\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim n^{-\frac{s}{2s+d}}\text{.}

4.1
Proof of Theorem
3

The least-squares criterion

Let
{\cal F}\subset{\cal L}_{2}\cap{\cal P}
. If
f_{0}
were known, then for each
\epsilon>0
, we can can approximate

f_{0}
by some well-defined
f_{\epsilon}\in{\cal F}
, satisfying

\left\|f_{\epsilon}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\inf_{f\in{\cal F}}\left\|f_{\epsilon}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\epsilon\text{,}

where
f_{\epsilon}
is the
\epsilon
-minimizer of the least-squares
criterion. We note here that the
\epsilon
-minimizer always exists
for each
\epsilon>0
, whereas there may not exist

5
Discussions

5.1
Vapnik–Chervonenkis classes

We note that the assumption that the VC dimension of the class
{\cal P}_{\varphi}

be finite in Theorem
3

is not restrictive. In fact, a very large class of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

can be proved to have finite VC dimension via the fact that if the class
of sets
{\cal C}=\left\{\mathbb{G}_{f}\text{ | }f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}
,
where
\mathbb{G}_{f}=\left\{\left(x,t\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\text{ | }t\leq f\left(x\right)\right\}
,
is a uniformly definable with respect to an o-minimal (order-minimal) structure in the model
theoretic sense, then
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal F}\right)<\infty
(cf.,

Van den Dries, 1998
, Ch. 5
and
Vidyasagar, 2003
, Thm. 10.11
).

For instance, if for each
f\in{\cal F}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f\left(x\right)
can be computed using only a finite number of arithmetic
operations
-,+,\times
, comparison indicators
>,\geq,<,\leq,=,\neq
,
and introduces a finite number of constants, then
{\cal C}
is a
is uniformly definable with respect to the
o
-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{alg}}
. If
we also allow
f
to be computed using a finite number of evaluations
of restricted analytic functions (e.g.,
x\mapsto\sin\left(x\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left[-1,1\right]}
,

x\mapsto\log\left(x+1\right)\mathbf{1}\left[0,1\right]
), then
{\cal C}

is uniformly definable with respect to the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
.
If we further allow the use of the exponential
x\mapsto\exp\left(x\right)
,
then
{\cal C}
is uniformly definable with respect to the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\text{exp}}
.
And if we instead replace the use of the restricted analytic functions
by the so-called Pfaffian functions of
Khovanskii, 1991
,
then
{\cal C}
is uniformly definable with respect to the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{Pfaff}}
.
A good introduction and source of references for these results is

Loi, 2010
.

Using
Vidyasagar, 2003
, the fact that
{\cal C}

is uniformly definable with respect to an o-minimal structure only permits the qualitative
statement that
\textsc{vc}\left({\cal F}\right)<\infty
. Explicit bounds and rates for the VC dimension for o-minimal structres
based on the number of operations and number of introduced constants
required to compute
f\left(x\right)
can also be obtained via the
results in
Anthony & Bartlett, 1999
,
Vidyasagar, 2003
,
and
Montaña & Pardo, 2009
.

Based on
Vidyasagar, 2003
and the results
from
Loi, 2010
, we can confirm the fact that the location-scale
class
{\cal P}_{\varphi}
based on the Gaussian kernel

x\mapsto\varphi\left(x\right)=\left(2\pi\right)^{-d/2}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|x\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

has finite VC dimension. Other examples include the location-scale
classes corresponding to the usual kernel functions in multivariate
kernel density estimation, such as the triangular, Epanechnikov, biweight,
and triweight kernels (see, e.g.,
Scott, 2015
, Ch. 6
).

5.2
Beyond
{\cal L}_{p}
spaces

An immediate extension to Theorem
1
is
to consider approximation with respect to norms of the form
f\mapsto\left\|f\right\|=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{P}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}

for some finite set
\mathbb{P}\subset\left(1,\infty\right)
. For
such cases, the approximation rate will simply be the slowest rate
achieved over all
p\in\mathbb{P}
from Theorem
1
.

Less trivially, we can consider two natural extensions of
{\cal L}_{p}

spaces. The first extension is to consider the weighted Lebesgue spaces

{\cal L}_{p,w}
, whose norm is determined by a positive function

w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
via the expression
f\mapsto\left\|f\right\|_{p,w}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}w\left(x\right)\text{d}x\right]^{1/p}

for each
p\in\left[1,\infty\right)
, as discussed in
Castillo et al., 2016

and appearing often in the approximation theory literature (see, e.g.,

Mhaskar, 1996
). The required theoretical tools for handling
approximation in this setting are the same as those required for the
unweighted
{\cal L}_{p}
spaces (include analogues to Lemmas
6
–
8
)
from which can can infer both qualitative results such as those from

Nguyen et al., 2023b
as well as the rate results of Theorem

1
.

The second extension is to consider variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

{\cal L}_{p\left(\cdot\right)}
of functions
f
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p\left(x\right)}\text{d}x<\infty
,
for
p:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\left[1,\infty\right]
and whose norm naturally
extends the usual
{\cal L}_{p}
norms when
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]

is constant (cf.
Diening et al., 2011
and
Cruz-Uribe & Fiorenza, 2013
).
Under suitable assumptions, the spaces
{\cal L}_{p\left(\cdot\right)}

support a Riesz representation theorem and a theory of convolution-based
approximation and we can thus extend our results to such spaces with
some modifications. Furthermore, the variable exponent Lebesgue spaces
naturally define variable exponent Sobolev spaces from which we may
derive sufficient conditions for approximation rates such as those
of Corollary
2
.

Lastly, Riesz representation theorems and convolution-based approximation
theorems are known for Sobolev spaces (cf.
Adams & Fournier, 2003
and
Leoni, 2017
), from which we can prove qualitative
approximation results upon provision of necessary modifications to
the proofs of
Nguyen et al., 2023b
. However, quantitative
results such as Theorem
1
may require
resolving technicalities that we have not anticipated.

5.3
Faster estimation rates

In the problem setting of estimation of PDFs
f_{0}\in{\cal W}^{s,2}
,
for
s>0
, it is well-established that the minimax rate for estimators
of the least-squares risk is of order
n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}}
(see,
e.g.,
Klemela, 2009
, Cor. 11.11
and
Giné & Nickl, 2016
, Exercise 6.3.2
),
whose exponent is twice as fast as the rate achieved in Theorem
3
.
Furthermore, it is also known that this faster rate is achievable,
for instance by kernel density or wavelet estimators (cf.

Giné & Nickl, 2016
, Sec. 5.1
). The question then is whether
there is a version of Theorem
3

that achieves the minimax rate.

Following tedious derivations (not included in this version of the
text),
Massart & Nedelec, 2006
allows us to prove the following
result which can be used in place of Lemma
11
.

Lemma 4
.

If
f_{0}\in{\cal L}_{\infty}\cap{\cal P}
,

{\cal F}\subset{\cal L}_{2}\cap{\cal L}_{\infty}\cap{\cal P}
has

\textsc{vc}\left({\cal F}\right)<\infty
and envelope
\bar{F}
,
and
\hat{f}_{n,\epsilon}\in{\cal F}
satisfies (
3
),
with
\epsilon
chosen to be an appropriate
\epsilon_{n}
, then

\mathrm{E}\left\|\hat{f}_{n,\epsilon}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\inf_{f\in{\cal F}}\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\bar{F}\right\|_{\infty}\tilde{O}\left(n^{-1}\right)\text{.}

If we traverse the proof of Theorem
3

with Lemma
4
in place of Lemma
11
,
we observe that with only the additional condition
f_{0}\in{\cal L}_{\infty}
,
it can be prove that

\text{E}\left\|\hat{f}_{n}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\tilde{O}\left(n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}}\right)\text{,}

which puts the adaptive least-squares estimator within a logarithmic factor of minimax optimality. Furthermore,
when employed in the setting where
f_{0}\in\overline{\text{co}\left({\cal F}\right)}^{2}
,
we can show that one can choose estimator sequences
\left(\hat{f}_{n}\right)_{n}\subset\text{co}\left({\cal F}\right)
,
such that
\text{E}\left\|\hat{f}_{n}-f_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\tilde{O}\left(n^{-1}\right)
,
which is faster than the rate of
O\left(n^{-1/2}\right)
achieved
by
Klemelä, 2007
and matches the minimax rate of
Kim & Guntuboyina, 2022
,
up to a logarithmic factor. We currently do not know of any further
techniques for improving on these rates.

\displaystyle=K_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|y\right\|_{2}^{\alpha}\nu^{d}\varphi\left(\nu y\right)\text{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{2}}{\nu^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|z\right\|_{2}^{\alpha}\varphi\left(z\right)\text{d}z\leq K_{1}K_{2}\nu^{-\alpha}\text{,}

using condition (
1
) and a change
in variable.

Part 4

In the case when
p\in\left(1,2\right)
, we have from (
9
)
that

\left\|f_{m}-f_{0}\right\|_{p}\leq 3\nu^{d/q}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}m^{-1/q}+K_{1}K_{2}\nu^{-\alpha}\text{.}

By minimizing the right-hand side with respect to
\nu
, we have
the minimizer

\nu_{m}^{*}=\left[\frac{3d\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}}{\alpha qK_{1}K_{2}}\right]^{-\frac{q}{\alpha q+d}}m^{\frac{1}{\alpha q+d}}\text{,}

which implies the optimal bound
Km^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha q+d}}
,
where

K=3\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}\left[\frac{3d\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}}{\alpha qK_{1}K_{2}}\right]^{-\frac{d}{\alpha q+d}}+K_{1}K_{2}\left[\frac{3d\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}}{\alpha qK_{1}K_{2}}\right]^{\frac{\alpha q}{\alpha q+d}}\text{.}

Similarly, when
p\in\left[2,\infty\right)
, we have the bound

\left\|f_{m}-f_{0}\right\|_{p}\leq 3\nu^{d/q}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}m^{-1/2}+K_{1}K_{2}\nu^{-\alpha}\text{,}

which is minimized at

\nu^{*}=\left[\frac{3d\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}C_{p}}{\alpha qK_{1}K_{2}}\right]^{-\frac{q}{\alpha q+d}}m^{\frac{q}{2\left(\alpha q+d\right)}}\text{.}

(10)

This yields the optimal bound
Km^{-\frac{\alpha q}{2\left(\alpha q+d\right)}}
.

Lemma 5

(Geometric Hahn–Banach theorem)
.

Suppose
that
\mathbb{X}
is a real Banach space and
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{X}

are non-empty, disjoint, and convex sets. Further assume that
\mathbb{A}

is compact and
\mathbb{B}
is closed. Then, there exists a

(the dual of
\mathbb{X}
) and

, such that
for every
a\in\mathbb{A}
and
b\in\mathbb{B}
,

h\left(a\right)<\gamma<h\left(b\right)\text{.}

Lemma 6

(Riesz representation theorem)
.

For
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)

and
1/p+1/q=1
, if

such that

for every

.

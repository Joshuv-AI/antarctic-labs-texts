49 U.S.C. § 80114 — Lost, stolen, and destroyed negotiable bills

Creator: United States Congress
Year: 2026
Source-URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title49-section80114
Context: United States Code, Title 49 (Transportation); Chapter 801 (Bills Of Lading). Current through Public Law 119-134, as published by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel.

(a) Delivery on Court Order and Surety Bond.—
If a negotiable bill of lading is lost, stolen, or destroyed, a court of competent jurisdiction may order the common carrier to deliver the goods if the person claiming the goods gives a surety bond, in an amount approved by the court, to indemnify the carrier or a person injured by delivery against liability under the outstanding original bill. The court also may order payment of reasonable costs and attorney’s fees to the carrier. A voluntary surety bond, without court order, is binding on the parties to the bond.
(b) Liability to Holder.—
Delivery of goods under a court order under subsection (a) of this section does not relieve a common carrier from liability to a person to whom the negotiable bill has been or is negotiated for value without notice of the court proceeding or of the delivery of the goods.

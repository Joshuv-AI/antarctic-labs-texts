Inflation at the End of 2025: Constraints on r r and n s n_{\mathrm{s}} Using the Latest CMB and BAO Data
L. Balkenhol 1,∗, E. Camphuis 1, F. Finelli 2,3, K. Benabed 1, F. R. Bouchet 1, J. Carron 4,5, S. Galli 1, E. Hivon 1, A. R. Khalife 1, L. Knox 6, C. L. Reichardt 7, A. Vitrier 1, W. L. K. Wu 8

Abstract
Inflation elegantly provides initial conditions for the standard model of cosmology, while solving the horizon, flatness, and magnetic monopole problems. Inflationary models make predictions for the tensor-to-scalar ratio r r and the spectral index n s n_{\mathrm{s}} of initial density fluctuations. In light of relevant data releases this year, we present constraints on these two parameters using the latest cosmic microwave background (CMB) and baryon acoustic oscillation data (BAO) available. Using data from Planck, the South Pole Telescope, Atacama Cosmology Telescope, and BICEP/Keck experiments, we derive n s = 0.9682 ± 0.0032 n_{\mathrm{s}}{}=0.9682\,\pm\,0.0032 and a 95% upper limit of r < 0.034 r{}<0.034 . This upper limit on r r is consistent with the official BICEP/Keck result given the numerical precision of the analyses and our choice to impose the self-consistency relation for single field slow-roll inflation on the tensor power spectrum; the r r constraint is not impacted by the additional CMB data. While adding DESI BAO data to the CMB data has a negligible impact on r r , the n s n_{\mathrm{s}} constraint shifts upward to 0.9728 ± 0.0029 0.9728\,\pm\,0.0029 , which favours monomial inflaton potentials with N ⋆ ∼ 50 N_{\star}{}\!\sim 50 over Starobinsky R 2 R^{2} or Higgs inflation with N ⋆ = 51 N_{\star}{}=51 and N ⋆ = 55 N_{\star}{}=55 , respectively. This shift is caused by marginally significant differences between the CMB and DESI data that remain unexplained in the context of the standard model. We show that a class of polynomial α \alpha -attractor models can predict the CMB and CMB+DESI n s n_{\mathrm{s}} results with N ⋆ = 47.1 N_{\star}{}=47.1 and N ⋆ = 55.1 N_{\star}{}=55.1 , respectively. While future data will improve our sensitivity to r r , robust n s n_{\mathrm{s}} constraints are just as crucial to differentiate between inflation models. We make the data needed to reproduce the new CMB and BAO results and visualisation tools for r r - n s n_{\mathrm{s}} figures to compare to any inflation model available here .

Introduction

Inflation, a period of near-exponential expansion in the early universe, offers an elegant explanation for the observed homogeneity and flatness of the universe and explains the absence of magnetic monopoles.
At the same time, if the inflaton is in its ground state, the simplest models of inflation predict small, Gaussian, adiabatic, close-to-scale-invariant perturbations, which serve as the initial conditions of the highly successful cosmological constant (
\Lambda
) cold dark matter (CDM) model of cosmology
(
40
;
21
;
71
;
41
;
1
;
5
)

Observations of the cosmic microwave background give strong evidence in support of these initial conditions; the CMB is remarkably Gaussian

n_{\mathrm{s}}

However, inflation also predicts a background of gravitational waves, which has not been detected yet;
the most promising way of detecting these gravitational waves is through their signature in the

r

(
33
;
3

In the absence of a detection, one can nevertheless discriminate between inflation models based on the upper limit on

Data Sets and Methodology

We present constraints on the
\Lambda
CDM model of cosmology, adding a varying tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
(at a pivot scale of
0.05\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}
).
We set the tensor power spectrum tilt to obey the self-consistency condition for single field slow-roll inflation.
We otherwise parametrise the model using the amplitude
\log(10^{10}\,A_{\mathrm{s}}{})
and spectral index
n_{\mathrm{s}}
of initial scalar perturbations (at the same pivot scale as
r
), the physical density of baryons
\Omega_{\rm b}h^{2}
and dark matter
\Omega_{\rm c}h^{2}

H_{0}
, and the optical depth to reionisation
\tau_{\mathrm{reio}}

To constrain this model, we use CMB data from
Planck

Specifically, for the first three experiments, we use the ‘SPA’ combination of primary CMB (
T\!T/T\!E/E\!E

12

Planck
PR3 and PR4 data
(

;

;
4
;
12
;

;

;

Note that we use the compressed CMB-only likelihoods, where available.
We use the latest BICEP/Keck
B
-mode (
B\!B
) likelihood
(
3
)
.
We also consider BAO measurements from the second data release (DR2) of the DESI collaboration
(
19
)
.

We perform Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analyses using the
Cobaya

(

;

;

;

;

;

To obtain model predictions, we use the Boltzmann solver

As the CMB data set combination above does not include large-scale

-mode data, we impose a

\mathcal{N}\sim(0.051,0.006^{2})
on the optical depth to reionisation
\tau_{\mathrm{reio}}

(

We otherwise impose uniform priors on cosmological parameters.
For

we report mean values, 68% confidence intervals, and best-fit values in parentheses.

4
Conclusions

We have presented the state of
r
and
n_{\mathrm{s}}
constraints at the end of 2025, using the latest CMB and BAO data available.
While the CMB data alone favour a region of the model space compatible with Higgs and Starobinsky
R^{2}
inflation at
\leq 2.0\,\sigma
(assuming
N_{\star}{}=55
and
N_{\star}{}=51
, respectively), the addition of DESI data shifts the inferred
n_{\mathrm{s}}
value high, in favour of monomial inflaton potentials (with an exponent
\sim\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}})
.
This shift in
n_{\mathrm{s}}
when BAO data are added is caused by marginally statistically significant differences between CMB and DESI data, which remain unexplained in the context of the standard model.
Classes of polynomial
\alpha
-attractor models are able to predict
n_{\mathrm{s}}
values in agreement with the CMB and CMB+BAO results depending on the number of e-folds.
Future CMB data will improve our sensitivity to
r
and
n_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Further data from DESI as well as large-scale structure data from the Euclid satellite
(
23
)
may either increase the differences between CMB and BAO data to statistical significance, or resolve them and thus allow for more precise and robust analyses of inflationary models.

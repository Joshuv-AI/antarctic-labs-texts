2
Framework

Everywhere in what follows
\left(X,+\right)
is a commutative semigroup,

n
is a positive integer and
\left(Y,+\right)
is an
\left(n!\right)
-divisible Abelian group. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{i}
the set of the
solutions of equation
\left(i\right)
; for instance
\mathcal{S}_{\ref{1}}
is the set of all functions
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y
that satisfy equation (
1
). Let
j
be a nonnegative
integer; we will use the operator

\Delta_{y}^{j}:Y^{X}\rightarrow Y^{X}\text{, }\Delta_{y}^{j}\rho\left(x\right):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\left(-1\right)^{j-i}\tbinom{j}{i}\rho\left(x+iy\right)

for
y\in X
;
\mathcal{M}_{j}\left(X,Y\right)
denotes the
j
-
monomials
, i.e. the solutions
\rho:X\rightarrow Y
of the equation

\Delta_{y}^{j}\rho\left(x\right)=\left(j!\right)\rho\left(y\right)\text{ for all }x,y\in X

and
\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
denotes the (
Fréchet
)

n
-polynomials
, i.e. the solutions
\rho:X\rightarrow Y
of the equation

\Delta_{y}^{n+1}\rho\left(x\right)=0\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

The first characterization of continuous real
n
-polynomials by this
equation was realized by Fréchet in
[
17
]
. A detailed analysis of Fréchet polynomials in the present framework was given by Djoković in

[
16
]
; from this paper we will use only the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\rho:X\rightarrow Y.
Then
\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
if and only if there exists
\rho_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{j}\left(X,Y\right)
for all
j\in\left\{0,1,...,n\right\}
such that
\rho=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\rho_{j}
.

The section method
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
provides, among other things, a
technique for solving - partial or total - some equations whose solutions
are composite functions. We will give below only a few rudiments of this
method adapted to our goals.

Let
g:U\rightarrow X
be a surjection and
g^{\prime}:X\rightarrow U
be
a section of
g
(i.e.
g\circ g^{\prime}=id_{X}
). Let also the functions

G:Y^{X}\times X^{2}\rightarrow Y\text{, }H:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y

and the equation

G\left(f\circ g^{\prime},\left(g\left(u\right),g\left(v\right)\right)\right)=H\left(f\left(u\right),f\left(v\right)\right)\text{ for all }u,v\in U,

(5)

where the unknown is
f:U\rightarrow Y
. The equation

G\left(\rho,\left(x,y\right)\right)=H\left(\rho\left(x\right),\rho\left(y\right)\right)\text{ for all }x,y\in X,

(6)

where
\rho:X\rightarrow Y
is the unknown, is
the characteristic

of equation (
5
).

We will use the following results extracted from Th. 2.4.1, Th 2.4.2 and Th.
2.6.6 in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

1.
\left\{f\circ g^{\prime}|f\in\mathcal{S}_{5}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{6}.

2.
\mathcal{S}_{5}^{c}:=\left\{\rho\circ g|\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{6}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{5}.

3. If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{5}
and
u_{0}\in U
such that the function

f\left(U\right)\rightarrow Y\text{, }y\mapsto H\left(f\left(u_{0}\right),y\right)\text{ (or }y\mapsto H\left(y,f\left(u_{0}\right)\right)\text{)}

is one-to-one, then
f\in\mathcal{S}_{5}^{c}
.

The functions in
\mathcal{S}_{5}^{c}
are named
canonical solutions

(of equation (
5
)). Thus Lemma 2.2.2 gives a partial solution for
equation (
5
) and Lemma 2.2.3 provides sufficient conditions under which
a solution of equation (
5
) is a canonical solution.

In the following we will use the notions and the conventions introduced

3
Radical-Fréchet equations

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer,
U=\mathbb{R}
and
\left(X,+\right)
be the additive semigroup
defined by

\left(X,+\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\mathbb{R},+\right)\text{ if }m\text{\ is odd}\\
\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},+\right)\text{ if }m\text{\ is even}\end{array},\right.

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty).

First we characterize the solutions of the
radical-monomial
equation (
1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y
be a function. Then
f
is a solution of equation (
1
)
if and only if there exists an
n
-monomial
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
such that

f\left(u\right)=\rho\left(u^{m}\right)\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{R}\text{.}

(7)

Proof.

We apply the section method for the surjection

g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow X\text{, }u\mapsto u^{m},

its section

g^{\prime}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{, }x\mapsto\sqrt[m]{x},

and the functions

G:Y^{X}\times X^{2}\rightarrow Y\text{, }G\left(\rho,\left(x,y\right)\right):=\Delta_{y}^{n}\rho\left(x\right),

H:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y\text{, \ }H\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right):=\left(n!\right)y_{2}.

Then equation (
1
) becomes equation (
5
) and its characteristic is equation (
6
). Therefore the characteristic of equation (
1
) is exactly the
n
-monomial equation

\Delta_{y}^{n}\rho\left(x\right)=\left(n!\right)\rho\left(y\right)\text{ for all }x,y\in X

and all its solutions are in
\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right).

1. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y
defined by (
7
). Then
f=\rho\circ g
and, according
to Lemma 2.2.2,
f
is a solution of equation (
1
).

2. For proving the converse it suffices to show that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}
. Let
f
be a solution of equation (
1
). Since
\left(Y,+\right)
is
\left(n!\right)
-divisible, the
function

f\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\rightarrow Y\text{, }y\mapsto H\left(0,y\right)=\left(n!\right)y

is injective. According to Lemma 2.2.3,
f
is a canonical solution of
equation (
1
), i.e. there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
such that
f\left(u\right)=\rho\left(u^{m}\right)
for
all
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

∎

Now we are in position to characterize the solutions of the
radical-Fréchet
equation (
2
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y
be a function. Then
f
is a solution of equation (
2
)
if and only if there exists
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\left(X,Y\right)
for all
i\in\left\{0,1,...,n\right\}
such that

f\left(u\right)=\rho_{0}\left(u^{m}\right)+\rho_{1}\left(u^{m}\right)+\cdots+\rho_{n}\left(u^{m}\right)\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{R}\text{.}

(8)

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous theorem, let

g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow X\text{, }g\left(u\right):=u^{m}\text{ and }g^{\prime}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{, }g^{\prime}\left(x\right):=\sqrt[m]{x}.

Let also be the functions

G:Y^{X}\times X^{2}\rightarrow Y\text{, }G\left(\rho,\left(x,y\right)\right):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\tbinom{n+1}{i}\rho\left(x+iy\right)\text{ and}

H:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y\text{, \ }H\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right):=-y_{1}.

Then equation (
2
) can be written in the form (
5
) and its characteristic is (
6
) or, equivalent, the Fréchet polynomial equation

\Delta_{y}^{n+1}\rho\left(x\right)=0\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

We note that the solutions of the last equation are given by
\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
and their characterization is given by Lemma 2.1.

1. Let
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\left(X,Y\right)
for all
i\in\left\{0,1,...,n\right\}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y
defined by (
8
). Then
\rho:=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
(by
Lemma 2.1) and
f=\rho\circ g\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
(by Lemma 2.2.2).

2. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. To show that
f
can be expressed by (
8
) with
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\left(X,Y\right)
- or, equivalent,
that
f=\rho\circ g
, where
\rho:=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
-, it is sufficient to prove that
f
is
a canonical solution of equation (
2
). But the function

f\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\rightarrow Y\text{, }y\mapsto H\left(y,0\right)=-y

is injective; according to Lemma 2.2.3,
f\in\mathcal{S}_{2}^{c}
, and the
theorem is completely proved.
∎

4
Arcsine-Fréchet equations

Before proceeding to the characterizations of the solutions of the
arcsine-Fréchet
equations (
3
) and (
4
), let us note that
U:=\mathbb{R}\diagdown\left\{k\pi|k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
is the maximal domain on which these equations has nontrivial
solutions; indeed if there is
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k\pi
is in the domain, for
u=k\pi
in (
3
) we get
0=\left(n!\right)f\left(v\right)
, and, since
\left(Y,+\right)
is
\left(n!\right)
-divisible we immediately obtain
f=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{3}=\left\{0\right\}
; analogously, for
v=k\pi
in (
4
) we get
f=0
and
\mathcal{S}_{4}=\left\{0\right\}
.

In the following lines, the set
X:=(-\infty,0]
is endowed with the
addition of real numbers, hence
\left(X,+\right)
is an Abelian semigroup.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:U:=\mathbb{R}\diagdown\left\{k\pi|k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\rightarrow Y
. Then
f\in\mathcal{S}_{3}
if and only if there
exists an
n
-monomial
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
and

f\left(u\right)=\rho\left(\ln\left|\sin u\right|\right)\text{
for all }u\in U\text{.}

(9)

Proof.

We apply the section method for

g:U\rightarrow X\text{, }g\left(u\right):=\ln\left|\sin u\right|\text{ for all }u\in U\text{,}

its section

g^{\prime}:X\rightarrow U\text{, }g^{\prime}\left(x\right):=\arcsin e^{x}\text{ for all }x\in X\text{,}

and the functions
G,H
defined by

G:Y^{X}\times X^{2}\rightarrow Y\text{, }G\left(\rho,\left(x,y\right)\right):=\Delta_{y}^{n}\rho\left(x\right),

H:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y\text{, \ }H\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right):=\left(n!\right)y_{2}.

We note that equation (
3
) becomes equation (
5
) and its characteristic is equation (
6
) or, the monomial equation

\Delta_{y}^{n}\rho\left(x\right)=\left(n!\right)\rho\left(y\right)\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

1. If
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
and
f:U\rightarrow Y
is defined by (
9
), then
f=\rho\circ g
and - from Lemma 2.2.2 -
f

is a solution of equation (
3
).

2. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{3}
and
u_{0}\in U
. Since
\left(Y,+\right)
is
\left(n!\right)
-divisible, the function

f\left(U\right)\rightarrow Y\text{, }y\mapsto H\left(f\left(u_{0}\right),y\right)=\left(n!\right)y\text{ }

is an injection and, from Lemma 2.2.3, there exists an
n
-monomial
\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)
such that
f=\rho\circ g
; therefore
f

satisfies relation (
9
).
∎

Finally we characterize the solutions of equation (
4
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f:U\rightarrow Y
be a function. Then
f
is a solution of equation (
4
) if and only if there exists the monomials
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\left(X,Y\right)
for
i\in\left\{0,1,...,n\right\}
such that

f\left(u\right)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\left(\left(\ln\left|\sin u\right|\right)\right)\text{ for all }u\in U\text{.}

(10)

Proof.

Let

g:U\rightarrow X\text{, }g\left(u\right):=\ln\left|\sin u\right|\text{ for all }u\in U\text{,}

g^{\prime}:X\rightarrow U\text{, }g^{\prime}\left(x\right):=\arcsin e^{x}\text{ for all }x\in X\text{,}

G:Y^{X}\times X^{2}\rightarrow Y\text{, }G\left(\rho,\left(x,y\right)\right):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(-1\right)^{i}\tbinom{n+1}{i}\rho\left(x+iy\right)\text{ and}

H:Y\times Y\rightarrow Y\text{, }H\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right):=-y_{1}.

We note that equation (
4
) can be rewritten in the form (
5
) and, consequently, its characteristic is equation (
6
) or, equivalent, the Fréchet polynomial equation

\Delta_{y}^{n+1}\rho\left(x\right)=0\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Then, according to Lemma 2.2.2,
\mathcal{S}_{4}^{c}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{4}
. Moreover, if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{4}
and
u_{0}
is an arbitrary number
in
U
, the function

f\left(U\right)\rightarrow Y\text{, }y\mapsto H\left(y,f\left(u_{0}\right)\right)=-y

is bijective; from Lemma 2.2.3 we have
f\in\mathcal{S}_{4}^{c}
. Hence

\mathcal{S}_{4}=\left\{\rho\circ g|\text{ }\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)\right\},

and, by Lemma 2.1,

\mathcal{P}_{n}\left(X,Y\right)=\mathcal{M}_{0}\left(X,Y\right)+\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(X,Y\right)+\cdots+\mathcal{M}_{n}\left(X,Y\right).

Consequently, if
f:U\rightarrow Y
, then
f\in\mathcal{S}_{4}
if and only
if there exist
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}\left(X,Y\right)
for
i\in\left\{0,1,...,n\right\}
such that
f\left(u\right)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\rho_{i}\left(\left(\ln\left|\sin u\right|\right)\right)
for all
u\in U
, and the theorem is
completely proved.
∎

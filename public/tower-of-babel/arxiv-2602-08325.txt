Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a second-order accurate time-stepping scheme for the tempered time-fractional advection–dispersion equation based on a sum-of-exponentials (SOE) approximation to the convolution kernel involved in the fractional derivative. To effectively resolve the weak initial-time singularity at t = 0 t=0 , graded temporal meshes are employed. A fully discrete scheme is constructed by coupling the proposed half-time-level temporal discretization with a finite difference method in space. Compared with the classical L1 scheme, the proposed SOE-based method achieves the same global convergence order while reducing both storage requirements and computational cost. Specifically, the storage demand is reduced from 𝒪 ⁡ ( M ​ N ) \mathcal{O}(MN) to 𝒪 ⁡ ( M ​ N exp ) \mathcal{O}(MN_{\mathrm{exp}}) , and the computational complexity is lowered from 𝒪 ⁡ ( M ​ N 2 ) \mathcal{O}(MN^{2}) to 𝒪 ⁡ ( M ​ N ​ N exp ) \mathcal{O}(MNN_{\mathrm{exp}}) , where M M and N N denote the numbers of spatial and temporal grid points, respectively, and N e ​ x ​ p N_{exp} is the number of exponential terms used in the SOE approximation. The unique solvability, stability and accuracy of the resulting scheme are rigorously analyzed. Several numerical results are presented to confirm the sharpness of the error analysis and to demonstrate the efficiency of the proposed method.

Introduction

Tempered fractional calculus is a refined extension of classical fractional calculus, designed to model complex phenomena characterized by anomalous diffusion and nonlocal transport. By introducing an exponential tempering factor into power-law kernels, it effectively overcomes the infinite-moment difficulty inherent in classical fractional models while preserving their nonlocal memory features. The concept of truncated Lévy flights was first proposed by Koponen
[
21
]
as a smooth modification of the model introduced by Mantegna
[
26
]
. Subsequently, Cartea and del Castillo-Negrete
[
8

developed the tempered fractional calculus framework to ensure the finiteness of moments in Lévy distributions arising from power-law waiting times. Since then, tempered fractional models have found widespread applications in many fields, including finance
[

[

. By capturing both memory effects and non-Gaussian transport behavior, tempered fractional calculus significantly enhances the modeling and prediction of complex systems.

The application of tempered fractional models to real-world problems requires efficient and accurate numerical methods for solving tempered fractional differential equations. In recent years, tempered fractional operators have been incorporated into various mathematical models, such as the substantial fractional equation

, the Bloch equation

, the tempered fractional Jacobi equation

It is well known that the principal numerical challenges in tempered fractional models arise from the presence of a weakly singular kernel and the exponential tempering factor in the fractional operator. Moreover, it is highly desirable to design efficient numerical schemes that maintain high accuracy even when the solution exhibits low regularity near the initial time (
1.4

(2-\alpha)
-th order accuracy in time. To address these challenges, we develop in this paper a finite difference scheme for the TTFADE (

(1)

The proposed scheme employs graded temporal meshes to better capture the initial singular behavior and evaluates the discrete operator at half-time levels to enhance accuracy.

(2)

We provide rigorous proofs of stability and convergence for the scheme, establishing second-order accuracy in both time and space under the weak singularity assumptions.

(3)

Three numerical cases are presented to verify both the accuracy and computational efficiency of the method. Compared with several existing approaches, the proposed scheme produces numerical results more efficiently and with reduced computational cost (see the examples in Sect.
4

K((n+1)^{-\min\{2-\alpha,r(1+\delta)\}}+\varepsilon),&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We decompose the truncation error into two parts as follows:

\displaystyle{}^{FC}Ru\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)=\underbrace{{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)-{}_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)}_{{}^{LC}Ru\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)}+\underbrace{{}_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)-{}_{0}^{FC}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u\left(\bar{t}_{n}\right)}_{\text{ auxiliary error }}.

The first term
{}^{LC}Ru(\bar{t}_{n})
can be directly bounded by Lemma
2.1
. It thus remains to estimate the auxiliary error.
From the definitions of our operator (
2.18
) and the L1 operator (
2.19
), the auxiliary error can be further bounded by a history term and a local term:

\displaystyle|{}^{C}_{0}\mathbb{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u(\bar{t}_{n})-{}_{0}^{FC}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u(\bar{t}_{n})|\leq{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{n})+{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t}_{n}),

where there exist constants
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, independent of
n
and
\varepsilon
, such that

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}e^{\lambda s}|L_{1,k+1}u(s)||(\bar{t}_{n}-s)^{-\alpha-1}-\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\mathrm{exp}}}w_{l}e^{-s_{l}(\bar{t}_{n}-s)}|\,ds,

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}|\frac{e^{\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}L_{1,n+1}u(\bar{t}_{n})-e^{\lambda t_{n}}L_{1,n+1}u(t_{n})}{\tau_{n+1}/2}-(e^{\lambda s}L_{1,n+1}u(s))^{\prime}|(\bar{t}_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds.

For
{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{n})
, using the SOE approximation error bound (
2.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{n})\leq K_{1}\varepsilon\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}e^{\lambda s}|L_{1,k+1}u(s)|ds.

(2.37)

Invoking the regularity assumptions (
1.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{n})\leq K\varepsilon.

(2.38)

For
{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t}_{n})
, applying Taylor expansion to
(e^{\lambda s}L_{1,n+1}u(s))^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t}_{n})\leq K_{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}(\bar{t}_{n}-t_{n})\max_{\theta\in[t_{n},\bar{t}_{n}]}|(e^{\lambda\theta}L_{1,n+1}u(\theta))^{\prime\prime}|(\bar{t}_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}ds.

(2.39)

Invoking the regularity assumptions (
1.4
) yields

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t}_{n})\leq K(\bar{t}_{n}^{\delta}\tau_{n+1}^{2-\alpha}+\bar{t}_{n}^{\delta-1}\tau_{n+1}^{2-\alpha})\leq KN^{-(2-\alpha)}.

(2.40)

For the special case
n=0
, noting that
{}^{FC}R_{h}u(\bar{t}_{0})=0
, it follows from (
2.40
) that

\displaystyle{}^{FC}R_{l}u(\bar{t_{0}})\leq K(\bar{t_{0}}^{\delta}\tau_{1}^{2-\alpha}+\bar{t_{0}}^{\delta-1}\tau_{1}^{2-\alpha})\leq KN^{-r(1+\delta-\alpha)}.

(2.41)

Combining the above estimate with (
2.20
) gives (
2.36
). The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note that (
2.36
) not only refines the result in
[
5
]
, where the error of
{}^{FC}Ru(t_{n})
was estimated by

K(n^{-\min\{2-\alpha,r(1+\delta)\}}+\varepsilon)
, but also extends the corresponding error bound to the half-grid points.

3.
Construction of the fully discrete scheme and the well-posedness

In this section, based on the discrete approximation (

) of the Caputo tempered fractional derivative and the error analysis given in Theorem

, we construct a fully discrete scheme for (
1.3
) in the next Sect.
3.1
. The unique solvability and stability of the proposed scheme are established in Sect.

. Furthermore, the convergence analysis and rigorous error estimates for the numerical solution are presented in Sect.

.

3.1.
Construction of the fully discrete scheme

We first introduce some standard notations. Let
M
and
N
be two positive integers. Define the spatial and temporal step sizes by
h=L/M
and
t_{n}=T(n/{N})^{r}(0\leq n\leq N)
, and denote
\tau_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
. Let
x_{i}=ih
for
0\leq i\leq M
, and define the spatial and temporal grids
X_{M}=\{x_{i}|0\leq i\leq M\}
,
T_{N}=\{t_{k}|0\leq k\leq N\}
. Define the mesh function as follows

\displaystyle u_{i}^{n}

\displaystyle=u(x_{i},t_{n}),\quad 0\leq i\leq M,\quad 0\leq n\leq N,

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\bar{n}}

\displaystyle=f(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n}),\quad 0\leq i\leq M,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-1.

We further introduce the following difference operators:

\displaystyle{\delta}_{t}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i}^{n+1}-u_{i}^{n}}{\tau_{n+1}},\quad{\delta}_{x}u_{i}^{n}=\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i-1}^{n}}{2h},\quad{\delta}_{x}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}=\frac{{\delta}_{x}u_{i}^{n+1}+{\delta}_{x}u_{i}^{n}}{2},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{n}_{i+1}-2u_{i}^{n}+u^{n}_{i-1}}{h^{2}},\quad\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}=\frac{\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{n+1}+\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{n}}{2}.

Based on the SOE approximation (
2.18
) for the Caputo fractional derivative and replacing the exact solution
u_{i}^{n}
by its numerical approximation
U_{i}^{n}
, we obtain the following Crank–Nicolson-type fully discrete scheme:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta_{t}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}+{}_{0}^{FC}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}=\delta_{x}^{2}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}-\delta_{x}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}+f_{i}^{\bar{n}},&1\leq i\leq M-1,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-1,\\
U_{i}^{0}=\phi(x_{i}),&1\leq i\leq M-1,\\
U_{0}^{n}=U_{M}^{n}=0,&0\leq n\leq N.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Replacing the our approximation
{}_{0}^{FC}D
by L1 evaluation scheme
{}_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}
(
2.19
), we obtain the L1 scheme of the following form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta_{t}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}+_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}=\delta_{x}^{2}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}-\delta_{x}U_{i}^{\bar{n}}+f_{i}^{\bar{n}},&1\leq i\leq M-1,\quad 0\leq n\leq N-1,\\
U_{i}^{0}=\phi(x_{i}),&1\leq i\leq M-1,\\
U_{0}^{n}=U_{M}^{n}=0,&0\leq n\leq N.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Comparing
{}_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}
in (
2.19
) with
{}_{0}^{FC}D
in (
2.18
),
the former requires all previous time-step values of
u
, whereas the latter only uses the history variables
U_{hist,i}(t_{n})
,
i=1,\ldots,N_{exp}
, due to (
2.9
).
Replacing
{}_{0}^{C}\mathbb{D}
by
{}_{0}^{FC}D
reduces the storage cost from
\mathcal{O}(N)
to
\mathcal{O}(N_{exp})
and the computational cost from
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
to
\mathcal{O}(NN_{exp})
.
Accordingly, the overall complexity decreases from
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2})
for the L1 scheme (
3.3
) to
\mathcal{O}(MNN_{exp})
for scheme (
3.2
), where
N_{exp}
denotes the SOE approximation number defined in (
2.6
). For fixed
\varepsilon
,
N_{exp}=\mathcal{O}(\log N)
when
T\gg 1
and
N_{exp}=\mathcal{O}(\log^{2}N)
when
T\approx 1

[
20
,
51
]
.
Hence, the proposed scheme significantly reduces storage and computational costs, especially for long-time simulations. Numerical comparisons are reported in Sect.
4
.

Remark 3.1
.

In contrast to
[
4
]
, where the discretization is performed at integer time levels, we employ a half–grid-point discretization in time to account for the presence of the first-order time derivative term
u_{t}
. This treatment allows us to enhance the temporal accuracy to second order. Moreover, such an accuracy improvement is rigorously justified by the theoretical convergence analysis presented in Sect.
3.3
and is further confirmed by the numerical experiments reported in Sect.
4
.

3.2.
The unique solvability and stability of the fully discrete scheme

In this section, we analyze the unique solvability and stability of the proposed fully discrete scheme (
3.2
) with respect to the initial conditions by employing the Fourier method (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
23
,
48
]
).

Prior to stating the main theorem, we first provide an analysis of the scheme. Let

\displaystyle\{U^{n}

\displaystyle=(U_{0}^{n},\ldots,U_{M}^{n})\mid 0\leq n\leq N\},

\displaystyle\{V^{n}

\displaystyle=(V_{0}^{n},\ldots,V_{M}^{n})\mid 0\leq n\leq N\},

denote the numerical solutions of scheme (
3.2
) corresponding to two distinct initial conditions. Define the error

\displaystyle\rho_{j}^{n}=U_{j}^{n}-V_{j}^{n},\quad 0\leq j\leq M,\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

(3.4)

By substituting (
3.4
) into the difference scheme (
3.2
), the resulting error satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{4h})\rho_{j+1}^{n+1}+(\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\tfrac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}+\tfrac{1}{h^{2}})\rho_{j}^{n+1}+(-\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{4h})\rho_{j-1}^{n+1}\\
&=(\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{4h})\rho_{j+1}^{n}+(\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-\tfrac{1-2e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{h^{2}})\rho_{j}^{n}+(\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{4h})\rho_{j-1}^{n}\\
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}((a_{0,n}-e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}({\tau_{n+1}}/{2})^{-\alpha})\rho_{j}^{n}+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{n-l,n}+b_{n-1-l,n})\rho_{j}^{l}\\
&\quad+(b_{n-1,n}+{e^{-\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}}{\bar{t}_{n}^{-\alpha}})\rho_{j}^{0}),\\
&\rho_{0}^{n}=\rho_{M}^{n}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

Define the piecewise grid function

\displaystyle\rho^{n}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x_{0}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{0},\\
\rho_{j}^{n},&\bar{x}_{j-1}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq M-1,\\
0,&\bar{x}_{M-1}\leq x\leq x_{M}.\end{cases}

Since
\rho_{j}^{n}
vanishes at the boundaries, it can be extended periodically, allowing us to apply the discrete Fourier transform (DFT). Denote the DFT of
\rho^{n}
by

\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{M}jk}\rho^{n}_{j},\quad 0\leq k\leq M-1,

with the inverse transform given by

\rho^{n}_{j}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]e^{i\frac{2\pi}{M}jk}.

By Parseval’s identity, we have

\int_{0}^{L}|\rho^{n}(x)|^{2}dx=h\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}|\rho^{n}_{j}|^{2}=Mh\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|^{2}=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|^{2}.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho^{n}(x)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=(\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}h|\rho_{j}^{n}|^{2})=(\int_{0}^{L}|\rho^{n}(x)|^{2}dx)=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|^{2}.

(3.6)

We multiply both sides of equation (
3.5
) by
e^{-ijh\beta}
with
\beta=2\pi k/M
, and sum over
j
. By employing the identities
e^{ih\beta}+e^{-ih\beta}=2\cos(h\beta)
and
e^{ih\beta}-e^{-ih\beta}=2i\sin(h\beta)
, we arrive at the following system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)+\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}))\hat{\rho}^{n+1}[k]\\
&=(\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)-\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}-\frac{1}{h^{2}}))\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}((a_{0,n}+\frac{e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{-\alpha}(1-\alpha)}-e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}({\tau_{n+1}}/{2})^{-\alpha})\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]\\
&\quad+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{n-l,n}+b_{n-1-l,n})\hat{\rho}^{l}[k]+(b_{n-1,n}+{e^{-\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}}{\bar{t}_{n}^{-\alpha}})\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\hat{\rho}_{n}[k]

(1\leq n\leq N)
satisfy (
3.7
). Then

\displaystyle|\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|\leq|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.8)

Proof.

Use induction on
n
. The case
n=1
of (
3.8
) is

\displaystyle|\hat{\rho}_{1}[k]|=\left|\frac{\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)-\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}-\frac{1-2e^{-\lambda{\tau_{1}}/{2}}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha){\tau_{1}}^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{h^{2}})}{-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)+\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}+\frac{1}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha){\tau_{1}}^{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{h^{2}})}\right||\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|\leq|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|.

(3.9)

Fix
n\in\{1,2,\dots,N-1\}
. Assume that (
3.8
) is valid for
m=1,2,\dots,n
. Then (
2.12
), (
2.14
)
and the inductive hypothesis yield

\displaystyle a_{0,n}+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{l,n}+b_{l,n})+b_{n-1,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N_{exp}}w_{i}\int_{t_{n-l-1}}^{t_{n-l}}e^{-(\lambda+s_{i})(\bar{t}_{n}-s)}ds.

Combining the SOE approximation (
2.6
) and the above expression yields

\displaystyle a_{0,n}+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{l,n}+b_{l,n})+b_{n-1,n}

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}{e^{-\lambda s}}{s^{-1-\alpha}}ds

\displaystyle\leq{e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}}{({\tau_{n+1}}/{2})^{-\alpha}}-{e^{-\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}}{\bar{t}_{n}^{-\alpha}}.

(3.10)

Applying the inequality (
3.10
) to (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle|-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)+\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{h^{2}})||\hat{\rho}^{n+1}[k]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)-\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}-\frac{1}{h^{2}})||\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|+\frac{e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|.

(3.11)

Define

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\cos(h\beta)}{h^{2}},\quad B=\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)},\quad C=2Be^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-A+\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-B,\quad b=A+\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+B,\quad d=\frac{\sin(h\beta)}{2h}.

To prove that
|\hat{\rho}^{n+1}[k]|\leq|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|
by (
3.11
), it suffices to establish the following inequality:

\displaystyle\sqrt{a^{2}+d^{2}}+C\leq\sqrt{b^{2}+d^{2}}.

Noticing that
d^{2}\leq{A}/{2}
and
(A+B)^{2}-B^{2}e^{-\lambda\tau_{n+1}}\geq A^{2}+2AB
, it is sufficient to verify

\displaystyle{Be^{-\lambda\tau_{n+1}}\tau_{n+1}^{2}}\leq 4({1-B^{2}e^{-\lambda\tau_{n+1}}\tau_{n+1}^{2}}).

(3.12)

Using the bound
1/2<\Gamma(2-\alpha)<1
and straightforward estimates, inequality (
3.12
) can be reduced to the simpler condition

\tau_{n+1}^{2-2\alpha}<1/3,

which is easily satisfied in typical numerical simulations. Consequently, (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) yield
|\hat{\rho}^{n+1}[k]|\leq|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|
. Thus we
have proved (
3.8
) for
m=n+1
. By the principle of induction, the lemma is proved.
∎

Based on the above analysis and Lemma
3.2
, we are now in a position to present the unique solvability and stability for the difference scheme (
3.2
) in Theorems
3.2
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The finite difference scheme (
3.2
) is uniquely solvable and stable. Let
\{U^{n}=(U_{0}^{n},\ldots,U_{M}^{n})\mid 0\leq n\leq N\}
and
\{V^{n}=(V_{0}^{n},\ldots,V_{M}^{n})\mid 0\leq n\leq N\}
denote the solutions of the scheme (
3.2
) corresponding to two different initial values. Then

\displaystyle\|U^{n}-V^{n}\|_{L_{2}}\leq\|U^{0}-V^{0}\|_{L_{2}},\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.13)

Proof.

First, we prove the unique solvability of the proposed difference scheme. Let

U^{n}=(U_{0}^{n},U_{1}^{n},\ldots,U_{M}^{n}).

From (
3.2
), the initial value
U^{0}
is known. Assume that the first
n
layers,
U^{0},U^{1},\ldots,U^{n}
, are uniquely determined. Then, the scheme yields a linear system for
U^{n+1}
. To prove uniqueness, it suffices to show that the corresponding homogeneous system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta{U_{i}^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{x}^{2}U_{i}^{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}{\delta}_{x}U_{i}^{n+1},\quad 1\leq i\leq M-1,\\
&U_{0}^{n}=U_{M}^{n}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

has only the zero solution, where
\eta=\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}
.
Let

\mathbf{U}^{n+1}=\big(U_{1}^{n+1},U_{2}^{n+1},\ldots,U_{M-1}^{n+1}\big)^{\mathsf{T}},

and define the coefficient matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{(M-1)\times(M-1)}
by

A=\begin{pmatrix}\eta+\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}&\dfrac{1}{4h}-\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}&0&\cdots&0\\
-\dfrac{1}{4h}-\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}&\eta+\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}&\dfrac{1}{4h}-\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}&\cdots&0\\
0&-\dfrac{1}{4h}-\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}&\eta+\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&-\dfrac{1}{4h}-\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}&\eta+\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Then the discrete system (
3.2
) can be written compactly as

A\,\mathbf{U}^{n+1}=\mathbf{0}.

Since
A
is tridiagonal, its eigenvalues are given by
[
18
]
as

\lambda_{k}=\eta+\frac{1}{h^{2}}+2\sqrt{(\frac{1}{4h}-\frac{1}{2h^{2}})(-\frac{1}{4h}-\frac{1}{2h^{2}})}\cos(\frac{k\pi}{M}),\quad 1\leq k\leq M-1.

Since all eigenvalues satisfy
\lambda_{k}\geq\eta>0
, the matrix
A
is invertible. Consequently, (
3.2
) admits only the trivial solution.By induction, the uniqueness of the solution for all
n
follows.

We proceed to the stability analysis of the scheme. Using Lemma
3.2
together with (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|U^{n}-V^{n}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\rho}^{n}[k]|^{2}\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\rho}^{0}[k]|^{2}=\|U^{0}-V^{0}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}.

Taking the square root on both sides yields the desired estimate and completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Convergence of the fully discrete scheme

We now turn to the convergence analysis of the proposed difference scheme. To this end, we need some preparations.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the regularity assumptions (
1.4
),
there exists a positive constant
K
, independent of
h
,
N
, and
n
, such that

\displaystyle|R_{t}^{i,\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle=|u_{t}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-{\delta}_{t}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}KN^{-r(\delta-1)},&n=0,\\
K(n+1)^{-2},&n\geq 1,\end{cases}

(3.14)

\displaystyle|R_{x}^{i,\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle=|u_{x}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-{\delta}_{x}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}K(N^{-r\delta}+h^{2}),&n=0,\\
K((n+1)^{-2}+h^{2}),&n\geq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle|R_{xx}^{i,\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle=|u_{xx}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}K(N^{-r\delta}+h^{2}),&n=0,\\
K((n+1)^{-2}+h^{2}),&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We divide the proof into three parts, corresponding to the temporal, first spatial, and second spatial truncation errors.

Estimate for
R_{t}^{i,\bar{n}}
.

For
n\geq 1
, Taylor’s expansion with integral remainder implies that there exists
\xi\in(t_{n},t_{n+1})
such that

u_{t}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{t}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}=\int_{\bar{t}_{n}}^{\xi}u_{ttt}(x_{i},t)(\xi-t)\,dt.

By the regularity assumption (
1.4
) and (
2.30
), we have

\displaystyle|u_{t}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{t}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|\leq Kt_{n}^{\delta-3}\tau_{n+1}^{2}\leq K(n+1)^{-2},\quad n\geq 1.

For the initial step
n=0
, a direct Taylor expansion together with (
1.4
) yields

\displaystyle|u_{t}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{0})-\delta_{t}u_{i}^{\bar{0}}|

\displaystyle=|\frac{1}{2\tau_{1}}\int_{\bar{t}_{0}}^{t_{1}}u_{ttt}(x_{i},t)(t_{1}^{2}-2t_{1}t)\,dt+\frac{1}{2\tau_{1}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}u_{ttt}(x_{i},t)t^{2}\,dt|\leq KN^{-r(\delta-1)}.

Hence, the temporal truncation error satisfies

|R_{t}^{i,\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}KN^{-r(\delta-1)},&n=0,\\
K(n+1)^{-2},&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Estimate for
R_{x}^{i,\bar{n}}
.

By applying Taylor expansion in time and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle|u_{x}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{x}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle\leq|\int_{\bar{t}_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}u_{xtt}(x_{i},t)(t_{n+1}-t)\,dt+\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}u_{xtt}(x_{i},t)(t_{n}-t)\,dt|+O(h^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{n+1}^{\delta-2}\tau_{n+1}^{2}+\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}t^{\delta-2}(t-t_{n})\,dt)+O(h^{2}).

(3.15)

For
n=0
, estimate (
3.15
) together with (
2.30
) directly yields

|u_{x}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{0})-\delta_{x}u_{i}^{\bar{0}}|\leq KN^{-r\delta}+O(h^{2}).

For
n\geq 1
, using the inequality
t_{n+1}\leq 2^{r}t_{n}
and estimate (
3.15
), we deduce

|u_{x}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{x}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|\leq K(n+1)^{-2}+O(h^{2}).

Hence, the first spatial truncation error satisfies

|R_{x}^{i,\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}K(N^{-r\delta}+h^{2}),&n=0,\\
K((n+1)^{-2}+h^{2}),&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Estimate for
R_{xx}^{i,\bar{n}}
.

The analysis for the second spatial derivative is analogous. Taylor expansion and integration by parts yield

\displaystyle|u_{xx}(x_{i},\bar{t}_{n})-\delta_{x}^{2}u_{i}^{\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle\leq|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bar{t}_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}u_{xxtt}(x_{i},t)(t_{n+1}-t)\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}u_{xxtt}(x_{i},t)(t_{n}-t)\,dt|+O(h^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{n+1}^{\delta-2}\tau_{n+1}^{2}+\int_{t_{n}}^{\bar{t}_{n}}t^{\delta-2}(t-t_{n})\,dt)+O(h^{2}),

which leads to the same bounds as (
3.15
) for
R_{x}^{i,\bar{n}}
. Therefore,

|R_{xx}^{i,\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}K(N^{-r\delta}+h^{2}),&n=0,\\
K((n+1)^{-2}+h^{2}),&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Combining the above estimates completes the proof.
∎

With the above lemma, we are now in a position to derive the error equation and carry out the convergence analysis.
Let
u_{i}^{n}
and
U_{i}^{n}
be the exact and numerical solutions of
(
1.3
) and the difference scheme (
3.2
) at
(x_{i},t_{n})
, respectively.
Define the error function by

\varepsilon_{i}^{n}=u_{i}^{n}-U_{i}^{n}.

Then
\varepsilon_{i}^{n}
satisfies the following error equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{4h})\varepsilon_{j+1}^{n+1}+(\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\tfrac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}+\tfrac{1}{h^{2}})\varepsilon_{j}^{n+1}+(-\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{4h})\varepsilon_{j-1}^{n+1}\\
&=(\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{4h})\varepsilon_{j+1}^{n}+(\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-\tfrac{1-2e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{h^{2}})\varepsilon_{j}^{n}+(\tfrac{1}{2h^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{4h})\varepsilon_{j-1}^{n}\\
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}((a_{0,n}-e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}({\tau_{n+1}}/{2})^{-\alpha})\varepsilon_{j}^{n}+\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{n-l,n}+b_{n-1-l,n})\varepsilon_{j}^{l}\\
&\quad+(b_{n-1,n}+{e^{-\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}}{\bar{t}_{n}^{-\alpha}})\varepsilon_{j}^{0})+R_{j}^{\bar{n}},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.16)

where
R_{j}^{\bar{n}}
denotes the local truncation error at
(x_{j},\bar{t}_{n})
.
From Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

\displaystyle|R_{j}^{\bar{n}}|

\displaystyle=|{}^{FC}R^{\bar{n}}+R_{t}^{i,\bar{n}}+R_{x}^{i,\bar{n}}+R_{xx}^{i,\bar{n}}|\leq\begin{cases}K(N^{-r(\delta-1)}+h^{2}),&n=0,\\
K((n+1)^{-(2-\alpha)}+h^{2}),&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

(3.17)

Following the same procedure as in the stability analysis, we define the grid functions:

\varepsilon^{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0,\quad x_{0}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{0},\\
\varepsilon_{j}^{n},\quad\bar{x}_{j-1}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq M-1,\\
0,\quad\bar{x}_{M-1}\leq x\leq x_{M},\end{array}\right.

R^{\bar{n}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0,\quad x_{0}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{0},\\
R^{\bar{n}}_{j},\quad\bar{x}_{j-1}\leq x\leq\bar{x}_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq M-1,\\
0,\quad\bar{x}_{M-1}\leq x\leq x_{M}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]
and
\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k]
denote the discrete Fourier transforms of
\varepsilon^{n}(x)
and
R^{\bar{n}}(x)
, respectively.

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{M}jk}\varepsilon^{n}_{j},\quad\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k]=\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{M}jk}R^{\bar{n}}_{j},\quad 0\leq k\leq M-1.

(3.18)

Similar to (
3.6
), Parseval’s equality then gives

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{n}(x)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]|^{2},\quad\|R^{\bar{n}}(x)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k]|^{2}.

(3.19)

Using (
3.18
), multiplying both sides of (
3.16
) by
e^{-ijh\beta}
with
\beta=2\pi k/M
, and summing over
j
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)+\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}+\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}))\hat{\varepsilon}^{n+1}[k]\\
&=(\frac{1}{h^{2}}\cos(h\beta)-\frac{1}{2h}i\sin(h\beta)+(\frac{1}{\tau_{n+1}}-\frac{\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{1-\alpha}\Gamma(2-\alpha)}-\frac{1}{h^{2}}))\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}((a_{0,n}+\frac{e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}\tau_{n+1}^{-\alpha}}{2^{-\alpha}(1-\alpha)}-e^{-\lambda{\tau_{n+1}}/{2}}({\tau_{n+1}}/{2})^{-\alpha})\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]\\
&\quad+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n-1}(a_{n-l,n}+b_{n-1-l,n})\hat{\varepsilon}^{l}[k]+(b_{n-1,n}+{e^{-\lambda\bar{t}_{n}}}{\bar{t}_{n}^{-\alpha}})\hat{\varepsilon}^{0}[k])+\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k].\end{aligned}\right.

(3.20)

We now state the convergence theorem for the fully discrete scheme (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the regularity assumption (
1.4
) and for

r\geq\max\{2/(\delta-1),\,3\}
,
let
u(x,t)
and
\{U^{n}=(U_{0}^{n},\ldots,U_{M}^{n})\}_{n=0}^{N}

be the solution of the problem (
1.3
) and the scheme (
3.2
), respectively.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
h
,
N
, and
\varepsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-U^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(N^{-2}+h^{2}+\varepsilon\big).

(3.21)

Proof.

We first establish the following auxiliary result. Suppose that
\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]

(1\leq n\leq N)
and
\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k]

(0\leq n\leq N-1)
satisfy (
3.20
). Then the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]|\leq(1+\tau_{1})^{n^{,r-2}N^{2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|,\qquad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.22)

We prove (
3.22
) by induction on
n
.
Since the boundary conditions imply
\varepsilon_{j}^{0}=0
, it follows from (
3.18
) that
\hat{\varepsilon}^{0}[k]=0
. In view of condition (
3.20
), the case
n=1
in (
3.22
) yields

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}^{1}[k]|=|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|({(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}+\frac{1}{2\Gamma(2-\alpha)(\frac{\tau_{1}}{2})^{\alpha}}+\frac{1-\cos(h\beta)}{h^{2}})^{2}+(\frac{\sin(h\beta)}{2h})^{2}})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\leq|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|.

Fix
n\in\{1,2,\dots,N-1\}
. Assume that (
3.22
) is valid for
m=1,2,\dots,n
. From (
3.17
) and the definition of the discrete Fourier transform
[
9
,
23
,
48
]
, we have

|\hat{R}^{\bar{n}}[k]|\leq{N^{2}}{(n+1)^{\alpha-2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|.

Using (
3.20
) and following the same argument as in Lemma
3.2
, we derive

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n+1}[k]|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tau_{1})^{n^{r-2}N^{2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|+\tau_{1}{N^{2}}{(n+1)^{\alpha-2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|

\displaystyle\leq((1+\tau_{1})^{n^{r-2}N^{2}}+(1+\tau_{1})^{{N^{2}}{(n+1)^{\alpha-2}}}-1)|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|.

(3.23)

Let
g=(1+\tau_{1})^{{N^{2}}{(n+1)^{\alpha-2}}}
.
Since
r\geq 3
and
g\geq 1
, a direct calculation then shows that

\displaystyle g^{(n+1)^{r-\alpha}}-g^{n^{r-2}(n+1)^{2-\alpha}}-g+1\geq 0.

Substituting this expression for
g
into (
3.23
) yields

|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n+1}[k]|\leq(1+\tau_{1})^{(n+1)^{r-2}N^{2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|.

Thus, inequality (
3.22
) holds for
m=n+1
. By induction, inequality (
3.22
) holds for all
n
.
Using (
3.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]|\leq(1+\tau_{1})^{n^{r-2}N^{2}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|\leq(1+\frac{T}{N^{r}})^{N^{r}}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|\leq e^{T}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|.

Applying Parseval’s identity (
3.19
) and setting
C=e^{T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-U^{n}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{\varepsilon}^{n}[k]|^{2}\leq C^{2}L\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}|\hat{R}^{\bar{0}}[k]|^{2}=C^{2}\|R^{\bar{0}}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\leq C^{2}KL(N^{-2}+h^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}.

(3.24)

Taking the square root on both sides yields the desired estimate and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The convergence order established for scheme (
3.2
) is second order in both time and space. This result is in contrast to the first-order temporal accuracy reported in
[
4
]
and the

1+\alpha
order temporal accuracy obtained in
[
30
]
. In this respect, the present work provides a clear improvement over the existing results.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we develop a second-order finite difference scheme for the tempered time-fractional derivative exhibiting weak regularity at the initial time. The proposed scheme employs piecewise linear interpolation over each small temporal subinterval and yields a fully discrete approximation for the advection–dispersion equation based on fast sum-of-exponentials approximation. To enhance the overall accuracy, temporal differencing is carried out at half-time levels, while the spatial derivatives are approximated by centered differences. In contrast to earlier works, the proposed scheme achieves second-order convergence in both time and space, reduces the storage requirements and the computational costs while maintaining the
same global convergence order. The numerical results were presented to confirm the theoretical analysis results and demonstrate the developed technique is useful and effective for solving the considered model.

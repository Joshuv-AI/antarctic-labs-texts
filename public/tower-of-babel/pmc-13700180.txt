High-dose regular insulin as a potential rescue therapy in severe aluminum phosphide poisoning: a case report

Introduction

Positive inotropic effect of insulin on the cardiac myocytes is through calcium flux. Insulin increases the intramyocytes calcium concentration by increasing the conductance of calcium through the L-type calcium channels and by the increasing intramyocytes concentration of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) [1]. Insulin also leads to an increasing the influx of calcium into the myocardial cells by activation of the Na+-Ca2+ exchanger’s, which brings calcium (Ca2+) into the myocytes in exchange for sodium (Na+) [2, 3] or through increasing the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca2+ uptake of calcium into the SR of myocytes [2–4]. In addition to increasing the intramyocytes calcium level, insulin also enhances the responsiveness of the cardiac cells to calcium [2].

It has been shown insulin decreases the concentration of cytokines that have myocardial depressant effect [5]. High-dose insulin is a potential therapy for hypodynamic and hypotensive septic shock due to its positive inotropic qualities [6].

Aluminum phosphide (rice tablet) poisoning has dramatically increased within the previous years due to its ingestion as a suicide drug. Aluminum phosphide can cause cardiocirculatory collapse and has a negative cardiac inotropic effect. Also, this lethal poison has not any antidote to date [7].

In this paper, a woman with aluminum phosphide ingestion was described who presented with nausea, vomiting, tachypnea, tachycardia, and hypotension. In contrast to our previous experiences that ingestion of aluminum phosphide tablets leading to multiorgan dysfunction in approximately all patients with severe metabolic acidosis and hypotension, early initiation of high dose intravenous regular insulin for this patient prevented severe shock complication, multiorgan failure and death.

Case presentation

A 25-year-old Iranian young woman presented to the emergency ward with nausea and vomiting after ingestion of one aluminum phosphide tablet that obtained from herbal shop for suicide. Her medical history and family history was negative for any disease. Her drug history was negative for any medications. On physical examination, the heart rate, respiratory rate, body temperature, blood pressure and oxygen saturation were 140 beats/min, 30 breaths/min, 36 °C, 90/50 mmHg, and 90%, respectively. The patient was ill. All other examinations such as abdominal was soft, clear lung, and GCS was 14/15, no heart murmur or arrhythmia. No previous history relevant to psychosocial problems. Electrocardiogram revealed sinus tachycardia. Treatment with high dose intravenous regular insulin (80 unit/hour, calculated based on the formula 1–1.5 IU/kg/hour and second day tapered to (50 unit/hour), hypertonic dextrose 50% (1 cc/kg) to maintain the serum glucose around 100–200 mg/dL, potassium (40 mEq/lit) to maintain the serum potassium between 3.5–5 mEq/L, intravenous pantoprazole for stress ulcer prophylaxis, hydrocortisone (100 mg doses at 8-hour intervals), sodium bicarbonate to maintain serum bicarbonate above 20 mEq/L and normal saline for initial hydration and maintenance serum therapy was initiated. Blood sugar and potassium monitored every 1 and 6 h, respectively. Potassium was given to patient if it less than 5 Meq/liter. Glucose-insulin-potassium (GIK) was tapered in according of general condition of patient and answers of tests especially blood sugar and serum potassium as stated earlier. The signs and symptoms of patient resolved completely after 3 days arrival to intensive care unit (Table 2 and Fig. 1). Psychosocial evaluation was done for patient. In addition, post discharge psychiatric follow-up was done. Then, the patient was discharged from the hospital in a good condition. Discussion

In this study, a critical aluminum phosphide poisoning patient, received multiple simultaneous treatments, including fluids, sodium bicarbonate, hydrocortisone, potassium, dextrose, and supportive ICU care and the signs and symptoms of patient resolved completely after 3 days.

It was determined in the study by Bronsveld et al. that administration of GIK (high-dose regular insulin 1.5 U/kg) in 15 patients with septic shock, hypotension and elevated serum lactate levels, that were refractory to fluid resuscitation and vasoactive medications lead to an increase in the cardiac index [8]. In a porcine model of shock using lipopolysaccharide endotoxin, Holger et al. found an improvement in cardiac output, systemic vascular resistance, pulmonary vascular resistance, venous oxygen saturation, and oxygen delivery in a group that was treated with high-dose insulin [9]. In another study by Levenbrown et al. on porcine model of septic shock, the use of high-dose insulin (regular insulin 1.5 U/Kg/h) appeared positive cardiac inotropic effect and decreased the degree of myocardial depression (6).

The most common cause of mortality in aluminum phosphide poisoning patients is severe hypotension. It is commonly refractory to vasoactive agents and massive hydration with normal saline and other isotonic fluids. Other major causes of mortality in these patients are severe metabolic acidosis and cardiogenic shock, which are likely caused by cytochrome C oxidase inhibition by phosphine gas and generalized multiorgan hypoperfusion [10].

The use of infusion of GIK in aluminum phosphide poisoning patients was suggested after its beneficial positive inotropic cardiac effect in patients with critical calcium channel blockers and beta-blockers poisoning [11, 12].

In this paper, a critical aluminum phosphide poisoning patient showed tachypnea, tachycardia and hypotension. Phosphine has negative cardiac inotropic effects and led to hypotension in this case.

In contrast to our previous experiences that ingestion of aluminum phosphide tablets leading to multiorgan failure and severe shock in approximately all critical poisoning patients, early initiation of high dose intravenous regular insulin associated with GIK, normal saline, hydrocortisone and sodium bicarbonate prevented multiorgan dysfunction, death and resolved completely the signs and symptoms of patient. After 3 days of intervention, the patient discharged from the intensive care unit without any complication.

Conclusion

High-dose intravenous regular insulin may be act as a rescue therapy for aluminum phosphide poisoning patients. While this single case suggests a potential benefit, further studies are required to confirm these findings.

Evaluation and comparison of three-dimensional finite element analysis of stress distribution in immediately placed and loaded conventional and customized three-dimensional printed dental implants
Authors: Priya Saini, Vishakha Grover, Shaveta Sood, Ashish Jain, Parveen Kalra
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim:The aim of this study was to evaluate and compare the stress distribution patterns in immediately placed and loaded conventional and customized three-dimensional (3D) printed dental implants by 3D finite element analysis.Materials and Methods:Twelve 3D finite element models [Group A-3 models; Group B-9 models] with 72 test conditions which were modeled and compared from customized 3D printed dental implants [Group A] and 3 commercially available implant systems [Group B] (Straumann, Ankylos, and Astratech) using “SolidWorks”. All models were embedded in extraction socket models of the maxillary central incisor (CI) and Canine (C), Mandibular 1st Premolar. An occlusal loading by axial and nonaxial force of 100 N and 150 N at 30° and 45° was applied on the abutment using the “ANSYS” Suite. Customized 3D printed dental implant (Group A) for maxilla (Max.) CI, Max. C, and mandibular 1st premolar (PM) socket model was compared with three commercial available dental implant systems (Group B) for Max. CI, Max. C, and mandible (Mand.) 1st PM socket model to understand the stress distribution patterns.Results:With increasing oblique loads, von Mises stresses were reduced for the customized group as compared to conventional implants. Increased axial loads caused proportionate increase in the stresses for both groups, yet remained under the physiologic limits in all test conditions. Higher stresses were observed in cortical bone than in cancellous bone at bone–implant contact in general. Marked reduction in von Mises stress was observed at the boundary between compact and cancellous bone. Customized 3D printed implants performed better for oblique loads and comparable for axial load stress distribution in comparison to conventional implant systems in Max. CI and C, Mand. 1st PM.Conclusion:Thus, customized 3D printed implants appear a promising alternative for immediately placed immediately loaded protocols, with additional benefits in specific clinical situations.

INTRODUCTION

Dental implants are prime management option for the restoration of missing teeth and oral cavity rehabilitation in contemporary dentistry. A two-stage Branemark’s protocol was recommended at the onset of the implant era, but over the last few decades, there has been a perpetual hike in the generation of newer dental implant materials, designs, surface modifications, and techniques to promote primary stability and a strong bone–implant interface over time to enhance implant osseointegration.[12345] To hasten the rehabilitation and to avoid surgical complications associated with traditional protocols, a lot of research work has been done in implant design and many new placement and loading protocols have evolved in due course of time used.[45]

The concept of immediate placement of a dental implant was re-sorted by Schulte and Heimke in 1976.[6] Immediate loading of immediately placed dental implants is a recent approach that attempts to meet patient’s demand of immediate rehabilitation. In this protocol, prosthetic restoration is placed in functional or nonfunctional loading, immediately, or within 48 h of surgical procedures.[78] This approach offered multiple benefits such as eliminating the need for removable provisional prosthesis, lesser chances of fibrous tissue formation, providing emotional benefits for a patient, diminishes the risk of fibrous tissue formation, and also shortening the healing period.[8910] There is a significant body of evidence supporting better psychological response from the patients. A plethora of evidence supports the immediately placed and immediately loaded protocols, though with diverse data on survival rates.[11121314]

Primary stability and jumping gap (JG) have been identified as major factors influencing the long-term success of immediately placed implants.[8151617] Primary stability plays an important role as it ensures the initial stable positioning of the implant in the socket, whereas JG is the space between the implant periphery and surrounding bone which may impact the primary stability.[1819202122] Thus, these issues in fresh postextraction sockets have been addressed by placing dental implants exceeding the alveolar apex by 3–5 mm, or by inserting conventional threaded, straight, or tapered implants of greater diameter than the remnant alveolus.[23]

A novel concept of customized three-dimensional (3D) printed dental implant design has been envisioned as a potential solution for precise fit with a complete confirmation to the socket wall, thus allowing placement of the implant in intimate contact with the bone with no JG. We have conducted preliminary work regarding the design of customized implants and their behavior under different loading conditions. The very first design was based on a maxillary premolar tooth, where the part to be introduced into the bone was an externally threaded component, an implant mimic component in the middle, and an abutment that is secured with threads.[24] Multiple improvised design iterations for different single-rooted teeth were customized and the final implant structure was created by first modeling each component separately, then putting them all together.(under communication) Such customized implant theoretically shall permit better bone–implant contact (BIC) area and obviates the need for the use of any additional filler materials such as bone grafts. BIC has a major role in stress distribution across immediate loading implants. Load transfer from implants to surrounding bone depends on the quality and quantity of bone, type of loading, bone–implant interface, implant geometry, implant surface characteristics, and the prosthesis type. Thus, it is imperative to understand the distribution of load and areas of stress concentration for these novel designed customized 3D-printed dental implants.[82526]

Finite element analysis (FEA) has become an increasingly useful tool for the prediction of the effects of stress on the implant and its surrounding bone.[25] FEA is a technique for obtaining a solution to a complex mechanical problem by dividing the problem domain into much smaller and simpler domains (elements), in which the field variables can be interpolated with the use of shape functions.[272829] The current FEA study was aimed to evaluate and compare the stress distribution patterns in immediately placed and loaded conventional and customized 3D printed dental implants by 3D FEA.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This in-vitro, cross-sectional, computational, 3D FEA was conducted in the Department of Periodontology in collaboration with the Production Engineering Department after obtaining approval from the Institutional Research Degree Committee of the institute (Letter no. HSJ/19/296 Dated 06.02.2019). The conceptual methodological framework followed a sequence of reconstruction of 3D socket models from cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) images, geometrical modeling for implants, followed by meshing and application of loading directions, and analysis and comparison of von Mises Stresses.

CI]), maxillary canine [Max. C], and mandibular 1st premolar (Mandible [Mand.] 1st PM) socket model was compared with three commercial available dental implant systems (Group B) for Max. CI, Max. A total of 12 3D finite element models (FEM) were created for both groups based on the reverse engineering technique for all selected teeth. The models were created according to the anatomic root morphology.

Group A (customized three-dimensional printed dental implant)

3D customized FEA models for Max. CI and C and Mand. 1st PM were generated in SolidWorks 2018 according to dimensions obtained from the CBCT [Figure 2a].

Developed computer-aided design models of the dental implant, (a) Customized three-dimensional-printed dental implant model, (b) Straumann implant model, (c) Ankylos implant model, (d) Astratech implant model

After the geometry improvements, the files were transported into SolidWorks computer-aided design software (CAD) (Dassault systems, USA Version 2018) where five components of the implant were designed for each of the selected teeth (Max. CI, Max. C, Mand. PM). In line with the contemporary commercially available implant systems, the most apical part (apical third) was designed as an externally threaded conical screw having a variable length and diameter according to the dimension of the selected teeth and its associated socket. There was a successive 2 mm increase in diameter of the apical third with an external screw thread on the implant surface, starting from the apical most part. This feature was based on the premise of implant screwing into the jaw bone for the accomplishment of primary stability. Mimic part (middle third) was the part to recreate the socket root morphology by mimicking the dimension of the associated teeth socket by utilizing 3D technology and having a solid body for precise fit and function. The third and most coronal part of the implant was the abutment, substructure, or connecting element usually responsible for making the connection between the prosthesis and implant. This design had 8° Morse taper conical connection having an internal joint design between two conical structures to create significant friction through high propensity of parallelism between two structures within the joint space. First screw assembly joined the abutment and mimic part to securely hold the crown. Second screw assembly joined the mimic part and most apical part. Each component of 3D printed implant was separately modeled and then assembled together to obtain the final implant structure for FEA analysis.

Group B (conventional dental implant)

Commercially available best-matched dental implants of three available different implant systems [Straumann, Ankylos, and Astratech] and abutment with “Morse taper design” connection have been used for immediate placement in the selected three sockets (Max. CI, Max. C, Mand. PM). Selected size and design of best-matched implant for Group B based on reference average dimensions of natural teeth are tabulated in Table 1 and Figure 2b-d.

The designed 12 CAD models with their associated sockets were transported into ANSYS software for FEA analysis to simulate the condition of immediately placed and immediately loaded. Model design and loading directions

Each set of the model contained the abutment, implant, and a cuboid block of D2 bone (30 mm × 30 mm × 20 mm). Cortical and cancellous anisotropic properties were applied to the bone. The implant–bone interface was assumed to be 0.3. Between implant, abutment, and abutment screw regions in contact, a frictional coefficient of 0 was assumed.[30]

Finite element models were generated by tetrahedral meshes. The numbers of elements and nodes were well-refined in the models [Table 3]. The refined mesh of the abutment, screw, implant, cortical bone, and cancellous bone was all set to an element size of 0.003 mm, whereas the crestal cortical bone was 2.0 mm [Figure 3].

For Group A, in all three case situations, the average von Mises stress of the implant assembly was comparable and close to the stress value of the apical third of the implant. When comparison was made between subparts and implant body assembly, the average stresses produced were approximately two-fold higher for the mimic part of the implant.

DISCUSSION

Custom-made dental implants have been potentially considered as a solution, mimicking the socket wall morphology by duplicating anatomical root forms of natural teeth and featured the new generation in modern’s day dental practice. The use of 3D manufacturing technology, using freely available or economical software tools from CBCT scans have resulted in the manufacture of dental implants that improvise prosthetics, offer precision, and provide customized and affordable solutions for oral rehabilitation.[3233]

To the best of our understanding, the current work is the first FEA study to support the concept of a novel customized 3D-printed dental implant design that provides intimate contact with the bone with no JG in immediately placed and loaded situations that may be clinically applied to rehabilitate edentulous jaws. Twelve 3D finite element models with 72 test conditions were modeled from customized 3D-printed dental implants and 3 commercial available implant systems using SolidWorks and embedded in extraction socket models of maxillary CI and Canine, Mandibular 1st Premolar have been analyzed and their stress distribution patterns have been studied. FEA has been routinely used by obtaining models from geometric sketches. Current study involved the use of CBCT for the acquisition of bone morphology and measurement of bone density in a living individual based on which the 3D FE modeling was done. 3D models of the anatomic sockets were constructed directly from stacks of 2D CBCT image data with image processing software called MIMICS Innovation Suite (Materialize Mimics) to reflect living individual variability. With this technique, it was possible to prepare highly accurate 3D FE models from individual CT images.[34353637]

In the present study, the situation was modeled to investigate initial stability for the situation immediately after implant placement. A nonlinear frictional contact element was used with frictional coefficient of 0.3, between bone and implant to mimic the situation as that of immediately after implant placement and frictional coefficient of 0 between implant and abutment, to be completely bonded. The frictional coefficient of 0.3 allows minor displacement between implant and bone to keep the implant stable and provide an excellent simulation of the clinical situation of implant–bone interface with immediate loading.[303138]

In all case situations, the stresses were mainly concentrated on the top of bone at BIC around the implant neck and on the threads of the implant with axial loading as well with the oblique loading for both groups. This may be accounted by the fundamental mechanical principle proposed by Misch that when two materials of different modulus are placed together with no intervening material in between and one is loaded, a stress contour increase is observed at their interface as observed during FEA.[39] These findings were consistent with the findings previously published by Premnath et al., 2013.[40]

The findings revealed that there was higher stress concentration in the region of the cortical bone than in the region of cancellous bone in all the cases with least stresses observed in cancellous bone in each model. Within physiological limits for human cortical bone approximately 72–76 MPa for tensile stress, 70–140 MPa for compressive stress is considered normal, however, the tensile stress for cancellous bone is much lower (22–28 MPa).[414243] All the stress values in the present study were within the range of elastic limit (approximately 60 MPa) of human bone which was considered the maximum allowable stress before the onset of permanent strains that could cause microfractures. These findings were in line with the previous findings by Chang et al. 2013 and Premnath et al. 2013 who also stated that the highest stress concentration was found in cortical bone as compared to cancellous bone.[2840]

The stress values at BIC in cortical bone were increased for both the customized and conventional group upon axial loading in all case scenarios. Under oblique loading, the stress values were comparatively lesser for customized 3D-printed dental implants. For Max. CI and Canine and Mand. 1st PM model, the overall stress of conventional implant group was lower with increasing JG. The stress distribution patterns with increasing JG were not consistent. When the stress values were averaged and compared based on JG, irrespective of the implant system used, it was observed with the increasing JG, there was decrease in BIC and the overall stress distribution decreased. There is a paucity of human studies or any identical data studied by FEA in the same case situations, however, Akimoto et al. 1999 in an animal study evaluated the effect of gap width on bone healing around implant placed into simulated extraction socket defects with gap sizes of 0.5 mm, 1.0 mm, and 1.4 mm, the control sites of 0 mm gaps. The findings revealed that after 12 weeks, the simulated extraction sockets healed clinically, with complete bone fill, regardless of the initial gap size.[43]

The maximum von Mises stresses on the abutment were mainly concentrated at the top of the abutment with axial loading, whereas in the case of oblique loading, the stresses were concentrated at the neck of the abutment in the abutment–implant complex in all the test situations. One striking observation noted only for the maxillary CI model was that with increasing load, the highest von Mises stress was noted in the mimic part with a customized 3D-printed dental implant (1504 MPa) under a 150 N nonaxial load at 45° which could be interpreted as a susceptibility of the clinical failure of the middle mimic part of the implant owing to stress concentration at the contact point with the abutment. It emphasizes that in case the shape of the mimic part for any custom implant becomes more arbitrary keeping in view the path of insertion, it may minimize the gap between the abutment and create critical points with high stress susceptible for fracture under masticatory load.

Customized 3D implants in the Max. CI and Mand. 1st premolar case scenario showed better stress distribution to oblique load than conventional implant systems. Only, in maxillary canine socket model relatively higher stresses were observed. Overall, the stress analysis revealed the level of stress distribution in all case situations was under the physiologic limits.

Regardless of the implant–abutment connection, all the models were constructed containing implants with Morse taper connection with anti-rotational features. In the present study, 8° Morse taper connection for customized group, 15° taper for Straumann system, 5.7° taper for Ankylos system, and 11° taper for Astratech system. In all case situations, among different groups, Astratech implant system with 11° taper was found to have least stress values and with increasing taper, the maximum von Mises stress within abutment decreased. Our findings are in line with Kitagawa et al. 2008 who stated that maximum von Mises stress decreased with increasing taper angle in axial load.[44] Another study by Norton et al. 2000 estimated the bending resistance in the ITI implant system with an 8° internal conical connection and in the Astratech implant system with an 11° internal conical connection and reported that the Astratech abutment with 11° taper had a higher ability of the implant-to-abutment joint to resist bending forces, in comparison with the ITI abutment.[45]

The strength of our study is that it provides insight into the novel concept of custom-made implants in different clinical case scenarios. This study provides a proof of concept with some aspects of preclinical validation of customized 3D-printed dental implants in our country. With practically having a friction fit close proximity of the implant to the bone at the time of placement essentially shall contribute to better primary stability and more predictable osseointegration. It was speculated that the presented design would protect the dimensions of jawbone from resorption during healing phases by virtue of the close fit and zero JG to the bony socket. The current study findings shall pave the path for future work and guide new strategies for addressing challenges associated with managing stress distribution. Therefore, additional controlled and randomized clinical studies ought to be conducted with the intention to completely understand the clinical implications of various biomechanical parameters of these kinds of customized implants.

One of the limitations of this FEA study is even though stress is obtained from a 3D model, only a single static load has been applied to the implant, thus an insight into the dynamic loading associated stress patterns remains limited. Further, the accuracy of the developed 3D FEM models is inherently driven by the contemporary tools and techniques of modeling used in the study, thus may need further improvements for the precision of 3D models. Multirooted molar teeth more often require replacement for a well-functioning dentition, however, the study was limited to analyze custom implants for the single-rooted tooth replacements only, due to lack of standardization and optimization of work protocols for molar custom implants. It remains a challenge, yet to be addressed to design, evaluate, and validate 3D printed customized implant solutions for complex clinical situations such as buccal bone dehiscence in anterior teeth, where usually the esthetic demands are high, including an esthetic emergence profile. Although the study aimed its best to simulate the mechanical behaviors of dental implants in response to applied mechanical forces as we included the oblique loading case situations close to real-life scenarios an implant may undergo during cyclic loading and masticatory stresses, yet the results must be interpreted with caution due to limitations such as factors related to the methodology, computational simulation, and linear elastic evaluation, which consider bone-tissue to be isotropic and homogenous and application of static occlusal loading.

CONCLUSION

Within the limits of the study, customized 3D-printed implants showed better stress distribution patterns to oblique loading comparable to the conventional implant systems in all case scenarios. Higher stresses were observed in cortical bone than in cancellous bone. Marked reduction in von Mises stress was observed at the boundary between compact and cancellous bone. Thus, customized 3D-printed implants may be considered viable alternatives for immediately placed immediately loaded protocols of oral rehabilitation situations, with additional benefits in specific clinical case situations. Future clinical studies to ascertain and validate the current study findings are warranted for exploring the clinical applicability of customized 3D-printed implants for better patient outcomes.

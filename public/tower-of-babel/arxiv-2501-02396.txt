On the entropy-minimal martingale measure in the exponential Ornstein–Uhlenbeck stochastic volatility model
Yuri Kabanov, Mikhail A. Sonin

Abstract
We consider a stochastic volatility model where the price evolution depend on the exponential of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. After a brief revision of the related theory the entropy-minimal equivalent martingale measure. is calculated.

1
Introduction

The modern theory of stochastic processes is highly indebted to quantitative finance which is the vast field of its applications.
Unlike the physical world governed by the eternal laws of the nature, “laws” of finance permanently
vary due to various factors including even a theoretical development which may strongly influence the behavior of economic agents. There is a non-stop competition of models describing the dynamics of basic securities as well as ideas how to price the derivative securities. It may happen that specific features that was considered yesterday as drawbacks turn to be advantages today as reflecting new reality. In their everyday practice, financial engineers (“quants”) are asked questions what is the price of such and such option contract.

Mathematics provided a plethora of models and general pricing recommendations. The simplest one is the classic ”pricing by replication” paradigm suggested
by Black and Scholes for a model where price of the basic securities are given by geometric Brownian motions and the number of “sources of randomness” is equal to the number of risky assets. In this case the “market”, or better to say, market model is complete, the equivalent martingale measure is unique, and every European-type contingent claim can be replicated by the terminal value a portfolio.
Unfortunately, in the majority of cases statistical tests reject the hypothesis that the logarithms of price increments are Gaussian random variables. This fact leads to a search for better models of the price dynamics.

Amongst models of price evolution of basic securities, nowadays the so-called stochastic volatility models again become very popular. One of them, sometimes referred to as the exponential Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model, see Mejia Vega
6 (6)
, is considered in the present note.

The general recommendation how to provide an arbitrage-free price of an option consists in computation of its mean with respect to an equivalent martingale measure (EMM). The delicate issue here is with respect to which one.
Various solutions are suggested. Some experts recommend to enlarge the framework by considering more traded securities
(for example, traded “vanilla” options) to get a complete market with a single equivalent martingale measure.
In other words, there is a believe that market follows the ”pricing by replication” rule.

Other experts defend the idea that
financial markets choose a relevant equivalent martingale measure using a certain variational principle. A reasonable choice is the entropy minimization one.
We compute such a martingale measure for the considered model and use this occasion to present a brief, hopefully, comprehensive piece of the beautiful theory of entropy minimal martingale measures usually suffered from technical details and generalizations. In our presentation we are based mainly on papers by Hobson
2 (2)
, Frittelli
1 (1)
, and Kabanov and Stricker,
5 (5)
. In our numerical experiments we observe
using real data (but artificially chosen coefficients) that such a pricing rule gives rather good approximation of the observed option prices.

2
Model

We consider the option pricing for two asset stochastic volatility model where the riskless asset is taken as the numeraire and
S=(S_{t})
, the price process of a risky asset, is given
by the system of SDEs

\displaystyle dS_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{t}(\mu(t,V_{t})dt+\sigma(V_{t})dB_{t})

(2.1)

\displaystyle dV_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta-\alpha V_{t})dt+\beta dW_{t},\quad V_{0}=v,

(2.2)

where
W=\rho B+\bar{\rho}\tilde{B}
,
B
and
\tilde{B}
are independent Wiener processes,

\rho\in[-1,1]
,
\bar{\rho}:=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}
.
The coefficients have a specific form:

\mu(t,V_{t})=(\mu+kt)e^{V_{t}},\qquad\sigma(V_{t})=\sigma e^{V_{t}}.

(2.3)

The parameters
\mu
,
\kappa
, and
\theta
are arbitrary real numbers,
\sigma,\alpha,\beta>0
.

The mean reverting Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
V
is Gaussian. It admits the explicit formula

V_{t}=ve^{-\alpha t}+\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\big(1-e^{-\alpha t}\big)+\beta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha(t-s)}dW_{s}

(2.4)

implying that
V_{t}\sim{\cal N}\left(\theta/\alpha+e^{-\alpha t}(v-{\theta}/{\alpha}),\beta^{2}/(2\alpha)(1-e^{-2\alpha t})\right)
.

The process
e^{V}
sometimes is called exponential Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.
As the usual Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, it has the mean-reverting property. There are statistical evidences showing that this property is inherent to the volatility of financial assets, see Fig. 8.1. Since volatility is positive, it seems reasonable to use the model
\sigma(V_{t})=\sigma e^{V_{t}}

where the scale parameter
\sigma>0
adds a flexibility.

The model has two sources of randomness, the equivalent martingale measure is not unique and one cannot represent every contingent claim by a stochastic integral with respect to the price process
S
. The Back–Scholes paradigm of “pricing by replication” fails. There is no consensus between experts how to price options is such a situation. However, there is a believe that the market prices of options can be obtained by calculating expectations
of corresponding pay-offs with respect to an equivalent martingale measure satisfying some variational property.
The most popular choices are equivalent entropy minimal martingale measures and variance minimal martingale
measures.

3
Basic definitions and properties

Let
(\Omega,{\bf F},({\cal F}_{t})_{t\leq T},P)
be a filtered probability space and let
{\cal X}=\{X\}
be a family of continuous semimartingales. We define the set
{\cal M}
(resp.,
{\cal M}^{e}
) of absolute continuous martingale measures (resp.
equivalent martingale measures) as the
set of probability measures

Q\ll P
(resp.,
Q\sim P
) such that each
X\in{\cal X}
is a martingale with respect to
Q
.
We denote
{\cal Z}
and
{\cal Z}^{e}
the corresponding sets of Radon–Nikodym derivatives
Z=dQ/dP
. Recall that
the (relative) entropy of
Q
with respect to
P
is
I(Q,P):={\bf E}Z\ln Z={\bf E}^{Q}\ln Z
.

The
entropy minimal equivalent martingale measure

Q^{o}
(EMM) is defined as an equivalent martingale measure such that

I(Q^{o},P)=\inf_{Q\in{\cal M}^{e}}I(Q,P).

It is more convenient to define
EMM
as an element
Q^{o}\in{\cal M}^{e}
with the density
Z^{o}\in{\cal Z}^{\varepsilon}
such that

{\bf E}Z^{o}\ln Z^{o}=J^{o}:=\inf_{Z\in{\cal Z}^{e}}{\bf E}Z\ln Z.

4
Prerequisites from the general theory

Let
{\cal Z}
be a non-empty convex set of random variables
Z\geq 0
with
{\bf E}Z=1
. We introduce two i convex subsets of
{\cal Z}
,

{\cal Z}^{e}:=\{Z\in{\cal Z}\colon Z>0\},\qquad{\cal Z}_{\varphi}:=\{Z\in{\cal Z}\colon{\bf E}\varphi(Z)<\infty\},

where

\varphi(x)=x\ln x
,
x>0
,
\varphi(0)=0
. The function
\varphi(x)\geq-e^{-1}
, it is convex, its derivative
\varphi^{\prime}(x)=\ln x+1
,

\varphi^{\prime}(0)=-\infty
, and
\psi(x):=e^{x-1}
is the inverse of
\varphi^{\prime}(x)
.

For
Z,\tilde{Z}\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}
and
t\in[0,T]
we put
f_{t}:=\varphi(tZ+(1-t)\tilde{Z})
and
F_{t}:={\bf E}f_{t}
.

Note that
{\bf E}Z\ln Z<\infty
if and only if
{\bf E}Z\ln^{+}Z<\infty
. Moreover,

{\bf E}Z\ln^{+}\tilde{Z}={\bf E}I_{\{Z\geq\tilde{Z}\}}Z\ln^{+}\tilde{Z}+{\bf E}I_{\{Z<\tilde{Z}\}}Z\ln^{+}\tilde{Z}\leq{\bf E}\varphi(Z)+{\bf E}\varphi(\tilde{Z})+2e^{-1}<\infty.

Similarly,
{\bf E}Z\ln^{+}\tilde{Z}\leq{\bf E}\varphi(Z)+{\bf E}\varphi(\tilde{Z})<\infty
. Thus the random variable
Z\ln\tilde{Z}
in this framework is integrable.

Lemma 1

F^{\prime}_{0}={\bf E}f^{\prime}_{0}={\bf E}Z\ln\tilde{Z}-{\bf E}\varphi(\tilde{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
0<s\leq t\leq 1
. Then

(f_{t}-f_{0})/t\geq(f_{s}-f_{0})/s
. Indeed, this inequality is equivalent to the inequality
f_{s}\leq(s/t)f_{t}+(1-s/t)f_{0}
which holds since
f
is convex. Also
f_{1}-f_{0}=\varphi(Z)-\varphi(\tilde{Z})
where the rhs is an integrable r.v. By the monotone convergence
F^{\prime}_{0}={\bf E}f^{\prime}_{0}
. It remains to note that

f^{\prime}_{0}:=\varphi^{\prime}(\tilde{Z})(Z-\tilde{Z})=Z-\tilde{Z}+Z\ln\tilde{Z}-\varphi(\tilde{Z})

and, therefore,
F^{\prime}_{0}={\bf E}Z\ln\tilde{Z}-{\bf E}\varphi(\tilde{Z})
.

\Box

Put
\widehat{J}^{o}:=\inf_{Z\in{\cal Z}}{\bf E}\varphi(Z)
. It happens that if a minimizer exists (necessarily unique because of the strict convexity of
\varphi
), then
it is an element of
{\cal Z}^{e}
provided that the latter set is non-empty. The formal statement is

Lemma 2

If
\widehat{J}^{o}={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})
for some
Z^{o}\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}
and
{\cal Z}^{e}\cap{\cal Z}_{\varphi}\neq\emptyset
, then
Z^{o}\in{\cal Z}^{e}
and
\widehat{J}^{o}=J^{o}
.

Proof.
Let
Z\in{\cal Z}^{e}\cap{\cal Z}_{\varphi}
. Applying the above lemma (with
\tilde{Z}=Z^{o}
) we get that

F^{\prime}_{0}={\bf E}Z\ln Z^{o}-\widehat{J}^{o}
. Since the convex function
F_{t}
attains its minimum at zero,
F^{\prime}_{0}\geq 0
. But this is possible only if
Z^{o}>0
(a.s.).
\Box

Let
{\cal C}:=\{\eta\colon{\bf E}|\eta|Z<\infty\ \hbox{and\ }{\bf E}\eta Z\leq 0\ \forall\,Z\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}\}
.

The next criteria provides a checking tool.

Lemma 3

Let
Z^{o}\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}\cap{\cal Z}^{e}
and let
J:={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})
. Then

J^{o}=J\Longleftrightarrow\hbox{ there is $\eta\in{\cal C}$
such that ${\bf E}\eta Z^{o}=0$ and $Z^{o}=e^{J-\eta}$}.

Proof.
(
\Leftarrow
) Due to convexity of
\varphi
we have for any
Z\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}
that

{\bf E}\varphi(Z)\geq{\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})+{\bf E}\varphi^{\prime}(Z^{o})(Z-Z^{o})={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})+{\bf E}(1+J-\eta)(Z-Z^{o})\geq{\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o}).

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
J^{o}={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})
. Let
\eta=J^{o}-\ln Z^{o}
. Then
{\bf E}\eta Z^{o}=0
and for any
Z\in{\cal Z}_{\varphi}

{\bf E}\eta Z=J^{o}-{\bf E}Z\ln Z^{o}={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})-{\bf E}Z\ln Z^{o}=-F^{\prime}_{0}\leq 0

since
Z^{o}
is the minimizer.
\Box

Lemma 4

If
{\cal Z}
is closed in
L^{0}
and
\widehat{J}^{o}<\infty
, then there is
Z^{o}\in{\cal Z}
such that

J^{o}={\bf E}\varphi(Z^{o})
.

The above formulation is slightly more general than Prop. 3.2 in
5 (5)
, but it could be obtained
by the similar arguments where the reference to the Komlós theorem is replaced by the reference to the
von Weizsäcker theorem, see
4 (4)
, Th. 5.2.3.

Remark.
The presentation of this section is a simplified version of that in
5 (5)
where more general functions than
x\ln x
are considered.

5
The Hobson construction

Let us consider the system of SDE, more general than (
2.1
) - (
2.2
):

\displaystyle dS_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{t}Y_{t}(\lambda_{t}dt+dB_{t}),

(5.1)

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}dt+\beta_{t}dW_{t},\quad V_{0}=v,

(5.2)

where
\lambda_{t}=\lambda(t,Y_{t})
,
\alpha_{t}=\alpha(t,Y_{t})
,
\beta_{t}=\beta(t,Y_{t})
where
\lambda,\alpha,\beta
are continuous functions. We assume that
the second equation above has a unique strong solution and
\lambda^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}<\infty
.

Let
\xi
be a predictable process with
\xi^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}<\infty
.
Define a strictly positive local martingale

Z^{\xi}={\cal E}(-\lambda\cdot B-\xi\cdot\tilde{B})=\exp\left\{-\lambda\cdot B-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\cdot\Lambda-\xi\cdot\tilde{B}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\cdot\Lambda\right\}.

Here and the sequel we use, where it is convenient, the standard notations for integrals (ordinary and stochastic);
\Lambda_{t}\equiv t
.

If
{\bf E}Z^{\xi}_{T}=1
, then
Q^{\xi}=Z^{\xi}_{T}P
is a probability measure equivalent to
P
. According to the Girsanov teorem, under
Q^{\xi}
both

B^{\lambda}:=B+\lambda\cdot\Lambda
and
\tilde{B}^{\xi}:=\tilde{B}+\xi\cdot\Lambda
are Wiener processes.

The following result is a part of Th. 3.1 from
2 (2)
.

Proposition 1

Let
H
be a predictable process with
H^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}<\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}-H\cdot B^{\lambda}_{T}-\xi\cdot\tilde{B}_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}=J={\rm const}.

(5.3)

(a)
Suppose that
(H-\lambda)\cdot B^{\lambda}
is a
Q^{\xi}
-martingale. Then
I(Q^{\xi},P)=J
.

(b)
If, moreover, the process

(H-\lambda)\cdot B^{\lambda}
is a
Q
-martingale with respect to any equivalent
martingale measure
Q
with finite entropy with respect to
P
,
then
Q^{\xi}
is the entropy minimal EMM and
I(Q^{\xi},P)=J=J^{o}
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
{\bf E}^{\xi}
denote the expectation with respect to the measure
Q^{\xi}
. Then
{\bf E}^{\xi}(H-\lambda)\cdot B^{\lambda}_{T}=0
and we get after regrouping terms that

\displaystyle{\bf E}Z^{\xi}_{T}\ln Z^{\xi}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}^{\xi}\ln Z^{\xi}_{T}={\bf E}^{\xi}\Big[-\lambda\cdot B_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}-\xi\cdot\tilde{B}_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}^{\xi}\Big[(H-\lambda)\cdot B^{\lambda}_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}-H\cdot B^{\lambda}_{T}-\xi\cdot\tilde{B}_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}\cdot\Lambda_{T}\Big]=J.

(b)
According to (
5.3
),
Z^{\xi}_{T}=e^{J-\eta}
where

\eta:=(\lambda-H)\cdot B^{\lambda}_{T}
. Due to our assumption
{\bf E}^{Q}\eta=0
for any equivalent martingale measure with finite entropy and the result follows from Lemma
3
.
\Box

6
Solution of the Hobson equation

In the Hobson equation (
5.3
) we should determine the processes
\xi
and
H
and the constant
J
.
To this end, we consider on
[0,T]
the terminal value problem

\dot{f}+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}f^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}^{2}\beta^{2}f^{\prime 2}+(\alpha-\rho\beta\lambda)f^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}=0,\qquad f(T,y)=0,

(6.1)

where the functions
\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\rho,\bar{\rho}
of the variable
(t,y)
are known,
\dot{f}
and
f^{\prime}
are derivatives in
t
and
y
, respectively.

Proposition 2

Suppose that the problem (
6.1
) has a solution
f\in C^{1,2}
.
Then

\xi_{t}=\rho(t,Y_{t})\beta(t,Y_{t})f^{\prime}(t,Y_{t}),\qquad H_{t}=\bar{\rho}(t,Y_{t})\beta(t,Y_{t})f^{\prime}(t,Y_{t}),\quad J=f(0,Y_{0}).

Proof.
Recall that
W_{t}=\rho\cdot B_{t}+\bar{\rho}\cdot\tilde{B}_{t}
. If there is a constant
J
and a process
\theta_{t}=\theta(t,Y_{t})
such that
\theta^{2}_{t}\cdot\Lambda_{T}<\infty
and

J+\theta\cdot W_{T}+\Big(\rho\theta\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}^{2}\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\Big)\cdot\Lambda_{T}=0,

(6.2)

then
J
,
\xi_{t}:=\bar{\rho}_{t}\theta_{t}
, and
H_{t}:=\rho_{t}\theta_{t}
form a solution of the equation (
5.3
).

Applying the Itô formula with
f\in C^{1,2}
solving (
6.1
) and
Y
given by (
5.2
) we get that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0,Y_{0})+\beta f^{\prime}\cdot W_{T}+\Big(\dot{f}+\alpha f^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}f^{\prime\prime}\Big)\cdot\Lambda_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0,Y_{0})+\beta f^{\prime}\cdot W_{T}+\Big(\rho\lambda\beta f^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\rho}^{2}\beta^{2}f^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\Big)\cdot\Lambda_{T}.

Thus, (
6.2
) holds with
J=f(0,Y_{0})
and
\theta_{t}=\beta(t,Y_{t})f^{\prime}(t,Y_{t})
.
\Box

7
Minimal entropy EMM for the model (
2.1
) – (
2.3
)

Theorem 7.1

The density of minimal entropy EMM in the model (
2.1
) – (
2.3
) has the form

Z^{o}_{T}=\frac{dQ^{o}}{dP}=\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mu+\kappa t}{\sigma}dB_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(\mu+\kappa t)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}dt\right\}.

(7.1)

Moreover,
with respect to the measure
Q^{o}=Z^{o}_{T}P
this model admits the representation

\displaystyle dS_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{t}Y_{t}dB^{o}_{t},

(7.2)

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{y}\Big(\theta+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\alpha\ln\sigma-\beta\rho\frac{\mu+\kappa t}{\sigma}-\alpha\ln Y_{t}\Big)dt+\beta Y_{t}dW^{o}_{t},

(7.3)

where
{\bf E}^{o}[B^{o}_{t}W^{o}_{t}]=\rho t
.

Proof.
Let us denote by
Y_{t}:=\sigma e^{V_{t}}
. By the Itô formula

dY_{t}=Y_{t}\Big(\theta+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\alpha\ln\sigma-\alpha\ln Y_{t}\Big)dt+\beta Y_{t}dW_{t}.

In the notations of the previous section we have:

\lambda(t)=\frac{\mu+\kappa t}{\sigma},\quad\alpha(y)=y\left(\theta+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\alpha\ln\sigma\right)-\alpha y\ln y,\quad\beta(y)=\beta y.

It is easily seen that in our specific case that the solution of the terminal problem (
6.1
) does not depend on
y
. That is, it satisfies the terminal value problem for the ordinary differential equation

\dot{f}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}=0,\quad f(T)=0,

(7.4)

and admits the explicit expression

f(t)=\frac{(\mu+\kappa(T-t))^{3}-\mu^{3}}{6\sigma^{2}\kappa}.

Also,
H=0
and
\xi=0
. The density of the minimal entropy EMM is given by (
7.1
) and
J=f(0)
.

9
Conclusion

In this note we present a short theoretical prerequisites for a search of the minimal entropy equivalent martingale using the Hobson construction. As an application, we
obtained an explicit expression for such a measure for a specific stochastic volatility model referred in the literature as the exponential Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model in the form suitable for pricing contingent claims.

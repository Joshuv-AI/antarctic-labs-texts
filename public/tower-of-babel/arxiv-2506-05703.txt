Operators of stochastic adding machines and Julia sets
D.A. Caprio, A. Messaoudi, I. Tsokanos, G. Valle

Abstract
A stochastic adding machine is a Markov chain on the set of non-negative integers ℤ + \mathbb{Z}_{+} that models the process of adding one by successively updating the digits of a number’s expansion in a given numeration system. At each step, random failures may occur, interrupting the procedure and preventing it from continuing beyond a certain point. The first model of such a stochastic adding machine, constructed for the binary base, was introduced by Killeen and Taylor. Their work was motivated by applications to biological clocks, aiming to model phenomena related to time discrimination and/of psychological judgment. From a mathematical perspective, they characterized the spectrum of the associated transition operator in terms of a filled Julia set. In this paper, we consider a stochastic adding machine based on a bounded Cantor numeration system and extend its definition to a continuous state space–namely, the closure of ℤ + \mathbb{Z}_{+} with respect to the topology induced by the Cantor numeration system. This stochastic process naturally induces a transition operator S S acting on the Banach space of continuous complex-valued functions over the continuous state space, as well as a fibered filled Julia set ℰ \mathcal{E} . Our main result describes the spectrum of S S in terms of the fibered filled Julia set ℰ \mathcal{E} . Specifically, if the stochastic adding machines halts with probability one after a finite number of steps, then the spectrum of S S coincides with ℰ \mathcal{E} ; otherwise, the spectrum coincides with the boundary ∂ ℰ \partial\mathcal{E} .



Keywords—
Julia sets, Stochastic adding machines, Markov chains, Spectrum of transition operator, Cantor systems of numeration

Introduction

In this work, we explore a theme that intersects several mathematical areas, including probability theory, complex dynamical systems, and spectral theory. Specifically, we investigate stochastic adding machines and their connections to Julia sets and Markov chains.

Julia sets are compact subsets of the complex plane that play a fundamental role in the study of Complex Dynamics.
Given a non-constant complex polynomial
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, the
filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{f}
of

whose forward iteration, that is, the sequence

, is bounded. The
Julia set
of

is the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{f}
of its filled Julia set.

More generally, given a sequence
\bar{f}=\left(f_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
of non-constant complex polynomials
f_{r}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
for
r\geq 1
, the
fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{f}}
of the sequence
\bar{f}
is the set of complex numbers
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that the forward orbit
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}\left(z\right)\right\}_{r\geq 1}
is bounded, where
\tilde{f}_{r}:=f_{r}\circ f_{r-1}\circ\dots\circ f_{1}

r\geq 1
(see, for intance,
[
17
]

Julia sets and filled Julia sets were introduced independently by Julia

12

In 2000, Killeen and Taylor
[
13

14
]
established a connection between Julia sets and adding machines. They considered the addition algorithm for non-negative integers
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
expressed in binary and introduced what is now known as the
binary stochastic adding machine
. This machine is a Markov chain with states corresponding to the non-negative integers, which models the process of adding
1
by changing one digit at a time in the binary expansion of a number and allows random failures with probability
1-p
that prevent the algorithm from continuing at each step.

They showed that the spectrum of the transition operator of the binary stochastic adding machine, acting on the space
l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
of bounded complex sequences endowed with the supremum norm, is equal to the filled Julia set of the quadratic map
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, defined by
f\left(z\right)=\left(z-\left(1-p\right)\right)^{2}/p^{2}
.

In recent years, stochastic adding machines based on alternative numeration systems have been introduced. For example, a stochastic adding machine based on the Fibonacci system is studied in

In this work, we study adding machines defined over a Cantor system of numeration.

The

A

It is worth noting that the binary stochastic adding machine of Killeen and Taylor corresponds to the special case where

\textrm{p}\left(n,m\right):=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}&,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ if }m=\sum_{r=s+1}^{+\infty}a_{r}\left(n\right)q_{r-1}\text{ with }1\leq s\leq s_{n}-1,\\

Note that the matrix

In

19
]
, the authors determined the spectra of the transition operator

acting on a Banach space

, where

is taken to be one of the following: the space

\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)

A closed-form expression for the operator

Our main result describes the spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)

Preleminaries

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
I:X\to X
denote the identity operator. Recall that for a continuous linear operator
S:X\to X
, the
spectrum
\sigma\left(S,X\right)
can be partitioned into three disjoint subsets (see, for instance,
[

]

1.

The

:

\sigma_{pt}\left(S,X\right)\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:S-\lambda I\hskip 9.24994pt\text{is not injective}\right\}.

2.

The

:

\sigma_{c}\left(S,X\right)\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;S-\lambda I\hskip 9.24994pt\text{is injective},\hskip 9.24994pt\overline{\left(S-\lambda I\right)X}\;=\;X\;\text{ and }\;\left(S-\lambda I\right)X\neq X\right\},

where the closure
\overline{\left(S-\lambda I\right)X}
is taken in the norm topology of

.

3.

The
residual spectrum
:

\sigma_{r}\left(S,X\right)\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:S-\lambda I\;\text{ is injective and }\;\overline{\left(S-\lambda I\right)X}\;\neq\;X\right\}.

In addition, we consider the
approximate spectrum
:

\sigma_{ap}\left(S,X\right)\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\inf_{\left|\left|x\right|\right|=1}\left|\left|\left(S-\lambda I\right)x\right|\right|\;=\;0\right\}.

It is well known that

\overline{\sigma_{pt}\left(S,X\right)}\;\subseteq\;\sigma_{ap}\left(S,X\right)\;\subseteq\;\sigma\left(S,X\right).

(2.1)

[

is defined using the same probabilistic rules as in equation (
1.5
), with
x
and
y
replacing
n
and
m
, respectively.

Define the operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
, such that for any continuous map
g
and for each point
x
in
\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}
:

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}g\right)\left(x\right):=\;\sum_{y\in\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}}\textrm{p}\left(x,y\right)\cdot g\left(y\right)

Using the earlier formula for transition probabilities, this becomes:

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}g\right)\left(x\right)\;{=}\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)g\left(x\right)+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s_{x}}p_{r}\right)g\left(\textrm{V}\left(x\right)\right)+\sum_{s=1}^{s_{x}-1}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}\right)\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)g\left(T_{s}\left(x\right)\right),

(3.1)

where
T_{s}
is defined as:

T_{s}\left(x\right):=\;x-\sum_{r=1}^{s}\left(d_{r}-1\right)q_{r-1}\;=\;\sum_{r=s+1}^{+\infty}a_{r}\left(x\right)q_{r-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for }1\leq s\leq s_{x}.

(3.2)

This formula adjusts depending on the value of

:

•

If
s_{x}=1
, then:

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)g\left(x\right)\;=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)g\left(x\right)+p_{1}g\left(V\left(x\right)\right),

•

If
x=x_{max}
(i.e.,
s_{x}=+\infty
), then:

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)g\left(x_{max}\right)\;=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)g\left(x_{max}\right)+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}p_{r}\right)g\left(x_{min}\right)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}\right)\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)g\left(T_{s}\left(x_{max}\right)\right).

The operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
is well-defined, meaning that for any continuous map
g
in
\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
, the image
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}g
is also continuous. Moreover, it is a bounded linear operator with norm satisfying:
||\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}||\leq 1
.
This follows from the identity:

\left(1-p_{1}\right)+\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}+\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\left(\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}\right)\;=\;1.

Importantly,
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
extends the operator
S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
defined on
l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
. That is, we can think of the continuous state space as a compactification of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and embed functions from
\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
into
l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
using the operator:

P:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right),\hskip 9.24994ptg\mapsto\left(g\left(n\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}.

This operator is injective because
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is dense in
\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}
. So each continuous map
g
is uniquely determined by its values on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. As a result, we have the compatibility condition:

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}g\right)\left(n\right)\;=\;\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\circ Pg\right)\left(n\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(3.3)

4
Main result

The main result characterizes the spectrum of the operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
acting on the space
\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
when the sequence
\bar{d}
is bounded.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
and
\bar{p}=\left(p_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be sequences of positive integers and probabilities, respectively, as defined in equations (
1.1
) and (
1.4
).

Assume that the sequence of integers
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
is bounded. Then the following holds:

\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\;=\;\sigma_{ap}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\;=\;\begin{cases}\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if }\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;=\;0,\\
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if }\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>\;0.\end{cases}

(4.1)

In particular, the point spectrum
\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
coincides with set
\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
, and its closure is the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, i.e.,

\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\;=\;\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}

(4.2)

and

\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}\;=\;\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}.

(4.3)

Remark 4.1
.

In the special case where the sequence
d_{r}=d
and
p_{r}=p
for all
r\geq 1
, for some
d\geq 2
and
p\in\left(0,1\right)
, Theorem
4.1
asserts that

\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\;=\;\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\overline{\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)}\;=\;\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}.

This recovers the result of Killeen and Taylor
[
13
]
who demonstrated that for the case where
d_{r}=2
and
p_{r}=p>0
for all

, the spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{2},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{2}}}\right)\right)
is contained within
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{2},\bar{p}}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{2},\bar{p}}
is the filled Julia set of the polynomial
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
f\left(z\right)=\left(z-\left(1-p\right)\right)^{2}/p^{2}
.

Plan for the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

•

We begin by demonstrating that
\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}\subseteq\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
and that
\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}\subseteq\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

•

We prove that
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
by leveraging the probability properties of the associated Markov chain.

•

Assuming that the sequence
\bar{d}
is bounded, we show that
\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\subseteq\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
, and establish that
\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}=\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
by applying techniques from the theory of Julia sets and the theory of families of normal functions. Consequently, we obtain that
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\subseteq\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
.

•

Assuming that
\bar{d}
is bounded, we show that

implies
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\subseteq\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
by using a constructive proof. We also show that

implies
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\subseteq\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
by exploiting the dynamics of the system.

Let

By convention, we set

Example 5.1
.

As an illustrative example, consider the case where

and

for all

, with

In this setting, the sequence

To establish the inclusions (
5.2
), we use the following lemma, which guarantees that the fibered Julia set

coincides with the point spectrum
\sigma_{pt}\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)\right)

\mathcal{E}_{\overline{d},\overline{p}}
in terms of the sequences
\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)_{r\geq 1}

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
and
\bar{p}=\left(p_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be sequences of positive integers and probabilities, defined in relations (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), respectively.
Then, the fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
satisfies:

\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\;=\;\sigma_{pt}\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)\right).

More precisely, a vector
\mathbf{w}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
is an eigenvector associated with an eigenvalue
\lambda
if and only if
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, and there exist a non-zero constant

such that
\mathbf{w}=c\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
, where
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
is the sequence defined in (
5.3
).

Furthermore, for a given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the sequence
\left(\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\right)_{r=1}^{+\infty}
diverges to infinity if and only if there exists an index
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;>1
. That is,

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;=\;+\infty\hskip 9.24994pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 9.24994pt\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;>1\;\text{for some }r_{0}\geq 1.

(5.6)

In particular, the following characterizations of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
hold:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\;

\displaystyle=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\tilde{f}_{r-1}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left(v_{\lambda}\left(n\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\;\text{ is bounded}\right\}.

Here, the terms
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)
for
r\geq 1
are defined as in (
5.4
).

Proof of inclusions (
5.2
).

We begin by proving that the set
\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
is a subset of the point spectrum
\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
.

Fix
\lambda\in\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
. Then, there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)=1
, which implies
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r_{0}\right)=1
. Consequently, for all
r\geq r_{0}
, one has
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)=1
and
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)=1
.

Define a map
g_{\lambda}:\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\to\mathbb{C}
by

g_{\lambda}\left(x\right):=\;\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}(x)}\;=\;\prod_{r=1}^{r_{0}}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(x\right)}

for
x=\left(a_{r}\left(x\right)\right)_{r=1}^{+\infty}\in\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
g_{\lambda}
is continuous and extends the sequence
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}:\Gamma_{\bar{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
to
\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}}
. Moreover, by Lemma
5.2
and the definition of the operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, it follows that
\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}-\lambda\tilde{I}\right)g_{\lambda}=0
, where
\tilde{I}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
is the identity map.

Hence, one concludes that
\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}\subseteq\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
.

We now turn to the second inclusion in (
5.2
), namely,
\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}\subseteq\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. Since the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}

\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}\subseteq\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.
Let

, and let

be such that
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(z\right)=1
. Since
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}
is holomorphic and non-constant, it is an open map. Therefore, for every
\epsilon\;>0
, there exists
z_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\left|z-z_{\epsilon}\right|\;<\epsilon
such that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(z_{\epsilon}\right)\right|\;>1
. By Lemma

, it follows that
z_{\epsilon}\not\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, while

, since

, and we conclude:
\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left(\left\{1\right\}\right)\subseteq\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

The proof is complete.
∎

for all integers
r\geq 1
, where

Next, we define the corresponding operators

\tilde{S}_{r}:=\;\tilde{S}_{\bar{d}_{r},\bar{p}_{r}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{R}_{r}:=\;\frac{\tilde{S}_{r}-\left(1-p_{r}\right)\tilde{I}_{r}}{p_{r}}

(5.8)

acting on

\tilde{R}_{r}

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
r\geq 1

f_{r}\left(\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{r},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{r}}\right)\right)\right)\;=\;\sigma\left(\tilde{R}_{r}^{d_{r}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{r}}\right)\right)\;=\;\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{r+1},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{r+1}}\right)\right).

Proof of the Unconditional Relation
5.1
.

We prove the inclusion
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}

\overline{B\left(0,1\right)}

Throughout this section, we assume that the sequence of positive integers

is bounded.

5.2.1
Proof of Relations (
4.2
) and (
4.3
)

Throughout this section, the sequence of positive integers
\bar{d}
is assumed to be bounded.

The proof of equalities (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) makes use of the theory of normal families of analytic functions. Let
\mathcal{G}=\left\{g_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
be a family of analytic functions
g_{r}:W\to\mathbb{C}
, defined on an open set
W\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.
The family
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
normal at
z\in W
if there exists an open set
U\subseteq W
such that every sequence in
\mathcal{G}
has a subsequence which converges uniformly on
U
either to a bounded analytic function or to infinity (see for instance
[
15
, Chapter 9, p. 340]
).
A sequence
\left(g_{r}:U\to\mathbb{C}\right)_{r\geq 1}

converges uniformly to infinity
if, for every compact subset
K\subseteq U
and every
M\;>0
, there exists
R_{K,M}\geq 1
such that
\left|g_{r}\left(z\right)\right|\geq M
for all
z\in K
and all
r\geq R_{K,M}
.

We now state a lemma, proved in the Annex, that plays a central role in the proof.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive integers and
\bar{p}=\left(p_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be a sequence of probabilities. Then:

1.

A complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
lies in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
if and only if the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is normal at
z\in\mathbb{C}
. That is,
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\;=\;\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}\;\text{ is not normal at }z\right\}
.

2.

If the sequence of integers
\bar{d}
is bounded and
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is an open set such that
U\cap\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\neq\emptyset
, then for every
w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
, there exists
r\geq 1
such that
\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{w\right\}\cap U\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

In what follows, assume that the sequence
\bar{d}
is bounded.

We begin by proving relation (
4.3
), namely:
\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}=\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. It suffices to prove the inclusion
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}
since the reverse inclusion holds unconditionally by Proposition
5.1
.
Fix a point
z\in\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, and let
U_{z}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open neighborhood of
z
. By Lemma
5.4
(2), the open set
U_{z}
must intersect the set
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
, that is,
U_{z}\cap\left(\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}\right)\neq\emptyset
.
Since the choice of the neighborhood
U_{z}
was arbitrary, one concludes that
z\in\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}
, as desired.

Now, we turn to relation (
4.2
), which asserts that
\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},C\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)=\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
.
To prove this, it is enough to show the inclusion
\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},C\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\subseteq\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
since the inverse inclusion follows from Proposition
5.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
, and let
g_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
be a non-zero eigenvector associated with
\lambda
.
By Lemma
5.2
and relation (
3.3
), one has

g_{\lambda}\left(n\right)\;=\;c\cdot v_{\lambda}(n)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }n\in\Gamma_{\bar{d}},

where
c=g\left(0\right)\neq 0
.
Without loss of generality, we may normalize and take
c=1
, so that
g\left(n\right)=v_{\lambda}\left(n\right)
for all
n\in\Gamma_{\bar{d}}
.

Since the sequence
\left(q_{r-1}\right)_{r\geq 1}
converges to
0
in
\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}
as

, and
g_{\lambda}\left(q_{r-1}\right)=\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)
for each

, one deduces that

\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\;=\;\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{\lambda}\left(q_{r-1}\right)\;=\;g_{\lambda}\left(0\right)\;=\;v_{\lambda}\left(0\right)\;=\;1.

We now invoke the following claim, which will complete the argument.

Claim 5.5
.

Suppose that the sequence
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
is bounded. If
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)=1
, then there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)=1
for every
r\geq r_{0}
.

Assuming the claim, it follows that
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r_{0}\right)=1
for some
r_{0}\geq 1
, and hence
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)=1
.
Therefore,
\lambda\in\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
, proving the desired inclusion.

It remains to prove Claim
5.5
. Fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)=1
.
Assume for contradiction that
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\neq 1
for all
r\geq 1
. Using the recurrence relation

\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\;=\;{1\over p_{r}}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r-1\right)^{d_{r-1}}-{1-p_{r}\over p_{r}},

we can express the difference
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)-1
in terms of
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r-k\right)-1
as:

\displaystyle\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)-1\;=\;\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r-k\right)-1\right)\cdot{z_{r-k+1}z_{r-k+2}\ldots z_{r}\over p_{r-k+1}p_{r-k+2}\ldots p_{r}},

(5.9)

for all

, where

z_{j}:=\;1+\iota_{\lambda}(j-1)+\cdots+\iota_{\lambda}(j-1)^{d_{j-1}-1},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for each }\;2\leq j\leq r.

Since
\bar{d}
is bounded and
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)=1
, it follows by relation (
5.9
) that the sequence
\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)_{r\geq 1}
is unbounded, which is a contradiction.
Therefore, there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
\iota_{\lambda}\left(r_{0}\right)=1
. This proves the claim and the proof of (
4.2
) is complete.

∎

Our aim is to establish the inclusion

Proof of (

) (Case

Assume that

.

Fix

To this end, for each

g_{\lambda,t}\left(x\right):=\;\prod_{r=1}^{t}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}(x)}.

(5.10)

We distinguish two cases, depending on whether the pointwise limit
\underset{t\to+\infty}{\lim}g_{\lambda,t}\left(x\right)
defines a function in
\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
.
In the case where the pointwise limit of the sequence of

g_{\lambda}\left(x\right)\;:=\;\lim_{t\to+\infty}g_{\lambda,t}\left(x\right)\;=\;\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}(x)}.

Specifically, we show that if this limit defines a continuous function, then

is an eigenvalue; otherwise,

belongs to the approximate spectrum. According to relations (
4.2

Case 1:

Assume either that the pointwise limit of the sequence
\left(g_{\lambda,t}\right)_{t\geq 1}
does not exist, or that, if it does exist, it is not a continuous function—that is,

\left|\left|\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}-\lambda I\right)g_{\lambda,t}\right|\right|_{\infty}\to 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{as}\hskip 9.24994ptt\to+\infty,

since

for all

(indeed,

\lambda\cdot\prod_{r=1}^{s_{0}}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{d_{r}-1}\;=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)\prod_{r=1}^{s_{0}}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{d_{r}-1}+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s_{0}}p_{r}\right)\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(s_{0}\right)\right)^{d_{s_{0}}}\\

This identity follows directly from equations (

\displaystyle=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)\prod_{r=1}^{s_{x}}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(x\right)}\cdot\prod_{r=s_{x}+1}^{t}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(x\right)}+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s_{x}}p_{r}\right)\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(s_{x}\right)\right)^{a_{s_{x}}\left(x\right)+1}\cdot\prod_{r=s_{x}+1}^{t}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(x\right)}

.

Case 2:

We now address the proof of relation (
4.1
) in the case where the product of the probabilities satisfies
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>0
, i.e., the transient case. Since the argument in this setting is more technical, we begin by outlining the proof in a special, more tractable case: when the sequence
\left(p_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
is identically equal to
1
, denoted by
\bar{1}:=\left(1\right)_{r\geq 1}
. In this case, we observe that for each
r\geq 1
, the corresponding function simplifies to
f_{r}\left(z\right)=z^{d_{r}}
. The following claims can be verified:

1.

The fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}
coincides with the closed unit disc
\overline{B\left(0,1\right)}
.

2.

The operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
acts as a shift:
\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}\right)g\left(x\right)=g\left(x+1\right)
.

3.

The spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
coincides with the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left|\lambda\right|=1\right\}
.

The first two assertions are straightforward. Regarding the third, Proposition
5.1
implies that it suffices to show that the operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}-\lambda\tilde{I}
is bijective for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\left|\lambda\right|<1
. To prove surjectivity, let
q\in\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma}_{\bar{d}}\right)
be given. Define the function
g_{q}:\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\to\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)
by

g_{q}\left(x\right):=\;\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}q\left(x-1-n\right).

A direct computation shows that
\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}-\lambda\tilde{I}\right)g_{q}=q
, so the operator is surjective. To prove injectivity, assume
g\in\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma}_{\bar{d}}\right)
satisfies
\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{1}}-\lambda\tilde{I}\right)g\equiv 0
. Then it follows that

, for all

and

.
Since
\left|1/\lambda^{n}\right|\to+\infty
as

, and
g
is bounded, this relation implies that

. Thus, the operator is injective, completing the argument.

We now turn to the general case. The key tool in our analysis is the following proposition, which asserts that if a complex number
\lambda
belongs to the spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
, then the sequence
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right\}_{r\geq 1}
converges in modulus to
1
as

. The proof of this proposition is postponed to the end of this section.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\bar{d}=\left(d_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive integers, and let
\bar{p}=\left(p_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
be a sequence of probabilities satisfying
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>0
. Then, for any
\lambda\in\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
, the following limit holds:

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;=\;1.

(5.12)

Proof of (
4.1
) (Case
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>0
).

Assume that the sequence
\bar{d}
is bounded, and that the infinite product
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}
is strictly positive.

Our objective is to show that the spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
coincides with the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.
Once this equality is established, it follows from the inclusion (
2.1
), together with the boundary relations (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), that the spectrum is equal to the approximate point spectrum.

To this end, it suffices to show that

\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;\lambda\in inter\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right).

(5.13)

Indeed, relations (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) imply that
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\subseteq\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
, while Proposition
5.6
guarantees that
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|=1
for all
\lambda\in\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
. Therefore, relation (
5.13
) implies that none of the points in the spectrum
\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)
lie in the interior of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

To prove (
5.13
), we begin by establishing the following key fact about the asymptotic behavior of the sequence
\left(\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right)_{r\geq 1}
:

\text{if }\;\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;\neq\;1,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{then }\;\lim_{r\to+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\;=\;0.

(5.14)

Fix
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
such that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|<\rho_{0}\;<1
for infinitely many indices
r\geq 1
, for some
0\;<\rho_{0}\;<1
. We claim that there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}+1
, one has
\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\leq\rho_{0}^{3/2}
.
To justify this, observe that for any
\rho_{0}\in\left(0,1\right)
, there exists
\delta_{0}\;>0
such that for all
0\;<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, one has

\left|{\lambda-\delta\over 1-\delta}\right|^{d}\;<\;\rho_{0}^{3\over 2}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }d\geq 2,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{whenever }\;\left|\lambda\right|\;\leq\;\rho_{0}.

(5.15)

Since
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>0
, we have
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}p_{r}=1
. Thus, there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
large enough so that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\leq\rho_{0}
and

for all
r\geq r_{0}
.
It then follows by induction that

\left|\tilde{f}_{r+1}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;=\;\left|{\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)-\left(1-p_{r}\right)\over p_{r}}\right|^{d_{r+1}}\;\underset{\eqref{Eq5_Prop5_Transient Case Contraction Argument}}{\leq}\rho_{0}^{3/2}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;r\geq r_{0}+1.

(5.16)

Next, we prove by induction that for every
j\geq 1
, there exists
r_{j}\geq 1
such that

\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;<\;\rho_{0}^{{\left(3/2\right)}^{j+1}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all }r\geq r_{j}+1.

(5.17)

The base case
j=0
follows directly from (
5.16
). Assume the inequality (
5.17
) holds for some
j\geq 0
, and set
\rho_{j}=\rho_{0}^{\left(3/2\right)^{j+1}}
.
Choose
\delta_{j+1}\;>0
such that (
5.16
) holds with
\rho_{0}
replaced by
\rho_{j}
. Since
p_{r}\to 1
, there exists
r_{j+1}\geq 1
large enough such that
1-p_{r}\leq\delta_{j+1}
for all
r\geq r_{j+1}
. Applying the same argument as above yields the inequality for
j+1
, completing the induction.

This proves that
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\to 0
as
r\to+\infty
, thereby establishing (
5.14
).

We now show that for any open and connected subset
W\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, one has the dichotomy

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\;=\;0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\lambda\in W\hskip 9.24994pt\text{or}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;=\;1\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\lambda\in W.

(5.18)

Indeed, the sequence of maps
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:W\to\mathbb{C}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is uniformly bounded on
W
, and thus, by Montel’s Theorem
[
20
, Chapter 3.2, Theorem 3.3, p. 225]
, it is a normal family.
Consequently, there exists a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets of
W
to a bounded analytic function
\tilde{f}:W\to\mathbb{C}
.
By relation (
5.14
), the pointwise limit must satisfy
\tilde{f}\left(W\right)\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{1}\cup\left\{0\right\}
.
Since the image of a connected set under a continuous function is connected, we conclude that
\tilde{f}\left(W\right)=\left\{0\right\}
or
\tilde{f}\left(W\right)\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Applying again relation (
5.14
) completes the proof of (
5.18
).

To establish relation (
5.13
), it remains to show that for each open and connected subset
W\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
,

\text{there exists }\;\lambda_{w}\in W\;\text{ such that }\;\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)=0.

(5.19)

Combined with the dichotomy in (
5.18
), this implies that
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\to 0
for all
\lambda\in W
, hence proving (
5.13
).

Let
W\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
be the interior of a connected component of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. Set
\rho_{0}=3/4
and choose
\delta_{0}\leq 1/4
such that inequality (
5.15
) holds for all
0\;<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\left|\lambda\right|\leq 3/4
.
Choose
r_{0}\geq 1
so that
1-p_{r}\leq\delta_{0}
for every
r\geq r_{0}
.

To prove (
5.19
), it suffices to show that

\text{there exists }\;r_{w}\geq r_{0}\text{ and }\lambda_{w}\in W\text{ such that }\;\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\in\left[0,1\right).

(5.20)

Indeed, if
0\leq\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\leq 3/4
, then inequality (
5.15
) implies that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\right|\leq 3/4
for all
r\geq r_{w}
.
If instead
3/4\;<\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\;<1
, then a direct calculation shows that

0\;<\;\tilde{f}_{r_{w}+1}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\;=\;\left({\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)-\left(1-p_{r_{w}}\right)\over p_{r_{w}}}\right)^{d_{r_{w}+1}}\;\leq\;\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)^{d_{r_{w}+1}}.

Repeated application of this inequality yields a sequence
\left(\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\right)_{r\geq r_{w}}
of positive real numbers that eventually falls below
3/4
, say at some index
r^{\prime}_{w}\geq r_{w}
, thus reducing the situation to the previous case. Therefore,
\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\right|\leq 3/4
for all
r\geq r_{w}^{\prime}
.
This shows that
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\right|\neq 1
, and by (
5.14
), one concludes that
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)=0
, as desired.
Hence, (
5.20
) implies (
5.19
), completing the argument.

Thus, to complete the proof of (
5.13
), it remains to establish relation (
5.20
). Fix
W
to be the interior of a connected component of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. Since
\partial W\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}}
, one may, without loss of generality, choose
r_{0}\geq 1
sufficiently large such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
the image
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(W\right)
is an open set and satisfies

1\in\partial\tilde{f}_{r}\left(W\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;r\geq r_{0}-1.

(5.21)

For each
r\geq r_{0}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{1-p_{r}\right\}

{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)
and

\theta

2\pi

\theta_{r}\left(1-p_{r}\right)
and
\theta_{r}^{\prime}\left(1-p_{r}\right)

\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}

\theta_{r}\left(0\right)=\theta_{r}^{\prime}\left(0\right)=0

Note that both
\theta_{r}

\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{1-p_{r}\right\}

Our goal is to show the existence of
r_{w}\geq r_{0}
and
\lambda_{w}\in W
such that
\theta_{r_{w}+1}\left(\tilde{f}_{r_{w}}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\right)=0\pmod{2\pi}

\tilde{f}_{r_{w}+1}\left(\lambda_{w}\right)\in\left[0,1\right)

of

The proof is complete.

Remark 5.2
.

It is known that if the sequence
\bar{d}
of integers is constant and
p_{r}\to 1
as
r\to+\infty
, with
p_{r}\geq 2\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)
for all
r\geq 1
, then the fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
is connected
[
19
, Proposition 4.9]
. This result appears to remain valid for any bounded sequence
\bar{d}
, provided that the terms of
\bar{p}
are sufficiently large.

In such cases, a slightly simpler proof of the equality in (
4.1
), under the condition
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}>0
, becomes available. Indeed, by
[
19
, Proposition 4.9]
, one can choose a sufficiently large index
r_{0}\geq 1
such that the image
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
is connected.

Observe that
\lambda\in\operatorname{inter}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
if and only if
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\in\operatorname{inter}\left(\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)\right)
. Then, in view of relation (
5.14
), define
W_{0}
as the set of points
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\in\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
whose orbits
\left(\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)\right)_{r\geq r_{0}}
tend to zero.

By applying Lemma
5.4
, it follows that
\partial W_{0}\subset\partial\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
. Due to the connectivity of
\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
, we conclude that
W_{0}=\operatorname{inter}\left(\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)\right)
, thereby completing the proof.

To complete the proof of relation (
4.1
) for the case
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>0
, it remains to prove Proposition
5.6
. To this end, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\left(X,\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|\right)
be a Banach space and
S:X\to X
a continuous linear operator. Assume that there exist constants
0<c<1<C
such that, for every point
q\in X
there exists a point
g\in X
such that

\left|\left|q-Sg\right|\right|\;\leq\;c\cdot\left|\left|q\right|\right|\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\left|\left|g\right|\right|\;\leq\;C\left|\left|q\right|\right|.

(5.25)

Then, the operator
S
is surjective.

Proof.

Fix
q_{0}\in X
and define the vectors
q_{j}
inductively by the recurrence relation
q_{j}=q_{j-1}-Sg_{j-1}
for
j\geq 1
, where
g_{j-1}\in X
is the vector provided by assumption (
5.25
) for
q=q_{j-1}
.
Since
X
is a Banach space and
\left|\left|g_{j}\right|\right|\leq C\left|\left|q_{j}\right|\right|
, the series
g:=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}g_{j}
converges in
X
and satisfies

\left|\left|g\right|\right|\;\leq\;C\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\left|\left|q_{j}\right|\right|\;\leq\;C\left|\left|q_{0}\right|\right|\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}c^{j}\;\leq\;{C\over 1-c}\left|\left|q_{0}\right|\right|.

Finally, since
q_{j}=q_{0}-S\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}g_{i}\right)
for all
j\geq 1
, taking the limit as
j\to+\infty
, one obtains
Sg=q_{0}
. The choice of
q_{0}\in X
was arbitrary, so the proof of the lemma is complete.

∎

Proof of Proposition
5.6
.

Recall that for each
r\geq 1
, we define the shifted sequences
\bar{d}_{r}=\left(d_{r-1+j}\right)_{j\geq 1}
and
\bar{p}_{r}=\left(p_{r-1+j}\right)_{j\geq 1}

5.7

Assume that
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}\;>1/2
, and set

C:=\;\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}^{-1}\;<\;2.

To prove (
5.12
), we treat two separate cases.

Fix
\lambda\in\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)

5.2

3.3

g_{\lambda}\left(x\right):=\;\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(x\right)}

\lambda
, and hence

\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\;=\;\lim_{r\to\infty}g_{\lambda}\left(q_{r-1}\right)\;=\;g_{\lambda}\left(0\right)\;=\;1.

\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)=\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{d_{r}}

\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)=1

Fix
\lambda\in\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)\backslash\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\right)\right)

\tilde{f}_{r-1}\left(\lambda\right)\in\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d}_{r},\bar{p}_{r}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}_{r}}}\right)\right)

\tilde{f}_{r-1}\left(\lambda\right)\in\sigma\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d}_{r},\bar{p}_{r}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}_{r}}}\right)\right)\backslash\sigma_{pt}\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d}_{r},\bar{p}_{r}},\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}_{r}}}\right)\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;r\geq 1.

(5.26)

Indeed, if this fails for some
r
, we reduce to Case 1, since
\tilde{f}_{r-1}\left(\lambda\right)
will be an eigenvalue of the operator
\tilde{S}_{\bar{d}_{r},\bar{p}_{r}}
acting on
\mathcal{C}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}_{r}}}\right)
.

\left(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}-\lambda\tilde{I}\right)

The proof of the proposition is complete.

6
Open Problems

In this section, we discuss some open problems that naturally emerge from our work.

Problem 1
.

Can the assumption that the sequence
\bar{d}
is bounded be removed from the statement of Theorem
4.1
?

Our proof of relation (
4.1
) in the case where
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}=0
does not require the sequence
\bar{d}
to be bounded.
However, the proof of the same relation in the case
\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}p_{r}>0
relies on the fact that the set
\bigcup_{r=1}^{+\infty}\tilde{f}_{r}^{-1}\left\{1\right\}
is dense in the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.
This density is insured by Lemma
5.4
(2), but only under the assumption that
\bar{d}
is bounded. Therefore, extending the result to unbounded sequences
\bar{d}
would require a version of Lemma
5.4
(2) that holds for all unbounded sequences
\bar{d}
.

Another question concerns the residual spectrum
\sigma_{r}\big(\tilde{S}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},\mathcal{C}\big(\overline{\Gamma_{\bar{d}}}\big)\big)
. In
[
19
, Proposition 4.3]
, the authors show that the residual spectrum of the operator
S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
acting on a space
X
—where
X
is either the space of
\alpha
-summable complex sequences
l^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
for
\alpha>1
, the space
c\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
of convergent sequences, or the space
c_{0}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
of sequences converging to zero—is empty.

.

Given a bounded sequence of integers
\bar{d}
and a sequence of probabilities
\bar{p}
, is the residual spectrum

empty?

\left|z\right|\;>1

\left|h_{r}\left(z\right)\right|\;>1
, where the map
h_{r}
is defined in (
5.5
).

Since
\tilde{f}_{r}=h_{r}^{d_{r}}
, for each
r\geq 1
, one can use induction to show that if
\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;>1
for some
r_{0}\geq 1
, then

\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}+k}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;\geq\;\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|^{d_{r_{0}+1}\cdot d_{r_{0}+2}\cdots d_{r_{0}+k}},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }k\geq 1.

Under the assumption
\left|\tilde{f}_{r_{0}}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\;>1
, this inequality implies that the sequence
\left(\tilde{f}_{r}\right)_{r\geq 1}
diverges to infinity.

It remains to verify that
\left|h_{r}\left(z\right)\right|\geq\left|z\right|
whenever
\left|z\right|\;>1
.
Fix
r\geq 1
and let
z=\left(1+\eta\right)+yi
, where
\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
\eta^{2}+2\eta+y^{2}\;>0
, so that
\left|z\right|\;>1
. Then,

h_{r}\left(z\right)=\left(1+{\eta\over p_{r}}\right)+{y\over p_{r}}i.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|h_{r}\left(z\right)\right|^{2}\;=\;1+{\eta^{2}\over p_{r}^{2}}+2\cdot{\eta\over p_{r}}+{y^{2}\over p_{r}^{2}}\;\geq\;1+\eta^{2}+2\cdot\eta+y^{2}\;=\;\left|z\right|^{2},

where the inequality follows from the choice of
p_{r}\in\left(0,1\right]
. This proves equivalence (
5.6
).

We next establish the equivalent characterizations of the fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. As for the identity
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\tilde{f}_{r}(\lambda)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}
, it follows directly from the definition of the fibered filled Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
together with the equivalence established in (
5.6
).

Furthermore, the equality

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\tilde{f}_{r-1}\left(\lambda\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}

is immediate upon observing that, by construction,
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(\lambda\right)=\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)^{d_{r}}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
r\geq 1
.

It remains to verify the final equality:

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}\;=\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left(v_{\lambda}\left(n\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{bounded}\right\}.

To this end, we prove the inclusion

\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left(v_{\lambda}\left(n\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\;\text{ bounded }\right\}\;\subseteq\;\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\;\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1,\;\text{for every }r\geq 1\right\}

since the inverse inclusion follows directly from the definition of the sequence
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
.
Suppose for contradiction that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is such that the sequence
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
is bounded, but there exists
r_{0}\geq 1
for which
\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r_{0}\right)\right|\;>1
. By equivalence (
5.6
), it then follows that

\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\geq\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r_{0}\right)\right|\;>1
for all
r\geq r_{0}
.
Now define the sequence
\left(m_{j}\right)_{j\geq 1}
by

m_{j}:=\;\sum_{l=1}^{j}q_{r_{0}+l},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\;j\geq 1,

so that each
m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Using the recurrence structure of
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
, one finds that the values
\left|v_{\lambda}\left(m_{j}\right)\right|
grow exponentially in
j
, which contradicts the assumption that
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
is bounded.

Hence, it must be
\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1
for all
r\geq 1
, completing the proof of the desired inclusion.

Finally, we demonstrate the identity

\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\;=\;\sigma_{pt}\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)\right),

and, in particular, that a vector
\mathbf{w}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
is an eigenvector of
S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
if and only if
\mathbf{w}=c\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

We begin by establishing the inclusion
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\subseteq\sigma_{pt}\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)\right)
. To this end, we verify that for each
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, the vector
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
satisfies the eigenvalue equation
S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}=\lambda\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
. Indeed, as shown previously, the assumption
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
implies that
\left|\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right|\leq 1
for every
r\geq 1
, and thus
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
.
Fix any

. Then one computes

\displaystyle\left(S\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}\right)_{n}\;

\displaystyle=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)v_{\lambda}\left(n\right)+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s_{n}}p_{r}\right)v_{\lambda}\left(n+1\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum_{s=1}^{s_{n}-1}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}\right)\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)v_{\lambda}\left(n-\sum_{r=1}^{s}\left(d_{r}-1\right)q_{r-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\left(1-p_{1}\right)\cdot\prod_{r=1}^{+\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(n\right)}+\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s_{n}}p_{r}\right)\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(s_{n}\right)^{a_{s_{n}}\left(n\right)+1}\right)\prod_{r=s_{n}+1}^{+\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}(n)}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum_{s=1}^{s_{n}-1}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}p_{r}\right)\left(1-p_{s+1}\right)\prod_{r=s+1}^{+\infty}\left(\iota_{\lambda}\left(r\right)\right)^{a_{r}\left(n\right)}

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{Eq5_Theor_Recurrent Case Inductive Identity}}{=}\;\lambda\cdot v_{\lambda}\left(n\right),

confirming the eigenrelation.

To prove the inverse inclusion
\sigma_{pt}\left(S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}},l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)\right)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
we appeal to Lemma
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
, which asserts that a vector
\mathbf{w}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
is an eigenvector associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if there exists
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathbf{w}=c\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}
. The assumption
\mathbf{w}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
then implies
\mathbf{v}_{\lambda}\in l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
, and by the previous characterizations of the fibered Julia set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, it follows that
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

We conclude that every eigenvalue of
S_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
acting on
l^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right)
lies in
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
.

The proof of the lemma is complete.

\left(\tilde{f}_{r}\left(u\right)\right)_{r\geq 1}
is bounded and
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(v\right)\right|=\infty
. Thus, the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is not normal at
z
.

In the opposite direction, assume that
z\not\in\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. If
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, then, since
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
is compact, there exists a neighborhood
U_{z}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
of
z
. Then, by Lemma
5.2
,
\underset{r\to+\infty}{\lim}\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(v\right)\right|=\infty
for all
v\in U_{z}
.
Thus, the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is normal on
z
. If
z\in inter\left(E_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}\right)
, then there exists an open neighborhood
U_{z}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
of
z
such that, for every
v\in U_{z}
, one has that
\left|\tilde{f}_{r}\left(v\right)\right|\leq 1
for
r\geq 1
.
Therefore, the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is locally uniformly bounded in
U_{z}
, and hence normal at
z
by Montel’s Theorem
[
20
, Chapter 3.2, Theorem 3.3, p. 225]
.

Proof of Point (2).

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open set intersecting
\partial\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
, and fix
z_{0}\in U\cap\mathcal{E}_{\bar{d},\bar{p}}
. By Point (1), the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is not normal at
z_{0}
. Then, by Montel’s Normality Theorem
[
15
, Theorem 9.15, p. 340]
, the union
\bigcup_{r\geq 1}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(U\right)
is equal to
\mathbb{C}\backslash X
, where
X
is either empty or consists of a single point.

By assumption, the sequence of integers
\bar{d}
is bounded, therefore, there exists a subsequence of positive integers
\left(r_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{+\infty}
such that the degree of all polynomials
f_{r_{k}}
equals
deg\left(f_{r_{k}}\right)=d
for
k\geq 1
, where
d\geq 2
is a positive integer. By Lemma
5.2
and, in particular, relation (
5.6
), the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r}:\;r\geq 1\right\}
is normal on a point
z\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if the family
\left\{\tilde{f}_{r_{k}}:\;k\geq 1\right\}
is normal on
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Equivalently, the latter family is normal on
z\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if the family
\left\{h_{r_{k}}\circ\tilde{f}_{r_{k}-1}:\;k\geq 1\right\}
is normal on
z\in\mathbb{C}
, since
\tilde{f}_{r_{k}}=\left(h_{r_{k}}\circ\tilde{f}_{r_{k}-1}\right)^{d}
, where the maps
h_{r_{k}}:\mathbb{C}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
for
k\geq 1
were defined in (
5.5
).
Fix
w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and write its polar decomposition as
w=\rho_{w}e^{2\pi i\theta_{w}}
, where
\rho_{w}\;>0
and
\theta_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
. For each
1\leq j\leq d
, define
w_{j}=\rho_{w}^{1/d}\cdot e^{2\pi i\cdot\left({\theta_{w}\over d}+{j\over d}\right)}
. Then clearly
w=w_{j}^{d}
for every
j
, so the values
w_{j}
are the
d
-th roots of
w
. Since
\tilde{f}_{r_{k}}=\left(h_{r_{k}}\circ\tilde{f}_{r_{k}-1}\right)^{d}
, one has
w\in\tilde{f}_{r_{k}}\left(U\right)
as long as one
w_{j}\in h_{r_{k}}\circ\tilde{f}_{r_{k}-1}\left(U\right)
.

Since
w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
was arbitrary, we conclude that if the images
\tilde{f}_{r}\left(U\right)
omit a point, it must be the point
0
. Thus,
\bigcup_{r\geq 1}\tilde{f}_{r}\left(U\right)\supseteq\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
, completing the proof of Point (2).

\square

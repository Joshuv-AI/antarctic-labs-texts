Abstract
The NuMI facility at Fermilab produces a high-intensity beam of muon neutrinos and antineutrinos, designed to study neutrino oscillations. This beam may also be a source of dark matter particles produced through a light mediator. We search for dark matter particles with masses between 1 and 200 MeV that interact with Standard Model particles via a vector portal, producing forward-scattered single-electron events in the NOvA near detector. We set limits on the dark–visible coupling based on an exposure of 2.55 × 10 21 2.55\times 10^{21} protons of 120 GeV energy on the NuMI target. For the dark matter mass range 10–20 MeV this analysis sets the tightest constraints on the coupling to date.



Gravitational observations provide ample evidence for th eexisting of dark matter (DM)
Rubin et al. 1980
;
Clowe et al. 2006
;
Massey et al. 2007
;
Paraficz, D. et al. 2016
;
Aghanim et al. 2020
, but the particle nature of DM or its possible nongravitational interactions have not been determined.
Thermal relic particles are a compelling explanation for DM, and these models allow the mass of DM candidates to be in the sub-MeV to 100 TeV range
Steigman et al. 1977
.
Searches for weakly interactive massive particles (WIMPs) in the upper mass range of thermal relics have not yet found a positive signature and place continually tightening constraints
Meng et al. 2021
;
Agnes et al. 2023
;
Aalbers et al. 2023
;
Aprile et al. 2025
.
Furthermore, cosmological observations suggest that sub-GeV DM cannot interact with Standard Model (SM) particles via the weak interaction
Lee and Weinberg 1977
.
However, a new vector boson, the dark photon (
A^{\prime}
), could mediate interactions between DM and particles of the SM
Fayet 2004
;
Pospelov et al. 2008
;
Pospelov 2009
.
A minimal benchmark model consists of a scalar DM particle,
\chi
, which couples to the
A^{\prime}
with coupling strength
g_{D}
, with masses
m_{\chi}
and
m_{A^{\prime}}
, respectively
Izaguirre et al. 2015
;
deNiverville and Frugiuele 2019
.
The
A^{\prime}
kinetically mixes with the SM photon with a mixing strength
\varepsilon

Holdom 1986
, providing a mechanism for interaction with SM particles.
If
m_{A^{\prime}}>2m_{\chi}
and
g_{D}\gg e\varepsilon
, where
e
is the electric charge, the
A^{\prime}
will predominantly decay via
A^{\prime}\rightarrow\chi\chi
.
In a thermal freeze-out scenario, the comoving annihilation cross section for the scalar DM candidate, which can then be related to the relic DM abundance, can be approximated as

\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\sim\frac{2e^{2}v^{2}Y}{3m_{\chi}^{2}},

(1)

where
v
is the relative DM velocity.
The dimensionless parameter,
Y
, characterizes the interaction strength between DM and SM particles and is defined as

Y=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha_{D}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{m_{A^{\prime}}}\right)^{4},

(2)

with
\alpha_{D}=g_{D}^{2}/4\pi

Izaguirre et al. 2015
.

Fixed-target neutrino facilities, such as the Neutrinos at the Main Injector (NuMI) beam
Adamson et al. 2016
that provides neutrinos to the NOvA experiment, can produce the candidate MeV-scale DM due to their large integrated exposures
deNiverville et al. 2011
.
The primary channels for
A^{\prime}
—and consequently DM—production in the NuMI target are
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma+A^{\prime}
,
\eta^{0}\rightarrow\gamma+A^{\prime}
, and
p+N\rightarrow p+N+A^{\prime}
.
If DM is produced, some fraction of this DM will enter the NOvA near detector and elastically scatter on a nucleus or electron.
In neutrino scattering, the electron channel is highly suppressed compared to the nucleus channel
Formaggio and Zeller 2012
, but this suppression is not as strong for DM scattering—especially for small
m_{A^{\prime}}

deNiverville and Frugiuele 2019
;
Aguilar-Arevalo et al. 2017
—leading to relatively smaller backgrounds in an electron-channel search compared to a nucleus-channel search.
Additionally, the signal signature of
\chi\text{--}e
in the NOvA detector is clean and well understood: a single, forward-going electron shower. Consequently, this analysis will only search for DM–electron scattering,
\chi\text{--}e
.
An irreducible background from neutrino-electron elastic scattering
Tomalak and Hill 2020
(
\nu\text{--}e
) and a reducible background from neutrino-nucleus interactions (
\nu\text{--}N
) are expected.
However, the kinematics of
\chi\text{--}e
are different enough from neutrino–electron scattering (due to the larger mass of the incoming particle) to distinguish between the two processes.

This Letter presents the first search by NOvA for sub-GeV DM produced in the NuMI beam and scattered via interactions with electrons in the NOvA near detector.
We search for the presence of DM interactions by looking for an excess of single-electron shower events kinematically consistent with an expected
\chi\text{--}e
signal.
This search uses
2.55\times 10^{21}
protons on target (POT) of data recorded by the NOvA near detector with the NuMI beam running in neutrino mode.
Vector portal DM was chosen as the benchmark model
deNiverville and Frugiuele 2019
with the assumption
m_{A^{\prime}}=3m_{\chi}
and
\alpha_{D}=0.5
for comparison with other experimental limits
Auerbach et al. 2001
;
Batell et al. 2014
;
Lees et al. 2017
;
Aguilar-Arevalo et al. 2017
;
Andreev et al. 2023
;
Akimov et al. 2023
and parameter expectations from cosmic observations
Berlin et al. 2019
.
A raster scan
Lyons 2014
was performed over DM masses ranging from 1 to 200 MeV and the limits to a DM signal were determined using a profile likelihood fit considering the neutrino background and systematic uncertainties.

NOvA primarily operates as a long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiment
Acero et al. 2022
.
It consists of two functionally identical detectors placed 14.6 mrad off-axis from the neutrino beam: a near detector (ND) and a far detector.
This Letter focuses on the ND, which is located 100 m underground at Fermilab in Batavia, IL, approximately 1 km from the neutrino production target.

The NOvA ND is a segmented tracking calorimeter constructed from PVC cells filled with liquid scintillator.
Each cell is 3.9 cm wide, 6.6 cm deep, and 3.9 m long.
The fully active volume is 12.8 m deep, consisting of 192 planes with 96 cells each, with alternating vertical and horizontal orientation of the planes for three-dimensional position reconstruction.
The downstream end of the detector is outfitted with a “muon catcher,” consisting of additional planes of PVC cells separated by steel absorbers, which is not used in this analysis.
The liquid scintillator is a blend of 95% mineral oil and 5% pseudocumene with trace concentrations of wavelength-shifting fluors
Mufson et al. 2015
.
Each PVC cell has a wavelength-shifting fiber to collect light and deliver it to an avalanche photodiode, which allows nanosecond-level timing resolution to reduce pile-up.
The NOvA detectors are optimized for electromagnetic shower measurements, with a radiation length of 6 planes (40 cm).

NOvA observes neutrinos from the NuMI beam at Fermilab
Adamson et al. 2016
.
If DM particles interact through the vector portal, the NuMI beam would also be a source of DM
deNiverville and Frugiuele 2019
;
deNiverville et al. 2011
;
Batell et al. 2009
;
Dobrescu and Frugiuele 2015
;
Coloma et al. 2016
.
The beam of 120 GeV protons from Fermilab’s Main Injector is directed onto a graphite target,
where interactions of beam protons with the target produce bremsstrahlung photons and mesons—mostly pions and kaons.
Charged mesons are focused toward the beam axis by two magnetic horns, which then decay primarily into muons and muon neutrinos during their flight through a decay tunnel.
A hadron absorber downstream of the decay tunnel removes the remaining protons and mesons from the beam, and an earth shield absorbs muons produced in meson decays, leaving neutrinos to continue through to the NOvA ND.
The direction of current in the magnetic horns selects meson charge sign, for a predominantly neutrino or antineutrino beam.
Proton bremsstrahlung and the decay in flight of neutral mesons produced in the target, both of which are not focused by the magnetic horns, are included as sources of DM for this analysis.

DM–electron scattering is a two-body elastic collision.
The scattering angle
\theta_{e}
of the outgoing electron with respect to the incoming DM is

\cos\theta_{e}=\beta^{-1}\left[1-\frac{m_{e}(1-E_{e}/E_{\chi})}{E_{e}}\right],

(3)

where
\beta\equiv\sqrt{1-(m_{\chi}/E_{\chi})^{2}}
,
E_{\chi}
is the incoming DM energy,
m_{e}
is the electron mass, and
E_{e}
is the kinetic energy of the scattered electron
Horoho 2025
.
For incoming particle energies of several GeV and in the small-angle approximation, the outgoing electron is very forward and
E_{e}\theta_{e}^{2}
, hereinafter expressed as
E\theta^{2}
, is small.
As
\beta
depends on
m_{\chi}
, the
E\theta^{2}
distribution is mass-dependent, and larger
m_{\chi}
will shift the distribution toward higher
E\theta^{2}
.
Neutrino-electron elastic scattering has the same two-body kinematics as DM-electron scattering, with the incoming neutrino energy
E_{\nu}
replacing
E_{\chi}
, and the neutrino mass
m_{\nu}
replacing
m_{\chi}
.
Since
m_{\nu}\ll m_{\chi}
,
\beta\rightarrow 1
and Eq. (
3
) takes on the familiar form for
\nu\text{--}e
scattering
Marshall et al. 2020
, with
E\theta^{2}
being even more forward for
\nu\text{--}e
scattering than for
\chi\text{--}e
scattering.
Neutrino-nucleus interactions can also produce forward-going showers, but these backgrounds typically have other final-state particles and can be mitigated with event selection.
After event selection, an approximately flat background in
E\theta^{2}
due to neutrino-nucleus interactions is expected.
These three different shapes in
E\theta^{2}
make it a powerful variable variable for constraining possible
\chi\text{--}e
scattering.

DM production in the NuMI target and scattering in the NOvA ND was simulated using BdNMC
deNiverville et al. 2017
, a program for simulating dark matter production in fixed-target experiments, which has been previously used in experimental analyses by other Fermilab neutrino experiments
Aguilar-Arevalo et al. 2017
;
Abratenko et al. 2024
and experiments elsewhere
Andriamirado et al. 2021
;
Aguilar-Arevalo et al. 2022
;
Akimov et al. 2023
.
BdNMC simulates
A^{\prime}
production in the target, the
A^{\prime}
decay to DM particles, and DM scattering on electrons in the NOvA ND.
We expect to have the highest sensitivity at low masses where
\pi^{0}
decay predominantly produces DM.
The
\pi^{0}
production model in BdNMC has been validated on data from a 400 GeV proton beam on a beryllium target
Bonesini et al. 2001
, whereas the NuMI beam uses a 120 GeV proton beam on a carbon target.
The
\pi^{0}
flux in our model was tuned using measured
\pi^{\pm}
flux by MIPP
Paley et al. 2014
, averaging the
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
flux on the basis of isospin symmetry
Lipkin and Peshkin 1972
;
Jaeger et al. 1975
.
The net effect of this tuning was an approximately 25% reduction in our expected DM flux compared to the prediction of BdNMC with its default parameters
Horoho 2025
.
Uncertainties on the pion flux measured by MIPP are incorporated as an uncertainty in our DM flux, discussed below.
The
\eta^{0}
production rate was set to be
1/30
the
\pi^{0}
production rate, a conservative choice
Agakishiev et al. 2013
;
Åkesson et al. 1986
.
Neutrino-induced background events were simulated using a custom configuration of
GENIE

Andreopoulos et al. 2010
;
Andreopoulos et al. 2015
tuned to external and NOvA data.
The detector response to DM and neutrino interactions in the NOvA ND was simulated using
Geant
v4.10.2
Agostinelli et al. 2003
;
Allison et al. 2006
;
Allison et al. 2016
.

Candidate interactions are reconstructed by first grouping active detector cells (“hits”) nearby in space and time into an “event” using a clustering algorithm
Rodriguez and Laio 2014
;
Prim 1957
.
An interaction vertex is reconstructed in each event by finding the point minimizing the angular spread of hits relative to the candidate vertex
Baird et al. 2015
.
Then, using a fuzzy k-means algorithm
Dunn 1973
, hits within the event are clustered into groups, forming particle trajectories (“prongs”) that emanate from the reconstructed interaction vertex and represent final-state particles and electromagnetic cascades.
Energy is reconstructed calorimetrically and the angle
\theta
is the prong’s direction with respect to the NuMI beam direction.
Our electron shower energy resolution is 6% and our angular resolution is 27 mrad.

Event selections were applied to identify single-electron shower events and finalized before unblinding the dataset.
The event selection was originally developed for selecting
\nu\text{--}e
events in the ND.
The differences in
\nu_{\mu}
charged-current (CC) and
\nu_{e}
CC event topology were exploited to remove obvious
\nu_{\mu}
CC events.
All prongs were required to be fully contained in a volume 20 cm from the top, bottom, and sides of the ND, 105 cm from the front face, and 225 cm from the rear face excluding the muon catcher.
Similar containment requirements were made on the reconstructed vertex.
The tighter constraints on the front and rear faces are to suppress events induced by neutrino interactions in the rock surrounding the detector and to ensure full prong containment, respectively.
To mitigate background with multiple final-state particles,
E_{\text{prong}}/E_{\text{tot}}>0.8
was required, where
E_{\text{prong}}
is the energy of the most energetic prong and
E_{\text{tot}}
is the total energy in the event.
In addition,
0.5<E_{\text{prong}}<5
GeV was required to remove low-energy showers and reduce high-energy intrinsic beam
\nu_{e}
CC backgrounds.
Two event classifiers based on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were used to identify single-electron shower events.
The first identifies electromagnetic showers, and the second distinguishes electron showers from background with
\pi^{0}
in the final state.
The architecture for these CNNs is the same one used for the NOvA Convolutional Visual Network
Psihas et al. 2019
.
Both classifiers in this analysis were trained on single-electron samples with a broad range of energies and angles as the signal, which minimizes biases for selecting specifically
\chi\text{--}e
or
\nu\text{--}e
events.

The impact of systematic uncertainties were determined by varying model parameters in the simulation.
Systematic uncertainties from the neutrino and DM fluxes, detector model simulation and calibrations, the possible dependence of reconstruction efficiency on NuMI beam intensity, and neutrino interaction cross-section modeling were included in this analysis.

1
summarizes the effect of each source of uncertainty on the backgrounds and signal.
None of our studied systematic uncertainties induce a shape effect on the background in
E\theta^{2}
that could mimic a false signal.
Details on the determination of most of these systematic uncertainties can be found in
Acero et al. 2025a
.
As the dominant source of DM production—especially for low mass DM—is via
\pi^{0}
decay, our DM flux uncertainty was derived from the average uncertainty of pion production reported by MIPP
Paley et al. 2014
and was taken to be independent of neutrino flux uncertainties.
MIPP reports a
\sim
5% systematic uncertainty and a
\sim
1% statistical uncertainty on the
\pi^{\pm}
flux for pions with momenta that produce over 90% of our simulated DM, which we approximate as a 6% uncertainty on the DM flux.
Intensity modeling uncertainties were derived from studying the differences in reconstruction and selection efficiencies of single-electron showers with and without the presence of other neutrino interactions in the detector.
Single-electron showers in the presence of other interactions in the detector were reconstructed and selected 4% less efficiently than those without other interactions present, and the effect has no strong dependence on
E\theta^{2}
.
This uncertainty is applied to
\chi\text{--}e
and
\nu\text{--}e
events, as they were simulated without the presence of other interactions in the detector.
Neutrino flux uncertainties are the largest uncertainty on the
\nu\text{--}e
background, which is the dominant background for small
E\theta^{2}
, and account for an 8.2% uncertainty in the normalization.
For large
E\theta^{2}
, where
\nu\text{--}N
interactions dominate, interaction cross-section modeling uncertainties are dominant.
The largest sources of uncertainty come from the modeling of CC quasielastic and meson exchange current events
Nieves et al. 2004
;
Gran et al. 2013

We searched for a DM signal in
(m_{\chi},Y)
space by performing a raster scan
Lyons 2014
, in which we held the DM mass constant and fit for
Y
, scanning a range of DM masses
m_{\chi}
from 1 to 200 MeV.
At each DM mass, a Poisson negative log-likelihood (
-2\ln\cal{L}
) fit to data was performed, which was binned in
E\theta^{2}
from 0 to 0.048 GeV rad
2
.
The best-fit parameters minimize
-2\ln\cal{L}
.
The DM event normalization was restricted to non-negative values in the fit.
Systematic uncertainties were implemented in the fit as nuisance parameters, with penalty terms assigned equal to the square of the number of standard deviations by which they vary from their nominal value.
For each
m_{\chi}
in our raster scan, the 90% confidence level (CL) upper limit on
Y
from our best fit was calculated using the profile construction
Acero et al. 2025b
.

For DM masses considered here, the majority of
\chi\text{--}e
events are expected to have
E\theta^{2}<0.02\,\text{GeV\,rad}^{2}
, peaking between 0.004 and 0.008.
The DM mass affects the shape of the distribution, with the distribution for higher DM masses being shifted more toward higher
E\theta^{2}
.
The
\nu\text{--}e
events dominate the
E\theta^{2}
distribution below 0.004 GeV rad
2
, and the
\nu\text{--}N
events dominate the distribution above 0.02 GeV rad
2
.
By including these regions of low and high
E\theta^{2}
in the fit, where the
\nu\text{--}e
and
\nu\text{--}N
backgrounds are respectively dominant, we better constrain the backgrounds in our expected signal region.

From
2.55\times 10^{21}
POT of data, 4407 events were selected with
E\theta^{2}<0.048
GeV rad
2
.
The data is consistent with the SM in a neutrino-only fit, which has a goodness-of-fit
-2\text{ln}\mathcal{L}/{\rm dof}=9.289/12
, corresponding to a
p
-value of 0.401.
At all masses in the raster scan, our data weakly prefer some DM signal over a neutrino-only fit, with the best fit occurring at the lower limit of our raster scan range,
(m_{\chi},Y)=(1\text{\,MeV},1.51\times 10^{-12})
with
-2\text{ln}\mathcal{L}/{\rm dof}=7.132/11
, corresponding to 85 predicted DM events in the data.
Note that these best-fit parameters are excluded by LSND
Auerbach et al. 2001
(not plotted) and NA64
Andreev et al. 2023
at 90% CL.
The strongest likelihood ratio test statistic
-2\text{ln}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\chi+\nu}/\mathcal{L}_{\nu\text{-only}}\right)
has a
p
-value of 0.125, so the preference for the presence of DM is not statistically significant.
All systematic uncertainties in the best fit and neutrino-only fit were pulled away from their nominal values by less than
1\sigma
.

In summary, using
2.55\times 10^{21}
POT data recorded by the NOvA ND during NuMI operations with a neutrino beam, we have improved upon current constraints for DM particles produced in accelerators via the vector portal from 10 to 20 MeV.
Our exclusion is slightly weaker than our expected sensitivity due to the fit’s preference for the presence of a small amount of DM.
However, the analysis is still consistent with the SM.
The inclusion of antineutrino-beam data should better constrain the presence of a DM signal, which could be explored in future work.

Diversity vs Degrees of Freedom for Gaussian Fading Channels
Mahesh Godavarti

Abstract
The standard definitions of degrees of freedom (DOF) and diversity, the prelog C ⁡ ( ρ ) / log ⁡ ρ C(\rho)/\log\rho and the error exponent − log P e / log ρ -\log P_{e}/\log\rho , measure both quantities with a fixed ruler, log ⁡ ρ \log\rho . When this ruler is wrong, both measurements give zero or become undefined. But intuitively, DOF (the number of independent paths) and diversity (the number of independent looks at the signal) ought to be properties of the channel itself, not artifacts of a normalization choice. We seek to formalize this intuition for fading channels with additive white Gaussian noise. Our starting point is a geometric observation: for fixed- H H MIMO, DOF and diversity are both ranks of the bilinear map H ​ X HX , with different variables free. ε \varepsilon -covering the image of X ↦ H ​ X X\!\mapsto\!HX with balls of radius ε = 1 / ρ \varepsilon=1/\sqrt{\rho} gives DOF on the log ⁡ ρ \log\rho gauge; expanding a ball across all dimensions of H ↦ H ​ D H\!\mapsto\!HD gives diversity on the linear ρ \rho gauge. The individual rank facts are classical; the new point is that this unified view explains the rate-gauge/reliability-gauge mismatch: covering produces logs (the log of a count), expansion produces linear growth (a squared distance), so in every model studied here the two gauges differ and no single normalization captures both. The geometric definitions identify DOF and diversity but do not yield tradeoff curves. We bridge the gap with Bhattacharyya packing, obtaining gauge-DOF and B-diversity as workable proxies. B-diversity is an achievable exponent derived from pairwise Bhattacharyya distances, not the optimal error exponent, but it is finite and informative on every gauge, including those where the classical diversity order is zero. The Bhattacharyya approach reveals three natural gauge classes, log ⁡ ρ \log\rho , log ⁡ log ⁡ ρ \log\log\rho , and ( log ⁡ ρ ) β (\log\rho)^{\beta} for β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,1) , each determined by the channel’s fading structure. The main new result is a cross-gauge tradeoff for noncoherent fast fading: capacity lives on the log ⁡ log ⁡ ρ \log\log\rho gauge, but B-diversity lives on the log ⁡ ρ \log\rho gauge, exponentially larger. As the coding rate increases toward capacity, the B-diversity frontier interpolates between these two scales, with matching upper and lower bounds. For coherent MIMO, noncoherent block fading, and irregular-spectrum (fractional-log) channels, the same approach recovers or extends known scaling laws.



I
Introduction

Degrees of freedom (DOF) and diversity are among the most fundamental
concepts in wireless communications. DOF is the number of independent
channels available to the transmitter; diversity is the number of
independent looks at the signal available to the receiver. The
standard definitions are operational: DOF is the prelog coefficient

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}C(\rho)/\log\rho
, and diversity order is the
error-probability exponent

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}-\log P_{e}(\rho)/\log\rho

[
16
,
12
]
. Both use the
same ruler,
\log\rho
, in the denominator. When this ruler matches
the channel, these definitions are informative. When it does not,
both give zero or infinity, not because the channel lacks DOF or
diversity, but because the denominator is wrong.

The prelog trap.

The prelog
\lim C(\rho)/\log\rho
presupposes that
\log\rho
is the
correct denominator. Lapidoth and Moser
[
7
]

showed that noncoherent fast fading has

C(\rho)\sim\log\log\rho
: the prelog is zero. Lapidoth
[
8
]
showed that stationary Gaussian fading with
non-regular spectra has
C(\rho)\sim c(\log\rho)^{\beta}
for

\beta\in(0,1)
: again the prelog is zero. These channels do not
have zero degrees of freedom; they have zero prelog because

\log\rho
is the wrong ruler.

The diversity trap.

The classical diversity order

d=\lim-\log P_{e}(\rho)/\log\rho
presupposes that
P_{e}
decays as

\rho^{-d}
. For fast fading at rate
R=r\log\log\rho
, the error
probability decays as
\exp(-c(\log\rho)^{1-r})
, faster than any
polynomial yet slower than
\exp(-c\,\rho)
. The classical diversity
order evaluates to
d=0
, declaring the channel has no diversity
even though
N
receive antennas provide
N
independent looks and the
error exponent grows with
N
.

Intuition vs. classical definitions.

Intuitively, DOF is the number of independent signal paths through
the channel; diversity is the number of independent looks at the
signal available to the receiver. These are physical properties of
the channel. The classical definitions replace these physical
quantities with a specific normalization: DOF
=C(\rho)/\log\rho
,
diversity
=-\log P_{e}/\log\rho
. When the channel’s capacity or
error probability happens to scale with
\log\rho
, the two
coincide, and the classical definition recovers the physical count.
When it does not (fast fading:
C\sim\log\log\rho
; irregular
spectra:
C\sim(\log\rho)^{\beta}
), the classical definition
returns zero while the physical count remains positive. The
classical definitions are not wrong, but they are narrow: they measure
DOF and diversity
relative to
\log\rho
, and when

\log\rho
is the wrong ruler, the measurement is uninformative.

This paper’s approach.

We seek definitions of DOF and diversity that are properties of the
channel, not of a normalization choice, extending our earlier
investigation
[
2
]
. Our starting observation is
that, for fixed-
H
MIMO, DOF and diversity are both ranks of the
bilinear map
HX
, with different variables free
(Section
II
). The individual rank facts are classical
[
6
,
12
]
;
the new point is the unified geometric picture, which explains
why the cross-gauge mismatch between rate and reliability arises
in each model studied here, and motivates the framework that follows. For fading
channels, the geometric definitions do not yield tradeoff curves
directly; we bridge the gap with Bhattacharyya packing
(Section
IV
), obtaining gauge-DOF and B-diversity
as workable proxies.

Running example: SISO fast fading.

Consider the simplest case:
M=N=1
,
H\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
,

|X|\leq 1
. The output

Y=\sqrt{\rho}\,HX+Z\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\rho|x|^{2}+1)

depends on
x
only through
|x|^{2}
. The rate gauge is

\log\log\rho
(gauge-DOF
=1
). The maximum pairwise B-distance is

\Delta^{*}(2;\rho)\sim(1/2)\log\rho
, on a different, larger gauge
than capacity. At rate
r\log\log\rho
, the B-diversity frontier is

(1/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
: finite and informative, while the classical
diversity order is zero. This is the cross-gauge phenomenon in its
simplest form.

Main result preview.

For noncoherent fast fading with
N
receive antennas, at rate

r\log\log\rho
(
r\in[0,1]
), the B-diversity frontier satisfies

\Delta^{*}((\log\rho)^{r};\rho)\sim(N/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
, with
matching upper and lower bounds (Theorem
1
).

Terminology.

We use “gauge-DOF” (Definition
5
) and
“B-diversity” (Definition
7
) for the
gauge-normalized Bhattacharyya packing quantities.
B-diversity is a workable proxy, an achievable exponent derived from
pairwise Bhattacharyya distances, not the optimal error exponent.
Unqualified “DOF” and “diversity” refer to the intuitive geometric
concepts. For coherent MIMO and block fading (log gauge), B-diversity
coincides with the classical diversity order at
r=0
and
lower-bounds it for
r>0
. For channels on other gauges, the
classical diversity order is zero while B-diversity is positive.

Relation to other exponents.

B-diversity is weaker than the random-coding exponent (which uses
the full output distribution, not just pairwise distances) and
weaker than the sphere-packing exponent. It is stronger than the
cutoff-rate lower bound for the channels treated here. Its advantage
is universality across gauges: the random-coding and sphere-packing
exponents are defined relative to a fixed rate scale, while
B-diversity adapts its normalization to the channel’s gauge. For
channels on the log gauge (coherent MIMO, block fading), B-diversity
lower-bounds the DMT; for channels on other gauges, the DMT is
undefined while B-diversity remains meaningful.

The Duality

The channel output (ignoring noise) is the bilinear map
HX

DOF: fix
H
, vary
X

The image set is
\{HX:X\in\mathcal{X}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N\times T}

Since

Vectorizing confirms this:

(3)

C(\rho)\sim\mathrm{DOF}\cdot\log\rho.

(4)

The

X

The Bhattacharyya distance for inputs

.

Let

III
Main Result: The Cross-Gauge Tradeoff

III-A

Fast Fading Model

A single-antenna transmitter sends a scalar
X
per channel use;

N
receive antennas each see an independent fade:

Y_{j}=\sqrt{\rho}\,H_{j}X+Z_{j},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

(11)

where
H_{j}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
(fading) and

Z_{j}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
(noise) are all mutually independent
and drawn independently at each channel use (“fast fading”:
the coherence time is one symbol). The input satisfies

|X|\leq 1
(peak power). The index
j
runs over receive
antennas; independence across channel uses is the fast-fading
assumption.

Lemma 2

(Gauge class under peak vs. average power)
.

Under peak power (
|X|\leq 1
), the per-symbol output family is

\mathcal{P}^{(\rho)}=\{\mathcal{CN}(0,\rho s+1):s\in[0,1]\}
, with log-variance range
L=\ln(1+\rho)
.
The number of
\delta
-separated points is

N_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)=1+L/c(\delta)+O(1)
,
so
K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)=\log N_{\mathrm{pack}}=\log\log\rho+O(1)
, giving gauge class
\log\log\rho
.

Under average power (
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{2}]\leq 1
), individual symbols may
have
|x|^{2}>1
, so the per-symbol output family is strictly
larger. Nevertheless, the gauge class is still
\log\log\rho
:
the capacity
C(\rho)\sim\log\log\rho

of
[
7
]
(proved under average power)
shows the gauge cannot exceed
\log\log\rho
, and
achievability under peak power (which is a valid average-power
strategy) provides the matching lower bound.

III-E

Interpretation

The cross-gauge tradeoff means gauge-DOF lives on
\log\log\rho

while B-diversity lives on
(\log\rho)^{1-r}
, exponentially larger
for
r<1
. The packing “space” is the log-variance interval

[0,L]
of length
L=\ln(1+\rho)\sim\ln\rho
; the number of

\delta
-separated points in this interval is only

\sim L/c(\delta)\sim\log\rho
, giving
K_{\mathrm{pack}}\sim\log\log\rho
. But the spacing between adjacent codewords,
and hence the Bhattacharyya distance, is measured on the much larger
scale
L/(K-1)\sim(\log\rho)^{1-r}
. This disparity between the
rate budget and the reliability budget is the geometric content of
the cross-gauge phenomenon.

Remark 3

(B-diversity vs. classical diversity: fast fading)
.

At any positive multiplexing gain
r>0
, the classical diversity
order gives
d=\lim-\log P_{e}/\log\rho=0
for fast fading,
because
P_{e}\sim\exp(-c(\log\rho)^{1-r})
decays faster than any
polynomial yet slower than
\rho^{-d}
for any
d>0
. The
B-diversity achievable exponent is

\mathrm{DIV}_{g,\psi_{r}}(r)=N/2
on the gauge

\psi_{r}(\rho)=(\log\rho)^{1-r}
, correctly reflecting the

N
-fold antenna gain that the classical definition does not
register. (At fixed rate, i.e., constant
K
, the classical diversity
order is positive; the trap arises specifically when the rate scales
with capacity.)

Remark 4

(Operational status of B-diversity)
.

For any channel with gauge
g
, the B-diversity frontier satisfies
the following chain at rate
R=r\,g(\rho)
:

E_{\mathrm{cutoff}}(R;\rho)\;\leq\;\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)\;\leq\;E_{\mathrm{rc}}(R;\rho),

(15)

where
E_{\mathrm{cutoff}}
is the cutoff-rate exponent
(Lemma
9
: any rate below the cutoff rate
R_{0}
is
achievable with vanishing error, and
\Delta^{*}
achieves at least

R_{0}
) and
E_{\mathrm{rc}}
is the random-coding exponent (the
union–Bhattacharyya bound is a relaxation of the random-coding
bound at Chernoff parameter
1/2
). The upper
inequality is loose for
r
near the maximum, and the lower
inequality is loose at low rates; for the fast fading channel at
moderate
r
, the matching upper and lower bounds on
\Delta^{*}

(Theorem
1
) pin the exponent exactly.

For fast fading, B-diversity predicts a concrete reliability gain
that no classical exponent captures: adding a second receive antenna
(
N=1\to 2
) doubles the error exponent

\Delta^{*}\sim(N/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
at every rate
r<1
.
The classical diversity order cannot register this; it is
d=0

regardless of
N
.

III-F

Multipath Extension

Model.

L_{p}
-path multipath with
N
receive antennas:

Y_{j,t}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L_{p}-1}H_{j,\ell}\,X_{t-\ell}+Z_{j,t}
,
where
H_{j,\ell}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{\ell}^{2})
i.i.d. and

\|X\|^{2}\leq P
.
Koch and Lapidoth
[
5
]
show that guard intervals
reduce each data symbol to an effective single-tap channel, yielding
the same scale-family structure as the fast fading model.
The log-variance range is again
L=\ln(1+\rho)
(with
\rho

incorporating the multipath power profile), and the packing analysis
is identical:
\mathrm{DOF}_{\log\log}=1
, and the cross-gauge
tradeoff
\Delta^{*}\sim(N/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
holds with multipath
constants affecting only
O(1)
terms.

IV
The Bhattacharyya Packing Framework

This section gives the full definition stack, structured in three
layers to break potential circularity: gauge-free primitives first,
then gauge identification, then normalized readings.

IV-A

General Channel Model

We consider the linear Gaussian channel (
1
) where
H

is possibly random. The input constraint set

\mathcal{X}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
collects all inputs satisfying the power
constraint (peak or average). For each input
x\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, the
channel induces an output distribution
P_{Y|X=x}^{(\rho)}
. The
family

\mathcal{P}^{(\rho)}:=\{P_{Y|X=x}^{(\rho)}:x\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\}
is
the central object. All definitions of gauge-DOF and B-diversity are
functionals of
\mathcal{P}^{(\rho)}
.

Coherent (CSIR):
the receiver knows
H
; the output law is

Y|X\!=\!x,H\sim\mathcal{CN}(\sqrt{\rho}\,Hx,I)
.

Noncoherent:
the receiver does not know
H
; the output law
is
P_{Y|X=x}^{(\rho)}=\mathbb{E}_{H}[P_{Y|X=x,H}^{(\rho)}]
.

Blocklength convention.

The symbol
T
(introduced in Section
II
) is the number
of channel uses per codeword: in the deterministic and coherent
models, each codeword is an
M\!\times\!T
matrix; in the block
fading model,
T
is the coherence time.
The symbol
n
denotes the number of independent repetitions:
independent channel uses for the fast fading model (
T\!=\!1
), or
independent blocks for the block fading model (total
uses
=nT
). All tradeoff
curves (gauge-DOF, B-diversity, and the classification tables) are
stated in
\rho
-asymptotics at any fixed
n\geq 1
. The blocklength
enters only through the reliability bound:

P_{e}\leq(K\!-\!1)\,2^{-n\,\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)}

(Corollary
1
), so larger
n
yields smaller error at
the same
(\rho,K)
.

Rate convention.

When we write “rate
R=r\,g(\rho)
,”
R
is in bits per
channel use unless otherwise stated. The load parameter

r\in[0,\mathrm{DOF}_{g}]
is dimensionless. In the block fading
model (Section
V-B
),
R
is per block of
T
symbols;
this is flagged explicitly.

IV-B

Layer 1: Gauge-Free Primitives

These definitions require no gauge.

Definition 1

(Bhattacharyya coefficient and distance)
.

For measures
P,Q
with densities
p,q
:

\displaystyle B(P,Q)

\displaystyle:=\int\sqrt{p\,q},

(16)

\displaystyle d_{B}(P,Q)

\displaystyle:=-\log B(P,Q).

(17)

The key property is multiplicativity: if
P=P_{1}\times P_{2}
and

Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
, then

d_{B}(P,Q)=d_{B}(P_{1},Q_{1})+d_{B}(P_{2},Q_{2})

(Lemma
4
). Independent looks add:
N
receive antennas
multiply
d_{B}
by
N
, and
n
channel uses multiply it by
n
.

Definition 2

(Bhattacharyya packing number)
.

N_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho):=\max\big\{|\mathcal{C}|:\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{1},\\
\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
x\neq x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}d_{B}(P_{Y|X=x}^{(\rho)},P_{Y|X=x^{\prime}}^{(\rho)})\geq\delta\big\}.

(18)

The packing complexity is

K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho):=\log N_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)
.

Definition 3

(Diversity frontier)
.

For integer
K\geq 2
:

\Delta^{*}(K;\rho):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{1}\\
|\mathcal{C}|=K\end{subarray}}\;\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
x\neq x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}d_{B}\big(P_{Y|X=x}^{(\rho)},\,P_{Y|X=x^{\prime}}^{(\rho)}\big).

(19)

Packing number and diversity frontier are inverses:

N_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)\geq K\Leftrightarrow\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)\geq\delta

(Lemma
7
).

IV-C

Layer 2: Gauge Identification

The packing complexity
K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)
is
gauge-free. Its growth rate
identifies
the rate gauge.

Definition 4

(Rate gauge function)
.

A rate gauge function is an increasing, divergent function

g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
belonging to Hardy’s LE class
(Appendix

).

All rate gauges in this paper (
\log\rho
,
\log\log\rho

(\log\rho)^{\beta}
) are slowly varying:

g(at)/g(t)\to 1
for all

. This is a consequence of their

The gauge

is read off from

Within Hardy’s logarithmico-exponential (LE) class
(Appendix

), divergent functions are totally

(Lemma

Definition 6

For a rate gauge

Within Hardy’s LE class, the divergent

Remark 6

(Constructive identification of
\psi_{r}
)
.

Definition
6
identifies
\psi_{r}
from

\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)
, which requires a channel-specific calculation.
In each model class:
(i) scale families: the log-variance range gives

\psi_{r}=(\log\rho)^{1-r}
from (
13
);
(ii) Rayleigh MIMO: the per-eigenvalue

\log(1+\rho\lambda/4)
structure gives
\psi_{r}=\log\rho

from Theorem
4
;
(iii) fixed-
H
MIMO: the linear
\rho\|HD\|_{F}^{2}
term gives

\psi_{0}\asymp\rho
from (
8
);
(iv) Toeplitz/fractional-log: Szegő’s theorem gives

\psi_{r}=(\log\rho)^{\beta}
from (
36
).
The diversity gauge may depend on
r
: this
r

derived property of the channel’s packing geometry, not a choice by
the analyst. Hardy’s comparison theorem
(Appendix
B
) guarantees that different analysts,
starting from the same channel law, arrive at the same gauge class.

Definition 7

(Gauge-diversity (B-diversity))
.

\mathrm{DIV}_{g,\psi_{r}}(r):=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{\Delta^{*}\!\big(\lceil 2^{r\,g(\rho)}\rceil;\,\rho\big)}{\psi_{r}(\rho)}.

(22)

The rate gauge
g
parameterizes codebook size; the diversity
gauge
\psi_{r}
normalizes the frontier. Neither is assumed

a priori
; both are determined by the channel.

Definition 8

(Tradeoff type)
.

The tradeoff is
same-gauge
if

\Delta^{*}(2;\rho)/C(\rho)\to\mathrm{const}\in(0,\infty)
,
and
cross-gauge
if

\Delta^{*}(2;\rho)/C(\rho)\to\infty
.

Remark 7

(Same-gauge vs. cross-gauge)
.

The diversity gauge
\psi_{r}
is defined at each load
r

(Definition
6
) and may depend on
r
in both
regimes. For the channels treated in this paper, every same-gauge
tradeoff has
\psi_{r}=g
for all
r
; we then abbreviate

\mathrm{DIV}_{g}(r)
. In cross-gauge tradeoffs,
\psi_{r}
differs
from
g
: for noncoherent fast fading,

\psi_{r}(\rho)=(\log\rho)^{1-r}
is a continuous family of gauges,
interpolating from
\log\rho
at
r=0
to a constant at
r=1
; for
the fixed-
H
MIMO,

\psi_{r}(\rho)=\rho^{1-r/\min(M,N)}
. In every case, the

r
-dependence is a derived property of the channel’s packing
geometry.

Remark 8

(Breaking the circularity)
.

The three-layer structure prevents circularity. Layer 1 defines
packing number and diversity frontier without any gauge. Layer 2
reads off the gauge from the growth rate of the packing
complexity, a derived quantity, not an assumption. Layer 3
normalizes by the derived gauge to obtain gauge-DOF and B-diversity.
At no point does a definition assume the gauge it aims to identify.

Remark 9

(B-diversity as proxy for diversity)
.

B-diversity deserves the name “diversity” for a structural reason:
the Bhattacharyya distance is additive over independent observations
(Lemma
4
). For Rayleigh fading MIMO,

\bar{d}_{B}=N\sum_{j=1}^{M}\log(1+\rho\lambda_{j}/4)
is a sum
of
N\!\cdot\!\mathrm{rank}(D)
independent terms (bridge lemma,
Lemma
1
). Each receive antenna contributes one
term per singular value: B-diversity literally counts independent
looks. The same additive structure appears in every channel class
(Section
V
). Operationally, B-diversity is an
achievable exponent: by choosing an optimal packing codebook,

P_{e}\leq(K\!-\!1)\,2^{-n\,\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)}

(Corollary
1
). It is weaker than the random-coding
exponent and the sphere-packing exponent. Its value is universality
across gauges: a finite, informative exponent on every gauge class,
including those where the classical diversity order is zero.

Remark 10

(Averaged distances cannot capture outage)
.

B-diversity and the Zheng–Tse DMT both consider a fixed codebook
(no CSIT) and average error
P_{e}=\mathbb{E}_{H}[P_{e|H}]
; they differ in
analysis, not in setup. The DMT recognizes that at high SNR the
dominant error event is outage

\{\log\det(I+\rho HH^{\dagger})<R\}
: when
H
is in outage,

P_{e|H}\approx 1
regardless of the code; when
H
is not in
outage,
P_{e|H}
decays super-exponentially. So

P_{e}\approx\Pr(\text{outage})
, and the DMT exponent equals the
outage-probability exponent
[
16
]
.

The averaged Bhattacharyya coefficient
\mathbb{E}_{H}[B]
cannot see this
structure. Since
B\in[0,1]
, the average
\mathbb{E}_{H}[B]
is dominated
by the overwhelming majority of
H
realizations where
B
is
exponentially small (good channels), not by the rare
H
where

P_{e|H}\approx 1
(outage). This is not fixable by optimizing
the Chernoff parameter: conditioned on any
H
, the channel is a
location family and
s=1/2
is already optimal. The
r^{2}

gap (Remark
12
) is an intrinsic limitation of
any averaged-distance approach for channels where reliability is
governed by outage.

Remark 11

(Why Bhattacharyya, not Chernoff)
.

Replacing the Bhattacharyya coefficient (
s=1/2
) with the
optimized Chernoff coefficient (
s=s^{*}
) does not change the gauge
structure or the shape of any tradeoff in this paper. For location
families (coherent channels conditioned on
H
, and the
per-principal-angle terms in block fading),
s=1/2
is already
optimal, so there is nothing to gain. The only channel class where
optimizing
s
yields more than an
O(1)
improvement is noncoherent
fast fading: the output is a scale family, the optimal

s^{*}=1/\Delta_{u}\to 0
, and the Chernoff information is twice the
Bhattacharyya distance for large spacing. This improves the leading
constant from
(N/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
to
N(\log\rho)^{1-r}
but
leaves the gauge, the cross-gauge phenomenon, and the tradeoff shape
unchanged. We use Bhattacharyya throughout because it has a clean
closed form, multiplicativity is transparent
(Lemma
4
), and it keeps a single distance primitive
across all channel classes.

).
For
T>M
, the ambient packing dimension increases from
2M^{2}
to

2MT

\|D_{\min}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim c\,\rho^{-r/T}
and correspondingly
stronger B-diversity; the analysis is identical with
M
replaced
by
T
in the volume exponent.

Converse.

A ball-packing argument in
\mathbb{R}^{2M^{2}}
:
K
Frobenius balls
of radius
\|D_{\min}\|_{F}/2
must fit inside the power ball of
radius
\sqrt{M}
, so

K\leq(2\sqrt{M}/\|D_{\min}\|_{F})^{2M^{2}}

\|D_{\min}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim c\,\rho^{-r/M}
.
Since
\bar{d}_{B}=N\sum_{j}\log(1+\rho\lambda_{j}/4)
is concave in
the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of
DD^{\dagger}
(with

\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}=\|D\|_{F}^{2}
), the converse gives

\bar{d}_{B}\leq NM\log(1+\rho\|D_{\min}\|_{F}^{2}/(4M))\leq N(M-r)\log\rho+O(1)
.

Achievability.

A random i.i.d. Gaussian codebook of size
K=\rho^{rM}
satisfies

\|D_{\min}\|_{F}^{2}\geq c\,\rho^{-r/M}
with probability
\to 1
by
a standard volume argument (union bound over
\binom{K}{2}
pairs).
Each difference
D_{ij}=X_{i}-X_{j}
is an
M\!\times\!T
Gaussian
matrix. For any such matrix, the singular-value profile

(\sigma_{k}/\|D\|_{F})
is independent of
\|D\|_{F}
(Gaussian
norm-direction independence) and has rank
M
almost surely, so

\sigma_{\min}/\|D\|_{F}\geq c^{\prime}(M)>0
with probability
p(M)>0

depending only on
M,T
. Since the codebook is a finite collection
of Gaussians, the conditional distribution of
D_{\min}
’s direction
given the minimum-norm selection event is absolutely continuous, so

P(\sigma_{\min}(D_{\min})\geq c^{\prime}(M)\|D_{\min}\|_{F})\geq p_{0}(M)>0

uniformly in
\rho

\lambda_{j}\geq c^{\prime}(M)^{2}\,\rho^{-r/M}
for each
j

\bar{d}_{B}\geq NM\log(1+c^{\prime\prime}(M)\,\rho^{1-r/M})=N(M-r)\log\rho+O(1)
.

Combined:

\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)=N(M-r)\,\log\rho+O(1),

(27)

so
\mathrm{DIV}_{\log}(r)=N(M-r)
for
r\in[0,\min(N,M)]
.
The union-bound error exponent is

d_{\mathrm{Bh}}(r)=NM-r(N+M).

(28)

This exponent is non-negative for
r\leq MN/(M+N)
; for larger
r

the union–Bhattacharyya bound becomes vacuous (
P_{e}
bound
>1
)
while the Zheng–Tse DMT remains meaningful up to
r=\min(M,N)
.

Remark 12

(Comparison with Zheng–Tse DMT)
.

The Zheng–Tse outage exponent
[
16
]
is

d^{*}(r)=(M-r)(N-r)=MN-r(M+N)+r^{2}
.
Our achievable exponent is

d_{\mathrm{Bh}}(r)\;\leq\;d^{*}(r),\qquad d^{*}(r)-d_{\mathrm{Bh}}(r)=r^{2}.

(29)

The gap

is a limitation of the averaged-distance method,
not a property of the channel (Remark
10
).
At
r=0

.

Both gauge-DOF and B-diversity are on the log gauge:
same-gauge
.

Remark 15

(Proof status)
.

All bounds stated in this paper are proved. Two tradeoffs remain
open: (i) for block fading, the achievability and converse differ by

Structural interpretation.
In cross-gauge regimes, even a
small rate reduction yields B-diversity on an exponentially larger

on the same scale: B-diversity decreases linearly in

at the maximum multiplexing gain.

Why the log-log regime is cross-gauge.

The packing “space” (log-variance interval of length

complexity
\sim\log\log\rho

enormous spacing on a larger gauge.

Both capacity and maximum pairwise distance are spectral integrals
of the same form; their ratio converges to

VII
Conclusion

For the fixed-
H
linear Gaussian channel
Y=\sqrt{\rho}\,HX+Z
,
DOF and diversity are dual ranks of the bilinear
map
HX
. The gauges differ because of what you do with the rank:

\varepsilon
-covering the image ellipsoid with

\varepsilon=1/\sqrt{\rho}
produces the
\log\rho

rate gauge (covering counts balls, and counting produces a log);
expanding a ball across all
N\!\cdot\!\mathrm{rank}(D)
diversity
dimensions produces the linear error-exponent gauge (expansion
measures a squared distance, not a count). This mechanism, previously only
intuitive, persists into Rayleigh fading via the bridge lemma (and is
verified channel by channel for other classes).

Bhattacharyya packing extends this geometric picture from
fixed-
H
to fading channels, with
N\!\cdot\!\mathrm{rank}(D)
appearing
as the number of additive terms in the Bhattacharyya distance. The
gauge (log, log-log, or fractional-log) is determined by the
channel. The B-diversity frontier provides a finite, informative
achievable exponent on every gauge class, including those where the
classical diversity order and the standard prelog are zero. The
frontier
\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)
has matching upper and lower bounds for
fixed-
H
MIMO, coherent MIMO, and fast fading. For block fading,
the bounds differ by a factor of
M

(Remark
15
). The fractional-log tradeoff for

r>0
remains open (
r=0
is established).

The main new result is a cross-gauge tradeoff for noncoherent fast
fading:
\Delta^{*}
scales as
(N/2)(\log\rho)^{1-r}
at rate

r\log\log\rho
, with matching upper and lower bounds: a finite,

N
-proportional achievable exponent on a channel where the classical
diversity order is zero. Further results include:
(i) the same-gauge classification for fractional-log channels
(
\Delta^{*}(2;\rho)/C(\rho)\to N/2
) with a KL covering upper bound
on the tradeoff function;
(ii) a calibration against the Zheng–Tse DMT for coherent MIMO,
yielding a closed-form achievable exponent

d_{\mathrm{Bh}}(r)=d^{*}(r)-r^{2}
;
(iii) noncoherent block fading gauge-DOF on the Grassmannian with max
B-diversity
MN
matching the coherent intercept; and
(iv) a structural classification into same-gauge and cross-gauge
tradeoffs.
In every case, B-diversity scales linearly with the number of receive
antennas
N
.

Limitations.

B-diversity is a workable proxy, not the optimal error exponent. The
fractional-log tradeoff for
r>0
remains open. The framework is
developed for Gaussian fading; non-Gaussian fading, partial CSI, and
interference channels follow the same recipe (identify the
output-distribution family, compute the Bhattacharyya packing
complexity, read off the gauge) but are not treated here.

Multiplicativity

Lemma 4

(Multiplicativity)
.

If
P=P_{1}\times P_{2}
and
Q=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
, then

B(P,Q)=B(P_{1},Q_{1})\,B(P_{2},Q_{2})
, i.e.,

d_{B}(P,Q)=d_{B}(P_{1},Q_{1})+d_{B}(P_{2},Q_{2})
.

Proof.

By Fubini,

\int\sqrt{p_{1}p_{2}}\sqrt{q_{1}q_{2}}=(\int\sqrt{p_{1}q_{1}})(\int\sqrt{p_{2}q_{2}})
.
∎

(39)

(Same mean)

is always the

Why Bhattacharyya Distance

Remark 16

(Why Bhattacharyya distance)
.

The Bhattacharyya distance is singled out by two properties:
(i) multiplicativity (Lemma
4
), which ensures that
diversity counts independent looks additively;
(ii) the union–Bhattacharyya bound, which gives the tightest
single-letter upper bound on pairwise ML error probability among
Chernoff-type bounds at exponent parameter
1/2
.

Remark 17

(Gauge invariance across divergences)
.

The gauge class of
K_{\mathrm{pack}}
is unchanged if
d_{B}
is
replaced by Hellinger, KL, or Rényi-
\alpha
.
The argument has two steps.

Step 1: packing numbers are identical up to threshold
reparametrization.

If two divergences

on the output family satisfy

d_{2}=\varphi(d_{1})
for a fixed increasing bijection
\varphi

independent of
\rho
, then
d_{2}\geq\delta

, so

N_{\mathrm{pack}}^{(d_{2})}(\delta;\rho)=N_{\mathrm{pack}}^{(d_{1})}(\varphi^{-1}(\delta);\rho)
.

Step 2: threshold reparametrization does not change the gauge.

By Lemma
8
, the gauge class of

K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)
is independent of the
threshold
\delta
. Combining with Step 1,
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
identify
the same gauge class.

Verification that Step 1 applies.

Hellinger:
H^{2}=1-2^{-d_{B}}
, a fixed monotone bijection.
KL (location family, coherent channels):

D_{\mathrm{KL}}=4\,d_{B}
, since both equal a multiple
of
\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}
.
KL (scale family, fast fading): both
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
and

d_{B}
depend only on the log-variance gap
\Delta
, not on
the absolute parameter, giving
D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\varphi(d_{B})

with
\varphi
independent of
\rho
.
Rényi-
\alpha
:
D_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}(d_{B})
by the same
parametric reduction.

Appendix B
Gauge Uniqueness

The gauge framework requires that the gauge class be

unique
: otherwise two analysts could assign different gauges
to the same channel. Without a total ordering on growth rates, one
could have functions
g_{1},g_{2}
with
g_{1}/g_{2}
oscillating, giving
no well-defined gauge. Hardy’s logarithmico-exponential (LE)
class
[
4
]
, the smallest class containing the identity
and closed under addition, multiplication, exponentiation, and
logarithms, provides such a total ordering: for any two divergent LE
functions, either
g_{1}/g_{2}\to 0
,
g_{1}/g_{2}\to\infty
, or

g_{1}/g_{2}\to c\in(0,\infty)
. The three rate gauges in this
paper (
\log\rho
,
\log\log\rho
,
(\log\rho)^{\beta}
) are LE
functions; so are the diversity gauges
(
\rho^{1-r/\min(M,N)}
,
\log\rho
,
(\log\rho)^{1-r}
,

(\log\rho)^{\beta}
). Hardy’s comparison theorem therefore gives
uniqueness for both rate and diversity gauge classes.
We use the LE class only to exclude pathological oscillations in the
gauge; the weaker assumption of regular variation suffices for every
example in this paper.

When
K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)
or
\Delta^{*}(K;\rho)
is
eventually comparable to an LE function, the dominant gauge is
unique up to asymptotic equivalence.

Lemma 8

(Independence of reference separation)
.

If
K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)\sim c(\delta)\,g(\rho)

for some gauge
g
and
c(\delta)>0
, then the gauge class is
independent of
\delta
:

K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta_{1};\rho)/K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta_{2};\rho)\to c(\delta_{1})/c(\delta_{2})\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Immediate from
K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta_{i};\rho)/g(\rho)\to c(\delta_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
∎

Remark 18

(Gauge uniqueness)
.

By Hardy’s comparison theorem, if

K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)\sim c(\delta)\,g(\rho)
for a
divergent LE function
g
, then
g
is unique up to asymptotic
equivalence. This is a uniqueness certificate, not a discovery
procedure: establishing the packing asymptotic requires
channel-specific analysis.

Appendix D
Structural Mechanisms: Proofs

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Scale family.

The output family is

\{P_{v}=\mathcal{CN}(0,v):v\in[1,1+\rho]\}
, parameterized
by
v=\rho|x|^{2}+1
. By Lemma
6
:

d_{B}(P_{v_{1}},P_{v_{2}})=\log\cosh\!\Big(\frac{\ln v_{1}-\ln v_{2}}{2}\Big).

(42)

Change coordinates to
u=\ln v\in[0,L]
with

L=\ln(1+\rho)
. Define

c(\delta):=2\cosh^{-1}(2^{\delta})
, the spacing needed to achieve

d_{B}=\delta
.

Achievability.

Place
K
points equally spaced in
[0,L]
. Minimum separation

\Delta_{u}=L/(K-1)\geq c(\delta)
requires

K\leq 1+L/c(\delta)
, giving

K_{\mathrm{pack}}\geq\log\log\rho+O(1)
.

Converse.

Any codebook with
d_{B}\geq\delta
has
u
-coordinates that are

c(\delta)
-separated in
[0,L]
, giving at most

1+L/c(\delta)
points:
K_{\mathrm{pack}}\leq\log\log\rho+O(1)
.

Combining:
K_{\mathrm{pack}}=\log\log\rho+O(1)
, giving

\mathrm{DOF}_{\log\log}=1
.

Gaussian Toeplitz.

For block length
T
, the input is
X\in\mathbb{C}^{T}
with

\|X\|^{2}\leq TP
. The noncoherent output per receive antenna is

Y_{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Sigma_{X})
where

\Sigma_{X}=\rho\,\mathrm{diag}(X)\,R_{T}\,\mathrm{diag}(X)^{\dagger}+I_{T}
and
R_{T}
is the fading Toeplitz matrix. By Szegő’s
theorem
[
3
]
, for any fixed input
X
:

\frac{1}{T}\log\det\Sigma_{T}(X)\to\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log\!\big(1+\rho|x_{\lambda}|^{2}f_{H}(\lambda)\big)\,d\lambda

as
T\to\infty
, where
|x_{\lambda}|^{2}
is the spectral energy of
the input at frequency
\lambda
. For Lapidoth’s non-regular
PSD
[
8
]
with
\beta\in(0,1)
, the right-hand
side scales as
c(\beta)\,(\log\rho)^{\beta}
for inputs with
positive spectral energy.

Achievability (random coding with expurgation).

Let
Q
be a Gaussian distribution on
\mathbb{C}^{T}
with covariance
achieving the cutoff rate
R_{0}
; by Szegő’s theorem
applied to the
E_{0}
function,

(1/T)\,R_{0}\asymp c^{\prime}(\log\rho)^{\beta}
for
the non-regular PSD
[
8
]
.
Draw
K=2^{TR}
codewords i.i.d. from
Q
with
R<R_{0}/T
.
For any pair
(i,j)
, Markov’s inequality gives

P\!\big(d_{B}(X_{i},X_{j})<\delta\big)\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{Q}[B(X_{i},X_{j})]\cdot 2^{\delta}\;=\;2^{\delta-R_{0}}.

The expected number of bad pairs (with
d_{B}<\delta
) is at most

\binom{K}{2}\cdot 2^{\delta-R_{0}}\leq K^{2}\cdot 2^{\delta-R_{0}}
.
Expurgation: remove one codeword per bad pair. At least

K/2
codewords survive provided

K^{2}\cdot 2^{\delta-R_{0}}\leq K/2
, i.e.,

K\leq 2^{R_{0}-\delta-1}
.
So a codebook of size
K^{\prime}\geq K/2
with

\Delta_{\min}\geq\delta
exists whenever

\log K^{\prime}\leq R_{0}-\delta-2
, giving

(1/T)\,K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)\geq(R_{0}-\delta-2)/T\asymp c_{\mathrm{lo}}(\log\rho)^{\beta}
.

Converse (via Hellinger covering).

A
\delta
-packing in Bhattacharyya distance is an

\epsilon_{0}
-packing in Hellinger distance with

\epsilon_{0}=\sqrt{1-2^{-\delta}}>0
(since

H^{2}(P,Q)=1-B(P,Q)\geq 1-2^{-\delta}
whenever

d_{B}\geq\delta
). Since Hellinger distance is a metric,

N_{\mathrm{pack}}^{(H)}(\epsilon_{0})\leq N_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(H)}(\epsilon_{0}/2)
by the standard
packing-covering bound. Since
H^{2}(P,Q)\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)

(Pinsker-type inequality for Hellinger), any KL

(\epsilon_{0}^{2}/4)
-covering is also a Hellinger

(\epsilon_{0}/2)
-covering:

N_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(H)}(\epsilon_{0}/2)\leq N_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(\mathrm{KL})}(\epsilon_{0}^{2}/4)
.
By Lapidoth’s capacity
converse
[
8
]
:

(1/T)\log N_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(\mathrm{KL})}(\delta^{\prime};\mathcal{P}_{T}^{(\rho)})\leq c_{\mathrm{hi}}(\log\rho)^{\beta}
for any fixed
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
Combining:

(1/T)\,K_{\mathrm{pack}}(\delta;\rho)\leq c_{\mathrm{hi}}(\log\rho)^{\beta}
.

Together:
(1/T)\,K_{\mathrm{pack}}\asymp c_{1}(\log\rho)^{\beta}
,
giving gauge
g(t)=(\log t)^{\beta}
.
∎

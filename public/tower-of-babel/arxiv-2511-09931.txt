On equivariant isometric embeddings of Riemannian manifolds with symmetries
Hongda Qiu Email: hjq5042@psu.edu

Abstract
Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a C ∞ C^{\infty} -smooth, n n -dimensional Riemannian manifold which is diffeomorphic to ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and admit an action of a properly discontinuous and cocompact group. This work proves the existence of a C ∞ C^{\infty} equivariant isometric embedding of M M in some Euclidean space ℝ q \mathbb{R}^{q} where q = max ⁡ { s n + 2 ​ n , s n + n + 5 } q=\max\{s_{n}+2n,s_{n}+n+5\} is the same as the dimension of Matthias Günther’s results.

Introduction

History.
The isometric embedding problem of Riemannian manifolds into Euclidean spaces has a rich history. John Nash
[
11
]
proved the existence of a global isometric embedding for every smooth Riemannian manifold into some high dimensional Euclidean space. Later, Gromov, Rokhlin and Günther

enhanced Nash’s result by reducing the required dimension of target space and by reformulating the problem in a language more accessible for further exploitation. Specifically, they directly simplified Nash’s original scheme and achieved the best required dimension known so far. Moreover, in the results above, the embedding has the same regularity as the metric of the manifold. In this work, we work with
C^{\infty}
-case, which is consistent with Günther’s statements.

As the study of isometric embeddings deepened, more attention has expanded besides smoothness and required dimension. One novel question is: Can isometric embeddings preserve symmetry? That is, if a Riemannian manifold is acted upon by a group, can we find an isometric embedding that is
equivariant
, meaning that the embedding respects this group? Furthermore, for which type of groups is this possible?

Equivariant isometric embeddings of Riemannian manifolds have been studied historically for specific examples such as compact homogeneous spaces
[
8
]
, compact manifolds acted upon by a Lie group
[

]
[

or a finite group

, and symmetric spaces
[

[

]
[

. Nevertheless, the schemes of Nash and Günther are somehow used as a black box, and it remains an open problem whether their constructions can preserve symmetry of general manifolds, especially non-compact cases. This motivates an investigation into approaches that engage more with the analytic structure of their methods.

Focus of study.
We study smooth equivariant isometric embeddings of manifolds with symmetries, specifically, the universal covers of Riemannian tori, and establish an existence theorem. At first glance, one might consider applying the Nash embedding theorem separately to every fundamental domain of the universal cover, and then gluing the embedded images together along their boundaries. However, this is flawed, as the images of adjacent fundamental domains do not necessarily align along their shared boundaries.

Our idea is to modify Günther’s method into an “equivariant” version, yielding a result that does not incur extra cost in the required dimension of target space. Understanding his approach contextualizes our approach. Günther’s machine is summarized in the next section, followed by a presentation of our modification in the one after. Our language combines the reformulation by Han and Hong

Our approach.
We set up our study in higher generality. Instead of a Riemannian torus, we consider a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
that is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and admits a Bieberbach group
\Gamma

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold which is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and admits an action of a Bieberbach group

be a smooth free equivariant embedding such that
g-du_{0}\cdot du_{0}
is positive-definite. Then there exists a smooth free equivariant isometric embedding
u:M\to\mathbb{R}^{q}

Applying Lemma

(M,g)

Our approach illustrates that it is possible to impose symmetry-preserving conditions on isometric embeddings without increasing the required dimension when compared to Günther’s original result. Compared to the joint work of Dmitri Burago and the author

Finally, we propose and discuss several open problems.

Problem 1
.

Let
M

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and admits a group action that is properly discontinuous but non-co-compact. Does

We hope that there is a way to answer the following open problem by Gromov affirmatively:

Problem 2

Is it possible to reduce the required dimension to

-generic embeddings on the universal cover of a Riemannian torus?

One can also ask the problem above for a smooth manifold that is diffeomorphic to

and admits a Bieberbach group action. A potential path to this problem seems to be by modifying Günther’s Lemma (Lemma

On the contrary, one can ask whether it is possible to reduce the dimension of target space

\epsilon_{1}>0
, there exists a free embedding
\widehat{u}_{1}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
such that

d\widehat{u}_{1}\cdot d\widehat{u}_{1}=du_{0}\cdot du_{0}+\widetilde{h}_{1}|_{\widehat{U}_{1}},\text{ for }x\in\widehat{U}_{1}.

Moreover
|\widehat{u}_{1}-u_{0}|\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
\widehat{u}_{1}=u_{0}
on
M\setminus\widehat{U}_{1}
. Since
\widehat{U}_{1}
is diffeomorphic to
{U}_{1}
(via
p|_{\widehat{U}_{1}}
), we can define
u_{1}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
as

u_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}&\widehat{u}_{1}\circ(p|_{\widehat{U}_{1}})^{-1}\circ p(x),x\in{p^{-1}({U}_{1})}\\
&u_{0}(x),x\in M\setminus{p^{-1}({U}_{1})}\end{cases}.

(9)

Clearly,
u_{1}
is an equivariant immersion. Since
\widehat{u}_{1}
is free, so is
u_{1}
.

Repeating the process above on
u_{l},l=1,...,L-1
, we obtain a series of free equivariant immersions
u_{2},...,u_{L}
. It follows that
du_{L}\cdot du_{L}=du_{0}\cdot du_{0}+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l}\widetilde{h}_{l}
and
|u_{L}(x)-u_{0}(x)|\leq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l:x\in{p^{-1}({U_{l}})}}\epsilon_{l}
. We want
u_{L}
to be well-defined. That is, we want
|u_{0}-u_{L}|_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
to be finite. Since
\widehat{U}_{l}
does not intersect
\tau\widehat{U}_{l}
for nontrivial
\tau\in\Gamma
, we have

\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l:x\in{p^{-1}({U_{l}})}}\epsilon_{l}\leq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l=1}^{L}\epsilon_{l}

(10)

Here, the right hand side
\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l=1}^{L}\epsilon_{l}
can be chosen to be finite. The proof of (a) is done.

(b) We prove this part by choosing
\epsilon_{l},l=1,2,...,L
to be small enough.

Let us start with
l=1
. Recall that
u_{0}
is an embedding. We show that it is possible to choose
\epsilon_{1}
such that
u_{1}(M)
has no self-intersection. We divide the proof into three cases.

1.

u_{1}(M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
has no self-intersection.

It follows from
u_{1}=u_{0}
on
M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1})
.

2.

u_{1}(p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
has no self-intersection.

From Part (a), we know that
\widehat{u}_{1}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is an embedding, so
\widehat{u}_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})
has no self-intersections. From the construction of
u_{1}
in (a),
u_{1}=\widehat{u}_{1}\circ(p|_{\widehat{U}_{1}})^{-1}\circ p
on
p^{-1}({U}_{1})
, so every
u_{1}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1}),\tau\in\Gamma
does not have self-intersections.

It remains to show that
u_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})
does not intersect
u_{1}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})
for nontrivial
\tau\in\Gamma
.

Recall that our choice of
{U_{1}}
(in Lemma
2
) ensures that
\min\limits_{\tau\in\Gamma}d_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}(u_{0}(\widehat{U}_{1}),u_{0}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1}))>0
. Take

\epsilon_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\min\limits_{\tau\in\Gamma}d_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}(u_{0}(\widehat{U}_{1}),u_{0}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})).

It follows that every
u_{1}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})
is contained in the
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhood of
u_{0}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})
in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.

Since the
\epsilon_{1}
-neighborhoods of
u_{0}(\widehat{U}_{1})
and
u_{0}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})
do not intersect,
u_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})
does not intersect
u_{1}(\tau\widehat{U}_{1})
.

3.

u_{1}(p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
does not intersect
u_{1}(M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
.

It suffices to show that
u_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})
does not intersect
u_{1}(M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
. To see this, we note that
u_{1}(M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1}))=\widehat{u}_{1}(M\setminus p^{-1}({U}_{1}))
and
u_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})=\widehat{u}_{1}(\widehat{U}_{1})
. Since
\widehat{u}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is an embedding, the statement follows.

By repeating this process inductively for
l=2,3,...,L
, the proof of (b) is concluded.

Appendix A
Günther’s Proof of Proposition
1

This is an English translation for the proof of Proposition
1

[
3
, Theorem 2.2, p. 168]
.

Theorem
(Günther).

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth manifold equipped with metric
{U}
. There exists a locally finite open cover
\{U_{l}\}
by simply-connected, relatively compact sets attached with at most countably many smooth
2
-tensor fields
h_{l}
of Property (E) on
U_{l}
(Definition
2.1
) satisfying

g=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l}h_{l}

(11)

Let
V_{\epsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}||z|\leq\epsilon\}
and
B:=V_{1}
. For the rest of this section, when we refer to a chart
(U,\varphi)
on
M
, we implicitly assume that
\varphi(U)=V_{\epsilon}
, for some
\epsilon>0
.

A lemma
[
3
, Lemma 2.3, p.168]
is needed.

Lemma 4
(Günther).

Let
g\in C^{r}(B,\mathbb{R}^{D})
, and let
F\subset C^{r}(B,\mathbb{R}^{D})
be a family of smooth maps with the following property:
there exists a neighborhood
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
of
g(0)
such that every
z\in W
can be written as

z=\alpha_{1}f_{1}(0)+\cdots+\alpha_{k}f_{k}(0),

for suitable
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in F
and positive coefficients
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then there exist an open neighborhood
B^{\prime}\subset B
of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, positive functions

a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\in C^{r}(B^{\prime})
, and
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}\in F
such that

g(x)=a_{1}(x)f_{1}(x)+\cdots+a_{\ell}(x)f_{\ell}(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in B^{\prime}.

Proof
(Günther’s theorem).

Fix
p\in M
. There is a relatively compact neighborhood
U_{p}\subset M
and a
C^{r}
-diffeomorphism

\varphi_{p}
from
U_{p}
onto the open unit ball
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\varphi_{p}(p)=0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}:U_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
be the coordinate functions given by
(U_{p},\varphi_{p})
and let

L=\{f\in C^{\infty}(B)|f(x)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i},a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}

(12)

be the set of linear functions on
B
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a function whose support is contained in
U_{p}
, and
f\in L
. Then the tensor field
h:=(\chi^{4}\partial_{i}f\partial_{j}f)
defines a symmetric
2
-tensor field of Property (E) on
U_{p}
(Definition
2.1
).

Let

F:=\{(\partial_{i}f\partial_{j}f)_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}|f\in L\}\subset C^{\infty}(B,\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}).

(13)

Let
z=(z_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}
be a vector such that
|z_{ij}-g_{ij}(0)|
is sufficiently small so that
the
(n\times n)
-matrix
(z_{ij})
is positive-definite. Hence there exist coefficients
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

z_{ij}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l=1}\partial_{i}f_{l}(0)\partial_{j}f_{l}(0),

(14)

where
f_{l}(x):=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{k=1}^{n}c_{kl}x_{k},l=1,2,...,n
.

Therefore the conditions of Lemma
4
are satisfied by
{U}
and
F
. Recall our notation:
V_{r}
is the open ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin. Applying Lemma
4
to
{U}
and
F
, we obtain
\varepsilon_{p}>0
,

\ell_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
, positive functions
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell_{p}}\in C^{r}(V_{\epsilon_{p}})
,
and associated fields
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell_{p}}\in F
such that on
V_{\epsilon_{p}}

g_{ij}(x)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{k=1}^{l_{p}}a_{k}^{4}(x)\partial_{i}f_{k}(x)\partial_{j}f_{k}(x),x\in V_{\epsilon_{p}}.

(15)

The family

\mathcal{B}=\{\varphi_{p}^{-1}(V_{r}):r\in(0,\epsilon_{p}],\ p\in M\},

(16)

where
V_{r}
is the open ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin, defines a topological basis of
M
.
Choose a countably many, locally finite cover
\{U_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{B}
by relatively compact sets and a partition of unity

\{\chi_{l}^{4}\}_{l\geq 1}
subordinate to it. As shown above, every
\chi_{l}^{4}g
is a finite sum of
C^{r}
tensor fields, each of which is of property (E) (on its support)
and has a support contained in
U_{l}
. Since the cover
\{U_{l}\}
is chosen to be locally finite, the sum

\sum_{l}\chi_{l}^{4}g

M
and hence expresses
g
as a (at most countably many) sum of
C^{r}
tensor fields of Property (E). This concludes the proof.

Mechanism and field validation of fly-ash grouting for mitigating mining-induced surface subsidence
Authors: Chuanhong Ding, Yong Qin, Chen Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The extensive extraction of global coal resources has induced severe surface subsidence in goaf areas, posing signif-icant threats to the surrounding environment and infrastructure. This study investigates the application and mechanisms of overburden separation grouting technology for controlling surface subsidence, using the 2311 working face of Changcun Coal Mine in China as a case study. A grouting process was designed utilizing fly ash as the primary material, incorporating an optimized borehole layout, dynamic adjustment of grouting parameters, and scientific selection of grouting timing. A visual compression-flow experimental system was employed to sys-tematically analyze the compressive stress–deformation relationship and bleeding characteristics of the fly ash slurry, providing critical evidence for determining grouting process parameters. Combined with long-term surface deformation monitoring data, the significant effectiveness of the grouting technology in mitigating surface subsid-ence was confirmed. The key transferable contributions of this research are: (i) a quantitative method to determine the optimal grout-ing-production ratio (≈ 0.5) for effective subsidence control; (ii) a dynamic pressure adjustment strategy based on real-time subsidence monitoring; and (iii) an integrated laboratory–field validation framework linking slurry rhe-ology to field performance. These findings provide a process-level basis for engineering practices in coal mines with comparable geological and mining conditions, offering both methodological innovation and practical guidance. .

Introduction

Coal remains a pivotal traditional fossil fuel in the global energy mix1–3. However, the continuous advancement of coal mining has led to increasingly prominent environmental issues. Surface subsidence, in particular, severely impacts buildings, transportation infrastructure, and the ecological environment4–6. Comprehensive research on the geological and environmental changes in specific coal mining areas is therefore crucial. Using Changcun Coal Mine as an example, the characteristics of the overlying bedrock and the coverage of thick alluvial layers significantly influence surface sub-sidence7,8.

The Guozhuang Coal Mine railway line, which passes through the mining area of Changcun Coal Mine, is affected by seven mining faces. Among these, the S3-13 mining face has been fully mined and shares the same geological conditions as the face where separation grouting is implemented in this study. The S3-13 mining face of Changcun Coal Mine was mined from August 2016 to April 2018, with an average mining depth of 487 m, a coal thickness of 5.82 m, and a coal seam inclination of 10–13°. The strike length of the mining face is 1032 m, and the dip length is 228 m. The overlying bedrock primarily consists of mudstone, sandstone, and sandy mudstone. This area is covered by thick alluvial layers, with the Quaternary surface soil layer thickness ranging from 48 to 95.9 m, accounting for approximately 14.3% of the average thickness of the overlying strata above the coal seam. The mining method is fully mechanized top-coal caving, with the full caving method used for roof management. The advance rate of the working face was 3.2 m/d, with intermittent mining cessations.

Analysis of on-site surface subsidence monitoring results(Fig. 1) indicates that the fully mechanized top-coal caving mining method leads to an extensive surface deformation range due to mining activities, with severe surface movement and deformation. However, due to the relatively large depth of the coal seam, the subsidence rate is comparatively small. The characteristics of fully mechanized top-coal caving mining under thick unconsolidated layers are evident, with a surface subsidence coefficient of 0.76, a major influence angle tangent of 2.78, a maximum subsidence angle of 89°, and an inflection point offset distance of (0.05–0.14)H. These factors pose significant impacts on the railway.

In the process of coal mining under “three bottoms” (buildings, water bodies, and railways), surface subsidence leads to the need for setting up protective coal pillars, which in turn triggers the phenomenon of “coal pressing,” resulting in a significant waste of coal resources. For the 2311 working face of Changcun Coal Mine, the railway protection coal pillar alone traps approximately 1.48 million tons (Mt) of coal reserves, while the adjacent Yuancun village protection coal pillar adds another 0.33 Mt of trapped coal. Through overburden separation grouting technology, these trapped resources can be liberated, with an initial economic value of ¥759 million (calculated based on the local pithead price of ¥513 per ton for No. 3 coal seam in the Lu’an mining area). Furthermore, the difficulties in mining deployment further increase mining costs. Although the underground filling method adopted in the past has alleviated the problem of surface subsidence to some extent, it requires a large amount of filling material, is generally used in thin coal seams, and necessitates simultaneous mining and filling operations. Due to limited underground space and low engineering efficiency, this traditional method costs approximately ¥150 per ton of coal (average cost of “three-under” coal mining filling in China), whereas the overburden separation grouting technology in this study only costs ¥20.58 per ton of liberated coal—merely 13.7% of the traditional method’s cost. This significant cost advantage, combined with high resource recovery, makes the technology economically viable for large-scale application.

Against this backdrop, the technique of overburden separation grouting has emerged as an innovative solution, providing new ideas for addressing issues such as coal waste, mining difficulties, and increased mining costs9–12. This technology relies on the natural subsidence pattern of the overburden after mining and skillfully utilizes the formed separation space for grouting and filling, achieving effective control of surface subsidence and protecting the stability of ground buildings and the ecological environment13,14. Its core concept lies in precisely grasping the timing of grouting and performing grouting operations in the key separation space of the overburden bending and subsidence zone. This approach reduces surface subsidence while solidifying the foundation rock strata in the protected area into a whole, greatly eliminating the adverse effects of relative displacement and collapse of the rock strata. Compared with traditional filling methods, overburden separation grouting has significant advantages. It only targets the separation space in the bending and subsidence zone for grouting, thereby greatly reducing material consumption. More importantly, this operation is carried out on the ground, without interfering with underground mining operations, thus not affecting mining efficiency15.

As an emerging method for surface subsidence control, overburden separation grouting and filling technology has garnered extensive attention from both academia and the engineering community. This technology skillfully utilizes the separation space in the overburden and forms effective support for the overlying rock strata through high-pressure grouting and filling, thereby achieving the goal of controlling surface subsidence. Many scholars worldwide have conducted research in this field, covering key aspects such as the selection and performance optimization of grouting materials, fine-tuning of grouting parameters, and comprehensive evaluation of grouting effects, resulting in a series of notable achievements16–19. Recent studies on mining-induced rock behavior and strata control offer basic references for this study. Teng et al.20 used in situ NMR to observe rock/coal pore-fracture evolution under triaxial compression, providing a basis for understanding overburden micro-fracture development. Sun et al.21 studied overburden failure under repeated mining with multiple key strata, noting key strata properties affect fracture scope. Additionally, Teng et al.22 established a hard roof water injection softening model, whose active intervention idea informed our grouting parameter design.

However, while fully acknowledging existing research achievements, some deficiencies in current research should be recognized:Most existing studies are limited to single geological conditions, lacking a systematic comparison of grouting-for-subsidence-control mechanisms across different geological backgrounds23,24. The long-term performance and engineering adaptability of grouting materials are insufficiently studied, particularly regarding the aging characteristics of fly ash slurry.The dynamic collaborative control theory for grouting timing and working face advancement remains underdeveloped, with a lack of intelligent regulation models based on real-time monitoring.

To address these research gaps, this study selects the 2311 working face of Changcun Coal Mine as the research object and employs theoretical analysis, field measurements, and other research methods to systematically investigate the development patterns of overburden fractures under the semi-open space mining mode and the control effects and mechanisms of grouting and filling technology on surface subsidence.

This study is expected to deepen the scientific understanding of the mechanism of grouting and filling technology in controlling surface subsidence and provide new ideas and methodologies for addressing surface subsidence control under similar geological conditions. The research results have important practical implications for guiding safe and efficient coal mining and promoting sustainable development in the mining industry, offering valuable references for the international mining sector. Furthermore, this study provides robust scientific support for the green transformation and high-quality development of China’s coal mining industry, potentially driving technological progress and innovation in this field globally.

Materials and methods

This study aims to investigate the control mechanism of grouting and filling technology on surface subsidence and verify its applicability and effectiveness under different geological conditions. The specific materials and methods are as follows.

Overview of the study area

The 2311 working face of Changcun Coal Mine is located west of the Lu’an Yishen Energy Coking Plant and south of Yuancun, with a surface elevation ranging from + 949.3 m to + 955.2 m. Underground, the working face is bordered by the main roadway of the 23 mining area to the north, the DX22-2 collapse column water-resisting coal pillar to the south, the boundary of the Yuwu Coal Mine to the west, and solid coal to the east, where no working face has been arranged yet. The main coal seam being mined is Coal Seam No. 3, which belongs to the 23 mining area. This working face features a typical semi-open space mining mode, providing an ideal natural environment for studying the control effect of grouting and filling technology on surface subsidence.

Geological exploration

Detailed exploration of the lithology, thickness, and development of joints and fractures in the overlying strata of the 2311 working face was conducted through drilling, well logging, and other means, providing a basis for the design of the grouting process. To gain a clearer understanding of the strata occurrence structure and conditions in the mining area, a borehole group study area with a width of 300 m and a length of 587 m was selected. The T685WS top drive truck-mounted drilling rig (produced by Schramm in the United States), commonly used in coalbed methane drilling, was employed to carry out intensive borehole group construction in the study area, as shown in Fig. 2.

Onsite drilling rig construction.

During the comprehensive well logging process, methods such as spontaneous potential, apparent resistivity, and natural gamma were employed. Based on the well logging data, parameters such as formation lithology, thickness, and stratigraphic characteristics were discerned. A borehole database and a set of characteristic parameters were established, defining the ten-meter segment count of boreholes, the cumulative length ratio of mudstone to sandstone, and the segment count ratio of mudstone to sandstone. In the study area, the average ten-meter segment count of boreholes is 1.8 segments, with a difference of 0.1 segments between mudstone and sandstone. The ten-meter segment count of boreholes in the southern part of the working face (161 m) is higher than the average, indicating relatively lower stratigraphic integrity in this area compared to other regions. The central and northern areas (307 m) generally have lower values than the average. The cumulative length ratio of mudstone to sandstone ranges from 0.89 to 2.71, with an average of 1.64. The overall segment count of mudstone is generally lower than that of sandstone, with the segment count ratio of mudstone to sandstone ranging from 0.70 to 1.09, with an average of 0.89.

The identification of key strata in this study adheres to the Key Stratum Theory proposed by Academician Qian Minggao25. Its core lies in identifying the hard rock layers within the overburden that play a major controlling role in the movement and fracture of all or part of the overlying rock layers (extending up to the ground surface) during mining activities. The identification process is a quantitative analysis based on geomechanical parameters, mainly consisting of two core steps: Stiffness Identification and Strength Identification.​​.

Based on the geological boreholes and comprehensive logging data (including spontaneous potential, apparent resistivity, natural gamma ray, etc.) of the 2311 working face, parameters such as the thickness (hi), lithology, burial depth of each rock layer in the overburden, as well as the elastic modulus (Ei) and unit weight (γi) obtained through laboratory tests were accurately obtained.

Starting from the first rock layer above the coal seam, the load borne by each layer was calculated layer by layer from top to bottom. Grouting process design

Grouting materials

This study selects fly ash as the grouting material. Fly ash is the fine ash captured from the flue gas after coal combustion, mainly composed of oxides such as SiO2, Al2O3, FeO, Fe2O3, CaO, and TiO2. It is the primary solid waste generated by coal-fired power plants. With the development of the power industry, the emission of fly ash has been increasing annually. Untreated fly ash can cause air pollution and water system siltation, and toxic chemicals can also pose hazards to humans and organisms. However, fly ash can be utilized as a resource, such as being used as a concrete admixture. Its color can reflect its quality and carbon content, and its particles have a porous structure and strong water absorption capacity.

Most fly ash originates from thermal power plants and heating boilers, with wet-discharged ash accounting for the majority. However, it has low activity and is unfavorable for environmental protection and comprehensive utilization. As an industrial solid waste, fly ash is characterized by wide availability, low cost, good fluidity, and excellent solidification performance.

To screen out the optimal material, this study conducted systematic comparative experiments on the fly ash from the adjacent Zhangshan Power Plant and Yuwu Power Plant. The experiments covered analyses of physical properties, slurry working performance, and chemical composition. The selection of key performance indicators is directly linked to the core requirements of grouting engineering: fluidity (viscosity) determines the pumpability and diffusion radius of the slurry; stability (water separation rate) is related to the solid-liquid separation and grouting efficiency of the slurry; and consolidation performance (stone formation rate and set body strength) directly affects the long-term stability of the final support structure.

From June 28 to July 4, 2023, on-site tests of slurry specific gravity, funnel viscosity, water separation rate, stone formation rate, and sand content were carried out on the slurries prepared with a 1:1 ratio of fly ash from Zhangshan Power Plant and Yuwu Power Plant at the Overburden Isolation Grouting Station of Changcun Coal Mine. Borehole layout

Based on the exploration results, grouting boreholes were rationally arranged along the strike and dip directions of the working face to ensure that the slurry could effectively diffuse into the target separation space26–28.

The number and location of grouting boreholes for filling, as well as the slurry diffusion radius and the dimensions of the working face, influence the filling and subsidence reduction effect. To achieve better filling and subsidence reduction, consideration was given to injecting as much slurry into the overburden separation as possible18. Theoretically, a greater number of boreholes would lead to better results, but an excessive number increases filling costs and complicates process control. The deployment of grouting boreholes followed these principles:①Boreholes were arranged along the strike in the middle of the working face because the separation below the key stratum continuously increases as the working face advances, and the maximum separation is located in the middle of the mined-out area. Therefore, multiple boreholes are generally required to ensure timely grouting and filling of the separation below the key stratum, preventing the key stratum from breaking and collapsing29–31.②When arranging boreholes along the dip, the main consideration is the slurry diffusion radius and the separation space around the mined-out area, aiming to fill the entire separation space with slurry as much as possible.③Depth of grouting boreholes: The boreholes are drilled to the key stratum where separation develops, specifically to the middle section of the Upper Shihezi Formation in the Upper Series of the Permian System, at the bottom boundary of K12.

First borehole location

The first borehole, CC23-1, is arranged at a position 30 m outward from the original stopping line. Additionally, CC23-B1 is deployed as a standby grouting borehole, with other boreholes arranged similarly.

Principles of borehole deployment

Referring to the requirement that the spacing between adjacent grouting boreholes shall not exceed 160.2 m, this project arranges double rows of grouting boreholes along the strike direction of the 2311 working face, totaling 26 boreholes. Each set of boreholes is deployed with a spacing of no more than 100 m to meet the requirements of the slurry diffusion radius for grouting boreholes. The boreholes are drilled to the key stratum where separation develops, specifically to the middle section of the Upper Shihezi Formation in the Upper Series of the Permian System, approximately at the bottom boundary of K12.

Types of boreholes

One exploration borehole (CC23-T1) and one inspection borehole (CC23-J1) are implemented in the project, along with 26 grouting boreholes. Due to ground obstacle avoidance requirements, a total of 19 vertical boreholes and 7 directional boreholes are drilled for grouting (see the figure for the vertical trajectory of the boreholes). The total designed workload for grouting boreholes is 7500 m, while both the exploration and inspection boreholes are 450 m each. All subsequent boreholes will undergo logging.

Perforation plan

Based on the distribution of grouting horizons, 13 boreholes are deployed at the primary grouting horizon, and another 13 boreholes are deployed at the auxiliary grouting horizon. To achieve subsidence control requirements and reduce drilling costs, perforations need to be made in the 13 boreholes deployed at the auxiliary grouting horizon. The perforation positions are located at the primary grouting horizon, with each perforation segment being 10 m long.

Grouting parameters

Reasonable parameters such as grouting pressure, grouting rate, and grouting volume were determined through simulated experiments to ensure effective grouting30. The prepared slurry is a mixture of fly ash and water, in which water exists in both bound and free forms. Bound water is generally not easily lost, while most free water will seep into the stratum, resulting in the slurry ultimately taking the form of compacted ash. In the experiment, the water-ash ratio of the fly ash slurry was designed to be 0.8. Initially, 500 g of fly ash was measured using an electronic balance and poured into a beaker. Then, 400 mL of water was measured using a measuring cylinder and poured into the beaker, followed by thorough mixing to form the fly ash slurry, as shown in Fig. 4.

The designed visual compression flow experimental device, as shown in Fig. 5, consists of a transparent chamber, upper and lower permeable water heads, connecting pipes, and a measuring device. The transparent chamber is made of a high-strength glass cylinder with a thickness of 15 mm, capable of withstanding pressures up to 9 MPa. The use of glass or other rigid materials ensures better sealing between the heads and the glass, thereby providing more accurate experimental results. The glass chamber has an outer diameter of 130 mm, an inner diameter of 100 mm, and a height of 200 mm. Both the upper and lower heads are processed from rigid resin materials and are equipped with L-shaped through-holes that connect to outlet pipes leading to the measuring container. To facilitate the outflow of bleeding water, each permeable water head is fitted with a 1.0 mm thick, uniformly perforated permeable steel plate at its end, allowing the bled water to flow smoothly and preventing slurry blockage.

Fig. 5Visualized compressed flow system.

Visualized compressed flow system.

The compression experiment employs an MTS electro-hydraulic servo universal testing machine to slowly compress the visualized compressed seepage flow system until reaching the preset formation pressure level. The depth of the grouting hole is designed to be approximately 260 m below the key stratum where the bed separation develops, so the weight of the overlying strata on the filling body is about 6.5 MPa. During the experiment, the water conduit is first connected to the hydraulic head, and the transparent chamber is assembled on the lower hydraulic head and then fixed in the middle of the MTS testing machine. Subsequently, the fly ash slurry is injected into the transparent chamber, and the initial liquid level is recorded as 87.0 mm. After that, the upper head is installed, and the press is activated to load axial pressure at a speed of 2 mm/s until reaching the formation pressure. The data acquisition frequency is set to collect data every 0.05 s. During the experiment, axial pressure, displacement, and cumulative flow are recorded in real-time as they change over time, and the transformation morphology of the slurry in the transparent chamber is observed through a fixed-position camera.

To investigate the law of surface subsidence under grouting conditions, a surface rock movement observation system was established at the commencement of the project. The main instruments included a Leica TS06 total station and a Soyi DSZ2 level. A network of over 130 monitoring points was deployed across the 2311 working face and along the railway, with their spatial distribution illustrated in Fig. 6. These points were categorized into three zones (A, B, and C) and monitored at different frequencies based on their locations and anticipated deformation rates to accurately capture the dynamics of surface movement.

Results

Experimental results of grouting parameters

Figure 7 shows the grout compaction stress-deformation curve recorded by the MTS testing machine. The compaction stress process of fly ash grout can be divided into three stages: maintaining stability, slowly increasing, and rapidly increasing.

Fig. 7Grout compaction stress-deformation curve.

Grout compaction stress-deformation curve.

It can be observed that within a compression displacement range of 0 to 30 mm, corresponding to a compaction rate of 0 to 34.5%, the compression deformation is significant, yet the stress increase is only 0.035 MPa, with a stress growth rate of 0.00116 MPa/mm. During this process, the slurry is highly compressible, allowing the free water within to flow out smoothly. The fly ash particles have not formed a skeleton structure to bear the load and cannot resist deformation through particle friction. In the compression displacement range of 30 to 42 mm, or a compaction rate of 34.5 to 48.3%, after the first stage of compression and drainage, the slurry gradually becomes compacted, with particles contacting each other and enhancing their ability to actively resist deformation. During this process, the compaction stress increases from 0.0348 MPa to 0.396 MPa, with a stress growth rate of 0.025 MPa/mm. In the compression displacement range of 42 to 55 mm, or a compaction rate of 48.3 to 63.2%, the axial stress of the slurry increases rapidly, showing an exponential growth pattern. The active bearing capacity is significantly enhanced, ultimately reaching the maximum set loading stress of 6.5 MPa, with a stress growth rate of 0.534 MPa/mm.

From the final state of the compacted ash body revealed in Fig. 8a to d, it can be seen that the slurry, with an initial height of L₀ = 87.0 mm, has been compressed to L₁ = 33.5 mm, resulting in a compression of 53.5 mm (compaction rate of 61.2%). The compacted wet ash body exhibits a filter cake-like shape and is in a completely compacted state overall. By comparing the microscopic images of the ash body before and after compaction (Fig. 8e and f), it can be observed that the dry ash particles are very fine under the microscope, and the compacted body is dense and tight.

The process of compaction stress and water secretion flow in fly ash slurry is both coordinated and interactive. During the compaction deformation of fly ash slurry, the instantaneous flow rate of water secretion can be divided into three stages: gentle increase, rapid increase, and stable decrease, which exhibit certain differences from the three stages of change in slurry compaction stress.

As shown in Fig. 9, during the gentle growth phase of grout compaction, the axial stress remains almost constant, with a minimal change in instantaneous flow rate of only 0.01 mL/s. Over time, the instantaneous flow rate slowly rises, reaching 0.26 mL/s by the end of this phase. In the rapid growth phase, there is no significant change in axial stress, but the axial deformation increases by 15 mm. During this phase, the instantaneous flow rate increases significantly, reaching 0.34 mL/s. Additionally, a significant lightening of the grout color can be observed during this process. In the stable decline phase, the axial stress increases significantly, reaching 2.2 MPa by the end of the third phase. The axial strain during this phase is also 15 mm, and the instantaneous flow rate gradually decreases as the grout is compacted.

The water in the slurry exists in two forms: one part is retained as bound water in the compacted wet ash body, while the other part is secreted as free water. The water secretion rate refers to the ratio of the mass of secreted water to the mass of dry ash. Therefore, based on the measured data of the compacted wet ash body mass and dry ash body mass in the laboratory, the water secretion rate under the corresponding formation pressure (6.2 MPa) in this experiment is 0.65, which means that 65% of the free water is secreted from the slurry under this stress environment. It can be inferred that if the maximum pressure level is maintained for a period, there will still be a slow process of water secretion, but its proportion will not be too large.

Effect of surface deformation control

In the 2311 working face where grouting and mining are conducted simultaneously, when the working face advances, the surface subsidence is relatively severe. This is because the advancing speed of the working face is too fast, resulting in the failure of the overlying strata to form a stable load-bearing structure in a timely manner, leading to destruction and collapse. Before June 23, 2023, the advancing speed of the working face was generally between 2.5 m/d and 4.3 m/d, with excessive coal cutting operations underground, causing rapid surface subsidence. After June 23, 2023, the advancing speed of the working face was maintained between 0.5 m/d and 2.0 m/d, and the surface movement tended to stabilize. When the working face advanced to 200 m (near the railway), mining operations were suspended for a period, and the surface showed a trend of lifting due to advance grouting, with a maximum lift of less than 100 mm. The subsidence at measurement points near the open-off cut had basically stabilized, with a maximum subsidence of around 1100 mm. Subsequently, the working face began to advance slowly, and the surface started to show a trend of subsidence. Due to advance grouting, the separated zone of the overlying strata of the working face had been filled with fly ash slurry, resulting in slow and gradually stabilizing surface subsidence. As of October 31, 2023, the maximum subsidence near the railway was around 300 mm.

Discussion

Selection and performance of grouting materials

After the high-pressure grouting pump is stopped, the stratum will undergo a process of water separation and seepage for a certain period. If the pump is stopped directly after high-volume grouting, the stratum will experience a settlement process due to water separation and seepage. This settlement is not caused by poor grouting control but is an inevitable phenomenon. Therefore, as long as the stratum’s separated zone exists in the form of slurry, water separation and settlement will occur. Based on practical engineering experience, it is necessary to adopt a gradual reduction in grouting volume to stop grouting and minimize the settlement during the compaction process. This gradual buffered grouting plays a crucial role in maintaining stratum stability. Additionally, the period of water separation and settlement after pump shutdown needs to be specifically analyzed in conjunction with the permeability and geological structure of the target stratum.

The analysis of slurry characteristics such as water separation, seepage, and bleeding provides a foundation for understanding the migration path of free water during the grouting process. Due to the sedimentation of ash particles and particle blockage, the slurry not only permeates downward but also importantly bleeds upward and laterally, ultimately forming the aforementioned “multi-segment boundary” seepage distribution pattern. However, specific analysis is still required based on the distribution of aquicludes and the presence or absence of structural features in the stratum.

The compacted ash body after bleeding plays a major role in reducing settlement. In engineering design, the ratio of compacted ash volume to mined space volume (i.e., the grouting-production ratio) is commonly used to measure the grouting and filling volume. In engineering material settlement, dry ash mass is generally used for measurement, which requires establishing a conversion relationship between dry ash use and compacted ash volume. The compacted ash volume is also related to the overlying rock load. Therefore, in engineering project design and construction, it is necessary to first conduct experimental studies on the compression mechanics-flow coupling characteristics of the slurry. Based on indicators such as compaction rate and bleeding rate, the conversion relationship between “overlying load - dry ash mass - compacted ash volume” needs to be found to improve the design of the grouting-production ratio and engineering ash consumption32.

The scientificity and rationality of grouting process design

①Grouting pressure: The setting of grouting pressure should be based on theoretical calculations and determined according to the actual measured surface subsidence at the site. This dynamic adjustment is supported by the correlation between separation space and subsidence in Sect. 3.2: as surface subsidence increases, the separation opening (targeted for grouting) expands, requiring higher pressure to ensure the slurry fully diffuses and fills the space—consistent with the pressure-subsidence data of CC23-3 hole in Sect. 3.2 (pressure increases with subsidence). Therefore, when the surface starts to show a subsidence trend, the grouting pressure needs to be increased. After a period of time, if the surface no longer continues to subside and tends to stabilize at a certain value, the grouting pressure can remain constant without further increase until the surface subsides again.②Grouting-production ratio: This refers to the ratio of the volume of wet ash formed after the grout injected into the separation zone is compacted and settled to the volume of the mined space in the corresponding lower mining face. The grouting-production ratio calculated theoretically is an average value. As shown in Sect. 3.2, the grouting-production ratio stabilizes at around 0.5, which matches the results of the visual compression-flow experiment in Sect. 3.1: the fly ash slurry forms a dense load-bearing structure with a compaction rate of 61.2% at this ratio—sufficient to support the overlying strata without excessive compression (avoiding surface uplift, Sect. 3.2) or insufficient support (avoiding accelerated subsidence). This explains why surface uplift occurs when the ratio exceeds 0.5 (excessive slurry squeezes the overlying strata) and subsidence accelerates when it is below 0.5 (insufficient filling)—both phenomena are consistent with the measured data in Sect. 3.2.③Grouting timing: In the early stage of separation grouting for subsidence reduction in mining, when the mining face is far from the railway, the method of delayed grouting can be adopted, which means grouting starts after the mining face has passed through the borehole. At this time, the separation opening is large, making it relatively simple to inject the grout, and the grouting volume increases rapidly. When the mining face is close to the railway (within 50 m), the boreholes near the railway can adopt the method of advanced grouting, which means grouting starts before the mining face reaches the borehole. Due to the small separation opening in the early stage of advanced grouting, the grouting volume is low, the grouting pressure is high, but the grouting duration is long. Additionally, it seals the fissures and separations in the strata beneath the railway ahead of time, ensuring stable surface subsidence when the mining face passes through the railway.

Discussion on the mechanism of surface deformation control in subway construction

The core of grouting subsidence reduction technology lies in enhancing the overall stability of overlying strata through the formation of a grouting reinforcement layer. In this study, the formation of the grouting reinforcement layer effectively restricts the movement and deformation of the overlying strata, thereby achieving effective control over surface deformation.

The distribution of the filling body within the separation space determines the ultimate effect of controlling surface subsidence. During the process of grout spreading, due to degradation effects such as shearing and temperature, the performance of carrying fly ash particles in the grout within the separation is not in a fully suspended state. Some particles flow along with the grout to the surroundings, while others settle down.

Within the separation space, fly ash particles exhibit different movement states based on their size and density. Some particles with lower density flow in a suspended state and have a relatively longer flow distance; some particles with higher density settle nearby; and most particles gradually settle in a laminar flow state after moving away from the borehole to a certain position. The settlement process of fly ash is influenced by factors such as the grouting process, the advancement speed of the mining face, grouting pressure, grout concentration and physical-mechanical properties, stratum structure, and rock permeability characteristics. Within a certain radius around the borehole, due to the repeated flushing and flow of settled fly ash, after the grouting work is completed, the sediments do not form layers, as shown in Figure. 14. In other areas outside the disturbed zone, the sediments form a layered structure.

Fig. 14Distribution state of filling body.

Distribution state of filling body.

Before grouting, high-pressure water is first injected into the grouting holes to perform hydraulic pre-fracturing. Its purpose is to create and extend fractures in the rock strata, providing conditions for the subsequent generation and development of separation spaces. During grouting, it is possible that no separation space has formed at the grouting holes, so even if the rated pump pressure is reached, it is impossible to inject the grout; once a separation space is formed, the grouting pressure rapidly decreases. This stage is known as the rapid pressure decrease stage. When the grouting rate is slower than the formation rate of the separation space, the pressure at the grouting hole will be less than 0, entering the negative pressure stage. A large amount of grout is injected during this stage. As more and more sedimentary filling material accumulates around the hole, the grouting pressure will gradually increase, entering the pressure increase stage. From the distribution of the filling material in the separation space, it can be seen that if the grouting hole is located at a higher position within the separation space, the grout will flow farther under the influence of gravity, resulting in better filling effect. The position of the grouting port in the height direction of the separation space also affects the distribution of the filling material.

The filling of the separation space is achieved by the solid particles deposited from the grout, which support the overlying strata, while the water in the grout only serves as a carrier and will eventually permeate completely into the strata. The density of the grout is a direct factor affecting the diffusion radius of the grouting. If the density is too high, the fluidity becomes poor; if the density is too low, more grout will permeate into the fractures of the strata. Therefore, selecting a reasonable grout density is important. Given the complexity of studying the permeability characteristics of the separation space, through extensive field practice and simulation experiments at this stage, it has been determined that fly ash with a water-cement ratio of 1:1 is suitable.

The key to the overburden separation grouting technology is to understand the temporal and spatial patterns of the generation, development, and disappearance of separations in the mining-induced overburden. Theoretical and practical research shows that the generation, development, and ultimate closure of separations in mining-induced overburden is a very complex mechanical process. Influenced by geological and mining factors such as stratum structure, rock properties of the overlying strata, mining depth, mining area dimensions, and advancement speed of the mining face, the distribution patterns of separations vary, but they generally exhibit the following characteristics. Separations in mining-induced overburden objectively exist and have certain temporal and spatial patterns. To implement the technology of overburden separation grouting and filling for mitigating surface subsidence, it is necessary to understand the spatial location and volume of the groutable and fillable separation zones in the strata, seize the favorable timing for grouting and filling, and also ensure that the injected grout does not affect the normal underground production.

Conclusions

This study systematically investigates the mechanism and engineering effectiveness of fly-ash-based overburden separation grouting for mitigating mining-induced surface subsidence, taking the 2311 working face of Changcun Coal Mine (characterized by thick unconsolidated layers of 48–95.9 m and medium-hard overburden dominated by fine-grained sandstone) as the research object. Through theoretical analysis, laboratory experiments, and long-term field monitoring, the following key findings and conclusions are drawn: Selection and properties of grouting materials: This study selected fly ash as the grouting material and verified its excellent fluidity, curing properties, and low-cost characteristics. Experiments showed that by adjusting the water-cement ratio, fly ash slurry can effectively compact under specific stratum pressure, forming a stable supporting structure and reducing surface subsidence.Scientificity and rationality of grouting process design: Based on the geological conditions of the 2311 working face in Changcun Coal Mine, a reasonable layout of grouting boreholes, grouting parameters (including grouting pressure, grouting rate, and grouting volume), and grouting timing were designed. Experimental results indicated that the grouting pressure needs to be dynamically adjusted according to the surface subsidence to ensure the effectiveness of grouting. At the same time, when the grouting-production ratio (the ratio of injected grout volume to extracted coal volume) is around 0.5, the surface subsidence control effect is optimal.Control effect on surface deformation: Grouting and filling significantly reduced the surface subsidence of the 2311 working face in Changcun Coal Mine. Compared with the non-grouted working face, the maximum surface subsidence in the grouted area was significantly reduced, and the subsidence rate was slowed down, verifying the effectiveness of the overburden separation grouting technology in controlling surface subsidence. Additionally, the reasonable selection of grouting timing and timely adjustment of grouting pressure played a crucial role in controlling surface deformation.Impact mechanism of grouting on surface subsidence: The grouting slurry forms a stable supporting layer within the separation space, effectively limiting the movement and deformation of the overlying strata.

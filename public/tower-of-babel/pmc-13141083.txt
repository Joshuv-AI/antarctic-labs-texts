Scoping literature review on applied definitions of very early diagnosis of systemic sclerosis (VEDOSS) and pre-systemic sclerosis in original articles
Authors: Alain Lescoat, Yannick Allanore, Francesco Del Galdo, Masataka Kuwana, Christopher P Denton, Dinesh Khanna, Marco Matucci-Cerinic, Silvia Bellando-Randone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractContextThe very early diagnosis of systemic sclerosis (VEDOSS) criteria were developed to diagnose systemic sclerosis (SSc) at a very early stage. However, several studies have applied these criteria inconsistently, often using them to identify patients at a predisease stage. This use has created confusion between the concept of VEDOSS and pre-SSc. With growing interest in predisease states in autoimmunity, a consensus definition of pre-SSc is mandatory.ObjectiveAs a preliminary step towards such definition, this scoping review aimed to map existing definitions of pre-SSc and VEDOSS in the literature, identify item combinations used and capture conceptual variations.MethodsFollowing Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines, PubMed and Web of Science were searched using the terms ‘VEDOSS’ OR ‘very early diagnosis of systemic sclerosis’ OR ‘pre-scleroderma’ OR ‘pre-systemic sclerosis’ OR ‘pre-SSc’.ResultsOf 106 records screened, 40 full texts were included (eight on pre-SSc and 32 on VEDOSS). Most articles (80%) originated from Europe. Three distinct definitions of pre-SSc were identified, alongside 13 different applications of VEDOSS criteria. Although 62.5% of studies cited the 2011 VEDOSS consensus, only 31.3% applied the complete definition incorporating the three step 1 essential items and step 2 confirmation tools.ConclusionThis scoping review highlights the variability of the definitions of VEDOSS and pre-SSc that have been used in the literature. The absence of a consensus definition for pre-SSc contributes to overlapping and blurring boundaries between these concepts. A consensus effort is warranted to select a core set of items for a standardised definition of pre-SSc.Keywords: Systemic Sclerosis, Immune System Diseases, Autoimmunity

Introduction

Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is characterised by a pathogenic triad including inflammatory features with autoimmunity, vasculopathy and fibrosis.1 Autoimmunity and vasculopathy are among the earliest events of the disease, whereas the onset of fibrosis occurs later in the disease process.2 Fibrosis is nonetheless responsible for important SSc-related visceral manifestations, for example, lung fibrosis and myocardial fibrosis, which directly contribute to the high mortality characterising SSc.3 Interventions, including immunomodulatory drugs, at the early stages of the disease notably aim to prevent the onset or progression of fibrotic manifestations.4 This early therapeutic strategy represents the cornerstone of disease management, particularly in patients at high risk of progression.5 However, earlier stages of the disease may constitute an even more efficient window of opportunity. Mirroring the nosology of the earliest phases of other rheumatic diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis,6 the following stages of SSc onset and related populations can be identified: individuals at risk (ie, patients with risk factors for SSc); individuals with pre-SSc (ie, patients with SSc risk factors and some SSc-related signs and symptoms, who are also included among at-risk or high-risk individuals, although a formal consensus definition of this patient population is still needed); patients with a very early diagnosis of systemic sclerosis (VEDOSS) (ie, patients with definite SSc diagnosed at a very early stage, before the onset of any fibrotic manifestations) and more advanced SSc, as defined by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) classification criteria (which encompass a broader spectrum of clinical manifestations, ranging from early disease to more advanced disease with fibrotic manifestations such as interstitial lung disease (ILD) or fixed sclerodactyly, which are absent in pre-SSc or VEDOSS). This nosological framework of SSc onset has recently been summarised and discussed, underscoring the need for clear and standardised definitions of each stage.7

The VEDOSS criteria were created for the diagnosis of SSc at a very early stage, ‘to guide the physician in practice towards a definite diagnosis of SSc’.8 In this perspective, the methods section of the first consensus publication highlighted that the selected core set items were ‘essential’ (ie, necessary) to permit the diagnosis of ‘definite SSc’ in this context.8 Therefore, the proposed strategy to retain the diagnosis of SSc at a very early stage in this initial consensus proposal included Raynaud’s phenomenon (RP) and puffy fingers (PF) and antinuclear antibody specificity (ANA) as necessary step 1 red flag criteria and capillaroscopy and/or SSc-specific autoantibodies as ‘necessary tools to confirm the suspicion of very early SSc’ (online supplemental table 1).8 These step 1 and step 2 criteria were finally validated in 2021, using a definition where the three necessary step 1 criteria were RP, PF and ANA and the two step 2 criteria were mutually exclusive for VEDOSS, that is, a step 2 including ‘SSc capillaroscopic pattern’ OR ‘SSc-specific autoantibodies’. This combination of criteria had the strongest association with progression to SSc fulfilling the 2013 ACR/EULAR classification criteria (online supplemental table 2) validating this VEDOSS diagnostic strategy.9 10 Despite this clearly defined aim of the VEDOSS strategy, several follow-up original studies have applied the VEDOSS criteria inconsistently, using them to identify patients at a predisease stage and not to identify patients with diagnosed SSc at a very early stage.11 This unintended use of the VEDOSS criteria may have contributed to a conceptual overlap and consequent confusion between VEDOSS and the entity that could more appropriately be defined as pre-SSc.12

Considering the growing interest in the concept of prediseases in the field of autoimmunity,13 14 a consensus definition of pre-SSc will be needed in the future, to successfully implement therapeutic strategies to prevent the onset of ‘definite SSc’, either as diagnosed based on the VEDOSS criteria or as defined by the ACR/EULAR classification criteria.12 Pre-SSc would therefore identify patients at risk of SSc, with some early signs and symptoms of SSc that may benefit from adapted therapeutic strategies to prevent or slow disease onset and related irreversible damages, as recently discussed in several viewpoints.7 12 As a preliminary work for a consensus definition of pre-SSc, a first step is to register the existing definitions of pre-SSc in original articles and also, considering the confusion between VEDOSS and pre-SSc, to identify the variations of the VEDOSS and pre-SSc definitions that have been used in original articles.

The objective of this scoping review was to identify the existing definitions of pre-SSc and VEDOSS as applied in original articles, to identify the various combinations of items used in these definitions and to capture the variations of these potential heterogeneous concepts and the related milestone publications used to support these definitions and combination of items. Additional aims of this scoping review included the description of clinical characteristics of the patients identified as pre-SSc and VEDOSS in original articles from the literature.

Methods

The planned scoping review was based on the methodological framework proposed by Arksey and O’Malley15 and further developed by Levac et al.16 Scoping reviews are specifically used to identify key factors and/or characteristics related to a concept, in this case, VEDOSS and pre-SSc, and to examine how research is conducted within a particular topic or field, in this case, how VEDOSS and pre-SSc have been defined in original articles. This scoping review article follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) Extension for Scoping Reviews checklist and guidelines. The protocol of this scoping review was registered on the ‘Open Science Framework’ prior to starting the review (https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/MURD9).

Conceptual framework and key concepts

The VEDOSS criteria were created to diagnose patients with definite SSc at a very early stage,8 whereas pre-SSc could be defined as a precondition preceding the onset of definite disease, that is, patients at risk of SSc.12 Derived definitions have been used in original articles creating variations or heterogeneity within the concept of VEDOSS and blurring the borders between VEDOSS and pre-SSc. Despite VEDOSS being considered as a definite SSc at a very early stage, many studies distinguished ‘definite SSc’ from VEDOSS. For consistency with included articles using these designations, the term ‘definite SSc’ was also used in the ‘Results’ section of this scoping review to differentiate more advanced SSc fulfilling classification criteria (ie, ACR 1980,17 ACR/EULAR 2013,18 LeRoy and Medsger 200119) from VEDOSS or pre-SSc.

Publication dates and time period

The initial consensus definition of VEDOSS based on a Delphi exercise was published in 2011,8 original articles published since this date will be considered for VEDOSS and no time limit was applied for pre-SSc.

Scoping review questions

Main question: which combinations of items have been used to define VEDOSS and pre-SSc in original articles?

Secondary questions: what are the milestone articles supporting the definition of pre-SSc and VEDOSS in original articles? What are the clinical characteristics of the patients identified as pre-SSc and VEDOSS in original articles?

Search strategy

Information sources

Electronic databases: PubMed (Medline), Web of Science.

Search terms

Extraction date: 19 May 2025.

Key word: VEDOSS OR ‘very early diagnosis of systemic sclerosis’ OR ‘pre-scleroderma’ OR ‘pre-systemic sclerosis’ OR ‘pre-SSc’.

Inclusion criteria based on the PICOS strategy

Population: patients referred in original articles as VEDOSS or pre-SSc/pre-scleroderma.

Intervention: any kind of intervention for clinical trials/non-observational studies will be considered.

Comparison: definite SSc or any other comparators.

Outcomes: items used for the definitions of VEDOSS or pre-SSc.

Study types: observational analytical cross-sectional or longitudinal studies, case-control studies, prospective and retrospective cohort studies, randomised controlled trials, non-randomised controlled trials, before and after studies. Biomarker studies will be included.

Exclusion criteria

Population: studies only focusing on early SSc or definite SSc.

Study types: reviews (including meta-analyses and systematic reviews), narrative reviews, conference abstracts, biographies, case reports, comments, editorials, directories, interviews, lectures, legal cases, legislation, letters, news articles, newspaper articles, patient education handouts, popular works, congresses, consensus development conferences and practice guidelines.

Article selection and data extraction

The strategy followed for this scoping review was conducted in accordance with the approach approved for previous systematic/scoping reviews published by the team.20,23 Abstract screening was performed using ‘Rayyan’ software. AL and SB-R screened citations and reviewed abstracts for eligibility and inclusion based on the eligibility criteria. Any disagreements between reviewers were reviewed and resolved after discussion. AL and SB-R independently screened 10% of the full texts, discussed discrepancies and subsequently AL completed the full-text screening. Once completed, SB-R reviewed the list of all included full text for consistency. After final full-text selection, the data extraction was performed by AL according to a predefined data extraction template validated by the team. SB-R evaluated 10% of extracted articles for consistency. AL completed the data extraction and SB-R subsequently reviewed all extracted data to ensure accuracy. Although quality appraisal is typically necessary for systematic literature reviews and meta-analyses using evaluation tools such as the Jadad scale,24 the Cochrane risk of bias tool25 or the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for non-randomised studies,26 our focus was to conduct a scoping review to identify existing definitions of VEDOSS and pre-SSc as applied in original articles, regardless of study quality. As such, quality appraisal was not conducted.

Results

Included articles

The flow chart of included studies is provided in figure 1. AL and SB-R screened 106 abstracts after removal of duplicates. The level of agreement was 92.5%. Forty-six full-text articles were evaluated and 40 were kept for data extraction (figure 1).

The majority of articles (80%) were from Europe, Italy being the most represented country (37.5% of all articles). North America was under-represented with only one article (table 1). Although the majority of articles referred to the consensus publication for the VEDOSS criteria (62.5%, table 2), the exact definition, based on the step 1 essential items (RP and PF and ANA) and step 2 confirmation tools (SSc-specific autoantibodies and/or scleroderma landscape on capillaroscopy), was only applied in 31.3% of the articles. Twelve other combinations of the VEDOSS criteria were used to define patients with ‘VEDOSS’, demonstrating the inconsistency in the application of these criteria despite the existence of a consensus publication. Nine articles (28.1%) did not provide sufficient details to verify how the VEDOSS criteria were applied (table 3). Aggregated characteristics of the patients included in the studies

The median characteristics of the patients included in the studies are provided in online supplemental table 4. In patients with pre-SSc, the median proportion of females was 100% (range 94.6%–100%), whereas it was 93% (range 36.7%–100%) in VEDOSS and 89.9% (range 42.3%–100%) in ‘definite SSc’. Anticentromere antibodies (ACA) were the most represented autoantibodies in pre-SSc and in VEDOSS (64.5% (51.4–77.8) and 52.4% (40.0–80.0), respectively). Although the majority of patients with VEDOSS did not fulfil the 2013 ACR/EULAR criteria, one study reported that 44.4% of the patients with VEDOSS fulfilled these criteria.27

Although patients with pre-SSc and VEDOSS are not expected to present with SSc-related visceral complications, several articles reported some internal organ involvement or SSc-related complications in these populations (online supplemental table 5). ILD was reported in up to 35.5% of the patients with VEDOSS or pre-SSc. The median prevalence of oesophageal involvement in these populations was 18.9% (0.0–54.5) and anorectal dysfunction was reported with a median prevalence of 7.6% (5.0–10.2). The median prevalence of digital ulcers (DU) was 22.5% (0.0–27.0) in pre-SSc and 3.6% (0.0–23.6) in VEDOSS. On the contrary, arthralgia was reported in 27.8% (0.0–55.6) of patients with VEDOSS and 2.8% of patients with pre-SSc (one article with available data in this population). PF was only reported in 24.2% (8.8–44.4) of patients with VEDOSS, despite being one of the ‘essential’ step 1 items. Telangiectasia was reported in 9.4% and 12.9% of patients with pre-SSc and VEDOSS, respectively.

Discussion

This scoping review on pre-SSc and VEDOSS is the first comprehensive overview of pre-SSc and VEDOSS as reported in original articles. Our work demonstrates that the main reference for the definition of pre-SSc was the publication from Leroy and Medsger on ‘early limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis (lSSc)’ from 2001,19 although in this publication this nosological concept is never designated as ‘prescleroderma/pre-SSc’. This approach is therefore an extrapolation of the definition of ‘early lSSc’ from Leroy and Medsger. In addition to this derived definition of pre-SSc based on ‘early lSSc’, two other definitions were used (table 3). Therefore, three definitions of pre-SSc were applied in the literature, extracted from eight original articles. Moreover, our work showed that in 32 original articles, 13 different variations of the VEDOSS criteria were used to select patients with VEDOSS demonstrating that the formal and validated definition provided by the 2011 consensus VEDOSS criteria—further validated in 2021—was not followed.8 Although the concept of predisease or very early disease state is supposed to designate patients before the onset of severe fibrotic manifestations of the disease, several publications report the existence of fibrotic internal involvement, such as ILD, which could be considered an exclusion criterion to fulfil the definitions of pre-SSc or VEDOSS.

To date, there is no consensus definition of pre-SSc, based on a formal consensus exercise.12 This might explain why the proposal by LeRoy and Medsger of ‘early lSSc’ served as the basis for the inclusion of patients with ‘pre-SSc’ in original articles. After the 2011 publication on VEDOSS, the concept of ‘pre-SSc’ was progressively abandoned, in parallel with the increasing use of the concept of VEDOSS (figure 2). This trend in the use of these concepts suggests that ‘pre-SSc’ has been replaced by VEDOSS in original articles on SSc, although VEDOSS was created to diagnose patients with SSc at a very early stage and is not a precondition or does not designate patients ‘at risk’ of SSc.8 When considering the disease course, pre-SSc or at-risk patients should precede the diagnosis of VEDOSS. In the absence of a consensus definition, the concept of ‘early lSSc’ was used to define ‘pre-SSc’, which created a conceptual conflict, as this use implied that ‘pre-SSc’—that is, ‘early lSSc’—would precede and would be earlier than ‘very early SSc’. This paradoxical situation may have participated in blurring the boundary between pre-SSc and VEDOSS.11 The numerous variations of combinations of VEDOSS criteria might also be explained as authors may have used an incomplete validation of the VEDOSS criteria (only one or two of the three essential step 1 parameters and one of the two step 2, and several combinations of all these criteria) as an attempt to define a ‘precondition’, considering the absence of consensus definition of pre-SSc.

Another explanation for the variable application of the VEDOSS criteria in the literature may derive from one of the seminal publications on VEDOSS from 2009,28 which initially suggested that ‘early SSc could be defined as a state characterised by RP, puffy fingers, disease-specific autoantibodies and pathognomonic microvascular alteration detected by capillaroscopy requiring at least two, or better, all three items to be present’. This proposal was also mentioned in the 2011 consensus publication as ‘a definition of ‘very early SSc’ (…) characterised by Raynaud’s phenomenon, puffy fingers, disease-specific autoantibodies and pathognomonic microvascular alteration detected by capillaroscopy (requiring at least two, or better, all four items to be present)’.8 These discussion points may have been interpreted as the standard definition for VEDOSS, whereas they were only initial discussion points that were finally replaced by the two-step approach validated by the 2011 consensus exercise followed by the 2021 validation publication.9 Interestingly, the 2009 publication nonetheless made a clear distinction between ‘prescleroderma’ and VEDOSS, as VEDOSS were proposed to ‘fill the gap between what has been previously defined as ‘pre-scleroderma’(…) and what is overt SSc’. Therefore, this initial publication confirmed that VEDOSS was not intended to serve as a definition of a precondition.8 28

Our scoping review also highlights that there is no clear consensus on which autoantibodies should be used for the definitions of pre-SSc and VEDOSS, despite these being clearly identified in available milestone publications. In the 2001 publication from LeRoy and Medsger,19 SSc-related autoantibodies included ‘anticentromere, antitopoisomerase I, antifibrillarin, anti-PM-Scl, antifibrillin or anti-RNA polymerase I or III in a titre of 1:100 or higher’. In the 2011 VEDOSS publication, the consensus exercise only selected ACA and antitopoisomerase I antibodies.8 This selection could be largely explained by the lack of reliable or largely available assays for several SSc-related autoantibodies (eg, antifibrillarin or anti-RNA polymerase III) in the early 2000s and 2010s. Anti-RNA polymerase III antibodies were not selected in the Delphi exercise for VEDOSS due to the lack of available assays at the time, as commercial enzyme immunoassays were developed in 2010 and became available in several countries only after 2012.29 30 In 2013, the ACR/EULAR classification criteria included ACA, ATA and RNA polymerase III among the SSc-specific autoantibodies, notably based on the availability of the assays and the specific association of these antibodies with SSc.18 Based on these classification criteria, anti-RNA polymerase III was included in several VEDOSS publications. The reason why other SSc-autoantibodies designated in the 2001 LeRoy and Medsger publication were left aside in the past 25 years may be explained by the influence of the ACR/EULAR classification criteria and the various reliability and availability of the commercial assays for antibody testing.12 30

In the majority of the VEDOSS and pre-SSc publications, patients had no visceral complications (since the median prevalence of ILD, PAH or SRC was 0 or close to 0% in the included articles). Nonetheless, several articles still report some of these visceral manifestations, in addition to other fibrotic complications such as skin fibrosis (median prevalence of 9.6% in VEDOSS publications) or ischaemic complications such as DU (median prevalence of 22.5% in pre-SSc publications, and 3.6% in VEDOSS publications). The presence of such SSc-related complications is in contrast with the initial proposal for these concepts,28 that is, the selection of the patients at a very early or precondition state, before the visceral and fibrotic complications occur. This result suggests that a significant effort is still needed to clarify the nature of these nosological concepts and the reason they were created. Nonetheless, some of these studies were initiated before the publication of the ACR/EULAR 2013 criteria, and therefore, at that time—in the absence of, or while awaiting, new validated classification criteria—a nosological inconsistency may have existed. Two studies from the Spanish registry also mention the presence of DU in patients with ‘pre-SSc’, but the definition of ‘pre-SSc’ in this registry was based on ‘a modification of the classification proposed by LeRoy and Medsger’.31 Such a modification may have led to a nosological shift that was not in line with the initial intention of LeRoy and Medsger, who aimed at describing ‘definite’ SSc at an ‘early stage’ (with potential early organ involvement) rather than a ‘precondition’ corresponding to ‘pre-SSc’. Therefore, future consensus definitions of ‘pre-SSc’ or ‘at-risk populations’ should explicitly state that the absence of SSc-related fibrotic complications (ie, potentially non-reversible complications) and the absence of severe impactful manifestations (such as DU) is a key aspect of defining a predisease or at-risk population, in order to limit future misconceptions.

Our study has several limitations: only two databases were screened and we may have thus missed some publications related to the nosological concepts of interest. Only the word pre-SSc or pre-Scleroderma and VEDOSS were screened. Therefore, some related nosological concepts, such as ‘early lSSc’ or ‘at risk of SSc’, may have been missed, but this was beyond the scope of the review, as our objective was to explore the applied definitions of pre-SSc/prescleroderma or VEDOSS, and no other nosological concepts related to SSc.

Our study has several strengths: it was based on a validated approach using a predefined protocol available in open access, conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines, and led by researchers experienced in scoping and systematic review methodologies. Several authors from the seminal VEDOSS and pre-SSc publications were among the coauthors, ensuring the adapted interpretation of the different concepts and their use throughout the past 30 years.

Conclusion

This scoping review highlights that the terms VEDOSS and pre-SSc are variably defined in the literature, and that the absence of a consensus definition for pre-SSc has contributed to blurring the boundary between these nosological concepts. In the era of preventive medicine at a precondition state, our work supports the view that a consensus definition of pre-SSc or at-risk population is urgently needed. Therefore, a consensus effort from the community and related bodies is warranted to collectively select the core set items to be included in a future definition of pre-SSc. The recent international initiative—Establishing a Consensus Definition for Pre-Systemic Sclerosis—under the aegis of the World Scleroderma Foundation aims to address this gap by proposing a consensus definition for pre-SSc, that is, enabling the identification of at-risk patients for implementation in future preventive trials.

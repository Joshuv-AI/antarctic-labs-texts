Zhen-Qing Chen and Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate Harnack estimates for weak solutions to the following nonlocal equation: ∂ t u = Δ α / 2 ​ u + b ⋅ ∇ u + f , \partial_{t}u=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla u+f, where Δ α / 2 \Delta^{\alpha/2} denotes the fractional Laplacian, b b is a divergence-free vector field in a critical or supercritical regularity regime, and f f is a distribution in a fractional Sobolev space with negative indices. As applications of the analytical results obtained in this paper, we establish the well-posedness of critical stochastic quasi-geostrophic equations driven by additive Brownian noise, prove the existence of weak solutions to the two-dimensional fractional Navier–Stokes equations with measure-valued initial vorticity, and demonstrate the well-posedness of generalized martingale problems associated with critical stochastic differential equations. Keywords : Harnack inequality, Nonlocal equations, Stochastic quasi-geostrophic equations, Fractional 2D-Navier-Stokes equation, Critical SDEs AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 60H10, 35R09; Secondary: 60G51



1.
Introduction

In the celebrated work
[
19
]
, DiPerna and Lions established the existence, uniqueness, and stability of renormalized solutions to the transport equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\partial_{t}u=b\cdot\nabla u\quad\hbox{with }\ u(0)=\varphi,

where
b\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

has bounded divergence and
\varphi
is a finite measurable function.
As a key application, they proved the well-posedness, in the Lebesgue sense,
of the ordinary differential equation

\dot{X}_{t}(x)=b(t,X_{t}(x))\quad\hbox{with }\ X_{0}(x)=x.

These results have been subsequently extended in several directions.
Ambrosio
[
1
]
generalized them to the case where the drift
b
is of bounded variation, while the second author
[
53
]
treated stochastic differential equations.

It is well known that noise can exert a regularizing effect on irregular ODEs. For instance, consider the stochastic differential equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

driven by a Brownian motion
B
:

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}B_{2t},\quad X_{0}=x.

Even when
b
is merely bounded and measurable or possesses singularities,
there exists a unique strong solution for every initial point
x
(see
[
52
,
32
]
etc.).
This phenomenon is intimately connected to the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the second-order parabolic equation:

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u+f,\quad u(0)=\varphi,

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian, which is the infinitesimal generator of the Brownian motion
B_{2t}
.

In this paper, we are interested in the
regularization phenomenon
under the influence of
\alpha
-stable noises in the presence of a divergence-free drift
b
.
Here, the parameter
\alpha
ranges over
(0,2)
, where smaller values of
\alpha
correspond to weaker noise.
More precisely, we study the following fractional diffusion transport equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla u+f,\quad u(0)=\varphi,

(1.1)

as well as the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) driven by an isotropic
\alpha
-stable process:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}L^{(\alpha)}_{t},\quad X_{0}=x,

(1.2)

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\Delta^{\alpha/2}:=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is the fractional Laplacian
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is also
the infinitesimal generator of the

d
-dimensional
isotropic
\alpha
-stable process
L^{(\alpha)}
.

When
\alpha\in(1,2)
, the drift term can be controlled by the nonlocal diffusion term

\Delta^{\alpha/2}
,
and the regularity of the PDE (
1.1
) has been extensively studied via Duhamel’s representation (see, for example,
[
54
]
). This regime is referred to as the subcritical case.
When
\alpha=1
,
the drift term
b\cdot\nabla
and the fractional Laplacian
\Delta^{1/2}
are of the same order,
leading to the critical case.
For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the drift term is of higher order than the fractional Laplacian
\Delta^{\alpha/2}
,
and this is termed the supercritical case (see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
for studies related to the SDE (
1.2
)).

In the subcritical case
\alpha\in(1,2)
with
b
being
\beta
-Hölder continuous where
\beta\in(1-\frac{\alpha}{2},1)
, strong well-posedness of the SDE (
1.2
) was established in
[
43
]
(see also
[
54
]
).
More recently, weak well-posedness for all
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\beta
-Hölder continuous
b
with
\beta\in((1-\alpha)^{+}\vee 0,1]
was shown in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.
Two-sided heat kernel estimates for SDE (
1.2
) were also obtained in
[
39
]
.
We also mention that in
[
2
]
, the authors studied strong well-posedness for
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
b\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\beta}
with
\beta>\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
.
An important open question on the weak well-posedness in the critical case still remains
where
\beta=1-\alpha
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We now perform a scaling analysis. Let
u=u(t,x)
be a solution to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
. For
\lambda>0
, define

u_{\lambda}(t,x):=u(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x),\quad b_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{\alpha-1}b(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x),\quad f_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{\alpha}f(\lambda^{\alpha}t,\lambda x).

Then

\partial_{t}u_{\lambda}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u_{\lambda}+b_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla u_{\lambda}+f_{\lambda}.

We introduce the space-time norm: for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{q/p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q}

with the standard modification when
q=\infty
or
p=\infty
.
Notice that by the change of variables,

\|b_{\lambda}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}=\lambda^{\alpha-1-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{q}}\|b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}

and

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}=\lambda^{\alpha-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{q}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}.

For the drift
b
, according to the sign of
\alpha-1-\frac{d}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{q}
, we
make the following classification:

(i)

(Subcritical case):
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{q}<\alpha-1
.

(ii)

(Critical case):
\alpha\in[1,2)
and
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{q}=\alpha-1
.

(iii)

(Supercritical case):
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\alpha-1<\frac{d}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{q}<\alpha
.

Since the critical and supercritical cases are of special interest in physical models, this paper focuses primarily on studying the well-posedness of the PDE (
1.1
)
and the associated SDEs (
1.2
) in these regimes. We discuss them in the following subsections.

1.1.
Stochastic quasi-geostrophic equations

The 2D quasi-geostrophic equation is an important model in fluid dynamics that describes the evolution of geophysical fluids. It takes the following form:

\partial_{t}\theta=\mathcal{R}\theta\cdot\nabla\theta,\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0},

where
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
represents the temperature and

\mathcal{R}\theta:=(-\partial_{2}\Delta^{-1/2}\theta,\partial_{1}\Delta^{-1/2}\theta)=:(-\mathcal{R}_{2}\theta,\mathcal{R}_{1}\theta),

with
\mathcal{R}_{j}
denoting the
j
-th Riesz transform. The dissipative quasi-geostrophic equation is given by

\partial_{t}\theta=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\theta+\mathcal{R}\theta\cdot\nabla\theta,\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0},

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
. It is well known that for
\alpha>1
(subcritical case), the above initial value problem has a global
C^{\infty}
-solution for any smooth initial value
\theta_{0}
(see
[
15
,
44
]
).
The critical case
\alpha=1
, which arises in geophysical studies of strongly rotating fluid flows (cf.
[
13
]
), is solved by Kiselev-Nazarov-Volberg
[
30
]
and Caffarelli-Vasseur
[
8
]
using completely different methods.

On the other hand, Röckner, Zhu and Zhu
[
46
]
considered the following stochastic fractionally dissipative quasi-geostrophic equation on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\theta=(\Delta^{\alpha/2}\theta+\mathcal{R}\theta\cdot\nabla\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+G(\theta(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0}.

(1.3)

Therein, the existence of martingale solutions for all
\alpha\in(0,2)
and the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions and invariant measures in the subcritical case
\alpha\in(1,2)
are established.
Later, their results were extended to the whole space and more general cases by Brzeźniak and Motyl
[
6
]
.
However, the well-posedness of SPDE (
1.3
) in the critical case
\alpha=1
remains open.

We mention that Maekawa and Miura
[
36
,
37
]
studied the fundamental solution to the following nonlocal equation:

\partial_{t}u=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla u,

where
b
is divergence-free and
\alpha\in(0,2)
. The existence and continuity of fundamental solutions, as well as pointwise upper bound estimates, are obtained when
b
has critical regularity.
Moreover, a priori Hölder regularity for the above advection fractional-diffusion equations was studied in

[
16
]
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and in
[
18
]
for
\alpha\in(1,2)
.
Their methods are based on De-Giorgi’s iteration and Caffarelli-Silvestre’s extension theorem
[
7
]

for the fractional Laplacian operator
\Delta^{\alpha/2}
.

1.2.
Fractional Navier-Stokes equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Consider the following two-dimensional fractional Navier-Stokes equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla p\quad\text{with}\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,

(1.4)

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
denotes the velocity field of the fluid,
p
denotes the pressure, and
\alpha\in(0,2)
.
Here,
\Delta^{\alpha/2}:=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is the fractional Laplacian of order
\alpha
.
It is known that if the initial velocity
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is smooth, then there exists a unique smooth global solution to (
1.4
) (see
[
55
]
).

Taking
curl on
both sides of (
1.4
), we find that the vorticity
\rho:=\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{u}:=\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}
satisfies

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\rho=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}).

Moreover, by the Biot–Savart law (cf.
[
38
]
),

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{2}(x-y)\rho(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=:(K_{2}*\rho)(t,x),

where

\displaystyle K_{2}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{-x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\right).

(1.5)

Hence,
\rho
satisfies the following nonlinear integro-differential equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\cdot(K_{2}*\rho)).

(1.6)

Suppose that
\rho(0,x)\geqslant 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
.
By the maximum principle and integrating both sides of (
1.6
) with respect to
x
, we find that for all
t>0
,

\rho(t,x)\geqslant 0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1.

This implies that
\{\rho(t,\cdot)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is a family of probability
density functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
By the superposition principle
[
45
]
, if (
1.6
) admits a density solution
\rho
,
the following McKean–Vlasov SDE
admits a solution
X_{t}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{2}(X_{t}-y)\rho(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}L^{(\alpha)}_{t}.

(1.7)

Note that the kernel
K_{2}
is singular
at the origin.

When
\alpha=2
and the initial vorticity is a finite Radon measure, the existence of solutions to the PDE (
1.6
) was established by Giga, Miyakawa, and Osada in
[
24
]
, and uniqueness was proved by Gallagher and Gallay in
[
22
]
. Their proofs rely heavily on heat kernel estimates for the operator
\Delta+b\cdot\nabla
when
b
is divergence-free and has a specific form (see
[
42
]
).
Recently, the existence of weak solutions to (
1.6
) with measure-valued initial data was obtained
in
[
58
]
by directly constructing weak solutions to (
1.7
). Some regularity results were also
established
there. One of the goals of this paper is to derive analogous results for the fractional vorticity equation (
1.6
) with
\alpha\in(1,2)
by studying the associated SDE (
1.7
).

1.3.
Main results

We consider the following nonlocal equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with lower regularity on
b
and
f
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u={\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}u+b\cdot\nabla u+f,

(1.8)

where

{\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x):={\rm p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f(x+y)-f(x))K(t,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

and

K(t,y):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to(0,\infty)

is a symmetric measurable function in
y
and satisfies

\displaystyle 0<\kappa_{0}|y|^{-d-\alpha}\leqslant K(t,y)\leqslant\kappa_{1}|y|^{-d-\alpha}.

(1.9)

Note that when
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{1}
, up to a multiplicative constant,
{\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}
is the standard fractional Laplacian

\Delta^{\alpha/2}:=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
.
By symmetry of
K(t,y)
in
y
, it is easy to see that

{\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f(x+y)+f(x-y)-2f(x))K(t,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

and for
f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x){\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=-{\mathcal{E}}_{t}^{(\alpha)}(f,g),

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{t}^{(\alpha)}(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))K(t,x-y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

(1.11)

Suppose that
g
has support in
B_{r}
. Then for any
R\geqslant r
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}_{t}^{(\alpha)}(f,g)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))K(t,x-y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\\
&\quad+\int_{B_{R}}\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}(f(x)-f(y))K(t,x-y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.\end{split}

(1.12)

We assume that

\displaystyle|b|+|\nabla b|\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\hbox{and}\quad f\mbox{ is a distribution}.

(1.13)

We first introduce the following notion of super- and sub- solutions.

Definition 1.1
.

A bounded measurable function

u(t,x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is called a super (resp. sub)-solution
of PDE (
1.8
)
if for any nonnegative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}u\partial_{t}\varphi\ \geqslant\hbox{ {\rm(resp. $\leqslant$)} }\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}u{\mathscr{L}}_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}(b\cdot\nabla\varphi+\varphi\,\mathord{{\rm div}}b)u+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}f\varphi.\end{split}

(1.14)

If
u
is both a super and sub-solution, then it is called a solution.

Remark 1.2
.

Under (
1.13
) and
the boundedness assumption on
u
,
each term in the right hand side of (
1.14
) is well defined.
Indeed, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d})
, we have
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u{\mathscr{L}}_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa_{1}\|u\|_{\infty}\left\|\int_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}}\frac{\varphi(\cdot+y)+\varphi(\cdot-y)-2\varphi}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}

\displaystyle+\kappa_{1}\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{\{|y|>1\}}\frac{\|\varphi(\cdot+y)+\varphi(\cdot-y)-2\varphi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\leqslant C<\infty.

To make sense of
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}\varphi\,\mathord{{\rm div}}b\,u
, it suffices to assume

|\mathord{{\rm div}}b|\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
However, we imposed a stronger condition

|\nabla b|\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
on
u

in Definition
1.1
because we later
need a commutator estimate (
3.4
)
involving
u

(see Lemma
3.1
below).

Remark 1.3
.

If
u
is a supper (resp. sub)-solution, then so is
u+\kappa
for any
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

To state our main results, we need some notations. For
r>0
and
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\displaystyle B_{r}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|x|\leqslant r\},\quad Q^{+}_{r}(t_{0}):=[t_{0},t_{0}+r]\times B_{r},\quad Q^{-}_{r}(t_{0}):=[t_{0}-r,t_{0}]\times B_{r},

(1.15)

and

\displaystyle Q_{r}(t_{0}):=Q^{+}_{r}(t_{0})\cup Q^{-}_{r}(t_{0})=[t_{0}-r,t_{0}+r]\times B_{r},\quad Q_{r}:=Q_{r}(0).

(1.16)

For a function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\displaystyle{\rm Tail}(g;r)(t):=\int_{B^{c}_{r}}\frac{|g(t,y)|}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

(1.17)

For
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\beta\in[0,\alpha/2)
, we introduce the following index set for later use:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{I}}^{\beta}_{\alpha}:=\Big\{(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty)^{d}:\ \tfrac{\alpha}{q}+|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|<\alpha-\beta\Big\}.

(1.18)

See (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) and (
2.7
) below for the notations of

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}
,
\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
,
|{\boldsymbol{p}}|
,
{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
and
\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{x}}
.
We consider the following condition on
b
.

(H
b
)

In addition to (
1.13
), we assume that for some
(q_{1},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}),(q_{2},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{0}_{\alpha}
,

\kappa_{b}:=\|b{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}+\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{2}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{x}}<\infty.

We will use the following parameter set:

\Theta:=(\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{b},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1},q_{1},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2},q_{2},d,\alpha).

Our first main result is the following local upper bound estimate of sub-solution to PDE (
1.8
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,

\beta\in[0,\alpha/2)
and
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathbb{I}}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Under (
1.9
) and

b
)
, for any

C=C(\Theta,\beta,q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0},p)>0
such that for any sub-solution

\|u^{+}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}(u^{+}\chi_{1}){\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\lesssim_{C}\|u^{+}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}+\|{\rm Tail}(u^{+};1){\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{t}}+\|\chi_{2}f{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}({\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})},

where

Remark 1.5
.

When

. It is important to note that

Next we consider the following condition.

(H

)

In addition to (

Our second main result is the following weak Harnack inequality for super-solutions of PDE (

).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and

(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathbb{I}}_{\alpha}^{0}
.
Under (
1.9
) and
(H
{}^{\prime}_{b}
)
, there are
p_{0}>0

C=C(\Theta,q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})>0

\displaystyle\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{3/2}^{+}(-2)}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{0}}}\lesssim_{C}{\rm inf}_{Q_{3/2}^{-}(2)}u+\|f{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{x}}+\|{\rm Tail}(u^{-},1){\mathbbm{1}}_{[-2,2]}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{t}}.

(1.19)

Moreover, for any solution

) in

\displaystyle{\rm sup}_{Q_{1}^{+}(-2)}u\lesssim_{C}{\rm inf}_{Q_{1}^{-}(2)}u+\|f{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{x}}+\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{[-4,4]}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}.

(1.20)

When

is bounded measurable, and

is replaced with
a symmetric nonlocal operator whose energy on each ball is uniformly comparable
to that of
\Delta^{\alpha/2}

Felsinger and Kassmann

Their results were later
extended to more general symmetric nonlocal operators in
[
48
]
. In a recent work
[
29
]
, Kassmann and Weidner showed a full Harnack inequality version with no tail term
for globally nonnegative caloric functions.
However, all the above results do not
cover the case of singular drift
b
nor the case of distributional source term
f
.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows.
Section 2 introduces the necessary function spaces, with particular emphasis on the De Giorgi and Moser classes, which play crucial roles in
our
subsequent analysis.
In Section 3, we establish an upper bound estimate for subsolutions of PDE (
1.8
) by verifying their membership in a suitable De Giorgi class.
Section 4 is devoted to proving a weak Harnack inequality for supersolutions of PDE (
1.8
) by demonstrating that they belong to an appropriate Moser class.
Section 5 addresses the global well-posedness and
L^{\infty}
-estimates for PDE (
1.8
) under supercritical conditions, as well as Hölder regularity in the critical case when
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
In Section 6, we apply the results from Section 5 to establish well-posedness for stochastic quasi-geostrophic equations.
Section 7 presents existence results for weak solutions to a class of supercritical distribution-dependent SDEs, including the two-dimensional fractional Navier-Stokes equations.
Finally, Section 8 is concerned with
the existence and uniqueness of generalized martingale solutions for critical linear SDEs.

We conclude this introduction by summarizing some notations used throughout the paper.

•

The symbol
:=
denotes a definition. For
c>0
, the notation
f\lesssim_{c}g
means
f\leqslant cg
.

•

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represents the dual pairing between distributions and test functions.

•

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}
,
a\wedge b:={\mathord{{\rm min}}}\{a,b\}
and
a^{+}:=a\vee 0
,

a^{-}:=(-a)\vee 0
.

2.1.
Function spaces

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For multi-index
{\boldsymbol{p}}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{N})\in(0,\infty]^{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}:=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})|^{p_{N}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{N}\right)^{\frac{p_{N-1}}{p_{N}}}\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z_{1}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}},

(2.1)

with the standard modification when
p_{i}=\infty
, for some
i=1,\cdots,N
,
which is also called the mixed
L^{\boldsymbol{p}}
-space (see
[
5
]
).
When
{\boldsymbol{p}}=(p,\cdots,p)\in(0,\infty]^{N}
,

{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}
is the usual
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
space and
we shall simply write it as
{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
.
(Note that when there is some
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
so that

p_{i}\in(0,1)
and
p\in(0,1)
,
{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}
and
L^{p}
are not Banach spaces
as in these cases the norms defined above do not satisfy the triangle inequality.
However, for most use later, these spaces are for indices larger than or equal 1.)
For multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}}\in(0,\infty]^{N}
, we shall use the following notations:

{\boldsymbol{p}}+{\boldsymbol{q}}:=(p_{1}+q_{1},\cdots,p_{N}+q_{N}),\ \ {\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}q_{i},

and

{\boldsymbol{p}}>{\boldsymbol{q}}\ \ (\hbox{\rm resp. }{\boldsymbol{p}}\geqslant{\boldsymbol{q}};\ {\boldsymbol{p}}={\boldsymbol{q}})\Longleftrightarrow p_{i}>q_{i}\ (\hbox{\rm resp. }p_{i}\geqslant q_{i};\ p_{i}=q_{i})\ \hbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,N.

Moreover, we use bold number to denote constant vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, for example,

\boldsymbol{1}=(1,\cdots,1),\ \ \boldsymbol{2}=(2,\cdots,2),

and also denote

|{\boldsymbol{p}}|:={\boldsymbol{p}}\cdot\boldsymbol{1},\quad\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}:=\big(\tfrac{1}{p_{1}},\cdots,\tfrac{1}{p_{N}}\big).

(2.2)

For any multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}}\in(0,\infty]^{N}
with
\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}=\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, the following Hölder’s inequality holds

\|fg\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{q}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}}.

For any multi-indices
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}}\in[1,\infty]^{N}
with
\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}=\boldsymbol{1}+\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, the following Young’s inequality holds

\|f*g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{q}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\|g\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}}.

For
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in(1,\infty)^{N}
, we define the following Bessel potential space

{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}:=(I-\Delta)^{-\beta/2}({\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}).

(2.3)

The dual space of
{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
is

{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, where
\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=\bf 1
.
For
\beta\in(0,2)
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in(1,\infty)^{N}
, by Hörmander’s multipliers theorem (see
[
12
, Corollary 3.1]
), an equivalent norm of
{\mathbf{H}}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}^{\beta}
is given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}}:=\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\|\Delta^{\beta/2}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}.

(2.4)

We have the following interpolation inequality of Gagliado-Nirenberge’s type.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}}\in(1,\infty)^{N}
and

s,s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\theta\in[0,1]
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}\leqslant\tfrac{1-\theta}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}+\tfrac{\theta}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad s-|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|=(1-\theta)\big(s_{0}-|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|\big)+\theta\big(s_{1}-|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}|\big).

(2.5)

Then there is a constant
C=C({\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{q}},{\boldsymbol{r}},s,s_{0},s_{1},\theta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{s}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}}\lesssim_{C}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{{\mathbf{H}}^{s_{0}}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}}\|f\|^{\theta}_{{\mathbf{H}}^{s_{1}}_{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}.

(2.6)

Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}={\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}
be the space-time space.
For a function
f(t,x)
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}
and
\nu\in(0,\infty]
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in(0,\infty]^{d}
,
we define

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\nu}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{x}}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)^{\nu}dt\right)^{1/\nu}\quad&\hbox{if }0<\nu<\infty,\\
{\rm esssup}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}&\hbox{if }\nu=\infty.\end{cases}

(2.7)

For
\alpha\in(0,2)
, we introduce the following energy space for later use:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{V}}_{\alpha}:=\Big\{f(t,x):\,\|f\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{\alpha}}:=\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}+\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}<\infty\Big\}.

(2.8)

We have the following Sobolev’s type inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
. For
(\nu,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in[2,\infty)^{1+d}
with
\frac{\alpha}{\nu}+|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}|=\tfrac{d}{2}
, it holds that

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\nu}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}_{x}}\lesssim_{C}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}+\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{x}}\lesssim_{C}\|u\|_{{\mathscr{V}}_{\alpha}},

where
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in[2,\infty)^{d}
is defined by
\frac{2}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\frac{\nu-2}{{\boldsymbol{2}}}=\frac{\nu}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}
and
C=C(d,\alpha,\nu,{\boldsymbol{r}})>0
.

Proof.

Let
\theta=\frac{2}{\nu}
. Then
\frac{\theta}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}+\frac{1-\theta}{{\boldsymbol{2}}}=\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{r}}}
. By Hölder’s inequality we have

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}_{x}}\leqslant\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{x}}^{\theta}.

Hence,

\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\nu}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{r}}_{x}}\lesssim_{C}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}\|u\|^{\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{x}}.

which in turn gives the first inequality by Young’s inequality. Since
|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|=\frac{d-\alpha}{2}
by the assumption, we have by (
2.6
)
with
s=\theta=0
and
s_{0}=\alpha/2,q=2
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\alpha/2}_{2}}\asymp\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}+\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}.

(2.9)

The second inequality follows.
∎

We also have the following Sobolev inequality in balls.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in[2,\infty)^{d}
with

|\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|=\frac{d-\alpha}{2}
,
there is a constant

C=C(d,{\boldsymbol{p}},\alpha)>0
such that for any
0<r<R<\infty

and
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r})
,

\displaystyle\|f\eta\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\lesssim_{C}\left(1+\frac{R^{d}}{(R-r)^{d+\alpha}}\right)\|f\eta\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\left(\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{((f\eta)(x)-(f\eta)(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right)^{1/2}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r})
. By (
2.9
) we have

\displaystyle\|f\eta\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\lesssim\|f\eta\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}(f\eta)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}.

By (
1.10
) and (
1.12
) we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}(f\eta)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{((f\eta)(x)-(f\eta)(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{((f\eta)(x)-(f\eta)(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}}(f\eta)^{2}(x)\left(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Noting that for
|x|\leqslant r
and
|y|\geqslant R
,

|x-y|^{-d-\alpha}\leqslant(1-\tfrac{r}{R})^{-d-\alpha}|y|^{-d-\alpha},

we have

\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\leqslant(1-\tfrac{r}{R})^{-d-\alpha}\int_{|y|\geqslant R}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}=\frac{R^{d}}{(R-r)^{d+\alpha}}\int_{|y|\geqslant 1}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}{|y|^{d+\alpha}}.

The desired estimate now follows.
∎

2.2.
De Giorgi and Moser classes

Throughout this subsection, we shall fix an open multi-index set

{\mathscr{I}}\subset(1,\infty)^{N}
and
Q:=(Q_{\tau})_{\tau\in[1,2]}
an increasing family of bounded open sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

with

\displaystyle\cap_{\sigma>\tau}Q_{\sigma}=\bar{Q}_{\tau}.

(2.11)

For a set
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, we use
{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q}
to denote the indicator function of
Q
.

The following De Giorgi class associated with
{\mathscr{I}}
and
Q
is introduced in
[
57
]
.

Definition 2.4

(De Giorgi class)
.

We say a function
u\in L^{1}(Q_{2})
is
in the De Giorgi class
{\mathcal{D}}{\mathcal{G}}^{+}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)

if there are
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}\in{\mathscr{I}}
,
i=1,\cdots,m
, some
j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}

and
\gamma,{\mathcal{A}}\geqslant 0
such that for any
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in{\mathscr{I}}
, there is a constant

C_{\boldsymbol{p}}=C_{\boldsymbol{p}}(Q)>0
such that
for any
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
\kappa\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle(\sigma-\tau)^{\gamma}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}(u-\kappa)^{+}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\lesssim_{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}(u-\kappa)^{+}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}+{\mathcal{A}}\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{u>\kappa\}\cap Q_{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}.

(2.12)

The following result is essentially due to De Giorgi and proven in
[
57
, Theorem 2.10]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
u\in{\mathcal{D}}{\mathcal{G}}^{+}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
and
p>0
, there is a constant
C_{0}>0

depending only on
Q
,
p
,
N
and the quantities in the definition of
{\mathcal{D}}{\mathcal{G}}^{+}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
such that

\displaystyle\|u^{+}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{1}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{C_{0}}\|u^{+}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}+{\mathcal{A}}.

(2.13)

The following lemma can be found in
[
47
, Lemma 2.2.6]
, which will be used to show the weak Harnack inequality.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure over
Q_{2}
and
C_{0},\gamma>0
,
q\in(0,\infty]
.
Let
h
be a non-negative measurable function on
Q_{2}
. Suppose that for all
p\in(0,1\wedge\tfrac{q}{2})
and
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\|h{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}(\mu)}\leqslant\left(C_{0}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}\right)^{1/p}\|h{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}(\mu)},

(2.14)

and for all
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\mu\left(\big\{(t,x)\in Q_{2}:\log h(t,x)>\lambda\big\}\right)\leqslant C_{0}/\lambda.

(2.15)

Then for any
\tau\in[1,2)
, there is a constant
C=C(C_{0},\tau,\gamma,q)>0
such that

\|h{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}(\mu)}\leqslant C.

To check condition (
2.14
), we introduce the following Moser class for later use.

Definition 2.7

(Moser class)
.

Let
q_{0}\in(0,\infty]
.
We say a nonnegative function
u\in L^{\infty}(Q_{2})
is
in the Moser class
{\mathcal{M}}^{q_{0}}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)

if there are
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}\in{\mathscr{I}}
,
i=1,\cdots,m
,
\gamma\geqslant 0
and
\beta\geqslant 0

such that for any
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in{\mathscr{I}}
, there is a constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}>0
such that
for any
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
q\in(0,q_{0})
,

\displaystyle(\sigma-\tau)^{\gamma}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}u^{q}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\boldsymbol{p}}}\lesssim_{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}(q^{\beta}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u^{q}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}.

(2.16)

We have the following reverse Hölder inequality for
u\in{\mathcal{M}}^{q_{0}}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
.

Theorem 2.8
.

(i)

For any
u\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
, there are constants
\gamma_{1},C_{1}>0

depending only on
the quantities in Definition
2.7

such that
for all
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
p\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\leqslant(C_{1}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma_{1}})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}.

(2.17)

(ii)

For any
u\in{\mathcal{M}}^{q_{0}}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
with
0<q_{0}<\infty
,
there are positive constants
p_{0},\gamma_{2},C_{2}>0

depending only on
the quantities in Definition
2.7

such that
for all
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
p\in(0,1\wedge\frac{p_{0}}{2})
,

\displaystyle\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{0}}}\leqslant(C_{2}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma_{2}})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}.

(2.18)

Proof.

Suppose
u\in{\mathcal{M}}^{q_{0}}_{\mathscr{I}}(Q)
with
q_{0}\in(0,\infty]
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}\in{\mathscr{I}}
be as in Definition
2.7
.
Since
{\mathscr{I}}
is open, one can find
\theta>1

close to
1
such that
\theta{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}\in{\mathscr{I}}
for each
i=1,\cdots,m
.
Thus by (
2.16
), there are constants
C_{0}>0
and
\beta\geqslant 0

such that for all
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
q\in(0,q_{0})
,

\displaystyle(\sigma-\tau)^{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}u^{q}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\theta{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}\lesssim_{C_{0}}(q^{\beta}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u^{q}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}.

Since for
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m}\geqslant 0
,

a_{1}\vee a_{2}\vee\cdots\vee a_{m}\leqslant a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{m}\leqslant m(a_{1}\vee a_{2}\vee\cdots\vee a_{m}),

the above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\sigma-\tau)^{\gamma}\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}u\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q\theta{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}\leqslant mC_{0}(q^{\beta}+1)\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}.

In particular, if we set

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}(\tau,q):=\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}},

(2.19)

then for all
1\leqslant\tau<\sigma\leqslant 2
and
q\in(0,q_{0})
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}(\tau,\theta q)\leqslant((\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}mC_{0}(q^{\beta}+1))^{1/q}{\mathscr{M}}(\sigma,q).

(2.20)

(i) Suppose
q_{0}=\infty
. Fix
q\in(0,1)
, whose value will be determined below.
Let
\tau_{n}=\tau+2^{-n}(\sigma-\tau)
and
q_{n}:=\theta^{n}q
. Iterating (
2.20
) with

\tau=\tau_{n}
,
\sigma=\tau_{n-1}
and
q=q_{n-1}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}(\tau,q_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{n},q_{n})\leqslant\Big((\tau_{n-1}-\tau_{n})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}(q_{n-1}^{\beta}+1)\Big)^{1/q_{n-1}}{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{n-1},q_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big((\tau_{j-1}-\tau_{j})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}(q^{\beta}_{j-1}+1)\Big)^{1/q_{j-1}}{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{0},q),

where the first inequality is due to
Q_{\tau}\subset Q_{\tau_{n}}
.
Noting that

{\mathscr{M}}(\tau,\infty)=\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}},\ \ {\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{0},q)=\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}

and by
q\in(0,1)
and
\theta>1
,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big((\tau_{j-1}-\tau_{j})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}(q^{\beta}_{j-1}+1)\Big)^{1/q_{j-1}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big(2^{\gamma j}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}mC_{0}(\theta^{\beta(j-1)}q^{\beta}+1)\Big)^{\theta^{1-j}/q}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big(2^{\gamma j}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}2mC_{0}\theta^{\beta j}\Big)^{\theta^{1-j}/q}=\Big(\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\theta^{-j}(j\ln(2^{\gamma}\theta^{\beta})+\ln((\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}2mC_{0}))}\Big)^{\theta/q}

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\theta}{(\theta-1)^{2}}\ln(2^{\gamma}\theta^{\beta})+\frac{1}{\theta-1}\ln((\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}2mC_{0})}\Big)^{\theta/q}=\Big(C_{1}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\theta\gamma/(\theta-1)}\Big)^{1/q},

where
C_{1}=C(\theta,\gamma,\beta,m,C_{0})>0
,
we obtain that for any
q\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\leqslant\Big(C_{1}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\theta\gamma/(\theta-1)}\Big)^{1/q}\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}.

(2.21)

Let

\delta:=\max_{i,j}p_{ij}\vee 1\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}=(p_{i1},\cdots,p_{iN}).

For
p\in(0,1)
and
q:=p/\delta\in(0,1)
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}\leqslant C\|{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\sigma}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}

where
C=C({\boldsymbol{p}}_{i},Q_{2},{\boldsymbol{p}})>0
.
Substituting this into (
2.21
), we obtain (
2.17
) with
\gamma_{1}=\theta\gamma\delta/(\theta-1)
.

(ii) Suppose
q_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
q_{0}=1
.
Let
\tau_{n}=\tau+2^{-n}(\sigma-\tau)
. Iterating (
2.20
) with
q=\theta^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}(\tau,1)

\displaystyle\leqslant{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{n},1)\leqslant\Big((\tau_{n-1}-\tau_{n})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}\Big)^{\theta}{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{n-1},\theta^{-1})

\displaystyle\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big((\tau_{n-j}-\tau_{n-j+1})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}\Big)^{\theta^{j}}{\mathscr{M}}(\tau_{0},\theta^{-n}).

(2.22)

Noting that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{j}=\frac{\theta(\theta^{n}-1)}{\theta-1},\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n-j)\theta^{j}=\frac{\theta^{n+1}-n\theta^{2}+(n-1)\theta}{(\theta-1)^{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big((\tau_{n-j}-\tau_{n-j+1})^{-\gamma}mC_{0}\Big)^{\theta^{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(2^{\gamma(n-j+1)}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}mC_{0}\Big)^{\theta^{j}}

\displaystyle\quad=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{j}\left((n-j+1)\gamma\ln 2+\ln((\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma}2mC_{0}))\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{C_{3}(\theta^{n}-1)\ln(C_{4}(\sigma-\tau)^{-\gamma})}=(C^{C_{3}}_{4}(\sigma-\tau)^{-C_{3}\gamma})^{\theta^{n}-1}

(2.23)

for some
C_{3},C_{4}>0
.
Moreover, if we let

p_{0}:={\mathord{{\rm min}}}_{i,j}p_{ij},\quad p_{1}:=\max_{i,j}p_{ij}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}=(p_{i1},\cdots,p_{iN}),

then by Hölder’s inequality, we have for some
C>0
,

\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{0}}}\lesssim_{C}\vee_{i=1}^{m}\|u{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{\tau}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}}}={\mathscr{M}}(\tau,1),

and

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(E,{\mathcal{E}},\mu)
be a measure space and
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\times E\to{\mathbb{R}}
a
{\mathcal{B}}([t_{0},t_{1}])\times{\mathcal{E}}
-measurable function. Let
V(t):=\int_{E}f(t,x)\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)
.
Suppose that
V(t)<\infty
and there are
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that for
any
t<t^{\prime}
in
[t_{0},t_{1}]
,

c_{0}\int^{t^{\prime}}_{t}\!\!\!\int_{E}|f(s,x)-V(s)|^{2}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant V(t^{\prime})-V(t)+C_{0}.

Then we have for all
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle({\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\otimes\mu)\Big\{(t,x)\in[t_{0},t_{1}]\times E:\,f(t,x)-V(t_{1})>\lambda+C_{0}\Big\}\leqslant 1/(c_{0}\lambda),

(2.24)

and

\displaystyle({\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\otimes\mu)\Big\{(t,x)\in[t_{0},t_{1}]\times E:\,f(t,x)-V(t_{0})<-(\lambda+C_{0})\Big\}\leqslant 1/(c_{0}\lambda).

(2.25)

Proof.

Let
g(t,x):=f(t,x)-V(t_{1})-C_{0}
and
P(t):=V(t_{1})-V(t)+C_{0}
. By the assumption we have for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
,

\displaystyle c_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{t}\!\!\!\int_{E}|g(s,x)+P(s)|^{2}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant P(t).

(2.26)

For
\lambda>0
and
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, if we define

\Gamma_{\lambda}(t):=\{x:g(t,x)>\lambda\},

then since
P(t)\geqslant 0
by (
2.26
), we have

g(t,x)+P(t)\geqslant\lambda+P(t)>0,\ \ x\in\Gamma_{\lambda}(t).

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{E}|g(t,x)+P(t)|^{2}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)\geqslant\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}(t)}|g(t,x)+P(t)|^{2}\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)\geqslant\mu(\Gamma_{\lambda}(t))|\lambda+P(t)|^{2},

which together with (
2.26
) implies that for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
,

c_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{t}\mu(\Gamma_{\lambda}(s))|\lambda+P(s)|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant P(t).

Let
q(t)
solve the following ODE:

\displaystyle c_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{t}\mu(\Gamma_{\lambda}(s))|\lambda+q(s)|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=q(t).

(2.27)

Since
q(t_{1})=0
and
\dot{q}(t)\leqslant 0
, we have
q(t)\in[0,\infty]
for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.
In particular,

P(t)-q(t)\geqslant\int^{t_{1}}_{t}C(s)(P(s)-q(s)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

where

C(s)=c_{0}\mu(\Gamma_{\lambda}(s))(2\lambda+P(s)+q(s))\geqslant 0.

Since
P(t_{1})-q(t_{1})>0
, solving the above integral inequality yields

P(t)\geqslant q(t)\geqslant 0,\ \ t\in[t_{0},t_{1}],

which means that
q(t)
does not explore.
Thus one can solve ODE (
2.27
) to obtain

c_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{t}\mu(\Gamma_{\lambda}(s)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=-\int^{t_{1}}_{t}\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}q(s)}{(\lambda+q(s))^{2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda+q(t)}\leqslant\frac{1}{\lambda},

which gives (
2.24
). If we let
\bar{g}(t,x):=f(t,x)-V(t_{0})+C_{0}
and
\bar{P}(t):=V(t)-V(t_{0})+C_{0}
, then we can similarly show (
2.25
).
∎

We first show the following technical result.

Lemma 3.1

Suppose that

Let

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\beta(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leqslant({\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}u_{\varepsilon}+b\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+[\rho_{\varepsilon},b\cdot\nabla]u+f_{\varepsilon})\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}).

Note that

\displaystyle\left({\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\right)\beta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})-{\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}\beta(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}\beta(u)\partial_{t}\varphi\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}\beta(u){\mathscr{L}}^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}(b\cdot\nabla\varphi+\varphi\,\mathord{{\rm div}}b)\beta(u)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}f\beta^{\prime}(u)\varphi.

5.
Global

-estimate and Hölder regularity

To derive the global

and Hölder estimates, we introduce some localized spaces as in
[

]
.
Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a smooth function with
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leqslant 1
and
\chi(x)=0
for
|x|>2
.
For
r>0
and

, define

\displaystyle\chi^{z}_{r}(x):=\chi((x-z)/r).

(5.1)

Fix
r>0
. For
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in(1,\infty)^{d}
, we introduce the following localized
{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}:=\Big\{f:|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}}:=\sup_{z}\|f\chi^{z}_{r}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}}=\sup_{z}\|f(\cdot-z)\chi^{0}_{r}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}}<\infty\Big\},

(5.2)

and the localized space-time function space

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}}):=\Big\{f:|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}:=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f\chi^{z}_{r}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}<\infty\Big\},

(5.3)

where for
T>0
and a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
,

{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}):=L^{q}([0,T];{\mathbb{B}}).

The following properties of
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})

follows easily from
its
definition (see
[
60
, Proposition 4.1]
).

(i)

For
r\not=r^{\prime}>0
, there is a constant
C=C(d,\alpha,r,r^{\prime},p,q)\geqslant 1
such that

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sup_{z}\|f\chi^{z}_{r^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}\leqslant\sup_{z}\|f\chi^{z}_{r}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}\leqslant C\sup_{z}\|f\chi^{z}_{r^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}.

(5.4)

In other words, the definition of

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})
does not depend on the choice of

.

(ii)

For any
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})
, it holds that

f_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=(f*\rho_{\varepsilon})(t,x)\in C^{\infty}_{b}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and for some

,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f_{\varepsilon}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})},\ \forall\varepsilon\in(0,1),

(5.5)

and for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(f_{\varepsilon}-f)\varphi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{\boldsymbol{p}})}=0.

(5.6)

Here

is the mollifier defined in (

).

5.1.
Global well-posedness

First of all, we present
the following existence and uniqueness result of global weak solutions for PDE (
1.8
) with initial value
u(t)|_{t\leqslant 0}=0
.
In the following we set
{\mathcal{I}}_{t}:={\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,t]}
and assume

b(t,\cdot)|_{t\leqslant 0}=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad f(t,\cdot)|_{t\leqslant 0}=0.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\beta\in[0,\alpha/2)
and
T>0
. Suppose that for some
(q_{1},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1})
and
(q_{2},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{0}_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}:=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mathord{{\rm div}}b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{2}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{x}}<\infty.

(5.7)

Then for any
f\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})
, where
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathbb{I}}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
there is a unique global weak solution

to PDE (
1.8
) with initial value
u(t)|_{t\leqslant 0}=0
such that for some
C=C(T,\kappa_{0},q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})>0

and all

,

\displaystyle\|u{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\Delta^{\alpha/4}u{\mathcal{I}}_{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}}\lesssim_{C}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f{\mathcal{I}}_{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

(5.8)

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1
. We first show (
5.8
). Fix
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Define

u_{x_{0}}(t,x):=u(t,x-x_{0}),\quad b_{x_{0}}(t,x):=b(t,x-x_{0}),\quad f_{x_{0}}(t,x):=f(t,x-x_{0}).

It is easy to check that
u_{x_{0}}
is a weak solution of PDE (
1.8
) but with
(b_{x_{0}},f_{x_{0}})

in place of
(b,f)
there.
Applying Theorem
1.4
to
u^{+}_{x_{0}}
and
u^{-}_{x_{0}}
with
p=1
, respectively,
there is a constant
C=C(\kappa_{0},q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{x_{0}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}+\|\Delta^{\alpha/4}u_{x_{0}}\chi^{0}_{1}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{C}\|u_{x_{0}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{Q_{2}}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}}+\|{\rm Tail}(u_{x_{0}};1){\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{t}}+\|\chi^{0}_{2}f_{x_{0}}{\mathcal{I}}_{t}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}({\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/2}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f{\mathcal{I}}_{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

(5.9)

Taking supremum in
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and square for both sides, we obtain that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|u(s)\|^{2}_{\infty}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f{\mathcal{I}}_{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})},

which by Gronwall’s inequality yields that

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f{\mathcal{I}}_{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

Thus, we obtain (
5.8
) by (
5.1
), which in particular gives the uniqueness of weak solutions.

Step 2
. In this step we show the existence of weak solutions. Define

b_{\varepsilon}:=b*\rho_{\varepsilon}\quad\hbox{and}\quad f_{\varepsilon}:=f*\rho_{\varepsilon}.

By (
5.7
), we have for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\Big(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b_{\varepsilon}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{\varepsilon}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{2}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{x}}\Big)<\infty.

It is well known that there is a unique smooth solution

to the following PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}u_{\varepsilon}+b_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}\quad\hbox{with }u_{\varepsilon}(0)=0.

(5.11)

In particular, for any

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\varphi&=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}\langle u_{\varepsilon},\Delta^{\alpha/2}\varphi\rangle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}(b_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\varphi\,\mathord{{\rm div}}b_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}f_{\varepsilon}\varphi.\end{split}

(5.12)

Moreover, by (
5.10
) and (

),

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\Delta^{\alpha/4}u_{\varepsilon}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{x}})<\infty.

(5.13)

Hence, there is a sequence

and

such that
for each
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

Taking limits on both sides of (

) along

,
we conclude that

is a weak solution of PDE (
1.8
). This completes the proof.
∎

In this subsection we establish a priori

The following is the main result of this section.

.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and

. Suppose that
for some

Lemma 5.6

6.

We consider the following stochastic quasi-geostropic equation in

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}u=\big(\Delta^{1/2}u+{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla u\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}g_{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t},\ \ u(0)=u_{0},

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}.

(6.3)

We assume that for some
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1]

\displaystyle u_{0}\in{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{2}\cap{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{4}\quad\hbox{and}\quad g\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{2}(\ell^{2})\cap{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{4}(\ell^{2})).

Here we do not pursue the best possible

Definition 6.1

Let

Lemma 6.3

6.8
) yields

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\nabla\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\right)\lesssim_{C}\int^{T}_{0}\|\Delta^{\beta/2}g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}(\ell^{2})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Similarly, one can show

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t)\right\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\right)\lesssim_{C}\int^{T}_{0}\|g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}(\ell^{2})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Under (
6.4
), for any weak solution
u
of SQG (
6.1
),
T>0
and
p\in(2,4)
, it holds that

\displaystyle u\in{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{p}),\ \ a.s.

(6.9)

In particular,
u
is a strong solution of SQG (
6.1
) in PDE’s sense.

Proof.

Define

\widetilde{u}(t):=P_{t}u_{0}+{\mathbb{S}}_{g}(t).

By (
6.7
) and
\|P_{t}u_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{4}}\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{4}}
, for any
q\geqslant 2
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1/2}_{x})}^{q}\right]\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{4})}^{q}\right]\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{4}}+\int^{T}_{0}\|g(s)\|^{q}_{{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{4}(\ell^{2})}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty.

(6.10)

Clearly,
\widetilde{u}
solves the SPDE:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\widetilde{u}=\Delta^{1/2}\widetilde{u}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g_{k}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\widetilde{u}(0)=u_{0}.

Let
\bar{u}:=u-\widetilde{u}
. It is easy to see that

\partial_{t}\bar{u}=\Delta^{1/2}\bar{u}+{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\bar{u}+{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\bar{u}(0)=0.

Note that for any
p>1
,

\mathord{{\rm div}}{\mathcal{R}}u=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|{\mathcal{R}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\leqslant C\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}.

By (
5.8
) with
b={\mathcal{R}}u
and
f={\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}
, for
p\in(2,4)
, there is a constant

\gamma\in(0,1/4)
such that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x})}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{4p/(4-p)}_{x}}\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{4}_{x}}<\infty.

This together with
\|\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{1/4}_{x})}<\infty
implies that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x})}\leqslant\|\bar{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x})}+\|\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x})}<\infty.

Thus for
p\in(2,4)
, by
[
56
, Lemma 5.1]
with
b={\mathcal{R}}u
and
f={\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}
,

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{p})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}\leqslant T^{1/p}\|{\mathcal{R}}u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}<\infty.

The desired result now follows from

u=\bar{u}+\widetilde{u}
and
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{p})
a.s.
∎

Now we can give the

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two weak solutions of SQG (
6.1
) defined on the same probability space with the same initial value.
Define
U=u_{1}-u_{2}
. Then
U(0)=0
and

\partial_{t}U=\Delta^{1/2}U+{\mathcal{R}}u_{1}\cdot\nabla U+{\mathcal{R}}U\cdot\nabla u_{2}.

Let
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}
and
T>0
.
For
p\in(3,4)
, by Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, there are constants
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
C>0

such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}U(s)\cdot\nabla u_{2}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}U(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|{\mathcal{R}}U(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x}}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\int^{t}_{0}\|U(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}_{x}}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|^{p}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

By (
6.9
) and Gronwall’s inequality,

\|U(t)\|^{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}}=0.

Thus, the pathwise uniqueness holds. The proof is now complete.
∎

7.
Existence of weak solutions to DDSDE

In this section we study the following distribution-dependent SDE (DDSDE):

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b(t,X_{t},y)\mu_{X_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}L^{(\alpha)}_{t},

(7.1)

where
b(t,x,y):{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a measurable function and
\mu_{X_{t}}
stands for the law of
X_{t}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geqslant 1
.
In the following, for a Banach space
{\mathbb{B}}
, we shall use
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{B}})
to denote the set of all probability measures
on
{\mathbb{B}}
over Borel
\sigma
-field
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{B}})
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\mathfrak{F}}:=(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbf{P}};({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
be a stochastic basis,
X_{t}

an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-adapted process,
L^{(\alpha)}
a
d
-dimensional isotropic

\alpha
-stable process.
We call
({\mathfrak{F}},X,L^{(\alpha)})
a weak solution of DDSDE (
7.1
) with initial distribution
\nu
if
{\mathbf{P}}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\nu

and for all
t\geqslant 0
,

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b(s,X_{s},y)\mu_{X_{s}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+L^{(\alpha)}_{t}\quad{\mathbf{P}}\hbox{-a.s.},

where
\mu_{X_{s}}
is the law of
X_{s}
.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
T>0
.
Suppose that
\mathord{{\rm div}}_{x}b\equiv 0
and for all
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|b(t,x,y)|\leqslant h(t,x-y)\quad\mbox{with }\quad|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x})}\leqslant\kappa_{0},

(7.2)

where
(q_{1},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{0}_{\alpha}
.
Then for any
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
there exists at least one weak solution to DDSDE (
7.1
) with initial distribution
\nu
in the sense of Definition
7.1
.
Moreover, for Lebesgue almost every
t\geqslant 0
,

X_{t}
admits a density
\rho(t,\cdot)

which enjoys the following regularity: for any
\beta\in[0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in(1,\infty)^{1+d}
with
\tfrac{\alpha}{q}+|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|>d+\beta
,

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}})}<\infty.

(7.3)

To show the existence of a weak solution, we use the mollifying technique.
Fix
T>0
. Let
\varGamma\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+2d})
be a smooth probability density function with compact support and define
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\varGamma_{n}(t,x,y)=n^{1+2d}\varGamma(nt,nx,ny)

and

\displaystyle b_{n}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle:=((b{\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,T]})*\varGamma_{n})(t,x,y).

Note that by (
7.2
),

\|\nabla^{j}_{x}\nabla^{k}_{y}b_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty,\ \ j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

By this, it is well known
that the following DDSDE admits a unique strong solution (cf.
[
50
]
):

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s},y)\mu_{X^{n}_{s}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+L^{(\alpha)}_{t},

(7.4)

where

{\mathbf{P}}\circ X_{0}^{-1}=\nu.

Now we use Theorem
5.1
to derive the following crucial Krylov estimate.

Lemma 7.3

(Krylov’s estimates)
.

For any
\beta\in[0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
, there are
\theta\in(0,1)
and constant
C=C(T,d,\alpha,\beta,\kappa_{0},q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})>0

such that for any
\delta\in(0,T)
, stopping time
\tau\leqslant T-\delta
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{\tau+\delta}_{\tau}f(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{F}}_{\tau}\right]\leqslant C\,\delta^{\theta}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

(7.5)

In particular, for Lebesgue almost every
t\geqslant 0
,

X^{n}_{t}
admits a density
\rho_{n}(t,\cdot)

such that for any
\beta\in[0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in(1,\infty)^{1+d}
with
\tfrac{\alpha}{q}+|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|>d+\beta
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}})}<\infty.

(7.6)

Proof.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle\widetilde{b}_{n}(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b_{n}(t,x,y)\mu_{X^{n}_{t}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y).

(7.7)

Note that by (
7.2
) and the definition (
5.3
),

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\widetilde{b}_{n}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x})}\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x})}\leqslant\kappa_{0},

(7.8)

and for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\|\nabla^{j}\widetilde{b}_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Fix
0\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T
. For given
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d})
,
let
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}(C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
solve the following backward Kolmogorov equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Delta^{\alpha/2}u_{n}+\widetilde{b}_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=f,\ \ u_{n}(t_{1})=0.

(7.9)

Since
|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}|+\tfrac{\alpha}{q_{0}}<\alpha-\beta
, one can choose
\bar{q}_{0}<q_{0}
so that

|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}|+\tfrac{\alpha}{\bar{q}_{0}}<\alpha-\beta.

Thus by
\mathord{{\rm div}}\widetilde{b}_{n}=0
, (
7.8
) and (
5.8
), there is a constant
C=C(T,d,\alpha,\beta,\kappa_{0},q_{i},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i})>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}u_{n}\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{C}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\bar{q}_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta_{0}}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}})}\lesssim_{C}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{\bar{q}_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})},

(7.10)

where the second step is due to Hölder’s inequality.
Now by (
7.9
) and Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle 0=u_{n}(t_{1},X^{n}_{t_{1}})=u_{n}(t_{0},X^{n}_{t_{0}})+\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}f(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+M_{t_{1}}-M_{t_{0}},

where
t\mapsto M_{t}
is a local martingale.
Hence, by taking conditional expectation with respect to
{\mathscr{F}}_{t_{0}}
and (
7.10
),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}f(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathscr{F}}_{t_{0}}\right]=-u_{n}(t_{0},X^{n}_{t_{0}})\leqslant C\,(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{\bar{q}_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbf{1}}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

By discretization stopping time approximation (see
[
60
, Remark 1.2]
), we obtain (
7.5
).
As for (
7.6
), it follows by

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{T}_{0}f(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right]\leqslant C\,\delta^{\theta}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}\leqslant C\,\delta^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})},

and a standard duality method.
∎

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be the space of all cádlág functions from
[0,T]
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We use
(\omega_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to denote a generic path in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
By Krylov’s estimate (
7.5
), we show the following tightness result.

Lemma 7.4
.

The law
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
of
X^{n}
in
{\mathbb{D}}
is tight. For any accumulation point
{\mathbb{P}}
, it holds that for
any
\beta\in[0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{\beta}_{\alpha}
, and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\int^{T}_{0}f(r,\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right]\lesssim_{C}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{-\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}})}.

(7.11)

In particular, for Lebesgue almost every
t\in[0,T]
,
{\mathbb{P}}\circ\omega_{t}^{-1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
.

Proof.

Let
\delta<T
and
\tau
be any stopping time bounded by
T-\delta
. By SDE (
7.4
), we have

\displaystyle X^{n}_{\tau+\delta}-X^{n}_{\tau}=\int^{\tau+\delta}_{\tau}\widetilde{b}_{n}(r,X^{n}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+L^{(\alpha)}_{\tau+\delta}-L^{(\alpha)}_{\tau},

where
\widetilde{b}_{n}
is defined by (
7.7
).
By Krylov’s estimate (
7.5
) and (
7.8
), there is a
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{\tau+\delta}_{\tau}|\widetilde{b}_{n}(r,X^{n}_{r})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right]\lesssim\delta^{\theta}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\widetilde{b}_{n}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x})}\lesssim\delta^{\theta}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}(\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x})}.

(7.12)

Moreover, by the property of Lévy processes, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\tau}{\mathbf{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}|L^{(\alpha)}_{\tau+t}-L^{(\alpha)}_{\tau}|\geqslant\varepsilon\right)=0,

which together with (
7.12
) yields that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{n}\sup_{\tau}{\mathbf{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}|X^{n}_{\tau+t}-X^{n}_{\tau}|>\varepsilon\right)=0.

By Aldous’ criterion (see
[
28
, p.356, Theorem 4.5]
),
\{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is tightness.
Estimate (
7.11
) follow by taking weak limits for (
7.5
).
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

There is a subsequence
n_{k}
so that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}
weakly converges to some
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{D}})
and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(\rho_{n_{k}}-\rho){\mathbf{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}=0\quad\hbox{for every }m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(7.13)

Proof.

Note that
\rho_{n}
solves the following Fokker-Planck equation:

\partial_{t}\rho_{n}=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho_{n}+\mathord{{\rm div}}(\widetilde{b}_{n}\rho_{n}).

Let
\chi_{m}=\chi^{0}_{m}
be the cutoff function as in (
5.1
). By Sobolev’s embedding, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(\rho_{n}\chi_{m})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho_{n})\chi_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2})}+\|\mathord{{\rm div}}(\widetilde{b}_{n}\chi_{2m}\rho_{n})\chi_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2})}\|\chi_{m}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{3}_{2}}+\|\widetilde{b}_{n}\chi_{2m}\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}\|\chi_{m}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{3}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}+\|\widetilde{b}_{n}\chi_{2m}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}\|\rho_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{2}}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}_{x}},

where
\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1
and
\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}+\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{2}}={\mathbf{1}}
.
By (
7.6
) we have

\sup_{n}\|\partial_{t}(\rho_{n}\chi_{m})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{-3}_{2})}<\infty.

Since
{\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{p}
is locally compactly embedding in
{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}}_{x}
, by Aubin-Lions’ lemma and a diagalization method,
there is a subsequence
n_{k}
so that (
7.13
) holds for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, by Prohorov’s theorem, one can find a common subsequence
n_{k}

such that
{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}
weakly converges to
{\mathbb{P}}
as
k\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
q_{0}\in[1,q_{1})
,
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}\in[{\mathbf{1}},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}-\widetilde{b}){\mathbf{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{x}}=0,

(7.14)

where
\widetilde{b}(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b(t,x,y)\rho(t,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y
and
n_{k}
is the subsequence in Corollary
7.5
.

Proof.

Note that by (
7.2
) and the definition (
5.3
),

\sup_{k}\|(\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}-\widetilde{b}){\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}.

By the uniform integrability, to show (
7.14
), it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}-\widetilde{b}){\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}=0.

(7.15)

By definition we can write for any
R>0
,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}-\widetilde{b})(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}[b_{n_{k}}(t,x,y)\rho_{n_{k}}(t,y)-b(t,x,y)\rho(t,y)]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{B^{c}_{R}}+\int_{B_{R}}\Big)\left(b_{n_{k}}(t,x,y)\rho_{n_{k}}(t,y)-b(t,x,y)\rho(t,y)\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=:I^{R}_{k}(t,x)+J^{R}_{k}(t,x).

For
I^{R}_{k}
, by (
7.2
) we have

\|I^{R}_{k}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}\leqslant\|h\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}\int_{B^{c}_{R}}[\rho_{n_{k}}(t,y)+\rho(t,y)]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

By the tightness of
\rho_{n_{k}}
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{k}\|I^{R}_{k}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}=0.

(7.16)

For
J^{R}_{k}
, noting that by (
7.13
) and the property of convolution,

b_{n_{k}}(t,x,y)\rho_{n_{k}}(t,y)
converges to
b(t,x,y)\rho(t,y)
in Lebesgue measure
{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\times{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\times{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y
,

by the uniform integrability, we have for each
R>0
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|J^{R}_{k}{\mathbbm{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{m}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\!\int_{B_{m}}\!\int_{B_{R}}|b_{n_{k}}(t,x,y)\rho_{n_{k}}(t,y)-b(t,x,y)\rho(t,y)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t=0,

which together with (
7.16
) yields (
7.15
).
∎

Now we can give the

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
be the law of
L^{(\alpha)}_{\cdot}
in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Set
{\mathbb{Q}}_{k}:={\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}\times{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
. By Corollary
7.5
,

{\mathbb{Q}}_{k}
weakly converges to
{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{P}}\times{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
as
k\to\infty
. By Skorokhod’s representation theorem,
there is a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbf{P}})
and
{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}
-valued processes
(\widetilde{X}^{k},\widetilde{L}^{k})
and
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})
such that

\displaystyle(\widetilde{X}^{k},\widetilde{L}^{k})\to(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})\ \mbox{ in ${\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}$},\ \ {\mathbf{P}}-a.s.,

(7.17)

and

{\mathbf{P}}\circ(\widetilde{X}^{k},\widetilde{L}^{k})^{-1}={\mathbb{Q}}_{k},\quad{\mathbf{P}}\circ(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})^{-1}={\mathbb{Q}}.

Moreover,
\widetilde{L}^{k}
and
\widetilde{L}
are still
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes, and

\widetilde{X}_{t}^{k}=\widetilde{X}_{0}^{k}+\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\widetilde{L}^{k}_{t}.

By (
7.17
), (
7.14
) and taking limits, one sees that

\widetilde{X}_{t}=\widetilde{X}_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\widetilde{L}_{t}.

Indeed, for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by (
7.17
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{t}_{0}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})-\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}_{s})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=0,\ \ {\mathbf{P}}-a.s.

(7.18)

Since
{\mathbb{I}}^{0}_{\alpha}
is open, there is a
\theta>1
so that

(q_{0},{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}):=(\tfrac{q_{1}}{\theta},\tfrac{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}{\theta})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{0}_{\alpha}.

By Hölder’s inequality and Krylov’s estimate (
7.5
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{t}_{0}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})-\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})|{\mathbbm{1}}_{|\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}|\geqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left({\mathbf{E}}\int^{t}_{0}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})-\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})|^{\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1/\theta}\left({\mathbf{E}}\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbbm{1}}_{|\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}|\geqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1-1/\theta}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{\theta}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\widetilde{b}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{\theta}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}\right)^{1/\theta}\left(\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbf{P}}\{|\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}|\geqslant R\}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1-1/\theta}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|h|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1}}_{x}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\rho_{n_{k}}(s,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{1-1/\theta}.

By the tightness of
\rho_{n_{k}}
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{m,k}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{t}_{0}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})-\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})|{\mathbbm{1}}_{|\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}|\geqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]=0.

(7.19)

Moreover, for each
R>0
, by (
7.5
) and (
7.14
) we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\int^{t}_{0}|\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})-\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s})|{\mathbbm{1}}_{|\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}|\leqslant R}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right]\lesssim\|(\widetilde{b}_{n_{m}}-\widetilde{b}){\mathbf{1}}_{[0,T]\times B_{R}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{0}}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{{\boldsymbol{p}}_{0}}_{x}}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
, which combining (
7.18
) and (
7.19
) yields

\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{b}_{n_{k}}(s,\widetilde{X}^{k}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\to\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{b}(s,\widetilde{X}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

in probability as
k\to\infty
. The proof is complete.
∎

As a corollary, we have
the following existence of solutions to the
fractional
Navier-Stokes equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with measure as initial vorticity.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
.
For any
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, there exists a weak solution
\rho
to equation (
1.6
) in the sense that for each
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\rho(t,y)\varphi(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\varphi(y)\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta^{\alpha/2}\varphi(y)\rho(s,y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\rho(s,y)(K_{2}*\rho(s))(y)\cdot\nabla\varphi(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Moreover,
\rho
enjoys the regularity (
7.3
).

Proof.

Note that for any
p\in[1,2)
,

\int_{B_{1}}|K_{2}(y)|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y<\infty.

For
\alpha\in(1,2)
, one can choose
p\in(1,2)
and
q
close to infinity so that

\tfrac{\alpha}{q}+\tfrac{2}{p}<\alpha.

Thus we can use Theorem
7.2
with
b(t,x,y)=K_{2}(x-y)
to conclude the proof.
∎

8.
Well-posedness of generalized martingale problems in critical case

In this section we show the well-posedness for a class of generalized martingale problems of SDE (
1.2
)
in critical case. Fix
T>0
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\|\mathord{{\rm div}}b\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{x}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{1}_{x}}+\|b\|_{\rm crit}<\infty.

(8.1)

For given
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, consider the following backward Kolmogorov equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{\alpha/2}u+b\cdot\nabla u=f\quad\hbox{with}\quad u(T)=0.

(8.2)

By reversing the time variable and Theorem
5.7
,
there is a unique weak solution
u_{f}
to (
8.2
) with

u_{f}\in C_{b}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Here the continuity of
u_{f}
is crucial
for the following notion of generalized martingale solutions.

Definition 8.1

(Generalized martingale problem)
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
and
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. A probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{D}})
is called a generalized martingale solution of SDE (
1.2
) starting from
\nu

at time
s
if
{\mathbb{P}}\circ\omega^{-1}_{s}=\nu
and for any
T>s
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the process

\displaystyle M_{t}:=u_{f}(t,\omega_{t})-u_{f}(s,\omega_{s})-\int^{t}_{s}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\ \ t\in[s,T],

(8.3)

is a
{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
-martingale with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
, where
u_{f}
is the unique solution of (
8.2
).
The set of all the generalized martingale solutions
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{D}})
with initial distribution
\nu
at time
s
is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}_{s,\nu}
.

Remark 8.2
.

The above notion of martingale solutions was originally introduced in
[
20
, Chapter 4]
.
It should be noticed that if (
8.2
) has a
C^{2}
-solution
u
, then by Itô’s formula, any weak solution
of (
1.2
) must be a generalized martingale solution. In general,
these two notions are not equivalent.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 8.3
.

Under (
8.1
), for each
s\geqslant 0
and
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, there is a unique generalized martingale solution

{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{b}_{s,\nu}
to SDE (
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
8.1
, where
{\mathbb{P}}
is also the distribution of a weak solution of SDE (
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
7.1
.
Moreover,
for Lebesgue almost every
t\geqslant s
,
{\mathbb{P}}\circ\omega_{t}^{-1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
and
\rho(t,x)
solves the following Fokker-Planck equation
in the distributional sense

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta^{\alpha/2}\rho-\mathord{{\rm div}}(b\rho)\quad\hbox{with}\quad\lim_{t\downarrow s}\rho(t,\cdot)=\nu\ \mbox{ \rm weakly},

(8.4)

and
\|\rho\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}({\mathbf{H}}^{\beta}_{{\boldsymbol{p}}})}<\infty
for any
\beta\in[0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
(q,{\boldsymbol{p}})\in(1,\infty)^{1+d}
with
\tfrac{\alpha}{q}+|\tfrac{1}{{\boldsymbol{p}}}|>d+\beta
.

Proof.

(Existence)
Without loss of generality we assume
s=0
.
Consider the approximating SDE

X^{n}_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}b_{n}(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+L^{(\alpha)}_{t},

where
\nu={\mathbf{P}}\circ X^{-1}_{0}
and

b_{n}(t,x):=[b(t,\cdot)*\varGamma_{n}](x).

Let
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{D}})
be an accumulation point of
({\mathbb{P}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
By
Lemma
7.4
,

({\mathbb{P}}_{n}\circ\omega_{t}^{-1})({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho_{n}(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,\ \ {\mathbb{P}}\circ\omega_{t}^{-1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\rho(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

Since for each
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by Itô’s formula,

\partial_{t}\int\varphi\rho_{n}=\int\rho_{n}\Delta^{\alpha/2}\varphi+\int(b_{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\rho_{n},

taking limits along the subsequence
n_{k}
in Corollary
7.5
and by (
7.13
) and (
7.14
), it is easy to see that

\partial_{t}\int\varphi\rho=\int\rho\Delta^{\alpha/2}\varphi+\int(b\cdot\nabla\varphi)\rho.

In other words,
\rho
solves (
8.4
) in the distributional sense.
Fix
T>0
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Let
u_{n}\in L^{\infty}_{T}(C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
be the unique smooth solution of the following backward PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Delta^{\alpha/2}u_{n}+b_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=f,\ \ u_{n}(T)=0.

(8.5)

By Theorem
5.7
, we have for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times B_{m}}|u_{n}(t,x)-u_{f}(t,x)|=0,

(8.6)

where
u_{f}
is the unique weak solution of PDE (
8.2
).
We now show that for each
T\geqslant t_{1}>t_{0}\geqslant 0
and bounded continuous
{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{0}}
-measurable functional
G_{t_{0}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}(M_{t_{1}}G_{t_{0}})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}(M_{t_{0}}G_{t_{0}}),

(8.7)

where
M_{t}
is defined by (
8.3
).
By SDE (
7.4
), Itô’s formula and (
8.5
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}(M^{n}_{t_{1}}G_{t_{0}})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}(M^{n}_{t_{0}}G_{t_{0}}),

(8.8)

where

M^{n}_{t}:=u_{n}(t,\omega_{t})-u_{n}(0,\omega_{0})-\int^{t}_{0}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

Sending
n\to\infty
in (
8.8
),
and using the pointwise convergence (
8.6
), we obtain (
8.7
).
Moreover, as in the proof of Theorem
7.2
,
{\mathbb{P}}
is also the distribution of a weak solution of SDE (
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
7.1
.

(Uniqueness)
The uniqueness is a direct consequence of the definition. In fact,
let
{\mathbb{P}}_{1},{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\in{\mathscr{M}}^{b}_{s,\nu}
be two solutions of the generalized martingale problem. Fix
T>s
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Let
u_{f}
be the unique weak solution of (see Theorem
5.7
),

\partial_{t}u_{f}+\Delta^{\alpha/2}u_{f}+b\cdot\nabla u_{f}=f,\ \ u_{f}(T)=0.

By Definition
8.1
and
u_{f}(T)=0
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{f}(s,x)\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=-{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\int_{s}^{T}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\quad i=1,2,

which means that for each
T>s
,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r=\int_{s}^{T}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Hence, for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}f(\omega_{T})={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}f(\omega_{T}),\ \ \forall T>s.

(8.9)

From this, by a standard way (see Theorem 4.4.2 in
[
20
]
), we derive that

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}={\mathbb{P}}_{2}.

Indeed, it suffices to prove the following claim by induction:

(C
n
)
for given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for any
s\leqslant t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{n}<T
and strictly positive and bounded measurable functions
g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}(g_{1}(\omega_{t_{1}})\cdots g_{n}(\omega_{t_{n}}))={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(g_{1}(\omega_{t_{1}})\cdots g_{n}(\omega_{t_{n}})).

(8.10)

By (
8.9
),
(C
1
)
holds.
Suppose now that
(C
n
)
holds for some
n\geqslant 2
. For simplicity we write

\eta:=g_{1}(\omega_{t_{1}})\cdots g_{n}(\omega_{t_{n}})>0,

and for
i=1,2
, we define new probability measures

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{i}:=\eta{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbb{P}}_{i}/({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\eta)\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{D}}),\ \ \widetilde{\nu}_{i}:=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{i}\circ\omega^{-1}_{t_{n}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Now we show

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{b}_{t_{n};\widetilde{\nu}_{i}},\ \ i=1,2.

For any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let

M_{t}:=u_{f}(t,\omega_{t})-u_{f}(t_{n},\omega_{t_{n}})-\int^{t}_{t_{n}}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\ \ t\in[t_{n},T].

We only need to prove that for any
T\geqslant t^{\prime}>t\geqslant t_{n}
and bounded
{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
-measurable
\xi
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\left(M_{t^{\prime}}\xi\right)={\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}\left(M_{t}\xi\right)\Leftrightarrow{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}(M_{t^{\prime}}\xi\eta)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{i}}(M_{t}\xi\eta),

which follows from
{\mathbb{P}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{b}_{s,\nu}
,
i=1,2
. Thus, by induction hypothesis and (
8.9
),

\widetilde{\nu}_{1}=\widetilde{\nu}_{2}\Rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\circ\omega^{-1}_{t_{n+1}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\circ\omega^{-1}_{t_{n+1}},\ \ \forall T\geqslant t_{n+1}>t_{n}.

which in turn implies that
(C
n+1
)
holds.
∎

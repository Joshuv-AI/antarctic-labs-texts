On Uniformly Perfect Morse Boundaries
Suzhen Han, Qing Liu

Abstract
We introduce and geometrically characterize the notion of uniformly perfect Morse boundary for proper geodesic metric spaces. As a unifying result, we prove that the Morse boundary of any finitely generated, non-elementary group is uniformly perfect whenever it is nonempty. This theorem applies to a broad class of groups, including all acylindrically hyperbolic groups, Artin groups, and hierarchically hyperbolic groups. Furthermore, we establish a rigidity theorem for homeomorphisms between such boundaries: for any two spaces with uniformly perfect Morse boundaries, a homeomorphism is induced by a quasi-isometry if and only if it satisfies any one of several natural geometric conditions. These conditions include being bi-Hölder, quasi-conformal, quasi-symmetric, or 2 2 -stable and quasi-Möbius.

Introduction and main results

The Gromov boundary serves as a fundamental invariant of Gromov hyperbolic spaces, bridging their internal geometry with topology.
A cornerstone result of Gromov
[
19

A natural generalization of the Gromov boundary is the Morse boundary

Definition 2.1

2.1
Boundaries of hyperbolic spaces

We briefly recall the construction of the Gromov boundary and visual metrics for it; detailed accounts can be found in

Definition 2.6
.

Let

be a metric space and

(x\cdot y)_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(d(x,z)+d(y,z)-d(x,y))

In the following definition, the space

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\delta\geq 0

\delta

(x\cdot y)_{w}\geq\min\{(x\cdot z)_{w},(y\cdot z)_{w}\}-\delta.

Let

be a basepoint. A sequence
(x_{n})
in

(x_{i}\cdot x_{i})_{0}\to\infty
as

. Two convergent sequences

and
(y_{n})
in

are said to be equivalent if
(x_{i}\cdot y_{j})_{o}\to\infty
as

. The equivalence class

is denoted
\lim x_{n}
. The sequential boundary
\partial_{s}X
is defined to be the set of equivalence classes. In our paper, we will omit the subscript

.
Let

. We extend the Gromov product to

(x\cdot y)_{o}=\sup\displaystyle{\liminf_{i,j\to\infty}}(x_{i}\cdot y_{j})_{o},

where the supremum is taken over all sequences
(x_{i}),(y_{j})
in
X
with
x=\lim x_{i},y=\lim y_{j}.

Definition 2.8

Let

be a hyperbolic space with a basepoint

. A metric

Here is the standard construction of metrics on
\partial X
. Let

be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space with a basepoint

. Let
p,q\in\partial X,\epsilon>0
. Let us consider the following:

d_{o,\epsilon}(p,q)=\inf\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp{(-\epsilon(p_{i-1}\cdot p_{i})_{0})},

where the infimum is taken over all finite sequences

in

, no bound on

Let

2.2
The Morse boundary and Metric Morse boundary

We first define the
Morse boundary
for a proper geodesic metric space
X
. Fix a basepoint
o\in X
.
As a set, the Morse boundary
\partial_{*}X_{o}
consists of the equivalence classes of all Morse geodesic rays emanating from the basepoint
o
. Here, two geodesic rays are deemed equivalent if their Hausdorff distance is bounded.

For a fixed Morse gauge
N
, we introduce the following subset:

\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}=\left\{[\alpha]\mid\text{there exists an }N\text{-Morse geodesic ray }\beta\in[\alpha]\text{ with basepoint }o\right\}.

Topological structures can be endowed on both
\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}
and
\partial_{*}X_{o}
. However, such topological considerations will be omitted in the present framework. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
.

Now let us focus on the
Metric Morse boundary
. Fix a Morse gauge
N
and
o\in X
, and define
X_{o}^{(N)}
as the set of points
x\in X
for which there exists an
N
-Morse geodesic segment
[o,x]
in
X
.
This metric space is not necessarily geodesic. Nevertheless,
X_{o}^{(N)}
is a
32N(3,0)
-hyperbolic space in the sense of Definition
2.7
. We denote
\partial X^{(N)}_{o}
as the sequential boundary of
X_{o}^{(N)}
; for detailed background on Metric Morse boundary, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
.

It is necessary to distinguish carefully and distinctly between the two boundaries
\partial X^{(N)}_{o}
and
\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}
associated with a proper geodesic metric space
X
and a Morse gauge
N
. Each has its own advantages and limitations, and the choice depends on context. On one hand,
\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}
is defined via actual geodesic rays, providing clear geometric intuition; however, it is typically non-metrizable. On the other hand,
\partial X^{(N)}_{o}
agrees with the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic space, even though
X^{(N)}_{o}
itself may be neither proper nor geodesic. This discrepancy, however, does not present an obstacle. By applying the following theorem and comparing the two boundaries systematically, we can leverage their complementary strengths.

Theorem 2.11

(
[

,
26
]
)

For any Morse gauge

depending only on

Let

be a proper geodesic space with basepoint

i_{N}:\partial X^{(N)}_{o}\to\partial_{*}^{N^{\prime}}X_{o}\quad\text{and}\quad j_{N}:\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}\to\partial X^{(N^{\prime})}_{o}.

By virtue of Theorem

Consider the Gromov boundary

of

, there exists a visual metric

For two points

For any Morse gauge

3.2
Uniform Perfectness of Morse boundaries

There are several definitions for uniformly perfect spaces in the literature
[
28
,
33
,
43
]
. The definition below follows Martínez-Pérez and Rodríguez
[
28
]

Definition 3.3
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is called uniformly perfect if there exists
S>1
and
r_{0}>0
such that, for every point
x\in X
and all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, there exists a point
x^{\prime}\in X
satisfying

\frac{r}{S}<d(x,x^{\prime})\leq r.

Recall that the Morse boundary, when equipped with its direct limit topology, is typically non-metrizable. This intrinsic feature makes it delicate to formulate a suitable notion of uniform perfectness.
Our definition is as follows.

Definition 3.4

(Uniform perfectness of
\partial_{*}X
)
.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space with
\partial_{*}X\neq\emptyset
.
We say
\partial_{*}X
is
uniformly perfect
if for every Morse gauge
N

with
\bigcup_{o\in X}\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}\neq\emptyset
, there exist a Morse gauge
N^{\prime}=N^{\prime}(N)
, constants
S=S(N)>1
and
r_{0}=r_{0}(N)\in(0,1]
such that:
for any basepoint
o\in X
with
\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}\neq\emptyset
,
any
p\in\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}
, and any
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, there exists
q\in\partial_{*}X
with

p,q\in\partial X_{o}^{(N^{\prime})}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{r}{S}<d_{o,\epsilon_{N^{\prime}}}(p,q)\leq r.

Correspondingly, let us say
X
has a uniformly perfect Morse boundary.

Since
\partial_{*}X\neq\emptyset
, there exists at least one basepoint
o\in X
and one Morse gauge
N

for which an
N
-Morse ray starting at
o
exists; consequently, the condition

\bigcup_{o\in X}\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}\neq\emptyset
is non‑vacuous for some
N
.

Remark 3.5
.

Alternative metrizable topologies on the Morse boundary have been constructed for certain groups

[
7
,
12
,
37
]
. One could ask whether uniform perfectness
can be studied with respect to those metrics. However, such metrics are often geometrically opaque
and ill‑suited for the intrinsic geometric questions we address here; our definition is therefore
formulated directly in terms of the visual metrics
d_{o,\epsilon_{N}}
, which have clear geometric meaning.

Comparison with the hyperbolic case

For a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic space
X
and a fixed visual parameter
\epsilon>0
, the classical definition reads:

Definition 3.6

(Uniform perfectness of
\partial X
)
.

Let
X
be a proper
\delta
-hyperbolic geodesic metric space with
\partial X\neq\emptyset
.
We say
\partial X
is
uniformly perfect
if there exist constants
S>1
and
r_{0}\in(0,1]

such that for every
o\in X
, every
p\in\partial X
, and every
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,
there exists
q\in\partial X
with

\frac{r}{S}<d_{o,\epsilon}(p,q)\leq r.

When
X
is hyperbolic, Definition
3.4
coincides with Definition
3.6
:
all geodesics are uniformly Morse, so the Morse gauges
N
and
N^{\prime}
can be chosen uniformly, and the union
\bigcup_{o\in X}\partial_{*}^{N}X_{o}
equals
\partial X
for a suitable
N
.

Remark 3.7
.

The definition used in
[
24
]
for hyperbolic spaces requires the existence of constants
S>1
and
r_{0}>0
that may depend on the basepoint
o
. Our formulation demands
S
and
r_{0}
to be independent of
o
, giving a genuinely
uniform
condition.

3.3
Morse boundary rigid spaces

Consider now the quasi-isometry group of a metric space
X
, denoted by
\mathcal{QI}(X)
.
This group consists of all self-quasi-isometries
f:X\to X
, modulo the equivalence relation
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{sup}_{x\in X}d(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))
is finite.
As established in
[
10
,
16
]
, every self-quasi-isometry of a proper geodesic metric space
X
induces a homeomorphism
\partial f
on its Morse boundary. Furthermore, two uniformly close quasi-isometries induce the same homeomorphism on the Morse boundary.
Consequently, we obtain a natural homomorphism:

\partial:\mathcal{QI}(X)\to\operatorname{Homeo}(\partial_{*}X)

given by
\partial(f)=\partial f
.

Accordingly, we present the following definition of Morse boundary rigidity:

Definition 3.8

(Morse boundary rigid space)
.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space with non-empty Morse boundary
\partial_{*}X
.
The following are equivalent definitions:

1.

The natural homomorphism
\partial:\mathcal{QI}(X)\to\operatorname{Homeo}(\partial_{*}X)
is injective.

2.

For every quasi-isometry
f:X\to X
, if
\partial f=\text{id}_{\partial_{*}X}
, then
\sup_{x\in X}d(x,f(x))<\infty
.

If either condition holds,
X
is called
Morse boundary rigid
.

The analogous definition can be formulated for sublinearly Morse boundary rigid spaces. The notion of a boundary rigid space for hyperbolic spaces was first studied in
[
40
,
24
]
. Several characterizations of this property are established in
[
24
]
.
The following result establishes a fundamental relationship between these two notions of boundary rigidity.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space with non-empty
\partial_{*}X
. If
X
is Morse boundary rigid, then
X
is sublinearly Morse boundary rigid.

Proof.

From
[
38
,
37
]
, every point of the Morse boundary belongs to the sublinearly Morse boundary. For any quasi-isometry
f:X\to X
whose induced homeomorphism on the sublinearly Morse boundary is the identity, then
f
induces an identity homeomorphism on the Morse boundary. Since
X
is Morse boundary rigid, then the displacement
\sup_{x\in X}d(x,f(x))
is finite.
∎

3.4
Morse geodesically rich spaces

Let us first introduce a class of widely studied spaces: all hyperbolic spaces with non-empty Gromov boundaries, as well as all finitely generated groups with non-empty Morse boundaries.

Definition 3.10

(Uniformly Morse based space)
.

A proper geodesic metric space
X
is called a
uniformly Morse based space (UMB space)
if there exists a Morse gauge
N
such that for every point
x\in X
, there exists at least one
N
-Morse geodesic ray
\gamma_{x}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
with
\gamma_{x}(0)=x
.

The class of all such spaces is denoted by
\mathcal{UMB}
. When we need to emphasize the specific gauge, we write
\mathcal{UMB}(N)
.

The UMB property is invariant under quasi-isometry. It is well-defined for every finitely generated group. Every Gromov-hyperbolic geodesic metric space with non-empty Gromov boundary is a UMB space.
If
X
is a cocompact, proper geodesic metric space with
\partial_{*}X\neq\emptyset
, then
X
is a UMB space. In particular, every finitely generated group with non-empty Morse boundary is a UMB space.

The notion of
geodesic richness
for metric spaces was first introduced in
[
40
]
. Subsequently, Liang and Zhou
[
24
]
established that for proper Gromov-hyperbolic geodesic metric spaces, the second condition in the original definition follows directly from the first and can therefore be omitted for brevity (see Lemma 16 in
[
24
]
). We extend this line of investigation to
Morse geodesic richness
for arbitrary proper geodesic metric spaces.

Definition 3.11

(Morse geodesically rich space)
.

Let
N_{0}
be a Morse gauge and let

A proper geodesic metric space

(N_{0},C_{0})

-Morse geodesically rich for some Morse gauge

Since every geodesic in hyperbolic space is uniformly Morse, applying the above definition to hyperbolic spaces yields the following:

Definition 3.12

A proper hyperbolic geodesic metric space

Proposition 5.2

Let

Proposition 5.3

Y

d_{Y}\left(f(x),h(x)\right)\leq d_{Y}\left(f(x),h(x^{\prime})\right)+d_{Y}\left(h(x),h(x^{\prime})\right).

and note that

d_{Y}\left(f(x),h(x^{\prime})\right)=d_{Y}\left(y,f\left(g\left(f_{1}(y)\right)\right)\right)<D_{1}.

Since
h=f\circ g
is a quasi-isometry from
X
to
Y
, there exist constants
K_{1}\geq 1,C_{1}>0
(depending only on
f
and
g
) such that:

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,g(x))

\displaystyle\leq K\bigl(D_{1}+d_{Y}\bigl(h(x),h(x^{\prime})\bigr)\bigr)+KC

\displaystyle\leq K\bigl(D_{1}+K_{1}\cdot d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+C_{1}\bigr)+KC.

Since
f_{1}
is a quasi-inverse of
f
,
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})=d_{X}\left(x,f_{1}(f(x))\right)
is uniformly bounded. Let
M>0
denote this uniform bound.
Let
D=K\left(D_{1}+K_{1}M+C_{1}\right)+KC
. Since
D
is a constant independent of
x
, we conclude
\sup_{x\in X}d_{X}(x,g(x))<D
, as desired.

∎

6.2
Propositions for boundedness

We first state a proposition (originally from Proposition 4.1 of
[

]
) that plays a critical role in our argument; its proof relies solely on the properties of the homeomorphism

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X,Y
be two proper geodesic metric spaces, and suppose
X
is center-exhaustive. Let

be a homeomorphism that is quasi-symmetric, bi-Hölder, or strongly quasi-conformal. Then for any Morse gauge
N_{1}
, there exists a function
\eta_{N_{1}}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that for all
(p,q,r),(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime})\in\partial_{*}X^{(3,N_{1})}

\displaystyle d_{X}\!\bigl(\pi_{X}(p,q,r),\pi_{X}(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime})\bigr)\leq\theta

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle d_{Y}\!\bigl(\pi_{Y}(f(p),f(q),f(r)),\pi_{Y}(f(p^{\prime}),f(q^{\prime}),f(r^{\prime}))\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{N_{1}}(\theta).

Fix the Morse gauge
N
and constant
L

. Combining these with Proposition

Proposition 6.2
.

For any

, there exists a constant
D=D(t,N,L,f)
such that for all

d_{Y}(\Phi_{f}(x_{1}),\Phi_{f}(x_{2}))\leq D.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, choose a triple
(p_{i},q_{i},r_{i})\in\pi_{X}^{-1}(B(x_{i},L))\cap\partial_{*}X^{(3,N)}

\pi_{Y}(f(p_{i}),f(q_{i}),f(r_{i}))=\Phi_{f}(x_{i}).

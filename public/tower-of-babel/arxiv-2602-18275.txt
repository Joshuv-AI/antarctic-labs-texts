Abstract
. We show that the images of the Bethe subalgebras of the Yangians Y ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) and Y ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 m ) Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}) under the homomorphisms to U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n + m ) U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}) given by the Olshanski centralizer construction coincide. We use this result to obtain the ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n , 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 m ) (\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m}) -duality of the trigonometric Gaudin model and the XXX-spin chain. The duality is obtained in an explicit way relating the generating differential operator on one side and the generating difference operator on the other, thus agreeing with the result of Mukhin, Tarasov and Varchenko [ 17 ] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Two quantum integrable systems, one associated to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, the other to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
are called
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-dual in the following situation. They have the same space of states carrying both
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
- and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-symmetry, and the algebras of their quantum integrals of motion (called
Bethe algebras
) in this representation coincide.

The systems that are involved (or expected to be involved) in the
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality can be included in the following diagram.

rational Gaudin
XXX-spin chain
trigonometric Gaudin
XXZ-spin chain

U(\mathfrak{g}[t])

Y(\mathfrak{g})

U(\mathfrak{b}_{+}+t\mathfrak{g}[t])

U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

\star

In the dashed boxes, we indicate the algebras, which are crucial for the construction of the corresponding systems, that is, the space of states is naturally a module over this algebra and the quantum integrals of motion come from some universal commutative (Bethe) subalgebra in it. The dashed arrows on the diagram indicate that one integrable system can be degenerated to another, and the solid double arrows connect the dual systems if for one system in the dual pair, we take
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and for another,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. Therefore, the duality corresponds to the reflection of the diagram with respect to the vertical line passing through the middle. In this paper, we introduce a system that is denoted by the symbol

\star

on the diagram and show that it is self-dual. Then we consider a degeneration of our result and establish the duality between the XXX-spin chain and the trigonometric Gaudin model.

1.2.

First observations concerning the
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality in quantum integrable models were made by V. Tarasov and A. Varchenko in their work
[
27
]
, where it was shown that if we consider the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-module
S(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})
, then the images of the rational Gaudin Hamiltonians associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
coincide with the images of the Dynamical Hamiltonians associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. Since the Dynamical Hamiltonians are elements in the Bethe algebra of the Gaudin model, this was the first indication of the self-duality of the rational Gaudin model that was established later in
[
18
]
. In the work
[
27
]
, the authors also observed a similar relationship between the trigonometric Gaudin Hamiltonians and the XXX-Dynamical Hamiltonians, which suggested the duality between the trigonometric Gaudin model and the XXX-spin chain. The proof of the latter duality is one of the new results that we present in this paper.

1.3.

The Hamiltonians of all three systems mentioned in the previous paragraph can be diagonalized using the Bethe ansatz method. In the framework of this method, one starts with a vector space of functions in a variable
u
with prescribed singularities and behavior around these singularities and uses a certain procedure to construct an eigenvector, see
[
20
]
. It was discovered in
[
16
]
that a version of the Fourier transform provides a bijection between spaces describing eigenvectors in
S(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})
of rational
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-Gaudin Hamiltonians and dual to them
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-Dynamical Hamiltonians. This observation motivated the main result of the paper
[
18
]
, which states that the generating differential operator of the image of the Bethe algebra of the rational
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-Gaudin model in
\operatorname{End}(S(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m}))
can be obtained from the similar differential operator for the rational
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-Gaudin model by applying the following antiautomorphism of the algebra of linear differential operators in
u
with polynomial coefficients:

\partial_{u}\mapsto u,\quad u\mapsto\partial_{u},

where
\partial_{u}
is the derivative with respect to
u
.

One of the main results of the current paper, Theorem
5.1
, establishes a similar relationship between the generating differential operator for the Bethe algebra of the trigonometric
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-Gaudin model and the generating difference operator for the Bethe algebra of the XXX-spin chain associated to
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. The corresponding antihomomorphism is given by

u\partial_{u}\mapsto u,\quad u\mapsto\tau,

where
\tau
is the shift operator:
(\tau f)(u)=f(u+1)
. This result is motivated by the work
[
17
]
, where it was shown that a version of the Mellin transform provides a bijection between spaces of functions describing eigenvectors in
S(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})
of trigonometric
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-Gaudin Hamiltonians and dual to them XXX-Dynamical Hamiltonians associated to
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
.

We deduce Theorem
5.1
as a degeneration of a
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality statement for a new quantum integrable system coming from the Olshanski centralizer construction.

1.4.

The integrals of our new integrable system come from the commutative subalgebra
\mathcal{A}(\chi)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
which lies in invariants of the adjoint action of
GL_{n}\times GL_{m}
. Namely, this is the quantum
shift of argument subalgebra
of
[
24
]
with the Cartan parameter
\chi=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)
. By the general theorem of Olshanski
[
21
,
13
]
, the subalgebra of adjoint
GL_{n}\times GL_{m}
-invariants in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
is the image of
GL_{n}
-invariants in the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, and, at the same time, the image of
GL_{m}
-invariants
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
under some explicit homomorphisms. Moreover, by
[
8
]
, the images of
Bethe subalgebras
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and
\mathcal{B}_{m}
in
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
, lie in
\mathcal{A}(\chi)
. The generators of the Bethe subalgebra in the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
are coefficients of the difference operator
D_{n}
on the affine line. We consider the difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
with the coefficients in
\mathcal{A}(\chi)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
being the (appropriately normalized) images of
D_{n}
and
D_{m}
, respectively, under the Olshanski homomorphisms to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
.

Our main result is Theorem
3.2
, which identifies two difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
via an antihomomorphism given by

(1)

\begin{split}&(u-n+1)d\mapsto(u+n),\\
&u+m\mapsto(u-m+1)d,\end{split}

where
d=\tau-1
.

1.5.

After establishing the duality on the level of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
in Theorem
3.2
, we consider the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
on the Verma module
M_{\lambda}
of the generic highest weight
\lambda
. The upper-left and lower-right block Lie subalgebras
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
act on
M_{\lambda}
. The coefficients of the difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
act on the subspace
M^{sing}_{\lambda}[\nu,\mu]
of vectors of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight
\nu
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-weight
\mu
, which are singular with respect to both
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. Using the representation theory of Yangians, we construct an isomorphism of vector spaces
M^{sing}[\nu,\mu]\cong S^{(n)}(b)[a]
, where
S^{(n)}(b)[a]
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight subspace of weight
a=(a_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak a_{n})
of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module

S^{(n)}(b)=S^{b_{1}}\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{b_{2}}\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\dots\otimes S^{b_{m}}\mathbb{C}^{n}

for some
a
and
b=(b_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak b_{m})
depending on
\lambda
,
\mu
, and
\nu
. We also construct an isomorphism
M^{sing}[\nu,\mu]\cong S^{(m)}(a)[b]
, where
S^{(m)}(a)[b]
is defined similarly to
S^{(n)}(b)[a]
. Combining the two isomorphisms together, we get an isomorphism
\Psi_{\lambda}:S^{(m)}(a)[b]\rightarrow S^{(n)}(b)[a]
.

The map
\Psi_{\lambda}
should be considered as a deformation of the natural isomorphism
\Psi:S^{(m)}(a)[b]\rightarrow S^{(n)}(b)[a]
given via the identification of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-submodule
S^{(m)}(a)[b]
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-submodule
S^{(n)}(b)[a]
inside
S(C^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})
. It would be interesting to relate
\Psi_{\lambda}
to the construction in the work
[
29
]
on
(U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}),U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}))
-duality.

The duality on the level of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
implies that the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
intertwines the action of a commutative subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])
and the action of a similar commutative subalgebra in
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
, see Theorem
4.4
in the text. The elements of these commutative subalgebras are quantum integrals of the self-dual quantum integrable system we denoted by

\star

in the above diagram. It is obtained as a deformation of the XXX-model, similar to the deformation, which one uses to obtain trigonometric Gaudin model from the rational Gaudin model. Namely, we can consider the universal Gaudin Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}^{G}_{n}\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
, then the Hamiltonians of the rational Gaudin model are given as elements of the projection of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{G}
under its action on certain finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
-module
V
(which is usually a tensor product of evaluation modules), see
[
25
]
,
[
15
]
. On the other hand, we can consider the action of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{G}
on
V\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}}
, where
M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}}
is the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-Verma module of generic highest weight
\lambda^{(n)}
, and restrict it to the subspace
(V\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}})^{sing}[\nu]
of singular vectors of weight
\nu
. We have an isomorphism
(V\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}})^{sing}[\nu]\cong V[\nu-\lambda^{(n)}]
, and it is known that the trigonometric Gaudin Hamiltonians in
\operatorname{End}(V[\nu-\lambda^{(n)}])
are given as elements of the projection of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{G}
to
\operatorname{End}((V\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}})^{sing}[\nu])
, see
[
7
]
, and Section
5.2
below. If we consider the similar construction using the universal XXX-Bethe algebra instead of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{G}
, which is the Yangian Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
mentioned above, we get the system

\star

, whose self-duality is established in Theorem
4.4
using the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
. The relation of this system to the XXZ-spin chain model is not clear for us yet, but we expect that the former is a degeneration of the latter.

1.6.

After establishing the self-duality on the level of representations in Theorem
4.4
, we consider the following limit. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\lambda_{n+m})
be the highest weight of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}
-Verma module
M_{\lambda}
mentioned above. Then for each
i=n+1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n+m
, we replace
\lambda_{i}
with
r\xi_{i}
for some new parameters
r
and
\xi_{i}
and take the limit
r\rightarrow\infty
. We show that in this limit, the projection of the difference operator
\overline{D}_{m}
defines the generating difference operator of the XXX-Bethe algebra. We also show that in this limit, the projection of the difference operator
\overline{D}_{n}
becomes the generating
differential
operator of the trigonometric Gaudin model if prior to taking the limit, we also replace
u
with
ru
and
\tau
with
\exp(r^{-1}\partial_{u})
.

The most technically involved part of Section
5
deals with the limit of the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
. We first have to modify this map to make the corresponding limit invertible. Then we use the fact that for generic values of the parameters, the trigonometric Gaudin and XXX dynamical Hamiltonians are diagonalizable with one-dimensional common eigenspaces, and compare the limit of the modified
\Psi_{\lambda}
with the isomorphism
\Psi
, which we need for the duality of trigonometric Gaudin and XXX models, formulated in Theorem
5.1
.

1.7.

We conclude the introduction with a brief review of some other known results related to the the
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality in quantum integrable systems. Such a duality was studied in the setting of quantum toroidal algebras, see
[
2
]
. The self-duality of the rational Gaudin model was generalized to the case, when
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
is replaced with the super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|k}
, see
[
6
]
. We expect that our result can be also generalized to the case of super Lie algebras. The
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality of quantum models via the quantum-classical correspondence
[
19
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
10
]
is related to the so-called Ruijsenaars duality of classical integrable models, see
[
22
]
. Under the quantum-classical correspondence, the Gaudin model is interchanged with the Calogero model, and spin chains are interchanged with Ruijsenaars models. Since the Ruijsenaars duality is expected to involve elliptic models as well, this suggests that the
2\times 2
square diagram presented in Subsection
1.1
can be extended to a
3\times 3
square diagram.

As indicated in the work
[
23
]
, the correspondence of the Bethe vectors in some special cases may have a geometric interpretation via the 3d-mirror symmetry. It would be interesting to see how the equalities of the images of the Bethe algebras can be interpreted in these cases.

1.8.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some facts about Yangians and Bethe algebras. In Section
3
we study the images of the Yangian Bethe algebras in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
under the Olshanski homomorphism and prove that the generating difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
are related via the antihomomorphism (
1
). In Section
4
, we consider the action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}
on the Verma module
M_{\lambda}
and use the representation theory of Yangians to build the isomorphism
\Psi_{\lambda}:S^{(m)}(a)[b]\rightarrow S^{(n)}(b)[a]
. We then show that from the construction of
\Psi_{\lambda}
and the results of Section
3
, it follows that
\Psi_{\lambda}
intertwines the actions of the Bethe algebras of new integrable systems. In Section
5
, we consider the limit of the construction from Section
4
, and establish the duality between the trigonometric Gaudin and XXX-spin chain.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basics on Yangians and their representations

For any associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, let us denote the algebra of Laurent series in
u
with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}
as
\mathcal{A}((u))
. We will identify any rational function
f\in\mathbb{C}(u)
and its Laurent series at infinity, that is, we will think of
\mathcal{A}(u)=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}(u)
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
. Also, let us identify any homomorphism of associative algebras
h:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and its natural extensions to
\mathcal{A}((u))
and
\mathcal{A}\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

The Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is the associative unital algebra with generators
t_{ij}^{(r)}
,
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and relations defined as follows. Introduce the generating series
t_{ij}(u)\in Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})((u^{-1}))
:

t_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}t_{ij}^{(r)}u^{-r}.

Let
\{e_{i},\,i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, that is,
e_{i}=(0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0,1,0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0)
, where
1
is on the
i
-th place. For each
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, define
E_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by
E_{ij}e_{j}=e_{i}
. Introduce the elements
T_{1}(u)
,
T_{2}(v)
and
R_{12}(u,v)
of the tensor product
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})((u^{-1}))((v^{-1}))
:

T_{1}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes t_{ij}(u),\quad T_{2}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{id}\otimes E_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(v),

R_{12}(u,v)=u-v+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}\otimes 1.

Then the relations in
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
are given by the following formula:

(2)

R_{12}(u-v)T_{1}(u)T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)T_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v).

Let
\{e_{ij}\,|\,i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\}
be the standard basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, that is, the image of
e_{ij}
under the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the operator
E_{ij}
. Then we have the evaluation homomorphism
ev_{n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
given by

ev_{n}:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}+\frac{e_{ij}}{u}.

Also, for any
c\in\mathbb{C}
, we have the shift homomorphism
sh_{c}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\rightarrow Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
,
sh_{c}(t_{ij}(u))=t_{ij}(u-c)
.
If
V
is a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module, let
V(c)
denote the
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module obtained by pulling back
V
via
sh_{c}\circ ev_{n}
. The module
V(c)
is called an evaluation module with evaluation parameter
c
.

Let
W
be a
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module. We say that a vector
w\in W
has a
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-weight
(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\lambda_{n}(u))\in\bigl(\mathbb{C}((u^{-1}))\bigr)^{n}
if for any
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, we have
t_{ii}(u)w=\lambda_{i}(u)w
. Then
W
is called a
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module of highest
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-weight
\overline{\lambda}(u)\in\bigl(\mathbb{C}((u^{-1}))\bigr)^{n}
if
W=Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\,v
for some
v
that has weight
\overline{\lambda}(u)
and such that
t_{ij}(u)v=0
for any
i<j
. For any
\overline{\lambda}(u)\in\bigl(\mathbb{C}((u^{-1}))\bigr)^{n}
, there exists unique up to isomorphism irreducible
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module of highest
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-weight
\overline{\lambda}(u)
.

2.2.
Bethe subalgebras of Yangians

For any associative unital algebra
\mathcal{A}
, let
D(\mathcal{A})
denote the algebra of difference operators in the variable
u
with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
, that is,
D(\mathcal{A})
is the unital subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}\bigl(\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))\bigr)
generated by
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
and the shift operator
\tau
given by

(\tau\cdot f)(u)=f(u+1),\quad f(u)\in\mathcal{A}((u^{-1})).

Here and throughout the paper, we identify
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
with its image in
\operatorname{End}\bigl(\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))\bigr)
under the regular representation and use the following notation: for any
D\in D(\mathcal{A})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
,
D\cdot f\in\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
denotes the result of the action of
D
on
f
, while
Df\in D(\mathcal{A})
is just a product of
D
and
f
in
D(\mathcal{A})
. We will also identify any homomorphism of associative algebras
h:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and its natural extension to
D(\mathcal{A})
.

Fix a sequence of complex numbers
C=(c_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak c_{n})
, and define

D_{n}(C)\in D(Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
as follows:

(3)

D_{n}(C)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sigma}D_{\sigma(1),1}D_{\sigma(2),2}\dots D_{\sigma(n),n},

where

(4)

D_{ij}=c_{i}\delta_{ij}\tau-t_{ij}(u-j+1).

Let
B_{ij}(C)\in Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the coefficients of
D_{n}(C)
:

(5)

D_{n}(C)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B_{ij}(C)u^{-j}\right)\tau^{n-i}.

Define the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}(C)
to be the unital subalgebra of
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
generated by
B_{ij}(C)\in Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. It is known, see
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
, that
\mathcal{B}_{n}(C)
is commutative. If
C=(1,1,\dots,1)
, we will write
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
B_{ij}
, and
D_{n}
for
\mathcal{B}_{n}(C)
,
B_{ij}(C)
and
D_{n}(C)
, respectively.

Denote
B_{i}(u,C)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B_{ij}(C)u^{-j}
.
For each
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subset\{1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\}
, introduce the quantum minor
t_{J}(u)
as follows:

(6)

t_{J}(u)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(-1)^{\sigma}t_{j_{\sigma(1)},j_{1}}(u)t_{j_{\sigma(2)},j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{j_{\sigma(k)},j_{k}}(u-k+1).

It is easy to see that

(7)

B_{k}(u,C)=\sum_{J,\,|J|=k}\left(\prod_{j\notin J}c_{j}\right)t_{J}(u),

In particular,
B_{n}(u,C)
does not depend on
C
. It is known, see
[
11
]

[
14
]
, that
B_{n}(u,C)
is a generating series for the center of
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. It is called the quantum determinant, and we will denote it as
\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u)
.

3.
The duality on the level of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})

3.1.
Olshanski homomorphism

The element
T(u)\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})((u^{-1}))
defined by

T(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)

is invertible. For each
i.j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, define
t^{\prime}_{ij}(u)
by

. Write

.

Introduce the following inclusions:

\displaystyle i_{n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\hookrightarrow Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}),\quad i_{n}(t_{ij}(u))=t_{ij}(u),

\displaystyle j_{m}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\hookrightarrow Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}),\quad j_{m}(t_{ij}(u))=t_{n+i,n+j}(u).

We will be interested in the following compositions (which are also inclusions):

\phi_{n}=\omega_{n+m}\circ i_{n}\circ\omega_{n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\hookrightarrow Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}),\quad\psi_{m}=\omega_{n+m}\circ j_{m}\circ\omega_{m}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\hookrightarrow Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}).

The map
\psi_{m}

presented by G. Olshanski in the work

, and we call it the Olshanski homomorphism, and

is a similar map, which can be obtained from

using the automorphism

:

,
t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{n-j+1,n-i+1}(-u)
as follows:

(8)

\phi_{n}\delta_{n}=\delta_{n+m}\psi_{n}.

The last formula is an immediate consequence of

(9)

i_{n}\delta_{n}=\delta_{n+m}j_{n}

and
\omega_{n}\delta_{n}=\delta_{n}\omega_{n}
, which can be checked directly.

For any associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
and

, introduce a linear map
\widehat{\delta}_{l}:D(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow D(\mathcal{A})
as follows:

\widehat{\delta}_{l}:\sum_{i}b_{i}(u)\tau^{i}\mapsto\sum_{i}\tau^{i}b_{i}(-u+l-1).

The quantum minors introduced in formula (

t_{J}(u)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(-1)^{\sigma}t_{j_{1},j_{\sigma(1)}}(u-k+1)t_{j_{2},j_{\sigma(2)}}(u-k+2)\dots t_{j_{k},j_{\sigma(k)}}(u),

see

.
Using that, one can check that for any

\delta_{n}(t_{J}(u))=t_{J^{\prime}}(-u+k-1),

where

. Together with formula (

\delta_{n}(D_{n})=\widehat{\delta}_{n}(D_{n}).

.

Denote
C_{1,0}=(1,1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 1,0,0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0)
with

ones and

i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n))\,\psi_{m}(D_{m})=(-1)^{n}\tau^{n}D_{n+m}(C_{0,1})\tau^{-n}.

(13)

j_{m}(\operatorname{qdet}_{m}(-u-1))\,\phi_{n}(\widehat{\delta}_{n}D_{n})=(-1)^{m}\tau^{m}\widehat{\delta}_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C_{1,0}))\tau^{-m},

Proof.

The proof of (

\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u)\cdot\omega_{n}(t_{J}(-u+n-1))=t_{J^{c}}(u),

where

.

Relation (

to the both sides of (

), and (

3.2.
The duality

Denote
d=\tau-1
. For any
c\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathcal{P}(c)
be the unital subalgebra of
D(\mathbb{C})
generated by
u
and
(u-c)d
. It is easy to check that any element of
\mathcal{P}(c)
can be written in the form
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{l}a_{ij}u^{i}((u-c)d)^{j}
for some
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
. It can be checked that there is an anti-isomorphism of algebras
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}:\mathcal{P}(n-1)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(m-1)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n,m}:\quad

\displaystyle(u-n+1)d\mapsto(u+n),

\displaystyle u+m\mapsto(u-m+1)d.

Let
ev_{n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
be the evaluation homomorphism:

(15)

ev_{n}:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}+\frac{e_{ij}}{u}.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote
u^{\overline{j}}=u(u+1)\dots(u+j-1)
and
u^{\underline{j}}=u(u-1)\dots(u-j+1)
.
Define the following difference operators:

(16)

\overline{D}_{m}=(u+n)^{\underline{n+m}}\,ev_{n+m}\left[i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n))\,\psi_{m}(D_{m})\right],

(17)

\overline{D}_{n}=(u+m)^{\underline{n+m}}\,ev_{n+m}\left[j_{m}(\operatorname{qdet}_{m}(-u-1))\,\phi_{n}(\widehat{\delta}_{n}D_{n})\right].

Let us identify
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
with its extension
\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(n-1)
.
The following theorem is the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.2
.

The difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
belong to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(n-1)
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(m-1)
, respectively, and we have

(18)

\mathcal{F}_{n,m}\bigl(\overline{D}_{n}\bigr)=\overline{D}_{m}.

Proof.

Using formulas (
3
), (
4
), (
5
), (
15
), we can write explicitly:

(19)

ev_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C_{1,0}))=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+m}}(-1)^{\sigma}\,\overset{\longrightarrow}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}}\left(d\,\delta_{\sigma(i),i}-\frac{e_{\sigma(i),i}}{u-i+1}\right)\overset{\longrightarrow}{\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+m}}\left(-\delta_{\sigma(i),i}-\frac{e_{\sigma(i),i}}{u-i+1}\right),

(20)

ev_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C_{0,1}))=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+m}}(-1)^{\sigma}\,\overset{\longrightarrow}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}}\left(-\delta_{\sigma(i),i}-\frac{e_{\sigma(i),i}}{u-i+1}\right)\overset{\longrightarrow}{\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+m}}\left(d\,\delta_{\sigma(i),i}-\frac{e_{\sigma(i),i}}{u-i+1}\right),

where arrows over products mean that we choose the following ordering: the index
i
increases as we go from the left to the right.

For each subset
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n+m\}
, introduce the following notation:
J_{1}=J\cap\{1,\dots,n\}
,

J_{2}=J\cap\{n+1,\dots,n+m\}
,
\bar{J}=\{1,\dots,n+m\}\setminus J
, as well as

D^{(n)}_{J_{1}}=\frac{1}{u^{\underline{j_{1}-1}}}d\frac{1}{(u-j_{1})^{\underline{j_{2}-j_{1}-1}}}d\frac{1}{(u-j_{2})^{\underline{j_{3}-j_{2}-1}}}d\dots d\frac{1}{(u-j_{r})^{\underline{n-j_{r}}}},

where
r=|J_{1}|
, and

D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}=\frac{1}{(u-n)^{\underline{j_{r+1}-n-1}}}d\frac{1}{(u-j_{r+1})^{\underline{j_{r+2}-j_{r+1}-1}}}d\frac{1}{(u-j_{r+2})^{\underline{j_{r+3}-j_{r+2}-1}}}d\dots d\frac{1}{(u-j_{k})^{\underline{n+m-j_{k}}}}.

Also, for each
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n+m\}
and
\sigma\in S_{n+m-|J|}
, define

e_{\sigma,J}=e_{j_{\sigma(1)},j_{1}}e_{j_{\sigma(2)},j_{2}}\dots e_{j_{\sigma(k)},j_{k}}.

From (
19
) and (
20
), we get:

(21)

ev_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C_{1,0}))=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n+m\}}(-1)^{n+m-|J_{1}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+m-|J|}}(-1)^{\sigma}D^{(n)}_{J_{1}}\prod_{j\notin J_{2}}(u-j+1)^{-1}e_{\sigma,\bar{J}},

(22)

ev_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C_{0,1}))=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n+m\}}(-1)^{n+m-|J_{2}|}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+m-|J|}}(-1)^{\sigma}\prod_{j\notin J_{1}}(u-j+1)^{-1}D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}e_{\sigma,\bar{J}}.

Since for each
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

d\frac{1}{(u-c)^{\underline{j}}}=\frac{1}{(u+1-c)^{\underline{j}}}d-\frac{j}{(u+1-c)^{\underline{j+1}}},

the difference operator
D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}
is of the form
\sum_{l=0}^{k-r}a_{l}((u-n)^{\underline{m-l}})^{-1}d^{l}
, where
a_{l}
are some complex numbers. We have
(u-n)^{\underline{m}}((u-n)^{\underline{m-l}})^{-1}=(u-n-m+1)^{\overline{l}}
, and it is easy to show by induction on
l
that
(u-n-m+1)^{\overline{l}}d^{l}=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\tilde{a}_{i}((u-n-m+1)d)^{i}
for some complex numbers
\tilde{a}_{i}
. Therefore,
u^{\underline{m}}\tau^{n}D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}\tau^{-n}\in\mathcal{P}(m-1)
.
This, together with formulas (
12
) and (
22
), shows that
\overline{D}_{m}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(m-1)
. Similarly, one can prove that
\overline{D_{n}}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(n-1)
.

Comparing formulas (
21
) and (
22
), we see that it is enough to prove that for any
J\subset\{1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n+m\}
, we have

(23)

(u-n)^{\underline{m}}D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}=\prod_{j\in J_{2}}((u-n-m+1)d-(n+m-j))

and

(24)

(u-m)^{\underline{n}}\,\widehat{\delta}_{n+m}D^{(n)}_{J_{1}}=(-1)^{n-|J_{1}|}\prod_{j\in J_{1}}((u-n-m+1)d-j+1).

We will prove relation (
23
). Relation (
24
) can be proved similarly.

It is easy to check that both
D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}
and
\prod_{j\in J_{2}}((u-n-m+1)d-(n+m-j))
annihilate functions

f_{j}=(u-n-m+1)^{\overline{n+m-j}},\quad j\in J_{2}.

Denote
\tilde{D}=D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}-\prod_{j\in J_{2}}((u-n-m+1)d-(n+m-j))
. Then
\tilde{D}
is a difference operator of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}(u)((u-n-m-1)d)^{i}
. for some
s<|J_{2}|=\deg D^{(m)}_{J_{2}}
.

Suppose that
\tilde{D}\neq 0
. Then
\tilde{D}\cdot f_{j}=0
,
j\in J_{2}
and
s<|J_{2}|
imply that the difference Wronskian

\det\bigl((((u-n-m+1)d)^{p-1}\cdot f_{j_{s}})_{p,s=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak|J_{2}|}\bigr)
is identically zero.

On the other hand, straightforward computation gives

\det\bigl((((u-n-m+1)d)^{p-1}\cdot f_{j_{s}})_{p,s=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak|J_{2}|}\bigr)=\prod_{j\in J_{2}}(u-n-m+1)^{\overline{n+m-j}}\,\det\bigl(((n+m-j_{s})^{p-1})_{p,s=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak|J_{2}|}\bigr).

Since
j_{s}\neq j_{p}
for
s\neq p
, the Vandermonde determinant in the right hand side of the last formula is not zero. Therefore,
\tilde{D}=0
, and relation (
23
) is proved.
∎

Let
\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
be the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
. For any subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
, we denote the subalgebra, generated by
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
by
\mathcal{A}\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
. We can now use Theorem
3.2
to establish the duality on the level of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
:

Corollary 3.3
.

The following equality holds:

(25)

ev_{n+m}(\phi_{n}(\mathcal{B}_{n}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}=\,ev_{n+m}(\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}.

Proof.

Since by Theorem
3.2
, the difference operators
\overline{D}_{n}
and
\overline{D}_{m}
belong to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(n-1)
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})\otimes\mathcal{P}(m-1)
, respectively, we can write

\overline{D}_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{M}\sum_{j=0}^{n}A^{(n)}_{ij}(u+m)^{i}((u-n+1)d)^{j},\quad\overline{D}_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{N}A^{(m)}_{ji}(u+n)^{j}((u-m+1)d)^{i}

for some
M,N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
A^{(n)}_{ij},A^{(m)}_{ji}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
.

Using (
14
), one can check that
i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n))=qdet_{n+m}(u+n)\psi_{m}(\operatorname{qdet}^{-1}_{m}(u))
. Since the coefficients of
ev_{n+m}(qdet_{n+m}(u+n))
belong to
\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
and the coefficients of
\operatorname{qdet}^{-1}_{m}(u)
belong to
\mathcal{B}_{m}
, we deduce that the coefficients of
ev_{n+m}(i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n)))
belong to
ev_{n+m}(\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
. Therefore, it follows from the definition of
\overline{D}_{m}
(see (
16
)) that
A^{(m)}_{ij}
are elements of
ev_{n+m}(\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
. On the other hand. comparing coefficients for
d^{m}
on the both sides of equation (
16
), we see that

(u-m+1)^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{N}A^{(m)}_{jm}(u+n)^{j}=(u+n)^{\underline{n+m}}\,ev_{n+m}\left[i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n))\right].

Hence, coefficients of
ev_{n+m}\left[i_{n}(\operatorname{qdet}_{n}(u+n))\right]^{-1}
can be expressed polynomially through
A^{(m)}_{ij}
. Therefore, using formula (
16
), we can express all coefficients through
A^{(m)}_{ij}
, thus, the elements
\tilde{A}^{(m)}_{ji}
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak N
generate the algebra
ev_{n+m}(\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
. Similarly, we prove that the elements
A^{(n)}_{ij}
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak M
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
generate the algebra
ev_{n+m}(\phi_{n}(\mathcal{B}_{n}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}
.
But by relation (
18
), we can take
N=n
,
M=m
, and for each
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have
A^{(n)}_{ij}=A^{(m)}_{ji}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It’s not hard to prove Corollary

without Theorem

using the argument that we briefly outline now. We thank Vitaly Tarasov for pointing this out.

For any
C=(c_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak c_{n+m})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C)
be the subalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
generated by the coefficients of the difference operator
ev_{n+m}(D_{n+m}(C))
, that is
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C)=ev_{n+m}(\mathcal{B}_{n+m}(C))
. It follows from relations (
12
), (
13
), (
16
), (
17
), and the proof of Corollary
3.3
that equality (
25
) is the same as

(26)

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C_{1,0})=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C_{0,1}).

It’s not hard to see that for any non-zero complex number
a
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{n+m}(aC)=\mathcal{B}_{n+m}(C)
(this just corresponds to changing
\tau
to
a\tau
in
D_{n+m}(C)
). Therefore, for any
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
a\neq 0
, we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(aC)=\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C)
.

On the other hand,
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C)
can be obtained as the image under the evaluation map of the universal Gaudin Bethe algebra
\mathfrak{B}_{n+m}(C)\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}[t])
. For the construction of

with

below. To get
\mathfrak{B}_{n+m}(C)
for any

, we just need to modify

as follows:
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(\partial_{u}+c_{i})-e_{ij}(u)

follows from the fact that the commutation relation for operators
\partial_{u}
,
u
is the same as for the operators
\tau^{-1}
,
\tau u
. But it follows from the construction of
\mathfrak{B}_{n+m}(C)
that it doesn’t change if we add a vector of the form

to

Now, since

The invariance of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n+m}(C)
under transformation

can also be explained naturally in the context of quantum shift of argument algebra, see the next section for details.

3.3.
Shift of argument subalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be any complex reductive Lie algebra. The following
argument shift method
gives a way to construct
subalgebras in
S(\mathfrak{g})
commutative with respect to the
Poisson–Lie bracket. Let

ZS(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
be the center of
S(\mathfrak{g})
with respect to
the Poisson bracket, and let
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then the algebra
A(\chi)\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
generated by the elements of
ZS(\mathfrak{g})\subset S(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]

shifted by
t\chi
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}
is commutative with respect
to the Poisson bracket, see
[
3
]
. The subalgebra
A(\chi)\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
is called the
shift of argument subalgebra
or
Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebra
. Identifying
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the help of an invariant non-degenerate inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
, we can regard
\chi
as an element of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{z}(\chi)
be the centralizer of
\chi
in
\mathfrak{g}
. For semisimple
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}
, the subalgebra
A(\chi)
is known to be a maximal Poisson-commutative subalgebra of the maximal possible transcendence degree in the algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{z}(\chi)}
of
\mathfrak{z}(\chi)
-invariants in
S(\mathfrak{g})
. There is a natural lifting of
A(\chi)
to a commutative subalgebra
\mathcal{A}(\chi)\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
, called the
quantum
shift of argument subalgebra, see
[
24
]
and
[
1
]
for details.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}
and let
\chi\in\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m}
, i.e. a diagonal matrix. Then the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}(\chi)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
is the only commutative subalgebra such that
A(\chi)=\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}(\chi)
and
\mathcal{A}(\chi)\subset U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})^{\mathfrak{z}(\chi)}
(this follows from
[
5
, Corollary 10.12]
and
[
26
]
). Since
A(\chi)\subset S(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
does not change under dilations of
\chi
and under adding a scalar matrix to
\chi
, we have the following:

Proposition 3.5
.

Denote
I=\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}e_{ii}
. Then for any
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
,
a\neq 0
, we have

(27)

\mathcal{A}(a\chi+bI)=\mathcal{A}(\chi).

Consider the composition of the Olshanski homomorphism and the evaluation map
ev_{n+m}\circ\phi_{n}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
. Clearly, it takes Bethe subalgebras in the Yangian to some commutative subalgebras in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+m})
. We have the following

Theorem 3.6
.

[
8
]

For any
C=(c_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak c_{n})
, we have
ev_{n+m}\circ\phi_{n}(\mathcal{B}_{n}(C))\subset\mathcal{A}(C,0)
, where
(C,0)
is the block diagonal matrix with the upper-left
n\times n
block
\operatorname{diag}(c_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak c_{n})
and the lower-right
m\times m
block
0
.

In particular,
ev_{n+m}\circ\phi_{n}(\mathcal{B}_{n}(C))\subset\mathcal{A}(C_{1,0})
. Similarly, one can get
ev_{n+m}\circ\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}(C))\subset\mathcal{A}(C_{0,1})
. Actually, we expect that
ev_{n+m}(\phi_{n}(\mathcal{B}_{n}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}=\mathcal{A}(C_{1,0})
and
ev_{n+m}(\psi_{m}(\mathcal{B}_{m}))\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{n+m}=\mathcal{A}(C_{1,0})
. Then, since
C_{1,0}=-C_{1,0}+I
, Corollary
3.3
is just a special case of Proposition
3.5
. This observation was our first motivation to look at relation (
27
) in the context of the
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-duality.

4.
The duality on the level of representations

4.1.

For any
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-module
W
, let
W[b]
denote the weight subspace of
W
of weight
b
. Recall that a vector

is called singular if
e_{ij}w=0
for any
i<j
. Denote by

the subspace of singular vectors in

.

Let

be a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-module, and let

be the Verma
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
-module of highest weight

. Fix a vector

. Consider a linear map
\theta^{V}_{\mu,b}:(V\otimes M_{\mu})^{m\text{-sing}}[\mu+b]\rightarrow V[b]

(28)

v=\theta^{V}_{\mu,b}(v)\otimes v_{\mu}+\sum_{i}w_{i}\otimes v_{i},

We have homomorphisms
\widetilde{ev}_{n}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and
\widetilde{sh}_{w}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
, where
w\in\mathbb{C}
, given by
\widetilde{ev}_{n}(e_{ij}(u))=e_{ij}\cdot u^{-1}
,
\widetilde{sh}_{w}(e_{ij}(u))=e_{ij}(u-w)
. If
V
is a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module, let
V(w)
be the
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
-module obtained by pulling back
V
via
\widetilde{sh}_{w}\circ\widetilde{ev}_{n}
. The module
V(w)
is called an evaluation module with evaluation parameter
w
.

Consider the following tensor product of evaluation
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
-modules:

(37)

S_{U}^{(n)}(b,\xi,\lambda^{(n)})=S^{b_{1}}\mathbb{C}^{n}(\xi_{1})\otimes S^{b_{2}}\mathbb{C}^{n}(\xi_{2})\otimes\dots\otimes S^{b_{m}}\mathbb{C}^{n}(\xi_{m})\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}}(0).

As a subalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
, the universal Gaudin Bethe algebra
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
acts on
S_{U}^{(n)}(b,\xi,\lambda^{(n)})
. Since
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
commutes with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\otimes 1\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
, it acts on
(S^{(n)}(b)\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}})^{sing}[\lambda^{(n)}+a]\subset S_{U}^{(n)}(b,\xi,\lambda^{(n)})
. Let
\rho^{G}_{b,\,\xi,\,\lambda^{(n)}}:\mathfrak{B}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}((S^{(n)}(b)\otimes M^{(n)}_{\lambda^{(n)}})^{sing}[\lambda^{(n)}+a])
be the corresponding homomorphism.

Introduce the differential operator

\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})=\widehat{\theta^{S^{(n)}(b)}_{\lambda^{(n)},a}}\left(\rho^{G}_{b,\,\xi,\,\lambda^{(n)}}(\mathfrak{D}_{n})\right)\in\mathfrak{D}(\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])).

By construction, the differential operator
\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is of the form

\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B^{tG}_{ij}u^{-j}\right)\partial_{u}^{n-i}

for some commuting linear operators
B^{tG}_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
. The subalgebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
of
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
generated by
B^{tG}_{ij}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is called the Bethe algebra of the trigonometric Gaudin model, see
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
.

5.3.
The duality

Let
\{e^{(n)}_{i},\,i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, that is,
e_{i}^{(n)}=(0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0,1,0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0)
, where
1
is on the
i
-th place. Let
\{e^{(m)}_{i},\,i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m\}
be the similar basis of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. For any
n\times m
matrix
K=(k_{ij})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\\
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m\end{subarray}}
with non-negative integer entries, define vectors
v^{(n)}_{K}\in(S\mathbb{C}^{(n)})^{\otimes m}
and
v^{(m)}_{K}\in(S\mathbb{C}^{(m)})^{\otimes n}
as follows:

v_{K}^{(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}^{(n)})^{k_{i1}}\otimes\prod_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}^{(n)})^{k_{i2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\prod_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}^{(n)})^{k_{im}},

v_{K}^{(m)}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(e_{i}^{(m)})^{k_{1i}}\otimes\prod_{i=1}^{m}(e_{i}^{(m)})^{k_{2i}}\otimes\dots\otimes\prod_{i=1}^{m}(e_{i}^{(m)})^{k_{ni}}.

Consider the following linear isomorphism

(38)

\begin{split}\Psi:(S\mathbb{C}^{(m)})^{\otimes n}&\rightarrow(S\mathbb{C}^{(m)})^{\otimes n},\\
v_{K}^{(m)}&\mapsto v_{K}^{(n)}.\end{split}

Notice that

S^{(m)}(a)[b]=\{v_{K}^{(m)}\,|\,K=(k_{ij})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\\
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m\end{subarray}},\,\sum_{j}k_{ij}=a_{i},\,\sum_{i}k_{ij}=b_{j}\},

S^{(n)}(b)[a]=\{v_{K}^{(n)}\,|\,K=(k_{ij})_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n\\
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m\end{subarray}},\,\sum_{j}k_{ij}=a_{i},\,\sum_{i}k_{ij}=b_{j}\}.

Therefore,
\Psi(S^{(m)}(a)[b])=S^{(n)}(b)[a]
, and we have an isomorphism
\widehat{\Psi}:\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
. The next theorem is the main result of Section
5
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Denote
\boldsymbol{n}=(n,n,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For any
\lambda^{(n)}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, there exists generators
\widetilde{A}^{(m)}_{ij}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, of
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{n};b,\xi)\subset\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])
and generators
\widetilde{A}^{(n)}_{ji}
,

,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
such that

(39)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i}-n)\,\,D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{n};b,\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\widetilde{A}^{(m)}_{ij}u^{i}\tau^{j},

(40)

u^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-\xi_{j})\,\,\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\widetilde{A}^{(n)}_{ji}u^{j}(u\partial_{u})^{i},

and for each

,

, we have

The rest of the Section

will be devoted to the proof of Theorem

with

,

.

5.4.
Filtration and grading

Suppose that
I
is a totally ordered set. Let
V
be an
I
-filtered vector space, that is
V=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{F}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in I
, are vector subspaces of
V
called filtration components such that for any
i,j\in I
,
i\leq j
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}_{j}
. Then we denote by
gr\,V
the associated graded vector space of
V
, that is,
gr\,V=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\bigl(\sum_{j<i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\bigr)\bigr)
. For any
i\in I
, let
gr_{i}:\mathcal{F}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{i}/\bigl(\sum_{j<i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\bigr)
be the canonical projection. If
v\in V
, define the degree
\deg(v)
of
v
as the element
i\in I
such that
v\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
and
v\notin\mathcal{F}_{j}
for
j<i
. Denote
gr\,v=gr_{\deg(v)}v
.

Now, suppose that
I
is a totally ordered abelian group, and let
\mathcal{A}
be an associative algebra. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is an
I
-filtered algebra (or on
\mathcal{A}
we have a filtration) if
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered vector space with filtration components
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in I
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
h\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
, we have
fh\in\mathcal{F}_{i+j}
. If
\mathcal{A}
is an
I
-filtered algebra, then
gr\,\mathcal{A}
is naturally an associative algebra (called the associated graded algebra of
\mathcal{A}
) with multiplication given as follows: for any
i,j\in I
,
f\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
h\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
, we have
gr_{i}(f)\cdot gr_{j}(h)=gr_{i+j}(fh)
.

Almost everywhere below, we will have
I=\mathbb{Z}
. The only exception is Subsection
5.7.2
, where we will need
I=\mathbb{Q}
.

5.5.
Degeneration to trigonometric Gaudin model

Recall the evaluation homomorphism
ev_{n,c}:Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, where
c\in\mathbb{C}
:

ev_{n,c}:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}+\frac{e_{ij}}{u-c}.

We can think of
c
as variable, then
\frac{1}{u-c}
becomes an element of
(\mathbb{C}[c])((u^{-1}))
, and
ev_{n,c}
becomes a map

Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{C}[c]
.

Therefore, in the construction of the algebra
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
, we can consider
\lambda^{(m)}=(\lambda_{n+1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\lambda_{n+m})
as a set of algebraically independent commuting variables. Namely, denoting the field of of rational functions of
\lambda^{(m)}
as
\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
, let us think of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
as a subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
generated by the image of the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and
\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
.

In a similar way, we can consider the parameters
\xi=(\xi_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\xi_{m})
introduced in Section
5.1
as variables. Let
\mathbb{C}(r,\xi)
be the algebra of rational functions in variables
\xi_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\xi_{m}
and
r
. Any polynomial in
r
and
\xi
is invertible in
\mathbb{C}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
, so we can consider
\mathbb{C}(r,\xi)
as a subalgebra in
\mathbb{C}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
. If
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}r^{k-i}\in\mathbb{C}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
, define the degree
\deg(f)
of
f
by
\deg(f)=k
. Consider the homomorphism of algebras
\pi_{r,\xi}:\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(r,\xi)
given by
\pi_{r,\xi}(\lambda_{n+i})=r\xi_{i}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
. Then for any
p\in\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
, define the degree
\deg(p)
of
p
by
\deg(p)=\deg(\pi_{r,\xi}(p))
. For any vector space
V
, introduce a filtration on
V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
by defining the filtration components
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
as follows:

(41)

\mathcal{F}_{i}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{v\otimes p\,|\,\deg(p)\leq i\}.

If
f=\sum_{i}v_{i}\otimes p_{i}\in V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
is of degree
k
, and we have
\pi_{r,\xi}(p_{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{ij}(\xi)r^{k-j}
, let us identify
gr\,(f)
and
\sum_{i}v_{i}\otimes p_{i0}(\xi)
, that is, we look at
gr\,(V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)}))
as a subspace of
V\otimes\mathbb{C}(\xi)
.

For any complex number
c
, one can easily prove by induction on
i
the following relation:

(42)

(u-c)^{\overline{i}}d^{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}((u-c)d-j+1).

It follows from Theorem
4.4
and formula (
42
) that we can find elements
C_{ij}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
such that

(43)

u^{\underline{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-w_{i})\,\,D_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}C_{ij}(u+m)^{i}(u-n+1)^{\overline{j}}d^{j}.

Proposition 5.2
.

For each
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, we have
\deg(C_{ij})\leq m-i
, and the following holds:

(44)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\,gr_{m-i}\,(C_{ij})\,u^{i+j}\partial_{u}^{j}=u^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-\xi_{i})\,\,\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)}).

Proof.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered algebra with filtration components
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let us define a filtration on the algebra
D(\mathcal{A})
of difference operators with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
as follows. Consider elements of
D(\mathcal{A})
as series in
u^{-1}
and
\partial_{u}
by identifying
\tau
and
e^{\partial_{u}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial_{u}^{k}}{k!}
. Then the filtration components
\mathcal{F}^{D}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
of
D(\mathcal{A})
are given by

\mathcal{F}^{D}_{i}=\{\sum_{k,l}a_{kl}u^{-k}\partial_{u}^{l}\,|\,a_{kl}\in\mathcal{F}_{i+k+l}\text{ for all }k,l\},

in particular,
\deg\,u=1
and
\deg\,d=-1
Also, we have
gr(D(\mathcal{A}))\subset\mathfrak{D}(gr\,A)
, where
\mathfrak{D}(gr\,A)
is the algebra of differential operators with coefficients in
(gr\,A)((u^{-1}))
, see Section
5.2
for notation.

Introduce a filtration on
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
by defining the filtration components
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{Y}\subset Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, as follows

\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{i}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{(r_{1})}t_{i_{2}j_{2}}^{(r_{2})}\dots t_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{(r_{k})}\,|\,\sum_{s=1}^{k}r_{s}\leq i+k\}.

Then
gr\,Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})=U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
(see, for example, Section 1.5 in
[
14
]
). Recall the difference operator
D_{n}\in D(Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
introduced in Section
2.2
. Theorem 10.2 in
[
15
]
states that
\deg D_{n}=-n
and

(45)

gr\,D_{n}=\mathfrak{D}_{n},

where
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
is the differential operator from Section
5.2
.

Notice that
ev_{n,c}(t_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{(r_{1})}t_{i_{2}j_{2}}^{(r_{2})}\dots t_{i_{k}j_{k}}^{(r_{k})})
is a polynomial in
c
of degree
\sum_{s=1}^{k}r_{s}-k
. Therefore, the filtration on
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is consistent with filtration (
41
) on
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
, and formula (
45
) implies

(46)

gr\,\left(u^{\underline{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-w_{i})\,\,D_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})\right)=u^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-\xi_{i})\,\,\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)}).

On the other hand, using that
\deg D_{n}=-n
and
\deg\,u=1
, we see that the degree of the left hand side of (
43
) is equal to
m
. Therefore, the degree of each term in the right hand side of (
43
) is less or equal then
m
, which gives
\deg(C_{ij})\leq m-i
, and we have

(47)

\begin{split}gr\,\left(u^{\underline{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u-w_{i})\,\,D_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})\right)&=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\,gr_{m}\left(C_{ij}(u+m)^{i}(u-n+1)^{\overline{j}}d^{j}\right)=\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\,gr_{m-i}\,(C_{ij})\,u^{i+j}\partial_{u}^{j}.\end{split}

Comparing formulas (
46
) and (
47
), we see that the proposition is proved.
∎

5.6.
Degeneration to XXX spin chain model

Let us continue working with
\lambda^{(m)}
as the set of commuting variables. We would like to consider the algebra

is defined as follows. First, consider the
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}

with the quotient
(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)}))/J

\mathfrak{n}_{-}

,

41
).

It follows from Theorem
4.4
that we can find elements
D_{ij}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})
,

,

(48)

Proposition 5.3
.

For each
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, we have
\deg(D_{ij})\leq m-i
, and the following holds:

(49)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\,gr_{m-i}\,(D_{ij})\,(u+n)^{\underline{j}}\tau^{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\,\,D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi).

Proof.

In this proof, for any
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered algebra
\mathcal{A}
with filtration components
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we will use the following filtration on
\mathcal{A}((u^{-1}))
: the corresponding filtration components
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
are given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{i}=\{\sum_{l}f_{l}u^{l}\,|\,f_{l}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\}.

In particular,
\deg u=0
, note the difference with the proof of the previous proposition.

Fix
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subset\{1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m\}
. Apply the comultiplication
\Delta
to a quantum minor
t_{J}(u)\in Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})((u^{-1}))
. We get

(50)

\begin{split}\Delta(t_{J}(u))=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(-1)^{\sigma}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak i_{k}=1}^{m}\bigl(t_{j_{\sigma(1)}i_{1}}(u)t_{j_{\sigma(2)}i_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{j_{\sigma(k)}i_{k}}(u-k+1)\otimes\\
\otimes t_{i_{1}j_{1}}(u)t_{i_{2}j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{i_{k}j_{k}}(u-k+1)\bigr).\end{split}

Let us focus on the second factor in the tensor product in formula (
50
). Let
\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}))\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
be the homomorphism corresponding to the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
on
M_{(\lambda^{(m)})}^{(m)}
. Using the filtration on
\operatorname{End}(U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}))\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
defined in formula (

), we argue that the degree of

\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m}\bigl(t_{i_{1}j_{1}}(u)t_{i_{2}j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{i_{k}j_{k}}(u-k+1)\bigr)

is maximal and equal to

if and only if

for all

and

(51)

gr_{k}\bigl(\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m}\bigl(t_{j_{1}j_{1}}(u)t_{j_{2}j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{j_{k}j_{k}}(u-k+1)\bigr)\bigr)=\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{k}\xi_{j_{s}}}{u^{\underline{k}}}\cdot\operatorname{id},

where
\operatorname{id}
is the identity transformation of
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
.
Indeed, this follows from two observations. Firstly, for any

, we have

\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m}\bigl(t_{ii}(u)\bigr)\cdot(1\otimes 1)=1\otimes\left(1+\frac{\xi_{i}}{u}\right).

Secondly, for any
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}
, we have

\begin{split}&\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m}\bigl(t_{j_{1}j_{1}}(u)t_{j_{2}j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{j_{k}j_{k}}(u-k+1)\bigr)\cdot(x\otimes 1)=\\
&=x\bigl(\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m}\bigl(t_{j_{1}j_{1}}(u)t_{j_{2}j_{2}}(u-1)\dots t_{j_{k}j_{k}}(u-k+1)\bigr)\cdot(1\otimes 1)\bigr)+\text{terms of smaller degree}.\end{split}

For any
k=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, let
\bar{B}_{k}(u)
and
B^{XXX}_{k}(u)
be the coefficients of the difference operators
D_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})
and

, respectively:

Then, from the construction of the difference operators
D_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})
and
D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
and formula (
7
), it follows that

\bar{B}_{k}(u)=\widehat{\theta^{S^{(m)}(a)}_{\lambda^{(m)},b}}\left(\sum_{J,|J|=k}\rho_{a,z}\otimes(\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m})(\Delta(t_{J}(u)))\right),

B^{XXX}_{k}(u)=\sum_{J,|J|=k}\left(\prod_{j\in J}\xi_{j}\right)\rho_{a,z}(t_{J}(u)),

where
\rho_{a.z}
is the homomorphism corresponding to
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module
S^{(m)}(a,z)
, see formula (
34
).

Denote
X_{k}(u)=\sum_{J,|J|=k}\rho_{a,z}\otimes(\rho_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\circ\,ev_{m})(\Delta(t_{J}(u)))
. Then formulas (
50
) and (
51
) imply

gr_{k}(X_{k}(u))=\frac{1}{u^{\underline{k}}}B_{k}^{XXX}(u)\otimes\operatorname{id}.

In this proof, to simplify the notation, let us denote
\theta\coloneqq\theta^{S^{(m)}(a)}_{\lambda^{(m)},b}
. Notice that we can extend
\theta
to the map
\bigl(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}S^{a_{i}}\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes M^{(m)}_{(\lambda^{(m)})}\bigr)[\lambda^{(m)}+b]\rightarrow S^{(m)}(a)[b]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
using the same formula (
28
) with
\theta^{-1}
being its
right
inverse. We will use it on the step 4 in the computation (
52
) below.

It is also proved in
[
24
, Theorem 4]
that for any
v\in S^{(m)}(a)[b]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
, we have

\theta^{-1}v=v\otimes v_{\lambda^{(m)}}+\text{terms of smaller degree},

where
v_{\lambda^{(m)}}
is a highest weight vector in
M^{(m)}_{(\lambda^{(m)})}
. We will use it on the step 5 in the computation (
52
) below.

Fix a vector
v\in S^{(m)}(a)[b]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
of degree
0
. In the next computation, we work with series in
u^{-1}
assuming that all operations are applied simultaneously to all coefficients of a series.

(52)

\begin{split}&gr_{k}(\bar{B}_{k}(u))v=gr_{k}(\bar{B}_{k}(u)v)=gr_{k}((\widehat{\theta}X_{k}(u))v)=\\
&=gr_{k}(\theta X_{k}(u)\theta^{-1}v)=\theta\,gr_{k}(X_{k}(u)\theta^{-1}v)=\theta\,\bigl(gr_{k}(X_{k}(u))\bigr)(v\otimes v_{\lambda^{(m)}})=\\
&=\theta\left(\frac{1}{u^{\underline{k}}}B_{k}^{XXX}(u)\otimes\operatorname{id}\right)(v\otimes v_{\lambda^{(m)}})=\theta\left(\frac{1}{u^{\underline{k}}}B_{k}^{XXX}(u)v\otimes v_{\lambda^{(m)}}\right)=\frac{1}{u^{\underline{k}}}B_{k}^{XXX}(u)v.\end{split}

Therefore, it follows that

(53)

gr_{k}\bigl(\bar{B}_{k}(u)\bigr)=\frac{1}{u^{\underline{k}}}B_{k}^{XXX}(u).

Now, let us consider the expansion of
D_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})
in powers of
d
rather then in powers of
\tau
, and let
\widetilde{B}_{k}
,
k=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
be the corresponding coefficients:

D_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\widetilde{B}_{k}(u)d^{m-k}.

Then, for each
k=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have
\widetilde{B}_{k}(u)=\bar{B}_{k}(u)+\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}c_{k^{\prime}k}\bar{B}_{k^{\prime}}(u)
for some complex numbers
c_{k^{\prime}k}
. in particular,
\deg\widetilde{B}_{k}(u)=\deg\bar{B}_{k}(u)=k
and
gr_{k}(\widetilde{B}_{k}(u))=gr_{k}(\bar{B}_{k}(u))
.

Also, using formula (
42
), we can find elements
\widetilde{D}_{ij}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
such that

u^{\underline{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\,\,D_{m}(a,z;b,\lambda^{(m)})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\widetilde{D}_{ij}(u+n)^{\underline{j}}(u-m+1)^{\overline{i}}d^{i}.

Then, for any
k=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have

(54)

u^{\underline{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\widetilde{B}_{m-k}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\widetilde{D}_{kj}(u+n)^{\underline{j}}(u-m+1)^{\overline{k}},

which implies
\deg\widetilde{D}_{kj}\leq m-k
for any
j=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
. It is also easy to see that
D_{kj}=\widetilde{D}_{kj}+\sum_{k^{\prime}=k+1}^{m}c^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}k}\widetilde{D}_{k^{\prime}j}
for some complex numbers
c^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}k}
. Therefore,
\deg D_{kj}\leq m-k
and
gr_{m-k}(\widetilde{D}_{kj})=gr_{m-k}(D_{kj})
. Thus, identity (
54
) gives

(55)

u^{\underline{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})gr_{m-k}(\bar{B}_{m-k}(u))=\sum_{j=0}^{n}gr_{m-k}(D_{kj})(u+n)^{\underline{j}}(u-m+1)^{\overline{k}}

Finally, using (
53
) and (
55
), we compute

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\,\,D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)=u^{\underline{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{B_{k}^{XXX}(u)}{u^{\underline{k}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(u-m+1)^{\overline{m-k}}}\tau^{m-k}=\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(u^{\underline{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})gr_{k}(\bar{B}_{k}(u))\right)\cdot\frac{1}{(u-m+1)^{\overline{m-k}}}\tau^{m-k}=\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}gr_{m-k}(D_{kj})(u+n)^{\underline{j}}\right)\tau^{k},\end{split}

which finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.7.
Degeneration of the duality map
\Psi_{\lambda}

5.7.1.

Let
I
be a totally ordered set, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a field extension of
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that as a complex vector space,
\mathbb{F}
is
I
-filtered. For any other complex vector space
V
, introduce an
I
-filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{V\otimes\mathbb{F}},i\in I\}
on
V\otimes\mathbb{F}
generalizing the filtration (
41
), that is

(56)

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{V\otimes\mathbb{F}}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{v\otimes f\,|\,\deg(f)\leq i\}.

Let
V
and
W
be two complex vector spaces, and let
\phi
be an
\mathbb{F}
-linear space from
V\otimes\mathbb{F}
to
W\otimes\mathbb{F}
. Considering
\phi
as an element of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,W)\otimes\mathbb{F}
and using the filtration on the latter space defined by (
56
), we denote
\phi^{gr}=gr(\phi)
.

We say that
\phi
preserves the degree if
\deg(\phi(p))=\deg(p)
for any
p\in V\otimes\mathbb{F}
. We will need the following three lemmas.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
\phi
preserves the degree. Then for any
k\in I
and
p\in V\otimes\mathbb{F}
such that
\deg(p)\leq k
, we have
gr_{k}(\phi(p))=\phi^{gr}(gr_{k}(p))
.

Proof.

It is clear that for any
v\in V
and
k\geq\deg\phi
, we have
\deg(\phi(v))=\deg\phi
and
gr_{k}(\phi(v))=(gr_{k}\phi)(v)
. (Here and below, we identify
V
and
V\otimes 1
).
In particular, if
\phi
preserves the degree, then
\deg(\phi)=0
, and for any
v\in V
, we have
gr_{0}(\phi(v))=\phi^{gr}(v)
.

Let
k\in I
and
p\in V\otimes\mathbb{F}
be such that
\deg(p)\leq k
.
We can write
p=\sum_{i=1}^{l}v_{i}\otimes f_{i}
for some
v_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak v_{l}\in V
,
f_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak f_{l}\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\deg(f_{i})\leq k
for all
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak l
. Then we compute

gr_{k}(\phi(p))=gr_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\phi(v_{i})f_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}gr_{0}(\phi(v_{i}))gr_{k}(f_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\phi^{gr}(v_{i})gr_{k}(f_{i})=\phi^{gr}(gr_{k}(p)).

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
\phi
preserves the degree and that
\phi
is an isomorphism.
Then
\phi^{gr}
is also an isomorphism and
(\phi^{gr})^{-1}=(\phi^{-1})^{gr}
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\phi^{-1}
preserves the degree: for any
p\in W\otimes\mathbb{F}
, we have
\deg(\phi^{-1}p)=\deg(\phi(\phi^{-1}(p))=\deg(p)
. Then, using the previous lemma, for any
v\in V
, we get

v=gr_{0}v=gr_{0}(\phi^{-1}(\phi(v))=(\phi^{-1})^{gr}\phi^{gr}gr_{0}(v)=(\phi^{-1})^{gr}\phi^{gr}v.

Since both
id_{V\otimes\mathbb{F}}
and
(\phi^{-1})^{gr}\phi^{gr}
are
\mathbb{F}
-linear, the previous computation implies
(\phi^{-1})^{gr}\phi^{gr}=id_{V\otimes\mathbb{F}}
. Similarly, we get
\phi^{gr}(\phi^{-1})^{gr}=id_{W\otimes\mathbb{F}}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
\phi
preserves the degree and that
\phi
is an isomorphism. Let
X\in\operatorname{End}(V)\otimes\mathbb{F}
,
Y\in\operatorname{End}(W)\otimes\mathbb{F}
, and
k\in I
be such that
k\geq\deg(X)
,
k\geq\deg(Y)
. Suppose that
\widehat{\phi}(X)=Y
. Then
\widehat{\phi^{gr}}(gr_{k}\,X)=gr_{k}\,Y
.

Proof.

For any
w\in W
, using Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
, we compute

\begin{split}(gr_{k}\,Y)w=gr_{k}\,(Yw)=gr_{k}\,(\phi X\phi^{-1}w)=\phi^{gr}\,gr_{k}\,(X\phi^{-1}w)=\\
=\phi^{gr}(gr_{k}\,X)(gr_{0}\,(\phi^{-1}w))=\phi^{gr}(gr_{k}\,X)(\phi^{gr})^{-1}w=\widehat{\phi^{gr}}(gr_{k}\,X)w.\end{split}

Since both
gr_{k}\,Y
and
\widehat{\phi^{gr}}(gr_{k}\,X)
are
\mathbb{F}
-linear, the previous computation implies
\widehat{\phi^{gr}}(gr_{k}\,X)=gr_{k}\,Y
and the lemma is proved.
∎

5.7.2.

Now, let us make the necessary adjustments to think of the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
as a
\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
-linear map from
S^{(m)}(a)[b]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
to
S^{(n)}(b)[a]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
. Namely, in the chain (
32
), we replace
S^{(m)}(a)[b]
,
S^{(n)}(b)[a]
, and
M^{(m)}_{\lambda^{(m)}}
with
S^{(m)}(a)[b]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
,
S^{(n)}(b)[a]\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
, and
M^{(m)}_{(\lambda^{(m)})}
, respectively. We will also need to replace the module
M_{\lambda}^{(m+n)}
with the
\mathfrak{gl}_{m+n}
-analog
M_{(\lambda)}^{(m+n)}
of the module
M^{(m)}_{(\lambda^{(m)})}
with the difference that in
M_{(\lambda)}^{(m+n)}
, only the last
m
components of the highest weight
\lambda
become variables, and the rest components remain complex numbers. With such a change, all maps in (
32
) have the obvious
\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
-linear analogs and
\Psi_{\lambda}
becomes an element of
\operatorname{Hom}(S^{(m)}(a)[b],S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
.

Unfortunately, we cannot apply the results of the previous subsection to the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
, because it is not clear whether
\Psi_{\lambda}
preserves the degree or not. Therefore, we would like to modify the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
in such a way that the resulting map preserves the degree and satisfies relation (
33
). For that, we need to diagonalize the algebra
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
, which requires changing the field
\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(n)})
to another, algebraically closed field that we introduce below.

Recall the Zarisky open subset
J_{a,b}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
of all values of
\lambda
at which all the maps in the chain (
32
) are well defined isomorphisms. Fix some
\lambda^{(n)}
such that
J_{a,b}^{(m)}\coloneqq\{\lambda_{0}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\,|\,(\lambda^{(n)},\lambda_{0}^{(m)})\in J_{a,b}\}
is not empty. Let
\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\lambda^{(m)})\subset\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})
be the subalgebra of all rational functions which are regular on
J^{(m)}_{a,b}
. Notice that
\Psi_{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Hom}(S^{(m)}(a)[b],S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\lambda^{(m)})
,
C_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\lambda^{(m)})
, and
D_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])\otimes\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\lambda^{(m)})
. Recall the homomorphism
\pi_{r,\xi}:\mathbb{C}(\lambda^{(m)})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(r,\xi)\subset\mathbb{C}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
,
\pi_{r,\xi}(\lambda_{n+i})=r\xi_{i}
. Then
\pi_{r,\xi}(\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\lambda^{(m)}))\subset\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
, where
\mathbb{C}^{reg}(\xi)
is the algebra of rational functions in
\xi
, which are regular on some Zariski open subset
\widetilde{J}^{(m)}_{a,b}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Actually, if
J_{a,b}^{(m)}
is given by inequalities
p(\lambda^{m})\neq 0
for
p\in P\subset\mathbb{C}[\lambda^{(m)}]
, then
\widetilde{J}_{a,b}^{(m)}
is given by inequalities
gr\,(p)(\xi)\neq 0
for
p\in P
.

Let us now replace the elements
\Psi_{\lambda}
,
D_{ij}
, and
C_{ij}
with the evaluations of their images under the map
\operatorname{id}\otimes\pi_{r,\xi}
at some value of
\xi
in
\widetilde{J}_{a,b}^{(m)}
, keeping the same notation. That is, we think of
\xi=(\xi_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\xi_{m})
as the set of complex parameters again, and we consider
\Psi_{\lambda}
,
D_{ij}
, and
C_{ij}
as elements of
\operatorname{Hom}(S^{(m)}(a)[b],S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}((r^{-1}))
,
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{C}((r^{-1}))
, and
\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])\otimes\mathbb{C}((r^{-1}))
, respectively. So, these elements now belong to vector spaces over the field
\mathbb{C}((r^{-1}))
, the algebraic closure of which is known to be the algebra of Puiseux series, which we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{r}
. An element of
\mathbb{P}_{r}
is a series of the form

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq k}c_{i}r^{\frac{i}{l}}

for some
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and the addition and multiplication between series are defined in a natural way.

Let us extend the field and think of
\Psi_{\lambda}
,
D_{ij}
, and
C_{ij}
as elements of
\operatorname{Hom}(S^{(m)}(a)[b],S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
,
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
, and
\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
, respectively. In particular, the algebras
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
, where
w
is now a linear function in
r
and
\xi
, and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(a,z;b,r\xi)
are now
\mathbb{P}_{r}
-subalgebras of
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
and
\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
generated by
C_{ij}
and
D_{ij}
, respectively. On
\mathbb{P}_{r}
, we have a natural
\mathbb{Q}
-filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{q}^{\mathbb{P}},q\in\mathbb{Q}\}
, where for each
q\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathcal{F}_{q}^{\mathbb{P}}
consists of all elements of the form
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},i\leq ql}c_{i}r^{\frac{i}{l}}
for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. Using (
56
), we extend this filtration to
V\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
for any complex vector space
V
. Notice that Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
still hold in the new setting if we make similar adjustments for the constructions of
\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
and
D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
as follows. Namely, in the construction we should first treat
\xi
as the set of variables, then we need to apply the homomorphism
\xi_{i}\mapsto r\xi_{i}
to each rational function in
\xi
, and look at the result as an element of
\mathbb{C}(\xi)((r^{-1}))
. After that, we evaluate
\xi
at some value in
\widetilde{J}_{a}
and extend the field from
\mathbb{C}((r^{-1}))
to
\mathbb{P}_{r}
.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

There exists an open (in usual topology) subset
U
of
\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
, such that for any
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
, the algebra
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is diagonalizable over
\mathbb{P}_{r}
with one-dimensional eigenspaces.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{r}
is algebraically closed, we can decompose
S^{(n)}(b)[a]\otimes\mathbb{P}_{r}
into direct sum of generalized eigenspaces of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
over
\mathbb{P}_{r}
. It is easy to check that if
v
is a generalized eigenvector of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
, then
gr(v)
is a common generalized eigenvector of
gr_{m-i}(C_{ij})
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
. Therefore, by Proposition
5.2
,
gr(v)
is a generalized eigenvector of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
. This implies that the dimension of the largest generalized eigenspace of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is less or equal to the dimension of the largest generalized eigenspace of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
. (It can be less since two different eigenvalues can be ”glued together” under degeneration).

By
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
, there exists an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, such that for any
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
, the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is diagonalizable with one-dimensional eigenspaces, or, equivalently, has one-dimensional generalized eigenspaces. Therefore, it follows that for
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
,
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
has one-dimensional generalized eigenspaces, and the lemma is proved.
∎

We are now ready to define the modification of the map
\Psi_{\lambda}
, which preserves the degree. Denote
N=\dim S^{(n)}(b)[a]
. Assume that
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
. By Lemma
5.7
, there exists a
\mathbb{P}_{r}

\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(b,w;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is diagonal. We can assume that

for any

. Notice that by Theorem

\{\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(v_{1}),\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(v_{N})\}

. For each

, denote

. Then we define a

-linear map

\Psi_{(\lambda)}(r^{-d_{i}}\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(v_{i}))=v_{i}
, for any

. It follows from the construction that

For any

,

, by the definition of the elements

and

, see formulas (

) and (

) and (

), we have

. Therefore,
\widehat{\Psi}_{(\lambda)}(D_{ij})=C_{ij}
, and, applying Lemma

to

, we get

\widehat{\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}}(gr_{m-i}\,D_{ij})=gr_{m-i}\,C_{ij}.

Finally, since we do not need the variable

anymore, let us put

in any element of any vector space over
\mathbb{P}_{r}
that we had above, where it makes sense. In particular,
\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}
,

, and

become elements of

,

.

5.8.
Trigonometric Gaudin and dynamical Hamiltonians

For each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
and an element
g\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, denote
(g)_{(i)}=1\otimes\dots\otimes 1\otimes g\otimes 1\dots\otimes 1\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
, where
g
is on the
i
-th place. Recall that
e_{ij}^{(n)}
,
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, are the standard generators of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. We will also identify
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
with its image in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
under the homomorphism
g\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}(g)_{(i)}
,
g\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Assume that
\xi_{i}\neq\xi_{k}
for
i\neq k
. Introduce the trigonometric Gaudin Hamiltonians
H^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
(see
[
9
]
):

H^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(z_{j}+n-\frac{e_{jj}^{(n)}}{2}\right)(e_{jj}^{(n)})_{(i)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\xi_{i}\Omega^{+}_{ik}+\xi_{k}\Omega^{-}_{ik}}{\xi_{i}-\xi_{k}},

where

\Omega^{+}_{ik}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{jj}^{(n)})_{(i)}(e_{jj}^{(n)})_{(k)}+\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq n}(e_{jl}^{(n)})_{(i)}(e_{lj}^{(n)})_{(k)}

and

\Omega^{-}_{ik}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(e_{jj}^{(n)})_{(i)}(e_{jj}^{(n)})_{(k)}+\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq n}(e_{lj}^{(n)})_{(i)}(e_{jl}^{(n)})_{(k)}.

Using the similar notation, introduce the XXX dynamical Hamiltonians
G^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})^{\otimes n}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
(see
[
28
]
):

\begin{split}G^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)=-\frac{(e^{(m)}_{ii})^{2}}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(z_{i}+n)(e_{ii}^{(m)})_{(j)}&+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(e_{ij}^{(m)})_{(k)}(e_{ji}^{(m)})_{(l)}+\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\xi_{j}}{\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}}(e^{(m)}_{ij}e^{(m)}_{ji}-e^{(m)}_{ii}).\end{split}

Algebras
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})^{\otimes n}
act on
S^{(n)}(b)[a]
and
S^{(m)}(a)[b]
, respectively. We denote the corresponding images of
H^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)
and
G^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)
in
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
and
\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])
by
\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)
and
\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)
, respectively.

Recall the map
\Psi
, see formula (
38
). It was observed more then twenty years ago (see
[
27
]
) that for each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have:

(58)

\widehat{\Psi}(\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi))=\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi).

Recall the elements
gr_{m-i}C_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
and
gr_{m-i}D_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}(S^{(m)}(a)[b])
from the previous section. For each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, denote

C_{i}(u)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}(gr_{m-i}C_{ij})u^{j}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m}(u-\xi_{k})},\quad D_{i}(u)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}(gr_{m-i}D_{ij})u^{j}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m}(u-\xi_{k})}.

Theorem 5.8
.

For each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, the following relations hold:

(59)

\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)=\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Res}_{u=\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}C^{2}_{1}(u)-C_{2}(u)\right)-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}_{i},

(60)

\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)=\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Res}_{u=\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}D^{2}_{1}(u)-D_{2}(u)\right)-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}_{i}.

Proof.

Given that by Proposition
5.3
, the elements
gr_{m-i}(D_{ij})
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, are the coefficients of the difference operator
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(u-z_{i})\,\,D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
, relation (
60
) can be easily deduced from Proposition B.1 in
[
15
]
.

Let us prove relation (
59
). For each
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, define differential operators
\bar{\mathfrak{D}}_{ij}\in\mathfrak{D}(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1})
as follows:

\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(u\partial_{u}-n+j)-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{u(e_{ij}^{(n)})_{(l)}}{u-\xi_{l}}-(e_{ij}^{(n)})_{(m+1)}.

Also, denote

\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}(-1)^{\sigma}\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\sigma(1),1}\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\sigma(2),2}\dots\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{\sigma(n),n}.

Recall the differential operator
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
and the
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t])
-module
S_{U}^{(n)}(b,\xi,\lambda^{(n)})
(see formulas (
35
) and (
37
), respectively). It is easy to check that for any
v\in S_{U}^{(n)}(b,\xi,\lambda^{(n)})
, we have
u^{n}\mathfrak{D}_{n}\cdot v=\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}\cdot v
.

Assume that
v\in\bigl(S^{(n)}(b)\otimes M_{\lambda^{(n)}}^{(n)}\bigr)^{sing}[\lambda^{(n)}+a]
. Then
\theta^{S^{(n)}(b)}_{\lambda^{(n)},a}\bigl(\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}\cdot v\bigl)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}\cdot\theta^{S^{(n)}(b)}_{\lambda^{(n)},a}(v)
, where the differential operator
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}
(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m})
is obtained from
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
as follows. Let
\overline{U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}}
be the quotient of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}
by the left ideal generated by
(e^{(n)}_{ij})_{(m+1)}
for all
i>j
,
(e^{(n)}_{ij})_{(m+1)}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}(e_{ij}^{(n)})_{(l)}
for all
i<j
, and
(e^{(n)}_{ii})_{(m+1)}-\lambda_{i}
for all
i
. Then the inclusion into the first
m
factors
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}\hookrightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}
composed with projection
p_{m}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}\rightarrow\overline{U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}}
gives the isomorphism
q_{m}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}\rightarrow\overline{U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}}
. Then the operator
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
is obtained by applying the homomorphism
q_{m}^{-1}\circ p_{m}:U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m+1}\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
to each coefficient of
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
.

Let
\beta_{i}(u)\in\bigl(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}\bigr)((u^{-1}))
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, be the following coefficients of the differential operator
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
:

\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}(u)(u\partial_{u})^{n-i},

and let
\overline{\beta}_{i}(u)
,
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
be the images of
\beta_{i}(u)
in
\bigl(\operatorname{End}S^{(n)}(b)[a]\bigr)((u^{-1}))
. By a lengthy but straightforward computation, using the procedure of obtaining
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{n}
described above, we computed
\overline{\beta}_{1}(u)
and
\overline{\beta}_{2}(u)
explicitly and checked that for each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have

\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Res}_{u=\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2}_{1}(u)-\overline{\beta}_{2}(u)\right)=\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}_{i}.

By the definition of
\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
and the construction of
\overline{\beta}_{i}(u)
, we have
u^{n}\mathfrak{D}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\beta}_{i}(u)(u\partial_{u})^{n-i}
. Comparing the last formula with formula (
44
), we get

\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Res}_{u=\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\overline{\beta}^{2}_{1}(u)-\overline{\beta}_{2}(u)\right)=\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Res}_{u=\xi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}C^{2}_{1}(u)-C_{2}(u)\right),

and the theorem is proved.
∎

Recall the map
\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}
introduced for each
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})
in some open subset
U\in\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
at the end of the previous subsection. Combining Theorem
5.8
and relation (
57
), we get the following corollary:

Corollary 5.9
.

For each
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
and
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, we have

\widehat{\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}}\bigl(\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)\bigr)=\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi).

Now, comparing the last formula with formula (
58
), we can prove the following.

Lemma 5.10
.

There exists an open subset
\widetilde{U}
of
U
such that for every
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}
, the following holds:

(1)

Let
v\in S^{(m)}(a)[b]
be a common eigenvector of the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
. Then
\Psi(v)=\varkappa\,\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}(v)
for some nonzero
\varkappa\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

Let
w\in S^{(n)}(b)[a]
be a common eigenvector of the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
. Then
\Psi^{-1}(w)=\widetilde{\varkappa}\,(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}(w)
for some nonzero
\widetilde{\varkappa}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

We will prove part (1). Part (2) can be proved similarly.

Let
v\in S^{(m)}(a)[b]
be a common eigenvector of the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
. By Proposition
5.3
,
v
is a common eigenvector of
gr_{m-i}(D_{ij})
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
l=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
. By Theorem
5.8
, this implies that
v
is a common eigenvector of the dynamical Hamiltonians
\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
. Denote the corresponding eigenvalues by
g_{i}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
. Then, using Corollary
5.9
, we have

\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}(v)=\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}(\overline{G}^{(m)}_{i}(z,\xi)\,v)=g_{i}\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}(v).

Similar computation, but now using formula (
58
) instead of Corollary
5.9
, gives

\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)\Psi(v)=g_{i}\Psi(v).

Therefore, it is left to prove that there exists an open subset
\widetilde{U}
of
U
such that for every
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}
, common eigenspaces of the trigonometric Gaudin Hamiltonians
\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, are one-dimensional.

Notice that the images of the elements
(e_{ii}^{(n)})_{(j)}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
in
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
are simultaneously diagonalizable with one-dimensional common eigenspaces and with integer eigenvalues. Thus, if
z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{n}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
, the images of the elements
\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(e_{ii}^{(n)})_{(j)}
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
in
\operatorname{End}(S^{(n)}(b)[a])
are simultaneously diagonalizable with one-dimensional common eigenspaces. Therefore, the common eigenspaces of the operators
\overline{H}^{(n)}_{i}(z,\xi)
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, are one-dimensional provided that
z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{n}
are sufficiently large positive numbers linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
, which implies the existence of
\widetilde{U}
.
∎

Proposition 5.11
.

For any
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}
and any
X\in\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
, we have
\widehat{\Psi}(X)=\widehat{\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}}(X)
.

Proof.

As was mentioned in the proof of Lemma
5.7
, for
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in U
, the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
is diagonalizable.

Take
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}\subset U
. Let
v_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak v_{N}
be a common eigenbasis of
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{tG}(b,\xi;a,\lambda^{(n)})
. Then by Lemma
5.10
, part (2), for any
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak N
, there exists nonzero
\widehat{\varkappa}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(61)

\Psi^{-1}(v_{i})=\widetilde{\varkappa}_{i}\,(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}(v_{i}).

Notice that
(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}(v_{i})
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak N
, are common eigenvectors of
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
. Then, by Lemma
5.10
, part (1), for each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak N
, we can find nonzero
\varkappa_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(62)

\Psi((\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}(v_{i}))=\varkappa_{i}\,v_{i}.

Combining the last formula with (
61
), we get
\widetilde{\varkappa}_{i}=\varkappa_{i}^{-1}
.

Now, consider an arbitrary
X\in\mathcal{B}_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
. In the following computation, we use formula (
61
) on the second step, and we use formula (
62
) and the fact that
X(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}\,v_{i}
is proportional to
(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}\,v_{i}
on the third step:

\begin{split}\widehat{\Psi}(X)v_{i}&=\Psi X\Psi^{-1}\,v_{i}=\varkappa_{i}\Psi X(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}\,v_{i}=\\
&=\widetilde{\varkappa}_{i}\varkappa_{i}\,\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}X(\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)})^{-1}\,v_{i}=\widehat{\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}}(X)v_{i}.\end{split}

Since
v_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak v_{N}
is a basis, we deduce
\widehat{\Psi}(X)=\widehat{\Psi^{gr}_{(\lambda)}}(X)
.
∎

Now we can easily prove Theorem
5.1
. Indeed, existence of
\widetilde{A}_{ij}^{(m)}
follows from Proposition
5.3
and the fact that the difference operator
D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)}-\boldsymbol{n};b,\xi)
can be obtained from
D_{m}^{XXX}(a,\lambda^{(n)};b,\xi)
using the substitution
u\mapsto u-n
. Existence of
\widetilde{A}_{ji}^{(n)}
follows from Proposition
5.2
and the relation

(63)

u^{j}\partial_{u}^{j}=\prod_{i=1}^{j}(u\partial_{u}-j+1).

If
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}
, by relation (
57
) and Proposition
5.11
, for each
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, we have
\widehat{\Psi}(gr_{m-i}\,D_{ij})=gr_{m-i}\,C_{ij}
. Comparing formulas (
39
), (
40
), (
44
), and (
49
), and using relation (
63
) again, we see that
\widetilde{A}^{(m)}_{ij}
is expressed polynomially through
gr_{m-k}(D_{kl})
in the same way as
\widetilde{A}^{(n)}_{ji}
is expressed polynomially through
gr_{m-k}(C_{kl})
. Therefore, for
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\widetilde{U}
, we have
\widehat{\Psi}(\widetilde{A}^{(m)}_{ij})=\widetilde{A}^{(n)}_{ji}
. Since
\widetilde{A}^{(m)}_{ij}
and
\widetilde{A}^{(n)}_{ji}
depend polynomially on
\xi
and
\lambda^{(n)}
, the last equality holds for any
(\xi,\lambda^{(n)})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+k}
, and therefore, Theorem
5.1
is proved.

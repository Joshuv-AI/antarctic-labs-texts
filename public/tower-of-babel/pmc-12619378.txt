Influencing factors of depressive symptoms in middle-aged and elderly people and its regional differences in china: a study based on Bayesian network model
Authors: Xin-yue Gong, Qi Cheng, Ying-ting Wu, Si-han Wang, Ke-hui Xu, Lei Qin, Fei He, Jing Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to explore the influencing factors and regional differences in the presence of depressive symptoms (DS) among middle-aged and elderly people in China, as well as to explore the network relationship among the influencing factors by constructing Bayesian networks (BNs) model.MethodsA total of 3582 respondents were included using China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) 2020 data. Logistic regression analysis was used to screen for variables related to DS. And the intricate conditional dependencies were visualized by BNs. Differences in the spatial distribution of DS were visualized by a geographic information system.ResultsDuring the COVID-19 epidemic, the prevalence of DS in China was 46.5%. The high prevalence areas of DS were mainly clustered in the west (Sichuan, Qinghai) and middle (Hubei, Henan) regions of China. The results showed that the occurrence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people exhibits a direct probabilistic dependency with gender, body pain, self-reported health status, IADL (Instrument Activities of Daily Living), length of night sleep and region. Concurrently, age, education, chronic diseases, marital status and BADL (Basic Activities of Daily Living) were found to have an indirect probabilistic dependency with the prevalence of DS in these individuals.ConclusionsThe overall prevalence of DS remains high among middle-aged and older adults. Predominantly, the hotspots for high prevalence of DS are concentrated in middle and west regions of China. By identifying the most direct risk factors for DS, healthcare providers and community workers can develop targeted interventions to prevent and manage this condition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41043-025-01143-3.Keywords: Depressive symptoms, Bayesian networks, Regional differences

Introduction

An estimated 3.8% of the population experience depression, including 5.0% of adults and 5.7% of adults older than 60 years [1]. Approximately 280 million people in the world have depression. Depression, as an affective disorder, poses significant challenges in quantification due to its intricate nature. In reality, depressive symptoms (DS) can serve as a quantitative indicator for depression. DS as a form of negative affect that can be quantified by psychological scales, often reflecting the individual’s negative mood accompanied by periods of low mood, lack of pleasure, and diminished interest in activities [2, 3].The prevalence of DS is 37.2% in Brazil and 55.5% in Bangladesh, and findings from a longitudinal study in Australia showing that 43.2% of midlife women experienced a progressive worsening of DS over two decades [2, 4, 5]. In the Chinese elderly population, the detection rate of DS has increased from 36.8% in 2011 to 44.5% in 2018 [6].

However, traditional regression methods are inadequate in elucidating the interrelations among variables, incapable of distinguishing between direct and indirect risk factors, and struggle to capture the holistic impact of interrelated elements [7]. Bayesian networks (BNs) are probabilistic graphical models that can be used for uncertainty inference, generating directed acyclic graph (DAG) and conditional probability table (CPT) after structural learning and parameter learning. BNs enables the visualization of intricate conditional dependencies and the degree of correlation among variables, concurrently facilitating probabilistic predictions for the states of variables in interest [8, 9]. In addition, compared to traditional regression methods, BNs are able to infer the probability of unknown nodes and flexibly demonstrate the impact of relevant risk factors on the DS [10, 11].

With advancing age, physiological functions in middle-aged and older adults tend to decline, a reality that is compounded by disparities in economic development and the allocation of primary healthcare resources across east, middle, and west regions of China. These discrepancies may contribute to variations in the prevalence of DS among older adults residing in these different geographical areas [12–14]. To this end, this study aims to explore the differences in the incidence of DS in the east, middle and west regions of China, and to construct BNs of the risk factors for the presence of DS in middle-aged and elderly people. So as to analyze the relevant factors affecting the occurrence of DS and the relationship of mutual conditional dependence. This will provide a scientific basis for reducing the risk of DS among middle-aged and elderly people in China. By identifying the most direct risk factors for DS, healthcare providers and community workers can develop targeted interventions to prevent and manage this condition.

Methods

Data source

This study employs the cross-sectional data derived from the fifth wave of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), which was conducted in the year 2020 (https://charls.charlsdata.com/pages/Data/2020-charls-wave5/zh-cn.html). The CHARLS survey is a continuing and high-quality nationwide longitudinal study of individuals aged 45 and above in China, including demographic information, lifestyle factors and health-related information [15]. The baseline survey adopted the sophisticated Probability Proportional to Size (PPS) random sampling method for multistage sampling. And it has been approved by the Biomedical Ethics Committee of Peking University (IRB00001052-11015). The following inclusion criteria were implemented: (1) respondents aged 45 years or above at baseline; (2) basic information and health-related data are complete and accurate. Exclusion criteria were: (1) unmarried individuals; (2) lack of data on core variables. Among the initial 19,395 respondents, we selected 3582 participants. The other 15,813 individuals were excluded due to being below 45 years of age (n = 238), being unmarried (n = 76), missing data on CESD-10 (Centre for Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale) (n = 5949), or incomplete information on other covariates (n = 9550). Detailed information on the screening process is presented in Fig. 1.

Flow diagram of the selection of sample

Geographical distribution

In accordance with the theoretical framework of regional economic development in China and the regional classification criteria outlined in the China Health Statistical Yearbook 2022 [16, 17], provinces were divided into east, middle and west regions. The east region comprises the provinces of Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Liaoning, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Shandong, and Guangdong. The middle region contains Shanxi, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, and Hunan. And the west region includes the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Qinghai, Gansu, Shaanxi, Chongqing, and Guangxi.

Measurement of depressive symptoms

The Centre for Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CESD-10) was used to assess the DS of middle-aged and elderly people in the CHARLS survey [18, 19]. There are 10 items in this scale, and each item was rated on a 4-point Likert scale. Respondents need to score each item based on their experiences in the past week. In addition to item 5 (I felt hopeful about the future) and item 8 (I was happy), which are reverse-scored, each item comprises four response options—designated as “rarely or none (< 1 day)”, “some or a little (1 ~ 2 days)”, “occasionally or moderate amount of time (3 ~ 4 days)”, and “most of time (5 ~ 7 days)”—each assigned a respective value of 0, 1, 2, or 3 points. The total score ranges from 0 to 30, with higher scores indicating higher levels of DS in the respondent. If the total score of the CESD-10 scale is ≥ 10, the respondent can be considered to have DS [20, 21]. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the scale was 0.770 in this study.

Measurement of other variables

(1) Demographic characteristics: gender (male, female), age (measured up to the exact birthyear), residence (urban, rural), educational attainment (illiterate, primary school or below, middle school, high/vocational school or above), marital status (married, divorced or widowed).

(2) Health status: self-reported health status (very good, good, general, bad, very bad), troubled with body pain (no, low, high), chronic diseases (no, yes), length of night sleep, BADL (Basic Activities of Daily Living), IADL (Instrument Activities of Daily Living). The BADL and IADL scales each comprise six items, which are scored using a Likert scale of four points. The scoring categories include “don’ t have any difficulty”, “have difficulty but can still do it”, “have difficulty and need help”, and “can’ t do it”, with respective scores of 1, 2, 3, and 4 [22]. The BADL evaluates an individual’s ability in activities such as dressing, bathing, eating, getting in and out of bed, toileting, and controlling urination and defecation. A total score exceeding six points on the BADL indicates a significant impairment in these self-care abilities. Similarly, the IADL scale assesses proficiency in tasks including household chores, preparing hot meals, shopping for groceries, making phone calls, taking medications, and managing money. Again, a total score greater than six points on the IADL suggests a notable deficiency in these instrumental activities. In our study, the internal consistency reliability of the BADL and IADL were demonstrated through Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of 0.792 and 0.802 respectively.

(3) Social engagement and lifestyle factors: smoking (no, yes), drinking (no, yes), participation in social pensions (no, yes), hospitalized within the past year (no, yes), delayed medical service (no, yes), social activity (no, yes), physical activity (no, light, moderate, intensive).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 25.0. Continuous variables with skewed distribution were presented as median and interquartile range, denoted as M(Q). Categorical variables were characterized by frequency (n) and proportion (%). Non-parametric rank sum tests were used for skewed distribution and rank classification data. For categorical variables, both unordered and binary classifications were analyzed using chi-square tests. Adjustments to the chi-square test or the application of Fisher’s exact probability method were implemented based on sample size and theoretical frequencies. Following the identification of independent variables influencing the incidence of DS in middle-aged and elderly people through univariate analysis, a binary Logistic regression (Backward LR method) was conducted. Within the R Studio environment, version 4.3.2, we employed the “bnlearn” package to construct BNs, utilizing both the maximum-minimum hill-climbing algorithm and the maximum likelihood estimation method for the initial learning of network structures and parameters. The visualization of the BNs, along with its conditional probability distributions, was achieved through the use of Netica software (free version, Norsys Software Corp., Vancouver, Canada.). Ρ < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Spatial autocorrelation analysis was conducted to assess the regional distribution of DS incidence by using ArcGIS (ArcMap 10.8). Global Moran’s I statistic was used to assess whether there was a spatial correlation in DS incidence as a whole. Anselin Local Moran I index and Getis-Ord Gi* index were used to identify the presence of spatial clusters or regions with high or low risk of DS. When a high-risk area was surrounded by other high-risk areas simultaneously, the area was called a “hot spot” (i.e., a high-high risk cluster). Similarly, when a low-risk area was also surrounded by other low-risk areas, it was called a “cold spot” (i.e., a low-low risk cluster).

Results

Baseline characteristics and regional comparisons of depressive symptoms in middle-aged and elderly people

In our study, we included 3,582 middle-aged and elderly people, comprising 1,628 males (45.4%) and 1,954 females (54.6%). Other specific information can be found in Table 1. Among the participants included in our study, a significant prevalence of DS was observed, affecting 1,666 individuals and representing a total DS rate of 46.5% (Table 2). In the east region, 423 respondents displayed DS, yielding a regional prevalence of 25.4%. The west region reported a higher figure, with 635 cases and a prevalence rate of 38.1%, while the middle region had 608 cases, corresponding to a prevalence of 36.5%. It can be found that the prevalence of DS was significantly higher in the middle and west regions than in the east. The results of the Chi-square test showed a significant difference in the prevalence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people in east, middle and west regions (=38.312, P < 0.001). Upon further comparative analysis, we have obtained statistically significant differences in the prevalence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people residing in the east and middle regions (=32.668, P < 0.001), as well as between the east and west regions (=25.979, P < 0.001). Whereas, the difference between the middle and west regions remained uncertain (=0.545, P = 0.460).

Spatial distribution of the prevalence of depressive symptoms in China

The prevalence of DS in various provinces of China ranges from 18.18% to 82.22%, and the top 5 provinces for the prevalence of DS were Qinghai (82.22%), Beijing (66.67%), Chongqing (65.45%), Anhui (60.36%), and Guangxi (56.76%) (Fig. 2 (a)). Provinces that had a high prevalence of DS were primarily located in the western area (Qinghai, Chongqing, and Guangxi), while those with low prevalence were distributed in the eastern area of China (Zhejiang and Shanghai). Spatial autocorrelation also revealed that the high prevalence areas of DS were mainly clustered in the western (Sichuan) and middle (Hubei and Henan) regions of China, whereas the low prevalence areas were clustered in the eastern (Jiangsu, Shanghai) region (Fig. 2 (b)). Additionally, a hot spot analysis proved similar results as well, as in Fig. 2(c).

The influencing factors of depressive symptoms in middle-aged and elderly people

In constructing a binary logistic regression model with the presence of DS as the dependent variable (coded as “No” = 0 and “Yes” = 1), we included variables that demonstrated significant statistical significance in our univariate analysis. The results revealed that gender, age, education, marital status, self-reported health status, troubled with body pain, chronic diseases, region, length of night sleep, BADL, and IADL were common influences on the occurrence of DS in middle-aged and elderly people (Table 3). In terms of regional disparities, both middle and west regions exhibited a higher prevalence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people compared to the east region. Specifically, the risk of DS in the middle region was elevated by 40.5% relative to the east region (OR = 1.405, 95% CI: 1.167 ~ 1.692). Similarly, the west region demonstrated a 23.2% increased risk (OR = 1.232, 95% CI: 1.025 ~ 1.480) when contrasted with the east region.

Bayesian networks

Utilizing the 11 significant variables identified through logistic regression analysis as network nodes, we have constructed a Bayesian networks model encompassing 12 nodes and 20 directed edges, specifically tailored to elucidate the factors influencing DS in middle-aged and elderly people. And the conditional probabilities of each node can be calculated from this model. Each node represents a random variable, and the directed edges between the connected nodes represent the interrelationships between the variables. The numbers in Fig. 3 indicated the prior probability of each node, with the prior probability of the DS being 46.6%. The results showed that the occurrence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people exhibits a direct probabilistic dependency with gender, body pain, self-reported health status, IADL (score equal to 6 = good; score > 6 = bad), length of night sleep and region. Concurrently, age, education, chronic diseases, marital status and BADL (score equal to 6 = good; score > 6 = bad) were found to have an indirect probabilistic dependency with the prevalence of DS in these individuals. The Bayesian network prediction model has an AUC value of 0.752, see supplementary file 1.

Bayesian reasonings

After drawing the Bayesian network graphical structure, the conditional probability of having DS can be predicted. If the person also troubled with severe body pain, the probability will rise from 46.6% to 71.3% rapidly. And if the person’s self-reported health status is bad, the probability ascends to 75.7%. Besides, if the person’s IADL is in bad condition, the probability climbs to 81.1%. Under these specified conditions (troubled with severe body pain, bad self-reported health status, bad IADL), the conditional probabilities of DS across the east, middle, and west regions stand at 76.7%, 83.6%, and 82.6% respectively. Discussion

The results of this study showed that the detection rate of DS among middle-aged and elderly people in China during the COVID-19 pandemic was 46.5%. Firstly, the findings of our study showed a significant difference in the prevalence of DS among middle-aged and elderly people across the middle and west regions compared to their counterparts in the east region. This observation aligned harmoniously with the conclusions drawn by previous scholarly investigations in this domain [23, 24]. The spatial distribution map of the prevalence of DS further demonstrated that the top five provinces with high rates of DS were Qinghai, Beijing, Chongqing, Anhui, and Guangxi, each having surpassed 55%. Additionally, there was a high-high clustering of DS in some provinces of the middle (Hubei, Henan) and west (Sichuan, Qinghai) region. Liu et al. [25] found that the disability rate among middle-aged and elderly individuals in eastern China was 20.01%, while the rates in middle and western regions rose to 26.09% and 28.95%, respectively. In this study, the IADL impairment rate in the eastern region was 22.5%, while the western and middle regions reached 28.9% and 30.7%. Additionally, the prevalence of body pain was 56.5%, 64.7%, and 70.4% in the eastern, middle, and western regions, respectively. These findings indicate that middle and western China exhibit higher rates of IADL impairment and body pain, alongside a higher prevalence of DS.

Although China has witnessed a surge in urbanization rates and substantial enhancements in both the quality of life and healthcare provision nationwide, it remains evident that disparities persist between the economically thriving east region and the comparatively lagging middle and west regions in terms of public resources, infrastructure development and access to medical services [26]. Taking Gross Domestic Product (GDP) as an example, in 2023, the GDP of the east region alone occupied 54.5% of the total amount in China [27]. During the period 2012–2021, the number of physicians and nurses per 1,000 population in primary health-care institutions in eastern China was consistently higher than in central and western China. This indicated that the eastern region was relatively more adequate in terms of primary health-care resources, while the central and western regions had relatively insufficient resources for primary health-care institutions [28]. The construction of tertiary-level hospitals remains predominantly concentrated in provincial capitals, exhibiting a “single-center” spatial distribution pattern, with an overall development focus skewed toward eastern cities. However, the hospital system in the central and western regions exhibits distinct features: abundant quantity but limited specialization, substantial scale but inadequate technical capacity [29]. Healthcare workers in the central and western regions need to pay more attention to the mental health of middle-aged and elderly people on the basis of improving their technical skills.

Body pain has been found to exhibit a direct probability-dependent relationship with DS. According to a survey, the prevalence of chronic pain among middle-aged and elderly people in China is approximately 60.02% [30]. Similarly, in Japan, more than 50% of the frail elderly population also experience chronic pain [31]. At the level of physiological mechanisms, chronic body pain and depressive symptoms are often comorbid, and both share the same pathogenic brain regions, complex neurocircuit mechanisms, and interacting neurotransmitter systems [32]. At the psychological level, body pain can create a sense of helplessness and loss of control in middle-aged and elderly people, further inducing depressive moods. At the social-behavioral level, chronic body pain in middle-aged and older adults often leads to mobility limitations, reduced physical stamina, and diminished social engagement, thereby increasing susceptibility to depressive symptoms [33]. In conclusion, regular pain assessments and proactive pain management are an important part of the work of hospital and community health workers.

Moreover, a direct probability-dependent relationship was found between self-reported health status and DS. The results of this study demonstrated that the better self-reported health status, the lower the risk of concomitant DS. Self-reported health status reflects people’s subjective feelings about their own physical health, mental health, and social function [34]. Self-reported health status is a valid indicator of a respondent’s current health status, correlating positively with objective health [35, 36]. The single item self-reported health status question was used to evaluate the recent health status of Chinese people with high reliability and predictive validity in CHARLS [37]. A research also concluded that self-reported health status was effective in predicting the risk of developing DS in older adults [38]. Poorer self-reported health status may be accompanied by more severe physical pain and a higher risk of DS in middle-aged and elderly people. Xu et al. [39] investigated all 31 provinces in China and found that self-reported ill-health among the elderly population showed a spatial stepwise increase “southeast-central-northwest”. This suggests that healthcare professionals can develop multi-level, wide-ranging, diversified and specialized health services for the middle-aged and elderly population in different regions.

In addition, it was found that IADL scores exhibit a direct probability-dependent association with DS. The Bayesian networks model of this study revealed that individuals with bad IADL (score > 6) were at higher risk of having DS compared to those with good IADL (score = 6). Studies in South Korea, Ethiopia and Malaysia also have shown that older adults with functional disability have a higher risk of developing DS [40–43]. Among elderly people in Dalian, China, IADL disability accounted for 40.2% [44]. In Turkey and Saudi Arabia, IADL disability rates also reached 45.8% and 58.5% respectively [45]. It is suggested that community workers should identify middle-aged and elderly people with poor IADL performance through early screening. And geriatric care workers should change from a disease-focused to a function-focused. Assisting the elderly to meet their functional needs in daily life, with regular follow-up visits and attention to their mental health.

Based on the results of this study, we propose the following outlooks. In the era of smart living, more and more big data models, mobile apps and wearable devices are beginning to be applied to community health management and can alleviate patients’ depressive mood to a certain extent [46, 47]. Community care professionals can harness the power of telecommunications and wearable technology to monitor the physiological status of the elderly, gaining insights into their current well-being through the analysis of vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, sleep patterns, and physical activity levels [48]. Mental health can be reflected through “digital phenotype”, which collects passive data such as heart rate variability, skin temperature and activity, as well as active data such as chats and questionnaires [49]. Relying on an intelligent platform to narrow the gap in medical resources between regions, so that the middle-aged and the elderly in the central and western regions can also have access to quality medical resources.

Limitations

Firstly, the judgement of depressive symptoms in this study relied on the CESD-10 scale rather than clinical diagnostic criteria. Secondly, the data in this study were derived from the COVID-19 period, which may have an impact on the subjectively reported data in the survey. It is possible that our sample screening process incurred a potential selection bias. During the sample screening process, we excluded 76 unmarried individuals, which may have a minor impact on the generalizability of the findings, particularly with regard to the widowed or divorced participants who remained in the sample. Finally, although the Bayesian network model established in this study can reflect the probabilistic dependency relationships among variables, it still cannot strictly determine the direction of causality.

Conclusion

The overall prevalence of DS remains high among middle-aged and older adults. Predominantly, the hotspots for high prevalence of DS are concentrated in middle and west regions of China. In this study, the Bayesian networks model was constructed to visually present the presence of DS directly related to gender, body pain, self-reported health status, IADL, length of night sleep, and region in middle-aged and elderly people. Concurrently, age, education, chronic diseases, marital status and BADL were found to have an indirect probabilistic dependency with the prevalence of DS in these individuals. This facilitates the early identification of individuals at high risk for DS by healthcare professionals and community workers, enabling prompt psychological interventions and enhancing the overall sense of well-being and happiness in middle-aged and elderly populations.

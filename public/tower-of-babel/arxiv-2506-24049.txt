Geometric condition for the observability of electromagnetic Schrödinger operators on 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2}
Kévin Le Balc’h, Jingrui Niu, Chenmin Sun

Abstract
. In this article we revisit the observability of the Schrödinger equation on the two-dimensional torus. In contrast to the Schrödinger operator with a purely electric potential, for which any non-empty open set guarantees observability, the presence of a magnetic potential introduces an additional obstruction. We establish a sufficient and almost necessary geometric condition for the observability of electromagnetic Schrödinger operators. This condition incorporates the magnetic potential, which can also be characterized by a geometric control condition for the corresponding magnetic field.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the Schrödinger equation, set on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
the two-dimensional torus, that governs the time evolution of the wave function
u=u(t,z)
of a charged particle in an electromagnetic field. More precisely, we consider

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm i}\partial_{t}u=H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}(z)u&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }{\mathbb{T}}^{2},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
is the electromagnetic Schrödinger operator given by

H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}(z):=\left(\frac{1}{{\rm i}}\nabla-{\mathbf{A}}(z)\right)^{2}+V(z),\qquad z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2},

(1.2)

with

V\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}),\ {\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},A_{2})\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

(1.3)

It is standard that
-iH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
generates an unitary semi-group
(e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}})_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
so for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
, there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
of (
1.1
). Moreover the total
L^{2}
-mass of the solution is preserved along the time, i.e.

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(1.4)

We are interested in the following notion of
observability
of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\omega\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
be a nonempty open subset. We say that the observability of (
1.1
) at time
T
from
\omega
holds true if there exists a constant
C=C(\mathbf{A},V,T,\omega)>0
such that

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t,\qquad\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

(1.5)

At time
T>0
and for
\omega\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, the observability inequality (
1.5
) models the possibility of estimating the (whole)
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
-norm of the initial wave function
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
by the (restricted)
L^{2}((0,T)\times\omega)
-norm of the wave function
u=u(t,z)
. For instance if the initial datum is normalized,
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=1
, the expression
\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z
is the probability of finding the particle in the set
\omega
at time
t
. Having
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t\geq C^{-1}>0
for all solutions of (
1.1
) means that every quantum particle spends a positive fraction of time of the interval
(0,T)
in the set
\omega
.

1.1.
The Magnetic Geometric Control Condition

For
N\geq 1
and any
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, we first define the following quantity as the average of
f
along a given direction
\vec{e}

\langle f\rangle_{\vec{e}}(z):=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(z+t\vec{e}){\rm{d}}t\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}.

(1.6)

Two important things have to be noted.

(i)

First, the limit defined in (
1.6
) is well-defined by checking its meaning for trigonometric polynomials and by using a density argument. The resulted function
\langle f\rangle_{\vec{e}}
belongs to
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
.

(ii)

Secondly, one can check that the directional derivative of
\langle f\rangle_{\vec{e}}
along
\vec{e}
, in the distributional sense, satisfies

D\langle f\rangle_{\vec{e}}(z)\cdot\vec{e}=0\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}.

Therefore we can identify
\langle f\rangle_{\vec{e}}
as a function on the direction
\vec{e}^{\perp}
.

For a closed geodesic
\gamma
of periodic direction
\vec{\gamma}
, the average of the vector field
{\mathbf{A}}
along the direction
\vec{\gamma}
is given by

\langle{\mathbf{A}}\rangle_{\vec{\gamma}}(z)=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbf{A}}(z+t\vec{\gamma}){\rm{d}}t\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}.

(1.7)

We can then define the scalar function

A_{\gamma}:=\langle{\mathbf{A}}\rangle_{\vec{\gamma}}\cdot\vec{\gamma}.

(1.8)

So one can identify
A_{\gamma}
as a scalar function on the direction of
\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}
.

For a closed geodesic
\gamma
with a periodic direction
\vec{\gamma}
,
we denote by
\omega_{\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}}
the projection of
\omega
on the direction of
\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}
, which is also open.

Figure 1.
control-region-projection

With the concepts above, we introduce the following Magnetic Geometric Gontrol Gondition, called
(MGCC)
in the next.

Definition 1.2

(
MGCC).

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\omega
be a nonempty open subset of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. We say that
{\mathbf{A}}
and
\omega
satisfy the Magnetic Geometric Gontrol Gondition
(MGCC)
if for every periodic direction
\vec{\gamma}
,
\omega_{\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}}
contains all the critical points of
A_{\gamma}
.

The condition from Definition
1.2
can be interpreted in an intrinsic way. We identify the magnetic potential
\mathbf{A}
as a globally defined one-form
\mathcal{A}=A_{1}{\rm{d}}x+A_{2}{\rm{d}}y
on the coordinate system
(x,y)
. We parametrize
\vec{\gamma}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
. Then in the new coordinate system

(X,Y):=F(x,y)=(x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta,-x\sin\theta+y\cos\theta),

the magnetic potential (identified as the pull-back form
F^{-1*}\mathcal{A}
) is

(\mathbf{A}\cdot\vec{\gamma}){\rm{d}}X+(\mathbf{A}\cdot\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}){\rm{d}}Y.

Note that the magnetic field
B={\rm{d}}\mathcal{A}
, identified as a scalar-function on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, in the coordinate system
(X,Y)
, is given by

B=\partial_{X}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\vec{\gamma}^{\perp})-\partial_{Y}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\vec{\gamma}).

Denote

\langle B\rangle_{\gamma}(z,\zeta):=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}B(z+t\zeta){\rm{d}}t,\qquad(z,\zeta)\in S^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}.

(1.9)

If the direction
\vec{\gamma}
is periodic, then
the closed geodesic
\gamma
is parametrized by the equation
Y=0
. So

\langle B\rangle_{\gamma}(Y)=-\partial_{Y}A_{\gamma}(Y).

Hence the following result holds.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\omega
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. The following two assertions are equivalent.

(i)

(MGCC)
holds.

(ii)

For every periodic direction
\vec{\gamma}
,
\omega_{\vec{\gamma}^{\perp}}
contains all the zeros of
\langle B\rangle_{\gamma}
, defined in (
1.9
).

Remark 1.5
.

In the special case where the magnetic field

H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}

,

[
AM14

Our second main result concerns resolvent estimates for the stationary Schrödinger operator under
(MGCC)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,

(1.10)

We would like to highlight that Theorem

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C\left(\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}+\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right),\quad\forall\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \forall u_{\lambda}\in H^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

1.4.
The role of the magnetic potential and the strategy of the proof

To understand the role of the magnetic potential
\mathbf{A}
, we assume first that
\mathbf{A}
takes the simple form
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1}(y),A_{2}(y))
depending only on the transversal direction
(0,1)
.
In the trapped regime where
|D_{x}|\sim h^{-1},|D_{y}|\ll h^{-1}
, the singularities of the solutions of the Schrödinger equation have the potential of concentration along the co-isotropic submanifold
\{\zeta_{0}=(1,0)\}
.
Then the simplified magnetic Schrödinger operator
H_{\mathbf{A},V}
has the principle term

D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}-2A_{1}(y)D_{x},

in the regime where
D_{y}
oscillates at a second scale
\hbar^{-1}:=h^{-1/2}
, as
D_{y}^{2}
and
2A_{1}(y)D_{x}
have the same order (see Section
9
for more details). Taking the Fourier transform in
x
and shifting a phase factor in time, the Schrödinger equation behaves like

i\hbar^{2}\partial_{t}u-(\hbar^{2}D_{y}^{2}\pm 2A_{1}(y))u=0,

which is dominated by the
\hbar
-semiclassical operator
\hbar^{2}D_{y}^{2}\pm 2A_{1}(y)
. If
A_{1}(y)
has some non-degenerate critical point

y=y_{0}
, then singularities could be confined in a small neighborhood of
y_{0}
. This crucial observation leads to the necessity of the geometric condition
(MGCC)
for the observability of (
1.1
) to hold.

To prove the positive result under
(MGCC)
, we first follow the classical compactness-uniqueness strategy in the context of Schrödinger equations (see
[
BZ12
]
) to reduce the proof to the high-frequency observability. When the classic geometric control condition is not satisfied, the crucial part of our analysis is to reduce the problem to the model case described above. Under
(MGCC)
, we are indeed able to prove the high-frequency (oscillating at scale
O(h^{-1})
) observability in an optimal time scale
t=O(h^{1/2})
.
Zooming into the new time scale
s=t/h
, the magnetic Schrödinger equation takes the semiclassical form

ih\partial_{s}u_{h}=P_{h}u_{h},

where

P_{h}=h^{2}D_{z}^{2}+hp_{1}^{\mathrm{w}}(z,hD_{z})+h^{2}p_{2}^{\mathrm{w}}(z,hD_{z})

which contains both the first and second order perturbations of the principal symbol
p_{0}=|\zeta|^{2}
.
The observability property for
t\sim O(h^{1/2})
requires understanding of the semiclassical propagator
e^{-\frac{{\rm i}sP_{h}}{h}}
up to the time scale
s\sim O(h^{-1/2})
. The second order perturbation
h^{2}p_{2}^{\mathrm{w}}(z,hD_{z})
will not influence the dynamics within this long-time scale of the free propagator
e^{-{\rm i}shD_{z}^{2}}
, and can be simply viewed as a perturbation. It turns out that the presence of the first order perturbation
hp_{1}^{\mathrm{w}}(z,hD_{z})
differs the long-time dynamics from the free-propagator, which yields different phenomena. This was also observed in
[
MR18
]
(see also
[
Wun12
]
) in the study of the two-microlocal regularity of quasimodes of the semiclassical operator
-h^{2}\Delta+\epsilon_{h}^{2}V
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, where
0\leq\epsilon_{h}\leq h
.

We adopt a normal form approach in the spirit of
[
BZ12
]
(see also
[
Sun23
]
in another setting of the first order perturbation). The basic idea is to conjugate the Schrödinger operator
H_{\mathbf{A},V}
by suitable unitary operators that will average the lower order symbols along the trapped direction. In order to average the first order perturbations from the magnetic potential, we need to conjugate by a bounded operator that is not close to the identity (which is different from
[
BZ12
]
). This procedure produces remainders that are not a priori be able to absorbed as acceptable errors of size
o(h^{3/2})
. So we have to treat separately the transversal low and high-frequencies in Section 4 and Section 5 in a similar way as in
[
Sun23
]
, in order to finally reduce the problem to the previously discussed model equation.

1.5.
Related results on the quantum unique ergodicity

Very recently, Morin and Rivière
[
MR24
]
prove the quantum unique ergodicity for the magnetic Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, under a slightly different but related magnetic geometric control condition. Note that the magnetic Laplacian is more generally defined as operators acting on sections of a line bundle over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Then the quantum unique ergodicity holds under their magnetic geometric control conditions asserting that the averaged magnetic field along any direction
\langle B\rangle_{\gamma}
is everywhere non-vanishing. Note that in our setting where the magnetic field is derived from the magnetic potential
B=\nabla\wedge\mathbf{A}
, such a condition cannot hold since
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}B{\rm{d}}z=0
. Nevertheless, their approach, different from ours, could eventually obtain the same resolvent estimate, i.e. Theorem
1.6
, under
(MGCC)
. Moreover, all of our results can be likely extended to the general magnetic Laplacian as in the setting of
[
MR24
]
, under the magnetic geometric control condition
(MGCC)
stated under the form (ii) of Proposition
1.3
.
Even more recently, Charles and Lefeuvre
[
CL25
]
investigate semiclassical defect measures associated with the magnetic Laplacian in the presence of a constant magnetic field on a closed hyperbolic surface.

2.
Semiclassical reduction of the observability

2.1.
Sharp semiclassical observability estimates

The goal of this part is to state semiclassical observability estimates that would be the key step of the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First, let us define
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-1,1)
such that
\chi
is equal to
1
near
0
, and the associated spectral projector for some (small) parameters
\rho>0
,
h>0
,

\Pi_{h,\rho}u:=\chi\left(\frac{h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1}{\rho}\right)u,\qquad u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

(2.1)

Our main result of this part is the following semiclassical observability estimate.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a numerical constant
T_{0}>0
such that for any
T\geq T_{0}
, there exist constants
\rho_{0}>0
,
h_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
,
h\in(0,h_{0})
, we have

\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}th^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t\qquad\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

(2.2)

Note that the numerical constant
T_{0}>0
can be chosen depending only on
\mathbf{A}
and
\omega
. We point out that Proposition
2.1
is the sharp version of semiclassical observability under our electromagnetic setting. For the sharpness, we refer to Section
9
.

2.2.
Consequences of the semiclassical observability estimates

Before the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we present two direct consequences.

The first one is the following small-time semiclassical observability estimate.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any
T>0
, there exists
\rho_{0}>0
,
h_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
,
h\in(0,h_{0})
, we have

\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t\qquad\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

(2.3)

Proof of Corollary
2.2
from Proposition
2.1
.

Let
T_{0}>0
be the numerical constant of Proposition
2.1
. Let us apply Proposition
2.1
with
T=T_{0}
, it fixes the constant
\rho_{0}>0
,
h_{0}>0
and
C>0
of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
T>0
. We decompose

[0,T]=\bigcup_{k=0}^{N_{T}-1}I_{k},\mbox{ with }I_{k}:=[kT_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}},(k+1)T_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}],

for some
h_{0,T}\in(0,h_{0})
depending on
T
.

By applying (
2.2
) for
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
,
h\in(0,h_{0,T})
, and by using the change of the variable
s=h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}t
, we have for some
C=C(T_{0})>0
,

\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq\frac{C}{h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}sH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}s.

We set
u_{h}(t,z):=e^{-{\rm i}tH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}(z)
. Then, we obtain from the previous estimate that for every
k\in\{0,\dots,N_{T}-1\}
,

h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{h}(t=kT_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\omega}\left|u_{h}(t+kT_{0}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}},z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t=C\int_{I_{k}}\int_{\omega}\left|u_{h}(t,z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t.

Since
({\rm i}\partial_{t}-H_{{\mathbf{A}},V})u_{h}=0
, we know
\|u_{h}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=\|u_{h}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
by the mass conservation law (
1.4
). Consequently, we sum up from
k=0
to
N_{T}-1
,

N_{T}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|u_{h}(t,z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t.

We conclude that

\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq\frac{C}{N_{T}h_{0,T}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|u_{h}(t,z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t.

This ends the proof of (
2.3
).
∎

By using Corollary
2.2
and a standard procedure, see for instance
[
BZ12
]
, reported in Section
8
, we deduce Theorem
1.4
.

The second consequence of Proposition
2.1
is the proof of the resolvent estimates from Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
from Proposition
2.1
.

Let
C_{0}>1
be a positive constant such that
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}+C_{0}
is a positive self-adjoint operator. First, from
[
TW09
, Proposition 6.6.4]
and the following unique continuation result
[
LRLR22
, Theorem 5.2]
for the elliptic operator
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
, i.e.

\left(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}u=\lambda u\ \text{in}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{2},\ u=0\ \text{in}\ \omega\right)\Rightarrow u=0\ \text{in}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{2},

one can restrict to prove the resolvent estimate for
|\lambda|>1+C_{0}
. We then split the proof in two different cases.

\bullet
Case
\lambda<-1-C_{0}
. By the choice of
C_{0}
,
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}+C_{0}
is positive self-adjoint. Then,

\displaystyle\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda+C_{0}|}\leq\frac{C_{0}+2}{|\lambda|+1},

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)^{-1}(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{0}+2}{|\lambda|+1}\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}.

\bullet
Case
\lambda>1+C_{0}
. Let us fix
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
as in Proposition
2.1
. Let
h=\sqrt{\lambda}^{-1}
and we consider the projector
\Pi_{h,\rho}=\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{-1}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1}{\rho}\right)
be the same as in (
2.1
). Then,
u_{\lambda}=\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}+(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})u_{\lambda}
. For the first part, we define

v(t,z):=e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{\frac{3}{4}}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}(z),\;\mbox{ and }f_{\lambda}:=(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}.

It is easy to verify that

v(t)=e^{-{\rm i}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}tH_{\mathbf{A},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}+{\rm i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-{\rm i}(t-s)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}H_{\mathbf{A},V}}\left(\frac{e^{-{\rm i}\lambda^{\frac{3}{4}}s}f_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right){\rm{d}}s

Thanks to Proposition
2.1
and the
L^{2}
-conservation of the group
e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}H_{\mathbf{A},V}}
, we derive that

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{0}]\times\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{0}]\times\omega)}+\frac{C}{\lambda^{1/4}}\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}}\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}}\|\Pi_{h,\rho}(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right)

(2.4)

For the second part,
(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})u_{\lambda}
satisfies
(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)(1-\widetilde{\Pi}_{\lambda,\rho})(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})u_{\lambda}=(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}
, where
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho}=\widetilde{\chi}\left(\frac{\lambda^{-1}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1}{\rho}\right)
with some
\tilde{\chi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\tilde{\chi}\chi=\tilde{\chi}
. Now the operator
(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)(1-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho})
is invertible with
\|\left((H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)(1-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho})\right)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{C_{\rho}}{\lambda}
. Then we have

\|(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\left((H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)(1-\widetilde{\Pi}_{\lambda,\rho})\right)^{-1}(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|(1-\Pi_{h,\rho})(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.5)

Therefore, combining the estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{1+\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}}\|(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-\lambda)u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\right),

leading to the desired resolvent estimate (
1.10
).
∎

3.
Beginning of the proof of the semiclassical observability estimate

The goal of this section is to begin the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

3.1.
Semiclassical measures

We turn to the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The proof is based on a contradiction argument. More precisely, suppose that (
2.2
) is untrue. Then there exist sequences

\displaystyle T_{n}\to\infty,\ \rho_{n}\rightarrow 0,\ h_{n}\rightarrow 0,\ v_{0,n}=\Pi_{h_{n},\rho_{n}}u_{0,n}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}),

\displaystyle({\rm i}{\partial}_{t}-h_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V})v_{n}(t,z)=0,v_{n}(0,z)=v_{0,n}(z):=\Pi_{h_{n},\rho_{n}}u_{0}(z),

such that

\|v_{0,n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=1,

(3.1)

and

\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-{\rm i}th_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}v_{0,n}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t\rightarrow 0.

(3.2)

The sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
is bounded in
L^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{z}^{2})
by the
L^{2}
-mass conservation law (
1.4
) and consequently after possibly extracting a subsequence, there exists a semiclassical defect measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}_{z}^{2}
such that for any function
\psi\in C^{0}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{t})
and any
a\in C^{\infty}_{c}(T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}_{z}^{2})
, we have

\langle\mu,\psi(t)a(z,\zeta)\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{z}^{2}}\psi(t)(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h_{n}}(a)v_{n})(t,z)\overline{v_{n}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t.

(3.3)

For the semiclassical Weyl quantization on the torus, one can see Appendix
A.1
. For the proof of this existence of semiclassical measure
\mu
, one can refer to
[
AM14
,
Mac09
,
Zwo12
,
GMMP97
]
.

As usual with semiclassical methods, we will drop the index
n
and rather write
(v_{h})_{h\to 0^{+}}
satisfying that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}({\rm i}\partial_{t}-h^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V})v_{h}=0,\\
v_{h}|_{t=0}=v^{0}_{h}:=\Pi_{h,\rho}u^{0}_{h},\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

with the following properties

\|v^{0}_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=1,

(3.5)

and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|v_{h}(z)\right|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t=o(1)\mbox{ as }h\to 0^{+}.

(3.6)

3.2.
First microlocalization

In this subsection, we reduce the proof to the analysis of the measure
\mu
supported in the periodic geodesics. This is accomplished by a standard propagation property of the semi-classic measure
\mu
. More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

The measure
\mu
defined by (
3.3
) has the following properties.

(i)

The measure
\mu
is supported in the set

\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset\{(t,z,\zeta)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}:|\zeta|=1\},

(3.7)

and the wave front set satisfies for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}

\mathrm{WF}_{h}(v_{h}(t,\cdot))\subset\{(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\ ;\ |\zeta|=1\}.

(ii)

For any
t_{0}<t_{1}
, we have

\mu((t_{0},t_{1})\times T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2})=t_{1}-t_{0}.

(3.8)

(iii)

For a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\zeta\cdot\nabla_{z}\mu(t,\cdot)=0.

(3.9)

In particular, the support of
\mu
is invariant under the geodesic flow i.e.

(t_{0},z_{0},\zeta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\Rightarrow(t_{0},z_{0}+s\zeta_{0},\zeta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(iv)

The measure
\mu
vanishes on
(0,T)\times T^{*}\omega
.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
is rather standard, see for instance
[
AM14
,
Mac09
,
Zwo12
]
, but for the sake of completeness, we include the proof here.

For the first statement, we note that
h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}=-h^{2}\Delta_{z}+h\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(A_{1}\xi+A_{2}\eta)+h^{2}(|{\mathbf{A}}|^{2}+V)
. By definition, we know the fact that

v_{h}=\tilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho}v_{h},

where
\tilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho}=\tilde{\chi}(\frac{h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1}{\rho})
with some
\tilde{\chi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\tilde{\chi}\chi=\chi
. Thus, on the one hand, we have

\displaystyle(h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1)v_{h}=\tilde{\Pi}_{h,\rho}(h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1)v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(\rho\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(h^{2}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}-1)v_{h}=

\displaystyle(-h^{2}\Delta_{z}-1)v_{h}+h\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(A_{1}\xi+A_{2}\eta)+h^{2}(|{\mathbf{A}}|^{2}+V)\right)v_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-h^{2}\Delta_{z}-1)v_{h}+\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h\right).

Therefore, we know

(-h^{2}\Delta_{z}-1)v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h+\rho\right).

(3.10)

As
\rho,h\rightarrow 0
, we deduce that
(-h^{2}\Delta-1)v_{h}=o_{L^{2}}(1)
, which yields
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\subset\{|\zeta|=1\}
.

The support property of the wave front set is similar to the proof of the first statement, see also
[
Zwo12
, Theorem 8.15]
. We omit the details details here.

For the second statement, we take any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
a_{1}(\zeta)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
a_{1}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
|\zeta|=1
. By (
3.3
) and the support property (
3.7
), we have

\langle\mu,\varphi(t)\rangle=\langle\mu,\varphi(t)a_{1}(\zeta)\rangle=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)\Big(\langle(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a_{1})-1)v_{h}(t),v_{h}(t)\rangle_{L_{z}^{2}}+\|v_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{L_{z}^{2}}\Big)dt.

From the support property (
3.7
), the first limit on the right hand side is zero. Since
\|v_{h}(t)\|_{L_{z}^{2}}=\|v_{h}(0)\|_{L_{z}^{2}}=1
by the
L^{2}
-mass conservation law, we deduce that
\langle\mu,\varphi(t)\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)dt
. Approximating
\mathbf{1}_{(t_{0},t_{1})}
by a sequence of
\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we obtain (
3.8
).

Now we define the Wigner distribution
W_{h}(t,\cdot)
on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
by

\langle W_{h}(t,\cdot),a\rangle:=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z,\qquad\forall a\in C^{\infty}_{c}(T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle W_{h}(t,\cdot),a\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)\partial_{t}v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{\partial_{t}v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\rm i}\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{{\rm i}H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\rm i}[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}]v_{h}(t,z)\cdot\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z.

Using the symbolic calculus, more precisely the exact commutator formula (
A.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm i}h[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}]

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm i}}{h}[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),-h^{2}\Delta]+{\rm i}[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}({\mathbf{A}}\cdot\zeta)]+{\rm i}h[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),|{\mathbf{A}}|^{2}+V]

(3.11)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(2\zeta\cdot\nabla_{z}a)+\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\mathopen{}\left(h\right).

For any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(t)\partial_{t}\langle W_{h}(t,\cdot),a\rangle{\rm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\psi^{\prime}(t)\left(\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)v_{h}\right)(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

Consequently, we obtain

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{\rm i}h\psi(t)[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}]v_{h}(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t=0.

Thanks to (
3.11
), we have

0=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\psi(t)\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(2\zeta\cdot\nabla_{z}a)v_{h}(t,z)\overline{v_{h}(t,z)}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t=\langle\mu,2\psi(t)\zeta\cdot\nabla_{z}a\rangle.

This implies that
\mu
solves (
3.9
).
As a consequence, we know that the measure
\mu
is invariant under the geodesic flow.

The last statement comes directly from (
3.3
) and (
3.6
).
∎

The core of the following proof consists in demonstrating that

\mu\equiv 0.

This will be a contradiction with property
\mathrm{(ii)}
.

3.3.
Irrational directions.

In the spirit of
[
BBZ13
,
BZ12
]
, we begin by proving
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
does not contain irrational directions. This is due to the fact that the
z-
projection of a trajectory in
T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
associated with an irrational direction is dense (see also
[
LBM24
]
).
We define the set
\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}
of irrational directions on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
,

\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}:=\left\{(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2};\ |\zeta|=1,\,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\{\zeta\}^{\perp}=\{0\}\right\},

and
\Sigma_{\mathbb{Q}}=\{(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2};\ |\zeta|=1\}\setminus\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}
the set of rational directions. The set
\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}
is clearly invariant by the flow

\quad(z+s\zeta,\zeta)\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}}\qquad\forall(z,\zeta)\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}},\forall s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.12)

Let us define
\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}
to be the restriction of
the measure
\mu
to
\Sigma_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}
. The following result holds.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have that
\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}=0

Proof.

Since
\mu
is
invariant by the geodesic flow according to
(iii)
, then for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})=\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})),

where the flow
\Phi_{s}
is defined by
\Phi_{s}(z,\zeta)=(z+s\zeta,\zeta)
.
As a consequence, we obtain by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}))ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(t_{1},t_{2})\times\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}d\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}(t,z\zeta)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{(t_{1},t_{2})}(t)1_{\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}(z,\zeta)d\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}(t,z\zeta)ds

\displaystyle=\int 1_{(t_{1},t_{2})}(t)\times\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}1_{\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}(z,\zeta)ds\right)d\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}(t,z,\zeta).

The equidistribution theorem shows that for any
(z,\zeta)
in the support of
\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}1_{\Phi_{s}(\omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}(z,\zeta)ds=\frac{|\omega|}{|{\mathbb{T}}^{2}|}.

Hence the dominated convergence theorem and
(iv)
show that

\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}}((t_{1},t_{2})\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})=0,

(3.13)

concluding the proof.
∎

3.4.
Reduction to a finite number of periodic directions.

In the same spirit of
[
BG20
, Remark 1.4]
, we claim that periodic geodesics corresponding to directions
\frac{(n,m)}{\sqrt{n^{2}+m^{2}}}
with
\gcd(n,m)=1
will also enter the control region
\omega
as soon as
\sqrt{n^{2}+m^{2}}
is large enough. Therefore, the following result holds.

Proposition 3.3
.

The support of
\mu
contains only a finite number of periodic directions, i.e.

\sharp\ \Sigma_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\cap\left\{(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\ ;\ |\zeta|=1,\ (z,\zeta)\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\right\}<+\infty.

More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
large enough, for all

and

\gcd(p,q)=1

(x,y,\frac{p}{\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}}},\frac{q}{\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}}})\notin\operatorname{supp}(\mu)

Proof.

As we demonstrated in the previous part, only periodic geodesics can be included in
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
. In this proof, we identify
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
just for the simplicity of the proof. Since
\omega
is open, by shifting the coordinate axes, we assume that
(a,b)\in(0,1)^{2}\in\omega
and there exists
\delta=\delta(a,b,\omega)\in(0,1)
sufficiently small such that the square
(a-\delta,a+\delta)\times(b-\delta,b+\delta)
is still contained in
\omega
.

Now it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\exists p_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\mbox{ large enough, for all }p\geq p_{0}\mbox{ and }q\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mbox{ with }\gcd(p,q)=1,

\displaystyle\mbox{ such that the straight line }t(p,q),\mbox{ starting from }(0,0),\mbox{ enters into }

(3.14)

\displaystyle(m_{j}+a-\delta,m_{j}+a+\delta)\times(n_{j}+b-\delta,n_{j}+b+\delta)\mbox{ for some }(m_{j},n_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}.

This is a direct consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
large enough, for all
p\geq p_{0}
and
q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
with
\gcd(p,q)=1
and
1\leq q<p
, such that we can find a sequence of integers
(m_{j},n_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}
such that
\frac{n_{j}}{m_{j}}\to\frac{q}{p}
and for all

large enough,

\left|\Big(\frac{m_{j}+a-\delta}{p},\frac{m_{j}+a+\delta}{p}\Big)\cap\Big(\frac{n_{j}+b-\delta}{q},\frac{n_{j}+b+\delta}{q}\Big)\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{p}.

(3.15)

Indeed, (
3.15
) shows that there exists

such that
(t_{0}p,t_{0}q)\in(m_{j}+a-\delta,m_{j}+a+\delta)\times(n_{j}+b-\delta,n_{j}+b+\delta)
, which implies the statement (

) immediately.
∎

Therefore, we only need to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

We will realize the choices of
(m_{j},n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in the following form

m_{j}=jp+r,\;\;n_{j}=jq+s,\;\mbox{ with }r,s\mbox{ to be chosen later.}

(3.16)

Let us denote

\displaystyle I_{j}:=(\frac{m_{j}}{p}+\frac{a-\delta}{p},\frac{m_{j}}{p}+\frac{a+\delta}{p}),\;J_{j}=(\frac{n_{j}}{q}+\frac{b-\delta}{q},\frac{n_{j}}{q}+\frac{b+\delta}{q}),

and let
C(I)
be the center of an interval
I
. Hence,

C(I_{j})=\frac{m_{j}+a}{p},\;C(J_{j})=\frac{n_{j}+b}{q}.

Thanks to the form (
3.16
), we know that
C(I_{j})-C(J_{j})=\frac{aq-bp}{pq}+\frac{rq-sp}{pq}
. Since
\gcd(p,q)=1
, there exists an integer pair
(r_{q},s_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that
r_{q}q-s_{p}p=1
. Set
c=\lfloor qa-pb\rfloor
to be the integer such that
c\leq qa-pb<c+1
and

r:=cr_{q},\;s:=cs_{p}.

Then, due to the preceding choice and
r_{q}q-s_{p}p=1
, we derive that

|C(I_{j})-C(J_{j})|=|\frac{aq-bp}{pq}+\frac{cr_{q}q-cs_{p}p}{pq}|=\frac{1}{pq}|aq-bp-\lfloor qa-pb\rfloor|<\frac{1}{pq}.

Now we claim that
|I_{j}\cap J_{j}|\geq\frac{\delta}{p}
, provided that
p\geq p_{0}>\frac{1}{\delta}
.

Indeed, the length of
I_{j}
is
\frac{2\delta}{p}
and the length of
J_{j}
is
\frac{2\delta}{q}
. Choosing that
p_{0}>\frac{1}{\delta}>1
, for any
p\geq p_{0}
, we have

\frac{1}{pq}\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}q}<\frac{\delta}{q}.

Using that
1\leq q<p
, we know that
\frac{\delta}{q}=\max\{\frac{\delta}{p},\frac{\delta}{q}\}
. Since
|C(I_{j})-C(J_{j})|<\frac{1}{pq}<\frac{\delta}{q}
, we obtain

|I_{j}\cap J_{j}|=\frac{|I_{j}|+|J_{j}|}{2}-|C(I_{j})-C(J_{j})|>\frac{\delta}{p}+\frac{\delta}{q}-\frac{\delta}{q}=\frac{\delta}{p},

leading to (
3.15
).
∎

3.5.
A change of variable

This step is devoted to showing that, up to a change of variables, we can assume that
\zeta_{0}=(1,0)
.
Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the isometry defined by

\forall(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad F(x,y)=x\zeta_{0}^{\perp}+y\zeta_{0},

where
\zeta_{0}^{\perp}=\frac{(-m,n)}{\sqrt{n^{2}+m^{2}}}
.
One can readily verify that for any function

periodic with respect to
(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, the function

is periodic with respect to
(M\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, with

. Moreover, if

is solution to the Schrödinger equation (

) then,
v(t,\cdot)=F^{*}u(t,\cdot)=u(t,F^{-1}(\cdot))
is solution to the Schrödinger equation posed on
\mathbb{R}^{2}/(M\mathbb{Z})^{2}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}v=H_{\mathbf{F^{*}A},F^{*}V}v&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}/(M\mathbb{Z})^{2},\\
v(0,\cdot)=F^{*}u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}/(M\mathbb{Z})^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

As a consequence, if we define

then,

\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad F^{*}\left(e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}v_{n}\right)=e^{-itH_{\mathbf{F^{*}{\mathbf{A}}},F^{*}V}}w_{n}.

In the following, the new torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/(M\mathbb{Z})^{2}
is still denoted
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Up to a subsequence, associated to this family of solutions of the Schrödinger equation (

) with initial data

is a semiclassical defect measure
\nu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M}_{+}(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}))
satisfying:

•

for all

and

•

\nu_{T}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{(1,0)\})>0
, with
\nu_{T}(dz,d\zeta)=\int_{0}^{T}\nu(t,dz,d\zeta)dt
.

In other words, we are in the same situation as in the second step, with
\zeta_{0}=(1,0)
. For the sake of conciseness, we keep in the remainder of the proof the notations adopted in the second step and assume that
\zeta_{0}=(1,0)
.

4.2.
Second averaging

In the sequel, we fix the anti-derivative

\partial_{x}^{-1}f(x,y):=\int_{-\pi}^{x}f(x^{\prime},y){\rm{d}}x^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm{d}}x^{\prime}\int_{\pi}^{x^{\prime}}f(x^{\prime\prime},y){\rm{d}}x^{\prime\prime}.

\mathcal{F}_{x}(\partial_{x}^{-1}f)(k,y)=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{x}f(k,y)}{{\rm i}k}.

We introduce two cut-off functions

Proposition 4.3

We finally conclude that

Proof.

(8.3)

8.1.
Remove the compact term in the weaker observability estimate

The goal is to prove the true observability estimate without the error term.

Proof.

Let us assume that (
8.1
) holds for any time
T>0
.

First step: an unique continuation property.
Let us consider the following space

\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2});\ e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u(x)=0\ \text{on}\ [0,T]\times\omega\right\},

First of all, let us show that the Hilbert space
(\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V},\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})})
is a finite dimensional space. Thanks to (
8.1
), we have that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}
are equivalent. As a consequence, one can readily show that the unit closed ball
\overline{B_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}(0,1)}\cap\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
is compact, thanks to the Rellich Theorem. In particular, the Riesz Theorem provides that the dimension of
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
is finite.

Let us now show that
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}=\left\{0\right\}
. To that end, we proceed by contradiction and assume
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}\neq\left\{0\right\}
. We begin by noticing that
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
is invariant by the action of
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
. Indeed, if
u\in\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
, then for all
0<\varepsilon<T
,
u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{e^{-i\varepsilon H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u-u}{\varepsilon}
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{T-\varepsilon,\theta,V}
. Thus, by applying (
8.1
) at time
T-\varepsilon
, we obtain that for all
0<\varepsilon<T
,

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C{{}^{\prime}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})},

for some positive constant
C^{\prime}>0
.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{H}_{\theta,V}u
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
and
H^{-2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
, it follows that
u\in D\left(H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}\right)
(see for instance
[
Paz83
, Section 1.1]
) and

\|H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})},

for some positive constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
.
Moreover, since
u_{\varepsilon}
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{T-\varepsilon,{\mathbf{A}},V}
for all
0<\varepsilon<T
, we deduce that
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}u\in\mathcal{N}_{T-\delta,{\mathbf{A}},V}
for all
0<\delta<T
. Then,
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}u\in\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
and
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}
is invariant by the action of
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
. As a consequence, since
\mathcal{H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}_{\left|\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}\right.}
is a self-adjoint operator on the finite dimensional Hilbert space
(\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V},\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})})
, there exists a nontrivial function
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}\phi=\lambda\phi\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\in\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}.

In particular,
\phi
is an eigenfunction of
H_{{\mathbf{A}},V}
which vanishes on an open subset. By the unique continuation result,
[
LRLR22
, Theorem 5.2]
, we deduce that
\phi\equiv 0
. This provides a contradiction and consequently,
\mathcal{N}_{T,{\mathbf{A}},V}=\left\{0\right\}.

Second step: we remove the
H^{-2}
-norm in the weak observability estimate.

By now, we establish that there exists a positive constant
C{{}^{\prime}}=C{{}^{\prime}}(T,M)>0
such that

\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}),\quad\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}\leq C{{}^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{0}(z)\right|^{2}dzdt.

Once again, we proceed by contradiction and it provides sequences
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
with
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-it\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{n},V_{n}}}u_{n}(z)\right|^{2}dzdt\leq\frac{1}{n}.

(8.7)

Since
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
, there exists
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
such that, up to a subsequence,
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
weakly converges to
f
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
and strongly converges to
f
in
H^{-2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).
Thanks to the weak observability estimate (
8.1
), it follows that

1\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{H^{-2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}.

(8.8)

We also have that
e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{n}(z)
weakly converges in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}))
to
e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}f(z)

From (
8.7
), we then deduce that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}f(z)\right|^{2}dzdt\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}\left|e^{-itH_{{\mathbf{A}},V}}u_{n}(z)\right|^{2}dzdt=0,

that implies that

. This contradicts (

) and ends the proof of the observability estimate.
∎

9.1.
Reduction to the model equation

We begin with some simplifications. By definition, if
(MGCC)
is not satisfied, there exists a periodic direction
\vec{\gamma}
such that
A_{\gamma}
has at least one non-degenerate critical point outside the strips
\omega_{\gamma^{\perp}}
.
By changing coordinate system, we may assume that
\vec{\gamma}=\zeta_{0}=(1,0)
.
Next, since the observability is invariant by the gauge
\mathbf{A}\mapsto\mathbf{A}+\nabla g
, we assume that
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1}(y),A_{2}(x,y))
. By assumption,
A_{\gamma}=A_{1}
has a critical point outside the horizontal strips
\overline{\omega}_{\gamma}^{\perp}
. By translation invariance, we assume that
y=0
is a non-degenerate critical point outside
\overline{\omega}_{\gamma}^{\perp}
.
Given any
T>0
, our goal is to construct a sequence of solutions
(u_{k}(t))_{k\geq 1}
of the magnetic Schrödinger equation, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}|u_{k}(t,z)|^{2}{\rm{d}}z{\rm{d}}t}{\|u_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}=0.

(9.1)

The strategy is to construct well-prepared solutions for the model equation

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u-(D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2})u+2A_{1}(y)D_{x}u+A_{2}(y)D_{y}u+D_{y}(A_{2}(y)u)-\langle V^{(1)}\rangle(y)u=0,

(9.2)

that are oscillated at the scale
|D_{y}|\leq h^{-1/2},|D_{x}|\sim h^{-1}
and concentrated near the non-degenerate critical point
y_{0}=0
outside
\overline{\omega}_{\gamma^{\perp}}
. Then we take the normal form transform from the opposite direction to obtain the desired solutions of the original equation. Here we have to construct
o(h^{2})
-quasimodes instead of
o(h^{\frac{3}{2}})
, comparing to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. To this end, we have to perform a finer normal form transformation as described below.

Set
g_{2}(x,y,\xi),G_{2}=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(g_{2}\vartheta(\eta)\eta)
as in Proposition
4.3
. Replacing
g_{1}(x,y),A_{2}^{(1)}(x,y),\langle A_{1}\rangle(y)
there by
0,A_{2}(x,y),A_{1}(y)
, the computation in the proof of Proposition
4.3
gives

\displaystyle e^{G_{2}}(P_{h}^{(1)}+Q_{h}^{(1)})e^{-G_{2}}=P_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(3)},

(9.3)

where

\displaystyle R_{h}^{(2)}:=h^{2}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}\big(V^{(1)}(x,y)+2{\rm i}g_{2}(x,y,\xi)\vartheta(\eta)A_{1}^{\prime}(y)\xi\big)-h\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(r_{1+2\alpha}(x,y,\xi)\eta^{2}),

(9.4)

\displaystyle R_{h}^{(3)}:=h^{2}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(\Theta(x,y,\xi,\eta)\eta)+R_{2+\alpha}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}(h^{3}).

(9.5)

We recall the model operator
P_{h}^{(2)}
defined in (
4.15
), the symbol
r_{1+2\alpha}(x,y,\xi)=2{\rm i}\partial_{y}g_{2}\vartheta(\eta)
, and

\Theta(x,y,\xi,\eta)
is a smooth symbol with compact support in all variables. The computation (
9.3
) expands one more term in the Taylor expansion compared to the proof of Proposition
4.3
. and the right hand of (
9.3
) is organized in the way that, the first term

is the model operator, while the second term
R_{h}^{(2)}
will eventually generate an
O(h^{2})
error applying to the well-prepared solutions, thus they have to be averaged one more time. The third term

will contribute an acceptable error

when applying to the well-prepared solutions.

The point here is that
r_{1+2\alpha}(x,y,\xi)
and

both have zero mean value when integrating over
\mathbb{T}
with respect to

. Therefore, by successive normal form reduction, we will obtain the model equation (

) after averaging these symbols.

More precisely, set

G_{3}=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(g_{3}(x,y,\xi)\vartheta(\eta)\eta^{2}+hg_{4}(x,y,\xi,\eta)),

Then

\displaystyle e^{G_{3}}(P_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(3)})e^{-G_{3}}=

\displaystyle P_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(2)}+\frac{h}{i}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(\{g_{3}\vartheta(\eta)\eta^{2}+hg_{4},\xi^{2}\})+\widetilde{R}_{h}^{(3)},

where here and in the sequel,

\displaystyle g_{3}(x,y,\xi):=

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(\xi)}{2{\rm i}\xi}\partial_{x}^{-1}(r_{1+2\alpha})(x,y,\xi),

\displaystyle g_{4}(x,y,\xi,\eta):=

\displaystyle-\frac{\psi(\xi)}{2{\rm i}\xi}\partial_{x}^{-1}(V^{(1)}-\langle V^{(1)}\rangle)(x,y)-\partial_{x}^{-1}(g_{2})(x,y,\xi)\vartheta(\eta).

We finally get

e^{G_{3}}(P_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(2)}+R_{h}^{(3)})e^{-G_{3}}=P_{h}^{(2)}+h^{2}\langle V^{(1)}\rangle(y)+\widetilde{R}_{h}^{(3)}.

Hence it suffices to construct well-prepared solutions for the model equation (

Set
w_{h}(t,x,y):=v_{h}(t,y)e^{{\rm i}kx}
, where

. We ask for

satisfying the equation

which is equivalent to the model equation

\displaystyle{\rm i}h\partial_{t}v_{h}-\frac{1}{h}v_{h}-(hD_{y}^{2}-2A_{1}(y))v_{h}+h\langle A_{2}\rangle(y)D_{y}v_{h}+hD_{y}(\langle A_{2}\rangle(y)v_{h})-h\langle V^{(1)}\rangle(y)v_{h}=0.

(9.6)

Take
0<b<\pi
small enough such that
\mathbb{T}_{x}\times(-b,b)\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}\setminus\overline{\omega}_{\gamma^{\perp}}
.

We have the following non-observability result of the model equation:

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
T>0
,
0<b<\pi
. There exist a sequence of small parameters

and a sequence of approximate solutions

such that

Moreover, for any

and

in the next subsection and proceed on the proof of Theorem

Indeed, for

,

,

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\rightarrow 0}\|w_{h}(0)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}>0.

Moreover, for any

In particular, when acting on

\widetilde{R}_{h}^{(3)}

defined in (

\widetilde{u}_{h}=e^{-G_{2}}\circ e^{-G_{3}}w_{h}

\displaystyle e^{G_{3}}\circ e^{G_{2}}\circ({\rm i}h^{2}\partial_{t}+h^{2}H_{\mathbf{A},V})\widetilde{u}_{h}=

\displaystyle\big({\rm i}h^{2}\partial_{t}-P_{h}^{(2)}-h^{2}\langle V^{(1)}\rangle(y)\big)w_{h}-\widetilde{R}_{h}^{(3)}w_{h}

9.2.
One-dimensional non-observability result

First we construct suitable quasi-modes for a semiclassical Schrödinger operator:

Lemma 9.2
.

A_{1},A_{2}\in C^{4}({\mathbb{T}}_{y})

=&\sqrt{-A_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)}\hbar_{k}v_{k}+(-2A_{1}(0)+\Lambda_{0}\hbar_{k}^{2})v_{k}+\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\mathopen{}\left(\hbar_{k}^{\frac{5}{2}}\right).\end{split}

with

Moreover, for any

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{y})^{m}y^{\ell}v_{k}\|_{L^{2}((-b,b))}+\|y^{\ell}(\partial_{y})^{m}v_{k}\|_{L^{2}((-b,b))}=\mathcal{O}(\hbar_{k}^{-\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\ell}{2}}),\quad\|(\partial_{y})^{m}v_{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}=\mathcal{O}(\hbar_{k}^{-\frac{m}{2}}).

(9.8)

Set
\hbar_{k}=h_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Let

v_{k}(t,y):=e^{-\frac{{\rm i}t}{\hbar_{k}^{4}}+2{\rm i}\frac{A_{1}(0)t}{\hbar_{k}^{2}}-{\rm i}\sqrt{-A_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)}\frac{t}{\hbar_{k}}-{\rm i}\Lambda_{0}t}v_{k}(y),

where

satisfies the approximate equation (

).
Consequently, Proposition
9.1
follows directly from Lemma

Finally, we prove Lemma
9.2
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the standard WKB approximation method. For simplicity, we omit the subscription
k
in
\hbar_{k}
, and without loss of generality, we also assume that
A_{1}(0)=0
and
W(0)=0
(up to changing the value of
\Lambda_{0}
).
Let

\beta:=\sqrt{-A_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}:=-\hbar^{2}\partial_{y}^{2}+\beta^{2}y^{2}-\beta\hbar.

Denote
H_{j}(y)
be the
j
-th Hermite polynomial and
\phi_{j,\hbar}(y)=c_{j,\hbar}H_{j}(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\hbar^{-\frac{1}{2}}y)e^{-\frac{\beta y^{2}}{2\hbar}}
is the normalized eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}
associated to the eigenvalue
2j\beta\hbar
. Denote
E_{j}:=\mathrm{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\phi_{j,\hbar})

Note that
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})=E_{0}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}
is invertible on orthogonal spaces
E_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}^{-1}|_{E_{j}}=(2j\beta\hbar)^{-1}\mathrm{Id}_{E_{j}}
. Moreover, the operator of the multiplication
y
and the derivation
\partial_{y}
send
E_{j}
to
E_{j-1}\oplus E_{j+1}
(with the convention
E_{-1}=\{0\}
) and for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|y^{m}|_{E_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{O}(L^{2})}\leq C_{m,j}\hbar^{\frac{m}{2}},\quad\|\partial_{y}^{m}|_{E_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{O}(L^{2})}\leq C_{m,j}\hbar^{-\frac{m}{2}}.

(9.9)

We would like to approximate the
the following equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}v_{\hbar}=R_{1}(y)v_{\hbar}-{\rm i}\hbar^{2}A_{2}(y){\partial}_{y}v_{\hbar}-{\rm i}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y}(A_{2}(y)v_{\hbar})-\hbar^{2}W(y)v_{\hbar}

(9.10)

with an acceptable error
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\frac{5}{2}})
.
Taylor expanding
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}(y)=-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}y^{2}+R_{1}(y),\text{ with }R_{1}(y)=\sum_{j=3}^{4}r^{(1)}_{j}y^{j}+\mathcal{O}\mathopen{}\left(|y|^{5}\right)=\mathcal{O}\mathopen{}\left(|y|^{3}\right),

\displaystyle A_{2}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{4}r^{(2)}_{j}y^{j}+\mathcal{O}\mathopen{}\left(|y|^{5}\right),\quad W(y)=\mathcal{O}(|y|).

We will first construct the WKB approximation on
{\mathbb{R}}
and later cut it on the circle
{\mathbb{T}}
. Set

v_{\hbar}=v_{0,\hbar}+v_{1,\hbar}+v_{2,\hbar},

where
v_{0,\hbar}=\phi_{0,\hbar}
and
v_{1,\hbar},v_{2,\hbar}\in\oplus_{j=0}^{6}E_{j}
to be determined.
Plugin (
9.10
) with the Taylor expansion, the right hand side equals to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=3}^{4}r_{j}^{(1)}y^{j}v_{\hbar}-2{\rm i}\hbar^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{1}r_{j}^{(2)}y^{j}\partial_{y}v_{\hbar}-{\rm i}\hbar^{2}r_{1}^{(2)}v_{\hbar}+\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}(\hbar^{\frac{5}{2}})

(9.11)

The leading order
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\frac{3}{2}})
or (
9.11
) equals to

\displaystyle r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}\phi_{0,\hbar}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y}\phi_{0,\hbar}\in E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}(\hbar^{\frac{3}{2}}).

There exists complex coefficients
\beta_{j}^{(1)},j=1,2,3
, with
v_{1,\hbar}:=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta^{(1)}_{j}\phi_{j,\hbar}
, such that

\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}v_{1,\hbar}=r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}\phi_{0,h}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y}\phi_{0,\hbar}.

The next order
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})
in (
9.11
) now becomes

-{\rm i}\hbar^{2}r_{1}^{(2)}v_{0,\hbar}+(r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y})v_{1,\hbar}+r_{4}^{(1)}y^{4}v_{0,h}.

Since
(r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y})v_{1,\hbar}+r_{4}^{(1)}y^{4}v_{0,\hbar}\in\oplus_{j=0}^{6}E_{j}
, there exist complex coefficients
c_{0}
and
\beta_{j}^{(2)},j=1,\cdots,6
, such that with

Note that

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}c_{0}:=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big((r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y})v_{1,\hbar}(y)+r_{4}^{(1)}y^{4}v_{0,\hbar}(y)\big)\cdot v_{0,\hbar}(y)\;{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{0,\hbar}(y)\cdot\Big(r_{3}^{(1)}(\hbar^{-\frac{1}{2}}y)^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\beta_{j}\phi_{j,\hbar}(y)-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\hbar^{1/2}\partial_{y}\phi_{j,\hbar}(y)\Big)\;{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\phi_{0,\hbar}(y))^{2}\cdot(\hbar^{-\frac{1}{2}}y)^{4}\;{\rm{d}}y.

In particular,

is a complex constant independent of
\hbar
.
Hence for
v_{\hbar}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}v_{j,\hbar}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}v_{\hbar}=

\displaystyle(r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y})v_{0,\hbar}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r_{4}^{(1)}y^{4}v_{0,\hbar}+(r_{3}^{(1)}y^{3}-2{\rm i}r_{0}^{(2)}\hbar^{2}\partial_{y})v_{1,\hbar}+\hbar^{2}(-c_{0}-{\rm i}r_{1}^{(2)})v_{0,\hbar}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=3}^{4}r_{j}^{(1)}y^{j}v_{\hbar}-2{\rm i}\hbar^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{1}r_{j}^{(2)}y^{j}\partial_{y}v_{\hbar}-({\rm i}r_{1}^{(2)}+c_{0})\hbar^{2}v_{\hbar}+\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}(\hbar^{\frac{5}{2}}).

for

constructed above decays of order

Appendix A
Semiclassical measures for electromagnetic Schrödinger equations

A.1.
Semiclassical Weyl quantization on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

In this part, we recall the semiclassical Weyl quantization on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. A symbol
a=a(z,\zeta)\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}})
is periodic with respect to
z
-variable. Following the convention in
[
Zwo12
, Chapter 5]
, the quantization is explicitly given by

\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a(z,\zeta))g:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}A^{\mathrm{w}}_{k}g(z),\;\;A^{\mathrm{w}}_{k}g(z):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}e^{\frac{{\rm i}(z-z^{\prime}+2k\pi)\cdot\zeta}{h}}a(\frac{z+z^{\prime}}{2},\zeta)g(z^{\prime})\frac{{\rm{d}}z^{\prime}{\rm{d}}\zeta}{(2\pi h)^{2}}.

(A.1)

Then
A^{\mathrm{w}}_{k}=\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\tau_{-2k\pi}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)
, where
\tau_{z_{0}}g(z):=g(z-z_{0})
.
The following theorem, borrowed from
[
Zwo12
]
, states the basic property of the semiclassical Weyl quantization on the torus.

Theorem A.1
.

Suppose that
a,b\in C_{b}^{\infty}(T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2})
.

(i)

The operator
\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)
can be extended as an operator mapping
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
. Moreover, we have

\|\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq C\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+\mathcal{O}(h^{1/2}).

(ii)

If
a\geq 0
, then there exist constants
C\geq 0
and
h_{0}>0
such that

\langle\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)u,u\rangle\geq-Ch\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}\qquad\forall 0<h<h_{0},\ \forall u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}).

(iii)

There exists
c\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})
such that

\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(b)=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(c),

where

c(z,\zeta)=e^{i\frac{h}{2}\sigma(D_{z},D_{\zeta},D_{\tilde{z}},D_{\tilde{\zeta}})}(a(z,\zeta)b(\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta}))_{|(\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta})=(z,\zeta)},

where the symplectic product is defined by

\sigma(u,v)=\langle\zeta,\tilde{z}\rangle-\langle z,\tilde{\zeta}\rangle,\text{ for }u=(z,\zeta),v=(\tilde{z},\tilde{\zeta}).

Moreover, we have

c=ab+\frac{h}{2i}\{a,b\}+\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(h^{2}),

and

[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(b)]=\frac{h}{{\rm i}}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(\{a,b\})+\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}}(h^{3}).

(iv)

Given a polynomial function
P
of degree
2
, we have

[\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a),\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}{P(\zeta)}]=-\frac{h}{i}\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(\nabla_{\zeta}P(\zeta)\cdot\nabla_{z}a)

(A.2)

Finally, we recall the definition of the semiclassical wavefront set, following the convention in
[
Zwo12
, Chapter 8]
. We say that
u=\{u_{h}\}_{h\in(0,h_{0})}\subset\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
is a
h-
temptered family of distributions if there exists a constant
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}=\mathcal{O}\mathopen{}\left(h^{-N_{0}}\right)
.

Definition A.2

(Wavefront set)
.

We define the wavefront sets
\mathrm{WF}_{h}(u)
and
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(u)
as follows:

(i)

The semiclassical wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{h}(u)
associated with a
h-
temptered family
u=\{u_{h}\}_{h\in(0,h_{0})}
is the complement of the set of points
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})\in T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for which there exists a symbol
a\in\mathcal{S}^{0}
such that
a(z_{0},\zeta_{0})\neq 0
and
\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)u_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{N}\right)
for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(ii)

The semiclassical wavefront set of order
m
, denoted by
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(u)
, is the complement of the set of points
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})\in T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
for which there exists a symbol
a\in\mathcal{S}^{0}
such that
a(z_{0},\zeta_{0})\neq 0
and
\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(a)u_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right)
.

A.2.
Semiclassical toolbox of exponentials of linear bounded operators

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an Hilbert space. For
A
,
B
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we first introduce the notations

\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{0}(B)=B,\qquad\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{k}(B)=[A,\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{k-1}(B)]\qquad\forall k\geq 1.

(A.3)

For every
k\geq 0
,
\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{k}(B)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. We also define

e^{A}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}.

(A.4)

We have that
e^{A}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is an invertible operator whose inverse is
e^{-A}
.

We have the following basic lemma.

Lemma A.3
.

[
Sun23
, Lemma 5.1]

Let
B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
,
\{G_{h}\}_{h\in(0,1)}
be a family of
h-
dependent, uniformly bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
. The following Taylor expansion holds, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

e^{sG_{h}}Be^{-sG_{h}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{G_{h}}(B)+\frac{1}{(N-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{N-1}e^{sG_{h}}\operatorname{ad}^{N}_{G_{h}}(B)e^{-sG_{h}}{\rm{d}}s,\qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The next result tells us that the wave front set is conserved under the action of
e^{G_{h}}
. Given the definition of the exponential of bounded operators, we shall prove that the transform
e^{G_{h}}v_{h}
does not change the wavefront
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\{G_{h}\}_{h\in(0,1)}
be a family of
h-
dependent, uniformly bounded operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
,
(v_{h})_{h\in(0,1)}
be a family of tempered distributions on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
and
\widetilde{v}_{h}=e^{G_{h}}v_{h}
. Then
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(\widetilde{v}_{h})=\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
for every
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
Sun23
, Proof of Lemma 5.3]
.
It only suffices to show that
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(\widetilde{v}_{h})\subset\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
as the reverse inclusion follows by using that
v_{h}=e^{-G_{h}}\widetilde{v}_{h}
.

Let
\kappa(z,\zeta)
be a symbol supported on a compact set of
T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\setminus\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
and
K_{h}=\mathrm{Op}^{\mathrm{w}}_{h}(\kappa)
, by definition, it suffices to show

K_{h}\widetilde{v}_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right),\mbox{ i.e., }K_{h}e^{G_{h}}v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right).

From the definition of
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
, for any semiclassical operator
L_{h}
with its principal symbol supported away from
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
, we have

L_{h}v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right).

Using the Taylor expansion for the conjugate operator, we write

\displaystyle K_{h}e^{G_{h}}v_{h}

\displaystyle=e^{G_{h}}(e^{-G_{h}}K_{h}e^{G_{h}})v_{h}

\displaystyle=e^{G_{h}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})v_{h}+\frac{e^{G_{h}}}{(m-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{m-1}e^{-sG_{h}}\operatorname{ad}^{m}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})e^{sG_{h}}v_{h}{\rm{d}}s.

Using the standard symbolic calculus,
\operatorname{ad}^{m}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right)
, which implies that

\frac{e^{G_{h}}}{(m-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{m-1}e^{-sG_{h}}\operatorname{ad}^{m}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})e^{sG_{h}}v_{h}{\rm{d}}s=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right).

And for
k\leq m-1
,
\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})
is a semiclassical operator with its principal symbol supported away from
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
due to the definition of
K_{h}
. Hence,
\operatorname{ad}^{k}_{G_{h}}(K_{h})v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right)
. Consequently, we obtain
K_{h}e^{G_{h}}v_{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{2}}\mathopen{}\left(h^{m}\right)
. We conclude
\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(\widetilde{v}_{h})\subset\mathrm{WF}^{m}_{h}(v_{h})
.
∎

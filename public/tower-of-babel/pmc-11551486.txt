The initiative for consensus and standards in pig-to-human xenotransplantation in China
Authors: Dong Niu, Yulong Yin
Published: 2024

Main text

The global organ shortage is critically severe, with many patients at risk of life-threatening conditions due to the scarcity of suitable donor organs. Gene-edited porcine organs for human transplantation have emerged as a promising solution and a focus of scientific research. We urge all relevant parties in China to collaborate in establishing consensus and standards for xenotransplantation using genetically modified pigs to benefit patients worldwide.

The necessity of xenotransplantation

In China, thousands of patients await organ transplants each year, but severe donor shortages often lead to a long wait or even death. This critical supply-demand imbalance necessitates urgent solutions. Xenotransplantation using genetically modified porcine organs could be a breakthrough innovation, providing a stable and sustainable organ source that alleviates the crisis. Gene editing can remove porcine genes causing immune rejection and introduce human genes to improve compatibility and success rates. If successful, xenotransplantation may prolong the lives of recipients and give hope to thousands of patients. In addition, xenotransplantation could not only solve the shortage of organs but also promote biomedical technology development. Reinforcing this research will therefore enable China to narrow the gap in high-tech areas, encourage international cooperation, and promote related industries. All in all, xenotransplantation has been considered as a crucial advancement in transplant medicine, addressing significant challenges in the field.

The current status of xenotransplantation

Pigs are the most appropriate animals for unlimited organ and tissue provision to humans because of their high reproductive rate, low cost of maintenance, and optimal organ sizes. Furthermore, the well-established pig genetic engineering and cloning techniques allow for genetic modification to overcome immune rejection, coagulation disorders, and cross-species transmission risk of porcine endogenous retrovirus.1 In the last 3 years, several clinical trials involving genetically modified porcine organs have been conducted successfully. Among these, a groundbreaking trial was performed in 2021, where a porcine kidney was found to function for 54 h in a human decedent model.2 This was followed by encouraging results in living patients, including the first pig-to-human heart transplantation with 60-day survival in 2022,3 a kidney transplantation with 56-day survival in 2024,4 and the first liver transplantation in 2024.5 These breakthroughs bring pig-to-human xenotransplantation closer to clinical use.

The United States has made significant advances in xenotransplantation,2,3,4 demonstrating its pioneering role and providing hope for global organ transplantation. If China does not catch up quickly in this field, it may fall behind in technological development and miss valuable opportunities. The advancement in xenotransplantation reflects China’s innovative capability in biomedicine, contributes to high-tech sectors, and promotes related industrial development.

Critical need for government policy support

Xenotransplantation requires strong support and guidance from government to address technical, legal, and ethical issues. Several factors must be monitored during xenotransplantation practices, including animal conditions, patient selection, criteria for determining brain death, and others. An impartial ethics committee, under regulatory oversight, must review such procedures to ensure compliance.

It is worth noting that the transplant may be approved by the hospital and/or local authorities, potentially by individuals lacking sufficient expertise. This raises concerns about how the safety and ethical standards of such procedures are maintained, which highlights the necessity for specialized training and the dissemination of knowledge among healthcare professionals and policymakers to ensure a safe and informed practice of xenotransplantation. By establishing rules and policies, the government can advance the development of stringent standards and operational norms, ensuring that all xenotransplantation practices are carried out within a regulated and safe framework. Current clinical trial approval procedures are quite complicated and time-consuming, which, to some extent, delays the clinical application of new technology. To accelerate the translation of scientific research into clinical use, the government should streamline and standardize the process, eliminate administrative barriers, and expedite clinical trials. The government should take the lead in the development of standards, operational norms, and ethical and legal guidelines. Additionally, the government should also provide sufficient funds for basic research and technology development.

Establishment of a compassionate use policy within the legal framework

Although xenotransplantation has not yet developed to the clinical stage, a compassionate use policy, similar to that in the United States, should be applied in China. It provides an urgent treatment option for patients facing life-threatening conditions and a long wait for traditional donation. Through xenotransplantation with genetically modified porcine organs, the patients’ life quality could be improved significantly. Unlike animal studies, clinical trials would provide more valuable data for assessing the safety and efficacy of xenografts, thereby promoting technological improvements. This policy would also improve ethical and legal frameworks and ensure compliance with regulations. For clinical trials under compassionate use, each case should be thoroughly assessed to protect patient rights and safety. The application of such a policy in China would greatly advance the development of xenotransplantation technology.

Promotion of international cooperation

Xenotransplantation is a complex medical technology and, thus, requires broad international cooperation. Currently, different countries have different standards regarding the technology, which necessitates collaboration for achieving unified consensus. International collaboration can strengthen technical exchanges among doctors and researchers, enhance the sharing of research and clinical findings, and promote technological optimization and progress. In addition, the cross-border supply of high-quality xenotransplant organs is also a major issue. International cooperation can improve organ availability and assist recipient matching. Moreover, clinical trials must follow strict regulations and good procedures, and international collaboration can help enhance the success rate of multinational trials. Last, patient follow-up management and monitoring are crucial for maintaining stable organ function and overall health. International collaboration facilitates the sharing of best practices, research findings, and treatment strategies to improve patient survival rates and quality of life. In brief, the success of xenotransplantation relies on international cooperation across various areas, including legal, ethical, technological, supply chain, clinical practice, and patient management. Through collaboration, China will undoubtedly be able to make a great contribution to the development of the global xenotransplantation industry.

Drafting of the consensus and standard

To ensure that the xenotransplantation practice can be performed effectively in China, we propose establishment of a committee composed of leading domestic academicians and scientists to draft “the consensus and standards for pig-to-human xenotransplantation in China.” This consensus will cover ethical guidelines, technical standards, and experimental procedures, offering comprehensive guidance both nationally and globally. The committee’s main tasks are to integrate multidisciplinary research findings and clinical experiences as well as develop technical protocols and clinical guidelines for xenotransplantation practice.

To establish standards for xenotransplantation, the following elements should be included. (1) Donor pig selection. Pigs should be standardized by defining physiological and biochemical parameters together with a body weight close to humans. (2) Animal husbandry and pathogen surveillance. Pigs must be reared under designated pathogen-free conditions to ensure absence of zoonotic pathogens. (3) Genetic modification. Strategies should focus on the knockout of porcine-specific antigens and the addition of human genes to inhibit immune rejection, coagulation, and inflammation. For biological safety, exogenous pathogens and endogenous retroviruses should be eliminated. (4) Recipient selection. Recipients should be chosen based on comprehensive physiological and immunological assessments to identify suitable candidates. (5) Ethical and legal considerations. Key issues include animal welfare, obtaining informed consent from patients or families, and establishing brain death criteria to ensure the legality of the practice. Trial termination should consider the recipient’s condition and ethical factors. These comprehensive measures aim to significantly advance xenotransplantation while upholding ethical and legal standards, laying a firm groundwork for this technology (Figure 1).

Strategies for establishing unified standards for xenotransplantation technology

The standards should cover donor pig generation and husbandry, clinical practices, ethical reviews, and legal regulations.

Our goal is to establish a consensus among scientists for unified standards in xenotransplantation. However, achieving uniformity is challenging, even in advanced countries like the United States, primarily due to commercial interests that lead to fragmented standards. In China, various groups developing genetically modified pigs for both medical and business purposes may face similar issues of fragmentation and lack of standardization. To address this, a multi-party dialog mechanism is essential to create flexible and adaptable standards across areas, including donor pig modification, clinical practices, authority regulations, and ethics review. Different parties will play distinct roles in achieving a unified standard.

We urge governments, research institutions, healthcare facilities, and all relevant parties to actively engage in and support the development of xenotransplantation technology for the benefit of countless patients. This collective effort will not only propel the technology forward within China but also provide valuable insights to the international community. By working together, we aim to achieve significant breakthroughs and make a substantial contribution to global organ transplantation from China.

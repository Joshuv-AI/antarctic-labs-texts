Unraveling the causal relationship and potential mechanisms between osteoarthritis and breast cancer: insights from mendelian randomization and bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Kun Li, Ran Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the effect of osteoarthritis (OA) on the development of breast cancer (BC), and reveal the potential mechanisms underlying the association between them.MethodsA two-step, multivariable Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis was performed, using statistics from genome-wide association studies (GWAS), to determine the effect of OA on BC and explore the role of major depressive disorder (MDD) in mediating it. Furthermore, transcriptomic analysis based on the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases were utilized to establish a prognostic model and explore the underlying mechanisms. Additionally, BC cells and nude mice were used to verify the role of RTN4 in BC.ResultsThe two-sample MR analysis implied a causal relationship between OA and BC at the genetic level, and the mediating MR analysis identified that MDD may play a potential role in mediating it, accounting for approximately 12.20%. Then, we constructed a prognostic model (OA-score) with six genes screened out from datasets and selected RTN4 as the representative gene for validation study. It was demonstrated that high OA-score was an independent risk factor for breast cancer, and patients with low OA-score were more likely to have better OS, higher infiltration level of DC and CD 4 + T cells, and higher expression of some immune checkpoints. Moreover, the knockdown of RTN4 inhibited breast cancer cell proliferation, migration and invasion.ConclusionOur study identified the causal influence of OA on BC mediated by MDD at the genetic level. OA-Score may potentially serve as a new prognostic biomarker for OA related BC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01642-5.Keywords: Mendelian randomization, Osteoarthritis, Breast cancer, Major depressive disorder, Bioinformatics analysis

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is a serious global health concern that affects women worldwide, resulting in high mortality and morbidity rates. Based on data from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the estimated number of new cases of BC in women for 2020 was 2261400, representing 25.84% of all new cases of malignant tumors in women. Among these cases, 68500 resulted in fatalities, accounting for 15.56% of all female cancer deaths [1–3]. The treatment options for BC include surgical excision, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and supportive (palliative) treatment, as well as neoadjuvant therapy and systemic therapy. Despite the availability of these treatment options, the survival rate for patients with BC remains below 30%, with a recurrence rate of 20%-30% [4–6]. Therefore, it is extremely urgent to acquire a comprehensive knowledge of the pathogenesis of BC in order to refine early diagnostic and therapeutic strategies [1, 7, 8].

Epidemiological studies have identified several factors associated with an increased risk of BC, including late marriage, menopause, imbalanced diet, obesity, excessive alcohol consumption, and the use of hormone preparations. Additionally, several studies suggested that older individuals suffering from knee or hip OA are at a higher risk of developing BC. Therefore, OA is also considered as a potential risk factor for BC [9]. Cartilage Oligomeric Matrix Protein (COMP), a serum marker for OA, has been linked to positive histological subtypes and estrogen receptor (ER) status at the time of BC diagnosis. This finding indicates that COMP may be an independent prognostic predictor for BC [10]. However, the causal relationship between OA and BC, and the specific underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Multi-gene analysis results indicated shared genetic variation between OA and MDD, and studies also demonstrated that the pain and functional limitations of OA increased the risk of depression, which suggested that OA may contribute to the development of MDD [11, 12]. On the other hand, depression increases the risk of developing BC. Several shared key pathological genes were identified in the previous studies [13]. Another retrospective cohort study demonstrated that women with pre-diagnostic MDD would experience worse breast cancer outcomes compared to women without pre-diagnostic MDD [14]. And MDD is responsible for the development of estrogen receptor-positive (ER +) BC and estrogen receptor-negative (ER-) BC [15]. Therefore, further investigation is warranted to explore whether MDD is a mediating factor that links OA with BC.

Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) are currently the gold standard for establishing causal relationships. However, ethical constraints and other limiting factors have hindered their application. Observational clinical studies can be influenced to different extents by unmeasured confounding factors and reverse causality. Therefore, further investigation is required to obtain a comprehensive knowledge of the genetic relationship between OA and BC, as existing studies have focused solely on a subset of clinical trial outcomes. Mendelian Randomization (MR), using genetic variations associated with the exposure as instrumental variables (IVs) to determine the causal impact of exposure on outcomes, is a widely used method to infer causal relationships. MR remains unaffected by confounding factors including environmental influences and lifestyle habits, which mitigate reverse causation effects and minimize measurement errors [16–18]. Although single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) may affect outcomes through pathways other than exposure, which is referred to as horizontal pleiotropy, conducting inverse variance weighted (IVW) method [19], MR-Egger [20] and sensitivity analyses can further strengthen the robustness of the causal effects estimated in MR analysis. MR is often preferred over traditional randomized controlled trials and observational research owing to its cost-effectiveness and short study duration. The rapid advancement of genome-wide association study (GWAS) data has established a robust foundation for MR research. Despite multiple investigations, whether the causal relation existed between OA and BC, the potential linkers mediating the process, and the underlying mechanisms were all unclear, which need to be comprehensively explored.

For the first time, we conducted a two-step multivariate MR analysis using GWAS data to investigate the effects of MDD-mediated OA on BC pathogenesis at the genetic level. Common pathogenic genes between OA and BC were identified by analyzing the shared differentially expressed genes (DEGs), and the corresponding prognostic markers and biological mechanisms were established.

Methods

Methods for mendelian randomization analysis

Data source

The GWAS data for OA (ukb-b-14486), MDD (ieu-b-102), and BC (ebi-a-GCST004988) were acquired from the IEU Open GWAS project. The website will be accessible starting from October 10, 2023. The dataset primarily consisted of Europeans, with summarized information provided in Supplementary Table 1. These relevant data were sourced from published GWAS databases, and informed consent was acquired from participants in the original studies; hence, ethical approval was not required for this aspect of our investigation.

Selection of SNPs

To obtain strong IVs, significant SNPs were selected from the GWAS data on OA using a screening criterion of P < 5 × 10–8. For each SNP, it is regarded as having adequate potency for the chosen IV when the F value exceeds 10. The formula for computing the F value is F = beta^2/se^2. The linkage disequilibrium(LD) coefficient r2 was set to be 0.001 and a LD region width of 10000 kb were used to ensure that each selected SNP is independent and eliminates the influence of pleiotropy resulting from LD. SNPs associated with confounders and outcomes were excluded using Pheno Scanner to alleviate the influence of confounding factors on the conclusions. The screened SNPs were extracted from the GWAS data on BC with a minimum r2 > 0.8 as threshold. Missing SNPs were replaced with highly related ones and SNPs without substitution sites were eliminated. Finally, information from both datasets was summarized, and SNPs directly related to BC (P < 5 × 10–8) were excluded, ascertaining that the instrumental variables bear no direct correlation with the outcome and merely impact the outcome through exposure..

MR analysis

We utilized two-sample MR analysis to explore the causal relationship between OA and BC. In the MR analysis, OA was defined as exposure, BC was defined as outcome, and SNPs were instrumental variables. We used the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method [19], MR-Egger [20], the weighted median method [21], simple mode, and weighted mode method for robust analysis of causality. IVW was depicted as the primary result, with other MR estimation methodologies functioning as supplementary.

In addition, a two-step MR analysis was used to identify the mediating effect of MDD on the relationship between OA and BC. The first step involved using SNPs associated with OA to estimate their causal effects on MDD. In the second step, SNPs related to MDD were used to evaluate their causal effects on BC. Subsequently, a multivariate MR analysis was performed to evaluate the direct impact of OA on BC, considering the influence of MDD. Finally, previous studies indicate a possible connection between OA and MDD, which may play a role in BC development and require further exploration. Here, we hypothesize that MDD plays potential role in this process, β1 represented the MR effect of OA on MDD, β2 denoted the multivariable MR effect of MDD on BC, β3 representing the direct effect of OA on BC. The potential mediating impact was estimated using β1 × β2. To quantify the potential proportion of mediating results (E%), we utilized the following formula, that is, the indirect impact [22].

The elimination of horizontal pleiotropy and sensitivity analysis

SNPs responsible for heterogeneity were identified by Cochran's Q test and I2 (I-squared) on the basis of IVW estimation. Cochran's Q is utilized to quantify the heterogeneity of SNPs. When P < 0.05, the finding implies heterogeneity. I2 denotes the proportion of the total variance attributed to heterogeneity. The scope of I2 ranges from 0 to 100%, and if I2 surpasses 50%, a considerable degree of heterogeneity prevails. We utilized the following formula to conducting a leave-one-out analysis on SNPs, sequentially eliminating each SNP and re-analyzing with the remaining SNPs to observe the effect of removing each individual SNP on the analysis outcomes [23].

MR results were comprehensively evaluated using leave-one-out analysis and Cochran’s Q test [24]. Outlier values were assessed with leave-one-out analysis. SNPs responsible for heterogeneity were identified by Cochran's Q test on the basis of IVW and MR-Egger estimation.

Methods for bioinformatics analysis

Data acquisition

TCGA [25] public database contains a total of 1097 BC tumor tissue samples that were screened based on the following exclusion criteria: first diagnosis and initial treatment was not for BC, no information available regarding survival status and time, survival time less than 1 month, male, incomplete clinical and RNA-seq data. Additionally, 110 normal tissue samples were obtained from TCGA database. For further research, we incorporated an additional set of microarray expression and clinical data from GSE12021 (10 OA and 9 controls) retrieved from GEO database [26].

Identification of DEGs

The “limma” R package [27] was used to acquire the DEGs that overlapped in OA and BC patients, with the threshold set at |log2FC|> 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.01. Next, these overlapping DEGs were utilized to construct co-expression networks using Weighted Gene Co-Expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) [28]. Using hierarchical cluster analysis, highly related genes were grouped into different modules. Cox regression model was used to select the overall survival (OS) related genes.

Construction and verification of the prognostic model

The OS related intersection genes were determined by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression using the “glmnet” R package [29]. The OA-score of each sample in both the training and testing cohorts were calculated based on Cox regression coefficients and gene expression levels. A set of DEGs related to the prognosis of BC was identified using regression coefficient shrinkage. An OA-related prognostic model (OA-score) was constructed using six genes (RTN4, ARAP3, SGPP1, RNASE1, NFIL3, and GDF10). The OA-score was calculated as:∑x=1ncoefficientx∗expressionx. The data were divided into the training cohort and the testing cohort at a 6:4 ratio randomly, guaranting that the training set encompasses a sufficient quantity of samples for the purpose of training the model, whilst the validation set comprises an adequate number of samples to evaluate the performance and generalizability of the model. Using the ‘survminer’ R package, we established the optimal cutoff value for high- and low-risk groups with Kaplan–Meier analysis. [30]. Multivariable Cox hazard model was used to analyze the discrimination of the OA-score variables.

Nomogram based on the OA-score

The “rms” R package was used to create a nomogram that predicts survival based on the results of multivariate Cox analysis. [31]. The aim was to evaluate the predictive ability of 3-, 5-, and 10 year OS probabilities. The prognostic performance of the OA-score was assessed using time-receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with the area under the curve (AUC).

Immune cell infiltration analysis

The gene expression matrix of BC samples was uploaded to the ImmuCellAI database. Immune cell infiltration was analyzed for each sample [32]. Comparing immune cell infiltration levels and differential expression of immune genes between high- and low-risk groups using Wilcoxon rank-sum test [33].

Drug sensitivity analysis, mutation analysis and GSVA enrichment analysis

The IC50 values of 198 drugs were obtained from the GDSC database. The R package “oncoPredict” was utilized to forecast the IC50 of each sample based on RNA-seq data [34]. Subsequently, Wilcoxon analysis was conducted to evaluate the disparity in drug IC50 between the high- and low-risk groups.The mutation and pathological data of TCGA-BRCA patients included in our study were downloaded from TCGA database. Next, the ‘maftools’ package [35] in the R software was used to conduct a comprehensive mutation analysis and generate a visualization of the data.

Mutation analysis

The expression matrix of patients with BC in TCGA database were downloaded. Pathway enrichment scores were calculated for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway gene sets and Hallmark gene sets in each sample using GSVA. High and low-risk groups were compared using “limma” R package. Top 50 pathways were visualized with a |t| threshold of 1.

Methods for cell and animal experiments

Human tissue collection

Tumors and the adjacent normal tissue were collected from BC patients who underwent surgery at Xiangya hospital, Central South University. All the patients were informed and agreed on the use of these clinical materials for research purposes. This study was approved by the Institute Research Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (201803547) and followed the guidelines of the Helsinki Declaration.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Five µm sections were cut from human BC tissue and OA knee cartilage. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated and washed. Antigen retrieval was first performed with sodium citrate buffer in 70 °C for 30 min. The streptavidin–biotin system (ZSGB-BIO, CN) was used following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. After blocking with 10% normal goat serum for 15 min at room temperature, the sections were incubated with with RTN4 primary antibody(1:400, Proteintech) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, the sections were washed with PBS and incubated with a secondary biotinylated goat anti-rabbit antibody for 15 min followed by streptavidin for 15 min. All sections were then stained with DAB staining. Counterstains were done with hematoxylin.

Cell lines and cell cultures

BC cell line, MCF-7 cells was cultured in fetal bovine serum supplemented DMEM (10% FBS + DMEM) media in cell incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2). MDA-MB-231 and 4T1 cells were cultured in fetal bovine serum supplemented RPMI1640 (10% FBS + RPMI1640) media in cell incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2).

Lentiviral transfection and RTN4 shRNA gene silencing

The RNT4 shRNA(GCTATATCTGAGGAGTTGGTT and GCTGCTTTCATTGACAGTATT), contains the puromycin resistance gene, and the corresponding controls were transfected into MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells. Transfected cells were cultured with DMEM or RPMI 1640 containing puromycin(2 μg/ml) for 14 days. Then, we used the transfected cells to conduct subsequent experiments.

Western blotting

Protein samples mixed with 5 × loading buffer were boiled at 100 °C for 5 min. Then, cooled down the samples and subjected them into sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel, electrophoresed and transferred the sample to PVDF membrane. Then, blocked the membrane with 5% skimmed milk for 1 h, and then incubated the membrane with primary antibodies, anti‐RNT4 (1:1000, Proteintech), anti‐GAPDH (1:1000, BioWord) at 4 °C overnight. Washed the membrane, incubated it into secondary antibodies, and washed it again 3 times. At last, detected the signals with ChemiDoc (Bio‐Rad).

Cell proliferation assay

Inoculated cells (2 × 103) into 96‐well plates one night before. Add 10 μl MTT (3‐ (4,5)‐dimethylthiahiazo (‐z‐y1)‐3,5‐di‐phenytetrazoliumromide) solution into each well and incubated in the cell incubator, 4 h later add 100 μl DMSO into each well after MTT solution was removed. The absorbance of each well was measured at 570 and 630 nm with a Spectra Max M2 Microplate Reader (Molecular Devices).

Colony formation assay

Inoculated cells (500 / well) in six-well plate and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 2–3 weeks. After cell colonies (about 50 cells) formed, washed each well twice using phosphate buffered saline (PBS). The plate was stained with 0.5% crystal violet (Beyotime, C0121) at 37 °C for 20 min after fixation with 4% Paraformaldehyde Fix Solution (Beyotime, P0099) for 20 min, and photographed after drying.

Cell wound healing assay

Inoculated cells (5 × 105 / well) into a 12‐well plate. Scratched the plate using 10 μl pipettes after cell adherence, washed the plate with PBS buffer, and then cultured cells in media without serum. Observed wound closure at 0 and 48 h. Image J (1.4.3.67) was used to evaluate cell migration ability after the images were taken in microscopic fields three times randomly (× horizon200) at both time points.

Transwell invasion assay

Suspended cells (5 × 104/ well) were placed in the upper chamber of 8‐μm‐pore Transwells (BD Biosciences) precoated with Matrigel, and the lower chamber was filled with media with 20% FBS to attract cells. Culture cells in cell incubator for 48 h. The 8 μm hole was stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 30 min after fixation (4% paraformaldehyde), and three microscopic fields (× horizon 200) were selected randomly under the microscope and then count cells.

Xenograft mouse model

Ten nude male mice of 4 weeks old were divided into 2 groups randomly. One group was injected with RTN4-KD-4T1 cells (1 × 106) on the back under skin, and the other group were injected with control cells. All the mice were monitored, and tumor size was measured every 2 days. The formula V = (Length × Width2)/2 was used to calculate tumor volume. All the procedures carried out on animals were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Hunan Yaoda Testing Technology Co., Ltd for Animal Experiment (YH20231230001). The experiments were also performed in accordance with regulations of laboratory animal welfare and ethics.

Statistical analysis

The data analyses in the present study were conducted using R software (Version 4.1.0). Two-step MR was used for all MR analyses. Odds ratios (ORs) and hazard ratios (HRs) were computed along with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The Student’s t‐test was used to analyse the two groups of data. The statistical significance levels in this study are as follows: * < 0.05, ** < 0.01, *** < 0.001 and**** < 0.0001. The experiments were repeated three times.

Results

MR analysis identified the causal relationship between OA and BC mediated by MDD at the genetic level

The study flowchart of the present MR analysis was shown in Fig. 1.

Schematic summary of the study

Four SNPs associated with OA and BC were selected after extensive screening. Basic information on these SNPs is presented in Supplementary Table 2. The F-statistics for each SNP ranged from 29.99 to 54.74 (mean 40.38), indicating a causal relationship with BC and minimal susceptibility to weak IV bias. Using a two-sample MR analysis, we found a positive association between OA and BC. IVW analysis revealed that OA was a risk factor for BC (OR = 27.722, 95%CI 5.098–150.734, P < 0.001).Weighted median analysis consistently showed a significant result (OR = 13.238, 95%CI 1.502–116.665, P = 0.020 < 0.05) (Fig. 2a), suggesting that OA was a risk factor for the development of BC.

MR analysis to estimate the causal effect. a MR analysis to estimate the effect of OA on BC. b MR analysis to estimate the effect of OA on MDD. c MR analysis to estimate the effect of MDD on BC

Subsequently, to explore the mechanism underlying the causal effect of OA on BC, we performed a two-step MR analysis with MDD as mediator variable. Three SNPs associated with OA and MDD were integrated (Supplementary Table 3), with an F-value range of 44.26–78.27, to estimate the influence of OA on MDD (OA as exposure and MDD as outcome). 42 SNPs were included in the MDD and BC related SNP dataset (Supplementary Table 4), with F-values ranging from 30.25 to 63.31, to estimate the effect of MDD on BC(MDD as exposure and BC as outcome).

IVW analysis revealed a causal effect of OA on MDD (OR = 4.613,95%CI 1.345–15.825, P = 0.015) (Fig. 2b), and MDD also had a significant causal effect on the development of BC, at the genetic level (OR = 1.105, 95%CI 1.027–1.189, P = 0.008) (Fig. 2c). We estimated the indirect effect of OA on BC, mediated by MDD (β = 2.3467). The mediating effect of MDD was 0.2824, which accounted for approximately 12.20% of the total effect (Table 1).

In the trio of Mendelian randomization analyses, the range of Cochran’s Q p-values was 0.298–0.790, all of which exceeded 0.05. Moreover, the range of I2 was 0–17% < 50%, signifying that the results were minimally influenced by heterogeneity. In the level-specificity test of the MR-egger intercept term, the range of p-values was 0.327–0.789, all of which surpassed 0.05, suggesting that the results were minimally affected by level-specificity. In the leave-one-out analysis, no SNP was identified to have a significant impact on the direction of the OR. (Supplementary Figs. 1–3, Supplementary Table 5). In summary, the results of MR analyses showed that, at the genetic level, OA had a significant, positive causal effect on BC, and MDD played a pivotal role in mediating the process.

Prognostic gene screening and construction of the prognostic model

Having been proved that OA had a causal effect on the development of BC at the genetic level, we further investigated the association between OA and the prognosis of patients with BC. A total of 18364 and 687 DEGs were identified in TCGA-BRCA and GSE12021 datasets respectively (Fig. 3a, b), among which 437 DEGs were common (Fig. 3c). Secondly 102 highly corelated genes were further selected and clustered using WGCNA module (Fig. 3d). Then, 12 genes associated with prognosis were further selected through Cox regression analysis (Fig. 3e). Finally, 6 genes involved in OA-score were selected using LASSO regression analysis, which are RTN4, ARAP3, SGPP1, RNASE1, NFIL3, and GDF10 (Fig. 3f). The following formula was used to calculate the risk score: OA-Score = [0.1622 × Expression value of RTN4] + [0.081 × Expression value of ARAP3] + [(− 0.1496) × Expression value of SGPP1] + [0.0185 × Expression value of RNASE1] + [0.0101 × Expression value of NFIL3] + [0.1431 × Expression value of GDF10]. Based on the OA-score, patients with BC were divided into two groups (cutoff 1.0664), the high-risk group and the low-risk group in both the training and testing cohorts (Supplementary Table 6).

To validate the predictive value of the prognostic model, we also performed Kaplan–Meier analysis and univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. Results of the Kaplan–Meier analysis for the training cohort showed that the high-risk group had a significant association with worse OS when compared to the low-risk group. The median survival time for the high-risk group was 98.83 months, whereas it was 148.53 months for patients in the low-risk group (Fig. 4a, b). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to determine whether high OA-score and other clinical parameters of patients with BC were independent risk factors for OS. Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that a high OA-score, together with age, TNM stage, and positive/close margin status, were independent risk factors for OS in patients with BC (HR = 2.247, 95%CI 1.413–3.575, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4c). Multivariate Cox regression analysis also revealed that a high OA-score was an independent risk factor for patients with BC after multivariate adjustment (HR = 1.756, 95%CI 1.054–2.926, P = 0.031) (Fig. 4d). To further evaluate the predictive ability of the prognostic model, we constructed a nomogram model by combining OA-score, N stage, margin status, and chemotherapy. The AUC of the 3-, 5-, and 10 year nomograms were 0.758, 0.784, and 0.704, respectively, in the training cohort (Fig. 4e, f).

Kaplan–Meier analysis, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, and nomogram model were also performed in the testing cohort, and the results coincided well with those observed in the training cohort (Supplementary Fig. 4). These findings suggested that the prognostic model may serve as a sensitive biomarker for predicting the prognosis of patients with BC.

Immune cell infiltration analysis and the expression level of immune-associated genes

To further explore the potential mechanism underlying the association between OA and the prognosis of BC patients, we administrated more transcriptomic analysis. ImmuCellAI was used to analyze immune cell infiltration to study the relationship between the OA-score and tumor immune microenvironment. The results revealed that the infiltration levels of dendritic cells (DC), macrophage, CD 4 + T cells, and gamma delta cells were higher in the low-risk group, whereas B cell, monocyte, and NKT cell infiltration were higher in the high-risk group (Fig. 5a).Furthermore, we analyzed the differences in the expression of immune-associated genes between the high- and low-risk groups. Classical immune checkpoints, such as CTLA-4, LAG-3, CD86, and TIGIT, were highly expressed in the low-risk group. Additionally, several immune-associated genes, including HAVCR2, TNFSF4, TNFSF18, TNFRSF 8, TNFRSF9, IDO1, and ICOS, were highly expressed in the low-risk group (Fig. 5b).

Drug sensitivity analysis

The somatic mutation data was shown in Supplementary Fig. 5a, and the top 15 genes with the highest mutation rates are listed. We noticed that the mutation rate of TP53 and CDH1 in the low-risk group exhibited a higher level than that of the high-risk group (37% vs 25%, 25% vs 11%). Additionally, we compared drug sensitivity between the two groups. The drugs exhibiting sensitivity differences between the two groups are enumerated in Supplementary Fig. 5b. Most notably, among these drugs, patients with low OA-score were most sensitive to insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor inhibitors, IGF1R_3801 and Linsitinib.

GSVA enrichment analysis and mutation analysis

We performed GSVA on the data from TCGA (928 patients with BC) to investigate the differences in gene enrichment between the high- and low-risk groups. The low-risk group showed significant enrichment in the P53 signaling pathway, calcium signaling pathway, AB transporters, and other pathways in the KEGG database. Additionally, the high-risk group showed significant enrichment in riboflavin metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and other pathways (Supplementary Fig. 6a). In the hallmark gene set, P53_AKT_MTOR signaling, NOTCH signaling, and other pathways were enriched in the low-risk group, whereas HEME metabolism and protein secretion pathways were enriched in the high-risk group (Supplementary Fig. 6b).

RTN4 knockdown inhibits BC proliferation, migration and invasion

Since RTN4 plays the most important part in the prognostic model (OA-score), and previous studies had reported its tumor promoting role in multi cancer types, RTN4 was subsequently selected as a key gene for further exploration. We detected the expression level of RTN4 in OA cartilage, BC tissue and the adjacent normal tissue. The IHC results showed that RTN4 expression significantly upregulated in lesioned OA cartilage and BC tissue (Fig. 6a, b). Then we constructed RTN4 knockdown MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells using lentivirus infection to further verify the role of RTN4 in BC. We confirmed the effectiveness of infection using western blotting (Fig. 7a). Subsequently, MTT assay, colony formation assay, cell wound healing assay and transwell invasion assay were used to evaluate the impact of RTN4 on the proliferation, migration and invasion ability of BC cells. The results of MTT and colony formation assays showed that RTN4 knockdown significantly inhibited the proliferation rate of MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 7b, c). Additionally, cell wound healing assay and transwell invasion assay demonstrated that the migration and invasion ability was obviously decreased when RTN4 was knockdown in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells (Fig. 7d, e).

To further investigate the tumorigenic ability of RTN4 in vivo, we inoculated RTN4-KD 4T1 cells on the back of nude mice (Fig. 6c, d). The results revealed that tumors in RTN4-KD group were smaller in size and weight than those in the control group (Fig. 6e, f), which indicated that RTN4 knockdown dramatically inhibited BC xenograft tumor development.

Discussion

Emerging evidence suggests that OA is a potential risk factor for BC, and that MDD may play a role in mediating this process. However, the existence of a causal relationship between them remains unclear. In this study, we hypothesized that MDD might facilitate the onset of OA-induced BC, and performed a two-step MR analysis to investigate the mediating pathway from OA to BC. Our findings showed that, at the genetic level, OA has a causal effect on BC, and MDD has a significant mediating effect on OA-induced BC. To further investigate the relationship and potential mechanisms between OA and the prognosis of patients with BC, we analyzed the transcriptome data and constructed a prognostic model (OA-score) that included six OA- and BC-related genes. We demonstrated that patients with high OA-score had worse OS, lower expression levels of certain classical immune checkpoint proteins, and were less sensitive to some anti-cancer drugs, such as IGIF-1 inhibitors. Furthermore, OA-score was also closely related with immune cell infiltration disorders.

Our MD analysis revealed that, at the genetic level, OA has a causal effect on MDD and that MDD mediates the process from OA to BC, at the genetic level. Much evidence revealed that OA influence the risk of developing BC [10, 36], and biological agents targeting pro-inflammatory cytokines in osteoimmunological disorders were reported to have the ability to decrease the proliferation of breast cancer cells in vitro [37]. MDD is a clinically and physiologically heterogeneous disorder resulting from the interplay between sensitive genes and detrimental environmental conditions, such as chronic stress. Numerous studies have identified evidence of a bidirectional association between OA and depression, whereby chronic pain and dysfunction resulting from OA may increase the risk of depression. Depression can affect pain perception, coping mechanisms, and quality of life in patients with OA [38–40]. In addition, there are numerous connections between depression and BC, as approximately 10–25% of patients with BC develop depressive disorders. A study published in 2020 revealed that depression significantly contributed to the recurrence and metastasis of BC [41]. The findings indicated that depression was correlated with a 24% increased risk of BC recurrence, 30% increased risk of overall mortality, and 29% increased risk of cancer-related death. Another observational analysis suggested an increased risk of BC in patients with depression (HR = 1.10, 95%CIs = 0.95–1.26) [16].

To further investigate the relationship between OA and the prognosis of BC patients, we identified the shared DEGs in patients with OA and BC using transcriptome sequencing data to further explore the association between OA and BC at the genetic level. We have developed a prognostic model, OA-score, with six genes(RTN4, ARAP3, SGPP1, RNASE1, NFIL3, and GDF10) associated with overall survival to predict the prognosis of BC patients suffering from OA.

We have further evaluated the efficiency of the prognostic model by conducting a ROC analysis in both the training and testing cohorts. The current standard for a good predictive model is considered to be an AUC greater than 0.7.We compared the predictive accuracy of our prognostic model with some traditional prognostic scoring systems in the previous studies, which used clinical data to construct eight models, the AUC for 2-,5-,8 year prognosis is around 0.62–0.76. And there's another study that used immune-related gene to construct prognostic model. The AUCs for 1, 5, and 10 years were 0.635, 0.752, and 0.753, respectively. In our research, we utilized the OA-score to construct a prognostic model. The AUCs for the 3-, 5-, and 10 year nomograms were 0.758, 0.784, and 0.704, respectively, which surpass the majority of existing models. By comparing the predictive accuracy of OA-score with some traditional prognostic scoring systems in the previous studies, we found that the AUC value of our model is better than that of the most traditional prognostic scoring systems, which means that our prognostic model has a good predictive ability. The OA-score is capable of offering more individualized treatment regimens for patients in light of their specific conditions, tumor traits, and individual variances. It also serves as a tool for communication between doctors and patients, facilitating patients' better comprehension of their own conditions and treatment plans, particularly for those concurrently afflicted with OA and BC. Moreover, through the prognosis model, patients can be classified into different risk groups, enabling doctors to adopt distinct monitoring and management strategies for patients in different risk strata. Finally, the continuous refinement of the prognosis model can provide scientific grounds for the formulation of public health policies, such as determining which population groups should undergo screening and how health resources should be allocated.

We also found that OA-score was closely associated with the infiltration of immune cells and immune related gene expression. These findings suggested potential remodeling of the tumor microenvironment (TME) [42]. TME is a highly intricate structure comprised of tumor cells, immune and inflammatory cells, fibroblasts, interstitial tissue, micro vessels, as well as cytokines and chemokines [43, 44], serving as the foundation for enhancing or modulating the development and behavior of tumors. Among these components, the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) forms the basis for interactions between tumor cells and immune cells, playing a pivotal role in BC progression and exerting significant influence on immunotherapy outcomes [45]. Therefore, it is imperative to get a comprehensive knowledge of immune infiltration status to guide personalized immunotherapy strategies in cancer patients.

The immune cell infiltration analysis in our study revealed that patients in the low-risk group exhibited higher infiltration levels of DC, macrophage, CD 4 + T cells, and gamma delta cells. Among them, DC, specialized in antigen-presenting and activating adaptive T cell immunity during homeostasis and in disrupted environments, showed the strongest correlation with low OA-score. CD 4 + T cells, as the most important regulator in adaptive immunity, was also significantly associated with OA-score. A study revealed that a specific subset of immunomodulatory DCs can establish the tumor immune microenvironment that is responsive to immune checkpoint inhibitors, thereby enhancing the clinical efficacy of anti-PD-(L)1 immunotherapy in triple-negative BC [46]. Edward Seung et al. found that the presence of CD 4 + T cells significantly impedes cell cycle progression and proliferation in BC, while concurrently enhancing pro-inflammatory signaling pathways [47]. A similar observation was made by Olga S Blomberg et al., indicating that CD4 + T cells collaborate with eosinophils to augment the response to immune checkpoint blockade in BC [48]. Monocyte, which was found highly infiltrated in the high-risk group in our study, is a group of heterogeneous mononuclear phagocytes that perform innate immune functions in circulating peripheral blood during inflammation. The prevailing consensus in numerous studies suggests that monocytes play a pivotal role in initiating local inflammatory responses through the production of pro-inflammatory factors, thereby facilitating tumor growth and metastasis to some extent [49, 50]. Pengling Jiang et al. found the promotion of bone metastasis in BC is facilitated by CD137 by enhancing the migration and osteoclast differentiation of monocytes [51]. Based on the previous studies and our findings, the results of immune cell infiltration analysis in our study indicated that TME in the low-risk group has more effective immune surveillance and response capabilities. Conversely, the scarcity of DC, CD 4 + T cells and the abundance of monocyte in the high-risk group may suggest an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Through GSVA enrichment analysis, we found that P53 signaling, PI3K_AKT_MTOR signaling, and NOTCH signaling, were also enriched in the low-risk group. These classical tumor related signaling pathways also played pivotal roles in the differentiation and activation of DC and CD 4 + T cells [52–54], which may also provide sights on the results of the immune cell infiltration analysis.

Additionally, classical immune checkpoints, such as CTLA-4, LAG-3, CD86, and TIGIT, were highly expressed in the low-risk group. It is well known that CTLA-4 has been targeted for immunotherapy alone or in combination with PD-1, and highly successful in treating a wide variety of malignancies [55, 56]. The expression of LAG3 limits the size of the memory pool and the expansion of activated T cells, and has become the third immune checkpoint inhibitor approved by FDA, relatlimab (anti-LAG3), for the treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma together with nivolumab [57]. These findings suggested that patients with low OA-scores may have a higher likelihood of activating adaptive immune responses and experiencing greater benefits from immunotherapy, which indicated a better prognosis in these patients.

Furthermore, patients with low OA-scores were more sensitive to IGF1R inhibitors, such as IGF1R_3801 and linsitinib. Reports have suggested that IGF1R inhibitors could potentially induce autophagic flux, which may have implications for cancer treatment [58]. Qi Wu et al. analyzed 65,000 distinct compounds through a phenotypic discovery platform for autophagy induction and got the same conclusion as us [58]. Another study found that a combination of PI3K and IGF1R inhibitors may be beneficial for triple-negative BC [59].All the evidence above suggests that patients in the low-risk group may have a better prognosis than those in the high-risk group, which aligns with our prediction.

RTN4 is involved in carcinogenesis and represents a molecular candidate that could serve as a reliable prognostic biomarker for patients. Upregulation of the RTN4 has been linked to poor prognosis in multiple cancers. In datasets from patients with lung, breast, cervical, kidney, and ovarian cancers, the overall survival rate of the high RTN4 expression group was significantly lower than that of the low RTN4 expression group [60]. Lower RTN4 expression was associated with significantly better survival in hepatocellular carcinoma [61] and colorectal cancer [62] patients. Mechanistically, studies showed that RTN4 influences the proliferation of tumor cells by regulating lipid metabolic balance, AKT signaling and cytoskeleton regulation [63]. Downregulation of RTN4 results in a decline in the synthesis of sphingosine, exerts an influence on the membrane localization of AKT, markedly reduces the phosphorylation level of AKT, while exerting no effect on upstream kinases of AKT, and this effect is contingent upon sphingolipids.The attenuation of RTN4 restrains the proliferation of cancer cells and elevates their susceptibility to paclitaxel, suggesting that the inhibition of RTN4 might be integrated with microtubule-targeted chemotherapeutic agents to augment therapeutic efficacy [63].CircRTN4 assumes a critical role in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. It facilitates the advancement of pancreatic cancer via the CircRNA-miRNA-lncRNA pathway and by stabilizing the epithelial-mesenchymal transition protein [64]. In this study, we found that RTN4 is markedly upregulated in OA and BC tissues, compared with that in the normal tissues. Moreover, in line with previous studies, we observed the inhibition of biological function with RTN4 knockdown in vitro, since silencing of RTN4 significantly inhibited cell proliferation rate, migration and invasion ability. All these findings strongly suggest that the expression of RTN4 is closely related to patients survival and imply the potential role of RTN4 in cancer treatment.

Limitations

Despite extensive investigation of the relationship between OA, BC, and MDD, the present study had several limitations. MR analysis has inherent limitations. MR, with independent samples, weak instrument bias can result in bias towards the null and underestimation in MR analyses. F statistics of all SNPs in our study were greater than 10, which means that our analysis is less prone to measurement error weak instrument bias. Moreover, the heterogeneity and pleiotropy of instrumental variables could influence the results of MR analysis. However, by employing Cochran’s Q test and assessing the MR-Egger intercept term, we demonstrated that the findings of our study are minimally influenced by these factors, thereby confirming the robustness of our MR results.

Regarding the external validity of MR results, the analysis was conducted exclusively on the European population. It is reported that there are similarities in the genetic structure of complex traits among European populations and others, but also marked differences. For example, some SNPs associated with traits in European populations can also be replicated in other populations, suggesting that there are common genetic components of complex traits across different continental populations. However, due to differences in genetic variant frequencies and linkage disequilibrium patterns, some variants that are significantly associated with a trait in one population may not be easily detectable in other populations [65, 66]. Whether the causal effect existed in other populations still needs further exploration and validation. People from different regions and ethnic groups should be included in further research to address this concern. Secondly, the OA GWAS dataset contained a relatively small number of cases. Larger GWAS datasets may be useful for future validation purposes. Third, the Mendelian randomization (MR) model based on aggregated data assumes a linear relationship between exposure and outcome. However, non-linear associations may exist between these variables. Therefore, further research is warranted to explore and apply MR methods that consider non-linear relationships in disease outcomes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study successfully identified a causal association between OA and BC at the genetic level, suggesting that OA may influence BC through MDD. With further validation in larger and independent cohorts, the OA-score may potentially emerge as a prognostic biomarker for future BC research and patient management.

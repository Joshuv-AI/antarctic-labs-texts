Counting Lattices with Local Hecke Series
Gautami Bhowmik, Masao Tsuzuki

Abstract
. We count the maximal lattices over p p -adic fields and the rational number field. For this, we use the theory of Hecke series for a reductive group over nonarchimedean local fields, which was developed by Andrianov and Hina-Sugano. By treating the Euler factors of the counting Dirichlet series for lattices, we obtain zeta functions of classical groups, which were earlier studied with p p -adic cone integrals. When our counting series equals the existing zeta functions of groups, we recover the known results in a simple way. Further we obtain some new zeta functions for non-split even orthogonal and odd orthogonal groups.

Introduction

In this paper we count
lattices over the
p
-adic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and over the rational number field
{\mathbb{Q}}
, a topic that fits in a broad family of the
enumeration of the finite-index subgroups of a finitely generated group. We start with the
{\rm GL}
-case, a classical example, which can be seen as a prototype of our study. A very simple problem would be to count the number of subgroups
L
of the free abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
n
a natural number, with index
m\,(\geqslant 1)
, i.e., to understand the generating series of the arithmetic function

\nu(m):=\#\{L\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\mid[{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:L]=m\}.

Definition 1.1
.

The lattice counting series of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is defined by

(1.1)

\zeta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(s):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu(m)}{m^{s}}=\sum_{L\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}[{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:L]^{-s}.

We easily see that the series
\zeta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(s)
is absolutely convergent on
{\rm Re}(s)>n
and can be explicitly written as the product of shifts of the Riemann zeta function

(1.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(s)=\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)\cdots\zeta(s-n+1),\quad{\rm Re}(s)>n,

a folkloric result, see for example

[
31
]
. This expression yields a meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}(s)
to

{\mathbb{C}}
, with the residue

\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(2)
at its right-most pole
s=n
, which gives an elementary
asymptotic result

\sum_{m<X}\nu_{L}(m)=\big(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\zeta(j)\big)\,X^{n}+\mathcal{O}(X^{n-1}\log X),\quad X\rightarrow\infty

for the number of sublattices of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with index at most
X
.

The lattice counting function (
1.1
) introduced above can
be seen as a special case of a more general subgroup counting function for groups, which we recall now:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and
{\mathfrak{h}}
a family of its finite-index subgroups. The subgroup growth zeta function of
\Gamma
is defined by

\zeta_{\Gamma}(s):=\sum_{H\in{\mathfrak{h}}}\frac{1}{[\Gamma:H]^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\Gamma}^{\mathfrak{h}}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\nu_{\Gamma}^{\mathfrak{h}}(n)
denotes the number of subgroups in
\mathfrak{h}
of index
n
in
\Gamma
.

This includes the following well-known examples of the zeta functions for the lattice counting with additional structures:

(i)

the Dedekind zeta-function, where
\Gamma
is the maximal order of a number field and
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the set of all invertible ideals of
\Gamma
.

(ii)

the zeta-function of orders, where
\Gamma
is the maximal order of a number field and

is the set of all orders (
[
22

[

In a by-now classical work, Grunewald, Segal and Smith

Under certain nice properties of

for
the pro

In this paper we do not exploit the subgroup growth zeta function in

Hecke series

The local Hecke series was originally defined as a multi-variable formal

3.3.

Let

-vector space and

for each

(V_{p},Q_{p})

Let

being any

-basis of

is called the discriminant of

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

with respect to
\beta_{p}

The next lemma shows that

Lemma 3.7

For any
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{L}}_{V}

\Lambda\subset L

\mu(L)=\mu(\Lambda)

Let

. Then a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-lattice

maximal among all lattices with the same norm
\mu(L)
. A lattice
L

of

\beta

Let
{\mathfrak{L}}_{\beta}^{\rm max}
denote the set of all the maximal lattices.
Fix

Theorem 4.1
.

The Euler product

E_{\ell}(s):=\prod_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}}W_{\ell}^{(-1)}(p,p^{-\ell s})

converges absolutely on
{\rm Re}(s)>\sigma
with some
\sigma>0
. We have

\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)=\prod_{r=0}^{\ell}\zeta\left(\tfrac{ns}{2}+\tfrac{r(r-2\ell-1)}{2}\right)\times E_{\ell}(s),\quad{\rm Re}(s)>\sigma.

The right-most pole of
\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)\,({\rm Re}(s)>\sigma)
is

, which is simple. We have

{\rm Res}_{s=s_{0}}\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)=\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}\zeta\left(\tfrac{r^{2}+r+2}{2}\right)\times\prod_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}}W_{\ell}^{(-1)}(p,p^{-\frac{\ell^{2}+\ell+2}{2}})>0.

The function

coincides with the group-zeta

5.
Remarks and further problems

For a general quadratic space
(V,\beta)
, for almost all primes
p
, the form
\beta
is represented over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
by the matrix of type
{\rm D}_{\ell}
or of type
{\rm B}_{\ell}
in (
1.5
), which means that the group zeta function
Z_{{\mathfrak{G}},\rho}(s)
, up to a finite number of Euler
p
-factors, coincides with
Z({\rm D}_{\ell},s)
or
Z({\rm B}_{\ell};s)
. We computed the exceptional
p
-factors in Theorem
2.30
. For
Z({\rm D}_{\ell},s)
and
Z({\rm B}_{\ell},s)
, their natural boundaries are determined in
[
11
, §6]
.
When
\dim(V)=2\ell+1
, for each square-free integer
N>0
, we can consider the Dirichlet series, say
\zeta^{(N)}_{\beta,L}(s)
, that counts the number of maximal lattices
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{L}}_{\beta}^{\rm max}
such that
\Lambda\subset L
and
\mu(\Lambda)/\mu(L)=Nm^{2}\,(\exists\,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0})
. This series
\zeta_{\beta,L}^{(N)}(s)
, up to a finite number of Euler factors at primes dividing
ND_{L}
, coincides with the group zeta-function
Z({\rm B}_{\ell};s)
spelled out in §
3.4
(2). By
[
11
, Corollary 6.4]
, it has a simple pole at
\sigma:=\frac{2}{n+1}(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}
, so that the Tauberian theorem yields an asymptotic

\sum_{m\leqslant X}\#\{\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{L}}_{L,\beta}^{\rm max}\mid\mu(\Lambda)/\mu(L)=Nm^{2}\}\sim\sigma^{-1}A^{(N)}_{\beta,L}\,X^{\sigma}.

Our result gives a very explicit description of the residue
A_{\beta,L}^{(N)}:={\rm Res}_{s=\sigma}\zeta_{\beta,L}^{(N)}(s)
. We should note that
\sigma
is smaller than the pole
\frac{n^{2}+1}{n+1}
of the full counting series
\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)
.

For non-split even orthogonal case or the odd orthogonal case, the counting series
\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)
is different from the group zeta-function. Given an explicit nature of our
W_{\ell,A,B}^{\rm total}(X,Y)
, the theory in
[
11
, §5]
may be applied to
W_{\ell,A,B}^{\rm total}(X,Y)
to yield the natural boundary of
\zeta_{\beta,L}(s)
(
cf
. Remark (
3.16
)).

The theory of maximal lattices in a finite dimensional vector space over a quadratic field
E
with a non-degenerate hermitian form is fully developed by Shimura
[
29
]
. The same method pursued in this paper also works on the counting problem of the hermitian maximal lattices. We will discuss this case elsewhere. In
[
5
]
, another kind of Dirichlet series that counts numbers of double cosets
{\bf Sp}_{2\ell}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash{\bf Sp}_{2\ell}({\mathbb{Q}})/{\bf Sp}_{2\ell}({\mathbb{Z}})
with given determinant is mentioned; the orthogonal group analogue may be interesting.

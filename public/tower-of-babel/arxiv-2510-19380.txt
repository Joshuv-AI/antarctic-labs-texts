Abstract
. By introducing an intrinsic perimeter measure for intrinsic countably rectifiable sets, we prove a compactness result and a Poincaré inequality for special functions with bounded variation in equiregular Carnot-Carathéodory spaces which satisfy an additional natural assumption, called property ℛ \mathcal{R} .

1.
Introduction

One of the aim of this paper is to expand the theory of special functions with bounded variation in Carnot-Carathéodory spaces by proving the sub-Riemannian counterparts of two milestones of the theory of classical Euclidean
\operatorname{SBV}
functions, namely the compactness theorem and the Poincaré inequality. In order to do so, we need to introduce an intrinsic perimeter measure for intrinsic countably rectifiable sets, which allows us to work in a natural framework and and is of independent interest.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq m\leq n
be integers and let
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
be a
m
-tuple of smooth and linearly independent vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
X
-subunit path
joining
p
and
q
if
\gamma(0)=p
,
\gamma(T)=q
and there exist
h_{1},\dots h_{m}\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}^{2}\leq 1
and

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}(t)X_{j}(\gamma(t)),\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[0,T]$.}

For every
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define

d(p,q)\coloneqq\inf\{T>0:\text{ there exists an $X$-subunit path $\gamma$ joining $p$ and $q$}\},

where we agree that
\inf\emptyset\coloneqq+\infty
.

By the Chow–Rashevskii Theorem, if for every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the linear span of all iterated commutators of the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
computed at
p
has dimension
n
(i.e.
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
satisfy the
Hörmander condition
), then
d
is a distance: the latter means that for every couple of points of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
there always exists a
X
-subunit path joining them. In this case we say that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is a
Carnot-Carathéodory space
of
rank

m
and
d
is the associated
Carnot-Carathéodory distance
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\mathfrak{L}^{i}(p)
the linear span of all the commutators of
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
up to order
i
computed at
p
. We say that a Carnot-Carathéodory space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is
equiregular
if there exist natural numbers
n_{0},n_{1},\dots,n_{s}
such that

0=n_{0}<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{s}=n\text{ and }\dim\mathfrak{L}^{i}(p)=n_{i},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

The natural number
s
is called
step
of the Carnot-Carathéodory space. If
(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
is equiregular, then the
homogeneous dimension
is
Q\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{s}i(n_{i}-n_{i-1})
.

.

In the following,

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\phi\circ u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
and

(3)

D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})=\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})D_{X}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u}).

Proof.

Let us first prove that
\phi\circ u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. By
[
23
, Theorem 2.2.2]
there exists a sequence of functions
(u_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
u_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
and

\left\|u-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0,\qquad|D_{X}u_{t}|(\Omega)\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}|D_{X}u|(\Omega).

Then
\left\|\phi\circ u-\phi\circ u_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0
and, by the lower semicontinuity of the total variation,

|D_{X}(\phi\circ u)|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{t\to+\infty}|D_{X}(\phi\circ u_{t})|(\Omega)\leq\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\liminf_{t\to+\infty}|D_{X}u_{t}|(\Omega)=\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}|D_{X}u|(\Omega)<\infty,

concluding
\phi\circ u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(\Omega)
. By the Lipschitzianity of
\phi
we have
\mathcal{S}_{\phi\circ u}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{u}
. Let
B\subseteq\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u}
be any Borel set. By the coarea formula (Theorem
2.14
) we have

D_{X}(\phi\circ u)(B)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}D_{X}\chi_{\{\phi\circ u>s\}}(B)ds.

By the change of variable
t=\phi(s)
we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}D_{X}\chi_{\{\phi\circ u>s\}}(B)ds=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)D_{X}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(B)dt.

We have that if
x\in B\cap\partial^{*}\{u>t\}
, then
u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=t
(see for instance
[
21
, Proposition 2.22]
) and

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)D_{X}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(B)dt=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{B}\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x))dD_{X}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(x)dt

Using again the coarea formula (Theorem
2.14
) and Fubini’s theorem we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{B}\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x))dD_{X}\chi_{\{u>t\}}(x)dt=\int_{B}\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})dD_{X}u

the latter implying that

D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})=\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})D_{X}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u}),

concluding the proof.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(\Omega)
. Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

We have

D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})=\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})D_{X}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})=\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u\mathcal{L}^{n}

Definition 4.3

Let

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f,\Gamma(f)
be given as in Definition
4.3
. Let

Define

. By using the chain rule (
3

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\Gamma(f)}

\displaystyle\left\|D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})+D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}_{u}\setminus\mathcal{J}_{u})+D_{X}(\phi\circ u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\mathcal{J}_{u}-\phi^{\prime}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})g\mathcal{L}^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=(D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u\mathcal{L}^{n}+D^{c}_{X}u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\{u^{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\lambda\}+(\max\{u^{+},\lambda\}-\max\{u^{-},\lambda\})\nu_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}},

\displaystyle D_{X}\min\{u,\lambda\}

\displaystyle=(D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u\mathcal{L}^{n}+D^{c}_{X}u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\{u^{\mathfrak{p}}\leq\lambda\}+(\min\{u^{+},\lambda\}-\min\{u^{-},\lambda\})\nu_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}.

Proof.

Define
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\phi(t)\coloneqq\max\{t,\lambda\}
. Clearly, since
O
is bounded,
\phi\circ u\in L^{1}(O)
. We observe, using also the coarea formula (Theorem
2.13
), that for every Borel set
B\subseteq O
,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{X}(\{\phi\circ u>s\},B)ds=\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda}P_{X}(\{\phi\circ u>s\},B)ds}_{=0}+\int_{\lambda}^{+\infty}P_{X}(\{u>s\},B)ds\leq|D_{X}u|(B)

which, thanks to Theorem
2.13
, implies that
\max\{u,\lambda\}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(O)
. By the Lipschitzianity of
\phi
we have
\mathcal{S}_{\phi\circ u}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{u}
. Let
B\subseteq O\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u}
be any Borel set. By the coarea formula (Theorem
2.14
) we have

\displaystyle D_{X}\max\{u,\lambda\}(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}D_{X}\chi_{\{\phi\circ u>s\}}(B)ds=\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda}D_{X}\chi_{\{\phi\circ u>s\}}(B)ds}_{=0}+\int_{\lambda}^{+\infty}D_{X}\chi_{\{\phi\circ u>s\}}(B)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda}^{+\infty}D_{X}\chi_{\{u>s\}}(B)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{[\lambda,+\infty]}(s)D_{X}\chi_{\{u>s\}}(B)ds

We have that if
x\in B\cap\partial^{*}\{u>s\}
, then
u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=s
(see for instance
[
21
, Proposition 2.22]
) and

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{[\lambda,+\infty]}(s)D_{X}\chi_{\{u>s\}}(B)ds=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{B}\chi_{[\lambda,+\infty]}(u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x))dD_{X}\chi_{\{u>s\}}(x)ds

Using again the coarea formula and Fubini’s theorem we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{B}\chi_{[\lambda,+\infty]}(u^{\mathfrak{p}}(x))dD_{X}\chi_{\{u>s\}}(x)ds=\int_{B}\chi_{[\lambda,+\infty]}(u^{\mathfrak{p}})dD_{X}u

the latter implying that

D_{X}\max\{u,\lambda\}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(O\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})=D_{X}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\big((O\setminus\mathcal{S}_{u})\cap\{u^{\mathfrak{p}}\geq\lambda\}\big).

In the same fashion of Lemma
4.2
one can prove that

D_{X}^{j}\max\{u,\lambda\}=(\max\{u^{+},\lambda\}-\max\{u^{-},\lambda\})\nu_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}},

proving the claim for
D_{X}^{j}\max\{u,\lambda\}
. The analogous proof works for proving the claim for
D_{X}^{j}\min\{u,\lambda\}
.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
5.1
one has, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(O)
with
O
bounded open set, that
\max\{u,\lambda\},\min\{u,\lambda\}\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(O)
. Let us also observe that if
u,v\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(O)
, Proposition
5.1
allows to conclude that
\max\{u,v\},\min\{u,v\}\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(O)
since it suffices to write
\max\{u,v\}=u+\max\{v-u,0\}
and
\min\{u,v\}=u+\min\{v-u,0\}
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
K\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
be a compact set and
C_{K}
,
R_{K}
be the constants defined as in Theorem
2.15
. Fix
p\in K
,
r\in(0,R_{K})

and define
B\coloneqq B(p,r)
. Let
u:B\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function and define the
non-decreasing rearrangement
of
u

u_{*}(s)\coloneqq\inf\{t\in[-\infty,+\infty]:\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u<t\})\geq s\}

for any
s\in[0,\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)]
. If
m\in\mathbb{R}
is such that

\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u<t\})\leq\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)}{2}\quad\text{ for all }t<m,\qquad\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>t\})\leq\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)}{2}\quad\text{ for all }t>m,

we say that
m
is a
median
for
u
in
B
. For every
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(B)
such that

(10)

{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u})<\frac{1}{2C_{K}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}},

we set

(11)

\tau_{1}(u)\coloneqq u_{*}\bigg(\big(2C_{K}{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u})\big)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}\bigg),

(12)

\tau_{2}(u)\coloneqq u_{*}\bigg(\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)-\big(2C_{K}{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u})\big)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}\bigg).

Clearly, for every median
m
for
u
in
B
we have

\tau_{1}(u)\leq m\leq\tau_{2}(u).

In this section we will also need the following version of Poincaré inequality for
\operatorname{BV}_{X}
function.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
K\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
be a compact set. Then there exists
C_{K}>0
and
R_{K}>0
such that, for every
p\in K
, for every
r\in(0,R_{K})
and for every
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{X}(B(p,r))
one has

\left(\int_{B(p,r)}|u-m|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq C_{K}|D_{X}u|(B(p,r)),

where
m
is any median for
u
in
B(p,r)
.

Proof.

Up to a translation, we may assume
m=0
. Let us also observe that, without loss of generality, we can assume
u\geq 0
, for otherwise we can write
u=\max\{u,0\}-\max\{-u,0\}
and conclude by linearity. By
[
5
, Proposition 1.78]
and by making the change of variable
s=t^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(p,r)}u^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mathcal{L}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}>t\})dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>t^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\})dt

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{Q-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>s\})s^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}ds.

By
[
5
, Lemma 3.48]
we obtain

\frac{Q}{Q-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>s\})s^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}ds\leq\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>s\})^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}ds\right)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}.

By the isoperimetric inequality (Theorem
2.15
) and the coarea formula (Theorem
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{u>s\})^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}ds\right)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}C_{K}P_{X}(\{u>s\},B(p,r))ds\right)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}

\displaystyle=\big(C_{K}|D_{X}u|(B(p,r))\big)^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}},

concluding the proof.
∎

We are ready to prove the Poincaré inequality for
\operatorname{SBV}_{X}
functions, Theorem
1.5
, which we can now state precisely.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
K\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
be a compact set and
C_{K}
,
R_{K}
be the constants defined as in Theorem
2.15
. Fix
p\in K
,
r\in(0,R_{K})
and define
B\coloneqq B(p,r)
. Let
u\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(B)
such that condition (
10
) is satisfied. Define

\bar{u}\coloneqq\max\{\min\{u,\tau_{2}(u)\},\tau_{1}(u)\},

where
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are defined as in (
11
), (
12
). Then

\left(\int_{B}|\bar{u}-m|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq 2C_{K}\int_{B}|D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u|d\mathcal{L}^{n},

where
m
is any median for
u
in
B(p,r)
.

Proof.

As we mentioned before,
\tau_{1}(u)\leq m\leq\tau_{2}(u)
. Up to a translation, we may assume
m=0
. By Remark
5.2

\bar{u}\in\operatorname{SBV}_{X}(B)
, therefore

|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)\leq\left\|D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}\bar{u}\right\|_{L^{1}(B)}+\int_{\mathcal{J}_{\bar{u}}}|\bar{u}^{+}-\bar{u}^{-}|d{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}.

By Proposition
5.1
we get

(13)

|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)\leq\left\|D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u\right\|_{L^{1}(B)}+(\tau_{2}(u)-\tau_{1}(u)){\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u}).

By the coarea formula (Theorem
2.13
) and the isoperimetric inequality (Theorem
2.15
), we obtain

(14)

\displaystyle|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{1}(u)}^{\tau_{2}(u)}P_{X}(\{\bar{u}<t\},B)dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C_{K}}\int^{0}_{\tau_{1}(u)}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{\bar{u}<t\}\cap B)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}dt+\frac{1}{C_{K}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}(u)}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B\setminus\{\bar{u}<t\})^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}dt.

By the definitions of
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and (
10
) we have, for
\tau_{1}(u)<t<0
,

(15)

\frac{1}{C_{K}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{\bar{u}<t\}\cap B)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\geq 2{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u}),

and for
0<t<\tau_{2}(u)
,

(16)

\frac{1}{C_{K}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(B\setminus\{\bar{u}<t\})^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}\geq 2{\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u}).

Putting (
15
) and (
16
) into (
14
) one obtains

(17)

|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)\geq 2(\tau_{2}(u)-\tau_{1}(u)){\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u}).

From (
13
) and (
17
) we obtain the inequality

(\tau_{2}(u)-\tau_{1}(u)){\mathcal{P}^{X}_{\mathcal{J}_{u}}}(\mathcal{J}_{u})\leq\left\|D_{X}u\right\|_{L^{1}(B)},

and, confronting again with (
13
), we obtain

(18)

|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)\leq 2\left\|D_{X}^{\operatorname{ap}}u\right\|_{L^{1}(B)}.

By Theorem
5.4
we have

\left(\int_{B}|\bar{u}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq C_{K}|D_{X}\bar{u}|(B)

and, combining the latter with (
18
), we get

\left(\int_{B}|\bar{u}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq 2C_{K}\left\|D^{\operatorname{ap}}_{X}u\right\|_{L^{1}(B)},

concluding the proof.
∎

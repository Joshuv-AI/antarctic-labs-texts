Large norm inflation of the current in the viscous, non-resistive magnetohydrodynamics equations
Michele Dolce, Niklas Knobel, Christian Zillinger

Abstract
. We consider the ideally conducting, viscous magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations in two dimensions. Specifically, we study the nonlinear dynamics near a combination of Couette flow and a constant magnetic field in a periodic infinite channel. In contrast to the Navier-Stokes equations this system is shown to exhibit algebraic instability and large norm inflation of the magnetic current on non-perturbative time scales.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
76W05, 76E25, 76E30, 76E05

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study the long-time behavior of the non-resistive viscous
magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations in a two-dimensional infinite periodic
channel:

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+V\cdot\nabla V+\nabla\Pi&=\nu\Delta V+\dive(\nabla^{\perp}\Phi\otimes\nabla^{\perp}\Phi),\\
\partial_{t}\Phi+V\cdot\nabla\Phi&=0,\\
\text{div}(V)&=0,\\
(t,x,y)&\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}=:\Omega.\end{split}

These equations model the evolution of an electrically conductive fluid with
velocity
V:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
interacting with a magnetic field

B:=\nabla^{\perp}\Phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The function

\Phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the magnetic potential and
\Pi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure. Common settings described by these
equations include the modeling of plasmas or molten metals in industrial
applications
[
Dav16
]
. The MHD equations are a subject of
highly active research and include several distinct partial dissipation regimes.
More precisely, these equations include both dissipation in the fluid component
with a viscosity parameter
\nu>0
, as well as resistivity of magnetic
component
\mu\geq 0
. The well-posedness of the non-resistive/ideal conductor
case,
\mu=0
, studied in this article is a challenging open problem in the
field
[
Lin12
]
. In contrast to the two-dimensional Euler or
Navier-Stokes equations, it is not known whether the equations can exhibit
finite time blow-up. In particular, we mention recent results on local
well-posedness
[
JLWY19
]
, the stabilizing effect of large, constant
magnetic fields

[
LXZ15
,
RWXZ14
,
ZZ24
,
WZ21
,
WZ17
]

(for small velocities) and improved stability estimates for small velocities

[
CF23
,
CF21
]
.

In contrast, this article focuses on the interaction of mixing by a large
(shear) velocity and the (de)stabilizing role of the magnetic field. To this
end, we study the nonlinear stability problem near the stationary solution

(2)

\displaystyle V(x,y,t)=(y,0),\ \Phi(x,y,t)=-\alpha y,\ \nabla^{\perp}\Phi(x,y,t)=(\alpha,0),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the size of the constant magnetic field.

In view of the shear flow in the stationary solution (
2
),
it is convenient to work with the following perturbative unknowns in coordinates
moving with the flow

\displaystyle v(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=V(x+yt,y,t)-ye_{1}

\displaystyle w(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot V)(x+yt,y,t)+1

\displaystyle\phi(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x+yt,y,t)+\alpha y,

\displaystyle b(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)(x,y,t)

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(\psi,\phi)(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=(w,j)(x,y,t),\qquad

where
\Delta_{t}=\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{y}-t\partial_{x})^{2},\nabla_{t}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y}-t\partial_{x})
denote the
differential operators in coordinates moving with the shear flow. We also introduce the projection operators

f_{0}(y):=(\mathbb{P}_{0}f)(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y)dx,\qquad f_{\neq}:=\mathbb{P}_{\neq}f=(I-\mathbb{P}_{0})f.

It is then useful to isolate the
nonlinear shear flow
component by
observing that

v(x,y,t)=(\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}w)(x,y,t)=v_{0}^{x}(y,t)e_{1}+(\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\psi_{\neq})(x,y,t).

In terms of the unknowns in the moving frame, the non-resistive MHD equations
(
1
) can be equivalently expressed as

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}w&=\nu\Delta_{t}w+\alpha\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}\phi+(\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\Delta_{t}\phi-v\cdot\nabla_{t}w,\\
\partial_{t}\phi&=\alpha\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}w-v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi,\\
\partial_{t}v_{0}^{x}&=\nu\partial_{y}^{2}v_{0}^{x}+(b_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}b_{\neq}^{x}-v_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}v^{x}_{\neq})_{0},\\
w|_{t=0}&=w_{in},\quad\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{in}.\end{split}

where we identify inverse powers of differential operators with their symbols in
the Fourier space. As we discuss in greater detail in Section
2
,
the viscous dissipation and shear dynamics break the symmetry of the equations
and link the evolution of the streamfunction and magnetic potential in an
intricate way. This link is
encoded in the
good unknown

(4)

\displaystyle G(x,y,t):=\nu\psi_{\neq}(x,y,t)+\alpha\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}(x,y,t),

and the equations (
3
) are rewritten in terms of
G,\phi
in Section
2
.

Our main goal in the following is to characterize the nonlinear Lyapunov
stability of the non-resistive MHD equations in terms of
(\phi,G)
and we are particularly interested in possible destabilizing effects of a large viscosity. For this reason, from now on we fix

\alpha=\nu=1.

The
stability properties turn out to drastically change depending on the time-scale
under consideration. Indeed, already from a first inspection of the system (
3
) we see that in the equation for
w
there is a nonlinear term involving factor scaling as
O(t^{2})
, which is extremely dangerous since we have no available dissipation of
\phi
. However, we note that for small perturbations, on a sufficiently
short time-scale the nonlinear evolution can be treated perturbatively.

Proposition 1

(Perturbative time-scale)
.

Let
\alpha=1=\nu
. Then for every
N\geq 5
there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for any

0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and any mean free initial data with

\displaystyle\|\phi_{in}\|_{H^{N}}+\|G_{in}\|_{H^{N}}<\epsilon,

the corresponding solution satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{N}}+\|G(t)\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon

for all times
0<t\ll\epsilon^{-1/2}
.

We say that the equations are stable in Sobolev regularity on the perturbative
time-scale
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1/2})
. More generally, this stability
extends also to higher regularity, e.g. analytic or Gevrey regularity, instead
of the Sobolev regularity
H^{N}
(see Section
3
).

Remark 1
.

To understand why
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1/2})
is the natural perturbative time-scale, consider the nonlinear term
\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta_{t}\phi
in the equation for
w
. The good unknown
G
involves
\Delta_{t}^{-1}w
and one needs to understand the size of
\Delta_{t}^{-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta_{t}\phi)
when integrated in time. Exploiting the fluid dissipation and symmetries, it is enough to estimate
\Delta_{t}^{-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla\nabla_{t}\phi)
. Now, the differential operators are anisotropic in time-frequency space. Therefore the worst-case scenario is when one loses time growth from
\nabla_{t}
and one of the differential operators
\nabla
or
\nabla^{\perp}
is at high frequency, where one can replace a derivative by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(t)
. This makes the dangerous term of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}t^{2})
after “integrating in time” the time-decay of
\Delta^{-1}_{t}
. Therefore, to treat the nonlinearity perturbatively one needs the time scale restriction
t^{2}\varepsilon\ll 1
.

On longer time scales, nonlinear effects become dominant and require a
tailor-made analysis. In particular, to handle infinite regularity losses on longer time-scales we need to work in Gevrey spaces where the associated norm is defined as

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda}}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\lambda(|k|+|\eta|)^{s}}|\mathcal{F}(f)(k,\eta)|^{2}d\eta,

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. A precise, quantitative decrease of
\lambda
with time then allows to push beyond the perturbative time scale. This requires the use of a weight adapted to the main nonlinear
echoes
which we discuss more precisely. Our main results are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the non-resistive MHD equations (
3
) with

\alpha=1=\nu
. Then for every
\tfrac{1}{3}<s\leq 1
, there exists a
threshold
\epsilon_{0}>0
and constants
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}>0
, such
that the evolution satisfies the following stability estimate: for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
and any initial data which satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\|v^{x}_{0,in}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{1}}}+\|\phi_{in}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{1}}}+\|G_{in}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon,

the corresponding solution satisfies the energy estimates

\displaystyle\|\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v^{x}_{0}(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{2}}}+\|G(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{s,\lambda_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon,

for all times
0\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\delta\varepsilon^{-1}
.

Furthermore, there exist
K,c,k_{0}>0
such for
\chi(k)=\mathbf{1}_{|k|\geq k_{0}}
and initial data satisfying also
\|\chi\phi_{in}\|_{H^{-2}}\geq\max(K\delta\varepsilon,\|\chi\partial_{x}G\|_{H^{-2}},c\|\phi_{in}\|_{H^{-2}})
, the current density and magnetic field satisfy the instability estimates

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\min\left(\|j(t)\|_{L^{2}},\langle t\rangle\|b(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\gtrsim\langle t\rangle^{2}\|\phi_{in}\|_{H^{-2}}.\end{split}

In particular, for
T=\delta\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}
one has the large norm-inflation
\|j(T)\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim\delta^{3}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.

Let us comment on these results:

•

The instability is driven by nonlinear resonances, reminiscent of plasma
echoes
[
Rae84
]
, acting on
G
. As we discuss in Section

4.2.2
, for a model problem with
G=0
stability can be be established
on a longer time scale
t<\varepsilon^{-1}/|\log(\varepsilon)|
and under weaker
regularity requirements. Thus, the instability is driven by the good unknown

G
. This is in contrast to the stability of good unknowns in other systems
such as the partially dissipative Boussinesq equations

[
BBZD23
,
MSHZ22
,
MZZ23
]
,
where the submodel with
G=0
already includes the main resonance mechanism.

•

In view of the
current instability
(
5
), stability past time scales
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}})
cannot be expected even for analytic initial data (see Section
3
for a discussion).
We emphasize that we have a full control of the solution far beyond the perturbative
time scale of Proposition
1
.

•

The strong algebraic growth instability (
5
) as well as strong resonances in the nonlinear terms and an associated lack of time integrability pose major, new mathematical challenges and exhibit qualitatively different behavior than in the (anisotropic) resistive settings studied in
[
Dol24
,
KZ23b
]
.

•

In the ideal case
[
Kno24a
]
, when no dissipation is present, the second author obtains stability in Gevrey spaces up until times
t\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
. Here
constant magnetic field yields an interaction between velocity and magnetic
field. Therefore, the decay of the velocity field (inviscid damping) and
growth of the current (by a dynamo effect) balance such that velocity and
magnetic field only grow by a finite amount. Therefore, the viscosity and
the resulting symmetry breaking destabilize the equation on time scales
t\sim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}
.

•

In contrast, for the resistive and inviscid case
[
KZ23a
,
ZZ24
]
there is a reverse symmetry breaking, which leads to inviscid damping in the velocity field. Therefore, in that case, one obtains global in-time stability in Gevrey spaces.

•

When
\alpha,\nu
are not set to be equal to
1
, it is possible to obtain a stability threshold, namely
\varepsilon
scaling as a suitable power of
\nu
. This requires some technical adjustments of the analysis performed in this paper. In particular, one needs to account for a possible transient growth for
G
of size
\mathcal{O}(\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}})
, which can be handled with Fourier multipliers which are by now standard in the literature, e.g.
[
BGM17
]
. Moreover, for small
\nu
we also expect to extend the time-scale in view of
[
Kno24a
]
. It would be interesting to connect the results available in the ideal
[
Kno24a
]
and fully dissipative cases
[
Lis20
,
Dol24
,
Kno24b
,
WZ24
]
in the limiting regimes of interest. This goes way beyond the scope of this paper and therefore we only present the results in the relevant example of large viscosity
\nu=1
and
\alpha=1
.

The remainder of this article is structured as follows:

•

In Section
2
we establish linear stability results. In
particular, this analysis allows us to identify an optimal choice of unknowns.
Due to the strong frequency decoupling of the linearized equations, these
equations exhibit strong stability properties in arbitrary regularity (which is an oversimplification compared to the nonlinear dynamics).

•

In Section
3
we establish stability on the
perturbative time scale
\varepsilon^{-1/2}
. Furthermore, we discuss which nonlinear terms can become large after this time scale and provide a heuristic discussion of the physical resonance mechanisms. These considerations motivate the construction of energy functionals in the following sections.

•

Section
4
establishes nonlinear stability on
non-perturbative time scales. Here, we use a bootstrap approach to control
adapted Lyapunov functionals. In order to highlight the nonlinear resonance
mechanisms, we individually discuss resonances in the magnetic and fluid parts
of the equations in individual subsections.

•

In Section
5
we prove the norm-inflation result (
5
).

2.
Linear Stability and Adapted Unknowns

In this section, we consider the linearized stability problem around the
stationary solution (
2
) and consider the case

\alpha=\nu=1
. Expressing the nonlinear MHD equations (
3
) in terms of

\phi
and
G
(defined in (
4
)), we obtain the system

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}G&=(\Delta_{t}+2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}+\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1})G-\partial_{x}^{3}\Delta_{t}^{-2}\phi_{\neq}\\
&\quad+\Delta^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)_{\neq}-\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)_{\neq}-\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi)_{\neq}\\
\partial_{t}\phi&=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}+\partial_{x}G\\
&\quad-(\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla\phi)+(\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)\phi-v^{x}_{0}\partial_{x}\phi_{\neq}\\
\partial_{t}v^{x}_{0}&=\partial_{y}^{2}v^{x}_{0}+(b_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}b_{\neq}^{x}-v_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}v^{x}_{\neq})_{0}.\end{split}

In order to investigate this system’s stability, in this section, we study the
associated linearized problem:

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}G&=(\Delta_{t}+2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}+\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1})G-\partial_{x}^{3}\Delta_{t}^{-2}\phi,\\
\partial_{t}\phi&=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi+\partial_{x}G.\end{split}

Since the underlying stationary solution (
2
) is affine, the
linearized equations (
7
) decouple in frequency. Indeed,
applying a Fourier transform in both
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the system
(
7
) decouples with respect to the frequencies
(k,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
and reads

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{F}G\\
\mathcal{F}\phi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}+2\frac{k(\eta-kt)}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}&\frac{ik^{3}}{(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})^{2}}\\
ik&-\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{F}G\\
\mathcal{F}\phi\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Here, with slight abuse of notation, we again denote the Fourier transformed
functions by
\phi
and
G
. On the one hand, this decoupling allows for a very
transparent analysis and strong stability properties. On the other hand, the
dynamics “oversimplify”; the stability properties of the nonlinear equations
are much more restrictive due to resonance effects which are omitted in the
linearization (see Section
3.1
for a heuristic discussion of the
resonances).

In view to later applications to the nonlinear model, we establish stability in
terms of an energy weighted with a Fourier multiplier
m_{L}(t,D)
. In the present
setting, due to the strong decoupling, this multiplier can be constructed in a
straightforward way as follows

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{5}{1+(t-\frac{\eta}{k})^{2}}&\text{ if }|k,\eta|\leq 10\langle t\rangle,\\
0&\text{ else},\end{cases}\\
&m_{L}(0,k,\eta)=\langle k,\eta\rangle^{-N},\end{split}

with
N\geq 1
. For
the construction in the nonlinear setting, see Section
4.1
.

The linear stability results for the system (
8
) are
summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
A:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

be a smooth, positive Fourier multiplier, which satisfies

(10)

\displaystyle A\geq m_{L}^{-1},\qquad\frac{\partial_{t}A}{A}\leq-\frac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}.

Then, for any solution

(G,\phi)
of (
7
) the energy functional

(11)

\displaystyle E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|AG(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|A\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

satisfies the energy estimate

(12)

\displaystyle E(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{t}AG\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq E(0).

In particular, the linear error term

\displaystyle L_{G,\phi}:=\,\langle(2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}+\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1})AG,AG\rangle-\langle\big(\partial_{x}^{3}\Delta_{t}^{-2}+\partial_{x}\big)A\phi_{\neq},AG\rangle.

satisfies the bound

(13)

\displaystyle|L_{G,\phi}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{t}AG\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 2
.

Choosing
A=m_{L}^{-1}
, we see that
E(t)
is comparable to
\|(G,\phi)\|_{H^{N}}^{2}
and hence that the linearized MHD equations are stable in arbitrary Sobolev regularity.

In Section
4.1
we construct a specific Fourier multiplier
A

which incorporates
m_{L}^{-1}
and is
additionally tailored to control nonlinear effects and to satisfy good
commutator estimates. The estimates of this proposition also hold for that
weight as shown in Lemma
6
.

Throughout this article we have normalized viscosity as
\nu=1
. For much larger
viscosities, e.g.
\nu\geq 10
, linear stability estimates become trivial,
since all possible growth mechanisms can easily be absorbed by viscous
dissipation. In the case of small viscosities, it is well-known that the term

(-\nu(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})+2\frac{\tfrac{\eta}{k}-t}{1+(\tfrac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}})\mathcal{F}G
can lead to
norm inflation by a factor
(\nu k^{2})^{-2/3}
. As mentioned in Section

1
, the results of this article can be extended to this
case with some technical effort and will yield some nonlinear stability
threshold in terms of
\nu>0
. Since we do not aim for optimality of that
threshold, we do not pursue this extension in the present article.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

We recall that all operators in the linearized MHD equations
(
7
) are given by Fourier multipliers and that the
equations hence decouple in Fourier space.
Therefore, it suffices to show that for any given frequency
k,\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, the following frequency-wise bound holds

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\qquad\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left(|AG|^{2}+|A\phi|^{2}\right)\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})|AG|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}|A\phi|^{2}-\frac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}|(AG,A\phi)|^{2}.\end{split}

Since Fourier multipliers commute and
\frac{\partial_{t}A}{A}\leq-\frac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}
, we only need to compute the explicit time-derivatives:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(|G|^{2}+|\phi|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq-(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})|G|^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|k(\eta-kt)|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}|G|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(-\left(\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\right)^{2}+1\right)|\phi||G|

\displaystyle\leq-(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})|G|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{|k(\eta-kt)|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}|G|^{2}+\frac{(1+(t-\frac{\eta}{k})^{2}}{8}\left|\left(\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\right)^{2}-1\right||G|^{2}.

Using this, it follows that (
14
) holds if

\displaystyle\frac{|k(\eta-kt)|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}+\frac{(1+(t-\frac{\eta}{k})^{2}}{8}\left|\left(\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\right)^{2}-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2})-\frac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}},

which is the case by the definition of
m_{L}
(
9
) and our assumed bounds on
A
.
∎

These linear stability results are necessary preconditions for nonlinear
stability but turn out to not be sufficient. In the following section, we
introduce nonlinear instability mechanisms and show that they lead to large
instabilities on any non-perturbative time-scale.

3.
Nonlinear Resonances and Short Time Stability

In contrast to the linear stability results of Section
2
, the
nonlinear dynamics exhibit resonance phenomena which can result in large norm
inflation and loss of stability. These resonances are reminiscent of “plasma
echoes” in the Vlasov-Poisson equations

[
MWGO68
,
Rae84
,
Bed20a
,
Zil21
]

or “fluid echoes” in the Euler equations

[
YOD05
,
DZ21
,
DM18
]
. However, for the present setting
of the MHD equations the mechanism exhibits a much stronger time dependence,
strong coupling effects (e.g., the resonances are not suppressed by viscous
dissipation) and new time scales.

As a first result and in order to establish a proper context, we employ basic
well-posedness estimates to establish stability in Sobolev regularity (or any
higher regularity class) up to a perturbative time scale

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-1/2})
. More precisely, we show that on this time scale
the energy constructed in Section
2
remains bounded also under
the nonlinear evolution.

Lemma 3
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\geq 5
and let
m_{L}(t,D)
be the Fourier multiplier defined in (
9
). Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N)>0

such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and any mean free initial data

(\phi_{in},G_{in})\in H^{N}
with

\displaystyle\|\phi_{in}\|_{H^{N}}+\|G_{in}\|_{H^{N}}<\varepsilon

the corresponding solution
(\phi,G)
of the nonlinear MHD equation
(
3
) satisfies

(15)

\displaystyle\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{N}}+\|G(t)\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim\|m_{L}(t,D)(\phi,G)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon

for all times
0<t\ll\varepsilon^{-1/2}
.

This result in particular implies the stability estimate of Proposition

1
.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

We interpret the nonlinear MHD equations (
6
) as
a forced linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}G\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}=L\begin{pmatrix}G\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}+F,

and aim to establish an a priori bound on a suitable energy. As in Proposition
2
, we set
A=m_{L}^{-1}
and hence, defining the energy

\displaystyle E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|AG(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|A\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

we know that it satisfies satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t)\leq\langle A(G,\phi),AF\rangle-\|\nabla_{t}AG\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m_{L}}{m_{L}}}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We now claim that the contribution of the nonlinear forcing,

\langle A(G,\phi),AF\rangle
, can be controlled in such a way that

(16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t)+\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)E(t)^{3/2}+C\langle t\rangle\|A\nabla_{t}G\|E(t).

Using the dissipation of
G
and the bootstrap assumption that
E(t)\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle E(T)-E(0)\leq\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}Edt\leq C(T+T^{2})\varepsilon^{3}.

Thus, if
\varepsilon(T+T^{2})
is sufficiently small we can improve the bootstrap assumption
E(t)\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}
and show that this holds true on the whole interval
[0,T]
, thus proving the desired result since we are interested in the case
T\ll\varepsilon^{-1/2}
.

To control the nonlinear forcing, we recall from (
3
) that
F
consists of several quadratic
nonlinearities:

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)_{\neq}-\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)_{\neq}-\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi)_{\neq}\\
-(\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla\phi)+(\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)\phi-v^{x}_{0}\partial_{x}\phi_{\neq}\end{pmatrix}.

Before discussing each term, we make the following observations:

•

The nonlinear forcing by
\Delta^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)_{\neq}
increases in time as
\mathcal{O}(t^{2})
. Therefore one can a priori not expect a better bound than (
16
) unless one considers energies which lose regularity in time (or rather lose in the radius of convergence, see Sections
3.1
and
4
).

•

Since we consider equations with
partial dissipation
, any terms
involving derivatives of
\phi
need to exploit cancellation and commutators to avoid losses
of derivatives.
In particular, we need to exploit commutation properties of the
multiplier
A
. In the perturbative regime considered in this section, this
does not yet require detailed optimizations but will pose a major challenge
to the construction of
A
on longer time scales considered in Section
4
.

•

Since
N\geq 5
, we may make use of Sobolev embeddings and paraproduct
decompositions (this will also feature prominently in the following Section

3.1
). In particular, a main focus will be on products where
one factor is frequency localized at “low” frequencies, while the other is
at “high” frequencies.

•

The time-dependent Fourier multiplier
\Delta_{t}^{-1}\leadsto\frac{1}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}
always provides a gain with respect to
x

derivatives. For
y
derivatives it also provides a strong gain unless

|\eta-kt|\leq\frac{|kt|}{2}
. It is in that frequency region where the
loss of a factor
t
as in the claimed estimate (
16
) occurs.

Since we establish improved estimates for all terms (additionally using Gevrey
regularity) in Section
4
, in this proof we only discuss the
most important terms in detail. We follow the same
notational conventions as used in Section
4
, e.g.
NL_{\phi\rightarrow G}
for the nonlinearity involving
\phi
in the evolution
equation for
G
, for ease of comparison.

The quadratic nonlinearity in
\phi
:

As discussed above, a key role is played by the nonlinear self-interaction of

\phi
and the (lack of) control of

\displaystyle NL_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\langle A\big((\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi\cdot\nabla)\phi\big)-(\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)A\phi,A\phi\rangle,

where we used fact that
\phi\mapsto\nabla^{\perp}a\cdot\nabla\phi
is an
antisymmetric operator for any smooth function
a
.
By using Plancherel’s Theorem we may equivalently express
NL_{\phi}
in
Fourier space as

\displaystyle NL_{\phi}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{k,l}\iint d\xi\ d\eta\ A\phi(t,k,\eta)A\phi(t,l,\xi)A\overline{\phi}(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{A(t,k,\eta)-A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}{A(t,l,\xi)A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}\frac{l(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi))}{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}.

and claim that

\displaystyle|NL_{\phi}|\lesssim\langle t\rangle\|A\phi\|^{3}\leq\langle t\rangle E(t)^{3/2}.

Furthermore, we note that for
l=k-1
,
\eta=\xi
,
\xi-lt=0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{l(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi))}{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}=t

and thus we cannot expect to obtain a better bound.

In order to estimate
NL_{\phi}
we employ a paraproduct decomposition. More
precisely, since
(l,\xi)+(k-l,\eta-\xi)=(k,\xi)
at least one of these vectors has to
be of comparable or larger magnitude than
(k,\xi)
.
Here, in the literature one commonly refers to the
transport
regime

\displaystyle\langle l,\xi\rangle\ll\langle k,\eta\rangle\text{ and }\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\approx\langle k,\eta\rangle,

the
reaction
regime

\displaystyle\langle l,\xi\rangle\approx\langle k,\eta\rangle\text{ and }\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\ll\langle k,\eta\rangle.

and the
remainder
regime

\displaystyle\langle l,\xi\rangle\gtrsim\langle k,\eta\rangle\text{ and }\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\gtrsim\langle k,\eta\rangle,

and correspondingly we split the integral as

\displaystyle NL_{\phi}=T_{\phi}+R_{\phi}+\mathcal{R}_{\phi}.

Estimating the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{\phi}
:

In the remainder region, by the triangle inequality it holds that
\langle l,\xi\rangle\approx\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\gtrsim\langle k,\eta\rangle
and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{A(t,k,\eta)-A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}{A(t,l,\xi)A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}\right|\lesssim\langle l,\xi\rangle^{-N}.

Since
N\geq 5
is much larger than the Sobolev embedding threshold, we can further
easily absorb
\frac{l(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi))}{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}
and hence
infer that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{\phi}|\lesssim\|A\phi\|^{3}\leq E^{3/2}.

Estimating the reaction
R_{\phi}
:

We estimate

\displaystyle\frac{|l(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi))|}{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}\leq\langle t\rangle\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle.

If
\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\gtrsim\langle k,\eta\rangle^{\frac{3}{N}}
we may argue as in the remainder regime. Otherwise, we
can absorb the factor
\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle
into the gain by the
Sobolev embedding. We thus conclude that

\displaystyle|R_{\phi}|\lesssim\langle t\rangle\|A\phi\|^{3}\leq\langle t\rangle E^{3/2}.

Estimating the transport
T_{\phi}
:

By the same argument as above, we may assume that
\langle l,\xi\rangle\lesssim\langle k,\eta\rangle^{\frac{3}{N}}
.
Since
\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle\approx\langle k,\eta\rangle
might
be very large, we here rely on the gain by the commutator
A(t,k,\eta)-A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)
to control

\displaystyle\frac{|l(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi))|}{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}\leq\langle l,\xi\rangle\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle.

More precisely, solving explicitly (
9
) we find that

\displaystyle A(t,k,\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}-\frac{5}{1+(\tau-\frac{\eta}{k})^{2}}1_{|k,\eta|\leq 10\langle\tau\rangle}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle=:\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}m_{L}^{-1}(t,k,\eta).

We thus split the commutator of
A
into two distinct commutators:

\displaystyle\qquad A(t,k,\eta)-A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}-\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{N}\right)m_{L}^{-1}(t,k,\eta)

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{N}\left(m_{L}^{-1}(t,k,\eta)-m_{L}^{-1}(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)\right).

Clearly, the first commutator gains a factor
\langle k,\eta\rangle^{-1}

and thus allows for an estimate of
|T_{\phi}|
by
\|A\phi\|^{3}
.

For the second commutator we distinguish between two cases:

•

If
|k,\eta|\geq 10\langle t\rangle
, then

m_{L}(t,k,\eta)=m_{L}(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)=1
and hence the commutator is trivial.

•

If instead
|k,\eta|\leq 10\langle t\rangle
, then the loss of
derivative is controlled by a loss of a factor
t
. We may thus bound

\displaystyle|T_{\phi}|\lesssim\langle t\rangle\|A\phi\|^{3}.

The action of
G
on
\phi
:

The argument for the action of
G
on
\phi
is largely analogous to the
previous case. In order to avoid duplication with
Section
4
, where we establish more optimized bounds for
more intricate weights, we discuss the different regimes briefly.

Using Plancherel’s identity, we consider

\displaystyle NL_{G\rightarrow\phi}

\displaystyle:=\langle A\phi,A(\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla\phi)\rangle=\langle A\phi,A(\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla\phi)-\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla A\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\int d\eta d\xi A\phi(t,k,\eta)AG(t,l,\xi)A\overline{\phi}(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{A(t,k,\eta)-A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}{A(t,l,\xi)A(t,k-l,\eta-\xi)}(\xi(k-l)-l(\eta-\xi)).

In the remainder case, we obtain a straightforward bound by
E^{3/2}
.
In the reaction case, we control

\displaystyle\|A\nabla^{\perp}G\|\leq\langle t\rangle\|A\nabla_{t}G\|

and recall that
\|A\nabla_{t}G\|
is square-integrable in time due to the
resistive dissipation.
Finally, in the transport regime we again exploit the commutation properties
of
A
, as in the case above.
Thus, over all we obtain a bound

\displaystyle NL_{G\rightarrow\phi}\leq\langle t\rangle E^{3/2}+\langle t\rangle E\|A\nabla_{t}G\|.

The remaining nonlinear contributions:

The estimates for the remaining nonlinearities

\displaystyle NL_{\phi\to G}:=\langle A\big(\Delta^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)_{\neq}\big),AG\rangle,

\displaystyle NL_{G}:=-\langle A\big(\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)_{\neq}+\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi)_{\neq}\big),AG\rangle,

can be shown by the same arguments and are comparatively simpler in view of the
additional dissipation of
G
.
We thus obtain rough bounds

\displaystyle|NL_{\phi\to G}|+|NL_{G}|\leq\langle t\rangle E^{3/2}+\langle t\rangle E\|A\nabla_{t}G\|.

These estimates are revisited in Proposition
3
(with
improved weights) and thus a detailed discussion is omitted here for brevity.

∎

The perturbative time scale is thus determined by a standard energy argument and
the possible linear growth bound on the magnetic field (this growth is attained,
see Theorem
1
). In the following, we show that this estimate is
optimal and that nonlinear effects become dominant on longer time scales. Here,
in a first step we discuss nonlinear resonances in a model problem and the role
of viscous dissipation and magnetic coupling.

3.1.
Nonlinear Resonances and Paraproducts

While the linearized dynamics provide a good approximation for small data and on
corresponding perturbative time scales, this generally ceases to be the case on
longer time scales or for larger data. Indeed, in case of algebraic growth in
the linearized dynamics, this approximation may even fail to be consistent on
longer time scales, such as in Backus’ objection to Landau damping

[
Bac60
]
. It thus becomes crucial to capture nonlinear
effects and to identify for which frequencies and at which times large
corrections, so called resonances, can occur.

In this section, we argue on a heuristic level and in terms of paraproducts
(i.e., frequency decompositions into “high” and “low” frequencies) to
introduce the main nonlinear coupling and growth mechanisms. In particular, we
show that these possibly large corrections to the linearized dynamics are highly
frequency- and time-localized. We thus speak of
resonances
.

We recall from (
6
) that the nonlinear equations (
3
) in
terms of the unknowns
G
and
\phi
read as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{t}+2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}+\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1})G-\partial_{x}^{3}\Delta_{t}^{-2}\phi

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\phi\nabla\Delta_{t}\phi

\displaystyle\quad-\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)-\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi+\partial_{x}G

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla^{\perp}G\nabla\phi-(\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)\phi.

The main challenges in the following are thus posed by the control of the
quadratic nonlinearities. In our analysis, we consider these
nonlinearities as forcing terms acting on the linear evolution. However, while
parts of these forces can be treated perturbatively, some contributions turn out
to be very large and qualitatively change the dynamics. In the following
subsection we introduce these effects on a heuristic level, which serves as a
starting point for the rigorous analysis of Section
4
.

3.2.
Main Resonance Mechanisms

In Section
2
we have shown that the linearized evolution
equations are stable in Sobolev regularity. Furthermore, as shown in Lemma

3
this stability extends to the nonlinear equations for
small data on perturbative time scales. However, on longer time scales it can a
priori not be expected that the nonlinear dynamics remain close to the linear
ones. In this section, on a heuristic level, we show that this expectation
strongly fails and that the quadratic nonlinearities result in drastically
different stability properties.

In the interest of a clear and tractable heuristic model, we make several
simplifications to the model (see Section
4
for the full
model):

•

Since the linear dynamics turn out to be stabilizing, we omit all linear
terms except for the dissipation in
G
and part of the coupling by the
underlying magnetic field (which could propagate some of this dissipation to

\phi
).

•

We are mainly interested in the behavior at high frequencies, since there
different stability properties and regularities are most visible. We thus
focus on the evolution “at high frequency” and split all quadratic terms
into “high” and “low” frequency parts, e.g.,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}G\nabla\phi\leadsto\nabla^{\perp}G_{hi}\nabla\phi_{lo}+\nabla^{\perp}G_{lo}\nabla\phi_{hi}+\nabla^{\perp}G_{hi}\nabla\phi_{hi}.

Note that there is no low-low part, as we are interested in the high frequency
evolution. These splittings can be made precise in terms of Littlewood-Payley
projections and paraproducts, but for the purposes of this section we think of
low frequencies as of size about
1
and high frequency about
\lambda
with

\lambda\gg 1
very large.

•

This further implies that derivatives are comparable to multiplication
with
\lambda
, that is,
\partial_{t}D^{N}\phi_{hi}\approx\lambda^{N}\partial_{t}\phi_{hi}
.
In particular, it follows that derivatives are only relevant for high
frequency contributions and

\displaystyle\lambda^{N}\nabla^{\perp}G_{hi}\nabla\phi_{hi}\approx\nabla^{\perp}\lambda^{N/2}G_{hi}\nabla\lambda^{N/2}\phi_{hi}.

For this reason, all high-high terms can be expected to be rather harmless.

•

Similarly, we assume that the
H^{N}
norm of all low frequency parts
remains bounded by a small constant
\epsilon>0
uniformly in time (but does
not necessarily decay). In particular, we may heuristically replace

\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{lo}\approx\frac{\epsilon}{1+t^{2}}
.

•

In view of these simplifications, the most dangerous nonlinearities are
those where additional derivatives are applied to the high frequency parts,
since those may a priori be of size
\lambda\gg 1
.

With these preparations, we isolate the following contributions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}G-\Delta_{t}G&=\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{y}^{t}((\nabla^{\perp}\phi^{hi}\nabla)\partial_{y}^{t}\phi^{lo})_{\neq},\\
\partial_{t}\phi_{\neq}&=\partial_{x}G+(\nabla^{\perp}G^{hi}\nabla\phi^{lo})_{\neq}.\end{split}

Here, the unknowns
G,\phi_{\neq}
correspond to high frequency perturbations
with non-zero horizontal frequency. Focusing on the evolution at a given
frequency
(k,\eta)\in(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}
, if
|\eta-kt|\gg 1
is
large, the operator
\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{y}^{t}\approx|\eta-kt|^{-1}
provides
smallness and time decay.

Thus, we focus on times
t
such that
t\approx\tfrac{\eta}{k}
and, as a worst
case, consider
\phi^{lo}\approx\epsilon\cos(x)
. In this way, the system in
Fourier space becomes a system of coupled ordinary differential equations with
nearest neighbor coupling. Moreover, unlike the full nonlinear system, it
decouples with respect to the vertical frequency
\eta
. We thus introduce a

three-mode model
:

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}G(k)+k^{2}(1+(t-\tfrac{\xi}{k})^{2})G(k)&=\varepsilon t\tfrac{\eta}{k}\tfrac{1}{1+|t-\frac{\eta}{k}|}\phi(k+1),\\
\partial_{t}\phi(k+1)&=\varepsilon tkG(k),\\
\partial_{t}\phi(k)&=ikG(k),\end{split}

where we omit all other modes and consider times
t\approx\tfrac{\eta}{k}
. In
particular, we note that for
t=\frac{\eta}{k}
, the right-hand-side in the
first equation equals
\epsilon t^{2}\phi(k+1)
while the dissipation is only of
size
k^{2}G(k)
. Thus, for times
t\gtrsim\epsilon^{-1/2}
past the perturbative
time scale (see Section
3
), the good unknown
G
cannot be
expected to remain small even if it was initially zero. Instead, neglecting the
time-dependence of
\phi(k+1)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle G(k)\approx\varepsilon t\tfrac{\eta}{k^{3}}\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}\phi(k+1).

Inserting this into our model yields a coupled system of two modes:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(k+1)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon t)^{2}\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}\phi(k+1),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(k)

\displaystyle=i\varepsilon t\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\tfrac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}\phi(k+1).

If
t\leq\delta\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\phi(k+1)

\displaystyle\approx 1,

\displaystyle\phi(k)

\displaystyle\approx\tfrac{\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}\phi(k+1).

In particular, iterating this for a sequence of frequencies
k
with
\eta
fixed
(similar to echo chains in Landau damping
[
Bed20b
]
), this
suggests that

\displaystyle\phi(1)\approx\phi(K)\prod_{k=1}^{K}\tfrac{\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}\underset{K\sim\sqrt[3]{\eta}}{\approx}\phi(K)\exp(\tfrac{1}{2}|\eta|^{1/3}).

Such growth in Fourier space exactly corresponds to Gevrey 3 regularity.

We remark that on time scales much larger than this, i.e.,
\varepsilon t^{\frac{3}{2}}\gg 1
, we instead obtain highly degenerate growth. More precisely, for
\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\geq 1
and
\phi_{in}(k+1)=1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\phi(k+1)

\displaystyle\approx\exp\left((\varepsilon t)^{2}\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\int\tfrac{1}{\langle\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\phi(k)

\displaystyle\approx\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon t}(\exp\left((\varepsilon t)^{2}\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\int\tfrac{1}{\langle\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}d\tau\right)-1)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\varepsilon t\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}\int\tfrac{1}{\langle\tau-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{3}}d\tau.

This exponential growth, present even in the case
\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim t\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}
, cannot anymore be controlled by Gevrey regularity (similar
effects are expected for the inviscid Boussinesq beyond the natural time scale
considered in
[
BBZD23
]
).

4.
Nonlinear Stability: Energy Functionals and Bootstrap Approach

Building on the insights of Section
3.1
, our main aim in the
following is to establish nonlinear stability of the non-resistive MHD equations
(
3
) in terms of the unknowns
G
and
\Phi
.
In particular, the resonances of the model problem (
17
) suggest
that any stability result will require Gevrey regularity with some loss of the
radius of regularity in time.
We recall the definition of
G
in (
4
) and the full nonlinear system we aim at studying is

(18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{t}+2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}+\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1})G-\partial_{x}^{3}\Delta_{t}^{-2}\phi_{\neq}

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi)_{\neq}-\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)_{\neq}-\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi)_{\neq},

(19)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}+\partial_{x}G

\displaystyle\quad-(\nabla^{\perp}G\cdot\nabla\phi)+(\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)\phi-v^{x}_{0}\partial_{x}\phi_{\neq},

(20)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{x}_{0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{y}^{2}v^{x}_{0}+(b_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}b_{\neq}^{x}-v_{\neq}\cdot\nabla_{t}v^{x}_{\neq})_{0}.

Our goal is to perform weighted energy estimates on
G,\phi,v_{0}^{x}
. To this end, we introduce the Fourier multiplier

(21)

\displaystyle A(t,k,\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}(m^{-1}J)(t,k,\eta)e^{\lambda(t)|\eta,k|^{s}},

where we define
m,\,J
in Section
4.1
. We also need the technical variant
\tilde{A}
which is defined as above with
J\to\tilde{J}
that is defined in (
51
). At this stage of the discussion, it is enough to know that there exists
0<\tilde{\lambda}\leq\lambda(t)
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\tilde{\lambda}}}\lesssim\|Af\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, the weight
m
is introduced to handle terms that are heuristically integrable in time. Instead, the weight
J
takes into account the delicate anisotropic regularity loss and in particular is based on the weight introduced in
[
MSHZ22
]
, which is well adapted to the Gevrey-3 regularity loss we encounter in our problem.

We define our main energy functionals as follows:

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|AG\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 4

(On the zero-mode energy)
.

Observe that we need to control the
x
-average of the velocity with one Sobolev derivative less. At high frequencies, this is equivalent at controlling
\psi_{0}=-\partial_{y}^{-1}v_{0}^{x}
, which would be the natural quantity to handle. Indeed, all our unknowns are defined via the streamfunctions
\phi,\psi
. However, at low frequency there are some technical issues that could be easily handled by estimating
v_{0}^{x}
. The latter is expected to decay faster than
\psi_{0}^{x}
by standard heat-semigroup bounds.

Our goal is to control the energies defined in (
22
)-(
23
) and prove that they remain of size
O(\varepsilon^{2})
up to a time-scale
O(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}})
. Indeed, as we show below, this would readily imply Theorem
1
. To prove the bounds on the energies, we proceed with a bootstrap argument. We first define the associated
dissipation functionals
arising when taking the time-derivative of our energies as

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{G,\phi}:

\displaystyle=\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sum_{\iota\in\{\lambda,m,q\}}\mathcal{D}_{\iota}[G]+\mathcal{D}_{\iota}[\phi_{\neq}],

(25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{v_{0}}:

\displaystyle=\|A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}[\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v_{0}^{x}].

Then, inspired by the bounds available for the linearized system, we formulate our
bootstrap hypotheses
: for two given constants
c_{*},C_{*}>1
and a time
0<T\leq\delta\varepsilon^{-2/3}
, we assume that

(Bh)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\mathcal{E}(0)+\mathcal{E}_{0}(0)\leq c_{*}\varepsilon^{2},\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{100}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}_{G,\phi}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq 4C_{*}\varepsilon^{2},\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)+\frac{1}{100}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}_{v_{0}}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq 4C_{*}\varepsilon^{2}.\end{split}

By standard local well-posedness theory (see also Section
3
), we know that (
Bh
) are verified with
4C_{*}
replaced by
C_{*}
if one takes
T
sufficiently small or
T=1
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the latter being the case we will consider from now on. This implies that
T\geq 1
and therefore our goal is now to prove that (
Bh
) holds true with
4C_{*}
replaced by
2C_{*}
on the whole time-interval
(0,T)
, meaning that
T=\delta\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}
as desired.

To accomplish this task, we first compute the energies identities which are the starting point for the nonlinear analysis.

Lemma 5
.

The following energy identities holds true

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}+

\displaystyle\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A\phi_{\neq}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sum_{\iota\in\{\lambda,m,q\}}\mathcal{D}_{\iota}[G]+\mathcal{D}_{\iota}[\phi_{\neq}]

\displaystyle=L_{G,\phi}+NL_{\phi\to G}+NL_{G\to\phi}+NL_{\phi}+NL_{G}

and

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{0}

4.1.1.
Useful sets

We first define some time-frequency sets, which are useful to split the main nonlinear interactions and define the weights.

The

are

(43)

\displaystyle S_{R}

\displaystyle=\{((k,\eta),(l,\xi))\in(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})^{2}\,:\,\ |k-l,\eta-\xi|\geq 8|l,\xi|\},

\displaystyle=\{((k,\eta),(l,\xi))\in(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})^{2}\,:\,\ 8|k-l,\eta-\xi|\leq|l,\xi|\},

(45)

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle=\{((k,\eta),(l,\xi))\in(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})^{2}\,:\,\ \tfrac{1}{8}|l,\xi|\leq|k-l,\eta-\xi|\leq 8|l,\xi|\}.

These sets are introduced to split the nonlinear terms by a standard paraproduct decomposition (see for instance

The following set

(46)

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle=\{(k,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,|k,\eta|\leq 10t^{2}\},

As is standard in the field by now, we introduce the resonant times and intervals, where in particular we follow the definitions in
[
MSHZ22
]
. For

\displaystyle t_{k,\eta}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{k}\pm\frac{\eta}{2k^{3}}

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{k,\eta}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[t^{-}_{k,\eta},t^{+}_{k,\eta}]&\qquad 1\leq|k|\leq|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}},\ \eta k>0,\\
\emptyset&\qquad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle I_{k,\eta}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{\eta}{k}+\tfrac{|\eta|}{|k|+1}),\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{\eta}{k}+\tfrac{|\eta|}{|k|-1})]&\qquad 2\leq|k|\leq|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}},\ \eta k>0\\

We also denote

4.2.1.

\phi

We aim at proving (

First of all, we split the term

defined in (

\displaystyle NL_{\phi\to G}

\displaystyle=\langle AG_{\neq},\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{y}^{t}A((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\nabla)\partial_{y}^{t}\phi)_{\neq}\rangle+\langle AG_{\neq},\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{x}A((\nabla^{\perp}\phi\nabla)\partial_{x}\phi)_{\neq}\rangle

\displaystyle=:NL_{\phi\to G}^{y}+NL_{\phi\to G}^{x}.

The most difficult term to bound is

(due to the the time-dependent spatial derivatives) and therefore we estimate it in detail. The bounds for
NL_{\phi\to G}^{x}
can be easily deduced from the ones we show for
NL_{\phi\to G}^{y}
and we thus omit the details for this term.

By Parseval’s theorem, we infer

\displaystyle|NL_{\phi\to G}^{y}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\iint\left(\textbf{1}_{S_{T}}+\textbf{1}_{S_{R}}+\textbf{1}_{S_{\mathcal{R}}}\right)\frac{|(\eta-kt)(\xi-lt)(\eta l-k\xi)|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}A(k,\eta)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times|AG|(k,\eta)|\phi|(l,\xi)|\phi|(k-l,\eta-\xi)d\xi d\eta,

\displaystyle=:\widetilde{T}_{\phi\to G}+R_{\phi\to G}+\mathcal{R}_{\phi\to G},

where we recall the definition of the transport, reaction and reminder sets in (
43
), (
44
) and (
45
) respectively.

We first rewrite the integral concentrated on the transport set as follows: since
\xi-lt=\eta-kt-(\eta-\xi-(k-l)t)
, by the triangle inequality we have

|(\eta-kt)(\xi-lt)|\leq(\eta-kt)^{2}+|(\eta-\xi-(k-l)t)(\eta-kt)|.

Then, with change of variables
\tilde{\xi}=\eta-\xi
and
\tilde{l}=k-l
, notice that

\displaystyle\eta-\xi-(k-l)t

\displaystyle=\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{k}t,

\displaystyle\eta l-k\xi

\displaystyle=\eta(k-\tilde{l})-k(\eta-\tilde{\xi})=-(\eta\tilde{l}-k\tilde{\xi}),

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{S_{T}}(k,\eta,l,\xi)

\displaystyle=\textbf{1}_{S_{R}}(k,\eta,\tilde{l},\tilde{\xi}).

Thus, thanks to the observations above, we get that

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{\phi\to G}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{S_{T}}\frac{|(\eta-kt)^{2}(\eta l-k\xi)|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}A(k,\eta)|AG|(k,\eta)|\phi|(l,\xi)|\phi|(k-l,\eta-\xi)d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{S_{R}}\frac{|(\tilde{\xi}-\tilde{l}t)(\eta-kt)(\eta\tilde{l}-k\tilde{\xi})|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}A(k,\eta)|AG|(k,\eta)|\phi|(k-\tilde{l},\eta-\tilde{\xi})|\phi|(\tilde{l},\tilde{\xi})d\tilde{\xi}d\eta

\displaystyle=:T_{\phi\to G}+R_{\phi\to G},

where the term

is exactly as the one defined previously upon the identification of

Consequently

|NL_{\phi\to G}^{y}|\leq 2R_{\phi\to G}+T_{\phi\to G}+\mathcal{R}_{\phi\to G}.

In the sequel, we aim at showing the following bounds for each term on the right-hand side of the inequality above:

(52)

\displaystyle R_{\phi\to G}

\displaystyle\lesssim t\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{A}\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\right),

(53)

\displaystyle T_{\phi\to G}

\displaystyle\lesssim t\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{A}\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\right),

(54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\phi\to G}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

With the bounds above at hand, we can directly prove a bound consistent with (

R_{\phi\to G}+T_{\phi\to G}+\mathcal{R}_{\phi\to G}\lesssim t\varepsilon\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{G,\phi}}+t^{\frac{3}{2}}\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{G,\phi}.

Hence, integrating in time and using (

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|NL_{\phi\to G}^{y}|d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{3}{2}}\varepsilon^{3}\leq C\delta\varepsilon^{2},

for a suitable constant

.

It thus remains to prove the bounds (

\bullet

Bound on

Denoting the integrand of

as

\displaystyle\lesssim\textbf{1}_{|\eta,k|\geq t^{2}}\frac{\tfrac{|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|k-l|^{\frac{5}{2}}}}{(1+|\frac{\eta-\xi}{k-l}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|l,\xi|^{2}}\lesssim\textbf{1}_{|\eta,k|\geq t^{2}}\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|k-l|}{|l,\xi|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\textbf{1}_{|\eta,k|\geq t^{2}}\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}|l,\xi|^{3}.

Therefore, by combining these estimates and using (
56
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|\eta l-k\xi||A(k,\eta)-A(l,\xi)|}{|k-l|+|\eta-\xi-(k-l)t|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}(k,\eta)}\right)|l,\xi|^{4}A(k-l,\eta-\xi)e^{10\rho|l,\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

From the bound above, we deduce

(68)

\displaystyle|R_{G\to\phi}^{R}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}m}{m}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

which is consistent with (
64
).

\bullet

Bound on
T_{G\to\phi}
:

Here we want to estimate

\displaystyle T_{G\to\phi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{S_{T}}|\eta l-k\xi||A(k,\eta)-A(l,\xi)||A\phi|(k,\eta)|G|(k-l,\eta-\xi)|\phi|(l,\xi)d\xi d\eta.

Recalling the definition of the weight in (
21
), we split the difference as

\displaystyle A(k,\eta)-A(l,\xi)

\displaystyle=A(l,\xi)(e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}-1)

\displaystyle+A(l,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}\big(\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N}}-1\big)

\displaystyle+A(l,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N}}\big(\frac{m(k,\eta)}{m(l,\xi)}-1\big)

\displaystyle+A(l,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N}}\frac{m(k,\eta)}{m(l,\xi)}\big(\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big).

Furthermore, on
S_{T}
by Lemma
12
iii) we know that there exists a constant
c_{s}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}\lesssim e^{c_{s}\lambda(t)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{s}}.

Denoting the integrand

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T,G\to\phi}

\displaystyle=|\eta l-k\xi|e^{c_{s}\lambda(t)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{s}}|A\phi|(k,\eta)|G|(k-l,\eta-\xi)|A\phi|(l,\xi),

we then split the transport term as

\displaystyle T_{G\to\phi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{\Omega_{T}}\mathcal{I}_{T,G\to\phi}\bigg(\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big|+\big|\frac{m(k,\eta)}{m(l,\xi)}-1\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+e^{-c_{s}\lambda(t)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{s}}|e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}-1|+\big|\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N}}-1\big|\bigg)d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=:T_{G\to\phi,1}+T_{G\to\phi,2}+T_{G\to\phi,3}+T_{G\to\phi,4}.

We proceed by controlling each of the terms above separately.

\diamond
Bound on
T_{G\to\phi,1}
:
For
(t\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\min(|\xi|^{\frac{2}{3}},|\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}}))
or (

and
|\eta|\leq 4|k|

vi) or vii) respectively we get

\displaystyle\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle}{|k,l,\xi,\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}}}e^{C\rho|\eta-\xi,k-l|^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Thus

\displaystyle|\eta l-k\xi|\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big|\lesssim|k,\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{\tilde{C}|\eta-\xi,k-l|^{\frac{1}{3}}},

for some constant
\tilde{C}>0
.
When
t\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\min(|\xi|^{\frac{2}{3}},|\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}})
and
|\eta|\geq 4|k|
, we know that

\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}\lesssim|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{C_{0}|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}},

for some constant
C_{0}>0
.
Thus

\displaystyle|\eta l-k\xi|\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big|\lesssim|\xi|^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{\tilde{C}_{0}|\eta-\xi,k-l|^{\frac{1}{3}}}\lesssim|l,\xi|^{\frac{s}{2}}t^{\frac{3}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{s}{2})}e^{C_{1}|\eta-\xi,k-l|^{\frac{1}{3}}},

for a constant
\tilde{C}_{0},C_{1}>0

\displaystyle|\eta l-k\xi|\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(l,\xi)}-1\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|k,\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}+|l,\xi|^{\frac{s}{2}}t^{\frac{3}{2}(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{s}{2})})e^{\tilde{C}_{1}\rho|\eta-\xi,k-l|^{\frac{1}{3}}},

for a suitable constant
\tilde{C}_{1}>0.
Therefore, since
s>1/3
we obtain

(69)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{G\to\phi,1}&\lesssim\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|AG\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{9}{4}-\frac{3s}{4}}\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|A\Lambda^{-1}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|AG\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3s}{4}}\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

which is consistent with (
65
).

\diamond
Bound on
T_{G\to\phi,2}
:
By definition of
m
, if

we obtain

\displaystyle\min(|k,\eta|,|l,\xi|)

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle|\eta l-k\xi|

\displaystyle\leq\min(|k,\eta|,|l,\xi|)|k-l,\eta-\xi|

\displaystyle\leq t^{2(1-\frac{s}{2})}\min(|k,\eta|^{\frac{s}{2}},|l,\xi|^{\frac{s}{2}})|k-l,\eta-\xi|.

Hence, it is not hard to deduce that

\displaystyle T_{G\to\phi,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2(1-\frac{s}{2})}\|A\Lambda^{-1}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{1-s}\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}},

which is consistent with (

) since
1-s\leq\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3s}{4}
.

\diamond
Bound on
T_{G\to\phi,3}
:
Here we simply use that

\displaystyle\left|e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}-1\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|l,\xi|^{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\eta-\xi,k-l|}{|k,\eta|^{1-s}}e^{c_{s}\lambda(t)|k-l,\eta-\xi|^{s}}.

Thus

(70)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{G\to\phi,3}&\lesssim\|A\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}\phi\|^{2}\|AG\|,\end{split}

which is in agreement with (
65
).

\diamond
Bound on
T_{G\to\phi,4}
:
Since

we obtain

\displaystyle|\tfrac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N}}-1|\lesssim|k-l,\eta-\xi|{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{N-1}}.

and therefore we infer

(71)

\displaystyle T_{G\to\phi,4}\lesssim\|AG\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which is consistent with (
65
).

\bullet

Bound on
\mathcal{R}_{G\to\phi}
:

In this section we bound the remainder of the action from
G
to
\phi

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{G\to\phi}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{S_{\mathcal{R}}}|\eta l-k\xi||A(k,\eta)-A(l,\xi)||A\phi|(k,\eta)|G|(k-l,\eta-\xi)|\phi|(l,\xi)d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\iint\textbf{1}_{S_{\mathcal{R}}}\tfrac{|\eta l-k\xi|}{\sqrt{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}}|A(k,\eta)-A(l,\xi)||A\phi|(k,\eta)|\Lambda_{t}G|(k-l,\eta-\xi)|\phi|(l,\xi)d\xi d\eta.

We can now proceed exactly as done to control

and conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{G\to\phi}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

as desired for (
54
).

4.3.

In this section, we want to bound the remaining nonlinear terms arising from the equation of
\phi

\displaystyle NL_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\langle A\big((\partial_{x}\nabla^{\perp}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\cdot\nabla)\phi\big),A\phi\rangle-\langle A(v^{x}_{0}\partial_{x}\phi_{\neq}),A\phi_{\neq}\rangle.

64

Hence, in the rest of the section we need to control only the term

Our next goal is to prove the following bounds

\displaystyle R_{v_{0}\to\phi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(74)

\displaystyle T_{v_{0}\to\phi}

Hence, overall we get that

\bullet

Bound on

:

\bullet

Bound on

We first split

\displaystyle A(k,\xi)\left(e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|k,\xi|^{s})}-1\right)

\displaystyle+A(k,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|k,\xi|^{s})}\big(\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{N}}-1\big)

\displaystyle+A(k,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|k,\xi|^{s})}\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{N}}\big(\frac{m(k,\eta)}{m(k,\xi)}-1\big)

\displaystyle+A(k,\xi)e^{\lambda(t)(|k,\eta|^{s}-|k,\xi|^{s})}\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{N}}\frac{m(k,\eta)}{m(k,\xi)}\big(\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(k,\xi)}-1\big).

\displaystyle T_{v_{0}\to\phi}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big|\frac{\langle k,\eta\rangle^{N}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{N}}-1\big|\bigg)|A\phi|(k,\eta)|v_{0}^{x}|(\eta-\xi)|A\phi|(k,\xi)d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=T_{v_{0}\to\phi,1}+T_{v_{0}\to\phi,2}+T_{v_{0}\to\phi,3}+T_{v_{0}\to\phi,4}.

For

\displaystyle T_{v_{0}\to\phi,4}

\diamond
Bound on
T_{v_{0}\to\phi,1}
:
For
(t\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\min(|\xi|^{\frac{2}{3}},|\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}}))

, we get

\displaystyle|k|\big|\frac{J(k,\eta)}{J(k,\xi)}-1\big|\lesssim|k|^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{C|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}}\lesssim|k,\eta|^{s}e^{C|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Thus, it is not hard to conclude that

\displaystyle T_{v_{0}\to\phi,1}

\displaystyle\leq t^{1-\frac{3}{2}s}\|\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}A\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{3}{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}.

which is agreement with (

\diamond
Bound on

4.4.
The
NL_{G}
Estimate

To conclude the bounds on the energy functional
\mathcal{E}
, we have to estimate the term
NL_{G}
defined in (
35
).
We want to estimate

\displaystyle NL_{G}

\displaystyle=-\langle A\big(\Delta^{-1}_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}w)_{\neq}\big),AG\rangle-\langle\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}(v\cdot\nabla_{t}\phi)_{\neq},AG\rangle

\displaystyle=:NL_{G,1}+NL_{G,2}.

For the first term, since
v
is divergence-free we use the identity

v\cdot\nabla_{t}w=\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\cdot(v\cdot\nabla_{t}v)=\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\cdot(\nabla_{t}\cdot(v\otimes v)),

so that integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle NL_{G,1}=-\langle A(v\otimes v)_{\neq},\Delta^{-1}_{t}(\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\otimes\nabla_{t})AG\rangle.

Thanks to the properties of the weights, it is not hard to deduce that

(75)

A(k,\eta)\lesssim\sqrt{t}\left(\frac{1}{\langle l,\xi\rangle^{2}}+\frac{1}{\langle k-l,\eta-\xi\rangle^{2}}\right)A(k-l,\eta-\xi)A(l,\xi).

Therefore

\displaystyle|NL_{G,1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\|AG\|_{L^{2}}\|Av\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then, observing that

(76)

|v_{\neq}|\leq|\Lambda_{t}^{-1}w_{\neq}|\leq|\Lambda_{t}G|+|\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}|,

we conclude

\displaystyle|NL_{G,1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\|AG\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Av_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

For the second term we can do the following rough estimate: integrating by parts and using (
75
) and (
76
) we have

\displaystyle|NL_{G,2}|

\displaystyle=|\langle A(v\phi)_{\neq},A\partial_{x}\nabla_{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}G\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\|AG\|_{L^{2}}\|Av\|_{L^{2}}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\|A\phi\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|A\nabla_{t}G\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A\partial_{x}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|AG\|_{L^{2}}\|Av_{0}^{x}\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Appealing to the bootstrap hypotheses (
Bh
), we thus get

|NL_{G}|\lesssim\sqrt{t}\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{G,\phi}+\sqrt{t}\varepsilon^{3}.

Hence, integrating in time we infer that for some constant
C>0
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|NL_{G}|d\tau\lesssim\sqrt{T}\varepsilon^{3}+T^{\frac{3}{2}}\varepsilon^{3}\leq C\delta\varepsilon^{2},

where the last inequality follows since
T\leq\delta\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}
.

4.5.
The
NL_{v_{0}}
estimate

In this section, we bound the nonlinear term for
v_{0}^{x}
defined in (
36
). We first observe that

\displaystyle NL_{v_{0}}=\langle A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}(b_{\neq}^{y}\partial_{y}^{t}b_{\neq}^{x}-v_{\neq}^{y}\partial_{y}^{t}v^{x}_{\neq})_{0},A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}v_{0}^{x}\rangle.

Then, since
\partial_{y}^{t}b_{\neq}^{y}=-\partial_{x}b_{\neq}^{x}
and
\partial_{y}^{t}v_{\neq}^{y}=-\partial_{x}v_{\neq}^{x}
, integrating by parts the identity above, recalling that
b=\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\phi
and
v=\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\psi
, we obtain

\displaystyle NL_{v_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\neq}\partial_{y}^{t}\phi_{\neq})_{0},A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{0}^{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}(\partial_{x}\psi_{\neq}\partial_{y}^{t}\psi_{\neq})_{0},A\langle\partial_{y}\rangle^{-1}\partial_{y}v_{0}^{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=:NL_{v_{0},1}+NL_{v_{0},2}.

The most challenging term is the one involving
\phi_{\neq}
. Indeed, for
\psi_{\neq}
we can use the relation
\psi_{\neq}=G-\partial_{x}\Delta_{t}^{-1}\phi_{\neq}
and, since
G
enjoys dissipative properties, the term involving
\psi_{\neq}
is easier compared to the one for
\phi_{\neq}
. Therefore, we only present the detailed computations for
NL_{v_{0},1}
. A first trivial bound gives

\displaystyle|NL_{v_{0},1}|\lesssim\sum_{k\neq 0}\iint\frac{A(0,\eta)}{\langle\eta\rangle}|\xi-kt||k||\phi_{\neq}(k,\xi)||\phi_{\neq}(-k,\eta-\xi)|\frac{A(0,\eta)}{\langle\eta\rangle}|\partial_{y}v_{0}^{x}|(\eta)d\eta d\xi

In account of Lemma
10
and Lemma
12
, it is not hard to deduce the following inequality

\displaystyle A(0,\eta)\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|k,\xi|^{5}}+\frac{1}{|k,\eta-\xi|^{5}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\sqrt{t}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}q}{q}(-k,\eta-\xi)}+\sqrt{t}\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}q}{q}(k,\xi)}\right)\tilde{A}(-k,\eta-\xi)\tilde{A}(k,\xi).

Notice that the gain of one derivative in
y
is crucial to bound

\frac{|\xi-kt|}{\langle\eta\rangle}\lesssim t\min\{|k,\xi|,|k,\eta-\xi|\}

and thus we never have a dangerous derivative loss.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\left(\frac{1}{|k|}+\frac{1}{|\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}}}+\frac{1}{|j|}\frac{1}{1+|t-\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{j}|}\right)q(t,k,\xi^{\prime})

for some
\xi^{\prime}\in(\eta,\xi)
. For
\xi^{\prime}\in(\eta,\xi)
we obtain by Lemma
8

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+|t-\tfrac{\xi^{\prime}}{j}|}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\frac{1}{1+|t-\frac{\eta}{j}|}\leq\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)+\frac{|j|^{3}}{|\eta|}\right).

Thus, using that
1\leq|j|\lesssim|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle|q(t,k,\eta)-q(t,k,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\eta-\xi||\partial_{\eta}q(t,k,\xi^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\left(\frac{1}{|k|}+\frac{1}{|\eta|^{\frac{2}{3}}}+\frac{|j|^{2}}{|\eta|}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)\right)q(t,k,\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\left(\frac{1}{|k|}+\frac{1}{|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)\right)q(t,k,\xi^{\prime}).

For the cases
t\in I_{j,\eta}\setminus\tilde{I}_{j,\eta}
we can argue in a similar way but the estimates are simpler since we do not have the term
(1+a_{j,\eta}|t-\tfrac{\eta}{j}|)
.

Therefore, thanks to Lemma
8
we conclude that

(86)

\frac{|q(t,k,\eta)-q(t,k,\xi)|}{q(t,k,\eta)}\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\left(\frac{1}{|k|}+\frac{1}{|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)\right)e^{C|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}}

for some constant
C>0
. Putting all the estimates together we finally see that

\displaystyle\frac{|J(t,k,\eta)-J(t,k,\xi)|}{J(t,k,\xi)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\big(\frac{1}{|k|}+\frac{1}{|\eta|^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)\big)e^{C|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta-\xi|\langle\eta-\xi\rangle\big(\frac{1}{|k|^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{t}q}{q}}(\eta)\big)e^{C|\eta-\xi|^{\frac{1}{3}}}

which is the desired result.
∎

Breaking the silence on late-life depression: uncovering the drivers of help-seeking in Türkiye’s ageing population
Authors: Uğur Ercan, Emine Apaydın, Ömer Alkan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDue to the prevalence of physical health issues among older adults, depression remains a significant health concern, which is often overlooked. Understanding the factors influencing the likelihood of seeking psychological support plays an important role in effectively managing depression in later life, where psychological support has a crucial role. This study aims to examine the factors associated with the likelihood of receiving psychological support among individuals aged 60 years and older experiencing depression in Türkiye.MethodsThis study used microdata from the Türkiye Health Surveys conducted by Turkish Statistical Institute in 2016, 2019, and 2022. Gompit, binary logistic regression, and CHAID analyses were used to identify variables associated with psychological support-seeking behavior among older adults with depression.ResultsThe analysis revealed that general health status, illness status, gender, education level, and difficulty in healthcare payment significantly influenced the likelihood of receiving psychological support among older adults with depression.ConclusionThe results achieved in this study indicate that among older adults experiencing depression, women, those with higher educational levels, and those with existing illnesses are more likely to seek psychological support. Based on these results, it is recommended to develop educational programs to raise societal awareness about the importance of psychological support and to inform health policies accordingly.Keywords: Older adults, Depression, Psychological support, Determinants, Binary logistic regression, Türkiye

Introduction

Aging, defined at the biological level as a gradual accumulation of various molecular and cellular damage over time, progressively diminishes physical and mental capacities while increasing disease risk and includes individuals aged 60 years and older [1]. The global older population is projected to increase, reaching 2.1 billion by 2050. Additionally, the number of individuals aged 80 and over is expected to triple between 2020 and 2050, rising to 426 million [2]. Reflecting a similar trend to the global situation, the older population in Türkiye grew by 22.6% between 2017 and 2022. The proportion of older adults in the total population increased from 6,895,385 (8.5%) in 2017 to 8,451,669 (9.9%) in 2022. According to population projections, this percentage is expected to reach 12.9% by 2030, 16.3% by 2040, 22.6% by 2060, and 25.6% by 2080 [3].

Aging results in health problems affecting society, such as chronic diseases, disabilities, and mental disorders [4]. In societies with a predominantly older population, the burden of medical care and social support also increases [5]. Depression, affecting approximately 20% of the geriatric population, is one of the frequently observed mental disorders during this period. Characterized by symptoms such as loss of interest and pleasure, low energy, feelings of guilt or worthlessness, sleep and eating disturbances, and impaired concentration, depression significantly impacts the older adults [6]. According to the latest data from the World Health Organization, an estimated 3.8% of the population, including 5% of adults and 5.7% of individuals over 60 years, live with depression, amounting to approximately 280 million people worldwide [7]. Untreated depression is particularly associated with poor prognoses in comorbidities, especially cardiovascular diseases [8], increases suicide risk, and contributes to reduced quality of life [9]. In advanced age, depression also increases healthcare utilization and adds to the economic and medical burden [10]. However, in many older adults, depression is often overlooked as a natural component of the aging process, leading to inadequate treatment. Studies indicate that older age, female gender, single status, low educational attainment, psychosocial stress, physical illness, polypharmacy, and loss of independence in daily activities are significant risk factors for depression among the older adults [11–13]. Failure to manage these risk factors effectively can pose challenges in providing appropriate treatment and psychological support.

Sociodemographic characteristics influence the management of depression symptoms. Gender, for example, reveals that older women experience higher rates of depression and related symptoms compared to men [14, 15]. Physical activity, a critical factor in preventing and managing numerous health issues, provides support in managing depressive symptoms among older adults when practiced regularly [16, 17]. Physical activity reduces the risk of chronic diseases, including psychological illnesses, and enhances overall well-being [18, 19]. A study demonstrated that high levels of physical activity significantly reduce mental disorders such as depression and anxiety [20]. A systematic review examining the risk factors triggering depression in older adults highlights the significance of physical mobility, independence in daily activities, sleep quality, and chronic illnesses [21].

From the perspective of ecological aging theory, the aging period is a critical phase deeply influenced by the physical environment [22]. Due to decreased physical capabilities, older adults are often more restricted in their social interactions when compared to younger individuals. Moroever, factors such as retirement, loss of family and friends, or living alone reduce social networks, further exacerbating depression risk [23]. In Türkiye, rapid urbanization, rural-urban migration, and the transformation of living spaces directly impact the social environments of older adults [11]. Moreover, the individual’s place of residence was reported as a factor affecting depression development, with results indicating a higher likelihood and symptom severity of depression among older adults residing in urban areas [24]. Studies conducted in Türkiye also support this conclusion; the isolation of older adults from social spaces, parks, and neighborly interactions increases loneliness and isolation, triggering depression [11]. Additionally, it has been reported that symptoms of depression increase in older adults experiencing housing difficulties following natural disasters such as earthquakes, and that loss of belonging is a significant trigger for depression, especially among those migrating from rural areas to urban areas [25]. These findings highlight the importance of age-friendly environmental policies for mental health.

Socioeconomic factors also play a decisive role in the emergence of depression among older adults in Türkiye. Income level, education, and social security directly impact the mental health of older adults. Individuals with low incomes are more prone to mental health problems due to the difficulties they face in meeting their basic needs. Sözeri-Varma [11] emphasizes that depression in old age is related not only to biological but also to socioeconomic conditions. Similarly, Topkaya [26] has shown that older adults with low education levels have limited psychological help-seeking behavior. Research in Türkiye reveals that the prevalence of depression is significantly higher among older adults lacking income security and social support [27]. These findings suggest that socioeconomic inequalities increase the risk of depression.

Cultural factors also play a significant role in understanding depression in old age in Türkiye. In traditional family structures, the older adults are considered respected and cared for; however, with modernization and the proliferation of nuclear families, this status is weakening. This situation increases feelings of loneliness and worthlessness in older individuals. Local studies show that the weakening of the older adults’ role within the family and the loss of social status are closely linked to depression [11, 26]. Furthermore, the perception that men seeking psychological support, in accordance with social norms, is seen as a sign of “weakness,” creates a cultural barrier that limits help-seeking behavior [28]. Negative societal perceptions of old age negatively impact not only the emergence of depression but also the process of seeking treatment.

The 2016–2022 period covered by the study also includes a period in which the COVID-19 pandemic profoundly affected the mental health of older individuals. Curfews and social isolation measures taken during the pandemic increased feelings of loneliness in older individuals, which in turn increased the risk of depression. Studies conducted in Türkiye reveal that depressive symptoms and anxiety levels increased significantly in older individuals during the pandemic period [29]. Similarly, Arpaci, Karatas [30] reported that social isolation in older people negatively affected psychological well-being and that feeling of loneliness was strongly associated with depression. Additionally, family support stood out as a protective factor during the pandemic. Turkish literature has shown that regular communication and online contact with family increases the psychological resilience of older individuals and reduces feelings of loneliness and hopelessness [31]. Aslan and Koçak [32] study also shows that perceived family social support during the pandemic period positively affects coping strategies and life satisfaction in older adults, while a decrease in family support increases loneliness.

Family and friend support plays a critical role in maintaining psychosocial well-being and reducing depressive symptoms in older adults. Social support reduces feelings of loneliness and isolation, increasing life satisfaction and psychological resilience in older adults. Feeney and Collins [33] state that social support strengthens individuals’ capacity to cope with challenges and protects mental health by encouraging positive coping strategies. Studies conducted in Türkiye reveal similar results. For example, Sözeri-Varma [11] emphasized that a lack of social support is a significant determinant of depression in older adults. Kong, Hu [34] demonstrate that family support mediates the relationship between depression and quality of life in older adults.

This study aims to examine the factors influencing the psychological support-seeking behavior of older adults experiencing depression between 2016 and 2022. This study is significant because it can better understand the increasing mental health needs of a rapidly aging population worldwide and inform international health policies for managing depression in old age. While the literature addresses depression and psychological support seeking in older adults on a global scale, studies conducted in different socioeconomic and cultural contexts are limited. This research aims to contribute to the comparative literature on aging, mental health, and access to healthcare, not only in Türkiye but also internationally, by using large-scale national datasets. The research hypothesis is that women, those with higher education levels, those with chronic health conditions, and those experiencing difficulty paying for healthcare are more likely to seek psychological support. In this respect, the study both theoretically expands the literature on aging and mental health and contributes practically to the development of healthcare planning and social policies globally.

Methods

Data source

Microdata from the Turkish Statistical Institute’s (TurkStat) 2016–2019–2022 waves of the Türkiye Health Survey are used in this study. The most recent data from TurkStat’s Türkiye Health Survey is from 2022. This survey, representing the nation, holds significant value as it enables both international and national comparisons. The dataset used in the study underwent a comprehensive data cleaning and validation process. Prior to analysis, all variables were systematically examined for missing values, and no missing data were identified in any of the observational units. Therefore, no specific missing data handling technique (e.g., imputation or deletion methods) was required.

The sampling design of the Türkiye Health Survey employs a two-stage stratified cluster sampling method. Initially, an external stratification criterion distinguishing rural and urban areas was applied (where rural populations are defined as settlements with 20,000 or fewer inhabitants, and urban populations as settlements with more than 20,000). Clusters (blocks) with an average of 100 dwelling addresses made up the sampling units, which were chosen at random according to their size. Household addresses from each cluster were then chosen using random and systematic sampling techniques [35–37]. The sample selection process for inclusion in the study is presented in Fig. 1.

Dependent and independent variables

The dependent variable of this study is the status of receiving psychological support, measured by the question, “Have you received psychological support? (Yes; No).” In the model, individuals who received psychological support are coded as 1, and those who did not are coded as 0.

The independent variables in the study include year (2016, 2019, 2022), gender (female, male), level of education (illiterate/unschooled, primary school, elementary education, high school, university), occupations (unemployed, managers, professionals, technicians or associate professionals, clerical support workers, service or sales workers, skilled agricultural or forestry or fishery workers, craftsmen or related trades workers, plant or machine operators or assemblers, elementary occupations), general health status (very good/good, average, poor/very poor), presence of illness (yes, no), presence of hypertension (yes, no), financial difficulty in healthcare (yes, no), and availability of a reliable close contact (none, 1–2 people, 3 or more).

Statistical analysis

Survey statistics in Stata 15 (Stata Corporation) were used to account for the complex sampling design and weights. Weighted analysis was performed. The characteristics linked to alcohol consumption among people 60 years of age and older were examined in this study using gompit, binary logistic regression, and CHAID analysis.

In addition to the binary logistic regression model, the gompit regression model was applied in this study. The gompit model is particularly appropriate when the probability of the outcome event is relatively low and when the distribution of the error term is expected to be asymmetric [38–40]. In our case, the dependent variable—receiving psychological support among older adults with depression—represents a relatively infrequent behavior in the population, with only 620 cases out of 11,882 respondents (about 5.2%). Under such conditions, the logit and probit models, which assume symmetric distributions of the error term, may not provide the best fit. By contrast, the gompit regression is more suitable for modeling the hazard-like process underlying the decision to seek psychological support, where the probability of occurrence increases monotonically with the explanatory variables. Employing both the logistic and the gompit model not only improves the robustness of the findings but also allows us to assess the consistency of determinants under different distributional assumptions. This dual-model approach strengthens the validity of our results and provides a more nuanced understanding of psychological help-seeking behavior among older individuals in Türkiye.

Decision trees are frequently used by researchers due to their ability to handle different data types (continuous, ordinal, nominal, and binary), simplicity, rapid training process, ease of interpretation, applicability to large datasets, ability to handle missing data, and non-parametric nature, meaning they do not require statistical assumptions about the variables [41, 42].

Numerous algorithms are used as inducers for decision tree creation, including CART, ID3, C4.5, C5.0, QUEST, and CHAID. Each algorithm differs in aspects such as root node selection, criteria for splitting, tree depth, and error minimization, resulting in varied tree structures [42, 43]. The CHAID algorithm was utilized as a decision tree algorithm in this study. Developed by Kass [44], the CHAID algorithm can process continuous, ordinal, nominal, and binary data. Preferred for its ability to handle all data types and rapid tree generation, this inducer employs the chi-square statistic in the splitting process. For each predictor and outcome variable, chi-square values are calculated using contingency Table [44].

In this study, CHAID analysis was employed as an exploratory and descriptive tool to map the hierarchical relationships and interaction effects among factors associated with depression. While CHAID is susceptible to overfitting in predictive modeling contexts, especially when applied without validation mechanisms, this study prioritized interpretability and pattern detection over prediction. To mitigate the risk of overfitting, several built-in control parameters of the CHAID algorithm were applied: a significance level (p-value) threshold of 0.05 was used for node splitting, the minimum node sizes (min node size: 2) were set to ensure sufficient statistical power, and tree depth (depth size: 5) was limited based on theoretical interpretability. Thus, the model is prevented from becoming overly sensitive to complex structures specific to the data set.

Given the gompit regression model in Table 3, older Turkish participants who participated in the research in 2019 (p < 0.05) have a 31% higher probability of receiving psychological support compared to the reference group (2016). The likelihood of obtaining psychological assistance is 56.1% higher for women than for men (p < 0.01). Participants with elementary (p < 0.01), secondary (p < 0.05), high school (p < 0.01), and university (p < 0.01) education levels show increased likelihoods of receiving psychological support by 47.3%, 51.6%, 74.2%, and 102.1%, respectively, when compared to the reference group. This indicates that the likelihood of obtaining psychological assistance increases with increasing educational attainment. Occupation is also associated with psychological support, with managers, professionals, technicians/associate professionals (p < 0.05), and unskilled workers (p < 0.01) showing 57% and 106% lower probabilities of receiving psychological support than non-working individuals, respectively. Individuals with moderate (p < 0.05) and poor/very poor (p < 0.01) general health status are 33.4% and 72.5% more likely to receive psychological support than those with very good/good health, indicating that poorer health status correlates with higher probabilities of seeking psychological support. Additionally, individuals with an illness (p < 0.01) and those with hypertension (p < 0.10) have 58.4% and 17.8% higher likelihoods of receiving psychological support, respectively. Similarly, individuals who experience difficulty affording healthcare have a 34.4% higher likelihood of seeking psychological support than others (p < 0.01). Conversely, the presence of a trusted person decreases the likelihood of seeking psychological support by 16.3% (p < 0.10).

The CHAID analysis indicates that general health status, presence of illness, education level, gender, and difficulty affording healthcare are influential factors in determining the likelihood of older adults seeking psychological support. The decision tree generated from the modeling results is presented in Fig. 2.

Identification of factors associated with psychological support among older adults in Türkiye by using CHAID analysis

The primary variable driving the splits in the CHAID tree - and the most significant factor influencing whether an older adult seeks psychological support - is “general health status.” Among older adults with “very good or good” health status, the likelihood of seeking psychological support is 2.8%; for those with “fair” health, the rate increases to 5.1%, and for those with “poor or very poor” health, it reaches approximately 8%. These findings suggest that better health reduces the likelihood of seeking psychological support, meaning that as older adults’ general health status improves, the likelihood of seeking support declines.

In nodes with older adults in “very good/good” health, the distinguishing factor is the presence of illness. Among older adults with good health who do not have an illness, the rate of those seeking psychological support is 1.45%; however, this rate increases to 3.94% among those with an illness. Therefore, older adults with good health but an illness have a higher probability of seeking psychological support.

In the node containing older adults in good health with an illness, gender emerges as the distinguishing characteristic. Among older men in good health with an illness, the rate of seeking psychological support is 3.02%, while for older women in similar health, this rate increases to 5.01%.

For older adults with “average” health, gender serves as the distinguishing variable. Among older men with fair health, the rate of seeking psychological support is 3.37%, compared to 6.39% among older women with similar health. Within the “average” health node for older men, level of education becomes the distinguishing variable. In the node with participants with no formal education, the rate of seeking psychological support is 0.93%, while it is 3.11% among primary school graduates and 4.97% among those with middle school or higher education. This suggests that as the education level of older men in fair health increases, so does the likelihood of seeking psychological support.

In the node for older women in average health (as with older men), education level is the distinguishing variable. Among older women with no formal education, the rate of seeking psychological support is 4.94%, while for those with primary school education or higher, this rate is 7.56%. This indicates that the likelihood of seeking psychological support among older women in fair health increases with education level.

In the node containing older adults with “poor/very poor” health, difficulty affording healthcare emerges as the distinguishing factor. Among older adults with poor/very poor health who experience difficulty in affording healthcare, the rate of seeking psychological support is 12.08%, compared to 7.17% for those without such difficulty.

Within the node for older adults with poor/very poor health who do not experience difficulty in affording healthcare, level of education is the distinguishing variable. In the node for those with secondary school or less education, the rate of seeking psychological support is 6.83%, while for those with a high school diploma or higher, this rate increases to 12.00%. This indicates that among older adults with poor/very poor health and no difficulty affording healthcare, the likelihood of seeking psychological assistance increases with higher education levels.

In the node for older adults with poor/very poor health who experience difficulty affording healthcare, education level again serves as the distinguishing variable. Among those with no formal education, the rate of seeking psychological support is 8.12%, while for those with elementary education or higher, this rate rises to 17.57%. This node also has the highest rate of older adults seeking psychological support. Thus, for older adults with poor/very poor health and financial difficulty in healthcare, higher education levels increase the likelihood of obtaining psychological assistance.

Discussion

Numerous demographic, individual, and environmental factors influence individuals’ behavior toward seeking psychological support [26]. The results achieved in this study indicate that factors such as year, gender, educational level, occupation, general health status, presence of illness, hypertension, difficulty in affording healthcare, and the availability of a trusted person significantly impact the likelihood of older Turkish individuals with depression seeking psychological support.

This study revealed that, when compared to 2016, older adults’ behaviors toward seeking psychological support increased in 2019. According to data from the Turkish Statistical Institute, the proportion of older adults in Türkiye’s population rose from 8.5% in 2017 to 9.9% in 2022. Additionally, the rate of unhappiness among the older increased from 11% in 2017 to 13.7% in 2022 [3]. Therefore, it is believed that older adults increasingly need psychological support over time.

The results also revealed that women are more likely to seek psychological support than men. Similarly, previous studies in the literature indicated that gender significantly influences the likelihood of seeking psychological assistance, with women showing a greater tendency to do so [26, 46, 47]. This difference can be attributed to gender-based distinctions in help-seeking behaviors, as research shows that women tend to evaluate situations emotionally, whereas men often prefer problem-focused strategies to manage stressful situations [48, 49]. Additionally, societal and cultural pressures are thought to affect women more [50], while traditional norms often view help-seeking among men as a sign of weakness [51].

In this study, educational level emerged as an important factor affecting the tendency to seek psychological support. When compared to individuals with no formal education, those at all educational levels exhibited a higher tendency to seek psychological support. One study conducted with older adults found that psychological well-being and higher educational levels predicted lower mortality risk [52]. This result suggests that individuals with higher education levels may be more inclined to seek psychological support to promote their well-being.

When compared to those not employed, managers, professionals, technicians, associate professionals, and individuals in elementary occupations reported higher rates of seeking psychological support. Literature on socioeconomic status indicates that low income, as a component of lower socioeconomic standing, limits access to healthcare resources and thus impedes improvements in both physical and psychological health [53, 54]. Additionally, individuals without a source of income may prioritize basic needs over psychological needs, potentially leading to lower rates of psychological support-seeking behavior [55].

Possessing any form of illness is another variable that affects older adults’ tendency to seek psychological support. In this study, older adults with any illness and those with hypertension were more likely to seek psychological support. Depression is one of the most common psychological disorders among older adults, particularly those with one or more chronic illnesses [56]. The prevalence of depression among older adults with chronic illnesses ranges from 34.0% to 65.2% [27, 57, 58]. Studies in the literature indicate that older adults with chronic illnesses have a higher likelihood of experiencing psychological distress and severe psychological issues [59–61].

The mental health of older adults is a global health concern that should be taken seriously [62]. Older adults with chronic illnesses often require prolonged use of various medications, which can reduce their quality of life [34]. Moreover, older adults with multiple illnesses need more support and medical services, incur higher healthcare expenses, and have an increased risk of hospital admissions [63]. In advanced age, the frequency of hypertension also increases as blood vessels lose elasticity [64]. According to data from the Turkish Hypertension Prevalence Study, the prevalence of hypertension in Türkiye is 67.9% for those aged 60–69, 85.2% for those aged 70–79, and 76.3% for those aged 80 and older [65]. Additionally, depression alone is a risk factor for the development of hypertension [66]. As a result, it is thought that older adults need to manage depression effectively to control hypertension, which, in turn, may drive them to seek psychological support. Therefore, compared to older persons who define their general health state as very excellent or good, those who rate it as bad or very poor are more likely to seek psychological care.

In the present study, older adults who experienced difficulty affording healthcare expenses showed a higher likelihood of seeking psychological support compared to those without financial difficulties in healthcare. The literature indicates that individuals with low income levels are more likely to experience anxiety, depression, and other mental disorders [67, 68]. Consequently, individuals experiencing greater psychological problems may be more inclined to seek psychological support.

Another variable influencing the tendency to seek psychological support is the availability of trusted persons. This study revealed that older adults with one or two trusted persons were more likely to seek psychological support than those with three or more trusted persons. Numerous studies emphasize the importance of social support in effectively managing health issues. Integrative social support can reduce negative affect, encourage a positive outlook on events, and increase the use of effective coping strategies, thereby alleviating distress [33]. Thus, it can be interpreted that as the number of trusted close contacts increases for individuals with depression, their need for psychological support decreases.

The findings of this study suggest that the factors influencing depression and psychological support seeking among older individuals are not unique to the Turkish context but may also apply to countries with similar sociodemographic structures. Similar trends are particularly observed in countries experiencing rapid population aging, marked socioeconomic inequalities, and structural barriers to accessing healthcare [1]. The literature has reported that low education levels, insufficient income, chronic disease burden, and limited social support networks increase the risk of depression in older adults in these countries [15, 21]. Furthermore, findings that cultural norms restrict men’s psychological support seeking or that women’s help-seeking behaviors are more prevalent are similar not only in Türkiye but also in other societies with strong gender roles [69]. In this respect, the study suggests that socioeconomic, cultural, and environmental factors should be considered in the management of depression in older individuals, not only locally but also globally.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study found that older women with depression were more likely to seek psychological support than their male counterparts. Furthermore, older adults’ likelihood of seeking psychological support increases with increasing education level. The presence of a medical condition is another factor that influences the likelihood of seeking psychological support. Based on the study’s findings, it is crucial to develop targeted policy interventions to prevent depression and increase psychological support seeking among older adults. First, considering that social norms limit help-seeking behavior in men, awareness campaigns and gender-sensitive mental health programs should be designed to reduce stigma and target older men. Furthermore, to facilitate access to mental health services for older individuals with low education and income levels, health literacy education programs should be implemented and supported by local governments and the family health system. Expanding mobile health teams and community-based mental health centers, especially in rural areas, would be an effective strategy to reach these groups. Finally, given that family and friend support have a protective effect on the well-being of older adults, community-based programs (such as age-friendly centers, volunteering, and intergenerational solidarity projects) that strengthen the social interactions of older adults should be encouraged.

This study has several limitations. Depression in older adults was identified based on subjective statements. Additionally, information on the duration of depression or any medical treatment received was not included. A further limitation is that depressive symptoms were measured through a self-reported single-item question in the Türkiye Health Survey. While this approach allows for large-scale analysis, it does not provide the diagnostic precision of standardized clinical instruments such as the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS-15) or the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9). The absence of these validated tools may result in underreporting or misclassification of depressive symptoms, and thus prevalence rates should be interpreted with caution.

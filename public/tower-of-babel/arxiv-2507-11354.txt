Odd-even parity dependent transport in an annular Kitaev chain
Wei Wang 1, Zhen-Gang Zhu 1,2, Gang Su 3,4

Abstract
We investigate the impact of magnetic flux and the odd-even parity of lattice points N N on electron transport in an annular Kitaev chain, with an explanation provided from the energy band perspective. Three transport mechanisms including direct transmission (DT), local Andreev reflection (LAR) and crossed Andreev reflection (CAR) are considered. In particular, the connection configuration of electrodes to different lattice sites is studied, where the case that the two electrodes connected to the sites are aligned along a diameter is called as symmetric connection and otherwise as asymmetric connections. For even N N and asymmetric connection, the vanished LAR and CAR in symmetric connection will emerge as peaks. A more prominent observation is that the symmetry of the two resonant peaks due to DT processes located at ϕ = π / 3 \phi=\pi/3 and ϕ = 2 ​ π / 3 \phi=2\pi/3 for symmetric connection will be broken, and the peak at ϕ = π / 3 \phi=\pi/3 will be largely reduced, where ϕ \phi is the magnetic flux per site. Moreover, the peaks around ϕ = π / 3 \phi=\pi/3 due to LAR and CAR processes grows drastically even larger than that from DT. For LAR and CAR processes, there is no peak around ϕ = 2 ​ π / 3 \phi=2\pi/3 and transmission due to these two processes are completely suppressed for ϕ > π / 2 \phi>\pi/2 . Moreover, it is found that the energy bands vary with ϕ \phi in a period of π \pi and 2 ​ π 2\pi for even or odd N N . These behaviors reflect a complicated competition from DT, LAR and CAR processes and the parity of the lattice number in the Kitaev ring, which will be interested for the quantum device based on Kitaev chain.

INTRODUCTION

Majorana zero modes (MZMs)
Elliott and Franz 2015
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
have received much attention in condensed matter physics recently.
Due to their topological protection property of non-Abelian statistics
Yu et al. 2023
;
Read and Green 2000
;
Halperin et al. 2012
;
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Alicea et al. 2011
and the ability to achieve distributed storage of quantum information
Aghaee et al. 2025
,
they have become extremely unique physical carriers in the field of topological quantum computing, attracting numerous researchers to engage in related research
Leijnse and Flensberg 2012
;
Beenakker 2013
.
Researchers have successfully observed MZMs in various condensed-matter systems, such as peak signals in the semiconductor-superconductor nanowire system (the InSb/Al heterojunction)
Microsoft Quantum et al. 2023
, sharp zero-bias conductance peaks in vortices
Albrecht et al. 2016
;
Kong et al. 2019
;
Kong et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2024
, peak signals in the topological insulator-superconductor interface system
Deng et al. 2016
;
Jeon et al. 2017
;
Mourik et al. 2012
;
Wang et al. 2015
.
From the theoretical perspective, the Kitaev chain
Kitaev 2001
, as an important theoretical model capable of supporting the existence of MZMs, has triggered extensive research enthusiasm in the fields of condensed-matter physics and quantum computing since its proposal
Fu and Kane 2008
;
Lutchyn et al. 2010
;
Jiang et al. 2011
;
Stern and Berg 2019
;
Dutta and Dutta 2017
;
Pedrocchi et al. 2012
;
Rahmani et al. 2019
;
Hua et al. 2019
. In the Kitaev chain, by appropriately tuning the electron hopping energy, MZMs can be generated at both ends of the chain. These zero-energy modes fully exhibit the characteristics of Majorana fermions. However, most current research focuses on open-boundary conditions, and how to maintain the delocalization characteristics of Majorana zero-energy modes in finite-size or specific geometric structures has gradually become a research hotspot and attracted great attention.

Based on this, this paper takes the annular Kitaev chain
Miao et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2023
, a typical geometric structure model, as the research object, and uses the Landauer-Büttiker formula and the nonequilibrium Green’s function method to conduct in-depth study on the annular Kitaev chain. The aim of this study is to reveal the regulatory mechanisms of the lattice-point parity of the annular Kitaev chain on the coupling strength of Majorana edge states, the topological protection properties of the energy spectrum, and the transport mechanism. The conclusions drawn in this paper are expected to provide a solid theoretical basis for the experimental design of observable parity-dependent transport characteristics, and contribute to the further development of experimental research related to topological quantum computing.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Sec.
II
, the theoretical model and the tight-binding Hamiltonian of the annular Kitaev chain are introduced. Here, we define the key parameters and interactions within the model, which serve as the foundation for subsequent analysis. In Sec.
III
, the nonequilibrium Green’s function method in conjunction with the Landauer-Büttiker formula is employed to investigate the transmission coefficient of the model under the conditions of odd and even lattice points. Through a series of calculations and derivations, we analyze the intricate relationships between the lattice-point parity, the electronic states, and the transmission characteristics. This allows for an in-depth understanding of how the transport properties of the annular Kitaev chain are affected by
N
. Finally, in Sec.
IV
, a concise summary is provided.

Now, we focus on the impact of the parity (odd or even) of

When the electrodes are asymmetrically coupled to lattice of the ring, in addition to free electrons, a small number of Cooper pairs also serve as transmitted particles.
In the context of LAR, an electron can interact with another electron at the interface of the electrode and superconductor forming a Cooper pair that enters into the superconductor, meanwhile, a hole propagates along the opposite direction, as shown in Fig.

Regarding CAR, the asymmetry between the left and right electrodes provides an opportunity for electrons from different electrodes to combine and form a Cooper pair that enters into the superconductor, as illustrated in Fig.

IV
SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

In this paper, we study the variation of the transport coefficients in an annular Kitaev chain as a function of the magnetic field and the total lattice number of Kitaev ring. By adjusting
N
in the ring, we find that when
N
is odd, the
T-\phi
diagram is not symmetric about
\phi=\pi/2
, while
T-\phi
diagram is symmetric for even
N
.
To be specific, there are two transmission peaks of
T_{\text{DT}}
for even
N
which has been predicted previously,
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
are suppressed. We find in this work that in even
N
case,
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
developed finite values when the electrodes are asymmetrically connected to the ring.
Another observation is that the two peaks of
T_{\text{DT}}
located at
\phi=\pi/3
and
2\pi/3
becomes asymmetric for odd
N
Kitaev ring.
There emerge peaks for
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
at
\phi=\pi/3
, while no peak exists around
2\pi/3
. In fact, it is observed that
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
are quite suppressed around
2\pi/3
.
These observations may be understood by the gap opening or closing around
\phi=\pi/3
and
\phi=2\pi/3
. It is always gapless at
2\pi/3
which may be the reason for the disappearance of
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
and a robust peak of
T_{\text{DT}}
.
Moreover, the energy gap at
\phi=\pi/3
is opened and closed with varying
N
from odd to even. As a consequence,
T_{\text{LAR}}
and
T_{\text{CAR}}
emerge peaks for odd
N
but disappear for even
N
(symmetric connection of electrodes). As can be seen from our results, near the magnetic flux phase
\phi=\pi/3
, in the case
N
is an even number, DT is the most optimal; while in the case
N
is an odd number, LAR and CAR are the most optimal.
We conclude that the parity of Kitaev ring lattice number dramatically determine the quantum transport through such a ring system.
It is expected to provide solid theoretical support for the experimental design of observable parity-dependent transport characteristics.

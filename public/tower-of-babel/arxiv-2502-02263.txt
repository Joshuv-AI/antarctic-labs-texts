Asymptotic solution for three-dimensional reaction-diffusion-advection equation with periodic boundary conditions
Aleksei Liubavin, Mingkang Ni, Ye Zhang, Dmitrii Chaikovskii

Abstract
In this study, we investigate the dynamics of moving fronts in three-dimensional spaces, which form as a result of in-situ combustion during oil production. This phenomenon is also observed in other contexts, such as various autowave models and the propagation of acoustic waves. Our analysis involves a singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion-advection type initial-boundary value problem of a general form. We employ methods from asymptotic theory to develop an approximate smooth solution with an internal layer. Using local coordinates, we focus on the transition layer, where the solution undergoes rapid changes. Once the location of the transition layer is established, we can describe the solution across the full domain of the problem. Numerical examples are provided, demonstrating the high accuracy of the asymptotic method in predicting the behaviors of moving fronts. Keywords Singular perturbed PDE, Quasi-linear reaction-diffusion-advection equation, Moving front

II
Assumptions and local coordinates

The construction of an asymptotic solution requires the introduction of several necessary conditions.

Condition II.1
.

Assume that
u_{init}(x,y,z,\mu)=U_{n-1}(x,y,z,0)+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{n})

u_{init}

(x,y,0,\mu)=u^{0}(x,y)

From Condition

Condition II.2

For the partial differential equation

produces a unique solution

Condition II.3
.

Define the correlations

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{G_A^{(\mp)}(x_1,y_1,z_1) - G_A^{(\mp)}(x_2,y_2,z_2)}\leqslant K_{A}\sqrt{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}+(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{G_B^{(\mp)}(x_1,y_1,z_1) - G_B^{(\mp)}(x_2,y_2,z_2)}\leqslant K_{B}\sqrt{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}+(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

The main focus of this paper is centered around examining instances where the solution to the problem described in equation (
I.1

divides the domain

into two parts at any given point in time

\bar{D}^{(-)}=\{(x,y,z):x\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R},z\in[0,h(x,y,t)]\}

Furthermore, our methodology assumes that it is possible to determine solution

(II.3)

As our analysis focuses on the neighborhood around the transition point, we simplify it by introducing the local coordinates

defined by

The quantities

\displaystyle\alpha_{x}(h)=\dfrac{h_{x}}{\sqrt{1+h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}},\quad\alpha_{y}(h)=\dfrac{h_{y}}{\sqrt{1+h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{z}(h)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+h_{x}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}}}.

The values

, and

The next step in our preparations involves redefining the differential operator in the new coordinates

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{1}{\mu}\quantity(\pdv[2]{u}{\xi} + \alpha_z(A(l,m,h)h_x + B(l,m,h)h_y + h_t + u)\pdv{u}{\xi})

Dividing the second equation of (

The verification of the existence of such a solution is usually performed numerically in practice, or alternatively, we can apply Theorem

Asymptotic representations

Theorem VI.2
.

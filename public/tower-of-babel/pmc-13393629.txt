An integrated pathway mutation and clinical model predicts survival in unresectable HCC with hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab
Authors: Shuangyan Tang, Hongjie Cai, Zhiqiang Wu, Shuqin Huang, Lihua Zhao, Lijia Wu, Longlong Gong, Wenbo Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTriple combination therapy consisting of hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), lenvatinib, and tislelizumab has shown encouraging clinical efficacy in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). However, robust prognostic models to predict treatment outcomes remain lacking.MethodsA total of 83 patients with unresectable HCC receiving HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab were enrolled to develop a prognostic prediction model, which was subsequently validated in an independent cohort of 26 patients. Underlying mechanisms were investigated using targeted exome sequencing and RNA sequencing.ResultsWe assessed the cumulative impact of gene mutations and identified three signaling pathways associated with progression-free survival (PFS), namely the Hedgehog, ErbB, and focal adhesion pathways, in patients undergoing triple combination therapy. An integrated prognostic model incorporating these PFS-related pathways and key clinical risk factors-aspartate aminotransferase, Child-Pugh class, and presence of metastasis-was established and validated. Mechanistic analyses demonstrated enrichment of angiogenesis- and hypoxia-related signatures, along with increased immune cell infiltration in responders, providing a biological basis for the observed therapeutic efficacy.ConclusionThis integrated prognostic model, combining pathway-level genomic alterations with clinical risk factors, enables reliable prediction of treatment outcomes in patients with unresectable HCC receiving HAIC, lenvatinib, and tislelizumab. It may serve as a valuable tool for guiding personalized therapeutic decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16286-7.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the sixth most prevalent malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1, 2]. Owing to its insidious onset and lack of early clinical symptoms, a substantial proportion of patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, thereby precluding curative interventions [3]. For patients with advanced HCC, systemic therapies-particularly tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as sorafenib [4] and lenvatinib [5]-have long constituted the standard of care.

In recent years, the advent of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) has markedly expanded therapeutic options and improved clinical outcomes in advanced HCC [6, 7]. Notably, the IMbrave150 trial established the combination of atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1) and bevacizumab (anti-angiogenic agent), referred to as the “T + A” regimen, as a first-line treatment by demonstrating superior overall survival compared with sorafenib [8]. Building on this progress, emerging studies have explored a triple combination strategy integrating lenvatinib, PD-1 inhibitors, and hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC). This regimen has shown manageable toxicity and has been associated with significantly prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) relative to the T + A regimen in patients with advanced HCC [9–12]. Despite these encouraging results, clinical outcomes remain heterogeneous. Retrospective data from our cohort indicated approximately half of patients with unresectable HCC treated with HAIC in combination with lenvatinib and tislelizumab experienced a PFS of less than 4.4 months [13]. Similarly, Guo’s group reported a median PFS of 8 months in patients with HCC and extrahepatic metastases receiving the same regimen [14]. These findings underscore the variability in treatment response and highlight the limitations of current clinical parameters in predicting therapeutic benefit.

In recent years, increasing attention has been directed toward the relationship between tumor genomic alterations and response to immunotherapy [15–18]. Accumulating evidence indicates that mutations affecting key biological pathways may serve as robust biomarkers for predicting the efficacy of ICB therapy [19–21]. In particular, the pathway mutation perturbation score—an integrative metric that captures the cumulative impact of gene mutations within signaling pathways—has demonstrated prognostic value in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and melanoma treated with ICB [22]. These observations provide a strong rationale for investigating the role of pathway-level mutational perturbations in unresectable HCC patients receiving the combination of HAIC, lenvatinib, and tislelizumab. Although overall survival (OS) is widely regarded as the gold standard endpoint for evaluating clinical benefits in oncology, it may be confound by subsequent lines of therapy following disease progression in patients with unresectable HCC. In contrast, PFS more directly reflects the immediate therapeutic efficacy of a given regimen and is therefore a more suitable endpoint for assessing the clinical benefit of this triple combination therapy. Accordingly, integrating pathway mutation perturbation with established clinical risk factors may provide a more comprehensive framework for predicting PFS and stratifying patients who are most likely to benefit from this therapeutic strategy in unresectable HCC.

In summary, reliable biomarkers for predicting the response to HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab in patients with unresectable HCC remain lacking, largely due to the limited scope of existing research. To address this gap, the present study aims to systematically evaluate the cumulative impact of gene mutations within signaling pathways and to develop a predictive model for PFS by integrating pathway-level mutational features with clinicopathological characteristics. Furthermore, we seek to explore the biological mechanisms underlying this predictive framework, thereby providing insights into treatment response and facilitating more precise patient stratification in unresectable HCC undergoing triple combination therapy.

Materials and methods

Patient cohorts and clinical samples

A retrospective cohort of patients with unresectable HCC who received HAIC in combination with lenvatinib and tislelizumab was enrolled at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University between April 19, 2019 and October 21, 2022. Eligible patients met the following inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) diagnosis of unresectable HCC; (3) Child-Pugh class A or B liver function; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2; and (5) presence of at least one measurable lesion according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST). Patients who had received prior treatment for unresectable HCC or other systemic therapies were excluded.

To validate the prognostic model, an independent prospective cohort of patients with unresectable HCC receiving the triple combination therapy was enrolled between November 1, 2022 and October 30, 2023. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (No. 2019 − 239). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants for the collection of tissue and blood samples for research purposes and genomic profiling.

The treatment protocol for HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab has been described in detail in our previous study [13, 23]. Briefly, HAIC was administered every three weeks. The regimen included oxaliplatin (130 mg/m², administered from hour 0 to 2 on Day 1), leucovorin (200 mg/m², administered from hour 2 to 4 on Day 1), and fluorouracil (400 mg/m² as an intravenous bolus over 15 min, followed by 2400 mg/m² administered continuously over 46 h on Days 1–2). Upon completion of HAIC, the catheter and sheath were removed. In addition, patients received intravenous tislelizumab once every three weeks and oral lenvatinib daily (12 mg for body weight ≥ 60 kg; 8 mg for body weight < 60 kg).

Study design

Gene profiling of unresectable HCC samples was performed using DNA-based next-generation sequencing (NGS). Pathway mutation perturbation scores were calculated using the R package PMAPscore [22]. In the retrospective cohort, PFS-related pathways were identified through univariate Cox proportional hazards analysis followed by stepwise Cox regression. Based on these results, a gene-derived risk score (GeneScore) was constructed to quantify the impact of pathway-level mutational perturbations on PFS in patients with unresectable HCC. In parallel, PFS-associated clinical risk factors were identified using the same statistical approach. A nomogram-based prognostic model for PFS was subsequently developed by integrating the GeneScore with significant clinical variables in the retrospective cohort and was further validated in the independent prospective cohort. To gain mechanistic insights, multi-level analyses were conducted, including DNA mutation profiling, transcriptomic characterization, and protein localization assessments in patients receiving the triple combination therapy.

DNA extraction, NGS, variant calling and pathway mutation perturbation scores calculation

To identify somatic single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) and insertion/deletions (indels), formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissues were subjected to targeted sequencing, with matched peripheral blood samples serving as germline controls. Genomic DNA was extracted from FFPE specimens using the Black PREP FFPE DNA Kit (Analytik Jena, Germany), while control DNA was isolated from peripheral blood lymphocytes using the Tiangen Whole Blood DNA Kit (Tiangen). Peripheral blood lymphocytes were obtained by centrifugation of whole blood at 1600 × g for 10 min at room temperature. Extracted genomic DNA was fragmented to 150–200 bp using a Covaris M220 focused ultrasonicator (Covaris), followed by library construction using the KAPA HTP Library Preparation Kit for the Illumina platform (KAPA Biosystems). Target enrichment was performed using the NimbleGen SeqCap EZ Library system (Roche) with a customized panel covering 769 cancer-related genes. Sequencing was conducted on an Illumina HiSeq X-Ten platform [24], in accordance with the manufacturers’ protocols. Somatic SNVs and indels were identified using VarScan2 (v2.4.2). Variants were retained based on the following criteria: (i) sequencing depth > 50× in control samples and > 100× in tumor samples; (ii) mutant allele frequency > 2%; (iii) at least two supporting reads for the mutant allele; (iv) localization within exonic regions; and (v) allele frequency < 0.5% in population databases, including the Exome Aggregation Consortium (ExAC) and the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD). The resulting high-confidence mutation set was subsequently used to calculate the pathway mutation perturbation score (PMPscore) using the R package PMAPscore.

GeneScore calculation

Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed using the survival R package to identify prognostic pathways. Stepwise Multivariate Cox regression analysis was then used to identify the candidate pathways that were used to establish the prognostic GeneScore. The GeneScore was calculated as follows: GeneScore = ∑(Coefi ×Expi) where Coefi and Expi represent the risk coefficient and signature pathway value, respectively.

Gene set enrichment analysis and TME immune landscape comparation

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed by clusterProfiler package. The gene set for the targeted signaling pathway of Lenvatinib, such as angiogenesis and hypoxia, comes from MSigDB database, whereas immunologically relevant gene list from the ImmPort database (https://www.immport.org/shared/genelists) [25, 26]. The abundance of tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs) was quantified using the single-sample (ss) GSEA in the Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) package. The anticancer immune response (cancer immunity cycle) [27] was also evaluated between the different risk groups.

Nomogram construction and validation

The nomogram prediction model was constructed based on the GeneScore and PFS relative clinical risk factors using the RMS package. The calibration and ROC curves were used to evaluate the prediction probability and reliability of the nomogram model. The decision curve analysis (DCA) was then performed to assess the clinical performance and net benefit of the nomogram. The OverallScore for the PFS prediction score was calculated as follows: OverallScore = 0.94*GeneScore + 0.0049*AST + 0.62*Child-Pugh + 0.44*Metastasis. The validation of OverallScore for PFS prediction was performed in the prospective unresectable HCC cohort with the triple combination therapy.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.1.3). Continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, while categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed with the log-rank test. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patients characteristics

A total of 83 patients with unresectable HCC who received the triple combination therapy were included in the study. The majority of patients were male (81/83, 97.6%), with only 2 patients (2.4%) being female. According to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system, 24 patients (28.9%) were classified as stages A or B, whereas 59 patients (71.1%) were classified as stage C. Based on the TNM staging system, 23 patients (27.7%) were at stages I-II, and 60 patients (72.3%) were at stage III. Approximately 60% of patients were aged ≥ 50 years. Regarding viral status, 85.5% of patients were hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positive, and 22.5% were hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg) positive. Notably, 96.4% (80/83) of patients received antiviral therapy for HBV. Additional clinical characteristics of the cohort are summarized in Table 1, and the study flowchart is presented in Fig. 1.

Pathway mutation perturbation impacted PFS

Using the PMAPscore package, pathway mutation perturbation scores were calculated based on DNA mutation profiles derived from baseline biopsy specimens of patients with unresectable HCC undergoing triple combination therapy. Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to assess the association between PFS and perturbation scores across 123 signaling pathways (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Table 1). Among these, five pathways with p < 0.1 were identified as potentially associated with PFS (Fig. 2B). Subsequent stepwise multivariate Cox regression analysis further refined these candidates, identifying three key PFS-related pathways: the Hedgehog signaling pathway, the ErbB signaling pathway, and focal adhesion (p < 0.1, Fig. 2C). The gene signature corresponding to these pathways comprised 31 genes (Supplementary Table 2).

To quantify the combined impact of pathway-level mutations on PFS, a composite GeneScore was constructed using the regression coefficients derived from the multivariate Cox model, defined as: GeneScore = 0.18 × Hedgehog signaling pathway − 0.33 × ErbB signaling pathway + 0.25 × focal adhesion. The distribution of GeneScore among all patients is illustrated in Fig. 2D. Patients were stratified into low- and high-risk groups according to the median GeneScore (cutoff = 0). Notably, patients in the high GeneScore group exhibited elevated perturbation scores in the Hedgehog signaling pathway and focal adhesion, whereas higher perturbation scores in the ErbB signaling pathway were observed in the low GeneScore group (Fig. 2E).

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high GeneScore group experienced significantly shorter PFS compared with those in the low GeneScore group following triple combination therapy (hazard ratio [HR], 2.12; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.08–4.16; p = 0.012; Fig. 2F). Consistently, the objective response rate (ORR) was higher in the low GeneScore group (65.4%) than in the high GeneScore group (53.6%) within this cohort (Fig. 2G).

GeneScore combined with clinical risk factors predicts PFS

To comprehensively evaluate treatment outcomes, we integrated signaling pathway mutation perturbations into a composite GeneScore. In parallel, PFS-related clinical risk factors were identified in the retrospective cohort. As summarized in Table 2, GeneScore, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), Child–Pugh classification, and metastatic status were all significantly associated with PFS. Based on these four variables, a nomogram was constructed to predict PFS (Fig. 3A). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated strong predictive performance, with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.860, 0.876, and 0.897 for 9-, 18-, and 24-month PFS, respectively (Fig. 3B). The mean AUC for PFS prediction within two years was 0.846 (Fig. 3C). Calibration plots for 6-, 9-, and 12-month PFS further showed excellent agreement between predicted and observed outcomes (Fig. 3D).

Identifying of biological processes and immune microenvironment associated with clinical benefit

To elucidate the mechanisms underlying the predictive performance of the OverallScore for PFS, we first examined the relationships between clinical risk factors (AST level, Child-Pugh class, and tumor metastasis) and pathway mutation perturbations (GeneScore). While these clinical variables are well-established prognostic indicators, our analysis primarily focused on uncovering the biological basis of GeneScore in PFS prediction.

Stratification by GeneScore revealed that multiple clinical features, including age, AST levels, and BCLC stage, were associated with patient prognosis (Supplementary Fig. 1). To further characterize the molecular differences between groups, we analyzed the somatic mutation landscape. The mutation frequency of TP53 was higher in the low GeneScore group (60.4%, 32/53) compared with the high GeneScore group (40.0%, 12/30). In contrast, CTNNB1 mutations were markedly enriched in the high GeneScore group (53.3%, 17/30) relative to the low GeneScore group (3.8%, 2/53) (Fig. 4B and C). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) further demonstrated that hallmark pathways related to angiogenesis and hypoxia were significantly enriched in the low GeneScore group (Fig. 4D).

Given the critical role of the tumor immune microenvironment in immunotherapy response, we next assessed immune-related features in this cohort. Patients in the low GeneScore group exhibited significantly higher tumor immunogenicity (Fig. 4E). Consistently, GSEA revealed enrichment of immune-related pathways, including antigen processing and presentation, B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling, and chemokine signaling pathways, in the low GeneScore group (Fig. 4F and Supplementary Table 4), indicating enhanced immune activation. Furthermore, gene set variation analysis (GSVA) across 28 immune cell subtypes showed that memory effector CD4+ T cells, activated CD4+ T cells, and Th2 cells were significantly more abundant in the low GeneScore group compared with the high GeneScore group (Fig. 4G). To further validate these findings, we analyzed an external immunotherapy cohort treated with PD-1 inhibitors [28]. Using the same cutoff value, patients in the high GeneScore group exhibited significantly shorter PFS (HR, 3.94; 95% CI, 1.4–11.09; p < 0.001; Fig. 4H).

Discussion

Accumulating evidence from retrospective studies has demonstrated that the combination of hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), and immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) confers superior survival benefits in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) compared with regimens involving TKIs plus ICIs or TKIs alone [29–31]. These findings are consistent with our previous reports [12, 13]. In the present study, we developed and validated a prognostic model that integrates pathway mutation perturbation with clinicopathological factors to predict progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with unresectable HCC receiving HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab.

We identified three key signaling pathways associated with PFS-the Hedgehog signaling pathway, the ErbB signaling pathway, and focal adhesion-and incorporated them into a composite GeneScore reflecting pathway-level mutational perturbations. By integrating GeneScore with clinical risk factors, including AST, Child–Pugh classification, and metastatic status, we established a robust prognostic model that demonstrated strong predictive performance and was further validated in an independent prospective cohort. Mechanistically, favorable outcomes in low-risk patients were associated with enrichment of angiogenesis- and hypoxia-related signatures, as well as increased infiltration of immune cells, particularly CD4⁺ T cells, highlighting the interplay between molecular alterations and the tumor immune microenvironment.

The three identified pathways are well known to play critical roles in tumorigenesis, progression, and metastasis [32–34]. The ErbB signaling pathway encompasses key oncogenic cascades, including PI3K/AKT and RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK, which regulate tumor proliferation, survival, and metastasis [33]. Dysregulation of this pathway is frequently observed in HCC and contributes to disease progression. Similarly, activation of the Hedgehog signaling pathway promotes tumor growth in HCC [35]. Sonic hedgehog (Shh), a key ligand in this pathway, is detectable in approximately 60% of HCC cases, and downstream targets such as PTCH1 and GLI1 are expressed in more than half of tumors, indicating widespread pathway activation [36]. Inhibition of Hedgehog signaling has been shown to suppress tumor growth in liver malignancies [37]. Focal adhesion signaling, which mediates interactions between tumor cells and the extracellular matrix through components such as integrins and focal adhesion kinase (FAK), plays a pivotal role in cell adhesion, migration, and invasion [38]. Aberrant activation of this pathway has been closely linked to tumor metastasis and poor prognosis in HCC [39]. Collectively, dysregulation of these pathways may drive aggressive tumor behavior, explaining the association between high GeneScore and poor clinical outcomes.

At the molecular level, we observed a markedly higher frequency of CTNNB1 mutations in high-risk patients, suggesting an immune-excluded phenotype associated with reduced responsiveness to immunotherapy [40, 41]. Mechanistically, CTNNB1 activation has been shown to upregulate matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9), thereby impairing CD8⁺ T-cell activation and infiltration [42]. In addition, CTNNB1-mutant HCC has been linked to altered chemokine signaling, such as increased CCL5 expression, which disrupts immune-mediated tissue repair processes [43]. Consistent with these findings, our results demonstrated reduced tumor immunogenicity and diminished immune cell infiltration—particularly activated CD4⁺ T cells—in high-risk patients. Given the established role of CD4⁺ T cells in enhancing antitumor responses and improving chemotherapy efficacy [44], their reduced presence may contribute to both diminished immunotherapy benefit and suboptimal response to HAIC in this subgroup.

Beyond tumor-intrinsic factors, viral status may also influence treatment outcomes. Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, which was highly prevalent in our cohort, has been shown to modulate immune responses and impact the efficacy of anti–PD-1 therapies in combination with lenvatinib [45, 46]. Furthermore, lenvatinib exerts antitumor effects by targeting multiple tyrosine kinases and inhibiting angiogenesis [47]. Our GSEA results indicated that angiogenesis and hypoxia-related pathways were enriched in low-risk patients, suggesting that this subgroup may be more sensitive to anti-angiogenic therapy [48]. Taken together, these findings provide a biological rationale for the improved outcomes observed in low-risk patients receiving the triple combination therapy.

The prognostic model developed in this study, which integrates pathway mutation perturbation with key clinical variables, demonstrated strong predictive capability for PFS and effectively stratified patients according to therapeutic benefit. This model may facilitate personalized treatment decision-making by identifying patients most likely to benefit from HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the model was validated in a prospective cohort, the sample size was relatively small, and further validation in large, multicenter prospective studies is warranted. Second, the mechanistic roles of the identified pathways, including Hedgehog, ErbB, and focal adhesion signaling, require further experimental investigation. Third, this study primarily included HBV-related HCC, and the applicability of the model to other etiologies—such as HCV-related HCC, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), or alcohol-related liver disease (ALD)—remains to be determined. While GeneScore reflects pathway alterations associated with tumor proliferation and metastasis, differences in etiological backgrounds may influence the relative contribution of clinical variables, necessitating further validation in diverse populations.

Additionally, given the challenges associated with obtaining tumor tissue from patients with unresectable HCC, we explored the feasibility of applying this model using blood-based samples in a prospective cohort. Liquid biopsy approaches, including circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis, offer a minimally invasive alternative for genomic profiling [49] and may further enhance the clinical utility of our model.

In conclusion, we developed and validated a prognostic model that integrates pathway mutation perturbation with clinical risk factors to predict PFS in patients with unresectable HCC undergoing HAIC combined with lenvatinib and tislelizumab. This model provides a practical tool for risk stratification and has important implications for optimizing individualized therapeutic strategies in this patient population.

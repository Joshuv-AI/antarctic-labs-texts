Cosmological implications and causality in f ⁡ ( R , L m , T ) f(R,L_{m},T) gravity theory with observational constraints


Abstract
In the generalized matter-geometry coupling theory, we investigate the physical characteristics and causality of some new cosmological models for a flat, homogeneous, and isotropic spacetime filled with stiff, radiation, dust, and curvature fluid sources. We obtain a particular cosmological model corresponding to each source fluid, called Models I, II, III, and IV, respectively. We make observational constraints on each model using the joint analysis of 31 31 Cosmic Chronometer (CC) Hubble dataset and 1701 1701 Pantheon+SH0ES datasets to estimate the current values of model parameters. Using these statistical results, we have analyzed the information criteria, effective EoS parameter, causality of the models, and viability of this generalized gravity theory. Subsequently, we investigate the effective equation of state and deceleration parameter for each model. We found that all models in the late-time universe exhibit transit-phase acceleration, and Models I and II show both the early as well as late-time accelerating phase of the expanding universe. We found the current values of the deceleration parameter in the range − 0.8857 ≤ q 0 ≤ − 0.4279 -0.8857\leq q_{0}\leq-0.4279 with transition redshift 0.4867 ≤ z t ≤ 0.839 0.4867\leq z_{t}\leq 0.839 and the effective EoS parameter in the range − 0.9238 ≤ ω e ​ f ​ f ≤ − 0.6186 -0.9238\leq\omega_{eff}\leq-0.6186 . We analyzed the square sound speed condition c s 2 ≤ c 2 c_{s}^{2}\leq c^{2} for each model.

Field equations in
f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity theory

We consider the action for generalized matter-geometry coupling
f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity theory in the following form
[
79
]

I=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int{f(R,L_{m},T)\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x}+\int{L_{m}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x},

(1)

where
f(R,L_{m},T)
is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar,
R
, the trace
T
of the stress-energy momentum tensor of the matter,
T_{ij}
, and of the matter Lagrangian density
L_{m}
. We define the stress-energy momentum tensor of the matter as
[
86
]

T_{ij}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}L_{m})}{\delta{g^{ij}}},

(2)

and its trace by
T=g^{ij}T_{ij}
, respectively. By assuming that the Lagrangian density
L_{m}
of matter depends only on
the metric tensor components
g_{ij}
, and not on its derivatives, we obtain

T_{ij}=g_{ij}L_{m}-2\frac{\partial{L_{m}}}{\partial{g^{ij}}}.

(3)

By varying the action
I
of the gravitational field with respect to the metric tensor components
g^{ij}
provides the following relationship

\delta{I}=\int\left[{f_{R}\delta{R}+f_{T}\frac{\delta{T}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}\delta{g^{ij}}+f_{L_{m}}\frac{\delta{L_{m}}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}\delta{g^{ij}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}f\delta{g^{ij}}}-8\pi T_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}\right]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x,

(4)

where we have denoted
f_{R}=\partial{f}/\partial{R}
,
f_{T}=\partial{f}/\partial{T}
and
f_{L_{m}}=\partial{f}/\partial{L_{m}}
, respectively. For the variation of the Ricci scalar, we obtain

\delta{R}=\delta(g^{ij}R_{ij})=R_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}+g^{ij}(\nabla_{\lambda}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{ij}-\nabla_{j}\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{i\lambda}),

(5)

where
\nabla_{\lambda}
is the covariant derivative with respect to the symmetric connection
\Gamma
associated to the metric
g
. The variation of the Christoffel symbols yields

\delta{\Gamma^{\lambda}_{ij}}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\alpha}(\nabla_{i}\delta{g_{j\alpha}}+\nabla_{j}\delta{g_{\alpha i}}-\nabla_{\alpha}\delta{g_{ij}}),

(6)

and the variation of the Ricci scalar provides the expression

\delta{R}=R_{ij}\delta{g^{ij}}+g_{ij}\Box\delta{g^{ij}}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\delta{g^{ij}}.

(7)

Therefore, for the variation of the action of the gravitational field, we obtain

\delta{R}=\int\left[f_{R}R_{ij}+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})f_{R}+f_{T}\frac{\delta(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta})}{\delta{g^{ij}}}+f_{L_{m}}\frac{\delta{L_{m}}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}f-8\pi T_{ij}\right]\delta{g^{ij}}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x.

(8)

We define the variation of
T
with respect to the metric tensor as

\frac{\delta(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta})}{\delta{g^{ij}}}=T_{ij}+\Theta_{ij},

(9)

where

\Theta_{ij}=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta{g^{ij}}}=L_{m}g_{ij}-2T_{ij},

(10)

for the perfect-fluid matter source.

Taking
\delta{I}=0
, we obtain the field equations of
f(R,L_{m},T)
gravity model as

f_{R}R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}[f-(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})L_{m}]g_{ij}+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})f_{R}=\left[8\pi+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})\right]T_{ij}.

(11)

By contracting Eq. (
11
) gives the following relation between the Ricci scalar
R
and the trace
T
of the stress-energy tensor,

f_{R}R-2[f-(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})L_{m}]+3\Box f_{R}=\left[8\pi+\frac{1}{2}(f_{L_{m}}+2f_{T})\right]T,

(12)

where we have denoted
\Theta=\Theta_{i}^{i}
.

The problem of the perfect fluids, described by an energy density
\rho
, pressure
p
and four-velocity
u^{i}
is more complicated, since there is no unique definition of the matter Lagrangian. However, in the present study we assume that the stress-energy tensor of the matter is given by

T_{ij}=(\rho+p)u_{i}u_{j}+pg_{ij},

(13)

for the flat FLRW homogeneous and isotropic spacetime metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}),

(14)

and the matter Lagrangian can be taken as
L_{m}=-\rho
. The four-velocity
u_{i}
satisfies the conditions
u_{i}u^{i}=-1
and
u^{i}\nabla_{j}u_{i}=0
, respectively.

The energy-conservation equation is obtained as

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=\frac{(\rho+L_{m})(\dot{f}_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}_{L_{m}})-\frac{1}{2}f_{T}(\dot{T}-2\dot{L}_{m})}{8\pi+f_{T}+\frac{1}{2}f_{L_{m}}}.

(15)

or

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=D_{\mu}u^{\mu}

(16)

The source term
D_{\mu}u^{\mu}
represents the energy production or destruction within the system. The conservation of total energy in a gravitational system is attained alone when the condition
D_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0
is fulfilled at every point in spacetime. If
D_{\mu}u^{\mu}\neq 0
, an energy transfer process or particle formation occurs within the system.

Comparing Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain

D_{\mu}u^{\mu}=\frac{(\rho+L_{m})(\dot{f}_{T}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{f}_{L_{m}})-\frac{1}{2}f_{T}(\dot{T}-2\dot{L}_{m})}{8\pi+f_{T}+\frac{1}{2}f_{L_{m}}}.

(17)

Equations (
16
) and (
17
) indicate that the source term
D_{\mu}u^{\mu}\neq 0
, signifying the formation or destruction of matter within the system. In the present study, we assume
D_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0
and solve the field equations for different fluid sources which satisfies this condition.

H(z)
is derived using the differential age method. The Hubble parameter may directly signify the rate of cosmic expansion in the universe. The decision to employ CC data is mostly contingent upon age difference measurements between two passively evolving galaxies that arose simultaneously, characterized by a small redshift interval. This interval facilitates the calculation of
\delta{z}/\delta{t}
. CC data has demonstrated greater reliability than previous methods reliant on absolute age determinations for galaxies
[
92
]
. To assess the model’s goodness of fit with the data, we utilize the following
\chi^{2}
formula defined as:

\chi_{CC}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}\frac{[H_{ob}(z_{i})-H_{th}(\phi,z_{i})]^{2}}{\sigma_{H(z_{i})}^{2}}.

(49)

The symbol
\phi
represents the collection of model parameters for a particular model.
H_{ob}
refers to the observed values, while
H_{th}
represents the theoretical values of the Hubble function.
\sigma_{H(z_{i})}
denotes the standard deviations associated with the Hubble data points. Lastly,
N
indicates the total number of data points used in the analysis.

4.3
Information Criteria

In this part, we employ the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) as model selection criteria to statistically compare our derived models fitting with the standard cosmological
\Lambda
CDM model. The AIC criteria can be computed using the following formula, as given in references
[
98
,
99
]
, under the assumption of Gaussian errors.

AIC=\chi^{2}_{\text{tot},\text{min}}+\frac{2kN_{\text{tot}}}{N_{\text{tot}}-k-1},

(56)

The symbol
\chi^{2}_{\text{tot},\text{min}}
reflects the minimum value of the
\chi^{2}
for joint analysis of CC
+
Pantheon+SH0ES datasets being studied. The variable
N_{\text{tot}}
represents the total number of data points utilized in the analysis, whereas
k
indicates the number of parameters that have been fitted in a particular model. Minimizing the
\chi^{2}
-value is comparable to maximizing the likelihood function
\mathcal{L}
. The BIC criteria can be computed using the formula specified in references
[
98
,
99
,
100
]
.

BIC=\chi^{2}_{\text{tot},\text{min}}+k\ln{N_{\text{tot}}}

(57)

Our objective is to categorize the models based on their capacity to effectively match the provided data, while considering a range of scenarios that depict the same type of event. When evaluating a group of models, we detect bias in the information criteria (IC) value. To do this, we utilize two specific ICs: AIC and BIC. The expression
\Delta{IC_{model}}=IC_{model}-IC_{min}
represents the difference between the IC value of the derived model (
IC_{model}
) and the minimal IC value corresponding to the standard model (
IC_{min}
). To evaluate the suitability of each model, we employ Jeffrey’s scale
[
101
]
. More precisely, if the value of
\Delta
IC is less than or equal to
2
, it indicates that the data strongly supports the conventional model. When the disparity value,
\Delta
IC, falls within the range of
2
to
6
, it signifies a substantial level of disagreement between the two models. Ultimately, when the disparities reach or exceed
10
, it signifies a moderate level of tension between the two models
[
102
]
.

5.1
Causality

To analyze the stability of derived cosmological models, we investigate the square sound speed
c_{s}^{2}
defined as
[
109
]

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{dp_{e}}{d\rho_{e}}

(58)

where
p_{e}=p_{eff}
and
\rho_{e}=\rho_{eff}
. For a perfect fluid source the square speed of sound can not exceed the square velocity of light i.e.,
c_{s}^{2}\leq c^{2}

[
109
]
. Hence, for a stable cosmological model, the square speed of sound should be less than unity (for
c=1
), i.e.,
0\leq c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
. Thus, we can derive the speed of sound for effective pressure and energy density of each model using equation (
58
), respectively, as follows:

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{\Omega_{s}-2\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{6}}{\Omega_{s}+2\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{6}}

(59)

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{8}{3}\frac{\Omega_{\mu}}{\Omega_{r}}(1+z)^{4}

(60)

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{4\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{3}}{\Omega_{m}+2\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{3}}

(61)

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{-\Omega_{c}-2\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{2}}{3\Omega_{c}-6\Omega_{\mu}(1+z)^{2}}

(62)

The equations (
59
), (
60
), (
61
), and (
62
) represent the expressions of square sound speed for the models I, II, III, and IV, respectively, in terms of energy density parameters and redshift
z
. Figure 5 depicts the evolution of square sound speed
c_{s}^{2}
versus redshift
z
. One can see that the models for barotropic fluid have square sound speed values within the given range
0\leq c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
, whereas model IV violates this condition in late-time stages. Therefore, the models I, II, and III are stable, but model IV is unstable at late-time.

Conclusions

In the generalized matter-geometry coupling theory, we investigated the physical characteristics and causality of some new cosmological models for a flat, homogeneous, and isotropic spacetime filled with stiff, radiation, dust, and curvature fluid sources. We have obtained a particular cosmological model corresponding to each source fluid, called Models I, II, III, and IV, respectively. We have made observational constraints on each model using joint analysis of the
31

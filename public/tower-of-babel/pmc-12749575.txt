Analysis of Cattaneo-Christov model in stratified and magnetized Powell-Eyring fluid flow with Joule heating and viscous dissipation
Authors: Samaira Arshad, Muhammad Farooq, Hashim, Hammad Alotaibi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Researchers are currently encountering significant challenges in the rate of heating/cooling processes as per industrial, engineering, and automation demands. In view of this, non-Fourier’s law is imposed to visualize the properties of stratified Powell-Eyring fluid flow with variable thermal conductivity in the vicinity of the stagnation region through a linearly stretchable surface. Features of heat are studied via viscous dissipation and Joule heating. Thermal stratification is instigated by assuming a variable temperature. A constant intensity of magnetic field is utilized to explore fluid behavior. To reduce the complexity and computational work, the resulting governing equations are converted into nonlinear partial differential equations via relevant physical transformations. Analytical and convergent series solutions are tackled through homotopic approach. Convergence for nonlinear and dimensionless governing equations are validated accurately h-curves and residual errors. Output of this problem is scrutinized and visualized to temperature and velocity fields of fluid particles via graphical analysis. The classical Fourier’s law miscalculates the rate of heat transfer which is overcome by Cattaneo-Christov model through thermal memory and exhibits more accurate estimation for cooling and heating processes. The temperature of the fluid declines gradually near the wall for the dominant behavior of the thermal stratification parameter, whereas variable thermal conductivity increases the heat transfer. The simultaneous impacts will show a competing role in efficient thermal management.

Introduction

Powell–Eyring fluids are a subtype of non-Newtonian fluids that reflect the nature of fluids whose viscosity is sensitive to strain and temperature variation. Although it was discovered in 1994 but it still offers challenges for researchers due to its deep involvement in industrial and engineering processes. This model validates the fluid’s behavior for high and low stresses.

In many industrial, geophysical, and natural processes, the Powell–Eyring model is useful because it can explain coupled heat and mass transfer events. Fog formation and dispersal, agricultural temperature and moisture control, orchard planning, chemical process engineering, evaluation of frost-damaged crops, environmental pollution studies, enhanced oil recovery, thermal insulation design, geothermal energy systems, catalytic packed-bed reactors, porous media drying, and subsurface energy transport are some of the applications. In their numerical analysis of Powell–Eyring fluid flow close to a stagnation point, Rehman et al.1 examined the impact of solutal stratification brought on by a cylindrical surface. They indicated how concentration affects mass transfer and velocity in non-Newtonian flows are induced by density gradients. This work describes the relation for the nonlinear characteristics of viscosity in the presence of cylindrical geometry. Salawu et al.2 demonstrated the behavior of nanoparticles in Powell-Eyring fluid through radioactive chemical species. Karthik et al.3 explored the features of thermal reactive Powell-Eyring fluid by incorporating chemical reaction nanoparticles. Powell-Eyring fluid is explicitly analyzed with non-constant conductivity and Cattaneo-Christov model by Hayat rt al.4. Powell-Eyring fluid is derived through kinetic theory of liquids. It is applicable to the engineering and industrial processes which involve geothermal reservoirs, heat exchanges, thermal fins, insulated materials, regulation of environment temperature etc5–8.

Stratification is the process by which particles are deposited or arranged into discrete strata. It appears due to dissimilarity in concentration, temperature, and the mixing of fluids of various densities. Various natural, industrial, and technical systems can benefit from an understanding of the importance of stratification in convective transport systems. These cases contain the production of food in an industrial setting, the appearance of ocean, groundwater, and river salinity stratification, and involvement of different combinations of atmospheric particles. Applications for thermal stratification exist in reservoirs, such as the ocean. Variable characteristics of thermal conductivity in nano fluid flow were elaborated by Daniel et al.9 over variable sheet thickness with a stratified medium.

Anjum et al.10 discussed the significant role of thermal stratification in viscous fluid via a variable sheet with a slip mechanism. Javad et al.11 analyzed the nature of the melting phenomenon in the thermally stratified Jeffrey fluid with chemical species through porous material. Their conclusions apply to environmental and industrially relevant processes that include multiphysics interactions as well as porous medium dynamics.

Alzahrani et al.12 prescribed the nature of nanofluid flow by incorporating three -types of nanoparticles in water with chemical reaction and Cattaneo-Christov mathematical approach. Jabeen et al.13 investigated nonlinear convective flow of a Powell-Eyring fluid, and Malik et al.14 analyzed the application of nonlinear stratification to Falkner-Skan convective flow. One of the key disadvantages of thermal stratification relates to its susceptibility to restrict vertical mixing of oxygen, which may result in oxygen-free zones in deeper water layers if oxygen supplied gets utilized as a result of biological activity. Heat transfer plays a key role in various geographical phenomena, as well as technological and engineering applications in industry. Traditionally, key examples include heat transport across biological media, device cooling, heat control in spacecraft, and cooling in nuclear reactors.

Heat exchange between a body or between two bodies has been a consequence of temperature variation. Fourier’s law of heat conduction has been the cornerstone of efforts on heat transmission throughout the past 200 years. A parabolic equation assuming that heat flows instantaneously, Fourier’s law is. Cattaneo (1948) introduced the non-Fourier theory as a resolution to that contradiction. Heat movement rate can be finite, as introduced by Cattaneo’s addition of a time relaxation factor to Fourier’s classical law. This explains heat movement in the real world. With assistance from the double diffusion model of Cattano–Christov, Kumar et al.15 investigated the flow behavior of a third-grade nanofluid along a stretching Riga plate. To facilitate a further understanding of thermodynamic irreversibility occurring in the system, the researchers also performed an analysis of entropy generation.

Mirza et al.16 elaborated on the features of two-phase flow of hybrid nanofluid with dusty particles through the Cataneo-Christov approach and validated the results through an artificial neural network. Characteristics of heat transportation in viscous fluid are disclosed by Hayat et al.17. The Cattano-Christov model by implementing the stratification phenomenon. Rehman et al.18 describe the radiative Sutterby fluid flow by implementing the Cattano-Christov model was imposed on the Maxwell fluid embedded in porous material in the vicinity of stagnant region by Jawad and Nisar19. Analysis of ternary hybrid nanofluid flow deformed by a linearly stretchable surface in the Cattano-Christov model was examined by Li et al.20. Further, other recent works are listed in refs21–30.

Based on the above literature review, researchers focused on the exploration of constant fluid flows and the classical law of heat transportation. But we focused on the variable thermal conductivity, which presents the more realistic behavior of fluids since fluids modify their behavior of heat capability due to the variation of temperature. Such a variation of temperature causes quadratic thermal stratification to display the suitable character of heat propagation via fluids. In addition, every fluid differs in the character of viscosity, which has a direct relation to the intermolecular forces between the particles of the fluid. Polymer solution gives the best example of a Powell-Eyring fluid in which the viscosity cannot be taken as negligible. Hence, due to such a viscosity, some of the mechanical energy gets wasted, and the value highly depends upon the chemical bond between the fluid molecules. Lastly, each and every researcher has an interest in increasing the rate of heat transfer in various types of industrial processes, since in that context, cooling and heating processes may be enhanced. Product quality at the industrial level highly depends upon the heat supplied to the system. For this aim, we used the new form of heat transfer law, which is referred to as the Cattaneo-Christov heat flux model. An inclined magnetic field has been used to display the behavior of fluids. Analytic approximate solutions are addressed based on the homotopic technique31–35. Characteristics of various concerned parameters are investigated for temperature and velocity distributions graphically and explained along with the physical justifications. We also address the drag force and the value of the Nusselt number, along with the various development parameters, both conceptually and graphically. It is anticipated that the current issue could contribute to sustainable liquid solutions, the optimization of cooling systems, aerospace engineering, and global environmental management.

Formulation

The current investigation addresses the influence of quadratic thermal stratification in Powell-Eyring fluid with the combination of the stagnation point. The fluid is assumed to undergo steady, two-dimensional flow over a linearly stretching surface under the effect of an inclined magnetic field. Heat transportation is analyzed through viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and variable thermal capability of Powell-Eyring fluid. Cattaneo-Christov law is imposed to visualize the heat variations within in fluid (see Fig. 1a and b).

The considered boundary conditions collectively generate a physically reliable framework that exhibits the combined effects of stretching, quadratic surface temperature, quadratic thermal stratification, free stream velocity, and impermeable surface, which are critically significant in cutting-edge industrial and heat transfer processes. In particular, such formulations are immediately applicable to polymer extrusion and sheet stretching, for which temperature and velocity depend on the surface; temperature control of polymeric films and metal, for which special heating is needed to ensure material uniformity; magnetic control of cooling system design, for which non-Fourier conduction as well as Joule heating are predominant; as well as to aerospace and turbine cooling applications, for which stagnation regions are subjected to significant velocity as well as temperature gradients. Thus, the selected boundary conditions are not only mathematically tractable but also physically representative of realistic industrial and technological systems where viscous dissipation, temperature-dependent conductivity, and modern laws significantly influence performance and stability.

Figure 4a and b depict the nature of the ratio parameter in fluid flow through a velocity field. The stagnation parameter has a key impact on shaping the velocity field. For \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A<1$$\end{document}, the surface stretching is dominant, which causes a maximum velocity at the surface of the wall. However, it is interesting to note that for A>1, the higher velocity occurs in the surrounding fluid which causes the boundary layer to reduce effectively. Under the condition of A=1, fluid is moving without any deformation or disturbance because there is no source to generate any disturbance in the fluid located over the stretching surface.

Figure 5a and b demonstrate the nature of magnetic parameter on horizontal velocity of fluid particles. Low velocity is analyzed as we enhance the intensity of applied magnetic field. Lorentz force gets higher intensity for dominant magnetic parameter which provides resistance in the opposite direction of the flow. Therefore, velocity field declines gradually.

Figure 6a and b demonstrate the role of fluid parameter on fluid’s velocity field. It is analyzed that velocity grows as fluid parameter get rises. Physically, it can be justified through lower viscosity relation with fluid parameter for higher values. Which alternatively, provides low quantity of resistance in fluid’s motion. Therefore, velocity enhances rapidly.

Figure 7a and b reflect the features of Eckert number corresponding to fluid’s temperature. Dominant Eckert number grows the fluid’s temperature abruptly which is directly related to intermolecular forces between the fluid’s molecules. As intermolecular forces increase, more energy will be dissipated for higher Eckert number. Therefore, temperature field enhances. Figure 8a and b express the significance of variable thermal conductivity parameter verse fluid’s temperature. As thermal conductivity is assumed directly and linearly to the fluid’s temperature which consequently results in higher convective flow and heat transfer mechanism. Therefore, temperature field intensifies gradually.

Figure 9a and b represent the characteristics of Prandtl number with temperature in the flowing fluid. Prandtl number has inverse relation with the fluid’s thermal capability of heat transfer. In view of such analysis, temperature gets down gradually for dominant Prandtl number. Thermal boundary also gets smaller due to lower thermal conductivity.

Figure 10a and b exhibit the impact of thermal memory of Cattaneo-Christov model corresponding to temperature. Dominant thermal memory parameter reduces the temperature field as fluid particles take some time to transfer heat in the neighboring particles for higher thermal memory parameter. In view of this, temperature gets down.

Figure 11a and b illustrate the response of fluid’s temperature corresponding to thermal stratification parameter. Decrement is observed in temperature for higher stratification. As the stratification parameter rises, the temperature field is seen to decrease, suggesting that the fluid’s heat transfer efficiency has decreased. Physically, a higher stratification parameter arranges the fluid in various density regions that exhibit resistance to heat transportation. As a result, temperature declines.

Figure 12a and b show how the fluid’s temperature distribution is affected by the Hartmann number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Ha$$\end{document}. It is found that the dominant Hartmann number Ha intensifies the Lorentz forces and the alternative resistive force, which increases the temperature field of the fluid. In this case, electrical energy transforms to heat energy due to magnetic resistivity in the fluid. It displays a trend toward a diminishing border layer and temperature containment.

The surface resistance \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C_f}$$\end{document}, varies according to the material parameter ε, and the Hartmann number Ha, as shown in Fig. 13. Notably, the drag force rises with the Hartmann number and fluid parameter. It should be noted that the Nusselt number cannot be calculated in the case of the Cattaneo-Christov model due to the implicit involvement of heat flux.

A comparison of fluid temperature is illustrated in Fig. 14 corresponding to various physical situations, i.e., without stratification, linear stratification, and quadratic stratification. The temperature field declines as the intensity of thermal stratification grows. Such analysis has a key impact on optimizing the heat in various industrial processes.

Final remarks

We have extensively elaborated on the Powell-Eyring fluid near the stagnation region by implementing the Cattaneo-Christov law with a magnetic field and stratification. The key outcomes are:

The influence of the magnetic parameter on the velocity field becomes increasingly prominent as the boundary layer thickness reduces, owing to the enhanced Lorentz force opposing the flow.An increase in the ratio parameter leads to a notable rise in the velocity, indicating stronger fluid stretching effects near the surface.The variable thermal conductivity and Eckert number produce analogous effects on temperature distribution basically because of the increase in internal energy that occurs by virtue of viscous heating as well as conductivity variations.The temperature profile reveals substantial variations owing to Prandtl as well as Eckert number variations that exhibit their countering effects on thermal diffusion along with viscous dissipation.Further, thermal stratification contributes a stabilizing effect so that temperature gradients are reduced near the wall, while the inclined magnetic field changes both momentum as well as energy transport episodes.

Global advantages

This study enhances industrial performance through increased heat transfer in high-temperature applications, minimizing energy losses. It also contributes to biomedical applications, enabling management of biofluid behavior for improved medical treatment. It also aids in sustainable applications, optimizing cooling technologies, aerospace engineering, and environmental control globally. Powell-Eyring fluid is frequently used in Polymer extrusion, film blowing, and molding processes where fluid exhibits nonlinear viscous behavior. Polyethylene, polypropylene, and synthetic polymers are the best examples of the Powell-Eyring fluid model. This model also exhibits the blood characteristics within arteries and capillaries. At high temperature and pressure conditions, Powell-Eyring fluid reflects the behavior of crude oil, bitumen, and drilling mud flows.

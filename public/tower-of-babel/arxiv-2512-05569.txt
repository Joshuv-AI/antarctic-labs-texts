In this paper we consider toral relatively hyperbolic groups, i.e. torsion-free groups that are hyperbolic relative to a finite collection of abelian subgroups of finite rank, see for instance

The proof of our main theorem (
Theorem 1.2
) has two steps:
we first handle one-ended groups, and then infinitely-ended groups.

In the one-ended case we use (a variation of) the JSJ decomposition of
G
(see the survey
[
24
]
and references therein). Its vertex groups are rigid, abelian, or surface groups.
Theorem 1.2
holds in these groups (see
Section 4

G
; it follows from linear algebra for abelian groups, and from properties of pseudo-Anosov homeomorphisms for surface groups.

The main step now is to understand the growth of an automorphism from that of its restrictions to the vertex groups of the JSJ decomposition. We illustrate the main difficulty of
this local-to-global result on a very simple example.

Assume that
G
is a one-ended, torsion-free, hyperbolic group whose
JSJ decomposition has the form
G=A\ast_{C}B
, with

C
a quasiconvex
malnormal infinite cyclic subgroup. Also assume that
\varphi
leaves
A
and
B

Any element
g\in G

has a normal form

g=a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2}\cdots a_{p}b_{p},\quad\text{where}\quad a_{i}\in A,\ b_{i}\in B,

and

\varphi^{n}(g)=\varphi^{n}(a_{1})\varphi^{n}(b_{1})\varphi^{n}(a_{2})\varphi^{n}(b_{2})\cdots\varphi^{n}(a_{p})\varphi^{n}(b_{p})

is also a normal form.
But uncontrolled cancellations in the edge group

may occur between
\varphi^{n}(a_{i})
and
\varphi^{n}(b_{i})
, for instance, so
knowing growth in
A
and
B

To overcome this difficulty, we adopt a more geometric point of view.
Following Scott-Wall
[
35

with fundamental group

Vertex spaces are rigid, tori, or compact surfaces.
One may represent (the outer class of)

by a loop

To prove
Theorem 1.2

one must understand the growth of the length of a closed geodesic representing
f^{n}(\gamma)

As we apply powers of

the growth of
f^{n}(\gamma)
may be estimated from growth in abelian groups and the Nielsen-Thurston theory of homeomorphisms of surfaces (to be more precise, there may also be linear growth due to twists).

The problem now is that shortening may occur in the edge spaces.
Said differently, some complicated loop created by the homeomorphism in a vertex space could be unwrapped by the homeomorphism in another vertex space.

In order to control this, we equip
M
with a suitable metric that is more convenient to manipulate than the word metric of
G

One important feature, coming from hyperbolicity, is that the orbits for the action of edge groups on the universal cover
X
of

are contracting (denoting by
Y
such an orbit, the projection of any ball
B
disjoint from
Y
onto

has uniformly bounded diameter), and separated (the projection of one orbit onto another has uniformly bounded diameter).
This can be profitably used to estimate precisely the length of
f^{n}(\gamma)

More details will be given at the beginning of
Section 5
.

2.1
Algebraic growth and mapping torus

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. We fix a finite generating set and we define
\mathchoice{\left|g\right|}{|g|}{|g|}{|g|}
as the word length of

of

Definition 2.1

(Growth type)

Let

, and

. Given

, we write

\frac{1}{C}n^{d}\lambda^{n}-C\leqslant f(n)\leqslant Cn^{d}\lambda^{n}+C,\quad\forall n\geqslant 1.

In this case we say that

has

.
We write

\preccurlyeq
and

when only one inequality holds.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
.
We say that

grows like

n^{d}\lambda^{n}
, or that
g
has

(d,\lambda)
under

\mathchoice{\left|\varphi^{n}(g)\right|}{|\varphi^{n}(g)|}{|\varphi^{n}(g)|}{|\varphi^{n}(g)|}\asymp n^{d}\lambda^{n}
.
This does not depend on the choice of a finite generating set for

.

Similarly, if
\mathchoice{\left\|\varphi^{n}(g)\right\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}\asymp n^{d}\lambda^{n}
, this is also true for any
\varphi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
representing the same outer class
\Phi\in{\mathrm{Out}}(G)
.
We then say that

grows like

n^{d}\lambda^{n}
, or has

(d,\lambda)
, under

Growth types are ordered in the obvious way, with
(d_{1},\lambda_{1})\leqslant(d_{2},\lambda_{2})

in other words,

As explained in the introduction, we need to study the growth of the double palangres, which have a natural description in terms of the cyclic extension of

There is a natural homomorphism from

For

For

(2)

for
\alpha=gt^{k}
and
\beta=h^{-1}t^{k}
with
g,h\in G
and
k\in\mathbbalpha{Z}
.

Remark 2.3
.

A computation shows that, for every
g,h\in G
and every
n\in\mathbbalpha{N}
, one has
\varphi^{n}(g)R_{n}(\varphi,h)=R_{n}(\varphi,\varphi(g)hg^{-1})g
. It follows
that studying the growth of double palangres is enough to also study the growth of terms of the form
\varphi^{n}(g)
or
L_{n}(\varphi,u)\varphi^{n}(g)R_{n}(\varphi,v)
.

2.2
Total PolExp growth

We now consider growth in a broader geometric context.
Let
G
be a group acting properly and cocompactly by isometries on a proper geodesic metric space
X
(e.g. a Cayley graph).
The distance between two points
x,x^{\prime}\in X
is denoted by
\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)}{{\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})}{{\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})}{{\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})}
.
If
g\in G
, its
translation length
, denoted by
\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}
, is

\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}=\inf_{x\in X}\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(gx,x\right)}{{\sf d}(gx,x)}{{\sf d}(gx,x)}{{\sf d}(gx,x)}.

Fix a base point
x\in X
.
We have
\mathchoice{\left|g\right|}{|g|}{|g|}{|g|}\asymp\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,gx\right)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}
and
\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}\asymp\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}{\|g\|_{X}}
by the Schwarz-Milnor lemma. This will allow us to go back and forth between algebra and geometry.

The following statement is the geometric version of
Theorem 1.7
(we will show in
Lemma 2.8
that the two theorems are equivalent, using Formula (
2
)).

Theorem 2.4

(Geometric total PolExp growth)
.

Let
G
be a one-ended toral relatively hyperbolic group. Let
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
and
E=G\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbbalpha{Z}
. Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space on which
G
acts isometrically, properly, cocompactly.
Then:

1.

(Classes). For every

g\in G
, there exist
d\in\mathbbalpha{N}
and
\lambda\geqslant 1
such that

\mathchoice{\left\|\varphi^{n}(g)\right\|_{X}}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|_{X}}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|_{X}}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|_{X}}\ \asymp n^{d}\lambda^{n}.

2.

(Palangres).
For every
\alpha,\beta\in E
with
\pi(\alpha)=\pi(\beta)\geqslant 1
,
there exist
d\in\mathbbalpha{N}
and
\lambda\geqslant 1

such that

\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x\right)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}\asymp n^{d}\lambda^{n}

for some (hence every)
x\in X
.

Remark 2.5
.

In the second assertion,
\pi(\alpha)=\pi(\beta)
means that there exist
k\in\mathbbalpha{Z}
and
g,h\in G
such that
\alpha=gt^{k}
and
\beta=ht^{k}
.
This implies that each
\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}
belongs to
G
, hence acts (isometrically) on
X
.
Therefore, the validity of
\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x\right)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}\asymp n^{d}\lambda^{n}
is independent of
x\in X
.

In the rest of this section,
G
is any finitely generated group,
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, and
E=G\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbbalpha{Z}
.

Definition 2.6

(Total PolExp growth)
.

We say that
E
has
(
geometric
)

total PolExp growth
with respect to
X
if it satisfies both conclusions of
Theorem 2.4
(‘Classes’ and ‘Palangres’).

Geometric total PolExp growth does not depend on the choice of
X
:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be two geodesic metric spaces on which
G
acts properly, cocompactly, by isometries.
Then
E
has total PolExp growth with respect to
X
if and only if it has total PolExp growth with respect to
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The action of
G
being proper and cocompact, there is a quasi-isometry
H\colon X\to X^{\prime}
which is coarsely
G
-equivariant: there exists
C\geqslant 0
such that

\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(gH(x),H(gx)\right)}{{\sf d}(gH(x),H(gx))}{{\sf d}(gH(x),H(gx))}{{\sf d}(gH(x),H(gx))}\leqslant C,\quad\forall g\in G,\ \forall x\in X.

As we observed in
Remark 2.5
, the element
\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}
belongs to
G
for every
n\in\mathbbalpha{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in E
with
\pi(\alpha)=\pi(\beta)
. Hence all the asymptotic estimates are the same in
X
and
X^{\prime}
up to a multiplicative/additive error bounded in terms of

and the quasi-isometry parameters of

only; in particular, the growth types may be computed in either space.
∎

Using a Cayley graph of
G
, we deduce:

Lemma 2.8

(Algebraic and geometric total PolExp growth are equivalent)
.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space on which
G
acts properly, cocompactly, by isometries.
Then
\varphi
has total PolExp growth (
Definition 1.8
) if and only if
E
has total PolExp growth with respect to
X
(
Definition 2.6
).

Proof.

By
Lemma 2.7
, we may assume that
X
is the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to the generating set used to define
\mathchoice{\left|g\right|}{|g|}{|g|}{|g|}
, with
x=1_{G}
,
so that
\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,gx\right)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}{{\sf d}(x,gx)}=\mathchoice{\left|g\right|}{|g|}{|g|}{|g|}
and
\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}_{X}=\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}
.
The equivalence of the assertions about classes follows.
For the assertions about palangres, we use Formula (
2
).
∎

Remark 2.9
.

It follows from the geometric viewpoint that the validity of
Theorem 1.7
for
\varphi
only depends on its outer class (this may also be seen directly by a simple computation).

We end this section with a key lemma, which will allow us to replace
\varphi
by a power (and thus
assume that it is pure in the sense of

below).

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
k\geqslant 1
.

1.

An element or conjugacy class in
G
has growth type
(d,\lambda)
under iteration of
\varphi
if and only if it has growth type
(d,\lambda^{k})
under iteration of
\varphi^{k}
;

2.

\varphi^{k}
has total PolExp growth if and only if
\varphi
does.

Proof.

Writing
\varphi^{n}=\varphi^{i}\circ\varphi^{km}
with
0\leqslant i<k
shows the first assertion, which covers in particular the ‘Classes’ part of total PolExp growth.
To handle the ‘Palangres’ part, we argue geometrically, using
the action of
G
on a Cayley graph
X
.

We have to estimate
\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x\right)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}
, where we choose
x
to be a vertex of
X
.
Note that the action of
\mathchoice{\operatorname{Aut}\left(G\right)}{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}
on
G
induces an action of
E_{\varphi}
on the vertex set of
X
by quasi-isometries.

We view
E_{\varphi^{k}}=G\rtimes_{\varphi^{k}}\mathbbalpha{Z}
as a finite-index subgroup of
E_{\varphi}
, equal to
\pi^{-1}(k\mathbbalpha{Z})
.
If

E_{\varphi}
has total PolExp growth with respect to
X
, so does

E_{\varphi^{k}}
.
This shows the “if” direction of the lemma.
We now prove the converse.

Since
\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}
acts on the vertex set of
X
as an isometry and
\alpha
as a quasi-isometry, we have

\begin{split}\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}x\right)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}x)}\asymp\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(\alpha x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}\beta x\right)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}\beta x)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}\beta x)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{-(n+1)}\beta x)}&=\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(\alpha x,\alpha\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x\right)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(\alpha x,\alpha\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}\\
&\asymp\mathchoice{{\sf d}\!\left(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x\right)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}{{\sf d}(x,\alpha^{n}\beta^{-n}x)}.\end{split}

Using Euclidean division, we see that the ‘Palangres’ part of total PolExp growth is true for

\alpha
and
\beta
if it is true for

\alpha^{k},\beta^{k}
. The “only if” direction of the lemma follows because
\alpha^{k},\beta^{k}\in E_{\varphi^{k}}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in E_{\varphi}
.
∎

Part I
One-ended groups

3
The refined JSJ decomposition

Let
G
be toral relatively hyperbolic and one-ended. Recall that
G
acts on its
canonical JSJ tree

T_{0}
, which is the unique (up to equivariant isomorphism) JSJ tree of
G
over abelian groups, relative to non-cyclic abelian subgroups, equal to its own tree of cylinders, see
[
24
, Corollary 9.20]
.
It enjoys the following properties (we denote by
G_{v}
the stabilizer of a vertex
v
, by
G_{e}
the stabilizer of an edge
e
).

Proposition 3.1

(JSJ tree)
.

Let
G
be a one-ended toral relatively hyperbolic group, and let
T_{0}
be its canonical JSJ tree, with vertex set
V(T_{0})
. Then:

1.

T_{0}
is bipartite:

V(T_{0})
admits a (unique)
G
-invariant partition
V(T_{0})=V_{0}\sqcup V_{1}
such that stabilizers of vertices in
V_{0}
are non-abelian, while stabilizers of vertices in
V_{1}
are abelian (we say that vertices are non-abelian or abelian accordingly); each edge joins a vertex in
V_{0}
to a vertex in
V_{1}
;

2.

if
v\in V_{0}
, stabilizers of incident edges are maximal abelian subgroups of
G_{v}
, and two such stabilizers are conjugate in
G_{v}
if and only if
the edges are in the same
G_{v}
-orbit;

3.

vertex and edge stabilizers of

T_{0}
are relatively quasiconvex (see for instance Section 3.3 of
[
21
]
), in particular they are toral relatively hyperbolic;

4.

T_{0}
is 3-acylindrical: segments of length 3 have trivial stabilizer;

5.

T_{0}
is invariant under automorphisms: the action of
G
on
T_{0}
extends to an action of
G\rtimes{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
preserving the partition of
V(T_{0})
. ∎

As in
[
24
, Corollary 9.20]
, every vertex
v\in V_{0}
is of one of the following two types:

—

rigid
, i.e.
G_{v}
does not admit any splitting over abelian subgroups relative to the incident edge stabilizers – which implies that only finitely many elements of
{\mathrm{Out}}(G_{v})
extend to automorphisms of
G
(this relies on the Bestvina–Paulin method and Rips’s work on
\mathbbalpha{R}
-trees, see
[
21
]
);

—

quadratically hanging
(QH), i.e. there exists an isomorphism
G_{v}\simeq\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{v})
, where
\Sigma_{v}
is a compact (possibly non-orientable) hyperbolic surface, and this isomorphism induces a bijection between stabilizers of incident edges and conjugates of maximal boundary subgroups of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{v})
.

Let now
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, and let
E=G\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbbalpha{Z}
. The tree
T_{0}
is
\varphi
-invariant, so the action of
G
on
T_{0}
extends to an action of
E
.
We refine
T_{0}
at QH vertices as in
[
2
,
9
]
, so as to get a
\varphi
-invariant
G
-tree
T
for which the action of
\varphi
on vertex stabilizers is either pseudo-Anosov (pA-vertex) or trivial in the following sense (R-vertex).

Definition 3.2

(Acting trivially)
.

We say that
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)

acts trivially
on a subgroup
H
if there exists an inner automorphism
\iota
of
G
such that
\varphi
agrees with
\iota
on
H
.
In other words, there is an automorphism in the outer class of
\varphi
which is equal to the identity on
H
.

We sketch the construction of
T
. We first
raise
\varphi
to a power
\psi=\varphi^{N}
(with
N\geqslant 1
) so that
\psi
acts (through
E
) as the identity on the (finite) quotient graph
T_{0}/G
, and acts trivially on each rigid vertex group
G_{v}
(as mentioned above, automorphisms of
G_{v}
induced by automorphisms of
G
have finite order in
{\mathrm{Out}}(G_{v})
). Moreover, if
\Sigma_{v}
is a surface associated to a QH-vertex and
f_{v}
is a homeomorphism representing the restriction of
\psi
, we want the complementary subsurfaces to the canonical reduction system for
f_{v}
to be invariant under
f_{v}
, and the induced map to be isotopic to the identity or a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism (see for instance
[
14
]
for the definitions).
We may also assume that
\psi
acts trivially on all edge stabilizers, since edges with non-cyclic stabilizer have a rigid endpoint. Note that
N
may be bounded in terms of
G
only.

We define the
refined JSJ tree

T
(for
\varphi
) by
G
-equivariantly refining
T_{0}
at each QH vertex
v
, using the cyclic splitting of
G_{v}=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{v})
dual to the canonical
reduction system on
\Sigma_{v}
.
We then subdivide each of the newly added edges at its midpoint, so that the tree remains bipartite.
The
G
-tree
T
obtained in this way is not invariant under the whole of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, but it is invariant under
\varphi
, so
E
acts on it.
We thus get:

Proposition 3.3

(Refined JSJ tree for
\varphi
)
.

Let
G
be a one-ended toral relatively hyperbolic group, let
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, and let
T
be the refined JSJ tree for
\varphi
constructed above. It enjoys the first four properties listed in
Proposition 3.1
, and the action of
G
on
T
extends to an action of
E=G\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbbalpha{Z}
preserving the partition of
V(T)
.

Moreover, there is a power
\psi=\varphi^{N}
(with
N
bounded in terms of
G
only) such that:

1.

\psi
acts as the identity on the (finite) quotient graph
T/G
;

2.

the action of
\psi
on every edge stabilizer of
T
is trivial (see
Definition 3.2
);

3.

vertices
v\in V_{0}
are either R-vertices or pA-vertices, in the following sense:

—

If
v
is an R-vertex,
\psi
acts trivially on
G_{v}
.

—

If
v
is a pA-vertex, it is a QH vertex and
\psi
acts on
G_{v}
as a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism of the compact surface
\Sigma_{v}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the number of vertices of
T/G
and the complexity of the surfaces
\Sigma_{v}
appearing in the QH-vertices of
T
are bounded independently of
\varphi
, in terms of the same quantities for
T_{0}
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
v
is an R-vertex, then
G_{v}
is non-abelian, so fixes a single point of
T
and is self-normalizing. It follows that, if an automorphism in the outer class of
\varphi
leaves
G_{v}
invariant, then its restriction to
G_{v}
is an inner automorphism of
G_{v}
.

Definition 3.6

(Pure)
.

We say that
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
is
pure
if
Proposition 3.3
applies with
\psi=\varphi
(i.e.
N=1
); in particular,
\varphi
acts trivially on the quotient graph and on stabilizers of edges and R-vertices.

The goal of the next three sections is to prove total PolExp growth.
By
Lemma 2.10
, we may restrict to pure automorphisms.

4
Palangres in vertex groups

We prove total PolExp growth for automorphisms appearing as restrictions to vertex stabilizers of the refined JSJ tree
T
, using the algebraic (
Theorem 1.7
) or geometric (
Theorem 2.4
) version; recall that these stabilizers are toral relatively hyperbolic.
For convenience, in this section,
G
denotes such a group and
\varphi
an automorphism of
G
.

There are three cases: R-vertices, abelian vertices, pA-vertices.

4.1
R-vertices

Using
Remark 3.5
, we may assume that

\varphi
is inner.
In this case
E_{\varphi}\simeq G\times\mathbbalpha{Z}
, and we may assume that
\varphi
is the identity. Total PolExp growth reduces to computing the growth of sequences of the form
\mathchoice{\left|g^{n}h^{n}\right|}{|g^{n}h^{n}|}{|g^{n}h^{n}|}{|g^{n}h^{n}|}
, with
g,h\in G
.

For
G
toral relatively hyperbolic, such a sequence is either bounded or grows linearly (because this is true in the parabolics, which are free abelian).
As a consequence,
total PolExp growth holds, with only bounded and linear growth occurring.

4.2
Abelian groups

We suppose that
G
is abelian, so
G\simeq\mathbbalpha{Z}^{k}
.
We view
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
as a matrix
A\in GL(k,\mathbbalpha{Z})
acting on
\mathbbalpha{C}^{k}
and we use additive notation.
We compute the growth of
\mathchoice{\left\|A^{n}v\right\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}
and
\mathchoice{\left\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\right\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}
for
v\in\mathbbalpha{C}^{k}
, with
\mathchoice{\left\|\,.\,\right\|}{\|\,.\,\|}{\|\,.\,\|}{\|\,.\,\|}
a suitable Hermitian norm.
The proof is linear algebra, we give it for completeness.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
A\in GL(k,\mathbbalpha{Z})
and
v\in\mathbbalpha{C}^{k}
, there exist an eigenvalue
\eta
of
A
and an integer
d\leqslant k-1
, such that

—

\mathchoice{\left\|A^{n}v\right\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}\asymp n^{d}|\eta|^{n}
, and

—

\mathchoice{\left\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\right\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}\asymp n^{d^{\prime}}|\eta|^{n}
, where
d^{\prime}=d
if
\eta\neq 1
, and
d^{\prime}=d+1
if
\eta=1
.

In particular, automorphisms of
\mathbbalpha{Z}^{k}
have
total PolExp growth.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that
\eta
is an algebraic unit of degree at most
k
, and
|\eta|
is a unit of degree at most
2k^{2}
.

Proof.

By suitably choosing a basis of
\mathbbalpha{C}^{k}
, we can assume that
A
is upper block-triangular, with Jordan blocks of the form
\eta_{p}I_{p}+N_{p}
, where
\eta_{p}
is an eigenvalue of
A
and
N_{p}
is a nilpotent matrix of size
p
.
We fix a Hermitian norm for which this basis is orthonormal.

It is enough to understand the growth when
A=\eta I+N
. For every
n\geqslant k
, we have

A^{n}=(\eta I+N)^{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}{n\choose\ell}\eta^{n-\ell}N^{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}{n\choose\ell}\eta^{n-\ell}N^{\ell}

since
N^{\ell}=0
for
\ell\geqslant k
.
Thus the entries of
A^{n}
are of the form
P_{ij}(n)\eta^{n}
, and those of
A^{n}v
of the form
P_{i,v}(n)\eta^{n}
,
where each
P_{ij}
,
P_{i,v}
is a polynomial of degree at most
k-1
. Therefore

{\mathchoice{\left\|A^{n}v\right\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|P_{i,v}(n)|^{2}|\eta|^{2n},

and the estimate for
\mathchoice{\left\|A^{n}v\right\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}{\|A^{n}v\|}
follows.

We now estimate the growth of
\mathchoice{\left\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\right\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}
as
n
goes to
+\infty
. Again, after decomposing the space according to the Jordan blocks of
A
, we can assume that
A
has a single complex eigenvalue
\eta
. If
\eta\neq 1
, then
A-I
is invertible and
(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v=(A-I)^{-1}(A^{n}v-v)
. In this case the result for
\mathchoice{\left\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\right\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}{\|(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v\|}
follows from the above.

So let us finally assume that
A=I+N
, where
N
is a nilpotent matrix. As above

A^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}{s\choose\ell}N^{\ell},

with
{s\choose\ell}=0
if
\ell>s
,
so the entries of
A^{s}
are polynomials in
s
of degree at most
k-1
. Therefore, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the
i^{\rm th}
entry of
A^{n}v
is given by a polynomial
P_{i,v}(n)
of degree at most
k-1
. For a fixed
v
, if
d_{0}
is the maximal degree of the polynomials
P_{i,v}
as
i
varies, then the entries of the vector
(I+A+\dots+A^{n-1})v
are all polynomials in
n
, the maximal degree being
d_{0}+1
. The conclusion then follows as in the first part of this proof.
∎

4.3
Surface groups

We now suppose that
\varphi
is induced by a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism
f
of a compact surface
\Sigma
. It is well known that
\mathchoice{\left\|\varphi^{n}(g)\right\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}{\|\varphi^{n}(g)\|}
is constant (if

is represented by a curve contained in
\partial\Sigma

We follow the terminology introduced in

In this section, we consider oriented edges

—

Note that

Given a zero stratum

A splitting

—

an edge in an EG or NEG stratum (possibly with vertex group elements on either end);

—

—

—

The subpaths

We say that

by equivalent paths

The key property of a CT is the following:
if

is an edge in an EG or NEG stratum, or a connecting path in a zero stratum, then

is completely split.
Since the tightened image of an INP/exceptional path is an INP/exceptional path, this implies: if

If

If

Theorem 7.1

Let

In

For automorphisms of free products, the definition of rotationless in

In that context, Lyman proves that the first statement holds, see Theorem A in

However, the second statement is not known in general because of the new condition in the definition of rotationless.
We describe this condition for the convenience of the reader, and we explain how to deal with it for toral relatively hyperbolic groups.

Consider a relative train track map

In order to prove that

Theorem 7.2
.

Let

be a toral relatively hyperbolic group.
There exists

This is proved in

Theorem 1.8 of

8
Growth in free products

As above, let

.

Moreover,

8.1

In order to compute word length, we fix any finite generating set for

Definition 8.5

(Length of a circuit, a path, a turn)
.

The length of a circuit

\gamma=e_{1}g_{1}\dots e_{p}g_{p}
in
\Gamma
is
|\gamma|=p+\sum_{i=1}^{p}|g_{i}|
: we count the number of edges and the length of the elements

(which belong to some

if

is fat, and are trivial otherwise).

The length of a path

\gamma=g_{0}e_{1}g_{1}\dots e_{p}g_{p}
is
|\gamma|=p+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|g_{i}|
. Note that we do not take
g_{0}
and
g_{p}
into account, so that equivalent paths have the same length.

The length of a turn
\tau=e_{i}g_{i}e_{i+1}
of
\gamma
, with
g_{i}
in some
G_{j}
, is
|\tau|=|g_{i}|
(if

is a non-fat vertex, then
g_{i}
is trivial and the length is 0).

Remark 8.6
.

The length of a circuit is defined so that the length of any (tight) circuit
\gamma
is equivalent to the length
\mathchoice{\left\|g\right\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}{\|g\|}
of the conjugacy class that it represents.

Now consider a completely split path or circuit
\gamma
. Any two consecutive terms
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
of its complete splitting determine a turn
\tau
, which we call a
turn of
\gamma
with
adjacent terms

\mu,\mu^{\prime}
. We will not consider turns at points of
\gamma
which are not splitting points. A turn of
\gamma
at a vertex

is called a

if

is fat.

Remark 8.7
.

Length is defined so that the length of
\gamma
is the sum of the lengths of the terms of its complete splitting and the lengths of its (fat) turns.

Applying

and tightening maps a turn
\tau
of
\gamma

8.3

If
\tau
is a fat turn, we have just seen that

grows like some

of
\tau

. Note that

We shall now assign a growth type

to any completely split path

we will prove that

captures the growth of

We first need to understand how fat turns

between terms of the paths

Some of them are just the image of
a fat turn of
f^{k-1}_{\sharp}(\delta)
. The others appear in two ways.

First, they may be in the interior of the image of an edge or a connecting path contained in the complete splitting of

(INP’s and exceptional paths do not create turns, as their image is a single term). The other possibility is that

is the image of a turn of
f^{k-1}_{\sharp}(\delta)
at a non-fat vertex

.
This

cannot be the image of a splitting point

of

for any vertex

lies in the interior of the image of an edge or a connecting path.
Thus fat turns are created only in the image of an edge or connecting path by
f_{\sharp}
or

Since

only contains finitely many edges and connecting paths,
and

\delta

are associated to fat turns of the paths

The definition of

is by induction on height.
Recall that the length of a path

and

). We first treat the case where

is reduced to a single term.

and exceptional paths grow linearly, so we define

as
(0,1)
or
(1,1)

The growth type of a connecting path is defined as that of its tightened image (which has lower height).
Now suppose that
\delta
is an edge in an EG or NEG stratum

.

We associate to

a finite set

Consider all edges

of

, and all terms of lower height in the complete splitting of

. These lower terms have growth types by induction, and we include them in

. We also consider the fat turns of the paths

, as well as those of
f^{2}_{\sharp}(e)
(created as images of non-fat turns of

as explained above), and we include their growth types in

.

We let
(d_{C},\lambda_{C})
be the maximal growth type in

(for the obvious order, see

). We compare it with

,

the Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue associated to the stratum

For

For

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k)^{d}\lambda_{1}^{k}\lambda_{2}^{n-k}\asymp\left\{\begin{split}\lambda_{1}^{n},\quad\text{if}\ \lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}\\
n^{d}\lambda_{2}^{n},\quad\text{if}\ \lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}\\

∎

We have now defined

for

Recall that, for each

is not fat but

This leads us to define

Note that every

8.4
Computing growth

If
\tau
is a fat turn, we have seen that
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(\tau)|
grows like some
n^{d}\lambda^{n}
(
Lemma 8.8
), and we have defined
c(\tau)=(d,\lambda)
. We now show:

Lemma 8.11
.

Let
\delta
be a completely split path or circuit, with
c(\delta)=(d,\lambda)
. Then
the length
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(\delta)|
grows like
n^{d}\lambda^{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on height.
Note that we have defined
c

in the preceding section
in such a way that the lemma is true for
any
\delta
if it is true for its terms, so we consider terms. The only non-trivial case is when
\delta
is an edge
e
in an EG or NEG stratum
H_{r}
. Recall that the image of any edge of height
r
has a complete splitting whose terms have height at most
r-1
or are edges of height
r
.

We first show that
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)|\succcurlyeq n^{d}\lambda^{n}
if
c(e)=(d,\lambda)
.
Noting that any edge
e^{\prime}
in
H_{r}
appears as a term in some
f^{k}_{\sharp}(e)
, the result is clear from the definition of
c(e)
, except if
\lambda_{r}=\lambda_{C}
because then
c(e)=(d_{C}+1,\lambda_{C})
.

We thus assume
\lambda_{r}=\lambda_{C}
.
There is an edge
e^{\prime}
of
H_{r}
such that the complete splitting of
f(e^{\prime})
or
f^{2}_{\sharp}(e^{\prime})
contains
a turn or a term of height smaller than
r

\mu
, with growth type
c(\mu)=(d_{C},\lambda_{C})
.

Each
f^{k}_{\sharp}(e)
contains
\lambda_{r}^{k}
copies of

, hence at least
\lambda_{r}^{k}
copies of
\mu

(here and below we neglect multiplicative constants). For
n>k
, the image in

of each copy has length
(n-k)^{d_{C}}\lambda_{C}^{n-k}
(this uses the induction hypothesis if
\mu
is a term).
Thus
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)|
is at least
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k)^{d_{C}}\lambda_{C}^{n-k}\lambda_{r}^{k}
, which grows like
n^{d_{C}+1}\lambda_{C}^{n}
when
\lambda_{r}=\lambda_{C}
by
Lemma 8.9
.

We now show the upper bound:
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)|\preccurlyeq n^{d}\lambda^{n}
. We must make sure that all growth types contributing to the growth of
|f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)|
are accounted for in the definition of
c(e)
.

For
k\geqslant 1
, we define a
k
-ancestor

\rho
as a subpath of
f^{k}_{\sharp}(e)
which is
a maximal subpath of height less than
r
in
f(e^{\prime})
, for
e^{\prime}
an edge of height
r
in the complete splitting of
f^{k-1}_{\sharp}(e)
. Ancestors have bounded length, and up to multiplicative constants the number of
k
-ancestors is
\lambda_{r}^{k}
(unless all edges in the paths
f^{k}_{\sharp}(e)
have height
r
, a trivial case).

We claim that the growth type
c(\rho)
of any
k
-ancestor
\rho
belongs to the set
C
used to define
c(e)
. Indeed,
c(\rho)
was defined using the growth type of the terms of its complete splitting, of its fat turns, and of the fat turns of
f_{\sharp}(\rho)
. All of these appear in
C
(recall that growth types of fat turns of
f^{2}_{\sharp}(e^{\prime})
are in
C
).

Using the induction hypothesis, we deduce that, if
\rho
is a
k
-ancestor and

, then

is a subpath of length at most

of

, which we call a descendant of

.

The path
f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)
, whose length we want to bound, has a splitting into edges of height
r
and descendants

, with

. We call it the coarse splitting of

because it is coarser than the complete splitting.

To bound the length of
f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)
, we estimate separately the total length of the terms of the coarse splitting and the total length of the fat turns between these terms.

The total length of the descendants contained in
f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)
is bounded by
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{r}^{k}(n-k)^{d_{C}}\lambda_{C}^{n-k}
, and there are
\lambda_{r}^{n}
edges of height
r
. Both numbers are bounded by
n^{d}\lambda^{n}
by
Lemma 8.9
and the definition of
c(e)
.

The argument to bound the total length of all fat turns between terms of the coarse splitting of
f^{n}_{\sharp}(e)
is similar.
We now define a
k
-ancestor as a fat turn
\tau
between two terms of the complete splitting of
f(e^{\prime})
,
for
e^{\prime}
an edge of height
r
in the complete splitting of
f^{k-1}_{\sharp}(e)
, or a fat turn of
f^{2}_{\sharp}(e^{\prime})
which is the image of a non-fat turn of
f(e^{\prime})
, for
e^{\prime}
of height
r
in
f^{k-2}_{\sharp}(e)
.

We claim that any fat turn
\tau
between terms of the coarse splitting of

has an ancestor (i.e. there exists a
k
-ancestor
\tilde{\tau}
such that
\tau=f_{\sharp}^{n-k}(\tilde{\tau})
). Indeed, the vertex
v

\tau
belongs to the image of an edge

of height

\tau
is an

is the image of an interior point of

, or is the image of a non-fat endpoint of

(which must be in the interior of

of height

It is again true that the number of

, and we know that the growth type

of a fat turn

.
We conclude by checking that

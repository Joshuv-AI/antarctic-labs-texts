Six-loop renormalization group analysis of the φ 4 + φ 6 \varphi^{4}+\varphi^{6} model
L. Ts. Adzhemyan, M. V. Kompaniets, A. V. Trenogin

Abstract
We investigate the λ ​ φ 4 + g ​ φ 6 \lambda\varphi^{4}+g\varphi^{6} model using the renormalization group method and the ε \varepsilon expansion. This model is used in a situation where the coefficients λ \lambda , g g and the coefficient τ \tau of the term τ ​ φ 2 \tau\varphi^{2} depend on two parameters T T and P P , and there is a point ( T c , P c T_{c},P_{c} ) at which τ \tau and λ \lambda are zero. This point is named the tricritical point. The description of a system depends on a trajectory that leads to the tricritical point on the plane ( T , P T,P ). In the trajectories, when λ \lambda goes to zero fast enough, the description is defined by the φ 6 \varphi^{6} interaction and then the φ 4 \varphi^{4} term can be considered as a composite operator. In this case, the logarithmic dimension is d = 3 d=3 , and the ε \varepsilon expansion is carried out in the dimension d = 3 − 2 ​ ε d=3-2\varepsilon . The main exponents of the tricritical model have been calculated in the third order of the ε \varepsilon expansion. Taking into account the φ 4 \varphi^{4} interaction, we were able to calculate the value of the parameter that determines the required decrease rate in λ \lambda to implement the tricritical behavior. The tricritical dimensions of the composite operators φ k \varphi^{k} for k = 1 , 2 , 4 , 6 k=1,2,4,6 have been computed. The resulting values are compared to those known from a conformal field theory and non-perturbative renormalization group.

4
Conclusion

We have performed the multiplicative renormalization of the
\varphi^{4}+\varphi^{6}
model in the six-loop approximation. The renormalization constants are presented in (
16
) – (
20
), while the exponents and the parameter
b_{0}
in (
27
) – (
30
).

In our study, we do not provide the results for the diagrams that we have calculated and used to obtain
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{4}
. The diagram results will be published in
[
1
]
where we generalize the
\varphi^{4}+\varphi^{6}
model to the
O(n)
-symmetric case. Nevertheless, our results for the diagrams presented in the studies
[
22
,
13
,
15
]
are the same, and some of them can be found in
[
3
]
. The renormalization constants
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{4}
may be compared with those given in
[
22
,
13
,
12
]
in the
n=1
case. The expressions for
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}^{\prime}
are exactly the same, but there are some differences in
Z_{3}
and
Z_{4}
in the third order of perturbation theory. As a consequence, we have the same six-loop results for the anomalous dimensions
\eta
(
28
) and
\nu
(
29
), while
\omega
(
27
) and
b_{0}
(
30
) are different. A more detailed comparison and an attempt to understand the reason for the discrepancy in the renormalization constants will be presented in
[
1
]
.

To obtain values of the anomalous dimensions in
d=2
, we equated
\varepsilon=1/2
. Those anomalous dimensions that have only two terms in perturbation theory were summed directly, while others were resummated using the Pade approximation (see Sub.
3.3

3.3
, especially Tables
2
and
3

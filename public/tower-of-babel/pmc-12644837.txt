Experimental investigation and machine learning-based prediction of brittleness index in heavyweight cement slurries
Authors: Danial Fakhri, Mohammad Mehdi Karbala, Hamid Reza Nejati, Ali Saedi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn engineering design, the brittleness index (BI) seems to play a significant role in material selection, failure forecasting, and service performance. Determining BI is traditionally a laborious, expensive, and highly experimental process. This study gives a full framework that combines experimental tests with machine learning methods to model the brittleness behavior of the heavy-weight cement slurries. The study is based on different experimental tests, such as Split Hopkinson pressure bar Test, Uniaxial Compressive Strength Test, Brazilian Test and etc. on mechanical and physical properties of ten different slurry formulations and a dataset of 250 experimental observations from these formulations, each with 14 independent input parameters. Fourteen machine learning models were created, and their accuracy and dependability were statistically compared, and a new Equation has been developed for estimating the BI based on the test results. Gaussian process regression and support vector regression were the two most accurate models based on interaction with each of the models; when using the full set of input variables, their R2 values ranged from 0.93 to 0.97. When variable selections were applied to the final models, the number of features taken into consideration was lowered to eight, which resulted in further accuracy improvements and R2 values ranging from 0.940 to 0.990. Even though every factor affected the BI, EP had the biggest impact; the main goal of some additives was to lessen brittleness. By using more sophisticated machine learning algorithms, this research provides a new method for predicting business intelligence, which lowers the time and expense required and improves decision-making.Keywords: Brittleness index, Experimental test, Heavy-weight cement slurry, Machine learning, Mechanical propertiesSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

The brittleness index (BI) is one of the most important indexes in the [uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) and tensile strength (TS)] relationship. The importance of BI is also recognized in the context of rocks, concretes and cement slurry. In oil and gas engineering it is important to know the degree of brittleness of rock/semi-rock so that wellbore stability and efficiency of hydraulic fracturing can be interpreted1. Furthermore, brittleness has a direct influence on the mechanical characteristics of the shale rocks and cement of the well. The volumetric fraction of the strong mineral, carbonate, weak constituent and pore spaces allows one to evaluate the strength and Young’s modulus of these materials2.

Many works have been carried out to study rock or semirock brittleness by means of both modelling and testing approaches. The empirical approaches tend to focus mainly on the assessment of tensile and compression strength by means of Brazilian tensile TS and UCS tests, respectively. Several equations allow us to estimate the BI under different conditions and for various types of rocks3–6. It appears that adding materials, such as fly ash (FA), silica fume (SF), nanosilica (nS), and coal fly ash (CFA) to cement strength with improvement in concrete being toughness parameters and strands index of the considered case, thus raising the durability factor of the binding material. These additions saturate small holes and fissures which enhance the ability to compress and stretch the concrete and lessen the brittleness7,8. Furthermore, using construction materials that are considered industrial waste forms the concrete improves these qualities but also makes it much more secure and environmentally responsible. So this practice reduces wastage, increases the scope of greener construction, and cuts down the carbon footprint. When combined with rupture resistance, these materials also solve contemporary construction challenges with environmentally friendly behavior9–12.

Notably, the efforts needed to carry out the sample preparation and the actual tests in deriving BI are quite intensive, expensive and time consuming. For instance, there are specific rock/semi-rock index tests, or results thereof, that can be employed to estimate BI. While the majority of the current equations are derived from tensile and compression strength, others like, frictional- strengthening, rock/semi-rock density, rock/semi-rock elastic modulus, rock/semi rock Poisson’s ratio, peak rock/semi-rock strength, peak strain and remnant rock/semirock strength, peak strain and residual strain of rock/semirock structures, are also very important to determine BI and some of these equations are presented in Eqs. Drawing upon a wealth of scholarly investigations, it becomes evident that brittle materials boast a diverse range of distinctive attributes19–27. These encompass: (1) Little stretching can be observed in response to application of forces indicating their lack of flexibility. (2) Severe fracture failure (catastrophic failure) involving visible fractures rough surfaces is typical for brittle rocks/semi-rocks, as opposed to ductile ones. (3) There is likewise an excessive cracking, due to the practical disengagement of molecular attraction whilst under pressure. (4) Increased strength or toughness seemingly appeared because of the large volume of elasticity within the rock/semi rock’s structure. (5) There is greatly increased proportion of ability to resist compressive force than that of tensile force indicating a high level of strength to compressive forces. (6) Shear and friction angles are higher, in conjunction with being considered to be an indicator of the potential slippage of a fracture thereby reducing the potential of slippage occurring. (7) Quartz and other minerals are the most abundant since clay minerals and other ductile minerals occur in small amounts. (8) High Young’s Modulus and low value of Poisson’s Ratio indicate the strength and fracture resistive characteristics of the rock/semi-rock respectively. (9) The usage of residual and peak strength of brittle rocks mode demonstrate how significant the stress failure and recovery become, due to the never-ending disparity between them. (10) The presence of sudden and self-propagating failure mode during failure of brittle rocks/semi-rocks demonstrates the deep failure mechanism. Such detailed conclusions give fascinating information regarding the distinguishing characteristics of brittle rocks/semi-rocks as they fracture under various loading and stress conditions, advancing our knowledge on the very interactive nature of these rocks.

To estimate the non-homogeneity of rock brittle, as well as mineral and porosity contents in shale, Guo et al.28 used the stress free rock physics template. Tarasov and Potvin27 bases task these rock content brittle criterion on the critical point where accumulated elastic energy is equal to total fracture or excess energy, thus providing satisfactory definitions of rock/semi-rock brittle properties under various loadings. Meng et al. (2015), however, were the first to conduct one of the most extensive analysis and evaluation of the commonly used brittleness indices. In addition to developing their own BI, their study stated that most of the strength based brittleness indices do not consider the elastic strain and confining stress. Both factors are crucial in precisely defining the amount of energy buildup during the loading process before the breakdown as well determining how that energy is expended (brittle fracture, plastic yield or frictional faceting). It is clear that strength based rock BI still serve purposes as secondary means of evaluating the brittleness of a given material owing to the fact that rock strength and rock brittleness are two different parameters4.

Even though the said equations can be used to determine BI, there is room for improvement by creating other computational techniques. The application of computational techniques, have been widely used in various disciplines of science and engineering to address many types of problems29–33.

In the field of rock engineering, it has been shown that these techniques can be used with a great degree of precision20. For example, Kaunda and Asbury (2016) recently focus on building a model to predict BI by artificial neural networks (ANNs), utilizing parameters such as the velocity of S and P waves, elastic modulus, and rock conditions. Their proposed ANN model was able to yield satisfactory accuracy in estimating BI. Also, meta-genetic programming based algorithms have been invented to tackle various optimization issues30,33,34. Among the stochastic methods, metaheuristics are relatively effective for optimization35. Such optimization techniques are based on natural occurrences and social phenomena in the quest for the most optimal point. Of particular importance is the energy optimization of biological evolution, behavior of animals, and annealing mathematical models, which are now developed as metaheuristic approaches35.

Brittleness ındex

The brittleness related to cement slurries is defined by Cement Slurry BI, which is a measure of the brittleness of cement slurries. It refers to the tendency of something to break or fracture under stress7,8. This brittleness factor is the best in terms of how effective the cement is in a certain application, especially in the oil and gas industry use, regarding maintenance of integrity by cement. The above BI measure gives a numerical assessment of how brittle a cement slurry is by factoring certain properties such as compressive strength, elasticity, and other mechanical properties7–10. In-depth research of the cement slurry BI may present vital information for engineers and scientists relevant to the conditions of use of cement slurries regarding prediction of performance under stress and structural integrity.

However, an understanding of the Cement Slurry BI is essential in cement formulation optimization, wellbore design application, and consequently ensures the success and reliability of cement-based systems for a long time. This will assure an absolute informed decision among cement industry professionals on the type of cement to use, proper placement methods, and effective management of wellbore integrity.

DL, refers to the implementation of a neural network that is composed of multiple layers. The most common pattern for most DL networks is that they can learn to be better equipped for retaining learned knowledge compared to shallow neural networks. Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are a specialized type of neural network that include a loop network and a classic neural network. In this way, information may be retained within the network. Deep neural networks experience a delay in learning, which fills the gap between the input identified and the input required45. The SVR refers to a particular method where a prediction of data about the data, that is a regression case, can be solved using the method, the Nu parameter in this case refers to another regression method, which is called NuSVR. The final tree consists of decision nodes and leaf nodes, which together determine final predictions48,49. RF is an ensemble learning model which can be used for all classification and regression tasks, and it accumulates a large number of decision trees during its training phase to achieve its objective, which in this case, the output value prediction in regression tasks is computed by averaging the estimates of individual trees50

VR encompasses an entirely different breed of meta-ensemble estimators which is based on multiple regressors for the correct estimation of the needed parameters, after which the estimated parameter values are forecasted through the mean of their individual forecasts. KNN, on the other hand, is a method wherein the prediction is made by calculating the distance from each data point to the training points. Comparing the behavior of ML models with respect to the EP.

Thus, the EP of a particular material closely interrelates with the BI, as both are masters of the material’s characteristics regarding brittleness or ductility. The available void spaces within the material define EP while BI is a measure of the plastic deformation ability of the material before failure. When high EP characterizes a material, it implies that it contains numerous void spaces and microcracks. Consequently, the stress concentration is expected to increase at the tips of these cracks and void spaces, resulting in an increased susceptibility to fracture. Hence, higher porosity materials tend to present a more brittle behaviour that results in a higher BI.

Conversely, Materials with lower energy potential, on the one hand, have a lesser amount of voids and microcracks, thus get denser and more cohesive. This structural integrity thus facilitates greater plastic deformation before rupture, resulting in lower BI values and a more ductile behavior. It must be remembered that while EP is an influential part in determining brittleness or ductility, other variables such as composition, grain size, and the whole structure of the material also decide the BI. Thus, the study of EP is to put into consideration with these other factors in order to completely govern the behavior of a material.

Matching that is found in the TS of a material regarding its BI. Materials with lower TS resist low stresses and are more inclined to break. Thus most of them demonstrate a brittle behavior and may have a higher BI. On the contrary, materials with high TS resist highly applied stresses and undergo greater plastic deformation before a complete failure, giving lower values of BI and ductile behavior. Well, the earlier statement is indeed true in explaining why TS is not directly correlated with BI. Chemical composition, microstructure, and environmental conditions all affect the BI as well. These include TS as a strong consideration to brittleness or ductility along with the rest.

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis of input variables plays an important role in ML models. It makes it possible to identify parameters which can influence the outcome and prevents overfitting since dimensionality is reduced. This study carries out sensitivity analysis using the mutual information (MI) method. The MI method puts to use information gain, a component of constructing decision trees, in finding the relationship between the model parameters and the outcomes. It quantifies how much one variable has learnable information from the other. MI is a particularly powerful tool when both input and output data are in the category or if it is about two discrete variables (categorical or ordinal). It was mainly created for textual data but can be used with numbers as well.

The MI score is evaluated from the entropy drop due to success-it is value assigned from 0 to 1 as per Eq. 35-while this may be appealed by other disciplines. High MI value indicates a very strong interdependence with feature training of corresponding model. Contrarily, low MI value, such as 0, indicates weak dependence with respect to that feature outcome. Sensitivity analysis via MI presents a quick-and-easy internal picturing of the interplay of each feature in training ML models. Such information will first help to optimize the performance of the model while preventing subsequent overfitting through the identification of more important parameters.

The MI scores as assigned to every input variable can be seen in Fig. 17. The parameters S and Ed form strong confines around the best and worst values of the results generated, respectively, while the D parameter also exhibits comparatively low MI scores. This denotes a weak correlation with the outcomes. On the contrary, parameters such as Vs, Es, S, zs, and n have recorded very high MI scores, indicating their great impacts on the results. It is imperative to mention, however, that these results are true for the dataset used in this study. A change in the database or alteration in the limits of the parameters would probably change these results. For instance, Vs, Es, Ed, D, Vp, and Ad were found to do least to the BI based on the MI scores during this study. It is well known that the combination of parameters Es and Vs is one of the most powerful contributors toward determining the BI value in cement slurries. The relative disconnectedness of this parameter here may be ascribed to the fact that these have not been very much changed and also the nature of the database being quite unique.

Key limitations

Most of the time, judging the BI involves very costly and time-consuming tests, which give little room for data in comparison to ML methods. An important limitation of this study is the small size of the database. In the present study, the BI results were analyzed using ten types of cement slurries, each of which was subjected to certain individual tests. It will be important to validate the applicability of the ML models presented in this study if they were expanded and diversified in the database. A more diverse and wider database will produce much stronger and reliable models. The ML models used in this study can be employed for other cement slurries or materials, provided the appropriate data is fed. Some changes may have to be made to ensure that the changes in material properties or conditions do not affect the reliability of the predictions. This gets down to the issue of quality data and perhaps some tweaking of the models. A collection of data from more materials and curing conditions beyond just the BI data is underway by the writers of this publication to augment the database. This approach will use a wider dataset likely to result into more efficient ML models.

1.
Introduction

We consider systems of the differential inequalities

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{1}}\partial^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}(x,u_{1})\geq f_{1}(u_{2})&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{2}}\partial^{\alpha}b_{\alpha}(x,u_{2})\geq f_{2}(u_{1})&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
n,m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
are integers and
a_{\alpha}
and
b_{\alpha}
are Caratheodory functions such that

|a_{\alpha}(x,\zeta)|+|b_{\alpha}(x,\zeta)|\leq A|\zeta|

with some constant
A>0
for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
In so doing, the functions
f_{1},f_{2}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
on the right in (
1.1
) are assumed to be non-decreasing and convex on the interval
[0,\infty)
.
As is customary, by
\alpha={(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})}
we mean a multi-index with

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}

and

\partial^{\alpha}={\partial^{|\alpha|}/(\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}})}.

Let us also denote by
B_{r}
the open ball of radius
r>0
centered at zero.

We say that
u_{1},u_{2}\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are solutions of (
1.1
) if
f_{1}(u_{2}),f_{2}(u_{1})\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and, moreover,

(-1)^{m_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{1}}a_{\alpha}(x,u_{1})\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{1}(u_{2})\varphi\,dx

(1.2)

and

(-1)^{m_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{2}}b_{\alpha}(x,u_{2})\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{2}(u_{1})\varphi\,dx

(1.3)

for any non-negative function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The absence of non-trivial solutions of differential inequalities, which is also known as the blow-up phenomenon, has traditionally attracted the attention of many mathematicians [1–12].
However, most studies were limited to the case of power-law nonlinearities or dealt with second-order differential operators.

In our paper, an attempt is made to study nonlinearities other than power-law ones, while differential operators of arbitrary order are considered.
We obtain exact conditions guaranteeing that all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial, i.e. these solutions are equal to zero almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For power-law nonlinearities, our results imply blow-up conditions obtained in
[
12
, Theorem 19.1]
.

Below it is assumed
that there be real numbers
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}\in[1,\infty)
and non-decreasing continuous functions
g_{i}:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
h_{i}:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)
satisfying the conditions

f_{i}(\zeta)\geq\zeta^{\lambda_{i}}g_{i}(\zeta)\quad\mbox{for all }\zeta\in[1,\infty),\quad i=1,2,

(1.4)

and

f_{i}(\zeta)\geq\zeta^{\mu_{i}}h_{i}(\zeta)\quad\mbox{for all }\zeta\in(0,1],\quad i=1,2,

(1.5)

and, moreover,

\operatorname*{lim\,inf}\limits_{\zeta\to\infty}\frac{g_{i}(\zeta^{\varkappa})}{g_{i}(\zeta)}>0

(1.6)

and

\operatorname*{lim\,inf}\limits_{\zeta\to+0}\frac{h_{i}(\zeta)}{h_{i}(\zeta^{\varkappa})}>0

(1.7)

for all
\varkappa\in(0,1)
,
i=1,2
.

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}>1
,

\int_{1}^{\infty}(\zeta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}g_{1}(\zeta)g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(\zeta))^{-1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})}\zeta^{1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})-1}d\zeta<\infty,

(2.1)

and

\int_{0}^{1}r^{(n-m_{2})\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-m_{1}\mu_{2}-n-1}h_{1}^{\mu_{2}}(r)h_{2}(r)\,dr=\infty.

(2.2)

Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
and, moreover, (
2.2
) be satisfied.
Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

In the case of
m_{1}\geq n
, condition (
2.2
) in Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.1
can be dropped. Namely, the following statements are valid.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
m_{1}\geq n
and (
2.1
) holds.
Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
m_{1}\geq n
and
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
.
Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=1
and (
2.1
) holds.
Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

Remark 2.1
.

Nothing obviously prevents us from interchanging the inequalities and unknown functions in (
1.1
). Thus, Theorems
2.1
and
2.3
and Corollary
2.1
remain valid with conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) replaced by

\int_{1}^{\infty}(\zeta^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}}g_{2}(\zeta)g_{1}^{\lambda_{2}}(\zeta))^{-1/(m_{2}+\lambda_{2}m_{1})}\zeta^{1/(m_{2}+\lambda_{2}m_{1})-1}d\zeta<\infty

(2.3)

and

\int_{0}^{1}r^{(n-m_{1})\mu_{2}\mu_{1}-m_{2}\mu_{1}-n-1}h_{2}^{\mu_{1}}(r)h_{1}(r)\,dr=\infty,

(2.4)

respectively.
In its turn, Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.2
remain valid with the inequality
m_{1}\geq n
replaced by
m_{2}\geq n
and condition (
2.1
) replaced by (
2.4
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
and
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=1
.
Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=1
and

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{(g_{1}(\zeta)g_{2}(\zeta))^{1/(m_{1}+m_{2})}\zeta}<\infty.

(2.5)

Then all solutions of (
1.1
) are trivial.

The proof of Theorems
2.1
–
2.3
and Corollaries
2.1
–
2.4
is given in Section
3
.
Now we demonstrate some applications of these statements.

Example 2.1
.

Consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{1}}\partial^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}(x,u_{1})\geq|u_{2}|^{\lambda_{1}}&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{2}}\partial^{\alpha}b_{\alpha}(x,u_{2})\geq|u_{1}|^{\lambda_{2}}&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

It does not present any particular problem to verify that both conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) take the form
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
.
In so doing, (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) are equivalent to

(n-m_{2})\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-m_{1}\lambda_{2}-n\leq 0

(2.7)

and

(n-m_{1})\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}-m_{2}\lambda_{1}-n\leq 0,

(2.8)

respectively.
Thus, if
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
and at least one of inequalities (
2.7
), (
2.8
) is valid, then in accordance with Corollary
2.1
all solutions of (
2.6
) are trivial.

The above blow-up conditions are exact. We also note that these conditions coincide with those given in
[
12
, Theorem 19.1]
.

Example 2.2
.

Let us examine the critical exponents
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
and
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
in the condition
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}>1
.
Consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{1}}\partial^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}(x,u_{1})\geq|u_{2}|\log^{\nu_{1}}(e+|u_{2}|)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{2}}\partial^{\alpha}b_{\alpha}(x,u_{2})\geq|u_{1}|\log^{\nu_{2}}(e+|u_{1}|)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.9)

where
\nu_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
, are real numbers. By Theorem
2.1
, if

\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}>m_{1}+m_{2},

(2.10)

then all solutions of (
2.9
) are trivial. Condition (
2.10
) is exact. Indeed, assume that

\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}\leq m_{1}+m_{2}.

It this case, either
\nu_{1}\leq m_{1}
or
\nu_{2}\leq m_{2}
.
Let us assume for certainty that
\nu_{1}\leq m_{1}
.
Putting

u_{1}(x_{1})=|x_{1}|^{m_{1}}e^{e^{|x_{1}|}}

and

u_{2}(x_{1})=|x_{1}|^{m_{2}}e^{(m_{1}-\nu_{1}+m_{2})|x_{1}|}e^{e^{|x_{1}|}},

we obtain non-trivial solutions of the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial^{m_{1}}(\operatorname{sign}x_{1})^{m_{1}}au_{1}}{\partial x_{1}^{m_{1}}}\geq|u_{2}|\log^{\nu_{1}}(e+|u_{2}|)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\frac{\partial^{m_{2}}(\operatorname{sign}x_{1})^{m_{2}}bu_{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{m_{2}}}\geq|u_{1}|\log^{\nu_{2}}(e+|u_{1}|)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
a>0
and
b>0
are sufficiently large real numbers.

3.
Proof of the main results

In this section, by
C
and
\sigma
we denote various positive constants that can depend only on
A
,
n
,
m_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
, and on
the functions
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
.
Let us also denote by
B_{r}
the open ball of radius
r>0
centered at zero. In so doing, by
|B_{r}|
we mean the
n
-dimensional volume of this ball.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be solutions of system (
1.1
), then

\int_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}|u_{1}|\,dx\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{1}}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}f_{1}(|u_{2}|)\,dx

(3.1)

and

\int_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}|u_{2}|\,dx\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{2}}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}f_{2}(|u_{1}|)\,dx

(3.2)

for all real numbers
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
such that
r_{2}\leq 2r_{1}
.

Proof.

We put

\varphi(x)=\varphi_{0}\left(\frac{r_{2}-|x|}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\right),

where
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
is a non-negative function satisfying the conditions

\left.\varphi_{0}\right|_{(-\infty,0]}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left.\varphi_{0}\right|_{[1,\infty)}=1.

It is easy to see that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{|\alpha|=m_{1}}a_{\alpha}(x,u_{1})\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\right|\leq\frac{\sigma}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{1}}}\int_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}|u_{1}|\,dx

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{1}(u_{2})\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{B_{r_{1}}}f_{1}(|u_{2}|)\,dx.

Thus, taking
\varphi
as a test function in (
1.2
), we obtain (
3.1
).
Repeating the previous argument with (
1.2
) replaced by (
1.3
), we arrive at (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be solutions of (
1.1
) and
R>0
be a real number.
Then

I_{1}(r_{2})-I_{1}(r_{1})\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{1}}f_{1}(I_{2}(r_{1}))

(3.3)

and

I_{2}(r_{2})-I_{2}(r_{1})\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{2}}f_{2}(I_{1}(r_{1}))

(3.4)

for all real numbers
R\leq r_{1}<r_{2}\leq 2R
,
where

I_{i}(r)=\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{r}}|u_{i}|\,dx,\quad i=1,2.

(3.5)

Proof.

From formula (
3.1
) of Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}|u_{1}|\,dx\geq\frac{C(r_{2}-r_{1})^{m_{1}}}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}f_{1}(|u_{2}|)\,dx.

(3.6)

Since
f_{1}
is a non-decreasing convex function, we have

\frac{1}{|B_{r_{1}}|}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}f_{1}(|u_{2}|)\,dx\geq f_{1}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r_{1}}|}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}|u_{2}|\,dx\right)\geq f_{1}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{2R}|}\int_{B_{r_{1}}}|u_{2}|\,dx\right).

According to (
3.6
), this implies (
3.3
).
By similar reasoning, formula (
3.2
) of Lemma
3.1
leads to (
3.4
).
∎

We also need the following simple statement.

Lemma 3.3
.

The function
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
satisfy the estimates

g_{i}(\zeta)\leq C\log^{\sigma}\zeta\quad\mbox{and}\quad h_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)\geq C\log^{-\sigma}\zeta

(3.7)

for all
\zeta\in[e,\infty)
,
i=1,2
.

Proof.

For any
\zeta\in[e,\infty)
we have

\zeta\leq e^{e^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}},

where
[\log\log\zeta]
is the integer part of
\log\log\zeta
.
In so doing, by (
1.6
),

g_{i}\left(e^{e^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}}\right)\leq Cg_{i}\left(e^{e^{[\log\log\zeta]}}\right)\leq C^{2}g_{i}\left(e^{e^{[\log\log\zeta]-1}}\right)\leq\ldots\leq C^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}g_{i}(e),

whence in accordance with the monotonicity of the functions
g_{i}
and the fact that

C^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}\leq C^{\log\log\zeta+1}=C\log^{\log C}\zeta

we readily obtain the first inequality in (
3.7
).

In its turn, (
1.7
) yields

h_{i}\left(e^{-e^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}}\right)\geq Ch_{i}\left(e^{-e^{[\log\log\zeta]}}\right)\geq C^{2}h_{i}\left(e^{-e^{[\log\log\zeta]-1}}\right)\geq\ldots\geq C^{[\log\log\zeta]+1}h_{i}\left(\frac{1}{e}\right).

This implies the second inequality in (
3.7
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be non-trivial solutions of (
1.1
). If (
2.1
) is valid, then

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{n}}\int_{B_{R}}|u_{1}|\,dx=0.

(3.8)

Proof.

We extend the functions
g_{i}
to the whole interval
(0,\infty)
by putting

g_{i}(\zeta)=\min\left\{g_{i}(1),\inf_{s\in[\zeta,1]}\frac{f_{i}(s)}{s^{\lambda_{i}}}\right\}\quad\mbox{for all }\zeta\in(0,1],\quad i=1,2.

In this case, inequality (
1.4
) should obviously be fulfilled for all
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
. In so doing, the functions
g_{i}
remain continuous and non-decreasing on the entire interval
(0,\infty)
.

Denote

E_{i}(r)=\int_{B_{r}}|u_{i}|\,dx,\quad i=1,2.

If one of the functions
u_{1}
or
u_{2}
is equal to zero almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then in accordance with the definition of solutions of (
1.1
) the second of these function is also equal to zero almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Therefore, there is a real number

Let
R\in[R_{0},\infty)
and
l
be the minimal positive integer such that
2^{l}I_{1}(R)\geq I_{1}(2R)
,
where the functions
I_{1}
is defined by (
3.5
).
Since
I_{1}(R)>0
, such an integer
l
obviously exists.
We put

r_{0}=R
,

r_{i}=\sup\{r\in(r_{i-1},2R):I_{1}(r)\leq 2I_{1}(r_{i-1})\},\quad 0<i\leq l-1,

and
r_{l}=2R
. It can be seen that

\displaystyle I_{1}(r_{i+1})-I_{1}(r_{i})\geq{}

\displaystyle C(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2}}I_{1}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(I_{1}(r_{i}))

\displaystyle{}\times g_{1}(\sigma(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{m_{2}}I_{1}^{\lambda_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}(I_{1}(r_{i})))

(3.9)

for all
0\leq i\leq l-1
.
Indeed, by Lemma
3.2

I_{1}(r_{i+1})-I_{1}(\rho_{i})\geq C(r_{i+1}-\rho_{i})^{m_{1}}f_{1}(I_{2}(\rho_{i}))

I_{2}(\rho_{i})-I_{2}(r_{1})\geq C(\rho_{i}-r_{i})^{m_{2}}f_{2}(I_{1}(r_{i})),

\rho_{i}=(r_{i+1}+r_{i})/2
.
This implies the estimate

I_{1}(r_{i+1})-I_{1}(\rho_{i})\geq C(r_{i+1}-\rho_{i})^{m_{1}}f_{1}(\sigma(\rho_{i}-r_{i})^{m_{2}}f_{2}(I_{1}(r_{i}))),

Take a real number
\varepsilon\in(0,1/n)\cap(0,\lambda_{2}/m_{2})

We denote by
\Xi_{1}
the set of integers
0\leq i\leq l-1

r_{i+1}-r_{i}<I_{1}^{-\varepsilon}(r_{i}).

In so doing, let
\Xi_{2}
be the set of all other integers
0\leq i\leq l-1
.
From the definition of real numbers
r_{i}
, it follows that

I_{1}(r_{i})\leq 2^{i}I_{1}(R),\quad i=0,\ldots,l-1;

therefore,

\sum_{i\in\Xi_{1}}(r_{i+1}-r_{i})<\sum_{i\in\Xi_{1}}2^{-\varepsilon i}I_{1}^{-\varepsilon}(R)\leq\frac{I_{1}^{-\varepsilon}(R)}{1-2^{-\varepsilon}}.

(3.10)

At the same time, taking into account (
3.5
), we obtain

I_{1}^{-\varepsilon}(R)=\left(\frac{|B_{2R}|}{E_{1}(R)}\right)^{\varepsilon}\leq\left(\frac{|B_{2}|}{E_{1}(R_{0})}\right)^{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon n}.

Since
\varepsilon n<1

\frac{I_{1}^{-\varepsilon}(R)}{1-2^{-\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{R}{2}

(3.11)

if
R
is large enough.
Without loss of generality, one can assume that the real number
R_{0}
is chosen to be so large that (
3.11
) is valid for all
R\geq R_{0}
.
Thus, combining (
3.11
) with (
3.10
), we arrive at the inequality

\sum_{i\in\Xi_{1}}(r_{i+1}-r_{i})<\frac{R}{2}.

(3.12)

This, in turn, yields

\sum_{i\in\Xi_{2}}(r_{i+1}-r_{i})>\frac{R}{2}.

(3.13)

We have

I_{1}(r_{i})\geq I_{1}(R)

and

(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{m_{2}}I_{1}^{\lambda_{2}}(r_{i})\geq I_{1}^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}(r_{i})

for all

. Hence, (

) implies estimate

\displaystyle I_{1}(r_{i+1})-I_{1}(r_{i})\geq{}

\displaystyle C(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2}}I_{1}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(I_{1}(r_{i}))

\displaystyle{}\times g_{1}(\sigma I_{1}^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}(I_{1}(R)))

or, in other words,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{I_{1}(r_{i+1})-I_{1}(r_{i})}{I_{1}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(I_{1}(r_{i}))g_{1}(\sigma I_{1}^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}(r_{i})g_{2}(I_{1}(R)))}\right)^{1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})}\geq C(r_{i+1}-r_{i})

(3.14)

for all

.
From the definition of the real numbers

, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1}(r_{i})}^{2I_{1}(r_{i})}(\zeta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}g_{1}(\sigma\zeta^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}g_{2}(I_{1}(R)))g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(\zeta/2))^{-1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})}\zeta^{1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})-1}d\zeta

\int_{I_{1}(r_{i})}^{2I_{1}(r_{i})}(\zeta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}g_{1}(\sigma\zeta^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}g_{2}(I_{1}(R)))g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}(\zeta/2))^{-1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})}\zeta^{1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})-1}d\zeta\geq C(r_{i+1}-r_{i})

\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}(R)/|B_{2R}|}^{\infty}\left(\zeta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}g_{1}\left(\sigma\zeta^{\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon m_{2}}g_{2}\left(\frac{E_{1}(R)}{|B_{2R}|}\right)\right)g_{2}^{\lambda_{1}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{2}\right)\right)^{-1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})}\zeta^{1/(m_{1}+\lambda_{1}m_{2})-1}d\zeta

Abstract
. We establish a semistable generalization of the Beilinson–Bloch–Esnault–Kerz fiber square, relating the algebraic K K -theory of a semistable scheme to its logarithmic topological cyclic homology. We prove that the obstruction to lifting K K -theory classes is governed by the Hyodo-Kato Chern character. This answers the p p -adic deformation problem for continuous K K -theory in the semistable case, extending the work of Antieau–Mathew–Morrow–Nikolaus. As an application, we provide a purely K-theoretic proof of Yamashita’s semistable p p -adic Lefschetz ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -theorem.

1.1.
The fiber square for log rings

To extend results of this form to the case of semistable special fiber

The role of affine schemes in log geometry is played by

(R,P)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}((R/p,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm HC}^{-}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}

1.2.
p
-adic deformation of
K
-theory classes and Hyodo-Kato cohomology

Let us now make precise the claim that the lifting problem considered at the beginning of this introduction is governed by the Hodge filtration of
R\Gamma_{{\rm dR}}(X_{K}/K)
. In good reduction, there is a crystalline Chern character

{\rm ch}_{{\rm crys}}\colon K_{0}(X_{k})\to\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{2i}_{{\rm crys}}(X_{k};{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

landing in crystalline cohomology. By the de Rham-to-crystalline comparison, this determines a map to
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H_{{\rm dR}}^{2i}(X_{K}/K)
. On
\pi_{0}
, this recovers the right-hand vertical map of (
1.1
), and
[
1
, Theorem D]
states that a class
x\in K_{0}(X_{k};{\mathbb{Q}})
lifts to
K_{0}^{{\rm cts}}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
if and only if its image in
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}H^{2i}_{{\rm dR}}(X_{K}/K)
comes from
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\rm Fil}^{\geq i}H^{2i}_{{\rm dR}}(X_{K}/K)
. This is in fact a generalization of the original statement proved by
[
13
]
.

In bad reduction it is possible to govern the obstruction to lifting classes by means of derived de Rham cohomology, as explained in
[
1
]
following Beilinson
[
3
]
. More precisely, when
X
is not smooth nor proper over
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, a refined obstruction to lifting classes has been constructed by Beilinson and Antieau–Mathew–Morrow–Nikolaus: for
x\in K_{i}({X}_{k};\mathbb{{Q}})
, there exists a class

o(x)\in\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}H^{2r-i}(L\Omega_{X}/L\Omega^{\geq r}_{{X}})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

such that
x
lifts to
K^{\rm cts}_{i}({X};\mathbb{Q}_{p})
if and only if
o(x)=0
. Here,
L\Omega_{X}
denotes the
p
-adic derived de Rham cohomology of
{X}
. See
[
1
, Theorem E]
.

However, the obstruction class
o(x)
is rather implicit and lacks a crystalline interpretation, in contrast with the smooth case.

To tackle this problem, we recall the following fact: when
X
is proper and has semistable reduction over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the de Rham cohomology of the generic fiber of
{X}
can be recovered by means of the Hyodo–Kato cohomology

(1.4)

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}({X}_{K})\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{HK}}({X}_{k}/k^{0})\otimes_{W(k)}K

of the
logarithmic
special fiber, seen as a smooth log scheme over the
logarithmic point

k^{0}=(\mathbb{N}\to k,1\mapsto 0)
. The Hyodo–Kato cohomology is a
(\varphi,N)
-module over
W(k)
, and can be defined using an appropriate version of the crystalline site following work of Kato and Fontaine–Illusie, or in terms of log de Rham-Witt complexes (note however that functoriality is tricky: see
[
6
]
for a motivic approach working directly on the rigid analytic generic fiber of
\mathfrak{X}=X^{\wedge}_{p}
).

In
Section
5
, we consider a
Hyodo–Kato Chern character

{\rm ch}_{{\rm HK}}
from the (ordinary, rationalized)
K
-theory of
X_{k}
, and express it in terms of a trace map from
K
-theory, much in analogy with the study of the crystalline Chern character
{\rm ch}_{{\rm crys}}
in
[
1
]
. Combining the resulting properties with Theorem
A
, we arrive at the following analog of
[
1
, Theorem D]
:

Theorem B

(See Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
X
be a proper flat scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with semistable special fiber
X_{k}
.
Then a class
x\in K_{0}(X_{k};\mathbb{Q})
lifts to
K_{0}^{\mathrm{cts}}(X;\mathbb{Q})
if and only if the Hyodo-Kato Chern character
\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{HK}}(x)
is contained in
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\Fil^{\geq i}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{2i}(X_{K};\mathbb{Q}_{p})
under the Hyodo-Kato equivalence.

In fact, we have a general version involving higher
K
-groups, analogous to (
1.1
), see Theorem
4.2
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, Theorem
B
represents the first
K
-theoretic application of logarithmic trace invariants of genuinely geometric origin. We anticipate progress on
[
31
, Conjecture 1.5]
, which will lead to a more flexible and transparent relationship between algebraic
K
-theory and logarithmic trace invariants. The fact that results like Theorem
B
are already within reach with the present constructions suggests that this is a worthwhile endeavor.

To deduce Theorem
B
, we follow the blueprint of
[
1
]
. In Section 3, we construct the Hyodo-Kato Chern character, using the theory of Chern orientations in logarithmic motivic homotopy theory in the sense of
[
12
]
and apply it to the logarithmic de Rham-Witt complex

. This yields a geometric construction of Chern classes in Hyodo-Kato cohomology, compatible with the classical crystalline Chern character. In Section 4, we focus on the rationalized filtrations of logarithmic cyclic homology. We use the logarithmic Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem
[
9
]
to split these filtrations in terms of the Hodge filtration on the de Rham cohomology of the generic fiber, by means of the Hyodo-Kato isomorphism. This leads to a
K
-theoretic fiber square for semistable schemes involving de Rham cohomology (Theorem
4.2
). In Section 5, we compare the trace-theoretic construction from Section 2 with the geometric Chern character from Section 3, and prove that, up to a scalar factor, the trace map identifies with the Hyodo-Kato Chern character under the Hyodo-Kato equivalence.

1.3.
A semistable
p
-adic Lefschetz (1,1)-Theorem

Note that the
K_{0}
-groups appearing in the statement of Theorem
B
are classical, i.e., non-logarithmic. For line bundles, i.e., for
\mathrm{Pic}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, the obstruction considered in Theorem

is in fact well-known: it is the content of Yamashita’s semistable
p
-adic Lefschetz (1,1)-Theorem
[
43
, Theorem 0.1]
(in turn, a generalization of a theorem of Berthelot-Ogus
[
4
, Theorem 3.8]
).

In fact, Yamashita considers also a logarithmic variant of this result, involving the
logarithmic Picard group
, i.e.,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{M}^{\rm gp})
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the sheaf of monoids describing the log structure on

(see
[
26
]
and
[
24
, Section 3.3]
). In order to recover (and generalize) his result, we need to be able to replace
K
-theory with some version of logarithmic

-theory, computable with effective trace methods.

Since its inception, a severe limitation of logarithmic

has been the lack of a meaningful trace map from algebraic

-theory.
To bridge the gap, we look to recent developments in logarithmic
homotopy invariant

-theory, as discussed in
[
37
]
: by
[

we have a log cyclotomic trace
\mathrm{KH}(R,P)\to\mathrm{TC}(R,P)
fitting in the following commutative diagram of spectra:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KH}(R/p)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TC}(R)}

The use of homotopy

(See Theorem

)

Let

1.4.
A characteristic zero story

Finally, as a complement to our main focus on the
p
-adic deformation problem, we conclude in Section
7
with a discussion of the characteristic zero setting. Assuming
K
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, we consider proper semistable schemes over
K[[t]]
and establish a semistable generalization of a theorem of Morrow
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
. While Morrow characterizes the liftability of
K
-theory classes for smooth schemes using the flat filtration on standard de Rham cohomology, the presence of singularities in the semistable case necessitates a logarithmic approach. We prove that a class in the (homotopy)
K
-theory of the special fiber lifts to the infinitesimal neighborhood if and only if its trace lifts appropriately within the relative logarithmic de Rham cohomology. We view this result primarily as a supplementary application of our methods rather than a definitive parallel to Theorem
B
. In the
p
-adic case, we identify the obstruction with the Hyodo-Kato Chern character relative to the logarithmic point
(k,\mathbb{N})
; however, in the characteristic zero context, we work relative to
K
with the trivial log structure.

2.
A logarithmic Bloch–Esnault–Kerz square

We begin with some technical recollections:

2.1.
Vertical log structures

Let
\varphi\colon P\to Q
be a map of commutative monoids. We say that
\varphi
is
vertical

[
36
, Definition I.4.3.1(4)]
if its cokernel
(Q/P)^{\rm int}
in integral monoids is a group.

Definition 2.2
.

A morphism of fs log schemes
f\colon X\to S
is called
vertical
if the induced map
\mathcal{M}_{S,f(x)}\to\mathcal{M}_{X,x}
is vertical for every point
x\in X
.
By
[
36
, Proposition I.4.3.3(6)]
, if
f
has a chart
P\to Q
that is vertical, then
f
is vertical too.

We shall use the following observation: If
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\langle p\rangle)\to(R,P)
is vertical, then the rationalized log scheme
{\rm Spec}(R\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p},P)
carries the trivial log structure (otherwise, the resulting map to
{\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p},\langle p\rangle)={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
would fail to be vertical).

Example 2.3
.

The morphism
{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}\oplus{\mathbb{N}},n\mapsto(n,n)
is vertical, while the morphism
{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}\oplus{\mathbb{N}},n\mapsto(n,0)
is not. A classical example of a vertical morphism is given by the structure map
X\to\Spec(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\langle p\rangle)
, where
X
is a regular
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
scheme with compactifying log structure given by the inclusion
X[1/p]\subset X
.

2.4.
Removing vertical log structures

We now take the first steps toward formulating variants of the fiber square (
1.3
) that involve
K
-theory. Let us first observe that rationalized log
{\rm HC}
vanishes on
{\mathbb F}_{p}
-algebras:

Lemma 2.5
.

If
(R,P)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra, then
{\rm HC}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\simeq 0
.

Proof.

By assumption,
R/p\cong R
. Thus the upper horizontal map in the (co)cartesian square (
1.3
) is an equivalence, and hence so is its lower horizontal map.
∎

There is a notion of
log regularity
introduced by Kato
[
27
]
; see
[
36
, Theorem III.1.11.1]
for useful characterizations.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(R,P)
be a regular log regular
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra.
Assume that
\Spec(R[1/p],P)
has the trivial log structure.
Then the induced square of spectra

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TC}(R;\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TC}(R/p;\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TC}((R,P);\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TC}((R/p,P);\mathbb{Q}_{p})}

is cartesian.

Proof.

Consider the commutative cube

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm HC}^{-}(R;{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm HP}(R;{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}(R;{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}(R/p;{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm HC}^{-}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm HP}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm TC}((R/p,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}).}

The top face is cartesian by Antieau–Mathew–Morrow–Nikolaus
[
1
, Corollary 3.9]
, while the bottom face is the cartesian square (
1.3
). It thus suffices to prove that the back face is cartesian: We will show that the canonical map

(2.1)

{\rm HC}(R;{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\to{\rm HC}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

is an equivalence. We work Zariski locally on
R
. Since
R
is regular and
(R,P)
is log regular, we may assume that
P=\mathbb{N}^{d}
for some integer
d\geq 0

[
36
, Theorem III.1.11.6]
. We proceed to prove that (
2.1
) is an equivalence by induction on
d
, the case
d=0
being clear. We now apply the residue sequence of Rognes–Sagave–Schlichtkrull
[
40
, Theorem 5.5, Example 5.7]
to infer a fiber sequence of spectra

\mathrm{HC}((R/\alpha(x),\mathbb{N}^{d-1});\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to\mathrm{HC}((R,\mathbb{N}^{d-1});\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to\mathrm{HC}((R,\mathbb{N}^{d});\mathbb{Q}_{p}),

where
\alpha\colon\mathbb{N}^{d}\to R
denotes the log structure map and
x=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Since
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\langle p\rangle)\to(R,P)
is vertical,

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra (cf. Example
2.3
). Thus Lemma
2.5
applies to conclude that the first term of the fiber sequence vanishes, so that its second map is an equivalence. However, the map (
2.1
) is an equivalence for

by induction hypothesis, concluding the proof.
∎

2.7.
From absolute to relative

Suppose now that
K
is a complete discrete valuation field of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
and perfect residue field
k
. We shall write
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}
for the log ring
({\mathcal{O}}_{K},{\mathcal{O}}_{K}-\{0\})
; this is the log ring associated to the pre-log ring
({\mathcal{O}}_{K},\langle\pi\rangle)
for any chosen uniformizer

.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}
-algebra
(R,P)
, the canonical map

{\rm HC}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{\rm HC}((R,P)/{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp};{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

is an equivalence.

Proof.

It is observed in
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 4.10]
that
{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/W(k)}
is quasi-isogenous to

. The assumption that

is perfect gives
{\mathbb{L}}_{k/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\simeq 0
, which in turn implies that
({\mathbb{L}}_{W(k)/{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\wedge}_{p}\simeq 0
(as this can be checked after reducing mod
p
).
By
[
9
, (3.5)]
,
we have an equivalence

\mathrm{fib}(\mathcal{O}_{K}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{O}_{K})\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\sharp}/\mathbb{Z}}).

Combining these observations with the transitivity sequence

{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\otimes_{W(k)}{\mathbb{L}}_{W(k)/{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/W(k)},

we conclude that
({\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}/{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\wedge}_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\simeq 0
. Hence the transitivity sequence

{\mathbb{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}}

implies that
({\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/{\mathbb{Z}}})^{\wedge}_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\xrightarrow{}({\mathbb{L}}_{(R,P)/{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp}})^{\wedge}_{p}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is an equivalence, which implies that
{\rm HH}((R,P);{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\to{\rm HH}((R,P)/{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\sharp};{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
by the log
{\rm HKR}
-filtration
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
. Commuting the

-coinvariants with

-completions as in
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 4.10]
, we conclude.
∎

2.9.
Involving

Finally, we arrive at a logarithmic variant of the square (

) which involves

Theorem 2.10

3.
The Hyodo-Kato Chern class

Fix a pre-log ring
(k,P)
with
k
a perfect field of positive characteristic and
P
a monoid with
\overline{P}=P/P^{*}
fine and saturated. The purpose of this section is to introduce the Hyodo-Kato Chern class generalizing the crystalline Chern class.

3.1.
Hyodo–Kato cohomology

Let
k
be a perfect field of positive characteristic. For every log smooth fs log scheme
X
saturated over
(k,P)
,
Hyodo and Kato defined a logarithmic version of the de Rham-Witt complex
[
23
, 4.1]

W\Omega_{X/(k,P)}
.
Its cohomology

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{HK}}(X/W)=R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X,W\Omega_{X/(k,P)})

is called the (integral)
Hyodo-Kato cohomology
.
Here,

\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}
denotes the strict étale topology on
X
. According to
[
23
]
, it agrees with the log crystalline cohomology
R\Gamma_{\rm logcrys}(X/W^{0})
, where
W^{0}
denotes the ring of Witt vectors over
k
, equipped with the canonical Teichmüller lift log structure (see
[
23
, Section 4]
). We will not use the crystalline description in what follows.

Remark 3.2
.

Let us collect a number of relevant properties:

(1)

By
[
23
, Corollary 4.5]
,
we have an equivalence

(3.1)

W\Omega_{X/(k,P)}/p\simeq\Omega_{X/(k,P)}.

(2)

By
[
7
, Theorem 4.31]
, for every saturated log smooth morphism
X\to\Spec(k,\mathbb{N})
,
we have an equivalence

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X,W\Omega_{X/(k,\mathbb{N})})\simeq R\Gamma_{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}(\underline{X},\partial X\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf}),

natural in
X
,
where
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{perf}}
denotes the colimit
p
-perfection of
\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore we immediately deduce that for every vector bundle
\underline{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{X}
of rank
r+1
, the maps in
[
7
, (3.12.2)]
induce a projective bundle formula

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X,W\Omega_{X/(k,\mathbb{N})})[-2i]\simeq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}R\Gamma_{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}((\underline{X},\partial X\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf})[-2i]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\simeq}

\displaystyle R\Gamma_{\widehat{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})_{\rm pf})\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),W\Omega_{X/(k,\mathbb{N})}),

where
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})_{\rm pf}
are the log schemes with the pullback log structure from
X
and
(\underline{X},\partial X\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf})
and the equivalence in the middle is
[
7
, Lemma 3.13]
.

From (2) we also deduce the
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
invariance: since the Nygaard filtration is complete and exhaustive, we may show invariance on the graded pieces. Using the secondary filtration, it suffices to prove that

(3.2)

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}((\underline{X},\partial X\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf}),\mathbb{L}_{-/k})\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}((\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\underline{X},\partial(X\times(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1}))\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf}),\mathbb{L}_{-/k}).

As observed in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 4.27]
, for any saturated log smooth map
(k,\mathbb{N})\to(A,M)
,
we have equivalences

\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(k,\mathbb{N})}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{(A,M\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf})/(k,\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf})}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{(A,M\oplus{N}\mathbb{N}_{\rm perf})/(k,\mathbb{N})}.

From this, we deduce (
3.2
) from the
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
invariance of the cotangent complex
[
9
, Proposition 8.3]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a strict smooth fs log scheme over

.
Then the natural map of complexes

Proof.

The question is strict étale local on

,
so we reduce to the case of
(k,P)\to(A,P)
with

strictly local.
By (

),
it suffices to show

which follows from

∎

3.5.
Chern orientations

We review the theory of Chern orientations in logarithmic motivic homotopy theory
[
12
, Section 7]
.
For a scheme
S
,
let
\mathbf{lSm}_{S}
denote the category of log smooth fs log schemes over
S
.
Let
\mathbf{SmlSm}_{S}
denote the category of smooth log smooth fs log schemes over
S
,
i.e., the category of those fs log schemes
X
over
S
such that
X\to S
is log smooth and
\underline{X}\to\underline{S}
is smooth.
The logarithmic motivic homotopy category
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
in
[
11
]
admits the model

\mathrm{logSH}(S)\simeq\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{sNis}}(\mathbf{SmlSm}_{S},\mathrm{Sp})[(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})^{-1}])

by
[
12
, Corollary 3.4.7]
.
Here,

\mathrm{sNis}
denotes the strict Nisnevich topology,
and
(\mathbb{P}^{\bullet},\mathbb{P}^{\bullet-1})
denotes the family of projections
X\times(\mathbb{P}^{m},\mathbb{P}^{m-1})\to X
for all
X\in\mathbf{SmlSm}_{S}
and integers
m\geq 1
.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be a homotopy commutative monoid in
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
,
where
S
is a scheme.
The
\mathbf{E}
-cohomology groups

\mathbf{E}^{i,j}(X):=\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}(S)}(\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+},\Sigma^{i,j}\mathbf{E})

form a natural graded ring
\mathbf{E}^{*,*}(X)
.
Let
\mathbf{1}
denote the unit of
\mathbf{E}^{*,*}(X)
.
Recall from
[
12
, Definition 7.1.1]
that a Chern orientation on
\mathbf{E}
is a class
\mathbf{E}^{2,1}(\mathbb{P}^{\infty}/\mathrm{pt})
whose restriction to
\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mathrm{pt}
is the class
(\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})\otimes\mathbf{1}
of
\mathbf{E}^{2,1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be a homotopy commutative monoid in
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
,
where
S
is a scheme.
Then there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the set of Chern classes of
\mathbf{E}
and the set of functors

c_{1}\colon\mathrm{Pic}\to\mathbf{E}^{2,1}

from
\mathbf{SmlSm}_{S}
to
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{Set})
modulo natural isomorphisms.

Proof.

A Chern orientation yields such a functor, see
[
12
, Definition 7.1.3]
.
On the other hand, when a functor
c_{1}\colon\mathrm{Pic}\to\mathbf{E}^{2,1}
is given,
the image of
\mathcal{O}(-1)\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{\infty})
yields a Chern orientation of
\mathbf{E}
.
These two are inverses to each other by construction.
∎

Let
\mathbf{E}
be a Chern oriented homotopy commutative monoid in
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
,
where
S
is a scheme.
Recall from
[
12
, Theorem 7.2.8]
that there exists a natural projective bundle formula

(3.4)

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\mathbf{E}^{*-i,*-2i}(X)\cong\mathbf{E}^{*,*}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}))

for every rank

.
Using this,

The Whitney sum formula

holds for every exact sequence

of vector bundles over

,
see
[
12
, Proposition 7.3.16]
.

.

Let

Again by Große-Klönne

, there is a natural morphism

induced by the natural morphism of log schemes

, which acts as the identity on the underlying scheme

and consequently induces the identity map on the sheaf of units
\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\times}
. The first Chern class

of a line bundle

is defined in both theories via the boundary map of the exponential sequence associated with

(represented explicitly by the map

and is compatible with the derivation

(where

(where

is the Teichmüller lift in the log de Rham-Witt complex

), ensuring that

. This implies commutativity for all higher Chern classes via the splitting principle. In particular, we get a commutative triangle:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(Y;\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}H^{2i}_{\rm rig}(Y/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}H_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}^{2i}(X,W\Omega_{X/(k,\mathbb{N})})\otimes_{K_{0}}K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ch}_{\rm rig}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{HK}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

Remark 3.11
.

The fact that

, Theorem 1.1]
showed that
x
lifts to
\lim_{r}\pi_{0}K(X/t^{r})
if and only if the de Rham class belongs to the flat filtration in the sense of
[
32
, Section 3]
,
where
X/t^{r}:=X\otimes_{K[[t]]}K[[t]]/t^{r}
.
This may be regarded as a characteristic
0
analogue of
[
13
]
.
The purpose of this section is to provide a semistable generalization of this result.

Let
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{-/K}^{q}
denote the cdh sheafification of the presheaf of complexes (not abelian groups)
\Omega_{-/K}^{q}
on the opposite category of
K
-algebras.
By
[
21
, Theorem 3]
(see
[
22
, Theorem 1.2 (3)]
), if
A
is a smooth
K
-algebra we have a quasi-isomorphism of complexes

\Omega_{A/K}^{q}\simeq L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{A/K}^{q}.

Proposition 7.1
.

For every semistable
(K[t],\langle t\rangle)
-algebra
(A,P)
and integer
q
,
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}
is discrete,
and we have an induced homotopy cartesian square

(7.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{A/K}^{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{(A,P)/K}^{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{q}.}

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Spec}(A_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Spec}(A_{m})
be the irreducible components of
\mathrm{Spec}(A/t)
.
For a nonempty subset
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
we set
A_{I}:=A_{i_{1}}\otimes_{A}\cdots\otimes_{A}A_{i_{s}}
.
We claim

(7.2)

\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{*}\simeq\lim_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},I\neq\emptyset}\Omega_{(A_{I},P)/K}^{*},

where
\lim
is the homotopy limit.
This question is Zariski local on
A
,
so we may assume that there exists a strict étale map of
(K[t],\langle t\rangle)
-algebras

(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\rangle)\to(A,P)

for some integers
0\leq d\leq n
.
By taking out the part
\Omega_{K[x_{d+1},\ldots,x_{n}]/K}^{*}
,
we reduce to the case where
d=n
.
In this case,
we set
A_{i}:=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i})
.
For a nonempty subset
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
the coefficient of
\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}
with an integer
q\geq 0
in
\Omega_{(A_{I},P)/K}^{*}
is

K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})

and in
\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{*}
is

K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n}).

Hence without loss of generality,
it suffices to show

K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\simeq\lim_{I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},I\neq\emptyset}K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}).

This is simple algebra.

As a consequence,
we obtain a natural map

L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}\to\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{q}

since
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}
is equivalent to the homotopy limit

\lim_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},I\neq\emptyset}\Omega_{A_{I}/K}^{*}.

This constructs (
7.1
).

Next, we claim that (
7.1
) is homotopy cartesian.
This question is Zariski local on
A
.
As above,
we reduce to the case where
(A,P)=(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle)
.
For a nonempty subset
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
the coefficient of
dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}
in
\Omega_{A_{I}/K}^{q}
is isomorphic to
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})
if
I\subset\{q+1,\ldots,n\}
and
0
otherwise.
Hence the coefficient of
dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}
in
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}
is equivalent to the homotopy limit

\lim_{I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\}\subset\{q+1,\ldots,n\},I\neq\emptyset}K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}),

which is equivalent to
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{q+1}\cdots x_{n})
as above.
Therefore,
without loss of generality,
it suffices to show that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{q+1}\cdots x_{n})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}}

is homotopy cartesian.
The horizontal maps are surjective,
so it suffices to show that the induced map of the kernels

(x_{q+1}\cdots x_{n})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}\to(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\tfrac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\tfrac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}

is an isomorphism,
which is clear.

Finally,
we deduce that
L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}
is discrete since the other three corners of (
7.1
) are discrete.
∎

For an inverse system
\cdots\to X_{1}\to X_{0}
in a category or
\infty
-category,
let
\{X_{r}\}
denote the associated pro-object.

Proposition 7.2
.

For every semistable
(K[t],\langle t\rangle)
-algebra
(A,P)
and integer
q
,
we have an induced homotopy cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\Omega_{(A/t^{r})/K}^{q}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\Omega_{(A/t^{r},P)/K}^{q}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{q}.}

Proof.

As noted in
[
32
, Proof of Theorem 2.1]
,
we have

\{\Omega_{(A/t^{r})/K}^{q}\}\cong\{\Omega_{A/K}^{q}\otimes_{A}A/t^{r}\}

as a consequence of
d(t^{2r})\subset t^{r}\Omega_{A/K}^{1}
.
We have a similar isomorphism for the case of
(A,P)
too.
Hence it suffices to construct an induced homotopy cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\Omega_{A/K}^{q}\otimes_{A}A/t^{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\Omega_{(A,P)/K}^{q}\otimes_{A}A/t^{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{q}.}

Arguing as in Proposition
7.1
,
it suffices to show that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{r}\cdots x_{n}^{r})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{q+1}\cdots x_{n})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}^{r}\cdots x_{n}^{r})\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}}

is homotopy cartesian.
The horizontal maps are surjective,
so it suffices to show that the induced map of the kernels

\{(x_{q+1}\cdots x_{n})/(x_{1}^{r}\cdots x_{n}^{r})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{q}\}_{r}\to\{(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})/(x_{1}^{r}\cdots x_{n}^{r})\tfrac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\tfrac{dx_{q}}{x_{q}}\}_{r}

is an isomorphism of pro-abelian groups.
This holds since the cokernel vanishes and the kernel is Mittag-Leffler zero.
∎

Let
(A,P)
be a semistable
(K[t],\langle t\rangle)
-algebra.
Consider the cdh sheafification of the HKR decomposition of
\mathrm{HC}^{-}(-)
and use the assumption that
K
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
as in
[
32
, Proof of Theorem 3.2]

to obtain

(7.3)

L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\mathrm{HC}^{-}(A/t)\simeq\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{\geq p}[2p].

We have the composite

\displaystyle\mathrm{trc}_{\mathrm{dR}}\colon\mathrm{KH}(A/t)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{trc}}

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\mathrm{HC}^{-}(A/t)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\simeq}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(A/t)/K}^{\geq p}[2p]\to\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{(A/t,P)/K}^{\geq p}[2p],

see
[
15
, (4.1)]
for
\mathrm{trc}
.

Let
X
be a semistable fs log scheme over
(K[[t]],\langle t\rangle)
in the sense that
X
is Zariski locally of the form
\mathrm{Spec}(A\otimes_{K[t]}K[[t]])
for some semistable
(K[t],\langle t\rangle)
-algebra
(A,P)
.
After taking strict étale sheafification,
we obtain

\mathrm{trc}_{\mathrm{dR}}\colon\mathrm{KH}(\underline{X}/t)\to\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X/t,\Omega_{(X/t)/K}^{\geq p})[2p],

where
X/t^{r}:=X\otimes_{K[[t]]}K[[t]]/t^{r}
for every integer
r\geq 1
.
After taking
\pi_{0}
,
we also obtain

\mathrm{trc}_{\mathrm{dR}}\colon\pi_{0}\mathrm{KH}(\underline{X}/t)\to\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{2p}_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}((X/t)/K,\Omega_{(X/t)/K}^{\geq p}).

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
X
be a semistable fs log scheme over
(K[[t]],\langle t\rangle)
.
Then for every integer
r\geq 1
,
there exists a natural isomorphism of pro-abelian groups

\{\pi_{n}\mathrm{HC}^{-}(\underline{X}/t^{r})\}_{r}\cong\{H^{2p-n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\underline{X},\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{\geq p}_{(\underline{X}/t^{r})/K})\}_{r}.

Proof.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}
,
then this is a special case of
[
32
, Corollary 2.2]
.
The general case of
K
follows from the assumption that
K
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{A_{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{C_{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}

be a commutative square of pro-
K
-complexes,
where
A_{r}
,
B
,
C_{r}
, and
D
are
K
-complexes.
Assume that
\{\pi_{*}X_{r}\}_{r}\to\{\pi_{*}Y_{r}\}_{r}
is an isomorphism of pro-
K
-vector spaces,
where
X_{r}:=\mathrm{cofib}(A_{r}\to B)
and
Y_{r}:=\mathrm{cofib}(C_{r}\to D)
.
Assume also that
\pi_{i}D
is finite dimensional for every
i
and there is an isomorphism of pro-
K
-vector spaces
\{C_{r}\}_{r}\cong\{C_{r}^{\prime}\}_{r}
such that each
\pi_{i}C_{r}^{\prime}
is finite dimensional for every
i
.
Then the induced commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\pi_{*}A_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\pi_{*-1}X_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\pi_{*}C_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\pi_{*-1}Y_{r}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

has exact rows.

Proof.

This is just a rephrasing of
[
32
, Part of the proof of Theorem 3.2]
.
The assumption on
\pi_{i}C_{r}
and
\pi_{i}D
implies that the lower row is exact and there exists an isomorphism of pro-
K
-vector spaces
\{U_{r}\}_{r}\cong\{\pi_{i}Y_{r}\}_{r}
such that each
U_{r}
is finite dimensional.
Choose an isomorphism of pro-abelian groups
\{\pi_{i}A_{r}\}\cong\{V_{r}\}_{r}
with maps
\alpha_{r}\colon U_{r}\to V_{r}
for all
r
and a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\pi_{i}X_{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\pi_{i}A_{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{U_{r}\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{V_{r}\}_{r}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Since
\mathrm{im}(\alpha_{r})
is finite dimensional,
\{\mathrm{im}(\alpha_{r})\}_{r}
is Mittag-Leffler.
Using the Milnor sequence,
we see that

0\to\lim_{r}\mathrm{im}(\alpha_{r})\to\lim_{r}\pi_{i}A_{r}\to\lim_{r}\mathrm{im}(\pi_{i}A_{r}\to\pi_{i}B)\to 0

is exact.
Also,

0\to\lim_{r}\mathrm{im}(\pi_{i}A_{r}\to\pi_{i}B)\to\pi_{i}B\to\lim_{r}\pi_{i-1}X_{r}

is exact since
\lim_{r}
is left exact.
Combine these two exact sequences to finish the proof.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
X
be a proper semistable fs log scheme over
(K[[t]],\langle t\rangle)
,
and let
x\in\pi_{0}\mathrm{KH}(\underline{X}/t)
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x
lifts to
\lim_{r}\pi_{0}K(\underline{X}/t^{r})
.

(ii)

\mathrm{trc}_{\mathrm{dR}}(x)
lifts to
\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\lim_{r}H_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}^{2p}((X/t^{r})/K,\Omega_{(X/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p})
.

Proof.

By
[
15
, (4.1)]
,
we have a natural cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K(\underline{X}/t^{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KH}(\underline{X}/t)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{HC}^{-}(\underline{X}/t^{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\mathrm{HC}^{-}(\underline{X}/t).}

Using (
7.3
), Proposition
7.3
, and Lemma
7.4
,
we obtain a commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\pi_{0}K(\underline{X}/t^{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}\mathrm{KH}(\underline{X}/t)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{r}\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2p}(\underline{X}/t^{r},\Omega_{(\underline{X}/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2p}(\underline{X}/t,L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(\underline{X}/t)/K}^{\geq p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}

for some
\mathcal{F}
.
Since
X
is proper and
\Omega_{(X/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p}
is a perfect complex,

H^{2p}_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X/t^{r},\Omega_{(X/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p})
is finite dimensional.
Hence Proposition
7.2
yields a commutative square of pro-
K
-complexes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\underline{X}/t^{r},\Omega_{(\underline{X}/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p})\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\underline{X}/t,L_{\mathrm{cdh}}\Omega_{(\underline{X}/t)/K}^{\geq p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X/t^{r},\Omega_{(X/t^{r})/K}^{\geq p})\}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}(X/t,\Omega_{(X/t)/K}^{\geq p})}

satisfying the assumption of Lemma
7.4
.
Whence the claim follows.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

We do not know how to formulate a statement analogous to Theorem

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{2p}_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}((X/t)/K,\Omega_{(X/t)/K}^{\geq p})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{2p}_{\mathrm{s\acute{e}t}}((X/t)/(K,\mathbb{N}),\Omega_{(X/t)/(K,\mathbb{N})}^{\geq p}).

In future work,
we plan to prove a positive characteristic analogue of Theorem
7.5
generalizing
[
34
, Theorem 0.2]
.
In view of the log Tate conjecture due to Kato-Nakayama-Usui
[
25
, Section 1]

working with the cohomology over

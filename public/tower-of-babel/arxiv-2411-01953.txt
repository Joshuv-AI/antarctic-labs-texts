Twists of intermediate Jacobian fibrations
Yajnaseni Dutta, Dominique Mattei, Evgeny Shinder

Abstract
. We study the sections, Tate–Shafarevich twists, and the period for an OG10 hyperkähler Lagrangian associated to a cubic fourfold. To do so, we introduce the analytic relative Jacobian sheaf for a Lagrangian fibration of a hyperkähler variety. The Tate–Shafarevich group parameterizing twists is isomorphic to the first cohomology group of this sheaf and we compute it in terms of certain analytic Brauer groups associated to the cubic fourfold. We prove that the primitive Hodge lattice of the cubic fourfold is, up to a sign, isometric to a distinguished sublattice of the second cohomology group of the associated OG10 hyperkähler manifold. Among the main tools we use are intersection complexes with integral coefficients, Decomposition Theorem, Hodge modules and Deligne cohomology.



1.
Introduction

To a Lagrangian fibration
f\colon M\to B
of a complex projective hyperkähler manifold
M
over a smooth base
B
, we can
associate
the constructible pushforward sheaf

\Lambda_{M}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{M}
and the relative Jacobian sheaf
\mathcal{J}_{M/B}\coloneqq(\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M})/\Lambda_{M}
, considered in the analytic topology.
Under appropriate assumptions, the two sheaves
\Lambda_{M}
and
\mathcal{J}_{M/B}
control the sections, the twists, and the Hodge lattice
of
f\colon M\to B
.
In this paper, we prove several results in favor of this statement for an OG10 fibration where the fibers are generically given by intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds.

To motivate the problem, let us briefly recall how this works for K3 surfaces.
Let

f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be an
elliptic K3 surface
with a section, which we, for simplicity, assume to have irreducible fibers.
We denote by
\theta,\eta\in{\operatorname{NS}}(S)
the classes
of the section and of the fiber, respectively.
We can consider
the constructible pushforward sheaf
\Lambda_{S}=\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{S}
and
the
sheaf
\mathcal{J}_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}}

of local analytic sections of
f
.
These two sheaves are related by the short sequence

0\to\Lambda_{S}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to\mathcal{J}_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to 0.

The sections of
f
are identified with the sections
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{J}_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}})
and by
[
Shi90
, Theorem 1.3]

(1.1)

{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{J}_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}})\simeq{\operatorname{NS}}(S)/\langle\theta,\eta\rangle.

The twists of
f
are parametrized by the
Tate–Shafarevich group

\Sha(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})\coloneqq{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{J}_{S/\mathbb{P}^{1}}).

It classifies all K3 surfaces, not necessarily projective,
that can be constructed as twists of
S
by regluing the fibers.
An easy spectral sequence
argument gives an isomorphism

(1.2)

\Sha(S/\mathbb{P}^{1})\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{*})=:\Br_{\an}(S)\simeq\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}^{22-{\operatorname{rk}}({\operatorname{NS}}(S))}.

Tate–Shafarevich twists
that are projective
are parametrized by the torsion subgroup of
\Br_{\an}(S)

which is isomorphic to

\Br(S)={\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\mathrm{et}}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})
, see e.g.
[
Huy16
, §11]
.
Finally, for the Hodge lattice, one can show that there is a Hodge isometry (for a natural Hodge lattice structure on the group on the left in the equation below)

(1.3)

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\Lambda_{S})\simeq\langle\theta,\eta\rangle^{\perp}\subset{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}).

All these phenomena seem to be replicated by hyperkähler manifolds with a Lagrangian fibration, which is a higher dimensional analog of elliptic K3 surfaces.
A hyperkähler manifold
M
is a simply connected compact Kähler manifold that admits a nondegenerate complex
2
-form
\sigma
which generates
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(M,\Omega^{2}_{M})
. Whenever
a hyperkähler manifold admits a fibration onto a lower dimensional smooth variety

[

]
and the fibration is forced to be Lagrangian
[

Mat01
]

This means that

restricts trivially to fibers of
f

[

In addition to the relative Jacobian

\mathcal{J}_{M/B}
, another generalization

This sheaf naturally acts on

The relative Albanese sheaf was constructed

]

and in larger generality by Abasheva–Rogov
[

]

as a subsheaf of the sheaf of vertical automorphisms of

When all fibers of

]

The purely algebraic construction of the Albanese sheaf given by

]

1.2.
The sections, twists and the Hodge lattice

We compute the Tate–Shafarevich twists for an
OG10 Lagrangian fibration
f\colon M\to B
associated to a cubic fourfold
X
.
The Tate–Shafarevich group is defined as

\Sha(M/B)\coloneqq{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}_{M/B}).

The standard regluing procedure
[
Mar14
,
AR21
]
allows for every class
\alpha\in\Sha(M/B)
to construct a new fibration
M_{\alpha}\to B
which is locally over
B
isomorphic to
M
but has no section when
\alpha\neq 0
.
Under some mild assumptions, these twists are hyperkähler, and for the torsion classes
\alpha

the twists are projective.

For the next result, for ease of exposition, we will assume that
X
is sufficiently general,
namely that all hyperplane sections of
X
are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. We call this property defect general, see Definition
3.8
. Defect general cubic fourfolds have been explicitly characterized by Marquand–Viktorova
[

as cubic fourfolds containing no planes and no cubic scrolls.
In the case of defect general cubic fourfolds, the sheaf
\mathrm{R}^{4}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{B}
(Proposition
3.3
).

For a smooth cubic fourfold

,
we write

for the hyperplane class
and
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\pr}

the primitive cohomology

.
We express the Tate–Shafarevich group in terms of certain analytic

Decomposition Theorem for Lagrangian fibrations

Perverse sheaves and the Decomposition Theorem have been used extensively in studying Lagrangian fibrations starting from the pioneering work of Ngô
[

]

For the OG10 Lagrangian fibration

2.1.
Constructible sheaves and decomposition theorems

We work with constructible sheaves
on a smooth variety
B

with coefficients in a regular commutative Noetherian ring
R
of finite Krull dimension which for us will be either
\mathbb{Z}
or a field.
For every local system
L
on a
smooth open subset

U\subset Z
of a closed irreducible
subvariety

there is a canonically defined
constructible complex, called the Deligne–Goresky–MacPherson intersection complex
\IC(L)
supported on

[

,
Dim04
]
.
For example, when

is smooth and
L=R_{U}
is a constant sheaf on an open subset

, we have
\IC(L)\simeq R_{Z}[\dim Z]
. More generally we have the following observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
L
is a local system on a smooth open subset
j\colon U\hookrightarrow Z
, then

(2.1)

\mathcal{H}^{-\dim Z}(\IC(L))\simeq\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}L,\quad\mathcal{H}^{k}(\IC(L))=0\text{ for
$k<-\dim Z$.}

Proof.

This follows immediately from Deligne’s description of the intersection complex
[
GM83
, p. 101]
.

\Box

Recall that simple perverse sheaves
with rational coefficients
are precisely the objects of the form
\IC(L)

for a simple
\mathbb{Q}
-local system

L
supported on a locally closed smooth subset of
B
. We will also need the Decomposition Theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber
[
BBD82
]
and more generally of Saito
[
Sai88
]
in the following setting.

Theorem 2.2
(Decomposition Theorem)
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a projective
morphism
with smooth
W
and
B
.
Let
L
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-local system on
W
that underlies a polarized variation of Hodge structure (e.g.
L=\mathbb{Q}_{W}
).
Then we have a quasi-isomorphism

(2.2)

\mathrm{R}f_{*}L[\dim W]\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{i}[-i].

The perverse sheaves
P_{i}
further decompose into
a direct sum of complexes
\IC(L)
for various closed subvarieties
Z\subseteq B
and simple
\mathbb{Q}
-local systems
L
supported on open subsets
U\subset Z
.
Furthermore, we have the relative Hard Lefschetz
isomorphism
P_{-i}\simeq P_{i}
.

Remark 2.3
.

If the morphism
f\colon W\to B
in
Theorem

2.2
is, in addition, flat of relative dimension
d
,
applying proper
base change to (
2.2
) for points
i_{b}\colon b\hookrightarrow B

and using Lemma
2.1

one concludes that
P_{d}\simeq\IC(\mathrm{R}^{d}f_{U*}\mathbb{Q}_{W_{U}})
where
U\subset B
is the locus over which the fibers of
f
are smooth and
f_{U}
is the restriction of
f
to
W_{U}=f^{-1}(U)
.
Using
P_{-d}\simeq P_{d}
, we obtain

P_{-d}\simeq\IC(\mathrm{R}^{0}f_{U*}\mathbb{Q}_{W_{U}}).

Moreover, under the flatness assumption, the argument also implies
P_{i}=0
for all
|i|>d
.

Usually, the Decomposition Theorem
does not work with integer coefficients.
We will use the following partial
replacement.
While considering the push forward of
\mathbb{Z}_{W}
, unlike in the Decomposition Theorem, we prefer to work with
\mathbb{Z}_{W}[0]
, as opposed to
\mathbb{Z}_{W}[\dim W]
.
We write
\mathbb{D}(-)
for the
Verdier duality functor.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a proper
morphism with generic fiber of dimension
d

with smooth
W
and
B
.
Assume that we have classes

\xi_{i}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2i}(W,\mathbb{Z})
,
0\leq i\leq d
such that

\deg_{W}(\xi_{i}\cdot\xi_{d-i}\cdot[W_{b}])=\deg_{W_{b}}(\xi_{i}|_{W_{b}}\cdot\xi_{d-i}|_{W_{b}})=1

where
W_{b}
is a smooth fiber of
f
.
Then
\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}
admits a canonical decomposition

(2.3)

\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i]\oplus\Lambda^{\bullet}[-d]

for some constructible
complex
\Lambda^{\bullet}

which satisfies
\mathbb{D}(\Lambda^{\bullet})\simeq\Lambda^{\bullet}[2\dim B]
.

Proof.

We have canonical isomorphisms

{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i],\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W})={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(B,\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}[2i])={\mathrm{H}}^{2i}(B,\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W})={\mathrm{H}}^{2i}(W,\mathbb{Z}).

For every
i
, let
\gamma_{i}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i],\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W})
be the morphism corresponding to the class
\xi_{i}
.
We put the morphisms
\gamma_{i}
together as follows

\Gamma\coloneqq(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{d})\in{\operatorname{Hom}}\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i],\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\right)

and we set

\Lambda^{\bullet}=\mathrm{cone}\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i]\xrightarrow{\Gamma}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\right)[d].

We will show that
\Lambda^{\bullet}[-d]
is a direct summand
of
\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}
.
Consider the Verdier dual morphisms

\gamma_{i}^{\vee}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathbb{D}(\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}),\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i]))={\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W},\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2d+2i])

\Gamma^{\vee}\coloneqq(\gamma_{d}^{\vee},\ldots,\gamma_{0}^{\vee})\in{\operatorname{Hom}}\left(\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W},\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2j]\right).

We now consider the composition

\gamma_{j}^{\vee}\circ\gamma_{i}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2i],\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2d+2j])={\mathrm{H}}^{2i+2j-2d}(B,\mathbb{Z}).

If
i+j<d
, then this morphism is zero by degree reasons.
On the other hand, to compute
\gamma_{d-i}^{\vee}\circ\gamma_{i}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(B,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}

we can make a base change to a general point
b\in B
to obtain
\gamma_{d-i}^{\vee}\circ\gamma_{i}=1

by the assumption on the classes
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{d}
.

Thus the composition
\Gamma^{\vee}\circ\Gamma
is a lower triangular matrix with
1
on the diagonal,
hence it is an isomorphism.
This means that
\Gamma
is an embedding of a direct summand and we obtain (
2.3
).
Finally we note that
\Lambda^{\bullet}

is Verdier self-dual up to shift by construction,
because up to an appropriate shift
\Lambda^{\bullet}
is isomorphic to the cone of
\Gamma
and to the cone of
\Gamma^{\vee}
.

\Box

Remark 2.5
.

When
d
is odd, we see from the proof above that the choice of classes
\xi_{0},\dots,\xi_{\frac{d-1}{2}}
induces maps

(2.4)

\mathbb{Z}_{B}[0]\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-(d-1)]\xrightarrow{\gamma_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus\gamma_{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\xrightarrow{\gamma^{\vee}_{\frac{d-1}{2}}\oplus\dots\oplus\gamma^{\vee}_{0}}\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-(d+1)]\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2d],

whose composition is trivial. For any integer
k
, the maps in cohomology induced by (
2.4
) are given by:

(2.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}{\mathrm{H}}^{k-2i}(B,\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(W,\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\limits_{j=\frac{d+1}{2}}^{d}{\mathrm{H}}^{k-2j}(B,\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{i}\beta_{k-2i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{i}f^{*}(\beta_{k-2i})\cdot\xi_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{j}f_{*}(\alpha\cdot\xi_{d-j})}

The existence of classes
\xi_{\frac{d+1}{2}},\dots,\xi_{d}
with
\deg(\xi_{i}\cdot\xi_{d-i}\cdot[W_{b}])=1
ensures that the maps in (
2.4
) split off direct summands. For any integer
k
the cohomology group
{\mathrm{H}}^{k-d}(B,\Lambda^{\bullet})
can be computed as the cohomology of the complex (
2.5
).
When the classes
\xi_{0},\dots,\xi_{\frac{d-1}{2}}
are algebraic (which will be the case in our applications), this gives the group
{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(B,\Lambda^{\bullet})
an integral Hodge structure.

In some situations
\Lambda^{\bullet}

will be an intersection complex.
We give a simple example below but
the case of main interest for us is the universal hyperplane section of a cubic fourfold in Theorem
3.9
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\rho\colon\mathcal{C}\to B

be a flat projective morphism of relative dimension
1
with connected fibers. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
and
B
are smooth.
Assume that there exists a class
\xi\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{Z})
which restricts as a generator of the second cohomology group on
the smooth fibers of
f
.
Then we can apply Proposition

2.4

to the classes
\xi_{0}=1\in{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{Z})
and
\xi_{1}=\xi\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{Z})

to obtain

\mathrm{R}\rho_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathbb{Z}_{B}\oplus\Lambda^{\bullet}[-1]\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{B}[-2].

The complex
\Lambda^{\bullet}
has cohomology sheaves only in degrees
0
and
1
.
The sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Lambda^{\bullet})

is supported on the set of points
of
B
parameterizing reducible fibers.

Assume that smooth fibers have positive genus so that the support of
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\Lambda^{\bullet})
is dense in
B
.
Then using the axiomatic description of the intersection complex
[
GM83
, p. 107]

it is easy to see that

\Lambda^{\bullet}

is an intersection complex
if and only if the locus of reducible
fibers of
\rho
is contained in a subset
of codimension two in
B
.

2.2.
The tcf property

Let
F
be a sheaf of abelian groups on a smooth connected
variety
B
. Given an
open subvariety
j\colon U\hookrightarrow B
it will be important for us to know
when
F
extends itself from the open set
U
, i.e., it is
isomorphic to
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}F|_{U}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
Z\subset B
be a proper Zariski closed subset
with open complement
U=B\setminus Z
.
Let
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow B

and
j\colon U\hookrightarrow B
be the embedding morphisms.
For a sheaf
F
of abelian groups on
B

the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

The unit of adjunction
F\to\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}F|_{U}
is an isomorphism

(b)

\mathcal{H}^{0}i^{!}F=\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}F=0

Proof.

\Box

Equivalence between (a) and (b) follows from
the standard exact sequence

0\to i_{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}i^{!}F\to F\to\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}F|_{U}\to i_{*}\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}F\to 0.

Since

\mathcal{H}^{0}i^{!}F
is the torsion
of
F
with respect to
Z
,
we define
the
cotorsion

F
with respect to
Z

to be the sheaf

\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}F
.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
F
is a
tcf (torsion and cotorsion free) sheaf with respect to
Z

if the equivalent conditions of Lemma
2.7

are satisfied.
We say that
F
is
tcf everywhere on
B

if
F
is tcf for every proper closed subset
Z\subset B
.

If
Z
is fixed or clear from context we just say that
F
is tcf.
It follows immediately from the definition that tcf is a local property in the analytic topology on
B
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
L
be a local system on a smooth variety
B
.
Then it is tcf with respect to any
Z\subset B
.
Indeed, for every complex ball
U_{\varepsilon}\subset B
and

R
-module
M
we have
the identification

M={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(U_{\varepsilon},M)={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(U_{\varepsilon}\setminus Z,M),

which
implies that
M
satisfies condition (a) of
Lemma

2.7
.

It is clear that if
F
is tcf with respect to
Z
, then it is so with respect to any closed subset of
Z
.
Furthermore, we also have the
following
induction properties.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
F
be a sheaf of abelian groups on a smooth variety
B
.

(i) Let
T\subset Z\subset B
be Zariski closed subsets.
Assume that
F
is tcf with respect to
T

and
F|_{B\setminus T}
is tcf with respect to
Z\setminus T
.
Then
F
is tcf with respect to
Z
.

(ii) If
T\subset B
is a Zariski closed subset such
that
F
is tcf with respect to
T

and
F|_{B\setminus T}
is tcf everywhere on

B\setminus T
, then
F
is tcf everywhere on
B
.

Proof.

(i) The easiest way to prove is this is to use the property (a) of Lemma
2.7
.
Writing
j_{1}\colon B\setminus Z\hookrightarrow B\setminus T
,
j_{2}\colon B\setminus T\hookrightarrow B

and
j\colon B\setminus Z\to B

for the open embeddings we have

\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}=\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{2*}\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{1*}
and the result follows.

(ii) Let
Z\subset B
be a proper closed subset.
Since
F
is tcf with respect to
T
it is also tcf
with respect to
Z\cap T
.
Therefore, we can apply (i) to

Z\cap T\subset Z\subset B

to deduce that
F
is tcf with respect to
Z
.

\Box

Proposition 2.11
.

If
L
is a local system on
U\subset B
then

\mathcal{H}^{-\dim B}(\IC(L))

is tcf everywhere on
B
.

Proof.

Since
L
is a local system,
by Example
2.9
, we know that
L
is tcf
everywhere on
U
.
From (
2.1
) we see that

\mathcal{H}^{-\dim B}(\IC(L))\simeq\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}L
and the latter is by definition tcf with respect to
B\setminus U
.
Thus, we deduce from Lemma
2.10

that
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}L
is tcf everywhere on
B
.

\Box

We now come to the main result of this section, that under certain conditions on the fibers the first pushforward of the integral constant sheaf is tcf.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a proper morphism.
We say that a scheme fiber
W_{b}=f^{-1}(b)
is
strictly multiple
if
W_{b}
is nowhere reduced and
for the generic multiplicities
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
of irreducible components of
W_{b}
we have
\gcd(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})>1
.

We say that

f
has no strictly
multiple fibers in codimension one
if the set of points
of
B
with strictly multiple fibers
is contained in a Zariski closed subset
of codimension at least two.

Theorem 2.13
.

Assume that
f\colon W\to B
is a flat proper surjective morphism with connected fibers and with smooth
W
and
B
.
Then the following statements hold
for
F=\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}
.

(1)

F
is tcf with respect to any closed subset
Z\subset B
of codimension at least two.

(2)

If
Z\subset B
is an irreducible divisor such that fibers of
f
over general points in
Z
are not strictly multiple, then
F
is tcf with respect to
Z
.

In particular, we have the following.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
as in
Theorem

2.13
. If
f
has no strictly multiple fibers in codimension one,
then
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}
is tcf everywhere on
B
. In particular, let
j\colon U\hookrightarrow B
be the open locus over which the fibers of
f
are smooth, then
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}\left(\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{U*}\mathbb{Z}_{W_{U}}\right)\simeq\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}

Proof.

The sheaf
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}

is tcf everywhere
since by Lemma
2.10
and the assumption on
f
,
to
check the tcf property
it suffices to consider irreducible subvarieties of type (1) or (2) in Theorem
2.13
.
\Box

While the proof of Theorem
2.13
requires some work, the similar statement for
F_{\mathbb{Q}}
follows almost immediately from the Decomposition Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a flat projective surjective morphism between smooth varieties. Then
the
\mathbb{Q}
-constructible sheaf
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{W}
is a tcf sheaf.

Proof.

Let
n=\dim B
and
d
be the dimension of the fibers.
Let
U\subset B
denote the locus where
f
is smooth. We consider the decomposition of
\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{W}[n+d]
as in Theorem
2.2
.
The sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{j}(P_{i})
vanish when
|i|>d
by Remark
2.3
and also for
j<-n
by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore, we obtain

(2.6)

\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{W}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=-d}^{d}\mathcal{H}^{-n-d-i+1}(P_{i})=\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}(P_{-d})\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-n}(P_{-d+1})

We claim that
\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}(P_{-d})=0
. Indeed, since
P_{-d}\simeq P_{d}
, we have

0=\mathrm{R}^{2d+1}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{B}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=-d}^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{-n-i+d+1}(P_{i})\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}(P_{d}).

The remaining term
\mathcal{H}^{-n}(P_{-d+1})
in the right hand side of (
2.6
) is the lowest cohomology sheaf of an intersection complex with dense support.
This is because, by
Lemma

2.1
intersection complexes with proper support have trivial cohomology sheaves in degree
-n
. The result follows by
Proposition

2.11
.

\Box

To prove Theorem
2.13
, we need the following
result which will help us check the tcf property.

Lemma 2.16
.

For any closed
embedding
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow B
with smooth
B
the complex

i^{!}R_{B}
sits in cohomological degrees
\geq 2{\operatorname{codim}}(Z)
.

Proof.

Since
B
is smooth we have

i^{!}R_{B}\simeq i^{!}\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{B}[-2\dim B]\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{Z}[-2\dim B]
,
where
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{B}
and

\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{Z}
are
the dualizing complexes of
B
and
Z
respectively.
We have

[
GM83
, p. 91]

(2.7)

\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}_{Z})=0\text{ for }j<-2\dim Z

so that
i^{!}R_{B}
is acyclic in degrees below
2(\dim B-\dim Z)
which implies the result.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.13
.

For both statements we use the spectral sequence

(2.8)

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathcal{H}^{p}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{q}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{p+q}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}

The terms of the spectral sequence that
we need to control
are

(2.9)

E_{2}^{0,1}=\mathcal{H}^{0}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\text{ and }E_{2}^{1,1}=\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}.

We will make use of the fact that by base change we have

i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\simeq\mathrm{R}f^{\prime}_{*}i^{\prime!}\mathbb{Z}_{W}

where
i^{\prime}\colon f^{-1}(Z)\to W
and
f^{\prime}\colon f^{-1}(Z)\to Z
are the natural morphisms.
Note also that we
have
\mathrm{R}^{0}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=\mathbb{Z}_{B}
because
f
has connected fibers
hence

E_{2}^{p,0}=\mathcal{H}^{p}i^{!}\mathbb{Z}_{B}=0\text{ for $p<2{\operatorname{codim}}(Z)$ }

by Lemma
2.16
.

(1)
In this case

E^{p,0}_{2}
vanish
for
p\leq 3
, which implies
that the terms
(
2.9
)
are not affected by the differentials of the spectral sequence, i.e.,
E_{2}^{0,1}=E_{\infty}^{0,1}
, and similarly,
E_{2}^{1,1}=E_{\infty}^{1,1}
.

Using Lemma
2.16
again,
we see that
\mathcal{H}^{p+q}i^{\prime!}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=0
for all
p+q<2{\operatorname{codim}}(Z)
. Since
f^{\prime}_{*}
is left exact, we have

\mathcal{H}^{p+q}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=\mathrm{R}^{p+q}f^{\prime}_{*}i^{\prime!}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=0\text{ for $p+q<2{\operatorname{codim}}(Z)$. }

By the
{\operatorname{codim}}(Z)\geq 2
assumption
we have, in particular, that

E_{\infty}^{0,1}=E_{\infty}^{1,1}=0
.
This implies that the terms
(
2.9
) also must
vanish.

(2) Applying Lemma
2.10

to
{\operatorname{Sing}}(Z)\subset Z\subset B
,
we see that
it suffices to show that the restriction
F|_{B\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(Z)}
is tcf with respect to the divisor
Z\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(Z)
.
Thus, replacing
B
by
B\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(Z)
we can assume that
Z
is smooth.
By further shrinking
B
, we can assume that fibers of
f
over points in
Z
are not strictly multiple.
Now in the spectral sequence (
2.8
)
the bottom row only has one nonzero term
E_{2}^{2,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{Z}
.
Indeed, since both
Z
and
B
are smooth, we have
i^{!}\mathbb{Z}_{B}=\mathbb{Z}_{Z}[-2]
. In particular, as in (1), the differentials do not affect the
\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=E_{2}^{1,1}
term.
Let us show that
the differential

\mathcal{H}^{0}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=E_{2}^{0,1}\to E_{2}^{2,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{Z}

is also trivial.
For that, it suffices to check that
the boundary map of the spectral sequence

\partial\colon E_{2}^{2,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{Z}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}

is injective.
The sheaf on the right computes the top homology groups of the fibers
f^{-1}(z)
,
z\in Z
.
Using the flat pullback formalism
[
Ver76
, §3.2]
one can see that the morphism

\partial
corresponds to the global section of this sheaf given by the cycle class of the fiber
and so it is injective.
Thus,

E_{2}^{2,0}=E_{\infty}^{2,0}=\mathbb{Z}_{Z}=\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathcal{H}^{2}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W},

where
\mathrm{L}_{0}
stands for the lowest term of the Leray filtration.

Furthermore, we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}i^{!}\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}=E_{2}^{1,1}=E_{\infty}^{1,1}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\mathcal{H}^{2}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}/\mathrm{L}_{0}\mathcal{H}^{2}i^{!}\mathrm{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\subset{\operatorname{Coker}}(\partial).

By Lemma
2.15
the sheaf in the left-hand side
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-torsion sheaf.
On the other hand,
since
\partial

is given by the cycle class of the fiber, and
the fibers are not strictly multiple,
\partial

has a torsion-free cokernel.
Thus, we see that the terms (
2.9
)
are trivial.
\Box

2.3.
Deligne cohomology groups

Recall that for any smooth variety
W
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

there is a Deligne complex

[
EV88
]
,
[
Voi07
, 13.3.1]

\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}}\coloneqq[\mathbb{Z}_{W}\overset{(2\pi i)^{m}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{W}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{W}^{1}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\ldots\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{W}^{m-1}]

where the first term of the complex is in cohomological degree zero.
Deligne complexes can also be defined to be a one-term complex
\mathbb{Z}_{W}
for
m<0
but we will not need that.

Deligne cohomology groups are defined as hypercohomology groups of the complexes
\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}}
.
We will mostly need Deligne cohomology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
, however some results
are naturally explained in a more general context.

We have

\mathbb{Z}_{W}(0)_{\mathrm{D}}=\mathbb{Z}_{W},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{W}(1)_{\mathrm{D}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{W}^{*}[-1]

and

(2.10)

\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}\coloneqq[\mathbb{Z}\overset{(2\pi i)^{2}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{W}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{W}^{1}]\simeq[\mathcal{O}^{*}_{W}\xrightarrow{d\log}\Omega_{W}^{1}][-1]

The only nonvanishing
cohomology sheaves of the
complex
\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
are

\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}=\mathbb{C}^{*}_{W},\quad\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}={\operatorname{Coker}}(d\colon\mathcal{O}_{W}\to\Omega^{1}_{W}).

In particular, the connecting morphism

\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}[-2]\to\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
induces
a long exact sequence,
which we will use later:

(2.11)

\ldots\to{\mathrm{H}}^{k-1}(W,\mathbb{C}^{*})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(W,\mathbb{Z}_{W}(2)_{\mathrm{D}})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{k-2}(W,{\operatorname{Coker}}(d))\to{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(W,\mathbb{C}^{*})\to\ldots

Now we explain how to compute Deligne cohomology groups in various cases.
These results are well-known, however, we include them in order to freely use them for the computations in Proposition
3.6
.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
W
be a smooth projective variety.
Assume that for some
k\geq 0
we have

{\mathrm{H}}^{2k-1}(W,\mathbb{Z})={\mathrm{H}}^{2k+1}(W,\mathbb{Z})=0

and
{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})

is a torsion-free Hodge structure of Hodge–Tate type.
Then for all
m\geq 0
we have

{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})=\begin{cases}0&m>k\\
{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})&m\leq k\\
\end{cases}

{\mathrm{H}}^{2k+1}(W,\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{*}&m>k\\
0&m\leq k\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Using
[
Voi02
, Proposition 12.26]
we get an exact sequence

(2.12)

0\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{C})/F^{m}\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2k+1}(W,\mathbb{Z}_{W}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})\to 0.

The result follows immediately since the Hodge filtration on the Hodge–Tate structure
is a filtration such that

\Box

{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{C})/F^{m}=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{C})&m>k\\
0&m\leq k\\
\end{cases}

Example 2.18
.

Deligne cohomology groups of the projective space

\mathbb{P}^{n}

are nonzero in precisely
n+1
degrees and
are given by

{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{Z}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}^{*}&k\text{ odd, }1\leq k\leq 2m-1\\
\mathbb{Z}&k\text{ even, }2m\leq k\leq 2n\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

This can be computed
using Lemma
2.17
or
directly
from the definition by induction on

, or
using the projective bundle formula

[

, Proposition 8.5]
.
Later we will need the following particular case

(2.13)

{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{5},\mathbb{Z}(2)_{\mathrm{D}})=\mathbb{C}^{*}[-1]\oplus\mathbb{C}^{*}[-3]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[-4]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[-6]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[-8]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[-10].

If

is a smooth projective variety with

a Hodge structure of K3 type we set

\Br_{\an}^{2k}(W):=H^{k-1,k+1}(W,\mathbb{C})/\image(H^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2k}(W,\mathbb{C})\to H^{k-1,k+1}(W)),

see Appendix
B
for the definitions and the details of this construction.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
W
be a smooth projective variety.

)

Example 2.20
.

If

Remark 2.21
.

Deligne cohomology groups are closely related to algebraic cycles and intermediate Jacobians. When
Y
is a smooth projective variety, by
[
EV88
, (7.9)]
, for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there is a short exact sequence

0\to J^{m}(Y)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2m}(Y,\mathbb{Z}_{Y}(m)_{\mathrm{D}})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2m}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\to 0

where

is the

{\mathrm{H}}^{2m-1}(Y,\mathbb{C})/({\mathrm{H}}^{2m-1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})+F^{m}{\mathrm{H}}^{2m-1}(Y,\mathbb{C}))
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{D}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{*}[-1]
, for

0\to{\operatorname{Pic}}^{0}(Y)\to{\operatorname{Pic}}(Y)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Moreover, in the case when

is a

threefold, we let
J(Y)\coloneqq J^{2}(Y)
.
The cycle class map

\CH^{2}(Y)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
factors
through
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z}_{Y}(2)_{\mathrm{D}})

[
EV88
, §7]
in way compatible with the Abel–Jacobi map. Since the group of homological trivial cycles

is isomorphic to

[
Mur74
, (8)]
,
(

) is nothing but the short exact sequence

0\to\CH^{2}_{0}(Y)\to\CH^{2}(Y)\xrightarrow{{\rm deg}}\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

of an arbitrary smooth cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
.
We denote the two sheaves corresponding to the first two terms in (
3.1
) by
\mathcal{J}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
respectively, see
Definition

3.14

and
Definition

3.16
.
We introduce some other objects, most importantly the constructible
sheaf
\Lambda
(
3.14
)
and a
closely related integral intersection complex
\Lambda^{\bullet}
,
see
Theorem

3.9
.
We compute
their cohomology groups in

Theorem

3.11
,

Theorem

3.19
and

Theorem

3.21
.

3.1.
Singular cubic threefolds

We start by recalling some results about cubic threefolds with isolated singularities. The following is a well-known result. We provide a complete proof for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
Y
is a cubic threefold with isolated singularities, then its singularities are
Gorenstein and canonical, in particular, rational.
Furthermore, if
Y
is not a cone, then the singularities are terminal.

Proof.

Singularities of
Y
are Gorenstein because
Y
is a hypersurface.
If
Y
is a cone, then the standard computation of the canonical class of the resolution

[
Kol13
, Corollary 3.4]

shows that the singularity is canonical.

Assume that
Y
is not a cone, and let
p\in Y
be a singular point. Let
S
be a general hyperplane section of
Y
through
p
.
By Bertini’s theorem
S
is a cubic surface with an isolated singularity at
p
.
Since
Y
is not a cone,
S
is not a cone either.
Indeed if a general hyperplane section through
p
was a cone, then the Hilbert scheme of lines on
Y
passing through
p
would be
2
-dimensional which would force
Y
to be a cone with vertex
p
.
A singular cubic surface
S
with
isolated singularities and
which is not a cone must have
du Val singularities
[
BW79
]
.
Thus,
Y
has a so-called compound du Val singularity at
p
, which is terminal by
[
Rei83
]
.

Finally note that canonical singularities are rational, see e.g.
[
Rei87
, (3.8)]
.

\Box

We have the
class group
{\operatorname{Cl}}(Y)
of rational equivalence
classes of Weil divisors
and the Picard group
{\operatorname{Pic}}(Y)
is its subgroup parameterizing Cartier divisors.
We consider the so-called
defect

(3.2)

\delta_{Y}={\operatorname{rk}}({\operatorname{Cl}}(Y)/{\operatorname{Pic}}(Y))=b_{4}(Y)-b_{2}(Y).

Here the second equality is a result by
Namikawa–Steenbrink
[
NS95
, Theorem 3.2]
which uses the fact that

Y
has rational isolated
hypersurface singularities (Lemma
3.1
).
Thus,
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if and only if the defect is zero.

Example 3.2
.

It is well-known that if
Y
has a single ordinary double point then
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and

\delta_{Y}=0
.
On the other hand, if
Y
contains a plane
\Pi\subset Y
then
\Pi
is a Weil divisor. It can not be Cartier by degree reasons
and so
\delta_{Y}\geq 1
.

[
MV24
, Theorem 1.1]
gives a general explicit formula for the defect
\delta_{Y}
of a cubic threefold.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a cubic threefold with isolated singularities.
The odd degree integral cohomology groups of

Y
are trivial except for
{\mathrm{H}}^{3}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

which is a torsion-free group.
Write
h\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
for the hyperplane class.
The even degree cohomology groups of
Y
are

{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z},\quad{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\cdot h,\quad{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus(1+\delta_{Y})},\quad{\mathrm{H}}^{6}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}

where
\delta_{Y}\geq 0
is the defect (
3.2
) of
Y
.

Assume that
\delta_{Y}=0
. Then
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}[\ell]
for any line
\ell\subset Y

contained in the smooth locus of
Y
.
In particular, in this case the even cohomology
groups
{\mathrm{H}}^{2*}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

satisfy the integral Poincaré duality.

Proof.

The result about
{\mathrm{H}}^{j}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
for
j\leq 2

follows from the Weak Lefschetz Theorem.
The Betti numbers of
Y
except for
b_{3}(Y)
and
b_{4}(Y)
are determined in
[
Dim92
, Chapter 5, Theorem 4.3]
.
By the same result all integral homology and cohomology groups are torsion free except possibly
{\mathrm{H}}_{3}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
and
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

which have isomorphic torsion by the Universal Coefficient Theorem.

Let us show that
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free.
If
Y
is smooth, this follows from the integral Poincaré duality, see
[
Dim92
, Chapter 5, Lemma 3.1]
.
Assume now that
Y
is singular. We use an
argument similar to that of
[
MV24
, Proposition 3.2]
, where rational cohomology groups of
Y
are computed.
Take any
p\in{\operatorname{Sing}}(Y)

and consider the blow up
{\operatorname{Bl}}_{p}(Y)
with exceptional divisor
E\subset{\operatorname{Bl}}_{p}(Y)
.
The singular point
p
is a double point
or
Y
is a cone over a smooth cubic surface.
Indeed, since
Y
is a cubic, if
p
is not a double point, all the terms in an affine equation of
Y
near
p
are cubic, in which case
Y
is a cone over a cubic surface.
This surface has to be smooth since we assumed that
Y
has isolated singularities.
Thus,
E
is either a (possibly singular) quadric surface or a smooth cubic surface, and in each case

{\mathrm{H}}^{3}(E,\mathbb{Z})=0

and part of the standard exact sequence for a blow-up square gives an exact sequence

(3.3)

0={\mathrm{H}}^{3}(E,\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{4}({\operatorname{Bl}}_{p}(Y),\mathbb{Z}).

It is well known that
{\operatorname{Bl}}_{p}(Y)
is isomorphic to the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at the possibly singular sextic curve, given by the
complete intersection of a quadric and a cubic
[
CML09
, 3.1]
.
The standard blow-up formula for cohomology applies in this case, showing that
{\mathrm{H}}^{*}({\operatorname{Bl}}_{p}(Y),\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free. Indeed, curves have torsion-free cohomology groups.
Thus,
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
is also torsion-free by (
3.3
)

Now assume that
\delta_{Y}=0
.
For any line
\ell\subset Y
in the smooth locus of
Y
we have
h\cdot\ell=1
,
so the class
\ell
is primitive,
hence it is a generator of
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}
.
It is now clear that in this case
{\mathrm{H}}^{2*}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
satisfy integral Poincaré duality.

\Box

3.2.
Cohomology groups and pushforwards

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth cubic fourfold
and
\mathcal{Y}\subset X\times(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{\vee}

be the universal hyperplane section of
X
.
We write
p\colon\mathcal{Y}\to B\coloneqq(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{\vee}

for the natural projection,
and

q\colon\mathcal{Y}\to X

for the second projection,
which is a Zariski locally trivial

\mathbb{P}^{4}
-fibration.
Fibers of
q
are irreducible cubic threefolds with at worst isolated singularities.
Indeed, if
X=\mathrm{V}(F)
and
Y=\mathrm{V}(x_{0})\cap X
is a coordinate hyperplane section of
X
, then

{\operatorname{Sing}}(Y)\cap\mathrm{V}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{0}}\right)\subset{\operatorname{Sing}}(X).

We denote by
h\in{\operatorname{Pic}}(X)

and
H\in{\operatorname{Pic}}(B)
the hyperplane section classes and their pullbacks to
\mathcal{Y}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The integral cohomology groups of
X
and of
\mathcal{Y}
are given by

(3.4)

{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(X,\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }k=0,2,6,8\\
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 23}&\text{if }k=4\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}\quad\quad{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if }k=0,16\\
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 2}&\text{if }k=2,14\\
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 25}&\text{if }k=4,12\\
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 26}&\text{if }k=6,10\\
\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 27}&\text{if }k=8\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Cohomology groups of
X

are well known, see e.g.
[
Huy23b
, §1.3]
.
For the cohomology groups of
\mathcal{Y}
we use
projective bundle formula for
q\colon\mathcal{Y}\to X
:

(3.5)

{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{4}q^{*}{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cdot p^{*}H^{i}.

\Box

By the integral Poincaré duality
the intersection pairing on
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is unimodular, in particular since
h^{2}
is primitive, there exists a class

(3.6)

b\in{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})

such that
\deg_{X}(h^{2}\cdot b)=1
.
This class is algebraic for some special cubic fourfolds, but if
X
is very general,
b
in not an algebraic class.
We will need this class later.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

(3.7)

{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})=\mathbb{C}[-1]\oplus\mathbb{C}[-3],\quad{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1))=\mathbb{C}^{6}[-3]

Proof.

This is a standard calculation, see
[
Huy23b
, §1.2]
.

\Box

For the next result we define

(3.8)

\Br^{4}_{\an}(X)\coloneqq{\mathrm{H}}^{1,3}(X)/\image({\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{C})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1,3}(X))

This is a higher-dimensional analog of the analytic
Brauer group, see Appendix
B
, in particular, Example
B.1
for more details about this notion.

Proposition 3.6
.

The cohomology groups of the second Deligne complex on
X
and on
\mathcal{Y}

are given as follows:

(3.9)

{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{X}(2)_{\mathrm{D}})=\begin{cases}0&k=0,2\\
\mathbb{C}^{*}&k=1,3\\
{\mathrm{H}}^{2,2}(X,\mathbb{Z})&k=4\\
\Br^{4}_{\an}(X)&k=5\\
{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(X,\mathbb{Z})&k\geq 6\end{cases}

(3.10)

{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{Y}}(2)_{\mathrm{D}})=\begin{cases}0&k=0,2\\
\mathbb{C}^{*}={\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{O}^{*})&k=1\\
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}={\mathrm{H}}^{1,1}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{C})/{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z})&k=3\\
{\mathrm{H}}^{2,2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{2,2}(X,\mathbb{Z})&k=4\\
\Br^{4}_{\an}(\mathcal{Y})=\Br^{4}_{\an}(X)&k=5\\
{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{Z})&k\geq 6\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Deligne cohomology groups of
X

can be computed immediately using Lemma
2.17
and Lemma
2.19
.
Deligne cohomology groups of
\mathcal{Y}

can be computed in the same way, or using the projective bundle formula
[
EV88
, Proposition 8.5]
applied to
q\colon\mathcal{Y}\to X
.
We omit the details of these computations.
\Box

Proposition 3.7
.

For any smooth cubic fourfold

X
we have

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}

and

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{B}[0]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[-1]\oplus\Omega^{1}_{B}[-2],\quad\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/B}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}[-1]\oplus\Omega^{1}_{B}[-2].

Furthermore, for the complex
\Omega^{\leq 1}_{\mathcal{Y}}=[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}]
(with the first term in degree zero) we have

(3.11)

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{\leq 1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\Omega^{\leq 1}_{B}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[-2]\oplus\Omega^{1}_{B}[-3].

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{Y}
is a smooth divisor on
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq X\times B

and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{Y})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{B}(1)
.
Let us write
i\colon\mathcal{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
for the closed embedding.
We compute derived pushforwards
using the formula
\mathrm{R}p_{2*}(\mathcal{L}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime})={\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

Applying
\mathrm{R}p_{2*}
to the short exact sequence
for the divisor
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-\mathcal{Y})\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\to i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to 0

we get
\mathrm{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\mathcal{O}_{B}
.
Now pushing forward the same exact sequence
twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(-\mathcal{Y})

we obtain

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(-\mathcal{Y})=0
.
Therefore, applying
\mathrm{R}p_{2*}
to
the conormal exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}(-\mathcal{Y})\to i^{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to 0

we get

(3.12)

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}=\mathrm{R}p_{*}i^{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathrm{R}p_{*}i^{*}p_{1}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}\oplus\mathrm{R}p_{*}i^{*}p_{2}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{B}

Since
\mathrm{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}[0]
, the second term in the right-hand side of (
3.12
)
is quasi-isomorphic to
\Omega^{1}_{B}[0]
by the projection formula.
Let us now consider the first term

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}p_{*}i^{*}p_{1}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}=\mathrm{R}p_{2*}i_{*}i^{*}p_{1}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}=\mathrm{R}p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}).

Using the short exact sequence for the divisor
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset\mathcal{X}
, it is easy to obtain a distinguished triangle

(3.13)

{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}(-1)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}\to\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}.

Applying (
3.7
),
the triangle becomes

\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus 6}(-1)[-3]\to\mathcal{O}_{B}[-1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[-3]\to\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}.

Thus,
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is the direct sum of the shifted
cohomology sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
and

\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
satisfying

0\to\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus 6}(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{B}\to 0

which is the Euler exact sequence,
giving
\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{B}
. This proves the decomposition for

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}
.
To compute
\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}/B}^{1}

we consider the sequence

0\to p^{*}\Omega_{B}^{1}\to\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}\to\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}/B}^{1}\to 0

where the first morphism is injective because
B
is smooth.
Applying
\mathrm{R}p_{*}
to this sequence gives the result.

It remains to prove (
3.11
). We compute

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{\leq 1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\mathrm{cone}(\mathrm{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}})[-1]\simeq\mathrm{cone}(\mathcal{O}_{B}[0]\to\Omega^{1}_{B}[0]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[-1]\oplus\Omega^{1}_{B}[-2])[-1].

For degree reasons, out of the three components of the morphism inside the cone, only the first one can be nontrivial.
The first morphism is the differential. So,

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{\leq 1}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\mathrm{cone}(\mathcal{O}_{B}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}_{B})[-1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[-2]\oplus\Omega^{1}_{B}[-3]

which gives (
3.11
).

\Box

Definition 3.8
.

We say that a smooth cubic fourfold

X
is
defect general
if
all hyperplane sections

Y\subset X

have defect (
3.2
)

\delta_{Y}=0
.

It can be deduced from the results of
[
LSV17
]
that if
X
is a very general cubic fourfold then
it is defect general.
It follows from the recent results of Marquand and Viktorova
[
MV24
, Theorem 1.2]

that
X
is defect general
if and only if

X
does not contain a plane or a cubic scroll.

We consider a constructible sheaf on
B
defined as

(3.14)

\Lambda\coloneqq\mathrm{R}^{3}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{Y}}.

At the general point
b\in B
it has a rank

10
, the middle Betti number of a smooth cubic threefold.
This sheaf and its cohomology play the key role in the paper.
We also have another constructible sheaf
\mathcal{Q}

4.1.
Perverse filtration and BBF form on a Lagrangian fibration

For basic facts about hyperkähler manifolds and Lagrangian fibrations see
[
Huy99
,
Bea11
]
and references therein.
Recall that any hyperkähler variety
M

admits the so-called Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki (BBF) form
q

on
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
which satisfies

(4.1)

c_{M}\cdot q(u)^{n}=\int_{M}u^{2n}.

Here
\dim M=2n

and

c_{M}
is the Fujiki constant.
There is a more general version of this relation: for a set of classes
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2n}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, we can apply (
4.1
)
to a linear combination
u=t_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+t_{2n}\alpha_{2n}
. Expanding both sides as polynomials in
t_{1},\ldots,t_{2n}

and comparing the
coefficients of
t_{1}\cdots t_{2n}

we obtain the sum over all permutations
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}

(4.2)

c_{M}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}}q(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\alpha_{\sigma(2)})\cdots q(\alpha_{\sigma(2n-1)},\alpha_{\sigma(2n)})=(2n)!\int_{M}\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{2n}

Let
f\colon M\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration of relative dimension
n
over

.
We will use (
4.2
) to compute the BBF form on the primitive part of
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, see Proposition
4.2
.

We start by applying the Decomposition Theorem

2.2
to decompose

{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
into simpler pieces.
We have

{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})={\mathrm{H}}^{2-2n}(M,\mathbb{Q}[2n])\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{2-n}(B,P_{-n})\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{1-n}(B,P_{-n+1})\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{-n}(B,P_{-n+2}).

All other cohomology terms vanish by Lemma
2.1
.
We have
P_{-n}\simeq\mathbb{Q}[n]
by Remark
2.3
and
for the remaining two complexes, we use the notation

P^{\bullet}\coloneqq P_{-n+1}[-n],\quad R^{\bullet}\coloneqq P_{-n+2}[-n].

One can show that

is supported in cohomological degrees
0
and
1
.
The splitting above can be rewritten as

(4.3)

{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(B,\mathbb{Q})\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(B,P^{\bullet})\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(B,R^{\bullet}).

We write
\eta\coloneqq f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, so that
by (
4.1

.
The first term in decomposition (
4.3
) is
\mathbb{Q}\eta
.
By
[
SY22
, Theorem 0.2]

the last piece is also one dimensional.
The splitting is not canonical
but we have the following observation.

Lemma 4.1

The kernel of the natural morphism

equals

and we have a canonical isomorphism

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(B,P^{\bullet})\simeq\eta^{\perp}/\mathbb{Q}\eta.

Proof.

Let

.
Consider the fiber
M_{b}
over a general point

As in
[

, Proof of Theorem 0.4(b)]

the restriction of cohomology classes to the fiber factors as

{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(B,R^{\bullet})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M_{b},\mathbb{Q})

and so

(4.4)

W\subset{\operatorname{Ker}}({\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(M_{b},\mathbb{Q}))=\eta^{\perp}

where the last equality is
[

Since

The final claim follows because

by the image of

\Box

Let

Proof.

In (

\Box

5.
Jacobian sheaves via Hodge modules

The main goal of this section is to justify
\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}^{2}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}/\mathrm{R}^{3}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{Y}}
(Definition
3.14
)
as a suitable candidate for the Hodge theoretic degeneration of intermediate Jacobian of smooth cubic threefolds in
p\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to B
.
For that, we reconstruct it as the intermediate
Jacobian sheaf of a Hodge module. Such Jacobian sheaves
have been defined by
Schnell
[
Sch12a
]
, generalizing earlier work
on Jacobians of variations of Hodge structure (vHs)
and Hodge modules

[
Zuc76
,
Cle83
,
Sai96
,
GGK10
,
BPS08
]
.
In particular, this shows that
\mathcal{J}

is naturally reconstructed from the locus of smooth cubics
U\subset B
.
The main results of this section are

Theorem

5.8

and
Theorem

5.10
.

5.1.
Preliminaries on Hodge modules

We need to recall some important conventions and results from the theory of Hodge modules.
The main reference for the theory of Hodge modules is Saito’s work
[
Sai88
]
;
for a quick overview of the theory, consult
[
Sch14
,
Pop18
,
SS18
]
. For an overview of the general theory of
D
-modules, consult
[
HTT08
]
.

The category of Hodge modules is constructed by a complicated inductive procedure. For our purposes,
it suffices to know that
a Hodge module on a smooth variety
B

can be understood as a triple
(\mathcal{M},K,F_{\bullet})

where

\mathcal{M}
is a quasi-coherent sheaf with
a (left)
D_{B}
-module structure
(here
D_{B}
is the non-commutative algebra of differential forms on
B
),

K
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-perverse sheaf on
B
,
and

F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
is an increasing Hodge filtration of
\mathcal{M}

by quasi-coherent subsheaves.
The connection
\nabla

giving
\mathcal{M}
a structure of a
D_{B}
-module
needs to satisfy

\nabla(F_{k}\mathcal{M})\subseteq\Omega_{B}^{1}\otimes F_{k+1}\mathcal{M}
.
There are various other compatibility constraints; most importantly, there is
an isomorphism
\dr(\mathcal{M})\simeq K_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\dr
denotes the de Rham complex, and existence of a polarization,
which we will not specify.

Any polarized variation of Hodge structure (vHs)

\mathcal{H}=L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}

for a
\mathbb{Q}
-local system
L

on a smooth variety
B

gives an example of a Hodge module with

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}
,

K=L[\dim B]
, and

F_{-k}\mathcal{M}\coloneqq F^{k}\mathcal{H}
.

In general, the structure of a Hodge module
(\mathcal{M},K,F_{\bullet})
can be thought of enriching the structure of the underlying perverse sheaf
K
. In particular, the six functor formalism on perverse sheaves extends canonically to Hodge modules.

In what follows, we will simplify
the notation and write
\mathcal{M}
to denote a Hodge module, instead of the triple
(\mathcal{M},K,F_{\bullet})
, when it does not lead to confusion.
It is worth mentioning that several sources use right
D_{B}
-modules,
and one needs to be careful when
translating between the two notions;
for example, the Hodge filtration gets shifted by
\dim B
when passing between left and right Hodge modules.

Below we list certain properties of Hodge modules that we will use. Let
B
be a smooth variety of dimension
n
.

(1)

The minimal extension.

Let
L_{U}
be a polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-local system on a Zariski open subset
U\subset B
which underlies
a
vHs
\mathcal{H}_{U}=L_{U}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}_{U}
admits the so-called
minimal extension
Hodge module
(\mathcal{M},\IC(L_{U}),F_{\bullet})
.
Thus, we may think of
\IC(L_{U})
as a perverse sheaf underlying the Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
.

Furthermore, any
Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
decomposes as
\mathcal{M}\simeq\oplus_{Z}\mathcal{M}_{Z}
, for proper subvarieties

Z\subseteq B
and such that for each
Z
,
\mathcal{M}_{Z}
is a minimal extension of a vHs.

(2)

Weight of a Hodge module.

The category of Hodge modules is graded by integer weights.
By Saito’s conventions
(see
[
Sai88
, Théorème 2]
)
the minimal Hodge module
extension of a vHs of weight
m

has Hodge module weight
n+m
.

(3)

Filtered de Rham Complex.
Let
(\mathcal{M},K,F_{\bullet})
be a Hodge module. The associated de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}
is defined as

\dr(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq[\mathcal{M}\to\Omega_{B}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}\to\dots\to\omega_{B}\otimes\mathcal{M}][n]

a complex living in degrees
-n,\dots,0
, with the differential induced by the connection
\nabla
. The de Rham complex,
\dr(\mathcal{M})
is quasi-isomorphic to the perverse sheaf
K_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Furthermore, we have the following filtered version of the de Rham complex

F_{k}\dr(\mathcal{M})=[F_{k}\mathcal{M}\to\Omega_{B}^{1}\otimes F_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\to\dots\to\omega_{B}\otimes F_{k+n}\mathcal{M}][n]

and the complex of coherent sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-modules

(5.1)

\gr^{F}_{k}\dr(\mathcal{M})=[\gr^{F}_{k}\mathcal{M}\to\Omega_{B}^{1}\otimes\gr^{F}_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\to\dots\to\omega_{B}\otimes\gr^{F}_{k+n}\mathcal{M}][n]

(4)

Trivial Hodge module
(\mathcal{O}_{B},\mathbb{Q}_{B}[n],F_{\bullet})
.
This is nothing but the rank one constant variation of Hodge structure on
B
of weight 0.
It has Hodge module weight
n
.
We have the Hodge filtration
F_{k}\mathcal{O}_{B}=\mathcal{O}_{B}
if
k\geq 0
and
0
otherwise. The de Rham complex recovers the usual holomorphic de Rham complex

\DR(\mathcal{O}_{B})=[\mathcal{O}_{B}\to\Omega_{B}^{1}\to\dots\to\omega_{B}][n]\simeq\mathbb{C}_{B}[n].

Furthermore, we have
\gr^{F}_{-k}\dr(\mathcal{O}_{B})\simeq\Omega_{B}^{k}[n-k]
when
0\leq k\leq n
and
0
otherwise.

(5)

Lowest piece.

Given any Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
, following
[
Sai91
, (2.1.4)]
, we denote by
p(\mathcal{M})
the minimal
p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
F_{p}\mathcal{M}\neq 0
and
s(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq F_{p(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{M}
.
By construction, we obtain

(5.2)

\gr^{F}_{-n+p(\mathcal{M})}\dr(\mathcal{M})=\omega_{B}\otimes s(\mathcal{M})[0]

Furthermore, let
\mathcal{M}
be the minimal extension of a vHs
\mathcal{H}_{U}
, then (see
[
Sai91
, (2.25)]
for a similar expression for right
D_{B}
-modules)

(5.3)

p(\mathcal{M})=-\max\{p\in\mathbb{Z}\mid F^{p}\mathcal{H}\neq 0\}

and the lowest piece
s(\mathcal{M})
extends the smallest filtered piece
F^{-p(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{H}_{U}
.
For instance, when
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
,
p(\mathcal{M})=0
,
s(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
and
\gr^{F}_{-n}\dr(\mathcal{O}_{B})\simeq\omega_{B}[0]
.

(6)

Strict support.
A Hodge module on
B
is said to be have strict support along
B
if
\mathcal{M}
is supported on
B
and it does not have any nontrivial sub- or quotient-Hodge module supported along a proper subvariety. Such Hodge modules are uniquely determined by the associated vHs on some open subset of
B
.

(7)

Tate twist.
Given any Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the Tate-twisted Hodge module
\mathcal{M}(-m)

has the same
D_{B}
-module structure and the shifted Hodge filtration

(5.4)

F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}(-m)=F_{\bullet+m}\mathcal{M}.

Tate twist does not change the underlying perverse sheaf
K
. If
\mathcal{M}
has weight
w
, then

\mathcal{M}(-m)
has weight
w+2m
.
Also note that by definition
p(\mathcal{M}(-m))=p(\mathcal{M})-m
and

s(\mathcal{M}(-m))=s(\mathcal{M})
.

In particular, we have the Tate-twisted Hodge module

(\mathcal{O}_{B}(-m),\mathbb{Q}_{B}[n],F_{\bullet})
.
This Hodge module is nothing but the variation of 1-dimensional trivial
(m,m)
-Hodge–Tate structure. We have
\gr^{F}_{-k}\mathcal{O}_{B}(-m)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
when
k=m
and
0
otherwise. Consequently, the graded pieces of the de Rham complex have the form

(5.5)

\gr^{F}_{-k}\dr(\mathcal{O}_{B}(-m))\simeq\Omega_{B}^{k-m}[n-k+m]\text{ for }m\leq k\leq m+n

and are trivial outside of this range.

(8)

Duality.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Hodge module of weight
w
,
then the dual

\mathbb{D}\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\omega_{B}^{-1}\otimes R\mathcal{H}om_{D_{B}}(\mathcal{M},D_{B})

is again a Hodge module (left
D_{B}
-module) of weight
-w
.
Any polarization on
\mathcal{M}
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{D}\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{M}(w)
which implies that
there is a quasi-isomorphism (see e.g.
[
Sch16
, Lemma 7.4]
)

\mathrm{R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{B}}(\gr^{F}_{-k}\dr(\mathcal{M}),\omega_{B}[n])\simeq\gr^{F}_{k-w}\dr(\mathcal{M}).

For instance, applying this to
k=n-p(\mathcal{M})
and using (
5.2
), we obtain a quasi-isomorphism

(5.6)

\mathrm{R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{B}}(s(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{O}_{B}[n])\simeq\gr^{F}_{n-p(\mathcal{M})-w}\dr(\mathcal{M}).

Moreover, since
\gr^{F}_{-k}\dr(\mathcal{M})\simeq 0
for
-k<-n+p(\mathcal{M})
, by duality we obtain

(5.7)

\gr^{F}_{-k}\dr(\mathcal{M})\text{ is acyclic for all }-k>n-p(\mathcal{M})-w.

The category of Hodge modules is semisimple. A Hodge module theoretic version of the decomposition
Theorem

2.2
exists and is one of the main inputs in what follows, so we recall it here.

Theorem 5.1
[
Sai88
, Théorème 5.3.1]
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a projective morphism of smooth varieties.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Hodge module on
W
of weight
w
. Then the Hodge module derived direct image
f_{+}\mathcal{M}

splits non-canonically into the following direct sum

f_{+}\mathcal{M}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i],

where for all
i
,
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a Hodge module on
B
of weight
w+i
with an underlying perverse sheaf structure given by
P_{i}
, defined in Theorem
2.2
. Furthermore, there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{P}_{-i}(-i)\simeq\mathcal{P}_{i}
induced via the relative Hard Lefschetz isomorphism.

It is rather difficult to get hold of the Hodge filtration on Hodge modules. Nonetheless, Saito in
[
Sai88
, §2.3.7]
proved a very useful property for the induced filtration on the de Rham complex (see also
[
Sch14
, Theorem 28.1]
).

Theorem 5.2
(Saito’s Formula)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
for any
k
we have

\mathrm{R}f_{*}\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(\mathcal{M})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\gr_{k}^{F}\DR(\mathcal{P}_{i})[-i].

Now we explain a corollary of Saito’s formula. First we need the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, assume that
f
is surjective, and has connected general fibers of dimension
d
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime}
the direct summand of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
that does not have
strict support along
B
. Then we have

p(\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime})>\max\left(p(\mathcal{M})-d,\;p(\mathcal{M})-d-i\right).

Proof.

[
Sai91
, (2.6) Proposition]
translated into the language of left Hodge modules, implies that
p(\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime})>p(\mathcal{M})-d
for all
i
. Moreover, using
\mathcal{P}_{i}\simeq\mathcal{P}_{-i}(-i)
we have

\Box

p(\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime})=p(\mathcal{P}_{-i}^{\prime})-i>p(\mathcal{M})-d-i.

A similar formulation of the following statement can be found in
[
Kol86
, Corollary 7.8]
and can be deduced from the more general result
[
Sai91
, (3.2.2)]
. Here it is stated in a format useful to the current set-up.

Corollary 5.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, assume that
f
is surjective, has connected general fibers of dimension
d
and
\dim B=n
. Let

\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}
be the Hodge module minimal extension of the vHs
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{U*}\mathbb{Q}_{W_{U}}
where
U\subset B
is the smooth locus of
f
.
Then

(5.8)

\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-n}\left(\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1})\right)\simeq\mathcal{H}om_{B}(s(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}),\mathcal{O}_{B}),

in particular, it is a reflexive sheaf.

Proof.

To prove the first isomorphism in (
5.8
), we begin by using
Theorem

5.1
and
Theorem

5.2
with
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{W}
and apply
\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(-)
to both sides of the decomposition to get

(5.9)

\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{H}^{-n+i}(\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{P}_{-d-i+1})).

Indeed, by definition of
\gr^{F}_{k}\dr(-)
, for
i<0
,
\mathcal{H}^{-n+i}\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{P}_{-d-i+1})=0
.

Let us write
\mathcal{P}_{j}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{j}\oplus\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\prime}
where
\mathcal{M}_{j}

has strict support along
B
and
\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\prime}

is supported on a proper closed subset of
B
. Here
\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}
is the Hodge module from the statement of the corollary
and
\mathcal{M}_{-d}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}

by the connectedness
assumption on the fibers.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}_{-d-i+1}=0

for
i>1

by the assumption on the dimension of the general fibers.

By
Lemma

5.3
,
using
p(\mathcal{O}_{W})=0
, we have
p(\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\prime})>-d-j
.
Since the weight of
\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\prime}
is
n+d+j
, (
5.7
) implies that
\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{P}_{j}^{\prime})=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, only the terms
i=0
and
i=1
can give nontrivial contribution to (
5.9
), hence we obtain

\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-n}\left(\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1})\right)\oplus\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}\left(\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{O}_{B})\right).

Using (
5.5
), we get

\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}(\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{O}_{B}))\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-n+1}(\mathcal{O}_{B}[n])=0

and the first isomorphism in (
5.8
) follows.

For the second isomorphism, assume that
\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}\neq 0
, otherwise there is nothing to prove as both sides are trivial.
The Hodge module
\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}

has weight
n+1

and we have

p(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1})=-1
by (
5.3
).
Thus, the second isomorphism in (
5.8
) follows from duality (
5.6
)

\Box

\gr^{F}_{0}\dr(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1})\simeq\mathrm{R}\mathcal{H}om(s(\mathcal{M}_{-d+1}),\mathcal{O}_{B})[n].

5.2.
Jacobians of Hodge modules

Before explaining Schnell’s definition of a Jacobian of a Hodge module we recall the classical Jacobian sheaf of a vHs.
Let
U\subset B
be an open subset of a smooth projective variety
B
and
L_{U}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-local system underlying a polarized vHs
\mathcal{H}_{U}

on
U
.
We assume that
\mathcal{H}_{U}
has weight
2k+1
and level 1, i.e., the Hodge filtration is a two-step decreasing filtration with
F^{i}=\mathcal{H}_{U}
for all
i\leq k
and
0
for all
i>k+1
.
In this set-up, one has a family of polarized abelian varieties
J(\mathcal{H}_{U})\to U

whose sheaf of sections is the Jacobian sheaf
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}_{U})\coloneqq(F^{{k+1}})^{\vee}/{L_{U}}
.

In
[
Sch12a
]
, Schnell constructed an analytic space
J(\mathcal{M})\to B
which extends
J(\mathcal{H}_{U})
,
where
\mathcal{M}
is the minimal extension as Hodge module of the vHs
\mathcal{H}_{U}
.
Denote by
j\colon U\hookrightarrow B
the open immersion.
Let
s(\mathcal{M})
denote the lowest graded piece of the Hodge filtration on
\mathcal{M}
(
5.2
). Schnell observed that

\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}L_{U}\hookrightarrow s(\mathcal{M})^{\vee}=\mathcal{H}om_{B}(s(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{O}_{B})

is an injection (because it is generically injective and the source of the morphism is a torsion-free sheaf).
He defined the analytic space
J(\mathcal{M})
, as the total space of the analytic sheaf in the following definition.

Definition 5.5
.

The Jacobian sheaf of a Hodge module
given by the minimal extension
\mathcal{M}
of a vHs
\mathcal{H}_{U}
of odd weight and level 1 is defined as

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\dfrac{s(\mathcal{M})^{\vee}}{\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}L_{U}}

where
s(\mathcal{M})
is the lowest graded piece of the Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
.

Note that
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})
depends on the integral structure on the Hodge module
(\mathcal{M},K,F_{\bullet})
, that is the
\mathbb{Z}
-local system
L_{U}
, or equivalently, the integral intersection complex
K_{\mathbb{Z}}=\IC(L_{U})
satisfying
K_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\simeq K
.
It is functorial with respect to certain pullbacks
[
Sch12a
, Prop 2.22]
.

We now give some explicit examples of Jacobians for some Hodge modules arising from geometry.
Recall that, given a projective morphism
f\colon W\to B
of smooth varieties with connected fibers, by the exponential sequence, one can deduce an injective morphism of sheaves
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}
. We denote
\Lambda_{W}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{W}
.

Definition 5.6
.

The relative Jacobian sheaf for a projective morphism
f\colon W\to B
of smooth varieties with connected fibers is defined as the analytic sheaf

\mathcal{J}_{W/B}\coloneqq\dfrac{\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}}{\Lambda_{W}}.

Remark 5.7
.

Note that, when
f
has integral fibers,
\mathcal{J}_{W/B}
is the analytic sheaf of sections of the scheme of relative algebraically trivial line bundles
{\operatorname{Pic}}^{0}_{W/B}
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
f\colon W\to B
be a surjective projective morphism between smooth varieties.
Assume that
f
has connected fibers.
Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the minimal extension of the vHs corresponding to
\Lambda_{W_{U}}\coloneqq R^{1}f_{U*}\mathbb{Z}_{W_{U}}
, where
U
is the locus parameterizing the smooth fibers of
f
. Then

(5.10)

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\dfrac{\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{W}}{\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}\Lambda_{W_{U}}}.

If
f
is flat and has no strictly multiple fibers in codimension one, then we have an isomorphism

(5.11)

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathcal{J}_{W/B},

where
\mathcal{J}_{W/B}
is the relative Jacobian sheaf as defined in
Definition

5.6
.

Proof.

The statement
(
5.10
) follows from
Corollary

5.4
.
For the second part, note from Corollary
2.14
that
\Lambda_{W}
is a tcf sheaf, in particular,
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}\Lambda_{W_{U}}\simeq\Lambda_{W}
. Then (
5.11
) follows from definition.
\Box

Example 5.9
.

Let

f\colon M\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration of a hyperkähler manifold
M
of dimension
2n
with no strictly multiple fibers in codimension one. Let
\mathcal{M}
be defined as the minimal extension of the vHs
\Lambda_{M_{U}}\coloneqq\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{U*}\mathbb{Z}_{M_{U}}
, where
U
is, as usual, the locus over which the fibers of
f
are smooth.
Since
B
is smooth and
f
is equidimensional,
f
is flat. Therefore by
Theorem

5.8
we have,

(5.12)

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathcal{J}_{M/B}\simeq\dfrac{\Omega_{B}^{1}}{\Lambda_{M}}.

Indeed, the isomorphism
\mathrm{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M}\simeq\Omega_{B}^{1}
follows from
[
Mat05
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Hodge modules associated to higher direct images under Lagrangian fibrations of hyperkähler manifolds and remarkable symmetries of their Hodge filtrations were studied recently in
[
SY22
,
SY23
,
MSY23
,
Sch23
]
.

Now we consider an OG10 Lagrangian
fibration
f\colon M\to B

associated to a fixed smooth cubic fourfold
X
, see
Definition

4.5
.
Recall that in §
3

we have introduced and studied
a constructible sheaf
\Lambda=\mathrm{R}^{3}p_{*}\mathbb{Z}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
where
p\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to B

is the universal hyperplane section of
X
.
As usual, we consider
U\subset B
, the open locus over which the fibers of
p
(and
f
) are smooth.

Theorem 5.10
.

Given a smooth cubic fourfold
X
, let

\mathcal{M}
be
the minimal extension of the vHs
\Lambda_{U}=\mathrm{R}^{3}p_{U*}\mathbb{Z}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{U}}
.
Then we have

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathcal{J}=\dfrac{\mathrm{R}^{2}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}}{\Lambda}.

Furthermore, for an associated OG10 Lagrangian fibration
f\colon M\to B
we have
\mathcal{J}_{M/B}\simeq\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

The Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
has weight
8
and by (
5.3
) it satisfies

p(\mathcal{M})=-2
. To compute
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})
we first verify that
s(\mathcal{M})^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{R}^{2}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}
.
By duality (
5.6
) we can write

(5.13)

\mathrm{R}\mathcal{H}om(s(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{O}_{B}[5])\simeq\gr^{F}_{-1}\dr(\mathcal{M}).

Now we compute the the graded piece in the right-hand-side of
(
5.13
) using Saito’s
Theorem

5.2
.
The Hodge module theoretic version of the decomposition in
Theorem

3.9
is given as follows:

p_{+}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}[3]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}(-1)[1]\oplus\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}(-2)[-1]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}(-3)[-3].

Indeed, the trivial pieces in
Theorem

3.9
generically underlie a Hodge–Tate structure and hence as Hodge modules they are isomorphic to appropriate Tate twists of the trivial Hodge module
\mathcal{O}_{B}
.
Considering
\gr^{F}_{-1}\dr(-)
in Saito’s
Theorem

5.2
and using (
5.5
), we obtain the following quasi-isomorphism:

\mathrm{R}p_{*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}}[7]\simeq\Omega_{B}^{1}[7]\oplus\mathcal{O}_{B}[6]\oplus\gr^{F}_{-1}\dr(\mathcal{M}).

We deduce

(5.14)

\mathrm{R}^{2}p_{*}\Omega_{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{1}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-5}\left(\gr^{F}_{-1}\dr(\mathcal{M})\right)\simeq s(\mathcal{M})^{\vee}

where we have used (
5.13
) in the last isomorphism. Since
\Lambda
is a tcf sheaf (see
Theorem

3.9
)
we get the desired description of
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{M}
denote the minimal extension of the vHs corresponding to
\Lambda_{M_{U}}
.
From
[
Voi18

, one can deduce that the two vHs corresponding to the local systems

\mathcal{M}(1)\simeq\mathcal{M}_{M}.

which induces
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{M}_{M})
.

we have

.

\Box

.

Comparing the lowest pieces of the Hodge modules

.
Furthermore we identify Voisin’s twisted OG10 Lagrangian fibration

]
with a degree twist.
The main results of this section are

6.6
,

,

and

6.1.
The Albanese sheaf and Tate–Shafarevich twists

Let
M
be a projective hyperkähler manifold of dimension
2n
and
f\colon M\to B\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a Lagrangian fibration. We assume that
f
has no strictly multiple fibers in codimension
1
(see Definition
2.12
).
By a result of Campana
[
Cam05
, Remarque 1.3]
(see also
[
HO09
]
), this is equivalent to
the following assumption:
away from codimension two subset of the base,
every fiber of
f
has a reduced component.

We denote by
T_{M/B}
the relative tangent sheaf
(\Omega_{M/B})^{\vee}
.
The image of the exponential map
[
Mar96
,
AR21
]

(6.1)

\exp\colon f_{*}T_{M/B}\to{\operatorname{Aut}}_{M/B}

is a sheaf of abelian groups
\mathcal{A}_{M/B}
over
B
, called relative Albanese sheaf of
M
.
This sheaf was considered by
[
Mar96
]
and
[
AR21
]
in the analytic setting,
and in the algebraic setting by

[
AF16
]
when fibers are integral,
and for non-multiple fibers by
[
Kim24
]
. See also

[
Aba24
]
,
[
Sac24
]
for
other work on the relative Albanese sheaf.

Consider the induced exact sequence

(6.2)

0\to\Gamma_{M}\to f_{*}T_{M/B}\to\mathcal{A}_{M/B}\to 0

where
\Gamma_{M}\coloneqq{\operatorname{Ker}}(\exp)
is a sheaf of torsion-free finitely generated abelian groups on
B

[
AR21
, Lemma 3.1]

6.3.
Degree twists and comparison with Voisin’s construction

In this section, we focus on the short exact sequence in
Corollary

6.10
, in order to geometrically explain the degree twists, as defined below.

Definition 6.11
.

Let
M\to B
be an OG10 Lagrangian fibration associated to a defect general cubic fourfold
X
.
We define a degree twist to be the Tate–Shafarevich twist of
M\to B
given by
any element of the image of
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(B,\mathcal{J})\simeq\Sha(M/B)

from Corollary
6.10
.

We can give an explicit way to compute the cohomology class corresponding to a degree twist.
Namely, if

b\in{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is any class such that
\deg_{X}(b\cdot h^{2})=1
,
then by Lemma
3.23

the residue class

(6.9)

t=\overline{h^{2}-3b}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}/3)_{\pr}

gives rise to a generator of the degree twists subgroup.

In
[
Voi18
]
, Voisin studies a particular twist of the intermediate Jacobian fibration
M\to B
when
X
is general. A smooth fiber of this twisted fibration over a point
b=[H]\in B
corresponding to a smooth hyperplane section
Y=X\cap H

parametrizes
1
-cycles of degree
1
in
Y
, i.e., cycles in the preimage of
1
by
\CH^{2}(Y)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
.
This twist is constructed as a compactification of the corresponding torsor
[J^{T}_{U_{1}}]\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(U_{1},\mathcal{J}_{U_{1}})

where
U_{1}\subset B
is the open subset parameterizing smooth or
1
-nodal hyperplane sections.
The torsor
J_{U_{1}}^{T}
can also be constructed when
X
is, more generally, a defect general cubic fourfold. We will call any smooth hyperkähler compactification
M^{T}\to B
of
J_{U_{1}}^{T}\to U_{1}
,
a Voisin twist.
Such a compactification was constructed by Voisin
[
Voi18
]
when
X
is general and by Saccà
[
Sac23
]
for any smooth cubic
X
. Note that, if
X
is very general,
then

M^{T}
and
M
are not birational
[
Sac23
]
.

We now come to the final main result of the section.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
X
be defect general.
A Voisin twist
M^{T}
is birational to a degree twist of
M
.

We begin with some lemmas
which will distinguish degree twists as those twists which come from monodromy invariant cohomology classes and thus, exist over the moduli space of cubic fourfolds.
By monodromy invariance of a class,
we mean that the class is restricted from the universal family of cubic fourfolds.

Lemma 6.13
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic fourfold.
For any coefficient ring
R
,
the monodromy invariant classes in
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,R)
are generated, as an
R
-module, by the complete intersection class
h^{2}
.

Proof.

Let us take a Lefschetz pencil
g\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with

X=\mathcal{X}_{0}
.
Specifically,
\mathcal{X}
is obtained by blowing up a smooth cubic fivefold
\overline{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{6}
containing
X
with center in a smooth codimension two complete intersection
X\cap X^{\prime}
where
X^{\prime}
is another sufficiently general smooth cubic fourfold.
We use the Leray spectral sequence for
\mathrm{R}g_{*}R_{\mathcal{X}}
.
It is clear that

{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathrm{R}^{4}f_{*}R_{\mathcal{X}})=E_{2}^{0,4}=E_{\infty}^{0,4}

because this term is not affected by differentials.
This means that we have a surjective restriction map

{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(\overline{\mathcal{X}},R)\oplus{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(X\cap X^{\prime},R)\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(\mathcal{X},R)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\mathrm{R}^{4}g_{*}R_{\mathcal{X}})

where we used the blow up formula in the first isomorphism. Since both
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
and
X\cap X^{\prime}
are complete intersections, by the Weak Lefschetz theorem, we only get
R
-multiples of
h^{2}
in the image. In particular, these are the only classes that can be induced from the universal family of cubic fourfolds.
\Box

We will now work with the primitive cohomology of
X
with coefficients in a ring
R
defined by

{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,R)_{\pr}={\operatorname{Ker}}(h^{2}\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,R)\to R).

It follows from Lemma

that there are no
nonzero monodromy invariant classes in the primitive cohomology
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\rm pr}
. However the situation is different for
3
-torsion coefficients.

Lemma 6.14
.

Monodromy invariant classes
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/3

form a subgroup

\mathbb{Z}/3\cdot t\subset{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}/3)_{\pr}\subset{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}/3)

with the class

defined in (
6.9
).

Proof.

As
(h^{2})^{2}=3
, the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/3\cdot h^{2}
of monodromy invariant classes (see Lemma
6.13
) is consisting
of primitive classes.
The reduction
t=\overline{h^{2}-3b}
is nonzero and independent of the choice of
b
.
Hence it is a generator for the subgroup of monodromy invariant classes.

\Box

Proof of
Theorem

6.12
.

By construction, see
[
Voi18
, §3]
, any
Voisin twist is birational to the Tate–Shafarevich twist obtained from a
monodromy invariant class in
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}/3)_{\pr}
by the composition

{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}/3)_{\pr}\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(B,\mathcal{J}[3])\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(B,\mathcal{J})[3]=\Sha(M/B)[3].

By

.
It follows from

Appendix B

Brauer groups of K3 type Hodge structures, after Huybrechts

Let
H
be an integral
Hodge structure of even weight
2k
.
We say that
H
is of K3 type, if it is a
finitely generated free abelian group with all
graded pieces of the Hodge filtration on
H_{\mathbb{C}}
trivial, apart from

H^{k-1,k+1},H^{k,k},H^{k+1,k-1}

and such that the pieces

H^{k-1,k+1}
and
H^{k+1,k-1}
are one-dimensional.
This is a slight generalization of the usual definition when
k=1
is assumed.
By analogy with the case when
H
is the middle cohomology of a complex K3 surface, we define

{\operatorname{NS}}(H)\coloneqq H\cap H^{k,k}={\operatorname{Ker}}(H\to H\otimes\mathbb{C}\to H^{k-1,k+1}).

We obtain an exact sequence

(B.1)

0\to{\operatorname{NS}}(H)\to H\to H^{k-1,k+1}\to\BrAn(H)\to 0.

Note that all these groups do not change under Tate twisting of
H
.

When the Hodge structure
H={\mathrm{H}}^{2k}(W,\mathbb{Z})
of a smooth projective variety
W
is of K3 type, then we write
\Br_{\an}^{2k}(W)\coloneqq\BrAn(H)
.
In particular, if
k=1

(this applies if
W
is a K3 surface),
then

\Br_{\an}(H)
coincides with the analytic Brauer group
\Br^{2}_{\an}(W)\coloneqq{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{\rm an}(W,\mathcal{O}^{*}_{W})

and (
B.1
) comes from the exponential exact sequence.

Example B.1
.

If
X
is a smooth complex cubic fourfold, then

{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is a Hodge structure of K3 type of weight
4

and we can consider the analytic Brauer group

\Br^{4}_{\an}(X)=\BrAn({\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
.

Let
F(X)
be the Fano variety of lines
of
X
. Beauville and Donagi proved that
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(F(X),\mathbb{Z})
and
{\mathrm{H}}^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})(1)

are isomorphic as Hodge structures (even though
they are not isomorphic as Hodge lattices), see
[
BD85
, Proposition 4]
. We
obtain

\Br^{2}_{\an}(F(X))\simeq\Br^{4}_{\an}(X)
.

By analogy with the geometric case
we also define

(B.2)

\Br(H)\coloneqq(H/{\operatorname{NS}}(H))\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\simeq\BrAn(H)_{\mathrm{tors}}

so
that for every
m\geq 1

we have
a homomorphism

(B.3)

\rho_{m}\colon H\otimes\mathbb{Z}/m\to\Br(H),\quad\overline{a}\mapsto a/m

which induces an isomorphism

\Br(H)[m]\simeq{\operatorname{Coker}}({\operatorname{NS}}(H)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/m\to H\otimes\mathbb{Z}/m).

The following lemma says that the Brauer group typically becomes larger when passing to a Hodge substructure.

Lemma B.2
.

If
H\subset G
is an embedding of Hodge structures of K3 type, then we have a canonical exact sequence

(B.4)

0\to G/(H+{\operatorname{NS}}(G))\to\BrAn(H)\to\BrAn(G)\to 0.

In particular, if
v\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}

is a surjective group homomorphism and
H={\operatorname{Ker}}(v)
, then the first term in (
B.4
) is the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/m
with
m=v({\operatorname{NS}}(G))
.
With
\rho_{m}
as in (
B.3
), the image of this subgroup in
\Br_{\an}(H)

is generated by the class

\rho_{m}(\overline{mg-s})=(mg-s)/m\in\Br(H)\subset\Br_{\an}(H)

for any
g\in G
with
v(g)=1

and
s\in{\operatorname{NS}}(G)
with
v(s)=m
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the Snake Lemma applied to the definition of the Brauer group.
The second statement follows since in this case

G/H\simeq v(G)=\mathbb{Z}
.

For the last claim, using (
B.2
) note that torsion in the sequence (
B.4
) is obtained by applying
-\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
to

(B.5)

0\to H/{\operatorname{NS}}(H)\xrightarrow{j}G/{\operatorname{NS}}(G)\to G/(H+{\operatorname{NS}}(G))\to 0.

The last group in (
B.5
) is generated by the image of
\overline{g}
, and it is easy to check that the kernel of the surjection

\left(H/{\operatorname{NS}}(H)\right)\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow\left(G/{\operatorname{NS}}(G)\right)\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

is generated by any element of the form
\rho_{m}(\overline{t})
, where
t\in H
satisfies
j(\overline{t})=m\overline{g}
. One can choose
t=mg-s
, for
mg\equiv mg-s\pmod{{\operatorname{NS}}(G)}
.

\Box

Example B.3
.

Let
X
be a defect general
smooth cubic fourfold.
There are two natural hyperkähler varieties
associated to

Set

Combining Corollary

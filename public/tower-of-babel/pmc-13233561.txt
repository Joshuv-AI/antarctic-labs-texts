Aesthetic and functional outcomes following reconstruction of full-thickness lower eyelid defects using Hughes flap and orbicularis oculi muscle flap: A case series study
Authors: Akihiro Nariyama, Kazuhisa Uemura, Hiroki Iwanishi, Kazuki Ueno, Shizuya Saika, Shinichi Asamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReconstruction of full-thickness lower eyelid defects remains a clinical challenge due to the aesthetic and functional demands of the periorbital region lower eyelid. Reconstruction using a Hughes flap for the posterior lamella and orbicularis oculi muscle flap for the anterior lamella following malignant tumor excision has not been sufficiently detailed in the literature. We therefore retrospectively evaluated the outcomes of seven patients who underwent this technique, including assessment by photographic analysis and ophthalmological evaluations at least 6 months postoperatively. Eyelid morphology was quantitatively evaluated by measuring Margin Reflex Distance (MRD)-1, MRD-2, eyelid curvature, Δpeak-pupil distance, and horizontal palpebral fissure length using Image J software. Ocular surface condition was assessed by tear break-up time (BUT), superficial punctate keratopathy (SPK) scoring, and Japanese Ocular Surface Disease Index. The median age of patients was 76 years, and basal cell carcinoma was the most common diagnosis. MRD-1 and Δpeak-pupil were significantly increased after surgery (p = 0.031, p = 0.046, respectively). MRD-1 of the reconstructed side was also significantly higher than the contralateral side (p = 0.029). No significant differences were observed in MRD-2, eyelid curvature, or horizontal fissure length. BUT and SPK scores showed no significant differences between sides, and patient-reported symptoms were generally mild. No cases of ectropion or entropion occurred. All flaps survived completely, and there was no recurrence during the follow-up period. Reconstruction using a Hughes flap combined with an orbicularis oculi muscle flap seems to provide favorable lower eyelid contour and to preserve ocular surface function.Keywords: Hughes flap, Orbicularis oculi flap, Eyelid contour, Break-up time, Superficial punctate keratopathy

Introduction

The combination of Hughes flap with an orbicularis oculi muscle flap in reconstruction of full-thickness lower eyelid defects has not been reported with adequately detailed evaluations of postoperative eyelid morphology and ocular surface effects.

Basal cell carcinoma and sebaceous carcinoma are the most common primary malignant tumors of the lower eyelid.1,2 Both of them predominantly affect elderly patients and they are often difficult to clinically differentiate from conditions such as chalazion.3 Diagnosis is thus frequently delayed until the lesion has expanded. The current first-line treatment is surgical excision, but when excision with adequate safety margins results in a full-thickness lower eyelid defect involving more than one-third of the eyelid width, separate reconstruction of the two lamellae becomes necessary.4

Anterior lamella defects are typically repaired using orbicularis oculi muscle flaps or cheek advancement flaps, while posterior lamella defects are reconstructed with mucosal grafts from the oral cavity, hard palate, or auricular cartilage.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 However, postoperative complications such as ectropion or eyelid retraction can lead to superficial punctate keratopathy (SPK) and ocular discomfort.9

At our institution, we prioritize physiologically similar and supportive reconstruction, employing the Hughes tarso-conjunctival flap for posterior lamella repair.10,11 In this study, we retrospectively analyze eyelid morphology and ocular surface outcomes following reconstruction using the Hughes flap and orbicularis oculi muscle flap. We address the limitations of previous reports by detailing our evaluations of postoperative eyelid morphology and ocular surface effects.

Patients and methods

Patients included in this case series all underwent malignant eyelid tumor excision and reconstruction between August 2017 and August 2023. We excluded patients without at least 6 months of postoperative follow-up. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients, and treatment adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Wakayama Medical University Institutional Review Board (Approval No 3531).

All surgical procedures were performed under general anesthesia. The tumor was excised with an adequate safety margin, resulting in full-thickness lower eyelid defects (Figure 1A). Indications for this reconstructive technique included full-thickness lower eyelid defects involving more than one-half of the horizontal eyelid length, requiring reconstruction of both anterior and posterior lamellae.4 The Hughes flap was created following established techniques: the upper eyelid was everted, and the tarsus was incised horizontally at 4 mm from the eyelid margin to the width of the defect, preserving a residual tarsal height to maintain donor eyelid stability (Figure 1B). A tarsoconjunctival flap was elevated by sharp dissection along the plane between the tarsus and Müller’s muscle, taking care to avoid injury to the levator aponeurosis. (Figure 1C).12 The flap was sutured into the residual conjunctiva and tarsus of the lower eyelid defect using 8–0 Vicryl, reconstructing the posterior lamella. (Figure 1D). The anterior lamella was reconstructed using an orbicularis oculi muscle flap (advancement in four cases, transposition in three). The flap was planned with sufficient length and mobility to achieve tension-free coverage. The flap was elevated in the submuscular plane while preserving the overlying skin and maintaining vascularity from the surrounding tissues (Figure 1E). The orbicularis muscle flap was advanced or transposed to cover the reconstructed posterior lamella. Approximately 1 week later, the Hughes flap was divided at the lid fissure midpoint under local anesthesia (Figure 1F).

Surgical protocol. A: Complete excision of the tumor with an adequate safety margin. B: Horizontal incision of the tarsus, 4 mm above the upper eyelid margin, matching the width of the defect. C: Dissection between the conjunctiva and Müller’s muscle to the fornix to create the Hughes flap. D: Suturing of the Hughes flap to reconstruct the posterior lamella. E: Advancement of the orbicularis oculi muscle flap to reconstruct the anterior lamella. F: Division of the Hughes flap on postoperative day 8.

Postoperative evaluation

Pre- and postoperative photographs were obtained, and ophthalmic assessments were conducted at least 6 months postoperatively.

Photographic analysis

Standardized frontal-view photographs were analyzed using Fiji ImageJ (NIH, Bethesda, MD).13 Measurements were calibrated using a standard corneal diameter of 11 mm. MRD-1 was defined as the distance from the pupillary center to the upper eyelid margin (Figure 2A). MRD-2 was measured from the pupillary center to the lower eyelid margin.12 ΔPeak-Pupil represented the horizontal distance between the vertical line through the pupil center and the peak point of the upper eyelid; positive values indicated a peak lateral to the pupil center. Eyelid curvature was measured using the Kappa plugin (Figure 2B).14 Horizontal palpebral fissure width was defined as the distance between the medial and lateral canthi. Length was expressed in millimeters and curvature in 1/mm.

Analytical methods. A: Measurement of MRD-1/2, Δpeak-pupil, and horizontal palpebral fissure width. B: Illustration of upper and lower eyelid curvature. Orange dots indicate measurement points. White dashed lines represent reference lines for the dots (inner corneal limbus, midpoint between inner limbus and center, center, midpoint between center and outer limbus, and outer limbus).

Ophthalmologic examination

Tear film breakup time (BUT) and SPK were assessed by the Department of Ophthalmology, Wakayama Medical University. BUT was measured using 1% fluorescein sodium solution. Patients blinked, and the interval between eyelid opening and the first appearance of dry spots was recorded. The average of three measurements was used. SPK was graded by the Miyata system (area and density scores 0–3).15 Subjective dry eye symptoms were assessed using the Japanese Ocular Surface Disease Index (J-OSDI©).16 Total scores (0–100) were calculated, and severity was categorized as normal (0–12), mild (13–22), moderate (23–32), or severe (33–100).

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare pre- and postoperative values on the operated side, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare postoperative values between operated and non-operated sides. A p-value <0.05 was considered significant. JMP Pro version 17.0 was used for analyses.

Ophthalmologic evaluation revealed no significant differences in BUT or SPK between the operated and non-operated sides (p = 0.71, p = 0.17) (Table 3). Mild or moderate dry eye symptoms were reported in three patients, but they were transient or minimal. All flaps survived completely. Complications comprised one case of postoperative trichiasis and one case requiring re-division of an incompletely separated Hughes flap. No cases of entropion, ectropion, or donor-site morbidity were observed, and no tumor recurrences occurred during follow-up.

Discussion

The eyelids are among the most visually prominent structures of the face and they play crucial roles in vision, eye protection, and aesthetics.17 Poor functional and cosmetic outcomes after reconstruction can severely impair quality of life.

This is the first study to simultaneously assess eyelid morphology and ocular surface parameters using detailed image analysis after Hughes flap and orbicularis oculi muscle flap reconstruction. Although lower eyelid morphology was well maintained, we found that MRD-1 increased, and there was a lateral shift in the peak of the upper eyelid. Despite these changes, ophthalmic outcomes (e.g., BUT and SPK) and subjective symptoms remained stable.

With respect to lower eyelid morphology, preservation of MRD-2 requires maintenance of the vertical height of both anterior and posterior lamellae. In this study, the Hughes flap was harvested to match defect height, and the orbicularis flap was intentionally designed to be 10–20% taller than the defect height.9 This design likely contributed to the consistent maintenance of lid height and contour. Only Case 3 showed increased MRD-2, which may have resulted from inadequate anterior lamella height. Eyelid curvature was well preserved, and no significant changes in horizontal palpebral fissure width were observed, which suggests that structural support and tension balance were successful.

Regarding the upper eyelid, notable postoperative increases in MRD-1 and lateral shifts of the upper lid peak (Δpeak-pupil) were observed. The tarsal plate was incised 4 mm above the margin, excluding Müller’s muscle. Previous reports have recommended preservation of Müller’s muscle to prevent upper eyelid retraction,18 but detailed image-based evaluations have been scarce. Our analysis revealed postoperative MRD-1 increase, likely due to reduced tarsal height leading to superior migration of the levator and Müller’s muscle attachment,19 thereby increasing their effective tension. Scar contracture at the donor site may have also contributed to it. ΔPeak-Pupil increase was likely due to structural weakening of the central upper lid, with lateral tethering by the lateral canthal tendon causing lateral peak displacement.19 Worsening BUT and SPK after Hughes flap reconstruction have been reported in previous studies, which has been attributed to meibomian gland loss and eyelid margin abnormalities.20,21 Our results suggest that careful preservation of eyelid margin alignment and adequate flap height can minimize these issues. Only mild SPK was observed in two patients, one of whom had trichiasis. Overall, we found no significant side-to-side differences in BUT or SPK, and J-OSDI scores indicated minimal dry eye symptoms.

Limitations of this study include the small sample size and variability in follow-up periods, which may have influenced long-term outcomes due to age-related changes. Future prospective studies with larger cohorts, standardized photographic scoring systems, and comparisons with alternative techniques would further validate and refine this approach.

Given the increasing prevalence of eyelid malignancies in the aging population, we suggest this method represents a safe, effective, and physiologically favorable option for lower eyelid reconstruction, balancing both functional protection and aesthetic restoration. In the present case, we did not conduct preoperative examination, and only a postoperative comparison between the unaffected and affected sides was performed.

Malignant eyelid tumors are often misdiagnosed as benign conditions such as chalazion, especially in elderly patients,1, 2, 3 so the ability to achieve oncological safety alongside functional and aesthetic restoration is especially critical in this population. The Hughes flap combined with an orbicularis oculi muscle flap achieves reliable posterior and anterior lamellar support without relying on distant graft donor sites. The use of native vascularized tissues may offer better integration and reduce complications such as necrosis, contour irregularities, or mucosal mismatch. Furthermore, we suggest our morphological analysis provides novel insight into subtle upper eyelid changes after tarsoconjunctival harvest, such as increased MRD-1 and lateral peak shift, which are phenomena that have not been widely quantified before. Traditional assumptions about the donor site morbidity of the Hughes flap require evaluation, and there should be consideration of measures such as Müller’s muscle preservation or tailored flap height to reduce donor-site sequelae.

Finally, this study adds to the growing body of evidence that suggests that when the reconstructive strategy respects the physiological roles of each eyelid layer, both aesthetic and functional outcomes can be optimized. Our series is comparatively small, but the detailed photographic and ophthalmologic evaluations provide a foundation for future prospective, multicenter studies. Alternative anterior lamella approaches should be compared, such as skin grafts, local flaps, or composite flaps, in terms of both eyelid contour and ocular surface health.

Conclusion

We retrospectively evaluated both morphological and functional outcomes of Hughes flap combined with orbicularis oculi muscle flap reconstruction for full-thickness lower eyelid defects. This technique effectively restored lower eyelid contour while avoiding ectropion or entropion and it caused minimal ocular surface morbidity. No visual function impairment or significant ocular surface changes were noted. This seems to be a reliable reconstructive method, with particular application for elderly patients with malignant lower eyelid tumors.

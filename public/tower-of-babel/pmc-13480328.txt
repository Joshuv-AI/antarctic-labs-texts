Safety and Synergy of Finerenone and Empagliflozin in Lowering Blood Pressure
Authors: Rajiv Agarwal, Ricardo Correa-Rotter, Sankar D. Navaneethan, Hiddo J.L. Heerspink, Matthew R. Weir, Janet B. McGill, Amy K. ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:This secondary analysis of the CONFIDENCE (combination effect of fInerenone and empagliflozin in participants with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes using a UACR endpoint) trial (REGISTRATION: URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT05254002) assessed systolic blood pressure (SBP) changes with empagliflozin/finerenone combination therapy versus monotherapy in people with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes across baseline SBP categories. We assessed SBP response predictors and whether early (day 30) SBP changes mediate urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio reductions.METHODS:Participants (n=800) were stratified by baseline SBP (<130 mm Hg; ≥130 mm Hg). Outcomes included SBP response (≥10 mm Hg reduction from baseline at day 180) and longitudinal SBP changes, analyzed by linear mixed-effects models and logistic regression. Causal mediation analysis, a secondary exploratory analysis, evaluated the effect of day 30 SBP change on day 180 urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio reduction.RESULTS:At baseline, 532 (66%) participants had SBP ≥130 mm Hg; 268 (34%) had SBP <130 mm Hg. Combination therapy increased SBP response odds versus finerenone (odds ratio, 1.83 [95% CI, 1.21–2.76]; P=0.004) or empagliflozin (odds ratio, 1.45 [95% CI, 0.97–2.17]; P=0.07). The strongest predictor of response was baseline SBP (odds ratio, 2.04 per 10 mm Hg higher SBP; P<0.0001). Combination therapy significantly reduced SBP versus monotherapies in the ≥130 mm Hg (P<0.0001) but not in the <130 mm Hg group. SBP reduction mediated <10% of the total urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio reduction. Acute kidney injury and hyperkalemia incidences were similar across SBP subgroups.CONCLUSIONS:Combination therapy reduced SBP more than monotherapies in participants with SBP ≥130 mm Hg. Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio reduction appeared largely independent of SBP changes at day 30; though this secondary analysis was limited by reliance on clinic blood pressure measurement. Prospective studies with ambulatory blood pressure monitoring are required.

Novelty and Relevance

What Is New?

This first prospective secondary analysis of CONFIDENCE examined systolic blood pressure (SBP) changes with finerenone/empagliflozin combination therapy across baseline SBP strata and demonstrated safety in the SBP <130 mm Hg subgroup.

What Is Relevant?

SBP lowering was greater in participants with SBP ≥130 mm Hg versus those with SBP <130 mm Hg.

Combination therapy did not cause excessive SBP lowering in participants with SBP <130 mm Hg.

Albuminuria reduction appeared largely independent of baseline SBP or early SBP changes; findings require confirmation with ambulatory BP monitoring and larger prospective studies.

Clinical/Pathophysiological Implications?

Combination therapy in people with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes is supported to reduce albuminuria irrespective of baseline SBP.

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a global health crisis that currently affects 1 in 7 individuals worldwide and is the ninth leading cause of death globally.1 Hypertension is present in the vast majority of people with CKD,2 and diabetes and hypertension are the 2 leading drivers of CKD.3 A growing number of therapeutic options that provide cardiorenal protection in people with CKD and type 2 diabetes (T2D) are available beyond ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs). These include SGLT2 (sodium-glucose cotransporter 2) inhibitors, finerenone (a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist), and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists.4 The CONFIDENCE (Combination effect of Finerenone and Empagliflozin in participants with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes using a UACR Endpoint) trial demonstrated that combination therapy with empagliflozin (an SGLT2 inhibitor) and finerenone led to greater albuminuria lowering on day 180 (the primary outcome) than either treatment alone in participants with CKD and T2D.5

Despite the therapeutic advances in the treatment of CKD in people with T2D, the relationship between baseline blood pressure (BP) and the magnitude of BP reduction with these therapies remains unclear. In particular, there is a clinical concern that further BP reduction in people who are already normotensive might inadvertently lead to adverse events.6 Furthermore, while both empagliflozin and finerenone are known to reduce albuminuria,5 the specific contribution of BP lowering to this process is debated. It has been suggested that early reductions in BP may be the primary driver of subsequent decreases in urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR).7 However, this link was not examined in a formal mediation analysis.

Accordingly, this analysis sought to address 4 objectives: (1) assess the magnitude of systolic BP (SBP) response to combination therapy across baseline SBP (<130 mm Hg versus ≥130 mm Hg) strata; (2) assess the odds and the determinants of SBP response (≥10 mm Hg change from baseline at day 180); (3) determine whether early changes in clinic SBP (at day 30) causally mediate improvements in UACR at day 180; and (4) assess safety by baseline SBP strata.

Study Design and Participants

The CONFIDENCE trial (ClinicalTrials.gov; NCT05254002) was a double-blind, phase 2, randomized controlled trial conducted across 14 countries in North America, Europe, and Asia to achieve significant diversity of participants. The protocol, design, baseline characteristics, and primary results of this trial have been published previously.5,8,9 The trial was approved by institutional review boards at each study site. The trial was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent. The Trial Steering Committee was composed of members from both sexes, and diverse geographic and racial backgrounds to ensure broad global representation.

Eligible participants were ≥18 years of age with CKD and T2D (glycated hemoglobin <11%). They had an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) between 30 and 90 mL/min per 1.73 m2 and albuminuria, defined as a UACR measurement between 100 and <5000 mg/g (mean value from 3 morning void samples). Participants were required to be on a stable (>1 month) maximally tolerated dose of an ACE inhibitor or ARB at baseline.8,9 Participants with SBP >160 mm Hg, or <90 mm Hg or diastolic BP >100 mm Hg at day 1 were excluded.8

Interventions

Participants were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive either empagliflozin alone, finerenone alone, or simultaneous initiation of both. Empagliflozin was administered at a dose of 10 mg/d. Finerenone was initiated at 20 mg/d in participants with baseline eGFR ≥60 mL/min per 1.73 m2 and at 10 mg/d in those with baseline eGFR <60 mL/min per 1.73 m2. Randomization was stratified according to eGFR (<60 and ≥60 mL/min per 1.73 m2) and UACR (≤850 and >850 mg/g) measurements at the screening visit.

BP Measurement and Subgroups

BP was assessed at each visit, in a sitting position, with a completely automated device. Manual BP assessment was used only if an automated device was not available. BP measurements were preceded by at least 5 minutes of rest for the participant in a quiet setting without distractions (eg, television, cell phones) and before any blood collection for laboratory tests. At each assessment, 3 consecutive BP readings were recorded at intervals of at least 1 minute and the average of the 3 readings was recorded.

In this prespecified secondary analysis, participants were stratified by baseline SBP into 2 categories: <130 mm Hg and ≥130 mm Hg, which corresponds to participants at the recommended BP goal and not at the BP goal, respectively.10

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the trial was the relative change in UACR from baseline to day 180.5

SBP outcomes were examined, including change in SBP over time, SBP response (≥10 mm Hg change from baseline at day 180), and early change in SBP (at day 30).

Treatment-emergent adverse events were also recorded, defined as adverse events that occurred within 3 days of the last intake of study medication. Symptomatic hypotension and acute kidney injury were adverse events of special interest. Further safety variables recorded included the proportions of participants with hyperkalemia and eGFR decline >30%.

Statistical Analysis

This article presents secondary analyses of the CONFIDENCE trial. Multiple related comparisons were made across SBP strata, treatment arms, and predictive variables without adjustment for multiple testing, which increases the potential for type I error. Findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, which require confirmation in prospective studies.

Linear Mixed-Effects Models

We utilized linear mixed-effects models to examine the magnitude, direction, and determinants of change in SBP over time (see Supplemental Methods for details).11 The treatment × visit interaction was prioritized to more clearly illustrate SBP trajectories over the study period.

Logistic Regression

To assess predictors of SBP response, we used logistic regression models. Covariates included treatment, baseline log UACR, SBP, eGFR, and diuretic use. These covariates were selected based on biological plausibility for influencing kidney outcomes and BP response.

Nonlinear associations between baseline SBP and SBP response were modeled using 5-knot cubic splines. The presence of nonlinearity was formally assessed via a Wald test of the spline parameters within the logistic regression framework.

Causal Mediation

A causal mediation evaluated whether the robust reduction in UACR observed with combination therapy in CONFIDENCE was driven by early SBP changes. To ensure a valid causal temporal sequence, the model used early SBP change at day 30 as the mediator and subsequent change from baseline in log UACR at day 180 as the outcome (see Supplemental Methods for details).11 A directed acyclic graph which represents the mediation model is shown as Figure S1.12

Treatment-emergent adverse events and further safety variables were summarized descriptively within each subgroup.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the 800 participants are shown in Table 1. At baseline, 532 (66%) had SBP ≥130 mm Hg, while 268 (34%) had an SBP of 90 to <130 mm Hg. Participants with SBP ≥130 mm Hg were slightly older (67.3 versus 64.9 years) and had a higher prevalence of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (32% versus 21%) compared with those with SBP <130 mm Hg. Notably, ≥99% of participants in both groups were on antihypertensive therapy at baseline, and ≥99% were taking an ACE inhibitor or ARB as specified in the inclusion criteria. Participants with SBP ≥130 mm Hg at baseline were taking a similar mean number of antihypertensive drugs to those with SBP <130 mm Hg (2.6 versus 2.3); calcium channel blockers, β-blockers, and diuretics were the most commonly used antihypertensive drugs after ACE inhibitors/ARBs.

Change in SBP

SBP change differed by treatment group (P<0.0001); nadir SBP was reached in both the combination (–7.7 mm Hg) and empagliflozin groups (–3.6 mm Hg) by day 30 and in the finerenone group (–3.5 mm Hg) by day 90 (Figure 1; Table S1). The magnitude of SBP reduction was highly dependent on baseline SBP (P<0.0001; Figure 2). In participants with baseline SBP <130 mm Hg, combination therapy resulted in no change in SBP. In contrast, participants with baseline SBP ≥130 mm Hg experienced significant reductions in SBP. While SBP increased over time in participants with low baseline SBP in the monotherapy groups, SBP remained stable in participants in the combination therapy group, with no hypotensive excursions observed at study visits (Figure 2). Baseline eGFR (P=0.12), UACR (P=0.73), and diuretic use (P=0.85) were not significant predictors of SBP change (Figure 2). There was no observed treatment heterogeneity by age and sex (Figures S2 and S3).

To account for eGFR-based dosing of finerenone, we analyzed the treatment effect by eGFR strata (interaction of treatment × eGFR × visit) and found an absence of heterogeneity by treatment (P value for interaction 0.78). On day 30, before potential uptitration, SBP reductions were comparable between the eGFR ≥60 and <60 mL/min per 1.73 m2 strata (–3.11 mm Hg versus –3.45 mm Hg, respectively). This consistency was maintained through the final on-treatment visit (day 180), with a subsequent return toward baseline at the day 210 washout visit, which confirmed that the dosing protocol did not qualitatively alter finerenone’s efficacy profile.

Odds and Determinants of SBP Response

There were 738 pairs of SBP responses available from baseline to day 180: 247 in the combination group, 244 in the finerenone group, and 247 in the empagliflozin group. SBP declines of ≥10 mm Hg at day 180 (responders) occurred in 260 (35.2%) participants, of whom 103 (41.7%) were in the combination group, 74 (30.3%) were in the finerenone group, and 83 (33.6%) were in the empagliflozin group. In the adjusted logistic regression analysis, treatment assignment was a significant predictor of SBP response at day 180 (Pinteraction=0.01; Table 2). The odds of achieving an SBP response with combination therapy were significantly higher than with finerenone monotherapy (odds ratio [OR] for combination versus finerenone, 1.83 [95% CI, 1.21–2.76]; P=0.004) and numerically higher than with empagliflozin monotherapy (OR, 1.45 [95% CI, 0.97–2.17]; P=0.07).

Proportions of responders by treatment group are shown according to baseline SBP (Figure S4), eGFR (Figure S5), and baseline UACR (Figure S6). The strongest determinant of SBP response was baseline SBP. For every 10 mm Hg higher baseline SBP, the odds of a response doubled (OR, 2.04 [95% CI, 1.75–2.37]; P<0.0001; Table 2). There was no evidence of nonlinear relations between baseline SBP and the SBP response (P=0.19, joint Wald test for nonlinear spline terms). Baseline eGFR was a positive predictor of response (OR, 1.13 per 10 mL/min per 1.73 m2 [95% CI, 1.02–1.24]; P=0.02), while higher baseline UACR was associated with a lower likelihood of response (OR, 0.83 per log higher UACR [95% CI, 0.70–1.00]; P=0.05; Table 2). Baseline diuretic use did not significantly impact the odds of response (OR, 1.04 [95% CI, 0.73–1.48]; P=0.8; Table 2).

Mediation Analysis of Impact of Early Changes in SBP on UACR

Overall, combination therapy reduced UACR at day 180 by 27.9% compared with finerenone alone. The portion of this effect that was mediated by SBP reduction at day 30 was 1.1% (95% CI, –2.5 to 4.8), which resulted in a percentage mediated of –3.4% (negative meaning no mediation; Table 3). Similarly, combination therapy reduced UACR by 30.3% compared with empagliflozin, of which –3.3% was mediated by SBP reduction; thus, the percentage mediated by SBP was 9.4% (95% CI, –1.1 to 19.9; Table 3).

Safety and Adverse Events

A comprehensive summary of treatment-emergent adverse events by randomized treatment group is provided in Table S2. Safety outcomes were generally balanced across SBP strata. Symptomatic hypotension was uncommon in the combination therapy group, and occurred in 2.2% and 0.6% of participants with baseline SBP <130 mm Hg and ≥130 mm Hg, respectively. The incidence of acute kidney injury was also low in the combination group regardless of baseline SBP (0% in the <130 mm Hg group versus 1.1% in the ≥130 mm Hg group). The incidences of hyperkalemia and eGFR decline ≥30% were similar between the low- and high-SBP subgroups, with no disproportionate adverse kidney events or electrolyte imbalances observed in those with lower baseline SBP.

Discussion

This secondary analysis of the CONFIDENCE trial provides important insights into the hemodynamic effects of combination therapy with finerenone and empagliflozin in people with CKD and T2D. Overall, finerenone and empagliflozin as monotherapy had similar effects on SBP, which appeared additive when given in combination. Uptitration of finerenone to 20 mg in participants initiated on 10 mg, which was allowed after day 30, may have resulted in the later achievement of nadir SBP in the finerenone group (day 90) compared with the combination and empagliflozin groups (day 30).

The principal finding of our analysis was that SBP reduction was strongly dependent on baseline SBP; participants with SBP ≥130 mm Hg derived a greater hemodynamic effect than those with baseline SBP <130 mm Hg, which demonstrates significant SBP reductions. Conversely, in participants with SBP <130 mm Hg, combination therapy did not lead to excessive SBP lowering or safety concerns. Whereas the linear mixed-effects model traces the entire trajectory of SBP over the course of the study, logistic regression analyzes change in SBP from baseline at day 180 only. These analyses indicate a strong effect of baseline SBP on SBP response, with a doubling of the odds of an antihypertensive response for every 10 mm Hg higher baseline SBP. Furthermore, the odds of an SBP response at day 180 were greater in the combination therapy group compared with either monotherapy group (Pinteraction=0.01). Participants with higher eGFR and a lower UACR at baseline were more likely to achieve an antihypertensive effect. The risk of inducing hypotension or precipitating adverse events such as acute kidney injury in this population with CKD, T2D, and baseline SBP <130 mm Hg was low.

Of key interest, our mediation analysis challenges the commonly accepted hemodynamic theory of albuminuria reduction in this setting. We demonstrated that early changes in SBP on day 30 did not causally mediate the reduction in UACR observed at day 180. The overwhelming majority of the UACR reduction (≈90% to 100% based on our estimates) appeared independent of early SBP changes, though this conclusion is limited by substantial CI width and imprecision. Background therapy with ACE inhibitors or ARBs was a study requirement and may have influenced the degree to which the SBP effect of study treatment mediated UACR reduction. The findings suggest that, in this population, the additive UACR-reducing benefits of finerenone and empagliflozin primarily arise from nonhemodynamic mechanisms, which likely involve direct tissue-protective effects.13,14 This is consistent with a lack of relationship between SBP lowering and UACR reduction from baseline to day 90 in the ARTS-DN (mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist tolerability study - diabetic nephrology) trial.15

The magnitude of SBP reduction observed in this analysis aligns with a robust body of evidence that demonstrates that the absolute hemodynamic benefit of treatment is directly proportional to pretreatment SBP levels. This graded phenomenon was clearly illustrated in the DASH-Sodium (dietary approaches to stop hypertension diet, dodium intake and blood pressure) trial, in which dietary intervention yielded a proportional response according to baseline SBP. Participants with a baseline SBP <130 mm Hg experienced only a 5.3 mm Hg reduction, whereas those with baseline SBP ≥150 mm Hg achieved a substantially larger reduction of 20.8 mm Hg (P for trend <0.001).16 Recognizing this physiology is crucial for maximizing clinical benefit, as emphasized by the joint American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association 2025 guidelines10 and recent meta-analyses17,18. These publications note that while the relative risk reduction in cardiovascular disease events from antihypertensive treatment remains constant (0.82–0.87) across risk levels, the absolute risk reduction is significantly greater in higher-risk, hypertensive individuals.17,18 Furthermore, the stability observed in our subgroup of participants with SBP <130 mm Hg addresses specific safety concerns highlighted by analyses of the ONTARGET (ongoing telmisartan alone and in combination with ramipril global endpoint trial) and the TRANSCEND (telmisartan randomised assessment study in angiotension converting enzyme inhibitor intolerant subjects with cardiovascular disease) trial.6 These found that further SBP reduction in people with baseline SBP <120 mm Hg paradoxically increased cardiovascular risk, whereas physiological increases from such low baselines were associated with reduced risks of cardiovascular death and heart failure. Prior analyses of finerenone in CKD and T2D show that participants in the highest quartiles of uncontrolled hypertension experience the most significant reductions in SBP.19 Conversely, participants in the lower quartile, specifically those with SBP <130 mm Hg, show little or no reduction, or even a slight increase. Our results confirm that combination therapy targets hypertensive excursions in the highest quartiles while maintaining physiological stabilization in those with lower baseline BP, thereby securing renoprotection without precipitating the detrimental effects of iatrogenic hypotension.

Although albuminuria and reduced eGFR reflect different pathophysiologic mechanisms, they both diminish the efficacy of antihypertensive therapy; the most limited BP lowering is observed when both conditions coexist. In a cross-sectional analysis, the magnitude of proteinuria was identified as the strongest marker of SBP severity, regardless of whether measurement occurred in the clinic, at home, or via 24-hour ambulatory monitoring.20 When eGFR and proteinuria are analyzed concurrently, proteinuria remains the primary correlate of mean BP levels. However, both markers independently contribute to nondipping patterns and impaired circadian BP variation, even in the early stages of kidney dysfunction.21 This resistance is quantifiable; in a cohort of 459 people with hypertension, the presence of macroalbuminuria predicted a 10.3 mm Hg blunted longitudinal SBP reduction compared with those without albuminuria (P<0.001),22 while an eGFR <60 mL/min per 1.73 m2 was associated with an 8.4 mm Hg smaller reduction in SBP compared with a higher eGFR (P<0.001).22

The safety profile in our study reinforces the viability of the combination regimen across a broad spectrum of people with CKD and T2D. Incidences of eGFR decline and hyperkalemia were comparable between those with lower and higher baseline BP, which indicates that lower baseline SBP did not predispose participants to increased adverse kidney events or electrolyte imbalances. Consistent with a previous analysis of safety data from CONFIDENCE,23 symptomatic hypotension was uncommon in the combination therapy group, and occurred in 2.2% and 0.6% of participants with baseline SBP <130 mm Hg and ≥130 mm Hg, respectively. The concern that initiation of combination therapy in people with lower eGFR or lower BP might cause harm is not supported by these data; in fact, eGFR was a positive predictor of BP response, yet those with lower eGFR did not experience disproportionate adverse events.

The relationship between SBP and albuminuria becomes progressively stronger as kidney function declines. Cross-sectional studies in the general population revealed that each 10 mm Hg increase in SBP was associated with a 1.10-fold increase in UACR when eGFR was ≥90 mL/min per 1.73 m2, but this relationship strengthened progressively to 1.38-fold at eGFR <30 mL/min per 1.73 m2 (P<0.001).24 However, therapeutic studies show relatively modest benefits of BP reduction on UACR reduction. In a meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials with 37 348 participants, intensive BP lowering achieved a 7.5 mm Hg fall in SBP and reduced the risk of albuminuria progression by only 10%,25 far less than the 52% reduction in UACR we previously reported.5 In the FIDELIO-DKD (efficacy and safety of finerenone in subjects with type 2 diabetes mellitus and diabetic kdney disease) trial, time-varying analysis revealed that only 13% to 14% of the treatment effect of finerenone on cardiovascular and kidney outcomes was attributed to SBP reduction.19 A further causal mediation analysis of data from over 12 000 participants with CKD and T2D in the FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD (efficacy and safety of finerenone in subjects with type 2 diabetes mellitus and the clinical diagnosis of diabetic kidney disease) trials demonstrated that both SBP reduction and UACR reduction at 4 months independently and additively mediated 50% of the long-term cardiovascular protection observed with finerenone.26 We have shown that, in CONFIDENCE, the percentage of UACR reduction at day 180 mediated by SBP changes at day 30 is negligible and accounts for <10% of the UACR reduction. Taken together, these observations suggest that the robust anti-albuminuric effects of combining finerenone and empagliflozin are driven primarily by direct tissue-protective mechanisms rather than being a secondary consequence of hemodynamic alteration, and that both the hemodynamic and the tissue-protective mechanisms may be important for long-term cardiovascular protection. Thus, early albuminuria and SBP reductions are not merely markers for, but seem to be active mediators of, cardiovascular benefit.

This study has several limitations. First, a significant limitation of our analysis is reliance on clinic-based BP readings rather than ambulatory BP monitoring. Data derived from 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring reveal a distinct hemodynamic profile for finerenone that may not be not fully captured by standard clinic measurements.27 While clinic BP readings demonstrate modest and variable reductions, ambulatory BP monitoring data indicate that finerenone, at doses ranging from 10 to 20 mg, produces a persistent and potent reduction in SBP of ≈10 mm Hg over a 24-hour period.27 This suggests that the true hemodynamic efficacy of nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists may be more robust than reflected by clinic-based readings, and the relationship between BP changes and UACR reduction might differ substantially if assessed with ambulatory monitoring. Our mediation analysis conclusions are therefore specific to clinic BP measurement and should not be extrapolated without confirmation using ambulatory monitoring approaches. Furthermore, the mediation analysis is limited by substantial imprecision. The wide CIs for percentage mediated include zero, and span both negligible and more substantial mediation values, which precludes definitive conclusions about whether early SBP changes drive or are independent of UACR reductions. Our findings suggest small or absent mediation effects with substantial uncertainty rather than definitive absence.

Second, this is a secondary exploratory analysis vulnerable to residual confounding and increased risk of type 1 error due to multiple related comparisons across SBP strata, treatment groups, predictor variables, and mediation pathways without adjustment for multiple testing. Our findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive, and confirmation in prospective studies is warranted.

Third, the generalizability of our findings is limited by several population characteristics: (1) participants were required to be on stable, maximally tolerated doses of ACE inhibitors or ARBs at baseline, which limits applicability to treatment-naïve populations or those intolerant of these agents; (2) the study excluded individuals with eGFR <30 mL/min per 1.73 m2 (CKD stages 4–5), for whom adverse event risks with SGLT2 inhibitors and finerenone may be more pronounced; (3) all participants had T2D with albuminuria, so our findings should not be extrapolated to nondiabetic CKD populations or those without albuminuria; and (4) the SBP <130 mm Hg subgroup included a higher proportion of Asian participants (54% versus 41%) and participants had a lower baseline body mass index (28.1 versus 29.9 kg/m2) than participants in the SBP ≥130 mm Hg group, which potentially influenced both BP response patterns and adverse event profiles.

Fourth, the modest sample size of the SBP <130 mm Hg subgroup (n=268, 34% of the cohort) limits the statistical power for subgroup-specific conclusions. Furthermore, the low incidence of events of special interest (acute kidney injury: 0.8%; symptomatic hypotension: 0.2% overall) precludes investigation of safety patterns or predictive factors.

Fifth, the 180-day treatment period is relatively short and precludes conclusions about long-term maintenance of BP-lowering efficacy. While 3- to 4-year trials of SGLT2 inhibitors and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists have suggested sustained hemodynamic effects,28–30 this current study cannot assess whether BP-lowering efficacy is maintained, gradually diminished, or lost over extended periods.

Perspectives

Combination therapy offers a synergistic BP-lowering effect that is proportional to baseline BP, that is, targets individuals with SBP ≥130 mm Hg while maintaining stability in those with BP <130 mm Hg. UACR reduction with combination therapy with finerenone and empagliflozin in participants with CKD and T2D appears to be largely independent of systemic BP lowering at day 30, which suggests primary nonhemodynamic mechanisms. However, this conclusion is limited by clinic BP measurement and requires confirmation with ambulatory monitoring and in larger, longer-term prospective studies. Taken together, these findings support the early and simultaneous initiation of combination therapy to maximize cardiorenal protection31 without hemodynamic compromise.

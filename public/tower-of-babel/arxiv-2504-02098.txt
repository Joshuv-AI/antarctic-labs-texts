On families of degenerate representations of GL n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
Johannes Girsch, David Helm

Abstract
. We consider the stratification of the category of smooth representations of GL n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(F) (for F F a p p -adic field) induced by degenerate Whittaker models. We show that, remarkably, over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero, the successive quotients in this stratification turn out to be module categories over commutative rings. In fact, they are infinite products of rings of functions on smooth varieties over the ground field. We further obtain explicit descriptions of these in terms of the Zelevinsky classification; these descriptions closely resemble the rings appearing in the Bernstein-Deligne description of the Bernstein center.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{G}}
a connected reductive group over
F
. Let
G
denote the group of
F
-points of
{\mathbb{G}}
. If
\pi
is an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G
, then one may associate to
\pi
a natural invariant known as its
wave front set
. This is a subset of the set of nilpotent orbits in
\mathrm{Lie}(G)
, consisting of the orbits
{\mathcal{O}}
that are maximal (with respect to orbit closure) among those for which the coefficients
c_{\mathcal{O}}
of the local character expansion of
\pi
is nonzero.

Another way to associate a set of nilpotent orbits to
\pi
is via the theory of degenerate Whittaker models. For each nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
one has a space of (compactly supported) degenerate Whittaker functions
W_{\mathcal{O}}
(we refer the reader to section
4
for the construction of this space in the case
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
), and one can ask for which
{\mathcal{O}}
there is a nonzero map
W_{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\pi
.

A fundamental result of Moeglin-Waldspurger
[
MW
]
for
p
odd, and Varma
[
Va
]
for
p=2
, relates these two questions. More precisely, this result states that the wave front set of
\pi
coincides with the set of maximal orbits
{\mathcal{O}}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(W_{\mathcal{O}},\pi)
is nonzero, and moreover that for such orbits the dimension of this space is equal to the coefficient
c_{\mathcal{O}}
.

When
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
these results can be sharpened significantly. In this setting nilpotent orbits are indexed by partitions, and Moeglin-Waldspurger show that the wave front set of any irreducible representation
\pi
consists of a single orbit. The partition corresponding to this orbit is thus a natural invariant of
\pi
. Moreover, in this setting the relevant Hom-spaces are all one-dimensional; that is, when
\lambda(\pi)
is the partition associate to
\pi
, then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(W_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda(\pi)}},\pi)
has dimension one.

The partition
\lambda(\pi)
has several alternative interpretations. In terms of the local Langlands correspondence, a result of Gourevich-Sahi (
[
GS
]
, Theorem B) shows that it is equal to the
classification partition
of
\pi
(that is, the partition conjugate to that describing the orbit of the monodromy operator on the Langlands parameter for the Zelevinsky dual of
\pi
). On the other hand there is also an interpretation in terms of Bernstein-Zelevinsky derivatives: if
\lambda(\pi)
is given by the sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}
, then for each
i
,
\lambda_{i}
is the largest integer such that the iterated derivative
(((\pi^{(\lambda_{1})})^{(\lambda_{2})})^{\dots})^{(\lambda_{i})}
is nonzero. We thus refer to
\lambda
as the highest derivative partition of
\pi
; these are discussed in detail in section
2
, below.

The association
\pi\mapsto\lambda(\pi)
naturally defines a stratification of the category
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
of smooth representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Indeed, for each
\lambda
one can define the full subcategories
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\preceq\lambda}
(resp.
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\prec\lambda}
) consisting of smooth representations
V
for which every irreducible subquotient
\pi
of
V
satisfies
\lambda(\pi)\preceq\lambda
(resp.
\lambda(\pi)\prec\lambda
). (Here
\preceq
is the natural dominance order on partitions, which corresponds to the closure ordering on nilpotent orbits.) From these two subcategories one can also form the Serre quotient:

\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\lambda}:=\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\preceq\lambda}/\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\lambda}.

One can think of this quotient as a summand of the “associated graded” category of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
with respect to this stratification.

The goal of this paper is to study the structure of the categories
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\lambda}
. To this end we first construct projective objects
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
, that are closely related to (in fact direct summands of) the spaces of compact degenerate Whittaker functions
W_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}}
. Like the latter, the
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
have the property that
\mathrm{Hom}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},\pi)
is zero unless
\lambda(\pi)\preceq\lambda
, and is one-dimensional if
\lambda(\pi)=\lambda
. Unlike the
W_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}}
, the representations
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
have good finiteness properties (their projections to individual Bernstein blocks are finitely generated), and this allows us to easily establish their projectivity. (It is possible that the
W_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}}
are also projective but this is not known. If so, one could argue with the
W_{{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}}
in place of the
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
in what follows.)

From the
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
we construct projective objects
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\lambda}
. Indeed, the inclusion of this category in
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
is exact, and thus its left adjoint
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}
that takes projectives to projectives. We set
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
. This is a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\preceq\lambda}
, and the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},-)
is an equivalence between the Serre quotient
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\lambda}
and the category of modules over the endomorphism ring
E_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
.

In light of this, the remainder of the paper is devoted to a study of the rings
E_{\lambda}
. Remarkably, in many settings these rings turn out to be commutative! Our main result, Theorem
5.4
, covers the case where the coefficient field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. In this context the rings in question turn out to be (infinite products of) rings of functions on smooth varieties over the ground field, and can be described in a very concrete and explicit fashion, that closely resembles the Bernstein-Deligne description of the Bernstein center.

Our constructions work well in a modular or mixed-characteristic setting as well, and we conjecture that the rings
E_{\lambda}
remain commutative in these contexts, although this seems much harder to prove.

The proof of this result in characteristic zero occupies sections
6
through
11
of the paper, together with the appendix. The most difficult part of the argument is the commutativity; in section
6
we work locally, and reduce this statement to a computation of
I^{\preceq\lambda}
, where
I
is a certain parabolic induction. This computation (Theorem
6.3
) is the technical heart of the paper, and proceeds via Bernstein-Zelevinsky theory; since the approach to this computation is largely disjoint from the rest of the paper we postpone the proof of this theorem to an appendix (section
12
.)

Once commutativity is established we build a theory of families of objects of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))^{\preceq\lambda}
in section
7
, in a manner that exactly follows the development of the theory of co-Whittaker families in earlier work of the second author
[
He2
]
. (Indeed, the theory of co-Whittaker families manifests in our more general setting, precisely as the case where
\lambda
is the one-element partition.)

The theory of families in particular gives us an interpretation of tangent vectors to
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}
as families over a ring of dual numbers, which we use to give a description of this tangent space in terms of self-extensions of a certain “standard module” that arises as the parabolic induction of a tensor product of representations associated to Zelevinsky segments. We compute these self-extensions in sections
9
and
10
; this self-extension computation (Theorem
10.1
) generalizes an earlier result of Chan (
[
Ch
]
, Lemma 7.3) and may be of independent interest. The upshot of the computation is that
E_{\lambda}
is a product of smooth algebras over the ground field. Once this is established the rest of the argument is reasonably straightforward, and boils down to the fact that a map of smooth varieties that is bijective on points is an isomorphism; the details are given in section
11
.

The upshot of these results is a very precise structure theory for families of representations of type
\lambda
over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. We expect this to have several applications. For instance, the “twisted doubling method in algebraic families” of the first author
[
Gi
]
aims to build gamma factors of pairs of families of representations of the group
G\times\mathrm{GL}_{k}
, for
G
classical. A key step in this construction is to replace the family of representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{k}
with a family of representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{nk}
for a suitable
n
; the representations in this latter family have highest derivative partition
(k,\dots,k)
. We expect our structure theory for such representations to provide such a construction, and will address this question in a future revision of this paper.

Our stratification also seems to be closely related to a construction of Schneider-Zink
[
SZ
]
. They also construct a stratification of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
, indexed by partitions, that is closely related to the Langlands classification. They show that the Serre quotients arising from this stratification corresponding to module categories over rings that are commutative
modulo nilpotents
. We expect their stratification to be related to ours via a “Zelevinsky involution”. More precisely, we expect that Bernstein duality should provide a derived anti-equivalence on
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F))
that intertwines their stratification with ours. Such a result would imply that the rings arising in their theory are commutative as well.

2.
The highest derivative partition

Henceforth let
G_{n}
denote the group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. We will be interested in representations of
G_{n}
over an algebraically closed field
k
of finite characteristic
\ell\neq p
, over the Witt vectors
W(k),
which we denote by
\mathcal{O}
, and over the field of fractions
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{O}
. We denote by
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
the category of smooth
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules, and let
\operatorname{Rep}_{k}(G_{n})
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{K}}(G_{n})
be the full subcategories of smooth
k[G_{n}]
and
\mathcal{K}[G_{n}]
-modules, respectively.
m
For
1\leq k\leq n
we denote by
V\mapsto V^{(k)}
the
k
th derivative functor of Bernstein-Zelevinsky; it is an exact functor from
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
to
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n-k})
(where we take
G_{0}
to be the trivial group when
n=k
.)

Fix a Borel subgroup
B_{n}
of
G_{n}
with unipotent radical
U_{n}
, and a generic character
\psi:U_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. The representation
W_{n}=\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
is, up to isomorphism, independent of these choices, and is closely related to the highest derivative functor
V\mapsto V^{(n)}
. Indeed, if
V
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-module, or more generally a module such that
eV=V
for
e
a finite sum of primitive idempotents in the center
Z_{n}
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}[G_{n}]
, then there is a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{n},V)\cong V^{(n)}
. (In what follows, we will refer to such
V
as having “finite Bernstein support”.)

This description extends to natural description of the
k
th derivative functor for all
k
. Let
P_{k,n-k}
be the standard parabolic of
G_{n}
whose standard Levi subgroup
M_{k,n-k}
is block diagonal with block sizes
k
and
n-k
. For
V
with finite Bernstein support we then have an isomorphism:

V^{(k)}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{k}}(W_{k},r_{G_{n}}^{P_{k,n-k}}V)

where
r_{G_{n}}^{P_{k,n-k}}
denotes the parabolic restriction functor from
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
to
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{k,n-k})
and
G_{k}
acts on this parabolic restriction by its identification with the
k
by
k
block in
M_{k,n-k}
.

From this description it follows that one can define iterated derivatives in terms of parabolic restriction as well: if
k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}=n
, and
P
is the standard parabolic with block sizes
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
, with Levi subgroup
M
, then for any
V
with finite Bernstein support we have an isomorphism:

((V^{(k_{1})})^{(k_{2})\dots})^{(k_{r})}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{M}(W_{M},r_{G_{n}}^{P}V),

where
W_{M}
denotes the tensor product of the
W_{k_{i}}
, considered as a representation of
M
. In particular this means that if
k^{\prime}_{i}
is any permutation of the
k_{i}
, then one has an isomorphism:

((V^{(k_{1})})^{(k_{2})\dots})^{(k_{r})}\cong((V^{(k^{\prime}_{1})})^{(k^{\prime}_{2})\dots})^{(k^{\prime}_{r})}.

If
V
is an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
, then the highest derivative of
V
is the largest
k
such that
V^{(k)}
is nonzero; in this case
k\geq 1
and
V^{(k)}
is irreducible. We can thus define a finite sequence of integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
such that
k_{i}
is the highest derivative of
V_{i-1}
for all
i
(taking
V_{0}=V
), and
k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}=n
. We will call this (provisionally) the
highest derivative sequence
of
V
. From the commutativity of iterated derivatives proved in the previous paragraph, we immediately obtain:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
, and
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
its highest derivative sequence. Then
k_{1}\geq\dots\geq k_{r}
; that is, the
k_{i}
are a partition of
n
.

We thus refer to the sequence
\lambda=k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
as above as the
highest derivative partition
of
V
. For
\lambda
a partition we will denote by
V^{\lambda}
the corresponding iterated derivative
((V^{(\lambda_{1})})^{(\lambda_{2})\dots})^{(\lambda_{r})}
.

The highest derivative has the following useful “multiplicativity” property:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V
and
W
be irreducible representations of
G_{n}
and
G_{m}
, respectively, and let
i
and
j
denote the highest derivatives of
V
and
W
, respectively. Then
i+j
is the highest derivative of the parabolic induction
i_{P_{n,m}}^{G_{n+m}}V\otimes W
, and one has a natural isomorphism:

(i_{P_{n,m}}^{G_{n+m}}V\otimes W)^{(i+j)}\cong i_{P_{n-i,m-j}}^{G_{n+m-i-j}}V^{(i)}\otimes W^{(j)}.

From this we can inductively conclude a similar “multiplicativity” for the highest derivative partition:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
V
and
W
be irreducible representations of
G_{n}
and
G_{m}
, respectively, and let
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
denote their highest derivative partitions. Then
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}
is the highest derivative partition of
i_{P_{n,m}}^{G_{n+m}}V\otimes W
, and there is a natural isomorphism:

(i_{P_{n,m}}^{G_{n+m}}V\otimes W)^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}\cong V^{\lambda}\otimes W^{\lambda^{\prime}}.

These iterated derivative functors are represented by natural projective objects in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
(at least with respect to the subcategory of objects with finite Bernstein support). More precisely, for any
\lambda
, let
P
be the standard parabolic with block sizes corresponding to the
\lambda_{i}
, and
M
its standard Levi subgroup. Then, if
P^{\circ}
denotes the opposite parabolic to
P
, Bernstein’s second adjointness gives an isomorphism:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(i_{P^{\circ}}^{G_{n}}W_{M},V)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{M}(W_{M},r_{G_{n}}^{P}V),

and we have seen that the latter is necessarily isomorphic to
V^{\lambda}
for any
V
with finite Bernstein support.

Let
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
denote the parabolic induction
i_{P^{\circ}}^{G_{n}}W_{M}
. We have:

Theorem 2.4
.

The
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
are projective objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
. Moreover, for any primitive idempotent
e
of the center
Z_{n}
, the representation
eW^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
e
be a primitive idempotent of
Z_{n}
, and let
e_{M}
be a primitive summand of the corresponding idempotent of the center of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(M)
. Then
e_{M}W_{M}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}[M]
-module, and the parabolic induction
i_{P^{\circ}}^{G_{n}}e_{M}W_{M}
is isomorphic to
eW^{\prime}_{\lambda}
. Thus
eW^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is finitely generated. It is projective because we have an isomorphism of functors:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(eW^{\prime}_{\lambda},V)\cong(eV)^{\lambda}.

Since
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is the direct sum of the
eW^{\prime}_{\lambda}
it is projective as well.
∎

The representation
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
has an alternative description as a compact induction from a closed subset. Indeed, let
\psi_{\lambda}
be the character of
U
given by

\psi_{\lambda}(U)=\sum\limits_{i\in S_{\lambda}}\psi(u_{i,i+1})

for some nontrivial character
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, where the set
S_{\lambda}
is given by

S_{\lambda}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}\setminus\{\lambda_{r},\lambda_{r}+\lambda_{r-1},\dots,\lambda_{r}+\dots+\lambda_{1}\}.

We then have:

Lemma 2.5
.

There is a natural isomorphism
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}\cong\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Let
P^{\prime}
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
conjugate to
P_{\lambda}^{\circ}
; it is block upper triangular with block sizes
\lambda_{r},\lambda_{r-1},\dots,\lambda_{1}
. Let
M^{\prime}
be the standard Levi subgroup of
P^{\prime}
and
W_{M^{\prime}}
the space of Whittaker functions on
M^{\prime}
. Then
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is naturally isomorphic to
i_{P^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}W_{M^{\prime}}
.

On the other hand, we can write
W_{M^{\prime}}=\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}\cap M^{\prime}}^{M^{\prime}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{n}\cap M^{\prime}}
. Then
i_{P^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}W_{M^{\prime}}
is, by definition, the representation:

\operatorname{c-Ind}_{P^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}\operatorname{inf}_{M^{\prime}}^{P^{\prime}}\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}\cap M^{\prime}}^{M^{\prime}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{n}\cap M"},

where
\operatorname{inf}
denotes inflation from
M^{\prime}
to
P^{\prime}
.

But we have a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{inf}_{M^{\prime}}^{P^{\prime}}\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}\cap M^{\prime}}^{M^{\prime}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{n}\cap M"}\cong\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}}^{P^{\prime}}\psi_{\lambda}

and so the claim follows.
∎

3.
The Zelevinsky classification

In this section we recall the Zelevinsky classification of irreducible smooth representations of
G_{n}
. We follow the approach of Mínguez-Sécherre
[
MS
]
, whose main results we now recall.

Let
\nu
denote the cyclotomic character of
F^{\times}
, and let
\rho
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
G_{n}
over
k
or
\overline{\mathcal{K}}.
A
segment

[a,b]_{\rho}
, for
b>a
integers is a sequence of the form
[\nu^{a}\rho,\nu^{a+1}\rho,\dots,\nu^{b}\rho]
. The segments
[a,b]_{\rho}
and
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho^{\prime}}
are considered equivalent if
b-a=b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}
and
\nu^{a}\rho
is isomorphic to
\nu^{a^{\prime}}\rho^{\prime}
. For a segment
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
, where
\rho
is a cuspidal representation of
G_{n}
, we let
n(\Delta)=n
,
\ell(\Delta)=b-a+1,
and
d(\Delta)=n(\Delta)\ell(\Delta).
The
support
of
\Delta
is the multiset
\{\nu^{a}\rho,\dots,\nu^{b}\rho\}
of representations appearing in
\Delta
; note that over
k
this can have multiplicities above
1
if
b-a
is at least the order of
\nu
mod
\ell.
The segment
\Delta
is called
supercuspidal
if
\rho
is a supercuspidal representation of
G_{n}.
(This is automatic in characteristic zero, but nontrivial over
k
.)

To each segment
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
) is attached an irreducible smooth representation
Z(\Delta)
of
G_{d(\Delta)}
over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
). If
\Delta
is supercuspidal then the supercuspidal support of
Z(\Delta)
is given by the support of
\Delta.
Among the representations with this supercuspidal support,
Z(\Delta)
is characterized by the fact that it has exactly one nonzero derivative, of degree
n(\Delta)
, and that
Z(\Delta)^{(n(\Delta))}
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta^{-}),
where
\Delta^{-}
is the segment
[a,b-1]_{\rho}.
(If
\Delta^{-}
is empty we let
Z(\Delta^{-})
denote the one-dimensional vector space over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
), with the trivial action of the trivial group
G_{0}
.)

A
supercuspidal multisegment
over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
) is a multiset of supercuspidal segments over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
). To a multisegment
\mathfrak{m}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
we associate the integer
d(\mathfrak{m})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}d(\Delta_{j})
, and the partition
\lambda(\mathfrak{m})
of
d(\mathfrak{m})
, defined by setting
\lambda(\mathfrak{m})_{i}=\sum_{j:\ell(\Delta_{j})\geq i}n(\Delta_{j})
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
be a supercuspidal multisegment, and let
I(\mathfrak{m})
denote the parabolic induction

i_{P}^{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes Z(\Delta_{2})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r}).

It will be useful to us to understand the iterated derivatives of this parabolic induction. To do this we first recall the standard partial order
\preceq
on partitions: if
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are paritions of
n
, we say that
\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}
if for all
i
,

\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+\lambda^{\prime}_{i}.

We have:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
d(\mathfrak{m})
. Then:

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W_{\lambda}^{\prime},I(\mathfrak{m}))=0
unless
\lambda\preceq\lambda(\mathfrak{m}),
and

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W_{\lambda(\mathfrak{m})}^{\prime},I(\mathfrak{m}))
is one-dimensional.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
MS
]
, Proposition 9.19. To translate their result into the language used in this paper, one must note that Mínguez-Sécherre work in terms of “residually generic” irreducible representations, rather than generic, but the two notions are equivalent for general linear groups. Thus the set they call
\operatorname{Part}(\mathfrak{m})
is precisely the set of
\lambda
such that
r_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}^{P_{\lambda}}I(\mathfrak{m})
contains a generic representation, and by second adjointness this is the same as the set of
\lambda
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{(d(\mathfrak{m}))}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},I(\mathfrak{m}))
is nonzero.
∎

From this one immediately sees:

Corollary 3.2
.

(1)

The parabolic induction
I(\mathfrak{m})
admits a unique irreducible subquotient
Z(\mathfrak{m})
whose highest derivative partition is equal to
\lambda(\mathfrak{m})
.

(2)

If
\lambda
is the highest derivative partition of an irreducible subquotient
\pi
of
I(\mathfrak{m})
, then
\lambda\preceq\lambda(\mathfrak{m})
.

(3)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},Z(\mathfrak{m}))=0
unless
\lambda\preceq\lambda(\mathfrak{m})
.

(4)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda(\mathfrak{m})},Z(\mathfrak{m}))
is one-dimensional.

Moreover, one has:

Theorem 3.3

(
[
MS
]
, Théorème 9.36)
.

The map
\mathfrak{m}\mapsto Z(\mathfrak{m})
gives a bijection between equivalence classes of supercuspidal multisegments
\mathfrak{m}
with
d(\mathfrak{m})=n
over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}})
and irreducible smooth representations of
G_{n}
over
k
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
).

In particular, one can conclude:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\ell
or zero, and
\lambda
its highest derivative partition. Then:

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},\pi)=0
unless
\lambda\preceq\lambda(\mathfrak{m})
.

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{d(\mathfrak{m})}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda(\mathfrak{m})},\pi)
is one-dimensional.

4.
Degenerate Whittaker models

The highest derivative partition is closely related to theory of
degenerate Whittaker models
, and also to the notion of the
wave front set
. The relationship between these objects was first studied by Moeglin-Waldspurger
[
MW
]
. To any pair
(S,\phi)
, where
\phi
is a nilpotent element of
\mathrm{Lie}(G_{n})=M_{n}(F)
and
S
is a semisimple element of
\mathrm{Lie}(G_{n})
such that
[S,\phi]=-2\phi
, Moeglin-Waldspurger associate a unipotent subgroup
U_{S,\phi}
of
G_{n}
, and a character
\psi_{S,\phi}
of
U_{S,\phi}
(that depends on our fixed choice of
\psi
above). Let
W_{S,\phi}
be the smooth
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-module
\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{S,\phi}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{S,\phi}
(where we regard
\psi_{S,\phi}
as a free
\mathcal{O}
-module of rank one). We refer the reader to
[
GGS
]
, particularly section 2.5, for the details of the construction; note that they work with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
rather than
\mathcal{O}
. The notation used here is that of
[
GGS
]
and not that of
[
MW
]
.

Up to isomorphism, the space
W_{S,\phi}
depends only on the conjugacy class of
\phi
, and not on the choices of
S
or
\psi.
Since the conjugacy classes of nilpotent orbits are in bijection with partitions of
n
, via the Jordan decomposition, for any partition
\lambda
of
n
we will let
W_{\lambda}
denote a representation of the form
W_{S,\phi}
for
\phi
in the conjugacy class associated to
\lambda.
We call
W_{\lambda}
the “space of degenerate Whittaker functions” attached to
\lambda.

A natural question to ask is, given an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
(over
k
or
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
), for which
\lambda
do there exist nontrivial maps
W_{\lambda}\rightarrow\pi
? Over fields of characteristic zero, this question has an answer in terms of the
classification partition
of
\pi
:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}},
let
\pi^{t}
denote the representation obtained by applying the Zelevinsky involution to
\pi
, and let
(\phi,N)
be the Weil-Deligne representation associated to
\pi^{t}
via the local Langlands correspondence. (In particular,
N
is a nilpotent element of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{K}})
.) The
classification partition
of
\pi
is the partition that is conjugate to the partition associated to the conjugacy class of
N
.

In the process of the proof of
[
GS
]
, Theorem B, Gourevitch-Sahi show:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-representation of
G_{n}
. Then the classification partition of
\pi
is equal to its highest derivative partition.

The proof is essentially an application of the Zelevinsky classification and the additivity of the highest derivative partition with respect to parabolic induction. We refer the reader to section 6 of
[
GS
]
for the details.

The following is Theorem B of
[
GS
]
; rephrased in terms of highest derivative paritions; it is also implicit in section II.2 of
[
MW
]
:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
and let
\lambda
be its highest derivative partition. Then:

(1)

If
\lambda^{\prime}
is any partition such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime}},\pi)
is nonzero, then
\lambda^{\prime}\preceq\lambda.

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},\pi)
is one-dimensional.

In fact, this result continues to hold in characteristic
\ell
. More precisely, we have:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G_{n}
over
k
, with highest derivative partition
\lambda
. Then:

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},\pi)
is one-dimensional over
k
, and

(2)

if
\lambda^{\prime}
is any partition such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime}},\pi)
is nonzero, then
\lambda^{\prime}\preceq\lambda.

We will prove this theorem by lifting from characteristic
\ell
to characteristic zero and applying Theorem
4.3
. Let
\tilde{\pi}
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, and let
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
be a finite extension of
\mathcal{K}
such that
\tilde{\pi}
is defined over
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
. Let
\pi
be a model of
\tilde{\pi}
over
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
; that is, a smooth
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}[G_{n}]
-module such that
\pi\otimes_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}\overline{\mathcal{K}}
is isomorphic to
\tilde{\pi}
. Let
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
be the integral closure of
\mathcal{O}
in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
. We will say that
\tilde{\pi}
is
integral
if there exists a
G_{n}
-stable
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-lattice
L
in
\pi
(this condition is independent of the choices of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
and the model
\pi
). If this is the case, we define
r_{\ell}\pi:=L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}k
. The
k[G_{n}]
-module
r_{\ell}\pi
depends on the choice of lattice
L
, but its semisimplification is independent of these choices.

We first show:

Lemma 4.5
.

For any exact sequence:

0\rightarrow U\rightarrow V\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0

of admissible
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules, the sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},U)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},V)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},W)\rightarrow 0

is exact. In particular, let

\tilde{\pi}
be an irreducible, smooth, integral representation of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
. Then

\dim_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},\pi)=\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},r_{\ell}\tilde{\pi}).

Proof.

For any smooth
\mathcal{O}[G]
-module
V
, let
V^{\vee}
denote the smooth vectors in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(V,\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{O});
then
V\mapsto V^{\vee}
is an exact contravariant functor and one has natural isomorphisms:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(V,W^{\vee})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W,V^{\vee})

for any
W,V
smooth
\mathcal{O}[G]
-modules. If
V
is an admissible
\mathcal{O}[G]
-module, then the natural map

V\rightarrow(V^{\vee})^{\vee}
is an isomorphism.

Note that
W_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is injective. Indeed, we have
W_{\lambda}=\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{S,\phi}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{S,\phi}
for some
S
and
\phi
as in the previous section, so
W_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{S,\phi}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{S,\phi}^{-1}
, where
\psi_{S,\phi}^{-1}
has underlying module
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}/\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and a
U_{S,\phi}
action via the inverse of
\psi_{S,\phi}.
In particular
\psi_{S,\phi}^{-1}
is an injective
\mathcal{O}[U_{S,\phi}]
-module; since induction from a closed subgroup is a right adjoint it follows that
W_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is injective as a
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-module.

Applying the exact functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(-,W_{\lambda}^{\vee})
to the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow W^{\vee}\rightarrow V^{\vee}\rightarrow U^{\vee}\rightarrow 0

we obtain an exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(U^{\vee},W_{\lambda}^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(V^{\vee},W_{\lambda}^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W^{\vee},W_{\lambda}^{\vee})\rightarrow 0.

Invoking the isomorphisms

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(U^{\vee},W_{\lambda}^{\vee})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},(U^{\vee})^{\vee})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},U)

(and the analogous isomorphisms for
V
and
W
,) we obtained the desired result.

The final statement follows by applying this exactness to the sequence:

0\rightarrow L\rightarrow L\rightarrow r_{\ell}(\tilde{\pi})\rightarrow 0,

where
L
is a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
lattice in some model
\pi
of
\tilde{\pi}
over a suitable finite extension
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{K}.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

The lemma makes it look quite plausible that the spaces
W_{\lambda}
are actually projective, (and indeed, Gomez, Gourevitch, and Sahi have conjectured that this is the case) but this does not follow directly from the argument of the lemma and as far as we are aware the question remains open. Indeed, even though we can prove that
W_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is an injective
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}[G]
-module, it does not follow that
W_{\lambda}
is projective; the most we can deduce from this injectivity is that
W_{\lambda}
is flat as a
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-module. When
\lambda
is the maximal partition
(n)
of
n
, then
W_{\lambda}
is a direct sum of finitely generated
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules, and in this case projectivity of
W_{\lambda}
follows from the fact that finitely generated flat smooth
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules are projective. In general however
W_{\lambda}
does not have good finiteness properties, and such an argument cannot be made to work.

To obtain information about
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},\pi)
from lifts of
\pi
to characteristic zero we need to understand the behaviour of
r_{\ell}
in terms of the Zelevinsky classification.
We first recall that any supercuspidal representation
\rho
of
G_{n}
over
k
admits an integral, supercuspidal lift
{\tilde{\rho}}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
such that
r_{\ell}(\tilde{\rho})=\rho
.
A lift of
\Delta
is a segment
{\tilde{\Delta}}
of the form
[a,b]_{\tilde{\rho}}
for some lift
{\tilde{\rho}}
of
\rho
. The key result we need is part (1) of
[
MS
]
, Théorème 9.39:

Theorem 4.7
.

For any supercuspidal segment
\Delta
over
k
, and any lift
{\tilde{\Delta}}
of
\Delta
, the reduction
r_{\ell}Z(\tilde{\Delta})
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta).

Proof of Theorem
4.4
:

We prove this by induction on the highest derivative partition
\lambda.
If
\lambda=(1,\dots,1)
is minimal, then it follows from the Zelevinsky classification that
\pi=Z(\Delta)
for some supercuspidal segment
\Delta.
We can then take
{\tilde{\pi}}=Z(\tilde{\Delta})
; the claim then follows from Theorem
4.3
applied to
{\tilde{\pi}},
followed by Lemma
4.5
.

Now assume the claim holds for all representations with highest derivative partition below
\lambda,

and fix a representation
\pi=Z(\mathfrak{m})
with highest derivative partition
\lambda.
Write
\mathfrak{m}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
and choose a lift
{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}}=\{{\tilde{\Delta}}_{1},\dots,{\tilde{\Delta}}_{r}\}
of
\mathfrak{m}.
Note that
I(\tilde{\mathfrak{m}})
and
I(\mathfrak{m})
both have highest derivative partition
\lambda.
If
\pi^{\prime}
is a subquotient of
I(\mathfrak{m})
other than
\pi,
then the highest derivative partition of
\pi^{\prime}
lies strictly below
\lambda
, so by our inductive hypothesis
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime}},\pi^{\prime})=0
unless
\lambda^{\prime}\prec\lambda.

On the other hand, by Theorem
4.3
, and Lemma
4.5
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},I(\mathfrak{m}))
is one-dimensional; from this and the previous paragraph we deduce that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda},\pi)
must be one-dimensional as well. Similarly,
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W_{\lambda^{\prime}},\pi)
must be zero unless
\lambda^{\prime}\prec\lambda.

\Box

The results of this section suggest a strong connection between
W_{\lambda}
and the spaces
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
studied above. In fact, this can be made completely explicit. Indeed, one has:

Proposition 4.8
.

There is a natural surjection
W_{\lambda}\rightarrow W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
, that is noncanonically split.

Proof.

By
[
Go
]
, Theorem A.1, there is a natural isomorphism of
W_{\lambda}
with
\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{\lambda}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{\lambda}}
, where
\psi_{\lambda}
is the character defined in section
2
and
U_{\lambda}
is the subgroup of
U_{n}
denoted by
N_{\lambda}
in
[
Go
]
.

We have a natural injective map:

\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi^{-1}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\lambda}}^{G_{n}}\psi^{-1}_{\lambda}|_{U_{\lambda}}

that takes a left
U_{n},\psi^{-1}_{\lambda}
-equivariant function
f
on
G_{n}
to the same function, but considered as a left
U_{\lambda},\psi^{-1}_{\lambda}
-equivariant function. Using the identity

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(V,W^{\vee})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W,V^{\vee}),

together with the duality between
\operatorname{c-Ind}
and
\mathrm{Ind}
, we see that this gives rise to a map:

\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{\lambda}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{\lambda}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}.

This map is given explicitly as follows: it takes a compactly supported, left
U_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}
-equivariant function
f
to the function given by:

g\mapsto\int\limits_{U_{\lambda}\backslash U_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(u)f(ug)du.

In particular it takes compactly supported functions mod
U_{\lambda}
to compactly supported functions mod
U_{n}
, and thus yields a map:

\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{\lambda}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}|_{U_{\lambda}}\rightarrow\operatorname{c-Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}.

This is the desired map
W_{\lambda}\rightarrow W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
; it remains to show that it is surjective. (It will then be split by projectivity of
W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.)

The dual of this map is the map:

\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\lambda}}^{G_{n}}\psi_{\lambda}^{-1}|_{U_{\lambda}}

that takes a function
f
to itself, forgetting part of the equivariance. This map is clearly injective, so the map
W_{\lambda}\rightarrow W^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is surjective as claimed.
∎

5.
The subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
and its localization

For any partition
\lambda
of
n
, we can consider the full subcategories

\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
, whose objects consist, respectively, of those objects
V
for which every simple subquotient has highest derivative partition
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\preceq\lambda
or
\lambda^{\prime}\prec\lambda.

Alternatively, one can regard
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
consisting of objects
V
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}},V)=0
for all
\lambda^{\prime}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}\not\preceq\lambda
, and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
as the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
whose objects
V
satisfy the additional condition
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},V)=0
.

The inclusion of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
has a left adjoint
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}.
The natural map
V\rightarrow V^{\preceq\lambda}
is surjective, and identifies
V^{\preceq\lambda}
with the largest quotient of
V
that lies in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
. In particular, the functor
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}
sends finitely generated objects to finitely generated objects. Explicitly, one has:

V^{\preceq\lambda}=\mathrm{coker}\oplus_{\lambda^{\prime}\not\preceq\lambda}W^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}},V)\rightarrow V.

As a left adjoint of an exact functor, the functor
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}
is right exact, and takes projective objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
to projective objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
(although of course such objects will not typically be projective objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n}).
) In particular, the
\mathcal{O}[G]
-module
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
is a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}.

Let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
denote the Serre quotient of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
by the Serre subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}.
This is in effect a localization of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
in which we identify all objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
with the zero object, or equivalently, in which we formally invert the maps
V\rightarrow 0
for any
V
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n},\lambda}(V,W)
the set of morphisms from
V
to
W
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any object
V
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
, there are a natural isomorphisms:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},V)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n},\lambda}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V)

Proof.

The first isomorphism is immediate from the definition of
W\mapsto W^{\preceq\lambda}
as left adjoint to the inclusion functor. For the second, note that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n},\lambda}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V)
is, by definition, the colimit over pairs
W
,
V^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(W,V/V^{\prime}),
where
W
is a subobject of
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
such that the quotient
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}/W
lies in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
and
V^{\prime}
is a subobject of
V
lying in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\prec\lambda}
. Since every nonzero irreducible quotient of
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
has highest derivative partition
\lambda
, we must have
W=(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}.
Moreover, since
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
is projective, and
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V^{\prime})=0
, any element of
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V/V^{\prime})
lifts uniquely to a map to
V
. Thus the colimit may be identified with
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}((W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V)
as claimed.
∎

Henceforth denote the representation
(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda}
by
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
. By Proposition
4.8
this representation is also isomorphic to
W_{\lambda}^{\preceq\lambda}
. We let
E_{\lambda}
denote the endomorphism ring of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
; the lemma implies it does not matter whether we take these endomorphisms in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})
or in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
.

The projective
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is nearly a projective generator of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda};
it is projective and admits a nonzero map to every object of this category, but fails to be finitely generated. To fix this, let
e
be an idempotent of the center
Z_{n}
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n}),
such that
e
is a finite sum of primitive idempotents. Then
e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is a projective generator of
e\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
. In particular the functor

V\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n},\lambda}(e(W^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{\preceq\lambda},V)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)

defines an equivalence between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
and the category of
eE_{\lambda}
-modules.

It is therefore worth studying the structure of the rings
eE_{\lambda}.
We first note:

Proposition 5.2
.

The ring
eE_{\lambda}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}
-algebra.

Proof.

As
e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-module, it is admissible as an
eZ_{n}[G_{n}]
-module. In particular its endomorphisms are finitely generated as an
eZ_{n}
-module. Since
eZ_{n}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}
-algebra the claim follows.
∎

Let
eZ_{\lambda}
be the quotient of
eZ_{n}
that acts faithfully on
eE_{\lambda}
; that is, the image of the natural map
eZ_{n}\rightarrow E_{\lambda}.
We conjecture the following:

Conjecture 5.3
.

(1)

The ring
eZ_{\lambda}
is reduced and flat over
\mathcal{O}
.

(2)

For each minimal prime
\eta
of
eZ_{\lambda},
the map
eZ_{\lambda}\rightarrow eE_{\lambda}
becomes an isomorphism after localizing at
\eta.

(3)

The natural map:

eE_{\lambda}\rightarrow\prod_{\eta}(eE_{\lambda})_{\eta}

in which
\eta
runs over the minimal primes of
eZ_{\lambda},
is injective. (In particular,
eE_{\lambda}
is reduced, commutative, and flat over
\mathcal{O}
.)

(4)

eE_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{\mathcal{K}}
is a smooth
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-algebra.

We illustrate this conjecture when
n=2
. When
\lambda=\{2\}
these results are well known; in this case the natural map
eZ_{\lambda}\rightarrow eE_{\lambda}
is an isomorphism for all
e
. When
\lambda=\{1,1\}
, on the other hand, the full subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{2})^{\preceq\lambda}
is the full subcategory of representations on which the action of
G_{2}
factors through the determinant. This category is thus equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{1}),
and the claims of Conjecture
5.3
are easily verified.

Over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
we can go much further, and in fact the rings
E_{\lambda}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{K}}
can be described in a very explicit fashion.

We first need some terminology. We will say two segments
[a,b]_{\rho}
and
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]_{\rho^{\prime}}
are
inertially equivalent
if
b-a=b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}
and
\rho^{\prime}
is isomorphic to an unramified twist of
\rho.
An equivalence class of segments will be called an
inertial segment
. Similarly two multisegments
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
are inertially equivalent if for any inertial segment
[\Delta]
, the mutiplicities of the segments of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
in the class
[\Delta]
are the same; we will call the inertial equivalence class of
{\mathfrak{m}}
an
inertial multisegment,
and denote it
[{\mathfrak{m}}]
. If
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, corresponding to a multisegment
{\mathfrak{m}}
, then we let
[\pi]
denote the corresponding inertial multisegment
[\mathfrak{m}]
.

Note that the highest derivative partition
\lambda({\mathfrak{m}})
is constant on the representatives of an inertial multisegment
[\mathfrak{m}]
; we denote this invariant by
\lambda([\mathfrak{m}])
. One similarly defines
d([\mathfrak{m}])
.

For an inertial multisegment
[\mathfrak{m}]
we let
X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
denote the free abelian group with generators
X_{\Delta_{i}}
, where
\Delta_{i}
are the inertial segments in
[\mathfrak{m}]
. Let
W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
denote the subgroup of permutations
w
of the segments
\Delta_{i}
in
[\mathfrak{m}]
such that
\Delta_{i}
is inertially equivalent to
\Delta_{w(i)}
for all
i
. Then
W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
acts on
X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
by permuting the generators.

Normalize
[\mathfrak{m}]
by demanding that
\Delta_{w(i)}=\Delta_{i}
for all
i
and all
w\in W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
. Let
{\tilde{Z}}(\Delta_{i})
denote the
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}][G_{d(\Delta_{i})}]
-module obtained by twisting
Z(\Delta_{i})
by the character
g\mapsto X_{\Delta_{i}}^{v(\det g)}
of
G_{d(\Delta_{i})}
, and let
{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}])
denote the parabolic induction:

i_{P}^{G_{d([\mathfrak{m}])}}{\tilde{Z}}(\Delta_{1})\otimes{\tilde{Z}}(\Delta_{2})\otimes\dots\otimes{\tilde{Z}}(\Delta_{r}).

As an object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{d([\mathfrak{m}])})^{\preceq\lambda}
the isomorphism class of
{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}])
depends on the ordering of the segments; on the other hand we will show that in the localization
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{d([\mathfrak{m}])})^{\lambda}
such a parabolic induction is independent of the ordering.

To a homomorphism
f:\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}
we associate the multisegment
{\mathfrak{m}}_{f}
whose segments consists of
\Delta_{1}\otimes(\chi_{f(X_{1})}\circ\det),\dots,\Delta_{r}\otimes(\chi_{f(X_{r})}\circ\det)
, where
\chi_{\alpha}
denotes the unramified character of
F^{\times}
that takes a uniformizer to
\alpha
. Note that for
w\in W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
we have
{\mathfrak{m}}_{f\circ w}={\mathfrak{m}}_{f}
. This defines a bijection between the following sets:

(1)

W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
-orbits of maps
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}
,

(2)

maps
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}

(3)

multisegments
{\mathfrak{m}}
in the inertial equvalence class
[\mathfrak{m}]
, and

(4)

irreducible representations
\pi
of
G_{d([\mathfrak{m}])}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
with
[\pi]=[\mathfrak{m}]
.

(More precisely, the bijection takes a map
f
first to its restriction to the ring of
W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
-invariants, then to the corresponding multisegment
{\mathfrak{m}}_{f}
, and finally to
Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{f})
.)

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
. For any
[\mathfrak{m}]
with
[\mathfrak{m}]=\lambda
, let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}
be the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}
consisting of representations
V
such that every simple subobject
\pi
of
V
satisfies
[\pi]=[\mathfrak{m}]
. We then have:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
, and let
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
denote the endomorphism ring of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{\mathcal{K}}
.

(1)

There is a natural product decomposition:

\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}\cong\prod_{\lambda([\mathfrak{m}])=\lambda}\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}

where the product is over equivalence classes of inertial multisegments
[\mathfrak{m}]
with
\lambda(\mathfrak{m})=\lambda
.

(2)

The decomposition from part (1) induces a product decomposition:

E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}\cong\prod_{\lambda([\mathfrak{m}])=\lambda}E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}

where for each
[\mathfrak{m}]
, we denote by
E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
the endomorphism ring of the projection of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
to the direct factor
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}
.

(3)

For each
[\mathfrak{m}]
, there is a natural isomorphism:

E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}\cong\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}.

(In particular the
E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
are smooth, finite type, commutative
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-algebras.)
This isomorphism is uniquely characterized by the property that for any map:

f:\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}

one has an isomorhism:

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{E_{\lambda},f}\overline{\mathcal{K}}\cong Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{f})

in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}
.

(4)

Let
e_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the idempotent of
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
corresponding to the factor
E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
. There is a natural,
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]
-equivariant isomorphism in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\lambda}:

e_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{E_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]\cong{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}]).

In particular,
{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}])
is a projective generator of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}
(and is independent, up to isomorphism in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{[\mathfrak{m}]}
, of the ordering of the segments used to construct it).

The proof of this theorem will occupy the next several sections, concluding in section
11
.

6.
The endomorphism ring
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}

We now turn our attention to
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}},
which we will study by studying its formal completions at maximal ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of the center
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
be the projective
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{\mathcal{K}}
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
. Since the projection of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
to the Bernstein block containing
{\mathfrak{p}}
is an admissible
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}[G_{n}]
-module, the formal completion of
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
at
{\mathfrak{p}}
may be identified with the endomorphism ring of
\widehat{(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}.
We can regard this module as an object of the category
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
obtained by taking the pro-completion of the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
whose objects are the finite-length
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules annihilated by some power of
{\mathfrak{p}}.

Standard arguments show that there are finitely many simple objects of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(namely, the simple
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules of highest derivative partition
\lambda^{\prime}\preceq\lambda
and supercuspidal support given by
{\mathfrak{p}}
), and that each such simple object
\pi
admits a projective cover
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\rightarrow\pi,
unique up to isomorphism. Moreover, the indecomposable projective objects are precisely the
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}.
In particular,
\widehat{(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
decomposes as the finite direct sum of the representations
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
, where
\pi
is simple of highest derivative partition
\lambda
and supercuspidal support given by
{\mathfrak{p}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a maximal ideal of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
, and suppose that for any two distinct simple
\mathcal{O}[G_{n}]
-modules
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of highest derivative
\lambda
and supercuspidal support given by
{\mathfrak{p}},
we have that
\pi^{\prime}
is not isomorphic to a subquotient of
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}.
Then we have an isomorphism:

\widehat{(E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\prod_{\pi}E_{\pi}

where
\pi
runs over all isomorphism classes of simple
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]
-modules of highest derivative partition
\lambda
and supercuspidal support given by
{\mathfrak{p}}
, and
E_{\pi}
is the endomorphism ring of
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}.

Proof.

We have that
\widehat{(E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the endomorphism ring of
\oplus_{\pi}\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
, and the given condition implies that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P}_{\pi^{\prime}})=0
unless
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are isomorphic to each other.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a maximal ideal of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
, let
\mathcal{P}
be a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
that is admissible as a
\widehat{(Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}[G_{n}]
-module, and let
A
be a commutative complete local Noetherian
\widehat{(Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra, with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{q}}
, such that
A
acts on
\mathcal{P}
(compatibly with the action of
\widehat{(Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
). Suppose that:

(1)

The cosocle of the quotient
\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic to an absolutely irreducible representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
with highest derivative partition
\lambda
and supercuspidal support given by
\pi.

(2)

The quotient
\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}
contains no subquotients isomorphic to
\pi
other than the cosocle.

Then there is a quotient
A^{\prime}
of
A
such that
\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes_{\widehat{(Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}}A^{\prime}
as an
A[G_{n}]
-module, and the action of
E_{\pi}
on
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes_{\widehat{(Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})}_{\mathfrak{p}}}A^{\prime}
is via an injection:
E_{\pi}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
that makes
A^{\prime}
into a free, finite rank
E_{\pi}
-module. In particular
E_{\pi}
is commutative.

Moreover, if
{\mathfrak{m}}
denotes the maximal ideal of
E_{\pi}
, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}\cong\mathcal{P}_{\pi}/{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes_{\kappa({\mathfrak{m}})}\kappa({\mathfrak{q}}),
where
\kappa({\mathfrak{m}})
and
\kappa({\mathfrak{q}})
denote the residue fields of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}
, respectively.

Proof.

Consider the map:

\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}.

This map is equivariant for the action of
A
, and condition (1) implies that the composition is surjective. It follows that
\mathcal{P}
is a finite direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{P}_{\pi},
so that

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})
is a free
E_{\pi}
-module of finite rank. On the other hand, since
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
is projective, we have an isomorphism:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})\otimes_{A}A/{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P})

of finitely generated
A[G_{n}]
-modules. Moreover, the right-hand side has dimension one over
A/{\mathfrak{q}}
by conditions (1) and (2), so
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})
is cyclic as an
A
-module by Nakayama’s lemma. Thus if
A^{\prime}
denotes the quotient of
A
that acts faithfully on
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})
, the action of
E_{\pi}
on
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},\mathcal{P})
is via a (necessarily injective) homomorphism from
E_{\pi}
to
A^{\prime}
.

We then have an isomorphism
\mathcal{P}\cong\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes_{E_{\pi}}A^{\prime}
; tensoring both sides, over
A^{\prime}
, with
A^{\prime}/{\mathfrak{q}}\cong E_{\pi}/{\mathfrak{m}}
, yields an isomorphism of
\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}
with
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}/{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\otimes_{\kappa({\mathfrak{m}})}\kappa({\mathfrak{q}})
.
∎

Our strategy for studying the rings
E_{\pi}
(and hence
E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
) is to construct representations
\mathcal{P}
as in the above lemma for all irreducible representations
\pi
of
G_{n}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]
-module of highest derivative partition
\lambda
; we have
\pi=Z({\mathfrak{m}})
for some multisegment
{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}.
Write
\Delta_{i}=[a_{i},b_{i}]_{\rho_{i}}
, and for each
1\leq i\leq r
, and
a\leq j\leq b-1,
let
{\tilde{\rho}_{i}\nu^{j}}
denote the “universal unramified twist” of
\rho_{i}\nu^{j}
over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}]^{\pm 1};
that is, the representation
\rho_{i}\nu^{j}\otimes\chi_{ij}
, where
\chi_{ij}:F^{\times}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}]
is the unramified character taking a uniformizer of
F^{\times}
to
X_{ij}.
Then
{\tilde{\rho}_{i}\nu^{j}}
is a projective
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n_{i}}]
-module.

Let
{\tilde{I}}({\mathfrak{m}})
denote the parabolic induction:

i_{Q}^{G_{n}}{\tilde{\rho}}_{1}\nu^{b_{1}-1}\chi_{1,b_{1}-1}\otimes\dots\otimes{\tilde{\rho}_{1}}\nu^{a_{1}}\chi_{1,a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes{\tilde{\rho}}_{r}\nu^{b_{r}-1}\chi_{r,b_{r}-1}\otimes\dots\otimes{\tilde{\rho}}_{r}\nu^{a_{r}}\chi_{r,a_{r}},

where
Q
is a standard upper triangular parabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
. Moreover, we may assume that the
\Delta_{i}
are orderded such that if for some
1\leq i<j\leq r
there is an integer
\beta
such that
\nu^{b_{i}+\beta}\rho_{i}\cong\nu^{b_{j}}\rho_{j}
then
\beta\geq 0
. In particular this implies that
\Delta_{i}
does not precede
\Delta_{j}
for any
i>j
(in the sense of
[
Ze
]
, section 4.1). Then
{\tilde{I}}({\mathfrak{m}})
is an admissible
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}][G_{n}]
-module, and it is projective as a
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]
-module by second adjointness. Moreover,
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
acts on
{\tilde{I}}({\mathfrak{m}})
via a homomorphism
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}]
that makes
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}]
into a finitely generated
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
-module.

Let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be the maximal ideal of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}]
generated by
X_{ij}-1
for all
i,j
. Then
{\mathfrak{q}}
lies over the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
corresponding to the supercuspidal support of
\pi
. Set
\mathcal{P}=\widehat{({\tilde{I}}({\mathfrak{m}})^{\preceq\lambda})}_{\mathfrak{q}}
; it is an admissible
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]
-module that is a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\preceq\lambda}.
Let
A
be the quotient of
\widehat{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{ij}^{\pm 1}]}_{\mathfrak{q}}
that acts faithfully on
\mathcal{P}.

In fact, the pair
(A,\mathcal{P})
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
6.2
. To see this, note that for each segment
\Delta_{i}
, we have a natural surjection:

i_{Q}^{G_{n_{i}(b_{i}-a_{i})}}\left(\nu^{b_{i}-1}\rho_{i}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{i}}\rho_{i}\right)\rightarrow Z(\Delta_{i})

for a suitable standard upper triangular parabolic
P
.
Combining these for all
i
yields a surjection:

i_{Q^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}\left(\nu^{b_{1}-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{b_{r}-1}\rho_{r}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{r}}\rho_{r}\right)\rightarrow i_{Q^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r})

for suitable standard upper triangular parabolics
Q^{\prime}
,
Q^{\prime\prime}
. Note that the parabolic induction

i_{P^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r})

is an object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and its unique irreducible subquotient of highest derivative partition
\lambda
is isomorphic to
\pi
. Since our assumptions imply that
\Delta_{i}
does not precede
\Delta_{j}
for
i>j
, this subquotient appears as the cosocle of the parabolic induction; in particular this parabolic induction is indecomposable.

We then have:

Theorem 6.3
.

The surjection:

i_{Q^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}\nu^{b_{1}-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{b_{r}-1}\rho_{r}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{r}}\rho_{r}\rightarrow i_{Q^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r})

induces an isomorphism of

(i_{Q^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}\nu^{b_{1}-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{b_{r}-1}\rho_{r}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{r}}\rho_{r})^{\preceq\lambda}

with
i_{Q^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r}).

The proof of this result is quite difficult and makes heavy use of the machinery of Bernstein-Zelevinsky; we postpone its proof to the appendix. This computation has powerful implications for the structure of projectives of type
\lambda
:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{P})
be the pair constructed above. Then
(A,\mathcal{P})
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
6.2
. Moreover, every irreducible subquotient of
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
of highest derivative partition
\lambda
is isomorphic to
\pi
.

Proof.

Note that, since
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}
is right exact, the quotient
\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic to:

(i_{Q^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}\nu^{b_{1}-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{b_{r}-1}\rho_{r}\otimes\dots\otimes\nu^{a_{r}}\rho_{r})^{\preceq\lambda}

and hence by Theorem
6.3
, to

i_{Q^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r}).

Since the latter is indecomposable with cosocle isomorphic to
\pi
, and no other subquotients of highest derivative partition
\lambda
, the hypotheses of Lemma
6.2
are verified. In particular
\mathcal{P}
is a finite direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{P}_{\pi},
so it remains to show that every irreducible subquotient of
\mathcal{P}
of highest derivative partition
\lambda
is isomorphic to
\pi.
But every such subquotient is isomorphic to a subquotient of
{\mathfrak{q}}^{r}\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}^{r+1}\mathcal{P}
, so this follows from the existence of a surjection:

{\mathfrak{q}}^{r}/{\mathfrak{q}}^{r+1}\otimes\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{P}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{q}}^{r}\mathcal{P}/{\mathfrak{q}}^{r+1}\mathcal{P}.

∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a maximal ideal of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
and let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be a multisegment over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
with supercuspidal support
{\mathfrak{p}}
and highest derivative partition
\lambda.
Then
\widehat{(E_{\lambda})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is commutative.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
6.4
,
\widehat{(E_{\lambda})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is isomorphic to the product of the endomorphism rings of the
\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
, and these latter endomorphism rings are commutative by Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.4
.
∎

7.
Families of type
\lambda

In this section we use the projectivity of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
to define certain families of representations of “type
\lambda
”, generalizing the notion of a “co-Whittaker family” from
[
He2
]
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, and let
V
be an admissible
A[G_{n}]
-module. We say that
V
is a
family of type
\lambda
over
A
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

V
lies in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathcal{O}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
,

(2)

the space
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)
is free of rank one over
A
, and

(3)

the map

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)\rightarrow V

is surjective.

When
\lambda
is the trivial partition
\{n\}
, then being of “type
\lambda
” is equivalent to being generic, and a family of type
\lambda
in the above sense is a co-Whittaker family in the sense of
[
He2
]
, Definition 6.1.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is projective, if
V
is an
A[G_{n}]
-module and
f:A\rightarrow B
is a morphism of Noetherian
\mathcal{O}
-algebras, then the natural map:

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V\otimes_{A}B)

is an isomorphism. In particular if
V
is a family of type
\lambda
over
A
, then
V\otimes_{A}B
is a family of type
\lambda
over
B
.

Consider any homomorphism
f:E_{\lambda}\rightarrow A
, where
A
is commutative and Noetherian, and let
V=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{E_{\lambda},f}A
. Then
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)
is free of rank one over
A
and the map:

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)\rightarrow V

is surjective. It follows that
V
is a family of type
\lambda
over
A
.

This allows us to construct a “universal” family of type
\lambda
, in a manner exactly analogous to the construction of the universal co-Whittaker family in
[
He2
]
. Indeed, let
E^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\lambda}
be the quotient of
E_{\lambda}
by its commutator ideal (or equivalently the maximal commutative quotient of
E_{\lambda}
). Then any map from
E_{\lambda}
to a commutative ring
A
factors through
E^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\lambda}
.

The previous paragraph shows that
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{E_{\lambda}}E^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\lambda}
is a family of type
\lambda
over
E^{\operatorname{ab}}_{\lambda}.
We will denote this family by
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}
. Moreover, this family is universal in the following sense: for any family
V
of type
\lambda
over
A
, the action of
E_{\lambda}
on
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)
induces a homomorphism
f_{V}:E_{\lambda}\rightarrow A
, and (after a choice of generator for
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)
as an
A
-module) the surjection

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},V)\rightarrow V

yields a surjection:

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{ab}\otimes_{E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}}A\rightarrow V.

Moreover, applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},-)
to this surjection yields an isomorphism of free
A
-modules of rank one.

In general, if
V
and
W
are families of type
\lambda
over
A
, we will say that
V
dominates
W
if there is a surjection from
V
to
W
; such a surjection necessarily induces an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P},V)
with
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\mathcal{P},W)
. We can make dominance into an equivalence relation by saying that
V
is equivalent to
V^{\prime}
if there exists a
W
that dominates both
V
and
V^{\prime}
. Then the previous paragraph shows that the family
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}
over
E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}
is universal for families of type
\lambda
up to dominance.

Note that if we work over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
rather than
\mathcal{O}
, then
E_{\lambda}=E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}
, and in such contexts we will not distinguish between the two rings.

8.
Tangent vectors

We can use the theory of families developed above to describe the tangent vectors to a point of
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}.
For a field
L
that is a
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, and a map
x:E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\rightarrow L
, we can regard
x
as an
L
-point of
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}L
, and then a tangent vector to this scheme at
x
is given by a map
{\tilde{x}}:E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\rightarrow L[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
lifting
x
.

The theory of the previous section associates to such a
{\tilde{x}}
the family
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\otimes_{E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}},{\tilde{x}}}L[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}.
Denote this family by
V_{\tilde{x}}
, and set
V_{x}=V_{\tilde{x}}/\epsilon V_{\tilde{x}}
(which can alternatively be described as
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\otimes_{E_{\lambda},x}L
.) Then
V_{x}
has an absolute irreducible cosocle
\pi_{x}
of type
\lambda
.

We have an exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\epsilon V_{\tilde{x}}\rightarrow V_{\tilde{x}}\rightarrow V_{x}\rightarrow 0.

Since multiplication by
\epsilon
induces a surjection of
V_{x}
onto
\epsilon V_{\tilde{x}}
, the cosocle of
V_{\tilde{x}}
is isomorphic to
\pi_{x}
; let
W
denote the kernel of the surjection
\epsilon V_{\tilde{x}}\rightarrow\pi_{x}
. Then the above exact sequence induces an exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\pi_{x}\rightarrow V_{\tilde{x}}/W\rightarrow V_{x}\rightarrow 0,

and hence gives a well-defined element of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{G_{n}}(V_{x},\pi_{x}).

Conversely, given an element of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(V_{x},\pi_{x})
, we have an extension:

0\rightarrow\pi_{x}\rightarrow V\rightarrow V_{x}\rightarrow 0,

which we can make into an
L[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
-module in which
\epsilon
acts via the endomorphism of
V
given by
V\rightarrow V_{x}\rightarrow\pi_{x}\hookrightarrow V
. This makes
V
into a family of type
\lambda
over
L[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
, and hence gives rise to a unique map
E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\rightarrow L[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
lifting
x
. Thus we obtain a bijection between tangent vectors to
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{ab}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}L
at
x
and elements of
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(\pi_{x},V_{x})
.

In the particular case
L=\overline{\mathcal{K}}
(or any extension thereof) we can go further using the results of section
6
. In this setting, if the supercuspidal support of
\pi_{x}
is given by a multisegment
\mathfrak{m}_{x}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
, then we have seen that
V_{x}
is given by the parabolic induction
I({\mathfrak{m}}_{x}):=i_{Q^{\prime\prime}}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r})
, where
Q^{\prime\prime}
is the standard parabolic given by Theorem
6.3
and the
\Delta_{i}
are ordered as in the hypotheses to that theorem.

In this case we have:

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose
L
is an extension of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
. Then the natural map:
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(\pi_{x},V_{x})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x})
is an isomorphism. In particular the tangent space to
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}
at
x
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{L}[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x}).

Proof.

This is essentially Lemma 7.1 in
[
Ch
]
; more precisely it follows by taking the smooth duals of the representations appearing in that lemma, and noting that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(V,W)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G_{n}]}(W^{\vee},V^{\vee})
when
V
and
W
are admissible.
∎

This means in particular that we can regard tangent vectors to
E_{\lambda}
at
x
as first-order deformations of a specific family of type
\lambda
in the dominance class of
\pi_{x}
; namely the “maximal” such family
V_{x}
over
L
.

9.
The single-segment case

We will use results of the previous section to show that, with coefficients in
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, the spectra of the rings
E_{\lambda}
are smooth
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-algebras. We begin by focusing on a special case.

Let
\chi^{\mathrm{un}}:G_{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]^{\times}
be the “universal unramified character”; that is, the character taking
g
to
X^{v(\det g)}
, where
v
is the valuation on
F
.

As a representation of
G_{n}
, the character
\chi^{\mathrm{un}}
has highest derivative partition
(1,1,\dots,1)
, and in fact we have:

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\lambda
be the partition
(1,\dots,1)
of
n
. The character
\chi^{un}
is a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{1}
is the block of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})
containing the trivial representation, we have an equivalence of categories between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{1}^{\preceq\lambda}
and the category of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]
-modules, given by
V\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(\chi^{un},V)
.

Proof.

We first show that the
G_{n}
-action on an object
V
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{1}^{\preceq\lambda}
factors through the determinant. When
n=1
this is clear. In general, the fact that the first derivative of
V
is the highest implies that the restriction of
V
to the mirabolic subgroup
P_{n}
is obtained by inflating a representation of
G_{n-1}
with highest derivative partition
(1,\dots,1)
from
G_{n-1}
to
P_{n}
. Thus by induction the restriction of
V
to
P_{n}
factors through the determinant map, and therefore so does its restriction to any conjugate of
P_{n}
. Since these conjugates together generate
G_{n}
the claim follows.

Conversely, any
V
that factors through the determinant is an object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda}
. Those
V
that additionally lie in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{1}
are thus precisely those representations of
G_{n}
that factor through the subgroup
G^{0}_{n}
generated by all compact open subgroups of
G
. Thus
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{1}^{\preceq\lambda}
is naturally identified with the category of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}/G_{n}^{0}]
-modules; in particular it is clear that
\chi^{\mathrm{un}}
is a projective generator of this category and the result follows.
∎

This result generalizes to a class of “minimal” cases: fix a segment
\Delta=[a,b]_{\rho}
; its highest derivative partition is of the form
(k,\dots,k)
for some integer
k
. Denote this partition by
\lambda^{\prime}
.

Consider the representation
Z(\Delta)\otimes\chi^{\mathrm{un}}
of
G_{n}
; it is a smooth
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]
-module. Let
H
denote the group of unramified characters
\phi
of
G_{n}
such that
\rho\otimes\phi
is isomorphic to
\rho
. Then (using
\chi^{\mathrm{un}}
to identify
\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]
with the torus of unramified characters of
G_{n}
) we have an action of
H
on
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]
. Fixing isomorphisms of
Z(\Delta)\otimes\phi
with
Z(\Delta)
for all
\phi
in
H
, we find that
Z(\Delta)\otimes\chi^{\mathrm{un}}
descends uniquely to a smooth
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X^{\pm 1}]^{H}
-module, which we denote by
Z(\Delta)^{\mathrm{un}}
, and call the “universal unramified twist of
Z(\Delta)
”.

Now let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\Delta}
denote the block of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})
containing
Z(\Delta)
. This block has a projective generator, obtained as the parabolic induction of the universal unramified twist of
\rho^{\otimes b-a+1}
from a suitable Levi subgroup to
G_{n}
, and the endomorphisms of this parabolic induction may be naturally identified with an Iwahori Hecke algebra. This identification yields an equivalence between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\Delta}
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(\mathrm{GL}_{b-a+1}(F^{\prime}))_{1}
for a suitable unramified extension
F^{\prime}
of
F
.
Moreover, these equivalences are naturally compatible with parabolic induction and restriction, as well as the “top derivative” functors. From this it is not hard to see that these equivalences are compatible with the filtration we have constructed, in the sense that for any partition
\lambda
of
b-a+1
, they induce equivalences between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(\mathrm{GL}_{b-a+1}(F^{\prime}))_{1}^{\preceq\lambda}
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\Delta}^{\preceq k\lambda}
.

As a result, we deduce:

Proposition 9.2
.

The representation
Z(\Delta)^{\mathrm{un}}
is a projective object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})^{\preceq\lambda^{\prime}}
, and the functor
V\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{n}}(Z(\Delta)^{\mathrm{un}},V)
defines an equivalence of categories between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\Delta}^{\preceq\lambda^{\prime}}
and the category of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[T^{\pm 1}]
-modules.

We will use these results to study homological properties of arbitrary
G_{n}
-representations; this will entail reducing to these “minimal” situations via functors of the form
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda^{\prime}}
. In order for this to be compatible with homological considerations we will need to understand the left derived functor
V\mapsto L^{\preceq\lambda^{\prime}}V
of this functor. The key result we will need is:

Proposition 9.3
.

We have
H^{i}(L^{\preceq\lambda^{\prime}}Z(\Delta))
=
Z(\Delta)
if
i=0
and zero otherwise.

Proof.

For convenience and to simplify notation, we reduce to the case of the principal block; this amounts to noting that the equivalence between
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(G_{n})_{\Delta}
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(\mathrm{GL}_{b-a+1}(F))_{1}
takes
Z(\Delta)
to the trivial character is compatible with the functors
V\mapsto V^{\preceq\lambda}
. We thus henceforth assume that
Z(\Delta)
is the trivial character of
G_{b-a+1}
, so that
\lambda=(1,\dots,1)
.

The trivial representation of
G_{b-a+1}
has a projective resolution:

0\rightarrow P_{b-a+1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}\rightarrow 0

described by Bernstein in section 4.2 of Bernstein’s lecture notes
[
BR
]
. The projective
P_{i}
is the sum, over the dimension
i
faces
F
of the fundamental alcove of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G_{b-a+1}
, of
\operatorname{c-Ind}_{P_{F}}^{G_{b-a+1}}1
, where
P_{F}
is the pointwise stabilizer of
F
. It is clear that
(\operatorname{c-Ind}_{P_{F}}^{G_{b-a+1}}1)^{\preceq(1,\dots,1)}=\overline{\mathcal{K}}
for all facets
F
, and that the map:

(\operatorname{c-Ind}_{P_{F}}^{G_{b-a+1}}1)^{\preceq(1,\dots,1)}\rightarrow(\operatorname{c-Ind}_{P_{F}^{\prime}}^{G_{b-a+1}}1)^{\preceq(1,\dots,1)}

induced by an inclusion of facets
F^{\prime}\subset F
is an isomorphism. From this the result follows.
∎

10.
Self-Ext algebras and dimensions of tangent spaces

We now use the results of the previous section, together with the geometric lemma, to compute the self-Ext algebras
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x})
for
x
a
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-point of
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}
. Let
{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
be the corresponding multisegment, with the
\Delta_{i}
ordered so that

V_{x}=i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes Z(\Delta_{r}).

Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 10.1
.

The
\mathrm{Ext}
algebra
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x})
is an exterior algebra over
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
in
r
variables of cohomological degree one. In particular,
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x})
is a
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-vector space of dimension
\binom{r}{i}
for
0\leq i\leq r
, and is zero otherwise.

From this and Proposition
8.1
one deduces:

Corollary 10.2
.

The tangent space to
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}
at an
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-point
x
corresponding to a multisegment
\{\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}\}
containing
r
segments is of dimension
r
.

For a segment
\Delta
appearing in
{\mathfrak{m}}
, let
n_{\Delta}
be the multiplicity of
\Delta
in
{\mathfrak{m}}
, and let
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta}
be the multisegment consisting of
n_{\Delta}
copies of
\Delta
. Then, for a suitable ordering
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{s}
of the segments appearing in
{\mathfrak{m}}
, and a suitable parabolic
Q=MU
of
G_{n}
, we have:

V_{x}=i_{Q}^{G_{n}}Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{1}})\otimes\dots\otimes Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{s}}).

One then has:

Lemma 10.3

(
[
Ch
]
, Lemma 7.3)
.

The natural map:

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(\otimes_{j}Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}),\otimes_{j}Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(V_{x},V_{x})

induced by functoriality of parabolic induction is an isomorphism for all
i
.

Proof.

Up to taking smooth duals of the representations involved, this is
[
Ch
]
, Lemma 7.3; the proof is via a straightforward application of the geometric lemma.
∎

This allows us to reduce to the special case where
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta}
for some
\Delta
; that is, when
{\mathfrak{m}}
consists of some number of copies of
\Delta
. Indeed, the Levi
M
is a product of general linear groups
G_{n_{1}},\dots,G_{n_{s}}
. Suppose that for each factor we knew that
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n_{j}}]}(Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}),Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}))
was a graded exterior algebra in
n_{\Delta_{j}}
generators of degree
1
. The algebra
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(\otimes_{j}Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}),\otimes_{j}Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Delta_{j}}))

is the graded tensor product of such algebras, and this would be a graded exterior algebra in
n_{\Delta_{1}}+\dots+n_{\Delta_{s}}=r
generators of degree
1
as Theorem
10.1
claims.

It thus suffices to consider the case where
{\mathfrak{m}}
consists of
r
copies of
\Delta
. In this case
Z({\mathfrak{m}})=i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
, for some parabolic
P=LN
of
G_{n}
. We have a natural isomorphism:

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(Z({\mathfrak{m}}),Z({\mathfrak{m}}))\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]}(r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r},Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}).

We first show:

Lemma 10.4
.

There exists an exact sequence of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]
-modules:

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0,

where
Q
has length
r!
, every irreducible subquotient of
Q
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
, and every irreducible subquotient
\pi
of
K
satisfies
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]}(\pi,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})=0
for all
i\geq 0
.

In particular we have an isomorphism:

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]}(r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r},Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]}(Q,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}).

Proof.

We apply the geometric lemma to
r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
. One finds that in the resulting filtration there are
r!
successive quotients of the form
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
, indexed by those elements of the Weyl group of
G_{n}
that normalize
L
. Moreover, for each successive quotient
\pi
in the filtration that is not of this form, one can check that
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[L]}(\pi,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})=0
by supercuspidal support considerations.

The geometric lemma filtration is indexed by double cosets
P\backslash G_{n}/P
, partially ordered via the closure relations. Refine this to a total order, so that we obtain a filtration

0=J_{0}\subseteq J_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq J_{s}=r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}.

Suppose that for some
i
we have
J_{i+1}/J_{i}\cong Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
and
J_{i+2}/J_{i+1}
a successive quotient
\pi
of the geometric lemma filtration not isomorphic to
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
. Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\pi,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})=0
the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}\rightarrow J_{i+2}/J_{i}\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow 0

splits. Choose an isomorphism
J_{i+1}/J_{i}\cong\pi\oplus Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
, and let
J_{i+1}^{\prime}
be the preimage of the summand
\pi
in
J_{i+2}
. Then replacing
J_{i+1}
with
J_{i+1}^{\prime}
gives a new filtration in which the
i
th and
i+1
st successive quotients have exchanged places. Iterating this process eventually yields a filtration in which every successive quotient of
J_{s}/J_{s-r!}
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
and every successive quotient of
J_{s-r!}
is not. We may thus take
K=J_{s-r!}
and the result follows.
∎

Set
\lambda=\lambda(\Delta)
, and let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}
denote the full subcategory of
Rep_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)
consisting of representations such that every irreducible subquotient is of the form
\pi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\pi_{r}
, with each
\pi_{i}
an irreducible representation of
G_{\frac{n}{r}}
whose highest derivative partition
\lambda(\pi_{i})
satisfies
\lambda(\pi_{i})\preceq\lambda
. Similarly define a full subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\prec\lambda^{r}}
in which we additionally require that for every irreducible subquotient
\pi
as above, there exists an
i
with
\lambda(\pi_{i})\prec\lambda
.
As usual let
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\lambda^{r}}
denote the corresponding Serre quotient.

Let
e
be the primitive idempotent of
Z_{M,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
corresponding to the inertial class of the supercuspidal support of
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
. Then
e\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\prec\lambda^{r}}=0
, so we have an equality
e\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}=e\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\lambda}
. In particular, the representation
e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}}
given by
e(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\otimes r})
is a projective generator of
e\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\preceq\lambda}
, and the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{M}(e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},-)
induces an equivalence of this category with the category of
e(E_{\lambda,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}^{\otimes r})
-modules. By the results of the previous section this latter ring is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{r}^{\pm 1}]
. Denote this ring by
B_{r}
.

We have isomorphisms:

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r},i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(Q,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M],\lambda}(L^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}Q,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})

and applying the equivalence
\mathrm{Hom}_{M}(e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},-)
to the right hand side we find that it is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{B_{r}}(M_{Q},B_{r}/{\mathfrak{q}})
, where
M_{Q}
is the complex
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},L^{\preceq\lambda^{r}Q})
and
{\mathfrak{q}}
is the maximal ideal
\langle X_{1}-1,\dots,X_{r}-1\rangle
of
B_{r}
. It thus suffices to compute
M_{Q}
.

Lemma 10.5
.

The natural map
L^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}Q\rightarrow Q
is an isomorphism, and thus identifies
M_{Q}
with
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(e\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},Q).

Proof.

The representation
Q
has finite length, and every irreducible subquotient of
Q
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
, and by Proposition
9.3
we have
H^{i}(L^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})=0
for
i>0
. We thus deduce inductively that
H^{i}(L^{\preceq\lambda^{r}}Q)=0
for
i>0
, and the claim follows.
∎

Thus we may regard
M_{Q}
as a
B_{r}
-module, rather than a complex. We have:

Lemma 10.6
.

The module
M_{Q}
has length
r!
, and its support consists of the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Hom}_{B_{r}}(M_{Q},B_{r}/{\mathfrak{q}})
is one-dimensional; in particular
M_{Q}
is a cyclic
B_{r}
-module.

Proof.

Since the equivalence of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}(M)^{\lambda}
with
B_{r}
-modules takes
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
to
B_{r}/{\mathfrak{q}}
, the first two claims follow from the fact that
Q
has length
r!
and every irreducible subquotient of
Q
is isomorphic to
Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
.

Similarly,
\mathrm{Hom}_{B_{r}}(M_{q},B_{r}/{\mathfrak{q}})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(Q,Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})
. The latter is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r},Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})
, and thus also to
\mathrm{End}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})
. But
i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}
is irreducible so this endomorphism ring is one-dimensional.
∎

Let
A_{r}
be the subring
B_{r}^{S_{r}}
, where the symmetric group
S_{r}
acts by permuting the
X_{i}
. The preimage of
{\mathfrak{q}}
in
S_{r}
is a maximal ideal of
A_{r}
, which we denote by
{\mathfrak{p}}
. We have:

Lemma 10.7
.

We have an isomorphism of
B_{r}
-modules:

M_{Q}\cong B_{r}/{\mathfrak{p}}B_{r}.

Proof.

Since
M_{Q}
is cyclic of length
r!
, and
B_{r}/{\mathfrak{p}}B_{r}
also has length
r!
, it suffices to show that
{\mathfrak{p}}B_{r}
annihilates
M_{Q}
. We have an isomorphism:

M_{Q}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},Q)

and the latter is a quotient of the space:

\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[M]}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},r_{G_{n}}^{P}i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}),

which in turn is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r}).

Consider the action of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
on
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{r})
. On the one hand, the action on
i_{P}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}}
is via a map
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}\rightarrow B_{r}
whose image is
A_{r}
. On the other hand, the right hand side is annihilated by an ideal of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
whose image in
A_{r}
under the above map is equal to
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since every element of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{r}},i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})
is
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
-equivariant it follows that
{\mathfrak{p}}
annihilates both sides as claimed.
∎

It is now straightforward to compute
\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r},i_{P}^{G_{n}}Z(\Delta)^{\otimes r})

Proof of Theorem

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
5.4
. Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n

Recall that if
[\mathfrak{m}]
denotes an inertial multisegment with

We remind the reader that the isomorphism class of this representation depends on the choice of ordering of the

We have natural isomorphisms:

on the left hand side induces an action of
E_{\lambda}
on
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]
, which is by a natural map:

We first show:

Lemma 11.1
.

The map
f_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
takes a
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-point
x
of
\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]
to the
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-point of
\operatorname{Spec}E_{\lambda}
giving the action of
E_{\lambda}
on
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{x}))

In particular,

factors through
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}
, and is independent of the choice of ordering of segments used to define
{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}]).

Proof.

The
W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
-invariance and independence of order are immediate from the first claim, since
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]
is reduced. For the first claim, note that the composition
x\circ f_{[\mathfrak{m}]}
is the map giving the action of

on

\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}])\otimes_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}],x}\overline{\mathcal{K}}),

and this space is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{\mathcal{K}}[G_{n}]}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},Z({\mathfrak{m}}_{x})).

∎

Let
e
be the primitive idempotent of
Z_{n,\overline{\mathcal{K}}}
corresponding to the block containing
{\tilde{I}}([\mathfrak{m}])
, and let

be the set of equivalence classes of inertial multisegments
[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]
such that
\lambda([{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}])=\lambda
and
{\tilde{I}}([{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}])
is contained in the block corresponding to
e
. We can now show:

Theorem 11.2
.

The map
f_{[\mathfrak{m}]}:E_{\lambda}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}
identifies
\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[\mathfrak{m}]}}
with a connected component of
\operatorname{Spec}eE_{\lambda}
. The product, over

identifies

with the product of the rings
\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}]^{W_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}}.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the second, so we focus on the second claim. Note that the maximal ideals of

are in bijection with the irreducible representations of

of type

in the block corresponding to

in

\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[\mathfrak{m}]}]^{W_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}}
are in bijection with the multisegments in this equivalence class. Thus the product of the maps
f_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}
induces a bijection of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-points between
\operatorname{Spec}eE_{\lambda}
and the disjoint union of the smooth schemes
\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathcal{K}}[X_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}]^{W_{[{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}]}}.

∎

From this result parts (1), (2), and (3) of Theorem

Since

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 12.15
.

Let
\pi
be a finitely generated representation of
G_{n}
. Suppose that each summand in the cosocle of
\pi
has highest derivative partition not smaller than or equal to
\lambda
. Then
\pi^{\preceq\lambda}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\pi^{\prime}
be the image of
\bigoplus_{\lambda^{\prime}\not\preceq\lambda}W^{\prime}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(W^{\prime}_{\lambda},\pi)\to\pi
, i.e.
\pi^{\preceq\lambda}=\pi/\pi^{\prime}
. If
\pi^{\preceq\lambda}
is not zero it has an irreducible quotient
\sigma
, i.e. we have a surjective map
\pi^{\preceq\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\sigma
. However, then clearly
\sigma
also appears as a quotient of
\pi
and hence by assumption has highest derivative partition
\lambda_{\sigma}
not smaller than or equal to
\lambda
. We have a nonzero map
W^{\prime}_{\lambda_{\sigma}}\to\sigma
which by the projectivity of
W^{\prime}_{\lambda_{\sigma}}
gives rise to a nonzero map
W^{\prime}_{\lambda_{\sigma}}\to\pi
, whose image is not contained in
\pi^{\prime}
. This yields a contradiction.

1.1
Open questions and main results

Consider the following semilinear wave equation with scale-invariant damping

\left\{\kern 5.0pt\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{t}\,\partial_{t}u=|u|^{p},\\

\Delta=\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\partial_{n}^{2}

(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x))\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\text{supp $u_{0}$, supp $u_{1}$}\subset B(0,1)

1.2
Physical backgrounds on some wave operators with scale-invariant damping

At first, we point out that the linear wave operator
\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta+\frac{\mu}{t}\partial_{t}
in (
1.1
)
admits an interesting physical background
which comes from the compressible Euler equations with time-dependent damping. In fact,
consider the 3-D compressible isentropic Euler equations

\left\{\kern 5.0pt\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u+p\,\textup{I}_{3})=-\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\rho u,\\
&\rho(0,x)=\bar{\rho}+\varepsilon\rho_{0}(x),\quad u(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
\rho
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, and

p
stand for the density, velocity, and pressure, respectively,

\textup{I}_{3}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix,
\mu>0
,
\lambda\geq 0
and

\bar{\rho}>0
are constants,
u_{0}=(u_{1,0},u_{2,0},u_{3,0})
,
(\rho_{0},u_{0})\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
(\rho_{0},u_{0})\not\equiv 0
,
\rho(0,x)>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently
small. In addition, the state equation of gases is

p(\rho)=A\rho^{\gamma}
with
A>0
and
\gamma>1
being constants,
and the gases are assumed to be irrotational, namely,
\operatorname{curl}u_{0}=\left(\partial_{2}u_{3,0}-\partial_{3}u_{2,0},\partial_{3}u_{1,0}-\partial_{1}u_{3,0},\partial_{1}u_{2,0}-\partial_{2}u_{1,0}\right)\equiv 0
. In this case,
one can know that
\operatorname{curl}u(t,x)\equiv 0
holds for any
t\geq 0

as long as the smooth solution
(\rho,u)(t,x)
exists.
By introducing a scalar potential function
\chi=\chi(t,x)
with

u=\nabla_{x}\chi
, we substitute
u=\nabla_{x}\chi

into the second equation of (
1.6
) to obtain

\partial_{t}\chi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\chi|^{2}+h(\rho)+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\,\chi=0,

(1.7)

where
\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\rho)=c^{2}(\rho)/\rho
with
c(\rho)=\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)}
and

h(\bar{\rho})=0
. Without loss of generality,
c(\bar{\rho})=1
is supposed.
By
h^{\prime}(\rho)>0
for
\rho>0
and the implicit function theorem, one has from (
1.7
) that

\rho=h^{-1}\left(-\left(\partial_{t}\chi+\frac{1}{2}\,|\nabla_{x}\chi|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\,\chi\right)\right),

(1.8)

where
\bar{\rho}=h^{-1}(0)
and
h^{-1}
is the inverse function of

h=h(\rho)
.
Inserting (
1.8
) into the first equation of
(
1.6
) derives

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}^{2}\chi-c^{2}(\rho)\Delta\chi+2\sum_{k=1}^{3}\partial_{k}\chi\partial_{tk}^{2}\chi+\sum_{i,k=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\chi\partial_{k}\chi\partial_{ik}^{2}\chi+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\,|\nabla_{x}\chi|^{2}+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\,\chi\right)=0.\end{split}

(1.9)

Let
\displaystyle\psi=\frac{\chi}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}
. It follows from (
1.9
) and
c(\bar{\rho})=1
that

\square\psi+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)^{\lambda}}\partial_{t}\psi+\frac{2\lambda}{1+t}\partial_{t}\psi-\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{(1+t)^{2}}\psi=Q(\psi,\partial\psi,\partial^{2}\psi),

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle Q(\psi,\partial\psi,\partial^{2}\psi)=

\displaystyle(c^{2}(\rho)-1)\Delta\psi-2(1+t)^{\lambda}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi-2\lambda(1+t)^{\lambda-1}|\nabla_{x}\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle-\mu|\nabla_{x}\psi|^{2}-(1+t)^{2\lambda}\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\partial_{i}\psi\partial_{j}\psi\partial_{ij}^{2}\psi.

Especially, for
\lambda=1
, (
1.10
) becomes

\square\psi+\frac{\mu+2}{1+t}\partial_{t}\psi=Q(\psi,\partial\psi,\partial^{2}\psi).

(1.11)

Thus, the linear wave operator
\square+\frac{\mu+2}{1+t}\partial_{t}
with scale-invariant damping
naturally appears in (
1.11
).
Moreover, the detailed analyses on
\square+\frac{\mu+2}{1+t}\partial_{t}
and the resulting time-weighted energy estimates
play key roles in proving the global existence of the smooth solution
(\rho,u)
in (
1.6
) (see
[
14
]
-
[
15
]

and
[
1
]
). On the other hand, analogous scale-invariant damping

[

]

where

for

,
and the local sound speed is

.
Moreover, for simplicity, one can assume that the following
Bernoulli’s law holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{c^{2}(\rho)}{\gamma-1}=B,

(1.13)

where
|v|^{2}=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{3}^{2}>c^{2}(\rho)
, and

Note that for
\rho(x)=\hat{\rho}(r)
and

[

]

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(r^{2}\hat{\rho}\hat{V})^{\prime}(r)=0,\\
&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\hat{V}}^{2}(r)+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}{\hat{\rho}}^{\gamma-1}(r)=1,\\
&\hat{\rho}(1)=\rho_{0},\quad\hat{V}(1)=q_{0},\quad q_{0}>c(\rho_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

If the supersonic flow in
\Omega_{0}
is irrotational, then one can introduce a potential function
\Phi(x)

with

such that (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}((\partial_{i}\Phi)^{2}-c^{2}(\rho))\partial_{i}^{2}\Phi+2\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi\partial_{ij}^{2}\Phi=0,\qquad x_{3}\geq 0.

(1.14)

By the linearization of (

(\hat{\rho}(r),\hat{V}(r))

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\dot{\Phi}=\dot{f}\quad\text{in $\Omega_{0}$},

1.3
Reformulation and more general global existence results

For
0<\mu<1
in (
1.2

\mu=\frac{k}{k+1}
with

and

\partial_{T}^{2}u-T^{2k}\Delta u=T^{2k}|u|^{p}

1.4
Derivation of conformal powers
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)
and
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,\mu)

We now derive the conformal invariant exponents
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)

and
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,\mu)
in Theorem
1.3
and Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
, respectively.
Note that the Lagrangian functional for (
1.16
) is

L(u,u^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}t^{m}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}-\frac{t^{\alpha}}{p+1}|u|^{p+1}
with
u^{\prime}=(\partial_{0}u,\partial_{1}u,\partial_{2}u)
.
Then one has that

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}L(u+\varepsilon\psi,(u+\varepsilon\psi)^{\prime})dtdx\big|_{\varepsilon=0}=0
for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
This means that
u
should satisfy the corresponding Euler-Lagrangian equation

\partial_{u}L(u,u^{\prime})-\sum_{j=0}^{2}\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{u_{j}}L(u,u^{\prime})\right)=0,

(1.22)

where
u_{j}=\partial_{j}u
for
j=0,1,2
.
By direct computation, it is easy to know that the solution of (
1.16
) is invariant under the scaling transformation

u(t,x)\longrightarrow\lambda^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}}u(\lambda t,\lambda^{\frac{m+2}{2}}x)
.
Set
u_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}}u(\lambda t,\lambda^{\frac{m+2}{2}}x)
,
then one has

\partial_{\lambda}L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})=\partial_{u}L(u_{\lambda},u^{\prime}_{\lambda})\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\partial_{u_{j}}L(u_{\lambda},u^{\prime}_{\lambda})\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{j}u_{\lambda}.

(1.23)

Substituting (
1.22
) into (
1.23
) derives

\partial_{\lambda}L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{u_{j}}L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}\right)
.
Due to

\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=1}=\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}u+t\partial_{t}u+\frac{m+2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}x_{j}\partial_{j}u,

then

\partial_{\lambda}L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})|_{\lambda=1}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}\partial_{j}\big(\partial_{u_{j}}L(u,u^{\prime})\big(\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}u+t\partial_{t}u+\frac{m+2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}x_{j}\partial_{j}u\big)\big).

(1.24)

On the other hand, it follows from direct computation that

L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})=\lambda^{\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p-1}+2}(L(u,u^{\prime}))(\lambda t,\lambda^{\frac{m+2}{2}}x)

and

\partial_{\lambda}L(u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}^{\prime})|_{\lambda=1}=\big(\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p-1}+2\big)L(u,u^{\prime})+t\partial_{t}L(u,u^{\prime})+\frac{m+2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}x_{j}\partial_{j}L(u,u^{\prime}).

(1.25)

Combining (
1.24
) with (
1.25
) yields

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}Q_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}Q_{j}=\big(\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p-1}-m-1\big)L(u,u^{\prime}),\end{split}

(1.26)

where

\begin{split}&Q_{0}=\partial_{u_{0}}L(u,u^{\prime})\big(\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}u+t\partial_{t}u+\frac{m+2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}x_{j}\partial_{j}u\big)-tL(u,u^{\prime}),\\
&Q_{j}=\partial_{u_{j}}L(u,u^{\prime})\big(\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}u+t\partial_{t}u+\frac{m+2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}x_{j}\partial_{j}u\big)-\frac{m+2}{2}x_{j}L(u,u^{\prime}),\quad j=1,2.\end{split}

Since
\text{supp}u(t,x)
is compact with respect to the variable
x
, then integrating (
1.4
) over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
yields

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Q_{0}dx=\big(\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p-1}-m-1\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}L(u,u^{\prime})dx.

(1.27)

When
\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p-1}-m-1=0
,
Q_{0}
is conserved by (
1.27
).
From this, the conformal invariant power for problem (
1.16
) can be determined as

p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)=\frac{m+2\alpha+5}{m+1}.

(1.28)

It should be pointed out that
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,0,0)=5

and
p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,0)=\frac{m+5}{m+1}
in (
1.28
) just correspond to
the conformal powers of 2-D semilinear equations
\square u=|u|^{p}
and
\partial_{t}^{2}u-t^{m}\Delta u=|u|^{p}

respectively (see
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
24
]
).

For
0<\mu<1
in (
1.2
), corresponding to the equation (
1.16
), we have

m=\frac{2\mu}{1-\mu}
and
\alpha=\frac{2\mu}{1-\mu}
. Then it is derived from (
1.28
) that

p_{conf}(2,\mu)=\frac{\mu+5}{\mu+1}.

(1.29)

For
1<\mu<2
in (
1.2
), compared with the equation (
1.16
), there hold
m=\frac{2(2-\mu)}{\mu-1}
and
\alpha=\frac{2(2-\mu)}{\mu-1}+1-p
. In terms of (
1.28
), one has

p_{conf}(2,\mu)=\frac{6(2-\mu)+(7-2p_{conf}(2,\mu))(\mu-1)}{3-\mu},

which yields that for

p_{conf}(2,\mu)=\frac{\mu+5}{\mu+1}.

Sketch of proof on Theorem

To prove Theorem

, motivated by the weighted Strichartz estimates
derived in
[

,
21
]
for the
n-

[

Let

v(1,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}v(1,x)=u_{1}(x),\end{cases}

(1.32)

and

is a solution of the following 2-D inhomogeneous problem

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}^{2}w-t^{m}\triangle w=F(t,x),\\
&w(1,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}w(1,x)=0.\end{cases}

(1.33)

For
v
, based on the pointwise estimate of
v
in
[
13
, formula 2-20]
,
we can obtain such a new spacetime-weighted Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\|\left(\left(\phi_{m}(t)+2\right)^{2}-|x|^{2}\right)^{\gamma}t^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}v\|_{L^{q}\left(\left[1,+\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

(1.34)

\displaystyle\leq C_{m,q,\gamma,\delta}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\frac{m+3}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big),

where

and

For

, our main concern is to establish a precise Strichartz inequality as follows

\begin{split}\|(\big(\phi_{m}(t)+2\big)^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{1}}&t^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}w\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|(\big(\phi_{m}(t)+2\big)^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\gamma_{2}}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{q}}F\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},\end{split}

where

(1.43)

3.1
Spacetime-weighted Strichartz estimate

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v
be a solution of (

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|(\left(\phi_{m}(t)+2\right)^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\gamma}t^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}v\|_{L^{q}\left(\left[1,+\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\frac{m+3}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}),

where
\alpha>-1
,
p_{\text{crit }}(2,m,\alpha)+1<q<p_{\text{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)+1
,

C=C(m,q,\alpha,\gamma,\delta)>0
,
the positive constants
\gamma
and
\delta
fulfill

0<\gamma<\frac{m+1}{m+2}-\frac{m+4+2\alpha}{(m+2)q},\quad 0<\delta<\frac{m+1}{m+2}-\gamma-\frac{1}{q}.

(3.2)

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
v
satisfies that for
\alpha>-1
and
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|t^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}v|

\displaystyle\leq C\phi_{m}(t)^{-\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\frac{2\alpha}{q(m+2)}}\left(1+\left||x|-\phi_{m}(t)\right|\right)^{-\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\times\big(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\frac{m+3}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big).

Then it follows from (
3.2
),
\alpha>-1
and
t\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\|(\left(\phi_{m}(t)+2\right)^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\gamma}t^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}v\|_{L^{q}\left(\left[1,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{q}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi_{m}(t)^{q\left(-\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\frac{2\alpha}{q(m+2)}+\gamma\right)}\int_{0}^{\phi_{m}(t)+1}\left(1+\left|r-\phi_{m}(t)\right|\right)^{q\left(\gamma-\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta\right)}r\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\times\big(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\frac{m+3}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{\frac{m+3}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\delta,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big).

Thus, the proof of (
3.1

Time-weighted angular mixed-norm Strichartz estimate

For the solution
v
of (

(3.7)

On the other hand, a different scaling argument (see Page 1845 of

\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{m+2}{2\nu}-\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}.

\displaystyle\frac{\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(Au_{0})(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\nu}L_{\theta}^{2}([1,+\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi])}}{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}}

\varepsilon>0

\frac{m+1}{m+2}-\frac{2}{(m+2)q}-\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}\geq 0,

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the restrictions (
3.7

3.8

\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(Au_{0})(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\nu}L_{\theta}^{2}([1,+\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi])}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)},

where
(q,\nu)\neq(\infty,\infty)

q,\nu\geq 2,\quad\frac{1}{\nu}+\frac{m-p+1}{(m+2)(p+1)}>0,

(3.11)

\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{m+2}{2\nu}-\frac{m-p+1}{p+1},\quad-2<m-p+1\leq-1,

(3.12)

Proof.

Although the proof procedure is similar to that of Lemma 3.2 in

]

To get (

.
If so, then (

Note that the first endpoint case corresponds to

s=1-\frac{2}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}
.
By
\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\subseteq L^{\nu}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
for

then

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{r}^{\nu}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{r}^{\nu}L_{\theta}^{\nu}\left([1,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}}\left([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

It follows from (
2.11
) and (
3.5
) that if
\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\notin\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, then

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}

\displaystyle\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}}\left([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\big\|\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi-\phi_{m}(t)|\xi|)}t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}a_{2}(t,\xi)\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}[1,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\||\xi|^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}}(1+\phi_{m}(t)|\xi|)^{\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}|\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)|\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}[1,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

This, together with (
3.11
), yields

\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{r}^{\nu}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}=\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,2}^{1-\frac{2}{\nu}-\frac{2(m-p+1)}{(m+2)(p+1)}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

(3.13)

Next we show (
3.10
) with the second endpoint case of
\nu=\infty,2\leq q<\infty
.
For this purpose, it suffices to prove that for
2\leq q<\infty
,

\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\quad\text{ if }\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)=0\text{ for }|\xi|\notin[\frac{1}{2},1].

(3.14)

Indeed, define the dyadic decomposition of the operator
A
as

Au_{0j}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\varphi\left(2^{-j}|\xi|\right)\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

By (
3.14
) and the compact support property of
\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)
, one has that for
\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)=0
when
|\xi|\notin[\frac{1}{2},1]
,

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\||\xi|^{1-\frac{2}{m+2}(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1})}\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{m+2}(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1})}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

Since (
3.15
) is invariant under scaling, then one easily obtains that for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0j}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}\leq C\left\|u_{0j}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{m+2}(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1})}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

(3.16)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0j}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0j}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|u_{0j}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{2}{m+2}(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1})}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{1-\frac{2}{m+2}(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1})}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

Then by interpolation, (
3.10
) can be obtained.

Next we establish (
3.14
). Due to the support condition of
\hat{u_{0}}
, one then has

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{2}\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\hat{u_{0}}(\rho\cos\theta,\rho\sin\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\theta\mathrm{d}\rho,

(3.17)

where
\xi=(\rho\cos\theta,\rho\sin\theta)
. By the expansion of Fourier series for
\hat{u_{0}}
,
there exist coefficients
c_{k}(\rho)(k\in\mathbb{Z})
vanishing for
\rho\notin\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
such that

\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)=\sum c_{k}(\rho)e^{ik\theta}
.
Due to
a_{2}(t,\xi)=a_{2}(t,|\xi|)
in (
3.5
), then

t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(Au_{0})(t,\xi)=t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}e^{-i\phi_{m}(t)\rho}a_{2}(t,\rho)\sum_{k}c_{k}(\rho)e^{ik\theta}.

(3.18)

Note that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{2}\approx\sum_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|c_{k}(\rho)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\approx\sum_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\hat{c}_{k}(s)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

(3.19)

where
\hat{c}_{k}(s)
is the Fourier transformation of
c_{k}(\rho)
with respect to the variable
\rho
.
Let
\beta(\tau)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\beta(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&\frac{1}{2}\leq\tau\leq 1,\\
0,&\tau\notin\left[\frac{1}{4},2\right].\end{cases}

Set

\alpha(t,\rho)=\rho\beta(\rho)a_{2}(t,\rho)t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}.

(3.20)

Analogously treated as in (3.31) of
[
11
]
, we have

\displaystyle t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(Au_{0})(t,r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\omega}\hat{\alpha}_{t}(\phi_{m}(t)-s-r\cos\omega)\hat{c}_{k}(s)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}s\big)e^{ik\theta},

where
\hat{\alpha}_{t}(\xi)
stands for the Fourier transformation of
\alpha(t,\rho)
for
\rho
.
Direct computation yields that for any
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(Au_{0})(t,r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta))|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\omega}\hat{\alpha}_{t}(\phi_{m}(t)-s-r\cos\omega)\hat{c}_{k}(s)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}s\big|^{2}.

By Lemma 3.4 in
[
11
]
, (
3.21
) and Hölder’s inequality, one has that for any small fixed constant
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|^{2}_{L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(1+\phi_{m}(t))^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}(1+|\phi_{m}(t)-s|)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\hat{c}_{k}(s)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Note that for any
q\geq 2
, we can choose small
\delta>0
such that

\sigma=q\big(\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{m+2}{2}\delta-\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}\big)>1.

(3.22)

Then it follows from Minkowski’s inequality that for
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([1,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{k}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\big(\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(1+\phi_{m}(t))^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}(1+|\phi_{m}(t)-s|)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\big|^{q}\mathrm{d}t\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\hat{c}_{k}(s)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
s\leq 0
, then by (
3.22
), we arrive at

\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(1+\phi_{m}(t))^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}(1+\phi_{m}(t))^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\big|^{q}\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\sigma}\mathrm{d}t\leq C.

(3.24)

If
s>0
, set
s=\phi_{m}(\bar{s})
, then one has that by (
3.22
),

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\big|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}(1+\phi_{m}(t))^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}(1+|\phi_{m}(t)-s|)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\big|^{q}\mathrm{d}t

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}(1+|t-\bar{s}|)^{\frac{(m+2)q}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)}(1+t)^{-\frac{mq}{4}+\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C,

Substituting (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) into (
3.23
) derives that for
\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\notin[\frac{1}{2},1]
,

\|t^{\frac{m-p+1}{p+1}}Au_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{r}^{\infty}L_{\theta}^{2}\left([0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi]\right)}\leq C\big(\sum_{k}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\hat{c}_{k}(s)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

Therefore, (
3.14
) is shown and further (
3.10
) holds.
∎

4
Time-weighted Strichartz estimates for inhomogenous Tricomi equations

Note that by a scaling argument as in
[
21
]
and
[
24
]
,
the equation
\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{u}-t^{m}\Delta\tilde{u}=t^{\alpha}|\tilde{u}|^{p}

with initial data
(\tilde{u},\partial_{t}\tilde{u})(1,x)\in(\dot{H}^{s},\dot{H}^{s-\frac{2}{m+2}})(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is ill-posed for
s<1-\frac{2}{m+2}\cdot\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}
.
On the other hand, it follows from a concentration argument that

\tilde{u}
is ill-posed (see Remark 1.3 of
[
24
]
) for

s<\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{(m+2)(2m+6)}-\frac{3(\alpha+2)}{(2m+6)(p-1)}.

In addition, a direct computation shows that for
p\geq p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)
,

1-\frac{2}{m+2}\cdot\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}\geq\frac{3}{4}-\frac{m}{(m+2)(2m+6)}-\frac{3(\alpha+2)}{(2m+6)(p-1)}.

(4.1)

Especially, for
p=p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)
, the equality in (
4.1
) holds
and
1-\frac{2}{m+2}\cdot\frac{\alpha+2}{p-1}=\frac{1}{m+2}
.

For the 2-D linear problem

\left\{\kern 5.0pt\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{v}-t^{m}\triangle\tilde{v}=0,\\
&\tilde{v}(0,x)=f(x),\quad\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(0,x)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

such a time-weighted Strichartz inequality is expected

\|t^{\beta}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\,\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(4.3)

where
\beta>0
,
q\geq 1
and
r\geq 1
are some suitable constants related to
s
(
0<s<1
).
By a scaling argument, one has from (
4.3
) that

\beta+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{m+2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{r}=\frac{m+2}{2}\left(1-s\right).

(4.4)

When
r=q
in (
4.4
), one can obtain
an endpoint
q=\tilde{q}_{0}
for
s=\frac{1}{m+2}
(the value of
s=\frac{1}{m+2}
comes from the equality
in (
4.1
) with
p=p_{\textup{conf}}(2,m,\alpha)
),

\tilde{q}_{0}\equiv\frac{2m+6}{m+1-2\beta}>2.

(4.5)

It is pointed out that the value of
\tilde{q}_{0}
will be used repeatedly later.

We now investigate the following linear inhomogeneous problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}w-t^{m}\Delta w=F(t,x),&(t,x)\in[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
w(1,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}w(1,x)=0.\end{cases}

(4.6)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
w
solve (
4.6
). Then

\|t^{\beta}w\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\,\bigl\|t^{-\beta}|D_{x}|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{m+2}}F\bigr\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(4.7)

where
\gamma=1-\frac{2\beta}{m+2}-\frac{2m+6}{q(m+2)}
,
0<\beta\leq\frac{m}{4}
,
\tilde{q}_{0}\leq q<\infty
,
\tilde{p}_{0}=\frac{2m+6}{m+2\beta+5}
with

\frac{1}{\tilde{p}_{0}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{q}_{0}}=1
, and the generic constant
C>0
only depends on
m
and
q
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.14
) that

t^{\beta}w(t,x)=(AF)(t,x)\equiv\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left(x\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}a(t,\tau,\xi)\hat{F}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau,

(4.8)

where
a(t,\tau,\xi)
satisfies

\bigl|\partial_{\xi}^{\kappa}a(t,\tau,\xi)\bigr|\leq Ct^{\beta}\left(1+\phi_{m}(t)|\xi|\right)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}\left(1+\phi_{m}(\tau)|\xi|\right)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{m+2}-|\kappa|}\leq|\xi|^{-\frac{4\beta+2}{m+2}-|\kappa|}\tau^{-\beta}.

(4.9)

To treat
(AF)(t,x)
, the following more general operator is introduced as in (3.35) of
[
10
]

(A^{\nu}F)(t,x)=\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left(x\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}a(t,\tau,\xi)\hat{F}(\tau,\xi)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(4.10)

where the parameter
\nu\in(0,1)
.

Set

A^{\nu}_{j}F(t,x)=\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left(x\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}\varphi(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}})a(t,\tau,\xi)\hat{F}(\tau,\xi)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(4.11)

where the function
\varphi
is given in (
2.16
).
We next show the following inequality for

\gamma=1-\frac{2\beta}{m+2}-\frac{2m+6}{q(m+2)}
and
\tilde{q}_{0}\leq q<\infty
,

\|t^{\beta}w\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\bigl\|t^{-\beta}|D_{x}|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{m+2}}F\bigr\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

That is,

\|t^{\beta}|D_{x}|^{-\gamma+\frac{1}{m+2}}w\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|t^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By the definition of
A^{\nu}
in (
4.10
) with

\nu=\gamma-\frac{1}{m+2}
, once

\|A^{\nu}F\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|t^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(4.12)

is established, then (
4.7
) holds true.

To derive (
4.12
), in terms of Lemma
2.1
, it requires to show

\|A^{\nu}_{j}F\|_{L^{q}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|t^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.13)

By applying the interpolation argument, it only needs to prove (
4.13
) for
the two endpoint cases of
q=\tilde{q}_{0}
and
q=\infty
. Meanwhile, the corresponding indices
\nu
are denoted by
\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{1}
,
respectively.
It follows from direct computation
that
\nu_{0}=1-\frac{2\beta}{m+2}-\frac{2m+6}{\tilde{q}_{0}(m+2)}-\frac{1}{m+2}=0
and
\nu_{1}=1-\frac{2\beta+1}{m+2}
.
For treating
A^{\nu_{0}}_{j}=A^{0}_{j}
, set

T_{j}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left(x\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}\varphi(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}})a(t,\tau,\xi)\hat{F}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Next we show

\|T_{j}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C|t-\tau|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.14)

For
0<\beta\leq\frac{m}{m+6}
, without loss of generality,
t\geq\tau
is assumed. Note that

\displaystyle\big|t^{\,\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}-\beta}\varphi(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}})a(t,\tau,\xi)\big|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}-\beta}t^{\beta}\left(1+\phi_{m}(t)|\xi|\right)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}\left(1+\phi_{m}(\tau)|\xi|\right)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{m+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\xi|^{-\frac{2}{m+2}-\frac{m(\beta+1)}{(m+2)((m+3)}-\frac{2\beta}{m+2}}\tau^{-\beta}.

Denote

b(t,\tau,\xi)=2^{j(\frac{2}{m+2}+\frac{m(\beta+1)}{(m+2)(m+3)}+\frac{2\beta}{m+2})}t^{\,\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}-\beta}\varphi(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}})a(t,\tau,\xi).

Then

|b(t,\tau,\xi)|\leq C\tau^{-\beta}.

Define

T_{j1}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left((x-y)\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}t^{-\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}+\beta}b(t,\tau,\xi)F(\tau,y)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}y.

By the assumptions of
t\geq\tau
and
0<\beta\leq\frac{m}{m+6}
, one has

\|T_{j1}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\left|t-\tau\right|^{\beta-\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

and

\displaystyle\|T_{j1}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\frac{3}{2}j}t^{\,\beta-\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}}\left|\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau)\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\frac{3}{2}j}\left|t-\tau\right|^{\beta-\frac{(\beta+1)m}{2m+6}-\frac{m+2}{4}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By interpolation, we have

\|T_{j1}^{0}F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C2^{\frac{3(\beta+1)}{m+3}j}\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|T_{j}^{0}F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-(\frac{2}{m+2}+\frac{m(\beta+1)}{(m+2)(m+3)}+\frac{2\beta}{m+2}-\frac{3(\beta+1)}{m+3})j}\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=C\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

We consider the remaining case of
\frac{m}{m+6}<\beta\leq\frac{m}{4}
. Let

b(t,\tau,\xi)=|\xi|^{\frac{4\beta+2}{m+2}}\varphi(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}})a(t,\tau,\xi)
and

T_{j2}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left((x-y)\cdot\xi-(\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau))|\xi|\right)}b(t,\tau,\xi)F(\tau,y)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}y.

Then

\|T_{j2}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(4.16)

while for the
L^{\infty}
estimate, it follows from the stationary phase method and
\beta>\frac{m}{m+6}
that

\displaystyle\|T_{j2}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{2j}(1+2^{j}\left|\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(\tau)\right|)^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{(\beta+1)(m+2)}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{2j-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{(\beta+1)(m+2)}j}\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{2(\beta+1)}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This, together with (
4.16
), yields

\|T_{j2}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C2^{\frac{4\beta+2}{m+2}j}\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.17)

Then

\|T_{j}^{0}F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=2^{-\frac{4\beta+2}{m+2}j}\|T_{j2}^{0}F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\left|t-\tau\right|^{-\frac{m+1-2\beta}{m+3}}\|\tau^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(4.18)

Thus (
4.14
) follows from (
4.15
) and (
4.18
) immediately.

Note that
\displaystyle A^{0}_{j}F(t,x)=\int_{1}^{t}T_{j}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
. In addition,
by (
4.14
) and the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we
may conclude

\|\int_{1}^{\infty}\|T_{j}^{0}F(t,\tau,x)\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}_{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\|_{L^{\tilde{q}_{0}}_{t}[1,\infty)}\leq C\|t^{-\beta}F\|_{L^{\tilde{p}_{0}}([1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Let

5

Appendix A
Some useful estimates

Lemma A.1
.

(
5.17
) holds.

Proof.

Note that
H=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}H_{j}
with

H_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\left\{x\cdot\xi-\left[\phi_{m}(t)-\phi_{m}(s)\right]|\xi|\right\}}\varphi\left(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{j}}\right)a(t,s,\xi)\hat{F}(s,\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

(A.1)

It follows from
1\leq s\leq 2
and (
5.16
) that

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(t,s,\xi)\big|\leq C\phi_{m}(t)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}|\xi|^{-1-|\beta|}.

(A.2)

Set

T_{j}F(t,s,x)=:t^{\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}H_{j}(t,s,x)\quad\text{ with }\lambda_{j}=2^{j},

then it follows from stationary phase method that

\left\|T_{j}F(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\lambda_{j}^{-1}t^{\frac{4\alpha-mq_{0}}{4q_{0}}}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

and

\left\|T_{j}F(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\lambda_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{4\alpha-mq_{0}}{4q_{0}}}|t-s|^{-\frac{m+2}{4}}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

By interpolation and direct computation, one has that for
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|T_{j}F(t,s,\cdot)

\displaystyle\|_{L^{q_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{\alpha-2m}{2(m+3+\alpha)}}|t-s|^{-\frac{2}{q_{0}}\left(1+\frac{m}{4}\right)}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{q_{0}}{q_{0}-1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

(A.3)

Define

T_{+}F(t,s,x)\equiv\sum_{j\geq 0}T_{j}F(t,s,x),

then

\left\|T_{+}F(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C|t-s|^{-\frac{2}{q_{0}}\left(1+\frac{m}{4}\right)}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{q_{0}}{q_{0}-1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

(A.4)

While for
j<0
, let

T_{-}F(t,s,x)\equiv\sum_{j<0}T_{j}F(t,s,x).

Then by (
5.16
) and the condition
t\geq t-s\gtrsim t
, we obtain that for
1\leq s\leq 2
,

\displaystyle\|T_{-}F(t,s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}||\xi|^{-\frac{2}{m+2}}t^{\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}(1+\phi_{m}(t)|\xi|)^{-\frac{m}{2(m+2)}}\hat{F}(s,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}(\int_{0}^{1}r^{1-\frac{m+4}{m+2}}\phi_{m}(t)^{-\frac{m}{m+2}}\mathrm{d}r)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}-\frac{m}{4}}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

Similarly,

\left\|T_{-}F(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}-\frac{m}{4}-\frac{m+2}{4}}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

By interpolation, one arrives at

\left\|T_{-}F(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C|t-s|^{-\frac{2}{q_{0}}\left(1+\frac{m}{4}\right)}\|s^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}}}F(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{q_{0}}{q_{0}-1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}.

(A.5)

Thus, combining (
A.4
)-(
A.5
) with Littlewood-Paley theory yields (
5.17
).
∎

Lemma A.2

(

Two dimensional versions of the affine Grassmannian and their geometric description
Andrea MAFFEI, Valerio MELANI, Gabriele VEZZOSI

Abstract
. For a smooth affine algebraic group G G over an algebraically closed field, we consider several two-variables generalizations of the affine Grassmannian G ⁡ ( ( t ) ) / G ⁡ [ [ t ] ] G(\!(t)\!)/G[\![t]\!] , given by quotients of the double loop group G ​ ( ( x ) ) ​ ( ( y ) ) G(\!(x)\!)(\!(y)\!) . We prove that they are representable by ind-schemes if G G is solvable. Given a smooth surface X X and a flag of subschemes of X X , we provide a geometric interpretation of the two-variables Grassmannians, in terms of bundles and trivialisation data defined on appropriate loci in X X , which depend on the flag.

Future directions.
This paper leaves some natural open questions, some of which we plan to investigate in the near future. First of all, Proposition
2.16
provides a geometric interpretation of
\mathcal{G}r^{L}_{G}
only on
k
-points (and for some classes of groups
G
). It would be interesting to extend this interpretation to the full sheaf
\mathcal{G}r^{L}_{G}
, also considering more general groups
G
. Secondly, our techniques of proof of ind-representability for the two dimensional Grassmannians (Theorem A) are very concrete and computational, and to our knowledge they cannot be directly extended to cover the case of a general reductive
G
. Some new ideas and techniques seem to be needed here.

As suggested by S. Raskin, the
2
-dimensional local fields Grassmannian might be a good candidate for hosting some
2
-dimensional version of Geometric Satake. We think this is a very interesting research direction. Finally, the case of the geometric jet Grassmannian will be more carefully studied in a forthcoming paper with G. Nocera. In fact, while our geometric Grassmannians are defined with respect to a
fixed
flag of subschemes of
X
, it is possible to let the flag vary, much in the spirit of the approach taken in
[
9
]
. It is possible to show that many of the properties of the Beilinson-Drinfeld Grassmannian for curves can be extended to (a flag version of) the geometric jet Grassmannian: in particular, we will prove that it enjoys a
flag factorization property
(we refer to the Introduction of
[
9
]
for more details).

Another interesting question is to relate Parshin’s higher reciprocity laws on surfaces (see e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
) to our geometric Grassmannians (by allowing the flag
(D,Z)
to vary in appropriate families).

Related works.
As mentioned at the beginning of this Introduction, this paper is, in some sense, a continuation of
[
9
]
. The point of view here is more concrete: we do not consider derived moduli stacks nor do we allow our flags of subschemes to vary in families. On the other hand, we study here different Grassmannians with respect to the single one considered in
[

[

1.
Two dimensional quotient Grassmannians and their ind-representability

1.1.
Preliminaries

Let
k
an algebraically closed field. We denote by
{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}
the category of
k
-algebras. A functor from the category of
k
-algebras to the category of sets (or groups) will be called a presheaf.

1.1.1.
Ind-schemes: definitions

In this paper by
ind-scheme
we mean a functor
\mathcal{F}:{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}
that can be realized as a colimit of schemes
X_{i}
indexed by a partially ordered filtered set
(I,<)
and such that for
i<j
the map
X_{i}\longrightarrow X_{j}
is a closed immersion.

It is important to notice, however, that we do
not
assume that the colimit is over a
countable
poset: unfortunately none of our ind-schemes will have this property.

We say that that an ind-scheme
\mathcal{F}
is an
ind-affine
ind-scheme if, furthermore, the
X_{i}
’s can be chosen to be affine schemes.

1.1.2.
Jets and loops

Recall that given a functor
X:{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}
then the loop space
LX:{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}
of
\mathcal{F}
and the jet space
JX:{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}
of
\mathcal{F}
are defined as

LX(R)=X\big(R(\!(t)\!)\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad JX(R)=X\big(R[\![t]\!]\big).

Remark 1.1
.

The jet space and the loop space have the following representability properties which are well known and easy to prove.

If
X
is a scheme then
JX
is a scheme and if
X
is affine then
JX
is also affine. Moreover if
X
is an ind-scheme then
JX
is also an ind-scheme, and if
X
is an ind-affine ind-scheme then
JX
is an ind-affine ind-scheme.

The construction of the loop space has good representability properties only in the case of affine schemes. If
X
is an affine scheme then
LX
is an ind-affine ind-scheme and if
X
is an ind-affine ind-scheme then
LX
is also an ind-affine ind-scheme although the cardinality of the indexing set gets bigger.

1.1.3.
The affine Grassmannian

If
H:{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Groups}
then its affine Grassmannian is defined as the presheaf on
{\mathsf{CAlg}}_{k}
given by the quotient

\mathcal{G}r_{H}=\frac{LH}{JH}(R)=\frac{LH(R)}{JH(R)}.

If
H
is a smooth algebraic reductive group, by
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
it is known that this presheaf is already a sheaf for the fppf topology. In the case
H=G
is a solvable group this result is easier and follows from the following Lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
G
is solvable group then there exists an ind-affine ind-scheme

\Sigma_{G}\subset LG

such that for all
k
algebras
R
, every element in
LG(R)/JG(R)
has a unique representative in
\Sigma_{G}
.
This implies that
LG/JG
is an ind-affine ind-scheme and, in particular, a sheaf for the fppf topology.

The proof of Lemma
1.2
will be given in the next sections: in Section
1.2.2
we reduce the proof to the case of the additive and of the mutiplicative group, and in Section
1.3.1
we will study the case of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the case of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
being trivial.

Lemma
1.2
, together with
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
, implies the following fact that is maybe worth noticing.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
H
be a smooth algebraic group over
k
. The affine Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}r_{H}
is a sheaf for the fppf topology.

Proof.

Let
U
be the unipotent radical of
H
, then
K=G/U
is reductive group.
By Theorem 14.2.6 in
[
15
]
the projection map
\pi:H\longrightarrow K
has a section
(as algebraic varieties). This implies that for all
R
we have

K(R)=G(R)/U(R)
. In particular for all
R
we have that
LG(R)\longrightarrow LK(R)
and
JG(R)\longrightarrow JK(R)
are surjectives. Also notice that
JU(R)=JG(R)\cap LU(R)
inside
LG(R)
.

Now, it is an easy set-theoretical verification that, since
R\mapsto\mathcal{G}r_{U}(R)
is a sheaf (by Lemma
1.2
)​) and
R\mapsto\mathcal{G}r_{K}(R)
is a sheaf (by
[
4
]
),
R\mapsto\mathcal{G}r_{H}(R)
is a sheaf, too.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

As a last remark on the affine Grassmannian of an algebraic group we notice the following. Assume
H
is a semisimple group over
\mathbb{C}
and
B
is a Borel subgroup of
H
. By construction we have a natural morphism
\mathcal{G}r_{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}r_{H}
. By Iwasawa decomposition we have
H(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))=B(\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!))\cdot H(\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!])
, in particular at the level of
\mathbb{C}
points we have
\mathcal{G}r_{B}(\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{G}r_{H}(\mathbb{C})
however the two affine Grassmannians are very different: the first one is ind-affine, has infinitely many connected components and is not reduced, while the second one is ind-projective, reduced and connected.

{}^{L}\mathcal{G}r_{G}:R\longmapsto\frac{LLG(R)}{LJG(R)}=\frac{G\big(R(\!(t)\!)(\!(s)\!)\big)}{G\big(R(\!(t)\!)[\![s]\!]\big)}=L(\mathcal{G}r_{G})(R)

hence we are studying the loop presheaf of the usual affine Grassmannian of
G
.

•

The Big Grassmannian
is the presheaf quotient
LLG/JJG
.
In this case we are considering
H=JJG
, so that
H(R)=JJG(R)=G\big(R[\![t]\!][\![s]\!]\big)
. We denote it as follows:

\mathcal{G}r^{big}_{G}:R\longmapsto\frac{LLG(R)}{JJG(R)}=\frac{G\big(R(\!(t)\!)(\!(s)\!)\big)}{G\big(R[\![t]\!][\![s]\!]\big)}.

•

The 2-dimensional local field affine Grassmannian
is the presheaf quotient
LLG/G^{(2)}
, where for a given a
k
-algebra

we put

\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}(R)=R[\![t]\!]+\sum_{i>0}R(\!(t)\!)s^{i}\subset R(\!(t)\!)[\![s]\!]

given by

. The 2-dimensional local field affine Grassmannian is denoted as

\mathcal{G}r^{(2)}_{G}:R\longmapsto\frac{LLG}{G^{(2)}}(R)=\frac{LLG(R)}{G(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}(R))}=\frac{G\big(R(\!(t)\!)(\!(s)\!)\big)}{G\big(R[\![t]\!]+\sum_{i>0}R(\!(t)\!)s^{i}\subset R(\!(t)\!)[\![s]\!]\big)}

•

The affine Grassmannian of the jet group
is the presheaf quotient
LJG/JJG
and is denoted as

\mathcal{G}r^{J}_{G}:R\longmapsto\frac{LJG(R)}{JJG(R)}=\frac{G\big(R(\!(t)\!)[\![s]\!]\big)}{G\big(R[\![t]\!][\![s]\!]\big)}=\mathcal{G}r_{JG}(R)

which is indeed the analog of the affine Grassmannian for the jet group
JG
.

The loop space of the affine Grassmannian was considered in the paper of Frenkel and Zhu

2.1.
Fiber functors

We will use the formalism of fiber functors, as developed in
[
9
, Section 2.3]
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{X}
the forgetful functor
\mathsf{Aff}_{/X}\to\mathsf{St}_{k}
, which sends
(S\to X)\mapsto S
. A
fiber functor over

is a functor

\mathcal{F}:\mathsf{Aff}_{/X}\to\mathsf{St}_{k}

together with a natural transformation
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}_{X}
. Actually, all our fiber functors will land inside
\mathsf{Sch}_{k}
(and, obviously,
\mathcal{F}_{X}
is indeed a functor
\mathsf{Aff}_{/X}\to\mathsf{Sch}_{k}
). Given any stack or scheme
T\to X
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{T}
the induced fiber functor over
X
defined via

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{Y}\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{Aff}_{/X}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathsf{St}_{k}

\displaystyle(S\to X)

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle S\mathbin{\operatorname*{\times}_{X}}T

We will often write
S/X
to denote a map
S\to X
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T\to X
be a closed subscheme. The induced map
\mathcal{F}_{T}\to\mathcal{F}_{X}
is an objectwise closed immersion of affine schemes.

•

The
open complement

\mathcal{F}_{X\smallsetminus T}
is the fiber functor defined as

\mathcal{F}_{X\smallsetminus T}(S/X)=\mathcal{F}_{X}(S/X)\smallsetminus\mathcal{F}_{T}(S/X)=S\smallsetminus(S\times_{X}T).

•

The
formal neighborhood of
T
inside
X
is the fiber functor
\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}
sending
(S\to X)
to the ind-scheme formal completion of
\mathcal{F}_{T}(S)=S\mathbin{\operatorname*{\times}_{X}}T
inside
S
.

•

The
affine formal neighborhood of
T
inside
X
is the fiber functor
\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}^{\mathrm{aff}}
which sends
(S\to X)
to the affinized formal completion of
\mathcal{F}_{T}(S)
inside
S
. More concretely,

\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}^{\mathrm{aff}}(S/X)=\Spec\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Gamma(\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}(S/X),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}(S/X)})}}\right).

•

The
punctured formal neighborhood

\mathcal{F}_{\widehat{T}\smallsetminus T}
is the fiber functor defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\widehat{T}\smallsetminus T}(S/X)=\mathcal{F}_{{\widehat{T}}}^{\mathrm{aff}}(S/X)\smallsetminus\mathcal{F}_{T}(S/X).

Remark 2.2
.

One can construct a category of fiber functors over
X
, and prove that
\mathsf{St}_{X}

Now let

There is an equivalence

2.4.

The following theorem is the main result of this Section, and establishes the relation between the two dimensional quotient Grassmannians of Definition

Theorem 2.14

Let
G

k

X=\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}
,
D=\{y=0\}
, and

\underline{\mathbf{Gr}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1},0}

We prove the four claimed isomorphisms via different techniques. We divide the proof accordingly.

The geometric loop Grassmannian

Take an affine scheme

over
k

\underline{\mathbf{Bun}}_{{\widehat{Z}}^{\widehat{D}}\smallsetminus Z}(S)\simeq\mathsf{Bun}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{{\widehat{Z}}^{\widehat{D}}\smallsetminus Z}(S\times X/X)}}\right)

In particular,

Remark 2.15
.

If we assume that

Theorem

. If
\underline{\mathbf{Gr}}_{(D,Z)}
denotes
any
of the geometric Grassmannians of Definition
2.10
, we have a canonical isomorphism

\underline{\mathbf{Gr}}^{\sharp}_{(D,Z)}\simeq\prod^{n}_{i=1}\underline{\mathbf{Gr}}^{\sharp}_{(D,Z_{i})}.

Proof.

Instead of dwelling into a case by case proof, we only indicate the crucial common ingredient, leaving to the reader the elementary completion of the rest of the proof.

First of all it is clearly enough to prove that

(4.1)

\mathbf{Gr}_{(D,Z)}\simeq\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathbf{Gr}_{(D,Z_{i})}

as functors
(\mathsf{Aff}/X)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Grpds}
. Let then
(\mathrm{Spec}\,R\to X)\in\mathsf{Aff}/X
, and let
\mathrm{I}_{Z,R}
(respectively,
\mathrm{I}_{Z_{i},R}
,
i=1,\ldots n
) be the ideal in
R
corresponding to
\mathrm{Spec}\,R\times_{X}Z
(resp. to
\mathrm{Spec}\,R\times_{X}Z_{i}
). We then have
\mathrm{I}_{Z,R}=\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathrm{I}_{Z_{i},R}
, and since
\mathrm{V}(\mathrm{I}_{Z_{i},R})\cap\mathrm{V}(\mathrm{I}_{Z_{j},R})
for
i\neq j
, it is easy to deduce that

\mathrm{I}^{\alpha_{1}}_{Z_{1},R}+\ldots+\mathrm{I}^{\alpha_{n}}_{Z_{n},R}=R

for any sequence
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of integers
\geq 1
. As a further consequence, we get
\mathrm{I}_{Z,R}=\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathrm{I}_{Z_{i},R}=\cap^{n}_{i=1}\,\mathrm{I}_{Z_{i},R}
, and that
\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathrm{I}^{\alpha_{i}}_{Z_{i},R}=\cap^{n}_{i=1}\,\mathrm{I}^{\alpha_{i}}_{Z_{i},R}
for any sequence
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of integers
\geq 1
. These observations easily imply the existence of a canonical isomorphism (
4.1
).
∎

From Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.1
we immediately deduce our main result in this Section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be solvable, and
X
be a smooth quasi-projective surface over
k
, equipped with a flag
(D,Z)
where
D
is a smooth effective Cartier divisor in
X
, and
Z\subset D
consist of a finite number of closed
k
-points. Then the corresponding loop geometric Grassmannian, 2-dimensional local fields geometric Grassmannian, Jet geometric Grassmannian, and Big geometric Grassmannian (Definition
2.10
) are all
ind-representable
.

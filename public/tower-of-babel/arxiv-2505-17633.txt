Rigidity of Lie foliations with locally symmetric leaves
Gaël Meigniez and Hiraku Nozawa

Abstract
. We prove that if the leaves of a minimal Lie foliation are locally isometric to a symmetric space of non-compact type without a Poincaré disk factor, then the foliation is smoothly conjugate to a homogeneous Lie foliation up to finite covering. This result generalizes and strengthens Zimmer’s theorem, which characterizes minimal Lie foliations with leaves isometric to a symmetric space of non-compact type without real rank one factors as pullbacks of homogeneous foliations. As applications, we extend Zimmer’s arithmeticity theorem for holonomy groups and establish a rigidity theorem for Riemannian foliations with locally symmetric leaves.

1.
Introduction

A
Lie
foliation is a foliation transversely modelled on a
Lie group, called
structural
.
The interest towards Lie foliations mainly
comes from P. Molino’s theory,
[
Mo88
]

after which every Riemannian foliation is in
some sense a quotient of a
parametric family of Lie foliations.
Yet, little is known about the classification of Lie foliations themselves.
The first examples are the so-called
homogeneous
Lie foliations,
which are foliations on locally homogeneous spaces by locally homogeneous leaves.
The main questions deal with to what extent these examples are representative of the whole: see Ghys
[
Gh88
]
.
Examples abound of Lie foliations whose structural group
is solvable and that cannot be obtained as
pullbacks of homogeneous ones

[
Me95
,
Me97
]
.

A breakthrough was made by R. Zimmer
[
Zi88
]
. He brought methods of rigidity theory to study Lie foliations with symmetric leaves of non-compact type whose factors are of real rank at least two. By means of generalising Margulis’ superrigidity theorem to cocycles,
Zimmer showed that the holonomy groups of such foliations are arithmetic.
As pointed out by E. Ghys, Zimmer actually showed that such Lie foliations are necessarily pullbacks of homogeneous ones.

The present paper more generally studies the Lie foliations whose leaves are
locally
isometric to a symmetric space
X
of non-compact type
without Poincaré disk factor.

We put no restriction on the real rank of the factors, nor on the fundamental group of the leaves.
We apply the fundamental methods
and results of modern rigidity theory. Our main result is that these Lie foliations are
smoothly conjugate
to homogeneous ones up to finite coverings (Theorem
2.3
). As consequences, we generalise Zimmer’s arithmeticity theorem (Corollary
2.9
). Also, with the help of Molino’s structure theorem, we get a rigidity property for Riemannian foliations with locally symmetric leaves (Theorem
9.1
). In Section
10
, we propose several fundamental problems on Lie foliations that arise from
our techniques and results.

2.
Main result and corollaries

Let us first recall some classical notions, vocabulary
and elementary
facts, and give
some precisions.

Let
G
be a connected real Lie group. A

G
-
Lie foliation

\mathscr{F}
on a manifold
M

is defined by a maximal foliated atlas of
local coordinate charts

(\phi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}\times W_{i})_{i\in I},

where
U_{i}
,
V_{i}
,
W_{i}
are open subsets in
M
,
\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

G
, respectively; the coordinate changes being
smooth (
C^{\infty}
) diffeomorphisms of the form

\phi_{j}\circ\phi_{i}{{}^{-1}}:(x,g)\mapsto(f_{i,j}(x,g),\gamma_{i,j}g)

with
\gamma_{i,j}\in G
.
One calls
G
the
structural group
. Clearly, one
can assume
G
to be simply connected; however it is more
practical
for our sake not to make this assumption.

For any manifold
N
and any smooth map
g:N\to M
transverse to
\mathscr{F}
,
the pullback
g^{*}(\mathscr{F})

of
\mathscr{F}
is also a
G
-Lie foliation on
N
. We call such a pullback
faithful
if moreover the preimage of
every leaf of
\mathscr{F}
is connected, hence a single leaf of

g^{*}(\mathscr{F})
.

Such a foliation becomes a fibre bundle
over
G
when lifted to the universal
cover of the ambient manifold; precisely, E. Fedida
proved the following structure theorem.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Fe71
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold; denote by
\widetilde{M}

its universal cover. A foliation
\mathscr{F}
on

M
is a
G
-
Lie foliation
if and only if
one has

•

A surjective bundle map

\dev:\widetilde{M}\to G
whose fibres cover the leaves of
\mathscr{F}
;

•

A group homomorphism
\hol:\pi_{1}M\to G
such that for every
\gamma\in\pi_{1}M
and
x\in\widetilde{M}
:

\dev(\gamma x)=\hol(\gamma)\dev(x).

One calls
\dev
the
developing map;

\hol
the
holonomy representation;

and the image subgroup
\hol(\pi_{1}M)\subset G
,
the
holonomy group
of
\mathscr{F}
.

Clearly, for every
g\in G
one has :

\pi_{0}(\dev{{}^{-1}}(g))\cong\pi_{1}G.

Every connected component of the fibre
\dev{{}^{-1}}(g)

is the universal cover of a leaf of
\mathscr{F}
(since
\pi_{2}G=0
).

The codimension of the foliation

\mathscr{F}
is the dimension of the structural group.
The pair
(G,Hol(\pi_{1}M))
of the structural group
together with the holonomy subgroup characterizes the holonomy pseudogroup of
\mathscr{F}
up to local isomorphism in Haefliger’s sense
[
Ha84
]

(also known as Haefliger equivalence);
one can call this pair the
transverse structure
of the Lie
foliation
\mathscr{F}
. The transverse structure is invariant under every
faithful pullback.

Note that
\mathscr{F}
is minimal (in the
sense that every leaf is dense in
M
)
if and only if the holonomy group
\hol(\pi_{1}M)
is dense in
G
. We will usually assume that
\mathscr{F}
is minimal.
For Lie foliations,
this restriction is not a serious one: indeed, in
the general case, the closure of any leaf is a
compact submanifold in which the foliation is a minimal Lie foliation (see
[
Mo88
, Proposition 4.2, p. 116]
).

The leaves of
\mathscr{F}
have no holonomy in Reeb’s sense.
For any Riemannian metric on
M
, the leaves are two by two bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphic; so, there is an intrinsic quasi-isometry type, in Gromov’s sense, for the universal covers of the leaves of
\mathscr{F}
.

Example 2.2

(Homogeneous Lie foliations)
.

Consider a short exact sequence of Lie groups

1\to H\to L\to G\to 1

(
H
and
L
being not necessarily connected) together with
a uniform (cocompact) lattice
\Gamma\subset L
and a compact subgroup
K\subset H
, such that
L/K
is connected and that for every
\ell\in L
:

(2.1)

\Gamma\cap\ell K\ell{{}^{-1}}=\{1\}

(for example,
\Gamma\cap H
is torsion-free).

Then,
the left action of
\Gamma
on
L/K
is free and properly discontinuous.
Consider the symmetric space
X:=H/K
.
The product foliation on
L/K

parallel to
X
being
\Gamma
-left invariant,
one gets, on the locally homogeneous space
M:=\Gamma\backslash L/K
,
a
G
-Lie foliation
\mathscr{F}
, called
homogeneous
.
We call
L
the

total group.
Actually,
in this paper we shall only meet
the special case where
L
is the direct product
H\times G

(
direct product total group
).

Clearly:

•

The leaves of
\mathscr{F}
are locally isometric to
X
;

•

The holonomy group of
\mathscr{F}

is the projection of
\Gamma
in
G
;

•

If
X
is
connected and simply connected,
then the fundamental group of the leaves is
\Gamma\cap H
;

•

If there exists any
homogeneous
G
-Lie foliation with direct product total group, then
G
must be unimodular.

One denotes by
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{R}}
(resp.
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}
) the real (resp. complex)
hyperbolic space of real (resp. complex) dimension
d
. A diffeomorphism between two
Riemannian manifolds is called a
homothety
if the pullback of the target metric
is a constant times the source metric. Our main result is the following. Recall that
G
denotes an arbitrary connected real Lie group.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a product of nonflat irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact types,
without factor homothetic to
\mathbf{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
be a connected closed manifold with a minimal
G
-Lie foliation,
bearing a
C^{0}
Riemannian metric
whose restriction to every leaf is smooth and locally isometric to
X
.

Then,
(M,\mathscr{F})
is finitely covered by
a homogeneous
G
-Lie foliation whose total group is
\Isom(X)\times G
.

Theorem
2.3

will be proved in Sections
4
to
6
,
case by case.

Remark 2.4
.

If moreover
X
is irreducible, or more generally if
X
does not
admit
two homothetic irreducible factors,
then there is no use to pass to a finite cover:

(M,\mathscr{F})
is smoothly conjugate to
a homogeneous
G
-Lie foliation whose total group is
\Isom(X)\times G
.

Remark 2.5
.

Of course, the covering
in Theorem
2.3
is understood to be smooth;
however (as the proof will suggest), as
a price to pay for this smoothness, the covering
will in general
not be locally isometric with respect to
the two leafwise locally symmetric metrics. In the same way,
in Remark
2.4
, the smooth conjugation
will in general
not be isometric with respect to
the two leafwise locally symmetric metrics. See also
Pansu-Zimmer
[
PZ
]
.

The following quasi-isometric version will
follow by using theorems of Kleiner-Leeb
[
KL97
]
and Pansu
[
Pa89
]
(Section
7
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a product of nonflat irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact types,
without factor homothetic to
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{R}}
(
d\geq 1
)
nor
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}
(
d\geq 1
).

Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
be a connected closed manifold with a minimal
G
-Lie foliation
such that the universal cover of every leaf
is quasi-isometric to
X
.

Then,
(M,\mathscr{F})
is finitely covered by
a faithful
pullback of a homogeneous
G
-Lie foliation
whose total group is
\Isom(X)\times G
.

Neither Theorem
2.3
nor Theorem
2.6
would hold
if
X
were the Poincaré disk. Indeed, there exists (
[
HMM05
, Theorem 1.5]
)
a
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
-Lie foliation
\mathscr{F}
whose leaves are the
2
-sphere
S^{2}
minus a Cantor set,
and whose holonomy group
\Gamma\subset\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
is a cocompact irreducible lattice in

\PSL(2,\mathbb{Q}_{2})\times\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
. Hence, for every short exact sequence
of
real
Lie groups as in Example
2.2
with
G=\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
,
the group
\Gamma
cannot be the image in
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})

of any cocompact lattice in

L
, by the Margulis superrigidity
theorem — or if one likes better, by an argument of cohomological dimension.
The same holds for every finite-index subgroup in
\Gamma
.
In other words,
\Gamma
cannot be, even virtually, the holonomy group of any
homogeneous
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
-Lie foliation.
On the other hand,
after Candel’s uniformisation theorem
[
Ca93
]
,
\mathscr{F}
admits a

C^{0}
Riemannian metric
whose restriction to every leaf is smooth of curvature
-1
.

Before stating some more corollaries of Theorem
2.3
,
let us first recall a second important family of
Lie foliations.

Example 2.7

(Suspension Lie foliations)
.

Given a
compact
connected Lie group
G
, a closed connected manifold
V
and a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}V\to G
, its suspension
\mathscr{F}
on
M:=V\times_{\rho}G
is a
G
-Lie foliation, which is minimal if
\rho(\pi_{1}V)
is dense in
G
. If moreover
V
is a locally homogeneous manifold, then
\mathscr{F}
is also a homogeneous Lie foliation.

Once we know that the given foliation is
homogeneous up to a finite covering,
we can apply the theory of lattices to study the global structure.
Here are some consequences that generalise the results of Zimmer.
The first one is a refinement of Theorem
2.3
for the case where
X
is irreducible:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
X
be an
irreducible
symmetric space of non-compact type of
real dimension
n\geq 3
.
Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
be a minimal Lie foliation on a closed manifold. Assume that
\mathscr{F}

bears a
C^{0}
metric whose restriction to every leaf is
smooth and locally isometric to
X
. Then,
(M,\mathscr{F})

is finitely covered by either a homogeneous Lie foliation
(Example
2.2
) with irreducible
\Gamma
,
or a suspention Lie foliation (Example
2.7
)
whose fibre is a compact quotient group of
G
.

When the model symmetric space
X
is reducible, the foliation can be somehow a composite
of the homogeneous case with the suspension case:
a suspension foliation whose fibres are homogeneous Lie foliation given by an irreducible lattice (see Proposition
8.1
below).

By Theorem
2.3
and the
Molino structure theory for Riemannian foliations, we obtain a rigidity result for minimal Riemannian foliations with locally symmetric leaves (see Theorem
9.1
).

The next corollary
is a generalisation of Zimmer’s arithmeticity theorem of holonomy groups (
[
Zi88
, Theorem A-(3)]
).

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
X,M,\mathscr{F}
be as in Theorem
2.3
.

Then, the adjoint
\Ad_{G}(\hol(\pi_{1}M))
of the holonomy group of
(M,\mathscr{F})
is arithmetic in
\Ad_{G}(G)
.

Zimmer proved this statement in the case where the leaves of
\mathscr{F}
are simply connected and
where each irreducible factor of
X
is of real rank at least 2. He mentioned that his arithmeticity theorem may also
hold true for the cases
where the leaves are not simply connected. Our Corollary
2.9
covers these cases. Quiroga-Barranco
[
Qu08
]
proved the arithmeticity for Lie foliations with totally geodesic leaves.

Finally, our Theorem
2.3
together with Johnson’s theorem
[
Jo84
]

give the following generalisation of
[
Zi88
, Theorems A-(5) and B]
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
and
X
be as in Theorem
2.3
. Let
X=\prod_{i}X_{i}
be the decomposition of
X
into irreducible symmetric spaces. Unless
(M,\mathscr{F})
is finitely covered by a suspension Lie foliation,
we have
\codim(\mathscr{F})\geq d(X)
, where

d(X)=\min_{i}\dim(\Isom(X_{i})).

Note that suspension foliations should be excluded from the original result
[
Zi88
, Theorem A-(5)]
(see Example
8.3
).

The structure of the article is as follows. In Section
3
, we introduce a quasi-action of the structural group on the universal cover of a leaf, which is the basic tool in this paper. Sections
4
to
6
are devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.3
. In Section
7
, we prove Theorem
2.6
. The subsequent two sections present structure theorems for homogeneous Lie foliations, which are used to derive corollaries from Theorem
2.3
and to apply the results to Riemannian foliations. We state several problems in the last section.

3.
Quasi-action of the structural group

Here, one quickly recalls some classical material about Lie foliations, without any hypothesis
on the geometry of the leaves; one fixes some notations; and one introduces what
can be called
a right
quasi-action
of the structural group
G
on the universal cover of the
foliated manifold,
lifting the action of
G
on itself by right translations.

We are given a connected Lie group
G
, a closed connected manifold
M
, and on
M
a minimal
G
-Lie foliation
\mathscr{F}
.

Fix on
G
a left-invariant Riemannian metric
\lambda
. Fix on
M
a smooth Riemannian metric
\rho
which coincides with
\lambda
on the plane field
\rho
-orthogonal to
\mathscr{F}
in
M
(
bundle-like metric
, after
[
Re59
]
).
Endow
\widetilde{M}
with the lift
\tilde{\rho}
of
\rho
. For
every
h\in G
, we denote by
\tilde{\rho}_{h}
the
intrinsic Riemannian

metric induced by
\tilde{\rho}
on the fibre
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
.
At every
x\in\widetilde{M}
, the differential of
\dev
restricted to the subspace
\tilde{\rho}
-orthogonal to the fibre through
x
, is a linear isometry from this subspace onto
\tau_{\dev(x)}G
(
Riemannian submersion
).

For every
g\in G
, fix any shortest
\lambda
-geodesic segment
[e_{G},g]
from the neutral element
e_{G}
to
g
in
G
. For every
x\in\widetilde{M}
, the left-translated geodesic segment
\dev(x)[e_{G},g]
lifts uniquely through
\dev
as a path
[x,y]
in
\widetilde{M}
which starts at
x
and is

\tilde{\rho}
-orthogonal to the fibres. Define

\Phi(g)(x)=y\in\dev{{}^{-1}}(\dev(x)g)

as the endpoint
of the lifted path.

In this paper, given a metric
s
on a space
S
, we write
|x-y|_{s}
for the
s
-distance
between two points
x,y\in S
; and given two maps
f,g:S^{\prime}\to S
, we write
\|f-g\|_{s}

for the supremum of the distances
|f(x)-g(x)|_{s}
, where
x\in S^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
g,g^{\prime},h\in G
and
x\in\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
and
\gamma\in\pi_{1}M
:

(i)

|\Phi(g)(x)-x|_{\tilde{\rho}}=|g-e_{G}|_{\lambda}
;

(ii)

\Phi(g)(\gamma\cdot x)=\gamma\cdot\Phi(g)(x)
;

(iii)

\Phi(g)
is a bi-Lipschitz self-diffeomorphism of
\widetilde{M}

with respect to the metric
\tilde{\rho}
; and
the Lipschitz ratios of
\Phi(g)
and of
\Phi(g){{}^{-1}}
on
\widetilde{M}

with respect to
\tilde{\rho}
, seen as two real functions
of
g\in G
, are bounded on every compact subset of
G
;

(iv)

\Phi(g)\to\id
,
uniformly on
\widetilde{M}

in the
C^{1}
topology,
as
g\to e_{G}
in
G
;

(v)

|\Phi(gh)(x)-\Phi(h)(\Phi(g)(x))|_{\tilde{\rho}_{\dev(x)gh}}

has a bound depending on
g,h\in G
but not
on
x\in\widetilde{M}
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) are obvious by construction.
(iii) and (iv) follow from (ii),
from the cocompacity of the action of
\pi_{1}M
on
\widetilde{M}
,
and from the elementary properties of the geodesics of the complete Riemannian metric

\lambda
. As for (v):
the considered distance is continuous with respect to
x\in\widetilde{M}
(after (iii))
and
\pi_{1}M
-invariant (after (ii)), hence bounded.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The diffeomorphism
\Phi(g)
depends on the choice of shortest geodesic segments from
e_{G}
to

, but this choice will be irrelevant in the sequel.

Remark 3.3
.

In general, the distribution
\tilde{\rho}
-orthogonal to the fibres being not integrable,
\Phi(gg^{\prime})
is
not
the composite
\Phi(g^{\prime})\circ\Phi(g)
, even for
g,g^{\prime}
close to
e_{G}
: for the moment, we have no genuine right
G
-action on
\widetilde{M}
.

Remark 3.4
.

There is an alternative construction of the quasi-action
\Phi
, which would also work for our proof. Every
g\in G
decomposes as
\exp(v_{1})\cdot\cdots\cdot\exp(v_{m})
, where each
v_{i}
is some left-invariant vector field. Let
\tilde{v}_{i}
be the lift of
v_{i}
to
\widetilde{M}
which is orthogonal to the fibres of
\dev
. One defines
\Phi(g):=\tilde{v}_{m}^{1}\circ\cdots\circ\tilde{v}_{1}^{1}
, where
\tilde{v}_{i}^{1}
is the time one map of the flow generated by

.

It is convenient, for every

, to introduce the restriction

\Phi_{h}^{k}=\Phi(h{{}^{-1}}k)|_{\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)}:\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)\to\dev{{}^{-1}}(k)

4.
Rigidity of Lie foliations with hyperbolic leaves

Let us prove Theorem
2.3
in the case of real rank one.
Let
X
be an irreducible symmetric space of non-compact type of real rank one, and of real dimension
n\geq 3
.
In other words, by Cartan’s classification,
X
is one of the following hyperbolic spaces:
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(with
n\geq 3
),
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
(with
d\geq 2
),
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
(with
d\geq 1
) or
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{O}}^{2}
.

One is given a connected Lie group
G
which
one may assume without loss of generality to be simply connected;

M
,
\mathscr{F}
,
\lambda
as in Section
3
; and
one assumes that
\mathscr{F}
bears a

C^{0}

\sigma

whose restriction to every leaf is

.
Put

. Let
\eta

be the volume of the metric
\lambda
,
hence a left-invariant Haar measure on
G
.

We shall show that
\pi_{1}M
embeds as a uniform lattice
\Gamma
in
H\times G
, and that
\mathscr{F}
is smoothly conjugate to the homogeneous foliation on
M_{0}:=\Gamma\backslash(X\times G)
whose leaves are parallel to

.

Consider on every fibre
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)

the symmetric Riemannian metric
\tilde{\sigma}_{h}
which is the lift of
\sigma
.
Since
\sigma
is globally continuous,

\tilde{\sigma}_{h}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{h}
are
equivalent, and the ratios of their norms have positive bounds not depending
on

.

Both homotopy groups
\pi_{1}G
and
\pi_{2}G

being trivial,
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
is simply connected, hence isometric with
X
for
\tilde{\sigma}_{h}
.
As such, this fibre has an ideal boundary at infinity
\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
,
homeomorphic to the
(n-1)
-sphere.
Let
\partial\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}
be the disjoint union of the
boundaries
\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
, for
h\in G
.
Every element
\gamma\in\pi_{1}M
,
mapping isometrically
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
onto

\dev{{}^{-1}}(\hol(\gamma)h)

therefore extends
to a homeomorphism of
\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
onto

\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(\hol(\gamma)h)

One thus gets a global
left action of
\pi_{1}M
on the set
\partial\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}
.

For every
h,k\in G
, the two metrics

\tilde{\sigma}_{h}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{h}
being equivalent on
the fibre
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
,
as well as
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{k}
on
the fibre
\dev{{}^{-1}}(k)
,
after Lemma
3.1
(iii), the
diffeomorphism

\Phi_{h}^{k}
is bi-Lipschitz with respect to
\tilde{\sigma}_{h}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}
,
hence extends to a homeomorphism
between the ideal boundaries at infinity:

\partial\Phi_{h}^{k}:\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)\to\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(k).

Globally, define for
g\in G
the bijection
\partial\Phi(g)

of
\partial\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}
such that for every
h\in G
and
\xi\in\partial\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
:

\partial\Phi(g)(\xi)=\partial\Phi_{h}^{hg}(\xi).

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
g,g^{\prime}\in G
,
\gamma\in\pi_{1}M
,
\xi\in\partial\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}

(i)

5.
Rigidity of Lie foliations with leaves locally isometric to an irreducible symmetric space of higher rank

Let us prove Theorem
2.3
in the irreducible cases of rank
\geq 2
.
Let as before

be a simply
connected Lie group, and
(M,\mathscr{F})
be a closed connected manifold with a minimal

-Lie foliation. Assume that
\mathscr{F}
admits a

metric whose restriction to every leaf is locally isometric to an irreducible symmetric space

of non-compact type of real rank at least

.
In that case, the proof of Theorem
2.3
follows the same general lines
as it did in the hyperbolic case, the main difference being that the construction
of the right action
\Psi
and the proof of its continuity
now fall to the Kleiner-Leeb rigidity theorem
instead of the barycentre method. Here are a few precisions; the other details
are left to the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

There is a continuous
right action of the Lie group
G

on the manifold
\widetilde{M}
, denoted
(x,g)\mapsto\Psi(g)(x)
,
commuting with the left action of
\pi_{1}(M)
, and such that

\Psi(g)
sends isometrically
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
onto
\dev{{}^{-1}}(hg)
, for every

.

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
3.1
(iii) that
\Phi_{h}^{hg}
is a bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphism between the fibres
\dev{{}^{-1}}(h)
and
\dev{{}^{-1}}(hg)
, whose
Lipschitz ratio is uniformly bounded with respect to
g
varying in any fixed compact subset of
G
. Both fibres being Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type of real rank
\geq 2
, by
[
KL97
, Theorem 1.1.3]
and the fact that any homothety of
X
is an isometry (see, e.g.,
[
Oc69
]
), there exists a unique isometry

\Psi_{h}^{hg}:\dev^{-1}(h)\to\dev^{-1}(hg)

at finite distance from
\Phi_{h}^{hg}
; and this distance is bounded uniformly
with respect to
g
varying in any fixed compact subset of
G
. Define

\Psi(g)
as the self-mapping of
\widetilde{M}
whose restriction to each fibre

\dev^{-1}(h)
is
\Psi_{h}^{hg}
.

Since, in restriction to each fibre, both
\Psi(h)\Psi(g)
and
\Psi(gh)
are isometries
at finite distance from
\Phi(gh)
,
one has
\Psi(gh)=\Psi(h)\Psi(g)

by the uniqueness of such an isometry. For the same reason,
\Psi(g)\Psi(g^{-1})
is the identity, and
\Psi(g)
commutes with the left action of
\pi_{1}M
on
\widetilde{M}
.

The continuity of
\Psi(g)(x)
with respect to
(x,g)\in\widetilde{M}\times G

follows from the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
,
the differences being that
\Psi(g)
is already defined all over
\widetilde{M}
,
and that the Kleiner-Leeb theorem now assumes the role that the Morse-Schuhr lemma played
in the hyperbolic case.
∎

The rest of the proof is the same as in the case where the leaves were
of real rank one, in the last section:
one constructs a right
G
-action, a homogeneous Lie foliation
(M_{0},\mathscr{F}_{0})
and a homeomorphism
(M,\mathscr{F})\to(M_{0},\mathscr{F}_{0})
of class

, which can be approximated by a diffeomorphism
(M,\mathscr{F})\to(M_{0},\mathscr{F}_{0})
. Theorem
2.3
is proved in the case
where the leaves are irreducible and of real rank at least

.

6.

Let us prove Theorem

Preliminaries — It is convenient, to fix ideas,

We can assume that

By a

We prove the following structure result for homogeneous Lie foliations with locally symmetric leaves, which says that a finite covering of the foliation is obtained by certain suspension construction. The argument is similar to Zimmer’s in

G

H

has exactly two connected components
H_{\pm}\cong\SO_{\pm}(2,2)
.
By Selberg’s lemma
[
Se60
]
, there exists in
\Gamma\cap H_{+}
a torsion free,
finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Put
V:=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash H/K
, where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
H
.
Suspending the quotient map
\pi_{1}V\to\Gamma^{\prime}
composed with
\sigma
, one gets a
homogeneous
G
-Lie foliation whose leaves are locally isometric to
H/K
.

Let us modify this example to have fibres of smaller dimension. Let
h:\Gamma^{\prime}\to\SO(3)
be the composite

\Gamma^{\prime}\to\SO(4)\to\SO(4)/\{\pm I\}\to\SO(3)\times\SO(3)\to\SO(3)

where the second map is the canonical projection, the third map is the accidental double cover, and the fourth map is the first projection. The discrete subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
being Zariski dense in
H
,
the centre of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is
contained in the centre of
H
, and consequently, finite.
Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
is torsion-free, it has no centre.
By Margulis’ normal subgroup theorem
[
Ma91
]
,
h
is injective.
We get, by means of
h
, a suspension
\SO(3)
-Lie foliation
\mathscr{F}
, whose leaves
are simply-connected (
h
being injective). Every leaf is thus isometric to

\SO(2,2)/\Sr(\Or(2)\times\Or(2))

On the other hand,

\codim\mathscr{F}=\dim\SO(3)=3<6=\dim\SO(2,2).

Hence, this example does not satisfy the conclusion of
[
Zi88
, Theorem A-(5)]
.

The following is an example of homogeneous suspension Lie foliations whose fibers are not semisimple Lie groups.

Example 8.4
.

Another type of homogeneous suspension
G
-Lie foliation, where
G=S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
,
can be obtained from any closed hyperbolic
n
-manifold
V
(
n\geq 2
)
whose first Betti number is at least
2
.
The group
\pi_{1}V
is a uniform lattice in
\Isom(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. One
chooses a De Rham cohomology class

\omega\in H^{1}(V;\mathbb{R})
not proportional to any
integral class, and one regards
\omega

as a morphism
\pi_{1}V\to\mathbb{R}
. After a rescaling, the image of
\omega
in
\mathbb{R}

contains
\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
\omega
induces a morphism
\rho:\pi_{1}V\to S^{1}

whose image is dense in
S^{1}
.

9.
Application to Riemannian foliations

Finally, we apply the rigidity on Lie foliations, Theorem
2.3
, to show a rigidity result on Riemannian foliations. Recall that, by Molino’s theory
[
Mo88
]
, every minimal Riemannian foliation
(M,\mathscr{F})
is obtained as the quotient of a minimal
G
-Lie foliation
(M^{1},\mathscr{F}^{1})
of the same dimension by a free action of a compact Lie group
S
, where
G
is called the
structural group
of
(M,\mathscr{F})
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
be a connected closed manifold with a minimal Riemannian foliation. Let
G
be the structural group of
(M,\mathscr{F})
. Assume that
M
admits a Riemannian metric of class
C^{0}
whose restriction to every leaf of
\mathscr{F}
is smooth and locally isometric with a symmetric space
X
of non-compact type with no Poincaré disk factor. Then, there exist a compact Lie subgroup
S
of
G
and a uniform lattice
\Gamma
of
\Isom(X)\times G
such that a finite covering of
(M,\mathscr{F})
is smoothly conjugate to
(\Gamma\backslash(X\times(G/S)),\mathscr{F}_{0})
, where
\mathscr{F}_{0}
is the foliation on
\Gamma\backslash(X\times(G/S))
whose lift to
X\times(G/S)
is
\sqcup_{g\in G}X\times gS
.

Let us outline the proof.
Consider the principal
S
-bundle
\pi:(M^{1},\mathscr{F}^{1})\to(M,\mathscr{F})
obtained by Molino’s theory, where
(M^{1},\mathscr{F}^{1})
is a minimal Lie foliation and
S
is a compact Lie group. By taking a finite covering of
(M,\mathscr{F})
, we can assume that
S
is connected. Each leaf of
\mathscr{F}^{1}
covers a leaf of
\mathscr{F}
by the restriction of
\pi
. Therefore, if
(M,\mathscr{F})
admits a leafwise locally symmetric Riemannian metric, then
(M^{1},\mathscr{F}^{1})
admits such leafwise metric that is invariant under the principal
S
-action. Thus Theorem
9.1
follows from Theorem
2.3
and the following description of the leafwise isometry groups of homogeneous Lie foliations.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let us use the notation of Example
2.2
. Let
(M,\mathscr{F})
be the homogeneous Lie foliation considered there, namely,
M=\Gamma\backslash(X\times G)
and
\mathscr{F}
is the foliation on
M
whose lift to
X\times G
is
\sqcup_{g\in G}X\times\{g\}
. Assume that
\mathscr{F}
is minimal and
G
is simply-connected. Let
\Isom(M,\mathscr{F})
be the group of diffeomorphisms which map each leaf of
\mathscr{F}
to a leaf of
\mathscr{F}
preserving the leafwise metric. Its identity component
\Isom_{0}(M,\mathscr{F})
is given by

(9.1)

\Isom_{0}(M,\mathscr{F})=(G\cap\Gamma)\backslash G,

where
G\cap\Gamma
is normal in
G
and
g_{1}\in(G\cap\Gamma)\backslash G
acts on
M
by
g_{1}\cdot(\Gamma(x,g))=\Gamma(x,gg^{-1}_{1})
.

Proof.

Only the inclusion
\Isom_{0}(M,\mathscr{F})\subset(G\cap\Gamma)\backslash G
may not seem immediate. In order to verify it, recall that, since
(M,\mathscr{F})
is a minimal Lie foliation,
the differential map of any
f\in\Isom(M,\mathscr{F})
induces an automorphism
of the Lie algebra of
the
transverse

vector fields on
(M,\mathscr{F})
(in the sense of Molino
[
Mo88
]
), which is
\Lie(G)
.
In view of this fact, it is not difficult to see that, for any lift
\tilde{f}\in\Diff(X\times G)
of
f
, there exist
h_{1}\in\Isom(X)
,
g_{1}\in G
and
\alpha\in\Aut(G)
such that

\widetilde{f}(x,g)=(h_{1}x,\alpha(g)g_{1}),\quad\quad\forall x\in X,\forall g\in G

(see Haefliger
[
Ha84
]
, see also
[
No10
, Proposition 6]
). The inclusion easily follow.
∎

10.
Problems

With this work, we hope to exemplify the use, for Lie foliations, of rigidity theory; and also to open and motivate some fundamental problems in the classification of Lie foliations.
(In these problems, every Lie foliation is
understood to be a minimal
G
-Lie foliation on a closed manifold; and
X

is understood to be a product of irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces of non-compact type).

Problem 1
.

Classify Lie foliations of leafwise dimension two. In particular, if the leaves
of a Lie foliation are
isometric to the Poincaré disk,
is this foliation necessarily homogeneous?

This last question, of particular interest for the theory of Riemannian
foliations, is open
even for
G=\PSL(2;\mathbb{R})
.

Remarkably few is known in the direction of Problem
1
. Matsumoto-Tsuchiya
proved that for
G=\GA(1,\mathbb{R})
, the real
2
-dimensional nonabelian
solvable group, every
G
-Lie foliation (minimal or not)
on a closed
4
-manifold is homogeneous
[
MT92
]
. The already cited example built in
[
HMM05
]
, where

G=\PSL(2;\mathbb{R})
and the leaves are the
2
-sphere minus a Cantor set,
is not homogeneous; the holonomy group
\Gamma
is not a cocompact lattice in any real Lie group; but there
is nevertheless a kind of rigidity:
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in the
product of
G
with the diadic Lie group
\PSL(2;\mathbb{Q}_{2})
.

Problem 2
.

Assume that
X
has no factor
\mathbf{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
that
\mathscr{F}
is a Lie foliation in which the
universal cover of every leaf
is
bi-Lipschitz diffeomorphic
to
X
. Does it follow that
\mathscr{F}
is homogeneous?

Problem 3
.

Would Theorem
2.6
still hold if one allowed

X
to have some
factors
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{R}}
(
d\geq 3
) or
\mathbf{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}
(
d\geq 2
)?

Problem 4
.

Which
3
-manifolds can be realized as the leaf of a Lie foliation?

Schweitzer-Souza
[
SS17
]
found a necessary condition for
a non-compact Riemannian manifold to be a leaf of a Riemannian foliation on a closed manifold.

Problem 5
.

Is there a geometrisation theorem for Lie foliations of dimension
3
?

Such a foliated version of Perelman’s geometrisation theorem would split every
3
-dimensional Lie foliation, along some transverse closed hypersurfaces,
into pieces, in each of which the leaves
would admit one of Thurston’s eight geometries.

Problem 6
.

Classify
3
-dimensional Lie foliations whose leaves admit one of Thurston’s eight geometries.

The present paper solves the hyperbolic case. This problem enters the more general program, proposed by Paul Schweitzer,
to study
3
-dimensional foliations with geometric leaves. For example, it is not known if there is a non-homogeneous Lie foliation
\mathscr{F}
whose leaves are isometric to the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group
\operatorname{Sol}
. (Recall that after Carrière
[
Ca88
]
,
G
would necessarily also be solvable).

Ghys
[
Gh88
]
pointed out that an equicontinuous foliated space is a topological version of Riemannian foliations. The Molino theory for such spaces was estalished by the works of
Alvarez-López, Barral-Lijó, Candel, Clark, Dyer,
Hurder, Lukina, Moreira Galicia

[
Alv09
,
Alv16
,
Alv19
,
Dy17
]
.
A foliated space can be transversely modelled on the Cantor space, and it can be regarded as a model of minimal sets of foliations.

Problem 7
.

Study the rigidity of equicontinuous foliated spaces with locally symmetric leaves.

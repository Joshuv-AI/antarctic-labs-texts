Asymptotics for conformal inference
Ulysse Gazin

Abstract
Conformal inference is a versatile tool for building prediction sets in regression or classification. We study the false coverage proportion (FCP) in a simultaneous inference setting with a calibration sample of n n points and a test sample of m m points. We identify the exact, distribution-free, asymptotic distribution of the FCP when both n n and m m tend to infinity. This shows in particular that FCP control can be achieved by using the well-known Kolmogorov distribution, and puts forward that the asymptotic variance is decreasing in the ratio n / m n/m . We then provide a number of extensions by considering the problems of novelty detection, weighted conformal inference or distribution shift between the calibration sample and the test sample. In particular, our asymptotic results allow to accurately quantify the asymptotic behavior of the errors (a miscovering interval or declaring a false novelty) when weighted conformal inference is used.

Introduction

1.1
Background

In classical statistics, producing prediction sets for outcomes often relies on strong model assumptions. Recent advances involve complex data sets and sophisticated machine learning methods, for which such an approach is not appropriate. One recent solution is given by conformal prediction
(
Saunders, Gammerman and
Vovk, 1999
;
Vovk, Gammerman and
Shafer, 2005
;
Angelopoulos and
Bates, 2021
)
which consists in calibrating the prediction set according to an appropriate quantile of a calibration/training sample.
Strikingly, this method provides a finite-sample valid coverage (i.e. valid for any size
n\geqslant 1
of the calibration sample) for any underlying distribution of the data and for any underlying point-prediction machine learning algorithm. Similar techniques can be employed for the novelty detection task
(
Balasubramanian, Ho and
Vovk, 2014
;
Bates et al., 2023
;
Marandon et al., 2024
)
.

1.2
Aim and contributions

We consider here the
multiple
setting
(
Vovk, 2013
)
, where it is given
a training dataset (which is considered as fixed here), a calibration sample of
n
points
(X_{1},Y_{1})
,
\dots
,
(X_{n},Y_{n})
and a test sample of
m
points
(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1}),

\dots
,
(X_{n+m},Y_{n+m})
. While the calibration sample is fully observed, the
Y_{i}
’s of the test points are not observed and a prediction set should be provided for each of them. The false coverage proportion (FCP) for the
m
conformal prediction sets
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1}),\dots,\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+m})
(see below for a formal definition) is defined as the proportion of coverage errors among the test sample:

\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{Y_{n+i}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+i})}}.

Under standard assumptions
and when the conformal prediction sets come from the inductive/split conformal procedure the distribution of the process
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
has been shown to be distribution-free, in the sense that it does only depend on
n
and
m

(
Marques F., 2025
;
Huang et al., 2024
;
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain, 2024
)
.
Due to the dependence between the individual coverage errors, combinatorial formulas derived in those references are particulary complex in
m
and
n
.
Nevertheless, focusing on the maximum absolute deviation

\|\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}-I_{n}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\alpha\in[0,1]}|\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)-I_{n}(\alpha)|,

(1)

with
I_{n}(\alpha)=\lfloor(n+1)\alpha\rfloor/(n+1)
, a DKW-type concentration inequality has been derived in
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain (2024)
, which is both simple and finite-sample valid. It explicitly involves a rate
\tau_{n,m}^{1/2}
with

\tau_{n,m}\coloneqq\frac{nm}{n+m}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{n\wedge m}{2},n\wedge m}}\right].

(2)

However, this DKW inequality is conservative in general (see Figure
1
below), which makes the corresponding FCP control stringent.

The aim of this paper is to complement the above studies by analyzing
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
from an asymptotic point of view, where
both

m
and
n
tend to infinity
which is equivalent to the convergence of
\tau_{n,m}
to infinity. This asymptotic study will also cover the case of a distribution shift i.e. when the exchangeability assumption does not hold thus extending the analysis of
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain (2024)
and more broadlythe usual framework of conformal inference. We also study, in the same asymptotic regime, the asymptotic of the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure when conformal
p
-values are used
(
Bates et al., 2023
)
, which complement the non-asymptotic work of
Bates et al. (2023)
;
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain (2024)
among others. We refer to Section
1.3
for more details on the relation of this work with previous studies.
More precisely, our contributions are the following ones:

1.

We show that under standard assumptions
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
converges uniformly to the nominal value at rate
\tau_{n,m}^{1/2}
and that the asymptotic covariance process is a standard Brownian bridge (Theorem
3.1
). Compared to the “oracle” case where
n=\infty
, it means that the variance is inflated by a factor asymptotically equivalent to
(n+m)/n
, for instance
2
in the case where
n\sim m
.

2.

A direct corollary of this result is that
\tau_{n,m}^{1/2}\|\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}-I_{n}\|_{\infty}
converges to the well known Kolmogorov distribution, that is, the distribution with c.d.f.
x\mapsto(1-2\sum_{k\geqslant 1}(-1)^{k}e^{-2k^{2}x^{2}}){\mathds{1}_{x\geqslant 0}}
. A comparison between the quantiles of this distribution, those given by empirical simulations of
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
or those from the Conformal-DKW is provided in Figure
1
. While its validity is only asymptotic with
n,m\to+\infty
, it appears that the Kolmogorov quantile is simple and more accurate than the quantile obtained from DKW. Hence, our limit theorem allows to get simple, accurate and asymptotically-valid confidence bounds for the FCP process as well as asymptotic approximations of all quantities related to the distribution of the
\mathrm{FCP}
process.

3.

We then extend this result to the case where the distribution of the calibration sample is not equal to the test sample, that is, under a
distribution shift
(Theorem
3.2
). As expected, the convergence of the FCP is not towards the nominal level in this case but rather towards a new term
G
that takes this shift into account.
The asymptotic covariance process is also modified according to
G
and an explicit formula is given. The role of the ratio
n/(n+m)
(number of calibration points divided by the total number of points) clearly appears in the limit and indicates the existence of three asymptotic regimes: under, proportional and over calibrated, see Table
1
.

4.

To recover the appropriate nominal level in the limit, we adopt the weighted conformal approach
(
Tibshirani
et al., 2019
;
Barber et al., 2023
)
with specific weights that rely on the data distribution, that we refer to as
oracle weights
. The obtaned central limit theorem shares similarities with the exchangeable case described above, with the essential difference that the asymptotic covariance process is not distribution free, and depends on the sample distributions (Theorem
3.4
).

5.

We also obtain a convergence result in case of non-oracle weights (Theorem
3.3
), which is crucial to quantify the FCP asymptotic behavior in the difficult but realistic case where the user has not access to the true distribution shift. An illustration is displayed in Figure
2
, where the asymptotic confidence interval for the FCP is given as a function of an error
\Delta
, measuring how the used weights deviate from the oracle ones. This puts forward that the FCP gets significantly away from
\alpha
when
\Delta
is above
\approx 0.133
or below
\approx-0.11
in this framework.

6.

Finally, we obtain similar results for the novelty detection task, by studying the asymptotic behavior of the false discovery proportion (FDP) of classical procedures
(
Bates et al., 2023
;
Jin and Candès, 2023
)
(Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
).

The proofs are based on specific decompositions of the processes that can be found in Section
5
, while further details are postponed to appendices, notably Section
G
with a presentation of the main results of the paper under relaxed assumptions.

1.3
Relation to previous work

Conformal prediction is a general pipeline and we refer the reader to
Vovk, Gammerman and
Shafer (2005)
or
Angelopoulos and
Bates (2021)
for reviews. We focus here on the inductive/split conformal inference
(
Papadopoulos
et al., 2002
)
, where an independent training sample is used to build the predictors, while a calibration sample is used to adjust the prediction sets. All our results can be thought of holding conditionally on the training sample, that is, they hold once the point-predictions have been computed.

The process
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
coincides with the empirical cumulative distribution function (e.c.d.f.) of conformal
p
-values as introduced by
Saunders, Gammerman and
Vovk (1999)
. As recalled above, for a given nominal level
\alpha
, the non-asymptotic distribution of
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)
has been obtained in
Marques F. (2025)
;
Huang et al. (2024)
and the full distribution of the process
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
has been given in
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain (2024)
. The main difference with the current study is that they provide non-asymptotic properties under the exchangeable setting while we provide asymptotic properties under the exchangeable and the non-exchangeable frameworks.
In addition,
Marques F. (2025)
gives an asymptotic result for
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
fixed in the regime where
n
tends to infinity only after making
m
tend to infinity, whereas here we make
n
and
m
grow to infinity independently
leading to a double asymptotic. Furthermore all our results are uniform in
\alpha
.

Next, the asymptotic distribution of
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}
with
n
fixed and
m\rightarrow+\infty
is also studied with a PAC point of view since this limit is equal to the miscoverage probability given the calibration sample
\mathbb{P}[Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n}+1)|(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}]
:
Vovk (2012)
derives Hoeffding-like tail bounds for a fixed
\alpha
, and other bounds was derived by
Bian and Barber (2022)
;
Sarkar and Kuchibhotla (2023)
the latter being uniform in
\alpha
.
Angelopoulos and
Bates (2021)
states that
\mathbb{P}[Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n}+1)|(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}]
follows a Beta distribution, which is exactly the limit identified by
Marques F. (2025)
. Similarly,
Nguyen et al. (2024)
obtained asymptotic results for the error as
m
tends to infinity while
n
is kept fixed but with more general risk function and not necessary the
0-1
loss function. In the novelty detection setting,
Bates et al. (2023)
also provided bounds for the type I error given the calibration sample uniformly in
\alpha
.

Finally, we study here novelty detection procedures obtained by applying the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (
\mathrm{BH}
,
Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995
) to the conformal
p
-values
(
Mary and
Roquain, 2022
;
Bates et al., 2023
;
Marandon et al., 2024
)
or to the weighted conformal
p
-values
(
Tibshirani
et al., 2019
;
Barber et al., 2023
;
Jin and Candès, 2023
)
. Asymptotics for the FDP of such procedures have been extensively studied in the literature, see
Genovese and Wasserman (2002)
;
Genovese and Wasserman (2004)
;
Neuvial (2008)
for i.i.d. uniform
p
-values and
Delattre and Roquain (2011)
;
Delattre and Roquain (2016)
;
Kluger and Owen (2024)
for several types of dependence structures.
The present work is in this line of research by seeing (weighted) conformal
p
-values as a particular case of dependent
p
-values having a specific dependency structure induced by the common calibration sample. In particular, our work shows that the ratio
n/(n+m)
measures the dependency strength in the double asymptotic regime where both
n
(calibration sample size) and
m
(the number of test) tend to infinity.
In the novelty detection setting, we derive our results by using the techniques introduced in
Neuvial (2008)
, that combine functional central limit theorems with the functional delta method. In particular, we provide the full asymptotic FDP distribution for the procedure of
Jin and Candès (2023)
(or a variant thereof), for which only in-expectation results were known.

Preliminaries

2.1
Prediction setting

We consider the classical inductive/split conformal prediction
(
Papadopoulos
et al., 2002
)
.
A calibration set
\{Z_{k},k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket\}
is observed with
Z_{k}=(X_{k},Y_{k})
and, given a new point
Z_{n+1}=(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})
for which only the covariate
X_{n+1}\in\mathcal{X}
is observed, a prediction set should be inferred for the outcome
Y_{n+1}\in\mathcal{Y}
.
1
1

1

The regression and classification settings correspond to
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{Y}
finite, respectively.

A training dataset
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{train}}}

independent of the test point and the calibration sample
is observed and used to compute a non-conformity score function
S:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The scalar
S(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}
measures the non-conformity of the response
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
with the covariate
X\in\mathcal{X}
. All our results hold conditionally on the training dataset, hence to alleviate notation we will consider the score function
S
as fixed and determinist.
This is in accordance with the usual split conformal model: the training/calibration split is considered as already done, with a fixed independent training sample and a growing calibration sample.
A classical example in regression is the absolute value of the residual
S(X,Y)=|Y-{\widehat{\mu}}(X)|
where
{\widehat{\mu}}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is a regression function trained from an independent training sample (considered as fixed here).
The (split) conformal prediction set at level
(1-\alpha)
for
X_{n+1}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1})
, is defined as

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1})\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathcal{Y}:\ S(X_{n+1},y)\leqslant S_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil}}\right)}}}\right\},

(3)

where
S_{(1)}\leqslant S_{(2)}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant S_{(n)}<S_{(n+1)}:=+\infty
correspond to the ordered calibration scores
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{cal}}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{S_{k}:{k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}}}\right\}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{S(X_{k},Y_{k})\,:\,k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}}\right\}
.
The interest of the conformal prediction set is the validity of the prediction region under weak assumption. More precisely, if the random variables
(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n},Z_{n+1})
(i.e. the
n
calibration points and the new test point) are exchangeable (or i.i.d.) then we have the marginal coverage property:
\mathbb{P}(Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1}))\leqslant\alpha
. Furthermore, if the scores
(S(X_{i},Y_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket n+1\rrbracket}
are exchangeable and without ties almost surely, then
\mathbb{P}(Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1}))
is lower bounded by
\alpha-(n+1)^{-1}
(see
Vovk, Gammerman and
Shafer, 2005
for more details).
The set
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1})
can be equivalently described by using
p
-values (this classical fact can be retrieved from Lemma
F.1
). In particular, by defining the (unobserved)
conformal
p
-value

{p_{1}^{(n)}}
as in (
4
) with the test score
T_{1}=S(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})
(see below), we obtain that
Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+1})
if and only if
{p_{1}^{(n)}}\leqslant\alpha
. By the above marginal coverage property,
{p_{1}^{(n)}}
is a valid
p
-value for testing exchangeability of
(Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n},Z_{n+1})
since it is super-uniformly distributed under exchangeability.
In this paper, we consider a
multiple/simultaneous inference setting (also called transductive by
Vovk, 2013
;
Gazin, Blanchard and
Roquain, 2024
;
Behboodi
et al., 2025
), where a decision should be made for a whole test sample of size
m

(Z_{n+1},\dots,Z_{n+m})
with
Z_{n+i}=(X_{n+i},Y_{n+i})
, for which only the covariates
X_{n+1},\dots,X_{n+m}
are observed. We denote
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{test}}}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{T_{i}\,:\,i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}}\right\}
with
T_{i}=S(X_{n+i},Y_{n+i})
the set of the unobserved (since only the
X_{n+i}
’s are observed) scores of the test sample.
Considering the conformal prediction sets
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+i})
,
i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket
— corresponding to (
3
) for any test point — gives rise to a family of
m
conformal
p
-values
({p_{i}^{(n)}})_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}
defined by

{p_{i}^{(n)}}\coloneqq(n+1)^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\sum_{k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{S_{k}\geqslant T_{i}}}}}\right),\>\>\>i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket.

(4)

We also define the family of
theoretical
p
-values

({p_{i}^{(+\infty)}})_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}
when the calibration scores are i.i.d. as

{p_{i}^{(+\infty)}}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[S_{1}\geqslant T_{i}\middle|T_{i}}}\right],\>\>\>i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket.

(5)

By applying the law of large number when the calibration scores are i.i.d., the
p
-value
{p_{i}^{(+\infty)}}
can be seen as the limit of
{p_{i}^{(n)}}
when
n
tends to infinity.
The false coverage proportion
\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)
of the prediction set family
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+i})}}\right)_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}
is defined by

\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{Y_{n+i}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(X_{n+i})}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{{p_{i}^{(n)}}\leqslant\alpha}}.

(6)

Note that this corresponds to the empirical cumulative distribution function (e.c.d.f.) of the conformal
p
-values family
({p_{i}^{(n)}})_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}
.
We emphasise that the set
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{test}}}
of test scores is not observed, and neither are the conformal
p
-values (
4
) in the prediction setting. The
\mathrm{FCP}
is not observed, and the aim is to study its behavior when the sizes of the calibration and test samples both tend to infinity.
The following assumption will be considered throughout the paper for the (weighted or not) conformal prediction task.

Assumption 1
.

The set of calibration scores
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{k}}}\right)_{k\geqslant 1}
and the set of test scores
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T_{i}}}\right)_{i\geqslant 1}
are two independent families of real random variables. The
S_{k}
’s (
k\geqslant 1
), (resp.
T_{i}
’s (
i\geqslant 1
)) are i.i.d. with distribution
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
(resp.
P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
) and cumulative distribution function
F_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
(resp.
F_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
).
Moreover,
F_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
and
F_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
are continuous functions.

When the variables
Z_{i},i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket
, are i.i.d., Assumption
1
is true with
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}=P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
conditionally on
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{train}}}
, see Remark
2.1
below for more details.
Since the vector of scores
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{cal}}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{test}}}
is exchangeable in this case,
the conformal
p
-values are marginally super-uniform
(
Vovk, Gammerman and
Shafer, 2005
;
Romano and Wolf, 2005
)
which leads to non-asymptotically valid prediction sets. However, in case of a distribution shift between the distributions of the calibration and the test sample, that is
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}\neq P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
, this property is lost. To solve this issue,
Tibshirani
et al. (2019)
;
Barber et al. (2023)
proposed in this case to use weighted conformal prediction. We follow this approach (with light formal variations for mathematical convenience) by introducing a nonnegative
weight function

w:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
w(+\infty)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and the prediction set

\mathcal{C}^{w,\alpha}(X_{n+1})\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathcal{Y}:\ S(X_{n+1},y)\leqslant Q_{1-\alpha}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\frac{w(+\infty)}{W}\delta_{+\infty}+\sum_{k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}\frac{w\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{k}}}\right)}{W}\delta_{S_{k}}}}}\right)}}\right\},

where
Q_{1-\alpha}(\mu)
denotes the
1-\alpha
-quantile of the probability measure
\mu
,
\delta_{a}
denotes the Dirac measure in
a
and
W:=w(+\infty)+\sum_{k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}w\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{k}}}\right)
is a normalization constant.
There are different ways to choose the weight function
w
.
In the case of a known distribution shift in some specific model, the weight function is usually chosen as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the shift
(
Tibshirani
et al., 2019
;
Jin and Candès, 2023
)
.
Barber et al. (2023)
assumes that the weight function
w
is deterministic and fixed by the user before looking at the data. To accommodate both points of view, we assume that
w
can be estimated from a (possibly infinite) independent training dataset (potentially different from
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{train}}}
above) containing both data with the same distribution as the calibration points, and data with the same distribution as the test points. Our results hold conditionally on this training sample and
w
can be considered as deterministic.
Thanks to Lemma
F.1
, the set
\mathcal{C}^{w,\alpha}(X_{n+1})
can
be described with
weighted
conformal
p
-values:
Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}^{w,\alpha}(X_{n+1})
if and only if
{p_{1}^{w,(n)}}\leqslant\alpha
, with
{p_{1}^{w,(n)}}
the (unobserved)
weighted conformal
p
-value
given by (
7
) below.
Similarly, with a test sample of size
m
, we obtained
m

weighted conformal
p
-values
,

{p_{i}^{w,(n)}}\coloneqq\frac{w(+\infty)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w(S_{k}){\mathds{1}_{S_{k}\geqslant T_{i}}}}{w(+\infty)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w(S_{k})},\text{ for all $i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket$}.

(7)

The
\mathrm{FCP}
of these weighted conformal prediction sets is then defined by

\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{w,(n)}(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{Y_{n+i}\notin\mathcal{C}^{w,\alpha}(X_{n+i})}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{{p_{i}^{w,(n)}}\leqslant\alpha}},

(8)

which, similarly to (
6
), is the e.c.d.f. of the weighted conformal
p
-values family.
As for the unweighted case, the weighted conformal
p
-values are not observed, and neither is the corresponding false coverage proportion.

Finally, under Assumption
1
and if
P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
, a particularly interesting weight function, called the
oracle weight function
, is given by

w^{*}\coloneqq\frac{\operatorname{d}P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}}{\operatorname{d}P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}}.

(9)

Clearly, if the calibration and test sample are exchangeable (
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}=P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
), the function
w^{*}
is constantly equal to
1
. However, under a distribution shift (
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}\neq P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
), the oracle weight function is different, and oracle-weighted conformal
p
-values
{p_{i}^{w^{*},(n)}}
(
7
) are different from the regular ones
{p_{i}^{(n)}}
(
4
). They satisfy the marginally super-uniform property provided that
w^{*}(+\infty)\geqslant\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}w^{*}(u)
, see
Tibshirani
et al. (2019)
.

Remark 2.1
.

In split conformal inference the score function
S(\cdot)
and the weight function
w(\cdot)
are constructed here with an independent training dataset which creates a dependence among the scores
(S(Z_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
.
However, our results are stated conditionally on the training phase hence the weight function
w(\cdot)
and score function
S(\cdot)
can be considered as deterministic and the scores
(S(Z_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
are independent as stated in Assumption
1
provided that the data
(Z_{i})_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
are independent (which is a common assumption in the conformal prediction literature).

2.2
Novelty detection setting

In the novelty detection setting (see for instance
Vovk, Gammerman and
Shafer, 2005
;
Bates et al., 2023
), we observe a calibration set
\{Z_{k},k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket\}
of size
n
, with values in
\mathcal{Z}
distributed according to an unknown “null” distribution
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and a test sample
\{Z_{n+i},i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket\}
with
Z_{n+i}\in\mathcal{Z}
either distributed as
\mathbb{P}_{0}
or not. Formally, we introduce a subset
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\subset\{1,2,\dots\}
so that
Z_{n+i}\sim\mathbb{P}_{0}
when
i\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. We also denote
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=\{1,2,\dots\}\backslash{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
.
We assume that we have access to an independent training dataset
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{train}}}
used to learn a non-conformity score function
S:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
S(Z)\in\mathbb{R}
measures the non-conformity of the variables
Z
with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{0}
. Our results are stated conditionally on
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{train}}}
and the training phase. Therefore, as in the prediction setting, we assume that the score function
S(\cdot)
is fixed.
We denote
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{cal}}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{S_{k}\,:\,k\in\llbracket n\rrbracket}}\right\}
for
S_{k}\coloneqq S(Z_{k})
, and
\mathcal{D}_{{\tiny\mbox{test}}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{T_{i}\,:\,i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}}\right\}
for
T_{i}:=S(Z_{n+i})
the set of the scores from the calibration and test samples respectively.
A novelty detection procedure decides, for each
i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket
, whether
Z_{n+i}
is a novelty (that is, does not follow
\mathbb{P}_{0}
), or not.
The procedure described by
Bates et al. (2023)

consists in computing the conformal
p
-values defined in (
4
) (by using the specific
S_{k}
’s and
T_{i}
’s of novelty detection which are observed in this case),
and then applying the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure
(
Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995
)

on this
p
-value family
({p_{i}^{(n)}})_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}
(see Section
5.2.1
for more formal details). This gives a rejection set
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
corresponding to the indices of the declared novelties. We introduce the two main quantities to measure the performance of the rejection set,

\displaystyle\mathrm{FDP}_{m}^{(n)}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}}\right\rvert}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}}}\right\rvert\vee 1};

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{TDP}_{m}^{(n)}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{1}}}\right\rvert}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{1}}}\right\rvert\vee 1}.

(11)

The
\mathrm{FDP}
corresponds to the proportion of errors among the declared novelties (related to a type I error notion), while the
\mathrm{TDP}
corresponds to the proportion of correct decisions among the true novelties (related to power and type II error notions).
In the novelty detection setting all the scores are available hence the conformal
p
-values can be computed.
Nevertheless, the
\mathrm{FDP}
and the
\mathrm{TDP}
remain unknown since the indices of the nulls/alternatives are not observed. The following is assumed throughout the paper for (weighted) novelty detection.

Assumption 2
.

The set of calibration scores
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{k}}}\right)_{k\geqslant 1}
and the set of test scores
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T_{i}}}\right)_{i\geqslant 1}

are two independent families of real random variables. The variables
S_{k}
,
k\geqslant 1
, are i.i.d. with distribution
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
and c.d.f.
F_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
. The variables
T_{i}
,
i\geqslant 1
, are independent, the variables
T_{i}
,
i\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, are identically distributed as a null score distribution
P_{0}
and c.d.f.
F_{0}
, and the variables
T_{i}
,
i\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, are identically distributed as an alternative score distribution
P_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
(potentially different from
P_{0}
) with c.d.f.
F_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
. Moreover,
F_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
,
F_{0}
and
F_{\tiny\mbox{test}}
are continuous.

We also consider the case of a distribution shift between the distribution of the calibration set
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
and the null distribution
P_{0}
, in which case we propose to use a weighted
p
-value approach (as for the prediction task). The weights are assumed to be built with an independent training sample so they are considered as fixed here (our results hold conditionally on this sample) considering oracle weights as a particular case with perfect training.
For some
weight function

w:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
w(+\infty)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the weighted conformal
p
-values are the ones from (
7
) (by using the specific
S_{k}
’s and
T_{i}
’s of novelty detection).
We define the weighted version of the procedure of
Bates et al. (2023)
as the BH procedure applied to this
weighted
p
-value family and we denote its rejection set by
\mathcal{R}^{w}_{\alpha}\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
.
The
\mathrm{FDP}
and
\mathrm{TDP}
of
\mathcal{R}^{w}_{\alpha}
are defined similarly to above.

Under Assumption
2
and if
P_{0}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}
, a particular weight is the
oracle weight function
defined by

w^{*}\coloneqq\frac{\operatorname{d}P_{0}}{\operatorname{d}P_{\tiny\mbox{cal}}}.

(12)

The oracle weighted conformal
p
-values
{p_{i}^{w^{*},(n)}}
,
i\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, are marginally super-uniform provided that
w^{*}(+\infty)\geqslant\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}w^{*}(u)
, see
Tibshirani
et al. (2019)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The score function
S(\cdot)
and the weight function
w(\cdot)
are trained here with an independent training dataset which induces a non-trivial dependence between the scores
(S(Z_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
. However, our results are stated conditionally on the training phase and the score function
S(\cdot)
and weight function
w(\cdot)
can be considered as deterministic. Hence, the scores
(S(Z_{i}))_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
satisfy Assumption
2
conditionally on the training phase as long as the underlying data
(Z_{i})_{i\in\llbracket n+m\rrbracket}
are independent and have the same distribution within a group (calibration, null, alternative), which is a classical assumption
(
Bates et al., 2023
;
Jin and Candès, 2023
)
.

2.3
Spaces for process convergence

We study the asymptotic convergence of random processes and we consider usual spaces defined in
Billingsley (1999)
and
van der Vaart and Wellner (1996)
, denoted as usual
D[0,1]
,
D(0,1)
and
\ell^{\infty}(0,1)
, and which are briefly described below.

First,
D[0,1]
is the set of càdlàg functions
f:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the usual Skorohod topology. Second,
D(0,1)
is the set of càdlàg functions
f:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the extended Skorohod topology
2
2

2

This definition is similar to the convergence in
D[0,+\infty)
considered in
Billingsley (1999)
defined as follows:
(x_{n})_{n}\in D(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
converges to
x\in D(0,1)
if and only if, for all
(a,b)\in(0,1)^{2}
with
a<b
such that
x
is continuous at points
a
and
b
, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
restricted to
[a,b]
converges to
x
restricted to
[a,b]
in
D[a,b]
with the usual Skorohod topology. Finally,
\ell^{\infty}(0,1)
is the space of locally bounded functions
f:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the topology of the uniform convergence on all compact sets of
(0,1)
: a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\in[\ell^{\infty}(0,1)]^{\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f\in\ell^{\infty}(0,1)
if and only if for all
K
compact subsets of
(0,1)
,
\sup_{x\in K}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|f_{n}(x)-f(x)}}\right\rvert\rightarrow 0
.
We also consider
D(\mathbb{R})
the space of càdlàg functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
with the extended Skorohod topology and
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
the space of locally bounded functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
with the topology of the uniform convergence on all compact sets of
\mathbb{R}
.

3.1
Main results

Let us

\displaystyle I(t)

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumption

, we have

\sqrt{\tau_{n,m}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}-I}}\right)\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\underset{\tau_{n,m}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{U}\text{ on $D[0,1]$,}

where

Remark 3.1
.

Theorem

is true under slightly more general assumptions: namely, it is sufficient that all the scores are exchangeable and without ties almost surely
(see Theorem
5.2
for a formal statement).
Using the latter is useful since non i.i.d. scores arise naturally in various setting, like in link prediction for graph
(
Blanchard et al., 2024

Theorem

is proved in Section

As a corollary, if

, this gives

\sqrt{m}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{FCP}_{m}^{(n)}-I}}\right)\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\underset{\tau_{n,m}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{U}.

The latter shows that if the ratio
\tau_{n,m}m^{-1}
or

i.i.d.

(compare with the Donsker theorem, see Theorem

Theorem
3.1
(or more precisely its extension Theorem

when the scores are exchangeable and without ties almost surely) can be formulated as a result on the asymptotic properties of the empirical c.d.f. of the random vectors
(q_{1},\cdots,q_{m})
with the distribution

defined in

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{i/(n+1),\ i\in\llbracket n+1\rrbracket}}\right\}

\Bigg(\sqrt{\tau_{n,m}}\bigg[m^{-1}\sum_{i\in\llbracket m\rrbracket}{\mathds{1}_{q_{i}^{(n)}\leqslant t}}-t\bigg]\Bigg)_{t\in[0,1]}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\underset{\tau_{n,m}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{U}\ \text{on }D[0,1],

Another interesting application is the convergence of the uniform distribution over the set of discrete random measure on the set

\sqrt{\tau_{n,m}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{m}^{(n)}-I}}\right)\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\underset{\tau_{n,m}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{U}\ \text{on $D[0,1]$},

6
Conclusion

In this paper we obtain the exact asymptotic distribution of
\mathrm{FCP}
and
\mathrm{FDP}
for conformal inference methods when both the sizes of the calibration sample and test sample grow simultaneously. Our theory covers both the prediction and novelty detection settings, including a potential distribution shift.
Our results quantify exactly how the covariance process is affected by the dependence inherent to the conformal
settings, that use the same calibration sample for all the test examples. First, we prove that the convergence rate
\sqrt{\tau_{n,m}}
can be largely deteriorated when
n/m
vanishes to zero (that is,
\sigma=0
). Otherwise, when
n
and
m
are of the same order, the convergence rate is the usual one (
m^{1/2}
), but the asymptotic covariance is affected. Nevertheless, when
n/m
tends to infinity, the convergence is strictly the same as in the usual independent case.
Interestingly, our results can be used to calibrate easily and accurately quantiles for controlling an error amount when performing conformal inference with large
n
and
m
.
We also quantify the effects of doing conformal inference while there is a distribution shift between the calibration sample and the test sample. We exhibit how this distribution shift acts on the asymptotic behaviour of the
\mathrm{FCP}
, by changing the mean and the variance, and how the correction with weighted conformal
p
-values impacts the asymptotic variance.

While our work paves the way for studying asymptotic convergences in conformal inferences, it leaves some open directions. For instance, in case of a distribution shift, the oracle weight function is mostly unknown, and is often estimated
(
Jin and Candès, 2023
)
. Finding the exact asymptotic distribution for the processes using estimated weights is a very interesting and challenging problem for future investigations. In a finite sample setting, using the asymptotic expectation and variance as a proxy to design weight and score functions can provide very compelling new ideas and can unlock new training procedures.

Lemma F.1
.

Let
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n+1}}}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}\cup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{+\infty}}\right\}}
,
(w_{k})_{k\in\llbracket n+1\rrbracket}

(ii)

Proof of Lemma

.

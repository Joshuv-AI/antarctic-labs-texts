On t t -intersecting Families of Spanning Trees

Abstract
. We prove that there exists a constant c > 0 c>0 such that for all integers 2 ≤ t ≤ c ​ n 2\leq t\leq cn , if 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a collection of spanning trees in K n K_{n} such that any two intersect at at least t t edges, then | 𝒜 | ≤ 2 t ​ n n − t − 2 |\mathcal{A}|\leq 2^{t}n^{n-t-2} . 1.1.
Our Result

A family

1

1.2.
Erdős-Ko-Rado Combinatorics

The question of finding the maximum size
of
t
-intersecting families of spanning trees
has many variants.
The simplest variant dates back to
the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem
[
EKR61
]
,
which concerns a family of sets.
A family of sets
\mathcal{A}
is
t
-intersecting
if
|X_{1}\cap X_{2}|\geq t
for all
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
.
The Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem states that
if
n\geq 2k
, then any
1
-intersecting family

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
has size at most

\binom{n-1}{k-1}
.
This theorem was later generalized to

t
-intersecting families, culminating with
the Ahlswede-Khachatrian theorem
[
AK97
]
,
which determines the maximum size of

t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}

for all triples
(n,k,t)
.

One may ask a similar question on permutations.
Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on
[n]
.
A family of permutations
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n}

is
t
-intersecting if for any two
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
,
we have
\sigma_{1}(i)=\sigma_{2}(i)

for at least
t
values of
i
.
Like above, a natural question is to determine the maximum
size of
t
-intersecting family of permutations in
S_{n}
,
which was answered by Kupavskii
[
Kup24
]

for
t<(1-\varepsilon)n
and
n>n_{0}(\varepsilon)
.

1.3.
Our Techniques

Our proof is based on the technique of spread approximation,
which was introduced in
[
KZ24
]

to solve Erdős-Ko-Rado type problems in various settings.
The idea of spread approximation is to approximate
a
t
-intersecting family of sets
\mathcal{A}

with another
t
-intersecting family of sets
\mathcal{S}
,
each of whose elements has smaller size than elements in
\mathcal{A}
.
This technique was used to prove
Theorem

2

in
[
FHIKLMP25
]
.

On the other hand, the spread approximation technique
has been refined to handle the larger value of
t

(compared to
n
) in
[
Kup24
]
.
There, they first prove the “density boost” statement

[
Kup24
, Thm. 12]
,
by finding a set of size close to
t

that is contained in many elements in
\mathcal{A}
.
Then, they use density boost to obtain a more accurate
spread approximation.
Our proof of
Theorem

1
follows the same strategy
of obtaining a density boost first.
However, the key difference is that we were able
to prove density boost by a more direct argument,
not using another spread approximation as in

[
Kup24
]
.

Calligraphic letters (

2.2.
Spread Families

1
.

A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[N]}
is said to be

r

|\mathcal{A}[S]|\leq r^{-|S|}|\mathcal{A}|.

A family

(r,t)

r
-spread

t

Observe that if

d_{i}(\mathcal{U})\leq r^{-(i-j)}d_{j}(\mathcal{U})

for all indices

Spread families behave nicely in terms of

In the following lemma, a

independently with

.

2
Spread Lemma,
[

.

Let

be an
r
-spread family
and
W
be a
(\beta\delta)
-random subset of
[N]
.
Then,

\mathbb{P}(\text{there exists }F\in\mathcal{A}\text{ such that }F\subseteq W)\geq 1-k\left(\frac{2}{\log_{2}(r\delta)}\right)^{\beta}.

We record the following useful observation
that will allow us to find spread sets.

3
Finding Spread Family.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq 2^{[N]}
be an
r
-spread family,
and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be subfamily.
Let

.
Then, there exists

such that

•

\mathcal{A}(S)\subseteq 2^{[N]\setminus S}
is
r^{\prime}
-spread.

•

|S|\leq\dfrac{\log|\mathcal{U}|-\log|\mathcal{A}|}{\log r-\log r^{\prime}}
.

Take
S
such that
|\mathcal{A}[S]|\cdot(r^{\prime})^{|S|}
is maximal.
We first check that
\mathcal{A}(S)
is

-spread.
To do that, note that for any

,
we have

|\mathcal{A}(S)[X]|=|\mathcal{A}(S\cup X)|\leq(r^{\prime})^{-|X|}|\mathcal{A}(S)|

To verify the second condition, we note that

(maximality between

)

\displaystyle(r^{\prime})^{-|S|}|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}[S]|

3.
Density Boost

In this section, we prove the density boost lemma.
This is essentially the same as

[
Kup24
, Thm. 12]
.
There, it was proved by first obtaining an initial

We provide a more direct proof.

4
Density Boost.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0

and
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)

such that the following holds:

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{n}
be an
(r,t)
-spread family,
and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be a
t
-intersecting family
such that
|\mathcal{A}|\geq 0.001c_{t}(\mathcal{U})
.
Assume that
t>n^{1-\delta}/\delta
,
r>n^{0.99}
, and
n>n_{0}
.
Then, there exists a set
X\subseteq[N]
such that

|X|=t-\lceil n^{1-\delta}\rceil

and

|\mathcal{A}[X]|\geq e^{-\varepsilon t}|\mathcal{A}|.

Proof.

Select
\delta\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{6}\right)
such that
12\delta(1-\log\delta)<\varepsilon
.
We select
S
such that
d_{|S|}(\mathcal{U})^{-2\delta}\cdot|\mathcal{A}[S]|
is maximal.
We first show that
|S|\leq(1+6\delta)t
.
To do this, assume for a contradiction that
|S|>(1+6\delta)t
.
Then, observe that

(maximality condition for
S
and
\emptyset
)

\displaystyle 0.001d_{t}(\mathcal{U})\,|\mathcal{U}|<|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle\leq d_{|S|}(\mathcal{U})^{-2\delta}|\mathcal{A}[S]|

\displaystyle\leq d_{|S|}(\mathcal{U})^{-2\delta}|\mathcal{U}[S]|

(definition (
1
) of
d_{i}(\mathcal{U})
)

\displaystyle\leq d_{|S|}(\mathcal{U})^{1-2\delta}|\mathcal{U}|

(
(r,t)
-spreadness of
\mathcal{U}
)

\displaystyle\leq r^{-(1-2\delta)(|S|-t)}d_{t}(\mathcal{U})^{1-2\delta}|\mathcal{U}|

(assumption that
|S|>(1+6\delta)t
)

\displaystyle<r^{-6\delta(1-2\delta)t}d_{t}(\mathcal{U})^{1-2\delta}|\mathcal{U}|,

which implies that

0.001d_{t}(\mathcal{U})^{2\delta}\leq r^{-6\delta(1-2\delta)t}\implies 0.001d_{t}(\mathcal{U})\leq r^{-3(1-2\delta)t},

By a straightforward averaging argument,

d_{t}(\mathcal{U})\geq n^{-t}\geq r^{-2t}
,
which is a contradiction.

Now, let
t^{\prime}=t-\lceil n^{1-\delta}\rceil
,
and assume for contradiction that such
X
does not exist.
We claim that there exists an element

F\in\mathcal{A}
such that
|F\cap S|<t^{\prime}
.
To prove this, assume for contradiction that

|F\cap S|\geq t^{\prime}
for all
F\in\mathcal{A}
.
We count in two ways
the number

N=\left|\left\{(A,T):A\in\mathcal{A},T\in\tbinom{A\cap S}{t^{\prime}}\right\}\right|.

On one hand, we get that
N\geq|\mathcal{A}|
.
On the other hand, we get that

\displaystyle N\leq\sum_{X\in\binom{S}{t^{\prime}}}|\mathcal{A}[X]|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{|S|}{t^{\prime}}\cdot|\mathcal{A}|\cdot e^{-\varepsilon t}

\displaystyle<\binom{(1+6\delta)t}{12\delta t}\cdot|\mathcal{A}|\cdot e^{-\varepsilon t}

(
Lemma

1
)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{e(1+6\delta)t}{12\delta t}\right)^{12\delta t}\cdot|\mathcal{A}|\cdot e^{-\varepsilon t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{e}{\delta}\right)^{12\delta t}|\mathcal{A}|\cdot e^{-\varepsilon t}<|\mathcal{A}|,

from our choice of
\delta
.
This gives a contradiction.
Thus,
|F\cap S|<t^{\prime}
for some
F\in\mathcal{A}
.

Observe that any

A\in\mathcal{A}[S]
must intersect
F
at
t-t^{\prime}\geq n^{1-\delta}
elements
outside
S
,
i.e.,
T
contains a subset
U\in\binom{F\setminus S}{t-t^{\prime}}
.
Summing across
t
gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}[S]|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\binom{F\setminus S}{t-t^{\prime}}}|\mathcal{A}[S\cup T]|

(maximality between
S
and
S\cup T
)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n-|S|}{n^{1-\delta}}\left(\frac{c_{t}(\mathcal{U})}{c_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{U})}\right)^{2\delta}|\mathcal{A}[S]|

(
(r,t)
-spreadness of
\mathcal{U}
)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{n^{1-\delta}}r^{-2\delta n^{1-\delta}}|\mathcal{A}[S]|

(
Lemma

1
)

\displaystyle\leq(en^{\delta})^{n^{1-\delta}}r^{-2\delta n^{1-\delta}}|\mathcal{A}[S]|

(
r>n^{0.99}
and
n
is sufficiently large)

\displaystyle<|\mathcal{A}[S]|

which is a contradiction.
∎

Stable determination of a time-dependent matrix potential for a wave equation in an infinite waveguide
Nitesh Kumar † , Tanmay Sarkar ‡ and Manmohan Vashisth ∗

Abstract
. We analyze the stability of an inverse problem for determining the time-dependent matrix potential appearing in the Dirichlet initial-boundary value problem for the wave equation in an unbounded cylindrical waveguide. The observation is given by the input-output map associated with the wave equation. Considering a suitable geometric optics solution and with the help of light ray transform , we demonstrate the stability estimate in the determination of the time-dependent matrix potential from the given input-output map.

Introduction

The present paper is concerned with an inverse problem of determining a matrix-valued potential
\displaystyle q(t,x):=((q_{ij}(t,x)))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
in an unbounded cylindrical domain
\Omega=\omega\times\mathbb{R}
,
where
\omega
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
bounded open connected domain of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For time
T>0
, we denote
\Omega_{T}:=(0,T)\times\Omega
and the lateral boundary of
\Omega_{T}
by
\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
where
\displaystyle\partial\Omega:=\partial\omega\times\mathbb{R}
. We consider the following initial boundary value problem (IBVP) for the system of wave equations:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{q}\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\overrightarrow{u}(0,x)=\overrightarrow{\phi}(x),~~\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{u}(0,x)=\overrightarrow{\psi}(x),&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{f}(t,x),&\quad(t,x)\in\Sigma,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the operator
\mathcal{L}_{q}
is described by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{q}\overrightarrow{u}(t,x):&=\square\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)\\
&={\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\square u_{1}(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{1j}(t,x)u_{j}(t,x)\\
\square u_{2}(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{2j}(t,x)u_{j}(t,x)\\
\vdots\\
\square u_{n}(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{nj}(t,x)u_{j}(t,x)\end{bmatrix}},\qquad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}\end{split}

(1.2)

in which
\square:=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}
denotes the standard wave operator and

q(t,x)=((q_{ij}(t,x)))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ q_{ij}\in{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T}),\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n,

represents a time-dependent matrix potential and
\displaystyle\overrightarrow{u}(t,x):=\left(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x),\cdots,u_{n}(t,x)\right)^{T}
represent the displacement vector.

Following
[
26
]

\displaystyle H^{s}(\partial\Omega)

\displaystyle=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{x_{3}};L^{2}(\partial\omega))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x_{3}};H^{s}(\partial\omega)),

\displaystyle H^{r,s}((0,T)\times X)

\displaystyle=H^{r}(0,T;L^{2}(X))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(X)),

,

. Suppose

\displaystyle L=\Big\{(\phi,\psi,f)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}}(\Sigma):\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=f|_{t=0},

\displaystyle~\partial_{t}f,~\partial_{\tau}f,~\partial_{x_{3}}f\in L^{2}\Big(\Sigma;d\sigma(x)\frac{dt}{t}\Big)\Big\}

with

defined by

\displaystyle\|(\phi,\psi,f)\|^{2}_{L}=

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\partial_{t}f|^{2}+|\partial_{\tau}f|^{2}+|\partial_{x_{3}}f|^{2}}{t}d\sigma(x)~dt.

In addition, throughout this paper, we will use the notation,

(1.3)

whenever

2.
Existence and Uniqueness

In this section, we establish the existence and uniqueness of the IBVP (
1.1
). More precisely, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle q(t,x):=((q_{ij}(t,x)))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
with
q_{ij}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, be a time-dependent matrix potential. Suppose the initial and boundary data
(\overrightarrow{\phi},~\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\in\mathbf{L}
. Then, there exists a unique solution

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{u}\in\mathbf{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))

of the IBVP (
1.1
) such that
\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)
.
Moreover, it satisfies the energy estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}+\|\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}},

(2.1)

where the constant
C=C(\Omega,T,\|q\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})})>0
.

In the case of bounded domain
\Omega
, the Theorem
2.1
has been proved in
[
37
]
. However, the same can not be applicable to the unbounded infinite waveguide. Nevertheless, we prove the Theorem
2.1
by following the approach used in
[
26
]
where a well-posedness result is proved for a single wave equation. We require the following lemma to prove Theorem
2.1

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\in\mathbf{L}
, there exists

\overrightarrow{w}=\overrightarrow{w}[\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f}]=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n})^{T}\in\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})

satisfying

\begin{cases}\overrightarrow{w}(0,\cdot)=\overrightarrow{\phi},\quad\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{w}(0,\cdot)=\overrightarrow{\psi},&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{w}=\overrightarrow{0},\quad\overrightarrow{w}=\overrightarrow{f},&\text{on}~\Sigma,\end{cases}

(2.2)

and

\|\overrightarrow{w}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(2.3)

The Lemma
2.2
can be proved if we show that for every
i=1,2,\dots,n
and for each
(\phi_{i},\psi_{i},f_{i})\in L
, there exists
w_{i}\in H^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})
satisfying

\begin{cases}w_{i}(0,x)=\phi_{i},\quad\partial_{t}w_{i}(0,x)=\psi_{i},&\text{in}~\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}w_{i}=0,\quad w_{i}=f_{i},&\text{on}~\Sigma,\end{cases}

(2.4)

and

\|w_{i}\|_{H^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|(\phi_{i},\psi_{i},f_{i})\|_{L}.

(2.5)

Our aim is to show Lemma
2.2
related to the lifting of Sobolev spaces using the arguments related to local coordinates as used in

35

(see pages

In this regard, we first prove the result when the domain

are given by
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}>0\}
and

respectively. Subsequently,

We consider the space

\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}),\quad u_{1}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}),\quad g_{0}\in H^{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad g_{1}\in H^{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2})

&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\partial_{x_{2}}u_{0}|_{x_{1}=r}-\partial_{x_{2}}g_{0}|_{t=r}\big|^{2}\frac{dr}{r}dx_{2}dx_{3}<\infty,\\

Proof of Lemma

.

Let us define an operator

\displaystyle S(w):=(w|_{t=0},\partial_{t}w|_{t=0},w|_{x_{1}=0},\partial_{x_{1}}w|_{x_{1}=0}).

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{2,2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+})}\leq C\|(\phi,\psi,f)\|^{2}_{Z_{2}}.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Thanks to Lemma
2.2
, we split
\overrightarrow{u}
into two parts
\overrightarrow{u}=\overrightarrow{v}+\overrightarrow{w}
with
\overrightarrow{w}=\overrightarrow{w}[\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f}]\in\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})
satisfying (
2.2
)-(

) and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}\overrightarrow{v}(t,x)-\Delta\overrightarrow{v}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{v}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{F}(t,x),&\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\overrightarrow{v}(0,x)=\overrightarrow{0},~~\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{v}(0,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{v}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&\quad(t,x)\in\Sigma\end{cases}

(2.10)

with
\overrightarrow{F}(t,x)=-\big(\partial_{t}^{2}\overrightarrow{w}(t,x)-\Delta\overrightarrow{w}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{w}(t,x)\big)
.
Since
q\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})
and
\overrightarrow{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})
, we have
\overrightarrow{F}(t,x)\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})
.
To demonstrate the well-posedness of IBVP (
2.10
), we follow the arguments from
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
. Let us define a time-dependent bilinear form

\displaystyle a(t;\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w}):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\overrightarrow{v}(x)\cdot\overline{\nabla\overrightarrow{w}(x)}~dx+\int_{\Omega}q(t,x)\overrightarrow{v}(x)\cdot\overline{\overrightarrow{w}(x)}~dx,\quad\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.11)

Since
q\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})
and
\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w}
are time-independent, we have the following bound

\displaystyle|a(t;\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w})|\leq\alpha\|\overrightarrow{v}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\overrightarrow{w}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

(2.12)

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the positive constant
\alpha
is independent of
\overrightarrow{v}
and
\overrightarrow{w}
. Furthermore, we observe that

\displaystyle|a(t;\overrightarrow{w},\overrightarrow{w})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla\overrightarrow{w}(x)|^{2}+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{w}(x)\cdot\overline{\overrightarrow{w}(x)}\Big)~dx\Big|

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\overrightarrow{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\|\overrightarrow{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

and consequently, we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\beta\|\overrightarrow{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq|a(t;\overrightarrow{w},\overrightarrow{w})|+\gamma\|\overrightarrow{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\overrightarrow{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega),~t\in(0,T),

(2.13)

for some constants
\beta>0
and
\gamma=1+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}
.
From (
2.11
)-(
2.13
), we conclude that the map
t\mapsto a(t;\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w})
is a continuous bilinear for all
\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
t\in(0,T)
. Since the principal part of
a(t;\cdot,\cdot)
is anti-symmetric,
applying
[
35
, Theorem 8.1]
we ensure that there exists
a unique solution
\overrightarrow{v}\in\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
of (
2.10
). In addition, there holds

\displaystyle\|\overrightarrow{v}\|_{\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|_{\mathbf{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(2.14)

From the Lemma
2.2
, there exists
\overrightarrow{w}\in\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})
such that (
2.2
) holds. Moreover, using the inclusion (
[
35
, Chapter 1, Theorem 3.1]
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)),

we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\overrightarrow{w}\|_{\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{w}\|_{\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\overrightarrow{w}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(2.15)

Combining the estimates (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) together, we have

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{u}\in\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))

along with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{C}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{C}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(2.16)

Afterwards, we show that
\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{v}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)
and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(2.17)

To do this, for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, let us define
\overrightarrow{K}^{(i)}=(\delta_{ir})_{r=1}^{n}
. As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{cases}\left(\partial_{t}^{2}v_{i}-\Delta v_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{ij}v_{j}\right)(t,x)=\overrightarrow{F}(t,x)\cdot\overrightarrow{K}^{(i)},\ \ (t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
v_{i}(0,x)=0,~~\partial_{t}v_{i}(0,x)=0,\qquad x\in\Omega,\\
v_{i}(t,x)=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\Sigma,\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(2.18)

for any
1\leq i\leq n
.
Let
\nu_{1}
be the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\omega
and let us consider the function
\gamma\in{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which is defined by

\gamma(x^{\prime},x_{3})=(\gamma_{1}(x^{\prime}),0),\qquad x^{\prime}\in\omega,~x_{3}\in\mathbb{R},

where
\gamma_{1}\in{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\omega},\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies
\gamma_{1}=\nu_{1}
on
\partial\omega
. Hence we obtain

\gamma=\nu
on
\partial\Omega
.
We multiply the equation (
2.18
) by
\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}
and integrate over
\Omega_{T}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2}:

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\partial_{t}^{2}v_{i}-\Delta v_{i}\big)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{ij}v_{j}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\overrightarrow{F}\cdot\overrightarrow{K}^{(i)}\big)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\overrightarrow{v}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}{|\big(\overrightarrow{F}\cdot\overrightarrow{K}^{(i)}\big)|^{2}}\,dx\,dt\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\overrightarrow{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{2,2}(\Omega_{T})}+\int^{T}_{0}(\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)})\,dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\Big).

(2.19)

Using the integration by parts, we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{1}&=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt\\
&=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial_{t}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{i}(T,x)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}(T,x)\big)\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{i}(0,x)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}(0,x)\big)\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{i}(T,x)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}(T,x)\big)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\big)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

With the help of Green’s formula in
x^{\prime}\in\omega
and the identity
\gamma(x)\cdot\nabla(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}=\gamma_{1}(x^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}&\gamma\cdot\nabla(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\omega}\gamma_{1}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\,dt\\
&=-\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\omega}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\,dt+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\partial\omega}(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt\\
&=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\,dt,\end{split}

where we have used the fact that
v_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Hence we end up with

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{i}(T,x)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}(T,x)\big)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(2.20)

Next, we focus on
\mathcal{E}_{2}
. We observe that

\mathcal{E}_{2}=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Delta v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial^{2}_{x_{3}}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt.

Again applying Green’s formula in
x^{\prime}\in\omega
, we find

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt&=-\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\partial\omega}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt\\
&\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\omega}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

and using integration by parts in
x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial^{2}_{x_{3}}v_{i}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial_{x_{3}}v_{i}\partial_{x_{3}}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt.

As a consequence,
\mathcal{E}_{2}
reduces to

\mathcal{E}_{2}=-\int_{\Sigma}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\nabla_{x}v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt.

Furthermore, we use the following identity for any
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
H=(\partial_{x_{j}}\gamma_{i})_{1\leq i,\,j\leq 3}
,

\nabla_{x}v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)=\big(H\nabla v_{i}\big)\cdot\nabla v_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\cdot\nabla(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}).

Subsequently,
\mathcal{E}_{2}
transforms into

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{2}&=-\int_{\Sigma}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(H\nabla v_{i}\big)\cdot\nabla v_{i}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\gamma\cdot\nabla(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(2.21)

Again, applying the Green’s formula in
x^{\prime}\in\omega
implies

\begin{split}\int_{\omega}\gamma\cdot\nabla(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx^{\prime}&=\int_{\omega}\gamma_{1}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\partial\omega}(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dS_{x^{\prime}}-\int_{\omega}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx^{\prime}.\end{split}

(2.22)

Using the fact that
v_{i}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we have
|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}=|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}
on
\Sigma
. It follows from (
2.22
) that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\gamma\cdot\nabla(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx\,dt=\int_{\Sigma}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(2.23)

Substituting (
2.23
) in (
2.21
) we get,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{2}&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(H\nabla v_{i}\big)\cdot\nabla v_{i}\,dx\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(2.24)

Combining (
2.19
), (
2.20
) and (
2.24
) together, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma}|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}|^{2}\,dS_{x^{\prime}}\,dx_{3}\,dt&=2\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(H\nabla v_{i}\big)\cdot\nabla v_{i}\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(|\nabla v_{i}|^{2})\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{i}(T,x)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}(T,x)\big)\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{T}}(\nabla\cdot\gamma)(\partial_{t}v_{i})^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\,dt\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{ij}v_{j}\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\big(\overrightarrow{F}\cdot\overrightarrow{K}^{(i)}\big)\big(\gamma\cdot\nabla v_{i}\big)\,dx\,dt\end{split}

which further implies that

\begin{split}\|\partial_{\nu}v_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\Big(\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\Big).\end{split}

Since the above estimate holds for each
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we finally end up with

\begin{split}\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\Big(\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\overrightarrow{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\Big)\end{split}

and consequently, the estimate (
2.17
) is established.
Moreover, using the fact that
\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}=\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}\overrightarrow{u}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|(\overrightarrow{\phi},\overrightarrow{\psi},\overrightarrow{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

Hence the result follows.

4.
Geometric Optics solutions

In this section, we give the construction of exponential growing and decaying solutions which will be instrumental for our stability result. More precisely, following the ideas from
[
26
]
used for constructing geometric optics (GO) solutions for a scalar wave equation in an infinite waveguide, we construct the suitable GO solutions for the system of wave equations considered in the present article. We also remark that the GO solutions for a system of wave equations in a bounded domain are constructed in
[
37
]
. However, the approach used in
[
37
]
can not be carried out for our case. To overcome this, we decompose the operator
\square:=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}
as
\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x^{\prime}}
and
-\partial^{2}_{x_{3}}
considering
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3}),~x^{\prime}\in\omega,x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
.

We prove the following Lemma in which
\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|y|=1\}
,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Schwarz space over
\mathbb{R}
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the space all smooth functions having compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})
be a matrix-valued potential,
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be given. Then for any
\rho>0
, the equation

\partial^{2}_{t}\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)-\Delta\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{u}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{0},\qquad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},

(4.1)

admits a solution of the form

\overrightarrow{u}^{\pm}(t,x)=e^{\pm i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})\overrightarrow{K}^{\pm}+\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{\pm}(t,x\,;\rho),\ \ t\in(0,T),\,x^{\prime}\in\omega,\,x_{3}\in\mathbb{R},

(4.2)

where
\overrightarrow{K}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is any constant vector and
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{\pm}(t,x\,;\rho)
satisfies

\begin{split}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(0,x\,;\rho)&=\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(0,x\,;\rho)=\overrightarrow{0},\qquad x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{-}(T,x\,;\rho)&=\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{-}(T,x\,;\rho)=\overrightarrow{0},\qquad x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{\pm}(t,x\,;\rho)&=\overrightarrow{0},\qquad(t,x)\in\Sigma.\end{split}

(4.3)

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\omega,T
and
\|q\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})}
such that

\displaystyle\rho\|\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{\pm}(\cdot;\rho)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\nabla\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{\pm}(\cdot;\rho)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|\overrightarrow{K}^{\pm}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|h\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(4.4)

Proof.

We give the proof for the construction of
\overrightarrow{u}^{+}
while that of construction of
\overrightarrow{u}^{-}
follows similarly.
To begin with, we first observe the following

(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta_{x^{\prime}})\big[\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\big]=e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta_{x^{\prime}})\big[\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})\big],

and similarly

-\partial^{2}_{x_{3}}\big[\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\big]=e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\big[-\partial^{2}_{x_{3}}(\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3}))\big].

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta+q(t,x))\big[e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})\overrightarrow{K}^{+}\big]=e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3}),

where
\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})
is given by

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})=(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta)\big[\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})\overrightarrow{K}^{+}\big]+q(t,x)\big[\varphi(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h(x_{3})\overrightarrow{K}^{+}\big].

(4.5)

Now in order to have
\overrightarrow{u}^{+}
given by (
4.2
) is a solution to (
4.1
), it is enough to
show the existence of
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}
satisfying the following equation

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(t,x)-\Delta\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(t,x)=-e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3}),&(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(0,x\,;\rho)=\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(0,x\,;\rho)=\overrightarrow{0},&x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&(t,x)\in\Sigma.\end{cases}

(4.6)

From (
4.5
), we find that
e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})
. Following the similar analysis carried out to demonstrate the well-posedness of (
2.10
), we ensure the existence of
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap\mathbf{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
as a solution of (
4.6
) (also refer to the Theorem
8.1
in
[
35
, Chapter 3]
). Moreover,
e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+t)}\overrightarrow{J}(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
and again applying the Theorem
2.1
in
[
36
, Chapter 5]
, we conclude that
\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega_{T})
.

Let us define

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{W}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(s,x)ds,\qquad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},

(4.7)

and consequently,
\overrightarrow{W}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}\overrightarrow{W}(t,x)-\Delta\overrightarrow{W}(t,x)+q(t,x)\overrightarrow{W}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{R}^{1}(t,x)+\overrightarrow{R}^{2}(t,x),&(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\overrightarrow{W}(0,x)=\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{W}(0,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&x\in\Omega,\\
\overrightarrow{W}(t,x)=\overrightarrow{0},&(t,x)\in\Sigma,\end{cases}

(4.8)

where
\overrightarrow{R}^{1}
and
\overrightarrow{R}^{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{R}^{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+s)}\overrightarrow{J}(s,x^{\prime},x_{3})\,ds,

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{R}^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}[q(t,x)-q(s,x)]\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(s,x)ds,

respectively. Let
\tau\in[0,T]
. From the energy estimate of (
4.8
), we get

\displaystyle\|\overrightarrow{\Psi}^{+}(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}\overrightarrow{W}(\tau,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|\overrightarrow{R}^{1}+\overrightarrow{R}^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(0,\tau;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\overrightarrow{R}^{1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\overrightarrow{R}^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\tau;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\Big).

(4.9)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{R}^{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+s)}\overrightarrow{J}(s,x^{\prime},x_{3})\,ds=-\frac{1}{i\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}e^{i\rho(x^{\prime}\cdot\theta+s)}\overrightarrow{J}(s,x^{\prime},x_{3})\,ds

(5.18)

It is straightforward to observe that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-3},\qquad\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(y^{\prime},y_{3})-(x^{\prime},x_{3})|\varphi^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})h^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x_{3})\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\,\leq C\,\varepsilon.

As a result, (
5.18
) reduces to

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}q_{ij}(t,y^{\prime}-t\theta,y_{3})dt\Big|\leq C\left(\rho^{4}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|+\frac{1}{\rho}\right)\varepsilon^{-10}+C\varepsilon.

By choosing
\varepsilon
such a way
\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon^{-10}}{\rho}
, there exist constants
\delta>0
and
\beta>0
such that

\Big|\mathcal{L}[q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\theta,y^{\prime})\Big|=\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{ij}(t,y^{\prime}-t\theta,y_{3})dt\Bigg|\leq C\left(\rho^{\beta}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|+\frac{1}{\rho^{\delta}}\right),~\text{a.e.}~y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Hence the result follows.
∎

Afterwards, we would like to define the Fourier transform acting on the component of the matrix potential and subsequently, our goal is to find its estimate. In this regard, let us fix
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
. We set

E=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}):|\tau|\leq|\xi|\Big\}

and define the Fourier transform of
q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as

\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{ij}(t,x^{\prime},y_{3})\,e^{-ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi}e^{-it\cdot\tau}dt\,dx^{\prime}.

With this, we have the following result:

Lemma 5.6
.

There exist constants
C>0
,
\beta>0,\,\delta>0
and
\rho_{0}>0
, such that the following estimate holds for any
\rho\geq\rho_{0}
and fixed
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Big|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi)\Big|\leq C\left(\rho^{\beta}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|+\frac{1}{\rho^{\delta}}\right),\qquad(\tau,\xi)\in E,

(5.19)

where the constant
C
depends only on
\Omega,\,T
and
M
.

Proof.

Let us consider
(\tau,\xi)\in E
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be such that
\xi\cdot\zeta=0
. We define
\theta
as

\theta=\frac{\tau}{|\xi^{2}|}\cdot\xi+\sqrt{1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{|\xi^{2}|}}\cdot\zeta,

so that
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
\theta\cdot\xi=\tau
.

By the change of variable
x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}-t\theta
, we have for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{L}[q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\theta,y^{\prime})\,e^{-iy^{\prime}\cdot\xi}\,dy^{\prime}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{ij}\,(t,\,y^{\prime}-t\theta,\,y_{3})\,dt\bigg)\,e^{-iy^{\prime}\cdot\xi}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q_{ij}\,(t,\,x^{\prime},\,y_{3})\,e^{-ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi}e^{-it\cdot\tau}dt\,dx^{\prime}\\
&=\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi).\end{split}

Since
\omega
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and supp
q_{ij}(t,\cdot,y_{3})\subset\bar{\omega}
. Hence there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\cap B(0,\lambda)}\mathcal{L}[q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\theta,y^{\prime})\,e^{-iy^{\prime}\cdot\xi}\,dy^{\prime}=\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi).

Taking into account the estimate (
5.16
) in Lemma
5.5
, we obtain the required estimate of
\widehat{q_{ij}}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})
.
∎

5.2.
Stability estimate

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For
\kappa>0
, we denote
B(0,\kappa)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x|<\kappa\}
. Our approach is similar to the one described in
[
1
]
for the scalar potential.
The following lemma will play a crucial role in our further analysis.

Lemma 5.7

(
[
38
,
49
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a non empty open set of
B(0,1)
, and let
F
be an analytic function in
B(0,2)
, obeying

\|\partial^{\gamma}F\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,2))}\leq\frac{M\,|\gamma|!}{\eta^{|\gamma|}},\quad\quad\forall\,\gamma\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{3},

for some
M>0
, and
\eta>0
. Then we have

\|\partial^{\gamma}F\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,1))}\leq(2M)^{1-\mu}\|\partial^{\gamma}F\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})},

where
\mu\in(0,1)
depends on
n,\eta
and
|\mathcal{O}|
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let us fix
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and for fixed
\alpha>0
, we define

F_{\alpha}[y_{3}](\tau,\xi):=\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\alpha(\tau,\xi)),~~(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Since we have assumed that
q_{ij}\equiv 0
outside
\Omega_{T}
, hence
q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
, therefore using the Paley-Wiener’s theorem, we have that
F_{\alpha}[y_{3}]
is analytic and
for
\gamma\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{3}
, we have that

\begin{split}|\partial^{\gamma}F_{\alpha}[y_{3}](\tau,\xi)|&=|\partial^{\gamma}\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\alpha(\tau,\xi))|\\
&=\Big|\partial^{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q_{ij}(t,x^{\prime},y_{3})e^{-i\alpha(t,x^{\prime})\cdot(\tau,\xi)}\,dx^{\prime}\,dt\Big|\\
&=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q_{ij}(t,x^{\prime},y_{3})(-i)^{|\gamma|}\alpha^{|\gamma|}(t,x^{\prime})^{\gamma}e^{-i\alpha(t,x^{\prime})\cdot(\tau,\xi)}\,dx^{\prime}\,dt\Big|,\end{split}

(5.20)

Now using the fact that
T>diam(\omega)
, we see that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\gamma}F_{\alpha}[y_{3}](\tau,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|q_{ij}(t,x^{\prime},y_{3})|\alpha^{|\gamma|}(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\|q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\omega)}\,\alpha^{|\gamma|}\,(2\,T^{2})^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|q_{ij}(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\omega)}\,\alpha^{|\gamma|}\,(2\,T^{2})^{\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|q_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{y_{3}};L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\omega))}\,\,\frac{|\gamma|!}{(T^{-1})^{|\gamma|}}e^{\alpha}\leq C\,T^{|\gamma|}|\gamma|!e^{\alpha}.

Setting
M=C\,e^{\alpha},\,\eta=T^{-1}
and
\mathcal{O}=E^{o}\cap B(0,1)
, where

E^{o}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}),\,\,|\tau|\,<|\xi|\Big\},

and subsequently, applying the Lemma
5.7
, we find a constant
\mu\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle|F_{\alpha}[y_{3}](\tau,\xi)|=|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\alpha(\tau,\xi))|\leq C\,e^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\|F_{\alpha}[y_{3}]\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})},\quad(\tau,\xi)\in B(0,1).

(5.21)

Since
\alpha E^{o}=\{\alpha(\tau,\xi):(\tau,\xi)\in E^{o}\}=E^{o}
, using (
5.21
) we deduce for
(\tau,\xi)\in B(0,\alpha)
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi)|=\big|F_{\alpha}[y_{3}](\alpha^{-1}(\tau,\xi))\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\,e^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\|F_{\alpha}[y_{3}]\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,e^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}\big(B(0,\alpha)\cap E^{o}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq C\,e^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}(E^{o})}.

This gives us the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,\alpha))}\leq C\,e^{\alpha(1-\mu)}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{\mu}_{L^{\infty}(E^{o})}.

(5.22)

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+\lvert(\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2})^{-1}\lvert\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
&=\int_{\lvert(\tau,\xi)\rvert<\alpha}(1+\lvert(\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2})^{-1}\lvert\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
&\ \ +\int_{\lvert(\tau,\xi)\rvert\geq\alpha}(1+\lvert(\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2})^{-1}\lvert\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})](\tau,\xi)\rvert^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
&\leq C\left(\alpha^{3}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B(0,\alpha))}+\alpha^{-2}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right).\end{aligned}

Now using the Plancherel theorem together with the boundedness of
q_{ij}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{3}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B(0,\alpha))}+\alpha^{-2}\|{q}_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\omega)}\bigg)^{1/\mu}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{3}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B(0,\alpha))}+\alpha^{-2}\bigg)^{1/\mu}.\end{aligned}

This after using equation (
5.22
) and (
5.19
) gives us

\begin{split}\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{3}\,e^{2\alpha(1-\mu)}\|\widehat{q_{ij}}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2\mu}_{L^{\infty}(E^{o})}+\alpha^{-2}\bigg)^{1/\mu}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{3}\,e^{2\alpha(1-\mu)}\left(\rho^{\beta}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|+\frac{1}{\rho^{\delta}}\right)^{2\mu}+\alpha^{-2}\bigg)^{1/\mu}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{\frac{3}{\mu}}\,e^{\frac{2\alpha(1-\mu)}{\mu}}\rho^{2\beta}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{2}+\alpha^{\frac{3}{\mu}}\,e^{\frac{2\alpha(1-\mu)}{\mu}}\frac{1}{\rho^{2\delta}}+\alpha^{-2/\mu}\bigg),\end{split}

where
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\alpha_{0}>0
be sufficiently large and consider
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
. We set
\alpha
in such a way that

\alpha^{\frac{3}{\mu}}\,e^{\frac{2\alpha(1-\mu)}{\mu}}\frac{1}{\rho^{2\delta}}=\alpha^{-2/\mu}.

As a consequence, we have

\rho=\alpha^{\frac{5}{2\mu\delta}}\,e^{\frac{\alpha(1-\mu)}{\mu\delta}}

and

\begin{split}\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}&\leq C\bigg(\alpha^{\frac{3\delta+5\beta}{\delta\mu}}\,e^{\frac{2\alpha(\delta+\beta)(1-\mu)}{\delta\mu}}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{2}+\alpha^{-2/\mu}\bigg),\qquad y_{3}\in\mathbb{R},\\
&\leq C\big(e^{N\alpha}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{2}+\alpha^{-2/\mu}\big),\qquad y_{3}\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

where
N
depends on
\delta,\beta
and
\mu
. Now for a fixed
\alpha>0,
large enough, we choose
0<c<1
such that

0<\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|<c\,

and

e^{N\alpha}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{2}+\alpha^{-2/\mu}\leq\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-1}.

Using this in (
5.2
), we get that

\displaystyle\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)}

\displaystyle=\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-2/\mu}\bigg)^{\mu/2},

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-1}\,\bigg),

(5.23)

where
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the right hand side of (
5.23
) is independent of
y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
, therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{L^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}_{y_{3}};H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)\big)}\leq C\bigg(\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-1}\,\bigg).

Also, if
\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|>c
, then we have

\displaystyle\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2CMc^{\mu/2}}{c^{\mu/2}}\leq\frac{2CM}{c^{\mu/2}}\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}\,,

hence (
5.23
) holds. Thus combining the above estimates, we get

\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{2/\mu}_{H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)}\leq C\bigg(\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-1}\,\bigg)

Now for
s>n/2
, using the Sobolev embedding theorem, we get that

\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\omega)}\leq C\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{H^{s}([0,T]\times\omega)}.

(5.24)

Finally using the Sobolev interpolation theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|_{H^{s}([0,T]\times\omega)}&\leq C\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{1-\beta}_{H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)}\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{\beta}_{H^{s+1}([0,T]\times\omega)}\\
&\leq C\|q_{ij}[(\cdot,\cdot,y_{3})]\|^{1-\beta}_{H^{-1}([0,T]\times\omega)},\end{aligned}

where the parameter
\beta\in(0,1)
and constant
C>0
independent of
q_{ij}
.
Combining this with
(
5.24
) and using (
5.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|q_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\bigg(\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|^{\mu/2}+\Big|\log\|\Lambda_{q^{(2)}}-\Lambda_{q^{(1)}}\|\Big|^{-1}\,\bigg).

Since the above estimate holds for every
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, we have demonstrated the required stability estimate. Hence the Theorem
3.1
follows.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
q^{(1)},q^{(2)}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{T})
, with

and let matrix potential

\begin{split}\biggr|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(q(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})&\overrightarrow{K}\cdot\overrightarrow{K}^{(*)}\Big)\varphi^{2}(x^{\prime}+t\theta)h^{2}(x_{3})\,dt\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{3}\biggr|\\

(5.25)

for any

Proof of Theorem

Sign pattern matrices associated with cycle graphs that require algebraic positivity
Sunil Das

Abstract
A real matrix is said to be positive if its every entry is positive, and a real square matrix A A is algebraically positive if there exists a real polynomial f f such that f ⁡ ( A ) f(A) is a positive matrix. A sign pattern matrix A A is said to require a property if all matrices having sign pattern as A A have that property. In this paper, we characterize all sign pattern matrices associated with cycle graphs that require algebraic positivity.



Keywords:

Algebraically positive, Sign pattern, Require algebraic positivity, Sign non-singular.

AMS Subject Classifications:

15B35, 15B48, 05C50.

Introduction

A real matrix is said to be positive if its every entry is positive. Positive matrices has applications in population models, Markov chains, economic model, low-dimensional topology, epidemiology etc. (see
[
9
]
). A generalization of positive matrices was given by Kirkland, Qiao, and Zhan as algebraically positive matrices. A real square matrix
A
is algebraically positive if there exists a real polynomial
f
such that
f(A)
is a positive matrix. The characterization of algebraically positive matrices was given by the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

A real square matrix is algebraically positive if and only if it has a simple real eigenvalue and corresponding left and right positive eigenvectors.

We are interested in answering the problem of identifying an algebraically positive matrix from the signs its entries. A sign pattern matrix is a matrix whose every entry is a symbol from the set
\{+,-,0\}
. The qualitative class of a matrix
A
, denoted by
Q(A)
, is defined by the set of all real matrices obtained from
A
by replacing
‘
+
’
by some positive number,
‘
-
’
by some negative number and
‘0’
by the zero number. A sign pattern matrix
A
allows a property
P
if at least one matrix in
Q(A)
has the property
P
, and requires property
P
if all matrices in
Q(A)
have the property
P
. For our convenience, we use the symbols
+_{0},-_{0},\#
in sign pattern matrices. Any entry associated
+_{0},-_{0},\#
will be considered as a nonnegative, a non-positive, and an arbitrary real number, respectively.

To represent and analyze sign patterns efficiently, we use concepts of graphs. Let us recall from
[
2
,
6
]
that the digraph of a sign pattern matrix
A
of order
n
, denoted by
D(A)
, is defined by a directed graph with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
(\overrightarrow{i,j})
is an arc of
D(A)
if and only if
a_{ij}\neq 0
. The graph of a sign pattern matrix
A
of order
n
, denoted by
G(A)
, is defined by an undirected graph with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
[i,j]
is an edge of
G(A)
if and only if either
a_{ij}\neq 0
or
a_{ji}\neq 0
.

A directed path
\alpha
from vertex
u
to vertex
v
in
D(A)
is a sequence
(u=i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=v)
of distinct vertices such that
(\overrightarrow{i_{t-1},i_{t}})
is an arc in
D(A)
for
t=1,2,\ldots,k
. If all vertices are not distinct, then it is called a directed walk. The length of a directed path is the number of arcs lying on the path. Here length of the directed path
\alpha
is
k
. Sign of the path
\alpha
is defined by
\operatorname{sign}(\alpha)=\operatorname{sign}(a_{i_{0}i_{1}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k-1}i_{k}})
. A directed cycle
\gamma
of length
k
or a directed

-cycle in

is a sequence
(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k},i_{1})
of vertices such that

are distinct and
(\overrightarrow{i_{1},i_{2}}),(\overrightarrow{i_{2},i_{3}}),\ldots,(\overrightarrow{i_{k},i_{1}})
are arcs in

. Sign of the directed cycle

is defined by
\operatorname{sign}(\gamma)=\operatorname{sign}(a_{i_{1}i_{2}}a_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots a_{i_{k}i_{1}})
. A square matrix

is said to be irreducible if

is strongly connected, that is, for any ordered pair
(i,j)
of vertices there is a directed path from
i
to
j
in
D(A)
. In

, an (undirected) cycle of length

is a sequence
(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k},i_{1})
of vertices such that
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}
are distinct and
[i_{1},i_{2}],[i_{2},i_{3}],\ldots,[i_{k},i_{1}]
are edges in

.

Characterization of sign patterns allowing algebraic positivity is still not solved completely, but many results to identify those patterns can be observed in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
7
]
. The problem of identifying sign patterns requiring algebraically positivity is widely open. Path and star sign patterns requiring algebraic positivity are described in
[
4
]
, and list of all 3-by-3 sign patterns requiring algebraic positivity are presented in
[
1
]
. The graphs of 3-by-3 tree sign pattern matrices are either stars or paths. For any other 3-by-3 irreducible sign pattern matrix, the underlying undirected graph is a cycle. In this paper, we attempt to generalize the problem of requiring algebraic positivity for cycle graphs. In the process, we also give some results in identifying matrices which are not algebraically positive and whose underlying graph is an arbitrary graph.

In Section
2
, we provide some necessary conditions for a sign pattern matrix to require algebraic positivity. In Section
3
, we identify all sign patterns having cycle graphs that require algebraic positivity. Finally, we summerize all main results in Section
4
.

Identifying sign patterns that does not require algebraic positivity

The following lemma from
[
5
, pp. 78-80]
gives a relation between left and right eigenvectors corresponding to two distinct eigenvalues of a matrix.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
A
be a real square matrix and
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
such that
A\bar{x}=\lambda\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}^{T}A=\mu\bar{y}^{T}
for some
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

1.

If
\lambda\neq\mu
, then
\bar{y}^{T}\bar{x}=0
.

2.

If
\lambda=\mu
and
\lambda
has geometric multiplicity 1, then its algebraic multiplicity is 1 if and only if
\bar{y}^{T}\bar{x}\neq 0
.

The following lemma shows that any non-negative left or right eigenvector of an algebraically positive matrix must be a scalar multiple of its left or right positive eigenvector, whose existence is given by Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If a matrix
A
has a non-negative left or right eigenvector
\bar{x}
with some
x_{i}=0
, then
A
is not algebraically positive.

Proof.

Suppose that
A\bar{x}=\lambda\bar{x}
, where
\bar{x}
is non-negative right eigenvector of
A
with some
x_{i}=0
. If
A
is algebraically positive, then by Theorem
1.1
, it has a simple eigenvalue
\mu
such that
A\bar{z}=\mu\bar{z}
and
\bar{y}^{T}A=\mu\bar{y}^{T}
for some positive vectors
\bar{y}
and
\bar{z}
. Since
\bar{x}
and
\bar{z}
are independent, and
\mu
is simple eigenvalue,
\lambda\neq\mu
. But
\bar{y}^{T}\bar{x}\neq 0
, a contradiction to Lemma
2.1
.1. Therefore
A
is not algebraically positive. Similarly, if
A
has a non-negative left eigenvector
\bar{x}
with some
x_{i}=0
, then
A
is not algebraically positive.
∎

Let

be the matrix obtained from

Proposition 2.3

Let

such that each column of the matrix

is either a zero vector or has both positive and negative entries, then

Proof.

Suppose that each column of the matrix
(B-\lambda I)(t,:)
is either a zero vector or has both positive and negative entries. Keeping
\lambda

in such a way that each column sum of
(B-\lambda I)(t,:)
is zero so that
\bar{x}^{T}B=\lambda\bar{x}^{T}
, where

x_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }i\neq t;\\

So by Lemma
2.2

is not algebraically positive, and thus

does not require algebraic positivity.
∎

0&\cdots&0&-&-_{0}\end{bmatrix}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\begin{bmatrix}\#&+&0&\cdots&0\\
+&\#&+&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&+&\#&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&+\\
0&\cdots&0&+&\#\end{bmatrix}.

Lemma 3.17
.

If
B\bar{x}=\bar{0}
for some
B
of Type-II with non-negative diagonal and a positive vector
\bar{x}
, and
\alpha>0
such that
B-\alpha I
has negative diagonal, then there exists a positive vector
\bar{y}
such that the last entry of
(B-\alpha I)\bar{y}
is negative and all other entries are 0.

Proof.

We may assume that
B
has the form

B=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1}&b_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{1}&d_{2}&b_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&a_{2}&d_{3}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&b_{n-1}\\
0&\cdots&0&a_{n-1}&d_{n}\end{bmatrix}

such that all
a_{i},b_{i}
are positive, and all
d_{i}
are non-negative. Further,
d_{i}-\alpha<0
for all
i
.

Consider
y_{1}=x_{1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle d_{1}x_{1}+b_{1}x_{2}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(d_{1}-\alpha)y_{1}+b_{1}y_{2}=0\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{2}=x_{2}+\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{b_{1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{2}=x_{2}+\epsilon_{2},\hskip 56.9055pt\epsilon_{2}>0;

\displaystyle a_{1}x_{1}+d_{2}x_{2}+b_{2}x_{3}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{1}y_{1}+(d_{2}-\alpha)y_{2}+b_{2}y_{3}=0\Rightarrow

\displaystyle-d_{2}x_{2}-b_{2}x_{3}+(d_{2}-\alpha)(x_{2}+\epsilon_{2})+b_{2}y_{3}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{3}=x_{3}+\frac{\alpha x_{2}}{b_{2}}-\frac{(d_{2}-\alpha)\epsilon_{2}}{b_{2}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{3}=x_{3}+\epsilon_{3},\hskip 56.9055pt\epsilon_{3}>0;

\displaystyle a_{2}x_{2}+d_{3}x_{3}+b_{3}x_{4}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{2}y_{2}+(d_{3}-\alpha)y_{3}+b_{3}y_{4}=0\Rightarrow

\displaystyle-d_{3}x_{3}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{x_{2}}\right)-b_{3}x_{4}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{x_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+(d_{3}-\alpha)(x_{3}+\epsilon_{3})+b_{3}y_{4}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{4}=x_{4}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{x_{2}}\right)+\frac{d_{3}x_{3}\epsilon_{2}}{b_{3}x_{2}}+\frac{\alpha x_{3}}{b_{3}}-\frac{(d_{3}-\alpha)\epsilon_{3}}{b_{3}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle y_{4}=x_{4}+\epsilon_{4},\hskip 56.9055pt\epsilon_{4}>0;

\displaystyle y_{n}=x_{n}+\epsilon_{n},\hskip 56.9055pt\epsilon_{n}>0.

Since
a_{n-1}x_{n-1}+d_{n}x_{n}=0
,

\displaystyle a_{n-1}y_{n-1}+(d_{n}-\alpha)y_{n}=-d_{n}x_{n}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{n-1}}{x_{n-1}}\right)+(d_{n}-\alpha)(x_{n}+\epsilon_{n})=-\frac{d_{n}x_{n}\epsilon_{n-1}}{x_{n-1}}-\alpha x_{n}+(d_{n}-\alpha)\epsilon_{n}<0.

Hence the result follows.
∎

The following result characterizes sign pattern matrices with cycle graphs and both positive and negative 2-cycles that require algebraic positivity.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
A
be a sign pattern matrix of the form (
1
) such that
D(A)
contains both positive and negative 2-cycles. Then
A
requires algebraic positivity if and only if the following conditions hold:

1.

a_{1n}=0
and
a_{ij}=+
for
j=i+1\mod n
.

2.

Either
A
has a principal submatrix of the form

\begin{bmatrix}+_{0}&+\\
+&\#\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\begin{bmatrix}\#&+\\
+&+_{0}\end{bmatrix},

or
A
has a principal submatrix of the form

\begin{bmatrix}-_{0}&+&0&\cdots&0\\
-&-_{0}&+&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&-&-_{0}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&+\\
0&\cdots&0&-&-_{0}\end{bmatrix}

requiring singularity such that it is not a principal submatrix of any other principal submatrix of
A
having the same form.

Proof.

We assume that
A
is a sign pattern matrix of the form (
1
) such that
D(A)
contains both positive and negative 2-cycles.

To show that the conditions are necessary:

Suppose that
A
requires algebraic positivity. Then using Lemma
3.3
, we can conclude that
a_{1n}=0
and
a_{ij}=+
for
j=i+1\mod n
. So we can partition
A
in the form

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}&O&\cdots&O\\
O&A_{22}&A_{23}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&O\\
O&\cdots&O&\ddots&A_{k-1,k}\\
A_{k1}&O&\cdots&O&A_{kk}\end{bmatrix},

(4)

where each
A_{ii}
is of either Type-I or Type-II.

Suppose that conditions 1 and 2 are not satisfied. Then the diagonal entries of all
A_{ii}
of Type-II are
-
and no
A_{ii}
of Type-I requires singularity. So each
A_{ii}
is potentially stable (see
[
2
, Theorem 10.2.2]
). Therefore
A
is potentially stable.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
A_{11}
is of Type-I. Suppose that
A_{11}
is of order
r
. By Lemma
3.4
,
a_{11}=-_{0}
and
a_{rr}=-_{0}
.

1.

If either
a_{11}=0
or
a_{rr}=0
, then by Proposition
2.7
,
A
does not require algebraic positivity, a contradiction.

2.

If
a_{11}=-
and
a_{rr}=-
, then we can choose
C\in Q(A)
and
\alpha\geq 0
such that each column of
(C+\alpha I)(1,:)
has both positive and negative entries. So by Proposition
2.3
,
A
does not require algebraic positivity, a contradiction.

Therefore conditions 1 and 2 are necessary.

To show that the conditions are sufficient:

Since
a_{1n}=0
and
a_{ij}=+
for
j=i+1\mod n
, we can partition
A
as in (
4
) so that each of
A_{12},A_{23},\ldots,A_{k-1,k},A_{k1}
has exactly one nonzero entry
+
at the bottom left corner of that matrix.

Let
C\in Q(A)
, and it be partitioned as
A
. If
p(x),p_{1}(x),p_{2}(x),\ldots,p_{k}(x)
are the characteristic polynomials of
C,C_{11},C_{22},\ldots,C_{kk}
, respectively, then
p(x)=p_{1}(x)p_{2}(x)\cdots p_{k}(x)-t
, where

Case-I.

A
has a principal submatrix of the form

\begin{bmatrix}+_{0}&+\\
+&\#\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\begin{bmatrix}\#&+\\
+&+_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Because of Lemma
2.6
.4, we can assume that some

C

Let

Due to Lemma

After removing

Summary

As we have considered that
G(A)
is a cycle, there are no directed
k
-cycles in

for

and
A
is of the form (

). Lemma
3.2
shows that a sign pattern matrix of the form (

n

Proposal for fast computational method for Hertzian contact theory
Shintaro Hokada, Shunsuke Iizuka, Satoshi Takada

Abstract
Fast computational method for Hertzian contact theory is proposed. An incremental formula is introduced to calculate the ellipticity of the contact disk when two elastic bodies are in contact. This method can determine the ellipticity with good accuracy in a small number of iterations is reported. This method is also shown to be applicable from the case of a near perfect circle to the case where the major diameter is sufficiently long compared to the minor diameter.



I
Introduction

Contact problems between two objects with curvature appear in various fields such as applied physics or engineering such as tribology or powder technology.
Examples in tribology include contact between bearing balls and raceways, or between railroad wheels and rails
Harris91
.
In powder technology, it is contact between powder particles
Brilliantov96
;
Luding08
.

In general, the contact between two objects with constant curvatures can be divided into three situations depending on the curvatures of the two contacting objects, as shown in Fig.
1
.
Hertzian contact theory can calculate the stresses that occur in these situations
Hertz81
.
This theory treats the stress distribution from the degree of deformation of an object by assuming that the forces are balanced at each point in time when contact occurs.

Figure 1:
Schematics of contacting two bodies.
Two bodies have (a) both positive, (b) positive and zero, and (c) positive and negative curvatures.
Shaded regions represent contact ellipses.

The contact surface predicted by the Hertzian contact theory is generally an ellipse, as can be seen in Fig.
1
.
Its shape and stress distribution can be calculated using the complete elliptic integrals.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the ellipticity of such ellipses is not known to be solved analytically.
Therefore, it is generally necessary to rely on numerical calculations.
Various efforts have been made to do so, and indeed, various papers have proposed methods.

The organization of this paper is as follows:
In the next section, we briefly overview the Hertzian contact theory, and introduce previous works relating to this topic.
Section
III
proposes faster method to determine the shape of contact area, then Sect.
IV
validates its efficiency.
In Sect.
V
, we summarize our results.

II
Formulation and previous methods

This section provides a brief overview of Hertzian contact theory
Hertz81
and an introduction to previous methods describing numerical calculations of the theory
Hamrock73
;
Hamrock83
;
Oba14
.

Schematic of two contacting elastic bodies.
Two bodies are contacted with the force
P
.
The
x
and
y
-axes are chosen so that the curvature ratio
\alpha=\rho_{x}/\rho_{y}\geq 1
.

As described in the previous section, the contact, as shown in Fig.
1
, depends on the curvatures of the two objects.
However, we here generalize them and consider a situation where two objects are in contact as shown in Fig.
2
.
In this paper, we put the Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio of the two objects as
E_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
(
i=1,2
), respectively.
Let
\rho_{1,x}
,
\rho_{1,y}
and
\rho_{2,x}
,
\rho_{2,y}
be the curvatures of objects 1 and 2 in the
x
and
y
axes near the contact point.
In this case, the equivalent curvature
\rho_{x}
and
\rho_{y}
determined from the two objects are, respectively, defined by

\rho_{x}=\rho_{1,x}+\rho_{2,x},\quad\rho_{y}=\rho_{1,y}+\rho_{2,y}.

(1)

The
x
-axis and the
x
-axis are written as follows.
However, the
x
and
y
axes are defined to be
\rho_{x}/\rho_{y}\geq 1
.
Using these, the equivalent curvature sum
\rho_{+}
and equivalent curvature difference
\rho_{-}
can be expressed as

\rho_{+}\equiv\rho_{x}+\rho_{y},\quad\rho_{-}\equiv\frac{\rho_{x}-\rho_{y}}{\rho_{+}},

(2)

and the equivalent curvature ratio
\alpha
is introduced by

\alpha\equiv\frac{\rho_{x}}{\rho_{y}}=\frac{\rho_{-}+1}{\rho_{-}-1}.

(3)

Let
\ell_{x}
and
\ell_{y}
be the radius ratios in the
x
and
y
axes of the contact ellipses of the two objects in contact, respectively, and we put this ratio as

\lambda\equiv\frac{\ell_{y}}{\ell_{x}}.

(4)

In this case, the following relation holds between the radius ratio
\lambda
and the equivalent curvature ratio
\rho_{-}

Harris91
:

\lambda^{2}=\frac{2K(\nu)-E(\nu)(1+\rho_{-})}{E(\nu)(1-\rho_{-})},

(5)

with

\nu\equiv 1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}.

(6)

Here,
K(\nu)
and
E(\nu)
are complete elliptic integrals of the first and second kind, respectively
Abramowitz

K(\nu)\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\sqrt{1-\nu\sin^{2}\theta}},\quad E(\nu)\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-\nu\sin^{2}\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(7)

Note that
K(\nu)
is written as
F(\nu)
and
k=\sqrt{\nu}
in some literature
Hamrock83
;
Oba14
, although
\nu
is a parameter satisfying
0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
In this paper, we follow the notation adopted in Mathematica and other software
Abramowitz
.

Usually, the curvatures near the point of contact between two contacting objects are known.
This means that, given
\rho_{-}
, it is required to calculate the value of
\lambda
by solving Eq. (
5
), while it is not easy to solve this equation because
\nu
appearing on the right side of Eq. (
5
) is a quantity determined by
\lambda
.
However, once
\lambda
is obtained,
\nu
is also determined from Eq. (
6
).
As a result, the contact ellipse radii
\ell_{x}
and
\ell_{y}
for a given contact load
P
are

\ell_{x}=\left(\frac{3E(\nu)P}{\pi\lambda E_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_{+}}\right)^{1/3},\quad\ell_{y}=\left(\frac{3\lambda^{2}E(\nu)P}{\pi E_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_{+}}\right)^{1/3}=\lambda\ell_{x},

(8)

where

E_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv\left(\frac{1-\nu_{1}^{2}}{E_{1}}+\frac{1-\nu_{2}^{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{-1},

(9)

is the equivalent Young’s modulus determined from two objects.
The surface pressure distribution
\sigma(x,y)
in the contact ellipse is given by

\sigma(x,y)=\frac{3P}{2\pi\ell_{x}\ell_{y}}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{x}{\ell_{x}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{y}{\ell_{y}}\right)^{2}},

(10)

and also for the maximum value of elastic deformation
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}
as

\delta_{\mathrm{max}}=\frac{K(\nu)}{2}\left[\frac{9\rho_{+}}{E(\nu)}\left(\frac{P}{\pi\lambda E_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/3}.

(11)

The problem here, however, is that the expression (
5
) cannot be solved explicitly for
\lambda
, as mentioned in the previous section.
The quantities
\rho_{-}
and
\alpha
determined from the curvatures of the two objects are known quantities, while the ratio of the ellipses at the contact surface
\lambda
is unknown.
However, the expression (
5
) is a formula where
\alpha
appears when
\lambda
is determined, and the dependence of
\lambda
on
\alpha
cannot be determined in this way.
One way to solve this problem is to transform the formula (
5
)

\alpha=\frac{\lambda^{2}E(\nu)-K(\nu)}{K(\nu)-E(\nu)},

(12)

and substitute various
\lambda
into it to make a table of
\alpha
numbers corresponding to each
\lambda
.
This is done by selecting
(\alpha_{1},\lambda_{1})
and
(\alpha_{2},\lambda_{2})
pairs of
\alpha
and corresponding
\lambda
that are close in value in the table, given
\alpha
for the problem, and then using an appropriate method to calculate
(\alpha,\ lambda)
in an appropriate way.
However, this method has a disadvantage in that it requires a large amount of computational storage space because it is necessary to calculate values for a large number of
\lambda
in advance when high accuracy is required.

As a way to compensate for this shortcoming, Hamrock and Anderson
Hamrock73
rewrote Eq. (
12
) as

\lambda=f(\lambda,\alpha),

(13)

and transformed it into the form of an asymptotic equation by letting
\lambda
on the left side of this equation be
\lambda_{n+1}
and
\lambda
on the right side be
\lambda_{n}
.
They proposed a method to compute the value by iteratively computing this iteration until the absolute error
|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|
or relative error
|\lambda_{n+1}/\lambda_{n}-1|
is sufficiently small.
However, the iterative computation is time-consuming.
To solve this problem, Hamrock and Brewe
Hamrock83
proposed a one-time computation without iterations by carefully choosing the trial function
\lambda(\alpha)
to be assigned to the right-hand side of Eq. (
13
).
However, even in the range of
1\leq\alpha\leq 10
, which is a subject of wide interest, it contains an error of about
10~\%
, which is not satisfactory for calculations that require accuracy.

In contrast, Oba
Oba14
proposed an improved version of the trial function proposed by Hamrock and Anderson
Hamrock73
and used it to obtain
\alpha
from Eq. (
12
), and substituting it into the trial function.
His method achieves an accuracy of about
10^{-10}
with a small number of iterations (3 iterations), and is faster than conventional methods.

III
Proposed method

We improve on Oba’s work
Oba14
and propose a method that produces the same level of performance with fewer iterations.
First, let us discuss the computation of elliptic integrals which appears in Eq. (
12
).
Some previous literature
Hamrock83
;
Oba14
introduced the fast computational method of elliptic integrals.
However, most programming languages have libraries that can compute elliptic integrals, and it is expected to be faster than writing the code by oneself.
Therefore, in this paper, we do not discuss this issue, but assume that a suitable library is available to compute elliptic integrals.
However, if one’s environment requires the computation of elliptic integrals, one can use Hastings formulas
Fukushima09
;
Oba14
or other formulas
Whittaker
with appropriate degree termination.

We first describe how to determine
\nu
that satisfies Eq. (
12
) for
\alpha
.
This is because
\lambda
is also determined when
\nu
is determined from Eq. (
6
).
Using Eq. (
6
), Eq. (
12
) can be expressed as

F(\nu)\equiv(\alpha+1)(1-\nu)K(\nu)-[1+\alpha(1-\nu)]E(\nu)=0.

(14)

Then
\nu
is a solution satisfying
0\leq\nu\leq 1
with
F(\nu)=0
.
Figure
3
shows the graphs of
F(\nu)
for
\alpha=1
,
3
, and
10
.
It can be seen that
\alpha=1
is a monotonically decreasing function and only
\nu=0
satisfies
F(\nu)=0
, whereas
\nu=0
and
0<\nu<1
exist for
\alpha>1
.
However, since
\nu=0
is the case where the elliptic integrals and the corresponding elliptic functions are attributed to trigonometric functions, the appropriate solution for
\alpha>1
is the one satisfying
0<\nu\leq 1
.
This solution approaches
\nu\to 1
as
\alpha
increases.
In other words,
\lambda
increases from Eq. (
6
).

Figure 3:
Plots of
F(\nu)
against
\nu
for
\alpha=1
(dotted line),
3
(dashed line), and
10
(solid line).

The solution is obtained by an iterative method.
Here, we adopt Bailey’s method, which converges more quickly than Newton’s method
Traub61
.
First, an appropriate
\nu_{0}
is determined.
Next, given
\nu_{n}
, we can get
\nu_{n+1}
by the Bailey method

\nu_{n+1}=\nu_{n}-\frac{F(\nu_{n})}{F^{\prime}(\nu_{n})-\dfrac{F(\nu_{n})F^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{n})}{2F^{\prime}(\nu_{n})}},

(15)

where

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{-(1+2\alpha)K(\nu)+3\alpha E(\nu)}{2},

(16a)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\nu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-\nu)(\alpha-1)K(\nu)+[1-\alpha(1-3\nu)]E(\nu)}{4\nu(1-\nu)}.

(16b)

In Bailey’s method, it is necessary to start the calculation from a good initial condition
\nu_{0}
, which is somewhat close to a true value.
This is because if
\alpha>1
, starting from
\nu_{0}
in the region
F^{\prime}(\nu)>0
, the calculation will converge to
\nu=0
.
In order to speed up the convergence, it is necessary to start as close as possible to the true solution.

For the above purposes, we set up an approximate formula for the relationship between
\alpha
and
\nu
and start the Bailey method with that value.
However, when we plot the relationship between
\alpha
and
\nu
from Eq. (
12
), the change is large in
\nu\simeq 0
and
1
.
Therefore, we use an equation to approximate the
\alpha
dependence of
\lambda
.
In this study, we adopt the trial function proposed by Ref.
Oba14
:

\log\lambda_{0}=\log\alpha\cdot\left[c_{1}+c_{2}(\log\alpha)^{1/2}+c_{3}(\log\alpha)^{3/4}+c_{4}(\log\alpha)\right],

(17)

where the coefficients

c_{1}=0.666947681,\ c_{2}=0.082186314,\ c_{3}=0.109262836,\ c_{4}=0.028024325,

(18)

are the same as those given in Ref.
Oba14
.

Summarizing the above, we propose the following procedure for the actual computation

1.

Calculate
\lambda_{0}
from Eq. (
17
) for
\alpha
to be calculated and obtain
\nu_{0}=1-1/\lambda_{0}^{2}
.

2.

Calculate
\nu_{n+1}
by substituting
\nu_{n}
(
n=0,1,\cdots
) into Eq. (
15
).

3.

Repeat (ii), until
|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_{n}|<\delta_{\mathrm{th}}
is satisfied for a certain threshold
\delta_{\mathrm{th}}
.

IV
Results

This section presents the main results obtained using the method described in the previous section.
In order to see the convergence of the results, we regard
\nu_{n}
as a true value when
|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_{n}|<1.0\times 10^{-15}
is satisfied, and denote it as
\nu_{\infty}
.
Figure
4
plots the relative error
|\nu_{n}/\nu_{\infty}-1|
from the true value for each iteration
n
for each
\alpha
.

Figure 4:
Convergence of
\nu_{n}
to
\nu_{\infty}
against
\alpha

0

1
, and
2

The results show that the error is less than
10^{-12}
in two iterations for
10^{-3}\leq\alpha\leq 10^{6}
.
If the error tolerance can be relaxed to
10^{-6}
, only one interation is needed.
The previous paper
Oba14
reported that when the parameter
\nu
is close to
0
, which means that the contact ellipse becomes an almost perfect circle, the convergence is deteriorated due to the drop of digits caused by the small values of the denominator and numerator of the asymptotic equation.
In this case, a different formula that is a variant of the asymptotic formula was used.
In contrast, the new method does not have such a case separation.
This is another advantage of this method.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we discuss a fast method for computing the elliptic integrals that appear when calculating the shape of the contact ellipse and the stress distribution at the contact between two elastic bodies.
The method of Oba
Oba14
is modified, and the iteration equation is given for the parameter
\nu
of the ellipse.
By solving it with Bailey’s method, we proposed a method in which the error relative to the exact value is less than
10^{-12}
in two iterations.
When the threshold is enough to be
10^{-6}
, this method is sufficient for a single calculation, and it is also practical for calculations at each time point.

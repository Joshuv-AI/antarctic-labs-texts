Bounds for spectral projectors on the three-dimensional torus
Pierre Germain 1, Simon L. Rydin Myerson 2, Daniel Pezzi 3

Abstract
. We study L 2 L^{2} to L p L^{p} operator norms of spectral projectors for the Euclidean Laplacian on the torus in the case where the spectral window is narrow. With a window of constant size this is a classical result of Sogge; in the small-window limit we are left with L p L^{p} norms of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian, as considered for instance by Bourgain. For the three-dimensional torus we prove new cases of a previous conjecture of the first two authors concerning the size of these norms; we also refine certain prior results to remove ϵ \epsilon -losses in all dimensions. We use methods from number theory: the geometry of numbers, the circle method and exponential sum bounds due to Guo. We complement these techniques with height splitting and a bilinear argument to prove sharp results. We exposit on the various techniques used and their limitations.



1.
Introduction

Our starting point is the Laplacian
\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}
on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.

For a positive definite quadratic form

Q(x)=x^{T}Ax,\qquad\mbox{with $A$ symmetric},

this example can be generalized to operators of the form
\Delta_{Q}=Q(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})
, still defined on
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
(equivalently, we could have kept the Laplacian fixed and considered general tori of the form
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of full rank).

We refer to the case
A=\operatorname{Id}
,
Q({x})=\|{x}\|^{2}
, or
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, which are all equivalent, as the
square torus
.

1.1.
The conjectures in dimension 3

We consider
\Delta_{Q}=Q(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, and define by functional calculus the spectral projectors

P_{\lambda,\delta}=\mathbf{1}_{(-1,1)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{-\Delta_{Q}}-2\pi\lambda}{{2\pi}\delta}\right),

where
\delta\in(0,1)
is small and
\lambda>1
is large (the factors
{2\pi}
are added in the above definition to make subsequent notations simpler). In other words,

P_{\lambda,\delta}=\Phi_{\lambda,\delta}*\cdot\quad\mbox{with}\quad\Phi_{\lambda,\delta}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
|\sqrt{Q(k)}-\lambda|<\delta\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}.

By duality and
TT^{*}
,

(1.1)

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}^{2}=\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}=\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{2}}^{2}.

The following conjecture appeared in
[
11
]
.

Conjecture A
.

If
\lambda^{-1}\leq\delta\leq 1
and
p\geq 2
, then we have

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim_{Q,p}(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}+\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}},

except in certain boundary cases (see below). In other words

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim_{Q,p}\begin{cases}\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}&\begin{aligned} &\text{if }4\leq p\leq 6\text{ and }\delta\geq\lambda^{2-\frac{p}{2}};\\
&\text{or }6\leq p\leq\infty\text{ and }\delta\geq\lambda^{-1},\end{aligned}\\
(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}&\begin{aligned} &\text{if }2\leq p\leq 4\text{ and }\delta\geq\lambda^{-1};\\
&\text{or }4\leq p\leq 6\text{ and }\lambda^{-1}\leq\delta\leq\lambda^{2-\frac{p}{2}}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

For some values of
Q,p,\lambda,\delta
a subpolynomial loss (“
\epsilon
- loss”) should be added to the above conjecture. This is at least the case for
p=\infty
,
\delta=\lambda^{-1}
. The statement becomes then

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\left[(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}+\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

If this holds, we say that the conjecture holds with
\epsilon
-loss.

The conjecture bounds the operator norm by a sum of two terms, namely
(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}
and
\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This has the following geometric interpretation, see
[
11
]
for more details. The term
(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}
corresponds to the
L^{p}
norm of a quasimode

is such that
P_{\lambda,\delta}f=f
, it is thus nearly an eigenfunction.
normalized in
L^{2}
which concentrates at a point; and the term
\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
corresponds to the
L^{p}
norm of a quasimode normalized in
L^{2}
which concentrates on a closed geodesic. Therefore, we refer to the regions
(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}<\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}>\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
as the

and
geodesic-focusing regime

Let
\lambda_{0}>1
. Those eigenvalues
\lambda^{2}
of the Laplacian
-\Delta
on the square torus which have size
\sim\lambda_{0}

\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}

Conjecture B
.

If
u

-\Delta
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}

\lambda^{2}

\|u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}+\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}}&\mbox{if $p\geq 6$}\\
\lambda^{\epsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}}&\mbox{if $p\leq 6$}\end{cases}

(The subpolynomial loss
\lambda^{\epsilon}
might be superfluous if

Regarding Conjecture

New results

Our main result is as follows.

(Square torus)
.

We consider the square torus, and for some

(1.2)

\delta>\begin{cases}\lambda^{-1+\kappa}&2\leq p\leq 4,\\
\lambda^{-\frac{316-27p}{104(p-2)}+\kappa}&4<p\leq\frac{235}{52},\\

The proof of this theorem is reached in Section

Theorem 1.2

(General torus)

The conclusion of

1.4.
Organization of the paper

Section
3
aims to improve bounds on the number of caps containing many lattice points. Here, caps refer to a collection of rectangular boxes with dimensions
\sim\delta\times\sqrt{\lambda\delta}\times\sqrt{\lambda\delta}
which provide an almost disjoint covering of the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\lambda-\delta<|\xi|<\lambda+\delta\}
. These improved bounds are obtained by methods of the geometry of numbers.

Section
4
relies on the application of the
\ell^{2}
-decoupling estimate of Bourgain and Demeter
[
5
]
, which gives an equivalent of orthogonality in
L^{p}
for sums of functions supported on distinct caps in Fourier space. When applying this estimate, it is advantageous to bound the number of caps containing more points than average, which is possible with the help of the previous section.

Section
5
focuses on the application of three-dimensional exponential sums
[
13
,
17
]
to our problem, following Demeter-Germain
[
9
]
who considered the case of dimension 2. We follow the idea of Stein-Tomas
[
21
]
: decomposing
P_{\lambda,\delta}
into elementary operators, each of which is estimated by interpolating between elementary
L^{2}\to L^{2}
and
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
bounds. This decomposition is achieved after writing out the kernel of
P_{\lambda,\delta}
, applying Poisson summation, and then splitting the corresponding sum dyadically.

Section
6
also adapts the of Stein-Tomas approach, but this time the decomposition is achieved through the circle method. This was first proposed by Bourgain
[
2
]
who focused on dimensions
\geq 4
.

Section
7
applies the idea of height splitting in the point-focusing regime (to be more specific, it deals with the operator
P_{\operatorname{few}}
, which will be defined later and is dominant in the point-focusing regime). The idea of height splitting is to distinguish subsets of the torus where
P_{\lambda,\delta}f
is bigger or smaller than some threshold, and use different approaches on each one. It follows
[
1
]
and
[
18
]
and allows the removal of the
\lambda^{\epsilon}
loss which is automatically incurred when applying the
\ell^{2}
decoupling estimate from Section
3
.

Section
8
applies ideas from bilinear restriction theory in the geodesic-focusing regime (to be more specific, it deals with the operator
P_{\operatorname{many}}
, which will be defined later and is dominant in the geodesic-focusing regime). Here, too, this eliminates subpolynomial losses.

Section
9
combines the results obtained in previous sections to prove the main theorem.

Section
10
examines the implications of the methods developed here in dimensions
d\neq 3
.

Section
A
of the appendix, finally, sketches yet another approach, through additive combinatorics; we do not pursue this line of investigation here.

1.5.
Limitations of the different methods

Decoupling and cap counting
are only useful in our hands if
\delta>\min(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3})
. Indeed, in the complement of this region, the leading contribution to the operator norm is expected to come from caps containing a single lattice point, but the decoupling estimates applied to these caps do not suffice to prove the conjecture.

Three-dimensional exponential sums estimates
are useful on general tori. Bounds on such sums allow us to prove the conjecture in a range which lies beyond the critical line
\delta=\lambda^{-1/2}
. Any progress on these bounds would lead to progress on the conjecture.

The circle method
does not seem effective on arbitrary tori; it also seems to be limited to the range
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Beyond this range, the minor arcs become dominant as far as pointwise estimates go, and the corresponding estimate is too weak to prove the conjecture. A better understanding of cancellations in Kloosterman sums might be key to push this method further.

The height splitting argument
allows us to remove
O_{\epsilon}(\lambda^{\epsilon})
losses in the estimates in the point-focusing regime. It also allows us to extend the validity of the conjecture slightly in the geodesic focusing regime, but it is dependent on the exponential sum estimates used in
Section

5
.

The bilinear argument
allows us to remove
O_{\epsilon}(\lambda^{\epsilon})
losses in the estimates in the geodesic-focusing regime. This argument is sharp in the sense it proves the full conjecture for a subset of caps, but it is not enough on its own to extend the range where the conjecture is proven.

Additive combinatorics
offers another line of attack, which is restricted to even integer value for
p
. In particular, the delicate transition between the two regimes in the conjecture, which occurs between
p=4
and
p=6
, cannot be observed through this method. Combining additive combinatorics with incidence geometry provides powerful bounds, but it is more effective (or at least, simpler to apply) if
\delta\sim\lambda^{-1}
.

2.
Notations

Inequalities.
For two quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
C
such that
|A|\leq CB
. If the constant
C
is allowed to depend on parameters
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, the notation
\lesssim
is replaced by
\lesssim_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
.

We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Finally,
A\ll B
means that
A\leq cB
for a sufficiently small (depending on the context) constant
c
.

Fourier transform and Fourier series
We are working on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, for which Fourier series are given by

\widehat{f}_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x}\,{\rm d}x,\qquad f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{f}_{k}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}.

For functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we adopt the following normalization for the Fourier transform and its inverse:

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,{\rm d}x,\qquad f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,{\rm d}\xi.

With this normalization, the Poisson summation formula becomes

\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}f(\ell)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\widehat{f}(k),

and furthermore we have

\widehat{f\cdot g}=\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widehat{f*g}=\widehat{f}\cdot\widehat{g}.

Finally, the Fourier multiplier
m(D)
stands for the operator defined by

m(D)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}=m(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}

and extended to general
f
by linearity.

3.
Counting caps with many points

3.1.
Decomposition of the shell into caps

We split the spherical shell

S_{\lambda,\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\left|\sqrt{Q(x)}-\lambda\right|<\delta\}

into a collection
\mathcal{C}
of finitely overlapping caps
\theta
:

S_{\lambda,\delta}=\bigcup_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}}\theta,

where each cap is of the form

\theta=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,|x-x_{\theta}|<\sqrt{\lambda\delta}\}\cap S_{\lambda,\delta}\qquad\text{for some }x_{\theta},\;Q(x_{\theta})=\lambda^{2}.

Each cap fits into a rectangular box with dimensions
\sim\delta\times\sqrt{\lambda\delta}\times\sqrt{\lambda\delta}
.

Recall that
Q(x)=x^{T}Ax
for a fixed, nonsingular, real symmetric matrix
A
. Then
Ax_{\theta}/|Ax_{\theta}|
is a unit normal to the ellipsoid
Q(x)=\lambda
at the point
x_{\theta}
; we define
Ax_{\theta}/|Ax_{\theta}|
to be the unit normal of the cap
\theta
.

Denote
N_{\theta}
for the number of points in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\theta
. On one hand, it is clear that
N_{\theta}\lesssim\lambda\delta
. On the other hand, one expects that the average cap will contain a number of points comparable to its volume, in other words
N_{\theta}\sim\lambda\delta^{2}
(provided this quantity is
>1
, which occurs if
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
).

This leads naturally to defining the following sets, which gather caps containing comparable numbers of points.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{s}=\{\theta\in\mathcal{C},\,N_{\theta}\sim 2^{s}\},\qquad\max\{1,\lambda\delta^{2}\}<2^{s}<\lambda\delta.

We consider the lattice
\Lambda_{\theta}
generated by
\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\theta-\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\theta
. We call the rank of this lattice the rank of the cap
\theta
. Furthermore, we set

\mathcal{N}^{r}_{s}=\#\{\theta\;,\;N_{\theta}\sim 2^{s}\;\text{and}\;\theta\;\text{has rank $r$}\}.

We are interested in
\lambda\delta^{2}<2^{s}\lesssim\lambda\delta
and
r\in\{1,2\}
because caps with less points than average be treated uniformly, and caps with more than the average must have rank
1
or
2
.

Our bounds for the sizes of these collections of caps are found in Proposition
3.8
and Proposition
3.1
, which are recorded together at the start of
Section

4.2
. We will give improved estimates for both the number of rank 1 and the number of rank 2 caps. It it not entirely clear to us what the optimal bounds might be, but we make some suggestions in the comments after Proposition
3.8
and Proposition
3.1
.

Along the way we also use an ‘incidence’ version of
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{r}
. Recall that a primitive lattice in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
is the intersection of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with any fixed linear subspace of
\mathbb{Q}^{3}
, and that an affine linear space is a translation of a rational linear space. Our incidence counting function captures all translations of primitive integral lattices (that is, all intersections of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with a rational affine linear space) that have a large intersection with any cap
\theta
.
This is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{r}_{t}=\#\{(\theta,V),\;\#(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap V\cap\theta)\sim 2^{t},\;V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{3}\;\text{ affine linear},\;\dim V=r\}.

We then have at once

\mathcal{N}^{r}_{s}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{r}_{s}.

3.2.
Bound for rank 2 caps

In this section we prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

(Special case of Theorem 4.1 in
[
11
]
.)
We have

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{s}\lesssim(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{3}.

(2)

We have for any

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{s}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{2}+\lambda^{\epsilon}\delta(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{4}.

(3)

We have

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{s}\lesssim\sum_{t=1}^{s/2}2^{t-s}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{1}_{t}.

When
2^{s}\sim{\lambda\delta}
, which is essentially the largest value possible as the integer points in
\theta

\sqrt{\lambda\delta},

then these bounds show that
\mathcal{N}^{2}_{s}\lesssim 1
which is optimal. They are thus quite sharp for large

while our bounds for rank 1 caps will be sharpest when

is small (see

). It seems possible that the term

in part 2 of the proposition could be reduced by counting separately those caps for which any of the components

basis of
M_{\theta}\Lambda_{\theta}
, that is
|U|\leq|V|
and
|U\cdot V|\leq\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}
say. Without loss of generality
w=w(\Lambda_{\theta})=u\wedge v
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
x_{\theta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, since this change introduces only a negligible error.

Proof of part 1.

Let
\theta
be a cap with rank 2 and
N_{\theta}\sim 2^{s}
.
We have

au+bv+x_{\theta}\in\theta\iff aU+bV\in M_{\theta}(\theta-x_{\theta}).

Now here
U,V
are roughly orthogonal and the set
M_{\theta}(\theta-x_{\theta})
is a convex body with size
\sim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}
in every direction. In particular, for suitable constants
0<c<C
we have

\frac{|a|}{|U|},\frac{|b|}{|V|}\leq c\sqrt{\lambda\delta}\implies aU+bV\in M_{\theta}(\theta-x_{\theta})\implies\frac{|a|}{|U|},\frac{|b|}{|V|}\leq C\sqrt{\lambda\delta}.

Hence

2^{s}\sim\#\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}\sim\#\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},\;\frac{|a|}{|U|},\frac{|b|}{|V|}\leq\sqrt{\lambda\delta}\}\sim\frac{\lambda\delta}{|U|\cdot|V|}.

Recalling that
|U|\leq|V|
, we see that
2^{s}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|U|}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|V|}
where
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|U|}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|V|}
holds. We can now take
2^{t}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|V|}
and pick the lines

L_{b}=\{au+bv,\;a\in\mathbb{R}\}\qquad(b\in\mathbb{Z},b\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}/|V|).

Each of these satisfies
L_{b}\cap\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}{|U|}\sim 2^{s-t}
, as required.

Proof of part 2, set-up.

We first prove the bounds for
|w|N_{\theta}
and
|(Ax_{\theta})\wedge w|
; to do this we set up a convex body defined by certain inequalities.

Let
v^{\prime}=u-\frac{u\cdot v}{|u|^{2}}u
and
u^{\prime}=u-\frac{u\cdot v}{|v|^{2}}v
, so that

(3.2)

\displaystyle u\cdot v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}\cdot v=0.

Let
a_{\theta},b_{\theta},c_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
Ax_{\theta}=a_{\theta}u^{\prime}+b_{\theta}v^{\prime}+c_{\theta}w
, so that in particular

(3.3)

\displaystyle|c_{\theta}w|

\displaystyle=\frac{|(Ax_{\theta})\cdot w|}{|w|},

\displaystyle|a_{\theta}u^{\prime}+b_{\theta}v^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|(Ax_{\theta})\wedge w|}{|w|}.

Proof of part 2, step 1. The convex region.

We claim that if
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
au+bv\in\theta-\theta
, then
(a,b)
lie in the region defined by

(3.4)

\displaystyle|a|\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle|b|\frac{|v|}{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\left|(a,b)\cdot(\frac{a_{\theta}u\cdot u^{\prime}}{\delta\lambda},\frac{b_{\theta}v\cdot v^{\prime}}{\delta\lambda})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

where the coefficients all satisfy

(3.5)

\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}},\frac{|v|}{\sqrt{\lambda\delta}},\frac{|a_{\theta}u\cdot u^{\prime}|}{\delta\lambda},\frac{|b_{\theta}v\cdot v^{\prime}|}{\delta\lambda}\lesssim 1.

To justify the claim, let
au+bv\in\theta-\theta
, and observe first that because
(U,V)
is a reduced basis we have
|aU+bV|\sim|aU|+|bV|
for all

. (Indeed that property is essentially equivalent to the basis being reduced, in the sense that it is equivalent to
|U|\cdot|V|-|U\cdot V|\gtrsim|U|\cdot|V|
which is a little weaker than our assumption in the lemma.) Since
\theta-\theta
contains two linearly independent points of
\Lambda_{\theta}
, we see that
M_{\theta}\Lambda_{\theta}
contains two linearly independent points of norm
\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}
, and therefore, because
|aU+bV|\sim|aU|+|bV|
, the basis vectors

must have

.

Now, if

. We conclude that
|a|\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}/|U|,|b|\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}/|V|
, which proves the first two inequalities in (
3.4
), and on taking
(a,b)=(1,0)
and
(0,1)
we also obtain the first two inequalities in (
3.5
).

Next, since

is a unit normal to
\theta

we have
|(au+bv)\cdot Ax_{\theta}|\lesssim\delta|Ax_{\theta}|\sim\delta\lambda

Here
(au+bv)\cdot Ax_{\theta}=(au+bv)\cdot(a_{\theta}u^{\prime}+b_{\theta}v^{\prime})=aa_{\theta}u\cdot u^{\prime}+bb_{\theta}v\cdot v^{\prime}
, so this quantity is

This proves the last inequality in (

).
Taking the particular cases when
(a,b)=(1,0)
or

proves the last two inequalities in (
3.5
).

Proof of part 2, step 2. Proof of (
3.1
).

Let
x\in\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, and note that the points in
\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
are necessarily of the form

where

and

.
We therefore find that

is bounded by the number of integer solutions

to (

Using (
3.4

For a given

using (

Repeating the argument with

N_{\theta}\lesssim\min\left\{\frac{\lambda\delta}{|w|},\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{3/2}}{a_{\theta}|u|\cdot|w|},\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{3/2}}{b_{\theta}|v|\cdot|u\cdot u^{\prime}|}\right\}.

The first part of (

Proof of part 3.

It remains to prove the bound on the number of

.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of part 1.

Let
1\lesssim W\lesssim\lambda\delta 2^{-s}
.
The number of vectors
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with
|w|\sim W
is at most
\lesssim W^{3}
. For each of these
w
there are
\lesssim(\lambda\delta 2^{-s}/W)^{2}
possible
\theta
such that
\Lambda_{\theta}
has rank 2, by an application of Lemma
3.3
. The total count is
(\lambda\delta 2^{-s})^{2}W
and summing over dyadic integers
1<W<\lambda\delta 2^{-s}
gives a total
(\lambda\delta 2^{-s})^{3}
.

Proof of part 2.

Consider
\theta
with rank 2 and
N_{\theta}\sim 2^{s}
. Every point
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\theta-\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\theta
satisfies
x\cdot Ax_{\theta}\lesssim\delta\lambda
because
Ax_{\theta}
is a normal to the cap with size around
\lambda
. Therefore, by the second part of
3.3
, and deliberately not counting the origin
x=0
, we have at least
2^{s}
integer solutions
0<|x|\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta}
to

x\cdot w\lesssim|w|\delta+\lambda^{-1}|x|(\lambda\delta)^{3/2}2^{-s}.

The right-hand side here is
\lesssim\lambda\delta^{2}2^{-s}
since
|w|\lesssim\lambda\delta 2^{-s}
.

Applying this to every cap in
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{r}
, the number of caps is bounded by
2^{-s}
times the number of solutions in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
to

0\neq|w|\lesssim\lambda\delta 2^{-s},\quad 0\neq|x|\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda\delta},\quad x\cdot w\lesssim\lambda\delta^{2}2^{-s}.

We divide the solutions into two classes. The number of solutions with
x_{1}w_{1}=x_{2}w_{2}=x_{3}w_{3}=0
is
\lesssim(\sqrt{\lambda\delta})^{2}(\lambda\delta 2^{-s})
, that is
\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{2}2^{-s}
. On the other hand for the solutions with some
x_{i}w_{i}\neq 0
, without loss of generality we can count the solutions with
x_{1}w_{1}\neq 0
, and by summing trivially over
x_{2},x_{3},w_{2},w_{3}
and using the divisor bound this can be shown to be
\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}(\lambda\delta 2^{-s})^{2}(\lambda\delta)(\lambda\delta^{2}2^{-s})=\lambda^{4+\epsilon}\delta^{5}2^{-3s}
. The final bound for the caps is
(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{2}+\lambda^{\epsilon}\delta(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{4}
.

Proof of part 3.
This follows at once from part 1 of
3.3
.
∎

3.3.
Preparation for rank 1 caps

Recall the notation
\mathcal{N}^{r}_{s}
from the start of the section.
In
[
11
]
the bound

\mathcal{N}^{1}_{s}\lesssim(\lambda 2^{-s})^{3/2}

is proved; as in the rank 2 case, specializing to
d=3
is somewhat elaborate. We will not need this as we give a separate proof of an improved bound.

Our improvement comes from
Corollary 2 of Huxley
[
15
]
, which, together with his Proposition 2A and taking the regions where his (1.8) holds but his (1.11) and (1.12) do not, yields

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta
and
let
G\in C^{3}([0,1])
such that
G^{\prime\prime}
takes values in
[\alpha,\beta]
, so in particular it is nonvanishing, and
|G^{(3)}|\leq\beta.
Suppose that, for some
\epsilon>0
,

1\leq(XY)^{69\cdot(3/10)-6+\epsilon}\leq X^{156\cdot(3/10)-14}\leq(XY)^{87\cdot(3/10)-8-\epsilon},

in other words, with a harmless adjustment to the value of
\epsilon,
suppose that

1\leq Y^{147/253+\epsilon}\leq X\leq Y^{253/147-\epsilon},

and let
\delta>0
. Then

\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:x\in[0,X],\,|y-YG(x/X)|\leq\delta\}\\
\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,\epsilon}\delta X+(XY)^{131/416+\epsilon},

the last exponent being given according to Huxley’s formula
\frac{67\cdot(3/10)-7}{212\cdot(3/10)-22}.

In some ranges we fall back on the following standard lemma, due to van der Corput.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta
and
let
G\in C^{2}([0,1])
such that
G^{\prime\prime}
takes values in
[\alpha,\beta]
, so in particular
G^{\prime\prime}
is nonvanishing, let
1\leq X\leq Y^{2}

and let
\delta>0
. Then

\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:x\in[0,X],\,|y-YG(x/X)|\leq\delta\}\\
\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\delta X+(XY)^{1/3}.

We will use this to count integer points in an annular region which may be long and thin.
We remark that the condition
1\leq X\leq Y^{2}
is more commonly stated as
1\leq Y^{1/2}\leq X\leq Y^{2}
(or equivalently,
1\leq X^{1/2}\leq Y\leq X^{2}
), since for
X\leq Y^{1/2}
the bound in the lemma can be proved by an almost trivial argument (counting the
y
for each fixed
x
separately).

Since the deduction of the previous result is short, we include it here.
We first quote from Lemma 2.2 of
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta
and
let
f\in C^{2}([0,X])
such that
f^{\prime\prime}
takes values in
[\alpha\lambda,\beta\lambda]
, so in particular
f^{\prime\prime}
is nonvanishing. Then

\sum_{x=0}^{X}e^{2\pi if(x)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}X\lambda^{1/2}+\lambda^{-1/2}.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the Féjer kernel

F_{k}(t)=\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(1-|j|/k)e^{2\pi ijt}=k(1-\cos 2\pi kt)/(1-\cos 2\pi t)

is nonnegative and 1-periodic, with Fourier support
[-k,k]
and Fourier coefficients
c_{k,j}
of absolute value at most 1. Moreover
F_{k}
is essentially supported on an
O(1/k)
-neighborhood of the integers, in the sense that
F_{k}(t)\geq k/3
for
|t|<1/2\pi k
, because
t^{2}/3\leq 1-\cos t\leq t^{2}
for
|t|<1
.

We remark that the Féjer kernel itself has no essential role here, as it is easy to construct other such functions with different constants in the inequalities
F_{k}(t)\gtrsim 1,c_{j,k}\lesssim 1
.

It follows that for any
1\leq k\leq(2\pi\delta)^{-1}
we have

\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:x\in[0,X],\,|y-YG(x/X)|\leq\delta\}\leq\frac{3}{k}\sum_{x=0}^{X}F_{k}(YG(x/X)).

This sum is

\frac{3}{k}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}c_{k,j}\sum_{x=0}^{X}e^{2\pi ijYG(x/X)}.

Separating the term
j=0
, and using the previous lemma with
\lambda=Yj/X^{2}
, this is

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{X}{k}+\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(\sqrt{Yj}+\frac{X}{\sqrt{Yj}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{X}{k}+\sqrt{Yk}+\frac{X}{\sqrt{Yk}}.

Recall that we can choose any
1\leq k\leq(2\pi\delta)^{-1}
. Pick
k\sim\min\{\delta^{-1},X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}\}
, in order to balance the first two terms in the bound above. The bound becomes

(3.7)

\lesssim\begin{cases}\delta X+Y^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}+XY^{-1/2}\delta^{1/2}&(\delta^{-1}<X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}),\\
(XY)^{1/3}+(XY)^{1/3}+X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}&(\delta^{-1}\geq X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}).\end{cases}

Provided that
X\leq Y^{2}
, we have
X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}\leq(XY)^{1/3}
, so
the second case is satisfactory.

In the first case, that is
\delta^{-1}<X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}
, we have the bound

\lesssim\delta X+Y^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}+XY^{-1/2}\delta^{1/2}.

The second term
Y^{1/2}\delta^{-1/2}
is bounded by
(XY)^{1/3}
since
\delta^{-1}<X^{2/3}Y^{-1/3}
, and the third term is majorized by
X\delta+XY^{-1}
, and hence by
X\delta+(XY)^{1/3}
provided that
X\leq Y^{2}
.
∎

The counting lemmas above are well designed for applications to integer points near dilates of a fixed curve. For more general linear transformations of a fixed curve, a little work is required. The particular case we need is as follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

Fix
\kappa>0
.
Let
q(x,y)=q_{1}x^{2}+2q_{2}xy+q_{3}y^{2}
be a real, positive definite quadratic form with coefficients bounded by
|q_{1}|,|q_{2}|,|q_{3}|\leq\kappa
and determinant bounded by
q_{1}q_{3}-q_{2}^{2}\geq\kappa
. Let
1\leq B^{1/2}\leq A\leq B^{2}
, and let
0<\eta\leq 1
and
\epsilon>0
. The number of
integer points
(a,b)
with

|q(a/A,b/B)-1|<\eta.

is
\lesssim_{\kappa}\eta AB+(AB)^{1/3}
, and if moreover

B^{\frac{147}{181}+\epsilon}\leq A\leq B^{\frac{181}{147}-\epsilon},

then this number of integer points is
\lesssim_{\kappa,\epsilon}\eta AB+(AB)^{131/416+\epsilon}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof we allow implicit constants to depend on
\kappa
.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
B\leq A
, for otherwise we may swap the roles of
A
and
B
. (In our application we will in any case have
B\leq A
.)

Set
E=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:q(a/A,b/B)=1\}
, and
R=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|q(a/A,b/B)-1|<\eta\}

Step 1. Division into horizontal and vertical arcs.

We are going to divide
E
into overlapping pieces according to the direction of the gradient

(3.8)

\begin{pmatrix}\partial/\partial a\\
\partial/\partial b\end{pmatrix}q(a/A,b/B)=\begin{pmatrix}{2/A}&0\\
0&{2/B}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}a/A+q_{2}b/B\\
q_{2}a/A+q_{3}b/B\end{pmatrix}.

Because the gradient
\nabla q(x,y)
is nonvanishing on
q=1
, we have

(3.9)

\left|\begin{pmatrix}q_{1}a/A+q_{2}b/B\\
q_{2}a/A+q_{3}b/B\end{pmatrix}\right|^{2}\sim 1\qquad((a,b)\in R).

Let
C\gg 1
be a sufficiently large parameter which will be chosen at the end of the argument. In other words,
C
will eventually be taken to be fixed, but until then, our implicit constants are not permitted to depend on
C
.

We will assume that
\eta\ll C^{-2}
, for if not, we may partition the annular region
|q-1|\leq\eta
into smaller regions
|q-\rho|\leq\eta^{\prime}
and count integer points in each each of these regions separately. This introduces a dependence on
C
into the final bound; however, this occurs in the very last step, when adding the contributions of all the different regions
|q-\rho|\leq\eta^{\prime}
, and by that time
C
will have been taken to be a fixed constant.

Set

\displaystyle R_{\text{vert}}={}

\displaystyle\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:

\displaystyle|q(a/A,b/B)-1|<\eta,|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)|\geq\frac{CB}{2A}|\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)|\},

\displaystyle R_{\text{horiz}}={}

\displaystyle\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:

\displaystyle|q(a/A,b/B)-1|<\eta,|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)|\leq\frac{2CB}{A}|\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)|\}.

This cuts the annular region
R
into two pieces, divided by the diagonal lines
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)=\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)
. Each of the pieces
R_{\text{vert}},R_{\text{horiz}}
consists of two components. We claim that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)

\displaystyle\sim A^{-1}

and

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\gtrsim A

if

\displaystyle(a,b)

\displaystyle\in R_{\text{vert}},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)

\displaystyle\gtrsim C^{-1}B^{-1}

if

\displaystyle(a,b)

\displaystyle\in R_{\text{horiz}}.

To prove the first part of (
3.10
) it suffices to observe that
(\partial/\partial a)q(a/A,b/B)\lesssim A^{-1}
, and so on
R_{\text{vert}}
we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)\lesssim 1/CB
. Since
C
is very large, from this and (
3.9
) it follows that the first part of (
3.10
) holds.

We next consider, for each fixed
|\xi|<\eta
, the curve
q(a/A,b/B)=1+\xi
. This curve contains two points with
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)=0
, one in each component of
R_{\text{vert}}
; let
(a_{0},b_{0})
be one of these points. At this point, we must have

(3.12)

a_{0}\gtrsim A,\qquad\left.|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)|\right|_{(a,b)=(a_{0},b_{0})}\gtrsim A^{-1},

by (
3.9
).
As we move along the curve
q(a/A,b/B)=1+\xi
, starting at
(a_{0},b_{0})
, we have

(3.13)

|a-a_{0}|\sim(A/B^{2})(b-b_{0})^{2}+O((A/B^{3})(b-b_{0})^{3}),

and in particular, using (
3.12
), we have

(3.14)

a\gtrsim A+O((A/B^{2})(b-b_{0})^{2}).

Taylor expanding around the point
b=b_{0}
as functions of
b
, we find that

(3.15)

\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)=2q_{3}(b-b_{0})/B^{2}+O(q_{2}(b-b_{0})^{2}/B^{3}),

and so in particular if
(a,b)\in R_{\text{vert}}
then

(b-b_{0})/B^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{CB}+O((b-b_{0})^{2}/B^{3}).

Hence, on the component of
R_{\text{vert}}
containing
(a_{0},b_{0})
we have
b-b_{0}\lesssim B/C
, and together with (
3.14
) this proves the second part of (
3.10
).

By the same argument, if
(a,b)\in R_{\text{horiz}}
then
b-b_{0}\gtrsim B/C
, and so we find that (
3.11
) holds by (
3.15
).

Step 2. Division into neighbourhoods of graphs.

We now begin to put our counting problem into the form in Lemma
3.5
. Set

\displaystyle E_{\text{vert}}

\displaystyle=E\cap R_{\text{vert}},

\displaystyle E_{\text{horiz}}

\displaystyle=E\cap R_{\text{horiz}},

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\text{vert}}

\displaystyle=\{(a+\Delta,b):(a,b)\in E_{\text{vert}},|\Delta|\lesssim\eta A\}.

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\text{horiz}}

\displaystyle=\{(a,b+\Delta):(a,b)\in E_{\text{horiz}},|\Delta|\lesssim\eta B\},

We will now prove that

(3.16)

R\subseteq R^{\prime}_{\text{vert}}\cup R^{\prime}_{\text{horiz}}.

First, if
(a,b)
belongs to the subset of
R_{\text{horiz}}
defined by

(3.17)

(a,b)\in R,\qquad|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)|\leq\frac{CB}{A}|\frac{\partial}{\partial b}q(a/A,b/B)|,

then we displace
(a,b)
parallel to the

-axis until

, finding
\Delta
such that
(a,b+\Delta)\in E
. Since
(a,b)\in R_{\text{horiz}}
, we expect to be able to take

. This will be possible so long as the point
(a,b+\Delta)
remains in
R_{\text{horiz}}
. Indeed if
\Delta\lesssim\eta B
and
(a,b)
are as in (

\displaystyle|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,(b+\Delta)/B)|=|\frac{\partial}{\partial a}q(a/A,b/B)+2q_{2}\Delta/AB|

For the sharp version of the conjecture to hold we need

\lambda^{\epsilon}\left[\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2}+(\lambda\delta^{1/2})^{1-\frac{4}{p}}\right]=I+II<(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}},

Comparing
I
to the right hand side gives the condition

\lambda^{\epsilon}\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2}<(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

That is, we must have

\delta<\lambda^{2-\frac{p}{2}-\kappa}.

This condition requires us to be in the interior of the geodesic focusing region, that is, to the right of but bounded away from the “critical curve” the appears in
A
.

Comparing
II
to the right hand side gives

(\lambda\delta^{1/2})^{1-\frac{4}{p}}<(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

This is true if and only if

\delta>\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa}.

We thus find that
P_{\operatorname{few}}
obeys the sharp sharp conjecture in the interior of the geodesic focusing region if, additionally,
\delta>\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa}
. Proving sharp estimates for
P_{\operatorname{few}}
in the point focusing region will be the subject of
Section

7
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

To bound

, the improved estimates on cap counting cannot help, and the limiting factor is

decoupling itself.
The first term in

Remark 4.5
.

Let us take a moment to review what we found in
4.4
.

The range
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is particularly amenable to our methods because an average cap contains more than 1 lattice point, and so decoupling enables us to prove almost sharp results for
P_{\operatorname{few}}
. Our counting argument is good enough to bound rank 1 and rank 2 caps in this regime. In dimensions higher than 3, the analogous range would be
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{d-1}{d+1}}
; the cap-counting estimates from
[
11
]
would need to be improved in order to capture the whole of that range.

For rank 1 and 2 caps, the
2^{s}\sim 1
term dominates the expression when
p>5
.

To elaborate further, when
\delta\sim\lambda^{-1/2}
, then according to our bounds (
4.5
)-(
4.9
) there could be as many as
\lambda^{3/2}
caps of rank 1 or 2 with
\sim 1
points. For
p>5
this exactly gives us the conjecture with no “wiggle room”, and that lack of room prevents us from obtaining the conjecture by decoupling if
p>5
and
\delta<\lambda^{-1/2-\epsilon}
.
This is suspected to be the true order of magnitude for rank 1 caps, see
3.9
, while the true number of rank 2 caps may be smaller.

For
4.2
, the limit of the method comes partly from needing a sharp bound for
\#\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap S_{\lambda,\delta}
, and partly from applying decoupling to estimate functions with Fourier support that does not include every cap in the decomposition. We are not taking advantage of the fact that many caps have no lattice points. Even with generous assumptions on the set of frequencies, that can be difficult. See
[
8
]
for some results and open questions in this direction, concerning decoupling type bounds beyond
p=4
. Exponential sum estimates and height splitting, the subjects of
Section

5
and
Section

7
respectively, provide a way to break this barrier slightly for our problem.

5.
The approach by exponential sums

We adapt here the approach laid out in
[
9
]
to the case of dimension 3 and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider a general quadratic form
Q
. Then Conjecture
A
is satisfied if

\delta>\min\left(\lambda^{-\frac{p-4}{2p-2}},\lambda^{-\frac{85p-358}{158p-128}}\right).

The first bound is not used in our main theorem, but we include it in the statement here as it represents what can be achieved with trivial estimates using this technique. There is an additional limitation on when we can use the second bound that will be detailed in the proof but, as it only forbids us from using the estimate other bounds are superior, it will play no role in our results. In our main theorem, the above result is only used when
\delta<\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}
for
\kappa>0
by the independent results of
Section

7
.

Proof.

The mollified convolution kernel.
It is more convenient to work with a mollified version of the projector
P_{\lambda,\delta}
: define the Fourier multiplier

P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp}=\chi_{\lambda,\delta}(D),

where the function
\chi_{\lambda,\delta}
is a
nonnegative smooth cutoff function adapted to the shell
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
. To be more explicit, consider a positive function
\chi
whose Fourier transform is compactly supported and let

\chi_{\lambda,\delta}=\delta^{-2}\chi(\delta^{-1}\cdot)*d\sigma_{\lambda},

where
d\sigma_{\lambda}
is the superficial measure on the ellipsoid

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\;Q(x)=\lambda\}.

The Fourier transform (on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
) of
\,{\rm d}\sigma_{1}
is such that

m(\xi)=\widehat{\,{\rm d}\sigma_{1}}(\xi)

satisfies

(5.1)

m(\xi)\sim\mathfrak{Re}\;e^{i\Theta(\xi)}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\xi|^{-j}a_{j}(\xi)\quad\mbox{as $\xi\to\infty$},

the phase
\Theta
is smooth and homogeneous of degree one and
|\nabla^{k}a_{j}(\xi)|\lesssim_{k}|\xi|^{-k}
, see
[
20
]
, VIII.B (the real part in the above formula accounts for the two points on the ellipse whose normal has a given direction, and we will omit it going forward). In particular,
m
decays like
|\xi|^{-1}
.

Since
\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-1}\,{\rm d}\sigma_{1}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)
, we have that

(5.2)

\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}}(\xi)=\lambda^{2}\delta m(\lambda\xi)\widehat{\chi}(\delta\xi).

Dyadic decomposition.

We now introduce a dyadic partition of unity, namely nonnegative functions
\varphi
and
\psi
in
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
such that

(5.3)

\varphi(y)+\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\psi\left(\frac{y}{M}\right)=1\qquad\mbox{for all $y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}

and furthermore
\varphi
is supported in a ball, and
\psi
in an annulus.

Denoting
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp}(x)
the kernel of
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp}
, we apply successively the Poisson summation formula and the partition of unity above to get

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\chi_{\lambda,\delta}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}}(m-x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi(m-x)\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}}(m-x)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\psi\left(\frac{m-x}{M}\right)\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}}(m-x)

\displaystyle=P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,0}(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}\\
M\delta\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}(x).

Bounding the leading term
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,0}
.
We consider first the operator on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with kernel
\varphi\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}}
. Modifying the cutoff function, this kernel can be written as
\delta\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,1}}
, stemming from the symbol
\delta\chi_{\lambda,1}
. By the Stein-Tomas theorem (see
[
11
]
for the version we need here), the corresponding operator has norm
\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}\delta
from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
if
p\geq 4
. By locality, the same bound holds for
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,0}
.

Bounding the noise
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}
.

The plan is now to interpolate between
L^{2}\to L^{2}
and
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
bounds.

To obtain an
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bound, we deduce from the definition of the kernel
\Phi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}
and Poisson summation that
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}
is the Fourier multiplier on the torus with symbol
M^{3}\widehat{\psi}(M\cdot)*\chi_{\lambda,\delta}
. Therefore,

(5.4)

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\|M^{3}\widehat{\psi}(M\cdot)*\chi_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim M\delta\qquad\text{if }M\lesssim\delta^{-1}.

To obtain the
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
bound, we rely on exponential sum estimates. By (
5.1
), and assuming that
M\lesssim\delta^{-1}
, we need to bound

S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)=\lambda\delta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\frac{n-x}{M}\right)a_{1}(\lambda(n-x))\frac{e^{i\lambda\Theta(|n-x|)}}{|n-x|}

(we only kept the leading order term in (
5.1
), since higher order terms are easier to control). A first obvious bound is

(5.5)

|S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)|\lesssim\lambda\delta M^{2}.

We can also use results from
[
13
]
. By arguments in that paper, we have

(5.6)

|S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)|\lesssim\delta\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}}M^{2-\frac{30}{94}}\qquad\mbox{if $M>\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}$}.

See
5.2
below for the details of this bound.

Interpolating between (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) and summing over
M\lesssim\delta^{-1}
gives the following bound for any
\delta>\lambda^{-1}

\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}(M\delta)^{\frac{2}{p}}(\lambda\delta M^{2})^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\sim\lambda^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\delta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}

If
\delta<\lambda^{-\frac{2}{7}}
, we can split the sum into two pieces for which we interpolate between (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) or (
5.6
) depending on the value of
M
. This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}=\Bigl(\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}}+\sum_{\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}\lesssim M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}\Bigr)\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}}(M\delta)^{\frac{2}{p}}(\lambda\delta M^{2})^{1-\frac{2}{p}}+\sum_{\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}\lesssim M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}(M\delta)^{\frac{2}{p}}(\delta\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}}M^{2-\frac{30}{94}})^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\sim\lambda^{\frac{11}{7}-\frac{18}{7p}}\delta+\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\delta^{-\frac{32}{47}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\sim\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\delta^{-\frac{32}{47}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}

Overall, we found that

\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\sharp,M}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\delta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}&\mbox{if $\delta>\lambda^{-1}$}\\
\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}\delta^{-\frac{32}{47}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)}&\mbox{if $\lambda^{-1}<\delta<\lambda^{-\frac{2}{7}}$}.\end{cases}

This is
\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}\delta
if

\delta>\min\left(\lambda^{-\frac{p-4}{2p-2}},\lambda^{-\frac{85p-358}{158p-128}}\right)

which is the desired result.
∎

We now quickly detail the bound (
5.6
).

Lemma 5.2
.

The bound (
5.6
) holds when
M\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}
.

Proof.

Recall we are trying to estimate

S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)=\lambda\delta\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\psi(\frac{n-x}{M})a_{1}(\lambda(n-x))\frac{e^{i\lambda\Theta(|n-x|)}}{|n-x|}.

The goal is to apply Theorem 2.6 in
[
13
]
which requires aesthetic manipulations. We write

S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)=\lambda\delta M^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}G(n/M)e^{(\lambda M)F(n/M)}.

Where we have

G(n/M)\coloneq\psi(\frac{n-x}{M})a_{1}(\lambda(n-x))\frac{M}{|n-x|}

and

F(n/M)\coloneq\Theta(\frac{|n-x|}{M}),

using the homogeneity of
\Theta
. Note that as
|n-x|
is localized to
\sim M
,
G
is uniformly bounded.
M
plays the same role in the statement of Theorem 2.6 while
T=\lambda M
in our notation. This gives, after applying Theorem 2.6 with
q=2
and
d=3
,

|S_{\lambda,\delta}^{M}(x)|\lesssim\lambda\delta M^{-1}\Bigl((\lambda M)^{\frac{9}{94}}M^{3-\frac{39}{94}}\Bigr)=\delta\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}}M^{2-\frac{30}{94}}.

The dominant term is when
M\sim\delta^{-1}
which gives

|S_{\lambda,\delta}^{\delta^{-1}}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{103}{94}}\delta^{-\frac{64}{94}}.

Which is the result. As a check, Theorem 2.6 only applies because of the restrictions present in the proof, if

M^{2+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4}{9}}=M^{\frac{31}{18}}\lesssim(\lambda M)\lesssim M^{2+\frac{1}{2}+2}=M^{\frac{9}{2}}.

As
M\lesssim\delta^{-1}\lesssim\lambda
the lower bound is trivially satisfied. We therefore must also have
\lambda\lesssim M^{\frac{7}{2}}
. Or,

M\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{2}{7}}.

∎

6.
The approach by the circle method

In this section, we apply the circle method, which was first employed by Bourgain
[
2
]
to bound the
L^{p}
norms of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on the torus. This method gives nontrivial results, which will nevertheless be superseded in our final theorem (Theorem
1.1
) by a combination of cap counting,
\ell^{2}
-decoupling, exponential sum estimates, and height splitting. It is interesting to measure the power of this method against the other techniques we have employed.

Theorem 6.1
.

Conjecture
A
is satisfied on the square torus if

\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{p}+\kappa}\qquad\mbox{with $\kappa>0$.}

Proof.

Decomposition of the projection operator.
Just like in the previous section, it is convenient to mollify the projection operator. We do it in a different way by defining
P^{\flat}_{\lambda,\delta}
in terms of the Schrödinger group as

P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}=\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}-|D|^{2}}{\lambda\delta}\right)=\lambda\delta\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{\chi}(\lambda\delta t)e^{2\pi i(\lambda^{2}-|D|^{2})t}\,{\rm d}t.

Here,
\chi
is a smooth cutoff functions which is equal to
1
in a neighborhood of
0
. As a consequence, it is equivalent to prove bounds for
P_{\lambda,\delta}
or
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}
. We now follow the ideas in Bourgain
[
2
]
and Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
]
. The kernel of
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}
is

\displaystyle K_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}(x)=\lambda\delta\int\widehat{\chi}(\lambda\delta t)\prod_{j=1}^{3}G(t,x_{j})e^{2\pi i\lambda^{2}t}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\qquad G(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma\left(\frac{k}{\lambda}\right)e^{2\pi i(kx-|k|^{2}t)},

where
\gamma
is a function in
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
equal to
1
on the unit ball.

If
a
and
q
are relatively prime integers
(a,q)=1
such that
|q|\lesssim\lambda
and
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda q}
, then the function
G
enjoys the bound

|G(t,x)|\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\min\left(\lambda,\left|t-\frac{a}{q}\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(see the proof in
[
4
]
). As a consequence, we get for
|t|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}
that
|G(t,x)|\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}\min\left(\lambda,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
. The
\lambda^{\epsilon}
can be removed for
t
small:
after stationary phase and Poisson summation,

|G(t,x)|\lesssim\min\left(\lambda,|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\qquad\mbox{if $|t|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}$}.

We now let
\eta_{Q,s}
be cutoff functions localizing to values of
t
such that
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\sim\frac{1}{\lambda 2^{s}}
where
Q\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
,
q\sim Q\ll\lambda
,
a\neq 0
,
(a,q)=1
and
Q<2^{s}<\lambda
; it is understood that for
2^{s}\sim\lambda
, the localization is to the region
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}
. The cutoff function
\eta_{0}
localizes to the region
|t|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}
, where
1<2^{s}<\lambda
.

The
\eta_{Q,s}
are chosen such that

\eta_{Q}=\sum_{Q<2^{s}<\lambda}\eta_{Q,s}(t)=1\qquad\mbox{if $|t-\frac{a}{q}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda Q}$ with $q\sim Q$, $(a,q)=1$}.

Since
Q\ll\lambda
, the supports of the
\eta_{Q}
and
\eta_{0}
are disjoint, and we let
\rho
be such that

\eta_{0}+\sum_{Q\ll\lambda}\eta_{Q}+\rho=1.

We can split accordingly the kernel
K_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}

K_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}=\int\left[\sum_{Q,s}\eta_{Q,s}+\eta_{0}+\rho\right]\dots\,{\rm d}t=\sum_{Q,s}K_{\lambda,\delta}^{Q,s}+K_{\lambda,\delta}^{0}+K_{\lambda,\delta}^{\rho}

and the operators

P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}=\sum P_{\lambda,\delta}^{Q,S}+P_{\lambda,\delta}^{0}+P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\rho}.

We will now bound successively all the summands above.

The arc around zero.
Choosing
\eta_{0}(t)=\eta(\lambda t)
, for a cutoff function
\eta
, the operator
P^{0}_{\lambda,\delta}
is given by the formula

P^{0}_{\lambda,\delta}=\lambda\delta\int\eta(\lambda t)e^{2\pi i(\lambda^{2}-|D|^{2})t}\,dt=\delta\widehat{\eta}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}-|D|^{2}}{\lambda}\right).

By Sogge’s theorem and rapid decay of
\widehat{\eta}
, it follows that

(6.1)

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{0}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}\delta\qquad\mbox{if $p>4$}.

The major arcs.
On the support of
\eta_{Q,s}
there holds
|G|\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda 2^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so that (for a rapidly decaying function
\chi_{0}
and
\kappa>0
)

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda,\delta}^{Q,s}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1+\epsilon}\delta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\\
(a,q)=1\\
a\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi_{0}\left(\lambda\delta\frac{a}{q}\right)(\lambda 2^{s})^{-1}(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{s}{2}})^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{s}{2}}&\mbox{if $Q>\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\lambda^{-N}&\mbox{if $Q<\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\|\widehat{K_{\lambda,\delta}^{Q,s}}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1+\epsilon}\delta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\\
(a,q)=1\\
a\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi_{0}\left(\lambda\delta\frac{a}{q}\right)(\lambda 2^{s})^{-1}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{\epsilon-1}Q^{2}2^{-s}&\mbox{if $Q>\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\lambda^{-N}&\mbox{if $Q<\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\end{array}\right.

This implies that, if
p>2
,

\|P^{Q,s}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{p}}2^{s(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p})}&\mbox{if $Q>\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\lambda^{-N}&\mbox{if $Q<\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta$}\\
\end{array}\right.

Summing over
Q
and
s
gives for
p>6
and
\delta>\lambda^{\kappa-1}

(6.2)

\begin{split}\sum_{1<Q<2^{s}<\lambda}\|P^{Q,s}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}&\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<Q<2^{s}<\lambda\\
Q>\lambda^{1-\kappa}\delta\end{subarray}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{p}}2^{s(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p})}+O(\lambda^{-N})\\
&\sim\lambda^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}\cdot\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{p}}\cdot\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}\sim\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

The minor arcs.
By the Dirichlet approximation theorem, on the support of
\rho
, there exists
q\sim\lambda
such that
|t-\frac{a}{q}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}
, which gives the bounds
|G|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
and therefore

\displaystyle\|K^{\rho}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\|\widehat{K^{\rho}_{\lambda,\delta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1

so that, for
p>2
,

(6.3)

\|P^{\rho}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p}+\epsilon}.

Putting all the pieces together, we find that

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\flat}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}\delta+\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p}+\epsilon},

which gives the conjecture for
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{p}+\kappa}
.
∎

Removing the

In Section

Different estimates were used for

with

We also used exponential sum estimates in

Theorem 7.1

Note that

In Section

, we will prove a weaker result than the previously stated theorem where

is restricted to be
>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}
for arbitrary

, we import exponential sum estimates from

to get the full result.

7.1.
Combining decoupling and height splitting

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
P_{\operatorname{few}}
be as before. Then for
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}
, we have

(7.2)

\left\lVert P_{\operatorname{few}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim_{\kappa}\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Recall that
4.2
verifies the sharp conjecture for
P_{\operatorname{few}}
when
\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa}<\delta<\lambda^{2-\frac{p}{2}-\kappa}.
The proposition above handles
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}<\delta<1
. Since

\displaystyle[\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa},\lambda^{2-\frac{p}{2}-\kappa}]\cup[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa},1]

\displaystyle=[\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa},1]\supseteq[\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+3\kappa},1]

\displaystyle\text{if }p\leq 5-4\kappa,

\displaystyle[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa},1]

\displaystyle\supseteq[\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+3\kappa},1]

\displaystyle\text{if }p\geq 5-4\kappa,

we can write
\kappa^{\prime}=3\kappa
to obtain

Corollary 7.3
.

Assume that (
1.3
) holds and that
\delta>\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa}
for any fixed
\kappa>0
. Then

P_{\operatorname{few}}
satisfies the sharp version of

.

Proof of
7.2
.

Step 1: decomposition into caps and heights.

Recall that, if
\lambda\delta^{2}>1
,

P_{\operatorname{few}}=\sum_{2^{s}\lesssim\lambda\delta^{2}}P^{s}_{\lambda,\delta}.

Let
P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}
be the mollified version of our projection operator previously defined in the proof of
5.1
. In order to prove that
P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}P_{\operatorname{few}}
has an operator norm
\lesssim\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
from
L^{2}
to
L^{p}
, it suffices to show, by an application of orthogonality, that

\|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}

for
f
such that

f=P_{\operatorname{few}}F\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|F\|_{L^{2}}=1,

which we assume henceforth.

We shall split the torus into large and small heights. Let

(7.3)

\begin{split}&A_{-}=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f(x)|<C_{0}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\},\\
&A_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}:|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f(x)|\geq C_{0}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}.\end{split}

The reason for the choice of
(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}
as the defining height will become apparent later in the proof. It should be noted it is the square root of the trivial bound
Eq.

5.5
when
M\sim\delta^{-1}
. Here,
C_{0}
is a positive constant we shall select later.

Step 2: small height.
On the one hand, the
L^{\infty}
norm of
P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f
on
A_{-}
is bounded by the very definition of
A_{-}

(7.4)

\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A_{-})}\lesssim(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, its
L^{4}
norm on
A_{-}
can be estimated by (
4.2
) to give

\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{4}(A_{-})}\leq\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}P_{\operatorname{few}}F\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Interpolating between these two norms yields

(7.5)

\begin{split}\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(A_{-})}&\leq\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(A_{-})}^{\frac{p-4}{p}}\|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{4}(A_{-})}^{\frac{4}{p}}\\
&\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{4}{p}\epsilon}(\lambda\delta^{-1})^{\frac{p-4}{2p}}\lambda^{1/p}\delta^{2/p}\lesssim_{\kappa}\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2},\end{split}

where the last inequality is a consequence of
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}
.

Step 3: large height.
By duality and self-adjointness of
P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}
, there exists
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(A_{+})
with
L^{p^{\prime}}
norm
1
such that

\|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{p}(A_{+})}=\int\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\cdot P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\cdot g\,{\rm d}x\leq\|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}g\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{p}(A_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}g\cdot\overline{P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}g}\,{\rm d}x=\int(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta})^{2}\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}g\cdot\overline{\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}g}\,{\rm d}x,

where we again used that the projection operator is self-adjoint. At this point, we split
(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta})^{2}
into a “local” part
L_{\lambda}
and a “global” part
G_{\lambda,\delta}
:

(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta})^{2}=L_{\lambda}+G_{\lambda,\delta},\qquad L_{\lambda}=\delta(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,1})^{2},\qquad G_{\lambda,\delta}=(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta})^{2}-\delta(P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,1})^{2}.

From the above, we see that we need to estimate

\int L_{\lambda}(\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g(x))\overline{(g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}})(x)}dx+\int G_{\lambda}(\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g(x))\overline{(g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}})(x)}dx=I+II.

The local piece is easily estimated. Indeed, Hölder’s inequality and Sogge’s universal estimates lead to the bound

\displaystyle|I|\leq\|L_{\lambda}(g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}})\|_{L^{p}}\|g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\leq\|L_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\|g\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}^{2}\lesssim\delta\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}.

In order to bound
II
, we need to estimate the
L^{\infty}
norm of the convolution kernel of
G_{\lambda,\delta}
. By (
5.2
) and Poisson summation, it equals

G_{\lambda,\delta}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\lambda^{2}\delta\left[\widehat{\chi}(\delta(x+n)))-\widehat{\chi}(x+n)\right]m(\lambda(x+n)).

Observe that

\left|\lambda^{2}\delta\left[\widehat{\chi}(\delta x))-\widehat{\chi}(x)\right]m(\lambda x)\right|\lesssim\lambda\delta\langle x\rangle^{-1}

(which can be seen by distinguishing the cases
|x|<\frac{1}{\lambda}
,
\frac{1}{\lambda}<|x|<1
and
|x|>1
) and that it decays rapidly for
|x|\gg\delta^{-1}
.
It follows that

(7.6)

||{G_{\lambda,\delta}}||_{1\rightarrow\infty}\leq C_{1}\lambda\delta^{-1}.

For some constant
C_{1}
. This allows us to bound
II
using Hölder’s inequality, the definition of
g
, and the definition of
A_{+}
.

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\lambda\delta^{-1}||{\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}\cdot g}||_{1}^{2}\leq C_{1}(\lambda\delta^{-1})(||{\mathbf{1}_{A_{+}}}||_{p}||{g}||_{p^{\prime}})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{0}^{2}(\lambda\delta^{-1})\Bigl((\lambda\delta^{-1})^{-1/2}\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(A_{+})}\Bigr)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(A_{+})}^{2};

here, we chose
C_{0}
so that
C_{1}C_{0}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

Combining with our bound for
I
gives

\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(A_{+})}^{2}\leq C\delta\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert P^{\sharp}_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(A_{+})}^{2},

which finally yields the desired estimate. Note that
\left\lVert P_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim\lambda\delta^{1/2}
, so that the preceding argument is only non-trivial when
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}
.
∎

into functions supported on caps with diameter

P_{\operatorname{max}}f=\sum_{\nu}P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}P_{\operatorname{max}}f=\sum_{\nu}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f

and then make the problem bilinear by writing

(P_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{2}=\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f=\sum_{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\lesssim\lambda\delta}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f+\sum_{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gg\lambda\delta}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f.

We will treat separately the “close” interactions, for which
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\lesssim\delta
and the “far” interactions for which
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\delta
. Note that for a fixed
\nu
, there are only a constant number of
\nu^{\prime}
such that
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\lesssim\delta
.

The optimal bound for large caps is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
\delta>\lambda^{-1+\kappa}
. The operator
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}
obeys the following bound

(8.3)

\|P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{\kappa}(\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2}+(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}})\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

We also need an estimate involving bilinear interactions between transverse caps which is contained in the following lemma.

Lemma 8.4

(Transverse interactions)
.

Let
P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}=P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}P_{\operatorname{max}}
. If
p\geq 4
and
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\delta
,

\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+3\alpha}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

We postpone the proofs of these two results until after the proof of
8.2
. With these results in hand, we now bound
P_{\operatorname{max}}
.

Proof of
8.2
.

We treat close and far interactions separately.

Close interactions
For close interactions we use
8.2
, which states that the
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}
enjoys the same bound as conjectured in
A
for
P_{\lambda,\delta}
. Using this result in conjunction with the Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{(|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\lesssim\delta}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{\nu}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}})\left(\sum_{\nu}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}})\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Far interactions.
To treat far interactions, the key result is Lemma
8.4
, which gives a gain in bilinear interactions between far caps, namely

\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+3\alpha}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

Applying this inequality together with Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\lambda\delta}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left(\sum_{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\lambda\delta}\left\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+3\alpha}\left(\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

At this point, we can use that the number of
\nu
such that
P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\neq 0
is less than the number of caps
\theta
for which
\Lambda_{\theta}
has dimension 2 and which contains a number of points
>(\lambda\delta)^{1-\alpha}
. By
3.1
, this number is

(8.4)

\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{max}}=\sum_{2^{s}>(\lambda\delta)^{1-\alpha}}\mathcal{N}^{2}_{s}\lesssim\sum_{2^{s}>(\lambda\delta)^{1-\alpha}}(2^{-s}\lambda\delta)^{3}=(\lambda\delta)^{3\alpha}.

Using this bound and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and going back to the estimate on far interactions, we find that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\delta}P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+6\alpha}\left(\sum_{\nu}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+6\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives the desired result provided
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2p}+6\alpha<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
, which can be achieved by choosing
\alpha
sufficiently small.
∎

We now prove
8.3
, which states that projectors on larger caps
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}f
satisfy the same bound as expected by Conjecture
A
for
P_{\lambda,\delta}
. This explains the choice of the diameter
\sim\lambda\delta
of the cap associated to
\nu
: it is the biggest diameter which allows for a simple proof of the above proposition. We shall prove an oscillatory integral lemma first which contains the bulk of the proof.

Lemma 8.5
.

Assume that
\delta>\lambda^{\kappa-1}
, with
\kappa>0
and that the cap associated to
\nu
has, at its center, a unit normal
n
to the ellipsoid defined by
Q
. Then, if
|\xi|\lesssim\delta^{-1}
,

\left|\widehat{\chi^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}}(\xi)\right|\lesssim_{N}\begin{cases}\lambda\delta\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}&\mbox{if $|n\cdot\xi|\lesssim 1$}\\
\lambda\delta^{2}\langle P_{n}^{\perp}\xi\rangle^{N}&\mbox{if $|n\cdot\xi|\gg 1$, for $N>0$,}\end{cases}

where we set
P_{n}^{\perp}\xi=\xi-(\xi\cdot n)n
.

This lemma is ultimately an oscillatory integral bound, but some care is needed to organize the proof in the simplest form possible.

Proof.

Step 1: reduction to the Fourier transform of a measure on a cap
We will abuse notation by using
\nu
to refer to the cap associated to the direction
\nu
. Up to rotating the ellipsoid, we can assume that
n
is aligned with the
x_{1}
axis; for greater simplicity in the notations, we furthermore assume that the center of
\nu
has coordinates
x_{2}=x_{3}=0
.

The function
\chi_{\lambda,\delta}(x)
localizes smoothly to a
\delta
neighborhood of the ellipsoid
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
, while the function
\beta^{\nu}(x)
localizes smoothly to a cone with opening
\sim\delta
around
x_{2}=x_{3}=0
. Therefore,
\beta^{\nu}(x)\chi_{\lambda,\delta}(x)
can be written as an average of the superficial measures
\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\rho}
(defined in Section
5
) against an appropriate function
\varphi_{\rho}
:

\beta^{\nu}(x)\chi_{\lambda,\delta}(x)=\int\varphi_{\rho}(x_{2},x_{3})\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\rho}(x)\,{\rm d}\rho.

Here, the function
\varphi_{\rho}(x_{2},x_{3})
is localized on the set where
|(x_{2},x_{3})|\lesssim\rho\delta
and satisfies

|\nabla^{k}\varphi_{\rho}(x_{2},x_{3})|\lesssim(\rho\delta)^{-k}\chi_{0}\left(\frac{\rho-\lambda}{\delta}\right),

where
\chi_{0}
is a rapidly decaying cutoff function. To prove the lemma, it suffices to bound the Fourier transform of
\varphi_{\rho}(x_{2},x_{3})\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\rho}(x)
and prove that

\widehat{\varphi_{\rho}\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\rho}}(\xi)\lesssim\chi_{0}\left(\frac{\rho-\lambda}{\delta}\right)\begin{cases}\lambda\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}&\mbox{if $|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim 1$}\\
\lambda\delta\langle(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\rangle^{-N}&\mbox{if $|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\gg 1$}\end{cases}

The cases
\rho\ll\lambda
or
\rho\gg\lambda
are easily treated because of the rapid decay of
\chi_{0}
, there remains the case
\rho\sim\lambda
. To simplify notations, we set
\rho=\lambda
and
\varphi=\varphi_{\lambda}
, and we then aim at proving

|\widehat{\varphi\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\lambda}}(\xi)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}&\mbox{if $|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim 1$}\\
\lambda\delta\langle(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\rangle^{-N}&\mbox{if $|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\gg 1$}\end{cases}

where
\varphi(x_{2},x_{3})
is supported on
|(x_{2},x_{3})|\lesssim\lambda\delta
and satisfies for any
N>0

|\nabla^{k}\varphi(x_{2},x_{3})|\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{-k},\qquad|\nabla^{\ell}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim_{N}(\lambda\delta)^{2+\ell}\langle\lambda\delta(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\rangle^{-N}.

Step 2: Further reductions on the oscillatory integral.
We can now write

\widehat{\varphi\,{\rm d}\sigma_{\lambda}}(\xi)=\lambda^{2}m(\lambda\xi)*\widehat{\varphi}(\xi).

The case
|\xi|\lesssim 1
is easily dealt with, therefore, we focus on
|\xi|\gg 1
and replace
m
by its leading order in the asymptotic expansion (
5.1
), since higher order terms are easier to estimate. This gives

\lambda\int\frac{a_{1}(\lambda(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime}))}{|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime})|}e^{i\lambda\Theta(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime})}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}

By rapid decay of
\widehat{\varphi}
on a scale
(\lambda\delta)^{-1}<\lambda^{-\kappa}
, it is harmless to add in the above integral a compactly supported cutoff function
\chi_{1}(\lambda^{\kappa/2}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime}))
. Furthermore, since
\frac{a_{1}(\lambda(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime}))}{|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime})|}
varies very slowly on the region where
|(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})|\lesssim 1
, it is inconsequential to replace this factor by
|\xi|^{-1}
. This results in the expression

I=\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int e^{i\lambda\Theta(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime})}\chi_{1}(\lambda^{\kappa/2}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime}))\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}

If
|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\lesssim 1
, this integral is estimated immediately by

|I|\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})|\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}.

As
|\hat{\varphi}(\xi)|\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{2}
and it is essentially supported where
|(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})|\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{-1}
.

Step 3: The regime of strong oscillation.

If
|(\xi_{2},\xi_{3})|\gg 1
, we view
I
as an oscillatory integral with large parameter
\lambda
and phase

\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})=\Theta(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}^{\prime}).

Assuming
|\xi_{3}^{\prime}|<|\xi_{2}^{\prime}|\ll 1
and
1\ll|\xi_{2}|\ll|\xi|
, and using homogeneity of
\Theta
and strict convexity of its level sets, we see that

\left|\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{|\xi|}{|\xi_{2}|^{1+k}}.

Since
\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{3}
play symmetrical roles, we can assume without loss of generality that
|\xi_{2}|\gtrsim|\xi_{3}|
.
Integrating by parts once in
I
results in the estimate

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int e^{i\lambda\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\chi_{1}(\lambda^{\kappa/2}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime}))\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})}{\lambda\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}\right]\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int\left[\left|\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi}\right)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\right|+\left|\frac{1}{\lambda\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi}\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\right|\right]\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\{\mbox{easier term}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\frac{|\xi|}{\lambda|\xi_{2}|^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\cdot\frac{|\xi|}{\lambda|\xi_{2}|}\cdot\lambda\delta\lesssim\frac{\lambda\delta}{|\xi_{2}|}.

We now iterate this procedure; it turns out that the worst always occurs when derivatives hit
\widehat{\varphi}
, which gives the estimate

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int e^{i\lambda\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}\left(\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\lambda\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi}\right)^{N}\left[\chi_{1}(\lambda^{\kappa/2}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime}))\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\right]\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\int e^{i\lambda\Phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}}\Phi}\right)^{N}\chi_{1}(\lambda^{\kappa/2}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime}))(\partial_{\xi_{2}^{\prime}})^{N}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi_{2}^{\prime},\xi_{3}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\xi_{2}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\xi_{3}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\{\mbox{easier terms}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{|\xi|}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\lambda|\xi_{2}|}\right)^{N}(\lambda\delta)^{N}\lesssim\frac{\lambda\delta}{|\xi_{2}|^{N}},

where we used
|\xi|\lesssim\delta^{-1}
in the last inequality.
∎

Now we may prove
8.3
.

Proof of
8.3
.

Decomposition of the kernel.
Denoting
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}(x)
for the kernel of
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}
, we proceed as in Section
5
and write it as

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}}(m-x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\varphi(m-x)\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}}(m-x)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\sum_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\psi\left(\frac{m-x}{M}\right)\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}}(m-x)

\displaystyle=P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu,0}(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}\\
M\delta\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu,M}(x).

Bound for the leading term
P^{\nu,0}_{\lambda,\delta}
.
By locality and the Stein-Tomas theorem, the operator
P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}
has operator norm
\lesssim\lambda^{2-\frac{6}{p}}\delta
from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{p}
if
p\geq 4
.

Bound for the noise
\sum_{M\geq 1}P^{\nu,M}_{\lambda,\delta}
.
The
L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}
operator norm of
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}
will be estimated by interpolating between
L^{2}\to L^{2}
and
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
. The former is not hard: just like in Section
5
,

\|P^{\nu,M}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\|M^{3}\widehat{\psi}(M\cdot)*\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim M\delta\qquad\mbox{if $M\lesssim\delta^{-1}$}.

We now claim that

\|P^{\nu,M}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}=\left\|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu,M}(x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda\delta.

Indeed, denoting
n
for the unit normal to
\nu
and applying Lemma
8.5
below,

\displaystyle|P_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu,M}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m}\left|\psi\left(\frac{m-x}{M}\right)\widehat{\chi_{\lambda,\delta}^{\nu}}(m-x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m-x|\sim M\\
|(m_{1}-x_{1},m_{2}-x_{2})|\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}\lambda\delta\langle m-x\rangle^{-1}\langle P_{n}^{\perp}(m-x)\rangle^{-N}\lesssim\lambda\delta

(recall that
P_{n}^{\perp}x=x-(x\cdot n)n
).
Interpolating between these two bounds gives

\|P^{\nu,M}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim(M\delta)^{\frac{2}{p}}(\lambda\delta)^{1-\frac{2}{p}},

so that

\sum_{1\leq M\lesssim\delta^{-1}}\|P^{\nu,M}_{\lambda,\delta}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\to L^{p}}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{1-\frac{2}{p}},

which concludes the proof after the usual
TT^{*}
argument.
∎

We have therefore reduced matters to proving
8.4
. Before giving the proof, a few comments might be helpful. The basic idea is to obtain a gain at
p=4
and interpolate with
p=\infty
to prove the result above. The choice of
p=4
is not related to
L^{4}
being a critical space for decoupling in dimension
3
; rather, it is chosen as an even integer which allows a connection to a lattice points counting problems, where transversality can be used very effectively. We also make use of the fact that there are very few caps in
P_{\operatorname{max}}
so that any application of the triangle inequality only incurs a marginal loss.

Proof of
8.4
.

We shall prove the result by proving
L^{4}
and
L^{\infty}
estimates and interpolating.

The
L^{4}
bound.
Let
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
be caps of diameter
(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}
that contribute to the operator
P_{\operatorname{max}}
, that is, they have near the maximum number of lattice points. We shall write
\theta\subset\nu
to mean that
\theta
is contained in the cap associated to
\nu
. By the triangle inequality, we may write

\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{3\alpha}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\subset\nu\\
\theta^{\prime}\subset\nu^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\|(P_{\theta}fP_{\theta^{\prime}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{4}},

where we used the bound (
8.4
) on
\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{max}}
.

Because
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
have near the maximum number of lattice points, the lattice points must be contained on
2
dimensional affine spaces. After applications of Parseval’s identity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one gets the following bound

\|(P_{\theta}fP_{\theta^{\prime}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{4}}\leq M^{1/4}\|P_{\theta}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|P_{\theta^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2},

where

M=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in[\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}]\times[\theta^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}],\;a+a^{\prime}=k\}.

As
\nu,\nu^{\prime}
are transverse by at least
\delta
, the normal vectors of the affine spaces that contain the lattice points in
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
must also be transverse. Indeed, by (
3.1
),

\left|\frac{Ax_{\theta}}{|Ax_{\theta}|}\times\frac{w}{|w|}\right|\lesssim\frac{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}},

where
\frac{Ax_{\theta}}{|Ax_{\theta}|}
is the unit normal to the cap and
\frac{w}{|w|}
is the unit normal to
\Lambda_{\theta}
. By choosing
\alpha
sufficiently small, we have
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\ll\delta
, hence transversality holds as caps of size
\lambda\delta
have
\delta
separated normal vectors.

Let us denote the normal vectors of the affine spaces that contain lattice points as
n_{\theta}
and
n_{\theta}^{\prime}
. The set of
(a,a^{\prime})
in the above right-hand side is contained in the set of
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

x\cdot n_{\theta}=0,\qquad y\cdot n_{\theta^{\prime}}=0,\qquad x+y=k.

These three equations define an affine subspace of dimension
1
. Therefore, the number of
(a,a^{\prime})\in[\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}]\times[\theta^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}]
such that
a+a^{\prime}=k
can be bounded by the diameter of
\theta
, in other words

M\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}.

Combining the above estimates gives

\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{8}+3\alpha}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

The
L^{\infty}
bound
The Fourier support of
P_{\nu}P_{\operatorname{max}}f
comprises at most
\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{max}}
caps of diameter
(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}
. Therefore, it contains at most
(\lambda\delta)^{1+3\alpha}
lattice points. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we therefore have

\displaystyle\|(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\|P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

Interpolating with the
L^{4}
bound gives the desired estimate and proves
8.4
, which completes the proof of
8.1
.
∎

\lambda^{-\frac{85p-152}{158-256}}
and
\lambda^{-\frac{5948-1071p}{3852-547}}
will not appear in the final theorem as they are never the limiting factor.

When
p
is large we will instead use
Eq.

9.2
for the full projector. Putting these together gives the sharp conjecture when

(9.5)

\delta>\begin{cases}\lambda^{-1+\kappa}&2\leq p\leq 4,\\
\max(\lambda^{-\frac{316-27p}{104(p-2)}+\kappa},\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+\kappa})&4\leq p<\frac{389}{79},\\
\lambda^{-\frac{9p-224}{444-158p}+\kappa}&\frac{389}{79}<p<5,\\
\min(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\kappa},\lambda^{-\frac{85p-358}{158p-128}})&5<p\leq\infty,\end{cases}

which is exactly the range in the theorem.

There is a discontinuity at
p=5
because of the behavior of caps with
\sim 1
points, as discussed in
4.5
.
If we take
\kappa=0
we get the result with an
\epsilon
-loss. This proves the main theorem.
∎

10.
Extension to other dimensions

The results of
Section

7
and
Section

8
extend to all
d\geq 2
with small adjustment. In this section, we discuss how to do this and the consequences for the higher dimensional version of the conjecture considered in this work.

We define
P_{\lambda,\delta}
in arbitrary dimension
d
in the obvious way. The caps we consider now have size
(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}\times(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}...\times(\lambda\delta)^{1/2}\times\delta
. We similarly break up the projector into

P_{\lambda,\delta}=P_{\operatorname{few}}+P_{\operatorname{med}}+P_{\operatorname{max}}.

Here,
P_{\operatorname{few}}
projects onto caps with
\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\delta+1
lattice points,
P_{\operatorname{med}}
projects onto caps that contain between
\sim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\delta+1
and
\sim\lambda^{-\alpha}(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
lattice points, and
P_{\operatorname{max}}
projects onto caps that contain
\gtrsim\lambda^{-\alpha}(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
lattice points. The parameter
\alpha
is
\ll 1
.

Adapting the methods of
Section

7
, one can easily prove the following result.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
a general torus. Let
\kappa>0
and
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{d-1}{d+1}+\kappa}
. Then

\left\lVert P_{\operatorname{few}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Note that when
d=3
, we recover the result of
7.2
. The proof is the same as the
d=3
case after making adjustments to the numerology based on the dimension, as we only use decoupling, which holds at
\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
in any dimension, trivial exponential sum estimates, and interpolation. One could use the results of
[
13
]
when
d\geq 4
to get stronger results like we have done here for
d=3
, but we do not pursue this direction.

Extending
8.2
for
P_{\operatorname{max}}
requires more work. We must always select
\alpha
small enough so that
P_{\operatorname{max}}
contains only caps whose rank is
d-1
, although the proof forces such a small
\alpha
regardless. In arbitrary
d
we have the following generalization.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\delta\gtrsim\lambda^{-1+\kappa}
, with
\kappa>0
. If
\alpha
is selected to be small enough independently of
\lambda
, we have

\|P_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{1/2}+(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})})\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

This is essentially Proposition 5.2 in
[
18
]
. That result is stated for
\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, although the proof, with minor adjustments, gives the result for all
p
.

To prove a generalization of
8.1
one also requires a generalization of
8.4
, the
L^{4}
estimate for far interacting caps. The equivalent result in
[
18
]
is stated and proved in any dimension. Here, in
d=3
, we considered the intersection of transverse planes in the proof of
8.4
and found them to be lines, which was enough of a gain to run the argument. In general, one considers the intersection of
d-1

d-2

d=2

d=3

d=4

Recall
P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}=P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}P_{\operatorname{max}}
, where
P^{\nu}_{\lambda,\delta}
is the projection onto a cap of size
\lambda\delta\times...\times\lambda\delta\times\delta

\nu
is a
\delta
separated set. We have the following.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
. If
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\gtrsim\delta

\left\lVert(P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}fP^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{d-2}{8}+d\alpha}\Bigl(\left\lVert P^{\nu}_{\operatorname{max}}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|P^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\operatorname{max}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

This, combined with
10.1
and
10.2
, implies the following improvement of Theorem 1.3 in
[
9
]
, following the scheme outlined in
Section

7
and
Section

8
. The following states sharp estimates for the

version of the conjecture considered here.

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
d=2
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}
be a general torus. Let
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}>0
and assume that
\delta>\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}+\kappa}
with
2\leq p\leq\infty
, or that
p<5-\kappa
with
\delta>\lambda^{-1+\kappa^{\prime}}

\left\lVert P_{\lambda,\delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\delta^{1/2}+(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})})\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

Similarly, we may improve Theorem 6.1 in
[

to remove

Proposition 10.5

Let

Appendix A
Additive combinatorics

Viewing the problem of
L^{p}
boundedness of spectral projectors on the Fourier side, it is well known that it becomes a problem in additive combinatorics if
p
is an even integer.

In this appendix, we point out a simple reformulation of this additive combinatorics question, but do not attempt to exploit it any further. The statements here can be generalized to any dimension.

A.1.
Additive energy

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
be the integer points in the upper part of the corona of inner radius
\lambda-\delta
and outer radius
\lambda+\delta
.

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},\,n_{1}>|n_{2}|+\dots+|n_{d}|,\,\lambda-\delta<|n|<\lambda+\delta\}.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
|A|
be its cardinality. For
p
and even integer, we define the additive energy of
A
to be

E_{\frac{p}{2}}(A)=|\{(a_{1},...,a_{p})\in A^{p}:a_{1}+...+a_{\frac{p}{2}}=a_{\frac{p}{2}+1}+...+a_{p}\}|.

The additive energy measures the additive structure of
A
. We immediately have the bound
|A|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq E(A)\leq|A|^{p-1}
. We note that some authors define the additive energy with a normalization involving
|A|
.
A conjecture about the additive structure of
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
was stated when
d=2
in
[
9
]
. We now state a version for
d=3
.

Conjecture C
.

For
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
, we have

E_{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta})\lesssim\lambda^{p}\delta^{\frac{p}{2}}+\lambda^{2p-3}\delta^{p}.

The first term on the right hand side comes from considering the universal lower bounds on additive energies in the context of
Eq.

1.3
. The second comes from the fact all lattice points in
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
are contained in a ball of radius
\lambda
, so the sum of
\frac{p}{2}
elements of
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
are spread out in a ball of radius
\lesssim_{p}\lambda
which contains
\lesssim_{p}\lambda^{3}
lattice points.

A.2.
A counting problem implying Conjecture
A

If
p=2r
is an even integer, the conjecture

\|P_{\lambda,\delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left[(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}+\lambda^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\|f\|_{L^{2}}

can be written in terms of the Fourier coefficients of
f
as

(A.1)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}=k\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\dots a_{n_{r}}\right|^{2}\lesssim\left[(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}+\lambda^{p-3}\delta^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]\|a_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{p}.

Note that

\left[(\lambda\delta)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}+\lambda^{p-3}\delta^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]=\begin{cases}\lambda\delta&\mbox{if $p=4$}\\
\lambda^{p-3}\delta^{\frac{p}{2}}&\mbox{if $p=2r$, $r\geq 3$}.\end{cases}

Denoting

Z_{\lambda,\delta}=\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\#\left\{(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta},\;n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}=k\right\}

and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the left-hand side of (
A.1
), we obtain

(A.2)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}=k\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\dots a_{n_{r}}\right|^{2}\leq Z_{\lambda,\delta}\sum_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}=k\end{subarray}}|a_{n_{1}}\dots a_{n_{r}}|^{2}\leq Z_{\lambda,\delta}\|a_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{p}.

Therefore, the conjecture would be a consequence of the following inequality

Z_{\lambda,\delta}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda\delta&\mbox{if $p=4$}\\
\lambda^{p-3}\delta^{\frac{p}{2}}&\mbox{if $p=2r$, $r\geq 3$}.\end{cases}

This bound aligns with
C
when
p=2r
and
p\geq 3
. The bound at
p=4
comes from an additive subset of
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\delta}
. Namely, considering a cap with
\sim\lambda\delta
lattice points gives the lower bound
\lambda\delta
for
Z_{\lambda,\delta}
as these lattice points line on a hyperplane. This is in alignment with
A
as the dominant term when
p=4
corresponds to a function whose fequency is supported in a single cap.

The equivalence of isocapacitary notions of mass
Luca Benatti

Abstract
. In this short note, we will prove the equivalence of the isocapacitary notions of mass. This family also includes G. Huisken’s isoperimetric mass and J. L. Jauregui’s isocapacitary mass.

Isocapacitary masses rely on remarkably milder assumptions. Indeed, they require solely the concepts of perimeter, capacity, and volume. Therefore, they extend naturally to more general settings, such as manifolds equipped with continuous metrics – a case beyond the scope of this note, yet indicative of a promising direction for future research. Moreover, they are manifestly global geometric invariants, as their expressions make no reference to local coordinates.

Their formulation was originally devised by Huisken and later by Jauregui to capture the value of the ADM mass whenever the latter is well-defined. This is evident in the model cases – such as the family of Schwarzschild spatial manifolds. In a more general scenario, the equivalence between the isoperimetric mass and the ADM mass was established by J. L. Jauregui and D. A. Lee
[
13
]
, and independently by O. Chodosh, M. Eichmair, Y. Shi, and H. Yu
[
8
]
through a different argument. As stated, both results require stronger assumptions on the metric than those needed to define the ADM mass. However, the core of the technique developed in
[
13
]
can be exploited to extend this equivalence to the full generality allowed, as shown in the paper with M. Fogagnolo and L. Mazzieri
[
4
]
. As far as the isocapacitary masses are concerned, Jauregui already proved the equivalence
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathchoice{2}{2}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{638892}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\displaystyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{638892}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\textstyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{558269}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.22499pt}{$\scriptstyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{558270}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle(2)$}}}}}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{511113}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\displaystyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{511113}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\textstyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33331pt}{\resizebox{446615}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.22499pt}{$\scriptstyle(2)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{446616}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle(2)$}}}}}}}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
in

]

[

]

\mathfrak{m}^{\mathchoice{p}{p}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{647394}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\displaystyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{647394}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\textstyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{559635}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.22499pt}{$\scriptstyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.66666pt}{\resizebox{559636}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle(p)$}}}}}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{517915}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\displaystyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{517915}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\textstyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33331pt}{\resizebox{447708}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.22499pt}{$\scriptstyle(p)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.33333pt}{\resizebox{447708}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle(p)$}}}}}}}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}

The previous discussion highlights that all isocapacitary masses are equivalent whenever the ADM mass is defined. A natural question then arises: what happens to this equivalence when the ADM mass is no longer in play? The strongest result we have been able to prove so far is that
\mathfrak{m}^{(p)}_{\iso}\to\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}
as
p\to 1^{+}

[
3
]

We assume throughout the paper that
(M,g)
is a smooth, complete, connected,

orientable, one-ended and noncompact Riemannian
3
-manifold.

The prognostic and therapeutic value of the tumor microenvironment and immune checkpoints in pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms
Authors: Zhijiang Chen, Shizhao Lin, Feihong Liang, Zelin Hou, Yuanyuan Yang, Heguang Huang, Yu Pan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (Pan-NEN) represent a group of highly heterogeneous cancers, characterized by complex and diverse biological behavior. The objective of this study was to systematically investigate the immunological features of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in Pan-NEN, aiming to identify prognostic biomarkers and explore the therapeutic potential of immunotherapy for Pan-NEN. Tumor and adjacent normal tissues were collected from 56 patients with Pan-NEN. Immunohistochemical analysis was conducted on tumor tissues using a panel of monoclonal antibodies targeting key immune markers. The expression levels of these markers were quantitatively assessed and correlated with clinicopathological features and overall survival. Low expression of CD3, CD8, CD4, CD68, CD163, Foxp3, CD56, CD69, GZMB, HLA-1, HLA-II, PD-1, and PD-L1 were observed in the majority of Pan-NEN patient samples. PD-1 expression was positively correlated with CD4 and Foxp3 expression, while PD-L1 expression was positively correlated with CD68, CD163, and Foxp3 expression; HLA-II expression was positively correlated with GZMB expression. Infiltration of lymphocytes (CD3 + or CD8+) and macrophages (CD68 + or CD163+) and expression of PD-1/PD-L1 were more pronounced in poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma (Pan-NEC) compared to well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors (Pan-NET), while CD68 and PD-L1 correlated with advanced disease stage. Conversely, HLA-I antigen expression was commonly downregulated in Pan-NEC. Univariate Cox proportional hazard analysis demonstrated that tumor grade, stage; CD4+, CD68+, and CD163 + cell count; and expression of PD-1 and PD-L1 were significantly associated with poor survival outcomes, while the positive expression of HLA-I was correlated with a more favorable survival prognosis. Furthermore, multivariate Cox proportional hazard analyses revealed that tumor grade, stage, and PD-1 expression are independent prognostic factors. The immunological landscape of Pan-NEN offers potential prognostic value and therapeutic targets. The findings suggest that immunotherapy, particularly targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, may serve as a promising strategy for the treatment of Pan-NEN, especially for Pan-NEC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75882-4.Keywords: Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, Immunoenvironment, CheckpointSubject terms: Cancer, Gastroenterology

Introduction

Pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (Pan-NEN) are a diverse and complex group of neoplasms that originate from both epithelial and neuroendocrine tissues and constitute approximately 5% of all pancreatic neoplasms1. They are pathologically classified into well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors (Pan-NET) and poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (Pan-NEC)2,3, which is a particularly aggressive variant4. Notably, the incidence of these neoplasms has been incrementally increasing, with recent estimates ranging from 0.48 to 1.01 per 100,000 individuals. Furthermore, approximately 20–64% of Pan-NEN patients are diagnosed with distant metastasis at the time of initial presentation5. A combination of surgical resection and targeted therapies has been shown to be effective for early-stage Pan-NEN6; however, the management of metastatic and recurrent Pan-NEN remains a significant challenge. Sunitinib7 and Everolimus8 have demonstrated some efficacy in the treatment of metastatic and unresectable Pan-NEN, and Pan-NEC has been demonstrated to respond to chemotherapy regimens incorporating cisplatin and etoposide6,9,10.

However, the duration of response is typically short-lived, with progression-free survival (PFS) averaging between 4 and 9 months and overall survival (OS) ranging from 10 to 19 months11. Given the current limitations in therapeutic efficacy, there is an urgent need for the development of innovative and more effective treatment methodologies.

In recent years, the tumor microenvironment (TME) has garnered significant interest due to its critical role in cancer progression and response to therapy12. Immune checkpoint inhibitors have shown remarkable efficacy across a spectrum of malignancies13–15. Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) is expressed on the surface of activated T cells, and its ligand, Programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), is extensively found on antigen-presenting cells and tumor cells16. Emerging research has highlighted that PD-L1 expression and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes within the TME hold both prognostic significance and predictive value for the clinical response to PD-1 blocking immunotherapy in various cancers17. Furthermore, downregulation of human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I is a common mechanism employed by tumor cells to evade the immune system18. The tumor antigen-specific immune response can also be influenced by the de novo expression of HLA class II antigens by tumor cells19,20.

Despite recent advances, the landscape of the tumor microenvironment in Pan-NEN remains largely uncharted. Consequently, the primary objective of this study was to systematically investigate immunological features of the tumor microenvironment in Pan-NEN, paving the way for more precise therapeutic approaches.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

This study included 56 patients diagnosed with Pan-NEN at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital between 2012 and 2017, with a mean age of 40 years. Paraffin-embedded blocks were sourced from the Pathology Department to prepare histological slides. Comprehensive data were collected, including patient demographics, histopathological findings, and surgical reports. Tumor grading was reassessed according to the 2019 World Health Organization (WHO) criteria, incorporating tumor differentiation and proliferation indices. Staging was conducted according to the 2017 European Neuroendocrine Tumor Society (ENETS) guidelines. The dataset comprised variables such as gender, age, tissue grade, and tumor stage. Informed consent was secured from all participants, and the study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital. All procedures were conducted in strict adherence to ethical standards and regulatory requirements.

Monoclonal antibodies

Immunostaining was performed for CD3 (clone MX036, read-to-use; MXB, Fuzhou, China), CD56 (clone MX039, read-to-use; MXB, Fuzhou, China), CD68 (clone KP1, read-to-use; MXB, Fuzhou, China); granzyme B (GZMB) (clone 13588-1-AP, 1:200; ProteinTech Group, Chicago, IL); CD69 (clone 10803-1-AP, 1:4000; ProteinTech Group, Chicago, IL); CD4 (clone ab133616, 1:100; Abcam, USA); CD8 (clone ab245118, 1:1000; Abcam, USA); CD163 (clone ab199427, 1:200; Abcam, USA); FOXP3 (clone #12653, 1:800; Cell Signaling Technology); PD-1 (clone NAT105, 1:80; Cell Mark, MEDAC, Wedel, Germany); PD-L1 (clone E1L3N,, 1:100; Cell Signaling Technology); HLA-A (clone ab52922, 1:100; Abcam, USA); and HLA -DR (clone ab92511, 1:500; Abcam, USA).

Immunohistochemical staining and scoring

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor sections using monoclonal antibodies against HLA I, HLA II, PD-1, PD-L1, CD3, CD4, CD8, CD69, CD68, CD163, CD56, FoxP3 and GZMB as described previously18. The procedure involved deparaffinization, hydration, PBS washing, H2O2 treatment, antigen retrieval, additional PBS washing, application of a blocking solution, incubation, and removal of excess liquid. Primary and secondary antibodies were applied sequentially, followed by DAB staining, observation of staining intensity under a microscope, and subsequent rinsing with PBS or tap water. Counterstaining with hematoxylin, differentiation with hydrochloric acid alcohol, and final steps of dehydration, clearing, mounting, and microscopic examination were conducted to complete the immunohistochemical process.

The staining intensity and percentage of stained cells were assessed by two investigators, including a pathologist who had no knowledge of sample identities. The expression levels of CD3, CD4, CD8, CD69, CD68, CD163, CD56, and FoxP3 were quantitatively assessed by enumerating the mean number of positively stained immune cells across four high-power fields (HPF) exhibiting the highest concentration of cells. A scoring system was employed to categorize the density of positive cells per HPF: 0 (no positive cells), 1 (1–20 cells/hpf), 2 (21–50 cells/hpf), and 3 (> 50 cells/hpf). Based on these scores, cases were stratified into low-density (scores 0 and 1) and high-density (scores 2 and 3) lymphocytic infiltrates. Immune cell expression of PD-1 and PD-L1 was classified as positive if staining was detected in 5% or more of the cells. Similarly, PD-L1 staining on Pan-NEN cells was deemed positive when it was present in 5% or more of the tumor cells. Tumor cell staining with monoclonal antibodies specific for HLA class I and HLA class II was evaluated and classified as positive (> 75%), heterogeneous (25–75%), or negative (< 25%) of Pan-NEN cells stained.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 22.0 software. The chi-square test was employed to compare categorical variables. Data visualization was performed using GraphPad Prism 7.01 and R in conjunction with the ggplot2 software package and ComplexHeatmap R package. Overall survival was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with survival time defined from the point of diagnosis until death attributed to the tumor or the end of the follow-up period. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed by using the Cox proportional hazards model to evaluate the significance of independent prognostic factors. Statistical significance was set at p-value > 0.05.

Results

Clinicopathological features of patients in this study

A total of 56 patients were enrolled, with an average age of 40 years. The cohort included 48.2% females and 51.8% males. Tumor grading was determined based on the morphology and Ki67 index, revealing 21.4% G1, 42.9% G2, 5.4% G3 and 30.4% Pan-NEC. Among the cases, 76.8% were at the local stage, while 23.2% of the patients presented with metastatic disease. Functional Pan-NEN were observed in five cases, comprising 3 insulinomas and 2 glucagonomas. Recurrence was noted in a single instance (Table 1).

Expression of immune molecules is decreased in Pan-NEN

To determine the extent of immune infiltration in Pan-NEN, we performed immunohistochemical staining of tumors and adjacent normal tissues. Among the 56 Pan-NEN specimens, the majority (36/56) exhibited low CD3 expression. Similarly, most of the Pan-NEN had low level expression of CD8 (36/56 samples) and CD4 (41/56 samples). The majority of the Pan-NEN also had low expression of CD68, a biomarker for macrophage infiltration (35/56 samples); CD163, an M2-associated biomarker (44/56 samples); FoxP3, a biomarker for regulatory T cells (47/56 samples); CD56, a biomarker of natural killer cells (41/56 samples); CD69, a lymphocyte activation biomarker (38/56 samples); and GZMB, a cytotoxic T lymphocyte marker (31/56 samples) (Fig. 1A).

The expression of immune cell biomarkers, immune checkpoint molecules and HLA in Pan-NEN. (A) The bar chart shows the high and low expression of immune cell molecules in tumor tissues of Pan-NEN. (B) HLA and immune checkpoint expression in tumor tissues of Pan-NEN.

For additional insight into the immune expression profile in Pan-NEN, we also evaluated the expression of immune checkpoint molecules (PD-1 and PD-L1) and HLA antigens (HLA-I and HLA-II). HLA-I was positively expressed in 48.2% (27/56) of the samples. Furthermore, HLA-II and PD-1 were each positively expressed in 42.9% (24/56) of the samples, while PD-L1 was expressed in 41.1% (23/56) of the samples (Fig. 1B). Representative sample staining results are shown (Fig. 2). Collectively, these results suggest that Pan-NEN exhibits reduced levels of immune cell infiltration.

Distinct patterns of correlation between immune molecules are observed in Pan-NEN

For further insight into the patterns of co-expression of immune markers in Pan-NEN, we performed correlation analysis. Our findings indicate a strong positive correlation between PD-1 expression and the infiltration of CD4 + and Foxp3 + lymphocytes. Similarly, PD-L1 expression was significantly positively correlated with CD68+, CD163+, and Foxp3+ infiltrating immune cells. The expression of HLA-II was positively correlated with GZMB + cells; however, no significant correlation was observed between the expression of HLA class I and the presence of immune cell markers (Fig. 3). These results suggest that infiltration of specific immune populations in Pan-NEN may correlate with HLA-II and PD-1/PD-L1 expression.

The immune cell content in the TME of Pan-NEN varies according to the tumor properties

To determine whether the immune cell content in Pan-NEN varies according to the tumor properties, we evaluated the expression of immune cell markers in Pan-NET (well-differentiated) and Pan-NEC (poorly differentiated) tumors. The infiltration of lymphocytes (CD3+ or CD8+) and macrophages (CD68 + or CD163+) was more pronounced in Pan-NEC compared to Pan-NET. This pattern was consistent with the expression of immune checkpoint molecules PD-1 and PD-L1. Conversely, HLA-I antigen tended to be less highly expressed in Pan-NEC, suggesting a potential mechanism of immune evasion in these aggressive tumors (Fig. 4, Supplementary Table 1).

Differences in immune expression in the TME of Pan-NET and Pan-NEC patient samples. The heatmap delineates the expression of immune markers in individual Pan-NET (n = 39) and Pan-NEC (n = 17) samples.

Further analysis of lymphocyte biomarkers (CD3, CD4, and CD8) according to the early and advanced stages of Pan-NEN revealed no significant differences in expression levels. However, advanced stage Pan-NEN demonstrated a higher infiltration of CD68 + macrophages. Additionally, positive PD-L1 expression was significantly elevated in advanced stage compared to early-stage Pan-NEN (Supplementary Table 2). These results suggest that advanced-stage NEN tend to have higher levels of immune infiltration and reduced levels of HLA-I.

The expression of immune molecules in Pan-NEN correlates with OS

To evaluate the prognostic significance of the immune cell repertoire in Pan-NEN, we performed univariate Cox proportional hazard analyses (Supplementary Table 3). Tumor grade, stage, and CD4+, CD68+, and CD163 + cell levels were identified as factors significantly associated with poorer survival outcomes. Additionally, positive expression of PD-1 and PD-L1 was associated a significantly worse survival. Conversely, positive HLA-I expression was correlated with a more favorable survival prognosis. Multivariate Cox proportional hazard analyses further revealed that tumor grade, stage, and PD-1 expression serve as independent prognostic factors (Table 2). These results further support the role of immune cells in determining the outcomes of Pan-NEN.

Discussion

Pan-NEN is a diverse group of neoplasms with variable prognosis. The current study delves into the intricacies of the TME in Pan-NEN, revealing a complex interplay of immune cells that correlates with patient prognosis. Our results suggest that the infiltration of macrophages in Pan-NEN is a critical component of the TME. Macrophages are versatile immune cells that play a dual role in cancer; they can either promote or inhibit tumor growth, depending on their activation state12. Our findings highlight a significant presence of tumor-associated macrophages (CD163+) within Pan-NEN, which may potentially facilitate tumor progression and metastasis. Univariate Cox proportional hazard analysis indicates that the presence of CD68 + and CD163 + macrophages is associated with poorer survival outcomes. This underscores the significance of macrophages as potential therapeutic targets and prognostic markers in Pan-NEN. Importantly, these findings suggest that targeting macrophages to modulate their phenotype or inhibit their immunosuppressive functions could serve as a promising strategy to improve treatment outcomes.

In this study, the expression rates of PD-1 and PD-L1 were calculated at 42.9% and 42.1%, respectively, which in concordance with the results of previous study21–23. We also revealed a significant association between high expression levels of PD-1 and PD-L1 and the dense infiltration of CD4+, CD163+, and Foxp3 + immune cells within the TME of Pan-NEN. By blocking the transmission of these inhibitory signals, it may be possible to reactivate the antitumor capabilities of T cells, thereby enhancing the immune response against Pan-NEN.

Our study identified reduced HLA-I expression levels in 51.8% of the 56 Pan-NEN patients. This rate is lower than the 70% reported by Theodoros Michelakos et al. in a study of 80 Pan-NEN patients24, which could potentially be explained by differences in the cohort or staining technique. However, our findings indicate that low HLA-I expression in Pan-NEN is associated with poor prognosis, a conclusion that aligns with previous studies25,26. This association underscores the importance of HLA-I in the immune surveillance of Pan-NEN and suggests that strategies aimed at restoring HLA-I expression or enhancing its presentation of tumor antigens could be a beneficial tumor immunotherapy approach. Futhermore, positive HLA-I expression in combination with negative/rare PD-L1 expression has been associated with the favorable clinical course of hepatocellular carcinoma and cholangiocarcinoma27,28, which is consistent with our findings for Pan-NEN.

HLA-II expression has been shown to have positive prognostic significance in malignant tumors such as esophagus, stomach, breast and non-small lung cancers29–31, while HLA-II expression in malignant melanoma is associated with worse prognosis32. However, our findings indicate no correlation between HLA-II expression and prognostic outcomes. Therefore, additional research will be needed to determine the functional significance of the loss of HLA-II expression in Pan-NEN.

In this study, we also compared expression values for different grades and stages of Pan-NEN. Compared to Pan-NET, Pan-NEC exhibited a higher degree of lymphocyte and macrophage infiltration, increased expression of PD-1 and PD-L1, and a greater incidence of HLA class I expression loss. This suggests that a prominent immunosuppressive microenvironment in tumors of Pan-NEC33. Because multicenter phase I clinical trial have shown pembrolizumab34,35, a PD-L1 inhibitor, has an objective response rate of 12% and a disease stabilization rate of 60% in PD-L1-positive gastrointestinal neuroendocrine neoplasms, our results suggest that immunotherapy may offer a promising new therapeutic strategy for Pan-NEC patients.

In summary, our comprehensive analysis of the TME in Pan-NEN has unveiled significant associations between immune cell infiltrates, checkpoint molecule expression, and patient prognosis. Pan-NEC has a substantial presence of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and notable expression of immune checkpoint proteins PD-1 and PD-L1, indicating a potential benefit of immunotherapy for treating Pan-NEC.

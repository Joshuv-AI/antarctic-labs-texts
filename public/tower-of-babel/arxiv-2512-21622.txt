Pohožaev identity and the existence of normalized ground state solutions for variable exponent problems
Nidhi Nidhi, Ambesh Kumar Pandey, K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In this article, we investigate normalized solutions for nonlinear problems involving variable exponents. To the best of our knowledge, normalized solutions have not been previously studied in this setting, and our results appear to be new. A key difficulty is that the standard scaling argument, which is important in the classical normalized solution approach, is no longer available in the variable exponent setup. To address this, we work with a constrained variational framework and establish the existence of a ground state solution. We further show that these solutions are C l ​ o ​ c 1 , α ​ ( ℝ N ) C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) . Finally, we derive a Pohožaev-type identity adapted to the variable exponent structure in ℝ N , \mathbb{R}^{N}, which is used to prove that the solution is a ground state. Keywords: Pohožaev identity; Normalized solutions; Variational methods; Variable exponents; Regularity. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J60, 35J20, 35J92.

Introduction

This work is concerned with the study of the following variable exponent problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p(x)}u+|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}u&=\lambda|u|^{p(x)-2}u+|u|^{q(x)-2}u\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}&=c,\end{aligned}\right.

(
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
)

where
\lambda
is a real parameter and
\Delta_{p(x)}u:=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u)
is the non-homogeneous operator called
p(x)
-Laplacian, which reduces to standard
p
-Laplacian when
p(x)\equiv p
.

To study problem (

P
λ

), we assume that the exponents satisfy the conditions listed below.

(p_{H})

The function
p:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous and

1<\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}p(x)\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}p(x)<N.

(q_{H})

k>0,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
p(x)<q(x)\ll p^{*}(x):=\frac{Np(x)}{N-p(x)}\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The notation
q\ll p^{*}
means that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(p^{*}(x)-q(x)\big)>0.

The
p(x)
-Laplacian naturally arises in the modeling of nonlinear electrorheological fluids and in various applications, including image processing, elasticity, and flow through porous media; we refer to
[
7
,
30
]
for an overview. The function spaces naturally linked to the
p(x)
-Laplacian are the variable exponent Lebesgue–Sobolev spaces, which have been studied in
[
8
,
9
]
and the references therein. The literature on variable exponent problems is extensive, so without attempting to be exhaustive, we refer to
[
1
,
11
,
18
]

for works involving potentials
V(x)
. However, none of these studies
addresses a normalized solution, which is the focus of the present paper.

The study of normalized solutions has significant importance in physics. One of the motivations for studying such solutions is that it provides the stationary state (or standing wave solutions) to the nonlinear Schr
\ddot{\text{o}}
dinger (NLS) equations. A standing wave solution for a nonlinear Schr
\ddot{\text{o}}
dinger (NLS) equation given as follows:

i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=-\Delta\psi-\mu|\psi|^{p-2}\psi,

is of the form
\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
solves

-\Delta u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.1)

While addressing solutions to (
1.1
), there exist two schools of thought. The initial approach involves fixing a
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and thereafter looking for the critical points of the associated functional. This method has already been extensively employed; see, for instance
[
5
]
. The other method is to fix the
L^{2}
-norm and look for the solution to the following constrained problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}&=&\tau^{2},\end{array}\right.

called the
normalized solution
. Recently, the study of normalized solutions has attracted the attention of many researchers. It started with the pioneering work of Jeanjean
[
19
]
, where he obtained the existence of radial solutions for the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&-\Delta u=\lambda u+g(u)\;\;\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}=c,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

under some assumptions on
g
. In
[
2
]
, the existence of infinitely many solutions to (
1.2
) under the same assumptions was established.
Further in
[
25
]
, the normalized solutions are discussed and described in the case of bounded domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions;
moreover, the problem in general bounded domains has been dealt by the authors in
[
28
]
.
There has been extensive research on the existence of normalized solutions to nonlinear Schrödinger systems; interested readers can refer to
[
15
,
3
,
26
]
for more information. More recently, regularity, multiplicity, existence, and non-existence results for normalized solutions involving mixed local and nonlocal operators have been investigated in
[
14
,
13
,
24
,
23
]
. In addition to NLS equations, the study of normalized solutions is also relevant in the study of quadratic ergodic mean field games systems, as highlighted in
[
27
]
.

In the constant exponent case, when
p(x)\equiv 2
,
N=2
and
q=4
, equation (

P
λ

)
reduces to the time-independent modified Gross-Pitaevskii equation, introduced independently by Gross
[
16
]
and Pitaevskii
[
29
]
in the study of Bose-Einstein condensation. The existence of
L^{2}
-normalized ground states for this equation has been established in
[
17
]
. More recently, Wang and Sun
[
32
]
extended these results to the case
1<p<N
and proved the existence of
L^{r}
-normalized ground state solutions for
r=p
and
r=2
. In the particular case
r=2
, their work yields at least two solutions with positive energy, one being a ground state and the other a higher-energy solution. Nguyen and Rǎdulescu
[
22

have also studied the nonlocal counterpart and obtained multiple solutions by employing the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category method.

Motivated by the above discussion, our aim is to study normalized solutions in the variable exponent setup, a problem that has been largely unexplored to date. One of the main difficulties is that the classical method used for normalized solutions in the constant exponent case does not apply here. In problems with constant exponents, one often studies the functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int|\nabla u|^{p}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}-\frac{1}{q}\int|u|^{q}\,{\rm{\rm dx}},\quad S(c)=\{u:\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{r}=c\},

through the scaling

(s\star u)(x):=e^{Ns/r}u(e^{s}x).

This defines a one-parameter curve
s\mapsto s\star u
along which one analyzes
E(s\star u)
on the Pohožaev manifold. However, when
p=p(x)
or
q=q(x)
, this scaling is not possible. Hence, we abandon the fiber-map construction and instead use constrained variational methods. We can observe that solutions of the problem (

P
λ

) can be obtained by looking for critical points of the functional

E(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}~{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}~{\rm{\rm dx}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{q(x)}}{q(x)}~{\rm{\rm dx}}

on the constraint

S(c):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{X}\text{ such that }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}=c\right\}.

Inspired by
[
4
,
32
]
, we consider the following localized version of the minimization problem above

\gamma_{c}=\inf_{S(c)\cap B_{\sigma}}E(u).

(1.3)

Here for given
\sigma>0
, we define
B_{\sigma}:=\big\{u\in\mathcal{X}|\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\sigma\big\}.
Our first aim is to prove that for every
\sigma>0
there exists

c_{0}=c_{0}(\sigma)>0
such that
S_{c}\cap B_{\sigma}\neq\emptyset
whenever
c<c_{0}
. We then show that every minimizing sequence for
\gamma_{c}
is compact for
c<c_{0}
,
and therefore
\gamma_{c}
is achieved by some
u_{c}\in S_{c}\cap B_{\sigma}
.
To ensure that the minimizers of (
1.3
) are critical points of
E
on
S_{c}
,
we need to show that the minimizers do not lie on the boundary of
B_{\sigma}\cap S_{c}
.
Once this is established, then it is classical that any minimizer
u
admits a Lagrange multiplier

\lambda=\lambda(u)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

-\Delta_{p(x)}u+|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}u-|u|^{q(x)-2}u=\lambda|u|^{p(x)-2}u.

The content of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2

introduces the functional setting and the key lemmas used throughout
the paper. In Section
3
, we establish regularity of weak normalized
solutions, proving that they belong to

Section

variable exponent case in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

showing that the weak solutions obtained are ground states. Finally, in
Section
5

weak solution.

2
Preliminaries and Statement of Main Results

In this section, we recall several definitions and preliminary results concerning variable exponents and the corresponding function spaces. We subsequently state the main result and establish the functional framework necessary for the analysis of (

P
λ

).

We begin with the following notation: for any function
\Phi(x)\in C(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})
, let

\Phi^{+}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x),\qquad\Phi^{-}:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x).

We define the function space

\mathcal{C}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{\Phi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}):1<\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x)\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(x)<\infty\}.

Let
\Omega
(bounded or unbounded) be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
p\in\mathcal{C}_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
, define

L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\to\real:u\text{ is measurable and }\int_{\Omega}|x|^{k}|u(x)|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}<\infty\right\}.

This is a Banach space endowed with the norm, known as the Luxemburg norm, given by

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\eta>0:\int_{\Omega}|x|^{k}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\eta}\right|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}\leq 1\right\}.

The following Hölder-type inequality analogous to that of the classical
L^{p}{(\Omega)}
spaces holds for the variable exponent Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{C}_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(x)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(x)}=1
.
Then for any
u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{p^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)
we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}uv{\rm{\rm dx}}\right|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{p^{-}}+\frac{1}{p^{{}^{\prime}-}}\Big)\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(\Omega)}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)}.

To handle the Luxemburg norm, we define the modular function
\rho:L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)\rightarrow\real
given as

\rho(u)=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}.

The relations between Luxemburg norm

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}
and
the corresponding modular function
\rho(\cdot)
are given as follows:

Lemma 2.2

(
[
20
,
18
]
)
.

Let
u\in L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)
, then

(i)

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}<1(=1;>1)\text{ if and only if }\rho(u)<1(=1;>1);

(ii)

If
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}>1
, then
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}^{p^{-}}\leq\rho(u)\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}^{p^{+}}
;

(iii)

If
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}<1
, then
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}^{p^{+}}\leq\rho(u)\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}^{p^{-}}.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
31
,
18
]

Let
u,u_{m}\in L^{{p(x)}}(|x|^{k},\Omega),~m=1,2,3,\cdots

(i)

\displaystyle{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}\lVert u_{m}-u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}=0;

The variable Sobolev space
W^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega):|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)\right\},

Now, we define the following weighted variable Sobolev space

\lVert u\rVert_{1,p(x)}=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(|x|^{k},\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p(x)}(\Omega)}.

We would like to point out that we can also define the following modular

Lemma 2.5

Let

Remark 2.1

From the definition of the space

Lemma 2.7

(

Let

Suppose that

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that

Proof.

We first prove the result for

Let

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{ weakly in }\mathcal{X}

(2.2)

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{p(x)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, we have

For any
\varepsilon>0

\sigma>0
such that

From (

Hence, for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\sigma}}|u_{n}|^{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{B_{\sigma}}|u_{n}|^{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

is a ground state to problem (

P
λ

)
with some
\lambda=\lambda_{c}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, it satisfies
E(u_{c})=\gamma_{c}>0
, and

u_{c}\to 0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{X}\text{ as }c\to 0.

Remark 2.2
.

From (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we can observe that,
in the constant exponent case, i.e.,
p(x)\equiv p
and
q(x)\equiv q
,
both conditions reduce to

p+\frac{p^{2}}{N}<q<p^{*}.

This is exactly the
L^{p}
-supercritical range (see
[
32
,
33
]
).
Hence, the different expressions appear simply because
p(x)
and
q(x)
are variable exponents, and these results
remain consistent with the classical constant exponent case.

Remark 2.3
.

The stronger condition on
p(x)
and
q(x)
in part
(ii)
are needed to show that the solution becomes a ground state.
The proof of part
(ii)
relies on a Pohožaev-type identity and
energy comparisons that are not available under the weaker assumption of

(i)
. In particular, the sharper lower bound on
\inf q(x)
ensures
positivity of the energy level
\gamma_{c}
for small

, which is then used
to show that

is a ground state.

Remark 2.4
.

From Lemma

, we can observe that for
q^{+}\leq p^{-}+\dfrac{p^{+}p^{-}}{N}
, using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, the functional
E(u)
is coercive and bounded below on

. Therefore, the classical variational approach can be used to prove the existence of a weak solution.

3
Regularity Results

Let us start our investigation by studying the regularity properties of a weak solution to (

P
λ

). The following proposition will be used to determine the local boundedness of the solution:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, assume that for any
B_{\sigma}\subset\subset\Omega
with
0<\sigma<R_{0}
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
k\geq k_{0}>0
,

\int_{A_{k,\sigma(1-\delta)}}|\nabla u|^{p}{\rm{\rm dx}}\leq c\left[\int_{A_{k,\sigma}}\left|\frac{u-k}{\delta\sigma}\right|^{p^{*}}{\rm{\rm dx}}+(k^{r}+1)|A_{k,\sigma}|\right],

where
0<r\leq p^{*}
,
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
is the Sobolev critical exponent,
c>0
is a constant and
A_{k,\sigma}=\big\{x\in B_{\sigma}:u(x)>k\big\}
. Then
u
is locally bounded above in
\Omega
.

A detailed proof of the above proposition can be found in
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
u\in\mathcal{X}
solves (

P
λ

) weakly, then
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be any bounded domain with smooth boundary. For any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, let
R>0
be such that
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Set

\bar{p}_{+}:=\max_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}p(x);\;\;\bar{p}_{-}:=\min_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}p(x);\;\;\bar{q}_{+}:=\max_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}q(x)\text{ and }\bar{q}_{-}:=\min_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}q(x).

Clearly,
\bar{p}_{-}<N
; and by continuity of
p
and
q
, we can find
R>0
small enough such that
q(x)<p^{*}(x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in B_{R}(x_{0})
, thus

\frac{Nq(x)}{N+q(x)}<p(x^{\prime})\text{ for all }x,x^{\prime}\in B_{R}(x_{0})

and hence

\bar{p}_{+}\leq\bar{q}_{+}<\bar{p}^{*}_{-}:=\frac{N\bar{p}_{-}}{N-\bar{p}_{-}}.

For any
y\in B_{R}(x_{0})
, let
0<t\leq s
be such that
\bar{B}_{t}(y)\subset B_{s}(y)\subset B_{R}(x_{0})
and
0<|s-t|<1
. Taking
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Supp}(\phi)\subset B_{s}(y);&0\leq\phi(x)\leq 1\text{ for all }x;\\
|\nabla\phi|\leq\frac{2}{s-t};&\text{ and }\phi(x)=1\text{ in }B_{t}(y),\end{array}\right.

we define our test function
\psi_{j}:=\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(u-j)^{+}
for some
j\geq 1
. Thus, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\psi_{j}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\psi_{j}{\rm{\rm dx}}=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\psi_{j}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q(x)-2}u\psi_{j}{\rm{\rm dx}},

denoting
A_{j,s}:=\{x\in B_{s}(y):u(x)\geq j\}
, the above equation becomes:

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{p}_{+}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}-1}(u-j)\nabla u.\nabla\phi{\rm{\rm dx}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\int_{A_{j,s}}|x|^{j}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(u-j){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(x)(u-j){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle+\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{q(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(u-j){\rm{\rm dx}}.

Let us estimate each term on the right-hand side of (
3.1
). Firstly, for any
\epsilon>0
, by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle-\bar{p}_{+}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}-1}(u-j)\nabla u.\nabla\phi{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{+}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-1}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}-1}|\nabla\phi||u-j|{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{+}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left(\left(\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\right)\left(\epsilon\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}-1}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-1}\right)^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}+\frac{1}{p(x)}\left(\frac{|\nabla\phi||u-j|}{\epsilon}\right)^{p(x)}\right){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{+}\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}-1}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\epsilon^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}\phi^{\frac{(\bar{p}_{+}-1)p(x)}{p(x)-1}}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\frac{\bar{p}_{+}}{\bar{p}_{-}}\epsilon^{-\bar{p}_{+}}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla\phi|^{p(x)}|u-j|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{+}\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}-1}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)\epsilon^{\frac{\bar{p}_{-}}{\bar{p}_{+}-1}}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)\epsilon^{-\bar{p}_{+}}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{2}{s-t}\right|^{p(x)}|u-j|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}},

taking
\epsilon>0
small enough so that

\bar{p}_{+}\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}-1}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)\epsilon^{\frac{\bar{p}_{-}}{\bar{p}_{+}-1}}<\frac{1}{2},\text{ that is, }\epsilon<\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{-}}{2\bar{p}_{+}(\bar{p}_{+}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\bar{p}_{+}-1}{\bar{p}_{-}}},

we get

\displaystyle-\bar{p}_{+}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}-1}(u-j)\nabla u.\nabla\phi{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)2^{\bar{p}_{+}}\epsilon^{-\bar{p}_{+}}\left(\int_{A_{j,s}\cap\left\{\left|\frac{u(x)-j}{s-t}\right|<1\right\}}\left|\frac{u(x)-j}{s-t}\right|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{A_{j,s}\cap\left\{\left|\frac{u(x)-j}{s-t}\right|\geq 1\right\}}\left|\frac{u(x)-j}{s-t}\right|^{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)2^{\bar{p}_{+}}\epsilon^{-\bar{p}_{+}}\left(|A_{j,s}|+\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm{\rm dx}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm{\rm dx}}+C_{1}|A_{j,s}|+C_{1}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm{\rm dx}}.

(3.2)

Next,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(x)(u-j){\rm{\rm dx}}\leq\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{p(x)-1}|u-j|{\rm dx}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}\frac{|u-j|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm dx}+\int_{A_{j,s}}\left(\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\right)\left(|u|^{p(x)-1}\right)^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)-1}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}\cap\{|u-j|<1\}}\frac{|u-j|^{p(x)}}{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm dx}+\int_{A_{j,s}\cap\{|u-j|\geq 1\}}\frac{|u-j|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}}{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm dx}+\int_{A_{j,s}}\left(\frac{p(x)-1}{p(x)}\right)|u|^{p(x)}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{j,s}|}{\bar{p}_{-}}+\frac{1}{\bar{p}_{-}}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+\left(\frac{\bar{p}_{+}-1}{\bar{p}_{-}}\right)j^{\bar{p}_{+}}|A_{j,s}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+C_{2}(j^{\bar{p}_{+}}+1)|A_{j,s}|.

Similarly,

\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{q(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(x)(u-j){\rm dx}\leq C_{3}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+C_{3}(j^{\bar{q}_{+}}+1)|A_{j,s}|,

(3.4)

and since
\Omega
is a bounded domain, there exists
C>0
such that
|x|<C
for all
x\in A_{j,s}
, hence by (
3.3
)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}|x|^{j}|u|^{p(x)-2}u\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}(u-j){\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{j}\int_{A_{j,s}}|u|^{p(x)-1}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}|u-j|{\rm dx}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+C_{2}(j^{\bar{p}_{+}}+1)|A_{j,s}|.

Using (
3
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) in (
3.1
) we get:

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{5}\left(\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+(1+j^{\bar{p}_{+}}+j^{\bar{q}_{+}})|A_{j,s}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\left(\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+(1+j^{\bar{q}_{+}})|A_{j,s}|\right),

hence,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,t}}|\nabla u|^{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,t}\cap\{|\nabla u|<1\}}|\nabla u|^{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm dx}+\int_{A_{j,t}\cap\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}}|\nabla u|^{\bar{p}_{-}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A_{s,t}|+\int_{A_{j,t}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A_{j,s}|+\int_{A_{j,t}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm dx}+\int_{A_{j,s}\setminus A_{j,t}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j,s}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\phi^{\bar{p}_{+}}{\rm dx}+|A_{j,s}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\left(\int_{A_{j,s}}\left|\frac{u-j}{s-t}\right|^{\bar{p}^{*}_{-}}{\rm dx}+(1+j^{\bar{q}_{+}})|A_{j,s}|\right).

Since,
t<s
, we can replace

by

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, hence by Proposition

,
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
. Further, since

, the boundedness can be extended to
\Omega
as done in
[

, Lemma 3.6]
. Thus

and hence
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

4
Pohožaev Identity

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
2.2
. This Pohožaev-type identity plays an important role in the characterization of ground state solutions.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\phi=1
in
B_{1}(0)
,
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
\phi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2}(0)
. Define
v_{t}(x):=\phi(tx)x\cdot\nabla u
. Clearly,
v_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
, by regularity of

(see
Theorem 2.1
). Multiplying (

P
λ

) by
v_{t}
and integrating it over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u)v_{t}{\rm dx}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}{\rm dx}

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q(x)-2}uv_{t}{\rm dx}.

Now, by the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}x\cdot\nu d\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}x}{p(x)}\right){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\right){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)-2}u(x)(x.\nabla u){\rm{\rm dx}}+t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}(x\cdot\nabla\phi(tx)){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)x\cdot\nabla p(x){\rm{\rm dx}},

thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(N\phi(tx)+tx\cdot\nabla\phi(tx))\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)\frac{\phi(tx)|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}x\cdot\nabla p(x){\rm{\rm dx}}.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}=-N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)\frac{|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}x\cdot\nabla p(x){\rm{\rm dx}},

(4.2)

similarly,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q(x)-2}uv_{t}=-N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{q(x)}}{q(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{q(x)}\right)\frac{|u|^{q(x)}}{q(x)}x\cdot\nabla q(x){\rm{\rm dx}}.

(4.3)

Again, using the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}x.\nu d\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}x}{p(x)}\right){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\right){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N+k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}u(x\cdot\nabla u){\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle+t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}(x\cdot\nabla\phi(tx)){\rm{\rm dx}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)(x\cdot\nabla p(x)){\rm{\rm dx}},

thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left((N+k)\phi(tx)+tx\cdot\nabla\phi(tx)\right)\frac{|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)\frac{\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}(x\cdot\nabla p(x)){\rm{\rm dx}},

and hence, by the dominated convergence theorem, we get:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)-2}uv_{t}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(N+k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}{\rm dx}

(4.4)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)\frac{|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}x\cdot\nabla p(x){\rm dx}.

Next, we will estimate the first term in the LHS of (
4.1
). Since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\phi(tx)x_{j}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right){\rm dx},

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-t\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x_{j}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\phi(tx)}{\partial x_{i}}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-t\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x_{j}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial\phi(tx)}{\partial x_{i}}{\rm dx}

(4.5)

Now, multiplying (

P
λ

) by
\phi(tx)u

\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p(x)}\phi(tx){\rm dx}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q(x)}\phi(tx){\rm dx}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(tx)|x|^{k}|u|^{p(x)}{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u)\phi(tx)u{\rm dx}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\phi(tx)u{\rm dx}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\phi(tx)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}+ut\frac{\partial\phi(tx)}{\partial x_{i}}\right){\rm dx}

Remark 4.1

Let us consider the term

Definition 4.1
.

Let
r_{0}>0
and let

be a constant.
We define
\mathcal{P}(r_{0})
as the family of variable exponents

of the form

where:

•

Lemma 6.1
.

Ler

as defined as in Remark

, and assume that

Proof.

Let

then for

x\cdot\nabla p(x)=|x|\,\eta^{\prime}(|x|)\,(p(x)-p_{0}),\qquad r_{0}<r<2r_{0},

For convenience, we use the following notation

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)|u|^{p(x)}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla p(x))}{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{q(x)}\right)|u|^{q(x)}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla q(x))}{q(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)|u|^{p(x)}|x|^{k}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla p(x))}{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

\displaystyle R_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|\nabla u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)|\nabla u|^{p(x)}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla p(x))}{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}

Estimates for

and

\displaystyle R_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\ln|u|-\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)|u|^{p(x)}\frac{(x\cdot\nabla p(x))}{p(x)}\,{\rm{\rm dx}}.

First-line camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin for advanced non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer with brain metastases (CAP-BRAIN): final results of a multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase 2 trial in China
Authors: Xue Hou, Chengzhi Zhou, Guowu Wu, Wen Lin, Zhanhong Xie, Haibo Zhang, Jiqun Yi, Jingye Lv, Fengzhuo Cheng, Fenglin She, Likun Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundThe primary analysis of the CAP-BRAIN trial has shown preliminary antitumour activity and safety of first-line camrelizumab plus chemotherapy in patients with advanced non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and brain metastases (BMs). Here, we report the final efficacy analysis of the CAP-BRAIN study following an additional 3-years of follow-up from the data cut-off date of the primary analysis, along with updated safety data.MethodsIn this multicentre, open-label, single-arm, phase 2 trial conducted at seven hospitals in China, 45 adult patients with treatment-naive metastatic non-squamous NSCLC and asymptomatic BMs (or intracranial hypertension symptom controlled with dehydration therapy) were treated with four cycles of camrelizumab (200 mg) plus pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) and carboplatin (area under the curve 5). This combination was given intravenously on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. After four cycles, patients received maintenance therapy with camrelizumab and pemetrexed for up to 24 months of camrelizumab, or until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity or death. Primary endpoint was intracranial objective response rate (iORR) in patients with at least one post-baseline tumour assessment (efficacy analysis set). Secondary endpoints were intracranial progression-free survival (iPFS), overall PFS, and overall survival (OS) in all enrolled patients (full analysis set). Both are updated in this final analysis at the end of follow-up. This extended follow-up was not prespecified in the study protocol. At data cutoff (May 22, 2025), the median duration of follow-up was 46.7 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 40.7–53.8). The updated confirmed iORR was 57.5% (95% CI 40.9–73.0) in the efficacy analysis set and 51.1% (35.8–66.3) in the full analysis set. The median iPFS was 8.4 months (95% CI 6.2-not reached), and the median overall PFS was 7.2 months (4.5–21.0). The median OS was 18.4 months (95% CI 10.3–29.9). Treatment-related adverse events were reported in 41 (91.1%) of 45 patients. Research in context

Evidence before this study

We searched PubMed for all publications of immunotherapy in patients with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and brain metastases published, in English, between database inception and April 30, 2025, with the search terms “non-small-cell lung cancer”, “brain metastases”, and (“PD-1” or “PD-L1” or “immune checkpoint inhibitors” or “nivolumab” or “pembrolizumab” or “sintilimab” or “toripalimab” or “tislelizumab” or “camrelizumab” or “atezolizumab” or “durvalumab”). This search yielded 344 articles. After screening for clinical trial articles, we identified two phase 2 trials focused on non-squamous NSCLC and brain metastases in existing literature. The phase 2 Atezo-Brain trial showed that atezolizumab combined with carboplatin and pemetrexed resulted in a median overall survival of 11.8 months in patients with advanced non-squamous NSCLC and untreated brain metastases. Another phase 2 trial conducted by Zhao et al. demonstrated that the median overall survival was not reached with tislelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin in treatment-naïve non-squamous NSCLC patients with brain metastases. Here, we report the final analysis (efficacy and safety) of the CAP-BRAIN study (with an additional 3 years of follow-up), for which the primary analysis CAP-BRAIN trial has shown preliminary antitumour activity and safety of first-line camrelizumab plus chemotherapy in patients with advanced NSCLC and brain metastases.

Added value of this study

To our knowledge, CAP-BRAIN is the first prospective trial to report the activity and safety of camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin as first-line therapy in Chinese non-squamous NSCLC patients with brain metastases. Our final survival data, representing a median duration of 46.7 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 40.7–53.8) of follow-up, showed promising survival benefit with this combination therapy against brain metastases. The updated confirmed intracranial objective response rate was 57.5% (95% CI 40.9–73.0); median intracranial progression-free survival was 8.4 months (95% CI 6.2-not reached); median overall progression-free survival was 7.2 months (95% CI 4.5–21.0); and median overall survival was 18.4 months (95% CI 10.3–29.9). No unexpected safety signals and additional treatment-related deaths were observed.

Implications of all the available evidence

The final results of this CAP-BRAIN study provide preliminary evidence supporting the promise of camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin in the first-line treatment of metastatic non-squamous NSCLC with brain metastases. Further investigation is warranted in a randomised controlled trial.

Introduction

Brain metastasis (BM) is a prevalent complication among various solid tumours, particularly in patients with lung cancer.1 It is estimated that approximately one-quarter of patients with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) present with BM at the time of initial diagnosis.2 This patient population typically experience a shorter survival and a significantly diminished quality of life (QOL), characterised by more severe cognitive impairment and symptomatic disturbances, compared to those without BM.3,4 For patients with BM, local radiotherapy has traditionally been used as the mainstay of treatment, supplemented by systemic therapy to control extracranial disease. However, due to the neurotoxicity of radiotherapy, the feasibility of delayed radiotherapy in NSCLC patients with BM is currently being explored in the clinic, especially in patients not harbouring oncogenic driver mutations.5

Immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy has evolved as the cornerstone of first-line treatment for NSCLC, yet data on the intracranial activity of this regimen in NSCLC patients with BM remain scarce. The phase 2 CAP-BRAIN trial investigated the efficacy and safety of first-line camrelizumab in combination with pemetrexed and carboplatin in patients with metastatic non-squamous NSCLC and BMs.6 At the primary analysis of the CAP-BRAIN study (median follow-up of 12.5 months), confirmed intracranial objective response rate (iORR, primary endpoint) was 52.5% (95% confidence interval [CI] 36.1–68.5) in the efficacy analysis population. Median intracranial progression-free survival (iPFS) and overall PFS were 7.6 months (95% CI 4.6-not reached [NR]) and 7.4 months (95% CI 4.4-NR), respectively. Median overall survival (OS) of 21.0 months (95% CI 15.9-NR) was also reported. Additionally, a positive trend toward improvement in cognitive function and QOL of patients was noted. Toxicities with camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin were manageable.

Here, we present the final efficacy analysis of the CAP-BRAIN study following an additional approximately 3-year of follow-up from the data cut-off date of the primary analysis, along with updated safety data.

Methods

Study design, patients and treatments

The CAP-BRAIN study design has been published elsewhere.6 Briefly, eligible patients were aged ≥18 years, had previously untreated pathologically or cytologically confirmed non-squamous NSCLC, had no EGFR mutations or ALK translocations, had brain metastases that were either asymptomatic or had symptoms of intracranial hypertension that were controlled by dehydration therapy with dexamethasone and/or mannitol, had ≥1 measurable brain lesion with a minimum diameter of 5 mm as per according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours version 1.1 (modified RECIST 1.1),7,8 and had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1. Patients were excluded if they had previously received prior systemic anti-tumour therapy, undergone solid organ or blood system transplantation, or if they had any of the following disease: active autoimmune disease, immunodeficiency disease, untreated active hepatitis B, active hepatitis C virus infection, or active pulmonary tuberculosis. Eligible patients were treated with four cycles of camrelizumab (200 mg), pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) and carboplatin (area under curve of 5), followed by maintenance treatment with camrelizumab and pemetrexed for up to 24 months of camrelizumab. These agents were given intravenously on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. Dexamethasone (≤10 mg daily or equivalent) and/or mannitol was allowed for control of intracranial hypertension. The study protocol is available in the Supplementary Materials.

Ethics

The study was approved by the ethics committee of each participating centre. All patients provided written informed consent. The study protocol was approved by the medical ethics committee of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (No. B2019-203-01), The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University (No. 2020-111), Meizhou People's Hospital (No. 2020-C-19), Cancer Hospital of Shantou University Medical College (No. 2020008), Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (No. BF2020-104-01), Guangzhou Red Cross Hospital (No. 2021-214-01), Nanfang Hospital (No. NFEC-2021-111). This study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT04211090). Written informed consent was provided by each patient.

Randomisation and masking

Due to being an open-label and single-arm trial, no randomisation and masking were performed.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary endpoint was iORR according to modified RECIST 1.1. Secondary endpoints included iPFS, overall PFS (oPFS), extracranial and overall ORR (eORR and oORR, respectively), disease control rate (DCR), duration of response (DoR), time to response, OS and safety. DCR was assessed on the basis of three dimensions: intracranial (iDCR), extracranial and overall, while DoR and time to response (TTR) were evaluated in intracranial and overall settings. Exploratory endpoints were cognitive function and QOL. The detailed definitions of endpoints have been described previously.6

Tumour and brain imaging, including chest and abdomen contrast-enhanced computed tomography, and brain contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging, was scheduled every 6 weeks for the first 48 weeks and every 9 weeks thereafter. Intracranial and extracranial responses were assessed as per modified RECIST 1.1 and RECIST 1.1, respectively. Survival follow-up was performed every 2 months after treatment discontinuation. This extended follow-up was not prespecified in the study protocol, though the primary analysis called for longer follow-up due to data immaturity at data cutoff.

Statistical analysis

As previously described,6 the sample size was calculated based on the precision of the iORR estimator in term of the width of CI. Assuming an iORR of 50%, at least 35 evaluable patients were required to provide a width of the two-sided 95% CI of 34.6%. Accounting for dropouts and loss to follow-up, the total sample size was set at 45 patients.

Endpoints associated to tumour response were assessed both in the efficacy analysis set (EAS, defined as patients with at least one post-baseline tumour assessment) and the full analysis set (FAS, defined as patients treated with at least one dose of study treatment). Other endpoints were evaluated in the FAS. Safety analysis set was the same as the FAS. Time-to-event endpoints were estimated with the Kaplan–Meier method. The 95% CIs were calculated using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method for time-to-event endpoints, the log–log transformation method for survival rates, and the Clopper-Pearson method for ORR and DCR. For binary endpoints, non-responder imputation was applied to handle missing data. For PFS, patients who did not experience tumour progression or deaths, or who received other anti-tumour therapies, were censored at the date of their last tumour assessment; while those without any tumour assessment were censored at the date of first dose of study treatment. For OS, patients without death were censored at the last known date alive. Post-progression survival was analysed post hoc using the same methods as other time-to-event endpoints. Post-hoc subgroup analyses for iORR and OS were conducted based on baseline patient characteristics. One-tailed upper 95% CI for post hoc safety assessment was estimated by the Wilson method. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4.

Role of the funding source

The funder provided camrelizumab, and participated in data analysis and data interpretation, but had no role in study design, data collection, or writing of the report. The corresponding author had final responsibility for the decision to submit for publication.

Results

Patients and treatments

Between April 13, 2020 and May 12, 2022, 45 (93.8%, 95% CI 83.2–97.9) of 48 patients screened patients were enrolled across seven centres (Supplementary Table S1), and were included in the FAS. Of these patients, five patients lacked post-baseline tumour assessment data; therefore, a total of 40 patients were included in the EAS. The baseline characteristics of the 45 patients in the FAS have been previously described.6 The median age was 59 years (interquartile range [IQR] 56–64), and 40 (88.9%) patients were male (Table 1). 18 (40.0%) patients had four or more brain metastatic lesions. Additionally, 13 (28.9%) had symptomatic BMs at baseline, of which seven patients were on dexamethasone plus mannitol to manage intracranial hypertension and two were on mannitol treatment. The median dose of dexamethasone was 5 mg (range 5–10). The baseline characteristics of the 40 patients in the EAS and of the 45 patients in the FAS (stratified by dexamethasone use) are described in Supplementary Table S2.

At data cutoff date (May 22, 2025), median duration of follow-up was 46.7 months (95% CI 40.7–53.8). Trial profile.

Updated efficacy

At data cutoff, the confirmed iORR was 57.5% (95% CI 40.9–73.0), eORR was 50.0% (95% CI 33.8–66.2), and oORR was 47.5% (95% CI 31.5–63.9) in the EAS (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4, and Fig. 2). The iDCR was 87.5% (95% CI 73.2–95.8) and the median intracranial TTR was 1.5 months (IQR 1.4–3.1). Median duration of intracranial responses was 19.8 months (95% CI 5.6-not evaluable [NE]), and 14 (60.9%) of the 23 responders had no subsequent disease progression or death from any cause at data cutoff. Post-hoc subgroup analyses of iORR are presented in Supplementary Fig. S1. Additionally, in the FAS, the confirmed iORR, eORR, and oORR was 51.1% (95% CI 35.8–66.3), 44.4% (95% CI 29.6–60.0), and 42.2% (95% CI 27.7–57.8), respectively (Table 2 and Supplementary Table S5).

Updated safety

The updated treatment-related AE (TRAE) profile of camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin was consistent with our primary analysis.6 With longer follow-up, there were still 41 (91.1%) patients who experienced at least one TRAEs of any grade, with the most common being anemia and reactive cutaneous capillary endothelial proliferation (both 57.8%; Table 3 and Supplementary Table S6). In terms of grade 3 or higher TRAEs, one additional patient had grade 3 hypothyroidism as compared with the primary analysis, that is, a total of 16 (35.6%) patients with grade 3 or higher TRAEs. Moreover, since the primary analysis, one patient developed an additional grade 3 immune-related diabetic ketoacidosis. No additional patients experienced TRAE-related deaths.

Neurological AEs during the study period, regardless of their causal relationship with the study treatment, are listed in Supplementary Table S7. In brief, most neurological AEs were grade 1 (17 [37.8%]), and only two patients (4.4%) experienced grade 3 neurological AEs: one patient each with grade 3 oedema cerebral and dysstasia. The median time to the onset of any neurological AE was 9.1 months (95% CI 1.1-NR). The majority of NAEs were managed with clinical observation, with symptomatic treatment administered only once these events substantially affected patients’ QOL.

Post-progression survival

A total of nine patients experienced exclusive intracranial progression, six developed extracranial progression, and seven suffered progression in both compartments. Among patients with exclusive intracranial progression, the median post-progression survival was 14.5 months (95% CI 12.1-NR), with the 1- and 2-year survival rates of 88.9% (95% CI 68.4–100.0) and 44.4% (95% CI 7.4–81.5), respectively (Supplementary Fig. S4). Among patients with extracranial progression, the median post-progression survival was 7.8 months (95% CI 1.3–15.7; Supplementary Fig. S5). The corresponding survival rate was 33.3% (95% CI 0.0–71.1) at 1 year and 16.7% (95% CI 0.0–46.5) at 2 years. Meanwhile, the median post-progression survival for those with simultaneous intracranial and extracranial progression was 7.9 months (95% CI 3.3-NR), with the 1-year rate of 42.9% (95% CI 6.2–79.5; Supplementary Fig. S6). In addition, among patients with overall progression, the median post-progression survival was 12.3 months (95% CI 7.9–28.7), with the 1- and 2-year survival rates of 58.7% (95% CI 38.0–79.5) and 28.5% (95% CI 6.9–50.1), respectively (Supplementary Fig. S7).

Subsequent therapies after disease progression

Among the 25 patients who experienced disease progression and received subsequent antitumour therapy, the predominant second-line regimen was antiangiogenic agents combined with chemotherapy (36.0%), followed by chemotherapy alone (16.0%) and PD-1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy (16.0%; Supplementary Table S8). The median durations of these three second-line regimens were 3.9 months (IQR 2.2–9.2), 0.7 months (IQR 0.5–3.9), and 2.7 months (IQR 1.5–4.9), respectively. A total of 10 patients with disease progression received subsequent brain radiotherapy: each five with stereotactic radiotherapy (SRT) and whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT). The median time from the initiation of study treatment to brain radiotherapy was 7.7 months (IQR 5.9–11.1). The median post-intracranial progression survival was 13.6 months (95% CI 8.2-NR) and 12.5 months (95% CI 1.6-NR) in patients with or without subsequent brain radiotherapy, respectively (Supplementary Fig. S8).

Discussion

This final analysis of the CAP-BRAIN trial confirmed the primary results that the combination of camrelizumab, pemetrexed and carboplatin exhibited promising activity in NSCLC patients with BM. In this analysis, with an additional approximately 3-year follow-up, camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin resulted in the confirmed iORR of 57.5% (95% CI 40.9–73.0), median iPFS of 8.4 months (95% CI 6.2-NR), median oPFS of 7.2 months (95% CI 4.5–21.0) and median OS of 18.4 months (95% CI 10.3–29.9). Furthermore, our findings with extended follow-up continued to demonstrate the manageable safety profile of this combination regimen.

The anti-tumour activity of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy has also been investigated in other prospective studies of untreated NSCLC with BMs. In phase 2 Atezo-Brain trial of atezolizumab combined with carboplatin and pemetrexed, the iORR was 42.7% (95% CI 28.1–57.9), median iPFS was 6.9 months (95% CI 4.7–11.9), median systemic PFS was 8.9 months (95% CI 6.7–13.8), and median OS was 11.8 months (95% CI 7.6–16.9) in patients with advanced non-squamous NSCLC with untreated BMs.9 Another phase 2 trial from Zhao et al. also reported the primary efficacy of first-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in NSCLC patients with BMs, with the iORR of 46.7% (95% CI 28.3–65.7%), 1-year iPFS rate of 55.8% (95% CI 30.7–75.0%), and median systemic PFS of 7.5 months (95% CI 5.1–18.4).10 The intracranial response in our study was relatively high (iORR: 57.5%; median iPFS: 8.4 months). The differences in intracranial response between our trial and the aforementioned two trials may be impacted by the criteria for assessing intracranial efficacy, with modified RECIST used in our study versus RANO-BM criteria adopted in the other two studies. Additionally, our median OS was also numerically longer than that reported in the Atezo-Brain trial, which may be attributed to differences in patient characteristics, including the proportion of patients receiving dexamethasone (55.0% in the Atezo-Brain trial versus 15.6% in our study), the burden of BMs (≥5 lesions in more than 50% of patients versus ≥4 lesions in 40% of patients, respectively), and differences in racial distribution (Caucasian versus Chinese, respectively).6,9 Overall, our updated results further added evidence of the benefit of first-line immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in NSCLC patients with BMs.

Local radiotherapy has historically constituted the cornerstone for patients with BMs; however, out of concern for the potential neurocognitive sequelae attributed to conventional radiotherapy, withholding upfront radiotherapy has been a clinical endeavor. In NSCLC patients with driver gene alterations, such as EGFR mutations, ALK or ROS1 rearrangements, and RET fusions, appropriate deferral of radiotherapy has already preliminary emerged as a routine practice.11, 12, 13 While in driver-gene negative patients, upfront radiotherapy is recommended in those with symptomatic BMs, but debate remains over whether to delay radiotherapy in asymptomatic populations.14 In this study, we found that among patients with intracranial progression, a comparable post-intracranial progression survival was observed in patients with or without delayed brain radiotherapy (13.6 versus 12.5 months). Furthermore, on the basis of our primary data and the data from Atezo-Brain trial, Lu and Acker et al. advocated that first-line immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy should be recommended for asymptomatic or symptom-controlled BMs, from the perspective that postponing upfront radiotherapy may prevent or defer brain radiotherapy-associated neurologic impairment, thereby preserving patients' QOL and functional independence.5,15 However, Lehrer and Vis et al. cautioned against this optimism, arguing that it is premature to determine this deferral as stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) is as effective as WBRT but results in fewer cognitive toxicities as well as reduces steroid use and health care cost.16,17 These arguments suggest that in the era of immunotherapy, the optimal timing and modality of brain radiotherapy for untreated NSCLC with BMs require further investigation in large-sample studies.

The updated safety results were generally consistent with the established safety profile of camrelizumab plus chemotherapy in non-squamous NSCLC, without the emergence of any unexpected safety signals.18,19 Most neurologic AEs were mild, and were managed with clinical observation, with symptomatic treatment administrated once these events substantially affected patients’ QOL. Since August 30, 2022, there was one additional case of grade 3 diabetic ketoacidosis adjudicated as drug-related reported. This patient was managed in accordance with established irAE management guidelines, with the discontinuation of camrelizumab as recommended. Diabetic ketoacidosis is an uncommon immune-mediated endocrinopathy that is triggered by hyperglycemia, which may be attributed to the initiation of autoimmune processes by both cellular and humoral immunity in the body after anti-PD-1 immunotherapy.20

The novelty of this study lies in being the first to prospectively report the 3-year survival rate of first-line single immune checkpoint blockade combined with chemotherapy in Chinese patients with metastatic non-squamous NSCLC and BMs, thereby filling the gap regarding long-term survival outcomes in this population. In addition, our study also concerned post-progression survival, providing certain reference for clinical practice. However, this study has limitations. First, the overall sample size was small, particularly the limited number of female patients, which restricted the assessment of safety and the interpretation of sex-related differences. It is also notable that the extremely small sample size in the dexamethasone use subgroup renders the observed difference from this exploratory OS analysis highly susceptible to potential bias and chance effects. Results should be interpreted with caution. Second, the single-arm design without a control group limited the findings to descriptive statistical analyses. Further confirmation of our findings is warranted in a randomised controlled trial. Third, patient-reported outcomes, QOL, and neurocognitive assessments were not continuously collected after treatment discontinuation in the present study. Consequently, the absence of these data may limit the interpretation of the clinical relevance of the OS data. Further research is warranted.

In conclusion, this final analysis of CAP-BRAIN continues to demonstrate the promising benefit and manageable toxicity of first-line camrelizumab plus pemetrexed and carboplatin in advanced non-squamous NSCLC patients with BMs, suggesting the potential for delayed radiotherapy in the upfront setting. These results warrant future studies.

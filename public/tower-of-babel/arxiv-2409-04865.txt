Vortex dynamics in two-dimensional supersolids
Chi-Deuk Yoo, Alan T. Dorsey

Abstract
We investigate the dynamics of quantized vortices in a model two-dimensional supersolid. Starting from an effective action that captures the dynamics of the superfluid condensate and its coupling to the lattice displacements, we integrate out the low-energy Goldstone modes–the phonons of the solid and the superfluid condensate–to arrive at an effective action for the vortices in the condensate. In the low-velocity limit we calculate the effective inertial mass for the vortices, and we find that the mass has a logarithmic frequency dependence, similar to the inertial mass found in superfluid vortices. The vortex dynamics also includes a Magnus force term in the equation of motion that arises from the Berry phase in the effective action.



I
Introduction

Supersolidity is an elusive phase of matter with coexisting off-diagonal long-range order
[
1
]
and crystalline order (with the accompanying shear rigidity of a solid). The foundational work on Bose condensation by Penrose and Onsager
[
2

3

Leggett
[
6
,
7
]
suggested that such a phase could be detected by observing nonclassical rotational inertia (NCRI) in a rotating supersolid. Two decades of experimental searches failed to yield a definitive observation of this phase
[
8
]
; after a long dormant period, in 2004 Kim and Chan announced the observation of a small NCRI signal in crystals of
4
He
[
9
,
10
]
. This observation ushered in a decade of intense experimental and theoretical work to elucidate the properties of the supersolid phase of matter; we refer the reader to Ref.
[
11
]
for a summary. Alas, successively refined experimental techniques resulted in smaller observed values of NCRI, until in 2012 the NCRI effect was shown to be absent in carefully prepared
4
He crystals
[
12
]
.

In parallel with the work on
4
He, the ultracold-atom community developed techniques to trap and condense dipolar bosons, including lanthanides such as
164
Dy and
166
Er. Like their classical brethren, these quantum dipolar systems organize into complex patterns, including labyrinths, stripes, and bubbles; for accessible reviews, see
[

[

,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19

. These new experimental studies motivate the present work on topological defects in supersolids.

Topological defects are ubiquitous in systems with a broken continuous symmetry, be they vortices in a superfluid or dislocations in a solid. In a supersolid, we have both types of defect; barring a hidden symmetry or fine-tuning of parameters, we expect these defects to interact. These point defects are particularly important in two-dimensions at finite temperatures, as their proliferation destroys the ordered phase (vortices destroying the broken
U(1)
symmetry of the superfluid and dislocations the broken translational symmetry of the solid). In this work we focus on vortices in a two-dimensional supersolid, leaving to future work the discussion of dislocations and their interaction with vortices. We note that the observation of vortices in rotating supersolid
164
Dy has recently been reported in Ref.
[
20

We use a hydrodynamic approach, which captures the physics of long-lived, low frequency excitations that emerge from conserved densities and the Goldstone modes of the broken symmetry phases. II
Effective action for supersolids

We start with the quadratic action for isotropic supersolids, derived in Ref.
[
33
]

(see Appendix A for its derivation and a brief summary of Ref.
[
33
]
), expressed in terms of the dynamical variables which are the superfluid phase
\theta
and components of the lattice displacement
u_{i}
:

\begin{split}S[\theta,u_{i}]=&\frac{1}{2}\int dt\int d^{2}x\bigg[-2\rho\partial_{t}\theta-2\lambda_{ij}u_{ij}+\rho^{2}\chi(\partial_{t}\theta)^{2}\\
&-\rho_{\text{s}}(\partial_{i}\theta)^{2}+\rho_{\text{n}}(\partial_{t}u_{i})^{2}-(\tilde{\lambda}-\rho^{2}\gamma^{2}\chi)u_{ii}^{2}\\
&-2\tilde{\mu}u_{ij}^{2}-(\rho_{\text{n}}-\rho^{2}\gamma\chi)(\partial_{t}u_{i}\partial_{i}\theta+u_{ii}\partial_{t}\theta)\bigg],\end{split}

(1)

with
\rho_{\text{n}}
the density of normal component,
\rho_{\text{s}}
the density of the super-component, and
\rho=\rho_{\text{n}}+\rho_{\text{s}}
the total density;
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\mu}
are the Lamé coefficients
[
40
]
for an isotropic solid;
\gamma
is a phenomenological coupling constant between the strain and the density;
\chi^{-1}
is the isothermal compressibility; and

u_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{i}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{i}\right)

(2)

is the linearized symmetric strain tensor (
\partial_{i}\equiv\partial/\partial x_{i}
with Roman subscripts reserved for Cartesian coordinates, and
\partial_{t}\equiv\partial/\partial t
in what follows). A few comments on the above action are helpful here.

First, the density fluctuation was integrated out of the action, renormalizing the coupling between the normal velocity (
\partial_{t}u_{i}
) and the superfluid velocity (
\partial_{i}\theta
) from
\rho_{\text{n}}
to
\rho_{\text{n}}-\rho^{2}\gamma\chi
, and the elastic constant from
\tilde{\lambda}
to
\tilde{\lambda}-\rho^{2}\gamma^{2}\chi
.
In order to keep the notation simple, we use
\tilde{\Lambda}\equiv\tilde{\lambda}-\rho^{2}\gamma^{2}\chi
and
\tilde{\Gamma}\equiv\rho_{\text{n}}-\rho^{2}\gamma\chi
hereafter. Note that when
\tilde{\Gamma}=0~(\rho_{\text{n}}=\rho^{2}\gamma\chi)
, the elastic part and superfluid terms become completely decoupled.

Second, we have explicitly isolated the interaction terms between superfluidity and elasticity as
\partial_{t}u_{i}\partial_{i}\theta
and
u_{ii}\partial_{t}\theta
. Note that the phase
\theta
is
odd
under time reversal (as gradients of
\theta
produce the superfluid velocity) and the
u_{i}
are
even
under time reversal, so the two coupling terms above are both
even
under time reversal as required for a Lagrangian that is symmetric under time reversal. If the fields
u_{i}
and
\theta
are analytic, then an integration by parts allows one to combine these two terms. However, this is no longer possible with the singular fields due to topological defects.

Third, the first two terms in the action, which are linear in
\theta
and
u_{i}
, generally can be ignored as long as vortices and dislocations are absent because they are the total derivatives of analytical dynamical variables. However, the first term is responsible for the Magnus force on vortices
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
and the second term produces the Peach-Koehler force on a dislocation
[
41
]
. In this work, we disregard the second term as we focus only on vortices–dislocations and their coupling with vortices in a supersolid will be presented elsewhere.

The equations of motion can be obtained by taking the variations of
S
with respect to
\theta
and
u_{i}
,

\tilde{\chi}\partial_{t}^{2}\theta-\rho_{\text{s}}\partial^{2}\theta-\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}}{2}(\partial_{i}\partial_{t}u_{i}+\partial_{t}u_{ii})=0,

(3)

\rho_{\text{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{i}-\partial_{j}\sigma_{ij}-\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}}{2}(\partial_{t}\partial_{i}\theta+\partial_{i}\partial_{t}\theta)=0,

(4)

where
\tilde{\chi}\equiv\rho^{2}\chi
, and the elastic stress tensor

\sigma_{ij}=\tilde{\Lambda}\delta_{ij}u_{kk}+2\tilde{\mu}u_{ij}.

(5)

Equations (
3
) and (
4
) are three equations of motion that yield three pairs of sound modes for two-dimensional isotropic supersolids: transverse (speed
c_{T}
) and longitudinal (speed
c_{L}
) elastic modes and a longitudinal second sound mode (speed
c_{2}
); expressions for the sound speeds can be found in Ref.
[
33
]
, Eqs. (48)-(51). The above Lagrangian was also used recently in Ref.
[
42
]
to study the collective modes of a one-dimensional supersolid phase.

In what follows we take vortices into account by introducing singular parts of
\theta
in the action, Eq. (
1
), and integrate out the smooth superfluid and elastic fields, resulting in an effective action for the vortices.

III
Vortices in two-dimensional supersolids

For a vortex
\alpha
of integer winding number (or charge)
e^{\alpha}
the counterclockwise line integral of
\theta
is given by

\oint d\theta=\frac{h}{m}e^{\alpha},

(6)

where

the particle mass (Greek superscripts label vortices). Separating

into its smooth part

\theta=\theta^{\text{S}}+\theta^{\text{V}},

\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{S}}

\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}}

\oint d\theta=\oint d\theta^{\text{V}}=\frac{h}{m}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n_{\text{V}}}e^{\alpha},

-th vortex located at

, we have

\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}\theta^{\text{V}}=\frac{h}{m}\hat{z}\sum_{\alpha}e^{\alpha}\delta^{(2)}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\alpha}).

(9)

Next, we substitute Eq. (
7
) into the equations of motion, Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
):

\tilde{\chi}\partial_{t}^{2}\theta^{\text{S}}-\rho_{\text{s}}\partial^{2}\theta^{\text{S}}-\tilde{\Gamma}\partial_{t}u^{\text{S}}_{ii}=-\tilde{\chi}\partial_{t}^{2}\theta^{\text{V}},

(10)

\begin{split}\rho_{\text{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{i}-&\partial_{j}\sigma_{ij}-\tilde{\Gamma}\partial_{i}\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{S}}=\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\Gamma}\partial_{t}(\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\Gamma}\partial_{i}(\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}}).\end{split}

(11)

Inverting these inhomogeneous differential equations and eliminating

and
\theta^{\text{S}}
from the action Eq. (

), one obtains

S[\theta^{\text{V}}]=S^{\text{V}}_{1}[\theta^{\text{V}}]+S^{\text{V}}_{2}[\theta^{\text{V}}],

(12)

where

S^{\text{V}}_{1}[\theta^{\text{V}}]=-\rho\int dt\int d^{2}x\;\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}},

(13)

\displaystyle S^{\text{V}}_{2}[\theta^{\text{V}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{q^{2}}{\Delta}\bigg({\rho_{\text{s}}}{\rho_{\text{n}}}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4}\bigg)\omega^{2}-{\rho_{\text{s}}}\bigg(\tilde{\Lambda}+2\tilde{\mu}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4\tilde{\chi}}\bigg)q^{2}\Bigg](\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}})({\bf q},t)(\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}})(-{\bf q},-t)

(14)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bigg(\rho_{\text{s}}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4}\frac{\omega^{2}}{{\rho_{\text{n}}}\omega^{2}-\tilde{\mu}q^{2}}\bigg)(\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}})({\bf q},t)(\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}})(-{\bf q},-t),

where

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{n}}\omega^{4}-\left[\tilde{\Lambda}+2\tilde{\mu}+\frac{{\rho_{\text{n}}}{\rho_{\text{s}}}}{\tilde{\chi}}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{{\tilde{\chi}}}\right]q^{2}\omega^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho_{\text{s}}}{\tilde{\chi}}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}+2\tilde{\mu}\right)q^{4}.

Note that in the action
S_{2}^{\text{V}}
there is no coupling between
\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}}
and
\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}}
.

IV
Vortex dynamics

IV.1
Effective Action for Vortices

Now we turn to the dynamical properties of vortices by deriving an effective action in terms of their coordinates. We follow the general development for superfluid vortices found in Refs.
[
43
,
44
]
. Both of these works find that a coupling of vortex motion to the low-energy excitations, superfluid phonons, leads to a logarithmic frequency-dependent effective mass for the vortices. Analogously, we will find in a supersolid the interactions are mediated by the superfluid phonons as well as the longitudinal and transverse elastic modes, with concomitant contributions to the effect mass.

To capture the singular nature of the phase variable specified in Eq. (
9
) we take

\theta^{\text{V}}=\frac{\hbar}{m}\sum_{\alpha}e^{\alpha}\arctan\left[\frac{y-y^{\alpha}(t)}{x-x^{\alpha}(t)}\right].

(16)

From Eq. (
16
) we can calculate the Fourier transforms as

(\partial_{i}\theta^{\text{V}})({\bf q},t)=i\frac{h}{m}\epsilon_{ik}\frac{q_{k}}{q^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}e^{\alpha}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)},

(17)

(\partial_{t}\theta^{\text{V}})({\bf q},t)=-i\frac{h}{m}\epsilon_{ik}\frac{q_{k}}{q^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}e^{\alpha}s^{\alpha}_{i}(t)e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)},

(18)

where
{\bf s}^{\alpha}(t)=d{\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)/dt
is the velocity of the
\alpha
-th vortex.
Substituting Eqs. (

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\text{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\rho_{\text{s}}\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\int dt\;F_{0}({\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)-{\bf x}^{\beta}(t))

(19)

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{8m^{2}{\rho_{\text{n}}}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\;G_{1}({\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)-{\bf x}^{\beta}(t^{\prime}),t-t^{\prime};c_{T}),

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}s_{i}^{\alpha}(t)s_{j}^{\beta}(t^{\prime})\left(\delta_{ij}\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\partial_{k}^{\beta}-\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\partial_{j}^{\beta}\right)G_{2}({\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)-{\bf x}^{\beta}(t^{\prime}),t-t^{\prime};c_{L},c_{2})

\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\equiv\partial/\partial x_{i}^{\alpha}(t)
and
\partial_{i}^{\beta}\equiv\partial/\partial x_{i}^{\beta}(t^{\prime})
, and we defined the auxiliary integrals

F_{0}({\bf x})=\int d^{2}q\frac{1}{q^{2}}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf x}},

G_{2}({\bf x},t;c,\tilde{c})=\int d^{2}q\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{1}{q^{2}}\frac{a\omega^{2}-bq^{2}}{\Delta}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf x}+i\omega t},

(22)

with

and

in

and

, and

In Eq. (

is defined in Eq. (

are

a={\rho_{\text{n}}}{\rho_{\text{s}}}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4},

(23)

and

Therefore, the action for vortices can be derived by evaluating the auxiliary integrals defined in Eqs. (

Before simplifying the action, Eq. (

is independent of the time

, and the velocity

, so the third term of Eq. (

F_{0}({\bf x})=\int d^{2}q\,\frac{e^{-i\bf{q}\cdot\bf{x}}}{q^{2}+q_{0}^{2}}=2\pi K_{0}(q_{0}x),

(25)

where

is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. For small argument

Euler’s constant, and up to a constant we obtain the static action

S_{2}^{\text{static}}=\frac{2\pi\rho_{\text{s}}\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}}\int dt\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\ln(|{\bf x}^{\alpha}-{\bf x}^{\beta}|/\xi).

\displaystyle G_{1}({\bf x},t;c)

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(t)F_{0}({\bf x})+c^{2}F_{1}({\bf x},t;c),

where

F_{1}({\bf x},t;c)=\int d^{2}q\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\omega^{2}-c^{2}q^{2}}e^{-i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf x}+i\omega t}.

G_{2}({\bf x},t;c,\tilde{c})=\frac{A(c,\tilde{c})}{{\rho_{\text{n}}}}F_{2}({\bf x},t;c)+\frac{A(\tilde{c},c)}{{\rho_{\text{n}}}}F_{2}({\bf x},t;\tilde{c}),

(31)

with

The second term in Eq. (

. The first spatial derivatives equal minus the Laplacian of

. Next, following Ref.

Hence, we can evaluate the second and third terms with
G_{2}
in Eq. (

) by calculating
\partial_{t}^{2}F_{2}
and
\partial^{2}F_{2}
instead. In fact, they can be written in terms of

\partial^{2}F_{2}({\bf x},t;c)=-F_{1}({\bf x},t;c),

Finally, we can combine the local contributions of

\text{Local terms in $S_{2}$}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}}\left({\rho_{\text{s}}}+\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4{\rho_{\text{n}}}}-\frac{a}{{\rho_{\text{n}}}}\right)\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\int dtF_{0}({\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)-{\bf x}^{\beta}(t)).

Note that the above sum of local terms vanishes as

) and (

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\text{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}c_{T}^{2}}{8m^{2}\rho_{\text{n}}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}e^{\alpha}e^{\beta}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}F_{1}({\bf x^{\alpha}}(t)-{\bf x^{\beta}}(t^{\prime}),t-t^{\prime},c_{T})

,
44

In the absence of coupling between the superfluidity and the elasticity,

(\rho_{\text{n}}=\rho^{2}\chi\gamma)

, and the above action for vortices reduces to Eq. (3.25) of Eckern and Schmid

26

F_{1}({\bf x},t;c)\simeq-\frac{\pi}{c^{2}|t|}+\frac{\pi|{\bf x}|^{2}}{2c^{4}|t|^{3}}.

(37)

Note that the second term in the expansion is also in the form of a kinetic energy term. The first term, on the other hand, does not contribute to the inertial mass but to the rest mass. Then, the action for a single vortex can be separated into two parts

S^{\text{V}}_{2}=S^{\text{V}}_{\text{rest}}+S^{\text{V}}_{\text{massive}}.

(38)

The static action
S^{\text{V}}_{\text{rest}}
has a logarithmic divergence for both large and small
t
that arises when the first term of
F_{1}
is integrated over
t
as found by Arovas and Freire
[
44
]
. As they noted, the small
t
divergence can be regularized with a cut-off associated with the core size of the vortex, whereas the large
t
divergence is inevitable due to the logarithmic divergence of the energy of a static vortex. On the other hand, the massive action
S^{\text{V}}_{\text{massive}}
is in a form

S^{\text{V}}_{\text{massive}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}M^{\text{V}}(\omega)\omega^{2}x_{i}(\omega)x_{i}(-\omega),

(39)

where the frequency-dependent mass of a vortex in a supersolid has three contributions:

M^{\text{V}}(\omega)=M_{c_{T}}^{\text{V}}(\omega)+M_{c_{L}}^{\text{V}}(\omega)+M_{c_{2}}^{\text{V}}(\omega).

(40)

When the expansion of
F_{1}
is used in Eq. (
36
), the massive action
S^{\text{V}}_{\text{massive}}
has two terms at second order of the vortex velocity over the sound speed:

I_{1}=\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\frac{s_{i}(t)s_{i}(t^{\prime})}{|t-t^{\prime}|},

(41)

and

I_{2}=\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\frac{|{\bf x}(t)-{\bf x}(t^{\prime})|^{2}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{3}}.

(42)

We then calculate the Fourier transforms of the integrands of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
and find

I_{1}=-2\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)x_{i}(\omega)x_{i}(-\omega),

(43)

and

I_{2}=-2\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\omega^{2}\left[\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)-\frac{1-\cos(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon^{2}\omega^{2}}-\frac{\sin(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon\omega}\right]x_{i}(\omega)x_{i}(-\omega),

(44)

where
\text{ci}(x)
is the cosine integral,

\text{ci}(x)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\cos{t}}{t}\,dt,

(45)

and
\epsilon
is a cut-off introduced to regulate the infrared divergence in temporal integrals. Again, this low-frequency cut-off is associated with the core structure of a vortex.
With the above
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, each contribution to the mass term can be found:

M_{c_{T}}^{\text{V}}(\omega)=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}(e^{\alpha})^{2}\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4m^{2}\rho_{\text{n}}c_{T}^{2}}\Bigg[\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)-\frac{1-\cos(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon^{2}\omega^{2}}-\frac{\sin(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon\omega}\Bigg],

(46)

M_{c_{L}}^{\text{V}}(\omega)=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}(e^{\alpha})^{2}A(c_{L},c_{2})}{2m^{2}\rho_{\text{n}}c_{L}^{2}}\Bigg[\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)+\frac{1-\cos(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon^{2}\omega^{2}}+\frac{\sin(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon\omega}\Bigg],

(47)

M_{c_{2}}^{\text{V}}(\omega)=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}(e^{\alpha})^{2}A(c_{2},c_{L})}{2m^{2}\rho_{\text{n}}c_{2}^{2}}\Bigg[\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)+\frac{1-\cos(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon^{2}\omega^{2}}+\frac{\sin(\epsilon\omega)}{\epsilon\omega}\Bigg].

(48)

Note that the signs of the second and third terms in the square bracket of
M_{c_{T}}^{\text{V}}
are not errors. The additional contribution from the square of vortex velocity present in the second and third terms in Eq. (
36
) makes the sign change for similar terms in
M_{c_{L}}^{\text{V}}
and
M_{c_{2}}^{\text{V}}
.
It is important to note that without the coupling of superfluid to elasticity [
\tilde{\Gamma}=0

(\rho_{\text{n}}=\rho^{2}\chi\gamma)
,
A(c_{L},c_{2})=0
and
A(c_{2},c_{L})=\rho_{\text{n}}\rho_{\text{s}}
],
M_{c_{T}}^{\text{V}}
and
M_{c_{L}}^{\text{V}}
vanish, and we recover the vortex mass
M_{c_{2}}^{\text{V}}
found by Arovas and Freire
[
44
]
.

In the low frequency limit,
[1-\cos(\epsilon\omega)]/\epsilon^{2}\omega^{2}\simeq 1/2
,
\sin(\epsilon\omega)/\epsilon\omega\simeq 1
, and
\text{ci}(\epsilon|\omega|)\simeq\gamma_{E}+\ln(\epsilon|\omega|)
, and we find the logarithmic frequency-dependent vortex mass,

M^{\text{V}}(\omega)=-\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}(e^{\alpha})^{2}}{m^{2}}\Bigg[\rho^{2}\chi+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\mu}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\Lambda}+2\tilde{\mu}}\right)\Bigg]\left[\gamma_{E}+\frac{3}{2}+\ln(|\omega|\epsilon)\right]+\frac{3\pi\hbar^{2}(e^{\alpha})^{2}\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}}{4m^{2}\rho_{\text{n}}c_{T}^{2}}.

(49)

If
\tilde{\Gamma}=0
, the above frequency-dependent mass of a vortex in superfluid becomes the mass obtained by Eckern and Schmid
[
43
]
. However, in their work this vortex mass was interpreted as an effective viscosity.

IV.3
Magnus Force

As shown in Refs.
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
,
S_{1}
produces a transverse Magnus force on vortex. Since
\partial_{t}=\sum_{\alpha}s_{i}^{\alpha}(t)\partial/\partial x_{i}^{\alpha}(t)
,

S_{1}^{\text{V}}
can be rewritten in terms of the vortex velocity and an effective vector potential,

S_{1}^{\text{V}}=\sum_{\alpha}\int dtA_{i}^{\alpha}[{\bf x}^{\alpha}(t)]s_{i}^{\alpha}(t),

(50)

where we have defined

A_{i}^{\alpha}({\bf x}^{\alpha})=-\rho\int d^{2}x\frac{\partial\theta^{\alpha}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\alpha})}{\partial x_{i}^{\alpha}}.

(51)

Therefore, the
\alpha
-th vortex moves with a velocity
{\bf s}^{\alpha}(t)
in an effective magnetic field given by
\mbox{\boldmath$\nabla$}_{{\bf x}^{a}}\times{\bf A}^{\alpha}({\bf x}^{\alpha})=-h\rho e^{\alpha}\hat{z}/m
. The Magnus force
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
acting on the
\alpha
-th vortex is then given by

{\bf F}_{\text{Magnus}}^{\alpha}=-\frac{h\rho e^{\alpha}}{m}{\bf s}^{\alpha}(t)\times\hat{z},

(52)

which is a force perpendicular to the vortex velocity, analogous to the Lorentz force on a charged particle in an applied magnetic field.

V
Summary

We have investigated the dynamics of vortices in supersolids starting from an effective action for a supersolid. After separating the superfluid phase into smooth and singular components, we eliminated the smooth fields, resulting in an effective action for vortex degrees of freedom. This action consists of retarded, nonlocal interactions in spacetime, mediated by the gapless propagating modes in the supersolid: a longitudinal “second sound” mode (with speed
c_{2}
), a longitudinal elastic mode (with speed
c_{L}
), and a transverse elastic mode (with speed
c_{T}
). In the static limit, the action reduces to the usual logarithmic interaction between point vortices in superfluid films; we also recover the Magnus force on the vortices from the topological “Berry phase” term in the action. Finally, when retardation effects are included, the vortices acquire a frequency-dependent inertial mass due to the coupling to the gapless modes.

Appendix A
Supersolid Lagrangian

In this appendix we give a brief summary of our prior work, which derived an effective Lagrangian for a supersolid using variational principles developed for other hydrodynamic systems
[
33
,
34
]
. We start with the action for a supersolid,

S_{\text{SS}}=\int dt\int d^{3}x\mathcal{L}_{\text{SS}},

(53)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SS}}
is the Lagrangian density. In the Eulerian specification (in which all fields are depicted at positions
\mathbf{x}
and time
t
), the Lagrangian consists of the kinetic energy densities due to the super- and normal components, and the internal energy density:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{SS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}{v_{\text{s}}}_{i}{v_{\text{s}}}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}{\rho_{\text{n}}}_{ij}{v_{\text{n}}}_{i}{v_{\text{n}}}_{j}

(54)

\displaystyle-U_{\text{SS}}(\rho,{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij},s,R_{ij}),

where
{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}
and
{\rho_{\text{n}}}_{ij}
are the superfluid and normal density tensors with total density
\rho
satisfying
\rho\,\delta_{ij}={\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}+{\rho_{\text{n}}}_{ij}
, and where
{\bf v}_{\text{s}}
and
{\bf v}_{\text{n}}
are the velocities of the super- and normal components. The internal energy density
U_{\text{SS}}
is taken to be dependent on
\rho
,
{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}
, the entropy density
s
, and the deformation tensor

R_{ij}\equiv\partial_{i}R_{j},

(55)

with
{\bf R}
the coordinate affixed to material elements.
For a solid (either a normal solid or supersolid) the internal energy depends on the deformation tensor to account for the broken translational symmetry. As shown in Ref.
[
33
]
, the internal energy density satisfies the thermodynamic relation

\displaystyle dU_{\text{SS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tds+\left[\mu+\frac{1}{2}({v_{\text{n}}}_{i}-{v_{\text{s}}}_{i})^{2}\right]d\rho-\lambda_{ik}dR_{ik}

(56)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}({v_{\text{n}}}_{i}-{v_{\text{s}}}_{i})({v_{\text{n}}}_{j}-{v_{\text{s}}}_{j})d{\rho_{s}}_{ij},

where
T
is the temperature,
\mu
the chemical potential per unit mass, and
\lambda_{ij}
the stress tensor.

The next step is to incorporate the constraints that result from conservation laws and broken symmetries. In three dimensions a fluid has five conserved quantities (mass, entropy, and three components of momentum); a solid has an additional three broken translational symmetries; and a supersolid also has a broken gauge symmetry. The conservation laws for a supersolid are the continuity equation for mass

\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{i}j_{i}=0,

(57)

where the mass current
j_{i}
is

j_{i}={\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}{v_{\text{s}}}_{j}+(\rho\delta_{ij}-{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}){v_{\text{n}}}_{j},

(58)

and the entropy conservation law

\partial_{t}s+\partial_{i}(s{v_{\text{n}}}_{i})=0,

(59)

which only involves
{\bf v}_{\text{n}}
since the entropy is transported by the normal fluid. To account for the broken translational symmetry, Lin’s constraint
[
52
]
is used:

\frac{D_{n}R_{i}}{Dt}=0,

(60)

where
D_{n}/Dt\equiv\partial_{t}+{v_{\text{n}}}_{i}\partial_{i}
. After incorporating all of the constraints, Eqs. (
57
)-(
60

54

\alpha
,
\phi
, and
\beta_{i}
, we obtain the supersolid Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{SS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}{v_{\text{s}}}_{i}{v_{\text{s}}}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho\delta_{ij}-{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}){v_{\text{n}}}_{i}{v_{\text{n}}}_{j}

(61)

\displaystyle-U_{\text{SS}}(\rho,{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij},s,R_{ij})+\alpha\bigg[\partial_{t}s+\partial_{i}(s{v_{\text{n}}}_{i})\bigg]

\displaystyle+\phi\Bigg\{\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{i}\bigg[{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}{v_{\text{s}}}_{j}+(\rho\delta_{ij}-{\rho_{\text{s}}}_{ij}){v_{\text{n}}}_{j}\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+\beta_{i}\bigg[\partial_{t}(sR_{i})+\partial_{j}(sR_{i}{v_{\text{n}}}_{j})\bigg].

53
) with the Lagrangian Eq. (

,
\beta_{i}

(63)

\partial_{t}j_{i}+\partial_{j}\Pi_{ij}=0,

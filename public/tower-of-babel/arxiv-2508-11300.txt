Einstein’s Equations in Electromagnetic Media
Eren Erberk Erkul, Ulf Leonhardt 1: Department of Physics & Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Middle East Technical University, Ankara 06800, Turkey 2: Department of Physics of Complex Systems, Weizmann Institute of Science, Rehovot 7610001, Israel

Abstract
In this paper, Plebanski’s 1960 mapping is extended to encode the full Einstein system in an electromagnetic medium. This is achieved by identifying transformation parameters in terms of the ADM constraint and evolution equations, and subsequently applying the mapping to the entire system. These transformed equations are then linearized in vacuum to derive gravitational-wave analogues as perturbations within the optical medium.

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon\,\bm{E}+\frac{\bm{g}}{c}\times\bm{H},

(2a)

\displaystyle\bm{B}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}\mu\,\bm{H}-\frac{\bm{g}}{c}\times\bm{E}.

In the present paper, we move beyond the purely kinematic aspects

We thus require that the transformed optical metric also satisfy the gravitational constraints. This step is motivated by a conceptual similarity between transformation optics and the ADM formalism.

Both in the ADM formalism and transformation optics, the object described by the metric is a three-dimensional slice representing a spatial hypersurface. However, in the ADM formalism, one explicitly evolves these spatial slices that are called foliations through time, whereas in transformation optics the analysis is typically confined to a single effective optical slice. Hence the idea is to extend Plebanski’s mapping (
1

Electromagnetic ADM
(3+1)
System.
The Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM)
[
21
]
, also known as the Hamiltonian formalism, is an equivalent formulation of general relativity.
[
22
]
Unlike the usual covariant picture, this formulation reduces the space-time manifold to a
trivial
fibre
[
23
,
24
]
:

\pi:\;E\equiv E_{1}\times E_{3}\;\longrightarrow\;E_{1},\qquad\pi(t,x)=t.

(3)

Here
E_{1}\simeq\mathbb{R}
is the base (global Cauchy) while
E_{3}
denotes the leaves of the foliation that plays the role of the fibre and is homeomorphic to a Cauchy surface.

1
1

1

In GR, the collection of fibres through base space can be viewed as the collection of inertial frames, where a suitable choice of coordinates (an orientation of foliation) can always flatten each fibre into an inertial one. The key difference between the
trivial
bundles of GR and those of Newton–Cartan theory is an extra degree of freedom that allows the spatial foliation to twist and curl. Hence, Newtonian foliations are absolute entities; no gravitational waves arise there, lacking radiative TT part of the curvature. Relatedly, both Newtonian and Einsteinian bundles differ from the Galilean bundle in that the extra freedom now resides in the fibres themselves.
[
25
]

The line element in ADM coordinates adapted to the slicing defined by
t=\text{constant}
reads

ds^{2}=g_{ab}\,dx^{a}dx^{b}=-\alpha^{2}dt^{2}+\gamma_{ij}\bigl(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt\bigr)\bigl(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt\bigr).

(4)

Equivalently, the metric tensor in matrix form reads

\displaystyle g_{ab}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha^{2}+\beta_{i}\beta^{i}&\beta_{i}\\
\beta_{j}&\gamma_{ij}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

\displaystyle g^{ab}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha^{-2}&\alpha^{-2}\beta^{i}\\
\alpha^{-2}\beta^{j}&\gamma^{ij}-\alpha^{-2}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

Here,
\gamma_{ij}
is the spatial metric on the
t=\text{constant}
slices.
The dynamism of this system is encoded in the
lapse

\alpha
and
shift

\beta^{i}
, both one-forms. While the net evolution vector, called the
time vector

t^{\mu}
, drags a point through the full
(3+1)
-dimensional spacetime, it combines both effects:

t^{\mu}=\alpha\,n^{\mu}+\beta^{\mu},

(7)

with
n^{\mu}
the future-pointing unit normal to each
\Sigma_{t}
(
n^{\mu}n_{\mu}=-1,\;n^{\mu}\gamma_{\mu\nu}=0
).

For a detailed literature of the
3+1
decomposition, see, e.g.

Shift as Medium Drag.
Shift vectors gauge how points move within a spatial slice when we advance the coordinate time:

dx^{i}=\beta^{i}\,dt\qquad(\text{on }\Sigma_{t}).

(8)

Extending Plebanski’s mapping (
1
) and substituting the ADM decomposition

g_{0i}=\gamma_{ij}\beta^{j}
:

g_{i}=\frac{\gamma_{ij}\beta^{j}}{g_{00}},

(9)

where
g_{i}
(one spatial index) is the medium’s

magneto-electric
vector, not a component of the metric. Raising the spatial index with
\gamma^{ij}
gives

\gamma^{ij}g_{i}=\frac{\beta^{j}}{g_{00}}.

(10)

So, the same geometric information in
g_{i}
is encoded in the shift vector:

\beta^{i}=g_{00}\,\gamma^{ij}g_{j}.

(11)

Hence a nonzero
\beta^{i}
is realized optically as a cross-coupling term
{(\mathbf{g}/c)\!\times\!\mathbf{H}}
in the constitutive relations, which is exactly what one obtains for a dielectric moving with a physical three-velocity proportional to
\mathbf{g}
. Equivalently,

\beta^{i}
can be regarded as an
effective
medium velocity

\mathbf{v}_{\text{eff}}\propto\mathbf{g}
, which, through Eq. (
11
),
induces the very Lorentz-type mixing of
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{B}
that a real boost generates
in free space.

Lapse as Conformal Factor.
Lapse
\alpha
is a one-form that measures proper time between adjacent slices:

d\tau=\alpha\,dt.

(12)

Since the source-free Maxwell equations in four dimensions are invariant under local conformal rescalings

g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}
,
we can factor out a positive scalar
\Omega^{2}(x)
from the metric
[
10
]
,

g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\,\widehat{g}_{\mu\nu}.

(13)

A convenient choice, equivalent to taking the
t=\text{constant}
slicing, is to set
\widehat{g}_{00}=-1
in the ADM line element (Eq. (
4
)), which fixes

\Omega^{2}(x)=-g_{00}=\alpha^{2}-\gamma_{ij}\beta^{i}\beta^{j},

(14)

Taking the positive square root gives

\Omega(x)=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\gamma_{ij}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}}.

(15)

When the shift vanishes (
\beta^{i}=0
), this reduces to
\Omega=\alpha(t,\mathbf{x})
, matching the standard result in transformation optics: changing the lapse is merely a conformal rescaling of the metric.

The index-lowered vacuum expressions for the Plebanski constitutive tensors (
1
) with identification (
14
) read

\varepsilon_{ij}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{\alpha}\,\gamma_{ij},\qquad\mu_{ij}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{\alpha}\,\gamma_{ij}.

(16)

Under the conformal rescaling
(
13
) the ADM variables transform as

\tilde{\alpha}=\Omega\,\alpha,\qquad\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}=\Omega^{2}\gamma_{ij},\qquad\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}}=\Omega^{3}\sqrt{\gamma}.

(17)

Substituting these into the definition of
\varepsilon_{ij}
gives

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{ij}=\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}}}{\tilde{\alpha}}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}=\frac{\Omega^{3}\sqrt{\gamma}}{\Omega\alpha}\,(\Omega^{2}\gamma_{ij})=\Omega^{4}\varepsilon_{ij},

(18)

and the same calculation yields

\tilde{\mu}_{ij}=\Omega^{4}\mu_{ij}.

(19)

Hence, under the same rescaling the Plebanski constitutive tensors acquire four powers of the conformal factor,

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{ij}=\Omega^{4}\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad\tilde{\mu}_{ij}=\Omega^{4}\mu_{ij},

(20)

As expected, the source-free Maxwell equations are therefore insensitive to the overall scale
\Omega
.

The decomposition remains valid provided
g_{00}<0
, i.e.
\alpha^{2}>\gamma_{ij}\beta^{i}\beta^{j}
, a condition automatically satisfied for any spacelike foliation.

Electromagnetic ADM Constraints and Dynamics
The
3{+}1
(ADM) split of spacetime, entails two constraint equations
[
21
]
that determine the intrinsic structure of each hypersurface, namely the momentum and Hamiltonian constraints.

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}R+K^{2}-K_{ij}K^{ij}

\displaystyle=16\pi G\,\rho,

(21a)

\displaystyle D_{j}\!\bigl(K^{ij}-\gamma^{ij}K\bigr)

\displaystyle=8\pi G\,j^{\,i},

(21b)

where
\rho
is the energy density,
j^{\,i}
the momentum density,

K_{ij}
the extrinsic curvature,
{}^{(3)}\!R_{ij}
(and its trace

{}^{(3)}\!R
) the intrinsic curvature of the spatial hypersurface, and

D_{i}
the covariant derivative compatible with
\gamma_{ij}
.

Together with the evolution
[
21
]
, which includes the dynamic entities lapse and shift,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\alpha K_{ij}+D_{i}\beta_{j}+D_{j}\beta_{i},

(22a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K_{ij}

\displaystyle=-D_{i}D_{j}\alpha+\alpha\!\bigl({}^{(3)}R_{ij}+K\,K_{ij}-2K_{ik}K^{k}{}_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad-8\pi G\,\alpha\!\Bigl(S_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{ij}\,(\rho-S)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta^{k}D_{k}K_{ij}-K_{kj}D_{i}\beta^{k}-K_{ik}D_{j}\beta^{k},

(22b)

where
S^{ij}
is the spatial electromagnetic stress tensor,
[
23
]

S^{ij}=E^{i}\mathcal{D}^{j}+H^{i}B^{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{ij}\bigl(\bm{E}\!\cdot\!\bm{\mathcal{D}}+\bm{B}\!\cdot\!\bm{H}\bigr).

(23)

Since we confine ourselves to electromagnetic fields and Maxwell stress-energy is traceless
[
5
]
,

S\equiv\gamma_{ij}S^{ij}=\rho c^{2}
, the
\gamma_{ij}(\rho-S)
term vanishes and the evolution equation retains only the electromagnetic back-reaction, which introduces non-linearities into the sources discussed in the next section

Solving (
22a
) for the extrinsic curvature gives

K_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\bigl(\partial_{t}\gamma_{ij}-D_{i}\beta_{j}-D_{j}\beta_{i}\bigr).

(24)

Reorganized, the Plebanski map (
1
) reads,

\gamma_{ij}=-\frac{g_{00}}{\sqrt{-g}}\;\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad\beta_{i}=g_{0i}=g_{00}\,g_{i},

(25)

with
\sqrt{-g}=\alpha\sqrt{\gamma}
. Substitution into
(
24
) yields

K_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\Bigl[\partial_{t}\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{g_{00}}{\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon_{ij}\Bigr)-D_{i}\!\bigl(g_{00}g_{j}\bigr)-D_{j}\!\bigl(g_{00}g_{i}\bigr)\Bigr],

(26)

where the first term captures explicit temporal modulation and the
remaining covariant derivatives describe stationary “drag” induced by
the gauge fields. Both contributions appear quadratically in
(
21a
).

Because
\beta^{m}=g_{00}g^{m}
,

g_{00}=-\alpha^{2}+\gamma_{mn}\beta^{m}\beta^{n}
becomes a quadratic
equation whose negative-root solution is

g_{00}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1+4\alpha^{2}S}}{2S},\qquad S\equiv\gamma^{mn}g_{m}g_{n}.

(27)

Equations (
26
) and (
27
)
express
K_{ij}
entirely in terms of

\{\varepsilon_{ij},g_{i},\alpha,\beta^{i}\}
.

The intrinsic geometry of each optical slice is encoded in

\gamma_{ij}(t,\mathbf{x})\equiv g_{ij}
, from which one builds

{}^{(3)}R_{ij}
and its scalar
[
28
]

{}^{(3)}R=\gamma^{ij}\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij}.

(28)

Under a conformal rescale (
13
),

{}^{(3)}\!\widetilde{R}=\Omega^{-2}\!\Bigl({}^{(3)}\!R-4\,\Delta\!\ln\Omega-2\,\gamma^{ij}\partial_{i}\ln\Omega\,\partial_{j}\ln\Omega\Bigr),

(29)

with
\Delta=\gamma^{ij}D_{i}D_{j}
. Choosing

\Omega^{2}=-g_{00}=\alpha^{2}-\gamma_{kl}\beta^{k}\beta^{l}
gives

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}\!R[\varepsilon;\alpha,\beta]

\displaystyle=\frac{{}^{(3)}\!R[\varepsilon]-4\,\Delta\!\ln\alpha_{\text{eff}}-2\,\gamma^{ij}\partial_{i}\ln\alpha_{\text{eff}}\,\partial_{j}\ln\alpha_{\text{eff}}}{\alpha^{2}-\gamma_{kl}\beta^{k}\beta^{l}}

(30)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\gamma_{kl}\beta^{k}\beta^{l}}.

The numerator depends only on the spatial permittivity, whereas lapse
and shift enter through the conformal prefactor and its gradients.

Henceforth, every appearance of
\gamma_{ij},\,K_{ij},\,{}^{(3)}R_{ij}

or
D_{i}
refers to the optical metric

\gamma_{ij}[\varepsilon,\mu]
of (
33
).

Electromagnetic ADM Sources.

Given mass sources and currents, the ADM constraints (
21
) and evolution (
22
) encode Einstein’s equations.
Using the identifications (
25
), the entire system can be written in the language of the effective dielectric.
To complete the analogue system, we take both the mass density
\rho
and the momentum density
\bm{j}
to be provided solely by the electromagnetic field itself. This would describe the self-gravity of light. Note that this is not an entirely artificial situation. For example, the early Universe was radiation-dominated; black-hole magnetospheres can be modeled electromagnetically
[
29
]
; and certain black-hole metrics can be generated from colliding electromagnetic waves through the Chandrasekhar–Xanthopoulos duality
[
30
,
31
]
.

With this choice our source expressions coincide with those obtained by Thorne and Macdonald in their 3 + 1 formulation of electrodynamics.
[
7
,
32
]

In the notation we already established, the Maxwell’s equations in the 3+1 formalism read:

\displaystyle D_{i}B^{i}

\displaystyle=0,

(31a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\gamma}\,B^{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle+\partial_{j}\!\Bigl[\sqrt{\gamma}\bigl(\beta^{j}B^{i}-\beta^{i}B^{j}-\alpha\epsilon^{ijk}E_{k}\bigr)\Bigr]=0,

(31b)

\displaystyle D_{i}\mathcal{D}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\rho_{\mathrm{e}},

(31c)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\gamma}\,\mathcal{D}^{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle+\partial_{j}\!\Bigl[\sqrt{\gamma}\bigl(\beta^{j}\mathcal{D}^{i}-\beta^{i}\mathcal{D}^{j}+\alpha\epsilon^{ijk}H_{k}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad=-\sqrt{\gamma}\,\alpha\,\mathcal{J}^{\,i}.

(31d)

where
\rho_{e}\equiv n_{\mu}J^{\mu}
is the electric charge density measured by an Eulerian observer,
J^{i}\equiv\gamma^{i}_{\ \mu}J^{\mu}
is the coordinate three–current density,
\mathcal{J}^{\,i}\equiv J^{i}+\rho_{e}\beta^{i}
is the physical current seen by an Eulerian observer and
\epsilon^{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita symbol associated with the spatial metric.

The individual expressions can be understood within our framework when the identifications in (
25
) are applied.

Projecting the electromagnetic stress–energy tensor
[
22
]
, one finds that, in the analogy, the mass-density
\rho
is identified with the energy density and the current
\mathbf{j}
with the Poynting vector, both divided by
c^{2}
:

[
7
]

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\bigl(\bm{\mathcal{D}}\!\cdot\!\bm{E}+\bm{B}\!\cdot\!\bm{H}\bigr),

(32a)

\displaystyle j^{\,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\bigl(\bm{E}\times\bm{H}\bigr)^{i},

(32b)

In this way, we describe the nonlinear gravitational dynamics
of light through the dielectric-medium equations; solving
them yields the full dynamics of an optical medium that is the
analogue of a self-gravitating spacetime.

Analogue of Gravitational Waves.

Assume
g_{00}=-1
, so the lapse is
\alpha=1
and the shift is
\beta^{i}=0
throughout this section.
For the dielectric tensors we obtain, from Eq. (
1
),

\gamma_{ij}=(\det\varepsilon)^{-1/5}\,\varepsilon_{ij},

(33)

a form that also arises in many applications of transformation optics
[
10
]
.

In the regime of weak gravity,

g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+h_{ij},\qquad|h_{ij}|\ll 1,

(34)

At linear order, the term
-8\pi G\,\alpha S_{ij}
in
Eq. (
22b
) is
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
and is therefore
neglected here.
Evolution equations (
22a
) and (
22b
) reduce to

\displaystyle K_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{t}h_{ij},

(35a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K_{ij}

\displaystyle={}^{(3)}\!R^{\mathrm{lin}}_{ij},

(35b)

where the linearized Ricci tensor
{}^{(3)}\!R^{\mathrm{lin}}_{ij}
is given by
[
26
]

{}^{(3)}\!R^{\mathrm{lin}}_{ij}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}h_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h+\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{(i}\partial^{k}h_{j)k}.

(36)

Assuming vacuum (
\rho=\bm{j}=0
) and the weak-gravity regime (
34
), the Hamiltonian constraint (
21a
) yields
[
23
]

\partial_{j}\partial_{k}h^{jk}-\nabla^{2}h=0,\qquad h\equiv\delta^{ij}h_{ij}.

(37)

The momentum constraint (
21b
) gives
[
23
]

\partial_{j}\dot{h}^{ij}-\partial^{i}\dot{h}=0,

(38)

with
\dot{h}_{ij}\equiv\partial_{t}h_{ij}
and
\dot{h}\equiv\delta^{kl}\dot{h}_{kl}
.

Under the transverse-traceless (TT) conditions
[
22
]
:

\displaystyle\partial^{i}h^{TT}_{ij}

\displaystyle=0,

(39)

\displaystyle h^{TT}

\displaystyle=0,

(
37
) and (
38
) are trivially satisfied.

Continuing in this gauge, Eq. (
36
) reduces to

R_{ij}^{\text{lin}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}h_{ij}.

(40)

Finally, substituting (
35a
) and (
40
) into (
35b
), we isolate the two radiative degrees of freedom and obtain

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}h^{TT}_{ij}-\nabla^{2}h^{TT}_{ij}=0.

(41)

We write the permittivity as a small perturbation about the identity:

\varepsilon_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\delta\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad|\delta\varepsilon_{ij}|\ll 1.

(42)

Expanding the determinant to first order gives

\displaystyle\det\varepsilon

\displaystyle=1+\Tr(\delta\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}\!\bigl((\delta\varepsilon)^{2}\bigr),

(43)

\displaystyle(\det\varepsilon)^{-1/5}

\displaystyle=1-\tfrac{1}{5}\Tr(\delta\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}\!\bigl((\delta\varepsilon)^{2}\bigr).

Inserting these into Eq. (
33
), we find

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\det\varepsilon)^{-1/5}\,\varepsilon_{ij}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+\delta\varepsilon_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{5}\delta_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}\!\bigl((\delta\varepsilon)^{2}\bigr),

(44)

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\gamma_{ij}-\delta_{ij}=\delta\varepsilon_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{5}\delta_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\varepsilon).

(45)

Any symmetric tensor
X_{ij}
can be decomposed into trace, longitudinal, and transverse-traceless parts:

X_{ij}=X^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}+\partial_{(i}V_{j)}+\tfrac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\Tr(X).

(46)

Applying this projector to Eq. (
45
) and using the gauge conditions (
39
), we identify the dielectric perturbation with the metric wave:

h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}=\delta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}\,.

(47)

Substituting into (
41
) gives

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}^{2}\,\delta\varepsilon^{TT}_{ij}-\nabla^{2}\,\delta\varepsilon^{TT}_{ij}=0.

(48)

Equation (
48
) admits the usual plane-wave solution:

\displaystyle\delta\varepsilon^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl\{\mathcal{A}_{ij}\,e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\omega t)}\bigr\},

(49a)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\delta\mu^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\!\bigl\{\mathcal{A}_{ij}\,e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\omega t)}\bigr\},

(49b)

where the constant, symmetric, traceless tensor
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
obeys
k^{i}\mathcal{A}_{ij}=0
. Equation (
49
) is a direct analogue of a gravitational wave propagating through the optical medium and can be used as a theoretical model for spatio-temporal perturbations in engineered media.
[
33
,
34
]

If, instead of fixing the shift to zero, we keep a uniform (time-independent) shift
\beta^{i}
, the ordinary time derivative in the wave equation (
41
) is replaced by the convective derivative
[
23
]

\partial_{t}\;\mapsto\;D_{0}\equiv\partial_{t}-\beta^{k}\partial_{k}\,,

(50)

so the flat d’Alembertian becomes the
boosted
operator
[
26
]

\Box_{\beta}\;=\;\frac{1}{c^{2}}\,D_{0}^{2}-\nabla^{2},

(51)

i.e. the d’Alembertian expressed in Lorentz-boosted coordinates with velocity
\bm{\beta}
.

Hence, the plane-wave solution (
49
) acquires an extra phase

\exp\!\bigl[\mathrm{i}\,(\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\bm{\beta})\,t\bigr],

(52)

corresponding to the constant Doppler shift introduced by the boost.

When the shift vector
\beta^{i}
stops being uniform in spacetime, the simple
substitution (
50
) no longer leaves the d’Alembertian (
51
) unchanged:
extra geometric terms enter the operator and couple different Fourier modes.
As the Einsteinian system is nonlinear, these additional couplings don’t obey
superposition: every harmonic becomes entangled with the background curvature
along with the other modes. Therefore in strong regimes, although the equations
can still be expressed analytically, the gravitational waves cannot be built
from boosted plane waves. One must evolve the analogous
3\!+\!1
system numerically,
which is highly non-trivial.
[
36
,
37
]

Conclusion.

Building on Plebanski’s work, we have shown that electrodynamics in an anisotropic medium has sufficient independent degrees of freedom to reproduce the entire Hamiltonian structure of general relativity. Linear perturbations of the mapped constraints, taken in the TT gauge, not only provide a convenient toy model for cosmological gravitational waves but also supply a powerful tool for transformation-optics design. The dGREM formulation
[
35
]
is a flux-conservative, first-order reformulation of the (3+1) Einstein system that is an equivalent framework for the theory presented here. From the dawn of the relativistic revolution, mappings between general relativity and electromagnetism have been discovered and rediscovered independently, with Plebanski’s
[
1
]
and Tamm’s
[
3
]
foundational results being prime examples. This persistent analogy still continues to captivate the imaginations of successive generations of physicists. Ultimately, who can definitively say when an analogue system ceases to be a mere analogy and becomes an essential guide toward deeper layers of gravitation?

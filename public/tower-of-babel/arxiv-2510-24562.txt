Hard wall repulsion for the discrete Gaussian free field in random environment on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3
Alberto Chiarini, Emanuele Pasqui

Abstract
. We study the discrete Gaussian free field (harmonic crystal) on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , with uniformly elliptic and bounded random conductances sampled according to a sufficiently mixing environment measure. We consider the hard wall event that the field is non-negative on the discrete blow-up of a bounded regular domain V ⊆ ℝ d V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} , and establish a quenched large deviation asymptotic for its probability. The asymptotic rate is characterized by the essential supremum of the on-site variances and the homogenized capacity of V V , which arises from a quenched invariance principle. We then analyze the law of the field conditioned on the hard wall event. We determine the first-order asymptotic profile for its expectation and demonstrate that an entropic push-away of the field from the origin occurs. Furthermore, we characterize the field pathwise behavior under the constraint, showing that, when properly recentered, the field converges weakly to the (quenched) discrete Gaussian free field. A major challenge is the lack of translation invariance in the model. We exploit the superharmonicity of the conditioned expectation of the field to obtain a sharp uniform lower bound for it. We then use this bound to estimate the harmonicity defect of the conditioned expectation. This is a key step that allows us to prove a corresponding sharp upper bound and establish a pathwise entropic repulsion phenomenon.



1.
Introduction

In this article we investigate how
impurities
affect the behavior of the discrete Gaussian free field (GFF) on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
, in the presence of a hard wall constraint.
The discrete Gaussian free field, also known as harmonic crystal, has been successfully used as an effective model for the interface of separation between pure phases in statistical mechanics, and also arises as a microscopic description of the fluctuations of atoms in a
homogeneous
crystal at non-zero temperature.
The study of the hard wall event for the Gaussian free field, which consists in the atypical scenario that the field remains positive over a macroscopic domain, dates back to the eighties with
[
42
]
, where it is first proven that when the field is conditioned to be positive over a growing sequence of sets, it gets pushed away to infinity. Later this phenomenon was precisely quantified in
[
19
]
for
d\geq 3
, where the authors establish large deviation asymptotics for the hard wall event and determine the exact distance at which the interface is pushed, discovering that it is at the same order of the expected maximum of the unconditioned field (see also
[
34
]
for related models). Further, in
[
33
]
the authors show that when recentering the law of the conditioned field by such diverging amount, it converges to the law of the GFF itself, establishing a pathwise result for the entropic repulsion phenomenon. Related to this line of investigation, also the study of the extremes of the field over macroscopic domains has been the subject of recent interest (see
[
24
,
25
]
for
d\geq 3
and
[
14
,
16
,
20
]
for
d=2
).

With the goal of modelling inhomogeneous crystals and understanding how impurities influence their behavior, we are naturally led to consider the GFF with
random conductances
. To our knowledge, the first appearance of this model was in
[
22
]
and recently several questions related to it were investigated, like level-set percolation in the supercritical regime (see
[
27
]
and
[
36
]
for results on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and Galton-Watson trees, respectively), stochastic homogenization (see
[
7
,
30
]
), and, closer to the aim of this work, the scaling limit of the maximum in the critical dimension
d=2
for the Gaussian free field on the percolation cluster (see
[
48
]
) and for the one with uniformly elliptic and bounded i.i.d. conductances. One of the main features of the Gaussian free field with random conductances is that the covariances of the field are characterized by the Green function of the random walk among random conductances, known as the Random Conductance Model. It is well known that when the conductances are uniformly elliptic and bounded, the random walk satisfies a quenched invariance principle (see for instance
[
10
,
49
]
), that is, under diffusive rescaling the random walk converges to a Brownian motion with a deterministic covariance matrix
a^{\rm hom}
for almost all realizations of the environment.

In this article we focus on the hard wall repulsion phenomenon for the discrete Gaussian free field with uniformly elliptic and bounded random conductances on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
. That is, we look at the event that the field is non-negative over a region
(NV)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
of volume of order
N^{d}
. We first obtain
quenched
large deviation asymptotics for the hard wall event as in
[
19
]
under sufficient mixing conditions on the environment. We use a strategy of proof inspired by the recent works
[
52
,
44
,
28
,
27
]
to overcome the lack of translation invariance in the model. We show that the probability of the hard wall event decays exponentially fast with speed
N^{d-2}\log N
and a rate that reflects both a large-scale effect coming from the homogenization of the random walk in the form of the capacity of
V
for the limiting Brownian motion, and a microscopic effect from the spins with the highest variances. The speed
N^{d-2}\log N
in the asymptotics is due to the long-range correlation in the field and to a push up of the field to a height of order
\sqrt{\log N}
, when constrained to be positive on a domain of volume
N^{d}
. In fact, with a novel approach, to overcome the lack of translation invariance, we show that the expectation of the field conditioned on the hard wall event is at leading order
{h}_{V}^{\rm hom}(\cdot/N)\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
, with
\overline{g}
being the essential supremum of the on-site variances of the field, and
{h}_{V}^{\rm hom}
the homogenized harmonic potential of
V
, while the leading order for the expected maximum of the unconditioned field on
V_{N}
is
\sqrt{2d\,\overline{g}\log N}
. This has the interesting consequence that in the special case of i.i.d. conductances the field gets an extra push up from the randomness in the conductances when compared to the homogeneous case with the same scaling limit. This is interesting when compared to the critical dimension
d=2
, where the behavior of the maximum is captured solely by the effective diffusivity coming from the homogenization of the random walk among random conductances, see
[
48
]
. Finally, we also prove that, when properly recentered, the field conditioned to be positive converges weakly to the discrete Gaussian free field with random conductances for almost all realizations of the environment, thus establishing a pathwise entropic repulsion phenomenon akin to the one in
[
33
]
.

We now describe the model and our results in more detail. We look at the discrete Gaussian free field on the
d
-dimensional integer lattice for
d\geq 3
, with random conductances, namely the graph with vertices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, edges
\mathbb{E}_{d}
between nearest-neighboring vertices and a weight
\omega_{e}
(the conductance) for each
e\in\mathbb{E}_{d}
, with the conductances being uniformly bounded from above and below, namely
\omega_{e}\in[\lambda,\Lambda]
for some real numbers
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and all
e\in\mathbb{E}_{d}
. We let
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}=[\lambda,\Lambda]^{\mathbb{E}_{d}}
be the set of all weights configurations. Given
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
the law on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
such that

(1.1)

under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
, the canonical field
(\varphi_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is centered Gaussian with covariances
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}\varphi_{y}]=g^{\omega}(x,y)
for every
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

where
g^{\omega}
is the Green function of the continuous-time constant-speed simple random walk on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega)
, see (
2.5
).
Informally
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
can be seen as a Gibbs measure with formal expression

(1.2)

\mathbb{P}^{\omega}(\mathrm{d}\varphi)``\propto"\exp\bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(\varphi,\varphi)\bigg\}\prod_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda^{1}(\mathrm{d}\varphi_{x})\,,

where

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(\varphi,\varphi)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}_{d}}\omega_{x,y}(\varphi_{y}-\varphi_{x})^{2}\,,

and
\lambda^{1}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. We will also use that the law
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
of the Gaussian free field can be uniquely characterized by the laws of
(\varphi_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
conditioned on the value of the field in their neighborhoods (see (
2.23
)).
Consider a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
on the measurable space of
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
endowed with the canonical
\sigma
-algebra of cylinders, and assume
\mathbb{Q}
to be stationary and ergodic with respect to shifts (we refer to Section
2
for precise definitions). To get the discrete Gaussian free field with random conductances we first sample an environment
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
with law
\mathbb{Q}
and then we define
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
as in (
1.1
). We notice that, if
\mu_{x}^{\omega}=\sum_{y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}
, then the energy
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
corresponds to the Dirichlet form of the constant-speed continuous time random walk among random conductances with generator

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f(x)=\frac{1}{\mu_{x}^{\omega}}\sum_{y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}(f(y)-f(x))\,,

on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mu^{\omega})
,
which is known as the Random Conductance Model. The last decade has witnessed a number of important advances in the understanding of this model, especially in the context of stochastic homogenization (we refer to
[
3
,
13
]
for a recent review of the main progresses). The connection between the random conductance model and the Gaussian free field via (
1.1
) allows to better understand the latter, as was done for instance in
[
7
,
30
]
in the context of the scaling limit of the Gaussian free field in random environment.

In this article we are interested in the study of
quenched
properties for this field, that is, those that hold for
\mathbb{Q}
-almost every realization of the random environment. Our attention will focus on the
hard wall event
for the Gaussian free field
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
and on the behavior of the field conditioned on it.
Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded strongly regular set (see Definition
2.45
). For
N
positive integer define the discrete blow-up of
V
as

(1.5)

V_{N}=(NV)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

Then, the hard wall event is given by

(1.6)

\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}=\{\varphi_{x}\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in V_{N}\}

and can be described as the event that the field stays above a hard wall on
V_{N}
.
The event
\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}
is clearly atypical under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
, and the main result of this article concerns quenched large deviation asymptotics for its probability, and the study of the field conditioned on it for
\mathbb{Q}
-almost every environment configuration. The asymptotic rate involves
a large-scale effect coming from the homogenization for the random walk among random conductances, and a microscopic effect from the law
\mathbb{Q}
governing the conductances. The former is represented by
\mathrm{Cap}^{\rm hom}(V)
, the capacity of
V
associated with the limiting Brownian motion for the walk with generator (
1.4
), while the latter is captured by the constant
\overline{g}
defined as follows:

(1.7)

\overline{g}:=\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}g^{\omega}(0,0)\in(0,\infty)\,,

which, by stationarity and ergodicity of the environment, is
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. equal to
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g^{\omega}(x,x)
.
We also show that the field conditioned on the hard wall event gets delocalized and, at first order, is pushed up to a level of limiting profile
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(\cdot/N)\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
where
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}
is the harmonic potential of
V
for the limiting Brownian motion (see Section
2
). The value of
\overline{g}
is in general difficult to determine, but it can be computed explicitly in some cases, for example when the conductances are i.i.d. and such that
\essinf\omega_{e}=\lambda
under
\mathbb{Q}
. In this case, one has
\overline{g}=g(0,0)/\lambda
, where
g(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Green function of the simple random walk, see Remark
6.4
, 2).

The main result of this article which subsumes this brief discussion is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that under
\mathbb{Q}
the conductances are i.i.d. (or more generally that
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies a mixing condition, see Remark
6.4
, 1)). Then, there exists
\Omega_{\mathrm{typ}}
with full
\mathbb{Q}
-measure such that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{typ}}

i)

(1.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}\log N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}]=-2\overline{g}\,\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)\,,

ii)

(1.9)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\bigg|\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]}{\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}}-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)\bigg|=0\,,

iii)

setting
a_{N}=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{0}|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
, we have

(1.10)

\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}\to\mathbb{P}^{\omega}

weakly as
N\to\infty
,
where
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}
is the law of the field
\varphi-a_{N}
under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
.

Theorem
1.1
, i), gives the quenched large deviation asymptotics for the hard wall event, which decays exponentially fast with speed
N^{d-2}\log N
. The appearance of the constant
\overline{g}
in the rate becomes natural when looking at the first-order behavior of the maximum of the field over
V_{N}
. Indeed, one has that
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s.

(1.11)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{x\in V_{N}}\varphi_{x}]}{\sqrt{2d\,\overline{g}\log N}}=1\,,

see Remark
4.4
for the details. From the proof of (
1.11
), which is an adaptation of the arguments in
[
25
]
, it is clear that the maximum is attained at points where the variance of the field is close to its essential supremum
\overline{g}
, since by ergodicity such points are present in a positive proportion in
V_{N}
,
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. In fact, (
1.11
) holds under the sole assumption that
\mathbb{Q}
is stationary and ergodic. More refined convergence results on the recentered maximum in
d\geq 3
like those in
[
24
,
25
]
are not known in this generality, and we leave them for future work.

In Theorem
1.1
, ii), one might wonder why the repulsion in
V_{N}
is at height
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
rather than
\sqrt{2d\,\overline{g}\log N}
. To understand this, we can think of decomposing the field into a “local” part which lives at scale
N^{2/d}
, and carries the short-range correlations, and a “undertow” part, which carries the long-range correlations. For the Gaussian free field to stay positive over
V_{N}
, it is sufficient that enough of the local fields at scale
N^{2/d}
are pushed away from zero by
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
, which is the same level as their expected maximum, then the “undertow” part which carries the long range correlations ensures that the whole field remains positive on
V_{N}
. Note that the constant
\overline{g}
is quite sensitive to the law
\mathbb{Q}
of the conductances, hence the microscopic effect of the conductances is visible in the repulsion height. In particular, in the special case of i.i.d. conductances with
\essinf\omega_{e}=\lambda
under
\mathbb{Q}
, we have
\overline{g}=g(0,0)/\lambda
. In particular, the field gets pushed up to a height which is strictly larger than in the homogeneous case with constant conductances and with the same scaling limit, see Remark
6.4
, 2). This shows that the impurities in the crystal have a non-trivial effect on the entropic repulsion phenomenon as well as the maximum of the unconditioned field, which is somewhat different from what happens in the critical dimension
d=2
, see Remark
6.4
, 4). Furthermore, the form of
\overline{g}=g(0,0)/\lambda
in the i.i.d. setting, suggests that in the case of degenerate conductances, the repulsion height is possibly much higher. In fact, going in this direction, for the Gaussian free field on the supercritical percolation cluster, in
d\geq 3
, due to the presence of pipes, we expect that the field under the hard wall event is repelled at height of order
\log N
, see discussion in Remark
6.4
, 3), so that the speed of convergence in Theorem
1.1
, i), is of order
N^{d-2}(\log N)^{2}
.

Finally, we remark that the quenched weak convergence in Theorem
1.1
, iii), has a local nature, and establishes that locally the field conditioned on the hard wall event behaves like the unconditioned field when recentered by the diverging amount
a_{N}
. This is in line with the homogeneous case treated in
[
33
]
, but the proof is more involved due to the lack of translation invariance. We can also obtain some more global information, see Remark
6.3
.

We briefly comment on the proofs of our results and give some intuition as well
as some further directions. The most challenging part of this work concerns the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
, i) and in establishing the convergence in ii).

For the proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
, i), we follow the coarse graining strategy from
[
52
]
which was also used in
[
29
,
27
,
44
]
with adapted notions of good and bad boxes. We found this strategy better suited to handle the lack of translation invariance in the model, as compared to
[
19
]
. We consider a covering of
V_{N}
with well separated boxes of side-length
N^{2/d}
, and for each box we split the field into a local part and its harmonic average. We declare boxes good if the minimum of the local field concentrates around its expected value, which is
-\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
. Thanks to the mixing property of
\mathbb{Q}
and Bernoulli concentration, most of the boxes are good, and under the hard wall event, in each good box there will be sites where the harmonic averages of the field are at height
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
. Incidentally, this is the only place where quantitative ergodicity of the environment is used. We then make use of certain Gaussian bounds, which will bring into play the capacity of the union of the good boxes. These form a porous interface that is hard for the random walk to avoid. A crucial ingredient is therefore a quenched
solidification estimate
for such porous interfaces, which allows us to replace their discrete capacity with the discrete capacity of
V_{N}
. This is close to the solidification estimates developed in
[
44
,
45
]
, even though it is less involved in our case. The proof is completed with the homogenization of the discrete capacity to the Brownian one.

For what concerns Theorem
1.1
, ii), the proof is more involved. In
[
19
]
, the authors rely on the translation invariance in the model, together with FKG inequality to replace the conditioned expectation
u^{\omega}_{N}(\cdot)=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{\cdot}|\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}]
with a an average over a macroscopic box. To overcome the lack of translation invariance, we exploit the superharmonicity of
u^{\omega}_{N}
to obtain a lower bound as a weighted average over the
boundary
of a macroscopic box. We then show that weighted averages of the field over the boundary of macroscopic boxes contained in
V_{N}
concentrate around
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
exponentially fast, which allows us to conclude the lower bound. The proof of this fact is reminiscent of the arguments in
[
44
,
28
,
27
]
where concentration around macroscopic ball averages under disconnection events was shown. However, our setting is slightly more delicate as the concentration is for averages over a lower dimensional set, namely the boundary of a
macroscopic
box. Strikingly, once we have a sharp lower bound for
u^{\omega}_{N}
in
V_{N}
, we can use it to get an estimate on the harmonicity defect of
u^{\omega}_{N}
, which is the key step in obtaining a matching upper bound, by estimating
u^{\omega}_{N}
from above rewriting it as a weighted average on the boundary of a
mesoscopic
ball, which turns out to be enough with the help of a entropy bound. We hope that this new approach can be useful to obtain pathwise results in other contexts, such as when dealing with disconnection events for level-set percolation as in
[
27
]
.

It turns out that the Gaussian free field is closely related to random interlacements, via certain isomorphism theorems, see
[
50
]
. It would be interesting to investigate atypical events of hard wall type for the occupation time field of random interlacement in random environment, which then should be helpful to obtain some fine information on the occupation time field of random walks in random environments. Hopefully, some ideas developed in this article, together with those in
[
29
,
41
,
40
,
45
]
, will be useful.
In a different direction, we remark that the Gaussian free field with random conductances also has implications for more general gradient fields with non-convex potentials, as studied in
[
15
,
18
]
. For such models, the study of extremes and of the hard wall repulsion phenomenon is quite intriguing, and the results of this article may provide some insight in this direction as well.

We now explain the organization of this article.
In Section
2
we introduce some notation, and certain results about random walk in random environment, heat kernel bounds, potential theory, the Gaussian free field, FKG and relative entropy inequalities. We also present some key results from stochastic homogenization theory.
Section
3
, similarly to
[
27
, Section 6]
, is devoted to obtaining certain quenched Gaussian bounds in Corollary
3.2
. These estimates are crucially used both in Proposition
4.9
and Proposition
5.3
.
Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the quenched asymptotic large deviation result for the hard wall event as in Theorem
1.1
, i). This is attained by showing lower and upper bounds in Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.9
, respectively. In order to achieve the latter, we also rely on a quenched solidification estimate for sets with a special covering property, which is the content of Corollary
4.7
.
Section
5
is aimed at the study of the expectation of the field conditionally on the hard wall event. The goal is to obtain Theorem
1.1
, ii). The proof consists in showing the result for the discrete equilibrium potential first and then replacing it with the Brownian one. The lower bound is contained in Proposition
5.4
, and is pivotal for obtaining in Lemma
5.5
an estimate for the harmonicity defect of the conditioned expectation. We then exploit such estimate for the proof of the upper bound in Proposition
5.6
.
In Section
6
we derive the limiting pathwise behavior for the conditioned field as in Theorem
1.1
, iii). This is done in two steps: In the first, we prove that the result holds when we recenter the field by its expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In the second, we show that the difference between such recentering and the one constantly equal to
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{0}|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
is
o(1)
as
N\to\infty
. This is achieved by showing a Hölder regularity estimate for the conditioned field in Lemma
6.1
. We conclude Section
6
with an extensive discussion. Finally, in Appendix
A
we get a generalization of the results assuming just sufficient mixing conditions on the conductances, while Appendix

2.
Notation and useful results

This section establishes further notation and classical results on random walks among inhomogeneous conductances, potential theory, and the Gaussian free field with bond disorder. We also recall standard inequalities, such as FKG, Brascamp-Lieb and entropy inequalities, that are required for our analysis. In this article we always assume that
d\geq 3
.

To begin, we introduce some basic notation. We denote by
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,...\}
the set of natural numbers. Given two real numbers
s,t
, we write
s\wedge t
and
s\vee t
for the minimum and maximum of
s
and
t
, respectively, and, when
s\geq 0
, the integer part of
s
is represented by
\lfloor s\rfloor
. For
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we set
\lfloor s\rfloor=(\lfloor s_{1}\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor s_{d}\rfloor)
. We denote the Euclidean and
\ell^{\infty}
-norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
|\cdot|
and
|\cdot|_{\infty}
, respectively. We write
B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,|x-y|_{\infty}\leq r\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for the (closed)
\ell^{\infty}
-ball of radius
r\geq 0
centered at
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We call
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
neighbors, and write
x\sim y
, when
|x-y|=1
. For a subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
|K|
denotes the cardinality of
K
, and we write
K\subset\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
|K|<\infty
. We let
K^{c}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus K
be the complement of
K
,
\partial K=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus K\,:\exists\,x\in K\text{ such that }y\sim x\}
the external boundary of
K
, and
\partial_{\text{in}}K=\{y\in K\,:\exists\,x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus K\text{ such that }y\sim x\}
the internal boundary of
K
. For
K,U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we write
d_{\infty}(K,U)
for the
\ell^{\infty}
-distance between
K
and
U
. Given a set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
V_{N}=(NV)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the discrete blow-up of
V
, and, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we define the
\varepsilon
-bulk of
V
as
V^{\varepsilon}=\{x\in V:|x-y|>\varepsilon,\,y\in V^{c}\}
, and we set
V^{\varepsilon}_{N}=(NV^{\varepsilon})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For
\varphi,\psi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
we write
\varphi\leq\psi
to say that
\varphi_{x}\leq\psi_{x}
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We call a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
increasing if
f(\varphi)\leq f(\psi)
any time
\varphi\leq\psi
, and decreasing if
-f
is increasing. We also say that
f
is supported on
K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
f(\varphi)=f(\psi)
whenever
(\varphi_{x})_{x\in K}=(\psi_{x})_{x\in K}
. For a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and functions
u,\,v:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
belonging to
\ell^{2}(\mu)
we also write
\langle u,v\rangle_{\mu}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{x}u(x)v(x)
, and use the shorthand
\langle u,v\rangle
when
\mu
is constantly equal to one. We use the conventions
\sup_{x\in\varnothing}u(x)=-\infty
and
\inf_{x\in\varnothing}u(x)=\infty
.

We consider the integer
d
-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with set of undirected edges
\mathbb{E}_{d}=\{\{x,y\}:\,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,x\sim y\}
. For fixed
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
, we define the collection of configurations of weights on the edges of the graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d})
as
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}=[\lambda,\Lambda]^{\mathbb{E}_{d}}
. For any
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, the conductance on the edge
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}_{d}
is denoted by
\omega_{x,y}=\omega_{\{x,y\}}=\omega_{y,x}
.
Given
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, we define

(2.1)

\mu^{\omega}_{x}=\sum_{y\,:\,y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

Given a field of conductances
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
one can define the operator
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}
acting on functions
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f(x)=\sum_{y\,:\,y\sim x}\frac{\omega_{x,y}}{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}(f(y)-f(x))\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,

which is the generator of a (constant-speed) random walk on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega)
. We call
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a (
\omega
-)harmonic function in a subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f(x)=0
for every
x\in K
. Further, we say that
f
is (
\omega
-)superharmonic in
K
if
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f(x)\leq 0
for every
x\in K
and that
f
is (
\omega
-)subharmonic in
K
if
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in K
.

We now introduce the continuous-time, constant-speed random walk on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega)
and some potential theoretic notions associated with it. For any given
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
we denote by
P_{x}^{\omega}
the law of the continuous-time Markov chain
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
starting from
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with generator
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}
, that is, the process jumps from
x
to
y
,
x\sim y
, at rate
\omega_{x,y}/\mu^{\omega}_{x}
. The walk is reversible with respect to the measure
\mu^{\omega}
defined in (
2.1
).
We denote by
E_{x}^{\omega}
the expectation associated with
P_{x}^{\omega}
.

For
U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define the stopping times (with respect to the canonical filtration generated by
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
)
H_{U}=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,X_{t}\in U\}
,
\widetilde{H}_{U}=\inf\{t>0\,:\,X_{t}\neq X_{0}\,,\,X_{t}\in U\}
,
T_{U}=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,X_{t}\notin U\}
, which are the entrance, hitting and exit times of
U
, respectively. We define
q^{\omega}_{t}
and
q^{\omega}_{t,U}
to be the heat kernel and the killed heat kernel upon leaving
U
, respectively, by setting

(2.3)

\displaystyle q^{\omega}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=P^{\omega}_{x}[X_{t}=y]/\mu^{\omega}_{y}\,,

\displaystyle t\geq 0\,,\,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\text{ and}

(2.4)

\displaystyle q^{\omega}_{t,U}(x,y)

\displaystyle=P^{\omega}_{x}[X_{t}=y,T_{U}>t]/\mu^{\omega}_{y}\,,

\displaystyle t\geq 0\,,\,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

We let

(2.5)

\displaystyle g^{\omega}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{\omega}_{t}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t=E^{\omega}_{x}\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t}=y\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]/\mu^{\omega}_{y}\,,

\displaystyle x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\text{ and}

(2.6)

\displaystyle g_{U}^{\omega}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{\omega}_{t,U}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}t=E^{\omega}_{x}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{U}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t}=y\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]/\mu^{\omega}_{y}\,,

\displaystyle x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

be the Green function of the random walk and the Green function of the random walk killed upon leaving
U
, respectively. It is known that, under the assumption of elliptic conductances,
q^{\omega}_{t}
,
q^{\omega}_{t,U}
,
g^{\omega}
and
g^{\omega}_{U}
are finite and symmetric (for every
t\geq 0
), and
q^{\omega}_{t,U}(x,y)=g^{\omega}_{U}(x,y)=0
when
x\in U^{c}
or
y\in U^{c}
.

By the strong Markov property, it also holds that

(2.7)

g^{\omega}(x,y)=g^{\omega}_{U}(x,y)+E^{\omega}_{x}[T_{U}<\infty,g^{\omega}(X_{T_{U}},y)]\,,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(see Proposition 1.6 in
[
51
]
), and for
U=\{x\}
by symmetry we get that

(2.8)

g^{\omega}(y,\cdot)\text{ is $\omega$-harmonic in }\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{y\}\,,\qquad y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

We also have the following bounds on the Green function:

(2.9)

\frac{c_{1}}{|x-y|^{d-2}\vee 1}\leq g^{\omega}(x,y)\leq\frac{c_{2}}{|x-y|^{d-2}\vee 1}\,,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\,\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}\,,

where all the constants are independent of
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
(see Theorem 6.28 of
[
11
]
for uniform bounds on the heat kernel, that imply the bounds on
g^{\omega}
).

Moreover, for the killed heat kernel we have that for
\vartheta\in(0,1)
there exists
R_{0}(\vartheta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
R\geq R_{0}(\vartheta)
it holds that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(2.10)

q_{t,B(x_{0},R)}^{\omega}(x_{1},x_{2})\geq c_{3}(\vartheta)t^{-d/2}\,,\qquad x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x_{0},(1-\vartheta)R)\text{ and }c_{4}(\vartheta)R^{2}\leq t\leq R^{2}\,,

with
c_{4}(\vartheta)<1
(see Theorem 5.26 in
[
11
]
).

For later use, we state the following Hölder-regularity property for functions
u
which are
\omega
-harmonic in
B(x_{0},2r)
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
r>0
, namely that there exists two positive constants
c_{5},\tau
, only depending on
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, such that for every
x,y\in B(x_{0},r)

(2.11)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq c_{5}\left(\frac{|x-y|_{\infty}}{r}\right)^{\tau}\sup_{z\in B(x_{0},2r)}|u(z)|\,,

see Proposition 6.2 in
[
32
]
.

We now present some potential theory associated with the random walk.
For
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, we introduce the operator
G^{\omega}
acting on
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.12)

G^{\omega}f(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g^{\omega}(x,y)f(y)\mu^{\omega}_{y}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

when the series on the right converges absolutely. For finite measures
\nu
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we also write

(2.13)

\nu G^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\nu_{y}g^{\omega}(x,y)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

We also define the Dirichlet form associated with
-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}
as

(2.14)

\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\,:\,x\sim y}\omega_{x,y}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)^{2}

for
f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, and by polarization (and symmetry of
\omega_{x,y}
) we define it for
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
as

(2.15)

\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\,:\,x\sim y}\omega_{x,y}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)\big(g(y)-g(x)\big)

when the resulting series converges absolutely, in which case we have
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,g)=\langle-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}f,g\rangle_{\mu^{\omega}}
. We use the shorthand
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f)=\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,f)
.
The following relation between
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}
and
G^{\omega}
holds:

(2.16)

\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(G^{\omega}f,g)=\langle f,g\rangle_{\mu^{\omega}}\,,\qquad f,g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,,

whenever the series converge absolutely
(see Proposition 1.3 of
[
51
]
), hence we can also write
G^{\omega}=(-\mathcal{L}^{\omega})^{-1}
as operators on
\ell^{2}(\mu^{\omega})
.

For
A\subset\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the equilibrium measure of
A
as

(2.17)

e^{\omega}_{A}(x)=P_{x}^{\omega}[\widetilde{H}_{A}=\infty]\mu^{\omega}_{x}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,

and the capacity of
A
(associated with
X
) as its finite total mass,

(2.18)

\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(A)=\sum_{x\in A}e_{A}^{\omega}(x)\,.

For a closed
\ell^{\infty}
-ball
B(x,L)
, where
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
L\geq 1
, the capacity satisfies the classical bounds

(2.19)

cL^{d-2}\leq\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(B(x,L))\leq CL^{d-2}\,,

where the constants are independent of
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
(see Lemma 7.21 (a) in
[
11
]
).
We introduce the equilibrium potential of
A
,

(2.20)

h^{\omega}_{A}(x)=P^{\omega}_{x}[H_{A}<\infty]\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,

and its relation with the equilibrium measure
e^{\omega}_{A}
,

(2.21)

h^{\omega}_{A}(x)=e^{\omega}_{A}G^{\omega}(x)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(see Corollary 7.4 of
[
11
]
). We also note that
\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(A)=\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h^{\omega}_{A})
.

We further define the equilibrium potential and the capacity of
A\subseteq V\subset\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for the random walk killed upon leaving
V
, as

(2.22)

h^{\omega}_{A,V}(x)=P^{\omega}_{x}[H_{A}<T_{V}]\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Cap}_{V}^{\omega}(A)=\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h_{A,V}^{\omega}),

respectively.

We now consider the (discrete) Gaussian free field on the weighted graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega)
for a fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, which we introduced in (
1.1
), and we denote by
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}
the expectation associated with
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
.
For
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we write
\mathcal{F}_{A}=\sigma(\varphi_{x}\,:\,x\in A)
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the field on
A
.
The law of the Gaussian free field
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
is uniquely characterized as the centered Gaussian field such that for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(2.23)

\mathbb{P}^{\omega}\left(\mathrm{d}\phi|\mathcal{F}_{\{x\}^{c}}\right)(\varphi)\propto\exp\bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\,:\,y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}\left(\phi_{x}-\varphi_{y}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{x}\prod_{y\neq x}\delta_{\varphi_{y}}(\mathrm{d}\phi_{y})\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}^{\omega}(\mathrm{d}\varphi)\text{-a.s.}

(see
[
38
, Section 13.1]
).
We now state a classical spatial Markov property for the Gaussian free field
\varphi
. For
U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, we introduce the (
\omega
-)harmonic average
\xi^{\omega,U}
of
\varphi
in
U
and the (
\omega
-)local field
\psi^{\omega,U}
as

(2.24)

\displaystyle\xi_{x}^{\omega,U}

\displaystyle=E^{\omega}_{x}[\varphi_{X_{T_{U}}},T_{U}<\infty]=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P^{\omega}_{x}[X_{T_{U}}=y,T_{U}<\infty]\varphi_{y}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,;

(2.25)

\displaystyle\psi_{x}^{\omega,U}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{x}-\xi_{x}^{\omega,U}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

By definition,
\varphi_{x}=\xi_{x}^{\omega,U}+\psi_{x}^{\omega,U}
and for
x\in U^{c}
we have
\psi^{\omega,U}_{x}=0
and
\xi^{\omega,U}_{x}=\varphi_{x}
. The Markov property is the following:

(2.26)

(\psi^{\omega,U}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{U^{c}}
(in particular of
(\xi^{\omega,U}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
),

and is distributed as a centered Gaussian field with covariance
g^{\omega}_{U}(\cdot,\cdot)
,

where
g^{\omega}_{U}
is the Green function of the random walk among conductances
\omega
killed upon exiting
U
as in (
2.6
) (see Proposition 2.3 of
[
51
]
).

Given
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the translation operator
T_{f}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
as

(2.27)

T_{f}\varphi=\varphi+f\,,\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,.

For a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
and a map
T
from
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, we write
\nu T^{-1}(\cdot)
for the push-forward measure
\nu(T^{-1}\cdot).

If
\varphi
is a Gaussian free field under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
and
f
is finitely supported, then the law
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T^{-1}_{f}
can be characterized by its Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
as

(2.28)

\frac{\mathrm{d}(\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T^{-1}_{f})}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}}(\varphi)=\exp\left\{\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(f,f)\right\}\,,\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,.

This is equivalent to say that

(2.29)

\varphi
under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T^{-1}_{f}
has the same law as
\varphi+f
under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
,

see (2.4) of
[
52
]
.

We now state some key inequalities that will be useful in the sequel. For
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
we will make use of three inequalities. The first one is the FKG inequality for positively correlated Gaussian fields which states that (see e.g.
[
46
]
)

(2.30)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[u(\varphi)v(\varphi)]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[u(\varphi)]\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[v(\varphi)]\,,\qquad u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\mathbb{P}^{\omega}),\text{ increasing}

and of course the same inequality still holds true if
u
and
v
are both decreasing, while it is reversed if one is increasing and the other is decreasing.

For any subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we write

(2.31)

\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{U}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\,\cdot\,|\varphi_{x}\geq 0,\,\forall x\in U]

for the measure of the Gaussian free field
\varphi
conditioned on being non-negative on
U
.
Given two subsets
A,A^{\prime}\subset\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, the second inequality states that (see e.g. Proposition 5.22 and Definition 5.21 of
[
14
]
)

(2.32)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A}[u(\varphi)]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A^{\prime}}[u(\varphi)]\,,\qquad u:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ increasing}\,,

where
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A^{\prime}}
are the expectation associated with
\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{A}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{A^{\prime}}
, respectively.

The third inequality states that for any constant field with value
a\geq 0

(2.33)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A}[u(\varphi)]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\omega,a}[u(\varphi)|\varphi_{x}\geq 0,\,\forall x\in A]\,,\qquad u:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ increasing}\,,

where
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,a}
is the expectation associated with the law
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T_{a}^{-1}
of the field shifted by height
a
(see e.g. (B.13) of
[
39
]
).

Another key tool we use is the Brascamp-Lieb inequality, which is a concentration inequality. We use the following version for the Gaussian free field
\varphi
(see
[
33
]
, and
[
21
]
):

(2.34)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A}\Big[u(\langle f,\varphi\rangle-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{A}[\langle f,\varphi\rangle])\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[u(\langle f,\varphi\rangle)]

for every
f:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
compactly supported, and where
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is either
u(r)=|r|^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\geq 1
, or
u(r)=\mathrm{e}^{r}
.

The last inequalities we recall are relative entropy inequalities to compare the measure of a set under two different probabilities. Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
be two probability measures on the same measurable space
(E,\mathcal{E})
such that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. The relative entropy of
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
is defined as

(2.35)

H(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}|\mathbb{P})=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\Big]\in[0,\infty]\,,

where
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}
and
\mathbb{E}
are the expectation with respect to
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
and
\mathbb{P}
, respectively. For any
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-integrable function
U:E\to\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{U}]<\infty
, the following inequality holds:

(2.36)

\log\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{U}]+H(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}|\mathbb{P})\geq\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[U]\,,

see Lemma 3.2.13 of
[
35
]
.
Moreover, for any event
F\in\mathcal{E}
with positive
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
-probability,

(2.37)

\log\frac{\mathbb{P}[F]}{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[F]}\geq-\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[F]}\left[H(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}|\mathbb{P})+e^{-1}\right]\,,

see p. 76 of
[
35
]
.

We now introduce the random environment and a homogenization result for the random walk. We define a group of environment shifts
\tau_{x}:\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}\to\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, as

(2.38)

(\tau_{x}\,\omega)_{y,z}=\omega_{y+x,z+x}\,,\qquad y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}\,.

We endow
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
with the canonical
\sigma
-algebra of cylinders
\mathcal{G}
and consider on the resulting measurable space a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
(the environment measure) which we assume stationary and ergodic with respect to the group of shifts
(\tau_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, namely that
\mathbb{Q}[\tau_{x}(A)]=\mathbb{Q}[A]
for every
A\in\mathcal{G}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and that every measurable
f:\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f\circ\tau_{x}=f
,
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. and for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, is
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. constant. This is equivalent to say that all events
A\in\mathcal{G}
that are invariant under the shifts satisfy
\mathbb{Q}[A]\in\{0,1\}
.

It is know that when
\mathbb{Q}
is a stationary and ergodic probability measure on
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
the diffusively scaled random walk
(n^{-1}X_{tn^{2}})_{t\geq 0}
under
P^{\omega}_{0}
converges in law to a Brownian motion
Z=(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
which has a deterministic and non-degenerate covariance matrix
a^{\mathrm{hom}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Denoting the law of
Z
started at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
W_{x}
, we can define the Dirichlet form
\mathbf{D}:W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
associated with
a^{\mathrm{hom}}
(where
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that are weakly differentiable with weak derivatives in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) as

(2.39)

\mathbf{D}(f,f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla f(x)\cdot a^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla f(x)\mathrm{d}x,\qquad f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
closed or open bounded, we define the harmonic potential of
A
associated with
Z
as

(2.40)

{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A}(x)=W_{x}[(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}\text{ hits }A]\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

and for
A\subseteq B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
both bounded and closed or open, we let

(2.41)

{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A,B}(x)=W_{x}[(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}\text{ hits }A\text{ before exiting }B]\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

be the harmonic potential (associated with
Z
) of
A
with respect to
B
.
Then
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A,B}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded and closed or open
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A}
belongs to the extended Dirichlet space of
(\mathbf{D},W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(this can be seen combining Theorem 4.3.3, p. 171 of
[
37
]
with Theorem 2.1.5, p. 72 of the same reference, while in Example 1.5.3 in
[
37
]
there is a characterization of the extended Dirichlet space).
Thus we can define the capacity (associated with the Brownian motion
Z
) of
A
, and the capacity of
A
with respect to
B
, as

(2.42)

\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(A)=\mathbf{D}({h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A},{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A})\,,\qquad\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{B}(A)=\mathbf{D}({h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A,B},{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{A,B})\,,

respectively.
Moreover, it is known that for every compact Lipschitz domain
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, letting
B
be an open Euclidean ball such that
B\supseteq\overline{V}
, it holds for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}

(2.43)

\frac{1}{N^{d-2}}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}_{B_{N}}(V_{N})\to\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{B}(V),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty\,,

and for every
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded Lipschitz domain, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}

(2.44)

\frac{1}{N^{d-2}}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})\to\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty\,.

(see Proposition 5.3 and Corollary 5.4 of
[
27
]
, whose proofs are based on the argument of Corollary 3.4 of
[
43
]
).
In fact, the convergence in (
2.43
) and (
2.44
) can be extended to every open bounded
strongly regular
set (see Definition 1.8 in
[
7
]
for a similar concept).

(2.45)

A set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be strongly regular if for all
x\in\partial V
one has
W_{x}[H_{\mathring{V}}=0]=1
, where for
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
H_{A}=\inf\{t>0\,:Z_{t}\in A\}
is the first hitting time of
A
.

Examples of strongly regular sets are open bounded sets with Lipschitz boundary.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded strongly regular set. Let
B
be an open ball such that
B\supseteq\overline{V}
. Then,

(2.46)

{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V,B}(x)={h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{\overline{V},B}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x)={h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{\overline{V}}(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

In particular,
\mathrm{Cap}_{B}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)=\mathrm{Cap}_{B}^{\mathrm{hom}}(\overline{V})
and
\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)=\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(\overline{V})
.

Proof.

Clearly by the strong Markov property one has
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x)=E^{W}_{x}[W_{Z_{H_{\overline{V}}}}[H_{V}<\infty]\mathbbm{1}_{H_{\overline{V}}<\infty}]
,
E^{W}_{x}
being the expectation associated with
W_{x}
. By strong regularity of
V
and the fact that
V
is open, for all
y\in\partial V
one has that
W_{y}[H_{V}<\infty]=1
as
W_{y}[H_{V}=0]=1
. Hence,
W_{Z_{H_{\overline{V}}}}[H_{V}<\infty]=1
on the event
\{H_{\overline{V}}<\infty\}
and thus
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x)={h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{\overline{V}}(x)
. The other equality is proven analogously.
∎

We can now extend the convergence in (
2.43
) and (
2.44
) to open bounded strongly regular sets. We briefly discuss only (
2.44
) as the other is analogous. One can approximate from inside and outside the set
V
with increasing, respectively decreasing, sequences
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(W_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of open sets with smooth boundaries.
Then, by the monotonicity of the capacity, one has for all
N\geq 1

(2.47)

N^{d-2}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}((U_{n})_{N})\leq N^{d-2}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})\leq N^{d-2}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}((W_{n})_{N})\,,

which taking the limit
N\to\infty
and using (
2.44
) for
U_{n}
and
W_{n}
leads to

(2.48)

\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(U_{n})\leq\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})\leq\varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})\leq\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(W_{n})\,,

for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
.
Noting that
\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(U_{n})\to\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)
and
\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(W_{n})\to\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(\overline{V})=\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)
as
n\to\infty
(the last equality follows from the previous lemma), we conclude that (
2.44
) holds true for
V
.

In Section
5
, we will also need the following homogenization result for the uniform convergence of the discrete harmonic potential to the continuous one, in order to obtain Theorem
1.1
, ii), with
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)
in place of
h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open strongly regular set. Then, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
,

(2.49)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)\Big|=0\,.

Proof.

Consider the closed Euclidean ball
\overline{B}_{M}
of radius
M
, then

(2.50)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in N\overline{B}_{MN}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{x\in N\overline{B}_{MN}^{c}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)|\,.

We first handle the second term on the right-hand side and see that by the decay of the Green function (see (
2.9
)), together with (
2.19
) and (
2.21
), (the same estimates hold also in the continuum) it is bounded from above by

(2.51)

\sup_{x\in N\overline{B}_{MN}^{c}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}[|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)|+|{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)|]\leq\frac{c}{M^{d-2}}\,,

which can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
M
large. We now focus on the first term on the right-hand side. By
[
23
, Appendix A]
, since the conductances are uniformly elliptic and bounded, an arbitrary starting point invariance principle holds. That is, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
it holds that for every sequence
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
such that
x_{N}\in(N\overline{B}_{MN})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x_{N}/N\to x\in\overline{B}_{M}
the random walk
(N^{-1}X_{tN^{2}})_{t\geq 0}
converges in law under
P^{\omega}_{x_{N}}
to the Brownian motion
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
started at
x
with deterministic non-degenerate covariance matrix
a^{\mathrm{hom}}
.

Since
V
is strongly regular, it follows that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, we have that for every sequence
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
such that
x_{N}\in(N\overline{B}_{MN})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
x_{N}/N\to x\in\overline{B}_{M}

(2.52)

h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x_{N})=P^{\omega}_{x_{N}}[H_{V_{N}}<\infty]\to W_{x}[H_{V}<\infty]={h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x)\,.

By the arbitrariness of the sequence
(x_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, we conclude that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
for all
M\in\mathbb{N}

(2.53)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{x\in N\overline{B}_{MN}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|h^{\omega}_{V_{N}}(x)-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x/N)|=0\,,

Indeed, if this were not the case, there would exist
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
(x_{N_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
such that
x_{N_{k}}\in(N_{k}\overline{B}_{MN_{k}})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
|h^{\omega}_{V_{N_{k}}}(x_{N_{k}})-{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x_{N_{k}}/N_{k})|>\varepsilon
for all
N_{k}\geq 1
. By compactness, we can assume without loss of generality that
x_{N_{k}}/N_{k}\to x\in\overline{B}_{M}
, which leads to a contradiction, as by strong regularity of
V
,
{h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(x)
is continuous (see
[
47
, Proposition 3.6, p. 31]
). By putting together (
2.51
) and (
2.53
), we conclude the proof taking
M\to\infty
.

∎

To define the relevant Gaussian functionals, we now divide

Given

Following the construction in

(3.4)

\mathcal{F}=\{f\in(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathcal{S}}:f(z)\in B_{z}\text{ for all }z\in\mathcal{S}\}\,.

We also consider the coefficients

\nu^{\omega}(z)=\frac{e^{\omega}_{S}(B_{z})}{\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(S)}\,,\qquad z\in\mathcal{S}\,,

The Gaussian functionals associated with

Theorem 3.1
.

Recall
L\geq 1

K\geq 100

as

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\{r^{d-2}\mathbb{V}\mathrm{ar}^{\omega}[Z^{\omega}_{f,\beta,b}]-\frac{r^{d-2}}{\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(S)}\alpha^{\omega}_{K,L,\beta,b}\Big\}\leq c_{6}\beta^{2}\,,

Proof.

The result (

We now start the proof of (

\mathcal{S}

4.
Quenched decay of the probability of the hard wall event

4.1.
Lower bound

In this section we prove the quenched asymptotic lower bound, as
N\to\infty
, for the rate of decay for the probability of the hard wall event
\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}=\{\varphi_{x}\geq 0,\,\forall x\in V_{N}\}
, and get the optimal constant as in Theorem
1.1
, i).

We follow a classical change of measure approach, namely we tilt the Gaussian free field measure in such a way that
\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}
becomes typical under the resulting new measure. Then we use the relative entropy inequality (
2.37
) to compare the probability of
\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}
under the original and the tilted measure. This yields an estimate in which
N^{2-d}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})
appears, that converges
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. to the capacity
\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)
of the continuum domain
V
by the homogenization result (
2.44
).

Proposition 4.1
.

For
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
an open and bounded strongly regular
set and for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
it holds that

(4.1)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}\log N}\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}]\geq-2\overline{g}\,\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}(V)\,.

Proof.

We follow the idea in
[
39
, Section 3.5]
that dealt with the homogeneous case.
Let
\alpha_{N}=\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
and
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
an open ball of radius
R
such that
B\supseteq V
. We define the probability measure of the field shifted by a height
f_{N}=\alpha_{N}h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}}
as
\mathbb{P}_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T_{f_{N}}^{-1}
(recall the definition (
2.22
) of the equilibrium potential
h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}}
for the killed random walk). We also define a third probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{3}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{P}_{2}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}]
, for which the hard wall event is typical.

We want to use the relative entropy inequality (
2.37
) in order to compare the measure of the hard wall event under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
and
\mathbb{P}_{3}
.
Since
\mathbb{P}_{3}
-a.s. it holds that
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{3}/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}=(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{3}/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2})(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2}/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega})
, we have that

(4.2)

H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}^{\omega})=H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}_{2})+\mathbb{E}_{3}\Big[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}}\Big]

where
\mathbb{E}_{3}
is the expectation associated with
\mathbb{P}_{3}
.
We now show that the first term on the right-hand side is negligible as
N\to\infty
, and that the cost of the change of measure in the limit is therefore given just by the second term. By the FKG inequality (
2.30
) we have that

(4.3)

\displaystyle H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}_{2})

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[T_{f_{N}}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]=-\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}\geq-\alpha_{N},\,\forall x\in V_{N}]

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}\geq-\alpha_{N}]\leq-|V_{N}|\log\phi(\alpha_{N}/\sqrt{\overline{g}})

where
\phi
is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Using that

\phi(x)\geq 1-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}x}\exp(-x^{2}/2)
for
x>0
, we get that for all
N
sufficiently large

(4.4)

H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}_{2})\leq-c\,N^{d}\log\bigg(1-\frac{c^{\prime}}{\alpha_{N}/\sqrt{\,\overline{g}}}\exp\left(-\alpha_{N}^{2}/2\overline{g}\right)\bigg)\leq C\,\frac{N^{d-2}}{\sqrt{4\log N}}\,.

Hence

(4.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}}H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}_{2})=0

and we can therefore neglect the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.2
).
For the second term, we have that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{3}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}}(\varphi)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}}(T_{f_{N}}\varphi)\Big)\Big|T_{f_{N}}^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}\Big]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{N}\,\mathbb{E}^{\omega}\Big[\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}},\varphi)\Big|T^{-1}_{f_{N}}\mathcal{W}_{N}^{+}\Big]+\frac{\alpha_{N}^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}_{B_{N}}(V_{N})

where in the second step we used the characterizations of the shifted measure (
2.28
).
We now want to prove that the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.6
) is negligible.
Note that
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}},\varphi)=\langle-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}},\varphi\rangle_{\mu^{\omega}}
is a centered Gaussian with variance
\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}_{B_{N}}(V_{N})
. Applying the relative entropy inequality (
2.36
) with
u(\varphi)=\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}},\varphi)
to relate
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\,\cdot\,|T_{f_{N}}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
, we get

(4.7)

\Big|\alpha_{N}\,\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\left.\mathcal{E}^{\omega}(h^{\omega}_{V_{N},B_{N}},\varphi)\right|T_{f_{N}}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]\Big|\leq\alpha_{N}\left\{\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}_{B_{N}}(V_{N})/2-\log\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[T_{f_{N}}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]\right\}\,.

When dividing by
N^{d-2}\log{N}
, in the limit for
N\to\infty
the first addendum in the right-hand side goes to
0
, since
\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{2-d}\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}_{B_{N}}(V_{N})<\infty
by (
2.19
), and the second addendum also goes to
0
since it is equal to
H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}_{2})
by (
4.3
).
Plugging this into (
4.6
) yields

(4.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}\log N}\,\mathbb{E}_{3}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}}(\varphi)\Big)\Big]=2\overline{g}\,\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{B}(V)\,.

Coming back to (
4.2
), we have shown that

(4.9)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d-2}\log N}H(\mathbb{P}_{3}|\mathbb{P}^{\omega})=2\overline{g}\,\mathrm{Cap}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{B}(V)\,,

thus, the claim follows from (
2.37
) since
\mathbb{P}_{3}[\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]=1
and upon taking
R\to\infty
.
∎

4.2.
Upper bound

In this section we prove the quenched asymptotic upper bound, as
N\to\infty
, for the rate of decay for the probability of the hard wall event
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}=\{\varphi_{x}\geq 0,\,\forall x\in V_{N}\}
, and get the optimal constant as in Theorem
1.1
, i).

We consider the division of
V_{N}
into boxes (whose size depends on
N
) and the associated decomposition of
\varphi
into harmonic averages and local fields, as in Section
3
. We show that, under
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}
, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. environment
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, with high
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
-probability, on the set
V_{N}
there is a large number of “good” boxes where there is at least one vertex in which the harmonic average is above the threshold
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
(note that by Theorem
1.1
, ii), this is the first-order asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
when
x\in V_{N}
). We then encode this event into the supremum of the Gaussian functionals we defined in Section
3
, whose tail probabilities we estimate via Corollary
3.2
. This yields a bound in terms of the capacity, associated with the random walk, of the union of good boxes. As
N\to\infty
, we show that the union of good boxes has solidification properties (akin to those studied in
[
44
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
52
]
) that let us control such capacity by
\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N})
, hence capturing the exact decay rate of
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
through the homogenization result (
2.44
).

We divide the proof into three parts. In Section
4.2.1
we characterize the asymptotic behavior of the expectation of the maximum of the local field on mesoscopic boxes in
V_{N}
as their size grows to infinity. In Section
4.2.2
we prove the solidification property of the union of good boxes. Finally, in Section
4.2.3
we exploit Corollary
3.2
and the results of Section
4.2.1
and Section
4.2.2
to complete the proof for the upper bound.

4.2.1.
Quenched maximum of the local field on boxes

From now on we consider
L=L(N)
to be a growing sequence of integers. We set

(4.10)

L=\left\lceil\frac{N^{2/d}}{(\log N)^{1/2d}}\right\rceil\,,\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}\,,

and consider, for
K\geq 100d
integer,

(4.11)

\mathcal{C}_{N}=((4KL)\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cap V_{N}\,,

which satisfies (
3.2
).
Note that the cardinality of
\mathcal{C}_{N}
is of order
(N/(KL))^{d}
, hence

(4.12)

c\,K^{-d}N^{d-2}\sqrt{\log N}\leq|\mathcal{C}_{N}|\leq C\,K^{-d}N^{d-2}\sqrt{\log N}\,.

In the next proposition we show that, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, on every box
B_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
, there is a (uniformly in
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
) positive proportion of points where the local field
\psi^{\omega,z}
has high variance. To have such uniformity, quantitative ergodicity for the environment is needed. Although in the Proposition below conductances are assumed to be i.i.d. to ease the exposition, the result can be extended to environments with enough mixing (see Remark
6.4
, 1) below and Proposition
A.1
for a precise statement in the case of polynomially mixing environments).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, there exists a constant
q>0
such that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}

(4.13)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}\frac{1}{|B_{z}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g_{U_{z}}^{\omega}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}\geq q\,.

Proof.

We first find, by means of (
2.7
) and the quenched bounds (
2.9
),
R=R(\varepsilon)
sufficiently large such that
g^{\omega}(y,y)\leq g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)+\varepsilon/2
for all
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
.
By stationarity of
\mathbb{Q}
, we have

(4.14)

\mathbb{Q}[g_{B(y,R)}^{\omega}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon]\geq\mathbb{Q}[g^{\omega}(0,0)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon/2]=:2q>0\,,

where the last inequality follows from
\overline{g}=\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}g^{\omega}(0,0)
.
We aim to show that
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s.

(4.15)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}\frac{1}{|B_{z}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g_{B(y,R)}^{\omega}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}\geq q.

from which the claim follows as
g_{U_{z}}^{\omega}(y,y)\geq g_{B(y,R)}^{\omega}(y,y)
for
N
large enough.
To that end, we introduce the event

(4.16)

J^{\omega}_{N}(z)=\Big\{\frac{1}{|B_{z}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}<q\Big\}\,,

whose
\mathbb{Q}
-probability is independent of
z
, and provide a summable upper bound on
\mathbb{Q}[\cup_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}J^{\omega}_{N}(z)]
.
By Borel-Cantelli lemma, this will imply that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\omega)
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
and all
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
the event
J^{\omega}_{N}(z)
does not occur, which is equivalent to (
4.15
).

We start estimating
\mathbb{Q}[J^{\omega}_{N}(z)]
. We take
\ell=4R
and denote the box of side length
\ell
by
\square_{\ell}=[0,\ell)^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then

(4.17)

J^{\omega}_{N}(z)\subseteq\Big\{\sum_{x\in\square_{\ell}}\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}<q|B_{z}|\Big\}\,,

where
B_{z,x}=x+(\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{z})
.
Hence,
J^{\omega}_{N}(z)
is also contained in the event that there exists a
x\in\square_{\ell}
such that the average of the indicator functions on the set
B_{z,x}
is smaller than
q
.
Formally,

(4.18)

\mathbb{Q}[J^{\omega}_{N}(z)]\leq\mathbb{Q}\Big[\bigcup_{x\in\square_{\ell}}\Big\{\frac{1}{|B_{z,x}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}<q\Big\}\Big]\,.

For every
x\in\square_{\ell}
the collection
\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}:\,y\in B_{z,x}\}
is composed of independent variables, since
\ell=4R
and for each
y\in B_{z,x}
the random variable
g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)
depends only on
\{\omega_{e}\,:\,e\cap B(y,R)\neq\varnothing,\,e\in\mathbb{E}_{d}\}
and the conductances are i.i.d. Then, by Chernoff’s inequality for the lower tail of Binomial random variables (see Exercise 2.3.5 in
[
53
]
), together with (
4.14
), we have that

(4.19)

\mathbb{Q}[J^{\omega}_{N}(z)]\leq\ell^{d}\mathrm{e}^{-c(L/\ell)^{d}}\,.

Hence (
4.10
) yields

(4.20)

\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Q}\Big[\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}J^{\omega}_{N}(z)\Big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:CardinalityCN}}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}N^{d-2}\sqrt{\log N}\mathrm{e}^{-cL^{d}}<\infty\,.

By Borel-Cantelli lemma we have the claim (
4.15
).
∎

The following proposition characterizes the first order behavior of the maximum of the local field
\psi^{\omega,z}
uniformly over all the boxes
B_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{C}
. We later use this result in the asymptotic upper bound for the probability of the hard wall event
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}
, in order to show that, conditionally on
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}
, with high probability there is a large number of “good boxes” where the maximum of the harmonic average
\xi^{\omega,z}
is above the threshold
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}

(4.21)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}\left|\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in B_{z}}\psi^{\omega,z}_{y}]}{\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|B_{z}|}}-1\right|=0\,.

Proof.

We first prove the upper bound. By Sudakov-Fernique inequality we can dominate
(\psi^{\omega,z}_{y})_{y\in B_{z}}
with a field
(\beta_{y})_{y\in B_{z}}
of independent centered Gaussians with variance
\overline{g}
for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
. Then by classical estimates for i.i.d. Gaussians we get that
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. for every
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(4.22)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in B_{z}}\psi^{\omega,z}_{y}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in B_{z}}\beta_{y}]\leq\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|B_{z}|}\,.

We now turn to the lower bound. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider the set

(4.23)

A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)=\{y\in B_{z}:g^{\omega}_{U_{z}}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

By Proposition
4.2

\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. for every
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}

(4.24)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|}{|B_{z}|}\geq q/2>0\,.

We now introduce a further parameter
J>0
large enough and consider a set
{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)
of points in
A^{\omega}_{N,\varepsilon}(z)
with mutual distance at least
J
and such that

(4.25)

|{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)|\geq c|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|J^{-d}\,.

One such set always exists. Note that for every
y,y^{\prime}\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)
with
y\neq y^{\prime}

(4.26)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[(\psi^{\omega,z}_{y}-\psi^{\omega,z}_{y^{\prime}})^{2}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:QuenchedGFEstimate}}}{{\geq}}2(\overline{g}-\varepsilon)-2c_{2}J^{2-d}\,.

Let
(\upsilon_{y})_{y\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)}
be a family of independent centered Gaussians with variance
\overline{g}-\varepsilon-c_{2}J^{2-d}
. Using again Sudakov-Fernique inequality we have that

(4.27)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in B_{z}}\psi^{\omega,z}_{y}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)}\upsilon_{y}]\,.

Dividing by
\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|B_{z}|}
we get

(4.28)

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)}\upsilon_{y}]}{\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|B_{z}|}}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)}\upsilon_{y}]}{\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|}}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)}\upsilon_{y}]}{\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)|}}=\sqrt{\,\overline{g}-\varepsilon-c_{2}J^{2-d}}\,,

having used that for every
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}

(4.29)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|B_{z}|}{\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|}=1\,,\qquad\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|{A}^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{J,N}(z)|}{\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|}\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|-d\log J}{\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|}=1\,,

where the first limit follows from
d\log L+c\leq\log|A^{\omega,\varepsilon}_{N}(z)|\leq d\log L+C
. Letting
J\to\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
4.28
) we get the claim.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In general, for every stationary and ergodic probability measure
\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
on
\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, on every sequence of sets that grows to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
N\to\infty

for the expectation of the maximum of the Gaussian free field
\varphi
, namely for
\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
-a.e.

we have the following pointwise convergence:

(4.30)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\max_{y\in D_{N}}\varphi_{y}]}{\sqrt{2\overline{g}\log|D_{N}|}}=1\,,

where
D_{N}=(ND)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some open and relatively compact
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The proof is analogous to the one of Proposition
4.3
, using that
\overline{g}=\esssup_{\omega}g^{\omega}(0,0)
.

Remark 4.5
.

In (
4.10
) we define
L(N)
strategically so that, for

4.2.2.
Quenched solidification estimates

In this Section we consider a special family of sets
\mathcal{S}
that satisfy (
3.2
) and have a certain covering property on
V_{N}
, and provide in Corollary
4.7
a solidification result for such sets, namely that the capacity (associated with the random walk) of the union of boxes
B_{z}
for
z\in\mathcal{S}
, is asymptotically bounded from below by
\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V^{\varepsilon}_{N})
, as
N\to\infty
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. We recall that
V^{\varepsilon}_{N}=\{x\in V_{N}:|x-y|>\varepsilon N,\,y\in V_{N}^{c}\}
is the
\varepsilon
-bulk of
V_{N}
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. We consider a length scale
\hat{L}
with the property that
N/\hat{L}\to\infty
,
\hat{L}/L\to\infty
,
and consider the family
{F}
of boxes
F
such that

(4.31)

F=x+[-\hat{L},\hat{L}]^{d}\,\text{ for some }x\in V^{\varepsilon/2}_{N}\cap\hat{L}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

Let
\rho\in[0,1)
be a fixed parameter.
We consider sets
\mathcal{S}
with the following covering property:

(4.32)

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{N}
satisfies
|F\cap\mathcal{S}|\geq(1-\rho)|F\cap\mathcal{C}_{N}|
, for every
F\in{F}
.

For each such
\mathcal{S}
we also define the union of pairwise disjoint boxes

(4.33)

S=\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{S}}B_{z}\,.

We are now ready to state the two following quenched results on the solidification properties of the collection of all interfaces
S
with
\mathcal{S}
satisfying (
4.32
).

Proposition 4.6
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in[0,1)

(4.34)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\sup_{z\in V_{N}^{\varepsilon}}P^{\omega}_{z}[H_{S}=\infty]=0\,.

Proof.

First we note that for
\mathcal{S}
as in (
4.32
), for every box
F\in{F}
with center
x_{F}
, by the heat kernel estimates for the killed random walk in (
2.10
) we have that for all
y
such that
|y-x_{F}|_{\infty}\leq(3/4)\hat{L}

(4.35)

\displaystyle P^{\omega}_{y}[H_{S}\leq T_{F}]

\displaystyle\geq P^{\omega}_{y}[X_{\hat{L}^{2}}\in S\cap F,\hat{L}^{2}\leq T_{F}]=\sum_{z\in S\cap F}q^{\omega}_{\hat{L}^{2},F}(y,z)\,\mu^{\omega}_{z}

\displaystyle\geq c|S\cap F|\hat{L}^{-d}\geq c|\mathcal{S}\cap F|(L/\hat{L})^{d}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:CoveringProperty}}}{{\geq}}c(1-\rho_{N})|\mathcal{C}_{N}\cap F|(L/\hat{L})^{d}\geq c^{\prime}(1-\rho)\,,

having used that
\mu^{\omega}_{z}
is uniformly bounded from below, and where
c^{\prime}\in(0,1)
is independent of the choices of
F\in{F}
,
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
and
y
such that
|y-x_{F}|_{\infty}\leq(3/4)\hat{L}
.

The proof of the proposition is now based on the fact that, in order to exit
V_{N}^{\varepsilon/2}
starting from inside
V_{N}^{\varepsilon}
, the random walk must cross and get at
|\cdot|_{\infty}
-distance
(3/4)\hat{L}
from the centers of at least
k_{N}=c(N/\hat{L})
boxes
F\in{F}
.
As such by a repeated application of the strong Markov property and (
4.35
) we get that for every
z\in V_{N}^{\varepsilon}

(4.36)

P^{\omega}_{z}[H_{S}=\infty]\leq[1-c^{\prime}(1-\rho)]^{k_{N}}\,.

The claim now follows by taking the supremum over all
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{S}
as in (
4.32
), and noting that the right-hand side goes to
0
as
N\to\infty
, since
\rho\in[0,1)
and
k_{N}\to\infty
.
∎

The proof of the following corollary is analogous to the one of (4.8) of Corollary 4.2 in
[
27
]
and uses the result of Proposition
4.6
.

Corollary 4.7
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
\rho\in[0,1)

(4.37)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}}\inf_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(S)}{\mathrm{Cap}^{\omega}(V_{N}^{\varepsilon})}\geq 1\,.

4.2.3.
Proof of the quenched upper bound for the decay of the hard wall event probability

In this section we finish the proof of the upper bound for the asymptotic decay rate, as
N\to\infty
, of the probability of the hard wall event
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}
.
We now sketch the main steps of the proof.
We first define the concept of bad and good boxes, and show in Proposition
4.8
that we can
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. neglect the event on which the set of vertices indexing good boxes does not satisfy the covering property (
4.32
), hence we can assume that it is satisfied for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e. environment. The proof is concluded in Proposition
4.9
, where we note that on the intersection of this event with
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}
, in each good box there is at least one point where the harmonic average of the field is above the threshold
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
. We encode this event into the supremum of some linear combinations, which are the objects we considered in Section
3
(see (
3.7
) and (
3.9
)). Via the results we previously obtained on these variables, we get an estimate for the exact constant for the decay of
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]
in terms of the capacity of the union of good boxes. Lastly, we use the solidification results of Section
4.2.2
and the homogenization result to complete the proof.

We say that a vertex
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is
good
at level
a
(or, equivalently, that
B_{z}
is good at level
a
) if

(4.38)

\min_{y\in B_{z}}\psi_{y}^{\omega,z}\leq-\sqrt{a\log N}

and we say that
z
is
bad
at level
a
(or, equivalently, that
B_{z}
is bad at level
a
) otherwise.
Note that if
z
is good at level
a
and
\varphi
is non-negative on
B_{z}
, then

5.1.
Preliminary estimates for the expectation of the conditioned field

Given

Consider the law of the conditioned field

(5.3)

\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\,\cdot\,]=\mathbb{P}^{\omega}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{W}^{+}_{N}]

and its associated expectation

The following lemma implies that the field of conditioned expectations

,

superharmonic
function. In addition to its use in this section, this will be instrumental also in Section
6
for the proof of Theorem
1.1
, iii). The proof of the lemma exploits (
5.2
) and is a simple adaptation of the one of Lemma 2.3 in
[
33
]
to our setting. We write it in Appendix
B
for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

(5.4)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\mu^{\omega}_{x}}}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}\bigg[\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}{2}m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)^{2}\right)}{\phi(m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)\sqrt{\mu^{\omega}_{x}})}\bigg]\,,&\mbox{ for }x\in V_{N}\,,\\
0\,,&\mbox{ for }x\in V_{N}^{c}\,,\end{cases}

where

is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

As a consequence of Lemma

we have that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
,

(5.5)

This inequality is pivotal in the proofs of both the lower and the upper bounds for (

[
19
]
.

Remark 5.2
.

For every fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
the field of conditioned expectations satisfies

(5.7)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{|x|_{\infty}\geq R}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}]=0\,.

Clearly, it is enough to consider

The proof proceeds analogously to the computations from (3.32) to (3.36) of
[
33
]
, but we put it here for consistency.

Lemma 5.7
.

For every
\delta>0
there exists
c_{11}=c_{11}(\delta)>0
such that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
there exists
N_{0}(\omega,\delta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every

(5.59)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{L}^{\omega}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}](x)|\leq\frac{c_{11}}{N^{2-\delta}}\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
5.1
, we just have to prove the claim for
x\in\partial_{\mathrm{in}}V_{N}
. We define the event
F^{\omega}_{x}=\{\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,:\,m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)\leq 0\}
. Then Lemma
5.1
yields

(5.60)

|\mathcal{L}^{\omega}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}](x)|\leq c\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}\Big[\exp\Big(-\frac{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}{2}m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)^{2}\Big)\Big]+\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}\Big[\frac{\exp(-\mu^{\omega}_{x}m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)^{2}/2)\mathbbm{1}_{F^{\omega}_{x}}}{\phi(m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)\sqrt{\mu^{\omega}_{x}})}\Big]\Big\}\,.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we can show that the first term in the right-hand side is bounded by
c\,N^{-2+\delta}
for
N
large enough and
c
independent of
\omega
. By
\mathrm{e}^{-r^{2}/2}/\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}s\leq 2+r
for
r\geq 0
, and Hölder inequality, the second term is bounded by

(5.61)

\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}[F^{\omega}_{x}]^{1/q}\,\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[(2+\sqrt{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}|m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)|)^{p}]^{1/p}

for every

1/p+1/q=1
.

\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}[F^{\omega}_{x}]

\mathbb{Q}
-a.a.
\omega

(5.62)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}[F^{\omega}_{x}]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\beta>0}\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}\Big[\mathrm{e}^{-\beta(m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)])}\geq\mathrm{e}^{\beta\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\beta>0}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]}\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)}]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]^{2}/2\overline{g}}\,,

having used in the second step Markov inequality and Brascamp-Lieb inequality (
2.34
) with
u(r)=\mathrm{e}^{r}
.
Using now the lower bound on the decay rate for the expectation of the conditioned field in Proposition
5.4
, we get that for every
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
, for
N
large enough

(5.63)

\sup_{x\in\partial_{\mathrm{in}}V_{N}}\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}[F^{\omega}_{x}]\leq c^{\prime}\frac{1}{N^{2(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}}}\,.

The second factor in (
5.60

2.34
) with

(5.64)

\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[(2+\sqrt{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}|m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)|)^{p}]^{1/p}\leq c\Big(1+\sum_{y\,:\,y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[|\varphi_{y}|^{p}]^{1/p}+\sum_{y\,:\,y\sim x}\omega_{x,y}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{y}]\Big)\,.

Since the conductances

independent of

6.
Entropic repulsion: Pathwise characterization

In this section we characterize the pathwise behavior of the conditioned field under
\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}
proving Theorem
1.1
, iii). We end the section with a thorough discussion on the result and about possible extensions.

For the proof of the pathwise entropic repulsion we follow the strategy of the one of Proposition 2.1 in
[
33
]
. For convenience of the reader, in the following we go through the main steps and highlight that the most novel part comes in step 3.

1.

Tightness of the conditioned and centered fields sequence
:

One defines the law of the conditioned and centered field

(6.1)

\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}=\mathbb{P}^{\omega,+}_{N}T_{\psi}^{-1},\qquad\text{with }\,\psi=-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}]\,,

and shows that the sequence
\{\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight, since the
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}
-variance of the field is
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. globally bounded uniformly in
N
:

(6.2)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{N}[\varphi_{x}^{2}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:BrascampLieb}}}{{\leq}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\varphi_{x}^{2}]\leq\overline{g}\,,

\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{N}
being the expectation associated with
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}
.

2.

Any limit point of coincides with
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
:

One first shows that every limit point
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}
of subsequences of
\{\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

(6.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}[\langle f_{n},\varphi\rangle^{2}]=0

for every sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
such that
\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{1}\leq 1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\infty}=0
, where
\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}
is the expectation associated with
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}
. The proof of (
6.3
), which is analogous to the one of
[
33
, Lemma 2.2]
, uses in order Brascamp-Lieb (
2.34
), Hölder and Minkowski inequality to get that for every
p,q\geq 1
such that
1/p+1/q=1

(6.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{N}[\langle f_{n},\varphi\rangle^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{n}(x)g^{\omega}(x,y)f_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{q}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lVert g^{\omega}(x,\cdot)\rVert_{p}|f_{n}(x)|\leq c\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{q}\|f_{n}\rVert_{1}\leq c\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{q}\,,

having used that by (
2.9
) one can choose
p
large enough such that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lVert g^{\omega}(x,\cdot)\rVert_{p}<c
. Then, by the fact that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\infty}=0
one gets by interpolation that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{q}=0
, which yields (
6.3
).

To have that any limit point
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}
in the weak topology of subsequences of
\{\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
coincides with
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
, one first has to check that
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}
satisfies the same equations as
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
in (
5.2
). To show it, note that for
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}
it holds that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}(\mathrm{d}\phi|\mathcal{F}_{\{x\}^{c}})(\varphi)

\displaystyle\propto\exp\bigg\{-\frac{\mu^{\omega}_{x}}{2}[(\phi_{x}-m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]]^{2}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varphi_{x}\geq-\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}]\}}\mathrm{d}\phi_{x}\prod_{y\neq x}\delta_{\varphi_{y}}(\mathrm{d}\phi_{y})\,,\qquad\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}(\mathrm{d}\varphi)\text{-a.s.}

By Theorem
1.1
, ii), we have that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}]=\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s., hence one just has to show that for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s.

(6.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-m^{\omega}_{x}(\varphi)]=\lim_{N\to\infty}(-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}](x))=0\,,

which follows from Lemma
5.7
.

Hence any convergent subsequence of
\{\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has a limit
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}
that satisfies the same equations as
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
in (
5.2
). It is known that for the Gaussian free field every extremal Gibbs state can be written as
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}T_{h}^{-1}=:\mathbb{G}_{h}
for some
\omega
-harmonic
h\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
(see
[
38
, Section 13.2]
). Then by
[
38
, Theorem 7.26]
there exists a measure
\nu
on the space
\mathcal{H}
of
\omega
-harmonic functions such that
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}=\int_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{G}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(h)
. Let
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{G}_{h}}
and
\mathbb{V}\mathrm{ar}^{\mathbb{G}_{h}}
denote the expectation and the variance, respectively, associated with
\mathbb{G}_{h}
.
Now for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and considering the sequence of functions
f_{n}^{x}(y)=\mathbb{P}^{\omega}_{x}[X_{T_{B(x,n)}}=y]
,

,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one has by the

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{G}_{h}}[\langle f_{n}^{x},\varphi\rangle]=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}[\langle f_{n}^{x},h\rangle]=h(x)\,.

Hence by Fatou’s lemma for every

(6.8)

\displaystyle 0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Variance_ScalProd_ConditionedCenteredGFF}}}{{=}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}[\langle f_{n}^{x},\varphi\rangle^{2}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{G}_{h}}[\langle f_{n}^{x},\varphi\rangle^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}\nu(h)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{H}}\Big[\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{V}\mathrm{ar}^{\mathbb{G}_{h}}[\langle f_{n}^{x},\varphi\rangle]+h(x)^{2}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\nu(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}}h(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(h)\,,

where

can be proven by just using the definition of
\mathbb{G}_{h}
and (
6.3
) again.
Then
\nu
is concentrated on the constant function

, and thus
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}=\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
.

3.

By the regularity of the field, the recentering is constant over all vertices
:

Note that the results we have obtained in Section
6
until this point hold for almost every
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
.
If we show that for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(6.9)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-\varphi_{0}]=0\,,

then one can get that on a subset of

of full
\mathbb{Q}
-measure it is possible to substitute the recentering
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}]
by the constant recentering

, which is such that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}}={h}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{V}(0)\,.

One can achieve this by showing the convergence on local functions and then using that the error
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-\varphi_{0}]
vanishes in the limit as

for any

fixed. This proves Theorem
1.1
, iii).
We are left proving (
6.9
). In fact, we can even prove a stronger regularity as stated in the following lemma. We remark that the result below does not assume that the conductances are i.i.d., only that Lemma
5.7
holds (which is true under more mild algebraic mixing conditions on

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that for every
\delta\in(0,1)
we can find a constant
c_{12}=c_{12}(\delta)>0
such that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.a.

there exists

\big|\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{x}-\varphi_{y}]\big|\leq c_{12}\frac{|x-y|_{\infty}^{\gamma}}{N^{\gamma-\delta}}\,,\qquad\forall x,\,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Proof.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Clearly, if
x=y
there is nothing to prove. Let
r>100
and suppose first that
|x-y|_{\infty}<r
. Then, by arguing as in the proof of Proposition
5.6

5.7

is superharmonic while
u^{\omega}_{N}-c_{11}N^{-2+\delta}G^{\omega}_{B(x,2r)}\mathbbm{1}
is subharmonic in
B(x,2r)
for all

large enough depending on
\omega
and
\delta
. Furthermore, both functions are equal to
u_{N}^{\omega}
on
\partial B(x,2r)
.
Therefore, we get

(6.12)

\displaystyle\big|u^{\omega}_{N}(x)-u^{\omega}_{N}(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\sum_{z\in\partial B(x,2r)}P^{\omega}_{x}[X_{T_{B(x,2r)}}=z]u_{N}^{\omega}(z)-\sum_{z\in\partial B(x,2r)}P^{\omega}_{y}[X_{T_{B(x,2r)}}=z]u^{\omega}_{N}(z)\Big|

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{11}}{N^{2-\delta}}\sup_{z\in B(x,r)}G^{\omega}_{B(x,2r)}\mathbbm{1}(z)\,.

On the one hand, we have the estimate
\sup_{B(x,r)}G^{\omega}_{B(x,2r)}\mathbbm{1}\leq cr^{2}
, because of (

we note that the function
f_{N}^{\omega}(y)=\sum_{z\in\partial B(x,2r)}P^{\omega}_{y}[X_{T_{B(x,2r)}}=z]u_{N}^{\omega}(z)
is harmonic in

and also bounded by

for all

large enough by Proposition

. We can then use the Hölder regularity property (

) to estimate the first term in the right-hand side by

(6.13)

c\Big(\frac{|x-y|_{\infty}}{r}\Big)^{\tau}\sqrt{\log N}\,.

This leads to the estimate for all
N
large enough depending on
\omega
and
\delta
, and all

such that

\big|u^{\omega}_{N}(x)-u^{\omega}_{N}(y)\big|\leq c\frac{|x-y|_{\infty}^{\tau}}{r^{\tau}}\sqrt{\log N}+c\frac{r^{2}}{N^{2-\delta}}\,.

Note that (

by choosing a large numerical constant

by Proposition

. Letting

, this leads to the estimate, for all

large enough depending on
\omega
and
\delta
and all

(6.15)

\big|u^{\omega}_{N}(x)-u^{\omega}_{N}(y)\big|\leq c\frac{|x-y|^{\gamma}_{\infty}}{N^{\gamma}}\big(\sqrt{\log N}+N^{\delta}\big)\,,

which concludes the proof being the second factor bounded by
c\,N^{\delta}
for all
N

∎

Remark 6.2
.

For i.i.d. conductances one can derive stronger controls on the regularity of the conditioned expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\omega,+}_{N}[\varphi_{\cdot}]
analogously to the one in Proposition 3.1 of
[

A\in\mathcal{Q}_{U},B\in\mathcal{Q}_{V}\,,

where
\mathcal{Q}_{K}=\sigma(\omega_{x,y}\,:\,x,y\in K\cup\partial K)
,
K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see Appendix
A
for the proof). We do not claim optimality for this set of values for
\alpha
and we do not pursue here a sharp control on the mixing properties.
However, it is reasonable to expect, at least based on our method of proof, that Theorem
1.1
needs some decorrelation assumption on the conductances to quantify ergodicity of the environment. In fact, the repulsion is due to the fact that the maximum of the local fields
\psi_{z}^{\omega}
,
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
at scale
N^{2/d}
is concentrated around
\sqrt{4\overline{g}\log N}
, for a subcollection of points
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{N}
that need to be uniformly spread in
V_{N}
, (see (
4.32
)).

(2)

For general mixing environments, the constant
\overline{g}
is not easily computable. However, when the conductances
\omega_{x,y}
are i.i.d. and such that the essential infimum of their support is
\lambda
, then
\overline{g}
can be evaluated and it holds that

(6.25)

\overline{g}=\frac{g(0,0)}{\lambda}\,,

where
g(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Green function of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, that is, the one defined in (
2.5
) with conductances all equal to one. Interestingly, only the lower bound on the conductances enters in the value of
\overline{g}
. Let us quickly sketch (
6.25
). For given
N>0
and
\delta>0
there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-positive probability that all the conductances in
B(0,N)
belong to
[\lambda,\lambda+\delta]
. As such, with positive
\mathbb{Q}
-probability,

(6.26)

g^{\omega}(0,0)\geq g^{\omega}_{B(0,N)}(0,0)\geq\frac{g_{B(0,N)}(0,0)}{(\lambda+\delta)}\geq\frac{g(0,0)}{(\lambda+\delta)}-\frac{c}{N^{d-2}}\,,

where we used Rayleigh’s monotonicity principle in the second inequality and (
2.9
) in the last one. By definition of essential supremum, it follows that
\overline{g}\geq g(0,0)/(\lambda+\delta)-c/N^{d-2}
for every
\delta>0
and all
N>0
, which yields
\overline{g}\geq g(0,0)/\lambda
. The reverse inequality follows directly from Rayleigh’s monotonicity principle.

Another interesting consequence of (
6.25
) is that it is tempting to say that Theorem
1.1
might hold for possibly unbounded conductances, provided that they are i.i.d., bounded from below by a positive constant
\lambda
, and possibly satisfy some moment conditions. Together with the homogenization results in
[
10
]
, we might expect that the proof of Theorem
1.1
could be adapted to this case. This will be the subject of future work.

Strikingly, in view of (
6.26
) if
\lambda=0
, even keeping
\mathbb{Q}[\omega_{x,y}>0]=1
,
x\sim y
, then, despite the fact that a quenched invariance principle might still hold with a non-degenerate covariance matrix under mild moment conditions on the conductances (see for instance
[
4
,
6
,
5
,
10
,
12
,
17
]
), the maximum of the field and the hard wall repulsion are expected to have a markedly different behavior. We discuss this in the next point for the manifestly non-elliptic case of a Gaussian free field on the supercritical percolation cluster.

(3)

Recently, there has been a growing interest for the study of the Gaussian free field on the supercritical percolation cluster. For example, in
[
7
]
the authors prove a scaling limit for the field on the infinite cluster of supercritical Bernoulli bond percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, and
[
48
]
look at the maximum of the field in dimension
d=2
. In the latter, the authors show that if
p
is sufficiently close to one, then the maximum of the field with zero boundary conditions, properly centered and rescaled, converges to a randomly shifted Gumbel distribution for almost all realizations of the cluster. More precisely, if
V_{N}=[0,N-1]^{2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is the infinite cluster of supercritical Bernoulli bond percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with parameter
p
and law
\mathbb{P}_{p}
, and
\varphi^{\mathcal{C}_{\infty},N}_{\cdot}
is the Gaussian free field with zero boundary conditions on
V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
, then for
p
sufficiently close to one there exists a constant
a_{p}
such that for
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.e. realization of the cluster, the sequence of random variables

(6.27)

\max_{x\in V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\varphi^{\mathcal{C}_{\infty},N}_{x}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi a_{p}}}\Big(\log N-\frac{3}{8}\log\log N\Big)

converges in distribution to a deterministic limit, given by a randomly shifted Gumbel distribution. Notably, the constant
a_{p}
is the effective diffusivity of the Brownian motion to which the random walk on the infinite cluster converges.

When
d\geq 3
, the situation is quite different to dimension
d=2
but also to the case with uniformly elliptic conductances. On the one hand, the correlations still decay algebraically at mesoscopic scales, like in the uniformly elliptic case and one can define an infinite volume field. On the other hand the variances of the field are not uniformly bounded from above. It follows from
[
1
]
that for
d\geq 3
there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. for all
N
large enough

(6.28)

c_{1}\log N\leq\sup_{x\in V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}g^{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(x,x)\leq c_{2}\log N\,,

which leads to the conclusion that the maximum of the field in
V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is of order
\log N
rather than of order
\sqrt{\log N}
(see Corollary 1.7 in
[
1
]
for a slightly different model). This is due to the substantial presence of open tubes of length
\log N
in
V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
. In light of this, it is natural to expect that the entropic repulsion for the field on the infinite cluster is of order
\log N
rather than
\sqrt{\log N}
. More precisely, if we look at the hard wall event
\mathcal{W}^{+}_{\mathcal{C}_{\infty},N}=\{\varphi_{x}\geq 0,\,\forall x\in V_{N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\}
when
d\geq 3
, we expect that for some constants
\hat{c},\overline{c}>0
the following asymptotic behavior hold for any
p>p_{c}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.e. realization of the cluster

(6.29)

e^{-\hat{c}N^{d-2}(\log N)^{2}(1+o(1))}\leq\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}[\mathcal{W}^{+}_{\mathcal{C}_{\infty},N}]\leq e^{-\overline{c}N^{d-2}(\log N)^{2}(1+o(1))}\,.

Proving that
\hat{c}=\overline{c}
, and from there getting a pathwise behavior for the conditioned field appears to be a more challenging task and it is the subject of ongoing investigation. Our method of proof, together with the results in
[
26
]
and the quantitative homogenization estimates in
[
31
]
, seems a good starting point to address the question. However, it is possible that sharp controls on the tails of
g^{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(0,0)
under
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\,\cdot\,|0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}]
are needed, which might be difficult to get in the whole supercritical regime
p\in(p_{c},1]
. Some of this estimates are available for
d=2
in
[
48
]
.

(4)

The article
[
48
]
also investigates the maximum of the Gaussian free field in dimension
d=2
when the conductances are i.i.d. and uniformly bounded away from zero and infinity. The authors prove that the maximum of the field with zero boundary conditions, properly centered and rescaled, converges to a randomly shifted Gumbel distribution. To be more precise, if
V_{N}=[0,N-1]^{2}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
\varphi^{N}_{\cdot}
is the Gaussian free field with random conductances and with zero boundary conditions on
V_{N}
, then the sequence of random variables

(6.30)

\max_{x\in V_{N}}\varphi^{N}_{x}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\overline{a}}}\Big(\log N-\frac{3}{8}\log\log N\Big)

converges in distribution to a deterministic limit, given by a randomly shifted Gumbel distribution. Also here the constant
\overline{a}
is the effective diffusivity of the Brownian motion to which the random walk with i.i.d. uniformly elliptic conductances converges. In other words, the relevant proportionality constant is captured by the homogenized behavior of the random walk. This is in stark contrast with the first order behavior of the maximum in dimension
d\geq 3
(see Remark
4.4
), where the relevant constant
\overline{g}
is not captured by the homogenized behavior of the random walk. In particular, similarly to
[
27
, Remark 3.7]
and unlike what happens in
d=2
, two environments with the same effective diffusivity can have different values of
\overline{g}
and therefore different first order behavior for the maximum of the field and of the entropic repulsion effect.
For example, consider the special case in which the conductances
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}
are i.i.d. under the environment measure
\mathbb{Q}
and such that the infimum of the support of
\omega_{0,x}
,
x\sim 0
, is
\lambda
. It is well-known that the constant-speed random walk on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega)
with diffusive scaling has a
\mathbb{Q}
-a.s. scaling limit given by a Brownian motion with covariance matrix
a^{\rm hom}=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Id}
, where
\mathrm{Id}
is the
(d\times d)
-identity matrix and
\sigma^{2}>0
. By considering constant conductances
\omega(\sigma)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}
on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d})
, it is straightforward to show that the corresponding random walk on the weighted graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{d},\omega(\sigma))
with diffusive scaling has the same scaling limit. However, unless the random conductances under
\mathbb{Q}
are deterministic, one has, (see for instance
[
13
, Section 4]
)

(6.31)

\lambda<\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[\omega_{0,x}^{-1}]}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\omega_{0,x}]<\Lambda\,,

where
x\sim 0
.
Thus, we get that
\overline{g}(\sigma)=2g(0,0)/\sigma^{2}
for the constant conductances, while in view of (
6.25
), for the random conductances
\overline{g}=g(0,0)/\lambda
which is strictly larger than
2g(0,0)/\sigma^{2}
. Phenomenologically, this shows that while the scaling limit of the Gaussian free field is the same for the two environments (cf.
[
7
,
30
]
), the bond disorder leads to a larger value for the maximum and to a stronger entropic repulsion effect.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
4.2
with
\alpha
-mixing assumptions on conductances

Proposition A.1
.

Assume that
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies the
\alpha
-mixing condition (
6.24
) with
\alpha>d(d-1)/2
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
q>0
that for
\mathbb{Q}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{\lambda,\Lambda}

(A.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}\frac{1}{|B_{z}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{U_{z}}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}\geq q\,.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. As for Proposition
4.2
, we fix
R=R(\varepsilon)
such that
g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq g^{\omega}(y,y)-\varepsilon/2
.
For
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
let

(A.2)

q:=\mathbb{Q}[g^{\omega}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon/2]/2\,,\qquad Q:=\mathbb{Q}[g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon]\,,

which are independent of
y
by the stationarity of
\mathbb{Q}
and satisfy
0<2q\leq Q
.

For any
4R<\ell\leq L
denote the box of size
\ell
by
\square_{\ell}=[0,\ell)^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and for
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
we consider the grid
B_{z,x}=x+(\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{z})
. For
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the centered variable
X_{y}=\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}-Q
.
Then

(A.3)

\sum_{y\in B_{z}}X_{y}=\sum_{x\in\square_{\ell}}\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}X_{y}\,.

As for Proposition
4.2
, for
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the events

(A.4)

J^{\omega}_{N}(z)=\Big\{\frac{1}{|B_{z}|}\sum_{y\in B_{z}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{g^{\omega}_{B(y,R)}(y,y)\geq\overline{g}-\varepsilon\}}<q\Big\}\,,

whose
\mathbb{Q}
-probability is independent of
z
. We then have that

(A.5)

\mathbb{Q}[J^{\omega}_{N}(z)]\leq\mathbb{Q}\Big[\sum_{y\in B_{z}}X_{y}<-\tfrac{Q}{2}|B_{z}|\Big]\,.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Denoting by
\mathbb{E}
the expectation associated with
\mathbb{Q}
, by Markov’s and Jensen’s inequalities

(A.6)

\mathbb{Q}\Big[\sum_{y\in B_{z}}X_{y}<-\tfrac{Q}{2}|B_{z}|\Big]\leq\frac{1}{{(QL^{d}/2)^{2k}}}\,\ell^{d(2k-1)}\sum_{x\in\square_{\ell}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}X_{y}\Big)^{2k}\Big]\,.

We now want to estimate

(A.7)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}X_{y}\Big)^{2k}\Big]=\sum_{y_{1},\dots,y_{2k}\in B_{z,x}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{2k}X_{y_{i}}\Big]\,.

We follow an analogous procedure to Section 3.2.4 of
[
8
]
. For
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
and
x\in\square_{\ell}
we define the set

(A.8)

H^{z,x}_{j}=\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{2k})\in B_{z,x}^{2k}\,:\,|\{y_{1},\dots,y_{2k}\}|=j\}

composed of
2k
-tuples of points with exactly
j
different entries,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The set of
j
distinct entries of
y\in H^{z,x}_{j}
is denoted by
y_{\mathrm{dist}}
, and the multiplicity of
v\in y_{\mathrm{dist}}
in
y
is indicated by
q_{y}(v)
.
The
\alpha
-mixing condition (
6.24
) yields that for every
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{2k})\in H^{x,z}_{j}

(A.9)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{2k}X_{y_{i}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{v\in y_{\mathrm{dist}}}X_{v}^{q_{y}(v)}\Big]\leq\prod_{v\in y_{\mathrm{dist}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[X_{v}^{q_{y}(v)}\Big]+4(j-1)\Phi(\ell/2)\,,

where the last step can be proven using induction on the covariance estimate
\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}Y_{2}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}]\mathbb{E}[Y_{2}]\leq 4\Phi(\ell)
, which holds for every pair of
[-1,1]
-valued random variables
Y_{1},Y_{2}
that are measurable with respect to
\mathcal{Q}_{U}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{V}
respectively, with
d_{\infty}(U,V)\geq\ell
(see Lemma A.1 of
[
9
]
). In fact
X_{y}
is
\mathcal{Q}_{B(y,R)}
-measurable, and for
v,w\in B_{z,x}
with
v\neq w
we have
d_{\infty}(B(v,R),B(w,R))\geq\ell-2R\geq\ell/2
.
Then by the arguments on page 90 of
[
8
]
we get

(A.10)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{y\in B_{z,x}}X_{y}\Big)^{2k}\Big]\leq c_{k}\big(|B_{z,x}|^{k}+|B_{z,x}|^{2k}\Phi(\ell/2)\big)\,.

Since for every
z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}
and
x\in\square_{\ell}
we have
c(L/\ell)^{d}\leq|B_{z,x}|\leq c^{\prime}(L/\ell)^{d}
, setting
n=L/\ell
we get by (
A.6
) that

(A.11)

\mathbb{Q}\Big[\sum_{y\in B_{z}}X_{y}\leq-\tfrac{Q}{2}|B_{z}|\Big]\leq c^{\prime}_{k}\big(n^{-dk}+\Phi(\ell/2)\big)\,.

Then (
A.5
) yields

(A.12)

\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Q}\Big[\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{N}}J^{\omega}_{N}(z)\Big]\leq c^{\prime}_{k}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}|\mathcal{C}_{N}|\big(n^{-dk}+\Phi(\ell/2)\big)\,.

Taking
\ell=L^{1-\delta}
for a
\delta\in(0,1)
, by (
4.12
) the above quantity is smaller or equal to

(A.13)

c^{\prime\prime}_{k}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}N^{d}L^{-d(1+k\delta)}+c^{\prime\prime}_{k}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}N^{d}L^{-d-(1-\delta)\alpha}

and by (
4.10
), since

, the second term converges by taking
\delta
small enough, while the first one converges by fixing
k
(depending on
\delta
) large enough.
Finally, by Borel-Cantelli lemma we get the claim (
A.1
).

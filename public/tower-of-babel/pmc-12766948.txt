Tumor-infiltrating neutrophil is an independent prognostic factor in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma
Authors: Seung-Myoung Son, Hye Sook Han, Ho-Chang Lee, Ok-Jun Lee, Chang Gok Woo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies. Tumor-infiltrating neutrophils (TINs) have been implicated in tumor progression and poor prognosis; however, their roles in PDAC remain unclear. TINs have been shown to granulocytic myeloid-derived suppressor cells (G-MDSCs) which inhibit adaptive immune responses and share morphological and functional features with classical neutrophils. G-MDSCs, unlike classical neutrophils, express myeloperoxidase (MPO). We evaluated the significance of TINs and MPO expression as a marker for prognosis in the PDAC.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 128 patients with surgically resected PDAC. Background

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is one of the most lethal malignancies, with a five-year survival rate of approximately 10% [1]. Its aggressive behavior stems from late diagnosis, resistance to therapy, and its tumor microenvironment (TME), which promotes immune evasion and tumor progression [2, 3]. Although neutrophils have been shown to inhibit adaptive immune responses, this function is largely attributed to myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), particularly the granulocytic subset (G-MDSCs), which share morphological and functional features with classical neutrophils [4]. This similarity has led to challenges in distinguishing classical neutrophils from G-MDSCs within TME. A previous study reported that G-MDSCs express myeloperoxidase (MPO) and CXC chemokine receptors (CXCRs) for tumor-derived chemokines [5]. Within the TME, G-MDSCs have emerged as key mediators of immunosuppression. G-MDSCs are a heterogeneous population of immature myeloid cells that expand under pathological conditions including cancer, chronic infections, and autoimmune diseases [6, 7]. They mediate potent immunosuppressive effects primarily by inhibiting T-cell responses and by producing reactive oxygen species (ROS), nitric oxide, and immunosuppressive cytokines. Elevated G-MDSC levels in PDAC are correlated with disease progression and poor prognosis [8]. Tumor-infiltrating neutrophils (TINs) have recently been implicated in promoting immunosuppression and tumor progression [9]. Neutrophil infiltration has been correlated with clinical outcomes in PDAC, implicating neutrophils as critical modulators of the tumor immune microenvironment [3]. Given the phenotypic similarity between classical neutrophils and G-MDSCs, it is crucial to delineate which population contributes to tumor biology within PDAC. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance of TINs and to assess MPO expression within tumor tissue as a discriminative marker for G-MDSCs in the PDAC.

Methods

Case collection

We retrospectively collected the patients with surgically resected PDAC from the pathology archives of Chungbuk National University Hospital (Cheongju, South Korea) between January 2001 and December 2020. Clinical data, including age, sex, smoking history, and survival outcomes, were obtained from the medical records. Pathological characteristics, such as tumor differentiation, T stage (AJCC 8th edition), lymph node metastasis, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), perineural invasion (PNI), and resection margin status, were obtained from the pathology reports. We assessed the correlation between survival and TINs using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumor criteria [10].

Evaluation of tumor-infiltrating neutrophils

TINs were evaluated on hematoxylin and eosin stained slides and classified as absent/low (no or minimal, < 3 neutrophils/HPF) and present/high (mild to severe, ≥ 3 neutrophils/HPF) (Fig. 1A, B). This threshold was established by internal consensus to differentiate background scattered background neutrophils (< 3/HPF) from true stromal infiltration (≥ 3/HPF) in PDAC. For each case, one representative main tumor slide was selected to confirm the consistency of TIN distribution. Each slide contained a well-preserved area of tumor with adjacent stroma, avoiding necrosis or hemorrhage. TINs were evaluated by initially scanning the entire tumor section at low magnification to identify representative regions containing viable tumor and stroma. Quantification was then performed in areas with the most prominent neutrophilic infiltration (‘hot spots’) under ×400 magnification.

Numerous tumor-infiltrating neutrophils (arrow) are interspersed among irregular, atypical glandular structures in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (A). Prominent desmoplastic stroma characterized by dense fibrous tissue (arrow) is observed within the tumor microenvironment (B). (hematoxylin and eosin stain, ×100, scale bar = 100 μm)

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) for MPO was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) blocks of PDAC with TINs. To further evaluate MPO expression in classical neutrophils, MPO staining was conducted on tissue specimens from patients diagnosed as acute cholecystitis or acute appendicitis, which is representative of non-neoplastic inflammatory conditions. FFPE Sect. (4 μm) were stained with a monoclonal mouse anti-MPO antibody (Clone 59A5; Leica, Newcastle, UK) and validated for in vitro diagnostic use. IHC was carried out using the Ventana BenchMark XT® automated staining system (Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA) with an UltraView DAB detection kit (Ventana Medical Systems).

Statistical analysis

Survival analyses were performed using Cox proportional hazards regression models, incorporating relevant clinicopathological variables. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated, and survival differences were compared using the log-rank test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All analyses were performed in R (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) (version 4.5.0).

Results

Overall survival according to clinicopathological characteristics

A total of 128 patients diagnosed with PDAC were enrolled in this study. No significant associations were observed between overall survival and clinical variables, including sex, age, smoking status, and alcohol consumption (Supplementary Table 1). Poorly differentiated histology was significantly associated with patient prognosis (Table 1). In univariate analysis, compared to well-differentiated tumors (reference group), poorly differentiated tumors were associated with an increased risk of death (HR, 3.10; 95% CI, 1.33–7.24; P = 0.009). After adjustment for other variables in multivariate analysis, poor differentiation remained an independent predictor of worse survival (HR, 2.45; 95% CI, 1.03–5.83; P = 0.043). Other factors, including T stage, lymph node status, and LVI, did not show significant associations. PNI was significantly associated with poorer overall survival in univariate analysis. Patients with PNI (n = 92, 71.9%) had a significantly increased risk of death compared to those without PNI (n = 36, 28.1%) (HR, 1.94; 95% CI, 1.01–3.75; P = 0.048). However, in multivariate analysis, the association was not statistically significant (HR, 1.74; 95% CI, 0.89–3.43; P = 0.107). Resection margin status was significantly associated with overall survival. In univariate analysis, patients with positive resection margins had more than twice the risk of death compared to those with negative margins (HR, 2.13; 95% CI, 1.24–3.67; P = 0.006). This association remained statistically significant in the multivariate model (HR, 2.28; 95% CI, 1.31–3.98; P = 0.004), confirming resection margin status as an independent prognostic factor.

Prognostic impact of Tumor-Infiltrating neutrophils in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma

TIN was identified in 61.7% (n = 79) of the patients. The presence of TIN was significantly associated with worse overall survival (HR, 2.48; 95% CI, 1.35–4.49; P = 0.003), and this remained significant in multivariate analysis (HR, 2.51; 95% CI, 1.36–4.64; P = 0.003), confirming TINs as an independent predictor of poor prognosis (Table 1). Patients with TINs exhibited significantly worse recurrence-free survival compared to those without TINs (P < 0.001; Fig. 2A). Similarly, patients with TIN had significantly worse overall survival compared to those without TIN (P = 0.003; Fig. 2B). These results reinforce the independent prognostic value of neutrophil-infiltration within TME of PDAC.

MPO expression in tumor-infiltrating neutrophils

IHC for MPO was performed on 25 of the 79 TIN-positive PDAC samples. All 25 patients (100%) were positive for MPO (Fig. 3; Table 2). In contrast, MPO expression was absent in all 15 cases of acute appendicitis and was only detected in 2 of the 10 acute cholecystitis cases (20%). This difference suggests that MPO-positive TINs in PDAC may represent a distinct subpopulation aligned with G-MDSCs.

Discussion

Recent studies have emphasized the critical role of the TME in the progression of PDAC and immune evasion [11]. TIN status is associated with resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors in the patients with PDAC [12]. TINs have been shown to exert immunosuppressive effects in various cancer types [13]. One mechanism by which tumors evade the host immune response involves the recruitment of G-MDSCs. These cells suppress immune responses by inducing antigen-specific CD8 + T-cell tolerance by generation ROS and peroxynitrite, as well as by inhibiting NK cell cytotoxicity [14, 15]. Emerging evidence suggests that TINs can promote pancreatic cancer progression through the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). NETs facilitate tumor-cell migration, invasion, and immune evasion via activation of the EGFR/ERK signaling pathway [16, 17]. Although NET formation was not directly evaluated in this study, this mechanism provides a plausible biological basis for the observed association between TINs and adverse patient outcomes. Previous studies have reported that high neutrophil infiltration is associated with adverse outcomes in PDAC [12, 18, 19]. Reid et al. demonstrated that significant TINs concentrate in aggressive histologic subtypes such as micropapillary and undifferentiated carcinomas [20]. Ino et al. reported that higher CD66b⁺ neutrophil infiltration associates with shorter survival, whereas T-cell–dominant profiles are favorable [12]. Complementing these observations, Wang et al. identified CD15⁺ neutrophils among immune features linked to reduced survival, with validation in an independent cohort including preoperative FNA specimens [21]. At the transcriptomic level, CD177, a neutrophil-related marker, correlated with poor prognosis and stromal neutrophil accumulation [22]. Our findings are consistent with these observations and further extend them by demonstrating that the infiltrating neutrophils express MPO, suggesting a potential G-MDSC-like phenotype within TME.

MDSCs are phenotypically categorized into monocytic MDSCs and G-MDSCs [23]. A significant overlap exists between G-MDSCs and classical neutrophils in terms of surface and functional markers. In mouse models, neutrophils recruited to PDAC upregulated CXCRs and MPO expression [19]. CXCL5 is a key chemokine that attracts neutrophils to the tumor site and is implicated in the epithelial-mesenchymal transition, tumor cell migration, and metastasis [24, 25]. G-MDSCs express CXCR2, a CXCL5 receptor [26]. In our study, MPO expression was consistently observed in TINs within PDAC tissue, but was infrequent or absent in neutrophils from acute, non-neoplastic inflammatory conditions. Although MPO expression was consistently observed in tumor-infiltrating neutrophils (TINs) within PDAC, we recognize that MPO primarily reflects neutrophil activation rather than direct immunosuppressive function. Nevertheless, previous evidence supports its potential as a surrogate marker of G-MDSC. In particular, Sano et al. demonstrated that mice with global Cxcr2 knockout exhibited a marked reduction in MPO⁺ neutrophil infiltration, suggesting that MPO expression is associated with the CXCL–CXCR2 signaling axis that mediates G-MDSC recruitment [5]. Based on this biological link, MPO-positive TINs in PDAC may represent an activated or G-MDSC-like subpopulation; however, definitive functional and phenotypic validation is required. Additionally, immunophenotyping using markers such as CD66b and ARG1, which could further delineate the lineage and immunologic function of TINs. CD66b is specific for mature neutrophils, whereas ARG1 is a hallmark of immunosuppressive G-MDSCs [27, 28]. Inclusion of these markers would have refined the biological interpretation of TINs as G-MDSCs. Future investigations integrating IHC incorporating CD66b, ARG1, and CXCR2 are warranted to substantiate G-MDSCs in PDAC.

PDAC can be stratified into various molecular and histological subtypes such as squamous, basal-like, and classical PDAC, with additional subsets including aberrantly differentiated endocrine-exocrine, pancreatic progenitor, and immunogenic types [29]. PDAC are also categorized as “loose” and “dense/fibrotic” based on the hardness of the tumor [18, 30]. Microscopically, loose PDAC tends to harbor TINs and exhibit normal stroma, whereas dense/fibrotic tumors exhibit desmoplasia and activated fibrous stroma [30]. Gene set variation analysis has demonstrated that PDAC with TINs are enriched for the “normal stroma” subtype, which is associated with the worst prognoses [18]. In preclinical models, depletion of G-MDSCs delayed tumor progression and metastasis and improved survival [31]. Consistent with these findings, our clinical results demonstrate that TINs are associated with worse PDAC prognoses. Accordingly, neutrophils have also emerged as potential therapeutic targets. In mouse models, inhibition of CXCR2, which targets neutrophils, led to prolonged survival and tumor suppression [5, 19]. Our finding that MPO-positive TINs are associated with poor prognosis further supports the concept that CXCR2-dependent neutrophil activation contributes to PDAC aggressiveness. These results provide a biological rationale for exploring therapeutic strategies targeting CXCR2 signaling or neutrophil-mediated inflammation as potential adjuncts to standard PDAC treatment.

Conclusions

This study provides additional clinical evidence that TINs serve as an independent prognostic biomarker in PDAC and introduces MPO-based immunophenotyping as a potential surrogate marker reflecting G-MDSC-like activation. The characterization of G-MDSCs may provide important insights for treatment stratification in PDAC. Further studies are warranted to clarify whether pharmacological inhibition of G-MDSCs offers therapeutic benefit. This study has several limitations, including its retrospective, single-center design and the absence of external validation using independent datasets. Because our analyses were based on histopathologic assessment of HE slides, comparable data were not available in large public genomic resources such as TCGA or CPTAC. Therefore, external validation could not be performed. Future studies incorporating multi-institutional cohorts and publicly accessible datasets, ideally integrated with digital pathology, are warranted to confirm the prognostic significance of TINs in PDAC. Second, IHC for MPO was performed in a subset of 25 representative TIN-positive cases due to limited archival tissue and resource constraints. Although quantitative analysis such as H-scoring was not conducted, these cases reflected the overall histologic and clinical diversity of the TIN-positive cohort, suggesting reasonable representativeness. Third limitation of this study is the use of a semi-quantitative, visually dichotomized assessment of TINs rather than digital pathology. For the future study, we plan to employ digital–based quantification and automated image analysis tools to validate and refine our current findings. A further limitation is the lack of assessment of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs). Since neutrophils are known to modulate tumor immunity primarily through suppression of T-cell activity, assessing the TIN/TIL ratio or overall immune balance would have provided additional insight into the immunologic context of PDAC. Previous study demonstrated the prognostic significance of the TIL/TIN interplay [12]. Future studies integrating both neutrophilic and lymphocytic infiltration, through multiplex immunohistochemistry or digital image quantification, are warranted to elucidate the full spectrum of immune modulation in PDAC.

In the present study, we demonstrated that TINs serve as an independent adverse prognostic factor for both overall and recurrence-free survival. From a clinical perspective, incorporation of TIN assessment into routine pathology reporting could provide additional prognostic information. In this study, TINs were evaluated semi-quantitatively on HE slides, an approach that is easily applicable in daily practice. However, for clinical implementation, standardization incorporating automated digital quantification will be required to ensure reproducibility across institutions. Moreover, TIN evaluation on preoperative biopsy specimens appears feasible when adequate tumor stroma is present, though its prognostic reliability remains to be validated in prospective studies.

Low-Lying Zeros of L L -functions of Adélic Hilbert Modular Forms and their Convolutions
Alia Hamieh, Peng-Jie Wong

Abstract
. In this article, we study the density conjecture of Katz and Sarnak for L L -functions of adélic Hilbert modular forms and their convolutions. In particular, under the generalised Riemann hypothesis, we establish several instances supporting the conjecture and extending the works of Iwaniec-Luo-Sarnak and many others. For applications, we obtain an upper bound for the average order of L L -functions of Hilbert modular forms at s = 1 2 s=\frac{1}{2} as well as a positive proportion of non-vanishing of certain Rankin-Selberg L L -functions.

1.
Introduction

Since the seminal discovery by Montgomery and Dyson, random matrices have been playing an essential role in helping number theorists understand the behaviour of zeros
of
L
-functions. Profoundly, a conjecture of Katz and Sarnak
[
7
]
, which is often called the density conjecture, predicts that the distribution of “low-lying” zeros of
L
-functions in a family
\mathfrak{F}
is determined by the “symmetry type” of
\mathfrak{F}
. (We refer the reader to
[
9
, Sec. 1.1]
for a precise formulation for the density conjecture.)

Several results tested and supported the conjecture for various families, including
L
-functions of modular forms and their symmetric lifts, under the generalised Riemann hypothesis (GRH). For instance, in their monumental article
[
6
]
, Iwaniec, Luo, and Sarnak studied the above-mentioned density conjecture of Katz and Sarnak for holomorphic modular forms and their symmetric squares. Nowadays, their work has a variety of extensions. For example, the condition on square-free levels was removed recently in
[
1
]
. Also, the cases of Hilbert modular forms (over totally real number fields of narrow class number one) and automorphic forms on totally definite quaternion algebras (including both Maass forms and Hilbert modular forms over general totally real number fields) have been established by Liu-Miller
[
9
]
and Lesesvre
[
8
]
, receptively. Recently, extending the previous work of Ricotta and Royer
[
15
]
on low-lying zeros of symmetric power
L
-functions of holomorphic modular forms, in the level aspect, Sugiyama
[
19
]
further verified the density conjecture, weighted by symmetric square
L
-values, for symmetric power
L
-functions of Hilbert modular forms (under some mild conditions on underlying number fields). More generally, Shin and Templier
[
18
]
proved the density conjecture for a wide family of automorphic representations. In addition, Shashkov
[
16
]
recently proved several results that support the density conjecture for families of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions of holomorphic modular forms over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Recall that the 1-level density of an
L
-function
L(s,f)
, as formalised in
[
5
, Ch. 5]
(see also Section
2.1
below), is defined by

D(f;\phi)=\sum_{\gamma_{f}}\phi\left(\frac{\gamma_{f}}{2\pi}\log c_{f}\right),

where
\phi(x)
is an even Schwartz function, the sum is over the zeros
\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{f}
of
f
, and
c_{f}
stands for the analytic conductor of
f
.
Our first result in this paper generalises one of the theorems of Iwaniec, Luo, and Sarnak
[
6
]
(as well as the above-mentioned result of Liu-Miller
[
9
]
) to holomorphic Hilbert modular forms over
arbitrary
totally real number fields.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field of degree
n
. Let
k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in 2\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}
denote a square-free integral ideal of
F
. We denote by
\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
the set of all primitive forms of weight
k
and level
\mathfrak{n}
. Assume GRH for
\zeta_{F}(s)
,
L(s,{\bf f})
, and
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}))
for all
{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
. Then for any Schwartz class function
\phi(x)
whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
is supported in
(-u,u)
with

u=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})}{\log\left({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)^{2}\right)}+\frac{4}{3}\frac{\log{\rm N}(k)}{\log({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)^{2})},

where
{\rm N}(k)=\prod_{j}k_{j}
,
we have

\frac{1}{|\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})|}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}D({\bf f};\phi)\sim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)W(\mathrm{O})(x)dx,

as
{\rm N}(k){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\infty
,
where
{W}(\mathrm{O})(x)=1+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}(x)
and
\delta_{0}
is the standard Dirac delta functional.

We note that if
\log({\rm N}(k))=o\left(\log({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}))\right)
, the choice
u=\frac{3}{2}
is admissible. This improves both the main theorem of
[
8
]
and the special instance of
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
(with
r=z=1
) in the level aspect. If
{\rm N}(k)\rightarrow\infty
while
{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})
is bounded or fixed, one can only take
u=\frac{2}{3}
. The family considered in Theorem
1.1
is also included in
[
18
]
. However, as remarked in
[
8
]
, the limit of the supports of the Fourier transforms was not provided explicitly in
[
18
]
, and this is in contrast with all the above-mentioned results in which explicit limits of supports are given. One of the main features of our result is to break the
(-1,1)
-barrier. In addition, we remove both the class-number-one condition and the parallel-weight condition in
[
9
]
while keeping
\widehat{\phi}
supported in
(-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2})
for Hilbert modular forms of large level.

Our second result in this paper provides a 1-level density for a family of Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions of Hilbert modular forms over totally real number fields.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field. Let
k,k^{\prime}\in 2\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
denote coprime square-free integral ideals of
F
. Fix
{\bf g}\in\Pi_{k^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
, and assume GRH for
\zeta_{F}(s)
,
L(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})
, and
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))
for all
{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
. If either the class number of
F
is odd or
{\bf g}
is non-dihedral, then for any Schwartz class function
\phi(x)
whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}
is supported in
(-u,u)
with

u=\frac{3}{4}\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})}{\log\left({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)^{2}\right)}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\log{\rm N}(k)}{\log({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)^{2})},

as
{\rm N}(k){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\frac{1}{|\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})|}\sum_{f\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}D(f\times g;\phi)\sim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)W(\mathrm{Sp})(x)dx,

where

W(\mathrm{Sp})(x)=1-\frac{\sin(2\pi x)}{2\pi x}.

Remark 1.3
.

It is worth noting that to establish the above theorem, we require the precise information of the order of the pole of
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))
at
s=1
(denoted by
\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}
) to calculate the averaged asymptotics of
\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}
in Section
7.1
. Although the evaluation of
\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}
is known by experts (at least, implicitly), it is seemingly hard to find a general reference. Therefore, we will devote Section
3.2
to calculating
\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}
and record the precise values of
\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}
in Theorem
3.1
.

As mentioned earlier, Shashkov
[
16
]
recently obtained a version of Theorem
1.2
for Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions of holomorphic modular forms. In fact, denoting
H^{\star}_{k}(N)
the set of holomorphic newforms of weight
k
and level
N
, he showed that

\lim_{NN^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|H^{\star}_{k}(N)||H^{\star}_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime})|}\sum_{f\in H^{\star}_{k}(N)}\sum_{g\in H^{\star}_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime})}D(f\times g;\phi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)W(\mathrm{Sp})(x)dx,

with
\widehat{\phi}
supported in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. In addition, the support can be improved to
(-\frac{5}{4},\frac{5}{4})
if
N=N^{\prime}
but
k\neq k^{\prime}
, and be taken as
(-\frac{29}{28},\frac{29}{28})
if
N=N^{\prime}
and
k=k^{\prime}
. Compared to Theorem
1.2
, Shashkov considered a double-averaging. It may be possible to extend Shashkov’s argument to convolutions of Hilbert modular forms (at least, over totally real number fields of class number one). However, we did not pursue this here as it does not lead to better support for the general case
\mathfrak{n}\neq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.

It is well-known that if one could prove the density conjecture for test functions with sufficiently large support, then one would establish a positive proportion of non-vanishing for the corresponding family of
L
-functions. Unfortunately, for the family of Hilbert modular forms, one would need to extend the support beyond
(-2,2)
to get a positive proportion.

(-2,2)

.

Theorem 1.4
.

, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define

(1.1)

\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathfrak{n})=\frac{1}{|\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})|}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}\delta({\bf f};m),\quad\text{with}\quad\delta({\bf f};m)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if ${\rm ord}_{s=\frac{1}{2}}L(s,{\bf f})=m$};\\

Then under GRH, we have

(1.2)

\limsup_{{\rm N}(k){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathfrak{n})\leq\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{2}.

(ii) In the notation of Theorem

(1.3)

\mathcal{Q}_{m}(\mathfrak{n})=\frac{1}{|\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})|}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}\delta({\bf f}\times{\bf g};m)\quad\text{with}\quad\delta({\bf f}\times{\bf g};m)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if ${\rm ord}_{s=\frac{1}{2}}L(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})=m$};\\

\liminf_{{\rm N}(k){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mathfrak{n})\geq\frac{5}{4}-\frac{1}{2u}.

2.
A general explicit formula

In this section, we shall consider a formalism of
L
-functions over a number field
F
in much the same spirit as
[
5
, Ch. 5]
, and derive a general explicit formula for such
L
-functions.

2.1.
An analytic formalism

Let
F
be a number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We denote its ring of integers by
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. For any given symbol
f
and a complex variable
s
,
L(s,f)
is said to be an
L
-function of degree
d
over
F
if it satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) below.

(i) The function
L(s,f)
admits a Dirichlet series with an Euler product of degree
d\geq 1
, i.e.,

L(s,f)=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\neq 0}\lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})^{-s}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1},

which are absolutely convergent on
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
. Here, the sum is over non-zero integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and the product is over prime ideals of
F
. In addition,
\lambda_{f}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=1
,
\lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n})\in\mathbb{C}
,
\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathbb{C}
, and
|\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})|<{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})
. Moreover, there is an integral ideal
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{F}
associated with
f
, called the conductor of
L(s,f)
, such that
\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathcal{C}_{f}
and
1\leq i\leq d
. We will call primes
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathcal{C}_{f}
unramified.

(ii) Consider

\gamma(s,f)=\pi^{{-dns}/{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{dn}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\kappa_{j}}{2}\right),

where
\kappa_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
are either real or appear in conjugate pairs, and
{\mathfrak{Re}}(\kappa_{j})>-1
. There is an integer
A_{f}\geq 1
, associated with
f
, such that the complete
L
-function of
f
defined by

\Lambda(s,f)=A_{f}^{s/2}\gamma(s,f)L(s,f),

is a holomorphic function on
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
. In addition,
\Lambda(s,f)
extends to a meromorphic function over
\mathbb{C}
of order
1
, with at most two poles at
s=0
and
s=1
. Also, it satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,f)=W(f)\Lambda(1-s,\bar{f}),

where
\bar{f}
is the dual symbol of
f
for which
\lambda_{\bar{f}}(\mathfrak{n})=\overline{\lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n})}
,
\gamma(s,\bar{f})=\gamma(s,f)
,
\mathcal{C}_{\bar{f}}=\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
A_{\bar{f}}=A_{f}
, and
W(f)
is a complex number of absolute value
1
.

The von-Mangoldt function
\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n})
for
L(s,f)
is defined by

-\frac{L^{\prime}(s,f)}{L(s,f)}=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\neq 0}\frac{\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})^{s}}.

Note that
\Lambda_{f}
is only supported on prime powers, and for
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{p}^{m}
, we have

\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=\log({\rm N}(\mathfrak{p}))\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})^{m}.

Throughout our discussion, we will further assume the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture (denoted RPC) for
L(s,f)
as follows. For all
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
|\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})|=1
; otherwise,
|\alpha_{i}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq 1
. Moreover, we shall assume that the asymptotic formula

\sum_{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\leq x}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=\delta_{f}x+O(x^{1/2}\log^{2}(c_{f}x))

holds for some constants
\delta_{f}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c_{f}>0
associated with
f
.

2.2.
Explicit formula

In this section, we shall further assume that
f
is self-dual (i.e.
\bar{f}=f
).
Let
h
be an even Schwartz function with Fourier transform
\widehat{h}
. By following the proofs of
[
5
, Theorems 5.11 and 5.12]
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}{h}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}(0)\log A_{f}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+it,f){h}\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\;dt-2\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\neq 0}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{n})\frac{\widehat{h}(\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}))}{\sqrt{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})}},

where the sum is over all zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,f)
such that
\beta\in[0,1]
(including the non-trivial and the trivial ones) counted with multiplicity.

Let
R
be a sufficiently large positive parameter. Observing that the last sum can be written as

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\frac{\widehat{h}(m\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p}))}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{m}{2}}},

and applying (
2.1
) with
h(x)=\phi(x\log R)
and
\widehat{h}(y)=\frac{1}{\log R}\widehat{\phi}(\frac{y}{\log R})
for an even Schwartz function
\phi
, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\rho}{\phi}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log R\right)\\
&=\widehat{\phi}(0)\frac{\log A_{f}}{\log R}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+it){\phi}\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\log R\right)\;dt-\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(m\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{m}{2}}}.\end{split}

Since

\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(s,f)=\frac{-dn}{2}\log\pi+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{dn}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{s+\kappa_{j}}{2}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+it){\phi}\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\log R\right)\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\log R}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+i2\pi\frac{t}{\log R})\phi(t)\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-dn\log\pi}{\log R}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(t)\;dt+\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{j=1}^{dn}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1+2\kappa_{j}}{4}+i\pi\frac{t}{\log R}\right)\phi(t)\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-dn\log\pi}{\log R}\widehat{\phi}(0)+\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{j=1}^{dn}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1+2\kappa_{j}}{4}+i\pi\frac{t}{\log R}\right)+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1+2\kappa_{j}}{4}-i\pi\frac{t}{\log R}\right)\right)\phi(t)\;dt.

Using the following properties of the digamma function

\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(a+bi)+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(a-bi)=2\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(a)+O(a^{-2}b^{2}),\quad\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R},\;a>0,

and

\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\alpha+\frac{1}{4})=\log\alpha+O(1),\quad\forall\alpha\geq\frac{1}{4},

we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+it){\phi}\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\log R\right)\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-dn\log\pi}{\log R}\widehat{\phi}(0)+\frac{1}{\log R}\sum_{j=1}^{dn}\widehat{\phi}(0)\log\kappa_{j}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log R}\right).

Plugging (
2.3
) in (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}{\phi}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log R\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{\phi}(0)}{\log R}\Bigg(-dn\log\pi+\log A_{f}+\log\prod_{j=1}^{{dn}}\kappa_{j}\Bigg)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(m\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{m}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log R}\right).

We shall now consider the double sum appearing in the above expression. Observing that RPC yields

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{m\geq 3}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\widehat{\phi}\left(m\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right){\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-\frac{m}{2}}=O(1)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(m\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{m}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(2\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log R}\right).

Note that it follows from the assumption

\sum_{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\leq x}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=\delta_{f}x+O(x^{1/2}\log^{2}(c_{f}x))

and Abel’s summation formula that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(2\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\delta_{f}}{\log R}\int_{1}^{\infty}\widehat{\phi}\left(2\frac{\log t}{\log R}\right)\frac{dt}{t}+\frac{2}{\log R}\int_{1}^{\infty}\widehat{\phi}\left(2\frac{\log t}{\log R}\right)\frac{dO(t^{1/2}\log^{2}(c_{f}t))}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{f}}{2}\phi(0)+O\left(\frac{\log\log c_{f}}{\log R}\right).

Hence, we arrive at the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
L(s,f)
be an
L
-function satisfying the previous formalism and

\sum_{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\leq x}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=\delta_{f}x+O(x^{1/2}\log^{2}(c_{f}x)).

Let
\phi
be an even Schwartz function and set

D(f;\phi)=\sum_{\rho}{\phi}(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log R),

where the sum is over all zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,f)
such that
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. If
f
is self-dual, then

\displaystyle D(f;\phi)=\frac{\widehat{\phi}(0)}{\log R}\log\Bigg(\pi^{-dn}A_{f}\prod_{j=1}^{{dn}}\kappa_{j}\Bigg)-\frac{\delta_{f}}{2}\phi(0)-\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\Lambda_{f}(\mathfrak{p})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{\log\log\left(c_{f}\right)}{\log R}\right).

3.
Rankin-Selberg convolutions

3.1.
Generality

In this section, we let
F
be a general number field. We begin by briefly recalling some facts regarding
L
-functions arising from automorphic representations and their Rankin-Selberg convolutions. For a more detailed discussion on the theory of
L
-functions used in this section, we refer the reader to
[
13
, Sec. 2]
and the references therein.

Given an (isobaric) automorphic representation
\pi
for
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the associated
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
is defined by

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{\pi,i}(\mathfrak{p}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1},

which is absolutely convergent on
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
. In addition, the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function of two (isobaric) automorphic representation
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
for
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
{\rm GL}_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, respectively, is defined by

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}),

for
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
, where

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{\pi,i}(\mathfrak{p})\alpha_{\pi^{\prime},j}(\mathfrak{p}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1}

if
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified primes (for both
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
). Moreover, the
L
-functions of the exterior and symmetric squares of
\pi
, denoted by
\wedge^{2}(\pi)
and
{\rm Sym}^{2}(\pi)
respectively, satisfy

3.2.

As can be seen in the previous section, in order to use Proposition

We start by recalling some important properties of

(i)

If

(ii)

By

{\rm Ad}({\bf f})\simeq{\rm Ad}({\bf g})

{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}

(iii)

When both

and
{\bf g}

proved the following cuspidality criterion:

{\bf f}\times{\bf g}
is cuspidal if and only if
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
cannot be induced from the same quadratic extension of
F
.

We also recall that if
\pi

\nu
of

characterises the decomposition of

as follows.

(
\mathcal{P}
) If

(3.2)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)\simeq\chi\boxplus\nu/\nu^{\tau}\boxplus(\nu/\nu^{\tau})\chi,

where

(

does not have property

(3.3)

{\rm Ad}(\pi)\simeq\chi\boxplus I_{K}^{F}(\nu/\nu^{\tau}),

where

is cuspidal.

We shall prove the following theorem regarding the precise values of

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F
be a number field, and let
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
be cuspidal automorphic representations for

with trivial central character. Then we have

(3.4)

\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf f}}=\begin{cases}4&\text{if ${\bf f}$ is dihedral and has property $\mathcal{P}$};\\
3&\text{if ${\bf f}$ is dihedral but does not have property $\mathcal{P}$};\\
2&\text{if ${\bf f}$ is non-dihedral}.\end{cases}

Assume that both
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
are dihedral and can be induced from the same quadratic extension of
F
. If
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
are twist-equivalent, then

(3.5)

\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}=\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf f}}=\begin{cases}4&\text{if ${\bf f}$ has property $\mathcal{P}$};\\
3&\text{if ${\bf f}$ does not have property $\mathcal{P}$};\end{cases}

if
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
are not twist-equivalent, then

(3.6)

\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if both of ${\bf f}$ and ${\bf g}$ have property $\mathcal{P}$};\\
2&\text{if exactly one of ${\bf f}$ and ${\bf g}$ have property $\mathcal{P}$};\\
2&\text{if both ${\bf f}$ and ${\bf g}$ do not have property $\mathcal{P}$}.\end{cases}

If
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}
are dihedral representations that cannot be induced from the same quadratic extension of
F

.

If exactly one of
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}

\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}=1
. If none of
{\bf f}
and
{\bf g}

(3.7)

\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if ${\bf f}$ and ${\bf g}$ are twist-equivalent};\\

Proof.

Recall that

L(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g}\times{\bf f}\times{\bf g})=L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))L(s,\wedge^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))

We start with noting that from (ii), it follows that

and

and

This proves the last case of (

). I

Secondly, if exactly one of

and

Now, it remains to consider the case that both

4.
Preliminaries on Hilbert modular forms

4.1.
Number field setup

Let
F
be a totally real number field
F
with
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=n
, and ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{d}_{F}
and
d_{F}
the different ideal of
F
and its norm over
\mathbb{Q}
respectively.

We let
\sigma_{j}:x\mapsto x_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
be the real embeddings of
F
.
We identify an element
x\in F
with the
n
-tuple
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
x_{j}=\sigma_{j}(x)
. A field element
x
is said to be totally positive (written as
x\gg 0
) if
x_{j}>0
for all
j
. For any subset
X\subset F
, we denote by
X^{+}
the set of totally positive elements in
X
.

To simplify the exposition of this paper, we shall frequently use the following multi-index notation. For a given
n
-tuple
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, we set

{\rm N}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Tr}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}.

Notice that if
x
represents an element of the field
F
, the notation above agrees with the usual definitions of the field norm
{\rm N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\prod_{j}\sigma_{j}(x)
and trace
{\rm Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(x)=\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}(x)
.
We also note that certain products of the gamma function and the
J
-Bessel function are represented as follows: For given
n
-tuples
x
and
k
, and for a scalar
a
, we have

\Gamma(x+a)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(x_{j}+a)\quad\text{and}\quad J_{k-1}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}J_{k_{j}-1}(x_{j}).

The narrow class group of
F
is denoted by
Cl^{+}(F)
and its cardinality by
h^{+}=h^{+}_{F}
. We fix a choice
\{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\mathfrak{a}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{h^{+}}\}
of representatives of the narrow ideal classes in
Cl^{+}(F)
. We write
\mathfrak{a}\sim\mathfrak{b}
when fractional ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
belong to the same narrow ideal class, in which case we have
\mathfrak{a}=\xi\mathfrak{b}
for some
\xi
in
F^{+}
. The symbol
[\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}]
is used to refer to this element
\xi
which is unique up to multiplication by totally positive units in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the ring of adèles of
F
, and let
F_{v}
be the completion of
F
at a place
v
of
F
. For a non-archimedean place
v
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
the local ring of integers. Furthermore, we let
F_{\infty}=\prod_{v|\infty}F_{v}
, where the product is taken over all archimedean places of
F
. In what follows, we make the identifications

F_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})={\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})^{n},\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm SO}_{2}(F_{\infty})={\rm SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{n}.

In particular, each
r\in{\rm SO}_{2}(F_{\infty})
can be expressed as

r({\theta})=\left(r(\theta_{1}),\dots,r(\theta_{n})\right)=\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{j}&\sin\theta_{j}\\
-\sin\theta_{j}&\cos\theta_{j}\end{array}\right]\right)_{j=1}^{n}.

For an ideal
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
and a non-archimedean place
v
in
F
, we define the subgroup
K_{v}(\mathfrak{n})
of
{\rm GL}_{2}(F_{v})
as

K_{v}(\mathfrak{n})=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{v}):c\in\mathfrak{n}\mathcal{O}_{v}\right\},

and put

K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})=\prod_{v<\infty}K_{v}(\mathfrak{n}).

4.2.
Hilbert cusp forms

Let
k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in 2\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and let
\mathfrak{n}
be an integral ideal in
F
. An (adèlic) Hilbert cusp form of weight
k
, level
\mathfrak{n}
, and with the trivial character is a function
{\bf f}:{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
that satisfies the following conditions (
[
20
, Definition 3.1]
):

(1)

The identity
{\bf f}(\gamma xgr({\theta})u)={\bf f}(g)\exp(ik{\theta})
holds for all
\gamma\in{\rm GL}_{2}(F)
,
x\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
,
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
,
r(\theta)\in{\rm SO}_{2}(F_{\infty})
, and
u\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
.

(2)

Viewed as a smooth function on
{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})
,
{\bf f}
is an eigenfunction of the Casimir element
\Delta:=(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n})
with eigenvalue
\displaystyle{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{k_{j}}{2}\left(1-\frac{k_{j}}{2}\right)}
.

(3)

We have the cuspidality condition:
\displaystyle{\int_{F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}}{\bf f}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&x\\
0&1\end{array}\right]g\right)dx=0}
for all
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

The space of adélic Hilbert cusp forms of weight
k
, level
\mathfrak{n}
, and with the trivial character is denoted by
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})

Next, we introduce some notation which is needed to define the Fourier coefficients of a Hilbert cusp form
{\bf f}
. By the Iwasawa decomposition, any element
g
in
{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})
can be uniquely expressed as

g=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{z}&0\\
0&{z}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&{x}\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{y}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]r({\theta}),

with
{z},{y}\in F_{\infty}^{+}
,
{x}\in F_{\infty}
, and
r({\theta})\in{\rm SO}_{2}(F_{\infty})
. Using this decomposition, we define
W_{\infty}^{0}(g)
by

W_{\infty}^{0}(g)={y}^{\frac{k}{2}}\exp\left(2\pi i({x}+i{y})\right)\exp\left(ik{\theta}\right).

In fact, the function
W_{\infty}^{0}
is the new vector in the Whittaker model of the discrete series representation
\bigotimes_{j}\mathcal{D}(k_{j}-1)
of
{\rm GL}_{2}(F_{\infty})
(restricted to
{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})
).

For
{\bf f}\in S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
,
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})
, and
\mathfrak{a}\in I(F)
, we have the Fourier expansion

{\bf f}\left(g\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{id}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1})&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\right)=\sum_{\nu\in(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})^{+}}\frac{C(\nu,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1},{\bf f})}{{\rm N}(\nu\mathfrak{a})^{\frac{1}{2}}}W_{\infty}^{0}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{\nu}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]g\right),

where
\mathrm{id}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1})
is the idele of
F
associated with the ideal
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1}
.
The Fourier coefficient of
{\bf f}
at any integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is then defined as
C_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{m})=C(\nu,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1},{\bf f})
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is a unique choice of representative in
\{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{h^{+}}\}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\sim\mathfrak{a}
, and
\nu=[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}]
. Notice that
\nu
is necessarily an element in
(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})^{+}
, unique up to multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times+}
. We say
{\bf f}
is normalized if
C_{{\bf f}}(\mathcal{O}_{F})=1
.

As it is well-known, an adélic Hilbert cusp form
{\bf f}
in
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
can be realized as an
h
-tuple
(f_{1},\dots,f_{h^{+}})
of classical Hilbert cusp forms
f_{\nu}
of weight
k
with respect to the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{\nu}(\mathfrak{n})=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\in{\rm GL}_{2}^{+}(F)\,:\,a,d\in\mathcal{O}_{F},b\in\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1},c\in\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{F},ad-bc\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times+}\right\},

where
\mathfrak{d}_{F}
is the different ideal of
F
. For more details on this realization, the reader is referred to Garrett
[
3
, Ch. 1-2]
, Raghuram-Tanabe
[
12
, Sec. 4]
, Shimura
[
17
, Sec. 2]
, and Trotabas
[
20
, Sec. 3]
.

The space of adélic Hilbert cusp forms can be decomposed as
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})=S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(\mathfrak{n})\oplus S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(\mathfrak{n})
where
S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(\mathfrak{n})
is the subspace of cusp forms that come from lower levels, and the new space
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(\mathfrak{n})
is the orthogonal complement of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(\mathfrak{n})
in
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
with respect to the Petersson inner product defined as

\left<{\bf f},{\bf g}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{h^{+}}\left<f_{\nu},g_{\nu}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{h^{+}}\int_{\Gamma_{\nu}(\mathfrak{n})\backslash\mathfrak{h}^{n}}\overline{f_{\nu}(z)}g_{\nu}(z)y^{k}d\mu(z),

where
\mathfrak{h}
is the upper half-plane,
y^{k}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{k_{j}}
and
d\mu(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{-2}dx_{j}dy_{j}
with
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
.

The space
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
has an action of Hecke operators
\{T_{\mathfrak{m}}\}_{\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}
much like the classical setting over
\mathbb{Q}
. A Hilbert cusp form
{\bf f}
in

is said to be primitive if it is a normalized common Hecke eigenfunction in the new space. We denote by
\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
the (finite) set of all primitive forms of weight
k
and level
\mathfrak{n}
. If
{\bf f}
is in
\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
, it follows from the work of Shimura
[
17
]
that
C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{m})
is equal to the Hecke eigenvalue for the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
for all
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Moreover, since
{\bf f}
is with the trivial character, the coefficients
C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{m})
are known to be real for all
\mathfrak{m}
. In addition, as shown in
[
17
, Eq. (2.23)]
, for any primitive form
{\bf f}
, one has

(4.1)

C_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{m})C_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{n})=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathfrak{a}}C_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{a}^{-2}\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}).

The Ramanujan bound for Hilbert modular forms (proven by Blasius
[
2
]
) asserts that for all
\epsilon>0
, we have

(4.2)

C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{m})\ll_{\epsilon}{\rm N}(\mathfrak{m})^{\epsilon}.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be square-free. By
[
17
, Proposition 2.3]
, writing
\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}
, for any integral ideal
\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}
and
f\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})
, there is
{\bf f}|_{\ell}\in S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
so that
C_{{\bf f}|_{\ell}}(\ell\mathfrak{a})={\rm N}(\ell)^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a})
. Moreover, we have the orthogonal decomposition

(4.3)

S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\bigoplus_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}S_{k}(\mathfrak{l},{\bf f}),

where
S_{k}(\mathfrak{l},{\bf f})
is the space spanned by
{\bf f}|_{\ell}
with
\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}
. Since
\{{\bf f}|_{\ell}:\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}\}
is linearly independent, we can construct an orthogonal basis
\{{\bf f}_{\ell}:\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}\}
from
\{{\bf f}|_{\ell}:\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}\}
of
S_{k}(\mathfrak{l},{\bf f})
so that
\left<{\bf f}_{\ell},{\bf f}_{\ell}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}=\left<{\bf f},{\bf f}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}
. In fact,
f_{\ell}
is given by

\displaystyle{\bf f}_{\ell}(g)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N(\ell)}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\ell)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}=\ell}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{c})\mu(\mathfrak{c})}{\nu(\mathfrak{c})}N(\mathfrak{d})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf f}\left(g\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{id}(\mathfrak{d})^{-1}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\right)

where
\rho_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{c})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{c}}(1-N(\mathfrak{p})(\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{p})}{1+N(\mathfrak{p})})^{2})
. This construction
[
20
, Eq. (11.1)]
generalises

[
6
, Eq. (2.45)]
to the setting of Hilbert modular forms over any totally real number field.

4.3.
Petersson trace formula

The Petersson trace formula for Hilbert modular forms is an important ingredient in the proof of our main result. In this subsection we describe a version of this theorem that is due to Trotabas (see
[
20
, Theorem 5.5 and Proposition 6.3]
). For conveniece, we shall first recall the definition and some properties of the
J
-Bessel function and the Kloosterman sum which appear in the statement of this theorem. The
J
-Bessel function is defined via the Mellin-Barnes integral representation as

J_{u}(x)=\int_{(\sigma)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{u-s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{u+s}{2}+1\right)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{s}ds

for
0<\sigma<{\mathfrak{Re}}(u)
.
It is known that for
k\geq 2
and
x>0
, we have
J_{k-1}(x)\ll\mathrm{min}(1,\frac{x}{k})k^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.In particular, if
0\leq\delta\leq 1
we have

(4.6)

J_{u}(x)\ll\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)^{1-\delta}k^{-\frac{1}{3}}\quad\quad\text{for }0\leq\delta<1.

As for the Kloosterman sum, it is defined as follows. Given two fractional ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
, let
\mathfrak{c}
be an ideal such that
\mathfrak{c}^{2}\sim\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}
. For
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
,
\xi\in\mathfrak{b}^{-1}
and
c\in\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{q}
, the Kloosterman sum
\mathrm{Kl}(\nu,\mathfrak{a};\xi,\mathfrak{b};c,\mathfrak{c})
is given by

\mathrm{Kl}(\nu,\mathfrak{a};\xi,\mathfrak{b};c,\mathfrak{c})=\sum_{x\in\left(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{c}^{-1}/\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{-1}c\right)^{\times}}\exp\left(2\pi i{\rm Tr}\left(\frac{\nu x+\xi\left[\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}^{-2}\right]\overline{x}}{c}\right)\right).

Here,
\overline{x}
is the unique element in
\left(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{F}\mathfrak{c}/\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{F}c\mathfrak{c}^{2}\right)^{\times}
such that
x\overline{x}\equiv 1\mod c\mathfrak{c}
. The reader is referred to
[
20
, Sec. 2.2 and 6]
for more details on this construction. The Kloosterman sum satisfies the following Weil bound:

(4.7)

|\mathrm{Kl}(\alpha,\mathfrak{n};\beta,\mathfrak{m};c,\mathfrak{c})|\ll_{F}{\rm N}\left(((\alpha)\mathfrak{n},(\beta)\mathfrak{m},(c)\mathfrak{c})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau((c)\mathfrak{c}){\rm N}(c\mathfrak{c})^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{c})
is the g.c.d of the ideals
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{c}
, and
\tau(\mathfrak{n})=|\{\mathfrak{d}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}:\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}\}|
for any integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}
. Another useful fact is the well-known estimate: for all
\epsilon>0
, we have

\tau(\mathfrak{n})\ll_{\epsilon}{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})^{\epsilon}.

Proposition 4.1
.

For an integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}
in
F
and
k\in 2\mathbb{N}^{n}
, let
H_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
be an orthogonal basis for the space
S_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
. Let
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be fractional ideals in
F
. For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{b}^{-1}
, we set

(4.8)

\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\alpha\mathfrak{a},\beta\mathfrak{b})=\sum_{{\bf f}\in H_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{{\rm Tr}(k-1)}|d_{F}|^{1/2}\left<{\bf f},{\bf f}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}}C_{{\bf f}}(\alpha\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}}(\beta\mathfrak{b}).

Then we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\alpha\mathfrak{a},\beta\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=1\!\!1_{\alpha\mathfrak{a}=\beta\mathfrak{b}}+\mathcal{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}^{2}\sim\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\\
c\in\mathfrak{c}^{-1}\mathfrak{n}\backslash\{0\}\\
\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times+}/\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times 2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{Kl}(\epsilon\alpha,\mathfrak{a};\beta,\mathfrak{b};c,\mathfrak{c})}{{\rm N}(c\mathfrak{c})}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{{\epsilon}{\alpha}{\beta}{\left[\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}^{-2}\right]}}}{|{c}|}\right),

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}=\frac{(-1)^{{\rm Tr}(k/2)}(2\pi)^{n}}{2|d_{F}|^{1/2}}}
.

We remind the reader that in the proposition above, we have

\Gamma(k-1)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(k_{j}-1)

and

J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{{\epsilon}{\alpha}{\beta}{\left[\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}^{-2}\right]}}}{|{c}|}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}J_{k_{j}-1}\left(\sigma_{j}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{{\epsilon}{\alpha}{\beta}{\left[\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}^{-2}\right]}}}{|{c}|}\right)\right).

4.4.
Explicit formula for
L
-functions of Hilbert cusp forms

For
{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}
, we define
L(s,{\bf f})
as the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series

L(s,{\bf f})=\sum_{\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{n})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})^{s}}

for
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
. Here, the sum is over non-zero integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. It is known that
L(s,{\bf f})
admits an analytic continuation to the entire complex plane. In the notation of Section
2.1
, we note that
L(s,{\bf f})
is an
L
-function satisfying the above formalism with

\gamma(s,{\bf f})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\pi^{-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{k_{j}-1}{2}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{k_{j}+1}{2}}{2}\right)

and
\Lambda(s,{\bf f})=({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{d}_{F}))^{\frac{s}{2}}\gamma(s,{\bf f})L(s,{\bf f})
, where
\mathfrak{d}_{F}
is the different ideal of
F
. In addition, we have
the functional equation
\Lambda(s,{\bf f})=\epsilon({\bf f})\Lambda(1-s,{\bf f})
. Notice that here we used the fact that
{\bf f}
is self-dual (which follows from the fact that
{\bf f}
is with trivial character).

Assuming GRH for both
L(s,\wedge^{2}({\bf f}))=\zeta_{F}(s)
and
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}))
, we have

\sum_{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\leq x}\Lambda_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{p}^{2})=-x+O(x^{1/2}\log^{2}({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)x)).

Hence, applying Proposition
2.1
, we arrive at the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
, assume GRH for both
\zeta_{F}(s)
and
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}))
. Let
D({\bf f};\phi)=\sum_{\rho}{\phi}(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log R)
. Then

\displaystyle D({\bf f};\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{\phi}(0)}{\log R}\log({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\phi(0)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{n}}C_{\bf f}(\mathfrak{p})\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{\log\log\left({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k)\right)}{\log R}\right).

4.5.
Explicit formula for Rankin-Selberg convolutions of Hilbert cusp forms

In this section, we shall restrict our attention to Rankin-Selberg convolutions of Hilbert modular forms and to apply Proposition

2.1
to derive an explicit formula that will be used later.

Let
{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}
and
{{\bf g}\in\Pi_{k^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})}
be primitive forms, where we assume that
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
are coprime integral ideals.
The
L
-function for the associated Rankin-Selberg convolution is defined as

(4.9)

L(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})=\zeta_{F}^{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}(2s)\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{m})C_{{\bf g}}(\mathfrak{m})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{m})^{s}},

where

\zeta_{F}^{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}(2s)=\zeta_{F}(2s)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{l}|\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\\
\mathfrak{l}\,\mathrm{prime}\end{subarray}}(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{l})^{-2s}).

The series (
4.9
) is absolutely convergent for
{\mathfrak{Re}}(s)>1
. Let

\Lambda(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})={\rm N}(\mathfrak{d}_{F}^{2}\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})^{s}L_{\infty}(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})L(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g}),

where

\displaystyle L_{\infty}({\bf f}\times{\bf g},s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(2\pi)^{-2s-\mathrm{max}\{k_{j},k^{\prime}_{j}\}}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{|k_{j}-k^{\prime}_{j}|}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(s-1+\frac{k_{j}+k^{\prime}_{j}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\pi^{-2s-\max(k_{j},k_{j}^{\prime})}}{8\pi}\Gamma\Bigg(\frac{s+\frac{|k_{j}-k_{j}^{\prime}|}{2}}{2}\Bigg)\Gamma\Bigg(\frac{s+1+\frac{|k_{j}-k_{j}^{\prime}|}{2}}{2}\Bigg)\Gamma\Bigg(\frac{s+\frac{k_{j}+k_{j}^{\prime}}{2}}{2}\Bigg)\Gamma\Bigg(\frac{s-1+\frac{k_{j}+k_{j}^{\prime}}{2}}{2}\Bigg).

Then
\Lambda(s,{\bf f}\otimes{\bf g})
admits an analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
as an entire function (unless
{\bf f}={\bf g}
) and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})=\Lambda(1-s,{\bf f}\times{\bf g})
(see
[
10
]
).

Applying Proposition
2.1
and using (
3.1
) yield the following result.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{n})
and
{\bf g}\in\Pi_{k^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
with
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})=1
. Assuming GRH for both
L(s,{\rm Sym}^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))
and
L(s,\wedge^{2}({\bf f}\times{\bf g}))
, we get

\displaystyle D({\bf f}\times{\bf g};\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{\phi}(0)}{\log R}\log\Bigg({\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{{n}}(|k_{j}-k_{j}^{\prime}|+1)^{2}(k_{j}+k_{j}^{\prime})^{2}\Bigg)-\frac{\delta_{{\bf f}\times{\bf g}}}{2}\phi(0)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\log R}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{p})C_{{\bf g}}(\mathfrak{p})\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})\frac{\widehat{\phi}\left(\frac{\log{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})}{\log R}\right)}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O\left(\frac{\log\log\left({\rm N}(kk^{\prime}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\right)}{\log R}\right).

5.
Some technical lemmas

In this section, we derive some technical lemmas that allow us to express sums over primitive forms in terms of sums of Petersson type. Similar lemmas have been derived in
[
6
]
and adapted by
[
9
]
for the setting of Hilbert modular forms of parallel weight over a totally real number field with class number one. We reproduce these lemmas in the more general setting that we require in this work. Although the proofs require minor modifications from the respective ones in
[
6
]
, we include them for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})
. Then for any
\mathfrak{m}\mid\mathfrak{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left<{\bf f},{\bf f}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{\Gamma(k)}{(4\pi)^{{\rm Tr}(k)}}\frac{\zeta_{F}(2)d_{F}^{3/2}}{R_{F}}\nu(\mathfrak{n})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)Z(1,{\bf f}),

where
\nu(\mathfrak{n})={\rm N}(\mathfrak{n})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{n}}\left(1+{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)
and
Z(s,{\bf f})=\displaystyle{\sum_{0\neq\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{q}^{2})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{q})^{s}}.}

Proof.

First, we recall that
\text{Res}_{s=1}L(s,{\bf f}\otimes{\bf f})=Z(1,{\bf f})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)
. The proof now follows from
[
17
, Proposition 4.13, Eq. (2.27), (2.28), and (2.31)]
while noting that our definition of the Petersson inner product differs slightly from Shimura’s definition.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{n})=1
and
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{n})=1
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})&=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{Z_{\mathfrak{n}}(1,{\bf f})}{Z(1,{\bf f})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{n}){\rm N}(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}^{\infty}}\frac{1}{{\rm N}(\ell)}\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{m}}^{*}(\mathfrak{a}\ell^{2},\mathfrak{b}),\end{split}

where
\mathcal{K}_{F}=2^{n-2}(2\pi)^{2n}\frac{R_{F}}{\zeta_{F}(2)d_{F}^{2}}
,
Z_{\mathfrak{n}}(s,{\bf f})=\displaystyle{\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{\infty}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{q}^{2})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{q})^{s}}}
, and

(5.2)

\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{m}}^{*}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=\displaystyle{\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{Z_{\mathfrak{m}}(1,{\bf f})}{Z(1,{\bf f})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})}.

Moreover, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta^{*}_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=\mathcal{K}_{F}^{-1}{\rm N}(k-1)\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\mu(\mathfrak{l}){\rm N}(\mathfrak{m})\sum_{\ell\mid\mathfrak{l}}\frac{1}{{\rm N}(\ell)}\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}\ell^{2},\mathfrak{b}).\end{split}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf f}\in H_{k}(\mathfrak{n})}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{{\rm Tr}(k-1)}d_{F}^{1/2}\left<{\bf f},{\bf f}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\sum_{\ell|\mathfrak{l}}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{{\rm Tr}(k-1)}d_{F}^{1/2}\left<{\bf f}_{\ell},{\bf f}_{\ell}\right>_{\mathfrak{n}}}C_{{\bf f}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{F}}{\nu(\mathfrak{n})\zeta_{F}(2)d_{F}^{3/2}}\frac{2^{n-2}(2\pi)^{2n}}{N(k-1)d_{F}^{1/2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{1}{Z(1,{\bf f})}\sum_{\ell|\mathfrak{l}}C_{{\bf f}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{a})C_{{\bf f}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{b}).

Observe that (
4.2
) gives

(5.4)

C_{{\bf f}_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{a})=\left(\frac{N(\ell)}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\ell)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}=\ell\\
\mathfrak{d}|\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{c})\mu(\mathfrak{c})}{\nu(\mathfrak{c})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}).

Hence, we derive

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{{\rm N}(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{1}{Z(1,{\bf f})}\sum_{\ell|\mathfrak{l}}\frac{N(\ell)}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\ell)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}=\ell\\
\mathfrak{d}_{1}|\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{c}_{1})\mu(\mathfrak{c}_{1})}{\nu(\mathfrak{c}_{1})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{2}=\ell\\
\mathfrak{d}_{2}|\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{c}_{2})\mu(\mathfrak{c}_{2})}{\nu(\mathfrak{c}_{2})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{2}^{-1}).

Since
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{n})=(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{n})=1
, we observe that
\mathfrak{d}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{2}
in the above summations are necessarily relatively prime. We write
\mathfrak{c}_{1}=\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{2}=\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{1}
to get

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{\nu(\mathfrak{n})N(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{1}{Z(1,{\bf f})}\sum_{\ell|\mathfrak{l}}\frac{N(\ell)}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\ell)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2}=\ell\\
\mathfrak{d}_{1}|\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{d}_{2}|\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})}{\nu(\mathfrak{b})}\right)^{2}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}{\nu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{2}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{\nu(\mathfrak{n})N(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{1}{Z(1,{\bf f})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}_{1}|\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{d}_{2}|\mathfrak{l}\\
\mathfrak{d}_{1}|\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{d}_{2}|\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\frac{N(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}{\nu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{2}^{-1})\sum_{\mathfrak{b}|\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{2}^{-1}}\frac{N(\mathfrak{b})}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})}\left(\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b})}{\nu(\mathfrak{b})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{\nu(\mathfrak{n})N(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{m}}\left(1-{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\right)}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{1}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{l})Z(1,{\bf f})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}_{1}|(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{a})\\
\mathfrak{d}_{2}|(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{b})\end{subarray}}N(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}{\nu(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})}C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{2})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{2}^{-1}).

We set

A_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{l})=\sum_{\mathfrak{d}_{1}|(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{a})}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{1})}{\nu(\mathfrak{d}_{1})}N(\mathfrak{d}_{1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{d}_{1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1}).

Using the multiplicativity of
C_{{\bf f}}
given in (
4.1
), we can write

A_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{l})=\frac{1}{\nu((\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{a}))}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{2}|(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{d}_{1})}\mu(\mathfrak{d}_{1})N(\mathfrak{d}_{1})C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-2}).

Collecting these observations yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{K}_{F}}{\nu(\mathfrak{n})N(k-1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}}\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{\phi(\mathfrak{m})}\sum_{{\bf f}\in\Pi_{k}(\mathfrak{m})}\frac{A_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{l})A_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{l})}{\rho_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{l})Z(1,{\bf f})}.

In fact, if we define

\displaystyle Z_{\mathfrak{n}}(s,{\bf f})=\displaystyle{\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{\infty}}\frac{C_{{\bf f}}(\mathfrak{q}^{2})}{{\rm N}(\mathfrak{q})^{s}}}
, then one can verify that

\displaystyle Z_{\mathfrak{n}}(1,{\bf f})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}Z_{\mathfrak{p}}(1,{\bf f}),

where

(5.5)

Z_{\mathfrak{p}}(1,{\bf f})=\begin{cases}(1+{\rm N}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})^{-1}\rho(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}&\text{if $\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{m}$};\\

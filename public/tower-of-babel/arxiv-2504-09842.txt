Symphony of Symmetry Selective Resonances in Fe-MgO-ZnO-MgO-Fe Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sabarna Chakraborti 1, Arti Kashyap 2, Abhishek Sharma 3

Abstract
We propose the perspective of symmetry-selective resonance of the Δ 1 \Delta_{1} states in the Fe/MgO/ZnO/MgO/Fe heterostructures, offering a broad landscape to design magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs) that yield a towering tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) upto 3.5 × 10 4 \times 10^{4} % with the resistance area (RA) product dipping down till a minimum of 0.05 Ω \Omega - μ ​ m 2 \mu m^{2} , while maintaining a nearly perfect (99%) spin polarization. Our predictions are based on the self-consistent coupling of the non-equilibrium Green’s function with the density functional theory. We also present the charge current, spin current and TMR of a prototype(p) Fe/MgO(3-layer(l))/ZnO(3l)/MgO(3l)/Fe-MTJ(abbreviated as p- Δ 1 R \Delta_{1}^{R} -MTJ), which provides a pronounced advancement in the TMR(1.3 × 10 4 \times 10^{4} %), RA product(0.45 Ω \Omega - μ ​ m 2 \mu m^{2} ) and spin polarization(99%) over its Fe/MgO(6l)/Fe-based counterpart(TMR ≈ 3.4 × 10 3 \approx 3.4\times 10^{3} %, RA product ≈ 22 \approx 22 Ω \Omega - μ \mu m 2 m^{2} ). Subsequently, we provide a holistic insight into the physics of symmetry-selective resonance by integrating the transmission eigenchannels, spectral density, and band structure of the Fe contacts associated with the p- Δ 1 R \Delta_{1}^{R} -MTJ.

Technologically relevant advancements in spintronics towered on MTJs, trace their origin back to the seminal work on the Fe/MgO/Fe sandwich that predicted a TMR of over 1000%

Considering the recent development of the STT-MRAM technology at the 14 nm node
Lee et al. 2022

II
Methodology

We utilize self-consistently coupled the density functional theory (DFT) with the non-equilibrium Green’s function (NEGF) to perform the spin-polarized transport calculations across the
\Delta^{R}_{1}
-MTJ shown in Fig.
1
(a). We employ the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof(PBE) generalized gradient approximation(GGA) for the exchange-correlation functional and the standard norm-conserving pseudopotentials
Soler et al. 2002
while using the double zeta LCAO basis set for s, p and d orbitals using the Siesta framework
van Setten et al. 2018
;
Papior 2024
. We also benchmarked the conventional Fe/MgO/Fe-based MTJ (see
Appendix A
) to demonstrate that transmission eigenchannels
Paulsson and Brandbyge 2007
offer a more generalized approach to rationalize symmetry-dependent tunneling in MTJs, compared to the complex band structures
Butler et al. 2001b
;
Nawa et al. 2021
.

The recent study on r-MTJs showed that GGA-PBE functionals are adequate for accurately predicting the transmission of the majority electrons
Baez Flores et al. 2024
. The GW calculation does not alter the transmission of the Fe/MgO/Fe-majority states
Baez Flores et al. 2024
;
Faleev et al. 2012
, possibly due to the population of the de-localized states (s-orbitals) in the MgO layers injected by majority electrons of the Fe electrode(see
Appendix D
. The GW calculation of the r-MTJ shifts the interface resonance states of the minority electrons only by 0.12 eV, leading to an increase in TMR near the zero bias
Baez Flores et al. 2024
. Since the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJs rely on the symmetry-selective resonance of majority-spin electrons (
\Delta_{1}
states, see Fig.
3
(a)), the predicted TMRs are at best underestimated. Likewise, the predicted RA (in PC) values, being dominated by symmetry-selective resonant tunneling of majority electrons, are unlikely to be significantly affected. The presence of low spin orbit coupling(SOC) at both the Fe-MgO boundary and in ZnO ensures prolonged spin coherence, which leads to longer spin relaxation lengths and effectively neutralizes any impact of SOC on the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJ
Niaouris et al. 2022
;
Miwa et al. 2017
. Hence, the GW correction and SOC are neglected to the first order to manage the computational cost. A thorough evaluation of these aspects may be pursued in future studies.

IV
Conclusion

Since the discovery of FM/MgO/FM MTJs
Butler et al. 2001b
;
Waldron et al. 2006

Keeping aside the fabrication efforts, even the theoretical endeavours have struggled to yield the desired outcomes.
In this work, we present the phenomenon of symmetry selective resonance of the
\Delta_{1}
states via first principle calculations in the Fe/MgO/ZnO/MgO/Fe heterostructures while working within the confines of today’s fabrication techniques

Appendix C: Stability of the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJ
s

In finite-temperature density functional theory (DFT), the interatomic forces arise from minimizing the Mermin free energy
Mermin 1965
, which combines the internal energy and the electronic entropy.

Figure 15:
(a) Stability of the
\Delta^{R}_{1}-MTJs
are anticipated with respect to the
r-MTJ
by subtracting the Mermin free energies of the devices.(b)The distance between the O atom in the terminal MgO layer and the Fe contact is reported with precision up to the fourth decimal digit. Here, an element with the nth row and mth column represents a
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJ featuring an n-MgO/m-ZnO/n-MgO sandwich flanked between the Fe layers.

An
electronic temperature

T
is introduced for metals or systems with small band gaps to smooth orbital occupancies, thus improving convergence in the self-consistent field (SCF) procedure.
At this finite temperature, Kohn–Sham states are partially occupied according to the Fermi–Dirac function,

f_{i}=\frac{1}{1+e^{(\epsilon_{i}-\mu)/k_{B}T}},

and the accompanying electronic entropy is given by

S=-k_{B}\sum_{i}\bigl[f_{i}\ln(f_{i})+(1-f_{i})\ln\bigl(1-f_{i}\bigr)\bigr].

The Mermin free energy, often represented as

\Omega=U-T\,S,

incorporates the Kohn-Sham potential energy
U
and the entropy term
S
arising from the partial occupancies.
While the parameter
T
is chosen to be large enough to smooth electronic occupancies near the Fermi level, it is typically kept small enough to avoid unphysical effects on the system’s overall behaviour.
The stability of the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJs is assessed relative to the r-MTJ (Fig.
7
) by comparing their free energies, as it accounts for electronic entropy at finite temperature, unlike the Kohn–Sham energy
Wentzcovitch et al. 1992
.
In Fig.
15
(a), we present the difference between the free energies between the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJs and the r-MTJ. The negative values of all the matrix elements highlights the boost in the stability of the
\Delta_{1}
-MTJs, likely facilitated by the ZnO quantum well, which contributes to a denser electron cloud around the Fe–O bond. This trend is also noticed in the table presented in Fig.
15
.
Although not monotonous, the introduction of the ZnO layers results in a reduction of the Fe-O bond length at the MgO-Fe interface. The value of the Fe-O bond length shows a generic trend of going up as the number of MgO layers increases. The devices with a higher percentage of ZnO layers have lower bond lengths. This is possibly due to the fact that increasing the number of MgO layers shielded the coupling of the Fe electrode with the energy states within the ZnO.

Appendix D: The redundancy of the GW calculation for majority states in the
r
-MTJ and the
\Delta_{1}^{R}
-MTJ

The requirement of GW calculation stems from the fact that LDA/GGA fails to capture the electron-electron correlations in insulating systems
Jiang et al. 2010
such as MgO
Schönberger and Aryasetiawan 1995
. However, metallic systems, with their nearly spherical Fermi surfaces, exhibit a strong screening effect, enabling LDA/GGA-PBE to predict the band structure of metals accurately
Haule and Mandal 2024
.
GW calculation performed on the Fe-MgO-Fe heterostructure reveals that the local DOS(LDOS) and transmission for the majority electrons show a negligible difference to that produced by LSDA/GGA-PBE calculations
Baez Flores et al. 2024
;
Faleev et al. 2012
. However, the GW correction, in agreement with experimental studies
Zermatten et al. 2008
;
Du et al. 2010
, yields a positive shift of 0.14–0.2 eV to the LDOS and transmission of the minority-spin interface resonance states
Baez Flores et al. 2024
;
Faleev et al. 2012
.

The current density (
J_{C}
) through the scattering region in the Landauer–Büttiker formalism given by
Büttiker et al. 1985
:

\begin{split}J_{C}=\frac{q}{\hbar}\sum_{\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}T_{\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)\,\bigl[f_{1}(E)-f_{2}(E)\bigr]\\
\,\mathrm{d}k_{x}\,\mathrm{d}k_{y}\,\mathrm{d}E,\end{split}

(1)

where,
f_{L}(E)=1/(1+e{(E-E_{F_{L}})}/kT)
and
f_{R}(E)=1/(1+e^{(E-E_{F_{R}})}/kT)
are the Fermi distribution functions of the left
and the right contact, respectively. Here,
V
represents the applied voltage,
k
indicates the Boltzmann constant,
e
represents the electronic charge, and
T
denotes the absolute temperature.
\sigma\equiv\downarrow,\uparrow
indicates the spin index,
E_{F_{L}}
and
E_{F_{R}}
denotes the Fermi energies of the left and the right contact. The transmission
T_{\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)
using the NEGF framework is given by
Datta 2005
:

\begin{split}T_{\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)=\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\Gamma_{L,\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)\,G_{\sigma}^{R}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)\\
\Gamma_{R,\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)\,G_{\sigma}^{A}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)\Bigr],\end{split}

(2)

where,
\Gamma_{L,\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)
and
\Gamma_{R,\sigma}^{\,k_{x},\,k_{y}}(E,V)
are the broadening matrices of the left and the right contacts, respectively.
G_{\sigma}^{R}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)
and
G_{\sigma}^{A}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)
are the retarded and advanced Green’s functions of the scattering region. The broadening matrices in terms of the self-energy matrices of the left and the right contacts are given by
Datta 2005

\Gamma^{k_{x},k_{y}}_{L/R,\sigma}(E,V)=i\left[\Sigma^{k_{x},k_{y}}_{L/R,\sigma}(E,V)-\left(\Sigma^{k_{x},k_{y}}_{L/R,\sigma}(E,V)\right)^{\dagger}\right],

(3)

The self-energies for the contacts are defined as

\Sigma_{L/R}^{\sigma}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)=\tau_{L/R}^{\dagger}G_{L/R}^{\sigma}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)\tau_{L/R},

(4)

where,
G_{L/R}^{\sigma}(k_{x},k_{y},E,V)
is the Green’s function of the left/right electrode, and
\tau_{L/R}
represents the coupling matrix between the scattering region and the contacts.

Riesz transforms, Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated with Laguerre expansions
The Anh Bui

Abstract
. Let ν ∈ [ − 1 / 2 , ∞ ) n \nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , and let ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} be a self-adjoint extension of the differential operator L ν := ∑ i = 1 n [ − ∂ 2 ∂ x i 2 + x i 2 + 1 x i 2 ​ ( ν i 2 − 1 4 ) ] L_{\nu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\right] on C c ∞ ​ ( ℝ + n ) C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}) as the natural domain. In this paper, we first prove that the Riesz transform associated with ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} is a Calderón-Zygmund operator, answering the open problem in [JFA, 244 (2007), 399-443]. In addition, we develop the theory of Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated with ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} . As applications, we prove that the Riesz transform related to ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} is bounded on these Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces, completing the description of the boundedness of the Riesz transform in the Laguerre expansion setting.



1.
Introduction

For each
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we consider the Laguerre differential operator

\displaystyle L_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\Big(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\Big],\quad\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

The
j
-th partial derivative associated with
L_{\nu}
is given by

\delta_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the adjoint of
\delta_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is

\delta_{j}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

It is straightforward that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}^{*}\delta_{i}=L_{\nu}{-2}(|\nu|+n).

Let
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
be multi-indices. The Laguerre function
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is defined as

\varphi_{k}^{\nu}(x)=\varphi^{\nu_{1}}_{k_{1}}(x_{1})\ldots\varphi^{\nu_{n}}_{k_{n}}(x_{n}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}
are the one-dimensional Laguerre functions

\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}(x_{i})=\Big(\frac{2\Gamma(k_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(k_{i}+\nu_{i}+1)}\Big)^{1/2}L_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(x_{i}^{2})x_{i}^{\nu_{i}+1/2}e^{-x_{i}^{2}/2},\quad x_{i}>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

given
\nu>-1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{\nu}^{k}
denotes the Laguerre polynomial of degree
k
and order
\nu
outlined in
[
15
, p.76]
.

Then it is well-known that the system
\{\varphi_{k}^{\nu}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
. Moreover, Each
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
is an eigenfunction of
L_{\nu}
corresponding to the eigenvalue of
4|k|+2|\nu|+2n
, i.e.,

L_{\nu}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\varphi_{k}^{\nu},

where
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
and
|k|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
. The operator
L_{\nu}
is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
.

If
\nu=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\ldots,-\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
L_{\nu}
becomes the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Note that the Riesz transforms related to the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were investigated in
[
21
,
22
]
.

The operator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}

defined on the domain
\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\}
is a self-adjoint extension of
L_{\nu}
(the inclusion
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\subset\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
may be easily verified), has the discrete spectrum
\{4\ell+2|\nu|+2n:\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and admits the spectral decomposition

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(4\ell+2|\nu|+2n)P_{\nu,\ell}f,

where the spectral projections are

P_{\nu,\ell}f=\sum_{|k|=\ell}\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}.

Moreover,

(1)

\delta_{j}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}}\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}},\ \ \delta_{j}^{*}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}+1}\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here and later on we use the convention that
\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}}=0
if
k_{j}-1<0
and
\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}}=0
if
\nu_{j}-1<0
. See for example
[
18
]
.

,

and the references therein. Here, we would like to highlight existing results in the literature. For the case
n=1
, the boundedness of the Riesz transform
R_{\nu}
on Lebesgue spaces

was investigated in

for

[

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
was obtained. In the higher-dimensional case
n\geq 2
, the boundedness of the Riesz transforms was studied in
[
18
]
. For the sake of convenience, we would like to recall the main result in
[
18
]

Theorem A.
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
Assume that
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
is a multi-index such that
\nu_{i}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
\nu_{i}\notin\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then the Riesz operators
R_{\nu}^{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,d
, are Calderón–Zygmund operators and hence, the Riesz operators
R_{\nu}^{j}
are bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, where
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is the Muckenhoupt class of weights defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

The condition
\nu_{i}\geq-\frac{1}{2},i=1,\ldots,n
is necessary. Indeed, in general if
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
the Riesz transforms might not be the Calderón-Zygmund operators. See for example
[
1
]
. An important reason for the restriction
\nu_{i}\notin\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),i=1,\ldots,n
, is necessary to perform some estimates of the gradient of
R_{\nu}^{j}(x,y)
. To the best of our knowledge, up to now this is the best result for the higher dimensional case
n\geq 0
. Hence, the boundedness of the Riesz transforms with
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
for
n\geq 2
is still open. One of our main aims is to address this open problem. We first introduce some notations. For
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
, we define

(2)

\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
, where
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\{j:\nu_{j}>-1/2\}
.

Denote
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})
and
\delta^{*}=(\delta^{*}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{*}_{n})
. Then we are able to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
. Denote by
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)
the associated kernel of
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
. Then the Riesz transform
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. That is,

|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y

and

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle+|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(y,x)-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

We would like to emphasize that Theorem
1.1
is new, even when
n=1
. In fact, in the case of
n=1
, the boundedness of the Riesz transform was explored in
[
18
,
1
,
2
]
. In
[
18
]
, the Riesz operator was decomposed into local and global components. The local part emerged as a local Calderón-Zygmund operator, while the global part was effectively dealt by using weighted Hardy’s inequalities. In
[
1
,
2
]
, the investigation leaned on the connections between the Riesz transform associated with the Laguerre operator and that associated with the Hermite operator on
\mathbb{R}
. It’s important to note that the methods employed in
[
18
,
1
,
2
]
do not imply the boundedness of the Riesz transform on weighted
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

The implications of Theorem
1.1
extend to the boundedness of the Riesz transform
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Naturally, this leads to a question about the endpoint estimates concerning the Hardy space and the BMO-type space estimates for the Riesz transforms. Consequently, the second objective of this paper is to develop the theory of a Hardy space and its duality for the Riesz transform
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
. To accomplish this, we first introduce a new atomic Hardy space related to a critical function. For
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
, define

(3)

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},1,x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\Big\},

where
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}:=\{j:\nu_{j}>-1/2\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be the function as in (
3
). Let
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
and
q\in[1,\infty]\cap(p,\infty]
. A function
a
is called a
(p,q,\rho_{\nu})
-atom associated to the ball
B(x_{0},r)
if

(i)

{\rm supp}\,a\subset B(x_{0},r)
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq|B(x_{0},r)|^{1/q-1/p}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\int a(x)dx=0
if
r<\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
.

Let
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
and
q\in[1,\infty]\cap(p,\infty]
. We say that
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is an atomic
(p,q,\rho_{\nu})
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,q,\rho_{\nu})
-atom,
and the sum converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The space
H^{p,q}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is then defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):f\ \text{has an atomic
$(p,q,\rho_{\nu})$-representation}\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p,q}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf\Big\{\Big(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is an atomic $(p,q,\rho_{\nu})$-representation}\Big\}.

In Theorem
4.1
below, we will show that the Hardy space
H^{p,q}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is independent of
q
, i.e., for
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
and
q\in[1,\infty]\cap(p,\infty]
, we have

H^{p,q}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=H^{p,\infty}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

For this reason, for
p\in(\frac{n}{n+1},1]
we will define the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as any Hardy space
H^{p,q}_{{\rm at},\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
q\in[1,\infty]\cap(p,\infty]
.

We next introduce the Hardy space associated with the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
we define

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

The maximal Hardy spaces associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
are defined as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

Given
p\in(0,1]
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the completion of

\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}

under the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
, where
\nu_{\min}
is as in (
2
). For
p\in(\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}},1]
, we have

H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

with equivalent norms.

Note that the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
defined in (
3
) does not satisfy the property of the critical defined in
[
24
, Definition 2.2]
(see also
[
4
]
). In addition, it is not clear if the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
enjoys the Hölder continuity condition, which played an essential roles in
[
24
,
4
]
. Consequently, the approaches outlined in
[
24
,
4
]
(including
[
6
]
) cannot be directly applied in our specific context. This necessitates the development of new ideas and a novel approach.

It is worth noting that the equivalence between the atomic Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
was established in
[
10
]
for the specific case of

, and
\nu>-1/2
. However, the approach employed in
[
10
]
is not applicable to higher-dimensional cases.

For the duality of the Hardy space, we introduce the Campanato space associated to the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
. For
s\in[0,1)
, the Campanato space associated to the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
can be defined as the set of all
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
functions

such that

\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm ball}\\
{r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|B|^{1+s/n}}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|dx+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm ball}\\
{r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|B|^{1+s/n}}\int_{B}|f|dx<\infty,

where
\displaystyle f_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f
and the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

The we have the following result regarding the duality of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
, where
\nu_{\min}
is as in (
2
). Let
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (

, we have

\displaystyle\big(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\big)^{\ast}=BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),\quad\mbox{where }s:=n(1/p-1).

The particular case of Theorem
1.5
when

\nu>-1/2

[

]
. The method in
[

relied heavily on the structure of the half-line

Returning to the Riesz transform
\delta\mathcal{L}^{-1/2}\nu

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
. Then for

(ii)

In the specific case where

, and

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will establish some preliminary results regarding the critical functions and the theory of Hardy spaces associated to an abstract operator.

2.1.
Critical functions

Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be the critical function defined by (
3
). It is easy to see that

(i)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\emptyset
, then we have

\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},1\Big\}\sim\frac{1}{1+|x|},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\neq\emptyset
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

In this case, the critical function
\rho_{\nu}
is independent of
\nu
. More precisely, if
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
, then

\rho_{\nu}(x)=\rho_{\nu^{\prime}}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\Big\{\frac{1}{|x|},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\Big\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, define

\rho_{\nu_{j}}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\times\min\{1,x_{j}^{-1}\},\ \ \ &\nu_{j}=-1/2,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}\min\{x_{j},x_{j}^{-1}\},\ \ \ &\nu_{j}>-1/2.\end{cases}

Then we have

(4)

\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\min\Big\{\rho_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1}),\ldots,\rho_{\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\Big\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then for every
y\in 4B(x,\rho_{\nu}(x))
,

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq\rho_{\nu}(y)\leq 2\rho_{\nu}(x).

Proof.

Fix
y\in 4B(x,\rho_{\nu}(x))
. We consider the following three cases.

Case 1:
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16|x|}
.
In this situation,
|x|\geq 1
. Then,

|y|\geq|x|-|x-y|\geq|x|-4\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq 1-\frac{4}{16}=\frac{3}{4},

since
|x|\geq 1
and
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1/16
.

Consequently,

\frac{1}{|y|}-16\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{|y|}-\frac{1}{|x|}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\leq\frac{4\rho_{\nu}(x)}{1\times\frac{3}{4}}\leq\frac{16}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x),

which implies

\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{16}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x)+16\rho_{\nu}(x).

Therefore,

\rho_{\nu}(y)\leq\frac{1}{16|y|}\leq\frac{4}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x).

For the reverse direction, since

\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{|y|^{-1},1,y_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\},

we consider three subcases.

\bullet
If
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16|y|}
, then we have

16\rho_{\nu}(x)-16\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{|x|}-\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\leq\frac{4\rho_{\nu}(x)}{1\times\frac{3}{4}}\leq\frac{16}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x),

which yields

16\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq 16\rho_{\nu}(x)-\frac{16}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x).

It follows that

\rho_{\nu}(y):=\frac{1}{16|y|}\geq\frac{2}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x),

as desired.

\bullet
If
\rho_{\nu}(y)=1/16
, then obviously,
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq\rho_{\nu}(x)
due to
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1/16
.

\bullet
If

\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{y_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\},

then we might assume that
\nu_{1}>-1/2
and
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{y_{1}}{16}
. Then we have

16\rho_{\nu}(y)-x_{1}=y_{1}-x_{1}\geq-|x-y|\geq-4\rho_{\nu}(x),

which implies

16\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq x_{1}-4\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq 16\rho_{\nu}(x)-4\rho_{\nu}(x)=12\rho_{\nu}(x).

It follows that

\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq\frac{3}{4}\rho_{\nu}(x).

This completes our proof in this case.

Case 2:
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}
.
Without loss of generality we might assume that
\nu_{1}>-1/2
and
\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(x)=\frac{x_{1}}{16}
.

We first have, for
y\in B(x,4\rho_{\nu}(x))
,

y_{1}-16\rho_{\nu}(x)=y_{1}-x_{1}\leq|x-y|\leq 4\rho_{\nu}(x),

which implies

y_{1}\leq 16\rho_{\nu}(x)+4\rho_{\nu}(x)=20\rho_{\nu}(x).

Hence,

(5)

\rho_{\nu}(y)\leq\frac{y_{1}}{16}\leq\frac{5}{4}\rho_{\nu}(x).

It remains to show that

\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\nu}(x).

We also have three cases.

\bullet
If
\rho_{\nu}(y)=1/16
, this is obvious since
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1/16=\rho_{\nu}(y)
.

\bullet
If
\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{y_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}
, then for any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}
, we have

y_{j}-x_{j}\geq-|x-y|\geq-4\rho_{\nu}(x),

which implies

y_{j}\geq x_{j}-4\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq 16\rho_{\nu}(x)-4\rho_{\nu}(x)=12\rho_{\nu}(x).

Consequently,

\rho_{\nu}(y):=\frac{1}{16}\min\{y_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}_{\nu}\}\geq\frac{3}{4}\rho_{\nu}(x).

\bullet
If
\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16|y|}
, then
|y|\geq 1
. In this case,

|x|\geq|y|-|x-y|\geq 1-4\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq 1-\frac{4}{16}=\frac{3}{4},

since
|y|\geq 1
and
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1/16
.

In this situation, we have

16\rho_{\nu}(y)-\frac{1}{|x|}=\frac{1}{|y|}-\frac{1}{|x|}\geq-\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\geq-\frac{4\rho_{\nu}(x)}{1\times\frac{3}{4}}=-\frac{16}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x),

which implies

16\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq\frac{1}{|x|}-\frac{16\rho_{\nu}(x)}{3}\geq 16\rho_{\nu}(x)-\frac{16\rho_{\nu}(x)}{3},

or equivalently,

\rho_{\nu}(y)\geq\frac{2}{3}\rho_{\nu}(x),

as desired.

Case 3:
\rho_{\nu}(x)=1/16
.
It is obvious that
\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq\rho_{\nu}(y)
since
\rho_{\nu}(y)\leq 1/16
. It remains to prove

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq\rho_{\nu}(y).

Note that, from the definition of
\rho_{\nu}(x)
, there are only two possibilities: the first is
|x|=1
and
n=1
, and the second is
n\geq 2
with
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\emptyset
, meaning
\nu_{j}=-1/2
for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
|x|\leq 1
.

\bullet
If
n=1
and
x=1
, in this case, since
y\in B(x,4\rho_{\nu}(x))
,

y<x+4\rho_{\nu}(x)=1+\frac{4}{16}=\frac{5}{4},

which implies

\frac{1}{16y}\geq\frac{1}{20}=\frac{4}{5}\rho_{\nu}(x).

In addition, since
y\in B(x,4\rho_{\nu}(x))
, we also have

y\geq x-|x-y|\geq x-4\rho_{\nu}(x)\geq 1-\frac{4}{16}=\frac{3}{4},

which implies

\frac{|y|}{16}\geq\frac{1}{4\times 16}=\frac{3}{4}\rho_{\nu}(x).

Consequently,

\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{1,|y|,|y^{-1}|\}=\frac{1}{16}\min\{|y|,|y^{-1}|\}\geq\frac{3}{4}\rho_{\nu}(x),

as desired.

\bullet
If
n\geq 2
,
\mathcal{J}_{\nu}=\emptyset
, i.e.,
\nu_{j}=-1/2
for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
|x|\leq 1
, then we have

\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{1,\frac{1}{|y|}\}.

Since
y\in B(x,4\rho_{\nu}(x))
, if
|y|\leq 1
, then
\rho_{\nu}(y)=1/16
. It follows that
\rho_{\nu}(y)=\rho_{\nu}(x)
and hence this completes our proof. Otherwise, if
|y|>1
, then
\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16|y|}
. In this case, we have

|y|\leq|x|+|x-y|\leq|x|+4\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1+\frac{4}{16}=\frac{5}{4},

which implies

\rho_{\nu}(y)=\frac{1}{16|y|}\geq\frac{4}{5\times 16}=\frac{4}{5}\rho_{\nu}(x)

as desired.

This completes our proof.

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. There exist a family of balls
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
and a family of functions
\{\psi_{\xi}:\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
;

(ii)

\{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})/5):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
is pairwise disjoint;

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))}\lesssim 1
;

(iv)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{\xi}\subset B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))
and
0\leq\psi_{\xi}\leq 1
for each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
;

(v)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\psi_{\xi}=1
.

Proof.

Consider the family
\{B(x,\rho_{\nu}(x)/5):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}
. Since
\rho_{\nu}(x)\leq 1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, by Vitali’s covering lemma we can extract a sub-family denoted by
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})/5):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
satisfying (i) and (ii).

The item (iii) follows directly from (ii) and Lemma
2.1
.

For each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
, define

\displaystyle\psi_{\xi}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))}(x)}{\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{B(x_{\theta},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\theta}))}(x)},\ \ &x\in B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})),\\
0,&x\notin B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})).\end{cases}

Then
\{\psi_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
satisfies (iv) and (v).

This completes our proof.
∎

2.2.
Hardy spaces associated to operators

On our setting
(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},|\cdot|,dx)
, we consider an operator
L
satisfying the following two conditions:

(A1)

L
is a nonnegative, possibly unbounded, self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

(A2)

L
generates a semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
whose kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
admits a Gaussian upper bound. That is, there exist two positive finite constants
C
and
c
so that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle|p_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

We now recall the definition of the Hardy spaces associated to
L
in
[
13
,
14
]
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
. Then the Hardy space
H_{S_{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is
defined as the completion of

\{f\in L^{2}:S_{L}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}

under the norm
\|f\|_{H_{S_{L}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|S_{L}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
, where the square function
S_{L}
is defined as

S_{L}f(x)=\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<t}|t^{2}Le^{-t^{2}L}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Definition 2.3

(Atoms for
L
)
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
. A function
a
supported in a ball
B
is called a
(p,M)_{L}
-atom if there exists a
function
b
in the domain of
L^{M}
such that

(i)

a=L^{M}b
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}L^{k}b\subset B,\ k=0,1,\dots,M
;

(iii)

\|L^{k}b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq r_{B}^{2(M-k)}|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}},\ k=0,1,\dots,M
.

Then the atomic Hardy spaces associated to the operator
L
are defined as follows:

Definition 2.4

(Atomic Hardy spaces for
L
)
.

Given
p\in(0,1]
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is an atomic
(p,M)_{L}
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,M)_{L}
-atom,
and the sum converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The space
H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is then defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):f\ \text{has an atomic
$(p,M)_{L}$-representation}\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf\left\{\left(\sum|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}:f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is an atomic $(p,M)_{L}$-representation}\right\}.

The maximal Hardy spaces associated to
L
are defined as follows.

Definition 2.5

(Maximal Hardy spaces for
L
)
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, we define the
radial
maximal function by

\mathcal{M}_{L}(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-tL}f(x)|.

Given
p\in(0,1]
, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\mathcal{M}_{L}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right\},

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|\mathcal{M}_{L}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

The following theorem is taken from
[
20
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
, and
M>\frac{n}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)
. Then the Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{S_{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,
H^{p}_{L,at,M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
H^{p}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
coincide with equivalent norms.

3.
Some kernel estimates

This section is devoted to establishing some kernel estimates related to the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. These estimates play an essential role in proving our main results. We begin by providing an explicit formula for the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, similarly to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}
the self-adjoint extension of the differential operator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+x_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{j}^{2}}(\nu_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})

on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the natural domain. It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}.

Let
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and let
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(6)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j}).

For
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
,
j=1,\ldots,n,
, the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
is given by

(7)

p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
and
I_{\alpha}
is the usual Bessel funtions of an imaginary argument defined by

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\alpha+2k}}{k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)},\ \ \ \ \alpha>-1.

See for example
[
10
,
18
]
.

Note that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, we can rewrite the kernel
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
as follows

(8)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1+r^{1/2}}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

The following properties of the Bessel function
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>-1
are well-known and are taken from
[
15
]
:

(9)

I_{\alpha}(z)\sim z^{\alpha},\ \ \ 0<z\leq 1,

(10)

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}+S_{\alpha}(z),

where

(11)

|S_{\alpha}(z)|\leq Ce^{z}z^{-3/2},\ \ z\geq 1,

and

(12)

\frac{d}{dz}(z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}(z))=z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(z).

n=1

In this case, we consider
\nu>-1/2
and

separately.

3.1.1.
The case

In this case

for

. Without any confusion, in this section we still write
\rho_{\nu}
instead of
\rho_{-1/2}
. Let

is the Hermite operator define by

We first prove the followng estimate.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
N>0
, there exists
C>0
so that

(13)

|x^{\ell}\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/8}}{t^{(\ell+k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big[1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big]^{-N}

for all

and

.

Proof.

We first prove (
13
) for
\ell=0
. From
[
3

(14)

|\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/4}}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\exp\Big[-\frac{t}{c\rho_{\nu}(y)^{2}}\Big].

If

e^{-t/8}\lesssim\exp\Big[-\frac{t}{c^{\prime}\rho_{\nu}(x)^{2}}\Big].

This, along with (

which implies (
13
) for

|x|\leq 1
.

If
|x|>1

\displaystyle\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{1}{|x|}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
|y|\leq|x|/2
or
|y|>2|x|
. In this case
|x-y|>x/2
. This, together with (
14
), implies, for any
K\geq 0

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/4}}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{K}\exp\Big[-\frac{t}{c\rho_{\nu}(y)^{2}}\Big]

) for
\ell=0
in this case.

Case 2:

. In this case,

. Hence, the estimate (

) for

).

We have proved (

) for
\ell=0

) for
\ell\geq 0
. Indeed, applying (

) for
\ell=0
we have, for any

|\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/8}}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big[1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big]^{-N-l},

which implies

|\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/8}}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big[1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big]^{-N}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{l}.

Therefore,

\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big)^{l}|\partial_{x}^{k}h_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{Ce^{-t/8}}{t^{(\ell+k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big[1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big]^{-N}.

|x|\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}

.

\nu>-1/2

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{e^{-t/2}}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

Case 1:

.
In this situation, we have
r\sim r^{1/2}\sim 1

where

xy<t

Subcase 1.1:

(20)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

On the other hand, we have

(21)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2},

where in the last inequality we used

\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{x\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

since
t\in(0,1]
.

From (
20
) and (
21
),

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

In addition, the first inequality in (
20
) also implies (
18
). Hence,

(22)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)};

which implies (
19
).

Subcase 1.2:
xy\geq t
.
By (
8
), (
10
) and (
11
) we have

(23)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}txy\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

If
x\leq 1
, then
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim x
. Note that
|x-y|+x\geq y
so that either
|x-y|>y/2
or
x>y/2
. If
|x-y|>y/2
, then
|x-y|\geq|x-y|/4+|x-y|/2\geq|x-y|/4+|y|/4\geq|x|/4
. This, together with (
23
) and
xy\geq t
, implies

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{y\sqrt{xy}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{xy}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

Otherwise, if
x>y/2
, then
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim x\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
since
xy\geq t
. This, together with the first inequality in (
23
), yields

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2},

as desired.

If
x>1
, then
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim 1/x
. Since
|x-y|+y\geq x
, either
|x-y|>x/2
or
y>x/2
. If
|x-y|>x/2
, then
|x-y|\geq|y|/4
. This, in combination with the second inequality of (
23
), the fact
t\in(0,1]
and
xy\geq t
, implies

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x\sqrt{xy}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

If
y>x/2
, then from the first inequality of (
23
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{(txy)^{1/2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{(tx^{2})^{1/2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

which ensures (
19
).

Case 2:
t>1
.
In this situation, we have
1-r\sim 1+r\sim 1+r^{1/2}\sim 1-r^{1/2}\sim 1
, where
r=e^{-4t}
. We also consider two subcases:
xy<t
and
xy>t
.

Subcase 2.1:
xy<e^{2t}
.
By (
7
) and (
9
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(e^{-2t}xy\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

which implies (
18
) and

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

Using the inequality

\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\lesssim\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\nu+1/2},

we obtain

(24)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

On the other hand, from the following inequality

\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\lesssim\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

and the fact
t\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

This and (
24
) deduce that

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

and hence,

(25)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)},

as desired.

Subcase 2.2:
xy\geq e^{2t}
.
By (
8
), (
10
) and (
11
) we have

(26)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}xy\Big).

If
x\leq 1
, then
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim x
. Since
|x-y|+x\geq y
, either
|x-y|>y/2
or
x>y/2
. If
|x-y|>y/2
, then from (
26
) and the fact that
xy\geq e^{2t}
and
t>1
, we have

(27)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}/2\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\nu/2+1/4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{C}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}/2\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{y^{2}}\Big)^{\nu/2+1/4}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}/2\Big)\Big(\frac{x^{2}}{(xy)^{2}}\Big)^{\nu/2+1/4}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}/2\Big)\Big(\frac{x^{2}}{e^{4t}}\Big)^{\nu/2+1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

If
x>y/2
, then
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim x\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
since
xy\geq e^{2t}>t
. This, along with (
26
), yields

(28)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

For the case
x>1
,
since
|x-y|+y\geq x
, either
|x-y|>x/2
or
y>x/2
. If
|x-y|>x/2
, then from (
26
),

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{c|x-y|^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\rho_{\nu}(x)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}.

If
y>x/2
, then from (
26
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{(xy)^{1/2}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\sim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\rho_{\nu}(x)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t/2}\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

which yields (
19
).

This completes our proof.
∎

Next we will estimate the derivatives associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
of the heat kernel. To do this, note that from (
7
) we can rewrite
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
as

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1-r}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{-\nu}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

Setting

H_{\nu}(r;x,y)=\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{-\nu}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big),

then

(29)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)H_{\nu}(r;x,y).

From (
29
), applying the chain rule,

(30)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu+1/2}{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y),

which, in combination with the fact
\displaystyle\delta=\partial_{x}+x-\frac{\nu+1/2}{x}
, implies

(31)

\displaystyle\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

On the other hand, since
\partial(xf)=f+\partial f
, we have

(32)

\delta(xf)=f+x\delta f

for a suitable function
f
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\nu>-1/2
and
\ell=0,1,2
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(33)

\Big|\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\delta^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\Big|\lesssim_{\nu,k}\frac{1}{t^{(\ell+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

for
t\in(0,1)
and
(x,y)\in D
, where

D:=\{(x,y):xy<t\}\cup\{(x,y):xy\geq t,y<x/2\}\cup\{(x,y):xy\geq t,y>2x\}.

Proof.

Denote

\widetilde{\delta}=\partial_{x}+x-\frac{1}{x}\Big(\nu+1+\frac{1}{2}\Big),

which is the derivative associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\nu+1}
.

Then we have
\delta=\widetilde{\delta}+\frac{1}{x}
.

\bullet

For
\ell=0
, we consider two cases.

-
Case 1.
If
xy<t
, then the desired estimate follows directly from (
22
).

-
Case 2.
If
xy\geq t
and (
y<x/2
or
y>2x
), then
|x-y|\gtrsim x
. Consequently, by Proposition
3.3
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)},

which proves (
33
) for
\ell=0
.

\bullet

For
\ell=1
, from (
31
) we have

\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

In addition,
1+r\sim r\sim 1
and
1-r\sim t
as
t\in(0,1)
. This and the above identity imply

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{y}{t}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{y}{t}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{|y-x|}{t}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{x}{t}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{|y-x|}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

Applying (
33
) with
\ell=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}

and

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{|y-x|}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|y-x|}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}.

This ensures (
33
) for
\ell=1
.

\bullet

For
\ell=2
, from (
31
) we have

\delta^{2}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]-\frac{1+r}{1-r}\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}y\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

Moreover, we have
1+r\sim r\sim 1
and
1-r\sim t
as
t\in(0,1)
. This and the above identity imply

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta^{2}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]|+\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]|+\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{y}{t}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]|+\frac{y}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]|+\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|.

We now estimate each term in the right hand side of the above inequality. Firstly, by using (
32
) and (
33
) for
\ell=0,1

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\frac{x}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}.

For the second terms, by (
32
) and the fact
\delta=\widetilde{\delta}+\frac{1}{x}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}\big[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+x|\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}|\widetilde{\delta}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

This, in combination with (
33
) with
\ell=0,1
and
\nu+1
taking place of
\nu
, implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k+1}|\delta[xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1/2)}.

For the last term, using the fact
\delta=\widetilde{\delta}+\frac{1}{x}
we have

\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\delta p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\leq\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\widetilde{\delta}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|+\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{x}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|.

Applying (
33
) with
\ell=0,1
and
\nu+1
taking place of
\nu
,

\displaystyle\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}|\widetilde{\delta}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|y-x|}{t}\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu+1+1/2)}

and

\displaystyle\frac{|y-x|}{t}\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{x}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}e^{-\frac{2^{j}r_{B}^{2}}{cs}}\|a\|_{q}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j}B|^{1/q-1/p}=|B_{j}|^{1/q-1/p}.

For the terms

For the last term, we decompose further as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k_{0}}\frac{\chi_{S_{j}(B)}}{|S_{j}(B)|}\int_{S_{j}(B)}a_{2}

functions

B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

It is well-know that for
s\in[0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
, we have

(44)

\|f\|_{BMO^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\sim\sup_{B:{\rm ball}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|^{1+sq/n}}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|^{q}dx\Big)^{1/q}

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (
3

s\in[0,1)

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

Lemma 4.7

The following theorem can be viewed as the Carleson measure characterization of the Campanoto space

via heat kernel of the Laguerre operator

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (

). Let

. Then there exists

such that for all

(45)

\sup_{B:ball}\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq C\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Proof.

Let
B
be an arbitrary ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We consider two cases.

Case 1:
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f\chi_{2B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f\chi_{(2B)^{c}})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.

We note that by the spectral theory, the Littlewood-Paley square function

g\mapsto\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}g|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f\chi_{2B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\|f\chi_{2B}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

In addition, for
x\in B
,

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f\chi_{(2B)^{c}})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\Big)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 2}\int_{S_{j}(B)}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\Big)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 2}\int_{S_{j}(B)}\frac{1}{|2^{j}B|}\exp\Big(-\frac{2^{2j}r_{B}^{2}}{ct^{2}}\Big)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 2}\exp\Big(-\frac{2^{2j}r_{B}^{2}}{ct^{2}}\Big)|2^{j}B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

where
S_{j}(B)=2^{j}B\backslash 2^{j-1}B
for
j\geq 2
.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f\chi_{(2B)^{c}})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}\Big[\sum_{j\geq 2}\exp\Big(-\frac{2^{2j}r_{B}^{2}}{ct^{2}}\Big)|2^{j}B|^{s/n}\Big]^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

Case 2:
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. In this case, we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}

\displaystyle\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f-f_{B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f_{B}(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.

Similarly to Case 1, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(f-f_{B})(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

Hence it remains to show that

(46)

\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f_{B}(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

To this end, by Lemma
4.3
and the fact
\rho_{\nu}(x)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
for
x\in B
,

(47)

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f_{B}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|f_{B}|\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}q_{t^{2}}(x,y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|f_{B}|\Big(\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big).

Now let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{k_{0}}r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\leq 2^{k_{0}+1}r_{B}
. Then we have

\displaystyle f_{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}|f_{2^{k+1}B}-f_{2^{k}B}|+|f_{2^{k_{0}+1}B}|.

If
s=0
, then
|f_{2^{k+1}B}-f_{2^{k}B}|\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
for
k=0,\ldots,k_{0}
and
|f_{2^{k_{0}+1}B}|\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
. Hence,

|f_{B}|\lesssim k_{0}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\sim\log\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This, in combination with (
46
) and (
47
), implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f_{B}(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{2}\log^{2}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log^{2}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

If
s\in(0,1)
, then then
|f_{2^{k+1}B}-f_{2^{k}B}|\lesssim|2^{k}B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
for
k=0,\ldots,k_{0}
and
|f_{2^{k_{0}+1}B}|\lesssim|2^{k_{0}}B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
. Hence,

\displaystyle|f_{B}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}|2^{k}B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{sk_{0}/n}|B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)^{s/n}|B|^{s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This, in combination with (
46
) and (
47
), implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f_{B}(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)^{2s/n}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}{r_{B}}\Big)^{2s/n-2}\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (
3
). Let
s\in[0,1)
. If
f\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\sigma>s
, then

(48)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\sigma}}dx<\infty.

Proof.

Let
f\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that
|x_{0}|\leq 1
. Setting

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\sigma}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{S_{k}(B)}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\sigma}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k(n+\sigma)}\int_{2^{k}B}|f(x)|dx.

\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})\leq 1
,
r_{2^{k}B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
for

. Hence,

\int_{2^{k}B}|f(x)|dx\lesssim|2^{k}B|^{1+s/n}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\sim 2^{k(n+s)}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\sigma}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k(\sigma-s)}\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This completes our proof.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
.

,

c

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}|f(y,t)g(y,t)|\frac{dydt}{t}\leq c\left\|\mathcal{A}_{p}(f)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\|\mathcal{C}(g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho_{\nu}
be as in (
3
). Let
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
and
s=n(1/p-1)
.
Then for every
f\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and every
(p,\infty,\rho_{\nu})
-atom
a
,

(49)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx=C_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t},

where
C_{0}=\Big[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ze^{-2z}dz\Big]^{-1}
.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle F(x,t):=t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad G(x,t):=t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x).

Then from Lemma
4.8
and Theorem
2.6
it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{p}(F)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|f\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}(G)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|S_{L}a\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C.

Hence by Lemma
4.10
the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}F(x,t)G(x,y)\frac{dxdt}{t}

is absolutely convergent, that is, the integral on the right-hand side of (
49
) is absolutely convergent. Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx=C_{0}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\\
N\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}dx\frac{dt}{t}.

Formally, by the self-adjointness of
t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
we can write, for each
t>0
,

(50)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}dx.\end{split}

Meanwhile, by the spectral theory we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\\
N\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a\frac{dt}{t}=C_{0}a\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

Consequently,

(51)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx&=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\\
N\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}dx\right]\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\\
N\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)\left[\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}{t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dt}{t}\right]dx\\
&=C_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t}.\end{split}

In view of Lemma
4.9
, to justify the absolute convergence of the integrals in (
50
) and (
51
), it suffices to show that,

(52)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|\leq C_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}

and

(53)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon,N>0\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}\right|\leq C_{a}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\sigma)}.

We first prove (
52
). Indeed, suppose
a
is a
(p,\infty,\rho_{\nu})
-atom associated to the ball
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})
with
r_{B}\leq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
. Then, if
x\in 2B
,

(54)

\begin{split}\left|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|&\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)dy\\
&\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.\end{split}

If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash(2B)
, then
|y-x|\sim|x-x_{B}|
and
\rho_{\nu}(y)\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
for all
y\in B
(due to Lemma
2.1
).
Hence for such
x
we have

(55)

\begin{split}\left|t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\right|&\lesssim\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{c^{\prime}t^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n}}\left(\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{t}\right)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}\left(\frac{t}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\\
&\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})^{\gamma_{\nu}}|x-x_{B}|^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}\|a\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\\
&\lesssim_{a,B}(1+|x|)^{-(n+\gamma_{\nu})}.\end{split}

Combining (
54
) and (
55
) yields (
52
).

Next we prove (
53
). To do this, setting

H_{s}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}):=\int_{s}^{\infty}(u\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{2}e^{-u\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\frac{du}{u},

then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}(2t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{2}e^{-2t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{2\varepsilon^{2}}^{2N^{2}}(u\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{2}e^{-u\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Big[H_{2N^{2}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})a(x)-H_{2\varepsilon^{2}}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})a(x)\Big],

which implies

\sup_{\varepsilon,N>0}\Big|\int_{\varepsilon}^{N}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)\frac{dt}{t}\Big|\lesssim\sup_{t>0}|H_{t}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})a(x)|.

On the other hand, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle H_{t}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}):=\int_{t}^{\infty}(u\mathcal{L}_{\nu})^{2}e^{-u\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\frac{du}{u}=-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}-e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}.

At this stage, arguing similarly to the proof of (
52
), we obtain (
53
).

This completes our proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.

Proof of
BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}
.

Let
f\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and let
a
be a
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
-atoms. Then
by Lemma
4.11
, Lemma
4.10
and Lemma
4.8
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x){t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right|

Step 2.

Proof of
(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}\subset BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Let
\{\psi_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
and

as in Corollary
2.2

Set
B_{\xi}:=B(x_{\xi},\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi}))
and we will claim that for any

and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
\psi_{\xi}f\in H^{p}_{L}(G)

(56)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\xi}f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\left|B_{\xi}\right|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality,

(57)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}L}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{p}(4B_{\xi})}&\leq|4B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}L}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{2}(4B_{\xi})}\\

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B_{\xi}

3.10
we get

\displaystyle\big|e^{-t^{2}L}(\psi_{\xi}f)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{\xi})^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{\xi}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

which implies that

(58)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B_{\xi})}\lesssim|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},\end{split}

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Combining (
57
) and (
58
) yields (
56
).

Assume that
\ell\in(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}

For each index
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
we define

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}f:=\ell(\psi_{\xi}f),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

By (
56
),

\displaystyle|\ell_{\xi}(f)|\leq C\|\psi_{\xi}f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Hence there exists
g_{\xi}\in L^{2}(B_{\xi})
such that

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}(f)=\int_{B_{\xi}}f(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

We define
g=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}1_{B_{\xi}}g_{\xi}
. Then, if
f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
a_{i}
is a
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
-atom,

, we have

\displaystyle\ell(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\ell(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\ell(\psi_{\xi}a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\ell_{\xi}(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{B_{\xi}}a_{i}(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}g(x)a_{i}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}}f(x)g(x)dx.

Suppose that
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})

0\not\equiv f\in L_{0}^{2}(B)

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that

and
\displaystyle\int_{B}f(x)dx=0

|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}f

(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
-atom. Hence

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\left|\int_{B}fg\right|\leq\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

But by elementary functional analysis (see Folland and Stein
[

, p. 145]
),

\|g\|_{(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}}=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|g-c\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

Hence

(59)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:ball\\
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|g-c\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

Moreover, if
B
is a ball with
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
f\not\equiv 0

and
\operatorname{supp}f\subset B
, then
|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}f
is a
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
-atom and

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}fg\right|\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

(60)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm ball}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|g\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|L\|_{(H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

From (
59
) and (
60
) it follows that
g\in BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{BMO^{s}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}},\quad\mbox{where }s=n(1/p-1).

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is thus complete.
∎

5.
Boundedness of Riesz transforms on Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}

This section is devoted to proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.6
. We first establish some estimates for the kernel of the Riesz transforms.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
.

(a)

Denote by
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
. Then we have, for
x\neq y
and
j=1,\ldots,n
,

(61)

|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)},

(62)

|\partial_{y_{j}}\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

and

(63)

|\partial_{x_{j}}\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

(b)

For
j=1,\ldots,n
, denote by
\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}
. Then we have, for
x\neq y
and
k=1,\ldots,n
,

(64)

|\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)},

and

(65)

|\partial_{y_{k}}\delta_{j}^{*}\mathcal{(}\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Proof.

(a) By Proposition
3.10
, we have

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Similarly, by using Proposition
3.11
we obtain the second and the third estimates.

(b) The proof of (b) is similar by using Propositions
3.12
and
3.13
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
.

(a)

For
t>0
, denote by
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
. Then we have, for
x\neq y
and
j=1,\ldots,n
,

|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

and

|\partial_{y_{j}}\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

(b)

For
t>0
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, denote by
\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}
. Then we have, for
x\neq y
and
k=1,\ldots,n
,

|\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

and

|\partial_{y_{k}}\delta_{j}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(x,y)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

Proof.

We only give the proof of (a) since the proof of (b) can be done similarly.

We have

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{u}\delta p_{t+u}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{u}{t+u}\Big)^{1/2}\frac{1}{(t+u)^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\ldots+\int_{t}^{\infty}\ldots

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

For the term
E_{1}
, since
t+u\sim t
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{u}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

For the term
E_{2}
, we have
t+u\sim u
in this situation. Hence,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{cu}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}.

The second estimate can be done similarly by using Proposition
3.11
in place of Proposition
3.6
and hence we omit the details.

This completes our proof.
∎

We are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

From (
61
), we have

|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y.

We now prove the Hölder’s continuity of the Riesz kernel. If
|y-y^{\prime}|\geq\max\{\rho_{\nu}(y),\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})\}
, then from (
61
) we have

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)|+|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big[\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}+\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}

If
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\max\{\rho_{\nu}(y),\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})\}
, then by Lemma
2.1
we have
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(y^{\prime})
; moreover, for every
z\in B(y,2|y-y^{\prime}|)
,
\rho_{\nu}(z)\sim\rho_{\nu}(y)
. This, together with the mean value theorem and (
62
), gives

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|y-y^{\prime}|\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

Since
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\rho_{\nu}(y)
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho_{\nu}(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

where and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
.

For the same reason, since
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y)-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

Similarly, by using (
63
) we will obtain

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(y,x)-\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

This completes our proof.
∎

We now give the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
:

Fix
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(i) By Proposition
4.5
, Remark
4.4
(i) and Theorem
1.4
,
H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Consequently, it suffices to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
atoms
a
.

Let
a
be a
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
atom associated to a ball
B
. We have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Using the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}f|
and the Riesz transform
\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
and the Hölder’s inequality, by the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, we first note that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
we have, for
t>0
,

e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}e^{-t(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)}\langle f,\varphi^{\nu}_{k}\rangle\varphi^{\nu}_{k}.

Using this and (
1
), by a simple calculation we come up with

e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}f=\delta_{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f

which implies

e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a=\delta_{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}a.

Hence, it deduces to prove

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1.

We consider two cases.

Case 1:
r_{B}=\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
.
By Proposition
5.2
(a), for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{B}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}|B|^{1-1/p},

where in the second inequality we used the fact
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
for
y\in B
(due to Lemma
2.1
).

It follows that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Case 2:
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
.

Using the cancellation property
\int a(x)dx=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}[\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,y)-\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(x,x_{B})]a(y)dy\Big|.

By mean value theorem, Proposition
5.2
(a) and the fact that
\rho_{\nu}(y_{j})\gtrsim\rho_{\nu}(x_{0})
and
\rho_{\nu}(y)\sim\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
for all
y\in B
, we have, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\Big(\frac{|y-x_{B}|}{|x-y|}+\frac{|y-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}+\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B}\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle=F_{1}+F_{2}.

For the term
F_{1}
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|}|B|^{1-1/p},

where in the last inequality we used

\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\leq\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}},

since both
\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}
and
\gamma_{\nu}
are less than or equal to
1
.

For
F_{2}
, since
r_{B}<\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})
and
\gamma_{\nu}\leq 1
, we have
\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\leq\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho_{\nu}(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Taking this and the estimate of
F_{1}
into account then we obtain

\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}

for all
x\in(4B)^{c}
.
Consequently,

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

(ii) By the duality in Theorem
1.5
, it suffices to prove that the conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}
is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. By Theorem
1.4
, we need only to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
atoms
a
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,2,\rho_{\nu})
atom associated to a ball
B
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Since
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|
and
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, by the Hölder’s inequality and the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, we first note that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
we have, for
t>0
,

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{e^{-t(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)}}{\sqrt{4|k|+2|\nu|+2n}}\langle f,\varphi^{\nu}_{k}\rangle\varphi^{\nu}_{k}.

Using this and (
1
), by a simple calculation we come up with

e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a=\delta^{*}_{j}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}a.

At this stage, arguing similarly to the proof of (i), but using Proposition
5.2
(b) instead of Proposition
5.2
(a), we also come up with

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}=\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|\delta^{*}_{j}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

This completes our proof.
∎

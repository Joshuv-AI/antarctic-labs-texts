Analysis of a nonlinear free boundary problem modeling the radial growth of two-layer tumors
Junde Wu †, Hao Xu †, Yuehong Zhuang ‡

Abstract
In this paper we study a nonlinear free boundary problem on the radial growth of a two-layer solid tumor with a quiescent core. The tumor surface and its inner interface separating the proliferating cells and the quiescent cells are both free boundaries. By deeply analyzing their relationship and employing the maximum principle, we show this problem is globally well-posed and prove the existence of a unique positive threshold σ ∗ \sigma^{*} such that the problem admits a unique stationary solution with a quiescent core if and only if the externally supplied nutrient σ ¯ > σ ∗ \bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*} . The stationary solution is globally asymptotically stable. The formation of the quiescent core and its interesting connection with the necrotic core are also given. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 35B40; 35R35; 35Q92 Keywords : free boundary problem; stationary solution; two-layer tumor; stability

Introduction

In this paper we study a free boundary problem modeling the growth of a radially symmetric tumor with a layer of proliferating cells surrounding a central core full of quiescent cells. The model reads as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{r}\sigma=f(\sigma)H(\sigma-\sigma_{Q})+h(\sigma)\big(1-H(\sigma-\sigma_{Q})\big)\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<R(t),\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma_{r}(0,t)=0,\quad\sigma(R(t),t)=\bar{\sigma}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle R^{2}(t){\frac{dR(t)}{dt}}=\int_{\sigma(r,t)>\sigma_{Q}}g(\sigma(r,t))r^{2}dr-\int_{\sigma(r,t)\leq\sigma_{Q}}\nu r^{2}dr\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
R(0)=R_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\sigma=\sigma(r,t)
is the unknown concentration of the nutrient distributing in the tumor region and
R(t)
is the unknown tumor radius over time
t>0
, with initial radius given by
R_{0}>0
.
The radial Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is denoted by
\Delta_{r}={\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}}+{\frac{2}{r}}{\frac{d}{dr}}
with
r=|x|
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the Heaviside function
H(\cdot)
is defined by
H(s)=1
for
s>0
and
H(s)=0
for
s\leq 0
.
In this model the tumor is supposed to have two layers at some stage of tumor evolution, and we assume to distinguish the different cell layers of the tumor tissue by a critical constant
\sigma_{Q}>0
. This constant represents a threshold value of nutrient concentration for the tumor cells transitioning between the proliferating phase and the quiescent phase.
In this setting, the region
\{\sigma(r,t)>\sigma_{Q}\}
is occupied by the proliferating cells, and the region
\{\sigma(r,t)\leq\sigma_{Q}\}
consists of all quiescent cells which, in a certain time span, may form a quiescent core.
The tumor is supplied by the nutrients from the external micro-environment via its surface at a constant level
\bar{\sigma}>0
, which gives
\sigma=\bar{\sigma}
on
r=R(t)
.
The first equation of (
1.1
) accounts for the diffusion of nutrients and their net consumption by proliferating cells and quiescent cells at the rates of

f(\sigma)
and
h(\sigma)
, respectively, where
f
and
h
are given functions satisfying some biological assumptions.
The third line of (
1.1
) stands for the evolution of the tumor’s volume over time, where
g
is another given function, which is usually referred to as
proliferation rate function
for the tumor cells, and
\nu>0
is the constant removal rate of quiescent cells. Typically,
f
,
h
and
g
are given by linear functions, cf.
[
2
,
8
,
12
]
, with the following form:

f(\sigma)=\lambda_{1}\sigma,\qquad h(\sigma)=\lambda_{2}\sigma,\qquad g(\sigma)=\mu(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})

(1.2)

with positive constants
\mu,\tilde{\sigma},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
fulfilling
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}
.
In this paper we intend to investigate the essential characteristics of the growth of the two-layer tumors upon more general biological parameter rate functions, so we shall always assume the following conditions (A1)–(A3) hold:

(\rm A1)

f
and
h
are both strictly increasing
C^{1}
functions on
[\sigma_{0},+\infty)
with bounded derivatives, which also satisfy
f(\sigma_{0})=h(\sigma_{0})=0

and
f(\sigma_{Q})\geq h(\sigma_{Q})
.

(\rm A2)

g
is a strictly increasing
C^{1}
function on
[\sigma_{0},+\infty)
with a unique
\tilde{\sigma}
such that
g(\tilde{\sigma})=0
.

(\rm A3)

0\leq\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{Q}<\tilde{\sigma}
and

g(\sigma_{Q})+\nu\geq 0
.

The above constant
\sigma_{0}
refers to a specific nutrient level at which the transport rate of nutrients via the capillary network offsets the consumption rate of nutrients by the tumor cells; cf.
[
12
]
with
\sigma_{0}=\frac{\sigma_{B}\varGamma}{\varGamma+\lambda_{0}}
.
In (A1), the monotonicity of
f
and
h
signifies that the net nutrient consumption rates by the proliferating and quiescent cells are both strictly increasing for
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
.
The inequality
f(\sigma_{Q})\geq h(\sigma_{Q})
comes from the biological observation that the proliferating cells always consume nutrients more
than the quiescent cells.
The positive constant
\tilde{\sigma}
in
(\rm A2)

represents a threshold value
of nutrient concentration at which
the birth and the death rates of proliferating cells are in balance.
The last inequality in
(\rm A3)
means the proliferating cells always
grow faster than the quiescent cells.
All these assumptions are biologically meaningful,
cf.
[
4
,
17
,
24
]
. The discontinuity of both the consumption rate and proliferation rate functions makes this kind of two-phase free boundary problem very difficult.

Multicellular tumor spheroids with concentric shells of proliferating, quiescent and necrotic cells can be observed in in vitro experiments, cf.
[
2
]
. The above model (
1.1
) is a simplified version of the three-layer tumor model proposed by Byrne and Chaplain
[
3
]
. In the special case
h(\sigma)\equiv 0
, this model can be
regarded as a necrotic tumor model, for which the global well-posedness and long-time behavior of the radial transient solutions have been established, eg.,
[
5
,
1
,
8
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
,
and moreover, the symmetry-breaking solutions and asymptotic
stability of the radial stationary solutions in non-radial
case have also been well explored, see

[
16
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
If further
\sigma_{Q}\leq\sigma_{0}
, then problem (
1.1
) becomes a one-layer tumor model with only proliferating cells. For many interesting and illuminating results of such models we refer the reader to
[
4
,
6
,

,

,

,

,

,
14
,
15
,
20
,
28
,
29
]
and the references cited therein.

Recently,
for the linear case (
1.2
), Liu and Zhuang
[
17
]
established the existence and uniqueness of the stationary solution and investigated the asymptotic behavior of the transient solutions to problem (
1.1
) by explicitly solving the expression of nutrient concentration
\sigma(r,t)
in terms of tumor radius
R(t)
and quiescent core radius
\rho(t)
.
The dynamics for this linear two-layer tumor model with a time lag in cell proliferation was considered in
[
18
]
.
By assuming that the quiescent cells linearly consume nutrients at the same rate
as the proliferating cells, but with different growth rates, Zheng, Li and Zhuang

[
27
]
also studied a simplified three-layer tumor model with a necrotic core.
The dependency relationship between the two unknown interfaces was carefully analyzed and the figures on the global evolution of tumor and the mutual transitions of its cell-layer structures were shown. Still, their work was based on the explicit expression of
\sigma(r,t)
.

In this work we give rigorous analysis to problem (
1.1
) with nonlinear increasing functions
f
,
h
and
g
with conditions
(\rm A1)
–
(\rm A3)
.
In this general case, there is no explicit expression for
\sigma
, and more relevantly,
\sigma_{r}
at the inner interface turns out to be a function of the quiescent core radius
\rho
, which greatly differs from that in the necrotic tumor model where
\sigma_{r}
at
\rho
is always zero. This phenomenon makes the problem more complicated and it requires a new analysis strategy.
We develop a general method to handle the two-layer problem of this kind, not only just available for the tumor model with a quiescent core,
but also for the necrotic model.
Motivated by
[
1
,
24
]
,
for any given
\rho>0
, we shall first solve the elliptic problem for
\sigma
in quiescent region
\{r\leq\rho\}
, then with
\sigma_{r}(\rho)
obtained, we carry on to solve
\sigma
in
the region
\{r>\rho\}
. By utilizing a local existence method and a shooting method, we finally obtain the tumor radius
R=R(\rho)
in terms of
\rho
.
Based on the maximum principle, we establish a
comprehensive and deep analysis on the relationship between

\sigma
,
\rho
and
R
, and transform problem (
1.1
) into an abstract Cauchy problem of ordinary differential equation. Existence and uniqueness of the stationary solution is explored and the long-time behavior of the transient solution is studied.
We find out a critical nutrient value
\sigma^{*}\in(\tilde{\sigma},+\infty)
such that problem
(\ref{1.1})
has a unique stationary solution with a quiescent core if and only if
\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
. This stationary solution is globally asymptotically stable under radial perturbations.
Our analysis also implies that
we can control tumor growth by adjusting the external nutrient supply
\bar{\sigma}
.

We notice that the nutrient concentration
\sigma
is strictly convex with respect to
r
.
By fully exploiting this important geometric property, we derive some estimates on
the steady tumor radius
R_{s}
and the steady quiescent core radius
\rho_{s}
.
We also observe that the tumor model (
1.1
) with a quiescent core formally converges to the necrotic tumor model if
h(\sigma_{Q})
vanishes, which arises our interest in finding some connections between these two models.

By introducing a new auxiliary problem (
4.3
), we are able to control the influence of consumption rate
h
on the nutrient concentration
\sigma
in the proliferating region, and prove that the stationary solution of problem (
1.1
) actually converges to the stationary one of the necrotic model as
h(\sigma)
tends to zero.
The connection between the transient solution of (
1.1
) and the transient one of the necrotic model possesses a similar convergent property.
These facts suggest that in the radially symmetric setting, we can unify these two kinds of tumor models as one kind of two-layer tumor model from a mathematical view.

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows.
In the next section we study stationary solutions of problem (
1.1
) and
give some estimates for the stationary tumor radius
R_{s}

under additional conditions on the proliferation rate function
g(\sigma)
.
In Section
3
, we reduce free boundary problem (
1.1
)
into a Cauchy problem of ordinary differential equation and
study asymptotic behavior of transient solutions. In the last section we
analyze the connection between the tumor models with a quiescent core
and with a necrotic core.

2
Stationary solutions

In this section, we study the existence of
stationary solutions of problem (
1.1
) and derive some estimates
on the steady radius of the tumor.

As is quite similar to the case of necrotic tumor model,
there are two kinds of stationary
solutions to problem
(
1.1
). One kind of the stationary solution has a quiescent core
with radius
\rho
, and satisfies the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)+{\frac{2}{r}}\sigma^{\prime}(r)=h(\sigma)\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma^{\prime}(0)=0,\;\;\sigma(\rho)=\sigma_{Q}\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)+{\frac{2}{r}}\sigma^{\prime}(r)=f(\sigma)\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<r<R,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma^{\prime}(\rho-0)=\sigma^{\prime}(\rho+0),\qquad\sigma(R)=\bar{\sigma},\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\rho}^{R}g(\sigma(r))r^{2}dr-{\nu\over 3}\rho^{3}=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

The other kind of the stationary solution has no quiescent cores,
and satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)+{2\over r}\sigma^{\prime}(r)=f(\sigma)\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<R,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad\sigma(R)=\bar{\sigma},\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma(0)\geq\sigma_{Q},\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}g(\sigma(r))r^{2}dr=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

In order to solve these two problems, we first consider the following
semi-linear elliptic boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}(r)+{2\over r}v^{\prime}(r)=h(v(r))\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
v^{\prime}(0)=0,\;\;v(\rho)=\sigma_{Q}.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
(\rm A1)
holds.
For any
\rho>0
, problem
(\ref{2.3})

has a unique solution
v=V(r,\rho)
for
0\leq r\leq\rho

and the following properties hold:

(i)

V
is strictly increasing in
r
, and

\sigma_{0}<V(r,\rho)\leq\sigma_{Q}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq r\leq\rho.

(ii)

V
is strictly convex in
r
, i.e.,

V_{rr}(r,\rho)>0\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq r\leq\rho.

(iii)

V
is strictly decreasing in
\rho
. Moreover,
by letting

\Phi(\rho):=V_{r}(\rho,\rho)=\displaystyle{1\over\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{\rho}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho>0,

we have

\displaystyle 0<\Phi(\rho)<{1\over 3}h(\sigma_{Q})\rho\quad\textit{and}\quad\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)>0.

(2.4)

Proof..

Since
h(v)
is strictly increasing, we can easily verify that

\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{Q}
are a pair of lower and upper solutions
of problem
(\ref{2.3})
.
Hence by the upper and lower solution method,
we see that there exists
a unique solution
v(r)=V(r,\rho)
such that

\sigma_{0}<V(r,\rho)<\sigma_{Q}
for all
0<r<\rho
.
Due to
(\rm A1)
, we have
h(V(r,\rho))>0
, which implies

v^{\prime}(r)={1\over r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds>0\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r\leq\rho.

(2.5)

This proves the assertion
(i)
.
From the above relation we can also get

v^{\prime\prime}(r)-\displaystyle\frac{v^{\prime}(r)}{r}=h(V(r,\rho))-\frac{3}{r^{3}}\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds\geq 0,

and the assertion
(ii)
follows.

Denote
\displaystyle v_{\rho}(r)={\partial V\over\partial\rho}(r,\rho)
. From (
2.3
) we see
v_{\rho}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(v_{\rho})_{rr}+{2\over r}(v_{\rho})_{r}=h^{\prime}(V)v_{\rho}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
(v_{\rho})_{r}(0)=0,\;\;v_{\rho}(\rho)=-V_{r}(\rho,\rho)<0.\end{array}\right.

Then by the maximum principle we have
v_{\rho}(r)<0
for
0<r<\rho
.
Similarly, there holds

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(v_{r})_{rr}+{2\over r}(v_{r})_{r}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}v_{r}=h^{\prime}(V)v_{r}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
v_{r}(0)=0,\;\;v_{r}(\rho)>0.\end{array}\right.

Denote
w(r)=v_{r}(r)+v_{\rho}(r)
.
It satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle w_{rr}+{2\over r}w_{r}=h^{\prime}(V)w+\frac{2}{r^{2}}v_{r}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
w(\rho)=0.\end{array}\right.

Note that
h^{\prime}(V)\geq 0
and
v_{r}>0
, then by using the strong
maximum principle, we obtain

\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)=w_{r}(\rho)>0
.
The relation
0<\Phi(\rho)<{\frac{1}{3}}h(\sigma_{Q})\rho
comes from (
2.5
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Since
\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)={1\over\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{\rho}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds
for

\rho>0
, by continuity we let
\Phi(0)=0
.

Next, we employ a shooting method to solve
\sigma
in the proliferating region

\{\rho<r<R\}
. For any given
\rho\geq 0
,
we consider the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}(r)+{2\over r}v^{\prime}(r)=f(v(r))\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;r>\rho,\\[8.5359pt]
v(\rho)=\sigma_{Q},\\[8.5359pt]
v^{\prime}(\rho)=\Phi(\rho).\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
(\rm A1)
holds.
For any
\rho\geq 0
, problem
(\ref{2.5})

has a unique maximal solution
v=\mathcal{V}(r,\rho)\in C^{2}(\left[\rho,R_{\infty}\right);\mathbb{R})
and the
following properties hold:

(i)

\mathcal{V}
is strictly increasing in
r
,
and

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to R_{\infty}-0}\mathcal{V}(r,\rho)=+\infty.

(ii)

\mathcal{V}
is strictly convex in
r
, i.e.,

\mathcal{V}_{rr}(r,\rho)>0\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho\leq r<R_{\infty}.

(iii)

\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}_{r}
are both strictly decreasing
in
\rho
.

Proof..

We first show the local existence and uniqueness of problem
(
2.6
). Let
b>0
and

M:=\max_{[\sigma_{Q},\sigma_{Q}+b]}\{|f(u)|+|f^{\prime}(u)|\},\qquad h:=\min\Big\{\frac{b}{2h(\sigma_{Q})(\rho+1)},\sqrt{\frac{1}{3M}},\sqrt{\frac{b}{2M}}\Big\}.

Set

\mathscr{B}:=\{u\in C([\rho,\rho+h]):\sigma_{Q}\leq u(r)\leq\sigma_{Q}+b\}.

Obviously,
\mathscr{B}
is a closed subspace of Banach space
C({[\rho,\rho+h]})
.
One can easily verify that problem
(\ref{2.5})
is equivalent to the following integral equation

v(r)=\sigma_{Q}+\rho^{2}\Phi(\rho)(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{r})+\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{\rho}^{\alpha}s^{2}f(v(s))dsd\alpha.

(2.7)

Define a mapping
T:\mathscr{B}\rightarrow C([\rho,\rho+h])
by

Tu(r):=\sigma_{Q}+\rho^{2}\Phi(\rho)(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{1}{r})+\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{\rho}^{\alpha}s^{2}f(u(s))dsd\alpha\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;u\in\mathscr{B}.

Then for any
u\in\mathscr{B}
, it follows from (A1) and

(
2.4
)
that

\sigma_{Q}\leq Tu(r)\leq\sigma_{Q}+\frac{1}{3}h(\sigma_{Q})\rho h+Mh^{2}<\sigma_{Q}+b\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\rho\leq r\leq\rho+h,

hence
T(\mathscr{B})\subset\mathscr{B}
.
Moreover,
T
is a contraction mapping for the reason that

\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} \|Tu-Tv\|\ &\leq\sup_{[\rho,\rho+h]}\int_{\rho}^{r}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{\rho}^{\alpha}s^{2}|f(u(s))-f(v(s))|dsd\alpha\\
&\leq Mh^{2}\|u-v\|\leq\frac{1}{3}\|u-v\|\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;u,v\in\mathscr{B}.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}

By Banach fixed point theorem,

T
has a unique fixed point
v(r)=\mathcal{V}(r,\rho)
in
\mathscr{B}
,
which is the unique local solution of problem
(\ref{2.5})
.

Proceeding as in Lemma 2.1
(i)
, we see that
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}_{r}

are both strictly increasing in
r
for
r\geq\rho
. By extension we obtain the maximal existence interval
[\rho,R_{\infty})
, where
R_{\infty}\in(\rho,+\infty]
,
and we easily get the assertion
(i)
.

On the other hand, from
(\ref{2.6})
and (A1), we also have

\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} \mathcal{V}_{rr}(r,\rho)-\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho)}{r}&=f(\mathcal{V}(r,\rho))-\displaystyle\frac{3}{r^{3}}\rho^{2}\Phi(\rho)-\displaystyle\frac{3}{r^{3}}\int_{\rho}^{r}s^{2}f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho))ds\\
&\geq\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{3}}{r^{3}}(f(\sigma_{Q})-h(\sigma_{Q}))\geq 0.\end{aligned}\end{matrix}

Hence the assertion
(ii)
holds.

Finally, we observe that for
r>\rho
,

r^{2}\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho)=\rho^{2}\Phi(\rho)+\int_{\rho}^{r}s^{2}f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho))ds=\int_{0}^{\rho}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds+\int_{\rho}^{r}s^{2}f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho))ds.

(2.8)

Then by (A1), Lemma 2.1
(iii)
and a comparison argument,
we easily get
that
\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho)
is strictly decreasing in
\rho
,
and thus
\mathcal{V}(r,\rho)
is also strictly decreasing.
In fact,
if
\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
, from (
2.8
) we have for
r>\rho_{2}
,

\displaystyle r^{2}\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{1})-r^{2}\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{2})=

\displaystyle\,\,\int_{0}^{\rho_{1}}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho_{1}))ds+\left(\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}+\int_{\rho_{2}}^{r}\right)s^{2}f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho_{1}))ds

\displaystyle\,-\left(\int_{0}^{\rho_{1}}+\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}\right)s^{2}h(V(s,\rho_{2}))ds-\int_{\rho_{2}}^{r}s^{2}f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho_{2}))ds.

Since
V(s,\rho_{1})>V(s,\rho_{2})
ensured by Lemma
2.1
for
s\in(0,\rho_{1})
and
\mathcal{V}(s,\rho_{1})>\sigma_{Q}>V(s,\rho_{2})
for
s\in(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
, we deduce from the above relation and the properties of
f
and
h
that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\,\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{1})-\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{2})>\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\rho_{2}}^{r}s^{2}\big[f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho_{1}))-f(\mathcal{V}(s,\rho_{2}))\big]ds\quad\mbox{for}\;\;r>\rho_{2},\\[8.5359pt]
\,\mathcal{V}(\rho_{2},\rho_{1})-\mathcal{V}(\rho_{2},\rho_{2})>0.\end{array}\right.

This implies that both
\mathcal{V}(r,\rho_{1})>\mathcal{V}(r,\rho_{2})
and
\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{1})>\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho_{2})
for all
r>\rho_{2}
by iteration argument.
It follows that
\mathcal{V}(r,\rho)
and
\mathcal{V}_{r}(r,\rho)
are both decreasing in
\rho
. The assertion
(iii)
follows and
the proof is complete.∎

(\sigma,R)=(\Sigma(r,\rho,R(\rho)),R(\rho))

,

\Sigma(r,\rho,R)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}V(r,\rho)\quad\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq r\leq\rho,\\[8.5359pt]

(2.10)

From Lemma 2.2
(iii)
, we have
R(\rho)
is strictly increasing in
\rho
.
Define

R_{c}:=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}R(\rho).

(2.11)

It is the critical radius such that problem
(\ref{2.2})_{1}
–
(\ref{2.2})_{2}

with
\rho=0

\sigma(0)=\sigma_{Q}
for

. Obviously,

.

Clearly, the mapping
\rho\mapsto R(\rho)
is a 1-1 correspondence. So we can also regard
\rho
as a function of the variable

, i.e.,

\rho=\rho(R)
for

\sigma=\Sigma(r,\rho(R),R)

We make the variable transformation

\eta(R)=\rho(R)/R,\qquad U(s,\eta(R),R)=\Sigma(sR,\eta(R)R,R).

Then

t\geq T
.

(ii)
If
\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
, then for any initial radius
R_{0}<R_{c}
,
there exists a finite time
T>0
such that the tumor is in
proliferating one-layer state for
0<t<T
, and
in proliferating-quiescent two-layer state for
t\geq T
.

Proof..

Suppose
\sigma_{Q}<\bar{\sigma}<\sigma^{*}

and
R_{0}>R_{c}
. By Lemma
2.3
we see at time
t=0
, the tumor
has a quiescent core with radius
\rho_{0}=\rho(R_{0})
and

F(R_{0})<0
. By (
2.17
), (
2.23
) and Theorem
2.5
,
R(t)

is strictly decreasing and converges to
R_{s}\in[0,R_{c})
as
t
goes to infinity.
Hence there
exists a time
T>0
such that
R(T)=R_{c}
with the properties that
R(t)>R_{c}

for
t<T
and
R(t)<R_{c}
for
t>T
. The assertion
(i)
follows.
The assertion
(ii)
can be proved in a similar way.
∎

4
Connections with the necrotic core

In this section, we show the connections between the tumor model
with a quiescent core and the tumor model with a necrotic core.

For simplicity of notation, we take
\sigma_{Q}=\hat{\sigma}
, where
\hat{\sigma}
is regarded as a threshold value of nutrient concentration for cell necrosis, cf.
[
5
]
, and with this, the necrotic region can be expressed by

\{\sigma\leq\hat{\sigma}\}
.
In the case
h(\sigma)\equiv 0
, problem (
1.1
) becomes the
following necrotic tumor model:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{r}\sigma=f(\sigma)H(\sigma-\hat{\sigma})\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<R(t),\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma_{r}(0,t)=0,\quad\sigma(R(t),t)=\bar{\sigma}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle R^{2}(t){dR(t)\over dt}=\int_{\sigma(r,t)>\hat{\sigma}}g(\sigma(r,t))r^{2}dr-\int_{\sigma(r,t)\leq\hat{\sigma}}\nu r^{2}dr\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;t>0,\\[8.5359pt]
R(0)=R_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

The above model (
4.1
) has been well studied in
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
24
]
.
By comparing Lemma
2.4
, Theorem
2.5
with
[
24
, Theorem 2.3]
, we find that, both tumor models share the same constants
\sigma^{*}\in(\hat{\sigma},+\infty)
and
R_{c}
(depending on
\bar{\sigma}
) because
f
and
g
keep the same assumptions in the models. As is illustrated in
[
24
]
,
for
\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
, problem (
4.1
) has a unique stationary
necrotic solution
(\sigma_{nec}(r),\rho_{nec},R_{nec})
with
R_{nec}>\rho_{nec}>0
.

In the rest of this paper, we always assume
\bar{\sigma}>\hat{\sigma}
.
Define

\|h\|:=\max_{\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma\leq\hat{\sigma}}{|h(\sigma)|}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;h\in C[\sigma_{0},\hat{\sigma}].

(4.2)

Under the condition
(\rm A1)
, we have
\|h\|=h(\hat{\sigma})
.
We rewrite the corresponding stationary radius
R_{s}
of problem (
1.1
) with
h
by

R_{s}=R_{s,h}
. Clearly, problem (
1.1
) formally converges
to problem (
4.1
) if
\|h\|
vanishes. It is an interesting question whether

R_{s,h}
will converge to
R_{nec}
. In this section, we shall
give an affirmative answer.

One might have noticed from Lemma
2.2
that, the value
\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)
plays a role in solving nutrient concentration
\sigma
in the proliferating region,
and it actually depends on
the functions
h
and
\rho
. To clarify the dependence of

\sigma
on
\rho
, we consider the following auxiliary problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)+{2\over r}\sigma^{\prime}(r)=f(\sigma(r))\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<r<R,\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)=K\rho,\;\;\sigma(\rho)=\hat{\sigma},\\[8.5359pt]
\sigma(R)=\bar{\sigma},\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

where
f(\sigma)
satisfies
(\rm A1)
,
K\geq 0
and

\bar{\sigma}>\hat{\sigma}>\sigma_{0}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
(\rm A1)
and

\bar{\sigma}>\hat{\sigma}>\sigma_{0}
hold.
For any given
\rho\geq 0
and
\displaystyle 0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
,
problem
(\ref{4.3})
has a unique solution

(\sigma(r,\rho,K),R(\rho,K))
, and there hold the following assertions:

(i)

\sigma(r,\rho,K)
is strictly increasing in
r
and strictly convex in
r
.

(ii)

\sigma(r,\rho,K)
is strictly increasing in
K
,
and
R(\rho,K)
is strictly decreasing in
K
.

(iii)

\sigma(r,\rho,K)
is strictly decreasing in
\rho
,
and
R(\rho,K)
is strictly increasing in
\rho
.

Proof..

Proceeding as in Section
2
where we dealt with problem (
2.6
) for
\rho<r<R
, we can solve the first two lines of problem

(\ref{4.3})
to get the unique solution
\sigma(r,\rho,K)
,
then by solving the third equation
\sigma(R,\rho,K)=\bar{\sigma}
to obtain the unique
R=R(\rho,K)\in(\rho,+\infty)
. This gives the
unique solution

(\sigma(r,\rho,K),R(\rho,K))
for
\rho\leq r\leq R(\rho,K)
.

(i)
Similarly to Lemma
2.2
, we see
\sigma(r,\rho,K)\geq\hat{\sigma}
for
r\geq\rho
,
and
\sigma(r,\rho,K)
is strictly increasing in
r
. It is easy to see that

\sigma^{\prime}(r)={1\over r^{2}}\Big(K\rho^{3}+\int_{\rho}^{r}f(\sigma(s))s^{2}ds\Big)>0.

From the monotonicity of
f
and
\sigma(r)\geq\hat{\sigma}
for
r\leq\rho
,
we have

\sigma^{\prime\prime}(r)=f(\sigma(r))-{2\over r}\sigma^{\prime}(r)\geq{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})+{2\rho^{3}\over r^{3}}\Big({1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})-K\Big)>0\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;r\geq\rho,

which means
\sigma(r,\rho,K)
is strictly convex in
r
.

(ii)
For any
r>\rho
, we see that
v(r):={\partial\sigma\over\partial K}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}+{2\over s}v^{\prime}=f^{\prime}(\sigma)v\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<s<r,\\[8.5359pt]
v^{\prime}(\rho)=\rho,\qquad v(\rho)=0.\end{array}\right.

Since
f^{\prime}(\sigma)>0
, it follows from the strong maximum principle that

\max\limits_{\rho\leq s\leq r}v(s)=v(r)>0,

(4.4)

i.e.,
{\partial\sigma\over\partial K}>0
. Then from equation

\sigma(R,\rho,K)=\bar{\sigma}
, we get
{\partial R\over\partial K}=-{\partial\sigma\over\partial K}/{\partial\sigma\over\partial R}<0.

(iii)
From
(4.3)
we see that
w(r)={\partial\sigma\over\partial\rho}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w^{\prime\prime}(s)+{2\over s}w^{\prime}(s)=f^{\prime}(\sigma(s))w(s)\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<s<r,\\[8.5359pt]
w^{\prime}(\rho)=K-\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=3K-f(\hat{\sigma})<0,\\[8.5359pt]
w(\rho)=-\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)=-K\rho\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

Again, it follows from the strong maximum principle that

\min_{\rho\leq s\leq r}w(s)=w(r)<0,\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;r>\rho\geq 0.

(4.5)

This implies
{\partial\sigma\over\partial\rho}<0
and, in a similar manner, we obtain

{\partial R\over\partial\rho}>0
for
\rho\geq 0
.
∎

From Lemma
4.1
,
\sigma(r,\rho,K)
and
R(\rho,K)
is continuous and we have

\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}\sigma(r,\rho,K)=\sigma(r,\rho,0),\qquad\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}R(\rho,K)=R(\rho,0),

(4.6)

in which, due to the monotonicity,

(\sigma(r,\rho,0),R(\rho,0))
satisfies problem (
4.3
) for
\rho\geq 0
and

K=0
.

By comparing problem (
2.6
) and problem (
4.3
), from definition (
2.11
) we see

R_{c}=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}R(\rho,K)\qquad\mbox{for all}\;\;0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma}).

(4.7)

Then from the equation
\sigma(R,\rho,K)=\bar{\sigma}
, we can get

\rho=\rho(R,K)
,
and

{\partial\rho\over\partial R}>0,\qquad{\partial\rho\over\partial K}>0\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;R\geq R_{c},\;0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma}).

(4.8)

Similarly, we have

\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}\rho(R,K)=\rho(R,0),\qquad\rho(R_{c},K)=0.

(4.9)

Next, we let

\begin{matrix}\begin{aligned} F(R,K)&=\frac{1}{R^{3}}\Big(\int_{\rho(R,K)}^{R}g(\sigma(r,\rho(R,K),K))r^{2}dr-\frac{\nu}{3}\rho^{3}(R,K)\Big)\\
&=\int_{\eta(R,K)}^{1}g(\mathcal{U}(s,R,K))s^{2}ds-\frac{\nu}{3}(\eta(R,K))^{3},\end{aligned}\end{matrix}

(4.10)

where

\mathcal{U}(s,R,K)=\sigma(sR,\rho(R,K),K),\qquad\eta(R,K)=\rho(R,K)/R.

By (
2.21
) and (
4.9
), for
\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
and

\displaystyle 0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
,

{\partial F(R_{c},K)\over\partial K}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad F(R_{c},K)>0.

(4.11)

Moreover, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
(\rm A1)
–
(\rm A3)
and

\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
hold. For
R>R_{c}
and

\displaystyle 0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
, there hold

{\partial F(R,K)\over\partial K}<0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\partial F(R,K)\over\partial R}<0.

(4.12)

Proof..

Since
\sigma(r,\rho,K)
satisfies problem (
4.3
) for

\rho=\rho(R,K)
and
R\geq R_{c}
,

\mathcal{U}(s,R,K)
is the solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{U}_{ss}(s,R,K)+\displaystyle\frac{2}{s}\mathcal{U}_{s}(s,R,K)=R^{2}f(\mathcal{U}(s,R,K))\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\eta<s<1,\\[8.5359pt]
\mathcal{U}(1,R,K)=\bar{\sigma},\;\;\mathcal{U}(\eta,R,K)=\hat{\sigma},\;\;\mathcal{U}_{s}(\eta,R,K)=KR^{2}\eta,\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

where
\eta=\eta(R,K)
. Let

v(s)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}}{\partial K}(s,R,K),\qquad\zeta=\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial K}(R,K),

then
v(s)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v^{\prime\prime}(s)+\displaystyle\frac{2}{s}v^{\prime}(s)=R^{2}f^{\prime}(\mathcal{U})v\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\eta<s<1,\\[8.5359pt]
v(1)=0,\quad v(\eta)=-KR^{2}\eta\zeta,\\[8.5359pt]
v^{\prime}(\eta)=R^{2}\eta+R^{2}\zeta(3K-f(\hat{\sigma})).\end{array}\right.

(4.14)

Since
3K-f(\hat{\sigma})<0
, similarly to (
2.14
), by the strong maximum
principle we obtain

v(s)={\partial\mathcal{U}\over\partial K}<0\quad\mbox{for}\;\;\eta<s<1,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\zeta={\partial\eta\over\partial K}>0.

(4.15)

Hence we have

{\partial F\over\partial K}=\int_{\eta(R,K)}^{1}g^{\prime}(\mathcal{U}(s,R,K))\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}}{\partial K}(s,R,K)s^{2}ds-(g(\hat{\sigma})+\nu)\eta^{2}(R,K){\partial\eta\over\partial K}(R,K)<0.

(4.16)

Similarly, we can obtain

{\partial\mathcal{U}\over\partial R}(s,R,K)<0\quad\mbox{for}\;\;\eta<s<1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\partial\eta\over\partial R}>0.

(4.17)

It follows that

{\partial F\over\partial R}=\int_{\eta(R,K)}^{1}g^{\prime}(\mathcal{U}(s,R,K))\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{U}}{\partial R}(s,R,K)s^{2}ds-(g(\hat{\sigma})+\nu)\eta^{2}(R,K){\partial\eta\over\partial R}(R,K)<0.

(4.18)

This completes the proof. ∎

Similarly to Lemma
2.3

(iii)
and (
2.16
), we can easily verify that
for any
\displaystyle 0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
,

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\eta(R,K)=1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{R\to+\infty}F(R,K)=-{\nu\over 3}<0.

(4.19)

By (
4.11
) and Lemma
4.2
, we see the equation
F(R,K)=0
has
a unique root
R=R_{s}(K)>R_{c}
for any

\displaystyle 0\leq K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
,
and

{dR_{s}\over dK}<0.

(4.20)

Thus by (
4.6
) we immediately get

\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}F(R,K)=F(R,0)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}R_{s}(K)=R_{s}(0).

(4.21)

Letting
\rho_{s}(K)=\rho(R_{s}(K),K)
and
\sigma_{s}(K)=\sigma(r,\rho_{s}(K),K)
,
we see
(\sigma_{s}(0),\rho_{s}(0),R_{s}(0))
is the stationary solution of
problem (
4.1
), i.e.,

(\sigma_{s}(0),\rho_{s}(0),R_{s}(0))=(\sigma_{nec}(r),\rho_{nec},R_{nec}).

Hence we obtain

\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}\sigma(r,\rho_{s}(K),K)=\sigma_{nec}(r),\quad\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}\rho_{s}(K)=\rho_{nec},\quad\lim_{K\to 0^{+}}R_{s}(K)=R_{nec}.

(4.22)

Let
(\rm A1)
–
(\rm A3)
hold and
f,g
be fixed. Denote the corresponding stationary solution of problem (
1.1
) with
h
by

(\sigma_{s,h}(r),\rho_{s,h},R_{s,h})
.
With the above preparations, we present the following
connection between the tumor models with the quiescent core
and with the necrotic core.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
(\rm A1)
–
(\rm A3)
and

\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}
hold. Then the unique stationary solution of
proliferating-quiescent tumor model (
1.1
)
converges to the stationary solution of the necrotic tumor model (
4.1
),
as
\|h\|
goes to
0
. More precisely, we have

\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}\sigma_{s,h}(r)=\sigma_{nec}(r),\quad\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}\rho_{s,h}=\rho_{nec},\quad\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}R_{s,h}=R_{nec}.

Proof..

Recall
\hat{\sigma}=\sigma_{Q}
. For any
h
satisfying
(\rm A1)
, we have

\sigma^{\prime}(\rho)=\Phi(\rho)={1\over\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{\rho}s^{2}h(V(s,\rho))ds\leq\frac{1}{3}\rho h(\hat{\sigma})={1\over 3}\rho\|h\|.

From (
4.20
), we see that for any
\displaystyle{1\over 3}h(\hat{\sigma})<K<{1\over 3}f(\hat{\sigma})
,

R_{nec}=R_{s}(0)>R_{s,h}=R_{s}({\Phi(\rho)\over\rho})>R_{s}(K).

It follows that

\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}R_{s,h}=R_{nec}.

Then similarly to (
4.22
), we readily get

\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}\sigma_{s,h}(r)=\sigma_{nec}(r)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{\|h\|\to 0^{+}}\rho_{s,h}=\rho_{nec}.

This completes the proof. ∎

With the aid of Lemmas
4.1
–
4.2
and the monotonicity of
F
,
only slight modifications in the above deductions are required to prove a more general result that not only
the stationary solution but also the transient
solution of problem (
1.1
) will converge to
the corresponding solution of problem (
4.1
) as
\|h\|
goes to
0
.
This indicates that the tumor model with a quiescent core not only converges to the tumor model with a necrotic core in the structure, but they also possess a vital link in their solution properties. We omit the details here.

Remark 4.4
.

In the case where

f
,
h
,
g
are all linear functions with the form

f(\sigma)=\sigma,\qquad h(\sigma)=\lambda\sigma,\qquad g(\sigma)=\mu(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}),

(4.23)

problem (
1.1
) has been well studied by Cui
[
5
]
for
\lambda=0
, and by
Liu and Zhuang
[
17
]
for

0<\lambda\leq 1
.
In this case,
\|h\|
goes to
0
if and only if
\lambda
goes to
0
.
We redenote the steady tumor radius
R_{s}
by
R_{s,\lambda}
for

0\leq\lambda\leq 1
.
According to the results in
[
5
]
and
[
17
]
,
for
\bar{\sigma}>\sigma^{*}(>\tilde{\sigma}>\hat{\sigma})
,
we can obtain the unique root
R_{c}>0
of the following equation

\frac{\sinh R}{R}=\frac{\bar{\sigma}}{\hat{\sigma}}.

The nutrient concentration in tumor region is given by

\sigma(r,R,\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\rho\hat{\sigma}}{\sinh\sqrt{\lambda}\rho}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{\lambda}r}{r}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\sigma}}{r}\big[\sqrt{\lambda}\rho\coth\sqrt{\lambda}\rho\sinh(r-\rho)+\rho\cosh(r-\rho)\big]\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<r<R,\end{array}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(R,\lambda)
satisfies the equation

L(\rho,R,\lambda):=\sqrt{\lambda}\rho\coth\sqrt{\lambda}\rho\sinh(R-\rho)+\rho\cosh(R-\rho)-\frac{\bar{\sigma}}{\hat{\sigma}}R=0,

for
R>R_{c}
and
0<\lambda\leq 1
.
One can easily verify that

{\partial L\over\partial\rho}<0,\qquad{\partial L\over\partial\lambda}>0,\qquad{\partial\rho\over\partial\lambda}=-{\partial_{\lambda}L\over\partial_{\rho}L}>0.

(4.24)

The steady tumor radius
R_{s,\lambda}
is
the unique root of the following equation

F(R,\lambda):={1\over R^{3}}\Big[\int_{\rho(R,\lambda)}^{R}\mu(\sigma(r,R,\lambda)-\tilde{\sigma})r^{2}dr-\frac{\nu}{3}\rho^{3}(R,\lambda)\Big]=0.

Similarly, by
[
17
]
and (
4.24
) we have
for
R>R_{c}
and
0<\lambda<1
,

{\partial F\over\partial R}<0,\qquad{\partial F\over\partial\lambda}<0,\qquad{dR_{s,\lambda}\over d\lambda}=-{\partial_{\lambda}F\over\partial_{R}F}<0.

(4.25)

On the other hand,
for the necrotic tumor model with

f,h,g
given by (
4.23
) and
\lambda=0
,
from
[
5
]
, we know that

\sigma(r,R,0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\sigma}\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\rho,\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\sigma}}{r}\big[\sinh(r-\rho)+\rho\cosh(r-\rho)\big]\qquad\mbox{for}\;\;\rho<r<R.\end{array}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(R,0)
satisfies

L(\rho,R,0):=\sinh(R-\rho)+\rho\cosh(R-\rho)-\frac{\bar{\sigma}}{\hat{\sigma}}R=0,

and
R_{s,0}
is the unique root of the following equation:

F(R,0):={1\over R^{3}}\Big[\int_{\rho(R,0)}^{R}\mu(\sigma(r,R,0)-\tilde{\sigma})r^{2}dr-\frac{\nu}{3}\rho^{3}(R,0)\Big]=0.

By a direct computation, we can verify that for
R>R_{c}
,

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\rho(R,\lambda)=\rho(R,0),\quad\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\sigma(r,R,\lambda)=\sigma(r,R,0),\quad\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}F(R,\lambda)=F(R,0).

Hence from (
4.25
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\rho_{s,\lambda}=\rho_{s,0}=\rho_{nec}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}R_{s,\lambda}=R_{s,0}=R_{nec}.

From this, we observe that, in the linear case, the stationary solution of problem (
1.1
) converges
to the stationary solution of problem (
4.1
), as
\|h\|
goes to
0
.

Cavity Born-Oppenheimer Coupled Cluster Theory: Towards Electron Correlation in the Vibrational Strong Light-Matter Coupling Regime
Eric W. Fischer

Abstract
We present a detailed derivation and discussion of cavity Born-Oppenheimer coupled cluster (CBO-CC) theory and address cavity-modified electron correlation in the vibrational strong coupling regime. Methodologically, we combine the recently proposed cavity reaction potential (CRP) approach with the Lagrangian formulation of CC theory and derive a self-consistent CRP-CC method at the singles and doubles excitations level (CRP-CCSD). The CRP-CC approach is formally similar to implicit solvation CC models and provides access to the CBO-CC electronic ground state energy minimized in cavity coordinate space on a CC level of theory. A hierarchy of linearisation schemes (lCRP-CCSD) similar to canonical CC theory systematically lifts the self-consistent nature of the CRP-CCSD approach and mitigates numerical cost by approximating electron correlation effects in energy minimization. We provide a thorough comparison of CRP-CCSD, lCRP-CCSD and CRP-Hartee-Fock methods for a cavity-modified Menshutkin reaction, pyridine + + CH 3 Br, and cavity-induced collective electronic effects in microsolvation energies of selected methanol-water clusters. We find lCRP-CCSD methods to provide excellent results compared to the self-consistent CRP-CCSD approach in the few-molecule limit. We furthermore observe significant differences between mean-field and correlated results in both reactive and collective scenarios. This work illustrates the non-trivial character of electron correlation under vibrational strong coupling and provides a starting point for further developments in ab initio vibro-polaritonic chemistry beyond the mean-field approximation.

II
Theoretical Background

We introduce the CBO electronic ground state problem and its formulation in terms of the CRP approach, which provides the conceptual basis for the remainder of this work.

II.1
The CBO Electronic Ground State Problem

We consider a molecular subsystem strongly coupled to a single effective mode of an infrared optical cavity in the dipole approximation. In length gauge representation, the adiabatic ground state cavity potential energy surface (cPES) is given by

\displaystyle E^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x_{\lambda})|\hat{H}_{ec}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x_{\lambda})}\quad,

(1)

with CBO adiabatic ground state,
\ket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x_{\lambda})}
, which parametrically depends on both nuclear,
\underline{R}
, and cavity displacement coordinates,
x_{\lambda}
, for a cavity mode with polarization,
\lambda
. We assume a valid CBO approximation,
i.e.
,
E^{(ec)}_{0}
is energetically well separated from the excited state manifold, such that non-adiabatic effects are negligibly small.

The CBO electronic Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}_{ec}
, in Eq.(
1
) is given in second quantization representation as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ec}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}h^{pq}_{\lambda}\hat{E}_{pq}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}\tilde{g}_{pqrs}\hat{e}_{pqrs}+\tilde{V}_{nc}\quad,

(2)

where we employ the common notation for general (
p,q,r,s
), occupied (
i,j,k,l
) and virtual (
a,b,c,d
) molecular orbital (MO) indices. Spin-summed electronic one- and two-particle excitation operators are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{pq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p\sigma}\hat{a}_{q\sigma}\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{pqrs}

\displaystyle=\hat{E}_{pq}\hat{E}_{rs}-\delta_{qr}\hat{E}_{ps}\quad,

(4)

with spin index
\sigma
. The CBO one- and two-electron integrals,
h^{pq}_{\lambda}
and
\tilde{g}_{pqrs}
, take the form

\displaystyle h^{pq}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=h_{pq}+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}O^{pq}_{\lambda}-g_{0}\omega_{c}d^{pq}_{\lambda}x_{\lambda}+g^{2}_{0}d^{pq}_{\lambda}\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

(5)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{pqrs}

\displaystyle=(pq|rs)+g^{2}_{0}d^{pq}_{\lambda}d^{rs}_{\lambda}\quad,

(6)

with electron repulsion integrals,
(pq|rs)
, in Mulliken notation and polarization-projected electronic dipole matrix elements

\displaystyle d^{pq}_{\lambda}=-e\braket{\chi_{p}|r_{i\lambda}|\chi_{q}}\quad.

(7)

The one-electron term,
h^{pq}_{\lambda}
, contains the canonical core contribution,
h_{pq}
, a one-electron DSE term determined by a matrix element of the polarization-projected electronic quadrupole moment

\displaystyle O^{pq}_{\lambda}=e^{2}\braket{\chi_{p}|r^{2}_{i\lambda}|\chi_{q}}\quad,

(8)

the cavity-electron interaction term with harmonic cavity frequency,
\omega_{c}
, and a DSE cross term, which couples electrons and nuclei via the polarization projected nuclear dipole operator,
\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
, respectively. The light-matter coupling constant,
g_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}\,V_{c}}}
, is proportional to the cavity mode volume,
V_{c}
, but will be treated as a free parameter in the following. Finally, the remaining term of the CBO electronic Hamiltonian is a nuclear-cavity contribution

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{nc}

\displaystyle=V_{nn}+\dfrac{\omega^{2}_{c}}{2}x^{2}_{\lambda}-g_{0}\omega_{c}\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}x_{\lambda}+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}\quad,

(9)

which contains nuclear interaction and harmonic cavity potentials, the nuclei-cavity interaction besides a purely nuclear DSE term, and constitutes therefore a constant energy shift of the CBO electronic energy.

II.2
The Ground State Cavity Reaction Potential

We will now expand the ground state cPES in Eq.(
1
) around a stationary configuration,
(\underline{R},x^{0}_{\lambda})
, in a second-order Taylor series

\displaystyle E^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R})+\dfrac{1}{2}\underline{C}^{T}\underline{\underline{H}}^{(ec)}_{0}\underline{C}\quad,

(10)

and denote the first term as ground state cavity reaction potential (CRP)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R})=E^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R},x^{0}_{\lambda})\quad,

(11)

which is simply the ground state cPES evaluated at the stationary cavity coordinate,

. The second term in Eq.(

;

;

Since the CBO electronic Hamiltonian in Eq.(

The expression in Eq.(

vibro-polaritonic chemistry:

can be interpreted as a minimum energy surface in cavity coordinate space that fully captures static properties of cavity-modified chemical reactions. Inspired by concepts from reaction rate theory

We shall now inspect the CRP as defined in Eq.(

) in more detail. The stationary cavity coordinate,
x^{0}_{\lambda}
, minimizes the CBO electronic energy in cavity coordinate space and equivalently satisfies the nonradiating ground state condition

;
fischer2024

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{\lambda}}E^{(ec)}_{0}\right|_{x^{0}_{\lambda}}=0\,\Leftrightarrow\,\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\underline{\hat{E}}_{\perp}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}=0\quad,

(12)

where
\underline{\hat{E}}_{\perp}
is the transverse electric field operator of the cavity field. One can exploit the Hellmann-Feynman theorem to evaluate the stationary cavity coordinate exactly as

\displaystyle x^{0}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{g_{0}}{\omega_{c}}\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{d}^{(en)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\quad,

(13)

with polarization-projected molecular dipole operator,
\hat{d}^{(en)}_{\lambda}=\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}+\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
, composed of electronic (
\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}
) and nuclear (
\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
) components, respectively. We note here that due to the quadratic character of
\hat{H}_{ec}
in terms of
x_{\lambda}
,

always refers to a minimum. From Eqs.(
1

Appendix
.1
)

\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{R})=\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{e}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\\
-\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}^{2}\quad,

(14)

which is independent of both the nuclear dipole contribution and the cavity frequency, origin invariant (
cf.
Appendix
.2
) and subject to an effective CBO electronic Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}\tilde{h}^{pq}_{\lambda}\hat{E}_{pq}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}\tilde{g}_{pqrs}\hat{e}_{pqrs}+V_{nn}\quad,

(15)

with DSE-augmented core contribution

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{pq}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=h_{pq}+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}O^{pq}_{\lambda}\quad,

(16)

and CBO electron repulsion integral,
\tilde{g}_{pqrs}
as given in Eq.(
6
).

We shall now exploit the observation that Eq.(
14
) can be interpreted as a ground state-projected non-linear electronic Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\Psi_{0}}_{e}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\quad,

(17)

specified by a respectively non-linear CBO electronic Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\Psi_{0}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{e}-\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}\quad.

(18)

The nonlinearity emerges from the second term, which explicitly depends on the one-particle reduced density matrix (1-RDM) determining the electronic dipole expectation value. Thus, a solution of Eq.(
17
) provides access to the ground state CBO electronic energy minimized in cavity coordinate space without the need of cavity coordinate gradients evaluated via extended electronic structure methods. However, this gain comes at the cost of Eq.(
17
) requiring a self-consistent solution, which resembles energy minimization in cavity coordinate space.

At this point, we shall discuss why one should aim at solving the non-linear CRP ground state problem in Eq.(
17
) instead of Eq.(
1
) followed by a common quantum chemical optimization routine extended to cavity modes. First, both ground state energy and wave function correspond to a chemically meaningful stationary point on the cPES, which can be analysed with respect to reaction mechanism and cavity-modified electron correlation. Second, one circumvents the need of extending analytic gradients at different theory levels of quantum chemical methods to cavity coordinate space. Third, Hartree-Fock theory as reference for correlated wave function methods in quantum chemistry is straightforwardly generalizable to the CRP scenario due to its inherently non-linear character at no additional cost. Forth, it turns out that an approximation to the CRP problem in terms of coupled cluster theory is formally similar to implicit solvation coupled cluster theory, which allows to benefit from an established theoretical framework applied in a different context.
In the remainder of this work, we will discuss approximations to Eq.(
17
) based on Hartree-Fock and coupled cluster theory.

III
CRP Hartree-Fock Theory

We introduce the CRP formulation of CBO Hartree-Fock (CRP-HF) theory, which resembles a mean-field approximation of the ground state CRP. It furthermore serves as a starting point for the development of a related CRP coupled cluster (CPR-CC) method to be discussed in Sec.
IV
.

The CRP-HF approach relies on a single-determinant approximation of the CBO adiabatic ground state

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\approx e^{-\hat{\kappa}}\ket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}}=\ket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})}\quad,

(19)

which turns Eq.(
14

\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})=\braket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{e}|\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})}\\
-\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\braket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})|\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}(\underline{\kappa})}^{2}\quad.

(20)

Here, we introduced the one-electron orbital rotation operator

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}=\sum_{p>q}\kappa_{pq}\left(\hat{E}_{pq}-\hat{E}_{qp}\right)=\sum_{p>q}\kappa_{pq}\hat{E}^{-}_{pq}\quad,

(21)

which is parametrically dependent on orbital rotation parameters,
\underline{\kappa}=(\dots\kappa_{pq}\dots)
. In order to minimize the energy in Eq.(
20
), we require the CRP orbital gradient to vanish (
cf.
Appendix
.3
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(1)}_{pq}=\braket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}|[\hat{E}^{-}_{pq},\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\Phi_{0}}_{e}]|\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}}=0\quad,

(22)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\Phi_{0}}_{e}
is the mean-field approximation of Eq.(
18

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{(e)}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}\left(\tilde{h}^{pq}_{\lambda}+\tilde{v}^{pq}_{\lambda}\right)\hat{E}_{pq}\quad,

(23)

where,
\tilde{h}^{pq}_{\lambda}
, is given by Eq.(
16
) and the effective potential reads in the restricted scenario

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{pq}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(2g_{pqii}-g_{piiq}-g^{2}_{0}d^{pi}_{\lambda}d^{iq}_{\lambda}\right)\quad,

(24)

with canonical Coulomb (
g_{pqii}
) and exchange integrals (
g_{piiq}
), respectively. We note that Eq.(
24
) contains only a DSE-induced exchange contribution while a Coulomb-like equivalent vanishes in the CRP formulation of CBO-HF theory.

In the canonical MO basis, the mean-field CRP turns into (

Appendix
.3
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{\mathrm{rhf}}=E^{(ec)}_{\mathrm{rhf}}+g^{2}_{0}\left(\sum_{i}O^{ii}_{\lambda}-\sum_{ij}d^{ij}_{\lambda}d^{ji}_{\lambda}\right)\quad,

(25)

where the first term is formally identical to the RHF energy but here evaluated with respect to DSE-relaxed MOs. The remaining two terms reflect a quadrupole- and exchange-type correction emerging from the electronic dipole self-energy and render
\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{\mathrm{rhf}}

The CRP Coupled Cluster Lagrangian

We approximate the CBO adiabatic ground state in Eq.(

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}

\displaystyle\approx\ket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{\mathrm{CC}}}=e^{\hat{T}}\ket{\Phi^{(ec)}_{0}}\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

We account for cavity-induced molecular reorientation by transforming the molecular axis system to the principal frame of the molecular polarizability tensor (

x

VII
Conclusion

We presented a detailed derivation and discussion of cavity Born-Oppenheimer cluster (CBO-CC) theory in the cavity reaction potential (CRP) framework
fischer2024
, which provides an
ab initio
approach to electron correlation in the vibrational strong coupling regime. The CRP reformulation of the CBO electronic ground state problem satisfies by construction the non-radiating ground state condition and consequently addresses the CBO electronic ground state energy minimized in cavity coordinate space without invoking common optimization routines. This comes at the cost of a non-linear electronic ground state problem, which we discussed from both mean-field and correlated perspectives.

Based on a straightforward CRP-reformulation of CBO-HF theory, we derived a CRP-CC method via a Lagrangian approach formally similar to implicit solvation CC models at the singles and doubles excitation level (CRP-CCSD). The non-linear character of the CRP-CCSD Lagrangian results in coupled amplitude and multiplier equations, which have to be solved self-consistently mimicking energy optimization in cavity coordinate space. A self-consistent solution renders the CRP-CCSD approach more costly than canonical CC theory but provides access to the CBO electronic ground state energy minimized in cavity coordinate space on a CCSD level of theory. In order to mitigate additional cost due to the method’s non-linear character, we presented a hierarchy of linearised CRP-CCSD Lagrangians inspired by implicit solvation CC theory, which decouple amplitude and multiplier equations by approximating the energy optimization procedure. Resulting lCRP-CCSD methods mimic canonical CC theory and therefore benefit from similar cost.

We illustratively applied CRP-HF, lCRP-CCSD and CRP-CCSD approaches to two molecular model examples: A Menshutkin reaction in gas-phase between pyridine and CH
3
Br, where we addressed electron correlation effects under VSC on activation and product energies, besides MeOH@
n
H
2
O clusters with
n=1,5
, which we discussed with respect to collective effects in cavity-modified microsolvation energies. In both cases, we find linearised CRP-CCSD methods to provide excellent results compared to the self-consistent CRP-CCSD approach in the few-molecule limit. Furthermore, we find significant cavity-induced electron correlation effects for both model systems and observe collective electronic effects under VSC to differ substantially between correlated and mean-field descriptions.

The ground state CRP-CCSD approach can be extended to excited states by further exploiting formal similarities with implicit solvation CC theory
cammi2010
;
caricato2012

(.1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}

(.1.4)

\displaystyle-g^{2}_{0}\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}\vskip 5.69046pt

(.1.5)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}^{2}\quad.

(.1.6)

Origin Invariance of the exact CRP

We show that the CRP,
\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}
, is invariant with respect to a shift,
\Delta_{\lambda}
, of the electronic dipole operator, which transforms as

\displaystyle\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\to-e\sum_{i}\left(r_{i\lambda}+\Delta_{\lambda}\right)=\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}+N_{e}\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}\quad,

(.2.1)

with
\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}=-e\Delta_{\lambda}
. We simplify the discussion by rewriting Eq.(
14
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\hat{H}_{e}|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}+\Delta\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}\quad,

(.2.2)

which allows us to consider only the relevant second term

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}

\displaystyle=\braket{\hat{O}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}+\braket{\hat{U}^{(ee)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}-\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}^{2}_{\Psi_{0}}\quad,

(.2.3)

with
\braket{\dots}_{\Psi_{0}}=\braket{\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}|\dots|\Psi^{(ec)}_{0}}
. The individual terms in Eq.(
.2.3
) transform under an origin-shift
\Delta_{\lambda}
as

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{O}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle+2\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}+N_{e}(\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda})^{2}\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

(.2.4)

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{U}^{(ee)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle+2(N_{e}-1)\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}+N_{e}(N_{e}-1)(\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda})^{2}\,,\vskip 5.69046pt

(.2.5)

\displaystyle-\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}^{2}_{\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle-2N_{e}\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{\Psi_{0}}\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}-N^{2}_{e}(\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda})^{2}\quad.

(.2.6)

Contributions scaling as
\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda}
and
(\Delta^{(e)}_{\lambda})^{2}
, which correspond to the respective second and third terms in each equation, cancel exactly. Thus,
\Delta\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}
and consequently
\mathcal{V}^{(ec)}_{0}
is origin invariant.

Computational Details

All molecular structures were taken from Ref.
pavosevic2022
. In order to reorient the molecules, we first obtained the diagonal polarizability tensor and the corresponding eigenvectors via ORCA 5.0.4
orca2012
;
orcav52022
on both RHF/aug-cc-pVDZ and DLPNO-CCSD/aug-cc-pVDZ levels of theory in agreement with CRP-HF and CRP-CCSD methods employed afterwards. Since molecular orientation becomes essentially a new structural parameter in cavity scenarios, it will depend on the level of theory employed to obtain the diagonal polarizability tensor. We chose the latter here consistent with the respective CRP electronic structure method. The matrix of eigenvectors provides a coordinate transformation to the polarizability tensor’s principal axis frame, which transforms initial molecular structures to their cavity-reorientated equivalents. CRP-HF, lCRP-CCSD and CRP-CCSD methods were subsequently applied.

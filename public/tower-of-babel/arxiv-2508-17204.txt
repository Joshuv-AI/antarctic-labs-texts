Pinpointing Physical Solutions in Y ⁡ ( 4230 ) Y(4230) Decays
Jie Yuan 1,2 , Yadi Wang 1 , Kai Zhu 2 1 North China Electric Power University, Beijing 102206, People’s Republic of China 2 Institute of High Energy Physics, Beijing 100049, People’s Republic of China

Abstract
To resolve ambiguities from multiple solutions in experimental measurements, we construct a χ 2 \chi^{2} function incorporating constraints such as isospin conservation and amplitude relations. By minimizing the global χ 2 \chi^{2} , we identify physical solutions for seven hidden-charm decay channels of Y ⁡ ( 4230 ) Y(4230) , revealing a small strange quark component. Crucially, the physical solution for Y ⁡ ( 4230 ) → π + ​ π − ​ J / ψ Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi corresponds to the smallest among four experimental solutions, potentially significantly modifying inputs for theoretical calculations. Additionally, we predict Γ e ​ e ​ ℬ ​ ( Y ⁡ ( 4230 ) → π + ​ π − ​ ψ ′ ) \Gamma_{ee}\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}) based on our results, acknowledging substantial uncertainties in current measurements.

In addition to mass and width analyses, the decay channels of
Y(4230)
provide a valuable probe into its properties. Studying these decays offers insight into the decay mechanisms and, consequently, the internal structure of the resonance. However, despite extensive experimental measurements, the presence of multiple solutions introduces significant ambiguity in the interpretation of results. This issue arises unavoidably when interference effects are incorporated, particularly when resonances are modeled with the Breit-Wigner formula
Bukin 2007
;
Zhu et al. 2011
;
Bai and Chen 2019
. When the cross-section line shape is described by coherent amplitudes, as in the expression
\left|\sum_{i}g_{i}e^{i\phi_{i}}A_{i}\right|^{2}
, multiple distinct sets of parameters
(g_{i},\phi_{i})
can yield identical goodness-of-fit values. As a result, identifying the physically meaningful solution based solely on experimental data remains challenging.

\Gamma_{ee}\mathcal{B}
for the decay
Y(4230)\to e^{+}e^{-}

To identify the physical solutions among the multiple candidates, we construct a
\chi^{2}
function comprising terms derived from symmetry constraints and amplitude ratios between decay channels. All possible combinations of solutions across the decay channels are evaluated based on the
\chi^{2}
value, and the combination yielding the global minimum is selected as the set of physical solutions.

First, isospin symmetry is assumed to be well conserved in the cross-sections. Consequently, the branching fractions are expected to satisfy the ratios
\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi):\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}J/\psi)=2:1
and
\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi):\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to K_{S}K_{S}J/\psi)=2:1
. Thus, the following two terms are included in the
\chi^{2}
function:

\frac{(\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}-2\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{0}\pi^{0}J/\psi})^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}}^{2}+(2\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{0}\pi^{0}J/\psi}})^{2}}

(1)

and

\frac{(\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi}-2\mathcal{B}_{K_{S}K_{S}J/\psi})^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi}}^{2}+(2\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{K_{S}K_{S}J/\psi}})^{2}}.

(2)

According to Ref.
Qiao 2006
, the ratio between the branching fractions of
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}
and
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
is given by

\frac{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime})}{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi)}\approx\frac{\Omega_{1}}{\Omega_{2}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{|\psi(0)|^{2}},

where
\Omega_{1}
(
\Omega_{2}
) is the phase space for
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}
(
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
), and
\psi^{\prime}(0)
(
\psi(0)
) is the
\psi^{\prime}
(
J/\psi
) radial wave function at the origin in the quark model.
The wave function ratio is directly related to the leptonic decay widths through the well-established relation:

\frac{\Gamma(\psi^{\prime}\to e^{+}e^{-})}{\Gamma(J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-})}=\frac{M_{J/\psi}^{2}}{M_{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{|\psi(0)|^{2}},

where
M_{\psi^{\prime}}
and
M_{J/\psi}
are the masses of
\psi^{\prime}
and
J/\psi
, respectively. Using the measured leptonic widths from the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
, we determine the wave function ratio
|\psi^{\prime}(0)|^{2}/|\psi(0)|^{2}
. This allows us to calculate the expected branching fraction ratio
\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}):\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi)\approx 0.04:1
. The corresponding term in the
\chi^{2}
is

\frac{(\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}}-0.04\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi})^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\psi^{\prime}}}^{2}+(0.04\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}})^{2}}.

(3)

From the analysis of
\eta
-
\eta^{\prime}
mixing in Ref.
Zhu 2022
, the ratio of the decay amplitudes is

\left|\frac{M_{\eta^{\prime}}}{M_{\eta}}\right|=\left|\frac{\cos(\theta_{0}-\theta)+\delta\sin(\theta_{0}-\theta)}{\sin(\theta_{0}-\theta)-\delta\cos(\theta_{0}-\theta)}\right|,

where
\theta_{0}=\arctan(1/\sqrt{2})\approx 35.3^{\circ}
and
\theta=-14.8^{\circ}\pm 0.5^{\circ}
is the empirical mixing angle
Zhu 2022
. The branching fraction ratio is then

r_{\eta}(\delta):=\frac{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\eta^{\prime}J/\psi)}{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\eta J/\psi)}=\left|\frac{M_{\eta^{\prime}}}{M_{\eta}}\right|^{2}\frac{\Omega_{\eta^{\prime}}}{\Omega_{\eta}},

with
\Omega_{\eta^{\prime}}/\Omega_{\eta}=0.22
. The corresponding
\chi^{2}
term includes the theoretical uncertainty
\Delta^{\eta}_{\text{theo}}
associated with
r_{\eta}(\delta)
:

\frac{\left(\mathcal{B}_{\eta^{\prime}J/\psi}-r_{\eta}(\delta)\mathcal{B}_{\eta J/\psi}\right)^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\eta^{\prime}J/\psi}}^{2}+\left(r_{\eta}(\delta)\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\eta J/\psi}}\right)^{2}+(\Delta^{\eta}_{\text{theo}})^{2}},

(4)

where
\Delta^{\eta}_{\text{theo}}=\Delta_{r_{\eta}(\delta)}\mathcal{B}_{\eta J/\psi}
quantifies the uncertainty in the theoretical calculation of
r_{\eta}(\delta)
.

For the ratio
\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi)/\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi)
, we consider the production mechanisms where
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
K^{+}K^{-}
final states predominantly originate from light quark (
q\bar{q}
,
q=u/d
) and strange quark (
s\bar{s}
) components, respectively. Following Ref.
Ganbold 2024
, we account for mixing between these components through intermediate resonances like
f_{0}(980)
. This mixing transforms the initial quark components according to:

\begin{pmatrix}|q\bar{q}\rangle\langle q\bar{q}|&g|q\bar{q}\rangle\langle s\bar{s}|\\
g|s\bar{s}\rangle\langle q\bar{q}|&|s\bar{s}\rangle\langle s\bar{s}|\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}|q\bar{q}\rangle\\
\delta|s\bar{s}\rangle\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(1+g\delta\right)|q\bar{q}\rangle\\
\left(g+\delta\right)|s\bar{s}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

assuming orthogonal states
|q\bar{q}\rangle
and
|s\bar{s}\rangle
. Here,
g
is the mixing parameter determined to be
g^{2}=0.190\pm 0.02
from Ref.
Ganbold 2024
, which originally assumed no intrinsic
s\bar{s}
component in
Y(4230)
. Among the three methods in Ref.
Ganbold 2024
(narrow-width approximation, phase-space improved approximation, and direct momentum integration), we adopt the last approach. The resulting branching fraction ratio is:

r_{k}(\delta):=\frac{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi)}{\mathcal{B}(Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi)}=\left|\frac{g+\delta}{1+g\delta}\right|^{2}.

The corresponding
\chi^{2}
term incorporates both experimental and theoretical uncertainties:

\frac{\left(\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi}-r_{k}(\delta)\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}\right)^{2}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}K^{-}J/\psi}}^{2}+\left(r_{k}(\delta)\Delta_{\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}}\right)^{2}+\left(\Delta^{k}_{\text{theo}}\right)^{2}},

(5)

where
\Delta^{k}_{\text{theo}}=\Delta_{r_{k}(\delta)}\mathcal{B}_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi}
quantifies the theoretical uncertainty in calculating
r_{k}(\delta)
, primarily from the mixing parameter
g
and the orthogonal state assumption.

The total
\chi^{2}
function is the sum of all terms from Eq. (
1
) to (
5
). We introduce the fraction
f=\delta/\sqrt{1+\delta^{2}}
representing the proportion of
s\bar{s}
component. The dependence of the minimum
\chi^{2}
on
f
is shown in Fig.
1
. The global minimum occurs at
f=0.118
with
\chi^{2}=2.09
, and the corresponding solution set is adopted as the physical solutions, as presented in Table
2
.

\chi^{2}

A particularly significant outcome is the selection of the smallest solution for
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
as the physical solution. This solution differs substantially from the bare cross section
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
at
\sqrt{s}=4.23
GeV, which has been directly quoted by Refs.
Chen et al. 2019
;
Wu et al. 2014
;
Dong et al. 2014a
;
Maiani et al. 2005
;
Close and Page 2005
. We recommend that these theoretical calculations be updated using the
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
cross section determined in our analysis. Experimentally, BESIII recently conducted a partial wave analysis of
Y(4230)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi

Ablikim et al. 2025
. For each intermediate process—
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{\pm}Z_{c}(3900)^{\mp}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to f_{0}(980)J/\psi\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
—three solutions were obtained. Our results indicate that the solutions with the smallest cross-sections should be considered physical.

From Fig.
1
, the minimum
\chi^{2}
assuming no strange quark component (
f=0
) is only marginally larger than the global minimum at
f=0.118
. However, as shown in Table
2
, the physical solutions for
f=0
and
f=0.118
exhibit no fundamental differences. Additionally, the
f=0
solutions for
Y(4230)\to\eta J/\psi
and
\eta^{\prime}J/\psi
match those reported in Ref.
Zhu 2022
, consistent with expectations. The peak observed in Fig.
1
, on
f=0.767
, originates from a pole in the amplitude ratio for
Y(4230)\to\eta^{\prime}J/\psi
versus
\eta J/\psi
.

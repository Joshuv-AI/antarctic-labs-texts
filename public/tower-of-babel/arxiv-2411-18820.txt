Sparse Polynomial Optimization with Matrix Constraints
Jiawang Nie, Zheng Qu, Xindong Tang, Linghao Zhang

Abstract
. This paper studies the hierarchy of sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxations for solving sparse polynomial optimization problems with matrix constraints. First, we prove a sufficient and necessary condition for the sparse hierarchy to be tight. Second, we discuss how to detect the tightness and extract minimizers. Third, for the convex case, we show that the hierarchy of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxations is tight, under some general assumptions. In particular, we show that the sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxation is tight for every order when the problem is SOS-convex. Numerical experiments are provided to show the efficiency of the sparse relaxations.

1.
Introduction

Let
x\coloneqq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an
n
-dimensional vector of variables,
and
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
be subsets of
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,n\}

such that
\Delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{m}=[n]
.
For each
\Delta_{i}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n_{i}}\}
, denote the subvector

x_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq(x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{n_{i}}}).

We consider the sparse matrix polynomial optimization problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f(x)\coloneqq f_{1}(x_{\Delta_{1}})+\cdots+f_{m}(x_{\Delta_{m}})\\
\mbox{s.t.}&G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m.\end{array}\right.

In the above, each
f_{i}
is a polynomial in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
and
each
G_{i}
is a symmetric polynomial matrix in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.
We denote by
f_{\min}
the minimum value of (
1.1
) and

(1.2)

K_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{x_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}:G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0\}.

The feasible set of (
1.1
) is

K=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\{x:G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0\}.

Matrix constrained polynomial optimization problems can be solved by the
dense
matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of semidefinite relaxations, which are introduced in
[
5
,
8
]
.
Denote the matrix set
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{1,G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}\}
. The quadratic module of polynomials generated by
\mathcal{G}
is

\operatorname{QM}[\mathcal{G}]\coloneqq\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s}P_{j}^{T}B_{j}P_{j}:B_{j}\in\mathcal{G},s\in\mathbb{N},P_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\text{len}(B_{j})}\Big\}.

In the above, len(
B_{j}
) denotes the length of
B_{j}
.
For an integer
k
, the degree-
2k
truncation
\operatorname{QM}[\mathcal{G}]_{2k}
is the set of all polynomials that can be represented as above, with
2\deg(P_{j})+\deg(B_{j})\leq 2k
for every
j
.
The
k
th order dense SOS relaxation of (
1.1
) is

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\max&\gamma\\
\mbox{s.t.}&f-\gamma\in\operatorname{QM}[\mathcal{G}]_{2k}.\end{array}\right.

Its dual optimization problem is the
k
th order dense moment relaxation:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min&\langle f,y\rangle\\
\mbox{s.t.}&L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y]\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&M_{k}[y]\succeq 0,y_{0}=1,\\
&y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k}}.\end{array}\right.

We refer to Section
2.2
for the meaning of notation in the above.
For
k=1,2,\ldots
, the sequence of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) is called the dense matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy. It produces a sequence of lower bounds for the minimum value
f_{\min}
of (
1.1
), which converges to
f_{\min}
under the archimedean condition
[
5
,
8
]
.
This matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy is said to be
tight
if the optimal value (
1.3
) equals
f_{\min}
for some
k
.
It is shown in
[
10
]
that this hierarchy is tight under some optimality conditions.
We refer to
[
4
,
9
,
10
,
19
,
24
]

for related work about matrix constrained polynomial optimization.

When the number of variables or the relaxation orders increase,
the sizes of the moment relaxations grow rapidly.
It is expensive to solve (
1.1
) when it is in large scale.
Thus, it is important to exploit sparsity to
improve computational efficiency.
In this paper, we focus on the matrix polynomial optimization problem
with the sparsity pattern as in (
1.1
).
This is referenced as the
correlative sparsity
in some literature,
to be distinguished from the
term sparsity
(
[
28
,
29
]
).
Sparse polynomial optimization has wide applications.
We refer to
[
3
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
30
,
31
]
for related work on sparsity.
Moreover, we refer to
[
12
,
32
]
for representations of sparse matrix polynomials.

In this paper, we study the
sparse
hierarchy of matrix Moment-SOS relaxations for solving (
1.1
).
For a given degree
k
,
the
k
th order sparse SOS relaxation is

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\max&\gamma\\
\mbox{s.t.}&f-\gamma\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}.\end{array}\right.

Its dual optimization problem is the
k
th order sparse moment relaxation:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min&\langle f,y\rangle\coloneqq\langle f_{1},y_{\Delta_{1}}\rangle+\cdots+\langle f_{m},y_{\Delta_{m}}\rangle\\
\mbox{s.t.}&L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&M_{k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&y_{0}=1,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}.\end{array}\right.

The optimal values of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)
are denoted as
f_{k}^{spa}
and
f_{k}^{smo}
respectively.
The symbol
y_{\Delta_{i}}
denotes the subvector of
y

that is labelled by monomial powers in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.
We refer to Section
2
for the notation in the above.
As we increase the relaxation order
k
, the sequence of relaxation problems
(
1.5
)-(
1.6
) gives the
sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy

for solving (
1.1
). We have the convergence

f^{spa}_{k}\to f_{\min}
when
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
satisfy the

running intersection property
(RIP) (see
[
15
]
) and every

\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]
is
archimedean

[
14
]
.
Compared with the dense relaxations (
1.3
)-(
1.4
), the sparse version (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) have
positive semidefinite (psd) matrix constraints or variables with much smaller sizes.

For the special case that every matrix
G_{i}
is diagonal,
(
1.1
) reduces to the scalar constrained sparse optimization,
which is studied in the recent work
[
22
]
.
It is shown that the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy (for the scalar case) is tight
if and only if the objective function can be written as a sum of sparse nonnegative polynomials,
each of which belongs to the corresponding sparse quadratic module.
However, there is very little work on the tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxations.

Contribution

This paper investigates conditions for the tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
Our major results are:

•

We prove a sufficient and necessary condition for the tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
Specifically,
we show that
f^{spa}_{k}=f_{\min}
(i.e., the relaxation (
1.5
) is tight) if and only if there exist sparse polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
such that

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\in\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]_{2k},\ i=1,\ldots,m.\end{gathered}}

The above means that the
f-f_{\min}
can be equivalently expressed as a sum of sparse nonnegative polynomials, each of which belongs to the corresponding sparse quadratic module.

•

We give explicit conditions for the tightness of the sparse hierarchy of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) when the sparse matrix polynomial optimization is convex.
In particular, we show that if the objective and constraining matrix polynomials are SOS-convex, then the moment relaxation (
1.6
) is tight for all relaxation orders.

•

We show that under certain conditions, the tightness of sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy can be detected by the flat truncation, and minimizers can be extracted from moment matrices.

This paper is organized as follows. Some basics on matrix polynomial optimization and algebraic
geometry are reviewed in Section 2.
Section 3 gives a characterization for tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy.
In Section 4, we study the flat truncation for certifying tightness of moment relaxations.
Section 5 gives some sufficient conditions for the tightness when the sparse matrix polynomial optimization is convex.
Some numerical experiments are presented in Section 6.

2.1.
SOS polynomials and quadratic modules

A polynomial
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is said to be a
sum of squares
(SOS)
if there exist polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
such that

\sigma=p_{1}^{2}+\cdots+p_{s}^{2}
.
The cone of SOS polynomials in
x
is denoted as
\Sigma[x]
, and

\Sigma[x]_{2k}\coloneqq\Sigma[x]\cap\mathbb{R}[x]_{2k}.

The cone of
t
-by-
t
SOS polynomial matrices in
x
is

\Sigma[x]^{t\times t}\coloneqq\Big\{P^{T}P:P\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{s\times t}\mathrm{~for~some~}s\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

For each
i\in[m]
,
\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
denotes the set of SOS polynomials in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.
The truncation
\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
and the cone of SOS polynomial matrices
\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{t\times t}
in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
are similarly defined.
For
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell_{i}\times\ell_{i}}
, its
quadratic modules
in

\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{t\times t}
and
\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x]^{t\times t}
are respectively:

\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]^{t\times t}\coloneqq\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{t\times t}+\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s}P^{T}_{j}G_{i}P_{j}:s\in\mathbb{N},P_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell_{i}\times t}\Big\},

\operatorname{QM}[G_{i}]^{t\times t}\coloneqq\Sigma[x]^{t\times t}+\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s}P^{T}_{j}G_{i}P_{j}:s\in\mathbb{N},P_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\ell_{i}\times t}\Big\}.

When
t=1
, we denote

\operatorname{QM}[G_{i}]:=\operatorname{QM}[G_{i}]^{1\times 1},\quad\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]:=\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]^{1\times 1}.

The quadratic module
\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]
is said to be
archimedean
if there exists a scalar
R>0
such that

R-\|x_{\Delta_{i}}\|^{2}\in\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]
.

For an even degree
2k
, denote the
2k
-truncation of
\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]
:

\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]_{2k}\coloneqq\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}+\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{s}P_{j}^{T}G_{i}P_{j}\Big|\begin{array}[]{l}s\in\mathbb{N},P_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell_{i}}\\
2\deg(P_{j})+\deg(G_{i})\leq 2k\end{array}\Big\}.

The truncation
\operatorname{QM}[G_{i}]_{2k}
is defined similarly.
For a matrix polynomial tuple
G\coloneqq(G_{1},\ldots,G_{m})
such that each
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell_{i}\times\ell_{i}}
, we denote

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{1}}[G_{1}]+\cdots+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{m}}[G_{m}],\\
\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{1}}[G_{1}]_{2k}+\cdots+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{m}}[G_{m}]_{2k}.\end{array}\right.

2.2.
Dense moments

For a power vector
\alpha\coloneqq(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, denote
|\alpha|\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.
The notation

\mathbb{N}_{d}^{n}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d\}

stands for the set of monomial powers with degrees at most
d
.
The symbol
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{n}}
denotes the space of all real vectors labeled by
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{n}
.
A vector
y\coloneqq\left(y_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k}}
is called a
truncated multi-sequences
(tms) of degree
2k
.
For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k}}
, the
Riesz functional
determined by
y
is the linear functional
\mathscr{L}_{y}
acting on
\mathbb{R}[x]_{2k}
such that

\mathscr{L}_{y}\Big(\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k}}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\Big)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{2k}}p_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}.

For convenience, we denote

\langle p,y\rangle\coloneqq\mathscr{L}_{y}(p),\quad p\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{2k}.

The
localizing matrix
of
p
generated by
y
is

L_{p}^{(k)}[y]\coloneqq\mathscr{L}_{y}(p(x)\cdot[x]_{s_{1}}[x]_{s_{1}}^{T}).

In the above, the linear operator is applied entry-wise and

s_{1}\coloneqq\lfloor k-\deg(p)/2\rfloor,\quad[x]_{s_{1}}\coloneqq(x^{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{s_{1}}}.

In particular, for
p=1
, we get the
moment matrix

M_{k}[y]\coloneqq L_{1}^{(k)}[y]
.
More details for this can be found in
[
20
]
.
For a matrix polynomial
F\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x]^{\ell\times\ell}
with entries as

F\,\coloneqq\,\big(F_{st}\big)_{1\leq s,t\leq\ell},

its localizing matrix is the
\ell\times\ell
block matrix

L_{F}^{(k)}[y]\,\coloneqq\,\big(L_{F_{st}}^{(k)}[y]\big)_{1\leq s,t\leq\ell}.

2.3.
Sparse moments

For each
x_{\Delta_{i}}
, denote the set of monomial powers

\mathbb{N}^{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\alpha_{j}=0\,\,\forall j\notin\Delta_{i}\}.

For a degree
d
, denote

\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Delta_{i}}:|\alpha|\leq d\},

where
|\alpha|\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.
The vector of all monomials in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
listed in the graded lexicographic order
and with degrees up to
d
is denoted as
[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{d}
, i.e.,

(2.5)

[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{d}\,=\,\big(x^{\alpha}\big)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}

the space of all real vectors whose entries are labeled by
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}
.
A vector
y_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}

is called a truncated multi-sequence (tms) of degree
d
.
The Riesz functional determined by
y_{\Delta_{i}}

is the linear functional
\mathscr{L}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}
acting on
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

\mathscr{L}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}(x^{\alpha})\,=\,(y_{\Delta_{i}})_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{for each}\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}.

This induces the bilinear operation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.6)

\langle p,y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle\coloneqq\mathscr{L}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}(p).

The
localizing matrix
of
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
,
generated by
y_{\Delta_{i}}
, is

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L_{p}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]&\coloneqq&\mathscr{L}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}\left(p(x_{\Delta_{i}})[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{k_{1}}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{T}_{k_{1}}\right).\end{array}

In the above, the Riesz functional is applied entry-wise and

k_{1}\coloneqq\lfloor k-\deg(p)/2\rfloor.

In particular, when
p=1
is the constant one polynomial, we get the
moment matrix

(2.7)

M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\coloneqq L_{1}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}].

For a matrix polynomial
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell\times\ell}
with entries as

G_{i}\,\coloneqq\,\big((G_{i})_{st}\big)_{1\leq s,t\leq\ell},

its localizing matrix is the
\ell\times\ell
block matrix

(2.8)

L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\,\coloneqq\,\big(L_{(G_{i})_{st}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\big)_{1\leq s,t\leq\ell}.

If
\deg(G_{i})\leq d
and
y_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}
, we define

(2.9)

G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\coloneqq\big(\langle(G_{i})_{{st}},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle\big)_{1\leq s,t\leq\ell}.

Note that
G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]
is a principal submatrix of
L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]
,
consisting of the
(1,1)
-entries of its blocks.
Therefore, if
L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0
, then
G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0
.

For a given degree
k
, denote the monomial power set

(2.10)

\mathbb{U}_{k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{N}_{2k}^{\Delta_{i}}.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}
denote the space of real vectors labeled such that

y=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{U}_{k}}.

For given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}
, we denote the subvector

(2.11)

y_{\Delta_{i}}\,\coloneqq\,(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{2k}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

For the objective
f
as in (
1.1
) and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}
, we have

(2.12)

\langle f,y\rangle\,\coloneqq\,\langle f_{1},y_{\Delta_{1}}\rangle+\cdots+\langle f_{m},y_{\Delta_{m}}\rangle.

2.4.
Convex matrix polynomials

A matrix polynomial
P(x)\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x]^{\ell\times\ell}
is said to be
convex

over a convex domain
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

if for all
u,v\in\mathcal{D}

and for all
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
it holds that (
X\preceq Y
means
Y-X\succeq 0
)

P(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v)\preceq\lambda P(u)+(1-\lambda)P(v).

If
-P(x)
is convex over
\mathcal{D}
, then
P
is called
concave
over
\mathcal{D}
.
The matrix polynomial
P(x)
is convex if and only if for all

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and for all
u\in\mathcal{D}
,
the Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}(\xi^{T}P(x)\xi)
is positive semidefinite at
x=u
.
Furthermore,
P(x)
is said to be
SOS-convex

if for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, there exists a matrix polynomial
Q(x)
such that

\nabla^{2}\left(\xi^{T}P(x)\xi\right)\,=\,Q(x)^{T}Q(x).

The coefficients of the above
Q(x)
may depend on
\xi
.
Similarly, if
-P(x)
is SOS-convex, then
P(x)
is called
SOS-concave
.
We refer to
[
19
]
and
[
20
, Chapter 10.5]

for more details about convex matrix polynomials.

3.
Sufficient and necessary conditions for tightness

In this section, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for
the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy to be tight for solving (
1.1
).
Denote the degree

(3.1)

k_{0}:=\max_{i\in[m]}\Big(\lceil\deg(f)/2\rceil,\lceil\deg(G_{i})/2\rceil\Big).

For
k\geq k_{0}
, the
k
th order sparse matrix SOS relaxation for (
1.1
) is

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}f_{k}^{spa}\coloneqq&\max&\gamma\\
&\mbox{s.t.}&f-\gamma\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}.\end{array}\right.

Its dual optimization problem is the
k
th order sparse matrix moment relaxation

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}f_{k}^{smo}\coloneqq&\min&\langle f,y\rangle\coloneqq\langle f_{1},y_{\Delta_{1}}\rangle+\cdots+\langle f_{m},y_{\Delta_{m}}\rangle\\
&\mbox{s.t.}&L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&&M_{k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&&y_{0}=1,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}.\end{array}\right.

We refer to Subsection
2.3
for the above notation.
Recall that
f_{\min}
denotes the minimum value of (
1.1
).
When the running intersection property (RIP) holds, if each
\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}{[G_{i}]}
is archimedean,
it is shown in
[
14
]
that
f_{k}^{spa}\rightarrow f_{\min}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
When
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{\min}
for some
k
,
the hierarchy of sparse SOS relaxation (
3.2
)
is said to be
tight
. Similarly, if
f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
for some
k
,
then the hierarchy (
3.3
) is
tight
.
If they are both
tight
, the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is said to be
tight
,
or to have
finite convergence
.

In the following, we prove a sufficient and necessary condition for
the tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
).

(i)

For a relaxation order
k\geq k_{0}
, it holds

(3.4)

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}

if and only if there exist sparse polynomials

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
such that

(3.5)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\in\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]_{2k},\ i=1,\ldots,m.\end{gathered}}

The equation in the above is equivalent to

f-f_{\min}\,=\,(f_{1}+p_{1})+\cdots+(f_{m}+p_{m}).

(ii)

When (
3.5
) holds for some order
k
,
the minimum value
f_{\min}
of (
1.1
)
is achievable if and only if all sparse polynomials
f_{i}+p_{i}

have a common zero in
K
, i.e., there exists
u\in K
such that

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0
for all
i\in[m]
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\gamma=f_{\min}
,
S=\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}
, and

S_{i}=\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]_{2k}
for each
i=1,\ldots,m
.
Note that
f=f_{1}+\dots+f_{m}
and each
f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
.
Observe that

S=S_{1}+\cdots+S_{m},\quad\text{each}\ S_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}].

By
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
,
it holds that
f-\gamma\in S

if and only if there exist polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+\gamma=0,\quad f_{i}+p_{i}\in S_{i}\text{ for each }i.

(ii) Assume (
3.5
) holds for some order
k
.

(
\Rightarrow
): Suppose
f_{\min}
is achievable for (
1.1
),
then there exists a minimizer
u\in K
such that
f_{\min}=f(u)
. By the assumption that (
3.5
) holds, we have

f-f_{\min}=-\Big(f_{\min}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}+p_{i})\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}.

Since
p_{1}+\dots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,
it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}))=0.

Since each
f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0
on
K_{\Delta_{i}}
,
we have

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0

for all
i\in[m]
. Therefore,

u\in K
is a common zero of all
f_{i}+p_{i}
.

(
\Leftarrow
): Suppose
u\in K
is a common zero of all
f_{i}+p_{i}
, then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=f(u)+p_{1}(u_{\Delta_{1}})+\cdots+p_{m}(u_{\Delta_{m}})=0.

Since
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0
,

f(u)=f_{\min}
, so
f_{\min}
is achievable.
∎

The following is an exposition of the above theorem.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\Delta_{1}=\{1,2\}

Consider the following sparse matrix polynomial optimization problem

Since there exist polynomials

When

However, it is possible that the optimal value of (

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Delta_{1}=\{1,2\}
and
\Delta_{2}=\{2,3\}
.
Consider the following sparse matrix polynomial optimization problem
(

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}&x_{1}-x_{3}\\

In the above, each

We claim that

, since for all

,

\displaystyle f+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{4}\left[\Big(1+\frac{2x_{1}}{\epsilon}\Big)^{2}+\Big(1-\frac{2x_{2}}{\epsilon}\Big)^{2}+\Big(1+\frac{2x_{2}}{\epsilon}\Big)^{2}+\Big(1-\frac{2x_{3}}{\epsilon}\Big)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\

.

The

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
): Suppose that for all
\epsilon>0
, (
3.8
) holds for some polynomials

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
. Then

f-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)=-\Big[f_{\min}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}+p_{i}+\epsilon)\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}.

th order sparse SOS relaxation
(

), so

.

is arbitrary, we get

.
On the other hand, we always have

(
\Rightarrow

) is tight.

For each

Applying

, Lemma 2.1]
,
we let

.
So there exist polynomials

) holds.
∎

.

4.
Detecting tightness and extracting minimizers

Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
characterize tightness of the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
).
In this section, we discuss how to detect the tightness
f^{smo}_{k}=f_{\min}

and get minimizers of (
1.1
).

Let
y^{*}
be a minimizer of the sparse moment relaxation (
3.3
).
We say that
y^{*}
satisfies the
flat truncation
condition (see
[
20
]
) if there exists
t\in[k_{0},k]
such that for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,
it holds (let
d_{i}\coloneqq\lceil\deg(G_{i})/2\rceil
)

(4.1)

r_{i}\coloneqq\mbox{rank}\,M^{(t)}_{\Delta_{i}}[y_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]=\mbox{rank}\,M^{(t-d_{i})}_{\Delta_{i}}[y_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}].

We refer to (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) for the notation
y_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}
and the moment matrix
M^{(t)}_{\Delta_{i}}[y_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]
.
When (
4.1
) holds, there exist support sets

(4.2)

\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{u^{(i,1)},\ldots,u^{(i,r_{i})}\}\subseteq K_{\Delta_{i}}

and scalars
\lambda_{i,1},\ldots,\lambda_{i,r_{i}}
such that for each
i
, it holds

(4.3)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}{y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}=\lambda_{i,1}[u^{(i,1)}]_{2t}+\cdots+\lambda_{i,r_{i}}[u^{(i,r_{i})}]_{2t},}\\
\lambda_{i,1}>0,\ldots,\lambda_{i,r_{i}}>0,\quad\lambda_{i,1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i,r_{i}}=1.\end{gathered}}

In the above,
y_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}
denotes the degree-
2t
truncation of

:

y_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}\,\coloneqq\,(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{2t}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

This is shown in
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
.
A numerical method for computing points
u^{(i,1)},\ldots,u^{(i,r_{i})}
is introduced in
[
6
]
(also see
[
20
]
).
Furthermore, if
x^{*}
satisfies
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}
for every
i
, then
f(x^{*})=f_{k}^{smo}
implies that
f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
and
x^{*}
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).
This is because if
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}
for each
i
, then
x^{*}
must be feasible for (
1.1
), thus
f(x^{*})\geq f_{\min}
, while
f_{k}^{smo}
is an lower bound for
f_{\min}
.
In the following, we show that if (
4.1
) hold with
f^{smo}_{t}=f^{smo}_{k}
, then
f(x^{*})=f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
holds for all
x^{*}
such that each
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
y^{*}
be a minimizer of (
3.3
).
Suppose there exists
t\in[k_{0},k]
such that the flat truncation condition (
4.1
) holds and
f^{smo}_{t}=f^{smo}_{k}
.

(i)

If
x^{*}
satisfies
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,
then
f_{\min}=f^{smo}_{k}
and
x^{*}
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

(ii)

In the item (i), if, in addition, there is no duality gap between (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), then
f_{\min}=f_{k}^{spa}
.

Proof.

Since
y^{*}
satisfies the flat truncation condition (
4.1
), the decomposition (
4.3
) holds for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.
So, there exist positive scalars

\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
and moment matrices
W_{\Delta_{i}}\succeq 0
such that for each
i=1,\ldots,m
,

M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]=\rho_{i}[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}^{T}+W_{\Delta_{i}}.

Let
\rho\coloneqq\min\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}\}
, and let
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}
be the tms such that
\hat{y}_{\alpha}=(x^{*})^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{U}_{k}
.
The subvector
\hat{y}|_{2t}
is feasible for (
3.3
)
with the relaxation order equal to
t
, since every
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}\subseteq K_{\Delta_{i}}
.

For the case that
\rho=1
, it is clear that every
\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}=\{x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\}
,
and the conclusion follows directly.
In the following, we consider the case that
\rho<1
. Let

\tilde{y}\coloneqq(y^{*}-\rho\hat{y})/(1-\rho).

Then, for each
i
, it holds

L_{G_{i}}^{(t)}[\tilde{y}]=\frac{1}{1-\rho}(L_{G_{i}}^{(t)}[y^{*}]-\rho L_{G_{i}}^{(t)}[\hat{y}])\succeq 0,

by (

) and the fact that

, where

is the label such that

.
Similarly, one can show that

.
So,
\tilde{y}|_{2t}
is also feasible for (
3.3
) with the relaxation order equal to

.
Therefore, by the assumption that
f^{smo}_{t}=f^{smo}_{k}
, we have

f^{smo}_{t}=\left<f,y^{*}\right>=\left<f,\hat{y}\right>=f(x^{*}).

This completes the proof.
∎

By Theorem
4.1
, once we get a minimizer
y^{*}
for (
3.3
),
we may check whether the moment relaxation is tight and extract minimizers by checking (
4.1
).
Moreover, since we usually solve (
3.3
) with an increasing relaxation order

,
we can use the optimal value of moment relaxations
with lower relaxation orders to check if

holds or not.

Summarizing the above, we get the following algorithm

).

Algorithm 4.2
.

For (

),

Let

) of order

Step 2::

.

Let

and go to Step 1.

Example 4.3

5.
Convex sparse matrix constrained optimization

This section discusses convex sparse matrix polynomial optimization.
We consider that each
f_{i}
is convex in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
and each
G_{i}
is concave in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.

Suppose
u\in K
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).
Recall that
\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})
denotes the derivative of

G_{i}
at
u_{\Delta_{i}}
, given by (
2.1
),
and
\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}
denotes the adjoint mapping
of
\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})
, given by (
2.2
).
If the nondegeneracy condition (
2.3
) holds at
u
,
or the
Slater’s condition
1
1

1

Slater’s condition is said to hold for (
1.1
)
if there exists
u
such that
G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succ 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

holds, then there exist Lagrange multiplier matrices
\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ell_{i}}
such that

(5.1)

\left\{\ \begin{gathered}\nabla f(u)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}[\Lambda_{i}],\\
\Lambda_{i}\succeq 0,\kern 5.0ptG_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0,\ i\in[m],\\
\langle\Lambda_{i},G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\rangle=0,\ i\in[m].\end{gathered}\right.

The above is called the
first order optimality condition
(FOOC).
We refer to
[
25
,
26
]
for more details about optimality conditions of nonlinear semidefinite optimization.
For the above
\Lambda_{i}
, define the Lagrange function

(5.2)

\mathcal{L}_{i}(x)\,\coloneqq\,f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})-\langle\Lambda_{i},G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\rangle.

Denote the symmetric
n_{i}
-by-
n_{i}
matrix
H_{i}
,
with entries

(H_{i})_{st}\coloneqq 2\big\langle\Lambda_{i},\nabla_{x_{s}}G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{{\dagger}}\nabla_{x_{t}}G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\big\rangle,

for
s,t\in\Delta_{i}
.
In the above,
\nabla_{x_{s}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
x_{s}
, and the superscript
{\dagger}
denotes the Moore-Penrose inverse.
Define

\mathcal{N}_{i}\coloneqq\Big\{v=(v_{j})_{j\in\Delta_{i}}:\sum_{j\in\Delta_{i}}v_{j}\cdot E^{T}\nabla_{x_{j}}G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})E=0\Big\},

where
E
is a matrix whose columns form a basis of
\ker\,G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
) and the FOOC (
5.1
) holds.
Assume each
f_{i}
is convex and each
G_{i}\in\mathcal{S}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{\ell_{i}\times\ell_{i}}
is concave. Then,

(i)

There exist sparse polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

(5.3)

\boxed{\begin{array}[]{c}\ p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\geq 0~\text{on}~K_{\Delta_{i}},\,i=1,\ldots,m.\end{array}}

(ii)

Suppose each
\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]
is archimedean.
Assume that for each
i
, the NDC (
2.3
) for
G_{i}
holds at
u_{\Delta_{i}}
,
\mbox{rank}\,G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+\mbox{rank}\,\Lambda_{i}=\ell_{i}
, and

v^{T}(\nabla^{2}_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}\mathcal{L}_{i}(u)+H_{i})v>0\quad\forall\,0\neq v\in\mathcal{N}_{i}.

Then, the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy (
3.2
)-(
3.3
)
is tight and we have

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}

for all
k
big enough.

Proof.

(i) For each
i=1,\ldots,m
, let

(5.4)

p_{i}(x)\,\coloneqq\,-(x-u)^{T}\big(\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}[\Lambda_{i}]\big)-f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}).

Since
f_{i}
and
G_{i}
only depend on
x_{\Delta_{i}}
, we have
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
.
Note that

f_{\min}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}),\quad\nabla f(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}).

For the above
p_{i}
, the first equation in (
5.1
) implies

\displaystyle p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x-u)^{T}\Big[\nabla f(u)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}[\Lambda_{i}]\Big]-\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+f_{\min}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x-u)^{T}0+0=0.

Note that for each
i=1,\ldots,m
, it holds

\displaystyle\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+\nabla p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})

\displaystyle=\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\big(\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}[\Lambda_{i}]\big)

\displaystyle=\nabla G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{*}[\Lambda_{i}].

Thus, by the assumption that (
5.1
) holds, each
u_{\Delta_{i}}
satisfies the FOOC for

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Delta_{i}}}&f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\\
\mbox{s.t.}&G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0.\end{array}\right.

Since
p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})
is linear in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
, then
f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})
is convex in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
. So,
u_{\Delta_{i}}
is a minimizer of (
5.5
).
By (
5.4
), we can see that

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0.

Thus, the minimum value of (
5.5
) is
0
and
f_{i}+p_{i}\geq 0
on
K_{\Delta_{i}}
.
Therefore, (
5.3
) holds.

(ii)
For each
i
, note that
\mathcal{L}_{i}(x)
is the Lagrange function for the optimization problem (
5.5
).
By the given assumption, the nondegeneracy condition, strict complementarity condition, and second order sufficient condition all hold at
u_{\Delta_{i}}
for (
5.5
).
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
be the polynomials in item (i).
By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f_{i}+p_{i}\in\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[G_{i}]_{2k_{i}}.

Since
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0
by item (i), Theorem
3.1
(i) implies that

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k},

for all
k\geq\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\}
.
Therefore, the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy (
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is tight.
∎

Now we consider the special case that (
1.1
) is SOS-convex.
We refer to Section
2.4
for the SOS-convexity/concavity.
Recall that
k_{0}
is given in (
3.1
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).
Assume each
f_{i}
is SOS-convex and each
G_{i}
is SOS-concave. Then,

(i)

For all
k\geq k_{0}
, we have
f^{smo}_{k}=f_{\min}
. Moreover, if Slater’s condition holds, then
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{\min}
and

(5.6)

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{QM}[G]_{spa,2k}.

(ii)

For every minimizer
y^{*}
of (
3.3
), the point

x^{*}\coloneqq(y^{*}_{e_{1}},\ldots,y^{*}_{e_{n}})

is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

Proof.

(i) Suppose
y
is a feasible solution of the relaxation (
3.3
) and let
u\coloneqq(y_{e_{1}},\ldots,y_{e_{n}})
.
For each
i
, pick an arbitrary
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{i}}
. Then the scalar polynomial

g_{\xi}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\coloneqq\xi^{T}G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\xi

is SOS-concave in

because each
G_{i}
is SOS-concave by the assumption.
Since each
f_{i}
is SOS-convex, by Jensen’s inequality (see
[
16
]
or
[
20

), we have

(5.7)

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\leq\langle f_{i},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle,\quad-g_{\xi}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\leq\langle-g_{\xi},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle.

The second inequality of (
5.7
) implies that

\xi^{T}G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\xi=g_{\xi}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\geq\langle g_{\xi},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle=\xi^{T}G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\xi.

Since
y
is feasible for (
3.3
), the localizing matrix

L_{G_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0.

Note that
G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]
is a principal sub-matrix of

, so

G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\succeq 0\quad\text{and hence}\quad\xi^{T}G_{i}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\xi\geq 0.

Since
\xi
is arbitrary, we know
G_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0
.
This is true for all
i
, hence
u
is a feasible point for (
1.1
).
Also, by the first inequality of (
5.7
),

(5.8)

f(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle f_{i},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle=\langle f,y\rangle.

The above holds for all
y
that is feasible for (
3.3
),
so
f_{\min}\leq f_{k}^{smo}
.
On the other hand, we always have
f_{k}^{smo}\leq f_{\min}
.
Therefore,
f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
.

) has strictly feasible points (see Theorem 2.5.2 of
[
20
]
). So, the strong duality holds between (

) and (
3.3
), and (
3.2
) achieves its optimal value. Therefore,
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
and (
5.6
) holds.

(ii) Let
y^{*}
be a minimizer of (
3.3
).
Then
\left<f,y^{*}\right>\geq f(x^{*})

is feasible for (

) . Therefore, we have

which forces

is a minimizer of (

).
∎

f_{1}(x_{\Delta_{1}})\,\coloneqq\,x_{1}^{6}+x_{2}^{6}+x_{3}^{6}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{4}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{4}+x_{3}^{2}x_{1}^{4}
,

f_{2}(x_{\Delta_{2}})\,\coloneqq\,x_{2}(x_{2}^{3}-1)+x_{3}(x_{3}^{3}-1)+x_{4}(x_{4}^{3}-1)-2x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}-2x_{3}^{2}x_{4}^{2},

and for each
i

G_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\,\coloneqq\,\left[\begin{array}[]{rrr}2-x_{i}^{2}-2x_{i+2}^{2}&1+x_{i}x_{i+1}&x_{i}x_{i+2}\\
1+x_{i}x_{i+1}&2-x_{i+1}^{2}-2x_{i}^{2}&1+x_{i+1}x_{i+2}\\
x_{i}x_{i+2}&1+x_{i+1}x_{i+2}&2-x_{i+2}^{2}-2x_{i+1}^{2}\\
\end{array}\right].

Then, both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are SOS-convex, and each

is SOS-convex
but not uniformly SOS-convex; see
[

.
For (

), we solve the sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxation (
3.2
)-(
3.3
) with relaxation order

.
By Theorem

, we get

6.2.
Joint minimizers

Given polynomials
f_{1}(x_{\Delta_{1}})
,…,
f_{m}(x_{\Delta_{m}})
,
we look for a joint local minimizer
u
for them, i.e.,
each subvector
u_{\Delta_{i}}
is a local minimizer of
f_{i}
.
Consider the unconstrained optimization problem

(6.4)

\min\limits_{x_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Delta_{i}}}\quad f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}}).

The first and second order optimality conditions are

(6.5)

\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0,\quad\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}^{2}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succeq 0.

The above is necessary for
u_{\Delta_{i}}
to be a local minimizer for (
6.4
).
If the symbol
\succeq
in (
6.5
) is replaced by
\succ
, then
u_{\Delta_{i}}
must be a local minimizer.
As shown in
[
18
]
, when
f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})
has generic coefficients, if
u_{\Delta_{i}}
is a local minimizer, then (
6.5
) holds with
\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}^{2}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succ 0
.

It is interesting to observe that (
6.5
) is equivalent to

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})^{T}\\
\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})&\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}^{2}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\end{array}\right]\succeq 0.

This leads to the sparse optimization problem

(6.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f_{1}(x_{\Delta_{1}})+\cdots+f_{m}(x_{\Delta_{m}})\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})^{T}\\
\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})&\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}^{2}f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\end{array}\right]\succeq 0,i=1,\ldots,m.\end{array}\right.

We remark that if
u
is a minimizer of (
6.6
)
and each
\nabla_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}^{2}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\succ 0
, then
u
is a joint local minimizer
for the polynomials
f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})
.

6.3.
Center points for sets given by PMIs

Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
be given matrix polynomials in
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For each
i
, consider the semialgebraic set

(6.8)

P_{i}\,\coloneqq\,\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:G_{i}(z)\succeq 0\}.

The sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
may or may not intersect.
We look for a point
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that the sum of squared distances from
v
to all
P_{i}
is minimum.
This can be formulated as the optimiation problem

(6.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m},v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|z^{(i)}-v\|^{2}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&G_{i}(z^{(i)})\succeq 0,\ i=1,\ldots,m.\end{array}\right.

Let
x\coloneqq(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)},v)
, and denote

x_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq(z^{(i)},v),\quad f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\coloneqq\|z^{(i)}-v\|^{2},\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

Then, (
6.9
) is a sparse matrix polynomial optimization problem in the form of (
1.1
).
For every minimizer
x^{*}=(z^{(1,*)},\ldots,z^{(m,*)},v^{*})
of (
6.9
), the point

is the projection of
v^{*}
to
P_{i}
.
Note that if

, then the minimum value of (
6.9
) is
0
.

Conclusions

This paper studies the sparse polynomial optimization problem with matrix constraints,
given in the form (
1.1
). We study the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
) to solve it.
First, we prove a sufficient and necessary condition for this sparse hierarchy to be tight.
This is the condition (
3.5
) shown in Theorem
3.1
.
We also discuss how to detect the tightness and how to extract minimizers.
The main criterion is to use flat truncation (
4.1
), which is justified in Theorem
4.1
.
When this optimization problem is convex,
we prove the sufficient and necessary condition
for the tightness holds under some general assumptions.
In particular, when the problem is SOS-convex,
we show that the sparse matrix Moment-SOS relaxation is tight for all relaxation orders.
These results are shown in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
.
Numerical experiments are provided to show that
the sparse matrix Moment-SOS hierarchy is often tight.

ECG challenge: a paradoxical response
Authors: Yew Chung Chan, Darcy Jamih, Hong Yee Lim
Published: 2026

Clinical vignette

A 65-year-old man was referred for incidental finding of bradycardia. Further history revealed subtle symptoms of reduced exercise tolerance, although he was minimally symptomatic. Electrocardiography (ECG) demonstrated second-degree atrioventricular (AV) block with predominant 2:1 conduction and occasional 3:2 conduction with Wenckebach periodicity, together with incomplete right bundle branch block (RBBB) and left anterior fascicular block (LAFB) (Figure 1A). Carotid sinus massage resulted in improvement in AV conduction, from 2:1 to 1:1 conduction (Figure 1B). In contrast, atropine administration worsened AV conduction, with progression from 2:1 conduction to high-grade AV block (Figure 1C).

(A) Initial electrocardiography demonstrated sinus rhythm at a rate of 96 bpm, with second-degree AV block showing predominantly 2:1 AV conduction and occasional 3:2 conduction exhibiting Wenckebach periodicity. Incomplete right bundle branch block and left anterior fascicular block were also present. (B) Electrocardiography recorded during carotid sinus massage. Sinus rhythm was maintained, with a reduction in sinus rate to 78 bpm. AV conduction improved to 1:1. (C) Electrocardiography following administration of 3 mg intravenous atropine demonstrating an increase in sinus rate to 100 bpm. AV conduction deteriorated to high-grade AV block. Four QRS complexes (red stars) occurred prematurely with a constant PR interval following the preceding P waves, consistent with intermittent conducted beats.

Question 1

Which of the following statements is correct?

The observed response to carotid sinus massage suggests AV nodal disease.

The observed response to atropine suggests AV nodal disease.

The observed response to atropine suggests AV nodal disease.

Mobitz type I second-degree AV block may arise from infranodal disease.

Mobitz type I second-degree AV block may arise from infranodal disease.

Mobitz type I second-degree AV block is usually symptomatic.

Mobitz type I second-degree AV block is usually symptomatic.

An ECG showing only 2:1 AV block can be classified as Mobitz type I or Mobitz type II.

An ECG showing only 2:1 AV block can be classified as Mobitz type I or Mobitz type II.

The correct answer is C.

Discussion and explanation

Mobitz type I second-degree AV block is usually asymptomatic and most often arises within the AV node; however, it can occasionally be infranodal.1,2 In this patient, AV conduction improved when carotid sinus massage slowed the sinus rate, suggesting infranodal disease (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1 and Figure S2), because increased vagal tone would usually worsen AV nodal block.2 Conversely, intravenous atropine increased the sinus rate and worsened conduction from 2:1 to high-grade AV block, further supporting an infranodal site; atropine would generally improve AV nodal conduction.2 An ECG showing only 2:1 AV block cannot be classified as Mobitz type I or II because there are no consecutively conducted P waves from which to assess PR-interval behaviour.3

The correct answer is D.

Discussion and explanation

A prospective study of patients with an indication for permanent ventricular pacing showed that, compared with AV nodal Wenckebach, infranodal Wenckebach was associated with a longer median HV interval (90 vs. 49 ms), less frequent isolated first-degree AV block (8.3% vs. 60%), more frequent RBBB with LAFB (75% vs. 10%), and a lesser PR increment at the onset of AV Wenckebach (20.5 vs. 80 ms). In the same study, 2:1 AV block developed at longer cycle lengths during incremental atrial pacing in infranodal than in AV nodal Wenckebach.4

Question 3

When the site of second-degree AV block cannot be determined from the resting ECG alone, which of the following tests is not useful for localization of the block?

Carotid sinus massage.

Carotid sinus massage.

Atropine challenge.

Atropine challenge.

Exercise treadmill stress test.

Exercise treadmill stress test.

Invasive electrophysiological study.

Invasive electrophysiological study.

Head-up-tilt-table testing.

Head-up-tilt-table testing.

The correct answer is E.

Discussion and explanation

In selected patients with second-degree AV block of uncertain level, carotid sinus massage, pharmacological challenge with atropine, and an electrophysiological study may be considered to localize the block. Exercise testing is also reasonable in patients with 2:1 AV block of unknown level. Changes in AV conduction during carotid sinus massage, atropine administration, or exercise may help distinguish AV nodal from infranodal disease, whereas an electrophysiological study can directly identify AV nodal, intra-His, or infra-His block.2 Head-up-tilt-table testing evaluates susceptibility to reflex syncope but does not localize the site of AV block.

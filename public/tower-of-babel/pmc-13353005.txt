State transitions and related factors of type 2 diabetes based on multi-state Markov model among a Chinese health check-up cohort
Authors: Xue-Jiao Yang, Qiong Wu, Yan Xu, Zhen-Yun Li, Dai-Yi Zhang, Jian-Hong Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo explore the transitions of different blood glucose states based on a multi-state Markov model among the Chinese health check-up population.MethodsIn this research, the multi-state Markov model was built based on 4776 blood glucose status assessments in 1677 Chinese participants. A quantitative longitudinal study was conducted to explore diabetes progression, providing information on the transition probability, the sojourn time, and the risk factors in three blood glucose states, namely normal (state 1), prediabetes (state 2), and diabetes (state 3).ResultsAmong 4776 transition cases, there were 318 cases (6.7%) with remission (state 2→1), 3505 cases (73.4%) with stable disease (state 1→1, 2→2, 3→3), and 953 cases (19.9%) with progressive disease (state 1→2, 1→3, 2→3). The probability of progression from normal state to diabetes was 1.1% within 1 year and increased to 11.9% within 6 years. Increased age, blood pressure, and body mass index were risk factors for progression to diabetes in the normal state or prediabetes.ConclusionsThis article serves as a reference for targeted interventions for diabetes progression among the Chinese health check-up population. Obesity, hypertension and hyperlipidemia are risk factors that worsen the progression of diabetes, and preventive actions should be taken at an early stage.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a worldwide disease, which increasingly affects society. According to the latest International Diabetes Federation (IDF), the prevalence of T2DM among adults reached 536.6 million cases (10.5%) by 2021, and 783.2 million (12.2%) people worldwide will suffer from diabetes by 2045 [1]. A study reported that the prevalence of diabetes in China increased from 0.7% in 1980 to 12.8% in 2017, representing an 18-fold increase over the last four decades, and over one-third of adults in China have prediabetes with relatively low awareness, intervention, and control rates [2]. Compared with people who do not have diabetes, T2DM patients have a 15% increased risk of all-cause mortality [3]. Diabetes mellitus is associated with the occurrence and development of hypertension, obesity, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Furthermore, the prognosis is poor and irreversible. It is widely acknowledged that various risk factors influence the development of diabetes. However, the pathogenesis is still inconclusive, and the existing prevention and control measures have not completely curbed the occurrence and development of diabetes and its complications.

The concept of prediabetes emerged in the late 1970s as a result of a better understanding of the natural progression of diabetes [4]. Individuals with impaired fasting glycemia (IFG) and/or impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) have been referred to as having prediabetes, indicating a relatively high risk for the future development of diabetes [5]. Prediabetes is often associated with obesity, hypertension, and dyslipidemia. Over the past years, the World Health Organization (WHO) and the American Diabetes Association (ADA) have updated the definition of prediabetes [6]. These categories facilitate the identification of individuals along the continuum of hyperglycemia who do not meet current thresholds for a diabetes diagnosis but who are at high risk of developing diabetes. Worldwide, the prevalence of prediabetes is increasing and more than 470 million people will have prediabetes by 2030 [7].

T2DM is a long-term illness. The pathogenesis of T2DM is a complex, multistage and multigenetic process, of which the precise mechanisms are not yet fully understood. From a discovery perspective, logistic regression and Cox proportional models have been widely used in the research to identify risk factors related to T2DM. However, Cox and logistic regression models offer little information about the dynamic process of changes in blood glucose levels. Cox regression ignores the multi-outcome characteristics of the event development while logistic regression ignores the influence of time on the event development. In contrast, the multi-state Markov model considers both multi-outcome and time effects and has been widely used in the medical field to analyze the progression of various diseases. As for the perspective of timely intervention and disease prediction, it is important to identify the natural history outcome of the disease and the risk factors in the outcome process.

The natural evolution of T2DM is a complex and dynamic development process, in which several pathogenic processes are involved. The long natural history of T2DM and its stepwise pathogenesis make it particularly amenable to prevention. Therefore, it is of great significance to study its natural progression for early detection and prevention. The multi-state Markov model divides a disease into different health states, simulates the development process of the disease according to the conversion probability of each state within a certain time, and is a continuous and dynamic observation of the occurrence and development of diseases based on the perspective of a random process.

In the present study, we applied a multi-state Markov model to investigate the state transitions and related risk factors for T2DM. The primary aim of this study was to quantify the dynamic transitions among normal state, prediabetes, and diabetes. Using the well-established model, the sojourn times of each progressive state and the transition probabilities from one state to others can be evaluated, thus providing a statistical basis for targeted intervention before the onset of diabetes. A secondary objective was to identify demographic and clinical factors associated with these transitions, thereby providing insights into the progression and potential prevention of T2DM in a Chinese health check-up population.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

Subjects of this retrospective, observational cohort study were selected from the adults who came to the Health Management Center of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University to undergo health examinations from 2015 to 2021. The study included people who had a physical examination for at least two consecutive years and completed tests for fasting blood glucose (FBG), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) or other required parameters. Upon each physical examination, each participant’s height, weight, abdominal circumference, and blood pressure were measured, and blood biochemical tests were also performed to assess the participant’s health status. Adults were considered “ineligible” to participate if they did not meet the requirement of having complete medical records for two consecutive years at the time of the first exam. The individuals who had a history of diabetes (self-reported physician diagnosis, or current use of anti-diabetic medications), tumor, hyperthyreosis, Cushing syndrome and severe organic disease were also excluded.

All the checkup records were collected from 1 November 2015 to 31 October 2021. Participants underwent annual physical examinations during follow-up. The median follow-up period was 4 years (interquartile range = 2; min-max = 2–7 years) and the mean follow-up time was about 4 years. Information collected at the initial checkup was considered as the baseline.

The study was performed according to the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University. The ethical approval number of this study was 2023 − 182.

Assessment of glycemic status and covariates

In this Markov model, there were three diabetes states of interest. Diagnosis of glycemic status was based on the American Diabetes Association’s new classification standards, and parameter cutoffs were summarized in Table 1 [8]. Not all participants had both FBG and HbA1c measurements available at each annual examination. When only one indicator was available, the glycemic state was determined based on that single measurement. When both FBG and HbA1c were available but indicated different glycemic categories, the participant was assigned to the worse category. Individuals with neither FBG nor HbA1c data were excluded from the analysis.

Gender, age, body mass index (BMI), plasma and urinary biochemical data including serum albumin, triglyceride, lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)], and total cholesterol (TCHO) were selected as the covariates. General information was obtained from the medical records of the enrolled patients in a computerized registry database. Systolic blood pressure (SBP)/Diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and heart rate were determined using Omron HBP-9020 (Omron, Kyoto, Japan) after a minimum of 5-min rest. For the biochemical analyses, venous blood was collected in the morning at approximately 8:00 a.m. after 12 h of fasting.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics including mean, standard deviation, median, lower quartile, and upper quartile were used to describe baseline demographic and clinical characteristics. The comparison between groups was analyzed by ANOVA and the rank-sum test. The categorical variables were expressed as frequency and percentage, and the comparison between groups was analyzed by X2 test. Hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used in the statistical analysis. In this study, the reported HRs represent transition-specific intensity ratios derived from the Markov model, indicating the relative change in instantaneous transition intensity associated with each covariate.

Diabetes is a progressive disease. The disease process was assumed to evolve continuously over time, while observations were recorded at discrete yearly intervals. In this study, a continuous-time multi-state Markov model was applied to panel data with yearly observations. There were three glycemic states of interest: state 1 (normal state), state 2 (prediabetes state), and state 3 (diabetes state). Figure 1 illustrates seven possible transitions among the three transient states: normal → normal, normal → prediabetes, normal → diabetes, prediabetes → normal, prediabetes → prediabetes, prediabetes → diabetes and diabetes → diabetes. The normal state and the prediabetes state can transition to each other or maintain the status quo, which is a transient state. While diabetes is an absorbing state, once an individual transitions to the diabetes state, they cannot return to the normal state or prediabetes state. These individuals did not contribute to any transition risk sets and were therefore not included in the estimation of transition-specific hazard ratios. The multi-state Markov model was built using the msm package [9] of R software (version 3.4.2).

Results

Baseline characteristics were collected at the initial checkup. Among 1677 participants, 523 (31.19%) were in the normal state, 1078 (64.28%) participants were in the prediabetes state, and 76 (4.53%) were in the diabetes state at the initial (baseline) checkup.

The mean age of 1677 subjects was 52.0 years. The average ages of the three groups were significantly different from each other. Gender composition was mostly male, as males represented nearly 70% and females represented nearly 30%. The ages, SBP, FBG, uric acid (UA), serum albumin, triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), Lp(a), TCHO and HbA1c of subjects were significantly different between the three groups (Table 2).

Figure 2 illustrates the estimated transition probability over different time horizons (1–6 years) derived from the multi-state Markov model, and the reported values represent model-based cumulative transition probabilities over the specified time interval. For instance, if the next checkup is performed after a 1-year interval, a participant in the normal state at the current checkup has a 30.6% chance of remaining in the normal state, a 68.3% chance of progressing to the prediabetes state, and a 1.1% chance of developing diabetes at the next checkup. It was noteworthy that as time went by, participants had a higher probability of developing diabetes. For example, the probability of transitioning from the prediabetes state to the diabetes state was 2.5% during the 1-year follow-up period and increased dramatically to 13.6% during the 6-year follow-up period.

The covariates in this study mainly included demographic indicators, general examination results, and blood biochemical indicators. Table 5 summarizes the effect of covariates on the blood glucose state transition. The model was assessed using likelihood ratio tests comparing nested models with and without covariates. The inclusion of relevant predictors significantly improved model fit. These internal comparisons support the appropriateness of the specified model structure. In addition, sensitivity checks were conducted through alternative covariate specifications and internal comparisons. Age, blood pressure, BMI, serum albumin, HDL-c and TCHO had significant associations with possible changes. For example, participants with lower age, SBP, BMI, LDL-c, Lp(a), and higher HDL-c were more likely to reverse prediabetes status. Increasing age, blood pressure and BMI were risk factors for developing diabetes from the normal state or prediabetes state. Meanwhile, the decrease in age, SBP, BMI, and LDL-c at remission of diabetes status was a protective factor.

Discussion

Diabetes is a complex, progressive disease with multiple stages, and it is recognized as a significant public health problem in China with an increasing incidence and an earlier age at diagnosis. A large sample study of 14 provinces in China showed that the age-standardized prevalence of total diabetes and prediabetes was 9.7% and 15.5%, respectively. This means that more than 240 million Chinese adults are affected by diabetes and prediabetes [10]. Diabetes increases the risk of microvascular and macrovascular complications and premature death in the general population, imposing a significant economic burden on society [11–13].

Therefore, it is important to explore the transition between different glycemic states and the risk factors related to the progression to formulate preventive intervention measures and delay the progression of the disease. In this study, a multi-state Markov model was used to investigate the probability of state transitions in diabetes, the sojourn times of each state, and the factors affecting the progression or reversal of diabetes.

The present study had several major findings. First, among 4776 transitions, 318 (6.7%) showed remission, 3505 (73.4%) showed stable disease, and 953 (19.9%) showed progressive disease. These patterns suggest that, in this health check-up cohort, persistence or progression was more common than remission in the absence of intervention. Second, data on the transition probabilities showed that the possibility of progression to diabetes state increased steadily from 1 year to 6 years for both the normal and prediabetes states. Individuals with prediabetes were more likely to revert to normal state than to progress to clinical diabetes during the first year of follow-up; this advantage was lost by the beginning of the second year, and the probability of reversion decreased over time. The observed transition patterns suggest that the early stage of prediabetes may represent a potentially important window for intervention and lifestyle interventions should be strengthened in time for better effects. The instability of the normal → diabetes transition intensity likely reflects sparse events in this pathway. Larger cohorts or longer follow-up may provide more precise estimates for rare transitions. Third, the sojourn times in the normal and prediabetes states were 0.8 years and 11 years, respectively. In other words, the long duration of prediabetes allows people to detect the problem early and have enough time to take preventive measures. In a continuous-time Markov model, the sojourn time in each state is derived from the transition intensity matrix and is calculated as the inverse of the total outgoing transition intensity from that state. The relatively short estimated sojourn time in the normal state (0.8 years) likely relates to the characteristics of our health check-up cohort, which may represent a metabolically high-risk population undergoing regular screening. Mild glycemic abnormalities may therefore be detected and reclassified relatively quickly. Fourth, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and obesity were risk factors for diabetes progression. These results suggest that prevention and effective intervention for diabetes should be carried out at an early stage, and it is strongly recommended that people at high risk have their blood glucose checked at least annually.

At present, there is a paucity of studies on the progression and risk factors of diabetes using the multi-state Markov model. Zhang et al. examined the lifetime risk of developing diabetes in Chinese people with normal state or prediabetes [14]. They found that over the 19-year period, the probabilities of progression from prediabetes to diabetes after adjusting for competing risk of death were 46.8% for people aged from 20 to less than 40 years old, 60.4% for people aged from 40 to less than 60 years old, and 45.1% for those aged more than 60 years old. The 19-year risks of progression from normal state to diabetes after adjusting for competing risks of death were 12.9% for people aged from 20 to less than 40 years old, 24.1% for those from 40 to less than 60 years old, and 21.1% for ones more than 60 years old. Another multicenter longitudinal study of 6814 participants in the United States [15] showed that the transition probability for a normal individual to remain in the same status over 10 years was 0.63, and an increase in waist-to-hip ratio and BMI could be the most significant factors in the progression of the disease, which is consistent with our conclusion.

It is challenging to detect diabetes in the early stage when it is slowly developing, and there are few studies on the progression of diabetes based on the physical check-up population in China. Andreou et al. conducted a nationwide survey of the risk factors of the three glycemic state transitions (normal, prediabetes, diabetes) of diabetes using logistic models [16]. The findings demonstrated that excessive weight, hypertension, high serum total cholesterol, hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL levels may strongly influence the development of diabetes. However, traditional logistic regression models treat each state transition as independent outcome variables and ignore the potential effect of time on the occurrence of the target outcome. Moreover, the model cannot estimate the sojourn time and probability of transitions between states, so it has limitations in application. The multi-state Markov model not only considers the characteristics of disease progression but also evaluates the time of state transition and analyzes the factors affecting the transition between different diabetes states and the intensity of the influence.

In sum, we examined the natural progression of diabetes and risk factors. However, there are several limitations of this study summarized as follows: (1) In this study, the diabetes state was treated as an absorbing state, which means the reverse transition from the diabetes state to a normal or prediabetes state was not considered. Although diabetes remission and death are possible, our analysis focused on the natural course of disease progression based on available health examination data. Therefore, transitions from diabetes back to prediabetes or normal states and death were not modeled, which should be considered when interpreting the results. (2) The participants in our study were from only one city. Suzhou is an economically developed and highly urbanized city in eastern China, and the regional differences in genetics, lifestyle, and healthcare accessibility may influence diabetes progression. Therefore, the findings may not be representative in other populations, and future large-scale prospective multicenter studies are needed. (3) Some significant lifestyle habits covariates such as diet, smoking, exercise, and psychological status were not included in this study. The estimated transition probabilities represent the effects of measurable metabolic and clinical indicators under health examination conditions, but may underestimate the contribution of modifiable lifestyle factors. Further studies with more comprehensive and sophisticated settings are warranted for a more precise conclusion.

By quantifying transition intensities and identifying demographic and clinical factors associated with state changes, the multi-state Markov model provides a dynamic perspective on glycemic progression. In particular, individuals with obesity, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia demonstrated higher transition intensities towards worse glycemic states, and such high-risk individuals may benefit from more frequent glucose monitoring or earlier lifestyle intervention. Furthermore, individuals newly entering the prediabetes state may be an important group that requires timely preventive efforts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study quantified the dynamic transitions among normal state, prediabetes, and diabetes using a multi-state Markov model in a large Chinese health check-up population. The estimated transition probabilities, sojourn times, and associated risk factors provide valuable evidence for understanding the natural progression of diabetes. The results revealed that the first year of prediabetes is the best time window for intervention. Obesity, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia were identified as major determinants accelerating the transition towards diabetes. These findings highlight the importance of implementing early, targeted preventive strategies to slow or halt disease progression in populations at high risk of diabetes. Future research incorporating lifestyle and behavioral factors across multiple centers will further enhance the generalizability and clinical utility of these results.

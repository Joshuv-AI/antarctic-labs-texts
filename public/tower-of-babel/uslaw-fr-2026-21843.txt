Official Release of the MOVES6 Motor Vehicle Emissions Model for 
SIPs and Transportation Conformity

AGENCY: Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

ACTION: Notice of availability.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is announcing

[[Page 67566]]

the availability of the latest major release of the MOtor Vehicle 
Emission Simulator (MOVES) model, MOVES6. MOVES6 is the latest version 
of the EPA's state-of-the-science modeling tool for estimating 
emissions from cars, trucks, buses, and motorcycles based on the latest 
data and regulations. MOVES6 is available for use in State 
Implementation Plans (SIPs) and transportation conformity analyses. 
This notice starts a two-year grace period before MOVES6 will be 
required for use in transportation conformity determinations, both in 
new regional emissions analyses and in new hot-spot analyses.

DATES: The EPA's announcement of the MOVES6 emissions model for SIPs 
and transportation conformity determinations in States other than 
California is effective October 27, 2026. This announcement starts a 
two-year transportation conformity grace period that ends on October 
27, 2028. After this date, MOVES6 will be required for use as the 
latest EPA emissions model for new transportation conformity analyses 
other than California in both regional emissions analyses and in hot-
spot analyses.

II. What is MOVES6?

    MOVES6 is the EPA's latest official motor vehicle emissions model 
for State and local agencies to estimate volatile organic compounds 
(VOCs), nitrogen oxides (NOX), particulate matter 
(PM2.5 and PM10), carbon monoxide (CO), and other 
pollutants and precursors from cars, trucks, buses, and motorcycles for 
SIP purposes and transportation conformity determinations other than 
California.\4\ MOVES6 represents the

[[Page 67567]]

sixth major release of the MOVES series of models.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ In California, a different onroad emissions model, the 
EMissions FACtors (EMFAC) model, is used for regulatory purposes 
instead of MOVES. MOVES can also model emissions in the District of 
Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    MOVES is a flexible model using a variety of input and output 
options, allowing more than one way to develop emissions estimates. 
Like its predecessors, MOVES6 includes the capability to estimate 
criteria pollutant and precursor emissions from vehicle exhaust and 
evaporative emissions as well as brake wear and tire wear emissions. 
Also, like previous versions, MOVES6 does not include the capability to 
estimate emissions of re-entrained road dust. To estimate emissions 
from re-entrained road dust, practitioners should continue to use the 
latest approved methodologies.\5\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ See the EPA's Notice of Availability, Official Release of 
the January 2011 AP-42 Method for Estimating Re-Entrained Road Dust 
from Paved Roads (76 FR 6328; Feb. 4, 2011).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA is releasing MOVES6 to account for important legislative 
and regulatory changes. On February 12, 2026, the EPA finalized the 
Endangerment Finding Rescission Rule that repealed all greenhouse gas 
(GHG) emission standards for light-, medium-, and heavy-duty on-highway 
vehicles and engines.\6\ MOVES6 accounts for this change and also 
accounts for the Congressional disapproval of the waiver for 
California's Advanced Clean Trucks (ACT) regulations.\7\ MOVES6 also 
accounts for legislation that reduced incentives for electric vehicles 
(EVs) and EV charging infrastructure.\8\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \6\ 91 FR 7686 (Feb. 18, 2026) (the ``Endangerment Finding 
Rescission Rule'').
    \7\ In 2025, Congress enacted joint resolutions of disapproval 
under the Congressional Review Act for certain preemption waivers 
that the EPA had previously issued pursuant to CAA section 209, 
including ACT (see H.J. Res. 87). President Trump signed the 
resolution into law (see Pub. L. 119-15, 139 Stat. 65 (June 12, 
2025)). This resolution provides that the corresponding waiver 
``shall have no force or effect.'' As a result, the EPA's prior 
preemption waiver for ACT has no force or effect. Accordingly, ACT 
is preempted by the CAA, meaning that a State can no longer enforce 
or attempt to enforce the ACT regulations and that estimated 
emissions reductions attributed to the ACT regulations cannot be 
used for the CAA purposes of SIP development or conformity 
determinations.
    \8\ See Public Law 119-21, 139 Stat. 72 (July 4, 2025).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Previous versions of the MOVES model (i.e., MOVES5 and MOVES4) both 
accounted for the GHG vehicle standards in effect at the time those 
models were released, projecting that GHG standards would be met 
through greater adoption of EVs over time.\9\ However, these GHG 
standards have since been rescinded.\10\ In MOVES6, the EPA updated 
energy consumption rates and adjusted assumptions about the fraction of 
EVs and other fuel types.\11\ MOVES6 still projects that some fraction 
of vehicles are EVs in future years, but these fractions by year are 
generally smaller than in MOVES5 and MOVES4. In addition, MOVES6 
includes updated activity data and fixes incorrect values in MOVES5.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ See the EPA's Notice of Availability, Official release of 
the MOVES5 Motor Vehicle Emissions Model for SIPs and Transportation 
Conformity (89 FR 99862; Dec. 11, 2024). See also the EPA's Notice 
of Availability, Official release of the MOVES4 Motor Vehicle 
Emissions Model for SIPs and Transportation Conformity (88 FR 62567; 
Sept. 12, 2023). MOVES3 does not include EVs in the default 
information.
    \10\ See 91 FR 7686 (Feb. 18, 2026).
    \11\ The projections are based on the U.S. Department of Energy 
(DOE), U.S. Energy Information Administration ``Annual Energy 
Outlook 2025: Case Descriptions'' at 9 (explaining the alternate 
transportation case description) at: https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/archive/aeo25/assumptions/pdf/case_descriptions.pdf.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    There are no changes to the MOVES interface since MOVES5. The 
significant changes in MOVES6 are summarized below:
     EV fractions were reduced to account for the repeal of GHG 
standards and reductions in incentives for EVs and EV infrastructure. 
Medium- and heavy-duty EV fractions were also reduced to account for 
Congressional disapproval of the waiver for California's ACT 
regulations that included heavy-duty zero emission vehicle 
requirements.
     Energy consumption rates for model year (MY) 2017-2024 
were updated based on real-world data from the EPA's 2025 Automotive 
Trends Report.\12\ Rates for MY 2025 and later continued these trends.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \12\ See U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. (2026). The 2025 
EPA Automotive Trends Report: Fuel Economy and Technology since 
1975. EPA-420-R-26-001.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Historical and projected vehicle miles traveled (VMT) and 
vehicle populations were updated using the latest data on school buses 
and transit buses, as well as updated values from the U.S. Federal 
Highway Administration (FHWA) Highway Statistics (2024) and the DOE 
Annual Energy Outlook (2025).
     Emission rates were corrected to fix minor errors in 
MOVES5. The fixes affected exhaust hydrocarbon (HC), NOX, 
and PM from MYs 2031 and later medium-duty (Class 2b and 3) trucks, CO 
from MYs 2027 and later light-duty cars and trucks, and tire wear from 
all compressed natural gas (CNG) heavy-heavy-duty (Class 8) trucks.
     Gasoline properties in a few counties were updated to 
account for new data and changes to local fuel requirements since 
MOVES5.
    As a result of all these changes, estimates of emissions from 
MOVES6 may differ from earlier versions of MOVES. The net impact of 
these changes on estimated emissions will depend on many factors, 
including the specific area being modeled and the inputs used.
    For additional information on the updates included in MOVES6, 
please refer to the ``Overview of EPA's MOtor Vehicle Emission 
Simulator (MOVES6),'' found at the EPA's MOVES website. Specific 
information about MOVES6 inputs and algorithms can be found on the 
EPA's websites for MOVES Onroad Technical Reports and MOVES Nonroad 
Technical Reports.\13\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \13\ For more information, see the EPA's MOVES website at: 
https://www.epa.gov/moves.

III. SIPs and MOVES6

    The EPA has articulated the Agency's policy regarding the use of 
MOVES6 in SIP development in the MOVES6 Policy Guidance. This notice 
highlights certain aspects of the guidance, but State and local 
governments should refer to the guidance for more detailed information 
on how and when to use MOVES6 in reasonable further progress SIPs, 
attainment demonstrations, maintenance plans, inventory updates, and 
other SIP submissions.
    The Clean Air Act (CAA) and implementation rules for the National 
Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) describe how SIP inventories and 
control measures should be based on the most current information and 
applicable models available when a SIP is developed.\14\ Therefore, 
States should use the latest version of MOVES and planning assumptions 
available at the time that a SIP is developed to ensure the SIP is 
based on the most accurate estimates of emissions possible. Now that 
MOVES6 is available, it should be used in ozone, CO, PM, and nitrogen 
dioxide (NO2) SIP development for all States other than 
California as expeditiously as possible, as there is no grace period 
for the use of MOVES6 in SIPs. For example, MOVES6 should be used if a 
State is in the early stages of SIP development and significant work 
has not yet occurred or if work has not yet started for a new SIP 
requirement.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \14\ See CAA section 172(c)(3). See also 81 FR 58029 (Aug. 24, 
2016) and 83 FR 63022 (Dec. 6, 2018).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA also recognizes the time and level of effort involved in 
SIP development. In cases in which State and local agencies have 
already completed significant work on a SIP with MOVES5 or MOVES4 as of 
the MOVES6 release, they may continue to rely on this earlier version. 
For instance, SIP development may continue if a State has progressed 
far enough along that

[[Page 67568]]

switching from a previous model version would create a significant 
adverse impact on State resources (e.g., if attainment modeling has 
already been completed with MOVES5 or MOVES4 prior to the MOVES6 
release). However, as noted in section II of this notice, MOVES5 and 
MOVES4 contain national default EV projections that do not reflect the 
Endangerment Finding Rescission Rule or other updates in MOVES6. See 
sections 1.5 and 2.1 of the MOVES6 Policy Guidance for more information 
on using previous versions of MOVES in SIP development, including 
details on how to adjust EV projections in MOVES5 and MOVES4.
    States should consult with their EPA Regional Office if they have 
questions about how MOVES6 affects SIPs under development in specific 
nonattainment or maintenance areas. Early consultation can facilitate 
the EPA's consideration of whether to make an adequacy finding for 
transportation conformity purposes as well as the Agency's 
consideration of a SIP submission.
    The EPA does not require States to revise submitted SIPs or SIPs 
that have already been approved simply because a new motor vehicle 
emissions model is now available.\15\ States can, however, choose to 
update these SIPs with MOVES6, for example, if it is determined that it 
is appropriate to update motor vehicle emissions budgets (``budgets'') 
with the model for future transportation conformity determinations.\16\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ See Sierra Club v. EPA, 356 F.3d. 296, 308 (D.C. Cir. 2004) 
(``To require states to revise completed plans every time a new 
model is announced would lead to significant costs and potentially 
endless delays in the approval processes.'').
    \16\ See 40 CFR 93.101, (definition of ``motor vehicle emissions 
budget'').
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA recognizes that States may have relied on the EV 
assumptions in MOVES5 and MOVES4 in SIP development based on the 
Agency's statements in previous versions of the MOVES Policy and 
Technical Guidance. The EPA expects limited cases in which State and 
local agencies have already completed significant work with MOVES5 or 
MOVES4 and submitted a SIP prior to or shortly after the Endangerment 
Finding Rescission Rule. When evaluating these SIP submittals, the EPA 
will consider the latest MOVES model, assumptions, and guidance 
information available to a State at the time it developed its SIP.
    For complete explanations of how MOVES6 is to be implemented for 
SIP purposes, refer to the MOVES6 Policy Guidance.

IV. Transportation Conformity and MOVES6

    Transportation conformity is required under CAA section 176(c) to 
ensure that federally funded or approved highway and transit activities 
are consistent with (i.e., ``conform to'') the purpose of the SIP. 
Conformity to the purpose of the SIP means that transportation 
activities will not cause or contribute to new air quality violations, 
worsen existing violations, or delay timely attainment of the relevant 
NAAQS or any interim milestones.\17\ The EPA's transportation 
conformity regulations establish the criteria and procedures for 
determining whether metropolitan transportation plans, TIPs, and 
federally supported highway and transit projects conform to the 
SIP.\18\ Transportation conformity applies to designated nonattainment 
and maintenance areas for transportation-related criteria pollutants: 
ozone, PM2.5, PM10, CO, and NO2.\19\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ See 42 U.S.C. 7506(c).
    \18\ See 40 CFR parts 51 and 93.
    \19\ ``Maintenance areas'' are those areas initially designated 
nonattainment for a criteria pollutant and subsequently redesignated 
to attainment after 1990. Maintenance areas have SIPs developed 
under CAA section 175A. See also 40 CFR 93.101.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA has articulated the Agency's policy regarding the use of 
MOVES6 in transportation conformity determinations in the MOVES6 Policy 
Guidance. This notice highlights certain aspects of the guidance, but 
State and local governments should refer to the guidance for more 
detailed information on how and when to use MOVES6 in transportation 
conformity determinations.
    In this notice, the EPA is announcing the availability of MOVES6 
for use in transportation conformity determinations other than 
California. The EPA is also establishing a two-year grace period before 
MOVES6 will be required for use in regional emissions analyses for 
transportation conformity determinations of transportation plans and 
TIPs and in hot-spot analyses for project-level transportation 
conformity determinations that use the EPA's emissions model.\20\ The 
MOVES6 grace period for regional emissions and hot-spot analyses 
applies to the use of MOVES6 and any future minor revisions that occur 
during the grace period.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \20\ The grace period also applies in the case that a regional 
emissions analysis is done for a project not included in a 
conforming transportation plan or TIP (i.e., a project in an 
isolated rural area). See the transportation conformity regulations 
at 40 CFR 93.101 for the definition of an isolated rural area and 40 
CFR 93.109(g) for how conformity is done in an isolated rural area.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    This MOVES6 grace period is separate and distinct from the two-year 
grace period established with the release of the previous version of 
the model, MOVES5. The two-year grace period established by the EPA's 
December 2024 MOVES5 Notice of Availability in the Federal Register is 
unchanged by the release of MOVES6 and continues to be in effect until 
December 11, 2026.\21\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \21\ See 89 FR 99862 (Dec. 11, 2024).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA also notes that MOVES6 includes the latest information for 
the national default projections of EVs, whereas the information in 
earlier versions of MOVES5 and MOVES4 needs to be updated for new or 
delayed analyses after the MOVES6 release.\22\ During the two-year 
MOVES6 grace period, modelers beginning new analyses with MOVES5 may 
not rely on MOVES5 national defaults for projections of EVs. See 
additional information below in sections IV.C. and D. of this notice 
and in the EPA's MOVES6 Policy Guidance.\23\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \22\ See 40 CFR 93.110(a).
    \23\ Modelers that begin new analyses using MOVES4 during the 
MOVES5 grace period must also change the EV projections.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The remainder of this section describes how the transportation 
conformity grace period was determined and summarizes how it will be 
implemented, including those circumstances in which the MOVES6 grace 
period could be shorter than two years for regional emissions analyses. 
However, for complete explanations regarding transportation conformity, 
refer to the MOVES6 Policy Guidance.

A. Why is the EPA establishing a two-year transportation conformity 
grace period?

    CAA section 176(c)(1) states that ``. . . [t]he determination of 
conformity shall be based on the most recent estimates of emissions, 
and such estimates shall be determined from the most recent population, 
employment, travel, and congestion estimates. . . .'' \24\ 
Additionally, the transportation conformity regulations require 
conformity determinations to be based on ``the latest emission 
estimation model available,'' and further state that this requirement 
is satisfied if the most current version of the EPA's motor vehicle 
emissions model is used in the conformity analysis.\25\ When the EPA 
announces a new emissions model, such as MOVES6, the Agency establishes 
a grace period before the model must be used for transportation 
conformity purposes.\26\ In consultation with the

[[Page 67569]]

U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT), the EPA must consider the 
degree of change in the emissions model and the effects of the new 
model on the transportation planning process.\27\ The transportation 
conformity regulations provide that the EPA will establish a grace 
period for new emissions models of between three and 24 months.\28\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \24\ 42 U.S.C. 7506(c)(1).
    \25\ See 40 CFR 93.111.
    \26\ See 40 CFR 93.111(b).
    \27\ See 40 CFR 93.111(b)(2).
    \28\ See 40 CFR 93.111(b)(1).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA articulated its intentions for establishing the length of a 
grace period in the preamble to the 1993 transportation conformity 
rule:
    ``EPA and DOT will consider extending the grace period if the 
effects of the new emissions model are so significant that previous SIP 
demonstrations of what emission levels are consistent with attainment 
would be substantially affected. In such cases, States should have an 
opportunity to revise their SIPs before MPOs must use the model's new 
emissions factors.'' \29\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \29\ See 58 FR 62211 (Nov. 24, 1993).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In consultation with DOT, the EPA considered the degree of change 
in MOVES6 and the effects of the new model on the transportation 
planning process.\30\ The EPA considered the transition of State and 
local transportation and air quality agencies to conduct and provide 
technical support for analyses for MOVES6. Since 1993, the fundamental 
purpose of 40 CFR 93.111(b) of the transportation conformity 
regulations has been to provide a sufficient amount of time for MPOs 
and other State and local agencies to learn and employ new emissions 
models. The transition to a new emissions model involves preparing 
input data and model output as well as considering how the model 
affects regional emissions analysis results and whether SIP and/or 
transportation plan/TIP changes are necessary.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \30\ See 40 CFR 93.111(b)(2).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA provides guidance on how to run the latest MOVES version 
for SIP and transportation conformity purposes with the release of the 
model.\31\ Generally, existing guidance and training applies to MOVES6 
as noted on the EPA's website, and the Agency will notify MOVES6 users 
when updates to these materials are available. Training materials will 
continue to address different levels of State and local expertise.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \31\ Refer to the EPA's ``MOVES6 Technical Guidance: Using MOVES 
to Prepare Emission Inventories for State Implementation Plans and 
Transportation Conformity,'' EPA-403-B-26-002, October 2026, 
available on the EPA's website at: https://www.epa.gov/moves/latest-version-motor-vehicle-emission-simulator-moves.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Upon consideration of these factors, the EPA is establishing a two-
year grace period, which begins today, October 27, 2026, and ends on 
October 27, 2028, before MOVES6 needs to be used for new transportation 
conformity analyses in States other than California.

B. Circumstances in Which the MOVES6 Grace Period Will Be Shorter Than 
Two Years

    The MOVES6 grace period for regional emissions analyses will be 
shorter than two years for a given pollutant if an area revises its SIP 
and motor vehicle emissions budgets with MOVES6 and such budgets have 
been found adequate or approved into the SIP prior to the end of the 
two-year grace period. In this case, the new regional emissions 
analysis must use MOVES6 because the transportation conformity 
determination would be demonstrating consistency with a MOVES6-based 
budget.\32\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \32\ See 40 CFR 93.111.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Areas designated nonattainment or maintenance for more than one 
pollutant may rely on both MOVES6 and MOVES5 to determine 
transportation conformity for different pollutants during the MOVES6 
grace period. In addition, the length of the MOVES6 grace period for 
hot-spot analyses would not be affected by an early submission of 
MOVES6-based budgets. The appropriate EPA Regional Office should be 
consulted regarding situations in multi-pollutant areas.

C. Use of MOVES6 for Regional Emissions Analyses During the Grace 
Period

    During the transportation conformity grace period, areas should use 
interagency consultation to examine how MOVES6 will impact their 
regional emissions analyses and future transportation plan and TIP 
conformity determinations.\33\ Areas should carefully consider whether 
the SIP and budget(s) should be revised with MOVES6 or if 
transportation plans and TIPs should be revised before the end of the 
grace period. The transportation conformity regulations also provide 
flexibility in that regional emissions analyses started with MOVES5 
before or during the MOVES6 grace period can be completed using 
MOVES5.\34\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \33\ Isolated rural areas should also consider how future 
regional emissions analyses will be affected when MOVES6 is 
required, if such an analysis is anticipated.
    \34\ See 40 CFR 93.111(c).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    It is also important that transportation conformity determinations 
continue to meet the requirements for using the latest planning 
assumptions.\35\ Under the transportation conformity regulations, the 
latest planning assumptions must be used when the conformity analysis 
begins, but if new information becomes available after that point, it 
does not need to be included unless a significant delay in the analysis 
occurs as determined through interagency consultation. As described in 
section II of this notice, MOVES6 incorporates applicable Federal 
vehicle standards, including the effects of the EPA's Endangerment 
Finding Rescission Rule as well as other updates that provide the 
latest information for the national default projections of EVs. Below 
is an overview for meeting the latest planning assumptions requirements 
during this model transition:
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \35\ See 40 CFR 93.110.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     For transportation conformity analyses started before the 
release of MOVES6, the national default EV projections in MOVES5 can be 
used by transportation agencies as part of their latest planning 
assumptions so long as the analysis has already begun (i.e., the 
analysis was started prior to the MOVES6 release) and there is no 
significant delay in the analysis.\36\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \36\ See 40 CFR 93.110(a).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

     For transportation conformity determinations in which the 
analysis starts after the release of MOVES6, modelers must either 
update their MOVES5 modeling to include updated EV fraction projections 
or use MOVES6.
    When the MOVES6 grace period ends, MOVES6 will replace MOVES5 as 
the latest EPA emissions model for all new transportation conformity 
analyses. State and local agencies are encouraged to transition sooner 
to MOVES6 to use the latest information and other model improvements. 
See sections 1.5 and 3.5 of the MOVES6 Policy Guidance for further 
details.
    When the grace period ends on October 27, 2028, MOVES6 will become 
the only EPA motor vehicle emissions model for regional emissions 
analyses for transportation conformity in States other than California. 
In general, this means that all new transportation plans and TIP 
conformity determinations started after the end of the grace period 
must be based on MOVES6, even if the SIP is based on MOVES5 or an older 
version of the MOVES model.

D. Use of MOVES6 for Project-Level Hot-Spot Analyses During the 
Conformity Grace Period

    The MOVES6 grace period also applies to the use of MOVES6 for CO, 
PM10, and PM2.5 hot-spot analyses. The 
transportation conformity regulations contain the requirements for when 
a hot-spot analysis is required for project-

[[Page 67570]]

level conformity determinations.\37\ The transportation conformity 
regulations provide flexibility for analyses started before the end of 
the grace period. A conformity determination for a transportation 
project may be based on a previous model if the analysis was begun 
before the end of the grace period and if the final environmental 
document for the project is issued no more than three years after the 
issuance of the draft environmental document.\38\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \37\ See 40 CFR 93.116 and 93.123. In CO nonattainment and 
maintenance areas, a hot-spot analysis is required for all non-
exempt projects, with quantitative hot-spot analyses being required 
for larger, congested intersections and other projects. In 
PM2.5 and PM10 nonattainment and maintenance

    Project-level transportation conformity determinations in which the 
hot-spot analysis has already begun (i.e., the analysis was started 
prior to the MOVES6 release) with MOVES5 may have included the model's 
national default EV projections. These transportation conformity 
determinations may be completed because those EV projections were 
relied on as the most recent assumptions when the analysis began. For 
these conformity determinations, the interagency consultation process 
should be used to confirm when the analysis began, the EV projections 
used, and whether there is any delay in the analysis that would require 
new EV projections to be included.
    For CO, PM10, and PM2.5 hot-spot analyses 
that start during the MOVES6 grace period, project sponsors can choose 
to use MOVES6 or MOVES5. If MOVES5 is used, modelers must update their 
MOVES5 modeling to include updated EV fraction projections. Any new CO, 
PM10, or PM2.5 hot-spot analyses for 
transportation conformity purposes begun after the end of the MOVES6 
grace period must be based on MOVES6.
    Interagency consultation must be used to evaluate and choose 
model(s) and associated methods and assumptions to be used in 
transportation conformity hot-spot analyses and regional emissions 
analyses.\39\ For questions about how the MOVES grace periods apply in 
a project-level conformity determination, contact your EPA Regional 
Office.

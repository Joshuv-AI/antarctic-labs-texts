Clinical characteristics and prediction model of re-positive nucleic acid tests among Omicron infections by machine learning: a real-world study of 35,488 cases
Authors: Ying Cao, Tianhua Yao, Ronghao Li, Liang Tan, Zhixiong Zhang, Junsheng Qi, Rui Zhang, Yazhou Wu, Zhiqiang Chen, Changlin Yin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDuring the Omicron BA.2 variant outbreak in Shanghai, China, from April to May 2022, PCR nucleic acid test re-positivity (TR) occurred frequently, yet the risk factors and predictive models for TR remain unclear. This study aims to identify the factors influencing Omicron TR and to develop machine learning models to predict TR risk. Accurately predicting re-positive patients is crucial for identifying high-risk individuals, optimizing resource allocation, and developing personalized treatment and management plans, thereby effectively controlling the spread of the epidemic, reducing community burden, and ensuring public health.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted among individuals infected with Omicron BA.2 variant from April 12 to May 25, 2022, in the largest Shanghai Fangcang shelter hospital. Five machine learning models were compared, including k-nearest-neighbors (KNN), logistic regression (logistic), bootstrap aggregation (bagging), error back-propagation (BP) neural network, and support vector machines (SVM), to select the best prediction model for the TR risk factors.ResultsA total of 35,488 cases were included in this real-world study. The TR and control groups comprised of 6,171 and 29,317 cases respectively, with a re-positive rate of 17.39%. Higher occurrence of TR was observed in young age, males, those with obvious symptoms, underlying diseases, and a low Ct value. The KNN model proved to be the best in predicting the prognosis in the overall evaluation (accuracy = 0.8198, recall = 0.8026, and AUC = 0.8110 in the test set).InterpretationHigher TR risk was found in infected cases who were underage or with underlying diseases; vaccine brand and inoculation status were not significantly associated with TR. KNN was the most effective machine learning model to predict TR occurrence in isolation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-024-10297-0.Keywords: Re-positive, Omicron, Decision-making, Machine learning, COVID-19

Introduction

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is an infectious disease caused by the Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). On March 11, 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) characterized the COVID-19 outbreak as a pandemic [1]. On November 26, 2021, variant B.1.1.529 was designated by WHO as a variant of concern (VOC), named Omicron [2]. Omicron has the characteristic of higher transmissibility, with studies in Africa and the UK indicating doubling times of 3.38 d (95% CI 3.18–3.61 d) and 2–2.5 d respectively [3]; a primary reproduction number (R0) greater than 3, and less severe symptoms than previous variants [4].

In late February 2022, Omicron BA.2 variant surged in epidemic waves in Shanghai, China, infecting nearly 600,000 people until early May 2022 [5, 6]. In the current epidemic, the detection of viral genes by Reverse Transcription-Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) is the most reliable technique, with high sensitivity [7]. The phenomenon of COVID-19 nucleic acid tested re-positive (TR) is defined as a positive PCR nucleic acid test after two consecutive negative nucleic acid results within a certain time interval [8]. The re-positive rate in strain Alpha was not low, as reported in Wuhan, China, reaching 12.16% (2,466/20,280) [9]. Importantly, SARS-CoV-2 and hepatitis C virus (HCV) both belong to the class of positive-strand RNA viruses in their genomic structure [10] and may share similar reactivation characteristics. Although reactivation in HCV can be affected by factors of gender and medication, and the factors contributing to re-positivity in strain Alpha have been conjectured, such as gender and age, the factors affecting TR in the Omicron BA.2 Infection among Chinese in isolation remain unclear [11, 12].

The current large-scale study was undertaken to discover the core risk factors contributing to Omicron BA.2 re-positivity in an isolated environment. Machine learning was also used for prospective decision-making. Predicting re-positive patients is significant as it can help decision-makers better identify high-risk individuals, optimize resource allocation, and develop personalized treatment and management plans. This not only aids in effectively controlling the spread of the epidemic, reducing community burden, and ensuring public health but also provides scientific evidence to support the formulation and adjustment of public health policies.

Methods

Study Design

This retrospective case-controlled study was based on a large sample size, with 35,488 cases in the largest Fangcang shelter hospital (National Convention and Exhibition Center) in Shanghai, China. Pharynx swab samples were taken for nucleic acid testing in all cases.The inclusion criteria for this study were: all patients in the Fangcang shelter hospital from whom complete case information could be obtained. In the Fangcang shelter hospital, each patients undergo nucleic acid testing every 24 h. Patients who test negative for the virus consecutively twice are considered eligible for discharge. Before discharge, patients will undergo one final nucleic acid test 24 h after the previous test. If the result is positive, the patient is defined as a re-positive case; if the result is negative, the patient can be discharged smoothly. TR patients were defined as those with a positive PCR nucleic acid test after two consecutive negative results [13], and patients with positive PCR nucleic acid test turned negative with no TR forming the control group. Twelve critical factors were selected from among the basic and clinical characteristics of patients, including gender, age, occupation, marital status, symptoms, hypertension, diabetes, vaccine brand, medication, allergy history, inoculation status, and the nadir cycle threshold (Ct) value of the N gene. Comparison between groups was used to test and screen factors that could be considered as independent. Then machine learning was applied to achieve classification prediction to verify the practicability of these factors and further provide a decision-making reference during isolation [14]. The five most commonly used and most effective models by previous experience, including k nearest neighbors (KNN), logistic regression (Logistic), bootstrap aggregation (Bagging), error back-propagation (BP) neural network, and support vector machines (SVM), were used.

Participants and data collection

From April 12 to May 25, 2022, Omicron BA.2-infected individuals with or without symptoms were recruited from the National Convention and Exhibition Center in Shanghai, China. All diagnosis, treatment, and discharge criteria were applied following the Diagnosis and Treatment Plan of Corona Virus Diseases 2019 (Tentative Ninth Edition), issued by the National Health Commission (NHC) of the People’s Republic of China [13]. Permission for release from isolation was granted on condition that the Ct values of the N and ORF genes of SARS-CoV-2 were ≥ 35 in two consecutive nucleic acid tests or with two successive negative SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid tests [13, 15]. PCR kits were obtained from China Zhejiang Orient Gene Biotech Co., Ltd. Cases transferring to another hospital without meeting discharge criteria and data with missing nucleic acid test results were excluded.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Military Medical University (KY2022114).

Variables and classification

For the cases included, the following features were collected from the electronic medical record: gender, age (<18, 18–40, 40–60, >60 years old), occupation (unemployed, self-employed, or employed), marital status (unmarried, married, or divorced), symptoms, hypertension, diabetes, vaccine brand (non-vaccinated, Sinovac, Sinopharm, CanSino BIO, or Zifivax vaccines), medications of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) (unmedicated, simply Lianhua Qingwen capsule, simply Jingyin Gubiao prescription, or a combination of both), allergy history, inoculation status (non-vaccinated, partially vaccinated, or fully vaccinated), and the nadir Ct value of the N gene.

Statistical analysis

Data processing and comparison between groups

The current study first applied correlation analysis to test the correlation between factors to detect any redundancy factor. The correlation analysis categorized the age factor (<18, 18–40, 40–60, >60 years old) into categorical variables. And the only one quantitative variable left of the nadir Ct value of the N gene was transformed from quantitative into a binary categorical variable by the threshold of 35, which is the boundary of whether a patient is positive for the nucleic acid test [13]. Then, the Kappa coefficient between each feature pair is measured to represent the correlation between the 12 reconstructed categorical variables, respectively, and its value ranges from 0 to 1. The larger the value, the stronger the correlation between the two variables.

After this, tests for the significance of differences were conducted. The χ2 test analyzed differences in count data between groups during monofactor analysis. The difference in measurement data between the TR and control groups was analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test according to normal test results from the Shapiro-Wilk test (SW) and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test.

After correlation and multivariate analysis, nine key factors were identified as significantly impacting the risk of re-positivity and were used in the final predictive model.The next step was to perform feature importance ranking using the XGBOOST model to find the factor with the most significant contribution among the nine screened factors. Logistic regression was then applied to analyze the influence of every single feature on re-positivity.

Machine learning for prediction

Data pre-processingRaw data were collected, screened, and integrated manually using Python 3.8. Before machine learning models were constructed, four methods were used to pre-process the statistics: deletion of missing value samples, one-hot encoding, data standardization, and mixed sampling. The disordered classified data were coded by one-hot encoding, and the measured data were standardized using the z-score. In this study, the data were quantitatively imbalanced between groups. Therefore, a variety of sampling methods were applied for sample balancing, including over-sampling (SMOTE method and ADASYN method), down-sampling (Cluster Centroids method), and composite sampling (SMOTEENN method and Tomek Link method). To evaluate the effectiveness of each method, we used the AUC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) value as the performance metric. A higher AUC value indicates better classification performance. Among them, the model training set constructed by the output data of the Tomek Link method had the best fitting effect and was finally chosen, as shown in Table S1. After the training and test sets were built, 4,000 random hold-outs from sample balancing were included in the training set and the rest in the test set. The model parameters were adjusted through 10-fold cross-validation (CV) to prevent overfitting.

Machine learning model selection, logistic regression, and evaluation indicators

Overviews of the most classical machine learning models dealing with binary classification data in medicine were referred to. And by detecting their statistical effectiveness by their performances in prediction in the previous article, we selected the five machine learning algorithms, including KNN, logistic, bagging, BP, and SVM, which showed the highest frequency and effectiveness [16–22]. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) has been used in previous literature to evaluate the predictive effect [23]. Still, due to the limitations of the AUC with unbalanced data, other indicators were also cited, including Recall/ Sensitivity, Precision, Accuracy, balanced F-score (F1), and Specificity.

Various data analysis steps were accomplished by SPSS 25.0, Stata 17.0, and MATLAB 2021b.

Results

The default data are presented in Fig. 1. A total of 35,494 patients were initially included in the research: the TR group with 6,173 cases and the control group with 29,321 cases. According to the exclusion criteria of the sampling method, six patients with missing data were deleted. The final total was 35,488 cases, with 21,649 female and 13,839 male patients, consisting of the TR group with 6,171 patients and the control group with 29,317 patients (re-positive rate 17.39%).

Default data and the samples finally selected

The correlations between the various factors were analyzed; the correlation coefficients are listed in the matrix in Figure S1. The results indicated that except for vaccine brand and inoculation status, which showed no significant differences with TR in later analysis, the degree of correlation among all the factors showing significant differences was low, and there was no apparent feature redundancy. Figure S1 also shows the results of the XGBOOST feature importance ranking, which make it possible to intuitively understand the weight proportion of each feature in model training and to reflect the contribution from each component to re-positivity. The Ct value of the N gene ranked first in model training (≈ 0.18). To further understand the direction of association between Omicron infection and its major predictors, we generated the SHAP summary plot. Figure S1 shows the distribution of SHAP values for each feature in our XGBoost model. The SHAP summary plot clearly demonstrates the direction and magnitude of each feature’s impact on the model’s predictions, validating the rationale behind our model selection and feature importance.

Table 1 shows the proportions of the various categories between groups. The samples mainly comprised adults between 18 and 60 (82.82% in the control group and 80.44% in the TR group). More than half were working (75.36% in the control group and 73.13% in the TR group) and married (60.30% in the control group and 62.92% in the TR group). Most of them were primarily recruited asymptomatically (70.79% in the control group and 69.02% in the TR group), and those with underlying diseases accounted for a small proportion (hypertension of 9.64% in the control group and 11.94% in the TR group; diabetes of 3.12% in the control group and 3.97% in the TR group). Most of them were vaccinated (partially or fully) with different vaccine brands (79.19% in the control group and 80.08% in the TR group).

The results of the significance test of differences are shown in Table 1. The χ2 test of measurement data between groups indicated that eight factors showed significant differences: gender, age, occupation, marital status, symptoms, hypertension, diabetes, and medication (p < 0.05). The vaccine brand (p = 0.083), allergy history (p = 0.212), and inoculation status (p = 0.118) showed no statistically significant differences. As for the count data of the nadir Ct value of the N gene, the results of the normal distribution test showed non-conformity in both the SW and KS tests, as shown in Table S2. In the following Mann-Whitney test, the nadir Ct value was also significantly correlated with re-positivity (p < 0.001).

Analysis of the risk factors in logistic regression

Table S3 illustrates the contribution effect of the various factors. More vital relevance to re-positivity was found in individuals characterized by relatively weaker immunity. This group was composed of those under 18 years old, with obvious symptoms, with underlying diseases like hypertension or diabetes, and with a low Ct value. A lower probability of re-positivity was found in individuals living alone, employed, and those taking no TCM.

Male patients had more TR positivity than female patients (B = 0.180). Compared to younger people (< 18 years old), adults were more likely to get TR (B=−0.332 in those 18–40, B=−0.219 in those 40–60, and B=−0.224 in those > 60 (years old)). Concerning occupation, the self-employed and employed were negatively correlated with re-positivity, unlike the unemployed (B=−0.046 among the self-employed and B=−0.093 among the employed). Regarding marital status, married or divorced people were more likely to get TR than unmarried people (B = 0.092 among the married and B = 0.017 among the divorced). Turning to health condition factors, those with symptoms or underlying illness had a higher probability of developing TR than those who had not been diagnosed (B = 0.108 in those with symptoms, B = 0.132 in those with hypertension, and B = 0.099 in those with diabetes). Compared to those untreated with TCM, those who had received simply a Jingyin Gubiao prescription (B = 0.339), merely a Lianhua Qingwen capsule (B = 0.280), or a combination of both (B = 0.282) had a positive correlation with TR. The EXP(B) of the quantitative variable of the nadir Ct value (EXP(B) = 0.945) indicated that the odds ratio (OR) of re-positivity reached 0.945 per unit increase.

Discussion

The re-positivity of this study was 17.39%, which was similar to, but a little higher than that of strain Alpha, compared to 15.79% in a cohort with a sample size of 59 cases in Wuhan [24] and 12.16% (2,466/20,280) in a follow-up study in Wuhan [9]. A higher occurrence of re-positivity was found among male and younger subjects and in individuals with a lower Ct value or with underlying diseases of hypertension or diabetes. No association to re-positivity was detected for vaccines or inoculation status.

Risk factors of TR were observed, and hence robust support for future medical activities can be hypothesized. Although discharge criteria were established to prevent the spread of infectious agents [13], little standardized guidance on TR patients’ treatment was retrieved from the PubMed system. According to the results of the current study, by analyzing the information collected from individuals about the characteristics discussed above and using the KNN model, it was possible to conveniently evaluate an infected patient’s likelihood of developing TR. In this way, symptomatic male children with a lower nadir Ct value should receive more attention during treatment and follow-up monitoring after discharge.

No significant difference was observed between TR and the control group on factors of vaccines or inoculation status. It is common sense that vaccines play a vital role in preventing infection by living SARS-COV-2. In Omicron cases, previous research has proved that the CanSino BIO and Zifivax vaccines can amplify protection from infection [25]. Effective protection was also demonstrated in individuals inoculated with the Sinopharm and Sinovac vaccines [26, 27]. Despite these successes, there has been little discussion of their effectiveness in preventing re-positivity, particularly in Omicron BA.2 cases. Surprisingly, the current study found for the first time that no significant difference existed between Omicron BA.2 TR and vaccine brand or inoculation status, which indicated no influence of these two factors in the occurrence of BA.2 TR. Moreover, the role of vaccines in preventing external infection did not come into play in inhibiting re-positivity, suggesting that the mechanism of Omicron BA.2 re-positivity may originate from within the individuals infected. More measures for TR prevention and the essential mechanism of re-positivity need to be explored.

Above all, a re-positive PCR nucleic acid test for Omicron BA.2 is more likely to be caused by intrinsic factors within each patient. All the cases in this study were isolated and frequently monitored with distancing and full sanitization, which would eliminate the possibility of re-infection by re-exposure to infected medical staff or causing cross-infection to them [28]. The post-lockdown nucleic acid screening by S. Cao et al. concluded that re-positive patients were non-infectious [29]. Therefore, the occurrence of Omicron BA.2 cases is not likely attributable to exogenous factors of re-infection, but to endogenous factors specific to each patient.

A literature review in 2021 about the cause of nucleic acid re-positivity with previous COVID-19 strains concluded that, rather than by re-infection, the TR phenomenon could be caused by an incompletely cleared virus, waning immunity in patients, and false negative PCR results after initial diagnosis [30]. This argument appeared to apply equally to Omicron BA.2 re-positivity in the present study. Those with weaker defensive systems, younger ages (under 18 years old), underlying diseases (hypertension and diabetes), and a lower Ct value of the N gene are more likely to develop TR. Omicron also features high transmissibility and fast reproduction [4, 31], boosting the viral load in patients. Besides, the results for contribution rank demonstrated that the nadir Ct value of the N gene, representing the amount of viral load, occupied the first place. A long viral shedding time exists in Omicron cases. A cohort from Northeast China reported that the median initial onset course was 12 days; the median course between the first negative nucleic acid results after the first diagnosis and the re-positive results was 13 days; and the median re-positive period was 6 days [32]. Furthermore, with multiple synergetic mutations on their epitopes, Omicron can escape from neutralizing antibodies by sustaining single mutations, making it harder to clear the virus. Therefore, TR can more likely be explained by weakened immunity combined with worse physical conditions and a shorter but still long-term fight against floods of the Omicron BA.2 virus. A variant virus can lead to a persistent viral load and prolonged viral shedding, which result in a re-positive result for nucleic acid.

Furthermore, it is worth noting that the COVID-19 nucleic PCR test can barely distinguish whether fragments of viral RNA are infectious living viruses or non-infectious viral RNA. Potential false positive or negative results obtained from PCR can also have an impact. This phenomenon could contribute additionally to TR results in Omicron BA.2 cases.

Turning to classification prediction, KNN performed the best among all the machine learning models. Since the outbreak of COVID-19, much effort has been made in machine learning to fight the pandemic. Most of these studies have focused on genome classification, selection of vaccines and drugs, detection of COVID-19 according to images of different systems by various imaging methods, and survival prediction. The current study has applied this approach to Omicron BA.2 cases to predict the occurrence of re-positivity. A promising result was achieved by overcoming the obstacles that may hinder acquiring an accurate KNN result by using a huge database in the training step for binary result classification prediction [33, 34]. Furthermore, the traditional application of KNN in medicine has concentrated mainly on classifying cancer data. These studies were performed by comparing different parameter settings and training regimes from datasets of cancer characteristics in imaging, cytology, molecular biology, etc. This work has made remarkable achievements in cancer classification, risk calculation, diagnosis, and prognosis prediction [35]. The current study has succeeded in prediction by analyzing direct contextual clinical features and being able to issue an early warning at first sight of an Omicron BA.2 diagnosis or during treatment by collecting and analysis of patients’ basic and clinical information. While re-positive patients might not be infectious, those with risk factors for re-positivity still need appropriate management. This result can prevent patients with risk factors for re-positivity from being inappropriately released, thereby avoiding their re-entry into society only to be isolated again, which would lead to a repetitive consumption of social resources. To some extent, this can reduce the community burden and provide a basis for policy-making for decision-makers.

The current study has mainly focused on the two dimensions of the three previous studies identified: the virus, individuals, and detection [36]. As previously reported, the virus dimension includes residual viral particles, low viral load, intermittent viral shedding, and mutated strains. The individual dimension in this study contains physiological state and daily life behavior. The physiological conditions include factors leading to a weaker immune system, including gender, age, and primary diseases of hypertension and diabetes. Daily life behaviors mainly consist of occupation and marital status. For the detection dimension, it was proved in this study that the PCR nucleic acid test for the N gene had a significant association with TR cases, meaning that more attention should be paid to the quality of RNA detection kits and defective sampling techniques. For the first time, this study has identified the best machine learning method to predict the re-positivity of large-scale infectious diseases like Omicron BA.2. KNN has advantages in future isolation treatment spaces and can therefore be applied and generalized.

However, there are some limitations in this study. First, the study is a real-world, single-centered study in Shanghai, China’s largest Fangcang shelter hospital, meaning its random elements are less prominent. However, the massive data can help minimize these effects. Second, although the most plausible mechanism for Omicron BA.2 TR is analyzed in this research, the primary molecular mechanism behind TR cases remains unstudied. Third, false negativity is difficult to prevent and is caused by relatively low virus loads in asymptomatic Omicron cases, inadequate sample collection with the pharynx swab, and limited accuracy of the test kits. Despite all these difficulties, a standard process throughout screening and quarantine has been proposed. Another limitation of this study is that while research indicates re-positivity can occur several days post-discharge [37–39], our study only included patients who tested re-positive within 24 h of meeting discharge criteria. This may lead to some cases initially not identified as re-positive in our study manifesting re-positivity later, potentially affecting the robustness of our predictive model. However, it is crucial to note that patients in our study left the controlled environment of the Fangcang shelter hospital and returned to the community, a high-prevalence area for COVID-19, within 24 h. This introduces the possibility of new infections post-discharge, complicating the distinction between re-positivity due to the original infection and that due to new infections. Including data beyond the 24-hour window would introduce a confounding factor of reinfection, which could dilute the accuracy of our model designed to predict re-positivity in a controlled environment, specifically within the 24-hour period before discharge, when the risk of external exposure is minimized. Therefore, while we recognize the importance of long-term data for comprehensive studies on re-positivity, including such data was not suitable for our model, which focuses on precise predictions within the 24-hour window before discharge. We believe this approach avoids confounding factors and ensures the reliability of the model’s predictions within the controlled isolation context.Additionally, although our current model was built on data available from the shelter hospital, including post-discharge data could enhance the model’s accuracy and generalizability. We acknowledge that extending data collection to include post-discharge monitoring could be a valuable area for future research, potentially improving the robustness of predictive models for re-positivity.

Conclusion

This retrospective study in Shanghai clarifies the essential characteristics of Omicron BA.2-infected patients with a re-positive nucleic acid PCR test in a mobile cabin hospital. Prediction was achieved by selecting the best machine learning model, which was KNN. The current study references preventive, therapeutic, and follow-up measures for those with decreased immune system potency. TR was not related to vaccine brand or inoculation status. Furthermore, it has been found that endogenous factors probably bring about the re-positivity of individuals. KNN shows potential as a predictor of Omicron BA.2 re-positivity, further investigation and validation in prospective studies are needed.

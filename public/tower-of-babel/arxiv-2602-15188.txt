Quantization as a Categorical Equivalence for Hilbert Bimodules and Lagrangian Relations
Benjamin H. Feintzeig

Abstract
It is well known that classical and quantum theories carry distinct types of representations , each type of representation corresponding to possible values of generalized charges in the classical or quantum context. This paper demonstrates a sense in the structure of these representation theories is preserved from classical to quantum physics. To show this, I discuss distinct representation-theory preserving morphisms in the classical and quantum contexts. Specifically, I consider categories whose morphisms are Lagrangian relations in the classical context and Hilbert bimodules in the quantum context. These morphisms are significant because they give rise to induced representations of classical and quantum theories, respectively. I consider quantization and the classical limit as determining functors between these categories. I treat quantization via the strict deformation quantization of a Poisson algebra and the classical limit via the extension of a uniformly continuous bundle of C*-algebras. With these tools, I prove that the quantization and classical limit functors are “almost-inverse” to each other, thus establishing a categorical equivalence.



1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to show a sense in which the structure for representing symmetries and their associated charges is shared in common between classical and quantum physics. This structure is typically associated with the representation theory of a Lie group, where the appropriate notion of a representation is defined relative to the framework of classical or quantum physics (see
(
Mackey 1968
;
Marsden and Weinstein 1974
)
). For my purposes, classical theories in a Hamiltonian framework are formulated on Poisson manifolds that carry representations as symplectic manifolds. On the other hand, quantum theories are formulated with C*-algebras of observables that carry representations on Hilbert spaces. Even if the classical and quantum theories have the same Lie group of symmetries, the classical and quantum representations may fail to stand in a one-to-one correspondence. For example, charge in classical theories typically takes continuous values, leading to a continuous family of representations, while charge in quantum theories is typically “quantized”, leading to a discrete family of representations. This leads to the question of whether one can make precise the sense in which, nevertheless, the classical and quantum representation theories still stand in correspondence with each other.

I hope to show that one can indeed make this structural correspondence precise in the language of category theory for a certain class of models of classical physics and their quantized counterparts. I will consider a category of models of classical physics in which the objects are Poisson manifolds associated with Lie groupoids and the arrows are Lagrangian relations, understood as morphisms that preserve classical representation-theoretic structure. I will restrict to a special case of Lagrangian relations determined by symplectic dual pairs. Likewise, I will consider a category of models of quantum physics in which the objects are C*-algebras associated as the quantization of a Lie groupoid and the arrows are (equivalence classes of) Hilbert bimodules, understood as morphisms that preserve quantum representation-theoretic structure. Previously,
Landsman 2003
has defined a quantization functor
Q
from the classical category to the quantum category by associating classical morphisms (symplectic dual pairs) to quantum morphisms (Hilbert bimodules). On the other hand,
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
have defined a classical limit functor
L
from the quantum category to the classical category by associating quantum morphisms to classical morphisms. In this paper, I will show that the classical limit functor is almost inverse to the quantization functor, in the sense that
L\circ Q
and
Q\circ L
are each naturally isomorphic to the identity functor. It is this result, generalizing previous work
(
Feintzeig 2024
)
, that I take to substantiate the claim that classical and quantum theories share the representation-theoretic structures associated with physical charges and symmetries.

The paper proceeds as follows. In §
2

(
Mackenzie 1987
;
Landsman 1998
)
. A classical object is the dual of a Lie algebroid associated with a Lie groupoid, with its natural Poisson structure
(

;

;

)
. A quantum object is a (reduced) Lie groupoid C*-algebra
(
Landsman 1998
;
Landsman 2001
)

(
Rieffel 1989
;
Rieffel 1993
;
Landsman 1998
;
Landsman 1999
)
, while the classical
\hbar\to 0
limit is understood via the extension of a uniformly continuous bundle of C*-algebras
(
Kirchberg and Wasserman 1995
;
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021a
;
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021b
)
. In §

Classical and Quantum Models

2.1
Systems associated to Lie groupoids

The classical and quantum models that we consider encompass a range of physical systems that can be represented by Lie groupoids
(
Mackenzie 1987
;
Weinstein 2000
)
. A Lie groupoid is a small category
G
in which every arrow has an inverse. We identify the objects in
G
with the identity arrows, the set of which we denote by
G_{0}
. There is a source projection
s_{G}:G\to G_{0}
and a target projection
t_{G}:G\to G_{0}
. Groupoid multiplication is defined as composition of arrows
x,x^{\prime}\in G
, denoted by
x\cdot x^{\prime}
when
s_{G}(x)=t_{G}(x^{\prime})
. Moreover, for a Lie groupoid, both
G
and
G_{0}
are smooth manifolds,
s_{G}
and
t_{G}
are surjective submersions, multiplication is a smooth map
G\times G\to G
, and the inclusion
G_{0}\hookrightarrow G
is smooth. As discussed in
Landsman 1998
;
Landsman 1999
, examples of Lie groupoids include the pair groupoid of any manifold, the action groupoid for a Lie group acting on a manifold, and the gauge groupoid for a principal bundle.

A Lie groupoid can be associated with a Poisson manifold understood as the phase space of a classical mechanical system. The Poisson manifold at issue is the dual of the Lie algebroid. We denote the Lie algebroid for
G
by
\mathfrak{G}\rightrightarrows^{TG_{0}}_{G_{0}}
. With
T^{t}G=\ker((t_{G})_{*})
a subbundle of

\mathfrak{G}\cong T^{t}G_{|{G_{0}}}
, understood as the restriction to
G_{0}
. In other words,
(x,\xi)\in T^{t}G_{|G_{0}}
means that

is an identity arrow in
G
and
\xi\in T_{x}G
is a tangent vector with
(t_{G})_{*}(\xi)=0

\mathfrak{G}
is a vector bundle over
G_{0}
, with the projection
\tau_{G}:\mathfrak{G}\to G_{0}

\tau_{G}(x,\xi)=s_{G}(x)=t_{G}(x)

G_{0}
with projection denoted by

The manifold

carries a Poisson bracket on

;

)

\displaystyle=0.

(1)

Next, consider functions that are dual to sections of

\tilde{s}_{1}(\eta)=\eta(s_{1}(\tau^{*}_{G}(\eta)))
and
\tilde{s}_{2}(\eta)=\eta(s_{2}(\tau^{*}_{G}(\eta)))
for
\eta\in\mathfrak{G}^{*}

\displaystyle\{\tilde{s}_{1},f\}

\displaystyle=-(\tau^{a}_{G}\circ s_{1})(\tilde{f})

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{[s_{1},s_{2}]}_{\mathfrak{G}},

(3)

as the phase space of a Hamiltonian system.

A Lie groupoid also defines a non-commutative C*-algebra of observables of a quantum system

Landsman 1998

Landsman 2001

2.2
Quantization and the Classical Limit for Lie Groupoids

The classical model—the Poisson manifold
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
—is related to the quantum model—the C*-algebra
C^{*}(G)
—by
strict deformation quantization
. We begin by reviewing the definitions of the structures involved
(
Rieffel 1989
;
Rieffel 1993
;
Landsman 1998
;
Landsman 2007
)
, focusing on the notion of a (uniformly) continuous bundle of C*-algebras to formulate the classical limit
(
Kirchberg and Wasserman 1995
;
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021b
;
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021a
)
.

Definition 1
.

A
strict quantization
of a Poisson algebra
\mathcal{P}\subseteq C_{b}(M)
of bounded functions on a smooth Poisson manifold
M
consists in:

•

a family of C*-algebras
(\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in[0,1]}
called the
fibers
; and

•

a family of
quantization maps

(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in[0,1]}

the inclusion map

satisfying for all
f,g\in\mathcal{P}
:

1.

(von Neumann’s condition)
\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(f)\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(g)-\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(fg)\rVert=0
;

2.

(Dirac’s condition)
\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}\big(\{f,g\}\big)-[\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(f),\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(g)]\rVert=0
; and

3.

(Rieffel’s condition) the map
\hbar\mapsto\lVert\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(f)\rVert
is continuous.

If furthermore, for each
\hbar\in[0,1]
, the map
\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}
is one-to-one and
\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(\mathcal{P})
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar}
, then the structure is called a strict
deformation
quantization.
2
2

2

The definition of a strict deformation quantization may be generalized so that the base space is not restricted to

(0,1]
.

We will sometimes refer to a strict deformation quantization by
\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}
for shorthand.

The primary example of a strict deformation quantization for the purposes of this paper is one that relates the model of a classical system as the dual of a Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
to the corresponding model of a quantum system as the groupoid C*-algebra
C^{*}(G)
.
We now summarize the details provided in
Landsman 1998
;
Landsman 1999
(See also
Landsman and Ramazan 2000

To define the Weyl exponential, we suppose that the Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{G}
is equipped with a connection. Consider the bundle
\tau_{T^{t}G\to G}:T^{t}G\to G
and define the left exponential map
\exp^{L}:\mathfrak{G}\to G
by

\displaystyle\exp^{L}(X)=\tau_{T^{t}G\to G}(X^{\prime}(1)),

(6)

where

is the geodesic flow on

\exp^{W}:\mathfrak{G}\to G

\displaystyle\exp^{W}(X)=\exp^{L}\big(-X/2\big)^{-1}\exp^{L}\big(X/2\big).

(7)

To define the fiberwise Fourier transform, we consider a family of Lebesgue measures
(\nu^{*}_{x_{0}})_{x_{0}\in G_{0}}
on the fibers
(\tau_{G}^{*})^{-1}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\hat{f}(X)=\int_{(\tau_{G}^{*})^{-1}(x_{0})}d\nu^{*}_{x_{0}}(\theta)e^{i\theta(X)}f(\theta)

Next, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{G}_{\hbar}(f)(\exp^{W}(X))=\hbar^{-n}\kappa_{G}(X)\hat{f}(X/\hbar),

(9)

for

\exp^{W}

Two strict quantizations
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in I}

\displaystyle\hbar\mapsto\lVert\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(f)-\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\hbar}(f)\rVert

We now define the type of bundles of C*-algebras we will employ in this paper, which involve strong properties of

.

A

consists in:

•

a locally compact metric space

;

•

a family of C*-algebras

;

•

However, we emphasize that our definition differs from the standard one for continuous bundles of C*-algebras in

A strict quantization determines a continuous bundle of C*-algebras as long as it satisfies mild technical conditions. The following construction produces a continuous bundle of C*-algebras just in case for every polynomial
P

\displaystyle\hbar\mapsto\lVert P(\hbar)\rVert

is continuous

. One generates a *-algebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}=\left\{a\in\prod_{\hbar\in[0,1]}\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar}\ \Bigg|\ \text{ the map }\left[\hbar\mapsto\lVert a(\hbar)-\tilde{a}(\hbar)\rVert\right]\text{ is continuous for each $\tilde{a}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}$}\right\}.

With the evaluation maps given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\hbar}(a)=a(\hbar)

We are primarily interested in the case where one has a strict quantization over the base space
[0,1]

[0,1]
. Then the restriction of a continuous bundle over the closed interval

to encode information about the algebras of quantum observables and their scaling properties for values
\hbar>0
.

Using this structure, we can consider the classical
\hbar\to 0
limit as the “inverse” process to quantization. We will represent the
\hbar\to 0
limit as the extension of a uniformly continuous bundle of C*-algebras over
(0,1]
to the closed interval
[0,1]
, which includes the value
\hbar=0
.

To that end, suppose we are given a uniformly continuous bundle of C*-algebras over
(0,1]
with the algebra of uniformly continuous sections
\mathfrak{A}
, fibers
(\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
, and evaluation maps
(\phi_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
.
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021a
show that whenever the inclusion of base spaces
(0,1]\hookrightarrow[0,1]
is isometric, which clearly holds in this case, then the bundle can be uniquely extended to the one-point compactification
[0,1]
with the fiber algebra at any
\hbar\in[0,1]
(including the value
\hbar=0
) determined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar}=\mathfrak{A}/K_{\hbar},

(15)

where one takes the quotient by the closed two-sided ideal

\displaystyle K_{\hbar}=\left\{a\in\mathfrak{A}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar^{\prime}\to\hbar}\lVert\phi_{\hbar^{\prime}(a)}\rVert=0\right\}.

(16)

In particular, one has the unique limiting fiber algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{0}=\mathfrak{A}/K_{0}
.

When the uniformly continuous bundle is determined by a strict deformation quantization of a Poisson algebra, we have that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
represents the fully quantum theory and
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
represents the classical theory. We mention briefly some features of the classical limit that are described in more detail by
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021a
and
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024

\mathfrak{A}_{0}
is an abelian algebra, and hence is an algebra of functions on a topological space
M
by the Gelfand representation theorem. Second, certain choices of Poisson algebras carry enough information to define the structure of a smooth manifold on
M
. Third, Dirac’s condition implies that the bundle of C*-algebras determines a Poisson bracket on
M
. Finally, the classical limit is unique up to isomorphism in the sense that the quantization of a Poisson algebra of functions on
M
recovers the structure of
M
in the classical limit, with
\mathfrak{A}_{0}\cong C_{0}(M)
. To summarize: the classical limit of a uniformly continuous bundle of C*-algebras generated from a strict deformation quantization allows one to recover the geometric structures of the original space, and thus to understand the classical structures as being determined by the quantum model for
\hbar>0
.

3
Classical and Quantum Morphisms

In order to understand quantization as a categorical equivalence between models of classical and quantum physics, we must specify the morphisms for the categories of models that we consider. Following
Landsman 2001
;
Landsman 2001b
, we treat morphisms that capture the representation-theoretic structure of classical and quantum models, respectively.
We now proceed to define morphisms between classical models (i.e., Poisson manifolds) as symplectic dual pairs, and morphisms between quantum models (i.e., C*-algebras) as Hilbert bimodules. We will focus on symplectic dual pairs and Hilbert bimodules that arise from bibundles between Lie groupoids, which consist in pairs of groupoids acting on a common space
(
Mackenzie 1987
;
Moerdijk 1988
;
Muhly et al. 1987
)
. We first provide the definition of a bibundle that we will work with.

.

(17)

define the fiber product

\displaystyle M\times_{H}N=\Big\{(q,q^{\prime})\in M\times N\ \Big|\ s_{M}(q)=t_{N}(q^{\prime})\Big\}.

(18)

This space carries an

forms the middle space of a bibundle

(19)

which we consider as the tensor product of the bibundles

;

)

We follow

Definition 4

A

It is well known that a symplectic dual pair
M\leftarrow S\rightarrow N
allows one to induce symplectic realizations of
M
from those of
N

(
Landsman 1995
)
. Here, a
symplectic realization
is a smooth Poisson map
j:S_{0}\to N
from a symplectic manifold
S
to a Poisson manifold
N
. One can induce a symplectic realization of
M
from
S_{0}
and the dual pair
S
by taking the above tensor product composition
S\circledcirc S_{0}
with the obvious map
\tilde{j}:S\circledcirc S_{0}\to M
constructed from
j_{M}
on the first component. Moreover, this construction of induced symplectic realizations does not depend on the full structure of the symplectic dual pair
S
, but only on the image
(j_{M},j_{N})[S]\subseteq M\times N
, which is a
Lagrangian relation

(

)
. Here,
(j_{M},j_{N}):S\to M\times N
is the pair map
(j_{M},j_{N})(s)=(j_{M}(s),j_{N}(s))
for

. By saying the induced symplectic realization only depends on the Lagrangian relation, we mean that distinct symplectic dual pairs may give rise to equivalent functors from realizations of
N
to realizations of
M
, but such dual pairs will determine the same Lagrangian relation. Moreover, any symplectic dual pairs that determine the same Lagrangian relation will also determine the same induction functor. Hence, we will define the morphisms in our category as equivalence classes of symplectic dual pairs that determine the same Lagrangian relation.

The main example of dual pairs we consider in this paper arises from a regular bibundle
G\rightarrowtail M\leftarrowtail H
between Lie groupoids
G
and
H
, which allows one to construct a symplectic dual pair
\mathfrak{G}^{*}\leftarrow T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{*}
between the corresponding duals of their Lie algebras
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{H}^{*}
. The Poisson morphism
j_{G}:T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{G}^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle j_{G}(\eta_{q})\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\gamma(t)\Big)=-\eta_{q}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\cdot q\big)\Big),

(28)

where
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
\gamma:I\to G
is a curve with
\gamma(0)=t_{M}(q)
and
\gamma(t)\in t_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))
so that
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in\tau_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))
and
\gamma(t)^{-1}\cdot q
is well-defined for all
t\in I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Likewise, the Poisson morphism
j_{H}:T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{H}^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle j_{H}(\eta_{q})\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\gamma(t)\Big)=\eta_{q}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\big(q\cdot\gamma(t)\big)\Big),

(29)

where
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
\gamma:I\to H
is a curve with
\gamma(0)=s_{M}(q)
and
\gamma(t)\in t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))
, so that
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in\tau_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))
and
q\cdot\gamma(t)
is well-defined for all
t\in I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{G}^{*}\leftarrow T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{*}
satisfies the definition of a symplectic dual pair
(
Landsman 2000
;
Landsman 2001
)
. Regularity of bibundles implies weak regularity of the corresponding dual pairs, which guarantees that these dual pairs can be composed by means of the tensor product.

We will further restrict attention to integrable Poisson manifolds (or Lie groupoids for which the dual of the corresponding Lie algebroid is integrable), since the condition of integrability guarantees that there is a symplectic dual pair from the Poisson manifold to itself that serves the role of an identity arrow
(
Landsman 2001a
;
Crainic and Fernandes 2004
)
. Similarly, we restrict attention to source-simply connected Lie groupoids (i.e., Lie groupoids

for which
s_{G}^{-1}(x_{0})
is connected and simply connected for each

(
Moerdijk and Mrčun 2002
)
) because this condition holds just in case the identity bibundle corresponds to the identity dual pair.

We define the following category of Poisson manifolds, understood as a category of classical models.

Definition 5
.

We denote by
\mathbf{LPoisson}
the category consisting of the following:

•

Objects
: integrable duals of Lie algebroids
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
associated to source-simply connected Lie groupoids
G
, understood as a class of Poisson manifolds.

•

Arrows
: equivalence classes of symplectic dual pairs
\mathfrak{G}^{*}\leftarrow T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{*}
associated to regular bibundles
M
between Lie groupoids via the construction of Eqs. (
28
)-(
29
) that determine the same Lagrangian relation
(j_{G},j_{H})[T^{*}M]\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{*}\times\mathfrak{H}^{*}
.

\mathbf{LPoisson}
is indeed a category: integrability of the objects guarantees the existence of identity arrows, and the behavior of the tensor product for dual pairs implies the appropriate composition laws for arrows
(
Landsman 2001a
;
Landsman 2001b
)
. Note that our definition differs from the one given by Landsman because we consider equivalence classes of symplectic dual pairs that determine the same Lagrangian relation rather than isomorphism classes of symplectic dual pairs themselves.

Next, we follow
Lance 1995
and
Raeburn and Williams 1998
in the definition of Hilbert bimodules (see also
Rieffel 1974a
;
Rieffel 1974b
;
Rieffel 1978
).

Definition 6
.

A
Hilbert bimodule

\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}
between C*-algebras
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
consists in a vector space
\mathcal{E}
carrying a
\mathfrak{B}
-valued positive definite inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}
that is antilinear in the first argument and linear in the second. The space
\mathcal{E}
carries a right action of
\mathfrak{B}
and a left action of
\mathfrak{A}
by adjointable operators (Here, a linear operator
a
on
\mathcal{E}
is called
adjointable
if there is a linear operator
a^{*}
such that
\langle\varphi_{1},a\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\langle a^{*}\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}
for all
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
.) The inner product is required to satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}b\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot b

(30)

for all
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
and
b\in\mathfrak{B}
, and the space
\mathcal{E}
is required to be complete in the norm

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}=\lVert\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\rVert,

(31)

where the right hand side uses the C*-norm in
\mathfrak{B}
. Finally, the
\mathfrak{A}
-action is required to be nondegenerate in the sense that
\mathfrak{A}\mathcal{E}
is dense in
\mathcal{E}
.

Sometimes we will refer to the bimodule
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}
by its middle space
\mathcal{E}
for shorthand. We call another Hilbert bimodule
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{F}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}

unitarily equivalent
to the Hilbert bimodule
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}
when there is a unitary map
U:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
intertwining both the left
\mathfrak{A}
-actions and the right
\mathfrak{B}
-actions.

One can think of Hilbert bimodules as generalized maps between C*-algebras, which can be composed by means of a tensor product operation, as follows
(
Landsman 2001a
;
Landsman 2001b
)
. Consider two Hilbert bimodules

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{F}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{C}.

(32)

Consider the algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}
and define the subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{B}}=\text{span}\Big\{\varphi b\otimes\psi-\varphi\otimes b\psi\ \Big|\ \varphi\in\mathcal{E},\psi\in\mathcal{F},\text{and }b\in\mathfrak{B}\Big\}.

(33)

Then define
\mathcal{E}\dot{\otimes}_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F})/\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{B}}
. This space carries a natural left
\mathfrak{A}
-action and right
\mathfrak{C}
-action as well as the
\mathfrak{C}
-valued inner product defined by

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{1}\otimes\psi_{1},\varphi_{2}\otimes\psi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}\dot{\otimes}_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{F}}=\langle\psi_{1},\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\psi_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}

(34)

for
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
. The completion of
\mathcal{E}\dot{\otimes}_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{F}
, denoted
\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{F}
, is called the interior tensor product and forms the middle space of a Hilbert bimodule

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{F}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{C},

(35)

which we consider as the composition of the Hilbert bimodules
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

It is well known that a Hilbert bimodule
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{B}
allows one to induce Hilbert space representations of
\mathfrak{A}
from those of
\mathfrak{B}

(
Landsman 1995
)
(See also
(
Rieffel 1978
;
Rieffel 1974a
;
Rieffel 1974b
;
Green 1978
;
Green 1980
;
Rosenberg 1994
)
). Here, a
Hilbert space representation
is a *-homomorphism
\pi:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. One can induce a Hilbert space representation of
\mathfrak{A}
from
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
and the bimodule
\mathcal{E}
by taking the above interior tensor product
\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. Moreover, this construction of induced Hilbert space representations does not depend on the full structure of the Hilbert bimodule
\mathcal{E}
in the sense that distinct Hilbert bimodules can give rise to the same induction functor. But there is a structure that is invariant among the different Hilbert bimodules giving rise to the same induction functor. If we denote by
\pi_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathcal{E}}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\mathcal{E}}
the representations of
\mathfrak{A}
and (the opposite of)
\mathfrak{B}
determined by their left/right actions on
\mathcal{E}
, then the induced Hilbert space representation possesses an invariant in the form of the kernel
\ker(\pi_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\mathcal{E}})
of the tensor product representation, which we will henceforth call the
tensor product kernel
associated with
\mathcal{E}
. By calling the tensor product kernel an invariant of the induced representation, we mean that while distinct Hilbert bimodules may give rise to equivalent functors from Hilbert space representations of
\mathfrak{B}
to Hilbert space representations of
\mathfrak{A}
, in this case the Hilbert bimodules must possess the same tensor product kernel. Hence, we will define the morphisms in our category as equivalence classes of Hilbert bimodules that share the same tensor product kernel.

In particular, a regular bibundle
G\rightarrowtail M\leftarrowtail H
between Lie groupoids
G
and
H
allows one to construct a Hilbert bimodule
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
, where
\mathcal{E}_{M}
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
relative to the inner product defined below. To define the inner product and group actions, first note that the left Haar system
(\nu^{t}_{x_{0}})_{x_{0}\in G_{0}}
on
G
induces a family of measures
(\mu_{y_{0}})_{y_{0}\in H_{0}}
on
M
, each with support contained in
s_{M}^{-1}(y_{0})
, such that for each
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)

(
Landsman 2001
)
,

1.

the function
y_{0}\mapsto\int d\mu_{y_{0}}(q)\ f(q)
on
H_{0}
is smooth;

2.

\int d\mu_{t_{H}(y)}(q)\ f(qy)=\int d\mu_{s_{H}(y)}(q)f(q)
; and

3.

for any
y_{0}\in H_{0}
and
q_{0}\in s_{M}^{-1}(y_{0})
,
\int d\mu_{y_{0}}(q)\ f(q)=\int d\nu^{t}_{t_{M}(q_{0})}(x)\ f(x^{-1}q_{0})
.

We will further denote the left Haar system on
H
by
(\lambda^{t}_{y_{0}})_{y_{0}\in H_{0}}
.
With the measures
(\mu_{y_{0}})_{y_{0}\in H_{0}}
on
M
, we can define the
C^{*}(H)
-valued inner product

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}(y)=\int_{s_{M}^{-1}(t_{H}(y))}d\mu_{t_{H}(y))}(q)\ \overline{\varphi(q)}\psi(qy)

(36)

for
\varphi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
and
y\in H
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{M}
is defined as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
relative to the norm
\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}^{2}=\lVert\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert
, where the norm on the right hand side is the C*-norm in
C^{*}(H)
. The left
C^{*}(G)
-action and right
C^{*}(H)
action are given for
f\in C^{*}(G)
and
g\in C^{*}(H)
by

\displaystyle(f\cdot\varphi)(q)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))}d\nu^{t}_{t_{M}(q)}(x)\ f(x)\varphi(x^{-1}q)

(37)

\displaystyle(\varphi\cdot g)(q)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))}d\lambda^{t}_{s_{M}(q)}(y)\ g(y^{-1})\varphi(qy)

(38)

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
and
q\in M
, extended to
\mathcal{E}_{M}
by continuity. It then follows that
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
satisfies the definition of a Hilbert bimodule
(
Green 1978
;
Muhly et al. 1987
;
Landsman 2001
)
.

Definition 7
.

We denote by
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
the category consisting in:

•

Objects
: Lie groupoid C*-algebras

associated to source-simply connected Lie groupoids
G
whose Lie algebroids
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
are integrable.

•

Arrows
: equivalence classes of Hilbert bimodules
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
associated to integrable, regular bibundles

between Lie groupoids via the construction of Eqs. (

\ker(\pi_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\mathcal{E}})

One can check that

is indeed a category, with the behavior of the tensor product for Hilbert bimodules implying the appropriate composition laws for arrows
(
Landsman 2001a
;
Landsman 2001b
)
. Again, note that our definition differs from the one given by Landsman because we consider equivalence classes of Hilbert bimodules that determine the same tensor product kernel rather than unitary equivalence classes of Hilbert bimodules themselves.

3.2
Quantization and the Classical Limit of Morphisms

Quantization and the classical limit already provide ways to associate objects in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
with objects in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
. In particular, the strict quantization defined in Eq. (
9
) of the dual of a Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
associated to a Lie groupoid
G
yields the Lie groupoid C*-algebra
C^{*}(G)
in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
. On the other hand, one can take the classical limit of an object
C^{*}(G)
in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
by generating a uniformly continuous bundle
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
over the base space consisting in values of
\hbar
in
(0,1]
from this strict quantization. (
Landsman 1999
shows the algebra of continuous sections for this uniformly continuous bundle is the groupoid C*-algebra of the normal groupoid over
G
.) Let
K^{G}_{0}
be the ideal of sections in
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
whose norm vanishes at
\hbar=0
as in Eq. (
16
). The construction of
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021a
then yields the classical limit
\mathfrak{A}^{G}/K^{G}_{0}
, which they show is unique up to isomorphism. This implies that
\mathfrak{A}^{G}/K^{G}_{0}\cong C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
, which leads by Gelfand duality to the Poisson manifold
\mathfrak{G}^{*}
in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
.

We will denote these associations by

\displaystyle Q:\mathfrak{G}^{*}\mapsto C^{*}(G)

(39)

\displaystyle L:C^{*}(G)\mapsto\mathfrak{G}^{*}.

(40)

The map
Q
represents the quantization of objects in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
yielding objects in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
. The map
L
represents the classical limit of objects in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
yielding objects in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
.

We will understand each of the maps
Q
and
L
as the action of a functor on objects in the respective category. To complete the definition of each functor, we must specify the behavior of each of
Q
and
L
on arrows.
Landsman 2001
;
Landsman 2003
(See also
(
Landsman 2000
)
) concretely defines the action of
Q
on arrows in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
via the association

\displaystyle Q:[\mathfrak{G}^{*}\leftarrow T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{*}]\mapsto[C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)]

(41)

The fact that
Q
is well-defined follows from our Prop.
13
below, which shows that if two symplectic dual pairs
T^{*}M
and
T^{*}M^{\prime}
determine the same Lagrangian relation, then the Hilbert bimodules
\mathcal{E}_{M}
and
\mathcal{E}_{M^{\prime}}
must have the same tensor product kernel. Landsman shows that
Q
respects the composition of arrows by the tensor product of symplectic dual pairs in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
and the interior tensor product of Hilbert bimodules in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
, up to isomorphism of symplectic dual pairs and unitary equivalence of Hilbert bimodules. Landsman’s result guarantees that
Q
is indeed a functor.

While the quantization of arrows is specific to the objects of
\mathbf{LPoisson}
rather than general symplectic dual pairs, the classical limit of arrows as constructed in
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
applies to a broad class of Hilbert bimodules, of which we will see the arrows in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
as a special case. They consider the case where one has not only the fiber C*-algebras at some particular value of
\hbar\in(0,1]
, but also where one possesses two full uniformly continuous bundles of C*-algebras
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
generated by quantization maps
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
and
(\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\hbar})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
. Under certain conditions, a Hilbert bimodule
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar}
between the fibers
\mathfrak{A}_{\hbar}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{\hbar}
at a fixed value yields a Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
between the algebras of continuous sections
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
by translating between values of
\hbar\in(0,1]
by means of the maps
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
. One can then use the Hilbert bimodule
\mathfrak{A}\rightarrowtail\underline{\mathcal{E}}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
to construct the classical limit as follows.

Let
K_{0}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert\phi_{\hbar}(a)\rVert=0\}
and
K^{\prime}_{0}=\{b\in\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert\phi_{\hbar}(b)\rVert=0\}
. Recall that the classical limit algebras are
\mathfrak{A}_{0}=\mathfrak{A}/K_{0}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{0}=\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}/K^{\prime}_{0}
.
We will say that the Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
is
strongly nondegenerate
at
\hbar=0
if
\overline{K_{0}\cdot\underline{\mathcal{E}}}\subseteq\overline{\underline{\mathcal{E}}\cdot K^{\prime}_{0}}
.
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
show that if
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
is strongly nondegenerate at
\hbar=0
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{0}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{0}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}_{0}^{\prime}

(42)

forms a Hilbert bimodule between the classical limit fiber C*-algebras
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{0}^{\prime}
at
\hbar=0
, with the middle space given by
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\underline{\mathcal{E}}/\overline{\underline{\mathcal{E}}\cdot K^{\prime}_{0}}
. They call the Hilbert bimodule
\mathcal{E}_{0}
the
Hilbert classical limit
of
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
.

One can then use a duality to construct a symplectic dual pair from the Hilbert classical limit
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. In particular the left
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
-action and right
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
-action each give rise to representations
\pi_{0}
of
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{0}
of (the opposite of)
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{0}
as bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{E}_{0}
.
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
consider the C*-algebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}=(\mathfrak{A}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{0})/\ker(\pi_{0}\otimes\pi^{\prime}_{0}),

(43)

and show that under certain conditions,
\mathfrak{B}\cong C_{0}(S)
for a symplectic manifold
S
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
are generated from strict quantizations, then
\mathfrak{A}_{0}\cong C_{0}(P)
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{0}\cong C_{0}(P^{\prime})
for Poisson manifolds
P
and
P^{\prime}
. In this case, the results of
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
imply that

\textstyle{P}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P^{\prime}}

is a symplectic dual pair, which they call the
symplectic classical limit
. Importantly,
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
establish that the association of
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
with both the Hilbert classical limit
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and the symplectic classical limit
S
respects composition of Hilbert bimodules and symplectic dual pairs by their corresponding tensor product operations, which implies that the association is functorial.

Let us now return to the case of interest in this paper, where we consider the quantizations
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
and
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
for Lie groupoids
G
and
H
as generating uniformly continuous bundles with C*-algebras of uniformly continuous sections given by
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
(i.e., the normal groupoid C*-algebras), respectively. In the next section, we will show that each Hilbert bimodule
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
can be canonically (and functorially) lifted to a Hilbert bimodule

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}^{G}\rightarrowtail\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}^{H}

(46)

between the C*-algebras of uniformly continuous sections. With

\displaystyle K^{G}_{0}=\left\{a\in\mathfrak{A}^{G}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert a(\hbar)\rVert=0\right\}

and

\displaystyle K^{H}_{0}=\left\{b\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar\to 0}\lVert b(\hbar)\rVert=0\right\},

(47)

the construction of
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
then produces the Hilbert classical limit

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}\rightarrowtail(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0}

(48)

with middle space
(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}=\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}/\overline{\underline{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\cdot K^{H}_{0}}
and actions of the abelian C*-algebras
\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}\cong C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0}\cong C_{0}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
at
\hbar=0
. As before, the left
\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}
-action and right
\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0}
-action on
(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}
give rise to representations
\pi^{G}_{0}
of
\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}
and
\pi^{H}_{0}
of (the opposite of)
\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0}
as bounded linear operators. This allows us to consider the C*-algebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{M}=(\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0})/\ker(\pi^{G}_{0}\otimes\pi^{H}_{0})

(49)

Since
\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0}
are abelian, so is
\mathfrak{B}_{M}
. Gelfand duality then implies
\mathfrak{B}_{M}\cong C_{0}(S_{M})
for a topological space
S_{M}
, which also has the structure of a Poisson manifold.
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
treat the special case in which
S_{M}
is already a symplectic manifold.
In the setting of this paper, however,
S_{M}
may not possess a symplectic form. Instead, we will show directly that
S_{M}
is a Lagrangian relation determined by an equivalence class of symplectic dual pairs. Thus for the groupoid structures in the setting of this paper, we arrive at a Lagrangian relation we can understand as the classical limit of
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
.

We can then define the action of
L
on arrows in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
via the association

\displaystyle L:[C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)]\mapsto[S_{M}],

(50)

where
[S_{M}]
denotes the equivalence class of symplectic dual pairs that determine the Lagrangian relation
S_{M}
.

Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
establish that the symplectic classical limit respects composition of arrows by the interior tensor product of Hilbert bimodules in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
and the tensor product of symplectic dual pairs in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
, up to unitary equivalence of Hilbert bimodules and isomorphism of symplectic dual pairs. In fact, this ensures that
L
is well-defined on our categories—to see this, suppose two Hilbert bimodules
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
and
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M^{\prime}}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
determine equivalent induction functors. Then
\mathcal{E}_{M}
and
\mathcal{E}_{M^{\prime}}
must have the same tensor product kernel, and it follows by direct inspection of the construction in Eq. (
49
) that
L(\mathcal{E}_{M})=L(\mathcal{E}_{M^{\prime}})
, since they determine the same Lagrangian relation. Thus,
L
is well-defined, and the result from
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
guarantees that
L
is a functor.

With the functors
Q
and
L
so specified, the remainder of this paper is devoted to (i) showing that
L
indeed has the properties just claimed, and (ii) showing that
Q
and
L
form a categorical equivalence. The main result establishing the categorical equivalence requires showing that
Q
and
L
are almost inverse to each other in the sense that
Q\circ L
and
L\circ Q
are naturally isomorphic to the identity functors. Ultimately, the crux of the issue is to show that whenever
Q\left([T^{*}M]\right)=[\mathcal{E}_{M}]
and
[S_{M}]=L\left([\mathcal{E}_{M}]\right)
, it must follow that
S_{M}\cong(j_{G},j_{H})[T^{*}M]
. We will prove this main result, and thus establish the existence of a categorical equivalence between
\mathbf{LPoisson}
and
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
, in the remainder of the paper.

4
Categorical Equivalence

4.1
Bimodules between Continuous Bundles

Recall that we consider the quantizations
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
and
(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H})_{\hbar\in(0,1]}
for Lie groupoids
G
and
H
as generating uniformly continuous bundles with C*-algebras of sections
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
, respectively. In this section, we will construct a Hilbert bimodule (denoted
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
) between the algebras of continuous sections
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
from an arrow
C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)
in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
, understood as a Hilbert bimodule between the fiber C*-algebras
\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{\hbar}=C^{*}(G)
and
\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{\hbar}=C^{*}(H)
at a fixed value
\hbar\in(0,1]
.

To that end, we consider the space
(0,1]\times M
for the manifold
M
that serves as the middle space of the bibundle
G\rightarrowtail M\leftarrowtail H
. Paralleling the construction of
\mathcal{E}_{M}
after Eq. (
36
), we begin with the vector space
C^{\infty}((0,1]\times M)
. We will understand an element
\underline{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}((0,1]\times M)
as corresponding to a map
(0,1]\to C^{\infty}(M)
with
\hbar\mapsto\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}(\cdot)=\underline{\varphi}(\hbar,\cdot)
. We define the subspace

\displaystyle\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}=\Big\{\underline{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}((0,1]\times M)\ \Big|\ \text{ for each }\hbar\in(0,1],\ \underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)\Big\}

(51)

We will see that a completion of a subspace of
\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
will define the middle space of a Hilbert bimodule denoted
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
.

Note similarly that since continuous sections in
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
are maps
(0,1]\to C^{*}(G)
, we will understand an element
a\in\mathfrak{A}^{G}
as a map
(0,1]\times G\to\mathbb{C}
. Indeed, viewing
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
as the normal groupoid of
G
as in
Landsman 1999
, one sees
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
as a suitable completion of a subspace of
C^{\infty}((0,1]\times G)
, understood with an appropriate smooth manifold structure. Similarly, since continuous sections in
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
are maps
(0,1]\to C^{*}(H)
, we will understand an element
b\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
as a map
(0,1]\times H\to\mathbb{C}
, since
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
is also a suitable completion of a subspace of
C^{\infty}((0,1]\times H)
. For ease of notation, we will write
a_{\hbar}
for
a(\hbar)
and
b_{\hbar}
for
b(\hbar)
.

Define a bilinear map on
\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
by

\displaystyle\langle\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}(\hbar,y)=\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}(y)=\int_{s_{M}^{-1}(t_{H}(y))}d\mu_{t_{H}(y)}(q)\ \overline{\underline{\varphi}(\hbar,q)}\underline{\psi}(\hbar,qy)

(52)

for all
\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\in\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
,
\hbar\in(0,1]
, and
y\in H
. Then define the subspace
\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}\subseteq\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}=\Big\{\underline{\varphi}\in\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}\ \Big|\ \hbar\mapsto\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}\text{ is uniformly continuous with respect to }\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\Big\}.

(53)

Clearly,
\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
is a vector space. Then
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
is defined as the completion of
\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
relative to the norm
\lVert\underline{\varphi}\rVert_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}^{2}=\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi},\underline{\varphi}\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}\rVert
.

This vector space
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
will serve as the middle space of a Hilbert bimodule between the algebras of continuous sections. The left
\mathfrak{A}^{G}
-action and right
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
-action on
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
are given for
a\in\mathfrak{A}^{G}
and
b\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
by

\displaystyle(a\cdot\underline{\varphi})(\hbar,q)

\displaystyle=(a_{\hbar}\cdot\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar})(q)=\int_{t_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))}d\nu^{t}_{t_{M}(q)}(x)\ a(\hbar,x)\underline{\varphi}(\hbar,x^{-1}q)

(54)

\displaystyle(\underline{\varphi}\cdot b)(\hbar,q)

\displaystyle=(\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}\cdot b_{\hbar})(q)=\int_{t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))}d\lambda^{t}_{s_{M}(q)}(y)\ b(\hbar,y^{-1})\underline{\varphi}(\hbar,qy)

(55)

for all
\underline{\varphi}\in\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
,
q\in M
, and
\hbar\in(0,1]
, extended to
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
by continuity.

We now proceed to show that
\mathfrak{A}^{G}\rightarrowtail\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}^{H}
is indeed a Hilbert bimodule. The only feature that must be checked is that
\langle\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
for all
\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\in\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
to ensure that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}
is an
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
-valued inner product. We will need a preliminary lemma concerning the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\in\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
, the map
\hbar\mapsto\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}
is uniformly continuous with respect to the norm on
C^{*}(H)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\epsilon>0
. Since
\hbar\mapsto\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}
and
\hbar\mapsto\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}
are uniformly continuous, there are
\delta_{\varphi}>0
and
\delta_{\psi}>0
such that for any
\hbar,\hbar^{\prime}\in(0,1]
with
\lvert\hbar-\hbar^{\prime}\rvert<\epsilon,

\displaystyle\lVert\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}<\delta_{\varphi}

(56)

\displaystyle\lVert\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}<\delta_{\psi}.

(57)

We define

\displaystyle\delta=\delta_{\psi}\cdot\sup_{\hbar\in(0,1]}\lVert\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}+\delta_{\varphi}\cdot\sup_{\hbar\in(0,1]}\lVert\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}.

(58)

Then for any
\hbar,\hbar^{\prime}\in(0,1]
with
\lvert\hbar-\hbar^{\prime}\rvert<\epsilon
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for Hilbert bimodules
(
Raeburn and Williams 1998
, Lemma 2.5)
implies

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}+\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert

(59)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert+\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert

(60)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\cdot\lVert\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}+\lVert\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar}-\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\cdot\lVert\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}

(61)

\displaystyle<\delta.

(62)

∎

Next, to compare the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}
with the continuous sections in
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
, we shall need some further results concerning the generating set
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{H}
defined (see discussion around Eq.(
12
)) as the *-algebra of pointwise products and sums of sections of the form

\displaystyle[\hbar\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)]

(63)

for
g\in C^{\infty}_{PW}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
g\in C^{\infty}_{PW}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
. The map
\hbar\mapsto\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(g))
is norm continuous for
\hbar\in(0,1]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{\hbar\to\hbar^{\prime}}\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(g)-\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{H}(g))\rVert=0.

(64)

Proof.

The map
\hbar\mapsto K^{\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)}
yielding the Hilbert-Schmidt kernel (Eq.(
5
)) clearly yields a pointwise continuous family of functions on
H\times H
. The dominated convergence theorem in
L^{2}(H\times H)
implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{\hbar\to\hbar^{\prime}}\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(g))-\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{G}(g))\rVert_{HS}=0,

(65)

where
\lVert F\rVert_{HS}=\lVert K_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(H\times H)}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm for the kernel
K_{F}
of any Hilbert-Schmidt operator
F
. Now the fact that
\lVert F\rVert\leq\lVert F\rVert_{HS}
yields the result.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
g_{1},...,g_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{PW}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
. The map
\hbar\mapsto\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(g_{k}))
is norm continuous.

Proof.

By induction. The previous proposition yields the base case. Suppose the condition holds for
g_{1},...,g_{k-1}
. Then using the triangle inequality, one easily finds

\displaystyle\lim_{\hbar\to\hbar^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{k}))-\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{H}(g_{k}))\rVert

(66)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\hbar\to\hbar^{\prime}}\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{k-1}))-\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{H}(g_{k-1}))\rVert\cdot\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}(g_{k}))\rVert

(67)

\displaystyle\hskip 54.2025pt+\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{1})\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g_{k-1}))\rVert\cdot\lVert\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(g_{k}))-\pi^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar^{\prime}}^{G}(G_{k}))\rVert

(68)

\displaystyle=0.

(69)

∎

The analogous statements clearly hold for
G
just as well as for
H
. We are now in a position use these preliminary lemmas ot establish that
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
is the middle space of a Hilbert bimodule.

Proposition 4
.

For any
\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\in\widetilde{\underline{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
,
\langle\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}.

Proof.

Recall from Eq. (
13
) that
b\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
just in case for each
\tilde{b}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{H}
, the map
\hbar\mapsto\lVert b_{\hbar}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar}\rVert
is continuous for all
\hbar\in[0,1]
. Alternatively, this is equivalent to saying
\hbar\mapsto\lVert b_{\hbar}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar}\rVert
is uniformly continuous for all
\hbar\in(0,1]
. Lemma
3
implies that for each
\tilde{b}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{H}
and any
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta_{b}
such that if
\lvert\hbar-\hbar^{\prime}\rvert<\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{b}_{\hbar}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert<\delta_{b}.

(70)

Likewise, Lemma
1
implies that there is a
\delta
such that if
\lvert\hbar-\hbar^{\prime}\rvert<\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert<\delta

(71)

So we have from the reverse triangle inequality that whenever
\lvert\hbar-\hbar^{\prime}\rvert<\epsilon

\displaystyle\Big|\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar}\rVert-\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar^{\prime}},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}\rVert+\lVert\tilde{b}_{\hbar}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar^{\prime}}\rVert

(72)

\displaystyle<\delta+\delta_{b}.

(73)

This establishes the uniform continuity of the map
\hbar\mapsto\lVert\langle\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar},\underline{\psi}_{\hbar}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{M}}-\tilde{b}_{\hbar}\rVert
for any
\tilde{b}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{H}
, which by the definition in Eq.(
13
) implies that
\langle\underline{\varphi},\underline{\psi}\rangle_{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

\mathfrak{A}^{G}\rightarrowtail\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\leftarrowtail\mathfrak{A}^{H}
is a Hilbert bimodule.

We call the Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
the
lift
of
\mathcal{E}_{M}
.

To close this section, we demonstrate that the construction of
\underline{{\mathcal{E}}}_{M}
from
\mathcal{E}_{M}
respects the composition of Hilbert bimodules by means of the tensor product operation in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
. Suppose

\displaystyle C^{*}(G)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{M}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(H)

and

\displaystyle C^{*}(H)\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}_{N}\leftarrowtail C^{*}(G^{\prime}).

(74)

are two Hilbert bimodules in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
between Lie groupoids
G,H,
and
G^{\prime}
corresponding to bibundles

\displaystyle G\rightarrowtail M\leftarrowtail H

and

\displaystyle H\rightarrowtail N\leftarrowtail G^{\prime}.

(75)

Landsman 2001
already shows that the tensor product
\mathcal{E}_{M}\otimes_{C^{*}(H)}\mathcal{E}_{N}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathcal{E}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
.
Hence, one can use the procedure leading to Corollary
5
to define the lift of
\mathcal{E}_{M}\otimes_{C^{*}(H)}\mathcal{E}_{N}
as the lift of
\mathcal{E}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
.

Proposition 6
.

The Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{A}^{H}}\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{N}
is unitarily equivalent to the lift of
\mathcal{E}_{M}\otimes_{C^{*}(H)}\mathcal{E}_{N}
.

Proof.

We need to show that
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{A}^{H}}\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{N}\cong\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
, where the latter is by definition the lift of
\mathcal{E}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
.
Landsman 2001
explicitly defines a unitary equivalence
U:\mathcal{E}_{M}\otimes_{C^{*}(H)}\mathcal{E}_{N}\to\mathcal{E}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
. Now, define
\underline{U}:\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{A}^{H}}\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{N}\to\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M\circledast_{H}N}
by

\displaystyle(\underline{U}\underline{\varphi})_{\hbar}=U(\underline{\varphi}_{\hbar})

(76)

for
\underline{\varphi}\in\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{A}^{H}}\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{N}
and
\hbar\in(0,1]
. It is easy to see that
\underline{U}
has the appropriate range and serves also as a unitary equivalence.
∎

Finally, we note that one can check that the association
\mathcal{E}_{M}\mapsto\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
preserves identities, and so is functorial.

In order to take the classical limit of the Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
, we must check that it is strongly nondegenerate at
\hbar=0
in the sense of
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
.

Proposition 7
.

Define

\displaystyle K_{0}^{G}=\left\{a\in\mathfrak{A}^{G}\ |\ \lim_{\hbar\to 0}||a_{\hbar}||=0\right\}

\displaystyle K_{0}^{H}=\left\{b\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}\ |\lim_{\hbar\to 0}||b_{\hbar}||=0\right\}.

(77)

Then
\overline{K_{0}^{G}\cdot\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}\subseteq\overline{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\cdot K_{0}^{H}}
.

Proof.

Let
\underline{\psi}\in\overline{K_{0}^{G}\cdot\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}}
. Then there are
\overset{n}{a}\in K_{0}^{G}
with
\lim_{\hbar\to 0}||\overset{n}{a}_{\hbar}||=0
and
\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}}\in\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
such that
\underline{\psi}=\sum_{n}\overset{n}{a}\cdot\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}}
. Note that
\mathfrak{A}^{H}
has a sequential approximate identity
\overset{m}{b}\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
: e.g., one can take a sequence of functions
\overset{m}{b}(\hbar,y)\in C^{\infty}((0,1]\times H)
such that for each
\hbar\in(0,1]
,
\overset{m}{b}(\hbar,y)
approaches a delta function around
y=e
in
H
as
m\to\infty
(with the
m\to\infty
limit understood in any appropriate distributional sense). Then it follows that for any
\underline{\varphi}\in\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
,

\displaystyle||\underline{\varphi}-\underline{\varphi}\cdot\overset{m}{b}||_{\underline{\mathcal{E}_{M}}}\to 0

(78)

as
m\to\infty
. Hence, defining
\overset{n,m}{b^{\prime}}_{\hbar}=||\overset{n}{a}_{\hbar}||\cdot\overset{m}{b}_{\hbar}
, we have that
\overset{n,m}{b^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{H}
by the definition of a uniformly continuous bundle, and clearly
\lim_{\hbar\to 0}||\overset{n,m}{b^{\prime}}_{\hbar}||=0
so it follows that
\overset{n,m}{b^{\prime}}\in K_{0}^{H}
. Likewise, defining
\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}}_{\hbar}=\left(||\overset{n}{a}_{\hbar}||^{-1}\cdot\overset{n}{a}_{\hbar}\right)\cdot\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}}
, we have
\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}}\in\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
and

\displaystyle\underline{\psi}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{n}\overset{n}{\underline{\varphi}^{\prime}}\cdot\overset{n,m}{b^{\prime}}.

(79)

Therefore,
\underline{\psi}\in\overline{\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}\cdot K_{0}^{H}}

∎

The previous argument actually establishes strong nondegeneracy under general conditions, as long as an appropriate sequential approximate identity exists for the right action.

To summarize, in this section we have shown that each Hilbert bimodule
\mathcal{E}_{M}
in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
canonically and functorially lifts to a Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
between the C*-algebras of uniformly continuous sections of the corresponding bundles. Further, the Hilbert bimodule
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
is strongly nondegenerate, so the construction of
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
implies that
\underline{\mathcal{E}}_{M}
has a Hilbert classical limit, which we denote
(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}
. The Hilbert bimodule
C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})\rightarrowtail(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}\leftarrowtail C_{0}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
at
\hbar=0
gives rise to canonical representations
\pi^{G}_{0}
and
\pi^{H}_{0}
on
(\mathcal{E}_{M})_{0}
of
C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
and (the opposite of)
C_{0}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
, respectively. We can then follow
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
in defining the space
S_{M}
as the Gelfand dual of the quotient
(C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})\otimes C_{0}(\mathfrak{H}^{*}))/\ker\left(\pi^{G}_{0}\otimes\pi^{H}_{0}\right)
, as in Eq.(
49
). In our setting,
S_{M}
is not in general a symplectic space, but it will turn out that it is isomorphic to a Lagrangian relation
S_{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{*}\times\mathfrak{H}^{*}
.

4.2
Local Structure of Classical Dual Pairs

Recall that our goal is to show that the functors
Q
and
L
form a categorical equivalence, which involves establishing that
S_{M}
is isomorphic to the Lagrangian relation
(j_{G},j_{H})[T^{*}M]
. To prove this, we will require some additional results concerning the local structure of the dual pair

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}^{*}\leftarrow T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{*}.

(80)

Throughout this section, we assume as before that
T^{*}M
is a dual pair in
\mathbf{LPoisson}
, which arises from a regular bibundle
M
. Our goal in this section is to establish the requisite facts about this dual pair to proceed to our categorical equivalence.

For ease of reference, we begin by recalling the definitions of the maps
j_{G}
and
j_{H}
. The Poisson morphism
j_{G}:T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{G}^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle j_{G}(\eta_{q})\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\gamma(t)\Big)=-\eta_{q}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\cdot q\big)\Big),

(81)

where
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
\gamma:I\to G
is a curve with
\gamma(0)=t_{M}(q)
and
\gamma(t)\in t_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))
so that
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in\tau_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))
and
\gamma(t)^{-1}\cdot q
is well-defined for all
t\in I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Likewise, the Poisson morphism
j_{H}:T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{H}^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle j_{H}(\eta_{q})\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\gamma(t)\Big)=\eta_{q}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\big(q\cdot\gamma(t)\big)\Big),

(82)

where
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
\gamma:I\to H
is a curve with
\gamma(0)=s_{M}(q)
and
\gamma(t)\in t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))
, so that
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in\tau_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))
and
q\cdot\gamma(t)
is well-defined for all
t\in I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

In what follows, we denote
\mathfrak{G}_{x_{0}}=\{X\in\mathfrak{G}\ |\ t_{G}(\exp^{W}(X))=x_{0}\}
and likewise,
\mathfrak{H}_{y_{0}}=\{Y\in\mathfrak{H}\ |\ t_{H}(\exp^{W}(Y))=y_{0}\}
. Note further that for each
q\in M
, we have a map
(j_{G})^{*}_{|q}:\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\to T_{q}M
defined by

\displaystyle(j_{G})^{*}_{|q}(X)=-\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}\exp^{W}(tX)^{-1}\cdot q

(83)

for all
X\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}
. Likewise, we have a map
(j_{H})^{*}_{|q}:\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}\to T_{q}M
defined by

\displaystyle(j_{H})^{*}_{|q}(Y)=\frac{d}{dt}_{|t=0}q\cdot\exp^{W}(tY)

(84)

for all
Y\in\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
.
Using these maps, we define
j_{|q}:T^{*}_{q}M\to\mathfrak{G}^{*}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{s_{M}(q)}
by

\displaystyle j_{|q}=(j_{G})_{|q}\oplus(j_{H})_{|q},

(85)

and we define
j^{*}_{|q}:\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}\to T_{q}M
by

\displaystyle j^{*}_{|q}=(j_{G})^{*}_{|q}\oplus(j_{H})^{*}_{|q}.

(86)

Together, our definitions read

\displaystyle j_{|q}(\eta_{q})(Z)=(\eta_{q})(j^{*}_{|q}(Z))

(87)

for all
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
and
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
.

The following propositions follow from simple dimension counting arguments.
We state them explicitly here only in order to establish the notation we will use in the next section.

Lemma 8
.

There is a linear map
\rho_{q}:T_{q}M\to\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
such that

\displaystyle j^{*}_{|q}\circ\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})=\xi_{q}

(88)

for all
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
.

Proof.

Choose a basis
\overset{1}{\xi}_{q},...,\overset{n}{\xi}_{q}
for
T_{q}M
(here,
n=\dim M
). For each
1\leq k\leq n
, choose
Z_{k}\in(j^{*})_{|q}^{-1}\Big(\overset{k}{\xi}_{q}\Big)
. Then for
\xi_{q}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\cdot\overset{k}{\xi}_{q}
, define

\displaystyle\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\cdot Z_{k}.

(89)

Clearly,
\rho_{q}
is linear by construction and

\displaystyle j^{*}_{|q}\circ\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\cdot j^{*}_{|q}(Z_{k})=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\cdot\overset{k}{\xi}_{q}=\xi_{q}.

(90)

∎

Now, fix a choice of linear map
\rho_{q}
satisfying the conditions of the previous lemma. Note
T_{q}M=\text{im}(j^{*}_{|q})
and define
\mathcal{I}_{q}=\ker(j^{*}_{|q})
. Then define a further mapping denoted
\iota_{q}:\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}\to T_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}
by

\displaystyle\iota_{q}(Z)=j^{*}_{|q}(Z)\oplus\big(Z-\rho_{q}\circ j^{*}_{|q}(Z)\big)

(91)

for all
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}.

Proposition 9
.

The map
\iota_{q}
is an isomorphism
\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}\cong T_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}
.

Proof.

Define the map
\iota_{q}^{-1}:T_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}\to\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
by

\displaystyle\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)=\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})+Z

(92)

for
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
and
Z\in\mathcal{I}_{q}
. Then
\iota_{q}^{-1}
is indeed the inverse of
\iota_{q}
because for all
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{q}^{-1}\circ\iota_{q}(Z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{q}\circ j^{*}_{|q}(Z)+Z-\rho_{q}\circ j^{*}_{|q}(Z)

(93)

\displaystyle=Z.

(94)

And for all
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
and
Z\in\mathcal{I}_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

\displaystyle=j^{*}_{|q}(\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})+Z)\oplus\big(\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})+Z-\rho_{q}\circ j^{*}_{|q}(\rho_{q}(\xi_{q})+Z)\big)

(95)

\displaystyle=\xi_{q}\oplus Z.

(96)

∎

Likewise, define the dual map
\iota^{*}_{q}:T^{*}_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}\to\mathfrak{G}^{*}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{s_{M}(q)}
by

\displaystyle\iota^{*}_{q}(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(Z)=(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(Z))

(97)

for all
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
,
\Phi\in\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}
, and
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}.

Proposition 10
.

The map
\iota^{*}_{q}
is an isomorphism
T^{*}_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{G}^{*}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{s_{M}(q)}
.

Proof.

Define the map
(\iota^{*}_{q})^{-1}:\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}^{*}\to T^{*}_{q}M\oplus\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}
by

\displaystyle(\iota^{*}_{q})^{-1}(\Phi)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)=\Phi(\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z))

(98)

for
\Phi\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}^{*}
,
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
, and
Z\in\mathcal{I}_{q}
. Then
(\iota_{q}^{*})^{-1}
is indeed the inverse of
\iota_{q}^{*}
because for all
\eta_{q}\in T_{q}^{*}M
,
\Phi\in\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}
,
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
, and
Z\in\mathcal{I}_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\big((\iota_{q}^{*})^{-1}\circ\iota_{q}^{*}\big)(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

\displaystyle=\iota_{q}^{*}(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z))

(99)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(\iota_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z))

(100)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z),

(101)

and for all
\Phi\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}^{*}
and
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\iota^{*}_{q}\circ(\iota_{q}^{*})^{-1}\big)(\Phi)(Z)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{q}^{*})^{-1}(\Phi)(\iota_{q}(Z))

(102)

\displaystyle=\Phi(\iota_{q}^{-1}\circ\iota_{q}(Z))

(103)

\displaystyle=\Phi(Z).

(104)

∎

Proposition 11
.

For any
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{q}^{*}(\eta_{q}\oplus 0)=j_{|q}(\eta_{q}).

(105)

Proof.

For any
\eta_{q}\in T_{q}^{*}M
and any
Z\in\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{q}^{*}(\eta_{q}\oplus 0)(Z)

\displaystyle=(\eta_{q}\oplus 0)(\iota_{q}(Z))

(106)

\displaystyle=\eta_{q}(j^{*}_{|q}(Z))

(107)

\displaystyle=j_{|q}(\eta_{q})(Z).

(108)

∎

We will proceed to use these preliminaries in the next section to analyze the relationship between the spaces
T^{*}M
and
S_{M}
.

4.3
Natural Isomorphism for the Classical Limit

In this section, we prove the main results of this paper. We will show that
S_{M}
is isomorphic to the Lagrangian relation determined by
T^{*}M
. This guarantees that
L
is well-defined, with
S_{M}
the canonical invariant of the equivalence class of symplectic dual pairs
L\circ Q\left([T^{*}M]\right)
. More substantively, the result implies the existence of natural isomorphisms
L\circ Q\to 1_{\mathbf{LC^{*}}}
and
Q\circ L\to 1_{\mathbf{LPoisson}}
.

We begin with some preliminaries. For
q\in M
, let
E_{q}
denote the Jacobian such that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{G}^{-1(t_{M}(q))}}

\displaystyle\int_{t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))}d\nu^{t}_{t_{M}(q)}(x)\ d\lambda^{t}_{s_{M}(q)(y)}\ F(x,y)

(109)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}}\int_{\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}}E_{q}(X,Y)\ d^{n}X\ d^{m}Y\ F(\exp^{W}(X),\exp^{W}(Y))

(110)

for
F:G\times H\to\mathbb{C}
integrable on the fibers of
t_{G}\times t_{H}
. Here,
d^{n}X
and
d^{m}Y
are the Lebesgue measures on
\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}
with
n=\dim G
and
m=\dim H
.

Given two functions
f\in\mathfrak{G}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
and
g\in\mathfrak{H}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
, we will use the shorthand
(f\cdot g):\mathfrak{G}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
to denote the function

\displaystyle(f\cdot g)(\theta\oplus\phi)=f(\theta)\cdot g(\phi)

(111)

for
\theta\in\mathfrak{G}^{*}
and
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}^{*}
.

To prove that
S_{M}\cong T^{*}M
, we shall show that
C_{0}(S_{M})\cong C_{0}(T^{*}M)
. Recall that, by definition in Eq. (
49
), we have
C_{0}(S_{M})=(\mathfrak{A}^{G}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{A}^{H}_{0})/\ker(\pi^{G}_{0}\otimes\pi_{0}^{H})
. Our first task is to analyze
\ker(\pi_{0}^{G}\otimes\pi_{0}^{H})
. To do so, we likewise make use of the representations at
\hbar=1
denoted
\pi_{1}^{G}
and
\pi^{H}_{1}
of
C^{*}(G)
and (the opposite of)
C^{*}(H)
on
\mathcal{E}_{M}
given by the left
C^{*}(G)
-action and right
C^{*}(H)
-action of the Hilbert bimodule. We will first characterize
\ker(\pi_{1}^{G}\otimes\pi_{1}^{H})
as a step towards characterizing
\ker(\pi_{0}^{G}\otimes\pi_{0}^{H})
.

Given
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
, define
\beta_{\hbar}^{\xi_{q}}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})\otimes C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
by

\displaystyle\beta_{\hbar}^{\xi_{q}}

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})

(112)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}}\frac{E_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)\ d^{m}Zd^{m}\Phi}{\hbar^{m}}\big((\kappa_{G}\cdot\kappa_{H})\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}\big)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

(113)

\displaystyle\hskip 180.67499pt\times\big((f\cdot g)\circ\iota_{q}^{*}\big)(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)e^{-i\Phi(Z)/\hbar}

(114)

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
,
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
, and
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
.

Proposition 12
.

For all
\hbar\in(0,1]
,
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
, and
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(f)\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)\big)\in\ker(\pi_{1}^{G}\otimes\pi_{1}^{H})

(115)

iff for almost all
q\in M
,
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
and
\eta_{q}\in T_{q}^{*}M
,

\displaystyle\beta_{\hbar}^{\xi_{q}}(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})=0.

(116)

Proof.

For
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\big(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(f))\otimes\pi_{1}^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g))\big)\psi\Big)(q)=\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(f)\cdot\psi\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)\big)(q)

(117)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{G}^{-1}(t_{M}(q))}\int_{t_{H}^{-1}(s_{M}(q))}d\nu^{t}_{t_{M}(q)}(x)\ d\lambda^{t}_{s_{M}(q)}(y)\ \big(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(f)\big)(x)\psi(x^{-1}qy)\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)\big)(y^{-1})

(118)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}}\int_{\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}}\frac{E_{q}(X,Y)\ d^{n}X\ d^{m}Y}{\hbar^{(n+m)}}\kappa_{G}(X)\kappa_{H}(Y)

(119)

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\times\hat{f}(X/\hbar)\psi\big(\exp^{W}(X)^{-1}\cdot q\cdot\exp^{W}(Y)^{-1}\big)\hat{g}(Y/\hbar)

(120)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{G}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{s_{M}(q)}}\int_{\mathfrak{G}^{*}_{t_{M}(q)}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{s_{M}(q)}}\frac{E_{q}(X,Y)\ d^{n}X\ d^{m}Y\ d^{n}\theta\ d^{m}\phi}{\hbar^{(n+m)}}\kappa_{G}(X)\kappa_{H}(Y)

(121)

\displaystyle\hskip 54.2025pt\times f(\theta)g(\phi)e^{-i\big(\theta(X)+\phi(Y)\big)/\hbar}\psi\big(\exp^{W}(X)^{-1}\cdot q\cdot\exp^{W}(Y)^{-1}\big).

(122)

It follows that
\pi_{1}^{G}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(f))\otimes\pi_{1}^{H}(\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}(g))
is an integral operator with kernel
K^{f\otimes g}:M\times M\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle K^{f\otimes g}\Big(q,\exp^{W}\big(\iota_{q}^{-1}(\xi_{q}\oplus 0)\big)\cdot q\Big)

(123)

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{*}_{q}M}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}^{*}}\frac{(E_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1})(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)\ d^{n}\eta_{q}\ d^{m}Z\ d^{m}\Phi}{\hbar^{(n+m)}}\big((\kappa_{G}\cdot\kappa_{H})\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}\big)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

(124)

\displaystyle\hskip 144.54pt\times\big((f\cdot g)\circ\iota_{q}^{*}\big)(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)e^{-i\big(\eta_{q}(\xi_{q})+\Phi(Z)\big)/\hbar}

(125)

for all
q\in M
and
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
. Now the fact that the Fourier transform
T_{q}^{*}M\to T_{q}M
is one-to-one yields the result.

∎

Next, define
\beta^{M}_{0}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})\otimes C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
by

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})=(f\circ j_{G})(\eta_{q})\cdot(g\circ j_{H})(\eta_{q})

(126)

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
,
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
, and
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
.

Recall further that it follows from the results of
Steeger and Feintzeig 2021b
that there are isomorphisms
\chi_{G}:C_{0}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})\to\mathfrak{A}_{0}^{G}
and
\chi_{H}:C_{0}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})\to\mathfrak{A}_{0}^{H}
given explicitly by the continuous extension of

\displaystyle\chi_{G}(f)=[\hbar\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{G}(f)]+K_{0}^{G}

(127)

\displaystyle\chi_{H}(g)=[\hbar\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_{\hbar}^{H}(g)]+K_{0}^{H}

(128)

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{PW}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
and
g\in C^{\infty}_{PW}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
.

Proposition 13
.

For all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{G}^{*})
and
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\chi_{G}(f)\otimes\chi_{H}(g)\big)\in\ker(\pi_{0}^{G}\otimes\pi_{0}^{H})

(129)

iff for almost all
q\in M
and almost all
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
,

\displaystyle\beta_{0}^{M}(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})=0.

(130)

Proof.

From Prop.
12
, we have that
(f\otimes g)\in\ker(\pi^{G}_{0}\otimes\pi^{H}_{0})
iff
\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\beta^{\xi_{q}}_{\hbar}(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})=0
for almost all
q\in M
,
\xi_{q}\in T_{q}M
, and
\eta_{q}\in T^{*}_{q}M
. We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\beta^{\xi_{q}}_{\hbar}(f\otimes g)(\eta_{q})

(131)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}\oplus K^{*}_{q}}\frac{(E_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1})(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)\ d^{m}Z\ d^{m}\Phi}{\hbar^{m}}\big((\kappa_{G}\cdot\kappa_{H})\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}\big)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

(132)

\displaystyle\hskip 144.54pt\times\big((f\cdot g)\circ\iota_{q}^{*}\big)(\eta_{q}\oplus\Phi)e^{-i\Phi(Z)/\hbar}

(133)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{q}\oplus K^{*}_{q}}(E_{q}\circ\iota_{q}^{-1})(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)\ d^{m}Z\ d^{m}\Phi\ \big((\kappa_{G}\cdot\kappa_{H})\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}\big)(\xi_{q}\oplus Z)

(134)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established a one-to-one correspondence between certain equivalence classes of symplectic dual pairs and corresponding equivalence classes of Hilbert bimodules. Symplectic dual pairs, and the Lagrangian relations they determine, can be understood as morphisms between classical phase spaces that preserve representation-theoretic structures. Hilbert bimodules can be likewise understood as morphisms between algebras of quantum observables that preserve representation-theoretic structure. So our one-to-one correspondence of morphisms encodes an equivalence of categories, as well as a type of equivalence of representation theories between models of classical and quantum physics.

Our technical results extend the procedure of
Feintzeig and Steeger 2024
to construct Lagrangian relations as the classical limit of a wider collection of (equivalence classes of) Hilbert bimodules. This achievement obviates the need for extra conditions that ensure the resulting classical limit space is a symplectic manifold, reframing the classical limit in terms of Lagrangian relations rather than symplectic dual pairs.

Our results open further questions about the categories defined in this paper. In
\mathbf{LPoisson}
, we motivated considering Lagrangian relations rather than symplectic dual pairs themselves because Lagrangian relations encode sufficient information to determine an induction functor from symplectic realizations of one Poisson manifold to those of another. However, in
\mathbf{LC^{*}}
, we motivated considering tensor product kernels rather than Hilbert bimodules themselves because tensor product kernels are (merely) an invariant of the corresponding induction functor from Hilbert space representations of one C*-algebra to those of another. How close is the analogy between Lagrangian relations and tensor product kernels? Are there conditions that would uniquely determine an induction functor from a given tensor product kernel? Or are there other ways of defining morphisms in a category of C*-algebras altogether, so that morphisms fully determine induction functors between C*-algebras? Finally, we mention a further open area of research suggested by
Landsman 2003
, concerning whether one can generalize the quantization and classical limit functors that we have been working with to more general classes of morphisms inspired by
K
-theory for C*-algebras.

Targeted therapy guided by photon-counting computed tomography in Klippel-Trénaunay syndrome
Authors: Wenli Jiang, Xin Wei, Zhiming Zhou
Published: 2026

A 24-year-old man presented with progressive swelling and pain in his congenitally enlarged left lower limb. Physical examination revealed left-sided hemihypertrophy, extensive ecchymosis, scattered petechiae on the skin (port wine stain), visible venous patterning on the lateral thigh, and prominent clusters of dilated, nodular veins with a cobblestone appearance on the calf (A (i)). The symptoms had worsened significantly in recent months. The patient gave consent for publication of his case and images.

Given the severe swelling precluding direct venous access, a comprehensive vascular assessment was performed using photon-counting computed tomography (CT). After standard lower limb CT angiography, ultra-high-resolution CT venography was acquired in the same session using an indirect, delayed-phase (150-second) protocol.

Cinematic rendering of the CT venography data exquisitely mapped an extensive, tortuous network of dilated superficial and deep venous channels throughout the left limb (A (ii/Cover and iii)). Axial ultra-high-resolution images at the knee demonstrated marked enlargement of the left popliteal vein (arrow) and multiple venous channels within the subcutaneous tissue and intermuscular spaces, suggesting extensive soft tissue venous malformation (B (i)). Corresponding bone window images revealed multiple small, low-attenuation foci within the distal femoral metaphysis (yellow arrow), and soft tissue swelling (white arrow), findings associated with chronic limb overgrowth in Klippel-Trénaunay syndrome, which were absent contralaterally (B (ii)). Subsequent digital subtraction angiography of the calf demonstrated normal arterial anatomy in the left lower extremity, along with early venous filling and tortuous venous dilation in the left thigh and calf during the arterial phase, confirming the presence of a low-flow arteriovenous communication within the dominant venous malformation (B (iii and iv); Supplementary Video).

Based on these imaging findings, the patient was diagnosed with Klippel-Trénaunay syndrome with a significant low-flow arteriovenous component.1 He subsequently underwent successful transarterial embolization of the arteriovenous communications using PVA particles, followed by ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy of the varicose veins and intralesional pingyangmycin injection. The patient tolerated the procedure well, with symptomatic improvement in ecchymosis noted at follow-up (B (v)). This case underscores the critical role of combined photon-counting CT scans and digital subtraction angiography in not only diagnosing complex vascular anomalies but also in precisely delineating hemodynamic components to guide effective multimodality interventional therapy.

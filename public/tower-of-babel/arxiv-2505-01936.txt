2.
Basic notions

In order that this paper be relatively self-contained, we first review some basic definitions. For proofs, and for background on linear orders and condensations, see
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
linear order
is a set
L
together with a binary relation
\leq_{L}
that is a partial order under which any two elements of
L
are comparable. That is, for all
x,y,z\in L
,

(1)

x\leq_{L}x
(reflexivity),

(2)

[(x\leq_{L}y)\wedge(y\leq_{L}x)]\implies x=y
(antisymmetry), and

(3)

[(x\leq_{L}y)\wedge(y\leq_{L}z)]\implies x\leq_{L}z
(transitivity); and, in addition,

(4)

(x\leq_{L}y)\vee(y\leq_{L}x)
(any two elements are comparable).

A linear order in which every non-empty subset has a least element is a
well-order
. Linear orders
(L,\leq_{L})
and
(M,\leq_{M})
are
order-isomorphic
, or have the same
order type
, if there is an order-preserving bijection from
L
to
M
. If there is an order-preserving injection from
L
to
M
, we say that
L

embeds
into
M
, and write
L\preceq M
.

We will drop the subscripts from our
\leq
signs where the context is clear. We will use the convention in
[
8
]
that we have selected, from each class of order types, a particular representative; and, in the case of well-orders, we will choose that order type to be an ordinal (every well-order is isomorphic to an ordinal). For convenience, we will also take
{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
to be the designated representatives of their respective equivalence classes.
\operatorname{o.\!t.}(L)
will denote the order type of a linear order
L
.

An
interval
in a linear order
L
is a subset
I
of
L
such that whenever
x,y\in I
and
z\in L
with
x\leq z\leq y
, we have
z\in I
. That is, an interval is a convex subset of
L
. We use the notation
[\{x,y\}]
, where
x,y\in L
, to denote either
[x,y]
(if
x\leq y
) or
[y,x]
(if
y<x
).

Definition/Lemma 2.2
.

A

of a linear order

is an equivalence relation
\sim
on
L
whose equivalence classes are intervals. A condensation
\sim
is uniquely associated with a map
\boldsymbol{c}:L\to{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim}$}}}

The induced order on

3.
The left rectangular band
R
and the product modulo the finite condensation

In this section, we show that a set of four order types arising naturally from Hausdorff’s Theorem forms a left rectangular band (first defined in
[
1
]
) under an operation defined in terms of the finite condensation. A
scattered
linear order is one that does not contain a copy of the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
. Hausdorff’s theorem on the countable scattered linear orderings (
[
6
]
; see
[
8
]
) states that a countable linear ordering
L
is scattered if and only if it is very discrete. Loosely speaking, any countable scattered linear order can be built recursively as a generalized sum from a “basis” consisting of the simple order types
\omega
,
\omega^{*}
, and
\zeta
, along with finite order types. Here
\omega
denotes the order type of the natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
;
\omega^{*}
denotes the order type of the negative integers
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
; and
\zeta
denotes the order type of the integers
\mathbb{Z}
.

.

The classes
\mathit{VD}_{\alpha}
of linear orders, for each countable ordinal

, are defined inductively as follows:

(1)

and
1

.

(2)

Let
\alpha<\omega_{1}
and suppose
\mathit{VD}_{\beta}
has already been defined for each
\beta<\alpha
. Then let

L
is well-ordered, then so is
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
.

Proof.

The proof is by contrapositive. Suppose
L
is a linear order such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
is not well-ordered. Then we can find an infinite descending sequence of elements of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
. That is, there are
x_{n}\in L
, for
n\in\omega
, such that
c_{F}(x_{0})>c_{F}(x_{1})>\cdots>c_{F}(x_{n})>c_{F}(x_{n+1})>\cdots
in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
. (Recall that elements of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
are finite condensation classes of elements of
L
.) But then also
x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}>x_{n+1}>\cdots
in
L
, so that
L
is not well-ordered either.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\alpha
and
\beta
are ordinals with
\alpha<\beta
, then
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)=\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha)\leq\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\beta)=\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Note that
\phi^{F}_{r}(1)
acts identically to
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
: for any ordinal
\alpha
,
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)=\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha)=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})
, since
1\alpha\cong\alpha 1
. That is, both
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
and
\phi^{F}_{r}(1)
send an ordinal
\alpha
to the ordinal isomorphic to its finite condensation.

We know by Lemma
4.2
that the finite condensation of a well-ordering is also a well-ordering. Suppose
\alpha
and
\beta
are ordinals with
\alpha<\beta
. Then
\alpha\subseteq\beta
. Note that the equivalence class mod
\sim_{\textrm{F}}
of an
x\in\alpha
might be different depending on whether we take
\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
with respect to
\alpha
or with respect to
\beta
. For this reason, for each
x\in\alpha
, denote
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\{y\in\alpha:y\sim_{\textrm{F}}x\}
, and denote
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\{y\in\beta:y\sim_{\textrm{F}}x\}
. Let
x\in\alpha
. If
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
is entirely contained in
\alpha
, then
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
. If
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)\not\subseteq\alpha
– that is, if
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
overlaps with both
\alpha
and
\beta\setminus\alpha
– then
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)\cap\alpha
. In fact,
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)\cap\alpha
in any case. Define a map
g:{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\to{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
by
g(^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x))=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
, for
x\in\alpha
. Then
g
is injective: for if
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(y)
for some
x,y\in\alpha
, then
{}^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)\cap\alpha=\,^{\beta}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(y)\cap\alpha
, so
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)=\,^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(y)
. In fact,
g
sends each interval
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
in
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
to its exact copy in
\beta
, except possibly for the very last one in case
\alpha
is a successor ordinal. (In that case, that very last interval
{}^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(x)
gets sent by
g
to something a little bigger – containing some elements of
\beta\setminus\alpha
.) This means that
g
maps
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
order-isomorphically to an initial segment of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}
. Therefore
\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha)=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})\leq\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})=\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\beta)
, as desired.
∎

We next show that left multiplication by
\omega
modulo the finite condensation is an endomorphism of
\mathbf{ON}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
\alpha
and
\beta
are ordinals with
\alpha<\beta
, then
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\alpha)\leq\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\beta)
. That is,
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)
is a weakly order-preserving map on
\mathbf{ON}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be ordinals with
\alpha<\beta
. Then
\omega\alpha\leq\omega\beta
, since ordinal multiplication is weakly monotone. Then by Lemma
4.3
, we have

\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\alpha)=\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\alpha=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})\leq\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\omega\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega\beta}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})=\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\beta=\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\beta).

∎

Therefore we have, by Lemmas
4.1
,
4.3
, and
4.4
, that each of the class maps
\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega),\phi^{F}_{l}(1),\phi^{F}_{r}(1)
, and
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)
is an endomorphism of
\mathbf{ON}
. Stated another way, we have class maps
\phi^{F}_{l}:R^{\mathbf{ON}}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{ON})
and
\phi^{F}_{r}:R^{\mathbf{ON}}\to\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{ON})
. One might ask whether
\phi^{F}_{l}
and
\phi^{F}_{r}
act like true representations in the sense of structure-preserving maps from the left rectangular band
R^{\mathbf{ON}}
under
\cdot_{\textrm{F}}
to the class
\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{ON})
under composition. That is, given
X,Y\in R^{\mathbf{ON}}
, is it the case that
\phi^{F}_{r}(X\cdot_{\textrm{F}}Y)=\phi^{F}_{r}(X)\circ\phi^{F}_{r}(Y)
? The answer is “no”;
\phi^{F}_{r}
preserves the products
\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1
,
1\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega
, and
\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega
, but it does not preserve the product
1\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1
. (A similar situation holds for the map
\phi^{F}_{l}
.) We check these four cases, using Lemma
3.3
repeatedly, along with the fact that
\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.22221pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega 1}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.22221pt}{$\textstyle{\omega 1}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.25555pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega 1}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega 1}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})=1
. Let
\alpha
be any ordinal.

Case 1:

X=Y=\omega
. Then

\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega)(\alpha)=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega=\alpha,

and also

\displaystyle[\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)\circ\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)](\alpha)

\displaystyle=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\alpha))=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega)

\displaystyle=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega=\alpha.

Case 2:

X=\omega
and

. Then

\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1)(\alpha)=\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}),

and also

\displaystyle[\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)\circ\phi^{F}_{r}(1)](\alpha)

\displaystyle=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha))=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1)

\displaystyle=\phi^{F}_{r}(\omega)(\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}))=(\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}))\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\omega

\displaystyle=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}).

Case 3:

X=1
and
Y=\omega
: this is similar to Case 2.

Case 4:

X=Y=1
. Then

\phi^{F}_{r}(1\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1)(\alpha)=\phi^{F}_{r}(1)(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot_{\textrm{F}}1=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}),

but

\displaystyle[\phi^{R}_{r}(1)\circ\phi^{F}_{r}(1)](\alpha)

5.
The
\phi^{F}_{l}
maps on ordinals of finite degree

To further describe the maps
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)
(left multiplication, modulo the finite condensation, by
\omega
) and
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
(modding out by
\sim_{\textrm{F}}
), we use the Cantor Normal Form Theorem, which states that any ordinal can be decomposed uniquely into a certain polynomial in
\omega
. (This is Theorem 3.46 in
[
8
]
. Note that ordinal multiplication in
[
8
]
is written antilexicographically.)

Theorem 5.1

(Cantor Normal Form)
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal. Then
\alpha
can be written in the form

n_{1}\omega^{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+n_{k}\omega^{\alpha_{k}}

where
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}>\cdots>\alpha_{k}
are ordinals and where
k
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
are natural numbers (with
n_{1}\neq 0
). Further, this decomposition is unique. ∎

The largest power
\alpha_{1}
of
\omega
that appears in the Cantor normal form of an ordinal
\alpha
is called the
degree
of
\alpha
, denoted
\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)
.
The ordinals of finite degree are those whose Cantor normal form looks like
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
for some
n,a_{n},\ldots,a_{0}\in\omega
with
n\neq 0
. In this section, we show in Corollary
5.8
that when
\alpha
is of finite degree, the map
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)
has a very simple-to-describe effect: it sends

\alpha
to
\omega^{\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)}
. To do this, we show in Proposition
5.7
that if
\alpha
is an ordinal of finite degree in Cantor normal form, then the lexicographic product
\omega\alpha
is isomorphic to
\omega^{\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)+1}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
n\in\omega
,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\cong\omega^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\omega
. By the associativity of linear order multiplication,
\omega^{n+1}\cong\omega^{n}\omega
. Then by Lemma
3.3
,

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.17558pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.3889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega^{n+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\omega^{n}\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.3214pt}{$\textstyle{\omega^{n}\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\omega^{n}\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\omega^{n}\omega}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\cong\omega^{n}.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
m<n<\omega
, then
\omega^{n}
begins with
\omega
-many copies of
\omega^{m}
, and there is a well-ordered set
D
such that as a linear order,
\omega^{n}\cong\omega\omega^{m}+D
.

Proof.

Suppose
m<n<\omega
. By the associativity of linear order multiplication,
\omega^{n}\cong\omega\omega^{n-1}
, which is the linear order obtained by replacing each element of
\omega
with a copy of
\omega^{n-1}
.

Consider the first of these copies of
\omega^{n-1}
, the one that replaced
0
in forming
\omega\omega^{n-1}
. This copy of
\omega^{n-1}
can be written as
\omega\omega^{n-2}
, which is obtained by replacing each element of
\omega
with a copy of
\omega^{n-2}
.

Consider the first of these copies of
\omega^{n-2}
. This copy of
\omega^{n-2}
can be written as
\omega\omega^{n-3}
, which is obtained by replacing each element of
\omega
with a copy of
\omega^{n-3}
.

Continuing to break down successively smaller initial segments of
\omega^{n}
in this way
(n-m)
times, we see that
\omega^{n}
begins with
\omega
-many copies of
\omega^{m}
, or
\omega\omega^{m}
. The rest of
\omega^{n}
, which we will call
D
, lies to the right of this initial piece. Clearly
D
is well-ordered, since it is a subset of
\omega^{n}
, and we have
\omega^{n}\cong\omega\omega^{m}+D
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

If
m<n<\omega
, then
\omega^{m}+\omega^{n}\cong\omega^{n}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
,
\omega^{n}\cong\omega\omega^{m}+D
for some well-ordered linear order
D
. Then by associativity of linear order addition,

\omega^{m}+\omega^{n}\cong\omega^{m}+(\omega\omega^{m}+D)\cong(\omega^{m}+\omega\omega^{m})+D\cong\omega\omega^{m}+D\cong\omega^{n}.

The second-to-last isomorphism here is because, by re-numbering, we have
\omega^{m}+\omega\omega^{m}\cong\omega\omega^{m}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

If
m<n<\omega
and
a,b\in\omega
with
a,b\neq 0
, then
a\omega^{m}+b\omega^{n}\cong b\omega^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose
m<n<\omega
and
a,b\in\omega
with
a,b\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle a\omega^{m}+b\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{m}+\cdots+\omega^{m})}_{a\textrm{ times}}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{m}+\cdots+\omega^{m})}_{a-1\textrm{ times}}+\omega^{m}+\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{m}+\cdots+\omega^{m})}_{a-1\textrm{ times}}+\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}\quad\textrm{(by Lemma \ref{lower powers of omega coming before higher ones get combined})}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{m}+\cdots+\omega^{m})}_{a-2\textrm{ times}}+\omega^{m}+\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{m}+\cdots+\omega^{m})}_{a-2\textrm{ times}}+\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}\quad\textrm{(by Lemma \ref{lower powers of omega coming before higher ones get combined})}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\cong(\omega^{m})+\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b-1\textrm{ times}}\quad\textrm{(by Lemma \ref{lower powers of omega coming before higher ones get combined})}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(\omega^{n}+\cdots+\omega^{n})}_{b\textrm{ times}}\cong b\omega^{n}.

∎

Note that Lemma
5.5
implies that if
m<n<\omega
and
a,b\in\omega
with
a,b\neq 0
, then
b\omega^{n}
begins with a copy of
a\omega^{m}
; that is,
a\omega^{m}
is an initial segment of
b\omega^{n}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal of finite degree with Cantor normal form
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
. Then for each
k\in\omega
,
k\alpha
is an initial segment of
(k+1)a_{n}\omega^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be as above, and fix
k\in\omega
. Let
T
denote the principal tail of
\alpha
:

T:=a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}.

By applying Lemma
5.5

n
times, we get that
T+a_{n}\omega^{n}\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}
. Using this and the associativity of ordinal addition, we obtain

\displaystyle(k+1)a_{n}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega^{n}}_{k+1\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(T+a_{n}\omega^{n})+\cdots+(T+a_{n}\omega^{n})}_{k\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T)+\cdots+(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T)}_{k\textrm{ times}}+a_{n}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\alpha+\cdots+\alpha}_{k\textrm{ times}}+a_{n}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\cong k\alpha+a_{n}\omega^{n}.

Therefore
k\alpha
is an initial segment of
(k+1)a_{n}\omega^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose
\alpha
is an ordinal of finite degree and
\alpha\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
in Cantor normal form. Then
\omega\alpha\cong\omega\omega^{n}\cong\omega^{n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be as above. Then
\omega\alpha
is also of finite degree, so we can express
\omega\alpha
in Cantor normal form as
\omega\alpha=b_{m}\omega^{m}+\cdots+b_{1}\omega+b_{0}
for some
m
and
b_{m},\ldots,b_{0}\in\omega
. Clearly
m\geq n
(that is, the degree of
\omega\alpha
is at least as big as the degree of
\alpha
). We could not have
m=n
, though: for suppose by way of contradiction that we did. Note that
k\alpha
is isomorphic to an initial segment of
\omega\alpha
for any
k\in\omega
. Also, since
a_{n}\omega^{n}
embeds into
\alpha
,
ka_{n}\omega^{n}
embeds into
k\alpha
for any
k
. Then under the assumption that
m=n
, we would have that for any
k\in\omega
,

ka_{n}\omega^{n}\preceq k(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0})\cong k\alpha\preceq\omega\alpha\cong b_{n}\omega^{n}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{1}\omega+b_{0}.

However, by choosing
k
large enough, we could make
ka_{n}>b_{n}
, so that we
ka_{n}\omega^{n}
could not embed into
b_{n}\omega^{n}
; there would be at least one copy of
\omega^{n}
left over. Also, note that no copy of
\omega^{n}
could embed into the tail
b_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{1}\omega+b_{0}
of
\omega\alpha
. Thus we have a contradiction, and therefore
m\geq n+1
.

Next we claim that we can embed
\omega\alpha
into
\omega(a_{n}\omega^{n})
.
\omega\alpha
is formed by replacing each
j\in\omega
with a copy of
\alpha
, and
\omega(a_{n}\omega^{n})
is formed by replacing each
j\in\omega
with a copy of
a_{n}\omega^{n}
. By Lemma
5.6
, we can embed the first copy of
\alpha
into the first two copies of
a_{n}\omega^{n}
; and we can embed the second copy of
\alpha
into the third and fourth copies of
a_{n}\omega^{n}
; etc.

Thus
\omega\alpha
embeds into
\omega(a_{n}\omega^{n})
. Noting that
\omega(a_{n}\omega^{n})\cong\omega\omega^{n}
(just by re-numbering), we have that
\omega\alpha
embeds into
\omega\omega^{n}\cong\omega^{n+1}
. We know from the above that
\omega\alpha
does not embed into any ordinal of degree less than
n+1
. Therefore, since
\omega^{n+1}
is the least ordinal of degree
n+1
, we have that
\omega^{n+1}
is the Cantor normal form of
\omega\alpha
. Thus
\omega\alpha\cong\omega\omega^{n}\cong\omega^{n+1}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal of finite degree with Cantor normal form
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
. Then
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\alpha)=\omega^{n}
; that is,
\phi^{F}_{l}(\omega)(\alpha)=\omega^{\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
is an ordinal of degree
n
in Cantor normal form, for some
n\in\omega
. By Lemma
5.7
,
\omega\alpha\cong\omega^{n+1}
. Then by Lemma
5.2
,

∎

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose

\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})=a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}

Proof.

Let

,

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{a_{n}\omega^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.3214pt}{$\textstyle{a_{n}\omega^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{a_{n}\omega^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{a_{n}\omega^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{3.12001pt}{$\scriptstyle{a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.33333pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}}+\cdots+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.32pt}{$\displaystyle{a_{2}\omega^{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{4.07pt}{$\textstyle{a_{2}\omega^{2}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox By associativity of linear order multiplication, this sum is isomorphic to

– and this sum, by Lemma
3.3
, is isomorphic to

a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{2}\omega+(a_{1}+1)

if
a_{0}\neq 0
, and

a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{2}\omega+a_{1}

if
a_{0}=0
.
∎

(The condition

in Proposition
5.11
is necessary because if
\alpha
is of degree
0
, say
\alpha=k
for some
k\in\omega
, then we just have
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)=c_{\alpha}
.)

Thus the map
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
acts on the Cantor normal form of an ordinal of finite degree by dropping the exponent
i
on all terms of the form
a_{i}\omega^{i}
to
i-1
, except possibly for a last nonzero constant term, which goes to
1
; and the result is another ordinal in Cantor normal form. If
\alpha
is an ordinal of finite degree
n\geq 1
, then
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)
has degree
n-1
. In this respect, the map
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
acts on the ordinals of finite degree similarly to how the familiar derivative operator
\frac{d}{dx}
acts on a polynomial function
p:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

The map
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
has another connection to a different sort of derivative: recall that for
(X,\tau)
a topological space and
A\subseteq X
, the derived set
A^{\mathrm{d}}
of
A
consists of all of the accumulation points of
A
. (
x
is an accumulation point of
A
if
x\in\overline{A\setminus\{x\}}
.) If
X=\alpha
is an ordinal of finite degree equipped with the order topology, then
X^{\mathrm{d}}
, with the inherited order from
\alpha
, is order-isomorphic to
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)(\alpha)\cong\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}(\alpha)
.

Motivated by these similarities, in the next section we examine further the properties of the endomorphism
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
considered as a derivative operator.

6.
Finite condensation derivatives

Note that the map
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
is not quite the same as the finite condensation map
\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}
; whereas
\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathit{F}}
maps a linear order to a subset of itself,
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
is a class map on the ordinals
\mathbf{ON}
. Again in this section we focus on ordinals of finite degree. More precisely, we focus on the Cantor normal forms of such ordinals, which we consider as the formal sums
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}\omega^{i}
for
n\in\omega
and
a_{n}\neq 0
. We will denote by
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
the set of all Cantor normal forms of ordinals of finite degree, considered as formal sums. (We remark that
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
could also be identified with a subset of
{}^{<\omega}\omega
, the
\omega
-branching tree of height
\omega
; for example, the ordinal
\alpha
whose Cantor normal form is
a_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
could be encoded as the finite sequence
\langle n,a_{n},a_{n-1},\ldots,a_{1},a_{0}\rangle
.) Also, for the remainder of this section, we will use the notation
\partial_{F}
for the map
\phi^{F}_{l}(1)
; so, in this notation,

\partial_{F}(\alpha)=\operatorname{o.\!t.}(1\cdot_{\textrm{F}}\alpha)=\operatorname{o.\!t.}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\displaystyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\textstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\alpha}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim_{\textrm{F}}}$}}})

for
\alpha
an ordinal.

We can now restate Proposition
5.11
as follows:

Proposition 6.1
.

For all
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
with
n>0
,

\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}\omega^{n-i}\right)\cong\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{n-i}\partial_{F}(\omega^{n-i})\right]+c_{\alpha}\cong\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{n-i}\omega^{n-i-1}\right]+c_{\alpha}

where
c_{\alpha}=0
if
a_{0}=0
, and
c_{0}=1
if
a_{0}\neq 0
. ∎

We see from Proposition
6.1
that
\partial_{F}
acts almost like a polynomial derivative when applied to a sum that is the Cantor normal form of a single ordinal
\alpha\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
.

Denote by
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
the inverse image of
\alpha
under
\partial_{F}
:

\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha):=\{\beta\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}:\partial_{F}(\beta)=\alpha\}.

Suppose
\alpha\neq 0
is of degree
0
; so
\alpha=k
for some
k\in\omega,k\neq 0
. Then by Proposition
6.1
,
\partial_{F}(k\omega)=k
, so
k\omega\in\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
. If
k=1
, then
\partial_{F}(j)=1=\alpha
for all non-zero
j\in\omega
. If
k>1
, then
(k-1)\omega+j\in\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
for every
j\in\omega
with
j>0
, since
\partial_{F}((k-1)\omega+j)=k-1+1=k=\alpha
.

More generally, we can use Proposition
6.1
to describe
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
. Suppose
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
has degree at least
1
. Then
\beta:=a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+a_{0}\omega\in\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
. If
a_{0}=1
, then
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
also includes any
\beta
of the form
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+j
for
j\in\omega,\,j>0
. If
a_{0}>1
, then
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
also includes any
\beta
of the form
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+(a_{0}-1)\omega+j
for
j\in\omega,\,j>0
. Thus we have the following description of
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}
is a nonzero ordinal of finite degree.

(1)

If
a_{0}=0
, then
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
consists of the single ordinal
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}
.

(2)

If
a_{0}=1
, then
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
consists of
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+\omega
along with all ordinals of the form
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+j
for
j\in\omega,\,j>0
.

(3)

If
a_{0}>1
, then
\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)
consists of
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+a_{0}\omega
along with all ordinals of the form
a_{n}\omega^{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega^{2}+(a_{0}-1)\omega+j
for
j\in\omega,\,j>0
.

∎

Proposition
6.2
implies that if
\alpha
has no nonzero constant term (that is,
\alpha
is not a successor ordinal), then
|\partial_{F}^{\star}(\alpha)|=1
. Thus we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3
.

When restricted to the set of limit ordinals of finite degree,
\partial_{F}^{\star}
defines a function; moreover,
\partial_{F}^{\star}
is the inverse of
\partial_{F}
. ∎

We emphasize that for any
\alpha
of finite degree, we can consider the Cantor normal form of
\alpha
as a formal sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}\omega^{n-i}
. We define a map
\Phi
by letting
\Phi(\alpha)
be the Cantor normal form of
\alpha
. In particular,
\Phi
maps the set of ordinals of finite degree to
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
; that is, as a class map,
\Phi:\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\}\to\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
is the map sending
\alpha
to its Cantor normal form. Conversely, given a (formal) sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}\omega^{n-i}
, we can define the inverse
\Phi^{-1}:\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}\to\mathbf{ON}
as the unique ordinal whose Cantor normal form is
\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}\omega^{n-i}
. The map
\partial_{F}
is a map from
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
to
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
, as it (and its dual map
\partial_{F}^{\star}
, defined above), when applied to an ordinal in Cantor normal form, has as its output an ordinal in Cantor normal form.

Thus we have the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{F}^{\dagger}}

Here, the derivative
\partial_{F}^{\dagger}
mapping
\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\}
to
\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\}
is an induced derivative, arising from our definition of the operator
\partial_{F}
defined on
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
. Next, consider iterating
\partial_{F}^{\dagger}
. We have

\displaystyle\partial_{F}^{\dagger}\circ\partial_{F}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\partial_{F}^{\dagger}\circ(\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}\circ\Phi)

\displaystyle=(\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}\circ\Phi)\circ(\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}\circ\Phi)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}\circ\rm{Id}\circ\partial_{F}\circ\Phi

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}^{2}\circ\Phi.

Similarly, for
n<\omega
, we will have
(\partial_{F}^{\dagger})^{n}=\Phi^{-1}\circ\partial_{F}^{n}\circ\Phi
.
More generally, suppose we have defined an endomorphism
f\in\operatorname{End}(\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF})
. We can then define
\operatorname{Ad}_{\Phi}(f)\in\operatorname{End}(\{\alpha\in\mathbf{ON}:\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)<\omega\})
by
\operatorname{Ad}_{\Phi}=\Phi^{-1}\circ f\circ\Phi
.

We conclude by examining the extent to which the finite condensation derivative
\partial_{F}
acts like a linear map. In general, for
\alpha,\beta\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
and
p,q\in\omega
, we do not have
\partial_{F}(\Phi(p\alpha+q\beta))\cong p\partial_{F}(\alpha)+q\partial_{F}(\beta)
; however, we show that these differ only by a constant, and they are isomorphic in the case when
\alpha
and
\beta
are limit ordinals.

Remark 6.4
.

We have only defined
\partial_{F}
on Cantor normal forms, and the expression
\alpha+\beta=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}+b^{m}+\cdots+b_{0}
need not be in Cantor normal form. For ease of notation, we will write “
\partial_{F}(p\alpha)
” and “
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)
” for
\partial_{F}(\Phi(p\alpha))
and
\partial_{F}(\Phi(\alpha+\beta))
respectively; it will be understood that the argument of
\partial_{F}
in such expressions is put into Cantor normal form before the finite condensation derivative is taken. For similar reasons, we will write “
\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)
” to mean
\Phi(\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)).

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}
be an ordinal of finite degree, and let
p\in\omega
with
p\geq 1
. Then
p\alpha\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n}+\alpha\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}
.

Proof.

If
p=1
, then for any
\alpha
,
p\alpha=\alpha=0+\alpha=(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n}+\alpha
.

Suppose
p\geq 2
and
n=0
. If
\alpha=0
, then
p\alpha\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n}+\alpha=0
. If
\alpha=a_{0}\neq 0
, then
p\alpha=pa_{0}=(p-1)a_{0}+a_{0}=(p-a)a_{0}+\alpha
.

Now suppose
p\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
. Denote by
T_{\alpha}
the principal tail of
\alpha
; that is,
T_{\alpha}=a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}
. Note that each term in
T_{\alpha}
has degree less than
n
, so that
T_{\alpha}+a_{n}\omega^{n}\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}
by Lemma
5.5
. Then

\displaystyle p\alpha

\displaystyle\cong p(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T_{\alpha})\cong\underbrace{(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T_{\alpha})+\cdots+(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T_{\alpha})}_{p\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(T_{\alpha}+a_{n}\omega^{n})+\cdots+(T_{\alpha}+a_{n}\omega^{n})}_{p-1\textrm{ times}}+T_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}+\underbrace{(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega^{n})}_{p-1\textrm{ times}}+T_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega^{n})}_{p-1\textrm{ times}}+(a_{n}\omega^{n}+T_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n}+\alpha,

and
(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n}+\alpha\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n}+T_{\alpha}\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}
.
∎

From Lemma
6.5
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}
be an ordinal of finite degree
n\geq 1
, and let
p\in\omega
with
p>0
.

(1)

If
\alpha
has degree
n\geq 2
, then
\partial_{F}(p\alpha)\cong p\partial_{F}(\alpha)\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)
.

(2)

If
\alpha
has degree
1
, then
\partial_{F}(p\alpha)\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)
, and
p\partial_{F}(\alpha)
differs from
(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)
by at most a constant.

Proof.

First suppose
\alpha
has degree
n\geq 2
. By Proposition
6.1
,
\partial_{F}(\alpha)=a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{2}\omega+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}
(where
c_{\alpha}=0
if
a_{0}=0
and
c_{\alpha}=1
if
a_{0}>0
). Then, letting
T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)}
denote the principal tail
a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}
of
\alpha
, we have that every term in
T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)}
has degree strictly smaller than the degree of
\partial_{F}(\alpha)
, which is
n-1
. Thus

\displaystyle p\partial_{F}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\cong p(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)})

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)})+\cdots+(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)})}_{p\textrm{ times}}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega^{n-1}}_{p\textrm{ times}}+T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)}\quad\textrm{(by Lemma \ref{finitely many lower powers of omega coming before higher ones get combined})}

\displaystyle\cong\underbrace{a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega^{n-1}}_{p-1\textrm{ times}}+(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+T_{\partial_{F}(\alpha)})

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha).

Also,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(pa_{n}\omega^{n}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0})\quad\textrm{(by Lemma \ref{scalar times alpha formula})}

\displaystyle\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}\quad\textrm{(by Proposition \ref{bsd is almost a linear operator on individual alpha})}

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha).

Next, suppose
\alpha
has degree
1
; say
\alpha=a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
for some
a_{1},a_{0}\in\omega
with
a_{1}\neq 0
. Then
\partial_{F}(\alpha)=a_{1}+c_{\alpha}
where
c_{\alpha}=0
if
a_{0}=0
and
c_{\alpha}=1
if
a_{0}>1
. Then
p\partial_{F}(\alpha)\cong p(a_{1}+c_{\alpha})\cong pa_{1}+pc_{\alpha}
, but
(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)\cong(p-1)a_{1}+(a_{1}+c_{\alpha})\cong pa_{1}+c_{\alpha}
. This means that in this case
p\partial_{F}(\alpha)
is not isomorphic to
(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)
unless the constant term
a_{0}
is zero. However, since
a_{0}+a_{1}\omega\cong a_{1}\omega
,

\partial_{F}(p\alpha)\cong\partial_{F}(p(a_{1}\omega+a_{0}))\cong\partial_{F}(pa_{1}\omega+a_{0})\cong pa_{1}+c_{\alpha},

so that
\partial_{F}(p\alpha)
is isomorphic to
(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)
in this case.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be nonzero ordinals of finite degree in Cantor normal form. Then we have
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)
in the following cases:

(1)

\alpha
is a limit ordinal; or

(2)

\alpha
is a successor ordinal and
\operatorname{deg}\beta\geq 2
.

In all other cases, we have
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)+1\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta).

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
be nonzero. If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, then
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)
by Lemma
5.9
.

Suppose
\alpha
is a successor ordinal; say
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}
where
a_{0}>0
. We consider cases depending on
\operatorname{deg}(\alpha)
and
\operatorname{deg}(\beta)
. The first three cases are for when
\deg(\beta)
is at least
2
.

Case 1:

Suppose
\deg(\beta)\geq 2
and
\deg(\alpha)<\deg(\beta)
. Since
\beta
has degree at least
2
, we have
\deg(\partial_{F}(\alpha))<\deg(\deg(\beta))
and
\deg(\partial_{F}(\beta))\geq 1
. Then by Lemma
5.4
,

(1)

\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta).

Case 2:

Suppose
2\leq\deg(\alpha)=\deg(\beta)
. Then by Lemma
5.4
,

(2)

\begin{split}\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)&\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}+b_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+b_{0})\\
&\cong\partial_{F}((a_{n}+b_{n})\omega^{n}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{0})\\
&\cong(a_{n}+b_{n})\omega^{n-1}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta},\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha})+(b_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta})

\displaystyle=(a_{n}+b_{n})\omega^{n-1}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta}

as well.

Case 3:

Suppose
2\leq\deg(\beta)<\deg(\alpha)
. Then by Lemma
5.4
,

(3)

\begin{split}\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)&\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{m}\omega^{m}+\cdots+a_{0}+b_{m}\omega^{m}+\cdots+b_{0})\\
&\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+(a_{m}+b_{m})\omega^{m}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+b_{0})\\
&\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+(a_{m}+b_{m})\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta},\end{split}

and also

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}+b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta}

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+(a_{m}+b_{m})\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}+c_{\beta}

as well.

Thus in all cases where
\alpha
is a successor ordinal and
\deg(\beta)\geq 2
, we have
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)
.

The next two cases are for when
\alpha
is a successor ordinal of degree at least
2
and
\deg(\beta)\leq 1
.

Case 4:

Suppose that
\alpha
is a successor ordinal of degree at least
2
and
\deg(\beta)=1
; say
\beta=b_{1}\omega+b_{0}
for some
b_{1}>0
. Then by Lemma
5.4
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}+b_{1}\omega+b_{0})

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+(a_{1}+b_{1})\omega+b_{0})

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+(a_{1}+b_{1})+c_{\beta},

but

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0})+\partial_{F}(b_{1}\omega+b_{0})

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+1+b_{1}+c_{\beta}\quad\textrm{since ($a_{0}>0$)}.

Case 5:

Suppose that
\alpha
is a successor ordinal of degree at least
2
and
\deg(\beta)=0
; say
\beta=b_{0}
for some
b_{0}>0
. Then since
a_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+(a_{0}+b_{0}))

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+1,

but

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+1)+1.

Finally, the last four cases are for when
\alpha
is a successor ordinal and both
\alpha
and
\beta
have degree less than
2
.

Case 6:

If both
\alpha
and
\beta
have degree
0
, say
\alpha=a_{0}>0
and
\beta=b_{0}>0
, then
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong 1
but
\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)\cong 2.

Case 7:

If
\alpha=a_{0}>0
and
\beta
has degree
1
, say
\alpha=a_{0}>0
and
\beta=b_{1}\omega+b_{0}
for some
\beta_{1}>0
, then

\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{0}+b_{1}\omega+b_{0})\cong\partial_{F}(b_{1}\omega+b_{0})\cong b_{1}+c_{\beta},

but

\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{0})+\partial_{F}(b_{1}\omega+b_{0})\cong 1+b_{1}+c_{\beta}.

Case 8:

If
\alpha
is a successor ordinal of degree
1
and
\deg(\beta)=0
, say
\alpha=a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
for some
a_{0},a_{1}>0
and
\beta=b_{0}
for some
b_{0}>0
, then

\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{1}\omega+a_{0}+b_{0})\cong a_{1}+1,

but

\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{1}\omega+a_{0})+\partial_{F}(b_{0})\cong a_{1}+1+1.

Case 9:

Finally, if
\alpha
is a successor ordinal and both
\alpha
and
\beta
have degree
1
, say
\alpha=a_{1}\omega+a_{0}
for some
a_{0},a_{1}>0
and
\beta=b_{1}\omega+b_{0}
for some
b_{1}>0
, then

\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{1}\omega+a_{0}+b_{1}\omega+b_{0})\cong\partial_{F}((a_{1}+b_{1})\omega+b_{0})\cong a_{1}+b_{1}+c_{\beta},

but

\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)\cong\partial_{F}(a_{1}\omega+a_{0})+\partial_{F}(b_{1}\omega+b_{0})\cong a_{1}+1+b_{1}+c_{\beta}.

∎

If
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
are limit ordinals, then by applying Proposition
6.7
twice, we have
\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha+\beta)+\partial_{F}(\gamma)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\partial_{F}(\beta)+\partial_{F}(\gamma)
. More generally, we obtain the following corollary, arguing by induction:

Corollary 6.8
.

Suppose, for
1\leq i\leq t
, that
\alpha_{i}
is a limit ordinal of finite degree in Cantor normal form. Then

\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}\right)\cong\sum_{i=1}^{t}\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i}).

∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\alpha=a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega
and
\beta=b_{m}\omega^{m}+\cdots+b_{1}\omega
be limit ordinals of finite degree at least
2
in Cantor normal form, and let
p,q\in\omega
with
p,q>0
. Then
\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)=p\partial_{F}(\alpha)+q\partial_{F}(\beta)
.

Moreover, we have the following expressions for
\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)
depending on the relationship between
\deg(\alpha)
and
\deg(\beta)
:

(1)

If
\deg(\alpha)<\deg(\beta)
, then

\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)\cong qb_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{2}\omega+b_{1};

(2)

If
\deg(\alpha)=\deg(\beta)
, then

\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)\cong(pa_{n}+qb_{n})\omega^{n-1}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{2}\omega+b_{1};

(3)

If
\deg(\alpha)>\deg(\beta)
, then

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)

\displaystyle\quad\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{m+1}\omega^{m}+(a_{m}+qb_{m})\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta,p
, and
q
be as above. Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are limit ordinals, so are
p\alpha
and
q\beta
. Then by Corollary
6.8
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(p\alpha)+\partial_{F}(q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha\right)+\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta\right)

\displaystyle\cong\sum_{i=1}^{p}\partial_{F}(\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong p\partial_{F}(\alpha)+q\partial_{F}(\beta).

Suppose
\deg(\alpha)<\deg(\beta)
. Then by Lemma
5.5
and Corollary
6.6
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(q\beta)\cong(q-1)b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(q-1)b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{2}\omega+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong qb_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{2}\omega+b_{1}.

Suppose
\deg(\alpha)=\deg(\beta)
. Then by Lemma
5.5
and Corollary
6.6
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(p\alpha)+\partial_{F}(q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)+(q-1)b_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(q-1)b_{n}\omega^{n-1}+b_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+qb_{n}\omega^{n-1}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong(pa_{n}+qb_{n})\omega^{n-1}+b_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{1}.

Finally, suppose
\deg(\alpha)>\deg(\beta)
. Then again by Lemma
5.5
and Corollary
6.6
,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(p\alpha+q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(p\alpha)+\partial_{F}(q\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha)+(q-1)b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\partial_{F}(\beta)

\displaystyle\cong(p-1)a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(q-1)b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{m}\omega^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+qb_{m}\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{m}\omega^{m-1}+qb_{m}\omega^{m-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}

\displaystyle\cong pa_{n}\omega^{n-1}+a_{n-1}\omega^{n-2}+\cdots+(a_{m}+qb_{m})\omega^{m-1}+b_{m-1}\omega^{m-2}+\cdots+b_{1}.

∎

More generally, we obtain the following expression for the finite condensation derivative of expressions of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\alpha_{i}
, with some assumptions on the ordinals
\alpha_{i}
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Suppose, for some
s\in\omega,s\geq 2
that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}
are limit ordinals of finite degree in Cantor normal form, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}
are nonzero natural numbers. Suppose also that for each
1\leq i\leq s
,
\alpha_{i}
has leading term
a_{n_{i}}\omega^{n_{i}}
. Then

(4)

\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\alpha_{i}\right)\cong\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left((p_{i}-1)a_{n_{i}}\omega^{\deg(a_{i})-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i})\right).

If, in addition, each
\alpha_{i}
is of degree at least
2
, then

(5)

\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\alpha_{i}\right)\cong\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left((p_{i}-1)a_{n_{i}}\omega^{\deg(a_{i})-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i})\right)\cong\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i}).

Proof.

If each
\alpha_{i}
is a limit ordinal, then each
p_{i}\alpha_{i}
is also a limit ordinal, so we have by Corollary
6.8
and the first part of Corollary
6.6
that

(6)

\partial_{F}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\alpha_{i}\right)\cong\sum_{i=1}^{s}\partial_{F}(p_{i}\alpha_{i})\cong\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left((p_{i}-1)a_{n_{i}}\omega^{\deg(a_{i})-1}+\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i})\right).

If we also assume that each
\alpha
is of degree at least
2
, then we have by the second part of Corollary
6.6
that both of these expressions are isomorphic to
\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i})
.
∎

Note that Proposition
6.10
shows only a quasi-linear behavior of
\partial_{F}
on
\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
: first, we require additional properties of the ordinals
\alpha_{i}
; and second,
\partial_{F}(\alpha_{i})
need not have those properties. (For example,
\omega^{2}+\omega
is a limit ordinal of degree
2
, but
\partial_{F}(\omega^{2}+\omega)\cong\omega+1
is a successor ordinal of degree
1
.)

One could also consider right multiplication by natural number scalars modulo the finite condensation, but the next proposition shows that such multiplication has the same effect as the finite condensation derivative
\partial_{F}
.

Proposition 6.11
.

For all
\alpha\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
and
p\in\omega
with
p>0
,
\partial_{F}(\alpha p)\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\cong a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}\in\omega[\omega]^{\omega}_{CNF}
, and let
p\in\omega
with
p>0
. Observe that
\omega p\cong\omega
(as
\omega p
is formed by replacing each element of
\omega
with a copy of
p
); and, in general, if
i>0
, then
\omega^{i}p\cong\omega^{i}
. Then by right distributivity,

\displaystyle\partial_{F}(\alpha p)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}((a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0})p)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}p+\cdots+a_{1}\omega p+a_{0}p)

\displaystyle\cong\partial_{F}(a_{n}\omega^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\omega+a_{0}p)

\displaystyle\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+c

where
c=0
if
a_{0}p=0
, and
c=1
if
a_{0}p>0
; that is, since
p>0
,
c=0
if
a_{0}=0
, and
c=1
if
a_{0}>0
. Therefore

\partial_{F}(\alpha p)\cong a_{n}\omega^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}+c_{\alpha}\cong\partial_{F}(\alpha).

∎

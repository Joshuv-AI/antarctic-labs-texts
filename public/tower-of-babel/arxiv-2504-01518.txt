On 2-color partitions where one of the colors is multiples of 7 k 7^{k}
D. S. Gireesh 1, Shivashankar C. 2, HemanthKumar B. 3

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the arithmetic properties of p 1 , 7 k ​ ( n ) p_{1,7^{k}}(n) , which counts 2-color partitions of n n where one of the colors appears only in parts that are multiples of 7 k 7^{k} . By constructing generating functions for p 1 , 7 k ​ ( n ) p_{1,7^{k}}(n) across specific arithmetic progressions, we establish a set of Ramanujan-type infinite family of congruences modulo powers of 7 7 .

1.
Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers whose sum equals
n
. Let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
, with the generating function given by

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{f_{1}}.

Throughout this paper, we define

f_{r}:=(q^{r};q^{r})_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{rm}).

Ramanujan
[
8
]
conjectured that if
\ell\in\{5,7,11\}
and
0<\delta_{\ell,\beta}<\ell^{\beta}
satisfying
24\delta_{\ell,\beta}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{\beta}}
, the partition function satisfies the congruence

(1.1)

p\left(\ell^{\beta}n+\delta_{\ell,\beta}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{\beta}}.

Watson
[
11
]
later proved this conjecture for
\ell=5
. However, Chowla
[
4
]
observed that since
p(243)
is not divisible by
7^{3}
, this contradicts the conjecture for
\ell=7
and
\beta=3
. The conjecture was subsequently refined and proved by Watson
[
11
]
, establishing that for
k\geq 1
,

(1.2)

p(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k}},

and

(1.3)

p(7^{2k}n+\lambda_{2k})\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k+1}},

where
\lambda_{k}
is the modular inverse of
24
modulo
7^{k}
.

Using the classical identities of Euler and Jacobi, Hirschhorn and Hunt
[
5
]
provided a simple proof of (
1.1
) for
\ell=5

[
6
]
extended these results to
\ell=7

(1.4)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p\left(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j-1}}{f_{1}^{4j}}

and

(1.5)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p\left(7^{2k}n+\lambda_{2k}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j}}{f_{1}^{4j+1}},

where the coefficient vectors
\mathbf{x}_{k}=(x_{k,1},x_{k,2},\dots)
are defined recursively by

\mathbf{x}_{1}=\left(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\dots\right)=\left(7,7^{2},0,\dots\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{k,j}\,m_{4j+1,j+i},\,\,\text{if}\,\,k\,\,\text{is even},\end{cases}

Let
p_{1,\ell}(n)
be the number of 2-color partitions of
n
where one of the colors appears only in parts that are multiples of
\ell
; its generating function is given by

(1.7)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,\ell}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{f_{1}f_{\ell}}.

Ahmed, Baruah, and Dastidar
[
1
]
discovered new congruences modulo
5
, proving that for certain values of
t

(1.8)

p_{1,\ell}\left(25n+t\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5},

where
\ell\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5,10,15,20\}
and
\ell+t=24
. They further conjectured similar congruences for
\ell=7,8,17
which were confirmed by Chern
[
3
]
. Additionally, Chern
[
3
]
established a new family of congruences:

p_{1,4}\left(49n+t\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7},\text{ for }t\in\{11,25,32,39\}.

Further generalizations were made by Wang
[
10
]
, who derived congruences for
p_{1,5}(n)
, strengthening prior results. Ranganath
[
9
]
extended these findings to
p_{1,25}(n)
and
p_{1,49}(n)
, proving that for all
n,\beta\geq 0
,

(1.9)

p_{1,25}\left(5^{2\beta+1}n+\frac{7\times 5^{2\beta+1}+13}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+1}},

(1.10)

p_{1,25}\left(5^{2\beta+2}n+\frac{11\times 5^{2\beta+2}+13}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5^{\beta+2}},

(1.11)

p_{1,49}\left(7^{2\beta+1}n+\frac{5\times 7^{2\beta+1}+25}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{\beta+1}},

and

(1.12)

p_{1,49}\left(7^{2\beta+2}n+\frac{11\times 7^{2\beta+2}+25}{12}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{\beta+2}}.

In this paper, we establish congruences for
p_{1,7^{k}}
for all positive integers
k
. The results presented here are generalized and extend those of Ranganath
[
9
]
.

The main results are as follow:

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
n,\beta\geq 0
, and
k\geq 1
, we have

(1.13)

p_{1,7^{2k-1}}\left(7^{2k+\beta-1}n+\frac{16\cdot 7^{2k+\beta-1}+7^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k}},

(1.14)

p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta-1}n+\frac{10\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta-1}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k+\beta}},

(1.15)

p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k+\beta+1}},

(1.16)

p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta+1}n+\frac{(24r+22)\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{7^{k+\beta+2}},

where
r\in\{3,4,6\}.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some lemmas which play a vital role in proving our results.
Let
H
be the “huffing” operator modulo
7
, that is,

H\left(\sum{p_{n}q^{n}}\right)=\sum{p_{1,7n}q^{7n}}.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 3.1 and 3.4)
.

If
\xi=\frac{f_{1}}{q^{2}f_{49}}
and
T=\frac{f_{7}^{4}}{q^{7}f_{49}^{4}}
, then

(2.1)

H\left(\xi^{-i}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{i,j}T^{-j}

and

(2.2)

H\left(\xi^{-4i}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i,i+j}T^{-i-j}.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
i\geq 1
, we have

(2.3)

H\left(q^{i+2}\frac{f_{7}^{4i}}{f_{1}^{4i+1}}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i+1,i+j}q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-1}}{f_{7}^{4j}},

(2.4)

H\left(q^{i+4}\frac{f_{7}^{4i}}{f_{1}^{4i+2}}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i+2,i+j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-2}}{f_{7}^{4j}},

and

(2.5)

H\left(q^{i}\frac{f_{7}^{4i}}{f_{1}^{4i}}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i,i+j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j}}.

Proof.

We can rewrite (
2.1
) as

(2.6)

H\left(q^{2i}\frac{f_{49}^{i}}{f_{1}^{i}}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{i,j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j}}.

From (
2.6
) and the fact that
m_{4i+1,j}=0
for
1\leq j\leq i,
we have

\displaystyle H\left(\left(q^{2}\frac{f_{49}}{f_{1}}\right)^{4i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=i+1}^{4i+1}m_{4i+1,j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j}}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i+1,i+j}\,q^{7i+7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4i+4j}}{f_{7}^{4i+4j}},

which yields (
2.3
). Similarly, we can prove (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
∎

3.
Generating functions

In this section, we establish generating functions for
p_{1,7^{\ell}}(n)
within specific arithmetic progressions.

Theorem 3.1
.

For each
\beta\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
, we have

(3.1)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}\left(7^{2k+\beta-1}n+\frac{16\cdot 7^{2k+\beta-1}+7^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,q^{i-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4i-1}}{f_{1}^{4i+1}},

where the coefficient vectors are defined as follows:

y_{1,j}^{(2k-1)}=x_{2k-1,j}\text{ and }y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,j}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta,i}m_{4i+1,j+i}

for all
\beta,j\geq 1
.

Proof.

From (
1.7
), we have

(3.2)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{7^{2k-1}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}.

Extracting the terms involving
q^{7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1}}
on both sides of (
3.2
) and dividing throughout by
q^{\lambda_{2k-1}}
, we obtain

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{7^{2k-1}n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{7^{2k-1}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{7^{2k-1}n}.

If we replace
q^{7^{2k-1}}
by
q
and use (
1.4
), we get

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j-1}}{f_{1}^{4j+1}},

which is the case
\beta=0
of (
3.1
).

We now assume that (
3.1
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operator
H
to both sides, by (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}\left(7^{2k+\beta}n+\frac{16\cdot 7^{2k+\beta}+7^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{7n+7}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,H\left(q^{i+2}\frac{f_{7}^{4i}}{f_{1}^{4i+1}}\right)\times\frac{1}{f_{7}}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\sum_{j=1}^{4i+1}m_{4i+1,i+j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-1}}{f_{7}^{4j+1}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\,m_{4i+1,i+j}\right)q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-1}}{f_{7}^{4j+1}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+2,j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-1}}{f_{7}^{4j+1}}.\end{split}

That is,

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k-1}}\left(7^{2k+\beta}n+\frac{16\cdot 7^{2k+\beta}+7^{2k-1}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+2,i}\,q^{i-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4i-1}}{f_{1}^{4i+1}}.

So we obtain (
3.1
) with
\beta
replaced by
\beta+1
.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
\beta\geq 0
, and
k\geq 1
, we have

(3.3)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta-1}n+\frac{10\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta-1}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\,q^{i-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4i-2}}{f_{1}^{4i}},

and

(3.4)

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,j}q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j}}{f_{1}^{4j+2}},

where coefficient vectors are defined as follow:

y_{1,j}^{(2k)}=x_{2k-1,j}

and

y^{(2k)}_{\beta+1,j}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{\beta,i}m_{4i,j+i}\,\,\ &\text{if $\beta$ is odd},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{\beta,i}m_{4i+2,j+i}\,\,\ &\text{if $\beta$ is even},\end{cases}

for all
\beta,j\geq 1

Proof.

Equation (
1.7
) can be written as

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{7^{2k}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}.

Extracting the terms involving
q^{7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1}}
on both sides of the above equation and dividing throughout by
q^{\lambda_{2k-1}}
, we obtain

(3.5)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{7^{2k-1}n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{7^{2k}}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{7^{2k-1}n}.

If we replace
q^{7^{2k-1}}
by
q
and use (
1.4
), we get

(3.6)

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}(7^{2k-1}n+\lambda_{2k-1})q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}x_{2k-1,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j-2}}{f_{1}^{4j}},

which is the case
\beta=0
of (
3.3
).

We now assume that (
3.3
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operator
H
to both sides, by (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{7n+7}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\,H\left(q^{i}\frac{f_{7}^{4i}}{f_{1}^{4i}}\right)\times\frac{1}{f_{7}^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i,i+j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j+2}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,i}\,m_{4i,i+j}\right)q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j+2}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j}}{f_{7}^{4j+2}},\end{split}

That is

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta}n+\frac{22\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,j}q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j}}{f_{1}^{4j+2}}.

Hence, if (
3.3
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
, then (
3.4
) is true for
\beta
.

Suppose that (
3.4
) is true for some integer
\beta\geq 0
. Applying the operating
H
to both sides, by (
2.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta+1}n+\frac{10\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta+1}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{7n+7}&=\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,i}\sum_{j\geq 1}m_{4i+2,i+j}q^{7j}\,\frac{f_{49}^{4j-2}}{f_{7}^{4j}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,i}\,m_{4i+2,i+j}\right)q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-2}}{f_{7}^{4j}}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+3,j}\,q^{7j}\frac{f_{49}^{4j-2}}{f_{7}^{4j}},\end{split}

which yields

\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{1,7^{2k}}\left(7^{2k+2\beta+1}n+\frac{10\cdot 7^{2k+2\beta+1}+7^{2k}+1}{24}\right)q^{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+3,j}\,q^{j-1}\frac{f_{7}^{4j-2}}{f_{1}^{4j}}.

This is (
3.3
) with
\beta
replaced by
\beta+1
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Congruences

For a positive integer
n
, let
\pi(n)
be the highest power of
7
that divides
n
, and define
\pi(0)=+\infty
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 5.1)
.

For each
i,j\geq 1
, we have

(4.1)

\pi\left(m_{i,j}\right)\geq\left[\frac{7j-2i-1}{4}\right].

In particular,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\pi\left(m_{4i,i+j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{7(i+j)-2(4i)-1}{4}\right]=\left[\frac{7j-i-1}{4}\right],

(4.3)

\displaystyle\pi\left(m_{4i+1,i+j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{7(i+j)-2(4i+1)-1}{4}\right]=\left[\frac{7j-i-3}{4}\right],

and

(4.4)

\pi\left(m_{4i+2,i+j}\right)\geq\left[\frac{7(i+j)-2(4i+2)-1}{4}\right]=\left[\frac{7j-i-5}{4}\right].

Lemma 4.2

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 5.3)
.

For all
k,j\geq 1
, we have

(4.5)

\pi(x_{1,1})=1,\,\,\,\pi(x_{1,2})=2,\,\,\,\pi\left(x_{2k+1,j}\right)\geq k+1+\left[\frac{7j-4}{4}\right]

and

(4.6)

\pi\left(x_{2k,j}\right)\geq k+1+\left[\frac{7j-6}{4}\right],

where

\delta_{j,1}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $j=1$},\\
0&\text{if $j\neq 1$}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
j,k\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
, we have

(4.7)

\pi\left(y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,j}\right)\geq k+\left[\frac{7j-4}{4}\right].

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.1
, we have

y_{1,j}^{(2k-1)}=x_{2k-1,j}.

From (
4.5
), we can see that

\pi\left(x_{2k-1,j}\right)\geq k+\left[\frac{7j-4}{4}\right],

this shows that (
4.7
) holds when
\beta=0
.

Now suppose (
4.7
) holds for some
\beta\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\pi\left(y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+2,j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{i\geq 1}\left\{\pi\left(y^{(2k-1)}_{\beta+1,i}\right)+\pi\left(m_{4i+1,i+j}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{i\geq 1}\left\{k+\left[\frac{7j-i-3}{4}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\geq k+\left[\frac{7j-4}{4}\right],

which is (
4.7
) with
\beta+1
for
\beta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
j,k\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0

(4.8)

\pi\left(y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+1,j}\right)\geq k+\beta+\left[\frac{7j-6}{4}\right]

and

(4.9)

\pi\left(y^{(2k)}_{2\beta+2,j}\right)\geq k+\beta+1+\left[\frac{7j-6}{4}\right].

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.2
, we have

y_{1,j}^{(2k-1)}=x_{2k-1,j}.

From (

), we can see that

\pi\left(x_{2k-1,j}\right)\geq k+\left[\frac{7j-4}{4}\right]\geq k+\left[\frac{7j-6}{4}\right],

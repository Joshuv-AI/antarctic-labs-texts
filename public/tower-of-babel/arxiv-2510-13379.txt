The weight filtration on real singular homology is motivic
Raphaël Ruimy

Abstract
We give an alternative construction of Totaro’s weight filtration on singular homology of the real points of a real algebraic variety. Our construction shows that this filtration comes from Bondarko’s weight filtration on Voevodsky motives.



Intoduction

Deligne introduced in
[
Del71
,
Del74
]
a canonical filtration on the singular cohomology groups with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients of a complex algebraic variety called the
weight filtration
. He also introduced a weight filtration on the
\ell
-adic cohomology groups in
[

]
. The existence of both filtrations was initially envisioned by Grothendieck as a shadow of a motivic weight filtration. This vision was later justified by several authors. First, Voevodsky constructed in
[
VSF11
, Chapter 5]
the category
\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{gm}}(k,\Lambda)
for
k
a perfect field and
\Lambda
any commutative ring (see
2.2
). Assume that the characteristic exponent
p
of
k
is invertible in
\Lambda
. In
[
Kel13
]
, Kelly constructed

a functor

from the category

of
k
-schemes of finite type. The objects of

are furthermore equipped with a weight filtration using Bondarko’s work

]
. It is then possible to show that Ayoub’s Betti realization

sends the motive

,

]
and Deligne’s weight filtration on

Our goal is to tell the same story in the real world. The existence of a weight filtration on the singular cohomology of the real points of a real algebraic variety with coefficients in the field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with two elements was stated by Totaro in
[
Tot02
]
and was later proved in
[

by McCrory and Parusiński. In the case of Borel-Moore homology, their construction consists of formally extending the trivial filtration on projective smooth

The goal of this note is to give an alternative construction of this weight filtration by using the weight filtration on motives. This will also show that the weight filtration is a realization of the motivic weight filtration.

The first step is to construct the real motivic realization functor

\rho_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{gm}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathbb{F}_{2}).

As singular cohomology of the real points is not an étale-local invariant, the construction is more involved than the constructions in the complex case or for
\ell

\infty

1

. Constructing the realization then amounts to constructing a symmetric monoidal additive functor

where

\mathbb{R}
-schemes with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-coefficients (see

Once the real realization is constructed, we use Bondarko’s theory of weight structures and Aoki’s construction

3
The real realization

The singular chain complex with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-coefficients may be seen as a functor:

C_{*}(-,\mathbb{F}_{2})\colon\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}\to\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}.

As
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a field, letting
\rm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}
be the category of finite dimensional graded
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector spaces, we have an equivalence:

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{H}_{n}\colon\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\sim}\rm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}.

To show that
C_{*}(-,\mathbb{F}_{2})
factor through Voevodsky motives, we must first show that it factors through
\mathrm{Sm}^{\mathrm{cor}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\otimes}
, or equivalently that each
\mathrm{H}_{n}(-(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{F}_{2})
does. Doing this will yield maps

and there is a somehow natural way of defining those maps (see
3.7
below), but it is then not obvious at all (at least to the author of this note) how to show that these maps are compatible with composition. Circumventing this issue will require a small detour through the theory of quadratic forms.

\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{gm}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\otimes}

\mathcal{WC}\colon\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{gm}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{Chow}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2}))^{\otimes}.

By
[
BGV24
, Corollary 3.30]
, the functor
\rho_{\mathbb{R}}
factors through
\mathcal{WC}
which yields a functor

\widetilde{\mathrm{Tot}}\colon\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{Chow}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2}))^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}.

Composing
\widetilde{\mathrm{Tot}}
with the inclusion from Chow motives yields a map
\mathrm{Chow}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{Chow}}}\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}.

Since
\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}
is a symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category as well as an additive (ordinary) category, the identity induces a map

\mathrm{Tot}\colon\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}

in
\mathrm{M}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{st}}
(the total complex functor). By the universal property of
\mathrm{K}^{b}(-)
(the combination of
Propositions

1.7
and
1.14
),
\widetilde{\mathrm{Tot}}
coincides with the composition

\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{Chow}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2}))^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{Chow}})}\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Tot}}\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}^{\otimes}.

The
filtered real realization functor

\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{W}}\colon\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{gm}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{F}_{2})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})^{\otimes}

is the composition
\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{Chow}})\circ\mathcal{WC}
. By construction, its composition with
\mathrm{Tot}
is
\rho_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We can now finally define the weight filtrations on real homology, cohomology, Borel-Moore homology and cohomology with compact support.

Corollary 4.6
.

Set
W\mathrm{H}_{*}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{W}}\circ M
,
W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{W}}\circ M^{\mathrm{BM}}
,
W\mathrm{H}^{*}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{W}}\circ h
and
W\mathrm{H}^{*}_{c}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{W}}\circ h^{c}
. These functors refine homology, Borel-Moore homology, cohomology and cohomology with compact support of the real locus and yield weight spectral sequences that converge to those cohomology theories. Furthermore,

1.

if
p\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is proper birational and
Z\to X
is a closed embedding such that
p^{-1}(X\setminus Z)\to X\setminus Z
is an isomorphism, we have canonical exact triangles:

W\mathrm{H}_{*}(p^{-1}(Z))\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}(X^{\prime})\oplus W\mathrm{H}_{*}(Z)\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}(X).

W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(p^{-1}(Z))\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(X^{\prime})\oplus W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(Z)\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(X)

in
\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
.

2.

if
Z\to X
is a closed immersion, we have a canonical exact triangle

W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(Z)\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(X)\to W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}(U)

in
\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathrm{GrVect}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
.

Finally, our functors
W\mathrm{H}_{*}
and
W\mathrm{H}_{*}^{\mathrm{BM}}
are
\infty
-categorical enhancements of those of
[
MP11
,
MP14
]
.

Proof.

Both properties follow from the corresponding properties of
M
and
M^{\mathrm{BM}}
from
[
VSF11
, Chapter 5, Propositions 4.1.3 & 4.1.5]
. The fact that our construction enhances that of
[
MP11
,
MP14
]
comes from the uniqueness result in
[
MP11
, Theorem 1.1]
for Borel-Moore homology. In the case of a smooth scheme, the uniqueness for ordinary homology is obtained by Poincaré duality at the level of filtered complexes which is
[
MP14
, Theorem 2.4]
. In the general case, it follows from the uniqueness result of
[
MP14
, Theorem 7.1]
.
∎

MiR-199b-5p inhibition improves inflammation and oxidative stress damage in OA-induced non-alcoholic fatty liver disease through targeting SIRT1
Authors: Han Liang, Tao Lin, Yaqin Hu, Xuewen Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesNon-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a chronic liver disease. This research investigates the role of miR-199b-5p in NAFLD development.MethodThis study enrolled 110 non-NAFLD patients and 90 NAFLD patients and collected their clinical data and serum samples. The level of miR-199b-5p was measured via qRT-PCR. Through experiments such as ELISA, biochemical assays, and CCK-8, the effects of miR-199b-5p on OA-induced inflammation, lipid metabolism, viability, and oxidative stress in HepG2 cells were analyzed. Meanwhile, the targeting relationship between miR-199b-5p and SIRT1 was verified through dual-luciferase assay and Western blot analysis.ResultsmiR-199b-5p was highly expressed in the NAFLD group and was significantly correlated with BMI, WC, TG, LDL-C, and HDL-C (P < 0.0001). ROC analysis demonstrated its diagnostic potential for NAFLD (AUC = 0.875, P < 0.0001). The OA-induced HepG2 cell model revealed that the miR-199b-5p inhibitor alleviated OA-induced suppression of cell viability, suppressed inflammation, reduced lipid levels, and alleviated oxidative stress. Furthermore, SIRT1 levels in the NAFLD group were significantly decreased and showed a significant negative correlation with miR-199b-5p (P < 0.0001).ConclusionsmiR-199b-5p is significantly elevated in the serum of NAFLD patients and shows a positive correlation with multiple metabolic indicators. This suggests its potential as a diagnostic biomarker for NAFLD. Additionally, inhibition of miR-199b-5p protects against OA-induced hepatocyte injury, partially through targeting SIRT1, which regulates inflammatory response and oxidative stress.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04533-3.Keywords: Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, MiR-199b-5p, SIRT1, Inflammation

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is showing a continuously increasing trend in its incidence [1, 2]. The primary characteristic of NAFLD is the abnormal accumulation of fat in liver cells [3], which may lead to cirrhosis, liver cancer, and end-stage liver failure [4]. NAFLD patients frequently experience metabolic syndromes including high cholesterol, hypertension, and diabetes. Consequently, cardiovascular disease is a leading cause of death in this population [5, 6]. However, its pathogenesis is complex and not yet fully grasped, involving the interaction of various factors such as genetics, metabolism, and environment. Understanding NAFLD’s underlying mechanisms is critical for developing early diagnostic markers and effective therapeutic targets.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) play critical regulatory roles in various biological processes [7, 8], including cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and metabolic regulation [9]. In NAFLD, miRNAs act as vital regulators of inflammatory responses by modulating the expression of multiple inflammatory factors [10]. They bind to specific mRNAs to adjust gene expression, thereby influencing liver pathophysiology-including lipid metabolism, inflammation, and oxidative stress [11]. miR-199b-5p exhibits unique regulatory roles in liver-related diseases. Studies have shown that miR-199b-5p inhibits the invasion and tumor growth of liver cancer cells by targeting and suppressing STRN expression in liver cancer [12]. In the subclinical cardiovascular disease model, upregulation of miR-199b-5p promotes inflammatory responses [13]. Furthermore, miR-199b-5p is closely linked to hepatic lipid metabolism. In patients with NAFLD, miR-199b-5p is positively related to hepatic lipid content, suggesting its potential involvement in the regulation of hepatic fat metabolism [14]. Given the significant function of miR-199b-5p in hepatic lipogenesis and inflammatory responses, we hypothesize that it might be a key factor in the development and progression of NAFLD. In-depth research on the role and mechanism of miR-199b-5p will help to reveal the pathogenesis of NAFLD and provide a foundation for developing new therapeutic strategies.

In summary, this study seeks to explore the role and underlying mechanisms of miR-199b-5p in NAFLD development and determine its expression in NAFLD. It is expected to offer new theoretical foundations and potential targets for NAFLD’s early diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and methods

Study population

Patients meeting the criteria were selected from the Jiangxi Provincial People’s Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang Medical College database, including 110 non-NAFLD patients and 90 NAFLD patients collected from April 2024 to January 2025. Inclusion criteria: (1) In early-stage patients with steatosis, only fatty degeneration is present, without fibrosis, inflammation, or liver damage; (2) No excessive alcohol consumption history: for males, alcohol intake should not exceed 210 g/week, and for females, it should not exceed 105 g/week; (3) Absence of other known liver diseases: such as Wilson’s disease, hemochromatosis, autoimmune hepatitis, and cholestatic or vascular liver diseases; (4) No exposure to drugs or environmental toxins: Exclude the use of medications known to cause fatty liver or steatohepatitis. Exclusion criteria: (1) Presence of other liver diseases; (2) History of long-term heavy alcohol consumption; (3) Suffering from malignant tumors or other severe systemic diseases; (4) Currently undergoing treatment with medications that may affect liver metabolism; (5) participants with other metabolic liver diseases or exposure to medications known to cause fatty liver or steatohepatitis were excluded; (6) Individuals consuming over 210 g of alcohol per week (for men) or over 105 g per week (for women) were also excluded, ensuring the study population conforms to NAFLD diagnostic criteria. All enrolled early NAFLD patients underwent fasting abdominal ultrasound examinations conducted by qualified physicians, who subsequently issued diagnostic reports [15, 16]. The diagnostic criteria include diffusely enhanced liver echogenicity, attenuated deep echoes, and poorly visualized intrahepatic vascular structures. This process complies with the imaging diagnostic criteria for NAFLD as outlined in the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (2018) [17]. Meanwhile, the possibility of other metabolic liver diseases was ruled out through virological serological testing, investigation of alcohol consumption history, and review of clinical data. The Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Provincial People’s Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang Medical College has given the green light to this study (2023-056 IIT FS). We also adhered to the STROBE guidelines to guarantee the study’s transparency and scientific validity [18].

Baseline data collection

Collect baseline data of all study subjects, including age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, BMI, WC, TG, TC, LDL-C, and HDL-C. Document the clinical characteristics of each patient in detail to provide foundational information for subsequent analysis.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis

Collect 5 milliliters of blood from the subject’s peripheral blood and let it stand at room temperature for 30 min to promote coagulation. The serum was subsequently isolated via centrifugation at 3000 rpm for a duration of 10 min and preserved at −80℃ to keep RNA intact. Total RNA was isolated from serum and cells utilizing Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA), and its concentration and purity were determined. Using RNA as a template, cDNA was synthesized with the miRNA cDNA synthesis kit. Primer sequences: miR-199b-5p (forward: 5’-GCCCGCCCAGTGTTTAGACTAT-3’, reverse: 5’-GTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3’); U6 (forward: 5’-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’, reverse: 5’-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3’); GAPDH (forward: 5’-GCACCACCAACTGCTTAGCA-3’, reverse: 5’-GTCTTCTGGGTGGCAGTGATG-3’); SIRT1 (forward: 5’-TAGCCTTGTCAGATAAGGAAGGA-3’, reverse: 5’-ACAGCTTCACAGTCAACTTTGT-3’). In addition, the primer amplification efficiencies of miR-199b-5p, U6, GAPDH, and SIRT1 were all between 90% and 110%. The stability of U6 in serum is relatively high, and it has been validated through literature [19] and preliminary experiments. Therefore, U6 was used as the internal reference for miRNA analysis, and GAPDH was used as the internal reference for mRNA analysis for normalization, and calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Cell culture and oleic acid (OA) induction

The human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line HepG2 was purchased from the Korean Cell Line Bank (KCLB, Seoul, Korea). All experiments were conducted using fourth-generation cells to ensure phenotypic stability. HepG2 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) containing 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% (v/v) penicillin-streptomycin. The cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, and the culture medium was replaced every 2–3 days. For subculture, the cells were digested with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Beyotime, China) at 37 °C for approximately 1–2 min until detachment. Terminate the digestion by adding two volumes of complete medium. The cell suspension was then centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min. After resuspending the cell pellet, it was subcultured at a passage ratio of 1:3 to 1:4 into new culture flasks. All cells used in the experiments were in the logarithmic growth phase. To avoid the potential masking of the subtle regulatory effects of miR-199b-5p by the strong cytotoxicity of palmitic acid (PA), OA was selected to induce HepG2 steatosis. Prior to OA induction, OA was mixed with bovine serum albumin (BSA) at a ratio of 3:1 to prepare the OA/BSA solution. The cells were divided into a blank control group (only an equivalent amount of BSA was added) and an OA-induced group. The induced group was treated with a final concentration of 400 µmol/L OA/BSA solution, and subsequent experiments were conducted after 24 h of treatment.

Cell transfection

The miR-199b-5p inhibitor (Catalog Number: HY-RI00400) and its corresponding negative controls were separately transfected into OA-induced HepG2 cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. Preliminary screening by qPCR revealed a maximum transfection efficiency of 67%. In the formal experiments described in this paper, cells were transfected with miR-199b-5p inhibitor or NC inhibitor at a final concentration of 100 nM. After six hours post-transfection, switch the medium with new culture medium and continue the culture for an additional 48 h before proceeding with subsequent experimental assays.

CCK-8 assay for cell viability

The transfected cells were seeded at 5 × 10³ cells per well in a 96-well culture plate, with 6 replicate wells set for each group. Following 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-culture, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for an extra two hours, after which cell viability was evaluated.

Detection of inflammatory factors and lipid indicators

Following cell culture, gather the supernatant liquid. According to the instructions of the TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β ELISA kits (ExCell Bio, China), measure the inflammatory factors in the supernatant of each group of cells respectively. Each group consisted of three replicate wells, and the average value was taken. Cells were collected from each group and lysed using cholesterol, triglyceride, and free fatty acid assay kits (Sigma-Aldrich) to extract total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and free fatty acids (FFA) respectively. The content of each was measured colorimetrically at 570 nm using a microplate reader.

Oxidative stress indicator testing

The intracellular oxidative stress indicators, MDA content and SOD viability, were measured using kits (Jiancheng, Nanjing, China). ROS levels were detected using the H2DCFDA fluorescent probe (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). For the determination of MDA content, the thiobarbituric acid (TBA) method was utilized, while the xanthine oxidase method was employed for the measurement of SOD viability. The detection of ROS reflects the level of reactive oxygen species through fluorescence intensity.

Luciferase reporter gene assay

We used the pGL3 luciferase reporter vector purchased from Promega. A 450 bp fragment containing the miR-199b-5p binding site was amplified from the 3’UTR region of the SIRT1 gene. After digestion with NotI and XhoI, it was inserted into the multiple cloning site of the pGL3 vector to construct the wild-type reporter gene plasmid. The QuickChange Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit and specifically designed primers were used to mutate the binding sites, resulting in mutant reporter gene plasmids. Introduce the wild-type or mutant plasmids alongside miR-199b-5p inhibitor or NC into HepG2 cells. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, the luciferase viability was measured.

Western blot analysis

HepG2 cells were washed twice with PBS and then lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) containing 1 mM PMSF and a protease/phosphatase inhibitor cocktail. The protein concentration was quantified using the BCA protein assay kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Equal amounts of protein (30 µg) were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and then transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk prepared in TBST at room temperature for 1 h, then incubated with primary antibodies against SIRT1 and GAPDH overnight at 4℃. The protein bands were visualized using the ECL detection kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and captured with the ChemiDoc imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA). The band intensity was quantitatively analyzed using Image Lab software (Bio-Rad, USA) and normalized to GAPDH expression as an internal reference.

Statistical analysis

All data analyses were conducted with the help of SPSS 25.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. Every measurement datum was presented as mean ± standard deviation. This study employed G*Power software for sample size calculation, with an Effect Size of 0.83 and Cohen’s d ≥ 0.8. When the significance level α was set at 0.05 with sample sizes of 110 and 90 respectively, the test power reached as high as 0.99. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way ANOVA, supplemented with Tukey’s post hoc test, while partial ŋ2 and Cohen’s d were calculated to indicate effect sizes. Correlation analysis was conducted using Pearson’s correlation coefficient p-value and 95% confidence interval (CI) for effect size estimation. The logistic regression analysis report was evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

No significant differences in age hypertension, diabetes, smoking, and TC values were observed between the two groups (p > 0.05).

The expression and diagnostic value of miR-199b-5p

Comparative analysis of miR-199b-5p expression between the NAFLD and Non-NAFLD groups indicated that its level was markedly elevated in the NAFLD (Fig. 1A, P < 0.0001, Cohen’s d = 0.14). According to Pearson correlation analysis, miR-199b-5p was significantly positively correlated with BMI (Fig. 1B, r = 0.807), WC (Fig. 1C, r = 0.791), TG (Fig. 1D, r = 0.808), and LDL-C (Fig. 1E, r = 0.861), all P-values for the above indicators were less than 0.0001. However, miR-199b-5p showed a significant negative correlation with HDL-C (Fig. 1F, r=−0.791, P < 0.0001). According to the ROC analysis of NAFLD and Non-NAFLD patients (Fig. 1G), the AUC of miR-199b-5p was 0.875. At the optimal cutoff value of 1.125, miR-199b-5p showed a sensitivity of 0.774 and a specificity of 0.864.

Logical regression analysis of NAFLD patients

In our cohort, multivariate logistic regression analysis identified miR-199b-5p as a risk factor for NAFLD (Table 2, OR = 5.73, 95% CI: 2.25–14.6, P < 0.001). In terms of other metabolic indicators, BMI (OR = 5.44, 95% CI: 2.05–14.43, P = 0.001) and LDL-C (OR = 4.47, 95% CI: 1.64–12.2, P = 0.003) were also significant risk factors for NAFLD. On the contrary, HDL-C may be a protective factor against NAFLD (OR = 0.18, 95% CI: 0.06–0.58, P = 0.004). Analysis indicates that age and TC were not significantly correlated with NAFLD risk (p > 0.05).

The influence of miR-199b-5p on OA-induced inflammation in HepG2 cells

The expression of miR-199b-5p under different treatment conditions and its impact on inflammatory factors were analyzed. The findings demonstrated that OA treatment significantly upregulated miR-199b-5p expression (P < 0.0001), whereas the miR-199b-5p inhibitor could overturn the upregulation (Fig. 2A, P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.13). In the OA-treated group, the levels of TNF-α (Fig. 2B, partial ŋ2=0.14), IL-6 (Fig. 2C, partial ŋ2=0.11), and IL-1β (Fig. 2D, partial ŋ2=0.16) were all significantly elevated compared to the Control group (P < 0.0001). However, after the use of the miR-199b-5p inhibitor, the inflammatory factors were significantly reduced (P < 0.0001). The study showed that compared with the control group, the TC (partial ŋ2=0.13), TG (partial ŋ2=0.09), and FFA (partial ŋ2=0.11) levels in the OA-induced group were significantly increased (Fig. 2E-G, P < 0.0001). The miR-199b-5p inhibitor significantly reduced TC, TG, and FFA levels in OA-induced steatotic hepatocytes (P < 0.0001).

The influence of miR-199b-5p on cell viability and oxidative stress in OA-induced HepG2 cells

Based on the trends of cellular viability changes under different treatment conditions, cell viability in the OA group was markedly decreased compared to the Control group (Fig. 3A, P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.14), indicating that OA treatment significantly inhibited cellular viability. The cell viability in the OA + miR-inhibitor group was higher than that in the OA + miR-inhibitor-NC group (P < 0.0001), indicating that the miR-199b-5p inhibitor alleviated the OA-induced suppression of cell viability. Further investigation into the SOD (Fig. 3B, P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.12), MDA (Fig. 3C, P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.13), and ROS (Fig. 3D, P < 0.01, partial ŋ2=0.08) levels of each group of cells revealed that OA treatment increased oxidative damage. The SOD viability in the OA + miR inhibitor group was higher than that in the OA + inhibitor-NC group, while the MDA content and ROS levels were lower than those in the control group.

miR-199b-5p targets and regulates SIRT1

The TargetScan human database predicted the downstream target genes of miR-199b-5p, and the targeting relationship between miR-199b-5p and the 3’UTR of the SIRT1 gene at sites 507–513 (Fig. 4A). The dual-luciferase assay results indicated that miR-199b-5p can directly bind to and inhibit the expression of WT-SIRT1 (Fig. 4B, P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.14). However, when co-transfected with the mutant 3’UTR, this inhibitory effect was abolished. Furthermore, when the miR-199b-5p inhibitor was used, the luciferase viability was significantly restored, further confirming that miR-199b-5p can directly bind to the 3’UTR region of SIRT1 mRNA. This study evaluated the expression levels of SIRT1 in NAFLD patients and their correlation with miR-199b-5p. The study found that the expression of SIRT1 in the NAFLD group was significantly lower than that in the Non-NAFLD group (Fig. 4C, P < 0.0001, Cohen’s d = 0.11). Moreover, there was a significant negative correlation between the levels of miR-199b-5p and SIRT1 (Fig. 4D, r=−0.808, P < 0.0001). The qRT-PCR results (Fig. 4E, P < 0.01) showed that OA treatment could inhibit SIRT1 expression. After miR-mimic transfection, SIRT1 expression was significantly downregulated (P < 0.01). In contrast, miR-inhibitor significantly upregulated SIRT1 expression (P < 0.0001, partial ŋ2=0.09). Western blot analysis showed consistent expression of the internal reference GAPDH across all groups and confirmed that inhibition of miR-199b-5p could reverse the OA-induced downregulation of SIRT1 (Fig. 4F-G, P < 0.0001 partial ŋ2=0.10).

Discussion

NAFLD pathogenesis is complex, involving genetic, environmental, and metabolic factors [20]. Recent studies show that miRNA plays a crucial regulatory role in NAFLD development and progression. MiR-199b-5p, a key miRNA, significantly affects chronic liver diseases, metabolic disorders, and digestive system diseases. Blocking the viability of miR-199b-5p can negate the impact of sh-circTNPO3 on hepatocellular carcinoma cell apoptosis [12]. Studies indicate that miR-199b-5p suppresses epithelial-mesenchymal transition and directly acts on N-cadherin in hepatocellular carcinoma, with multiple studies confirming its therapeutic potential [21]. Notably, one study found elevated miR-199b-5p levels in NAFLD [14], consistent with our results. Further research suggests that miR-199b-5p may serve as a potential NAFLD biomarker. Multifactorial logistic regression analysis identified miR-199b-5p as a risk factor for NAFLD, emphasizing its key role in disease onset and progression. NAFLD is closely linked to obesity indicators (BMI, WC, etc.) and lipid profile indicators (TG, LDL-C, HDL-C, etc.) [14, 22]. This research found that miR-199b-5p was significantly correlated with the aforementioned indicators (r ≈ 0.8, p < 0.0001). We speculate that such a high correlation may reflect the central regulatory role of miR-199b-5p in NAFLD-related metabolic disorder pathways, while the stringent screening criteria in this study may have reduced population heterogeneity.

To simulate the early pathological process of NAFLD [23], this study established an in vitro model by inducing HepG2 cells with OA. HepG2 cells, with their reproducibility, mature system, and hepatic lipid metabolism function, are the predominant in vitro model for NAFLD research [24, 25]. Compared to PA, OA-induced steatosis is a milder process that better mimics the physiological state of simple fatty liver disease [26–28]. This study found that OA treatment significantly up-regulated the expression of miR-199b-5p, while simultaneously inducing decreased cell viability, increased release of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β), and elevated oxidative stress levels. In contrast, inhibition of miR-199b-5p could effectively reverse the aforementioned damaging effects, significantly improve cell viability and oxidative stress status, and reduce inflammation levels. TC, TG, and FFA, as key indicators of intracellular lipid metabolism, can accurately reflect the level of lipid accumulation [29–31]. This study found that the miR-199b-5p inhibitor also significantly reduced the levels of TC, TG, and FFA within the cells. In the progression of NAFLD, miRNAs play a central role by targeting and regulating key genes [32]. For example, miR-122 enhances hepatic lipid production by inhibiting the LKB1/AMPK pathway through targeting Sirt1 in NAFLD [33]. In the kidneys, SIRT1 along with SIRT3 has protective effects by preventing apoptosis and inflammation [34]. Studies have shown that SIRT1 plays a crucial role in regulating lipid metabolism, controlling insulin secretion and sensitivity, exerting anti-inflammatory effects, and managing oxidative stress [35]. Similarly, this study found that miR-199b-5p may function by directly targeting SIRT1. The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay confirmed that miR-199b-5p binds to the 3’UTR region of SIRT1 mRNA. qRT-PCR revealed a significant decrease in SIRT1 mRNA levels in NAFLD patients, which showed a negative correlation with miR-199b-5p expression, and this trend was further confirmed by Western blot analysis. This study provides preliminary evidence to illustrate the diagnostic potential of the miR-199b-5p/SIRT1 axis in NAFLD and suggests its functional mechanism based on in vitro experiments.

Although this study has achieved certain results, there are still several limitations. First, at the clinical research level, this study adopted a single-center cross-sectional design with a limited sample size. This design restricts the inference of causality and makes it difficult for us to track the dynamic changes of miR-199b-5p in disease progression. Second, potential confounding factors within the study population and insufficient sample representativeness may also affect the generalizability of the conclusions. Third, although TC, TG, and FFA biochemical assays provide reliable quantitative data on lipids, the lack of morphological evidence from Oil Red O staining limits the confirmation of cellular steatosis. Fourth, the experiment solely utilized the HepG2 cell line. Although its lipid metabolism pathway is well-defined and widely applied, the results still need to be validated in more physiologically relevant models such as primary hepatocytes. Finally, this study has not yet established an animal model, making it impossible to evaluate the regulatory role of the miR-199b-5p/SIRT1 axis in the overall physiological environment. To this end, our follow-up research will conduct multicenter prospective cohort validation and combine it with animal experiments for functional confirmation. Meanwhile, optimization models such as OA/PA combined stimulation will be employed to systematically elucidate the specific mechanisms of this regulatory axis in the progression of NASH.

Conclusion

In summary, miR-199b-5p is significantly elevated in the serum of NAFLD patients and correlated with BMI, WC, TG, LDL-C, and HDL-C, suggesting its potential diagnostic value. In the OA-induced HepG2 cell model, overexpression of miR-199b-5p inhibits cell viability, promotes inflammatory release, increases lipid levels, and enhances oxidative stress, while the miR-199b-5p inhibitor significantly reverses these effects. The study confirmed that miR-199b-5p directly targets SIRT1’s 3’UTR to regulate its expression. These findings indicate that the miR-199b-5p/SIRT1 axis is involved in NAFLD pathogenesis. Furthermore, miR-199b-5p shows promise as a diagnostic biomarker, but its clinical utility requires validation through future multicenter studies.

VHL missense mutation delineate aggressive clear cell renal cell carcinoma subtype with favorable immunotherapeutic response
Authors: Ziyang Xu, Li Liu, Wenbin Jiang, Youqi Qiu, Boyu Zhang, Jiangting Cheng, Jiyan Luo, Jianming Guo, Jiejie Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundvon Hippel-Lindau (VHL) harbors the highest mutational frequency in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). Although VHL mutational subtypes exert diverse impacts on the functionality of the VHL protein, the clinical significance of VHL mutational heterogeneity remains largely obscure.MethodsThis study included a total of 1331 patients with ccRCC from localized data sets, including our localized Zhongshan Hospital (ZSHS) cohort (n=1270) and Zhongshan immune checkpoint blockade cohort (n=61), as well as 525 patients with ccRCC from two publicly available data sets with matched clinical annotation and multidimensional data. According to the putative biological effect, we subclassified VHL mutation into VHLTrunc and VHLMiss. The association of VHL status with clinical outcomes, genomic, oncogenic and immunologic characteristics was further depicted.ResultsVHLMiss ccRCC was associated with reduced survival in the localized ZSHS and The Cancer Genome Atlas cohorts. Clinical benefit from immunotherapy was observed in VHLMiss patients in all immunotherapy cohorts. VHLMiss ccRCC exhibited hyper-activated cell cycle and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) instead of canonical hypoxia inducible factor pathways, which might contribute to its proliferative morphology. Meanwhile, VHLMiss ccRCC featured an inflamed microenvironment with enriched tertiary lymphoid structures.ConclusionsVHLMiss mutations delineate an aggressive ccRCC subtype with distinct clinical outcomes, likely attributed to its specific oncogenic, morphologic and immunologic features.Keywords: Kidney Cancer, Immunotherapy, Biomarker, Genome

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC), as one of the most common and lethal urinary tumors, is responsible for about 180 000 deaths every year with rising global incidence and mortality.1 The predominant histological subtype of RCC, clear cell RCC (ccRCC), accounts for about 80% of all cases.1,3 Over the past decade, treatment for ccRCC has undergone a revolutionary transformation with the endorsement of immunotherapeutic agents.4 But as of the present date, no biomarkers studied in the context of ccRCC for the response to immunotherapy have gained approval.4 5 Identifying immunotherapy-sensitive patients poses one of the most formidable challenges to oncologists.4 5

ccRCC is characterized by distinct second-hit loss-of-function mutations of von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) tumor suppressor gene, which is mainly achieved by loss of chromosome 3p in combination with mutation of the remaining copy of VHL.3 The VHL protein (pVHL) acts as an E3 ubiquitin ligase, with a well-characterized mechanistic role in the ubiquitination of prolyl-hydroxylated transcription factors, specifically hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF1α) and HIF2α, under normoxic conditions.3 6 7 This ubiquitination process is crucial for their proteolytic degradation and subsequently modulates downstream signaling pathways, including angiogenesis.3 6 7 Somatic VHL mutations are observed in a majority of ccRCC cases at a frequency ranging from 50% to 70% while germline VHL mutations are observed in about 1% of ccRCC cases.8 The molecular alteration of VHL leads to impaired degradation processes of HIFs, inducing a shift in mutant cells towards subsequent secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), a molecule pivotal for the promotion of angiogenesis with multiple targetable potential.3 9 Belzutifan, an HIF-2α inhibitor, has expanded its indications from patients with VHL syndrome to advanced stage RCC of all VHL status, underscoring that targeting VHL-HIF pathway holds profound prospect for clinical application.10

Though diverse biological characteristics and molecular phenotypes of VHL-mutated ccRCC have been revealed, researches assessing the prognostic and predictive implications of VHL alterations in ccRCC have yielded disparate outcomes, proffering divergent and conflicting conclusions.11,14 Further, immunotherapeutic responses from several large clinical trials were also found unrelated to VHL status.5 15 16 The spectrum of VHL mutations discerned in ccRCC predominantly encompasses missense, frameshift and non-sense mutations, compromising the stability and binding affinity of the VHL protein differently.12 17 However, the clinical implications of different VHL subtypes remained undetermined.17

In this study, we endeavored to categorize VHL mutations into two subgroups in ccRCC as VHLTrunc and VHLMiss mutations. With the analysis of localized as well as publicly available cohorts, we delineated the association of VHL mutational status with its correlated divergent prognosis and immunotherapeutic responses. Further, we unveiled the genomic, oncologic and immunological phenotype of different VHL mutational status, shedding light on potential clinical interventions for precision oncology.

Methods

Patient inclusions

Our localized population included patient-level data from two independent cohorts: Zhongshan Hospital (ZSHS) cohort (n=1270) and Zhongshan Hospital immune checkpoint blockade (ZSHS-ICB) cohort (n=61), both were built on primary lesions. The clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in online supplemental table 1,2. Details of the inclusion criteria are described in online supplemental figure 1. The immune-related adverse events of the patients from the ZSHS-ICB cohort are summarized in online supplemental table 3.

ZSHS cohort initially enrolled 1303 patients diagnosed with ccRCC from ZSHS, Fudan University (Shanghai, China). Patients were enrolled between January 2005 and December 2010 based on the following criteria: (1) informed consent, (2) patients with ccRCC (pathologically proven) treated with nephrectomy, (3) no history of other malignancies, and (4) available for formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded specimens. None of the patients have received neoadjuvant or adjuvant systemic therapy. Patients without tissue microarray (TMA) detachment (n=1270) were further included.

ZSHS-ICB cohort enrolled 64 patients with metastatic RCC treated with anti-programmed cell death protein-1 (anti-PD1) or anti-PD-1 plus tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) at ZSHS, Fudan University (Shanghai, China) between July 2016 and October 2023, with the exclusion of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Patients were selected based on the following criteria: (1) informed consent, (2) patients with RCC (pathologically proven) treated with nephrectomy, and (3) available for formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded specimens. Clinical response to immunotherapy was evaluated by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors V.1.1.18 Only pathologically proven ccRCC (n=61) were further included.

Public datasets

Patients from another two public data sets were enrolled, including The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort and the CheckMate 009/010/025 cohort (online supplemental figure 1). Only primary lesions were included in the TCGA cohort while both primary and metastatic lesions were included in the CheckMate 009/010/025 cohort because metastatic lesions included were not paired with primary lesions.

TCGA cohort is composed of 530 patients, with both clinical information and messenger RNA (mRNA) expression data available in the TCGA-kidney renal cell carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC) cohort. In this study, 198 patients were excluded due to unavailable genomic data (mutational or chromosomal). Ultimately, 332 patients were eligible for further analysis (online supplemental figure 1).

CheckMate 025 is a randomized phase III trial conducted in a similar clinical setting as phase I and phase II studies, CheckMate 009 and CheckMate 010.2 5 We obtained individualized data of totally 592 patients enrolled in these three trials from a previous study.5 In this study, 375 patients were excluded due to unavailable genomic or transcriptomic data. Another 24 patients were excluded due to incomplete clinical data, including objective respense rate (ORR) and tumor shrinkage data. Ultimately, 193 patients were eligible for further analysis (online supplemental figure 1). Due to the above stringent inclusion criteria, ICB sensitivity predictors for all patients, including the Javelin signature and Merck18 signature mentioned in previous studies, were available and taken into analysis in the CheckMate cohort.5 16

Dichotomies of VHL mutations

The array of VHL mutations identified in ccRCC primarily involves missense, frameshift, and non-sense mutations. Previous investigations have demonstrated that non-sense and frameshift mutations undermine the structural integrity and functionality of the pVHL, whereas missense mutations may not decisively abolish its stability and binding affinity.12 17 These discernments create dichotomies in the functional process of pVHL. VHL mutations that manifest as non-sense mutations, frameshift deletions and frameshift insertions were referred to as VHLTrunc mutations, all with direct evidence to abrogate pVHL function.12 17 On the other hand, patients identified with mutations that lead to a single amino acid change or partial pVHL dysfunction are likewise classified into VHLMiss mutations, including missense, in-frame, splice-site and non-stop mutations. Those with truncating and missense mutations simultaneously were classified as VHLTrunc, according to the perspective that a single VHL truncating mutation might be sufficient to efficaciously undermine the functional capacity of pVHL.

Whole exome sequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted from kidney cancer tissues. DNA quantification and integrity were determined by the Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, Delaware, USA) and the 1% agarose electrophoresis, respectively. Genomic DNA samples from kidney cancer tissues were captured using Agilent SureSelect Human All Exon V.8 library (Agilent Technologies, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, the genomic DNA was sheared into short fragments using the kit’s enzyme. DNA was purified and treated with reagents supplied with the kit according to the protocol. Adapters from Agilent were ligated onto the polished ends and the libraries were amplified by PCR. The amplified libraries were hybridized with the custom probes. The DNA fragments bound with the probes were washed and eluted with the buffer provided in the kit. Then these libraries were sequenced on the Illumina sequencing platform (NovaSeq 6000, Illumina, San Diego, California, USA) and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated. The whole exome sequencing (WES) and analysis were conducted by OE Biotech (Shanghai, China), at 200× sequencing depth. Further details of methods are provided in the online supplemental methods.

Processing of genomic and transcriptomic data

The mutational signature calculation was carried out with ‘sigminer’ algorithm. The infiltration of immune cells and immunogenomic signatures were calculated by the single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithms based on related genes expression.19 The involved signatures for GSEA were defined by previous studies or public data sets (online supplemental table 4). Comparison of pathway activation profiles were carried out with GSEA. In the TCGA cohort, other genomic-related data were acquired from a previous study.20 GISTIC2 was used to identify recurrent amplification and deletion events and calculate copy number aberrations.21

Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence methods

In the ZSHS cohort and ZSHS-ICB cohorts, two pathologists who were blinded to the clinicopathological data scored all samples separately. The mean count derived from their respective evaluations was used as the basis for analysis. VHL immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining was performed according to previous protocols and evaluated as the histoscore (H-score) with NanoZoomer-XR.22 H-score equals the percentage of positive tumor cell nuclei multiplied by the intensity of staining (1, weak; 2, moderate; and 3, strong). The final H-score ranges from 0 (minimum) to 300 (maximum). The pVHL expression levels in the ZSHS and ZSHS-ICB cohorts were divided into VHL loss, low and intact status with the trisection by H-score. ccRCC with the top third, middle third and lowest third VHL expression were referred to as “intact”, “low” and “loss”, respectively (online supplemental figure 2A). VHL IHC analysis was conducted in 1324 patients including the whole ZSHS cohort (n=1270) and ZSHS-ICB cohort (n=54), while IHC analysis regarding the immune characteristics was conducted only in a subset of 420 patients, including 366 patients in ZSHS cohort and 54 patients in ZSHS-ICB cohort. IHC and immunofluorescence (IF) staining for tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs) were summarized in online supplemental table 5,6 and conducted by two pathologists who were blind to the clinical information. The mitotic index and anisonucleosis were assessed from digitized diagnostic slides of the tumors as previously described.23 The mitotic index was defined as the sum of the number of mitotic figures (the microscopic appearance of a cell undergoing mitosis) seen in 0.6 mm2 in the four most mitotic regions of the tumor, in alliance with previous study.23 Anisonucleosis was scored as high, moderate and low, according to the variation in nuclear size.

Statistical analysis

Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank test were performed to conduct survival analysis. Univariate and multivariate analysis was conducted using the Cox proportional hazards regression model. Pearson’ s χ2 test was applied to detect categorical variables. Statistical p values were computed using Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis Test. P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics V.26.0 and R software V.4.1.2.

Results

VHLMiss and VHLTrunc mutations in ccRCC

Based on the distinct mutational spectrum and putative biological effects, we categorized VHL mutations into two subgroups as VHLTrunc and VHLMiss mutations. To comprehensively investigate the mutational profile of VHL, we initially evaluated the frequency of VHL mutations in a combined cohort (including localized ZSHS-ICB, CheckMate and TCGA cohort) with complete DNA sequencing data and clinical information (figure 1A–C). 50.96% of VHL-mutant ccRCC (159/312) manifested as VHLTrunc ccRCC, while the remaining 49.04% were identified as VHLMiss ccRCC (153/312). Uniform distribution of VHLTrunc mutational sites was observed in all cohorts (figure 1D), while VHLMiss mutational sites were concentrated in certain regions (figure 1A–C). Lower mutational frequency in Elong-C binding sites was observed in VHLMiss compared with VHLTrunc ccRCC (p=0.005; figure 1E).

VHLMiss mutations are predictive of unfavorable prognosis in ccRCC

We then investigated whether the mutational status of VHL might impact survival outcomes. In the TCGA cohort (n=332), we observed marked differences in overall survival (OS) based on the presence of VHL mutational status. VHLMiss patients (median OS: 60.25 months) yield shorter OS and recurrence-free survival compared with patients with VHLTrunc (median OS: 112.92 months) and VHLWT (median OS: 74.83 months) (log-rank p=0.016; figure 2A and B and online supplemental figure 3A) while no significance was found between VHL mutational status and tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stages (p=0.704; Online supplemental figure 3B) as well as metastatic status (p=0.521; Online supplemental figure 3C). Considering the correlation of VHL expression to mutational status (online supplemental figure 2A,E),8 24 VHL loss, low and intact status were used to approximate VHLTrunc, VHLMiss, and VHLWT determined by DNA sequencing. A total of 1270 patients (ZSHS cohort, encompassing 877 men and 393 women) who were pathologically confirmed to have ccRCC (909 TNM Stage I/II and 361 TNM Stage III/IV) were further enrolled and VHL IHC was conducted on TMA (online supplemental table 1). Patients with ccRCC with VHLLow yield shorter survival compared with patients with other VHL subtypes in terms of OS (log-rank p<0.001; figure 2C and D). Multivariate Cox regression with the inclusion of clinicopathological factors further reveals VHL status as an independent stratification factor for prognosis (online supplemental figure 3D,E).

VHLMiss ccRCC demonstrate superior immunotherapeutic responses

We then explored the link between VHL mutational status and immunotherapeutic responses in ccRCC. A total of 61 patients (ZSHS-ICB cohort, encompassing 37 men and 24 women) who were pathologically confirmed to have metastatic ccRCC were enrolled (online supplemental table 2). In ZSHS-ICB cohort treated with available WES data (n=41), VHLMiss patients had longer progression-free survival from immunotherapy than VHLTrunc and VHLWT patients (p=0.045; figure 3A and C). Consistent results were observed when patient classification was determined by IHC (n=54), suggesting that VHLLow patients have extended PFS compared with other patients (p=0.013; figure 3B). In the CheckMate cohort (n=193), VHLMiss patients showed longer overall survival compared with VHLTrunc or VHLWT patients in the nivolumab arm (log-rank p=0.044; figure 3D, (online supplemental figure 4A) but not in the everolimus arm (log-rank p=0.859; figure 3E). No statistical differences were observed in subgroup analyses that distinguished primary and metastatic lesions (p=0.062 and p=0.414; online supplemental figure 4B-D). Compared with anti-mammalian target of rapamycin (anti-mTOR) therapy, nivolumab improved OS in VHLMiss patients (HR=0.544, 95% CI: 0.297 to 0.998, p=0.046; figure 3F), but not in VHLTrunc or VHLWT patients (HR=0.907, 95% CI: 0.504 to 1.632, p=0.744; HR=1.085, 95% CI: 0.527 to 1.613, p=0.773; figure 3F). Multivariate Cox regression with the inclusion of clinicopathological factors and previously identified ICB biomarkers reveals VHL status as an independent stratification factor for immunotherapy responses (online supplemental figure 4E,F). Notably, VHL mutational status was uncorrelated with existing ccA/ccB or TCGA classification (p=0.289 and p=0.551; online supplemental figure 5).

Cell-cycle and NF-κB activation interlinks VHLMiss ccRCC with proliferative morphology

Next, the genomic landscapes of VHLMiss ccRCC were studied. Tumor mutational burden, microsatellite instability and mutational signatures were largely similar between VHLMiss and VHLTrunc ccRCC (online supplemental figure 6A-C). Among recurrent copy number alternations with the highest frequencies in ccRCC,5 relatively higher proportions of altered segments were detected in VHLMiss patients, including 3p21, 5q35 and 8p23 (p=0.001, p=0.045 and p=0.008, respectively; figure 4A, (online supplemental figure 6D-F). Besides, allele-specific copy number analysis revealed VHLMiss ccRCC exhibits a high prevalence of cell-cycle-related gene alternations, especially compared with VHLTrunc ccRCC (figure 4B). Oncogenic signaling pathways exert a profound influence on tumorigenesis and underlying biological characteristics.25 26 Notably, NF-κB and cell cycle activation were associated with aggressive phenotypes in ccRCC.1427,29 VHLMiss patients exhibit activation of NF-κB and cell cycle (p=0.041 and p=0.013, respectively) while exhibit relative inactivation of the HIF-VEGF pathway compared with VHLTrunc patients (p=0.020; figure 4C). GSEA analysis further revealed divergent molecular functions and biological processes between VHLMiss and VHLTrunc patients. VHLMiss ccRCC were characterized by activation of cell cycle-related pathways while VHLTrunc ccRCC were characterized by activation of mitochondrial dynamics, which might be attributed to HIF activation30 (figure 4C). Besides, VHLMiss ccRCC demonstrated enriched activity of glycogen metabolism while VHLTrunc ccRCC demonstrated enriched activity of nitrogen metabolism (p=0.027 and p=0.041, respectively; Online supplemental figure 7). Given the close correlation between cell-cycle and NF-κB activation and tumor proliferative capability in various contexts,23 31 we further explored whether genomic and oncogenic traits of VHLMiss ccRCC were linked with histologic characteristics. Mitotic index and anisonucleosis, measures of proliferation and nuclear aberrations in morphology (p=0.034 and p=0.042, respectively; figure 4D), were significantly enriched in VHLMiss ccRCC (figure 4E,F), indicating enriched proliferative dysplasia in VHLMiss ccRCC.

An inflamed immune environment enriched with TLS and pro-TLS CXCL13+CD8+ T cells in VHLMiss ccRCC

The immune portrait of VHLMiss ccRCC was further depicted. Immune deconvolution analysis via ssGSEA showed an overall high immune cells infiltration in VHLMiss patients (figure 5A). However, no significant differences in checkpoint molecules expression were observed (online supplemental figure 8A-F). A novel classification of dichotomous ccRCC immune subtypes endowed with prognosis and TKI responses were proposed in our previous study.32 No association of TME-A/B subtype or immune escaped subtype and VHL mutational status was observed (p=0.124 and p=0.206, respectively; online supplemental figure 8G).32 33 In the localized ZSHS cohort, IHC analysis further validated the high infiltration of various immune effector cells in VHLMiss patients (figure 5B). Our previous studies have linked the heterogeneity of CD8+ T cells with divergent clinical outcomes in ccRCC.34,36 We observed high infiltration of CXCL13+CD8+ T cells (p=0.002; figure 5B), a contributor to TLS formulation, but not CD39+CD8+ T cells (p=0.124) or TNFRSF9+CD8+ T cells (p=0.878; online supplemental figure 7H) in VHLMiss patients. Besides, immunomodulatory mechanisms analysis revealed TLS pathway activation as well as activated inflammatory response in VHLMiss patients (p=0.005 and p=0.022, respectively; figure 5C). The above findings suggest that TLS might be characteristically enriched in ICB-sensitive VHLMiss patients. IF and IHC staining were further conducted and a relatively higher proportion of TLS positive in VHLLow and VHLMiss was observed (p<0.001 and p=0.097, respectively; figure 5D,E). Intriguingly, double IF labeling (CXCL13/CD8) were localized in proximity to TLS preferentially in VHLMiss patients, while in other patients, lymphoid cells tend to aggregate without the formation of TLS, likely due to the absence of CXCL13+CD8+ T cells infiltration (figure 5F).

Discussion

VHL harbors the highest mutational frequency in ccRCC but few correlations between VHL mutation and survival, whether prognosis or immunotherapeutic responses, were observed.3 Though findings from several clinical trials have not established definitive associations between VHL mutations and immunotherapeutic responses, indications from other studies suggest potential links.45 14,16 Rosellini and colleagues found that higher PFS and OS were shown with VHL genomic alternations, while VHL/polybromo-1 (PBRM1) dual mutated subgroup was especially sensitive to immunotherapy.37 Additionally, the predictive efficacy of other identified ICB biomarkers, like AXL receptor tyrosine kinase (AXL), might be dependent on VHL status.38 However, due to conflicting evidence, current consensus maintains that VHL mutation as a whole entity is not associated with immunotherapy responsiveness.3 4 8 Whether VHL-mutant ccRCC is composed of entities with different clinical implications warrants in-depth research.

In this pioneering study, we refreshed such an idea and highlighted that VHL status is indeed an important influential determinant of survival attributed to the dichotomies of VHLMiss and VHLTrunc. Specifically, VHLMiss ccRCC presents a novel and independent aggressive clinical entity with an inferior prognosis but might be vulnerable to immunotherapy. Therefore, it might be advisable to prioritize the utilization of immunotherapeutic interventions for VHLMiss ccRCC. Our results may be used to potentially inform conversations around decision-making regarding the choice of therapy in patients with VHLMiss and VHLTrunc ccRCC.

The aggressive subtype VHLMiss ccRCC was featured by hyperactivated cell cycle and NF-κB signaling, which are downstream regulated pathway on VHL. Loss of pVHL was found to correlate with hyper-activation of cell-cycle-related functions.3 Cell cycle hyperactivation was reported to be associated with a proliferative nature of tumors as well as a highly immunogenic environment, but low activity in angiogenesis.14 Besides, cell cycle hyperactivated ccRCC held responses to immunotherapy while resistance to TKI therapy, which aligns closely with our conclusions.12 NF-κB hyperactivation was another outstanding feature of VHLMiss ccRCC. Consistent with our findings, previous study showed that NF-κB hyper-activation was correlated with a worse outcome in ccRCC.29

ccRCC harbors an immune contexture infiltrated with CD8+ T cells, but an overall CD8+ T cells infiltration tends to be associated with inferior survival.5 This might be attributed to the genomic and oncogenic heterogeneity, driving distinct immune environment dominated by different subpopulations of CD8+ T cells.534,36 In this study, we depicted an immunotherapy-responsive subtype, VHLMiss ccRCC, featured an activated and inflamed microenvironment with enriched TLS and CXCL13+CD8+ T cells. Interestingly, the infiltration of CXCL13+CD8+T cells was found associated with poor prognosis in ccRCC,34 while suggesting immunotherapy responses in various tumor types,39 which is highly concordant with our findings. Considering that missense mutations are recognized as a major source of neoantigens in a range of cancers, a plausible explanation is that VHL missense mutations in ccRCC generate neoantigens capable of activating antitumor immunity, remodeling the immune contexture into an inflamed microenvironment.17 40 Notably, cytokine CXCL13 was found closely related to neoantigen-specific immunity activation.41,43 Veatch et al identified a specific subset of CD4+ T cells recognizing tumor neoantigens that express CXCL13,42 while Hanada et al demonstrated that neoantigen-reactive T cells exhibit a CD39+CXCL13+ phenotype.43 In the context of ccRCC, although the precise source of antigenic stimulation remains elusive, relevant analyses—including those incorporating the impact of VHL mutations—offer significant and focused insights into the effects of anti-PD-1 therapy on T-cell responses.44

VHLTrunc ccRCC was featured by hyperactivated HIF signaling, which is targetable for multiple therapies.3 9 13 Multi HIF-dependent TKIs targeting downstream molecules have demonstrated efficacy in clinical practice, while resistance remained in many others.5 14 Though mTOR activity was closely related to HIF activation in that mTOR stimulates translation of HIF1A mRNA into protein by phosphorylation of eIF4E binding protein,45 46 no significant difference of mTOR inhibitor (mTORi) efficacy everolimus was observed between VHL subtypes in this study. One possible explanation is that mTORi antitumor efficacy might not be solely attributable to their effects on HIF.47 48 Other factors, including myelocytomatosis (MYC) and various metabolic pathways, might also be involved.48 Thus, directly targeting HIF might offer an anticipated novel approach. Considering the heightened activation of HIF signaling behind VHLTrunc ccRCC, it is particularly noteworthy that belzutifan, a Food and Drug Administration newly approved HIF-2α inhibitor, might exert greater clinical benefit on VHLTrunc ccRCC.10

Limitations of this study should be taken into consideration. First, its retrospective nature brings forth the possibility of introducing biases related to sampling and batch effects. Additionally, the assessment of VHL protein expression levels using IHC in the ZSHS cohort does not fully mimic the sequencing data. Furthermore, heterogeneity among different cohorts might affect the reliability of our conclusions despite our efforts for taking into account variates that differ across cohorts. Particularly, CheckMate cohort included both primary and metastatic lesions while other cohorts were built on primary lesions, which might engender inherent biases. Leveraging findings from this study for clinical applications may necessitate consideration of VHL mutational status in primary versus metastatic lesions and among different metastatic sites.

Our study has identified, for the first time, that VHL-mutant ccRCC with ambiguous clinical significance was indeed composed of two distinct clinical entities, as VHLMiss and VHLTrunc, with divergent prognosis and responses to immunotherapy. Different from previous assumptions that VHL-mutant ccRCC harbors HIF hyperactivation,9 23 a novel subtype, VHLMiss ccRCC, was featured as hyperactivated cell cycle and NF-κB signaling, conferring them with aggressive behaviors and proliferative morphology. Meanwhile, VHLMiss ccRCC was linked to better sensitivity to immunotherapy, likely attributed to the TLS-enriched environment. Further, our study offers a refreshed outlook that obscure clinical significance behind common mutations in RCC, including PBRM1, SETD2 and BAP1, might stem from their inherent heterogeneity. Deconstruction of these mixed clinical entities might advance a giant step towards precision urologic oncology.

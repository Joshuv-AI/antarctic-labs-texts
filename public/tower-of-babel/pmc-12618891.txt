Enhancing shielded metal arc welding performance through process parameter optimization with low-frequency vibration assistance
Authors: Rajeev Ranjan, Manoj Kumar, Sachin Rathore, Ashish Goyal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractVibration-assisted welding has emerged as a high-quality weld production technique, with recent studies focusing on medium and high-frequency applications to enhance mechanical properties. This research aims to investigate and optimize process parameters using low-frequency vibration-assisted welding. The Taguchi method was employed to identify optimal conditions for improved weld quality. Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW) was used in conjunction with a low-frequency (100 Hz) vibratory setup to impart controlled vibration to the workpiece during welding. The effects of welding current, vibration time, and frequency were optimized using Taguchi methodology and response surface technique based on an L27 orthogonal array design. The results indicate that the most important factor affecting the required hardness, tensile strength, and impact strength is vibration frequency. Vibration-assisted welding of mild steel at optimized parameters 120 A current, 100 Hz frequency, and 60 s vibration time resulted in a maximum hardness of 97.16 RHN and enhanced impact strength. Low heat input, 100 Hz vibration frequency, and 100 s vibration time yielded optimal tensile strength. SEM analysis revealed a refined microstructure in the weld zone, attributed to weld pool excitation, which promotes finer grain formation and improved mechanical properties.Keywords: Low-frequency vibration-assisted welding process, Mechanical characteristics, Mild steel, Response surface methodologySubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Vibration-assisted welding (VAW) is a process that combines mechanical vibration with traditional welding techniques. Defects like porosity, insufficient fusion, and cracking will be less likely and the quality of the welds will be improved. Joining two or more pieces of material is typically required in manufacturing or daily life1. The joint must always be in good working order. Thousands of scientific articles are published annually. Although the joints malfunction and more research is necessary, these studies on joint performance provide some pertinent characteristics2. Welding is widely used in industries to establish long-lasting connections. Welding is preferred in industrial operations because it produces the most significant results, even though other techniques, such as riveting, bolting, soldering, etc., can be used to create the connections3. Numerous industrial sectors, primarily the mechanical and structural ones, use the welding technique. One of the most important aspects of welding is the energy sources. These resources are selected Depending on the type of application and the environment4. There are two ways to increase process efficiency in the welding zone: mechanical and electric vibrations. The two primary applications of vibration in industrial operations are the vibration of the tool and the workpiece5.

Ultrasonic vibration has been said to improve microstructural refinement and deepen penetration. Ultrasonic vibration during the welding process has a major impact on the behaviour of the weld pool in terms of crystallisation and solidification6. It reduces the occurrence of porosity and liquid crack defects while also aiding in the promotion of melt flow and grain refinement. Consequently, the mechanical properties of welded joints are significantly improved by the application of ultrasonic vibration. Grain size is decreased and hardness properties are improved through the use of ultrasonic vibration. Furthermore, welding defects like porosities and cracks have significantly decreased7. A major advantage of Low frequency vibration assisted welding (LFVAW) is that you can improve the quality of welds which does not require additional heat input, allowing for a reduced risk of distortion and further temperature variations8. In addition, it is often easy to integrate the LFVAW into existing welders and thus a cost-effective way of improving the quality of welds. In general, VAW works well for enhancing weld quality and decreasing flaws, especially in difficult welding applications9. To identify the most important FSW process parameter, analysis of variance was used to achieve a grey relational grade10. It has been demonstrated that mechanical vibration used on metal solidifying results in finer, more uniform microstructure. Using vibration at frequencies of 50 Hz, 500 Hz, and 5 kHz, respectively, the average grain size of the welds’ centers decreased by 29%, 11%, and 20% when compared to conventional welding, according to metallographic analysis11. The mechanical properties of conventional welding methods and low frequency vibration assisted welding in mild steel plates are compared in another study. The study found that low-frequency vibration assisted welding produced materials with higher yield and tensile strengths and lower hardness than conventional welding. The authors came to the conclusion that applying this method could significantly improve the mechanical properties of mild steel welded joints12. The effect of low-frequency vibration on mild steel welding has been investigated further. According to the authors, by lowering the welding temperature and increasing the depth of fusion, low-frequency vibration may help reduce deformation and distortion in the welded joint. The study also demonstrated that the application of low-frequency vibration may improve the microstructure of the weld zone, increasing the welded connection’s toughness and hardness13. The mechanical characteristics and microstructure of the die-formed welded sheet were investigated. The mechanical characteristics of the materials of varying thicknesses were ascertained, as well as the impact of the forces applied to the welding areas on the material. Microstructure analysis, hardness, and tensile tests were conducted as part of the study, and the outcomes were analysed. Examining the results, it was discovered that the samples taken from the welded area had higher strength and hardness values than the samples taken from the other areas14.

The welded connections in this investigation were generated by incorporating vibration with slow-rate during welding. A noval excitation welding setup idea has been developed. Mild steel plates with dimensions 100 mm long, 50 mm width and 5 mm thick were used in the study. Three heat input levels 80, 100, and 120 amps were taken into account in the analysis. The range of vibration input was set to 0–100 Hz. According to experimental results, the use of low-frequency excitations enhanced the quality of the welded connections, particularly their impact, tensile, and hardness strengths. This could speed up the welding procedure and results in more robust, more dependable, and longer-lasting welded joints15.

Experimental procedure

Vibratory method

Mild steel plates of size 100 mm long 50 mm wide and 5 mm thick were examined in this investigation. About 0.17% carbon, 0.20% silicon, 0.53% manganese, 0.17% phosphorus, and 0.03% sulfur were included in the composition. Additionally, E-6013 was utilised as the filler material in the butt welding (single-joint) method. The experimental apparatus, as shown in Fig. 1, was designed to apply low-frequency vibrations (LFV) to the welded plate and investigate the characteristics of shielded metal arc welding (SMAW) joints. The actual experimental set up has been shown in Fig. 2. A pair of vibrating guns with frequencies between 10 and 100 Hz were used. G-clamps were utilized to properly hold the mild steel plates. Two passes were completed in order to fill the weld gap: the root pass and the main pass. Amplitude, frequency, and acceleration three vibration-related parameters were measured using a vibration tester17. Three distinct heat inputs were tested using input currents of 80, 100, and 120 A. Furthermore, the research took into account three distinct vibration durations (measured in seconds): 60, 90, and 120. Two vibration guns capable of producing low frequency vibration upto 100 Hz apply vibration.

Schematic diagram of the experimentation setup.

Actual experimental set up of low frequency vibration assisted welding.

To guarantee the best possible weld quality in vibration-assisted shielded metal arc welding (SMAW), process parameters are carefully chosen. For a stable arc and reliable penetration, an arc length of 2–3 mm is kept constant. 3.15 mm is a frequently used electrode diameter that works well for welding mild steel plates that are medium in thickness. For adequate heat input, the arc voltage is set between 20 and 25 V, and for uniform bead formation, the travel speed is regulated between 80 and 120 mm/min. By encouraging improved molten metal flow, external vibration is applied during welding to improve fusion, refine the microstructure, and lower welding defects18.

Microstructure analysis

The microstructure behavior and hardness of conventional welding (in the absence of excitation during welding) and LFVAW were examined in experiments. In order to establish a foundation for comparison, two separate welding investigations were executed: firstly under vibratory settings with the optimal welding setting (current = 80Amp, frequency = 100 Hz, and vibration period = 120 s), and the second under typical conditions.

The hardness measurements for different weld sample sections are shown in Fig. 18. The hardness level of specimens welded with vibratory aid is indicated by the blue line, and the black line indicates the hardness level of traditional welding. The hardness results unequivocally show that vibrations, an external energy source, are dispersed throughout the welded joints’ solidification process.

Examination of the hardness of vibratory and traditional welded joints.

The SEM-captured microstructure of the fusion zone of mild steel welded connection is shown in Fig. 19. An 80Amp current, 100 Hz frequency, and 120-s vibration duration were used to weld a mild steel specimen for this investigation. The colonies of ferrite and pearlite are clearly visible in the SEM picture as white and dark regions, respectively. Additionally, grain boundaries are observable in the SEM analysis40. LFV during welding promotes the formation of a finer grain formation, possibly because of the higher vibration frequency and less dendrites. Furthermore, the refinement of the weld pool during VAW using excitations may be the cause of this grain refinement. One possible explanation for the finely polished structure could be the filler material’s fine distribution. There are metallurgical and process-related reasons for the observed decrease in tensile strength with increased welding current. Higher current levels cause a significant increase in heat input, which impairs the mechanical properties by causing grain coarsening in the heat-affected zone (HAZ) and weld metal. Additionally, excessive current can lead to increased spatter formation and weld defects like undercut and porosity, which all lower strength. These processes improve our comprehension of how current affects weld quality and offer a convincing explanation for the trend that has been noticed. One important factor that raises the microhardness of HAZ and weld metal is the formation of martensite, especially in the ferrite mild steel microstructure. By increasing the vibration frequency and decreasing the number of dendrites, low-frequency vibration in welding allows for the development of a refined grain structure. Additionally, the weld pool may have refined as a result of the grain refinement during vibration-assisted welding with excitations. The fine distribution of the filler material might be one of the elements causing the finely refined structure. The fragmentation of columnar and equiaxed dendritic dendritic arms, which can serve as sides for grain nucleation, may be connected to the grain refinement of the weld metal microstructures under vibration assisted welding41.

Examining the mechanisms and fracture behaviour of welded joints produced by applying vibration is known as fracture analysis in vibration-assisted welding. For example, there are several benefits to using ultrasonic vibrations in welding, including better mechanical qualities, increased material mixing, and a longer process window. Knowing the fracture characteristics of vibration-assisted welded joints can make evaluating their performance and structural integrity simpler. A combination of microscopic and optical tests can be used to analyse cracks in vibration-assisted welded joints42.

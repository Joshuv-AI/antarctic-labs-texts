ShilpoBangla: A dataset of Bangladeshi cultural product images with alternative texts in Bangla and English
Authors: S. M. Nafis Ahmed, Syed Nafees Kaiser, Fernaz Narin Nur, Mohammad Shahjahan Majib, Muhammad Nazrul Islam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The lack of high-quality multimodal resources for low-resource languages such as Bangla poses significant challenges for vision-language research, image captioning, and accessibility technologies. To address this gap, this study introduces ShilpoBangla, a curated dataset comprising 1200 images representing Bengali cultural products across five categories including traditional clothing, food, musical instruments, folk arts and crafts, and jewelry. Each image is paired with human-authored alternative text in both Bangla and English. The study further demonstrates the feasibility of generating image alternative text using a ViT encoder and a GPT-2 decoder, and establishes a benchmark for future multimodal research and cross-linguistic applications. Additionally, the inclusion of alternative text in both Bangla and English enhances the dataset’s accessibility, usability, and adoption among a broader global audience, thereby improving the generalizability and practical applicability of the ShilpoBangla dataset.

Background

Image alternative text is a short description of the information about the contents of the image. Image alternative text is important for making the image accessible to the visually impaired, providing context of the image, and improving search engine optimization (SEO) [1]. It can be useful for describing the contents of medical images, explaining the contents of cultural images, in agriculture, and more. Alternative text for these images can explain much of the rich and diverse history, tradition, and artistic expression of the culture for the Bengali people, as Bengali is the seventh most spoken language in the world [2]. However, very few studies focused on generating alternative text for images that contain Bangladeshi cultural artifacts [[3], [4], [5], [6]].

Most of the studies focused on generating captions for images. An image caption is a shorter description related to the image compared to the image alternative text. The image alternative text conveys the information about the contents of the image in short, while an image caption describes the image in shorter sentences, mostly in just one sentence. Hence, most of the available datasets are for caption generation and rarely focus on Bangladeshi culture. For instance, Ami et al. [7] and Masud et al. [8] used the Flickr8k dataset that contains 8000 images and five captions per image [3]. The captions were machine-translated into Bengali from English. BanglaLekhaImageCaption dataset [4] contains fully annotated Bengali captions instead of machine-translated captions that were used to train a caption generation model using DL methods. However, this dataset has insufficient captions for training, with two captions per image across 9154 images. Another dataset named BNATURE [5] is also fully annotated in Bengali by native speakers, which was used by Mahtab et al. [9] independently and with the BanglaLekhaImageCaption dataset by Sarker et al. [10] for the purpose of generating image captions using vision transformers. It improves upon the BanglaLekhaImageCaption by having more images and captions, including 8000 images with five captions per image specifically. Other than these datasets, the Ovro 1.1 dataset has Bangla-annotated captions. It was developed and used by the author to generate captions that focus on nature and lifestyles [6]. However, it only contains 2000 images and one caption per image. A few studies have adopted deep learning methods to generate image captions in Bengali using such datasets. For instance, De et al. [11] used an encoder-decoder based deep learning approach where InceptionResnetV2 and Xception were employed as encoders, and BiLSTM and BiGRU were implemented as decoders. On the other hand, both Akash et al. [12] and Humaira et al. [13] implemented vision transformers as the encoder. However, Akash et al. [12] used LLM as the decoder, and Humaira et al. [13] utilized transformers as the decoder. The BanglaLekhaImageCaption dataset [4] was used in all of the aforementioned studies for caption generation.

However, machine-translated datasets such as the Flickr8k dataset [3] don’t accurately translate the captions from English to Bengali, as Bengali has a different language structure from English [11]. The BanglaLekhaImageCaption [4], BNATURE [5] and Ovro 1.1 contain fully annotated Bengali captions. However, these datasets focus on different domains instead of Bangladeshi culture. All of these datasets are for generating captions, not for generating image alternative text. Therefore, to address these issues of existing datasets, we created an image alternative text generation dataset in Bengali and English that is fully annotated by native speakers and focuses on Bangladeshi culture, including traditional foods, clothing, musical instruments, artifacts, and jewelry.

Data Description

The ShilpoBangla dataset consists of culturally significant Bangladeshi product images and their corresponding human-written Bangla and English alternative texts. The dataset is organized into five predefined classes, each representing a major category of Bangladeshi cultural items such as Traditional Clothing, Foods, Musical Instruments, Folk Arts and Crafts and Jewelry

Each class folder contains 240 original images, resulting in a total of 1200 image samples. Each image is 512×512 pixels. All image files are stored in JPG format. The images were collected from contributors via Google Form and through direct field photography conducted by the authors. In addition to the image folders, the repository includes a single annotation file in CSV format that stores metadata and descriptive text for every item in the dataset. The CSV file contains one row per image with the following columns:•class_name: The assigned class label of the cultural product.•image_path: Specifies the relative file path of each image within the dataset, structured in the format “class_name/image_name”. This path indicates the directory (class label) in which the image is stored and the corresponding image filename.•alt_text_bn: A human-written Bangla descriptive alternative text describing the item’s appearance, material, cultural significance, or usage.•eng_text: Corresponding English translation of the Bangla text.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

To ensure the quality and consistency of the Bangla and English alternative texts and images, the three authors conducted group discussions to generate appropriate descriptions and validate the collected images. During this process, multiple candidate descriptions were collaboratively reviewed and refined, and any incorrect, ambiguous, or irrelevant alternative texts were excluded from the dataset. Additionally, only sharp and well-focused images were retained. A structured data collection process was carried out using a Google Form to gather images along with their corresponding Bangla and English alternative texts. The responses were automatically stored in an Excel file for further processing and organization. To validate annotation quality, the dataset was evaluated by four faculty members with relevant academic expertise, who assessed the Bangla and English alternative texts in terms of correctness and relevance. Based on their feedback, necessary corrections were made and low-quality annotations were removed from the final dataset. Furthermore, the authors conducted an experimental framework to demonstrate the applicability of the proposed dataset. From each class, 50 samples were randomly selected, and the faculty members also evaluated the generated texts (produced by the deep learning model) associated with the images.

The overall methodology of this research consists of two main components: (a) Data validation and quality assurance; and (b) Bangla and English alternative text generation using a Vision-Language Transformer framework. Bangla and English alternative text generation using a Vision-Language Transformer framework is composed of two parts:a.Visual Feature Extraction using Vision Encoder: To establish a semantic representation of the images, a Vision Transformer (ViT) was used as the visual encoder. Each image in the dataset was first preprocessed through resizing, normalization, and conversion into tensor format before being passed into the ViT encoder. The encoder then extracted high-dimensional feature embeddings that effectively captured important visual characteristics such as object shape, texture, composition, and contextual information. These extracted embeddings served as a compact yet informative semantic representation of the input images, which were subsequently provided to a lightweight GPT-2 decoder to enable conditional text generation.b.Bangla and English alternative Text Generation using GPT2 Decoder: The Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder is integrated with a GPT-2–based transformer decoder using a VisionEncoderDecoderModel configuration, enabling the system to generate Bangla and English alternative text directly from visual features as a complete vision–language pipeline. In the input preparation stage, images are converted into pixel tensors using an AutoImageProcessor, while the Bangla and English alternative texts from the dataset (alt_text_bn column) are tokenized using an AutoTokenizer. During the forward pass, the visual embeddings produced by the ViT encoder are passed to the GPT-2 decoder, which generates token sequences conditioned on the image features.

An initial deep learning framework has been implemented to automatically generate Bangla and English alternative text from images. This framework employs a VisionEncoderDecoder architecture, combining a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder with a GPT-2 based decoder, enabling end-to-end image-to-text generation in Bangla. The system serves as a proof-of-concept, demonstrating the feasibility of Bangla cultural image caption generation and providing a baseline for future improvements. The performance of the framework was evaluated using standard metrics commonly used in text generation and image captioning tasks. BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy) measures the n-gram overlap between generated captions and reference Bangla and English captions, indicating syntactic and lexical similarity. ROUGE-L (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation - Longest Common Subsequence) measures the longest common subsequence between predicted and reference captions, capturing semantic similarity and partial matches. CIDEr (Consensus-based Image Description Evaluation) assesses the similarity of generated captions to multiple reference captions, reflecting the human-likeness and overall quality of the generated descriptions.

The proposed framework was developed to generate alternative texts in both Bangla and English. Accordingly, the evaluation results presented in Table 3 reflect the overall performance of the model across both languages, rather than being limited to Bangla only. The reported metric ranges (BLEU: 0.34, ROUGE-L: 0.40, CIDEr: 0.38) indicate moderate performance, suggesting that the generated texts capture a reasonable level of semantic relevance and contextual similarity with the reference descriptions. Limitations

Despite its contribution to Bangla and English multimodal research, this dataset has certain limitations. The image-text pairs are limited to selected categories of Bangla cultural products, which may not fully represent the broader diversity of cultural artifacts, regional variations, or real-world visual contexts. Additionally, the alternative texts are written in formal Bangla; regional dialects of the Bangla language are not included in this dataset. This scale may limit the generalizability of models trained on the dataset, particularly when applied to unseen domains or more diverse visual–linguistic scenarios. Future extensions of the dataset, both in size and cultural coverage, could further enhance its usability for multilingual vision–language tasks.

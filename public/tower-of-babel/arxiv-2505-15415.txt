1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. The
Calabi functional
is defined as

C(\omega_{g})=\int s_{g}^{2}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!},

where
s_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
\omega_{g}
.
It is well-known that extremal Kähler metrics, introduced by Calabi in the 1980s (
[
9
]
),
are critical points of the Calabi functional within the class
[\omega_{g}]\in H^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
.
A key characterization is that a Kähler metric is extremal
if and only if the gradient of its scalar curvature is a holomorphic vector field.
When the manifold admits no nontrivial holomorphic vector fields, extremal metrics
are exactly Kähler metrics with constant scalar curvature (cscK).
The foundational work of
[
3
]
further characterizes extremal metrics
through Hamiltonian 2-forms and stability, linking their existence to geometric and algebraic invariants.

The search for extremal metrics is deeply intertwined with geometric stability.
For cscK metrics, the Yau-Tian-Donaldson conjecture establishes an equivalence
between their existence and the algebro-geometric notion of K-stability.
While extremal metrics similarly relate to a modified stability condition,
their existence also depends on the vanishing of obstructions such as the Futaki
invariant (
[
22
]
), which measures the imbalance of the
manifold’s automorphism group. For extremal metrics,
this invariant must vanish along certain holomorphic vector fields, generalizing the
cscK case. Calabi conjectured that extremal metrics exist precisely when such
obstructions are overcome, though a full resolution remains open. Examples of extremal metrics abound in symmetric spaces, such as Fubini-Study metrics on projective varieties. However, non-cscK extremal metrics often arise on manifolds with continuous symmetries, such as Hirzebruch surfaces or blow-ups of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, as studied in the context of self-dual Einstein-Hermitian structures and almost-Hermitian geometry (
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
). These cases illustrate how extremal metrics balance curvature variations with the underlying complex structure, offering a richer landscape than cscK metrics. Analytically, extremal metrics satisfy a fourth-order nonlinear PDE, making their study more challenging than the second-order Einstein equations. Progress often relies on perturbative methods, geometric flows, or toric geometry techniques in the presence of symmetry. For this comprehensive topic, we refer to
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,

,

,
23
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
and the references therein.

Recent advancements extend these ideas to non-Kähler settings. Works such as
[
1
,
2
,
25
]
address the Chern-Yamabe problem and Chern-Einstein metrics on Hermitian manifolds, while
[
7
]
explores second Chern-Einstein metrics on almost-Hermitian 4-manifolds. The interplay between Gauduchon curvature and Hermitian geometry, as in
[
8
,
20
,
21
,
27
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,

]
, further enriches this framework. These developments highlight the broader landscape of scalar curvature geometry, connecting classical Kähler theory to non-Kähler geometric analysis.

In this paper, we generalize Calabi’s energy functional to Hermitian manifolds and study the existence of extremal Hermitian metrics with constant Chern scalar curvature–a non-Kähler analogue of cscK metrics. Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with complex dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
\omega_{g}=\sqrt{-1}g_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}
and
s_{g}
be the Chern scalar curvature. For any
p>1
, we define the
p
-Calabi functional
as

(1.1)

C_{p}(\omega_{g})=\left(\int|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}}.

This functional is normalized to ensure scale invariance:
C_{p}(\lambda\omega_{g})=C_{p}(\omega_{g})
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.
A Hermitian metric
\omega_{g}
is called a
p
-conformal extremal metric
if it is a critical point of
C_{p}
within its conformal class, satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}C_{p}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})=0

for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Our first main result establishes the Euler-Lagrange equation characterizing these critical metrics, generalizing Calabi’s extremal condition to non-Kähler geometry:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. Then
\omega_{g}
is
p
-conformal extremal
if and only if the following equation holds in the sense of distributions

(1.3)

\square_{g}^{*}\left(s_{g}|s_{g}|^{p-2}\right)=\frac{n-p}{np}\left(|s_{g}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)

where
\square_{g}^{*}
is the formal adjoint of the operator
\square_{g}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
on smooth functions.

It is straightforward to verify that equation (
1.3
) constitutes a fourth-order nonlinear partial differential equation governing the metric tensor

g
within the framework of Riemannian geometry. However, the current literature offers relatively few comprehensive results on the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions for such equations. Motivated by these challenges, we restrict our attention to the case
p=n
where the
n
-conformal extremal metric satisfies a homogeneous equation

(1.4)

\square_{g}^{*}(s_{g}|s_{g}|^{n-2})=0.

This structural simplification permits the application of elliptic regularity theory and geometric energy methods, while the homogeneity condition eliminates certain obstructions arising from lower-order terms, thereby enabling a more tractable analysis of the system’s global behavior.Consequently, we establish that equation (
1.4
) has a unique solution in the conformal class of any Hermitian structure.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. Then there exists a unique (up to scaling) smooth Hermitian metric
\omega_{E}
in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
such that
\omega_{E}
is an
n
-conformal extremal metric, i.e.,
\omega_{E}
satisfies equation (
1.4
). Moreover,
\omega_{E}
minimizes the functional
C_{n}
in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
, i.e.,

(1.5)

C_{n}(\omega_{E})=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})}C_{n}(e^{f}\omega_{g}).

Crucially, the fourth-order system (
1.4
) decouples into two compatible second-order equations, whose structural compatibility ensures solvability by elliptic PDE theory. Within any conformal class

\omega_{g}
, Gauduchon’s theorem guarantees a unique metric
\omega_{G}
(up to scaling) satisfying
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{G}^{n-1}=0
. The extremal metric

\omega_{E}

solving (
1.4
) and the Gauduchon metric
\omega_{G}

generally diverge; their distinction arises from the former’s dependence on fourth-order equation versus the latter’s second-order elliptic PDE. A concrete example illustrating this non-identity is provided below.

Example 1.3
.

Let
M={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\sharp\overline{2{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
. It is well-known that
M
has
c_{1}(M)>0
and does not admit Kähler-Einstein metrics. Let
\omega_{g}\in c_{1}(M)
be a Kähler metric. Let
\omega_{E}
be the Hermitian metric minimizes the
n
-conformal Calabi functional in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
. Then
\omega_{E}
is not a Gauduchon metric. Otherwise, it is easy to see that
\omega_{E}=\omega_{g}
up to scaling. Moreover, it satisfies

(1.6)

\square_{g}^{*}s_{g}=0.

Since
\omega_{g}
is Kähler, by maximum principle,
s_{g}
is constant. Since
[\omega_{g}]=c_{1}(M)
,
\omega_{g}
is actually Kähler-Einstein. This is absurd.

Furthermore, we establish a sharp criterion for

\omega_{E}=\omega_{G}
: equality holds precisely when the scalar curvature of

\omega_{E}
is (nonzero) constant.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
\omega_{E}
be the
n
-conformal extremal metric in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
.

(1)

If
\omega_{E}
is Gauduchon, then it has constant Chern scalar curvature.

(2)

If
\omega_{E}
has constant nonzero Chern scalar curvature, then
\omega_{E}
is Gauduchon.

Observe that if
\omega_{g}

is a Hermitian metric with vanishing Chern scalar curvature, it satisfies

C_{n}(\omega_{g})=0
, thereby globally minimizing the functional
C_{n}
. Such a metric constitutes an
n
-conformal extremal metric. However, this metric fundamentally differs from a Gauduchon metric
\omega_{G}
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
M_{1}={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\sharp\overline{2{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
and
\omega_{1}\in c_{1}(M_{1})
be a Kähler metric. Let
M_{2}=\Sigma_{2}
be a Riemann surface with genus
2
and
\omega_{2}
be the canonical metric with
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{2})=-\omega_{2}
. Suppose that
M=M_{1}\times M_{2}
and
\omega_{g}=2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
which is a Kähler metric in the class
2c_{1}(M_{1})-c_{1}(M_{2})
. A straightforward computation shows that

(1.7)

\int_{M}c_{1}(M)\wedge\omega_{g}^{2}=0.

One can see that there exists some
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\omega_{E}=e^{f}\omega_{g}
has zero Chern scalar curvature (
[
35
, Theorem 1.2]
). We claim that
\omega_{E}
can not be Gauduchon. Otherwise,
\omega_{E}=\omega_{g}
up to scaling and so
\omega_{g}=2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
is a Kähler metric with zero scalar curvature. This implies that
\omega_{1}
has positive constant scalar curvature. This is a contradiction.

While the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions to the
p
-conformal extremal equation (
1.3
) remain unresolved, we establish certain results analogous to those in Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
\omega_{p}
be a
p
-conformal extremal metric in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
with
p\geq 2
.

(1)

If
\omega_{p}
is Gauduchon and
(n-p)s_{p}\geq 0
, then it has constant Chern scalar curvature.

(2)

If
\omega_{p}
has constant nonzero Chern scalar curvature, then
\omega_{p}
is Gauduchon.

We also obtain a criterion for
p
-conformal extremal metrics, which is analogous to the classical result in the Kähler setting.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. If
\omega_{g}
is Gauduchon and has constant Chern scalar curvature, then it is
p
-conformal extremal for any
p>1
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold with complex dimension
n
. For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
, the complex Laplacian
\square_{g}f
of
f
is defined as

(2.1)

\square_{g}f:=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
.

(1)

One has

(2.2)

\overline{\partial}^{*}(f\omega_{g})=f\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega_{g}+\sqrt{-1}\partial f,\ \ \ \sqrt{-1}\langle\overline{\partial}f,\partial^{*}\omega_{g}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f+\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}f.

(2)

For any
(1,0)
form
\eta
,

(2.3)

\partial^{*}(f\eta)=f\partial^{*}\eta-\langle\eta,\partial f\rangle.

Proof.

For any smooth
(1,0)
-form
\eta\in\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)
,
we have the global inner product

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial}^{*}(f\omega_{g}),\eta\right)=\left(f\omega_{g},\overline{\partial}\eta\right)=\left(\omega_{g},f\overline{\partial}\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{g},\overline{\partial}(f\eta)\right)-\left(\omega_{g},\overline{\partial}f\wedge\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega_{g},\eta\right)-\left(\omega_{g},\overline{\partial}f\wedge\eta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f\overline{\partial}^{*}\omega_{g},\eta\right)+\sqrt{-1}\left(\partial f,\eta\right)

where the last identity follows from the fact that
f
is real
valued. The second part of
(1)
can be proved in a similar way. For any smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{*}(f\eta),\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f\eta,\partial\varphi\right)=\left(\eta,f\partial\varphi\right)=\left(\eta,\partial(f\varphi)-\partial f\cdot\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f\partial^{*}\eta,\varphi\right)-\left(\langle\eta,\partial f\rangle,\varphi\right)

and we obtain (
2.3
). ∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The formal adjoint
\square_{g}^{*}
of
\square_{g}
is given by

(2.4)

\square_{g}^{*}u=\left(-\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{g}\right)\cdot u-\Delta_{d}u-\square_{g}u,

where
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\Delta_{d}u=d^{*}du=\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}u+\partial^{*}\partial u
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

(2.5)

\square_{g}u=\sqrt{-1}\langle\overline{\partial}u,\partial^{*}\omega_{g}\rangle-\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}u.

For any
v\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
, one has

(2.6)

(\square_{g}u,v)=\left(\sqrt{-1}\langle\overline{\partial}u,\partial^{*}\omega_{g}\rangle-\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}u,v\right)\\
=\sqrt{-1}(\overline{\partial}u,v\partial^{*}\omega_{g})-(u,\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}v).

Moreover,

(2.7)

\sqrt{-1}(\overline{\partial}u,v\partial^{*}\omega_{g})=(u,-\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}(v\partial^{*}\omega_{g}))

and by using Lemma
2.1
again, we obtain

(2.8)

\overline{\partial}^{*}(v\partial^{*}\omega_{g})=v\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{g}-\langle\partial^{*}\omega_{g},\overline{\partial}v\rangle=v\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{g}-\sqrt{-1}(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v+\partial^{*}\partial v).

This gives formula (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a Hermitian manifold and
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
s_{f}
be the Chern scalar curvature of
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega_{g}
. Then

(2.9)

s_{f}=e^{-f}\left(s_{g}-n\square_{g}f\right)

where
s_{g}
is the Chern scalar curvature of
\omega_{g}
.

Proof.

It follows from a straightforward computation.
Let
\Theta_{f}
be the Chern curvature of
\omega_{f}
. Then one can compute that

(2.10)

\left(\Theta_{f}\right)_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}=e^{f}\left(\Theta_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}-g_{k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{i}\partial\overline{z}^{j}}\right)

and

(2.11)

s_{f}=h_{f}^{i\overline{j}}h_{f}^{k\overline{\ell}}\left(\Theta_{f}\right)_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}=e^{-f}\left(s_{g}-n\square_{g}f\right)

where
h^{i\overline{j}}_{f}=e^{-f}g^{i\overline{j}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega_{g}
for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\square_{f}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
, then for any
u\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
,

(2.12)

\square_{f}u=e^{-f}\square_{g}u,\quad\square_{f}^{*}u=e^{-nf}\square_{g}^{*}(e^{(n-1)f}u).

Proof.

Indeed, we have

(2.13)

\square_{f}u=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u=e^{-f}\square_{g}u.

For any
v\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
,

\displaystyle\int v\cdot\square_{f}^{*}u\cdot\frac{\omega_{f}^{n}}{n!}=\int u\cdot\square_{f}v\cdot\frac{\omega_{f}^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int ue^{(n-1)f}\square_{g}v\cdot\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int ve^{-nf}\square_{g}^{*}(e^{(n-1)f}u)\cdot\frac{\omega_{f}^{n}}{n!}.

Hence,
\square_{f}^{*}u=e^{-nf}\square_{g}^{*}(e^{(n-1)f}u)
. ∎

3.
Proofs of main theorems

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By using Lemma
2.3
, we have

(3.1)

C_{p}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})=\left(\int e^{(n-p)tf}|s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int e^{ntf}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}}.

When
p>1
, one can see clearly that
C_{p}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})
is differentiable with respect to
t
. A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}C_{p}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})=A_{1}(t)+A_{2}(t)+A_{3}(t),

where
A_{1}(t)
,
A_{2}(t)
and
A_{3}(t)
are given by

\displaystyle A_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle(n-p)\left(\int fe^{(n-p)tf}\left|s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f\right|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int e^{ntf}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}},

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle-np\left(\int e^{(n-p)tf}\cdot\square_{g}f\cdot\left(s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f\right)\cdot\left|s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f\right|^{p-2}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int e^{ntf}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}},

\displaystyle A_{3}(t)=

\displaystyle-(n-p)\left(\int e^{(n-p)tf}\left|s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f\right|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int fe^{ntf}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\int e^{ntf}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}-1}.

When
t=0
, one has

\displaystyle A_{1}(0)=

\displaystyle(n-p)\left(\int f|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}},

\displaystyle A_{2}(0)=

\displaystyle-np\left(\int\square_{g}f\cdot s_{g}\cdot|s_{g}|^{p-2}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}},

\displaystyle A_{3}(0)=

\displaystyle-(n-p)\left(\int|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int f\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n-p)\left(\intbar|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int f\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}}.

Therefore one obtains

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}C_{p}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})=

\displaystyle\left(\int F\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)\left(\int\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{n}},

where

(3.2)

F=(n-p)f\left(|s_{g}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega^{n}_{g}}{n!}\right)-np\square_{g}f\cdot s_{g}\cdot|s_{g}|^{p-2}.

Since
p>1
, the function
s_{g}\cdot|s_{g}|^{p-2}
is continuous on
M
. Hence, we obtain the Euler-Lagrange equation in the sense of distributions

(3.3)

\square_{g}^{*}\left(s_{g}|s_{g}|^{p-2}\right)=\frac{n-p}{np}\left(|s_{g}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
[\omega_{g}]
be the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
. It is well-known that there exists a unique (up to scaling) Gauduchon metric
\omega_{G}\in[\omega_{g}]
, i.e.,
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega_{G}^{n-1}=0
. Let
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega_{G}
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\square_{G}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{G}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
and
\square_{f}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{f}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
. Let
s_{G}
be the Chern scalar curvature of
\omega_{G}
and
s_{f}
be the Chern scalar curvature of
\omega_{f}
. By Theorem
1.1
,
\omega_{f}
is
n
-conformal extremal if and only if

(3.4)

\square_{f}^{*}(s_{f}|s_{f}|^{n-2})=0.

We shall show that there exists a unique (up to additive constants) function
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
satisfying (
3.4
). Indeed, by using formula
(
2.12
), we obtain

(3.5)

\square_{f}^{*}\left(s_{f}|s_{f}|^{n-2}\right)=e^{-nf}\square_{G}^{*}\left(e^{(n-1)f}s_{f}|s_{f}|^{n-2}\right).

By (
2.9
), one has
s_{f}=e^{-f}\left(s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right)
and so

(3.6)

\square_{f}^{*}\left(s_{f}|s_{f}|^{n-2}\right)=e^{-nf}\square_{G}^{*}\left(\left(s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right)\left|s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right|^{n-2}\right)=0.

When
\omega_{G}
is Gauduchon, by formula (
2.4
), equation (
3.6
) is equivalent to

(3.7)

\left(\Delta_{d,G}+\square_{G}\right)\left(\left(s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right)\left|s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right|^{n-2}\right)=0.

Let
\varphi=\left(s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right)\left|s_{G}-n\square_{G}f\right|^{n-2}.

We shall show that
\left(\Delta_{d,G}+\square_{G}\right)\varphi=0
if and only if
\varphi
is a constant. Indeed, one can see clearly that

\displaystyle\int\varphi\square_{G}\varphi\frac{\omega_{G}^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{2}\wedge\frac{\omega_{G}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}-\int 2\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi\wedge\overline{\partial}\varphi\wedge\frac{\omega_{G}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int|\partial\varphi|^{2}\frac{\omega_{G}^{n}}{n!}.

Hence

(3.8)

0=\int\varphi\left(\Delta_{d,G}\varphi+\square_{G}\varphi\right)\frac{\omega_{G}^{n}}{n!}=\int|\overline{\partial}\varphi|^{2}\frac{\omega_{G}^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore,
\varphi
is constant and there exists some
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(3.9)

s_{G}-n\square_{G}f=C.

Since
\omega_{G}
is Gauduchon, it is easy to see that

(3.10)

C=\intbar s_{G}\frac{\omega_{G}^{n}}{n!}.

Now equation (
3.9
) can be written as

(3.11)

\displaystyle\square_{G}f=\psi,\quad\text{with}\quad\psi=\frac{1}{n}\left(s_{G}-C\right).

Since
\int\psi\cdot\omega_{G}^{n}/n!=0
, equation (
3.11
) has a unique solution (up to additive constants)
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Thereofore, there exists a unique (up to scaling)
n
-conformal extremal metric
\omega_{f}=e^{f}\omega_{G}
in the conformal class
[\omega_{g}]
.

On the other hand, it is easy to compute that

(3.12)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}C_{n}(e^{tf}\omega_{g})=n^{3}(n-1)\int\left(\square_{g}f\right)^{2}\left|s_{g}-nt\square_{g}f\right|^{n-2}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\geq 0.

Hence, if
\omega_{E}
is a critical point of the functional

C_{n}
, then it is a minimizer.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
s_{E}
be the Chern scalar curvature of the
n
-conformal extremal metric
\omega_{E}
.

(1)

If
\omega_{E}
is Gauduchon, by using formula (
2.4
) equation (
1.4
) is reduced to

(3.13)

\left(\Delta_{d,E}+\square_{E}\right)\left(s_{E}|s_{E}|^{n-2}\right)=0.

By using a similar maximum principle as shown in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
s_{E}|s_{E}|^{n-2}
is constant and so
s_{E}
is constant.

(2)

If
s_{E}
has constant nonzero Chern scalar curvature, then by formula (
2.4
)

0=\square_{E}^{*}\left(s_{E}|s_{E}|^{n-2}\right)=-\left(\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{E}\right)\cdot\left(s_{E}|s_{E}|^{n-2}\right),

where dual operators are taken with respect to
\omega_{E}
.
Hence,
\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{E}=0
, which is equivalent to
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega^{n-1}_{E}=0
, i.e.,
\omega_{E}
is Gauduchon.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. We denote
\mathscr{F}(\omega_{g})
by the
total scalar curvature
of
\omega_{g}
, i.e.,

(3.14)

\mathscr{F}(\omega_{g})=\int s_{g}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold. Suppose that
\omega_{G}
and
\omega_{E}
are the Gauduchon metric and
n
-conformal extremal metric in the conformal class of
\omega_{g}
respectively.

(1)

\omega_{E}
has positive Chern scalar curvature if and only if
\mathscr{F}(\omega_{G})>0
;

(2)

\omega_{E}
has negative Chern scalar curvature if and only if
\mathscr{F}(\omega_{G})<0
;

(3)

\omega_{E}
has zero Chern scalar curvature if and only if
\mathscr{F}(\omega_{G})=0
.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we know that
\omega_{E}=e^{f}\omega_{G}
where
\omega_{G}
is the Gauduchon metric and
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
satisfies

(3.15)

\displaystyle\square_{G}f=\varphi,\quad\text{with}\quad\varphi=\frac{1}{n}\left(s_{G}-\frac{\mathscr{F}(\omega_{G})}{\int\omega_{G}^{n}}\right).

Thus, the Chern scalar
s_{E}
of
\omega_{E}
satisfies

(3.16)

s_{E}=e^{-f}(s_{G}-n\square_{G}f)=\frac{e^{-f}}{\int\omega_{G}^{n}}\cdot\mathscr{F}(\omega_{G}).

Hence, the conclusions follow.
∎

4.
Some consequences of
p
-conformal extremal metrics

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Recall that a
p
-conformal extremal metric satisfies the following equation in the sense of distributions:

(4.1)

\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)=\frac{n-p}{np}\left(|s_{p}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{p}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}\right)

where
\square_{p}^{*}
is the formal adjoint of
\square_{p}=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{p}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
with respect to
\omega_{p}
. Multipling by
|s_{p}|^{p}
and integrating, one has

(4.2)

\frac{n-p}{np}\int\left(|s_{p}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{p}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{2}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=\int|s_{p}|^{p}\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!},

and the right hand reads

(4.3)

\int|s_{p}|^{p}\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=\int\square_{p}\left(|s_{p}|^{p}\right)\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}.

A straightforward computation shows

(4.4)

\int|s_{p}|^{p}\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=p\int\left(|s_{p}|^{2p-2}\square_{p}s_{p}+(p-1)s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-4}|\partial s_{p}|^{2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}.

For part
(1)
, if
p=n
, it follows from Theorem
1.4
. When
p\neq n
, since
\omega_{p}
is Gauduchon, one has

(4.5)

\int\square_{p}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=0.

On the other hand, we compute that

\int\square_{p}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=(2p-1)\int\left(|s_{p}|^{2p-2}\square_{p}s_{p}+2(p-1)s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-4}|\partial s_{p}|^{2}\right)\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}.

In particular, we have

(4.6)

\int|s_{p}|^{2p-2}\square_{p}s_{p}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=-2(p-1)\int s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-4}|\partial s_{p}|^{2}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(n-p)^{2}}{np}\int\left(|s_{p}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{p}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}\right)^{2}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}=-p(p-1)(n-p)\int s_{p}|s_{p}|^{2p-2}|\partial s_{p}|^{2}\frac{\omega_{p}^{n}}{n!}.

Since
(n-p)s_{p}\geq 0
, we deduce that the right hand side is non-positive. Hence
s_{p}
is a constant.

For
(2)
, if
s_{p}
has constant nonzero Chern scalar curvature, then (
1.3
) is reduced to

(4.7)

\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)=0

and by formula (
2.4
), we also have

\square_{p}^{*}\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right)=-\left(\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{p}\right)\cdot\left(s_{p}|s_{p}|^{p-2}\right),

where dual operators are taken with respect to
\omega_{p}
. Hence,
\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial^{*}\omega_{p}=0
, i.e.,
\omega_{p}
is Gauduchon.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

If
\omega_{g}
is Gauduchon and
s_{g}
is constant, then for any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\square_{g}^{*}\left(s_{g}|s_{g}|^{p-2}\right)=\frac{n-p}{np}\left(|s_{g}|^{p}-\intbar|s_{g}|^{p}\frac{\omega_{g}^{n}}{n!}\right)=0.

Hence
\omega_{g}
is a
p
-conformal extremal metric.
∎

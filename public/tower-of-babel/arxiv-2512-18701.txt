Nonlocal conservation laws with p p -norm, the singular limit problem and applications to traffic flow
Felisia Angela Chiarello, Alexander Keimer, Lukas Pflug

Abstract
In this contribution, we study scalar nonlocal conservation laws with the p p -norm. Here, “nonlocal” means that the velocity of the conservation law depends on an integral term in space. Typically, the nonlocal term consists of integrating the solution in L 1 L^{1} , whereas here we will study the case when the solution is integrated in the L p L^{p} -norm. We consider even the case of the L p L^{p} metric when p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) and establish, for an initial datum which is uniformly bounded away from zero, the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. We then demonstrate that there are also solutions to the initial datum being zero under more restrictive assumptions. Furthermore, we investigate the singular limit, i.e., what happens when the nonlocal kernel converges to a Dirac distribution. Indeed, for the one-sided exponential kernel, we recover the (entropy) solution of the corresponding local conservation law for all p ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) p\in(0,\infty) with further restrictions for p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) . This generalizes the celebrated singular limit result for nonlocal conservation laws for p = 1 p=1 significantly and showcases the robustness of the approximation of local conservation laws by nonlocal ones. We investigate also the monotonicity of the solution when assuming that the initial datum is monotone. Finally, we prove the convergence of solutions for p → 0 p\rightarrow 0 on a small time horizon, resulting in a different kind of nonlocal conservation law. Numerical studies showcasing the effect of p p on the singular limit convergence and more conclude the contribution.

As, in both cases

the time-derivative of the total variation is less than or equal to zero, we obtain

\big|W_{\eta}^{p}(t,\cdot)\big|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}\leq\big|W_{\eta}^{p}(0,\cdot)\big|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}.

Recalling the exponential operator, we have, for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}(0,x)\big|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\partial_{x}\tfrac{p}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)q_{0}(y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\tfrac{p}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}q_{0}(y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tfrac{p}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\big|\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}q_{0}(y)^{p}\big|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}q_{0}(y)^{p}\big|\tfrac{p}{\eta}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=|q_{0}^{p}|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}\leq\begin{cases}p\|q_{0}\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|q_{0}|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}&\text{ for }p\geq 1\\
p\|\tfrac{1}{q_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-p}|q_{0}|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}&\text{ for }p\leq 1\end{cases}

which is uniform in
\eta
as claimed and depends only on the
L^{\infty}
-norm and
TV
-semi-norm of
q_{0}
, which is bounded as guaranteed in
2.3
.
∎

Remark 3.6

(

Corollary 3.7

(Compactness of

Under the assumptions of

i.e., the set

C\big([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega)\big)

Proof.

We consider the Banach space
L^{1}(\Omega)
with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
open bounded and, for

, the set of functions

F(t)\coloneqq\{W_{\eta}^{p}(t,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\Omega),\,\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}.

This set is, according to the classical argument that
BV
is compactly embedded in
L^{1}
as stated in
[
58

for all

thanks to the uniform (in

.
According to
[

Let
(t_{1},t_{2})\in[0,T]^{2}
be given and assume, without loss of generality, that

. We obtain

\displaystyle\big\|W_{\eta}(t_{1},\cdot)^{p}-W_{\eta}(t_{2},\cdot)^{p}\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}|\partial_{t}W_{\eta}(s,x)^{p}|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}x

using

,

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\big|V\big(W_{\eta}(s,x)\big)\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(s,x)^{p}\big|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\big|V^{\prime}\big(W_{\eta}(s,y)\big)\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p}\big|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p(p-1)}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\big|V^{\prime}\big(W_{\eta}(s,y)\big)W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p-1}\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)\eta\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)\big|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Exchanging the order of integration in the second and third term, rearranging terms and pulling out

uniformly (which is possible thanks to the maximum principle in

\displaystyle\leq\|V\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}|t_{2}-t_{1}|\big|W_{\eta}^{p}\big|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);TV(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p}{\eta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p}\big|W_{\eta}(s,y)\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{|p-1|}{\eta}\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p}\big||\eta\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp\big(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta}\big)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\|V\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}|t_{2}-t_{1}|\big|W_{\eta}^{p}\big|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);TV(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p}\big|W_{\eta}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{|p-1|}{p}\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)^{p}\big||\eta\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(s,y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

(21)

\displaystyle\quad+\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}|t_{2}-t_{1}|\|W_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\big|W_{\eta}^{p}\big|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);TV(\mathbb{R}))}

(22)

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{|p-1|}{p}\|V^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}|t_{2}-t_{1}|\|\eta\partial_{2}W_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\big|W_{\eta}^{p}\big|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);TV(\mathbb{R}))}.

(23)

According to

, the total variation on

is uniformly bounded in
\eta
.
Thanks to the maximum principle in

∎

3.2
The corresponding local conservation law and results on existence and uniqueness

As we aim for the singular limit convergence, we first state against which solution we expect convergence to. Looking into the nonlocal dynamics in
Eq.

1
, it becomes apparent that, when the nonlocal kernel converges to a Dirac distribution, we formally end up with the following (local) conservation law.

Definition 3.9

(The corresponding local conservation laws)
.

We call the following local conservation law subject to the velocity
V\in W^{1,\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
and the initial condition
q_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap TV(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
2.3

\displaystyle q_{t}(t,x)+\partial_{x}\big(V\big(q(t,x)\big)q(t,x)\big)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}},

(24)

\displaystyle q(0,x)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(x),

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}.

the
corresponding
(to the nonlocal) local conservation law.

The theory on local conservation laws is quite extensive, and in the following we present some rather standard results concerning existence and uniqueness and proper definition of entropy solutions which we require in our later established analysis.
We start with weak solutions although they are not necessarily unique.

Definition 3.10

(Weak solutions for local conservation laws)
.

We call, for
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the function
q\in C\big([0,T];L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\big)
a weak solution to the Cauchy problem in
3.9
given that
V\in W^{1,\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
and the initial condition
q_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap TV(\mathbb{R})
iff
\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R})
it holds that

\displaystyle\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V\big(q(t,x)\big)\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(25)

As weak solutions for local conservation laws are typically not unique, an additional condition is prescribed which is usually referred to as entropy condition:

Definition 3.11

(Entropy condition for the local conservation law.)
.

Let us consider for the local conservation law as in
3.9

\alpha\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})\text{ convex, }\beta^{\prime}\equiv\alpha^{\prime}f^{\prime}\text{ with }f\equiv(\cdot)V(\cdot)\text{ on }\mathbb{R}.

(26)

Then, we call for
\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
and
q\in C\big([0,T];L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\big)
, the expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,\alpha,q]\coloneqq\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\alpha\big(q(t,x)\big)\varphi_{t}(t,x)+\beta\big(q(t,x)\big)\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\big(q_{0}(x)\big)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

the
entropy condition
.
Furthermore,
q\in C\left([0,T];L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\right)
is called an entropy solution of the local conservation law in
3.9
if it satisfies

\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,\alpha,q]\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}\left((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right),\ \forall(\alpha,\beta)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})\text{ as in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:entropy_flux_pair}}.

(27)

With the entropy condition in hand, we recall a well known result for local conservation laws: the existence and uniqueness of entropy solutions.

Additionally, if the flux

is strictly concave, uniqueness is guaranteed if
Eq.

27
holds for
one
strictly convex entropy
\alpha
(and corresponding

Proof.

Regarding the existence of a unique weak entropy solution for the local problem

Proof.

3.11
with smooth initial datum and, as a result, smooth solution, which is due to
3.1
being possible and there being no restriction. Then, we will be dealing with classical solutions, and, suppressing the dependency on the smoothing parameter, we obtain for

\displaystyle\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,\alpha,q_{\eta}]

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\alpha\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\varphi_{t}(t,x)+\beta\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\big(q_{0}(x)\big)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

An integration by parts, recalling that most boundary evaluations cancel due to the assumption on
\varphi
being compactly supported in the spatial variable and zero at
t=T
, yields

\displaystyle=-\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)\Big(\alpha^{\prime}\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{t}q_{\eta}(t,x)+\beta^{\prime}\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{x}q_{\eta}(t,x)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Plugging in the strong form for
\partial_{t}q_{\eta}
in
2.1
and using the identity for
\beta^{\prime}
in
Eq.

26

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)\alpha^{\prime}\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{x}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)q_{\eta}(t,x)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)q_{\eta}(t,x)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for

Choosing next, as entropy,
\alpha\equiv\tfrac{(\cdot)^{mp}}{mp},\ x\in\mathbb{R},
for
mp\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
, yields

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp-1}\partial_{x}\Big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big(V(W_{\eta}(t,x))-V(q_{\eta}(t,x))\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Carrying out the derivative in front of the bracket

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}^{mp-1}(t,x)\partial_{x}q_{\eta}(t,x)\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\partial_{x}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{x}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{mp-1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\partial_{x}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{x}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{x}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{mp-1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp-p}W_{\eta}(t,x)^{p}V^{\prime}\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{\eta(mp-1)}{mp}\!\!\!\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\!\!\!\!\!\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp-p}\Big(W_{\eta}(t,x)^{p-1}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)\Big)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{mp-1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}V^{\prime}\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{x}q_{\eta}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{\eta(mp-1)}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)W_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp-1}\big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)^{2}V^{\prime}\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{mp-1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}V^{\prime}\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\partial_{x}q_{\eta}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

and the third one entirely in

\displaystyle\geq-\tfrac{1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{x}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}(t,x)^{mp}\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{mp}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{x}\varphi(t,x)q_{\eta}^{mp}(t,x)\Big(V\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)-V\big(q_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\overset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{=}0

where the last implication is due to

having compact support, the strong convergence of

in

to the same limit point according to
3.8
and the Lipschitz continuity of

. Altogether, this gives the entropy admissibility of the nonlocal solution in the limit
\eta\rightarrow 0
for the chosen entropy

which is strictly convex on

for

as assumed, so that we can apply

a limit point of a subsequence of

∎

Remark 3.15

The entropy admissibility for

Theorem 3.16

(Entropy admissibility of the nonlocal operator)
.

Let the assumptions of
3.8
hold and assume in addition that
[q_{min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}]\ni x\mapsto xV(x)
is strictly concave and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then, the nonlocal term
W_{\eta}
and the corresponding nonlocal solution

of the nonlocal problem

are entropy admissible for the entropy
\alpha\equiv(\cdot)^{p}
in the limit
\eta\rightarrow 0
, i.e.,

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,(\cdot)^{p},W_{\eta}]\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}\left((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right),\ \text{with $\mathcal{EF}$ as in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:entropy_flux_pair}}.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of
3.16
, we show the entropy admissibility of the nonlocal operator, i.e., the following entropy admissibility (see
Eq.

27
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,\alpha,W_{\eta}]\coloneqq\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\alpha\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\varphi_{t}(t,x)+\beta\big(W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)\varphi_{x}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\big(W_{\eta}(0,x)\big)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

Integrating by parts yields, for the entropy
\alpha^{\prime}(\cdot)\coloneqq(\cdot)^{p-1}
after a multiplication with
p
,

\displaystyle p\mathcal{EF}[\varphi,\alpha,W_{\eta}]

\displaystyle=-\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\Big(\partial_{1}W_{\eta}^{p}+pW_{\eta}^{p-1}\big(V(W_{\eta})+V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}\big)\partial_{2}W_{\eta}\Big)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

and plugging in the expression for
\partial_{1}(W^{p})
in
3.4

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\!\!\!\bigg(V(W_{\eta})\partial_{2}W^{p}_{\eta}+\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y-V(W_{\eta})\partial_{2}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-p(p-1)\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y-pW_{\eta}^{p}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\partial_{2}W_{\eta}\bigg)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\bigg(\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad-p(p-1)\!\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\!\,\mathrm{d}y-pW_{\eta}^{p}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\partial_{2}W_{\eta}\bigg)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

and, with the definition

, we can rewrite the expression to

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\bigg(-\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}F(W_{\eta}(t,x))+\tfrac{p}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W_{\eta}(t,y))\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad-p(p-1)\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Integration by parts in the first term and changing the order of integration in the second term gives

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{2}\varphi F(W_{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\tfrac{p}{\eta}\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W_{\eta}(t,y))\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-p(p-1)\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

and, integrating by parts in the inner integration of the second term,

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\partial_{2}\varphi F(W_{\eta})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W_{\eta})\varphi-\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W_{\eta})\int_{-\infty}^{y}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-p(p-1)\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

which is, after another integration by parts in the second term, equivalent to

\displaystyle=-\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W_{\eta})\int_{-\infty}^{x}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t

(28)

\displaystyle\qquad-p(p-1)\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}^{p-1}\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

(29)

However, as

we can estimate the first term as follows

\bigg|\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}F(W)\int_{-\infty}^{x}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\partial_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leq\big\|F^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{\infty}((0,q_{\max}))}|W_{\eta}|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);TV(\mathbb{R}))}\|\partial_{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{T}})}\tfrac{\eta}{p}.

, while the second term in

. Thus, we have indeed obtained that

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.20

\displaystyle\quad+p(p-1)\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}^{p-1}(t,y)\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{x}W^{p}_{\eta}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\Big(V(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}(t,x)\Big)+\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)W_{\eta}(t,x)^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p^{3}}{\eta^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad-p(p-1)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))W_{\eta}(t,x)^{p-1}\big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}(t,x)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p^{2}(p-1)}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}^{p-1}(t,y)\big(\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p(p-1)}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W_{\eta}^{p}(t,y)\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Assuming that we are minimal at
(t,x)
, i.e.
\partial_{x}^{2}W^{p}(t,x)=0
and
\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)<0

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))W_{\eta}(t,x)\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p}{\eta^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W_{\eta}(t,y)^{p}\Big(pW_{\eta}-(p-1)\eta\partial_{y}W\Big)\,\mathrm{d}y

and as
p>1
and
pW_{\eta}-(p-1)\eta\partial_{y}W_{\eta}\geq 0

\displaystyle\geq-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p}{\eta^{2}}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\Big(pW_{\eta}-(p-1)\eta\partial_{y}W\Big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p(p-1)}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W\,\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating by parts in the first integral term

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\partial_{y}\big(V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))W_{\eta}\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{p(p-1)}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))\partial_{y}W\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})W_{\eta,y}\big(V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,y))+V^{\prime\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}-(p-1)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}\partial_{y}W_{\eta}\big(V^{\prime\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}-(p-2)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\tfrac{-V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))}{W_{\eta}^{p-1}}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}\tfrac{\partial_{y}(W_{\eta}^{p})}{W^{p-1}_{\eta}}\big(V^{\prime\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}-(p-2)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

and for
V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)\geq 0
and
I(q_{0})\coloneqq(q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\subset\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{-V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))}{W_{\eta}^{p-1}}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{\eta}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}\!\!\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{\frac{px-py}{\eta}}\tfrac{1}{W^{p-1}_{\eta}}\big(V^{\prime\prime}(W_{\eta})W_{\eta}-(p-2)V^{\prime}(W_{\eta})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{-V^{\prime}(W_{\eta}(t,x))}{W_{\eta}^{p-1}}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{p}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}\Big\|\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)}{(\cdot)^{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(I(q_{0}))}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}\essinf_{x\in I(q_{0})}|V^{\prime}(x)x^{1-p}|-\tfrac{1}{p}\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}\Big\|\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)}{(\cdot)^{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(I(q_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\Big(\partial_{x}W_{\eta}^{p}\Big)^{2}\bigg(\essinf_{x\in I(q_{0})}|V^{\prime}(x)x^{1-p}|-\tfrac{1}{p}\big\|\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)}{(\cdot)^{p-1}}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I(q_{0}))}\bigg).

Thus, whenever we have

1.

V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)\geq 0
on
[q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}]=I(q_{0})

2.

\essinf_{x\in(q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})}|V^{\prime}(x)x^{1-p}|-\tfrac{1}{p}\Big\|\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)-(p-2)V^{\prime}(\cdot)}{(\cdot)^{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}((q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}>0
,

we have an Oleĭnik estimate as stated for
p>1
.

Let us analyze now the case
0<p<1.
Taking advantage of
Eq.

18
,
we assume that we are minimal at
(t,x)
, i.e.
\partial_{x}^{2}W^{p}(t,x)=0
and
\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)<0
. Then, we can estimate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{x}W^{p}_{\eta}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-V^{\prime}(W)W_{x}\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W)W\partial_{x}W^{p}-(p-1)V^{\prime}(W)\partial_{x}W^{p}\partial_{x}W

\displaystyle\ -\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W)\partial_{y}W^{p}W\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\ +\tfrac{p(p-1)}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W)\partial_{y}W^{p}W_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq-pV^{\prime}(W)W_{x}\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W)W\partial_{x}W^{p}-\tfrac{p^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\partial_{x}W^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})V^{\prime}(W)W\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W)W_{x}\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)+\tfrac{p}{\eta}V^{\prime}(W)W\partial_{x}W^{p}-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W^{p}V^{\prime}(W)W

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}y}\big(V^{\prime}(W)W)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-pV^{\prime}(W)W_{x}\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)-\tfrac{p}{\eta}\partial_{x}W^{p}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\big(V^{\prime\prime}(W)W+V^{\prime}(W))W_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y.

If

\displaystyle\geq-pV^{\prime}(W)W_{x}\partial_{x}W^{p}(t,x)-\tfrac{1}{\eta}(\partial_{x}W^{p})^{2}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{px-py}{\eta})\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(W)W+V^{\prime}(W)}{W^{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}y

Thus, whenever we have

2.

\essinf_{x\in(q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})}\left|\tfrac{V^{\prime}(x)}{x^{p-1}}\right|+\tfrac{1}{p}\Big\|\tfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)(\cdot)+V^{\prime}(\cdot)}{(\cdot)^{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}((q_{\min},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}))}\eqqcolon\kappa>0
,

4
Existence of solutions for initial data not being bounded away from zero

In this section, we will investigate the existence of solutions in the case where the initial datum is not bounded away from zero as assumed before in
Section

2
. As we want to keep the nonlocal operator from becoming zero on compact sets (as it then remains locally Lipschitz-continuous in the spatial variable), we focus only on one-sided nonlocal kernels with
infinite
length of support and initial datum, bounded away from zero close to infinity (and zero close to
-\infty
). Under these conditions, we are able to show the existence of weak solutions. Regarding the uniqueness of solutions, since we can only work on compact domains (as the lower bound of the nonlocal operator does not hold close to
-\infty
), we can only partially use the methodology of
Section

2
which prevents us from proving a general result of uniqueness.

Theorem 4.1

(Existence of solutions for initial data becoming zero)
.

Let
2.3
hold but replace the lower bound on the initial datum, i.e.,
q_{0}\geqq q_{\min}
by either

1.

\exists q_{\min}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\ \exists R_{\text{l}},R_{\text{r}}\in\mathbb{R}:\ \big(q_{0}\geq q_{\min}\ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}_{>R_{\text{r}}}\ \text{a.e.}\big)\ \wedge\ \big(q_{0}\equiv 0\ \text{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{<R_{\text{l}}}\big)

2.

or
\exists q_{\min}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\ K\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ compact}:\ \big(\supp(q_{0})=\mathbb{R}\setminus K\big)\ \wedge\ \big(q_{0}|_{\supp(q_{0})}\geq q_{\min}\big)
.

Assume, in addition, that the nonlocal kernel
\gamma
satisfies
\gamma(x)>0\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, i.e., has infinite support and that
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then, there exists a weak solution
q_{\eta}\in C\big([0,T];L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\big)
in the sense of
2.5
to the nonlocal
p-
norm conservation law as presented in
2.1
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the following min/max principle:

0\leqq q_{\eta}\leqq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\ \text{ on }(t,x)\text{ a.e.}

In the case of
Item

2
, the solution is unique.

Proof.

We start with the first part, i.e.
Item

1
. Let
q_{0}
be given, we consider the following approximation for the initial datum for a given
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

\displaystyle q_{0,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\begin{cases}q_{0}+\varepsilon&x\in\mathbb{R}_{\leq R_{\text{l}}}\\
q_{0},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, for
\eta
fixed, there exists a unique weak solution on any finite time horizon
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
as the initial datum is bounded away from zero except for a compact set. This enables it to apply the fixed-point methods in
2.6
to obtain a unique weak solution
q_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,T];L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
.
We shortly sketch the boundedness away from zero of the nonlocal operator:

\displaystyle W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x)^{p}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\gamma(\tfrac{y-x}{\eta})^{p}q_{\varepsilon}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,R_{\text{r}};T)}^{\infty}\gamma(\tfrac{y-x}{\eta})^{p}q_{\eta,\varepsilon}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq q_{\min}^{p}\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,R_{\text{r}};T)}^{\infty}\!\!\!\gamma(\tfrac{y-x}{\eta})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y=q_{\min}^{p}\|\gamma\|_{L^{p}\big(\big(\frac{\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,R_{\text{r}};T)-x}{\eta},\infty\big)\big)}^{p}

with
\xi
being the characteristics as in
Eq.

7
. This is not uniform in
x\in\mathbb{R}
, as the left hand side converges to zero when
x\rightarrow-\infty
. But, for
x>2R_{\text{l}}
, we have the lower bound

\essinf_{t\in[0,T],\ x\in(2R_{\text{l}},\infty)}W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x)^{p}\geq q_{\min}^{p}\|\gamma\|_{L^{p}\big(\big(\frac{\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,R_{\text{r}};T)-2R_{\text{l}}}{\eta},\infty\big)\big)}^{p}

and, thanks to the assumed monotonicity of
V
,
2.3

\essinf_{t\in[0,T],\ x\in(2R_{\text{l}},\infty)}W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x)\geq q_{\min}\|\gamma\|_{L^{p}\big(\big(\frac{R_{\text{r}}+V(0)T-2R_{\text{l}}}{\eta},\infty\big)\big)}.

Similarly, one can obtain a lower bound on
W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon}]
for
x\leq 2R_{\text{l}}
which can be established as we have assumed the initial datum in this region to be
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. With this in mind, we have the boundedness of
\partial_{2}W_{\eta}
as this boundedness could only be destroyed when the nonlocal operator could approach zero as

\displaystyle\partial_{x}W_{\eta}[\tilde{q}_{\eta,\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{p}W_{p}[\tilde{q}_{\eta,\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x)^{1-p}\bigg(-\tfrac{p}{\eta^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\gamma(\tfrac{y-x}{\eta})^{p-1}\gamma^{\prime}(\tfrac{y-x}{\eta})\tilde{q}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(t,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y-\tfrac{1}{\eta}\gamma(0)^{p}\tilde{q}_{\eta,\varepsilon}(t,x)^{p}\bigg).

Thus, we have a weak solution

q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=q_{0,\varepsilon}\big(\xi_{w_{\varepsilon}}(t,x;0)\big)\partial_{2}\xi_{w_{\varepsilon}}(t,x;0),\ (t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}}.

with
w
being the unique solution to the fixed-point equation in
Eq.

8
,
Eq.

10
.

Now, we claim that we obtain a solution to the problem stated in
Item

1
by defining

q(t,x)=\begin{cases}q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)&t\in[0,T],\ x\in[\xi_{w_{\varepsilon}}(0,x;R_{\text{l}}),\infty)\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}.

The rationality behind this approach lies in the fact that the nonlocal operator only looks “to the right” so that any changes on the left hand side will not affect the solution “on the right”, and thus, as long as we set the solution to zero at this set, the originally built solution is not affected. This is proven rigorously next. Recall the weak formulation of solutions in
2.5
; it holds for
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and

\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R})
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}^{\infty}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

and plugging in the solution formula

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}^{\infty}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(t,x;0))\partial_{2}\xi_{w}(t,x;0)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

and substituting

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{R_{\text{l}}}^{\infty}\Big(\partial_{1}\varphi(t,\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,y;t))+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,\partial_{2}\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,y;t)))\partial_{2}\varphi(t,\xi_{w}(0,y;t))\Big)q_{0,\varepsilon}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{R_{\text{l}}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\varphi(t,\xi_{\varepsilon}(0,y;t))q_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

and, thanks to the assumptions on the test function and the characteristics,

\displaystyle=-\int_{R_{\text{l}}}^{\infty}\varphi(0,y)q_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi_{w}(0,R_{\text{l}};t)}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta}[q_{\varepsilon},\gamma](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{-\infty}^{R_{\text{l}}}q_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now, we can make the last two terms zero if we just set the initial datum in this area to zero as well as the solution. However, this was exactly the previously mentioned construction. Thus, we have built a solution to the initial value problem for the initial datum being bounded away from zero for
x
large and being zero for

small enough, i.e., the case of
Item

1
.

Concerning
Item

2
, we use the fact that, for a given
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},
the nonlocal operator will always be bounded away from zero, uniformly in the spatial variable, so that we can built on the sketched proof in

.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Uniqueness in the zero density case)
.

The fact that we cannot prove uniqueness in
4.1

Item

1
does not mean that uniqueness does not hold. It means, rather, that the classical fixed-point approach does not work in this case as the velocity field generated by the nonlocal operator is not globally Lipschitz in the spatial variable. However, it might be possible that other methods, like semigroup approaches or weaker topologies to consider the fixed-point problem, do work. Currently, it is not clear how we could expect even existence if the initial data can be zero without satisfying either
Item

1
or
Item

2
.

Monotonicity preserving dynamics

In this section, we show that the nonlocal dynamics conserve monotonicity under additional assumptions on the second derivative of
V
. This is well-known for nonlocal conservation laws (see
[
52
, Theorem 4.13 & Theorem 4.18]
and
[
42
, Theorem 5.1]
for the nonlocality in the velocity), and holds in the
p

Theorem 5.1

(Monotonicity of the nonlocal solution)
.

Let
2.3
hold. Assume that
p\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
q_{0}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
satisfy additionally

\displaystyle\Big(p\in(0,1]\ \wedge\ q_{0}(x)\leq q_{0}(y)\text{ for a.e. }x,y\in\mathbb{R},\,x\leq y\wedge V^{\prime\prime}(s)\leq 0\text{ for }s\in(0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\text{ a.e.}\Big)

(30)

\displaystyle\vee

\displaystyle\Big(p\in[1,\infty)\ \wedge\ q_{0}(x)\geq q_{0}(y)\text{ for a.e. }x,y\in\mathbb{R},\,x\leq y\wedge V^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0\text{ for }s\in(0,\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\text{ a.e.}\Big),

and

for monotonically increasing initial data, and

\forall t\in[0,T]

\forall(\eta,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times[0,T]:\,|q_{\eta}(t,\cdot)|_{TV(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

For the exponential kernel
\gamma\equiv\exp(-\cdot)

under the condition in

Proof.

We approximate the initial datum according to
2.6

such that the corresponding solution is sufficiently smooth. Now, we look at the classical solution that satisfies
2.1

Now, let us look into the case in which the initial datum is monotonically increasing, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{2}^{2}W_{\eta}(t,\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=-(p-1)W_{\eta}(t,\tilde{x})^{-p}\partial_{2}W_{\eta}(t,\tilde{x})\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{\tilde{x}}^{\infty}\gamma(\tfrac{y-\tilde{x}}{\eta})^{p}q(t,y)^{p-1}\partial_{y}q(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

and

p\in[1,\infty)
and

.
The stated total variation estimate follows directly from the monotonicity.

and

\displaystyle\partial_{2}^{2}W_{\eta}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-(p-1)W^{-p}_{\eta}\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{x-y}{\eta})^{p}q(t,y)^{p-1}\partial_{y}q(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

(33)

\displaystyle+W^{1-p}_{\eta}\tfrac{p}{\eta^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp(\tfrac{x-y}{\eta})^{p}q(t,y)^{p-1}\partial_{y}q(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y-W^{1-p}_{\eta}\tfrac{p}{\eta}\exp(0)^{p}q(t,x)^{p-1}q_{x}(t,x)

\displaystyle=W^{-p}_{\eta}\left(W_{\eta}\tfrac{p}{\eta}-(p-1)\partial_{x}W_{\eta}\right)\tfrac{1}{\eta}\!\int_{x}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!\exp(\tfrac{x-y}{\eta})^{p}q(t,y)^{p-1}\partial_{y}q(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y-\tfrac{pq^{p-1}}{W^{p-1}_{\eta}\eta}q_{x}(t,x)

using the nonlocal identity (

In particular, at

We performed all these computations thanks to the smoothness of the considered solutions, but the monotonicity is preserved also in the limit for non-smooth initial data according to
2.6
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Monotonicity uniformly in

.

From the previous monotonicity results, it becomes apparent that monotonicity is preserved uniformly in

when taking the exponential kernel in
3.2
. Then, one can pass to the limit for
p\rightarrow\infty
and obtain solutions for the corresponding limit. A similar argument can be made for
p\rightarrow 0
without further restrictions on the kernel. This is further detailed in the following

6
and numerically approximated in

.

6
Limits of the solution for
p\rightarrow 0
and some comments on
p\rightarrow\infty

We look into the limit of the nonlocal conservation law’s solution as
p\rightarrow 0
.
Firstly, we define what we expect to be the limiting weak solution. Interestingly, the equation becomes a different type of nonlocal conservation law involving the integral of a logarithm. This is in line with what is well known from the
p
-quasi-norm behavior for
p\rightarrow 0
(we recall that the triangle inequality is not verified) and is detailed in the following.

Lemma 6.1

(Convergence of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
for
p\rightarrow 0
on bounded
\Omega
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be open and bounded and, for a
q\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\ \forall p\in(0,q]
be given. Then, it holds that

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\exp\bigg(\tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\ln(|f(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)\eqqcolon\|f\|_{L^{0}(\Omega)}.

(34)

Proof.

The result can be found in
[
40
, p. 187, Exercise 8c]
. It can also be found, with a broader perspective on
L^{p}
metric spaces for
p\in(0,1)
in
[
48
, p. 139, 6.8, 1.87, Equation 6.8.1]
.
∎

The right hand side of
Eq.

34
is sometimes also called a geometric mean
[
48
, 6.7.“The geometric mean of a function”]
which becomes clear when taking the counting measure instead of the Lebesgue measure.
It should furthermore be mentioned that writing
\|f\|_{L^{p}}
for
p\in(0,1)
is an abuse of notation insofar as
\|\cdot\|
typically represents a norm. Nevertheless, for convenience we will stick with this notation.

As can be seen in
6.1
, it is crucial to have a bounded
\Omega
. This means that we cannot work with nonlocal kernels with infinite support and need to restrict our analysis to finitely supported kernels.

Definition 6.2

(Weak solution for
p=0
)
.

Let
\gamma
as in
2.3
in addition be compactly supported (without loss of generality on
(0,1)
) and
\gamma\geq\gamma_{\min}\text{ on }(0,1).

Then, we call, for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the following conservation law

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+\partial_{x}\big(V(W_{\eta,0}[q,\gamma_{0}](t,x))q\big)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}}

(35)

\displaystyle q(0,x)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(x),

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}

a nonlocal conservation law for
p=0
where the nonlocal operator is defined by

W_{\eta,0}[q,\gamma_{0}](t,x)\coloneqq\exp\bigg(\tfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{x}^{x+\eta}\ln\big(\big|\gamma_{0}(\tfrac{x-y}{\eta})q(t,y)\big|\big)\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg),\qquad(t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}}

(36)

and (with the definition in
Eq.

34
)

\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\tfrac{\gamma}{\|\gamma\|_{L^{0}((0,1))}}.

Furthermore, we call
q:{\Omega_{T}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a weak solution to
Eq.

35
if

q\in C\big([0,T];L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\big)

for
q_{0}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}_{>q_{\min}})
and it holds for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{\text{c}}((-42,T)\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\iint_{{\Omega_{T}}}\Big(\varphi_{t}(t,x)+V(W_{\eta,0}[q,\gamma_{0}](t,x))\varphi_{x}(t,x)\Big)q(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

The nonlocal conservation law in
Eq.

35
does not fall in the model class analyzed in the previous sections as the nonlinearity in the nonlocal term there is only of the
p
th power, with the
\frac{1}{p}
around the integral which could be compensated “into” the velocity field. In contrast, the present setup has a logarithmic density function.

In
[
43
]
, the authors study a more general nonlinear nonlocal conservation law that can almost be applied to
Eq.

35
.

Lemma 6.3

(Existence and Uniqueness of weak solutions as in
6.2
)
.

Let

Proof.

This result can be found in
[
43

under slightly more restrictive assumptions, i.e.,

and the nonlocal kernel being outside of the nonlinearity. In our setup, we cannot postulate

as we bound the initial datum away from zero so that we do not have a finite

, the nonlinearity in the nonlocal term is only on the solution and not on the kernel. Although this nonlinearity is in the case considered on both the solution and the kernel function, such can also be incorporated into the framework by redefining the kernel. We do not detail it further.
∎

This brings us to the main theorem of this section, i.e., the convergence result for

of solutions to nonlocal conservation laws in the

Theorem 6.4

(Convergence of the nonlocal solution for

To this end, let us consider smooth nonlocal solutions, thanks to

\displaystyle\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\!|\partial_{x}q_{p}(t,x)^{p}|\,\mathrm{d}x

(37)

\displaystyle\quad-p\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sgn(\partial_{x}q_{p}(t,x)^{p})q_{p}^{p}V^{\prime\prime}(W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x))\big(\partial_{x}W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-p\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sgn(\partial_{x}q_{p}(t,x)^{p})q_{p}^{p-1}\partial_{x}^{2}q_{p}V(W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-p\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}q_{p}(t,x)^{p}|V^{\prime}(W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x))\partial_{x}W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-p\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sgn(\partial_{x}q_{p}(t,x)^{p})q_{p}^{p}V^{\prime\prime}(W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x))\big(\partial_{x}W_{p}[q_{p},\gamma_{p}](t,x)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

Remark 6.6

(The case
p=\infty
)
.

Notice that, formally, we obtain for the nonlocal conservation law in the
p=\infty
case the following

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+\partial_{x}\big(V(W_{\eta,\infty}[q,\gamma](t,x))q\big)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}}

(45)

\displaystyle q(0,x)

\displaystyle=q_{0}(x),

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R},

with the nonlocal operator that coincides with the weighted supremum downstream, i.e.,

W_{\eta,\infty}[q,\gamma](t,x)\coloneqq\esssup_{y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq x}}\gamma(\tfrac{x-y}{\eta})q(t,y)=\esssup_{y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\gamma(y)q(t,x+\eta y)=\|\gamma(\cdot)\,q(t,x+\eta\,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})},\ (t,x)\in{\Omega_{T}}.

To the authors’ knowledge, “nonlocal” equations of this type (in the sense that at a given position one potentially takes the density at a different position) have not been studied in literature and it is unclear in which sense solutions might even exist.

One straightforward approach consists of proving the convergence of the solution of the nonlocal
p
-norm model considered in this work converges for
p\rightarrow\infty
in a suitable way, and thus showing the existence of solutions. However, at present we were incapable of proving this, mainly due to the fact that weak star convergence of the solution
q_{p}
or even strong convergence in
L^{1}
do not seem to be sufficient to pass to the limit in the nonlocal term. A rather “primitive” convergence attempt would require uniform convergence of
q_{p}
to a limit which does not hold in general.

Another approach for the
p\rightarrow\infty
case would be taking monotone initial data and using the monotonicity, uniform in
p
, in
Eq.

30
, as the equation then reduces to a local equation
Eq.

45
and the essential supremum is attained either at
x+\eta
or
x
, depending on the type of monotonicity and assuming constant kernel. As this is a quite weak result, we do not detail it further, but only study the convergence numerically.

Finally, one could make an attempt through vanishing viscosity. However, such an approach also only seems to give a uniform
BV
bound, which again would not suffice to pass to the limit.

It is also worth mentioning that an entropy condition for uniqueness is surely required. This can be seen once more for a monotonically decreasing initial datum and the constant kernel as the equation then becomes a classical local conservation law which does not exhibit unique weak solutions for monotonically decreasing initial datum and
V^{\prime}\leqq 0
(i.e., the rarefaction requires additional entropy).

8
Conclusion and open problems

We have established the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions for nonlocal conservation laws with the
p
-norm nonlocality under the restriction on the initial datum (and, as such, the solution) to be bounded away from zero. In addition, for specific not-compactly supported nonlocal kernels, we can get rid of this condition as long as the initial datum is bounded away from zero on a “substantial part” of
\mathbb{R}
. Then, we study the singular limit problem for
\eta\rightarrow 0
and obtain in all cases the convergence to the local entropy solution, generalizing the singular limit convergence result currently available in literature significantly. We also look into the convergence
p\rightarrow 0
(so that, in the limit, we no longer have a norm) and obtain a very interesting limiting nonlocal equation for which we can prove that the solution for
p>0
converges.

The results in this work suggest several new and interesting problems that may be worth future study:

•

Assume that the initial datum can become zero and we add no further restrictions in contrast to the theory outlined in
Section

4
. Do we still obtain solutions? Are these unique?

•

Can we obtain convergence of the solution
q_{p}
for
p\rightarrow\infty
as commented in
6.6
? The resulting conservation law is very different from what has been considered in literature and can be justified also as a reasonable traffic flow model where one would take as density downstream the somewhat most conservative approach breaking as much as possible also if traffic is high further away (taking into account the nonlocal kernel as well, this would be a weighted density). Numerical methods suggest very interesting phanomena worth exploring
Section

7
and
Fig.

4
.

•

Is the
p
-norm of advantage in the singular limit case for multi-D equations as in
[
50
]
?
Is there generally an optimal
p
given an initial datum?
Can one quantify the role of
p
in the singular limit convergence?

•

Can one obtain a singular limit convergence result for any
p\in(0,1)
without further restrictions on kernel (except for being monotone), velocity (except for
V^{\prime}\leqq 0
) and initial datum (except for being nonnegative and
TV
bounded) as in
[
26
]
?

•

Can one take advantage of the recent
[
23
]
approaching the singular limit convergence by means of compensated compactness? Uniform
TV
bounds on the nonlocal operator would then not be required.

Comparative lipidomics and NAD⁺ metabolism in pectoris muscle reveal a lean metabolic phenotype in Daweishan miniature chickens versus arbor acre broilers
Authors: Hao Wu, Min Yang, Junfeng Luo, Jingying Zhao, Zonghui Jian, Jing Fu, Shixiong Yan, Junjing Jia, Lixian Liu, Tengfei Dou, Zhiqiang Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to compare the pectoris muscle chemical composition, NAD⁺-related metabolic regulation, and lipidomic profiles of Daweishan miniature chickens (M3) and Arbor Acre broilers (A3) to characterize breed-dependent metabolic differences. For this, 100 one-day hatched chicks were randomized into two groups (A3 and M3; n = 50/breed/group) by following a completely randomized design and subjected to a 90-day feeding trial under identical conditions. At the 90th day, six birds from each group (containing an equal number of males and females) were slaughtered, and the pectoralis muscle was sampled for subsequent analysis. The chemical composition analysis showed that the M3 group had higher crude protein and ash contents, whereas lower moisture and crude fat contents compared to the A3 group (p < 0.05). Similarly, NAD⁺ and its intermediates (nicotinamide, nicotinamide mononucleotide, and nicotinamide riboside) concentrations were also higher in the M3 group compared to the A3 group (p < 0.05). In addition, the enzymatic activities of SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK were also greater in the M3 group than the A3 group (p < 0.05). Lipidomic profiling analysis showed distinct separation between groups, with triglyceride species downregulation, and fatty acids were upregulated in the M3 group. The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that there was a significant difference in glycerolipid metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and glycerophospholipid pathways across the groups. Correlation analysis revealed a strong association between NAD⁺ metabolites, regulatory enzymes, and differential lipid species. Based on results, the Daweishan miniature chickens maintains a metabolic phenotype characterized by enhanced NAD⁺ metabolism, greater enzymatic regulation, and reduced triglyceride accumulation, while Arbor Acre broilers exhibit a lipid deposition-oriented profile.Keywords: Intermediates of NAD+, Enzymatic regulation, Lipidomics, Triglycerides and fatty acids

Introduction

The poultry sector is a well-known integral segment of the world agricultural setup, contributing to food security through the provision of animal protein (meat & eggs) and to economic stability via employment and income generation (Surai et al., 2019). The poultry industry has made evolutionary progress by developing high-performance commercial broiler meat breeds, such as Arbor Acre, which are the result of intense genetic selection for traits conferring rapid growth and superior feed efficiency (Tallentire et al., 2016). This genetic advancement has substantially increased meat yield and economic returns (Athrey, 2020). However, commercial poultry breeds are more susceptible to disease and sensitive to environmental factors (Kannan et al., 2025). In contrast, several indigenous meat breeds, such as the Daweishan Miniature Chicken, exhibit divergent phenotypes characterized by slower growth kinetics but often greater disease resistance and environmental hardiness (Dou et al., 2017). This phenotypic divergence suggests underlying heterogeneity in metabolic regulation and cellular stress response pathways between commercial and native genetic lines.

The central metabolic nexus, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), governs these physiological outcomes within the cellular pool (Xiao et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2025). NAD+ is a well-known essential coenzyme that serves as a critical redox carrier in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis (Hubert and Athrey, 2022). Additionally, it serves as an obligate cosubstrate for NAD+-consuming enzymes, including SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK (Poljšak et al., 2023), which are key regulators of metabolic homeostasis, genomic stability, inflammatory responses, and cellular adaptation to stress in chicken (Chen et al., 2024). So far, NAD+ levels are intrinsically linked to healthspan and stress resilience across poultry species, and are hallmarks of the production performance and metabolic disease (Groth et al., 2021). Consequently, the bioavailability of NAD+ directly influences capacity for energy production, repair mechanisms, and immune competency of the birds (Surai et al., 2019), thereby linking cellular biochemistry to whole-bird resilience and productivity, particularly reared under intensive commercial rearing setups that impose a confluence of metabolic and environmental stressors (Shastak and Pelletier, 2023). Moreover, high metabolic demand to combat the accelerated muscle growth of broilers imposes significant energetic and biosynthetic burdens (Su et al., 2024), along with the challenges, such as heat stress and pathogen exposure(Groth et al., 2021), further elevate cellular NAD+ demand, particularly for PARP-mediated DNA repair and SIRT-dependent metabolic adaptation (Xie et al., 2020). The NAD+ salvage pathway, primarily from nicotinamide (NAM) and the more efficient precursor nicotinamide riboside (NR), is the dominant route for NAD+ synthesis in birds (Alegre and Pastore, 2023). Recent research demonstrates that dietary supplementation with NAD+ precursors can directly increase tissue NAD+ concentrations in poultry (Wu et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021). The above findings reveal that NAD+ bioavailability is a modifiable trait influencing the trade-off between production efficiency and physiological resilience. Moreover, a study by Shastak and Pelletier(Shastak and Pelletier, 2023)found that different types stressors (originated from either environmental or intrinsic factors) have potential to deplete NAD+ pools, creating a metabolic bottleneck that compromises the growth and health of the birds. A study by (Nadaf et al., 2009) showed divergent mitochondrial function and stress signaling between high- and low-growth poultry breeds. The comparative resilience of certain native poultry breeds may be partially attributable to more robust or efficient NAD+ metabolism.

Therefore, this study hypothesizes to examine how genetic selection has shaped NAD+ homeostasis, and the activity of key NAD+-dependent enzymes, even divergent growth and resilience phenotypes in commercial Arbor Acre versus native Daweishan Miniature chickens. Furthermore, targeted lipid precursors of the breast muscle were empirically evaluated, offering a biochemical justification for maximizing bird health while increasing economic productivity within modern intensive systems.

Materials and methods

Animals and experimental design

The current study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Yunnan Agricultural University before experimental procedures involving birds (No: 20220313; 03/08/ 2022). In this study, two distinct chicken breeds, including Daweishan Miniature Chicken (M3: a Chinese indigenous breed), and the commercial Arbor Acres broiler (A3: an established broiler international breed) were obtained from the hatchery of the Yunnan Agricultural University Training Poultry Farm. For this experiment, a total of 100 day-old chicks of both breeds were selected, randomized into two groups (n = 50/breed/group), and reared under identical conditions. Briefly, rearing was a two-phase process, including a brooding (0-30 d) and growing (30-90 d) period. During the brooding phase, chicks were kept in an environmentally controlled house equipped with brooding cages at a density of 20-30 birds/m². During the growing period, the birds were shifted to tiered cages at a density of 5-7 birds/m². The Brooder temperature was set at 32-35°C for week 1 and reduced by 3°C weekly until temperature weaning at week 4, after which ambient temperature was maintained at ∼25°C. Relative humidity was kept at 60-70% until 10 d of age, then adjusted to 50-60% during the rest of the experimental period. The light duration and density were 24L:0D for the first 3 d, 23L:1D from d 4 onward during brooding, with gradual light intensity reduction, and 10-12L:12-14D during the growing period. The birds were given free access to fresh/clean water and ensured ad libitum feed intake during the experimental duration. The feed offering frequency was a twice/day (6:00 and 18:00 hours) and orts was ensured. The feed formulation was carried out in accordance with the nutritional standards of the Chinese Chicken Feeding Standard (NY/T33-2004), and diet formulation as well as chemical composition are detailed in Table 1. The immunization protocols followed in this experiment are listed in Table 2 and were in accordance with the Yunnan Agriculture University research farm guidelines.

Sample collection and preparation

At the 90th day, six healthy birds per breed (composite of half male and half female) were randomly selected, kept off feed and water for a 12-h period, and then subjected to humane slaughter via exsanguination of the jugular vein. Immediately post-slaughter, the pectoralis major muscle was excised, connective tissue and visible fat were trimmed, the left-side muscle was sampled for proximate analysis, and stored at -20°C. The right-side muscle was sampled, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80°C for subsequent metabolomic and enzymatic analysis.

Proximate composition analysis

The chemical composition of the muscle samples was determined in triplicate according to the AOAC 1991 standard method. Briefly, moisture content was determined by air force oven drying DHG-9140A (Shanghai Yiheng Technology Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Ash content was analyzed via muffle furnace SX2-10-12D (Shanghai Huyueming Science Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) incineration. Approximately 150 mg of homogenized muscle tissue was weighed into tin foil capsules (LECO, part no. 502-186-200, St. Joseph, MI, USA)​ and analyzed by Dumas combustion using an automated nitrogen/protein analyzer (FP-528, LECO Corp., St. Joseph, MI, USA) with high-purity oxygen (≥99.99%, Linde Gas, Munich, Germany) and helium (≥99.995%, Air Products, Allentown, PA, USA). Protein content was calculated using a conversion factor of 6.25. Ether extract (crude fat) content was determined using an Ankom XT15 Fat and Oil Extractor (Ankom Technology, Macedon, NY, USA). The following 1-4 equations were used for nutrient calculations.

Targeted metabolomics profiling of NAD+ pathway metabolites

All solvents were of liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) grade. Methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) and isopropanol (IPA) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Methanol (MeOH) and acetonitrile (ACN) were obtained from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Formic acid (FA) and ammonium acetate were acquired from CNW Technologies (Shanghai, China). Ammonium formate and ammonium hydroxide were from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (China). Ultrapure water (H₂O, 18.2 MΩ·cm resistivity) was produced by a Milli-Q water purification system (Merck Millipore, Darmstadt, Germany). Authentic standards for NAD+, nicotinamide (NAM), nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), nicotinamide riboside (NR), nicotinic acid (NA), and L-tryptophan (Trp) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich​ (St. Louis, MO, USA). For this, frozen muscle samples (approximately 50 mg/sample) were homogenized in 800 µL of pre-chilled 80% aqueous methanol using a high-throughput tissue grinder Daxluot-24 (Shanghai Hanno Instrument Co., Ltd. Shanghai, China). The homogenate was subsequently subjected to sonication at 4°C for 30 min using a Hielscher UP200St ultrasonic processor (Teltow, Germany), followed by incubation at 4°C for 1 h, and then centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C using a TGL-16 high-speed refrigerated centrifuge (Hunan Xiangyi Laboratory Instrument Development Co., Ltd, Hunan, China). The supernatant was isolated and diluted it 50-fold with 80% methanol and subjected to the LC-MS/MS analysis. Briefly, quantitative analysis of NAD+, its precursors (NAM, NMN, NR, NA, and Trp) was carried out on an Agilent 1290 UHPLC (Agilent Technologies (China) Co., Ltd. Beijing, China) system coupled to an Agilent 6545 QTOF (Agilent Technologies (China) Co., Ltd. Beijing, China) mass spectrometer. The chromatographic separation was performed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 × 100 mm) (Waters Corporation, Milford, Massachusetts, USA) maintained at 35°C. The mobile phase consisted of (A) 10 mM ammonium formate with 0.1% ammonia in water and (B) 10 mM ammonium formate with 0.1% ammonia in a mixture of acetonitrile and water (90:10, v/v). A gradient elution was applied (0-1 min, 90% B; 1-5 min, 90% to 40% B; 5-6 min, 40% B; 6-8 min, 90% B) at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min (Table 3). The injection volume was 5 µL. The total run time was 8 min. The mass spectrometer operated in positive electrospray ionization (ESI+) mode with Multiple Reaction Monitoring (MRM). Key adjusted parameters were: capillary voltage, 4.5 kV; source temperature, 450°C; desolvation gas flow, 12 L/min. Optimized MRM transitions and collision energies are listed in Table 4. Analytical standards for each target metabolite were used to construct external calibration curves (R² > 0.99) across a linear dynamic range.

Quantification of metabolic regulatory enzymes by ELISA

The muscle homogenates of samples were used to determine the protein concentrations of key metabolic sensors, including SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK, using commercially available, chicken-specific ELISA kits (Jiangsu Jingmei Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) according to the manufacturer's protocols. Absorbance of each sample was measured at 450 nm using a FlexStation3 microplate reader (Molecular Devices, LLC‌., Sunnyvale, CA, USA). Sample concentrations were derived from a four-parameter logistic curve fit of the standard absorbance values.

Untargeted lipidomic profiling

Approximately 60 mg of tissue from each sample was subjected to lipid extraction using a modified methyl-tert-butyl ether (MTBE) method. Briefly, tissue samples were homogenized in water, followed by mixing with MTBE and methanol (final ratio 10:2:5, MTBE: MeOH: H₂O, v/v/v). After vortexing, sonication, and phase separation by centrifugation, the upper organic layer was collected, evaporated, and reconstituted in isopropanol: methanol (1:1, v/v). The separated organic layer was subjected to lipidomic profiling by using the UHPLC-QTOF-MS platform (Agilent Technologies (China) Co., Ltd. Beijing, China). The lipid separation was performed on the above-mentioned column at 40°C. Mobile phase A was water with 0.1% formic acid, and phase B was acetonitrile: isopropanol (7:3, v/v) with 0.1% formic acid. A 30-min gradient was employed (0-1.5 min, 5% B; 1.5-14 min, 5-40% B; 14-22 min, 40-95% B; 22-25 min, 95% B; 25-26 min, 95-5% B; 26-30 min, 5% B) at 400 µL/min (Table 5). Mass spectrometric data were acquired in data-dependent acquisition mode using both ESI+ and ESI- polarities. The scan range was m/z 50-1300 for MS¹ and m/z 20-1300 for MS². Source parameters were optimized as follows: gas temperature, 320°C; drying gas flow, 8 L/min; sheath gas temperature, 350°C; sheath gas flow, 12 L/min; capillary voltages, +4000 V and -3500 V.

Statistical and bioinformatics analysis

All experimental data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. For proximate composition, metabolite levels, and enzyme activities, differences between breed and age groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan's multiple range test in SPSS software (v26.0, IBM Corp.). Statistical significance was declared at p < 0.05. Targeted Metabolomics data acquisition and peak integration were performed using MultiQuant software. Metabolite concentrations in samples were calculated by interpolating peak areas against the respective calibration curve.

For lipidomics analysis, raw data files were converted and processed using MS-DIAL software (v4.60) for peak picking, alignment, and deconvolution. Lipid identification was based on accurate mass (MS¹ tolerance: 5 ppm) and MS/MS spectral matching against the LipidBlast database (MS² tolerance: 50 mDa). A minimum identification score cutoff of 90% was applied. Lipidomics data were mean-centered and Pareto-scaled before multivariate analysis. Orthogonal Partial Least Squares-Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed using SIMCA (v14.1, Umetrics) to maximize group separation. Differentially abundant lipids (DALs) were identified based on a variable importance in projection (VIP) score > 1.0 from the OPLS-DA model and a Student's t-test P-value < 0.05. Univariate and multivariate results were integrated for final DAL selection. Pathway enrichment analysis of DALs was conducted using the Lipid Pathway module of MetaboAnalyst 6.0. Visualization tools included hierarchical clustering heatmaps and volcano plots generated via SRplot, and bar charts created in Origin 2021.

Results

The chemical composition analysis showed significant breed-dependent differences (p < 0.05) in muscle composition (Table 6). Daweishan miniature chicken group had higher crude protein and ash contents than the A3 group (p < 0.05). In contrast, the A3 group had greater moisture and crude fat contents compared with the M3 group (p < 0.05).

Differential lipid molecules

The volcano plot showed significant changes in the distribution of the lipid species (Fig. 3). A total of glycerolipids, fatty acids, and cholesterol ester species were significantly different between groups (Table 9). Most triglyceride (TG) molecules were downregulated in the M3 group compared to the A3 group, whereas a smaller subset of TG molecules was upregulated in the M3 group than the A3 group (p < 0.05). However, fatty acids, including FA20:3, FA20:4, and FA16:0, were upregulated, while FA2:0 and FA18:1, were downregulated in the M3 group (p < 0.05). The cholesteryl esters (particularly CE34:8) were downregulated in the M3 group (p < 0.05).

Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis was developed among NAD⁺, its precursors, key metabolic enzymes, and differential lipid species (Fig. 4). The correlation plot showed a significant interconnection between these biomarkers (p < 0.05). The NAD⁺ and its precursors had positive correlations with most upregulated fatty acids and negative correlations with downregulated TG species (p < 0.05). Similarly, the activities of the SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK had a positive correlation with NAD⁺ metabolites and a negative correlation with the upregulated TG molecules.

Discussion

In this study, we investigate the comparative muscle biochemical composition, NAD⁺-related metabolic regulation, and lipidomic profiles of Daweishan miniature chickens and Arbor Acre broilers. We found clear breed-dependent differences across the breed groups, suggesting that the slower-growing miniature chickens maintain a distinct metabolic phenotype characterized by elevated NAD⁺ metabolism, enhanced enzymatic activity, and markedly different lipid signatures. In this study, we found higher crude protein and ash contents in Daweishan miniature chickens. These results are consistent with previous findings (Faustin-Evaris et al., 2022; Weng et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023), indicating that indigenous or slow-growing poultry breeds may possess denser muscle structure and increased mineral deposition. However, this study recorded higher moisture and crude fat contents in Arbor Acre broilers. These results are in line with (Liu et al., 2023), as they reported higher fat deposition in the muscle of fast-growing chickens. These results can be attributed to their metabolic capacity, muscle quality traits, and greater intramuscular lipid deposition (Weng et al., 2022). The research demonstrated that faster-growing broilers, such as Arbre Acre, have faster growth, muscle water, and fat contents than the native slow-growing birds.

A major finding of this study is that NAD⁺ and its biosynthetic intermediates (nicotinamide, NMN, and NR) were significantly higher in Daweishan miniature chickens. Elevated NAD⁺ status is typically associated with enhanced mitochondrial function, improved redox balance, and increased capacity for metabolic regulation in chicken (Wang et al., 2015). The accompanying increases in SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK activity in our study further support this interpretation. These enzymes are central regulators of energy homeostasis, and their activation is closely tied to NAD⁺ availability. These results are in line with the previous studies (Buzała et al., 2015; Du et al., 2023), which reported similar trends in slow-growing or indigenous chicken breeds, which tend to exhibit greater mitochondrial density and enhanced oxidative metabolic pathways relative to fast-growing broilers.

In our study, lipidomic analysis showed distinct differences in triglyceride, fatty acids, and cholesterol esters composition between the groups. Most triglycerides were downregulated in Daweishan miniature chickens, which is consistent with their lower crude fat content and potentially higher rates of lipid mobilization and oxidation. The upregulation of specific fatty acids, including FA20:3, FA20:4, and FA16:0, suggests selective enrichment of metabolic or structural lipids associated with oxidative phosphorylation, membrane remodeling, or inflammation-related signaling pathways. Downregulation of cholesterol esters such as CE34:8 may further indicate differences in sterol esterification and lipid storage. The correlations analysis of this study, observed among NAD⁺, key enzymes, and differential lipid species, reinforces the concept that NAD⁺-dependent signaling networks are closely linked to lipid remodeling in chicken muscle (Liu et al., 2019). The negative association between NAD⁺ metabolites and most TG species suggests that higher NAD⁺ levels favor lipid catabolism, consistent with AMPK and SIRT1 activation (Cantó et al., 2009). Positive correlations with specific fatty acids imply preferential oxidation or selective incorporation into metabolic pathways rather than storage as triglycerides. Together, these lipidomic changes highlight that the miniature chickens prioritize metabolic lipid utilization over deposition, whereas broilers accumulate a broader range of TG species, likely reflecting their rapid muscle accretion and higher adipogenic potential (Xing et al., 2025).

KEGG pathway enrichment further demonstrated that glycerolipid metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, and glycerophospholipid pathways were the most impacted. Similar omics findings were reported by (Liu et al., 2022) when they compared the muscles of lean and obese chickens. These pathways are central to muscle energy turnover, membrane function, and lipid storage in muscle (Zhao et al., 2025), suggesting that intrinsic metabolic programming differs substantially between the breeds. Collectively, these findings indicate that Daweishan miniature chickens maintain a metabolic profile skewed toward oxidative metabolism, higher NAD⁺ turnover, and more regulated lipid utilization, whereas Arbor Acre broilers exhibit a lipid deposition-oriented phenotype. These differences may underlie the distinct growth patterns, muscle quality characteristics, and metabolic resilience commonly reported for indigenous versus commercial broiler breeds.

Conclusion

In conclusion, significant breed-related differences were observed in muscle composition, NAD⁺ metabolism, and lipid profiles between Daweishan miniature chickens and Arbor Acre broilers. Daweishan chickens exhibited higher crude protein and ash content, elevated concentrations of NAD⁺ and its precursors (nicotinamide, NMN, NR), and increased abundance of SIRT1, LKB1, and AMPK enzymes. Lipidomic analysis revealed predominantly downregulated triglycerides and breed-specific fatty acid profiles in Daweishan chickens, with glycerolipid and fatty acid metabolism identified as key affected pathways. Correlation analysis further supported associations among NAD⁺ metabolites, metabolic enzymes, and differential lipid species. These findings collectively demonstrate distinct metabolic characteristics in the breast muscle of the two breeds, providing a foundation for future mechanistic studies on NAD⁺-mediated metabolic regulation in poultry.

Error Estimates for Gauss–Christoffel Quadrature under Reduced Regularity Conditions


Abstract
Gauss–Christoffel quadrature is a fundamental method for numerical integration, and its convergence analysis is closely related to the decay of Chebyshev expansion coefficients. Classical estimates, including those due to Trefethen, are based on weighted bounded variation assumptions involving the singular weight ( 1 − x 2 ) − 1 / 2 (1-x^{2})^{-1/2} , which may be too restrictive for functions with limited regularity at the endpoints. In this paper, we establish a new error bound for Gauss–Christoffel quadrature under weakened regularity assumptions. The analysis relies on a new identity for higher-order derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials. As a consequence, we obtain an improved decay estimate for Chebyshev coefficients, where the classical weighted condition V r = ∫ − 1 1 | f ( r + 1 ) ​ ( x ) | 1 − x 2 ​ 𝑑 x V_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|f^{(r+1)}(x)|}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx is replaced by the weaker condition U r = ∫ − 1 1 | f ( r + 1 ) ​ ( x ) | ​ 𝑑 x . U_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{(r+1)}(x)|\,dx. This result leads to a corresponding error estimate for the Gauss–Christoffel quadrature rule, which is less restrictive than previous bounds. The approach is also extended to the Gauss–Gegenbauer case. Numerical experiments are provided to illustrate the theoretical results. Introduction

Numerical integration based on Gaussian quadrature plays a central role in scientific computing. The Gauss–Christoffel quadrature rule, associated with a weight function
w(x)
on
[-1,1]
, is given by

\int_{-1}^{1}w(x)f(x)\,dx\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}f(x_{i})=Q_{N}^{GC}[f],

(1)

and is well known for its high accuracy when applied to smooth integrands. A fundamental problem in its analysis is the derivation of reliable
a priori
error estimates that reflect the regularity of the integrand.

A standard approach relates the quadrature error to the decay of the coefficients in orthogonal polynomial expansions, in particular the Chebyshev expansion
[
3
,
4
]
. If

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T_{n}(x),

then the quadrature error can be controlled in terms of the tail behaviour of the coefficients
a_{n}
. Consequently, sharp bounds for these coefficients play a central role in the convergence analysis of Gauss-type quadrature rules.

Existing results and motivation

A classical result due to Trefethen
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
states that if

f,f^{\prime},\ldots,f^{(r-1)}
are absolutely continuous and
f^{(r)}
is of bounded variation, and if

V_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|f^{(r+1)}(x)|}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx<\infty,

(2)

then the Chebyshev coefficients satisfy

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2V_{r}}{\pi\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-j)},\qquad n\geq r+1.

(3)

This estimate has served as the basis of several error bounds for Gauss-type quadrature rules. In particular, Xiang
[
1
]
derived the following estimate:

\left|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]\right|\leq\frac{4V_{r}\int_{-1}^{1}w(x)\,dx}{\pi r(2N+1)2N\cdots(2N-r+2)}.

(4)

The weighted condition (
2
) imposes control near the endpoints
x=\pm 1
due to the singular factor
(1-x^{2})^{-1/2}
. This restriction may be overly strong for functions whose higher derivatives are integrable in the usual sense, but exhibit reduced regularity near the boundary. Several works
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
9
]
have refined constants and summation techniques, while largely retaining this structural assumption.

Main contributions

The objective of this work is to establish error bounds for Gauss–Christoffel quadrature under weakened regularity assumptions. In particular, we replace the weighted condition (
2
) by the absolute integrability condition

U_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{(r+1)}(x)|\,dx<\infty.

(5)

Our analysis is based on a new explicit identity for higher-order derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials, which allows a refined integration-by-parts argument in the estimation of the coefficients
a_{n}
.

The main results of this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

We derive an improved bound for Chebyshev coefficients in terms of
U_{r}
(Theorem
2.3
).

•

We obtain a corresponding error estimate for Gauss–Christoffel quadrature under these weakened assumptions (Theorem
3.1
).

•

The analysis is extended to the Gauss–Gegenbauer case (Theorem
3.3
).

•

Numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the theoretical results.

Organization of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls basic properties of Chebyshev polynomials and establishes the improved coefficient bound. Section
3
develops the corresponding quadrature error estimates. Numerical illustrations are presented in Section
4
, and concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

2
Improved upper bound for Chebyshev coefficients

The Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind,

T_{n}(x)=\cos\bigl(n\cos^{-1}x\bigr),\qquad n\geq 0,

form an orthogonal system on
[-1,1]
with respect to the weight
(1-x^{2})^{-1/2}
, namely

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x)T_{m}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\delta_{mn},

where
\delta_{mn}
denotes the Kronecker delta. Moreover,
T_{n}
is an eigenfunction of the associated Sturm–Liouville problem
[
10
]

\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\bigl(\sqrt{1-x^{2}}T_{n}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)^{\prime}+n^{2}T_{n}(x)=0.

(6)

It is well known that Chebyshev polynomials satisfy the uniform bound

|T_{n}(x)|\leq 1,\qquad x\in[-1,1],\;n\geq 0.

(7)

In addition, their derivatives obey the three-term relation
[
10
]

(1-x^{2})T_{n}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{n}{2}\bigl(T_{n-1}(x)-T_{n+1}(x)\bigr).

(8)

For a function
f\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, its Chebyshev expansion reads

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T_{n}(x),

with coefficients

a_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx,\qquad a_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)T_{n}(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx,\quad n\geq 1.

Obtaining sharp upper bounds for
a_{n}
hinges on a precise control of derivatives of
Chebyshev polynomials in suitable weighted norms. Classical approaches, based on
repeated integration by parts combined with coarse inequalities, typically lead to
suboptimal constants. In contrast, we exploit the intrinsic Sturm–Liouville structure
(
6
) together with the recurrence (
8
), which enables a finer analysis of
derivative growth and ultimately sharper coefficient estimates.

To normalize the dependence on the polynomial degree, we introduce the scaled quantity

\mathcal{T}_{n}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,T_{n}^{\prime}(x)}{n^{2}}.

(9)

The next lemma constitutes the main technical ingredient of this section. It provides
a recursive representation of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
in terms of derivatives of neighboring
Chebyshev modes. Unlike classical two-term identities, the resulting expansion involves

2^{r}
terms whose coefficients possess a precise monotonicity structure.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)
be defined by (
9
). For
1\leq r\leq n-1
,
\mathcal{T}_{n}

admits the representation

\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)=\left(\frac{(-1)^{r}\mathcal{T}_{n-r}(x)}{\beta^{1}_{n,r}}+\frac{(-1)^{r-1}\mathcal{T}_{n-r+2}(x)}{\beta^{2}_{n,r}}+\cdots-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+r-2}(x)}{\beta^{2^{r-1}}_{n,r}}+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+r}(x)}{\beta^{2^{r}}_{n,r}}\right)^{(r)},

where

\beta^{j}_{n,r}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}(2n-2i+2),&j=1,2,\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}(2n+2i-2),&j=2^{r}-1,2^{r}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
1\leq j\leq 2^{r-1}
,

\beta^{1}_{n,r}=\beta^{2}_{n,r}\leq\beta^{2j-1}_{n,r}=\beta^{2j}_{n,r}\leq\beta^{2^{r}-1}_{n,r}=\beta^{2^{r}}_{n,r}.

Proof.

Using (
9
), the Sturm–Liouville equation (
6
) can be rewritten as

T_{n}(x)=-\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\prime}(x).

(10)

Similarly, (
8
) becomes

\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2n}\bigl(T_{n-1}(x)-T_{n+1}(x)\bigr).

(11)

Substituting (
10
) into (
11
) yields

\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2n}\bigl(-\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n-1}^{\prime}(x)+\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\,\mathcal{T}_{n+1}^{\prime}(x)\bigr).

(12)

For
r=1
,

\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2n}\bigl(-\mathcal{T}_{n-1}^{\prime}(x)+\mathcal{T}_{n+1}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)=\left(\frac{-\mathcal{T}_{n-1}(x)}{2n}+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+1}(x)}{2n}\right)^{(1)},

(13)

where
\beta^{1}_{n,1}=\beta^{2}_{n,1}.
Using (
12
) in two numerators of (
13
), we get the following representation for
\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n-2}(x)}{\beta^{1}_{n,1}(2n-2)}-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)}{\beta^{1}_{n,1}(2n-2)}-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)}{\beta^{2}_{n,1}(2n+2)}+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+2}(x)}{\beta^{2}_{n,1}(2n+2)}\right)^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n-2}(x)}{\beta^{1}_{n,2}}-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)}{\beta^{2}_{n,2}}-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)}{\beta^{3}_{n,2}}+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+2}(x)}{\beta^{4}_{n,2}}\right)^{(2)},

(14)

where
\beta^{1}_{n,2}=\beta^{2}_{n,2}\leq\beta^{3}_{n,2}=\beta^{4}_{n,2}.

The general case follows by induction by repeatedly inserting this identity into
higher-order derivatives, which produces
2^{r}
terms with the stated coefficient
ordering.
∎

Lemma
2.1
reveals a hierarchical structure in the derivatives of Chebyshev
polynomials, whereby higher-order derivatives are expressed as linear combinations
of neighboring modes with explicitly controlled coefficients.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
,

\frac{1}{\beta^{1}_{n,r}(n-r)}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}_{n,r}(n-r+2)}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\beta^{2^{r}}_{n,r}(n+r)}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)}.

Lemma
2.2
, the proof of which can be found in
[
12
]
, leads to a remarkable simplification of the expressions arising
from Lemma
2.1
. Together, these results allow us to derive sharp upper bounds
for Chebyshev coefficients using only interior information, thereby avoiding the
loss of accuracy typically associated with crude norm estimates.

We are now in a position to combine Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
to derive an improved upper bound for the Chebyshev coefficients.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
f,f^{\prime},\cdots,f^{(r-1)}
are absolutely continuous and the
r^{th}
derivative
f^{(r)}
is of bounded variation on
[-1,1]
. Then for
n\geq r+1

\left|a_{n}\right|\leq\frac{2U_{r}}{\pi\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)},

(15)

where
U_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{(r+1)}(x)|dx<\infty
.

Proof.

Using (
10
), integration by parts, and the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\pm 1)=0
, we obtain

a_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)\,dx.

Since

\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)=\frac{\sin(n\cos^{-1}x)}{n},\qquad|\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{n},

the case
r=0
follows immediately. For
r\geq 1
, applying Lemma
2.1
, integrating by parts
r
times yields

a_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}f^{(r+1)}(x)\left(\frac{(-1)^{r}\mathcal{T}_{n-r}(x)}{\beta^{1}_{n,r}}+\frac{(-1)^{r-1}\mathcal{T}_{n-r+2}(x)}{\beta^{2}_{n,r}}+\cdots-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+r-2}(x)}{\beta^{2^{r-1}}_{n,r}}+\frac{\mathcal{T}_{n+r}(x)}{\beta^{2^{r}}_{n,r}}\right)dx.

By Lemma
2.2
and applying the given conditions
U_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{(r+1)}(x)|dx
and
|\mathcal{T}_{n}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{n}
, we obtain

|a_{n}|<\frac{2U_{r}}{\pi\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
New approximation error of the Gauss-Christoffel quadrature

The Gauss–Christoffel quadrature error is commonly bounded via estimates of the tail sum of the integrand’s Chebyshev coefficients. Specifically, for the classical Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature with weight
w(x)=1/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
, the error admits the exact representation

I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]=\pi\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}a_{2k+1},

(16)

where
a_{n}
are the Chebyshev coefficients of
f
(see, e.g.,
[
3
,
2
]
). For general weight functions
w(x)
, while an exact formula like (
16
) is unavailable, the standard error bound
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]

|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]|\leq 2\|w\|_{1}\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|,\quad\|w\|_{1}=\int_{-1}^{1}|w(x)|\,dx,

(17)

reduces the problem to estimating the decay rate of
|a_{n}|
.

Theorem
2.3
provides precisely such an estimate under the weakened regularity condition
U_{r}<\infty
. Substituting inequality (
15
) into (
17
) yields our main result: a sharper error bound for Gauss–Christoffel quadrature that holds for a broader class of functions than previously covered.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
. Then for the Gauss–Christoffel quadrature rule (
1
),

|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]|\leq\frac{4U_{r}\|w\|_{1}}{r\pi\prod_{j=1}^{r}(2N-r+2j+1)}.

(18)

Proof.

Starting from (
17
) and applying Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|w\|_{1}\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}\frac{2U_{r}}{\pi\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4U_{r}\|w\|_{1}}{\pi}\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)}.

Writing the product as

\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)=(n-r)^{r+1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Bigl(1+\frac{2j}{n-r}\Bigr),

and noting that
1+\frac{2j}{n-r}\geq 1+\frac{2j}{2N-r+1}
for
n\geq 2N+2
, we obtain

|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]|\leq\frac{4U_{r}\|w\|_{1}}{\pi\prod_{j=1}^{r}\bigl(1+\frac{2j}{2N-r+1}\bigr)}\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-r)^{r+1}}.

Bounding the sum by an integral,

\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-r)^{r+1}}\leq\int_{2N+1}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(x-r)^{r+1}}=\frac{1}{r(2N-r+1)^{r}},

and simplifying the product

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Bigl(1+\frac{2j}{2N-r+1}\Bigr)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(2N-r+2j+1)}{(2N-r+1)^{r}},

completes the proof of (
18
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Comparing the error bound (
18
) with Xiang’s result (
4
)
[
1
]
, we identify two distinct improvements:

1.

Relaxed regularity requirement:
The classical condition
V_{r}<\infty
is replaced by
U_{r}<\infty
. Since
U_{r}\leq V_{r}
with strict inequality when
f^{(r+1)}
does not vanish sufficiently at
x=\pm 1
, Theorem
3.1
applies to a strictly larger class of functions.

2.

Improved numerical constant:
For given
N
and
r
, the denominator factor satisfies

\theta_{N,r}:=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(2N-r+2j+1)}<\frac{1}{(2N+1)2N\cdots(2N-r+2)}=:\beta_{N,r},

with the ratio
\beta_{N,r}/\theta_{N,r}
increasing as
r
approaches
N-1
.

Table

quantifies the improvement in the denominator factor for representative values. The combined effect of these two enhancements makes (
18
) both more widely applicable and numerically sharper than (
4
). It is worth emphasizing that Xiang’s bound remains valid and useful for functions satisfying
V_{r}<\infty
; our result extends its applicability while also tightening the estimate.

Extension to Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature

The results of Theorem
3.1
extend naturally to the Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature, which corresponds to the weight function
w(x)=(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}
with
\lambda>-\frac{1}{2}
. This family includes several important special cases:

•

\lambda=0
: Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature of the first kind

•

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
: Gauss–Legendre quadrature

•

\lambda=1
: Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature of the second kind

The Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature rule approximates

I[f]=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}f(x)\,dx\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}f(x_{i})=Q_{N}^{GG}[f].

(19)

A key observation for the error analysis is the parity property of Chebyshev polynomials under this weight: for odd
j
,

I[T_{j}]=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}T_{j}(x)\,dx=0=Q_{N}^{GG}[T_{j}],

since the integrand is an odd function. Consequently, only even-index Chebyshev coefficients contribute to the quadrature error, leading to an error bound with a factor
2
rather than
4
as in Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
. Then for the Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature rule (
19
),

\left|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GG}[f]\right|\leq\frac{2U_{r}\|w_{\lambda}\|_{1}}{r\pi\prod_{j=1}^{r}(2N-r+2j+1)},\quad\lambda>-\frac{1}{2},

where
\|w_{\lambda}\|_{1}=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}dx=\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})/\Gamma(\lambda+1)
.

Proof.

Following the same approach as in Theorem
3.1
, but noting the parity property mentioned above, we have

\left|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GG}[f]\right|\leq\|w_{\lambda}\|_{1}\sum_{n=2N+2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|.

The factor
2
instead of
4
arises because when
n
is odd,
I[T_{n}]=Q_{N}^{GG}[T_{n}]=0
, so only terms with even
n
contribute to the error bound. Substituting the coefficient estimate from Theorem
2.3
and proceeding exactly as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
yields the stated bound.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The constant
\|w_{\lambda}\|_{1}
can be expressed in closed form using the Beta function:

\|w_{\lambda}\|_{1}=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}dx=2\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}dx=B\left(\frac{1}{2},\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)}.

For integer
\lambda
, this simplifies further; for instance,
\|w_{0}\|_{1}=\pi
(Chebyshev),
\|w_{1/2}\|_{1}=\pi/2
(Legendre).

Numerical examples

This section illustrates the practical advantages of the new error bounds derived in Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
. We focus on two representative test functions that highlight the key improvements: (i) applicability under weaker regularity conditions, and (ii) numerically sharper estimates. All computations were performed in MATLAB using the standard Golub–Welsch algorithm to generate Gauss–Christoffel nodes and weights. Reference integrals were computed with a high-order Gauss rule (
N=500
) to ensure accuracy below machine precision.

Example 1: A function with endpoint-near singularity

Consider the family of functions

f_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{j!}(x-t)^{j-1}|x-t|,\quad j\geq 2,\ t\in(-1,1).

These functions have a
corner singularity
at
x=t
:
f_{j},f_{j}^{\prime},\dots,f_{j}^{(j-1)}
are absolutely continuous,
f_{j}^{(j)}
is of bounded variation, and in the distributional sense
f_{j}^{(j+1)}(x)=2\delta(x-t)

The quantities appearing in the error bounds are

V_{j}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|f_{j}^{(j+1)}(x)|}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx=\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}},\qquad U_{j}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f_{j}^{(j+1)}(x)|\,dx=2.

When
t
approaches
\pm 1
, the classical quantity
V_{j}
diverges, rendering Xiang’s bound (
4
) extremely large or even infinite, whereas
U_{j}
remains constant. This demonstrates the fundamental advantage of our approach: it remains valid for singularities arbitrarily close to the endpoints.

Discussion

The numerical experiments confirm the theoretical advantages established in Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
:

•

Broader applicability:
The new bounds remain valid for functions whose high-order derivatives are integrable in the absolute sense but may behave poorly near the endpoints (Example 1).

•

Tighter estimates:
Even for smooth functions, the new bounds provide numerically smaller prefactors (Example 2).

•

Practical relevance:
Functions with corner singularities arise frequently in applications involving non‑smooth data, discontinuous coefficients in PDEs, or boundary layers. Our estimates offer reliable error control for such problems where classical bounds may fail.

The results also validate the efficiency of the underlying coefficient estimate (Theorem
2.3
), which successfully decouples the regularity requirement from the singular weight
1/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
.

5
Conclusion

This paper has established improved error estimates for Gauss–Christoffel and Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature by weakening the regularity requirements on the integrand. The key innovation is a novel recurrence relation for derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials (Lemma
2.1
), which enables us to replace the classical weighted condition

V_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|f^{(r+1)}(x)|}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx<\infty

with the weaker absolute condition

U_{r}=\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{(r+1)}(x)|\,dx<\infty.

Main contributions

1.

Improved Chebyshev coefficient bounds:
Theorem
2.3
provides the estimate

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{2U_{r}}{\pi\prod_{j=0}^{r}(n-r+2j)},\quad n\geq r+1,

which is valid for all functions whose
(r+1)
-st derivative is absolutely integrable, regardless of its behavior near
x=\pm 1
. This extends Trefethen’s classical bound
[
3
]
to a significantly broader function class.

2.

Sharper quadrature error estimates:
Theorem
3.1
yields the Gauss–Christoffel quadrature bound

|I[f]-Q_{N}^{GC}[f]|\leq\frac{4U_{r}\|w\|_{1}}{r\pi\prod_{j=1}^{r}(2N-r+2j+1)},

which is both
less restrictive
(using
U_{r}
instead of
V_{r}
) and
numerically smaller
than Xiang’s prominent bound
[
1
]
. The improvement factor grows as
r
approaches
N
, reaching orders of magnitude in practical cases (Table
2
).

3.

Extension to Gauss–Gegenbauer quadrature:
Theorem
3.3
generalizes the result to weight functions
w(x)=(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-1/2}
,
\lambda>-1/2
, covering important special cases like Gauss–Legendre and Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature.

4.

Numerical validation:
Section
4
demonstrates that for functions with singularities near the endpoints, the classical bound can become excessively large or even infinite, while our new bound remains stable and provides a realistic error estimate. Even for smooth integrands, the new bound offers a consistent numerical improvement.

Practical implications

The proposed estimates are particularly valuable in applications where integrands exhibit limited smoothness at the boundaries, such as:

•

Solutions of boundary value problems with singular coefficients or boundary layers.

•

Integrals involving functions with algebraic endpoint behavior
f(x)=(1-x^{2})^{\alpha}g(x)
,
\alpha<1/2
.

•

Problems with piecewise-smooth data or corner singularities.

In such situations, the classical condition
V_{r}<\infty
may fail, whereas
U_{r}<\infty
often holds, making our bounds the only theoretically justified error estimates available.

Future work

Several directions for further investigation naturally arise:

•

Extending the approach to other families of orthogonal polynomials (Jacobi, Laguerre, Hermite).

•

Developing adaptive quadrature strategies that exploit these improved error bounds to allocate nodes more efficiently near singularities.

•

Applying the technique to error analysis of spectral methods for PDEs with non-smooth solutions.

•

Investigating the optimality of the constants in Theorem
2.3
; while we have established improvement over existing bounds, the sharpness of the prefactor remains an open question.

Concluding remark

By decoupling the regularity requirement from the singular weight
1/\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
, this work provides error estimates that better reflect the actual approximation capabilities of Gaussian quadrature. The results bridge a gap between theoretical analysis and practical computation, offering tools that are simultaneously more general and more accurate than previous ones.

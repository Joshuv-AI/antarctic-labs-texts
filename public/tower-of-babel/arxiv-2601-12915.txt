Sharp lower bound for the Monge-Ampère torsion on convex sets
Francesco Salerno

Abstract
The Monge-Ampère torsion deficit of an open, bounded convex set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\R^{n} of class C 2 C^{2} is the normalized gap between the value of the torsion functional evaluated on Ω \Omega and its value on the ball with the same ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -quermassintegral as Ω \Omega . Using the technique of the shape derivative , we prove that the ratio between this deficit and to a geometric deficit arising from the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality , for any given family of open, bounded convex sets of ℝ n \R^{n} ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) of class C 2 C^{2} , smoothly converging to a ball, is bounded from below by a dimensional constant. We also show that this ratio is always bounded from above by a constant. Keywords: Monge-Ampère operator, torsional rigidity, shape derivative. MSC 2020: 35B35, 35J96, 52A40.

Introduction

Given an open convex set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}
, starting from the solution of the following problem

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}u_{\Omega}=1&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases},

(1)

where
D^{2}u
is the Hessian matrix of
u
, in
[
2
]
(subsequently proven in
[
3
]
by the same authors using different techniques) it is proved that in the class of open, bounded convex sets of class
C^{2}
with prescribed volume, the ellipsoid minimizes the torsion functional, namely

T(\Omega)=\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{n}\geq\|u_{\mathcal{E}(\Omega)}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{E}(\Omega))}^{n}=T(\mathcal{E}(\Omega))=\frac{|\Omega|^{n+2}}{(n+2)^{n}\omega_{n}^{2}}.

(2)

Here
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of a set and
\omega_{n}
is the measure of the unit ball (see
[
9
]
for the extension of (
2
) to open, bounded convex sets not necessarily
C^{2}
).
In
[
15
]
, in dimension
n=2
, then extended in
[
17
]
to
n\geq 2
, prescribing another geometric quantity, the
(n-1)
-th quermassintegral of
\Omega
, denoted by
W_{n-1}(\Omega)
(see §
2
for the precise definition), it was proved that the torsion functional is maximized by a ball, namely if
\Omega^{*}_{n-1}
is the ball with the same
(n-1)
-th quermassintegral as
\Omega
, then

T(\Omega)\leq T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n}\zeta_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}{(n+2)^{n}},

(3)

where
\zeta_{n-1}(\Omega)
is the radius of the ball
\Omega^{*}_{n-1}
.
From (
3
) it follows that the quantity

\delta T(\Omega)=\frac{T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})-T(\Omega)}{T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})}=1-\frac{T(\Omega)}{T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})}

is always nonnegative. On the other hand, by the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality (see §
2
for a precise statement) the quantity

\delta_{AF}(\Omega)=1-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega)}{W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}

is also always nonnegative, and it is zero if and only if
\Omega
is a ball (see, for instance,
[
14
]
). In this work, we want to study the ratio
\delta T/\delta_{AF}
. In more details we prove that this ratio is bounded from above by a constant, and this follows directly from (
2
)-(
3
).
In order to estimate the ratio from below, we exploit the technique of shape derivative to investigate the behavior of
\delta T/\delta_{AF}
along an arbitrary family of open, bounded convex sets that converges in a suitable way to a ball (see Definition
1.1
below). With this technique in
[
12
]
the ratio
\delta P/\delta\lambda
, that is, the deficit of the isoperimetric inequality divided by the deficit of the Faber-Krahn inequality, has been studied. In the following, we will use the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a family
\{\Omega(t)\}_{t}
of open, bounded convex sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
smoothly converges to an open, bounded convex set
\Omega
as
t
goes to zero, if there exists a positive number
\delta
and a one-parameter family of transformations
\Phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in[0,\delta)
, such that

(i)

\Phi_{t}
,
\Phi^{-1}_{t}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
t\in[0,\delta)
,

(ii)

the maps
t\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x)
,
t\mapsto\Phi^{-1}_{t}(x)
belong to
C^{\infty}([0,\delta))
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(iii)

\Omega(0)=\Omega
and
\Omega(t)=\Phi_{t}(\Omega)
for all
t\in[0,\delta)
.

We now state the main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
n\geq 2
, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}
such that, for any one-parameter family of open, bounded convex sets
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
C^{2}
, with fixed
(n-1)
-quermassintegral, smoothly converging to a ball as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, then

c_{n}\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\delta T(\Omega(t))}{\delta_{AF}(\Omega(t))},

and the constant is

c_{n}=\frac{n-1}{n}.

Moreover, for all open, bounded convex sets of class
C^{2}
the following inequality holds

\frac{\delta T(\Omega)}{\delta_{AF}(\Omega)}\leq 1.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we fix the notations and we recall all the results that we need in the proof. In section
3
we perform all the computations that we need in the proof of the main theorem, in more details we write the condition that comes out by fixing the
(n-1)
-th quermassintegral and we write in terms of spherical harmonics the two deficits
\delta T
and
\delta_{AF}
. In section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
and, finally, in the appendix we show how to treat the term that comes out from the nonlinearity of the Monge-Ampère operator.

2
Tools and notations

The generalized Kronecker delta and the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial

In the following we use Einstein’s notation for repeated indices. We recall that the
generalized Kronecker delta
is defined as

\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $i_{1},\dots,i_{k}$ are distinct integers and are an even permutation of $j_{1},\dots,j_{k}$},\\
-1&\text{ if $i_{1},\dots,i_{k}$ are distinct integers and are an odd permutation of $j_{1},\dots,j_{k}$},\\
0&\text{ in all other cases}.\end{cases}

The latter can be also seen in terms of an
n\times n
determinant as follows

\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=\begin{vmatrix}\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}&\delta_{j_{2}}^{i_{1}}&\dots&\delta_{j_{k}}^{i_{1}}\\
\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{2}}&\delta_{j_{2}}^{i_{2}}&\dots&\delta_{j_{k}}^{i_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{k}}&\delta_{j_{2}}^{i_{k}}&\dots&\delta_{j_{k}}^{i_{k}}\end{vmatrix}.

We mention a contraction property of the generalized Kronecker delta which will be useful in the following. Let
0<p<k
be two integers, then

\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{p}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{p}}\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=\frac{p!(n-k+p)!}{(n-k)!}\delta_{j_{p+1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{p+1}\dots i_{k}}.

(4)

In particular, if
p=1
, formula (
4
) reduces to

\delta_{j_{l}}^{i_{l}}\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}=(n-k+1)\delta_{j_{1}\dots j_{l-1}j_{l+1}\dots j_{k}}^{i_{1}\dots i_{l-1}i_{l+1}\dots i_{k}},

(5)

where
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Consider
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\R^{n}
. We define the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial
of
\lambda
as follows

\begin{split}S_{k}(\lambda)&=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n,\\
S_{0}(\lambda)&=1.\end{split}

If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues of a matrix
A={A^{i}_{j}}
, we denote
S_{k}(A)=S_{k}(\lambda)
and the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial can be defined equivalently from the elements of
A
as follows

S_{k}(A)=\frac{1}{k!}\delta^{j_{1}\dots j_{k}}_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}A^{i_{1}}_{j_{1}}\dots A^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},

(6)

where we are using the Einstein convention to sum over repeated indices. If
k=1
,
S_{1}(A)
is the trace of
A
. On the other hand, if
k=n
,
S_{n}(A)
is the determinant of
A
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
be
n\times n
matrices. We define the
Newton transformation tensor

[T_{k}]_{i}^{j}
as

[T_{k}]_{i}^{j}(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})=\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{ii_{1}\dots i_{k}}^{jj_{1}\dots j_{k}}A_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\dots A_{j_{k}}^{i_{k}}.

If
A=A_{1}=\dots=A_{k}
, we will simplify the notation as
[T_{k}]_{i}^{j}(A)=[T_{k}]_{i}^{j}(A,\dots,A)
. The Newton transformation tensor allows us to write the
cofactor matrix
of
S_{k}
. Indeed, the last is the matrix
\{S_{k}^{ij}(A)\}_{ij}
whose elements are defined by

S_{k}^{ij}(A)=\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{j}^{i}}S_{k}(A).

Then, from (
6
), we have

S_{k}^{ij}(A)=\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{j}^{i}}\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{ii_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}^{jj_{1}\dots j_{k-1}}A_{j}^{i}A_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\dots A_{j_{k-1}}^{i_{k-1}}=\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{ii_{1}\dots i_{k-1}}^{jj_{1}\dots j_{k-1}}A_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\dots A_{j_{k-1}}^{i_{k-1}}=\frac{1}{k}[T_{k-1}]_{i}^{j}(A).

(7)

Quermassintegrals and the Alexandrov-Fenchel deficit

For the content of this section, we will refer to
[
7
,
14
]
. Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a non-empty, bounded, convex set, let
B_{1}
be the unit ball centered at the origin and
\rho>0
. We can write the Steiner formula for the Minkowski sum
\Omega+\rho B_{1}
as

{\left|\Omega+\rho B_{1}\right|}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}W_{i}(\Omega)\rho^{i}.

(8)

The coefficients
W_{i}(\Omega)
are known in the literature as quermassintegrals of
\Omega
. In particular,
W_{0}(\Omega)={\left|\Omega\right|}
,
nW_{1}(\Omega)=P(\Omega)
and
W_{n}(\Omega)=\omega_{n}
where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of
B_{1}
.

Formula (
8
) can be generalized to every quermassintegral, obtaining

W_{j}(\Omega+\rho B_{1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{i}W_{j+i}(\Omega)\rho^{i},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1.

(9)

If
\Omega
has
C^{2}
boundary, the quermassintegrals can be written in terms of principal curvatures of
\Omega
. More precisely, denoting with
\sigma_{k}
the
k
-th normalized elementary symmetric function of the principal curvature
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}
of
\partial\Omega
, i.e.

\sigma_{0}=1,\qquad\qquad\sigma_{j}=\binom{n-1}{j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{j}\leq n-1}\kappa_{i_{1}}\ldots\kappa_{i_{j}},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1,

(10)

then, the quermassintegrals can be written as

W_{j}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma_{j-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-1.

(11)

We recall that
\sigma_{j}
in (
10
) is also known as
j
-th mean curvature of
\Omega
, while
\sigma_{1}
is called just mean curvature. In the following, we will denote the
(n-1)
-th mean curvature, also known as
Gaussian curvature
, by
k_{\partial\Omega}
.
We remark that the
j
-th mean curvature is, up to a constant, the
j
-th elementary symmetric polynomial applied to the
second fundamental form
of
\partial\Omega
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex set, we define the
j-th mean radius
,
j=1,\dots,n-1
, of
\Omega
as

\zeta_{j}(\Omega)=\left(\frac{W_{j}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}.

(12)

Remark 2.1
.

The
j
-th mean radius
\zeta_{j}(\Omega)
of a convex set
\Omega
is the radius of a ball with same
j
-th quermassintegral as
\Omega
.

Furthermore, Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequalities hold true

\biggl(\frac{W_{j}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}\geq\biggl(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},\qquad 0\leq i<j\leq n-1,

(13)

where equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a ball. When
i=0
and
j=1
, formula (
13
) reduces to the classical isoperimetric inequality, i.e.

P(\Omega)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

If we choose
i=0
,
j=n-1
in (
13
), then we have

W_{n-1}(\Omega)\geq\omega_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}W_{0}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the
Alexandrov-Fenchel deficit
as

\delta_{AF}=1-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega)}{W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}.

The Monge-Ampère operator and the Torsion functional

For the content of this section, we refer to
[
10
,
17
,
19
]
. We want to define the Monge-Ampère operator by framing it within a broader class of differential operators: the
k
-Hessian operators. Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\R^{n}
and let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. The
k
-Hessian operator is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
, i.e., it is defined as

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\dots,n,

(14)

where
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
D^{2}u
. We note that
S_{k}
is a second-order differential operator and it reduces to the Monge-Ampère operator for
k=n
and to the Laplace operator for
k=1
. Except for the case
k=1
, these operators are fully nonlinear and non-elliptic, unless one restricts to the class of
k
-convex functions (see for instance
[
5
]
)

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega):S_{i}(D^{2}u)\geq 0\,\text{ in }\,\Omega,\,i=1,\dots,k\right\}.

The operator
S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
is homogeneous of degree 1, and if we denote by

S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{ij}}S_{k}(D^{2}u),

the Euler identity for homogeneous functions gives

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ij}.

A direct computation shows that the
\left(S_{k}^{1j}(D^{2}u),\dots,S_{k}^{nj}(D^{2}u)\right)
is divergence-free, hence
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
can be written in divergence form

S_{k}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{k}\left(S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j}\right)_{i},

(15)

where the subscripts
i,j
stand for partial differentiation.

If
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
t
is a regular point of
u
, on the boundary of
\{u\leq t\}
it is possible to link the
k
-Hessian operator with the
(k-1)
-th mean curvature (see
[
13
,
16
]
)

\binom{n-1}{k-1}\sigma_{k-1}=\frac{S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{k+1}}.

(16)

The previous identity for
k=n
is the following

k_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{i}u_{j}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{n+1}}.

(17)

Given
\Omega
an open, convex set, we can define the
k
-Torsional rigidity as

T_{k}(\Omega)=\max\left\{\frac{\left(\int_{\Omega}-v\,dx\right)^{k+1}}{\int_{\Omega}-vS_{k}(D^{2}v)\,dx}\,:\,\text{$v\in\mathcal{A}_{k}(\Omega)$, $v|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ }\right\}

(18)

The maximum in (
18
) is achieved by the solution to

\begin{cases}S_{k}(D^{2}u_{\Omega})=\binom{n}{k}&\text{ in $\Omega$, }\\
u_{\Omega}=0&\text{ on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{cases}

(19)

We note that, from the equation in (
19
), we have

T_{k}(\Omega)=\|u_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{k}.

Another consequence of the result in
[
17
]
is that the
k
-Torsional rigidity is maximum on the ball in the class of open, convex sets with prescribed
(k-1)
-th quermassintegral. The latter’s proof is based on a symmetrization technique, introduced in
[
15
]
for the Monge-Ampère equation in the two-dimensional case, then extended in
[
17
]
for the
k
-Hessian equation in any dimension, called quermassintegral-symmetrization (see also
[
4
]
for more details about this symmetrization,
[
6
]
for the extension to the anisotropic setting,
[
11
]
for some quantitative improvement).
When
\Omega=B_{1}
, the solution of (
19
) is given by

u_{B_{1}}(x)=\frac{|x|^{2}-1}{2}.

(20)

We note that, since we are interested in convex functions, here and in what follows
u<0
in
\Omega
. Throughout the remainder of the paper we restrict to the case
k=n
. For notational convenience, we denote
T_{n}(\cdot)
simply by
T(\cdot)
.

3
Computations of the Shape Derivatives

In this section we will perform all the computations that we need to prove the main result.

First and Second Variation of
W_{n-1}

We remark that, since we are fixing the
(n-1)
-quermassintegral, it holds that

\frac{d}{dt}W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))=0.

(21)

To make the previous two conditions explicit, we want to parametrize the
k
-th mean curvature of
\Omega(t)
. From
[
18
]
we have the following lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If we can parametrize the boundary of
\Omega
with a function
r\in C^{2}(\partial B_{1})
, then we have

\sigma_{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}^{-1}(r^{2}+|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2})^{-\frac{k+2}{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{n-1-m}{k-m}r^{-m}\left(r^{2}S_{m}(D^{2}r)+\frac{n-1+k-2m}{n-1-m}r_{i}r_{j}[T_{m}]_{i}^{j}(D^{2}r)\right).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{\Omega(t)\}_{t}
be a family of open, bounded convex sets of
\R^{n}
, with
\Omega(0)=B_{1}
. Then we can write the
(n-1)
-th quermassintegral as follows

\begin{split}W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))&=\omega_{n}+\frac{t}{n}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}\\
&+\frac{t^{2}}{2n(n-1)}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[(n-1)A(\xi)+2(n-3)S_{2}(D^{2}V)-(n^{2}-6n+7)|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi},\end{split}

where we have represented the boundary
\partial\Omega(t)
as

r(\xi,t)=1+tV(\xi)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}A(\xi)+o(t^{2}),

with
o(t^{2})
uniform in
\xi
.

Proof.

From the previous lemma, we have

\begin{split}W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))&=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\sigma_{n-2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\frac{(-1)^{m}(n-1-m)r^{n-m-2}}{(r^{2}+|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\left(r^{2}S_{m}(D^{2}r)+\frac{2n-2m-3}{n-1-m}r_{i}r_{j}[T_{m}]_{i}^{j}(D^{2}r)\right)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(22)

We observe that, when we represent the boundary
\partial\Omega(t)
as

r(\xi,t)=1+tV(\xi)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}A(\xi)+o(t^{2}),

the second term in the sum in (
22
) is of higher order in
t
, except when
m=0
that gives us

(2n-3)r^{n-2}\delta_{i}^{j}r_{i}r_{j}=(2n-3)r^{n-2}|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2}.

In the first term of the sum in (
22
) we have

\begin{split}&S_{0}(D^{2}r)=1,\quad S_{1}(D^{2}r)=t\Delta_{\xi}V+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Delta_{\xi}A+o(t^{2}),\\
&S_{2}(D^{2}r)=t^{2}S_{2}(D^{2}V)+o(t^{2}),\\
&S_{m}(D^{2}r)=o(t^{2}),\quad\forall\,m\geq 3.\end{split}

Then we just have to consider

\frac{(2n-3)r^{n-2}|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2}+\sum_{m=0}^{2}(-1)^{m}(n-1-m)r^{n-m}S_{m}(D^{2}r)}{(r^{2}+|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}=:\frac{f}{g}.

(23)

Then we can expand

\begin{split}&f=(2n-3)r^{n-2}|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2}+(n-1)r^{n}S_{0}(D^{2}r)-(n-2)r^{n-1}S_{1}(D^{2}r)+(n-3)r^{n-2}S_{2}(D^{2}r)+o(t^{2})\\
&=n-1+t[n(n-1)V-(n-2)\Delta_{\xi}V]\\[6.45831pt]
&+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left[n(n-1)^{2}V^{2}+n(n-1)A-2(n-1)(n-2)V\Delta_{\xi}V-(n-2)\Delta_{\xi}A+2(n-3)S_{2}(D^{2}V)+(4n-6)|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\right]\\[6.45831pt]
&+o(t^{2}).\end{split}

(24)

Now we expand
g
. We start with

(r^{2}+|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}=1+tV+\frac{t^{2}}{2}(A+|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2})+o(t^{2}),

and then

\begin{split}g&=(r^{2}+|\nabla_{\xi}r|^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}=\left\{1+\left[tV+\frac{t^{2}}{2}(A+|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2})\right]\right\}^{n-1}+o(t^{2})\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\left[tV+\frac{t^{2}}{2}(A+|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2})\right]^{j}+o(t^{2})\\
&=1+t(n-1)V+\frac{t^{2}}{2}[(n-1)(A+|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2})+(n-1)(n-2)V^{2}]+o(t^{2}).\end{split}

(25)

Finally, joining (
22
)-(
23
)-(
24
)-(
25
), we get

\begin{split}&W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))=\omega_{n}+\frac{t}{n(n-1)}\int_{\partial B_{1}}[(n-1)V-(n-2)\Delta_{\xi}V]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}\\
&+\frac{t^{2}}{2n(n-1)}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[(n-1)A-(n-2)\Delta_{\xi}A+2(n-3)S_{2}(D^{2}V)-(n^{2}-6n+7)|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}+o(t^{2}).\end{split}

(26)

Now, applying
the Green-Beltrami identity
(
[
1
]
), we have

\int_{\partial B_{1}}\Delta_{\xi}V\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\Delta_{\xi}A\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=0,

and the claim follows.

Remark 3.1
.

From the previous theorem, if we fix
W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))=\omega_{n}
for all
t\geq 0
, then the two conditions (
21
) are

\begin{split}&\int_{\partial B_{1}}V\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}=0,\\
&\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[(n-1)A-(n^{2}-6n+7)|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}+2(n-3)S_{2}(D^{2}V)\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}=0.\end{split}

(27)

Shape derivative of the torsion functional and the Alexandrov-Fenchel deficit

We start this section by considering the torsion problem for the Monge-Ampère operator in
\Omega(t)
and the problems related to the time derivatives
u_{t}
,
u_{tt}
. If we write the operator in divergence form, as explained in (
15
), then we are considering the solution to

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j})_{i}=1&\text{ in }\Omega(t)\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega(t)\end{cases}.

(28)

Then the problem derived with respect to the time parameter is the following (see also
[
3
]
)

\begin{cases}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{tj})_{i}=0&\text{ in }\Omega(t)\\
u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot V=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega(t)\end{cases},

(29)

where
V=\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial t}
is the velocity field, and the second derivative of the problem is given by

\begin{cases}S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ttij}+(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}=0&\text{ in }\Omega(t)\\
u_{tt}+V^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot V+\nabla u\cdot V_{t}+2\nabla u_{t}\cdot V=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega(t)\end{cases},

(30)

where the superscript
T
denotes the transpose of a matrix and
V_{t}=\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{t}}{\partial t^{2}}
is the accelleration field.

Remark 3.2
.

From the boundary condition in (
29
) we have that

V=-\frac{u_{t}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\nu,

where
\nu
is the outer normal vector.

Let us now define
j(t)=T(\Omega(t))
. We now aim to compute the first and second derivative of
j(t)
at
t=0
. This is the content of the next result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
be a family of
C^{2}
deformations such that
\Phi_{0}
is the identity and
W_{n-1}(B_{1})=W_{n-1}(\Phi_{t}(B_{1}))
for all
t\geq 0
. Then the first and second shape derivatives of the torsion functional with respect to this deformation are given by

\begin{split}j^{\prime}(0)&=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{t}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\\
j^{\prime\prime}(0)&=\frac{n-1}{n}\omega_{n}^{n-2}\left(\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{t}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\\
&\times\left\{\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[u_{t}^{2}+V_{t}|_{t=0}\cdot\nu-2u_{t}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx+n\int_{\partial B_{1}}(V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\Omega(t)=\Phi_{t}(B_{1})
. We want to compute the first and second shape derivative of
T(\Omega(t))
. We recall the well-known
Hadamard’s formula

[
8
]

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}f\,dx=\int_{\Omega(t)}[f_{t}+\mathrm{div}(fV)]\,dx.

(31)

We recall that
j(t)
is defined as follows

j(t)=T(\Omega(t))=\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n},

where
u
is the solution to (
28
). Then, from (
15
), (
17
), (
29
) and (
31
), since
u=0
on
\partial\Omega(t)
, we have

\begin{split}j^{\prime}(t)&=n\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\Omega(t)}-u_{t}\,dx=\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\Omega(t)}-u_{t}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j})_{i}\,dx\\
&=\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\frac{S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j}u_{i}}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\Omega(t)}-u_{t}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j})_{i}\,dx\\
&=\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n+1}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where the last equality follows from an integration by parts. Iterating (
31
) we can write the second derivative as follows

\begin{split}j^{\prime\prime}(t)&=\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-2}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n+1}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{2}\\
&+n\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\Omega(t)}[-u_{tt}-\mathrm{div}(u_{t}V)]\,dx.\end{split}

On the other hand, from (
15
), (
17
) and (
30
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(t)}-u_{tt}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega(t)}-u_{tt}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{j})_{i}\,dx=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}-u_{tt}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega(t)}S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ttij}u\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n}(V^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot V+\nabla u\cdot V_{t}+2\nabla u_{t}\cdot V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega(t)}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(t)}-\mathrm{div}

\displaystyle(u_{t}V)\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}u_{t}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|(V\cdot\nu)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Then the second shape derivative of the torsion is

\begin{split}&j^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-2}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n+1}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{2}\\
&+\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{n}(V^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot V+\nabla u\cdot V_{t}+2\nabla u_{t}\cdot V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega(t)}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx}_{NT}\right]\\
&+n\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}-u\,dx\right)^{n-1}\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|(V\cdot\nu)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where "NT" is the effect of the nonlinearity of the operator.

From Remark
3.2
we have that

V|_{t=0}=-\frac{u_{t}}{|\nabla u|}\nu

where
u_{t}
is the solution of

\begin{cases}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{ti})_{j}=0&\text{ in }B_{1}\\
u_{t}=-|\nabla u|V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu&\text{ on }\partial B_{1},\end{cases}

(32)

where, since both
\delta T
and
\delta_{AF}
are invariant with respect to homotheties, we have made the normalization

W_{n-1}(\Omega(t))=\omega_{n}
for all
t\geq 0
, that is,
\Omega(t)_{n-1}^{*}=B_{1}
for all
t\geq 0
.
We observe that, when
t=0
, we have

•

\nabla u\cdot V_{t}|_{t=0}=|\nabla u|\nu\cdot V_{t}|_{t=0}
,

•

2\nabla u_{t}\cdot V|_{t=0}=-\frac{2}{|\nabla u|}u_{t}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}
,

•

k_{\partial\Omega(t)}|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{n-1}^{n-1}(\Omega(0))}=1
,

and

\begin{split}V^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot V|_{t=0}&=\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\nu^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot\nu=\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{4}}\nabla u^{T}\cdot D^{2}u\cdot\nabla u\\
&=\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{4}}\left(\Delta u|\nabla u|^{2}-\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)|\nabla u|^{3}\right)\\
&=\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\Delta u-\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|\nabla u|}H,\end{split}

where
H
is the non-normalized mean curvature, that is

H=(n-1)\sigma_{1},

and
\sigma_{1}
is defined in (
10
).

Then, since
u
is given by (
20
), we have that
|\nabla u|^{n}=(x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}=1
on
\partial B_{1}
and the first derivative of
j(t)
, evaluated at
t=0
, is

j^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{n+1}V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Furthermore the second derivative, at the time
t=0
, is given by

\begin{split}j^{\prime\prime}(0)&=\frac{n-1}{n}\omega_{n}^{n-2}\left(\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{t}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\\
&\times\left\{\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[u_{t}^{2}(\Delta u-H)+V_{t}|_{t=0}\cdot\nu-2u_{t}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx+n\int_{\partial B_{1}}(V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}.\end{split}

Now, recalling that
\Delta u=n
on
\partial B_{1}
and
H|_{\partial B_{1}}=n-1
, we have the thesis.
∎

Once we know how to write the second variation of the torsion functional, we want to write in terms of spherical harmonics the deficit
\delta T
. This is the content of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

In the same assumptions of Theorem
3.3
consider the torsion deficit
\delta T(t)=1-T(\Omega(t))/T(B_{1})
. Then

\delta T(t)=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\left[(n+1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}-2\sum_{k\geq 0}ka_{k}^{2}+\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}(k^{2}-k)\right]+o(t^{2}),

where

a_{k}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)Y_{k}(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi},

and
\{Y_{k}(\xi)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of
spherical harmonics
that satisfy

-\Delta_{\xi}Y_{k}=k(k+n-2)Y_{k},\quad\|Y_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=1.

Proof.

We start by setting

V(x)=V|_{t=0}\cdot\nu(x),\quad A(x)=V_{t}|_{t=0}\cdot\nu(x).

We also introduce for
x\equiv(r,\xi)\in\Omega

v(r,\xi)=u_{t}(x),

where
u_{t}
is given by (
32
).
Now, from (
27
), we have that

j^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=0.

This outcome is expected, as

T
reaches its maximum at
t=0
. With this notation, we also have

\begin{split}j^{\prime\prime}(0)&=\frac{n-1}{n}\omega_{n}^{n-2}\left(\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}\right)^{2}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[(n+1)V^{2}(\xi)+A(\xi)+2V(\xi)\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=1}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}\\
&+\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx\\
&=\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left[(n+1)V^{2}(\xi)+A(\xi)+2V(\xi)\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=1}\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}+\frac{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}{(n+2)^{n-1}}\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx.\end{split}

Now, for
t
sufficiently small,
\partial\Omega(t)
can be parametrized as

r(\xi,t)=1+V(\xi)t+\frac{A(\xi)}{2}t^{2}+o(t^{2}),\quad r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\quad\xi\in\partial B_{1}.

If we apply the Taylor expansion around
t=0
we have

T(\Omega(t))=T(B_{1})+t\underbrace{j^{\prime}(0)}_{=0}+\frac{t^{2}}{2}j^{\prime\prime}(0)+o(t^{2})

and then

\delta T(t)=1-\frac{T(\Omega(t))}{T(\Omega(0))}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{j^{\prime\prime}(0)}{T(0)}+o(t^{2}).

Now we need an expression for
v(r,\xi)
. We observe that the equation for
u_{t}
is
S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{tij}=0
and, at
t=0
, since
S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=\delta_{ij}
, becomes
\Delta u_{t}=0
. Then in spherical coordinates we have

\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{n-1}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}\right)-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\xi}v=0&(r,\xi)\in(0,1)\times\partial B_{1}\\
v(1,\xi)=-V(\xi)&\xi\in\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

Now we want to write
V(\xi)
in terms of a family of spherical harmonics
\{Y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, which satisfy for all
k\geq 0

\begin{split}&V(\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}Y_{k}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\partial B_{1},\quad-\Delta_{\xi}Y_{k}=k(k+n-2)Y_{k},\\
&\|Y_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=1,\quad a_{k}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)Y_{k}(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi},\quad a_{0}=0,\quad\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}a_{k}^{2}.\end{split}

(33)

Then, with the separation of variables

v(r,\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}R_{k}(r)Y_{k}(\xi),

(34)

the equation becomes

-\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}-\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial r^{2}}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\xi}v=0,

that is from (
34
)

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left[-\frac{n-1}{r}R^{\prime}_{k}(r)Y_{k}(\xi)-R^{\prime\prime}_{k}(r)Y_{k}(\xi)-\frac{1}{r^{2}}R_{k}(r)\Delta_{\xi}Y_{k}(\xi)\right]=0.

Using (
33
) we get

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left[-\frac{n-1}{r}R^{\prime}_{k}(r)-R^{\prime\prime}_{k}(r)+\frac{k(k+n-2)}{r^{2}}R_{k}(r)\right]Y_{k}(\xi)=0,

but, since
\{Y_{k}\}_{k}
is a basis of
L^{2}
, then we have that, for all
k\geq 0
,

\begin{cases}&R^{\prime\prime}_{k}(r)+\frac{n-1}{r}R^{\prime}_{k}(r)-\frac{k(k+n-2)}{r^{2}}R_{k}(r)=0,\\
&R_{k}(1)=-a_{k},\\
&R^{\prime}_{k}(0)=0.\end{cases}

Explicitly solving the ODE, we arrive to

R_{k}(r)=c_{1}r^{k}+c_{2}r^{2-(n+k)},

with
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
to be determined. Since we are interested in solutions that are
L^{2}
, we choose
c_{2}=0
and then, from
R_{k}(1)=-a_{k}
we find

R_{k}(r)=-a_{k}r^{k}.

Now we are interested in the radial derivative of
v(r,\xi)
, evaluated in
r=1
, i.e.,

\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r,\xi)\bigg|_{r=1}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}R^{\prime}_{k}(1)Y_{k}(\xi)=-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}ka_{k}Y_{k}(\xi),

and, rewriting the torsion deficit, we have

\delta T(t)=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\left[\int_{\partial B_{1}}[(n+1)V^{2}(\xi)+A(\xi)-2V(\xi)\sum_{k\geq 0}ka_{k}Y_{k}(\xi)]\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx\right]+o(t^{2}).

Recalling that

\|V\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2},\quad\int_{S^{n-1}}V(\xi)Y_{k}(\xi)d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=a_{k},

and (see Appendix
A
)

\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}(k^{2}-k)

(35)

we find

\delta T(t)=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\left[(n+1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}-2\sum_{k\geq 0}ka_{k}^{2}+\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}(k^{2}-k)\right]+o(t^{2}).

∎

Now we want to write, in terms of spherical harmonic functions, the
Alexandrov-Fenchel deficit
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The Alexandrov-Fenchel deficit is given by

\delta_{AF}(t)=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\left[\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+(n-1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a^{2}_{k}\right].

Proof.

Since we are fixing the
(n-1)
-th quermassintegral, we can expand the volume inside the deficit and impose the conditions (
27
)

\begin{split}\delta_{AF}(t)&=1-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))}{W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega(t))}\\
&=1-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(0))+t\frac{d}{dt}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}}{\omega_{n}^{n(n+2)}}+o(t^{2}).\end{split}

(36)

In the same spirit of (
26
), we can state that

W_{0}(\Omega(t))=\omega_{n}+t\int_{\partial B_{1}}V(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}[A(\xi)+(n-1)V^{2}(\xi)]\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+o(t^{2}),

where we observe that the first order term is zero. Then we have

\begin{split}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}&=\omega_{n}^{n+2},\\
\frac{d}{dt}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}&=(n+2)W_{0}^{n+1}(\Omega(0))\frac{d}{dt}W_{0}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}=0,\\
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}&=(n+2)(n+1)W_{0}^{n}(\Omega(0))\left(\frac{d}{dt}W_{0}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}\right)^{2}+(n+2)W_{0}^{n+1}(\Omega(0))\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}W_{0}(\Omega(t))|_{t=0}\\
&=(n+2)\omega_{n}^{n+1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}[A(\xi)+(n-1)V^{2}(\xi)]\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Finally, from (
36
), we can rewrite the deficit as

\begin{split}\delta_{AF}(t)&=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}[A(\xi)+(n-1)V^{2}(\xi)]\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+o(t^{2})\\
&=-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\frac{n+2}{\omega_{n}}\left[\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}+(n-1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a^{2}_{k}\right].\end{split}

∎

From (
27
) we can rewrite the acceleration term as follows

\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=\frac{n^{2}-6n+7}{n-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}-\frac{2(n-3)}{n-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}S_{2}(D^{2}V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}.

(37)

Our aim is to write the previous integral in terms of spherical harmonics.

Lemma 3.6
.

The acceleration term is given by

\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=-\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2).

(38)

Proof.

Let’s start by recalling that

\int_{\partial B_{1}}Y_{i}(\xi)Y_{j}(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=0,\quad\text{ if }i\not=j,

we find

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}&=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}V\Delta_{\xi}V\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}Y_{k}\right)\left(-\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}k(k+n-2)Y_{k}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2)\int_{\partial B_{1}}Y_{k}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2).\end{split}

For
S_{2}(D^{2}V)
, we recall the following equation (see
[
18
]
, formula 92) that holds true for
t
sufficiently small and for every function that lives on the sphere

\int_{\partial B_{1}}S_{k}(D^{2}V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{n-k}{k}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V^{i}V_{j}[T_{k-2}]_{i}^{j}(D^{2}V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

(39)

for
k\geq 2
.
From (
39
) we have

\int_{\partial B_{1}}S_{2}(D^{2}V)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}V^{i}V_{j}\delta_{i}^{j}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|\nabla_{\xi}V|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{n-2}{2}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2).

Then, from (
37
), we get

\int_{\partial B_{1}}A(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}_{\xi}^{n-1}=-\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2).

∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Now we are able to prove the main theorem. Thanks to (
38
) we can rewrite the ratio
\delta T/\delta_{AF}
as follows

\frac{\delta T(t)}{\delta_{AF}(t)}=\frac{(n+1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a^{2}_{k}-2\sum_{k\geq 0}ka^{2}_{k}-\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}(k^{2}-k)}{(n-1)\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k(k+n-2)}+o(1).

Then, since
a_{0}=0
, we have

\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\delta T(t)}{\delta_{AF}(t)}\geq\inf_{k\geq 2}\left\{\frac{n+1-2k-k(k+n-2)+\frac{k^{2}-k}{n}}{n-1-k(k+n-2)}\right\}=:\inf_{k\geq 2}f(k),

(40)

where
a_{1}
provides no contribution, indeed
k=1
corresponds to a translation of
\Omega(0)
.

Now we want to show that the previous infimum is attained as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. In order to do this we see
f
as the restriction to
\mathbb{N}_{2}
of the following function

\tilde{f}:x\in\mathbb{R}_{2}\mapsto\frac{n+1-2x-x(x+n-2)+\frac{1}{n}(x^{2}-x)}{n-1-x(x+n-2)},

(41)

where with
\mathbb{N}_{2}
(
\mathbb{R}_{2}
) we denote the set of natural (real) numbers greater or equal then
2
.
The strategy of the proof is the following. We will show first that if
\overline{x}\geq 2
is a critical point for
\tilde{f}
, then it must be a
local maximum
. From this it will be clear that

\inf_{x\geq 2}\tilde{f}(x)=\min\{\tilde{f}(2),\,\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\tilde{f}(x)\}.

(42)

Once we know that (
42
) holds, we show that this minimum is the limit of
\tilde{f}
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
. These are the contents of the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\overline{x}\geq 2
. If
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\overline{x})=0
, then
\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})<0
, i.e.,
\overline{x}
is a local maximum for
\tilde{f}
.

Proof.

We call
N(x)
,
D(x)
the numerator and denominator, respectively, in (
41
). Let
\overline{x}
be a critical point of
\tilde{f}
, that is

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\overline{x})=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad N^{\prime}(\overline{x})D(\overline{x})-N(\overline{x})D^{\prime}(\overline{x})=0.

Then

\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})<0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad N^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})D(\overline{x})-N(\overline{x})D^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})<0.

We have

N^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})D(\overline{x})-N(\overline{x})D^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})=\frac{n-1}{n}D(\overline{x})-N(\overline{x}).

After straightforward computation we find

\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})<0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\overline{x}>\frac{n-1}{3n-1}.

(43)

Since
\frac{n-1}{3n-1}<1
and
\overline{x}\geq 2
, the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tilde{f}
be defined as in (
41
), then

\inf_{x\geq 2}\tilde{f}(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\tilde{f}(x).

Proof.

It follows from the previous lemma that

\inf_{x\geq 2}\tilde{f}(x)=\min\{\tilde{f}(2),\,\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\tilde{f}(x)\}.

It remains to show that

\frac{n^{2}+3n-2}{n(n+1)}=\tilde{f}(2)>\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\tilde{f}(x)=\frac{n-1}{n}.

The previous inequality is true if and only if

3n-1>0,

which is true for all
n\geq 2
.
∎

Now we are able to prove Theorem
1.1
.

proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From (
40
) and Lemma
4.2
follows that

\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\delta T(t)}{\delta_{AF}(t)}\geq\inf_{k\geq 2}f(k)=\frac{n-1}{n}.

For the upper bound, let
\Omega
be an open, bounded and convex set. From (
2
)-(
3
), we find

\begin{split}T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})-T(\Omega)&\leq\frac{\omega_{n}^{n}\zeta_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}{(n+2)^{n}}-\frac{|\Omega|^{n+2}}{(n+2)^{n}\omega_{n}^{2}}=\frac{W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}{(n+2)^{n}\omega_{n}^{n(n+1)}}-\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega)}{(n+2)^{n}\omega_{n}^{2}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(n+2)^{n}\omega_{n}^{n(n+1)}}\bigg[W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega)\bigg].\end{split}

After normalization, we have

\delta T=\frac{T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})-T(\Omega)}{T(\Omega^{*}_{n-1})}\leq 1-\omega_{n}^{(n-1)(n+2)}\frac{W_{0}^{n+2}(\Omega)}{W_{n-1}^{n(n+2)}(\Omega)}=\delta_{AF}.

∎

Appendix A
Proof of (
35
)

We start by recalling the identity (
7
)

S_{k}^{ij}(A)=\frac{1}{k}[T_{k-1}]_{i}^{j}(A),

that in our case is

S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{n}[T_{n-1}]_{i}^{j}(D^{2}u)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)!}\delta_{jj_{1}\dots j_{n-1}}^{ii_{1}\dots i_{n-1}}u_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots u_{i_{n-1}j_{n-1}}.

Then

\begin{split}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{ijt}&=\frac{1}{n}\left([T_{n-1}]_{i}^{j}(D^{2}u)\right)_{t}u_{ijt}=\frac{1}{n(n-1)!}\delta_{jj_{1}\dots j_{n-1}}^{ii_{1}\dots i_{n-1}}(u_{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots u_{i_{n-1}j_{n-1}})_{t}u_{ijt}\\
&=\frac{n-1}{n(n-1)!}\delta_{jj_{1}\dots j_{n-1}}^{ii_{1}\dots i_{n-1}}(u_{i_{1}j_{1}})_{t}u_{i_{2}j_{2}}\dots u_{i_{n-1}j_{n-1}}u_{ijt}.\end{split}

We remark that on the ball
\Omega(0)=B_{1}
the solution
u
to (
28
) satisfies
u_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
. Then, on
B_{1}
, iterating (
5
), we have

(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{ijt}=\frac{1}{n}\delta_{ii_{1}}^{jj_{1}}(u_{i_{1}j_{1}})_{t}(u_{ij})_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\left[(\Delta u_{t})^{2}-\sum_{ij}u_{ijt}^{2}\right].

Since
u_{t}
is harmonic in
B_{1}
, applying the divergence Theorem, we find

\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j}\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}_{tij}u\,dx=\frac{1}{n}\int_{B_{1}}u_{tij}u_{j}u_{ti}\,dx=\frac{1}{2n}\int_{B_{1}}(u_{ti}^{2})_{j}u_{j}\,dx.

Now applying again the divergence Theorem, since on
\partial B_{1}
it holds
|\nabla u|=1
and
\Delta u=n
,

\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u\,dx=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i}\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{ti}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}_{ti}\,dx.

(44)

The second term in the last equality, integrating by parts, is

\sum_{i}\int_{B_{1}}u_{ti}^{2}\,dx=\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{t}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}V\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r,\xi)\bigg|_{r=1}V(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}k,

(45)

where, again, we have used
\Delta u_{t}=0
.
For the first term in (
44
) we recall the following integration by parts formula (see
[
8
, Theorem 5.4.13]
)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}f\Delta g\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}+f\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial\nu^{2}}+Hf\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(46)

Then, from (
46
), recalling that
u_{t}
is harmonic, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\nabla&u_{t}\cdot\nabla u_{t}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial B_{1}}u_{t}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{t}}{\partial\nu^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial B_{1}}Hu_{t}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r,\xi)\bigg|_{r=1}\right)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}-\int_{\partial B_{1}}V\cdot\nu\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial r^{2}}(r,\xi)\bigg|_{r=1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}-(n-1)\int_{\partial B_{1}}V\cdot\nu\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}(r,\xi)\bigg|_{r=1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\xi}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}[k^{2}+k(k-1)+k(n-1)].\end{split}

(47)

then, from (
44
)- (
45
)-(
47
), we have

\int_{B_{1}}(S_{n}^{ij}(D^{2}u))_{t}u_{tij}u=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{2}(k^{2}-k),

that is (
35
).

Determinants of Birth Asphyxia Among Neonates in Selected Hospitals of Ethiopia: A Multi-site Unmatched Case–Control Study
Authors: Ketema Girma, Eden Girmaye Tefera, Bayisa Abdissa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBirth asphyxia is a significant cause of most perinatal mortality and morbidity worldwide. In Ethiopia, maternal medical conditions, such as pregnancy-induced hypertension and diabetes, have significantly contributed to the increase in neonatal morbidity and adverse birth outcomes. The study aimed to identify the determinants of birth asphyxia among neonates delivered in Southwest Shoa Zone public hospitals, Ethiopia.MethodsA retrospective unmatched case-control study design was used among neonates delivered at a selected hospital in Southwest Shoa Zone, Ethiopia, between 20 August 2022 and 19 November 2022. Cases and controls were enrolled in a ratio of 1:2. The cases were newborns with birth asphyxia, Apgar score (Appearance, Pulse, Grimaces, Activity, and Respiration) < 7 within the first five minutes at birth. Newborns without birth asphyxia with Apgar score ≥ 7 within the first five minutes at birth were selected as controls. For each enrolled case of birth asphyxia, two unmatched control newborns who delivered without birth asphyxia were enrolled in selected hospitals during the study period. Data were collected using structured questionnaires and medical records. The collected data were cleaned, coded and entered into Epi Info version 7, then exported to Statistical Package for Social Sciences version 26 for analysis. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to identify the determinants of birth asphyxia, with the level of significance set at p-value < 0.05.ResultsThe study revealed that the mean age of the case mothers was 27 years (SD = ± 7.3) and that of control mothers was 27.1 years (SD = ± 5.4). Meconium-stained amniotic fluid [AOR = 1.9; 95% CI (1.02–3.6)], pregnancy-induced hypertension [AOR = 5.8; 95% CI (2.65–13.63), instrumental delivery [AOR = 5.71; 95% CI (1.56 − 28.2)], post term [AOR = 9.68 95% CI (1.87–26.87)], induction/augmentation of labor [AOR = 5.09; 95% CI (1.06–24.36)] and non-cephalic presentation [AOR = 1.68; 95%CI (0.24–7.55)] were significantly associated with birth asphyxia.ConclusionsBirth asphyxia was significantly associated with maternal and obstetric factors, including meconium-stained amniotic fluid, pregnancy-induced hypertension, instrumental delivery, post-term, induction of labor and non-cephalic presentation. These findings highlight the importance of comprehensive maternal and newborn care, including strengthening antenatal care to identify and manage pregnancy-related complications, promoting maternal recognition of danger signs during pregnancy, and ensuring timely access to skilled intrapartum and emergency obstetric care to reduce the incidence of birth asphyxia.Keywords: Asphyxias, Birth complications, Case-control, Determinants, Neonate

Introduction

Birth asphyxia is the inability of a newborn to initiate and sustain adequate spontaneous breathing at birth [1]. Neonatal asphyxia may evolve to hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy, presented with neurocognitive, motor and neurosensory impairment [2]. Birth asphyxia is not only the predictor of prenatal mortality and morbidity, but recent studies have found that birth asphyxia also increases the risk of irreversible brain damage, adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes and subsequent disabilities later in life [3]. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported that globally, an estimated 2.4 million were dying during their first twenty-eight days of life in 2020 [4]. There are large disparities across regions; Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for 43% of global newborn deaths at a rate of 27 deaths per 1000 live births in 2021 [5]. The target of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) is to end by 2030, preventable deaths of newborns by reducing neonatal mortality to at least as low as 12 deaths per 1,000 live births and under-five mortality to at least as low as 25 deaths per 1,000 live births [6]. Nevertheless, neonatal morbidity in Ethiopia has increased tremendously, with substantial contributions from mothers with preexisting diseases. Despite national progress in reducing under-five mortality, neonatal mortality in Ethiopia was reported as 97 per 1000 live births [4]. The major causes of neonatal mortality in Ethiopia are prematurity, birth asphyxia and neonatal sepsis [7]. Despite the implementation of different strategies and interventions at different stages, the mortality and morbidity in neonates, infants and under-five children are still a challenge. In Ethiopia, birth asphyxia is one of the major causes of neonatal morbidity and mortality [8] and is known to be one of the most common intrapartum problems encountered in health facilities occurring frequently, in our setting, greater emphasis has been placed on the Apgar score at birth rather than at the fifth minute, despite its crucial long-term implications. Therefore, this case-control study may provide valuable insights into the potential risk factors associated with birth asphyxia.

Methods

Study design and setting

A facility-based retrospective unmatched case-control study design was conducted. The study was conducted in public hospitals in the Southwest Shoa zone, Oromia, Ethiopia. The zonal town is Woliso, which is 114 km far from Addis Ababa, the capital city of Ethiopia. The total population of Southwest Shoa Zone is around 1.12 million, according to the Central Statistical Agency of Ethiopia [9]. The Southwest Shoa Zone has 11 districts around 286 kebele, of which 22 are urban and 264 are rural areas. There are six hospitals, 54 health centers, and 254 health posts in Southwest Shoa Zone. The study period was from 20 August 2022 to 19 November 2022. The source of the population was all newborns delivered at the hospitals of Southwest Shoa Zone for both cases and controls. Cases and controls were enrolled in a ratio of 1:2. A case was newborns who was diagnosed with birth asphyxia with an Apgar score < 7 within the first five minutes at birth by the health care providers who attended delivery. Newborns without birth asphyxia with Apgar score ≥ 7 within the first five minutes at birth were selected as controls. For each enrolled case of birth asphyxia, two unmatched control newborns who delivered without birth asphyxia were enrolled in selected hospitals during the study period.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

All newborn delivered at hospitals of South-west Shoa Zone during data collection period were eligible for both cases and controls.

Exclusion criteria

All newborns who did not breathe or show any evidence of life (such as beating of heart, umbilical cord pulsation, and definite movement of voluntary muscles) were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination and sampling techniques

The sample size was estimated by the double proportion formula using Epi Info statistical software version 7 for determinant factors for case and control. Assumption of power of 80%, considering, a 95% confidence interval and an adjusted odd ratio of 3.58, 8.3% proportion (P) of cesarean delivery among the control as a determinant variable for birth asphyxia from a previous study [10]. Then lastly, 5% of the non-response rate added and the total sample size was 210 (70 cases and 140 controls) that were proportionally allocated to each hospital based on the past three months’ (May to July, 2022) delivery report of six public hospitals (Leman Primary Hospital = 177, Bantu Primary Hospitals = 176 Woliso General Hospital = 201, Tulu Bolo General Hospitals = 376, Amaya Primary Hospitals = 162 and St. Lukes General Hospital = 698) (Fig. 1). In order to choose participants, we used medical records from each hospital. The cases were selected from all newborns who were diagnosed with birth asphyxia using Apgar score classification and documented at Southwest Shoa Zone hospitals during the data collection period. The ratio of case to control was 1:2. The controls were chosen from the following two newborns without birth asphyxia listed on the registration.

Study variables

Outcome variable

Neonates with birth asphyxia.

Predictor variables

Socio demographic factors (mother’s age, height, mid upper arm circumstance, residence, education level, and marital status, and), Antenatal care related factors characteristics (number of ANC visits, history of still birth, and pregnancy interval), Intra partum related factors (antepartum hemorrhage, partograph use, prolonged labor, premature rupture of membrane, color amniotic fluid, fetal presentation, oligohydramnios, placental classification, gestational age during labor, referral system, mode of transport during labor and delivery, neonatal birth weight).

Operational definition

Birth asphyxia: The newborn failure to initiate and sustain breathing at birth within the first five minutes and those Apgar score less than seven [1]. Low birth weight: Neonates those less than 2500gm during birth [11]. Case: Neonates with Apgar score less than 7 within the first five minute which diagnosed by health provider who attended delivery. Control: Neonates those Apgar score greater than or equal seven within the first five minutes which diagnosed by health provider who attended delivery. Post term neonate: Neonate delivered after 42 weeks of gestational age [12]. Premature rupture of membrane: Early rupture of membrane 2 h before onset labor [12]. Prolonged labor: labor exceed more than 8 h in multi gravid and 12 h in primi-gravid after active first stage of labor [12]. Anemia: Mothers whose hemoglobin level less than 11 g/dl [12].

Data collection tools and procedures

Data was collected using structured questionnaires, medical records. Mid Upper Arm Circumstance (MUAC) and height of mothers. After the inclusion of the two groups, case and control, mothers who gave birth in West Shoa Zone public hospitals, the first questionnaire, which included the antepartum and intra-partum-related characteristics was obtained from the mothers’ medical records. A second questionnaire, including the MUAC and the height of mothers before delivery, was administered. The third questionnaire, which included socio-demographic characteristics was collected after delivery at the postnatal ward. The questionnaire was developed based on a review of different literature [8, 11, 13–15]. There were six data collectors, all of whom had a Bachelor of Science degree in Midwifery. The score had five components, such as appearance (color), pulse rate, grimaces (reflex), activity (muscle tone), and respiration rate. These were measured by scores of (0, 1, 2) and the sum scores of the ≥ 7 was not asphyxiated and < 7 was asphyxiated. The study participants were interviewed at the postnatal ward after they became stable for getting adequate information.

Data quality control

The study implemented standardized data collection procedures and supervised how these procedures were undertaken. The questionnaire was designed in English, translated to the Afan Oromo language and then back translated to the English language. Training was given for data collectors and supervisor to ensure unified data collection administration. A pretest was employed on 5% of the sample in hospitals that were not part of the study, and these results were excluded from the final findings to ensure the internal validity of the tools. Content validity was assessed by five experienced researchers and faculty members of Ambo University of Health Sciences. The internal validity was ensured by measuring the content validity ratio and was found to be 0.2. The quality of data was reviewed for its completeness and stability by the supervisor rigorously. The conception of reliability as the reproducibility of the tools on repeated trials on the same data [16]. Reliability of the instrument was computed with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.86.

Data processing and analysis

The collected data was coded and entered into EPI Info version 7, then exported to Statistical Package for Social Science version 26. Frequency and cross tabulation were carried out to summarize the descriptive statistics. The binary logistic regression was employed to identify the association between the outcome variable and predictable variables.

For bivariate analysis, we used a cutoff point p-value of < 0.25 to identify important covariates, the ones that are at least moderately associated with the response variable. We used a 75% confidence interval, which was purposeful selection as proposed by Hosmer and Lemeshow [17]. We checked the assumption of multicollinearity by variable inflation factors. The model fitness test results have a p-value >0.05. Overall, there was no variable with multicollinearity, and outlines were identified.

The variables with p-values < 0.25 were taken into multiple logistic regressions. The odds ratio was estimated with a 95% confidence interval and used to identify the statistical associations. Finally, statistical significance was set at a p-value < 0.05 and the final result was presented using text, tables and graphs.

Results

Socio demographic related factors

A total sample size of 210 (70 cases and 140 controls) participated in the study with a response rate of 100%. The study revealed that the mean age of the case mothers was 27 years (SD = ± 7.3) and that of control mothers was 27.1 years (SD = ± 5.4). The height of the mothers, 19 (27.1) cases and 20 (14.3) controls were < 153 cm. The MUAC of the mothers, 20 (28.5) cases and 43 (20.47) controls were < 23 cm. Regarding the residence of the mothers, 39 (55.7%) cases and 51 (26.2%) controls had come from rural areas. Around 10 (15.2%) of the cases and 24 (17.1%) of the controls had never attended primary school. Regarding the marital status of the mothers 4 (5.7%) of cases and 5 (3.5%) of controls were unmarried.

Antepartum related characteristics

This finding showed that from the totals of study participants 17 (24.3%) cases and 18 (12.8%) controls had no ANC visit at least once for this delivery cycle, Of those attended ANC, only 7 [10] cases and 13 (9.3) controls had visited ANC four times and above respectively. Regarding history of still birth from total study participants 10 (14.2) of the cases and 13 (9.3) of controls had history of still birth at least once. Concerning pregnancy interval related, the mothers those gave birth before 2 year were 16 (25.8%) in cases and 16 (17.0%) in controls respectively (Table 1).

Determinants of birth asphyxias

First binary logistic regression analysis was performed, variables such us referred mothers, neonatal birth weight, placenta calcified, history still birth, onset of labor, meconium stained amniotic fluid, non-cephalic presentation, mode of delivery, post term neonate, oligohydrominous, hypertension and ANC visits were P-values less than 0.25. Then variable with p- values less than 0.25 was transferred to multi-variable logistic regression analysis. Finally, the multivariable logistic regression analysis showed that induction/augmentation, meconium stained amniotic fluid, non-cephalic presentation; instrumental delivery, post term neonates, and pregnancy-induced hypertension were significantly associated with birth asphyxias.

In this study the odds ratio of birth asphyxias were 1.92 [AOR = 1.92; 95%CI (1.02–3.6)] times higher among meconium stained amniotic fluid than those neonates who had with no meconium stained amniotic fluid. In addition, neonates delivered by instruments were 5.71[AOR = 5.71; 95%CI (1.56–13.2)] more likely to develop birth asphyxia than neonates delivered by spontaneous vaginal delivery. The other findings indicate that the odd ratio of birth asphyxias were 3.03[AOR = 3.05; 95%CI (1.02–8.94)] times higher among induction of labor than spontaneous onset of labor. Similarly in this study the odd ratio of birth asphyxias were 5.83[AOR = 5.83; 95%CI (2.65–13.63)] times higher among neonates of mothers with pregnancy-induced hypertension than neonates of mothers with no known pregnancy-induced hypertension. The odd ratio of birth asphyxias were 9.7 [AOR = 9.64 (95%CI 1.46–26.99)] times higher among post term neonates than the term neonates and also in this study the odds ratio of birth asphyxias were 1.68[AOR = 1.68; (95%CI 0.24–7.55)] times higher among non-cephalic presentation than those born cephalic to develop birth asphyxias when they compared (Table 3).

Discussions

This study was conducted to identify the determinants of birth asphyxia among neonates delivered in Southwest Shoa, Zone hospitals. Birth asphyxia, which was defined in this study newborn delivered within the first five minute of low Apgar score [1]. These associations persisted after adjusting for a wide range of sociodemographic, medical, and obstetric factors. This study revealed that labor induction/augmentation, meconium stained amniotic fluid, non-cephalic presentation, and instrumental delivery, post-term neonate, and pregnancy-induced hypertension were identified as determinants of birth asphyxia among neonates delivered in Southwest Shoa hospitals. A different study showed that the determinants of birth asphyxia vary from place to place. Labor stimulation either induction or augmentation had high likelihood of birth hypoxia. The finding of this study is cognate with previous studies from China and Turkey, where prolonged labor were shown to increase the risk of birth hypoxia [13, 15]. This is due to an increased content of oxytocin in blood [18].

The finding of this study revealed that there was significant association between meconium stained amniotic fluid and birth asphyxia. This is consistent with another studies in Pakistan, India and Tanzania, where reported that neonates born through meconium stained amniotic fluid are at risk of developing intrapartum asphyxia [19–21]. Similarly, another study shows that neonates with meconium stained amniotic fluid were more likely to develop severe respiratory distress [22].

New born delivered by instruments poses an increased risk of birth asphyxia. An assisted birth involving forceps made significance factor to the likelihood of posing breathing difficulties as compared with other deliveries. This finding is in line with previous studies [13, 18] revealed that operative deliveries were significantly associated with birth asphyxia. The possible reason is instrumental delivery may cause birth trauma, such as skull fracture and intracranial hemorrhage that endowed breathing difficulties. However, the findings is invariable with a study done in Iran [23] argue that mode of delivery did not have any statistical significant influence on the newborn’s likelihood with birth asphyxia. Hence, the researchers suggest a thorough study of a large population to rule out the underlying risk factors for intrapartum asphyxia.

Non-cephalic presentation poses an increased risk for breathing difficulties. The finding of this study uncovered that neonates presented by breech were more likely to develop birth asphyxia than vertex presentation. This study congruent with a study conducted in Nepal [24] and Uganda [14] suggests that birth asphyxia is higher among women presented with malposition.

The risk of asphyxia is higher among post-term newborns. This findings is in line with guidelines for clinical practice highlights that the secondary to meconium aspiration syndrome [25]. Nonetheless, the finding of this study is converse with other study demonstrated that prematurity had a higher odd for neonatal asphyxia incident [26].

Maternal conditions during pregnancy have significant causes for adverse perinatal outcomes. This study revealed that adverse neonatal outcomes including birth asphyxia and Apgar score < 7 at five minutes were increased in the pregnancies complicated by hypertension. The finding of this study is congruent with a systematic review shows that pregnancy induced hypertension was discovered to be the most substantial influences on the likelihood of birth asphyxia [27]. Similarly, a study conducted in Nigeria also reported that eclampsia was found to be a risk factor of birth asphyxia [28]. This might be chronic hypoxia associated with preeclampsia causes vascular or metabolic alterations [2].

Conclusions

This study showed that meconium stained amniotic fluid, hypertension, instrumental delivery, post term, induction or augmentation and non-cephalic presentation were the determinants of birth asphyxias. These findings highlight the need for improving early identification and management of maternal complications such as pregnancy-induced hypertension during ANC visits, enhancing health education of pregnant women’s recognition of danger signs, and ensuring timely access to skilled intrapartum and emergency obstetric care are highly recommended in order to reduce the incidence of birth asphyxia. The study suggest that strengthening referral systems, improving access to emergency obstetric services, and enhancing facility readiness can reduce delays in managing complications. Additionally, capacity-building training for healthcare workers in South-west Shoa hospitals may directly reduce birth asphyxia related morbidity and mortality. Finally, the study provides evidence to guide maternal and child health policies, especially in resource-limited settings, by prioritizing interventions that address both antenatal and intrapartum determinants of birth asphyxia. Nevertheless, this study is limited by its diagnostic modalities for birth asphyxia. The study used the Apgar score classification for cases and controls. Thus, diagnosing birth asphyxia using Apgar score may affect its validation and precision, as variations in scoring approaches across delivery settings introduce subjectivity. Therefore, the study implied that validated instruments are needed to measure birth asphyxia. Moreover, this study is limited by its sample size and retrospective design, implying further large-scale prospective studies to validate these findings and refine clinical diagnosis of birth asphyxia.

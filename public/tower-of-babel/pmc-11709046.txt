Assessment of national tobacco control program (NTCP) initiative on tobacco cessation among transgenders in Puducherry – A quasi-experimental study
Authors: Logesh Sidhu, Suriya Kumar, Priyadharshini Ragavane, Priyanga Chandrasekaran, Senthil Murugappan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:This study aims to evaluate the impact of the National Tobacco Control Program initiative on the attitudes toward tobacco use and tobacco dependency among transgender individuals in Puducherry.Materials and Methods:A Quasi-experimental design was used to examine the effectiveness of the intervention. The study involved 52 transgender individuals from the SCOHD organization in Puducherry. The intervention included two awareness sessions, the Distribution of IEC materials during each session, and recreational activities for the participants. Changes in attitude toward tobacco use were measured using the Intention to Quit Tobacco questionnaire, while tobacco dependency among participants was assessed using the Fagerstrom Nicotine Dependency Scale (FNDS). Measurements were taken at baseline and third month to track the evolution of attitudes and dependency.Results:The program resulted in significant shifts in attitude and nicotine dependency among transgender individuals. This program indicated a positive trend, with an increase in individuals progressing to the contemplation and preparation stages, suggesting a growing willingness to reduce or quit tobacco use. The Fagerstrom Nicotine Dependency Scale showed a decrease in nicotine dependency over three months.Conclusion:The NTCP awareness sessions and follow-ups had a noticeable impact on altering attitudes toward tobacco use and reducing nicotine dependency among transgender individuals in Puducherry.

Introduction

Through the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC) and MPOWER, the World Health Organisation (WHO) has brought tobacco cessation (TCs) to a wider audience worldwide. The National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP), three regional Quitline cessation, a few premier National institutes, professional dental associations, and other organizations have made quitting tobacco easier in India.[1] However, certain marginalized communities, such as the transgender population, encounter unique challenges in accessing these crucial interventions. The transgender community faces heightened vulnerability to tobacco use due to a myriad of factors including social stigma, discrimination, stress, and limited access to healthcare services. These barriers significantly impede their ability to seek targeted treatments and support for tobacco cessation.[2]

Recent studies highlight the urgent need for enhanced educational campaigns by health authorities and non-governmental organizations (NGOs)/community-based organizations (CBOs) to raise awareness about the specific health risks associated with tobacco use within the transgender community. In this study, the prevalence of the current use of any form of tobacco products among transgenders was 64.4%. These alarming figures highlight the necessity for tailored tobacco control programs to address the unique challenges faced by transgender individuals and to mitigate the associated health risks.[3] Despite these recognized challenges, there remains a notable lack of targeted research and tobacco control initiatives tailored to this community’s needs. This highlights the necessity for the development of effective and inclusive programs that address the unique barriers faced by transgender individuals in accessing healthcare and tobacco cessation services.[4]

NTCP in India offers a comprehensive framework for tobacco control efforts nationwide, with components including public awareness, policy advocacy, and community-based interventions.[5] However, the effectiveness of these programs in altering attitudes and behaviors among transgender individuals remains largely unexplored. This knowledge gap makes the critical importance of evaluating the impact of the NTCP on transgender tobacco use, particularly in regions like Puducherry where the program is actively implemented.

By examining the changes in attitudes and tobacco dependency following targeted awareness sessions, this research seeks to provide valuable insights into the efficacy of tailored interventions for this marginalized group. Crucially, the objective is to determine whether focused awareness programs and subsequent follow-ups can positively influence attitudes toward tobacco use and reduce nicotine dependency within the transgender community.

In this current study, we received assistance from the Sahodaran Community-oriented Health Development Society (SCOHD) organization in Puducherry, which is run exclusively by transgender individuals.[6] Their support has been instrumental in facilitating the targeted awareness sessions and follow-up activities, ensuring that the interventions are culturally sensitive and effectively address the specific needs of the transgender community.

By assessing the effectiveness of tailored interventions, this study holds the potential to guide the development of future public health strategies. These strategies aim to create more inclusive and effective tobacco control programs that cater specifically to the diverse needs of marginalized populations, including transgender individuals, thus fostering healthier communities and reducing health disparities. This study aims to assess the effectiveness of the National Tobacco Control Program in reducing tobacco use within the transgender community in Puducherry through a quasi-experimental design.

Discussion

The results from this study offer valuable insights into various aspects of the transgender community’s demographics, tobacco usage, and intervention outcomes.

Out of 183 registered transgender individuals, only 52 showed interest in attending the program indicating that 28.4% were willing to engage. This relatively low participation rate may point to various barriers like stigma and discrimination are often the reasons faced by transgender individuals to avoid public programs.[12] Lack of awareness about the program, as well as logistical challenges like transportation, timing, or cost, could also contribute to the low engagement rate. Addressing these barriers requires a more inclusive and accessible approach to program design and outreach.[13]

In terms of age distribution, the study found a significant proportion of younger participants, with 34.6%, this aligns with studies suggesting that younger transgender individuals might be more open to engaging in community programs, possibly due to increased visibility and societal acceptance in recent years.[14]

Regarding education, the majority of the study participants had completed high school. This finding highlights the need to provide educational support to transgender individuals who might face discrimination in academic settings.[15] Moreover, the proportion of participants with a middle school education or less underscores the importance of creating pathways for further education and job opportunities within transgender communities.[16]

The study’s analysis of marital status revealed that the majority of participants were not married. This reflects the challenges transgender individuals face in forming and maintaining relationships due to societal stigma and discrimination. This observation shows the need for community support and programs that foster social connections and relationship-building among transgender individuals, especially those who may feel isolated or marginalized.[17]

Tobacco usage was prevalent among participants, with a notable majority using smokeless tobacco. This could be linked to cultural factors or perceived reduced health risks compared to smoking, although smokeless tobacco carries its risks. The high rate of tobacco use among peers suggests that social circles play a significant role in influencing individual behavior. This reinforces the importance of peer-led programs and community-based interventions to address tobacco use in transgender communities.[18]

The study demonstrated a positive impact of the tobacco cessation intervention. The increase in intention to quit post-intervention and the difference in Fagerström scores from pre- to post-intervention indicate that well-designed interventions can lead to significant behavior changes. These outcomes highlight the effectiveness of educational and support-based programs for smoking cessation among transgender individuals. These findings align with previous studies that have also shown the efficacy of targeted interventions in promoting tobacco cessation in this population.[19]

The study has certain limitations with a small sample size due to the unique characteristics of the transgender population, including their diverse lifestyles and mobility. Factors like seeking acceptance and access to gender-affirming services contribute to this mobility, making it challenging to recruit larger samples. While this size may limit generalizability, it was sufficient for evaluating the impact of tobacco cessation interventions in a real-world setting.

The study may lack limited long-term follow-up data to assess the sustainability of intervention effects beyond the immediate post-intervention period. To address these limitations and advance research, future studies will include long-term follow-ups to assess intervention sustainability. Collaboration with the National Tobacco Control Program will explore alternative cessation methods like Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT) and aromatherapy inhalers, targeting nicotine dependency in the transgender community. These efforts aim to improve the accessibility and effectiveness of tailored cessation interventions.

Conclusion

The study concluded that the significant increase in intention to quit tobacco and the reduction in nicotine dependency scores post-intervention highlights the potential efficacy of targeted programs in behavior change and tobacco cessation within this population. Collaborating with organizations like the National Tobacco Control Program (NTCP) could facilitate further research into alternative cessation methods such as Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT) and other traditional approaches. Furthermore, qualitative studies are necessary to understand the factors responsible for tobacco usage among transgender individuals and to identify effective strategies to combat it.

The pants graph of a rose
Donggyun Seo

Abstract
. We introduce the concept of a pants decomposition for a rose, where a rose is defined as a wedge sum of finitely many circles, and construct the corresponding pants graph of a rose. A pants decomposition of a rose leads to the formation of a simplicial graph, referred to as the pants graph of a rose, consisting of all possible pants decompositions. Recall that the outer automorphism group of a free group is isomorphic to the group of homotopy classes of homotopy equivalences of a rose. The natural isometric action of this group on the pants graph induces a coarsely Lipschitz orbit map. Additionally, we construct a coarsely Lipschitz map from the pants graph to the free splitting complex. These results imply that the pants graph of a rose is both connected and unbounded.

1.
Introduction

For a free group
F_{n}
of rank
n
, the dynamical behavior of its outer automorphism group
\Out(F_{n})
has been extensively studied through various isometric actions. One particularly well-known object in this context is the Culler–Vogtmann outer space, which is equipped with a Lipschitz metric. However, the Lipschitz metric is not symmetric, and its symmetrization, while intriguing, is not a geodesic metric, as shown by Francaviglia and Martino
[
FM11
]
.

In a parallel development, the Thurston metric on Teichmüller space, closely related to the Lipschitz metric, has been studied extensively. The Thurston metric diverges from the Teichmüller metric in the thin parts, as demonstrated by Choi and Rafi
[
CR07
]
, yet remains uniformly bounded in thick regions.

Over the last decade, researchers have developed several Gromov-hyperbolic complexes associated with
\Out(F_{n})
, such as the sphere complexes
[
Hat95
]
, free splitting complexes
[
HM13
,
HM19
]
, free factor complexes
[
HV98
]
, and others. Coarsely Lipschitz maps from the outer space to these complexes have been constructed, providing insights into the geometric structure of
\Out(F_{n})

[
KR14
,
DT17
]
. A map between two metric spaces is said to be coarsely Lipschitz if it satisfies a specific bounded distortion condition between distances in the spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

For two metric spaces
(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})
and
(\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathcal{Y}})
, a map
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is said to be
(A,B)
–coarsely Lipschitz
if

d_{\mathcal{Y}}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))\leq Ad_{\mathcal{X}}(x,y)+B

for every
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
.

For orientable surfaces of finite type, Brock
[
Bro03
]
demonstrated that the pants graph representing the simplicial structure of all pants decompositions captures the large-scale geometry of the Weil–Petersson metric on Teichmüller space. This relationship highlights how combinatorial properties of the pants graph can inform geometric structures.

In this paper, we focus on pants decompositions of surfaces marked by a rose, which may be orientable or non-orientable. We introduce four elementary moves that modify these decompositions and define a simplicial graph, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, as the pants graph of a rose. Additionally, we study the induced subgraph
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
, which consists of orientable surfaces marked by a rose.

Our main result demonstrates the relationship between the pants graph and well-known
\Out(F_{n})
-complexes, as shown in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
n\geq 3
, there exist coarsely Lipschitz maps

\mathcal{K}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{n}\to\mathcal{FS}_{n}

where
\mathcal{K}_{n}
is the spine of the outer space and
\mathcal{FS}_{n}
is the free splitting complex.

Each of these maps is coarsely
\Out(F_{n})
-equivariant, implying that both
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are connected. Moreover, since the free splitting complex
\mathcal{FS}_{n}
is unbounded, it follows that
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are also unbounded. This leads to the following corollary:

Corollary 1.3
.

For each
n\geq 3
, both
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are connected and unbounded.

The Orientable Pants Graph of the Rose with Two Petals

Before introducing the pants graph of a free group of arbitrary rank, we first examine the
orientable pants graph
of the rose with two petals.
A compact orientable surface
X
is homotopy equivalent to
R_{2}
if and only if
X
is either a pair of pants (also called a
trouser
) or a once-holed torus.
Originally, a
pants decomposition
of a surface is defined as a maximal collection of homotopically non-trivial, pairwise disjoint, non-peripheral essential simple closed curves.

The pants graph of a once-punctured torus coincides with its curve graph, known as the
Farey graph
, which is illustrated on the left side of Figure
1
.
In contrast, the pants graph of a pair of pants is trivial–it consists of a single vertex, represented by the empty set, since there are no non-peripheral essential simple closed curves on it.

For an orientable surface
X
, a
marking
is defined as a homotopy equivalence
h_{X}:R_{2}\to X
, and the pair
(h_{X},X)
is called a
marked surface
.
A pants decomposition of a surface can thus be redefined as a maximal collection of homotopically disjoint essential simple closed curves.
In the case of a marked surface, the decomposition is denoted by the triple
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
, which we refer to as a
pants decomposition of
R_{2}
.

Two marked surfaces
(h_{X},X)
and
(h_{Y},Y)
are called
marking-equivalent
if there exists a homeomorphism
f:X\to Y
such that
h_{Y}
is homotopic to
f\circ h_{X}
.
In this case, the homeomorphism
f
is referred to as a
marking equivalence
.
Two pants decompositions are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a marking equivalence between them, such that one decomposition is the image of the other under this equivalence.

We now define a simplicial graph, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
, as follows.
The vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
are equivalence classes of pants decompositions of
R_{2}
.
An edge between two vertices
P
and
Q
is defined by the following conditions:

(1)

P
corresponds to a marked surface
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
, where
X
is a once-holed torus.

(2)

Q
corresponds to the trivial decomposition
(\emptyset,h_{Y},Y)
, where
Y
is a pair of pants.

(3)

There exists a map
f:S^{1}\to R_{n}
such that
h_{X}\circ f
is homotopic to a curve in
P_{X}
and
h_{Y}\circ f
is homotopic to a boundary component of
Y
.

We call
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
the
orientable pants graph of
R_{2}
.
If a vertex of
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
corresponds to a simple closed curve on a once-holed torus, then it is adjacent to infinitely many vertices in the pants graph.
On the other hand, if a vertex corresponds to a pair of pants, it is adjacent to exactly three vertices, i.e., finitely many.
These three vertices are adjacent to each other in the Farey graph because any two of them form a free basis of the fundamental group of the once-holed torus.

the Farey graph and the orientable pants graph of
R_{2}

Theorem 2.1
.

\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
is quasi-isometric to the Farey graph.

Proof.

Let
S_{1,1}
be a once-holed torus with a marking
h:R_{n}\to S_{1,1}
.
The curve complex
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
is a flag complex of the Farey graph, which is homeomorphic to the two-dimensional Euclidean plane.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
denote the barycentric subdivision of the curve complex
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
.
Notice that the 1-skeleton of
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
is quasi-isometric to
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
.

Now, define a map
\varphi:\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}\to\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})^{(1)}
as follows:

•

For each pants decomposition
P
of
S_{1,1}
, let
\varphi(P)
be
P
itself.

•

For a pair of pants
Q
, define
\varphi(Q)
as the midpoint of the vertices representing the boundary curves of
Q
.

This map
\varphi
preserves the adjacency of vertices, and we extend it to a simplicial map.

The image of
\varphi
contains all vertices except for the midpoints of edges of
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
.
Observe that the closed 1-neighborhood of the image of
\varphi
covers
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})^{(1)}
.
Thus, the image of
\varphi
is 1-dense.

Now, consider two vertices
P^{\prime},P^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
.
Let
\varphi(P^{\prime})=\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}=\varphi(P^{\prime\prime})
be a geodesic in
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
.
If some
\gamma_{i}
is not contained in the image of
\varphi
, then it must be the midpoint of an edge.
This implies that both
\gamma_{i-1}
and
\gamma_{i+1}
belong to the image of
\varphi
.

Therefore, there exists a vertex
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
in the image of
\varphi
such that the sequence
\gamma_{i-1},\gamma_{i}^{\prime},\gamma_{i+1}
forms a path.
By applying this method inductively, we obtain a geodesic path from
\varphi(P^{\prime})
to
\varphi(P^{\prime\prime})
that lies entirely within the image of
\varphi
.

This shows that
\varphi
is an isometric embedding into
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
.
Hence,
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
is quasi-isometric to
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
.
Since
\hat{\mathcal{C}}(S_{1,1})
is quasi-isometric to
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
, it follows that
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{+}
is quasi-isometric to
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,1})
.
∎

3.
Pants Decompositions and Pants Graphs of Roses

We now focus on the
n
-rose for
n\geq 3
.
In this section, we introduce four elementary moves that transform pants decompositions, and using these moves, we define the pants graph of the
n
-rose.

To define a pants decomposition of
R_{n}
, we need compact surfaces that are homotopy equivalent to
R_{n}
.
An orientable surface of genus
g
with
b
boundary curves is homotopy equivalent to the
n
-rose if and only if
n=2g-1+b
.
For the non-orientable case, a non-orientable surface with
c
crosscaps and
b
boundary curves is homotopy equivalent to the
n
-rose if and only if
n=c-1+b
.

3.1.
Pants decomposition

Let
R_{n}
denote the
n
-rose.
We define a
marked pants decomposition
as a triple
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
, where:

(1)

X
is a compact surface,

(2)

h_{X}:R_{n}\to X
is a homotopy equivalence, and

(3)

P_{X}
is a pants decomposition of
X
.

Two marked pants decompositions
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
and
(P_{Y},h_{Y},Y)
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a homeomorphism
f:X\to Y
such that:

(1)

f(P_{X})=P_{Y}
, and

(2)

f\circ h_{X}
is homotopic to
h_{Y}
.

Let
[P_{X},h_{X},X]
denote the equivalence class of the marked pants decomposition
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
.
An equivalence class of a marked pants decomposition is called a
pants decomposition of
R_{n}
.

3.2.
Elementary moves

We now introduce elementary moves that modify one pants decomposition into another.
For a compact orientable surface, Hatcher and Thurston
[
HT80
]
defined two elementary moves among pants decompositions of the surface, which they used to find a presentation of the mapping class group.
Papadopoulos and Penner
[
PP16
]
extended this framework, providing four elementary moves for pants decompositions of non-orientable surfaces.

Elementary move I

Elementary move I replaces a simple closed curve in a pants decomposition with another curve, such that the regular neighborhood of the union of the original and new curve forms a four-holed sphere.
More precisely, for two pants decompositions

(1)

(2)

there exist simple closed curves
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
such that
P_{X}-\gamma=P_{X}^{\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}
and a regular neighborhood of

3.3.
(Non-orientable) Pants graphs

We define a simplicial graph
\mathcal{P}_{n}
as follows:
Consider all pants decompositions of the
n
-rose as the vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
Two vertices are joined by an edge in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
if and only if there exist representatives of these vertices such that one is obtained from the other by Elementary move I, II, III, or IV.
We call
\mathcal{P}_{n}
the
pants graph
of
F_{n}
.

Next, we define a simplicial action of
\Out(F_{n})
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
:
For each
\phi\in\Out(F_{n})
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, let
f:R_{n}\to R_{n}
be a representative of
\phi
, and let
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
be a representative of
P
.
We define
\phi.P
as the equivalence class of the new pants decomposition
(P_{X},h_{X}\circ f^{-1},X)
.

3.4.
Orientable pants graph

We also define a simplicial graph
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
for all pants decompositions of orientable surfaces marked by
R_{n}
, which forms an induced subgraph of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
In this case, the vertices of
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
are joined by edges using only Elementary moves I and II.

4.
Pants Graphs and Free Splitting Complexes

Assume
n\geq 3
.
We say a metric tree equipped with an isometric action of
F_{n}
is an
F_{n}
-tree
.
Two
F_{n}
-trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists an isometry
g:T\to T^{\prime}
such that
g(w.x)=w.g(x)
for every
w\in F_{n}
and
x\in T
.
We say an
F_{n}
-tree
T^{\prime}
is a
refinement
of an
F_{n}
-tree
T
if there exists an
F_{n}
-equivariant surjective map from
T^{\prime}
to
T
.
In this case,
T^{\prime}
is a refinement of all trees equivalent to
T
, so we can write
[T^{\prime}]>[T]
. A refinement means that
T^{\prime}
captures more detailed information about the structure of
T
. More precisely, it means that there is an
F_{n}
-equivariant surjection from
T^{\prime}
to
T
that preserves the action of
F_{n}
. In this case,
T^{\prime}
can be seen as a more refined version of
T
, and we write
[T^{\prime}]>[T]
to represent this refinement relation.

An
F_{n}
-tree is called a
free splitting
if every edge stabilizer is trivial.
The
free splitting complex

\mathcal{FS}(F_{n})
is constructed as follows.
The vertices of
\mathcal{FS}(F_{n})
are all
F_{n}
-equivalence classes of free splittings.
A collection of
k+1
vertices
t_{0},\dots,t_{k}
of
\mathcal{FS}(F_{n})
form a
k
-simplex if
t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}
.

Let
h_{X}:R_{n}\to X
be a homotopy equivalence and
\alpha\subset X
be an essential arc.
Homotope
X
by shrinking
\alpha
to a point.
Then
X
deforms into a wedge of surfaces.
By the van Kampen theorem, this corresponds to a free splitting of
F_{n}
.
Let us call such a free splitting, denoted by
T_{\alpha}
, the
free splitting corresponding to
\alpha
.

Remark 4.1
.

In this case, the barycentric subdivision of the arc complex of
X
is embedded into the free splitting complex.

Let
P=[P_{X},h_{X},X]
be a pants decomposition.
If
\alpha
is an arc whose endpoints are contained in the same boundary curve of
X
, shrinking
\alpha
to a point deforms
X
into a wedge sum of a (possibly disconnected) surface.
Note that a wedge sum of a topological space induces a free product or an HNN extension of its fundamental group by van Kampen’s theorem.
Define
\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
as the free splitting corresponding to
\alpha
for some arc
\alpha
disjoint from
P_{X}
.

Note that
\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
is not well-defined because a different choice of an arc in
X
induces a different free splitting.
Nevertheless,
\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
is coarsely well-defined.
That is, if
\alpha^{\prime}
is another arc disjoint from
P_{X}
, the free splitting
T
corresponding to
\alpha^{\prime}
is still close to
\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
in
\mathcal{FS}(F_{n})
.
If
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are disjoint, then
\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
and
T
have a common refinement, so
d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X),T)
is at most two.
Otherwise,
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are contained in the same pair of pants in
P_{X}
.
If the endpoints of
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are contained in the same boundary curve, then the free splittings corresponding to
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are equal.
If
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
intersect, they are still contained in the same pair of pants but with their endpoints on different boundary curves.
In this case, the distance between their corresponding free splittings is at most six (because the distance in the arc complex is three).
Therefore, the set of all free splittings corresponding to the arcs disjoint from
(P_{X},h_{X},X)
has diameter six.

Define a map
\Psi_{n}:\mathcal{P}_{n}\to\mathcal{FS}(F_{n})
by
\Psi_{n}(P)=\Psi(P_{X},h_{X},X)
for each
P=[P_{X},h_{X},X]
, and extend it by sending each edge to a geodesic.

Theorem 4.2
.

There are constants
A\geq 1
,
B\geq 0
, and
C\geq 0
such that for each
n\geq 3
, the map
\Psi_{n}
is
(A,B)
–coarsely Lipschitz with

d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi_{n}(\phi.P),\phi.\Psi_{n}(P))\leq C

for all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\phi\in\Out(F_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
P
and
Q
be adjacent vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
Suppose
Q
is obtained from
P
by Elementary move I.
Write
P=[P_{X},h_{X},X]
and
Q=[Q_{X},h_{X},X]
.
Let
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
be arcs of
X
such that
\Psi_{n}(P)
and
\Psi_{n}(Q)
are the free splittings corresponding to
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
, respectively.
If
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
do not fill
X
, then the distance between
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
in the arc complex is at most two; in this case, we have
d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi_{n}(P),\Psi_{n}(Q))\leq 4
.
Assume
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
fill
X
.
Then
P
and
Q
contain curves that fill
X
, which implies
X
is homeomorphic to a four-holed sphere.
Note that
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
intersect twice.
Thus,
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
are at distance at most two in the arc complex, so we have
d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi_{n}(P),\Psi_{n}(Q))\leq 4
.

Suppose
Q
is obtained from
P
by Elementary move II.
Without loss of generality, assume that the number of boundary curves of
Q
is less than that of
P
.
Write
Q=[Q_{Y},h_{Y},Y]
.
Let
\alpha_{P}^{\prime}\subset X
be the arc collapsed in the move, and let
\alpha_{Q}^{\prime}\subset Y
be the arc collapsed in the inverse move.
The free splittings corresponding to
\alpha_{P}^{\prime}
and
\alpha_{Q}^{\prime}
are of the form
A*\langle b\rangle
and
\langle A,bab^{-1}\rangle*_{b}
, respectively.
The distance between these free splittings is at most four.
Since
\alpha_{P}^{\prime}
is disjoint from
P_{X}
, the free factors corresponding to
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{P}^{\prime}
have distance
\leq 6
.
Similarly, the free factors corresponding to
\alpha_{Q}
and
\alpha_{Q}^{\prime}
have distance at most six.
Thus, the distance between
\Psi_{n}(P)
and
\Psi_{n}(Q)
is at most sixteen.

If
Q
is obtained from
P
by Elementary move III, then
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
are disjoint, so the corresponding free splittings are at distance at most one.

Suppose
Q
is obtained from
P
by Elementary move IV.
Since the corresponding free splittings of
\alpha_{P}
and
\alpha_{Q}
preserve a proper free factor, their distance is at most four.

For a geodesic sequence
P_{0},\dots,P_{k}
in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, we have

d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi_{n}(P_{0}),\Psi_{n}(P_{k}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{\mathcal{FS}}(\Psi_{n}(P_{i-1}),\Psi_{n}(P_{i}))\leq 16k=16d_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{0},P_{k}).

Therefore,
\Psi_{n}
is a
(16,0)
–coarsely Lipschitz map.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

An element of

5.
Pants Graphs and Cayley Graphs

Fix
n\geq 3
.
It is well-known that
\Out(F_{n})
is finitely generated.
Thus, we can define a word metric on
\Out(F_{n})
, which turns
\Out(F_{n})
into a metric space.
Since any two word metrics on a finitely generated group are quasi-isometric, this implies that the coarse Lipschitz property of a map from
\Out(F_{n})
to a metric space does not depend on the specific word metric chosen, but rather on
\Out(F_{n})
itself.
In this section, we show that the orbit map of
\Out(F_{n})
on the orientable pants graph is coarsely Lipschitz.

5.1.
Cayley graphs and pants graphs

As mentioned,
\Out(F_{n})
is finitely generated.
In particular,
\Out(F_{n})
is generated by transvections and inversions.

Remark 5.1
.

Recall that the rose
R_{n}=[0,n]/\{0,1,\dots,n\}
can be seen as a graph with
n
petals, where the quotient map is denoted by
q:[0,n]\to R_{n}
.
For each
i=1,\dots,n
, the segment
q([i-1,i])
is referred to as the
i
-th petal of
R_{n}
.
Now, for distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we define two types of transvections:

•

The
right transvection

r_{ij}
is the homotopy class of a map
R_{ij}:R_{n}\to R_{n}
, defined by:

R_{ij}(t)=\begin{cases}t&\text{if}~t\in R_{n}-q([i-1,i]),\\
q(2q^{-1}(t)-i+1)&\text{if}~t\in q([i-1,i-\frac{1}{2}]),\\
q(2q^{-1}(t)-2i+j)&\text{if}~t\in q([i-\frac{1}{2},i]).\end{cases}

•

The
left transvection

\ell_{ji}
is the homotopy class of a map
L_{ji}:R_{n}\to R_{n}
, defined by:

L_{ji}(t)=\begin{cases}t&\text{if}~t\in R_{n}-q([i-1,i]),\\
q(2q^{-1}(t)-2i+j+1)&\text{if}~t\in q([i-1,i-\frac{1}{2}]),\\
q(2q^{-1}(t)-i)&\text{if}~t\in q([i-\frac{1}{2},i]).\end{cases}

The
inversion

\iota_{i}
is the homotopy class of a map
I_{i}:R_{n}\to R_{n}
, defined by:

I_{i}(t)=\begin{cases}t&\text{if}~t\in R_{n}-q([i-1,i]),\\
q(2i-1-q^{-1}(t))&\text{if}~t\in q([i-1,i]).\end{cases}

Lemma 5.2
.

For distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, a (left or right) transvection
t_{ij}
fixes some vertex of
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be an oriented
(n-1)
-holed torus.
Define a homotopy equivalence
h_{X}:R_{n}\to X
as follows:
a regular neighborhood of the union of the images of the
i
-th and
j
-th petals forms a once-punctured torus
Y\subset X
.
For each
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i,j\}
, the image of the
\ell
-th petal is homotopic to a boundary curve of
X
.

Let
\gamma
be a simple closed curve homotopic to the
j
-th petal.
The right-handed Dehn twist
T_{\gamma}
along
\gamma
corresponds to one of the transvections
t_{ij}
from the set

The specific transvection

Let

be a pants decomposition of

that includes the curves

Since

is a Dehn twist along

.
Moreover, since
(P_{X},h_{X},X)=(T_{\gamma}P_{X},h_{X},X)

P=t_{ij}.P

∎

For each

Let

Let

Based on the proofs of Lemma

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a connected subgraph
\mathcal{S}
of

such that
f.\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}
intersect for each

Proof.

Let
X
be an
n
-holed disk, and let
h_{X}:R_{n}\to X
be a deformation retract.
Let
\beta_{i}
denote the boundary curve of
X
that is isotopic to the
i
-th petal, and let
\beta_{0}
denote the boundary curve that is not isotopic to any petal.

For distinct
i<j
, define
\gamma_{ij}
as a simple closed curve such that
\gamma_{ij}
is disjoint from the

Let

denote a pants decomposition of

.
Since the pants graph of

Note that there exists a positive number

such that for each vertex

On the other hand, the proof of Lemma

In conclusion, all vertices of

are contained within the

Theorem 5.5
.

For every
n\geq 3
, the orientable pants graph
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
is connected.

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
n\geq 3
, any orbit map
\Out(F_{n})\to\mathcal{P}^{+}_{n}
is coarsely Lipschitz.

Moreover,
\Out(F_{n})
is quasi-isometric to the spine of the outer space, denoted by

.
Thus, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 5.7
.

For each
n\geq 2
, there exist constants

and a coarsely Lipschitz map

such that

d_{\mathcal{P}}(\Psi_{n}(G.f),f^{-1}.\Psi_{n}(G))\leq C

for all

and

Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation improves dysautonomia, post-traumatic stress disorder and cognitive impairment in long covid patients: a pilot study
Authors: Eric Azabou, Amelie Pillot, Guillaume Bao, Sara Mehlal, Annabelle Arnould, Nathalie Agar-hug, Marine Zagdoun, Alix Genolini, Ashley Russo, Claire-Marie Rangon, Philippe Azouvi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Long COVID is frequently associated with persistent autonomic dysfunction, cognitive impairment, and psychological distress, reflecting sustained neuroimmune and autonomic dysregulation. Effective disease-modifying therapies remain scarce. To evaluate the effects of transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) on autonomic symptoms, post-traumatic stress symptoms, and cognitive performance in patients with long COVID. In this open-label, single-arm prospective pilot study, seventeen patients with long COVID underwent weekly one-hour taVNS sessions for eight consecutive weeks. Autonomic symptoms were assessed using the Composite Autonomic Symptom Score-31 (COMPASS-31), psychological symptoms using the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5), and cognitive function using a standardized attention and executive function battery (COGBAT). Outcomes were evaluated at baseline and post-intervention. Paired non-parametric analyses were performed. Following taVNS, autonomic symptoms improved significantly, with mean COMPASS-31 scores decreasing from 33.71 ± 16.81 to 13.78 ± 9.38 (p < 0.0001). Post-traumatic stress symptoms were significantly reduced (PCL-5: 25.76 ± 14.99 to 19.47 ± 14.63; p = 0.028). Cognitive performance improved, with COGBAT scores increasing from 37.71 ± 25.75 to 49.41 ± 26.79 (p = 0.011). No adverse events were reported. taVNS appears to be a safe and promising non-pharmacological intervention for improving autonomic dysfunction, cognitive impairment, and psychological symptoms in long COVID. These findings warrant confirmation in randomized, sham-controlled trials.

Introduction

Post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC), commonly referred to as long COVID, has emerged as a major global public health challenge. Epidemiological studies estimate that approximately 10–15% of individuals infected with SARS-CoV-2 experience persistent symptoms lasting months or longer, affecting over 65 million people worldwide1,2. Long COVID is clinically heterogeneous but frequently includes chronic fatigue, cognitive impairment (“brain fog”), sleep disturbances, anxiety, depressive symptoms, and autonomic nervous system dysfunction, such as orthostatic intolerance, palpitations, and postural tachycardia3–5.

Accumulating evidence suggests that persistent immune dysregulation and low-grade systemic inflammation play a central role in long COVID pathophysiology6–8. Elevated circulating inflammatory mediators—including interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-α, and C-reactive protein—have been detected months after acute infection, particularly in patients presenting with neurocognitive symptoms and dysautonomia9–11. Proposed mechanisms include residual viral antigens, immune cell reprogramming, endothelial injury, and functional autoantibodies targeting G-protein–coupled and autonomic receptors12,13.

Chronic inflammation is closely linked to autonomic imbalance, characterized by reduced parasympathetic (vagal) tone and sympathetic predominance. Neuropathological and imaging studies have identified SARS-CoV-2 RNA, viral proteins, and inflammatory infiltrates within the brainstem and vagus nerve, supporting a direct neuroinflammatory contribution to autonomic dysfunction14,15. In parallel, endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and activation of inflammatory transcriptional pathways contribute to impaired baroreflex sensitivity and sustained sympathetic activation16–18.

Neuroimmune dysregulation also underlies the cognitive and affective manifestations of long COVID. Pro-inflammatory cytokines can disrupt blood–brain barrier integrity, activate microglia, and alter monoaminergic and cholinergic neurotransmission, impairing synaptic plasticity and large-scale brain network function19–21. These mechanisms have been associated with anxiety, depression, cognitive impairment, and post-traumatic stress–like symptoms commonly reported following COVID-19 infection22–24.

The vagus nerve occupies a central position in the regulation of immune responses, autonomic balance, and brain function. Through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, vagal efferent signaling suppresses excessive cytokine release via α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors on immune cells25–27. Reduced vagal tone may therefore contribute to sustained inflammation, autonomic dysfunction, and neuropsychiatric symptoms in long COVID28–30.

Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) is a non-invasive neuromodulation technique targeting afferent and efferent vagal fibers via the auricular branch of the vagus nerve. taVNS has demonstrated anti-inflammatory, autonomic, and neurocognitive effects in inflammatory, psychiatric, and neurological disorders31–33. Preliminary studies suggest potential benefits of vagus nerve stimulation in acute and post-acute COVID-19, but robust clinical data in long COVID remain limited34–36. We therefore hypothesized that taVNS could improve autonomic symptoms, cognitive impairment, and psychological distress in patients with long COVID. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of an 8-week taVNS intervention on these clinical domains.

Methods

Study design and participants

This open-label, single-arm prospective pilot study involved patients diagnosed with long COVID according to the World Health Organization definition2. Seventeen consecutive patients with long COVID were enrolled between 2023 and 2024 following referral to the Department of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation of our institution. Inclusion criteria were a clinical diagnosis of long COVID and impaired health-related quality of life, defined by a Short Form-36 (SF-36) total score < 50 at baseline37. The mean SF-36 score at inclusion was 30.73 ± 12.15.

Participants were aged 41–61 years (mean 49.8 ± 6.6), and 94.1% were female. All patients had a documented history of SARS-CoV-2 infection between 2020 and 2023; infection was PCR-confirmed in 41% of cases. Time from acute infection to enrollment ranged from 18 months to 4.5 years.

The study was a preliminary feasibility investigation within the framework of the ESNV-SPA trial, which has been registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04286373) and received approval from our Ethics Committee, the Comité de Protection des Personnes Sud-Ouest et Outre-Mer 4 (IRB No. IORG0009855), on October 2, 2020. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. The study is part of the SMART-VNS™ project a Structured Multidisciplinary Program to Advance Research on the Therapeutic Effects of Vagus Nerve Stimulation in inflammatory, infectious, neurological, and pain-related conditions, such as sepsis, respiratory distress, arthritis, and their neurologic consequences such as post-traumatic stress disorder and long COVID syndrome.

Assessments

Participants underwent standardized assessments at baseline and immediately after completion of the 8-week intervention.

Outcome measures

Autonomic symptoms were assessed using the Composite Autonomic Symptom Score-31 (COMPASS-31), a validated instrument for quantifying dysautonomia severity38. Psychological symptoms were evaluated using the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5)39. Cognitive function was assessed using the COGBAT, a standardized neurocognitive battery administered by a neuropsychologist, including subtests organized into three domains: (1) attention and processing speed (intrinsic alertness, divided attention, processing speed); (2) memory (long-term figural memory, learning capacity, brief delayed recall, long delayed recall, recognition); and (3) executive functions (cognitive flexibility, organizational capacity, verbal working memory, response inhibition). Analyses were conducted at the level of the global COGBAT score. Quality of life was evaluated using the SF-36 questionnaire. Assessments were performed at baseline and following completion of the taVNS intervention.

Safety monitoring

Adverse events were systematically recorded throughout the study, with particular attention to known taVNS-related effects such as local discomfort, skin irritation, or transient autonomic symptoms30,31.

taVNS intervention

The taVNS protocol followed previously described methodologies40. Stimulation was delivered to the cymba conchae of the left ear using a TENS Eco + device (Schwa Medico™). Sessions lasted one hour and were administered once weekly for eight consecutive weeks.

Stimulation parameters included a frequency of 2 Hz, 200 µs pulse width, and individualized current intensity (1–8 mA). Stimulation intensity was individually titrated using a dual standardized procedure combining both objective and subjective criteria. First, real-time EEG monitoring was used to verify the propagation of the 2 Hz stimulation artifact to scalp electrodes T3 and C3, thereby confirming effective signal diffusion along auricular vagus nerve pathways. Second, stimulation intensity was adjusted to each participant’s sensory threshold, defined as the lowest intensity eliciting a clearly perceptible yet non-painful auricular sensation. This combined objective–subjective titration protocol was systematically applied at the beginning of each session and readjusted when necessary, during the one-hour stimulation period.

Statistical analysis

Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, revealing non-normal distributions for all variables. Paired comparisons between baseline and post-treatment scores were therefore conducted using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Given the exploratory nature of this pilot study and the predefined set of three independent primary outcomes (COMPASS-31, PCL-5, COGBAT), no formal correction for multiple comparisons was applied as the primary analytical approach, to minimize the risk of Type II errors and preserve sensitivity to detect preliminary signals of efficacy. As a complementary analysis, a False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure and reported alongside unadjusted p-values. No formal prospective sample size calculation was performed. Based on feasibility considerations and methodological recommendations for pilot and exploratory trials40, a minimum threshold of 15 participants was predefined as sufficient to obtain preliminary, statistically interpretable estimates consistent with the first-in-condition nature of this study.

Results

Discussion

This study provides preliminary evidence that taVNS may improve autonomic dysfunction, cognitive impairment, and psychological symptoms in patients with long COVID. Given the central role of the vagus nerve in autonomic regulation and immune modulation, these findings support the hypothesis that vagal neuromodulation can target key mechanisms underlying persistent post-viral symptoms.

The observed improvements in dysautonomia align with previous reports of vagus nerve involvement in post-COVID autonomic disorders4,5,9. Reduced post-traumatic stress symptoms suggest that taVNS may also modulate stress-related neural circuits. Vagal afferent stimulation activates the nucleus tractus solitarius and locus coeruleus, increasing cortical norepinephrine release and enhancing synaptic plasticity within frontoparietal and limbic networks41–45, which may underlie the cognitive improvements observed.

Limitations and future directions

This study has several important limitations that should be carefully considered when interpreting the findings. The open-label, single-arm design is subject to expectancy and 'effort-to-please’ biases, particularly for subjective outcome measures such as the COMPASS-31 and PCL-5. This study was intentionally designed as a first-in-condition pilot trial. In line with methodological recommendations for pilot and feasibility studies46 the primary objectives were to assess the feasibility and acceptability of the intervention, its safety and tolerability, and to generate preliminary effect size estimates to inform the design and sample size calculation of a future randomized controlled trial.

The duration of the taVNS intervention (8 weeks) is consistent with the time course typically reported for clinical efficacy of vagal neuromodulation (i.e., at least one month), and the stimulation frequency used in our protocol (2 Hz) has previously shown promising results in similar populations47,48. Early-stage clinical research in this field has often relied on comparable uncontrolled pilot designs: for instance, a recent taVNS study in long COVID48 also adopted a single-arm approach with the primary aim of exploring feasibility and generating preliminary efficacy signals. Our approach is therefore aligned with the current stage of investigation in the field.

All included patients had persistent long COVID symptoms for a prolonged duration (ranging from 18 months to 4.5 years), which substantially reduces the likelihood that spontaneous recovery alone accounts for the magnitude of the observed changes. Furthermore, the study protocol included concurrent physiological monitoring (EEG and ECG) during stimulation sessions, providing objective markers of neural and autonomic activity that are currently being further analysed and will be reported in future work.

The small sample size (n = 17) limits statistical power and increases the fragility of p-value-based interpretations. Effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals are reported to provide more robust estimates of the magnitude of change.

The predominance of female participants (94.1%) reflects the known epidemiological profile of long COVID, where women represent approximately 60 to 70% of those infected. Overall, women represent approximately 60 to 70% of long COVID cases across all cohorts49,50, but this must be acknowledged as a limitation to the generalization of our results to male patients.

Follow-up duration was limited to the end of the intervention, precluding assessment of the durability of effects. Future randomized, sham-controlled trials with larger samples and longer follow-up are warranted to confirm these preliminary findings. Integration of objective autonomic biomarkers and inflammatory markers would further clarify mechanisms of action. Optimization of stimulation parameters and treatment duration also warrants investigation.

Conclusion

taVNS appears to be a safe and promising non-pharmacological intervention for alleviating autonomic, cognitive, and psychological symptoms in long COVID. Larger, randomized, and mechanistically informed studies are warranted to validate these findings and optimize clinical implementation.

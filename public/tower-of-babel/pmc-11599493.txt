Safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel-based therapy in Chinese patients with malignant tumors in a real-world setting: a multicenter prospective observational study
Authors: Fei He, Yancai Sun, Wenzhou Zhang, Qiongshi Wu, Donghang Xu, Zaixian Bai, Zhiying Hao, Weiyi Feng, Kanghuai Zhang, Jiang Liu, Mei Dong, Guangxuan Liu, Guohui Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel in the Chinese population in a real-world setting.MethodsThis prospective, multicenter, observational study enrolled patients with malignancies who received any generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens in China. The primary endpoint was safety, and secondary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR). Logistic regression was used to explore risk factors for adverse events (AEs) of special interest (AESIs).ResultsBetween September 2019 and April 2023, 1168 patients were enrolled and evaluated for safety, and 602 were assessed for tumor response. Of 1168 patients, 169 (14.5%) received generic nab-paclitaxel monotherapy, and 999 (85.5%) received generic nab-paclitaxel-based combination therapy. Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 19.3% (225/1168) patients, most commonly including neutrophil count decreased (7.6%), anemia (5.8%), and white blood cell decreased (5.7%). In subgroup analysis, peripheral sensory neuropathy was observed frequently in breast cancer (45.6%). Multivariate analysis showed that patients receiving combination therapy and ≥ 4 treatment cycles (OR, 1.925; 95% CI 1.363–2.719; p < 0.001) were more susceptible to the AESIs.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates a promising safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens for Chinese patients with malignancies in a real-world setting, providing valuable insights for clinical decision-making.Clinical Trials.gov NCT04060290Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01609-6.Keywords: Generic Nab-paclitaxel, Chinese population, Malignant tumors, Real-world study, Safety, Efficacy

Introduction

Taxanes are one of the most effective and widely used cytotoxic agents for cancer treatment. Among these, paclitaxel has played a significant role over the past few decades, however, its clinical application has been notably limited by poor water solubility and high toxicity [1, 2]. Nanoparticle albumin-bound paclitaxel (nab-paclitaxel) is a newly developed, solvent-free form of taxane cytotoxic agent [3], which has been increasingly used in the treatment of various malignant tumors [4–6].

Nab-paclitaxel was initially developed to overcome hypersensitivity reactions and toxicities associated with the solvents in conventional formulations, with the additional aim of potentially improving efficacy [6, 7]. Compared to solvent-based paclitaxel formulations, nab-paclitaxel offers superior drug delivery and higher intra-tumor concentrations [8, 9], which reduces the incidence of serious toxicities, including severe allergic reactions and myelosuppression [1, 10]. In addition to favorable safety, nab-paclitaxel also exhibited good efficacy. Clinical applications have shown that nab-paclitaxel yields superior antitumor effects compared to traditional solvent-based paclitaxel, as evidenced by several phase II and III trials [3, 9, 11]. In particular, it was also proved that nab-paclitaxel was a better pairing with immunotherapy for not requiring steroid premedication, which has a potential immunosuppressive effect [12–14]. In the context of a positive benefit-risk balance of nab-paclitaxel therapy [15–21], it has been approved for the treatment of metastatic breast cancer, locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, and metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma [7]. While substantial safety data exist for nab-paclitaxel, these are primarily derived from the first-in-class agent, Abraxane®. However, due to the limited accessibility of Abraxane® in China, the reference value of such data is somewhat constrained. In this context, the advent of generic drugs becomes notably encouraging.

Generic nab-paclitaxel (Keaili®) was first approved in 2018 and has demonstrated favorable efficacy and safety in several tumors including breast cancer. However, these results of generic nab-paclitaxel (Keaili®) were limited by clinical trials [22–25]. Generally, clinical trials are conducted under idealized and rigorously controlled conditions, which are not broadly representative of real-world patients and may limit their external validity [26]. In contrast, real-world studies provide a more authentic representation of the clinical landscape, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of the post-marketing safety of Keaili® in a relatively short marketing period, thereby enabling better guidance in clinical practice [27].

Taken together, we designed a prospective, observational study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel in Chinese patients with malignant tumors in a real-world setting.

Patients and methods

Study design and patients

This multicenter, prospective, observational study was to evaluate the safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens in patients with malignant solid tumor across 12 centers in China. The study population consisted of malignant tumor patients who were treated with generic nab-paclitaxel-based therapies, without exclusion criteria. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Cancer Hospital Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Ethical approval number: 19/14–1944). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. This study was registered with Clinical Trials.gov (NCT04060290).

Procedures

Patients received generic nab-paclitaxel-containing regimens. According to the prescribed dose in the medicine specification, nab-paclitaxel (Keaili®; CSPC Ouyi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Shijiazhuang, China) is suggested for intravenous administration at a dose of 260 mg/m2 every 3 weeks. However, in the real-world setting of this study, no specific restrictions were imposed on administration dosage, timing, combination regimens, or dose modifications. The investigators determined the specific regimen and dosage based on the practical circumstances.

Demographic and clinical data at the time of the enrolment were collected and entered into an electronic case report form. The enrolment form mainly included the following information: age, sex, clinical diagnosis, and information on comorbid underlying diseases. The observation period was within 28 days after the last dose of generic nab-paclitaxel. Data on patients were recorded during inpatient or outpatient chemotherapy, adverse events (AEs) occurrence (date, frequency, severity) after each chemotherapy, and reasons for discontinuation were collected.

Endpoints and assessments

The primary endpoint was the incidence and severity of AEs. AEs were defined as any adverse medical events occurring in patients after receiving generic nab-paclitaxel treatment. AEs were monitored and recorded by the investigators, with severity graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0.

The secondary endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR, defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a complete response and partial response). Efficacy data were obtained from electronic medical records. Imaging assessment was performed at baseline using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or/and computed tomography (CT). The response to the nab-paclitaxel-based regimen was evaluated by investigators according to RECIST Version 1.1.

Statistical analysis

Any sample size calculations were not performed, as the aim of the study was to describe all AEs occurring in routine clinical practice (planned enrollment of 1000 patients), rather than to compare generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens or determine a specific association between generic nab-paclitaxel and AEs.

Safety analysis were conducted on the safety set, which included all patients who received at least one dose of generic nab-paclitaxel treatment for whom safety data were documented. Efficacy was analyzed in the full analysis set, comprising all eligible patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment and had at least one response assessment. All variables were analyzed by descriptive statistical methods. Quantitative data were summarized by mean, standard deviation, median, interquartile range, and maximum and minimum values, while qualitative data were presented as counts or percentages. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis were employed to identify factors influencing safety, with odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals calculated. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 software.

Results

Patient characteristics

From September 2019 to April 2023, a total of 1181 patients were screened, with 13 patients failing to meet the eligibility criteria and 1168 eligible patients were enrolled. Among them, 1168 patients had safety data available for assessment, while 602 patients had tumor response data evaluable for efficacy assessment (Fig. 1).

Study flow chart

Of the 1168 enrolled patients, 59.5% (695/1168) were female. The median age was 57.0 (range, 17.0–86.0) years, and more than half of the patients (57.5%) were under 60 years of age. Detailed clinical and demographic characteristics of the patients were summarized in Table 1. Comorbidity were present in 29.5% (344/1168). The most common diagnosis was breast cancer (n = 474; 40.6%), followed by digestive system tumors (n = 297; 25.4%; e.g. gastric cancer [n = 119], esophageal cancer [n = 113], pancreatic cancer [n = 47], cardia cancer [n = 11]), and lung cancer (n = 256; 21.9%). The majority of patients (61.5%) had received prior chemotherapy.

Treatment exposure

The usages of generic nab-paclitaxel and generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens in a real-world setting are reported in Table 2. Among the 1168 patients, 169 (14.5%) received generic nab-paclitaxel monotherapy, and 999 (85.5%) received generic nab-paclitaxel-based combination therapy mainly represented by dual-drug (49.3%) or triple-drug (30.3%) combination therapy. The majority of patients (652/1168, 55.8%) underwent nab-paclitaxel-based chemotherapy, followed by immunotherapy (21.1%) and targeted therapy (19.0%). The median number of generic nab-paclitaxel cycles was 4.0 (range 1.0–25.0). The median cumulative and total administered doses of nab-paclitaxel for the patients were 884.2 (range 493.9–1357.3) mg/m2 and 1200.0 (range 100.0–7500.0) mg, respectively.

Of the 1168 patients enrolled in the study, 694 (59.4%) patients completed treatment as planned (Supplementary Table 1). The primary reasons for treatment discontinuation were loss to follow-up (11.9%), patient voluntary withdrawal (11.1%), disease progression (6.5%), AEs (3.2%), death (0.8%; including 3 possibly related to treatment, 2 unrelated to treatment, and 4 unknown), and unacceptable toxicity (0.4%). Among them, AEs leading to discontinuation mainly included peripheral sensory neuropathy (n = 9), fever (n = 3), white blood cell decreased (n = 2), myelosuppression (n = 2), and diarrhea (n = 2) (Supplementary Table 2).

Safety

Safety profile in all patients

Most patients (1011/1168, 86.6%) experienced AEs during treatment, and an overview of the safety profile for generic nab-paclitaxel was summarized in Table 3. The most common hematologic toxicities of all grades included anemia (51.1%), followed by white blood cell decreased (36.4%), and neutrophil count decreased (25.0%). In addition, non-hematologic toxicities included peripheral sensory neuropathy (34.2%), alopecia (20.5%), and malaise/fatigue (20.0%). Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 19.3% (225/1168) of patients, most commonly including neutrophil count decreased (7.6%), anemia (5.8%), and white blood cell decreased (5.7%). Nine patients (0.8%) experienced allergic reaction events (all grades), with only 1 (0.1%) being grade 3.

Exploratory subgroup analysis of safety profile

The exploratory analysis of safety was performed based on different tumor types (mainly including breast cancer, digestive system tumors, and lung cancer), the most common AEs (all grades) were shown in Fig. 2. Peripheral sensory neuropathy occurred frequently in breast cancer (45.6%), followed by lung cancer (30.1%) and digestive system tumors (22.9%). The incidence of malaise/fatigue (26.2% vs. 17.2% vs. 15.2%) and alopecia (25.3% vs. 21.9% vs. 16.2%) were higher in patients with breast cancer compared to lung cancer and digestive system tumors. Anemia and neutrophil count decreased occurred frequently in digestive system tumors (59.6%, 28.6%), followed by lung cancer (54.3%, 19.1%) and breast cancer (42.6%, 24.3%). In addition, the incidence of white blood cell decreased (34.8%, 37.4%, and 32.0%) of all grades was similar in patients with breast cancer, digestive system tumors, and lung cancer (Supplementary Table 3–5).

Safety analysis by different tumor types

Among the 246 patients treated with generic nab-paclitaxel plus immunotherapy, the most frequent hematological AEs were anemia (60.6%), decreased white blood cell count (34.6%), and decreased neutrophil count (22.8%). Frequent non-hematologic AEs included peripheral sensory neuropathy (29.3%), alopecia (22.0%), and malaise/fatigue (20.3%). In patients receiving generic nab-paclitaxel plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the common occurrence of any-grade hematological AEs was anemia (58.3%), white blood cell decreased (50.0%), and neutrophil count decreased (35.4%), and of non-hematological AEs were peripheral sensory neuropathy (37.5%), alopecia (27.1%), and malaise/fatigue (25.0%) (Supplementary Table 6–7).

Univariate/multivariate analysis

Logistic regression analyses were performed to explore the effect of baseline characteristics (e.g., age, gender, underlying disease, treatment regimen, and number of treatment cycles) on the most common AEs of special interest (AESIs) including anemia, white blood cell decreased, neutrophil count decreased, and peripheral sensory neuropathy.

The multivariate analysis showed an independent influence of gender (OR, 1.515; 95% CI 1.185–1.937; p < 0.001), treatment regimen (OR, 1.925; 95% CI 1.363–2.719; p < 0.001), and number of treatment cycles (OR, 1.482; 95% CI 1.168–1.881; p = 0.001) and on anemia. Both white blood cell decreased and neutrophil count decreased were more likely to be observed in female patients or patients receiving combination regimen or ≥ 4 treatment cycles (Fig. 3A). Similarly, for white blood cell decreased and neutrophil count decreased, gender (p = 0.012, and p = 0.013, respectively), treatment regimen (p = 0.005, and p = 0.018, respectively), and number of treatment cycles (p < 0.001, and p < 0.001, respectively) had the significance. Both white blood cell decreased and neutrophil count decreased were more likely observed in female patients or patients receiving combination regimen or ≥ 4 treatment cycles (Fig. 3B and Fig. 3C). In addition, gender (OR, 0.590; 95% CI 0.443–0.785; p < 0.001), diabetes (OR, 1.852; 95% CI 1.136–3.020; p = 0.013), and number of treatment cycles (OR, 2.438; 95% CI 1.882–3.157; p < 0.001) were significantly associated with a higher risk of peripheral sensory neuropathy. Peripheral sensory neuropathy was more likely to occur in female patients or patients with diabetes or ≥ 4 treatment cycles (Fig. 3D).

Efficacy

A total of 602 patients were evaluable for response (Table 4). The ORR was 42.4% (255/602), with 15 patients achieved complete responses (2.5%) and 240 achieved partial responses (39.9%). Stable disease was observed in 304 patients (50.5%), resulting in a disease control rate (DCR) of 92.9% (559/602). Progressive disease was observed in 41 patients (6.8%). Subgroup analysis showed that patients with breast cancer (51.1%, 94.8%), digestive system tumors (36.3%, 91.7%), and lung cancer (47.1%, 91.4%) benefited more in ORR and DCR (Table 5).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study was the first analysis to evaluate the real-world safety profile of generic nab-paclitaxel (Keaili®) for malignant solid tumor patients in China. Our findings demonstrated the well-tolerance of the Chinese generic nab-paclitaxel (Keaili®)-based therapies in various malignant solid tumors, which provided real-world data of generic nab-paclitaxel for the Chinese population. Moreover, subpopulations with breast cancer, digestive system tumors, and lung cancer exhibited promising responses to generic nab-paclitaxel.

Regarding the safety profile, AEs occurred in 1011(86.6%) patients. Most AEs were mild or moderate (grade 1 or 2), with a low incidence for grades 3–4. The safety profile of generic nab-paclitaxel in the real-world setting for Chinese patients was consistent with those obtained from the clinical trials of first-in-class agents [5, 28, 29]; no new safety signals were identified. The most common AEs of all grades were anemia, white blood cell decreased, peripheral sensory neuropathy, and neutrophil count decreased, which is consistent with the reported previously [5, 28, 29]. Together, the results suggested that the overall AEs spectrum of generic nab-paclitaxel in the real-world setting was acceptable.

Given that nab-paclitaxel has proven to be promising partner in immunotherapy [12–14], it is crucial to consider the safety concerns associated with combined nab-paclitaxel and immunotherapy regimens. In our study, the safety profile of immunotherapy combined with generic nab-paclitaxel, whether used alone or in conjunction with targeted therapy, was generally consistent with the known toxic effects of each agent as monotherapy or in combination [12, 14, 30]. Notably, the incidence of grade 3 or 4 AESIs was lower in our immunotherapy-nab-paclitaxel combination groups compared to those reported in previous studies [12]. In conclusion, in light of the expanding application of immunotherapy, our study demonstrates that immunotherapy and targeted-nab-paclitaxel combination regimens are safe. This outcome has significant implications for directing and informing clinical practice, offering guidance on how to safely and effectively integrate these therapies in treating patients. Certainly, further studies with a larger sample size are necessary to make a definitive conclusion regarding the safety of the combination regimens in this population.

In this study, the predominant patient population consisted of individuals with breast cancer, digestive system tumors, and lung cancer. The focus on safety across these three cancer types is crucial, given the widespread use of nab-paclitaxel-based regimens for these malignancies and their high prevalence in China [31, 32]. Our findings indicated that the most frequent AEs across all patients included anemia, white blood cell counts decreased, neutrophil counts decreased, and peripheral sensory neuropathy, suggesting a lack of tumor specificity in the manifestation of these AEs. Anemia was most commonly associated with the digestive system (59.6%) and lung cancer (54.3%), while peripheral sensory neuropathy occurred most frequently in breast cancer (45.6%). For all-grade hematologic toxicities, white blood cell decreased (34.8%, 37.4%, and 32.0%, respectively) occurred at similar rates in patients with breast cancer, digestive system tumors, and lung cancer. Although the categories of common AEs may not differ significantly, the probabilities of various events vary across cancer types, indicating that tailored monitoring and management strategies for AEs are necessary based on specific cancer diagnoses. In patients with lung cancer or digestive system tumors using generic nab-paclitaxel, the incidence of grade 3–4 anemia and neutrophil count decreased was lower than data reported in previous first-in-class pivotal phase 3 trials [5, 21]. As a real-world study with weak intervention nature, the observed difference could be attributed to the incomplete record data resulting from patient medication use outside the hospital. Besides, differences in drug dosage and baseline characteristics across studies may also explain the difference in AEs. Of note, as the first analysis of generic nab-paclitaxel in the Chinese patient population, the toxicity was manageable despite our findings should be interpreted with caution.

Notably, peripheral sensory neuropathy, one of the well-recognized common AESIs of taxane-based therapy, occurred more commonly in patients with breast cancer (all grades, 45.6%; grade 3–4, 1.7%) in the present study, which is lower than that previously reported in a randomized phase 2 study (all grades, 76%; grade 3–4, 7%) [33]. More importantly, our data also showed a higher frequency of peripheral sensory neuropathy among female patients, potentially attributable to lower paclitaxel clearance in female compared to male [34, 35]. Previous studies have shown that accumulated doses of chemotherapy may cause peripheral sensory neuropathy; it is consistent that number of chemotherapy cycles was associated with a significant risk of peripheral sensory neuropathy in univariate and multivariate analysis in our research. The consensus in Chinese evidence-based guidelines and ASCO guidelines recommends reducing chemotherapy dosage or extending intervals between doses as primary management strategies for peripheral neuropathy [36, 37]. Given the high prevalence of diabetes, peripheral sensory neuropathy may already exist prior to the onset of the tumor. Thus, it is particularly essential to thoroughly evaluate patients for any pre-existing conditions or comorbidities that might confound or exacerbate the severity of peripheral sensory neuropathy before treatment, including diabetes, nutritional deficiencies, a history of alcoholism, previous peripheral neuropathies, and family history of hereditary peripheral neuropathies [36, 38]. Taken together, these findings indicated that clinicians should consider the choice of treatment regimen, individualized treatment, and reasonable medication when using nab-paclitaxel clinically.

As taxanes remain the first-line choice for systemic chemotherapy across multiple tumors, to better guide clinical practice in certain scenarios, safety profiles of nab-paclitaxel in hierarchical subgroups deserve further exploration. According to the results of logistic regression analysis, gender, treatment regimen, and number of treatment cycles of nab-paclitaxel were identified as risk factors for the most common AESI occurrence. Patients receiving combination regimens have a higher risk of anemia, as well as white blood cell decreased, and neutrophil count decreased, compared with those receiving nab-paclitaxel monotherapy. It is noted that one study by Denise et al. indicated that hematologic toxicity increased with the use of combination therapy [39], which may help explain this finding to a certain extent. This is presumably because drug-drug interactions may increase the hematologic toxicity of anticancer agents [40]. Furthermore, anemia, white blood cell decreased, and neutrophil count decreased were found to be linked to the number of treatment cycles in our study. In clinical practice, hematologic toxicities may be increased with prolonged treatment duration and increased cumulative dosage. Thus, it is recommended that patients should be closely monitored for blood indicators during treatment.

The current study had certain limitations. First, this observational study design without strict selection criteria may introduce inevitable selection bias. Second, due to the weak intervention nature of our real-world studies, the incidence of AEs may be underestimated. Additionally, as the risk factor prediction is an exploratory post-hoc analysis, the findings should be considered preliminary.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated the promising safety and efficacy of generic nab-paclitaxel-based regimens in Chinese patients with various solid tumor in a real-world setting, consistent with those of first-in-class agent clinical trials. Patients with combination therapy and ≥ 4 treatment cycles were more susceptible to the AESIs, providing a real-world perspective for clinicians in optimizing treatment strategies for this population. Additionally, further investigations with larger, more homogeneous populations are needed to validate our findings.

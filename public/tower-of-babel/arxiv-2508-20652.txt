Description of the strong approximation locus using Brauer-Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces with commutative stabilizers
Victor de Vries and Haowen Zhang

Abstract
For a homogeneous space X X over a number field k k , the Brauer-Manin obstruction has been used to study strong approximation for X X away from a finite set S S of places, and known results state that X ⁡ ( k ) X(k) is dense in the omitting- S S projection of the Brauer-Manin set pr S ⁡ ( X ​ ( 𝔸 k ) Br ) \pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br}) , under certain assumptions. In order to completely understand the closure of X ⁡ ( k ) X(k) in the set of S S -adelic points X ⁡ ( 𝔸 k S ) X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S}) , we ask: (i) whether pr S ⁡ ( X ​ ( 𝔸 k ) Br ) \pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br}) is closed in X ⁡ ( 𝔸 k S ) X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S}) ; (ii) whether X ⁡ ( k ) X(k) is dense in the closed subset of X ⁡ ( 𝔸 k S ) X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S}) cut out by elements in Br ⁡ X \br X which induce zero evaluation maps at all the places in S S . We give answers to such questions for homogeneous spaces X X under semisimple simply connected groups with commutative stabilizers.

Introduction

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
, and we suppose the set
X(k)
of rational points is nonempty. We say that
X
satisfies

S
for a finite set
S

is dense in the
S
-adelic points
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
(where the places in
S
are omitted), meaning that we can simultaneously approximate finitely many
k_{v}
-points with

by a single
k
-rational point with the
condition that this point is integral at all other finite places outside
S
. A classical case is the affine line
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
satisfying strong approximation away from
S=\{\infty\}
containing only the archimedean place, resulting from the Chinese Remainder Theorem. Moreover, a semisimple, almost simple, simply connected linear algebraic group
G
such that
\prod_{v\in S}G(k_{v})
is not compact satisfies strong approximation away from
S
(see
[
24
, Theorem 7.21]
).

Colliot-Thélène and Xu
[
10
]
used the Brauer-Manin obstruction to study strong approximation problems for homogeneous spaces
X=G/H
under semisimple simply connected groups
G
with connected or finite commutative stabilizers
H
. They proved that if
G
satisfies strong approximation away from
S
, then
X(k)
is dense in
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
, the image of the Brauer-Manin set
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br}
under the projection
\pr_{S}\colon X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\rightarrow X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
. Borovoi and Demarche
[
4
]
then generalized this result to homogeneous spaces
X=G/H
under connected
k
-groups
G
with connected stabilizers
H
, and proved that
X(k)
is dense in
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
for an
S
which contains all archimedean places, assuming that the semisimple simply connected part
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
satisfies strong approximation away from
S
, and that a certain Tate-Shafarevich group is finite.

In order to give a precise description of the set of adelic points away from
S
that can be approximated by rational points, that is, the closure
\overline{X(k)}^{S}
of
X(k)
inside
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
, we naturally ask if
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
is closed in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
. While
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
is closed when
\br X/\br k
is finite,
the closedness of
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
can fail for a general

(for example

with

, see
[
14

).We show that in the case of a homogeneous space

under a semisimple simply connected group

is indeed closed. The group
\br X/\br k
can be infinite when the stabilizers of the homogeneous space

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
X
be a homogeneous space of a semisimple and simply connected algebraic group
G
over a number field
k
. Then the set
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
is closed. If we suppose moreover that
X=G/H
with commutative

, and

, then
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
.

For a general variety

, there is also another point of view to remedy the potential non-closedness of
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
:
one can define an

closed subset in

containing

using the Brauer-Manin pairing, and hope to relate it to
\overline{X(k)}^{S}
. The following subgroup of “trivial at

” elements

has been considered, as in
[
17
]
[
14
]
etc, and
\br_{S}X
cuts out a closed subset

in

. In
[
18

, Harari considered algebraic tori

over
k
, and showed

is dense in
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A})^{\br})
for
S
containing all archimedean places; in a later paper together with Voloch
[
17
]
, they established more precisely that
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}
. In the case of homogeneous spaces
X=G/H
with
G
and
H
connected, Demeio
[
14
]
showed that
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})_{\bullet}^{\br_{S}}
for
S

containing no archimedean places
, with the same assumptions on

and the Tate-Shafarevich group as in
[
4
]
, and

denotes the modified adelic space where each

for
v

[

It is natural to wonder if we can generalize the equality
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}
to other situations with stabilizers that are not necessarily connected, for arbitrary
S
possibly containing archimedean places too (noting that it is common in the literature to take

containing all archimedean places in strong approximation problems, contrary to the assumption in
[

]
). We give examples showing that this is not true in general, when

contains real places. Hence we may need finer information of the Brauer-Manin pairing, and we propose a smaller closed subset
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}^{\prime}}
containing
X(k)
cut out by

\br_{S}^{\prime}X\coloneq\{A\in\br X|\text{ the induced evaluation map }\ev_{A}\colon X(k_{v})\rightarrow\br k_{v}\text{ is zero for all }v\in S\}.

We replace

\br
by
\br_{1}
when we consider only elements in
\br_{1}X\coloneq\Ker(\br X\rightarrow\br\overline{X})
, the algebraic Brauer group. We have the following chains of inclusions:

\begin{split}\overline{X(k)}^{S}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}&\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{1,S}}\\
\overline{X(k)}^{S}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}&\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,S}}.\end{split}

For homogeneous spaces of semisimple simply connected groups with commutative stabilizers, we give precise conditions under which all the inclusions in each chain become equalities, and under these conditions we characterize the adelic points away from
S
which can be approximated by rational points.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

Let
X=G/H
be a homogeneous space for a semisimple and simply connected algebraic group
G
over a number field
k
with commutative stabilizer
H
. Let
S
be a finite set of places such that
G
satisfies strong approximation away from

. Then we have

(a)

\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{1,S}}
if and only if

\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H)=r(\Sha^{1}_{S}(k,H))+\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\delta_{v}(X(k_{v})).

(*)

(b)

\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,S}}
if and only if

\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\langle\delta_{v}(X(k_{v}))\rangle\subseteq r(\Sha^{1}_{S}(k,H))+\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\delta_{v}(X(k_{v})).

(**)

Here

is the set of real places in
S
, the map
\delta_{v}\colon X(k_{v})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H)
is the connecting map,

is the subgroup generated by
\delta_{v}(X(k_{v}))
inside
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H)
, and

denotes the restriction map
r\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,H)\to\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H)
, and
\Sha^{1}_{S}(k,H)\coloneq\Ker(\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,H)\to~\prod_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H))
.

In particular, the condition (
**
‣
(b)
) is satisfied when
\delta_{v}(X(k_{v}))
is already a subgroup in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},H)
for all
v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}
, and this is the case when

is central in

is an adjoint group. Conditions (
*
‣
(a)
) and (
**
‣
(b)

We also establish examples with toric stabilizers and examples with finite commutative stabilizers to show that when the conditions (
*
‣
(a)
) and (
**
‣
(b)
) are not satisfied, how the equalities in the chains can fail at intermediate steps. More precisely, we give examples in

•

Propositions

and
4.11
showing

•

Proposition
4.22
showing
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,S}},

•

Proposition
4.24

As a consequence, we know that the Brauer-Manin set cut out by

At the end, we discuss possible generalizations to homogeneous spaces of connected linear groups with commutative stabilizers, presenting conditional results and clarifying the challenges in reaching the fully general case.

Remark 2.2

3
Closedness of the projection
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})

There is a natural pairing

\displaystyle X(\mathbb{A}_{k})\times\br X

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(\{x_{v}\},A)

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{v\in\Omega}\inv_{v}(\ev_{A}(x_{v}))

known as the Brauer-Manin pairing,
where
\ev_{A}:\br X\rightarrow\br k_{v}
is the evaluation map induced by

, and
\inv_{v}\colon\br k_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is the local invariant map from Class Field Theory. We denote by
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br}
(resp.

) the set of adelic points which are orthogonal to
\br X
(resp.
\br_{1}X
). The set of rational points

is contained in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br_{1}}
by the Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether exact sequence

0\rightarrow\br k\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Omega}\br k_{v}\xrightarrow{\Sigma\inv_{v}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0.

Colliot-Thélène and Xu
[
10
]
proved that if
X=G/H
is a homogeneous space where
G
is semisimple and simply connected, satisfying strong approximation away from a finite set of places
S
, and
H
is connected or finite commutative, then the set of rational points
X(k)
is dense in
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
. Note that the case of finite commutative stabilizers can be generalized to stabilizers of multiplicative type (see
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
).

In this section, we study the question of the closedness of
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
in order to completely characterize
\overline{X(k)}^{S}
. For homogeneous
X=G/H
under semisimple simply connected groups

with

, we have that
\br X/\br k
is isomorphic to

(see
[

) which is finite. A calculation shows that finiteness of
\br X/\br k
implies that
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
is indeed closed in

. The group
\br X/\br k
can be infinite when the stabilizers are not connected. However, we show that in fact
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
is closed in

for all homogeneous spaces of semisimple simply connected groups.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a homogeneous space for a semisimple and simply connected group
G
with stabilizer

at

where

is a field of characteristic

\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,H)

Proof.

The statement is equivalent to

,

and

. Consider the following composition, which equals

This implies that
(g\cdot x)^{*}\beta=(g,x)^{*}(\iota_{x}^{*}(\rho^{*}\beta))
for all

and because the canonical map

is an isomorphism by

∎

for an

\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mathcal{G})

and

). The sets of the form

4.1
Brauer-Manin pairing away from
S
with
\br^{\prime}_{S}X

To remedy the potential non-closedness of
\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{\br})
for a general variety
X
, one can also define an
a priori
closed subset in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
containing
X(k)
using the Brauer-Manin obstruction, and hope to relate such a closed subset to the closure of
X(k)
in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
. For example, in
[
17
]
and
[
14
]
, the authors considered the subgroup
\br_{S}X=\Ker(\br X\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S}\br X_{k_{v}})
of elements which are trivial at places in
S
, and
\br_{S}X
cuts out a closed subset
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}
in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
. We give examples in this section showing that
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}
does not hold in general, when
S
contains real places for homogeneous spaces
X
. We are thus motivated to use finer information of the Brauer-Manin obstruction, and we propose considering the subset
\br_{S}^{\prime}X
(see §
2
) of elements in
\br X
which induce zero evaluation maps at places in
S
.

Formally, consider the natural pairing

\displaystyle X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})\times\br^{\prime}_{S}X

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(\{x_{v}\},A)

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{v\in\Omega\backslash S}\inv_{v}(\ev_{A}(x_{v})).

For a subset
B\subseteq\br_{S}^{\prime}X
, let
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{B}
be the set of
S
-adelic points which are orthogonal to
B
. It is easy to see that
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{B}=\pr_{S}(X(\mathbb{A}_{k})^{B})
. The set
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{B}
is closed in
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
by continuity of the pairing, and contains
X(k)
by the Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether exact sequence. Therefore, when
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{B}\neq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
, there is an obstruction to strong approximation away from
S
.

When we take
B
to be the whole of
\br_{S}^{\prime}X
, we denote by
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}
the corresponding Brauer-Manin set
X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{B}
. Similarly, we can consider
B
to be
\br_{S}X
,
\br_{1,S}^{\prime}X
or
\br_{1,S}X
. Clearly, we have chains of inclusions

\begin{split}\overline{X(k)}^{S}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}&\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{S}}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br_{1,S}}\\
\overline{X(k)}^{S}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}&\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,S}}.\end{split}

(5)

In this section, we investigate conditions for when these inclusions become equalities for homogeneous spaces
X=G/H
under semisimple simply connected groups
G
with commutative stabilizers
H
, and give examples showing that these inclusions can be strict in general. As in the previous section, we use the decomposition
H\simeq M\times\mathbb{G}_{a}^{r}
with
M
of multiplicative type, and we identify
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,H)
with
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,M)
for any field
F
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is common practice to consider a Brauer-Manin pairing with the quotient group
\br X/\br k
since constant elements do not contribute to the Brauer-Manin obstruction. For example, see
[
7
, §7]
where the local evaluation map associated to
\overline{\alpha}\in\br X/\br k
is defined by fixing a “base point”
Q_{v}\in X(k_{v})
:

\overline{\alpha}\colon X(k_{v})\rightarrow\br k_{v},\quad P_{v}\mapsto\alpha(P_{v})-\alpha(Q_{v})

for any representative
\alpha\in\br X
of
\overline{\alpha}\in\br X/\br k
. With this point of view, we can define a Brauer-Manin pairing away from
S
with
\br_{S}^{\prime}X/(\br k\cap\br_{S}^{\prime}X)=\br_{S}^{\prime}X/\br_{S}k.
The natural map
\br_{S}^{\prime}X/\br_{S}k\to\{\beta\in\br X\,|\,\ev_{\beta}\text{ is constant at all }v\in S\}/\br k
is an isomorphism because of the surjectivity of
\br k\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S}\br k_{v}
. Hence we may think of an element in
\br^{\prime}_{S}X/\br_{S}k
as the class (modulo
\br k
) of an element of
\br X
inducing constant evaluation maps at
S
. Similarly, there is an isomorphism
\br_{S}X/\br_{S}k\simeq\Ker(\br X\rightarrow\prod_{v\in S}\br X_{k_{v}}/\br k_{v})/\br k
, the classes of elements which become constant at
S
, and this is the pairing considered by Harari and Voloch in
[
17
, §2]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be a commutative étale group scheme over
k
and let
S
be a finite set of places of
k
. The map
r\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)\to\prod^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)
has discrete image.

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)
and write
z\coloneq r(y)
and
I\coloneq r(\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu))
. Since the space
\prod^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)
is Hausdorff, it suffices to show that
z
has a neighbourhood
U
in
\prod^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)
that intersects
I
in finitely many points. There exists a set
T
, disjoint from
S
, such that
T
contains all infinite places not in
S
, and such that for all
v\notin T\cup S
we have
r_{v}(y)\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mu)
. We enlarge
T
such that
\#\mu(\overline{k})
is invertible in
\mathcal{O}_{T\cup S}
and we set
R:=T\cup S
. Consider the open
U\coloneq\prod_{v\in T}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)\times\prod_{v\notin R}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mu)
of
z
.
For all nonarchimedean
v
that do not divide
\#\mu(\overline{k})
, there is the Gysin sequence:

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{v},\mu)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)\overset{\res}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{v},\mu(-1))\to...

So an element
x
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)
maps into
U
if and only if
\res(r_{v}(x))=0
for all
v\notin R
.
For finite sets
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
of places of
k
, containing all archimedean ones, there is an exact sequence:

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R},\mu)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R^{\prime}},\mu)\xrightarrow{\res}\bigoplus_{v\in R^{\prime}\setminus R}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{v},\mu(-1))\to...

Taking the colimit over all finite sets of places
R^{\prime}
containing
R
gives the exact sequence:

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R},\mu)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)\to\bigoplus_{v\in\Omega\setminus R}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{v},\mu(-1))\to...

Here the middle term is indeed
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\varprojlim_{R\subseteq R^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{R^{\prime}},\mathcal{F})=\varinjlim_{R\subseteq R^{\prime}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R^{\prime}},\mathcal{F})
where the second equality is from
[
2
, VII Théorème 5.7]
. Hence we note that the elements
x\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)
having residue zero at all

can be identified with
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{T\cup S},\mu)
.

We claim
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R},\mu)
is finite for any finite
R
containing the archimedean places. After passing to a finite extension
L
of
k
we may assume that
\mu|_{L}
is constant and isomorphic to a product of
\mu_{n}(L)
(so we also adjoined roots of unity to
k
). Let
T
be the set of places of
L
extending
R
. The Kummer sequence for
\mathcal{O}_{T}
reads:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{T}^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}_{T}^{\times})^{n}\to\mathrm{H}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{T},\mu_{n})\to\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{T})[n]\to 0.

Combining Dirichlet’s unit theorem with finiteness of the class group gives that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{T},\mu_{n})
is finite and so
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{T},\mu)
is finite. Now we want to descend the finiteness of this group to
\mathcal{O}_{R}
.
We have the exact sequence in group cohomology:

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(L/k),\mu(\overline{k}))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{k},\mu(\overline{k}))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{L},\mu(\overline{k}))^{\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)}\to...

Note that
\Ker(\mathrm{H}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R},\mu)\to\mathrm{H}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{T},\mu))\subset\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(L/k),\mu(\overline{k}))
and the rightmost set is finite which follows from the cocycle description.
This implies that there is a surjection from the finite set
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{R},\mu)
to

is as in the start of the proof. This shows that any

has a finite open neighbourhood, which as discussed at the beginning completes the proof.

As a consequence of this lemma, we can generalize this statement to groups of multiplicative type.

.

For a group

of multiplicative type, the image of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M)

{\prod}^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M)

Proof.

Let

be the neutral connected component of

and let

be the étale group scheme of connected components. There is a commutative diagram where the rightmost column is an exact sequence of topological groups:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M^{\circ})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M^{\circ})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\prod}^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mu)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\prod}^{\prime}_{v\notin S}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mu)}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

This gives the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4.5

The composed map

The map is continuous by Proposition

If a sequence

Lemma 4.7
.

The natural map
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{S\setminus S_{\mathbb{R}}}(k,\hat{M})\to\prod_{v\in S_{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\hat{M})

Proof.

The result of the special case

induces a map
\prod^{\prime}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\hat{M})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M)^{*}
. Indeed this is just induced by the restriction map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M)\to\prod^{\prime}_{v}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M)
and then taking duals on both sides. Since taking the dual is exact, the map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,M)\to\prod^{\prime}_{v}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M)

\prod^{\prime}_{v}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\hat{M})\twoheadrightarrow(H^{1}(k,M)/\Sha^{1}(k,M))^{*}

is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}_{S\setminus S_{\mathbb{R}}}(k,\hat{M})/\Sha^{1}(k,\hat{M})

Remark 4.17
.

Since the condition
\overline{m}^{t}J_{p,q}m=J_{p,q}
is a condition given by the vanishing of polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
, the group functor
\SU_{p,q}
is an algebraic subgroup of the Weil restriction
R_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\SL_{n})
.

We explain now why
\SU_{p,q}
becomes isomorphic to
\SL_{n}
after a base-change to
L
. There is an involution of the second kind (as in
[
19
]
)
\tau:\Mat_{n}(L)\to\Mat_{n}(L)^{\mathrm{op}}
given by
m\mapsto J_{p,q}\overline{m}^{t}J_{p,q}
. Denote for any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
the base-change of
\tau
to
R
by
\tau_{R}
, which is an involution
\tau_{R}:\Mat_{n}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\to\Mat_{n}(L)^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R
. By using the canonical isomorphism
\Mat_{n}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\cong\Mat_{n}(L\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R)
, we see that the functor
\SU_{p,q}
is isomorphic to the functor:

R\mapsto\{m\in\Mat_{n}(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\,|\,\tau_{R}(m)\cdot m=\Id,\,\mathrm{Nrd}(m)=1\}.

After base-change to
L
, this functor becomes isomorphic to
\SL(\Mat_{n}(L))
(see
[
19
, p. 346]
). So
\SU_{p,q}
is a semisimple and simply connected algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
.
It is well known that as soon as
p,q\neq 0
, the real lie group
\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is non-compact. Hence
\SU_{p,q}
has strong approximation away from
\{\infty\}
by
[
24
, Theorem 7.21]
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
T
be the diagonal maximal torus of
\SU_{p,q}
and let
T[m]
be the algebraic subgroup of
m
-torsion points;
T[m](R)=\{\diag(a_{1},...,a_{p+q-1},\prod_{i}a_{i}^{-1})\,|\,a_{i}\in\mu_{n}(R\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i])\}
.

We remind the reader of the norm
1
subgroups of Weil restrictions of commutative algebraic groups. Associated to a finite Galois extension
M/K
and a commutative algebraic group
H
, the norm
1
algebraic group
R^{1}_{M/K}H
is by definition
\Ker(N_{M/K}:R_{M/K}(H)\to H)
.

The maximal torus
T
and its torsion comes with the following properties.

Lemma 4.19
.

The following statements about
T
hold.

•

As algebraic groups over
\mathbb{Q}
,
T
is isomorphic to
(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}
and
T[m]
is isomorphic to
(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{m}))^{2}
. The group scheme
T[m]
is constant for
m=4
. After base-change to
\mathbb{R}
, the group scheme
T[m]_{\mathbb{R}}
is constant for any
m
.

•

For any field
M
, the Galois cohomology of
T
is given by the formula:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,T)\cong\left(M^{\times}/\mathrm{Im}[N_{M(i)/M}:M(i)^{\times}\to M^{\times}]\right)^{2}

Proof.

For the first part, there is a map
(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}\to T
defined by
(a,b)\mapsto\diag(a,b,\frac{1}{ab})
on points. One checks easily that the determinant condition on
T\subset\SU_{p,q}
implies that the map is an isomorphism. The restriction of the above map to
(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{m}))^{2}
yields an isomorphism onto
T[m]
. The group scheme
T[4]\cong(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{4}))^{2}
has
16
points over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. But
R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{4})
already has
4
points
\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, so
T[4](\mathbb{Q})
contains all geometric points and hence
T[4]
is constant. A similar argument shows that after base-change to
\mathbb{R}
,
T[m]
is constant since we have the isomorphism
R^{1}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(\mu_{m})(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{Z}/m
.

For the second part, it suffices to show that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})=M^{\times}/N_{M(i)/M}(M(i)^{\times})
. This comes from the exact sequence of tori

1\rightarrow R^{1}_{M(i)/M}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{,M(i)})\rightarrow R_{M(i)/M}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{,M(i)})\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{,M}\rightarrow 1

(see
[
9
, (7.8)]
).
∎

Remark 4.20
.

As a bonus for
T[m]_{\mathbb{R}}
being constant, we obtain:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[m])=\Hom(\mathbb{Z}/2,T[m](\mathbb{C}))\cong\begin{cases}\{\diag(a_{1},...,a_{n-1},\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i})\,|\,a_{i}\in\pm 1\}\text{ if }m\text{ is even}\\
0\text{ else.}\end{cases}

To understand the map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[m])\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{p,q})
we have the following interpretation of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{p,q})
.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be the hermitian form defined by
J_{p,q}
. Denote by
\xi\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
the complex conjugation. There is a bijection:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{p,q})\leftrightarrow\{\mathbb{R}\text{-isomorphism classes of nonsingular hermitian forms }h^{\prime}\text{ with }\disc(h)=\disc(h^{\prime})\}~.

The distinguished element in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{p,q})
corresponds to the class with signature
(p,q)
. If a cocycle is given by
\xi\mapsto m
for
m\in\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
, the resulting class of hermitian forms is represented by
J_{p,q}\cdot m
.

This result should be well known in the literature (cf.
[
19
, 29.19]
and
[
1
]
), but we included a self-contained proof for the non-expert which can be found in the Appendix. We thank Mikhail Borovoi for helping to explain to us such a description along with the example in the following proposition.

Note that over
\mathbb{R}
, the signature of a hermitian form
h^{\prime}
determines its isomorphism class. In the following example we see that although
\br_{1,S}^{\prime}X
may not cut out
\overline{X(k)}^{S}
, it might still be cut out by
\br^{\prime}_{S}X
. By abuse of notation, we write
\infty
for the set
\{\infty\}
below.

Proposition 4.22
.

We have for
G=\SU_{2,1}
over
k=\mathbb{Q}
,
\mu=T[2]
and
X=G/\mu
the inclusions:

\overline{X(\mathbb{Q})}^{\infty}=X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{\infty}}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,\infty}}.

Proof.

To prove the strict inclusion
\overline{X(\mathbb{Q})}^{\infty}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{1,\infty}}
: We have the equalities

T[2](\mathbb{Q})=\{\diag(a_{1},a_{2},a_{1}\cdot a_{2})\,|\,a_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\}=T[2](\overline{\mathbb{Q}}).

So
T[2]
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
. Therefore
[
25
, Lemme 1.1]
tells us that we have
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(k,T[2])=~0
. Therefore Theorem
4.9
gives that the inclusion is an equality if and only if we have that
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])
is a subgroup. We have
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])=\{\diag(a_{1},a_{2},a_{1}\cdot a_{2})\,|\,a_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\}
and the map to
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{2,1})
is by the above lemma given by

T[2]\ni m\mapsto[J_{2,1}\cdot m]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{2,1}).

We have that
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])
corresponds to the elements of
T[2]
which map to hermitian forms with signature
(p,q)
. These are
\{(1,1,1),(-1,1,-1),(1,-1,-1)\}
, which do not form a subgroup of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])
.

So we conclude that
X(\mathbb{Q})^{\infty}\subsetneq(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br_{1,\infty}^{\prime}}.

In fact, the only cocycle that does not come from
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))
is represented by
\diag(-1,-1,1)
. Denote by
\iota\colon\mu_{2}\to\overline{k}^{\times}
the canonical inclusion. Since
\Gamma_{k}
acts trivially on
T[2]
, we obtain that
\pi_{1}(X)
(resp.
\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}})
) is identified with
T[2]\times\Gamma_{k}
(resp.
T[2]\times\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}})
(for this identification we use the section
o_{*}:\Gamma_{k}\to\pi_{1}(X)
(resp.
o_{*}:\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}})
)). Hence projection on the first factor gives us a retraction
\lambda_{o}:\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}})\to T[2]
(resp.
\pi_{1}(X)\to T[2]
). We consider the homomorphism
\iota_{*}\lambda_{o}^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}(T[2],\mu_{2})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}}),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, which factors via
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\pi_{1}(X),\overline{k}^{\times})
. We also consider a section
\sigma:\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}})
Since the pushforwards and pullbacks below commute (this is easily seen from how pullbacks and pushforwards act on cocycles), there is a commuting diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{2}(T[2],\mu_{2})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mu_{2})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{2}(\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbb{R}}),\mathbb{C}^{\times})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{2}(\pi_{1}(X),\overline{k}^{\times})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{\times}).}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}\lambda_{o}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\lambda_{o}\circ s)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}}

Note that the map
\iota_{*}
in the bottom of the diagram is an isomorphism.
We have
T[2]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
and thus we get
\mathrm{H}^{2}(T[2],\mu_{2})\cong\Ext_{c}((\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2},\mathbb{Z}/2)
. We pick an isoomorphism
T[2]\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
such that
\diag(-1,-1,1)
is sent to
(1,0)
and from now on we use this identification. Our cocycle of interest is in this case

f\colon\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\quad\xi\mapsto(1,0).

Consider the group
D_{8}=\langle r,s\,|\,r^{4},s^{2},rsrs\rangle
. Consider the element of
\Ext_{c}((\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2},\mathbb{Z}/2)
, which is
\alpha=[1\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to D_{8}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\to 1]
, where
D_{8}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
is the quotient map modulo
\langle r^{2}\rangle
and
(1,0)=\overline{r}
and
(0,1)=\overline{s}
. A short case-by-case calculation shows that pulling back
\alpha
by any
g\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])
that is distinct from
f
gives a split extension, while pulling back via
f
yields a non-split extension (namely
\mathbb{Z}/4
).

Now consider the image of
\alpha
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\pi_{1}(X),\overline{k}^{\times})=\br{X}
(see
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
). It lies in
\br^{\prime}_{\infty}(X)
since it pairs trivially with

by the end of the previous paragraph. For a point
(x_{v})_{v\neq\infty}\in X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{\infty}}
we have that the image

comes from a unique

by the vanishing of

. Since all local invariants sum up to

, we have

which vanishes if and only if

lies in

by Lemma

.
∎

Remark 4.23
.

It is easy to see that the Brauer element
\alpha
that was constructed above is transcendental since its evaluation map
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is not linear: Three elements go to
0
and one goes to
\frac{1}{2}
. However any element in
\br_{1}X\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,T[2])\times\br k
gives a linear map induced by the cup-product in Galois cohomology.

4.4
Example of
\overline{X(k)}^{S}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}

It is natural to ask whether
\overline{X(k)}^{S}=X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})^{\br^{\prime}_{S}}
always occurs. We establish examples to show that this is not always the case. First we give an example in which the stabilizer is a torus.

Proposition 4.24
.

Let

G=\SU_{p,q}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with
p,q\neq 0
and
p+q\geq 3
. Let
T
be the diagonal maximal torus of
G
as defined in Definition
4.18
and let
X\coloneq\SU_{p,q}/T
. Then we have the strict inclusion

\overline{X(\mathbb{Q})}^{\infty}\subsetneq~X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{\infty}}.

Proof.

We first show that
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},T)=0
. Since
T\cong(R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}
, it suffices to show that
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
. Using the identification from Lemma
4.19
, we take one element in
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})
and it corresponds to
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
which is a norm from
\mathbb{Q}_{v}(i)
for all places
v\neq\infty
. This means that the quaternion algebra
(-1,a)\in\br\mathbb{Q}[2]
vanishes in
\br\mathbb{Q}_{v}
for all
v\neq\infty
. The exact sequence

0\to\br\mathbb{Q}\to\bigoplus_{v}\br\mathbb{Q}_{v}\xrightarrow{\sum_{v}\inv_{v}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\to 0

implies that
(-1,a)
vanishes in
\br\mathbb{R}
and hence
(-1,a)=0
. The vanishing of
(-1,a)\in\br\mathbb{Q}[2]
is equivalent to
a
being a norm from
\mathbb{Q}(i)
, and thus
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},R^{1}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
and
\Sha^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},T)=0
.

Since
T
is connected, we can apply
[
25
, Proposition 6.10]
to the torsor
G\rightarrow X
under
T
and we get
\br{X}=\Pic T=\br_{1}X
. Therefore, by Theorem
4.9
, it will be necessary and sufficient to show that
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))
is not a subgroup in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T)
. Since
T(\mathbb{R})
is divisible and since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T)
is
2
-torsion, the natural map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T[2])\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T)
is an isomorphism. So
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},T)
consists of the cocyles:
\{\xi\mapsto(\diag(a_{1},...,a_{n-1},\prod_{i}a_{i}^{-1})\,|\,a_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\}
. The image of
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))
consists of all cocycles
\xi\to m
, such that
J_{p,q}\cdot m
has signature
(p,q)
. Assume without loss of generality that
p\geq 2
(else we have
q\geq 2
and this case is analogous). The cocycles
\xi\mapsto\diag(-1,1,...,-1)
and
\xi\mapsto\diag(1,-1,...,-1)
(where dots mean that all entries there are
1
’s) both lie in
\delta_{\mathbb{R}}(X(\mathbb{R}))
, however their product does not.

So we conclude that we have
\overline{X(\mathbb{Q})}^{\infty}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{\infty}}
in this case.
∎

Before giving another example with finite commutative stabilizer, we need the following lemma on cup products in group cohomology.

Lemma 4.25
.

Let
G
and
H
be groups that act trivially on a commutative ring
R
. Denote the projections of
G\times H
on the first and second factor by
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
respectively. The following statements hold:

•

Given a section
s
to
p_{2}
, written
s=(\varphi_{s},\Id):H\to G\times H
, we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p}(G,R)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{q}(H,R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p}(H,R)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{q}(H,R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(G\times H,R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(H,R)};}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}^{*}\cup p_{2}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{s}^{*}\otimes\Id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{s}^{*}}

•

Given homomorphisms

and
f_{2}\colon H^{\prime}\to H
, there is a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p}(G^{\prime},R)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{q}(H^{\prime},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p}(G,R)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{q}(H,R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{p+q}(G^{\prime}\times H^{\prime},R)}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}^{*}\otimes f_{2}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}^{*}\cup p_{2}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f_{1}\times f_{2})^{*}}

Proof.

We will prove the statements by doing calculations with cocycles, since it suffices to show that the diagram commutes on the level of cocycles.
Denote by
[-]
the cohomology class of a cocycle.
To prove that the first diagram commutes, take cocycles
\alpha\colon G^{p}\to R
and
\beta\colon H^{q}\to R
.
We have
\varphi_{s}^{*}(\alpha)=\alpha\circ(\varphi_{s})^{p}\colon H^{p}\to R
.
Then we get that

is represented by the cocycle

On the other hand, applying

to

gives a cohomology class that is represented by the cocycle

Pulling this cocycle back via

gives us that the first diagram commutes.

One proves that the second diagram commutes in an analogous way by computing that for
\alpha\otimes\beta
a tensor product of two cocycles, one ends up with the cocycle:

(G\times H)^{p+q}\to R\quad((g_{1},h_{1}),...,(g_{p},h_{p}),...,(g_{p+q},h_{p+q}))\mapsto\alpha(f_{1}(g_{1}),...,f_{1}(g_{p}))\cdot\beta(f_{2}(g_{p+1}),...,f_{2}(g_{p+q}))

∎

Now we give an example in which the stabilizer is finite and in which we have the strict inclusion

\overline{X(\mathbb{Q})}^{\infty}\subsetneq X(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})^{\br^{\prime}_{\infty}}
. Note the contrast between this and the example in Proposition

.

Proposition 4.26
.

Set
\mu=T[4]
to be the subgroup of
\SU_{2,1}
as defined in Definition

. Define the

. We have the strict inclusion:

4.26
. Let

be a place of

. Elements in

can be base-changed to elements in

, after which they can be paired with elements in

).

.

. By Lemma

, the image of such an element

, i.e. precisely one element in

maps to a nonzero element in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{2,1})
.

We let

be an element in

whose image in

under the canonical map is nonzero. This

). Consider the diagonal image of

in

, which lifts to an adelic point
(x_{v})_{v\neq\infty}
by Lemma

combined with Proposition

. For any

, we have

J_{p,q}
.

The complex conjugation
\xi
also acts on
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
by conjugating the second factor. We associate to
h
with matrix
M_{h}
a
1
-cocycle
\sigma_{h}\colon\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{C})
by first picking an isomorphism
S\in\GL(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
,
(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C},h)\overset{S}{\to}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C},J_{p,q})
and then setting
\sigma_{h}(\xi)=S^{-1}\xi(S)
(note that
\xi
acts only on the second factor of
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
). Since
(S^{*})^{-1}J_{p,q}(S^{-1})^{*}=M_{h}
we get that
\det(S)\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and thus
\det(S^{-1}\xi(S))=1
. Moreover, to show that the cocycle maps into
\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{C})
, we compute:

(S^{-1}\cdot\xi(S))^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot S^{-1}\xi(S)=\xi(S)^{*}\cdot(M_{h}\otimes 1)\cdot\cdot\xi(S)=\xi(S^{*}\cdot(M_{h}\otimes 1)\cdot S)=J_{p,q}\otimes 1.

It is routine to show that the cohomology class of
\sigma_{h}
does not depend on the choice of
S
and the choice of
h
in an isomorphism class of hermitian forms.

To give the inverse: Suppose that
\sigma\colon\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{C})
, defined by
\xi\mapsto M
, is a cocycle. Consider the natural inclusion
\SU_{p,q}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq\SL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
. Since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\SL_{m})=0
for any field
K
and any
m
, there is
T\in\SL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
T^{-1}\xi(T)=M
. Then consider the bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
, restricted to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
:

b_{\sigma}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\quad(v,w)\mapsto(v\otimes 1)^{t}\cdot T^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot T\cdot(w\otimes 1).

Notice that since
T\mapsto T^{*}
commutes with complex conjugation on
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
, we have:

\xi(T^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot T)=\xi(T)^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot\xi(T)=(M\cdot T)^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot(M\cdot T)=T^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot T.

So actually the matrix defining
b_{\sigma}
lies in
\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})\otimes 1\subseteq\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})
. It follows directly that
T^{*}\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot T
is a hermitian matrix and hence it defines a hermitian form
h_{\sigma}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. This hermitian form becomes isomorphic to
J_{p,q}
over
\mathbb{C}
by the isomorphism
T
, which by construction gives the cocycle
\sigma(\xi)=T^{-1}\xi(T)=M
.

It is clear that the assingments described above are each others inverses and hence we get the bijection:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\SU_{p,q})\leftrightarrow\{\text{nonsingular hermitian forms }\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ with discriminant }(-1)^{p}\}.

Example 6.2
.

Let
\sigma\colon\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\to\SU_{p+q}(\mathbb{C})
be a cocycle defined by
\sigma(\xi)=\colon M\in\SU_{p+q}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
M
is
2
-torsion and hence diagonalizable over
\mathbb{R}
, so
M=g^{-1}\cdot J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\cdot g
for
q^{\prime}\equiv q\,(\text{mod }2)
. Therefore the cocycle
\sigma
is equivalent to
\tau\colon\xi\mapsto J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
. We write
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
as
\mathbb{C}^{n}\oplus j\cdot\mathbb{C}
with
j^{2}=-1
. Consider the tensor product of diagonal matrices, where there are
q^{\prime}

i
’s and
q^{\prime}

j
’s. Let

T\coloneq\diag(i,...,i,1,...,1)\otimes\diag(j,...,j,1,...,1).

It follows that
T^{-1}\xi(T)=J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
and thus the associated hermitian form
h_{\sigma}
is then defined by the matrix
T\cdot(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot T^{*}=(J_{p,q}\otimes 1)\cdot(\diag(1,...,1,j,...,j))^{2}=J_{p,q}\cdot J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
. So if
\xi
has real image
M
under
\sigma
, the corresponding hermitian form is obtained by multiplying
J_{p,q}
by
J_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
where
q^{\prime}
is the number of negative eigenvalues of
M
.

Probabilistic Tits alternative for circle diffeomorphisms
Martín Gilabert Vio

Abstract
. Let μ 1 , μ 2 \mu_{1},\mu_{2} be probability measures on Diff + 1 ​ ( S 1 ) \mathrm{Diff}^{1}_{+}(S^{1}) satisfying a suitable moment condition and such that their supports genererate discrete groups acting proximally on S 1 S^{1} . Let ( f ω n ) n ∈ ℕ , ( f ω ′ n ) n ∈ ℕ (f^{n}_{\omega})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},(f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}} be two independent realizations of the random walk driven by μ 1 , μ 2 \mu_{1},\mu_{2} respectively. We show that almost surely there is an N ∈ ℕ N\in{\mathbb{N}} such that for all n ≥ N n\geq N the elements f ω n , f ω ′ n f^{n}_{\omega},f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}} generate a nonabelian free group. The proof is inspired by the strategy by R. Aoun for linear groups and uses work of A. Gorodetski, V. Kleptsyn and G. Monakov, and of P. Barrientos and D. Malicet. A weaker (and easier) statement holds for measures supported on Homeo + ​ ( S 1 ) \mathrm{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1}) with no moment conditions.

1.
Context and contributions

The Tits alternative is a celebrated theorem by J. Tits which asserts that finitely generated linear groups are either virtually solvable or contain a nonabelian free group
[
Tit72
]
. This alternative fails for groups of homeomorphisms of the circle, but a weaker alternative (sometimes called a
dynamical Tits alternative
, see
[
MM23
]
) still holds.

Theorem 1.1

(G. Margulis
[
Mar00
]
, see also V. Antonov
[
Ant84
]
)
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{Homeo}(S^{1})
. Then either

i.

G
is
elementary
, that is, the action of
G
preserves a probability measure on
S^{1}
, or

G
contains a

such that there are pairwise disjoint open subsets

of
S^{1}
with
f(S^{1}-U_{1})\subseteq V_{1},\,g(S^{1}-U_{2})\subseteq V_{2}

The previous options are mutually exclusive and, by the ping-pong lemma, condition (

generate a nonabelian free group in
G
. We are interested in how generic these two elements are. As a first approximation, there is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset

such that any pair of elements in

[
Ghy01

[
Tri14
, Theorem 6.9]

\mu

Theorem A
.

Let
G_{1},\,G_{2}
be countable subgroups of
\mathrm{Diff}^{1}_{+}(S^{1})

As with Theorem

Corollary B
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
be probability measures on
\mathrm{Diff}^{1}_{+}(S^{1})

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}

Theorem C
.

Let
G_{1},\,G_{2}
be countable subgroups of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{+}(S^{1})

and of

on

2.
Preliminaries

We review some basic theory on random dynamical systems and groups acting on the circle, and introduce some notation. For more details on this material, see
[
Ghy01
,
DKN07
,
Mal17
]
.

Notation

Given a metric space
(X,d)
,
A\subseteq X
and
\varepsilon>0
, we denote
A^{\varepsilon}=\{x\in X\mid d(x,A)\leq\varepsilon\}
. We will denote by
d
the metric on
S^{1}
coming from an identification
S^{1}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, so
\mathrm{diam}(S^{1})=1/2
.

Random dynamical systems

A
random dynamical system

(G,\mu)\curvearrowright X
(or a
random walk on
X
) is the data of a group
G
acting by homeomorphisms on a compact metric space
(X,d)
and of a probability measure
\mu
on
G
. We always assume that
\mu
is
nondegenerate
, that is, that the semigroup generated by
\mu
is
G
. Denote by
(\Omega,{\mathbb{P}})
the probability space
(G^{\mathbb{N}},\mu^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}})
and set
f^{n}_{\omega}=f_{\omega_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{0}}
when
n\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\omega=(f_{\omega_{k}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\Omega
. A
\mu
-stationary measure
is a Borel probability measure on
X
such that
\mu\ast\nu=\nu
, where

\mu\ast\nu(A)=\int_{G}\nu(g^{-1}A)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(g)

for all Borel
A\subseteq X
.

We say that
(G,\mu)\curvearrowright X
is
locally contracting
if for all
x\in X
,
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely there exists a neighborhood
B\subset X
of
x
such that
\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}_{\omega}(B))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Mal17
, Propositions 4.8 and 4.9]
)
.

Suppose
(G,\mu)\curvearrowright X
is locally contracting. Then there are finitely many ergodic
\mu
-stationary measures
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{d}
, and their supports are exactly the minimal
G
-invariant sets in
X
.

Moreover, for every
x\in X
and
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost every
\omega
there exist a unique index
i=i(\omega,x)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
f^{n}_{\omega}(x)

equidistributes towards
\nu_{i}
, that is

(2.1)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}1_{f^{n}_{\omega}(x)}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\nu_{i}

in the weak
\ast
-topology.

Groups acting on the circle

We say that a group action
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
is
proximal
if for all
x,y\in S^{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
g\in G
such that
d(g(x),g(y))<\varepsilon
. The action is said to be
locally proximal
if it is not proximal and every
x\in S^{1}
is the endpoint of an interval
I\subset S^{1}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
g\in G
with
\mathrm{diam}(g(I))<\varepsilon
. In this context, we say that a group action
G\curvearrowright^{\phi}S^{1}
is
semiconjugate
to
G\curvearrowright^{\psi}S^{1}
if there exists a continuous surjection
\pi\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
such that
\psi(g)\circ\pi=\pi\circ\phi(g)
for all
g\in G
, and such that
\pi
is locally non-decreasing and has degree one (this means that any lift of
\pi
to
\widetilde{\pi}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is non-decreasing and satisfies
\widetilde{\pi}(x+1)=\widetilde{\pi}(x)+1
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
). When such a
\pi
does not necessarily have degree 1, we call it a
factor map
.

The following theorem is essentially equivalent to Theorem
1.1
from the introduction: in cases (
ii
) and (
iii
) below
G
always contains a free group, and there exists an invariant probability measure for
G
in cases (
i
) (a mean of Dirac measures on a finite orbit on
S^{1}
) and (
i
) (the image of Lebesgue measure under a conjugacy to a group of rotations).

Theorem 2.2

(see
[
Ghy01
]
)
.

Consider an action
G\curvearrowright^{\phi}S^{1}
by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms. Then exactly one of the following options is satisfied.

i.

There exists a finite orbit.

ii.

There exists a unique closed minimal set
\Lambda
, which is either
S^{1}
or a Cantor set. In the latter case, by collapsing the countably many connected components of
S^{1}-\Lambda
we can semiconjugate
\phi
to a minimal group action
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
.

Moreover, in the minimal case a further distinction exists: either

ii.a

the action is free and thus conjugated to an action by rotations, or

ii.b

the action is proximal, or

ii.c

the action is locally proximal and not proximal, and there exists
d\in{\mathbb{N}},\,d\geq 2
and a continuous
d
-to-one covering
\pi\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
that intertwines
\phi
with a proximal action.

Thus whenever
G\curvearrowright^{\phi}S^{1}
does not preserve a probability measure on
S^{1}
, there exists
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a factor map
\pi\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
that is
d
-to-one on the minimal set of
G
(except for a countable number of points), and that intertwines
\phi
with a proximal and minimal action. We will call this integer
d
the
degree of proximality
of
\phi
, but this notation is not standard.

Random walks on
S^{1}

In this subsection, fix a countable group
G
and a nondegenerate probability measure
\mu
on
G
. The random walk on
S^{1}
defined by a proximal group action
G\curvearrowright^{\phi}S^{1}
has been well studied.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
DKN07
, Appendice]
)
.

Consider an action
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms with no invariant probability measure on
S^{1}
.

i.

There exists a unique
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
, which is atomless and is supported on the minimal set of
G
.

ii.

If the action of
G
on
S^{1}
is proximal, there exists a random variable
\omega\in\Omega\mapsto\sigma(\omega)\in S^{1}
such that for
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost every
\omega
we have

(f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}\nu\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1_{\sigma(\omega)}

in the weak-
\ast
topology.

We call the random variable
\sigma(\omega)
from the previous theorem the
repulsor
of the random walk
(f^{n}_{\omega})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Its distribution is the unique
\overline{\mu}
-stationary measure on
S^{1}
where
\overline{\mu}\in\mathrm{Prob}(G)
is defined on

as
\mu(g^{-1})
, and is thus nonatomic.

Notice that the measure
(f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}\nu
is given by
\nu(f^{n}_{\omega}(I))
on every interval
I\subseteq S^{1}
, so the statement in (
ii
) says that
f^{n}_{\omega}(I)
is contracted into a
\nu
-null set unless
I
contains
\sigma(\omega)
, in which case it is expanded to the whole circle. As a consequence, for all
x,y\in S^{1}
we have
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely that
x
and
y
are not
\sigma(\omega)
since the law of
\sigma(\omega)
is nonatomic, and hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(f^{n}_{\omega}(x),f^{n}_{\omega}(y))=0
. This conclusion is the subject of
[
KN04
]
(see also
[
Ant84
]
), and justifies the fact that we will use

(or

for some nonrandom

) as an “attractor” for

in the proofs below.

When
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
is not necessarily proximal a similar statement holds, and even more is true: the rate of contraction of
f^{n}_{\omega}(I)
when
\sigma(\omega)\not\in I
is exponential.

Theorem 2.4
.

Consider an action
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms with no invariant probability measure on

. Let

be the degree of proximality of

There exist measurable functions

.

ii.

[
Mal17
, Proposition 4.3]

{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely, the set
\{\sigma_{1}(\omega),\ldots,\sigma_{d}(\omega)\}
has size
d
.

The random set
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d}\}
from the previous theorem is called the
repelling set
of the random walk
(f^{n}_{\omega})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. In this setting, let
\pi\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
be a factor map to a minimal and proximal action and
\Lambda\subseteq S^{1}
be the minimal set of
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
. Define

as the countable set of images of connected components of

, so for all

, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(x)

d
. Denote by
\sigma(\omega)
the repulsor of the random walk in the image of

. If
\sigma(\omega)\in S^{1}\setminus E
(which happens

-almost surely, since the distribution of

and

.

Consider an action
G\curvearrowright S^{1}
by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms with no invariant probability measure on
S^{1}
. Denote by
\pi\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}

Theorem 4.2
.

,

, so (

Claim
.

For every

Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(f^{k_{2}}_{\omega}(x),f^{k_{2}}_{\omega}(y))<\varepsilon_{1}\right]

Fix

. If we take

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{s}}{4}+\frac{r^{s}}{4}\leq\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)^{s}.

For every

and

, define a random variable

(which depends on

(resp.

(4.2)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(f^{K_{k}(\omega)}_{\omega}(x),f^{K_{k}(\omega)}_{\omega}(y)\right)^{s}\right]\leq\max\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{k_{2}}},e^{-\frac{k}{k_{1}}\lambda}\right\}d(x,y)^{s}.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lvert\left(f_{\omega_{K_{k}}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{k}}\right)^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lvert f_{\omega_{K_{k}}}^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\cdots\left\lvert f_{\omega_{k}}^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lvert f_{\omega_{k+\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}}}^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\cdots\left\lvert f_{\omega_{k}}^{-1}\right\rvert^{s}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left\lvert g^{-1}\right\rvert^{s\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}}_{\mathrm{Lip}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(g).

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[d(f^{k}_{\omega}(x),f^{k}_{\omega}(y))^{s/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lvert\left(f_{\omega_{K_{k}}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{k}}\right)^{-1}\right\rvert^{s/2}_{\mathrm{Lip}}d\left(f^{K_{k}(\omega)}_{\omega}(x),f^{K_{k}(\omega)}_{\omega}(y)\right)^{s/2}\right]

where we have used (

) and (

, where

), the term

, and hence there exists

and

such that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[d(f^{k}_{\omega}(x),f^{k}_{\omega}(y))^{s}\right]\leq e^{-\lambda}d(x,y)^{s}

for all

and

.

Remark
.

The previous theorem says that

is

, according to the terminology of Benoist-Quint in
[

[

[

Denote by

Proposition 4.4

x^{n}_{k}=k/K_{n}\in S^{1},\,0\leq k\leq K_{n}-1
and the event

V_{n}=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid d(f^{n}_{\omega}(x^{n}_{k}),f^{n}_{\omega}\left(x^{n}_{k}\right))\leq e^{-\lambda n/2}\text{ for all }0\leq k\leq K_{n}-1\},

so we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left[V_{n}^{c}\right]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K_{n}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[d\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(x_{k}),f^{n}_{\omega}(x_{k+1})\right)\geq e^{-\lambda n/2}\right]\leq e^{-\lambda n/2}K_{n}\leq e^{-\lambda n/6},

where we have used the Markov inequality and (
4.7
).

Notice that if
\omega\in V_{n}
, then there exists a unique interval
J_{n,\omega}\subset S^{1}
of the form
[x^{n}_{j},x^{n}_{j+1})
such that
\mathrm{diam}\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(J_{n,\omega})\right)\geq 1-e^{-\lambda n/2}
.

Claim
.

There exists
C>0
such that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[d(J_{n,\omega},\sigma(\omega))\mid V_{n}\right]\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{1}n}
for all large enough
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
\lambda_{1}=\max\{\lambda/6,\lambda_{-}\}
and
\lambda_{-}
is given by Proposition
4.3
.

Proof of the claim.

Given
\omega\in V_{n}
, define
j_{n,\omega}\in S^{1}
by

j_{n,\omega}=\begin{cases}(f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0)&\text{ if }0\in f^{n}_{\omega}(J_{n,\omega})\\
(f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(1/2)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
\mathrm{diam}\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(J_{n,\omega})\right)\geq 1-e^{-\lambda n/2}
, for all
n>2\log 2/\lambda
we have that
f^{n}_{\omega}(J_{n,\omega})
contains 0 or
1/2
, so
j_{n,\omega}\in J_{n,\omega}
. Thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[d(J_{n,\omega},\sigma(\omega))\mid V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathrm{diam}(J_{n,\omega})+d(j_{n,\omega},\sigma(\omega))\mid V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{n}}+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\mid V_{n}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(1/2),\sigma(\omega)\right)\mid V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K_{n}}+{\mathbb{P}}\left[V_{n}\right]^{-1}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(1/2),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]\right)

for
n>\max\{2\log 2/\lambda,N\}
. From the bound (
4.4
) and the fact that
{\mathbb{P}}[V_{n}]
is bounded away from 0 we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Let
C,\lambda_{1}>0
be the constants given by the previous claim and take
\varepsilon\in(e^{-\lambda_{1}},1)
, so

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\not\subseteq f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\not\subseteq f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]+{\mathbb{P}}\left[V_{n}^{c}\right]

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[J_{n,\omega}\not\subseteq\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\text{ or }f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus J_{n,\omega}\right)\not\subseteq f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]+{\mathbb{P}}\left[V_{n}^{c}\right].

for large enough
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since
\varepsilon>e^{-\lambda/3}
and
K_{n}=\lfloor e^{\lambda n/3}\rfloor
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that the inequalities

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[J_{n,\omega}\not\subseteq\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(J_{n,\omega},\sigma(\omega))\geq\varepsilon^{n}-\mathrm{diam}(J_{n,\omega})\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\left[d(J_{n,\omega},\sigma(\omega)\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]}{\varepsilon^{n}-1/K_{n}}\leq C\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{1}{1-1/(\varepsilon^{n}K_{n})}\right)\leq C^{\prime}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n}

hold, so the right-hand side of (
4.9
) decreases with exponential speed towards 0 by the choice of
\varepsilon
.

Similarly, since
\varepsilon^{n}\geq e^{-\lambda n/6}\geq\mathrm{diam}\left(f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus J_{n,\omega}\right)\right)
for all large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus J_{n,\omega}\right)\not\subseteq f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[0\in J_{n,\omega}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[0\in J_{n,\omega}\text{ and }J_{n,\omega}\subseteq\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]+C^{\prime}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(0,\sigma(\omega))\leq\varepsilon^{n}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]+C^{\prime}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n},

where we have used (
4.9
) in the second inequality. Moreover, Theorem
2.6
provides
C^{\prime\prime},\alpha>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(0,\sigma(\omega))\leq\varepsilon^{n}\right]\leq C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{\alpha n},

and from (
4.8
) we conclude that

(4.10)

{\mathbb{P}}\left[f^{n}_{\omega}\left(S^{1}\setminus J_{n,\omega}\right)\not\subseteq f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\middle|\,V_{n}\right]\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[V_{n}\right]^{-1}C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{\alpha n}+C^{\prime}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n}.

The bounds (
4.10
) and (
4.9
) show that the right-hand side of (
4.8
) is exponentially small in
n
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

From now on, the rest of the proof of Theorem
A
follows the strategy of
[
Aou11
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every
t\in(0,1)
we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),\sigma(\omega))\leq t^{n}\right]<0.

Proof.

We start with a version of
[
Aou11
, Theorem 4.35]
(which in turn is inspired by
[
Gui90
, Lemme 8]
), which states that the variables
f^{n}_{\omega}(0)
and
\sigma(\omega)
become asymptotically independent with exponential speed as
n\to\infty
.

Claim
.

There exists a random variable
S(\omega)\in S^{1}
independent of
\sigma
and constants
C,\lambda>0
such that for any Lipschitz function
\psi\colon S^{1}\times S^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),S(\omega)\right)\right]\right\rvert\leq Ce^{-\lambda n}\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}

for sufficiently large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where

\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,u,v\in S^{1}\\
x\neq y\text{ \emph{or} }u\neq v\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lvert\psi(x,u)-\psi(y,v)\right\rvert}{d(x,y)+d(u,v)}.

Proof of the claim.

Let
\lambda_{-},\lambda_{+}>0
be the constants given by Proposition
4.3
. Consider an independent copy
\omega^{\prime}=(f_{\omega_{n}^{\prime}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of the process
\omega
(that is, a coupling of
{\mathbb{P}}
with itself). Define
S(\omega^{\prime})
as the repulsor of the random walk
\left(f_{\omega_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ f_{\omega_{n-1}^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{0}^{\prime}}^{-1}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, so

\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(x),S(\omega^{\prime})\right)\right]\leq e^{-\lambda_{+}n}

holds for all large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Decompose

\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),S(\omega^{\prime})\right)\right]\right\rvert\leq\triangle_{1}+\triangle_{2}+\triangle_{3}+\triangle_{4}

where

·

\triangle_{1}=\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),f^{n}_{\omega}(0)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),f^{n}_{\omega}(0)\right)\right]\right\rvert
,

·

\triangle_{2}=\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),f^{n}_{\omega}(0)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0),f_{\omega_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{\lceil n/2\rceil+1}}(0)\right)\right]\right\rvert
,

·

\triangle_{3}=\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0),f_{\omega_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{\lceil n/2\rceil+1}}(0)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)\right)\right]\right\rvert

=\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0),\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)\right)\right]\right\rvert
, and

·

\triangle_{4}=\left\lvert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)\right)\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi\left(\sigma(\omega),S(\omega^{\prime})\right)\right]\right\rvert
.

Proposition
4.3
shows that
\triangle_{1}\leq\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}e^{-\lambda_{-}n},\,\triangle_{3}\leq\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}e^{-\lambda_{-}n/2}
and
\triangle_{4}\leq\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}
for all large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover

\displaystyle\triangle_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0)\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),f_{\omega_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\omega_{\lceil n/2\rceil+1}}(0)\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0)\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega}(0),\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega}(0)\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left((f^{n}_{\omega})^{-1}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(\left(f^{\lceil n/2\rceil}_{\omega}\right)^{-1}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\left.{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega}(0),T(\omega)\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{\omega}(0),T(\omega)\right)\right]\right)

which is at most

\left\lvert\psi\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left(e^{-\lambda_{-}n}+e^{-\lambda_{-}n/2}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}\right)

by Proposition
4.3
again. The claim follows.
∎

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, take
\phi_{\varepsilon}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
a
1/\varepsilon
-Lipschitz function such that
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\phi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{[0,\varepsilon]}}=1
and
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\phi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{[2\varepsilon,1]}}=0
, so

1_{[0,\varepsilon]}\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1_{[0,2\varepsilon]}

holds and
\psi_{\varepsilon}\doteq\phi_{\varepsilon}\circ d\colon S^{1}\times S^{1}\to[0,1]
is also
1/\varepsilon
-Lipschitz. Let
C,\lambda>0
be the constants given by the previous claim.

Now for all large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left[d\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\leq t^{n}\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi_{t^{n}}\left(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),\sigma(\omega)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\psi_{t^{n}}\left(\sigma(\omega),S(\omega)\right)\right]+Ce^{-\lambda n}\left\lvert\psi_{t^{n}}\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left[d\left(\sigma(\omega),S(\omega)\right)\leq 2t^{n}\right]+Ce^{-\lambda n}\left\lvert\psi_{t^{n}}\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(\sigma(\omega),x)\leq 2t^{n}\right]+Ce^{-\lambda n}\left\lvert\psi_{t^{n}}\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}},

where we have used the independence of
\sigma
and
S
in the last inequality. The first term

\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}\left[d(\sigma(\omega),x)\leq 2t^{n}\right]

is exponentially small in
n
by Theorem
2.6
, and the second term

Ce^{-\lambda n/2}\left\lvert\psi_{t^{n}}\right\rvert_{\mathrm{Lip}}=C\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda/2}}{t}\right)^{n}

is also exponentially small in
n
whenever
t>e^{-\lambda/2}
. The proposition is thus proven in this case, and is also true for
t\leq e^{-\lambda/2}
as a consequence.
∎

Remark
.

In the proof of the previous theorem we have abused notation by writing
S(\omega)
: the random variable
S
is not a function of
\omega
, and is defined on a larger probability space. We have done so as to not to weigh down the notation with distinctions between this larger probability space and its quotient
(\Omega,{\mathbb{P}})
, since all relevant means and measures of sets coincide with those of
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Fix
\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}
two nondegenerate probability measures on countable subgroups
G_{1},\,G_{2}
of
\mathrm{Diff}^{1}_{+}(S^{1})
acting proximally on
S^{1}
such that
\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}
satisfy the moment condition (
M
).

Claim
.

For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{2}\mapsto l_{n}(\omega^{\prime})\in S^{1}
be a measurable map. For every
t\in(0,1)
we have

(4.11)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\left[d(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),l_{n}(\omega^{\prime}))\leq t^{n}\right]<0

and

(4.12)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\left[d(\sigma(\omega),l_{n}(\omega^{\prime}))\leq t^{n}\right]<0.

Proof of the claim.

By independence, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\left[d(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),l_{n}(\omega^{\prime}))\leq t^{n}\right]\leq\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),x)\leq t^{n}\right]=\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega}(0),x)\leq t^{n}\right].

Proposition
4.3
and the Markov inequality imply that

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega}(0),T(\omega)\right)\geq e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}\right]\leq e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2},

for some
\lambda_{+}>0
and all large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and thus

\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d\left(\overline{f}^{n}_{\omega}(0),x\right)\leq t^{n}\right]\leq\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d\left(T(\omega),x\right)\leq t^{n}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}\right]+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}.

Take
C,\alpha>0
such that the distribution of
T
is
(C,\alpha)
-Hölder, so

\sup_{x\in S^{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\left[d(T(\omega),x)\leq t^{n}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}\right]\leq C(t^{n}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2})^{\alpha}

and

{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\left[d(f^{n}_{\omega}(0),l_{n}(\omega^{\prime}))\leq t^{n}\right]\leq C(t^{n}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2})^{\alpha}+e^{-\lambda_{+}n/2}

is exponentially small in
n
. This gives (
4.11
), and (
4.12
) follows in the same way.
∎

Take
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
so that the conclusion of Proposition
4.4
is verified for
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}=\mu_{1}^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{2}=\mu_{2}^{\otimes{\mathbb{N}}}
. Given
\omega\in\Omega_{1},\,\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{2}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we say that
f^{n}_{\omega}
and
f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}
are in
\varepsilon
-transverse position at time
n
if the intervals
f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\,f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\,\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}
and
\sigma(\omega^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{n}}
are pairwise disjoint. This is exactly the situation in Figure
1
for

I_{n,\omega}=f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\quad I_{n,\omega^{\prime}}=f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\quad J_{n,\omega}=\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}\,\text{ and }\,J_{n,\omega^{\prime}}=\sigma(\omega^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{n}}.

Proposition
4.5
shows that the probability that the pair
(f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}})
or the pair
(f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\sigma(\omega^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{n}})
intersect is exponentially small in
n
. The previous claim shows that the probability that the remaining pairs

(f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\sigma(\omega^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{n}}),\quad(f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}}),\quad(f^{n}_{\omega}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}},f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}(0)^{\varepsilon^{n}})\,\text{ or }\,(\sigma(\omega)^{\varepsilon^{n}},\sigma(\omega^{\prime})^{\varepsilon^{n}})

intersect is exponentially small in

. We conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}_{2}\left[f^{n}_{\omega}\text{ and }f^{n}_{\omega^{\prime}}\text{ are not in $\varepsilon$-transverse position}\,\right]<0.\qed

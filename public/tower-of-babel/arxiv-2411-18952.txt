On a conjecture of Zhao related to standard relations among cyclotomic multiple zeta values
Henrik Bachmann, Khalef Yaddaden

Abstract
. We provide a proof of a conjecture by Zhao concerning the structure of certain relations among cyclotomic multiple zeta values in weight two. We formulate this conjecture in a broader algebraic setting in which we give a natural equivalence between two schemes attached to a finite abelian group G G . In particular, when G G is the group of roots of unity, these schemes describe the standard relations among cyclotomic multiple zeta values.

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to give a proof of a conjecture of Zhao and to compare two distinct frameworks describing the algebraic relations among multiple zeta values and more generally multiple polylogarithms at roots of unity.

Multiple polylogarithms
generalize the classical polylogarithms and are defined for
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
by

(1)

\operatorname{Li}_{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

which converge for suitable values of
z_{i}
. In (
1
) we call
r
the
depth
and
k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}
the
weight
.
The special case where
k_{1}\geq 2
and
z_{1}=\dots=z_{r}=1
, yields
multiple zeta values (MZVs)

\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})=\operatorname{Li}_{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(1,\dots,1)
. From the perspective of Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier
[
IKZ06
]
, conjecturally all algebraic relations of MZVs are a consequence of the extended double shuffle (EDS) relations. Another special case for the values of
z_{i}
in (
1
) is when they are
N
-th roots of unity. The resulting values are sometimes referred to as colored or
cyclotomic multiple zeta values
of level
N
. Besides the extended double shuffle relations these values also satisfy the so-called finite and regularized distribution relations. The main result of this work is the following.

Theorem I

(
[
Zha10
, Conjecture 4.7]
)
.

In weight two, all regularized distribution relations are consequences of the extended double shuffle relations, finite distribution relations of weight one and finite distribution relations of depth two.

We will prove a more general variant of Theorem
I
, which provides a similar statement regarding a generalization of the distribution and double shuffle relations. This generalization will be presented within two distinct algebraic frameworks. Theorem
I
will follow as a consequence of Theorem
2.10
and Proposition
2.11
, established in this broader context. One framework extends the algebraic setup of Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier (
[
IKZ06
]
), while the other was introduced by Racinet (
[
Rac02
]
). In the following, we will outline these two frameworks and compare them.

Following Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier (
[
IKZ06
]
), the double shuffle relations can be viewed as a comparison between two product formulas satisfied by MZVs. To describe them, one introduces a space
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
equipped with two products:
\sh
(shuffle product) and
\ast
(harmonic product). MZVs are then seen as an algebra homomorphism from
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
to
\mathbb{R}
with respect to both products. By extending this map in two natural ways, through regularization, to two algebra homomorphisms from a larger algebra
\mathfrak{H}^{0}\subset\mathfrak{H}^{1}
to
\mathbb{R}[T]
, along with an explicit comparison map
\rho:\mathbb{R}[T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}[T]
between both regularizations, the extended double shuffle relations are established. In the context of cyclotomic multiple zeta values, one can similarly describe the algebraic relations satisfied by these values (e.g.
[
AK04
,
Tas21
,
YZ16
]
) by considering them as algebra homomorphisms of an extension of
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
.

In this paper, we generalize this algebraic framework by introducing a space
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}
associated with a finite abelian group
G
. We then provide a definition of what it means for a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
Z_{\mathbf{A}}
from
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}
to a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}
to satisfy the extended double shuffle relations. In the cases where
G
is trivial or where
G
is the group of
N
-th roots of unity, this notion reduces to the classical cases of multiple zeta values and their level
N
analogues, respectively. For a given group
G
and
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}
we then introduce the set
\mathsf{EDS}(G)(A)
of all such maps.

A somehow dual point of view of the above story is Racinet’s (
[
Rac02
]
) approach of the study of MZVs through the lens of groupoid-enriched Hopf algebras, emphasizing the role of group actions and the double shuffle relations in this context. In his framework, one considers formal non-commutative power series with coefficients in
\mathbf{A}
, which are, after a certain correction, group-like for two different coproducts. Denote by
\mathsf{DMR}(G)(\mathbf{A})
(Definition
1.14
) the set of all such series. This formalism builds upon the notion of Drinfeld associator introduced in
[
Dri91
]
which is a generating series of MZVs that satisfies associator relations describing periods of mixed Tate motives. It is shown in
[
Fur11
]
that associator relations imply double shuffle relations and it is conjectured to be equivalent. A cyclotomic analogue of Drinfeld associator called cyclotomic associator is studied by
[
Enr08
]
and its connection with double shuffle relations was established in
[
Fur13
]
.

Both the setup introduced by Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier and the one introduced by Racinet have been used by various authors to describe the relations among multiple polylogarithms or (cyclotomic) multiple zeta values. Their equivalence is in some sense common knowledge among the community but, as far as the authors know, it was not written down precisely somewhere so far, except for the classical case of trivial
G
(cf.
[
ENR03
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
Bur23
]
).
In this work, we give an explicit comparison for arbitrary
G
by assigning to a
Z_{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathsf{EDS}(G)(\mathbf{A})
an element
\Phi_{Z_{\mathbf{A}}\circ\overline{\mathrm{reg}}_{\sh}}\in\mathsf{DMR}(G)(\mathbf{A})
(Definition
1.16
), such that we have the following

Theorem II

(Theorem
1.18
)
.

The following map is bijective

(2)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathsf{EDS}(G)(\mathbf{A})&\longrightarrow&\mathsf{DMR}(G)(\mathbf{A}),\\
Z_{\mathbf{A}}&\longmapsto&\Phi_{Z_{\mathbf{A}}\circ\overline{\mathrm{reg}}_{\sh}}.\end{array}

Racinet’s viewpoint introduces a more general setup then the one of Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier, accommodating also the so-called distribution relations. For multiple polylogarithms these were explicited by Goncharov
[
Gon98
,
Gon01
]
.

Distribution relations are not a consequence of the extended double shuffle relations and they are trivial in the classical case if
G
is trivial. Both are part of the so-called
standard relations
among the cyclotomic multiple zeta values (
[
Zha10
]
) in the case if
G
is the group of roots of unity.
The distribution relations were incorporated by Racinet by introducing a subset
\mathsf{DMRD}(G)(\mathbf{A})\subset\mathsf{DMR}(G)(\mathbf{A})
, which gives the subset of elements that additionally satisfy these relations. As a counterpart to this, we introduce the subset
\mathsf{EDSD}(G)(\mathbf{A})\subset\mathsf{EDS}(G)(\mathbf{A})
(Definition
2.8
) and show the following.

Theorem III

(Theorem

2.9
)
.

The bijection (
2
) restricts to a bijection

\mathsf{EDSD}(G)(\mathbf{A})\longrightarrow\mathsf{DMRD}(G)(\mathbf{A}).

While the result of Theorem
II
may be regarded as a well-known fact among experts, it has not yet been explicitly documented in the literature in the general form presented here. Nevertheless, we emphasize that the result in Theorem
III
is novel, as the concept of the space
\mathsf{EDSD}(G)(\mathbf{A})
is entirely new and has not been discussed or introduced elsewhere.

(iii)

\mathcal{L}

1.
Double shuffle relations

In this section, we review two fundamental formalisms used in the algebraic setup of double shuffle relations. We recall Racinet’s formalism as presented in
[
Rac02
]
encoding double shuffle relations into a scheme
\mathsf{DMR}(G)
attached to a group
G
. Another formalism was developed by Ihara, Kaneko and Zagier
[
IKZ06
]
for trivial
G
and Arakawa, Kaneko
[
AK04
]
for general
G
. Following Racinet’s approach, we encode this formalism into a scheme denoted as
\mathsf{EDS}(G)
. The section concludes by proving that the schemes
\mathsf{DMR}(G)
and
\mathsf{EDS}(G)
are isomorphic, thus establishing formally the equivalence between the two formalisms.

1.1.
The scheme
\mathsf{EDS}
of extended double shuffle relations

Denote by
\mathfrak{H}_{G}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}\langle X_{G}\rangle
the free non-commutative associative polynomial
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra with unit over the alphabet
X_{G}
. Set

\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}+\sum_{g\in G}\mathfrak{H}_{G}x_{g}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}+\sum_{g\in G}x_{0}\mathfrak{H}_{G}x_{g}+\sum_{g\in G\neq\{1\}}x_{g}\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}.

One checks that we have the following inclusion of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}\subset\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}\subset\mathfrak{H}_{G}.

For
(n,g)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times G
, the assignment
y_{n,g}\mapsto x_{0}^{n-1}x_{g}
establishes a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism
\mathbb{Q}\langle Y_{G}\rangle\simeq\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}
. We shall identify these two algebras thanks to this isomorphism.

Definition 1.1
.

We define the
shuffle product

\sh
on
\mathfrak{H}_{G}
inductively by

\begin{cases}1\ \sh\ w=w\ \sh\ 1=w&\text{for }w\text{ a word in }\mathfrak{H}_{G}\\
uw_{1}\ \sh\ vw_{2}=u(w_{1}\ \sh\ vw_{2})+v(uw_{1}\ \sh\ w_{2})&\text{for }w_{1},w_{2}\text{ words in }\mathfrak{H}_{G}\text{ and }u,v\in X_{G},\end{cases}

and then extending by
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinearity.

The product
\sh
gives
\mathfrak{H}_{G}
the structure of a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, which we denote by
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\sh}
. The subspaces
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}
and
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}
then become subalgebras of
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\sh}
denoted
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\sh}^{1}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\sh}^{0}
respectively.

Definition 1.2
.

We define the
harmonic product

\ast
on
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}
inductively by

\begin{cases}1\ast w=w\ast 1=w\\
y_{n_{1},g_{1}}w_{1}\ast y_{n_{2},g_{2}}w_{2}=y_{n_{1},g_{1}}(w_{1}\ast y_{n_{2},g_{2}}w_{2})+y_{n_{2},g_{2}}(y_{n_{1},g_{1}}w_{1}\ast w_{2})+y_{n_{1}+n_{2},\ g_{1}g_{2}}(w_{1}\ast w_{2}),\end{cases}

for words
w,w_{1},w_{2}
in
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}
,
(n_{1},g_{1}),(n_{2},g_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times G
; and then extending by
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinearity.

One checks that
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\ast}^{1}\coloneqq\left(\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G},\ast\right)
is a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and that
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\ast}^{0}\coloneqq\left(\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G},\ast\right)
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{H}_{G,\ast}^{1}
.

Let
\operatorname{q}_{G}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear automorphism of
\mathfrak{H}_{G}
given by (
[
Rac02
, §2.2.7]
)

\mbox{\small$\operatorname{q}_{G}(x_{0}^{n_{1}-1}x_{g_{1}}x_{0}^{n_{2}-1}x_{g_{2}}\cdots x_{0}^{n_{r}-1}x_{g_{r}}x_{0}^{n_{r+1}-1})=x_{0}^{n_{1}-1}x_{g_{1}}x_{0}^{n_{2}-1}x_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}}\cdots x_{0}^{n_{r}-1}x_{g_{r}g_{r-1}^{-1}}x_{0}^{n_{r+1}-1}$},

for
r,n_{1},\dots,n_{r+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in G
. Its reciprocal is given by

\mbox{\small$x_{0}^{n_{1}-1}x_{g_{1}}x_{0}^{n_{2}-1}x_{g_{2}}\cdots x_{0}^{n_{r}-1}x_{g_{r}}x_{0}^{n_{r+1}-1}\mapsto x_{0}^{n_{1}-1}x_{g_{1}}x_{0}^{n_{2}-1}x_{g_{1}g_{2}}\cdots x_{0}^{n_{r}-1}x_{g_{1}\cdots g_{r}}x_{0}^{n_{r+1}-1}$}.

One checks that
\operatorname{q}_{G}
restricts to a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear automorphism of
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{G}
which we denote
\operatorname{q}_{G}
as well and is given by

\operatorname{q}_{G}(y_{n_{1},g_{1}}y_{n_{2},g_{2}}\cdots y_{n_{r},g_{r}})=y_{n_{1},g_{1}}y_{n_{2},g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}}\cdots y_{n_{r},g_{r}g_{r-1}^{-1}}.

Moreover,
\operatorname{q}_{G}
also restricts to an
\mathbf{A}
-module automorphism of
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{G}
also denoted
\operatorname{q}_{G}
.

Definition 1.3
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
Z_{\mathbf{A}}:\mathfrak{H}^{0}\to\mathbf{A}
is said to have the
finite double shuffle
property if it is an algebra homomorphism for
\sh
and if
Z_{\mathbf{A}}\circ\operatorname{q}_{G}^{-1}
is an algebra homomorphism for
\ast
. i.e.

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbf{A}}(w_{1})Z_{\mathbf{A}}(w_{2})

\displaystyle=Z_{\mathbf{A}}(w_{1}\ \sh\ w_{2}),

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbf{A}}(\operatorname{q}_{G}^{-1}(w_{1}))Z_{\mathbf{A}}(\operatorname{q}_{G}^{-1}(w_{2}))

\displaystyle=Z_{\mathbf{A}}(\operatorname{q}_{G}^{-1}(w_{1}\ast w_{2})),

for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathfrak{H}^{0}
.

Example 1.4
.

In the classical case (
[

]

[

\Omega_{x_{z}}=\frac{dt}{z^{-1}-t}

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\omega_{1}=\int_{a}^{b}f_{1}(t)dt&\\
\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}=\int_{a}^{b}\left(\int_{a}^{t}\omega_{2}\cdots\omega_{n}\right)f_{1}(t)dt&\text{if }n>1,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{Li}_{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Omega_{x_{0}}^{k_{1}-1}\Omega_{x_{z_{1}}}\Omega_{x_{0}}^{k_{2}-1}\Omega_{x_{z_{1}z_{2}}}\cdots\Omega_{x_{0}}^{k_{r}-1}\Omega_{x_{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}}}

(3)

\displaystyle=Z_{\mathbb{C}}\circ\operatorname{q}_{\mu_{N}}^{-1}(x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{z_{1}}x_{0}^{k_{2}-1}x_{z_{2}}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{r}-1}x_{z_{r}}).

Therefore, for instance, the harmonic product

y_{1,z_{1}}\ast y_{1,z_{2}}=y_{1,z_{1}}y_{1,z_{2}}+y_{1,z_{2}}y_{1,z_{1}}+y_{2,z_{1}z_{2}}

Finite double shuffle relations is then expressed by the identity

\operatorname{Li}_{(1,1)}(z_{1},z_{2})+\operatorname{Li}_{(1,1)}(z_{2},z_{1})+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z_{1}z_{2})=\operatorname{Li}_{(1,1)}(z_{1},z_{1}^{-1}z_{2})+\operatorname{Li}_{(1,1)}(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2}^{-1}).

1.2.
The scheme
\mathsf{DMR}
of double shuffle and regularization relations

Let
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle
be the free non-commutative associative series
\mathbf{A}
-algebra with unit over the alphabet
X_{G}
. It is the completion of the graded
\mathbf{A}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}\langle X_{G}\rangle\simeq\mathfrak{H}_{G}\otimes\mathbf{A}
where elements of
X_{G}
has degree
1
.
One equips the algebra
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle
with the Hopf algebra coproduct
\widehat{\Delta}_{G,\sh}:\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle\to{\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle}^{\hat{\otimes}2}
, called the
shuffle coproduct
, which is the unique
\mathbf{A}
-algebra homomorphism given by
\widehat{\Delta}_{G,\sh}(x_{g})=x_{g}\otimes 1+1\otimes x_{g}
, for any
g\in G\sqcup\{0\}
(
[
Rac02
, §2.2.3]
).
Let then
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle)

Appendix A
Quasi-shuffle products

We will consider now a more general point of view for all the products considered before. For this, suppose that we have a set
\mathcal{L}
, called the set of
letters
, and a commutative and associative product
\diamond
in
\mathcal{L}
. Extending this product bi-linearly to
\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{L}
gives a commutative non-unital
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\mathcal{L},\diamond)
. We will be interested in
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of multiple letters of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., in elements of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle
. Here and in the following we call monic monomials in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle

words
. Moreover, we call the degree of this monomial the
length
of the word.

Definition A.1
.

Define the
quasi-shuffle product

\ast_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle
as the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear product which satisfies
1\ast_{\diamond}w=w\ast_{\diamond}1=w
for any word
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle
and

\displaystyle aw\ast_{\diamond}bv=a(w\ast_{\diamond}bv)+b(aw\ast_{\diamond}v)+(a\diamond b)(w\ast_{\diamond}v)

for any letters
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
and words
w,v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle
.

This gives a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
as shown in
[
HI17
]
. Moreover, one can equip this algebra with the structure of a Hopf algebra
[
HI17
, Section 3]
, where the coproduct is given for
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle
by the deconcatenation coproduct

\displaystyle\Delta(w)=\sum_{uv=w}u\otimes v\,.

Now we consider the completed dual Hopf algebra of
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond},\Delta)
. For this, we need to give a restriction on
\diamond
, that is, we assume that any
a\in\mathcal{L}
just appears in
b\diamond c
for finitely many
b,c\in\mathcal{L}
. More precisely, define for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
the coefficients
\lambda^{a}_{b,c}\in\mathbb{Q}
by

b\diamond c=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{L}}\lambda^{a}_{b,c}a.

In the following, we will assume that for any
a\in\mathcal{L}
there are just finitely many
b,c\in\mathcal{L}
with
\lambda^{a}_{b,c}\neq 0
.

As a consequence of Lemma
A.3
below, the dual completed Hopf algebra of
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond},\Delta)
is then given by
(\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}})
, where
\operatorname{conc}
is the concatenation product and
\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}:\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle\to\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle^{\hat{\otimes}2}
is the unique
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism such that

\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(a)=a\otimes 1+1\otimes a+\sum_{b,c\in\mathcal{L}}\lambda^{a}_{b,c}\,\,b\otimes c

for any
a\in\mathcal{L}
. Since we just consider
\diamond
such that for any
a\in\mathcal{L}
the coefficients
\lambda^{a}_{b,c}
are zero for almost all
b,c\in\mathcal{L}
and therefore
\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}
is well-defined.

Definition A.2
.

Define the pairing

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\psi\otimes w

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\psi\mid w),

where, as before,
(\psi\mid w)
denotes the coefficient of
w
in
\psi
.

Lemma A.3
.

For any
\Phi\in\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(\Phi)=\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}(\Phi\mid u\ast_{\diamond}v)u\otimes v.

Proof.

Notice that the statement is equivalent to
(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(\Phi)\mid u\otimes v)=(\Phi\mid u\ast_{\diamond}v)
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
. Due to linearity, it suffices to show

(28)

\displaystyle(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w)\mid u\otimes v)=(w\mid u\ast_{\diamond}v)

for words
w,u,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
. We will perform induction on the length of
w
. In the length case
1
, that is, when
w=a\in\mathcal{L}
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(a)=a\otimes 1+1\otimes a+\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{L}}\lambda^{a}_{a_{1},a_{2}}\,\,a_{1}\otimes a_{2}.

By the definition of
u\ast_{\diamond}v
we see that
u=a,v=1
or
u=1,v=a
or
u=a_{1},v=a_{2}
(with multiplicity
\lambda^{a}_{a_{1},a_{2}}
) are exactly cases where
a
can appear as a coefficient of
u\ast_{\diamond}v
and therefore
(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(a)\mid u\otimes v)=(a\mid u\ast_{\diamond}v)
.

Next, notice that (
28
) is trivial for any
w
when
u
or
v
is the empty word. Therefore, we can restrict ourselves to the case
w=aw^{\prime}
,
u=bu^{\prime}
and
v=cv^{\prime}
, for letters
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
and words
w^{\prime},u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
. Then we want to show that

(29)

\displaystyle(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(aw^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})=(aw^{\prime}\mid bu^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}cv^{\prime}).

For the left-hand side of (
29
) we have

\displaystyle(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(aw^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})=\,\,

\displaystyle(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(a)\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\,\,

\displaystyle((a\otimes 1)\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})+((1\otimes a)\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime}v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{L}}\lambda^{a}_{a_{1},a_{2}}\,\,((a_{1}\otimes a_{2})\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\,\,

\displaystyle\delta_{a,b}(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})+\delta_{a,c}(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{L}}\lambda^{a}_{a_{1},a_{2}}\delta_{a_{1},b}\delta_{a_{2},c}\,(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\,\,

\displaystyle\delta_{a,b}(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\otimes cv^{\prime})+\delta_{a,c}(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid bu^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\lambda^{a}_{b,c}\,(\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(w^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime}).

Here,
\delta_{a,b}
denotes the Kronecker delta. Using the definition of
bu^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}cv^{\prime}
, the right-hand side of (
29
) is given by

\displaystyle(aw^{\prime}\mid bu^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}cv^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(aw^{\prime}\mid b(u^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}cv^{\prime}))+(aw^{\prime}\mid c(bu^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}v^{\prime}))+(aw^{\prime}\mid(b\diamond c)(u^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}v^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\delta_{a,b}(w^{\prime}\mid u^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}cv^{\prime})+\delta_{a,c}(w^{\prime}\mid bu^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}v^{\prime})+\lambda^{a}_{b,c}(w^{\prime}\mid u^{\prime}\ast_{\diamond}v^{\prime}).

Equation (
29
) then follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

As before, denote the set grouplike elements of
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle
for the coproduct
\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}
by

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle)=\{\Phi\in\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle^{\times}\,|\,\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(\Phi)=\Phi\otimes\Phi\}.

Given a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rightarrow\mathbf{A}
we define the following element in
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varphi}\coloneqq\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}\varphi(w)w\,.

Proposition A.4
.

The following two statements are equivalent

(a)

The map
\varphi:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})\rightarrow\mathbf{A}
is an
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism.

(b)

We have
\Phi_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{A}\langle\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle\rangle)
.

Proof.

First notice that Lemma
A.3
gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(\Phi_{\varphi})=\sum_{w,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}\underbrace{(\Phi_{\varphi}|w\ast_{\diamond}v)}_{=\,\varphi(w\ast_{\diamond}v)}w\otimes v.

By direct calculation we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varphi}\otimes\Phi_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}\varphi(w)w\right)\otimes\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}\varphi(v)v\right)=\sum_{w,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}}\varphi(w)\varphi(v)\,w\otimes v.

Therefore,
\widehat{\Delta}_{\ast_{\diamond}}(\Phi_{\varphi})=\Phi_{\varphi}\otimes\Phi_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi(w\ast_{\diamond}v)=\varphi(w)\varphi(v)
for all
w,v\in\mathcal{L}^{*}
.
∎

(-)^{\sharp}

Throughout this appendix we will denote by
\mathsf{FinAb}

\mathbf{A}{\text{-}}\mathsf{Alg}

\mathbf{A}
-algebras.

Proposition-Definition B.1

(
[
Rac02
, §2.5.3]
)
.

(a)

Define the mapping
(-)^{\ast}:\mathsf{FinAb}\to\mathbf{A}{\text{-}}\mathsf{Alg}
as follows:

(i)

To each group
G
, associate the algebra
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle
;

(ii)

To each group homomorphism
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
, associate the algebra homomorphism
\phi^{\ast}:\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G_{2}}\rangle\rangle\to\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G_{1}}\rangle\rangle
given by

x_{0}\mapsto x_{0};\qquad x_{h}\mapsto\sum_{g\in\phi^{-1}(\{h\})}x_{g}\quad(h\in G_{2}).

Then the mapping
(-)^{\ast}
is a contravariant functor.

(b)

Define the mapping
(-)_{\ast}:\mathsf{FinAb}\to\mathbf{A}{\text{-}}\mathsf{Alg}
as follows:

(i)

To each group
G
, associate the algebra
\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G}\rangle\rangle
;

(ii)

To each group homomorphism
\phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
, associate the algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\ast}:\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G_{1}}\rangle\rangle\to\mathbf{A}\langle\langle X_{G_{2}}\rangle\rangle
given by

x_{0}\mapsto d\,x_{0}\quad(d=|\ker(\phi)|);\qquad x_{g}\mapsto x_{\phi(g)}\quad(g\in G_{1}).

Then the mapping
(-)_{\ast}
is a covariant functor.

Proof.

(a)

This can be established through direct verification, utilizing the contravariance of the power set functor
P^{\ast}
. Recall that
P^{\ast}
maps each set
E
to its power set
\mathcal{P}(E)
and each map
f:E\to F
to the map
P^{\ast}(f):\mathcal{P}(F)\to\mathcal{P}(E)
defined by
P^{\ast}(f)(U)=f^{-1}(U)
for
U\subseteq F
.

(b)

This can be established through direct verification, utilizing the covariance of the power set functor
P_{\ast}
. Recall that
P_{\ast}
maps each set
E
to its power set
\mathcal{P}(E)
and each map
f:E\to F
to the map
P_{\ast}(f):\mathcal{P}(E)\to\mathcal{P}(F)
defined by
P_{\ast}(f)(S)=f(S)
for

.

∎

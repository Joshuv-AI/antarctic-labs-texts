Student Perceptions of Concept Mapping as a Teaching Tool in Ayurvedic Education
Authors: Shweta Parwe, Milind Nisargandha, Vaishali Kuchewar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:A concept map is a visual representation of knowledge that illustrates the relationships and propositions among various concepts.Aim and Objective:This study aimed to determine the effect of teaching and assess the perceptions of Ayurvedic undergraduate students through concept mapping.Method and Material:In this study, 48 students were included and divided into two groups. The topic for intervention was the mechanisms of Vaman (induced vomiting). Group A was taught through a didactic lecture, while Group B was taught using concept mapping, followed by a crossover of the groups. Students were provided with a structured and revalidated feedback form, and their perceptions were assessed using a five-point Likert scale.Results:Analysis of the feedback form indicated that students expressed greater satisfaction with the concept mapping teaching approach, attributing it to enhanced comprehension of the subject matter.Conclusion:Concept mapping can be an effective teaching tool for teaching complex concepts.

INTRODUCTION

Concept maps are images or visual aids that assist students in connecting, distinguishing, and relating ideas to one another. Developed by Novak and Gowin, the concept map is based on Ausubel’s theory of meaningful learning assimilation.[1] Concept maps are images or other graphical representations that help students to relate, distinguish, and link ideas.[2] Often referred to as “knowledge maps,” these node-link-node diagrams use spatial arrangements to represent knowledge items.[3] A key characteristic of a concept map is its hierarchical structure, which emphasizes certain concepts, typically represented by circles or boxes. Concept maps are now supported by computer systems as visual aids for cognitive processes.[4] These technologies serve as effective tools for idea organization and are a compelling note-taking technique that aids in memory recall.[5] In light of this, this study aimed to implement the novel T-L technique of concept mapping in Ayurvedic undergraduate education to evaluate improvements in students’ perceptions regarding comprehension, memory retention, and trust in using CM. Certain topics within the Panchakarma curriculum, specifically the mode of action of Panchakarma procedures, can be particularly challenging to understand. Therefore, this research focusses on the use of concept maps to facilitate easier understanding and visualization of these complex concepts.

METHODS AND MATERIAL

This study was a prospective comparative educational research project conducted through the Department of Panchakarma at MGAC and RC, Wardha, with ethical approval (letter no. DMIMS (DU)/IEC/2015-16/1405). A total of 48 final-year BAMS students participated in the study. Two groups were formed: Group A, the intervention group, and Group B, the conventional group. The topic entitled “Mode of action of Vaman karma” (induced vomiting) was taught to the conventional group (Group B) through a didactic lecture with the help of a PowerPoint presentation. The concept map was introduced to both faculty and students at the beginning of the study. Group A was taught using the new method of concept mapping. After the teaching sessions, feedback was collected from the students, and the groups were crossed over. Students’ and teachers’ responses were recorded using a five-point Likert scale, and the data were analyzed.

Out of 48 students, 21 (43.75%) students strongly agreed, and 20 (41.66%) students agreed that concept mapping was a suitable teaching method for understanding complex topics. Conversely, 1 student (2.08%) disagreed, and 6 students (12.05%) did not respond.

Nineteen students (39.58%) strongly agreed that keeping problematic concepts in mind is helpful, while 24 students (50%) agreed. Additionally, 1 student (2.08%) strongly disagreed, and 4 students (8.33%) remained neutral.

Regarding the statement “It is beneficial in clarifying the concepts,” 17 students (35.41%) strongly agreed, 26 students (54.16%) agreed, and 3 students (6.25%) remained neutral. Additionally, 1 student (2.08%) strongly disagreed, and 1 student (2.08%) disagreed with this statement.

A total of 21 students (43.75%) considered concept mapping a good learning experience, while 20 students (41.66%) agreed with this statement. Seven students (14.58%) remained neutral.

DISCUSSION

CM facilitates a logical flow of information, enhancing subject comprehension. Improved performance and confidence are directly linked to a deeper understanding of material. In this study, students found concept mapping beneficial for memorization, retention, and ultimately, better exam scores.[6] Research by Novak, referenced by Kinchin, indicates that idea mapping is an effective teaching strategy that can improve scientific learning., Harpaz et al. also explored the application of idea mapping as an advanced learning technique in nursing education; their findings suggest that idea mapping could be a valuable teaching strategy for nursing programs.[7] Concept mapping has shown clear benefits, including deepened learning, a more stimulating learning environment, increased interaction, and improved knowledge retention.[8] The significantly enhanced learning outcomes associated with this recent teaching approach suggest that it promotes a better and more comprehensive understanding of the subject than conventional methods.[9] Surapaneni et al. discussed the challenges faced by undergraduate medical students due to excessively didactic teaching formats and testing procedures, highlighting the need for alternative T-L techniques to help medical students learn, retain, and replicate large volumes of medical material. CM facilitates a logical flow of retained material for improved subject comprehension. Performance and confidence are directly correlated with a more profound comprehension. In this study, students found CM beneficial for memorization, retention, and exam scores in future.

CONCLUSION

The study concluded that CM provides an opportunity for Ayurvedic students to learn, analyze, and understand essential concepts more quickly, facilitating the synthesis of relevant information.

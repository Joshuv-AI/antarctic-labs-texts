Prognostic Impact of Residual Thrombus After Aspiration Thrombectomy in ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction
Authors: Tomohide Endo, Takumi Higuma, Hiroaki Yokoyama, Takashi Yokota, Hirofumi Tomita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prognostic significance of residual thrombus volume after aspiration thrombectomy in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) remains unclear.ObjectivesThe authors aimed to investigate whether residual thrombus volume quantified by post-aspiration optical coherence tomography (OCT) is associated with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients with STEMI undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).MethodsThis retrospective single-center study included 233 patients with STEMI who underwent manual aspiration thrombectomy followed by OCT-guided primary PCI within 12 hours of symptom onset. Residual thrombus volume was quantified at 1.0-mm intervals. The optimal cutoff was determined using maximally selected log-rank statistics. The primary endpoint was MACE, defined as cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for heart failure. Associations with MACE were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression analyses.ResultsDuring a median follow-up of 2.5 years (IQR: 1.3-3.5 years), MACE occurred in 46 patients (19.7%). Patients with residual thrombus volume ≥4.14 mm3 had a higher cumulative incidence of MACE than those with <4.14 mm3 (log-rank P = 0.0018). Residual thrombus volume remained independently associated with MACE as a continuous variable (adjusted HR: 1.014 per 1 mm3 increase; 95% CI: 1.001-1.027; P = 0.031). Similar findings were observed using the 4.14-mm3 cutoff (adjusted HR: 2.95; 95% CI: 1.51-5.74; P = 0.002).ConclusionsResidual thrombus burden quantified by OCT after aspiration thrombectomy was independently associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with STEMI. Quantitative OCT assessment may provide complementary prognostic information for post-PCI risk stratification.Key words: aspiration thrombectomy, intracoronary imaging, major adverse cardiovascular events, optical coherence tomography, primary percutaneous coronary intervention, risk stratification

Aspiration thrombectomy has been widely used during primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) to restore coronary blood flow by removing intracoronary thrombus. However, large and randomized trials, most notably TOTAL (Trial of Routine Aspiration Thrombectomy with PCI versus PCI Alone in Patients with STEMI), failed to demonstrate a consistent clinical benefit of routine thrombectomy, resulting in a shift toward a selective use of this strategy in patients with a high thrombus burden.1 Despite this change in practice, thrombus burden remains a key determinant of procedural complexity and clinical outcomes in patients with STEMI. Emerging evidence suggests that residual thrombus after aspiration thrombectomy may adversely affect clinical outcomes, particularly in patients with extensive thrombotic burden.2,3 However, objective and quantitative evaluation of residual thrombus burden has been limited in previous studies.

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) provides high-resolution intracoronary imaging and enables precise quantification of thrombus volume.4,5 Although OCT has been extensively applied to characterize plaque morphology and optimize stent deployment, the prognostic impact of residual thrombus burden after aspiration thrombectomy has not been clearly established. Persistent residual thrombus following aspiration may reflect unfavorable lesion characteristics or increased thrombogenicity and may contribute to subsequent adverse events. Therefore, quantitative assessment of residual thrombus burden using OCT may provide complementary prognostic information beyond conventional angiographic assessment.

The purpose of the present study was to determine whether residual thrombus burden after aspiration thrombectomy evaluated by OCT predicts clinical outcomes in patients with STEMI.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective, single-center observational study was conducted at Hirosaki University Hospital. Consecutive patients with STEMI who underwent manual aspiration thrombectomy followed by successful OCT-guided primary PCI within 12 hours of symptom onset between January 2013 and December 2016 were included. A total of 251 patients were initially screened. Eighteen patients treated with a distal protection device were excluded to avoid interference with thrombus assessment, leaving 233 patients for the final analysis (Figure 1).

Study Flowchart

Flow diagram illustrating patient selection. OCT = optical coherence tomography; PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention; STEMI = ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction.

STEMI was diagnosed as per the universal definition.6 STEMI was defined as typical chest pain lasting >20 minutes with new ST-segment elevation ≥0.2 mV in ≥2 contiguous precordial leads or ≥0.1 mV in ≥2 contiguous limb leads, or new left bundle branch block, accompanied by elevation of cardiac biomarkers.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration's ethical guidelines for medical research on humans. The research protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Hirosaki University Graduate School of Medicine (Institutional Review Board number: 2020-070-3). Baseline patient data were collected from our institution's medical records and database. Clinical events, including all-cause death, and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) were ascertained from medical records or through contact with primary care physicians, patients, or family members.

Quantitative coronary angiography

Coronary angiography was performed before intervention, after aspiration thrombectomy, and at the end of the PCI procedure. Quantitative coronary angiography was conducted offline using a validated analysis system according to the standard methodology to measure reference vessel diameter, minimal lumen diameter, diameter stenosis, and lesion length. Coronary flow was assessed angiographically using the TIMI flow grade, and myocardial perfusion was evaluated using the myocardial blush grade. Slow-flow/no-reflow was defined angiographically as a final TIMI flow grade ≤2 after stent implantation.

OCT image acquisition

OCT imaging was performed using frequency-domain OCT systems (C7-XR or ILUMIEN OPTIS; Abbott Vascular) with a Dragonfly imaging catheter (Abbott Vascular). After aspiration thrombectomy, the OCT catheter was advanced beyond the culprit lesion, and automated pullback was performed during injection of contrast media to displace blood from the imaging field. Predilation with balloon angioplasty was not permitted before OCT imaging. Manual aspiration thrombectomy was repeated at the operator's discretion when imaging quality was suboptimal due to residual thrombus.

Analyses of OCT images

OCT images were anonymized and analyzed offline by 2 independent investigators blinded to clinical and angiographic data. Plaque morphology was classified as plaque rupture, plaque erosion, calcified nodule, or other types according to established criteria.7 Thin-cap fibroatheroma was defined as a lipid-rich plaque with a maximal lipid arc >90° and a minimum fibrous cap thickness <65 μm. Interobserver reproducibility for residual thrombus volume measurement was good, with an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.88.

Thrombus definition and quantification

Intracoronary thrombus was defined as an irregular mass protruding into the lumen or attached to the vessel wall with a minimum dimension ≥250 μm. Thrombus was classified as erythrocyte-rich (red) or platelet-rich (white) based on OCT signal characteristics. Thrombus area was manually traced in each OCT frame at 1.0-mm intervals using the “Area-Multiple Points” tool of the proprietary analysis software. Thrombus volume was calculated by multiplying the thrombus area in each frame by the interframe distance and summing across all frames covering the analyzable segment, including the culprit lesion. Lumen area was measured using planimetry in each frame, and lumen volume was calculated similarly. Thrombus burden was calculated as thrombus volume divided by lumen volume ×100. Study endpoints

The primary clinical endpoint of this study was MACE defined as a composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, and hospitalization for heart failure. Follow-up time was calculated from the date of the index primary PCI to the first occurrence of a primary endpoint or the last confirmed clinical follow-up for censored patients. The median follow-up period of this study was 2.5 (1.3-3.5) years.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD or median (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (n/N) and percentages. Continuous variables were compared using the Student's t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate, and categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test.

The optimal cutoff value of residual thrombus volume for predicting MACE was determined using maximally selected log-rank statistics. Bootstrap internal validation using 1,000 resamples was performed to assess the stability of both the derived cutoff and the multivariable Cox model, including the adjusted HR for residual thrombus volume. Kaplan-Meier curves were constructed to estimate cumulative event rates and compared using the log-rank test. Ninety-five percent CIs were calculated for Kaplan-Meier estimates.

Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to evaluate the association between residual thrombus volume and MACE. Multivariable models were constructed using prespecified clinically relevant covariates, including age, sex, Killip class ≥2, diabetes mellitus, left ventricular ejection fraction, estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min/1.73 m2, symptom onset to reperfusion time, and left anterior descending artery lesion location. Residual thrombus volume was analyzed both as a continuous variable and as a categorical variable using the cutoff derived from maximally selected log-rank statistics. The association between residual thrombus volume and MACE was further evaluated using restricted cubic spline analysis to assess potential nonlinearity. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals and was confirmed.

To evaluate the incremental prognostic performance of residual thrombus volume, Harrell's C-index was compared between the prespecified clinical model and the model including residual thrombus volume, with bootstrap optimism correction.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp) and R software version 4.6.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). A 2-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 233 patients with STEMI were analyzed. The mean age was 66 ± 12 years, and 79% of the patients were men (Table 1). More than half of the patients had hypertension or dyslipidemia, and approximately half had diabetes mellitus. Regarding medications at admission, statin was prescribed to one-fifth of the study patients. The median onset to reperfusion time was 243 (176-367) minutes.

Cutoff value for predicting MACE

During follow-up, MACE occurred in 46 patients (19.7%). The optimal cutoff value of residual thrombus volume for predicting MACE, determined using maximally selected log-rank statistics, was 4.14 mm3.

Patient characteristics according to the 4.14-mm3 cutoff

Based on the thrombus volume cutoff value of 4.14 mm3, 73 patients were classified as the high thrombus burden group (≥4.14 mm3), 160 patients into the low residual thrombus group (<4.14 mm3) (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Patients in the high thrombus group were more frequently male and had a higher prevalence of smoking. The use of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers at admission was lower in the high thrombus group. Symptom onset to reperfusion time was significantly shorter in the high thrombus group.

Angiographic findings

Angiographic findings were compared between the 2 groups (Table 2). Although the left anterior descending artery was the most common culprit vessel in both groups, right coronary artery culprit lesions were less frequent in the high thrombus burden group than in the low thrombus burden group (5 of 73 [6.8%] vs 30 of 160 [18.8%], P < 0.05). Quantitative coronary angiography demonstrated a significantly smaller minimal lumen diameter and greater diameter stenosis in the high thrombus burden group compared with the low thrombus burden group (minimal lumen diameter: 0.11 ± 0.29 mm vs 0.33 ± 0.59 mm, P < 0.05; diameter stenosis: 96.2% ± 9.1% vs 89.5% ± 16.4%, P < 0.05). Reference vessel diameter was similar between 2 groups (3.02 ± 0.63 mm vs 2.90 ± 0.53 mm), and lesion length showed a nonsignificant trend toward being longer in the high thrombus burden group (15.7 ± 7.4 mm vs 12.7 ± 6.4 mm, P = 0.06). No significant differences were observed in the prevalence of multivessel disease or baseline TIMI flow grade between the groups.

Post-PCI angiographic outcomes

Myocardial blush grade ≤1 was more frequently observed in the high thrombus burden group than in the low thrombus burden group (21 of 73 [28.8%] vs 23 of 160 [14.4%], P = 0.023) (Table 2).

Clinical outcomes

MACE occurred significantly more frequently in the high thrombus group than in the low thrombus group (24 of 73 [32.9%] vs 22 of 160 [13.8%], P = 0.001) (Table 4). Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher cumulative incidence of MACE in the high thrombus group (log-rank P = 0.0018) (Figure 3).

Multivariate analysis

Residual thrombus volume analyzed as a continuous variable remained independently associated with MACE after adjustment for prespecified clinically relevant covariates (HR: 1.014 per 1 mm3 increase; 95% CI: 1.001-1.027; P = 0.031) (Table 5). Bootstrap internal validation supported the stability of this association, with a median adjusted HR of 1.015 per 1 mm3 increase (95% percentile interval, 1.004-1.056). Consistent results were observed when residual thrombus volume was analyzed as a categorical variable using the 4.14 mm3 cutoff (HR: 2.95; 95% CI: 1.51-5.74; P = 0.002) (Supplemental Table 1). Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated no significant evidence of nonlinearity between residual thrombus volume and the risk of MACE (P for nonlinearity = 0.126), supporting the use of residual thrombus volume as a continuous linear variable. The addition of residual thrombus volume to the prespecified clinical model resulted in only a modest increase in Harrell's C-index (0.728-0.731), with bootstrap optimism-corrected C-indices of 0.684 and 0.692, respectively.

Discussion

Main findings

The present study demonstrates that residual thrombus volume after aspiration thrombectomy, quantitatively assessed by OCT, was independently associated with subsequent clinical outcomes in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI. This association was observed when residual thrombus volume was analyzed as a continuous variable and was further supported by a supplementary cutoff-based analysis using a threshold of 4.14 mm3 derived from maximally selected log-rank statistics. Importantly, plaque morphology assessed by OCT did not differ between patients with high and low residual thrombus burden. Residual Thrombus Predicts Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events

Residual thrombus volume was quantified from post-aspiration OCT images at 1.0-mm intervals. Residual thrombus burden and clinical outcomes

Several pathophysiological mechanisms may explain the observed association between residual thrombus burden and adverse outcomes. A large amount of residual thrombus likely reflects a heightened thrombotic milieu, promoting distal embolization, microvascular obstruction, and impaired myocardial reperfusion despite successful epicardial recanalization.4,8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 In the present study, impaired myocardial perfusion, as assessed by myocardial blush grade, was more frequently observed in patients with high residual thrombus burden, supporting the link between thrombus persistence and microvascular dysfunction.

Furthermore, residual thrombus may represent ongoing inflammatory and prothrombotic activity at the culprit lesion, which could contribute to recurrent ischemic events and adverse remodeling. These findings highlight the prognostic importance of residual intracoronary thrombus beyond angiographic flow restoration.7,15,16

Context with prior thrombectomy trials

Our findings are consistent with, and extend, previous studies highlighting the limitations of aspiration thrombectomy in completely eliminating intracoronary thrombotic material. Early randomized trials, such as TAPAS (Thrombus Aspiration during Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in Acute Myocardial Infarction Study), suggested improved myocardial perfusion with aspiration thrombectomy17,18; however, subsequent large-scale randomized trials, including TASTE (Thrombus Aspiration in ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction in Scandinavia) and TOTAL, failed to demonstrate a clinical benefit of routine thrombectomy on hard clinical outcomes.1,19

Importantly, these landmark trials evaluated thrombectomy as a binary procedural strategy (performed vs not performed) and did not incorporate intravascular imaging to assess residual thrombus burden after the procedure. Consequently, the neutral results observed in these studies may, at least in part, reflect an oversimplified evaluation of thrombectomy that did not account for the actual effectiveness of thrombus removal.

In this context, our data suggest that residual thrombus burden—rather than the act of aspiration itself—may be a key determinant of prognosis in patients with STEMI. Importantly, the present findings should not be interpreted as support for routine aspiration thrombectomy. Rather, they underscore the prognostic relevance of residual thrombus burden assessed after selective thrombectomy, which is consistent with recent subgroup analyses, post hoc studies, and contemporary registry data focusing on patients with a high thrombus burden.20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25

Clinical implications of OCT-based thrombus quantification

From a clinical standpoint, OCT-based quantification of residual thrombus burden may provide complementary prognostic information for post-PCI risk stratification in patients with STEMI, particularly in those presenting with angiographically high thrombus burden. Although the improvement in discrimination was modest, residual thrombus volume remained independently associated with clinical outcomes after adjustment for established risk factors, supporting its role as a complementary imaging-derived prognostic marker. Identification of patients with substantial residual thrombus after aspiration thrombectomy may facilitate a more individualized management approach, including intensified antithrombotic therapy, closer clinical surveillance, or adjunctive pharmacological or mechanical strategies aimed at improving microvascular perfusion and downstream myocardial recovery.21, 22, 23, 24,26

Because adverse outcomes after STEMI are multifactorial, residual thrombus volume should be regarded as a complementary imaging-derived marker rather than a standalone risk prediction tool. In the present study, residual thrombus volume remained associated with adverse clinical outcomes when analyzed as a continuous variable, supporting the clinical relevance of quantitative thrombus assessment beyond dichotomized thresholds and its potential integration with established clinical, angiographic, and procedural risk factors.20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25,27,28

Study limitations

First, this was a single-center, retrospective observational study including only patients who underwent aspiration thrombectomy followed by successful OCT imaging. Therefore, operator-related selection bias, residual confounding, and limited adjustment for procedural characteristics, antithrombotic therapy, and infarct severity cannot be entirely excluded. Second, OCT imaging was performed after aspiration thrombectomy; accordingly, the quantified thrombus volume reflects residual rather than initial thrombus burden at the culprit lesion, as previously described in OCT-based thrombus studies.2,16 Third, although OCT provides high spatial resolution for intracoronary imaging, thrombus quantification may be affected by imaging artifacts, signal attenuation, or incomplete blood clearance, which are recognized limitations of OCT technology.27,28 Fourth, although the cutoff value of 4.14 mm3 was derived using maximally selected log-rank statistics and was assessed by bootstrap internal validation, this threshold should be regarded as exploratory and requires external validation in independent cohorts. Finally, the relatively modest sample size and number of clinical events may limit the statistical power and generalizability of the findings. Although clinically relevant covariates were prespecified for multivariable adjustment, residual overfitting cannot be excluded.

Conclusions

Residual thrombus burden after aspiration thrombectomy, quantified by OCT, was independently associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with STEMI. These findings underscore the value of intravascular imaging in assessing residual thrombus beyond angiographic evaluation and support the potential role of OCT-based thrombus quantification as a complementary tool for post-PCI risk stratification.

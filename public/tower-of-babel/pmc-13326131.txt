A draft genome assembly of the planorbid snail Armiger crista (Hygrophila, Planorbiidae)
Authors: Panagiota Kotsakiozi, Marina Karoumpali, Erika Šlachtová, Elena Sarropoulou, Maria Stoumpoudi, Aristeidis Parmakelis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe draft genome assembly was generated within the framework of a greater study aiming to investigate the differences in genomic features characterizing freshwater gastropod species differing in their threat status according to the IUCN assessments. Data descriptionMollusca is among the most species-rich animal groups, yet their genomes are underrepresented in genomic databases. Major drawbacks for this are the complexity of Mollusca genomes as well as the fact that due to the presence of mucopolysaccharides in their tissues, they tend to provide low quality DNA extracts. Therefore, the generation of high-quality Molluscan genome assemblies is a challenging issue. The A. crista draft genome of the present study will serve as an addition to the planorbid genomes assembled today representing three different genera (Anisus, Biomphalaria, Bulinus). Therefore, it will serve as an additional genomic asset in the effort to elucidate the ecology, evolution and taxonomy of the highly diverse taxon of Mollusca. Keywords : Freshwater gastropod, Genomic features, De novo assembly, Illumina NGS, IUCN threat status

Objective

Gastropod species are a taxon of conservation priority, with a very high number of species threatened with extinction. More specifically, 58.6% of freshwater & 21.8% of terrestrial species are assessed as being severely threatened by the International Union for the Conservation of Nature (IUCN) [1]. The genomic traits of species and their relationship to vulnerability or invasiveness and adaptability have already been investigated in plants and animals [2–5] but has not been investigated in the extremely vulnerable aquatic gastropod species. Within an ongoing project of our group, several other planorbid species have been sequenced. In the present study, the genome of the species Armiger crista that exhibits a widespread distribution throughout Europe, Russia and North America [6] is assembled using Illumina short read sequencing data. Τhe draft assembly genome of A. crista will be used as a reference genome for the assembly of the remaining species in our ongoing project.

Data description

Sample’s origin, DNA isolation and sequencing

The samples of Armiger crista were collected by Dr Slachtova as part of her personal research collection from Prutník pond in Ostrava (Czech Republic). High molecular weight was extracted from an individual specimen using the DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kits for DNA Isolation (Qiagen, Europe). Slight modifications to the original DNA isolation protocol were applied to ensure that DNA fragmentation will be avoided. Therefore, no mechanical pressure was applied during the tissue lysis step to avoid the fragmentation of the high molecular weight DNA extracted, whereas in the final elution steps from the kit’s column, two consecutive elutions were performed in equal elution buffer volumes (30 uL). Only the second elution was used in downstream applications and sequencing. The use of the second elution was preferred under the reasoning that in the first elution, small size fragments would be in higher concentration, an issue that could limit the efficiency of the Illumina sequencing. Illumina library preparation and sequencing were carried out by Novogene (Europe). Library preparation was carried out using the Illumina DNA Prep Kit and the manufacturer’s protocol. The sequencing of the libraries was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 system, by applying a 150 bp pair-end approach.

De novo genome assembly

The sequencing by Novogene generated 24.6 Gigabases of sequence data. A K-mer analysis of the Illumina reads with a kmer size of 23 was performed using GenomeScope [7] and the output indicated an estimated genome size of approximately 802 Mbps. The genome size estimated for A. crista is very close to the expected size (916 Mb to 1.2 Gb) of a planorbid genome [8, 9]. The raw Illumina reads were assembled using v4.1.0 of the MasuRCA assembler [10] after employing the default settings. The quality of the de novo assembly was evaluated using QUAST v5.3.0 [11] on the scaffolds’ assembly of MasuRCA. The N50 was estimated to be 13,547 bp, and the largest scaffold was 282,038 bp long, whereas the GC content was estimated to be 35.52%. Assessment of the completeness of the de novo genome assembly using BUSCO v5.8.0 [12], the metazoan database and the Metaeuk gene predictor, revealed adequate genetic content [Completeness: 77,9 (single copy complete: 75.6%, duplicated: 2.3%), fragmented: 17.1%, missing: 5%]. However, it must be noted that mollusks are a highly diverse group, and the BUSCO completeness level reported for A. crista herein, lies within the levels reported in other studies dealing with mollusks genomics, especially those for which limited genetic and genomic resources are available in databases [13–15].

Limitations

In the de novo assembly generated, the completeness analyses indicated an adequate level of gene content, and the quality of the assembled genome was of a high level. However, the reconstruction of the full genome of the species would likely benefit from a long-read sequencing approach using a platform like Nanopore or PacBio. Gastropod genomes are quite complex, and a combination of short and long sequencing approaches will probably provide a more complete understanding of the species’ genome.

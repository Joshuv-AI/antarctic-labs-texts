Invertible sheaves and Π \Pi -invertible sheaves on the isomeric supergrassmannian and its toric subvarieties
Eric Jankowski

Abstract
. We provide an elementary proof that with the exceptions of certain Π \Pi -projective spaces, both the Picard group and the Π \Pi -Picard set of the isomeric (i.e. type-Q) supergrassmannian are trivial. We extend this technique to show that the Picard group and the Π \Pi -Picard set of a supertorus orbit closure within the isomeric supergrassmannian can be easily calculated from its defining polytope by counting the number of simplex factors. Since the presence of nontrivial invertible sheaves and Π \Pi -invertible sheaves depends entirely on factors of Π \Pi -projective space, we construct them as symmetric powers of the tautological sheaf and its dual.

1.
Introduction and some reference ideas

The isomeric supergrassmannian,
\QGr(r,n)
, is a homogeneous space for the supergroup
Q(n)
, isomorphic to its quotient by a maximal parabolic subgroup. By considering the action of a Cartan subgroup
Q(1)^{n}\subseteq Q(n)
on
\QGr(r,n)
, one finds that its orbit closures are normal, making them toric supervarieties as defined by the author in
[
8
]
. These toric supervarieties admit a description by subobjects of the superpolytope which occurs as the image of an “odd momentum map” on
\QGr(r,n)
.

In this paper we study two different kinds of locally free sheaves on
\QGr(r,n)
and its toric subvarieties: invertible sheaves and
\Pi
-invertible sheaves. An invertible sheaf is merely a
(1|0)
-dimensional vector bundle; as usual, these assemble into the Picard group
\Pic(X)
. A
\Pi
-invertible sheaf is a
(1|1)
-dimensional vector bundle equipped with an odd involution; these assemble into the
\Pi
-Picard set
\Pic_{\Pi}(X)
, which is not a group but a pointed set with distinguished point
\mathscr{O}_{X}\oplus\Pi\mathscr{O}_{X}
. While invertible sheaves characterize morphisms to projective superspace
\mathbb{P}^{m|n}
,
\Pi
-invertible sheaves characterize morphisms to
\Pi
-projective superspace
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n}\cong\QGr(1,n+1)
. Such embeddings have been studied by various authors, in e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
18
]
.

The study of
\Pi
-invertible sheaves originates with Manin and Skornyakov in
[
11
]
, where it is expounded that
\Pic_{\Pi}(X)\cong H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})
. In many ways this isomorphism makes the
\Pi
-Picard set a good analogue of the classical Picard group of a variety, but in other ways it is not so nice. For instance, although the tensor product of two
\Pi
-invertible sheaves decomposes into a sum of two rank-
(1|1)
locally free sheaves (see e.g.
[
13
]
or
[
12
]
), the summands cannot in general be equipped with odd involutions to make them
\Pi
-invertible.

Nonetheless, the calculation of
\Pic_{\Pi}(X)
for various supervarieties
X
remains an interesting problem. Cacciatori and Noja calculate it for projective superspace
\mathbb{P}^{m|n}
in
[
2
, Corollaries 1 and 2]
. Manin and Skornyakov do so for
\Pi
-projective superspace
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n}
in
[
12
, Chapter 2, Section 8.11.f]
. Manin, Voronov, and Penkov generalize this to
\QGr(r,n)
in
[
13
, p. 2078]
, although the description is somewhat opaque and does not clearly indicate when the result is 0.

There have also been some efforts to find the Picard groups of various supervarieties. The result for the type-A supergrassmannian is due to Penkov and Skornyakov in
[
16
]
, while Cacciatori and Noja treat the exceptional case of
\mathbb{P}^{1|n}
in
[
2
]
. The computation of
\Pic(\QGr(r,n))
, which will be done in this paper, is known to experts and often attributed to Penkov
[
15
]
. However, neither he nor the author could locate a written copy of his proof.

The Picard superscheme captures some subtleties of superschemes over a base; Bruzzo, Hernández Ruipérez, and Polishcuk calculate in
[
1
]
that the Picard superscheme of
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n}
is
\mathbb{A}^{0|1}
, in contrast to the Picard group
\Pic(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n})=0
. While Picard superschemes are not the focus of this paper, determining them for the examples at hand would constitute an interesting, if rather straightforward, future direction.

On the classical side, Picard groups of toric varieties are well-studied and combinatorial. If
X
is a toric variety whose fan
\Sigma
contains at least one cone of maximal dimension, then
\Pic(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r-n-\lambda}
where
r
is the number of rays in
\Sigma
,
n
is its dimension, and
\lambda
is the dimension of the space of linear dependencies of rays of maximal cones (see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem VII.2.16]
).

Our contribution to this ongoing story is the following result, which is the amalgamation of Propositions
3.4
,
3.8
,
4.6
, and
4.12
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be
\QGr(r,n)
or the closure of a
Q(1)^{n}
orbit inside
\QGr(r,n)
. Then

\displaystyle\Pic(X)

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{\text{factors }\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1}\text{of }X}\mathbb{Z}\quad\quad\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\Pic_{\Pi}(X)

\displaystyle\cong\left\{(\ell_{i},c_{i})\in\prod_{\text{factors }\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{d_{i}}\text{ of }X}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{C}\;\Bigg|\;\text{at most one }c_{i}\neq 0,\text{and if }d_{i}>1,\text{then }c_{i}^{2}=\ell_{i}\right\}

The number of factors of
\Pi
-projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{d}\cong\QGr(1,d+1)
can be detected easily by counting the number of
d
-simplex factors in the defining polytope. We emphasize that for
X=\QGr(r,n)
, we have

\displaystyle\Pic(\QGr(r,n))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&r=1,n=2\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Pic_{\Pi}(\QGr(r,n))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{C}&r=1,n=2\\
\{(\ell,c)\in\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mid c^{2}=\ell\}&r\in\{1,n-1\},n>2\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

so in particular
\Pic(\QGr(r,n))=\Pic_{\Pi}(\QGr(r,n))=0
unless
\QGr(r,n)\cong\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n-1}
.

The setting
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{C}
of
\Pic(X)
(for
X=\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n}
) arises as the Picard group of the (non-super) scheme with the same topological space and the structure sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2}
, where
\mathscr{J}_{X}
is the odd ideal. Note that the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading must be forgotten in order to make it into a non-super scheme, though it is equivalent to consider the
\Pi
-Picard set of the corresponding superscheme. Equivalently,
\mathbb{Z}
is the Picard group of the underlying variety (given by
\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{J}_{X}
), and
\mathbb{C}\cong H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1})
.

The tautological sheaves
\mathscr{O}_{\Pi}(-1)
and
\mathscr{O}_{\Pi}(1)
of
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n}
have been studied in e.g.
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
, and
[
9
]
. We generalize their construction to the
\Pi
-invertible sheaves
\mathscr{O}_{\Pi}(\ell)
. Since the choice of square root
c=\pm\sqrt{\ell}
determines only the odd involution of the
\Pi
-invertible sheaf, these
\mathscr{O}_{\Pi}(\ell)
form a complete set of representatives of
\Pic_{\Pi}(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{n})
for
n>1
. The following theorem summarizes the observations of Remarks
3.10
,
4.4
, and
4.13
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be the closure of a
Q(1)^{n}
orbit inside
\QGr(r,n)
. Then:

(a)

An invertible sheaf on
X
is given by the tensor product of an invertible sheaf
\mathscr{O}(\ell),\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
on each factor of
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1}
in
X
.

(b)

A
\Pi
-invertible sheaf on
X
is given by the tensor product of such an invertible sheaf with either:

(i)

A
\Pi
-invertible sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{\Pi}(\ell)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
on some factor
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{d}
for
d>1
, or

(ii)

A
\Pi
-invertible sheaf arising from
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\Omega^{1})\cong\mathbb{C}
on some factor of
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1}
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We work over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.1.
Toric Supervarieties

As in
[
8
]
, we use supervariety to mean a GFRR (i.e. generically fermionically regular and reduced) separated superscheme of finite type over an algebraically closed field. A normal supervariety is one that in addition satisfies the
(R_{1})
and
(S_{2})
criteria of Serre (subsuming the GFRR condition), and a toric supervariety is an irreducible normal supervariety equipped with an action by a supertorus for which there is a dense open orbit.

A supertorus is a supergroup such as
Q(1)^{n}\subseteq Q(n)
that occurs as a Cartan subgroup of a quasireductive supergroup; equivalently, it is a supergroup whose even part is a central torus.

For the purposes of this paper, a toric supervariety is determined by the usual data of a polyhedral fan
\Sigma
, together with a decoration
V_{\rho}
on each of its rays
\rho
. The decorations are 0- or 1-dimensional subspaces of the odd part of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(1)^{n}
, such that whenever
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are rays in the same cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, it holds that
[V_{\rho},V_{\rho^{\prime}}]\subseteq\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\sigma
. For more on supertori and toric supervarieties, see the author’s prior papers
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.

If
X=(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X})
is a supervariety, we write
i_{X}:\mathscr{O}_{X}\to\mathscr{K}_{X}
for the natural embedding of the sheaf of regular functions into the sheaf of rational functions. Then, writing
(\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})
for the ideal sheaf generated by odd elements in
\mathscr{K}_{X}
, there is a filtration

\mathscr{O}_{X}\supset i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}}))\supset i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})^{2})\supset...\supset i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})^{n})\supset i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})^{n+1})=0

where
n
is the odd dimension of
X
. We write

\mathscr{J}_{X}=i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}}))

for the ideal sheaf of the underlying variety
X_{\text{red}}
in
X
, and

\mathscr{F}_{X}=\mathscr{J}_{X}/i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})^{2})

for the
fermionic sheaf
of
X
. The fermionic sheaf is essentially an odd cotangent sheaf that smooths out pill-type singularities (defined in
[
8
, Example 3.4]
). For the purposes of
\QGr(r,n)
and its subvarieties of concern, we have
i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}})^{j})=\mathscr{J}_{X}^{j}
, and so
\mathscr{F}_{X}=\mathscr{J}_{X}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2}
.

2.2.
The Isomeric Supergrassmannian

We write
\QGr(r,n)
for the isomeric supergrassmannian. As mentioned above, it is a homogeneous space for
Q(n)
, isomorphic to
Q(n)/P_{Q(n)}
for a maximal parabolic
P_{Q(n)}\subseteq Q(n)
.

Another important viewpoint on
\QGr(r,n)
is as a super moduli space of
(r|r)
-dimensional
\Pi_{V}
-invariant subspaces of an
(n|n)
-dimensional isomeric super vector space
(V,\Pi_{V})
, per the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

An
isomeric super vector space
is a pair
(V,\Pi_{V})
consisting of a super vector space
V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}
equipped with an odd involution
\Pi_{V}:V\to V
.

That is, if
A
is a commutative superalgebra, the
A
-points of
\QGr(r,n)
parameterize free, rank-
(r|r)
,
\Pi_{A\otimes V}
-invariant submodules of
A\otimes V
. In coordinates, this means
\QGr(r,n)(A)
is the space of full-rank matrices of the form

\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}+\eta_{11}&\cdots&a_{1r}+\eta_{1r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n1}+\eta_{n1}&\cdots&a_{nr}+\eta_{nr}\end{pmatrix}

for
a_{ij}\in A_{\overline{0}}
and
\eta_{ij}\in A_{\overline{1}}
, modulo the right action of
Q(r)(A)
. As seen in
[
14
]
, it is covered by
\binom{n}{r}
affine charts (one for each size-
(n-r)
subset
I
of
\{1,...,n\}
), consisting of matrices of the form

\displaystyle M^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{I}+\eta_{I}\\
1_{r\times r}\end{pmatrix}

where
a_{I}+\eta_{I}
is the submatrix of
M
consisting of the rows corresponding the indices in
I
, and where the rows of
M^{\prime}
have been rearranged accordingly for notational convenience.

2.3.
Supertorus Orbits

Using the above coordinates on
\QGr(r,n)
, the left action of
Q(1)^{n}
is via matrix multiplication, where
Q(1)^{n}(A)
consists of diagonal matrices

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}(1+\xi_{1})&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&t_{n}(1+\xi_{n})\end{pmatrix}

for
t_{i}\in A_{\overline{0}}^{\times}
and
\xi_{i}\in A_{\overline{1}}
. As a result, the affine charts of a supertorus orbit closure will have coordinates of the forms
t_{i}t_{j}^{-1}(1-\xi_{i}\xi_{j})
and
t_{i}t_{j}^{-1}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{j})
. Sometimes we will act by a subsupertorus
Q(1)^{d}\subseteq Q(1)^{n}
, in which case some coordinates will have the forms
t_{i}
and
t_{i}\xi_{i}
.

The Lie superalgebra of
Q(1)^{n}
can be written as
\mathfrak{q}(1)^{n}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{1},...,x_{n},\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n}\}
such that
[\theta_{i},\theta_{j}]=\delta_{ij}x_{i}
.

2.4.
Polytopes and the Momentum Map

The classical type-A Grassmannian
\Gr(r,n)
admits a momentum map to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose image is the
(r,n)
-hypersimplex
\Delta_{r,n}
, the convex hull of the vectors in
\{0,1\}^{n}
with exactly
r
instances of 1. Here, the target space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is really the dual space of
t_{\mathbb{R}}
, a real form of the Lie algebra of a maximal torus
T\subseteq\GL(n)\to\Gr(r,n)
.

Remarkably, the momentum map image of a torus orbit closure (i.e. a toric variety) in
\Gr(r,n)
is a subpolytope of
\Delta_{r,n}
whose normal fan is the fan of the toric variety (see e.g.
[
6
]
). The edges and vertices of such a subpolytope must be a subset of those of
\Delta_{r,n}
. A similar phenomenon occurs in the super case for the action of
Q(1)^{n}
on
\QGr(r,n)
(see
[
8
]
), but this will not be needed in the present paper.

2.5.
Standard Extensions

In this section we introduce an embedding of Grassmannians (see e.g.
[
17
]
) that will be a useful tool in Lemmas
3.3
and
4.5
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
standard extension
of Grassmannians is an embedding of the form

\displaystyle\Gr(r,n)

\displaystyle\to\Gr(r^{\prime},n^{\prime})

\displaystyle\{V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{V\oplus W\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{n^{\prime}-n}\}

where
W
is a fixed
(r^{\prime}-r)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n^{\prime}-n}
.

Standard extensions generalize easily to the super setting. In the type-Q case, the map is given by taking the direct sum with a fixed isomeric
(r^{\prime}-r|r^{\prime}-r)
-dimensional subspace
W
of
\mathbb{C}^{n^{\prime}-n|n^{\prime}-n}
. Functorially, the map on
A
-points can be written as

\displaystyle M\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}M&0_{n\times(r^{\prime}-r)}\\
0_{(r^{\prime}-r)\times r}&1_{(r^{\prime}-r)\times(r^{\prime}-r)}\\
0_{(n^{\prime}-n-r^{\prime}+r)\times r}&0_{(n^{\prime}-n-r^{\prime}+r)\times(r^{\prime}-r)}\end{pmatrix}.

3.
Picard Groups

3.1.
Picard Group of the Isomeric Supergrassmannian

Let
X=\QGr(r,n)
for some integers
n\geq r\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
r=1
and
n=2
, so that
X=\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1}
, then
\Pic(X)=\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Since the odd dimension of
\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1}
is 1, we have
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1},{\overline{0}}}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
and hence
\Pic(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{1})=\Pic(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Recall that
X_{\text{red}}\cong\Gr(r,n)
is the underlying variety of
X\cong\QGr(r,n)
, given by quotienting the structure sheaf by the ideal
\mathscr{J}_{X}=i_{X}^{-1}((\mathscr{K}_{X,{\overline{1}}}))
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The restriction map
\Pic(X)\to\Pic(X_{\text{red}})
is injective.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of successive quotients

\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}\to\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2k})_{\overline{0}}\to\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(k-1)})_{\overline{0}}\to...\to\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{4})_{\overline{0}}\to\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2})_{\overline{0}}

where
k=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, and note that
\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(k+1)}=0
. Write
X_{i}
for the scheme
(|X|,\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}})
. For
i=1,...,k
, we have an exact sequence of sheaves of abelian groups

0\to(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}}\to\left(\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}}\right)^{\times}\to\left(\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}\right)^{\times}\to 0

in which the first nonzero map is given by
f\mapsto 1+f
. This yields the long exact sequence in cohomology

...\to H^{1}(X,(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}})\to\Pic(X_{i+1})\to\Pic(X_{i})\to...

where we have used the isomorphism
\Pic(X)\cong H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})
.

We claim that
\Pic(X_{i+1})\to\Pic(X_{i})
is injective, for which it suffices to show that
H^{1}(X,(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}})=0
. We first observe that
(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}}\cong\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i+1}
. It is seen in
[
14
]
that
\mathscr{J}_{X}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2}\cong\Omega_{X_{\text{red}}}^{1}
, and indeed
\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i}/\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i+1}\cong\Omega^{2i}_{X_{\text{red}}}
. But the Hodge numbers
h^{q}(X_{\text{red}},\Omega_{X_{\text{red}}}^{p})
are nonzero only if
p=q
, so
H^{1}(X,(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2i})_{\overline{0}}/(\mathscr{J}_{X}^{2(i+1)})_{\overline{0}})=0
. Therefore

\Pic(X)\to\Pic(X_{k})\to...\to\Pic(X_{1})\to\Pic(X_{\text{red}})

is injective, and we are finished.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
0<r<n-1
, then
\Pic(X)=0
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gr(r,n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\QGr(r,n)}

where the vertical arrows are the natural closed immersions and the horizontal arrows are standard extensions. Applying the Picard group functor, we obtain
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pic(\QGr(r,n)),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

so it suffices to show that
\Pic(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{2})=0
.

Using the description of
X=\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{2}
as a toric supervariety with supertorus
Q(1)^{2}
, it is straightforward to compute the even coordinates on the affine charts:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{0}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},t_{1}t_{2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{1}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{-1},t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}),t_{1}^{-2}t_{2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{2}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{2}^{-1},t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}(1-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}),t_{1}t_{2}^{-2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{01}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2},t_{2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{02}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},t_{1}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[U_{12}]_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{-1},(t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}))^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{-1}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}]

Now from the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times}\to\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times}\to\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times}/\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times}\to 0

we obtain

H^{0}(\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times})\to H^{0}(\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times}/\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})\to\Pic(X)\to 0

since
\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times}
is a constant sheaf. Using the trivialization over the open cover
\{U_{0},U_{1},U_{2}\}
, we note that

H^{0}(\mathscr{K}_{X}^{\times}/\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\times})=\{(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})\mid f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}(U_{i})^{\times}/\mathbb{C}[U_{i}]^{\times},f_{i}/f_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[U_{ij}]^{\times}\}.

Hence
\Pic X
consists of the data

\displaystyle\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{\ell}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{m_{2}>0}b_{m}t^{m}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{2}}{f_{0}}

\displaystyle=t_{2}^{\ell}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{m_{1}>0}c_{m}t^{m}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)

for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and
b_{m},c_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}}

\displaystyle=(t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}))^{\ell}\cdot\left(1+...\right).

An explicit computation reveals that this is not possible unless
\ell=0
, meaning the image of
\Pic(\mathbb{P}_{\Pi}^{2})\to\Pic(\mathbb{P}^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is 0, as desired.
∎

Accumulating the results of the Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
together with the cases of
X
a point, we obtain:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
0\leq r\leq n
. Then

3.2.
Picard Groups of Toric Subvarieties

In this section we use essentially the same techniques as in the prior section to compute the Picard group of toric supervarieties in
\QGr(r,n)
.

Recall that we write
\mathfrak{q}(1)^{n}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{1},...,x_{n},\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n}\}
, where
[\theta_{i},\theta_{j}]=\delta_{ij}x_{i}
, for the Lie superalgebra of the supertorus
Q(1)^{n}
.

Let
y\in|\QGr(r,n)|
be a closed point. As in
[
8
]
, its stabilizer is

\Stab_{q(1)^{n}}y=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\sum_{i\in I_{j}}x_{i}\;\Bigg|\;j=1,...,s\right\}+\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\sum_{i\in I_{j}}\theta_{i}\;\Bigg|\;j=1,...,s\right\}

for some partition
I_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup I_{s}=\{1,...,n\}
. We may then let

be the subgroup

T=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\prod_{i\in I_{j}\backslash\max(I_{j})}Q(1)

so that the orbit closure
X=\cl(Q(1)^{n}\cdot y)
is a faithful toric supervariety for

. Its Lie superalgebra is

\mathfrak{t}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{i},\theta_{i}\mid i\neq\max(I_{j})\text{ for any }j=1,...,s\},

and we write
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
for the
\mathbb{R}
-linear span of the same basis vectors. Then, since stabilizers of points are sums of unit vectors, it follows that the decorated fan will be a complete fan on rays of the form
\pm\mathbb{R}_{+}(x_{i_{1}}+...+x_{i_{k}})
decorated by
\mathbb{C}(\theta_{i_{1}}+...+\theta_{i_{k}})
.

In particular, the fermionic sheaf
\mathscr{F}_{X}\cong\Omega_{X_{\text{red}}}^{1}
as in the case of
X=\QGr(r,n)
. This can also be seen at the level of affine toric charts of
X
, each of which is generated by a collection of even weight vectors that reduce to the ordinary even generators, as well as one odd generator in each of the same weight spaces. We then have:

Lemma 3.5
.

The restriction map
\Pic(X)\to\Pic(X_{\text{red}})
is injective.

Proof.

A result of Danilov (
[

) yields

for

, so the argument of Lemma

∎

Let

be the polytope corresponding to

Definition 3.6

Proposition 3.8
.

\Pic(X_{P})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{pc(P)}
, a saturated subgroup of
\Pic((X_{P})_{\text{red}})

Proof.

An element of
\Pic(X)\cong H^{1}(\mathscr{O}_{X,0}^{\times})
contains the data of a transition function from each maximal affine toric open to each other with which it shares a maximal proper subset that is an affine toric open, i.e. a codimension-1 cone in the language of fans. This can be encoded by a transition function from each vertex of
P
to each adjacent vertex, along the incident edge. Since the 1-skeleton of
P
is connected, the data of these transition functions conversely determine a unique element of
\Pic(X)
.

A transition function
f_{vw}
from
U_{v}
to
U_{w}
is a unit in
\mathscr{O}_{X,{\overline{0}}}(U_{v}\cap U_{w})
, and they are subject to cocycle conditions
f_{v_{1}v_{2}}f_{v_{2}v_{3}}\cdots f_{v_{k-1}v_{k}}f_{v_{k}v_{1}}=1
. By Lemma
3.5
, we may write the transition functions in the form
t_{i}^{\ell}
or
(t_{i}t_{j}^{-1}(1-\xi_{i}\xi_{j}))^{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}

or
x_{i}^{*}-x_{j}^{*}

Applying the cocycle condition, we see that the only way to cancel a

term. Hence
\ell=0
unless the cycle contains another edge parallel to
x_{i}^{*}-x_{j}^{*}

t_{i}^{\ell}
can be canceled out only if the cycle contains another edge parallel to
x_{i}^{*}
. In particular, a transition function along an edge

can be nonzero only if every cycle containing

contains another edge parallel to

3.6

Conversely, for each direction counted by

is free and independent of other such choices. These choices yield the Picard group

∎

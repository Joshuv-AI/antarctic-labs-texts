ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHeart failure affects almost 64 million people, with more than half of it constituting heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). Angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNI) and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (SGLT2i) are in the first line for HFrEF, but no head-to-head trials are available. Moreover, growth differentiation factor-15 (GDF-15) has been demonstrated as a promising prognostic marker, specifically for HFrEF, but has not been explored much.MethodsThis pragmatic randomised controlled trial recruits 100 patients with HFrEF (ejection fraction <40%) of New York Heart Association (NYHA) II–III and allocates them in a 1:1 ratio to the dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan groups. The primary objective is to assess the difference in N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide serum levels at the end of 16 weeks. The secondary efficacy objectives are to assess GDF-15, Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire-overall summary score and estimated glomerular filtration rate. Patients will be assessed at baseline, fourth week and 16th week after randomisation. As health technology assessment practices widely differ in countries, cost assessment is a vital factor to consider. The cost needed to treat one cardiovascular event is also compared between both groups. The occurrence of safety events will also be evaluated at each follow-up point.ConclusionThis pragmatic study aims to compare the efficacy, safety and cost-effectiveness of dapagliflozin versus sacubitril/valsartan in patients with HFrEF in real-world settings. The study aims to provide clinicians with data to make informed decisions regarding the preferred drug class. Additionally, examining the impact of ARNI and SGLT2i on GDF-15 levels could offer better insights into prognosis among patients with HFrEF.Ethics and disseminationThis study involves human participants and was approved by Institutional Ethics Committee at AlIMS Jodhpur with reference number AIIMS/IEC/2023/5842 approved this study. Participants gave informed consent to participate in the study before taking part. The research findings will be disseminated via closed group discussions at the site of study, scientific conferences, peer-reviewed published manuscripts, and social media.Trial registration numberCTRI/2023/12/060772.Keywords: Echocardiography, Heart failure, CARDIOLOGY, Research Design

Introduction

Background and rationale

Heart failure (HF) impacts around 64 million worldwide1 and is categorised as per left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) into preserved, mildly reduced and reduced.2 Almost 55% of patients with HF belong to the reduced EF group.3 Pocock et al suggested that every 5% reduction in EF leads to around 13% rise in both mortality and morbidity.4

Markers like N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), troponin T or I and galectin-3 are widely used for the diagnosis and prognosis of management of HF. NT-proBNP is the most commonly used level I evidence recommendation marker for HF.5 Growth differentiation factor-15 (GDF‐15) was superior among all biomarkers and showed even better accuracy than NT-proBNP in predicting mortality among patients with HF.6 Binder et al concluded that raised GDF-15 levels are correlated with increased morbidity in patients with HF,7 but the role of GDF-15 has not been well explored in HF. To date, there have been insufficient studies investigating the role of GDF-15 as a biomarker in HF, particularly in the context of HF with reduced EF (HFrEF).

Pharmacotherapy remains the cornerstone of HFrEF by showing clinical improvement and increasing the overall quality of life (QoL).8,10 HF medications such as angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNI) and SGLT2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) are first-line treatments for HFrEF.2 However, which of them is preferred and precisely for comorbidities is still unclear. Teo et al compared placebo, SGLT2i and sacubitril/valsartan in their network meta-analysis and concluded that sacubitril/valsartan leads to more reduction for the composite of heart failure hospitalisation and cardiovascular (CV) death risk compared with SGLT2i. However, no difference in all-cause mortality was found.11 Moreover, there was no exploration of individual SGLT2i agents, and it is well known that SGLT2i have extra mechanisms of action apart from class effects, too.12 13

Clinical improvement is also essential and is needed to assess the progress. New York Heart Association (NYHA) and 23-item Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-23) are reliable tools for determining functional and clinical improvement, including predicting future hospitalisation and death.14 ARNI and SGLT2i both improve the functional status and quality of life (QOL) among patients with HF.15 16

ARNI and SGLT2i, both categories of medications, operate distinctly in the body, with their own set of actions and potential side effects, ranging from mild inconveniences to more severe complications.17 18 There is a gap in research comparing the relative efficacy, impact on QOL, adverse events and treatment discontinuation rates among patients. Cost attributes in decision-making regarding allocating resources in the health sector and the cost of illness represent the economic burden on society.19 Middle-income countries (MICs) lag from high-income countries (HICs) in adopting and understanding the implications of health technology assessment (HTA) practices.20 This is especially important in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) like India, as HIC HTA practices cannot be directly employed. Moreover, there is a considerable price difference, with sacubitril/valsartan being 2–3 times more expensive than dapagliflozin.

This is the first trial specifically designed to address the existing gap by comparing the efficacy, safety and cost-effectiveness of dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan in individuals diagnosed with HFrEF. These insights will help clinicians make more informed decisions regarding the preferred treatment in HFrEF, helping manage the burden of this rising health issue. It can help establish GDF-15 as a prognostic biomarker in HF. Moreover, it can also improve healthcare resource utilisation and medical expenditure in our resource-constrained setting.

Rationale of intervention

Rationale of sacubitril/valsartan

Previous trials have shown that sacubitril/valsartan reduces CV death and hospitalisation for HF (HHF) in HFrEF.21 Sacubitril/valsartan showed more significant improvements in cardiac remodelling, including reductions in the left atrial volume index, left ventricular end-diastolic volume index, left ventricular end-systolic volume index and mitral E/e′ ratio.22 Overall, these trials support sacubitril/valsartan as a preferred treatment option in HFrEF and are suggested as the strong class of recommendation with an A level of evidence.23

Rationale of dapagliflozin

Several trials have explained the role of the CV benefits of SGLT2i and concluded that they reduced the risk of HHF or CV death among patients with HF, irrespective of diabetes status.24,27 Among patients with HF, a network meta-analysis concluded that dapagliflozin decreased maximum CV death when compared with different SGLT2i.28

Trials and previous meta-analyses support the use of dapagliflozin as a preferred treatment option in HFrEF, and it is suggested as a strong class of recommendation with an A level of evidence.23

Rationale for primary outcome

Our primary objective is to investigate the change in serum NT-proBNP levels in dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan groups from baseline to 16 weeks after enrolment. In the Guiding Evidence Based Therapy Using Biomarker Intensified Treatment in Heart Failure (GUIDE-IT) study, researchers examined the relationship between repeated NT-proBNP levels and CV death or HHF. The larger adjusted HR suggests that measuring repeated measurements of NT-proBNP over time provides better prognostic information for predicting the risk of HHF or CV death. Furthermore, this study indicates that changes in NT-proBNP levels over time are more strongly associated with adverse CV outcomes than baseline NT-proBNP levels alone.29 This implies that monitoring NT-proBNP levels over time provides valuable prognostic information when compared with a single baseline measurement.

Rationale for secondary outcomes

EF and end diastolic volume (EDV)

In HFrEF, left ventricle systolic dysfunction leads to reduced EF, accompanied by increased EDV. Less EF is associated with poor survival outcomes; clinical trials have traditionally focused on patients with EF ≤40%.30

This study also intends to measure echocardiography parameters (EDV and left ventricular EF (LVEF)) at baseline and at each time point.

Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)

HF often results in decreased stroke volume and a fall in renal perfusion and eGFR. Patients who experience a decrease in baseline eGFR have higher readmission rates than those with an increase in eGFR. Furthermore, elevated creatinine levels during hospitalisation are associated with raised readmission risk.31

Growth differentiation factor-15

GDF-15 outperformed even NT-proBNP and other biomarkers in predicting all-cause mortality in patients with HF, indicating that GDF-15 is a more powerful predictor of mortality in HF.6

A meta-analysis reveals that elevated levels of circulating GDF-15 are linked to a higher risk of all-cause mortality in patients with congestive HF, particularly among those with an ischaemic cause, such as coronary atherosclerosis. They further suggested that GDF-15 might offer a more significant prognostic value in individuals with reduced EF.32

A substudy of the Vericiguat Global Study in Subjects with Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (VICTORIA) trial compared biomarkers among patients with HF. They have highlighted the role of GDF-15 after adjusting NT-proBNP. They concluded that higher levels of GDF-15 are an independent indicator of myocardial infarction and a valuable prognostic factor for HHF.33 However, they discussed Verciguat comparison with a placebo, and in our study, we assessed the impact of sacubitril/valsartan and dapagliflozin on GDF-15 levels. The GDF-15 levels will be measured at baseline and last follow-up visits.

KCCQ-overall score (KCCQ-OS)

The impact of HF on a patient’s symptoms and physical health is also an essential factor. We will assess the physical health and clinical outcome by using 23 question-based questionnaires. In this study, KCCQ-OS, NYHA and 6-minute walk tests were compared to determine the clinical change. KCCQ revealed the difference between significant, moderate and minor changes, but NYHA and the 6-minute walk test cannot differentiate small changes.34 The KCCQ is a self-administered set of questions consisting of 23 items. In our study, KCCQ-23 will be assessed at each follow-up time point.

The cost needed to treat to prevent one event of CV death

The cost of treatment is also one of the major factors for deciding the correct drug, as not all patient populations can afford all drugs. HTA considers medical, social, ethical and economic dimensions to inform decision-making in healthcare. HTA aims to enhance the adoption of cost-effective new technologies while discouraging the use of questionable technologies within the healthcare system.35 So, HIC HTA practices cannot be implicated directly in MIC. Hence, healthcare resource utilisation costs will be assessed as medical and non-medical costs. All health economic analyses will be done per Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022.36

We also assess the cost needed to prevent one CV event by comparing the expenditures during the hospitalisation of patients due to any CV event.

Safety profile comparison

Reporting safety is equally important as reporting efficacy. A study reported that very few studies appropriately report safety outcomes. Most studies have reported safety events as statements and do not provide numerical data.37

Drug safety is an essential concept of benefit-risk balance, underscoring that drugs with high risks should be reserved for necessary cases. In our study, both minor and significant safety events will be reported as numerical data for both groups. Drug withdrawal or discontinuation due to adverse events will also be reported.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a pragmatic randomised controlled clinical trial. This study is focused on generating practical insights by employing minimal inclusion criteria and recruiting patients directly from healthcare settings.38 Minimally restrictive inclusion criteria are used to ensure that the study population is diverse, allowing the results to be applicable to everyday clinical practice. This approach makes the findings more relevant to real-world healthcare settings.38 This clinical trial compares the efficacy and safety of two approved medicines, dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan, commonly in use among patients with HFrEF. The trial aims to generate generalisable knowledge that can be applied to the general population of patients with HFrEF without compromising internal validity. The protocol adheres to Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials standards.39 Patient randomisation and study flow chart are explained in figure 1.

Only HFrEF participants are included in our study because the management guidelines for HFrEF and HFpEF differ significantly. According to the latest American Heart Association guidelines, SGLT2i is recommended as first-line treatment for HFrEF, but this is not the case for HF with preserved EF.40 We have excluded NYHA IV patients due to their high risk of acute decompensated HF, which is our exclusion criteria. They are often on additional medications or may require hospitalisation, receiving treatments beyond the scope of our study, at the discretion of their physicians. To ensure consistency and avoid potential discrepancies, we have decided to exclude this group. Similarly, NYHA I patients have been excluded because their symptoms are typically less severe, and they are more likely to be lost to follow-up.

100 patients will be enrolled in the department of pharmacology, cardiology, medicine, endocrinology and biochemistry at All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Jodhpur, Rajasthan, India. All enrolled patients will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to the dapagliflozin group and sacubitril/valsartan group using blocks sized four. Randomisation in the study was performed using computer-generated random numbers through R software. This is an open-label trial, as blinding was not feasible due to the possibility of needing to adjust or discontinue medications based on side effects. Patients allocated to dapagliflozin will be initiated at a dose of 10 mg once daily. Patients allocated to sacubitril/valsartan will be started on a half tablet of 24/26 mg two times a day. The dosage will be modified by the physician as and when needed. This fits well with the pragmatic nature of the trial as it resembles real-world settings where different patients with the same disease may be given personalised therapy with varying dosages of the same drug, based on several participant and disease factors.

Patients will be serially screened at baseline, after 4 weeks and 16 weeks after randomisation.

Various large trials, such as EMPA-VISION,41 EMPERIAL42 and CANA-HF,43 had follow-ups of 12 weeks and have set benchmarks and been recognised as valuable contributions in the field of cardiology research. So, we believe that a follow-up period of 16 weeks is sufficient to obtain valuable results.

At each follow-up visit, NT-proBNP, KCCQ-OS, renal function tests, liver function tests, serum electrolytes and echocardiography will be repeated. GDF-15 will be measured at baseline and at the end of 16 weeks. At each follow-up, adverse events will be assessed. All the concomitant medications and previous records will be recorded at each follow-up visit. This study is being carried out after the Clinical Trial Registry-India (CTRI) clearance till the scheduled completion of 16 weeks of follow-up after the registration of the last enrolled patient.

Safety reporting

Multiple testing, insufficient power, and complex data structures are challenges in analysis approaches for adverse events (AEs). A serious adverse event is any unexpected adverse event, occurring at any dose that may require hospitalisation or disability.44 In our study, we will calculate and report the power to detect clinically significant differences in AEs.45 Clearly defined and prespecified analysis plans are necessary for proper AE reporting and assessment.37 In this study, AE will be assessed at each time point of follow-up and numerical data of each event will be reported. Events of discontinuation and withdrawal of drugs will also be reported. The prespecified AEs field is available in case record form (online supplemental file 1). For reporting emerging AEs, there is provision available for other AEs. Emerging AEs will also be reported event-wise at each time point.46 Major AEs will be visually summarised as a volcano plot.

Statistical analysis

The sample size is calculated based on McMurray et al.47 Assuming an SD of 200 pg/mL in the dapagliflozin group and 300 pg/mL in the sacubitril/valsartan group with an effect size of 0.6 and a clinically meaningful mean difference of change of 150 pg/mL in NT-Pro BNP in patients with HF in both treatment groups, with 80% power and alpha error of 5%, the sample size is estimated to be 45 per treatment group. With a dropout rate of 10%, 50 participants per treatment group will be recruited.

Quantitative information will be presented as percentages for categorical data and mean (or median) and SD (or IQR) for continuous data, depending on the data distribution. Comparisons between dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan groups will be performed using an unpaired two-sample t-test, Mann-Whitney test, or χ2 test as appropriate. Logistic regression is done for categorical variables. Intention-to-treat (ITT) analyses will be applied as the primary analysis. Analysis of the primary endpoint compares dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan group differences in the change of NT-proBNP from baseline to 16 weeks. The primary endpoint will be analysed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), with treatment as a fixed factor, the baseline NT-proBNP level as a covariate, and with adjustment for age, sex, history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension and myocardial infarction. Missing data in the primary analysis will be estimated using imputation. We will manage the imputation using multiple imputation methods as provided in various literature.48 49 Log transformation will be performed if the assumption of normality is not met. After log transformation, the parameter will be further tested for normality, as indicated. A two-tailed p-value ≤0.05 is considered statistically significant. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered significant. Data will be analysed using R V.4.3.1.

Patient and public involvement

The authors were motivated to undertake this study based on their clinical experience with participants suffering from HF. Due to time constraints during the development of the protocol, patients and public members could not be directly involved in the study as it was conducted as part of an academic dissertation. However, the authors plan to share the findings with patients and the public through medical conferences and social media.

Ethics and dissemination

We have received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee at AlIMS Jodhpur. It was approved at a meeting held on 13/10/2023, and the certificate reference number is AIIMS/IEC/2023/5842. We plan to disseminate the research findings via closed group discussions at the site of study, scientific conferences, peer-reviewed published manuscripts and social media.

The study follows the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019, the National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research involving human participants and other relevant applicable rules and regulations. Vulnerable populations like tribals, mentally ill people, children, women in particular situations, refugees, terminally ill patients and those with diminished autonomy will not be recruited.

Trial status

This study is registered under the Clinical Trial Registry-India (CTRI) with the number CTRI/2023/12/060772. The study begun recruiting on 10 January 2024 and is expected to complete the follow-up of patients by August 2024, followed by data analysis.

Implications

This study is designed to assess and compare the impacts of sacubitril/valsartan and dapagliflozin on patients with HFrEF. We have explored the ongoing randomised controlled trials of dapagliflozin and sacubitril/valsartan among patients with HF and mentioned it in table 1. While ongoing trials are comparing the efficacy of sacubitril/valsartan and dapagliflozin, none have explicitly investigated their effects on GDF-15 despite its established superiority over NT-proBNP as a biomarker. This study focusses on exploring the impact of these drugs on GDF-15 levels, which could provide valuable insights into their therapeutic mechanisms.

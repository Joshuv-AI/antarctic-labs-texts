Hypertension and global DNA methylation: a population-based study in rural, Punjab, India
Authors: Anshika Kaushik, Debashis Bhattacharjee, Vineet Chaudhary, Suresh Dahal, Naorem Kiranmala Devi, R P Mitra, Rajinder K Dhamija, Kewal Krishan, Ranjita Pandey, Kallur Nava Saraswathy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypertension is a significant public health concern and a modifiable risk factor for increased mortality worldwide. It is reported to be influenced by gene-environment interactions and micronutrient intake. This study aims to understand the relationship between global DNA methylation levels and hypertension, independently and in the context of micronutrient status, among rural population in Punjab, India. A total of 2300 individuals, aged 30–75 years, (54.9% females) were screened for blood pressure. Of 2300 screened individuals, 900 (age sex matched 450 cases and 450 controls of hypertension) individuals were selected to examine the relationship between hypertension, global DNA methylation (5mC), and biochemicals (Folate, Vitamin B12, and Homocysteine). Folate, vitamin B12, and homocysteine levels were estimated using chemiluminescence technique. The ELISA-based colorimetric technique was used for performing peripheral blood leucocyte (PBL) global DNA methylation (5mC). Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0. Hypertensives were found to have significantly lower levels of global DNA methylation than normotensives (0.65 vs. 0.72 respectively; p-value = 0.01*). Individuals in the 1st quartile of 5mC were at significantly (OR: 1.671; 95% CI: 1.206–2.315; p-value = 0.01*) increased risk for hypertension in comparison to those in the 4th quartile (reference). Further hypertensives on medication with controlled blood pressure (BP) were significantly hypermethylated as compared to hypertensives on medication with uncontrolled BP (0.70 vs. 0.62 respectively; p-value = 0.04*). Folate appeared to mediate global DNA methylation among hypertensives on medication-controlled BP. Further hypertension driven hypomethylation hints towards accelerated biological aging among younger hypertensives. Hypertension may be associated with Global DNA hypomethylation in the studied rural population of Punjab, India. Folate sufficiency may prove to be an aid in improving the methylation levels among the cases of hypertension who were on medication and had controlled BP.Keywords: Hypertension, Global DNA methylation, Folate, Rural populationSubject terms: Biological techniques, Molecular biology, Diseases, Health care, Medical research

Introduction

Hypertension or high blood pressure, represents a significant public health concern affecting 1.5 million people globally. It has been identified as a modifiable risk factor for other cardiovascular diseases (CVDs)1,2. Hypertension is contributing to around 7.5 million yearly deaths globally, indicating an upsurge in the global burden of hypertension3. The number of adult hypertensives is expected to increase about 60% in 20254,5. However, the global prevalence of hypertension seems to be stable from 1990 (32%) to 2019 (33%), which may be attributable to the higher control rate of hypertension in developed countries6,7. On the contrary, an increasing burden of hypertension is observed in lower-middle-income countries (LMICs), contributing to 82% of hypertensives worldwide6. Over the past two decades, a substantial increase in the prevalence of hypertension has been reported in India8. National Health Family Survey 5 data reported, the overall prevalence of hypertension to be 18.3% in India9. A recent study reported that 90% of hypertensives in India remain undiagnosed, untreated, or with uncontrolled blood pressure even after getting treatment10. This underscores the urgent need to develop a more effective care continuum for hypertension in India.

Considering the rising problem of hypertension, a number of studies try to understand the complex etiology of hypertension. Research has identified various risk factors (socio-demographic, lifestyle, biochemical, and genetic) associated with hypertension11–13. However, the risk factors of hypertension and their magnitude have been found to vary from one population to another14. This is mainly due to the complex interplay between genetic and environmental factors leading to epigenetic alterations, highlighting an urgent need to understand the epigenetic alterations associated with hypertension15. DNA methylation is the most widely studied epigenetic mechanism in relation to aging and various age-related health conditions, including hypertension16. The aberrant DNA methylation patterns linked with hypertension have attracted significant scientific attention because these changes are associated with accelerated aging, which is known as the major risk factor for hypertension17. Further, these changes are reversible and, therefore can be used as an important pharmacological target in formulating effective interventions for reducing blood pressure18.

Biochemical markers, for instance, folate, vitamin B12, and homocysteine, are the crucial regulators of one-carbon metabolic pathway (OCMP), which may influence DNA methylation19. Previous, studies have also reported the effect of these micronutrients on hypertension, however, the results are inconsistent20. Thus, it is necessary to consider them in studies to understand the relationship between DNA methylation and hypertension. Further, socio-cultural and lifestyle factors may affect the relationship between global DNA methylation and hypertension, underscoring the need for population-specific studies. Addressing these research gaps, the present population-based study tries to understand the association between peripheral blood leucocyte (PBL) global DNA methylation levels and hypertension independently and also in light of folate, vitamin B12, and homocysteine status in a rural population of Punjab, India.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

The data for the present cross-sectional study has been derived from a major project supported by the Indian Council of Medical Research. A total of 2300 individuals, of either sex (54.9% females), aged 30–75 years with median age (Inter Quartile Range: IQR) of 45 (37–55) years, belonging to Mansa district, rural Punjab, India were recruited and screened for hypertension in the present study. Pregnant women, lactating mothers, individuals with self-reported chronic diseases (such as cancers), and mental health problems were excluded from the study. Prior to the recruitment and data collection, informed written consent, transcribed in their local language, was obtained from each participant.

Data collection

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Department of Anthropology, University of Delhi (Ref. No. Anth./2018/2890/28-12-18). Data pertaining to socio-demographics (age, sex, education, socio-economic status, etc.) and lifestyle (diet, smoking, alcohol) were collected using pre-tested interview schedules through door-to-door household surveys.

The methods used in the present study are in accordance with the institutional (Department of Anthropology) research ethics committee and with 1964 Helniski Declaration and its ethical standards.

Blood pressure measurement

Blood pressure (BP) was measured by a digital sphygmomanometer on the left arm of the individual. Individuals were made to sit for five minutes before measuring blood pressure. Three consecutive BP readings were obtained from each participant after a time interval of five minutes. Hypertension was estimated based on the average of the three readings of systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP). To maintain accuracy in the readings, the sphygmomanometer was calibrated after the measurement on every 50 individuals. The identification of hypertensives was done as per the JNC-7 (mentioned below)21.

Normal blood pressure (BP): SBP < 120 mm Hg and DBP < 80 mm Hg,

Normal blood pressure (BP): SBP < 120 mm Hg and DBP < 80 mm Hg,

Prehypertension: SBP = 120–139 mm Hg and DBP < 90 mm Hg or SBP < 140 mm Hg and DBP = 80–89 mm Hg.

Prehypertension: SBP = 120–139 mm Hg and DBP < 90 mm Hg or SBP < 140 mm Hg and DBP = 80–89 mm Hg.

Hypertension: SBP ≥ 140 mm Hg or DBP ≥ 90 mm Hg.

Hypertension: SBP ≥ 140 mm Hg or DBP ≥ 90 mm Hg.

Individuals who were found to be pre-hypertensives were excluded from the study.

To understand the effect of medication on blood pressure and methylation levels, hypertensives were further divided into three categories:

Hypertensives not on medication: Hypertensives (identified during the study) who were not taking anti-hypertensive drugs.

Hypertensives on medication-controlled BP: Hypertensives (already diagnosed) who were taking anti-hypertensive drugs for at least past three months and whose blood pressure is controlled.

Hypertensives on medication uncontrolled BP: Hypertensives (already diagnosed) who were taking anti-hypertensive drugs for at least past three months and whose blood pressure is uncontrolled.

Blood sample collection

Of 2300 screened individuals, overnight fasting blood (5 mL) could be collected from 1900 participants only. Serum and plasma were separated from the blood for further biochemical analysis. DNA extraction was performed using the salting-out method22. All the biological (serum, plasma, and DNA) samples were stored at -80º Celsius for further analysis. DNA quality check was executed via nanodrop spectrophotometer with A260/A280 ~ 1.8 as a standard. Of 1900 samples, 900 age-sex matched (450 cases and 450 controls of hypertension) individuals were selected for understanding the relationship between hypertension, global DNA methylation, and biochemicals.

Biochemical analysis

Serum was used for estimating vitamin B12 and folate levels, wherein plasma was used for assessing homocysteine levels. Biochemical markers were estimated using the Immulite 1000 Analyser (Siemens Diagnostic products, USA) employing the chemiluminescence technique. Homocysteine ≥ 15 µmol/L was defined as hyperhomocysteinemia, vitamin B12 ≤ 220 pg/ml was defined as vitamin B12 deficiency, and folate < 3 ng/ml was defined as folate deficiency23. Of 900 individuals, the data for 844, 865, 877 participants were successfully obtained for folate, vitamin B12, and homocysteine respectively.

Global DNA methylation

PBL global DNA methylation was performed using the MethylFlash™ Global DNA Methylation (5-mC) Kit (Colorimetric) by EpiGentek Group Inc. USA. The methylated antibodies provided by manufacturers in the kit were used for estimating 5-methylcytosine (5mC) content in PBL DNA samples, followed by reading absorbance at 450 nm through MultiscanGo Spectrophotometer, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA. 200 ng of each DNA sample were used, and methylation assays were performed in duplicates. Inter- and intra-assay variances were observed to be less than 5%. Global DNA methylation was performed by a single individual to constrain handling variation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS version 22 and MS Excel 2019. The Kolmogorov Smirnov test implied to check the normality of continuous variables. Variables with a non-normal distribution were represented by their median and respective inter-quartile range (IQR). Appropriate non-parametric tests; Mann-Whitney U-test and Kruskal-Wallis, were used to determine significant differences in the median levels of the two and more than groups respectively. The chi-square test was used to study the variations in frequency distribution. Spearman correlation and logistic regression analysis were executed to understand the relationship between global DNA methylation and hypertension, in the studied participants. Major confounders for hypertension and methylation, such as age, sex, and body mass index (BMI), were adjusted in regression models. Statistical significance was considered at p-value ≤ 0.05.

Results

Variations in median global DNA methylation levels among cases and controls

Overall, the median (IQR) global DNA methylation levels (5mC) in hypertensives [0.65 (0.40–0.99)], were found to be significantly lower as compared to normotensives [0.72 (0.45–1.14)]. Based on the median (IQR) age (years), 45 (37–55) of the studied participants, they were categorized into two age cohorts (≤ 45 years and > 45 years). In both the age cohorts, hypertensives were found to be significantly hypomethylated as compared to normal individuals. Similarly, sex-wise distribution of median 5mC levels in normotensives and hypertensives revealed significant hypomethylation in hypertensive males and females as compared to their healthy counterparts (Table 1).

Median global DNA methylation levels among normotensives and different categories of hypertensives

Based on medication and blood pressure status, hypertensives were further divided into the following categories; hypertensives not on medication, hypertensives on medication-controlled BP, and hypertensives on medication uncontrolled BP. The least median (IQR) global DNA methylation was observed in hypertensives on medication uncontrolled BP [0.62 (0.37–0.90)], followed by hypertensives not on medication [0.65 (0.39–0.99)], hypertensives on medication-controlled BP [0.70 (0.43–1.14)], and normotensives [0.72 (0.45–1.14)] (Fig. 1). Normotensives were found to be significantly hypermethylated in comparison to hypertensives not on medication and hypertensives on medication with uncontrolled BP. Similarly, significantly higher global DNA methylation levels were observed in hypertensives on medication-controlled BP than hypertensives on medication uncontrolled BP (Table 3).

A higher proportion of normotensives and hypertensives on medication-controlled BP were observed in the 4th quartile, whereas a higher proportion of hypertensives not on medication and hypertensives on medication uncontrolled blood pressure were observed in 1st. Discussion

The present study investigated the association between global DNA methylation and hypertension independently and also in light of biochemicals (vitamin B12, folate, and homocysteine). The findings from the present study revealed significantly lower global DNA methylation levels among hypertensive individuals than normotensives. Overall, global DNA methylation revealed a significant negative relationship with blood pressure variables (SBP and DBP). Further, distribution analysis and adjusted regression models revealed a significantly increased risk for hypertension among individuals having lower methylation levels. Sex-wise analysis also revealed significant hypermethylation in normotensives than those of hypertensives among both sexes as expected. Altogether, these findings suggest, a significant inverse relationship between global DNA methylation level and hypertension. The findings from the present study are in concordance with previous studies, suggesting hypomethylation in hypertensives as compared to their healthy counterparts13,24,25. Candidate gene studies in animal and human models have confirmed the role of DNA methylation in the regulation of blood pressure26,27. Moreover, EWAS studies also suggested the significance of DNA methylation in etiology of hypertension28.

One of the possible explanations for hypomethylation among hypertensives is that DNA methylation may play a role in the regulation of various physiological pathways for instance, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), sympathetic nervous system (SNS), and renal sodium retention system (RSRS), which are actively involved in the etiology of hypertension. In addition, dysregulation of these processes was associated with aberrant DNA methylation patterns29.

In the age-stratified analysis, hypertensives were found to be significantly hypomethylated than normotensives among both younger (≤ 45 years) and older (> 45 years) age groups. Further, an inverse relationship between methylation and blood pressure variables (SBP and DBP) was observed across both age groups. This relationship was significant for all the variables, except for DBP in the younger age group (borderline significance). In both age groups, a weak and inverse relationship between SBP and methylation levels was observed, however, DBP showed a weaker relationship with methylation among the younger age group, which has become significantly prominent in the older age group. Further, these findings suggest, that DBP is a major contributor to hypertension-driven hypomethylation. Literature has reported that increase in DBP was associated with younger age30,31 and affects the methylation of numerous genes across the genome. This influence becomes more pronounced with increasing age32.

Interestingly, similar median global DNA methylation levels were observed between hypertensives in the younger age group and normotensives in the older age group (Fig. 2), suggesting hypomethylation-driven accelerated aging in younger hypertensives, which may lead to negative health consequences for instance early onset of comorbidities. The findings from previous studies were inconsistent, but global DNA hypomethylation has been linked with aging, particularly among older individuals33. Evidence from the existing literature supported the role of DNA methylation in biological aging and age-related diseases for instance, cognitive impairment, hypertension13,34. Moreover, a recent study reported accelerated biological aging among hypertensives than normotensives reflecting greater age-related health consequences among hypertensives35.

Age-wise variations in global DNA methylation levels among normotensives and hypertensives.

The overall findings also hint towards the role of One Carbon Metabolic Pathway, in regulating the relationship between these variables (blood pressure, methylation), as both hypertension and methylation were reported to be affected by dysregulation of OCMP13,36.

Of all the defined categories, the least global DNA methylation level was observed in hypertensives on medication uncontrolled BP, followed by hypertensives not on medication, hypertensives on medication-controlled BP, and normotensives. Further, significant hypermethylation was observed among hypertensives who are on medication and had controlled BP than hypertensives who are on medication and had uncontrolled BP. A similar trend was observed significantly with normal levels of Vitamin B12, and Folate. This inference is further validated through distribution analysis signifying higher proportion of normotensives and hypertensives on medication with controlled BP in 4th quartile (higher methylation levels) as compared to hypertensives not on medication and hypertensives on medication with uncontrolled BP.

These findings suggest restoration of normal methylation levels among hypertensives on medication with controlled BP. This underscores the two possible working mechanisms of antihypertensive drugs i.e. either anti-hypertensive drugs control blood pressure by improving the methylation level or they control blood pressure by other physiological mechanisms resulting in improved DNA methylation levels. However, from this inference, the exact cause-and-effect relationship between methylation and hypertension cannot be determined. A recent study has also reported the similar finding that global DNA methylation seems to improve after medication among cases13. Though very few studies have examined the association between methylation and antihypertensive drugs, previous studies hint towards the contribution of effective treatment interventions in improving methylation status among cases. Previous studies have highlighted the potential of DNA methylation as a pharmacological target in treating hypertension18.

Despite being on medication, the observed uncontrolled bp and hypomethylation among hypertensives could be explained by the ineffectiveness of drugs in controlling blood pressure within these individuals. This could be attributable to the pharmacogenetic effect, i.e. genetic architecture of an individual plays an imperative role in the effective processing of drugs37. Since this study focuses on a rural population, individuals typically visit local health centres where they are often prescribed common antihypertensive medications (amlodipine, telmisartan etc.). These drugs also seem to influence genes involved in blood pressure regulation24. Further, the data from the present study revealed that despite being on medication, 75% hypertensives with uncontrolled BP were recommended to take the same medicine, which seems to be a major concern in the studied population.

Delving into the role of biochemicals in moderating the association between hypertension and global DNA methylation, significantly lower 5mC levels were observed among hypertensives on medication and having controlled BP, within the low folate group as compared to folate replete group. Moreover, a strong significant, and positive relationship between folate and global DNA methylation was observed within the same category. Similar findings were also observed with Vitamin B12 and homocysteine status, with albeit no statistical significance. This implies that low levels of methylation among hypertensives on medication with controlled BP may be affected significantly by folate levels in the studied participants. These findings also hint towards the restoration of normal biological processes, resulting in improved methylation levels, among folate replete group which is not the case among folate deplete group. However, in the normotensive group, a weak positive correlation between folate and global DNA methylation was observed, with borderline significance conferring to the normal relationship between folate and methylation. The strength of this relationship among normal individuals is weak because they have not experienced a decline in methylation levels due to diseased conditions as in hypertensives, leading to stable methylation levels among them. These inferences also hint towards hypomethylation being a consequence rather than the cause of hypertension because, despite having controlled BP after medication, hypomethylation was observed within the folate deplete group.

One crucial pathway that can be used to understand the relationship between folate (B vitamins), hypertension, and methylation is the One Carbon Metabolic Pathway. OCMP is the major methyl donor for methylation mechanism throughout the genome regulating various genes. Global DNA methylation considered in the present study is a crude estimate of the total percentage of methylation in the whole genome which would be affected by the dysregulation of the OCMP pathway. In OCMP cycle, micronutrients for instance, folate (methyl donor) and Vitamin B12 (co-factor) are the crucial regulators of DNA methylation38. Some intervention studies also reported dietary effects on DNA methylation39.

Previous studies have reported mixed findings indicating either positive or no association between folate and global DNA methylation40–42. Further, in individuals who are folate deficient, folate levels have been recognized as a predictor for methylation levels43. Moreover, a higher intake of B vitamins was found to reduce the odds of hypertension. A study on hypertensives on treatment without CVD history revealed that folate intake may reduce the risk of hypertension-associated co-morbidities and mortality20. Further, dose dependent relationship was observed between folic acid supplementation and global DNA methylation among patients of colorectal mucosa44. However, contradictory findings have also been reported45.

The rebuilding of normal process between the variables of one carbon metabolic pathway i.e., folate and DNA methylation among hypertensives on medication with controlled BP and having normal folate levels also suggests the reversible nature of DNA methylation once the diseased condition is controlled. This may be effectively used in various treatment strategies. These epigenetic alterations can be passed from one generation to another, accounting for missing heritability of various disorders for instance hypertension46. Unlike genetic alterations, epigenetic alterations are reversible in nature, and can be restored with proper interventions including micronutrients. However, if these alterations are not reversed, they may result in increased epigenetic load and early onset of diseases in future generation.

Conclusion

The present study suggests hypomethylation among hypertensives as compared to normotensives. Further hypertension-driven hypomethylation also hints toward accelerated biological ageing. The restoration of normal methylation levels among hypertensives on medication-controlled BP suggest the reversible nature of epigenetic alterations which prove to be an aid in developing effective treatment strategies. Interestingly, in the studied participants, folate levels were observed to play an important role in improving the methylation levels within this particular category. This also hints that the observed hypomethylation is a consequence rather than a cause of hypertension in the studied population and could potentially be reversed with effective treatment and micronutrients rich dietary interventions. However, more population specific research is needed to understand the adequate quantity and form of micronutrients to be recommended among hypertensives for reducing the burden of disease.

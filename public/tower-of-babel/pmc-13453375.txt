An Injectable AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Gel Designed for Damaged Intervertebral Discs Therapy
Authors: Shirong Gu, Xiaoxia Le, Mengchao Gu, Kaifeng Gan, Haiming Fang, Hui Shang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is a major cause of low back pain and imposes a significant burden on individuals and society. Bioactive materials are widely used for tissue repair. Here, we designed a calcium alginate (Alg-Ca2⁺) modified with N, N-dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate (DMAEMA) and acrylamide (AAm), named AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺, and evaluated its effects on IVDD repair.MethodsMorphology of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ (referred to as AAm) was analyzed by SEM. IVDD was induced in rats via Co6/7 and Co7/8 disc puncture, followed by AAm treatment. Disc height, tissue integrity, and matrix composition were assessed by X-ray, MRI, HE, and Safranin O staining. Aggrecan, collagen I/II, MMP3/13, and Ki-67 were analyzed by immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence. NP cells exposed to H2O2 were used to model inflammation in vitro; cell viability, proliferation, and inflammatory factor expression were measured by CCK-8, EdU, qPCR, and immunofluorescence (IF) assays.ResultsAAm treatment significantly enhanced the viability and proliferation of NP cells by almost 50% percent. AAm treatment notably alleviated disc height loss, restored tissue integrity, and increased the DHI value in rat model. MRI images showed that while the endplate signal was decreased in the model group, AAm treatment increased the signal at 4 and 8 weeks post-modeling. AAm mitigated disc height loss, improved MRI signals, preserved nucleus pulposus structure, reduced fibrous tissue formation, and promoted bone formation and chondrogenesis. AAm treatment repressed the levels of TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, and NLRP3 in NP cells. H2O2 induction suppressed the levels of Aggrecan and collagen II and elevated MMP3 and MMP13 expression in NP cells, effects that were reversed by the administration of AAm.ConclusionAAm treatment facilitates the proliferation of NP cells, represses the inflammatory response, alleviates tissue damage, and enhances bone formation in IVDD. These results indicate that AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ hydrogel is a promising bioactive scaffold for promoting IVDD.Keywords: intervertebral disc degeneration, calcium alginate, nucleus pulposus, extracellular matrix, inflammation

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is a major cause of low back pain and represents a significant burden on both individuals and society.1,2 The intervertebral disc (IVD) plays a critical role in spinal flexibility, weight-bearing, and motion,3–5 but is susceptible to structural changes caused by aging, mechanical stress, genetic factors, and lifestyle influences.6,7 These factors trigger inflammatory responses and oxidative stress, leading to extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation and loss of nucleus pulposus (NP) components such as Aggrecan and collagen II, which compromise disc height and hydration.8–11

Current therapies for IVDD primarily include surgical and pharmacological interventions; however, these approaches have limited efficacy in restoring disc structure and long-term function.12,13 Bioactive materials, particularly hydrogels, have emerged as promising strategies for tissue engineering. Alginate-based hydrogels provide biocompatibility and ease of gelation, while DMAEMA and AAm are commonly incorporated to enhance cell adhesion, mechanical stability, and bioactivity.14–16

Despite extensive studies, current biomaterials for IVDD repair often lack sufficient mechanical strength, bioactive signaling, or multifunctionality to fully support NP regeneration. Few studies have explored materials that simultaneously modulate inflammation and promote ECM synthesis in a controlled manner.17–19

To address these deficiencies, we developed a ternary hydrogel system composed of calcium alginate (Alg-Ca2⁺) modified with N, N-dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate (DMAEMA) and acrylamide (AAm). This system integrates mechanical stability, cell-adhesive functionality, and bioactive signaling, offering potential advantages over previously reported hydrogels. The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ hydrogel in promoting NP cell proliferation, reducing inflammation, and restoring ECM in a rat IVDD model.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Statement

All experiments were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Li Huili Hospital Affiliated to Ningbo University. This study was conducted in strict accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (8th edition, 2011). Surgery was performed under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection), and all efforts were made to minimize animal suffering. At the experimental endpoint, mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation, consistent with the AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2020).

Synthesis of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Hydrogel

The AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ hydrogel was prepared by mixing calcium alginate (Alg-Ca2⁺, 2% w/v) with N, N-dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate (DMAEMA) and acrylamide (AAm) at a mass ratio of 1:0.5:0.5. The mixture was stirred gently at 25°C and crosslinked by adding 50 mM CaCl2. Gelation occurred rapidly within 3–5 minutes at 37°C.

The Intervertebral Disc Degeneration (IVDD) Rat Model

In vivo IVDD was mimicked by establishing a needle puncture injury in rats. Female Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats, aged 3 months and weighing around 200 g, were purchased from Vital River Laboratory (Beijing, China). The rats were randomized into control, IVDD, and AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ (AAm) treatment groups, with 8 mice in each group. The rats in the IVDD group were anesthetized intraperitoneally with ketamine (90 mg/kg body weight). The 7th, 8th, and 9th coccygeal discs were localized under a microscope. Then, the Co6/7 and Co7/8 discs were punctured through the center with a 20-gauge needle, rotated 360°, and held for 30 seconds. For treatment, 20 μL of the AAm hydrogel (2% w/v) was injected into the center of injured junction using a 30-gauge needle under sterile conditions. The injection was performed immediately after disc puncture to ensure proper filling and distribution within the nucleus pulposus region.

Examination of Tissue Damage

Disc height and structure were measured using X-ray (Kubtec, USA) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) analysis (Pfirrmann). Disc height index (DHI) was analyzed to assess the extent of disc height loss.

Histological Staining

The discs and adjacent vertebral bodies of the rats were isolated, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA), decalcified, dehydrated, and embedded in paraffin. The samples were then cut into 5-µm thick slices. The tissues were analyzed using Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE; SolarBio, China) and Safranin O staining (SolarBio, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. For Safranin O staining, tissues were dyed with safranin for 1 hour, decolorized in an alcohol series, and stained with a fast green solution. Images were captured under a microscope (Leica, Germany).

Immunohistochemical (IHC) Examination

The paraffin-embedded tissue samples were deparaffinized, incubated with 3% H2O2 for 10 minutes to block endogenous peroxidase activity, and then incubated with goat serum at 37°C for 30 minutes. After overnight incubation at 4°C with primary antibodies against Aggrecan, collagen I, collagen II, MMP13, and Ki-67, the samples were reacted with a diaminobenzidine (DAB)-based peroxidase substrate. Images were taken under a light microscope.

Cell Culture and Induction

Nucleus pulposus (NP) cells were extracted from NP tissues according to a previous study. Briefly, NP tissues were collected and digested in a 0.25% trypsin solution (Thermo, USA) at 37°C for 2 hours, followed by shaking digestion with 0.1% type II collagenase solution (Thermo, USA) at 37°C for 8 hours. After centrifugation, the NP cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Hyclone, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were stimulated with 20 µM H2O2 for 12 hours to induce an inflammatory response.

Quantitative PCR (qPCR) Assay

Total RNA was extracted from cells and tissues using TRIzol reagent (Thermo, USA). After reverse transcription to cDNA using a First Strand Synthesis Kit (Transgen, China), gene expression was quantified using a TaqMan Assay kit (Transgen, China). GAPDH was used for normalization of gene levels. The 2⁻ΔΔCT method was adopted to compare gene expression between control and other groups.

Cell Viability and Proliferation

Cell viability was measured using a Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) assay (Beyotime, China). In brief, cells were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 1.0 × 104 cells per well. After treatment with or without AAm for 24 hours, the CCK-8 reagent was added to each well for a 2-hour incubation. Absorbance values were then measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Thermo, USA).

The EdU assay was performed to measure cell proliferation. Cells were seeded into a 24-well plate at a density of 105 cells per well and treated with AAm. Following treatment, cells were fixed with 4% PFA and incubated with an EdU solution (Beyotime, China) for 2 hours. Nuclei were stained with DAPI reagent. Images were captured under a confocal microscope (Leica, Germany).

Immunofluorescence Assay

Cells were seeded onto confocal dishes, induced with H2O2, and subsequently treated with AAm. The cells were then incubated overnight at 4°C with either an anti-collagen II or an anti-MMP13 antibody. Following this, the cells were incubated with a FITC-conjugated anti-rabbit antibody. Images were taken under a confocal microscope (Leica, Germany).

Statistics

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For in vitro experiments, each assay (eg, CCK-8, EdU, qPCR, immunofluorescence) was performed in triplicate and independently repeated three times. For in vivo experiments, each experimental group included n = 8 rats. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test. Comparisons among three or more groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version XX, IBM, USA). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characterization and Biocompatibility of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺

We first evaluated the morphology of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ (shortened to AAm) using SEM (Figure 1A) and then assessed its effects on the viability and proliferation of nucleus pulposus (NP) cells. Results from the CCK-8 and EdU experiments indicated that AAm treatment significantly elevated the viability of NP cells (Figure 1B, p<0.001) and gradually enhanced their proliferation within 7 days of treatment (Figures 1C and S1).

Characterization and biocompatibility of the AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+. (A) SEM image of AAm-DMAEMA/alg-Ca2+ (AAm). (B) Viability of nucleus pulposus cells was checked by CCK-8 assay. (C) Cell proliferation was measured by EdU assay. NC, control groups; AAm, AAm treatment group. ***p<0.001.

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Maintains the Structure of Rat Vertebrae in vivo

We established an in vivo rat model to mimic IVDD. The injuries to the coccygeal discs were examined by X-ray and MRI at 4 and 8 weeks after model establishment. As shown in Figure 2A, results from X-ray imaging revealed reduced height and a blurred endplate boundary at 4 weeks post-injury, along with a decreased disc height index (DHI). AAm treatment significantly alleviated the height loss in the IVDD model and increased the DHI value compared with the IVDD group. The MRI images demonstrated a decreased endplate signal in the model group, whereas AAm treatment increased the signal at 4 and 8 weeks after modeling (Figure 2B, p<0.001).

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ maintains the structure of rat vertebrate in vivo. (A) X-ray images of rat discs and calculation of disc height index (DHI). (B) MRI images of rat discs. ***p<0.001 vs NC group, ###p<0.001 vs IVDD group.

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Alleviates Damage and ECM Degradation in vivo

The discs and adjacent vertebral bodies of the rats were isolated, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into slices. Tissue structure and bone generation were measured by HE and Safranin O staining. The structure of the discs in the IVDD group showed notable degeneration compared with the control group. The nucleus pulposus disappeared, chaotic fibrous tissue appeared, and a portion of the endplate degenerated (Figure 3A). Decreased red and blue staining in the IVDD group indicated impaired bone formation and chondrogenesis (Figure 3B). These data suggested that AAm treatment significantly alleviated the disc degeneration and facilitated bone formation. Moreover, the protein levels of Aggrecan, collagen I, collagen II (Figures 3C and S2, p<0.001), and Ki-67 (Figures 3D and S2, p<0.001) were decreased, while the expression of MMP13 was increased (Figure 3D), whereas AAm treatment reversed these effects. Furthermore, the histological score for tissue damage was markedly increased in the IVDD group compared with the control group, which was reduced by AAm treatment (Figure 3E, p<0.001).

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ alleviates damage and ECM degradation in vivo. (A) HE staining and (B) Safranine O staining of disc tissues. (C) IHC staining of Aggrecan, collagen I (Col I), collagen II (Col II). (D) IHC staining of MMP13, and Ki-67. (E) Histological score was calculated to measure tissue damages. ***p<0.001 vs NC, ###p<0.001 vs IVDD.

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Reduces Inflammation Induced by H2O2 in NP Cells

Subsequently, we established a cell model using H2O2-stimulated NP cells. Results from the qPCR experiment indicated that the RNA levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 were significantly induced by H2O2, which were effectively suppressed by AAm (Figure 4, p<0.001). The results also suggested that AAm repressed the protein expression of TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, and NLRP3 in NP cells.

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ can reduce inflammation induced by H2O2 in NP cells. The qPCR experiment was conducted to measure the RNA levels of TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6 in NP cells. ***p<0.001 vs NC, ###p<0.001 vs IVDD.

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2⁺ Modulates the Matrix in NP Cells

Similar to the results from the in vivo model, we found that the expression of collagen II (Figures 5A and S3, p<0.001) was decreased and MMP13 (Figures 5B and S3, p<0.001) was upregulated in NP cells upon H2O2 stimulation, an alteration that was abolished by AAm treatment. Further results also indicated that H2O2 treatment suppressed the levels of Aggrecan and collagen II and elevated MMP3 and MMP13 expression, which was abolished by the administration of Aam (Figure 5C).

AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ can modulates matrix in NP cells. (A) Immunofluorescence staining of collagen II in NP cells. (B) Immunofluorescence staining of MMP13 in NP cells. (C) Western blot confirmed expression of collagen II and MMP13 in NP cells.

Discussion

IVDD is attributed to the imbalance between catabolic and anabolic processes in the intervertebral disc, which leads to abnormal extracellular matrix (ECM) structure, excessive inflammation and oxidative stress, and a reduced number of nucleus pulposus (NP) cells. Therefore, restoring matrix integrity, promoting NP cell proliferation, and suppressing inflammation are regarded as promising strategies for IVDD treatment.20,21 In this study, we employed a bioactive material composed of calcium alginate (Alg-Ca2+) modified with N, N-dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate (DMAEMA) and acrylamide (abbreviated as AAm) for the treatment of IVDD. We observed that AAm treatment effectively promoted NP cell proliferation and alleviated inflammatory responses in vitro. Furthermore, results from in vivo experiments in a rat model confirmed that AAm treatment facilitated ECM remodeling and restored intervertebral disc structure.

At the molecular level, pro-inflammatory responses in IVDD are largely mediated through NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, which upregulate cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 and drive ECM degradation.22 Hydrogels with bioactive functionalities have been shown to modulate these pathways, reducing inflammatory signaling and preserving ECM integrity.23 In this study, the observed reduction in inflammatory factors following AAm treatment suggests that the AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ hydrogel may influence NF-κB and MAPK cascades, thereby tipping the cellular response toward an anti-inflammatory and pro-regenerative state.

To date, therapies for IVDD mainly include surgical and pharmacological interventions.24–26 However, these methods show limited efficacy in preventing disease progression and restoring long-term intervertebral disc function.27 Bioactive materials have emerged as a promising strategy for tissue engineering over the past decades. Various therapeutic materials and approaches, such as cell-based therapies and hydrogels, have been investigated for IVDD treatment. For instance, multipotent mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) can be recruited to injury sites and exhibit low immunogenicity, while secreting cytokines, anti-inflammatory molecules, and immune receptors to restore the ECM. Thus, MSCs are considered ideal candidates for IVDD therapy.28 Autologous NP cell transplantation is another cell-based therapeutic approach for IVDD repair. A clinical trial demonstrated that transplantation of autologous NP cells alleviated postoperative back pain but did not improve intervertebral disc height.29 In addition, exosomes secreted by MSCs, which are loaded with diverse signaling molecules, have shown promising potential for tissue repair.30,31 Exosomes derived from NP cells have been shown to induce the differentiation of MSCs into NP-like cells while simultaneously enhancing their migration to injured tissues.32,33 Despite these encouraging advances in cell-based therapies for IVD regeneration, ethical and biological limitations remain significant obstacles.

Recent advances in bioprinting technologies have enabled precise fabrication of cell-laden hydrogel constructs for intervertebral disc regeneration. Bioprinting approaches, including extrusion-based and inkjet systems, allow spatially controlled deposition of biomaterials and cells to mimic the native disc architecture, thereby improving integration and tissue function.34 The AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ hydrogel could be adapted for bioprinting, enabling customizable shape and mechanical gradients that better recapitulate the complex disc microenvironment.

Alginates are natural products obtained from bacteria and brown marine algae that can be processed into diverse biomedical materials, such as microspheres, hydrogels, and fibers. For example, titanium (Ti) implants coated with Alg-Ca2+ were reported to enhance the viability and differentiation of human bone marrow-derived MSCs, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation and bone regeneration.35,36 N, N-dimethylaminoethyl methacrylate (DMAEMA) and acrylamide (AAm) are commonly utilized in the preparation of drug-loading hydrogels.37–39 An Alg-Ca2+/poly(N-isopropyl acrylamide) (NIPAAm) hydrogel was reported to exhibit good biocompatibility and to promote collagen production and alignment.17 Despite promising initial outcomes, further investigations are required to fully establish the clinical potential of AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+. Long-term safety and bio-distribution studies, evaluation in large animal models, and exploration of drug-loading potential for co-delivery of anti-inflammatory agents are warranted.

Conclusion

In this study, the AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ hydrogel significantly enhanced nucleus pulposus (NP) cell proliferation by 50% and restored disc height index (DHI) in a rat IVDD model, while effectively reducing inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6). These findings demonstrate that the hydrogel not only promotes ECM remodeling and tissue regeneration but also provides anti-inflammatory bioactivity. Overall, this work establishes AAm-DMAEMA/Alg-Ca2+ as a promising bioactive scaffold with potential applications in intervertebral disc repair and the broader field of functional biomaterials for musculoskeletal tissue engineering.

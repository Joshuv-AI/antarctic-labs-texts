Path-filtration for modal logics applied to quasi-dense modal logics
Olivier Gasquet, olivier.gasquet@irit.fr

Abstract
In [ 8 ] , the authors answer the question of the decidability of quasi-dense modal logics, and give an upper bound in 𝐄𝐗𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 \mathbf{EXPSPACE} . Unfortunately, their proof is erroneous, leaving the question wide open. In this paper we provide a quite direct proof of their decidability by introducing a new variant of the well-know filtration method based on paths in a canonical model and prove that satisfiability is in 𝐍𝐄𝐗𝐏𝐓𝐈𝐌𝐄 \mathbf{NEXPTIME} .



Introduction

This paper addresses the decidability conjecture of modal logics based on the standard logic
\mathbf{K}
plus a finite number of so-called quasi-dense axioms of the form
\Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{l}p
with
k<l
. They belong to the class of grammar logics of
[
5
]
which are known to be undecidable in general. But before entering the subject, we must begin by supporting our affirmation about the wrongness of the proof in
[
8
]
, here we only sketch it. For those who did, or will do, read this paper, they will understand the consequences of the following: “the core of a tree (finite or not) is the complete 2-vertices graph
K_{2}
” and ask themselves “how can it be possible to encode a whole infinite Kripke model coherently into a 2-vertices graph ?”. The answer is simple: it is not possible. For more details, the reader is invited to go through explanations on
this note on arxiv
.

A long standing conjecture.

As pointed by Zhe Lin and Minghui Ma in
[
4
]
:
In particular, it is unknown whether all normal modal logic of
the form
K\oplus\Box^{l}p\rightarrow\Box^{k}p
with
k\neq l\geq 1
have the FMP. This most intriguing open problem in modal logic was highlighted by Zakharyashev
[
10
]
as follows:

Unfortunately, the technical apparatus developed is applicable only to logics with transitive frames, and the situation of extensions of K by modal reduction principles, even by axioms
\Box^{n}p\rightarrow\Box^{m}p
still remains unclear. I think at present this is one of the major challenges in completeness theory.

The rest of this paper solves the question of the decidability for quasi-dense logics i.e. those where
k<l
. More precisely we prove that they have the exponential-model property and that validity is in co-
\mathbf{NEXPTIME}
. The other half, namely quasi-transitive modal logics, thus remains an open problem.

Our proof makes use of quite standard modal tools, but we introduce a novelty. Usually, filtration is based in equivalence classes of individual possible worlds of a canonical model. Various filtrations (finest, coarsest, and in between) may be defined by restricting the conditions for worlds to be related. We introduce so-called path-filtration for which we define equivalence classes of whole paths, making if finer than the standard usual finest filtration and thus distinguishing worlds that nevertheless contain the same formulas. This is possible only because of the bounded-depth nature of models for these logics, beginning with the basic logic
\mathbf{K}
. It would not fit with transitive-like logics (extensions of K4, PDL with star, etc.), neither would it be for quasi-transitive (or weakly-transitive) logics based on axioms
\Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{l}p
where
l<k
: the other half of the above decidability conjecture (and their mix) is still open.

We briefly recall the set of rewrite rules he uses (but with unsigned formulas), they simply mimic the semantics, at each step the corresponding instance of the right-hand side is added to the tableau):

\bullet

\sigma:\neg\neg\phi\longmapsto\sigma:\phi

\bullet

\sigma:\phi\wedge\psi\longmapsto\sigma:\phi,\sigma:\psi

\bullet

\sigma:\phi\vee\psi\longmapsto\sigma:\chi
(where
\chi=\phi
or
\chi=\psi
, thus leading to two branches)

\bullet

\sigma:\Box\phi,\sigma R\tau\longmapsto\tau:\phi

\bullet

\sigma:\neg\Box\phi\longmapsto,\sigma R\tau,\tau:\neg\phi
(
\tau
is new on the branch)

\bullet

\sigma_{0}R\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{k-1}R\sigma_{k}\longmapsto\sigma_{0}=\sigma^{\prime}_{0}R\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{l-1}R\sigma^{\prime}_{l}=\sigma_{k-1}

(for

Remark 1

The shortest path is unique, due to the fact that
(k,l)
properties only add strictly longer paths between worlds. Accordingly, we define
\Delta(x)
be equal to the length of a shortest path from
s
to
x
, i.e.
\Delta(x)=\min\{n\colon(s,x)\in R^{n}\}
.

From such a canonical model, in many cases, the so-called
filtration method
may be used to prove the finite property of the logic under concern. As presented in
[
3
]
among others (though it goes back at least to
[
6
]
), it mainly consists in defining an equivalence relation by identifying worlds that contain the same subformulas of the one under investigation, and then to define an appropriate relation on them in order to preserve satisfiability. But this fails for quasi-dense logics. Many attempts have probably been made (the present author did try) but it seems there is no way of preserving both satisfiability and relational properties when they involve the existence of intermediary worlds.

The idea we explore is rather to change the basic equivalence relation to whole path in the model, from the root to worlds. Of course, this has sense only because these logic have a bounded-depth property: a world of depth
n
only plays a role w.r.t. subformulas of depth
n
or greater (in terms of nested modalities). Another idea is that worlds that are “too far” from the root may be identified as a unique one, in the spirit of classical filtration.

The proof runs as follows: starting with this acyclic countable canonical pointed model
M
, we introduce the path-filtration which is in some sense generalizes standard filtration, and which provides a finite, satisfiability-preserving model, of exponential size in the length of the initial formula.

Conclusion and open problems

We provided here a direct proof of the exponential model property for
\mathbf{KL}
by means of a finer type of filtration – called here
path filtration
– than the standard ones and based on finer-grained equivalence classes over the sets of the canonical model.

This tool cannot be applied for the other half of the conjecture which concerns logics based on
\mathbf{K}
plus axioms of the form
\Diamond^{k}p\rightarrow\Diamond^{l}p
where
l<k
called quasi-transitive logics, since they have not the bounded-depth model property. One may also think of grammar logics containing axioms of the form
\Diamond_{a_{1}}\cdots\Diamond_{a_{k}}p\rightarrow\Diamond_{b_{1}}\cdots\Diamond_{b_{l}}p
(where again
l>k
), unfortunately, our proof does not transfer easily: lemma
2
and by consequence lemma
3
do not hold anymore. As far as we could try, there is no easy solution for treating empty (w.r.t.
\Phi
) worlds. It could be the case that these logics are really much harder and even undecidable.

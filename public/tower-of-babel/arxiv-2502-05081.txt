Abstract
Growth models with internal habit formation have been studied in various settings under the assumption of deterministic dynamics. The purpose of this paper is to explore a stochastic version of the model in Carroll et al. (1997) ; Carroll et al. (2000) , one of the most influential on the subject. The goal is twofold: on the one hand, to determine how far we can advance in the technical study of the model; on the other hand, to assess whether at least some of the deterministic outcomes remain valid in the stochastic setting. The resulting optimal control problem proves to be challenging, primarily due to the lack of concavity in the objective function. This feature is present in the model even in the deterministic case (see, e.g., Bambi and Gozzi (2020) ). We develop an approach based on dynamic programming to establish several useful results, including the regularity of the solution to the corresponding HJB equation, a verification theorem and the existence of a unique optimal control in feedback form.



Keywords:
Habit formation; Optimal investment and consumption; Stochastic control; Dynamic programming; Viscosity solution; Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation.

Introduction

In two highly influential works,
Carroll et al. (1997)
;
Carroll et al. (2000)
analyze the
dynamics of an endogenous growth model incorporating internal habit
formation, showing that this feature plays a key role in explaining how
increased economic growth can lead to higher savings. This finding is
particularly significant because it relies on a “multiplicative” habit,
as opposed to the “subtractive” form, which fosters addictive behaviour by
requiring consumption to consistently exceed the habit stock, potentially
resulting in infinitely negative utility. Yet the subtractive form dominates the continuous-time stochastic literature
(see, e.g.,
Detemple and Karatzas (2003)
;
Englezos and Karatzas (2009)
), where the
linearity of the habit makes concavity-based methods available.

In
Bambi and Gozzi (2020)
, the optimality of the solution in
Carroll et al. (2000)
is
revisited via the Dynamic Programming approach, which, unlike the Maximum Principle,
provides sufficient optimality conditions without concavity, a property that
fails in this model even in the deterministic case. In stochastic models,
multiplicative preferences have been studied mainly in portfolio choice
problems, see for instance
van Bilsen et al. (2020)
;
Angoshtari et al. (2022)
;
Guasoni et al. (2020)
and
Angoshtari et al. (2026)
. In particular, in
Angoshtari et al. (2022)
;
Guasoni et al. (2020)
and
Angoshtari et al. (2026)
, homogeneity reduces the problem to one dimension;
the investment control in a complete market allows the HJB equation to
be linearized by convex duality and transversality to be obtained through
the state-price density. None of these tools is available in a production
economy with exogenous noise.

This paper investigates a stochastic version of the model in

Carroll et al. (2000)
with independent multiplicative noises on capital and
habit, and extends to it the Dynamic Programming approach in

Bambi and Gozzi (2020)
. Besides the lack of concavity, the problem is made delicate
by the singularity of the utility, which diverges to
-\infty
as
consumption vanishes, and by the state constraint on capital.
The main results that we establish are the following: (i) the value function, denoted by
V
, is continuous (Theorem
4.4
); (ii)
V
is a classical solution of the HJB equation in the open first quadrant
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
(Theorem
5.4
);
(iii) a verification theorem (Theorem
6.3
), which gives a sufficient condition for optimality; (iv) the existence and uniqueness of an optimal control in feedback form (Theorem
6.6
). In particular, (ii) is obtained in two steps: we first prove that
V
is a viscosity solution of the HJB equation, see Theorem
5.2
, and then apply a local regularization approach based on crucial results by
Safonov (1989)
and
Crandall et al. (1992)
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the model and the optimal control problem we are interested in. In Section
3
, the corresponding HJB equation is formally obtained, together with some properties of the maximum value Hamiltonian. Some important properties (such as monotonicity and continuity) of the value function
V
associated with the control problem are then investigated in Section
4
.
In Section
5
, we prove that
V
is a classical solution of the HJB equation, see Theorem
5.4
. This section is divided into two subsections: in the first (Subsection
5.1
), we demonstrate that
V
is a viscosity solution, while in the second (Subsection
5.2
), we prove the main result, Theorem
5.4
. Finally, a verification theorem and the existence and uniqueness of an optimal control in feedback form are established in Section
6
. In Appendix
A
, we prove some technical results which are employed in the arguments of Subsection
5.2
and Section
6
.

2
The Model

Consider a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
which supports two independent Brownian motions
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. We endow
\Omega
with the minimal augmented filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t}
generated by
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. The model state variables are the processes
k=(k_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
h=(h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, representing the capital and the habit, respectively. Their evolution is described by the following controlled linear stochastic differential equations (henceforth, SDEs):

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}k_{t}=\big(Bk_{t}-c_{t}\big)\mathrm{d}t+\beta_{1}k_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{1,t},\quad k_{0}>0,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}h_{t}=\rho\big(c_{t}-h_{t}\big)\mathrm{d}t+\beta_{2}h_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{2,t},\quad h_{0}>0.

(2)

Here,
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
are the volatilities of capital and habit,
\rho\in(0,1)
and
B>0
is the maximal expected exponential growth rate of the capital stock. The positive initial conditions
k_{0}
and
h_{0}
are assumed to be deterministic.
The control process
c=(c_{t})_{t\geq 0}
represents the consumption; it is progressively measurable and (strictly) positive, with locally integrable trajectories. For any choice of
c
and
h_{0},\,k_{0}
, the dynamics (
1
)-(
2
) admit unique solutions, up to indistinguishability. Their explicit expressions are given by

\displaystyle k_{t}=\mathcal{E}(Bt+\beta_{1}W_{1,t})\left(k_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{s}}{\mathcal{E}(Bs+\beta_{1}W_{1,s})}\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad t\geq 0,

(3)

\displaystyle h_{t}=\mathcal{E}(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t})\left(h_{0}+\rho\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad t\geq 0,

(4)

where
\mathcal{E}(X_{t})
denotes the Doléans-Dade exponential of a continuous semimartingale
X
evaluated at time
t
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}(X_{t})=\mathcal{E}(X)_{t}
.
In the sequel, when the context requires to clarify the dependence of these solutions on the initial state and the control, we will denote by

h^{h_{0},c}\text{ the solution of (\ref{eq:h}) controlled by $c$ starting from $h_{0}$ at time $0$.}

(5)

An analogous notation will be used for the capital process
k^{k_{0},c}.

For a fixed initial condition
(k_{0},h_{0})
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
the set of admissible controls. It is determined by imposing the positivity state constraints

k_{t}>0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad h_{t}>0,\qquad\text{for every }t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.,}

(6)

together with the following relation between
c
and
k
:

c\leq R\,k,\quad\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-\text{a.e., for a fixed constant $R>0$ independent of $c$ and $k$.}

(7)

We refer the reader to
Magill (1981)
,
Becker et al. (1989)
, and
Balder (1993)
for control constraints similar to (
7
).
Summarizing,

\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0}):=\left\{c\colon\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\text{ progr. meas.}\,\Bigg|\,\begin{aligned} &\text{$c(\omega,\cdot)\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(0,\infty),$ for $\mathbb{P}-$a.e. $\omega\in\Omega,$}\\
&c>0,\,\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-\text{a.e., the solutions of }\\
&\text{\eqref{eq:k}-\eqref{eq:h} satisfy \eqref{constraints-kh}},\text{ and \eqref{bdd_control} holds.}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(8)

Given
\theta>0,\,\sigma\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
, we are interested in the objective functional

\displaystyle J(k_{0},h_{0};c)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{c_{t}}{h_{t}^{\gamma}}\right)^{1-\sigma}e^{-\theta t}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\leq 0,\quad k_{0},\,h_{0}>0,\,c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0}).

(9)

From now on, we write
\mathbb{R}_{+}
for
(0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
for
(0,\infty)^{2}
. The value function
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}\to[-\infty,0]
associated with
J
in (
9
) is

V(k_{0},h_{0})=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})}J(k_{0},h_{0};c),\quad(k_{0},\,h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.

(10)

We assume that the parameters of the model fulfill the following requirement (cf.
(
Freni et al., 2008
, Assumption 2.4)
and also
Freni et al. (2006)
).

Assumption 2.1
.

Suppose that
R\geq B
. Furthermore, the parameters
(\rho,\,\beta_{1},\,\beta_{2},\,\gamma,\,\sigma,\,\theta)
satisfy

\theta>(\sigma-1)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\beta_{1}^{2}(1-\gamma)+\gamma\big(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})\big)^{-}+\max\left\{2,\frac{1}{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\right\}(\sigma-1)(\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\beta_{2}^{2})\bigg),

(11)

where
x^{-}=\max\{-x,0\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The condition
R\geq B
is consistent with the general framework where the asymptotic regime
R\to\infty
is considered. Condition (
11
) ensures that the discount rate
\theta
dominates the
maximal growth of the state variables including their stochastic parts. From a technical point of view, it guarantees the existence of strategies with finite utility, see Proposition
4.2
.

We conclude this section by showing that the set of admissible controls is nonempty.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
(k_{0},\,h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
, the set
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
of admissible controls is nonempty. In addition,
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
does not depend on
h_{0}
, that is,

\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})=\mathcal{A}(k_{0}).

Proof.

Fix
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
. The fact that
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})=\mathcal{A}(k_{0})
follows immediately from (
4
), which implies the positivity of the habit process corresponding to positive controls. Choosing
c=\nu\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1}),
for some constant
\nu>0

to be determined
, (
3
) reads

k_{t}=e^{Bt}\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1,t})\left(k_{0}-\frac{\nu}{B}(1-e^{-Bt})\right).

Therefore, fixing
\frac{\nu}{B}<k_{0}
,

k_{t}\geq\left(k_{0}-\frac{\nu}{B}\right)\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1,t})>0,\quad t\geq 0,

hence the positivity constraint (
6
) on
k
is satisfied. If we further require that
\frac{R}{\nu}(k_{0}-\frac{\nu}{B})>1
, then

Rk_{t}\geq\frac{R}{\nu}\left(k_{0}-\frac{\nu}{B}\right)c_{t}>c_{t},\quad t\geq 0,

which proves (
7
).
The two previous conditions are satisfied by
\nu
sufficiently small. Thus
c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})\neq\emptyset
, and the proof is complete.
∎

3
HJB Equation: Formal Derivation

Given
(k,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
,
\pi=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\pi_{k}\\
\pi_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
, the current value Hamiltonian is

H(k,h,\pi,Q;c)=a(k,h;c)^{\top}\pi+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma(k,h)^{\top}Q\Sigma(k,h)\big)+\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\Big(\frac{h^{\gamma}}{c}\Big)^{\sigma-1},\quad c\in(0,Rk].

Here,
a(k,h;c)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}Bk-c\\
\rho(c-h)\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and
\Sigma(k,h)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{1}k&0\\
0&\beta_{2}h\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. The maximum value Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{\text{max}}(k,h,\pi,Q)=\sup_{c\in(0,Rk]}H(k,h,\pi,Q;c)

\displaystyle\qquad=Bk\pi_{k}-\rho h\pi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma(k,h)^{\top}Q\Sigma(k,h)\big)+\sup_{c\in(0,Rk]}\Big\{-c\pi_{k}+\rho c\pi_{h}-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\Big(\frac{h^{\gamma}}{c}\Big)^{\sigma-1}\Big\}.

(12)

Define by
g(c;k,h,\pi)=-c\pi_{k}+\rho c\pi_{h}-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\big(\frac{h^{\gamma}}{c}\big)^{\sigma-1}
for
c\in(0,Rk]
. From a straightforward study of its derivative, we infer the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
k,h>0
and
p=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\pi_{k}\\
\pi_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the map
g(\cdot;k,h,\pi)
attains its supremum on
(0,Rk]
at a unique point. Specifically, defining by
G(k,h,\pi)=\sup_{c\in(0,Rk]}g(c;k,h,\pi)
,

G(k,h,\pi)=\begin{cases}-Rk(\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h})-\dfrac{1}{\sigma-1}\Big(\dfrac{h^{\gamma}}{Rk}\Big)^{\sigma-1},&\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h}\leq h^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}(Rk)^{-\sigma},\\
-\Big(1+\dfrac{1}{\sigma-1}\Big)[h^{\gamma}(\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h})]^{1-\frac{1}{\sigma}},&\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h}>{h^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}}{(Rk)^{-\sigma}},\end{cases}

(13)

and, defining by
\Gamma(k,h,\pi)=\underset{(0,Rk]}{\arg\max}\,\,g(\cdot;k,h,\pi)
,

\Gamma(k,h,\pi)=\begin{cases}Rk,&\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h}\leq h^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}(Rk)^{-\sigma},\\
\Big(\dfrac{h^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}}{\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h}}\Big)^{1/\sigma},&\pi_{k}-\rho\pi_{h}>{h^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}}{(Rk)^{-\sigma}}.\end{cases}

(14)

In particular,
\Gamma
is positive and has linear growth in
k
, namely,
0<\Gamma(k,h,\pi)\leq Rk
.

The HJB equation associated with the control problem with unknown
v
then reads, formally,

\displaystyle\theta v(k,h)=H_{\text{max}}(k,h,\nabla v(k,h),D^{2}v(k,h))=\mathcal{L}_{k,h}v+G(k,h,\nabla v(k,h)),

\displaystyle\qquad\text{where }\mathcal{L}_{k,h}v\coloneqq Bk\,\partial_{k}v(k,h)-\rho h\,\partial_{h}v(k,h)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma(k,h)^{\top}D^{2}v(k,h)\Sigma(k,h)\big).

(15)

Here,
D^{2}v
denotes the Hessian matrix of
v
.

Given a point
\pi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
and
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}(\pi)
the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
centered at
\pi
of radius
r
, and by
\overline{B}_{r}(\pi)
its closure. For any
n\in\mathbb{N},
let

\mathcal{S}^{n}\text{ be the space of symmetric matrices in $\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$.}

For
M,N\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
, the expression
M\leq N
means that
N-M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
is positive semidefinite.
We conclude this section with two lemmas collecting some properties of
H_{\text{max}}
in (
12
); their proofs are postponed to Appendix
A
. In Subsection
5.2
, they will be used to study the regularity of
V
via PDE techniques.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open and bounded set such that its closure
\overline{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
. Define

\tilde{F}:\mathcal{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{D}\to\mathbb{R}\quad\text{by}\quad\tilde{F}(Q,\pi,v,(k,h))=-\theta v+H_{\emph{max}}(k,h,\pi,Q).

(16)

Then there exists a positive constant
\bar{C}>0
such that, for every
Q=(Q_{ij})_{i,j}\in\mathcal{S}^{2},\,(k,h)\in\overline{D},\,\pi=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\pi_{k}\\
\pi_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right],\bar{\pi}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\bar{\pi}_{k}\\
\bar{\pi}_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
v,\bar{v}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|\tilde{F}(Q,\pi,v,(k,h))-\tilde{F}(Q,\bar{\pi},\bar{v},(k,h))|\leq\bar{C}\big(|\pi_{k}-\bar{\pi}_{k}|+|\pi_{h}-\bar{\pi}_{h}|+|v-\bar{v}|\big),

(17)

and, for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\langle\tilde{F}(Q,\pi,v,\cdot)\rangle_{D}^{\alpha}\leq\bar{C}\bigg(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}|Q_{ij}|+|\pi_{k}|+|\pi_{h}|+|v|\bigg),

(18)

where

\langle f\rangle^{\alpha}_{D}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k,h)\in D\\
\delta>0\end{subarray}}\delta^{-\alpha}\Big(\sup_{D\cap B_{\delta}(k,h)}f-\inf_{D\cap B_{\delta}(k,h)}f\Big),\text{ for a map $f:D\to\mathbb{R}$.}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
be an open and bounded set such that its closure
\overline{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
. Define the function
\tilde{F}:\mathcal{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times D\to\mathbb{R}
as in Lemma
3.2
. Then there is a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(Q,\alpha(k-\bar{k},h-\bar{h}),v,(k,h))-\tilde{F}(\bar{Q},\alpha(k-\bar{k},h-\bar{h}),v,(\bar{k},\bar{h}))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\widetilde{C}(\alpha|(k-\bar{k},h-\bar{h})|^{2}+|(k-\bar{k},h-\bar{h})|),

(19)

for every
\alpha>0
,
(k,h),\,(\bar{k},\bar{h})\in D,\,v\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q,\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
satisfying

\begin{bmatrix}Q&&0\\
0&&-\bar{Q}\end{bmatrix}\leq 3\alpha\begin{bmatrix}I&&-I\\
-I&&I\end{bmatrix}.

(20)

4
Properties of the Value Function
V

This section analyzes the value function
V
in (
10
). In particular, we will show that
V
is finite-valued and continuous, from which we deduce the dynamic programming principle in Proposition
4.5
.
We start with a preliminary result concerning the homogeneity of
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\alpha>0
,

V(\alpha k_{0},\alpha h_{0})=\alpha^{(1-\gamma)(1-\sigma)}V(k_{0},h_{0}),\quad(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.

(21)

Proof.

Fix
\alpha>0
and an initial condition
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.
Recall the definition of
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
and
\mathcal{A}(\alpha k_{0},\alpha h_{0})
in (
8
). According to Lemma
2.3
, these sets only depend on
k_{0}
and
\alpha
, i.e., they are independent of
h_{0}
. Moreover, by the explicit expression of the capital
k
in (
3
), it is straightforward to conclude that

\mathcal{A}(\alpha k_{0},\,\alpha h_{0})=\alpha\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0}).

Using the notation introduced in (
5
), by (
4
)

h^{\alpha h_{0},\alpha c}_{t}=\alpha h^{h_{0},c}_{t},\quad t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s., for every }c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0}).

As a result, also using (
9
) and (
10
),

\displaystyle V(\alpha k_{0},\alpha h_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\bigg(\frac{{(h^{\alpha h_{0},\alpha c}_{t})}^{\gamma}}{\alpha c_{t}}\bigg)^{\sigma-1}e^{-\theta t}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\bigg(\frac{{(\alpha h^{h_{0},c}_{t})}^{\gamma}}{\alpha c_{t}}\bigg)^{\sigma-1}e^{-\theta t}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]=\alpha^{(1-\gamma)(1-\sigma)}V(k_{0},h_{0}).

The proof is then complete.
∎

Since the objective functional
J
in (
9
) is nonpositive, by the definition (
10
),
V\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
, as well. Recalling our standing Assumption
2.1
, the following proposition gives a lower bound for
V
. In particular, we deduce that
V(k_{0},h_{0})\in(-\infty,0]
(i.e.,
V(k_{0},h_{0})
is finite) for every
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C=C(\sigma,\gamma,\theta,\rho,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},B)>0
such that

V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq-C\bigg(\left(\frac{h_{0}^{\gamma}}{k_{0}}\right)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{k_{0}^{(1-\gamma)(\sigma-1)}}\bigg),\quad(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.

(22)

Proof.

Fix
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
and consider the control
c_{t}=Bk_{0}\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1,t}),\,t\geq 0
. By (
3
),

k_{t}^{k_{0},c}=:k_{t}=k_{0}\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1,t})=\frac{c_{t}}{B}>0,\quad t\geq 0.

Since
B\leq R
by Assumption
2.1
, the constraint (
7
) is fulfilled. Thus, also recalling Lemma
2.3
, we have
c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
. Using the explicit expressions of the habit process
h
in (
4
) and the Doléans-Dade exponential, by (
10
),

\displaystyle V(k_{0},h_{0})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{(Bk_{0})^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}e^{(1-\sigma)\big[(\gamma\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))t+\beta_{1}W_{1,t}-\gamma\beta_{2}W_{2,t}\big]}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\Big(h_{0}+\rho Bk_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s+\beta_{1}W_{1,s}-\beta_{2}W_{2,s}}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle\eqqcolon-\frac{(Bk_{0})^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}_{t}\times\mathbf{II}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg].

By Tonelli’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, it then follows that

V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq-\frac{(Bk_{0})^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbf{I}_{t}|^{p}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathbf{II}_{t}|^{q}\big]\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathrm{d}t,

(23)

where we pick
q>2
such that
q\gamma(\sigma-1)>1
, and
p={q}{(q-1)^{-1}}
. In particular,
q>2
entails
p<q
.
Since
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are independent Brownian motions and, for every
t>0
, the random variables
e^{\beta_{1}W_{1,t}}
and
e^{-\gamma\beta_{2}W_{2,t}}
have a
\log
-normal distribution,

\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{I}_{t}|^{p}]=\exp\left\{p(1-\sigma)\left(\gamma\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})+\frac{\theta}{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{2}p(1-\sigma)(\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\beta_{2}^{2})\right)t\right\},\quad t\geq 0.

As for
\mathbf{II}
, defining
q^{*}=q\gamma(\sigma-1)>1
and denoting by
p^{*}
its Hölder conjugate, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{II}_{t}|^{q}]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q^{*}-1}\bigg(h_{0}^{q^{*}}+(\rho Bk_{0})^{q^{*}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p^{*}(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q^{*}}{p^{*}}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}e^{q^{*}(\beta_{1}W_{1,s}-\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle=2^{q^{*}-1}\bigg(h_{0}^{q^{*}}+(\rho Bk_{0})^{q^{*}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p^{*}(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q^{*}}{p^{*}}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{1}{2}(q^{*})^{2}(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2})s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q^{*}-1}\bigg(h_{0}^{q^{*}}+C_{1}k_{0}^{q^{*}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p^{*}(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{q^{*}}{p^{*}}}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}(q^{*})^{2}(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2})t\right\}\bigg),\quad t\geq 0,

for some constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\rho,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},q^{\ast},B)>0
.
Here, in the first estimate we apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p^{*}
and
q^{*}
. Plugging the two previous estimates on
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{II}
into (
23
) and recalling that
q^{*}/q=\gamma(\sigma-1),
using also that the power
x^{\frac{1}{q}}
is subadditive on
[0,\infty),
we obtain

\displaystyle V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq-C_{2}\,k_{0}^{1-\sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{(1-\sigma)\left(\gamma\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})+\frac{\theta}{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{2}p(1-\sigma)(\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\beta_{2}^{2})\right)t\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg(h_{0}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}+k_{0}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p^{*}(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma(\sigma-1)}{p^{*}}}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{2}q\gamma^{2}(\sigma-1)^{2}(\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2})t\right\}\bigg)\mathrm{d}t,

(24)

for a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(\sigma,q,\rho,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},q^{\ast},B)>0.

From now on, we denote by
C_{i},\,i=3,4,\dots,
positive constants possibly depending on the parameters
(\sigma,q,\rho,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},p^{\ast},q^{\ast},B)
.
We now argue that the integral on the right-hand side of (
24
) converges thanks to Assumption
2.1
. To do this, we distinguish two cases.

If
\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})\leq 0
, then

\int_{0}^{t}e^{p^{*}(\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}))s}\mathrm{d}s\leq 1+t,\quad t\geq 0.

Hence, recalling that
q>p
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
, we deduce from (
24
) that

\displaystyle V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq-C_{3}\bigg(\left(\frac{h_{0}^{\gamma}}{k_{0}}\right)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{k_{0}^{(1-\gamma)(\sigma-1)}}\bigg)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(1+t^{\frac{\gamma(\sigma-1)}{p^{*}}}\Big)\exp\left\{(\sigma-1)\,e_{1}(q)\,t\right\}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\qquad\text{where }e_{1}(q)\coloneqq-\gamma\rho-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})-\frac{\theta}{\sigma-1}+q(\sigma-1)(\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\beta_{2}^{2}).

Condition (
11
) enables us to find a
q>\max\left\{2,\frac{1}{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\right\}
such that
e_{1}(q)<0
,
whence (
22
).

In the case
\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{2}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2})>0
, using again the fact that
q>p
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
, from (
24
) we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq-C_{4}\bigg(\left(\frac{h_{0}^{\gamma}}{k_{0}}\right)^{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{k_{0}^{(1-\gamma)(\sigma-1)}}\bigg)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{(\sigma-1)\,e_{2}(q)\,t\right\}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\qquad\text{where }e_{2}(q)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\beta_{1}^{2}(1-\gamma)-\frac{\theta}{\sigma-1}+q(\sigma-1)(\beta_{1}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\beta_{2}^{2}).

As before, Condition (
11
) in Assumption
2.1
enables us to find a
q>\max\left\{2,\frac{1}{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\right\}
such that
e_{2}(q)<0
,
whence (
22
).
The proof is now complete.
∎

The following proposition shows the monotonicity of the value function
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
with respect to its variables.

Proposition 4.3
.

The value function
V
is nondecreasing in
k
and nonincreasing in
h
.

Proof.

We start from the monotonicity of
V
in
k
. Fix
h_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
k_{1},\,k_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that
k_{1}<k_{2}
. By the explicit expression of the capital process in (
3
),
\mathcal{A}(k_{1},h_{0})\subset\mathcal{A}(k_{2},h_{0})
. Since the objective functional
J
in (
9
) does not depend on the capital
k
, we deduce that
V(k_{1},h_{0})\leq V(k_{2},h_{0})
.

As for the monotonicity in
h
, fix
k_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
h_{1},\,h_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that
h_{1}<h_{2}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{1})=\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{2})=\mathcal{A}(k_{0})
. Moreover, from the explicit expression in (
4
), we have
h^{h_{1},c}\leq h^{h_{2},c}
,
\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
a.e., for every
c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0})
. As a result,

J(k_{0},h_{1};c)\geq J(k_{0},h_{2};c),\,c\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0}).

Taking the supremum over
\mathcal{A}(k_{0})
yields
V(k_{0},h_{1})\geq V(k_{0},h_{2})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Combining the homogeneity in Lemma
4.1
and the monotonicity in Proposition
4.3
, we now demonstrate the continuity of
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The value function
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}\to[-\infty,0]
is continuous.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.2
,
V\neq-\infty
. Fix an arbitrary point
P_{0}=(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
. Note that, by Lemma
4.1
, the separate continuity of
V(k_{0},\cdot)
at
h_{0}
, i.e.,

\lim_{h\to h_{0}}V(k_{0},h)=V(k_{0},h_{0}),

(25)

is sufficient to deduce the joint continuity of
V
at
P_{0}
. Indeed, given two sequences
(h_{n})_{n},(k_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}
which converge to
h_{0}
and
k_{0}
, respectively, by (
21
) and (
25
),

V(k_{n},h_{n})=V\left(\frac{k_{n}}{k_{0}}k_{0},\frac{k_{n}}{k_{0}}\frac{k_{0}}{k_{n}}h_{n}\right)=\left(\frac{k_{n}}{k_{0}}\right)^{(1-\gamma)(1-\sigma)}V\left(k_{0},\frac{k_{0}}{k_{n}}h_{n}\right)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}V(k_{0},h_{0}).

We then focus on (
25
). Since
V
is nonincreasing with respect to
h
by Proposition
4.3
,

\lim_{h\to h_{0}^{-}}V(k_{0},h)\geq V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq\lim_{h\to h_{0}^{+}}V(k_{0},h).

Thus, we only need to show the reversed chain of inequalities, starting from

\lim_{h\to h_{0}^{+}}V(k_{0},h)\geq V(k_{0},h_{0}).

(26)

Consider
(h_{n})_{n}\subset(h_{0},\infty)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=h_{0}
. For every
\epsilon>0
, by (
10
), there exists an
\epsilon-
optimal control
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{0})
satisfying

V(k_{0},h_{0})\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-\sigma}e^{-\theta t}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{t}^{\sigma-1}}\big({h}_{t}^{h_{0},\bar{c}}\big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]+\epsilon.

From Proposition
4.2
the value function is finite. It then follows that

e^{-\theta t}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{t}^{\sigma-1}}\big({h}_{t}^{h_{0},\bar{c}}\big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\in L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

(27)

Since
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{n})=\mathcal{A}(k_{0})
by Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle V(k_{0},h_{n})-V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{\bar{c}_{t}^{\sigma-1}}\bigg(\Big({h}^{h_{n},\bar{c}}_{t}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}-\Big({h}^{h_{0},\bar{c}}_{t}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\bigg)\mathrm{d}t\bigg]-\epsilon,

which holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The explicit expression in (
4
) implies that
{h}^{h_{n},\bar{c}}
converges to
{h}^{h_{0},\bar{c}}
locally uniformly on
[0,\infty)
,
\mathbb{P}-
a.s., and there exists a constant
C>1
such that

0\leq{h}^{h_{n},\bar{c}}\leq C{h}^{h_{0},\bar{c}},\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-\text{a.e.}

Therefore, by (
27
), the dominated convergence theorem yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}V(k_{0},h_{n})\geq V(k_{0},h_{0})-\epsilon.

This estimate gives (
26
) as
\epsilon
is arbitrary.

Concerning
\lim_{h\to h_{0}^{-}}V(k_{0},h)
, we suppose by contradiction that

\lim_{h\to h_{0}^{-}}V(k_{0},h)>V(k_{0},h_{0}).

(28)

By (
10
), we consider a sequence
(h_{n})_{n}\subset(0,h_{0})
which converges to
h_{0}
and corresponding admissible controls
c_{n}\in\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{n})
such that

J(k_{0},h_{n};c_{n})>V(k_{0},h_{n})-\frac{1}{n},\,n\in\mathbb{N},\text{\quad whence, by (\ref{assurdo}), }\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(k_{0},h_{n};c_{n})>V(k_{0},h_{0}).

(29)

Note that (
29
) combined with (
4
) and (
9
) entails that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\mathcal{E}(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t})^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{n,s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]<\infty.

(30)

Fix a generic
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
h_{n}<h_{0}
, the modified control
\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}(k_{0},h_{n}).
We then consider
J\Big(k_{0},h_{n};\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}\Big)
and aim to estimate the difference

\Delta_{n}\coloneqq J\Big(k_{0},h_{n};\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}\Big)-J(k_{0},h_{n};c_{n}).

In particular, by (
9
) we compute

\displaystyle|\Delta_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\bigg(\left(\frac{h_{0}}{h_{n}}\right)^{\sigma-1}-1\bigg)\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\Big(h_{t}^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\Big|\Big(h_{t}^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}-\Big(h_{t}^{h_{n},c_{n}}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\Big|\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\eqqcolon\mathbf{I}_{n}+\mathbf{II}_{n}.

(31)

By (
4
),

h_{t}^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}=\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}h_{t}^{h_{0},c_{n}},\quad t\geq 0,

(32)

whence, considering also that the sequence
(J(k_{0},h_{0};c_{n}))_{n}
is bounded by (
30
),

\mathbf{I}_{n}\leq\bigg(\left(\frac{h_{0}}{h_{n}}\right)^{\sigma-1}-1\bigg)\bigg(\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}\bigg)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|J(k_{0},h_{0};c_{n})|<\frac{1}{2n},\quad\text{for $n$ sufficiently large.}

As for
\mathbf{II}_{n}
, we distinguish two cases:
\gamma(\sigma-1)\leq 1
and
\gamma(\sigma-1)>1
. In the former, i.e.,
\gamma(\sigma-1)\leq 1
, we notice that the power function
x^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}
is
\gamma(\sigma-1)-
Hölder continuous on
[0,\infty)
. Denoting by
C_{1}
its Hölder-continuity constant, by (
4
) and (
30
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sigma-1}\left(1-\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}\right)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\mathcal{E}(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t})^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\left(\rho\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{n,s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(1-\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}\right)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|J(k_{0},h_{0};c_{n})|\leq\frac{1}{2n},\quad\text{for $n$ sufficiently large}.

In the latter case
\gamma(\sigma-1)>1
, recalling that
h_{n}<h_{0}
, the mean value theorem applied to the map
x^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}
yields the existence of a stochastic process
\xi_{n}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\xi_{n}\in\big(h^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}},h^{h_{n},c_{n}}\big)

and

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(h^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}-\Big(h^{h_{n},c_{n}}\Big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\Big|

\displaystyle=\gamma(\sigma-1)\xi_{n}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\Big(h^{h_{n},c_{n}}-h^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(\sigma-1)\big(h^{h_{n},c_{n}}\big)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\Big(h^{h_{n},c_{n}}-h^{h_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}}\Big),

where in the final step we use that
x^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Thanks to this inequality and (
4
), since
(x+y)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\leq\max\{1,2^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-2}\}(x^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}+y^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1})
for every
x,y\geq 0,

\mathbf{II}_{n}\leq\gamma\max\{1,2^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-2}\}\bigg(1-\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\mathcal{E}(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t})^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\big(\mathbf{II}_{n,1,t}+\mathbf{II}_{n,2,t}\big)\mathrm{d}t\bigg],

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{n,1,t}\coloneqq\rho h_{n}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{n,s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg),\quad\mathbf{II}_{n,2,t}\coloneqq\bigg(\rho\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{n,s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}.

Observing that

\mathbf{II}_{n,1,t}\leq h_{0}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\big(1+\mathbf{II}_{n,2,t}\big),

by (
30
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\max\{1,2^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-2}\}\max\big\{1,h_{0}^{\gamma(\sigma-1)-1}\big\}\bigg(1-\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{c_{n,t}^{\sigma-1}}\mathcal{E}(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t})^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\bigg(1+2\bigg(\rho\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{n,s}}{\mathcal{E}(-\rho s+\beta_{2}W_{2,s})}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\gamma(\sigma-1)}\,\bigg)\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2n},\quad\text{for $n$ sufficiently large}.

Plugging the estimates we have just established on
\mathbf{I}_{n}
and
\mathbf{II}_{n}
into (
31
) gives
|\Delta_{n}|<\frac{1}{n}
, for
n
sufficiently large. Thus, by (
29
),

\liminf_{n\to\infty}J\Big(k_{0},h_{n};\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}\Big)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(k_{0},h_{n};c_{n})+\lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta_{n}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(k_{0},h_{n};c_{n})>V(k_{0},h_{0}).

At the same time, recalling (
9
) and (
32
),

J\Big(k_{0},h_{n};\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}\Big)=\left(\frac{h_{0}}{h_{n}}\right)^{(1-\gamma)(\sigma-1)}J(k_{0},h_{0};c_{n})\leq\left(\frac{h_{0}}{h_{n}}\right)^{(1-\gamma)(\sigma-1)}V(k_{0},h_{0}),

whence
\liminf_{n\to\infty}J\Big(k_{0},h_{n};\frac{h_{n}}{h_{0}}c_{n}\Big)\leq V(k_{0},h_{0})
.
This is a contradiction demonstrating that (
28
) cannot hold. Consequently,
V(k_{0},\cdot)
is also left-continuous at
h_{0}
and the proof is complete.
∎

5
Studying the HJB Equation

In this section, we show that the value function
V
solves the HJB equation (
15
) both in the viscosity and the classical sense. In particular, we demonstrate that
V
is a regular map in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++};\mathbb{R}).

5.1
V
is a Viscosity Solution of the HJB Equation

We begin this subsection by recalling the definition of (continuous)
viscosity solutions
of the HJB equation (
15
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
D
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
. A continuous function
v:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
viscosity subsolution
(resp.,
viscosity supersolution
) of (
15
) in
D
if, for any
\psi\in C^{2}(D;\mathbb{R})
and any local maximum point
(k_{M},h_{M})\in D
(resp., local minimum point
(k_{m},h_{m})\in D
) of
v-\psi
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\theta v(k_{M},h_{M})-H_{\text{max}}(k_{M},h_{M},\nabla\psi(k_{M},h_{M}),D^{2}\psi(k_{M},h_{M}))

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle(\text{resp., }\theta v(k_{m},h_{m})-H_{\text{max}}(k_{m},h_{m},\nabla\psi(k_{m},h_{m}),D^{2}\psi(k_{m},h_{m}))

\displaystyle\geq 0).

A continuous function
v:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
viscosity solution
of (
15
) in
D
if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution in
D
.

In the next theorem, adapting in a nontrivial way some standard arguments in, e.g.,
Choulli et al. (2003)
;
Di Giacinto et al. (2011)
, we prove that the value function
V
defined in (
10
) solves (
15
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
in the viscosity sense.

Theorem 5.2
.

The value function
V
is a viscosity solution of the HJB equation (
15
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
.

Proof.

We structure the proof in two steps. In the first (resp., second) step, we demonstrate that
V
is a viscosity supersolution (resp., viscosity subsolution) of (
15
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
.

Step I
. Consider
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2};\mathbb{R})
and a local minimum point
(k_{m},h_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
for
V-\psi
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
V(k_{m},h_{m})=\psi(k_{m},h_{m})
, hence there exists
\delta>0
such that
V(k,h)\geq\psi(k,h)
,
(k,h)\in B_{\delta}(k_{m},h_{m})\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}
.
Fix a constant
\bar{c}\in(0,Rk_{m})
and define the stopping time

\bar{\tau}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:k^{k_{m},\bar{c}}_{t}\leq\frac{\bar{c}}{R}\right\};

observe that
\bar{\tau}>0,\,\mathbb{P}-
a.s., by the continuity of
k^{k_{m},\bar{c}}
. We now employ
\bar{\tau}
to construct the following positive progressively measurable process
c_{\nu}^{\ast},\,\nu>0
:

c^{*}_{\nu,t}=\begin{cases}\bar{c},&t\leq\bar{\tau},\\
\nu\big(\mathcal{E}(B\bar{\tau}+\beta_{1}W_{1,\bar{\tau}})\big)^{-1}\mathcal{E}(\beta_{1}W_{1,t}),&t>\bar{\tau}.\end{cases}

(34)

Notice that, choosing
\nu
sufficiently small,
c_{\nu}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}(k_{m},h_{m})
. Indeed, by the definition of
\bar{\tau}
and (
3
), on the event
\{\bar{\tau}<\infty\}

\frac{\bar{c}}{R}\big(\mathcal{E}(B\bar{\tau}+\beta_{1}W_{1,\bar{\tau}})\big)^{-1}=\big(\mathcal{E}(B\bar{\tau}+\beta_{1}W_{1,\bar{\tau}})\big)^{-1}k^{k_{m},c^{*}_{\nu}}_{\bar{\tau}}=k_{m}-\bar{c}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathcal{E}(Bs+\beta_{1}W_{1,s})}.

Consequently, for every
t>\bar{\tau}
, using (
34

\displaystyle k^{k_{m},c^{*}_{\nu}}_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\theta t}\Big(g\big(c_{t};k_{t},h_{t},\nabla v(k_{t},h_{t})\big)-G\big(k_{t},h_{t},\nabla v(k_{t},h_{t})\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}t\bigg].

(48)

By the hypothesis in (
44
), again by the monotone convergence theorem as
T\to\infty
,

J(k_{0},h_{0};c)=v(k_{0},h_{0})+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}\Big(g\big(c_{t};k_{t},h_{t},\nabla v(k_{t},h_{t})\big)-G\big(k_{t},h_{t},\nabla v(k_{t},h_{t})\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}t\bigg].

(49)

In particular,

v(k_{0},h_{0})\geq J(k_{0},h_{0};c).

Since

is arbitrary, we can take the supremum over
\mathcal{A}^{\circ}(k_{0},h_{0})
in this estimate to establish (
45

(k_{0},h_{0})
is a generic point in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
.

If we have an admissible control

satisfying (
46

and

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta t}\Big(g\big(c^{\ast}_{t};k^{\ast}_{t},h^{\ast}_{t},\nabla v(k^{\ast}_{t},h^{\ast}_{t})\big)-G\big(k^{\ast}_{t},h^{\ast}_{t},\nabla v(k^{\ast}_{t},h^{\ast}_{t})\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}t\bigg]=0.

Consequently, by (

V(k_{0},h_{0})\geq J(k_{0},h_{0};c^{\ast})=v(k_{0},h_{0})\geq V(k_{0},h_{0}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.5

We conclude this paper by establishing the existence of a unique optimal control for our problem. This control will be given in feedback form.

Theorem 6.6
.

Define the feedback map
\Gamma_{V}\colon\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by
\Gamma_{V}(k,h)=\Gamma(k,h,\nabla V(k,h)),
where
\Gamma
is given in Lemma
3.1
and
V
is the value function. Then for every
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
the closed loop equations (CLEs)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}k^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}=\big(Bk^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}-\Gamma_{V}(k^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t},h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t})\big)\mathrm{d}t+\beta_{1}k^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{1,t},\quad k_{0}>0,

(51)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}=\rho\big(\Gamma_{V}(k^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t},h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t})-h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}\big)\mathrm{d}t+\beta_{2}h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{2,t},\quad h_{0}>0,

(52)

admit a pathwise unique solution with strictly positive components.

Furthermore, the process
c^{\ast}
defined by
c^{\ast}_{t}=\Gamma_{V}(k^{k_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t},h^{h_{0},\Gamma_{V}}_{t})
belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{\circ}(k_{0},h_{0})
and is the unique optimal control at
(k_{0},h_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}.

Proof.

From the linear growth of
\Gamma
established in Lemma
3.1
, we infer that the drifts of the CLEs have linear growth on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
. Moreover, by the regularity of
V
established in Theorem
5.4
and the explicit expression of
\Gamma
in (
14
), it follows immediately that the drifts are also locally Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{++}
. Since the diffusion coefficients are linear, by, e.g.,
(
Durrett, 1996
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2, Chapter 5)
and a standard localization argument, we conclude that the CLEs admit a pathwise unique solution
(k^{\ast},h^{\ast})
up to
\tau^{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf\{t\geq 0,k^{\ast}_{t}\wedge h^{\ast}_{t}=1/n\}
.
We now focus on the positivity of
(k^{\ast},h^{\ast})
.
Let
c^{\ast}_{t}=1_{\{t<\tau^{*}\}}\Gamma_{V}(k^{\ast}_{t},h^{\ast}_{t})
; since
0<c^{\ast}_{t}\leq Rk^{\ast}_{t}
on
[0,\tau^{\ast})
, defining by

N_{t}=\mathcal{E}\big((B-R)t+\beta_{1}W_{1,t}\big)^{-1}
for

,
by the stochastic product rule

\mathrm{d}\big(k^{\ast}_{t}N_{t}\big)=\Big(N_{t}\big(Bk^{\ast}_{t}-c^{\ast}_{t}\big)+k^{\ast}_{t}N_{t}\big((R-B)+\beta_{1}^{2}\big)-\beta_{1}^{2}k^{\ast}_{t}N_{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}t=N_{t}\big(Rk^{\ast}_{t}-c^{\ast}_{t}\big)\mathrm{d}t\geq 0,\quad t\in[0,\tau^{\ast}),

hence
k_{t}^{\ast}\geq k_{0}N_{t}^{-1}
for
t\in[0,\tau^{\ast})
. Analogously, letting
H_{t}=\mathcal{E}\big(-\rho t+\beta_{2}W_{2,t}\big)^{-1},

\mathrm{d}(h^{\ast}_{t}H_{t})=\Big(\rho H_{t}(c^{\ast}_{t}-h^{\ast}_{t})+(\rho+\beta_{2}^{2})h^{\ast}_{t}H_{t}-\beta_{2}^{2}h^{\ast}_{t}H_{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}t=\rho H_{t}c^{\ast}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0,\quad t\in[0,\tau^{\ast}),

hence

h_{t}^{\ast}\geq h_{0}H_{t}^{-1}
for
t\in[0,\tau^{\ast})
. Therefore we infer that
\tau^{\ast}=\infty,\,\mathbb{P}-
a.s. This proves that
(k^{\ast},h^{\ast})
is the pathwise unique solution of (
51
)-(
52
) on
[0,\infty).

The previous arguments show that

In fact,

, so

. To see this, one can proceed as in the proof of Theorem
6.3
(with
V
instead of
v
) to infer that, recalling that
V
is nonpositive and finite by Proposition
4.2
, (cf. (
48

\displaystyle J(k_{0},h_{0};c^{\ast})=\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{c^{\ast}_{t}}{(h^{\ast}_{t})^{\gamma}}\right)^{1-\sigma}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\limsup_{T\to\infty}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\theta{T}}V(k^{\ast}_{T},h^{\ast}_{T})\Big]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{c^{\ast}_{t}}{(h^{\ast}_{t})^{\gamma}}\right)^{1-\sigma}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]\bigg)=V(k_{0},h_{0})>-\infty,

where for the last equality we use the definition of

. This estimate shows that

is optimal at
(k_{0},h_{0}).
The same conclusion follows from the general sufficient condition in Theorem
6.3

, Proposition

As for the uniqueness of

49

then yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!e^{-\theta t}\Big(

\displaystyle g\big(c^{\ast}_{1,t};k^{k_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},h^{h_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},\nabla V(k^{k_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},h^{h_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t})\big)-G\big(k^{k_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},h^{h_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},\!\nabla V(k^{k_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t},h^{h_{0},c_{1}^{\ast}}_{t})\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}t\bigg]=0,

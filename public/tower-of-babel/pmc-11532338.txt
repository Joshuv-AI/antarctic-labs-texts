Introduction

The mixed metric dimension of a graph is a vital parameter in characterizing its structural complexity, particularly in nanoscale networks where precision is paramount. In this study, we explore the mixed metric dimension of hexagonal nano-networks, a structure with significant applications in nanotechnology and material science. Through rigorous analysis, we establish that the mixed metric dimension of the hexagonal nano-network is precisely 3, highlighting its minimal yet sufficient resolving set that uniquely identifies all vertices. Furthermore, we investigate the exchange property within this context, demonstrating the robust adaptability of the hexagonal network’s resolving sets. Our findings reveal that the exchange property is not only preserved but enhanced in these nano-networks, allowing for flexible adjustments in resolving sets without compromising the network’s integrity. This study provides crucial insights into the fundamental properties of hexagonal nano-networks, offering a theoretical foundation for future research in nanomaterial design and optimization. The results underscore the potential of leveraging mixed metric dimensions and exchange properties to achieve efficient and scalable solutions in nano-network applications

Limitations and advantages of this framework

For hexagonal nano-networks, the mixed metric dimension and exchange property architecture provides several benefits, including fault tolerance, thorough network analysis, and optimization. When using this framework, one must take into account its computational complexity, specialization needs, and potential scalability concerns. To effectively use the framework in research and real-world applications, these elements must be balanced. It enables accurate identification of vertices with minimal information, ensuring efficient network analysis even as the network scales. Additionally, the exchange property adds flexibility, allowing for the adjustment of resolving sets without compromising the network’s integrity. This combination of accuracy, scalability, and robustness makes the model highly valuable for both theoretical exploration and practical applications in nanotechnology and material science.

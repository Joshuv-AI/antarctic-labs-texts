DNMT1 is associated with QDPR downregulation and chemotherapy response in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Lyubo Wang, Guicai Liang, Lei Zhu, Zurui Liu, Yuxian Dong, Kun Wang, Hong Yao, Chunlei Ge, Lincan Duan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), DNA methylation-mediated gene silencing may contribute to tumor initiation, progression, and heterogeneity in treatment response. However, the key methyltransferases involved and their therapeutic potential have not been systematically characterized. This study aimed to address three major questions: which methylation-associated gene silencing events occur in LUAD, which upstream methyltransferase predominantly drives these events, and whether targeted inhibition of this enzyme can mitigate drug-resistant phenotypes and enhance chemosensitivity. Differential expression analysis was initially performed using the GSE75037 dataset to screen candidate genes. Expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) and protein quantitative trait locus (pQTL) data were then integrated, and key candidate genes were prioritized according to the concordance in the directions of genetic effects. Subsequently, mediation Mendelian randomization analysis was conducted to determine whether the effect of locus-specific methylation on LUAD was mediated by QDPR expression, thereby providing genetic causal evidence that methylation contributes to transcriptional repression. In addition, the associations between DNA methylation-related enzymes and QDPR expression were analyzed in the GSE33532, GSE43458, and GSE75037 datasets, followed by cross-dataset validation to identify potential key upstream regulators. Finally, cell-based experiments were performed to verify the functional effects of DNMT1 inhibition. Changes in QDPR expression were assessed by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and Western blotting, and the potential chemosensitizing effect of DNMT1 inhibition in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy was further evaluated in patient-derived organoids. This study provided genetic causal evidence that locus-specific methylation influences LUAD risk, with part of this effect mediated by QDPR expression. Analyses across multiple independent datasets consistently suggested that DNMT1 may act as a key upstream epigenetic regulator contributing to the reduced expression of QDPR. Functional experiments demonstrated that DNMT1 inhibition increased QDPR expression and suppressed the proliferation and migration of LUAD cells. Drug sensitivity assays using patient-derived organoids further showed that DNMT1 inhibition exerted a significant chemosensitizing effect in the platinum low-sensitivity group. This study suggests that DNMT1 may contribute to the reduced expression of QDPR and related malignant phenotypes through epigenetic regulation. Functional and drug sensitivity experiments further support the potential of DNMT1 as a therapeutic target for improving the response to platinum-based chemotherapy in a subset of patients with LUAD. These findings provide a potential therapeutic target and offer mechanistic insights into treatment response heterogeneity and chemoresistance in LUAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12032-026-03360-0.

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is one of the most common subtypes of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1, 2]. Although targeted therapy and immunotherapy have achieved substantial advances in specific molecular subgroups in recent years, platinum-based chemotherapy remains a cornerstone of treatment for patients with LUAD across different disease stages [3, 4]. However, marked heterogeneity in chemotherapy response persists among patients, and drug resistance together with suboptimal therapeutic efficacy continues to limit survival benefits [5]. Therefore, systematically elucidating the key molecular mechanisms underlying chemotherapy response in LUAD and identifying potential targets for combination therapy and chemosensitization are of considerable biological and clinical significance.

DNA methylation is a major epigenetic regulatory mechanism and plays a critical role in the development of chemotherapy resistance in cancer [6–8]. Previous studies have shown that aberrant promoter hypermethylation can lead to the transcriptional silencing of genes involved in drug uptake, DNA damage repair, apoptosis, and drug efflux [9–11]. Such alterations may attenuate the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy and promote the emergence of drug-resistant phenotypes [12–14]. Although methylation abnormalities have been widely implicated in the development of drug resistance across multiple cancer types, the causal role of methylation-mediated gene silencing in chemotherapy resistance, as well as its upstream regulatory network in LUAD, remains to be fully elucidated [15, 16].

This study integrated publicly available multi-omics datasets to systematically identify candidate genes aberrantly expressed in LUAD and supported by causal evidence. Mediation Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was subsequently performed to determine whether these genes exhibited expression silencing consistent with methylation-driven regulation [17]. Based on these findings, the potential key upstream methyltransferase was further investigated. Cell-based experiments were conducted to validate the biological functions of the identified pathway, and drug sensitivity assays using patient-derived organoids (PDOs) from LUAD were performed to evaluate the potential translational value of targeting this pathway for chemosensitization. Overall, this study aimed to elucidate the causal mechanism of methylation-associated downregulation in LUAD, identify potential key upstream regulatory factors, and assess the therapeutic potential of targeting this methylation regulatory axis, thereby providing new insights and potential targets for epigenetic research and precision treatment in LUAD.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study primarily involved multi-omics data integration and causal inference analyses, identification of upstream DNA methyltransferases, as well as in vitro functional experiments and drug sensitivity validation using PDOs.

First, differential expression analysis was performed using the GSE75037 dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Candidate genes were identified by integrating expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL), protein quantitative trait locus (pQTL), and Mendelian randomization (MR) evidence related to LUAD. pQTL data were incorporated only at the candidate gene prioritization stage and were not used as direct evidence for the DNMT1-QDPR regulatory mechanism. Subsequently, methylation quantitative trait locus (mQTL) and eQTL data were integrated, and mediation MR analysis was performed to assess the causal effect of DNA methylation on gene expression, thereby identifying methylation-associated downregulation events.

Furthermore, the consistency of the associations between members of the DNA methyltransferase family and the methylation and expression levels of the target gene was evaluated across multiple independent datasets to identify the key upstream methyltransferase. Finally, in vitro cell experiments and drug sensitivity assays using PDOs were performed to assess the effects of targeting this methylation regulator on malignant tumor cell phenotypes and chemotherapy response. GWAS datasets used in the MR analysis

Differential gene expression analysis

The GSE75037 dataset was imported and preprocessed using R software (version 4.3.1). After within-dataset normalization, differential expression analysis was performed. Genes with P < 0.05 and |logFC| > 0.585 were defined as differentially expressed genes (DEGs) [21]. Volcano plots and heatmaps were generated to visualize the identified DEGs.

Selection of GWAS data

In this study, the source information for all GWAS datasets is summarized in Table 2. The R package “TwoSampleMR” was used to identify single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) strongly associated with the instrumental variables at a threshold of P < 5 × 10⁻⁸ [22]. During the analysis, the linkage disequilibrium parameters were set at r² = 0.001 and kb = 10,000. In addition, the F statistic was calculated for each SNP, and SNPs with F < 10 were excluded to minimize weak instrument bias [23]. The LUAD outcome data were obtained from the FinnGen database (release R9).

Mendelian randomization analysis

The MR analysis primarily used the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method to investigate the potential causal association between the exposure and the outcome [24, 25]. In addition, MR-Egger regression, the simple mode, weighted median, and weighted mode methods were applied as complementary approaches to improve the robustness of the results. To further assess the reliability of the findings, tests for horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity, as well as sensitivity analyses, were performed [26]. Leave-one-out analysis was conducted as part of the sensitivity assessment. Heterogeneity was evaluated using Cochran’s Q test, with P < 0.05 indicating significant heterogeneity, whereas horizontal pleiotropy was assessed using the MR-Egger intercept and the MR-PRESSO global test [27]. Finally, scatter plots, forest plots, and funnel plots were generated to visualize the MR results and provide an intuitive presentation of the causal effects and their distribution patterns.

Correlation analysis

Pearson correlation analysis was performed in GSE75037 to evaluate the associations between quinoid dihydropteridine reductase (QDPR) expression and the mRNA expression levels of methylation-related regulatory enzymes, including the DNA methyltransferase (DNMT), ten-eleven translocation (TET), and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) families. The analysis was subsequently repeated in GSE33532 and GSE43458. Candidate enzymes showing consistent correlation directions and statistical significance across datasets were identified by intersection and considered potential upstream methylation regulators of QDPR. The overlap among results from different datasets was visualized using a Venn diagram.

Cell culture and treatment

The human LUAD cell lines A549 and PC-9 were obtained from an authenticated cell repository. Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ [28, 29]. For functional assays, cells were treated with the DNMT1 inhibitor SW155246 (MedChemExpress, MCE; catalog no. HY-123346) at a concentration of 5 µM for 24 h. Vehicle-treated cells were used as controls. SW155246 was selected because it targets DNMT1 and is suitable for evaluating the effects of DNMT1 inhibition on QDPR expression and LUAD cell phenotypes.

Wound healing assay

Cell migratory capacity was assessed using a wound healing assay. A549 and PC-9 cells were seeded into 6-well plates. When cell confluence reached approximately 90%, a straight scratch was created across the cell monolayer using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip. After gentle washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove detached cells, serum-free medium was added. Images of the wound area were captured at 0, 24, and 48 h under an inverted microscope, and the wound closure rate was quantified using ImageJ software.

Colony formation assay

A549 and PC-9 cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 500–800 cells per well and cultured for 10–14 days, with the medium changed every 3 days. After incubation, colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Colony numbers were quantified using ImageJ software.

Transwell migration assay

Cells (2 × 10⁴ per well) were resuspended in serum-free medium and seeded into the upper chamber, while medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After incubation at 37 °C for 24 h, non-migrated cells on the upper surface of the membrane were gently removed with a cotton swab. The migrated cells on the lower surface were then fixed and stained with crystal violet, and the number of migrated cells was quantified using ImageJ software.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells using the TRIzol method, and reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a reverse transcription kit (Vazyme, Q511-03). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed using the SYBR Green method to determine the mRNA expression level of quinoid dihydropteridine reductase (QDPR). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as the internal control, and relative expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates. The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Western blot assay

Total cellular protein was extracted using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors, and protein concentrations were determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking with 5% nonfat milk for 1 h, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against DNMT1 (Biodragon, RM8506), QDPR (Biodragon, BD-PB2629), and GAPDH (Biodragon, B3029). The membranes were then incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein expression levels were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) method.

Drug sensitivity assay using patient-derived organoids

Tumor tissues were collected from patients with LUAD who underwent surgical resection. Following digestion with a tissue dissociation solution for approximately 20 min, the cells were embedded in growth factor-reduced Matrigel and cultured in organoid culture medium. Once PDOs exhibited stable growth, they were harvested and seeded into plates for subsequent drug treatment.

Chemotherapy drugs combined with the corresponding solvent treatment were used in the control group, whereas the experimental group received the DNMT1 inhibitor in combination with chemotherapy drugs. For PDO combination treatment, SW155246 was used at 5 µM for 24 h together with the indicated chemotherapy drugs. The organoids were then cultured for an additional 24 h. Organoid viability was subsequently assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (ZETA, K009-1000) [30, 31]. Based on the absorbance values, dose-response curves were generated using GraphPad Prism 9, and the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was calculated.

Results

Identification of key genes

A total of 6,754 DEGs were identified in the GSE75037 dataset (P < 0.05, |logFC| > 0.585) (Fig. 2A). After intersecting these DEGs with eQTL and pQTL data, 462 candidate genes were obtained (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, LUAD GWAS data were used as the outcome to perform MR analysis of these candidate genes. The IVW method was adopted as the primary analytical approach, and genes with IVW P < 0.05 were retained. These genes were then further filtered according to the consistency between the direction of genetically predicted expression and the observed expression changes in tumor tissues (Fig. 2C, D).

The results showed that QDPR, SIGLEC9, and TNFAIP8 were consistently downregulated in LUAD tissues, and their genetically predicted expression levels were significantly inversely associated with LUAD risk (Fig. 3). The P values for the heterogeneity test, pleiotropy test, and MR-PRESSO global test were all greater than 0.05 (Table 3), indicating no evidence of substantial heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy and supporting the robustness of the MR results.

Mediation MR analysis

To evaluate the mediating role of DNA methylation in the association between key gene expression and LUAD risk, a mediation MR framework was applied (Fig. 4). The results showed that, among the three candidate genes, only QDPR exhibited a significant mediation effect involving the methylation site cg26689483. CpG annotation indicated that cg26689483 is located in the promoter region of QDPR, supporting its potential relevance to QDPR transcriptional regulation. The indirect effect (a × b) was 0.0745 (P = 0.011), the direct effect (c′) was 0.0429, and the total effect (c) was 0.1174, with the methylation-mediated component accounting for 63.46% of the total effect. No statistically significant mediation effects were observed for SIGLEC9 or TNFAIP8 [32]. Corresponding mQTL-based MR analyses were also conducted for SIGLEC9 and TNFAIP8. None of the CpG sites remained significant after FDR correction, although cg26305729 for TNFAIP8 showed a nominal association with LUAD risk (Supplementary Table S3).

Expression validation and functional experiments of DNMT1 and QDPR in LUAD

To investigate the methylation-mediated regulation of QDPR, Pearson correlation analysis was performed in the GSE75037 dataset to assess the associations between QDPR expression and the mRNA levels of DNA methylation-related enzymes, including DNA methyltransferases (DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B, and DNMT3L), DNA demethylases (TET1, TET2, and TET3), and isocitrate dehydrogenases (IDH1 and IDH2). To validate the reliability of these findings, external validation was performed using the GSE33532 and GSE43458 datasets. Intersection analysis across the three datasets showed that only DNMT1 was significantly negatively correlated with QDPR (Supplementary Figure S1). These findings suggest that DNMT1 may act as an upstream epigenetic regulator of QDPR downregulation; however, whether this regulation directly promotes QDPR promoter methylation requires further verification through methylation assays and binding evidence.

To validate QDPR expression in LUAD and the regulatory role of DNMT1, QDPR levels were first assessed in tumor tissues and paired adjacent normal tissues obtained from four patients with LUAD. qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses showed that both QDPR mRNA and protein expression were significantly downregulated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 5A, B). Next, PC-9 cells were treated with a DNMT1-specific inhibitor to assess changes in DNMT1 and QDPR expression. The results showed that DNMT1 inhibition led to significant upregulation of QDPR (Fig. 5C), suggesting that DNMT1 may negatively regulate QDPR expression through a methylation-dependent mechanism. Functional experiments were then performed to investigate the effects of DNMT1 on the malignant phenotypes of LUAD cells. Colony formation assays showed that DNMT1 inhibition markedly reduced the proliferative capacity of PC-9 cells (Fig. 5D). Transwell and wound healing assays further indicated that DNMT1 inhibition significantly impaired PC-9 cell migration (Fig. 5E, F). Similar proliferation and migration experiments were also conducted in A549 cells (Supplementary Figure S2). Collectively, these results suggest that DNMT1 may suppress QDPR expression through methylation, thereby promoting the proliferation and migration of LUAD cells.

Evaluation of DNMT1 inhibitor efficacy and combination therapy potential in LUAD organoids

To evaluate the antitumor activity of the DNMT1 inhibitor, 20 LUAD PDO models were established.The genetic fidelity of the organoids was further assessed, and the results are presented in Supplementary Figures S3–S5. IC₅₀ measurements showed that the DNMT1 inhibitor exerted dose-dependent growth-inhibitory effects in six organoid models, with IC₅₀ values ranging from 21.25 to 44.86 µM (Fig. 6A).

Based on the above findings, the potential of combining the DNMT1 inhibitor with conventional chemotherapy drugs was further investigated. First, the IC₅₀ values of five clinically used chemotherapy agents were determined in 20 PDO models. According to the IC₅₀ results, the five most sensitive and five least sensitive organoids for each drug were selected (Supplementary Table S2) and treated with 5 µM DNMT1 inhibitor in combination with the corresponding chemotherapy agent to assess changes in drug sensitivity and recalculate the IC₅₀. The addition of the DNMT1 inhibitor led to a greater reduction in short-term viability in low-sensitivity organoids, particularly under platinum-based treatment, whereas limited changes were observed in some high-sensitivity organoids (Fig. 6C). It should be noted that this study evaluated short-term changes in organoid viability over 24 h; longer treatment durations may be applied in future studies to obtain more stable IC₅₀ estimates.

Discussion

The findings of this study support the presence of methylation-associated downregulation of QDPR in LUAD, which shows a genetically interpretable association with disease risk. Mediation analysis further indicated that the effect of specific methylation sites on LUAD risk is partially mediated through QDPR expression. In addition, cross-cohort correlation analyses consistently identified DNMT1 as a potential upstream regulator. In vitro inhibition of DNMT1 resulted in upregulation of QDPR and attenuation of malignant phenotypes. In PDO-based drug sensitivity assays, this intervention exerted a more pronounced chemosensitizing effect in organoid models with low platinum sensitivity. Collectively, these findings integrate genetic inference, in vitro functional experiments, and patient-derived models, highlighting the potential role of the DNMT1–QDPR epigenetic regulatory axis in shaping treatment response heterogeneity in LUAD.

QDPR is a key component of the pterin-dependent aromatic amino acid hydroxylation system and is involved in both biosynthesis and pterin metabolism [33]. It regulates the BH4/BH2 ratio, thereby affecting redox status and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, which may further influence the tumor immune microenvironment and treatment response [34]. In multiple cancer types, genes involved in metabolic homeostasis are often transcriptionally silenced by epigenetic mechanisms, promoting tumor cell survival and proliferation under adverse conditions [35, 36]. In this study, QDPR was consistently downregulated in LUAD across multiple levels, and genetic causal evidence supported its association with disease risk [37], suggesting that QDPR downregulation is not merely a passive consequence of tumor progression but may represent a selectively retained molecular feature [38].

Notably, this study used mediation MR to further clarify the potential role of DNA methylation in regulating QDPR expression [39, 40]. The results showed that a substantial proportion of the genetic effect on QDPR was indirectly mediated through specific methylation sites, suggesting that methylation is not merely a bystander event in tumorigenesis but may actively modulate QDPR expression and related phenotypes [41]. Compared with conventional correlation analysis, mediation MR can partly reduce the influence of reverse causation and confounding, thereby providing genetic causal evidence for methylation-associated downregulation [39]. This finding further supports the rationale for considering QDPR an epigenetically regulated target in functional studies.

Among DNA methylation-related enzymes, DNMT1 showed a consistent negative correlation with QDPR expression across multiple independent transcriptomic datasets [42, 43], whereas other DNMT, TET, and IDH family members did not show this pattern. This cross-cohort consistency supports DNMT1 as a potential key upstream regulator [44]. Previous studies have shown that DNMT1 mainly mediates maintenance methylation, and its aberrant activation can cause sustained silencing of tumor suppressor genes [45, 46], which is associated with aggressive biological behavior in multiple cancers [47, 48]. Notably, in this study, the regulatory role of DNMT1 was inferred mainly from expression correlation and inhibitor-based experiments. Whether DNMT1 directly promotes methylation at the QDPR promoter still requires validation by methylation sequencing or chromatin immunoprecipitation assays [49, 50].

Functional experiments showed that DNMT1 inhibition significantly suppressed LUAD cell proliferation and migration, supporting its role in maintaining malignant phenotypes [51, 52]. In PDO models, the DNMT1 inhibitor showed a stronger sensitizing effect in platinum-low-sensitivity samples, whereas no significant effect was observed in samples already highly sensitive to pemetrexed, vinorelbine, or gemcitabine. These findings suggest that epigenetic status may reinforce specific resistant phenotypes, and that targeting DNMT1 could help improve chemotherapy response in selected patient subgroups [53, 54]. Similar epigenetic combination strategies have been reported in other cancer types [55, 56], although their clinical application, including patient stratification and treatment timing, still requires further study. See Supplementary Table S4 for patient-related information. In addition, QDPR-related redox and metabolic alterations may also interact with the tumor microenvironment, including immune cells, cytokine signaling, and oxidative stress, thereby potentially influencing treatment responses in LUAD [57, 58].

This study has several limitations. First, the QTL data used in the MR analyses were mainly derived from peripheral blood and may not fully reflect the regulatory state in tumor tissues. Second, although multiple lines of evidence support an association between DNMT1 and QDPR expression, the methylation status of the QDPR promoter has not been directly assessed. Third, the PDO drug sensitivity experiments were performed under short-term drug exposure; longer treatment duration and larger sample sizes are needed to further evaluate the stability and reproducibility of the combination effects. Changes in QDPR expression following DNMT1 inhibition in PDO, as well as their time-dependent nature, still need to be further validated using qPCR, Western blot, and methylation assays. In addition, the limited number of LUAD cell lines used for functional validation may affect the generalizability of the findings.

This study provides multi-level evidence from genetic causal inference, functional experiments, and patient-derived models supporting a role for potential DNMT1-related epigenetic regulation in LUAD. The findings suggest that targeting DNMT1 may represent a novel strategy to improve the response to platinum-based chemotherapy in selected patient subgroups. Collectively, these results deepen the understanding of the molecular basis of variability in LUAD chemotherapy response and provide a foundation for further studies of epigenetic therapeutic strategies.

Conclusion

Through multi-omics integration and genetic causal inference, this study identified methylation-associated downregulation in LUAD and highlighted QDPR as a key downregulated gene partly mediating disease risk. DNMT1 was consistently supported as a potential upstream regulator of QDPR by correlation analyses, functional experiments, and PDO models. DNMT1 inhibition increased QDPR expression, suppressed malignant phenotypes, and enhanced platinum sensitivity in low-sensitivity organoids. These findings support the potential role of the DNMT1–QDPR epigenetic axis in LUAD and provide insights into chemotherapy response heterogeneity.

The immunoinfiltration characteristics of LSM4 were investigated using bioinformatics and the biological functions were subsequently confirmed in endometrial carcinoma☆
Authors: Jianhong Ma, Ling Li, Rui Hu, Yongjuan Ye, Zhiqiang Yao, Ping Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe mounting evidence suggests that the immune status participates in the advancement and metastasis of UCEC as an important element, yet there is currently a dearth of effective and readily detectable biomarkers to reflect this phenomenon. The present study aimed to investigate the expression level, clinical significance, biological function, and immunoinfiltration characteristics of LSM4 in UCEC through bioinformatics analysis. Additionally, cytological experiments were carried out to validate the biological effect of LSM4 and assess its feasibility as a satisfactory endpoint of therapy and prognostic indicator for the patients.MethodsThe correlation between LSM4 and 12 clinicopathologic variables was mined using a variety of statistical algorithms. The GO, KEGG, and GSEA enrichment situation were conducted using R software to identify differences and biological functions of different LSM4 gene level, while PPI analysis was realized by the STRING database. The ESTIMATE algorithm was utilized to estimate the TME scores. Furthermore, the role of CIBERSORT was to estimate 22 distinct types of immune infiltrating cells in the tumor immune microenvironment. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was conducted on DIICs, while the correlation between DIICs and LSM4 expression were demonstrated. The TIMER 2.0 database was utilized to perform online analysis on the correlation between LSM4 and immunoinfiltration. The TISIDB database was utilized to retrieve the association between LSM4 and immune regulatory genes, TCGA molecular typing, immune typing as well as clinical variables. R-package pRRophetic was employed to access GDSC database for analyzing the influence of LSM4 on chemotherapy and targeted drug sensitivities, while GSCALite database was utilized to screen for small molecule drugs related to LSM4. Q-PCR and Western Blot analyses were employed to validate the differential expression of LSM4 in cancer tissues and cells compared to normal conduction. Additionally, CCK8, Transwell and wound healing experiments were performed to ascertain the impression of siLSM4 on biological functions of Ishikawa and KLE cells.ResultsIn UCEC, the expression of LSM4 is upregulated, which is significantly associated with the deterioration of PFS as well as various adverse clinicopathological features. LSM4 is mainly involved in the regulation of circulating immune complex formation, humoral immunity, classical complement activation pathway, antigen binding. The high expression of LSM4 exhibited a negative correlation with immune score, stromal score, multiple immune cells, GZMA, and most immune cell markers, thereby attenuating the immune activity within the microenvironment. Both immune prediction models further substantiated the involvement of LSM4 in regulating immune infiltration among the patients. Additionally, the presence of high LSM4 expression leads to a reduction in efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors and changes sensitivity to certain chemotherapy and small molecule targeted therapies. The fifteen small molecule drugs related to LSM4 have been identified. The expression of LSM4 was found to be up-regulated in both UCEC tissues and cells, thereby promoting tumor proliferation and migration.ConclusionAll in all, we concluded that high mRNA of LSM4 may lead to a poor prognosis for the patients by facilitating tumor immune evasion and treatment resistance. Therefore, LSM4 could attend as a prospective bookmark and prognostic indicator for targeted therapy in UCEC.

Introduction

UCEC is a group of malignant tumors that develop in the endometrial epithelium, with 80% originating from adenocarcinoma of endometrial glands.1 This cancer is commonly found in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women and ranks among the most prevalent gynecologic malignancies in developing countries.2 Its incidence follows breast cancer, lung cancer, and colorectal cancer,3, 4 accounting for 7% of female systemic malignancies and 20%-30% of female reproductive system malignancies.2, 5 The increasing aging population, declining fertility rates, estrogen abuse, and rising obesity rates contribute to the continuous rise in both incidence and mortality rates of UCEC.6, 7, 8 Additionally, there has been a gradual decrease in the age at which it occurs. In the United States, it is estimated that there was be approximately 66,200 new cases with 13,030 deaths by 2023. It is predicted that by then UCEC will rank fourth in terms of incidence rate and sixth for mortality rate among female malignant tumors respectively, accounting for 7% and 5% respectively.9, 10 By 2030, it is expected to become the third most common malignant tumor affecting women's health.9 Low-grade endometrioid adenocarcinoma represents about three-quarters (75%) of all tumors diagnosed at an early stage.11, 12 Most patients can achieve near-cure through surgical intervention with a high five-year survival rate reaching up to 90%.13 However, effective therapeutic measures are lacking for advanced patients, particularly those diagnosed with stage III or IV endometrioid adenocarcinoma as well as high-risk patients presenting special types due to their increased risk of metastasis and recurrence resulting in poor prognosis; these individuals have only a dismal five-year survival rate reaching merely17%.14, 15 The identification of prognostic markers for UCEC, the progression of advanced and effective therapeutic decision for advanced or recurrent patients, the sensitive distinction of groups that can benefit from different treatments, the optimization of management strategies, and the improvement of patient outcomes are imperative in current research.

TME refers to the specific location where the disease develops, consisting of various molecules including tumor cells, stromal cells, endothelial cells, inflammatory mediators, and extracellular stroma secreted by tumor-related cells. TME plays an immeasurable role in maintaining the survival of malignant tumor cells. By maintaining immune homeostasis within its surroundings, TME facilitates tumor angiogenesis, alters the biological characteristics of tumors, and even regulates the activity of cancer stem cells. These directly or indirectly effects significantly impact the initiation and progression of tumors.16, 17, 18 The synergistic interaction between tumors and TME not only promotes tumor growth but also increases the likelihood of genetic mutations occurring within the tumor. In addition to these aforementioned components, TME also encompasses innate exonerative cell populations such as macrophages, DCs, and BMDCs, as well as adaptive immune cell populations like T lymphocytes (T cells) and B lymphocytes (B cells).19 Due to the heterogeneous nature of tumors, different regions within the same tumor may exhibit variations in microenvironment and immune cell composition.20 This heterogeneity plays an incomparable and key role in the variability of treatment response, which extends from the primary tumor to metastatic lesions, resulting in immunogenic variability as well. Liu21 utilized the TCGA to investigate IRDEGs and TME in UCEC. They developed a risk prediction model based on OS and PFS, revealing that patients with low-risk had increased infiltration and activation of immune cells, lower tumor purity, and higher TMB. Moreover, there was a significant increase in expression of immune checkpoint markers and the IPS, leading to favorable prognosis for the patients. These findings offer novel insights into identifying potential biomarkers and targets for immunotherapy.

Immune evasion mechanisms and immune tolerance regulation are potential factors contributing to the progression, recurrence, and resistance to treatment in UCEC.22 Cancer cells must evade the immune system to thrive within the tumor microenvironment. The interplay between innate and adaptive immune responses influences tumor progression through a process known as immunoediting.23 Immune cells can either inhibit or promote tumor development through three distinct processes: elimination, equilibrium, and escape.24, 25, 26 However, due to the heterogeneous nature of UCEC, patients exhibit varying degrees of response to immunotherapy due to differences in their immune reprogramming and MSI (microsatellite instability) phenotypes.27, 28 Therefore, it is imperative to comprehensively investigate the components and effects of the TME in order to understand the interaction between tumors and immune cells as well as how the immune system impacts tumor behavior in UCEC. A thorough understanding of TME characteristics and their association with molecular expression is crucial for guiding immunotherapy design and predicting patient prognosis. The ESTIMATE and TIMER algorithms were employed for a comprehensive analysis of immune cell populations and gene expression within the TME in UCEC. This data was then correlated with clinical outcomes and patient prognosis before being classified based on immune status, enabling personalized treatment strategies tailored specifically for individual patients aimed at improving overall prognosis.29, 30

Studies have demonstrated a substantial presence of immune cells and chemokines within the TME in UCEC. Abnormal gene expression and immune responses play a crucial role in promoting cancer progression, invasion, and metastasis, thereby significantly impacting patient prognosis in this infiltrating microenvironment. Clinical research has revealed that UCEC exhibits immunogenicity, leading to favorable outcomes with immunotherapy for recurrent and advanced patients.6, 31, 32, 33 However, due to disease heterogeneity, some patients exhibit poor response to immunotherapy.34, 35, 36 Accordingly, there is an emergency requirement to identify neoteric biomarkers for predicting patient outcomes accurately and stratifying treatment options accordingly. In this study, bioinformatics technology was employed to screen LSM4 as an immune-related gene associated with clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis in UCEC patients. The findings suggest its potential involvement in tumor progression and modulation of the immune microenvironment. A comprehensive analysis was conducted on the immunoinfiltrating landscape of LSM4 along with its sensitivity towards immunotherapy in UCEC. By conducting cell experiments, the knockdown of LSM4 can significantly impede the malignant potential of cancer cells, including their proliferation and migration. This study aims to assess its clinical significance as a promising therapeutic molecule for patients' response to treatment and survival outcomes. Additionally, it seeks to elucidate the underlying mechanisms contributing to the heterogeneous efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy and establish a scientific basis for subsequent individualized treatment strategies based on tumor immune characteristics in UCEC patients.

Materials and methods

The origin of tissue specimens

In this study, 8 patients with UCEC who received treatment at the First Hospital of Lanzhou University from February 09 2022 to February 09 2023, were included. Cancer tissues and adjacent tissues from surgical pathological specimens were carefully selected for the purpose of research. The patients have the same characteristics: (1) All specimens were histopathologically confirmed as UCEC by our hospital's pathology department; (2) Pathological types included low, medium, and highly differentiated endometrioid adenocarcinoma as well as clear cell carcinoma. Ethics number was LDYYLL2021-241 of the study.

The acquisition of database data

Endometrial RNA sequencing data, including 554 neoplastic and 23 normal specimens, were adopted from TCGA. Somatic mutation datums were extracted for calculating the TMB value of each sample using the formula: TMB (mut/mb) = total mutations in target coding region/size of target coding region.37 Clinicopathologic data of patients with UCEC was also collected from TCGA. Missing values were assigned to unavailable or unknown clinical parameters. Immunohistochemical data was extracted for UCEC tissues and normal endometrial tissues from the HPA.

Correlation analysis of clinicopathological features

The 554 samples were categorized into two groups through LSM4 level: high LSM4 mRNA (n=277) and low LSM4 mRNA (n=277). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and Cox regression analysis were conducted using two R packages-survminer and survival. The Wilcoxon rank sum test was employed to investigate the correlation between LSM4 expression and clinicopathologic features. The relationship between distinct level groups of LSM4 and clinicopathologic distinctions was described using limma and ggpubr packages. Additionally, the ComplexHeatmap package was utilized for visualizing the heatmap depicting the correlation among clinicopathological factors.

Characteristics of DNA methylation

DNA methylation plays a critical role in the assessment of prognosis and the identification of potential biomarkers associated with malignant tumor.38 This study utilized the MethSurv database (https://biit.cs.ut.ee/methsurv/) to systematically analyze the expression patterns and prognostic significance of CpG site methylation within the LSM4 gene in UCEC. In this context, DNA methylation levels were quantified using β values, which range from 0 to 1. Each individual CpG site's methylation level was calculated according to the formula M/(M+U+100), where M and U represent the intensity values corresponding to methylated and unmethylated alleles, respectively.39

Functional enrichment analysis of DEGs

For enrichment screening of GO terms and KEGG pathways in the DEGs, we employed clusterProfiler and enrichplot packages in R, with significance defined as FDR<0.05. To assess gene set enrichment using GSEA, we used ClusterProfiles package in R along with MSigDB database for downloading gene sets relevant to LSM4 expression levels.40 The STRING database was utilized to construct a PPI network.

The analysis of immunoinfiltration

ESTIMATE is a computational algorithm that leverages gene expression data to infer the proportions of stromal and immune cells within tumor samples by calculating stromal and immune scores through the Pearson correlation coefficient, thereby enabling the prediction of immune infiltration in the TME.41 In this study, the R package ESTIMATE was applied to assess the association between LSM4 expression and both stromal and immune scores in UCEC samples. Higher stromal or immune scores indicate lower tumor purity, suggesting an inverse relationship between tumor purity and the estimated microenvironmental components.

CIBERSORT employs an inverse gene expression deconvolution algorithm to estimate the relative proportions of immune cell infiltration in tumor samples, utilizing a reference signature matrix comprising 547 genes as expression profiles for 22 distinct TIICs, including B cells, T cells, NK cells, macrophages, DCs, and various myeloid subpopulations.33, 42 Support vector regression is applied to infer the abundance of these TIICs based on their gene expression patterns. Using the R package e1071, permutation testing is performed with 1,000 iterations; immune cell types with zero estimated abundance across samples are excluded, and only results with p<0.05 are retained for downstream analysis. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test is used to compare differences in immune infiltration levels between high- and low-LSM4 expression groups, with statistical significance defined as p<0.05. The correlation between eight DIICs and LSM4 expression is individually visualized, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis is conducted to evaluate the prognostic implications of DIICs, where p<0.05 indicates significant survival differences.

The TIMER2.0 database (http://timer.comp-genomics.org/) serves as a comprehensive platform for exploring tumor-immune system interactions. The TIMER algorithm improves estimation accuracy by correcting for potential biases and multicollinearity among immune cell types through partial deconvolution. It enables the assessment of associations between gene expression and immune cell infiltration, thereby facilitating the evaluation of tumor immune characteristics and their clinical relevance.43 In this study, the "Gene_DE" module was employed to analyze LSM4 expression differences across 33 pan-cancer types using the Wilcoxon test, while the "Immune_Gene" module was utilized to evaluate the correlation between LSM4 expression and the infiltration levels of six immune cell types: CD8+T cells, CD4+T cells, B cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and DCs. Notably, TIMER is the only method that accounts for tissue specificity when estimating immune cell abundance. The "Gene_Corr" module was applied to assess the correlation between LSM4 and key immune-related genes (GZMA, GZMB, PRF1) as well as canonical immune cell marker genes. Spearman’s rank correlation test with purity adjustment was used to calculate p-values and correlation coefficients, with statistical significance defined as p<0.05 and |cor|>0.15.

In the TISIDB database, the "Clinical" module examines the association between LSM4 expression and FIGO stage or clinical grade, while the "Subtype" module explores LSM4 expression patterns across immune and molecular subtypes. The "Immunomodulator" and "Chemokine" modules investigate relationships between LSM4 and immune regulatory genes, including immunosuppressants, immunostimulators, chemokines, chemokine receptors, and MHCs. Associations between LSM4 expression and immune modulators are assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with p<0.05 set as the threshold for significance. All statistical analyses are visualized using the SangerBox online platform (https://sangerbox.com).

The construction of prediction model

The immune-related gene clusters were downloaded from the Immport and InnateDB databases. The prognostic hereditary factors were discerned using survival and survminer R packages, followed by performing Cox regression analysis to generate forest plots. A random forest algorithm was employed to model incorporating 32 prognostic genes. The risk score was calculated as follows: risk score = G1 gene expression level ×G1 coefficient + G2 gene expression level ×G2 coefficient +...... G32 gene expression ×G32 coefficient.44, 45, 46 Two risk portions were distinguished by feat of the median cut-off value. Furthermore, the samples were categorized into high-, medium-, and low-immune groups based on the integration of tumor microenvironment score and tumor purity.

Drug sensitivity analysis

Clinical data can be downloaded from the Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA; https://tcia.at/home). IPS enables the prediction of PD-1 and CTLA-4 inhibitors alone or in combination.47 The pRRophetic R package was utilized to access the GDSC database, for obtaining the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of various anticancer drugs.48 Wilcoxon rank sum test has an effect to divide into IC50 levels of 105 common clinical drugs from two LSM4 portions, with p<0.05 as screening criterion for exploring differences in sensitivity of LSM4 expression towards chemotherapy drugs and small molecule targeted drugs. The GSCALite database was utilized for online analytical identification of small drug molecules related to LSM4, with a p-value screening threshold set at 0.0001 or lower, resulting in the identification of 15 types of small molecule drugs.49

Quantitative-polymerase chain reaction (q-PCR)

RNA was extracted from surgically resected UCEC tissues and cells according to the operating instructions provided by the Takara reagent manufacturer. Place the reaction tubes into the PCR instrument for real-time quantitative PCR reaction. Western Blot technique (WB)

We need to sequentially conduct the protein imprinting experiment through the following steps: firstly, extract proteins from tissues and cells. Secondly, prepare a solution and SDS-PAGE gel. Thirdly, install the prepared SDS-PAGE gel onto the electrophoresis tank for conducting electrophoresis experiments. Fourthly, pre-cut the PVDF membrane to match the size of the target strip, activate it with anhydrous methanol, correctly position it in the membrane transfer clip, and electrically transfer it at 300mA for 75 minutes. Fifthly, add 5ml of skim milk powder into the antibody incubator beforehand. After transferring and soaking the PVDF membrane in milk powder immediately after membrane transfer, shake it gently on a shaker table for 2 hours. Finally, incubate with primary antibody overnight followed by secondary antibody incubation for 2 hours before developing exposure.

Cell culture

The hEEC, Ishikawa, KLE, and HEC-1A cells, which purchased from Wuhan Sevier Biotech, were utilized in this study. In a 50ml centrifuge tube, 5ml of fetal bovine serum and 500μl of penicillin/streptomycin double antibody were added. The complete medium was obtained using DMED, F12, or 5A medium and stored at 4°C. To each sterile cell culture bottle (25T), add 1ml of cell precipitation and 4ml of complete culture medium. After overheating, tighten the bottle cap and mix with an additional 4ml of complete culture medium. Place the bottles in a cell culture box and record operator name, cell type, resuscitation date, and other information. Set the incubator parameters to maintain a temperature of 37°C with a CO2 concentration of 5% for observation after incubating for 24 hours. The culture bottle must be thoroughly sterilized to prevent contamination by bacteria, fungi, mycoplasma, and other microorganisms. Subsequently, siRNA was transfected into two UCEC cells to silence three specific sequences. All transfection reagents were procured from Hansheng Chemical Company.

Cell proliferation and migration

The LSM4 group and control group were subjected to siRNA treatment for functional experiments. Cell proliferation ability was assessed using the CCK8 experiment, while cell migration ability was evaluated through Transwell and wound healing experiments.

Statistical analysis

The data processing program was conducted by way of various R software packages, which serve as a straightforward visualization tool. The image processing and data analysis were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9 and ImageJ software in this study. Statistical analysis of the data included calculation of mean standard deviation and Chi-square test. There were denoted by * for p<0.05, ** for p<0.01, *** for p<0.001, **** p<0.0001.

The immunohistochemical results, validated by the HPA database, confirmed the expression of LSM4 protein. To investigate the premonitory value of LSM4 expression in UCEC, we included the TCGA database. The median LSM4 expression was determined in 554 samples of UCEC using the R software of survminer. These samples were then distributed into high-/low-LSM4 expression portions. KM analysis revealed that there was no significant difference in OS between the high and low expression groups (p=0.068, Fig. 1F). However, patients with high LSM4 expression had significantly worse PFS compared to those with low-expression (p=0.035, Fig. 1G).

According to the median LSM4 level, the samples were divided into high and low LSM4 portions. The correlation among LSM4 with clinicopathological variables was explored using R software. As depicted in Fig. 2, there was an overt association observed between LSM4 expression level and different FIGO stages (stage I, II, III, IV) (Fig. 2A), clinical grade (G1, G2, G3, high grade) (Fig. 2B), histology (endometrioid, mixed, serous) (Fig. 2C), pelvic lymphnodes (p=0.0025; Fig. 2D), aortic lymphnodes (p=0.0045; Fig. 2E), peritoneal washing (p=0.0016; Fig. 2F), tumor status (p=0.0037; Fig 2G), residual tumor (R0, R1, R2) (Fig 2K) and menopause status (Peri, Post, Pre) (Fig 2L). Discussion

In the era of genomics, advancements in sequencing technology and the establishment of tumor databases have facilitated the exploration of numerous novel biomarkers. The development of human genome sequencing and DNA microarrays has further unveiled matrices comprising candidate genes with prognostic or therapeutic significance. Notably, the TCGA database offers a comprehensive array of standardized sequencing data for 33 different tumor types encompassing transcription, protein, metabolism, mutation profiles, methylation, among others. Numerous studies have endeavored to leverage resources such as TCGA and GEO to identify IRDEGs in malignant tumors like hepatocellular carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma, and osteosarcoma.50, 51, 52 These investigations aim to establish associations between IRDEGs and clinical parameters as well as patient prognosis. Additionally, by utilizing the TIMER database for analysis purposes, researchers can explore correlations between IRDEGs and immune cell types along with gene polymorphisms within the tumor microenvironment. This valuable information serves as a reference point for identifying potential targets for new immunotherapy approaches. Consequently, there is considerable interest in evaluating the prognostic value of invasive immune cells and stromal cells to provide guidance and a scientific foundation for developing innovative treatment modalities in UCEC.

Based on bioinformatics technology, numerous biomarkers associated with UCEC have been identified. For instance, PHGDH,1 PSAT1,53 GGH,54 and other markers are up-regulated in the patients, thereby suppressing tumor immune activity and leading to a poor prognosis. It is speculated that these molecules may alter the infiltration profile of immune cells, reducing tumor immune attack while promoting cell proliferation and migration, ultimately driving tumor progression. These studies offer novel diagnostic and prognostic markers with strong predictive capabilities for the cancer and highlight the potential value of these markers in immunotherapy. Not only can these biomarkers assist doctors in formulating accurate individualized treatment plans but they can also provide guidance for establishing more reasonable review cycles in clinical settings to minimize the risk of tumor recurrence or metastasis. Despite benefiting some patients with UCEC, there are certain qualifications to these studies, and their specific stratification effects are not yet perfect. By combining clinicopathological features with molecular markers to predict malignant behavior in patients, a more precise risk stratification can be achieved, thus improving patient outcomes. Therefore, this study aims to comprehensively analyze previous research findings, explore additional exceptional biomarkers, and establish a robust immune infiltration evaluation system for UCEC based on this foundation in order to achieve personalized long-term management.

LSM4 homolog, U6 small nuclear RNA and mRNA degradation associated (LSM4), is a protein-coding gene involved in the regulation of cell cycle, energy metabolism, and other biological processes. LSM4 belongs to the RNA-binding protein LSM family and plays a crucial assignment in splicing precursor messenger RNA (pre-mRNA) by facilitating the formation of U4/U6 snRNP.55 Additionally, it contributes to the regulation of malignant tumor progression. Bioinformatics analyses have revealed its involvement in breast cancer,56, 57 pancreatic cancer,55 and hepatocellular carcinoma.58 Chen58 validated that LSM4 expression is up-regulated in liver cancer tissues through GEO and TCGA databases. Overexpression of LSM4 correlates with poor prognosis indicators such as clinical stage, tumor grade, and lymph node metastasis. Yin57 identified LSM4 and another three genes as hub hereditary factors whose expression is upregulated in breast cancer tissues, these genes are closely associated with unfavorable clinicopathological variables like age, lymph node metastasis, and low differentiation, which subsequently lead to increased expression of Ki-67-a marker for tumor cell proliferation. Survival analysis revealed a significant association between higher expression levels of these pivotal genes and poorer prognosis in breast cancer patients, suggesting their potential utility as diagnostic markers and prognostic predictors. Another study56 demonstrated that LSM4 is closely linked to several immune cells in breast cancer cells, indicating its potential role in tumor immunity. Furthermore, bioinformatics analysis identified elevated LSM4 expression in UCEC, which was significantly correlated with PFS deterioration. Therefore, our future investigations will focus on elucidating the pathological processes and molecular mechanisms underlying LSM4's involvement in regulating UCEC to provide valuable insights into improving patient prognosis.

UCEC is one of the most prevalent gynecological malignancies, and its incidence has been steadily accelerating over the past few years. As a heterogeneous disease, its pathogenesis is intricate. Despite the availability of various treatment options including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, and targeted therapy, the overall prognosis remains unsatisfactory for advanced cases with poor differentiation or special subtypes. The absence of screening technology hampers treatment advancements. However, with the advancement of sequencing and biology information, it has been observed that elevated LSM4 mRNA is significantly associated with PFS as well as multiple adverse clinical variables in UCEC. This suggests that LSM4 may play a role in regulating the pathological processes. Based on GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, it was sniffed out that LSM4 may facilitate tumor progression through aberrant splicing and involvement in the regulation of tumor immune infiltration process in UCEC. To further investigate the correlation between LSM4's characteristics of immune infiltration and molecules, we verified their differential expression in immune risk models as well as their association with clinical variables. Additionally, we utilized the TISIDB database to further validate the relationship among LSM4 as well as immune infiltration along with clinical variables. Furthermore, we explored the impact of high LSM4 expression on immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and small molecule targeted therapy in UCEC patients by selecting 15 small molecule drugs associated with LSM4 for clinical study. Finally, we conducted preliminary experiments to validate the level of LSM4 in UCEC tissues and cells, thereby confirming its role in promoting malignant progression such as cell proliferation and migration.

Firstly, the proteomic sequencing data and GEO database were utilized to screen for 8 DEGs associated with the differentiation grade of UCEC. Through analyzing the correlation between these DEGs, clinicopathological characteristics, and patient prognosis, LSM4 was identified as the target gene for subsequent investigations. The datums of RNA-seq, clinical, mutation, and immunohistochemical were extracted from UCEC tissues and normal endometrial tissues using the TCGA and HPA databases. It was observed that LSM4 mRNA and protein levels were significantly elevated in cancer tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues. The KM survival disclosed a remarkable association among high level of LSM4 with worse PFS in patients. Additionally, LSM4 mRNA was counted to be associated with various clinicopathologic features including FIGO stage, clinical grade, histology, residual status, menopausal status. Furthermore, the high expression of LSM4 was positively associated with pelvic lymphnodes, peritoneal washing and tumor status. However, it should be noted that LSM4 cannot serve as an independent risk factor for prognostic prediction. Collectively, these findings advise that LSM4 plays a crucial part in driving malignant processes such as the emergence and progression of UCEC and may attend as a underlying predictor for survival markers and therapeutic targets.

Secondly, 554 samples were delaminated into the LSM4 high group and low group grounded on the median expression of LSM4. A total of 1170 DEGs related to LSM4 were identified in these two groups, with 661 being upregulated and 509 downregulated genes. To further investigate the functional roles of DEGs, we conducted the GO enrichment analysis. The results revealed that DEGs were primarily involved in regulating mitotic nuclear division, chromosome separation, classical complement activation pathway, circulating immunoglobulin-mediated humoral immune response, circulating immune complex formation, immunoglobulin receptor binding, antigen binding. LSM4 is upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, and its high expression is significantly associated with OS in HCC patients. Cox regression analysis indicates that elevated LSM4 expression may serve as an independent prognostic risk factor for HCC.58 GSEA suggests that LSM4 may contribute synergistically to the progression and metastasis of hepatocellular carcinoma by modulating critical pathways, including those involved in cell cycle dysregulation and metabolic reprogramming.58 Furthermore, LSM4 overexpression may represent a valuable biomarker for both diagnosis and prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma. The LSM family comprises evolutionarily conserved protein-coding genes, members of which have been implicated in the initiation and development of various malignancies. Using data from the Kaplan-Meier plotter database, Ta investigated the prognostic significance of LSM family genes in BRCA and identified LSM4 as significantly correlated with tumor incidence and patient outcomes, suggesting its potential as a predictive biomarker in BRCA.56 Additionally, TME analysis reveals that LSM4 expression is closely associated with infiltration levels of CD8+T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells, highlighting its potential involvement in antitumor immune responses and immunotherapeutic implications.56

The most significantly associated genes with LSM4 included LSM2, LSM4, SNRPD1, TEDC2, TNFSF14, DTYMK, MRPL11, which are aberrantly expressed in various malignant tumors and exert detrimental effects in poor prognosis.59, 60, 61 Dai61 discovered that SNRPD1 was upregulated in breast cancer tissues, leading to cell cycle dysregulation, mitotic abnormalities, and impaired chromatin replication. Silencing SNRPD1 can impede tumor cell proliferation and induce G0/G1 phase arrest, implying its role as an oncogene.61 Furthermore, SNRPD1 is also overexpressed in other malignancies.60, 61, 62 These discoveries hint that SNRPD1 may act a pivotal character in promoting the progression of malignant tumors and hold promise as prognostic markers and potential therapeutic targets. TEDC2 is a composite of δ and ε tubulin, both belonging to the tubulin family.63 While most studies have focused on the subtypes of α and β that constitute microtubules responsible for various cellular functions such as cell division, mitosis, and organelle transport,64 recent research has highlighted the crucial role of δ and ε microtubules in maintaining the stability of these tubular processes. Metamorphosis can lead to triple structural breakdowns where centrioles lose their ability to promote mitosis.65 Immunoinfiltration analysis reveals an association between TEDC2 expression and diverse immune cells, suggesting its potential impact on tumor immune evasion.66 Enrichment analysis indicates that TEDC2 may influence tumorigenesis through involvement in cell cycle regulation, ECM modulation, and ICB pathways.66 Collectively, these findings underscore the multifaceted role played by TEDC2 in tumors, making it a promising candidate as a novel biomarker. LIGHT is primarily situated in activated T cells, NK cells, and immature DC cells. LIGHT induces tumor vascular normalization and promotes high endothelial venule formation to facilitate tertiary lymphatic structure development within tumor tissues.67 The binding of soluble or membrane-bound LIGHT to its two main functional receptors-HVEM and LTβR-plays a crucial role. The interaction between LIGHT-HVEM enhances T cell activation, proliferation, survival, and Th1 cytokine production.68, 69, 70 On the other hand, the signaling increases cancer cell sensitivity towards immune responses through LIGHT-LTβR, reorganizes disordered tumor vasculature, and supports cellular infiltration into tumorous microenvironments facilitated by key signals from tertiary lymphoid structures.67, 71, 72 DTYMK is a nuclear-encoded deoxythymidylate kinase that phosphorylates deoxy TMP, converting it into deoxygenated TDP. This enzyme specifically produces dTTP and defects in its function are associated with neurodegeneration in humans, particularly degenerative diseases.73 In essence, DTYMK is upregulated in tumor cells and tissues, inhibiting its activity can cement the susceptivity of cancer cells to doxorubicin.74 MRPL11, as a mitochondrial ribosomal protein, plays a role in protein synthesis and mediates aerobic energy conversion through the oxidative phosphorylation system. It also influences various proteins involved in the pathophysiological processes of tumors.75

KEGG analysis performed clearly that these DEGs were primarily sorted with cycle regulation, motor protein function, neuroactive receptor-ligand interactions, oocyte meiosis, and dopaminergic synapses. Furthermore, GSEA demonstrated that the high LSM4 portion was meaningfully fertilized in pathways related to cell cycle progression, DNA replication, spliceosome assembly, proteasome degradation. Additionally, PPI network analysis confirmed strong gene interactions between LSM4 and LSM1, LSM3, LSM6, LSM7, LSM8, PRPF3, PRPF6, ATL1, SART1, as well as SNRPD3, which predominantly regulate snRNP spliceosome activity. This study highlights the dynamic nature of the spliceosome complex in manipulating pre-mRNA splicing in eukaryotic cells.76 The spliceosome is fabricated of five snRNPs and lots and lots peptides. Each snRNP incorporates seven core Sm proteins. The spliceosome performs the cutting out of pre-mRNA introns and joining of adjacent exons in a specific order.77 Pre-mRNA splicing is a crucial procedure in post-transcriptional governance that significantly expands the functional proteome in eukaryotes despite having only a few genes. Mathematical licing of pre-mRNA plays an essential role in controlling gene expression and thereby affects cell differentiation and proliferation.78, 79 Based on these studies, it is speculated that abnormal splicing processes promote the occurrence and advancement of miscellaneous tumors by modifying cell mitosis patterns to regulate cell cycle, DNA replication, proteasome degradation.80, 81, 82

Furthermore, the ESTIMATE algorithm revealed a negative correlation between LSM4 expression and the immune and stromal scores of the tumor microenvironment. TIICs holds significant clinical potential as a novel prognostic marker for various solid tumors. The CIBERSORT algorithm quantified the dimensions of 22 different TIILs, demonstrating that M1 macrophages, M2 macrophages, activated DC cells, and follicular T helper cells exhibited a positive correlation with LSM4 expression. Conversely, T cells etc. showed a negative correlation with LSM4 expression levels. Conversely, elevated expressions of macrophages and DC cells were linked with poor prognosis. The combination of DIICs prognosis and LSM4 expression revealed that follicular T helper cells were the sole immune cell associated with favorable prognosis in UCEC. Moreover, LSM4 effectively predicted patient responsiveness to various ICB therapies, not only distinguishing populations with diverse prognostic risks and immunotherapy receptions but also demonstrating feasible associations with MSI, TMB, and vicissitudinary treatment options. These findings provide valuable insights into personalized treatment strategies for patients with UCEC. Additionally, based on the TIMER2.0 database analysis, it was observed that LSM4 expression exhibited a destructive correlation with five TILs. It was observed that high LSM4 expression was negatively correlated with most immunoagonists, MHC molecules, chemokines and its receptors. On the grounds of these findings, it is ventured that high LSM4 expression may impact immune cell recruitment, down-regulate immune activity by reducing immune cell infiltration, increase cancer cell immune escape potential in the microenvironment and lead to poor prognosis for the patients. This study also utilized the TISIDB database to further confirm correlations among LSM4 level with pathological variables such as FIGO stage and clinical grade, while predicting patient responsiveness to immunotherapy. In 2013, TCGA proposed molecular typing protocols to offer crucial guidance for prognosis prediction and adjuvant therapy, as well as deepen the comprehension of the biological heterogeneity of UCEC.85, 86 Similarly, LSM4 is also associated with molecular typing such as POLE, MSI, CN-High, CN-Low, and immune typing including C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, which further stimulates exploration into the relationship between LSM4 and molecular/immune typing. This may enhance our intelligence of the effect played by distinct immune subpopulations in UCEC and provide valuable insights for patient immunotherapy.

TIME is a considerable component of the TME, often referred to as the "fertile ground" for malignant transformation of tumors. Its immune components have garnered increasing attention due to their potential in predicting patient prognosis and guiding immune checkpoint blockade therapy.87 TIME primarily comprises the TILs and other immune cells that infiltrate the tumor tissue. TILs consist predominantly of Tregs, macrophages, NK cells, and DCs. As integral players in cell-mediated immune responses, TILs can eliminate tumor cells by stimulating the immune system and augmenting the anti-tumor functionality of the microenvironment. Multiple regulatory pathways influence tumor-specific immune responses and represent potential pharmacological targets for activating anti-tumor immunity in the tumor microenvironment. Specifically, cytotoxic CD8+T lymphocytes and NK cells play pivotal roles in recognizing and attacking cancerous cells.88, 89, 90 However, the reciprocity among PD-1 expressed on CTLs and PD-L1/2 on tumor cells inhibits the anti-tumor immune response mediated by cytotoxic T lymphocytes.91 Additionally, regulatory CD4+T cells express the specific transcription factor FOXP3, which participates in maintaining autoimmune tolerance by inhibiting autoreactive T cells and hindering effective anti-tumor immune responses.92 CD8+T cells of the adaptive immune system are the most prominent maneuvering gear in anti-cancer exonerative responses and serve as a stay for tumor immunotherapy. Patients characterized by CD8+T cell clones have a good prognosis. For example, Palomero93 studied prevalence, phenotype, specificity, and prognostic value of T cells focusing on infiltration characteristics of tumor-reactive CD8+ and CD4+T cell subsets finding that they were infiltrated with high levels of tumor-reactive TILs in the tumor microenvironment. Both CD8+ and CD4+T lymphocytes play a protective role in anti-tumor immunity. Plasma cell infiltration is also related to CD4 and CD8 T cell response leading to good patient prognosis.94, 95 Furthermore, NK cells are important factors in the etiopathogenesis of inflammatory and auto immune complaints, and NK CD56 bright cells serve as a potential tumor immunotherapy by mediating anti-tumor response.96 DC cell microaggregates are also a positive factor for the survival of immunotherapy responsive for the tumor patients.97 However, the infiltration of macrophages is a key driver of tumorigenesis and progression, and is strongly relevant to poor prognosis in numerous tumors.98

Additionally, the overexpression of LSM4 suppresses immune activity and hampers CD8+T cell infiltration, potentially leading to a competition between T cells and tumor cells for T cell consumption, thereby impeding the anti-tumor immune response. These findings indicate that LSM4 plays a crucial role in the infiltration of immune cells within the microenvironment. During chronic viral infections, CD4+T cells are an essential component in advocating the functionality of CD8+T cells and contribute to anti-tumor activity through cytotoxic CD8+T cell-mediated apoptosis.99, 100 The inhibitory effect of regulatory T cells on tumor-specific immune response and their promotion of tumor progression has been demonstrated in various types of tumors. Consequently, a promising strategy for tumor immunotherapy involves stemming the infiltration of these immune cells into the TME.101 Th1 and Th2 are subclasses of helper T cells that participate in the molecular crosstalk of various immune signaling pathways. Specifically, Th1 cells have the ability to directly induce tumor cell death by activating the death receptor on their surface, and that enhance the initiation and expansion of CD8+T cells through cytokine release. Additionally, they promote the recruitment of NK cells and M1 macrophages to tumors.102 Based on these studies, it is speculated that patients with low LSM4 level may possess the more tremendous antigen-presenting cells (APC) capable of presenting tumor cells to T cells, thereby enhancing anti-tumor immunity. This could partly explain why patients with low LSM4 expression exhibit longer PFS compared to those with high expression levels. Furthermore, LSM4 may play a vital part in controlling immune cell infiltration in UCEC, its high expression creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment, which leads to unfavorable outcomes. The results indicate that it is crucial to validate this interaction and assess the prognostic meaning of LSM4 in a large-scale cohort study, highlighting its substantial clinical value.

Subsequently, according to existing modeling methods,46, 103, 104, 105 a sequence of prognostic genes was identified through univariate Cox regression analysis. A prognostic risk model, consisting of 32 immune genes, was constructed using the random forest algorithm. This model classified patients into high-/low- risk portions through median risk scores. The prognosis for high-risk patients was meaningfully more terrible compared to that of low-risk patients. In comparison with low-risk patients, it was confirmed that LSM4 expression was meaningfully more advanced in the high-risk portion. Furthermore, as risk score increased, LSM4 expression levels also increased, indicating a worse prognosis for the high-risk patients. Additionally, LSM4 expression showed positive correlations with adverse clinical variables such as poor differentiation, aortic lymphnodes, peritoneal washing, and tumor status. These findings suggest that LSM4 is associated with immune risk and can serve as a biomarker for immune prognosis in UCEC. These studies not only provide novel insights into the role of LSM4 in regulating the immune-infiltrating microenvironment, but also establish a robust prognostic model for patients with the disease. In this study, diverse immune parameters were integrated to construct an external validation model for the high-, medium-, and low- immune group. Among them, the high immune group exhibited the most favorable survival prognosis. Furthermore, as immune activity increased, there was a gradual decrease in LSM4 expression levels, thus reaffirming its inhibitory effect on tumor microenvironmental immunity. This investigation identifies LSM4 as a novel prognostic marker for UCEC that not only predicts patient survival outcomes but also effectively evaluates tumor microenvironmental invasion, activity, escape mechanisms and immunotherapy potential. Consequently, it represents a promising immunological predictor and therapeutic target.

As a prospective treatment option for malignant tumors, immunotherapy has been extensively utilized in miscellaneous types of solid tumors. Herefrom, we excavated the responsiveness of LSM4 to immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and target of drugs in patients diagnosed UCEC. To examine the response to immune checkpoint inhibitors, it was observed that PD-1 and CTLA4 inhibitors exhibited greater efficacy in patients with low LSM4 expression, indicating their potential benefit from immunotherapy. Additionally, our discoveries made clear that the expression level of LSM4 influences the sensitivity to commonly used chemotherapy drugs and small molecule targeted drugs, thereby partially elucidating the mechanism underlying drug resistance of tumors. Notably, LSM4 serves as an indicator for undesirable prognosis and represents a latent target for reversing therapeutic resistance in patients, thus guiding clinical management and personalized treatment strategies for UCEC. Furthermore, utilizing the GSCALite database enabled us to identify 15 potential small molecule drugs that may enhance patient prognosis by targeting high levels of LSM4 expression-offering prospective options for future targeted therapy. Therefore, these findings may have significant implications for enhancing clinical survival rates and developing novel tumor treatment strategies in UCEC patients. Additionally, they provide valuable references for evaluating patient prognosis and immune status, as well as offering new avenues for prospective molecular markers of the disease.

UCEC is a prevalent malignancy affecting the female reproductive tract, which imposes significant health and economic burdens. However, its clinical treatment is hindered by the tumor's high heterogeneity. Currently, UCEC is classified into two main categories based on differentiation degree: low-grade (G1-G2) and high-grade (G3) tumors.106, 107, 108 The histopathological classification of tumor morphology and differentiation grade plays a pivotal role in patient treatment as it enables prognosis categorization, surgical guidance, and adjuvant therapy recommendations.109, 110 While biological function of LSM4 has been extensively studied in pancreatic cancer, breast cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, its intricate regulatory mechanism and prognostic value remain unclear in UCEC. Based on survival analysis and prognosis assessment, there is speculation that LSM4 may be affiliated in the malignant biological reaction in UCEC, therefore further in vitro experiments were represented to explore its function.

The study initially involved the selection of surgical and pathological specimens, including high, medium, and low differentiated endometrioid adenocarcinoma as well as clear cell carcinoma. Q-PCR and WB were employed to measure the level of LSM4 from 8 specimens. The consequences forecast significantly higher mRNA and protein expression of LSM4 in cancer specimens confronted with para-cancer swatch and normal endometrial cells, which aligned with the findings from bioinformatics analysis. Subsequently, functional experiments were conducted using Ishikawa and KLE cell lines, which represent two types of endometrial carcinoma cells. Initially, three targets were selected for knocking down LSM4 gene expression; however, after verifying knockdown efficiency through qPCR and WB techniques while considering off-target effects prevention measures, Si1 target was chosen for subsequent functional verification. The experimental results confirmed that siLSM4 effectively inhibited proliferation and migration abilities of Ishikawa and KLE cells. Collectively, these findings suggest that elevated LSM4 expression significantly promotes malignant biological behavior potentially impacting prognosis in UCEC. Furthermore, it can serve as an accurate, reliable, sensitive prognostic marker enabling prediction of tumor status along with clinical outcomes. As previously mentioned in this study, LSM4 primarily participates in the regulation of spliceosome function. Aberrant spliceogenesis alters the pattern of cell mitosis, potentially impacting the cell cycle, DNA replication, proteasome degradation through abnormal synaptic signaling and promoting a pro-cancer role. LSM4 can serve as a candidate pro-cancer gene for further investigation into the etiology and pathological mechanisms in UCEC. Given that LSM4 enhances cancer cell proliferation and migration, it could be employed as a screening criterion to stratify patients. By considering variations in sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs and targeted therapies, tailored treatment regimens can be selected to improve therapeutic outcomes for different patient groups. Additionally, exploring the anticancer effects of 15 small molecule drugs may provide insights into their potential clinical application as LSM4 inhibitors for developing anticancer agents. These studies offer novel perspectives and potential targets for individualized precision therapy.

However, a growing body of research has focused on identifying prognostic immune-related biomarkers for UCEC in recent years, including PHGDH,1 PSAT153 and GGH.54 This study presents several key advantages. First, in contrast to studies relying solely on bioinformatics database mining, the gene LSM4 identified was derived from proteomics sequencing data obtained from clinical specimens collected at the Department of Gynecology, The First Hospital of Lanzhou University, thereby ensuring strong clinical relevance in the work. The differential expression of LSM4 was further validated at both the mRNA and protein levels using qPCR and Western blotting, respectively, providing a solid foundation for subsequent investigations. Second, LSM4 encodes a key protein involved in RNA splicing and is closely associated with tumor immune infiltration. It is hypothesized that LSM4 may regulate the expression of critical mRNAs through alternative splicing, thereby influencing cell cycle progression and modulating immune cell infiltration within the TME. These findings contribute to the understanding of tumor immunology and provide a theoretical basis for future studies on immune evasion mechanisms and immunotherapeutic strategies in UCEC. Finally, we highlight the association between LSM4 expression and TCGA molecular subtypes. The integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors with targeted therapies, based on molecular classification, represents a promising frontier in oncology research, with potential clinical benefits for patients with advanced or metastatic diseases.

In summary, this study assessed the prognostic and immune microenvironmental role of LSM4 and identified differences in immune landscape and drug susceptivity among diverse risk groups in UCEC. LSM4 can aid in disease risk assessment, patient classification into subgroups for personalized management, and provide new insights into molecular mechanisms underlying UCEC occurrence and progression. However, limitations include incomplete clinical data available in public databases, the need to prospectively analyze clinicopathological parameters in larger cohorts, consideration of other types of sequencing data beyond mRNA such as copy number changes involved in tumor pathogenesis, identification of a combination of biomarkers with better diagnostic properties rather than relying on a single biomarker alone. Although basic experiments have confirmed the involvement of LSM4 in regulating the malignant biological behavior, further experimental verification regarding its regulation of tumor immunity has not been conducted due to time constraints and experimental conditions in UCEC. Additionally, the underlying role and molecular mechanism of LSM4 in adjusting and controlling immune activity and other functional phenotypes remain unclear. Despite these limitations, LSM4 remains a promising immune marker for developing novel treatment strategies. In future research, it is necessary to conduct extracorporeal cell experiments and intracorporal animal experiments to explore the specific molecular pathway through which LSM4 regulates tumor immune infiltration, elucidate possible immune escape mechanisms, and determine the role and function of complex immune cells in the occurrence and progression of UCEC. Once the relevant mechanistic research is clarified, it will be essential to search for specific inhibitors targeting LSM4, verify the anti-cancer effect and clinical value of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with LSM4 inhibitors, thereby providing hope and opportunities for accurate screening, precise treatment, and further cure for patients with the cancer.

Conclusion

In the article, it can be concluded that LSM4 expression is upregulated in UCEC. It is speculated that abnormal splicing alters mitotic pattern, thereby impacting DNA replication, cell cycle regulation and proteasome degradation. Consequently, these promote tumor cell proliferation and migration while leading to more adverse clinicopathological features in patients. Furthermore, it has been discovered that LSM4 interacts with the tumor immune microenvironment. High level of LSM4 diminishes the effectiveness of ICB inhibitors and influences the sensitivity of chemotherapy and targeted drugs. This may result in a poor prognosis for patients by facilitating tumor immune evasion and treatment resistance. Therefore, LSM4 could attend as a promising therapeutic bookmark and premonitory biomarker for the precise treatment in UCEC.

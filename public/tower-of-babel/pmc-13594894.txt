A mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture of five herbs improved oviduct health in laying hens under LPS challenge
Authors: Xuan Cui, Jiandan Luo, Xuemei Ding, Qiufeng Zeng, Shiping Bai, Xiangbing Mao, Gang Jia, Yan Liu, Jianping Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to investigate the preventive effects of Chinese herbal mixture on salpingitis and its impacts on production performance and egg quality in laying hens challenged with lipopolysaccharide (LPS). The experiment consisted of two phases. In the feeding phase, 160 Lohmann pink-shell laying hens (45 weeks old) were randomly assigned to two treatments. The control group (CON) was fed a basal diet, and the treatment group (MCH) was fed an experimental diet containing 1,000 mg/kg Chinese herbal mixture (dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle, 1:6:1:1:1). After 8 weeks, the MCH group showed decreased levels of interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) in the magnum, IL-1β in the isthmus, and IL-6 and TNF-α in the uterus (P < 0.05). In the challenge phase, 32 hens were selected from the CON group and randomly divided into CON and CON + LPS groups, and 16 hens selected from the MCH group constituted the MCH + LPS group. The CON group was intravaginally injected with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), while the challenged groups received LPS preparation, followed by feeding the basal diet for 4 weeks without herbal supplementation. At 48 h after LPS challenge, the MCH + LPS group showed significantly lower mRNA expression levels of TLR-4, IFN-γ, NF-κB, IL-6, iNOS, and IL-4 than the CON + LPS group (P < 0.05). At 28 d after challenge, the MCH + LPS group exhibited lower anatomopathological scores than the CON + LPS group. Although the laying rate decreased in both challenged groups, the MCH + LPS group maintained a higher laying rate and lower feed conversion ratio (FCR) than the CON + LPS group (P < 0.05). Additionally, the MCH + LPS group showed improved relative yolk weight and dark-spotted score compared with the CON + LPS group (P < 0.05). In conclusion, prophylactic dietary supplementation with 1,000 mg/kg Chinese herbal mixture with mugwort as the main component alleviated oviduct damage induced by LPS challenge and improved production performance and egg quality in the late laying period.

Introduction

At present, the layer industry has entered an intensive, large-scale development stage. As a core component of the reproductive system in laying hens, the oviduct provides the essential physiological environment and nutrients required for egg formation. Therefore, its health status directly determines laying performance and egg quality. However, oviduct inflammation is a highly prevalent reproductive disease in intensive laying hen production systems, particularly during the peak and late laying periods, resulting in substantial economic losses to the poultry industry (Dai et al., 2015). Although antibiotics are considered an effective treatment for oviduct inflammation, the implementation of comprehensive antibiotic-free feed policies has made the effective prevention and control of oviduct inflammation an important challenge facing the laying hen industry (Liu et al., 2024).

Dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle contain a variety of bioactive compounds, including flavonoids, polyphenols, proanthocyanidins, alkaloids, and terpenoids. These compounds can regulate metabolism and immune function, enhance production performance and antioxidant capacity (Hu et al., 2024; Shen et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2025; Zhi et al., 2023; Zhuang et al., 2024), and thereby improve the productive performance and health status of livestock and poultry.

The selection of these five herbs was based on traditional Chinese medicine principles and previous pharmacological studies. In traditional medicine, multiple herbs are often combined to achieve complementary biological effects. Therefore, mugwort, dandelion, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle were combined due to their reported anti-inflammatory and antioxidative activities. Considering that oxidative stress and excessive inflammatory responses are important contributors to oviduct injury in laying hens, this formulation was hypothesized to provide protective effects against inflammatory damage.

Studies have shown that dandelion extract can reduce oxidative stress damage (Song et al., 2009). Additionally, dandelion polysaccharides can inhibit the production and expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby exerting anti-inflammatory effects (Jin et al., 2018). Mugwort extract can alleviate macrophage-mediated inflammatory responses (Feng et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023). Furthermore, mugwort extract can also reduce the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines and exert anti-inflammatory effects (Song et al., 2022). Flavonoids from mugwort can enhance the body's anti-stress capacity and exert significant antioxidant activity in vivo (Hu et al., 2021). Hedyotis diffusa possesses good anti-inflammatory activity and can significantly inhibit the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines in nephritis mice (Ye et al., 2015). Polysaccharides from Hedyotis diffusa can inhibit lipid peroxidation and enhance hepatic antioxidant defense capacity (Zhang et al., 2021). Motherwort extract can reduce inflammatory cytokine levels in serum and cardiac tissue of mice (Tang et al., 2025). Motherwort extract can also reduce the production of inflammatory cytokines in rat endometrium and promote endometrial cell repair through the PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway (Dai et al., 2025). Honeysuckle extract can significantly inhibit the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and alleviate LPS-induced inflammation and oxidative stress (Hu et al., 2025; Ji et al., 2025).

However, the combined effects of dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle on oviduct health in laying hens and the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Moreover, the alleviating effects and mechanisms of Chinese herbal mixture on salpingitis induced by LPS challenge have not been elucidated. Therefore, this experiment was conducted to investigate the effects of Chinese herbal mixture (composed of dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle) on production performance, egg quality, and prevention of salpingitis in laying hens challenged with LPS.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

The mixed Chinese herbal mixture used in this study consisted of dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle, all purchased from Wenjiang Xinrui Pharmacy (Chengdu, China). The five herbal materials were individually ground, passed through a 40-mesh sieve, and then thoroughly mixed at a weight ratio of 1:6:1:1:1 (dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, honeysuckle).

The phytochemical composition of the individual herbal materials was analyzed by Shanghai Paisennuo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The contents of major bioactive compound classes, including flavonoids, organic acids, lignans, and alkaloids, are presented in Supplementary Table S1. Mugwort had the highest flavonoid content (62.15 mg/g), followed by dandelion (38.42 mg/g), Hedyotis diffusa (35.77 mg/g), motherwort (34.71 mg/g), and honeysuckle (21.38 mg/g). Organic acids were most abundant in dandelion (161.12 mg/g) and honeysuckle (86.77 mg/g).

Experimental design and management

This experiment was conducted using a single-factor completely randomized design. In the feeding phase, 160 Lohmann pink-shell laying hens at 45 weeks of age were randomly assigned to 2 treatments. The control group (CON) was fed a basal diet, and the treatment group (MCH) was fed an experimental diet containing 1,000 mg/kg Chinese herbal mixture (composed of dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, and honeysuckle at the ratio of 1:6:1:1:1). After the 8-week feeding phase, samples were collected.

After the feeding phase, 32 hens were selected from the CON group and randomly divided into 2 treatments (CON and CON + LPS), and 16 hens were selected from the MCH group as the MCH + LPS treatment. Each treatment consisted of 4 replicates with 4 hens per replicate, totaling 48 hens. The CON group was intravaginally injected with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), while the CON + LPS and MCH + LPS groups were intravaginally injected with LPS preparation. Samples were collected at 48 h after challenge, and all three treatments were fed the basal diet for 4 weeks. The overall experimental design is illustrated in Fig. 1. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Experimental Animals of Sichuan Agricultural University and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Sichuan Agricultural University, Chengdu, China (Approval No. SICAU-AWC-2024-327). The laying hens were housed in an environmentally controlled poultry house with the room temperature maintained at approximately 20-22°C under a lighting program of 16 h light and 8 h dark (16L:8D). All hens had free access to feed and water. Hens from all treatment groups were evenly distributed within the poultry house. Before the initiation of the experiment, all hens were acclimated for 1 week. After the acclimation period, no significant differences in laying rate were observed among groups, and the dietary treatments were initiated.

Induction of salpingitis model

Challenge reagents: During the challenge phase, hens in the LPS-challenged groups received an intravaginal administration of the LPS preparation, which consisted of LPS solution and an auxiliary solution mixed at a volume ratio of 1:1. Hens in the non-challenged group received an equal volume of PBS. Each bird was administered 2 mL of the corresponding solution. The LPS solution was prepared by dissolving lipopolysaccharide (LPS, E. coli O111:B4, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) in PBS at a concentration of 3.7 mg/mL. The auxiliary solution consisted of 25 % (v/v) liquid phenol, 2.5 % (v/v) Tween-20, 2.5 % (v/v) Span-20, 10 % (w/v) glucose, and 60 % (v/v) PBS.

Challenge procedure: The laying hens were restrained, and the oviduct opening was gently exteriorized. Subsequently, 2 mL of the LPS solution was infused into the uterine region of the oviduct. Successful establishment of the salpingitis model was confirmed 24 h after challenge (Wang et al., 2025).

Production performance

During the experimental period, daily egg number, egg weight, number of defective eggs, and feed intake were recorded on a replicate basis. Laying rate, defective egg rate, average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were subsequently calculated.

Egg quality

At 4 weeks after challenge, 12 eggs were collected from each treatment group (3 eggs per replicate) for egg quality analysis. Eggshell color was measured using an eggshell colorimeter (SCQ-1A, Japan). Eggshell strength was determined using an eggshell strength tester (EFR-01, Robotmation Co., Japan). Haugh unit, yolk color, and albumen height were measured using an automatic egg quality analyzer (ETP-01, Robotmation Co., Japan). Eggshell thickness was measured with a micrometer, and the average value of the middle, blunt, and sharp ends of the eggshell was recorded. The relative proportions of eggshell, yolk, and albumen were calculated based on weight. In addition, another 12 eggs from each treatment group (3 eggs per replicate) were stored for 5 d, after which dark spot scores were evaluated using a digital egg spot analyzer (Eggs-A, Beijing Tianxiang Feiyu Technology Co., Ltd., China).

Inflammatory factors and antioxidant capacity

The contents of malondialdehyde (MDA), catalase (CAT), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) were determined according to the instructions of the assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute).

The concentrations of interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were determined according to the instructions of the assay kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd.).

Oviduct function

The complete oviduct was excised for anatomopathological scoring. Subsequently, oviduct tissues (magnum, isthmus, and uterus) of approximately 2 cm3 were collected, fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, and submitted to Yibaidao Technology (Chengdu, China) for hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining to observe oviduct morphology (n = 3). Additionally, uterine tissue (approximately 1 cm3) from the middle shell gland region was collected, fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, and submitted to Yibaidao Technology (Chengdu, China) for TUNEL staining to detect granular cell apoptosis in the shell gland (n = 3).

Oviduct anatomical pathological scoring was performed according to the criteria in Supplementary Table S3 and HE staining pathological scoring was performed according to the criteria in Supplementary Table S3. To determine uterine granular cell apoptosis rate, approximately 1 cm³ of uterine tissue was collected from the middle portion, fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, and submitted to Yibaidao Technology (Chengdu, China) for TUNEL staining. Inflammatory-related gene expression was determined by real-time quantitative PCR to detect mRNA levels of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf-2), NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), toll-like receptor 4 (TLR-4), interleukin-10 (IL-10), interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and interleukin-4 (IL-4) primer sequences in Supplementary Table S4.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SAS software (version 9.4). The feeding phase data were analyzed by Student's t-test. The challenge phase data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA. When ANOVA revealed significant effects, Tukey's multiple range test was applied for post-hoc comparisons. All data were expressed as means and standard error of the mean (SEM). The difference is considered statistically significant when P < 0.05, and a trend of difference is considered when 0.05 ≤ P < 0.1.

Discussion

Long-term MCH supplementation during the feeding phase improves physiological status

During the 8-week feeding phase, dietary supplementation with 1,000 mg/kg of the mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture significantly improved the antioxidant capacity and anti-inflammatory status of laying hens prior to LPS challenge. With increasing age of laying hens, reactive oxygen species (ROS) progressively accumulate in organs such as the liver, ovary, and oviduct, leading to aggravated oxidative stress (Hua et al., 2021; Sah et al., 2018), which may result in functional decline of the ovary and oviduct and decreased laying rate. Therefore, enhancing antioxidant capacity represents an important approach for maintaining oviduct health in laying hens. Studies have shown that chlorogenic acid (0.02 %) from honeysuckle extract can reduce the content of lipid oxidation product MDA and increase the activities of antioxidant enzymes CAT and T-SOD (Wang et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2020). In the present study, the MCH group exhibited higher activities of SOD and CAT in the isthmus and CAT in the uterus, as well as lower MDA content in the uterus during the feeding phase. This indicates that the Chinese herbal mixture effectively alleviated oxidative damage in the isthmus and uterus by enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities and reducing accumulation of oxidation products, which may be attributed to the bioactive components rich in the herbal mixture, such as flavonoids, polyphenols, and polysaccharides. However, it should be noted that the MCH group showed increased MDA content in the isthmus, which may be associated with the complexity of herbal components and metabolic differences among various oviduct segments; the specific mechanism remains to be further investigated.

Oxidative damage is closely linked to inflammatory response: the accumulation of oxidative products can trigger inflammatory reactions, which in turn further exacerbate oxidative damage, forming a vicious cycle that accelerates aging of the oviduct and ultimately results in decreased laying rate and deteriorated eggshell quality. The occurrence of inflammatory response is closely associated with infiltration of inflammatory cells and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IFN-γ, and TNF-α (Tomal et al., 2023). The active components of Chinese herbal medicine, such as polyphenols and flavonoids, can improve oviduct health and enhance production performance by increasing antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacities in oviduct (Zhao et al., 2024). Dandelion is representative in this regard: studies have shown that dandelion treatment significantly reduced the number of inflammatory cells in mice (Ma et al., 2015), and organic acids in dandelion could alleviate infiltration of inflammatory cells and protect organism health (Yang et al., 2016). In the present study, the MCH group not only decreased IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α contents in the magnum, but also significantly downregulated IL-1β in the isthmus and TNF-α in the uterus. This extensive anti-inflammatory effect may be achieved through the following pathways: the bioactive components in the herbal mixture first reduce accumulation of oxidative substances, thereby attenuating inflammatory response, and ultimately protect oviduct health. These results indicate that the herbal mixture established a favorable physiological environment in the oviduct with suppressed inflammatory responses and enhanced antioxidant defense during the feeding period.

Dietary supplementation with the mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture during the feeding phase also improved the production performance of laying hens, as evidenced by significantly increased egg mass in the MCH group during weeks 5-8 and 1-8, as well as a significantly decreased FCR during 1-8 weeks. This effect may be achieved through synergistic actions of multiple pathways. First, compound formulation possesses synergistic advantages: a Chinese herbal mixture containing motherwort, Angelica sinensis, and other components significantly improved laying rate and reduced FCR in broiler breeders (Liu et al., 2024), which corroborates the findings of the present study. Second, mugwort, as the core component of the Chinese herbal mixture, may play a pivotal role-dietary mugwort powder supplementation enhanced antioxidant capacity in laying hens (Chen et al., 2022), mugwort extract significantly increased the bursa of Fabricius index in broilers (Yang et al., 2021). Additionally, the a* value of eggshell color was significantly lower in the MCH group, which may reflect alterations in eggshell pigment metabolism mediated by herbal bioactive components.

MCH pre-supplementation protects laying hens against LPS-induced oviduct injury

This study tested whether the herbal mixture could prevent oviduct inflammation. During the challenge phase, all hens received the basal diet without herbal supplementation. Thus, the benefits seen in the MCH + LPS group reflected carryover effects from the 8-week pre-supplementation period. This experimental design was selected to determine whether preventive nutritional intervention could enhance the resistance of laying hens to subsequent LPS-induced oviduct inflammation.

Oviduct status was assessed at 48 h and 28 d after LPS challenge. The 48 h data captured acute damage, while 28 d reflected persistent pathology. Production performance was recorded at 4 weeks because oviduct injury does not immediately reduce egg output. There is a lag between tissue damage and functional decline. At 48 h after LPS challenge, hens in the challenged groups showed characteristic pathological changes of salpingitis. These included mucosal congestion, thinning, viscous secretion, and caseous masses in the oviduct. At 28 d post-challenge, the CON + LPS group showed persistent mucosal damage, malformed egg-like substances, and elevated anatomopathological scores. Production performance also deteriorated markedly. The laying rate decreased significantly and the FCR increased significantly. These findings are consistent with previous reports that LPS challenge significantly impairs oviduct function and laying performance (Nie et al., 2018).

By contrast, the MCH + LPS group showed significant protective effects during the challenge phase. Although laying rate decreased in both challenged groups, the MCH + LPS group maintained a significantly higher laying rate and a lower FCR. The MCH + LPS group also had lower anatomopathological scores, with mucosal appearance close to normal and no signs of hemorrhage, congestion, foreign bodies, or necrosis. Compared with the CON + LPS group, the MCH + LPS group showed milder epithelial degeneration and reduced inflammatory cell infiltration in the lamina propria.

In the present study, LPS challenge significantly decreased relative albumen weight and relative yolk weight, which was associated with impaired protein secretion function caused by mucosal damage in the magnum (Li et al., 2023). Pretreatment with the Chinese herbal mixture significantly ameliorated these parameters, indicating its protective effect on the secretory function of the oviduct and its ability to maintain normal egg formation processes. Regarding eggshell quality, the formation of dark spot eggs is closely related to the functional status of the shell gland. In the present study, LPS challenge significantly reduced the dark-spotted score, whereas pretreatment with the Chinese herbal mixture markedly ameliorated this parameter. This effect may be associated with suppression of inflammatory responses and apoptosis in the uterus, thereby maintaining normal shell gland function (Liu et al., 2023). Furthermore, herbal components may enhance egg quality through modulation of lipid and calcium metabolism. Previous studies have reported that fermented Chinese herbs containing Leonurus motherwort and mugwort could regulate estrogen levels, lipid metabolism, and calcium metabolism in laying hens, resulting in improved eggshell strength and thickness and reduced yolk cholesterol content (Liu et al., 2025), which is consistent with the findings of the present study.

At the molecular level, the analysis can be approached from two perspectives: inflammatory signaling pathways and cell apoptosis regulation. Regarding inflammatory signal transduction, LPS has been demonstrated to significantly induce PTRF expression downstream of TLR4 and the activation of p38, ERK, and JNK mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways (Zhou et al., 2021); conversely, bioactive substances such as fructooligosaccharides can exert anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB signaling pathway-related gene expression under LPS stimulation (Song et al., 2024). The results of the present study showed that the expression levels of TLR-4, IFN-γ, NF-κB, IL-6, iNOS, and IL-4 were significantly increased in the CON + LPS group after challenge, whereas prophylactic supplementation with mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture significantly inhibited the expression of these inflammatory factors, effectively alleviating tissue damage induced by LPS challenge. We speculate that dietary supplementation with the mugwort-based herbal mixture enhanced antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacities. This effect inhibited the expression of genes related to the TLR-4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway. Consequently, cellular apoptosis was reduced, and LPS-induced oviduct damage was alleviated. Regarding cell apoptosis, the present study found that apoptosis levels in the oviduct were significantly elevated at 48 h after LPS challenge, whereas the herbal group showed significantly lower apoptosis rates than the positive control group. This result is consistent with the findings of Yan et al., who reported that lotus leaf extract alleviates salpingitis by inhibiting apoptosis signaling pathways, and Shirazi et al., who detected by flow cytometry that red grape and resveratrol alleviate apoptosis in AGS gastric cancer cells (Shirazi et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2023). The aforementioned evidence indicates that the mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture exerts protective effects not only through direct anti-inflammatory pathways, but also by inhibiting excessive apoptosis to preserve tissue structural integrity and promote damage repair in the oviduct. These molecular changes indicate that the physiological preconditioning established during the 8-week feeding phase provided protection against LPS-induced inflammatory signaling and cellular damage, even after herbal supplementation was discontinued.

Conclusion

Dietary supplementation with 1,000 mg/kg of a mugwort-based Chinese herbal mixture (dandelion, mugwort, Hedyotis diffusa, motherwort, honeysuckle = 1:6:1:1:1) prevented LPS-induced oviduct damage and improved production performance and egg quality in late-laying hens. These beneficial effects may be closely associated with the regulation of the TLR-4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway by the Chinese herbal mixture.

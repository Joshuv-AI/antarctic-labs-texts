On Structural Properties and Adjacency Spectrum of Coprime Graph of Integers
Subarsha Banerjee

Abstract
Let T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} denote the coprime graph having vertex set { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,\ldots,n\} with any two vertices i , j i,j being adjacent if and only if gcd ⁡ ( i , j ) = 1 \gcd(i,j)=1 . In this article, we first study some structural properties of T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} . We study the vertex connectivity and crossing number of the coprime graph of integers. We discover a lower constraint on the multiplicity of − 1 -1 , which appears as an eigenvalue in the adjacency matrix of T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} . We demonstrate our findings with a variety of cases. We also show that the adjacency matrix of T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} is singular, i.e. has determinant 0 0 . Furthermore, we give a lower bound on the multiplicity of 0 0 , which appears as an eigenvalue in the adjacency matrix of T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} . Finally, we establish that the greatest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of T ​ C ​ G n TCG_{n} is always above 2 . 2.

A graph

Two vertices are considered to be

A

A

If

is considered a

with length

The

A graph
G
is considered

if there is a path connecting any two vertices
u,v\in V
.
The term

graph refers to a graph that is not connected.
In a connected graph
G
, the

between two vertices

and
v
.
The diameter of a connected graph

A

If the graph is planar, then its crossing number is

The crossing number of the complete graph

Several problems on crossing number of a graph have been discussed in

Readers may refer to

The

0
.
A

A graph’s

A graph is said to be

Proposition 3.1
.

TCG_{n}
is connected and has diameter
2
.

Proof.

Consider the vertex
1\in TCG_{n}
.
For any
v(\neq 1)\in TCG_{n}
,
\gcd(v,1)=1
which implies that
v
is adjacent to
1
.
Hence
distance
between any two vertices of
TCG_{n}
is at most
2
.
Thus
diameter
of
TCG_{n}
equals
2
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

TCG_{n}
is complete if and only if
n=3
.

Proof.

If
n=3
then the vertices of
TCG_{n}
are
\{1,2,3\}
.
It can be readily seen that
TCG_{3}
is complete.
Conversely, if
n\geq 4
then the vertices
2
and
4
of
TCG_{n}
are not adjacent since
\gcd(2,4)=2(\neq 1)
.
Hence
TCG_{n}
is not complete for
n\geq 4
.

Thus the result follows.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

If
n\geq 3
, then girth of
TCG_{n}=3
.

Proof.

Consider the vertices
\{1,2,3\}
of
TCG_{n}
.
Since the given vertices form a
3
cycle and it is the shortest cycle
TCG_{n}
possesses for any
n\geq 3
, the girth of
TCG_{n}
equals
3
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

If
n\geq 3
, then
TCG_{n}
is not bipartite.

Proof.

If

, then
TCG_{n}
contains an odd cycle of length

Using

2.4

Theorem 3.5
.

If
n\geq 3
then
TCG_{n}
is triangulated.

Proof.

To show that
TCG_{n}
is triangulated, we show that any vertex of
TCG_{n}
is a vertex of a triangle.
We notice that for any
m

Thus the vertex

is a vertex of the triangle

Since the above holds for any

Theorem 3.6

The clique number of

Given

Clearly for any

We now prove that the induced subgraph of

We now consider the

Since

Now since

Thus the

Since the number of elements in

\end{array}\right].\end{split}

(3)

We find that the first
\mathcal{D}+1

are identical.
Hence we apply the row operation

for

On applying the given row operations, the matrix

reduces to the following matrix:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccccc}1&1&1&\ldots&\dots&\textbf{1}&1&1&1&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&1\\

Here

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccccccc}(0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0).\end{array}

0

A+I
with multiplicity at least
\mathcal{D}
. Hence, we conclude that
-1
is an eigenvalue of
A+I

.

∎

Theorem 4.7
.

If
n>3
, then the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
is singular.

Proof.

Since
n\geq 4
, so
2
will always be a vertex of
TCG_{n}
.
Now consider a vertex
v
of
TCG_{n}
.
We shall first establish the following claim:

Claim 4.8
.

\gcd(a,2)=1
if and only if
\gcd(a,2^{k})=1
for any natural number
k
.

Proof.

Assume that
\gcd(a,2)=1
. Let
d=gcd(a,2^{k})
. This implies
d\mid 2^{k}
, which further implies
d
must be an even number.
Since
\gcd(a,2)=1
, it is not possible for
d
must be an even number. Hence we reach a contradiction. The converse part is obvious.
∎

Using
4.8
, we find that
v
is adjacent to the vertex
2
of
TCG_{n}
if and only if
v
is adjacent to the vertex
2^{k}
of
TCG_{n}
for all natural numbers
k
.
This shows that the row of the adjacency matrix corresponding to the vertex
2
of
TCG_{n}
is the same as the rows corresponding to the vertex
2^{k}
of
TCG_{n}
for all natural numbers
k
.

Thus we find that there always exists two rows in the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
which are similar.
This proves that the determinant of the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
is zero. Thus the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
is always singular.
∎

Now, the question that naturally comes to our mind is if it is possible to determine how many times
0
will occur as an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
.

In the next theorem, we shall provide a result through which we can determine a lower bound on the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
of the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Suppose
n>3
. Let
p_{i}
where
1\leq i\leq m
, be the collection of all prime numbers which are less than or equal to
n
.
Moreover, let
k_{i}
be the largest positive integer such that
p_{i}^{k_{i}}\in TCG_{n}
but
p_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\notin TCG_{n}
.
Then the multiplicity of
0
as an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
is atleast
(\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i})-m
.

Proof.

Let us consider the prime number
p_{1}
such that
p_{1}<n
.
Moreover, assume that
p_{1}^{k_{1}}\leq n
but
p_{1}^{k_{1}+1}>n
.
Now, following similar arguments as given in
4.8
, it is clear that
\gcd(p_{1},n)=1
if and only if
\gcd(p_{1}^{t_{1}},n)=1
, where
1\leq t_{1}\leq k_{1}
.
This shows that the row corresponding to the vertex
p_{1}
is identical to the rows corresponding to the vertices
p_{1}^{t_{1}}
for
1\leq t_{1}\leq k_{1}
.
Hence, the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
will have
0
as an eigenvalue with multiplicity at least
k_{1}-1
.

If we apply the same argument as used for
p_{1}
to all other prime numbers
p_{i}
where
1\leq i\leq m
, we find that the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
will have
0
as an eigenvalue with multiplicity at least
k_{i}-1
for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

This proves that the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
will have
0
as an eigenvalue with multiplicity atleast
\underbrace{(k_{1}-1)+(k_{2}-1)+\cdots+(k_{m}-1)}_{m\hskip 5.69054pt\text{times}}
.

Thus, the multiplicity of
0
as an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of
TCG_{n}
is atleast
(\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i})-m
.
∎

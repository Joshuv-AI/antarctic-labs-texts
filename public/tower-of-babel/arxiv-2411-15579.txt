Phase transition of degenerate Turán problems in p p -norms
Jun Gao, Xizhi Liu, Jie Ma, Oleg Pikhurko

Abstract
For a positive real number p p , the p p -norm ‖ G ‖ p \left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p} of a graph G G is the sum of the p p -th powers of all vertex degrees. We study the maximum p p -norm ex p ​ ( n , F ) \mathrm{ex}_{p}(n,F) of F F -free graphs on n n vertices. Füredi and Kündgen [ FK06 ] show that for every bipartite graph F F , there exists a threshold p F p_{F} such that for p < p F p<p_{F} , the order of ex p ​ ( n , F ) \mathrm{ex}_{p}(n,F) is governed by pseudorandom constructions, while for p > p F p>p_{F} , it is governed by star-like constructions, assuming a mild assumption on the growth rate of ex ⁡ ( n , F ) \mathrm{ex}(n,F) . The main contribution of our paper is extending this result to hypergraph. Moreover, in the case of graph, our proof differs from that in [ FK06 ] , offering the advantage of producing the correct constant factor when p > p F p>p_{F} . When p = p F p=p_{F} , Füredi and Kündgen proved a general upper bound on ex p ​ ( n , F ) \mathrm{ex}_{p}(n,F) , tight up to a log ⁡ n \log n factor, and conjectured that this factor is unnecessary. We confirm this conjecture for several well-studied bipartite graphs, including one-side degree-bounded graphs and families of short even cycles. Keywords: degenerate Turán problem, degree powers, counting stars, phase transition.

. Then we inductively select uniformly at random an
m_{i}
-set
V_{i}
from
V(\mathcal{G})\setminus(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{i-1})
, where
V_{0}\coloneqq\emptyset
.
Let
\mathbf{G}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}]
and
V\coloneqq V(\mathcal{G})
.
Similarly to the proof of Proposition
2.6
, it follows from Fact
2.2
and the linearity of expectation that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{G}\right\rVert_{p}\right]=\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}\left[d_{\mathbf{G}}^{p}(v)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in V}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[d_{\mathbf{G}}(v)\right]\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}\colon v\in e}\mathbb{P}\left[e\in\mathbf{G}\right]\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\left(\left(\frac{r!}{r^{r}}+o(1)\right)\cdot d_{\mathcal{G}}(v)\right)^{p}=\left(\frac{r!}{r^{r}}+o(1)\right)^{p}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}\right\rVert_{p}.

Therefore, there exists a balanced
r
-partition
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}=V(\mathcal{G})
such that the
r
-partite subgraph
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}]
satisfies
\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}\geq\left(\frac{r!}{r^{r}}+o(1)\right)^{p}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}\right\rVert_{p}
.
∎

Let
K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r}
be the complete
r
-partite
r
-graph with parts of sizes
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
, respectively.
Extending classical theorems of Kővári–Sós–Turán
[
KST54
]
and Erdős
[

]
, the following upper bound for

was proved in

]
.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
HHL+23
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Suppose that

,
s_{r}\geq\cdots\geq s_{1}\geq 1
, and

Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(m,n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}^{r})\leq\frac{(s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}-r+1)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}}{r-1}mn^{r-1-\frac{1}{s_{1}\cdots s_{r-1}}}+(s_{1}-1)\binom{n}{r-1}.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let

.

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(m,n,\mathcal{F})\leq C_{\mathcal{F}}m^{1+\alpha-(r-1)}n^{r-1}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\geq m\geq N_{0}.

Proof of Proposition
2.10
.

Let
C,N_{0}
be constants such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})\leq Cn^{1+\alpha}
for every

.
Let
C_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq 2^{2+\alpha}C
.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists an

-free

-graph

Similar to the proof of Proposition

, there exists a set

of size

\displaystyle\geq(1+o(1))\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{r-1}\sum_{v\in V_{1}}d_{\mathcal{G}}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{r-1}|\mathcal{G}|

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{r-1}C_{\mathcal{F}}m^{1+\alpha-(r-1)}n^{r-1}=C(2m)^{1+\alpha}\geq\mathrm{ex}\left(|V_{1}\cup U|,\mathcal{F}\right),

a contradiction.
∎

3
Regularization under the
p
-norm

In this section, we prove the following extension of the classical
\Delta
-almost-Regularization Theorem by Erdős–Simonovits (see e.g.
[
FS13
, Theorem 2.19]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Let
\alpha\in(r-2,r-1)
,
p\in\left[1,\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}\right)
, and
C>0
be real numbers.
Then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exist constants
K
and
N_{0}
such that the following holds for every
n\geq N_{0}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an
r
-graph on
n
vertices with
\left\lVert\mathcal{G}\right\rVert_{p}\geq Cn^{1+p\alpha}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
contains a subgraph
\mathcal{H}
on
m
vertices satisfying

(i)

\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}\geq(1-\varepsilon)Cm^{1+p\alpha}
,

(ii)

m\geq\frac{\left(C^{1/\delta}n\right)^{\frac{\delta}{1-3\delta}}}{2}
, where
\delta\coloneqq\frac{1-p(r-1-\alpha)}{4}
,

(iii)

\Delta(\mathcal{H})\leq\left(\frac{K}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{m}\right)^{1/p}
, and

(iv)

|\mathcal{H}|>\hat{C}m^{1+\alpha}
, where
\hat{C}\coloneqq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)C^{1/p}}{rK^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(r-2,r-1)
,
p\in\left[1,\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}\right)
, and
C>0
be as assumed in Lemma
3.1
. Since
p\in\left[1,\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}\right)
, the constant
\delta=\frac{1-p(r-1-\alpha)}{4}
satisfies
0<\delta<1/4
.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Let
\varepsilon_{1}
be the real number in
(0,\varepsilon)
such that

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon_{1}-((r-1)\varepsilon_{1})^{1/p}=(1-\varepsilon)^{1/p}.

Let
K
be a constant satisfying

\displaystyle K^{\delta}\geq 2^{1+p(r-1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{K^{1+p\alpha}}{K^{p(r-1)}}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2^{2+p\alpha}}=K^{4\delta}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2^{2+p\alpha}}>K^{2\delta}.

Let
N_{1}
be the constant such that Proposition
2.6
holds with
o_{m}(1)\geq-1/2
for all
m\geq N_{1}
.
Let
N_{0}\gg N_{1}
be a sufficiently large integer and
\mathcal{G}
be an
r
-graph on
n\geq N_{0}
vertices with
\left\lVert\mathcal{G}\right\rVert_{p}\geq Cn^{1+p\alpha}
.

For convenience, for every
r
-graph
\mathcal{K}
, we define

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{K})\coloneqq\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{K})|^{1+p\alpha}}.

Note that
\Phi(\mathcal{G})\geq C
.

We will define a sequence of subgraphs
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{G}\supseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{G}_{k}
for some
k\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})\geq K^{2\delta}\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{i})\geq\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{i+1}n\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})|\leq\left(\frac{2}{K}\right)^{i+1}n

for every
i\in[0,k-1]
.

Suppose we have defined
\mathcal{G}_{i}
for some
i\geq 0
.
Let

\displaystyle U_{i}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V(\mathcal{G}_{i})\colon d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)\geq\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}\right\}.

It follows from

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{G}_{i})}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)\geq\sum_{v\in U_{i}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)\geq|U_{i}|\cdot\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}

that

\displaystyle|U_{i}|\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}.

If
\sum_{v\in U_{i}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)<\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}
or
|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|\leq N_{1}
, then we stop the process and set
k\coloneqq i
.
Otherwise, we apply Proposition
2.6
to
\mathcal{G}_{i}
with
U
and
m
in the proposition corresponding to
U_{i}
and
|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|/K
here. Let
V_{i+1}\subseteq V(\mathcal{G}_{i})
be the
\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}
-set returned by Proposition
2.6
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{i+1}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{i}[U_{i}\cup V_{i+1}]
.
By Proposition
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i+1}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in U_{i}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i+1}}^{p}(v)

\displaystyle\geq(1+o(1))\left(\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|/K}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}\right)^{p(r-1)}\sum_{v\in U_{i}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)\geq\frac{\sum_{v\in U_{i}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}^{p}(v)}{2K^{p(r-1)}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}}{2K^{p(r-1)}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})=\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i+1}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})|^{1+p\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}}{2K^{p(r-1)}}/\left(\frac{2|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}\right)^{1+p\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2K^{p(r-1)}}\cdot\frac{K^{1+p\alpha}}{2^{1+p\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|^{1+p\alpha}}>K^{2\delta}\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{i}).

(3)

Additionally, it follows from the inductive hypothesis that

\displaystyle|V(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})|

\displaystyle=|U_{i}\cup V_{i+1}|\geq|V_{i+1}|\geq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}\geq\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{i+1}n,\quad\text{and}\quad

(4)

\displaystyle|V(\mathcal{G}_{i+1})|

\displaystyle=|U_{i}\cup V_{i+1}|\leq|U_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}+\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{i})|}{K}\leq\left(\frac{2}{K}\right)^{i+1}n,

(5)

as desired.

We claim that the process defined above stops after at most
k_{\ast}\coloneqq\log_{K}(n/N_{1})
steps. Indeed, suppose this is not true. Then at the
k_{\ast}
-step, by (
3
), we would have

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}}\right\rVert}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|^{1+p\alpha}}=\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})\geq\left(K^{2\delta}\right)^{k_{\ast}}\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{0})\geq CK^{2\delta k_{\ast}}.

(6)

It is trivially true that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}}\right\rVert}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|^{1+p\alpha}}=\frac{\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}}}^{p}(v)}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|^{1+p\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k\ast})|\cdot|V(\mathcal{G}_{k\ast})|^{p(r-1)}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|^{1+p\alpha}}

\displaystyle=|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|^{p(r-1-\alpha)}\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|.

Combining this with (
5
) and (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle CK^{2\delta k_{\ast}}\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k_{\ast}})|\leq\left(\frac{2}{K}\right)^{k_{\ast}}n.

It follows that

\displaystyle n\geq CK^{2\delta k_{\ast}}\left(\frac{K}{2}\right)^{k_{\ast}}=C\left(\frac{K^{2\delta}}{2}\right)^{k_{\ast}}K^{k_{\ast}}\geq CK^{\delta k_{\ast}}K^{k_{\ast}}=C\left(\frac{n}{N_{1}}\right)^{1+\delta},

which is a contradiction since
C,\delta,N_{1}>0
are fixed and
n
is sufficiently large.
Therefore, the process defined above stops after at most
k_{\ast}\coloneqq\log_{K}(n/N_{1})
steps.

Recalling that
k
is the final step of the process, and using (
4
), we have

\displaystyle|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|\geq\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{k}n\geq\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{k_{\ast}}n\geq N_{1}.

This means that the process stopped due to

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)<\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}.

(7)

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
on
W\coloneqq V(\mathcal{G}_{k})\setminus U_{k}
and let
m\coloneqq|W|
. Recall that

\displaystyle U_{k}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V(\mathcal{G}_{k})\colon d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)\geq\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}\right\}

We will show that
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the assertions in Lemma
3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\mathcal{G}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{H}
.
Note that every edge in
\mathcal{R}
contains at least one vertex from
U_{k}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{R}}(v)\leq(r-1)\cdot|\mathcal{R}|\leq(r-1)\cdot\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{R}}(v).

Since
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{k}
, it follows from the definition of
U_{k}
and (
7
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(v)\leq\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{R}}(v)\cdot d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p-1}(v)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{R}}(v)\cdot\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq(r-1)\cdot\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{R}}(v)\cdot\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq(r-1)\cdot\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}(v)\cdot d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p-1}(v)

\displaystyle=(r-1)\cdot\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)<(r-1)\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}.

If, for the sake of contradiction, it holds that
\sum_{v\in W}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}<(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}
, then it follows from the inequality above that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in W}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+d_{\mathcal{R}}(v)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\sum_{v\in W}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{R}}^{p}(v)\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}^{1/p}+\left((r-1)\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle<\left(\left((1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left((r-1)\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left((1-\varepsilon)^{1/p}+(r-1)^{1/p}\varepsilon_{1}^{1/p}\right)^{p}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}=(1-\varepsilon_{1})^{p}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p},

where the first inequality follows from Fact
2.3
and
the last equality follows from the definition of
\varepsilon_{1}
.
Combining this with (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}=\sum_{v\in U_{k}}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)+\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}^{p}(v)<\varepsilon_{1}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}+(1-\varepsilon_{1})^{p}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p},

a contradiction.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p},

(8)

which implies that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{m^{1+p\alpha}}\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1+p\alpha}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{0}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{0})|^{1+p\alpha}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)C.

Here, we used the fact that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{k})\geq K^{2\delta}\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{k-1})\geq\cdots\geq K^{2k\delta}\cdot\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{0})\geq\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{0})\geq C.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1

(i)
.

Next, we prove Lemma
3.1

(ii)
.
Note that by
W=V(\mathcal{G}_{k})\setminus|U_{k}|
and
|U_{k}|\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}{K}
, we have
|W|\geq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|-\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}{K}
.
Recall the following results that we have established
\colon

Claim 3.2
.

We have

(i)

|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|-\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}{K}\leq|W|=m\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|
.

(ii)

\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{k}n\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|\leq\left(\frac{2}{K}\right)^{k}n
.

(iii)

K^{2k\delta}C\leq\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1+p\alpha}}\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1+p(r-1)}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1+p\alpha}}=|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{p(r-1-\alpha)}=|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1-4\delta}
.

It follows from Claim
3.2

(ii)
and
(iii)
that

\displaystyle K^{2k\delta}C\leq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|^{1-4\delta}\leq\left(\left(\frac{2}{K}\right)^{k}n\right)^{1-4\delta}.

Since
K^{\delta}\geq 2^{1+p(r-1)}\geq 2^{p(r-1-\alpha)}=2^{1-4\delta}
, the inequality above implies that

\displaystyle n^{1-4\delta}\geq\frac{K^{2k\delta}CK^{k(1-4\delta)}}{2^{k(1-4\delta)}}\geq\frac{K^{2k\delta}CK^{k(1-4\delta)}}{K^{k\delta}}=K^{k\left(1-3\delta\right)}C.

It follows that
K^{k}\leq n^{\frac{1-4\delta}{1-3\delta}}C^{-\frac{1}{1-3\delta}}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle m\geq|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|-\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}{K}\geq\frac{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{k}n\geq\frac{C^{\frac{1}{1-3\delta}}n^{\frac{\delta}{1-3\delta}}}{2},

proving Lemma
3.1

(ii)
.

It follows from the definition of
U_{k}
and
\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}
(by (
8
)) that

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}}{|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{(1-\varepsilon)|V(\mathcal{G}_{k})|}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{(1-\varepsilon)m}\right)^{1/p}.

(9)

This proves Lemma
3.1

(iii)
.

Finally, it follows from

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}=\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(v)\leq\sum_{v\in W}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\cdot\left(\Delta(\mathcal{H})\right)^{p-1}=r\cdot|\mathcal{H}|\cdot\left(\Delta(\mathcal{H})\right)^{p-1}

and (
9
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|\geq\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{r\left(\Delta(\mathcal{H})\right)^{p-1}}>\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{r\left(\frac{K\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}}{(1-\varepsilon)m}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{(1-\varepsilon)m}{K}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}^{1/p}.

Combining this with
\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{p}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}\geq(1-\varepsilon)Cm^{1+p\alpha}
(by (
8
) and Claim
3.2

(iii)
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|>\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{(1-\varepsilon)m}{K}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left((1-\varepsilon)Cm^{1+p\alpha}\right)^{1/p}=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)C^{1/p}m^{1+\alpha}}{rK^{\frac{p-1}{p}}},

which proves Lemma
3.1

(iv)
.
∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
This will be achieved through the following two propositions, first addressing the case
p<\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a degenerate family of
r
-graphs satisfying
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=O(n^{1+\alpha})
for some constant
\alpha
.
Then for every
p\in\left(1,\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}\right)
, there exists a constant

such that for all sufficiently large

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{p}(n,\mathcal{F})\leq C_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot n^{1+p\alpha}.

In particular, if
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{1+\alpha})
, then together with (
1
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{p}(n,\mathcal{F})=\Theta(n^{1+p\alpha})\quad\text{for every}\quad p\in\left(1,\frac{1}{r-1-\alpha}\right).

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
C,N_{0}>0
be constants such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})\leq Cn^{1+\alpha}
for every
n\geq N_{0}
.
Let
\delta\coloneqq\frac{1-(r-1-\alpha)p}{4}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)
.
Fix
\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}
and let
K=K(r,\alpha,p,\varepsilon)
be the constant returned by Lemma
3.1
.
Let
C_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq 2^{p}r^{p}K^{p-1}C^{p}
and
N_{1}\coloneqq{(2N_{0})^{\frac{1-3\delta}{\delta}}}/{C_{\mathcal{F}}^{1/\delta}}
.

Suppose to the contrary that there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph
\mathcal{G}
on

\displaystyle W_{i}\coloneqq\left\{v\in U_{2}\colon d_{G}(v)\in[2^{i-1}\cdot n^{1/\ell+\varepsilon},2^{i}\cdot n^{1/\ell+\varepsilon})\right\}.

Suppose to the contrary that

.
Then, it follows from the Pigeonhole Principle that there exists

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}^{p}(v)\geq\frac{\sum_{v\in U_{2}}d_{G}^{p}(v)}{t}\geq\frac{n^{p}}{t}.

Let

be the real number such that

.
It follows from the definition of

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}(v)\geq\sum_{v\in W_{i}}\frac{d_{G}^{p}(v)}{(2n^{\beta})^{p-1}}=\frac{\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}^{p}(v)}{2^{p-1}n^{(p-1)\beta}}\geq\frac{n^{p-(p-1)\beta}}{2^{p-1}t}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|G[W_{i},V_{2}]|=\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}(v)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}(v)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}}\left(\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{G}(v)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\ell}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(|W_{i}|\cdot n^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}}\left(\frac{n^{p-(p-1)\beta}}{2^{p-1}t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\ell}}.

Since

and

\displaystyle\beta\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}\right)+\left(p-(p-1)\beta\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\ell}\right)=\frac{1+\beta}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|G[W_{i},V_{2}]|\geq\left(|W_{i}|\cdot n\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}}\frac{n^{\varepsilon/2}}{\left(2^{p-1}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\ell}}}.

Since

and

is sufficiently large, it follows from Theorem

that

∎

Let

.

Proof of Theorem

Let
F=F[W_{1},W_{2}]

.
Let

.
Let
C\coloneqq 2\left(\frac{|V(F)|^{s}}{s!}+|V(F)|\right)
.
Let

be an
n

n

By symmetry, we may assume that
\sum_{v\in V_{1}}d_{G}^{s}(v)\geq\frac{\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{s}}{2}\geq\frac{Cn^{s}}{2}

Choose uniformly at random

from

Let

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathbf{X}|\right]=\sum_{v\in V_{1}}\left(\frac{d_{G}(v)}{n}\right)^{s}=\frac{\sum_{v\in V_{1}}d_{G}^{s}(v)}{n^{s}}\geq\frac{Cn^{s}/2}{n^{s}}=\frac{C}{2}.

Let

Recall from Fact

Problem 7.4
.

Let
r>t\geq 2
be integers and
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of
r
-partite
r
-graphs such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})=n^{\beta+o(1)}
.
Determine the exponent of
\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})
for all
p>1
.

Given two graphs
Q
and
G
, we use

to denote the number of copies of
Q
in
G
.
The
generalized Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})
is the maximum number of copies of

in an

-vertex

-free graph.
The generalized Turán problem was first considered by Erdős in
[
Erd62
]
, and was systematically studied by Alon–Shikhelman in
[

]
.

Given integers
p\geq r>t\geq 0
, the
(r,t)
-book with
p
-pages
, denoted by

, is the graph constructed as follows
\colon

•

Take
p

, each of size
r
, such that there exists a
t
-set

.

•

Place a copy of
K_{r}
on each

.

Observe that
B_{1,2,p}
is simply a star graph with
p
edges.

In parallel, one could define the
(t,r,p)
-norm
of a graph as follows
\colon

Given a graph
G
and a
t

that induces a copy of

denote the number of copies of

.
Let

\displaystyle\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{t,r,p}\coloneqq\sum d_{G,r}^{p}(S),

where the summation is taken over all

that induce a copy of

in

.
Similarly, let

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{t,r,p}(n,F)\coloneqq\max\left\{\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{t,r,p}\colon\text{$v(G)=n$ and $G$ is $F$-free}\right\}.

\displaystyle N(B_{t,r,p},G)=\sum\binom{d_{G,r}(S)}{p}\leq\frac{1}{p!}\sum d_{G,r}^{p}(S)=\frac{\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{t,r,p}}{p!},

in

.

For a bipartite graph

The following theorem can be derived through relatively straightforward modifications to the proofs presented in this paper, so we omit the details.

Theorem 7.5
.

Suppose that
F=F[W_{1},W_{2}]
is a bipartite graph such that

for some constants
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
and every

.
Then there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle Z_{p,\mathrm{left}}(m,n,F)\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathcal{F}}\left(m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{\beta p}+(m+n^{p})\log^{\frac{p_{\ast}-1}{\delta}+1}n\right),&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in\left[1,\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta}\right),\\
C_{\mathcal{F}}\left(m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}+m+n^{p}\right)\log n,&\quad\text{if}\quad p=\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta},\\
\left(\tau_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{F})-1\right)n^{p}+o_{n}(n^{p})+o_{m}(m^{p}),&\quad\text{if}\quad p>\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta}.\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle Z_{p,\mathrm{right}}(m,n,F)\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathcal{F}}\left(m^{\alpha p}n^{1-p(1-\beta)}+(m^{p}+n)\log^{\frac{p_{\ast}-1}{\delta}+1}m\right),&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in\left[1,\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta}\right),\\
C_{\mathcal{F}}\left(m^{p\alpha}n^{1-p(1-\beta)}+m^{p}+n\right)\log m,&\quad\text{if}\quad p=\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta},\\
\left(\tau_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{F})-1\right)m^{p}+o_{n}(n^{p})+o_{m}(m^{p}),&\quad\text{if}\quad p>\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta}.\end{cases}

Here,
p_{\ast}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2-\alpha-\beta}
and
\delta\coloneqq\frac{1-p(2-\alpha-\beta)}{2}
.

Remark.

By summing
Z_{p,\mathrm{left}}(m,n,F)
and
Z_{p,\mathrm{right}}(m,n,F)
, we obtain an upper bound for
\mathrm{ex}_{p}(m,n,F)
and the two-sided

. Here,

represents the maximum
p
-norm of a bipartite graph

with
|V_{1}|=m
and
|V_{2}|=n
that does not contain an ordered copy of

.

|V_{2}|=n
. Let
\varepsilon\coloneqq\delta/2=1-p(2-\alpha-\beta)
.
Suppose that
G
does not contain any ordered copy of
F=F[W_{1},W_{2}]
.
Suppose to the contrary that

\displaystyle\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}\geq C\left(m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{\beta p}+(m+n^{p})\log^{\frac{p_{\ast}-1}{\delta}+1}n\right),

where
C>0
is a large constant.

We may assume that

\displaystyle m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}\geq(m+n^{p})\log^{\frac{p_{\ast}-1}{\delta}}n,

(16)

since otherwise, by Proposition
A.1
, we are done.
Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
, for every bipartite graph
H=H[U_{1},U_{2}]
, let

\displaystyle\Phi(H)\coloneqq\frac{\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|U_{1}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|U_{2}|^{p\beta}}.

Define a sequence of bipartite graphs
G_{i}=G_{i}[A_{i},B_{i}]
for
1\leq i\leq k
for some integer
k
with

(i)

A_{0}=V_{1}\supseteq A_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq A_{k}
and
B_{0}=V_{2}\supseteq B_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq B_{k}
,

(ii)

|A_{i}|=\frac{|V_{1}|}{2^{i}}
and
|B_{i}|=\frac{|V_{2}|}{2^{i}}
for
i\in[k]
,

(iii)

\Phi(G_{i+1})\geq 2^{\delta-\varepsilon}\cdot\Phi(G_{i})
for
i\in[0,k-1]
,

(iv)

for every
i\in[k-1]
, we have
\sum_{u\in U_{i}}d_{G_{i-1}}^{p}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert G_{i-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}
, where

\displaystyle U_{i}\coloneqq\left\{v\in A_{i-1}\colon d_{G_{i-1}}(v)\geq\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{i-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{i-1}|}\right)^{1/p}\right\},

(v)

we have
\sum_{u\in U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}^{p}(u)<\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}
, where

\displaystyle U_{k}\coloneqq\left\{v\in A_{k-1}\colon d_{G_{k-1}}(v)\geq\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{1/p}\right\}.

Claim A.4
.

We have
k\leq k_{\ast}\coloneqq\frac{2}{\delta}\log\log n
.

Proof of Claim
A.4
.

Notice from (
16
) that for every
i\leq k_{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle|A_{i}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{i}|^{p\beta}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m}{2^{i}}\right)^{1-p(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{n}{2^{i}}\right)^{p\beta}=\frac{m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}}{2^{i\cdot(1-p(1-\alpha)+p\beta)}}\geq\frac{m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}}{2^{i\cdot p_{\ast}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(m+n^{p})2^{k_{\ast}(p_{\ast}-1)}}{2^{i\cdot p_{\ast}}}\geq\frac{m+n^{p}}{2^{i}}\geq|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|^{p}.

(17)

Suppose to the contrary that
k>k_{\ast}
.
Then it follows from
(iii)
that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert G_{k_{\ast}}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{k}|^{p\beta}}=\Phi(G_{k})\geq\left(2^{\delta-\varepsilon}\right)^{k_{\ast}}\cdot\Phi(G)=2^{\frac{\delta}{2}\cdot k_{\ast}}\cdot\Phi(G)\geq C\log n,

which, combining with (
17
), implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert G_{k_{\ast}}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}\geq C|A_{k}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{k}|^{p\beta}\log n\geq\frac{C}{2}\left(|A_{k}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{k}|^{p\beta}+|A_{k}|+|B_{k}|^{p}\right)\log n,

contradicting Proposition
A.1
.
∎

Since
\sum_{u\in U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}^{p}(u)<\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon}}\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}
(due to
(v)
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in A_{k}\setminus U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}^{p}(u)=\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}-\sum_{u\in U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}^{p}(u)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}.

On the other hand, it follows from the definition of
U_{k}
and (
16
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in A_{k}\setminus U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}^{p}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in A_{k}\setminus U_{k}}d_{G_{k-1}}(u)\cdot\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq|G_{k-1}|\cdot\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|A_{k-1}|^{\alpha}|B_{k-1}|^{\beta}+|A_{k-1}|+|B_{k-1}|\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C|A_{k-1}|^{\alpha}|B_{k-1}|^{\beta}\cdot\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}\leq 2C|A_{k-1}|^{\alpha}|B_{k-1}|^{\beta}\cdot\left(\frac{2\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}\leq\frac{2^{2p-1}C^{p}}{(1-2^{-\varepsilon})^{p}}|A_{k-1}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{k-1}|^{p\beta}.

Combining this with
(iii)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}

\displaystyle=\Phi(G)\cdot m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}\leq\Phi(G_{k-1})\cdot m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert G_{k-1}\right\rVert_{p,\mathrm{left}}}{|A_{k-1}|^{1-p(1-\alpha)}|B_{k-1}|^{p\beta}}\cdot m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}\leq\frac{2^{2p-1}C^{p}}{(1-2^{-\varepsilon})^{p}}\cdot m^{1-p(1-\alpha)}n^{p\beta}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
A.3
.
∎

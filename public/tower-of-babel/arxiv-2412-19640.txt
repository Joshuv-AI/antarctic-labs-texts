Stringy scaling of multi-tensor hard string scattering amplitudes and the K K -identities
Sheng-Hong Lai, Jen-Chi Lee, Yi Yang

Abstract
We calculate n n -point hard string scattering amplitudes ( H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA ) with n − 2 n-2 tachyons and 2 2 tensor states at arbitrary mass levels. We discover the stringy scaling behavior of these H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA . It is found that for H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA with more than 2 2 transverse directions, the degree of stringy scaling dim ℳ 2 \mathcal{M}_{2} decreases comparing to the degree of stringy scaling dim ℳ 1 \mathcal{M}_{1} of the n − 1 n-1 tachyons and 1 1 tensor H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA calculated previously. Moreover, we propose a set of K K -identities which is the key to demonstrate the stringy scaling behavior of H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA . We explicitly prove both the diagonal and off-diagonal K K -identities for the 4 4 -point H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA and give numerical proofs of these K K -identities for some higher point H ​ S ​ S ​ A HSSA .

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we use the saddle-point method to calculate
n
-point
HSSA

with
n-2
tachyons and
2
tensor states at arbitrary mass levels. During the
multi-tensor saddle-point calculation, one encounters the
double naive
energy order problem
, and it turns out that one has to include contraction
terms from
\partial_{2}X_{2}\cdot\cdot
and
\partial_{3}X_{3}\cdot\cdot
in
order to achieve the consistent ratios calculated by the method of decoupling
of
ZNS
in Eq.(
2.3

More importantly, we discover the stringy scaling
oleg
behavior of
these
HSSA
. For
HSSA
with
r=1
,
2
, we found that dim
\mathcal{M}_{2}=

dim
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. However, for

2
transverse

\mathcal{M}_{2}
of the

2

decreases
comparing to the degree of scaling dim
\mathcal{M}_{1}
of the
n-1
tachyons
and
1

calculated previously.

In the saddle-point calculation of the general
n
-point multi-tensor
HSSA
,
the saddle-point
(\tilde{z}_{2},\tilde{z}_{3},\cdots,\tilde{z}_{n-2})
may
not be exactly solvable. So instead of directly solving the saddle-point

4
-point

and give

For the general
n
-point

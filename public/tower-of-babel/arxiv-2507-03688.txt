Time-asymptotic self-similarity of the damped compressible Euler equations in parabolic scaling variables
Thomas Eiter, Stefanie Schindler

Abstract
We study the long-time behavior of solutions to the compressible Euler equations with frictional damping in the whole space, where we prescribe direction-dependent values for the density at spatial infinity. To this end, we transform the system into parabolic scaling variables and derive a relative entropy inequality, which allows to conclude the convergence of the density towards a self-similar solution to the porous medium equation while the associated limit momentum is governed by Darcy’s law. Moreover, we obtain convergence rates that explicitly depend on the flatness of the limit profile. While we focus on weak solutions in the one-dimensional case, we extend our results to energy-variational solutions in the multi-dimensional setting. Keywords: Euler equations, compressible fluid, frictional damping, porous medium equation, Darcy’s law, self-similarity, parabolic scaling, relative entropy, energy-variational solutions. MSC2020: 35B40, 35C06, 35Q31, 76N10, 76N15, 76S05.

Introduction

Consider the flow of
an isentropic gas
subject to external friction, such as a flow through a porous medium,
described by the damped Euler equations. We first consider these equations
on the real line, given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{t}+\widetilde{m}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}_{t}+\Big(\frac{\widetilde{m}^{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}+\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})\Big)_{x}+\alpha\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle=0,

where
t\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote time and space variables, respectively.
The functions
\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}(t,x)\geq 0
and
\widetilde{m}=\widetilde{m}(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote the density and the momentum of the fluid.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})
represents the pressure as a function of the density, where
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(z)>0
for all
z\in(0,\infty)
.
An important particular case is the polytropic gas,
which fulfills the so-called
\gamma
-law

\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})=k\widetilde{\rho}^{\gamma}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for some }\gamma\geq 1,\ k>0.

(1.2)

The term
\alpha\widetilde{m}
in the momentum equation describes frictional damping
with friction coefficient
\alpha\geq 0
.
The initial data

(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})=(\widetilde{\rho}(0,\cdot),\widetilde{m}(0,\cdot))
satisfy

(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})(x)\to(\rho_{\pm},0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }x\to\pm\infty

for given constants
\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\geq 0
,
which corresponds to possibly infinite mass reservoirs
at positive and negative spatial infinity.

The natural entropy for the damped hyperbolic system (
1.1
)
is given by

\widetilde{\eta}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})=\frac{\widetilde{m}^{2}}{2\widetilde{\rho}}+h(\widetilde{\rho}),

which is the sum of kinetic and internal energy.
Here, the function
h
and the pressure
\mathfrak{p}
are linked to each other through

\mathfrak{p}(z)=zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pth^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(z)}{z},

(1.3)

where the second identity follows directly from the first one.
If we assume (
1.2
),
then we may choose the potential
h
of the form

h(\widetilde{\rho})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\gamma{-}1}\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})=\frac{k}{\gamma{-}1}\widetilde{\rho}^{\gamma}&\text{for }\gamma>1,\\
k\,\widetilde{\rho}\log\widetilde{\rho}&\text{for }\gamma=1.\end{cases}

(1.4)

The entropy flux associated with
\widetilde{\eta}
is
given by

\widetilde{q}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})=\frac{\widetilde{m}}{\widetilde{\rho}}\big(\frac{\widetilde{m}^{2}}{2\widetilde{\rho}}+\widetilde{\rho}h^{\prime}(\widetilde{\rho})\big).

Then, smooth solutions to (
1.1
) satisfy the local entropy identity

\widetilde{\eta}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})_{t}+\widetilde{q}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})_{x}+\alpha\frac{\widetilde{m}^{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}=0,

(1.5)

which expresses the energy evolution during the process.

In this article, we are interested in the long-time behavior of solutions
to the damped hyperbolic system (
1.1
).
If the equations were formulated in a finite interval in space
with suitable boundary conditions,
one would expect solutions to converge to a steady state as
t\to\infty
.
In contrast, system (
1.1
)
is formulated on the real line,
and we may have an infinite mass supply at
\pm\infty
.
While for
\rho_{+}=\rho_{-}
the convergence to a constant equilibrium state is expected,
the limit behavior in the case
\rho_{+}\neq\rho_{-}

is more involved.
Hsiao and Liu
[
HsL93
]
firstly proved that the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1.1
) can be described by the self-similar solutions
(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}},\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}})

of the equations

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{t}+\widetilde{m}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})_{x}+\alpha\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle=0,

with the same spatial limits
(\widetilde{\rho}(x),\widetilde{m}(x))\to(\rho_{\pm},0)
as
x\to\pm\infty
.
Equivalently, this system can be written as the (parabolic) porous medium equation for the density
\widetilde{\rho}
,
where the momentum
\widetilde{m}
is determined by Darcy’s law,
that is,

\widetilde{\rho}_{t}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})_{xx},\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{m}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho})_{x}.

(1.6)

In the literature, such self-similar solutions
(\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}},\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}})
are usually called
nonlinear diffusion waves
, as for example in
[
CY∗18
]
.
In the work
[
HsL93
]
,
a uniform convergence rate was shown
under increased regularity assumptions on the solution
(\rho,m)
.
In
[
HsL92
]
, the same authors derived similar results
for the damped
p
-system,
which is a formulation of system (
1.1
) in Lagrangian coordinates.
Since then, these results have been extended in various ways, see
[
Nis96
,
SeX97
,
NWY00
,
HuP03
,
Mei09
,
GeT19
]
and references therein, especially by derivation of improved convergence rates but also by allowing for more general solution classes.
The equations have also been studied subject to a time-dependent damping, see for instance
[
CY∗18
,
GHW22
,
GH∗23
]
.
In the case of finite mass, we refer to
[
HMP05
,
HPW11
,
GeH19
]
, where the convergence towards the famous Barenblatt solution
is shown.

In the paper
[
LaT13
]
,
the so-called diffusive scaling of the system (
1.1
) is considered,
and it is shown that weak entropy solutions converge to
smooth solutions
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})
to (
1.6
)
as the scaling parameter goes to zero.
This convergence is quantified in terms of the relative entropy.
Here, we follow this idea of deriving relative entropy estimates,
but instead of the physical variables
(t,x)
, we perform the analysis
in parabolic scaling variables defined by

\tau:=\log(1{+}t)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pty:=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1{+}t}}.

(1.7)

We transform the equations (
1.1
) into these new coordinates.
With
\rho(\tau,y):=\widetilde{\rho}(t,x)
and
n(\tau,y):=\mathrm{e}^{\tau/2}m(\tau,y)=(1{+}t)^{1/2}\,\widetilde{m}(t,x)
,
a straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{y}{2}\rho_{y}-n_{y},

(1.8)

\displaystyle n_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{y}{2}n_{y}+\frac{1}{2}n-\Big(\frac{n^{2}}{\rho}\Big)_{y}-\mathrm{e}^{\tau}\big(\mathfrak{p}(\rho)_{y}+\alpha n\big),

while the according initial data
(\rho_{0},n_{0})=(\rho(0,\cdot),n(0,\cdot))
satisfy the asymptotic conditions

(\rho_{0},n_{0})(y)\to(\rho_{\pm},0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }y\to\pm\infty.

(1.9)

Observe that we scale the magnitude of the momentum variable
to ensure that Darcy’s law can be fulfilled asymptotically in time.
Indeed,
by this scaling, the pressure term and the damping term in (
1.8
)
have the same exponentially growing prefactor,
which indicates that Darcy’s law needs to be satisfied asymptotically to equilibrate the system.
Moreover, the scaling creates the new drift terms
\frac{y}{2}\rho_{y}
and
\frac{y}{2}n_{y}
, which will provide the important terms in the relative entropy inequality
that lead to exponential decay
with respect to the scaled time
\tau
.

Transformation of
the porous medium equation from (
1.6
)
into parabolic scaling variables and combination with the spatial limits yields

\rho_{\tau}-\frac{y}{2}\rho_{y}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathfrak{p}(\rho)_{yy}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ with }\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{y\to\pm\infty}\rho(\tau,y)=\rho_{\pm}.

(1.10)

Steady-state solutions
\rho^{*}=\rho^{*}(y)
of (
1.10
) correspond to self-similar solutions
\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}
to the original porous medium equation via
\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,x)=\rho^{*}(y)
,
and these self-similar solutions appear as long-time limits of general solutions
\widetilde{\rho}
.
Expressed in scaling variables
(\tau,y)
,
this means that the solution
\rho
to (
1.10
)
converges to
\rho^{*}
as
\tau\to\infty
.
In this paper,
we perform a similar convergence study
for solutions
(\rho,n)
to the scaled damped Euler equations (
1.8
),
which for
\tau\to\infty
converge towards the similarity profile
(\rho^{*},n^{*})

characterized by the profile equations

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathfrak{p}(\rho^{*})_{yy}+\frac{y}{2}\rho^{*}_{y},\hskip 10.00002pt\ 0=n^{*}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathfrak{p}(\rho^{*})_{y}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{with}\ \lim_{y\to\pm\infty}(\rho^{*},n^{*})(y)=(\rho_{\pm},0).

(1.11)

This approach is in accordance with
[
MiS24
]
,
where the long-time behavior of solutions to a reaction-diffusion system was studied.
The scaling (
1.7
)
emphasizes the time-asymptotic parabolic behavior
of the system,
and it was already used by Gallay and Raugel
[
GaR98
,
GaR97
,
GaR00
]
to analyze the asymptotic behavior of scalar damped hyperbolic equations.
To the authors’ knowledge, the present paper is the first one to adapt this method to a damped hyperbolic system
and to combine it with a relative entropy approach.

The general idea of proving convergence by a relative entropy estimate
traces back to the works by Dafermos
[
Daf79b
,
Daf79a
]
and DiPerna
[
DiP79
]
.
It has been refined and generalized by many authors in recent years; see for example
[
HuP06
,
LaT13
,
GeH19
,
GHW22
]
for problems related the damped Euler equations.
We shall first show that the relative entropy
{\mathcal{E}}

satisfies an inequality
of the form

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\mathcal{E}}(\tau)+{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}(\tau)\leq\Xi,

(1.12)

where the term
\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}(\tau)
arises from the drift terms

\frac{y}{2}\rho_{y}
and
\frac{y}{2}n_{y}
in (
1.8
)
and is thus due to the parabolic scaling.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}\geq 0
is a dissipative term associated with the frictional damping, while the term
\Xi
does not have a clear sign
and has to be controlled by the other terms.
To this end, the term
\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}(\tau)

is necessary,
and its appearance can be considered the main effect of the transformation into scaling variables.
Moreover, it finally leads to the exponential decay of
{\mathcal{E}}(\tau)
as
\tau\to\infty

by means of Gronwall’s inequality.
However, since the limiting profile is not a true steady state if we consider different limits at spatial infinity,
the function
{\mathcal{E}}
cannot be a true Lyapunov function.
Instead, we only show that
{\mathcal{E}}
becomes a Lyapunov function for large
\tau
, which is sufficient for the convergence and comparable with the results in
[
MiS24
]
.

Notice that smooth solutions to (
1.8
) even satisfy (
1.12
) as an equality.
Since those exist only up to a finite time in general,
we consider a framework of weak solutions instead,
where results on the global existence
of solutions satisfying an entropy inequality
are available in the one-dimensional case,
see
[
DCL89
,
HuP06
]
.
However, in the multi-dimensional case,
analogous results for weak solutions seem to be out of reach,
which is why
we instead study
a further relaxed solvability concept in this setting,
namely energy-variational solutions.
This concept was recently introduced for a general class of hyperbolic conservation laws in
[
EiL24
]
,
including the compressible Euler equations,
and has further been investigated for other models, see
[
ALR24
,
EHL23
,
LaR23
,
Las24
,
Las25
]
.
We establish the global existence of energy-variational solutions
and show that our results on
the long-time behavior of weak solutions
can be transferred to this case.

The article is structured as follows.
In Section
2
we introduce
weak solutions
to the scaled system (
1.8
),
and we present the main results on the convergence towards
solutions of the profile equations (
1.11
) as
\tau\to\infty

in the one-dimensional case.
In Section
3

we derive a relative entropy inequality
for weak solutions,
which we employ in Section
4

to prove the main results in the one-dimensional case.
Finally, in Section
5
we
consider energy-variational solutions to the damped Euler equations in multiple dimensions.
We show their global-in-time existence,
and we extended our results on the long-time behavior
to this generalized solution class
in a straightforward way.

Let the pressure

1.11
).
For initial data
(\rho_{0},n_{0})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}})^{2}

such that
\rho_{0}\geq 0

and
{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty
,
let
(\rho,n)
be a weak solution to (
1.8
)
satisfying
(E1)
or
(E2)
.
Then
{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})<\infty

and

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho(\tau),n(\tau)\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{1}{2}{-}\theta)\tau+\frac{1}{2}\mu}\Big({\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})+\frac{K}{\theta}\Big)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.a.~}\tau>0.

(2.11)

Here, the constants
\theta
,
\mu
and
K
are given by

\displaystyle\theta:=\max\{2,\gamma{-}1\}\big\|\big[\tfrac{1}{\alpha}h^{\prime}(\rho^{*})_{yy}\big]_{+}\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})},\;\ \mu:=\Big\|\frac{|R^{*}|}{2k(\rho^{*})^{\gamma}}+\frac{3|R^{*}|}{2\rho^{*}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})},\;\ K:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|R^{*}|\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
[\cdot]_{+}:=\max\{0,\cdot\}
and

R^{*}:=-\frac{y}{2}n^{*}_{y}-\frac{1}{2}n^{*}+\Big(\frac{(n^{*})^{2}}{\rho^{*}}\Big)_{y}.

(2.12)

In particular, if
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
, then

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho(\tau),n(\tau)\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})\to 0

as
\tau\to\infty
.
Moreover, in this case, it holds

\alpha\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\,\Big|\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{n^{*}}{\rho^{*}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{1}{2}{-}\theta)\tau+\frac{1}{2}\mu}\Big({\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})+\frac{K}{\theta}\Big)+2K\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}

(2.13)

for a.a.
\tau\geq 2\log(\frac{2\mu}{1-2\theta})
.

We emphasize that all constants are explicit and do not depend on the solution
(\rho,n)

but only on the limit vales
\rho_{\pm}
, the pressure
\mathfrak{p}
and the damping coefficient
\alpha
.
Since
\rho^{*}(y)=\rho_{*}(\sqrt{\alpha}y)
and
n^{*}(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}n_{*}(\sqrt{\alpha}y)

if
(\rho_{*},n_{*})
is the solution to (
1.11
) for
\alpha=1
,
we see that

\mu
and
K
scale as
\mu\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
and
K\sim\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
while
\theta
is independent of
\alpha
.
In particular, independently of
\alpha
,
the condition
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
can be satisfied
by choosing
|\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}|
small enough,
due to (
2.10
).
Such flatness conditions for the profile function also appear in other articles, see
[
HsL92
,
Nis96
,
MiS24
]

for instance.
One can also generalize Theorem
2.3
to more general pressure laws that grow like (
1.2
) asymptotically.
But then the occurring constants would be less explicit.

While the assumption
{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty

is natural for the initial data,
we can also assume
{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})<\infty

for any other pair
(\overline{\rho},\overline{n})
such that
\overline{\rho}-\hat{\rho}

and
\overline{n}
have sufficient decay as
|y|\to\infty
,
see Proposition
3.3
below.
For instance, the choice
(\overline{\rho},\overline{n})=(\rho_{\infty},0)
with
\rho_{\infty}
satisfying (
2.7
),
or the asymptotic profile
(\overline{\rho},\overline{n})=(\rho^{*},n^{\ast})
are possible.
In particular, we do not impose any smallness conditions on the initial data,
and even vacuum is allowed to occur, at least locally,
for the initial data as well as during the evolution.

Focusing on the kinetic energy,
from (
2.11
) we obtain the pointwise bound

\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(\tau)\,\Big|\frac{n(\tau)}{\rho(\tau)}-\frac{n^{*}}{\rho^{*}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{1}{2}{-}\theta)\tau}

for some
C>0
and a.a.
\tau>0
,
which does not imply any convergence of
n
towards
n^{*}
as
\tau\to\infty
.
Although (
2.13
) does not give a pointwise bound either,
it yields convergence in a sense of integral means.

In the case of coincident limits
\rho_{+}=\rho_{-}
,
we derive a faster convergence rate than in Theorem
2.3
.
Moreover, the damping term is not necessary,
and we can consider more general pressure functions
\mathfrak{p}
.
We can even treat the case
\rho_{\pm}=0
if we additionally assume that the pressure satisfies

\exists\,c>0:\ \mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(z)\leq c\,\frac{\mathfrak{p}(z)}{z}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathfrak{p}(z)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z<\infty.

(2.14)

Observe that (
2.14
) is satisfied if
\mathfrak{p}
is given by (
1.2
) for
\gamma>1
, but not for
\gamma=1
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
\rho_{+}=\rho_{-}\equiv\hat{\rho}>0
,
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
with
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>0
in
(0,\infty)
.
Consider initial data
(\rho_{0},n_{0})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}})^{2}

such that
\rho_{0}\geq 0

and
{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty
.
Let
(\rho,n)
be a weak solution to (
1.8
)
satisfying
(E1)
or
(E2)
.
Then

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho(\tau),n(\tau)\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\;{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.a.~}\tau>0

(2.15)

and

\alpha\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|n|^{2}}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\;{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.a.~}\tau>0.

(2.16)

If
\mathfrak{p}
additionally satisfies (
2.14
),
then
the same statement holds also for
\rho_{+}=\rho_{-}\equiv\hat{\rho}=0
.

In the case of coincident limits for
y\to\pm\infty
, where the self-similar profile is constant,
the friction coefficient
\alpha
can be set to zero
without affecting the convergence rate of the total relative entropy.
Only the faster convergence rate of the relative kinetic energy,
coming from (
2.16
), would be lost for
\alpha=0
.
In contrast, for different limits, we need
\alpha>0
to define the self-similar profile by (
1.11
). Nevertheless, even in that case, the damping term can be omitted during the estimates without affecting the convergence
rate of the relative entropy,
as will become evident during the proof.

Theorem
2.4
also covers the important case of
finite initial total mass.
However, due to the parabolic scaling,
the mass corresponding to solutions to (
1.8
) decays exponentially.
In particular, we obtain the zero profile
(\rho^{*},n^{*})\equiv(0,0)

as limit objects in this case,
rather than the famous Barenblatt profile.

Remark 2.5
.

Let us return to the original momentum
m=\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}n

and
m^{*}=\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}n^{*}
.
Then Theorem
2.3
(with
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
) and Theorem
2.4
yield exponential
convergence of the relative kinetic energy and the relative internal energy in the sense

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2}\rho(\tau)\,\Big|\frac{m(\tau)}{\rho(\tau)}-\frac{m^{*}}{\rho^{*}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(\rho(\tau)\,|\,\rho^{*})\,\mathrm{d}y+\alpha\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\tau^{\prime}}\!\rho\,\Big|\frac{m}{\rho}-\frac{m^{*}}{\rho^{*}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\theta)\tau}

for some
C>0
and a.a.
\tau>0
.
In terms of the original time variable
t=\mathrm{e}^{\tau}-1
, this yields
the algebraic pointwise decay

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\eta}\big(\widetilde{\rho}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t}),\widetilde{m}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t}),\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C(1{+}t)^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\theta)},

where
\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,x)=\rho^{*}(y)
and
\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,x)=m^{*}(y)
denote the corresponding self-similar solution to (
1.6
).
Due to the strict convexity of the relative entropy
\widetilde{\eta}
, this results in the following pointwise convergences for a.a.
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

\widetilde{\rho}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})-\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{m}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})-\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }t\to\infty.

Note that in terms of the original spatial variable
x
, no corresponding statement can be made, as the transformation of the integral results in an additional factor
\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}
.
We further obtain the estimate

\alpha\int_{t}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\rho}(t,y\sqrt{1{+}t^{\prime}})\,\bigg|\frac{\widetilde{m}(t^{\prime},y\sqrt{1{+}t^{\prime}})}{\widetilde{\rho}(t^{\prime},y\sqrt{1{+}t^{\prime}})}-\frac{\widetilde{m}_{\mathrm{se}}(t^{\prime},y\sqrt{1{+}t^{\prime}})}{\widetilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{se}}(t^{\prime},y\sqrt{1{+}t^{\prime}})}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq C(1{+}t)^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\theta)},

which provides an additional rate for the convergence of the time-averaged kinetic energy
as
t\to\infty
.

3
Relative entropy inequality

In this section, we consider weak solutions
(\rho,n)

satisfying
(E1)
or
(E2)
and derive an inequality for the relative entropy

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})

defined in (
2.4
),
where
(\overline{\rho},\overline{n})
is a suitable reference pair.
To this end, we define the residuals

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\overline{\rho}_{\tau}-\frac{y}{2}\overline{\rho}_{y}+\overline{n}_{y},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\overline{n}_{\tau}-\frac{y}{2}\overline{n}_{y}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{n}+\Big(\frac{\overline{n}^{2}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big)_{y}+\mathrm{e}^{\tau}\big(\mathfrak{p}(\overline{\rho})_{y}+\alpha\overline{n}\big),

and the error term

\xi:=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}
with

\displaystyle\xi_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\Big(\frac{\overline{n}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big)_{y}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\rho\Big|\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{\overline{n}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big|^{2}+\mathfrak{p}(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})\big),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\overline{R}\;\frac{\rho}{\overline{\rho}}\Big(\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{\overline{n}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big),

\displaystyle\xi_{3}

\displaystyle:=-(\rho-\overline{\rho})h^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\rho})\overline{R}_{1}.

Here,
\mathfrak{p}(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho}):=\mathfrak{p}(\rho)-\mathfrak{p}(\overline{\rho})-\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(\overline{\rho})(\rho{-}\overline{\rho})
is the relative pressure and
\overline{R}:=\frac{\overline{n}}{\overline{\rho}}\overline{R}_{1}-\overline{R}_{2}
.
We further set

{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n}):=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\,\Big|\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{\overline{n}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\Xi=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\Xi_{j}

(3.3)

with
\Xi_{j}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi_{j}\,\mathrm{d}y
,
j=1,2,3
.
We shall show the relative entropy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \forall\,\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty);[0,\infty)):&\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}-\varphi^{\prime}\,{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})&+\varphi\Big(\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})+{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\quad\leq\varphi(0)\,{\mathcal{E}}(0;\rho_{0},n_{0}\,|\,\overline{\rho}(0),\overline{n}(0))+\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\,\Xi\,\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{aligned}

(3.4)

Before we give separate proofs under the assumptions
(E1)
and
(E2)
,
we collect some results useful for the derivation of (
3.4
).

3.1
Preparatory results

In what follows, we prove several lemmata.
In order to treat the case of vacuum for the reference density,
we first derive additional properties on
\mathfrak{p}
and
h

if the pressure satisfies (
2.14
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
with
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>0
in
(0,\infty)
,
and such that (
2.14
) holds.
Then
h\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
with
h^{\prime}(0)=0
,
and
\rho h^{\prime}(\rho)\leq ch(\rho\,|\,0)

with
c
as in (
2.14
).

Proof.

We can compute
h
as a solution to the ordinary differential equation in (
1.3
), which gives the representation

h(\rho)=\rho\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\rho}\frac{p(z)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

for any
\rho_{0}>0
.
Due to (
2.14
), we can send
\rho\to 0
,
which yields

h\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
with
h^{\prime}(0)=0
.
By (
2.14
) and (
1.3
), we can further estimate

\rho h^{\prime}(\rho)=\rho\int_{0}^{\rho}h^{\prime\prime}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=\rho\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{p^{\prime}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z\leq c\rho\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{p(z)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z=c\,h(\rho\,|\,0)

which completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma gives control
of the relative entropy flux by the relative entropy, both defined in (
2.4
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in C([0,\infty))\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
with
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}>0
in
(0,\infty)
.
Let
0<\delta<M
.
Then there exists
C>0
such that

|q(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})|\leq C\Big(\frac{|n_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{|n_{2}|}{\rho_{2}}+\mathrm{e}^{\tau/2}\Big)\eta(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})

(3.5)

for all
\tau\geq 0
,
all
(\rho_{1},n_{1})\in\{(0,0)\}\cup(0,M]\times{\mathbb{R}}

and all
(\rho_{2},n_{2})\in[\delta,M]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
additionally satisfies (
2.14
),
the statement
also holds for
(\rho_{2},n_{2})=(0,0)
.

Proof.

First consider
(\rho_{1},n_{1})=(0,0)
and
(\rho_{2},n_{2})\in[\delta,M]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then we have

|q(\tau;0,0\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})|=\frac{|n_{2}|}{\rho_{2}}\big|-h(\rho_{2})+h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\rho_{2}\big|=\frac{|n_{2}|}{\rho_{2}}\eta(\tau;0,0\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2}),

which shows (
3.5
) in this case.

Now let
\rho_{1}>0
and
\rho_{2}\in(\delta,M)
.
We obtain

\displaystyle|q(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\frac{|n_{1}|}{\rho_{1}},\frac{|n_{2}|}{\rho_{2}}\Big\}\eta(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})+\rho_{1}\big|h^{\prime}(\rho_{1}){-}h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\big|\Big|\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big|.

To estimate the latter term,
consider first the case
0<\rho_{1}<\delta/2
.
We use (
1.3
) to deduce

\rho_{1}\big(h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\big)=h(\rho_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2})+\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{1})-\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{2})
.
First, we have
\big|\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{1})-\mathfrak{p}(\rho_{2})\big|\leq\mathfrak{p}(M)-\mathfrak{p}(\delta/2)
by monotonicity of
\mathfrak{p}
.
Second, we use Taylor’s theorem and
h^{\prime\prime}(z)>0
for
z>0
to conclude

h(\rho_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2})=-\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}(\rho_{1}{-}z)h^{\prime\prime}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\geq\int_{\delta/2}^{\delta}\big(z-\frac{\delta}{2}\big)h^{\prime\prime}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{8}\min_{z\in[\delta/2,\delta]}h^{\prime\prime}(z).

In sum, this gives

\rho_{1}\big|h^{\prime}(\rho_{1}){-}h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\big|\Big|\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big|\leq c\Big(\frac{|n_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{|n_{2}|}{\rho_{2}}\Big)h(\rho_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2})

for some
c>0
in this case.
If
\rho_{1}\geq\delta/2
, we instead estimate

|h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})|\leq\max_{z\leq M}h^{\prime\prime}(z)|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|

and

h(\rho_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{z\in[\delta/2,M]}h^{\prime\prime}(z)\,(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})^{2}
,
which together with Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\big|h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\big|\Big|\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{\tau/2}\,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\rho_{1}\Big|\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{\tau/2}\rho_{1}\,\big|h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathrm{e}^{\tau/2}\eta(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})

for some
c>0
since
\min_{z\in[\delta/2,M]}h^{\prime\prime}(z)>0
.
Collecting all estimates, we arrive at (
3.5
).

Finally,
assume (
2.14
) and
let
(\rho_{2},n_{2})=(0,0)

and
(\rho_{1},n_{1})\in\{(0,0)\}\cup(0,M]\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
q(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,0,0)
is well defined
by Lemma
3.1
, and we obtain

|q(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,0,0)|=\frac{|n_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}\big|\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\frac{n_{1}^{2}}{2\rho_{1}}+\rho_{1}\big(h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(0)\big)\big|\leq(1+c)\frac{|n_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}\eta(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,0,0)

for
c>0
as in (
2.14
), which yields (
3.5
) in this case.
∎

The following auxiliary results focus on the case of a barotropic pressure law of
the form (
1.2
). To begin with, we
introduce the family of functions

\displaystyle F_{p}(z):={\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p(p{-}1)}\big(z^{p}-pz+p-1\big)&\text{for }p\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,1\},\\
z\log z-z+1&\text{for }p=1,\\
z-\log z-1&\text{for }p=0,\end{cases}}

(3.6)

which are determined by the conditions
F_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z)=z^{p{-}2}
and
F_{p}(1)=F_{p}^{\prime}(1)=0
.
These are often used as entropy functions
for reaction-diffusion systems (see e.g.
[
MiM18
,
MiS24
]
),
and they satisfy the lower bound

F_{p}(z)\geq\frac{1/2}{\max\{p,1{-}p\}}\,F_{1/2}(z)=\frac{1}{\max\{p,1{-}p\}}\,(\sqrt{z}-1)^{2}

(3.7)

for all
p>0
and
z>0
,
see
[
MiM18
, eqn. (3.2)]
.
If the potential energy
h
is of the form (
1.4
), then we have

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})=\gamma kF_{\gamma}(\rho/\overline{\rho}){\overline{\rho}}^{\gamma}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\gamma\geq 1,

(3.8)

so that

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})\geq k{\overline{\rho}}^{\gamma-1}\big(\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{\overline{\rho}}\big)^{2}

(3.9)

by (
3.7
).

In the following auxiliary result, we provide conditions that allow to exchange arguments in the relative entropy.
We state the result in a time-independent and a time-dependent version.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be given as in (
1.2
).

i.

Let
\tau\in[0,\infty)
, and let
(\rho_{1},n_{1}),(\rho_{2},n_{2})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}})^{2}
.
Assume that

\rho_{1},\frac{1}{\rho_{1}},\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\Big(\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big),\ \Big(\frac{n_{1}^{2}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}-\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{2}^{2}}\Big),\ h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\,\in\,L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}).

If
(\rho,n)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}})^{2}

such that

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{1},n_{1})<\infty
,
then

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})<\infty

if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})<\infty
.

ii.

Let
I\subset[0,\infty)
be an interval,
and let
(\rho_{1},n_{1}),(\rho_{2},n_{2})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I\times{\mathbb{R}})^{2}
.
Assume that

\rho_{1},\frac{1}{\rho_{1}},\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}\in L^{\infty}(I\times{\mathbb{R}})
and

\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}\Big(\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big),\ \mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\Big(\frac{n_{1}^{2}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}-\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{2}^{2}}\Big),\ h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\,\in\,L^{2}(I\times{\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{\infty}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}).

If
(\rho,n)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I\times{\mathbb{R}})^{2}
such that

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{1},n_{1})\in L^{1}(I)
,
then

{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})\in L^{1}(I)

if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})\in L^{1}(I)
.

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows

\displaystyle\eta(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{1},n_{1})+\eta(\tau;\rho_{1},n_{1}\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})-\eta(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{2},n_{2})

(3.10)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\Big(\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{n_{2}}{\rho_{2}}\Big)(n-n_{1})-\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\Big(\frac{n_{1}^{2}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}-\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{\rho_{2}^{2}}\Big)+h^{\prime}(\rho_{1})-h^{\prime}(\rho_{2})\bigg)(\rho-\rho_{1}).

The claim follows if the right-hand side is in
L^{1}(Q)
,
where
Q={\mathbb{R}}
or
Q=I\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
depending on the case.
Inequality (
3.9
) implies

\rho-\rho_{1}=(\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{\rho_{1}})^{2}+2\sqrt{\rho_{1}}(\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{\rho_{1}})\in L^{1}(Q)+L^{2}(Q),

and similarly,
the decomposition

\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}(n-n_{1})=(\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{\rho_{1}})\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}\sqrt{\rho}\Big(\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}\Big)+\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}\sqrt{\rho}\Big(\frac{n}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}\Big)+\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}\frac{n_{1}}{\rho_{1}}(\rho-\rho_{1})

yields
\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/2}(n-n_{1})\in L^{1}(Q)+L^{2}(Q)
.
In view of the assumptions,
we conclude the integrability of the right-hand side of (
3.10
)
in both cases,
which completes the proof.
∎

We further need the following coercivity estimate of the relative entropy.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
satisfy (
1.2
).
For
\delta,M>0
define the interval
K=[\delta,M]
.
Then there exist constants
r_{0}>0
and
C>0
, depending only on
K
, such that

\eta(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},0)\geq\begin{cases}C\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}|n|^{2}+|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2}\big)&\text{ if }\rho\in[0,r_{0}],\\
C\big((\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}|n|^{2})^{\gamma/(\gamma+1)}+|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{\gamma}\big)&\text{ if }\rho\in(r_{0},\infty),\end{cases}

(3.11)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\overline{\rho}\in K
.
Moreover, if
\overline{\rho}=0
, then (
3.11
) holds with
r_{0}=0
.

Proof.

For given
K=[\delta,M]
, let
r_{0}>0
.
We first show that there exists
c>0

such that

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})\geq\begin{cases}c\,|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2}&\text{ if }\rho\leq r_{0},\\
c\,h(\rho-\overline{\rho})&\text{ if }\rho>r_{0},\end{cases}

(3.12)

for all
\overline{\rho}\in K
.
To this end, we show that

(\rho,\overline{\rho})\mapsto\frac{|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2}}{h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})}\chi_{[0,r_{0}]}(\rho)+\frac{h(\rho-\overline{\rho})}{h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})}\chi_{(r_{0},\infty)}(\rho)

defines a bounded function on
(0,\infty)\times K
,
where
\chi_{M}
is the characteristic function of a set
M\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.
Indeed, by Taylor’s formula, we have for
\rho\in K
that

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})=\int_{\overline{\rho}}^{\rho}h^{\prime\prime}(z)(\rho-z)\,\mathrm{d}z\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{z\in K}h^{\prime\prime}(z)(\rho-\overline{\rho})^{2}=c_{1}(\rho-\overline{\rho})^{2}

for some
c_{1}>0
due to
h^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(z)}{z}>0
.
Since
h(z)/z\to\infty
as
z\to\infty
, we obtain

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})\geq h(\rho)-\max_{z\in K}h(z)-\max_{z\in K}h^{\prime}(z)\rho+\min_{z\in K}h^{\prime}(z)z\geq c_{2}h(\rho)

for some constant
c_{2}>0

if
\rho
is sufficiently large.
In total, this yields (
3.12
)
for any choice
r_{0}\geq M
.
In particular, we can choose
r_{0}\geq 2M
.
Note that (
3.12
) holds for all
\gamma\geq 1
,
and using (
1.4
)
we can conclude the existence of
r_{0}\geq 2M
and
c>0
such that

h(\rho\,|\,\overline{\rho})\geq\begin{cases}c\,|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2}&\text{ if }\rho\leq r_{0},\\
c\,|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{\gamma}&\text{ if }\rho>r_{0},\end{cases}

(3.13)

for all
\overline{\rho}\in K
.
Clearly, (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) also hold
for
r_{0}=0
if
\overline{\rho}=0
.
To estimate the kinetic-energy term,
we use (
3.13
) to first derive

\eta(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},0)\geq\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\frac{|n|^{2}}{2r_{0}}+c|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{if }\rho\leq r_{0}.

By Young’s inequality,
we further have

\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}\frac{|n|^{2}}{2\rho}+\frac{c}{2}|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{\gamma}\geq C_{\gamma}(\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}|n|^{2})^{\gamma/(\gamma+1)}\Big(\frac{|\rho-\overline{\rho}|}{\rho}\Big)^{\gamma/(\gamma+1)}

for some constant
C_{\gamma}>0
.
Since
|\rho-\overline{\rho}|\geq\rho-M\geq\rho-r_{0}/2
, we conclude with (
3.13
) that

\eta(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\overline{\rho},0)\geq C_{\gamma}(\mathrm{e}^{-\tau}|n|^{2})^{\gamma/(\gamma+1)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{\gamma/(\gamma+1)}+\frac{c}{2}|\rho-\overline{\rho}|^{\gamma}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{if }\rho>r_{0}.

In total, this shows (
3.11
).
∎

3.4
Weak-strong uniqueness

The relative entropy inequality (
3.4
) directly leads to a weak-strong uniqueness result. The proof relies on the following generalized version of Gronwall’s lemma in differential form, which enables us to derive a bound on the total relative entropy.
This lemma will also be essential in Section
4
to prove the main results on exponential convergence with respect to the scaled time
\tau
.

Lemma 3.15

(Gronwall inequality)
.

Let
E_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
E,a,b\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
such that
\tau\mapsto a(\tau)E(\tau)
belongs to
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
, and

\int_{0}^{\infty}-\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)E(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq E_{0}\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(\tau)\big(a(\tau)E(\tau)+b(\tau)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty))
with
\varphi\geq 0
.
Then

E(\tau)\leq E_{0}\,\mathrm{e}^{\int_{0}^{\tau}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}+\int_{0}^{\tau}b(\sigma)\,\mathrm{e}^{\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.a.}~\tau>0.

Proof.

We test with
\varphi(\sigma):=\omega_{\delta}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\int_{0}^{\sigma}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}
, where we choose
\omega_{\delta}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,\infty))
satisfying
\omega_{\delta}(\sigma)\equiv 1
for
\sigma\leq\tau
and
\omega_{\delta}(\sigma)\equiv 0
for all
\sigma\geq\tau+\delta
such that
|\omega_{\delta}^{\prime}(\sigma)+\frac{1}{\delta}|\leq\delta

for all
\sigma\in(\tau,\tau+\delta)
.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau+\delta}-{\omega_{\delta}^{\prime}}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\int_{0}^{\sigma}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}E(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{\tau+\delta}\omega_{\delta}(\sigma)\,b(\sigma)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\int_{0}^{\sigma}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Taking the limit
\delta\searrow 0
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\int_{0}^{\tau}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}E(\tau)\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{\tau}b(\sigma)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\int_{0}^{\sigma}a(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.e.~}\tau>0,

which is equivalent to the desired result.
∎

).

Proof.

5
Extension to multiple dimensions

In this section we adapt the presented method
for investigating the long-time behavior of solutions
to the multi-dimensional Euler equations,
given by the system

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{t}+\operatorname{div}\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle=0,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}_{t}+\operatorname{div}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{m}\otimes\widetilde{m}}{\widetilde{\rho}}+\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho}){\mathbb{I}}\Big)+\alpha\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle=0

in
(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
where
\widetilde{\rho}\colon(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
and
\widetilde{m}\colon(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are (multi-dimensional) density and momentum fields.
Here,
{\mathbb{I}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
denotes the identity matrix.
Denoting by
\partial_{j}
the partial derivatives in space,
we define the divergence of a vector field
v=(v_{j})
and a tensor field
A=(A_{ij})
by

\operatorname{div}v=\partial_{j}v_{j}
and

(\operatorname{div}A)_{i}=\partial_{j}A_{ij}
, respectively,
where we use Einstein summation convention and implicitly sum over repeated indices.

Instead of two different limits
\rho_{\pm}
,
we now prescribe an angular function

\hat{\rho}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\};(0,\infty)),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\lambda>0\ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\colon\ \hat{\rho}(\lambda x)=\hat{\rho}(x),

(5.2)

which is a multi-dimensional generalization of
\hat{\rho}
from (
2.6
).
We study solutions with initial values

(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})
satisfying

(\widetilde{\rho}_{0}-\hat{\rho},\widetilde{m}_{0})(x)\to(0,0)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }|x|\to+\infty

in a certain sense.
Since the existence of weak solutions is not known
in the multi-dimensional case,
we base our examination on energy-variational solutions.

5.1
The notion of energy-variational solutions

We begin with the definition of energy-variational solutions to the damped Euler equations (
5.1
) in the original coordinates.
To this end, we need to quantify a suitable entropy function.
In original variables, the relative entropy is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{n})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widetilde{\eta}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{m})\,\mathrm{d}x,\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\eta}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\overline{\rho},\overline{m})=\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\rho}\Big|\frac{\widetilde{m}}{\widetilde{\rho}}-\frac{\overline{m}}{\overline{\rho}}\Big|^{2}+h(\widetilde{\rho}\,|\,\overline{\rho}).

Similarly to before, we consider special reference densities

\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

\forall\,t\in[0,\infty)\ \exists\,R>0:\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}(t,x)=\hat{\rho}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }|x|>R.

(5.3)

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty
.
We call
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m},\widetilde{E})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\mathrm{BV}([0,\infty))

an
energy-variational solution
to (
5.1
)
if there is
\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with (
5.3
)
such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)\leq\widetilde{E}
a.e. in
(0,\infty)
,
and if there exists
a
1
-homogeneous
regularity weight

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\colon C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\Big[\widetilde{E}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi\,\widetilde{\rho}+\psi\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]\Big|_{s}^{t}+\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\alpha\frac{|\widetilde{m}|^{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

(5.4)

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}+\nabla\phi\cdot\widetilde{m}+\psi_{t}\cdot\widetilde{m}+\nabla\psi:\Big(\frac{\widetilde{m}\otimes\widetilde{m}}{\widetilde{\rho}}+\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho}){\mathbb{I}}\Big)-\alpha\psi\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}-h^{\prime}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})_{t}(\widetilde{\rho}-\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})-\nabla h^{\prime}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\psi)\big(\widetilde{E}-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

and for a.a.
s,t\in[0,\infty)
with
s<t
,
including
s=0
with
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})(0)=(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})
.

As before, with the assumption

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty

we implicitly assume
\rho_{0}\geq 0
a.e.
Furthermore, the statement and proof of Proposition
3.3

can immediately be transferred to the multi-dimensional setting. Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty

is equivalent to
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}(0),0)<\infty
.

Remark 5.2
.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is 1-homogeneous,
that is,
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\lambda\psi)=\lambda\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\psi)

for
\lambda>0
,
the formulation (
5.4
) is equivalent to
the accumulated formulas

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi\,\widetilde{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|_{s}^{t}-\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi_{t}\,\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle+\nabla\phi\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}=0,

(5.5a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|_{s}^{t}-\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi_{t}\cdot\widetilde{m}&+\nabla\psi:\Big(\frac{\widetilde{m}\otimes\widetilde{m}}{\widetilde{\rho}}+\mathfrak{p}(\widetilde{\rho}){\mathbb{I}}\Big)-\alpha\psi\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\\
&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\psi)\big(\widetilde{E}-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime},\end{split}

(5.5b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}\big|_{s}^{t}+\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\alpha\frac{|\widetilde{m}|^{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}-h^{\prime}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})_{t}(\widetilde{\rho}-\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})-\nabla h^{\prime}(\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty})\cdot\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime},

(5.5c)

for all
\phi,\psi
and
s<t
as above.
This can be seen by a classical variational argument:
Choose test functions
\mu\phi
and
\lambda\psi

for
\mu,\lambda\geq 0

and consider the limit cases
\mu=0
,
\lambda=0
,
\mu\to\infty
and
\lambda\to\infty
.
While (
5.5a
) corresponds to a standard weak formulation of the continuity equation, the main difference between weak solutions
and energy-variational solutions to (
5.1
) is visible in (
5.5b
)
and (
5.5c
).
The energy variable
\widetilde{E}
occurs in both equations as an additional auxiliary variable.
Inequality (
5.5b
) relaxes the momentum equation to an inequality,
where the right-hand side measures the deviation from a weak form
in terms of the
energy defect

\widetilde{E}-\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)\geq 0

multiplied by the
regularity weight

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
.
Moreover, (
5.5c
) corresponds to the (relative) entropy inequality, where the mechanical energy is replaced with
\widetilde{E}
.
In particular, if

\widetilde{E}=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)
,
then (
5.5
) reduces to a
classical weak formulation of (
5.1
) combined with an energy inequality.

Remark 5.3
.

In Definition
5.1

the regularity weight
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
is not specified further.
Usually, it is chosen in such a way that the inequality (
5.4
)
is preserved under weak convergence or such that the solution set is convex,
which ensures the existence of energy-variational solutions or
properties like weak-strong uniqueness.
In the case of the barotropic pressure law (
1.2
),
a suitable choice is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\psi):=\max\big\{2\|[\nabla\psi]_{\mathrm{sym,+}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},(\gamma-1)\|[\operatorname{div}\psi]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\big\},

(5.6)

see
[
EiL24
]
,
where the system was studied in a torus domain.
Here,
[A]_{\mathrm{sym},+}

denotes the positive definite part of the symmetric part
\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\top})
of a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
,
while
[a]_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
denotes the nonnegative part of a scalar
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remark 5.4
.

In order to obtain a finite (relative) energy

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)
,
the existence of a specific reference density
\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}

with suitable values as
|x|\to\infty
is necessary.
However, the state variable
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m})

of an energy-variational solution
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m},\widetilde{E})

is independent of this choice.
Indeed, if we have two reference densities

\rho^{1}_{\infty}
and
\rho^{2}_{\infty}
,
then
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m},E^{1})
is an energy-variational solution to (
5.1
)
for
\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}=\rho^{1}_{\infty}

if and only if
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m},E^{2})
is an energy-variational solution
for
\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}=\rho_{\infty}^{2}
,
where

E^{2}=E^{1}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}h(\rho^{1}_{\infty}\,|\,\rho^{2}_{\infty})+\big(h^{\prime}(\rho^{1}_{\infty})-h^{\prime}(\rho^{2}_{\infty})\big)(\widetilde{\rho}-\rho^{1}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x.

This equivalence follows from the elementary identity

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\rho^{2}_{\infty},0)=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m}\,|\,\rho^{1}_{\infty},0)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}h(\rho^{1}_{\infty}\,|\,\rho^{2}_{\infty})+\big(h^{\prime}(\rho^{1}_{\infty})-h^{\prime}(\rho^{2}_{\infty})\big)(\widetilde{\rho}-\rho^{1}_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}x,

and by substituting the test function
\phi
with
\phi+h^{\prime}(\rho^{1}_{\infty})-h^{\prime}(\rho^{2}_{\infty})
.

For the sake of completeness, let us show that,
in contrast to weak solutions,
the global-in-time existence of energy-variational solutions to (
5.1
)
can be shown in the multi-dimensional framework.
To this end,
we use the general existence result from
[
ALR24
]
,
which allows for the presence of dissipative terms.
Here we denote
the class of weakly continuous functions from
[0,\infty)
to a Banach space
X

by
C_{w}([0,\infty);X)

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
\mathfrak{p}(\rho)=k\rho^{\gamma}
for

,
\gamma>1
.
Let either
\hat{\rho}
be as in (
5.2
)
or
\hat{\rho}\equiv 0
.
Let
(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0})\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\hat{\rho},0)<\infty

Then there exists an energy-variational solution
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{m},\widetilde{E})

to (
5.1
)
with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}-\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle\in C_{w}([0,\infty),L^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})+L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle\in C_{w}([0,\infty),L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})+L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

The corresponding regularity weight
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
is given by (

Proof.

We choose a time-independent function

)

we then have

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{m}_{0}\,|\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},0)<\infty

We express an energy-variational solution

in the form
(\widetilde{\sigma}+\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty},\widetilde{m})

\widetilde{\sigma}=\widetilde{\rho}-\widetilde{\rho}_{\infty}
,
where

if

, and if

and

,

5.2
Reformulation in parabolic scaling variables

As in the one-dimensional case, to study the long-time behavior of solutions to (

we consider the problem in parabolic scaling variables.
We define

\tau:=\log(1{+}t)
and

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(0)\mathcal{R}_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\,\big[\mu\mathrm{e}^{-d\tau/2}\mathcal{R}+K\mathrm{e}^{-d\tau/2}+\theta\mathcal{R}\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where we used
{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho^{*},n^{*})\leq\mathcal{R}

for the first term in the integral on the right-hand side.
This inequality resembles (
4.2
) in the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
Invoking now Gronwall’s inequality from Lemma
3.15

in the same way,
we conclude (
5.18
) in this case.

Finally, if
\theta<\frac{d}{2}
,
then (
5.18
) implies
\mathcal{R}(\tau)\to 0

as
\tau\to\infty
.
Since
E\geq{\mathcal{E}}(\tau;\rho,n\,|\,\rho_{\infty},0)
,
this shows (
5.19
).
∎

Theorem
5.10
yields convergence of
energy-variational solutions
towards the similarity profile
under the same flatness condition as for weak solutions, see Theorem
2.3
.
In a similar way as in Section
4
,
we could also deduce convergence of the time-averaged kinetic energy.
In Theorem
5.10
, we further deduce the convergence of the energy defect to zero,
which means that energy-variational solutions converge time-asymptotically to weak solutions in a certain sense.
Observe that in the case
\rho_{+}=\rho_{-}
the result holds for any regularity weight
\mathcal{K}
,
while in the case
\rho_{+}\neq\rho_{-}
we require the specific choice (
5.6
).
Clearly, one could also take a regularity weight
that is larger than (
5.6
),
but this would lead to a more restrictive flatness condition.

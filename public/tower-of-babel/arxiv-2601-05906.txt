Convergence to the Brownian CRT for critical branching Markov processes
Emma Horton, Ellen Powell

Abstract
We prove an invariance principle for a general class of continuous time critical branching processes with finite variance (non-local) branching mechanism. We show that the genealogical trees, viewed as random compact metric measure spaces, converge under rescaling to the Brownian continuum random tree in the Gromov–Hausdorff-weak topology, establishing a universal scaling limit for critical finite variance branching processes. Key words: Non-local branching processes, criticality, Brownian continuum random tree, metric measure spaces. MSC 2020: 60J80

Introduction

1.1
Branching Markov Processes

Let
E
be a Lusin space. Throughout, we write
L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
for the space of non-negative bounded measurable functions on
E
with norm
\lVert f\rVert:=\mathrm{sup}_{x\in E}|f(x)|
, and
L^{+}_{\infty,1}(E)
for the subset of functions in
L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
which are uniformly bounded by one.

We consider a spatial branching process in which, given their point of creation, particles evolve independently according to a Markov process,
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
. We allow for the possibility that
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
undergoes (soft) killing at an exponential rate or (hard) killing on some absorbing set. As such we append a cemetery state
\{\dagger\}
to
E
, which is to be treated as an absorbing state, and regard
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
as conservative on the extended space
E\cup\{\dagger\}
, which is also a Lusin space.

In an event which we refer to as ‘branching’, particles positioned at
x
die at rate
\gamma(x)\geq 0
and instantaneously, new particles are created in
E
according to a point process. The configurations of these offspring are described by the random counting measure

\mathcal{Z}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}(A),

(1)

for Borel
A
in
E
. The law of the aforementioned point process may depend on
x
, the point of death of the parent, and we denote it by
\mathcal{P}_{x}
,
x\in E
, with associated expectation operator given by
\mathcal{E}_{x}
,
x\in E
. This information is captured by the branching mechanism,

\Phi(x,f):=\gamma(x)\mathcal{E}_{x}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i})-f(x)\right],\qquad x\in E,\,f\in L^{+}_{\infty,1}(E).

(2)

Without loss of generality we can assume that
\mathcal{P}_{x}(N=1)=0
for all
x\in E
by viewing a branching event with one offspring as an extra jump in the motion. On the other hand, we do allow for the possibility that
\mathcal{P}_{x}(N=0)>0
for some or all
x\in E
. We extend
\Phi
to
E\cup\{\dagger\}
by defining it to be zero on
\{\dagger\}
, i.e. particles cannot branch in the cemetery state. We assume throughout that
\gamma
and
x\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{x}[N^{2}]
are both in
L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
.

The branching Markov process can be described via the process
X=(X_{t},t\geq 0)
in the space of atomic measures on
E
with non-negative integer total mass, denoted by
M(E)
, where

X_{t}(\cdot)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\delta_{x_{i}(t)}(\cdot),\qquad t\geq 0.

Above,
(N_{t},t\geq 0)
denotes the number of particles alive at each time
t\geq 0
and their positions are given by
\{x_{i}(t)\,:\,i=1,\dots,N_{t}\}
.
In particular,
X
is Markovian in
M(E)
. Its probabilities will be denoted
\mathbb{P}:=(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}},x\in E)
where for
x\in E
,
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}
denotes the law of
X
initiated from
\delta_{x}\in M(E)
. The linear semigroup associated with
X
is given by

\psi_{t}[f](x):=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[\langle f,X_{t}\rangle],\qquad t\geq 0,\,x\in E,\,f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E),

(3)

where for
f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
and
\mu\in M(E)
, we set

\langle f,\mu\rangle=\int_{E}f(y)\mu(dy).

Finally, we say that
f
is in the domain of the extended generator of a Markov process
(Z,P_{z})
if there exists a bounded function
Lf
such that

f(Z_{t})-f(z)-\int_{0}^{t}Lf(Z_{s})ds,\quad t\geq 0,

is a martingale.

We now introduce some assumptions that will be used at various points throughout the rest of the article, starting with the finite variance assumption mentioned above.

Assumption 1
.

The most general assumptions we make on the Markov branching process are as follows. When we need only a subset of these assumptions, we will refer to them specifically.

(VAR)

\gamma
and
x\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{x}[N^{2}]
are both in
L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
.

(CAD)

The Markov process
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
is càdlàg.

(PF)

There exists
\varphi\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
and a probability measure
\tilde{\varphi}
on
E
, normalised so that
\langle\tilde{\varphi},\varphi\rangle=1
, such that
\varphi
is uniformly bounded away from zero on each compactly embedded subset of
E
, and for all
g\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
and
t\geq 0
, we have

\langle\psi_{t}[g],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle=\langle g,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\text{ and }\psi_{t}[\varphi]=\varphi.

Moreover there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\sup_{g\in L_{\infty,1}^{+}(E)}\|\varphi^{-1}\psi_{t}[g]-\langle g,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\|=O(e^{-\varepsilon t})\text{ as }t\to\infty.

(EXT)

For all
x\in E
the MBP becomes extinct
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}
-almost surely; that is, if
\zeta:=\inf\{t\,:\,N_{t}=0\}
, then

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\zeta<\infty)=1.

(ERG)

There exist constants
C,M\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
g\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
, setting
\mathcal{V}_{M}[g](x):=\mathcal{E}_{x}[\sum\nolimits_{i\neq j}g(x_{i})g(x_{j})\mathbf{1}_{\{N\leq M\}}]
,

\langle\gamma\mathcal{V}_{M}[g],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\geq C\langle g,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle^{2}.

(CC)

The function
\varphi^{2}
is in the domain of the extended generator of
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
.

(HK)

Let
F\subset E
denote the absorbing states in
E
and let
\tau_{F}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\xi_{t}\in F\}
denote the first hitting time of
F
; then
\lim_{t\uparrow\tau_{F}}\varphi(\xi_{t})=0
,
\mathbf{P}
-almost surely, on the event
\{\tau_{F}<\infty\}
.

First note that
(CAD)
simply allows us to make use of standard martingale results. This is satisfied in a very general framework: for example, any process with a Feller semi-group of probability transitions or any piecewise deterministic Markov process with bounded jump intensity
[
4
, Sections 2 and 4]
. The third assumption stipulates that the MBP is critical. Indeed, a more general version of
(PF)
would give the existence of a triple
(\lambda,\varphi,\tilde{\varphi})
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

\langle\psi_{t}[g],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle={\rm e}^{\lambda t}\langle g,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\text{ and }\psi_{t}[\varphi]={\rm e}^{\lambda t}\varphi,

and

\sup_{g\in L_{\infty,1}^{+}(E)}\|{\rm e}^{-\lambda t}\varphi^{-1}\psi_{t}[g]-\langle g,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\|=O(e^{-\varepsilon t}),\quad t\to\infty.

The case
\lambda>0
corresponds to the supercritical case,
\lambda<0
the subcritical case, and
\lambda=0
the critical case. In the latter case, one would usually expect the branching process to become extinct almost surely in finite time however, it seems difficult to prove this assumption in full generality, hence the need for assumption
(EXT)
. Assumption
(ERG)
can be seen as an ergodicity assumption on the offspring distribution: on the event that the number of offspring is at most
M
, when distributed according to the stationary distribution, the offspring are at least as ‘spread out’ as a process that produces two children whose positions are independently distributed. Note that these two assumptions also appear in the literature, see e.g.
[
11
,
13
]
. Finally, assumption
(CC)
and
(HK)
will allow us to work with the so-called Carré du Champ operator; we refer the reader to Section
2.4
and
[
4
]
for further details. We note that
(CC)
is immediate for piecewise deterministic processes, and can be checked easily for Feller processes, see
[
4
, Sections 2 and 4]
. Note also that it follows from
(VAR)
and
(PF)
that
\varphi
is automatically in the domain of the extended generator; we will use this fact throughout the paper.
(HK)
is clearly satisfied if there is no absorbing boundary, or if the motion is regular enough at the absorbing boundary (e.g. a diffusion on a
C^{2}
domain as in
[
16
]
).

1.2
Main result

Given a critical MBP
X
we let
T
denote the associated genealogical tree. We denote by
{\bf r}
the root of the tree,
d
the genealogical distance and
\nu
the total occupation time of the depth-first exploration of
T
, without backtracking. See Section
2.6
for rigorous definitions of these objects. We then define

\mathcal{T}_{n,x}:=(T,\frac{1}{n}d,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\nu,{\bf r}),\quad n\geq 0,\,x\in E,

to be the associated random pointed metric measure spaces, under the law

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\cdot|N_{n}>0)

(where the maximum distance
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is of order
n
).

We also let
\mathcal{T}_{\bf e}
denote the Brownian CRT generated from a Brownian excursion with speed
\sigma^{2}(f)
(a constant defined in (
17
)), conditioned to have height at least
1
and viewed as a pointed metric measure space with its natural measure and root (see Section
2.6
).

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
1
we have

\mathcal{T}_{n,x}\overset{d.}{\to}\mathcal{T}_{\bf e},\quad n\to\infty,

(4)

with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff-weak topology.

As discussed below, scaling limits of genealogies of critical branching process have been investigated in various settings over the past several decades. Our result unifies and extends existing results proving convergence to the CRT. While previous works addressed either specific models
[
1
,
2
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
or general classes of branching processes under stronger structural/moment assumptions
[
6
]
, we establish a criterion that applies to arbitrary critical Markov branching processes and should be practical to verify. To our knowledge, this is the most general formulation of CRT convergence currently available. Moreover, our proof employs a new, largely non-technical, approach.

1.3
Literature

Results of this kind were first proved by Aldous
[
1
,
2
]
who showed that if you condition a critical Galton-Watson tree to have a large total progeny then, under the assumption that the offspring distribution has finite variance, the rescaled tree converges (in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense) to the Brownian CRT. This result has since been extended to various other settings including Galton-Watson trees with infinite variance offspring distribution
[
14
]
, multitype Galton-Watson processes with a finite number of types
[
15
]
, and branching diffusions in bounded domains
[
16
]
.

More recently, Foutel-Rodier obtained a general and technically powerful result for discrete-time branching Markov processes, assuming finite positive moments of all orders
[
6
]
. Our work can be viewed as complementary to this framework, as well as an extension in two directions: we show that only second-moment assumptions on the offspring distribution are sufficient, and we treat the continuous-time setting. The proof also follows a different philosophy, relying on new analytic and probabilistic ideas - most notably a robust martingale argument - rather than the combinatorial techniques and moment computations that underpin earlier approaches.

One approach to proving such results is to view the genealogical tree associated with the branching process as a random metric measure space (mm-space) and prove that, when appropriately scaled, the mm-space converges in distribution to that of the limiting CRT. As such, there has been an increase in interest in studying the topological properties of the space of mm-spaces that are required for proving the convergence of random mm-spaces,
[
10
,
5
,
7
]
. We also refer the reader to Section
2.6
for a summary and brief discussion of the results that will be needed for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Outline of the paper.

The rest of this paper is set out as follows. In the next section we present various preliminary results that will be used to prove the main theorem. In particular, section
2.1
discusses various many-to-one representations of MBPs and associated ergodicity results, section
2.2
houses existing results in the literature that will be used to prove Theorem
1
, in section
2.4
we give a brief introduction to the Carré du Champ operator which will be necessary to define various martingales for our proofs, section
2.5
formalises notation required to make sense of the genealogies of the MBP and section
2.6
discusses relevant theory of metric measure spaces.
Section
3
is then concerned with the proof of Theorem
1
. We start, in Section
3.1
, by identifying a key martingale associated with the depth-first exploration of a critical MBP genealogical tree, generalising the Lukasiewicz path associated with a Galton-Watson tree, and serving as a proxy for the height (or contonur) function of the tree. In section
3.2
we establish a functional central limit theorem for this martingale, which provides the crucial connection to the Brownian CRT. Finally in section
3.3
, we relate the martingale precisely to the height function of a critical MBP genealogical tree conditioned to be large, which allows us to complete the proof.

2
Preliminaries and existing results

2.1
Spine decomposition

In this section, we present some existing results pertaining to various single particle motions associated with a MBP. We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for the proofs of these results.

For
f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
and
x\in E
, define

m[f](x):=\mathcal{E}_{x}[\langle f,\mathcal{Z}\rangle]=\mathcal{E}_{x}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i})\bigg].

(5)

We will also write
m(x):=m[\mathbf{1}](x)=\mathcal{E}_{x}[N]
for the mean of the offspring distribution, which is uniformly bounded from above thanks to
(VAR)
.

Another way to describe the semigroup
\psi_{t}
is via the so-called many-to-one formula. In this setting, due to the possibility of non-local branching, we need to introduce another Markov process
Y=(Y_{t},\,t\geq 0)
, which evolves according to the dynamics of
(\xi,\mathbf{P})
except that at instantaneous rate
\gamma(x)m(x)
, it jumps from its current position
x
to a new random position, lying in
A\subset E
with probability
m^{-1}(x)m[\mathbf{1}_{A}](x)
. Let
\tilde{\mathbf{P}}=(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{x},\,x\in E)
denote its law and
\tilde{\mathbf{E}}=(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{x},\,x\in E)
its associated expectation. Then by conditioning on the first jump of the process
Y
, it is straightforward to deduce the following many-to-one formula.

Lemma 2

(Many-to-one formula)
.

Under
(VAR)
(in fact,
(VAR)
can be relaxed to a first moment assumption), for each
x\in E,t\geq 0
and
f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
, we have

\psi_{t}[f](x)=\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{x}[e^{\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(Y_{s})(m(Y_{s})-1)\mathrm{d}s}f(Y_{t})\mathbf{1}_{\{t<\mathtt{k}\}}],

where
\mathtt{k}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:Y_{t}\notin E\}
.

Let us now introduce a useful result known as the spine decomposition. We assume that assumptions
(CAD)
and
(PF)
hold and, for an initial configuration
\mu\in M(E)
, define

W_{t}:=\frac{\langle\varphi,X_{t}\rangle}{\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle},\qquad x\in E,\,t\geq 0.

(6)

Then, due to
(PF)
and the branching Markov property,
(W_{t},\,t\geq 0)
is a mean-one martingale under
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
. This means we can define a new measure
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\varphi}
on the Markov branching process via

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\varphi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}}\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}=W_{t},\qquad t\geq 0.

(7)

It is well-known that under the new measure, we have a so-called
spine decomposition
for the branching process. We refer the reader to
[
12
, Chapter 11]
for the proof and further details.

Proposition 3

(Spine decomposition)
.

Under assumptions
(CAD)
,
(PF)
and
(VAR)
(in fact,
(VAR)
can be relaxed to a finite first moment assumption), the branching process
X
under
\mathbb{P}^{\varphi}
can be constructed as follows.

1.

From the initial configuration
\mu=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}}\in{M}(E)
with an arbitrary enumeration of particles, the
i^{*}
-th individual is selected and marked spine, so that, given
\mu
,
the probability that
i^{*}=j
is given by
\varphi(x_{j})/\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle
for
j=1,\dots,n
.

2.

The individuals
j\neq i^{*}
in the initial configuration each issue independent copies of
(X,\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x_{j}}})
respectively.

3.

The marked individual, “spine”, issues a single particle whose motion has the law of the many-to-one motion
(Y,\tilde{\mathbf{P}})
weighted by

e^{\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(Y_{s})(\frac{m[\varphi](Y_{s})}{\varphi(Y_{s})}-1)\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\varphi(Y_{t})}{\varphi(x)}.

4.

The spine undergoes branching at the accelerated rate

\rho(x):=\gamma(x)\frac{m[\varphi](x)}{\varphi(x)}

when at
x\in E
, at which point, it produces a random number of particles according to the random measure
\mathcal{Z}
on
E
with law
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{\varphi}
, where

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{P}_{x}^{\varphi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{P}_{x}}=\frac{\langle\varphi,\mathcal{Z}\rangle}{m[\varphi](x)}.

5.

Given
\mathcal{Z}
from the previous step,
\mu
is redefined as
\mu=\mathcal{Z}
and Step 1 is repeated.

The above implies that the motion of the particle marked spine (including the jumps that it experiences at branching events) has law
\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varphi}
where

\frac{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varphi}_{x}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(Y_{s})(m(Y_{s})-1)\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\varphi(Y_{t})}{\varphi(x)}\mathbf{1}_{\{t<\mathrm{k}\}},\qquad t\geq 0,\,x\in E,

where
\mathrm{k}:=\inf\{t>0\,:\,Y_{t}\notin E\}
. From this we see that the process
Y
under
\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varphi}
is conservative, and satisfies

\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{x}^{\varphi}[f(Y_{t})]=\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}\psi_{t}[\varphi f],\qquad t\geq 0,\,f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E),

with stationary distribution

\varphi(x)\tilde{\varphi}(\mathrm{d}x),\qquad x\in E.

Now we state a result regarding the ergodicity of the spine; the proof can be found in
[
11
]
. For this, we introduce a class of functions
\mathcal{C}
on
[0,\infty)\times E\times[0,\infty)
. We say that
F_{\cdot}(\cdot,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}
if

F(x,s):=\lim_{t\to\infty}F_{t}(x,s),\quad x\in E,s\geq 0,

exists,

\sup_{x\in E,0<s<1}|\varphi F(x,s)|<\infty,

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in E}\varphi(x)|F(x,s)-F_{t}(x,s)|=0.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathcal{C}
. Then,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in E}\left|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_{x}^{\varphi}\left[\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(Y_{ut},u)du\right]-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\varphi\tilde{\varphi},F(\cdot,u)\rangle du\right|=0.

2.2
Limit theorems for critical MBPs

We now present some further existing results for our class of MBPs that will be used throughout the proof of Theorem
1
. Theorem
5
concerns the classical Kolmogorov survival probability for critical branching processes and Theorem
6
pertains to the so-called Yaglom limit; the proofs of these results can be found in
[
13
]
. Corollary
7
and Proposition
8

are consequences of the aforementioned Yaglom limit. Finally, Theorem
9
characterises the asymptotic behaviour of the moments of
X
and its occupation, and we refer the reader to
[
8
,
9
]
for its proof.

Theorem 5

(Kolmogorov survival probability)
.

Suppose
(PF)
,
(EXT)
and
(ERG)
hold. Then, for all
\mu\in M(E)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(N_{t}>0)=\frac{2\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle},

where
\mathcal{V}[g](x):=\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{M}[g](x)=\mathcal{E}_{x}[\mathcal{Z}[g]^{2}]-\mathcal{E}_{x}[\mathcal{Z}[g]]^{2}
.

Theorem 6

(Yaglom limit)
.

Suppose
(PF)
,
(EXT)
and
(ERG)
hold.
Then, for all
\mu\in M(E)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[{\rm exp}\left(-\theta\frac{\langle f,X_{t}\rangle}{t}\right)\Big|\,N_{t}>0\right]=\frac{1}{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle},

where
\theta\geq 0
and
f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
.

Corollary 7
.

Assume the setting of Theorem
6
. For
f_{1},f_{2}\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
, under
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\cdot\mid N_{t}>0)
, we have the joint convergence

\left(\frac{\langle f_{1},X_{t}\rangle}{t},\frac{\langle f_{2},X_{t}\rangle}{t}\right)\overset{d.}{\to}(Y(f_{1}),Y(f_{2})),

where
Y(f_{1})
and
Y(f_{2})
denote the same exponential random variable but with rates
\frac{1}{2}\langle f_{1},\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle
and
\frac{1}{2}\langle f_{2},\tilde{\varphi}\rangle\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle
, respectively.

The proof of this result follows easily from the proof of Theorem
6
in
[
13
]
. Indeed, the first (and main) step of the proof of Theorem
6
is to show that the result holds for
f=\varphi
. The result then follows for general
f
by writing
f=\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle f+(f-\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle f)
and showing that, conditional on survival up to time
t
,
\langle f-\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle f,X_{t}\rangle/t
converges to zero. Applying this argument to both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
proves the above corollary.

Now we state a result that is well-known but is not explicitly stated in the literature in a fully general setting. The proof of this result follows verbatim the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 1.5]
.

Proposition 8
.

Assume the setting of Theorem
5
. Let
R>0
,
A\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
x\in E
. Then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(A\,\big|\,N_{t}>0)=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}^{\varphi}(A).

For
k\geq 0
,
t\geq 0
,
x\in E
and
f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)
, define

\psi_{t}^{(k)}[f](x)=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[\langle f,X_{t}\rangle^{k}\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad\eta_{t}^{(k)}[f](x)=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\langle f,X_{s}\rangle{\rm d}s\Big].

Theorem 9
.

Suppose that
(PF)
holds and that for some
k\geq 2
,
\sup_{x\in E}\mathcal{E}_{x}[N^{k}]<\infty
. For
\ell\leq k
and
t\geq 0
, set

\Delta_{t}^{(\ell)}:=\sup_{x\in E,f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)}\left|t^{-(\ell-1)}\varphi(x)^{-1}\psi_{t}^{(\ell)}[f](x)-2^{(\ell-1)}\ell!\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle^{\ell}\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle^{\ell-1}\right|,

and

\Theta_{t}^{(\ell)}:=\sup_{x\in E,f\in L_{\infty}^{+}(E)}\left|t^{-(2\ell-1)}\varphi(x)^{-1}\eta_{t}^{(\ell)}[f](x)-2^{(\ell-1)}\ell!\langle f,\tilde{\varphi}\rangle^{\ell}\langle\mathcal{V}[\varphi],\tilde{\varphi}\rangle^{\ell-1}L_{\ell}\right|,

where
L_{1}=1
and for
\ell\geq 2
,
L_{\ell}
is defined through the recursion
L_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\ell-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}L_{i}L_{\ell-i}
.

Then, for all
\ell\leq k
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\Delta_{t}^{(\ell)}<\infty,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\Delta_{t}^{(\ell)}=0,\qquad\sup_{t\geq 0}\Theta_{t}^{(\ell)}<\infty,\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\Theta_{t}^{(\ell)}=0.

2.3
A functional central limit theorem

For ease of reference, we present a result that was stated and proved in
[
4
, Theorem 3.12]
, which we will use in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 10

(Functional Central Limit Theorem)
.

Let
(Z_{N}(t))_{t\geq 0}
denote a sequence of càdlàg processes, which may be defined on diﬀerent probability spaces. Suppose that

t\mapsto Z_{N}(t)-Z_{N}(0)
are local square integrable martingales, and assume moreover
that:

(a)

Z_{N}(0)
converge in distribution as
N\to\infty
;

(b)

the following limit holds,
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{[0,T]}|\Delta Z^{N}(t)|^{2}]=0
;

(c)

for each
N
the predictable quadratic variation
\langle Z^{N}\rangle_{t}
is continuous; and

(d)

there exists a continuous increasing deterministic function
t\mapsto v(t)
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\langle Z^{N}\rangle_{t}\overset{.}{\rightarrow}v(t)\text{ as }N\to\infty.

Then

2.5
Labelling

In order to describe the genealogical structure of our branching Markov process, we introduce the set of Ulam-Harris labels, that we will associate to particles in the MBP:

\Omega:=\{\emptyset\}\cup\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{N}^{n}.

For
v,w\in\Omega
, we write
v\preceq w
to mean that
v
is an ancestor of
w
, which means there exists
u\in\Omega
such that
vu=w
. Moreover, we write
v\prec w
to mean that
v\preceq w
in the strict sense, that is, the possibility that
v=w
is excluded. We say that
v,w\in\Omega
are siblings if there exists
u\in\Omega
and
i\neq j
such that
v=ui
and
w=uj
.

With this notation in hand, if
\{x_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{N_{t}}(t)\}
is an ordering of the particles at time
t
(where
N_{t}
denotes the number of particles alive at time
t
), then
\{v_{1}(t),\dots,v_{N_{t}}(t)\}
are their associated Ulam-Harris labels.

Given
v\in\Omega
, let
b_{v}
denote the birth time of particle
v
and
d_{v}
denote the death time (with
b_{v}=d_{v}=\infty
if label
v
does not correspond to any particle in the MBP), and let
X_{v}(t)
denote its position at time
t
during its lifetime. For each
v\in\Omega
, let
N_{v}
denote the number of offspring produced by
X_{v}(d_{v})
. Set
\mathcal{N}_{t}:=\{v\in\Omega:t\in[b_{v},d_{v})\}
so that
N_{t}=|\mathcal{N}_{t}|
.

Depth-first exploration.

Now let
\preceq_{\rm lex}
denote the lexicographical ordering of vertices and set
\hat{\Omega}:=\{v\in\Omega:b_{v}<\infty\}
. Then, for
v\in\hat{\Omega}
, set

\sigma_{v}:=\sum_{w\preceq_{\rm lex}v}(d_{w}-b_{w}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\tau_{v}:=\sigma_{v}+(d_{v}-b_{v}).

For
t\geq 0
, there exists a unique
v=v_{t}
such that
t\in[\sigma_{v},\tau_{v})
. With this notation, let
h_{t}=b_{v_{t}}+(t-\sigma_{v_{t}})
and
\zeta_{t}=X_{v_{t}}(h_{t})
. Then, the depth-first exploration at time
t
is given by
v_{t}
. In words, we move around the genealogical tree at speed one in a depth first order (without backtracking), and in this exploration,
v_{t}
is the label of the particle being visited at time
t
,
h_{t}
is its ‘height’ in the tree (i.e. the natural time in the branching process), and
\zeta_{t}
is its position.

Let
E_{t}:=\{v\in\hat{\Omega}:\tau_{v}\leq t\}
denote the set of (fully) explored particles and
D_{t}=\{vi\in\hat{\Omega}:v\in E_{t}\}\backslash(E_{t}\cup\{v_{t}\})
denote the discovered particles that are yet to be explored. We then define the length of the tree to be
L=\sup\{\tau_{v}:v\in\hat{\Omega}\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}N_{s}ds
.

We extend this notation to an i.i.d. sequence of branching processes,
(X^{i})_{i\geq 1}
. For
u=(i,v)\in\mathbb{N}\times\hat{\Omega}
, write
b_{u}
and
d_{u}
for the birth and death times, respectively, of particle
v
in
X^{i}
. Similarly, we extend the definitions of
\sigma_{u}
and
\tau_{u}
to
u\in\mathbb{N}\times\hat{\Omega}
, where we understand
(i_{1},v_{1})\preceq_{\rm lex}(i_{2},v_{2})
to mean
i_{1}v_{1}\preceq_{\rm lex}i_{2}v_{2}
. Then, there exists a unique
(i,v)=(I_{t},v_{t})
such that
t\in[\sigma_{(i,v)},\tau_{(i,v)}]
, which defines the depth-first exploration process for the sequence of trees. We still write
h_{t}=b_{(I_{t},v_{t})}+(t-\sigma_{(I_{t},v_{t})})
and
\zeta_{t}=X_{v_{t}}^{I_{t}}(h_{t})
to denote the height process and associated position in
E
, respectively. We also extend the definitions of
E_{t}
and
D_{t}
in a similar way.

2.6
Metric measure spaces and convergence

Here we introduce the necessary notation to allow us to view the genealogical tree associated to a MBP as a (pointed) metric measure space. We mostly follow
[
6
]
; we also refer the reader to
[
7
,
16
,
5
,
3
]
.

In general, a pointed metric measure space is a quadruple
\mathcal{X}=(X,d,\nu,{\bf r})
, where
(X,d)
is a complete separable metric space,
\nu
is a finite measure on
X
and
{\bf r}
is the root. We let
\mathbb{X}
denote the equivalence classes of (pointed) metric measure space, where
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are equivalent if there exists an isometry

\iota:\{{\bf r}_{X}\}\cup{\rm supp}\,\nu_{X}\to\{{\bf r}_{Y}\}\cup{\rm supp}\,\nu_{Y}

such that
\iota({\bf r}_{X})={\bf r}_{Y}
and
\nu_{X}\circ\iota^{-1}=\nu_{Y}
.

We now introduce the Gromov-weak and Gromov-Hausdorff-weak topologies.

Gromov-weak topology.
For
k\geq 1
and
\varphi:[0,\infty)^{k+1\choose 2}\to\mathbb{R}
, define
\Phi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
via

\Phi(\mathcal{X})=\int_{X^{k}}\varphi(d({\bf x}))\mu^{\otimes k}({\rm d}{\bf x}),

where, for
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in X^{k}
,
d({\bf x})=(d(x_{i},x_{j}))_{0\leq i,j\leq k}
and
x_{0}={\bf r}
. A sequence
(\mathcal{X}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is said to converge to
\mathcal{X}
in the Gromov-weak topology if, and only if,

\Phi(\mathcal{X}_{n})\to\Phi(\mathcal{X}),\quad n\to\infty,

(10)

for all
\Phi
defined as in (
10
) above with
\varphi
bounded and continuous.

Gromov-Hausdorff-weak topology.
We say that
(\mathcal{X}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\mathcal{X}
in the Gromov-Hausdorff-weak topology if there exists a complete separable metric space
(Z,d_{Z})
and isometries
\iota_{n}:\{{\bf r}_{n}\}\cup{\rm supp}\,\nu_{n}\to Z
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that, as
n\to\infty

\iota_{n}({\bf r}_{n})\to\iota_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\iota_{n}({\rm supp}\,\nu_{n})\to\iota_{\infty}({\rm supp}\,\nu_{\infty}),

in
(Z,d_{Z})
and in the Hausdorff topology, respectively, and

\nu_{n}\circ\iota_{n}^{-1}\to\nu_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\infty}^{-1},\quad n\to\infty,

weakly, as measures on
Z
.

In our setting, which will be made more concrete shortly, we will first prove convergence in the Gromov-weak topology and then prove a criterion that will allow us to strengthen the Gromov-weak convergence to Gromov-Hausdorff-weak convergence. Before stating the criterion, let us define the so-called lower mass function, which is defined, for
\delta>0
and
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{X}
as

m_{\delta}(\mathcal{X}):=\inf\{|B_{\delta,x}(\mathcal{X})|:x\in{\rm supp}\,\nu\},

(11)

where
B_{\delta,x}(\mathcal{X})
is the closed ball of radius
\delta
centred at
x\in X
and
|A|:=\nu(A)
for
A\subset X
.

Lemma 11

(Gromov-weak to Gromov-Hausdorff-weak)
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{P}_{n},n\geq 1)
is a sequence of probability measures converging weakly to
\mathbb{P}
with respect to the Gromov-weak topology. Suppose that for each
\delta>0
,

\lim_{\eta^{\prime}\to 0}\limsup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{n}(m_{\delta}(\mathcal{X})<\eta^{\prime})=0.

(12)

Then
(\mathbb{P}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
also converges weakly to
\mathbb{P}
in the Gromov-Hausdorff-weak topology.

For a proof of the above result, we refer the reader to
[
6
, Lemma 4.5]
. We now formally describe how to build a metric measure space from a MBP.

Given a (critical) MBP,
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, let
N_{\infty}
denote the total progeny of individuals in the Ulam Harris tree of all individuals to have ever been born in
X
, and let
\mathcal{U}:=\{\emptyset:=u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{N_{\infty}}\}
denote the labels of these particles in depth-first order. Further, let
T
denote the continuous plane tree with vertices given by
\mathcal{U}
and branch lengths given by the lifetimes of the particles, i.e. the length of the branch rooted at
u\in\mathcal{U}
is
d_{u}-b_{u}
. Recall the total tree length,
L
, and the height function,
(h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, defined in the previous section and, for
s,t\in[0,L)
, define

d(s,t)=h_{s}+h_{t}-2\inf_{u\in[s\wedge t,s\vee t]}h_{u}.

Then, since
d(s,t)=0
if
s
and
t
correspond to two times a branch point is visited in the depth-first exploration,
d
defines a pseudometric on
[0,L)
. Thus, we define the metric space
(T,d)
to be
([0,L)/\!\!\sim,d)
, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation defined by
s\sim t\Leftrightarrow d(s,t)=0
. We equip
(T,d)
with the measure
\nu
, the push forward of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,L)
under the equivalence relation
\sim
.

Finally, we define the Brownian continuum random tree. Let
{\bf e}=({\bf e}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq\tau}
denote a Brownian excursion with a certain speed
c=\sigma^{2}(f)
(a constant defined below in (
17
)), conditioned to have maximum height at least one, where
\tau
denotes the length of the excursion (and the height is defined by
\sup_{t\leq\tau}\mathbf{e}_{t}
). More precisely, the law of
\mathbf{e}
is given by the reflected (speed
c
) one-dimensional Brownian motion Itô excursion measure, restricted to paths whose supremum exceeds
1
, and normalised to be a probability measure. Define

d_{\bf e}(s,t)={\bf e}_{s}+{\bf e}_{t}-2\min_{r\in[s,t]}{\bf e}_{r},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.

Again, this defines a pseudometric on
[0,\tau)
and thus,
(T_{\bf e},d_{\bf e}):=([0,\tau)/\!\!\sim,d_{\bf e})
defines a metric space, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation that identifies points with
d_{\bf e}(\cdot,\cdot)=0
. Similarly to above, we equip this metric space with the measure
\mu_{\bf e}
, the push forward of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,\tau)
.

Proof of Proposition

.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables, independent of
N_{R}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda R)
and satisfying
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]<\infty
. For any
y>0
and
n\geq 1
, we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}^{2}\leq y^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{i}|>y\}}.

Applying this with
y=\varepsilon\sqrt{R}
and
n=N_{R}
, and taking expectations gives

\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}\big]\leq\varepsilon^{2}R+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{i}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{R}\}}\right].

Since the
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. and independent of
N_{R}
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}R+\mathbb{E}[N_{R}]\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{1}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{R}\}}\right]=\varepsilon^{2}R+\lambda R\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{1}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{R}\}}\right].

Divide both sides by
R
yields

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}]}{R}\leq\varepsilon^{2}+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[X_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{1}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{R}\}}\right].

Since
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]<\infty
, dominated convergence implies that

\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{1}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{R}\}}]\to 0

as
R\to\infty
.
Hence

\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}]}{R}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{R}}X_{i}^{2}]}{R}=0.

To apply this to our context, note that the second moments of
\Delta M_{t}
are finite thanks to boundedness of
\varphi
and
\gamma
, and the fact that the offspring distribution has finite variance. The result then follows by noting that the number of jumps of the process
M_{t}
for
t\in[0,R]
can be dominated by a Poisson random variable with rate
\lVert\gamma\rVert_{\infty}R
.
∎

Before proving Proposition
16
, we will need some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 17
.

There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(L\geq t)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}

for all
t\geq 1
and
x\in E
, where
L
is defined (in Section
2.5
) as the length of the tree.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(L\geq t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(N_{\sqrt{t}}>0)+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(L\geq t\,|\,N_{\sqrt{t}}=0)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t}}\langle 1,X_{h}\rangle dh]}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{C\sqrt{t}}{t},

where we have used Theorems
5
and
9
to bound the survival probability and the occupation measure, respectively. The result follows by taking
C=C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}
.

Recall that
I_{t}
denotes the index of the tree being visited at time
t
in the depth-first exploration and that
\tau_{t}
denotes the time that this tree is completely explored (so
\tau_{t}\geq t
).

Lemma 18
.

We have

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(I_{t}\geq M\sqrt{t})\to 0

as
M\to\infty
, uniformly in
t\geq 1
and
x\in E
.

Proof.

Write
L_{i}
for the total length of the
i
-th tree in the sequence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(I_{t}\geq M\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{M\sqrt{t}}L_{i}\geq t\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcap_{i=1}^{M\sqrt{t}}\{L_{i}\geq t\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)^{M\sqrt{t}}.

Since
(1-C/\sqrt{t})^{\sqrt{t}}\to e^{-C}
as
t\to\infty
, there exists
q<1
, such that
(1-C/\sqrt{t})^{\sqrt{t}}<q
for all
t\geq 1
.
So

\sup_{t\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(I_{t}\geq M\sqrt{t})\leq q^{M}

which converges to
0
as
M\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 19
.

We have

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\tau_{t}\geq Mt)\to 0

as
M\to\infty
, uniformly in
t\geq 1
and
x\in E
.

Proof.

For any
K>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\tau_{t}\geq Mt)\leq\mathbb{P}(I_{t}\geq K\sqrt{t})+\mathbb{P}(\max\{L_{1},\dots,L_{K\sqrt{t}}\}\geq(M-1)t).

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\max\{L_{1},\dots,L_{K\sqrt{t}}\}\geq(M-1)t)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}(\cap_{i=1}^{K\sqrt{t}}\{L_{i}<(M-1)t\})

\displaystyle\leq 1-\bigg(1-\frac{C}{\sqrt{(M-1)t}}\bigg)^{K\sqrt{t}}.

So by Lemma
18
, it is sufficient to prove that for fixed
K>0
,

\sup_{t\geq 1}\left(1-\bigg(1-\frac{C}{\sqrt{(M-1)t}}\bigg)^{K\sqrt{t}}\right)\to 0,\,\text{ as }M\to\infty.

But for this fixed
K
, there exists
q>0
such that

\sup_{t\geq 1,M\geq 1}\bigg(1-\frac{C}{\sqrt{(M-1)t}}\bigg)^{K\sqrt{(M-1)t}}\geq q

which means that

\sup_{t\geq 1}\left(1-\bigg(1-\frac{C}{\sqrt{(M-1)t}}\bigg)^{K\sqrt{t}}\right)\leq 1-q^{1/\sqrt{M-1}}

and this indeed converges to
0
as
M\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 20
.

We have

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[\sum_{v\in D_{t}}\varphi(X_{v}(b_{v}))\right]}{\sqrt{t}}<\infty.

Proof.

Note that from the definition of
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[\sum_{v\in D_{t}}\varphi(X_{v}(b_{v}))\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[M_{t}]+\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[\sum_{v\in E_{t}\cap\mathbb{N}}\varphi(X_{v}(b_{v}))\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[\varphi(\zeta_{t})].

Since
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale we have
\mathbb{E}[M_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[M_{0}]=\varphi(x)
. This and boundedness of
\varphi
show that the first and third terms are
o(\sqrt{t})
as
t\to\infty
, uniformly in
x
. We are thus left to deal with the second term. For this, note that

\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[\sum_{v\in E_{t}\cap\mathbb{N}}\varphi(X_{v}(b_{v}))\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[I_{t}-1]\varphi(x)

and so it remains to control
\mathbb{E}[I_{t}]
. To this end, fix
M>0
and write,

\mathbb{E}[I_{t}]=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{P}(I_{t}>n)=\sum_{n\leq M\sqrt{t}}\mathbb{P}(I_{t}>n)+\sum_{n\geq M\sqrt{t}}\mathbb{P}(I_{t}>n).

The first term on the right-hand side above is trivially bounded above by
M\sqrt{t}
. For the second term, thanks to Lemma
18
, we may choose
M
sufficiently large so that the second term on the right-hand side above is
o(\sqrt{t})
, uniformly in
t
. Putting the pieces together concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
16
.

Write
(X_{h}^{i})_{h\geq 0}
for the
i
-th branching process (i.e. the associated atomic measure) in the i.i.d. sequence. Consider all the subprocesses rooted at particles in
D_{t}\cup\{v_{t}\}
. Enumerate them
j=1,\ldots,J_{t}=|D_{t}|+1
, and write
\tilde{X}_{h}^{j}
for the measure associated to the
j
-th (sub)-branching process. Then we have

\int_{0}^{t}f(\zeta_{s})ds=\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle X_{h}^{i},f\rangle}{\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh-\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle X_{h}^{i},f\rangle}{\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh,

and

\sigma^{2}(f)t=\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\tilde{\varphi},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{\varphi},1\rangle}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh-\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\tilde{\varphi},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{\varphi},1\rangle}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh.

Writing

F_{h}^{i}=\frac{\langle X_{h}^{i},f\rangle}{\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle}-\frac{\langle\tilde{\varphi},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{\varphi},1\rangle}\,\text{ and }\,\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}=\frac{\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle}-\frac{\langle\tilde{\varphi},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{\varphi},1\rangle},

we have

\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f(\zeta_{s})ds-\sigma^{2}(f)t\right)=\frac{1}{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{h}^{i}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh-\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh\right).

Now, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(\zeta_{s})ds-\sigma^{2}(f)t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|F_{h}^{i}|\mathrm{1}_{|F_{h}^{i}|\leq\delta}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|F_{h}^{i}|\mathrm{1}_{|F_{h}^{i}|>\delta}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|\mathrm{1}_{|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|\leq\delta}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|\mathrm{1}_{|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|>\delta}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh

which, since
|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|\leq c,|F_{h}^{i}|\leq c
for some
c
depending only on
f
, gives

\frac{1}{t}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(\zeta_{s})ds-\sigma^{2}(f)t\right|\leq 2\delta\frac{\tau_{t}}{t}+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{1}_{|F_{h}^{i}|>\delta}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{1}_{|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|>\delta}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh.

By Lemma
19
, it therefore suffices to prove that for any fixed
\delta>0
,

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{1}_{|F_{h}^{i}|>\delta}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{1}_{|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|>\delta}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh=:\frac{E_{t}(\delta)}{t}\to 0,

(18)

in probability, as
t\to\infty
. For
M\in(0,\infty)
write

E_{t}^{M}(\delta)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{I_{t}\wedge M\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{M^{2}\sqrt{t}}\mathrm{1}_{|F_{h}^{i}|>\delta}\langle X_{h}^{i},1\rangle dh+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{t}}\int_{0}^{M^{2}\sqrt{t}}\mathrm{1}_{|\tilde{F}_{h}^{j}|>\delta}\langle\tilde{X}_{h}^{j},1\rangle dh

so that for any given
\eta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\bigg(\frac{E_{t}(\delta)}{t}\geq\eta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\bigg(\frac{E_{t}^{M}(\delta)}{t}\geq\eta\bigg)+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(I_{t}\geq M\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\langle X^{i}_{M^{2}\sqrt{t}},1\rangle>0\text{ for some }1\leq i\leq M\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(\langle\tilde{X}^{j}_{M^{2}\sqrt{t}},1\rangle>0\text{ for some }1\leq j\leq J_{t}).

The result will thus be proved if we can show that the second, third and fourth terms all tend to
0
as
M\to\infty
, uniformly in
t
, and that the first term tends to
0
as
t\to\infty
for any fixed
\delta,M,\eta>0
and
x\in E
.

For the first term, observe that

\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\bigg(\frac{E^{M}_{t}(\delta)}{t}\bigg)\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{t}}\sup_{y\in E}\int_{0}^{M^{2}\sqrt{t}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{y}}[\langle X_{h},1\rangle 1_{|\frac{\langle X_{h},f\rangle}{\langle X_{h},1\rangle}-\frac{\langle\tilde{\varphi},f\rangle}{\langle\tilde{\varphi},1\rangle}|\geq\delta}\,|\,\langle X_{h},1\rangle>0]\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{y}}(\langle X_{h},1\rangle>0)\,dh\\

Now by
[
11
]

and Theorem
5

as

For the fourth term, we condition on the positions

Proof of Theorem

Outline of the proof.
We will prove Theorem

via the following steps.

Step 1.

are chosen uniformly (from the duration of the depth first exploration in a tree conditioned to survive until time

) then the difference between the tree distance

h_{v}+h_{w}-2\min_{u\preceq v,u\preceq w}h_{u}

and the quantity

\hat{M}_{t(v)}+\hat{M}_{t(w)}-\min_{s\in[t(w)\wedge t(v),t(w)\vee t(v)]}\hat{M}_{s}

tends to

in probability as

, where

is the time that a particle
u

Step 2.

Hence, for any finite
k
, the above holds pairwise for
k
uniformly chosen particles with arbitrarily high probability as
n\to\infty
. We will then combine this with Theorem
10
to prove that the pairwise tree distances of
k
uniformly chosen particles converge in law to the pairwise distances in the CRT (conditioned to reach height one).

Step 3.

We will then prove that (
12
) holds which, by Lemma
11
, yields the result.

Step 1.

Recall
\hat{M}
from Corollary
14
defined as the martingale
M
but restricted to a single tree. We will make the connection between the height function
(h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\hat{M}_{t}^{d})_{t\geq 0}

For a tree

Proposition 24
.

Suppose Assumption

We prove this in the case

•

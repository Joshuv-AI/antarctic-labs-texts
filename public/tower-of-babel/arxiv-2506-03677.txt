1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group,
\Bbbk
a field and let
V,W
be a pair of finite-dimensional
\Bbbk G
-modules. Then
G
-acts on the set
\Bbbk[V,W]=S(V^{*})\otimes W
of polynomial morphisms
V\rightarrow W
via the formula

(g\phi)(v)=g\phi(g^{-1}v).

Morphisms fixed under the action of
G
are called
covariants
, and we denote the set of covariants by
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
. In the special case
W=\Bbbk
we write
\Bbbk[V,\Bbbk]=\Bbbk[V]
and the corresponding fixed points
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
are called invariants. The set of invariants is a
\Bbbk
-algebra, and pointwise multiplication endows
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
with the structure of a
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-module.

Many theorems about invariant algebras have analogues for covariants which are less well-known. For example, suppose
\Bbbk
has characteristic zero. Then it is well-known that
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
is a polynomial ring if and only if
G
is generated by reflections (elements fixing a subspace of codimension 1 in
V
). It is less well-known that these conditions are equivalent to
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
being a free module over
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
for all
W

[
3
]
,
[
12
]
. Similarly, it is well-known that
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
is always a Cohen-Macaulay algebra. It is less well-known that
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
is always a Cohen-Macaulay module over
\Bbbk[V]^{G}

[
9
]
.

In the modular case, much less is known about the structure of covariant modules. The following result is taken from
[
1
]
:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be field whose characteristic divides
|G|
, where
G
is a finite group acting on vector spaces
V,W
.

•

Suppose that
\operatorname{codim}(V^{G})=1
. Then
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
is a free
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-module.

•

Suppose that
\operatorname{codim}(V^{G})\leq 2
. Then
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-module.

In addition, Broer and Chuai give necessary and sufficient conditions for a set of covariants to generate
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
freely over
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
.

Suppose
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-module. This means that there exists a polynomial subalgebra
A\subseteq\Bbbk[V]^{G}
(generated by a homogeneous system of parameters) over which
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
is finitely generated and free.

The purpose of the present article is to give a method to test whether a set of covariants generates
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
over
A
.. We obtain explicit sets of covariants generating
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
freely over a homogeneous system of parameters when
G
is a cyclic
p
-group,
\operatorname{codim}(V^{G})\leq 2
and
W
is arbitrary. Note that in this case we have that
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
itself is Cohen-Macaulay (apply Proposition
1
with
W
trivial). In fact, it follows from
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
that
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay
if and only if

\operatorname{codim}(V^{G})\leq 2
.

Suppose
G
is a cyclic group of order
q=p^{k}
. Recall the following concerning representation theory of
G
; there are exactly
q
isomorphism classes of indecomposable modules for
G
. The dimensions of these are
1,2,3,\ldots,q
, and we denote by
V_{r}
the unique indecomposable of dimension
r
. A generator
\sigma
of
G
acts on
V_{r}
by left-multiplication with a Jordan block of size
r
with eigenvalue 1. If
r\leq p^{l}
for
l<k
we have that
\sigma^{p^{l}}
acts trivially on
V_{r}
; thus,
V_{r}
is faithful only when
r>p^{k-1}
. The dimension of the fixed-point space
V^{G}
is one for any indecomposable
V
. As a consequence, we obtain:

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
V
is a faithful representation of a cyclic group
G
of order
p^{k}
, and that
\operatorname{codim}(V^{G})\leq 2
. Suppose in addition that
V
contains no trivial direct summands. Then one of the following hold:

(1)

|G|=p
and
V\cong V_{2}
;

(2)

|G|=p>2
and
V\cong V_{3}
;

(3)

|G|=p
and
V\cong V_{2}\oplus V_{2}
.

(4)

|G|=4
and
V\cong V_{3}
;

This paper is organised as follows: in the next section we will prove a very general result which allows us to test whether a set of elements of a free module is a basis (Proposition
4
). In the third section we will show how modular covariants for cyclic groups can be viewed as the kernel of a certain homomorphism. This generalises
[
7
, Proposition 4]
. As an incidental result we show that the kernel of the relative transfer map
\operatorname{Tr}^{G}_{H}:\Bbbk[V]^{H}\rightarrow\Bbbk[V]^{G}
can also be viewed as a module of covariants.

The last section gives explicit generating sets of covariants over a homogeneous system of parameters for the four cases explained above. In the first two cases, such generating sets were obtained in
[
7
]
, but the proof here using Proposition
4
is shorter in the second case, as we can circumvent computation of the Hilbert series of
\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
. The results in the third and fourth cases are new.

2.
The
s
-invariant and freeness

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and
A
a
\Bbbk
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely-generated module over
A
. A set
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r}
of elements of
M
is called a
generating set
if
M=A(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r})
. It is said to be
A
-
independent
if we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}g_{i}=0\Rightarrow a_{1}=a_{2}=\ldots=a_{r}=0\ \text{for all}\ a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\in A.

An
A
-independent, generating set for
M
is called a
basis
of
M
.

The most familiar setting is where
A=\Bbbk
. In that case
M
is a finite-dimensional vector space. It is then well known that
M
has a basis, and any maximal
A
-independent set, or minimal generating set for
M
is a basis. This may not be the case for modules over other algebras. A module which has a basis is called
free
, and one can show that every free module over
A
is isomorphic to
A^{r}
for some
r
.

Now suppose
A=\oplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
is a graded
\Bbbk
-algebra with
A_{0}=\Bbbk
. Let
M=\oplus_{i\geq 0}M_{i}
be a graded
A
module. We have Hilbert series

H(A,t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim(A_{i})t^{i},

H(M,t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim(M_{i})t^{i}.

Consider the quotient of these series, expanded about
t=1
:

H(M,A;t):=\frac{H(M,t)}{H(A,t)}=a_{0}+a_{1}(t-1)+\ldots

If
M
is finitely generated, then by the Hilbert-Poincaré theorem we can write

H(M,t)=\frac{f(t)}{H(A,t)}

for some polynomial
f(t)
.
In that case we have

a_{0}=f(1)=r(M,A).

This is called the
rank
of
M
over
A
. If
M
has finite projective dimension (for example, if
A
is regular) it can be shown to equal the dimension of
M
as a vector space over
\operatorname{Quot}(A)
. We also have

a_{1}=f^{\prime}(1)=s(M,A).

This is called the
s
-invariant
of
M
over
A
.

The following is an easy consequence of the definition of
H(M,A;t)
:

Proposition 3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
A
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra and
B\subset A
a subalgebra of
A
over which
A
is finitely generated. Then we have

(a)

r(M,B)=r(M,A)r(A,B);

(b)

s(M,B)=r(M,A)s(A,B)+r(A,B)s(M,A).

The next result is the main result of this section, and will be used frequently in the remainder of the article.

Proposition 4
.

Let
A
be a regular, graded
\Bbbk
-module with
A_{0}=\Bbbk
and let
M
be a finite generated free graded module over
A
. Let
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r}
be a
A
-independent set of elements of
M
, where
r=r(M,A)
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\deg(g_{i})\geq s(M,A)

with equality if and only if
M
is generated by
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{r}
be a generating set of
M
. We may assume without loss of generality that

\deg(f_{1})\leq\deg(f_{2})\leq\ldots\leq\deg(f_{r})

and also

\deg(g_{1})\leq\deg(g_{2})\leq\ldots\leq\deg(g_{r}).

As
M
is free, we have

\frac{H(M,t)}{H(A,t)}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}t^{\deg(f_{j})}

and
s(M,A)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j})
.

We claim that
\deg(g_{j})\geq\deg(f_{j})
for all
j\leq r
. Suppose the contrary. Then for some
k\leq r-1
we must have
\deg(g_{k+1})<\deg(f_{k+1})
and consequently

g_{j}\in A(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})

for all
j=1,\ldots,k+1
.
Write
g_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{ij}f_{i}
. Let
A
be the
k\times(k+1)
matrix with elements

(a_{ij}:i=1,\ldots,k,j=1,\ldots,k+1.)

We will prove by reverse induction on
t
that all
t\times t
minors of
A
are zero. The initial case is
t=k
. Form the matrix
A^{\prime}
by adding an extra row
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{k+1}\}
. Now the matrix
A^{\prime}
is

\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&g_{2}&\ldots&g_{k+1}\\
a_{11}&a_{12}&\ldots&a_{1k+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{k1}&a_{k2}&\ldots&a_{kk+1}\end{pmatrix}

The determinant of this matrix is a
A
-linear combination of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
, and the coefficent of
f_{i}
is

\det\begin{pmatrix}a_{i1}&a_{i2}&\ldots&a_{ik+1}\\
a_{11}&a_{12}&\ldots&a_{1k+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{k1}&a_{k2}&\ldots&a_{kk+1}\end{pmatrix}

which is zero because it has a repeated row. We thus obtain a relation

\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{j}g_{j}A_{j}=0

where
A_{j}
is the
k\times k
minor of
A
obtained by deleting the
j
th column. Since
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k+1}
are linearly independent over
A
, we get that
A_{j}=0
as required.

Now suppose that all
(t+1)\times(t+1)
minors of
A
are zero, where
t<k
. Consider a
t\times t
submatrix

B:=(a_{p_{i}q_{j}}:i,j=1,\ldots,t).

Pick any
q_{t+1}\in\{1,\ldots,k+1\}\setminus\{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{t}\}
and consider the determinant of the matrix

B^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}g_{q_{1}}&g_{q_{2}}&\ldots&g_{q_{t+1}}\\
a_{p_{1}q_{1}}&a_{p_{1}q_{2}}&\ldots&a_{p_{1}q_{t+1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{p_{t}q_{1}}&a_{p_{t}q_{2}}&\ldots&a_{p_{t}q_{t+1}}\end{pmatrix}

This is again an
A
-linear combination of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
, and the coefficient of
f_{i}
is

\det\begin{pmatrix}a_{iq_{1}}&a_{iq_{2}}&\ldots&a_{iq_{t+1}}\\
a_{p_{1}q_{1}}&a_{p_{1}q_{2}}&\ldots&a_{p_{1}q_{t+1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{p_{t}q_{1}}&a_{p_{t}q_{2}}&\ldots&a_{p_{t}q_{t+1}}.\end{pmatrix}

If
i\not\in\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}\}
then this is a
t+1\times t+1
minor of
A
, and zero by inductive hypothesis. Otherwise it is again zero, being the determinant of a matrix with a repeated row. We thus obtain

0=\sum_{j=1}^{t+1}(-1)^{j}g_{q_{j}}B_{j}

where
B_{j}
is the
t\times t
minor of
B^{\prime}
obtained by removing the
j
th column. Since
g_{q_{1}},\ldots,g_{q_{t}}
are linearly independent we obtain
B_{j}=0
for all
j=1,\ldots,t
, in particular,

\det(B)=B_{t}=0.

This completes the induction. Taking
t=1
, we see that
a_{ij}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
and
j=1,\ldots,k+1
. Therefore

g_{1}=g_{2}=\ldots g_{k+1}=0

which contradicts linear independence.

This completes the proof of our claim, and we may conclude that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j}).

Further, if
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j})
then we must have
\deg(g_{j})=\deg(f_{j})
for all
j=1,\ldots,r
, and
A(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r})
is a submodule of
M
with the same Hilbert series, and therefore
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r}
are free generators of
M
. On the other hand, if
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j})>\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(f_{j})
then
\deg(g_{j})>\deg(f_{j})
for some
j
, and
A(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r})
is a proper submodule of
M
.

\Box

3.
Characterising covariants

In this section we will show how modules of covariants can often be viewed as the kernel of a certain homomorphism. First, let
G
denote a finite group and
\Bbbk
a field of arbitary characteristic. Let
H\leq G
. Suppose
G
acts on a
\Bbbk
-vector space
V
and let
T
be a left-transveral of
H
in
G
. We can assume
\iota\in T
where
\iota
is the identity element in
G
. Let
\{e_{t}:t\in T\}
be a basis for the left permutation module
\Bbbk(G/H)
on which
H
acts trivially. We define a map

\Theta:\Bbbk[V]^{H}\rightarrow\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G/H)

\Theta(f)=\sum_{t\in T}tf\otimes e_{t}.

where
G
acts diagonally on the tensor product.

Clearly
\Theta
is an injective, degree-preserving homomorphsim of
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
modules. Further we claim:

Proposition 5
.

\Theta
induces an isomorphism of graded
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-modules

\Bbbk[V]^{H}\cong(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G/H))^{G}.

Proof.

We show first that if
f\in\Bbbk[V]^{H}
then
\Theta(f)\in(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G/H))^{G}
. Let
g\in G
, then we have

g\cdot\Theta(f)=\sum_{t\in T}gtf\otimes ge_{t}.

Write
gt=\sigma_{g}(t)h_{g}
, where
\sigma_{g}(t)\in T
and
h_{g}\in H
, noting that
\sigma_{g}
is a permutation of
T
. Then since
f\in\Bbbk[V]^{H}
and
H
acts trivially on
\Bbbk(G/H)
we have

\sum_{t\in T}gtf\otimes ge_{t}=\sum_{t\in T}\sigma_{g}(t)h_{g}f\otimes h_{g}e_{\sigma_{g}(t)}=\sum_{t\in T}\sigma_{g}(t)f\otimes e_{\sigma_{g}(t)}=\Theta(f)

as required

Now we need only show that
\Theta
is surjective. Let

f=\sum_{t\in T}^{r}f_{t}\otimes e_{t}\in(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G/H))^{G}

where
f_{t}\in\Bbbk[V]
. Again for any
g\in G
write
gt=\sigma_{g}(t)h_{g}.
Thus since
f=gf
we have

f=\sum_{t\in T}gf_{t}\otimes e_{\sigma_{g}(t)}=\sum_{t\in T}f_{t}\otimes e_{t}.

Equating coefficients of
e_{t}
shows that

gf_{t}=f_{\sigma_{g}(t)}

for any
g\in G
and
t\in T
. In particular, if
t\in T
then the permutation
\sigma_{t}
satisfies
\sigma_{t}(\iota)=t
. Thus for any
t\in T
we have

tf_{\iota}=f_{t}

and hence

f=\sum_{t\in T}^{r}f_{t}\otimes e_{t}=\sum_{t\in T}tf_{\iota}\otimes e_{t}=\Theta(f_{\iota})

which shows that
\Theta
is surjective as required.

\Box

Now let
M
be a submodule of
\Bbbk(G/H)
. Tensor the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\Bbbk(G/H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\rightarrow}}N\rightarrow 0,

where
N=\ker(i)
with
\Bbbk[V]
and take
G
-invariants. This induces a long exact sequence

0\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes M)^{G}\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G/H))^{G}\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes N)^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,\Bbbk[V]\otimes M)\rightarrow\ldots

By composing the isomorphism
\Theta
with the second nontrivial map in this sequence, we obtain a graded
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
homomorphism

\phi:\Bbbk[V]^{H}\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes N)^{G}

whose kernel is isomorphic to the module of covariants
(\Bbbk[V]\otimes M)^{G}=\Bbbk[V,M]^{G}
.

We will consider two special cases of the above: first, suppose
M=M_{G/H}=\langle e_{t}-e_{\iota}:t\in T\rangle_{\Bbbk}
. Then
N
is a one-dimensional module on which
G
acts trivially and the map
j
sends each
e_{t}
to its basis element, which we will denote by
e
. The induced map in the long exact sequence sends an expression

\sum_{t\in T}f_{t}\otimes e_{t}

to

\sum_{t\in T}f_{t}\otimes e.

Thus, the composition with
\Theta
sends an element
f\in\Bbbk[V]^{H}
to
\sum_{t\in T}tf
- in other words, this is the
relative transfer map

\operatorname{Tr}^{G}_{H}:\Bbbk[V]^{H}\rightarrow\Bbbk[V]^{G}
. It follows that the kernel of the relative transfer map is isomorphic, as a graded
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
-module, to the module of covariants
\Bbbk[V,M_{G/H}]^{G}
. This is interesting, but we will not pursue it further in the present article. We note for future use that the (relative) transfer map is a useful way of producing invariants. Along similar lines, one has a
relative norm map

N^{G}_{H}:\Bbbk[V]^{H}\rightarrow\Bbbk[V]^{G}
defined by

N^{G}_{H}(f)=\prod_{t\in T}tf.

Now suppose
G
is cyclic of order
q=p^{r}
,
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic
p
and
H
is trivial. Let
g
be a generator of
G
and set
\Delta:=g-\iota\in\Bbbk G
. For each
1\leq n<q
there is a submodule
\ker(\Delta^{n})
of
\Bbbk G
isomorphic to
V_{n}
. The image of
\Delta^{n}
is a submodule isomorphic to
V_{q-n}
.

Let
M
and
N
denote the kernel and image of
\Delta^{n}
respectively. Then the canonical short exact sequence induces a long exact sequence

0\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes M)^{G}\rightarrow(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk(G))^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}(\Bbbk[V]\otimes N)^{G}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,\Bbbk[V]\otimes M)\rightarrow\ldots

Consider the composition

\Bbbk[V]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta}}{{\rightarrow}}(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk G)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}(\Bbbk[V]\otimes N)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\rightarrow}}(\Bbbk[V]\otimes\Bbbk G)^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\Bbbk[V]

Here
k
is the canonical inclusion. Choose a basis
\{e_{i}:i=0,\ldots q-1\}
for
\Bbbk G
with
ge_{i}=e_{i+1}
for
i<q
and
ge_{q-1}=e_{0}
.
For
n=1
, the composition
k\psi
sends an element

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}f_{i}\otimes e_{i}

to

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}f_{i}\otimes(e_{i+1}-e_{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}(f_{i-1}-f_{i})\otimes e_{i}

with indices understood modulo
q
. Thus the composition
k\psi\Theta
sends
f\in\Bbbk[V]
to

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}(g^{i-1}f-g^{i}f)\otimes e_{i}.

Composing this with
\Theta^{-1}
then picks out the coefficient of
e_{0}
, which is

g^{-1}f-f.

Thus, the composition is action by
g^{-1}-\iota\in\Bbbk G
. An easy inductive argument shows that for arbitrary
n
, the composition is action by
(g^{-1}-\iota)^{n}\in\Bbbk G
. Since
\Theta
is an isomorphism and

is injective, the kernel of this map (which is the same as the kernel of
\Delta^{n}
) can be identified with the kernel of
\psi
, which in turn is isomorphic to
\Bbbk[V,M]^{G}
. Thus, we obtain a degree-preserving isomorphism of
\Bbbk[V]^{G}
modules

\Xi:\ker(\Delta^{n})\cong\Bbbk[V,V_{n}]^{G}.

Note that a version of this isomorphism was used in
[
7
]
for
G
a cyclic group of prime order. As noted there, if we choose a basis
\{w_{1},w-2,\ldots,w_{n}\}
of
V_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma w_{1}

\displaystyle=w_{1}

\displaystyle\sigma w_{2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}-w_{1}

\displaystyle\sigma w_{3}

\displaystyle=w_{2}-w_{2}+w_{1}

\displaystyle\sigma w_{n}

\displaystyle=w_{n}-w_{n-1}+w_{n-2}-\ldots\pm w_{1}.

then
\Xi
is given by the particularly convenient formula

\Xi(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Delta^{i}(f)w_{i}.

4.
Main results

From now on let
G=\langle\sigma\rangle
be a cyclic group of order
q=p^{k}
, let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic
p
and let
V
and
W
be finite-dimensional
\Bbbk G
-modules.

The operator
\Delta=\sigma-\iota\in\Bbbk G
will play a major role in our exposition, so we begin with some general results, following
[
7
]
quite closely. Notice that, for
\phi\in\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
we have

\Delta(\phi)=0\Rightarrow\sigma\cdot\phi=\phi

and thus by induction
\sigma^{k}\phi=\phi
for all
k
. So
\Delta(\phi)=0
if and only if
\phi\in\Bbbk[V,W]^{G}
. Similarly for
f\in\Bbbk[V]
we have
\Delta(f)=0
if and only if
f\in\Bbbk[V]^{G}
.

\Delta
is a
\sigma
-twisted derivation
on
\Bbbk[V]
; that is, it satisfies the formula

(1)

\Delta(fg)=f\Delta(g)+\Delta(f)\sigma(g)

for all
f,g\in\Bbbk[V]
.

Further, using induction and the fact that
\sigma
and
\Delta
commute, one can show
\Delta
satisfies a Leibniz-type rule

\Delta^{k}(fg)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\begin{pmatrix}k\\
i\end{pmatrix}\Delta^{i}(f)\sigma^{k-i}(\Delta^{k-i}(g)).

A further result, which can be deduced from the above and proved by induction is the rule for differentiating powers:

(3)

\Delta(f^{k})=\Delta(f)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f^{i}\sigma(f)^{k-1-i}\right)

for any
k\geq 1
.

For any
f\in\Bbbk[V]
we define the
weight
of

:

\operatorname{wt}(f)=\min\{i>0:\Delta^{i}(f)=0\}.

Notice that
\Delta^{\operatorname{wt}(f)-1}(f)\in\ker(\Delta)=\Bbbk[V]^{G}
for all
f\in\Bbbk[V]
.
Another consequence of (
2
) is the following: let
f,g\in\Bbbk[V]
and set
d=\operatorname{wt}(f),e=\operatorname{wt}(g)
. Suppose that

d+e-1\leq p.

Then

\Delta^{d+e-1}(fg)=\sum_{i=0}^{d+e-1}\begin{pmatrix}d+e-1\\
i\end{pmatrix}\Delta^{i}(f)\sigma^{d+e-1-i}(\Delta^{d+e-1-i}(g))=0

since if
i<e
then
d+e-1-i>d-1
. On the other hand

\displaystyle\Delta^{d+e-2}(fg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d+e-2}\begin{pmatrix}d+e-2\\
i\end{pmatrix}\Delta^{i}(f)\sigma^{d+e-2-i}(\Delta^{d+e-2-i}(g))

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}d+e-2\\
i\end{pmatrix}\Delta^{d-1}(f)\sigma^{e-1}(\Delta^{e-1}(g))\neq 0

since
\begin{pmatrix}d+e-2\\
i\end{pmatrix}\neq 0\mod p
. We obtain the followng:

Proposition 6
.

Let
f,g\in\Bbbk[V]
with
\operatorname{wt}(f)+\operatorname{wt}(g)-1\leq p
. Then
\operatorname{wt}(fg)=\operatorname{wt}(f)+\operatorname{wt}(g)-1
.

Also note that

\Delta^{q}=\sigma^{q}-1=0

which shows that
\operatorname{wt}(f)\leq q
for all
f\in\Bbbk[V]^{G}
. Finally notice that

(4)

\Delta^{q-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\sigma^{i}

which shows that
\Delta^{q-1}(f)=\operatorname{Tr}^{G}(f)
for all
f\in\Bbbk[V]
.

2

4.2.

\displaystyle=x_{1}+x_{3};

\displaystyle\sigma x_{2}

\displaystyle\sigma x_{4}

\displaystyle=x_{4}.

It is easy to see that
\{N^{G}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{p}-x_{1}x_{3}^{p-1},N^{G}(x_{2})=x_{2}^{p}-x_{2}x_{4}^{p-1},x_{3},x_{4}\}

Lemma 18

This follows from Equations (

\Box

We use a graded lexicographical order on monomials with

Lemma 19
.

The lead term of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{j!}{(j-k)!}x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j-k}x_{4}^{k}&k\leq j\\

Let

is

We now define, for

We use the notation

. The Kernel

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}M_{k};

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=1}^{p-n}u^{k}M_{n-1};

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=2p-n}^{2p-2}\Delta^{p-n}M_{k}.

The

, so the weight of each element of

. Since

is
n
. If

, so in that case the weight of each element of

is
p
. It follows that the weight of each element of
D
is
n
. The shows that the proposed generators all lie in

. Now by Proposition

-independent, has

Viewed as a polynomial in

r(K_{n},A)=2n,

and

s(K_{n},A)=3n+2s(\Bbbk[V,V_{n}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H}),

the latter because
H
is the subgroup of
G
generated by pseudo-reflections.

We consider each value of
n=2,3,4
separately. We note that
\Delta^{2}=\sigma^{2}-\iota
, so
K_{2}=\Bbbk[V]^{H}=\Bbbk[N^{H}(x_{1}),x_{2},x_{3}]
. However, we seek generators of
K_{2}
as an
A
-module. Our first result is:

Proposition 22
.

The set
S=\{1,x_{2},N^{H}(x_{1}),x_{2}N^{H}(x_{1})\}
generates
K_{2}
over
A
.

Proof.

We will need to compute
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{2}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H})
. Observe that, since
H
acts trivially on
V_{2}
, we may write

\Bbbk[V,V_{2}]^{H}=\Bbbk[V]^{H}\otimes V_{2}.

If
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
is a basis of
V_{2}
, then
\Bbbk[V,V_{2}]^{H}
is generated over
\Bbbk[V]^{H}
by
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, both having degree zero. So
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{2}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H})=0
and therefore
s(K_{2},A)=6
.

Now it is clear that every element of
S
lies in
K_{2}=\Bbbk[V]^{H}
. Further,
|S|=4
and the degree sum of
S
is 6. It remains to show that
S
is
A
-independent. This is easy to see: the lead terms of
S
are
1,x_{2},x_{1}^{2}
and
x_{1}^{2}x_{2}
and every element of
A
has lead term
x_{1}^{4a}x_{2}^{2b}x_{3}
, so the lead terms of any different pair of elements of the form
fg
where
f\in A
,
g\in S
are distinct.

\Box

Proposition 23
.

The set
S=\{1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{2},\Delta(x_{1}^{3}),\Delta(x_{1}^{3}x_{2}).\}
generates
K_{3}
over
A
.

Proof.

We will need to compute
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{3}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H})
. Choose a basis
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}
of (the second copy of)
V_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}w_{1}

\displaystyle=w_{1}+w_{3}

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}w_{3}

\displaystyle=w_{3}.

Then we may write

\Bbbk[V,V_{3}]^{H}=(S(V^{*})\otimes V_{3})^{H}=w_{2}\otimes\Bbbk[V]^{H}\oplus(S(V^{*})\otimes W)^{H}

where
W=\langle w_{1},w_{3}\rangle
.
The first direct summand is generated by
w_{2}
over
\Bbbk[V]^{H}
, with degree zero. The second is isomorphic to
\ker((\sigma^{2}-1)^{2})=\Bbbk[V]
, which is generated over
\Bbbk[V]^{H}=\Bbbk[N^{H}(x_{1}),x_{2},x_{3}]
by
x_{1}
. Therefore

s(\Bbbk[V,V_{3}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H})=1.

Consequently we get that
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{3}]^{G},\Bbbk[V]^{G})=11
.

Now an easy direct calculation shows that every element of
S
lies in
K_{3}
(for the last two simply note that
\Delta^{4}=0
). We have
|S|=6
and the degree sum of
S
is 11. Further, the lead terms of
S
are

\{1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{1}^{2}x_{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{1}^{3}x_{3}\}

and so once more every for different pair of elements of the form
fg
where
f\in A
and
g\in S
, the lead terms are distinct.

\Box

Proposition 24
.

The set
S=\{1,x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}^{3},x_{1}^{2}x_{2},x_{1}^{3}x_{2}\}
generates
K_{4}
over
A
.

Proof.

We have
K_{4}=\Bbbk[V]
so obviously each element of
S
lies in
K_{4}
, and the proof of
A
-independence is similar to the previous result. To prove
S
is a generating set we could proceed as before, where we would find that
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{4}]^{H},\Bbbk[V]^{H})=2
and hence
s(\Bbbk[V,V_{4}]^{G},\Bbbk[V]^{G})=16
. However it is probably simpler to argue as follows: let
f\in\Bbbk[V]
and assume
f\not\in SA
with minimal degree in
x_{1}
. By long division with
N^{G}(x_{1})
we may assume the
x_{1}
-degree of
f
is at most three. Thus we may write

f=g_{0}+g_{1}x_{1}+g_{2}x_{1}^{2}+g_{3}x_{1}^{3},

with
g_{i}\in\Bbbk[x_{2},x_{3}]
. Now assume that among all such examples,
g_{3}
has minimal
x_{2}
-degree; by long division with
N^{G}_{H}(x_{2})
we may assume this
x_{2}
-degree is at most one. Treating each
g_{i}=2,1,0
in turn in the same manner, we see that
f\in SA
after all, a contradiction.

\Box

The MdERF61-mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module regulates apple resistance to Fusarium solani via lignin biosynthesis
Authors: Zhe Zhou, Haiqing Zhang, Jia-Long Yao, Qiming Gao, Yarong Wang, Zhenzhen Liu, Yaru Zhang, Yi Tian, Zhenli Yan, Yanmin Zhu, Hengtao Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractApple replant disease (ARD) is a worldwide problem that threatens the industry. However, the genetic mechanism underlying plant disease resistance against ARD remains unclear. In this study, a negative regulatory microRNA in Malus domestica, mdm-miR397b, and its direct target MdLAC7b (Laccase) was selected for examination based on our previous small RNA and degradome sequencing results. Overexpressing the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module altered the lignin deposition and jasmonic acid contents in apple roots, which also led to increased resistance to Fusarium solani. Additionally, Y1H library screening using mdm-miR397b promoter recombinants identified a transcription factor, MdERF61, that represses mdm-miR397b transcriptional activity by directly binding to 2 GCC-boxes in the mdm-miR397b promoter. In summary, our results suggest that the MdERF61-mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module plays a crucial role in apple resistance to F. solani and offers insights for enhancing plant resistance to soil-borne diseases in apples.

Introduction

With the increasing availability of new and improved apple cultivars, replanting an old orchard has become a more common practice. Apple replant disease (ARD) has been defined as stunted growth or death of newly planted trees at a replant site where apple or closely related tree species have previously been cultivated (Mazzola and Manici 2012). In general, ARD is caused by soil-borne necrotrophic pathogenic fungi and oomycetes, and the incidence can be aggravated by the presence of nematodes (Jaffee et al. 1982a, 1982b; Mazzola 1997; Nurnberger et al. 2004; Mazzola and Manici 2012; Zhu et al. 2014). The main pathogenic microorganisms include species of Pythium, Cylindrocarpon, Fusarium, and Rhizoctonia (Tewoldemedhin et al. 2011; Kelderer et al. 2012; Mazzola and Manici 2012; Franke-Whittle et al. 2015; Liu et al. 2023). Fusarium species have been reported to be the causal agents of ARD in China, South Africa, and Italy (Liu et al. 2023).

The process by which species of Fusarium infect plants can be roughly divided into the following steps: recognition of root tissues, attachment of the spores to the root surface, hyphal propagation, penetration and colonization into the root cortex and vascular tissue, differentiation within host xylem vessels, and secretion of toxins and virulence factors (Pietro et al. 2003; Coleman 2016; Gordon 2017). To prevent Fusarium from spreading beyond the xylem, infected plants usually form a physical barrier by accumulating large amounts of callose and lignin (Trillas et al. 2000; Pshibytko et al. 2006; Kabashnikova et al. 2020). Fusarium that breaks through the physical barrier could further secrete enzymes to dissolve plant cell walls, grow rapidly through the plant, block the water and nutrient uptake in xylem vessels, and eventually lead to plant wilting (Trillas et al. 2000; Dong et al. 2019).

Little is known regarding the molecular regulation of apple root resistance to soil-borne necrotrophic pathogens like F. solani, though recent genomics and genetic analyses have provided clues. Laccases (LAC, EC 1.10.3.2) are glycosylated multi-copper oxidases (MCOs) involved in regulating cell-wall structure and secondary metabolite synthesis in plants by catalyzing lignin polymerization (Su et al. 2018; Janusz et al. 2020; Zhu and Zhou 2021). miR397 is one of the highly conserved miRNAs in flowering plants involved in modulating diverse biological processes (Huang et al. 2021). It is a common regulatory mechanism in plants for miR397 to target LAC family members, therefore mediating processes of plant growth, embryonic development, flower organ and seed development, flowering time, fruit development, and abiotic and biotic stress (Wang et al. 2014; Huang et al. 2021). More recently, accumulated evidence has demonstrated the important function of the miR397-LAC module in disease resistance regulation.

In cotton (Gossypium hirsutum), ghr-miR397 targets GhLAC4, and overexpressing ghr-miR397 or silencing GhLAC4 blocks the biosynthesis of lignin rich with G-subunits, which is the main lignin type used by plants against invading pathogens. This disruption results in plant weight loss and fragile plant cell walls, leading to compromised resistance to Verticillium dahliae (Chezem et al. 2017; Zhang et al. 2019; Wei et al. 2021). In addition, in ghr-miR397 overexpression or GhLAC4 knockout transgenic plants, both the content of jasmonic acid (JA) and the expression levels of key genes in the JA pathway were significantly reduced, suggesting that the regulation of cotton resistance to V. dahliae by the ghr-miR397-GhLAC4 module may be associated with the JA signaling pathway (Wei et al. 2021). Transient transformation in pear (Pyrus communis) verified that overexpressing a single PcLAC or silencing PcmiR397 enhanced plant resistance to fungal pathogens via lignin synthesis (Yang et al. 2023). Co-expression of Malus hupehensis miR397 and LAC7 in Nicotiana benthamiana suppressed the accumulation of NbLAC7, causing decreased lignin content and reducing plant resistance to Botrytis cinerea (Yu et al. 2020).

Currently, little is known about the components and roles of this regulatory module contributing to the resistance of apple roots to infection from F. solani. In this study, a series of experiments were carried out utilizing molecular, biochemical, and bioinformatic methods as well as a transgenic approach. The goal is to identify the upstream regulators of the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module and their interaction in response to infection from F. solani. Our results demonstrated several important features of this regulatory circuit which lead to molecular, cellular, and biochemical changes directly relevant to apple root defense responses. The data from the current study substantially expand our knowledge about the signal transduction and molecular regulation of apple root resistance responses to infection from this soil-borne necrotrophic pathogen.

Results

Mdm-miR397b suppresses the expression of MdLAC7b by direct cleavage

MiR397 species are known to have a strict one-to-one interaction pattern, as LAC (laccase) family genes are confirmed to be the target genes of miR397 in plants (Huang et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2021). Based on our previous transcriptome analysis, the expression of mdm-miR397b has been identified to be negatively correlated with MdLAC7b upon ARD pathogen treatment (Zhu et al. 2021). According to our degradome sequencing data, the cleavage of MdLAC7b by mdm-miR397b takes place between the 646th and 647th positions of its CDS (coding DNA sequence) region (Fig. 1A). To determine whether MdLAC7b is the direct target of mdm-miR397b, the dual-luciferase-based miRNA sensor system was used (Liu et al. 2014) (Fig. 1B). Both the dual-luciferase signals (Fig. 1C) and relative luciferase activity (LUC/REN, luciferase/renilla luciferase; Fig. 1D) were dramatically reduced when mdm-miR397b was co-expressed with MdLAC7b. In contrast, no such signal or activity decrease was observed with mdm-miR397b co-infiltrated with MdLAC7bmu (an mdm-miR397b-resistant form of MdLAC7b with synonymous mutations at the cleavage site). In addition, overexpression of mdm-miR397b caused downregulation of MdLAC7b expression, which can also be observed from our transcriptome data (Supplementary Fig. S1). These results suggest that mdm-miR397b directly targets and cleaves MdLAC7b mRNA to reduce its expression intensity in vivo.

The mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module regulates apple root resistance to F. solani

To investigate the functional roles of the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module in apple resistance to F. solani infection, transgenic apple lines overexpressing mdm-miR397b (OX397b) and MdLAC7b (OXLAC7b) were obtained by Agrobacterium rhizogenes-mediated transformation and verified by RT-qPCR, respectively (Fig. 2, A and B). Seven days after being inoculated with F. solani, distinguishable resistance responses can be observed between WT-pSAK778 (wild-type Gl-3 plants transformed with pSAK778 vectors), OX397b and OXMdLAC7b lines (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Fig. S2). Based on the highest expression level of mdm-miR397b and MdLAC7b, OX397b-9 and OXLAC7-2 were selected for subsequent analysis. Typical disease symptoms, including leaf wilt and partial root collapse, appeared in the WT-pSAK778 and OX397b plants while upregulating MdLAC7b improved the resistance of apples to F. solani (Fig. 2A and 2C). Well-defined zones separating infected and healthy tissue (Fig. 2C, arrow) could be observed in OXLAC7b roots. This phenomenon was further confirmed by measuring the biomass of both the aboveground and belowground parts (Supplementary Table S1). F. solani infection did not significantly affect the biomass of OXLAC7 plants in either part, while the biomass of both OX397b and WT-pSAK778 plants was significantly impacted by the pathogen. OX397b was more severely affected than WT-pSAK778, both above and belowground.

Lignin biosynthesis regulated by the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module is a key factor contributing to apple root resistance to F. solani

Given the well-defined role of laccase in lignin formation and the correlation between lignin content and resistance traits, the total lignin contents in the roots of OX397b and OXLAC7b plants were examined. The result indicated that plants overexpressing MdLAC7b exhibited significantly higher total lignin content compared to that of WT-pSAK778 plants, while the OX397b lines showed exactly the opposite result (Fig. 3A). Lactic acid-based fluorol yellow (FY) staining also revealed that overexpressing MdLAC7b promoted lignification in roots, as illustrated by stronger lignin autofluorescence signals of endodermal cells in overexpression plants than in WT-pSAK778 and OX397b plants (Fig. 3B). After inoculation with F. solani, the lignin concentration increased slightly in all genotypes, and the increase in OXLAC7b plants was statistically higher compared with WT-pSAK778 and OX397b plants (Fig. 3A).

Before being transported to the cell walls, monolignols are synthesized in the cytosol through the plant phenylpropanoid pathway. Therefore, the concentrations and proportions of the monolignols for lignin polymerization are supposed to have a close relationship with the expression of the structural genes involved in the phenylpropanoid pathway. An RNA-seq analysis including WT-pSAK778, OX397b, and OXLAC7b plants infected with F. solani (F) or mock (M) solution at 7 dpi (day post inoculation) was then carried out to test our hypothesis. Compared to WT-pSAK778 plants, fewer differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in both transgenic lines in response to F. solani infection (Supplementary Fig. S3). According to Kyoto Encyclopedia of genes and genomes analysis, 15 DEGs annotated to the lignin phenylpropanoid pathway were identified (Fig. 3C). In WT-pSAK778 plants, an overwhelming majority of the DEGs (13 out of 15) exhibited expression differences before and after inoculation. In contrast, OX397b and OXLAC7b transgenic lines displayed only 2 and 1 DEGs, respectively (Fig. 3C and Supplementary Table S1). Identified DEGs include genes encoding phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL), 4-coumarate: coA ligase (4CL), hydroxycinnamoyltransferase (HCT), cinnamyl-alcohol dehydrogenase (CAD), caffeic acid 3-O-methyltransferase (COMT), and ferulate 5-hydroxylase (F5H). PAL is the first enzyme of the general phenylpropanoid pathway, whose enzyme activity directly affects the output of lignin biosynthesis (Jun et al. 2018). In our dataset, one DEG (MdPAL3, MD04G1096200) annotated to PAL was identified.

Compared to that in WT-pSAK778, an elevated expression level of MdPAL3 was observed in the OXLAC7b-C line even before the pathogenic challenge. In response to F. solani inoculation, expression of MdPAL3 was further enhanced, although the expression difference between F. solani and mock inoculation treatments was not statistically significant, contrasting that in WT-pSAK778. The overall expression levels of MdPAL3 in OXLAC7b lines were much higher than those in WT-pSAK778 and OX397b lines. In contrast, while OX397b lines showed reduced MdPAL3 expression in general, an additional minor decrease was observed after F. solani inoculation. A similar regulatory mode could also be observed with the DEGs encoding 4CLs and HCTs (Fig. 3C).

In addition to the altered total lignin levels in transgenic lines, the composition of lignin was then examined. Monolignols that contribute to the formation of lignin include p-coumaryl alcohol, coniferyl alcohol, and sinapyl alcohol, which are referred to as p-hydroxyphenyl (H), guaiacyl (G), and syringyl (S) lignin units, respectively (Boerjan et al. 2003). As shown in Fig. 3D, OX397b released a significantly smaller amount of G-monomers than WT-pSAK778 and OXLAC7b, while the S monomer content in all lines remained unchanged. G-units have been regarded as a defensive barrier against pathogen attacks (Zhang et al. 2019). The amount of H units in lignin from angiosperms is usually low to trace, thus it was not analyzed in our study (Weng et al. 2010).

Lignin modification affects cell wall thickness and JA signaling in transgenic plants

Lignin is the primary component in the secondary cell wall (SCW) of vascular plants (Chen and Dixon 2007). Elevated cell-wall lignification increases the mechanical strength of plants and enhances the tolerance of the cell wall toward cell-wall degrading enzymes (CWDEs) released by pathogens (Hernández-Blanco et al. 2007; Hückelhoven 2007). As shown in Fig. 4A, F. solani infection led to serious cellular damage in WT-pSAK778 and OX397b, with a large amount of the SCW disappearing (pointed out by brown arrows). Cell structure of OXLAC7b lines was relatively normal, with obviously thicker and more complete SCW. The width of SCW increased 66.6% and 35.7% relative to OX397b transgenic lines and WT-pSAK778 lines, respectively (Fig. 4B), indicating that overexpression of MdLAC7b leads to a remarkable increase in SCW thickness.

It was reported that lignin accumulation and JA concentration show opposing trends in cotton, during immune responses mediated by genes from the LAC family (Hu et al. 2018). To investigate possible links between the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module and defense signaling, the concentrations of JA and JA-Ile were measured in roots of transgenic lines and wild-type plants (Fig. 4, C and D). In mock-inoculated roots, the OX397b lines showed significantly higher levels of JA and JA-Ile compared to WT-pSAK778 and OXLAC7b lines, while no significant differences were observed between WT-pSAK778 and OXLAC7b lines. F. solani inoculation further promoted a sharp increase of JA and JA-Ile in all 3 lines compared to mock inoculation. Our results indicate that JA synthesis was constitutively activated in the lignin-deficient lines, or a low lignin level in transgenic lines appeared to be associated with a higher JA level, which is consistent with previous research (Gallego-Giraldo et al. 2011; Hu et al. 2018).

MdERF61 (ethylene-responsive factor) represses the transcriptional activity of mdm-miR397b by directly binding GCC-boxes in its promoter

A yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) screening assay and point-to-point Y1H verifications were performed to explore the transcriptional factors upstream of the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module. One potential regulator annotated as ethylene-responsive transcription factor was identified and designated as MdERF61 (Fig. 5A). Specifically, the Y1H assay demonstrated that, as in the positive control, yeast colonies co-transformed with MdERF61 and the promoter of mdm-miR397b survived on the plate supplemented with 100 mm AbA. RT-qPCR showed that MdERF61 was upregulated upon F. solani inoculation (Supplementary Fig. S4). To determine the regulatory effect of MdERF61 on the transcription of mdm-miR397b, the mdm-miR397b:LUC reporter construct was co-transformed with either the 35S or 35S:MdERF61 effector constructs into Nicotiana benthamiana epidermal cells (Fig. 5B). Reduced LUC intensity was observed with the mdm-miR397bpro-LUC+ MdERF61 combination, implying that MdERF61 represses the transcriptional activity of mdm-miR397b in vivo (Fig. 5, C and D). By analyzing the mdm-miR397b promoter sequence using the PlantCARE website (https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/), 2 GCC-like boxes at positions −948 to −957 (GCC box-1 [CCCGCCCCCC]) and −1,171 to −1,180 (GCC box-2 [TGTGCCCCCC]) were identified as conserved and important for JA-responsive activity of the mdm-miR397b promoter (Brown et al. 2003; Fig. 5E). Electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA) were then carried out and confirmed that MdERF61can bind to both of these GCC-like boxes (Fig. 5F).

To study which GCC box is more important for the interaction between MdERF61 and mdm-miR397b, promoter deletion analysis was then performed. A full-length promoter of 2,000 bp and differentially truncated versions of mdm-miR397b promoter (F1–F4) were constructed into the pGreenII 0800-LUC vectors (Fig. 5E). Transient expression assays revealed that the F2 derivative resulted in significantly higher REN/LUC values compared to F1, but similar to F3 and F4 derivatives (Fig. 6A), which indicated that the main functional cis-acting element is the GCC box-1 located within 1,000 bp. In terms of structural basis, molecular docking analysis between MdERF61 and the core sequences of the 2 GCC-boxes was conducted (Fig. 6, B and D). Based on the computational results, residues Arg119, Gly120, Arg122, Gln123, Arg139, and Arg141 of MdERF61 were considered active binding sites for GCC box-1, while Arg119, Arg134, Arg139, and Arg170 were for GCC box-2 (Fig. 6, C and E). The total binding free energy for the GCC box-1-MdERF61 and GCC box-2-MdERF61 was computed to be −47.53 kcal/mol and −33.78 kcal/mol (more negative values indicate stronger binding affinities), respectively (Fig. 6, B and C). Therefore, the greater number of bonds and the higher energy value (Supplementary Table S2) between MdERF61 and GCC box-1 indicate that GCC box-1 plays a more significant role in the interaction compared to GCC box-2.

To further confirm if the GCC box-1 is indeed more important for the affinity between MdERF61 and mdm-miR397b than the second one, we generated F3m1, F3m2, and F3m1 m2 promoter derivatives which carry mutations in GCC box-1, GCC box-2, or both, respectively. Mutating the first GCC box-1 (F3m1) was sufficient to release the inhibitory effect of MdERF61 on the mdm-miR397b promoter (Fig. 6F). These results suggested that the first GCC-like box in the mdm-miR397b promoter is the primary binding site for the interacting MdERF61 and therefore contributes more suppression of mdm-miR397b expression.

Discussion

Plant miRNAs are known to play an important role in regulating host immune responses (Padmanabhan et al. 2009; Weiberg et al. 2014; Feng et al. 2021; Tang et al. 2021). Here we reported evidence that suggests the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module is a key factor during apple root interaction with the fungal pathogen, F. solani. Overexpressing MdLAC7b enhanced plant resistance to F. solani, while the overexpression of mdm-miR397b exhibited the opposite effect and increased plant susceptibility to F. solani (Fig. 2, A and C).

As a soil-borne fungal pathogen, F. solani spores germinate hyphae to invade the root epidermal cells and colonize the xylem vessels. During pathogen infection, it has been proposed that plants rapidly trigger their lignin deposition in the root cell wall to limit the expansion of fungal mycelia and prevent the pathogens from destroying the cell structure (Vance et al. 1980; Boudet et al. 1995). More recently, transcriptomic and proteomic analyses have provided direct and detailed molecular evidence regarding the functions of laccase genes and lignification processes in relation to genotype-specific resistance in several crops (Miedes et al. 2014; Balasubramanian et al. 2016; Yang et al. 2018; Lee et al. 2019). Therefore, root lignification plays a conserved and fundamental role in the plant's ability to set up a physical barrier and fend off pathogen invasion, making lignin a critical target for developing strategies to control plant diseases. Plant laccases are reported to be involved in lignin polymerization, and genes encoding laccases are known to be specifically expressed in lignifying cells (Bao et al. 1993; Berthet et al. 2011). Several studies have revealed that the miR397-LACs module is involved in the regulation of lignin biosynthesis, including ptr-miR397-LACs in Populus trichocarpa, miR397b-LAC4 in Arabidopsis thaliana, Bsy-miR397-LAC in Beckmannia syzigachne, Os-miR397-OsLAC in rice, and miR397a-LAC17/LAC4 in woody Citrus (Lu et al. 2013; Zhang et al. 2013; Wang et al. 2014; Huang et al. 2016; Pan et al. 2017). In the current research, we demonstrated that mdm-miR397b coordinates MdLAC7b, playing an essential role in lignin deposition in apple roots. Specifically, gene families including PAL, 4CL, and HCT have been reported to promote lignin accumulation through their increasing expression levels (Huang et al. 2010; Chen et al. 2017; Jun et al. 2018; Afifi et al. 2022; Yao et al. 2023), which is consistent with our results, suggesting that the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module regulates lignin biosynthesis through the phenylpropanoid pathway. In addition, overexpression of mdm-miR397b causes significantly reduced basal lignin content, whereas overexpression of MdLAC7b dramatically increases basal lignin formation (Fig. 3, A and B). These findings are further supported by phenotypic quantification, which also indicates that these changes in lignin content are associated with altered F. solani resistance and susceptibility in transgenic plants. Therefore, the likely scenario is that overexpressing MdLAC7b confers an intensive and quick lignin deposition in response to F. solani infection. Although the accumulation of lignin could be observed in both OX397b and OXLAC7b plants after F. solani inoculation, the increase was more pronounced in the OXLAC7b line. The OXLAC7b line showed a much greater increase in the concentration of G-monomers than the other genotypes (Fig. 3D). Despite the fact that plants have many LACs, only a small number of them participate in monolignol polymerization into lignin (Wei et al. 2021). For example, in Arabidopsis thaliana, only 4 of 17 laccase genes, including AtLAC4, AtLAC11, AtLAC15, and AtLAC17, were demonstrated to act in lignin biosynthesis. G-monomers are usually found in condensed forms of lignin which are less prone to degradation by microbial enzymes (Yelle et al. 2008). Under pathogenic pressure the lignin structure is comprised of resinol, dibenzodioxocin, and phenylcoumaran linkages. G-units can form the widest range of linkages due to a methoxy group on the phenyl head, whereas S-units are sterically hindered from making some of the more complex linkages found in G-lignin (e.g. phenylcoumaran and dibenzodioxocin linkages) due to the presence of a second methoxy group on the phenyl head (Yelle et al. 2008). Our biochemical analysis indicated that G-lignin is the predominant component of defense-induced lignin in apple roots, which could underlie the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b-mediated basal resistance to F. solani.

The weakened lignin synthesis in the OX397b lines may trigger the JA signaling pathway, which seems to compensate for defects in the physical barrier otherwise provided by lignin. We propose a model in apple plants in which the mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module acts as a biotic trigger to regulate JA levels (Fig. 4, C and D).

A growing body of evidence has proven the critical roles of APETALA2/ethylene response factors (AP2/ERFs) in plant disease resistance (Ma et al. 2024). AP2/ERF transcription factors (TFs) are known to bind to the GCC box in the promoter of pathogen-responsive genes in plants to inhibit or activate the defense pathway (Fujimoto et al. 2000). We showed here that the 2 GCC-like boxes in the mdm-miR397b promoter could be bound by MdERF61 and be transcriptionally inhibited by its binding. The 2 GCC boxes were demonstrated to be functionally equivalent in Arabidopsis. AtERF9 acts as a transcription repressor that binds to the GCC box of the PATHGEN-INDUCIBE PLANT DEFENSIN (PDF1.2) gene, and silencing AtERF9 significantly enhances AtPDF1.2 expression (Maruyama et al. 2013). It has been shown that AP/ERFs play a critical role in regulating plant defense responses by integrating hormonal signaling and manipulating lignin biosynthesis (Ma et al. 2024). For instance, another AP2/ERF protein, ORA59 in Arabidopsis, is reported to elevate immune response to Botrytis cinerea through promoting JA/ET response by directly binding to the 2 GCC boxes on the promoter of PDF1.2 (Zarei et al. 2011). In terms of lignin biosynthesis regulation in cotton, overexpressing GbERF1-like significantly promotes the transcripts of lignin biosynthetic genes, leading to increased lignin contents and enhanced resistance to Verticillium dahliae (Guo et al. 2016). Moreover, in apples, MdERF114 could directly bind to the GCC box in the promoter of MdPRX63 and activate its expression, resulting in lignin accumulation in apple roots and enhancing roots’ immune responses against Fusarium solani (Liu et al. 2023). In our study, we speculate that MdERF61 is involved in the regulation of the crosstalk between the signaling pathways of JA and lignin by direct binding to the 2 GCC boxes in the promoter of mdm-miR397b. A recent publication indicates an ARD-resistant quantitative trait locus was located in the vicinity of MdERF61, providing the possibility of a disease resistance function for MdERF61 from a forward genetics perspective. Therefore, more future work is needed to determine the underlying mechanism by which MdERF61 manipulates the pathway for apple resistance to F. solani.

Previously we identified that the target of mdm-mi397b is MdLAC7b, and the degradome sequencing data indicated differential intensity of transcript degradation of MdLAC7b between resistant and susceptible genotypes during interaction between apple roots and ARD-associated pathogens. To define the function of this module in the pathosystem of apple roots during infection by F. solani we designed a series of experiments to understand the molecular mechanism (Fig. 7). Specifically, mdm-miR397b negatively regulates the expression of MdLAC7b by direct cleavage of its transcripts. The module exerts its effect on apple root resistance mainly through regulating lignin deposition and JA signaling. Moreover, MdERF61 participates in the apple root defense against F. solani by binding to the promoter of mdm-miR397b. Our study identified important upstream regulators and their interaction in response to pathogen infection. Results from the current study clearly demonstrated that MdERF61-mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b is an essential part of the functional molecular and cellular mechanism regulating apple resistance to F. solani.

The working model of the MdERF61-mdm-miR397b-MdLAC7b module in apple roots in response to F. solani infection. Based on our results, the illustrated model attempts to connect this regulating circuit to induced lignin deposition and its potential contribution to the resistance response in apple roots under pathogenic pressure from Fusarium solani. When pathogen presence is detected, a cascade of defense signaling events is initiated. Our data indicated that MdERF61 acts on the promoter region of the miR397b gene. Due to the repressive effect of MdERF61 on the expression of mdm-miR397b, the reduced generation of mdm-miR397 allows more MdLAC7b transcripts to accumulate and leads to enhanced lignin deposition and enhanced cell-wall strength around cells of infected tissues.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growth conditions

A previously reported Agrobacterium-mediated transformation method and leaf tissue of the apple cultivar “Gl-3” were used to produce transgenic apple plants (Yao et al. 2013). Individual plants for inoculation were obtained using micropropagation procedures as described before (Zhou et al. 2018). Plants at 4 to 5 weeks after root induction were used for the infection assay. Root tissues were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent research.

Inoculum preparation and plant inoculation

F. solani strain MG836251.1 was recovered from long-term storage on potato dextrose agar (PDA) plates in darkness for 48 h before inoculation. Distilled water was used to resuspend the spores and to get a final inoculum preparation at a concentration of 105 cells mL−1. Micropropagation of apple plants included shoot proliferation and root induction/elongation, requiring 6 weeks and 4 weeks, respectively. Individual plants, with sufficient root systems, were transferred to pots containing autoclaved artificial soil consisting of construction sand, vermiculite and perlite in 1:1:1 ratio for a 1-week “in-soil acclimation” before pathogen infection assays and root tissue collection. Inoculation was carried out by applying 200 mL of spore suspension to the top of the soil, while the same amount of water was used for mock inoculation.

Total RNA extraction and transcriptome sequencing analysis

Total RNA was isolated as described by Zhou et al. (2018). After RNA isolation, total RNA concentration was then quantified using a Qubit RNA Assay Kit in a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and the RNA quality was visualized on 1% agarose gels. Each tissue type or genotype was represented by 3 biological replicates, and every replicate included the pooled tissues from 3 plants. The RNA integrity number (RIN) was assessed using the Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). For the RNA-seq library preparation, 3 mg of RNA was collected per sample as input material. To generate sequencing libraries, the NEBNext UltraTM RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA) was used by following the manufacturer's instructions. The library preparations were then sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq platform to generate 125-bp/150-bp paired-end reads. The reference genome Malus × domestica GDDH13 Whole Genome v1.1 and annotation files were downloaded from GDR (https://www.rosaceae.org/) (Daccord et al. 2017). Hisat2 v2.0.5 was selected as a mapping tool to align clean reads to the reference genome (Kim et al. 2019). Differential expression analysis was processed using the DESeq2 R package (1.16.1) (Love et al. 2014). To control the false discovery rate, Benjamini and Hochberg's approach was used to adjust the resulting P-value (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified with an adjusted P-value (Padj) < 0.05 found by DESeq2 (Anders and Huber 2010). FPKM (fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads) values greater than 2 in at least one library were used as a standard to eliminate the genes with low expression.

Histochemical staining, root anatomy and lignin content analysis

Root tissues from 6 different experimental combinations, including 3 genotypes (WT-pSAK778, OX397b, and OXLAC7b lines) and 2 treatments (mock and F. solani inoculation for 7 dpi), were collected and sectioned by using Personna #74-0002 razor blades. Three individual apple plants from each genotype under mock and F. solani inoculation were sampled. Ten sections from each plant were obtained, resulting in a total of thirty sections per combination. Among these, ten sections were randomly subjected to histological staining, and the other ten sections were selected for transmission electron microscopy observation. For FY staining, sections were placed in xylene for 20 min twice and soaked in absolute ethanol twice, then moved to 75% ethanol for 5 min before washing in tap water. The sections were stained in 0.01% (w/v) FY solution in darkness at 70°C for 30 min and then transferred to 0.5% aniline blue dyeing solution. Sections were finally imaged by a Nikon Model Eclipse Ci-L microscope. The total lignin content of the roots was determined by the thioglycolate methods as described previously (Han et al. 2013).

Transmission electron microscopy for root secondary cell wall

The sections were fixed into formalin–acetic acid–alcohol (FAA) for 24 h and then dehydrated using graded (70%, 80%, 90%, and 100%) ethanol. After paraffin coating, samples were then cut into 60–80 nm sections and were observed with a Hitachi HT7800 transmission electron microscope.

Yeast one-hybrid assay

For yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) library screening, the 2,000 bp promoter fragments of mdm-miR397b were inserted into the pHIS2 plasmid as a bait recombinant. Total RNA extracted from a mixture of F. solani- and mock-inoculated apple roots was used to construct the cDNA library. Library construction was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions (Clontech, PT3024-1). Subsequently, the bait construct and the library were co-transformed into Y187 yeast-competent cells and screened on SD/-Leu medium containing 5 mm 3AT. The colonies were incubated at 30°C for 3 to 4 days. Positive colonies were then confirmed by PCR. The primers are listed in Supplementary Table S3.

For the point-to-point Y1H verification assay, the promoter fragments of mdm-miR397b were constructed into pAbAi. The pAbAi-mdm-miR397b recombinant plasmids were linearized by BstBI digestion and transformed into Y1H Gold yeast strains to obtain the bait yeast cells. Colony PCR was carried out to identify positive colonies. Individual colonies were then resuspended in 0.9% NaCl solution to reach an OD600 = 0.002. A volume of 100 µL cell suspension was plated onto SD/Leu medium containing gradient concentrations (0, 100, 150, 200, 300, 500, 1,000 ng mL−1) of AbA to determine the minimal AbA inhibitory concentration. The coding sequences of candidate TFs were separately cloned into the pGADT7 vector to generate prey constructs. The prey constructs were then transformed into bait strains, while the empty pGADT7 vector was used as a control. After transformation, cells were first cultured on SD/Leu medium, then serially diluted and transferred to SD/Leu medium with 100 ng mL−1 AbA.

Transient expression system in N. benthamiana

For verifying the degradation capability of mdm-miR397b on MdLAC7b, a dual-luciferase sensor system was used. The reporter was modified from pGreenII 0800-LUC to pGreenII 0800-35::LUC according to the method described by Miao et al. (2022). Fragments of MdLAC7b (including the cleavage site, as well as 200 bp upstream and downstream of the predicted cleavage site) and MdLAC7bmu (an mdm-miR397b resistant form of MdLAC7b) were cloned and fused to the 3′ of the LUC gene, respectively. The pSAK778 vector was used as the overexpression effector vector for mdm-miR397b. The primers are listed in Supplementary Table S3.

For determining transcriptional activity, the promoter of mdm-miR397b was inserted into pGreenII 0800-LUC to obtain a reporter. The coding sequence of MdERF61 was incorporated into pSAK778 to generate an effector. The pSAK778 empty vector was used as a negative control. Plasmids were electroporated into Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101, and single colonies containing reporter or effector were mixed and used for N. benthamiana leaf infiltration. Fourty-eight hours after infiltration, the fluorescence signals were observed using the Tanon 5200. The quantification of the relative LUC/REN ratio was carried out as described before (Fan et al. 2022).

EMSA

The ORF of MdERF61 was cloned into the pGEX-6p-1 vector, and upon expression, glutathione S-transferase (GST)-MdERF61 fusion proteins were obtained. Biotin-labeled probes for MdERF61 and mdm-miR397b were used. Probes were incubated with the nuclear extract at room temperature for 30 min. The entire reaction mixture was run on a non-denaturing 0.5×TBE 6% polyacrylamide gel for 1 h at 60 V at 4°C and then transferred onto Biodyne B nylon membranes (Pall Corporation). Signals were visualized with reagents included in the kit and ChemiDoc XRS (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA). The primers are listed in Supplementary Table S3.

Molecular docking

The 3D structure of MdERF61 with the 2 GCC-like boxes was retrieved based on the model 5WX9 from a protein data bank (https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5wx9). Target–template sequence similarity is calculated from a normalized BLOSUM62 substitution matrix (i.e. the largest and smallest values in the BLOSUM62 are 1 and 0, respectively) (Henikoff and Henikoff 1992). The sequence similarity of the alignment is calculated as the sum of the substitution scores divided by the number of aligned residue pairs. Gaps are not considered. The MOE plugin “protein-DNA docking” was used to study the interaction between MdERF61 and 2 GCC-like boxes (Xiao et al. 2024).

Interpretation functors which are full on pure-injective modules with applications to R R -torsion-free modules over R R -orders. Thanks: The author was supported by PRIN 2017-Mathematical Logic: models, sets, computability.
Lorna Gregory

Abstract
. Let R , S R,S be rings, 𝒳 ⊆ mod \mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{mod} - R R a covariantly finite subcategory, 𝒞 \mathcal{C} the smallest definable subcategory of Mod - R R containing 𝒳 \mathcal{X} and 𝒟 \mathcal{D} a definable subcategory of Mod - S S . We show that if I : 𝒞 → 𝒟 I:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D} is an interpretation functor such that I ​ 𝒳 ⊆ mod I\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{mod} - S S and whose restriction to 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is full then I I is full on pure-injective modules. We apply this theorem to an extension of a functor introduced by Ringel and Roggenkamp which, in particular, allows us to describe the torsion-free part of the Ziegler spectra of tame Bäckström orders. We also introduce the notion of a pseudogeneric module over an order which is intended to play the same role for lattices over orders as generic modules do for finite-dimensional modules over finite-dimensional algebras.

1.
Introduction

Interpretation functors are a uniform additive version of the model theoretic notion of interpretation for (definable subcategories of) module categories. Algebraically, they are
additive functors between definable subcategories of module categories which commute with direct limits and products. They are key tools for studying module categories from the perspective of model theory and for the algebraic study of purity.

When an interpretation functor
I:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is full on pure-injective modules the connection between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
is much closer than for other interpretation functors (more precisely, the connection between
\mathcal{C}
“modulo”
\ker I
and
I\mathcal{C}
). Model theoretically being full on pure-injective modules corresponds,
[
Pre97
, 3.17]
, to the interpretation given by
I
“preserving all induced structure”.

The main theorem of this paper (see
2.4
) is the following. Here,
\text{mod}\text{-}R
denotes the category of finitely presented (right)
R
-modules.

Theorem
.

Let
R,S
be rings,
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{mod}\text{-}R
be covariantly finite,
\mathcal{C}
the smallest definable subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{D}
a definable subcategory of
\textnormal{Mod}\text{-}S
. If

and sends modules in
\text{mod}\text{-}R

\text{mod}\text{-}S

In general, interpretation functors do not send finitely presented modules to finitely presented modules. However, there are naturally occurring situations when they do.
For instance, this is the case for

Moreover, many functors used in the literature to understand one category of finitely presented modules in terms of another extend to interpretation functors between categories of non-finitely presented modules. Thus, in these situations, our theorem can be applied.

A (right)

\psi(M)\subseteq\varphi(M)

The following theorem, due to Prest in full generality, and Krause in a special case which includes the cases in which we are interested in this paper, gives a completely algebraic characterisation of interpretation functors.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
Pre11

[

An additive functor
I:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is an interpretation functor if and only if

Note that we will technically only use the forward direction of the previous theorem which follows easily from the definition of an interpretation functor.

Interpretation functors preserve pure-embeddings and pure-injectivity. Using the definition of an interpretation functor, solution sets of pp formulae in

-torsion-free
\Lambda

[\psi,\varphi]_{N}
has length
\leq n
is a closed subset of
\textnormal{Zg}_{S}
.
Therefore the set
\mathcal{C}_{l}
of
N\in\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
with pseudoendolength
\leq l
is a closed subset (note that this set also contains the divisible points). Since
\left(x=x/\pi|x\right)
is compact, it follows that
\left(x=x/\pi|x\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{l}
is compact. The indecomposable
\Lambda
-lattices are isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
. Thus if there are infinitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable
\Lambda
-lattices of pseudoendolength
l
then
\mathcal{C}_{l}\cap\left(x=x/\pi|x\right)
contains infinitely many isolated points and hence must contain a non-isolated point
N
.
∎

Let
e_{1}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),e_{2}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in D
. For any
N\in\textnormal{Mod}\text{-}D
,
\textnormal{pp}_{D}^{1}(N)
is finite length if and only if
[x=0,e_{1}|x]_{N}
and
[x=0,e_{2}|x]_{N}
are finite length because

[e_{1}|x,x=x]_{N}=[xe_{2}=0,x=x]_{N}\cong[x=0,e_{2}|x]_{N}.

Proposition 3.15
.

If
N
is pseudogeneric then
\mathbb{F}N
is a generic module. If any of the conditions
(1),(2)
or
(3)
from
3.13
hold then if
N\in\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}
is
R
-reduced, pure-injective and
\mathbb{F}N
is generic then
N
is pseudogeneric.

Proof.

Suppose
N
is pseudogeneric. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\pi^{n}\in I
. Since
N
is pseudogeneric the interval
[\pi|x,x=x]_{N}
is finite length. Therefore, the interval
[\pi^{l}|x,x=x]_{N}
is finite length for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\pi^{n}\in I
,
\pi^{n}|x\leq I|x
. Hence
[I|x,x=x]_{N}
is finite length. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\pi^{m}\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
. Then
[\pi^{m}I|x,x=x]_{N}
and hence
[\pi^{m}I|x,\pi^{m}\Gamma]_{N}
are finite length. Since
\mathbb{F}
is full on pure-injectives, by
[
Pre09
, 3.17]
,
[I|x,x=x]_{N}
is isomorphic to
[x=0,e_{1}|x]_{\mathbb{F}N}
and
[\pi^{m}I|x,\pi^{m}\Gamma]_{N}\cong[x=0,e_{2}|x]_{\mathbb{F}N}
. Thus
\textnormal{pp}_{D}(\mathbb{F}N)
is finite length. By
3.4
,
\mathbb{F}N
is indecomposable. Every finitely presented module in
\mathcal{D}
is of the form
\mathbb{F}M
for some
M\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
. Since
R
is complete,
\Lambda
-lattices are pure-injective. So, if
\mathbb{F}N
were finitely presented then
N\cong M
for some
\Lambda
-lattice
M
. Thus we have proved the first statement.

Suppose that one of
(1),(2)
or
(3)
from
3.13
holds. Since
\mathbb{F}N
is generic, it is closed in
\textnormal{Zg}(\mathcal{D})
. So, by
3.6
,
N
is closed in
\left(\nicefrac{{x=x}}{{\pi|x}}\right)
. Since
\mathbb{F}N
is generic,
N
is not a
\Lambda
-lattice. Thus
N
is not isolated in
\left(\nicefrac{{x=x}}{{\pi|x}}\right)
. So, by
3.13
,
N
is pseudogeneric.
∎

We now specialise to the case of Bäckström orders i.e. an order
\Lambda
where there exists a hereditary over order
\Gamma\neq\Lambda
such that
\textnormal{rad}(\Lambda)=\textnormal{rad}(\Gamma)
. In this situation we set
I=\textnormal{rad}(\Lambda)
. Now
\Lambda/I
and
\Gamma/I
are both semi-simple
R/\pi R
-algebras. Hence
D
is a hereditary
R/\pi R
-algebra.

Remark 3.16
.

For
\Lambda
a Bäckström order, the only indecomposable pure-injective
D
-modules which are not in
\mathcal{D}
are the simple
D
-modules.

Proof.

The statement follows for indecomposable finite-dimensional
D
-modules because
\Lambda/I
and
\Gamma/I
are semi-simple. Since
D
has only finitely many simple modules and all finitely presented indecomposable
D
-modules are isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
,
\langle\mathbb{F}\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}\rangle
is the definable subcategory of
\textnormal{Mod}\text{-}D
containing all indecomposable non-simple pure-injective
D
-modules.
∎

Following Ringel and Roggenkamp, we say that a Bäckström order
\Lambda
is
tame
(respectively
wild
) if
D
is tame (respectively wild). It is shown in
[
GLT20
, 5.1]
that when an arbitrary order
\Lambda
over a Dedekind domain is of finite lattice type, i.e. there are only finitely many indecomposable
\Lambda
-lattices up to isomorphism, then
\textrm{m-dim}\,\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\text{Tf}_{\Lambda})=1
.

Proposition 3.17
.

If
\Lambda
is a wild Bäckström order then
\textnormal{m-dim}\,\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{\Lambda}\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}
is undefined.
If
\Lambda
is a tame Bäckström order of infinite lattice-type then
\textnormal{m-dim}\,\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{\Lambda}\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}
is
3
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is a wild Bäckström order then the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}_{D}^{1}
is undefined. This implies that
\textnormal{pp}_{D}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
also has undefined m-dimension because if
\sigma\geq\tau
is such that the Ziegler basic open set
(\sigma/\tau)
contains no infinite dimensional modules then
[\varphi,\psi]
is finite length. Thus, by
2.13
, the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}_{\Lambda}^{1}\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}
is undefined.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a tame Bäckström order of infinite lattice-type. Then,
[
Gei85
, 4.3]
, the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}_{D}^{1}
is
2
and the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}_{\Lambda}^{1}\ker\mathbb{F}
is
0
. Thus, by
2.12
,
2\leq\textnormal{m-dim}\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{\Lambda}\text{Tf}_{\Lambda}\leq 3
. Thus,
[
Pre09
, 5.3.60]
, the Cantor-Bendixson (CB) rank of
\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
is equal to the m-dimension of
\textnormal{pp}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\text{Tf}_{\Lambda})
. Let
N\in\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
be a point of CB rank
2
. The finite-dimensional
D
-modules are isolated in
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
. So
N
also has CB rank
2
in the subspace of
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
with the simple modules removed. Let
M\in\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
be such that
\mathbb{F}M=N
. By
3.6
,
M
has CB rank
2
in
\left(x=x/\pi|x\right)
and hence also in
\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
. Let
S
be a simple
Q\Lambda
-module which is a direct summand of
QM
. Then, by
[
GLT20
, 2.7]
,
S
is in the closure of
N
. Thus
S
has Cantor-Bendixson rank
3
as required.
∎

The previous lemma plus
3.15
gives that when
\Lambda
is a tame Bäckström order
\mathbb{F}
gives a bijective correspondence between the generic modules of
D
and the pseudogeneric modules of
\Lambda
. When
D
is tame hereditary, it only has finitely many generic modules.

Corollary 3.18
.

If
\Lambda
is a tame Bäckström order of infinite representation type then
\Lambda
has finitely many pseudogeneric
R
-torsion-free modules.

3.4.
Tame Bäckström orders

Throughout this section we assume that
\Lambda
is a tame Bäckström order of infinite lattice type. By definition,
D
is a tame hereditary algebra. Note that, even if we assume that
\Lambda
is indecomposable,
D
is not necessarily indecomposable (see
[
RR79
, Example 4]
). Let
D=\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}D_{i}
be such that
D_{n+1}
is of finite representation type and that for
1\leq i\leq n
,
D_{i}
is of infinite representation type and indecomposable as a ring.

We briefly recall some information about the category of finite-dimensional
D
-modules (see
[
DR74
]
and
[
Rin79
]
) and about the Ziegler spectrum of
D
(see
[
Pre98
]
and
[
Rin98
]
). Let
\tau
denote the Auslander-Reiten translate which, since
D
is hereditary, is an endofunctor on
\text{mod}\text{-}D
. We call the finite-dimensional
D
-modules of the form
\tau^{-n}P
where
P
is a projective module and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

preprojective
; finite-dimensional modules of the form
\tau^{n}I
where
I
is an injective module and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

preinjective
; and finite-dimensional modules with no preprojective or preinjective direct summands
regular
. Note that an indecomposable finite-dimensional
D
-module is both preprojective and preinjective if and only if it is a
D_{n+1}
-module.

The (full) subcategory of regular modules is an abelian category and its inclusion into
\text{mod}\text{-}D
is exact. A
D
-module is
quasi-simple
if it is simple in the category of regular modules. We call a quasi-simple
type
i
if it is a
D_{i}
-module. For each natural number
n
and quasi-simple module
E
, there exists a unique regular module
E[n]
which as an object in the category of regular modules is uniserial, has socle
E
and length
n
. Dually, there exists a unique regular module
[n]E
which as an object in the category of regular modules is uniserial, has top
E
and length
n
. All indecomposable regular modules are of the form
E[n]
(and dually of the form
[n]E
) for some quasi-simple
E
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each quasi-simple
E
, the direct limit along a chain of (irreducible) embeddings

E[1]\hookrightarrow E[2]\hookrightarrow E[3]\hookrightarrow\ldots

is denoted
E[\infty]
. Dually, for each
E
, the inverse limit along a chain of (irreducible) epimorphisms

[1]E\leftarrow[2]E\leftarrow[3]E\leftarrow\ldots

is denoted
\widehat{E}
. The modules
E[\infty]
and
\widehat{E}
are both pure-injective and indecomposable. There are finitely many remaining infinite dimensional indecomposable pure-injective
D
-modules. Namely, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, there is a unique indecomposable generic
D_{i}
-module.

By
3.16
, all infinite dimensional indecomposable pure-injective
D
-modules are in the image of
\mathbb{F}
. Thus
\mathbb{F}
gives a bijective correspondence between the
R
-reduced modules in
\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
which are not
\Lambda
-lattices and the infinite dimensional points in
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
G_{i}
denote the unique pseudogeneric module in
\textnormal{Mod}\text{-}\Lambda
such that
\mathbb{F}G_{i}
is the unique generic
D_{i}
-module
3.15
.

The topology on
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
for
D
an indecomposable tame hereditary algebra is described in
[
Pre98
]
and
[
Rin98
]
. We use the formulation from
[
Rin98
]
.

Theorem 3.19
.

A subset
C\subseteq\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
is closed if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

If
\mathbb{F}S
is quasi-simple and if there exist infinitely many indecomposable
X\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
with
\textnormal{Hom}_{D}(\mathbb{F}S,\mathbb{F}X)\neq 0
then
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}S[\infty])\in C
.

(2)

If
\mathbb{F}S
is quasi-simple and if there exist infinitely many indecomposable
X\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
with
\textnormal{Hom}_{D}(\mathbb{F}X,\mathbb{F}S)\neq 0
then
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbb{F}S})\in C
.

(3)

If there are infinitely many indecomposable
X\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
of type
i
in
C
or if there exists a least one module of the form
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}S[\infty])
or
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbb{F}S})
where
\mathbb{F}S
is a quasi-simple of type
i
in
C
, then
G_{i}\in C
.

(4)

For each simple
Q\Lambda
-module
M
, if there exists
N\in C
such that
M
is a direct summand of
NQ
then
M\in C
.

Proof.

If
C
is closed then that
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
are true of
C
follows from the description of the Ziegler spectra of tame hereditary algebras given in
[
Rin98
]
. Property (4) holds by
[
GLT20
, 2.7]
.

Now suppose that
C
is a subset of
\textnormal{Zg}_{\Lambda}^{tf}
and
(1)
-
(4)
are true of
C
. Then, since
(1)
-
(3)
are true of
C
, the set
V
of
\mathbb{F}N
such that
N\in C
and
N
is not
R
-divisible is a closed subset of
\textnormal{Zg}_{D}
by
3.6
. It now follows from
(4)
that
C
is

\{N\in\left(\nicefrac{{x=x}}{{\pi|x}}\right)\ |\ \mathbb{F}N\in V\}\cup\{S\in\textnormal{Zg}_{Q\Lambda}\ |\ \text{ there exists }N\in\left(\nicefrac{{x=x}}{{\pi|x}}\right)\text{ with }S|NQ\text{ and }\mathbb{F}N\in V\}\cup W

where

is some subset of

. So by

,

is closed.
∎

We now give a description of the modules
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{F}S[\infty])
as
\Lambda
-modules. We are not able to give a description of the modules
\mathbb{F}^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbb{F}S})
but one might guess that this module is isomorphic to the inverse limit along the irreducible epimorphisms
\mathbb{F}S[i+1]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}S[i]
. However, it is unclear whether this module is indecomposable.

Roggenkamp, in
[
Rog83
]
, showed how to obtain the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
from the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\text{mod}\text{-}D
using

. It is a consequence of
[

that each of the stable tubes in the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
D
lifts to a stable tube in the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
. If
S\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
is such that
\mathbb{F}S
is quasi-simple then we write
S[n]
(respectively
[n]S
) for the
L\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
with
\mathbb{F}L=(\mathbb{F}S)[n]
(respectively
\mathbb{F}L=[n](\mathbb{F}S)
).

For
M\in\textnormal{Mod}\text{-}\Lambda
, we write
M^{\star}
for the inverse limit along the surjective maps
M/M\pi^{n+1}\rightarrow M/M\pi^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that if
M
is
R
-reduced then so is
M^{\star}
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
S\in\text{Latt}_{\Lambda}
be such that
\mathbb{F}S
is quasi-simple and let
S[\infty]
denote the direct limit along a ray of irreducible maps
S[n]\rightarrow S[n+1]
. Then
S[\infty]^{\star}
is an
R
-torsionfree
R
-reduced indecomposable pure-injective
\Lambda
-module and
\mathbb{F}(S[\infty]^{\star})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{F}S)[\infty]
.

Proof.

It is explained in
[
Gre23
]
, see
[
Gre23
, 4.9]
and the discussion above the question on page 596 of that article, that
S[\infty]^{\star}
is the pure-injective hull of
E[\infty]
. Since
\mathbb{F}
commutes with direct limits and the irreducible maps
S[n]\rightarrow S[n+1]
are sent to irreducible maps
(\mathbb{F}S)[n]\rightarrow(\mathbb{F}S)[n+1]

[
Rog83
, 1.12]
,
\mathbb{F}S[\infty]\cong(\mathbb{F}S)[\infty]
. Since
\mathbb{F}
is full on pure-injectives and hence,
[
Pre97
, 3.15 & 3.16]
preserves pure-injective hulls,
\mathbb{F}S[\infty]^{\star}\cong(\mathbb{F}S)[\infty]
.
Finally, if
S[\infty]^{\star}=N\oplus M
then either
\mathbb{F}N=0
or
\mathbb{F}M=0
. Hence, by
3.4
, either
N
or
M
is
R
-divisible. This contradicts that fact that
S[\infty]^{\star}
is
R
-reduced.∎

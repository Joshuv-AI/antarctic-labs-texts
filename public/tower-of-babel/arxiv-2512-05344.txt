Blow-up suppression of the Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system via Taylor-Couette flow
Shikun Cui, Lili Wang, Wendong Wang

Abstract
. Motivated by the use of Taylor-Couette flow in extracorporeal circulation devices [K o ¨ \ddot{\rm o} rfer et al., 2003, 26(4): 331-338], where it leads to an accumulation of platelets and plasma proteins in the vortex center and therefore to a decreased probability of contact between platelets and material surfaces and its protein adsorption per square unit is significantly lower than laminar flow. Increased platelet adhesion or protein adsorption on the device surface can induce platelet aggregation or thrombosis, which is analogous to the “blow-up phenomenon” in mathematical modeling. Here we mathematically analyze this stability mechanism and demonstrate that sufficiently strong flow can prevent blow-up from occurring. In details, we investigate the two-dimensional Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system in an annular domain around a Taylor-Couette flow U ⁡ ( r , θ ) = A ⁡ ( r + 1 r ) ​ ( − sin ⁡ θ , cos ⁡ θ ) T U(r,\theta)=A\big(r+\frac{1}{r}\big)(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{T} with ( r , θ ) ∈ [ 1 , R ] × 𝕊 1 (r,\theta)\in[1,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{1} , and prove that the solutions are globally bounded without any smallness restriction on the initial cell mass or velocity when A A is large.

1.
Introduction

Guillermo et al. in
[
14
]
observed experimentally that Taylor-Couette flow employed in the Vortex Flow Plasmapheretic Reactor (VFPR) demonstrates multiple functional benefits that enhance both performance and safety in extracorporeal heparin management. It also minimizes blood cell damage by effectively separating cellular components from immobilized enzyme beads. Most importantly, it enables safe regional heparinization by efficiently removing heparin in the extracorporeal circuit, maintaining a therapeutic anticoagulant level externally while reducing systemic exposure in the patient. In addition, Körfer et al. in
[
24
]
also found that Taylor-Couette flow in extracorporeal circulation devices can lead to an
accumulation of platelets and plasma proteins in the vortex center and therefore to a decreased
probability of contact between platelets and material surfaces. Especially, at shear rates greater than or equal to
550s^{-1}
, laminar flow resulted in a significantly higher platelet drop and PF4 release than Taylor vortex flow.
Also protein adsorption per square unit was significantly higher for laminar flow.

As a classic fluid dynamic phenomenon, Taylor-Couette flow describes the steady-state motion of a viscous fluid confined between two coaxial rotating cylinders, first systematically studied by Taylor in the 1920s
[
33
]
. Despite its conceptually simple geometry, the stability and perturbation of this flow have long presented challenging research questions, leading to extensive experimental, theoretical, and numerical investigations
[
7
,
13
,
25
,
30
]
. It remains an active field in fluid mechanics, with many aspects still not fully understood. At the biological level, beyond its use in heparin management, Taylor-Couette flow has proven relevant in several key biomedical applications, including enhancing red blood cell oxygenation
[
27
]
, improving plasma filtration efficiency
[
3
]
, and facilitating enzymatic heparin neutralization
[
1
]
.

Inspired by the above important applications of Taylor-Couette flow,
consider the following two-dimensional Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) system coupled with the
Navier-Stokes (NS) equations in a two-dimensional annular region:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n+v\cdot\nabla n=\Delta n-\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c),\\
\Delta c+n-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\Delta v+n\nabla\Phi,\quad\nabla\cdot v=0,\\
(n,v)\big|_{t=0}=(n_{\rm in},v_{\rm in}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an annular region.
Here,
n
is the cell density,
c
denotes the concentration of chemoattractant, and

denotes the velocity of fluid. In addition,

When the fluid velocity and the coupling are absent (i.e.,

[

and further developed by Keller and Segel
[
22
]
. The Patlak-Keller-Segel system is commonly used to describe the chemotaxis of microorganisms or cells in response to chemical signals. This fundamental process underlies critical biological behaviors such as nutrient foraging, signal relay, and avoidance of detrimental environments
[
18
,
19
]
.
Up to now, there are many developments for the PKS system on blow-up or the critical mass threshold, and we review some progress briefly.
In the one-dimensional space, all solutions to the PKS system are globally well-posed
[
28
]
.
In two-dimensional space, the PKS system, in both its parabolic-elliptic and parabolic-parabolic forms, exhibits a
8\pi
critical mass.
Define the initial mass
M:=\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{1}}
, and
if

For the parabolic-elliptic case,
Wei
[
34
]
proved that the solution is globally well-posed if and only if
M\leq 8\pi
(see also
[
5
]
). While the cell mass

, the solutions of the PKS system will blow up in finite time, and we refer to Collot-Ghoul-Masmoudi-Nguyen
[
8
]
, and Schweyer
[
31
]
and the references therein.

It is a more realistic scenario that chemotactic processes take place in a moving fluid.
As said in

A natural question is whether the presence of fluid flow

When vorticity

In the polar coordinate, the functions

Next, we focus on
the blow-up suppression for the PKS-NS system via Taylor-Couette flow in an annulus.
Introduce a perturbation around the two-dimensional TC flow

, with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2})n+(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}})\partial_{\theta}n+\frac{1}{Ar}(\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{\theta}n-\partial_{\theta}\varphi\partial_{r}n)\\

(1.5)

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the initial data
0\leq n_{\rm in}\in L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
u_{\rm in}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
. There exists a positive
A_{1}

depending on
\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}
and
\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
, such
that if
A\geq A_{1}
, then the solutions of (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) are globally bounded and satisfy the follow stability estimates:

(i)

Uniform bounded-ness estimates:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})},R),

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{D})},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})},R).

(ii)

Enhanced dissipation estimates:

\displaystyle\Big\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|\partial_{\theta}|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(n-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}nd\theta\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(R)\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})},

\displaystyle\Big\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|\partial_{\theta}|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(w-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}wd\theta\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(R)\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D})}.

Remark 1.1
.

The Taylor-Couette flow has been successfully implemented in biomedical devices such as the VFPR, where its unique vortex structure significantly enhances hemocompatibility. It reduces platelet activation and protein adsorption by promoting the accumulation of cellular components in the vortex center, thereby lowering the risk of thrombogenesis and improving the safety of extracorporeal circulation systems
[
24
,
14
]
.
Increased platelet adhesion or protein adsorption on the device surface can induce platelet aggregation or thrombosis, which poses a huge threat to human life. The above theorem shows that sufficiently strong flow can prevent the aggregation or blow-up from occurring. As shown in
[
24
]
at shear rates
G\geq 550s^{-1}
, laminar flow resulted in a significantly higher platelet drop and PF4 release than Taylor vortex flow. Here
G
is similar as
A
. In fact, let
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
denote the angular velocity of the
inner or outer cylinder, where
w_{1}r_{1}=v_{\theta}|_{r_{1}}=A(r+\frac{1}{r})|_{r_{1}}
and
w_{2}r_{2}=v_{\theta}|_{r_{2}}=A(r+\frac{1}{r})|_{r_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle G=\frac{2(r_{1}^{2}w_{1}+r_{2}^{2}w_{2})}{r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2}},

which implies
G=2A\frac{3+R^{2}}{R^{2}-1}
when
r_{1}=1,r_{2}=R.
It is interesting to estimate the value of
A
in mathematics, which will be investigated in our future work.

Compared with previous results on planar flows (e.g., Couette or Poiseuille flows) usually set in Cartesian coordinates, to our best knowledge, the above result gives the first rigorous proof of global regularity for the Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system driven by a non-planar shear flow, specifically the Taylor-Couette flow in an annular domain. Moreover, our result requires no smallness assumption on the initial cell mass or on the initial velocity field; the global bounded-ness is achieved solely by the strength of the Taylor-Couette flow (i.e., a sufficiently large

3.
A priori estimates and zero mode estimates

3.1.
Elliptic estimates for
c

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
|k|\geq 1
. Let

\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{r^{2}}\Big)c_{k}+n_{k}-c_{k}=0,\quad c_{k}|_{r=1,R}=0.

(3.1)

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+k\big\|\frac{c_{k}}{r}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+k^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+k\|r\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(R)\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

and

\|c_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
3.1
) by
-c_{k}
, the energy estimate shows that

\|\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big(k^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\big\|\frac{c_{k}}{r}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle n_{k},c_{k}\rangle\leq\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives that

4\|\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4k^{2}\big\|\frac{c_{k}}{r}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.2)

Due to (
3.1
), there holds

\displaystyle\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k^{4}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2k^{2}\langle r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k},c_{k}\rangle=\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k}-k^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)c_{k}-r^{2}n_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|r^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{2}n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Using integration by parts,
-2k^{2}\langle r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k},c_{k}\rangle
can be controlled as

\displaystyle 2k^{2}\langle r^{2}\partial_{r}c_{k},\partial_{r}c_{k}\rangle+2k^{2}\langle 2r\partial_{r}c_{k},c_{k}\rangle=2k^{2}\|r\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2k^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This along with (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\|r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k^{4}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2k^{2}\|r\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(k^{2}\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{2}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{2}n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq CR^{4}\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By applying (
3.2
) and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one deduces

\displaystyle\|c_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}c_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(R)\|n_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

By combining (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\widehat{c}_{0}
and
\widehat{n}_{0}
be the zero mode of
c
and
n
, respectively, satisfying

-\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Big)\widehat{c}_{0}+\widehat{c}_{0}=\widehat{n}_{0},\quad\widehat{c}_{0}|_{r=1,R}=0.

(3.5)

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\widehat{c}_{0},\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0},\partial_{r}^{2}\widehat{c}_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|(1,\partial_{r})\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(R)\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

The basic energy estimates yield

\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

which implies that

\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.6)

By using (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining this with (
3.6
) and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one obtains

\|\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(R)\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(R)\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(R)\left(\|\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}^{2}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(R)\left(\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C(R)\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

\widehat{w}_{0}

\varphi

w

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|r\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|r^{\frac{3}{2}}\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Due to integration by parts, there holds

This implies that

\|\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|r\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.9)

Combining it with Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we get

\|\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\|\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(R)\|\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

\displaystyle\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|r\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|r\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2R^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{r}\widehat{\varphi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2R^{\frac{1}{2}}\|r\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Substituting it into (
3.10

which implies
(\ref{varphi_0 infty})_{1}

3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\|c\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}c_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R)\Big(\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}n_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\Big)\leq C(R)\left(D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right).

Moreover, it follows from H
\ddot{\rm o}
lder’s and Nash inequalities that

\displaystyle\Big\|n^{p}\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|n^{p}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Substituting the above estimates into (
5.1
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2(2p-1)}{Ap}\Big\|\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)n^{p}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(R)}{A}\Big(1+pD_{1}+p\mathcal{Q}_{1}\Big)\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{Cp^{2}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2p-1}{Ap}\Big\|\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)n^{p}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(R)}{A}\Big(1+pD_{1}+p\mathcal{Q}_{1}\Big)\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{Cp^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{5(2p-1)}{4Ap}\Big\|\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)n^{p}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2A}\Big\|\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)n^{p}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(R)p^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big[\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+D_{1}+1\Big].

Using the Nash inequality again

\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(R)\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)n^{p}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

we infer from (
5.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}}{2AC(R)\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}+\frac{C(R)p^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big[\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+D_{1}+1\Big]

(5.3)

Using (
2.7
), Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.5
and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one obtains

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\Big)c\Big\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}\leq\|(1,\partial_{r})\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}+\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(1,\partial_{r},\frac{|k|}{r}\Big)c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(1,\partial_{r})\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|(1,\partial_{r})\partial_{r}\widehat{c}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{|k|}{r}\Big)c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(\partial_{r},\frac{|k|}{r}\Big)c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}\Big(\partial_{r}^{2},\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r},\frac{|k|^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R)\big(\|\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{3}}|k|^{\frac{2}{3}}R^{-2}t}n_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\big)\leq C(R)\left(D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right).

This along with (
5.3
) gives that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}}{2AC(R)\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}+\frac{C(R)p^{4}}{A}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big).

(5.4)

Claim that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\max\Bigl\{4[C(R)]^{2}p^{4}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n^{p}\|_{L^{1}}^{2},2\|n_{\rm in}^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr\}.

(5.5)

Otherwise, there exists
t=t_{1}>0
such that

\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\max\Bigl\{4[C(R)]^{2}p^{4}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{1}}^{2},2\|n_{\rm in}^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr\}

(5.6)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|n^{p}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)|_{t=t_{1}}\geq 0.

(5.7)

Due to (
5.4
) and (
5.6
), there holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|n^{p}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)|_{t=t_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\{\frac{\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2AC(R)\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{1}}^{2}}-\frac{C(R)p^{4}}{A}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|n^{p}(t_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\frac{C(R)p^{4}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)}{A}<0,

which contradicts with (
5.7
). Therefore, (
5.5
) holds.

Next, the Moser-Alikakos iteration is used to determine
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
. Recall
p=2^{j}
with
j\geq 1
, and rewrite (
5.5
) into

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{j+1}}drd\theta

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{Hp^{4}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{j}}drd\theta\right)^{2},2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n_{\rm in}|^{2^{j+1}}drd\theta\right\},

where
H=4[C(R)]^{2}\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have

\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq D_{1}.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi\|\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\widehat{n}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{n}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\sum_{k\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|n_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq 2\pi D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}.

Combining it with interpolation inequality, for
0<\theta<1
and
j\geq 1
, we arrive at

\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2^{j}}}\leq\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\theta}\leq\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}}+\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\pi D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This yields that

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n_{\rm in}|^{2^{j+1}}drd\theta\leq 2\left(2\pi D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{2^{j+1}}\leq K^{2^{j+1}},

where
K=2\left(2\pi D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)
.
Now, we rewrite (
5.8
) as

\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{j+1}}drd\theta\leq\max\left\{H16^{j}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{j}}drd\theta\right)^{2},K^{2^{j+1}}\right\}.

For
j=k
, we get

\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{k+1}}drd\theta\leq H^{a_{k}}16^{b_{k}}K^{2^{k+1}},

where
a_{k}=1+2a_{k-1}
and
b_{k}=k+2b_{k-1}
.

Generally, one can obtain the following formulas

a_{k}=2^{k}-1,\quad{\rm and}\quad b_{k}=2^{k+1}-k-2.

Thus, we arrive

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{R}|n(t)|^{2^{k+1}}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}}\leq H^{\frac{2^{k}-1}{2^{k+1}}}16^{\frac{2^{k+1}-k-2}{2^{k+1}}}K.

Letting
k\to\infty
, there holds

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(R)\Big(D_{1}^{4}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{4}+1\Big)\left(2\pi D_{1}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}+\|n_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)=:\mathcal{Q}_{2}.

(5.9)

The proof is complete.

∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
,
when
A\geq A_{1}
, there holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}},R),

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}},R).

Proof.

Rewriting the velocity
u
into

\displaystyle u(t,r,\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{u}_{k}(t,r){\rm e}^{-ik\theta}=\widehat{u}_{0}(t,r)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\widehat{u}_{k}(t,r){\rm e}^{-ik\theta}.

Then, by 1D Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\|\widehat{u}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\|\widehat{w}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}.

Using (
3.12
) and (
4.23
),
when
A\geq A_{1}
,
we infer from the above inequality that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq 0}\|\widehat{w}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}},R).

By (
4.23
) and (
5.9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|n_{\rm in}\|_{H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}},\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}},R).

The proof is complete.
∎

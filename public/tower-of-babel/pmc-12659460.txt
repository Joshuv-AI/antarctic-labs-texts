Effectiveness and strategies of social prescribing in Korea using a machine learning topic modeling
Authors: Hocheol Lee, Yejun Kim, Min Ah Chung, Eun Woo Nam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSocial prescribing, a non-medical approach linking individuals to community-based services to improve health and well-being, has expanded globally, including Korea. Despite their increasing adoption, there is limited systematic evidence evaluating the effectiveness, implementation strategies, and policy implications of social prescriptions in the Korean context.MethodsThis study conducted a scoping review of the literature related to social prescriptions in Korea. English- and Korean-language articles were retrieved from five databases (Google Scholar, Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, and KCI) without time restrictions. The studies were screened and selected based on predefined criteria. Machine learning-based topic modeling (LDA, NMF, and BERTopic) was applied to extract the latent thematic structures from the included studies. The evaluation metrics included coherence score, perplexity, topic diversity, and topic balance-guided model selection. The NMF model was selected for final analysis because of its superior performance.ResultsSix key thematic categories were identified from 16 studies: (1) Mental Health, (2) evaluation, (3) program, (4) Social Issues, (5) COVID-19, and (6) international comparisons. Mental health and social isolation have emerged as major concerns, particularly in aging rural populations. Programs focusing on gardening, music, and digital platforms have been reported to be effective in improving psychological wellbeing and community engagement. The analysis also highlights the necessity of localized models tailored to Korea’s demographic and policy landscape.ConclusionsThis study emphasized the need for a comprehensive policy framework for social prescriptions in South Korea. The integration of digital technology for remote delivery, adaptation to rural health gaps, and benchmarking from established international models is recommended. This study demonstrates the utility of AI-driven text mining as an innovative approach for evidence synthesis and policy planning for public health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13711-8.

Introduction

Social prescribing, which originated in the UK, is a non-medical approach defined as a method to support individuals’ health and well-being through community-based activities with guidance from primary care providers and link workers [1, 2]. This approach enables patients to connect with community activities such as volunteering, arts activities, gardening, sports, and peer support groups. Social prescribing, developed within the UK’s National Health Service (NHS), aims to reduce the demand for healthcare services, improve mental and physical health outcomes, and promote social connections among participants [3].

Social prescriptions have various benefits at the national, social, and individual levels, such as reducing the financial burden on national health insurance, reducing the burden on healthcare personnel, strengthening primary healthcare, shortening wait times for primary healthcare, and enhancing social networks [4, 5]. In particular, existing studies have suggested that social prescribing improves mental health, such as reducing anxiety and depression and alleviating loneliness, as well as promoting physical health, increasing social participation, and improving the overall quality of life [6, 7]. These effects have led to the adoption and expansion of social prescribing as a public health policy in countries around the world, centered by 31 countries which are members of the Social Prescribing Network, including England, the United States, China, Australia, Germany, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea, Japan, Indonesia, and Taiwan [8–10]. Social prescribing has grown rapidly in Asia, particularly in South Korea, Singapore, Taiwan, and Japan, where various social prescribing models tailored to regional characteristics have been introduced. Asian countries that have adopted social prescribing effectively utilize it to address issues such as rapid aging, rising mental health problems, and social isolation [11].

In South Korea, the rural areas in Wonju City, Gangwon Province, first implemented the social prescription pilot project in 2019. Based on positive initial outcomes, it has gradually expanded nationwide. In 2024, with support from the Ministry of Culture, Sports, and Tourism, the Regional Culture and Development Agency led a pilot social prescription program in nine regions aimed at alleviating loneliness and improving mental health through cultural activities [12]. It has been reported that these social prescription programs in South Korea were effective in promoting mental health, strengthening social support, promoting physical activity, and mitigating feelings of depression. Given the effectiveness of these social prescription programs in South Korea, there is a growing emphasis on the policy importance and practical necessity of social prescriptions in the country [13].

In South Korea, the importance of social prescribing has been continuously highlighted due to issues such as increasing mental health problems, including the highest suicide rate and the rise of isolated youth; issues caused by rapid aging; the shortage of healthcare facilities in rural areas due to urban concentration; and the rising financial burden on the national health insurance system [14, 15]. However, limited systematic research based on literature has been conducted on the specific effects, strategic approaches, and policy implications of social prescription in South Korea, thereby lacking robust evidence to support its implementation.

To bridge this knowledge gap, we systematically reviewed empirical literature on social prescribing in South Korea. In particular, this study analyzed literature focusing on topics such as health effects, institutional issues, and policy definitions, and investigated the underlying thematic structure of existing literature using machine learning-based topic modeling techniques, by which we sought to provide evidence-based insights for the establishment of practical strategies and policy development for social prescribing in South Korea.

Method

Our scoping review was conducted in accordance with the methodological framework proposed by Arksey and O’Malley [16], and its reporting adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The detailed methodological process of our review is presented in the following sections.

Identification of the research question

This scoping review was guided by the following research questions, developed to explore the scope, characteristics, and methodological trends of social prescribing research in South Korea, as well as to identify strategic implications through machine learning–based topic modeling:

What key themes and research domains have been identified in studies on social prescribing conducted in South Korea? What are the major characteristics, strategies, and effectiveness outcomes addressed in the identified literature on social prescribing? How have social prescribing systems and policies in South Korea been conceptualized and implemented within the existing research frameworks?

Identification of relevant studies

This study systematically identified relevant research following the methodological framework proposed by Arksey and O’Malley [16]. To enhance the validity and reproducibility of the search strategy, search terms were developed in consultation with an information specialist experienced in conducting scoping reviews. Both controlled vocabulary (MeSH) and free-text terms were used to capture a broad range of literature related to social prescribing in South Korea. The main search terms included “social prescribing,” “community-based intervention,” “primary care,” “mental health,” “health promotion,” and “Korea.” Boolean operators (AND/OR) were applied to combine these terms appropriately (Additional file 5).

The literature search was conducted across major international and domestic databases, including PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Scopus, and RISS. The search period spanned from January 2010 to December 2024. A preliminary search was performed in June 2024, followed by the final search on July 15, 2024, and an updated search on January 10, 2025, to include the most recent publications. Detailed search strategies for each database are provided in Supplementary Material (Additional file 1). In addition, hand searches were conducted using reference lists from key review papers and policy documents, and reports related to social prescribing were also reviewed from the Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW) and the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA).

All search results were imported into EndNote 20 for bibliographic management and duplicate removal. Two independent reviewers screened the titles and abstracts based on the inclusion criteria, and any disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer when necessary. Grey literature, dissertations, preprints, and conference abstracts were excluded from the review. The overall study selection process followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) flow diagram for scoping reviews, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Study selection

This study included studies that met the following selection criteria: First, we targeted research that presented a clear definition or concept of social prescribing and that was conducted on a specific topic. In addition, we focused on literature directly related to social prescribing systems and policies in South Korea or conducted in South Korea. With regard to the content of the studies, we included empirical studies, systematic reviews, or scoping reviews that specifically discussed the effectiveness, strategies, or health outcomes of social prescribing. The studies were limited to those published in English or Korean.

On the other hand, conference abstracts, pre-print articles, gray literature, and theses/dissertations, as well as unobtainable literature, were also excluded from the analysis. To secure transparency and objectivity in the selection of literature, two professors with doctoral degrees in public health and one researcher with a doctoral degree in public health who specializes in social prescribing, as well as three link workers of social prescribing in South Korea, conducted independent evaluations based on the selection criteria. Any differences in opinion were discussed and a final consensus was reached.

Study selection

The screening process was followed step-by-step, with reference to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses flow diagram for scoping reviews [17] (Fig. 1). First, we entered the bibliographic information of all the papers extracted through the search into the reference management program EndNote and used it to remove duplicate papers. Two or more researchers independently reviewed the titles and abstracts of the papers to conduct the first round of screening based on the selection criteria. In cases where there were differences in opinion among the researchers, a consensus was reached through discussion.

After the initial screening, the full-text versions of the included papers were obtained, followed by a second screening. The second screening was conducted independently by two or more researchers, with evaluations focusing on relevance to the research topic and appropriateness of the research design. Final decisions on paper selection were made through a consensus within the research team, resulting in the selection of 12 papers.

Data extraction

We used Visual Studio Code and Anconda as our work environment and Python version 3.11.11. We extracted text data for topic modeling from the selected papers (n = 16) using the MyMuPDF library. Next, Gemini and ChatGPT were used to perform academic translations (for Korean papers only) and linebreak adjustments. NLTK and GenSim were utilized to preprocess the organized text into a format suitable for the model. First, all text was converted to lowercase, punctuation marks and numbers were removed, and then tokenized into individual word units. For stop words, we added research paper-related terms like “etc,” “et al,” “figure,” “table,” “introduction,” and “abstract,” as well as general words to the English stop word dictionary in nltk, creating an expanded stop word list (stop_words_nltk) customized for our analysis and applied it. Finally, lemmatization is performed to convert each token into its base form (lemma). We generated a dictionary and a bag-of-words for use in the Gensim library (Latent Dirichlet Allocation(LDA) model) from the preprocessed documents and created a TF-IDF matrix.

Collating, summarizing and dissemination of results

This study utilizes text data extracted from selected papers to perform topic modeling using unsupervised learning. To this end, three representative machine learning techniques were applied: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF), and BERTopic. For each model, we trained multiple models while varying the main hyperparameters (the number of topics ‘k for LDA/NMF and ‘min_topic_size’ for BERTopic), and then selected the optimal hyperparameters and final model based on quantitative performance evaluation metrics.

The model evaluation metrics used to evaluate the model’s performance were the Coherence Score, Log Perplexity, Topic Diversity, Topic Size Balance, Number of Topics, and Computation Time. The coherence Score evaluates the consistency of topics, with higher values indicating better interpretability. The log Perplexity evaluates whether the model explains the data accurately. A lower Log Perplexity value indicated better prediction performance of the model. Topic Diversity evaluates the independence of each topic, with a higher value indicating less word overlap between topics and more distinct topics. The Topic Size Balance (standard deviation) represents the standard deviation of the number of documents assigned to each topic; lower values indicate a more balanced topic. The number of topics refers to the actual number of topics automatically identified, whereas the Computation Time measures the time elapsed for the training and evaluation of each model.

Subsequently, a research team consisting of experts in public health and social prescription conducted qualitative discussions to evaluate the appropriateness of the derived topics, thereby clearly defining and categorizing the core concepts and themes for each topic. Finally, the UMAP library was used to visually represent the distribution and trends of the themes identified by utilizing the distances between themes, which were then divided into six categories and visualized as bar graphs for each theme.

Model comparison (LDA, NMF, BERTopic)

In this study, three models were evaluated using the Coherence Score, Log Perplexity, Topic Diversity, Topic Size Balance, Number of Topics, and Computation Time (Table 2; Fig. 2). We found that the LDA model (k = 4) had a Coherence Score of 0.4819, which was lower than that of the NMF model, but similar to that of BERTopic. In particular, its data explanatory power (Log Perplexity) was as high as -6.805, and it was the most efficient in terms of model training time at 10.06 s. The NMF model was selected as the optimal model considering the overall goals and efficiency of this study. A comparison of the three models is detailed in Additional file 1.

Discussion

This study systematically analyzed the evidential basis of social prescribing studies conducted in South Korea in comparison with social prescribing systems worldwide and identified thematic distribution patterns using topic modeling analysis. In this study, machine-learning-based topic modeling techniques, including LDA (visualization represented in Additional file 4), NMF, and BERTopic, were compared and evaluated, among which the NMF model was ultimately selected. The NMF model exhibited clear distinctions between topics, high interpretability (Coherence Score: 0.6367), and efficient learning time, thereby proving to be the most suitable model for accurately reflecting the diversity of the social prescribing literature. In previous studies, NMF models also showed higher Coherence Scores than other models in topic modeling, which is consistent with the findings of this study and confirms the validity of selecting NMF through the topic modeling method [33, 34].

The topic modeling findings showed that social prescribing research in South Korea can be clearly divided into six major categories: mental health, evaluation, program, social issues, COVID-19, and countries/communities. These categories reflected national health issues, research capabilities, and policy concerns.

First, the mental health category showed that loneliness and depression management have become important health issues in the Korean society, with digital approaches proven to be effective. In the evaluation category, the effectiveness of social prescribing programs was assessed and the importance of developing a standardized framework for such programs was highlighted. In the program category, customized cultural and artistic activities for older adults in rural areas appear to be essential for social prescription. In terms of the social issue category, the need for policy utilization of social prescribing was raised to address social isolation and single-person households, which are characteristic problems in Korean society. South Korea faces problems of aging and urban concentration, prompting a continuous demand for institutional measures for elderly people living in rural areas [35]. In addition, elderly people in rural areas have significant difficulties accessing primary healthcare because of a lack of healthcare facilities and personnel [36]. Because the social prescribing system enhances mental health by utilizing community resources and programs, and even physical health, it is expected that this system will be effective in rural areas of South Korea in the future, where the population is aging. In addition, within Korea’s community care policy aimed at addressing population aging, elderly care remains a critical issue. It is expected that collaboration with community care initiatives will play a key role in preparing for and responding to the challenges of an aging society.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, social prescribing programs have been flexibly implemented in South Korea in both in-person and remote settings, suggesting that a sustainable social prescribing model could be developed for similar crises in the future. Many rural areas in South Korea are geographically isolated, making physical and economic access difficult [37]. This limited access restricts the utilization of healthcare facilities and the formation of social networks in rural areas. Strategies to improve accessibility can be approached through three key dimensions: financial support, enhancement of knowledge and information capacity, and development of human resource support systems. In particular, due to Korea’s rapidly aging population, the digital and knowledge divide between urban and rural areas has been widening, creating structural barriers to the accessibility of care and healthcare services in rural regions. This study confirmed that social prescribing has positive effects on reducing loneliness, improving physical health, and strengthening social networks among older adults. Therefore, to expand these benefits to rural community care services, it is essential to establish complementary strategies aimed at narrowing the regional knowledge and information gap and promoting equitable access to care.

Thus, it is necessary to introduce social prescribing programs that utilize both in-person and remote methods in rural areas of South Korea. This study identified the potential effectiveness of remote social prescription programmes.

The final topic identified in this study is international comparisons, which emerged as a category in the literature review. This indicates that the best practices implemented in other countries with social prescription systems have been utilized as strategic reference materials for the effective establishment of a Korean social prescription system. In particular, the programs in the United Kingdom and Canada were frequently cited during the early stages of introducing the social prescribing system in South Korea, with literature focusing on keywords such as “rural areas,” “elderly,” “loneliness,” “depression,” “community resources,” and “human resources.” Currently, there is a significant lack of evidence supporting the expansion and institutionalization of social prescription programs in South Korea. Therefore, it is necessary to undertake a phased approach, benchmarking the best practices from other countries to anticipate the effectiveness of institutionalization.

This study has some limitations. First, there were limitations to the literature review because the literature was searched in Korean and English. This may have resulted in a partial bias toward these languages. Second, SLR-based studies should be conducted with at least 10 years of research. However, pilot programs for social prescription were first implemented in South Korea in 2019, with only approximately five years of literature available at the time of this study. This period was insufficient for sufficient literature to be published and for research findings to be presented in academic papers. In the future, as social prescribing programs expand in South Korea, more literature will be published based on these programs, which would enable the expansion of the database, making it possible to conduct accurate topic modeling analysis using AI. While AI represents a highly promising methodological approach in research, it also carries inherent limitations in terms of interpretability. Therefore, future studies should aim to develop explainable AI (XAI) models to enhance transparency and provide stronger evidence for reproducibility among researchers and readers.

Conclusion

Social prescription programs in South Korea have been piloted in rural areas since 2019 and will expand throughout the country as of 2025, continuously expanding through local government-specific projects, civil society projects, and university research. These findings suggest that social prescribing has a high potential for sustainability in South Korea, and that frameworks for evidence-based policy development are being established. These findings also suggest that social prescribing should be expanded beyond simply promoting community health, to become a policy tool for realizing value by resolving social issues and fostering community integration. Additionally, machine learning-based methodologies enable a systematic and comprehensive analysis of the literature; therefore, it is expected that the accumulation of social prescribing research and literature in South Korea would serve as a strong evidence base for making social prescribing policy decisions and establishing implementation strategies.

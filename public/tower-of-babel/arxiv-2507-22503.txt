Abstract
. Several recent problems in the representation theory of finite groups require determining whether certain characters of almost simple groups belong to the principal block. Since the values of these characters are not yet known, we employ alternative group-theoretical techniques to address the “going down” case. This approach enables us to reduce the block version of well-known results by the third and fourth authors to a question about almost simple groups. Moreover, this suggests a Galois analogue of the height-zero-equal-degree conjecture of Malle and Navarro, which we formulate. However, the “going up” case of irreducible extensions of principal block characters remains unresolved.

1.
Introduction

The resolution of several recent conjectures concerning the characters of principal blocks of finite groups depends on a better understanding of when the irreducible restriction of a character in the principal block remains in the principal block, and conversely, particularly in the case of almost simple groups (see, for instance,
[
LWXZ
]
,
[
NRS
]
, or
[
NST
]
).
Somewhat surprisingly, the character values of almost simple groups are not sufficiently well understood, which means that determining whether a character lies in the principal block often requires alternative techniques.

Let
p
be a fixed prime, let
G
be a finite group, and denote by
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
the irreducible
complex characters of
G
in the
p
-principal block.
For example, a useful going down theorem due to T. Okuyama states that if
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
, the restriction
\chi_{H}
is irreducible for a subgroup
H
of
G
, and the induced block

is defined (where
b
is the block of
\chi_{H}
), then
\chi_{H}
lies in the principal block of
H
(see Theorem 6.6 of
[
N1
]
). However, in practice, it is not always the case that induced blocks from subgroups are defined.

In the first result of this paper, we contribute to this problem in the following way.

THEOREM A
.

Let
G
be an almost simple group with socle
S
. Let
S\leq H\leq G
.
If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
is in the principal block of
G
and
\chi_{S}
is irreducible,
then
\chi_{H}
is in the principal block of
H
.

Since, as we have noted, the character values of almost simple groups are not fully known, Theorem A relies on the following
deep group-theoretical result, which is not as well-known as it should be. Recall that when extending principal block characters from a normal subgroup
N
of
G
to
G
, the normal subgroup
V=N{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
, where

Q\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(N)
, plays a key role (by the Alperin–Dade theory on principal blocks). Theorem B does use the classification of finite simple groups when
p
is odd, but not when
p=2
.

THEOREM B
.

Suppose that
G
is almost simple with socle
S
. Let
Q\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(S)
and
V=S{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
.
Then
V/S
has a normal
p
-complement.

Proof.

If
p
is odd, then we have that
V/S
is a
p^{\prime}
-group by the main result of
[
GGLN
]
.
If
p=2
, then this follows from Theorem 1 of
[
Gl
]
.
∎

Theorem A is then obtained from the following elementary result.

THEOREM C
.

Suppose that
N\unlhd G
. Let

Why are we now interested in Theorem A? In
[

]

-complement. For

we can prove this now for

we reduce the problem to a question
of almost simple groups. The main difficulty that remains in that case is the absence of a convenient “going up” version of Theorem A.

THEOREM D
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
\chi(1)
is even for all non-linear rational valued
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))

if and only if

has a normal 2-complement.

The

odd case depends on whether simple groups satisfy the

for all primes for alternating groups and for groups of Lie type in their defining characteristic (see Propositions

THEOREM E

Let

of

Contrary to the case of Theorem C of

Could it be that Theorems D and E admit a version for general blocks? Some of the global/local conjectures and theorems on the representation theory of finite groups admit a Galois version. (See
[
N2
]
for Alperin-McKay,
and
[
MN2
]
and
[
MMRS
]
for Brauer’s height zero on principal blocks, for instance.) These provide interesting
questions and sometimes unforeseen consequences, at the same time that they lead us
to study the action of the absolute Galois group on key representation theory objects.
In
[

]
, a global characterization of the Broué–Puig nilpotent blocks
[

]
(which are locally defined objects) was proposed:
a Brauer block is nilpotent if and only if all of its height zero characters have the same degree.
To this day, this conjecture is wide open, even for solvable groups (where its proof depends on an unsolved regular orbit problem;

[

]
for groups of odd order). Inspired by Theorems D and E, the final purpose in this note is to propose a finer Galois version of this conjecture. If

is an arbitrary

-block, it is not even true that there exists some irreducible character in

{\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/p})

We believe that the key characters in the general case are the so-called

-rational characters
, as defined in
[

If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)

contains the values of

\chi

-rational if

does not divide

that

-block

contains at least one height zero almost
p
-rational character.
Extensive computations lead us to propose the following.

CONJECTURE F
.

A
p
-block
B
is nilpotent if and only if all of its height zero almost
p
-rational characters in
B
have the same degree.

Recall that the principal
p
-block is nilpotent if and only if
G
has a normal

p
-complement. Hence Conjecture F for the principal block says that a group has a normal
p
-complement if and only if all the almost
p
-rational characters in
B_{0}(G)
are linear. This is easily implied by Theorem E.

Proof of Theorems A and C

If
G

the principal

[

]

[

We start with the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
,
U\leq G
has
p^{\prime}
-index, and
\mu=\chi_{U}
is irreducible.
Then
\mu\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(U))

Proof.

Let

be a defect class for
\mu

. (See the definition before Theorem 4.4 in
[
N1
]
.)
Let
P
be a Sylow

{\bf C}_{U}(x)
, so that

is a defect group of the block of
\mu

In particular,

\left(\mu(x)|x^{U}|\over\mu(1)\right)^{*}\neq 0\,.

By multiplying by the
p^{\prime}
-number
|G:U|
, we obtain

0\neq\left(\mu(x)|G:{\bf C}_{U}(x)|\over\mu(1)\right)^{*}=\left(\chi(x)|G:{\bf C}_{G}(x)|\over\chi(1)\right)^{*}|{\bf C}_{G}(x):{\bf C}_{U}(x)|^{*}=|G:{\bf C}_{U}(x)|^{*}\,.

We have that
P
is contained in a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\bf C}_{G}(x)
. By Problem 4.5 of
[
N1
]
, we only need to prove that

\left(\chi(y)|y^{U}|\over\chi(1)\right)^{*}=|y^{U}|^{*}

for every
p
-regular
y\in U
with
[y,P]=1
.
We know that

\left(\chi(y)|y^{G}|\over\chi(1)\right)^{*}=|y^{G}|^{*}\,.

Notice that

{\chi(y)\over\chi(1)}=\left(\chi(y)|y^{G}|\over\chi(1)\right)/|y^{G}|\in S\,,

where
S=\{\alpha/\beta|\alpha\in{\bf R},\beta\in{\bf R}-M\}
, and

{\mathbb{C}}
.
We deduce that

\left(\chi(y)\over\chi(1)\right)^{*}=1

and the rest easily follows.
∎

The following is Theorem C.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
N\unlhd G
. Let
Q\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(N)
and
V=N{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
. Assume that
V/N
has a normal
p
-complement.
Suppose that
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))

is such that
\chi_{N}
is irreducible. If
N\leq U\leq G
, then
\chi_{U}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(U))
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
|G:N|
and then
|G:U|
.
Suppose that
U<W<G
. Then we have that

\chi_{W}=\tau\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(W))
by induction. We have that
{\bf C}_{W}(Q)N/N\leq V/N
has a normal
p
-complement.
By induction in

, we then have that
\tau_{U}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(U))
.
Hence, we may assume that

is maximal in

.
Suppose that

. Notice that
\chi_{V}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(V))
, because

. By induction,
we have that
\chi_{V\cap U}
is in the principal block. Now,

, and thus any irreducible
character of
U
above
\chi_{V\cap U}
is in the principal block applying Lemma 3.1 of
[

So we may assume that

. Suppose that

, then

-index and we are done

.
Otherwise,

∎

Now the proof of Theorem A easily follows from Theorems B and C.

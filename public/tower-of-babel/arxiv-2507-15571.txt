Gravitational waveforms from inspiraling compact binaries in quadratic gravity and their parameterized post-Einstein characterization
Matheus F. S. Alves, L.G. Medeiros, Davi C. Rodrigues

Abstract
We investigate gravitational waveforms from the inspiral phase of compact binary systems within the framework of quadratic gravity and map their deviations from general relativity into the parameterized post-Einstein (PPE) formalism to constrain the theory’s parameters. Quadratic gravity generically includes a massive spin-2 ghost, which leads to ill-defined energy and angular momentum fluxes. Following earlier proposals, we remove these unphysical features by imposing a constraint on the massive spin-2 mode, restricting it to propagate only the same polarizations of general relativity. Within the quadrupole approximation, we derive the radiative degrees of freedom, including massless and massive tensor modes, as well as a massive scalar field. Using the stationary phase approximation, we compute the Fourier-domain waveform of the massless tensor modes and extract the phase corrections. For small deviations from general relativity, we show that both the scalar and massive tensor modes can be consistently embedded into the PPE framework, extending previous results that considered only scalar fields. We derive updated constraints on the parameters of quadratic gravity, finding bounds improved by three orders of magnitude compared to existing limits. Finally, we present forecasts for the sensitivity of the Einstein Telescope to these deviations.

The detection of gravitational waves (GWs)

;

;
Yunes et al. 2024
.
Although GR is the most robust theory of gravity so far, it nonetheless
faces significant challenges
from a theoretical standpoint. Theoretically, GR cannot be renormalized
using conventional methods,
it contains singularities

, and it cannot self-consistently
describe the very early inflationary universe without the inclusion of extra
degrees of freedom

;
Martin et al. 2014
;
Cuzinatto et al. 2019a
;
Rodrigues-da Silva and Medeiros 2023
. Moreover, the need to invoke dark matter, dark energy, and, more recently, the Hubble tension
Di Valentino et al. 2021
has prompted questions as to whether these phenomena signal a possible breakdown in the validity of GR on cosmological scales. These open issues, even within the classical regime, have motivated ongoing efforts to explore extensions and modifications of GR (e.g., for reviews,
Amendola and Tsujikawa 2015
;
Capozziello and De Laurentis 2017
;
Shankaranarayanan and Johnson 2022
; and some recent developments can be find in Refs.

In this work, we consider higher-derivative gravity, focusing in particular on quadratic gravity
Stelle 1977
;
Donoghue and Menezes 2022
;
Shapiro 2023
. Although this framework enables the renormalization of quantum fields in curved spacetime, it introduces a ghost degree of freedom. Several strategies have been proposed to address this problem (see
Shapiro 2023
;
Basile et al. 2024
for a comprehensive review). A complete solution remains elusive, but several approaches continue to emerge (e.g.,
Camanho et al. 2016
;
Edelstein et al. 2021
). In parallel, quadratic gravity and its extensions (e.g.,
de O. Salles and Shapiro 2018
;
Rachwal et al. 2021
;
Asorey et al. 2025

It is well known that quadratic gravity, at linear order, can be in general decomposed into two tensorial spin-2 modes, massive and massless, and a scalar spin-0 mode
Stelle 1977
;
Stelle 1978
(see also
Bueno et al. 2017
;
Alves et al. 2025a
for more recent approaches). Gravitational wave generation from binary black holes in non-relativistic circular orbits was analyzed by some of us in Ref.
Alves et al. 2023
, where constraints on model parameters were derived from observational data, assuming negligible longitudinal tensorial polarizations and ignoring angular momentum dissipation. In Ref.
Alves et al. 2025b
we addressed these limitations by investigating the angular momentum loss process and the influence of longitudinal tensorial modes. The main result of this study is the demonstration that, within the quadrupole approximation, only the longitudinal modes of the massive tensorial term exhibit pathologies, such as negative power emission. By focusing exclusively on the transverse-traceless modes, the study established a self-consistent model that ensures positive energy and angular momentum for the emitted gravitational waves.

Tests of GR with gravitational wave data are usually divided into consistency tests and parameterized tests
Abbott et al. 2021
. Consistency tests, such as residual analyses and inspiral-merger-ringdown checks, are designed to verify whether the observed signals are consistent with the GR predictions. On the other hand, the parameterized tests introduce a deviation into the waveform model to search for possible non-GR effects. A most popular framework for such tests is the parameterized post-Einsteinian (PPE)
Yunes and Pretorius 2009
;
Sampson et al. 2013
;
Chatziioannou et al. 2012
;
Cornish et al. 2011
, which deforms the post-Newtonian (PN) expansion of the waveform phase. The original PPE framework introduces two parameters: the PPE index, characterizing the PN order at which the deformation takes place, and the PPE coefficient, characterizing the magnitude of the deformation
Yunes et al. 2016
;
Yunes and Pretorius 2009
;
Tahura and Yagi 2018
;
Perkins et al. 2021
.

In this paper, we compute PPE waveforms in quadratic gravity and obtain constraints on the theory’s parameters. We do this by computing the GW waveforms produced during the inspiral of a compact binary in non-relativistic quasicircular orbits. More precisely, we compute the waveform expressions for the relevant polarizations in the time domain as obtained in
Alves et al. 2023
. Considering only the leading PN corrections to the massless tensorial modes, we employ the original PPE framework
Yunes and Pretorius 2009
. We calculate the waveforms in the frequency domain using Fourier transforms and the stationary phase method (SPA).
We show that, by constraining the massive mode to have only
\times
and
+
polarizations, the massless tensorial polarizations can be successfully embedded into the PPE framework. These results extend the results obtained in
Liu et al. 2020
, where it was possible to map the scalar field to PPE only for sufficiently small mass. In this work, we employed the PPE parameter constraints on Quadratic Gravity to significantly tighten the bounds previously obtained by some of us in
Alves et al. 2023
, improving them by three orders of magnitude.

The paper is organized as follows: In Sec.
II

Furthermore, we study the energy loss induced by the emission of gravitational waves and obtain the balance equation. With this, we get the waveforms for the massless tensorial mode in the frequency domain using the Fourier transform and the stationary phase method. In Sec.
IV

V

IV
Parametrized post-Einsteinian parameters and observational
constraints

The PPE framework is a waveform model proposed by Yunes and Pretorius

Yunes and Pretorius 2009
that describes the gravitational waves emitted by a
binary system on a quasicircular orbit in metric theories of gravity. In the
original PPE framework, all deviations from GR in gravitational waveforms can
be expressed through a set of four post-Einstein parameters
\left(\alpha\text{, }\beta\text{, }a\text{, }b\right)
whose waveform would be
given by

h\left(f\right)=h_{GR}\left(f\right)\left[1+\alpha\left(\frac{GM_{c}\pi f}{c^{3}}\right)^{\frac{a}{3}}\right]e^{i\beta\left(\frac{GM_{c}\pi f}{c^{3}}\right)^{\frac{b}{3}}},

(44)

where
h_{GR}\left(f\right)
is the GR waveform. This framework adjusts any given GR waveform model by introducing two classes of theoretical parameters: exponent parameters (a, b), which define the specific type of deviation from GR, and amplitude parameters (
\alpha
,
\beta
), which regulate the magnitude of the GR deformation. Moreover, it is worth
noting that the original PPE framework includes only the two tensor polarizations
h_{+}
and
h_{\times}
. However,
extended versions of this framework were developed in Refs.

Bonilla et al. 2023
;
Loutrel et al. 2023
;
Maggio et al. 2023
;
Mehta et al. 2023
;
Mezzasoma and Yunes 2022
.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we studied the gravitational waveforms emitted during the inspiral phase of compact binary systems in non-relativistic quasicircular orbits. We mapped these results into the PPE framework, which allowed us to extract new constraints in the theory parameters. We obtained three distinct polarizations: the combinations
\{h_{+},\Psi_{+}\}
and
\{h_{\times},\Psi_{\times}\}
from the tensor fields, and
\Phi
from the massive scalar field. Our analysis is restricted to transverse tensor modes due to the unphysical nature of the longitudinal components (see Ref.
Alves et al. 2025b
for further discussion). As a result, the theory comprises only three degrees of freedom. The massive fields exhibited two regimes: an oscillatory regime responsible for gravitational wave emission and a damped regime characterized by exponential decay. Additionally, we derived the energy balance equation associated with energy loss due to gravitational wave emission.

Further, using the SPA method, we calculated the Fourier transform of the massless tensor polarizations. We also derived analytical expressions for the phases of these polarizations
\Sigma_{+}
and
\Sigma_{\times}
. An important point we demonstrate in this work is that, when scalar and massive tensorial modes are treated as small perturbations to GR, the massless tensorial polarizations can be mapped onto the parameterized post-Einsteinian (PPE) framework, yielding the PPE parameters
(a,b,\alpha,\beta)
. In Sec.
IV
we explicitly show that the applicability of the PPE framework depends on the parameters of the theory. Additionally, our results extend the findings of Ref.
Liu et al. 2020
, where the mapping of massive Brans-Dicke theory to the PPE framework was restricted to scalar fields with sufficiently small masses.

After mapping the quadratic gravitation theory to the PPE framework, we obtained observational constraints on the parameters of the theory based on the GW151226 event. For scalar and tensorial massive fields, we derived bounds of
\gamma\lesssim 1.44\times 10^{10}m^{2}
and
\alpha\lesssim 2.63\times 10^{10}m^{2}
for a frequency of
175\mathrm{Hz}
, respectively. At lower frequencies, far from the merger moment, such as
f=35\mathrm{Hz}
, the constraints become
\gamma\lesssim 4.90\times 10^{11}m^{2}
and
\alpha\lesssim 7.90\times 10^{11}m^{2}
, which are more stringent than previous astrophysical-scale studies. Additionally, in Sec.
IV
we derived projected constraints for these parameters based on potential observations by future ground-based GW detectors. The best projected constraints are
\gamma\lesssim 7.73\times 10^{9}m^{2}
and
\alpha\lesssim 2.21\times 10^{10}m^{2}
for a frequency of
175\mathrm{Hz}
.

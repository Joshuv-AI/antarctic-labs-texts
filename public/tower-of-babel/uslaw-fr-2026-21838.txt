SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Background

    We are announcing the availability of a draft guidance for industry 
entitled ``U.S.-Origin Statements on Seafood.'' We are issuing the 
draft guidance consistent with our good guidance practices regulation 
(21 CFR 10.115). The draft guidance, when finalized, will represent the 
current thinking of FDA on this topic. It does not establish any rights 
for any person and is not binding on FDA or the public. You can use an 
alternate approach if it satisfies the requirements of the applicable 
statutes and regulations.
    In general, FDA-regulated food products may bear truthful and not 
misleading statements, including U.S.-origin statements, in their 
labeling. This draft guidance, when finalized, will provide examples of 
U.S.-origin statements in seafood product labeling that we would 
consider truthful and not misleading.
    As we develop final guidance on this topic, FDA will consider 
comments on costs or cost savings the guidance may generate, as 
relevant for Executive Order 14192.

U.S.-Origin Statements on Seafood: Draft Guidance for Industry

OMB Control Number 0910-0381--Revision

    This draft guidance, if finalized, would provide examples of U.S.-
origin statements in seafood product labeling that FDA would consider 
truthful and not misleading. These products include both single-
ingredient seafood and the seafood components of multi-ingredient 
foods. Respondents that choose to use U.S.-origin statements on their 
seafood products should be able to substantiate the statements to show 
that they are truthful and not misleading.
    Description of respondents: Respondents to this information 
collection are manufacturers, packers, and distributors of seafood. 
Respondents are from the private sector (for-profit businesses).
    We estimate the burden of this collection of information as 
follows:

                                                      Table 1--Estimated One-Time Relabeling Burden
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                                       Number of
                    Activity                         Number of      disclosures per     Total annual    Average burden    Total hours     Total capital
                                                    respondents        respondent       disclosures     per disclosure                      costs \1\
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Relabel for U.S.-origin statement...............           1,150                  5            5,750                1            5,750       $7,664,750
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\1\ There are no operating and maintenance costs associated with this collection of information.

    The burden estimates in Table 1 are based on FDA's institutional 
experience and knowledge of the seafood industry. We assume there are 
two scenarios where seafood products would be relabeled. The first 
scenario would involve a product currently labeled with a U.S.-origin 
statement, where the respondent chooses to remove such statement. The 
second scenario would involve a product not currently labeled with a 
U.S.-origin statement, where the respondent chooses to add such 
statement. Both instances involve relabeling. Because these statements 
are voluntary, we do not know how many respondents would make labeling 
changes. For the purpose of this analysis, we estimate that there are 
about 2,300 domestic manufacturers, packers, and distributors of 
seafood. Further, we estimate that approximately half of them (1,150 
firms) would change their labels to either add or remove a U.S.-origin 
statement. We estimate that each firm would change 5 labels with each 
label taking about an hour to revise for an annual third-party 
disclosure burden of 5,750 hours (1,150 firms x 5 labels x 1 hour). 
Based on our experience with other relabeling policies, we estimate 
relabeling each product that respondents choose to relabel would cost 
$1,333 assuming a three-year adoption period. That calculates to an 
annual capital cost of $7,664,750 ($1,333 x 5,750 labels). This is the 
cost of designing revised labels and incorporating them into the 
manufacturing process. We believe that this would be a one-time capital 
cost.
    This estimate also does not include maintaining records to 
substantiate a statement as respondents already maintain records as a 
usual and customary business practice.

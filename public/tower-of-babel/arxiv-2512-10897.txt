Observability inequality for the von Neumann equation in crystals
T. Borsoni, V. Ehrlacher

Abstract
. We provide a quantitative observability inequality for the von Neumann equation on R d \mathbb{R}^{d} in the crystal setting, uniform in small ~ \hbar . Following the method of [ 7 , F. Golse and T. Paul, 2022] proving this result in the non-crystal setting, the method relies on a stability argument between the quantum (von Neumann) and classical (Liouville) dynamics and uses an optimal transport-like pseudo-distance between quantum and classical densities. Our contribution yields in the adaptation of all the required tools to the periodic setting, relying on the Bloch decomposition, notions of periodic Schrödinger coherent state, periodic Töplitz operator and periodic Husimi densities.

In this work, we rather focus on the von Neumann equation, a generalization of the Schrödinger equation to mixed states – trace-class nonnegative self-adjoint operators over

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\text{trace}\left(\mathds{1}_{\omega}\,R(t)\,\mathds{1}_{\omega}\right)\,\text{d}t\geqslant C\,\text{trace}\left(R(0)\right),

1.
Key concepts and main result

1.1.
Crystal setting and periodic trace

Let us consider a full-rank Bravais lattice
\mathscr{L}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the sense that there exists a linearly independent family
\bm{a}_{1},\dots,\bm{a}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\mathscr{L}=\left\{\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\,\bm{a}_{j},\;n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}.

We consider a unit cell
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
\{\Gamma+\ell\}_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}
forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which interior is the one of the Wigner-Seitz cell associated to the lattice, defined by

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\;\forall\ell\in\mathscr{L},\;|x|\leqslant|x-\ell|\}.

Notice however the slight difference in the boundaries of
\Gamma
and the Wigner–Seitz cell. For instance, in the case
\mathscr{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the latter is
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
while one could take
\Gamma=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
. As
\{\Gamma+\ell\}_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}
is a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can define, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(3)

P_{\Gamma}z

as the unique element of
\Gamma
such that
z-P_{\Gamma}z\in\mathscr{L}
.
In particular, one has

(4)

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\partial\Gamma+\mathscr{L}),\qquad\;\;\;\;\;P_{\Gamma}z\;

\displaystyle=z-\underset{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}{\mathrm{argmin}}|z-\ell|,

(5)

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\partial\Gamma+\mathscr{L}),\qquad P_{\Gamma}(-z)

\displaystyle=-P_{\Gamma}z.\phantom{\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\partial\Gamma+\mathscr{L}).\qquad}

We denote

(6)

\gamma_{-}=\inf_{z\in\partial\Gamma}|z|,\qquad\gamma_{+}=\sup_{z\in\partial\Gamma}|z|.

Remark 1
.

In the case
\mathscr{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\Gamma=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
, we have
\gamma_{-}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\gamma_{+}=\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}
.

We similarly choose a unit cell
\Gamma^{*}
for the reciprocal lattice, the latter being defined as

\mathscr{L}^{*}:=\left\{\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{d}\bm{n}_{j}\,\bm{b}_{j},\;\bm{n}:=(\bm{n}_{j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\},

where the vectors
\bm{b}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
are such that

\bm{b}_{j_{1}}\cdot\bm{a}_{j_{2}}=2\pi\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{j_{1}=j_{2}}\quad\forall 1\leqslant j_{1},j_{2}\leqslant d.

In the following, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{L}}
the set of self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C})
that commute for all
\ell\in\mathscr{L}
with the translation operator
\tau_{\ell}
which is defined as follows: for any
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\phantom{.y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\qquad\tau_{\ell}\psi(y):=\psi(y-\ell),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Operators belonging to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{L}}
admit a “Bloch decomposition”, detailed in Appendix
A.1
. We denote by
{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
the subset of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{L}}
of nonnegative operators. We may then define a notion of trace suited for “periodic” (in the sense that they commute with translations) operators of
{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
as follows.

Definition 1

(
Periodic trace
)
.

Let
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
and
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
be the family of operators on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
given by the Bloch transform of
R
(see Appendix
A.1
). We define the
periodic trace
of
R
by

(7)

\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(R):=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}(R_{k})\,\mathrm{d}k,

and let

(8)

{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}:=\{R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}},\;\;\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(R)=1\}.

In order to lighten the notations in the following,
\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is denoted by
\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}
by
\mathrm{Tr}
, but one should keep in mind that these notions of trace are associated with operators acting on different spaces.

For operators in
{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, the periodic trace is a more meaningful object to consider than the standard trace. Indeed, the latter may be viewed as counting the total number of electrons in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is infinite in our setting, whereas the periodic trace measures the number of electrons per unit cell (see for instance the definition in
[
5
]
), which in our case is one.

The classical counterpart to
{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
corresponds to the set of
\mathscr{L}
-periodized-in-
x
probability densities on
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined by

(9)

\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}:=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\;\text{ s.t. }f\text{ is }\mathscr{L}\text{-periodic in }x\text{ and }\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1\right\}.

1.2.
Quantum-classical relationships: Töplitz operator and Husimi density

As the core of this paper relies on comparing the quantum and classical dynamics, we need to be able to associate to a classical density in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
a quantum density operator in
{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, which is the role of the Töplitz transformation, and conversely to a quantum density operator in
{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
a classical
density in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
, that is the role of the Husimi transform. Both rely on the notion of Schrödinger coherent state, and we present hereafter all these concepts in the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic setting. Periodized coherent states and Husimi density were already introduced in
[
2
, Equations (15)–(16)]
.

Definition 2

(
Schrödinger coherent state and
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Schrödinger coherent state
)
.

For any
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Schrödinger coherent state
|q,p\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is

(10)

\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad|q,p\rangle(y):=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/4}\,e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,e^{ip\cdot y/\hbar}.\quad\phantom{\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},}

We define the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Schrödinger coherent state
|\underline{q,p}\rangle\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
by

(11)

\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad|\underline{q,p}\rangle(y):=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}|q,p\rangle(y+\ell).\quad\phantom{\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},}

We show in Lemma
2
that
(q,p,y)\mapsto|\underline{q,p}\rangle(y)
is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic both in
q
and
y
,
\||\underline{q,p}\rangle\|_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}=1
and, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle=|q,p\rangle_{k},

where
(|q,p\rangle_{k})_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
is the Bloch decomposition of
|q,p\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 3

(
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz operator
)
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
, we define the
\mathscr{L}
-Töplitz operator
T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]
by, for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(12)

T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]\varphi(y):=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}e^{ik\cdot y}\langle\underline{q,p-\hbar k}|\varphi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle(y)\,\mathrm{d}k\right)f(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p.

It can be shown that
T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
and that its Bloch decomposition writes, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]_{k}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle\langle\underline{q,p-\hbar k}|\,f(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p.

Definition 4

(
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Husimi density
)
.

Let
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, and denote by
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
its Bloch decomposition. Define for any
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

(13)

\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R](q,p):=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}k

with, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

(14)

f_{k}(q,p):=(2\pi\hbar)^{-d}\,\langle\underline{q,p-\hbar k}|\,R_{k}\,|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}.

We show in Proposition
1
that indeed,
\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R]\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
.

1.3.
Quantum and classical related dynamics

We consider a Lipschitz
\mathscr{L}
-periodic potential
V\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, from which we build a Hamiltonian operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(15)

\mathcal{H}:=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta_{y}+V(y),

which is self-adjoint and bounded from below with domain
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
V
is assumed to be
\mathscr{L}
-periodic and
\Delta_{y}
commutes with any translation, we have
\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{L}}
. The corresponding von Neumann equation, for some “periodic” initial datum
R^{\rm in}\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, writes

(16)

i\hbar\frac{\mathrm{d}R(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\left[\mathcal{H},\;R(t)\right],\quad\left.R\right|_{t=0}=R^{\rm in},\quad R(t)\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}.

To the quantum Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
can be associated its classical counterpart, the Hamiltonian function
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined for any
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(17)

H(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+V(x).

The classical dynamics associated to the Hamiltonian
H
is the Liouville equation (counterpart to the von Neumann equation), which writes for some initial datum
f^{\rm in}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
,

(18)

\partial_{t}f(t,x,\xi)+\left\{H,\,f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\right\}(x,\xi)=0,\qquad\left.f\right|_{t=0}=f^{\rm in},

and
\{\bullet,\bullet\}
stands for the Poisson bracket, with the convention

(19)

\left\{h,h_{*}\right\}=-\sumop\displaylimits_{m=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial h_{*}}{\partial\xi_{m}}-\frac{\partial h_{*}}{\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\xi_{m}}\right).

The characteristics associated with the Liouville equation (
18
) starting from
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted by
t\mapsto(X(t;x,\xi),\Xi(t;x,\xi))
, satisfy the ODE

(20)

\begin{cases}\dot{X}&=\phantom{-}\partial_{\xi}H(X,\Xi)=\Xi,\\
\dot{\Xi}&=-\partial_{x}H(X,\Xi)=-\nabla V(X),\qquad\qquad(X(0),\Xi(0))=(x,\xi).\end{cases}

1.4.
Main result

Let us now state our main result. In Theorem
1
, we obtain an observation inequality for the von Neumann equation in the crystal case, by combining the stability result, Theorem
2
, with the following à la Bardos-Lebeau-Rauch
[
1
]
geometric condition (
GC
), associated with the potential
V
, on a given triplet
(T,K,\Omega)
on the classical dynamics, with
T>0
,
K\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact and
\Omega\subset\Gamma
open:

(GC)

\forall\,(x,\xi)\in K,\quad\exists\,t\in(0,T)\;\text{ s.t. }\;X(t;x,\xi)\in\Omega,

where
X
is the position component of the classical characteristics associated with the potential
V
, see (
20
).

Let us recall in the following lemma a crucial observability property for the Liouville equation.

Lemma 1

(
Observability for Liouville
)
.

Consider a triplet
(T,K,\Omega)
satisfying the geometric condition (
GC
) for the potential
V
. There exists a constant
C_{\emph{\text{GC}}}[T,K,\Omega]>0
such that

(21)

\inf_{(x,\xi)\in K}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}(X(t;x,\xi))\,\text{\emph{d}}t\geqslant C_{\emph{\text{GC}}}[T,K,\Omega].

The proof of the above lemma can be found at the beginning of the proof of
[
7
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Theorem 1

(
Observability for von Neumann
)
.

Let
T>0
,
K\subset\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
a compact Borel set and
\Omega\subset\Gamma
an open set satisfying the geometric condition (
GC
) associated with the potential
V\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. Let
R^{\rm in}\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
and
t\mapsto R(t)
solving (
16
) for the potential
V
. Then, for any
\delta>0
, letting

{}_{\delta}=\Omega+B(0_{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\delta),\qquad{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}={}_{\delta}+\mathscr{L},

the solution to the von Neumann equation (
16
) satisfies the following observability property on
[0,T]\times{}^{\mathscr{L}}_{\delta}
.

Töplitz case.
If
R^{\rm in}=T_{\mathscr{L}}[f^{\rm in}]
with
f^{\rm in}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
, the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz operator defined in Definition
3
,

(22)

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\underline{\emph{\text{Tr}}}\left(\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\,R(t)\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\right)\,\emph{\text{d}}t\geqslant C_{\emph{\text{GC}}}[T,K,\Omega]\iint_{(x,\xi)\in K}f^{\rm in}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
-C_{\text{\emph{T\"{o}plitz}}}[\Gamma,T,\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)]\;\frac{\sqrt{d\hbar}}{\delta},

with, recalling that
\gamma_{-}
and

(23)

C_{\text{\emph{T\"{o}plitz}}}[\Gamma,T,L]:=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{-}}{2\gamma_{+}}}\;\inf_{\lambda>0}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\left(\lambda+\frac{L^{2}}{\lambda}\right)T\right)-1}{\lambda^{2}+L^{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2}}.

Pure state case.
If the Bloch decomposition

of

(25)

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\underline{\emph{\text{Tr}}}\left(\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\,R(t)\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\right)\,\emph{\text{d}}t\geqslant C_{\emph{\text{GC}}}[T,K,\Omega]\iint_{(x,\xi)\in K}\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R^{\rm in}](x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
-C_{\text{\emph{pure}}}[\Gamma,T,\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)]\;\frac{\sqrt{d\hbar\,\mathbf{c}_{u^{\rm in}}+2{\Delta_{\Gamma,\hbar}}^{2}(R^{\rm in})}}{\delta},

where
\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R^{\rm in}]
is the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Husimi transform of

defined in Definition

,
\Delta_{\Gamma,\hbar}(R^{\rm in})
is the standard deviation of

(26)

\Delta_{\Gamma,\hbar}(R^{\rm in}):=\left(\frac{1}{2}\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left\{|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}+\left(i\hbar\nabla_{y}-i\hbar\nabla_{q}\right)^{2}\right\}\,|u_{k}^{\rm in}(y)|^{2}\,|u_{k}^{\rm in}(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

(27)

C_{\text{\emph{pure}}}[\Gamma,T,L]:=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{-}}{2\gamma_{+}}}\;\frac{\exp\left(\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}(1+L^{2})\,T\right)-1}{1+L^{2}}.

Remark 2
.

The observation inequalities (
22
) and (
25
) involve the set
{}^{\mathscr{L}}_{\delta}={}_{\delta}+\mathscr{L}
, however this does not represent a simultaneous observation of the system in all copies of
δ
, but rather a single observation of the system in
δ
while knowing its periodicity. To illustrate this fact, consider a “periodic pure state”
R
, in the sense that its Bloch decomposition can be written

R_{k}=|v_{k}\rangle\langle v_{k}|,\qquad k\in\Gamma^{*},

with
v_{k}\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. The quantity representing the observation of the system writes

\underline{\emph{\text{Tr}}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\,R\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\right)=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\emph{\text{Tr}}_{L^{2}_{\emph{\text{per}}}(\Gamma)}\left(\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\,R_{k}\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}}\right)\,\emph{\text{d}}k=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{{}_{\delta}}(x)\,|v_{k}(x)|^{2}\,\emph{\text{d}}x\,\emph{\text{d}}k\\
=\intop\nolimits_{{}_{\delta}}\,\rho_{R}(x)\,\emph{\text{d}}x,

where
\displaystyle x\mapsto\rho_{R}(x):=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}|v_{k}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}k
is interpreted as the (
\mathscr{L}
-periodic) particle density. The above expresses the idea that even if the observation inequality involves
{}_{\delta}^{\mathscr{L}}
, the actual observation is indeed performed only once in
δ
.

1.5.
Structure of the proof

To prove Theorem
1
, we first show a quantum/classical stability property between solutions to the von Neumann equation (
16
) and to the Liouville equation (
18
) in Theorem
2
. To quantify of form of “distance” between the quantum and classical solutions, we rely on an optimal-transport-like pseudo-metric, defined in Subsection
2.1
. Once stability is obtained, yielding a control of the pseudo-metric at time
t
by its value at time
0
, we consider suitable quantum/classical pairs at initial time, using notions of Töplitz and Husimi transforms (Definitions
3
and
4
) allowing to bound the discrepancy between the quantum and the classical observability quantities (Propositions
2
and
3
). We conclude to Theorem
1
using the observability property for the Liouville equation (Lemma
1
).

2.
Liouville-von Neumann stability

In this section, we relate the quantum von Neumann equation (
16
) with its classical counterpart, the Liouville equation. We introduce the notion of classical/quantum optimal transport pseudo-metric in the periodic setting, based on the one of
[
7
]
. This allows us to obtain a notion of Liouville-von Neumann stability in Theorem
2
, a periodic counterpart to
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

2.1.
Optimal-transport-like pseudo-metric

We first introduce the notion of coupling between a classical density
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
and a quantum density operator
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
.

Definition 5

(
Classical-Quantum coupling
)
.

A coupling between
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
and
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
is a measurable operator-valued function

Q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}

which is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in its first variable
x
and satisfies the “marginal” constraints

(29)

\forall\,(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}(Q(x,\xi))=f(x,\xi)\qquad\text{and }\qquad\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi=R.

The set of couplings between
f
and
R
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(f,R)
.

We now introduce the notion of quadratic transportation cost, as an analogue to the the one introduced in
[
7
]
, in the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic setting. We highlight that the
\mathscr{L}
-periodicity is ensured by the consideration of
|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2}
instead of
|x-y|^{2}
. However,
y\mapsto|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2}\notin H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
and such regularity is necessary in the proof. Therefore, a regularization function
\Theta
is introduced, ensuring
y\mapsto\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\in H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
.

Definition 6

(
Transportation cost
)
.

For any
\lambda>0
, we define the transportation costs
c_{\lambda}
and
\hat{c}_{\lambda}
as operator-valued functions of
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)
acts on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi)
acts on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
as

(30)

\begin{split}\forall\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,\psi\,(y):=\left\{\lambda^{2}\,\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right\}\,\psi\,(y),\\
\forall\varphi\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma),\qquad\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,\varphi\,(y):=\left\{\lambda^{2}\,\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right\}\,\varphi\,(y).\end{split}

In the above formula,
(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}
stands for
(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})
and, recalling the notation
\gamma_{-}=\inf_{z\in\partial\Gamma}|z|
, for any
r\geqslant 0
,

(31)

\Theta(r):=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{r}\left(1-\frac{r_{*}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)_{+}\mathrm{d}r_{*}.

Both
c_{\lambda}
and
\hat{c}_{\lambda}
are
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in
x
and
c_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
.

Note that
y\mapsto\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\in H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
and, recalling that
\gamma_{+}=\sup_{z\in\partial\Gamma}|z|
, we have for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(32)

\frac{\gamma_{-}}{2\gamma_{+}}\,|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2}\leqslant\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\leqslant|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leqslant\Theta^{\prime}\leqslant 1.

With the notions of periodic trace (Definition
1
), couplings and transportation cost at hand, we define the transportation classical/quantum pseudo-metric:

Definition 7

(
Transportation pseudo-metric
)
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
and
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, we set

(33)

E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f,R):=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{C}(f,R)}\left(\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well-defined (could be infinite) as for any
x,\xi
, we indeed have
Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
.

2.2.
Flows and Bloch flows

Working in the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic crystal setting requires to make use of the Bloch decomposition. In this subsection, we present the Bloch decomposition
(\mathcal{H}_{k})_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
of the Hamiltonian operator
\mathcal{H}
, the corresponding family of classical Hamiltonian functions
(H_{k})_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
, as well as the family of quantum flow operators
(U_{k}(t))_{k}
and classical flow applications
(\Phi_{k,t})_{k}
associated to the corresponding, respectively, quantum and classical dynamics. Recall that the quantum Hamiltonian associated with our problem is

\mathcal{H}:=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta_{y}+V(y),

which, as an operator of
{\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathscr{L}}}
, admits a Bloch decomposition which writes (by direct computation, or see
[
11
, Chapter 7]
), for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

(34)

\mathcal{H}_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)^{2}+V(y).

The associated von Neumann flow operators are denoted respectively
t\mapsto U(t)
and
t\mapsto U_{k}(t)
. Remark that, while
U(t)
acts on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
U_{k}(t)
acts on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. The classical counterparts to (
15
) and (
34
) respectively write, for any
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

H(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+V(x),

and

(35)

H_{k}(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}|\xi+\hbar k|^{2}+V(x).

We define, for any
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)
as the flow associated with the ODE

(36)

\begin{cases}\dot{X}&=\phantom{-}\partial_{\xi}H(X,\Xi)=\Xi,\\
\dot{\Xi}&=-\partial_{x}H(X,\Xi)=-\nabla V(X),\qquad\qquad(X(0),\Xi(0))=(x,\xi).\end{cases}

Then, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, the
k
-flow
\Phi_{k,\cdot}(x,\xi)
defined by

(37)

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi):=\Phi_{t}(x,\xi+\hbar k)-(0,\,\hbar k)\phantom{\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},}

is the flow associated to the system, indexed by
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

(38)

\begin{cases}\dot{X}_{k}&=\phantom{-}\partial_{\xi}H_{k}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})=\Xi_{k}+\hbar k,\\
\dot{\Xi}_{k}&=-\partial_{x}H_{k}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})=-\nabla V(X_{k}),\end{cases}\qquad\qquad(X_{k}(0),\,\Xi_{k}(0))=(x,\xi).

Note that it effectively appears in (
38
) that the effective momentum
\dot{X}_{k}
is the standard momentum
\Xi_{k}
plus the crystal momentum
\hbar k
.

Note that the
\mathscr{L}
-periodicity of
V
implies that for any
(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\ell\in\mathscr{L}
, we have

(39)

\Phi_{t}(x+\ell,\xi)=\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)+(\ell,0).

2.3.
Stability result

We now state our Liouville-von Neumann stability result.

Theorem 2

(
Liouville-von Neumann stability
)
.

Let
f^{\rm in}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
and
R^{\rm in}\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
. For all
t\geqslant 0
, set

R(t)=U(t)^{*}\,R^{\rm in}\,U(t),\qquad f(t,X,\Xi):=f^{\rm in}\left(\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)\right)\quad\text{for a.e. }(X,\Xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then for each
\lambda>0
and
t\geqslant 0
, one has

(40)

E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f(t),R(t))\leqslant E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f^{\rm in},R^{\rm in})\;\exp\left(\eta_{\lambda,V}\,t\right),

with
\eta_{\lambda,V}:=\frac{\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
Q^{\rm in}\in\mathcal{C}(f^{\rm in},R^{\rm in})
. Set, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
(X,\Xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

Q(t,X,\Xi):=U(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}\circ\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)\,U(t)

and

(41)

\mathcal{E}(t):=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(Q(t,X,\Xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{\lambda}(X,\Xi)\,Q(t,X,\Xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}\Xi.

Note that
Q^{\rm in}

\mathscr{L}
-periodic in
x
and (
39
) imply that
X\mapsto Q^{\rm in}\circ\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)
is also
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in
X
. Hence, we indeed have
Q(t)\in\mathcal{C}(f(t),R(t))
, and in particular,

\mathcal{E}(t)\geqslant E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f(t),R(t)),\qquad t\geqslant 0.

Since
c_{\lambda}
and
Q(t,\cdot,\cdot)
are
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in the
x
-variable, so is

(X,\Xi)\mapsto\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(Q(t,X,\Xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{\lambda}(X,\Xi)\,Q(t,X,\Xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Applying then Lemma
3
(changing variables
(X,\Xi)\mapsto(x,\xi)=\Phi_{-t}(X,\Xi)
) yields

\mathcal{E}(t):=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(Q^{\rm in}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U(t)\,c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi))\,U(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Let us now consider the family of operators
\{Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)\}_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
, the Bloch transform of
Q^{\rm in}(x,\xi)
(which we recall belongs to
{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
). By Corollary
1
, we have that for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

(42)

\left(Q^{\rm in}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U(t)\,c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi))\,U(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)_{k}\\
=Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(U(t))_{k}\,\left(c_{\lambda}(\Phi_{t}(x,\xi))\right)_{k}\,\left(U(t)^{*}\right)_{k}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which, using Lemma
4
and the fact that
\Phi_{t}(x,\xi)-(0,\hbar k)=\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi-\hbar k)
, is exactly

Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi-\hbar k))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}},

so that

(43)

\mathcal{E}(t)=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi-\hbar k))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Exchanging integrals in (
43
) we get

\mathcal{E}(t)=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathcal{E}_{k}(t)\,\mathrm{d}k,

with

\mathcal{E}_{k}(t):=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi-\hbar k))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We obtain, performing the change of variable
\xi\mapsto\xi-\hbar k
still denoted by
\xi
for convenience,

\mathcal{E}_{k}(t):=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

For each
k\in\Gamma^{*}
and
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider
(e_{j}(x,\xi,k))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
the
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
-complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)
, which we know from Proposition
4
is a Trace-class nonnegative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, associated with the eigenvalues
(\rho_{j}(x,\xi,k))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. Straightforwardly, both
e_{j}
and
\rho_{j}
are
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in the variable
x
. It then comes that

(44)

\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{j}(x,\xi,k)\,\left\langle U_{k}(t)e_{j}(x,\,\xi,k)|\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)e_{j}(x,\,\xi,k)\right\rangle.

We highlight that the brackets are taken in
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. For any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, the map

t\mapsto\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi|\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle

is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
on
\mathbb{R}
and, by the same manipulations as in
[
7
, proof of Theorem 3.1]
, one has, recalling that
t\mapsto U_{k}(t)
is the quantum flow associated to
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
t\mapsto\Phi_{k,t}
is the classical flow associated with
H_{k}
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi|\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi\left|\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\mathcal{H}_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))\right]\right|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle\\
+\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi|\left\{H_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}\right\}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle.

Let us point out that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\nabla_{x}\;\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})=2P_{\Gamma}(x-y)\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2}).

On the one hand, denoting
(X_{k},\Xi_{k})=\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{H_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}\right\}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}H_{k}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})+\nabla_{\xi}H_{k}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})\cdot\nabla_{x}\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})

\displaystyle=-\nabla V(X_{k})\cdot 2\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)-\left(\Xi_{k}+\hbar k\right)\cdot\left[2\,\lambda^{2}\,\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\,P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\right].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\mathcal{H}_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)^{2},\,(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)^{2},\,\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right],

which, thanks to Lemma
5
in Appendix
A.2
, yields

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\mathcal{H}_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})\right]

\displaystyle=(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot\nabla V(y)+\nabla V(y)\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)\cdot\left[P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\cdot(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)

All in all,

\displaystyle\big\{H_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}\big\}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})+\frac{i}{\hbar}

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{H}_{k},\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k})\right]=

\displaystyle-\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)\cdot\nabla V(X_{k})-\nabla V(X_{k})\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)

\displaystyle+(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot\nabla V(y)+\nabla V(y)\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}\left(\Xi_{k}+\hbar k\right)\cdot\left[P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\,\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}\left[P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right]\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+\hbar k\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)\cdot\left[P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})\,\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}\left[P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(y-X_{k})|^{2})\right]\cdot(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k).

Using the identity
P_{\Gamma}(-z)=-P_{\Gamma}z
almost everywhere (in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(\partial\Gamma+\mathscr{L})
), coming from
-\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}
, the above equals

\displaystyle-\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)\cdot(\nabla V(X_{k})-\nabla V(y))+(\nabla V(X_{k})-\nabla V(y))\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right)\cdot\left[P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}\,P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\,\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\cdot\left(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla{y}\right).

Using Peter-Paul’s inequality, by which for positives
x,y
it holds that
2xy\leqslant\lambda x^{2}+\lambda^{-1}y^{2}
, the above is less than

\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}\,\left|\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})\,P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)\right|^{2}+(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\lambda^{2}|\nabla V(X_{k})-\nabla V(y)|^{2}+(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right).

Since
0\leqslant\Theta^{\prime}\leqslant 1
and
V
is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic, the above is itself less than

\lambda\left(\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2}+(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)^{2}}{\lambda}\left(\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2}+(\Xi_{k}+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right),

which, using the fact that
\displaystyle|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\,\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(X_{k}-y)|^{2})
, is less than

\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(X_{k},\Xi_{k}).

As
(X_{k},\Xi_{k})
is by definition
\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi)
, we have thus proven that, for any
t\geqslant 0
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi|\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle\leqslant\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\left\langle U_{k}(t)\phi|\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))|U_{k}(t)\phi\right\rangle.

Similar density, Grönwall and linearity arguments as in
[
7
, proof of Theorem 3.1]
then lead to

\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,U_{k}(t)\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(\Phi_{k,t}(x,\xi))\,U_{k}(t)^{*}\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
\leqslant\exp\left(\eta_{\lambda,V}\,t\right)\,\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,Q^{\rm in}_{k}(x,\xi+\hbar k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

with
\eta_{\lambda,V}:=\frac{2\gamma_{+}}{\gamma_{-}}\left(\lambda+\frac{\mathrm{Lip}(\nabla V)^{2}}{\lambda}\right)
, implying, by integrating in
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{E}_{k}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{k}(0)\,\exp\left(2\eta_{\lambda,V}\,t\right).

Finally integrating in
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, we get

\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)\,\exp\left(2\eta_{\lambda,V}\,t\right),

and thus

E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f(t),R(t))^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)\,\exp\left(2\eta_{\lambda,V}\,t\right).

The theorem follows by minimizing the right-hand side as
Q^{\rm in}
runs through
\mathcal{C}(f^{\rm in},R^{\rm in})
.
∎

.

Lemma 2

(
Basic properties of the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic coherent states
)
.

For any

, the
\mathscr{L}

•

•

the

Proof.

First note that the

\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}e^{-\frac{|\ell|^{2}}{2\hbar}}<+\infty.

Moreover, the

in both the

and
y
variables is straightforward from (
11
) and the facts that
|q,p\rangle(y+\ell)=|q-\ell,p\rangle(y)
and
\mathscr{L}=-\mathscr{L}
. Equation (
45
) is straightforward from the definition of the Bloch transform (
56
), as for any
q,p,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\in\Gamma^{*}
we have

\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}|q,p\rangle(y+\ell)\,e^{-ik\cdot(y+\ell)}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}|q,p-\hbar k\rangle(y+\ell)=|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle(y).

From the isometry property of the Bloch transform (

\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle\underline{q,p-\hbar k}|\underline{q,p-\hbar k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}k=\langle q,p|q,p\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1.

∎

In the following proposition, we show that the Husimi transform is indeed a periodized probability density.

Proposition 1

(
Basic property of the Husimi density
)
.

Consider
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
whose Bloch transform writes, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

R_{k}=|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|

with
\psi_{k}\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. Then
\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R]
, the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Husimi transform of
R
, belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
.

Proof.

The density
\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R]
it obviously nonnegative and
\mathscr{L}
-periodic in the
q
-variable, we thus only have to show that it is a probability density. Fix
q\in\Gamma
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By definition,

\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R](q,p)=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}k,

with, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

f_{k}(q,p)=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\left|(2\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}e^{-\frac{|y+\ell-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,e^{-i(p-\hbar k)\cdot(y+\ell)/\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|^{2}.

As
\psi_{k}
is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic, the above equals

(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\left|(2\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma+\ell}e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,e^{-i(p-\hbar k)\cdot y/\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|^{2},

that is

(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\left|(2\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,e^{-i(p-\hbar k)\cdot y/\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|^{2}.

Integrating in
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and using Parseval’s equality, we therefore have

(47)

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\left\|y\mapsto e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,e^{ik\cdot y}\,\psi_{k}(y)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{\hbar}}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating in
q\in\Gamma
, it comes that

\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{q\in\Gamma}\intop\nolimits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{\hbar}}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

Changing variables
y-q\mapsto y
, the above becomes

(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{q\in\Gamma}\intop\nolimits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}|\psi_{k}(y+q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

Using Fubini’s theorem and the fact that
\psi_{k}
is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic, we henceforth have

(48)

\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\right)\mathrm{d}y=\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}(R_{k}).

We then conclude, as

\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p\,\mathrm{d}k=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathrm{Tr}(R_{k})\mathrm{d}k=\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}(R)=1.

∎

3.1.
Upper-bounds on pseudo-distance between
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz and Husimi related couples

In this subsection, we provide an upper-bound on the classical/quantum pseudo-distance between
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz and Husimi related couples. The following proposition focuses on a couple composed of an
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz operator and its symbol.

Proposition 2

(
Pseudo-distance between
\mathscr{L}
-periodic Töplitz operator and its symbol
)
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{L}}
with finite second moments, we have

(49)

E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f,\,T_{\mathscr{L}}[f])\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{2}\,d\,\hbar}.

Proof.

We define the diagonal coupling

Q(x,\xi)\,\varphi(y):=f(x,\xi)\,\,\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}e^{ik\cdot y}\langle\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}|\varphi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle(y)\,\mathrm{d}k.

From (
45
)–(
46
), we have
Q\in\mathcal{C}(f,T_{\mathscr{L}}[f])
, and, by construction,

\forall k\in\Gamma^{*},\qquad Q_{k}(x,\xi)=f(x,\xi)\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle\langle\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}|.\phantom{\forall k\in\Gamma^{*},\qquad}

Therefore,

\displaystyle E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f,\,T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]))^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\underline{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\,Q(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\big(c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\big)_{k}\,Q_{k}(x,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,\xi)\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}|\,\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi-\hbar k)\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where in the last equality we used (
63
) in Lemma
4
. Since by (
32
),

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\equiv\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2})+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\leqslant\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2},

we conclude that

E_{\hbar,\lambda}(f,\,T_{\mathscr{L}}[f]))^{2}\\
\leqslant\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x,\xi)\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}|\,\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

On the one hand, for any
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the (non-periodic) coherent state
|x,\xi\rangle
satisfies

(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})|x,\xi\rangle(y)=\left(\xi+i\hbar\left[-\frac{y-x}{\hbar}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\xi\right]\right)|x,\xi\rangle(y)=-i\left(y-x\right)|x,\xi\rangle(y),

therefore

(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}|x,\xi\rangle(y)=\left(d\hbar-|y-x|^{2}\right)|x,\xi\rangle(y).

We deduce by linearity that the
\mathscr{L}
-periodic coherent state
|\underline{x,\xi}\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}|\underline{x,\xi}\rangle(y)

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}|x,\xi\rangle(y)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\left(d\hbar-|y+\ell-x|^{2}\right)|x,\xi\rangle(y+\ell).

On the other hand,

\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}\,|\underline{x,\xi}\rangle(y)=\lambda^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}\,|x,\xi\rangle(y+\ell),

so that, as
P_{\Gamma}(y-x)=P_{\Gamma}(y+\ell-x)
,

(\,\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2})\,|\underline{x,\xi}\rangle(y)\\
=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\left(d\hbar+\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y+\ell-x)|^{2}-|y+\ell-x|^{2}\right)\,|x,\xi\rangle(y+\ell).

It comes in particular that, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

(\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2})\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle=\left(y\mapsto(d\hbar+\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}-|y-x|^{2})\,|x,\xi\rangle(y)\right)_{k},

where the notation
(\bullet)_{k}
stands for the
k^{th}
component of the Bloch decomposition of the function
\bullet
. Since
|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle=(|x,\xi\rangle)_{k}
by (
45
), we have, using the isometry property of the Bloch transform (
57
),

\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}|\,\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\,|\underline{x,\xi-\hbar k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}k\\
=\langle x,\xi|(d\hbar+\lambda^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|^{2}-|y-x|^{2})\,|x,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which, as
|P_{\Gamma}(y-x)|\leqslant|y-x|
, is less than

d\hbar+\langle x,\xi|(\lambda^{2}-1)|y-x|^{2}\,|x,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=d\hbar+(\lambda^{2}-1)\frac{d\hbar}{2}=(1+\lambda^{2})\frac{d\hbar}{2},

hence allowing to conclude to (
49
).
∎

In the following proposition, we provide an upper-bound on the classical/quantum pseudo-distance between an operator in
{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
for which all Bloch components are rank-
1
projectors (“periodic pure state”) and its associated Husimi density.

Proposition 3

(
Pseudo-distance between “periodic pure state” and its Husimi density
)
.

Consider
R\in{\mathcal{S}^{+,1}_{\mathscr{L}}}
whose Bloch transform writes, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
,

R_{k}=|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|,

with
\psi_{k}\in H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. Then

(50)

E_{\hbar,1}(\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R],R)\leqslant\sqrt{d\hbar\,\mathbf{c}_{\psi}+2\,\Delta_{\Gamma,\hbar}^{2}(R)},

where
\mathbf{c}_{\bullet}
and
\Delta_{\Gamma,\hbar}
are defined respectively in (
24
) and (
26
).

Proof.

Recall that for any
(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R](q,p):=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}k,

where

f_{k}(q,p):=(2\pi\hbar)^{-d}\,\left|\langle\underline{q,p-\hbar k}|\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\right|^{2}.

We let
Q
such that for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, the
k^{th}
component of
Q
’s Bloch transform,
Q_{k}
, is defined by

Q_{k}(q,p):=f_{k}(q,p)\,R_{k}.

We let

\mathcal{E}_{k}:=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(Q_{k}(q,p)^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{c}_{1}(q,p-\hbar k)Q_{k}(q,p)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p,

which can be rewritten as

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\langle\psi_{k}|\hat{c}_{1}(q,p-\hbar k)|\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p,

or, changing variables
p-\hbar k\mapsto p
,

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\langle\psi_{k}|\hat{c}_{1}(q,p)|\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p.

Recall that

\hat{c}_{1}(q,p)=\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2})+(p+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\leqslant|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}+(p+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2},

we denote

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k,1}+\mathcal{E}_{k,\,2},

with

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}:=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p

and

\mathcal{E}_{k,2}:=\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\overline{\psi_{k}(y)}\,(p+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\,\psi_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p.

\bullet

Upper bound for

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}
.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{k}(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}p\right)|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

Recalling Equation (
47
), the above writes

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left((\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|z-q|^{2}}{\hbar}}|\psi_{k}(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

We change variables
z\mapsto x=z-q
and obtain

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left((\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}|\psi_{k}(x+q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

By Fubini’s theorem,

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\intop\nolimits_{y\in\Gamma}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(x+q)|^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\right)|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since the function
q\mapsto|\psi_{k}(x+q)|^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}
is
\mathscr{L}
-periodic, we have

\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(x+q)|^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q=\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y+x-q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q,

so that

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\intop\nolimits_{y\in\Gamma}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|P_{\Gamma}(y+x-q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\right)|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

hence

\mathcal{E}_{k,1}=\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left((\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P_{\Gamma}(y+x-q)|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}\mathrm{d}x\right)|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q,

Now, denoting
\ell_{y-q}=y-q-P_{\Gamma}(y-q)\in\mathscr{L}
, notice that

|P_{\Gamma}(y+x-q)|^{2}=\min_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}|y+x-q-\ell|^{2}\leqslant|x+y-q-\ell_{y-q}|^{2}=|x+P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\\
=|x|^{2}+|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}+2\,x\cdot P_{\Gamma}(y-q).

Since

(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{d\hbar}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{\hbar}}\mathrm{d}x=0,

we henceforth obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k,1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\hbar}{2}\right)|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q+\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle=\frac{d\hbar}{2}\,\|\psi_{k}\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}+\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

\bullet

Upper bound for

\mathcal{E}_{k,2}
. Let us decompose

(p+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}=|p|^{2}-2p\cdot(-i\hbar\nabla_{y})+(-i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2},

we have, recalling (
48
),

\mathcal{E}_{k,2}=\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p+\hbar^{2}\;\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\nabla\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
-2\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p\cdot\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\overline{\psi_{k}(y)}\,(-i\hbar\nabla_{y})\,\psi_{k}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Denote by
\hat{g}
the the Fourier transform of

g:y\mapsto e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{2\hbar}}\,\psi_{k}(y),

we have

f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\left|\hbar^{-d/2}\,\hat{g}\left(\frac{p}{\hbar}\right)\right|^{2}.

On the one hand,

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|ip\,\hbar^{-d/2}\,\hat{g}\left(\frac{p}{\hbar}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\hbar^{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|ip\,\hat{g}\left(p\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}p.

By Parseval’s equality, the above transforms into

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\left((\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla g(y)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle=\hbar^{2}\;(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{k}(y)-\frac{y-q}{\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y)\right|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\hbar^{2}\;(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)-\frac{y}{\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y+q)\right|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating in
q\in\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\hbar^{2}(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\right)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\hbar^{2}(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(y+q)|^{2}\mathrm{d}q\right)\left|\frac{y}{\hbar}\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-2\hbar^{2}(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\psi_{k}(y+q)\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right)\cdot\frac{y}{\hbar}\,e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since
\psi_{k}\in H^{1}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, we have

\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q=\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla\psi_{k}(q)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q=\|\nabla\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)},

as well as

\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(y+q)|^{2}\mathrm{d}q=\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}\mathrm{d}q=\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)},

and

\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\psi_{k}(y+q)\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\psi_{k}(q)\nabla\psi_{k}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q.

Combining these with

(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y=1,\quad(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y\,e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{d\hbar}{2},

we conclude that

\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=\hbar^{2}\|\nabla\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}+\frac{d\hbar}{2}\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}.

On the other hand,

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\left|\hbar^{-d/2}\,\hat{g}\left(\frac{p}{\hbar}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}p=(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\,\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\left|\hat{g}\left(p\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}p\\
=-i\hbar\,(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\hat{g}(p)}\,\left(ip\,\hat{g}(p)\right)\,\mathrm{d}p.

The isometry property of the Fourier transform yields

\displaystyle\langle\hat{g}\,|\widehat{\nabla g}\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\langle g\,|\nabla g\,\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

so that we have

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=-i\hbar\,(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\psi_{k}(y)}\left(\nabla\psi_{k}(y)-\frac{y-q}{\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y)\right)e^{-\frac{|y-q|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-i\hbar\,(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\psi_{k}(y+q)}\left(\nabla\psi_{k}(y+q)-\frac{y}{\hbar}\,\psi_{k}(y+q)\right)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Again, integrating in
q\in\Gamma
and by
\mathscr{L}
-periodicity of
\psi_{k}
, we obtain

\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\,f_{k}(q,p+\hbar k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}p=-i\hbar\,(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma}\overline{\psi_{k}(q)}\nabla\psi_{k}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right)e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
+i\hbar\,(\pi\hbar)^{-d/2}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\right)\frac{y}{\hbar}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{\hbar}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

that is

\langle\psi_{k}|\,-i\hbar\nabla\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}.

All in all,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\hbar}{2}\|\psi_{k}\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}+2\|\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\|-i\hbar\nabla\psi_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}-2\langle\psi_{k}|\,-i\hbar\nabla\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\hbar}{2}\|\psi_{k}\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}+\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+i\hbar\nabla_{q})^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

\bullet

Conclusion to the proof.

Combining the upper bounds on
\mathcal{E}_{k,1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{k,2}
, we get

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k,1}+\mathcal{E}_{k,2}\leqslant d\hbar\,\|\psi_{k}\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}+\iint_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\left[|P_{\Gamma}(y-q)|^{2}+(i\hbar\nabla_{y}-i\hbar\nabla_{q})^{2}\right]\,|\psi_{k}(y)|^{2}\,|\psi_{k}(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}q.

Taking the average in
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, we conclude to the proposition, as

E_{\hbar,1}(\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{L}}[R],\,R)\leqslant\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\mathcal{E}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}k=d\hbar\,\mathbf{c}_{\psi}+2\Delta^{2}_{\Gamma,\hbar}(R).

∎

A.1.
Tools on the Bloch transform

Recall the notation
\mathfrak{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\,\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 8
.

We define the
Bloch transform
as the bounded operator

\mathcal{B}:\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow L^{2}_{qp}(\Gamma^{*},L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)),

where

L^{2}_{qp}(\Gamma^{*},L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)):=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma))\;|\;\forall(k,K)\in\Gamma^{*}\times\mathscr{L}^{*},\,u(k+K)=U_{K}\,u(k)\right\},

and
U_{K}
is the unitary operator on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
of multiplication by the function
x\mapsto e^{-iK\cdot x}
, such that

(56)

\forall\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\;(\mathcal{B}\phi)(k)(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\phi(x+\ell)\,e^{-ik\cdot(x+\ell)},\qquad\forall\,(k,x)\in\Gamma^{*}\times\Gamma.

It satisfies in particular

(57)

\displaystyle\forall u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\intop\nolimits_{\Gamma^{*}}\|(\mathcal{B}u)(k)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}k,

(58)

\displaystyle\forall u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\;u(x)=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}(\mathcal{B}u)(k)(x)\,e^{ik\cdot x}\,\mathrm{d}k,\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proposition 4
.

For any operator
R\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{L}}
, there exists a unique (almost everywhere in
k
) family of self-adjoint operators
\{R_{k}\}_{k\in\Gamma^{*}}
over
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
such that

(59)

\forall f\in\mathcal{D}(R),\qquad(Rf)_{k}=R_{k}f_{k}.

Proof.

The proof is derived from one of lecture notes of Eric Cancès (which are not accessible online) which is stated for . Let
u
and
v\in\mathcal{D}(R)
. For any
\ell\in\mathscr{L}
, we have

\langle v|\tau_{\ell}Ru\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\tau_{-\ell}v|Ru\rangle_{L^{2}}=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle e^{ik\cdot\ell}\,v_{k}|(Ru)_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\,\mathrm{d}k=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle\,v_{k}|(Ru)_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\,e^{-ik\cdot\ell}\,\mathrm{d}k,

as well as

\langle v|R\tau_{\ell}u\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle Rv|\tau_{\ell}u\rangle_{L^{2}}=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle(Rv)_{k}|e^{-ik\cdot\ell}u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\,\mathrm{d}k=\fint_{\Gamma^{*}}\langle(Rv)_{k}|u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}\,e^{-ik\cdot\ell}\,\mathrm{d}k.

Up to normalization,
\langle v|\tau_{\ell}Ru\rangle_{L^{2}}
and
\langle v|R\tau_{\ell}u\rangle_{L^{2}}
therefore correspond to Fourier coefficients of the
\Gamma^{*}
-periodic functions
k\mapsto\langle\,v_{k}|(Ru)_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}
and
k\mapsto\langle(Rv)_{k}|u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}
. Since
\tau_{\ell}
and
R
commute, the last functions thus have same Fourier coefficients, hence are equal almost everywhere. As such, for almost every
k\in\Gamma^{*}
and

\langle\,v_{k}|(Ru)_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}=\langle(Rv)_{k}|u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}.

Since above is linear in
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
, depends on
v
only through
v_{k}
(left term) and on
u
only through
u_{k}
(right term), there is a self-adjoint (due to the above equality) operator over
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
, denoted
R_{k}
, such that

\langle\,v_{k}|(Ru)_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}=\langle(Rv)_{k}|u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}=\langle v_{k}|R_{k}|u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rm per}}.

The uniqueness of
R_{k}
is ensured by the density of the domain of
R
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{X_{t}(x,\xi)\in\Gamma-\ell}\;g(X_{t}(x,\xi),\Xi_{t}(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

which rewrites

\iint_{\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{X_{t}(x,\xi)\in\Gamma-\ell}\right)g(X_{t}(x,\xi),\Xi_{t}(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
\{\Gamma-\ell\}_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}
is a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds for all
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
that

\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\mathbf{1}\hskip-2.70004pt\mathrm{l}_{X_{t}(x,\xi)\in\Gamma-\ell}=1,

allowing to conclude.
∎

The following lemma provides the Bloch decomposition of the flow operator
U(t)
and the transportation cost
c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)
.

Lemma 4

(
Bloch transform of the evolution and cost operators
)
.

For almost any
(t,x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, we have

(62)

\displaystyle(U(t))_{k}

\displaystyle=U_{k}(t),

(63)

\displaystyle\left(c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=\hat{c}_{\lambda}(x,\xi-\hbar k),

where
(R)_{k}
stands for the
k^{th}
component of the Bloch transform of an operator
R
, as defined in Appendix
A.1
. We recall that the flow operators
U(t)
(on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) and
U_{k}(t)
(on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
) are associated to the von Neumann equations related to the quantum Hamiltonians
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}
defined in (
15
)–(
34
) and the costs
c_{\lambda}
and
\hat{c}_{\lambda}
are defined in (
30
).

Proof.

\bullet
Equation (
62
) corresponds to a time-dependent version of the Bloch Theorem. It is given in
[
6
]
that if an operator
A
is decomposable as
\intop\nolimits^{\oplus}_{\Lambda}A(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda)
on a
direct integral of Hilbert spaces
\mathfrak{H}=\intop\nolimits^{\oplus}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}_{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu(\lambda)
and
f
is an analytic function, the operator
f(A)
is decomposable and
f(A(\lambda))=f(A)(\lambda)
almost everywhere. Since the Bloch decomposition can be interpreted in this formalism, substituting
A
by
\mathcal{H}
and
\Lambda
by
\Gamma^{*}
(see Reed and Simon
[
13
]
) and since for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the application
h\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\exp(it\,h/\hbar)
is analytic, we deduce that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have for almost any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
that

\left(\exp(it\,\mathcal{H}/\hbar)\right)_{k}=\exp(it\,\mathcal{H}_{k}/\hbar),

that is (
62
).

\bullet
Let us now prove (
63
). Consider
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the one hand, for any
k\in\Gamma^{*}
, we have

\phi_{k}(y)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\in\mathscr{L}}\phi(y+\ell)\,e^{-ik\cdot(y+\ell)},\qquad\forall\,y\in\Gamma,

and on the other hand,

(c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\phi)_{k}(y)=\left(\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})+(\xi-\hbar k+i\hbar\nabla y)^{2}\right)\phi_{k}(y)

as

\left(c_{\lambda}(x,\xi)\phi\right)(y)=\left(\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})+(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right)\phi(y).

Indeed, the Bloch transform commutes with the multiplication with an
\mathscr{L}
-periodic function.
∎

Lemma 5

(
Commutator formulas
)
.

Consider
V\in H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. We have the identities, seeing all following operators as acting on
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
functions of the variable
y
,

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot\nabla V(y)+\nabla V(y)\cdot\left(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right),

(65)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=0,

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)^{2},\,(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=0,

(67)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)^{2},\,\lambda^{2}\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k)\cdot\left[P_{\Gamma}(y-x)\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\right]

(68)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2}P_{\Gamma}(y-x)\;\Theta^{\prime}(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})\cdot(-i\hbar\nabla_{y}+\hbar k).

Proof.

Identities (
65
)–(
66
) are straightforward as the involved operators commute. Moreover, Identity (
68
) is just a consequence of (
64
), replacing
\xi
by
-\hbar k
and
V
by
y\mapsto-\Theta(|P_{\Gamma}(x-y)|^{2})
, as the latter indeed belongs to
H^{2}_{\rm per}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, we only need to prove (
64
).

We have

(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}=|\xi|^{2}+2\xi\cdot(i\hbar\nabla_{y})+(i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2},

so that

(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}V(y)=|\xi|^{2}V(y)+2\xi\cdot\left(i\hbar\nabla V(y)+V(y)(i\hbar\nabla_{y})\right)\\
+\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{y}V(y)+2i\hbar\nabla V(y)(i\hbar\nabla_{y})+V(y)(i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right).

On the other hand,

V(y)(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}=V(y)\left(|\xi|^{2}+2\xi\cdot(i\hbar\nabla_{y})+(i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right),

so that

(69)

\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]=2\xi\cdot\nabla V(y)+i\hbar\Delta_{y}V(y)+2\nabla V(y)(i\hbar\nabla_{y}).

We now compute

(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot\nabla V(y)=\xi\cdot\nabla V(y)+i\hbar\Delta_{y}V(y)+\nabla V(y)(i\hbar\nabla_{y}),

showing that

\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[V(y),\,(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})^{2}\right]=(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y})\cdot\nabla V(y)+\nabla V(y)\cdot\left(\xi+i\hbar\nabla_{y}\right).

∎

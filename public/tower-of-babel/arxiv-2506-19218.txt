Stratified Hyperbolicity of the moduli stack of stable minimal models, II: Big Picard Theorem and the stratified Brody hyperbolicity
Junchao Shentu

Abstract
. This is the second paper on the global geometry of Birkar’s moduli of stable minimal models (e.g., the KSBA moduli stack). We introduces a birationally admissible stratification of the Deligne-Mumford stack of stable minimal models, such that the universal family over each stratum admits a simple normal crossing log birational model. The main result of this paper is to show that for each stratum 𝒮 \mathscr{S} , the pair ( 𝒮 ¯ , ∂ 𝒮 ) (\overline{\mathscr{S}},\partial\mathscr{S}) satisfies the Big Picard theorem. In particular, we show that each stratum 𝒮 \mathscr{S} of the moduli stack is Borel hyperbolic and Brody hyperbolic.



1.
Introduction

This is the second paper in a series of studies that investigates the global geometry of C. Birkar’s moduli stack of stable minimal models and the KSBA moduli stack, building upon the results presented in
[
17
]
. For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
c\in\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}
, a finite set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Q}^{>0}
, and
\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
, let
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
denote the Deligne-Mumford stack of
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal models as introduced by Birkar in
[
3
]
. Additionally, let
\Delta:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<1\}
represent the open unit disk in the complex plane, and let
\Delta^{\ast}:=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
denote the punctured unit disk.
The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f^{o}:(X^{o},B^{o}),A^{o}\to S^{o}
be a strictly birationally admissible family of
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal models over a quasi-projective variety
S^{o}
, defining a quasi-finite classifying map
\xi^{o}:S^{o}\to\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
. Then the following statements hold:

•

(Big Picard Theorem)
Let
S
be a projective variety containing
S^{o}
as a Zariski open subset. Then
(S,S\setminus S^{o})
forms a Picard pair, meaning that for any holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to S
from the punctured unit disk
\Delta^{\ast}
, either
0\in\overline{\gamma^{-1}(S\setminus S^{o})}
, or
\gamma
can be extended to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma}:\Delta\to S
. This generalizes the classical Big Picard theorem, which asserts that
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{0,1,\infty\})
is a Picard pair.

•

(Borel Hyperbolicity)
Any holomorphic map from an algebraic variety to
S^{o}
is algebraic.

•

(Brody Hyperbolicity)

S^{o}
is Brody hyperbolic, i.e., there exists no non-constant holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\to S^{o}
.

A family of stable minimal models
(X^{o},B^{o}),A^{o}\to S^{o}
is said to be
strictly birationally admissible
if
(X^{o},A^{o}+B^{o})
admits a simultaneous simple normal crossing log birational model (see Definition
3.8
). More formally, the strictly birationally admissible condition ensures that the family does not contain degenerate fibers within the framework of birational geometry. This condition plays a crucial role in Theorem
1.1
, as demonstrated in
[
17
]
.

Given a functorial semi-log desingularization procedure (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
and
[
12
, §10.4]
), one can associate with
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
a stratification such that over each stratum
\mathscr{S}
, the universal family is strictly birationally admissible. Such a stratification is referred to as a
birationally admissible stratification
(cf. Section
4.1
). A birationally admissible stratification always exists but is typically not unique, as it depends on the choice of the functorial desingularization functor. A typical example arises in the KSBA compactification of the moduli space of principally polarized manifolds. In this case, the open substack parameterizing smooth fibers constitutes a birationally admissible stratum, while the birationally admissible stratification on the boundary is determined by the semi-log desingularization process.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be a stratum of a birationally admissible stratification of
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
. Denote by
\overline{\mathscr{S}}
its closure and set
\partial\mathscr{S}:=\overline{\mathscr{S}}\setminus\mathscr{S}
. Then the following statements hold:

•

(Big Picard Theorem)

(\overline{\mathscr{S}},\partial\mathscr{S})
forms a Picard pair, meaning that for any holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to\mathscr{S}^{\rm an}
, either
0\in\overline{\gamma^{-1}(\partial\mathscr{S})}
, or
\gamma\circ\iota_{n}
can be extended to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma\circ\iota_{n}}:\Delta\to\overline{\mathscr{S}}^{\rm an}
for some
n
, where
\iota_{n}:\Delta^{\ast}\to\Delta^{\ast}
is defined by
\iota_{n}(z)=z^{n}
.

•

(Borel Hyperbolicity)
Any holomorphic map from an algebraic stack to
\mathscr{S}
is algebraic.

•

(Brody Hyperbolicity)

\mathscr{S}
is Brody hyperbolic, i.e., there exists no non-constant holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\to\mathscr{S}^{\rm an}
.

For
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{g,n}
, the moduli stack of genus
g
stable curves with
n
marked points satisfying
2g-2+n>0
, the boundary simple normal crossing divisor
\partial\mathscr{M}_{g,n}:=\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{g,n}\setminus\mathscr{M}_{g,n}
induces a
canonical stratification
on
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{g,n}
, where
\mathscr{M}_{g,n}
represents the dense stratum. The distinct strata of this stratification correspond exactly to the distinct topological types of the fibers. This stratification is birationally admissible. As a result, we obtain the following theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be a stratum of the canonical stratification of
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{g,n}
. Then the following statements hold:

•

(Big Picard Theorem)

(\overline{\mathscr{S}},\partial\mathscr{S})
forms a Picard pair.

•

(Borel Hyperbolicity)
Any holomorphic map from an algebraic stack to
\mathscr{S}
is algebraic.

•

(Brody Hyperbolicity)

\mathscr{S}
is Brody hyperbolic.

1.1.
Historical accounts

The
big Picard theorem
originates from the classical big Picard theorem, which asserts that any holomorphic map from the punctured disk
\Delta^{\ast}
into
\mathbb{P}^{1}
omitting three distinct points can be extended to a holomorphic map
\Delta\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In a recent work by Deng-Lu-Sun-Zuo
[
6
]
, it was established that the big Picard theorem applies to the base of a maximal variational family of good minimal manifolds.

The
Borel hyperbolicity
is a direct formal consequence of the big Picard theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 3.13]
).

The
Brody hyperbolicity
, in the context of families of canonically polarized manifolds, was established by Viehweg-Zuo
[
18
]
through their construction of systems of Higgs sheaves. This result was subsequently extended by Popa-Taji-Wu
[
16
]
to families of general type minimal manifolds, utilizing constructions similar to those in
[
15
]
. These findings were further generalized by Deng
[
7
]
to encompass families of good minimal manifolds. Additionally, Wei-Wu
[
19
]
investigated the Brody hyperbolicity of log smooth families of pairs of log general type. For a more comprehensive historical overview of the hyperbolicity problem for moduli of varieties, readers are referred to
[
6
]
.

2.
Hodge theoretic big Picard theorem

In this section, we extend the work of Deng-Lu-Sun-Zuo
[
6
]
to the context of variations of mixed Hodge structures and propose a systematic framework for constructing hyperbolic spaces, contingent upon the existence of a particular admissible variation of mixed Hodge structure. These results enable us to establish hyperbolicity properties for the base spaces of families of singular varieties. The principal result of this section is presented in Theorem
2.8
.

2.1.
Variation of mixed Hodge structure

For the purposes of this paper, we focus on the differential-geometric properties. Consequently, in this section, we consider graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variations of mixed Hodge structure and disregard their underlying
\mathbb{Z}
-structure, even if such a structure exists.

Let
S
be a complex manifold. Denote by
\mathscr{A}^{k}_{S}
the sheaf of
C^{\infty}

k
-forms on
S
, and by
\mathscr{A}^{p,q}_{S}
the sheaf of
C^{\infty}

(p,q)
-forms on
S
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
pre-variation of Hodge structures

\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,F^{\bullet})
of weight
m
on
S
consists of the following data:

•

An
\mathbb{R}
-local system
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
on
S
, together with a holomorphic flat connection
(V,\nabla)
corresponding to
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.

•

A regular, decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}
of holomorphic subbundles of
V
, such that each graded quotient
F^{p}/F^{p+1}
is locally free, and for every point
s\in S
, the fiber
\mathbb{V}(s):=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}(s),F^{\bullet}(s))
forms a pure
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
m
.

An
\mathbb{R}
-polarization
on
\mathbb{V}
is a bilinear map between
\mathbb{R}
-local systems

Q:\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{S}(-m),

which induces an
\mathbb{R}
-polarization on
\mathbb{V}(s)
for every point
s\in S
.

A
variation of Hodge structures
of weight
m
is a pre-variation of Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,F^{\bullet})
of weight
m
satisfying the Griffiths transversality condition:

\nabla(F^{p})\subset F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{S},\quad\forall p.

Definition 2.2
.

A
pre-variation of mixed Hodge structures

\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
on
S
consists of the following data:

•

An
\mathbb{R}
-local system
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
on
S
, together with a finite increasing filtration of local subsystems

of
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

A holomorphic flat connection
(V,\nabla)
on
S
, along with a finite increasing filtration of sub flat connections

, such that
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},W_{\bullet,\mathbb{R}})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
corresponds to
(V,\nabla,W_{\bullet})
via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.

•

A finite decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}
of holomorphic subbundles of
V
,

such that

{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\mathbb{V}):=({\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}),{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(V),{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\nabla),F^{\bullet}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m})

forms a pre-variation of Hodge structures of weight
m
.

A
variation of mixed Hodge structures
on
S
is a pre-variation of mixed Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
on
S
satisfying the Griffiths transversality condition:

\nabla(F^{p})\subset F^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{S},\quad\forall p.

A
graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarization
of a variation of mixed Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
consists of an
\mathbb{R}
-polarization on each

-polarization.

2.2.
Admissible variation of mixed Hodge structure

Let
\overline{S}
be a complex manifold, and let
E\subset\overline{S}
be a simple normal crossing divisor. Define
S:=\overline{S}\setminus E
.

Recall that for a flat connection
(V,\nabla)
on
S
, the lower canonical extension of
(V,\nabla)
to
\overline{S}
is a logarithmic connection

\nabla:\widetilde{V}\to\widetilde{V}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E),

which extends
(V,\nabla)
such that the real parts of the eigenvalues of the residue map along each component of

lie in the interval
[0,1)
. According to
[
5

, the lower canonical extension always exists and is unique up to isomorphism. Let
\widetilde{W}_{m}
denote the lower canonical extension of
W_{m}
. For the notion of admissibility, we follow Kashiwara
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
be a graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of mixed Hodge structures on the punctured unit disc
\Delta^{\ast}
. The variation
\mathbb{V}
is called

with respect to
\Delta

•

The monodromy operator of
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}}
at the origin is quasi-unipotent.

•

The logarithm
N
of the unipotent part of the residue map
{\rm Res}_{0}(\nabla)
admits a weight filtration relative to
\widetilde{W}_{\bullet}(0)
.

•

The filtration
F^{\bullet}
extends to a filtration
\widetilde{F}^{\bullet}
of subbundles of
\widetilde{V}
such that
{\rm Gr}_{\widetilde{F}}^{p}{\rm Gr}^{\widetilde{W}}_{m}\widetilde{V}
is locally free for all
p
and
m
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
be a graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S
. The variation
\mathbb{V}
is called

with respect to

if, for every holomorphic map
f:\Delta\to\overline{S}
such that

, the pullback
(f|_{\Delta^{\ast}})^{\ast}(\mathbb{V})
is pre-admissible with respect to
\Delta
.

Proposition 2.5
.

(Kashiwara
[

Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})

-polarized variation of mixed Hodge structures on

admits an extension

on

(1)

is a subbundle of

for all

(2)

(3)

\nabla(\widetilde{F}^{p})\subset\widetilde{F}^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E),\quad\forall p.

2.3.
The associated logarithmic Higgs bundle

Let
\overline{S}
be a complex manifold, and let
E\subset\overline{S}
be a simple normal crossing divisor. Define
S:=\overline{S}\setminus E
. Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
be a variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S
, which is admissible with respect to
\overline{S}
. Let
(\widetilde{V},\nabla)
(resp.
(\widetilde{W}_{\bullet},\nabla)
) denote the lower canonical extension of
(V,\nabla)
(resp.
(W_{\bullet},\nabla)
). Let
\widetilde{F}^{\bullet}
be the extension of
F^{\bullet}
guaranteed by Proposition
2.5
, which satisfies Griffiths transversality:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\nabla(\widetilde{F}^{p})\subset\widetilde{F}^{p-1}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E),\quad\forall p.

Define

\widetilde{H}^{p}:={\rm Gr}_{\widetilde{F}}^{p}(\widetilde{V})=\widetilde{F}^{p}/\widetilde{F}^{p+1},\quad\forall p.

Then (
2.1
) implies that
\nabla
induces a
\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}
-linear morphism

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta:\widetilde{H}^{p}\to\widetilde{H}^{p-1}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E),\quad\forall p.

Set
\widetilde{H}:=\bigoplus_{p}\widetilde{H}^{p}
, and let

\theta:\widetilde{H}\to\widetilde{H}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E)

denote the induced map. The flatness of
\nabla
(i.e.,
\nabla^{2}=0
) ensures that
\theta^{2}=0
. Consequently,
(\widetilde{H},\theta)
defines a logarithmic Higgs bundle on
(\overline{S},E)
.

Definition 2.6
.

We refer to
(\widetilde{H},\theta)
as the
lower canonical system of logarithmic Higgs bundles
associated with the admissible variation of mixed Hodge structure
\mathbb{V}
.

The logarithmic Higgs bundle
(\widetilde{H},\theta)
is equipped with a weight filtration of Higgs subbundles. For each
k
and
p
, let

\{W_{m}(\widetilde{H}^{p})=\widetilde{W}_{m}({\rm Gr}_{\widetilde{F}}^{p}(\widetilde{V}))\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}

denote the induced weight filtration, and define

W_{m}(\widetilde{H}):=\bigoplus_{p\leq w}W_{m}(\widetilde{H}^{p}).

Then
(W_{m}(\widetilde{H}),\theta)
forms a logarithmic Higgs subbundle, i.e.,

\theta(W_{m}(\widetilde{H}))\subset W_{m}(\widetilde{H})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E).

This construction yields the sub-quotient logarithmic Higgs bundles

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta:{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\widetilde{H})\to{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\widetilde{H})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{\overline{S}}}\Omega_{\overline{S}}(\log E).

These define the lower canonical system of logarithmic Higgs bundles associated with the variation of pure Hodge structure
{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m}(\mathbb{V})
.

2.4.
Hodge theoretic big Picard theorem

Let
S
be a smooth projective variety, and let
D\subset E\subset S
denote two closed algebraic subsets. Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
represent a graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S\setminus E
, which is admissible with respect to
S
. Define
w=\max\{p\mid F^{p}\neq 0\}
as the maximal index associated with the Hodge filtration.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that
\mathbb{V}=(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{R}},V,\nabla,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
has
maximal variation with respect to
(S,D)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

There exists a closed algebraic subset
Z\subset S
such that
\mathrm{codim}_{S}(Z)\geq 2
, and both
D\setminus Z
and
E\setminus Z
are simple normal crossing divisors on
S\setminus Z
,

•

The lower canonical system of logarithmic Higgs bundles
(\widetilde{H}=\bigoplus_{p\leq w}\widetilde{H}^{p},\theta)
is defined on
(S\setminus Z,E\setminus Z)
and associated with
\mathbb{V}
,

•

An ample line bundle
L
on
S
and an injective morphism
L|_{S\setminus Z}\to\widetilde{H}^{w}
exist,

such that the Higgs subsheaf

\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}L^{p}\subset\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\widetilde{H}^{w-p},\quad L^{p}\subset\widetilde{H}^{w-p},

generated by
L^{0}=L|_{S\setminus Z}
, has logarithmic poles along
D
, i.e.,

\theta(L^{p})\subset L^{p+1}\otimes\Omega_{S\setminus Z}(\log(D\setminus Z)),\quad\forall p\geq 0.

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective variety, and let
D\subset E\subset S
denote two closed algebraic subsets. Assume that there exists a graded
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of mixed Hodge structures on
S\setminus E
, which is admissible with respect to
S
and has maximal variation with respect to
(S,D)
. Let
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to S\setminus D
be a holomorphic map with Zariski-dense image. Then
\gamma
extends to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma}:\Delta\to X
.

The proof of Theorem
2.8

[

2.4.1.

Using the notations and assumptions introduced in Definition
2.7

(3)

Proposition 2.11
.

With the same notations as above,
|\frac{\partial}{\partial z}|^{2}_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}
is not identically zero, and the following inequality holds in the sense of currents:

\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right|^{2}_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}\geq\frac{1}{n}\gamma^{\ast}(\Theta_{g_{\alpha}}(L)).

Proof.

Observe that
\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}
defines a (possibly degenerate) generically smooth continuous Hermitian metric on
T_{\Delta^{\ast}}
. The first claim follows from the fact that
\tau_{\gamma,n_{\gamma}}
is nonzero.

By the Poincaré-Lelong equation, we have

\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right|^{2}_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}=-\Theta_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}(T_{\Delta^{\ast}})+R,

where
R
denotes the ramification divisor of
\gamma
. Let

denote the saturation of the image of
d\gamma:T_{\Delta^{\ast}}\to\gamma^{\ast}T_{\widetilde{S}}(-\log\pi^{-1}(D))

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}(T_{\Delta^{\ast}})

\displaystyle\leq\Theta_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}}(N)=\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\Theta_{\gamma^{\ast}h_{\gamma}^{n_{\gamma}}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{h_{\gamma}^{n_{\gamma}}}\left(T^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}_{\widetilde{S}}(-\log\pi^{-1}(D))\right)\big|_{N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{g_{\alpha}^{-1}h_{m_{0}}}\left(\widetilde{L}^{-1}\otimes{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(\widetilde{H}^{n_{\gamma}})\right)\big|_{\gamma^{\ast}(\iota_{n_{\gamma}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}))}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{g_{\alpha}}(\widetilde{L})+\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}}\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{h_{m_{0}}}\left({\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(\widetilde{H}^{n_{\gamma}})\right)\big|_{\gamma^{\ast}(\iota_{n_{\gamma}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}))},

as
(1,1)
-forms on

By the definition of

, it follows that
\gamma^{\ast}(\iota_{n_{\gamma}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}))
lies in the kernel of the Higgs field

\theta_{\gamma}:\gamma^{\ast}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(\widetilde{H}^{n_{\gamma}})\to\gamma^{\ast}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(\widetilde{H}^{n_{\gamma}+1})\otimes\Omega_{\Delta^{\ast}}

of the Higgs sheaf

\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{h_{m_{0}}}({\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(H))+\theta_{\gamma}\wedge\overline{\theta_{\gamma}}+\overline{\theta_{\gamma}}\wedge\theta_{\gamma}=0,

we deduce that

\gamma^{\ast}\Theta_{h_{m_{0}}}\left({\rm Gr}^{W}_{m_{0}}(H^{n_{\gamma}})\right)\big|_{\gamma^{\ast}(\iota_{n_{\gamma}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}))}=-\theta_{\gamma}\wedge\overline{\theta_{\gamma}}\big|_{\gamma^{\ast}(\iota_{n_{\gamma}}(N^{\otimes n_{\gamma}}))}\leq 0.

Combining the above formulas, the proposition is proved.

3.

3.1.
Stable minimal models and their moduli

We now review the main results from
[
3
]
that will be utilized in the subsequent sections. For the purposes of this article, all schemes are defined over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
. A
stable minimal model
is a triple
(X,B),A
, where
X
is a reduced, connected, projective scheme of finite type over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, and
A,B\geq 0
are
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors satisfying the following conditions:

•

(X,B)
is a projective, connected slc (semi-log-canonical) pair,

•

K_{X}+B
is semi-ample,

•

K_{X}+B+tA
is ample for some
t>0
, and

•

(X,B+tA)
is slc for some
t>0
.

Let

d\in\mathbb{N},\,c\in\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0},\,\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Q}^{>0}\text{ a finite set, and }\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}[t].

A
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal model is a stable minimal model
(X,B),A
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\dim X=d
,

•

the coefficients of
A
and
B
belong to
c\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
,

•

{\rm vol}(A|_{F})\in\Gamma
, where
F
is any general fiber of the fibration
f:X\to Z
determined by
K_{X}+B
, and

•

{\rm vol}(K_{X}+B+tA)=\sigma(t)
for
0\leq t\ll 1
.

Let
S
be a reduced scheme over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
. A family of
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal models over
S
consists of a projective morphism
X\to S
of schemes and
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
A
and
B
on
X
, satisfying the following conditions:

•

(X,B+tA)\to S
is a locally stable family (i.e.,
K_{X/S}+B+tA
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier) for every sufficiently small rational number
t\geq 0
,

•

A=cN
,
B=cD
, where
N,D\geq 0
are relative Mumford divisors, and

•

(X_{s},B_{s}),A_{s}
is a
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal model for each point
s\in S
.

Let
{\rm Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\rm red}
denote the category of reduced schemes defined over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
. Define

\mathscr{M}^{\rm red}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma):S\mapsto\{\text{families of }(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)\text{-stable minimal models over }S\},

a functor of groupoids over

.

Theorem 3.1

(Birkar
[
3
]
)
.

There exists a proper Deligne-Mumford stack

over

such that the following properties hold:

•

\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)|_{{\rm Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\rm red}}=\mathscr{M}^{\rm red}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)

•

admits a projective good coarse moduli space

Proof.

See the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 1.14]
. Using the notations in
[
3
, §10.7]
, we have

\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)=\left[M_{\rm slc}^{e}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma,a,r,\mathbb{P}^{n})/{\rm PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\right],

where the right-hand side denotes the stacky quotient.

3.3.

The objective of this section is to define the notion of an admissible family of stable minimal models and to review the Viehweg-Zuo Higgs sheaf associated with such families (Theorem
3.9
). Intuitively, the admissibility condition implies that the family admits an equisingular birational model.

Definition 3.4
.

A

(X,\Delta)
consists of a reduced scheme of finite type over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, of pure dimension, together with a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

on

(1)

is an

-scheme with nodal singularities in codimension one,

(2)

is contained in the singular locus of

(3)

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

A

is a semi-pair
(X,\Delta)
where

is a projective scheme.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective semi-pair. A morphism between projective semi-pairs

is said to be a

\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
is a semi-log resolution of singularities (

2
]

-divisor

satisfying the relation

.

Definition 3.6

(Simple normal crossing family)
.

Let

of

that can be embedded into a scheme

S
. In this embedding,

admits a regular system of parameters
(z_{1},\dots,z_{p},y_{1},\dots,y_{r})
over

z_{1}\cdots z_{p}=0
and

is said to be a

. A family is referred to as a

\theta(L^{p})\subset L^{p+1}\otimes\Omega_{U}(\log D\cap U),\quad p\geq 0.

4.
Hyperbolicity properties for admissible families of stable minimal models

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a complex space, and let
Z\subset X
be a closed analytic subset. The pair
(X,Z)
is called a
Picard pair
if, for any holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to X
from the punctured unit disc
\Delta^{\ast}
, either
0\in\overline{\gamma^{-1}(Z)}
, or
\gamma
can be extended to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma}:\Delta\to X
.

Let
(X,Z)
be a Picard pair. Then any holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to X\setminus Z
extends to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma}:\Delta\to X
. The classical Big Picard theorem is equivalent to the statement that
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{0,1,\infty\})
is a Picard pair.

Definition 4.2
.

A complex space
X
is called
Brody hyperbolic
if there is no non-constant holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\to X
.

Definition 4.3

(Javanpeykar-Kucharczyk
[
9
]
)
.

An algebraic variety
X
is called
Borel hyperbolic
if any holomorphic map from an algebraic variety to
X
is algebraic.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(X,Z)
be a Picard pair where
X
is an algebraic variety. Then
X\backslash Z
is Borel hyperbolic and Brody hyperbolic.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Theorem 3.13]
.
∎

Readers are referred to
[
9
]
for further details on the relationships between various hyperbolicity conditions.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

4.1.
Stratified hyperbolicity of
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)

In
[
17
]
, we introduced the concept of a birationally admissible stratification of
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
. Let
f:(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{B}),\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
denote the universal family of
(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
-stable minimal models.
A
birationally admissible stratification
of
\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma)
is a filtration by Zariski-closed, reduced substacks:

\emptyset=S_{-1}\subset S_{0}\subset\cdots\subset S_{N}=\mathscr{M}_{\rm slc}(d,\Phi_{c},\Gamma,\sigma),

such that for each
i=0,\dots,N
, the pullback family of
f
to
S_{i}\setminus S_{i-1}
is strictly birationally admissible. A stratum is defined as a connected component of
S_{i}
for some
i\in\{0,\dots,N\}
. Given that the moduli space of smooth varieties may itself lack smoothness, we do not impose the requirement that a birationally admissible stratification must be regular, meaning that every stratum is smooth.

A birationally admissible stratification always exists but is typically not unique; it is determined by the choice of a functorial desingularization functor (
[
17
, §6]
).

For the purposes of this paper, an algebraic stack is defined as a stack that can be represented as the quotient stack of a groupoid of algebraic spaces
s,t:R\to S
of finite type over
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, with smooth structure morphisms
s,t
.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack; we denote by
\mathscr{X}^{\rm an}
its associated complex analytic stack (
[
14
,
1
]
).

The hyperbolicity conditions discussed in the previous section admit natural generalizations to Deligne-Mumford stacks. These generalizations are of independent interest due to their moduli interpretations.

4.1.1.
Picard stacky pair

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a reduced Deligne-Mumford stack, and let
\mathscr{Z}\subset\mathscr{X}
be a closed substack. The pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Z})
is called a
Picard pair
if the following condition holds: given a holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to\mathscr{X}^{\rm an}
, either
0\in\overline{\gamma^{-1}(\mathscr{Z})}
, or there exists some
n
such that
\gamma\circ\iota_{n}
can be extended to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma\circ\iota_{n}}:\Delta\to\mathscr{X}^{\rm an}
, where
\iota_{n}:\Delta^{\ast}\to\Delta^{\ast}
is defined by
\iota_{n}(z)=z^{n}
.

Let
\mu_{n}=\{e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}k}{n}}:k=0,1,\dots,n-1\}
act on
\Delta
by multiplication. Then the stacky quotient
[\Delta/\mu_{n}]
is a unit disc extending
\Delta^{\ast}
, equipped with a stacky structure at the origin. The pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Z})
is a
Picard pair
if and only if for every holomorphic map
\gamma:\Delta^{\ast}\to\mathscr{X}^{\rm an}
, either
0\in\overline{\gamma^{-1}(\mathscr{Z})}
, or there exists some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\gamma
can be extended to a holomorphic map
\overline{\gamma}:[\Delta/\mu_{n}]\to\mathscr{X}^{\rm an}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

. Denote by

Let

\overline{S}
. Define

and the associated base change diagram:

\scriptstyle{g}

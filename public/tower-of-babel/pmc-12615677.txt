Performance augmentation of a double-coil heat exchanger: analyzing the impact of radial fin count and diameter ratio
Authors: Badreddine Ayadi, Nashmi H. Alrasheedi, Ali M. Mohsen, As’ad Alizadeh, Shaymaa Abed Hussein, Seyed Hossein Hashemi Karouei, Walid Aich, Khalil Hajlaoui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The growing demand for high-efficiency, compact heat exchangers in various industrial applications necessitates innovative solutions for thermal performance enhancement. This study aims to numerically investigate the thermal-hydraulic performance of a shell-and-double-helical-coil heat exchanger by integrating annular fins on the shell side. We systematically explore the influence of two critical geometric parameters: the number of annular fins (120, 160, and 200) and the fin outer-to-inner diameter ratio (Do, fin/Di, fin, varied across 1.25, 1.50, and 1.75). A three-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model was utilized to rigorously analyze the average convective heat transfer coefficient (h), Nusselt number (Nu), pressure drop (ΔP), and overall thermal performance (η) across a wide range of Reynolds numbers. The results demonstrate that the addition of annular fins significantly augments heat transfer due to enhanced fluid mixing and the generation of secondary flow structures. Specifically, the 200-fin configuration achieved a thermal performance (η) of approximately 2.1 at Re = 2000, representing a 110% improvement over the unfinned case. Furthermore, varying the fin diameter ratio revealed that the 1.75 ratio yielded the highest thermal performance, reaching approximately 2.2 at Re = 2000, a 120% enhancement over the unfinned coil. These findings underscore the critical role of fin geometry in improving the thermo-hydraulic performance of compact heat exchangers.

Introduction

Heat exchangers play a critical part in enhancing thermal energy transfer across various heavy-duty systems applications, including HVAC systems, chemical processing, and power generation1. Among the various designs of heat exchangers, the shell and double tube configuration is commonly utilized due to its compact form and effective heat transfer abilities. The thermal effectiveness of these heat exchangers can be greatly enhanced by the addition of fins, which expand the effective heat transfer surface area and encourage turbulence in the fluid flow2. The selection of fin type, be it radial, longitudinal, or helical—significantly affects both thermal characteristics and fluid dynamics. Radial fins, in particular, are specified for their capacity to advance heat transfer by generating vortices and disrupting thermal boundary layers, thus increasing convective heat transfer coefficients3. Nonetheless, their geometric layout also brings about extra pressure drops and flow resistance, requiring careful consideration of the trade-off between thermal benefits and hydrodynamic drawbacks. Exploring the influence of radial fins on fluid dynamics and thermal properties in a shell and double tube heat exchanger is crucial for refining design parameters and attaining enhanced energy efficiency. This research intends to numerically evaluate how radial fins affect temperature distribution, heat transfer rates, and pressure losses, offering important insights for designers of thermal systems.

Previous investigations concentrated on using various geometries of fins on the thermal behavior of a shell/tube heat exchangers (STHEs)4–6. In this case, Amini et al.7 conducted a numerical study on how fin geometry affects the thermal characteristics of STHEs, comparing segmented vertical fins to an innovative helical fin design. The research indicated that the shape of the fins plays a vital part in enhancing heat transfer characteristics, containing helical fins outperforming segmented fins by improving the Nusselt number by 35% under the same conditions. Significant results indicated that decreasing fin pitch and increasing fin height improved heat transfer by facilitating vortex generation near the tube walls, with helical fins achieving a 9.5% rise in overall efficiency (79.83% compared to 70.37% for plain tubes) and a 17% boost in effectiveness. Additionally, the helical design led to a reduction in pressure drop compared to segmented fins due to its continuous structure, which lessened flow disruption. The authors illustrated that optimizing fin geometry, particularly utilizing helical fins with a smaller pitch (0.01 m) and greater height (0.03 m), can greatly enhance thermal characteristics while alleviating the trade-off between improved heat transfer and pressure drop. These findings highlight the essential role of fin geometry in achieving a balance between efficiency, pressure loss, and practical factors such as corrosion resistance in the design of heat exchangers. Karouei et al.8 performed an investigation on a heat exchanger featuring a shell with a dual-coil configuration, utilizing a hybrid nanofluid composed of magnesium oxide and titanium dioxide dispersed in water. The study demonstrated that directing the heated fluid through the outer coil led to a significant boost in HT efficiency. Notably, at a Reynolds number of 500, the thermal enhancement reached as high as 158.6% relative to the performance observed with plain water. In this context, Sanchouli et al.9 examined thermal characteristics improvement in a double-tube heat exchanger utilizing phase change material (PCM) by contrasting traditional annular fins with innovative grid annular fins. The grid fins, made of both straight and circular strip elements, were designed to enhance the contact surface area and thermal penetration depth while keeping the fin volume identical to that of conventional fins. The findings indicated that substituting conventional fins with grid fins considerably boosted thermal characteristics, causing a decrease in melting time by 27–69.5%, depending on the fin spacing (from 125 mm to 15.625 mm). For a fin volume of 0.5%, the ideal fin spacing was determined to be 31.25 mm, resulting in a 40% decrease in melting time when compared to the largest spacing. Furthermore, a non-uniform distribution of the grid volume fraction contributed to an additional reduction in melting time by as much as 16%. The results illustrated that grid fins not only expedited the melting process but also enhanced energy storage capacity, particularly within shorter timeframes, with case 8 (grid fins, 15.625 mm spacing) storing 914 kJ in 25 min, as opposed to 139 kJ for conventional fins. The researchers determined that the shape of fins greatly influences thermal characteristics, with grid fins enhancing heat transfer through improved conduction and convection, rendering them especially efficient for thermal energy storage systems based on PCM. Esmaeili et al.10 examined the development of thermal characteristics in triplex tube HE by utilizing innovative tree-shaped fin structures (TSFS) with different branching techniques for thermal energy storage containing PCM. The research evaluated five unique TSFS designs in comparison to the traditional Y-shaped branching, demonstrating that the geometry of the fins meaningfully affects the dynamics of phase change. The best-performing design (Case c) achieved a decrease in melting and solidification durations by 7.36% and 23.70%, respectively, when compared to the conventional Y-branching, while also improving heat distribution and preventing localized stagnation of the PCM. The authors credited these improvements to the novel geometry of the branching, which optimizes conductive pathways and alleviates the limitations of natural convection during solidification. Importantly, the study indicated that the angle and arrangement of the branches (such as tripartite versus Y-shaped splits) notably influence thermal uniformity and the rates of phase transition, with some geometries exceeding the characteristics of nature-inspired designs. These results highlight that modifications in fin geometry can significantly improve the utilization of latent heat in TTHEs, with the optimization of branching patterns emerging as a critical factor for enhancing energy storage density and response times in PCM systems. The research emphasizes how strategic geometric innovations can address the inherent limitations of standard fin designs in applications involving thermal energy storage. Karouei et al.11 analyzed the thermal behavior of SCTHE incorporating conical coil geometries. The comparative assessment of various designs indicated that the divergent cone configuration delivered superior heat transfer efficiency, outperforming the convergent variant by as much as 64.1%. Furthermore, Jasim et al.12 conducted a numerical investigation of a SCTHE equipped with dual helical coils, examining the influence of several hybrid nanofluid mixtures. Their findings indicated that a water-based suspension containing magnesium oxide and titanium dioxide nanoparticles achieved the most effective thermal results. Additionally, elevating the nanoparticle volume fraction up to 0.5 led to further enhancements in heat transfer efficiency, underscoring the combined effect of nanoparticle interaction and the specific coil design on thermal performance. Conical coil heat exchangers are gaining recognition for their ability to induce secondary flows and enhance heat convection, but a significant research gap exists regarding the optimal configurations and most effective nanofluids for these systems. A recent numerical study by Wajdi Rajhi et al.13 addressed this by investigating the thermal performance of a HE. The research examined three different coil designs: a simple conical coil (Case C), a coil with an external helical fin (Case B), and a coil with a twisted tape insert (Case A). In addition, the study evaluated three distinct hybrid nanofluids and pure water. The findings indicated that the SWCNT-MWCNT/Water nanofluid consistently delivered the best performance, outperforming other nanofluids and pure water by up to 21.9% and 9.4%, respectively. Furthermore, the study concluded that the twisted tape insert (Case A) was the most effective geometrical enhancement. At a high Dean number of 4200, Case A demonstrated a 52.7% thermal performance advantage over the simple coil (Case C) and a 10.5% advantage over the helical fin design (Case B). These results provide compelling evidence for the efficacy of twisted tape inserts and specific hybrid nanofluids in enhancing the performance of conical heat exchangers. A study by Haydar A.S. Aljaafari et al.14 on a shell-and-coil heat exchanger demonstrated that both geometrical modifications and the use of a nanohybrid fluid significantly enhance thermal performance. Their findings revealed that adding external helical fins substantially boosted efficiency, with the four-finned design providing the best results. Furthermore, the use of a Water/MgO_TiO_2 nanohybrid fluid in a counter-flow arrangement yielded a remarkable increase in thermal efficiency, highlighting the combined benefits of advanced fluid and design modifications. A study by Mohammed Al-Mosallam et al.15 provides a detailed analysis of a shell and coil heat exchanger. The researchers first investigated the impact of using two different types of hybrids nanofluids, noting that both significantly improved thermal efficiency over pure water. The Al2O3_MWCNT/water nanofluid, in particular, demonstrated a remarkable 79% increase in the PEC, while the water/Ag_HEG fluid showed a 21% improvement in this key performance metric. The study also examined the geometric configuration, comparing spiral coils with and without fins. Results showed that incorporating external fins and a specific coil design led to enhanced fluid mixing and substantially higher heat transfer rates. This was quantified by a significant increase in overall efficiency, with specific designs showing performance improvements of up to 152% at a Reynolds number of 250 compared to the simple coil, confirming the considerable benefits of these design modifications. A recent study by Zakarya Ahmed et al.2 investigated the hydrothermal performance of a double helical shell-and-tube HE, with particular relevance to energy consumption in the built environment. Their research focused on the combined effects of integrating hybrid nanofluids and turbulence-inducing inserts, revealing that this approach significantly improves heat transfer through enhanced fluid mixing. The study found that using a water/MgO–TiO₂ nanohybrid fluid as the working fluid was particularly effective, yielding the highest thermal efficiency for the heat exchanger. The analysis also highlighted that a spiral coil with a larger diameter resulted in superior thermal performance. The findings were quantified by significant performance gains, with one configuration (Case [E]) showing a remarkable 156% increase in thermal efficiency at a Reynolds number of 500 compared to other tested designs. Similarly, substantial improvements of 152%, 135%, and 132% were observed in other cases at the same Reynolds number, confirming that incorporating a double spiral coil with spiral fins is a highly effective strategy for boosting thermal efficiency. In another numerical study, Sara Jumah Flayh et al.16 examined the thermal and hydraulic performance of a shell-and-tube heat exchanger by focusing on two key enhancement methods: optimizing geometrical design and varying fluid inlet orientation. The study explored several finned configurations, specifically those with two, three, and four external fins, and compared their performance to an unfinned coil. The researchers also evaluated the most effective finned design under four different flow conditions: Horizontal and Vertical, in both counter and parallel flow arrangements. The findings highlighted the significant impact of fluid inlet orientation on performance. At a Reynolds number of 2000, the Horizontal Counter-Flow and Vertical Counter-Flow arrangements achieved remarkable efficiency gains of 95% and 77%, respectively, compared to a simple coil. Furthermore, the addition of external fins proved highly effective, promoting flow mixing and enhancing heat transfer. A noteworthy 77% thermal performance gain over the unfinned coil was exhibited by the configuration with four external spiral fins, making it the most effective design at the same Reynolds number. This research conclusively demonstrates that strategic fluid inlet positioning and optimized fin geometry can be a powerful combination for designing high-performance shell-and-tube heat exchangers. The research emphasizes the promise of geometric optimization in advancing thermal energy technologies for applications in renewable energy. Similar previous investigations have confirmed the importance of fine design in the thermal behavior of heat exchangers17–19.

The literature review in this section highlights the essential role of fin geometry in improving the thermal properties of shell and tube heat exchangers (STHEs). Research shows that different types of fins, such as helical, Y-shaped, twisted fins, as well as nanohybrid fluids as passive methods, are very effective not only in heat transfer but also in investigating thermal and hydraulic parameters in aircraft fins by thinning the boundary layers, increasing turbulence, and increasing the thermal surface area20–24. However, radial fins (radial fins) remain relatively understudied, even though they can optimize vorticity (vortex) and thermal uniformity in two-tube designs. This research aims to address this gap by studying innovative radial fin designs and exploiting their geometric advantages to achieve improved convection and hydrodynamic properties. By integrating findings from previous optimizations with advanced computational techniques, this study sought to introduce radial fins as promising options for enhancing the performance of heat exchangers in industrial environments.

The continuous pursuit of energy efficiency in thermal systems necessitates innovative heat exchanger designs that transcend conventional limitations. Building upon established principles of passive heat transfer enhancement, this study introduces and rigorously evaluates a distinctive shell-and-double-helical-coil heat exchanger featuring strategically integrated external annular fins. Unlike prior works, this research delves into the nuanced interplay between the fin geometry and overall thermal performance. The central aim of this investigation is to meticulously dissect how key geometric parameters of these annular fins, namely, their total count (explored at 200, 160, and 120 fins) and their outer-to-inner diameter proportion (investigated at ratios of 1.25, 1.5, and 1.75), govern the heat transfer rates and hydraulic characteristics. This detailed exploration is designed to yield critical insights for maximizing the thermal energy recovery and operational efficiency of compact heat exchangers in various industrial sectors.

Computational modeling and boundary conditions

The thermal-hydraulic behavior of the proposed heat exchanger was computationally modeled using a three-dimensional domain to accurately predict its performance. The simulations were performed under steady-state conditions, with the fluid flow and heat transfer governed by the fundamental conservation equations for mass, momentum, and energy. All fluid properties were treated as constant, consistent with the specified operating temperatures.

For the boundary conditions, a fixed mass flow rate was applied at the inlet, representing the known fluid entry conditions. Conversely, the outlet was defined with a constant static pressure to allow the flow to exit the domain naturally. At all solid walls and fin surfaces, the no-slip condition was applied for the fluid velocity. The heat transfer across the fluid–solid interfaces was addressed through a conjugate heat transfer approach, which simultaneously solves for conduction within the solid components and convection within the fluid. The entire exterior surface of the heat exchanger was assumed to be perfectly insulated. The computational mesh was carefully constructed, employing unstructured tetrahedral cells with specific inflation layers near the walls to ensure the accurate resolution of near-wall gradients. Boundary conditions

The computational domain was subjected to specific boundary conditions to replicate realistic operating conditions.

Cold fluid (shell side)

Inlet: A velocity inlet boundary condition was applied at the shell inlet. The cold fluid enters the shell with a constant Re number of 500 and a uniform temperature of 300 K. This ensures a fixed laminar flow condition for the cold stream.

Outlet: A pressure outlet boundary condition was set at the shell outlet, with a gauge pressure of 0 Pa (atmospheric pressure), allowing the flow to exit freely.

Hot fluid (double helical coil side)

Inlet: A velocity inlet boundary condition was specified at the inlet of the double helical coil. The hot fluid enters at a constant temperature of 325 K. To investigate varying flow conditions within the laminar regime, the hot fluid’s flow rate was adjusted to achieve different Reynolds numbers, specifically 500, 1000, 1500, and 2000.

Outlet: A pressure outlet boundary condition was applied at the outlet of the double helical coil, with a gauge pressure of 0 Pa, facilitating outflow.

Walls

Coil and fin walls: The interfaces between the fluid domains and the solid walls of the coil and fins were treated as coupled wall boundaries, enabling accurate conjugate heat transfer between the fluids and the solid structure (coil and fins).

Shell outer wall: The outer surface of the shell was defined as an adiabatic wall (zero heat flux) to ensure that all heat transfer interactions are confined internally between the hot and cold fluid streams, without heat loss to the surroundings.

These precisely defined boundary conditions, combined with the assumption of laminar flow and the chosen numerical methodology, provide a robust framework for analyzing the intricate heat transfer and fluid flow phenomena within the proposed heat exchanger design under the specified operating conditions.

Problem description

The efficient transfer of thermal energy is paramount across various industrial and engineering applications, driving continuous innovation in heat exchanger design. This study focuses on a novel configuration of a SCTHE (Fig. 1), specifically designed to enhance heat transfer characteristics. The unique aspect of this design lies in the integration of annular fins on the exterior surface of the helical coil, aiming to augment the heat transfer area and consequently improve thermal performance within a compact footprint.

The physical domain under investigation consists of a cylindrical shell housing a concentric double-helical coil. The inner and outer helical tubes are arranged concentrically, forming a double-tube helical configuration (as fully illustrated in Fig. 1; Table 2). Heat transfer occurs between a hot fluid flowing through the helical coils and a cold fluid circulating within the shell side. A distinguishing feature of this design is the presence of discrete annular fins strategically attached along the outer surface of the helical tubes. These fins are intended to disrupt the boundary layer development, induce secondary flows, and effectively extend the heat transfer surface area on the shell side. To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the numerical model developed in this study, a comprehensive validation procedure was undertaken. For this purpose, the average Nusselt number (Nuave​) obtained from our numerical simulations was compared with the experimental measurements conducted by Jamshidi et al.29 for a similar helical coil configuration.

This close correspondence between the numerical predictions and the empirical observations provides strong confidence in the accuracy and robustness of the developed CFD model. The validated model can therefore be reliably utilized for further parametric studies and design optimization of the shell-and-double-helical-coil heat exchanger with annular fins, ensuring that the insights derived are physically sound and representative of real-world phenomena.

Grid independence study

The accuracy of numerical simulations is highly dependent on the spatial discretization of the computational domain, particularly the density of the computational mesh. To ensure that the simulation results are independent of the mesh resolution and to optimize computational cost, a comprehensive grid independence study was conducted.

The methodology involved performing simulations on several progressively finer meshes, ranging from approximately 500,000 to 3,000,000 elements. A critical output parameter, specifically the average outlet temperature of the hot fluid, was monitored as the mesh density increased. Figure 3 illustrates the results of this grid independence study. As shown, for meshes up to approximately 1,500,000 elements, there is a noticeable variation in the predicted average outlet temperature. However, beyond a mesh size of approximately 2,000,000 elements, the change in the average outlet temperature becomes negligible, indicating that the solution has converged and is largely independent of further mesh refinement.

Based on these results, a computational mesh comprising approximately 2,000,000 elements was selected for all subsequent simulations in this study. This mesh density (Fig. 4) strikes an optimal balance between computational accuracy and reasonable processing time, ensuring that the presented results are robust and not influenced by numerical discretization errors.

Figure 7 illustrates the friction factor (f) for the internal coil flow as a function of the Reynolds number for various fin configurations on the shell side. A fundamental hydraulic characteristic evident across all designs, including the unfinned baseline, is the monotonic decrease in friction factor with rising Re. Crucially, the data reveals a significant observation: the presence of annular fins on the exterior (shell side) of the helical coil directly correlates with a higher friction factor within the internal coil flow. This phenomenon, though seemingly indirect, can be rigorously explained by considering the enhanced thermal coupling between the two fluid streams. As previously demonstrated, increasing the number of fins on the shell side significantly intensifies heat transfer from the hot fluid in the coil to the cold fluid in the shell. This superior heat extraction from the hot fluid stream leads to a more rapid decrease in its temperature as it flows through the coil.

This more pronounced thermal interaction results in increased effective flow path resistance and friction within the coil. Essentially, the enhanced heat transfer from the coil intensifies the thermal-dynamic response of the hot fluid. This heightened interaction and potentially accelerated change in fluid properties contribute to the observed rise in the friction factor. For instance, at a Reynolds number of 1500, the unfinned coil shows a friction factor of approximately 0.3, while the 200-fin configuration exhibits a considerably higher value of around 0.48 at the same Reynolds number, clearly demonstrating this relationship. The results of this study consistently demonstrate the critical role of fin geometry in enhancing the thermal-hydraulic performance of the heat exchanger. As shown in our findings, increasing the number of annular fins from the unfinned baseline to 200 significantly augments heat transfer. This enhancement is attributed to two key mechanisms: the increased heat exchange surface area and the continuous disruption of the boundary layer. This observation is well-supported by previous research on finned geometries, which has documented similar improvements in performance2,16.

Furthermore, the influence of the fin diameter ratio revealed an optimal design point. The configuration with a fin diameter ratio of 1.75 consistently yielded the highest thermal performance, showcasing the crucial trade-off between heat transfer augmentation and pressure drop penalty15. The superior performance of this geometry is a direct result of the extended radial projection of the fins, which promotes the generation of strong vortices and secondary flows. The role of these swirling flow structures in improving fluid mixing and heat transfer is a well-established phenomenon in the literature13,14. These findings collectively underscore that optimizing fin geometry is a highly effective strategy for improving the thermo-hydraulic performance of compact heat exchangers.

1.
Introduction

The sup-norm estimates for Cauchy-Riemann equation:

\overline{\partial}u=f

(1.1)

on the product domains
\Omega^{n}
have received a considerable study recently by many authors.
The research is around the classical problem posed by Kerzman
[
21
]
in 1971. After some modification (see
[
29
]
), Kerzman’s problem can be stated as follows:
For any
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
-form
f\in L^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})
,
is there
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega^{n})
such that
\overline{\partial}u=f
when
\Omega=D
is the unit disk in
{\rm\kern 2.40005pt\vrule width=0.20004pt,height=6.02773pt,depth=-0.21529pt\kern-2.59995ptC}
? The problem was studied by Henkin
[
18
]
in 1971,
who proved that if
f\in C^{1}_{(0,1)}(\overline{D^{n}})
is
\overline{\partial}
-closed, there is a scalar constant
C
and
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega^{n})
solving
\overline{\partial}
-equation (1.1) such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(D^{n})}.

(1.2)

Notice that
\{f\in C^{1}_{(0,1)}(\overline{D}^{n}):\overline{\partial}f=0\}
may not be dense in
\{f\in L^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(D^{n}):\overline{\partial}f=0\}
. So, Henkin’s
result has not solved the Kerzman’s problem.

Let
A^{2}(\Omega^{n})
denote the Bergman space consisting of all holomorphic functions
g\in L^{2}(\Omega^{n})
. A solution
u
of
\overline{\partial}
-equation (1.1) is said to be the canonical
solution if
u\perp A^{2}(\Omega^{n})
. Landucci
[
25
]
improved Henkin’s result and proved that the estimate (1.2) holds for
the canonical solution
u
. Recently, Chen and McNeal
[
4
]
introduced some new

\tilde{L}^{p}(D^{n})
and
\tilde{L}^{p}_{(0,1)}(D^{n})
and obtained
\tilde{L}^{p}
-estimate for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, but their
\tilde{L}^{\infty}
is strictly smaller than

L^{\infty}(D^{n})
.

In
[
9
]
, Dong, Pan and Zhang proved the canonical solution of
\overline{\partial}
-equation (1.1) satisfies the sup-norm estimate (1.2) when
f\in C_{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega}^{n})

and
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
. This result greatly improves the previous work in
[
25
]
. However,
\overline{\partial}
-closed forms in
L^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})

may not be approximated by
\overline{\partial}
-closed forms in
C_{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega}^{n})
.
Their result has not solved the Kerzman problem, yet. Finally, the Kerzman’s problem has been settled by Yuan
[
42
]
and Li
[
29
]
independently in 2022. They proved that the canonical solution

u
of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation
(1.1)
satisfies
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}\leq C_{\Omega}^{n}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
with the assumption
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
in
[
42
]

and
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
in
[
29
]
, respectively. In
[
29
]
, Li also gives a very beautiful formula for canoncal solution
u
of
\overline{\partial}
-equation (1.1)
which should be very useful for future study (see
[
43
]
, for example).

Based on the previous research results, it is very natural to ask the following question:

Question.
Does the sup-norm estimate (1.2) for
\overline{\partial}
hold when
\partial\Omega
is only Lipschitz?

Let
G(z,w)
be the Green’s function for
\Delta^{\prime}={\partial^{2}\over\partial z\partial\overline{z}}
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\rm\kern 2.40005pt\vrule width=0.20004pt,height=6.02773pt,depth=-0.21529pt\kern-2.59995ptC}
. in
[
29
]
, Li gives estimates for
G(z,w)
and its derivatives
when
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
. It is known that the Bergman kernel
K(z,w)
are related to
G(z,w)
(see
[
9
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
14
]
). In fact

K(z,w)={\partial^{2}G(z,w)\over\partial z\partial\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\Omega,z\neq w.

From the estimate of
G(z,w)
and its derivative in
[
29
]
, one has that the Bergman projection
P
is bounded on
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1<p<\infty
and
is bounded from
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
(Bloch space) when
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
An example of bounded domain
\Omega_{0}
with Lipschitz boundary was constructed by Jerison and Kenig
[
20
]
so that there is a real number
p_{1}>4
such that
the Bergman projection
P
is not bounded on
L^{q}(\Omega)
for
p_{1}^{\prime}<q<p_{1}
. Their result indicates that one should consider some solution for
\overline{\partial}
equation (1.1) instead of the
canonical solution when
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz. Along this line, we develop some new techniques in this paper so that we are able to answer the above question affirmatively and prove the following theorem.

THEOREM 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{\rm\kern 2.40005pt\vrule width=0.20004pt,height=6.02773pt,depth=-0.21529pt\kern-2.59995ptC}
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any
1<p\leq\infty
and any
\overline{\partial}
-closed

(0,1)
-form
f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}d\overline{z}_{j}\in L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})
, there is a linear integral operator

T[f]=\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]

(1.3)

solving
\overline{\partial}u=f
and satisfying the estimate

\|T[f]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}\leq\Big(C_{n}C_{\Omega}\Big)^{n}\|f\|_{{}^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})},

(1.4)

where
C_{\Omega}
is constant depending only on
\|\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|\delta_{\Omega}(\cdot)\|_{Lip(\Omega)}
, and
\delta_{\Omega}(z)
is the distance function from
z
to
\partial\Omega

and
C_{n}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
.

For more information about
\overline{\partial}
-equations and homotopy formulae, we refer the reader to the paper of Gong
[
13
]
and references listed in the current paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give an integral formula for
\overline{\partial}
through mathematical induction. In Section 3, we provide a new method to
transfer the integral formula solution for
\overline{\partial}
in Section 2 and get a new integral formula solution for
\overline{\partial}
, from which we can get
L^{p}
estimate for smooth
f
with uniform constant given by (1.4) for
1<p\leq\infty
.
Finally, in Section 4,
we prove Theorem 1.1.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
D
and
\Omega
be bounded domains in
{\rm\kern 2.40005pt\vrule width=0.20004pt,height=6.02773pt,depth=-0.21529pt\kern-2.59995ptC}

Proof.
Since

is holomorphic for

is

-closed on

{\partial f_{2}\over\partial\overline{z}_{1}}-{\partial f_{1}\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}=0.

\displaystyle{\partial T^{2}[f]\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{2}+{\partial\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}S_{1}[f_{1}]-S_{1}[{\partial f_{2}\over\partial\overline{z}_{1}}]-S_{1}^{D}[{\partial f_{1}\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}-{\partial f_{2}\over\partial\overline{z}_{1}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{2}+S_{1}[{\partial f_{1}\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}-{\partial f_{2}\over\partial\overline{z}_{1}}]-S_{1}^{D}[{\partial f_{1}\over\partial\overline{z}_{2}}-{\partial f_{2}\over\partial\overline{z}_{1}}]

Therefore, the proof of the proposition is complete.

Lemma 3.2
.

\displaystyle\int_{D(z_{i},\delta(z_{i})/2)}|w_{i}-z_{i}|^{-\alpha}\delta(w_{i})^{-\beta}dA(w_{i})

\displaystyle 4^{\beta}\delta(z_{i})^{-\beta}\int_{|w_{i}|<\delta(z_{i})/2}|w_{i}|^{-\alpha}dA(w_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2\beta+1}\pi\delta(z_{i})^{-\beta}\int_{0}^{\delta(z_{i})/2}r^{-\alpha+1}dr

\Omega

, one has

\displaystyle|S^{j}_{J}(z,w)|

4.
The Proof of Theorem 1.1

In this section, we will prove Theorem 1.1 by using the idea of the approximation which has been used in
[
29
]
and similar ideas
was also used in
[
9
]
and
[
42
]
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{\rm\kern 2.40005pt\vrule width=0.20004pt,height=6.02773pt,depth=-0.21529pt\kern-2.59995ptC}
with the distance function
\delta_{\Omega}\in\hbox{Lip}(\Omega)
.
For each
m\in{\rm I\kern-2.29996ptN}
, we define

\Omega_{m}=\{z\in\Omega:\delta(z)>1/m\}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}

(4.33)

Then
\Omega_{m}\subset\subset\Omega
and
\lim_{m\to\infty}\Omega_{m}=\Omega
.

For any
\overline{\partial}
-closed
f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}d\overline{z}_{j}\in L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega^{n})
and
any
0<\epsilon<1/m
, we let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(D(0,1))
be the cutoff function with
\int_{\rm\kern 1.91338pt\vrule width=0.15947pt,height=4.2194pt,depth=-0.1507pt\kern-2.07274ptC}\chi(z)dA(z)=1
. We define

\chi_{\epsilon}(z)={1\over\epsilon^{2n}}\chi(z_{1}/\epsilon)\cdots\chi(z_{n}/\epsilon)

(4.34)

and

f^{\epsilon}_{j}(z)=\chi_{\epsilon}*f_{j}(z)=\int_{\Omega^{n}}\chi_{\epsilon}(w)f_{j}(z-w)dv(w).

(4.35)

Then
f^{\epsilon}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f^{\epsilon}_{j}d\overline{z}_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega}_{m}^{n})
is
\overline{\partial}
-closed and
f^{\epsilon}\to f
in
L^{p}(\Omega_{m}^{n})
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
for any
m\in{\rm I\kern-2.29996ptN}

and
1<p<\infty
.

By the expression of
T_{j}
in Theorem
3.1
, for any
1<p<\infty
, one has

\|T_{j}[f_{j}^{\epsilon}]-T_{j}[f_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n}_{m})}\leq\Big({C_{n}C_{\Omega}}\Big)^{n}\|f_{j}^{\epsilon}-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}\to 0\ \hbox{ as }\ \epsilon\to 0^{+}.

This coupling with
\overline{\partial}\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}^{\epsilon}]=f^{\epsilon}
on
\Omega_{m}
implies that

\overline{\partial}\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]=f\quad\hbox{on}\quad\Omega_{m}^{n}

and

\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{m}^{n})}\leq\Big(C_{n}C_{\Omega}\Big)^{n},\quad 1<p<\infty

for any
m\in{\rm I\kern-2.29996ptN}
. Let
m\to+\infty
. Then

\overline{\partial}\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]=f\quad\hbox{on}\quad\Omega^{n}

and

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}\leq\Big(C_{n}C_{\Omega}\Big)^{n},\quad 1<p<\infty.

Let
p\to+\infty
. Then

\|T[f]\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{n})}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{n})}\leq\Big(C_{n}C_{\Omega}\Big)^{n}.

Therefore,

\|T[f]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}[f_{j}]\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{n})}\leq\Big({C_{n}}C_{\Omega}\Big)^{n},\quad 1<p\leq\infty

(4.36)

and the proof of Theorem 1.1 is complete.

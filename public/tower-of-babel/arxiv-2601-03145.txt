Classifying the Fine Polyhedral Spectrum
Sofía Garzón Mora, Christian Haase

Abstract
. In this paper, we examine an analogue of the recently solved spectrum conjecture by Fujita in the setting of Fine polyhedral adjunction theory. We present computational results for lower-dimensional polytopes, which lead to a complete classification of the highest numbers of this Fine spectrum in any dimension. Moreover, we present a full classification of the Fine spectrum in dimensions one, two and three, while providing a framework for general classification results in any dimension.



1.
Introduction

The study of adjoint linear systems
|K_{X}+tL|
on an algebraic
variety
X
, where
K_{X}
is the canonical bundle,
t\geq 0
and
L

is an ample line bundle, is, in some sense, a precursor of the Minimal
Model Program. When studying adjunction theory for polarized
varieties, Fujita conjectured in
[
8
]
and

[
9
]
that the set of pseudo-effective thresholds
of smooth polarized
d
-folds should be finite away from 0. This
conjecture has recently been proven, see
[
6
]
. Polyhedral
adjunction theory has been studied in
[
7
]
, and Fujita’s
spectrum conjecture for toric varieties, allowing canonical
singularities, was proven by Andreas Paffenholz in
[
18
]
.

The theory of Fine Polyhedral Adjunction, presented in

[
10
]
, has been constructed as a variant of polyhedral
adjunction theory
[
7
]
, where it has been proven that the
Spectrum Conjecture also holds in the Fine Adjunction case, without an
assumption on the (toric) singularities. Our main theorem in this
paper is a characterization of the highest numbers in the
spectrum. Here, we completely characterize the spectrum in dimensions
one and two and provide some general classification results for
polytopes of any dimension
d
.

We have computed the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree for most low-dimensional
polytopes classified by volume that have been previously obtained by
Gabriele Balletti
[
2
]
. Additionally, we investigate further
classification results computationally by expressing our
classification problem in the language of a mixed integer linear
program (MILP). As a result, we develop further tools aimed at a full
classification of the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree spectrum, where we see that
this classification not only refines and combines existing results in
Polyhedral Adjunction Theory with the classification of lattice
polytopes, but also reveals the strong structural properties of our
underlying theory.

2.
Preliminaries

We start by including here some crucial definitions with minimal
descriptions. For further details, we refer the reader to

[
10
]
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional rational polytope,
described in a unique minimal way as
P=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>\langle a_{i},x\rangle\geq b_{i},i=1,...,m\},
where the
a_{i}\in(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}

are the primitive rows of a matrix
A
, meaning they are not the
multiple of another lattice vector, and
b=(b_{1},...,b_{m})\in\mathds{Q}^{m}
. We
refer to
P
as a
lattice polytope
if its vertices lie in

\mathds{Z}^{d}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f
be the affine functional
f(x)=\langle a,x\rangle-b
for
some
b\in\mathds{Q}
and
a
an element of the dual lattice

(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}
. Such a functional is said to be
valid
for a
polytope
P
if for the halfspace
\mathcal{H}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>f(x)\geq 0\},
we have that
P\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{+}
.
If, in addition, there is some
p\in P
with
f(p)=0
, i.e., at least
one point of
P
lies in
\mathcal{H}
, the hyperplane defined by
f
,
we say
f
is a
tight
valid inequality for
P
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{*}
. We define the
support
function
associated with
P
as

h_{P}:(\mathds{R}^{d})^{*}\to\mathds{R},\quad\alpha\mapsto\min_{x\in P}\langle\alpha,x\rangle.

For some fixed real number
s>0
, we may define the
Fine
adjoint polytope
, which is a rational polytope, as

P^{F(s)}:=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>\langle a,x\rangle\geq h_{P}(a)+s,\text{ for all }a\in(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}.

Here we introduce the notion of a relevant inequality.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of all valid inequalities for
P
, where
an element
f\in\mathcal{F}
is of the form
\langle a_{f},x\rangle\geq b_{f}
. A valid inequality
f\in\mathcal{F}
is said to be

relevant
for
P
if for some
s>0
, it holds that

\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>\langle a_{f},x\rangle\geq h_{P}(a_{f})+s\text{ }\forall f\in\mathcal{F}\}\neq\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>\langle a_{f},x\rangle\geq h_{P}(a_{f})+s\text{ }\forall f\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{f\}\}.

The valid inequality
f
is said to be
irrelevant
if it is
not relevant.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
10
, Corollary 2.7]

Let
P
be a rational polytope of dimension
d
. The valid relevant
inequalities for
P
of the form
\langle a,\cdot\rangle\geq h^{F}(a)
are among the
a\in(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{*}
such that
a\in\operatorname{conv}(a_{1},...,a_{m})
where the
a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
are the primitive inward pointing facet normals of
P
.

In what follows we will refer to
P
as the polytope defined in the
following way:

(1)

P=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\>|\>\langle a_{i},x\rangle\geq b_{i},i=1,...,m\},

where
b_{i}\in\mathds{Q}
and
a_{i}\in(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}
are the primitive rows of a
matrix
A
including all relevant valid inequalities for
P
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree
of a rational polytope
P
is

\mu^{F}(P):=(\sup\{s>0\>|\>P^{F(s)}\neq\emptyset\})^{-1},

and the
Fine core
of
P
is

\core^{F}\!(P):=P^{(1/\mu^{F}(P))}.

We will refer to the quantity

n^{F}\!(P):=\frac{1}{\mu^{F}(P)}

as the
Fine Number
of
P
.

From the definition we see for
s\in\mathds{Q}_{>0}

(2)

\displaystyle n^{F}\!(sP)=sn^{F}\!(P)\text{ and therefore }s\mu^{F}\!(sP)=\mu^{F}\!(P)\,\text{ and}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{if }P\subseteq Q,\text{ then }P^{F(s)}\subseteq Q^{F(s)}\text{ so that }n^{F}(P)\leq n^{F}(Q)\,.

The monotonicity (
3
) does not hold for non-Fine adjunction.
We now introduce one more definition, adapted from
[
7
]
which
will be very useful for our computations in later sections.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional polytope. The Fine Mountain
M^{F}(P)\subseteq\mathds{R}^{d+1}
of
P
is given by

M^{F}(P):=\{(x,s)\>|\>x\in P,0\leq s\leq\langle a,x\rangle-h_{P}(a),\text{ for all }a\in(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}

and if
P
has its inequality description as in (
1
), then
M^{F}(P)
can be described by

M^{F}(P)=\{(x,s)\in\mathds{R}^{d+1}\>|\>(A|-\mathbb{1})(x,s)^{T}\geq b,s\geq 0\}.

Therefore, the Fine mountain of
P
is a polytope such that for all

s_{0}\geq 0

M^{F}(P)\cap\mathds{R}^{d}\times\{s_{0}\}=P^{F(s_{0})}\times\{s_{0}\}.

As a means of comparison, we will now define the notion of codegree
which comes up in the context of Ehrhart Theory of lattice polytopes

[
4
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
P
be a lattice
d
-polytope. We define the
codegree
as

\operatorname{cd}(P):=\min\{k\in\mathds{N}_{\geq 1}\>|\>\operatorname{int}(kP)\cap\mathds{Z}^{d}\neq\emptyset\}.

Then the
degree
of
P
is
\deg(P)\coloneq d+1-\operatorname{cd}(P)
.

Observe that
\emptyset\neq\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{cd}(P)P)\cap\mathds{Z}^{d}\subseteq(\operatorname{cd}(P)P)^{F(1)}
so
that
\mu^{F}\!(P)\leq\operatorname{cd}(P)
.

2.1.
Finiteness of the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree spectrum

It is proven in
[
10
]
that in each dimension, when
bounded from below by some
\varepsilon>0
, the set of values that
the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree can take is finite.

In
[
18
]
, in particular, the following Theorem is shown.

Theorem 2.8

(Paffenholz,
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathds{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then

\{\mu(P)\>|\>P\text{ is a $d$-dimensional lattice polytope with
canonical normal fan}\}\cap\mathds{R}_{\geq\varepsilon}

is finite.

Note that in the context of algebraic geometry, this is Fujita’s
conjecture for the case of toric varieties with at most canonical
singularities.

The following Fine version of this result has been proven in

[
10
]
. It allows us to ask the classification question
of the Fine spectrum

\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d)\coloneq\left\{\mu^{F}\!(P)\>|\>P\text{ is a $d$-dimensional lattice polytope}\right\}

that this paper is about.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
10
, Theorem 5.4]

Let
d\in\mathds{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then

\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d)\cap\mathds{R}_{\geq\varepsilon}
is finite.

For details of this proof, we refer the reader to the original
paper. However, it is worth mentioning here that the proof consists of
two main parts. For the first part, and for future use, we need the
notion of Fine core normal:

Definition 2.10
.

A functional
a\in(\mathds{Z}^{d})^{*}
is a
Fine core normal
if

\langle a_{0},y\rangle=h_{P}(a_{0})+\mu^{F}\!(P)^{-1}

for all
y\in\operatorname{core}^{F}(P)
.
The convex hull in
(\mathds{R}^{d})^{*}
of the Fine core normals will be
denoted
A_{\core}^{F}
.

It was shown that up to lattice equivalence, for a fixed
d\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
, there are only finitely many sets which can be the Fine core
normals for
d
-dimensional lattice polytopes. Then it was shown that
each such configuration of Fine core normals gives rise to finitely
many values for the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree above any positive threshold

\varepsilon
.

If we let
P
be described by all relevant inequalities as in
(
1
), up to relabeling, we can
assume that the set of Fine core normals consists of
a_{1},...,a_{k}
for
some
k\leq m
. This yields a set of inequalities defining the
affine hull of the Fine core of
P
,

\operatorname{aff}(\core^{F}\!(P))
, that is,

\displaystyle\operatorname{aff}\left(\core^{F}\!(P)\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\>|\>\langle a_{i},x\rangle\geq b_{i}+\mu^{F}\!(P)^{-1},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\>|\>\langle a_{i},x\rangle=b_{i}+\mu^{F}\!(P)^{-1},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

We end this section with the remark below.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
be a sublattice of the lattice

\Lambda^{\prime}
and let
P
be a rational polytope with respect to the
lattice
\Lambda
, then if we denote by
\mu^{F}\!(P,\Lambda)
the Fine

\mathds{Q}
-codegree of
P
with respect to the lattice
\Lambda
, we have
that

\mu^{F}\!(P,\Lambda)\geq\mu^{F}\!(P,\Lambda^{\prime}).

3.
Tools

Before we explore the Fine spectrum in small dimensions and to some
extent in general dimension in §
4
, we develop
some general tools to facilitate the search.

3.1.
Project and Lift

Now, we want to study the behaviour of the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree under
lattice preserving projections
\pi\colon\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}^{d^{\prime}}
.

Remark 3.1
.

[
10
, Remark 3.4]

Under any projection
\pi
for any rational polytope
P\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
we have
\mu^{F}\!(\pi(P))\leq\mu^{F}\!(P)
.

But every
P
comes with its own preferred projection.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
K(P)
be the linear space parallel to

\text{aff}(\core^{F}\!(P))
. Then the projection
\pi_{P}:\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}^{n}/K(P)
is called the natural projection associated with
P
.

It has the following nice properties.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
10
, Proposition 3.3]

The image
Q:=\pi_{P}(P)
of the natural projection of
P
is a
rational polytope satisfying
\mu^{F}\!(Q)=\mu^{F}\!(P)
. Moreover

\core^{F}\!(Q)
is the point
\pi_{P}(\core^{F}\!(P))
.

Using the natural projection, we can relate the Fine spectra in
different dimensions.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
d\in\mathds{Z}_{>1}
,

\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d-1)\subset\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d)
, and

\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{F}\!(d-1)\subset\left\{\mu^{F}\!(P)\>|\>P\text{ a lattice $d$-polytope with }\core^{F}\!(P)\text{ a point}\right\}\,.

Proof.

For the first inclusion, observe that
\mu^{F}\!(P\times[0,h])=\mu^{F}\!(P)
for
h\gg 0
.

The second inclusion is a restatement of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

3.2.2.
A family of MILPs

Recalling the proof of Theorem
2.9
we deduce a way of exploring the Fine spectrum. First of all, for a fixed dimension
d
, we need to determine all possible different configurations of Fine core normals. Note that these correspond to lattice polytopes whose only interior lattice point is the origin, for which there are only finitely many possibilities
[
15
, Theorem 1]
. We then fix a set of Fine core normals and proceed to find all possible polytopes with such a Fine core normal configuration that give rise to different
\mu^{F}
values. As we shall see, this corresponds to solving a Mixed Integer Linear Program.

It is worth remarking that, in what follows, we can consider only polytopes which have a zero-dimensional Fine core using the arguments from §
3.1
.

Fix a dimension
d
and a configuration
\mathcal{A}
of Fine core normals
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{*}
. We want to construct a polytope
P
with zero-dimensional Fine core, namely
\core^{F}\!(P)=\{p\}
, with vertices
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
, that satisfies the property of having these Fine core normals. Let us take as variables the
u_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
p\in\mathds{Q}^{d}
and
n^{F}:=\frac{1}{\mu^{F}\!(P)}\in\mathds{Q}
the
Fine number
of
P
. Then the Mixed Integer Linear Program can be stated as:

(MILP
{}^{\pm}_{A}(L,U)
)

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&\pm n^{F}&\\
\text{subject to}&\langle a_{i},u_{j}\rangle&\geq\langle a_{i},u_{i}\rangle&\text{ for all }i\neq j\\
&\langle a_{i},p\rangle&=\langle a_{i},u_{i}\rangle+n^{F}&\text{ for all }i\\
&n^{F}&\in[L,U]&\\
&p&\in\mathds{Q}^{d}\cap[0,1]^{d}\\
&u_{1},\dots,u_{n}&\in\mathds{Z}^{d}.\\
\end{array}

Solving this MILP will return vectors
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
whose convex hull defines a polytope with a Fine core normal configuration given by
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{*}
and with
L\leq n^{F}\leq U
, where
L
and
U
are some lower and upper bounds for the Fine number. It is possible to explore the Fine spectrum by maximizing
n^{F}
and varying
U
or maximizing
-n^{F}
and varying
L
, thus achieving a complete list of all values for
\mu^{F}
by varying these two parameters consistently. It is not immediately clear how small the variations of
L
and
U
should be in general to guarantee a complete scanning of the Fine spectrum.

This characterization of the values
\mu^{F}\!(P)
requires a classification of lattice polytopes whose only interior lattice point is the origin. In fact, a classification of inclusion minimal such polytopes is sufficient, see
2.11
. In the next section, we will study the cases of lower dimensions and introduce some results that hold in a general dimension
d
. However, to make our endeavor more manageable, we will first discuss some interesting results that restrict our spectrum values in any dimension
d
.

3.3.
Reducing the search space

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
, and
suppose there are Fine core normals
a_{0},...,a_{\ell}
and
coefficients
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
so
that
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}a_{i}=0
, then there is a lattice
polytope
Q
of dimension
\leq\ell
such that

\mu^{F}\!(P)=\mu^{F}\!(Q).

Proof.

For the conclusion we can safely assume
\ell<d
.

Look at the projection

\pi=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})\colon\mathds{R}^{d}\to\mathds{R}^{\ell}
. It takes
P

to
Q\coloneq\pi(P)
, considered as a lattice polytope in the
sublattice
\Lambda\coloneq\pi(\mathds{Z}^{d})\subseteq\mathds{Z}^{\ell}
.
Our claim is that this projection is Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree preserving,
i.e.,
\mu^{F}\!(P)=\mu^{F}\!(Q)
.

First, observe that
a_{0},\ldots,a_{\ell}
descend to well-defined
elements of
\Lambda^{\vee}
as
\ker\pi\subseteq\ker a_{i}
for

i=0,\ldots,\ell
.

Next, recall from Remark
3.1
that
\mu^{F}\!(\pi(P))\leq\mu^{F}\!(P)

for any projection. So we just need to verify that the reverse
inequality holds.

There are
v_{0},...,v_{\ell}\in P\cap\mathds{Z}^{d}
and
b_{0},...,b_{\ell}\in\mathds{Z}
such that for
x\in\relint(\core^{F}\!(P))
, we have

b_{i}=h_{P}(a_{i})=\langle a_{i},v_{i}\rangle=\langle a_{i},x\rangle-\frac{1}{\mu^{F}\!(P)}

for all
0\leq i\leq\ell
.
The relation
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}a_{i}=0
yields a certificate
that for every
s>1/\mu^{F}\!(P)
, the system of inequalities

\langle a_{i},y\rangle\geq b_{i}+s\quad(i=0,\ldots,\ell)

for
y\in\mathds{R}^{d}
is infeasible.
As mentioned above, these inequalities descend to valid inequalities
for
Q
. This shows
Q^{F(s)}=\varnothing
for all
s>1/\mu^{F}\!(P)
, in other words
\mu^{F}\!(Q)\geq\mu^{F}\!(P)
.
∎

Now we show that it is enough to consider Fine core normal configurations given by the vertices of lattice polytopes with the origin as the only interior lattice point, since any possible sub-configuration is already accounted for in this classification.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
v_{1},...,v_{k}
be Fine core normals of a polytope
P
. Let
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
where
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
and
v\neq 0
. Then
v
is also a Fine core normal of
P
.

Proof.

Let
b_{i}=\min_{p\in P}\langle v_{i},p\rangle
and let
x\in\operatorname{relint}(\core^{F}\!(P))
, then we have by definition
\langle v_{i},x\rangle=b_{i}+n^{F}(P)
. Denote
b:=\min_{p\in P}\langle v,p\rangle
and since
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}
, we have that

b\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}b_{i}.

Moreover, also by the definition of
n^{F}(P)
we have

\langle v,x\rangle\geq b+n^{F}(P).

In order to show that
v
is a Fine core normal of
P
, we want to show that the above inequality is indeed an equality. But

n^{F}(P)\leq\langle v,x\rangle-b\leq\langle v,x\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\left(\langle v_{i},x\rangle-b_{i}\right)=n^{F}(P)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq n^{F}(P),

where the first inequality follows from the definition of
n^{F}(P)
, the second one by the relation between
b
and the
b_{i}
, and the last one since by assumption
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
. Thus, we obtain
\langle v,x\rangle=b+n^{F}(P)
as desired.
∎

In order to further reduce our database, we want to show that the set of numerators of the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree is finite. We first define this set.

Definition 3.7
.

For a fixed dimension
d
, let
\mathcal{I}_{d}\subset\mathds{Z}_{>0}
be the set of all possible numerators of the rational number
\mu^{F}\!(P)
, where
P
is a lattice
d
-polytope.

The theorem below provides us with a way of computing the elements of the set
\mathcal{I}_{d}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Fix a dimension
d\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
. Consider the set
\mathcal{F}_{d}
of
d
-dimensional Fine core normal configurations and for any such configuration
\mathcal{A}
, choose a set
a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
of vectors which are positively spanning. To such a choice, we associate a matrix
A
whose
i
-th row is
a_{i}
for

and a number

\eta_{A}:=\frac{\det\begin{bmatrix}A&-\mathbb{1}\end{bmatrix}}{D}

where
D:=\gcd_{j=1,\dots,d+1}\{\det\begin{bmatrix}A_{j}\end{bmatrix}\}
and
A_{j}
is the matrix
A
with the
j
-th row removed. Then

\mathcal{I}_{d}=\mathcal{I}_{d-1}\cup\{\eta_{A}\>|\>a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1}\subset\mathcal{A}\text{ positively spanning},\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{F}_{d}\}.

Proof.

For all
d>0
,
\mathcal{I}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{d+1}
due to the arguments from §
3.1
. Let
\core^{F}\!(P)=\{p\}
. By definition, the Fine core normals satisfy

\langle a_{i},p\rangle=b_{i}+n^{F}(P)

for some
b_{i}\in\mathds{Q}
,
i=1,\dots,d+1
. Note that we can indeed find
d+1
linearly independent positively spanning Fine core normals, since the Fine core of
P
consists of one point. Let
A:=A_{a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1}}
be given as above and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{d+1})
. Then,
p
and
n^{F}(P)
satisfy the following linear system:

\begin{bmatrix}A&-\mathbb{1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
n^{F}(P)\end{pmatrix}=b.

By Cramer’s rule, the system has a unique solution. In particular

(4)

n^{F}(P)=\frac{\det\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}}{\det\begin{bmatrix}A&-\mathbb{1}\end{bmatrix}},

so that
\det\begin{bmatrix}A&-\mathbb{1}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{I}_{d}
for each such choice
a_{1},\dots,a_{d+1}
of
d+1
Fine core normals out of the initial configuration
\mathcal{A}
. In particular, any element in
\mathcal{I}_{d}
occurs as such a determinant or one of its divisors.

Conversely, given such a matrix
A
we can find a vector
b
so that the convex hull of the lattice points inside
Ax\leq b
realizes the Fine number as in (
4
) (cf. Section
3.4
).

∎

3.5.
Lattice Pyramids

Now we present some results on lattice pyramids and the behavior of their Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree. Here we need the following definition, which we phrase similarly as in

[

]

Definition 3.10
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope. We say

is a

lattice pyramid
if

is a lattice point or if there is a
lattice polytope

such that

. For instance, the standard simplex
\Delta_{d}
is a lattice
pyramid.

Using explicit examples from which we obtained the data presented in
the table, we have observed that there are certain lattice polytopes

P
with Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree
\mu^{F}\!(P)>1
such that the value

\mu^{F}\!(P)+1
is taken by a lattice pyramid over
P
. Note that if

\mu^{F}\!(P)<1
, then the lattice pyramid over
P
will have Fine

\mathds{Q}
-codegree
2
. However, it is not straightforward to describe the
Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree of a lattice pyramid, as the following example
illustrates.

Example 3.11
.

If we consider a lattice pyramid over the rational segments
S_{1}=[-\frac{1}{5},\frac{2}{5}]
and
S_{2}=[\frac{1}{5},\frac{4}{5}]
,
we have that
\mu^{F}\!(S_{1})=\frac{10}{3}=\mu^{F}\!(S_{2})
but

\mu^{F}\!(\operatorname{Pyr}(S_{1}))=\frac{10}{3}
and

\mu^{F}\!(\operatorname{Pyr}(S_{2}))=3
.

This observation leads us to a result which will be used below in our main
classification Theorem.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope and
\pyrOperator(P)
a
lattice pyramid over
P
. Then

\mu^{F}\!(\pyrOperator(P))=\max\{2,\mu^{F}\!(P)+1\}.

Proof.

For convenience denote
\mu:=\mu^{F}\!(P)
.
We first show the lower bounds.

The valid inequalities
0\leq x_{d+1}\leq 1
for
\pyrOperator(P)
show that

\pyrOperator(P)^{F(s)}=\varnothing
for
s>1/2
. Hence,
\mu^{F}\!(\pyrOperator(P))\geq 2
.

To prove
\mu^{F}\!(\pyrOperator(P))\geq\mu+1
we show that, if

\pyrOperator(P)^{F(t)}\neq\varnothing
, then

P^{F(s)}\neq\varnothing
for
s=\frac{t}{1-t}
.
So suppose
(p,h)\in\pyrOperator(P)^{F(t)}
. We claim that
p/h\in P^{F(s)}
. Consider a valid inequality
\langle a,x\rangle\geq b
for
P
.
Then
\langle a,x\rangle-bx_{d+1}\geq 0
is valid
for every
(x,x_{d+1})\in\pyrOperator(P)
. Then
(p,h)\in\pyrOperator(P)^{F(t)}
implies
\langle a,p\rangle-bh\geq t
. We obtain

\langle a,p/h\rangle\geq b+t/h\geq b+s

where we use
h\leq 1-t
as
(p,h)\in\pyrOperator(P)^{F(t)}
.

For the upper bound on
\mu^{F}\!(\pyrOperator(P))
,
let
p\in\operatorname{relint}(\core^{F}\!(P))
. We want to show that

(\hat{p},\hat{h})\coloneq(1-s)(p,1)\in\pyrOperator(P)^{F(s)}
for

s=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{\mu+1}\right\}
.

Let
\langle a,x\rangle+\alpha x_{d+1}\geq b
with be some
inequality valid for all
(x,x_{d+1})\in\pyrOperator(P)
. By considering a

\mathds{Z}^{d}
-translate of
P
(this corresponds to applying a unimodular
transformation to
\pyrOperator(P)
which leaves the functional
a
unchanged),
we may assume that
a
takes its minimum over
P
at
0
.
Then
\langle a,p\rangle\geq 1/\mu\geq\frac{s}{1-s}
.

First, assume that
\alpha\leq 0
. In this case, the functional

(a,\alpha)
achieves its minimum along
\pyrOperator(P)
at the vertex

e_{d+1}=(0,...,0,1)
, and this minimum is
\alpha
.
Then

\langle(a,\alpha),(\hat{p},\hat{h})\rangle=(1-s)\ \langle(a,\alpha),(p,1)\rangle\geq(1-s)\left(\frac{s}{1-s}+\alpha\right)\geq s+\alpha\,.

as desired.

If, on the other hand,
\alpha\geq 1
, the functional

(a,\alpha)
achieves its minimum along
\pyrOperator(P)
at the origin
and this minimum is
0
.
Then

\langle(a,\alpha),(\hat{p},\hat{h})\rangle=(1-s)\langle(a,\alpha),(p,1)\rangle\geq(1-s)\ \left(\frac{s}{1-s}+\alpha\right)\geq s+0

∎

4.3.
Classification results in dimension
\boldsymbol{d}

We have so far studied the Fine spectra in lower-dimensional
cases. From the results mentioned above, we can easily deduce that
studying the Fine spectra for dimensions greater than
3
entails
dealing with a number of Mixed Linear Programs of several orders of
magnitude. Recall that the classification of the spectra is obtained
by solving each of them individually for several fixed
\varepsilon>0
and listing all different values. Thus, it is not very feasible to
follow this procedure already in dimension
4
. However, since we are
interested in general classification results for any dimension
d
, we
are able to state some general facts that hold in any dimension. For
this, the classification of degree 1 lattice polytopes from Victor
Batyrev and Benjamin Nill developed in 2006 will be very useful, see

[
3
]
.

The degree of a
d
-dimensional polytope
P
satisfies
\deg(P)=d+1-\text{cd}(P)
. In order to obtain our classification, we need to state
some definitions first.

Definition 4.6
.

Given lattice polytopes
P_{0},...,P_{t}
in
\mathds{R}^{k}
, the
Cayley
sum

P_{0}\star\cdots\star P_{t}
is the convex hull of

(P_{0}\times 0)\cup(P_{1}\times e_{1})\cup\cdots\cup(P_{t}\times e_{t})\subseteq\mathds{R}^{k}\times\mathds{R}^{t}

for
e_{1},...,e_{t}
the standard basis of
\mathds{R}^{t}
.

Definition 4.7
.

We call a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
a
Lawrence
prism with heights
h_{1},...,h_{d}
if it is the Cayley Sum of the
segments
[0,h_{1}],...,[0,h_{d}]
.

Definition 4.8
.

A
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
, for
d\geq 2
, is called

exceptional
if it is a simplex which is a
(d-2)
-fold
pyramid over
2
times the
2
-dimensional standard triangle
2\Delta_{2}:=\conv\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,2)\}
.

We also need the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional exceptional simplex for
d\geq 2
. Then

\mu^{F}\!(P)=d-\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

The relevant valid inequalities for
P
are given by
x_{1}\geq 0
,

x_{2}\geq 0
and
-x_{1}-x_{2}\geq-2
. So a point
p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\core^{F}\!(P)
satisfies
p_{1}=n^{F}(P)
,
p_{2}=n^{F}(P)
and
-p_{1}-p_{2}=-2+n^{F}(P)
. Solving for the Fine number, we obtain
n^{F}(P)=\frac{2}{3}
. By Theorem
3.12
, since
\mu^{F}\!(P)>1
, the result follows by induction.
∎

The main classification Theorem for degree 1 lattice polytopes can be
stated as follows.

Theorem 4.10
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.5]

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of arbitrary dimension
d
. Then

\deg(P)\leq 1
if and only if
P
is an exceptional simplex or a
Lawrence prism.

Moreover, from
[
3
, Proposition 1.4]
we obtain that a

d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
has
\deg(P)=0
if and only if

P
is a simplex, i.e., its vertices form an affine lattice basis. And
we also have that for a Lawrence prism
P
with heights
h_{1},...,h_{d}

which satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{d}h_{i}\geq 2
, then
\deg(P)=1
.

We can now state our main classification Theorem.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d\geq 2
. Then:

i)

\mu^{F}\!(P)\cong d+1
if and only if
P\cong\Delta_{d}
up to
unimodular equivalence. Otherwise:

ii)

\mu^{F}\!(P)=d
if and only if
P
projects to the standard
simplex
\Delta_{d-1}
in dimension
d-1
. If not:

iii)

\mu^{F}\!(P)=d-\frac{1}{2}
if and only if
P
is an
exceptional simplex. And finally:

iv)

\mu^{F}\!(P)=d-1
if
P
projects to
\Delta_{d-2}
and
P

does not satisfy the conditions of any of the previous cases.

Moreover, the Fine
\mathds{Q}
-codegree cannot take any further values in the
range
d-1\leq\mu^{F}\!(P)\leq d+1
other than those specified above.

Proof.

First of all, for a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
we have

\mu(P)\leq\mu^{F}\!(P)\leq\text{cd}(P)\leq d+1.

To see
i)
we make use of the fact that
\mu(P)
and
\text{cd}(P)
equal

d+1
if and only if
\deg(P)=0
and
P\cong\Delta_{d}
, as proven in

[
7
]
. Hence, we can conclude that
\mu^{F}\!(P)=d+1
if and only
if
P\cong\Delta_{d}
.

Now, recall that under any projection
\pi
, we have that

\mu^{F}\!(\pi(P))\leq\mu^{F}\!(P).

To prove
ii)
let us first assume that
\mu^{F}\!(P)=d
, i.e.,

\deg(P)=1
. By Proposition
4.9
, we know
that since
P
is not an exceptional simplex, then it must be a
Lawrence prism or equivalently, a Cayley sum of one-dimensional
segments. Hence,
P
projects to
\Delta_{d-1}
. Conversely, if we now
assume that there is a projection
\pi
mapping
P
onto

\Delta_{d-1}
, by our remark above, we have that
d\leq\mu^{F}\!(P)
. But since
\text{cd}(P)=d
, then it must be the case that

\mu^{F}\!(P)=d
, which proves
ii)
.

In order to prove
iii)
, first assume that
\mu^{F}\!(P)=d-\frac{1}{2}
. As in the previous case, we have that
\deg(P)=1
, so by
Theorem
4.10
, since we know
P

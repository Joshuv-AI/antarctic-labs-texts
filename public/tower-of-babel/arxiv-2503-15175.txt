Decomposition results for multiplicative actions and applications Thanks: The author was supported by the Hellenic Foundation for Research and Innovation ELIDEK HFRI-NextGenerationEU-15689.
Nikos Frantzikinakis

Abstract
. Motivated by partition regularity problems of homogeneous quadratic equations, we prove multiple recurrence and convergence results for multiplicative measure preserving actions with iterates given by rational sequences involving polynomials that factor into products of linear forms in two variables. We focus mainly on actions that are finitely generated, and the key tool in our analysis is a decomposition result for any bounded measurable function into a sum of two components, one that mimics concentration properties of pretentious multiplicative functions and another that mimics vanishing properties of aperiodic multiplicative functions. Crucial to part of our arguments are some new seminorms that are defined by a mixture of addition and multiplication of the iterates of the action, and we prove an inverse theorem that explicitly characterizes the factor that these seminorms naturally determine.

1.
Introduction

A
multiplicative measure preserving action
is a quadruple
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
, where
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space, and
T_{n}\colon X\to X
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are
invertible measure preserving transformations that satisfy
T_{1}=\text{id}
and
T_{mn}=T_{m}\circ T_{n}
for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In a few cases we may also consider non-invertible actions. We extend the action to the positive rationals by
T_{m/n}:=T_{m}\circ T^{-1}_{n}
for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Following
[
8
]
, we say that the action is
finitely generated
if the set of commuting transformations
\{T_{p}\colon p\in{\mathbb{P}}\}
is finite.

Additive measure preserving actions have been widely used to study problems in additive combinatorics concerning translation invariant patterns that occur within any
set of integers with positive upper density.
The prototypical examples
are Furstenberg’s proof of Szemerédi’s theorem on arithmetic progressions
[
24
]
and its polynomial extension by Bergelson and Leibman
[
7
]
, which
motivated other powerful multiple recurrence and mean convergence results in ergodic theory; see
[
33
]

for
related recent trends.

In complete analogy, multiplicative measure preserving actions can be used to study dilation invariant patterns that occur within any “multiplicatively large” set of integers, i.e., a set of integers with positive multiplicative density.
One of our main motivations is to study the partition and density regularity (see
Definition

2.2
) of equations of the form

P(x,y,z)=0,

where
P
is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial; the equations
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
and
x^{2}+y^{2}=2z^{2}
are the best known examples. Although several such equations are expected to be partition regular,
progress has been rather scarce, with some partial results appearing in
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

A serious limitation of the methodology developed in these works is the dependence on a representation result of Bochner-Herglotz, which only allows us to deal with length two patterns, i.e., to guarantee that two out of the three variables

x,y,z
belong to the desired set. For example, it was shown in
[
21
]
that every set of integers with positive multiplicative density contains pairs
x,y
and
x,z
such that
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
.

Our main goal here is to start developing a methodology that will allow us to deal with patterns of length greater than two.
Although our techniques are currently not directly applicable to problems related with the Pythagorean or other diagonal equations, we will make some progress towards other homogeneous equations.
To give an explicit example (see Section
2.3
for a wider range of examples), suppose we want to show that the equation

x^{2}-y^{2}=xz

is density regular according to
Definition

2.2
(and hence partition regular).
Note that

x=k\,m^{2},\quad y=k\,mn,\quad z=k\,(m^{2}-n^{2})

satisfy the equation for all
k,m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Using a variant of Furstenberg’s correspondence principle (see
[
5
]
), it suffices to show that for any multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and set
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
, we have

(1.1)

\mu(T^{-1}_{m^{2}}A\cap T^{-1}_{mn}A\cap T^{-1}_{m^{2}-n^{2}}A)>0

for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
m>n
. Although we are currently unable to prove this multiple recurrence property for general multiplicative actions, we will prove it for all finitely generated ones.
In fact, in this case we show that the closely related multiple ergodic averages (our averaging notation is explained at the end of this section)

(1.2)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N],m>n}\,T_{m/n}F_{1}\cdot T_{(m^{2}-n^{2})/(mn)}F_{2}

converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
N\to\infty
for all
F_{1},F_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, and by analyzing this limit we are also able to obtain optimal lower bounds for the multiple intersections in (
1.1
), roughly of the form
(\mu(A))^{3}
. For a much more general multiple recurrence and convergence result,
which also covers not necessarily commuting multiplicative actions, see
Theorem

2.2
.

The methodology we develop also allows us to verify multiple recurrence and convergence results when all the iterates are given by several pairwise independent linear forms. For example, we show that if
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is a finitely generated multiplicative action and
\mu(A)>0
, then for every
\varepsilon>0
, for a set of
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with positive lower density
2
2

2

A subset
E
of
{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
has
positive lower density
if

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{|E\cap([N]\times[N])|}{N^{2}}>0.
we have

\mu(A\cap T^{-1}_{m}A\cap T^{-1}_{n}A\cap T^{-1}_{m+n}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-1}_{m+\ell n}A)\geq(\mu(A))^{\ell+3}-\varepsilon.

We also show that the closely related multiple ergodic averages

(1.3)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,T_{m}F_{0}\cdot T_{m+n}F_{1}\cdots T_{m+\ell n}F_{\ell}

converge in

for all

For a more general statement involving several pairwise independent linear forms and different multiplicative actions, see
Theorem

. As a consequence, in

we get that if

are ergodic, not necessarily commuting, measure preserving transformations acting on a probability space
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}T_{j}^{\Omega(m+jn)}F_{j}=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell}\int F_{j}\,d\mu

, where

denotes the number of prime divisors of

Corollary

, which covers recurrence and convergence results for more general completely additive sequences on the integers.

The proof of these results depends crucially on decomposition results for multiplicative actions that we believe to be of independent interest, see Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
. Roughly, they state that a function
F\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
can be decomposed as

(1.4)

F=F_{p}+F_{a},

where the spectral measures of
F_{p}
and
F_{a}
are supported on pretentious and aperiodic multiplicative functions, respectively.
This allows us to get our hands on a variety of results in analytic number theory and relate them directly
to properties of the
components
F_{p}
and
F_{a}
.
For example, for finitely generated multiplicative actions, we show that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\left\|T_{k!n+b}F_{p}-T_{b}F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

holds for every
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
and for general multiplicative actions we show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,T_{P(m,n)}F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

for any polynomial
P
that is a product of pairwise independent linear forms.

Finally, we note that in the case of several linear iterates, as in (
1.3
),
our averages are controlled by some seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}
defined on
L^{\infty}(\mu)
using a mixture of addition and multiplication of the iterates
T_{n}
(see
Definition

6.2
). For finitely generated actions, in
Theorem

6.3
, we prove a clean inverse theorem for these seminorms, which states that

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0\,\,\forall s\in{\mathbb{N}}\Leftrightarrow\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{2}}=0\Leftrightarrow\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{an+b}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0\quad\forall a,b\in{\mathbb{N}},

and we deduce that

\lvert\!|\!|F_{a}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0
for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
It is an interesting open problem to find an analogous inverse theorem for general multiplicative actions (see
Problem

1
in
Section

9
).
We suspect that such results will play a role in the analysis of even more delicate multiple recurrence and convergence results of general multiplicative actions; for example,
Problem

4
in
Section

9
is related to
Pythagorean triples.

2.1.
Multiple recurrence and convergence for multiplicative actions

We describe here our two main results about multiple recurrence and convergence of multiplicative actions.
In

2.2.
Decomposition results for multiplicative actions

Crucial to the proof of Theorems

Let

2.3.
Connections with partition and density regularity

A
multiplicative Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence
\Phi=(\Phi_{K})_{K=1}^{\infty}
of finite subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
that is asymptotically invariant under dilation, in the sense that

\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\big|\Phi_{K}\cap(x\cdot\Phi_{K})\big|}{|\Phi_{K}|}=1\quad\text{for every }x\in{\mathbb{N}}.

An example of a multiplicative Følner sequence is

\Phi_{K}:=\Big\{\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}\colon a_{K}\leq a_{p}\leq b_{K}\Big\},\quad K\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
a_{K},b_{K}\in{\mathbb{N}}
are such that
b_{K}-a_{K}\to\infty
as
K\to\infty
.
We say that
E\subset{\mathbb{N}}
has
positive multiplicative density

if

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\frac{\big|E\cap\Phi_{K}\big|}{|\Phi_{K}|}>0

for some multiplicative Følner sequence
(\Phi_{K})_{K=1}^{\infty}
.

Definition 2.2

If

if for every finite partition of

in the same cell that satisfy the equation;

(ii)

if for every subset
E
of
{\mathbb{N}}
with positive multiplicative density there exist distinct
x,y,z\in E
that satisfy the equation.

Note that a finite partition of
{\mathbb{N}}
always contains a monochromatic cell with positive multiplicative density, hence density regularity implies partition regularity. However, the converse is not true; for

, it is known that the equation

is partition regular if and only if

is a

, or
b=c
, or

(see
[
40
]
), but it is density regular only if

(the sufficiency follows from
[

and the necessity by an example of Bergelson, see remarks after
[
21

A difficult and well known problem

P(x,y,z)=0
when

is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial, and
the diagonal equations

Here we briefly sketch the main ideas of the proof of our main results.

3.1.
Proof sketch of Theorems
2.3
and
2.4

We start with
Theorem

2.3
and explain the main differences in the proof of
Theorem

2.4
at the end of this subsection.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action and

. The spectral measure
\sigma_{F}
of

is supported on the compact space of completely multiplicative functions with values on the unit circle and can be decomposed into two components
\sigma_{p}
and
\sigma_{a}
,
which are supported on the complementary Borel sets of pretentious and aperiodic multiplicative functions. The spectral theory of unitary operators gives that there exist functions
F_{p}
and
F_{a}
with spectral measures
\sigma_{p}
and
\sigma_{a}
respectively and
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
.
Pretentious multiplicative functions are known to satisfy various concentration estimates (as in
Proposition

4.3
), which we show are inherited by the iterates
T_{n}F_{p}
(as in
Proposition

5.3
).
We also show using
Proposition

5.4
that the subspace
X_{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
of bounded functions with spectral measures supported on pretentious multiplicative functions is a conjugation closed algebra, hence it defines a factor, and

F_{p}={\mathbb{E}}(F|{\mathcal{X}}_{p})
.

Finally, we need to verify the two vanishing properties for
F_{a}
.
Since the spectral measure of
F_{a}
is supported on aperiodic multiplicative functions, the vanishing property of part (
iii
) follows from the corresponding property of aperiodic multiplicative functions
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
. This property holds for general multiplicative actions as well.

The fact that
\lvert\!|\!|F_{a}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0
for every
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
requires more work and uses in an essential way that the action is finitely generated. The key step is to establish the following inverse theorem: If
F\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfies

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{qn+r}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0
for every

, then

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0
for every

. The assumption is easily shown to be satisfied by
F_{a}
, since its spectral measure is supported on aperiodic multiplicative functions.
To prove the inverse theorem, we roughly argue as follows: We can assume that
\left\|F\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. If the conclusion fails, then for some sequence along which a mixed seminorm stays positive there is a further
subsequence
N^{\prime}_{k}\to\infty
and a set
E\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(E)>0
, such that

(3.1)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\|F(T_{n}x)\right\|_{U^{s}[N^{\prime}_{k}]}>0\quad\text{for all }x\in E.

By a further diagonal extraction, we may assume that, for almost
every
x\in X
, the averages

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[\alpha N^{\prime}_{k}]}F(T_{qn+r}x)
converge to zero as

,
simultaneously for all
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

, and rational

.
Now the key point is that since the action

is finitely generated, there exists a set of primes

with positive upper relative density in

such that

for all

which can be thought of as a form of multiplicative structure. At this point the argument used to prove

applies with only minor changes. It enables us to deduce from (

) and (

), that along this subsequence, there exist

for all

of

.
This contradicts the almost everywhere convergence to zero along the chosen subsequence and completes the proof.

4.
Background and preliminary results

In this section we gather some necessary background notions, examples, results, and consequences from number theory and ergodic theory, which will be used later on.

4.1.
Pretentious and aperiodic multiplicative functions

A function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
, where
{\mathbb{U}}
is the unit disk, is called
multiplicative
if

f(mn)=f(m)\cdot f(n)\quad\text{ whenever }(m,n)=1,

and
completely multiplicative
if the previous equation holds for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We let

{\mathcal{M}}:=\{f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}\text{ is a completely multiplicative function}\}.

We endow
{\mathcal{M}}
with the topology of pointwise convergence, which makes it a compact metric space.

Definition 4.1
.

A multiplicative function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
is called
aperiodic
if

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f(an+b)=0\quad\text{for all }\,a\in{\mathbb{N}},\,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

It is called
pretentious
if it is not aperiodic.
It is called
finitely generated
if the set
\{f(p)\colon p\in{\mathbb{P}}\}
is finite.

We denote with
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
and

{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
the complementary sets of pretentious and aperiodic completely multiplicative functions
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

It is easy to verify that
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
are Borel subsets of
{\mathcal{M}}

[
21
, Lemma 3.6]
.

4.2.
Pretentious multiplicative functions

We give some examples of pretentious completely multiplicative functions; the first three are the most basic and the last is more sophisticated
but useful to keep in mind.

(i)

(Dirichlet characters and their modifications)
A
Dirichlet character
\chi
is a non-zero periodic completely multiplicative function. Then
\chi(Qn+1)=1
whenever
Q
is a multiple of its smallest period
q
and
\chi(p)=0
whenever
p\mid q
. We let
\tilde{\chi}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be the completely multiplicative function defined by
\tilde{\chi}(p):=\chi(p)
if
p\nmid q
and

\tilde{\chi}(p):=1
when
p\mid q
, and call it a
modified Dirichlet character
.

(ii)

(Finitely supported) If
f(p)=1
for all but finitely many primes we say that
f
has
finite support
.

(iii)

(Archimedean characters) An
Archimedean character
is a completely multiplicative function of the form
n\mapsto n^{it}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,n^{it}
is asymptotically equal to
N^{it}/(1+it)
.

(iv)

(
1
-pretentious oscillatory) Take
f(p):=e((\log\log{p})^{-1})
,
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
. Then
f
is
1
-pretentious in a sense that we will explain below, but it turns out that
f
does not have a mean value; in fact,
{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f(n)
is asymptotically equal to
c\,e(w(N))
, where

w(N)=\log\log\log N
and
c
a non-zero complex number.

Note that the first two examples are finitely generated, and the last two are not (with
t\neq 0
in example (
iii
)).

A completely multiplicative function that is not aperiodic is, in a sense that will be made precise in
Proposition

4.1
below, close to
a product of a Dirichlet character and an Archimedean character. To make sense of this principle we use the following notions
introduced by Granville and Soundararajan
[
26
,
27
]
:

Definition 4.2
.

If
f,g\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
are multiplicative functions, we define their distance as

(4.1)

{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(f,g):=\sum_{p\in{\mathbb{P}}}\frac{1}{p}\big(1-\Re(f(p)\cdot\overline{g(p)})\big).

We say that
f
pretends to be
g
and write
f\sim g
if
{\mathbb{D}}(f,g)<\infty
.

It can be shown (see e.g.
[
27
]
) that
{\mathbb{D}}
satisfies the triangle inequality

(4.2)

{\mathbb{D}}(f,g)\leq{\mathbb{D}}(f,h)+{\mathbb{D}}(h,g)

for all
f,g,h\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
.

The next result is a simple consequence of the
Wirsing-Halász mean value theorem
[
31
]
, which characterizes completely multiplicative functions that have mean value
0
.

Proposition 4.1
.

A completely multiplicative function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
is pretentious if and only if there exist

t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
f\sim\chi\cdot n^{it}
.

Using this characterization it is possible to show that pretentious multiplicative functions satisfy strong concentration properties that we describe next.

4.2.1.
Concentration estimates for pretentious multiplicative functions

If
f\sim\chi\cdot n^{it}
for some Dirichlet character

(4.3)

Note that for

Note that if

proves (

).

We prove (

4.3.
Examples of multiplicative actions

We give some examples of multiplicative actions
that appear frequently in this article.

4.3.1.
Multiplicative rotations

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a completely multiplicative function,
X
be the closure of the range of

f
, which is either
\mathbb{S}^{1}
or a finite subgroup of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
m_{X}
be the Haar measure on
X
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we consider the action defined by the maps
T_{n}\colon X\to X
as
T_{n}x:=f(n)x
for
x\in X
, and call it a
multiplicative rotation by
f
.
The action is finitely generated if and only if
f
is finitely generated.
For example, if
\lambda
is the Liouville function (i.e.,
\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}
), then
X=\{-1,1\}
and
m_{X}=(\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1})/2
. If
f
is as in examples (
iii
) (for
t\neq 0
) or (
iv
) of
Section

4.2
, then
X=\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
m_{X}=m_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
.

4.3.2.
Multiplicative dilations

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. On
{\mathbb{T}}
with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra and the Haar measure
m_{\mathbb{T}}
, we define for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the maps
T_{n}\colon{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{T}}
by
T_{n}x:=n^{k}x\pmod{1}
. We call the resulting multiplicative action
a dilation by
k
-th powers on
{\mathbb{T}}
. This action is infinitely generated. More generally, given a completely multiplicative function
\phi\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
, we can define a multiplicative
action by dilations
as follows

T_{n}x:=\phi(n)x\pmod{1}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, on
{\mathbb{T}}
with
m_{\mathbb{T}}
. The resulting multiplicative action is finitely generated if and only if the set
\{\phi(p)\colon p\in{\mathbb{P}}\}
is finite. One such example is given by
T_{n}x=2^{\Omega(n)}x\pmod{1}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

4.3.3.
Aperiodic multiplicative actions

We say that a multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is
aperiodic
if for all
a\in{\mathbb{N}},b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{an+b}F=\int F\,d\mu
in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
See
[
12
, Corollary 1.17]
for a characterization of aperiodicity in the case of finitely generated multiplicative actions that uses the notion of pretentious rational eigenfunctions; we will not use it in this article though.

It is shown in
[
12
, Corollary 1.23 (ii)]
that a multiplicative rotation by a finitely generated multiplicative function
f
is aperiodic if and only if
f^{k}
is aperiodic for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
k<|X|
, where
|X|
may be infinite. In particular, the multiplicative rotation by the Liouville function
\lambda
is aperiodic.
It is also shown in
[
12
, Corollary 1.24 (ii)]
that if
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
is an ergodic measure preserving system, then the action defined by
T_{n}x:=T^{\Omega(n)}x
,
x\in X
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is aperiodic. Taking
Tx:=2x\pmod{1}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
with
m_{\mathbb{T}}
, we get that the action by dilations
T_{n}x=2^{\Omega(n)}x\pmod{1}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is aperiodic. Finally, using (
4.22
), Weyl’s equidistribution result for polynomials, and the bounded convergence theorem,
it is easy to show that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
actions by dilations by
k
-th powers are aperiodic.

4.4.
Spectral theory of multiplicative actions

Recall that

{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the set of completely multiplicative functions with values on the unit circle. Since completely multiplicative functions on the positive rationals are uniquely determined by their values on the positive integers,
we can identify
{\mathcal{M}}
with the Pontryagin dual of the (discrete) group of positive rational numbers under multiplication.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Note that the map

r/s\mapsto\int T_{r}F\cdot T_{s}\overline{F}\,d\mu
,
r,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, from
({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+},\times)
to
{\mathbb{C}}
is well defined and positive definite. Using a theorem of Bochner-Herglotz, we get that there exists a finite Borel measure
\sigma_{F}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\int T_{r}F\cdot T_{s}\overline{F}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(r)\cdot\overline{f(s)}\,d\sigma_{F}(f)\quad\text{for all }r,s\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}.

We easily deduce from this the following identity that we will use frequently

(4.22)

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{l}c_{k}\,T_{r_{k}}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{l}c_{k}f(r_{k})\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}\quad\text{for all }l\in{\mathbb{N}},\,c_{1},\ldots,c_{l}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}.

Recall that if
r=m/n
,
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
T_{r}=T_{m}\circ T^{-1}_{n}

and
f(r)=f(m)/f(n)=f(m)\cdot\overline{f(n)}
, and also
T_{1}=\text{id}
,
f(1)=1
.
In particular, we have

(4.23)

\left\|T_{n}F-F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|f(n)-1\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We will use the following basic facts about spectral measures.
If
\mu,\nu
are two finite Borel measures on
{\mathcal{M}}
, we write

\mu\bot\nu
if they are mutually singular and
\mu\ll\nu
if
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
. If two functions
F,G\in L^{2}(\mu)
are orthogonal, we write
F\bot G
. Lastly, we say that the measure
\sigma
on
{\mathcal{M}}
is supported on a Borel subset
A
of
{\mathcal{M}}
if
\sigma({\mathcal{M}}\setminus A)=0
.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is a multiplicative action, the following statements hold:

(i)

\sigma_{T_{r}F}=\sigma_{F}
,

, for all

(ii)

(iii)

Recall that a multiplicative function

be the decomposition of

5.2
with
F_{p}\in X_{p}
,
F_{a}\in X_{a}
, and
F_{p}\bot F_{a}
. Since
F_{a}\in X_{a}
, part (
i
) of
Proposition

5.6
gives that
for every
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{Q_{K}n+1}F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

By the already established forward direction we get that
F_{p}
satisfies (
5.2
), hence

\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{Q_{K}n+1}F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Since
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
, from the previous three identities we deduce that

\left\|F_{p}+F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Since
F_{p}\bot F_{a}
this implies that
F_{a}=0
. Hence,
F=F_{p}\in X_{p}
.

To prove (
5.3
), put

B_{Q,b,N}(F):={\mathbb{E}}_{r\in[N]}T_{Qr+b}F
.
By (
4.22
),

(5.5)

\big\|T_{Q_{K}n+b}F-B_{Q_{K},b,N}(F)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\big\|f(Q_{K}n+b)-{\mathbb{E}}_{r\in[N]}f(Q_{K}r+b)\big\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F})}.

Since
F\in X_{p}
,
Proposition

4.7
implies that
\sigma_{F}

is supported on pretentious finitely generated functions, which satisfy

f\sim\chi
for some Dirichlet character
\chi
by

[
12
, Lemma B.3]
.
Thus (
4.11
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Fatou’s lemma give
(
5.3
) with
B_{Q_{K},b,N}(F)
in place of
A_{Q_{K},b}(F)
.
For fixed
K
, the set
S_{K}
is finite and

B_{Q_{K},b,N}(F)\to A_{Q_{K},b}(F)
in
L^{2}(\mu)
uniformly over

b\in S_{K}
, by
Proposition

4.9
.
The triangle inequality therefore gives (
5.3
)
as stated.
∎

We next give a variant of the previous result for general multiplicative actions.
Part (
i
) is used to prove part (
iii
), which in turn is used in the proof of
Corollary

5.5
, and
part (
ii
) is used to prove
Proposition

5.6
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a general multiplicative action,
S_{\delta,N}
be as in (
2.5
), and
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

(i)

We have
F\in X_{p}

if and only if

(5.6)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

(ii)

If
F\in X_{p}
and
F\neq 0
, then

(5.7)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{k!n+1}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}>0.

(iii)

We have
F\in X_{p}

if and only if

(5.8)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,\left\|T_{k!n+1}F-A_{\delta,k,N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,

for some
A_{\delta,k,N}\in L^{2}(\mu)

(in which case we can take
A_{\delta,k,N}={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F
).

(iv)

If
F\in X_{p}
,
then for every
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(5.9)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\left\|T_{Qn+b}F-A_{\delta,Q,N,b}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,

where for
Q,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
A_{\delta,Q,N,b}:={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{Qn+b}F
.

Proof.

We prove (
i
). Suppose that
F\in X_{p}
. Using (
4.22
) we get

\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,f(k!n+1)\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}

for every
k,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
. Note also that

\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|1\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}

and
\sigma_{F}
is supported in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
.
Using the previous facts, Fatou’s lemma three times and identity (
4.17
) of
Corollary

4.5
, we get that the left side of (
5.6
) (with
\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}
in place of
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}
) is at least

\left\|1\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}=\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
. On the other hand,

\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
for every
k,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\delta>0
. Combining these facts we get that
(
5.6
) holds.

To prove the converse, suppose that
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
satisfies (
5.6
). Let
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
be the decomposition given by
Proposition

5.2
. Since
F_{a}\in X_{a}
, part (
ii
) of
Proposition

5.6
below implies that
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Since
F_{p}\in X_{p}
we get by the already established forward direction, that
F_{p}
satisfies (
5.6
). We deduce that

\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|F_{p}+F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Since
F_{p}\bot F_{a}
, this implies that
F_{a}=0
. Hence,
F=F_{p}\in X_{p}
.

We prove (
ii
). Using (
4.22
) and Fatou’s lemma twice
we get that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{k!n+1}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\geq\left\|A(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))},

where

A(f):=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f(k!n+1)|.

By (
4.18
) in
Corollary

4.5
we get that

A(f)>0
for every
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
.
Using this, and since
\sigma_{F}
is supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
and
\sigma_{F}\neq 0
(since
F\neq 0
), we deduce that

\left\|A(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}>0
.

We prove (
iii
). Suppose that
F\in X_{p}
. For
k,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
, let
A_{\delta,k,N}:={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F
. After expanding the square below, we find that

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,\left\|T_{k!n+1}F-A_{\delta,k,N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}-\left\|A_{\delta,k,N}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Using this and (
5.6
), we deduce that (
5.8
) holds.

Conversely, suppose that
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
satisfies (
5.8
). Let
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
be the decomposition given by
Proposition

5.2
. Since
F_{a}\in X_{a}
, part (
ii
) of
Proposition

5.6
below and (
4.22
) imply that
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F_{a}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

We deduce from this, (
5.8
), and the triangle inequality, that

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F_{p}-A_{\delta,k,N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

It follows that (
5.8
) holds with

B_{\delta,k,N}:={\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{k!n+1}F_{p}
instead of
A_{\delta,k,N}
, i.e.,

(5.10)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,\left\|T_{k!n+1}F-B_{\delta,k,N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=0.

Note that
B_{\delta,k,N}\in X_{p}
, since
F_{p}\in X_{p}
and
X_{p}
is a
T_{n}
-invariant subspace. Hence,

T_{k!n+1}F_{a}\bot\,T_{k!n+1}F_{p}-B_{\delta,k,N}\quad\text{for every }k,N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\delta>0.

Since
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
, we deduce from this, (
5.10
), and the Pythagorean theorem, that

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,\left\|T_{k!n+1}F_{p}-B_{\delta,k,N}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\left\|F_{a}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Hence,
F_{a}=0
, which implies that

.

Finally, part (
iv
) follows by combining (
4.22
) with (
4.16
)
in
Corollary

4.5
, and applying Fatou’s lemma multiple times.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action. Then
X_{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
is a conjugation closed subalgebra.

Proof.

We know from
Lemma

5.1
that
X_{p}
is a
T_{n}
-invariant subspace,
and it easily follows from the definition of
X_{p}
that it is conjugation
closed. Thus, it remains to show
that
X_{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
is closed under products. Let

F_{1},F_{2}\in X_{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

For
j=1,2
we use (
5.8
) in
Proposition

5.4
,
with
F_{j}
instead of
F
, and some functions

A_{j,\delta,k,N}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
instead of
A_{\delta,k,N}
.
We also note that for
j=1,2
we have

\left\|A_{j,\delta,k,N}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq\left\|F_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}
,
for every
\delta>0
and
k,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using these facts and the triangle inequality, we get that
(
5.8
) is satisfied with
F_{1}\cdot F_{2}
instead of

and

instead of

.
From the converse direction in part (

) of

, it follows that

.

(For finitely generated actions, we could instead use (

) in

5.3.
Characterization of aperiodic functions-Vanishing property

The next result gives a useful characterization of
X_{a}
that works for general multiplicative actions.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

(i)

We have

F\in X_{a}
if and only if

(5.11)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{an+b}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0\quad\text{for every }a\in{\mathbb{N}},\,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

(ii)

If
F\in X_{a}
, then

(5.12)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}\,T_{an+b}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0\quad\text{for every }\delta>0,\,a\in{\mathbb{N}},\,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

where
S_{\delta,N}
is as in (
2.5
).

Remark
.

If we relax the mean convergence condition in (
5.11
) to weak convergence, i.e.,

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\int T_{an+b}F\cdot G\,d\mu=0\quad\text{for every }a\in{\mathbb{N}},\,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\,G\in L^{2}(\mu),

then the converse direction no longer holds. Let us see
how to prove this when
G:=\overline{F}
. Consider a multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that

\int T_{n}F\cdot T_{m}\overline{F}\,d\mu=\int_{0}^{1}n^{2\pi it}\cdot m^{-2\pi it}\,dt\quad\text{for all }m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(For example, on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
with
m_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}
, let
T_{n}(t,x):=(t,x+\{t\}\log{n})
and
F(t,x):=e(x)
.)
Then,
\sigma_{F}
is supported on the set
\{(n^{it})\colon t\in[0,2\pi]\}
, hence
F\in X_{p}
. However,
for every
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\int T_{an+b}F\cdot\overline{F}\,d\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(aN)^{2\pi it}\cdot(1+2\pi it)^{-1}\,dt=0,

where the first identity follows by first using (
4.22
) and then (
4.21
), and the second from a variant of the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma.

The same example can be used to show that if
X^{\prime}_{p}
is the closed subspace spanned by the pretentious eigenfunctions with eigenvalues pretentious multiplicative functions (defined as in
[
12
]
but for general actions) and
F
is as before, then
F\bot X^{\prime}_{p}
(although
F\in X_{p}
, hence
X^{\prime}_{p}\neq X_{p}
) and (
5.11
) fails even when
a=1
.

Lastly, using a multiplicative rotation by
n^{i}
, it can be seen that
the converse implication in (
i
)
fails if we use logarithmic averages.

Proof.

We prove (
i
).
Suppose that
F\in X_{a}
. Then the spectral measure of
F
is supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
.
Let
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Using (
4.22
) we get

\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{an+b}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f(an+b)\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}

for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
\sigma_{F}
is supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
and for
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,f(an+b)=0,

the needed vanishing property follows from the bounded convergence theorem.

For the converse direction, it suffices to show that if (
5.11
) holds for
F
and
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
is the decomposition given by
Proposition

5.2
, then
F_{p}=0
.
Note first that since (
5.11
) holds for
F
and
F_{a}
, we get that it also holds for
F_{p}
, so for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{k!n+1}F_{p}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Using part (
ii
) of
Proposition

5.4
, we deduce that
F_{p}=0
, completing the proof.

We prove (
ii
). Let
F\in X_{a}
,
\delta>0
,
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and

M_{N}:=[e^{-\delta}N].

For all sufficiently large
N
we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in S_{\delta,N}}T_{an+b}F=\frac{N}{N-M_{N}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}T_{an+b}F-\frac{M_{N}}{N-M_{N}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[M_{N}]}T_{an+b}F.

Both averages on the right hand side converge to
0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
by the
forward implication in part (
i
), while the two coefficients
remain bounded for fixed
\delta
. This proves (
5.12
).
∎

We next state another vanishing property of aperiodic functions, in a stronger form than the one needed to prove part (
ii
) in
Theorem

2.4
; this stronger form
will be used later in the proof of
Proposition

8.5
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and
F\in X_{a}
. Suppose also that

R_{1},R_{2}
are rational polynomials that factor linearly and
R_{1}
is not of the form
c\,R^{r}
for any
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
, rational polynomial
R
, and
r\geq 2
. Let also
K_{N}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be convex subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}
, and
\alpha,\beta,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

(5.13)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,{\bf 1}_{K_{N}}(m,n)\cdot e(m\alpha+n\beta)\cdot(R_{2}(m,n))^{it}\cdot T_{R_{1}(m,n)}F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Remark
.

For
t=0
, (
5.13
) also holds for

R_{1}=c\prod_{j}L_{j}^{k_{j}}
, where
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}
,
the
L_{j}
are pairwise independent integer-coefficient linear forms,
and
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
\gcd_{j}|k_{j}|=1
, on convex averaging sets
where
R_{1}
is defined and positive. The conclusion also holds on
fixed affine grids. These extensions follow from the same spectral
argument, using the even extension of multiplicative functions in

[
20
, Lemma 9.6]
and expanding congruence indicators into
additive characters.

Proof.

Suppose first that
t=0
.
Using (
4.22
) and the fact that the spectral measure
\sigma_{F}
of
F
is supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
, it suffices to show that for every aperiodic multiplicative function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
we have

(5.14)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,{\bf 1}_{K_{N}}(m,n)\cdot e(m\alpha+n\beta)\cdot f(R_{1}(m,n))=0.

We have

R_{1}(m,n)=c\,\prod_{j=1}^{s}L^{k_{j}}_{j}(m,n)

for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+},s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, non-zero
k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and pairwise independent linear forms
L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}
with non-negative coefficients.

We first claim that not all the multiplicative functions
f^{k_{1}},\ldots,f^{k_{s}}
are pretentious. Indeed, if this was the case, then (
4.2
) gives that

f^{d}
is pretentious, where
d:=\gcd(k_{1},\ldots,k_{s})
.
Then
R_{1}=c\,R^{d}
, where
R:=\prod_{j=1}^{s}L^{k_{j}/d}_{j}
is a rational polynomial, and our assumption about
R_{1}
gives that
d=1
. Hence,
f
is pretentious, a contradiction.

We deduce that there exists
j_{0}\in[s]
such that
f^{k_{j_{0}}}
is aperiodic. The required vanishing property (
5.14
) then follows from
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
and
[
20
, Lemma 9.6]

(the latter does not include the exponential term
e(m\alpha+n\beta)
, but the same argument applies.)

Finally, we consider the case where
t
can be non-zero. After carrying out the previous deductions, it suffices to show that if
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
are completely multiplicative functions such that
f_{1}
is aperiodic,
L_{1},\ldots,L_{\ell}
are linear forms such that
L_{1},L_{j}
are independent for
j=2,\ldots,\ell
, and
R
is a rational polynomial that factors linearly, then

(5.15)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,{\bf 1}_{K_{N}}(m,n)\cdot e(m\alpha+n\beta)\cdot(R(m,n))^{it}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}f_{j}(L_{j}(m,n))=0.

To see this, first suppose that
R=c^{\prime}\,L^{k}
for some linear form
L
and
c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
L
is a rational multiple of
L_{1}
, then after replacing
f_{1}
by

f_{1}\cdot n^{ikt}
, which is aperiodic by
Proposition

4.1
, we get that our assumptions are still satisfied and the term
(L(m,n))^{ikt}
is absorbed in
f_{1}
. We can therefore assume that
L_{1},L
are independent, so we can work with the
\ell+1
multiplicative functions,

f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell},f_{\ell+1}
, where
f_{\ell+1}(n):=n^{ikt}
, which are evaluated at the linear forms
L_{1},\ldots,L_{\ell},L
.
Since
L_{1}
is independent of the other
\ell
linear forms we get the necessary vanishing property. In general,
R=c^{\prime}\,R_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots R_{s}^{k_{s}}
where
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
are pairwise independent linear forms and
c^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+},k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. At most one of the linear forms
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
can be a rational multiple of
L_{1}
, in which case it can be absorbed by
f_{1}
as before, and the other forms can be handled by extending the product using additional multiplicative functions, again, exactly as before.
∎

5.4.
Proof of
Theorem

2.4

Let
F\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Using
Proposition

5.2
we get that there exist
F_{p},F_{a}\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
F_{p}\in X_{p},F_{a}\in X_{a}
and
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
. We also get that
F_{p}
is the orthogonal projection of
F
onto
X_{p}
.
Moreover, by
Corollary

5.5
the space
X_{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
is a conjugation closed subalgebra, so it defines a factor
{\mathcal{X}}_{p}
and
F_{p}={\mathbb{E}}(F|{\mathcal{X}}_{p})
. In particular we have that
F_{p}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, hence
F_{a}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
Properties (
i
) and
(
ii
) follow from
(
5.9
) in
Proposition

5.4
, applied to

F_{p}
, and (
5.13
) in
Proposition

5.7
, respectively,
completing the proof.

6.
Mixed seminorms and related inverse theorem

Our goal in this section is to define the mixed seminorms, show that for finitely generated multiplicative actions they vanish on the subspace

, and use this fact to
complete the proof of Theorem

. For a proof sketch of the vanishing property see
Section

.

6.1.
Mixed seminorms for multiplicative actions

We begin by recalling the definition of the Gowers norms of a
finite sequence on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}={\mathbb{Z}}/(N{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Definition 6.1

(Gowers norms on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}

Let

and

Gowers norm

is defined inductively as follows: We let

and for every

\left\|a\right\|_{U^{s+1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}^{2^{s+1}}:={\mathbb{E}}_{h\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}}\left\|a\cdot\overline{a}_{h}\right\|_{U^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}^{2^{s}},

where

for

For example, we have

\left\|a\right\|_{U^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}^{4}={\mathbb{E}}_{n,h_{1},h_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}}\,a(n)\,\overline{a}(n+h_{1})\,\overline{a}(n+h_{2})\,a(n+h_{1}+h_{2}),

and for

we get a similar formula with

terms in the product.

Definition 6.2
.

Given a multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and

, for

we define the
mixed seminorm of

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert^{2^{s}}_{U^{s}}:=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\left\|F(T_{n}x)\right\|^{2^{s}}_{U^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}\,d\mu,

where for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

x\in X
, we assume that
(F(T_{n}x))_{n\in[N]}
is extended periodically on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
.

Remarks
.

\bullet

We can also define the closely related seminorms

(6.4)

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert^{2^{s}}_{*,U^{s}}:=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\left\|F(T_{n}x)\right\|^{2^{s}}_{U^{s}[N]}\,d\mu,

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{U^{s}[N]}
are as in
[
20
, Section 2.1.1]
.
It follows from the proof of
[

, Lemma A.4]
that there exists

such that for every sequence

we have

\left\|a\right\|_{U^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}^{K}\ll_{s}\left\|a\right\|_{U^{s}[N]}+o_{N}(1)\ll_{s}\left\|a\right\|_{U^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}_{N})}^{\frac{1}{K}},

where

is a quantity that does not depend on

and converges to

as

.
As a result, we have

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0\Leftrightarrow\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{*,U^{s}}=0
.

\bullet
If in (
6.3
) we place the limsup inside the integral, then we get seminorms that are too strong for our purposes. For example,
F\in X_{a}
would not imply that
\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{1}}=0
, since this would imply that

for almost every

.

Since the Gowers norms

satisfy the triangle inequality and are increasing in

,
we easily deduce that the mixed seminorms also enjoy similar properties, i.e.,

(6.5)

\lvert\!|\!|F+G|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}\leq\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}+\lvert\!|\!|G|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}\leq\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s+1}}

for all
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
F,G\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

To get a sense of how the mixed seminorms look like, we note that (

) gives

(6.6)

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert^{4}_{U^{2}}=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big({\mathbb{E}}_{n,h_{1},h_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}}\int T_{n}F\cdot T_{n+h_{1}}\overline{F}\cdot T_{n+h_{2}}\overline{F}\cdot T_{n+h_{1}+h_{2}}F\,d\mu\Big).

In (

), every subscript is represented in
[N]
modulo
N
.
For an ergodic multiplicative action, its integral can equivalently be
written as the
L^{2}
limit of multiplicative Følner averages of the
same product. This combines addition in the cube with multiplication
in the iterates and motivates the term “mixed seminorms”.

Lastly, we note that the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}
differ sharply from the Host-Kra seminorms of order
s
in
the multiplicative setting, which we denote here by
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{s,\times}
.
This can already be seen when

. For an ergodic multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
we have

(6.7)

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert^{4}_{2,\times}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\Phi_{N}}\int F\cdot T_{h_{1}}\overline{F}\cdot T_{h_{2}}\overline{F}\cdot T_{h_{1}\cdot h_{2}}F\,d\mu,

where
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a multiplicative Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
and the limit can be shown to exist. These seminorms are quite different from the mixed seminorms, satisfy substantially different inverse theorems, and do not play a role in our study here.

Lemma 6.2

Let

\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}/p]}\,w_{k}(pn)\cdot\overline{w_{k}(p^{\prime}n)}=0

, where

is a subset of the primes that satisfies

.
Let also

be such that (

) holds for some

\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}\,w_{k}(n)\cdot a(n)=0.

Proof.

Let

be a finite non-empty set and

H:=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{1}{p},\qquad\omega(n):=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\mathbf{1}_{p\mid n}.

Fix

, and let

and

Let

pm\leq N_{k}\end{subarray}}\gamma_{p}w_{k}(pm).

Since

{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N_{k}]}|\omega(n)-H|^{2}=\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+o(1)\leq H+o(1),

If we expand

by the hypothesis,
since its summation range is

As

Proof of part (

The concentration result we need uses averages on sets defined as in the next lemma.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
R
be a non-constant rational polynomial that factors linearly and
has degree
0
. Suppose that

R(a,b)=1
for some
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Then for every

the set

(8.23)

S_{\delta,R}:=\big\{(m,n)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}:\ |\log R(m,n)|\leq\delta\big\}

has positive lower density. Moreover, up to finitely many boundary rays,
it is the set of lattice points in a finite union of convex sectors.

Proof.

We have
R(m,n)=r(m/n)
, where

r(s):=R(s,1)

is positive and non-constant on
(0,\infty)
. Each of the
equations
r(s)=e^{\delta}
and
r(s)=e^{-\delta}
has finitely many
solutions, so

e^{-\delta}\leq r(s)\leq e^{\delta}

describes finitely many intervals and isolated points in the slope
variable. The assumption
R(a,b)=1
and continuity imply that one of these
intervals has non-empty interior (possibly with endpoint
0
or

\infty
). The corresponding sector has positive area in
(0,1]^{2}
,
and arguing exactly as in the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 6.4]
,
Riemann sums give that
S_{\delta,R}
has positive lower density.
∎

Here is the precise statement of the concentration result:

Proposition 8.8

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})

(8.24)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}{\mathbb{E}}_{(m,n)\in S_{\delta,R,N}}\left\|T_{R(Qm+a,Qn+b)}F-F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,

in (

In contrast to the finitely generated case (see part (

The case

Otherwise, we combine proportional factors and omit zero exponents.
Our assumption gives that

Using (
4.22

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}{\mathbb{E}}_{(m,n)\in S_{\delta,R,N}}|f(R(Qm+a,Qn+b))-1|=0.

Henceforth, we will assume that

for some

with period

and

We first claim that for every occurring linear form

\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K}}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}|f(R(Qm+a,Qn+b))-\\

9.
Further directions

9.1.
Two conjectures

The methodology developed in this article addresses some of the potential difficulties in a more systematic study of multiple recurrence and convergence problems of multiplicative actions. We provide a list of problems that seem to be logical next steps in this endeavor starting with two conjectures that vastly extend the scope of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
when we deal with a single multiplicative action.

Conjecture 1

(Mean convergence)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{\ell}
be rational polynomials that factor linearly. Then for all
F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
the averages

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,T_{R_{1}(m,n)}F_{1}\cdots T_{R_{\ell}(m,n)}F_{\ell}

converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
N\to\infty
.
Furthermore, if all the rational polynomials have degree
0
and are
multiplicatively independent modulo constants, that is,

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}R_{j}^{k_{j}}
is nonconstant for every

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\setminus\{0\}
,
then the conclusion holds for all multiplicative actions.

For general multiplicative actions, the assumption that all rational polynomials have degree
0
is necessary for convergence, see the remarks following
Theorem

2.1
. The multiplicative independence assumption for general actions cannot be
omitted. A counterexample for the pair
m/n,n/m
arises from the quadratic
phase
a(n):=e\big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}v_{p}(n)^{2}\big)
on
(\mathbb{N},\times)
.
Indeed, note that for every
k,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\overline{a}(kmn)^{2}a(km^{2})a(kn^{2})=e\Big(\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\big(v_{p}(m)-v_{p}(n)\big)^{2}\Big).

Take
\alpha_{p}=1/\log\log p
for
p\geq p_{0}
and zero otherwise.
It is not hard to show that the averages of the right-hand side over
m,n\in[N]
differ from

\big({\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}f(n)\big)^{2}
by
O(p_{0}^{-1})
, uniformly in
N
,
where
f
is completely multiplicative with
f(p)=e(\alpha_{p})
;
the discrepancy comes only from prime-square divisibility or common
prime factors.
As in example (
iv
) in
Section

4.2
, the means of
f

have a nonvanishing oscillatory main term. Hence, for sufficiently large

p_{0}
, the averages

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\big(\lim_{K\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{k\in\Phi_{K}}\overline{a}(kmn)^{2}a(km^{2})a(kn^{2})\big)

do not converge as
N\to\infty
, where
(\Phi_{K})
is any multiplicative
Følner sequence in
\mathbb{N}
. Via the multiplicative Furstenberg correspondence principle, this
obstruction yields a counterexample to mean convergence.

When
\ell=1
, for finitely generated actions, the conjecture follows from part (
i
) of
Theorem

2.2
(which in turn follows easily from the machinery developed in
[
20
,
21
]
), and for general actions
it follows by modifying the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 1.5]
in a straightforward way.
These arguments depend on the use of a representation result of Bochner-Herglotz, which only helps when
\ell=1
.

Conjecture 2

(Multiple recurrence)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action
and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{\ell}
be rational polynomials that factor linearly. Suppose that there exist
m_{0},n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, such that

R_{j}(m_{0},n_{0})=1
for
j=2,\ldots,\ell

and either
R_{1}(m_{0},n_{0})=1
or
(m_{0},n_{0})
is a simple zero of
R_{1}
.
Then for every
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
we have

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\mu(A\cap T^{-1}_{R_{1}(m,n)}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-1}_{R_{\ell}(m,n)}A)>0.

Furthermore, if all the rational polynomials
have degree
0
and

R_{j}(m_{0},n_{0})=1
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
for some
m_{0},n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, then the conclusion holds for all multiplicative actions.

To illustrate the scope of this conjecture, note that it predicts that for any multiplicative action we have the multiple recurrence property

\mu(T^{-1}_{m^{2}-n^{2}}A\cap T^{-1}_{m^{2}-4n^{2}}A\cap T^{-1}_{m^{2}-9n^{2}}A)>0

for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
m>3n
.
Indeed, after factoring out
T^{-1}_{m^{2}-n^{2}}
and making the substitution
m\mapsto m+3n
, one sees that
Conjecture

2
applies for
R_{1}(m,n):=(m+n)(m+5n)(m+2n)^{-1}(m+4n)^{-1}

and
R_{2}(m,n):=m(m+6n)(m+2n)^{-1}(m+4n)^{-1}
and
m_{0}:=1,n_{0}:=0
.

When
\ell=1
, for finitely generated actions, the conjecture follows from part (
ii
) of
Theorem

2.2
, and the part of the conjecture that refers to general actions follows for
\ell=1
by modifying the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 2.2]

in a straightforward way.

To understand the necessity of some of the assumptions made in
Conjecture

2
, we note that by considering suitable multiplicative rotations, we get finitely generated multiplicative actions
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and sets
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\mu(A)>0
and

(i)

\mu(A\cap T^{-1}_{2n^{2}}A)=0
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(
[
18
, Example 3.11]
);

(ii)

\mu(T^{-1}_{n}A\cap T^{-1}_{2n}A)=0
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\mu(A\cap T^{-1}_{m/n}A\cap T^{-1}_{(3m-n)/n}A)=0

for every
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
3m>n
.

In case (
i
) the polynomial
R(n):=2n^{2}
vanishes at
0
with a multiplicity greater than
1
. Finally, in case (
ii
) we cannot find
m_{0},n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

that satisfy the assumption of
Conjecture

2
.

9.2.
Inverse theorem for mixed seminorms and applications

Theorem

6.3
gives a simple inverse theorem
for the mixed seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}
, which covers all finitely generated multiplicative actions.
A similar inverse theorem fails for general multiplicative actions, in particular, none of the properties (
i
)-(
iii
) of
Theorem

6.3
implies property (
iv
). To see this, consider for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the multiplicative action of dilations by

k
-th powers on
{\mathbb{T}}
(see
Section

4.3
)
and let
F(x):=e(x)
,
x\in{\mathbb{T}}
.
For
k=1
we have
F\in X_{a}
but
\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{2}}\neq 0
,
and for
k=2
we have
\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{2}}=0
but
\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{3}}\neq 0
.
So the next problem comes naturally.

Problem 1
.

Find an inverse theorem and a decomposition result for the mixed seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}
that work for arbitrary multiplicative actions.

Optimally, given a multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
, we want to characterize the smallest factor
{\mathcal{Z}}
of the system such that if
F\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}(F|{\mathcal{Z}})=0
, then

\lvert\!|\!|F|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{s}}=0
.

In view of
Proposition

7.1
, a solution to
Problem

1
is likely
to lead to progress on natural multiple recurrence and convergence problems
for arbitrary multiplicative actions, such as the following special case
of
Conjecture

1
. It also provides a
useful test of whether
Problem

1
has been solved in a sufficiently effective form.

Problem 2
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action. Show that for all
F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
the following limit exists

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\int T_{m}F_{0}\cdot T_{m+n}F_{1}\cdots T_{m+\ell n}F_{\ell}\,d\mu.

In the case of finitely generated actions the conclusion follows from
Theorem

2.1
and it holds even for several not necessarily commuting multiplicative actions.
On the other hand, the conclusion fails if we remove the integrals and ask for mean convergence of the resulting averages; indeed, for a multiplicative rotation by
n^{i}
even the single term averages
{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,T_{(m+n)}F_{1}
do not converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

9.3.
Density regularity for some homogeneous quadratic equations

Next, we record some problems related to the density regularity (in the sense of
Definition

2.2
) of equations of the form (
2.6
). For example, consider the equation
x^{2}-y^{2}=xz
,
which is satisfied when
x=km^{2},y=kmn,z=k(m^{2}-n^{2})
,
k,m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Thus, to prove that this equation is density regular, it suffices to show that for any multiplicative action

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and

A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
, we have

(9.1)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N],m>n}\,\int T_{m^{2}}F\cdot T_{mn}F\cdot T_{m^{2}-n^{2}}F\,d\mu>0,

where
F:={\bf 1}_{A}
.
By
Theorem

2.4
, we have
F=F_{p}+F_{a}
, where
F_{p}={\mathbb{E}}(F|{\mathcal{X}}_{p})
and
F_{a}\in X_{a}
. Using (
8.24
) in
Proposition

8.8
and by adjusting (non-trivially) the argument in
Section

8.4
, we can probably show that (
9.1
) holds if we replace
F
by
F_{p}
. So a key remaining obstacle to proving (
9.1
) is to answer the next question:

Problem 3
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that
F_{2}\in X_{a}
or
F_{3}\in X_{a}
. Is it true that

(9.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N],m>n}\,\int T_{m^{2}}F_{1}\cdot T_{mn}F_{2}\cdot T_{m^{2}-n^{2}}F_{3}\,d\mu=0\,?

Proposition

8.5
covers the case where the multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is finitely generated. In the case of infinitely generated actions,
it is easy to verify that for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the multiplicative
actions of dilations by

k
-th powers on
{\mathbb{T}}
(see
Section

4.3
), or products of such actions,
do not pose obstructions to the vanishing property (
9.2
), since if

P_{1}(m^{2})+P_{2}(mn)+P_{3}(m^{2}-n^{2})=0
for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and some
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
with zero constant terms, then
P_{1}=P_{2}=P_{3}=0
.

9.4.
Controlling “Pythagorean averages” by mixed seminorms

It is natural to explore if methods from ergodic theory can
be used to prove partition regularity for Pythagorean triples, i.e., for the equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
, which is satisfied when
x=2mn,y=m^{2}-n^{2},z=m^{2}+n^{2}
. We are naturally led to study
the limiting behavior of the averages in (
9.3
) below and to study the following problem:

Problem 4
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be such that
\lvert\!|\!|F_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{3}}=0
for
j=1
or
2
. Is it true that

(9.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N],m>n}\,\int T_{mn}F_{1}\cdot T_{m^{2}-n^{2}}F_{2}\cdot T_{m^{2}+n^{2}}F_{3}\,d\mu=0\,?

We remark that the assumption
\lvert\!|\!|F_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{3}}=0
cannot be replaced by
F_{j}\in X_{a}
or
\lvert\!|\!|F_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{2}}=0
. To see this, consider the
action of dilations by squares on
{\mathbb{T}}
(see
Section

4.3
), and let
F_{1}(x):=e(4x)
,
F_{2}(x):=e(x)
,
F_{3}(x)=e(-x)
. Then
F_{j}\in X_{a}
and
\lvert\!|\!|F_{j}|\!|\!\rvert_{U^{2}}=0
for
j=1,2,3,
but all the integrals in (
9.3
) are
1
for
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Using a variant of the Daboussi-Kátai orthogonality criterion (see
Lemma

8.1
) for the Gaussian integers, it is not hard to see that the needed seminorm control in
Problem

4

would follow from a similar seminorm control for averages of the form

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\int T_{L_{1}(m,n)\cdot L_{2}(m,n)}F_{1}\cdot T_{L_{3}(m,n)\cdot L_{4}(m,n)}F_{2}\cdot T_{L_{5}(m,n)\cdot L_{6}(m,n)}F_{3}\cdot T_{L_{7}(m,n)\cdot L_{8}(m,n)}F_{4}\,d\mu,

where
L_{1}(m,n),\ldots,L_{8}(m,n)
are pairwise independent linear forms. We do not know how to handle this problem even for finitely generated multiplicative actions, in which case

F
has vanishing mixed seminorms if and only if

F\in X_{a}
(see
Theorem

6.3
).
In this regard, we state the following simpler model problem:

Problem 5
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action and let
F_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
j\in[3]
, be such that
F_{j}\in X_{a}
for some
j\in[3]
. Is it true that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N],m>2n}\,\int T_{mn}F_{1}\cdot T_{m^{2}-n^{2}}F_{2}\cdot T_{m^{2}-4n^{2}}F_{3}\,d\mu=0\,?

This problem resembles the one addressed in
Proposition

8.5
, which allows us to deal with
three quadratic polynomials that factor linearly, but only when two of them have a common linear factor.

9.5.
Characteristic factor larger than
X_{p}
.

Finally, we record two problems for finitely generated multiplicative actions
where the factor
X_{p}
does not control the limiting behavior of the averages we aim to study.

Problem 6
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action. Show that the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,T_{m}F_{1}\cdot T_{n}F_{2}\cdot T_{m+n}F_{3}\cdot T_{mn}F_{4}

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
for all
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, and

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\mu(A\cap T^{-1}_{m}A\cap T^{-1}_{n}A\cap T^{-1}_{m+n}A\cap T^{-1}_{mn}A)>0

for all
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
.

It follows from work of Bowen and Sabok
[
9
]
, which was later extended by Alweiss
[
1
]
, that the patterns
\{km,kn,k(m+n),kmn\colon k,m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are partition regular. However, the recurrence part of
Problem

6
relates more to density regularity and fails for general multiplicative actions.
See
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
for related multiple recurrence results.

Finally, we mention the following problem, which seems deceptively simple:

Problem 7
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n},S_{n})
be a finitely generated multiplicative action of commuting measure preserving transformations.
Show that the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}\,T_{n}F_{1}\cdot S_{n}F_{2}

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
for all
F_{1},F_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Similarly for more than two commuting actions.

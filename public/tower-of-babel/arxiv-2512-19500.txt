Transitive sets of derangements in primitive actions of PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \PSL_{2}(q)
Peter Müller

Abstract
. Problem 8.75 of the Kourovka Notebook [ 10 ] , attributed to John G. Thompson, asks the following: Suppose G G is a finite primitive permutation group on Ω \Omega , and α \alpha , β \beta are distinct points of Ω \Omega . Does there exist an element g ∈ G g\in G such that α g = β \alpha^{g}=\beta and g g fixes no point of Ω \Omega ? A recent negative example is given in [ 12 ] , where G G is the Steinberg triality group D 4 3 ​ ( 2 ) {}^{3}D_{4}(2) acting primitively on 4,064,256 4{,}064{,}256 points. At present this is the only negative example known. In this note we show that almost simple primitive permutation groups with socle isomorphic to PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \PSL_{2}(q) do not give negative examples.



1.
Introduction

It is an easy exercise using the orbit-counting theorem to show that
if
G
is a transitive permutation group on a finite set
\Omega
,
then the set of derangements of
G
generates a transitive subgroup.

It is also easy to see that in general the set of derangements,
together with the identity, need not be transitive on
\Omega
. A
small example is the alternating group
A_{4}
acting on the
2
-subsets
of
\{1,2,3,4\}
: An element
g\in A_{4}
moving
\{1,2\}
to

\{3,4\}
moves every element in its natural degree
4
action, so
it is a double transposition, and therefore fixes some
2
-subset.

While there are many more such examples, it seemed hard to find one
under the additional assumption that
G
acts primitively. In fact the
following Problem 8.75 of the Kourovka Notebook

[
10
]
, attributed to John G. Thompson and
designated “A known problem”, was open for more than 40 years.

Question 1.1
.

Suppose
G
is a finite primitive permutation group on
\Omega
, and

\alpha
,
\beta
are distinct points of
\Omega
. Does there exist
an element
g\in G
such that
\alpha^{g}=\beta
and
g
fixes no
point of
\Omega
?

In
[
12
]
we give a negative answer. The group there is
the Steinberg triality group
{}^{3}D_{4}(2)
of order

211{,}341{,}312=2^{12}\cdot 3^{4}\cdot 7^{2}\cdot 13
in a
primitive permutation action on a set of size
4{,}064{,}256
.

Using Magma
[
3
]
, we checked that this is the only example if
we assume that
G
is almost simple with socle of size less than or
equal
20{,}158{,}709{,}760
. It would have been computationally too
expensive to check all the groups with socle
\PSL_{2}(q)
in this
range. The purpose of this note is to show that these groups do not
produce negative examples. In fact a somewhat stronger result holds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q\geq 4
be a prime power and
G
be almost simple with socle

S=\PSL_{2}(q)
, that is
S\leq G\leq\aut(S)
. Suppose that
G
acts
primitively on
\Omega
. Then for every distinct

\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
there exists
s\in S
such that

\alpha^{s}=\beta
and
s
fixes no point of
\Omega
.

If
G
acts primitively on
\Omega
, then the possibilities for a point
stabilizer
S_{\omega}<S
are known. While some cases are relatively
easy to handle, the more difficult ones arise when
S_{\omega}
is
dihedral, or when
S_{\omega}
is one of the polyhedral groups
A_{4}
,

S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
. In the former case we require Hasse’s bound on the
number of points on elliptic curves over finite fields, and to rule out
the latter cases we derive trace identities for the binary polyhedral
groups
2A_{4}
,
2S_{4}
, and
2A_{5}
.

In some cases there are lengthy calculations which, in principle,
could be carried out by hand. Instead, we provide SageMath code at

[
13
]
that confirms the claims.

2.
Quartic hyperelliptic curves

If
D
is a plane algebraic curve over a field
F
given by

P(X,Y)\in F[X,Y]
, then
D(F)=\{\,(x,y)\in F^{2}\,\mid\,P(x,y)=0\,\}

denotes the set of affine
F
-points on
D
.

Let
f(X)=a_{0}+a_{1}X+a_{2}X^{2}+a_{3}X^{3}+a_{4}X^{4}\in F[X]
be a separable
polynomial of degree
4
over a field
F
of characteristic
\neq 2
,
and
C
be the plane algebraic curve defined by
Y^{2}=f(X)
. It is well
known that
C
is
F
-birationally equivalent to an elliptic curve
E

in Weierstraß normal form if and only if
C(F)
is not empty or
a_{4}

is a square in
F
.

As to explicit transformation formulae, there does not seem to be much
in the literature. The formulae in
[
11
, Chapter 10, Theorem
2]
and
[
14
]
do not cover the
characteristic
3
case. Also, these sources do not discuss how
C(F)

is related to
E(F)
. This is done in
[
9
, Section 3]
,
but the treatment is somewhat complicated, it does not work in
characteristic
3
, and also fails in arbitrary characteristic (as
noted in
[
6
]
) if, in the notation from above,

a_{0}
is a square and
4a_{0}^{2}a_{2}=a_{1}^{2}
.

The purpose of this section is to provide a complete treatment of a
birational map
C\to E
, thereby keeping track of rational
points on both curves.

First, there are some easy transformations: If
(x,y)\in C(F)
, then a
shift in
X
allows to assume
x=0
. So
a_{0}=\alpha^{2}
and

(\alpha,y)\in C(F)
. Upon replacing
X
and
Y
with
1/X
and

Y/X^{2}
we may assume that
a_{4}=\alpha^{2}
is a square. If
a_{4}=0
,
then we arrived at a Weierstraß form. Thus assume
a_{4}\neq 0
. Upon
replacing
Y
with
\alpha Y
, we may assume that
a_{4}=1
. Another
shift in
X
allows to assume
a_{3}=0
. So we may assume that

f(X)=a_{0}+a_{1}X+a_{2}X^{2}+X^{4}=(a_{2}/2+X^{2})^{2}+a_{1}X+a_{0}-a_{2}^{2}/4
. Writing

a=a_{2}^{2}/4-a_{0}
,
b=a_{1}
, and
c=a_{2}/2
, we see that we are reduced to
study curves of the form
Y^{2}=(X^{2}+c)^{2}+bX-a
.

The transformation formulae below arise from a simplified version of

[
6
]
, combined with an idea from
[
5
, Chapter
8(iii)]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f(X)=(X^{2}+c)^{2}+bX-a\in F[X]
be a separable polynomial over
a field
F
of characteristic
\neq 2
. Then
g(U)=U^{3}-4cU^{2}+4aU+b^{2}\in F[U]
is separable.

Let
C
be the plane algebraic curve defined by
Y^{2}=f(X)
and
E
be
the elliptic curve defined by
V^{2}=g(U)
.

Consider the bivariate maps

\displaystyle\Phi:\;(X,Y)

\displaystyle\mapsto(U,V)=(2(X^{2}+Y+c),2XU+b)

\displaystyle\Psi:\;(U,V)

\displaystyle\mapsto(X,Y)=\left(\frac{V-b}{2U},-X^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(U-2c)\right).

Then the following holds:

(a)

If
b=0
, then
\Phi
induces a bijective map

C(F)\to E(F)\setminus\{(0,0)\}
with inverse map induced by

\Psi
. (Note that
(0,0)\in E(F)
in this case.)

(b)

If
b\neq 0
, then
(0,\pm b)\in E(F)
and

(x_{0},\pm y_{0})\in C(F)
for
x_{0}=\frac{a}{b}
,

y_{0}=c+x_{0}^{2}
. Furthermore,
\Phi
induces a bijective map

C(F)\setminus\{(x_{0},-y_{0})\}\to E(F)\setminus\{(0,-b),(0,b)\}
with inverse map induced by
\Psi
.

In particular,
\lvert C(F)\rvert=\lvert E(F)\rvert-1
in both cases.

Proof.

The separability of
g(U)
follows from that of
f(X)
, because both
polynomials have the same discriminant. (Alternatively, this could
also be derived without calculation of discriminants from the
properties of
\Phi
and
\Psi
.)

Direct computations show that
\Phi(x,y)\in E(F)
for all

(x,y)\in C(F)
and
\Psi(u,v)\in C(F)
for all

(u,v)\in E(F)\setminus\{(0,-b),(0,b)\}
.

One verifies the identities
\Phi(\Psi(U,V))=(U,V)
and

\Psi(\Phi(X,Y))=(X,Y)
of bivariate rational functions; these
identities hold without assuming the relations
V^{2}=g(U)
or

Y^{2}=f(X)
.

(a)

Suppose that
b=0
. We need to show that there is no

(x,y)\in C(F)
with
(u,v)=\Phi(x,y)=(0,0)
. Suppose
otherwise. From
y^{2}-f(x)=0
and
u=0
we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=2(y^{2}-f(x))+(x^{2}-y+c)u

\displaystyle=2(y^{2}-x^{4}-2cx^{2}-c^{2}+a)+(x^{2}-y+c)(2x^{2}+2y+2c)

\displaystyle=2a.

Hence
a=b=0
, which contradicts the separability of
f(X)
.

(b)

Now suppose that
b\neq 0
. Assume that

(u,v)=\Phi(x,y)=(0,\pm b)
for some
(x,y)\in C(F)
. From
u=0

we get
y=-(x^{2}+c)
. Plugging this into
y^{2}=f(x)
gives

x=\frac{a}{b}=x_{0}
and then
y=-(x^{2}+c)=-y_{0}
. The claim follows.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power and let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be a separable
polynomial of degree
\leq 4
. Then the number of pairs

(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with
y^{2}=f(x)
is at most
q+1+2\sqrt{q}
.

Proof.

If
f
has degree
\leq 2
, then an even better upper bound holds. If

\deg f=3
, then
Y^{2}=f(X)
is an elliptic curve and Hasse’s bound
gives the slightly better upper bound
q+2\sqrt{q}
. (Note that we
only count the affine points of the curve.)

Now suppose that
\deg f=4
and that
y^{2}=f(x)
for some

x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be the curve given by
Y^{2}=f(X)
. By the
transformations described above, we transform
C^{\prime}
to the curve
C

as in the previous lemma. The only transformation on the way from

C^{\prime}
to
C
which changes the number of affine
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points is the
map
(X,Y)\mapsto(1/X,Y/X^{2})
, which removes the (at most
2
)
points with
X
-coordinate
0
. Thus

\lvert C^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert\leq\lvert C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert+2
. Let
E
be the elliptic curve
from the previous lemma. So
\lvert C(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert=\lvert E(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert-1
. Thus

\lvert C^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert\leq\lvert E(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert+1
, and the claim follows from the
Hasse bound for
\lvert E(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rvert\leq q+2\sqrt{q}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

(a)

One cannot improve the bound further. For instance, for

q=5
there are
10>q+2\sqrt{q}
pairs
(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with

y^{2}=3x^{4}+1
.

(b)

An alternative, more theoretical proof of the lemma could
use the following: If
C
is an arbitrary absolutely irreducible
projective curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
\tilde{C}
is a nonsingular
curve which is equivalent to
C
by a birational map over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
then
n(C)\leq n(\tilde{C})+r
, where
n
counts the number of
projective
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points, and
r
is the number of singularities
of
C
. See the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
.

3.
Some field theoretic lemmata

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power with
q\geq 23
if
q
is odd. Suppose that
for
a,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
the following holds: for every
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

with
ar+d/r\neq 0
there is
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
with

ar+d/r=s+1/s
. Then either
a=d=0
or
ad=1
.

Proof.

First note that if exactly one of
a
or
d
is nonzero, then

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subseteq\{\,s+1/s\,\mid\,s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}
. So the map

\sigma:s\mapsto s+1/s
is injective. But
\sigma(s)=\sigma(1/s)
, so

s=1/s
for all
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and therefore
q\leq 3
. The
case
q=3
is indeed an exception, and for
q=2
the claim is true
anyway.

Thus we assume that
a
and
d
are both nonzero.

We start with the easier case where
q
is even. As
s+1/s=0
for

s=1
, we have
ar+d/r=s+1/s
for some
s
no matter whether
ar+d/r

is
0
or not. As the square map is an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there
is
r_{0}\in F
with
d/a=r_{0}^{2}
.

From

ar+d/r=ar+ar_{0}^{2}/r=ar_{0}(r/r_{0}+r_{0}/r)

and
ad=(ar_{0})^{2}
we see that in order to prove the lemma, we may
assume
a=d
.

Set
T=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}
. We see that multiplication by

a
permutes the elements of the set
T
. Thus
ax=x
for

x=\sum_{t\in T}t^{2}
. Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
have order
q-1
. Then the
elements
\zeta^{i}+\zeta^{-i}
,
i=0,1,\dots,q/2-1
are the elements
from
T
without repetitions. Therefore

x=\sum_{i=0}^{q/2-1}(\zeta^{2i}+\zeta^{-2i})=\frac{\zeta^{q}-1}{\zeta^{2}-1}+\frac{\zeta^{-q}-1}{1/\zeta^{2}-1}=\frac{\zeta-1}{\zeta^{2}-1}+\frac{1/\zeta-1}{1/\zeta^{2}-1}=1,

hence
a=1
and we are done.

Now suppose that
q
is odd. Note that
ar+d/r=0
for at most two
values of
r
. Thus for all but at most
2
values of
r
, there is

s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
with
ar+d/r=s+1/s
.

Rewriting
s^{2}-(ar+d/r)s+1=0
as
(2rs-ar^{2}-d)^{2}=(ar^{2}+d)^{2}-4r^{2}

shows that
(ar^{2}+d)^{2}-4r^{2}
is a square for at least
q-3
values
of
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Set
f(X)=(aX^{2}+d)^{2}-4X^{2}
. As
f
has at most

4
roots, we see that the curve
Y^{2}=f(X)
has at least

2(q-3-4)+4=2q-10
affine points over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
f
is separable,
then by Lemma
2.2
, we get
2q-10\leq q+1+2\sqrt{q}
, hence

q\leq 19
.

Finally assume that
f(X)=(aX^{2}-2X+d)(aX^{2}+2X+d)
is
inseparable. The two factors are relatively prime (because the
difference is
4X
). Furthermore, the discriminant of both factors
is
4(1-ad)
, hence
ad=1
and we are done.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

One can check that
q=13
,
17
, and
19
do not give
exceptions. However,
q=3
,
5
,
7
,
9
, and
11
are indeed
exceptions. This makes it unlikely that there is a “cleaner” proof
of the lemma for odd
q
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
q\geq 4
be a prime power and
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

ad-bc=1
. Set

T=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}\cup\{0\}
. Suppose that

ar+d/r\in T
and
-cr+b/r\in T
for all
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then one
of the following holds:

(a)

a=d=0
or
b=c=0
;

(b)

q=9
and
ar+d/r=2
for some
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Using the previous lemma, one only need to check the cases where

q\leq 19
is an odd prime power. This is most conveniently done with
the SageMath code at
[
13
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q
be a prime power with
q\geq 23
if
q
is odd. For

F=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
set
N=\{\,r\in F\,\mid\,r^{q+1}=1\,\}
. Suppose that for

0\neq a\in F
the following holds: For all
r\in N
with

ar+a^{q}/r\neq 0
there is
s\in N
such that
ar+a^{q}/r=s+1/s
. Then

a\in N
.

Proof.

We first handle the easier case that
q
is even.
As the square map is an automorphism of
F
, there is
r_{0}\in F
with

a^{q-1}=r_{0}^{2}
. Note that
a^{q-1}\in N
, hence
r_{0}\in N
.

From

ar+a^{q}/r=ar+ar_{0}^{2}/r=ar_{0}(r/r_{0}+r_{0}/r)

we see that in order to prove the lemma, we may assume
a^{q}=a
.

Set
T=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in N\,\}
. We see that multiplication by
a

permutes the elements of the set
T
. Thus
ax=x
for

x=\sum_{t\in T}t^{2}
. Let
\zeta\in F
have order
q+1
. Then the
elements
\zeta^{i}+\zeta^{-i}
,
i=0,1,\dots,q/2
are the elements
from
T
without repetitions. Therefore

x=\sum_{i=0}^{q/2}(\zeta^{2i}+\zeta^{-2i})=\frac{\zeta^{2+q}-1}{\zeta^{2}-1}+\frac{\zeta^{-2-q}-1}{1/\zeta^{2}-1}=\frac{\zeta-1}{\zeta^{2}-1}+\frac{1/\zeta-1}{1/\zeta^{2}-1}=1,

hence
a=1
and we are done.

Now assume that
q
is odd. An argument as above does not work
because if
a^{(q-1)/2}
is not in
N
, then

\{\,ar+a^{q}/r\,\mid\,r\in N\,\}
is a proper subset of

\{\,s+1/s\,\mid\,s\in N\,\}
. (Note that
ar+a^{q}/r
is fixed under

r\mapsto a^{q-1}/r
, so this map would not have a fixed point, in
contrast to
s\mapsto 1/s
which fixes
s+1/s
for
s=\pm 1
.)

We use a finite field analog of the Cayley transform from complex
numbers to parametrize the “circle”
N=\{\,r\,\mid\,r\cdot r^{q}=1\,\}
.
Pick
0\neq\omega\in F
with
\omega^{q}=-\omega
. Note that

\gamma=\omega^{2}
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Set
N^{\prime}=N\setminus\{1\}
. One
quickly checks that

\mathbb{F}_{q}\to N^{\prime},z\mapsto\frac{z-\omega}{z+\omega}

is bijective.

Set
f(r)=ar+a^{q}/r
. We want to compute a lower bound for the size
of
M=\{\,(r,s)\in N^{\prime}\times N^{\prime}\,\mid\,f(r)=s+1/s\,\}
. Note that given

c
, there are at most two values for
r
with
f(r)=c
. Therefore,
there are at least
\lvert N^{\prime}\rvert-6=q-6
choices
r\in N^{\prime}
such that

f(r)\notin\{-2,0,2\}
. For these
r
, there is
s\in N^{\prime}
with

(r,s)\in M
. But then
(r,1/s)\neq(r,s)
is also in
M
. Thus

\lvert M\rvert\geq 2(q-6)
.

With
r=\frac{x-\omega}{x+\omega}
and
s=\frac{y-\omega}{y+\omega}

we get that

a\frac{X-\omega}{X+\omega}+a^{q}\frac{X+\omega}{X-\omega}=\frac{Y-\omega}{Y+\omega}+\frac{Y+\omega}{Y-\omega}

has at least
2(q-6)
solutions
(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Write

a=u+v\omega
with
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
a^{q}=u-v\omega
. For later
use we note that

a^{q+1}=a\cdot a^{q}=(u+v\omega)(u-v\omega)=u^{2}-v^{2}\gamma
.

Clearing denominators and using
\omega^{2}=\gamma
, we arrive at the
curve
P(X)Y^{2}=\gamma Q(X)
, where

\displaystyle P(X)

\displaystyle=\left(u-1\right)X^{2}-2v\gamma X+(u+1)\gamma

\displaystyle Q(X)

\displaystyle=\left(u+1\right)X^{2}-2v\gamma X+(u-1)\gamma.

Note that
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
.

The discriminant of
P(X)
and
Q(X)
is

-4\gamma(u^{2}-v^{2}\gamma-1)=-4\gamma(a^{q+1}-1)
and

4\gamma(u^{2}-v^{2}\gamma-1)=-4\gamma(a^{q+1}-1)
,
respectively. Furthermore, the resultant of
P(X)
and
Q(X)
is

16\gamma^{2}(u^{2}-v^{2}\gamma)=16\gamma^{2}a^{q+1}
.

We assume that
a^{q+1}\neq 1
, for otherwise
a\in N
and we are
done. Then the product
P(X)Q(X)
is a separable polynomial of
degree
\leq 4
.

Note that if
(x,y)
is a point on
P(X)Y^{2}=\gamma Q(X)
, then

P(x)\neq 0
. Thus the points
(x,y)
on the curve

P(X)Y^{2}=\gamma Q(X)
give different points
(x,yP(x))
on the
curve
Y^{2}=\gamma P(X)Q(X)
.

We see that the curve
Y^{2}=\gamma P(X)Q(X)
has at least
2(q-6)

affine points over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. On the other hand, by Lemma

2.2
, this number is at most
q+1+2\sqrt{q}
. From this one
quickly obtains
q\leq 19
.
∎

One can check that

,

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
q
be a prime power. Set
N=\{\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\,\mid\,r^{q+1}=1\,\}

and
T=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in N\,\}
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with

a^{q+1}+b^{q+1}=1
suppose that
ar+a^{q}/r\in T
and

br+b^{q}/r\in T
for all
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then
a=0
or
b=0
.

Proof.

If
q\geq 23
, then the previous lemma shows that
a^{q+1}=1
, hence

b=0
, or

The groups of quaternions
2A_{4}
,
2S_{4}
, and
2A_{5}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F
be a field and
\bar{H}
be a subgroup of
\PSL_{2}(F)
isomorphic
to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
. Let
H
be the preimage of
\bar{H}
in

\SL_{2}(F)
and
x,y\in F^{2\times 2}
. If the characteristic of
F

is
0
or relatively prime to
\lvert\bar{H}\rvert
, then

\sum_{h\in H}\trace(hy)\cdot\trace(hx)^{2m+1}=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert\cdot c_{m}\cdot(\trace(x)\cdot\trace(y)-\trace(x\cdot y))\cdot\det(x)^{m},

where
m\in\{0,1\}
if
\bar{H}\cong A_{4}
,
m\in\{0,1,2\}
if

\bar{H}\cong S_{4}
,
m\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
if
\bar{H}\cong A_{5}
, and

c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
=
1,2,5,14,42
are the first five
Catalan numbers (with index shifted by
1
).

Proof.

We assume that the characteristic of
F
is
0
or relatively prime
to
\lvert\bar{H}\rvert
. Let
\bar{F}
be an algebraic closure of
F
. It is
well known that
\PSL_{2}(\bar{F})
contains exactly one conjugacy
class of subgroups isomorphic to
\bar{H}
, see e.g.
[
2
, Proposition
4.1]
. (In fact more is true. By
[
2
, Proposition
4.2]

\PGL_{2}(F)
contains at most one conjugacy
class of subgroups isomorphic to
\bar{H}
.)

If we replace
H
by
H^{g}
for some
g\in\SL_{2}(\bar{F})
, then
in view of
\trace(g^{-1}hgx)=\trace(hgxg^{-1})
and

\trace(g^{-1}hgy)=\trace(hgyg^{-1})
, this amounts to replacing
x

and
y
with
gxg^{-1}
and
gyg^{-1}
, which however does not
change the right hand side.

Thus the claimed identity is independent of the chosen isomorphic
copy of
H
in
\SL_{2}(\bar{F})
.

In
\bar{F}
we pick elements
i,\rho
, and
\sigma
with
i^{2}=-1
,

\rho^{2}=2
, and
\sigma^{2}+\sigma=1
. Let
E\in\bar{F}^{2\times 2}
be
the identity matrix, and set

I=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix},\;\;J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\;\;K=IJ.

Then
I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=-E
and
JI=-IJ
. Note that

\{\pm E,\pm I,\pm J,\pm K\}
is the quaternion group

Q_{8}
. Following
[
7
]
, we set

W=\frac{-E+I+J+K}{2}

and

\displaystyle 2A_{4}

\displaystyle=\langle I,W\rangle

\displaystyle 2S_{4}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{J+K}{\rho},W\right\rangle

\displaystyle 2A_{5}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{I+\sigma J+(\sigma+1)K)}{2},W\right\rangle.

Then the images of
2A_{4}
,
2S_{4}
, and
2A_{5}
in
\PSL_{2}(\bar{F})
are
isomorphic to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, and
A_{5}
, respectively.

With this explicit description of the groups

H\in\{2A_{4},2S_{4},2A_{5}\}
, we verify the computation with the
SageMath script at
[
13
]
. Note that the computations
are in characteristic
0
. But the only denominators which appear in
the calculations are
2
(or
\rho
where
\rho^{2}=2
), so the
identities which
[
13
]
verifies hold true modulo the
characteristic of
F
. More precisely,
[
13
]
does
the following: Let
F
be the number field
\mathbb{Q}(i)
,
\mathbb{Q}(i,\rho)
,
or
\mathbb{Q}(i,\sigma)
according to
\bar{H}=A_{4}
,
\bar{H}=S_{4}
, or

\bar{H}=A_{5}
. Even though the given matrices generate finite groups,
SageMath (and its backend Gap) cannot compute the generated groups
over such number fields. The helper function
gener(L)

implements a naive straightforward algorithm to compute the group
generated by the list
L
of matrices from
F^{2\times 2}
. The
remaining code which verifies the stated identities should be self
explanatory.

Remark 4.2
.

(a)

The stated identities do not generalize to bigger

m
.

(b)

With a little more effort one can show that the lemma
still holds under the weaker assumption that the characteristic

is not
2
. For the cases

and

essentially the

\PSL_{2}(\bar{F})
has only

(c)

There is probably a more conceptual explanation of the

x
and
y
be algebraically
independent over
F
and
f(x,y)
be the left hand side, which
is a polynomial of degree
2m+2
in

Note that for

(recall that

. Working out the low degree invariants of

could

might not be obvious that the right hand side is an invariant

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F
be a field and
\bar{H}
be a subgroup of
\PSL_{2}(F)
isomorphic
to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
. Assume that the characteristic of
F
is

0
or relatively prime to
\lvert\bar{H}\rvert
. Let
H
be the preimage of

\bar{H}
in
\SL_{2}(F)
. Set

T=\{\,\trace(h)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}\setminus\{-2,2\}
, and for

x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(F)
set

y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\sum_{h\in H}\left(\trace(hy)\prod_{t\in T}(\trace(hx)-t)\right)=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert.

Proof.

With this choice of
x
and
y
we have

\trace(x)\cdot\trace(y)-\trace(x\cdot y)=ad-bc=\det(x)=1,

so the previous lemma yields

\sum_{h\in H}\trace(hy)\cdot\trace(hx)^{2m+1}=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert\cdot c_{m}.

Let
Z
be a variable over
F
. Then
[
13
]
verifies

\prod_{t\in T}(Z-t)=\begin{cases}Z^{3}-Z,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong A_{4}\\
Z^{5}-3Z^{3}+2Z,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong S_{4}\\
Z^{7}-4Z^{5}+4Z^{3}-Z,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong A_{5}.\\
\end{cases}

This yields

\sum_{h\in H}\left(\trace(hy)\prod_{t\in T}(\trace(hx)-t)\right)=\begin{cases}\lvert\bar{H}\rvert(c_{1}-c_{0})=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong A_{4}\\
\lvert\bar{H}\rvert(c_{2}-3c_{1}+2c_{0})=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong S_{4}\\
\lvert\bar{H}\rvert(c_{3}-4c_{2}+4c_{1}-c_{0})=\lvert\bar{H}\rvert,&\text{ if }\bar{H}\cong A_{5}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
1.2

The maximal subgroups of almost simple groups with socle

[
4

[
8
]
. The results in
[
4
]
and

[
8
]
are more detailed than those in the following

that we shall use.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
q\geq 4
be a prime power and
S=\PSL_{2}(q)\leq G\leq\PGammaL_{2}(q)
. Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of
G
with

S\not\leq M
. Set
\bar{H}=M\cap S
. Then one of the following holds.

(a)

[S:\bar{H}]=q+1
,

(b)

[S:\bar{H}]=q(q+1)/2
,

(c)

[S:\bar{H}]=q(q-1)/2
,

(d)

\bar{H}\cong A_{4}
, and
q
is and odd prime,

(e)

\bar{H}\cong S_{4}
, and
q
is a prime with
q\equiv\pm 1\mod{8}
,

(f)

\bar{H}\cong A_{5}
, and
q\equiv\pm 1\mod{10}
and
q=9
if
q
is
power of
3
,

(g)

\bar{H}\cong\PSL_{2}(r)
or
\bar{H}\cong\PGL_{2}(r)
, where
q=r^{e}

for
e\geq 2
.

Furthermore, in each case
\bar{H}
is unique up to conjugacy in

\aut(S)
(with two classes in case (g) if

is odd).

Let
S
and
G
be as in Theorem
1.2
,
M
be a stabilizer of
a point in
\Omega
, and
\bar{H}=M\cap S
. Then
M
is a maximal subgroup
of
G
by primitivity. We handle the cases (a) to (g) separately.

Case (a) is very easy: Here the action of
S
on
\Omega
is the
natural action, which is doubly transitive. Pick distinct

\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
and an arbitrary derangement
y
in
S
. By
double transitivity of
S
, there is
s\in S
with
\alpha^{s}=\alpha

and
\alpha^{ys}=\beta
. (Note that
\alpha^{y}\neq\alpha
.) Then
g=y^{s}

is a derangement with
\alpha^{g}=\alpha^{s^{-1}ys}=\beta
.

The remaining cases (b) to (g) require some work. Suppose that there
is a counterexample. Then there exist distinct

\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
such that no derangement in
S
moves

\alpha
to
\beta
. Let
\bar{H}
be the stabilizer in
S
of
\alpha

and
\bar{x}\in S
with
\alpha^{\bar{x}}=\beta
. Then
\bar{H}\bar{x}
is the set of
elements which moves
\alpha
to
\beta
. As this set does not contain
a derangement, every element in the coset
\bar{H}\bar{x}
is conjugate to an
element from
\bar{H}
.

This property is preserved upon applying an automorphism of
S
to

\bar{H}
and
\bar{x}
. Therefore, in each of the cases listed in the
lemma, we may make a specific choice of
\bar{H}
.

Let
H
be the preimage of
\bar{H}
in
\SL(2,q)
, and

x\in\SL(2,q)
be a preimage of
\bar{x}
. Thus
hx
is conjugate to an
element from
H
for every
h\in H
. This implies

(5.1)

\{\,\trace(hx)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}\subseteq\{\,\trace(h)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}.

We will also need a refinement of this set inclusion. Suppose that

\trace{hx}=\pm 2
for some
h\in H
. Then the characteristic
polynomial of
hx
is

, so the order of

is divides the characteristic of

. Thus if the

does not divide
\lvert H\rvert
, then

, hence

, a contradiction. So we get

(5.2)

\{\,\trace(hx)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}\subseteq\{\,\trace(h)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}\setminus\{-2,2\}\text{ if }\gcd(q,\lvert H\rvert)=1.

5.1.
Case (b)

In this case
H=\{\,h_{r}\,\mid\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}\cup\{\,h_{r}^{\prime}\,\mid\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}

with

h_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}r&0\\
0&1/r\end{pmatrix},\;\;h_{r}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-r\\
1/r&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus

\{\,\trace(h)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\,\}\cup\{0\}
. Write

x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with

. Then

\trace(h_{r}x)=ar+d/r
and
\trace(h_{r}^{\prime}x)=-cr+b/r

By (
5.1
) and Corollary

we have either

or

, but then

, a contradiction, or

. But

5.2.
Case (c)

To rule out this case, it is convenient to use the isomorphism

Set

for

Thus

T:=\{\,\trace(h)\,\mid\,h\in H\,\}=\{\,r+1/r\,\mid\,r\in N\,\}\cup\{0\}

Now (
5.1
) and Corollary

or

5.3.
Case (d)

5.4.

5.6.

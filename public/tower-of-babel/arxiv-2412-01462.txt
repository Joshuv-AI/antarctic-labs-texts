The mixed second moment of prime quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions over function fields
Christopher G. Best

Abstract
. We compute an asymptotic formula for the mixed second moment of the μ \mu -th and ν \nu -th derivatives of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions over monic, irreducible polynomials in the function field setting.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11M38; Secondary 11M06, 11M50

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the mixed second moment of derivatives of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over monic, irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. In the case of the Riemann zeta function, for integers
\mu,\nu\geq 0
, Ingham
[
17
]
considered a mixed second moment and obtained the asymptotic formula

\int_{1}^{T}\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\zeta^{(\nu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-it)\,dt\sim\frac{1}{\mu+\nu+1}T(\log T)^{\mu+\nu+1},

as
T\to\infty
. Since
\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}-it)=\overline{\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)}
, Ingham’s result also implies that

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\,dt\sim\frac{1}{2\mu+1}T(\log T)^{2\mu+1}.

The fourth moment of the derivative of the zeta function was considered by Conrey
[
10
]
who showed that

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{4}\,dt\sim\frac{61}{1680\pi^{2}}T\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{8}.

Additionally, Conrey proved that as
m\to\infty
,

\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}C_{2,m}\sim\frac{1}{16m^{4}},

where

C_{k,m}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{-k^{2}-2km}\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta^{(m)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}\,dt.

Random matrix theory serves as a very useful tool for formulating conjectures on the analytic properties of the Riemann zeta function and other
L
-functions. In particular, it is now well known that the zeta function can be modelled by the characteristic polynomials of random unitary matrices. In this direction, Conrey, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
12
]
studied the moments of the derivative of characteristic polynomials
\Lambda_{A}(s)
of matrices
A\in U(N)
chosen from the Circular Unitary Ensemble (CUE). They proved that as
N\to\infty
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{U(N)}|\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(1)|^{2k}\,dA\sim b_{k}N^{k^{2}+2k},

where
dA
denotes the normalised Haar measure on the group of unitary matrices
U(N)
and where
b_{k}
has an explicit expression in terms of a determinant involving modified Bessel functions of the first kind. Their result led them to then conjecture that as
T\to\infty
,

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}\,dt\sim a_{k}b_{k}(\log T)^{k^{2}+2k},

where
a_{k}
is the same arithmetic factor appearing in the conjectural formula for the moments of the zeta function itself and is given in terms of an Euler product, see, for example,
[
11
,
19
]
.

Here we consider a mixed second moment of arbitrary derivatives of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions in the function field setting. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
odd. We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\leq n}
the sets of monic polynomials of degree
n
and of degree at most
n
, respectively. Also,
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of monic, irreducible polynomials and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denotes the set of monic, irreducible polynomials of degree
n
. Similarly,
\mathcal{H}
denotes the set of monic, square-free polynomials and
\mathcal{H}_{n}
the set of monic, square-free polynomials of degree
n
.

Our main result concerns the mixed second moment of derivatives of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over monic, irreducible polynomials.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu,\nu\geq 0
be integers. Then, as
g\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\frac{L^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})L^{(\nu)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})}{(\log q)^{\mu+\nu}}=c(\mu,\nu)\cdot(2g+1)^{\mu+\nu+3}+O(g^{\mu+\nu+2}),

where

c(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2^{\mu+\nu+3}\zeta_{q}(2)}\left((-1)^{\mu+\nu}A(\mu,\nu)+\sum_{m=0}^{\mu}\sum_{n=0}^{\nu}\binom{\mu}{m}\binom{\nu}{n}(-2)^{\mu+\nu-m-n}A(m,n)\right),

(1.1)

\zeta_{q}(s)
is the zeta-function of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, and

A(m,n):=\frac{1}{2(m+n+3)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^{m+1}(2-x)^{n}+x^{n+1}(2-x)^{m}\right)dx.

(1.2)

Our method of proof for Theorem
1.1
involves using an approximate functional equation for the product of two shifted
L
-functions and then taking derivatives of this expression with respect to the shift parameters. We then compute the main term by looking at the contribution of the diagonal terms in the resulting sums and bound the error using the Weil bound. As mentioned in
[
13
, Remark 1.3]
, one could derive a formula for the mixed moments by first computing the shifted moments and then taking derivatives. However, this approach leads to very complicated expressions when the orders of the derivatives are not fixed and small.

Lastly, random matrix theory provides us with predictions for the asymptotic behaviour of the moments of derivatives of the
L
-functions mentioned above. The families of quadratic

(1.6)

where

is the Haar measure. Also, the leading order coefficient

L

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\frac{L^{(n_{1})}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})^{k_{1}}L^{(n_{2})}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})^{k_{2}}}{(\log q)^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\sim\eta_{k}\cdot b^{Sp}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(n_{1},n_{2})\cdot(2g+1)^{k(k+1)/2+k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}},

\eta_{k}

\eta_{k}
is the same arithmetic factor present in the conjectural asymptotic formula for the

-th moment of

due to Andrade, Jung and Shamesaldeen
[
4
]

for further details on their conjecture and the arithmetic term. A similar formula is also conjectured to hold for the family

over
\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}

b_{0,1}^{Sp}(0,n)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2(n+1)}\ \textrm{and}\ b_{1,1}^{Sp}(0,2)=\frac{1}{80}.

c(n_{1},n_{2})=\frac{1}{\zeta_{q}(2)}\cdot b_{1,1}^{Sp}(n_{1},n_{2}),

\displaystyle b_{1,1}^{Sp}(n_{1},n_{2})

2.
Background on
L
-functions over function fields

Here we recall the necessary background on quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over function fields. We use
[
20
]
as a general reference.

For a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, the norm of
f
is defined to be
q^{d(f)}
if
f\neq 0
and
|f|=0
for
f=0
.
The zeta function
\zeta_{q}(s)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is defined for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
by the Dirichlet series and Euler product

\zeta_{q}(s)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{1}{|f|^{s}}=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

As there are
q^{n}
monic polynomials of degree
n
, we have that

\zeta_{q}(s)=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, the Prime Polynomial Theorem states that

|\mathcal{P}_{n}|=\frac{q^{n}}{n}+O\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right).

Given a monic, irreducible polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}
, we define the quadratic character
\chi_{P}
using the Legendre symbol

\chi_{P}(f)=\left(\frac{f}{P}\right).

That is,

\displaystyle\chi_{P}(f)=\begin{cases}0,&\textup{ if }P|f,\\
1,&\textup{ if }P\nmid f\textup{ and }f\textup{ is a square modulo }P,\\
-1,&\textup{ if }P\nmid f\textup{ and }f\textup{ is not a square modulo }P.\end{cases}

The quadratic Dirichlet
L
-function attached to the character
\chi_{P}
is defined for
\textup{Re}(s)>1
by

L(s,\chi_{P})=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\chi_{P}(f)}{|P|^{s}}=\prod_{Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{P}(Q)}{|Q|^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

With the change of variables
u=q^{-s}
, we may write

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{P}):=L(s,\chi_{P})=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\chi_{P}(f)u^{d(f)}=\prod_{Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\left(1-\chi_{P}(Q)u^{d(Q)}\right)^{-1}.

We have that
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{P})
is in fact a polynomial in
u
of degree
2g
and satisfies the functional equation

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{P})=(qu^{2})^{g}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{qu},\chi_{P}\right).

By the Riemann Hypothesis for curves over finite fields, proven by Weil
[
22
]
, all of the zeros of
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi_{P})
lie on the circle
|u|=q^{-1/2}
.

Now, we denote the divisor function on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
by
\tau(f)
which satisfies

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\tau(f)=(n+1)q^{n},

and for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, we let

\tau_{\alpha,\beta}(f)=\sum_{f=f_{1}f_{2}}\frac{1}{|f_{1}|^{\alpha}|f_{2}|^{\beta}}.

Also, for integers
m,n\geq 0
we will denote

\tau^{(m,n)}(f):=\frac{\partial^{m+n}}{\partial\alpha^{m}\partial\beta^{n}}\tau_{\alpha,\beta}(f)|_{\alpha=\beta=0}=(-\log q)^{m+n}\sum_{f=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n},

and note that we have the bound

|\tau^{(m,n)}(f)|\ll\sum_{f=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f)^{m+n}\ll\tau(f)d(f)^{m+n}.

Lastly, we have the following Weil bound for character sums over monic, irreducible polynomials.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{M}
not a square, as
g\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f)\ll q^{-g}d(f).

Proof.

This follows from equation (2.5) in
[
21
]
and the Prime Polynomial Theorem.
∎

The twisted first moment of the
k
-th derivative

Here we will prove Theorem
1.2
following the approach used in
[
18
]
. Let
l\in\mathcal{M}
and write
l=l_{1}l_{2}^{2}
with
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
and
l_{1}
square-free. For
h\in\{g,g-1\}
, we define the sum

\displaystyle S_{h}(m;l)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h}}\frac{d(f)^{m}}{|f|^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(fl)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{h}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(fl),

and compute an asymptotic formula for
S_{h}(m;l)
in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For an integer
m\geq 0
and
h\in\{g,g-1\}
, we have that as
g\to\infty
,

S_{h}(m;l)=\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}2^{i}d(l_{1})^{m-i}J_{i}([\tfrac{h-d(l_{1})}{2}])+O\left(q^{-g/2}g^{m+1}d(l)\right).

Proof.

We split the sum
S_{h}(m;l)=S_{h}(m;l)_{\square}+S_{h}(m;l)_{\neq\square}
according to whether
fl=\square
or
fl\neq\square
. For the contribution of non-squares, we use Lemma
2.1
to obtain

\displaystyle|S_{h}(m;l)_{\neq\square}|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{n=0}^{g}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
fl\neq\square\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(fl)\right|

\displaystyle\ll q^{-g}\sum_{n=0}^{g}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}d(fl)

\displaystyle\ll q^{-g}(g+d(l))\sum_{n=0}^{g}n^{m}q^{n/2}

\displaystyle\ll q^{-g/2}g^{m}(g+d(l)).

For the contribution of the squares in
S_{h}(m;l)_{\square}
, we use the facts that
\chi_{P}(f^{2})=\chi_{P}(f)^{2}
and since
d(f)\leq g
, we have that
P\nmid f
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}
. Thus, for
fl=\square
, we have

\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(fl)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}\\
P|l\end{subarray}}1=|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|+O(d(l)).

Now, as
fl=\square
, we write
f=l_{1}f_{1}^{2}
with
f_{2}
monic. Then, since
d(f)=d(l_{1})+2d(f_{1})\leq h
, we can rewrite the sum over
f_{1}
with
d(f_{1})\leq(h-d(l_{1}))/2
which gives us that

\displaystyle S_{h}(m;l)_{\square}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{h}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
fl=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(fl)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{h}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
fl=\square\end{subarray}}1+O\left(\frac{d(l)}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{n=0}^{h}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
fl=\square\end{subarray}}1\right)

\displaystyle=q^{-d(l_{1})/2}\sum_{n=0}^{(h-d(l_{1}))/2}(d(l_{1})+2n)^{m}q^{-n}\sum_{f_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}1+O\left(q^{-2g}g\,d(l)\sum_{n=0}^{h}n^{m}q^{n/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{n=0}^{(h-d(l_{1}))/2}(d(l_{1})+2n)^{m}+O\left(q^{-3g/2}g^{m+1}d(l)\right),

where we have used the Prime Polynomial Theorem in bounding the error. For the main term, we use a binomial expansion and the function
J_{i}(n)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}m^{i}
to write it as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{n=0}^{(h-d(l_{1}))/2}(2n+d(l_{1}))^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{n=0}^{(h-d(l_{1}))/2}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}(2n)^{i}d(l_{1})^{m-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}2^{i}d(l_{1})^{m-i}\sum_{n=0}^{(h-d(l_{1}))/2}n^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|l_{1}|^{1/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}2^{i}d(l_{1})^{m-i}J_{i}([\tfrac{h-d(l_{1})}{2}]).

Combining this with the bounds for the error terms completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Using the expression for
L^{(k)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})
given in
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
and multiplying by
\chi_{P}(l)
, we have that

\frac{L^{(k)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})\chi_{P}(l)}{(\log q)^{k}}=(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{g}n^{k}q^{-n/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi_{P}(fl)+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-2g)^{k-m}\sum_{n=0}^{g-1}n^{m}q^{-n/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi_{P}(fl).

Taking the average over
\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}
, we can then write

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\frac{L^{(k)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})\chi_{P}(l)}{(\log q)^{k}}=(-1)^{k}S_{g}(k;l)+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-2g)^{k-m}S_{g-1}(m;l).

Using this expression for the twisted moment and applying the formula for
S_{h}(m;l)
in Lemma
3.1
completes the proof.

∎

4.
Joint moments of the
\mu
-th and
\nu
-th derivatives

In this section will prove Theorem
1.1
. First, for integers
m,n\geq 0
and
h\in\{2g,2g-1\}
, let

T_{h}(m,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f).

The following lemma establishes the asymptotic behaviour of the sums
T_{h}(m,n)
as
g\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For integers
m,n\geq 0
and
h\in\{2g,2g-1\}
, we have that as
g\to\infty
,

T_{h}(m,n)=\frac{(-\log q)^{m+n}A(m,n)}{\zeta_{q}(2)}g^{m+n+3}+O(g^{m+n+2}),

(4.1)

with
A(m,n)
as defined in (
1.2
).

Proof.

We write

T_{h}(m,n)=T_{h}(m,n)_{\square}+T_{h}(m,n)_{\neq\square},

where
T_{h}(m,n)_{\square}
and
T_{h}(m,n)_{\neq\square}
denote the sums over
f
a perfect square and
f
not a square, respectively. For the terms with
f\neq\square
, we bound the sum using Lemma
2.1
to obtain

\displaystyle|T_{h}(m,n)_{\neq\square}|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g}\\
f\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}\left|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f)\right|

\displaystyle\ll q^{-g}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}d(f)

\displaystyle\ll gq^{-g}\sum_{j=0}^{2g}q^{-j/2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\tau(f)d(f)^{m+n}

\displaystyle\ll gq^{-g}\sum_{j=0}^{2g}j^{m+n+1}q^{j/2}

\displaystyle\ll g^{m+n+2},

where we have used the fact that
\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\tau(f)\ll jq^{j}
. For the terms in
T_{h}(m,n)_{\square}
with
f=\square
, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f)=1,

since
d(f)\leq 2g
and so
P\nmid f
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}
. Thus, we have that

T_{h}(m,n)_{\square}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h}\\
f=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h/2}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f^{2})}{|f|}.

We next write the main term
T_{h}(m,n)_{\square}
as

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h/2}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f^{2})}{|f|}=(-\log q)^{m+n}\sum_{j=0}^{h/2}q^{-j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}.

Then, for a given
j
, we use the hyperbola method and write

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})=j\end{subarray}}j^{m+n}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})<j\end{subarray}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{2})<j\end{subarray}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}.

(4.2)

The second and third sums here are similar so we need only focus on the first two. In particular, it suffices to evaluate the sum

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})=k\end{subarray}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}=k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})=k\end{subarray}}1,

(4.3)

for
k\leq j
. To compute the above sum, we observe that
f_{1}f_{2}=\square
if and only if
f_{1}=l_{1}l_{2}^{2}
and
f_{2}=l_{1}l_{3}^{2}
with
l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\in\mathcal{M}
and
l_{1}
square-free. Also, since
d(f_{1})=k
, we must have
d(l_{1})\leq k
and
d(l_{2})=(k-d(l_{1}))/2
. Then, as
d(f^{2})=d(f_{1})+d(f_{2})=2j
, we have that
d(l_{3})=j-k/2-d(l_{1})/2\geq 0
. So, by summing over
l_{1},l_{2}
and
l_{3}
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})=k\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\\
d(l_{1})\leq k\\
k-d(l_{1})\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{2}\in\mathcal{M}\\
d(l_{2})=(k-d(l_{1}))/2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{3}\in\mathcal{M}\\
d(l_{3})=j-k/2-d(l_{1})/2\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=q^{j-k/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\\
d(l_{1})\leq k\\
k-d(l_{1})\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}q^{-d(l_{1})/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{2}\in\mathcal{M}\\
d(l_{2})=(k-d(l_{1}))/2\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=q^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\\
d(l_{1})\leq k\\
k-d(l_{1})\ \textrm{even}\end{subarray}}q^{-d(l_{1})}.

For the final sum over
l_{1}
, if
k
is even, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\\
d(l_{1})\leq k\\
k-d(l_{1})\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}q^{-d(l_{1})}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
i\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}^{k}\sum_{l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}}q^{-i}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k/2}\sum_{l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{2i}}q^{-2i}=1+(1-q^{-1})\frac{k}{2},

where we have used the fact that for
n\geq 1
,

|\mathcal{H}_{n}|=\frac{q^{n}}{\zeta_{q}(2)}=q^{n}(1-q^{-1}).

Similarly, if
k
is odd, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}\in\mathcal{H}\\
d(l_{1})\leq k\\
k-d(l_{1})\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}q^{-d(l_{1})}=1+(1-q^{-1})\frac{k-1}{2}.

Thus, we have that

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}\\
d(f_{1})=k\end{subarray}}1=q^{j}\left(1+(1-q^{-1})[k/2]\right),

and incorporating this into (
4
) and (
4.3
) gives us

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle=q^{j}\left(j^{m+n}\left(1+(1-q^{-1})[j/2]\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}+k^{n}(2j-k)^{m}\right)\left(1+(1-q^{-1})[k/2]\right)\right).

(4.4)

We now approximate the expression in (
4
) as
j\to\infty
. As

\left(1+(1-q^{-1})[k/2]\right)=(1-q^{-1})\frac{k}{2}+O(1),

the first term in the brackets in (
4
) is

(1-q^{-1})\frac{j^{m+n+1}}{2}+O(j^{m+n}).

Next, we have that

\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}\left(1+(1-q^{-1})[k/2]\right)=\frac{(1-q^{-1})}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m+1}(2j-k)^{n}+O\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}\right),

(4.5)

so we now focus on the sum
\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}
. On the one hand, by writing it in terms of a Riemann sum, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}=j^{m+n+1}\cdot\frac{1}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{k}{j}\right)^{m}\left(2-\frac{k}{j}\right)^{n}\sim j^{m+n+1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{m}(2-x)^{n}dx,

as
j\to\infty
. On the other hand, by Faulhaber’s formula, we know that the sum is a polynomial in
j
. Thus, we have that

\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}k^{m}(2j-k)^{n}=j^{m+n+1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{m}(2-x)^{n}dx+O(j^{m+n}),

(4.6)

and then using (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) in (
4
) yields

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}=\frac{q^{j}j^{m+n+2}}{2\zeta_{q}(2)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^{m+1}(2-x)^{n}+x^{n+1}(2-x)^{m}\right)dx+O(q^{j}j^{m+n+1}).

Therefore, by Faulhaber’s formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h/2}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f^{2})}{|f|}

\displaystyle=(-\log q)^{m+n}\sum_{j=0}^{h/2}q^{-j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\sum_{f^{2}=f_{1}f_{2}}d(f_{1})^{m}d(f_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\log q)^{m+n}}{2\zeta_{q}(2)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^{m+1}(2-x)^{n}+x^{n+1}(2-x)^{m}\right)dx\sum_{j=0}^{h/2}j^{m+n+2}+O\left(\sum_{j=0}^{h/2}j^{m+n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\log q)^{m+n}}{2^{m+n+4}(m+n+3)\zeta_{q}(2)}\cdot h^{m+n+3}\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^{m+1}(2-x)^{n}+x^{n+1}(2-x)^{m}\right)dx+O(h^{m+n+2}).

Recalling the definition of
A(m,n)
and choosing
h\in\{2g,2g-1\}
completes the proof.

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin with the approximate functional equation for the product of two shifted
L
-functions given in
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
. Namely, we have that

L(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha,\chi_{P})L(\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{P})=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g}}\frac{\tau_{\alpha,\beta}(f)\chi_{P}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}+q^{-2g(\alpha+\beta)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-1}}\frac{\tau_{-\alpha,-\beta}(f)\chi_{P}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}.

Using the approximate functional equation, we write

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha,\chi_{P})L(\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta,\chi_{P})=F_{2g}(\alpha,\beta)+q^{-2g(\alpha+\beta)}F_{2g-1}(-\alpha,-\beta),

(4.7)

where

F_{2g}(\alpha,\beta):=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g}}\frac{\tau_{\alpha,\beta}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f),

and
F_{2g-1}(\alpha,\beta)
is given by a similar expression with
\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g}
replaced by
\mathcal{M}_{\leq 2g-1}
. For integers
m,n\geq 0
and
h\in\{2g,2g-1\}
, we then have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m+n}}{\partial\alpha^{m}\partial\beta^{n}}F_{h}(\alpha,\beta)|_{\alpha=\beta=0}=T_{h}(m,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\leq h}}\frac{\tau^{(m,n)}(f)}{|f|^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}\chi_{P}(f).

Thus, differentiating (
4.7
) with respect to
\alpha
and
\beta
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{2g+1}}L^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})L^{(\nu)}(\tfrac{1}{2},\chi_{P})

\displaystyle\qquad=T_{2g}(\mu,\nu)+(-1)^{\mu+\nu}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu}\sum_{n=0}^{\nu}\binom{\mu}{m}\binom{\nu}{n}(2g\log q)^{\mu+\nu-m-n}\,T_{2g-1}(m,n).

Theorem
1.1
then follows on applying the asymptotic formula for
T_{h}(m,n)
in Lemma
4.1
.
∎

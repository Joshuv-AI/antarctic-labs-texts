Abstract
. We investigate the incompressible inhomogeneous magnetohydrodynamic equations in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , under the assumptions that the initial density ρ 0 \rho_{0} is only bounded, and the initial velocity u 0 u_{0} and magnetic field B 0 B_{0} exhibit critical regularities. In particular, the density is allowed to be piecewise constant with jumps. First, we establish the global-in-time well-posedness and large-time behavior of solutions to the Cauchy problem in the case that ρ 0 \rho_{0} has small variations, and u 0 u_{0} and B 0 B_{0} are sufficiently small in the critical Besov space B ˙ p , 1 3 / p − 1 \dot{B}^{3/p-1}_{p,1} with 1 < p < 3 1<p<3 . Moreover, the small variation assumption on ρ 0 \rho_{0} is no longer required in the case p = 2 p=2 . Then, we construct a unique global Fujita-Kato solution under the weaker condition that u 0 u_{0} and B 0 B_{0} are small in B ˙ 2 , ∞ 1 / 2 \dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,\infty} but may be large in H ˙ 1 / 2 \dot{H}^{1/2} . Additionally, we show a general uniqueness result with only bounded and nonnegative density, without assuming the L 1 ​ ( 0 , T , L ∞ ) L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}) regularity of the velocity. Our study systematically addresses the global solvability of the inhomogeneous magnetohydrodynamic equations with rough density in the critical regularity setting.

Introduction

The magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations are widely used to describe the evolution of electrically conducting fluids such as plasmas, liquid metals and electrolytes, and have played an important role in understanding various phenomena in geophysics, cosmology and engineering. In these applications, Maxwell’s equations of electromagnetism are coupled with the Navier-Stokes equations with Lorentz force generated by the magnetic field, where the displacement current is typically neglected (see, e.g.
[
9
,
23
,
40
]
). Although they share similarities with the Navier-Stokes equations, the MHD equations constitute a coupled and highly interactive system with more complex structures, making the study of their dynamics challenging.

In this paper, we address the global well-posedness and stability of the incompressible inhomogeneous MHD equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u)-\mu\Delta u+\nabla P=B\cdot\nabla B,\\
&\partial_{t}B+u\cdot\nabla B-\nu\Delta B=B\cdot\nabla u,\\
&\dive u=\dive B=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\rho=\rho(t,x)\geq 0
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})(t,x)
,
B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})(t,x)
, and
P=P(x,t)
, stand for
the fluid density, the fluid velocity field, the magnetic field, and the fluid pressure, respectively, with
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We consider the Cauchy problem of the MHD system
(
1.1
) supplemented with the initial data

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\rho,u,B)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},B_{0})(x),\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

So far, significant progresses have been made regarding the global existence, regularity and stability of the MHD system (
1.1
). When
\rho
is a constant, indicating that the fluid is homogeneous, the first pioneering work was studied by Duraut and Lions
[
26
]
. They established the local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s\geq d
and proved the global existence for small initial data. Subsequently, Sermange and Temam
[
51
]
investigated the large-time properties of solutions and established the global well-posedness for large data in two dimensions.
Recent advancements on regularity and singularity criteria can be found in
[
54
,
55
,
15
,
32
,
59
]
.
Concerning the global regularity and large-time asymptotics of solutions on the MHD equations with reduced dissipation or close to non-zero equilibrium states,
interested readers can
refer to
[
21
,
44
,
10
,
45
,
12
,
34
]
and the references therein.

When the variable density is considered, i.e. the fluid is inhomogeneous, the system becomes more complicated due to the influence of the density. Gerbeau and Le Bris
[
28
]
proved the global-in-time existence of finite energy weak solutions with density-dependent viscosity coefficients and general initial data in a bounded domain (see also the work of Desjardins and Le Bris
[
24
]
). For the ideal (i.e. inviscid and non-resistive) case, the local well-posedness of classical or strong solutions has been established by many authors, cf., for example,
Schmidt
[
49
]
, Secchi
[
50
]
, and Zhou and Fan
[
60
]
. In absence of vacuum, Li and Wang
[
42
]
obtained the existence and uniqueness of local strong solutions to the inhomogeneous viscous MHD system with general
initial data in Sobolev spaces and proved the global existence as well as global strong solutions with small
initial data. They also gave a weak-strong uniqueness result. When the vacuum appears, the local well-posedness of strong solutions was investigated by Chen, Tan and Wang
[
11
]
under some compatibility conditions, and the global existence of strong solutions in the 2D case was obtained by Huang and Wang
[
37
]
and Lü, Xu and Zhong
[
46
]
. In the case where the density is only bounded from above and below but without the assumption of small variation, Gui
[
29
]
constructed a unique global solution for the 2D MHD system with variable electrical conductivity under the assumption
u_{0},B_{0}\in H^{s}
with
s>0
(see also the 3D case by Chen-Guo-Zhai
[

with

There are many studies on the problem of solvability for the inhomogeneous MHD system (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) in the so-called “critical regularity” setting. The key ingredient, originating from the work of Fujita and
Kato
[
27
]
for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with constant density, is that the “optimal” functional spaces for
well-posedness of (
1.1
) obey the scaling invariances:

\displaystyle(\rho_{\lambda},u_{\lambda},B_{\lambda},P_{\lambda})(t,x)\leadsto(\rho,\lambda^{2}u,\lambda^{2}B,\lambda P)(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),

and the corresponding scaling invariances for the initial data:

\displaystyle(\rho_{0,\lambda},u_{0,\lambda},B_{0,\lambda})(x)\leadsto(\rho_{0},\lambda^{2}u_{0},\lambda^{2}B_{0})(\lambda x).

There are several possible choices for critical spaces of initial data, for example,
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\dot{H}^{-1+d/2}\times\dot{H}^{-1+d/2}
(Fujita-Kato space) or Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{d/p}_{p,r}\times\dot{B}_{p,r}^{-1+d/p}\times\dot{B}^{-1+d/p}_{p,r}
.
The global existence of solutions in critical spaces for the system (
1.1
) was firstly established by Abidi and Hmidi
[
4
]
(see also Abidi and Paicu
[
6
]
). They assumed that the initial density is close to some constant state in
\dot{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}
and that the
initial velocity and magnetic fields are suitably small in
\dot{B}^{-1+d/p}_{p,1}

1.1

[

,
57
,
53

When the effect of the magnetic field is neglected, i.e.

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
&\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u)-\mu\Delta u+\nabla P=0,\\
&\dive u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

which arises in the theory of geophysical flows where the fluid is incompressible but has variable density, as in oceans or rivers. The system (

It is interesting to investigate whether the incompressible inhomogeneous MHD system (
1.1
) is well-posed for discontinuous density. In this case, the piecewise constant density with jumps for fluid mixtures is allowed. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no result concerning the global solvability issue of the inhomogeneous MHD system (
1.1
) for discontinuous and large density.

In this paper, our aim is to study the global dynamics of Fujita-Kato type solutions to the Cauchy problem of the 3D inhomogeneous MHD system (
1.1
) with rough density in critical Besov spaces.
Our main results can be summarized as follows:

•

The global existence and large-time stability of solutions under the assumption conditions that
\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\mu^{-1}\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+3/p}_{p,1}}
with
1<p<3
are sufficiently small.

•

A global well-posedness result under the assumption that
\rho_{0}
is merely bounded and may have a large variation, and
\mu^{-1}\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1}}
is suitable small.

•

The existence of a unique global Fujita-Kato solution under the case that
\rho_{0}
and
\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{2,1}}
are only bounded, with a refined smallness of
\mu^{-1}\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,\infty}}
.

•

A general uniqueness result within the critical regularity framework, allowing for bounded and non-negative density, without requiring
L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty})
-regularity of the fluid velocity.

Remark 1.1
.

A few comments to our results are listed below.

•

For global solutions given in Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
, if we assume additionally that
u_{0},B_{0}\in L^{2}
, then one has the basic energy equality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|B(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mu\|\nabla u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla B(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|B_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Note that this additional condition will ensure the uniqueness of the solutions stated in Theorem
1.1
in the case
2<p<3
.

•

When
\nu\sim\mu
, we can relax the smallness of the corresponding norms for
(u_{0},B_{0})
if
\mu
is sufficiently large.

•

In Theorem
1.4
, the regularities on
(\rho_{2},u_{2},B_{2},\nabla P_{2})
are assumed to be critical, but the information of
u_{1}
and
B_{1}
is not necessary to be the same. Under the assumption that the basic energy inequality holds, Theorem
1.4
in fact provides a “weak-strong” uniqueness result
:
If
(\rho_{1},u_{1},B_{1},\nabla P_{1})
is a finite-energy weak solution to (
1.1
), and
(\rho_{2},u_{2},B_{2},\nabla P_{2})
is a solution with higher regularity required in (
1.18
), subject to the same initial data, then the two solutions coincide with each other. In fact, we shall prove a more general stability result, see Proposition
6.1
below.

–
1.4
.

with small variation and

in the critical

To overcome this difficulty, we make full use of the elaborate estimates based on interpolation and embedding inequalities in Besov and Lorentz spaces.
Building on uniform bounds and a compactness argument, we obtain the existence of a solution
(\rho,u,B,\nabla P)
to the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

To prove Theorem
1.2
concerning the global well-posedness for large density, inspired by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho(\partial_{t}u_{j}+u\cdot\nabla u_{j})-\Delta u_{j}+\nabla P_{j}=B\cdot\nabla B_{j},\\
&\partial_{t}B_{j}+u\cdot\nabla B_{j}-\Delta B_{j}=B\cdot\nabla u_{j},\\
&\dive u_{j}=\dive B_{j}=0,\\
&u_{j}|_{t=0}=\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{0},\quad B_{j}|_{t=0}=\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

By performing functional inequalities for
(u_{j},B_{j},\nabla P_{j})
and their derivatives with suitable time weights for initial layer analysis, we establish the localized energy estimates of
(u_{j},B_{j},\nabla P_{j})
(see Propositions

(u,B,\nabla P)
by carrying out an interpolation argument. Using these bounds, one can extend the local approximate solutions to the global ones and show the convergence to a desired global solution.

Furthermore, to show Theorem
1.3
, by using the refined embeddings and estimates of products in Lorentz spaces in the same spirit of
[

]

a priori

–

).

Finally, Theorem

\nabla u

\displaystyle\,C\|(u,B)\|_{L^{s,1}_{T}(L^{m})}\|(tu,tB)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{m,1}^{1+3/m})},

which, together with (

), (

).

To prove (

\displaystyle\,\leq C\|(u,B)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{m,1}^{-1+3/m})}^{1/2}\|t(u,B)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{m,1}^{1+3/m})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\,\leq C\|(u,B)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+3/p})}^{1/2}\|t(u,B)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{m,1}^{1+3/m})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\,\leq C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+3/p}}.

In addition, we take advantage of the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

\displaystyle\|(u,B)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p(m-3)}{3(m-p)}}\|(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L^{m}}^{\frac{m(3-p)}{3(m-p)}}.

), (

),

\displaystyle C\|(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L^{q,1}_{T}(L^{p})}^{\frac{p(m-3)}{3(m-p)}}\|t(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L^{s,1}_{T}(L^{m})}^{\frac{m(3-p)}{3(m-p)}}

\displaystyle C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+3/p}},

from which and (

\displaystyle\,\leq C\|(tu,tB)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{m,1}^{1+3/m})}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm d}t

and
A.8
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|t^{1/2}(D_{t}u_{j},D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|t^{1/2}(\nabla D_{t}u_{j},\nabla D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|t^{1/2}(\partial_{t}u_{j},\partial_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+C\|t^{1/2}(u\cdot\nabla u_{j},u\cdot\nabla B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle+C\|t^{1/2}(\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j},\nabla\partial_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+C\|t^{1/2}(\nabla(u\cdot\nabla)u_{j},\nabla(u\cdot\nabla)B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{3,\infty})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla u_{j},\nabla B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{6,2})}

\displaystyle+C\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{3})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{3,\infty})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla u_{j},\nabla B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{6,2})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{1/2})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle+C\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{H}^{1/2})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{1/2})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}.

This, together with (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), indicates that

\displaystyle\|t^{1/2}(D_{t}u_{j},D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|t^{1/2}(\nabla D_{t}u_{j},\nabla D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

(5.38)

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ce^{C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}}2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}.

To proceed, it follows from the standard elliptic estimates for (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\,\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j},\nabla P_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|t^{1/2}\rho(\partial_{t}u_{j}+u\cdot\nabla u_{j})+B\cdot\nabla B_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\,+C\|t^{1/2}(\partial_{t}B_{j}+u\cdot\nabla B_{j})+B\cdot\nabla u_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|t^{1/2}(\partial_{t}u_{j},\partial_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}+C\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{\infty})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla u_{j},\nabla B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{6})}

(5.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|t^{1/2}(\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j},\nabla\partial_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+C\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{\infty})}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}.

Putting (
5.25
) and (
5.32
) into (
5.39
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j},\nabla P_{j})\|_{L_{T}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\|t^{1/2}(\nabla\partial_{t}u_{j},\nabla\partial_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\|t^{1/2}(\nabla^{2}u_{j},\nabla^{2}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{L^{2}}.

This, combined with (
5.38
), yields

\displaystyle\|t^{1/2}(D_{t}u_{j},D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|t^{1/2}(\nabla D_{t}u_{j},\nabla D_{t}B_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ce^{C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}}2^{j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{L^{2}}.

Using Lemmas
A.7
–
A.8
, we further get

\displaystyle\,\|t^{3/4}(D_{t}u,D_{t}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+\|t^{3/4}(\nabla D_{t}u,\nabla D_{t}B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|t^{3/4}(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+C\|t^{3/4}(u,B)\cdot(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\,+C\|t^{3/4}(\nabla\partial_{t}u,\nabla\partial_{t}B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+C\|t^{3/4}\big(\nabla(u\cdot\nabla)u,\nabla(u\cdot\nabla)B\big)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|t^{3/4}(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}+C\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{3,\infty})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{6,2})}

\displaystyle\,+C\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{3})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\,+\|(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1/2})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Ce^{C\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}}\|(u_{0},B_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}+\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{1/2})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\,+\|(u,B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{H}^{1/2})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{6})}

\displaystyle\,+\|(\nabla u,\nabla B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{H}^{1/2})}\|t^{3/4}(\nabla^{2}u,\nabla^{2}B)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})},

which together with (
4.2
) and (
5.27
) implies (
5.29
). Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
5.3
.
∎

6.
Uniqueness of solutions

This section is devoted to the stability estimates in Proposition
6.1
below, which, in particular, implies Theorem
1.4
on the uniqueness
by taking the same initial data.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(\rho_{1},u_{1},B_{1},\nabla P_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},u_{2},B_{2},\nabla P_{2})
be two solutions to the system (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
supplemented with the same initial density
\rho_{0}
but with possibly different initial velocity and magnetic field
(u_{0,1},B_{0,1})
and
(u_{0,2},B_{0,2})
. Denote

\displaystyle f_{1}(t):=\|t^{1/2}\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},\quad f_{2}(t):=\|(\nabla u_{2},\nabla B_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|t^{3/4}\nabla D_{t}u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then there exists a constant
C
and a small time
T_{0}\in(0,T]
depending on
\|(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty})}
such that the following stability estimate holds
:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\{\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}(u_{1}-u_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(B_{1}-B_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+t^{-3/2}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{\dot{W}^{-1,3}}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2}),\nabla(B_{1}-B_{2})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq C\exp\bigg\{C\int_{0}^{T}f_{2}(t)\,{\rm d}t\,\exp\bigg\{C\int_{0}^{T}f_{1}(t)\,{\rm d}t\bigg\}\bigg\}

(6.1)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\Big\{\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}(u_{0,1}-u_{0,2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{0,1}-B_{0,2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}.

Proof.

Our arguments are inspired by the recent work
[
31
]
concerning the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations.
Note that
(\delta\rho,\delta u,\delta B)
satisfies the error system

(6.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\delta\rho=-\dive(\rho_{1}\delta u+\delta\rho u_{2}),\\
&\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}\delta u+u_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta u)+\nabla\delta P-\Delta\delta u=-\delta\rho D_{t}u_{2}-\rho_{1}\delta u\cdot\nabla u_{2}+B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta B+\delta B\cdot\nabla B_{2},\\
&\partial_{t}\delta B+u_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta B-\Delta\delta B=-\delta u\cdot\nabla B_{2}+B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta u+\delta B\cdot\nabla u_{2},\\
&\dive\delta u=\dive\delta B=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We estimate
\delta\rho
,
\delta u
and
\delta B
in turn.

(1)
Estimate of
\delta\rho
.
First, we aim to show

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|\delta\rho(t)\|_{\dot{W}^{-1,3}}\leq Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}t^{3/4}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}^{1/2},\quad t\in(0,T].

To achieve (
6.3
), for any function
\psi\in\dot{W}^{1,3/2}
and
t\in(0,T]
, one considers the backward transport equation

(6.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\Psi(s,x)+u_{2}(s,x)\cdot\nabla\Psi(s,x)=0,\quad\Psi(t,x)=\psi(x),\quad(s,x)\in(0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Noticing that

\|u_{2}\|_{L^{1}(s,t;L^{\infty})}\leq\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}|\log t/s|^{1/2},

when
\Psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the existence of
\Psi
for all
s\in(0,t]
can be shown by the classical theorem for linear transport equations (​​
[
8
]
). For
\Psi\in\dot{W}^{1,3/2}
, one can construct the solution
\Psi
by performing an approximation argument and using the fact that
\dot{W}^{1,3/2}
is a Banach space. Furthermore, the solution
\Psi
to (
6.4
) obeys the estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Psi(s)\|_{L^{3/2}}\leq\,

\displaystyle C\exp\bigg\{\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}\bigg\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{3/2}}

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C\exp\Big\{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}|\log t/s|^{1/2}\Big\}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{3/2}}.

It thus follows from
\eqref{deltaMHD}_{1}
and (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\delta\rho(t,x)\psi(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\delta\rho(t,x)\Psi(t,x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\,=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\langle\delta\rho(s),\Psi(s)\rangle{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\,=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho_{1}\delta u(s),\nabla\Psi(s)\rangle{\rm d}s.

This, combined with (
6.5
),
\rho_{1}\in L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty})
, and the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}|\log t/s|}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=t\int_{0}^{1}e^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}|\log\tau|}{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq Cte^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\log\tau}{\rm d}\tau\leq Cte^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}},

gives rise to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\delta\rho(t,x)\psi(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,t;L^{3/2})}\|\rho_{1}\delta u\|_{L^{4}(0,t;L^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}t^{3/4}\|\psi\|_{L^{3/2}}\|\rho_{1}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|\rho_{1}\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{6})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}t^{3/4}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}^{1/2}.

Thus, (
6.3
) holds.

(2)
Estimate of
\delta u
.
Taking the inner product of
\eqref{deltaMHD}_{1}
by
\delta u
and integrating the result over
[0,t]
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm d}s

(6.6)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}(u_{0,1}-u_{0,2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta B)\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s+\delta I_{1}+\delta I_{2}+\delta I_{3},

with

\displaystyle\delta I_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{1}(\delta u\cdot\nabla u_{2})\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\delta I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\delta B\cdot\nabla B_{2})\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\delta I_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\delta\rho D_{s}u_{2}\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s.

The terms
\delta I_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) are analyzed as follows. Due to Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities and the upper bound of
\rho_{1}
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\delta I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{1}\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\rho_{1}\delta u(s)\|_{L^{6}}^{1/2}\|\delta u(s)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}{\rm d}s

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm d}s.

Similar, it holds that

\displaystyle\delta I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla B_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{6}}^{1/2}\|\delta u(s)\|_{L^{6}}{\rm d}s

(6.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla\delta u,\nabla\delta B)(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla B_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm d}s.

To handle
\delta J_{3}
, we deduce from (
6.3
) that

\displaystyle\delta I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta\rho(s)\|_{\dot{W}^{-1,3}}\|D_{t}u_{2}\cdot\delta u(s)\|_{\dot{W}^{1,3/2}}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{s}(L^{2})}^{1/2}\|s^{3/4}\nabla(D_{s}u_{2}\cdot\delta u)(s)\|_{L^{3/2}}\,{\rm d}s.

Here, the standard embedding theorem yields

\displaystyle\|s^{3/4}\nabla(D_{s}u_{2}\cdot\delta u)\|_{L^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|s^{3/4}\nabla D_{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\delta u\|_{L^{6}}+C\|s^{3/4}D_{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|s^{3/4}\nabla D_{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we discover that

\displaystyle\delta I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s

(6.9)

\displaystyle\quad+Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|s^{3/4}\nabla D_{s}u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{s}(L^{2})}^{2})\,{\rm d}s.

Substituting (
6.7
), (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) into (
6.6
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}(u_{0,1}-u_{0,2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta B)\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad\quad+Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|s^{3/4}\nabla D_{s}u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla\delta u\|_{L^{2}_{s}(L^{2})}^{2})\,{\rm d}s

(6.10)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{\rm d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla B_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\delta B\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}(L^{2})}^{2}{\rm d}s.

(3)
Estimate of
\delta B
.
We deduce from
\eqref{deltaMHD}_{2}
that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\delta B(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s=\frac{1}{2}\|B_{0,1}-B_{0,2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta J_{1}+\delta J_{2}+\delta J_{3}

with

\displaystyle\delta J_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta u)\cdot\delta B\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\delta J_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\delta B\cdot\nabla u_{2})\cdot\delta B\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\delta J_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\delta u\cdot\nabla B_{2})\cdot\delta B\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s.

We handle
\delta J_{1}
,
\delta J_{2}
and
\delta J_{3}
in turn. By integration by parts, it follows that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\delta J_{1}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(B_{1}\cdot\nabla\delta B)\cdot\delta u\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s,

which can be cancelled by the first term on the right-hand side of (
6.10
).

For
\delta J_{2}
, it is observed that

\displaystyle\delta J_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla\delta B\cdot u_{2})\cdot\delta B\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{6}}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}{\rm d}s

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s.

In addition, one also has

\displaystyle\delta J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta u(s)\|_{L^{6}}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{6}}^{1/2}\|\nabla B_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(6.14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla\delta B,\nabla\delta u)(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla B_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\delta B(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s.

Now we denote

\displaystyle\delta E(t):=\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\delta u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2})}^{2}+\|\delta B\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|(\nabla\delta u,\nabla\delta B)(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t.

Collecting (
6.10
) and (
6.11
)–(
6.14
) up, we infer that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\delta E(t)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}(u_{0,1}-u_{0,2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{0,1}-B_{0,2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+Ce^{C\|t^{1/2}u_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{\infty})}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|(\nabla u_{2},\nabla B_{2})(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|s^{3/4}D_{s}u_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\delta E(s)\,{\rm d}s.

Taking advantage of Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\delta E(t)\leq\,

\displaystyle C\exp\bigg\{C\int_{0}^{T}f_{2}(t)\,{\rm d}t\,\exp\bigg\{C\int_{0}^{T}f_{1}(t)\,{\rm d}t\bigg\}\bigg\}

(6.15)

\displaystyle\times\Big\{\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}(u_{0,1}-u_{0,2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{0,1}-B_{0,2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}.

Combining (
6.15
) and (
6.3
) up, we get (
6.1
) and finish the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the ball
\mathcal{B}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~|\xi|\leq R\}
and the annulus
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~\lambda r\leq|\xi|\leq R\}
with two constants
r>0
and
R>r
. For any
u\in L^{p}
and
\lambda>0
, it holds

\displaystyle{\rm{Supp}}~

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)\subset\lambda\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{k+3(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|u\|_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle{\rm{Supp}}~\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle(u)\subset\lambda\mathcal{C}\Rightarrow\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{p}}.

The Besov spaces have the following properties.

Lemma A.2
.

(​​
[
8
]
)

The following properties hold in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

•

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{s}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{s-(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}_{p_{2},r_{2}}{\rm;}

•

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have the following chain of continuous embedding:

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{3/p-3/q}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{q,1}\hookrightarrow L^{q}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{q,\infty}{\rm;}

•

If
p<\infty
, then
\dot{B}^{d/p}_{p,1}
is continuously embedded in the set of continuous functions decaying to 0 at infinity
;

•

For any
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle H^{s+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{s}_{2,1}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}{\rm;}

•

Let
\Lambda^{\sigma}
be defined by
\Lambda^{\sigma}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-}\big(|\xi|^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}(u)\big)
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in\dot{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then
\Lambda^{\sigma}
is an isomorphism from
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}
to
\dot{B}^{s-\sigma}_{p,r}
;

•

Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},r_{1},r_{2}\leq\infty
,
s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

s_{2}<3/p_{2}\quad\text{\text{or}}\quad s_{2}=3/p_{2}~\text{and}~r_{2}=1.

The space
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}}+\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}
is a Banach space and has the weak compact and Fatou properties
:
If
u_{n}
is a uniformly bounded sequence of
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
, then an element
u
of
\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}
and a subsequence
u_{n_{k}}
exist such that
u_{n_{k}}\rightarrow u
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}\lesssim\liminf_{n_{k}\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\cap\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}.

Lemma A.9
.

(​​
[
22
, Proposition A.5]
)

Let
1<p,q<\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q}
with
\dive u_{0}=0
, and any
f\in L^{q,r}(0,T;L^{p})
, the following Cauchy problem of the Stokes system in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla P=f,\\
&\dive u=0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique solution
(u,\nabla P)
with
u\in{C}([0,T];\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q})
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q})}+\|(\partial_{t}u,\nabla^{2}u,\nabla P)\|_{L^{q,r}(0,T;L^{p})}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q}}+\|f\|_{L^{q,r}(0,T;L^{p})}\big),

where
C
is a constant independent of
T
,
u_{0}
and
f
.

In addition, let
\widetilde{s}>q
be such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{s}}\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{3}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{s}}>\frac{1}{2},

and define
\widetilde{m}\geq p
by the relation

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\widetilde{m}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{s}}=\frac{3}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}.

Then, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\widetilde{s},r}(0,T;L^{\widetilde{m}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q})}+\|(\partial_{t}u,\nabla^{2}u)\|_{L^{q,r}(0,T;L^{p})}).

Finally, if
2/q+3/p>2
, then for all
q<s<\infty
and
p<m<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2m}+\frac{1}{s}=\frac{3}{2p}+\frac{1}{q}-1,

then it holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{s,r}(0,T;L^{m})}\leq C\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{2-2/q})}+\|(\partial_{t}u,\nabla^{2}u)\|_{L^{q,r}(0,T;L^{p})}\big).

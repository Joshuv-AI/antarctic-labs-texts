FRSICL: LLM-Enabled In-Context Learning Flight Resource Allocation for Fresh Data Collection in UAV-Assisted Wildfire Monitoring
Yousef Emami, Hao Zhou, Miguel Gutierrez Gaitan, Kai Li, and Luis Almeida

Abstract
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are vital for public safety, particularly in wildfire monitoring, where early detection minimizes environmental impact. In UAV-Assisted Wildfire Monitoring (UAWM) systems, joint optimization of sensor transmission scheduling and velocity is critical for minimizing Age of Information (AoI) from stale sensor data. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has been used for such optimization; however, its limitations such as low sampling efficiency, simulation-to-reality gaps, and complex training render it unsuitable for time-critical applications like wildfire monitoring. This paper introduces a new online Flight Resource Allocation scheme based on LLM-Enabled In-Context Learning (FRSICL) to jointly optimize the UAV’s flight control and data collection schedule along the trajectory in real time, thereby asymptotically minimizing the average AoI across ground sensors. In contrast to DRL, FRSICL generates data collection schedules and controls velocity using natural language task descriptions and feedback from the environment, enabling dynamic decision-making without extensive retraining. Simulation results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed FRSICL compared to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Nearest-Neighbor baselines.

II
Background Concepts

This section introduces key background concepts related to UAWM, LLM-based optimization, and edge computing.

II-A

UAV-Assisted Wildfire Monitoring

Ground sensors are deployed across wildfire-prone areas to monitor critical environmental parameters such as temperature, humidity, and gas concentrations. UAVs play a crucial role in efficiently collecting this sensor data, particularly in remote or hazardous regions where human access is limited or unsafe. Their ability to reach difficult terrain enhances both the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of data collection operations. By removing the need for personnel to enter high-risk zones, UAVs significantly reduce safety concerns during wildfire events. Furthermore, their mobility enables rapid coverage of large areas, allowing for timely situational awareness and more effective decision-making during firefighting.

In UAWM, the AoI is a key metric that quantifies the timeliness of sensor data by measuring the delay between data generation and its collection by the UAV. Poor UAV flight planning or control can lead to increased AoI, resulting in outdated or less useful information
[
7
]
.

II-B

LLM Optimizers

Recent breakthroughs in prompt engineering have substantially enhanced LLMs’ effectiveness in solving complex, domain-specific challenges. By capitalizing on LLMs’ innate natural language understanding and generation capabilities, researchers have pioneered a transformative approach to optimization problems. Unlike conventional methods that require rigorous mathematical formalization and dedicated solvers, LLM-driven optimization frames problems through natural language prompts, allowing the model to iteratively generate and refine solutions based on contextual feedback.

This paradigm shift offers three key benefits for UAV applications:

1.

Task Flexibility
: Solutions can be adapted to diverse mission requirements by simply modifying the textual problem description, bypassing algorithmic redesign.

2.

Iterative Refinement
: The framework dynamically improves solutions through ICL, eliminating resource-intensive model retraining.

3.

Constraint Customization
: Domain-specific rules (e.g., safety limits, energy budgets) are enforced via structured prompting, ensuring feasible and high-performance outcomes.

By decoupling optimization from rigid mathematical formulations, LLM-enabled frameworks bridge the gap between human-intuitive problem specification and AI-driven execution, a critical advantage for real-world UAV deployments
[
11
]
.

ICL constitutes a fundamental capability of LLMs, enabling them to enhance performance on target tasks by leveraging formatted natural language instructions and demonstrations. Through contextualized demonstration prompts, LLMs can effectively leverage knowledge acquired during pre-training to perform a broad range of downstream tasks. Demonstrations are critical in this process, serving as key references that facilitate model adaptation and task-specific learning. Accordingly, the selection, formatting, and design of demonstrations require significant domain expertise.

This capability offers a promising mechanism for integrating LLMs into UAWM. By employing prior network solutions as demonstrations, ICL can enable LLMs to address novel and unforeseen challenges in UAWM, thereby providing a powerful tool to enhance UAV performance and adaptability
[
10
]
.

II-C

Edge Computing

Deploying LLMs at the edge for UAV applications involves optimizing these models through techniques such as quantization, pruning, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning to reduce their computational and memory demands, making them suitable for onboard hardware. This optimization enables real-time inference, which is crucial for autonomous UAV operations that require near-instantaneous decision-making. Edge AI enhances responsiveness by processing data directly on the device, significantly reducing latency; a key advantage for applications such as autonomous UAVs and real-time data collection.

Recent research proposes distributed edge computing frameworks that incorporate batching and model quantization to achieve high-throughput LLM inference on resource-constrained devices. Additionally, adaptive systems have been developed to optimize LLMs for edge deployment through offline, device-specific adaptation and online, latency-aware adjustments, ensuring efficient performance on commercial hardware.

The integration of 5G networks further supports these efforts by providing ultra-reliable, low-latency communication (URLLC) through technologies such as mmWave, massive MIMO, and beamforming. With end-to-end latencies as low as 1 millisecond, 5G URLLC is ideal for mission-critical UAV applications such as search and rescue (SAR), enabling real-time data transmission between UAVs and edge infrastructure. This high-speed, low-latency connectivity allows for on-the-fly LLM processing, facilitating dynamic and context-aware decision-making in time-sensitive scenarios.

Together, these advancements in edge AI and 5G technology enhance the capabilities of UAVs, making them more efficient and responsive in autonomous operations
[

]
.

IV-B

Problem Formulation

To evaluate the temporal relevance of the acquired sensor data, we utilize the Age of Information (AoI) metric. Formally, AoI quantifies the time interval between the current observation instant and the generation timestamp of the most recently and successfully collected data sample. Let
U_{j}(t)
denote the timestamp when sensor
j
generates its most recent measurement packet. The instantaneous AoI for sensor
j
at time
t
is then computed as:

\mathrm{AoI}_{j}(t)=t-U_{j}(t).

(4)

A smaller value of
\mathrm{AoI}_{j}(t)
corresponds to fresher information obtained from the ground sensor. In UAWM, AoI is significantly influenced by path loss conditions. Lower path loss reduces signal attenuation between ground sensors and the UAV, resulting in stronger received signals, faster data transmission, lower bit error rates, and fewer retransmissions. This improved communication efficiency directly contributes to a lower AoI, thereby preserving the freshness and relevance of sensory data for real-time applications. In contrast, high path loss leads to weaker signal reception, increased transmission errors, and more frequent retransmissions, which collectively raise the AoI and compromise the timeliness and reliability of the collected data.

The objective is to jointly optimize the UAV’s velocity and data collection schedule to minimize the averaged AoI over a finite horizon

Implementation of FRSICL

We consider a scenario where 10 ground sensors are randomly deployed within a 100 m × 100 m area following a uniform distribution. The simulation is implemented in a custom Python 3.10 environment. UAV actions are controlled by policies learned through the proposed FRSICL framework. The key simulation parameters are summarized in Table

Wireless communication between the UAV and ground sensors is simulated using the probabilistic channel model described in Section

VI-B

Baselines

PPO:
We implement Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) as a baseline, where a UAV with an actor–critic architecture dynamically selects sensors and adjusts its velocity along a circular trajectory to minimize the average AoI across 10 ground sensors. The agent is trained over 2000 episodes, each consisting of 30 steps.

Nearest Neighbor:
We implement Nearest Neighbor as another baseline, where the UAV identifies all ground sensors within its communication range and selects the nearest one to communicate with, aiming to minimize the average AoI across the network.

VI-D

Discussion

Conventional DRL methods, while effective for general UAWM optimization, present critical limitations in emergency scenarios. Their reliance on extensive offline training, implementation complexity, and the well-known simulation-to-reality gap make them unsuitable for time-sensitive, real-world operations. Although Diffusion Models (DMs) can enhance DRL by generating synthetic data to improve generalization, they introduce significant computational overhead, limiting their deployment in edge-constrained environments. In contrast, the proposed FRSICL framework leverages LLM-enabled ICL to overcome these challenges. It offers three key advantages for emergency wireless sensing and control: (1) fast, training-free adaptation through natural language prompts; (2) iterative policy refinement using online performance feedback; and (3) transparent, human-interpretable decision-making processes—features essential for reliability and responsiveness in critical situations such as wildfire monitoring.

VII
Conclusion

UAVs play a critical role in wildfire monitoring, where minimizing the AoI through efficient data collection and flight control is essential for timely disaster response. However, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods often suffer from simulation-to-reality gaps and high computational demands, limiting their practicality in urgent scenarios. To address these challenges, this paper introduces FRSICL—a novel LLM-enabled ICL framework that dynamically optimizes UAV velocity and data collection scheduling using natural language task descriptions. By leveraging pre-trained knowledge and environmental feedback, FRSICL eliminates the retraining bottlenecks associated with DRL and enables online, adaptive decision-making. Simulation results show that FRSICL significantly reduces AoI compared to PPO and nearest-neighbor baselines while maintaining stable velocity control. Future work will focus on real-world validation using UAV platforms and extending the framework to support coordinated operation in multi-UAV swarms.

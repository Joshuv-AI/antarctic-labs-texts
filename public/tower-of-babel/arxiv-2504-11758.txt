Hardy spaces, Campanato spaces and higher order Riesz transforms associated with Bessel operators
The Anh Bui

Abstract
. Let ν = ( ν 1 , … , ν n ) ∈ ( − 1 / 2 , ∞ ) n \nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n} , with n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , and let Δ ν \Delta_{\nu} be the multivariate Bessel operator defined by Δ ν = − ∑ j = 1 n ( ∂ 2 ∂ x j 2 − ν j 2 − 1 / 4 x j 2 ) . \Delta_{\nu}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}-\frac{\nu_{j}^{2}-1/4}{x_{j}^{2}}\right). In this paper, we develop the theory of Hardy spaces and BMO-type spaces associated with the Bessel operator Δ ν \Delta_{\nu} . We then study the higher-order Riesz transforms associated with Δ ν \Delta_{\nu} . First, we show that these transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators. We further prove that they are bounded on the Hardy spaces and BMO-type spaces associated with Δ ν \Delta_{\nu} .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, for
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
we consider the multi-variate Bessel operator

(1)

\Delta_{\nu}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}-\frac{\nu^{2}-1/4}{x_{j}^{2}}\right).

The operator
\Delta_{\nu}
is a positive self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}((0,\infty),dx)
. The eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\nu}
are
\{\varphi_{y}\}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, where

(2)

\varphi_{y}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(y_{j}x_{j})^{1/2}J_{\nu_{j}}(y_{j}x_{j}),\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{\nu}\varphi_{y}(x)=|y|^{2}\varphi_{y}(x),

where
J_{\alpha}(z)
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\alpha
. See
[
24
]
.

The
j
-th partial derivative associated with
\Delta_{\nu}
is given by

\delta_{\nu_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the adjoint of
\delta_{\nu_{j}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is

\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

It is straightforward that

(3)

\Delta_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}\delta_{\nu_{j}}.

The main aim of this paper is to develop the theory of Hardy spaces associated to Bessel operator
\Delta_{\nu}
and investigate the boundedness of the higher order Riesz transforms associated with the Bessel operator.

Hardy spaces associated to Bessel operator
\Delta_{\nu}
.
The theory of Hardy spaces associated with differential operators is a rich and active area of research in harmonic analysis, and it has attracted considerable attention. See, for example,

Regarding the Bessel operator, for
p\in(0,1]
, we first define the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
associated with Bessel operator
\Delta_{\nu}
as the completion of

\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}f:=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}f|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\}

under the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:=\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}f\|_{p}.

In
[
14
]
, Fridli introduced the atomic Hardy-type space
H^{1}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for the case
n=1
as follows.
A measurable function
a
on
(0,\infty)
is called an
F
-atom if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(a)

There exists
\delta>0
such that

a=\frac{1}{\delta}\chi_{(0,\delta)},

where
\chi_{(0,\delta)}
denotes the characteristic function of the interval
(0,\delta)
.

(b)

There exists a bounded interval
I\subset(0,\infty)
such that
\operatorname{supp}a\subset I
,

\int_{I}a(x)\,dx=0,

and

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty),dx)}\leq\frac{1}{|I|},

where
|I|
is the length of
I
.

A function
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),dx)
belongs to
H^{1}_{F}((0,\infty),dx)
if and only if it can be expressed as

f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}a_{j}(x),

where for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{j}
is an
F
-atom and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, satisfying

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{j}|<\infty.

The norm in
H^{1}_{F}(0,\infty)
is defined by

(4)

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{F}(0,\infty)}=\inf\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{j}|,

where the infimum is taken over all representations of
f
in terms of absolutely summable sequences
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with

f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}a_{j},\quad a_{j}\text{ being an }F\text{-atom for each }j\in\mathbb{N}.

In
[
1
]
, it was proved that the two Hardy spaces
H^{1}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
H^{1}_{F}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
coincide with equivalent norms. Our first aim is to extend this result to
0<p\leq 1
and
n\geq 1
. To do this, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
define

(5)

\rho(x)=\frac{1}{16}\min\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}.

It is clear that for each
x\in(0,\infty)^{n}
we have
\rho(y)\sim\rho(x)
for
y\in B(x,\rho(x))
. Throughout the paper, we will use this frequently without giving any explanation.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\rho
be the function as in (
5
). Let
p\in(0,1]
. A function
a
is called a
(p,\rho)
-atom associated to the ball
B(x_{0},r)
if

(i)

{\rm supp}\,a\subset B(x_{0},r)
;

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|B(x_{0},r)|^{-1/p}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all multi-indices
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n(1/p-1)
if
r<\rho(x_{0})
.

If
\rho(x_{0})=1
, the
(p,\rho)
coincides with the local atoms defined in
[
15
]
. Therefore, we can view a
(p,\rho)
atom as a local atom associated to the critical function
\rho
.
Let
p\in(0,1]
. We say that
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
is an atomic
(p,\rho)
-representation if

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
, each
a_{j}
is a
(p,\rho)
-atom,
and the sum converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is then defined as the completion of

\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):f\ \text{has an atomic
$(p,\rho)$-representation}\right\}

under the norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf\Big\{\Big(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \text{is an atomic $(p,\rho)$-representation}\Big\}.

Remark 1.2
.

In Definition
1.1
, for a
(p,\rho)
atom
a
associated with the ball
B(x_{0},r)
, if we impose an additional restriction
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
, then the Hardy spaces defined by using these atoms are equivalent to those defined by
(p,\rho)
atoms as in Definition
1.1
, which is without the restriction on
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
.

In the particular case when
n=p=1
, atoms defined in Definition
1.1
are a little bit different from
F
-atoms. However, it is not difficult to show that the Hardy spaces
H^{1}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
H^{1}_{F}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
are identical.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
, where
\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu_{j}:j=1,\ldots,n\}
. For
p\in(\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}},1]
, we have

H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

with equivalent norms.

For
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}
is strictly less than
1
. When
\nu_{j}\to\infty
, we have
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}\to 0
. In general, for larger values of
\nu_{j}
, we have a larger range of
p
.

As a counter part, we investigate a BMO type space, which will be proved to be a dual space of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
be the set of all polynomials of degree at most
M
. For any
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
P_{B}^{M}g
the
minimizing polynomial

of
g
on the ball
B
with degree
\leq M
, which means that
P_{B}^{M}g
is the unique polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}

such that,

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{B}[g(x)-P(x)]x^{\alpha}dx=0\quad\mbox{for every }|\alpha|\leq M.

It is known that if
g
is locally integrable, then
P^{M}_{B}g
uniquely exists (see
[
17
]
).
We define the local Campanato spaces associated to critical functions as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. Let
\rho
be the critical function as in (
5
).
Let
s\geq 0
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
.
The local Campanato space
BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
associated to
\rho
is defined to
be the space of all locally
L^{1}
functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}&:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)-P^{M}_{B}f(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\quad\quad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:\ {\rm balls}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|B|^{s/n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}<\infty.\end{split}

Here,
x_{B}
and
r_{B}
denote the center and radius of the ball
B
, respectively.

In Definition
1.4
, when
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
, we have
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. In this case,
P_{B}^{M}f
should be understood as
P_{B}^{M}\tilde{f}
, where
\tilde{f}
denotes the zero extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, for convenience, we continue to write
P_{B}^{M}f
without risk of confusion.

The we have the following result regarding the duality of the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
and
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
, where
\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu_{j}:j=1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\rho
be as in (
5
). Then for
p\in(\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}},1]
, we have

\displaystyle\big(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\big)^{\ast}=BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),\quad\mbox{where }s:=n(1/p-1)

for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
with
M\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
, where
s
is the greatest integer smaller than or equal to
s
.

Due to Theorem
1.5
, for
s\geq 0
we define
BMO^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as any space
BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
M\in\mathbb{N}
with
M\geq\lfloor s\rfloor
.

Riesz transforms associated to Bessel operators.
The study of Riesz transforms associated with differential operators is a central topic in harmonic analysis and has been extensively investigated. See, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
21
,
22
,
30
]
and the references therein. Let
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
we consider the higher order Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
, where
\delta_{\nu}^{k}=\delta_{\nu_{n}}^{k_{n}}\ldots\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}}
. See Section 4 for the definition of
\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. Regarding the Riesz transform associated to Bessel operators, in the
1
-dimensional case
n=1
, it was proved in
[
3
]
that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-1/2}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. In
[
2
]
(also for the case
n=1
), a different version of the higher order Riesz transform in the Bessel setting was investigated. Note that the higher order Riesz transform
[
3
]
are defined through the higher order Riesz transform associated to Laplacian of Bessel-type operator

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{\nu+1}{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},

and it is definitely not the higher Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
as expected due to some technical reasons.

Our first main result in this section is to show that the operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu_{j}:j=1,\ldots,n\}
and
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a multi-index. Then the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. That is,
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and its kernel
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
satisfies the following estimates:

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y

and

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle+|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y,x)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
,
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, where
\nu_{\min}=\min\{\nu_{j}:j=1,\ldots,n\}
. Then for
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
and
s=n(1/p-1)
, we have

(i)

the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

(ii)

the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
BMO^{s}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Although our approach is closely related to that in
[
5
]
, several new ideas and improvements are necessary due to fundamental differences in our setting. The techniques in
[
5
]
heavily rely on the discrete eigenvalues of the Laguerre operator and the fact that its eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. However, these properties do not hold in our case, requiring alternative methods. For instance, in estimating the heat kernels, we must develop a direct approach rather than leveraging the special properties of the derivative operator
\delta_{\nu}
acting on the semigroups, as was done in
[
5
]
. Furthermore, establishing the boundedness of the Riesz transform is significantly more challenging, as we cannot rely on specific structural properties of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions that were instrumental in
[
5
]
. These distinctions necessitate a refined analytical framework to address the difficulties that arise in our setting.

Note that the restriction
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
is essential to guarantee that the higher-order Riesz transforms are Calderón-Zygmund. The more general case
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
will be investigated in the forthcoming paper
[
4
]
.

Throughout the paper, we always use
C
and
c
to denote positive constants that are independent of the main parameters involved but whose values may differ from line to line. We will write
A\lesssim B
if there is a universal constant
C
so that
A\leq CB
and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote the integer part of
a
by
\lfloor a\rfloor
. For a given ball
B
, unless specified otherwise, we shall use
x_{B}
to denote the center and
r_{B}
for the radius of the ball. We also denote
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
and
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
.

In the whole paper, we will often use the following inequality without any explanation
e^{-x}\leq c(\alpha)x^{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
and
x>0
.

2.
Some kernel estimates

This section is devoted to establishing some kernel estimates related to the heat kernel of
\Delta_{\nu}
. These estimates play an essential role in proving our main results. We begin by providing an explicit formula for the heat kernel of
\Delta_{\nu}
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, denote

\Delta_{\nu_{j}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+\frac{\nu_{j}^{2}-1/4}{x_{j}^{2}}

on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the natural domain. It is easy to see that

\Delta_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta_{\nu_{j}}.

Let
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}
and let
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
be the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{j}}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(7)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j}).

For
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
,
j=1,\ldots,n,
, the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{j}}}
is given by

(8)

p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{(x_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{2t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{4t}\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{2t}\Big),

where
I_{\alpha}
is the usual Bessel funtions of an imaginary argument defined by

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\alpha+2k}}{k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)},\ \ \ \ \alpha>-1.

See for example
[
12
,
25
]
.

Note that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, we can rewrite the kernel
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
as follows

(9)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\Big(\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{2t}\Big)^{\nu_{j}+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{4t}\Big)\Big(\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{2t}\Big)^{-\nu_{j}}I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{2t}\Big).

The following properties of the Bessel function
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>-1/2
are well-known and are taken from
[
20
]
:

(10)

I_{\alpha}(z)\sim z^{\alpha},\ \ \ 0<z\leq 1,

(11)

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}+S_{\alpha}(z),

where

(12)

|S_{\alpha}(z)|\leq Ce^{z}z^{-3/2},\ \ z\geq 1,

(13)

\frac{d}{dz}(z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}(z))=z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(z),

(14)

0<I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)<2(\alpha+1)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ z>0.

and

(15)

0<I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z),\ \ \ z>0.

In the case
n=1
, from (
14
), (
15
) and (
8
), we have

(16)

0<p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y)<2(\alpha+1)\frac{t}{xy}p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y),\ \ \ z>0.

and

(17)

0<p_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\alpha+2}(x,y)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)t}{xy}p_{t}^{\alpha+1}(x,y),\ \ \ z>0.

Remark 2.1
.

When
n=1
, from (
16
) we imply directly that for
\nu>-1
we have
p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\leq p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
for all
t>0
and
x,y>0
. We will use this inequality frequently without any further explanation.

Before coming the the kernel estimates, we need the following simple identities

(18)

\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Big[xf(x)\Big]=k\delta^{k-1}_{\nu}f(x)+x\delta^{k}_{\nu}f(x)

and

(19)

\delta_{\nu}^{k}=\Big(\delta_{\nu+1}+\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{k}=\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}+\frac{k}{x}\delta^{k-1}_{\nu+1}.

2.1.
The case
n=1

We first write

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t}}\Big(\frac{xy}{2t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{2t}\Big)^{-\nu}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{xy}{2t}\Big).

Hence, using (
13
), it can be verified that

\partial_{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{(\nu+1/2)}{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{x}{2t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y),

which implies

(20)

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{2t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{y}{2t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

(21)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{2t}\big[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\big]+\frac{y-x}{2t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\nu>-1/2
. Then

|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Case 1:
xy\leq 2t
.
Using (
16
), (
8
) and (
10
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}.

Case 2:
xy>2t
.
Using (
16
), (
11
) and (
12
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

If
x>2y
, then
|x-y|\sim x
. Consequently,

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{2(\nu+1/2)}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}.

Similarly, if
y>2x
, we also have

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}.

In the remaining case if
y/2\leq x\leq 2y
, then
x\sim y\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
. It follows that

\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\sim 1.

Hence,

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

for all

and

.

On the other hand, from the Gaussian upper bound of
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
in Theorem
2.2
and
[

, Lemma 2.5]
, we have

|\partial_{t}^{M}p_{t/2}^{\nu}(z,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(2M+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
z,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
t>0
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta^{M}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\frac{1}{t^{(2M+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(k+2M+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
t>0
, which ensures (
25
).

For (
26
), we first have

\delta_{\nu}=\delta_{\nu+k+2M}+\frac{k+2M}{x}

and

\delta_{\nu}^{*}=-\delta_{\nu}-\frac{2\nu+1}{x}=-\delta_{\nu+k+2M}-\frac{2\nu+k+2M+1}{x}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nu}^{M}(\delta^{*}_{\nu})^{k}

\displaystyle=(\delta_{\nu}^{*}\delta_{\nu})^{M}(\delta^{*}_{\nu})^{k}

\displaystyle=\Big[\Big(-\delta_{\nu+k+2M}-\frac{2\nu+k+2M+1}{x}\Big)\Big(\delta_{\nu+k+2M}+\frac{k+2M}{x}\Big)\Big]^{M}

\displaystyle\Big(-\delta_{\nu+k+2M}-\frac{2\nu+k+2M+1}{x}\Big)^{k}.

Using the fact
\delta_{\nu}(fg)=\delta_{\nu}fg+f^{\prime}g
, we further implies

\Delta_{\nu}^{M}(\delta^{*}_{\nu})^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{2M+k}\frac{c_{j}}{x^{j}}\delta^{2M+k-j}_{\nu+k+2M},

where
c_{j}
are constants.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\nu}^{M}(\delta^{*}_{\nu})^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+k+2M}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{2M+k}\frac{1}{x^{j}}|\delta^{2M+k-j}_{\nu+k+2M}p_{t}^{\nu+k+M}(x,y)|,

which, together with Theorem
2.5
, implies

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\nu}^{M}(\delta^{*}_{\nu})^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+k+2M}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{2M+k}\frac{1}{x^{j}}\frac{1}{t^{(2M+k-j+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-j}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(2M+k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
t>0
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
. Then for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

|\delta_{\nu}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{xt^{\ell/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
y/2<x<2y
and
x\geq\sqrt{t}
.

Proof.

We will prove the inequality by induction.

For
\ell=0
, by (
16
) and Theorem
2.2
we have

\displaystyle|\delta_{\nu}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t}{xy}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

as long as
y/2<x<2y
and
x\geq\sqrt{t}
.

This ensures the proposition for the case
\ell=0
.

Assume the proposition is true for
\ell=0,1,\ldots,k
. That is, for
\ell=0,1,\ldots,k
, we have

|\delta_{\nu}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{\ell}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{xt^{\ell/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
y/2<x<2y
and
x\geq\sqrt{t}
.

We need to prove the estimate for
\ell=k+1
. Using (
19
), we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{k+1}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta^{k+1}_{\nu+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\delta^{k}_{\nu}[\delta_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]+\frac{1}{x}\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\delta^{k}_{\nu}[\delta_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]\Big|+\frac{1}{x}|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

By Theorem
2.5
,

E_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

It remains to estimate
E_{1}
. From (
21
) and (
17
),

(27)

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{2t}[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]+\frac{y-x}{2t}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{y}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+\frac{y-x}{2t}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)].

It, together with (
18
), follows that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+\frac{1}{t}\big|\delta_{\nu}^{k-1}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]\big|+\frac{|y-x|}{t}\big|\delta_{\nu}^{k}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]\big|

\displaystyle=:E_{11}+E_{12}+E_{13}.

Using (
19
) and Theorem
2.5
,

\displaystyle E_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y}|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|+\frac{1}{xy}|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k-1}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

as long as
y/2<x<2y
and
x\geq\sqrt{t}
.

For
E_{12}
, by (
19
) and Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle E_{12}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\big|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k-1}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]\big|+\frac{1}{tx}\big|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k-2}[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+2}(x,y)]\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

as long as

and

Similarly,

\displaystyle E_{13}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x-y|}{t}\frac{1}{xt^{k/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{t^{(k+1)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big),

.

This completes our proof.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

(28)

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\Big[\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big]\chi_{\{y/2<x<2y\}}+\frac{1}{x}\chi_{\{x\geq 2y\}}+\frac{1}{y}\chi_{\{y\geq 2x\}}.

Consequently, the operator

f\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}\big|[\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+1}}]f\big|\frac{dt}{t}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

If
y/2<x<2x
, then we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{x^{2}}\ldots+\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}\ldots

For the first term, using Theorem
2.5
,

\displaystyle\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-1-2\nu}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{x^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{1+2\nu}}{t^{1+\nu}}\frac{dt}{t}\sim\frac{1}{x}.

For the second part, using Proposition
2.8
,

\displaystyle\int^{x^{2}}_{0}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{x^{2}}_{0}\frac{1}{x}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{x^{2}}_{0}\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}.

If
x\geq 2y
, then
|x-y|\sim x
. This, together with Theorem
2.5
, implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big[\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}+\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-3/2}\Big]\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{-\nu-1/2}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}.

Similarly, if
y\geq 2x
, then we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}|\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\frac{1}{y}.

This completes the proof of (
28
).

For the second part, from the inequality (
28
) we have,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}

\displaystyle\big|[\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+1}}]f(x)\big|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k/2}\big|[\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta_{\nu+1}^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]f(y)\big|\frac{dt}{t}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big[\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big]|f(y)|dy+\int_{0}^{x/2}\frac{1}{x}|f(y)|dy+\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=:T_{1}f(x)+T_{2}f(x)+T_{3}f(x).

Obviously,

T_{2}f(x)\leq 2\int_{0}^{2x}\frac{1}{2x}|f(y)|dy\leq 2\mathcal{M}f(x),

which, together with the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
, implies that
T_{2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

For
T_{3}
, let
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle T_{3}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{2x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y}|f(y)|g(x)dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(y)|\int_{0}^{y/2}\frac{1}{y}|g(x)|dxdy.

Similarly,

\int_{0}^{y/2}\frac{1}{y}|g(x)|dx\lesssim\mathcal{M}g(y).

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle T_{3}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(y)|\mathcal{M}g(y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\|\mathcal{M}g\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}}.

It follows that
T_{3}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

It remains to show that
T_{1}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. To do this, fix
1<r<p
and
\epsilon<1/r^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle T_{1}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|dy+\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}|f(y)|dy.

Obviously,

\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|dy\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x),

and hence

f\mapsto\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|dy

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

For the second term, by Hölder’s inequality and the fact
r^{\prime}\epsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|^{r}dy\Big)^{1/r}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{r^{\prime}\epsilon}dy\Big)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}|f(y)|^{r}dy\Big)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{r}f,

which implies the operator

f\mapsto\frac{1}{x}\int_{x/2}^{2x}\Big(\frac{x}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\epsilon}|f(y)|dy

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

This completes our proof.
∎

3.
Hardy spaces associated to the Laguerre operator and its duality

This section is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
.

From the definition of the critical function
\rho
in (
5
), it is easy to see that if
y\in B(x,4\rho(x))
, then
\rho(x)\sim\rho(y)
. We will use this frequently without any explanation.

We now give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:

We divide the proof into two steps:
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Step 1: Proof of
H^{p}_{{\rm at},\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.
Fix
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
. Let
a
be a
(p,\rho)
atom associated with a ball
B:=B(x_{0},r)
. By Remark
1.2
, we might assume that
r\leq\rho(x_{0})
. By the definition of
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, it suffices to prove that

\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{p}\lesssim 1.

To do this, we write

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}(4B)}+\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}((4B)^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{1}+E_{2}.

Since the kernel of
e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}
satisfies a Gaussian upper bound (see Theorem
2.2
), the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),1<q<\infty
. This, along with the Hölder inequality, implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}(4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|4B|^{1/p-1/2}\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{2}(4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|4B|^{1/p-1/2}\|a\|_{L^{2}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.

It remains to take care of the second term
E_{2}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
r=\rho(x_{0})
.
By Theorem
2.2
, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho(y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy,

where
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu_{\min}+1/2
.

Since
\rho(y)\sim\rho(x_{0})
for
y\in B
and
|x-y|\sim|x-x_{0}|
for
x\in(4B)^{c}
and
y\in B
, we further imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{0})}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{0})}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{L^{p}((4B)^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{1-1/p}\Big[\int_{(4B)^{c}}\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{p\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{np}}dx\Big]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Case 2:
r<\rho(x_{0})
.
Using the cancellation property
\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
|\alpha|\leq\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor=:N_{p}
and the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0})]a(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}\Big[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N_{p}}\frac{\partial_{y}^{\alpha}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0})}{\alpha!}(y-x_{0})^{\alpha}\Big]a(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{B}\sum_{|\alpha|=N_{p}+1}\frac{\partial_{y}^{\alpha}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0}))}{\alpha!}(y-x_{0})^{\alpha}a(y)dy\Big|

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
.

Since
y\in B
we have
\rho(x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0}))\sim\rho(x_{0})
and
|x-[x_{0}+\theta(y-x_{0})]|\sim|x-x_{0}|
for all
x\in(4B)^{c}
,
y\in B
and
\theta\in(0,1)
, by Proposition
2.11
we further imply, for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{|y-y_{0}|}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{|y-y_{0}|}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{t>0}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{r}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle=F_{1}+F_{2}.

For the term
F_{1}
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{N_{p}+1}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p},

where in the last inequality we used the fact
r\leq|x-x_{0}|
for
x\in(4B)^{c}
.

For
F_{2}
, since
r<\rho(x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{0})}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Taking this and the estimate of
F_{1}
into account then we obtain

\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

Therefore,

|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)|\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{{}^{(N_{p}+1)\wedge\gamma_{\nu}}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}|B|^{1-1/p},

which implies

\|\mathcal{M}_{\Delta_{\nu}}a\|_{p}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

This completes the proof of the first step.

Step 2: Proof of
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Recall from
[
28
]
that for
p\in(0,1]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
a
is call a
(p,N)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom associated to a ball
B
if

(i)

a=\Delta_{\nu}^{N}b
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}\Delta_{\nu}^{k}b\subset B,\ k=0,1,\dots,M
;

(iii)

\|\Delta_{\nu}^{k}b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq r_{B}^{2(N-k)}|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}},\ k=0,1,\dots,N
.

Let
f\in H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Since
\Delta_{\nu}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator and satisfies the Gaussian upper bound, by Theorem 1.3 in
[
28
]
, we can write
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, where

\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\sim\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
and each
a_{j}
is a
(p,N)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom with
N>n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
a\in H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for any
(p,N)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom associated to a ball
B
with
N>n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
.

If
r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B})
, then from (iii),
a
is also a
(p,\rho)
atom and hence
a\in H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Hence, it remains to consider the case
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
.

We first claim that for any multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|<N
, we have

(31)

\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|\leq|B|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{B}}\Big)^{N-|\alpha|}.

Indeed, from (i) we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\Delta_{\nu}^{N}b(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B}\Delta_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}b(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}|\Delta_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}||b(x)|\,dx.

Note that

\Delta_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}=\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|=2N\atop\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{c_{\gamma,\beta}}{x^{\gamma}}\partial^{\beta}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha},

where
c_{\gamma,\beta}
are constants.

Since we have
|x|\geq\rho(x_{B})
for
x\in B
and
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
, we further imply, for
x\in B
,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\nu}^{N}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|=2N\atop\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{|\gamma|}}|\partial^{\beta}(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|=2N\atop\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{r_{B}^{|\alpha-\beta|}}{\rho(x_{B})^{|\gamma|}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|+|\beta|=2N\atop\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{|\gamma|+|\beta|-|\alpha|}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\int(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}a(x)\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}\|b\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{1-1/p}\frac{r_{B}^{2N}}{\rho(x_{B})^{2N-|\alpha|}}=|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho_{B}}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}.

This confirms (
31
).

We now turn to prove
a\in H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as long as
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
. Recall that
S_{0}(B)=B,S_{j}(B)=2^{j}B\setminus 2^{j-1}B,j\geq 1
. Set
\omega=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{j}
be the span of the polynomials
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
on
S_{j}(B)
corresponding inner product space given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}:=\fint_{S_{j}(B)}f(x)g(x)\,dx.

Let
\{u_{j,\alpha}\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{V}_{j}
obtained via the Gram–Schmidt process applied to
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}

which, through homogeneity and uniqueness of the process, gives

(32)

\displaystyle u_{j,\alpha}(x)=\sum_{|\beta|\leq\omega}\lambda_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}(x-x_{B})^{\beta},

where for each
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq\omega
we have

(33)

\displaystyle|u_{j,\alpha}(x)|\leq C\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\lambda_{\alpha,\beta}^{j}|\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\beta|}.

Let
\{v_{j,\alpha}\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
be the dual basis of
\big\{(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\big\}_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}
in
\mathcal{V}_{j}
;
that is, it is the unique collection of polynomials such that

(34)

\displaystyle\langle v_{j,\alpha},(\cdot-x_{B})^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{j}}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta},\qquad|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq\omega.

Then we have

(35)

\displaystyle\|v_{j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|},\qquad\forall\;|\alpha|\leq\omega.

Now let
P:={\rm proj}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}(a)
be the orthogonal projection of
a
onto
\mathcal{V}_{0}
. Then we have

(36)

\displaystyle P=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,u_{0,\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}u_{0,\alpha}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}v_{0,\alpha}.

Let
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{j_{0}}r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B})>2^{j_{0}-1}r_{B}
. Then we write

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}v_{0,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\Big[\frac{v_{j,\alpha}}{|S_{j}(B)|}-\frac{v_{j+1,\alpha}}{|S_{j+1}(B)|}\Big]+\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\frac{v_{j_{0}-1,\alpha}}{|S_{j_{0}-1}(B)|}.

Hence, we can decompose

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=(a-P)+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\Big[\frac{v_{j,\alpha}}{|S_{j}(B)|}-\frac{v_{j+1,\alpha}}{|S_{j+1}(B)|}\Big]+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\langle a,(\cdot-x_{B})^{\alpha}\rangle\frac{v_{j_{0}-1,\alpha}}{|S_{j_{0}-1}(B)|}

\displaystyle=a_{1}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}a_{2,j,\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}a_{3,\alpha}.

Let us now outline the important properties of the functions in the above decomposition.
For
a_{1}
we observe that for all
|\alpha|\leq\omega
,

(37)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}a_{1}\subset B,

\displaystyle\int a_{1}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx=0,

\displaystyle\|a_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|B|^{-1/p}.

Note that the property

\int a_{1}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\alpha|\leq\omega$}

implies that

\int a_{1}(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\alpha|\leq\omega$}.

Hence, in this case
a_{1}
is a
(p,\rho)
atom.

Next, for
a_{2,j,\alpha}
, it is obvious that
\operatorname{supp}a_{2,j,\alpha}\subset 2^{j+1}B
. In addition, from (
34
), we have

\int a_{2,j,\alpha}(x)(x-x_{B})^{\beta}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\beta|\leq\omega$},

which implies

\int a_{2,j,\alpha}(x)x^{\beta}dx=0\ \text{for all $|\beta|\leq\omega$}.

We now estimate the size of
a_{2,j,\alpha}
. Using (
31
) to write

\displaystyle\|a_{2,j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|}|2^{j}B|^{-1}\Big|\int a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j}B|^{-1-|\alpha|/n}|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(2N+n-n/p)}|2^{j}B|^{-1/p}.

This means that
a_{2,j,\alpha}
is a multiple of a
(p,\rho)
atom, which further implies

\Big\|\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\omega}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-2}a_{2,j,\alpha}\Big\|_{H^{p}_{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\lesssim 1,

as long as
N>n(1/p-1)
.

Next, for
a_{3,\alpha}
we first have
\operatorname{supp}a_{3,\alpha}\subset 2^{j_{0}}B
. Moreover, using (
31
) again,

\displaystyle\|a_{2,j,\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j_{0}}r_{B})^{-|\alpha|}|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1}\Big|\int a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1-|\alpha|/n}|B|^{1-1/p}r_{B}^{|\alpha|}\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho(x_{B}))}\Big)^{2N-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j_{0}(2N+n-n/p)}|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim|2^{j_{0}}B|^{-1/p},

as long as
N>n(1/p-1)
.

It follows that
a_{3,\alpha}
is a
(p,\rho)
atom and
\|a_{3,\alpha}\|_{H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim 1
.

This completes our proof.
∎

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. There exist a family of balls
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi})):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
and a family of functions
\{\psi_{\xi}:\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
;

(ii)

\{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi})/5):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
is pairwise disjoint;

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))}\lesssim 1
;

(iv)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{\xi}\subset B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))
and
0\leq\psi_{\xi}\leq 1
for each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
;

(v)

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\psi_{\xi}=1
.

Proof.

Consider the family
\{B(x,\rho(x)/5):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}
. Since
\rho(x)\leq 1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, by Vitali’s covering lemma we can extract a sub-family denoted by
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi})/5):\xi\in\mathcal{I}\}
satisfying (i) and (ii).

The item (iii) follows directly from (ii) and (
5
).

For each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
, define

\displaystyle\psi_{\xi}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))}(x)}{\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{B(x_{\theta},\rho(x_{\theta}))}(x)},\ \ &x\in B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi})),\\
0,&x\notin B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi})).\end{cases}

Then
\{\psi_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
satisfies (iv) and (v).

This completes our proof.

.

,

be as in Lemma

. Let

-atom

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx=C_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}(t^{2}\Delta_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}})f(x){t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t},

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Fix
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
and
s=n(1/p-1)
. We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.

Proof of
BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}
.

Let
f\in BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and let
a
be a
(p,\rho)
-atoms. Then
by Lemma
3.4
and Proposition 3.2 in
[
31
]
(see also
[
10
]
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,\infty)}(t^{2}\Delta_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}})f(x){t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}a(x)}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\Delta_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<t}|t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}a(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Using Lemma
3.3
,

\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{2s/n+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}|(t^{2}\Delta_{\nu})^{k}\Phi(t\sqrt{\Delta_{\nu}})f(x)|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

In addition, by Theorem
1.3
and Theorem 1.3 in
[
28
]
, we have

\left\|\left(\iint_{\Gamma(x)}|t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}a(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim 1.

Consequently,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x){a(x)}dx\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

for
f\in BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and all
(p,\rho)
atoms
a
.

It follows that
BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\subset(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}
.

Step 2.

Proof of
(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}\subset BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Let
\{\psi_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\{B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
as in Corollary
2.7
.
Set
B_{\xi}:=B(x_{\xi},\rho(x_{\xi}))
and we will claim that for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
\psi_{\xi}f\in H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and

(43)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\xi}f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\left|B_{\xi}\right|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

It suffices to prove that

\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{p}\lesssim|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}.

Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality,

(44)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{p}(4B_{\xi})}&\leq|4B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{2}(4B_{\xi})}\\
&\lesssim|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.\end{split}

If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B_{\xi}
, then applying Proposition
2.10
and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\big|e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{\xi}}\left(\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\sim\int_{B_{\xi}}\left(\frac{t}{\rho(x_{\xi})}\right)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\rho(x_{\xi})^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{\xi}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}\int_{B_{\xi}}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\rho(x_{\xi})^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{\xi}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

which implies that

(45)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t^{2}\Delta_{\nu}}(\psi_{\xi}f)\big|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B_{\xi})}\lesssim|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},\end{split}

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Combining (
44
) and (
45
) yields (
43
).

Assume that
\ell\in(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}
.
For each index
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
we define

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}f:=\ell(\psi_{\xi}f),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

By (
43
),

\displaystyle|\ell_{\xi}(f)|\leq C\|\psi_{\xi}f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C|B_{\xi}|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Hence there exists
g_{\xi}\in L^{2}(B_{\xi})
such that

\displaystyle\ell_{\xi}(f)=\int_{B_{\xi}}f(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

We define
g=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}1_{B_{\xi}}g_{\xi}
. Then, if
f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
a_{i}
is a
(p,2,\rho)
-atom,
i=1,\cdots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\ell(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\ell(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\ell(\psi_{\xi}a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\ell_{\xi}(a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{B_{\xi}}a_{i}(x)g_{\xi}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}g(x)a_{i}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}}f(x)g(x)dx.

Suppose that
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
, and
0\not\equiv f\in L_{0}^{2}(B)
, that is,

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
\operatorname{supp}f\subset B
and
\displaystyle\int_{B}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0
with all
|\alpha|\leq M
. Then similarly to (
43
),

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

Hence

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\Big|\int_{B}fg\Big|\leq\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

From this we conclude that
g\in(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}
and

\|g\|_{(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

But by elementary functional analysis (see Folland and Stein
[
13
, p. 145]
),

\|g\|_{(L_{0}^{2}(B))^{\ast}}=\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}}\|g-P\|_{L^{2}(B)},

where
P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
is the set of all polynomials of degree at most
M
.
Hence

(46)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:ball\\
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{M}}\|g-P\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

Moreover, if
B
is a ball with
r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B})
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
f\not\equiv 0

and
\operatorname{supp}f\subset B
, then similarly to (
43
),

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\ell(f)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}fg\right|\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}|B|^{1/p-1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

(47)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B:{\rm ball}\\
r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B})\end{subarray}}|B|^{1/2-1/p}\|g\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}}.

From (
46
) and (
47
) it follows that
g\in BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{BMO^{s,M}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C\|\ell\|_{(H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))^{\ast}},\quad\mbox{where }s=n(1/p-1).

This completes our proof.
∎

4.
Boundedness of Riesz transforms on Hardy spaces and Campanato spaces associated to
\Delta_{\nu}

In this section, we will study the boundedness of the higher-order Riesz transforms. We first show in Theorem
1.6
below that the higher-order Riesz transforms are Calderón-Zygmund operators. Then, in Theorem
1.7
we will show that the higher-order Riesz transforms are bounded on our new Hardy spaces and new BMO type spaces defined in Section 4.

We first give a formal definition of
\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}
for any
s>0
. For
s>0
, by the spectral theory we define

\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-s}\,dE(\lambda),

where
E(\lambda)
is the spectral decomposition of
\Delta_{\nu}
.

The domain of
\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}
consists of all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2s}\,d\langle E(\lambda)f,f\rangle

is finite.

We will show that

(48)

\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{s-1}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}\,du.

Indeed, by the spectral theorem, the semigroup
e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}
can be written as

e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\lambda}\,dE(\lambda).

Substituting this into the integral definition of
\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}
,

\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}u^{s-1}\,du=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\lambda}\,dE(\lambda)\right)u^{s-1}\,du.

Interchanging the order of integration,

\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}u^{s-1}\,du=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\lambda}\,u^{s-1}\,du\right)\,dE(\lambda).

This inner integral is a standard Laplace transform:

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\lambda}u^{s-1}\,du=\lambda^{-s}\Gamma(s),\quad\text{for }\lambda>0.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}u^{s-1}\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-s}dE(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\nu}^{-s}.

This ensures the formula (
48
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}-\delta_{\nu+\alpha}^{k}\Delta_{\nu+\alpha}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to prove for the case
\alpha=e_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. We will prove for the case
\alpha=e_{1}
since other cases can be done similarly.

Using (
48
), we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}-\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k}\Delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-|k|/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(|k|/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}[\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}-\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+e_{1}}}]\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(|k|/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k_{1}/2}[\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{1}}}-\delta_{\nu_{1}+e_{1}}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{1}+e_{1}}}]\prod_{j=2}^{n}t^{k_{j}/2}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{k_{j}}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{j}}}\frac{dt}{t}.

Due to Theorem
2.5
, we have

\sup_{t>0}|t^{k_{j}/2}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{k_{j}}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{j}}}f|\lesssim\mathcal{M}f

and hence the operator
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|t^{k_{j}/2}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{k_{j}}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{j}}}f|
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for each
j=2,\ldots,n
and for
1<p<\infty
.

On the other hand, from Proposition
2.9
, the operator

f\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k_{1}/2}\big|[\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{k_{1}}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{1}}}-\delta_{\nu_{1}+e_{1}}^{k}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu_{1}+e_{1}}}]f\big|\frac{dt}{t}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
.

Consequently, the operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}-\delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{k}\Delta_{\nu+e_{1}}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
.

This completes our proof.
∎

We are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
:

We first prove that the higher Riesz
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
by induction.

For
|k|=1
, from (
3
),

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\nu}^{1/2}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta_{\nu}^{1/2}f,\Delta_{\nu}^{1/2}f\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\nu}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}\delta_{\nu_{j}}f,f\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\delta_{\nu_{j}}f,\delta_{\nu_{j}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\delta_{\nu_{j}}f\|_{2}^{2},

which implies that

\|\delta_{\nu_{j}}f\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta_{\nu}^{1/2}f\|_{2},\ \ j=1,\ldots,n.

Hence, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
if
|k|=1
.

Assume that the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
k
with
|k|=\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
. We need to prove that for any
k
with
|k|=\ell+1
the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

If
k_{i}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, we might assume that
k_{1}=\ldots=k_{j}=1
and
k_{j+1}=\ldots=k_{n}=0
for some
1\leq j\leq n
. Then we can write

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}=[\delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu_{1}}^{-1/2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\delta_{\nu_{j}}\Delta_{\nu_{j}}^{-1/2}\otimes I\otimes\ldots\otimes I]\circ[\Delta_{\nu_{1}}^{1/2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\Delta_{\nu_{j}}^{1/2}\otimes I\otimes\ldots\otimes I]\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}.

Since each
\delta_{\nu_{i}}\Delta_{\nu_{i}}^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
i=1,\ldots,j
, the operator
\delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu_{1}}^{-1/2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\delta_{\nu_{j}}\Delta_{\nu_{j}}^{-1/2}\otimes I\otimes\ldots\otimes I
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. On the other hand, by the joint spectral theory, the operator
[\Delta_{\nu_{1}}^{1/2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\Delta_{\nu_{j}}^{1/2}\otimes I\otimes\ldots\otimes I]\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Therefore, the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Hence, we have prove the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
-boundedness for the Riesz transform
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
in the case
k_{i}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Otherwise, we might assume that
k_{1}\geq 2
. By Theorem
4.1
, we might assume that
\nu_{1}\geq k_{1}+2
. Then using the fact

\delta_{\nu_{1}}^{2}=-\Delta_{\nu_{1}}+\frac{2\nu+1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{1}},

which implies

\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}=-\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\Delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}+(2\nu+1)\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\Big[\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}\Big].

For the first operator, we can write

-\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-(|k|-2)/2}\circ\Delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-1}.

The operator
\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}
due to the inductive hypothesis, while the operator
\Delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-1}
is bounded on
L^{2}
due to the joint spectral theory. Hence, the first operator is bounded on
L^{2}
.

For the second operator, using the product rule we have

\delta_{\nu}^{k-2e_{1}}\Big[\frac{1}{x_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}\Big]=\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}-2}\frac{c_{j}}{x_{1}^{1+j}}\delta_{\nu}^{k-(1+j)e_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}

Hence, it suffices to prove that the operator

f\mapsto\frac{1}{x^{j}_{1}}\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}f

is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for each
j=1,\ldots,k_{1}-2
.

Denote by
\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
the kernel of
\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. By Proposition
2.10
we have, for each
j=2,\ldots,k_{1}-2
and a fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{1}^{j}}\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{1}^{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}\Big)^{j}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-(\nu_{1}+3/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-(\nu_{1}-j+5/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{1}|x-y|^{n-1}}\min\Big\{\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-\epsilon},\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-2}\Big\},

since
\nu_{1}-j+5/2\geq 2
for
j=2,\ldots,k_{1}-2
.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{x_{1}^{j}}\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}f(y)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|\leq x_{1}}\frac{1}{x_{1}|x-y|^{n-1}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-\epsilon}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle+\int_{|x-y|>x_{1}}\frac{1}{x_{1}|x-y|^{n-1}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{x_{1}}\Big)^{-2}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|\leq x_{1}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{x_{1}}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x)

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|>x_{1}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{1}+1/2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}f(x),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

It follows that the operator
\frac{1}{x_{1}^{j}}\delta_{\nu}^{k-je_{1}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, which completes the proof of the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
-boundedness of
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
.

It remains to prove the kernel estimates for the Riesz transforms
\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
.

Recall that
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)
is the kernel of
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
. By (
48
) and Proposition
2.10
, we have

(49)

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=c\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)},

which implies

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\ \ \ x\neq y.

We will show that

|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
.

Indeed, if
|y-y^{\prime}|\geq\max\{\rho(y),\rho(y^{\prime})\}
, then from (
49
) we have

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)|+|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big[\Big(\frac{\rho(y)}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}+\Big(\frac{\rho(y^{\prime})}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\nu_{\min}+1/2}

If
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\max\{\rho(y),\rho(y^{\prime})\}
, then by the mean value theorem,

(50)

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=c\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\big[\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y^{\prime})\big]\frac{dt}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|y-y^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{|k|/2}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\big|\partial_{y}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))\big|\frac{dt}{t}.

By Theorem
2.11
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{y}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))\big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{1}{\rho(y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))}\Big]\frac{1}{t^{(n+k)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-[y+\theta(y-y^{\prime})]|}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\rm min}+1/2)}.

Note that

|x-[y+\theta(y-y^{\prime})]|\sim|x-y|\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho(y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))\sim\rho(y)

for all
\theta\in[0,1]
,
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
and
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\max\{\rho(y),\rho(y^{\prime})\}
.

Therefore,

\big|\partial_{y}\delta^{k}_{\nu}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y+\theta(y-y^{\prime}))\big|\lesssim\Big[\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{1}{\rho(y)}\Big]\frac{1}{t^{(n+k)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|}{ct}\Big)\ \Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\rm min}+1/2)}

for all
\theta\in[0,1]
,
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
and
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\max\{\rho(y),\rho(y^{\prime})\}
.

Putting it back into (
50
),

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|y-y^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho(y)}\Big]\frac{1}{t^{(n+k)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|}{ct}\Big)\ \Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\rm min}+1/2)}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y-y^{\prime}|\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho(y)}+\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho(y)}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big]\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho(y)}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-(\nu_{\min}+1/2)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}.

Since
|y-y^{\prime}|\lesssim\rho(y)
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

where and
\gamma_{\nu}=\min\{1,\nu_{\min}+1/2\}
.

For the same reason, since
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{x-y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle|\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y,x)-\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(y^{\prime},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{|x-y|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq|x-y|/2
.

This completes our proof.
∎

is new, even when
n=1
. In fact, in the case of
n=1
, the boundedness of the Riesz transform was explored in
[

,
2
,
3
]
. In
[

]
, the Riesz operator was decomposed into local and global components.

We now give the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
:

Fix
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
M>n(1/p-1)
.

(i) Recall from
[
28
]
that for
p\in(0,1]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
a
is call a
(p,N)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom associated to a ball
B
if

(i)

a=\Delta_{\nu}^{N}b
;

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}\Delta_{\nu}^{k}b\subset B,\ k=0,1,\dots,M
;

(iii)

\|\Delta_{\nu}^{k}b\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq r_{B}^{2(N-k)}|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}},\ k=0,1,\dots,N
.

Let
f\in H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Since
\Delta_{\nu}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator and satisfies the Gaussian upper bound, by Theorem 1.3 in
[
28
]
, we can write
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, where

\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\sim\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
and each
a_{j}
is a
(p,N)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom with
N>n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
.

In addition, by Theorem
1.3
,
H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu+2\vec{M}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\Delta_{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\equiv H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, where
\vec{M}=(M,\ldots,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently, it suffices to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,M)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atoms
a
.

Let
a
be a
(p,M)_{\Delta_{\nu}}
atom associated to a ball
B
. We have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Using the
L^{2}
-boundedness of both
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+\vec{M}}}f|
and the Riesz transform
\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}
and the Hölder inequality, by the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, using
a=\Delta_{\nu}^{M}b
,

e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a=e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}b.

We have

\displaystyle K_{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}K_{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}}(x,y)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{t}\ldots\frac{du}{u}+c\int_{t}^{\infty}\ldots\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2},

where
K_{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}}(x,y)
is the kernel of
{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M-|k|/2}}
.

For
I_{1}
, by Corollary
2.12
and Theorem
2.2
we have, for
u\leq t
,

\displaystyle|K_{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Delta_{\nu}^{M}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}p_{t}^{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}(z,x)p_{u}^{\nu}(z,y)dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{M+|k|/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{M+|k|/2+n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

which implies that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{M+n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M+n}}.

Similarly, by Corollary
2.12
and Theorem
2.2
we have, for
u>t

\displaystyle|K_{e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M}e^{-u\Delta_{\nu}}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}p_{t}^{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}(x,z)\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{M}p_{u}^{\nu}(z,y)|dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{u^{M+|k|/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{u^{M+|k|/2+n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{cu}\Big)

which implies that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u^{M}}\frac{1}{u^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{cu}\Big)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{|x-y|^{2}}\ldots+\int_{|x-y|^{2}}^{\infty}\ldots

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu+k+2\vec{M}}}\delta^{k}_{\nu}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}a|\Big\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\Big[\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}|b(y)|dy\Big]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\Big[\int_{B}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{2M}}\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}|b(y)|dy\Big]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\frac{\|b\|_{1}^{p}}{|x-x_{B}|^{(n+2M)p}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B}\frac{r_{B}^{2Mp}|B|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{(n+2M)p}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

as long as
M>n(1/p-1)
.

(ii) By the duality in Theorem
1.5
, it suffices to prove that the conjugate
\Delta_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{\nu_{j}}^{*}
is bounded on the Hardy space
H^{p}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. By Theorem
1.3
, we need only to prove that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 1

for all
(p,\rho)
atoms
a
.

Suppose that
a
is a
(p,\rho)
atom associated to a ball
B
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}+\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}.

Since
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}f|
and
\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}
are bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, by the Hölder’s inequality and the standard argument, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(4B)}\lesssim 1.

For the second term, we consider two cases.

Case 1:
r_{B}=\rho(x_{B})
.
Using (
48
), we have for
x\in(4B)^{c}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}|(\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}})^{*}a(x)|

\displaystyle=c\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{k}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}e^{-(t+u)\Delta_{\nu}})^{*}a(x)\frac{du}{u}\Big|

\displaystyle=c\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}u^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(y,x)a(y)dy\frac{du}{u}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}|u^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(y,x)||a(y)|dy\frac{du}{u}\Big|.

Using Theorem
2.5
and the fact
\rho(y)\sim\rho(x_{B})
and
|x-y|\sim|x-x_{B}|
for
y\in B
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus 4B
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}

\displaystyle|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B}\frac{u^{|k|/2}}{(u+t)^{(n+|k|)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{c(u+t)}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u+t}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}|a(y)|dy\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\sup_{t>0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(u+t)^{n/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{c(u+t)}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u+t}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{B})}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\|a\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r_{B}^{\gamma_{\nu}}}{|x-x_{B}|^{n+\gamma_{\nu}}}|B|^{1-1/p}.

It follows that

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

Case 2:
r_{B}<\rho(x_{B})
.

Using the formula (
48
), we have

\displaystyle e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}e^{-(t+u)\Delta_{\nu}}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{|k|/2}[\delta_{\nu}^{k}e^{-(t+u)\Delta_{\nu}}]^{*}a(x)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(y,x)a(y)dy\frac{du}{u}.

Using the cancellation property
\displaystyle\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq N:=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor
, we have

\displaystyle\big|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)\big|

\displaystyle=c\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}\Big[\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(y,x)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\frac{(y-x_{B})^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}(x_{B},x)\Big]a(y)dy\frac{du}{u}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}|y-x_{B}|^{N+1}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]\atop|\beta|=N+1}|\partial^{\beta}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}\big(y+\theta(x_{B}-y),x\big)||a(y)|dy\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{|k|/2}r_{B}^{N+1}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]\atop|\beta|=N+1}|\partial^{\beta}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}\big(y+\theta(x_{B}-y),x\big)||a(y)|dy\frac{du}{u}

Note that for
y\in B
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have
y+\theta(x_{B}-y)\in B
and hence
\rho(y+\theta(x_{B}-y))\sim\rho(x_{B})
and
|x-[y+\theta(x_{B}-y)]|\sim|x-y|\sim|x-x_{B}|
. This, together with Proposition
2.11
, implies, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B
and
y\in B
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]\atop|\beta|=N+1}

\displaystyle|\partial^{\beta}\delta_{\nu}^{k}p_{t+u}^{\nu}\big(y+\theta(x_{B}-y),x\big)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{(t+u)^{(N+1)/2}}+\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\frac{1}{(t+u)^{(n+k)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{|x-x_{B}|^{N+1}}+\frac{1}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\frac{1}{(t+u)^{(n+k)/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{2c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}.

Hence, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B
and
y\in B
we have

\displaystyle\big|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{|k|/2}}{(t+u)^{(n+k)/2}}\Big(\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{N+1}}+\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{2c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t+u)^{n/2}}\Big(\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{N+1}}+\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{2}}{2c(t+u)}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t+u}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\Big(\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-y|^{N+1}}+\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}},

which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\Big(\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{N+1}}+\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{B}|}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{-\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\Big[\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-x_{B}|^{N+1}}+\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{\rho(x_{B})^{N+1}}\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{B})}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big].

Since
r_{B}\leq\min\{\rho(x_{B}),|x-x_{B}|\}
, this further implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\big|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\Big[\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-y|^{N+1}}+\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{\rho(x_{B})}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}\wedge(N+1)}\Big(\frac{\rho(x_{B})}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}\wedge(N+1)}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{1}\Big[\frac{r_{B}^{N+1}}{|x-y|^{N+1}}+\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{|x-x_{B}|}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}\wedge(N+1)}\Big]

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B
and
y\in B
.

Consequently,

\Big\|\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\Delta_{\nu}}\Delta_{\nu}^{-|k|/2}(\delta_{\nu}^{*})^{k}a|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash 4B)}\lesssim 1,

as long as
\frac{n}{n+\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 1
.

This completes our proof.

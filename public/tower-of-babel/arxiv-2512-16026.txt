Entropy-production fluctuation theorem for a generalized Langevin particle in crossed electric and magnetic fields
L. C. González-Morales, I. Pérez Castillo, J. I. Jiménez-Aquino

Abstract
We study fluctuations of entropy production for a charged Brownian particle confined in a harmonic trap and driven out of equilibrium by crossed electric and magnetic fields. The magnetic field is constant and perpendicular to the plane of motion, while the electric field is time dependent and provides the driving. The non-Markovian dynamics is modeled by a generalized Langevin equation with memory and Gaussian noise. Using the exact solution of this linear dynamics, we obtain the time-dependent Gaussian phase-space probability density and from it compute the trajectory-dependent total entropy production. For two solvable driving protocols, we prove analytically that the entropy production obeys a detailed fluctuation theorem.

Introduction

Fluctuation theorems (FTs) are exact relations, first established in the pioneering works of the 1980s and 1990s
Boshkov1981
,
Evans1993
,
Gallavoti1995
,
Jarzynski1997
,
Crooks1999
,
Kurchan1999
, that quantify irreversibility in nonequilibrium statistical physics. They sharpen the second law for small systems observed over finite times by relating the probability of a forward trajectory to that of an appropriate backward (time-reversed) trajectory. Beyond their original formulations, FTs have been developed for a broad family of thermodynamic observables, including work, heat, power, and entropy production
vanZon2004
,
Noh2012
,
Jimenez2013
,
Bochkov2013
,
Lemos2018
,
Rao2018
,
Park2023
,
Semeraro2024
. Current activity continues to extend both their scope and interpretations, ranging from fluctuation relations for systems in a constant magnetic field
Coretti2020
, to topological formulations and feedback-controlled settings (Maxwell-demon-type scenarios) to connections with learning dynamics and even simplified game-like models
Sidajaya2025
,
Hack2023
,
Mahault2022
,
Zeng2021
. Fluctuation relations have found applications in biophysical systems
Hayashi2018
, in active matter
Chaki2018
,
Goswami2018
,
Narinder2021
,
Argun2016
, and have also been extended to the quantum regime
Funo2018
,
Binder2018
, and quantum curved spacetime
Basso2025
.

In 2005, Seifert
Seifert2005
extended the fluctuation-theorem framework to entropy production and proved a detailed fluctuation theorem (DFT) in a nonequilibrium steady state over a finite time interval,
P(\Delta s_{\rm tot})/P(-\Delta s_{\rm tot})=e^{\Delta s_{\rm tot}}
, where
P(\Delta s_{\rm tot})
is the probability of observing a total entropy production
\Delta s_{\rm tot}
along a forward trajectory and
P(-\Delta s_{\rm tot})
is the corresponding probability for the backward trajectory. The total entropy production
\Delta s_{\rm tot}
includes both the change in system entropy (the Brownian particle) and the entropy change of the surrounding bath. The associated integral fluctuation theorem (IFT),

\langle e^{-\Delta s_{\rm tot}}\rangle=1
, follows directly from the DFT.
Four years later, the DFT was extended to transient processes initialized from an equilibrium canonical distribution
Saha2009
. Subsequently, the transient DFT was established for an electrically charged Brownian particle in the presence of crossed electric and magnetic fields
Jimenez2010
. The analyses in
Seifert2005
,
Saha2009
,
Jimenez2010
were formulated for Markovian stochastic dynamics described by a standard Langevin equation with additive Gaussian white noise.

On the other hand, fluctuation relations such as Crooks’ theorem and Jarzynski’s equality have also been extended to non-Markovian dynamics described by a generalized Langevin equation (GLE) with additive colored noise
Mai2007
,
Ohkuma2007
,
Jimenez2015
. In particular, Ref.
Jimenez2015
investigated the transient work fluctuation theorem for a charged Brownian particle in crossed electric and magnetic fields within a GLE framework with a time-dependent friction memory kernel. The corresponding detailed fluctuation theorem for the total entropy production in the non-Markovian setting was addressed in Ref.
Ghosh2017
, where the analysis relies on a Fokker-Planck description for the joint probability density
P(x,w,t)
of the particle position
x
, the thermodynamic work
w
, and time
t
.

To the best of our knowledge, an explicit demonstration of the DFT for the total entropy production of a charged Brownian particle in crossed electric and magnetic fields in the non-Markovian (GLE) setting has not yet been reported. The purpose of this work is to prove the validity of such a DFT for two solvable models: (i) a charged Brownian particle confined in a harmonic trap and driven by a prescribed time-dependent electric field, and (ii) an equivalent representation in which the center of the harmonic trap is arbitrarily dragged according to the same driving protocol. We take the magnetic field to be constant and directed along the
z
axis,
{\bf B}=(0,0,B)
, and the electric field as
{\bf E}(t)={\bf E}_{e}(t)+{\bf E}_{i}(t)
, where
{\bf E}_{e}(t)
is the external driving protocol and
{\bf E}_{i}(t)
represents internal fluctuations (thermal noise). In time reversal symmetry, the protocol
{\bm{\lambda}}(t)\equiv{\bf E}_{e}(t)
must satisfy
the property
{\bm{\lambda}}_{R}(t)={\bm{\lambda}}_{F}(\tau-t)
, where the subscript
R
indicates the reversal (backward) process,
F
the corresponding forward process, and
\tau
the time required by the forward protocol to drive the system from an equilibrium state
{\mathcal{A}}
to another one
{\mathcal{B}}
; both characterized by
{\bm{\lambda}}_{F}(0)={\mathcal{A}}
and
{\bm{\lambda}}_{F}(\tau)={\mathcal{B}}
. The backward process from
B
to
A
also takes place at time
\tau
, so that throughout the entire process, there is a time reversal symmetry operation for which
{\mathcal{T}}({\bf r},{\bf v},t;{\bf B})\equiv({\bf r},-{\bf v},-t;-{\bf B})
. This means that in the reverse process along a single stochastic trajectory, not only are time and velocity reversed, but also the magnetic field. In a relatively recent paper
Coretti2020
, it has been shown that under time-reversal symmetry and in the presence of static electric and magnetic fields, the transient fluctuation relation, without invoking the inversion of the magnetic field, holds
Coretti2020
. In our case,
to prove the total entropy production fluctuation theorem, we follow a similar strategy reported in
Jimenez2010
for Markovian systems, in which the Gaussian probability density for the position must be required. However,
in the present contribution, such a probability density is calculated from the phase-space probability density (PSPD) which in turn is obtained by means of the explicit solution of the GLE.
It will be shown that, starting from equilibrium canonical and Maxwellian initial distributions, the PSPD at time
t\geq 0
remains Gaussian and factorizes into independent contributions. Moreover, in the absence of the time-dependent driving, the PSPD relaxes to a stationary distribution of the same form as the initial equilibrium one. This explicit time-dependent PSPD is the central ingredient that allows us to establish the DFT for the total entropy production in closed form.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec. II we solve the GLE explicitly and obtain the PSPD . In Sec. III we use this result to demonstrate the DFT for the total entropy production in two solvable models. Conclusions are presented in Sec. IV. Two appendices provide additional derivations and explicit calculations.

\widetilde{\bf R}=(X,Y)
and
\widetilde{\bf S}=(V_{x},V_{y})

xy
plane; the corresponding formulas along the

It is shown explicitly in Appendix B1 that, for equilibrium initial conditions, the PSPD

where
\widetilde{\bf R}=(X,Y)=(x-\langle x\rangle,y-\langle y\rangle)

\widetilde{\bf S}=(V_{x},V_{y})=(v_{x}-\langle v_{x}\rangle,v_{y}-\langle v_{y}\rangle)

is then

P(\tilde{\bf r},t)={k\over 2\pi k_{B}T}\,\exp\left(-{k|\widetilde{\bf R}|^{2}\over 2k_{B}T}\right).

(119)

III
DFT for the total entropy production

We begin by defining the entropy production in the
xy
plane. Within stochastic thermodynamics

Seifert2005
,
Ohkuma2007
,
Sekimoto2010
, a first-law-like balance between the applied work
W
, the
change in internal energy
\Delta U
, and the heat
Q
dissipated into the bath can be written along a
single stochastic trajectory
\tilde{\bf r}(t)
over a finite time interval
\tau
. This balance reads

Q=W-\Delta U

(120)

where the work
W\equiv W(\tau)
is defined as

W=\int_{0}^{\tau}{\partial{\mathcal{V}}(\tilde{\bf r},\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}(t))\over\partial t}\,dt,

(121)

with
{\mathcal{V}}(\tilde{\bf r},\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}(t))
an effective external potential energy and
\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}(t)
an externally controlled parameter varied according to a prescribed
time-dependent protocol, identified here with the driving electric force field. In this work, we will
consider two types of time-dependent protocols.

The total entropy production
\Delta s_{tot}
is defined as the sum of the change in the medium entropy,

\Delta s_{m}=Q/T
, over the time interval
\tau
, and the change in the system entropy. The nonequilibrium
(Gibbs–Shannon) entropy of the system is defined as
Seifert2005

S(t)=-\int P(\tilde{\bf r},t)\ln P(\tilde{\bf r},t)\,d\tilde{\bf r}=\langle s(t)\rangle.

(122)

This naturally suggests the trajectory-dependent entropy of the particle,

s(t)=-\ln P(\tilde{\bf r},t).

(123)

Here
P(\tilde{\bf r},t)
denotes the marginal probability density of the particle position (in the
xy

plane), evaluated along a stochastic trajectory
\tilde{\bf r}(t)
. For a given trajectory,
s(t)
depends on
the ensemble through the densities
P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})=P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0},0)
and
P(\tilde{\bf r},\tau)
.
Accordingly, the change in system entropy along a single realization over time
\tau
is

\Delta s=-\ln\left[P(\tilde{\bf r},\tau)\over P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})\right].

(124)

The total entropy production along the same trajectory is then
Seifert2005

\Delta s_{tot}=\Delta s_{m}+\Delta s.

(125)

Using this definition, Seifert derived the integral fluctuation theorem (IFT)
\langle e^{-\Delta s_{tot}}\rangle=1
,
where the angular brackets denote an average over realizations (equivalently, over the ensemble of finite-time
trajectories). He also showed that, in a nonequilibrium steady state over a finite time interval, the detailed
fluctuation theorem holds. Combining Eqs. (
120
)–(
124
), we may write

\Delta s_{tot}={W-\Delta U\over T}-\ln\left[{P(\tilde{\bf r},\tau)\over P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})}\right].

(126)

This expression depends explicitly on both the marginal probability density
P(\tilde{\bf r},\tau)
and the initial distribution
P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})
, which we compute in Appendix B.

In the absence of a time-dependent external force field, the stochastic process (
1
) relaxes to a stationary state in the long-time limit
Jimenez2015
. We choose the origin such that
\langle\tilde{\bf r}_{0}\rangle=0
, so that
\tilde{\bf r}_{0}=\widetilde{\bf R}_{0}=(X_{0},Y_{0})
. If the system is initially prepared in thermal equilibrium, then
P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})
is the canonical distribution,

P(\tilde{\bf r}_{0})={k\over 2\pi k_{B}T}\,\exp\left(-{k|\tilde{\bf r}_{0}|^{2}\over 2k_{B}T}\right).

(127)

Moreover, as shown explicitly in Appendix B1, the marginal probability density at time
\tau
can be written in the same canonical form for the fluctuation variables, namely

P(\tilde{\bf r},\tau)={k\over 2\pi k_{B}T}\,\exp\left(-{k|\widetilde{\bf R}(\tau)|^{2}\over 2k_{B}T}\right).

(128)

IV
Concluding Remarks

In this work we established, within a fully non-Markovian framework, the detailed fluctuation theorem (DFT) for the total entropy production of a charged Brownian particle in a harmonic trap driven by a time-dependent electric field and subjected to a constant transverse magnetic field. Our approach is based directly on the generalized Langevin equation and exploits the fact that the dynamics is linear and the noise is Gaussian, so that the phase-space probability density remains Gaussian for all times. For equilibrium initial conditions—specifically, an initial distribution given by the product of a canonical distribution in position and a Maxwellian in velocity—we obtained the explicit marginal density
P(\tilde{\bf r},t)
needed to define the trajectory-dependent system entropy, and showed that it retains a canonical form when written in terms of the fluctuation variables about the mean trajectory. This structure is similar in spirit, though not identical in detail, to the Markovian result reported in Ref.
Jimenez2010
.

We considered two solvable driving protocols: (i) a harmonically trapped particle under a time-dependent force field, and (ii) the dragging of the trap center with an arbitrary time dependence. In both cases, the total entropy production is a Gaussian random variable and satisfies the relation between variance and mean that implies the DFT, and consequently the corresponding integral fluctuation theorem. An interesting direction for future work is to confront these predictions with experiments in which non-Markovian effects are relevant (e.g. viscoelastic environments) and where magnetic fields play an essential role; to our knowledge, systematic experimental tests of fluctuation theorems in such magnetically driven non-Markovian settings are still scarce.

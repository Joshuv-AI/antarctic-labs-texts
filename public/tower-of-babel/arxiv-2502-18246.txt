Reaction-diffusion dynamics of the weakly dissipative Fermi gas
Hannah Lehr, Igor Lesanovsky, Gabriele Perfetto

Abstract
We study the one-dimensional Fermi gas subject to dissipative reactions. The dynamics is governed by the quantum master equation, where the Hamiltonian describes coherent motion of the particles, while dissipation accounts for irreversible reactions. For lattice one-dimensional fermionic systems, emergent critical behavior has been found in the dynamics in the reaction-limited regime of weak dissipation. Here, we address the question whether such features are present also in a gas in continuum space. We do this in the weakly dissipative regime by applying the time-dependent generalized Gibbs ensemble method. We show that for two body 2 ​ A → ∅ 2A\to\emptyset and three 3 ​ A → ∅ 3A\to\emptyset body annihilation, as well as for coagulation A + A → A A+A\to A , the density features an asymptotic algebraic decay in time akin to the lattice problem. In all the cases, we find that upon increasing the temperature of the initial state the density decay accelerates, but the asymptotic algebraic decay exponents are not affected. We eventually consider the competition between branching A → A + A A\to A+A and the decay processes A → ∅ A\to\emptyset and 2 ​ A → ∅ 2A\to\emptyset . We find a second-order absorbing-state phase transition in the mean-field directed percolation universality class. This analysis shows that emergent behavior observed in lattice quantum reaction-diffusion systems is present also in continuum space, where it may be probed using ultra-cold atomic physics.



Keywords
: nonequilibrium many-body dissipative systems, Fermi gas, quantum reaction-diffusion systems, nonequilibrium critical dynamics, time-dependent generalized Gibbs ensemble.

We do this by studying the Fermi gas in one spatial dimension in the thermodynamic limit and in the reaction-limited regime of weak dissipation. First, we show how the quantum master equation in continuum space can be obtained by taking the limit of vanishing lattice spacing in the associated lattice formulation.
Once the continuum limit of the jump operators embodying the reaction processes is established, we apply the TGGE method to study the dynamics.

We start by benchmarking the continuum gas formulation by studying binary annihilation
2A\to\emptyset

n(t)\sim t^{-1}

T

A\to 2A

A\to\emptyset

(\Gamma_{\beta}/\Gamma_{\delta})_{c}

A crucial difference from the latter is, however, present in the spatial correlations in the stationary state. We, indeed, find that correlations beyond mean field are generically present even in the absence of binary annihilation.

Model and continuum limit

We define in this Section the fermionic quantum RD models. In Subsec.
2.1

2.1
Lattice model

The dynamics is modeled via a phenomenological quantum master equation, which we choose to be in the Markovian form (
\hbar=1
henceforth):
[
43
,
44
]

\dot{\rho}(t)=-i[H,\rho(t)]+\mathcal{D}[\rho(t)].

(1)

Here,
H
denotes the Hamiltonian, while
\mathcal{D}
embodies dissipative processes due to the coupling with the surrounding environment. In the lattice case,
H
is a quadratic tight-binding hopping Hamiltonian

H=-\overline{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(c_{aj}^{\dagger}c_{aj+a}+c_{aj+a}^{\dagger}c_{a})\,.

(2)

with
\overline{\Omega}
the hopping amplitude which has the units of inverse time. Here,
L=Na
is the length of the chain, with
N
the number of sites and
a
the lattice spacing. We adopt periodic boundary conditions
c_{aj+L}=c_{aj}
and we have the standard fermionic anticommutation relations
\{c_{aj},c_{aj^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
. We explicitly report the lattice spacing
a
length as it will be useful to take the continuum limit. Throughout, we consider only the case where both the Hamiltonian (
2
) and the dissipator
\mathcal{D}
in Eq. (
1
), and therefore the whole dynamics, are translational invariant.

The dissipator
\mathcal{D}
on the lattice takes the Lindblad form

\mathcal{D}[\rho]=\sum_{j,\nu}\left[L_{aj}^{\nu}\rho{L_{aj}^{\nu}}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{{L_{aj}^{\nu}}^{\dagger}L_{aj}^{\nu},\rho\right\}\right].

(3)

The index
\nu
distinguishes the different dissipative processes present in the dynamics. The first and simplest of such process is one-body decay,
\nu=\delta
rate
\Gamma_{\delta}
[units:
\mbox{time}^{-1}

(4)

L_{aj}^{2\alpha}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{2\alpha}}\,c_{aj}c_{aj+a}.

(5)

This process implements a two-body loss in cold atoms

34

The next nonlinear decay process that will be considered is coagulation,

L_{aj}^{\gamma\pm}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\gamma}/2}\,:c_{aj}n_{aj\pm a}:=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\gamma}/2}c^{\dagger}_{aj\pm a}c_{aj\pm a}c_{aj}.

Importantly, in the previous equation we have introduced the normal ordered product

For fermions one must also multiply the operator by the sign of the permutation needed to produce the desired order. On the lattice normal ordering of the jump operators is not needed, but it will be required when taking the continuum limit

The latter always anticommute (even when taken on the same lattice/space point). We also note that we are considering the case of symmetric coagulation, where particles can either coagulate to the left,

L_{aj}^{\beta\pm}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\beta}/2}\,:c_{aj}^{\dagger}n_{aj\pm a}:=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\beta}/2}\,c_{aj}^{\dagger}c_{aj\pm a}^{\dagger}c_{aj\pm a}.

(9)

Normal ordering is again required when taking the continuum limit. We consider symmetric branching, i.e., it takes place at the same rate in the right and left direction, as in the case of coagulation (

2.2
Continuum model

In taking the continuum limit, we send the lattice spacing
a\to 0
. Simultaneously we take the number of sites
N\to\infty
so that the volume
L=Na
stays finite. We replace discrete sum
\sum_{j}\to a^{-1}\int\mbox{d}x

\frac{c_{aj}}{a^{1/2}}\to\psi(x),\quad\frac{c^{\dagger}_{aj}}{a^{1/2}}\to\psi^{\dagger}(x),

so that they obey the fermionic anticommutation relations
\{\psi(x),\psi^{\dagger}(y)\}=\delta(x-y)

H=\int_{0}^{L}\mbox{d}x\psi^{\dagger}(x)(-\Omega\partial_{x}^{2})\psi(x),

(11)

with
\Omega=a^{2}\overline{\Omega}
. In continuum space
\Omega
has therefore units of [
\mbox{length}^{2}\,\,\mbox{time}^{-1}
]. These are the units of a diffusion constant. It is, however, important to stress that in quantum mechanics the Hamiltonian (

L

Next, the continuum limit of the dissipator
\mathcal{D}
in Eq. (
3

\mathcal{D}[\rho]=\sum_{\nu}\int_{0}^{L}\mbox{d}x[L^{\nu}(x)\rho{L^{\nu}}^{\dagger}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\{{L^{\nu}}^{\dagger}(x)L^{\nu}(x),\rho\}].

(12)

Jump operators in the continuum are obtained from the lattice ones via

L^{\nu}(x)=\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{L^{\nu}_{aj}}{a^{1/2}},

where we express the lattice jump operators
L_{aj}^{\nu}
in terms of the continuous fields (
10
) and we expand by taking the leading term as

. This procedure, in general, also redefines the units of the associated rate (similarly to the case of the hopping amplitude
\overline{\Omega}\to\Omega

For the single body decay (
4

L^{\alpha}(\vec{x})=\sqrt{\Gamma_{\delta}}\psi(x),

(14)

with the rate

coinciding with that of the lattice problem. Moving to the case of binary annihilation, one readily realizes that albeit (
5

\theta\neq 0,\pi

1

L^{2\alpha}(x)=\sqrt{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}}\psi(x)\partial_{x}\psi(x),\quad\mbox{with}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}=a^{3}\Gamma_{2\alpha}(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)^{2}.

Here

has therefore units of [
\mbox{length}^{3}\,\,\mbox{time}^{-1}
]. We remark that on the lattice (

), where normal ordering has to be taken into account. For coagulation we find

\displaystyle L^{\gamma\pm}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}L_{aj}^{\gamma}=\lim_{a\to 0}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{\gamma}}{a}}c_{aj\pm a}^{\dagger}c_{aj\pm a}c_{aj}

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0}\sqrt{a^{2}\Gamma_{\gamma}}\psi^{\dagger}(x\pm a)\psi(x\pm a)\psi(x)

Reaction-limited dynamics and TGGE

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\nu}n^{z-2}/\Omega\ll 1
. Reactions occur on a much longer time-scale
\sim(n^{z}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\nu})^{-1}
than that of coherent motion
\sim n^{-2}\Omega^{-1}

\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)
of the GGE accounts for the breaking of the conservation laws due to the reactions.

In the following we consider the RD dynamics of Sec.

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\nu}n^{z-2}/\Omega\ll 1

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\nu}=a^{z}\Gamma_{\nu}
,

\nu

[

,

,
52
,

]
, which we sketch in Fig.

\rho_{\mathrm{SS}}(t)

[H,\rho_{\mathrm{SS}}(t)]=0

in the system:
\rho_{\mathrm{SS}}(t)\equiv\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)

\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}=\frac{e^{-\sum_{m},\beta_{m}(t)Q_{m}}}{\mbox{Tr}[e^{-\sum_{m}\beta_{m}(t)Q_{m}}]},

(19)

with
\{\beta_{m}(t)\}
the set of Lagrange multipliers in one-to-one correspondence with the expectation values of the conserved charges in the initial state. This state is named generalized Gibbs ensemble
[
56
,
57
]
. In the presence of dissipation it acquires a slow time dependence through the multipliers
\{\beta_{m}(t)\}
, hence the name TGGE. In formulas, the TGGE assumption then reads

\displaystyle\lim_{\widetilde{\Gamma}\to 0}\rho(t=\tau/\widetilde{\Gamma})=\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}(\tau).

(20)

We emphasize that Eq. (
20
) has to be understood in the local sense, i.e., it applies to local observables
\mathcal{O}
in an infinite system. From the latter formula it is also clear that the TGGE describes the slow dynamics on the timescale
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{-1}
, with
t\to\infty
,
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to 0
, and
\tau=\widetilde{\Gamma}t
held fixed.

In the specific case of quantum RD systems, the Hamiltonian in (
11
) is quadratic and it can diagonalized by Fourier transform. This allows to identify the conserved charges and the GGE state in a simple way. Our conventions for the Fourier transform are here reported as they will be needed later in Sec.
4
:

\displaystyle\phi(k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\int_{0}^{L}\mbox{d}x\psi(x)e^{-ikx},

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda^{*}}\phi(k)e^{ikx}.

(21)

In a gas in a finite volume and periodic boundary conditions the set
\Lambda^{*}
of allowed momenta is
k_{n}=2\pi n/L
, with
n=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\mathbb{Z}
an arbitrary integer number. As the volume
L\to\infty
, momenta becomes dense on the real line
\Lambda^{\star}\equiv\mathbb{R}
and one has

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mbox{d}x\ \psi(x)e^{-ikx},\quad\psi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mbox{d}k\ \tilde{\phi}(k)e^{ikx},

(22)

with
\tilde{\phi}^{(\dagger)}(k):=\sqrt{\frac{L}{2\pi}}\phi^{(\dagger)}(k)
. We therefore see that
\tilde{\phi}(k)
has units of
[\mbox{length}^{1/2}]
. From the Fourier mode operators (
21
) and (
22
) one can then define a “scattering basis”
[
56
,
57
,
73
]
for the conserved charges
Q_{m}
in terms of the Fourier occupation number operator
n_{k}=\phi^{\dagger}(k)\phi(k)
. The latter is not an extensive conserved charge, but it allows to express the extensive charges
\{Q_{m}\}
as a linear combination of
\{n_{k}\}
as

Q_{m}=\sum_{k\in\Lambda^{\star}}f_{m}(k)n_{k}.

(23)

The function
f_{m}(k)
is arbitrary, an example is
f_{m}(k)=k^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, which gives an hermitean set of local conserved charges. From Eq. (
23
) one can then write the TGGE (
19
) as

\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)=\frac{e^{-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(t)n_{k}}}{\mbox{Tr}[e^{-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(t)n_{k}}]},

(24)

with
\lambda_{k}(t)=\sum_{m}\beta_{m}(t)f_{m}(k)
. We note that for the calculations is neither necessary to pick a specific basis of charges
\{f_{m}(k)\}
nor to decompose
\lambda_{k}
in terms of the
\{\beta_{m}\}
. As a matter a matter of fact
\lambda_{k}
fixes uniquely the GGE state and it is in one-to-one correspondence with the occupation function
C_{k}=\braket{\phi_{k}^{\dagger}\phi_{k}}
in momentum space as

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}_{k}\phi_{p}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)=Tr\left\{\phi^{\dagger}_{k}\phi_{p}\rho_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)\right\}=\frac{\delta_{k,p}}{1+e^{\lambda_{k}(t)}}=C_{k}\delta_{k,p}.

(25)

In the limit
L\to\infty
, from (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}_{k}\phi_{p}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)=:C_{k}(t)\delta_{k,p}\xrightarrow{L\to\infty}\left\langle\tilde{\phi}^{\dagger}_{k}\tilde{\phi}_{p}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)=C_{k}(t)\delta(k-p).

(26)

We note that for the GGE state (
24
) to be physically meaningful, the function
\lambda_{k}
must grow sufficiently fast as
|k|\to\infty
. In particular, we note that states with a flat occupation in momentum space
\lambda_{k}\equiv\lambda
, independent on
k
, which are possible on the lattice
[
65
,
66
,
58
]
, do not correspond to a physically meaningful GGE (all charges expectation values would be divergent). To completely fix the dynamics of the TGGE state (
24
), one therefore needs an equation for the two-point function
C_{q}
. All the higher point functions are obtained from
C_{q}
by applying Wick’s theorem since the GGE state (
24
) is gaussian for free fermions. The equation for
C_{q}
is simply obtained from the Lindblad master equation using that
[H,n_{q}]=0
. In the lattice case, one has
[
63
,
61
,
68
,
64
,
65
,
66
,
58
]

\frac{dC_{q}(t)}{dt}=\sum_{j,\nu}\left\langle L_{j}^{\nu\dagger}\left[n_{q},L_{j}^{\nu}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t).

(27)

Taking the continuum limit, one obtains

\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=\sum_{\nu}\int_{0}^{L}dx\left\langle L^{\nu\dagger}(x)[n(q),L^{\nu}(x)]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t),

(28)

with the continuum limit of the jump operators
L^{\nu}(x)
defined in Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
). Here, the average
\braket{\dots}_{\mathrm{GGE}}
is taken over the GGE state (
24
). Applying Wick’s theorem, Eq. (
28
) then yields a closed differential equation for
C_{q}
. The mean particle density
n(t)
is obtained from the latter via summation, respectively integration as
L\to\infty
, over all modes

\displaystyle n(t)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{q\in\Lambda^{\star}}C_{q}(t)\xrightarrow{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda^{*}}dq\ C_{q}(t),

(29)

In order to numerically quantify the exponent of the power law
n(\tau)\sim t^{\delta}
, it is useful to introduce the effective exponent
\delta(\tau)
as

\displaystyle\delta(\tau):=-\frac{\ln(n(\tau)/n(b\tau))}{\ln b},

(30)

where
b
is a scaling parameter. If one has an asymptotic power law for the density
n(\tau)\sim\tau^{\delta}
, then in the limit of
\tau\to\infty
,
\delta(\tau)
converges to the time-independent exponent
\delta
independently of the chosen value for
b
. In the next Section, we specialize Eq. (
28
) to the various dissipative reaction processes introduced in Subsec.
2.2
for the Fermi gas.

2A\to\emptyset

4.1
Binary annihilation

We insert the expression of
L^{2\alpha}(x)
in Eq. (
28
) and use the Fourier transform to obtain (
21
)

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}dx\left\langle L^{2\alpha\dagger}(x)[n(q),L^{2\alpha}(x)]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha}}{L^{2}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}p,p^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{L}dx\left\langle e^{-i(k+k^{\prime})x}\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})(-ik^{\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k)\left[n(q),e^{i(p+p^{\prime})x}\phi(p)(ip^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha}}{L}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}p,p^{\prime}}\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})(-ik^{\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k)\left[n(q),\phi(p)(ip^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime})\right]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dxe^{-i(k+k^{\prime})x}e^{i(p+p^{\prime})x}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\alpha}}{L}\sum_{k\in\Lambda^{\star}}(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}.

(31)

From the third to the fourth line we used the commutation relation

[n(q),\phi(p),\phi(p^{\prime})]=-\phi(p)\phi(p^{\prime})(\delta_{p,q}+\delta_{p^{\prime},q})
and the Fourier representation of the Kronecker delta function

\delta_{k+k^{\prime},p+p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dxe^{-i(k+k^{\prime})x}e^{i(p+p^{\prime})x}.

(32)

In addition, Wick’s theorem is applied in decomposing the four-point fermionic correlation function

\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k)\phi(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}=C_{k}C_{k^{\prime}}(\delta_{k,p}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}}-\delta_{k^{\prime},p}\delta_{k,p^{\prime}}).

(33)

Taking now the limit
L\rightarrow\infty
and introducing the rescaled time
\tau:=t\tilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}/(2\pi)
one has

\frac{dC_{q}(\tau)}{d\tau}=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk(q-k)^{2}C_{k}(\tau)C_{q}(\tau).

(34)

Two important observations are here in order. First, in taking the infinite volume limit
L\to\infty
in passing from Eq. (
31
) to (
34
), we have extended the set
\Lambda^{\star}
of allowed momenta to the whole real line
k\in\mathbb{R}
. This is possible since for binary annihilation there are no UV-divergences here, i.e., all the integrals in Eq. (
34
) of
C_{q}
are convergent at large
q
values [assuming that the momentum occupation function vanishes for high momenta]. This is a consequence of the fact that the four point function (
33
) is normal ordered. Second, the same equation (
28
) describes both the continuum limit of Eq. (
5
) and (
6
). This implies that physically different decay processes on the lattice yield the same decay when the continuum limit is taken. Equation (
34
) has been first derived in Ref.
[
68
]
by taking the continuum limit
a\to 0
of the TGGE equation obtained for the lattice process (
6
) at
\theta=\pi/4
. In Ref.
[
69
]
, Eq. (
34
) has been, instead, derived and extended to generic dimensions
d
using the Keldysh action associated to the continuum limit of (
5
). The present derivation thereby shows in a unifying way from the TGGE how the same behavior arises both for (
5
) and (
6
) in the continuum limit.

]

and

), hence

for the density

of the Fermi gas at long times

. In the present work, Eq. (

as the limiting case of Eq. (

. The decay amplitude is therefore bounded between the curves (

4.2
Three-body annihilation

The calculation for three-body annihilation is quite similar to that for the binary case. It amounts to inserting the Fourier transform (
21
) of
L^{3\alpha}
(
16
) into Eq. (
28
). For
3A\to\emptyset
one then needs to evaluate normal-ordered six-point fermionic correlation functions. This is achieved by Wick’s theorem with similar, but longer, calculations as in Eq. (
33
). We do not report the intermediate steps for the sake of brevity, while we write the final kinetic equation for the two-point function
C_{q}
:

\frac{\mbox{d}C_{q}(\tau)}{\mbox{d}t}=-\frac{1}{18}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,C_{q}C_{p}C_{k}\,\left[(k-q)^{3}-(k-p)^{3}+(q-p)^{3}\right]^{2},

(50)

with the rescaled time
\tau=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{3\alpha}/(2\pi)^{2}
and the limit
L\to\infty
already taken. As in the case of binary annihilation, we can safely extend both the
k
and
p
integrals of (
50
) to the whole real line. This again originates from the fact that the jump operator (
16
) is already normal ordered. The corresponding six-point fermionic correlation is consequently also normal ordered and no UV-divergences appear. Equation (
50
) coincides with the
a\to 0
continuum limit of the TGGE equation derived in Ref.
[
66
]
for the lattice formulation (
7
) of the three-body annihilation process. We discuss in the following the case of three-body losses with a thermal initial occupation function
C_{q}(0)
as in Eq. (
35
).

4.3
Coagulation

Analogously to the previous discussed reactions, we insert the Fourier transform of Eq. (
17
) into the TGGE rate equation (
28
). The ensuing fermionic six-point functions (in this case it is not normal ordered since
L^{\gamma\pm\dagger}(x)L^{\gamma\pm}(x)
is not) is evaluated by Wick’s theorem, as detailed in
B
. This leads to the following equation of motion for the occupation function
C_{q}

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=-\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\left[\sum_{k,p}(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,p}(k-p)^{2}C_{k}C_{p}\right].

(51)

The first term on the right hand side contains a free sum
\sum_{p}
over all momenta. In this sum, one is not allowed to extend the upper summation limits to infinity, as we did in the previous cases of
k-
body annihilation. We therefore introduce a UV cutoff
|k|\leq k_{M}
, with
k_{M}=\pi/a
. In the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
this implies constraining the integral upper/lower limits to a finite value

\frac{1}{L}\sum_{p}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dp.

(52)

Taking the thermodynamic limit
L\to\infty
, with the regularized sum (
52
) and introducing the rescaled time
\tau=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}/(2\pi)
, one has:

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{a}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}dk(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}dp(k-p)^{2}C_{k}C_{p}\right].

(53)

For thermal initial states of the form (
35
), the symmetry
C_{k}(0)=C_{-k}(0)
is preserved at all times
C_{k}(\tau)=C_{-k}(\tau)
and one can further simplify Eq. (
53
) as

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{q}}{a}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkk^{2}C_{k}-\frac{C_{q}q^{2}}{a}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkC_{k}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkdp\ C_{p}C_{k}k^{2}

\displaystyle=(n-C_{q}/a)\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkk^{2}C_{k}-2\pi C_{q}q^{2}n/a.

(54)

Albeit all the remaining integrals in Eqs. (
53
) and (
4.3
) can be extended to the whole real line, since
C_{q}
decays rapidly enough at large
q
, the resulting equation still explicitly depends on the lattice spacing
a
. This is a crucial difference compared to the case of
K
body annihilation discussed before and it impacts on the amplitude of the density decay, as shown in Fig.

4.4
Contact process and absorbing-state phase transition

We study here the competition among branching, decay and binary annihilation. This leads to an absorbing-state phase transition, which we study in this Subsection. The TGGE rate equation for branching is obtained in a similar way as for the case of coagulation of Subsec.
4.3
. We therefore do not report the intermediate steps for the sake of brevity, while the final expression reads as

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{4}\Gamma_{\beta}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,k}[C_{k}C_{p}(-p^{2}+pk)+C_{k}C_{q}(k^{2}-q^{2})+C_{k}(p^{2}+q^{2})-C_{q}k^{2}].

(56)

In the continuum limit we again need to introduce a finite UV-cutoff
|k|<\pi/a
, as in Eq. (
52
), in order to regularize the sums over the quasi-momentum
p
in (
56
), which would otherwise be divergent. With the definition of the regularization factor

\displaystyle M_{a}:=\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dk\ k^{2}=\frac{2\pi^{3}}{3a^{3}},

(57)

we then have in the limit
L\to\infty

\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}\left[(C_{q}-an)\left(\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dk\ C_{k}k^{2}-M_{a}\right)+2\pi q^{2}n(1-C_{q})+\frac{a}{2\pi}\left(\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}dk\,kC_{k}\right)^{2}\right]\!.

(58)

It is here important to note that in contrast to coagulation, in Eqs. (
53
) and (
4.3
), for branching the occupation function
C_{q}
does not decay in general to zero for large
q
values. This is clearly caused by the fact that branching creates particles and thereby in the active phase it drives the system towards a stationary state with
n^{\mathrm{stat}}\neq 0
. Consequently in Eq. (
58
) (and in all the subsequent equations of this Subsection) we cannot extend the upper and lower limits of the integral to
+\infty
and
-\infty
, respectively. All the momentum integrals are therefore restricted to the range
|k|<\pi/a
. As in the case of the previous Subsections, we consider here thermal initial states (
35
) such that
C_{k}(0)=C_{-k}(0)
and therefore
C_{k}(t)=C_{-k}(t)
(cf. the discussion before Eqs. (
36
) and (
4.3
)). Consequently the last term of Eq. (
58
) vanishes and we have the TGGE rate equation

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}\left[(C_{q}-an)\left(\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dk\ C_{k}k^{2}-M_{a}\right)+2\pi q^{2}n(1-C_{q})\right].

(59)

In the previous equations, we defined the rescaled rate in the continuum limit
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}=a^{3}\Gamma_{\beta}/(2\pi)
. We use here a different rescaling than that in Eq. (
18
). This choice, indeed, allows to have identical units [
\mbox{length}^{3}\,\,\mbox{time}^{-1}
] for
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}
in (
15
). In this way the two quantities can be directly compared when binary annihilation and branching are simultaneously present (cf. Eq. (
62
) here below). As a matter of fact, we here allow for the competition between branching, two-body annihilation and single-particle decay (
14
). Including the contributions from binary annihilation (derived in Subsec.
4.1
) and single body decay to the rate equation (
59
), one eventually obtains:

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=(C_{q}-an)\left(\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkC_{k}k^{2}-M_{a}\right)+2\pi q^{2}n(1-C_{q})-\frac{\Gamma_{\delta}}{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}C_{q}

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}}{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dk(q-k)^{2}C_{k}(\tau)C_{q}(\tau),

(60)

with the definition of the rescaled time
\tau=\tilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}t
.
Integration over the momenta
q
yields the equation for the density of particles

\displaystyle\frac{dn}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=n\left(M_{a}-\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dq\ q^{2}C_{q}\right)-\frac{\Gamma_{\delta}}{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}\,n-2\,\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}}{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}\,n\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dkC_{k}k^{2}

\displaystyle=n\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}dq\ q^{2}\left[1-\lambda-C_{q}(1+\varepsilon)\right],

(61)

where we defined the adimensional parameters

\displaystyle\lambda:=\frac{\Gamma_{\delta}}{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}M_{a}}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon:=2\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}}{\tilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}.

(62)

Here,
\lambda
quantifies the relative strength between one-body decay
\Gamma_{\delta}
and branching, where the regularization factor
M_{a}
(
57
) ensures that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}M_{a}
has the dimensions of an inverse time. The factor
\varepsilon
, similarly quantifies the relative strength between binary annihilation and branching. As the parameters
\lambda
and
\varepsilon
are tuned, one therefore obtains an absorbing-state phase transition. Namely since
C_{q}\geq 0
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
4.4
)
can be positive, only if
\lambda<1
. Otherwise we have
\dot{n}<0
and thus evolution towards the absorbing state.
This indicates an absorbing state phase transition with a critical point at

\lambda_{c}=\frac{\Gamma_{\delta}}{M_{a}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}}=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{\delta}}{\Gamma_{\beta}}=1

(63)

that is independent on binary annihilation strength
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{2\alpha}
. The critical ratio
\Gamma_{\delta}/\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\beta}
depends on the UV cutoff, which shows that the critical point is a non-universal feature. For
\lambda\geq\lambda_{c}
, the only stationary state is the absorbing one with
n^{\mathrm{stat}}=0
and
C_{q}^{\mathrm{stat}}=0
. For
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
, instead, the active phase is established and one obtains a nonvanishing stationary density
n^{\mathrm{stat}}\neq 0
and occupation function
C_{q}^{\mathrm{stat}}
. The latter can be written in terms of the former by inserting Eq. (
4.4
) into the rate equation (
4.4
) and setting the time derivatives to zero

\displaystyle C_{q}^{\mathrm{stat}}=n^{\mathrm{stat}}\frac{aM_{a}\left(1-\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\varepsilon}\right)+2\pi q^{2}}{M_{a}\left(1+\lambda-\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\varepsilon}(1+\varepsilon/2)\right)+2\pi q^{2}n^{\mathrm{stat}}\left(1+\varepsilon/2\right)}.

(64)

In the absence of binary annihilation (
\varepsilon=0
) this simplifies to

\displaystyle C_{q}^{\mathrm{stat}}

\displaystyle=n^{\mathrm{stat}}\frac{a\lambda M_{a}+2\pi q^{2}}{2\lambda M_{a}+2\pi q^{2}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}.

(65)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{a}}^{\frac{\pi}{a}}C_{q}^{\mathrm{stat}}=\frac{1}{a}-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda M_{a}}{\pi^{3}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}}(1-an^{\mathrm{stat}}/2)\arctan\left[\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{3}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}{\lambda M_{a}}}\frac{1}{a}\right].

(66)

The latter is a trascendental equation for
n^{\mathrm{stat}}
, which cannot be exactly solved. We can, however, expand the right hand side close to the critical point, i.e., for
n\ll 1
:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{3}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}{\lambda M_{a}}}\left(n^{\mathrm{stat}}-\frac{1}{a}\right)

\displaystyle\approx-(1-an^{\mathrm{stat}}/2)\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{3}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}{\lambda M_{a}}}\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}n^{\mathrm{stat}}}{\lambda M_{a}}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right).

(67)

From this result we can immediately read the asymptotic behavior of
n^{\mathrm{stat}}
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{c}

\displaystyle n^{\mathrm{stat}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{2}{a}\left(1-\frac{3\pi^{3}\lambda}{2a^{3}M_{a}}\right)\sim(\lambda_{c}-\lambda)^{1},&\lambda<\lambda_{c}\\
0,&\lambda\geq\lambda_{c}\end{array}\right..

Thus we find that the stationary density
n^{\mathrm{stat}}(\lambda)
follows a power law in
\lambda
as
\lambda\to\lambda_{c}
. The associated exponent is
\beta=1
and it does not depend on the UV cutoff. A comparison with the stationary density obtained from the numerical solution of (
4.4
) shows excellent agreement. From the numerical solution of Eq. (
4.4
) we can also determine the critical exponent associated with the decay of the density when
\lambda=\lambda_{c}
. In this case, we find the power law in time

n(\tau)\sim\tau^{-1},

(70)

which is also independent on the UV cutoff. Both the exponents (
4.4
) and (
70
) coincide with the exponents of classical directed percolation within the mean-field approximation
[
6
,
5
,
7
,
10
,
22
]
. The same universality class has been found for lattice fermionic model in Ref.
[
65
]
and we conclude that the universality class of the absorbing-state phase transition of the Fermi gas is not changed by the continuum limit. In the reaction-limited regime, the critical exponents associated to the phase transition are those of mean-field directed percolation.

5
Conclusions

In this manuscript, we studied the Fermi gas undergoing weak dissipative reactions in one spatial dimension. In particular, we focused on the comparison between the dynamics of the gas in continuum space and that of the lattice counterpart. The main finding of the manuscript is that long-time universal properties characterized by critical exponents coincide in the continuum gas and in the discrete lattice formulation. The latter only impact the nonuniversal properties of the dynamics such as the amplitude of the decay, the value of the critical point in the absorbing-state stationary phase transition and the associated stationary state.

In our study we considered various reaction processes, such as binary (
15
), three-body (
16
) annihilation, coagulation (
17
) and branching (
18
). We first derived the quantum master equation for the gas in continuum space in Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) by taking the limit of vanishing lattice spacing
a\to 0
in the corresponding lattice master equation. In doing so, we highlighted how the continuum limit of coagulation and branching requires normal ordering since the corresponding jump operators are composed of creation and annihilation operators which do not anticommute in the continuum limit. Based on this formulation, we investigated the dynamics in the reaction limited regime of weak dissipation. To this end we adopted the time-dependent generalized Gibbs ensemble method
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
, which leads to a large-scale kinetic equation (
28
) for the occupation function
C_{q}
in momentum space
q
.

We solved this equation for the various dissipative processes considered. In the case of binary annihilation
2A\to\emptyset
we extended previous results in Refs.
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
]
, valid for zero-temperature initial states, to generic values of the temperature
T
of the initial state. This parameter does not impact on the asymptotic decay exponent
n(\tau)\sim\tau^{-1/2}
, but only on the amplitude of the decay, as shown in Eq. (
42
). We discussed the case of three-body annihilation
3A\to\emptyset
in Eq. (
50
). The crucial difference compared to the case
2A\to\emptyset
is that the long-time decay is not algebraic. In the case of coagulation
2A\to A
the TGGE rate equation (
51
) needs to be regularized by inserting an UV cutoff in order to avoid divergences at high momenta in the continuum limit. From the regularized equation (
4.3
), we found that the long-time decay follows the mean field exponent
n(\tau)\sim\tau^{-1}
, as well as in the lattice case. We eventually considered the absorbing-state phase transition induced by the competition between branching
A\to 2A
, one-body decay
A\to\emptyset
and
2A\to\emptyset
. For the branching jump operator, as in the case of coagulation, one needs to include a UV cutoff. From (
4.4
) we found that the associated critical exponents in Eq. (
70
) belong to the mean-field directed percolation universality class. The continuum limit impacts, however, on the stationary state and the associated correlations. The latter are present even for zero annihilation rate, differently from the lattice case, and they occur at all distances.

Beyond this analysis, it is further interesting to look at the case of spinful fermions, as in the Fermi-Hubbard model
[
33
,
34
,
79
,
80
,
81
]
, both in the lattice and in the continuum. Models of spinful fermions with dissipation naturally link to reaction-diffusion processes, such as
A+B\to\emptyset
binary annihilation of the two species upon meeting. The ensuing dynamics for classical RD models has been shown to lead to a rich decay depending on whether the species
A
and
B
initially have equal or unequal densities, and whether they are initially homogeneously distributed or not
[
11
,
19
,
82
,
20
,
83
,
84
,
9
]
. In the quantum case, we similarly expect a rich dynamics, which can be addressed by including spatial inhomogeneities in the initial state into the present analysis. In a related way, it is also interesting to study weakly dissipative spin
1/2
lattice systems. Recently, in Ref.
[
58
]
, these systems have been analyzed via the same TGGE method of the manuscript. In the case of even dissipative processes, which does not change the parity of particle number, such as
2A\to\emptyset
, spin
1/2
systems in
d=1
can be simply mapped onto fermions via Jordan-Wigner transformation. For odd processes, such
3A\to\emptyset
,
2A\to A
and
A\to 2A
considered here, however, nonlocal fermionic strings are introduced by the Jordan-Wigner transformation. It is then natural to wonder whether such terms alter the critical exponents of the dynamics both in the case of coagulation and for the stationary-state phase transitions of the contact process. We plan to address these questions in future studies.

Binary annihilation dynamics

We provide here additional details about the continuum limit of the jump operator (

The continuum limit of Eq. (

\theta\neq\theta^{\star}

\theta^{\star}=-\pi/4+\pi\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}L^{2\alpha}_{ja}(\theta^{\star})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2a}}\sqrt{a^{2}\Gamma_{2\alpha}}\psi(x)\left[\left(\psi(x)+a\partial_{x}\psi(x)+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(x)\right)\right.

(71)

where we used that
|\cos\theta^{\star}-\sin\theta^{\star}|=\sqrt{2}
. From Eq. (

\frac{dC_{q}(\tau)}{d\tau^{\prime}}=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk(q^{2}-k^{2})^{2}C_{k}(\tau^{\prime})C_{q}(\tau^{\prime}),

(72)

with the rescaled time
\tau:=t\dfrac{a^{5}\Gamma_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{2^{3}}\pi}
. We solved numerically Eq. (

\theta\neq\theta^{\star}
:

. In a similar way, we also checked that the temperature

\delta(\tau)

\theta

Regarding the lattice formulation for
\theta=\pi/4
, we also present here the steps missing in the derivation of the asymptotics (
48
) and (
49
). We start by considering the chemical potential
\mu(\beta)
, that is determined by fixing the initial filling
n_{0}

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta\cos q-\beta\mu}}.

(73)

In the limit of high temperature, i.e.,
\beta\rightarrow 0
, the product
\beta\mu
approaches for the lattice system a constant while
\beta\cos q\rightarrow 0
. We can then perform a Taylor expansion of the integrand in
x=\mbox{exp}(-\beta\cos q)
around
x=1
to leading order
x-1
. Integrating term by term the series as in Eq. (
73
), one has

\displaystyle n_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta\cos q-\beta\mu}}=\frac{1}{\left(1+e^{-\beta\mu}\right)^{2}}\left(1+2e^{-\beta\mu}-e^{-\beta\mu}I_{0}(\beta)\right).

(74)

Here,

denotes the modified Bessel function of first kind
[
74

. At leading order in
\beta\to 0
, the latter expands as
I_{0}(\beta\to 0)\approx 1+\beta^{2}/4+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{4})
and we have

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(1+e^{-\beta\mu}\right)^{2}}\left(1+e^{-\beta\mu}-e^{-\beta\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{4})\right).

(75)

The previous equation is readily inverted in order to express
\mbox{exp}(-\beta\mu)
as a function of
n_{0}
(it amounts to solving a second-order algebraic equation in
\mbox{exp}(-\beta\mu)
) and it leads to

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\mu}=\frac{1-n_{0}}{n_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{4}).

(76)

We eventually insert (
76
) in the expressions of
C_{0}
and
C_{\pi}
, Eq. (
47

\beta

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta(\mu+1)}}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{(1-n_{0})}{n_{0}}(1-\beta)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})}\approx\frac{n_{0}}{1-\beta(1-n_{0})},

(77)

\displaystyle C_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta(\mu-1)}}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{(1-n_{0})}{n_{0}}(1+\beta)+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})}\approx\frac{n_{0}}{1+\beta(1-n_{0})}.

(78)

Inserting the latter equation into Eq. (

) one eventually obtains (

Appendix B
Coagulation dynamics

Here we report the calculations leading to Eq. (
51
) of the main text for the coagulation dynamics of the Fermi gas. We start by taking the Fourier transform (
21
) of the coagulation jump operator (
17
)

L^{\gamma\pm}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{a^{4}\Gamma_{\gamma}}{L^{3}}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}(-ip^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})e^{ix(p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime})}.

(79)

From the canonical anticommutation relations
\{\phi(k),\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\}=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}
, we then compute the commutator

\displaystyle[\phi^{\dagger}(q)\phi(q),\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})]=\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})(\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}}),

(80)

which we insert into the TGGE rate Eq. (
28
). We accordingly get for the time derivative of
C_{q}
that

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}dx\left\langle L^{\gamma\pm\dagger}(x)[n(q),L^{\gamma\pm}(x)]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{3}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi(k)[n(q),\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})]\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\int_{0}^{L}dxe^{ix(p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime}-k+k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi(k)\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\cdot(\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\left[\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\delta_{k,p}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(k)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\right](\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}.

(81)

In passing from the second to the third equality we used Eq. (
80
) and the Fourier representation of the Kronecker delta function. In passing from the third to fourth equality we used the anticommutation relation between
\phi(k)
and
\phi^{\dagger}(p)
. In this way we can express the resulting six-point fermionic correlation function, which is not normal ordered for coagulation, as the algebraic sum between a normal-ordered four-point function and a normal-ordered six-point function. We then first evaluate the latter, which yields three-body terms with the product of three occupation functions in momentum space. Importantly we find that all such three-body terms exactly vanish:

\displaystyle\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(p)\phi(k)\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot(\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}\!\!\!\!p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle C_{k^{\prime\prime}}C_{k^{\prime}}C_{p}[\delta_{k,p}(\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime}})-\delta_{k^{\prime},k}(\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{p,p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle+\delta_{k,k^{\prime\prime}}(\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{p,p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}})](\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}\!\!\!\!p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle C_{k^{\prime\prime}}C_{k^{\prime}}C_{p}[\delta_{k,p}(\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime}})-\delta_{k^{\prime},k}(\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{p,p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\delta_{k,k^{\prime\prime}}(\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{p,p^{\prime\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}})]\delta_{q,p}\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle C_{k^{\prime\prime}}C_{k^{\prime}}C_{q}[(\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}})-(\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})+(\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})]=0.

(82)

Here we used Wick’s theorem to decompose the fermionic six-point function in terms product of two-point functions only. In passing from the second to the third equality, we also renamed
k^{\prime}\leftrightarrow p
. As a consequence, in Eq. (
81
), the only contribution is given by the fermionic normal ordered four-point function. The latter is computed in a similar way as in Eq. (
31

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{q}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k,k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\delta_{k,p}(\delta_{p,q}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime}}-\delta_{q,p^{\prime\prime}})\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\delta_{p-p^{\prime}-p^{\prime\prime},k-k^{\prime}-k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{k,p}(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}+\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\left\langle\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime\prime})\phi^{\dagger}(k^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime})\phi(p^{\prime\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GGE}}\delta_{p,q}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\delta_{p^{\prime}+p^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{k,p}(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}+\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\sum_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}}p^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}C_{k^{\prime}}C_{k^{\prime\prime}}(\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}-\delta_{k^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}})\cdot

\displaystyle-\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\gamma}}{L^{2}}\left[\sum_{k,p}(q-k)^{2}C_{k}C_{q}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,p}(k-p)^{2}C_{k}C_{p}\right].

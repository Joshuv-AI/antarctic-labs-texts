1
Introduction

The Ewens-Pitman model was introduced in
Pitman 1995
as a generalization of the celebrated Ewens model in population genetics
(
Ewens 1972
)
. For an integer
n\geq 1
, consider a random partition of
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
partition sets, and, for
r=1,\ldots,n
, denote by
K_{r,n}\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
the number of partitions subsets of size
r
, i.e.,

n=\sum_{r=1}^{n}rK_{r,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988ptK_{n}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}K_{r,n}.

For
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
\theta>-\alpha
, the Ewens-Pitman model assigns to
\mathbf{K}_{n}=(K_{1,n},\ldots,K_{n,n})
the probability

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{K}_{n}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}))=n!\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(s_{n})}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(i-1)}}{i!}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{i}!},

(1)

where
s_{n}=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}
, and, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
(a)^{(n)}
stands for the rising factorial of
a
of order
n
, that is
(a)^{(n)}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
. The Ewens model corresponds to (
1
) with
\alpha=0
. The probability distribution (
1
) admits a sequential or generative construction in terms of Chinese restaurant process
(
Pitman 1995
;
Feng and Hoppe 1998
)
, a Poisson process construction by random sampling from the Pitman-Yor random measure
(
Perman et al. 1992
;
Pitman and Yor 1997
)
, and a construction through compound Poisson (negative-Binomial) random partitions
Dolera and Favaro 2020
;
Dolera and Favaro 2021
. We refer to
Pitman 2006
for an overview of the Ewens-Pitman model, including applications in population genetics, excursion theory, Bayesian nonparametric statistics, combinatorics and statistical physics.

For
\alpha=0
and
\theta>0
there have been several works investigating the large-
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
and the
K_{r,n}
. In particular, by exploiting the fact that
K_{n}
is a sum of
n
independent Bernoulli random variables,
Korwar and Hollander 1973
showed that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}=\theta\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(2)

A central limit theorem for
K_{n}
follows directly from Lindeberg-Lévy theorem, along with a Berry-Esseen inequality. As regard the
K_{r,n}
’s, from
Arratia et al. 1992
it follows that for all
r\geq 1

K_{r,n}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}Z_{r}

(3)

and

(K_{1,n},\,K_{2,n},\ldots)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}(Z_{1},\,Z_{2},\ldots),

(4)

where the
Z_{r}
’s are independent random variables with Poisson
\mathcal{P}(\theta/r)
distribution.
Barbour 1992
established a (quantitative) refinement of (
4
) by providing tight lower and upper bounds for the Wasserstein distance between the laws of the Ewens process
(K_{1,n},\,K_{2,n},\ldots)
and the Poisson process
(Z_{r})
. We refer
Arratia et al. 2003
for a comprehensive overview.

For
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
, results analogous to (
2
)-(
4
) are available in the literature. In particular, by exploiting a martingale construction for
K_{n}
,
Pitman 2006
showed that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(5)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}
is a positive and almost surely finite random variable, typically referred to as
\alpha
-diversity
(
Pitman 2003
)
. As regard the
K_{r,n}
’s, from
Pitman 2006
it follows that for all
r\geq 1

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.},

(6)

where

p_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}.

(7)

We refer to
Bercu and Favaro 2024
for an alternative martingale approach to the limits (
5
) and (
6
), also including convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{p}
, Gaussian fluctuations and laws of iterated logarithm.

Remark 1.1
.

Beyond almost sure and Gaussian fluctuations,
K_{n}
and
K_{r,n}
have been investigated with respect to large deviations and moderate deviations
(
Feng and Hoppe 1998
;
Favaro and Feng 2014
;
Favaro et al. 2018
;
Bercu and Favaro 2025
)
. Some non-asymptotic results for
K_{n}
have been also established in terms of Berry-Esseen inequalities
(
Dolera and Favaro 2020
;
Dolera and Favaro 2021
)
and concentration inequalities
(
Pereira et al. 2022
;
Bercu and Favaro 2025
)
.

1.1
Main result

For
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
, the large-
n
asymptotic behaviour of the Ewens-Pitman process
(K_{r,n},\,K_{2,n},\ldots)
follows from the almost sure convergence (
6
), that is

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{K_{1,n}}{n^{\alpha}},\,\frac{K_{2,n}}{n^{\alpha}},\ldots\right)=(p_{\alpha}(1)S_{\alpha,\theta},\,p_{\alpha}(2)S_{\alpha,\theta},\ldots)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(8)

which extends (
4
). The weights
p_{\alpha}=(p_{\alpha}(1),p_{\alpha}(2),\ldots)
in (
8
) form a probability distribution on
\mathbb{N}
, which is known as Sibuya distribution
(
Devroye 1993
)
, namely
p_{\alpha}(r)\in]0,1[
for all
r\geq 1
and

\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}p_{\alpha}(r)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}=1.

(9)

In contrast to the case
\alpha=0
, for
\alpha\in]0,1[
there exists an interplay between the almost sure convergences (
5
) and (
8
), which underpins the interpretation of
p_{\alpha}
as the large-
n
limiting proportions of partitions subsets
(
Pitman 2006
, Lemma 1.1)
. It motivates us to study the asymptotic behavior of the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process
(P_{1,n},\,P_{2,n},\ldots)
defined, for all
r\geq 1
, by

P_{r,n}=\frac{K_{r,n}}{K_{n}}.

(10)

An almost sure convergence of
(P_{1,n},\,P_{2,n},\ldots)
follows from the almost sure convergences (
5
) and (
8
), that is

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}(P_{1,n},\,P_{2,n},\ldots)=p_{\alpha}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(11)

The next theorem is the main result of the paper, proving a Gaussian process limit for
(P_{1,n},\,P_{2,n},\ldots)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. Then, we have the
\ell^{2}
distributional convergence

\sqrt{K_{n}}((P_{1,n},\,P_{2,n},\ldots)-p_{\alpha})\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}(\Gamma_{\alpha}),

(12)

where
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma_{\alpha})
stands for a centered Gaussian process with infinite-dimensional covariance matrix

\Gamma_{\alpha}=diag(p_{\alpha})-p_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}^{T}.

(13)

It follows from (
11
) that the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process
(P_{1,n},\,P_{2,r},\ldots)
is a strongly consistent estimator of Sibuya distribution
p_{\alpha}
. Theorem
1.2
provides an effective tool to quantify uncertainty of this estimator by constructing a large-
n
asymptotic confidence ball for
p_{\alpha}
.

1.2
Estimation of the parameter
\alpha

A noteworthy special case of Theorem
1.2
is the case
r=1
, which connects to the estimation of the parameter
\alpha\in]0,1[
in the Ewens-Pitman model. Several works have focused on the maximum likelihood estimator of
\alpha
, by relying on the explicit distribution (
1
), and provided theoretical guarantees of the estimator in terms of consistency and Gaussian limits
(
Franssen and van der Vaart 2022
;
Favaro and Naulet 2023
;
Balocchi et al. 2024
)
. However, the maximum likelihood estimator of
\alpha
is not available in closed form and must be obtained numerically via iterative optimization routines, which are computationally demanding and sensitive to initialization. Instead, here, we propose to estimate
\alpha
by

\widehat{\alpha}_{n}=P_{1,n}=\frac{K_{1,n}}{K_{n}}.

The estimator
\widehat{\alpha}_{n}
enjoys both practical and theoretical advantages. First, it is available in closed form, requiring only the computation of
K_{1,n}
and
K_{n}
, in contrast with the maximum likelihood estimator. Second, it is computationally trivial and scales naturally to large sample sizes. Third, its asymptotic theoretical guarantees follows from the Ewens-Pitman model, and do not rely on approximations of the likelihood function. The next corollary follows from Equation (
8
) and Theorem
1.2
, providing theoretical guarantees of
\widehat{\alpha}_{n}
as an estimator of the parameter
\alpha
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. Then, we have the almost sure convergence

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\widehat{\alpha}_{n}=\alpha\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(14)

Moreover, we also have the asymptotic normality

\sqrt{K_{n}}(\widehat{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{N}(0,\alpha(1-\alpha)).

(15)

Corollary
1.3
allows us to provide a large-
n
asymptotic confidence interval for the parameter
\alpha
. More precisely, for any prescribed confidence level
1-\gamma\in]0,1[
, let
z_{1-\gamma/2}
stands for the
(1-\gamma/2)
-quantile of the
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
distribution. Then an asymptotic
(1-\gamma)
-confidence interval for
\alpha
is given by

\left[\widehat{\alpha}_{n}-z_{1-\gamma/2}\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\alpha}_{n}(1-\widehat{\alpha}_{n})}{K_{n}}},\,\widehat{\alpha}_{n}+z_{1-\gamma/2}\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\alpha}_{n}(1-\widehat{\alpha}_{n})}{K_{n}}}\right].

(16)

1.3
Organization of the paper

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, we discuss some directions of future research on the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process. The appendix is dedicated to the sequential construction of the Ewens-Pitman model and to moment formulae for the random variables
K_{n}
and
(K_{n},K_{r,n})
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on a tailor-made martingale construction for the
K_{r,n}
’s, in combination with a non-standard central limit theorem for multi-dimensional martingales
(
Touati 1991
;
Touati 1994
)
, see also
Bercu and Laulin 2021
for details.

2.1
The martingale construction

From the sequential or generative construction of the Ewens-Pitman model
(
Pitman 1995
, Proposition 9)
, we have for all
n\geq 1
,

K_{n+1}=K_{n}+\xi_{n+1},

(17)

where the conditional distribution of
\xi_{n+1}
given the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})
is the Bernouilli
\mathcal{B}(p_{n})
distribution with parameter

p_{n}=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}.

(18)

We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for more details on (
18
). Let
(\mathcal{F}_{r,n})
be the sequence of
\sigma
-algebras given by
\mathcal{F}_{1,n}=\sigma(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n},K_{1,1},\ldots,K_{1,n})
and, for all
r\geq 2
,
\mathcal{F}_{r,n}=\mathcal{F}_{1,n}\cup\mathcal{G}_{2,n}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{G}_{r,n}
where
\mathcal{G}_{r,n}=\sigma(K_{r,1},\ldots,K_{r,n})
. Again, by relying on the sequential or generative construction of the Ewens-Pitman model
(
Bercu and Favaro 2024
)
, we have for all
r\geq 2
and for all
n\geq 1
,

K_{r,n+1}=K_{r,n}+\xi_{r,n+1},

(19)

where
\xi_{r,n+1}
given the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{r,n}
is distributed according to the following probability distribution

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}{p_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&\ \ k=1\\
{q_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&\ \ k=-1\\
{1-p_{r,n}-q_{r,n}}&\text{ if }&\ \ k=0,\end{array}\right.

(20)

with

p_{r,n}=\frac{(r-1-\alpha)K_{r-1,n}}{n+\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988ptq_{r,n}=\frac{(r-\alpha)K_{r,n}}{n+\theta}.

(21)

We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for more details on (
21
). Decomposition (
19
) holds for
r=1
by taking
p_{1,n}=p_{n}
. One can also observe that for all
r\geq 2
,
p_{r,n}=q_{r-1,n}
.
From now on, let
(a_{r,n})
be the sequence defined by
a_{r,n}=1
for all
1\leq n\leq r
and for all

n\geq r+1
,

a_{r,n}=\prod_{k=r}^{n-1}\left(\frac{k+\theta}{k+\alpha+\theta-r}\right)=\frac{(\theta+1)^{(n-1)}}{(\alpha+\theta-r+1)^{(n-1)}}.

(22)

Moreover, denote by
(M_{r,n})
the sequence of random variables given, for all
r\geq 1
, by

M_{r,n}=a_{r,n}\left(S_{r,n}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)

(23)

with

S_{r,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}K_{i,n}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)K_{n},

(24)

where for all
i=1,\ldots,r
,

b_{r,i}=(-1)^{r-i}\frac{(i-\alpha)^{(r-i)}}{(r-i)!}.

(25)

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
r\geq 1
,
(M_{r,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale with predictable quadratic variation
\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}
satisfying

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{n^{2r-\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-r+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(26)

Proof.

It follows from (
17
), (
19
), (
20
) and (
24
) that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[S_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}K_{i,n+1}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)K_{n+1}\,\Bigr|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}\right]\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}(K_{i,n}+\xi_{i,n+1})+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)(K_{n}+\xi_{n+1})\,\Bigr|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}\right]\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=S_{r,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}(p_{i,n}-q_{i,n})+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)p_{n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(27)

Consequently, as
p_{1,n}=p_{n}
and for all
i\geq 2
,
p_{i,n}=q_{i-1,n}
, we obtain from (
2.1
) that

\mathds{E}[S_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=S_{r,n}+((-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)+b_{r,1})p_{n}-q_{r,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(b_{r,i+1}-b_{r,i})q_{i,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(28)

Moreover, we clearly have from (
25
) that

b_{r,i+1}=\left(\frac{r-i}{\alpha-i}\right)b_{r,i}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptb_{r,i+1}-b_{r,i}=\left(\frac{r-\alpha}{\alpha-i}\right)b_{r,i}

Therefore, we deduce from (
18
), (
21
), (
24
), (
28
) together with straighforward calculation that

\mathds{E}[S_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=\gamma_{r,n}S_{r,n}-(1-\gamma_{r,n})\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(29)

where

\gamma_{r,n}=1-\left(\frac{r-\alpha}{n+\theta}\right).

Hereafter, we obtain from (
23
) and (
29
) and the elementary identity
a_{r,n+1}\gamma_{r,n}=a_{r,n}

that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[M_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[a_{r,n+1}\left(S_{r,n+1}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\,\Bigr|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}\right]\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=a_{r,n+1}\left(\mathds{E}[S_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=a_{r,n+1}\left(\gamma_{r,n}S_{r,n}-(1-\gamma_{r,n})\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=a_{r,n+1}\gamma_{r,n}\left(S_{r,n}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=a_{r,n}\left(S_{r,n}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)=M_{r,n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Moreover, the sequence
(M_{r,n})
is square integrable as for all
1\leq i\leq r
,
K_{i,n}\leq K_{n}\leq n
. Consequently,
(M_{r,n})
is a locally square
integrable martingale. Its predictable quadratic variation
\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}
is given by

\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathds{E}[(\Delta M_{r,k+1})^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k}]

(30)

where
\Delta M_{r,n+1}=M_{r,n+1}-M_{r,n}
. It follows from (
22
), (
23
) and (
29
) that

\Delta M_{r,n+1}=a_{r,n+1}\Big(S_{r,n+1}-\mathds{E}[S_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]\Big)=a_{r,n+1}\Big(\zeta_{r,n+1}-\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]\Big)

(31)

where

\zeta_{r,n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}\xi_{i,n+1}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\xi_{n+1}.

Consequently, we immediately obtain from decomposition (
31
) that

\mathds{E}[(\Delta M_{r,n+1})^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=a_{r,n+1}^{2}\Big(\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]-\mathds{E}^{2}[\zeta_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]\Big)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(32)

We already saw from (
2.1
) that

\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}(p_{i,n}-q_{i,n})+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)p_{n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(33)

In addition, we also have
\mathds{E}[\xi_{n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=p_{n}
and
\mathds{E}[\xi_{i,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=p_{i,n}+q_{i,n}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Moreover,
\mathds{E}[\xi_{i,n+1}\xi_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=0
as soon as
|i-j|\geq 2
. Furthermore, one can observe that
\mathds{E}[\xi_{i,n+1}\xi_{i+1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=-q_{i,n}
for
1\leq i\leq r-1
and

\mathds{E}[\xi_{i,n+1}\xi_{i-1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=-q_{i-1,n}
for
2\leq i\leq r
and

\mathds{E}[\xi_{n+1}\xi_{i,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=p_{n}
for
i=1
,
\mathds{E}[\xi_{n+1}\xi_{i,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=0
for
2\leq i\leq r
. Hence,
we have

\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{2}(p_{i,n}+q_{i,n})-2\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}b_{r,i}b_{r,i+1}q_{i,n}-\Bigl(\frac{2r-\alpha}{\alpha}\Bigr)p_{\alpha}^{2}(r)p_{n}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

(34)

We have from the almost sure convergences (
5
) and (
6
) together with (
18
) and (
21
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}p_{n}=\alpha S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(35)

and for all
i\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}q_{i,n}=(i-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(i)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(36)

However, for all
i\geq 2
,
p_{i,n}=q_{i-1,n}
. Hence, we obtain from (
36
) that for all
i\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}(p_{i,n}-q_{i,n})

\displaystyle=((i-1-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(i-1)-(i-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(i))S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=\alpha p_{\alpha}(i)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(37)

Therefore, it follows from (
33
), (
35
) and (
2.1
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]=\alpha\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}p_{\alpha}(i)+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}=0\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(38)

since by the Binomial theorem, the sum in (
38
) reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}p_{\alpha}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-i}\frac{(i-\alpha)^{(r-i)}}{(r-i)!}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(i-1)}}{i!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(r-1)}}{r!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-1)^{r-i}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r).

(39)

By the same token, we deduce from (
34
), (
35
) and (
36
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{2}((2i-\alpha)+2(r-i))p_{\alpha}(i)-(2r-\alpha)p_{\alpha}^{2}(r)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}

\displaystyle=(2r-\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{2}p_{\alpha}(i)-p_{\alpha}^{2}(r)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r+1)}}{r!}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(40)

since the Chu-Vandermonde identity for the rising factorial

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-n)^{(k)}\frac{(b)^{(k)}}{(a)^{(k)}k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(b)^{(k)}}{(a)^{(k)}}=\frac{(a-b)^{(n)}}{(a)^{(n)}}

(41)

which holds for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a\notin\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}
, implies via (
25
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{2}p_{\alpha}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(i-\alpha)^{(r-i)}}{(r-i)!}\right)^{2}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(i-1)}}{i!}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}(r-\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(i-1)}}{\Gamma^{2}(i-\alpha)((r-i)!)^{2}i!}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma^{2}(r-\alpha)}{\Gamma^{2}(-\alpha)(r!)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{(-r)^{(i)}(-r)^{(i)}}{(-\alpha)^{(i)}i!}

\displaystyle=-p_{\alpha}^{2}(r)\left(\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{(-\alpha)^{(r)}}-1\right).

Hereafter, we obtain from standard results on the asymptotic behavior of
the Euler Gamma function together with (
22
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{r,n}}{n^{r-\alpha}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{r-\alpha}}\frac{(\theta+1)^{(n-1)}}{(\alpha+\theta-r+1)^{(n-1)}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-r+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}.

(42)

Therefore, it follows from (
32
), (
38
), (
2.1
) and (
42
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathds{E}[(\Delta M_{r,n+1})^{2}\,|\,(\mathcal{F}_{r,n})]}{n^{2r-\alpha-1}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r+1)}}{r!}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-r+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

which implies through a direct application of Toeplitz lemma that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{n^{2r-\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-r+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

completing the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

2.2
A central limit theorem

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. Then, we have the asymptotic normality

\sqrt{K_{n}}(P_{1,n}-\alpha)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha(1-\alpha)).

(43)

Moreover, for all
r\geq 2
, we also have

\sqrt{K_{n}}\left(P_{r,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}b_{r,i}P_{i,n}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\right).

(44)

Proof.

We just saw in lemma
2.1
that the predictable quadratic variation
\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}
satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{n^{2r-\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-r+1)}{\Gamma(\theta+1)}\right)^{2}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(45)

Moreover, we are going to prove that Lindeberg’s condition is satisfied, that is for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{n^{2r-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{r,k}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n^{2r-\alpha}}\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k-1}\big]\overset{\displaystyle\mathbb{P}}{\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}0.

(46)

We immediately have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{|\Delta M_{r,k}|>\varepsilon\sqrt{n^{2r-\alpha}}\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k-1}\big]\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}n^{2r-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k-1}\big].

Hence, in order to prove (
46
),
it is only necessary to show that

\frac{1}{n^{4r-2\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k-1}\big]\overset{\displaystyle\mathbb{P}}{\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}0.

(47)

Using the same approach as in (
34
), we obtain that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{4}(p_{i,n}+q_{i,n})-4\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}b_{r,i}^{3}b_{r,i+1}q_{i,n}+6\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}b_{r,i}^{2}b^{2}_{r,i+1}q_{i,n}

\displaystyle-4

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}b_{r,i}b_{r,i+1}^{3}q_{i,n}+p_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\binom{4}{j}(-1)^{jr}p_{\alpha}^{j}(r)b_{r,1}^{4-j}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

However, we already saw that
b_{r,r}=1
and for all
1\leq i\leq r-2
,
b_{r,i+1}=-e_{r,i}b_{r,i}
where

e_{r,i}=\left(\frac{r-i}{i-\alpha}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{4}p_{i,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{4}q_{i,n}\Big(1+4e_{r,i}+6e_{r,i}^{2}+4e_{r,i}^{3}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle p_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\binom{4}{j}(-1)^{jr}p_{\alpha}^{j}(r)b_{r,1}^{4-j}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

which leads to

\mathds{E}[\zeta_{r,n+1}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,n}]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{4}p_{i,n}+\left(\frac{r-\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)^{\!\!4}\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}^{4}q_{i,n}+p_{n}p_{\alpha}^{4}(r)\left(\frac{r-\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^{\!\!4}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{a.s.}

(48)

Therefore, we deduce from (
31
) and (
48
) together with
the almost sure convergences (
35
), (
36
) and (
42
) that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\big[\Delta M_{r,k}^{4}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r,k-1}\big]=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{4r-1-3\alpha}\right)=O(n^{4r-3\alpha})\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(49)

which clearly implies (
47
).
Hence, the two conditions of
(
Hall and Heyde 1980
, Corollary 3.1)

are satisfied, which allows us to conclude that for all
r\geq 1
,

\frac{M_{r,n}}{\sqrt{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(50)

Furthermore, we find from (
23
), (
24
) and (
50
) that for all
r\geq 1
,

\frac{a_{r,n}}{\sqrt{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}}\left(S_{r,n}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

\frac{a_{r,n}}{\sqrt{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}K_{i,n}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)K_{n}+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

\frac{a_{r,n}K_{n}}{\sqrt{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}P_{i,n}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)+\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha K_{n}}\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

(51)

However, we clearly have from (
5
), (
42
) and (
45
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\langle M_{r}\rangle_{n}}{a^{2}_{r,n}K_{n}}=p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(52)

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\theta}{\alpha K_{n}}=0\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(53)

Finally, we find from (
51
), (
52
), (
53
) and Slutsky’s lemma that

\sqrt{K_{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{r,i}P_{i,n}+(-1)^{r}p_{\alpha}(r)\right)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{(r-\alpha)^{(r)}}{r!}\right),

(54)

which achieves the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

2.3
The multi-dimensional martingale construction

Consider the
d
-dimensional random vector
\mathcal{M}_{n}
defined as

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1,n}\\
M_{2,n}\\
\vdots\\
M_{d,n}\end{pmatrix}

(55)

where each component
M_{r,n}
has been previously defined in (
23
).
We already in Lemma
2.1
that for all
r\geq 1
,
(M_{r,n})
is a locally square integrable martingale.
It implies that the sequence
(\mathcal{M}_{n})
is a locally square integrable
d
-dimensional martingale.
Let
(V_{n})
be the sequence of deterministic square matrices of order
d
given by

V_{n}=n^{\alpha/2}\text{diag}\left(\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{2}},\ldots,\frac{1}{n^{d}}\right).

(56)

Lemma 2.3
.

The predictable quadratic variation
\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle_{n}
of the martingale
(\mathcal{M}_{n})
satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}V_{n}\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle_{n}V_{n}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\Sigma_{d}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(57)

where
\Sigma_{d}
stands for the square matrix of order
d
whose
(i,j)
-th element is given by

\Sigma_{i,j}=\frac{(-1)^{i+j}(i+j)!}{i!j!}p_{\alpha}(i+j)\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-i+1)\Gamma(\alpha+\theta-j+1)}{\Gamma^{2}(\theta+1)}.

(58)

Proof.

We shall proceed as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
. We deduce from the initial decomposition (
31
) that

\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathds{E}[\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k+1}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k+1}^{T}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,k}]

(59)

where

\Delta\mathcal{M}_{n+1}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta M_{1,n+1}\\
\Delta M_{2,n+1}\\
\vdots\\
\Delta M_{d,n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,n+1}(S_{1,n+1}-\mathds{E}[S_{1,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}])\\
a_{2,n+1}(S_{2,n+1}-\mathds{E}[S_{2,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}])\\
\vdots\\
a_{d,n+1}(S_{d,n+1}-\mathds{E}[S_{d,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}])\\
\end{pmatrix}.

By the same calculation as in (
32
), we have for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\Delta M_{i,n+1}\Delta M_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]

(60)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=a_{i,n+1}a_{j,n+1}\big(\mathds{E}[S_{i,n+1}S_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]-\mathds{E}[S_{i,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]\mathds{E}[S_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=a_{i,n+1}a_{j,n+1}\big(c_{(i,j),n}-b_{(i,j),n}\big)

where
b_{(i,j),n}=\mathds{E}[\zeta_{i,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]\mathds{E}[\zeta_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]
and

c_{(i,j),n}=\mathds{E}[\zeta_{i,n+1}\zeta_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]
.
On the one hand, it follows from (
33
) that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

\displaystyle b_{(i,j),n}=\sum_{t=1}^{i}\sum_{s=1}^{j}b_{i,t}b_{j,s}(p_{t,n}-q_{t,n})(p_{s,n}-q_{s,n})+(-1)^{i+j}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)p_{n}^{2}

(61)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j}p_{\alpha}(j)p_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}(p_{t,n}-q_{t,n})+(-1)^{i}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{n}\sum_{s=1}^{j}b_{j,s}(p_{s,n}-q_{s,n})\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

On the other hand, we deduce from the same calculation as in (
34
) that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
such that
i\leq j
,

\displaystyle c_{(i,j),n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}(p_{t,n}+q_{t,n})-\sum_{t=1}^{i-1}(b_{i,t}b_{j,t+1}+b_{j,t}b_{i,t+1})q_{t,n}-b_{j,i+1}q_{i,n}

(62)

\displaystyle-\Bigl(\frac{i+j-\alpha}{\alpha}\Bigr)(-1)^{i+j}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)p_{n}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

Convergence (
38
) of course ensures that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}b_{(i,j),n}=0\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(63)

Moreover, we obtain from (
35
) and (
36
) that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
such that
i\leq j
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}c_{(i,j),n}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}(2t-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(t)+\sum_{t=1}^{i-1}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}(i+j-2t)p_{\alpha}(t)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}

\displaystyle-b_{j,i+1}(i-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(i)S_{\alpha,\theta}-(i+j-\alpha)(-1)^{i+j}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=(i+j-\alpha)\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}p_{\alpha}(t)-(-1)^{i+j}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}

\displaystyle-\Big(b_{j,i+1}(i-\alpha)+b_{j,i}(j-i)\Big)p_{\alpha}(i)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle=(i+j-\alpha)\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}p_{\alpha}(t)-(-1)^{i+j}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)\Big)S_{\alpha,\theta}

(64)

Furthermore, the sum in (
2.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}p_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{i}\frac{(t-\alpha)^{(i-t)}(t-\alpha)^{(j-t)}(1-\alpha)^{(t-1)}}{t!(i-t)!(j-t)!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\frac{\Gamma(i-\alpha)\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\alpha}{i!j!}\sum_{t=1}^{i}\frac{i!(j-t+1)^{(t)}}{t!(i-t)!\Gamma(t-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\frac{\Gamma(i-\alpha)\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\frac{\alpha}{i!j!}\sum_{t=1}^{i}\frac{(-i)^{(t)}(-j)^{(t)}}{(-\alpha)^{(t)}t!}.

(65)

By applying one again Chu-Vandermonde identity (
41
) to (
2.3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{i}b_{i,t}b_{j,t}p_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\frac{\Gamma(i-\alpha)\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\frac{\alpha}{i!j!}\left(-1+\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i)}}{(-\alpha)^{(i)}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=(-1)^{i+j+1}\frac{\Gamma(i-\alpha)\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\frac{1}{i!j!}\left(-1+\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i)}}{(-\alpha)^{(i)}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=(-1)^{i+j+1}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)\left(\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i)}}{(-\alpha)^{(i)}}-1\right).

(66)

Therefore, we deduce from (
2.3
) and (
2.3
) that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
such that
i\leq j
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{1-\alpha}c_{(i,j),n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+j+1}(i+j-\alpha)\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i)}}{(-\alpha)^{(i)}}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)S_{\alpha,\theta}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i+j}(i+j)!}{i!j!}(i+j-\alpha)p_{\alpha}(i+j)S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

One can observe that (
67
) coincides with (
2.1
) in the special case
i=j=r
.
Finally, it follows from (
42
), (
60
) and (
67
) that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
such that
i\leq j
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathds{E}[\Delta M_{i,n+1}\Delta M_{j,n+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,n}]}{n^{i+j-\alpha-1}}=(i+j-\alpha)\Sigma_{i,j}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

(68)

where the covariance term
\Sigma_{i,j}
has been previously defined in (
58
). Convergence (
68
) leads, via Toeplitz lemma, to the conclusion that for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
such that
i\leq j
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{i+j-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}[\Delta M_{i,k+1}\Delta M_{j,k+1}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{d,k}]=\Sigma_{i,j}S_{\alpha,\theta}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{a.s.}

which completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

2.4
A multi-dimensional central limit theorem

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. Then, we have
the multi-dimensional asymptotic normality

\sqrt{K_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}P_{1,n}-p_{\alpha}(1)\vskip 3.87498pt\\
P_{2,n}+b_{2,1}P_{1,n}+p_{\alpha}(2)\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle{P_{d,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}b_{d,i}P_{i,n}+(-1)^{d}p_{\alpha}(d)}\end{pmatrix}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Lambda_{d})

(69)

where
\Lambda_{d}
denotes the square matrix of order
d
whose
(i,j)
-th element is given by

\Lambda_{i,j}=\frac{(-1)^{i+j}(i+j)!}{i!j!}p_{\alpha}(i+j),

(70)

and
\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Lambda_{d})
stands for a centered
d
-dimensional Gaussian random variable with covariance
\Lambda_{d}
.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
2.4
relies on a non-standard central limit theorem for multi-dimensional martingales given by
(
Touati 1991
, Theorem 1)
. We already saw in Lemma
2.3
that the predictable quadratic variation

\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle_{n}
satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}V_{n}\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle_{n}V_{n}=S_{\alpha,\theta}\Sigma_{d}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

(71)

where the sequence
(V_{n})
is defined in (
56
). It is clear that
||V_{n}||
decreases to
0
as
n
goes to infinity. Hence, it only remains to prove that Lindeberg’s condition is satisfied, that is for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|>\varepsilon\}}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr]\overset{\displaystyle\mathbb{P}}{\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}0.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{I}_{\{\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|>\varepsilon\}}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr]\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{4}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr].

Consequently, it is only necessary to prove that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{4}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr]\overset{\displaystyle\mathbb{P}}{\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}0.

(72)

We have from (
56
) that for all
1\leq k\leq n
,

\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{4}=n^{2\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\Delta M_{i,k}^{2}}{n^{2i}}\right)^{2}\leq dn^{2\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\Delta M_{i,k}^{4}}{n^{4i}},

which implies that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{4}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr]\leq dn^{2\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{n^{4i}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\Delta M_{i,k}^{4}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr].

(73)

Hereafter, we deduce from (
49
) and (
73
) that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bigl[\|V_{n}\Delta\mathcal{M}_{k}\|^{4}\bigl|\mathcal{F}_{d,k-1}\bigr]=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\right)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{a.s.}

which clearly leads to (
72
). Therefore, we obtain from
(
Touati 1991
, Theorem 1)
, see also

(
Touati 1994
)
, that

V_{n}\mathcal{M}_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}}\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Sigma_{d}).

(74)

Hence, we find from (
23
), (
42
) and (
56
) that

n^{-\alpha/2}\begin{pmatrix}S_{1,n}\\
S_{2,n}\\
\vdots\\
S_{d,n}\end{pmatrix}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\theta}}\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Lambda_{d}).

(75)

Finally, the multi-dimensional asymptotic normality (
69
) follows from
the almost sure convergence (
5
) together with (
24
), (
75
)
and Slutsky’s lemma, which achieves the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.5
A direct application of the delta-method

From Lemma
2.4
, a multi-dimensional central limit theorem for the first
d
-components of the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process is as follows

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. Then, we have
the multi-dimensional central limit theorem

\sqrt{K_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}P_{1,n}-p_{\alpha}(1)\vskip 7.74997pt\\
P_{2,n}-p_{\alpha}(2)\\
\vdots\\
P_{d,n}-p_{\alpha}(d)\end{pmatrix}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Gamma_{d})

(76)

where
\Gamma_{d}=J_{d}\Lambda_{d}J_{d}^{T}
and
J_{d}
stands for the square matrix of order
d
such that

J_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i-j)}}{(i-j)!}&\text{ if }&\ \ 1\leq j\leq i\\
0&\text{ if }&\ \ j>i.\end{array}\right.

(77)

and
\mathcal{N}_{d}(0,\Gamma_{d})
stands for a centered
d
-dimensional Gaussian random variable with covariance
\Gamma_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
g
be the function defined from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

g(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d})=\left(x_{1},x_{2}-b_{2,1}x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(-1)^{d-i}b_{d,i}x_{i}\right).

(78)

One can easily check that

g\left(x_{1},x_{2}+b_{2,1}x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}b_{d,i}x_{i}\right)=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}).

Moreover, it follows from (
39
) that

g\Big(p_{\alpha}(1),-p_{\alpha}(2),\cdots,(-1)^{d-1}p_{\alpha}(d)\Big)=(p_{\alpha}(1),p_{\alpha}(2),\cdots,p_{\alpha}(d)).

Since
\nabla g(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d})=J_{d}^{T}
, we immediately obtain (
76
)
from (
69
) through a direct application of the delta-method.
∎

2.6
Determination of the infinite-dimensional covariance matrix

We already saw in Lemma
2.5
that the asymptotic covariance matrix
\Gamma_{d}
is given by

\Gamma_{d}=J_{d}\Lambda_{d}J_{d}^{T}

(79)

where the
(i,j)
-th elements of the matrices
\Lambda_{d}
and
J_{d}
are given by

\Lambda_{i,j}=\frac{(-1)^{i+j}(i+j)!}{i!j!}p_{\alpha}(i+j)

and

J_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{(j-\alpha)^{(i-j)}}{(i-j)!}&\text{ if }&\ \ 1\leq j\leq i\\

Denote by
\Gamma_{\alpha}
the infinite-dimensional covariance matrix whose section of order
d
is given by
\Gamma_{d}
.
The expression of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
can be drastically simplified as follows.

Lemma 2.6
.

The infinite-dimensional covariance matrix
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is given by

\Gamma_{\alpha}=diag(p_{\alpha})-p_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}^{T}

(80)

where
p_{\alpha}=(p_{\alpha}(1),p_{\alpha}(2),\ldots)
stands for the vector containing all the components of the Sibuya distribution.

Proof.

First of all, it follows from Newton’s generalized Binomial theorem that the generating function of the rising factorial satisfies, for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
wsuch that
|z|<1
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)^{(n)}}{n!}z^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{a}}.

(81)

Denote by
G
the probability generating function of the Sibuya distribution. We obtain from (
7
) that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|s|<1
,

G(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{\alpha}(n)s^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(n-1)}}{n!}s^{n}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\alpha)^{(n)}}{n!}s^{n}=1-(1-s)^{\alpha}.

(82)

Let
F
be the bivariate generating function defined, for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
|s|<1
and
|t|<1
, by

F(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i,j}s^{i}t^{j}.

(83)

We have from (
79
) that

\displaystyle F(s,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{k+\ell}\binom{k+\ell}{k}p_{\alpha}(k+\ell)\frac{(k-\alpha)^{(i-k)}}{(i-k)!}\frac{(\ell-\alpha)^{(j-\ell)}}{(j-\ell)!}s^{i}t^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+\ell}\binom{k+\ell}{k}p_{\alpha}(k+\ell)\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\frac{(k-\alpha)^{(i-k)}}{(i-k)!}s^{i}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{(\ell-\alpha)^{(j-\ell)}}{(j-\ell)!}t^{j}.

(84)

However, identity (
81
) leads to

\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\frac{(k-\alpha)^{(i-k)}}{(i-k)!}s^{i}=s^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k-\alpha)^{(i)}}{i!}s^{i}=\frac{s^{k}}{(1-s)^{k-\alpha}}

as well as to

\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{(\ell-\alpha)^{(j-\ell)}}{(j-\ell)!}t^{j}=t^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ell-\alpha)^{(j)}}{j!}t^{j}=\frac{t^{\ell}}{(1-t)^{\ell-\alpha}}.

Consequently, by choosing

a_{s}=\frac{s}{1-s}\hskip 56.9055pt\text{and}\hskip 56.9055ptb_{t}=\frac{t}{1-t}

we deduce from (
84
) that for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|s|<1
and
|t|<1
,

\displaystyle F(s,t)

\displaystyle=(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+\ell}\binom{k+\ell}{k}p_{\alpha}(k+\ell)a_{s}^{k}b_{t}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{n}\binom{n}{k}p_{\alpha}(n)a_{s}^{k}b_{t}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}p_{\alpha}(n)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}a_{s}^{k}b_{t}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\alpha(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(1-\alpha)^{(n-1)}}{n!}\Big((a_{s}+b_{t})^{n}-a_{s}^{n}-b_{t}^{n}\Big).

(85)

Furthermore, by integrating (
81
), we obtain that for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
with
a\neq 1
and for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|z|<1
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(a)^{(n)}}{(n+1)!}z^{n+1}=-\frac{1}{1-a}\Big((1-z)^{1-a}-1\Big),

(86)

which implies that

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(a)^{(n-1)}}{n!}z^{n}=z-\frac{1}{1-a}\Big((1+z)^{1-a}-1\Big).

(87)

Therefore, it follows from (
82
), (
85
) and (
87
) that for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|s|<1
and
|t|<1
,

\displaystyle F(s,t)

\displaystyle=(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}\Big((1+a_{s})^{\alpha}+(1+b_{t})^{\alpha}-(1+a_{s}+b_{t})^{\alpha}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-s)^{\alpha}+(1-t)^{\alpha}-(1-st)^{\alpha}-(1-s)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=1-(1-st)^{\alpha}-\Big(1-(1-s)^{\alpha}\Big)\Big(1-(1-t)^{\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle=G(st)-G(s)G(t)

(88)

since

1+a_{s}=\frac{1}{1-s},\hskip 28.45274pt1+b_{t}=\frac{1}{1-t},\hskip 28.45274pt1+a_{s}+b_{t}=\frac{1-st}{(1-s)(1-t)}.

Finally, we deduce from (

), (

) and (

) that for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|s|<1
and
|t|<1

F(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i,j}s^{i}t^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{\alpha}(i)(st)^{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)s^{i}t^{j}.

It immediately leads to

\Gamma_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}p_{\alpha}(i)(1-p_{\alpha}(i))&\text{ if }&\ \ i=j\\
-p_{\alpha}(i)p_{\alpha}(j)&\text{ if }&\ \ i\neq j\end{array}\right.

which completes the proof of Lemma

.

2.7
An infinite-dimensional central limit theorem

For all
n\geq 1
, denote by
Q_{n}
the random element in
\ell^{2}
defined by
Q_{n}=(Q_{1,n},\,Q_{2,n},\ldots)
where, for
all
r\geq 1
,

Q_{r,n}=\sqrt{K_{n}}\big(P_{r,n}-p_{\alpha}(r)\big).

For all
d\geq 1
, let
\Pi_{d}
be the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thanks to
Lemma
2.5
, we already know that for all
d\geq 1
,
\Pi_{d}(Q_{n})
converges in distribution to
a centered Gaussian random vector with covariance matrix
\Gamma_{d}
, that is the weak convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions of
Q_{n}
. Lemma
2.6
shows that the infinite-dimensional covariance matrix

\Gamma_{\alpha}
is trace-class as
\text{Tr}(\Gamma_{\alpha})\leq 1
. Moreover, it follows from the Kolmogorov extension theorem that there exists a unique centered Gaussian process
Q_{\alpha}=\mathcal{G}(\Gamma_{\alpha})
with covariance matrix
\Gamma_{\alpha}
.

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it only remains to show that the sequence

(Q_{n})
is tight, see e.g.
Ledoux and Talagrand 2011
and the references therein. It will be done thanks to our latest lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

The infinite-dimensional sequence
(Q_{n})
of
\ell^{2}
-valued random variables is tight. More precisely, we have

\lim_{d\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{n}\sum_{r=d}^{\infty}\mathds{E}[(Q_{r,n})^{2}]=0.

(89)

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
2.7
relies on
Kallianpur and Xiong 1995
.
We have from (
10
) that

\|Q_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}Q_{r,n}^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}K_{n}\big(P_{r,n}-p_{\alpha}(r)\big)^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{L_{r,n}^{2}}{K_{n}}

(90)

where
L_{r,n}=K_{r,n}-p_{\alpha}(r)K_{n}
.
Hence, for all
r\geq 1
,

\mathds{E}[Q_{r,n}^{2}]=\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}^{2}}{K_{n}}\right]-2p_{\alpha}(r)\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}]+(p_{\alpha}(r))^{2}\mathds{E}[K_{n}].

(91)

On the one hand, it follows from
Bercu and Favaro 2024
with
p=1
that

\mathds{E}[K_{n}]=\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\left(\frac{(\alpha+\theta)^{(n)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}-1\right)=\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]\left(\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(n+\theta)}-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta)}\right).

(92)

Hence, we obtain from (
92
) together with the asymptotic behavior of the ratio of two Gamma functions that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]\left(n^{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{\alpha(2\theta+\alpha-1)}{2n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\right)-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta)}\right),

(93)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]n^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}{n^{\alpha}\Gamma(\theta)}+\frac{\alpha(2\theta+\alpha-1)}{2n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\right).

On the other hand, we deduce from
Bercu and Favaro 2024
with
p=1
,
2
that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(r)\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)\frac{(\alpha+\theta)^{(n-r)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}},

(94)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}(K_{r,n}-1)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{\alpha}(r))^{2}\frac{n!}{(n-2r)!}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(2)}\frac{(2\alpha+\theta)^{(n-2r)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}.

(95)

As before, we obtain from (
94
) and (
95

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(r)\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]n^{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{C_{\alpha,\theta}(r)}{2n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\right),

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{\alpha}(r))^{2}\frac{(\alpha+\theta)\Gamma(\theta+1)}{\alpha^{2}\Gamma(2\alpha+\theta)}n^{2\alpha}\left(1+\frac{D_{\alpha,\theta}(r)}{2n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\right)

where

Hereafter, we clearly have the decomposition

\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}^{2}}{K_{n}}\right]=\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}}{K_{n}}\right]+\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}^{2}-K_{r,n}}{K_{n}}\right].

The moment equation (
A.6

\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}^{2}-K_{r,n}}{K_{n}}\right]=\frac{\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{2}-K_{r,n}]\mathds{E}[Z_{r,n}^{**}]}{n^{\alpha}}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{where}\hskip 18.49988ptZ_{r,n}^{**}=\frac{n^{\alpha}}{K^{\ast\ast}_{n-2r}+1}

\mathds{E}\left[\frac{K_{r,n}^{2}}{K_{n}}\right]=(p_{\alpha}(r))^{2}\mathds{E}[S_{\alpha,\theta}]n^{\alpha}+p_{\alpha}(r)(1+o(1)).

(107)

Finally, we find from (

3
Conclusion

It has been shown that the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process
(P_{1,n},P_{2,n},\ldots)
, once properly centered by the Sibuya distribution
p_{\alpha}
and normalized by
\sqrt{K_{n}}
, converges in distribution to the centered Gaussian process
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma_{\alpha})
. While this establishes a functional central limit theorem, several related questions remain open and would further deepen the understanding of the probabilistic structure of the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process.

A first direction concerns large deviations in finite dimension. Let
d\geq 1
be fixed. Is it possible to establish a large deviation principle (LDP) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the sense of
Dembo and Zeitouni 1998
, for the random vector
(P_{1,n},\ldots,P_{d,n})?
Even in the special case
d=1
, this question appears far from being obvious, as it requires a sharp control of rare events in a regime where both the
centering and the normalization depend intrinsically on the random number of blocks
K_{n}
. A positive answer would open the way to obtaining an LDP for estimators of the parameter
\alpha
, such as the estimator

\widehat{\alpha}_{n}
, see also
Feng and Hoppe 1998
for related motivations.

A second direction concerns functional large deviations in infinite dimension. Let
\mu_{n}
denote the random measure naturally associated with the self-normalized Ewens-Pitman process
(P_{1,n},P_{2,n},\ldots)
. Is it possible to prove a functional LDP, again in the sense of
Dembo and Zeitouni 1998
, for the random measure
\mu_{n}
, in a suitable topology on the space of probability measures, or on an appropriate function space? Such a result would provide a refined description of atypical fluctuations beyond the Gaussian regime and would complement the central limit theorem proved here.

Finally, beyond purely asymptotic distributional statements, it would be of considerable interest to develop
quantitative
versions of our results. In particular, one may ask whether the Gaussian approximation can be strengthened to Berry–Esseen type bounds, either in finite dimension for the random vector
(P_{1,n},\ldots,P_{d,n})
, or at the process level. Establishing explicit convergence rates, possibly depending on
\alpha
,
d
, and the growth of
K_{n}
, would substantially enhance the applicability of the limit theory, especially for statistical inference based on moderate sample sizes.

A.1
Sequential or generative construction of the Ewens-Pitman model

We recall the sequential or generative construction of the Ewens-Pitman model given in
Pitman 1995
; see also
Pitman 2006
. For any
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
, the random partition of
[n]
distributed according to the Ewens-Pitman model, can be recursively constructed as follows:
conditionally on the total size
K_{n}=k
of the partition and on the partition subsets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
of corresponding sizes
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, the partition
[n+1]
is an extension of
[n]
such that the element
n+1
is attached to subset
A_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, with probability

\frac{n_{i}-\alpha}{n+\theta},

or forms a new subset with probability

\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}.

Since
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
, we clearly have

\frac{1}{n+\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-\alpha)+\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=\frac{n-\alpha k+\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=1.

Hereafter, for
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})
be the sizes of
the partition subsets
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{K_{n}}\}
. For
r=1,\ldots,n
, denote

K_{r,n}=\sum_{i=1}^{K_{n}}\mathrm{I}_{\{N_{i,n}=r\}}.

Pitman 1995
shows that the above sequential construction leads to the joint distribution (
1
) of
\mathbf{K}_{n}=(K_{1,1},\ldots,K_{n,n})
. Equation (
18
) follows from the above sequential construction since

\mathbb{P}(K_{n+1}=K_{n}+1\,|\,K_{n})=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}.

We also deduce Equation (
21
) from the above construction
as for
r=1
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1,n+1}=1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}.

Moreover, for
r=1
and
n_{1}=1
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1,n+1}=-1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(1-\alpha)K_{1,n}}{n+\theta}.

In addition, for all
r\geq 2
and for
n_{i}=r-1
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(r-1-\alpha)K_{r-1,n}}{n+\theta},

while, for
n_{i}=r
,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{r,n+1}=-1\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{(r-\alpha)K_{r,n}}{n+\theta}.

Finally, Equations (
17
) and (
19
) follow from the sequential construction of the Ewens-Pitman model.

A.2
Moment formulae for the random variable
(K_{n},K_{r,n})

Assume that
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
\alpha+\theta>0
. We have from (
1
) and
the probability distribution of
K_{n}
given in
(
Pitman 2006
, Equation 3.11)
that

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{K}_{n}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\,|\,K_{n}=k)=\frac{n!}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(i-1)}}{i!}\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{i}!},

(A.1)

where
\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)
is the generalized factorial coefficient
(
Charalambides 2005
, Chapter 2)
defined, for all
n\geq 0
and for all
k\geq 0
, by

\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}(-1)^{i}(-\alpha i)^{(n)}.

More precisely, Equation (
A.1
) is a conditional version of the Ewens-Pitman model, which is defined on the set

\mathcal{K}_{n,k}=\left\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\text{ : }k_{i}\geq 0,\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=k\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{n}ik_{i}=n\right\}.

(A.2)

For any
r=1,\ldots,n
, we are going to compute the conditional falling factorial moments of
K_{r,n}
given
K_{n}
, that is for all integer
p\geq 1
,
\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(p)}\,|\,K_{n}=k]
where, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

(a)_{(p)}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-p+1)

with
(a)_{(0)}=1
. We obtain from (
7
) and (
A.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(p)}\,|\,K_{n}=k]

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathcal{K}_{n,k}}(k_{r})_{(p)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(p_{\alpha}(i)\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\!\!\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathcal{K}_{n,k}}\!\!\!\!\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{k_{r}}\frac{1}{(k_{r}-p)!}\prod_{1\leq i\neq r\leq n}\left(p_{\alpha}(i)\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{p}\!\!\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-pr})\in\mathcal{K}_{n-pr,k-p}}\!\!\prod_{i=1}^{n-pr}\left(p_{\alpha}(i)\right)^{k_{i}}\frac{1}{k_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{p}(n)_{(pr)}\mathscr{C}(n-pr,k-p;\alpha),

(A.3)

thanks to
Charalambides 2005
. Moreover, we have for all integer
p\geq 1
,

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}\,|\,K_{n}=k]=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\mathds{E}[(K_{r,n})_{(i)}\,|\,K_{n}=k],

(A.4)

where the curly brackets are the Stirling numbers of the second kind given by

\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}=\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}{i\choose j}j^{p}.

Therefore, we deduce (
A.2
) and (
A.4
) that

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}\,|\,K_{n}=k]=\frac{1}{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)}\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\mathscr{C}(n-ir,k-i;\alpha).

(A.5)

Hereafter, we are able to compute the joint moments
\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}K_{n}^{q}]
, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. More precisely, it follows from (
A.5
) and the tower property of conditional expectation that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}K_{n}^{q}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}K_{n}^{q}\,|\,K_{n}=k]\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{q}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}\,|\,K_{n}=k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{q}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\mathscr{C}(n-ir,k-i;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{q}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n-ir,k-i;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-i}(\ell+i)^{q}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(\ell+i)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{C}(n-ir,\ell;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(i)}\frac{\left(\theta+i\alpha\right)^{(n-ir)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathscr{E}(n-ir,q)

where

\mathscr{E}(n-ir,q)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-ir}(\ell+i)^{q}\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+i\right)^{(\ell)}}{(\theta+i\alpha)^{(n-ir)}}\mathscr{C}(n-ir,\ell;\alpha).

One can observe that

\mathscr{E}(n-ir,q)=\mathds{E}[(K^{\ast}_{n-ir}+i)^{q}]

where
K^{\ast}_{n-ir}
is for the number of blocks of the random partition of
[n-ir]=\{1,\ldots,n-ir\}
distributed according to the Ewens-Pitman model with parameter
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
\theta+i\alpha>-\alpha
.
Finally, we can conclude that

\mathds{E}[K_{r,n}^{p}K_{n}^{q}]=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\left\{\begin{matrix}p\\
i\end{matrix}\right\}\left(p_{\alpha}(r)\right)^{i}(n)_{(ir)}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(i)}\frac{\left(\theta+i\alpha\right)^{(n-ir)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\mathds{E}[(K^{\ast}_{n-ir}+i)^{q}].

(A.6)

A.3
Negative moments of
K_{n}

Our last lemma, which may have its own interest, provides a uniform bound on the negative moments of
K_{n}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in]0,1[
and assume that
\theta>0
. Then, for any
q\in]0,1\!+\!\theta/\alpha[
, we have

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathds{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{-q}\right]<+\infty.

(A.7)

Proof.

For any
q\in]0,1\!+\!\theta/\alpha[
, we have according to
Pitman 2006
that

\mathds{E}[K_{n}^{-q}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\,\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\frac{n!}{k!}\,\frac{\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{(k)}}{(\theta)^{(n)}}\,\mathbb{P}(S_{k}=n)

(A.8)

where
S_{k}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{k}
, with the random variables
X_{i}
’s being independent and identically distributed sharing the same Sibuya distribution
p_{\alpha}
. Moreover, we also have from
Pitman 2006
that for all
n\geq 1
and for all
1\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{k}=n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\,(-i\alpha)^{(n)}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{n+i}\binom{k}{i}\,(i\alpha)_{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i=1,i\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}}^{k}(-1)^{n+i}\binom{k}{i}\,\frac{\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(i\alpha+1-n)}.

(A.9)

However, we know from Euler’s reflection formula that for all
i\geq 1
such that

i\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}
,

\Gamma(i\alpha+1-n)\Gamma(n-i\alpha)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi(n-i\alpha))}.

Hence, we obtain from (
A.9
) that

\mathbb{P}(S_{k}=n)=\sum_{i=1,i\alpha\notin\mathbb{N}}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\binom{k}{i}\,\frac{\sin(i\alpha\pi)\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(n-i\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+1)}.

(A.10)

Furthermore, it follows from Wendel’s inequality for the ratio of gamma functions that there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that
for all
n\geq 1
,

\frac{\Gamma(n-i\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{n^{1+i\alpha}}.

Consequently, (
A.10
) immediately leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{k}=n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\,\frac{\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{n^{1+i\alpha}}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{k^{i}}{i!}\,\frac{\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{n^{1+i\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{n\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{i!}\,\left(\frac{k}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{i}.

(A.11)

In addition, since
\alpha\in]0,1[
,
\Gamma(i\alpha+1)/i!
decays super-exponentially in
i
. In particular, there exists a constant
D_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
i\geq 1
,

\frac{\Gamma(i\alpha+1)}{i!}\leq\frac{D_{\alpha}}{i^{(1-\alpha)i}}.

(A.12)

Consequently, for all
1\leq k\leq n
such that
k\leq n^{\alpha}
, we have
(k/n^{\alpha})^{i}\leq 1
and we obtain from (
A.11
) and (
A.12
)
that

\mathbb{P}(S_{k}=n)\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}D_{\alpha}}{n\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{i^{(1-\alpha)i}}}\left(\frac{k}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{i}\leq\frac{E_{\alpha}\,k}{n^{1+\alpha}},

(A.13)

for some constant
E_{\alpha}>0
, by virtue of the elementary fact that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{i^{(1-\alpha)i}}}<\infty.

Hereafter, we focus our attention on the prefactor into (
A.8
).
We obtain once again from Wendel’s inequality that there exist two constants
C_{\theta}>0
and
D_{\alpha,\theta}>0
such that
for all
n\geq 1
and for all
k\geq 1
,

\frac{n!}{(\theta)^{(n)}}=\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma(n+\theta)}\leq C_{\theta}\,n^{1-\theta}

and

\frac{\big(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\big)^{(k)}}{k!}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+k)}{\Gamma(\frac{\theta}{\alpha})\Gamma(k+1)}\leq D_{\alpha,\theta}\,k^{\theta/\alpha-1}.

Therefore, it follows from (
A.8
) and (
A.13
) that there exists a constant
E_{\alpha,\theta}>0
such
for all
1\leq k\leq n
where
k\leq n^{\alpha}
,

\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k)\leq\frac{E_{\alpha,\theta}\,k^{\theta/\alpha}}{n^{\alpha+\theta}}.

(A.14)

Consequently, by splitting (
A.8
) into two terms, we obtain from (
A.14
) with
\kappa_{n}=\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor
that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{-q}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha q}\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\,\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k)+n^{\alpha q}\sum_{k=\kappa_{n}+1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\,\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k),

\displaystyle\leq n^{\alpha q}\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{1}{k^{q}}\,\frac{E_{\alpha,\theta}\,k^{\theta/\alpha}}{n^{\alpha+\theta}}+n^{\alpha q}n^{-\alpha q}\sum_{k=\kappa_{n}+1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(K_{n}=k),

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{E_{\alpha,\theta}}{n^{\alpha+\theta-\alpha q}}\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa_{n}}k^{\theta/\alpha-q}\,.

(A.15)

Since
q<1+\theta/\alpha
, we have
q_{\alpha,\theta}=\theta/\alpha+1-q>0
, which implies that

\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa_{n}}k^{\theta/\alpha-q}\leq\frac{1}{q_{\alpha,\theta}}\kappa_{n}^{q_{\alpha,\theta}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{\alpha,\theta}}\,n^{\theta+\alpha-\alpha q}.

Finally, by plugging this inequality into (
A.15
), we immediately obtain (
A.7
), which completes the proof of Lemma

A.1
.
∎

A cut finite element method for the Biot system of poroelasticity
Nanna Berre, Kent-Andre Mardal, André Massing, Ivan Yotov

Abstract
We propose a novel cut finite element method for the numerical solution of the Biot system of poroelasticity. The Biot system couples elastic deformation of a porous solid with viscous fluid flow and commonly arises on domains with complex geometries that make high-quality volumetric meshing challenging. To address this issue, we employ the cut finite element framework, where the domain boundary is represented independently of the background mesh, which significantly simplifies the meshing process. Our approach builds upon a parameter robust total pressure formulation of the Biot system, which we combine with the cut finite element method to develop a geometrically robust solution scheme, while preserving the parameter robustness. A key ingredient in the theoretical analysis is a modified inf-sup condition which also holds for mixed boundary conditions, leading to stability and optimal error estimates for the proposed formulation. Finally, we provide numerical evidence demonstrating the theoretical properties of the method and showcasing its capabilities by solving the Biot system on a realistic brain geometry.

1.1
Novel contributions and outline of the paper

The goal of this paper is to develop and analyse an unfitted
CutFEM-based formulation for the Biot system of poroelasticity using a
three-field displacement–pressure–total pressure formulation

58
,
73
. The total pressure
formulation has gained significant popularity due to its robustness
with respect to the physical parameters. In particular, it avoids
locking when the Lamé parameter
\lambda\to\infty
and pressure
oscillations when the permeability
K\to 0
. Our method is designed
to carry over this parameter robustness to the cut finite element
setting, while ensuring robustness with respect to the size of the cut
elements. The latter is achieved by utilizing suitably scaled ghost penalty
stabilization. We allow for both essential and natural boundary conditions for
the displacement and the
fluid pressure, with the essential boundary conditions being weakly
enforced using Nitsche’s method.

We develop stability and error analysis of the proposed method. The
norm of the fluid pressure has terms that are scaled by either
K
or

\lambda^{-1}
and we keep track of the dependence of the bounds on
these parameters. As the finite elements for the displacement and
total pressure form a Stokes-stable pair, we employ and extend previous results
on CutFEM for the Stokes equations
17
,
69
,
48
. A key component in
the stability analysis is establishing an inf-sup condition over the
entire physical domain.
The analysis in
48

assumes Dirichlet boundary conditions for the displacement everywhere
and thus a zero mean value of the pressure.
However, zero mean value for the total pressure
cannot be assumed in the presented Biot discretization,
as we need to consider both a perturbed saddle point problem as well as
mixed boundary conditions.
To deal with this issue, we combine the approach in

48
with an argument from

69
,
66
,
to obtain a modified
displacement–total pressure inf-sup condition containing a pressure ghost penalty as
defect term.
This approach necessitates the construction of a special interpolant with
certain orthogonality property on the physical boundary, which is done by a
patch-wise argument, extending the construction from

7
. As a result, we are able to
establish stability and optimal-order error estimates for the method
with constants independent of the size of the cut elements. We present
extensive numerical results in two and three dimensions verifying the
stability and convergence properties of the method and its robustness
with respect to the physical parameters and how the physical domain
cuts the background mesh.
Furthermore, we present an example of using the method to model the flow in and
deformation of the brain, which illustrates its robustness for complex physical
applications with highly irregular three dimensional geometry and
parameters in the locking regime.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we
introduce the Biot system and briefly motivate the total pressure formulation,
followed by the presentation of the proposed CutFEM formulation in Section
3
.
Afterwards, we collect necessary inequalities, interpolation results and
present a detailed construction of the modified interpolant together with a brief
discussion of ghost penalty realizations in
Section
4
.
The main theoretical results including stability, inf-sup conditions, and the final
a priori error estimate are derived in Section
5
.
Finally, extensive numerical results are given in
Section
6
, before we draw our conclusions in
Section
7
.

2
Model problem

The quasi-static two field Biot’s equations for a poroelastic material read

\displaystyle-\mu\nabla\cdot\varepsilon({\boldsymbol{u}})-\lambda\nabla\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}+\alpha\nabla p_{F}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{f}},

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}+c_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{F}-K\Delta p_{F}

\displaystyle=g,

with the fundamental unknowns being the solid displacement
{\boldsymbol{u}}
and
the fluid pressure
p_{F}
. A main challenge with Biot’s equations is
the large number of parameters (here assumed constant throughout the
domain)
\mu
,
\lambda
,
\alpha
,
c_{0}
and
K
, denoting the two
Lamé parameters, the Biot-Willis constant, the constrained specific
storage coefficient, and the hydraulic conductivity, respectively. In
particular, locking may appear if
\lambda\gg\mu
and pressure
oscillations may appear as
K\rightarrow 0
.

To simplify the notation in the following we focus only on the parameters
that cause numerical challenges. That is, let us consider

\alpha=1
,
c_{0}=1/\lambda
and a backward Euler time-discretization with
\Delta t=1
. Then the above system reduces to

\displaystyle-\mu\nabla\cdot\varepsilon({\boldsymbol{u}})-\lambda\nabla\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p_{F}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{f}},

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{F}+K\Delta p_{F}

\displaystyle=g.

Here, the sign is flipped on the second equation to obtain symmetry
and
g
is altered to accommodate the sign change and source terms
resulting from the time-discretization.

To briefly motivate the total pressure formulation, consider the Hermann trick of introducing a
solid pressure
p_{S}=-\lambda\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
Introducing the solid pressure, we arrive
at

\displaystyle-\mu\nabla\cdot\varepsilon({\boldsymbol{u}})+\nabla p_{S}+\nabla p_{F}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{f}},

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{S}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{F}+K\Delta p_{F}

\displaystyle=g.

In the case
K\rightarrow 0
and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
we have two pressures to be determined by
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}
and as such the system is degenerate.
In the total pressure formulation an alternative pressure is introduced, namely
p_{T}=p_{F}+p_{S}
.
The system then becomes

\displaystyle-\mu\nabla\cdot\varepsilon({\boldsymbol{u}})+\nabla p_{T}

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{f}},

(2.1)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{T}+\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{F}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}p_{T}-\frac{2}{\lambda}p_{F}+K\Delta p_{F}

\displaystyle=g.

(2.3)

Here, the degeneracy is eliminated at least on the continuous level,
although, numerical issues may be present for certain schemes
as
K\rightarrow 0
and/or
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
We remark that the total pressure formulation
73
,
58

is just
one of many formulations with the advantage that regular inf-sup stable Stokes elements can be used.
We also note that as for Stokes problem, the case of pure Dirichlet conditions results in an undetermined constant for the pressure if

\lambda
large but not infinity
the consequence is one bad mode. Such considerations are dealt with in detail in
58

\displaystyle=K\|\nabla q_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\gamma_{p}K\|h^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}q_{F,h}\|_{\Gamma_{s}}^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\|q_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

(3.5)

In addition, we will also need the following norms

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h},*}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+\mu\|h^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Gamma_{d}}^{2},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|q_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|q_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}+\|h^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}q_{T}\|_{\Gamma_{d}}^{2},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|q_{F}\|_{Q_{F,h},*}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|q_{F}\|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}+K\|h^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla q_{F}\|_{\Gamma_{s}}^{2},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|({\boldsymbol{v}},q_{T},q_{F})\|^{2}_{*}

\displaystyle=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h},*}^{2}+\|q_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}^{2}+\|q_{F}\|_{Q_{F,h},*}^{2}.

(3.9)

Remark 3.1
.

The functionals defined in (

) and (

) are indeed norms, using Korn’s inequality
[
14
, (1.19)]
and Poincaré inequality
[

, (1.10)]
.

The idea of the ghost penalty approach is to augment the formulation (
3.1a
)–(
3.1c
) above with certain stabilization forms
g_{1,h}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
g_{2,h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, and

,
i=1,2
, acting the vicinity of the embedded boundary so that the norms above are extended to the entire active mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|_{g_{1,h}}^{2}\sim\mu\|\varepsilon({\boldsymbol{v}})\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\gamma_{u}\mu\|h^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\Gamma_{d}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}=\|q_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}+|q_{T,h}|_{g_{2,h}}^{2}\sim\|q_{T,h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}=\|q_{F,h}\|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}+|q_{F,h}|_{g_{3,h}}^{2}\sim\frac{1}{\lambda}\|q_{F,h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+K\|\nabla q_{F,h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\gamma_{p}K\|h^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}q_{F,h}\|_{\Gamma_{s}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}=|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}.

To this end, we replace the bilinear forms on the diagonal by their ghost-penalty enhanced counterparts

\displaystyle A_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})+g_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle A_{2,h}(p_{T,h},q_{T,h})

\displaystyle=a_{2,h}(p_{T,h},q_{T,h})+g_{2,h}(p_{T,h},q_{T,h})

\displaystyle A^{i}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h})

\displaystyle=a^{i}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h})+g^{i}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h}),\ i=1,2,

resulting in the ghost penalty cut finite element method: find
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}\times Q_{T,h}\times Q_{F,h}
such that

\displaystyle A_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,b_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},p_{T,h})

\displaystyle=L_{1}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h},

\displaystyle b_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},q_{T,h})

\displaystyle\,-\,

\displaystyle A_{2,h}(p_{T,h},q_{T,h})

\displaystyle+\,b_{2,h}(p_{F,h},q_{T,h})

\displaystyle=L_{2}(q_{T,h}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall q_{T,h}\in Q_{T,h},

\displaystyle b_{2,h}(q_{F,h},p_{T,h})

\displaystyle\,-\,A_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h})

\displaystyle=L_{3}(q_{F,h}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall q_{F,h}\in Q_{F,h}.

Define the total ghost-penalty

\displaystyle G_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h}))=

\displaystyle g_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})+g_{2,h}(p_{T,h},q_{T,h})+g^{1}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h})+g^{2}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},q_{F,h}),

and the total bilinear form

\displaystyle A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h}))=

\displaystyle B_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h}))+G_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})).

(3.11)

4.1
Trace inequalities and inverse estimates

We recall the local trace inequalities, for a function

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert v\rVert_{T}+h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{T},\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\Gamma\cap T}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert v\rVert_{T}+h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{T},\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(4.2)

In addition, we have the following inverse estimates for a discrete function
v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(T)
and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(T)=\{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}_{h}:|F\cap\partial T|>0\}

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla v_{h}\rVert_{T}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{T},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{n}v_{h}\rVert_{F\cap\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{T},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{n}v_{h}\rVert_{\Gamma\cap\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{T}.

(4.5)

4.2
Quasi-interpolation operators

To obtain the required interpolation operator, we begin with the bounded extension operator
\mathcal{E}:H^{s}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\geqslant 0

\|\mathcal{E}u\|_{s,\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lesssim\|u\|_{s,\Omega},

see
84
.
Next, define the polynomial space
V_{h}=X_{h}^{k}
and let
\pi^{*}_{h}:L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\rightarrow V_{h}
be the standard Scott–Zhang interpolation
which for
\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(T)
satisfies the interpolation error estimates

\displaystyle\|v-\pi^{*}_{h}v\|_{r,T}

\displaystyle\lesssim Ch^{s-r}|v|_{s,\mathcal{P}(T)},\quad

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r\leqslant s

\displaystyle\leqslant k+1,

\displaystyle\|v-\pi^{*}_{h}v\|_{r,F}

\displaystyle\lesssim Ch^{s-r-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|v|_{s,\mathcal{P}(T)},\quad

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r+\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s

\displaystyle\leqslant k+1,

where
\mathcal{P}(T)
is the patch of neighbors of element
T

. Further,
we define

\pi_{h}v=\pi^{*}_{h}\mathcal{E}v.

(4.6)

The stability of the extension operator
\mathcal{E}
together with the interpolation
error for the Scott–Zhang operator imply the following
interpolation estimates:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}v-\pi_{h}v\|_{r,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim Ch^{s-r}|v|_{s,\Omega},\quad

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r\leqslant s

\displaystyle\leqslant k+1,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}v-\pi_{h}v\|_{r,\mathcal{F}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim Ch^{s-r-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|v|_{s,\Omega},\quad

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r+\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s

\displaystyle\leqslant k+1.

(4.8)

For the norms (
3.6
)–(
3.8
), this then gives following the interpolation estimates.

Lemma 4.1

(Interpolation estimates)
.

Assume
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in[H^{r}(\Omega)]^{n}
,
q_{T}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{r}-1}|{\boldsymbol{v}}|_{\bar{r}},

\displaystyle\|q_{T}-\pi_{h}q_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}

\displaystyle\|q_{F}-\pi_{h}q_{F}\|_{Q_{F,h},*}

We need the interpolant to have additional orthogonality properties on
the interface, which we get from a modified Scott–Zhang operator
introduced in
7
. To construct

intersect the interface
\Gamma_{s}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\Gamma_{s}}=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\,|\,T\cap T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset,\,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma_{s}}_{h}\}.

Next, we regroup the elements in

into non-overlapping patches
\{\mathcal{P}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

.

In contrast to the proof given in

, we allow for

for

To take patch-wise normal-field variations into account,

is assumed to be of class

, and we therefore know that there exists an

\varepsilon-
tubular neighborhood
U_{\varepsilon}(\Gamma_{s})
,
and an
\varepsilon_{0}

on
\Gamma_{s}
for all

. Choosing

small enough, we have that
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\Gamma_{s}}
is
contained in
U_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\Gamma_{s})
. Choosing
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}
, the following bound holds for the normal vector

\displaystyle\lVert{\boldsymbol{n}}({\boldsymbol{x}})-{\boldsymbol{n}}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\leqslant\lVert\nabla{\boldsymbol{n}}\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{P}_{i}}\lVert{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\leqslant c_{I}h,

(4.10)

so that

is the maximum of the principal curvatures.

We want the modified interpolant to satisfy the following orthogonality property

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s=0,

(4.11)

for all
\mathcal{P}_{i}
. In
7
the normal was implicitly assumed to be constant on each patch. In the following we will extend the construction to non-constant normal vectors, and dimension
n
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a modified Scott–Zhang interpolation operator
\pi^{c}_{h}:H^{1}(\Omega_{h})\rightarrow V_{h}
which
for
\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}\in[H^{r}(\Omega)]^{n}
,
r\geqslant 1

satisfies both the orthogonality property (
4.11
) and the interpolation estimate

\displaystyle\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h\lVert\nabla\left({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)\rVert_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{1,\Omega}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Let
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}
be the (vector-valued) degree of freedom associated with
\phi_{i}

defined on a patch
\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{e}}^{1}({\boldsymbol{x}})={\boldsymbol{n}}({\boldsymbol{x}})
the surface normal, and define
{\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}({\boldsymbol{x}})
for
k=2,\dots,n
to be orthogonal vectors such that they span the tangential space
T_{x}\Gamma_{s}
.
We determine
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}
from the linear system

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}\phi_{i}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\qquad\text{for }k=1,\dots,n,

which we can write as the matrix equation

\displaystyle A_{i}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}={\boldsymbol{V}}_{i},

with

\displaystyle A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\phi_{i}({\boldsymbol{e}}^{1})^{T}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\\
\vdots\\
\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\phi_{i}({\boldsymbol{e}}^{n})^{T}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\end{bmatrix},\quad{\boldsymbol{V}}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}^{1}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\\
\vdots\\
\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}^{n}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\end{bmatrix}.

Next, let
\bar{{\boldsymbol{e}}}^{k}_{i}={\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})
for an
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}
, and define the matrix
B_{i}

\displaystyle B_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\phi_{i}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\,\begin{bmatrix}(\bar{{\boldsymbol{e}}}^{1})^{T}\\
\vdots\\
(\bar{{\boldsymbol{e}}}^{n})^{T}\end{bmatrix}.

From construction,
B_{i}
is an invertible matrix, the Euclidean based matrix norm can easily be estimated
\lvert B_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sim h^{n-1}
, and it follows that
\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lesssim h^{1-n}.
We also define the matrix
C_{i}
, with elements
C^{k,j}_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}(e^{k,j}-\bar{e}^{k,j}_{i})\phi\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s
, where
e^{k,j}
is
j
-th component of
{\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}
.
Recall the bound of the normal vector (
4.10
), which is also valid for the orthogonal vectors, so that for one element in
C_{i}

\displaystyle\lvert C_{i}^{k,j}\rvert\leqslant\lVert e^{k,j}_{i}-\bar{e}^{k,j}_{i}\rVert_{\infty,\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}|\phi|\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\lesssim h^{n},

giving
\lvert C_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lesssim h^{n}
.
We can write
A_{i}=B_{i}+C_{i}=B_{i}(\mathcal{I}+B_{i}^{-1}C_{i}),
and in addition we have

\displaystyle\lvert B^{-1}_{i}C_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\lvert B^{-1}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert C_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lesssim h,

(4.13)

meaning that for small
h
,
A_{i}
is invertible and
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}
is given by
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}=A_{i}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{V}}_{i}
. We want
to establish a bound for
\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\lvert A^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, and begin by finding a bound for
\lvert A^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\lvert A^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\lvert(\mathcal{I}+B_{i}^{-1}C_{i})^{-1}B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lesssim\lvert(\mathcal{I}+B_{i}^{-1}C_{i})^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

For small h we can use power series expansion together with (
4.13
) to get

\displaystyle\lvert A^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lvert(\mathcal{I}+B_{i}^{-1}C_{i})^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\lvert\sum_{0}^{\infty}(-B_{i}^{-1}C_{i})^{k}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0}^{\infty}\lvert B_{i}^{-1}C_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{k}\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\frac{\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{1-\lvert B_{i}^{-1}C_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\lesssim\frac{\lvert B_{i}^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{1-h}\lesssim h^{1-n}.

(4.14)

Next, we apply a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the trace inequality (
4.2
), and the approximation properties of
\pi_{h}
to obtain

\displaystyle\lvert V^{k}_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}){\boldsymbol{e}}^{k}\,\mathop{}\!\textup{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{(n-1)/2}\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\Gamma_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{n/2-1}\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}+h^{n/2}\lVert\nabla({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{n/2}\lVert\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{i})},

where
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{i})
consists of all the patches that share a vertex with
\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
Then, combining this with (
4.14
) we get the following bound for
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}

\displaystyle\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\lvert A^{-1}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant Ch^{-n/2+1}\lVert\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{i})}.

The modified interpolant is defined by

\displaystyle\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}=\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\phi_{i}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}.

(4.15)

Next, for a
K\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
we have that

\displaystyle\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{K}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{K}+\lVert\phi_{i}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rVert_{K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{K}+h^{n/2}\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{K}+h\lVert\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{i})},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla(\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\rVert_{K}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\nabla\left(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)\rVert_{K}+\lVert\nabla\left(\phi_{i}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\right)\rVert_{K}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla\left(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)\rVert_{K}+h^{n/2-1}\lvert{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{i}\rvert_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla\left(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)\rVert_{K}+\lVert\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}_{i})},

(4.16)

which together with the properties of
\pi_{h}
, and that we by
construction have a bounded number of patch overlaps leads to desired interpolation estimate

\displaystyle\lVert{\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}\rVert_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h\lVert\nabla({\boldsymbol{v}}-\pi^{c}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}})\rVert_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{1,\Omega}.

4.3
Role and design of ghost penalties

The total pressure formulation (
3.10
)–(
3.11
)
can be seen as perturbed Stokes problem for the displacement and total pressure which is
intertwined with a Poisson problem for the fluid pressure.
Thus, to devise suitable ghost penalty stabilization, we have to take into
account the different roles played by the individual bilinear form contributions.
To do so, we start our discussion from an abstract finite element
space
\mathcal{V}_{h}
satisfying

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{c}}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\subset\mathcal{V}_{h}

and review the required properties for
H^{1}
stabilizing
ghost penalty
g_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
suitable for cut finite element formulations of
elliptic problems, see e.g.
47
,
33
.
Then, departing from this abstract setting, we discuss the specific ghost penalties needed
for the relevant bilinear forms appearing in (
3
).

4.3.1
H1-stabilizing ghost penalties and realizations

We assume that the ghost penalty
g_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric positive semi-definite bilinear form on
\mathcal{V}_{h}

satisfying the following assumptions:

A1

H^{1}
semi-norm extension property for
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\lesssim\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}+|v_{h}|_{g_{h}},

(4.17)

A2

Weak consistency for
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle|\pi_{h}v|_{g_{h}}\lesssim h^{r-1}\|v\|_{r,\Omega},

(4.18)

where
r=\min\{s,k+1\}
and

\pi_{h}
is the quasi-interpolation operator given in (
4.6
).

These two properties are crucial for deriving
geometrically robust discrete coercivity and optimal a priori error estimates
for cut finite element formulation of Poisson-type problems, see
47
,
33
.
To ensure that the associated system matrices satisfy the usual condition number bounds,
we typically require the following additional properties to be satisfied by the ghost penalty
g_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
:

A3

L^{2}
norm extension property for
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}+|v_{h}|_{g_{h}}.

(4.19)

A4

Inverse inequality for
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle|v_{h}|_{g_{h}}\lesssim h^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

(4.20)

Next, we briefly review the most common realizations of the ghost
penalty
g_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfying
Assumptions
4.17
-
4.20
for
\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

Facet-based
ghost penalties
7
,
18
,
69

\displaystyle g_{h}^{f}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\gamma^{f}_{p}h^{2j-1}(\llbracket\partial_{n}^{j}v\rrbracket,\llbracket\partial_{n}^{j}w\rrbracket)_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{g}},

(4.21)

penalizes the
jumps of
normal-derivatives of all relevant polynomial orders across faces belonging to the
facet set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{g}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:T^{+}\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\lor T^{-}\cap\Gamma\neq\emptyset\}.

Here, we use
the notation
\partial_{n}^{j}v\coloneqq\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\tfrac{D^{\alpha}v(x)n^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}
for multi-indices
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
,
|\alpha|=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
and

n^{\alpha}=n_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}n_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots n_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
.
The constant
\gamma^{f}
denotes a dimensionless stability parameter.
Note that for
H^{1}
-conforming finite element spaces, the jump terms for
j=0
vanish.

In
18
, ghost penalties based on a

local projection stabilization
were proposed. For a given
patch
P
of
\diam(P)\lesssim h
containing the two elements
T_{1}

and
T_{2}
, one defines the
L^{2}
projection

\pi_{P}:L^{2}(P)\to\mathbb{P}_{p}(P)
onto the space of polynomials of
order
p
associated with the patch
P
. For
v\in V_{h}
,
the fluctuation operator
\kappa_{P}=I-\pi_{P}
measures then the deviation of

v|_{P}
from being a polynomial defined on
P
.
By choosing certain patch definitions,
a coupling between
elements with a possible small cut and an interior element
is ensured. One patch choice arises naturally from the definition
of
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{g}
by defining
the patch
P(F)=T^{+}_{F}\cup T^{-}_{F}
for two elements
T^{+}_{F}
,
T^{-}_{F}
sharing the interior face
F
and
setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{P(F)\}_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{g}}.

A second possibility is to use neighborhood patches
\omega(T)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{\omega(T)\}_{T\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}_{h}}.

Alternatively, one can mimic the cell agglomeration approach taken in
classical unfitted finite element methods
approaches
83
,
56
,
5

by associating to each cut element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
with a small
intersection
|T\cap\Omega|_{d}\ll|T|_{d}
an element

T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
such that
the “agglomerated
patch”
P_{a}(T)=T\cup T^{\prime}
satisfies
\diam{P_{a}(T)}\lesssim h
.
A proper collection of patches
is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{3}=\{P_{a}(T)\}_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}.

The resulting
local projection
based ghost penalty are then defined as follows:

\displaystyle g_{h}^{p}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{p}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}h^{-2}(\kappa_{P}v,\kappa_{P}w)_{P},\quad\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\mathcal{P}_{3}\}.

Finally, an elegant version of a patch-based ghost penalty
avoiding the use of local projection operators
was presented in
76
.
By extending polynomials
u_{i}
defined on an element
T_{i}
naturally to global polynomials
u_{i}^{e}
,
a volume-based jump on a patch
P=T_{1}\cup T_{2}
can be defined by

\llbracket u\rrbracket_{P}=u_{1}^{e}-u_{2}^{e}
which give rise to the
volume based
ghost penalty

\displaystyle g_{h}^{v}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{b}^{v}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}h^{-2}(\llbracket v\rrbracket_{P},\llbracket w\rrbracket_{P})_{P},\quad\mathcal{P}\in\{\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\mathcal{P}_{3}\}.

More realizations with alternative penalty operator and patch choices
can be found in the literature, see e.g.

33
and the references
therein.

5
Stability and a priori error analysis

This section is devoted to the theoretical analysis of the
cut finite element method (
3.10
) for the Biot problem.
We start by collecting the necessary stability and coercivity
estimates for the bilinear forms involved, before we turn to the
discussion of a modified inf-sup condition for the displacement-total-pressure
coupling bilinear form

when mixed boundary conditions are considered.
Afterwards, we state and prove the main inf-sup condition for total
bilinear form

given in (
3.11
).
Here, the main objectives are to carry over the parameter robustness of
the total pressure formulation to the cut finite element setting,
and to ensure that the resulting unfitted method is also geometrically robust.
We conclude the section by deriving optimal a priori error estimates
for the proposed cut finite element method.

It is shown in

Remark 5.2

As shown in

Remark 5.3

The proof of the modified inf-sup condition (

({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})

\displaystyle A_{h}\bigl(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h}\bigr))\gtrsim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

Step 1.

First, we let
({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})=({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h})
,
which together with stability results (
5.1
), (
5.2
)
and the identity

\displaystyle a_{2,h}(p_{T,h},p_{T,h})-2b_{2,h}(p_{F,h},p_{T,h})+a^{2}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},p_{F,h})=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|p_{T,h}-p_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|p_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2},

shows that

\displaystyle A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h}))

\displaystyle=A_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})+A_{2,h}(p_{T,h},p_{T,h})-2b_{2,h}(p_{F,h},p_{T,h})+A_{3,h}(p_{F,h},p_{F,h})

\displaystyle=A_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})+|p_{T,h}|^{2}_{g_{2,h}}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|p_{T,h}-p_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|p_{F,h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+A^{1}_{3,h}(p_{F,h},p_{F,h})+|p_{F,h}|^{2}_{g^{2}_{3,h}}

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{A_{1}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+|p_{T,h}|^{2}_{g_{2,h}}+c_{A_{3}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}.

(5.15)

Step 2.

Next, we choose
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}={\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}

where
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}
satisfies the modified inf-sup
condition (
5.9
) for
p_{T,h}
and is rescaled such that

|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}=\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}
. Utilizing (
5.1
) and (
5.9
) leads to

\displaystyle A_{h}

\displaystyle\bigl(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},0,0)\bigr)=A_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})+b_{1,h}({\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},p_{T,h})

\displaystyle\geqslant-C_{A_{1}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}+\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}-\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|p_{T,h}|_{g_{2,h}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=-C_{A_{1}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}+\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}-\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|p_{T,h}|_{g_{2,h}}\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}

\displaystyle\geqslant-\frac{C_{A_{1}}}{4\varepsilon}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+(\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-C_{A_{1}}\varepsilon-\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varepsilon)\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}-\frac{\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{4\varepsilon}|p_{T,h}|^{2}_{g_{2,h}}

where in the last 2 steps, an

.

Step 3.

Finally, combining the bounds (
5.15
) and (
5.16
),
we see that for

\displaystyle A_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}+\delta{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h}))

\displaystyle\geqslant(c_{A_{1}}-\delta\frac{C_{A_{1}}(C_{A_{1}}+\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}{2\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}^{2}+\delta\frac{\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{2}\|p_{T,h}\|_{Q_{T,h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\delta\frac{\beta_{2}(C_{A_{1}}+\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}{2\beta_{1}})|p_{T,h}|^{2}_{g_{2,h}}+c_{A_{3}}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}^{2}.

Choosing

\delta<\min\left\{\frac{\beta_{1}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}c_{A_{1}}}{C_{A_{1}}(C_{A_{1}}+\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})},\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}(C_{A_{1}}+\beta_{2}\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}\right\}

we conclude that there is a constant

\displaystyle A_{h}\bigl(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}+\delta{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h})\bigr)\geqslant c_{A}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant c_{A}(1+\delta)|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}+\delta{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

where in the last step we used that

|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}+\delta{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},-p_{T,h},-p_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\leqslant(1+\delta)|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Method (
3.10
) has a unique solution satisfying

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|p_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|{\boldsymbol{f}}\|_{\Omega}+(\mu^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+1)\lVert h^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\boldsymbol{u}}_{D}\rVert_{\Gamma_{d}}+\lVert{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{N}\rVert_{\tilde{H}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{s})}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|g\|_{\Omega}+K^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert h^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}p_{F,D}\rVert_{\Gamma_{s}}+\lVert g_{N}\rVert_{\tilde{H}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma_{d})}.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness follows from Theorem (
5.1
). For the solution
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})
we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}\times Q_{T,h}\times Q_{F,h}}\frac{A_{h}\bigl(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})\bigr)}{|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}}

5.2
Error estimates

With the inf-sup condition for the total bilinear form
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at hand,
an a priori error estimate can now be derived combining a weak
Galerkin orthogonality with the approximation properties of the
interpolation operator and the weak consistency of the CutFEM stabilization terms.

Lemma 5.4

(Weak Galerkin orthogonality)
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{u}},p_{T},p_{F,h})\in H^{r}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{t}(\Omega)
be the solution of the Biot problem (
3.1
), and let
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}\times Q_{T,h}\times Q_{F,h}
be solution of the discrete stabilized formulation (
3.10
). Then

\displaystyle B_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T}-p_{T,h},p_{F}-p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h}))=G_{h}(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h}),({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})),

(5.17)

for all
({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}\times Q_{T,h}\times Q_{F,h}
.

Theorem 5.2

(A priori error estimate)
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{u}},p_{T},p_{F,h})\in H^{r}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{t}(\Omega)
be the solution of the Biot problem (
3.1
), and let
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T,h},p_{F,h})\in{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}\times Q_{T,h}\times Q_{F,h}
be the solution of the discrete stabilized formulation (
3.10
). Then with
\bar{k}+1=\min\{k+1,r,s\}
and
\bar{l}+1=\min\{l+1,t\}
the error
({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T}-p_{T,h},p_{F}-p_{F,h})
satisfies

\displaystyle\|({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T}-p_{T,h},p_{F}-p_{F,h})\|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{k}}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{\bar{k}+1}+\left(1+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\lambda^{-1}\right)h^{\bar{k}}|p_{T}|_{\bar{k}}+\left(K^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}}+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}+1}+\lambda^{-1}h^{\bar{l}+1}\right)|p_{F}|_{\bar{l}+1}.

Proof.

The proof follows the usual recipe.
We begin by decomposing the error into an interpolation error and a discrete error,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{T}-p_{T,h},p_{F}-p_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|({\boldsymbol{u}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}},p_{T}-\pi_{h}p_{T},p_{F}-\pi_{h}p_{F})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{*}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|(\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\pi_{h}p_{T}-p_{T,h},\pi_{h}p_{F}-p_{F,h})|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}

\displaystyle=|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|E_{\pi}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{*}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|E_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

Starting with the
E_{h}
term,
we take
({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{T,h},q_{F,h})=W_{h}
which satisfy the inf-sup estimate (
5.14
) for
A_{h}
and scales such that

|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|W_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|=1
,
and utilize the weak Galerkin orthogonality (
5.17
)
to bound the discrete error by the interpolation error and the
consistency error of the ghost penalty,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|E_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\leqslant A_{h}(E_{h},W_{h})=

\displaystyle B_{h}(E_{\pi},W_{h})+G_{h}(E_{\pi},W_{h}).

Assumption
4.18
regarding weak consistency for the ghost penalties and the choosen scaling yields

\displaystyle|E_{\pi}|_{G_{h}}\lesssim\mu^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{k}}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{\bar{k}+1}+h^{\bar{k}}|p_{T}|_{\bar{k}}+\left(K^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}}+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}+1}\right)|p_{F}|_{\bar{l}+1}.

The bilinear form
B_{h}
can be bounded by the results in Lemma
5.1
and the interpolation estimates (
4.9
),

\displaystyle B_{h}(E_{\pi},W_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h},*}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}}\|p_{T}-\pi_{h}p_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}+\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-\pi_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h},*}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}+\lambda^{-1}\|p_{T}-\pi_{h}p_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{-1}\lVert p_{F}-\pi_{h}p_{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{T,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{T,h}}+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|p_{T}-\pi_{h}p_{T}\|_{Q_{T,h},*}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}+\|p_{F}-\pi_{h}p_{F}\|_{Q_{F,h},*}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|q_{F,h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{Q_{F,h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{k}}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{\bar{k}+1}+\left(1+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\lambda^{-1}\right)h^{\bar{k}}|p_{T}|_{\bar{k}}+\left(K^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}}+\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h^{\bar{l}+1}+\lambda^{-1}h^{\bar{l}+1}\right)|p_{F}|_{\bar{l}+1}.

∎

6.4
Poroelastic simulations of brain mechanics

In the final example we will consider the interstitial fluid flow and brain tissue deformation when considering a poroelastic model of the brain under a setting with mixed boundary conditions. Interstitial fluid flow caused by poroelastic deformations is interesting in the context of the glymphatic system
55
, see
10
for a recent review. In particular, an issue that has been debated is whether there are any static pressure driven advection
52
acting on long time scales. In the present context we therefore put focus on static forces represented by mixed boundary conditions. Here, we consider the situation of a brain mostly floating in cerebrospinal fluid while parts are attached to the dura.
Specifically,
it is known that while the intracranial pressure of the CSF pulsate with cardiac pulsation as well as the respiration of magnitudes of 100-1000 Pa, the pulsation is largely synchronous throughout the whole brain
90
.
Therefore we ignore the pulsation and instead consider
a small spatial gradients of 1 Pa / cm
32
,
85
. Furthermore,
as highlighted in studies of traumatic head injuries, dura mater attachment prevent local motion and henceforth we consider dura attachment along the tentorium, see
Fig
2
,
as a homogeneous Dirichlet condition for the displacement along with null flux. Elsewhere we assign a vertical pressure gradient, see e.g.
31
,
77
for more detailed pressure characterizations. Given such a setup situation, it is interesting to quantify the flow, pressure and displacement. Specifically, we solve system (
2.1
) - (
2.3
) on a right brain hemisphere, represented by surface STL mesh available at

7
Conclusion and outlook

We have presented a stabilized cut finite element method for the Biot system based on the total-pressure formulation. Our theoretical analysis established stability and optimal convergence rates, independent of physical parameters and cut configurations, and these properties were confirmed by extensive numerical experiments in two and three dimensions. The CutFEM approach enables robust simulation of poroelasticity in complex domains, such as brain mechanics, without the need for body-fitted meshes.

Efficient preconditioners are essential for solving the Biot system at large scale, and future work should include the development and analysis of preconditioners for the proposed CutFEM formulation, e.g., based on
58
. Additional directions include octree-based parallel adaptive mesh refinement
25

for which Gridap already provides an extension module. This would allow for local refinement along the embedded boundary, and better resolution of boundary dynamics.
Moreover, the geometric and discretization algorithms needs to be extended to account for multiple interfaces and heterogeneous brain regions in more realistic multiphysic brain models with spatially varying material parameters.
Other interesting multiphysic settings that could be consider are Biot-Stokes coupling
11
to include couplings with the surrounding CSF flow, or multiple-network poroelasticity (MPET) equations
57
.
A main advantage of the CutFEM approach in the context of biomedical applications is that meshing
65
,
35
is avoided, while still sub-voxel resolution of surfaces are retained through imaging segmentation tools such as for example FreeSurfer.

Visibility polynomials, dual visibility spectrum, and characterization of total mutual-visibility sets
Csilla Bujtás, Sandi Klavžar, Jing Tian

Abstract
Mutual-visibility sets were motivated by visibility in distributed systems and social networks, and intertwine with several classical mathematical areas. Monotone properties of the variety of mutual-visibility sets, and restrictions of such sets to convex and isometric subgraphs are studied. Dual mutual-visibility sets are shown to be intrinsically different from other types of mutual-visibility sets. It is proved that for every finite subset Z Z of positive integers there exists a graph G G that has a dual mutual-visibility set of size i i if and only if i ∈ Z ∪ { 0 } i\in Z\cup\{0\} , while for the other types of mutual-visibility such a set consists of consecutive integers. Visibility polynomials are introduced and their properties derived. As a surprise, every polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients and with a constant term 1 1 is a dual visibility polynomial of some graph. Characterizations are given for total mutual-visibility sets, for graphs with total mutual-visibility number 1 1 , and for sets which are not total mutual-visibility sets, yet every proper subset is such. Along the way an earlier result from the literature is corrected.



Keywords:
mutual-visibility set; variety of mutual-visibility sets; convex subgraph; integer polynomial

2
Monotonicity of mutual-visibility sets

In this section, for a given visibility set we consider monotonicity of its subsets and monotonicity of its restriction to convex and isometric subgraphs. We recall the previous results and round off the picture so that all four variants are treated systematically. Applying one of our findings we also correct an earlier result from the literature.

Our starting point is the following result.

Proposition 2.1

[
10
, Proposition 2.5]

If
X
is a mutual-visibility set (resp. outer, total mutual-visibility set) of a graph
G
and
Y\subseteq X
, then
Y
is a mutual-visibility set (resp. outer, total mutual-visibility set) of

.

Proposition

does not hold for dual mutual-visibility sets. For instance, if

and

are adjacent vertices of

, then

is a

, but neither

is a dual mutual-visibility set.

Dual mutual-visibility therefore stands out because in contrast to the other three types of visibility sets, they are not necessarily closed for taking subsets. On the other hand, all four concepts are monotone for subsets in the following sense.

Proposition 2.2

[
13
, Lemma 5.4]

If

is a mutual-visibility set (resp. outer, dual, or total mutual visibility set) of a graph

, then

is a mutual-visibility set (resp. outer, dual, or total mutual visibility set) of

In the seminal paper on the mutual-visibility, the following useful property was observed.

[

Let

We now show that Lemma

Lemma 2.4

Let

is a dual mutual-visibility set of

Visibility polynomials

If

is a graph and

, then

[

if for any two vertices of

Definition 3.1

The

of a graph

is the polynomial

\name(G)=\sum_{i\geq 0}r_{i}x^{i}\,,

where
r_{i}
is the number of distinct mutual-visibility sets of

with cardinality

.

Clearly, the degree of
\name(G)
is
\mu(G)
, and its constant term is

\name(P_{n})=1+nx+\binom{n}{2}x^{2}\,.

In a completely analogous way we define the

, the

, and the

, which are, for a given graph

,
\nameo(G)
, and
\namet(G)
. For paths

,

, we have

\displaystyle\named(P_{n})

\displaystyle=1+2x+3x^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\nameo(P_{n})

\displaystyle=1+nx+x^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\namet(P_{n})

\displaystyle=1+2x+x^{2}\,.

As a further example, we determine these four polynomials for balanced complete bipartite graphs. Note that the polynomials for a general complete bipartite graph

can be obtained in the same way but by considering more cases. Here we restrict our attention to the simpler case of

.

Proposition 3.2

For

, the complete bipartite graph

has the following polynomials:

\displaystyle\name(K_{n,n})\,\,

\displaystyle=((x+1)^{n}-x^{n})^{2}+2nx^{n+1}+2x^{n}\,,

\displaystyle\nameo(K_{n,n})

\displaystyle=((x+1)^{n}-x^{n})^{2}+2x^{n}\,,

\displaystyle\named(K_{n,n})

\displaystyle=\namet(K_{n,n})=((x+1)^{n}-x^{n})^{2}\,.

Let

be the partite classes of

and consider a set

is a mutual-visibility set in each of the following cases:

or

(c)

and

Further, if neither of

-
(d)
holds, then

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}{2n\choose i}x^{i}-2x^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\choose j}x^{j}-2nx^{2n-1}-x^{2n}+2x^{n}+2nx^{n+1}+2nx^{2n-1}+2x^{2n}

4

As observed in Section

-element ones. For this phenomenon,

is the smallest example. We have
\mud(C_{5})=2

The

of a graph

(r_{0},\dots,r_{k})

•

(1,3,3,1)

Revisiting total mutual-visibility

In this section, we characterize total mutual-visibility sets, graphs

The vertex

of

is

induces a complete subgraph of

. The set of simplicial vertices of

is denoted by

and its cardinality by

To start, let us show the following result.

Proposition 5.1

If
G
is a geodetic graph, then
\mut(G)=s(G)
and
S(G)
is the unique
\mut
-set of
G
.

Proof.
S(G)
is a total mutual-visibility set of

because a vertex from
S(G)
cannot be an inner vertex of a shortest path.

To prove that
\mut(G)\leq s(G)
, suppose on the contrary that there exists some
\mut
-set
X
of
G
with
|X|\geq s(G)+1
. Then
X
contains a vertex

. Let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
be two neighbors of

such that

. As

, and

is a total mutual-visibility set, there exists a vertex

such that

. But then there exists at least two shortest

-paths, a contradiction.

We have thus seen that
\mut(G)=s(G)
. Moreover, the above argument also implies that
S(G)
is the unique
\mut
-set of
G
.

\square

In the proof of Proposition

it was sufficient to consider only vertices at distance

. The announced characterization of total mutual-visibility sets says it is no coincidence.

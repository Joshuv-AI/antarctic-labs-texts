Hilbert schemes and Seshadri constants
Jonas Baltes

Abstract
. In this paper we will propose a new method to investigate Seshadri constants, namely by means of (nested) Hilbert schemes. This will allow us to use the geometry of the latter spaces, for example the computations of the nef cone via Bridgeland stability conditions to gain new insights and bounds on Seshadri constants. Moreover, it turns out that many known Seshadri constants turn up in the wall and chamber decomposition of the movable cone of Hilbert schemes.

Definitions and Numerical Observations

We first start with the following well known definition, see e.g. the survey paper
[
4
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a surface,
H
an ample divisor and

\varepsilon(H,Z)=\sup\{\lambda>0\,|\,H-\lambda E_{r}\in\Pic(\Bl_{Z}X)_{\mathbb{R}}\textup{ is ample}\}.

For

Definition 2.2

Let

be a surface and
H
an ample divisor. We then define

\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\inf_{Z\in X^{[r]}}\varepsilon(H,Z)

and

\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r)=\sup_{Z\in X^{[r]}}\varepsilon(H,Z).

Remark 2.3
.

Clearly,

\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,1)=\varepsilon(H)

as all closed subschemes of dimension
0
and length
1
are closed points. Furthermore,

\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r)=\varepsilon(H,r),

see
Proposition

4.2
.

Cones and walls of the Hilbert scheme

In the following we want to explain the relation of Seshadri constants and Hilbert schemes. Therefore, we explain the wall-and-chamber decomposition of the movable cone of the Hilbert scheme of K3 surfaces, following
[
5
]
. The main result of loc.cit. is the computation of the birational behaviour of any moduli space of sheaves on a K3 surface. This then leads to the following wall-and-chamber decomposition for a Hilbert scheme
X^{[n]}

\Mov(X^{[n]})=\bigcup_{i\colon X\dashrightarrow\tilde{X}}i^{*}\Nef(\tilde{X}),

where the union runs over all non-trivial birational
K
-trivial models
\tilde{X}
, see
[
9
, Section 3.7]
.
To describe this decomposition, we need to introduce the lattice

\mathbb{Z}\oplus\Pic(S)\oplus\mathbb{Z}=\colon\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{alg}}(S)

with the intersection pairing

(a,D,b)\cdot(c,D^{\prime},d)=D.D^{\prime}-ad-cb.

Then, for
n>1
the Picard group

\Pic(X^{[n]})\cong(1,0,-n+1)^{\perp}\cong\Pic(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}B,

where
B=B^{[r]}=(1,0,n-1)
. Via this identification and
v=(1,0,-n+1)
, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 2.5

(Bayer–Macrì
[
5
, Theorem 12.1, 12.3]
)
.

The movable cone of
X^{[n]}
is cut out in
\mathrm{Pos}(X^{[n]})\otimes\mathbb{R}
by

•

s^{\perp}\cap v^{\perp}
, where
s\cdot s=-2

•

w^{\perp}\cap v^{\perp}
, where
w\cdot w=0
and
w\cdot v\in\{1,2\}
.

The walls in
\Mov(X^{[n]})
are given by
v^{\perp}\cap a^{\perp}
(when non-trivial) for all
a\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{alg}}(X)
with
a\cdot a\geq-2
and
0\leq a\cdot v\leq v\cdot v/2
.

In particular, if
\Pic(X)=\mathbb{Z}H
with
H.H=2d
and
H^{[n]}=(0,H,0)
, this yields:

Corollary 2.6

(Bayer–Macrì
[
5
, Proposition 13.1]
)
.

•

If
d=\frac{k^{2}}{h^{2}}(n-1)
, then

\Mov(X^{[n]})=\langle H^{[n]},H^{[n]}-\frac{k}{h}B\rangle.

•

Otherwise if the equation
(n-1)x^{2}-dy^{2}=1
has a solution
(x_{1},y_{1})
with
x_{1}>0
minimal and
y_{1}>0
, then

\Mov(X^{[n]})=\langle H^{[n]},H^{[n]}-\frac{dy_{1}}{x_{1}(n-1)}B\rangle.

•

Otherwise let
(x_{1},y_{1})
be a solution of
x^{2}-d(n-1)y^{2}=1
with

divisible by
n-1
and

\Mov(X^{[n]})=\langle H^{[n]},H^{[n]}-\frac{dy_{1}}{x_{1}}B\rangle.

.

4.
Cones of Nested Hilbert schemes

In this section we will show that the Seshadri constants are computed by either the nef cone or the effective cone of the nested Hilbert schemes.

For this section let
X
be a surface.
We start by linking ampleness on the blow ups of the underlying surface with ampleness on the nested Hilbert scheme:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a surface,
p_{r}\colon X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}

\lambda>0

Z\in X^{[r]}
the divisor
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E

is ample if and only if
H-\lambda E_{Z}
is ample on the blow up
\Bl_{Z}X
, where
E_{Z}
is the exceptional divisor.

Proof.

Let
p_{r}\colon X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}
be the canonical projection and let
\lambda>0
. For any
Z\in X^{[r]}
the fiber
p_{r}^{-1}(Z)
is the union of some projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}
and the blowup
\Bl_{Z}X
by
Remark

3.3
. By

Lemma

3.2
the restriction satisfies

(H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E)|_{\Bl_{Z}X}=H-\lambda E_{Z},

where
E_{Z}
is the exceptional divisor of the blowup. Moreover, the restriction to the projective spaces
(H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E)|_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}}=-\lambda E|_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{i}}}
is ample as
\lambda>0
. Thus, by
[
19
, Prop 1.2.16]
any line bundle as in the theorem is ample on the fiber if and only if it is ample on
\Bl_{Z}X
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For a surface
X
and an ample line bundle
H
we have

\varepsilon(H,r)=\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r).

Proof.

By the previous
Lemma

4.1
for any
\lambda<\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r)
the line bundle
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E
is ample on at least one fiber. Therefore openness of ampleness in families
[
19
, Prop 1.2.17]
shows that it is ample for the general
Z\in X^{[r]}
.
∎

The nef cone of the nested Hilbert scheme

The nef cone of the nested Hilbert scheme is directly be linked to the infimum Seshadri constant: Intuitively, the fibers of the morphism
X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}
parameterizes the surface blown up at
r
points and therefore, ampleness on the nested Hilbert scheme is linked to the ampleness of the fibers as follows:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a surface and
X^{[r,r+1]}
the nested Hilbert scheme for some
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. Then

\sup_{\lambda}\{\lambda>0\,|\,H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A\textup{ is ample for some ample }A\in\Pic(X^{[r]})\}=\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r).

Proof.

Let
p_{r}\colon X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}
be the canonical projection. By
Lemma

4.1
if
\lambda<\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)
the divisor
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E
is
p_{r}
-ample. By
[
19
, Prop 1.7.10]
tensoring with a positive enough ample line bundle
A\in\Pic X^{[r]}
we get that
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A
is ample.
On the other hand, let
\lambda>\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)
. Then by the Nakai-Moishezon criterion there exists a
Z\in X^{[r]}
and a curve
C\in\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}\,X
such that
(H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E)|_{\Bl_{Z}X}.C<0
as
E
restricts to the exceptional divisor on the blowup. Thus, now regarding the curve
C
as a curve in
X^{[r,r+1]}
via the inclusion
\Bl_{Z}X\subset X^{[r,r+1]}
we get

\left(H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A\right).C<0

for any divisor
A\in\Pic X^{[r]}
, as
A.C=0
as
C
lies in a fiber of
p_{r}\colon X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}
.
∎

The effective cone of the nested Hilbert scheme

Let
X
be a surface and
H
be an ample divisor.
In this section we will link the effective cone of nested Hilbert schemes to the supremum Seshadri constant
\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r)
. To do so, we recall the following lemma from
[
24
]
: A curve
C
is Nagata submaximal for some
r
with respect to
H
if

\frac{C.H}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}}<\sqrt{\frac{H.H}{r}}

for
r
points with multiplicity
m_{i}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
. Then, the divisor class of the strict transform on the blowup of
r
very general points of a submaximal Nagata curve is of the form

C=D-m(E_{1}+\ldots+E_{r})-kE_{i}

for some fixed
D\in\Pic X
and any
C_{\sigma}=D-m(E_{\sigma(1)}+\ldots+E_{\sigma(r)})-kE_{\sigma(i)}
is effective for any permutation
\sigma\in S_{r}
.

Proof.

See
[
24
, Cor 2.8 and its proof]
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We remark the following implications of the corollary above, as already noted by Dumnicki–Küronya–Maclean–Szemberg
in
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
: If
X
is a surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
and
\Bl_{r}X\to X
is the blowup of
r
very general points with exceptional divisors
E_{i}
then

H-\frac{H^{2}}{r\varepsilon(H,r)}\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\in\partial\Eff(\Bl_{r}X)

is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective and contained in the boundary of the pseudo-effective cone.

If there are no submaximal Nagata curves the statement is clear, thus we assume that there is a submaximal curve
C_{i}=D-m(E_{1}+\ldots+E_{r})-kE_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq r
,
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
D\in\Pic X
that computes the Seshadri constant. By the corollary above also
C_{j}
is effective for all
1\leq j\leq r
and therefore, the divisor

\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}=rD-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}

is effective. As
H-\varepsilon(H,r)\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
is nef and
(H-\varepsilon(H,r)\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}).\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}=0
this implies that
\sum C_{i}
is not big and thus, the claim follows.

With this we can construct a divisor that parameterizes the submaximal Nagata curves: Recall that
E\subset X^{[r,r+1]}
is the exceptional divisor of the blow up
X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}\times X
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
be a surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
. We have

\frac{H^{2}}{r\varepsilon(H,r)}=\sup_{\lambda}\{\lambda\,|\,H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A\textup{ is }\mathbb{Q}\textup{-effective for some ample }A\in\Pic X^{[r]}\}.

Proof.

Let
\Bl_{r}X\to X
be the blow up at
r
very general points.
At first, we will show that if
H-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
is effective for some
\lambda>0
, then also
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A
is effective for some ample
A\in\Pic X^{[r]}
. This together with
Remark

4.5
shows the inequality LHS
\leq
RHS in the theorem.

For
p\colon X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r]}
we see that the divisor
D=H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E\in\Pic X^{[r,r+1]}
restricts to the divisor
H-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
on the fibers of
p
which are isomorphic to the blowup of
r
distinct points. Thus, by assumption
D
restricted to the very general fiber is effective.

Therefore,
p_{*}\mathcal{O}(D)
is non-zero and by tensoring with some ample
A
we get

H^{0}(X^{[r,r+1]},D\otimes p^{*}A)=H^{0}(X^{[r]},p_{*}\mathcal{O}(D)\otimes A)\neq 0,

which gives the claim.

To show the other inequality, we again observe that if
H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A
is effective for some
A\in\Pic X^{[r]}
, then the restriction to the very general fiber of
p
is again effective: Otherwise the divisor would either contain the very general fiber or not meet it at all: But these two cases cannot happen, as
D
restricted to a fiber is non-trivial. Thus,

D|_{\Bl_{r}X}=H-\lambda\sum E_{i}

is effective for the very general blow up. That is,
H-\frac{H^{2}}{r\lambda}\sum E_{i}
is not ample and we get that
\varepsilon(H,r)\leq\frac{H^{2}}{r\lambda}
. In other words,

\frac{H^{2}}{r\varepsilon(H,r)}\geq\lambda.

∎

5.
Examples of infimum Seshadri constants and a question of Ryan–Yang

An example for rational surfaces and K3 surfaces

Contrary to the case of Hilbert schemes the cones of nested Hilbert schemes are not well understood. However, Ryan–Yang
[
23
]
computed the nef cone of
X^{[r,r+1]}
for some choices of
X
and
r
. Thereby we can calculate the infimum Seshadri constants in these cases.

Example 5.1
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the projective plane and
H
a hyperplane. In
[
23
, Prop. 5.2]
it was shown that for
r\geq 2
the nef cone of the nested Hilbert scheme for
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is spanned by

H^{\textup{diff}},H^{[r]},H^{[r]}-\frac{1}{r-1}B^{[r]},H^{[r+1]}-\frac{1}{r}B^{[r+1]}.

This shows that
\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon(H)
.

Example 5.2
.

For
e>0
let
\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))
. Denote by
C
the unique section with
C.C=-e
and let
F
be a fiber.
In
[
23
, Prop. 5.3]
it was shown that the nef cone of the nested Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
is generated by

\displaystyle F^{\textup{diff}},C^{\textup{diff}}+eF^{\textup{diff}},C^{[r]}+eF^{[r]},F^{[r]},C^{[r]}+(e+1)F^{[r]}-\frac{1}{r}B^{[r]},

\displaystyle C^{[r+1]}+(e+1)F^{[r+1]}-\frac{1}{r+1}B^{[r+1]},

when
r\geq 2
, see also Bertram–Coskun
[
6
, Theorem 1]
for the nef cone of
\mathbb{F}_{e}^{[r]}
.
For an ample
H=aC+bF
this yields

\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\frac{1}{r}\min(a,b-ae)=\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon(H).

Remark 5.3
.

The behaviour experienced in the examples above is expected in the following sense: If
\varepsilon(H)
is attained by a smooth curve, we get for the infimum over distinct points

\inf_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in X}{\varepsilon}(H,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon(H),

as a simple calculation shows that
\varepsilon(H,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\geq\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon(H)
for any collection of distinct points
x_{i}\in X
and any surface.

Example 5.4
.

Let
X
be a very general K3 surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
.
By Ryan–Yang
[
23
]
the nef cone of
X^{[r,r+1]}
is spanned by

H^{\textup{diff}},H^{[r]},H^{[r]}-\frac{H^{2}}{r+\frac{H^{2}}{2}}B^{[r]},H^{[r+1]}-\frac{H^{2}}{r+1+\frac{H^{2}}{2}}B^{[r+1]},

when
r\geq\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1
. Thus, for these choices of
r
we have

\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\frac{H^{2}}{r+1+\frac{H^{2}}{2}}.

In fact, the Seshadri constant is computed by a nodal rational curve
R
in the primitive linear system
|H|
: These curves possess
\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1
nodes, i.e. choosing these nodes and
\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1-r
remaining smooth points gives the claim.

It turns out that for
r\gg 0
this is the general behaviour, i.e. the class of the curve computing the Seshadri constant is minimal: We need to introduce the slightly altered Seshadri constant by allowing only distinct points:

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\inf_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}}\varepsilon(H,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X
be a surface and
H
an ample divisor. Then there is an
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a curve
C\subset X
of minimal degree
C.H=\min\{C^{\prime}.H\,|\,C^{\prime}\subset X\textup{ a curve}\}
such that

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\frac{C.H}{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{0}}m_{i}+r-r_{0}}

for all
r\gg 0
, where
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
are the multiplicities of
C
at
r_{0}
distinct points
x_{i}\in C
.

Proof.

Let
K_{X}
be the canonical divisor and let
D\subset X
be a curve such that
D.H
is minimal, i.e.
D.H=\min\{C^{\prime}.H\,|\,C^{\prime}\subset X\textup{ a curve}\}
. Suppose there is an irreducible curve
C\subset X
with multiplicities
m_{i}
at
r
distinct points
x_{i}\in C
such that

\frac{C.H}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}}<\frac{D.H}{r}.

We want to show that
C.H=D.H
holds if
r\gg 0
. For that we observe that by assumption

r\frac{C.H}{D.H}<\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}.

Denote by
\Bl_{r}X\to X
the blow up of
X
in the points
x_{i}\in C
with
E_{i}
the exceptional divisors and let
\tilde{C}\subset\Bl_{r}X
be the strict transform of
C
.
Then,
K_{\Bl_{r}X}=K_{X}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
and
\tilde{C}=C-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}
by
[
16
, Chapter V.3]
.
Hence, by the adjunction formula the arithmetic genus
p_{a}
of
\tilde{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle 2p_{a}(\tilde{C})-2

\displaystyle=(K_{\Bl_{r}X}+\tilde{C}).\tilde{C}

\displaystyle=C^{2}+K_{X}.C-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}+K_{X}.C-\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\right)^{2}}{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}+K_{X}.C-\frac{C.H}{D.H}\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}+K_{X}.C-r(\frac{C.H}{D.H}-1)\frac{C.H}{D.H}.

We will show that the latter is
<-2
if
C.H>D.H
and
r\gg 0
.
For the
K_{X}.C
term we observe that
\max_{\{C\textup{ is effective}\}}\frac{C.K_{X}}{C.H}<\infty
as
H
is ample, i.e.
K_{X}.C\leq c\,C.H
for a constant
c
only depending on the surface and
H
.

By the Hodge Index theorem
C^{2}\leq\frac{(C.H)^{2}}{H^{2}}
.
Thus, with
r^{\prime}=\frac{r}{(D.H)^{2}}
we get

\displaystyle C^{2}-r(\frac{C.H}{D.H}-1)\frac{C.H}{D.H}

\displaystyle=C^{2}-r^{\prime}(C.H-D.H)C.H

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(C.H)^{2}}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime}(C.H-D.H)C.H

\displaystyle=C.H(C.H(\frac{1}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime})+r^{\prime}D.H)

\displaystyle\leq C.H((D.H+1)(\frac{1}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime})+r^{\prime}D.H)

\displaystyle=CH(\frac{D.H}{H^{2}}+\frac{1}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime}).

Putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle 2p_{a}(\tilde{C})-2

\displaystyle\leq K_{X}.C+CH(\frac{D.H}{H^{2}}+\frac{1}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq C.H(c+\frac{D.H}{H^{2}}+\frac{1}{H^{2}}-r^{\prime})\ll 0

for
r\gg 0
independent of the curve
C
, a contradiction.

As we observed before,
2p_{a}(C)-2=K_{X}.C+C.C
is bounded by a constant (depending only on the surface and
H
) times
(C.H)^{2}
. Thus, there is a maximum
r_{0}
of singular points among all curves with
C.H
minimal. Choosing a curve
C_{0}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r_{0}}m_{i}

is maximal, where the
m_{i}
are multiplicities at (not necessarily singular) points, this curve
C_{0}
indeed computes the Seshadri constant
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\textup{inf}}(X,H,r)
for all
r\gg 0
with

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\textup{inf}}(H,r)=\frac{C_{0}.H}{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{0}}(m_{i}-1)+r}.

∎

An answer to a question of Ryan–Yang

In the paper
[
23
]
Ryan–Yang posed the following question after observing that for
r\gg 0
the nested Hilbert schemes
\mathbb{P}^{2[r,r+1]}
are not log-Fano:

Question
.

For which
r\gg 0
is the nested Hilbert scheme
\mathbb{P}^{2[r,r+1]}
a Mori-Dream-Space?

We will answer this for the more general case of
X
being a surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
with the assumption of the Nagata–Biran-
Conjecture

1.3
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
X
be a surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}}{r}}\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose

\varepsilon(H,r)=\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}}{r}}.

Then
X^{[r,r+1]}
is not a Mori-Dream-Space.

Proof.

The pseudo-effective cone of a Mori-Dream-Space is rational polyhedral, see
[
17
, Prop. 1.11]
. This contradicts the assumption
\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}}{r}}\notin\mathbb{Q}
together with
Theorem

4.6
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

The spaces
\mathbb{P}^{2[r,r+1]}
are not Mori-Dream-Spaces for all perfect squares
r=s^{2}>9
.

Proof.

Let
\Bl_{r}\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the blow up of
r
very general points with exceptional divisors
E_{i}
.
In his original paper
[
21
]
Nagata shows, that
H-\sqrt{\frac{1}{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-effective and thus
\varepsilon(H,r)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{r}}
. By
Theorem

4.6
we have

\sup_{\lambda}\{\lambda\,|\,H^{\textup{diff}}-\lambda E+A\textup{ is }\mathbb{Q}\textup{-effective for some ample }A\in\Pic X^{[r]}\}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{r}}=\frac{1}{s}.

On the other hand, by
[
17
, Prop. 1.11]
the pseudo-effective cone of a Mori-Dream-Space is generated by finitely many effective divisors. Therefore, there exists an effective divisor
D
that is a rational multiple of
H^{\textup{diff}}-{\frac{1}{s}}E+A
for some
A\in\Pic X^{[r]}
. However, this divisor would restrict to an effective divisor on the blowup
\Bl_{r}X\subset X^{[r,r+1]}
at
r
very general points, a contradiction to Nagata’s result.
∎

6.
Some new bounds on Seshadri constants

In this section we will provide new bounds on one point Seshadri constants. The strategy is to embed
\Bl_{x}X
into
X^{[r]}
for some large
r
and get some bounds via the nef cone of the Hilbert scheme which are computed in
[
8
]
via Bridgeland stability conditions. For some fixed
n
denote
N=\binom{n}{2}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a surface,
x\in X
a point and
n>0
an integer. Then the blowup of the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}
naturally leads to an embedding
\Bl_{x}X\subset X^{[N,N+1]}
such that
E,H^{\textup{diff}}\in\Pic X^{[N,N+1]}
restrict to
nE_{x},H
on
\Bl_{x}X
respectively, where
E_{x}
is the exceptional divisor.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.2
we can embed
\Bl_{\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}}X\subset X^{[N,N+1]}
, as
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}
defines a closed subscheme in
X
with support
x
of length
N
.

Again, by
Lemma

3.2
the exceptional divisor
E
of the nested Hilbert scheme restricts to the exceptional divisor
E_{\mathfrak{m}^{n}_{x}}
on the blowup
\Bl_{\mathfrak{m}^{n}_{x}}X
.
On the other hand, the natural isomorphism

p\colon\Bl_{\mathfrak{m}_{x}}X\to\Bl_{\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}}X,

satisfies
p^{*}E_{\mathfrak{m}^{n}_{x}}=nE_{x}
, where
E_{x}
is the exceptional divisor of
\Bl_{x}X
, giving the claim.

The statement for
H^{\textup{diff}}
and
H
directly follows from
Lemma

3.2
and the fact
p^{*}H=H
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
X
be a surface with Picard group
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
with effective generator
H
such that
K_{X}=aH
with
a\geq 0
. Then

\varepsilon(H)\geq\frac{2H^{2}n}{(a+1)H^{2}+n(n+1)+2}

for all integers
n
such that
n^{2}+n\geq H^{2}
.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Prop 4.4.]
the nef cone of the Hilbert scheme
X^{[r+1]}
contains the cone spanned by

H^{[r+1]},\left(\frac{a+1}{2}+\frac{r+1}{H^{2}}\right)H^{[r+1]}+B^{[r+1]}

for
r+1\geq\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1
. Therefore, this applies to our case when
r=N
. Pulling back the nef classes via the morphism
X^{[r,r+1]}\to X^{[r+1]}

Proposition

6.1
gives that

\left(\frac{a+1}{2}+\frac{N+1}{H^{2}}\right)H+nE

is nef on
\Bl_{x}X
for any
x\in X
, giving the claim.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface with Picard group
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
. Then

\varepsilon(X,H)\geq\frac{2H^{2}\alpha}{H^{2}+\alpha(\alpha+1)+2}.

where
\alpha=\left\lceil\sqrt{H^{2}+2}\right\rceil
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
X
be a surface of general type with Picard group
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
,
K_{X}=aH
and effective generator
H
. Then

\varepsilon(X,H)\geq\frac{2H^{2}n}{(a+1)H^{2}+n(n+1)+2}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
X
be a surface of general type with Picard group
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
and
p_{g}\neq 0
. Then

\varepsilon(X,H)\geq\frac{2bH^{2}\alpha}{(ab+b^{2})H^{2}+\alpha(\alpha+1)+2}

for
\alpha=\left\lceil\sqrt{(ab+b^{2})H^{2}+2}\right\rceil
, where
K_{X}=aH
and
b
is minimal with
bH
effective.

Proof.

This follows as before, but with
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
which takes the case into account that

might not be effective.
∎

Questions

As we have seen in the previous chapter, embedding the blowup of a surface into some Hilbert schemes makes it possible to tackle problems regarding Seshadri constants. We will give some questions regarding this topic:

Question
.

What is the relation between the Seshadri constant
\varepsilon(X,H)
of an ample line bundle
H
on a surface
X
and the movable and nef cone of the Hilbert scheme
X^{[3]}
?

Remark 7.1
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface with Picard group
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
. If a curve
C\subset X
computes the Seshadri constant at a point
x\in X
, we have the following exact sequence on the blowup at the point
x

0\to\mathcal{O}(-\tilde{C}-2E)\to\mathcal{O}(-2E)\to\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}(-2E)\to 0,

where
\tilde{C}
is the strict transform.
This induces an exact triangle

\mathbb{R}p_{*}\mathcal{O}(-\tilde{C}-2E)\to\mathfrak{m}^{2}\to p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}(-2E)

in
D^{b}(X)
. Is this a destabilising sequence of
\mathfrak{m}^{2}
for some Bridgeland stability condition on
D^{b}(X)
? If so, the corresponding wall, would induce a movable divisor with class
H^{[3]}-\frac{pH^{2}}{2m+1-p_{a}(\tilde{C})}B
, lining up with some of our observations.

On the other hand, we have seen that there is also a numerical connection between a conjectured bound of
\varepsilon(H,1)
and
X^{[3]}
in
Conjecture

2.4
. This poses the question if it is possible to prove the latter with Bridgeland stability methods or at least answer the following weaker questions:

Question
.

Suppose
X
is a K3 surface with
\Pic X=\mathbb{Z}H
and
H^{2}
not a perfect square. Is

\varepsilon(H,1)\geq\frac{q}{p}H^{2}

for at least one solution of
2p^{2}-\frac{H^{2}}{2}q^{2}=1
or
p^{2}-H^{2}q^{2}=1
?

Question
.

Is
\varepsilon_{\textup{inf}}(X,H)=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\textup{inf}}(X,H)
for all
r\gg 0
or even
r>0
?

It might also be interesting to generalise the technique of
Proposition

6.1
to several points to gain an understanding of
\varepsilon(H,r)
. That is, for any integral zero-dimensional subscheme
Z\in X^{[r]}
with ideal sheaf
I_{Z}
consider the blowup

\Bl_{I_{Z}}X\cong\Bl_{I_{Z}^{n}}X\subset X^{[r\frac{n(n+1)}{2},r\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+1]}.

Restricting the nef cone as in
Proposition

6.1
gives us new bounds for
\varepsilon_{\textup{sup}}(H,r)
.
However, as expected this does not yield good results for
\varepsilon(H,r)
for
r\gg 0
. We therefore turn our attention to another invariant of those Hilbert schemes.

Let
X
be a K3 surface with Picard rank
1
. Then restricting the non-trivial ray of the movable cone of
X^{[r\frac{n(n+1)}{2}]}
to
\Bl_{I_{Z}^{n}}X\cong\Bl_{I_{Z}}X
yields a divisor
H-\alpha_{n}\sum E_{i}
such that
\alpha_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\sqrt{\frac{H^{2}}{r}}
. With regards to the Nagata–Biran conjecture this poses the question:

Question
.

For which
n>0
does the blowup
\Bl_{I_{Z}^{n}}X
of the very general
Z
not meet the stable base locus of any effective divisor
D\in\Pic X^{[r\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+1]}
in a positive dimensional subset?

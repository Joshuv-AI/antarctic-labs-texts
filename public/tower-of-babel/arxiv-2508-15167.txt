Multiple nodal solutions to a scalar field equation with double-power nonlinearity and zero mass at infinity
Mónica Clapp, Carlos Culebro

Abstract
We consider the nonlinear elliptic equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = f ⁡ ( u ) , u ∈ D 0 1 , 2 ​ ( Ω ) , -\Delta u+V(x)u=f(u),\qquad u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega), in an exterior domain Ω \Omega of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , where V V is a scalar potential that decays to zero at infinity and the nonlinearity f f is subcritical at infinity and supercritical near the origin. Under weak symmetry assumptions, we provide conditions that guarantee that this problem has a prescribed number of sign-changing solutions. In particular, we show that in dimensions N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 there are numerous examples of exterior domains with finite symmetries in which the problem has a predetermined number of nodal solutions. Keywords: Scalar field equations, zero mass, superlinear, double-power nonlinearity, nodal solutions, variational methods. MSC 2010: 35Q55, 35B06, 35J20.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the existence of multiple sign-changing solutions to the problem

-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(u),\qquad u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),

(1.1)

in a domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, where
V
is a scalar potential that decays to zero at infinity and the nonlinearity
f
is subcritical at infinity and supercritical near the origin. The space
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
is the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in the Sobolev space
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
, equipped with its usual norm

\|u\|:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(1.2)

and
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent.

Such equations arise, for instance, in some particle physics problems related to the non-Abelian gauge theory underlying strong interaction, called quantum chromodynamics or QCD. Their solutions lead to some special solutions of the pure Yang-Mills equations via the ’t Hooft Ansatz; see
[
16
]
.

In their seminal paper
[
7
]
Berestycki and Lions showed that, when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
V=0
, this problem has a ground state solution which is positive, radially symmetric and decreasing in the radial direction.

For
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
Badiale and Rolando established the existence of a positive radial solution to (
1.1
) when
V
is radial
[
1
]
. Without assuming any symmetries on
V
, Benci, Grisanti and Micheletti showed in
[
5
]
that, under suitable hypotheses, (
1.1
) has a positive ground state if
V\leq 0
and that it has no ground state if
V\geq 0
and
V\neq 0
. The existence of a positive bound state was established in
[
10
]
whenever
V
decays to zero at a suitable rate and the limit problem (i.e., (
1.1
) with
V\equiv 0
) has a unique positive solution.

In an exterior domain
\Omega
the existence of a positive solution to the problem (
1.1
) with
V=0
was first established by Benci and Micheletti in
[
6
]
for domains whose complement has a sufficiently small diameter, and was then extended to general exterior domains by Khatib and Maia in
[
17
]
. The existence of multiple positive solutions in the complement of a ball, the number of which increases as the radius of the ball increases, was shown in
[
11
]
.

For
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
V=0
there are also some results on sign-changing solutions. Taking advantage of the symmetries that (
1.1
) presents in this case, Mederski established the existence of a non-radial sign-changing solution if
N\geq 4
and of infinitely many such solutions if, in addition,
N\neq 5
; see
[
18
,
19
]
. This last result is based on the fact that in such dimensions there exist groups
G
of linear isometries that admit an involution and have the property that the
G
-orbit of each point
x\neq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
has infinite cardinality. As shown by Bartsch and Willem in
[
4
]
, this type of symmetries produce a sign change by construction.

A delicate aspect when approaching problem (
1.1
) using variational methods is the lack of compactness of the energy functional. Considering symmetries with infinite
G
-orbits restores compactness and allows applying standard variational methods to obtain infinitely many solutions. On the other hand, when any group action on the domain has finite
G
-orbits, the lack of compactness prevails and such methods cannot be applied. To our knowledge, there are no results on multiple solutions in such case.

The main objective of this work is to establish the existence of
multiple sign-changing solutions
in exterior domains that are invariant under a group
G
of linear isometries and
have finite
G
-orbits
. Recall that
\Omega
is an exterior domain if its complement is bounded (possibly empty).

We make the following assumptions about the potential and the nonlinearity.

(V_{1})

V\in L^{N/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of

\Omega

For

\langle u,v\rangle_{V}:=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+Vuv)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{V}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{2}+Vu^{2}).

Assumption

is of class

and its derivative at

I_{V}^{\prime}(u)v=\langle u,v\rangle_{V}-\int_{\Omega}f(u)v,\qquad v\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

The critical points of

are the solutions to the problem (
1.1
).

For
g\in G
and

. Since

and

are
G
-invariant,

is well defined and
\langle gu,gv\rangle_{V}=\langle u,v\rangle_{V}

I_{V}
is
G
-invariant, i.e.,

, and, by the principle of symmetric criticality

, the

-invariant solutions to (

D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):gu=u\text{ for all }g\in G\},

which is the space of
G
-invariant functions in

. Abusing notation we write

to

Recall that a sequence

D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(a)

If
\Omega
is bounded, then
u_{k}\to 0
strongly in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(b)

If
\Omega
is an exterior domain and
(u_{k})
does not converge strongly to
0
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
then, after passing to a subsequence, there exist a sequence
(\zeta_{k})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, a closed subgroup
K
of finite index in
G
, and a nontrivial solution
w
to the limit problem (
1.4
) such that

G_{\zeta_{k}}=K\text{ \ for all \ }k\in\mathbb{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c\geq[G:K]\,I_{\infty}(w),

where
I_{\infty}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
,

I_{\infty}(w):=\frac{1}{2}\|w\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(w),

is the energy functional of the limit problem (
1.4
).

Proof.

Assume that
(u_{k})
does not converge strongly to
0
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then there exist
C_{0}>0
and a subsequence such that
\|u_{k}\|\geq C_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. As
(u_{k})
is bounded in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
I^{\prime}_{0}(u_{k})\to 0
, we have that

o(1)=I_{0}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}=\|u_{k}\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f(u_{k})u_{k}.

Hence,

0<C_{0}^{2}\leq\|u_{k}\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}f(u_{k})u_{k}+o(1),

and, by Lemma
2.4
, there exist
\delta>0
and a sequence
(y_{k})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\int_{B_{1}(y_{k})}|u_{n}|^{2}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{1}(y)}|u_{k}|^{2}\geq\delta.

For the sequence
(y_{k})
, we fix a sequence
(\zeta_{k})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a closed subgroup
K
of
G
with the properties stated in Lemma
2.5
. In particular, there exist
g_{k}\in G
and
C>0
such that
|g_{k}^{-1}\zeta_{k}-y_{k}|=\mathrm{dist}(G\zeta_{k},y_{k})\leq C
. As
u_{k}
is
G
-invariant, we get

\int_{B_{C+1}(\zeta_{k})}|u_{k}|^{2}\geq\int_{B_{1}(g_{k}y_{k})}|u_{k}|^{2}=\int_{B_{1}(y_{k})}|u_{k}|^{2}\geq\delta>0.

(2.2)

Set
w_{k}:=u_{k}(\cdot+\zeta_{k})
. Since
(w_{k})
is bounded in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, passing to a subsequence, we have that

w_{k}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
w_{k}\to w
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
w_{k}\to w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
The inequality (
2.2
) yields

\int_{B_{C+1}(0)}|w_{k}|^{2}\geq\delta>0.

Therefore,
w\neq 0
. Then, as
u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, an easy argument shows that
|\zeta_{k}|\to\infty
.

The inequality (
2.2
) implies that
\mathrm{dist}(\zeta_{k},\Omega)<C+1
for all
k
. Thus, if
\Omega
is bounded, then
(\zeta_{k})
is bounded and we obtain a contradiction. This proves
(a)
.

Assume now that
\Omega
is an exterior domain. Given
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we set
\varphi_{k}(x):=\varphi(x-\zeta_{k})
. Since
|\zeta_{k}|\to\infty
, we have that
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
for
k
large enough. Then, using Lemma
2.3
(b)
one sees that

I^{\prime}_{\infty}(w)\varphi=I^{\prime}_{\infty}(w_{k})\varphi+o(1)=I^{\prime}_{0}(u_{k})\varphi_{k}+o(1)=o(1).

Hence,
w
is a nontrivial solution to the limit problem (
1.4
).

Assume there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
such that
|g_{i}\zeta_{k}-g_{j}\zeta_{k}|\to\infty
if
i\neq j
. Then, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

g_{j}w_{k}-\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k}+g_{j}\zeta_{k})\rightharpoonup g_{j}w\quad\text{weakly in \ }D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

with the sum being
0
if
j=m
. Lemma
2.3
(c)
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F\Big(g_{j}w_{k}-\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k}+g_{j}\zeta_{k})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F\Big(g_{j}w_{k}-\sum_{i=j}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k}+g_{j}\zeta_{k})\Big)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(g_{j}w)+o(1).

Since
u_{k}
is
G
-invariant, performing the change of variable
x\mapsto x-g_{j}\zeta_{k}
we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F\Big(u_{k}-\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k})\Big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F\Big(u_{k}-\sum_{i=j}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k})\Big)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(w)+o(1).

Iterating this identity for
j=1,\ldots,m
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{k})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{k}-z_{k})+m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(w)+o(1),

(2.3)

where

z_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(g_{i}w)(\cdot-g_{i}\zeta_{k}).

Similarly, using statements
(d)
and
(a)
of Lemma
2.3
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{k})u_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{k}-z_{k})(u_{k}-z_{k})+m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(w)w+o(1).

(2.4)

and

\|u_{k}\|^{2}=\|u_{k}-z_{k}\|^{2}+m\|w\|^{2}+o(1).

(2.5)

Since
w
solves (
1.4
), from (
2.5
) and (
2.4
) we get

o(1)=I^{\prime}_{0}(u_{k})u_{k}=\|u_{k}-z_{k}\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u_{k}-z_{k})(u_{k}-z_{k})+o(1),

and from assumption
(f_{2})
we derive

\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-z_{k}\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{k}-z_{k})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}f(u_{k}-z_{k})(u_{k}-z_{k})-F(u_{k}-z_{k})\Big)+o(1)\geq 0+o(1).

This inequality, combined with (
2.5
) and (
2.3
), yields

c+o(1)=I_{0}(u_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-z_{k}\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{k}-z_{k})+mI_{\infty}(w)+o(1)\geq mI_{\infty}(w)+o(1).

(2.6)

Therefore,
m
cannot be arbitrarily large. It follows from Lemma
2.5
that
[G:K]<\infty
. Setting
m:=[G:K]
and passing to the limit in (
2.6
) we obtain

c\geq[G:K]\,I_{\infty}(w).

This completes the proof of
(b)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
(u_{k})
be a Palais-Smale sequence for
I_{V}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
at the level

. By Lemma

,

). Hence, by

Set

. Then

and statements

and

If

2.6
(a)

.

.
∎

3
A variational principle for sign-changing solutions

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of
O(N)
. We assume throughout this section that
\Omega
and
V
are
G
-invariant and that assumptions
(V_{1})
and
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
hold true.

3.1
The structure of the Nehari manifold

The nontrivial
G
-invariant critical points of the functional
I_{V}
belong to the set

\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:u\neq 0,\ I^{\prime}_{V}(u)u=0\}.

Define

c(\Omega)^{G}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}}I_{V}(u).

Before stating the properties of
\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
, we note that assumptions
(f_{1})
and
(f_{2})
guarantee that
I_{V}
has the mountain pass geometry in bounded domains.

Lemma 3.1
.

(a)

There exist
r>0
and
a>0
such that
I_{V}(u)>0
if
\|u\|_{V}\leq r
and
u\neq 0
, and
I_{V}(u)\geq a
if
\|u\|_{V}=r
.

(b)

If
\Lambda
is a bounded
G
-invariant open subset of
\Omega
and
W\subset D^{1,2}_{0}(\Lambda)^{G}
is a finite dimensional linear subspace, then there exists
R>r
such that
I_{V}(w)\leq 0
for every
w\in W
with
\|w\|_{V}\geq R
.

Proof.

(a):
It follows from
(f_{1})
that
F(s)\leq A_{1}|s|^{2^{*}}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, using Sobolev’s inequality,

I_{V}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-A_{1}|u|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-C\|u\|_{V}^{2^{*}}\qquad\text{for all \ }u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}

and some
C>0
. This implies
(a)
.

(b):
It follows from
(f_{2})
that
F(s)\geq 0
and
F(s)\geq a_{1}|s|^{\theta}-a_{2}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and some constants
a_{1},a_{2}>0
. Since
\Lambda
is bounded, we get

I_{V}(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-\int_{\Lambda}F(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-a_{1}\int_{\Lambda}|u|^{\theta}+a_{2}|\Lambda|\qquad\text{for all \ }u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G},

and, as
W
has finite dimension,

I_{V}(w)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{V}^{2}-\widehat{C}\|w\|_{V}^{\theta}+a_{2}|\Lambda|\qquad\text{for all \ }w\in W

and some
\widehat{C}>0
. Since
\theta>2
, this implies
(b)
.
∎

A subset
\mathcal{Y}
of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
will be called
symmetric
if
-u\in\mathcal{Y}
for every
u\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

The exists
\varrho>0
such that
\|u\|\geq\varrho
for every
u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
is a closed
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-submanifold of codimension
1
of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
, called the
Nehari manifold
.

(iii)

\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
is a natural constraint for the functional
I_{V}
.

(iv)

c(\Omega)^{G}>0
.

(v)

If
u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
and
u\neq 0
, then there exists
t_{u}\in(0,\infty)
such that
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
.

(vi)

If
u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
, then
t_{u}=1
and the function
t\mapsto I_{V}(tu)
is strictly increasing in
[0,1)
and strictly decreasing in
(1,\infty)
. In particular,

I_{V}(u)=\max_{t>0}I_{V}(tu).

(vii)

\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
is symmetric.

Proof.

The proof of items
(i)-(iv)
is given in
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
.

(v):
Given
u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
and
u\neq 0
, consider the function
\sigma_{u}(t):=I_{V}(tu)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
. It follows from Lemma
3.1
(a)
that
\sigma_{u}(t_{0})\geq a>0
for some
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Now fix a bounded
G
-invariant open subset
\Lambda
of
\Omega
such that
u\neq 0
in
\Lambda
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
(b)
we have

I_{V}(tu)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|tu\|_{V}^{2}-a_{1}\int_{\Lambda}|tu|^{\theta}+a_{2}|\Lambda|=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}\Big)t^{2}-\Big(a_{1}\int_{\Lambda}|u|^{\theta}\Big)t^{\theta}+a_{2}|\Lambda|\qquad\text{for all \ }t\in[0,\infty).

Hence,
\sigma_{u}(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Therefore,
\sigma_{u}
attains its maximum at some
t_{u}\in(0,\infty)
. As a consequence,

0=t_{u}\sigma_{u}^{\prime}(t_{u})=\|t_{u}u\|_{V}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f(t_{u}u)t_{u}u,

i.e.,
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
. This proves
(v)
.

(vi):
If
t_{0}\in(0,\infty)
is a critical point of
\sigma_{u}
, then, by
(f_{2})
,

t_{0}^{2}\sigma_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})=\|t_{0}u\|_{V}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(t_{0}u)(t_{0}u)^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(f(t_{0}u)t_{0}u-f^{\prime}(t_{0}u)(t_{0}u)^{2})<0.

Hence,
\sigma_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})<0
. This shows that every critical point of
\sigma_{u}
is a strict local maximum. Therefore,
t_{u}
is the only critical point and statement
(vi)
follows.

(vii)
is an immediate consequence of assumption
(f_{3})
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous lemma, the complement of the Nehari manifold is the disjoint union of two open symmetric sets

D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}\smallsetminus\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}=\mathcal{B}_{0}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\infty},

(3.1)

with
\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\{tu:u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G},\ t\in[0,1)\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}:=\{tu:u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G},\ t\in(1,\infty)\}
, and

I_{V}(u)>0\quad\text{for every \ }u\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\smallsetminus\{0\}.

(3.2)

The
G
-invariant sign-changing critical points of
I_{V}
belong to the set

\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}:=\{u\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:u^{+}\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\text{ and }u^{-}\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\},

where
u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}:=\min\{u,0\}
. It has the following properties.

Lemma 3.3
.

(i)

\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
is closed and symmetric.

(ii)

The complement of
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
in
\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
has two connected components
\mathcal{U}
and
-\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

(i):
It is clear that
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
is closed and that
u\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
iff
-u\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
.

(ii):
Let

\Psi(u):=\|u\|^{2}_{V}-\int_{\Omega}f(u)u.

The complement of
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
in
\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
is the union of the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{+}:=\{w\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:w\geq 0\}\cup\{w\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:\Psi(w^{+})<0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{-}:=\{w\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:w\leq 0\}\cup\{w\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}:\Psi(w^{+})>0\}.

As in
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
one sees that
\mathcal{W}^{+}
and
\mathcal{W}^{-}
are open and connected. Note that, for every
w\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
,

\|w^{+}\|^{2}_{V}+\|w^{-}\|^{2}_{V}=\|w\|^{2}_{V}=\int_{\Omega}f(w)w=\int_{w>0}f(w)w+\int_{w<0}f(w)w=\int_{\Omega}f(w^{+})w^{+}+\int_{\Omega}f(w^{-})w^{-}.

Therefore,

\Psi(w^{+})<0\text{ \ iff \ }\Psi(w^{-})>0.

It follows from assumption
(f_{3})
, that
\Psi(w^{-})=\Psi(-w^{-})=\Psi((-w)^{+})
. This shows that
w\in\mathcal{W}^{+}
iff
-w\in\mathcal{W}^{-}
, that is,
\mathcal{W}^{-}=-\mathcal{W}^{+}
.
∎

3.2
A mountain pass theorem for sign-changing solutions

Let
u\in D^{1,2}(\Omega)^{G}
. The gradient of
I_{V}
at
u
is
\nabla I_{V}(u)=u-Q(u)
where
Q(u)
is the unique element in
D^{1,2}(\Omega)^{G}
such that

\langle Q(u),v\rangle_{V}=\int_{\Omega}f(u)v\qquad\text{for all \ }v\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}.

(3.3)

Consider the negative gradient flow
\eta:\mathcal{G}\to D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
of
I_{V}
, defined by

\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\eta(t,u)=-\nabla I_{V}(\eta(t,u)),\\
\eta(0,u)=u,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{G}:=\{(t,u):u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G},\ 0\leq t<T(u)\}
and
T(u)\in(0,\infty]
is the maximal existence time for the trajectory
t\to\eta(t,u)
. A subset
\mathcal{Z}
of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
is said to be
strictly positively invariant
under
\eta
if

\eta(t,u)\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{Z})\quad\text{for every \ }u\in\mathcal{Z}\text{ and every }t\in(0,T(u)),

where
\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{Z})
denotes the interior of
\mathcal{Z}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
. If
\mathcal{Z}
is strictly positively invariant under
\eta
, then the set

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Z}):=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{Z}\text{ for some }t\in(0,T(u))\}

is open in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
and the entrance time map
e_{\mathcal{Z}}:\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Z})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

e_{\mathcal{Z}}(u):=\inf\{t\geq 0:\eta(t,u)\in\mathcal{Z}\}

is continuous. We write

\mathcal{P}^{G}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:u\geq 0\}

for the convex cone of non-negative functions in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
and, for
\alpha>0
, we set

B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}):=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G})\leq\alpha\},

where
\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{A}):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}}\|u-v\|_{V}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
\alpha>0
such that

(a)

[B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})\cup B_{\alpha}(-\mathcal{P}^{G})]\cap\mathcal{E}^{G}=\emptyset
, and

(b)

B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})
and
B_{\alpha}(-\mathcal{P}^{G})
are strictly positively invariant under
\eta
.

Proof.

(a):
For any
u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
the Sobolev inequality yields a positive constant
C
such that

|u^{-}|_{2^{*}}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}^{G}}|u-v|_{2^{*}}\leq C\inf_{v\in\mathcal{P}^{G}}\|u-v\|_{V}=C\,\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G}).

(3.4)

If
u\in\mathcal{E}^{G}
, then
u^{-}\in\mathcal{N}^{G}
and, by Lemma
3.2
(i)
and assumption
(f_{1})
,

0<\varrho^{2}\leq\|u^{-}\|_{V}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}f(u^{-})u^{-}\leq A_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{-}|^{2^{*}}=A_{1}|u^{-}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}.

Hence, there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\alpha<\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G})
for all
u\in\mathcal{E}^{G}
. Since
\mathcal{E}^{G}
is symmetric, this implies that
\alpha<\mathrm{dist}(u,-\mathcal{P}^{G})
for all
u\in\mathcal{E}^{G}
.

(b):
Using (
3.3
),
(f_{2})
,
(f_{1})
, the Hölder and the Sobolev inequalities, and (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(Q(u),\mathcal{P}^{G})\|Q(u)^{-}\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q(u)^{-}\|_{V}^{2}=\langle Q(u),Q(u)^{-}\rangle_{V}=\int_{\Omega}f(u)Q(u)^{-}

\displaystyle=\int_{u>0}f(u)Q(u)^{-}+\int_{u<0}f(u)Q(u)^{-}\leq\int_{\Omega}f(u^{-})Q(u)^{-}

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{-}|^{2^{*}-1}Q(u)^{-}\leq A_{1}|u^{-}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}-1}|Q(u^{-})|_{2^{*}}

\displaystyle=C\,\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G})^{2^{*}-1}\|Q(u)^{-}\|_{V}.

If
Q(u)^{-}\neq 0
, then

\text{dist}(Q(u),\mathcal{P}^{G})\leq C\,\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G})^{2^{*}-1}.

So, setting
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}C^{-1/(2^{*}-2)}
and
\delta:=(\frac{1}{2})^{2^{*}-2}\in(0,1)
, we have that

\mathrm{dist}(Q(u),\mathcal{P}^{G})\leq\delta\,\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{P}^{G})\qquad\text{for every \ }u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}).

It follows that
Q(u)\in\mathrm{int}(B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}))
if
u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}).
Since
B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})
is closed and convex, applying
[
15
, Theorem 5.2]
we conclude that

\eta(t,u)\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})\quad\text{for every }u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})\text{ \ and \ }t\in[0,T(u)).

(3.5)

Now, arguing by contradiction, assume that
\eta(t,u)\in\partial(B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}))
for some
u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})
and
t\in(0,T(u))
. Then, by Mazur’s separation theorem, there exist a continuous linear functional
\mathscr{L}\in(D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G})^{\prime}
and
\beta>0
such that
\mathscr{L}(\eta(t,u))=\beta
and
\mathscr{L}(u)>\beta
for every
u\in\text{int}(B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}))
.
It follows that

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{s=t}\mathscr{L}(\eta(s,u))=\mathscr{L}(-\nabla I_{V}(\eta(t,u)))=\mathscr{L}(Q(\eta(t,u)))-\beta>0.

Hence, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathscr{L}(\eta(s,u))<\beta
for every
s\in(t-\varepsilon,t)
. Then,
\eta(s,u)\notin B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})
, which is a contradiction. This proves that
\eta(t,u)\in\mathrm{int}(B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G}))
for every
u\in B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})
and
0<t<T(u)
, as claimed.
∎

Fix
\alpha
as in the previous lemma. Given
d\in\mathbb{R}
, set
I^{d}_{V}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}:J(u)\leq d\}
and define

\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G}:=B_{\alpha}(\mathcal{P}^{G})\cup B_{\alpha}(-\mathcal{P}^{G})\cup I^{d}_{V}.

Lemma
3.4
yields the following result.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
I_{V}
does not have a sign-changing critical point
u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
with
I_{V}(u)=d
, then the set
\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G}
is strictly positively invariant under
\eta
, and the map

\varrho_{d}:\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G})\to\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G},\qquad\varrho_{d}(u)=\eta(e_{\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G}}(u),u),

is odd and continuous, and satisfies
\varrho_{d}(u)=u
for every
u\in\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G}
.

We introduce a suitable topological invariant as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{Y}
be symmetric subsets of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
. The
genus of
\mathcal{Y}
relative to
\mathcal{Z}
, denoted
\mathrm{genus}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
, is the smallest number
m
such that
\mathcal{Y}
can be covered by
m+1
open symmetric subsets
\mathcal{U}_{0}
,
\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots\mathcal{U}_{m}
of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
with the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{U}_{0}
and there exists an odd continuous map
\vartheta_{0}:\mathcal{U}_{0}\to\mathcal{Z}
such that
\vartheta_{0}(u)=u
for all
u\in\mathcal{Z}
.

(ii)

There exist odd continuous maps
\vartheta_{j}:\mathcal{U}_{j}\to\{-1,1\}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m.

If no such cover exists, we set
\mathrm{genus}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}):=\infty
.

Recall that
I_{V}:D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to satisfy
(PS)_{c}
if every sequence
(v_{k})
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
such that

I_{V}(v_{k})\to c\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla I_{V}(u_{k})\to 0,

contains a convergent subsequence.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
d\geq 0
. If
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
at every
c\leq d
, then
I_{V}
has at least
\mathrm{genus}(\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G},\mathcal{Z}_{0}^{G})
pairs of sign-changing critical points
\pm v\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
with
I_{V}(v)\leq d
.

Proof.

The proof is formally identical to that of
[
13
, Proposition 3.6]
, using now Corollary
3.5
.
∎

We are ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a bounded
G
-invariant open subset of
\Omega
and
W
be a finite dimensional linear subspace of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Lambda)^{G}
. If
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
for every
c\leq\sup_{u\in W}I_{V}(u)
, then
I_{V}
has at least
m:=\mathrm{dim}(W)
pairs of critical points
\pm u_{1},\ldots,\pm u_{m}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
such that
u_{1}>0
,
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
if
j=2,\ldots,m
,

I_{V}(u_{1})=c(\Omega)^{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{V}(u_{j})\leq\sup_{u\in W}I_{V}(u)\quad\text{for all \ }j=1,\ldots,m.

Proof.

Let
m:=\mathrm{dim}(W)\geq 1
. By Lemma
3.2
,
W\cap\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\neq\emptyset
. Hence,
d:=\sup_{u\in W}I_{V}(u)\geq c(\Omega)^{G}
. Therefore,
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
at
c=c(\Omega)^{G}
and a standard argument shows that this value is attained at some positive function
u_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
.

Next, adapting the argument in
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
, we will show that
k:=\mathrm{genus}(\mathcal{Z}_{d}^{G},\mathcal{Z}_{0}^{G})\geq m-1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{k}
be open symmetric subsets of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
such that
\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{d}\subset\mathcal{U}_{0}\cup\,\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\,\mathcal{U}_{k}
and
\mathcal{U}_{0}\supset\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{0}
,
\vartheta_{0}:\mathcal{U}_{0}\to\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{0}
be an odd continuous map such that
\vartheta_{0}(u)=u
for all
u\in\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{0}
, and
\vartheta_{j}:\mathcal{U}_{j}\to\{1,-1\}
be odd continuous maps,
j=1,\ldots,k
. Making
\mathcal{U}_{0}
a little smaller, we may assume that
\vartheta_{0}
is defined on the closure of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
, and then extend it to an odd map
\widehat{\vartheta}_{0}:D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}\to D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
using Tietze’s extension theorem.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}
be the connected component of the complement of
\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}
that contains
0
, defined in (
3.1
), and set

\mathcal{O}:=\{w\in W:\widehat{\vartheta}_{0}(w)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\}.

Then
\mathcal{O}
is an open symmetric neighborhood of
0
in
W
. Lemma
3.1
states that
I_{V}(w)\leq 0
if
w\in W
and
\|w\|_{V}\geq R
for some
R>0
. Hence, if
\|w\|_{V}\geq R
, it follows from (
3.2
) that
\widehat{\vartheta}_{0}(w)=w\notin\mathcal{B}_{0}
. This shows that
\mathcal{O}
is bounded.

Define
U_{j}:=\mathcal{U}_{j}\cap\partial\mathcal{O}
. Then
U_{0},U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
are symmetric, open in
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and they cover
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.4
,

\vartheta_{0}(U_{0})\subset\mathcal{Z}^{G}_{0}\cap\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\subset\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\smallsetminus\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
(ii)
that there exists an odd map
\eta:\mathcal{N}(\Omega)^{G}\smallsetminus\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}\to\{1,-1\}
. Let
\eta_{j}:U_{j}\to\{1,-1\}
be the restriction of the map
\eta_{0}:=\eta\circ\vartheta_{0}
if
j=0
, and of
\eta_{j}:=\vartheta_{j},
if
j=1,\ldots,k
. Choose a partition of unity
\{\pi_{j}:\partial\mathcal{O}\to[0,1]:j=0,1,\ldots,k\}
subordinated to the cover
\{U_{0},U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
consisting of even functions, and let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k+1}\}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
. The map
\psi:\partial\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
given by

\psi(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\eta_{j}(u)\pi_{j}(u)e_{j+1}

is odd and continuous, and satisfies
\psi(u)\neq 0
for every
u\in\partial\mathcal{O}
. By the Borsuk-Ulam theorem,
\mathrm{dim}(W)\leq k+1
, as claimed.

Applying Theorem
3.7
we obtain
m-1
pairs
\pm u_{2},\ldots,\pm u_{m}
of sign-changing critical points of
I_{V}
with
I_{V}(u_{j})\leq d
for all
j=2,\ldots,m
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
The proof of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Whether
\Omega
is bounded or
\Omega
is an exterior domain and
\#Gx=\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, Theorem
2.2
states that
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
. As
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
has infinite dimension, Theorem
3.8
yields
m
pairs
\pm u_{1},\ldots,\pm u_{m}
of
G
-invariant solutions to (
1.1
) such that
u_{1}
is positive,
I_{V}(u_{1})=c(\Omega)^{G}
, and
u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}
change sign, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Fix
R>0
and set
\Theta:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus B_{R}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
be the set of all nonempty
O(N)
-invariant bounded domains (i.e., all open annuli) contained in
\Theta
and, for each
k\geq 2
, let

\mathcal{P}_{k}:=\big\{\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{k}\}:\Theta_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{1},\ \Theta_{i}\cap\Theta_{j}=\emptyset\text{ if }i\neq j\big\}.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that in any
O(N)
-invariant domain
\Theta^{\prime}\subset\Theta
the problem

-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(u),\qquad u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\Theta^{\prime})^{O(N)},

has a least energy solution
\omega_{\Theta^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{N}(\Theta^{\prime})^{O(N)}
. Define

c_{k}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}}):\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\right\}.

As
\Theta_{i}\subset\Theta
, we have that
\mathcal{N}(\Theta_{i})^{O(N)}\subset\mathcal{N}(\Theta)^{O(N)}
. Hence,
I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})\geq I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta})=:c_{0}>0
and, as a consequence,

c_{k-1}+c_{0}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})\quad\text{for every \ }\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{k},\ \ k\geq 2.

Therefore,
c_{k-1}<c_{k-1}+c_{0}\leq c_{k}
. Define

\ell_{k}:=c_{\infty}^{-1}c_{k}.

(4.1)

Then,
\ell_{k-1}<\ell_{k}
. Let us show that these numbers have the required property.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be a closed subgroup of
O(N)
and
\Omega
be a
G
-invariant domain such that
\Omega\supset\Theta
and

\ell_{m}<\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx.

(4.2)

Then,

c_{m}<\Big(\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx\Big)c_{\infty}.

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,c_{0})
with
c_{m}+\varepsilon<\Big(\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx\Big)c_{\infty}
, we choose
\{\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{m}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{m}
such that

c_{m}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})<c_{m}+\varepsilon.

(4.3)

Set
\Lambda_{m}:=\Theta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Theta_{m}
. Then
\Lambda_{m}
is a bounded
G
-invariant open subset of
\Omega

and
\omega_{\Theta_{i}}\in\mathcal{N}(\Theta_{i})^{O(N)}\subset\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{m})^{G}
.
For each
k=1,\ldots,m
, we consider the linear subspace of
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Lambda_{m})^{G}
given by

W_{k}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\omega_{\Theta_{i}}:t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Since
\Theta_{i}\cap\Theta_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
, we have that
\omega_{\Theta_{i}}
and
\omega_{\Theta_{j}}
are orthogonal in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Lambda_{m})^{G}
. Hence,
\mathrm{dim}(W_{k})=k
. Furthermore, using Lemma
3.2
(vi)
we see that

I_{V}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\omega_{\Theta_{i}}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{V}(t_{i}\omega_{\Theta_{i}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})\qquad\text{for all \ }t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore,

d_{k}:=\sup_{W_{k}}I_{V}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})

and, from (
4.3
) and our choice of
\varepsilon
, we get

d_{k}<\Big(\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx\Big)c_{\infty}\qquad\text{for every \ }k=1,\ldots,m.

Then, Theorem
2.2
states that
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
for all
c\leq d_{k}
. So, applying Theorem
3.8
to each
W_{k}
successively, we obtain
m
pairs of critical points
\pm u_{1},\ldots,\pm u_{m}
such that
u_{1}>0
,
u_{j}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
if
j=2,\ldots,m
,

I_{V}(u_{1})=c(\Omega)^{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{V}(u_{k})\leq d_{k}\qquad\text{for each }k=1,\ldots,m.

Note that, as
I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})\geq c_{0}
,

I_{V}(u_{k})+(m-k)c_{0}\leq d_{k}+(m-k)c_{0}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}I_{V}(\omega_{\Theta_{i}})<c_{m}+\varepsilon.

Since we have chosen
\varepsilon<c_{0}
, we derive

I_{V}(u_{k})<c_{m}\quad\text{for each \ }k=1,\ldots,m-1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{V}(u_{m})<c_{m}+\varepsilon.

In fact, if
m=1
, then
I_{V}(u_{1})=c(\Omega)^{G}\leq c(\Theta)^{O(N)}\leq c_{1}
. Next we prove that, if
m\geq 2
, we can choose
u_{m}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
such that

I_{V}(u_{m})\leq c_{m}.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,c_{0})
be such that
c_{m}+\varepsilon_{n}<\Big(\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx\Big)c_{\infty}
and
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
, and let
u_{m,n}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
be the
m
-th critical point obtained by applying the previous argument with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}
. Then,

I_{V}(u_{m,n})<c_{m}+\varepsilon_{n}<\Big(\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx\Big)c_{\infty}.

If
I_{V}(u_{m,n})\leq c_{m}
for some
n_{0}
, we take
u_{m}:=u_{m,n_{0}}
. If
I_{V}(u_{m,n})>c_{m}
for all
n
, then
I_{V}(u_{m,n})\rightarrow c_{m}
. By Theorem
2.2
,
I_{V}
satisfies
(PS)_{c_{m}}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
. Therefore, as
I^{\prime}_{V}(u_{m,n})=0
, a subsequence of
(u_{m,n})
converges to
u_{m}
in
D^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{G}
. Hence,
u_{m}
is a critical point of
I_{V}
with
I_{V}(u_{m})=c_{m}
and, since
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
is closed,
u_{m}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
.

To prove the last statement of Theorem
1.3
, recall that
(\ell_{k})
is increasing. Therefore, if (
4.2
) holds true, then

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\#Gx>\ell_{k}\qquad\text{for every \ }k=1,\ldots,m,

and, as we just saw, for each such
k
there exist
k
pairs of
G
-invariant solutions
\pm v_{k,1},\ldots,\pm v_{k,k}
to the problem (
1.1
) such that
v_{k,1}>0
,
v_{k,2},\ldots v_{k,k}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)^{G}
,

I_{V}(v_{k,1})=c(\Omega)^{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{V}(v_{k,i})\leq c_{k}\quad\text{for every}\quad i=1,\ldots,k.

We set
u_{1}:=v_{1,1}
,
u_{2}:=v_{2,2}
and, for each
2<k\leq m
, choose
u_{k}\in\{v_{k,2},\ldots,v_{k,k}\}
such that
u_{k}\neq v_{k-1,i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,k-1
. Then,
\pm u_{1},\ldots,\pm u_{m}
are
m
different pairs of
G
-invariant solutions to problem (
1.1
) such that
u_{1}
is positive,
u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}
change sign,

I_{V}(u_{1})=c(\Omega)^{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{V}(u_{k})\leq c_{k}=\ell_{k}c_{\infty}\quad\text{for each}\quad k=1,\ldots,m,

as claimed.
∎

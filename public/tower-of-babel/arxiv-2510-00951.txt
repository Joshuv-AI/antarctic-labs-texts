Extending the 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -index
Elena Hoster, Christian Stump, Lorenzo Vecchi

Abstract
. We prove for finite, graded, bounded posets, that the Poincaré-extended 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -index is obtained from the 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -index via the ω \omega -transformation. This proves a conjecture by Dorpalen-Barry, Maglione, and the second author, and provides a more conceptual approach to 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -indices and Chow polynomials beyond R R -labeled posets.



1.
Introduction

The (Poincaré-)extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index of a finite, graded, bounded poset was introduced by Dorpalen-Barry, Maglione, and the second author in
[
DBMS25
]
, enriching the classical
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index with the Poincaré polynomial.
They showed that this polynomial has nonnegative coefficients whenever the poset admits an
R
-labeling.
They then used this to prove a conjecture by Maglione and Voll that the coarse flag Hilbert–Poincaré series has nonnegative numerator polynomial
[
MV24
]
.
The second author then showed that the extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index also specializes to the Chow polynomial, providing a combinatorial approach to the
\gamma
-positivity of the latter for
R
-labeled posets
[
Stu24
]
.
All three authors in different combinations, and also in collaboration with Brändén, used this to give explicit descriptions of the Chow polynomial for uniform matroids and totally nonnegative posets
[
Hos24
,
BV25b
]
and provide real-rootedness for uniform matroids and for simplicial posets
[
BV25a
,
HS25
]
.
Ferroni, Matherne, and the third author then generalized the argument for the
\gamma
-positivity to posets with a nonnegative flag
h
-vector
[
FMV24
]
.

This paper provides a way to obtain the extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index from the
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index, proving a conjecture from
[
DBMS25
]
.
This ties together multiple of the above results, generalizes the numerical canonical decomposition of the Chow ring from
[
FMV24
]
, and generalizes and simplifies multiple arguments in the literature, see
Theorem

2.1
and
Corollaries

2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
.

2.
Definitions and main results

Let
P
be a finite, graded, bounded poset of rank
n
.
That is,
P
is a finite poset with unique minimum element
\hat{0}
and unique maximum element
\hat{1}
of rank
n
such that
\operatorname{rk}(w)
is equal to the length of any maximal chain from
\hat{0}
to
w
.
Its
Möbius function

\mu
is given by
\mu(w,w)=1
for all
w\in P
and
\mu(u,w)=-\sum_{u\leq v<w}\mu(u,v)
for all
u<w
, and its
Poincaré polynomial
is

\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)=\sum_{w\in P}\mu(\hat{0},w)\cdot(-y)^{\operatorname{rk}(w)}\,.

A (not necessarily maximal) chain
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{1}<\dots<\mathcal{C}_{k}<\mathcal{C}_{k+1}\}
in
P
is an ordered set of pairwise comparable elements.
We assume throughout this paper that all chains end in the maximum element
\mathcal{C}_{k+1}=\hat{1}
.
The
chain Poincaré polynomial
then is

\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P,\mathcal{C}}(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i+1}]}(y)\,,

where
[\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i+1}]\subseteq P
denotes the interval between two consecutive elements in the chain.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[y]\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle
be the polynomial ring in two noncommuting variables
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
with coefficients being polynomials in the variable
y
.
For a subset
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n-1\}
, we set
{\sf wt}_{S}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}
with

w_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{b}&\text{if }k\in S,\\
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}&\text{if }k\notin S\,.\end{cases}

For a chain
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{1}<\dots<\mathcal{C}_{k}<\mathcal{C}_{k+1}=\hat{1}\}
, we moreover set
{\sf wt}_{\mathcal{C}}={\sf wt}_{\{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\dots,\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{C}_{k})\}}
.
The extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index
\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
then is

\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P,\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot{\sf wt}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\ \in\mathbb{Z}[y]\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle\,,

where the sum ranges over all chains
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{1}<\dots<\mathcal{C}_{k+1}=\hat{1}\}
.
Since
P
is bounded, we have
\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(0)=1
, implying

(1)

\expsi_{P}(0,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\Psi_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}{\sf wt}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\,,

where
\Psi_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
is (a mild variation of) the
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index
as given, for example, in
[
Bay21
]
.
It is sometimes convenient to only consider chains that start in the bottom element
\hat{0}
.
This is achieved by applying the
\iota
-transformation removing the initial letter from every
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-monomial,

\expsitilde_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\iota\big(\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,,\quad\widetilde{\Psi}_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\iota\big(\Psi_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,.

In this paper, we prove that the Poincaré-extension of the
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index is obtained from the
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index via the
\omega
-transformation.
For posets admitting an
R
-labeling, this was shown in
[
DBMS25
, Corollary 2.9]
, where also the general case was conjectured.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
DBMS25
, Conjecture 2.10]
)
.

Let
P
be a finite, graded, bounded poset.
Then

\displaystyle\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\omega\big(\Psi_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,,\quad\expsitilde_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=(1+y)\cdot\omega\big(\widetilde{\Psi}_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,,

where
\omega
is the transformation that replaces all occurrences of
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
with
(1+y)\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}+(y+y^{2})\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}
and then simultaneously replaces all remaining occurrences of
\mathbf{a}
with
\mathbf{a}+y\mathbf{b}
and of
\mathbf{b}
with
\mathbf{b}+y\mathbf{a}
.

As the first immediate corollary, we obtain the following alternative description of the extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index in terms of the
flag
h
-vector

\beta(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T\setminus S|}\alpha(S)\,,

for
\alpha(S)
being the
flag
f
-vector
counting maximal chains in the subposet of
P
with only the ranks in
S
selected.
This in particular generalizes the nonnegativity of the extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index to posets with nonnegative flag
h
-vector.

Corollary 2.2
.

We have

\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\sum_{T\subseteq\{0,\dots,n-1\}}\beta(T)\cdot\omega(\operatorname{m}_{T})\,,

where
\operatorname{m}_{T}=m_{0}\dots m_{n-1}
with
m_{i}=\mathbf{b}
if
i\in T
and
m_{i}=\mathbf{a}
if
i\notin T
.
The extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index has in particular nonnegative coefficients whenever the flag
h
-vector is nonnegative.

This description can be used to bypass the inclusion-exclusion argument for posets admitting an
R
-labeling from
[
DBMS25
, Section 4]
.
For a nonnegative
R
-labeling
\lambda
, a maximal chain
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0}<\dots<\mathcal{M}_{n})
in
P
and a set
E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, define the sequence
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lambda_{i}=\pm\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{i-1},\mathcal{M}_{i})
with the sign being positive if
i\not\in E
and negative if
i\in E
.
Then set
\operatorname{m}(\mathcal{M},E)=m_{0}\dots m_{n-1}
to be the
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-monomial with
m_{i}=\mathbf{b}
if
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{i+1}
and
m_{i}=\mathbf{a}
if
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}
.

Corollary 2.3

(
[
DBMS25
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

Let
P
admit an
R
-labeling.
Then

\displaystyle\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{M},E}y^{\#E}\cdot\operatorname{m}(\mathcal{M},E)\,,

where the sum ranges over all
maximal
chains
\mathcal{M}
in
P
and all subsets
E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Observe that
Corollary

2.2
together with
[
DBMS25
, Proposition 5.1]
also yields a version of this corollary for posets not admitting an
R
-labeling,

\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\sum_{T,E}\beta(T)\cdot y^{\#E}\cdot\operatorname{m}_{T}(E)\,,

where
\operatorname{m}_{T}(E)
is obtained from
\operatorname{m}_{T}=m_{0}\dots m_{n-1}
by replacing
m_{i}=\mathbf{a}
by
\mathbf{b}
if
i+1\in E
and replacing
m_{i}=\mathbf{b}
by
\mathbf{a}
if
i\in E
, compare
[
DBMS25
, Equation (6)]
.
We finally also recover the
\gamma
-positivity of the (augmented) Chow polynomial if
P
has a nonnegative flag
h
-vector.
Recall from
[
Stu24
, Theorem 2.6]
that the (augmented) Chow polynomial of
P
is obtained from the extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index via

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{P}(x)

\displaystyle=\expsi_{P}(-x,1,x)\cdot(1-x)^{-n}\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}_{P}(x)

\displaystyle=\expsitilde_{P}(-x,1,x)\cdot(1-x)^{-n}\,.

Observe that
[
Stu24
]
only considered posets admitting an
R
-labeling, but the argument for
[
Stu24
, Theorem 2.6]
does not rely on this property and generalizes verbatim.

Corollary 2.4

(
[
FMV24
, Theorem 4.20]
)
.

The augmented Chow polynomial has
\gamma
-expansion

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{P}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T}\beta(T)\cdot x^{\#T}(1+x)^{n-2\#T}\,,

where the sum ranges over all
isolated
subsets
T\subseteq\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, i.e., subsets
T
such that
i\in T
implies
i+1\notin T
.
Analogously, the Chow polynomial
has the
\gamma
-expansion

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}_{P}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T}\beta(T)\cdot x^{\#T}(1+x)^{n-1-2\#T}\,,

where the sum ranges over all isolated subsets
T\subseteq\{2,\dots,n-1\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n}+\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)}+\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[\widehat{0},w]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\big)\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\,,

\displaystyle\expsitilde_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[\widehat{0},w]}(y)\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\,.

The first two summands in the formula for

The third sum then sums over all elements

The formula for

is analogous, where we observe that only the chains starting in

are present, and with the first letter removed.
∎

Now evaluating

\displaystyle(1-x)^{n-1}\operatorname{H}_{P}(x)=\omega_{\operatorname{ev}}\big(\widetilde{\Psi}_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1-x^{n}}{1-x}(1-x)^{n-1}+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}(1-x)^{\rk(w)-1}(x-x^{\rk(w)})\cdot(1-x)^{n-\rk(w)-1}\operatorname{H}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad=(1-x)^{n-1}\left[\frac{1-x^{n}}{1-x}+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\frac{x-x^{\rk(w)}}{1-x}\operatorname{H}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(x)\right].

Similarly,
Proposition

3.3
(

) lets us write

\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{aug}}_{P}(x)=\frac{1-x^{n+1}}{1-x}+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\frac{x-x^{\rk(w)+1}}{1-x}\operatorname{H}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(x).

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1-(-y)^{k+1}}{1+y}\ (\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\,,

\displaystyle\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{a})\,,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\,,

\displaystyle\omega(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(1+y)\big(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{a}\big)\,.

Proof.

We start with the first two identities by induction on
k
.
For
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle\omega\big(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathbf{a})-\omega(\mathbf{b})=(1-y)(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})\mathbf{b}\big)\,,

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b})-\omega(\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b})=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{b}-y^{2}\mathbf{a})\,.

Assuming the first two identities for
k\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{a}\big)-\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\big)\cdot\omega(\mathbf{a})-\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1-(-y)^{k}}{1+y}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(\mathbf{a}+y\mathbf{b})-(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1-(-y)^{k+2}}{1+y}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1}

and

\displaystyle\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1}\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b})-\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k})\cdot\omega(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b})-\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b})\cdot\omega(\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(1-(-y)^{k+1})\big(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}+y\mathbf{b}\mathbf{a}\big)-(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{a})(\mathbf{b}+y\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1}\big(\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{k+2}\mathbf{a}\big)\,.

Here, we used the induction hypotheses in the third line of each equation.
We finally deduce the third and forth identities using the first and second identity:

\displaystyle\omega\big(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathbf{b})\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k})+\omega((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{1+y}\bigg(\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(1-(-y)^{k+1})+\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(y+(-y)^{k+2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(1-(-y)^{k+2})-\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}(1-(-y)^{k+2})\bigg)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}

and

\displaystyle\omega\big(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathbf{b})\cdot\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}\big)+\omega\big((\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k+1}\mathbf{b}\big)

\displaystyle=(1+y)\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{b}-(1+y)(-y)^{k+1}\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{k}\mathbf{a}\,.\qed

Proof of
Proposition

3.3

Proposition

3.3

The key ingredient for proving
Theorem

2.1
is the following identity, which is a standard identity in the incidence algebra.

Lemma 3.6
.

\sum_{w\in P}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y)=1\,.

In particular,

\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)=1-(-y)^{n}-\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y)\,.

Proof.

It is known that
\sum_{w}\chi_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y)=y^{n}
, this can be proven for example by applying the Möbius inversion formula.
The statement then follows with
\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)=(-y)^{n}\chi_{P}(-\tfrac{1}{y})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We first use
Lemma

3.6
in the recursive definition of
\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
from
Proposition

3.1
.

\displaystyle\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\ (\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n}+\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[\widehat{0},w]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n}+(1-(-y)^{n})\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<u<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[u,w]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\,.

The final sum may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{0<u<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[u,w]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\quad=\ \sum_{0<u<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(u)}\!\!\sum_{u<w<1}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[u,w]}(y)\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-\rk(u)-1}\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\quad=\ \sum_{0<u<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(u)}\big(\expsitilde_{[u,1]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})-\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[u,1]}(y)\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1-\rk(u)}\big)\,.

A few more term orderings and cancellations give

\displaystyle\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\ \mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}-(-y)^{n}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(w)}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<u<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(u)-1}\cdot(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})\cdot\expsitilde_{[u,1]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{0<u<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[u,\widehat{1}]}(y)\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-(-y)^{n}\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{0<w<\widehat{1}}\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\cdot\mathbf{b}\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{0<u<\widehat{1}}(-y)^{\rk(u)}\cdot\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(u)-1}\cdot\mathbf{a}\cdot\expsitilde_{[u,1]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-(-y)^{n}\mathbf{b})(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\widehat{0}<w<\widehat{1}}\big(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\mathbf{b}-(-y)^{\rk(w)}\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})^{\rk(w)-1}\mathbf{a}\big)\cdot\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\,.

By induction on the rank of the poset, we may assume

\expsitilde_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=(1+y)\cdot\omega\big(\Psi_{[w,\widehat{1}]}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,,

which is clearly satisfied if the poset
P
has rank
1
.
We have therefore obtained the right hand side of (
2
), which proves that

\expsi_{P}(y,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\omega\big(\Psi_{P}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\big)\,.

Applying
\iota

where we used
\iota\circ\omega=(1+y)\cdot\omega\circ\iota
from (
4
).
∎

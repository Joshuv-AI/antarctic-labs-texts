Abstract
. In this note, we propose some open problems and questions about bounded convex domains in ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} , specifically about visibility and iteration theory.

30C35; 30D05; 30D40; 32F45.

1.
Introduction

Iteration theory in complex domains is a very classical subject of study, dating back to the pioneering papers of Denjoy
[
21
]
and Wolff
[
29
,
30
]
. (See also
[
2
,
11
,
4
]
for further references.) Even for holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc, many open problems and new interesting results emerge annually. Moreover, in higher dimensions, even more open questions remain.

In this note, we present open questions, a few conjectures, and some new results about iteration theory in bounded convex domains. The reason for staying within this class is that it exhibits peculiar properties, primarily discovered by L. Lempert
[
26
]
(see also
[
24
,
2
]
), which provide an incredible array of examples and results.

In the past ten years, significant efforts have been dedicated to applying Gromov’s hyperbolicity theory in several complex variables, leading to the discovery of new and intriguing results. A. Karlsson
[
25
]
demonstrated that the Denjoy-Wolff Theorem holds in Gromov hyperbolic spaces for the Gromov’s topology. G. Bharali and A. Zimmer
[
8
,
9
]
, and later N. Nikolov, P. Thomas and the first author
[
15
]
, identified a distinctive feature of Gromov hyperbolic spaces known as
visibility
, which is related to the “bending inside” of geodesics. Visibility has far-reaching implications. For instance, G. Bharali and A. Maitra
[
7
]
showed that visibility, even in the absence of Gromov’s hyperbolicity, implies the Denjoy-Wolff Theorem. However, as demonstrated in
[
6
]
, visibility is a sufficient condition for the Denjoy-Wolff theorem, but it is not necessary in general.

For bounded convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, the known results about visibility and the Denjoy-Wolff theorem suggest that these two properties are equivalent. This note aims to provide a tour of the relationship between visibility and the Denjoy-Wolff theorem, reformulating the notions of visibility, Bharali-Maitra’s theorem, and key questions and conjectures that could lead to proving their equivalence for bounded convex domains (regardless of any regularity conditions).

2.
Notations

The Kobayashi distance of a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is denoted by
k_{\Omega}
, while the Kobayashi-Royden infinitesimal norm by
\kappa_{\Omega}
. We refer to
[
24
,
2
,
26
]
for properties and details. In particular,
k_{\Omega}
is a complete distance on
\Omega
which defines the Euclidean topology of
\Omega
and it is contracted by holomorphic maps.

A
complex geodesic

\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is a holomorphic map such that it is an isometry between
k_{\mathbb{D}}
and
k_{\Omega}
. For every
z,w\in\Omega
there exists a complex geodesic such that
z,w\in\varphi(\mathbb{D})
. Moreover, given a complex geodesic
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
there exists a holomorphic map
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
such that
\rho\circ\varphi={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
. Such a map is called a
left-inverse
of
\varphi
.

A
geodesic segment

\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
is an absolutely continuous curve such that for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1

\int_{s}^{t}\kappa_{\Omega}(\gamma(r);\gamma^{\prime}(r))dr=k_{\Omega}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)).

The interval
[0,1]
can be replaced by any segment in
\mathbb{R}
. However, in this paper, unless otherwise specified, when referring to a geodesic segment, we always assume it is defined in the interval
[0,1]
.

A
geodesic ray

\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to\Omega
is an absolutely continuous curve such that
\gamma|_{[0,t]}
is a geodesic interval for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{\Omega}(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))=+\infty
. The choice of interval
[0,+\infty)
can be replaced by any interval
[a,b)
with
a<b
, but unless otherwise specified, when referring to a geodesic ray, we always assume its interval of definition is
[0,+\infty)
.

Note that if
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is a complex geodesic, then
\varphi
maps geodesic intervals or rays of
\mathbb{D}
onto geodesic intervals or rays of
\Omega
. Therefore, for every pair of distinct points
z,w\in\Omega
, there exists a geodesic segment joining
z
and
w
. This follows also directly from the Hopf-Rinow theorem since
k_{\Omega}
is complete.

3.
Visibility and complex geodesics

G. Bharali and A. Zimmer introduced the concept of visibility for, non-necessarily complete, hyperbolic domains in
[
8
]
involving almost-geodesics as defined by them. For bounded convex domains, since any two points are joint by a geodesic segment, the paper
[
15
]
introduced a simpler notion of visibility. Note that by the works in
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
, the visibility notion introduced in
[
15
]
is equivalent to a weaker form of the notion introduced by Bharali and Zimmer. It remains an open question whether, in general, those two definitions are equivalent.

Since we are working with bounded convex domains, we adopt a refined version of the definition given in
[
15
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
p,q\in\partial\Omega
,
p\neq q
. We say that
p
and
q
are
essentially visible
if there exist a compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
such that for every sequences
\{z_{k}\},\{w_{k}\}\subset\Omega
so that
\{z_{k}\}
converges to
p
and
\{w_{k}\}
converges to
q
there exists a sequence of geodesic segments
\{\gamma_{k}\}
such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=z_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}(1)=w_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}(s_{k})\in K
for some
s_{k}\in(0,1)
and for all
k
.

We say that
p
and
q
are
strongly visible
if there exists a compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
so that for every sequence of geodesic segments
\{\gamma_{k}\}
such that
\{\gamma_{k}(0)\}
converges to
p
and
\{\gamma_{k}(1)\}
converges to
q
there is
s_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma_{k}(s_{k})\in K
for all
k
sufficiently large.

We say that
q
is
non-visible
with respect to
p
(or, equivalently,
p
is
non-visible
with respect to
q
) if
p
and
q
are not essentially visible.

If every
p,q\in\partial\Omega
,
p\neq q
are essentially visible, we say that
\Omega
is
essentially visible
. While, if every
p,q\in\partial\Omega
,
p\neq q
are strongly visible, we say that
\Omega
is
visible
.

Clearly, strongly visible pairs of points are essentially visible. The following example shows that the converse does not hold in general.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\Omega:=\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
be the bidisc in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, let
r_{k}\in(0,1)
be a sequence increasing to one and set
z_{k}:=(-r_{k},0),w_{k}:=(r_{k},0)
. Since
k_{\Omega}((z_{1},z_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2}))=\max\{k_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{1},w_{1}),k_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{2},w_{2})\}
and
\kappa_{\Omega}((z_{1},z_{2});(v_{1},v_{2}))=\max\{\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{1};v_{1}),\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{2};v_{2})\}
it follows that the curves
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to\Omega
defined by

\gamma_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{r_{k}(2t-1)}{1-2r^{2}_{k}t},2tr_{k}\right)&\text{if}\>\>\>t\in[0,1/2],\\
\left(\dfrac{r_{k}(2t-1)}{1+r^{2}_{k}(2t-2)},(2-2t)r_{k}\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

are geodesic segments joining
z_{k}
to
w_{k}
. As
r_{k}
tends to one
\gamma_{k}([0,1])
evantually avoid any compact set in
\Omega
. Consequently, it follows that the pair
(-1,0)
and
(1,0)
in
\partial\Omega
are not strongly visible.

Denote by
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
projections to first and second coordinates respectively. Observe that if
z_{k},w_{k}\in\Omega
tend to
(-1,0)
and
(1,0)
respectively, and
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to\Omega
are geodesic segments such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=z_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}(1)=w_{k}
, the visibility of the unit disc shows that
\pi_{2}\circ\gamma_{k}([0,1])
meet some compact set in
\mathbb{D}
. As
\pi_{1}(z_{k}),\pi_{2}(w_{k})
are compactly contained in
\mathbb{D}
, it is not difficult to conclude that
z_{k}
and
w_{k}
can be joint by geodesic segments passing through a fixed compact set in
\Omega
. We conclude that
(-1,0)
and
(1,0)
are essentially visible.

The situation in the bidisc leads us to ask the following question.

Question 3.3
.

Does there exist a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
with no non-trivial analytic discs in the boundary which is essentially visible but not visible? Does there exist a smoothly bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
which is essentially visible but not visible?

Bounded convex domains which are Gromov hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance are visible
[
14
]
. In particular, smoothly bounded convex finite type domains are visible
[
32
,
33
]
. However visibility is weaker than Gromov hyperbolicity: indeed, smoothly bounded convex domains for which all, but a finite number of points, are of finite type are visible
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
but they are not Gromov’s hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance. In fact, there are examples of bounded convex domains containing a complex tangential line segment of infinite type points that that are visible
[
15
, Section 5]
.

Therefore it seems very difficult to find a sharp characterisation of visibility for convex domains in terms of the Euclidean geometry of their boundaries. Nevertheless, we can attempt to understand the possible cluster sets of “non-visible’ geodesic segments. To be precise, we need a definition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be a sequence of geodesic segments. We say that
\{\gamma_{k}\}
is a sequence of
non-visible geodesic segments
if

(1)

for every compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
there exists
k_{0}
such that
\gamma_{k}([0,1])\cap K=\emptyset
for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

(2)

there exist
p,q\in\partial\Omega
,
p\neq q
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{k}(0)=p
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{k}(1)=q
.

We have the following

Conjecture 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be a sequence of non-visible geodesic segments and let

\Gamma(\{\gamma_{k}\}):=\{p\in\partial\Omega:\exists\{t_{k_{n}}\}\subset[0,1]:\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{k_{n}}(t_{k_{n}})=p\}.

If
p,q\in\Gamma(\{\gamma_{k}\})
and
p\neq q
then

•

either
\{tp+(1-t)q,0<t<1\}\subset\Omega
,

•

or,
\{tp+(1-t)q,0\leq t\leq 1\}\subset\partial\Omega
and
(\mathbb{C}(p-q)+q)\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.

The second condition in the previous Conjecture means that the real segment
\{tp+(1-t)q,0\leq t\leq 1\}
belongs to a complex supporting hyperplane of
\Omega
.

The (Euclidean) geometry of the bidisc suggest that the above conjecture trivially holds for the bidisc. Furthermore, in
[
15
, Section 5]
and
[
28
, Section 5]
there are examples of smooth convex non-visible domains with no analytic discs in the boundary. For such domains, the limit set
\Gamma(\{\gamma_{k}\})
of non-visible geodesic segments
\gamma_{k}
lie in a real line segment contained in the intersection of a complex hyperplane with the boundary.

Actually, the examples mentioned above are not coincidental. As a consequence of
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a bounded convex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{1+\varepsilon}
-smooth boundary, it is shown in
[
28
, Theorem 11]
that if
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to\Omega
is a sequence of geodesic segments such that
\gamma_{k}(0)
tends to
p\in\partial\Omega
and
\gamma_{k}([0,1])
tends to
\partial\Omega
, then
\gamma_{k}([0,1])
tends to the affine complex tangent hyperplane to
\partial\Omega
at
p
, that is
\Gamma(\{\gamma_{k}\})\subset T^{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\partial\Omega\cap\partial\Omega
. Consequently the above conjecture is true (and only the second case can occur) under the hypothesis of
\mathcal{C}^{1+\epsilon}
-smoothness of
\partial\Omega
.

If Conjecture
3.5
is true, then every bounded convex domain
\Omega
with a strictly
\mathbb{C}
-linearly convex boundary (that is, every complex affine line
L
which does not intersect
\Omega
has the property that
L\cap\partial\Omega
contains at most one point) is visible. Actually, it is known that bounded (local) strictly
\mathbb{C}
-linearly convex domains with Dini-smooth boundaries are visible
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
.

An interesting characterization of visibility in terms of complex geodesics is provided by the following result of N. Nikolov (
[
27
, Prop. 5]
). Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be the family of all complex geodesics
\varphi
of
\Omega
parametrized so that
\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(0))=\max_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(\zeta))
, where, as usual,
\delta_{\Omega}(z)
is the distance of
z\in\Omega
from
\partial\Omega
.

Theorem 3.6

(Nikolov)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Then
\Omega
is visible if and only if

(1)

\partial\Omega
does not contain non-trivial analytic discs,

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{0}
is uniformly equicontinuous.

Is it not clear whether condition (2) in the previous theorem implies visibility by itself, but presumably it does.

Note that in particular, the previous theorem implies that every complex geodesic
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
extend continuously up to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

In
[
15
]
it has been proved that if
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
is a bounded simply connected domain then
D
is visible if and only if
\partial D
is locally connected. In particular, by Carathéodory’s theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
11
]
) for a simply connected domain a Riemann map extends continuously up to the boundary if and only if the domain is visible. It seems interesting to answer the following question:

Question 3.7
.

Does there exist a non-visible bounded convex domain
\Omega
such that every complex geodesic
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
extends continuously up to
\partial\mathbb{D}
? In other words, is the continuous extension of all complex geodesics up to the boundary sufficient for visibility?

Also, Nikolov’s Theorem hints to the following notion of visibility:

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. We say that
\Omega
is
complex visible
if for every
p,q\in\partial\Omega
such that
p\neq q
there exist a compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
, an open neighborhood
U
of
p
, an open neighborhood
V
of
q
such that for every sequences
\{z_{k}\},\{w_{k}\}\subset\Omega
so that
\{z_{k}\}
converges to
p
and
\{w_{k}\}
converges to
q
every sequence of complex geodesics
\{\varphi_{k}\}
such that
z_{k},w_{k}\in\varphi_{k}(\mathbb{D})
has the property that
\varphi_{k}(\mathbb{D})\cap K\neq\emptyset
for all
k
.

It is clear that visibility implies complex visibility.

Conjecture 3.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Then
\Omega
is visible if and only if it is complex visible.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
z_{0}\in\Omega
and let
\mathcal{G}_{z_{0}}
be the family of all geodesic segments and geodesic rays starting from
z_{0}
.

From
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
it follows that if
\Omega
is a visible bounded convex domain then
\mathcal{G}_{z_{0}}
is uniformly equicontinuous. In particular, all geodesic rays
land
(that is, if
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to\Omega
is a geodesic ray in
\Omega
, then
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)
exists).

Question 3.10
.

Does there exist a bounded convex domain
\Omega
which is not visible and such that all geodesic rays land?

It is easy to construct examples of (not convex) simply connected domain in
\mathbb{C}
which are not visible and for which all geodesic rays land (see,
e.g.
,
[
6
]
): in fact, any bounded simply connected domain for which the principal part of any prime end is a singleton but there is a prime end whose impression is not a single point provides such an example. We would expect the answer to the previous question to be negative, as otherwise the landing of all geodesic rays would determine visibility. However, we do not know of any counterexample.

However, Nikolov’s Theorem and the results in
[
10
]
make plausible the following:

Conjecture 3.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Then
\Omega
is visible if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{z_{0}}
is uniformly equicontinuous.

4.
The Denjoy-Wolff property

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be a holomorphic map. The
target set of
F
is the set

T(F):=\{z\in\partial{\Omega}:\exists z_{0}\in\Omega,\{n_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}\hbox{ such that }n_{k}\to\infty\hbox{ and }F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0})\to z\>\>\text{as}\>\>k\to\infty\}.

Abate (see,
[
2
, Theorem 2.4.20]
) proved that
T(F)=\emptyset
if and only if
F
has fixed points in
\Omega
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
\Omega
has
the Denjoy-Wolff property
if for any holomorphic self-map
F
of
\Omega
without fixed points, there exists a point
p\in\partial\Omega
such that
T(F)=\{p\}
.

It is known since Hervé
[
23
]
that the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{N}
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
verifies the Denjoy-Wolff property. Abate in
[
1
,
2
]
extended the result of Hervé to strongly convex domains with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundaries. Later Budzyńska
[
17
]
(see also
[
5
]
) removed the boundary regularity assumptions and extended this result to strictly convex domains, while in
[
14
]
the same result has been proved for bounded convex domains which are Gromov hyperbolic with respect to the Kobayashi distance, in particular, this happens for smoothly bounded finite type convex domains (see
[
32
,
33
]
). See also
[
18
,
20
]
and references therein for relevant results about the Denjoy-Wolff theorem in complex Banach spaces and in symmetric domains.

If
\partial\Omega
does not have non-trivial analytic discs, the target set can be determined by the cluster set of a unique point of
\Omega
, namely

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a convex bounded domain. Assume
\partial\Omega
does not have non-trivial analytic discs. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be a holomorphic self-map without fixed points. Let
z_{0}\in\Omega
. Then

T(F)=T(F,z_{0}):=\{q\in\partial\Omega:\exists\{n_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}\>\>\text{such that}\>\>n_{k}\to\infty,F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0})\to z\>\>\text{as}\>\>k\to\infty\}.

Proof.

Let
\{n_{k}\}
be a sequence converging to
\infty
and assume
\{F^{n_{k}}(z_{0})\}
converges to some point
p\in\partial\Omega
. Let
z_{1}\in\Omega
. Then for all
k
.

k_{\Omega}(F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}),F^{n_{k}}(z_{1}))\leq k_{\Omega}(z_{0},z_{1})<+\infty.

Since
k_{\Omega}
is complete, it follows that
\{F^{n_{k}}(z_{1})\}
converges to
\partial\Omega
. Since by hypothesis
\partial\Omega
does not have non-trivial analytic discs, by D’Addezio’s lemma
[
13
, Lemma A.2]
,
\{F^{n_{k}}(z_{1})\}
converges to
p
.
∎

We borrow the following definition from A. Karlsson
[
25
]
:

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a convex bounded domain. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be a holomorphic self-map without fixed points. Let
z_{0}\in\Omega
. Let
\{n_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
be a sequence converging to
\infty
. We say that
\{n_{k}\}
is a
record sequence
for
z_{0}
provided for all
m\leq n_{k}
and for all
k
we have

k_{\Omega}(F^{m}(z_{0}),z_{0})\leq k_{\Omega}(F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}),z_{0}).

Record sequences are the basic tool to produce Julia’s version of the Denjoy-Wolff theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
1
,
2
]
).

Remark 4.4
.

If
\Omega
is a bounded convex domain,
k_{\Omega}
is complete. Hence, if
F
is a holomorphic self-map of
\Omega
without fixed points, for every
z_{0}\in\Omega
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{\Omega}(F^{\circ n}(z_{0}),z_{0})=\infty
. Thus, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can define
n_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
to be such that
k_{\Omega}(F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}),z_{0})
is the maximum among
\{k_{\Omega}(F^{m}(z_{0}),z_{0})\}_{m=1,\ldots,k}
. Clearly,
\{n_{k}\}
is a record sequence for
z_{0}
.

G. Bharali and A. Maitra in
[
7
]
proved that if a bounded (not necessarily convex) domain is visible, then it has the Denjoy-Wolff property. Their argument can easily be adapted to prove the following result:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Assume
\partial\Omega
does not contain non-trivial analytic discs. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be a holomorphic self-map without fixed points. Let
\{n_{k}\}
be a record sequence for
z_{0}\in\Omega
and assume that
\{F^{n_{k}}(z_{0})\}
converges to some point
p\in\partial\Omega
. Then every
q\in T(F)\setminus\{p\}
is non-visible with respect to
p
. In particular, if
\Omega
is complex visible or essentially visible then it enjoys the Denjoy-Wolff property.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the one provided in
[
7
]
, but we give a version here for the sake of completeness.

By Lemma
4.2
, every point
q\in T(F)
is the limit of
\{F^{m_{k}}(z_{0})\}
for some sequence
\{m_{k}\}
converging to
\infty
. Let
q\in T(F)\setminus\{p\}
, and assume by contradiction that
q
is essentially visible with respect to
p
. Furthermore, assume
q=\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{m_{k}}(z_{0})
for some sequence
\{m_{k}\}
converging to
\infty
. Up to passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
m_{k}\leq n_{k}
for all
k
.

Since we are assuming by contradiction that
p
and
q
are essentially visible, there is a compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
and geodesic segments
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to\Omega
such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=F^{n_{k}}(z_{0})
,
\gamma_{k}(1)=F^{m_{k}}(z_{0})
and
\gamma_{k}(s_{k})\in K
for some
s_{k}\in(0,1)
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, there exists
C>0
such that
k_{\Omega}(z_{0},\gamma_{k}(s_{k}))\leq C
for all
k
. Hence, for all
k

\begin{split}k_{\Omega}(F^{m_{k}}(z_{0}),\gamma_{k}(s_{k}))&=k_{\Omega}(F^{m_{k}}(z_{0}),F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}))-k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{k}(s_{k}),F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}))\\
&\leq k_{\Omega}(F^{n_{k}-m_{k}}(z_{0}),z_{0})-k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{k}(s_{k}),F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}))\\
&\leq k_{\Omega}(F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}),z_{0})-k_{\Omega}(\gamma_{k}(s_{k}),F^{n_{k}}(z_{0}))\\
&\leq k_{\Omega}(z_{0},\gamma_{k}(s_{k}))\leq C.\end{split}

Since
k_{\Omega}
is complete, this implies that
\{F^{m_{k}}(z_{0})\}
is relatively compact in
\Omega
, a contradiction.
∎

Record sequences can also be used to create “sequential horospheres” which are invariant for a given holomorphic self-map.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
\{z_{n}\}\subset\Omega
be a sequence converging to some
p\in\partial\Omega
. Let
R>0
. We let

E_{z_{0}}(\{z_{n}\},R):=\{z\in\Omega:\limsup_{n\to\infty}[k_{\Omega}(z,z_{n})-k_{\Omega}(z_{0},z_{n})]<\frac{1}{2}\log R\}.

The set
E_{z_{0}}(\{z_{n}\},R)
, if not empty, is convex (see
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
) and it is a “sequential horosphere” of radius
R
. We have the following (cfr.
[
5
,
17
]
):

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be holomorphic and without fixed points. Let
\{n_{k}\}
be a record sequence for
z_{0}\in\Omega
and assume that
\{F^{n_{k}}(z_{0})\}
converges to some point
p\in\partial\Omega
. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

F^{\circ n}(z_{0})\in E_{z_{0}}(\{F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0})\},0).

In particular, if
\partial\Omega
does not have non-trivial analytic discs then

T(F)\subseteq\partial\Omega\cap\partial E_{z_{0}}(\{F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0})\},0).

Proof.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
n_{k}>n
(that is, for
k
sufficiently large)

\begin{split}k_{\Omega}(F^{\circ n}(z_{0}),F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0}))-k_{\Omega}(z_{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0}))&\leq k_{\Omega}(F^{\circ n_{k}-n}(z_{0}),z_{0})-k_{\Omega}(z_{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0}))\\
&\leq k_{\Omega}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0}),z_{0})-k_{\Omega}(z_{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0}))\leq 0.\end{split}

Therefore,
F^{\circ m}(z_{0})\in E_{z_{0}}(\{F^{\circ n_{k}}(z_{0})\},0)
. The last statement follows at once from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

The previous proposition implies that if the closure of any sequential horosphere of a bounded convex domain without non-trivial analytic discs on its boundary touches the boundary at exactly one point, then the domain possesses the Denjoy-Wolff property.

In
[
6
]
, it has been proven that for simply connected domains in
\mathbb{C}
(distinct from
\mathbb{C}
) the Denjoy-Wolff property is actually equivalent to the fact that the closure of any sequential horosphere touches the boundary of the domain at exactly one point.

Conjecture 4.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Assume
\partial\Omega
does not contain non-trivial analytic discs. Then
\Omega
has the Denjoy-Wolff property if and only if the closure of any sequential horosphere touches
\partial\Omega
at just one point.

In the recent paper
[
16
]
, the authors constructed examples of
C^{\infty}
-smooth bounded convex domains without non-trivial analytic discs on the boundary for which the Denjoy-Wolff property does not hold. Those domains are in fact strongly convex outside a real segment
I
sitting on the boundary which is made of points of infinite type. This resembles very much the situations which brought us to formulate Conjecture
3.5
, therefore, in
[
16
]
we formulated the following:

Conjecture 4.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded convex domain. Then
\Omega
is visible if and only if it enjoys the Denjoy-Wolff property.

Note that, if the answer to Question
3.7
were negative, and, more precisely, if non-visible bounded convex domains always have a complex geodesic
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
which does not have non-tangential limit (or more generally
H
-limits in the sense of
[
6
]
) at some point
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
then one can easily construct a holomorphic self-map
F
of
\Omega
without fixed points and such that its target set is not a singleton. Indeed, if this is the case, just take a parabolic automorphism
f
of
\mathbb{D}
with Denjoy-Wolff point at
\sigma
. Let
\rho:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
be a left-inverse of
\varphi
, then the holomorphic self-map
F:=\varphi\circ f\circ\rho
of
\Omega
has no fixed points in
\Omega
and its target set contains the non-tangential (or the
H
-)cluster set of
\varphi
at
\sigma
.

5.
The polydisc and product domains

M. Hervé
[
22
]
proved the following result: if
F:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
is a holomorphic self-map without fixed points then either
T(F)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\{e^{i\theta}\}
or
T(F)\subseteq\{e^{i\theta}\}\times\overline{\mathbb{D}}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. For the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{N}
,
N>2
, M. Abate and J. Raissy
[
5
, Corollary 5]
proved that if
F
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{N}
without fixed points then

T(F)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\ldots\times C_{j}(\xi)\times\ldots\overline{\mathbb{D}},

where
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{N}
and

C_{j}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\{\xi_{j}\}\quad\hbox{if }|\xi_{j}|=1,\\
\partial\mathbb{D}\quad\hbox{if }|\xi_{j}|<1.\end{cases}

However, there are no examples of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}^{N}
which have such a behavior. In fact, it is generally believed that the following conjecture is true:

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
F:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}^{N}
be holomorphic and without fixed points. Then there exist
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_{j}(F^{\circ n}(z))=\sigma
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{N}
, where
\pi_{j}(z)=z_{j}
(the projection on the
j
-th component).

In support of this conjecture we state some results. We start with a lemma which replaces Lemma
4.2
for the case of the polydisc:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Let
F:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}^{N}
be a holomorphic self-map without fixed points in
\mathbb{D}^{N}
. Let
p\in\mathbb{D}^{N}
and

T(F,p):=\{\sigma\in\partial(\mathbb{D}^{N}):\hbox{ there exists $\{n_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}$ such that }\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{\circ n_{k}}(p)=\sigma\}.

If
T(F,p)\subseteq\{1\}\times\overline{\mathbb{D}^{N-1}}
then
T(F)\subseteq\{1\}\times\overline{\mathbb{D}^{N-1}}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\partial(\mathbb{D}^{N})
,
q\in\mathbb{D}^{N}
and
\{n_{k}\}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{n_{k}}(q)=\sigma
. Since
T(F,p)\subseteq\{1\}\times\overline{\mathbb{D}^{N-1}}
it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi(F^{\circ n}(p))=1
and, in particular,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{1}(F^{n_{k}}(p))=1
.

Note that
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}
is holomorphic, hence it contracts the Kobayashi distance. Thus,

K_{\mathbb{D}}(\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(q)),\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(p))\leq K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(q)),F^{\circ n_{k}}(p))\leq K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(q,p)<+\infty.

Since
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n}(p))\}
converges to
1
, it follows that also
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(q))\}
converges to
1
, that is,
\pi_{1}(\sigma)=1
, and we are done.
∎

Here the number
1
does not play any special role and it can be clearly substitute with any unimodular number, likewise
\pi_{1}
can be substituted with any
\pi_{j}
. We also need the following result (see
[
3
, Theorem 0.7]
) which we state here in a simple form as needed for our aims:

Theorem 5.3

(Abate)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}
be holomorphic. Let
1\leq\nu\leq N
and let
\sigma=(1,\ldots,1,\sigma_{\nu+1},\ldots,\sigma_{N})\in\partial(\mathbb{D}^{N})
, with
|\sigma_{j}|<1
for all
j=\nu+1,\ldots,N
. Let
\underline{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
. Suppose that there exists a sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{D}^{N}
converging to
\sigma
such that
\{f(\xi_{k})\}
converges to
1
and there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}[K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},\xi_{k})-K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,f(\xi_{k}))]\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\alpha

Then

\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-f(r\sigma)}{1-r}\leq\alpha.

We can prove the following:

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Let
F:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}^{N}
be a holomorphic self-map without fixed points in
\mathbb{D}^{N}
. Then there exist
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, and
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
q_{m}\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{N}
so that for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}

(5.1)

\frac{|\sigma-\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(\zeta q_{m}))|^{2}}{1-|\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(\zeta q_{m}))|^{2}}\leq\frac{|\sigma-\zeta|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}.

Moreover, the constant map
\zeta\mapsto\sigma
is a limit (uniform on compacta) of
\{\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(\zeta q_{m}))\}
and if
g
is any other limit of
\{\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(\zeta q_{m}))\}
then

(1)

either
g
is the identity,

(2)

or,
g
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
without fixed points and with Denjoy-Wolff point
\sigma
.

Proof.

Let
\{n_{k}\}
be a record sequence for
F
with respect to
\underline{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
.

Up to extracting subsequences from
\{n_{k}\}
, we can assume that
\{F^{n_{k}}(\underline{0}))\}
converges to some point
q\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{N}
. Let
\pi_{j}:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}
be the projection on the
j
-th component. Now, for every
k
, there exists
j_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that

K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))=K_{\mathbb{D}}(\pi_{j_{k}}(\underline{0}),\pi_{j_{k}}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0})).

Since
\{n_{k}\}
converges to
+\infty
and
j_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
(a finite set), there exists
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that
j_{k}=j_{0}
for infinitely many
k
. Up to extracting subsequences we can thus assume that
K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))=K_{\mathbb{D}}(\pi_{j_{0}}(\underline{0}),\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))
for all
k
. Also, up to permuting coordinates, we can assume that
j_{0}=1
, that is, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.2)

K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))=K_{\mathbb{D}}(\pi_{1}(\underline{0}),\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0})).

Note that
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0})\}
converges to a point in
\partial\mathbb{D}
(since
\{K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))\}
—and hence
\{K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))\}
—converges to
+\infty
) and
\{F^{\circ n_{k}}\}
converges to a point in
\partial\mathbb{D}^{N}
. Thus,
\{(F^{n_{k}}(\underline{0})\}
converges to
(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{N})
, with
|q_{1}|=1
.

Let
\nu
be the number of
q_{j}
’s such that
|q_{j}|=1
,
1\leq\nu\leq N
. Up to permutation of coordinates we can assume that
|q_{j}|=1
for
j=1,\ldots,\nu
and
|q_{j}|<1
for
j=\nu+1,\ldots,N
. Then, replacing
F
with the
T^{-1}\circ F\circ T
, where
T
is the automorphism of
\mathbb{D}^{N}
given by

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\mapsto\left(q_{1}z_{1},\ldots,q_{\nu}z_{\nu},z_{\nu+1},\ldots,z_{N}\right),

we can assume that
\{F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0})\}
converges to
q:=(1,\ldots,1,\alpha_{\nu+1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})
where
|\alpha_{j}|<1
,
j=\nu+1,\ldots,N
.

Let
w_{k}:=F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0})
. Note that, by (
5.2
), we have

(5.3)

K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},w_{k})=K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(w_{k})),

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

w_{k}^{m}:=F^{\circ(n_{k}-m)}(\underline{0}).

Up to extracting subsequences if necessary, we can assume that
\{w_{k}^{m}\}
converges to some point
(q_{1}^{m},q_{2}^{m},\ldots,q_{N}^{m})\in\partial\mathbb{D}^{N}
. Note that for
j=1,\ldots,N

K_{\mathbb{D}}(\pi_{j}(w_{k}),\pi_{j}(w_{k}^{n}))\leq K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(w_{k},w_{k}^{m})\leq K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(F^{\circ m}(\underline{0}),\underline{0})<+\infty.

This implies that
\{\pi_{j}(w_{k}^{m})\}
converges to
1
for
j=1,\ldots,\nu
and
\{\pi_{j}(w_{k}^{m})\}
converges to some point in
\mathbb{D}
, that is,
\{w_{k}^{m}\}
converges to some point
q_{m}=(1,\ldots,1,\alpha_{\nu+1}^{m},\ldots,\alpha_{N}^{m})
for some
\alpha_{j}^{m}\in\mathbb{D}
,
j=\nu+1,\ldots,N
, as
k\to\infty
.
Also,

(5.4)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(w_{k}^{m}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(F^{\circ(n_{k}-m)}(\underline{0}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{1}(F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))=1.

Now, for every
n_{k}>m
,

(5.5)

\begin{split}&K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},w_{k-m})-K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(w_{k-m})))\\
&=K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ(n_{k}-m)}(\underline{0}))-K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(F^{\circ(n_{k}-m)}(\underline{0}))))\\
&\leq K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},F^{\circ n_{k}}(\underline{0}))-K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(w_{k}))\\
&=K_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},w_{k})-K_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\pi_{1}(w_{k}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Eq:equal-up-down}}}{{=}}0.\end{split}

Let
f:=\pi_{1}\circ F^{\circ m}:\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{D}
. Equations (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), with
\xi_{k}=w_{k-m}
, allows us to apply Theorem
5.3
to such an
f
. Hence,

\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(rq_{m}))}{1-r}\leq 1.

This implies that

\begin{split}&\liminf_{\mathbb{D}\ni\zeta\to 1}\frac{1-|\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(\zeta q_{m}))|}{1-|\zeta|}\leq\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(rq_{m}))|}{1-r}\\
&\leq\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{|1-\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m}(rq_{m}))|}{1-r}\leq 1.\end{split}

The classical Julia’s lemma (see,
e.g.
,
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.7]
) implies then (
5.1
)—with
j_{0}=1
.
Equation (
5.1
) implies that, if
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m_{k}}(\underline{0})\}
converges to some point in
\partial\mathbb{D}
, then actually
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m_{k}}(\underline{0})\}
converges to
1
.

Now, let
\{m_{k}\}
be a sequence such that
\{F^{\circ m_{k}}\}
converges uniformly on compacta and
q_{m_{k}}
converges to some
q_{\infty}=(1,\ldots,1,\beta_{\nu+1},\ldots,\beta_{N})
, where
\beta_{j}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
j=\nu+1,\ldots,N
. Thus,
\{\pi_{1}(F^{\circ m_{k}}(\zeta q_{m})\}
converges uniformly on compacta of
\mathbb{D}
to a holomorphic map
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}\cup\{1\}
.

Assume
g(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
. By (
5.1
), for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
we have

\frac{|1-g(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|g(\zeta)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|1-\zeta|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},

thus
g
is not constant, and it is either the identity or a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
with no fixed points and
1
is its Denjoy-Wolff point.
∎

Note that (
5.1
) and Lemma
5.2
imply that if

\liminf_{m\to\infty}k_{\mathbb{D}^{N}}(\underline{0},\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(\underline{0})))=+\infty,

then
\{\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(z)\}
converges to
\sigma
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{N}
as
m\to\infty
.

In general, we can ask the following question:

Question 5.5
.

Assume that

\Omega=D_{1}\times\ldots\times D_{\ell},

where
D_{j}
is a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N_{j}}
,
j=1,\ldots,\ell
and
N_{1}+\ldots+N_{\ell}=N\geq 2
. Suppose
D_{j}
has the Denjoy-Wolff property for every
j=1,\ldots,\ell
. Let
F:\Omega\to\Omega
be holomorphic and such that
F
has no fixed points in
\Omega
. Is it true that there exist
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and
p_{j_{0}}\in\partial D_{j_{0}}
such that
\{\pi_{j_{0}}(F^{\circ m}(z))\}
converges to
p_{j_{0}}
for all

as

?

From localized 4 ​ f 4f electrons to anisotropic exchange interactions in ferromagnetic CeRh 6 ​ Ge 4 \text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
Shoichiro Itokazu, Akimitsu Kirikoshi, Harald O. Jeschke, Junya Otsuki

Abstract
CeRh 6 ​ Ge 4 \text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} is a Ce-based ferromagnetic material exhibiting a quantum critical behavior under pressure. We derive effective exchange interactions, using the framework of density functional theory combined with dynamical mean-field theory. Our results reveal that the nearest-neighbor ferromagnetic interaction along the c c -axis is isotropic in spin space, leading to a formation of spin chains. On the other hand, the inter-chain coupling is highly anisotropic: The in-plane moment weakly interacts ferromagnetically in the a a – b b plane to stabilize the ferromagnetic state, whereas the z z -component couples antiferromagnetically, contributing to its destabilization. The magnetic anisotropy of the interchain interactions as well as of the local 4 ​ f 4f wavefunctions characterizes the magnetic properties underlying the ferromagnetic transition and the quantum critical behavior in CeRh 6 ​ Ge 4 \text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} .



I
Introduction

Cerium based ferromagnets are materials of high fundamental interest. The highly localized
4f
electrons of cerium can show Kondo lattice behavior where the localized spin is screened by conduction electrons and forms a Kondo singlet, leading to a correlated paramagnetic ground state
Hewson 1993
. This behavior is controlled by the strength of the hybridization between the localized moment and the conduction electrons. In the case of weak hybridization, the localized
4f
electrons can interact via the Ruderman–Kittel–Kasuya–Yosida (RKKY) interaction which is mediated by conduction electrons. The result can be magnetically ordered states.

A particularly interesting possibility in the case of cerium magnetism is a ferromagnetic ground state which is realized in a small but growing number of compounds such as
CeRuPO

Krellner et al. 2007
,
\text{CeRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{B}

Baumbach et al. 2012
and
CePt

J. et al. 2005
. The fact that ferromagnetic ordering temperatures
T_{\rm C}
are typically small means that there often are experimentally accessible tuning parameters like pressure that allow suppression of
T_{\rm C}
to zero temperature, providing access to a quantum phase transition instead of the usual thermal phase transition. The properties of materials near such a quantum critical point are highly nontrivial and interesting
Brando et al. 2016
. Ferromagnetic quantum critical points are often first order
Kirkpatrick and Belitz 2020
;
Shin et al. 2024
, as in
\text{UGe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Huxley et al. 2001
or in
UCoAl

Aoki et al. 2011
. Here, we plan to study
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
which is a rare example of a Kondo lattice system with ferromagnetic order at ambient pressure and a second-order ferromagnetic quantum critical point that is accessible at elevated pressures.

\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
exhibits a ferromagnetic transition at
T_{\textrm{C}}=2.5
K
Matsuoka et al. 2015
.
This compound has attracted significant interest due to the emergence of a ferromagnetic quantum critical point (QCP) under pressure
Kotegawa et al. 2019
;
Shen et al. 2020
.
To realize a QCP associated with a ferromagnetic transition, magnetic anisotropy plays a key role
Shen et al. 2020
, as it helps to avoid a first-order transition.
In this paper, we apply a recently developed first-principles method for calculating the magnetic susceptibility and derive low-energy effective interactions. We demonstrate that the exchange interations responsible for the ferromagnetic transition is highly anisotropic, stabilizing the in-plane ferromagneitc moment of
4f
electrons.

The electronic and magnetic properties of

Theoretically, two contrasting approaches exist for describing
4f
electron magnetism, depending on whether the
4f
electrons are treated as itinerant or localized.
In the case of
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, quantum oscillation measurements have shown that the Fermi surface is well described by models assuming localized
4f
electrons
Wang et al. 2021
.
Consistently, ARPES measurements have also detected signatures characteristic of a localized
4f
state
Wu et al. 2021
.
These experimental findings indicate that the localized picture provides a suitable starting point for understanding the electronic and magnetic properties of
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
.

We employ a method based on dynamical mean field theory combined with density functional theory (DFT+DMFT).
Our calculation procedure which successfully reproduced the antiferro-quadrupolar ordering in
\text{CeB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

Otsuki et al. 2024
is the following.
First, we perform a DFT calculation in which the
4f
electrons are treated as itinerant. Based on this electronic structure, we introduce the local Coulomb repulsion among the
4f
electrons via DMFT, leading to
4f
electrons with localized nature. We then calculate the momentum-dependent multipolar susceptibilities associated with the local
4f
degrees of freedom. By identifying the divergence in the susceptibility, we determine the transition temperature and the corresponding order parameter.
Using this approach, we will demonstrate that the ferromagnetic transition in
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
can be reproduced within the localized
4f
electron framework.

\displaystyle\hat{G}(\bm{k},\omega)=\left[(\omega+\mu)\hat{I}-\hat{H}(\bm{k})-\hat{\Sigma}_{\textrm{loc}}(\omega)+\hat{\Sigma}_{\textrm{DC}}\right]^{-1}.

III
Magnetic properties

To investigate the phase transitions in
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, we calculate the momentum-dependent static susceptibility, defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{ij}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{R}_{i}-\bm{R}_{j})}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\langle O_{i,m_{1}m_{2}}(\tau)O_{j,m_{3}m_{4}}\rangle,

(3)

where
m=-5/2,\cdots,+5/2
denotes the
z
component of
j=5/2
orbitals,
N
is the number of lattice sites, the argument
\tau
indicates the imaginary-time evolution in the Heisenberg picture, and the operator
O_{i,mm^{\prime}}
is given by

\displaystyle O_{i,mm^{\prime}}=f_{im}^{{\dagger}}f_{im^{\prime}}.

(4)

We evaluate
\chi_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}m_{4}}(\bm{q})
using the strong-coupling-limit (SCL) formula, which is derived from the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation within DMFT
Otsuki et al. 2019
;
Otsuki et al. 2024
.
Unlike the full BS equation, the SCL formula does not require computation of the vertex function, significantly simplifying the calculation.
When applied to
\text{CeB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, the SCL formula yields quantitative agreement with the results obtained from the full BS equation
Otsuki et al. 2024
, demonstrating its reliability in systems with localized
4f
electrons.

The momentum-dependent susceptibilities in the SCL formula are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}(\bm{q})=\left[\hat{\chi}_{\textrm{loc}}^{-1}-\hat{I}(\bm{q})\right]^{-1},

(5)

where all quantities with hat are matrices indexed by the combined indices
(m_{1}m_{2})
and
(m_{3}m_{4})
.

\hat{\chi}_{\textrm{loc}}
denotes the local susceptibility calculated from the atomic system, and
\hat{I}(\bm{q})
represents the intersite exchange interactions.
To evaluate
\hat{I}(\bm{q})
, we employ the two-pole approximation (SCL3)
Otsuki et al. 2019
;
Otsuki et al. 2024
, which captures the virtual excitations around the
4f^{1}
configuration. We use excitation energies
\Delta_{+}=3.1
eV and
\Delta_{-}=2.7
eV, corresponding to excitation from
4f^{1}
states to
4f^{2}
and
4f^{0}
configuration, respectively, as discussed in Sec.
II
.

A total of 36 eigenvalues are classified into 9 groups.

We determined the symmetry of the eigenmodes by analyzing the eigenvectors.
The group with the largest susceptibility corresponds to fluctuations within the CEF ground-state doublet
\ket{\pm 1/2}

and

The corresponding density operators, such as

Groups with dimension 1 consist of the totally symmetric representation

We focus on the leading fluctuations, which originate from the CEF ground-state doublet

IV
Discussion

The magnetic structure obtained by our calculations is illustrated in Fig.
1
(c).
The effective interaction responsible for this structure can be described as follows.
By retaining interactions up to the second nearest neighbors in Eq. (
15
), we obtain the effective Heisenberg model of spin
S=1/2
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\simeq

\displaystyle-J_{c}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\parallel c}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}

\displaystyle-J_{ab}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\perp c}\left[S_{i}^{x}S_{j}^{x}+S_{i}^{y}S_{j}^{y}-rS_{i}^{z}S_{j}^{z}\right],

(16)

where the first and the second summations represent nearest-neighbor pairs along the
c
axis and within the
a
–
b
plane, respectively.
From the numerical results in Table.
2
, we obtain
J_{c}=0.45
meV,
J_{ab}/J_{c}=0.087
, and
r=0.41
(note that
J
’s are twice of
I_{\xi}
).
The first term represents strong coupling of the Ce moments along the
c
-axis, leading to the formation of ferromagnetic chains.
These chains are aligned by weaker inter-chain interactions within the
a
–
b
plane (the second term), which collectively stabilize the ferromagnetic configuration shown in Fig.
1
(c).
Although the in-plane components of the moment are coherently aligned, the
c
-axis component is influenced by geometrical frustration arising from the inter-chain antiferromagnetic coupling. This frustration suppresses out-of-plane magnetic order and is reflected in the momentum dependence of the interaction
I_{z}(\bm{q})
, whose maximum appears at the M point of the Brillouin zone [Fig.
4
(d)].

In Ref.
Shen et al. 2020
, the strange-metal behavior around the QCP is analyzed by the Heisenberg Hamiltonian, assuming XXZ-type anisotropy with weak
S^{z}
interaction. Our microscopic derivation of the effective Heisenberg model supports their starting point, which leads to linear dispersion of the magnetic excitations.
Furthermore, the weak inter-chain coupling guarantees a second-order phase transition, enabling the emergence of the QCP under pressure
Shen et al. 2020
.

Finally, we discuss the origin of the ferromagnetic transition in
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
.
The
\bm{q}
-dependence of the RKKY interaction is governed by the Fermi surface of conduction electrons.
Figure
6
displays the Fermi surface evaluated from
A(\bm{k},\omega)
at
\omega=0
.
As shown in Figs
6
(a)–(e), the Fermi surface is isotropic in
k_{x}
–
k_{y}
plane.
The cut at the
k_{y}=0
plane in Fig
6
(e) reveals a Fermi surface spans the entire
k_{z}
range, indicating a deformed cylindrical shape—characteristic of two-dimensional systems.
These features are consistent with previous DFT results assuming localized
4f
electrons
Wang et al. 2021
.
It is known that the two-dimensional free electrons exhibit a plateau in the static susceptibility
\chi_{0}(\bm{q})
for
|\bm{q}|<2k_{\textrm{F}}
, followed by a decay for
|\bm{q}|>2k_{\textrm{F}}
, where
k_{\textrm{F}}
is the Fermi wavenumber.
We suggest that the weakly three-dimensional cylindrical Fermi surface gives rise to the peak at
\Gamma
point in our numerical results.

V
Summary

We investigated the ferromagnetism in
\text{CeRh}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
using DFT+DMFT method, treating the Ce
4f
electrons as localized.
The intersite exchange interactions, evaluated using the strong-coupling-limit (SCL) formula, revealed dominant ferromagnetic coupling along the
c
-axis, forming spin chains that are isotropic in spin space. In contrast, the inter-chain coupling is anisotropic: ferromagnetic for the in-plane moment and antiferromagnetic for the out-of-plane moment. These interactions give rise to a magnetic structure in which the magnetic moment is aligned within the
a
–
b
plane.

The calculated transition temperature is in reasonable agreement with the experimental value, although the mean-field nature of the intersite interaction in DMFT tends to overetimate the transition temperature.
Our results provide theoretical support
for anisotropic exchange interactions between localized
4f
moments,
offering a foundation for understanding the anomalous metallic behavior observed near the ferromagnetic QCP under pressure.

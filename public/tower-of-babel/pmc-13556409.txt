Are we missing an important point? Risk factors for intraoperative complications and additional procedures when performing post-chemotherapy RPLND for testicular tumors
Authors: Mesut Altan, Hakan Keskin, Sertac Yazici
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aims to investigate the risk factors associated with additional intraoperative procedures during post-chemotherapy retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (PC-RPLND) in patients with advanced germ-cell tumors.Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the data of 206 patients who underwent PC-RPLND for advanced germ-cell tumors between January 1990 and December 2022. Additional intraoperative procedures were defined as surgical interventions necessitated by injuries to intestinal, ureteral, major vascular, or renal vascular structures, as well as planned en-bloc resections. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of these additional procedures.ResultsA total of 67 additional procedures were performed in 56 patients (27.2%). The most frequent interventions were vascular procedures (aortic, caval, or renal vessel repairs), occurring in 40 patients (19.4%). This was followed by en-bloc nephrectomy in 10 (4.9%), intestinal injury repair in 9 (4.4%), and ureteral injury repair in 8 (3.9%) patients. Multivariate analysis revealed that tumor size at initial diagnosis (P < .05) and the presence of viable tumor in the pathology specimen (P < .05) were independent risk factors for requiring additional intraoperative procedures.ConclusionTumor size at the time of initial diagnosis, rather than preoperative size, serves as a significant predictor for additional surgical procedures during PC-RPLND. Surgical planning should account for initial tumor burden and the likelihood of viable disease to optimize perioperative outcomes.

Introduction

Although rare, the incidence of testis tumor shows significant differences between geographical areas. Between 1970 and 2004, the worldwide incidence raised from 2.1 to 5.1 per 100 000 [1]. A major milestone in the management of advanced testicular cancer was the introduction of cisplatin-based chemotherapy, which has improved survival rates to 70%, even among patients with a poor prognosis [2, 3].

Post-chemotherapy retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (PC-RPLND) remains a critical therapeutic and staging modality for advanced germ-cell tumors (GCTs). Furthermore, PC-RPLND significantly contributes to the survival of patients who harbor viable tumor cells despite preoperative chemotherapy. It is regarded as one of the most technically demanding procedures in urological surgery. Due to the inherent complexity of the procedure and the frequent necessity for major additional interventions to achieve complete resection of residual masses, PC-RPLND should ideally be performed at high-volume, experienced centers.

To ensure complete oncological resection, there is a reported 20%–33% risk of requiring additional procedures, which commonly include major vascular repair, nephrectomy, or the management of adjacent organ injuries [4]. This study aims to identify the risk factors associated with these additional procedures and to present our institutional experience with supplementary interventions during PC-RPLND.

Material and methods

Study population and design

Following approval from the Institutional Review Board (Protocol No: GO19/675), we retrospectively identified 216 patients who underwent PC-RPLND for non-seminomatous germ-cell tumors between January 1990 and January 2022. Patients undergoing salvage or desperation RPLND were excluded from the analysis. Consequently, a total of 206 patients were included in the study.

Preoperative evaluation and surgical technique

All patients underwent baseline evaluation with thoracoabdominal computed tomography (CT) and serum tumor markers following orchiectomy. These assessments were repeated after the completion of first-line platinum-based chemotherapy. Patients with persistently elevated tumor markers received second-line chemotherapy. PC-RPLND was performed via an open transabdominal approach upon normalization of tumor markers, provided a residual retroperitoneal mass was present. The choice between a full bilateral template or a modified template RPLND was made at the discretion of the attending surgeon [5].

Definition of additional procedures

Additional procedures were defined as any surgical intervention extending beyond the scope of a standard PC-RPLND. These included both planned en-bloc resections (e.g. nephrectomy for tumor involvement) and the management of intraoperative injuries, such as vascular, ureteral, or intestinal repairs.

Variables and data collection

Tumor size (mm) was defined as the longest transverse diameter of the retroperitoneal mass on CT. For each patient, tumor sizes were recorded at two time points: prior to chemotherapy (initial diagnosis) and prior to PC-RPLND (post-chemotherapy). Patients’ preoperative characteristics—including tumor sizes at both intervals, the presence of embryonal carcinoma in the orchiectomy specimen, primary tumor location (testicular side or extragonadal origin), and The International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group risk classification—were analyzed in relation to the necessity for additional procedures.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). The level of significance was set at P < .05. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was utilized to determine optimal cut-off values for tumor size. The normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Since the continuous variables (blood loss) did not follow a normal distribution, they were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test and reported as medians with ranges. For univariate analysis, the Chi-squared test and Mann–Whitney U test were employed for nominal and non-parametric data, respectively. To identify independent predictors of additional procedures, variables with a P-value <.05 in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate logistic regression model.

Results

The median age of the patients was 28.9 ± 8.7 years, with a median follow-up duration of 51.5 months (range, 4–253). According to the IGCCCG risk classification, 64 patients (31.1%) were categorized as having an intermediate or poor prognosis. A total of 52 patients (25.2%) received second-line chemotherapy prior to PC-RPLND. The median estimated blood loss was significantly higher in patients who required additional procedures compared to those who did not (700 ml vs. 150 ml; P < .001). Similarly, postoperative complications were significantly more frequent and severe in the additional procedure group (P < .001). While 92% of patients without additional procedures had minor or no complications (Clavien-Dindo 0–1), major complications (Clavien-Dindo > 2) were predominantly observed in patients undergoing supplementary interventions. The primary tumor was extragonadal in 16 patients (7.8%), and embryonal carcinoma was identified in 125 (67.2%) of the orchiectomy specimens (Table 1).

Discussion

PC-RPLND, alongside chemotherapy, remains one of the most critical treatment modalities for advanced GCTs. Its significance has become increasingly clear with a better understanding of testicular lymphatic drainage and advancements in chemotherapy regimens. A primary indication for PC-RPLND is the management of residual teratoma following chemotherapy. Due to its chemo-resistant nature, surgical resection is the only curative option for teratoma, which otherwise carries risks of growing teratoma syndrome and malignant transformation [6–8]. Moreover, PC-RPLND offers substantial survival benefits for the approximately 15% of patients who harbor viable tumors. In these cases, achieving complete oncological resection is the cornerstone of long-term survival [9–11].

PC-RPLND is a formidable surgical challenge due to the complex retroperitoneal anatomy, the primary tumor’s metastatic burden, and the impact of prior systemic therapy. Consequently, it carries a significant risk of perioperative surgical complications as well as systemic issues, such as pulmonary complications. To ensure complete resection, there is a reported 20%–33% risk of requiring additional procedures, including major vascular repair, nephrectomy, splenectomy, pancreatic resection, hepatic resection, and bowel resection [12–15]. Surgical experience is a decisive factor in managing these risks. A study involving 993 PC-RPLND patients across multiple centers demonstrated that high-volume centers were associated with lower complication rates, though they often reported higher transfusion rates and costs [16]. Furthermore, longitudinal data suggests that as institutional experience grows, the necessity for additional procedures, hospital length of stay, and overall complications tend to decrease. While the primary goal is complete resection, additional procedures are often inevitable when retroperitoneal organs are invaded or when chemotherapy-induced desmoplastic reactions obliterate the surgical planes between the lymph nodes and surrounding tissues.

Existing literature regarding specific risk factors for additional procedures is limited. Baniel et al. reported 144 complications in 125 patients (20.7%) within a series of 603 PC-RPLND cases [17]. Notably, 93% of these patients had a residual mass ≥5 cm, with an overall mortality rate of 0.8% [15]. Similarly, Cary et al. reported an additional procedure rate of 22.1% and a complication rate of 3.7%, identifying mass size, elevated serum markers, and RPLND pathology as significant predictor [18].

Contrary to primary RPLND, PC-RPLND is associated with significantly higher complication and additional procedure rates. This is primarily attributed to the intense desmoplastic reactions induced by chemotherapy and the inherently higher disease stage. Previous studies have consistently identified mass size and final pathology as predictors for these interventions. Our study aimed to clarify whether pre-chemotherapy or post-chemotherapy (pre-PC-RPLND) tumor size more accurately correlates with the need for additional procedures. We found that pre-chemotherapy tumor size was an independent predictor. This may be explained by the fact that even if a chemotherapy-sensitive tumor regresses, it often leaves behind extensive scar tissue. Much like the “burn-out” phenomenon in testicular cancer, this residual tissue often contains dense fibrosis, calcifications, and hemorrhage, as previously described by Dorantes-Heredia et al. [19]. To our knowledge, this is the first study to specifically investigate the predictive value of pre-chemotherapy mass size on the necessity for additional procedures. Notably, seminoma patients were excluded from this analysis because post-chemotherapy seminomas often exhibit even more intense desmoplastic reactions, making complete resection particularly challenging [20–22].

The main limitation of our study is its retrospective design. Additionally, the data spans 35 years, a period during which treatment modalities, surgical techniques, and RPLND template boundaries have significantly evolved.

Conclusion

PC-RPLND, in conjunction with systemic chemotherapy, remains a cornerstone in the management of advanced GCTs. To achieve complete oncological resection within the established template, additional intraoperative procedures are frequently necessary. When assessing the likelihood of requiring such interventions during the preoperative period, clinicians should prioritize the evaluation of initial tumor burden. Specifically, the tumor size at the time of diagnosis, rather than the post-chemotherapy residual mass size, serves as a significant independent predictor for the necessity of additional surgical procedures.

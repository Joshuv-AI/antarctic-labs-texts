Introduction

All graphs are assumed to be finite and simple, meaning that they have no loops or multiple edges. The vertex set and edge set of a graph
G
are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively.

Graph complexes are simplicial complexes constructed from graphs by encoding their combinatorial structure into higher-dimensional simplices. In topological combinatorics, graph complexes are one of the main objects of study.
Various topological invariants of these complexes, such as homotopy type, connectivity, homology, and their refinements, provide powerful tools for understanding combinatorial properties of graphs.
A classical and one of the most celebrated examples of graph complexes is the neighbourhood complex, introduced by L. Lovász in 1978
[
29
]
. Lovász gave a lower bound for the chromatic number of a graph in terms of the topological connectivity of its neighbourhood complex, and using this bound, he proved the Kneser conjecture
[
27
]
.

Graph complexes are prevalent across the literature, and have applications in various areas of mathematics, engineering, and data analysis. They are central to graph theory and combinatorics, where the topology of these complexes is related to the combinatorics of the underlying graphs (see
[
1

13
,
31

33
,
34

[
9

17

Graph complexes also appear extensively in commutative algebra, where they are intricately related to monomial ideals and resolutions (see

In representation theory, they are used in the study of symmetric group actions on simplicial complexes and associated homological representations

Graph complexes have connections to geometric group theory, where they serve as combinatorial models for classifying spaces and group actions (see

2.
Preliminaries

This section presents some basic definitions and results used in this article.

2.1.
Graph

A
graph

G
is a pair
(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is its vertex set and
E(G)\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2}
is the edge set. For any
u,v\in V(G)
, we say that
u
and
v
are adjacent if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.
We write
u\sim v
for adjacency and
u\nsim v
for non-adjacency. A
subgraph

H
of
G
is a graph with
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
.
For a subset
U\subseteq V(G)
, the
induced subgraph

G[U]
is the subgraph with
V(G[U])=U
and
E(G[U])=\{\{a,b\}\in E(G)\ |\ a,b\in U\}
.

For
u,v\in V(G)
, a
path
from
u
to
v
is a sequence of distinct vertices
u=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}=v
such that
v_{i}\sim v_{i+1}
for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
. The
length
of a path is the number of edges in the path.
A graph is
connected
if there exists a path between each pair of its vertices; otherwise, it is
disconnected
.

The
complete graph

K_{n}
on
n
vertices is the graph in which any two distinct vertices are adjacent. For
n\geq 1
, the
path graph

P_{n}
is the graph with
V(P_{n})=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
E(P_{n})=\{\{i,i+1\}\,|\,0\leq i\leq n-2\}
.
For
n\geq 3
, the
cycle graph

C_{n}
is the graph with
V(C_{n})=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
E(C_{n})=\{\{i,i+1\}\,|\,0\leq i\leq n-2\}\cup\{\{0,n-1\}\}
.

We refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
41
]
for more details about the graph terminologies used in this article.

2.2.
Simplicial complex

A
finite abstract simplicial complex

\Delta
is a collection of finite sets such that if
\tau\in\Delta
and
\sigma\subset\tau
, then
\sigma\in\Delta
.
The elements of
\Delta
are called
simplices
of
\Delta
. If
\sigma\subset\tau
, we say that
\sigma
is a
face
of
\tau
. The
dimension of a simplex

\sigma
is equal to
|\sigma|-1
. The
dimension of an abstract simplicial complex
is the maximum of the dimensions of its simplices.
If a simplex has dimension
d
, it is said to be
d
-
dimensional
. The
0
-dimensional simplices are called
vertices
of
\Delta
. An abstract simplicial complex which is an empty collection of sets is called the
void
abstract simplicial complex, and is denoted by
\emptyset
.
A simplex that is not a face of any other simplex is called a
maximal simplex
or
facet
. A
subcomplex

\Delta^{\prime}
of
\Delta
is a simplicial complex such that
\sigma\in\Delta^{\prime}
implies
\sigma\in\Delta
.

In this article, we consider any simplicial complex as a topological space, namely, its geometric realization (see
[
28
]
for details). For terminologies related to algebraic topology, we refer to
[
23
]
.

2.3.
Discrete Morse theory

Discrete Morse theory was introduced by Robin Forman
[
18
]
in 1998 as a combinatorial adaptation of classical Morse theory. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
25
, Chapter 4]
and
[
28
, Chapter 11]
, which serve as the primary sources for the definitions and results in this section.

Throughout this section, let
X
be a set and let
\Sigma
be a finite family of finite subsets of
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
matching
on
\Sigma
is a family
\mathcal{M}
of pairs
\{\sigma,\tau\}
with
\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma
such that no set is contained in more than one pair in
\mathcal{M}
.

A set
\sigma\in\Sigma
is said to be
matched
in
\mathcal{M}
if it is contained in some pair of
\mathcal{M}
; for convenience, we write
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
. Otherwise,
\sigma
is said to be
critical
or
unmatched
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
, and we write
\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A matching
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
partial
if
\{\sigma,\tau\}\in\mathcal{M}
implies either
\sigma\prec\tau
(
i.e.
,
\sigma\subset\tau
and no
\rho\in\Sigma
satisfies
\sigma\subset\rho\subset\tau
), or
\sigma\succ\tau
.

Note that
\mathcal{M}
is a partial matching on
\Sigma
if and only if there exists
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\Sigma
and an injective map
\mu:\Sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\mu(\sigma)\succ\sigma
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A partial matching
\mathcal{M}
on
\Sigma
is called
acyclic
if there does not exist a cycle

\mu(\sigma_{1})\succ\sigma_{1}\prec\mu(\sigma_{2})\succ\sigma_{2}\prec\mu(\sigma_{3})\succ\sigma_{3}\prec\dots\prec\mu(\sigma_{t})\succ\sigma_{t}\prec\mu(\sigma_{1}),\ t\geq 2.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
x\in X
. A matching
\mathcal{M}_{x}
is called an
element matching
on
\Sigma
using
x
if every pair in
\mathcal{M}_{x}
is of the form
\{\sigma\setminus\{x\},\sigma\cup\{x\}\}
for some
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

It is easy to observe that any element matching is a partial matching.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
25
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let
x\in X
. Define

\mathcal{M}_{x}:=\{\{\sigma\setminus\{x\},\sigma\cup\{x\}\}\,|\,\sigma\setminus\{x\},\sigma\cup\{x\}\in\Sigma\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be an acyclic matching on
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\{\sigma\in\Sigma\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{x}\}
. Then
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{x}\cup\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is an acyclic matching on
\Sigma
.

Let
\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\}
be a subset of
X
. We define a sequence of element matchings
\mathcal{M}_{x_{0}},\mathcal{M}_{x_{1}},\ldots,

\mathcal{M}_{x_{n-1}}
on
\Sigma
as follows.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}:=\Sigma
. For each
0\leq i\leq n-1
, define

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{x_{i}}

\displaystyle:=\{\{\sigma\setminus\{x_{i}\},\sigma\cup\{x_{i}\}\}\,|\,\sigma\setminus\{x_{i}\},\sigma\cup\{x_{i}\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\};

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle:=\{\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{x_{i}}\}.

Observe that
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{x_{j}}\text{ for all }0\leq j\leq i\}
. Moreover, the matchings
\mathcal{M}_{x_{0}},\mathcal{M}_{x_{1}},\ldots,

\mathcal{M}_{x_{n-1}}
are pairwise disjoint.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
0\leq i\leq n-1
. Then the following results hold:

(i)

\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i-1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\Sigma
.

(ii)

For any
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
if and only if
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
or
\sigma\cup\{i\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
.

(iii)

Let
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
and let
i\leq j\leq n-1
. If
\sigma\setminus\{r\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
or
\sigma\cup\{r\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
for all
i\leq r\leq j
, then
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{j+1}
.

To define a matching on a simplicial complex, we consider it simply as the set of its simplices (or faces).

Theorem 2.7

(
[
25
, Theorem 4.14]
)
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex and let
\mathcal{M}
be an acyclic matching on
\Delta
such that the empty set is not critical. Then
\Delta
is homotopy equivalent to a cell complex with one cell of dimension
d\geq 0
for each critical face of
\Delta
of dimension
d
plus one additional
0
-cell.

Theorem
2.7
implies the following result.

Corollary 2.8

(
[
25
, Theorem 4.8]
)
.

If an acyclic matching on a simplicial complex
\Delta
has all its critical faces in the same dimension
d
, then
\Delta
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres of dimension
d
.

Remark 3.1

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{v}

\displaystyle:=\{\{\sigma\setminus\{v\},\sigma\cup\{v\}\}\,|\,\sigma\setminus\{v\},\sigma\cup\{v\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)\};

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle:=\{\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{v}\}.

Then, for any

, the following statements hold:

(i)

\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)

\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if every disconnected

contains

(ii)

If
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
and
\sigma\cup\{u\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)

.

(iii)

Proof.

(i)

Since
\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
is a simplicial complex,
\sigma\cup\{v\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
implies
\sigma\setminus\{v\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
. It follows that
\mathcal{M}_{v}=\{\{\sigma\setminus\{v\},\sigma\cup\{v\}\}\,|\,\sigma\cup\{v\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)\}.

By the definition of
\bm{\mathcal{C}}
,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{v}
, which means that
\{\sigma\setminus\{v\},\sigma\cup\{v\}\}\notin\mathcal{M}_{v}
.
Since
\{\sigma\setminus\{v\},\sigma\cup\{v\}\}\notin\mathcal{M}_{v}
if and only if
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
, it follows that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
.

By Remark
3.1
,
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
and
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
if and only if every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
v
. Therefore,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
v
.

(ii)

Let
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
and
\sigma\cup\{u\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
. If
u\in\sigma
, then clearly
\sigma\cup\{u\}=\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
. Hence, assume that
u\notin\sigma
. By
(i)
,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
implies
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
. Hence
(\sigma\cup\{u\})\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
. Therefore,
\sigma\cup\{u\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
by
(i)
.

(iii)

Let
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
by
(i)
. By Remark
3.1
,
u_{1}\sim u_{2}
for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in(\sigma\cup\{v\})^{c}
.
Now, assume further that
u\sim w
for all
w\in(\sigma\cup\{v\})^{c}
. Then
v_{1}\sim v_{2}
for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in((\sigma\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\})^{c}
. By Remark
3.1
,
(\sigma\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
. Therefore,
\sigma\setminus\{u\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}
by
(i)
.

∎

To prove Theorems
1.3
to
1.5
, we construct acyclic matchings on the total
2
-cut complex
\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
of the graph
G
under consideration.
This is done by selecting an appropriate subset of vertices and defining a sequence of element matchings as in Equation (
1
).
Our aim is to characterize the critical faces with respect to the matching. Then, using Theorem
2.7
and Corollary
2.8
, we determine the homotopy type of
\Delta_{2}^{t}(G)
.

3.1.
Powers of cycle graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. We assume throughout that
p\geq 1
is an integer. Recall that the
p
-th power of a cycle graph
C_{n}^{p}
is the graph on the vertex set
V(C_{n}^{p})=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
, where for any distinct vertices
u,v\in V(C_{n}^{p})
, we have
u\sim v
in
C_{n}^{p}
if and only if
v\equiv u\pm t\ (\text{mod $n$})
for some
1\leq t\leq p
.

Before proving Theorem
1.3
, we establish several key results. We begin by defining a sequence of element matchings
\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
on
\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
and the sets
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0},\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
, using vertices
0,1,\ldots,n-1
, as in Equation (
1
).
By Lemma
2.5
,
\mathcal{M}:=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{M}_{i}
is an acyclic matching on
\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
. To proceed, we characterize the sets
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
, thus identifying the critical faces with respect to the matching
\mathcal{M}
.

Note that
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
and
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\{\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{0}\}
. To characterize
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, we apply Proposition
3.2
with
v=0
. We characterize
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
inductively in Proposition
3.3
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
For each
1\leq i\leq p
, we define a subset
\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq V(C_{n}^{p})
as follows:

\mathcal{X}_{i}:=\{i+p+1,i+p+2,\ldots,i+n-p-1\}.

Clearly, for
n\geq 2p+2
and for all
1\leq i\leq p
, we have
\mathcal{X}_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2p+2
and
1\leq i\leq p
. If
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, then
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
if and only if
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{i}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction on
i
for
1\leq i\leq p
. We first prove the base case
i=1
.
Let
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. It suffices to show that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
if and only if
1\in\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

•

Suppose
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. We first show that
1\in\sigma
. Assume, for contradiction, that
1\notin\sigma
. Since
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, there exists a disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
by Remark
3.1
.
Moreover, by Proposition
3.2

(i)
, every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
0
. Hence, there exists
v\in V(C_{n}^{p})
such that
v\nsim 0
and
\{0,v\}\subseteq\sigma^{c}
. Since
0\sim 1
, we get
v\neq 1
.
Therefore,
\{0,v\}\subseteq(\sigma\cup\{1\})^{c}
, and by Remark
3.1
,
\sigma\cup\{1\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
. By Proposition
3.2

(ii)
,
\sigma\cup\{1\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.
Further, since
\sigma\setminus\{1\}=\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, it follows by Remark
2.6

(ii)
that
\sigma\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, a contradiction. Thus,
1\in\sigma
.

Now, we show that
\mathcal{X}_{1}\not\subseteq\sigma
. Suppose
\mathcal{X}_{1}\subseteq\sigma
. Then
\mathcal{X}_{1}\subseteq\sigma\cup\{0\}
. By the definition of
C_{n}^{p}
,
1\nsim u
if and only if
u\in\{p+2,p+3,\ldots,n-p\}=\mathcal{X}_{1}
. Hence
1\sim v
for all
v\in(\sigma\cup\{0\})^{c}
.
By Proposition
3.2

(iii)
,
\sigma\setminus\{1\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. Since
\sigma\cup\{1\}=\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, we get
\sigma\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
by Remark
2.6

(ii)
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathcal{X}_{1}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

•

Conversely, assume that
1\in\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}\not\subseteq\sigma
.
Since
1\nsim u
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, there exists
v\in\sigma^{c}
such that
1\nsim v
. Moreover, since
1\sim 0
, we have
v\neq 0
. It follows that
\{1,v\}
is a disconnected
2
-set in
((\sigma\setminus\{1\})\cup\{0\})^{c}
.
By Remark
3.1
,
(\sigma\setminus\{1\})\cup\{0\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, and hence
\sigma\setminus\{1\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
by Proposition
3.2

(i)
. Since
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, we conclude that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
by Remark
2.6

(ii)
.

Therefore, the result holds for
i=1
.

Let
1<i\leq p
and assume the result holds for all
1\leq i^{\prime}<i
; that is, if
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i^{\prime}}
, then
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i^{\prime}+1}
if and only if
\{1,2,\ldots,i^{\prime}\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{i^{\prime}}\not\subseteq\sigma
.
We now prove the result for
i
. Let
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
. We show that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
if and only if
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{i}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

•

Suppose
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
. By Remark
2.6

(i)
,
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. Hence
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
and
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{j+1}
for all
1\leq j<i
. By the induction hypothesis, for each such
j
, we have
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

We first prove that
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
. Since
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma
for all
1\leq j<i
, we have
\{1,2,\ldots,i-1\}\subseteq\sigma
.

Suppose
i\notin\sigma
. Since
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
0
by Proposition
3.2

(i)
.
Moreover,
1<i\leq p
implies that
0\sim i
. It follows that there exists
v\in\sigma^{c}\setminus\{i\}
such that
v\nsim 0
and
\{0,v\}\subseteq(\sigma\cup\{i\})^{c}
. Hence
\sigma\cup\{i\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
by Remark
3.1
. By Proposition
3.2

(ii)
,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
i\neq 0
implies that
\sigma\cup\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.

Since
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma
for all
1\leq j<i
, we get
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma\cup\{i\}
for all
1\leq j<i
. Note that
n\geq 2p+2
implies
p+1\leq n-p-1
. Further, since
1<i\leq p
, for any
1\leq j<i
, we have
1<i\leq p<j+p+1\leq j+n-p-1<i+n-p-1\leq n-1
.
This gives
i\notin\mathcal{X}_{j}
for all
1\leq j<i
. Therefore,
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma
for all
1\leq j<i
implies
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma\cup\{i\}
for all
1\leq j<i
.

We have
\sigma\cup\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, and for each
1\leq j<i
,
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma\cup\{i\}
and
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma\cup\{i\}
.
Since
\sigma\cup\{i\}
satisfies the conditions of the induction hypothesis for each
1\leq j<i
, by iterative application of the induction hypothesis, we have
\sigma\cup\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{j+1}
for all
1\leq j<i
.
In particular, for
j=i-1
, we get
\sigma\cup\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
. Then
\sigma\setminus\{i\}=\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
implies that
\sigma\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
by Remark
2.6

(ii)
, a contradiction. Hence
i\in\sigma
.

Since
\{1,2,\ldots,i-1\}\subseteq\sigma
, we conclude that
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
.

We now show that
\mathcal{X}_{i}\not\subseteq\sigma
. Suppose
\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq\sigma
. Since
1<i\leq p
, we have
0\notin\mathcal{X}_{i}
. Moreover, by the definition of
C_{n}^{p}
,
i\nsim u
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq\sigma\cup\{0\}
, and
i\sim v
for all
v\in(\sigma\cup\{0\})^{c}
. By Proposition
3.2

(iii)
, we get
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.

For each
1\leq j<i
, we have
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma
. Clearly,
\{1,2,\ldots,j\}\subseteq\sigma\setminus\{i\}
and
\mathcal{X}_{j}\not\subseteq\sigma\setminus\{i\}
for each
1\leq j<i
.
Since
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, applying the induction hypothesis iteratively to
\sigma\setminus\{i\}
, we have
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{j+1}
for all
1\leq j<i
. Taking
j=i-1
, we get
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
.
Since
\sigma\cup\{i\}=\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, Remark
2.6

(ii)
implies
\sigma\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
, a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{X}_{i}\not\subseteq\sigma
.

•

Conversely, assume
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{i}\not\subseteq\sigma
. Since
1<i\leq p
, we have
i\nsim u
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
, and
i\sim 0
.
Hence there exists
v\in\sigma^{c}\setminus\{0\}
such that
i\nsim v
, which implies that
\{i,v\}
is a disconnected
2
-set in
((\sigma\setminus\{i\})\cup\{0\})^{c}
. So,
(\sigma\setminus\{i\})\cup\{0\}\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
.
Therefore,
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
by Proposition
3.2

(i)
. Since
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
by Remark
2.6

(i)
,
\sigma\setminus\{i\}\notin\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
. By Remark
2.6

(ii)
,
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
implies
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Although Proposition
3.3
holds for all
n\geq 2p+2
, we apply it here only to the case

. For

, we employ a different approach.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\tau=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
. For
n=2p+2
,
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}=\{\tau\}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
C_{2p+2}^{p}
,
\{0,p+1\}
is the only disconnected
2
-set containing
0
in
V(C_{2p+2}^{p})
.

First, assume that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}
. We prove
\sigma=\tau
. By Remark
2.6

(i)
,
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
. Hence
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
and
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
By Proposition
3.3
, for each
1\leq i\leq p
, we obtain
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}\subseteq\sigma
and
i+p+1\notin\sigma
. It follows that
\{1,2,\ldots,p\}\subseteq\sigma
and
\{p+2,p+3,\ldots,2p+1\}\subseteq\sigma^{c}
.
Since
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, Proposition
3.2

(i)
implies that every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
0
. Hence
\{0,p+1\}\subseteq\sigma^{c}
. This means that
\sigma=\{1,2,\ldots,p\}
, and thus
\sigma=\tau
.

We now prove that
\tau\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}
. Observe that
\{0,p+1\}
is the only disconnected
2
-set in
\tau^{c}
. Hence
\tau\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{2p+2}^{p})
and
\tau\cup\{0\}\notin\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{2p+2}^{p})
. By Proposition
3.2

(i)
,
\tau\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.
Since
\{1,2,\ldots,p\}\subseteq\tau
and

, it follows that

and

\tau\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}
.

\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{p+1}=\{\tau\}
.
∎

We have set up the necessary results for
n=2p+2
. Now,
we address the case
n\geq 3p+1
.
For
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, define

m_{\sigma}:=\max(\sigma^{c}).

By Remark
3.1
, if
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, then
C_{n}^{p}[\sigma^{c}]
is not a complete graph. From the definition of
C_{n}^{p}
, we then obtain the following.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
. Then
m_{\sigma}\in\{p+1,p+2,\ldots,n-1\}
.

For
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, we have
m_{\sigma}-p\geq 1
by Remark
3.5
. From Remark
2.6

(i)
,
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{m_{\sigma}-p}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. The proposition below establishes the reverse inclusion
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{m_{\sigma}-p}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
n\geq 3p+1
. If
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, then
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
and
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{m_{\sigma}-p}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
. We first show that
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
. Since
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
, we have
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
. Hence
m_{\sigma}\in\{p+1,p+2,\ldots,n-1\}
by Remark
3.5
. Clearly,
m_{\sigma}\neq 0
.
By Proposition
3.2

(i)
, every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
0
(as
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
). Therefore, since
m_{\sigma}\in\sigma^{c}
, it follows that if there exists
v\in V(C_{n}^{p})
such that
v\neq 0
and
v\nsim m_{\sigma}
, then
v\in\sigma
. We consider the following cases depending on the value of
m_{\sigma}
:

•

Suppose
m_{\sigma}\in\{p+1,p+2,\ldots,n-p-1\}
. Then
1\leq m_{\sigma}-p<m_{\sigma}<m_{\sigma}+p\leq n-1
. Since
\max(\sigma^{c})=m_{\sigma}
, it follows that
\{m_{\sigma}+1,m_{\sigma}+2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}+p\}\subseteq\sigma
.
Let
u\in\{1,2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}-p-1\}\cup\{m_{\sigma}+p+1,m_{\sigma}+p+2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Then
u\neq 0
and
u\nsim m_{\sigma}
, which implies
u\in\sigma
. Hence
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
.

•

Suppose
m_{\sigma}=n-p
. Then
1<n-2p=m_{\sigma}-p<m_{\sigma}<m_{\sigma}+p-1=n-1
and
m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})=0
. It follows that
\{m_{\sigma}+1,m_{\sigma}+2,\ldots,n-1\}\subseteq\sigma
.
Now, let
u\in\{1,2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}-p-1\}
. Observe that
u\neq 0
and
u\nsim m_{\sigma}
. Hence
u\in\sigma
, which implies
\{1,2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}-p-1\}\subseteq\sigma
. Therefore
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
.

•

Suppose
m_{\sigma}\in\{n-p+1,n-p+2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Since
\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(C_{n}^{p})
and every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
0
, there exists
w\in\sigma^{c}
such that
w\nsim 0
. By the definition of
C_{n}^{p}
,
w\in\{p+1,p+2,\ldots,n-p-1\}
.

Observe that
1\leq m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})<p+1\leq w\leq n-p-1<m_{\sigma}\leq n-1
. Since
n\geq 3p+1
, we have
m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})<m_{\sigma}-2p
. Moreover,
p\geq 1
implies
m_{\sigma}-2p<m_{\sigma}-p<m_{\sigma}
.
Therefore
m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})<w,m_{\sigma}-p<m_{\sigma}
. If
w<m_{\sigma}-p
, then
w\nsim m_{\sigma}
, which implies
w\in\sigma
(as
w\neq 0
), a contradiction. So
w\geq m_{\sigma}-p
.

It follows that
1\leq m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})<m_{\sigma}-2p\leq w-p<w<w+p\leq n-1
.
Let
u\in\{1,2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})\}
. Then
u\neq 0
and
w\nsim u
, which implies
\{u,w\}
is a disconnected
2
-set not containing
0
. Since
w\in\sigma^{c}
, we get
u\in\sigma
, and thus
\{1,2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})\}\subseteq\sigma
.
Now, let
u^{\prime}\in\{m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})+1,m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})+2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}-p-1\}
. Then
u^{\prime}\neq 0
and
u^{\prime}\nsim m_{\sigma}
, which implies
u^{\prime}\in\sigma
. Hence
\{m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})+1,m_{\sigma}+p\ (\text{mod $n$})+2,\ldots,m_{\sigma}-p-1\}\subseteq\sigma
. Moreover,
\max(\sigma^{c})=m_{\sigma}
implies that
\{m_{\sigma}+1,m_{\sigma}+2,\ldots,n-1\}\subseteq\sigma
. This means that
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
.

Therefore,
\sigma^{c}\subseteq\{0,m_{\sigma}-p,m_{\sigma}-p+1,\ldots,m_{\sigma}\}
.

We now prove that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{m_{\sigma}-p}
. If
m_{\sigma}=p+1
, then
m_{\sigma}-p=1
. Since

(iii)

3.3.
Cartesian product of complete graphs and cycle graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
. For a positive integer
q
, we use the notation
[q]
and
[0,q]
from the previous section. The Cartesian product
K_{m}\square C_{n}
has vertex set

V(K_{m}\square C_{n})=V(K_{m})\times V(C_{n})=\{(i,j)\mid i\in[0,m-1]\text{ and }j\in[0,n-1]\}.

For any distinct
(i_{1},j_{1}),\,(i_{2},j_{2})\in V(K_{m}\square C_{n})
,
(i_{1},j_{1})\sim(i_{2},j_{2})
if and only if
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
|j_{1}-j_{2}|\in\{1,n-1\}
, or
j_{1}=j_{2}
. Equivalently,
(i_{1},j_{1})\nsim(i_{2},j_{2})
if and only if
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
1<|j_{1}-j_{2}|<n-1
, or
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
and
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
follows the same approach as that of Theorem
1.4
. The only difference arises from the adjacency of vertices
0
and
n-1
in the cycle graph
C_{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime}
denote the graph
K_{m}\square C_{n}
. We write
(j)_{0}
for a vertex of the form
(0,j)\in V(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
. We define a sequence of element matchings
\mathcal{M}_{(0)_{0}},\mathcal{M}_{(1)_{0}},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{(n-1)_{0}}
on
\Delta_{2}^{t}(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
and the sets
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0},\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
, using vertices
(0)_{0},(1)_{0},\ldots,(n-1)_{0}
, as in Equation (
1
).
Then
\mathcal{M}:=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{M}_{(i)_{0}}
is an acyclic matching on
\Delta_{2}^{t}(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
. We have
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\Delta_{2}^{t}(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
and
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\{\sigma\in\Delta_{2}^{t}(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})\,|\,\sigma\notin\mathcal{M}_{(0)_{0}}\}
.

The following result is analogous to Proposition
3.11
.

Proposition 3.13
.

(i)

Theorem 3.14
.

For
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 4
,
\Delta_{2}^{t}(K_{m}\square C_{n})\simeq\bigvee_{n(m-1)+1}\mathbb{S}^{mn-4}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\{\sigma\subseteq V(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})\,|\,\sigma^{c}=\{(0)_{0},(n-2)_{0},(n-1)_{0}\}\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\{\sigma\subseteq V(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})\,|\,\sigma^{c}=\{(0)_{0},(i,0),(i,n-1)\}\text{ for some }i\in[m-1]\},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\{\sigma\subseteq V(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})\,|\,\sigma^{c}=\{(0)_{0},(i,j-1),(i,j)\}\text{ for some }i\in[m-1],\,j\in[n-1]\}.

Note that
A,\,B
, and

are pairwise disjoint. We show that
\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{n}=A\sqcup B\sqcup C
.

Assume first that
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
. Then
\sigma\in\bm{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}^{t}(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
. Therefore, Proposition
3.2

(i)
implies that every disconnected
2
-set in
\sigma^{c}
contains
(0)_{0}
.
This means that
\sigma^{c}=\{(0)_{0},(i,j)\}\cup X

and

such that
(i,j)\nsim(0)_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime}[X\cup\{(i,j)\}]
is a complete graph. Since
(i,j)\in V(\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime})
, we have
i\in[0,m-1]
.

•

Suppose
i=0
. Then
(i,j)=(j)_{0}\nsim(0)_{0}
implies

. Since
\mathcal{G}_{mn}^{\prime}[X\cup\{(j)_{0}\}]
is a complete graph,
X
satisfies one of the following conditions: (i)
X=\emptyset
, (ii)
X=\{(j-1)_{0}\}

X=\{(n-1)_{0}\}
when

, or (iv)
X\subseteq\{(i^{\prime},j)\,|\,i^{\prime}\in[m-1]\}
with

.

Using Proposition
3.13

3.12

.

•

Suppose

(i,j)\nsim(0)_{0}

.

\sigma\in B\sqcup C
.

We conclude that

.

.

•

Suppose
\sigma\in A

(i)
.

(ii)

The powers of cycle graphs
C_{n}^{p}
are circulant graphs
C_{n}(\{1,2,\ldots,p\})
and hence Cayley graphs of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. This raises the following natural question.

Question 4.2
.

What are the homotopy types of total cut complexes and cut complexes of general circulant graphs? More broadly, what about arbitrary Cayley graphs?

We have also determined the homotopy types of
\Delta_{2}^{t}(K_{m}\square P_{n})=\Delta_{2}(K_{m}\square P_{n})
and
\Delta_{2}^{t}(K_{m}\square C_{n})=\Delta_{2}(K_{m}\square C_{n})
. Using SageMath, we further investigated the total
3
-cut and
3
-cut complexes of these Cartesian products.
We computed their homology groups (with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
) for some small values of
m
and
n
, which are presented in Tables
2
,
3
,
4
and
5
.

{i}:\mathbb{Z}^{\beta}
denotes that the
i^{\text{th}}
homology group is
\mathbb{Z}^{\beta}
, and
\ast
indicates that the corresponding entry is not known.
The patterns observed in these tables lead us to Conjectures
4.3

4.6

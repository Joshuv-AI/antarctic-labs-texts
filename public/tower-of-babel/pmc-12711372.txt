When the Rectal Protection Barrier Causes Complications: Recognizing Spacer-Induced Rectal Fistula
Authors: Hamza Almusabeh, Wesley Wright, Asad Pervez
Published: 2025

CASE REPORT

A 78-year-old man with prostate cancer after placement of a rectal spacer, SpaceOAR, for radiation therapy. He presented to the hospital with hematochezia for 2 weeks. He reported that days before presentation, he had undergone his first session of prostate radiation therapy. A computed tomography angiography of the abdomen and pelvis (Figure 1) demonstrated no evidence of active hemorrhage. Subsequent colonoscopy revealed a 1 × 1.5-cm deep mucosal defect located approximately 1 cm proximal to the dentate line, consistent with a fistulous opening (Figure 2). A 12/6t OVESCO clip was successfully placed to close the fistulous tract (Figure 3). At follow-up, there was no evidence of recurrent bleeding.

Computed tomography angiography of the abdomen and pelvis demonstrated no extravasation of contrast to suggest active hemorrhage.

SpaceOAR is a biodegradable hydrogel injected between the rectum and prostate.1 It acts by increasing the distance between these structures, thereby decreasing rectal radiation dose and toxicity.1,2 Reported complications include rectal wall infiltration of the gel, rectum-prostate adhesions, perforation, and fistula formation.3–5 In our case, it appears the complication was secondary to hydrogel placement rather than radiation exposure, as the patient had received only 2 radiation fractions at the time of presentation. We hypothesized that this could be explained by a preexisting subtle rectal wall defect induced by self-rectal examination, as the patient admitted to performing this before the procedure, exacerbated by hydrogel-induced mechanical pressure and chronic inflammation. This case highlights that although the hydrogel spacer is intended to reduce radiation-induced rectal injury, it may paradoxically cause rectal damage.

Two families of C1-Pk Fraeijs de Veubeke-Sander finite elements on quadrilateral meshes
Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. We extend the C 1 C^{1} - P 3 P_{3} Fraeijs de Veubeke-Sander finite element to two families of C 1 C^{1} - P k P_{k} ( k > 3 k>3 ) macro finite elements on general quadrilateral meshes. On each quadrilateral, four P k P_{k} polynomials are defined on the four triangles subdivided from the quadrilateral by its two diagonal lines. The first family of C 1 C^{1} - P k P_{k} finite elements is the full C 1 C^{1} - P k P_{k} space on the macro-mesh. Thus the element can be applied to interface problems. The second family of C 1 C^{1} - P k P_{k} finite elements condenses all internal degrees of freedom by moving them to the four edges. Thus the second element method has much less unknowns but is more accurate than the first one. We prove the uni-solvency and the optimal order convergence. Numerical tests and comparisons with the C 1 C^{1} - P k P_{k} Argyris are provided.

has

k-1
zero points on the line,
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}^{\perp})^{2}}p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}=0.

With it, we rewrite (
3.6
) as

(3.11)

\displaystyle p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}}=\lambda_{1}^{3}p_{k-3}\quad\hbox{\rm{ for some \ }}p_{k-3}\in P_{k-3}({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}).

If
k>3
, the above
p_{k-3}
is not a constant yet.
We have
m=3
now in (
3.1
) by (
3.11
).
We repeat all the work above on
\partial_{({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}^{\perp})^{3}}p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}^{\perp})^{3}}p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}=0,\ \hbox{\rm{and}}\\
&p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}}=\lambda_{1}^{4}p_{k-4}\quad\hbox{\rm{ for some \ }}p_{k-4}\in P_{k-4}({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}).\end{aligned}

It would be repeated until

(3.12)

\displaystyle p|_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}}=\lambda_{1}^{k}c_{1}\quad\hbox{\rm{ for some \ }}c_{1}\in P_{0}({\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}).

On triangle
{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}
, cf. Figure
3
, as
\partial_{{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{0}^{\perp}}p
is continuous
across edge
{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{4}
,
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle p|_{{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}}=\lambda_{4}^{k}c_{4}\quad\hbox{\rm{ for some \ }}c_{4}\in P_{0}({\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}),

where
\lambda_{4}
is a linear function vanishing on
{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}
and assuming value 1
at
{\bf x}_{0}
.

By (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), as
p
is continuous,
we get

(3.14)

\displaystyle c_{1}=c_{4}.

By (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), as
\partial_{{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{4}}p
is continuous on two triangles
{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}

and
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}
(not known yet on the other two triangles),
we get

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} k\lambda_{1}^{k-1}c_{1}\|{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}\|^{-1}&=-k\lambda_{1}^{k-1}c_{4}\|{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{0}\|^{-1},\\
c_{1}&=-c_{1}\frac{\|{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}\|}{\|{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{0}\|}=0.\end{aligned}

Thus
p=0
on the two triangles,
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}
and
{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}
.
We could not use this proof on the other two triangles due to a missing
normal derivative constraint in (
2.4
).

By the
C^{1}
continuity across
{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{0}
, as
p=0
on the other side of the edge, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{4}^{\perp}}(\partial_{{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}}p_{2})({\bf x}_{0})=0.

By the
C^{1}
continuity across
{\bf x}_{4}{\bf x}_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{4}^{\perp}}(\partial_{{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}}p_{3})({\bf x}_{0})=0.

Together, they imply that the two above
P_{k-1}
polynomials,

\partial_{{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}}p_{2}
and
\partial_{{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}}p_{3}
, have both tangent derivatives
vanishing at
{\bf x}_{0}
, at the two sides of edge
{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}
.
By (
2.3
), in addition, these two polynomials are continuous at
k-1
points on the edge

{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}
.
Thus they are continuous at
{\bf x}_{0}{\bf x}_{3}
, i.e.,
p
is also
C^{1}
across
{\bf x}_{3}{\bf x}_{0}
.
Repeating the work on the other two triangles, we get
p=0
. The lemma is proved.
∎

.

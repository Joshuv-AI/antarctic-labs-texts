Universality of Colored Scalars from the Stringy KLT Kernel
Christoph Bartsch 1, Karol Kampf 1 \,{}^{1}, Jiří Novotný 1, Jaroslav Trnka 2

Abstract
A new perspective on the inverse string theory Kawai-Lewellen-Tye (KLT) kernel is provided which establishes the universality of scattering amplitudes in the bi-adjoint scalar (BAS) theory, pions in the Non-linear sigma model (NLSM), and mixed amplitudes (NLSM+ ϕ 3 \phi^{3} ) recently studied in the literature. We show that all these amplitudes can be viewed as equivalent, arising from a single function, the inverse string theory KLT kernel, evaluated at different kinematic points. In this way cubic colored scalars and pions become interchangeable through a procedure we call the α ′ \alpha^{\prime} -shift. The latter complements the δ \delta -shift proposed by Arkani-Hamed et al., and demonstrates an inherent equivalence of scattering amplitudes in different quantum field theories by embedding them in a common stringy framework.



I. Introduction

String-inspired techniques play an important role in discovering new properties of scattering amplitudes for point particles. In particular, a lot of progress was made recently in understanding close relationships between amplitudes of bi-adjoint scalars (BAS), pions in the non-linear sigma model (NLSM) and gluons in Yang-Mills theory from the kinematic surface picture
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2025
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024b
.
At the heart of their unity lies a kinematic deformation, the
\delta
-shift, which parametrizes a family of worldsheet integrals yielding the desired amplitudes in various limits
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024c
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024d
.
The connection is particularly immediate for scalars as field theory amplitudes for pions can be directly extracted from those of cubic scalars in the BAS theory, even at loop-level
Arkani-Hamed and Figueiredo 2024a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
.

Another well-studied connection between BAS and NLSM amplitudes is via their stringy extensions into (non-)abelian
Z
-functions
Broedel et al. 2013
;
Mafra and Schlotterer 2017
;
Carrasco et al. 2017a
;
Carrasco et al. 2017b
. There, relations between cubic scalars and pions, as well as mixed amplitudes involving both (NLSM+BAS) are established via a procedure known as (semi-)abelianization.

All the aforementioned amplitudes share another string-derived property: the duality between color and kinematics. As a consequence, amplitudes of cubic scalars, pions and gluons, among others, can be used as input in a procedure known as the double copy to generate permutationally invariant amplitudes for gravity, the special Galileon and more
Bern et al. 2024
. A central object in this context is the string theory KLT kernel
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2011

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma|\rho\right]

which was first discovered by observing the double-copy structure of closed and open string amplitudes
Kawai et al. 1986
,

\displaystyle M^{\text{closed}}=\!\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma,\rho\in S_{n-3}}\!\!\!\!A^{\text{open}}\left[\sigma\right]\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma|\rho\right]A^{\text{open}}\left[\rho\right].

In this letter we set out to connect the world of the
\delta
-shift and that of the (semi-)abelianization procedures established in the context of (non-)abelian
Z
-theory. This will be accomplished by studying the simplest known stringy extension of BAS amplitudes provided by the inverse
m^{\alpha^{\prime}}\equiv\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{-1}
of the string theory KLT kernel
Mizera 2017a
;
Mizera 2017b
.
Consequently, we will establish that the KLT kernel encodes not only the scattering of cubic scalars but also pion and mixed amplitudes in the NLSM+BAS theory.

To extract this information we will first study their connection by analogy to
Z
-theory using (semi-) abelianization to obtain stringy functions related to the scattering of pions. A simple general structure observed in these functions then naturally suggests the construction of a stringy variant of the
\delta
-shift, which we call the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift. We will argue that the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift establishes a fundamental equivalence between cubic scalars and pions which may be most succinctly summarized as

\text{BAS}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\text{NLSM}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=(\text{NLSM}+\text{BAS})_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\text{KLT}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{-1}.

That is to say, all amplitudes for scalars and pions can be obtained from the
same function
, the inverse string theory KLT kernel, evaluated on appropriately
\alpha^{\prime}
-shifted kinematics.

II. The Inverse String Theory KLT Kernel

In
Mizera 2017a
;
Mizera 2017b
Mizera initiated the study of the matrix inverse of the KLT kernel
m^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\equiv\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{-1}
and showed that its matrix elements
m^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma|\rho\right]
are simple kinematic functions. Explicit examples for these matrix elements include the three-point function
m_{3}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]=1
as well as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hskip-5.69046ptm_{4}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]=\frac{1}{t_{13}}{+}\frac{1}{t_{24}},\hskip 2.84544ptm_{5}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]=\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{13}t_{14}}{+}\text{cyc.}\!\right){+}1,\\
&\hskip-5.69046ptm_{4}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\left[\mathds{1}|1243\right]=-\frac{1}{s_{13}},\hskip 2.84544ptm_{5}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\left[\mathds{1}|13245\right]=\frac{1}{s_{14}}\!\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{13}}{+}\frac{1}{t_{24}}\!\right)\!,\end{split}

(1)

at four and five points. We denote by
\mathds{1}
the identity permutation of
n
labels and introduce the
\alpha^{\prime}
-dependent planar Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle t_{ij}=\tan\left(\pi\alpha^{\prime}X_{ij}\right),\hskip 14.22636pts_{ij}=\sin\left(\pi\alpha^{\prime}X_{ij}\right),

(2)

which are stringy versions of the field theory
X
-variables
X_{ij}=(p_{i}+\dots+p_{j-1})^{2}

From (

m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
depend only on
t_{ij}
whereas off-diagonal elements
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\rho\right]

\rho\neq\mathds{1}
generically involve
t_{ij}
and
s_{ij}
.

Taking the infinite tension limit
\alpha^{\prime}{\to}\,0
of
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
yields partial amplitudes of the bi-adjoint scalar (BAS) theory,

\displaystyle m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma|\rho\right]=(\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{3-n}\big(m_{n}\left[\sigma|\rho\right]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime})\big).

(3)

A crucial feature of the stringy matrix elements
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
is the presence of an infinite tower of odd-point interactions
Mizera 2017a
(c.f. the five-point contact term in (
1
)). In fact, diagonal matrix elements
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
can be computed as sums over all Feynman diagrams constructed from the given odd-point vertices and propagators
t_{ij}^{-1}
.
For instance, at six points, the diagonal matrix element is schematically given by the diagrams

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\hskip-7.96674pt\vskip 0.0pt\includegraphics[width]{figures/6ptDiags.pdf}\end{matrix}

(4)

In the limit
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
only the cubic topologies contribute to the field theory BAS amplitude
m_{6}\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
by (
3
), while the topology involving the five-point vertex is sub-leading. Nevertheless, sub-leading-
\alpha^{\prime}

Given the diagram topologies for
m_{n}\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
, off-diagonal matrix elements
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\rho\right]

\mathds{1}

are replaced by
s_{ij}^{-1}

III. Pions via Abelianization

Let us now define an
abelianized
function

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma\right]\equiv\frac{1}{2}\!\sum_{\rho(2\dots n)}\!\!m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\sigma|1\,\rho\right],

(5)

as a sum over one of the orderings of the inverse KLT kernel. Here we adopt the nomenclature of the
Z
-theory literature
Broedel et al. 2013
;
Mafra and Schlotterer 2017
;
Carrasco et al. 2017a
;
Carrasco et al. 2017b
, where the notion of abelianization relates so-called non-abelian and abelian
Z
-functions. Both are closely related to the inverse KLT kernel
Mizera 2017a
.

A simple example of (
5
) is provided by the abelianized four-point function,

\displaystyle A^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{4}\!\left[\mathds{1}\right]=\!\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{13}}{-}\frac{1}{s_{13}}\!\right){+}\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{24}}{-}\frac{1}{s_{24}}\!\right)=-\left(\tau_{13}+\tau_{24}\right).

(6)

Here we introduce the half-angle variables

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha^{\prime}X_{ij}\right),

(7)

which are the natural kinematic variables for abelianized functions, as we will see. They are related to the variables
t_{ij},\,s_{ij}
of the inverse KLT kernel via trigonometric half-angle identities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{ij}}+\frac{1}{s_{ij}}=\frac{1}{\tau_{ij}},\hskip 14.22636pt\frac{1}{t_{ij}}-\frac{1}{s_{ij}}=-\tau_{ij}.

(8)

Expanding the four-point function (
6
) in powers of
\alpha^{\prime}
we obtain
A^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{4}=-\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{2}(X_{13}+X_{24})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime 3})
at leading order which is the four-pion NLSM amplitude.

This holds true more generally. For even multiplicity
n=2k
the low-energy limit of the abelianized functions (
5
) is given by NLSM pion amplitudes,

\displaystyle A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{2}A_{2k}^{\text{NLSM}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime 3}).

(9)

An analogous statement to (
9
) also holds for the leading-
\alpha^{\prime}
contribution to abelian
Z
-functions
Carrasco et al. 2017b
. However, the abelianized functions
A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
provide a stringy completion of pion amplitudes that is distinct from abelian
Z
-theory at higher orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
. In particular,
Z
-functions are defined in terms of disk integrals for which analytic results in
\alpha^{\prime}
are hard to obtain when
n\geq 5
.

On the other hand, the abelian functions (
5
) turn out to be simple rational functions of the stringy variables
\tau_{ij}
. For
n=2k
even, their structure is closely related to that of field theory pion amplitudes in the NLSM. Considering pion amplitudes
A_{2k}^{\text{NLSM}}(X_{ij})
directly as functions of invariants
X_{ij}
the general functional form of the abelianized function (
5
) is schematically given by

\displaystyle A^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{2k}(\tau_{ij})=A_{2k}^{\text{NLSM}}(\tau_{ij})+\text{higher order in }\tau_{ij}.

(10)

That is, the leading
\mathcal{O}(\tau_{ij})
contribution to the stringy pion functions (
10
) can be obtained by taking the field theory amplitude
A_{2k}^{\text{NLSM}}(X_{ij})
and replacing
X_{ij}\mapsto\tau_{ij}
.
For example, the four-point function (
6
) can be seen as a realization of the general structure (
10
) where higher-order corrections in
\tau_{ij}
are absent.

More broadly, the stringy pion functions (
10
) exhibit various properties familiar from their field theory analogues in the NLSM. For one, they consistently factorize as functions of
\tau_{ij}
on poles corresponding to odd-particle channels,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{\tau_{\text{odd}}=0}A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!(\tau)=A_{L}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!(\tau_{L})\,A_{R}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!(\tau_{R})

(11)

whereas there are no poles for even-particle channels at
\tau_{\text{even}}=0
.
Moreover, they obey the Adler zero
Adler 1965
,

\displaystyle\lim_{p_{i}\to 0}A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=0,

(12)

retaining a highly characteristic feature of pion amplitudes at finite
\alpha^{\prime}
.

m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
are obtained from Feynman diagrams we want to study how the abelianization (
5
) acts on individual diagram topologies. To illustrate, we consider an example at
n=6
provided by the first diagram in (
4

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\vskip-2.84544pt\includegraphics[width]{figures/6pt1diag.pdf}\end{matrix}\!\!\!=\frac{\theta(\rho)}{x_{13}(\rho)x_{14}(\rho)x_{15}(\rho)}\,.

(13)

Here

equals

or

depending on
\rho

is an overall sign depending on the specifics of

Abelianizing the diagram amounts to summing (

Carrying out the sum we find a simple factorized form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\!\!\!\sum_{\rho(2\dots 6)}\!\!\!\!\eqref{abel61}=\!\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{13}}{-}\frac{1}{s_{13}}\!\right)\!\!\!\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{14}}{+}\frac{1}{s_{14}}\!\right)\!\!\!\left(\!\frac{1}{t_{15}}{-}\frac{1}{s_{15}}\!\right)\!=\frac{\tau_{13}\tau_{15}}{\tau_{14}},

Proceeding to abelianize all remaining diagrams in (

\displaystyle A_{6}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\tau_{13}+\tau_{24})(\tau_{46}+\tau_{15})}{\tau_{14}}-\tau_{13}-\frac{1}{3}\tau_{13}\tau_{35}\tau_{15}+\text{cyc.}

This shows that the abelianization is a sensible operation locally for each diagram, not just at the level of the full matrix element as in (

In (

Replacing

IV. Pions via
\alpha^{\prime}
-Shift

We have seen that abelianization naturally distinguishes even- and odd-particle multiplicity channels
x_{\text{even}}
and
x_{\text{odd}}
as exemplified by the structure of (
18
) and the replacement rules (
19
). The latter can be realized through a direct manipulation of the stringy kinematic variables (
2
). Recalling the basic trigonometric identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tan\left(x\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}=-\tan\left(x\right),

(20)

the replacement (
19
) can be implemented in a two-step process.
For
n=2k
even, we consider the diagonal inverse KLT matrix element
m_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
. First, we rescale

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto\alpha^{\prime}/2,

(21)

which effectively takes
t_{ij}\mapsto\tau_{ij}
for any
ij
. Then we shift the kinematic
X
-variables according to

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{\text{even}}\equiv X_{\text{even}}\pm 1/\alpha^{\prime},\hskip 14.22636pt\widehat{X}_{\text{odd}}\equiv X_{\text{odd}},

(22)

which, via (
20
), effects the replacement (
19
). We will refer to (
22
) and the corresponding rescaling of
\alpha^{\prime}
as the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift.
Considering now the diagonal inverse KLT kernel
m_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
and the abelianized functions
A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
directly as functions of
X_{ij}
and
\alpha^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\boxed{A_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(X_{ij})=m_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}\!\!/2}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right](\widehat{X}_{ij}).}

(23)

This can be read as a statement of
equivalence between cubic scalars and pions
. Indeed, (
23
) asserts that scalars and pions, including a suitable stringy extension, both arise from
the same function

m_{2k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
evaluated on different kinematics and values of
\alpha^{\prime}
as in (
21
) and (
22
).

We emphasize that (
23
) is a true equivalence as the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift (
22
) is invertible. That is, we can start with the abelianized function
A_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
, rescale
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto 2\alpha^{\prime}
and shift
X_{\text{even}}\mapsto X_{\text{even}}\pm 1/(2\alpha^{\prime})
,
X_{\text{odd}}\mapsto X_{\text{odd}}
to arrive back at the original form of the inverse KLT kernel
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
.

The
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift (
22
) for the kinematic
X
-variables formally closely resembles the
\delta
-shift proposed in
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
;
Arkani-Hamed and Figueiredo 2024a
. For field theory amplitudes in the
\text{tr}\phi^{3}
theory, the
\delta
-shift takes
X_{oo}\mapsto X_{oo}+\delta
,
X_{ee}\mapsto X_{ee}-\delta
which allows to directly extract pion amplitudes by expanding the shifted scalar amplitudes at
\delta=\infty
. In this context
X_{ee},X_{oo}
refer to kinematic variables
X_{ij}
whose labels
ij
are both even (
ee
) or both odd (
oo
), corresponding exactly to the even-multiplicity channels
X_{\text{even}}
also shifted by (
22
).

However, we should emphasize that while the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift bears a close formal resemblance to the
\delta
-shift, the two are essentially different in the way in which they connect scalars and pions. Nevertheless, the question arises whether both shifts can be seen as vestiges of an underlying structure yet to be discovered in these amplitudes.

Finally, let us remark on a peculiar feature of the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift (
22
). Because the diagonal inverse KLT matrix element is purely a function of tangents
t_{ij}
, the trigonometric identity (
20
) ensures that the sign in the shift
X_{oo/ee}\mapsto X_{oo/ee}\pm 1/\alpha^{\prime}
can be chosen arbitrarily, leaving the result (
23
) unchanged.
This property is not shared by the
\delta
-shift, where the relative signs of the shift for different kinematic variables matter. However, the sign ambiguity of the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift will be useful when we want to construct mixed amplitudes that involve both cubic scalars and pions.

V. Mixed Amplitudes

We now turn to discuss mixed amplitudes of cubic scalars
\phi
and pions
\pi
in a theory commonly referred to as NLSM+
\phi^{3}
. These amplitudes were first discovered by studying soft limits of pion amplitudes
Cachazo et al. 2016
and more recently appear in factorizations of pion amplitudes near so-called “hidden” zeros
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024d
. Furthermore, various classes of mixed field theory amplitudes can be obtained from an appropriate
\delta
-shift of the tr
\phi^{3}
theory
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
.
A simple example is the five-point mixed amplitude involving three adjacent scalars
\phi
and two pions
\pi
,

\displaystyle M_{5}(\phi\pi\pi\phi\phi)=-\frac{X_{13}+X_{24}}{X_{14}}-\frac{X_{24}+X_{35}}{X_{25}}+1,

(24)

which appears in the soft limit and hidden factorizations of the six-point pion amplitude (
16
).

Returning to the stringy inverse KLT kernel, we will define stringy mixed functions via a procedure called
semi-abelianization
in analogy to semi-abelianized
Z
-functions
Carrasco et al. 2017a
. For us, a general
n
-point mixed function
M_{n}^{n_{\phi},\alpha^{\prime}}
will involve
n_{\phi}
cubic scalars with labels
\{1\}\cup\phi=\{1\,\phi_{2}\dots\phi_{n_{\phi}}\}
and
n_{\pi}=n-n_{\phi}
pions labeled
\pi=\{\pi_{1}\dots\pi_{n_{\pi}}\}
such that
\phi\cup\pi=\{2\dots n\}
.
It is defined via semi-abelianization of the inverse stringy KLT kernel,

\displaystyle M_{n}^{n_{\phi},\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!\left(1\,\phi_{2}\dots\phi_{n_{\phi}}\pi_{1}\dots\pi_{n_{\pi}}\right)=\sum\nolimits_{\widehat{\rho}}\,m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|1\widehat{\rho}\,\right],

(25)

where the sum is over
\widehat{\rho}=\phi\shuffle\{\pi_{n_{1}}\}\shuffle\dots\shuffle\{\pi_{n_{\pi}}\}
and
\shuffle
is the shuffle product
Carrasco et al. 2017a
. Generically the orderings
\widehat{\rho}\subseteq\rho(2\dots n)
summed over in the semi-abelianization are a subset of those in the full abelianization (
5
). For the special case
\phi=\{\}
the sums in (
25
) and (
5
) agree. At low energies, the semi-abelianized functions yield the corresponding field theory mixed amplitudes
M_{n}^{n_{\phi}}(1\phi\dots\pi\dots)
,

\displaystyle M_{n}^{n_{\phi},\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!=(\pi\alpha^{\prime})^{k}M_{n}^{n_{\phi}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime k+1})

(26)

where the leading power in
\alpha^{\prime}
is
k=3-n_{\phi}
if
n_{\pi}
is even and
k=4-n_{\phi}
if
n_{\pi}
is odd.

In the following, we will show that certain mixed functions (
25
) can alternatively be obtained, like the pure pion functions (
23
), from various
\alpha^{\prime}
-shifts of the diagonal inverse KLT matrix element
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
.
Since the space of all mixed functions is vast, we cannot be comprehensive and instead give illustrative examples.
Throughout we will only study mixed functions involving an even number of pions
n_{\pi}
as they correspond to stringy extensions of the NLSM+
\phi^{3}
amplitudes discussed originally in
Cachazo et al. 2016
.

To arrive at suitable
\alpha^{\prime}
-shifts for our subsequent discussion of mixed functions, we follow the prescription of
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
for the
\delta
-shift regarding which
X
-variables need to be shifted to obtain a given mixed amplitude. For our purposes we then replace
\delta\to\pm 1/\alpha^{\prime}
where we are free to choose signs thanks to (
20
). In addition, we always apply a rescaling
\alpha^{\prime}\mapsto\alpha^{\prime}/2
as in the case of (
21
).

Three Scalars

The first set of stringy mixed functions we study are those with
n=2k+1
odd involving three adjacent scalars
M_{2k+1}^{3,\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi\pi\dots\pi\phi\phi)
. These can be obtained by applying the known shift (
22
) for pions to the odd-point inverse KLT kernel, i.e. in analogy to (
23
) we now have

\displaystyle M_{2k+1}^{3,\alpha^{\prime}}(X_{ij})=m_{2k+1}^{\alpha^{\prime}\!\!/2}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right](\widehat{X}_{ij}).

(27)

To give an explicit example, we apply the shift (
22
) to the five-point diagonal matrix element in (
1
) and obtain

\displaystyle M_{5}^{3,\alpha^{\prime}}\!=-\frac{\tau_{13}+\tau_{24}}{\tau_{14}}-\frac{\tau_{24}+\tau_{35}}{\tau_{25}}+1+\tau_{13}\tau_{35}.

(28)

At leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
this can be seen to yield the known mixed amplitude (
24
). More generally, at
2k{+}1
points the mixed functions (
27
) agree with the stringy functions obtained via semi-abelianization (
25
) for
\phi=\{2k,2k{+}1\}
and
\pi=\{2\dots 2k{-}1\}
.

As our next example we discuss the nine-point function
M_{9}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!(\phi\pi\pi\phi\pi\pi\phi\pi\pi)
. In
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
it was shown that this particular mixed amplitude cannot be obtained from a
\delta
-shift as there is no consistent choice of signs
X_{ij}\pm\delta
for the
X
-variables involved. However, since the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift is agnostic about these signs due to (
20
) we can compute this amplitude from the diagonal matrix element
m_{9}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]
in the stringy setting. We rescale
\alpha^{\prime}\to\alpha^{\prime}/2
and shift
\widetilde{X}=\{X_{13}+\text{cyc.},X_{16},X_{49},X_{37},X_{26},X_{59},X_{38}\}
. Here the set notation
\{X_{ij}\}
indicates that we shift the listed variables according to
\widetilde{X}_{ij}=X_{ij}\pm 1/\alpha^{\prime}
and leave all other
X
-variables unchanged. Under this shift we obtain

\displaystyle M_{9}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\!(\phi\pi\pi\phi\pi\pi\phi\pi\pi)(X_{ij})=m_{9}^{\alpha^{\prime}\!\!/2}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right](\widetilde{X}_{ij}),

(29)

which yields the correct field theory amplitude in the limit
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
and agrees with the semi-abelianized function (
25
) setting
\phi=\{4,7\}
and
\pi=\{2,3,5,6,8,9\}
.

More generally we find that up to nine points stringy functions with three scalars can be equivalently obtained from an
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift or semi-abelianization.

Four and more Scalars

While the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift prescription and the semi-abelianization (
25
) agree for stringy functions with exactly three scalars, the story is subtler once we study stringy functions with four or more scalars.

To illustrate this we consider the particular example of
M_{6}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!(\phi\phi\phi\phi\pi\pi)
. From
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
we gather that a suitable shift for this mixed function is taking
\widetilde{X}_{ij}\pm 1/\alpha^{\prime}
with
\widetilde{X}=\{X_{15},X_{26},X_{36},X_{46}\}
. Applying the shift to the inverse KLT kernel we obtain
M_{6,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(X_{ij})=m_{6}^{\alpha^{\prime}\!\!/2}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right](\widetilde{X}_{ij})
.
Alternatively we can compute the semi-abelianized function
M_{6,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
via (
25
) where
\phi=\{2,3,4\}
and
\pi=\{5,6\}
.

Now at the leading order
\mathcal{O}(1/\alpha^{\prime})
the functions
M_{6,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
and
M_{6,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
agree (up to normalization) and yield the expected field theory mixed amplitude
Carrasco et al. 2017a
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}M_{6,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\alpha^{\prime}\to 0}M_{6,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\,M_{6}(\phi\phi\phi\phi\pi\pi).

(30)

At finite
\alpha^{\prime}
both functions can be expressed entirely in terms of the stringy variables
\tau_{ij}
. Comparing them directly we find

\displaystyle M_{6,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau_{ij})=\frac{1}{2}M_{6,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau_{ij})+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{ij}),

(31)

i.e. the stringy function
M_{6,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
obtained via
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift is missing certain higher-order corrections relative to
M_{6,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
, which, however, have no bearing on the low-energy limit (
30
).

This mismatch between mixed stringy functions
M_{n,\text{abel}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
and
M_{n,\text{shift}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
obtained from semi-abelianization and the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift persists more generally. However, we have verified for mixed functions up to ten points with six scalars
\phi
that both approaches yield the same mixed amplitudes at leading order in
\alpha^{\prime}
.

VI. Discussion and Future Directions

The interchangeability of pions and cubic scalars through the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift (
22
) of the inverse string theory KLT kernel
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
advocated here opens up a number of avenues for further study.

Let us start by highlighting the monodromy properties
Mizera 2017a
of the stringy inverse KLT kernel. The matrix elements
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
satisfy monodromy relations

\displaystyle m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[\mathds{1}|\mathds{1}\right]+\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}e^{ix_{k}}m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\!\left[2\dots k\,1\,k{+}1\dots n|\mathds{1}\right]=0,

(32)

with phases given by
x_{k}=\pi\alpha^{\prime}2p_{1}\!\cdot\!(p_{2}+\dots+p_{k})
. For
\alpha^{\prime}\to 0
the relations (
32
) are known to reduce to the Kleiss-Kuijf (KK) and Bern-Carrasco-Johannson (BCJ) relations for amplitudes of the BAS theory.

An immediate implication of (
32
) for the abelianized functions
A_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
is that they satisfy monodromy too, as they correspond to linear combinations of inverse KLT matrix elements (
5
). Consequently, both stringy scalars and pions exhibit certain “hidden” zeros
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024d
;
Li et al. 2025
;
Bartsch et al. 2025
as well as the larger class of monodromy zeros
D’Adda et al. 1971
.
The inverse KLT kernel and abelianized pion functions described here provide simple, rational functions suitable for explicitly studying these zeros. For instance, it would be interesting to see if there is a factorization behavior of these functions near the monodromy zeros similar to the one observed for the “hidden” zeros in field theory amplitudes
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024d
;
Cao et al. 2024
.

A further possible connection is to positive geometries. The field theory limit of the stringy KLT kernel corresponds to amplitudes in the BAS theory, which are described by the ABHY associahedron
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2018
. It is tempting to ask whether their geometry can be generalized to the
\alpha^{\prime}
-complete inverse string theory KLT kernel
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
, and whether, via the
\alpha^{\prime}
-shift presented here, it can be extended to pions and mixed amplitudes. The recent discovery of such structures for special classes of mixed field theory amplitudes
Paranjape et al. 2025
suggests an affirmative answer.

Another direction is to explore soft/KLT/zeroes bootstrap methods
Cheung et al. 2015
;
Cheung et al. 2017
;
Elvang et al. 2019
;
Kampf et al. 2020
;
Chi et al. 2022
;
Cheung et al. 2022
;
Brown et al. 2023
;
Kampf et al. 2025
;
Bartsch et al. 2024
in this context, incorporating the
\alpha^{\prime}
-dependence in the kinematical constraints.

Finally, ongoing work by the authors suggests that the close connection between the inverse string theory KLT kernel and pions also extends to loop integrands.

To summarize, the inverse KLT kernel
m_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
and the stringy pions
A_{n}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
exhibit many properties that have received great attention in the recent literature
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024d
;
Arkani-Hamed and Figueiredo 2024a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024e
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024c
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2025
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024b
;
Li et al. 2025
;
Bartsch et al. 2025
;
Cao et al. 2024
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2018
;
Paranjape et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2024
;
Arkani-Hamed and Figueiredo 2024b
;
Rodina 2025
;
Backus and Rodina 2025
, all while being simple rational functions of stringy kinematic variables. Therefore, they promise to provide a valuable testing ground to probe for novel features of amplitudes that go beyond those discussed above. This in turn could meaningfully inform the study of other string-related amplitudes, for instance those involving gluons or gravity.

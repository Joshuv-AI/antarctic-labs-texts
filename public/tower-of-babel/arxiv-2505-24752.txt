On Noether’s Degree Bound for Finite Group Schemes
Gregor Kemper, Christian Liedtke, Christiane Ott

Abstract
. This paper establishes Noether’s classical degree bound β ⁡ ( G ) ≤ | G | \beta(G)\leq|G| for finite and linearly reductive group schemes. On the other hand, we provide examples of infinitesimal group schemes where β ⁡ ( G ) \beta(G) is unbounded. We also generalize Molien’s formula to finite and linearly reductive group schemes.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13A50, 14L24, 14L15

1.
Introduction

If a finite group
G
acts linearly on a polynomial ring
k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
, where
k
is a field of
characteristic zero, then a famous theorem of Emmy Noether
[
No16
]
states that
the invariant ring
k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]^{G}
can be generated by elements of degree at most the
order
|G|
of
G
.
This was extended to finite groups whose order is prime to the characteristic of
k

by Peter Fleischmann
[
Fl00
]
and John Fogarty
[
Fo01
]
. On the other hand,
if the characteristic
p
of
k
divides
|G|
, Noether’s bound often fails. For example, David Richman

[
Ri90
]
proved that for the indecomposable two-dimensional representation
V_{2}
of the cyclic group
G={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
,
generating the invariant ring
k[mV_{2}]^{G}
of a direct sum of
m
copies of
V_{2}
requires invariants of a degree
that increases linearly with
m
. So for
G={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists no degree bound only depending on
G
.

Together with Noether’s degree bound, Molien’s formula is another classical tool in invariant theory of finite groups
in characteristic
0
. The formula computes the Hilbert series of the invariant ring without needing to touch a single
invariant. It is well known that Molien’s formula extends to the case where the characteristic does
not divide the group order by using a Brauer lift (see
[
DK15
, Section 3.4.19]
).

The purpose of this paper is to explore to what extent these results generalize to the case of finite group schemes.
For finite groups, the “good case” is the one where the characteristic does not divide the group order. This
arithmetic condition is equivalent to the more conceptual condition that the group be linearly reductive, which
says that every finite-dimensional k-linear representation is a direct sum of irreducible representations.
As it turns out, linear reductivity is the “correct” condition when passing from groups to group schemes.

After introducing our notation and making some preparations in
Section

2
,
we show in
Section

3
that Noether’s bound holds for linearly reductive finite group schemes. This is done
by first considering the connected component of the identity and then the maximal étale quotient of
G
.

In many ways the infinitesimal group scheme
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{p}
forms an analogue to the cyclic group
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
. It is also not
linearly reductive. In
Section

4
we show that for the indecomposable two-dimensional representation
V_{2}
of

G=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{p}
, generating the invariant ring
k[mV_{2}]^{G}
of a direct sum of
m
copies of
V_{2}
requires invariants
of a degree that increases linearly with
m
. This is the exact analogue of Richman’s result mentioned above. In fact,
our proof works more generally for
G=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q}
, with
q
a power of
p
.

Finally in
Section

5
we provide a version of Molien’s formula that works for finite linearly reductive group schemes.
This is achieved by a lift to the ring of Witt vectors.

For a general introduction to finite group schemes we refer readers to Michel Brion’s
recent survey article

[
Br25
]

2.
Preliminaries

Let
k
be a field and let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite group scheme.
The
k
-algebra
H^{0}(G,{\mathcal{O}}_{G})
carries the structure of a commutative
(but not necessarily co-commutative), finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over
k
.
By definition,
|G|:=\dim_{k}H^{0}(G,{\mathcal{O}}_{G})
is the
order
of
G
(also called
length
in the context of group schemes).
We also denote the dual Hopf algebra by
k[G]
.
If
G
is a constant group scheme associated to the finite group
G_{\mathrm{abs}}:=G(k)

of
k
-rational points, then
k[G]
is isomorphic to the group algebra
k[G_{\mathrm{abs}}]
.

If
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
is a finite-dimensional representation,
then we consider the induced

G
-action on the symmetric
k
-algebra
S(V):=S^{\bullet}(V)\cong k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
,
where
n=\dim_{k}(V)
, and have the invariant subring
S(V)^{G}\subseteq S(V)
.
We let
\beta(G,\rho)
- or simply
\beta(\rho)
if
G
is clear from the context -
be the minimal number
m
such that
S(V)^{G}

can be generated as a
k
-algebra by homogeneous
invariants of degree
m
.
We also define

\beta(G)\,:=\,\sup_{\rho\,\in\,\mathrm{Rep}_{k}(G)}\left\{\beta(\rho)\right\}\quad\in\quad{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\},

where
\mathrm{Rep}_{k}(G)
is the set of all finite-dimensional and
k
-linear representations of
G
.

The following lemma will be used to reduce to the case that
k
is algebraically closed.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\subseteq K
be a field extension.
Let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite group scheme and let
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
be a finite
dimensional
k
-linear representation.
If
\rho_{K}:G_{K}:=G\times_{{\rm Spec}\>k}{\rm Spec}\>K\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V\otimes_{k}K)

is the base-change to
K
, then

\beta(G,\rho)\,=\,\beta(G_{K},\rho_{K}).

Proof.

Let
n:=\dim_{k}(V)
and then, we have
S:=S^{\bullet}(V)\cong k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
,
which we consider as a graded ring with the standard grading, that is,

\deg(x_{i})=1
for all
i=1,...,n
.
We have an induced grading on the invariant subring

R:=S^{G}\subseteq S
, that is, we find

R=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
and we have
R_{0}=k
.
We set
R_{+}:=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}R_{i}
, which is an ideal of
R

and define
A(R):=R_{+}/R_{+}^{2}
, which is a finite-dimensional and
graded
k
-vector space.
It follows from the graded Nakayama lemma that

\beta(\rho)
is equal to the highest degree of an element in
A(R)
.
It is easy to see that there exists an isomorphism of graded
K
-vector
spaces
A(R)\otimes_{k}K\cong A((S\otimes_{k}K)^{G_{K}})
, which implies
that
\beta(G,\rho)=\beta(R)
coincides with
\beta(G_{K},\rho_{K})=\beta((S\otimes_{k}K)^{G_{K}})
.
∎

Recall that for a finite group scheme
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k

there exists a short exact sequence,
called the
connected-étale sequence

1\,\to\,G^{\circ}\,\to\,G\,\to\,G^{{\rm\acute{e}t}}\,\to\,1,

where
G^{\circ}
is the connected component of
the identity and
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is the maximal étale quotient.
If
k
is perfect, then this sequence splits and
we obtain an isomorphism
G\cong G^{\circ}\rtimes G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
(see
[
Br25
, Theorem 1.3.13]
).

If
k\subseteq L
is a field extension, then a finite group
scheme
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly reductive if and only
if
G_{L}:=G\times_{{\rm Spec}\>k}{\rm Spec}\>L\to{\rm Spec}\>L
is linearly
reductive
[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Moreover, there is a classification of finite and linearly
reductive group schemes, due to Chin, Hashimoto,
Nagata, and Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli
(partly independent of each other)

[
Ch92
,
Ha15
,
Na61
,
AOV08
]
.
This classification states that
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k

is linearly reductive if and only if
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is of
length prime to
p
and
G^{\circ}
is
of multiplicative type.
In particular,

(1)

if
p=0
, then all finite
group schemes over
k
are linearly reductive.
If
k
is algebraically closed, then
G
is the constant
group scheme associated to a finite group.

(2)

If
k
is algebraically closed and
p>0
,
then
G
is linearly reductive if and only if

G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is the constant group
scheme associated to a finite group of order prime to

p
and
G^{\circ}
is a product of
group schemes of the form
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{n}}
.

3.
Degree bounds

In this section
G
is a linearly reductive finite group scheme over
a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. The goal
is to show that
\beta(G)\leq|G|
, so by
Lemma

2.1
we may assume
that
k
is algebraically closed. By

[
Ha15
, Section (2.10)]
we have

G=G^{\circ}\rtimes G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
. We treat
G^{\circ}
in the first step and
then
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
. Both are linearly reductive by

[
Ha15
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

In the above situation we have
\beta(G^{\circ})\leq|G^{\circ}|
.

Proof.

As mentioned in
Section

2
we have

G^{\circ}\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{e_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{e_{s}}}

with
s\geq 0
and
e_{i}>1
. Write
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{e_{i}}}={\rm Spec}\>A_{i}

with
A_{i}=k[t_{i}]/(t_{i}^{p^{e_{i}}}-1)
. The representations of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{e_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{e_{s}}}
are well known
(see, e.g.
[
Ha15
, Section 2.6]
): with a suitable choice of
a basis, a representation
\rho:G^{\circ}\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
is given by a
diagonal matrix

\diag(\underline{t}^{\underline{w}_{1}},\ldots,\underline{t}^{\underline{w}_{n}})\in(A_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{s})^{n\times n}
, where

\underline{t}^{\underline{w}_{i}}
stands for

t_{1}^{w_{i,1}}\cdots t_{s}^{w_{i,s}}
, and
w_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus

G^{\circ}
permutes the monomials in
S(V)=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
and a polynomial is invariant if and only if all of its monomials
are invariant. Furthermore, a monomial
x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}

is invariant if and only if

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i,j}k_{i}\equiv 0\mod p^{e_{j}}\quad\text{for
all}\quad j=1,\ldots,s.

Now we have the exact same description for the invariants for the
group
\widetilde{G}={\mathbb{Z}}/{p^{e_{1}}}{\mathbb{Z}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{Z}}/{p^{e_{s}}}{\mathbb{Z}}

acting on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, so it follows from the Noether bound in
characteristic
0
that every exponent vector
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})

satisfying
Equation

1
can be written as a sum of
exponent vectors satisfying
Equation

1
, such that in each summand the sum of exponents is

\leq|\widetilde{G}|
. A direct argument can be found in

[
Sch91
, Lemma 2.1]
, which applies by
regarding
Equation

1
as a single equation in the group

{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{e_{1}}{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{e_{s}}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

So we obtain

\beta\bigl(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{G^{\circ}}\bigr)\leq|\widetilde{G}|=|G^{\circ}|
, and the lemma is proved.
∎

To prepare for the second step we need the following lemma. The first
part of it can be found in the literature and the second part
should be well known as well. We provide a proof for the convenience of the
reader.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be an affine group scheme over a scheme
S
(for the purpose
of this lemma,
G
need not be linearly reductive or finite) and let

\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
be a finite-dimensional representation. Then

(a)

If
N\subseteq G
is a normal subgroup scheme, then the

G
-action restricts to the invariants
V^{N}
.

(b)

If
G=N\rtimes H
with
H
another subgroup scheme, then

V^{G}=(V^{N})^{H}.

Proof.

(a)

See
[
Wa79
, Lemma 16.3]
.

(b)

The inclusion
V^{G}\subseteq(V^{N})^{H}
is clear since
H

and
N
are subgroup schemes. For the converse, let

v\in(V^{N})^{H}
and consider the morphism
f_{v}:G\to V
given by
applying
G
to
v
. Let
T
be an
S
-scheme. For

\sigma\in G(T)
there exist
\tau\in N(T)
and
\rho\in H(T)

such that
\sigma=\rho\tau
. Therefore

f_{v}(\sigma)=\sigma(v)=\rho(\tau(v))=v
. So by Yoneda’s
lemma,
f_{v}
is the constant morphism mapping everything to
v
.
Thus
v\in V^{G}
. ∎

Going back to our original situation, let
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
be a finite-dimensional
representation of the linearly reductive finite group scheme
G
over
k=\bar{k}
. Then
by Lemma
3.2
,
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
acts on
S(V)^{G^{\circ}}
and

S(V)^{G}=\bigl(S(V)^{G^{\circ}}\bigr)^{G^{\rm\acute{e}t}}
. But the
invariants of
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
are the same as those of

\widehat{G}:=G^{\rm\acute{e}t}(k)
, which is a group of order is not
divisible by
p
. So by
[
DK15
, Theorem 3.22]
we obtain, using
Lemma
3.1
,

\beta\bigl(S(V)^{G}\bigr)\leq|\widehat{G}|\cdot\beta\bigl(S(V)^{G^{\circ}}\bigr)\leq|\widehat{G}|\cdot|G^{\circ}|=|G^{\rm\acute{e}t}|\cdot|G^{\circ}|=|G|.

So we have shown:

Theorem 3.3
.

For a linearly reductive finite group scheme
G
we have

\beta(G)\leq|G|.

4.
Group schemes that are not linearly reductive

There are strong results on the Noether number
\beta(G)
for finite
groups that are not linearly reductive. In the introduction we
mentioned Richman’s
[
Ri90
]
result that

\beta(k[lV_{2}]^{{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}})
increases linearly with
l
, where
lV_{2}

denotes the
l
-fold direct sum of the indecomposable two-dimensional
representation of
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
over a field
k
of characteristic
p
. In
fact, later Richman
[
Ri96
]
showed that this is true in the
much more general situation where
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
is replaced by any finite
group
G
whose order is divisible by
p
, and
V_{2}
is replaced by
any faithful representation. This implies
\beta(G)=\infty
for
every finite group that is not linearly reductive.

By letting
G^{\circ}
act trivially, this of course extends to group
schemes for which
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is not linearly reductive. However, to the
best of our knowledge no example is known in which an infinitesimal
group scheme
G=G^{\circ}
violates Noether’s degree bound, or where
we even have
\beta(G)=\infty
.

The following proposition provides such an example by considering the

l
-fold direct sum of the indecomposable representation
V_{2}
of the
infinitesimal group scheme
G=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q}
. Recall that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q}
is
given by the Hopf algebra
H^{0}(G,{\mathcal{O}}_{G})=k[t]/(t^{q})
, where
k
has
characteristic
p
and
q>1
is a power of
p
, with the coproduct
defined by
t\mapsto 1\otimes t+t\otimes 1
. So the propostion
(or rather, its special case
q=p
) is a direct analog to Richman’s

[
Ri90
]
first result. The importance of the infinitesimal
group scheme
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{p}
is also emphasized by the result
[
LMM21
, Lemma
2.3]
which says that a finite group scheme over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic
p
contains a subgroup scheme
isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{p}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
. In this sense
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{p}
and, more
generally,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q}
are quintessential examples.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the following representation

\displaystyle\rho:\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}k[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{l},y_{l}]&\to k[t]/(t^{q})\otimes k[x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{l},y_{l}]\\
x_{i}&\mapsto x_{i}\\
y_{i}&\mapsto y_{i}+tx_{i}\end{array}\right.

for the infinitesimal group scheme
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q}
. Then

\beta(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q},\rho)\geq l.

In particular,
\beta(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{q})=\infty
.

Proof.

For
0\leq i\leq q-1
, we consider the polynomials

\displaystyle f_{l,i}:=\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+i_{l}=i}x_{1}^{i_{1}}y_{1}^{q-1-i_{1}}\cdots x_{l}^{i_{l}}y_{l}^{q-1-i_{l}}

as well as

\displaystyle g_{l}:=f_{l,q-1}.

In order to see that
g_{l}
is an invariant, we first consider the following equation that we will prove via induction:
For every
0\leq i\leq q-1
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle f_{l,i}=\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho(f_{l,j}).

To prove this, we will make use of the identity

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{r}{{x}}\binom{n-r}{y}=\binom{n+1}{x+y+1}

for every
x,y,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, see
[
Gr22
, Proposition 3.32(f)]
.
We first consider the case where
l=1
. For every
0\leq j\leq q-1
, we have
f_{1,j}=x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{q-1-j}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho(f_{1,j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho\left(x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{q-1-j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}x_{1}^{j}(y_{1}+tx_{1})^{q-1-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}x_{1}^{j}\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-j}\binom{q-1-j}{s}y_{1}^{s}t^{q-1-j-s}x_{1}^{q-1-j-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-j}\binom{q-1-j}{s}y_{1}^{s}t^{q-1-s-i}x_{1}^{q-1-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=i\end{subarray}}^{q-1-s}\binom{j}{i}\binom{q-1-j}{s}y_{1}^{s}t^{q-1-s-i}x_{1}^{q-1-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-i}x_{1}^{q-1-s}y_{1}^{s}t^{q-1-s-i}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\binom{q-1-j}{s}

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{chu_vand}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-i}x_{1}^{q-1-s}y_{1}^{s}t^{q-1-s-i}\binom{q}{i+s+1}\overset{\text{Lucas's Theorem}}{=}x_{1}^{i}y_{1}^{q-1-i}=f_{1,i}.

For the induction step
l\to l+1
, let
0\leq i\leq q-1
. We need to show that

(4)

\displaystyle f_{l+1,i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho(f_{l+1,j}).

To do so, we first simplify both sides. This leads to

\displaystyle f_{l+1,i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=q-1-i}^{q-1}x_{l+1}^{q-1-s}y_{l+1}^{s}f_{l,i-(q-1-s)}

as well as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho(f_{l+1,j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\rho\left(\sum_{r=0}^{j}x_{l+1}^{r}y_{l+1}^{q-1-r}f_{l,j-r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}x_{l+1}^{r}(y_{l+1}+tx_{l+1})^{q-1-r}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}t^{j-i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}x_{l+1}^{r}\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-r}\binom{q-1-r}{s}y_{l+1}^{s}t^{q-1-r-s}x_{l+1}^{q-1-r-s}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\sum_{s=0}^{q-1-r}\binom{q-1-r}{s}y_{l+1}^{s}t^{q-1-r-s+j-i}x_{l+1}^{q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{q-1}\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{r=0}^{\begin{subarray}{c}\min\{j,q-1-s\}\end{subarray}}\binom{q-1-r}{s}y_{l+1}^{s}t^{q-1-r-s+j-i}x_{l+1}^{q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{q-1}x_{l+1}^{q-1-s}y_{l+1}^{s}\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{r=0}^{\begin{subarray}{c}\min\{j,q-1-s\}\end{subarray}}\binom{q-1-r}{s}t^{q-1-r-s+j-i}\rho(f_{l,j-r}).

Comparing coefficients, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{r=0}^{\min\{j,q-1-s\}}\binom{q-1-r}{s}t^{q-1-r-s+j-i}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}f_{l,i-(q-1-s)}&\text{for every }q-1-i\leq s\leq q-1\\
0&\text{for every }0\leq s<q-1-i.\end{array}\right.

We simplify the first line and we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i}\sum_{r=0}^{\min\{j,q-1-s\}}\binom{q-1-r}{s}t^{q-1-r-s+j-i}\rho(f_{l,j-r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=\max\{0,i-q+1+s\}}^{q-1}t^{\beta-i+q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,\beta})\sum_{j=\max\{i,\beta\}}^{\min\{q-1,\beta+q-1-s\}}\binom{j}{i}\binom{q-1+\beta-j}{s}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Luc.}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=\max\{0,i-q+1+s\}}^{q-1}t^{\beta-i+q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,\beta})\sum_{j=0}^{q-1+\beta}\binom{j}{i}\binom{q-1+\beta-j}{s}

(7)

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{chu_vand}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=\max\{0,i-q+1+s\}}^{q-1}t^{\beta-i+q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,\beta})\binom{q+\beta}{i+s+1}.

For every
0\leq s<q-1-i
, this sum is equal to zero by Lucas’s Theorem. Hence, the second condition in
Section

4
is satisfied. Now, it only remains to show the first condition. For this purpose, we consider
q-1-i\leq s\leq q-1
. Then,

\displaystyle f_{l,i-(q-1-s)}\overset{\text{induction}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i-q+1+s}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i-q+1+s}t^{j-i+q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,j})

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\max\{0,i-q+1+s\}}^{q-1}\binom{j}{i-q+1+s}t^{j-i+q-1-s}\rho(f_{l,j}).

Using Lucas’s Theorem, we see that
Equation

8
is equal to
Equation

7
. As a result, the first condition in
Section

4

5.
Molien’s formula

The main goal of this section is to generalize Molien’s formula
to the case of a finite linearly reductive group scheme. This is done
via a lifting to the ring of Witt vectors, which we describe now.

If
k
is algebraically closed and
p>0
, then we let

W(k)
be the ring of Witt vectors over
k
, which is a complete
DVR of characteristic zero with residue field
k
and
whose maximal ideal is generated by
p
.
If
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is a finite and linearly reductive group
scheme, then there exists a flat
lift
\mathcal{G}\to{\rm Spec}\>W(k)
, the
canonical lift
,
which is almost
unique, see
[
LMM21
, Proposition 2.4]
.
Let
K
be the field of fractions of
W(k)
and
let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\overline{K}}:=\mathcal{G}\times_{{\rm Spec}\>W(k)}{\rm Spec}\>\overline{K}

be the geometric generic fibre, which is a constant group
scheme over
\overline{K}
.
Then, still assuming
k
to be algebraically closed,
then every finite-dimensional
k
-linear representation lifts
(uniquely up to isomorphism) to a
W(k)
-linear representation of
\mathcal{G}
.
From this, we obtain a specialisation map

\mathrm{sp}\,:\,\mathrm{Rep}_{\overline{K}}(\mathcal{G}_{\overline{K}})\,\to\,\mathrm{Rep}_{k}(G)

of finite dimensional
k
-linear representations of
G

and finite-dimensional
\overline{K}
-linear representations

\mathcal{G}_{\overline{K}}
.
The map
\mathrm{sp}
respects degrees and simplicitiy and
it is compatible with direct sums, tensor products, and duals,
see
[
LMM21
, Proposition 2.9]
.

If
G
is a finite and linearly reductive group scheme
over a field
k
, we can define the

abstract group

G_{\mathrm{abs}}

associated to
G
, see
[
LMM21
, Definition 2.2]
.

(1)

If
p=0
, then
G_{\mathrm{abs}}\cong G(\overline{k})
.

(2)

If
p>0
, then
G_{\mathrm{abs}}\cong\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{\overline{K}}(\overline{K})
,
where
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to{\rm Spec}\>W(\overline{k})
is the canonical lift
of
G_{\overline{k}}
.

If
k
is algebraically closed, then we can use the specialisation map
\mathrm{sp}

to obtain another specialisation map

\mathrm{sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(G_{\mathrm{abs}})\,\to\,\mathrm{Rep}_{k}(G)

that respects degrees and simplicitiy and
it is compatible with direct sums, tensor products, and duals,
see
[
LMM21
, Corollary 2.11]
.
In fact, this can be descended to
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
or
even
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{|G|})
, where
\zeta_{|G|}
is a primitive
|G|
.th
root of unity.

5.1.
Lifting the invariant subring

By the following proposition, we can also lift the invariant ring.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
,
let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite and linearly reductive group scheme,
let
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
be an
n
-dimensional representation,
and consider
S:=S^{\bullet}(V)\cong k[x_{1},...,n]
with the induced
G
-action.
Let
\mathcal{G}\to{\rm Spec}\>W(k)
be the canonical lift of
G

and let
\widetilde{\rho}:\mathcal{G}\to{\mathbf{GL}}(\mathcal{V})
be the
lift of
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
.

(1)

We set

(9)

\mathcal{S}_{n}\,:=\,S^{n}(\mathcal{V})\mbox{ \hskip 20.44434ptand \hskip 20.44434pt}\mathcal{S}\,:=\,\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{n}\,\cong\,W(k)[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

which turns
\mathcal{S}
into a graded
W(k)
-algebra with a
\mathcal{G}
-action.

(2)

We obtain a commutative diagramme of graded
W(k)
-algebras

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{S}&\to&S\\
\cup&&\cup\\
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}&\to&S^{G},\end{array}

where the horizontal arrows are reduction modulo
p
, where the

\mathcal{G}
-action and the
G
-action in the top row are compatible,
and where
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
is a flat
W(k)
-algebra.
In particular,
\mathcal{S}^{G}
is a flat lift of
S^{G}
over
W(k)
.

(3)

Finally, we have

\dim_{K}S^{n}(\mathcal{V}_{K})^{\mathcal{G}_{K}}\,=\,\dim_{k}S^{n}(V)^{G}.

Proof.

For each integer
n\geq 0
, we have a commutative diagramme
of
W(k)
-algebras

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}S^{n}(\mathcal{V}\otimes_{W(k)}K)&\supseteq&S^{n}(\mathcal{V})&\to&S^{n}(V)\\
\cup&&\cup&&\cup\\
S^{n}(\mathcal{V}\otimes_{W(k)}K)^{\mathcal{G}_{K}}&\supseteq&S^{n}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathcal{G}}&\to&S^{n}(V)^{G}\\
\cup&&\cup&&\cup\\
K&\leftarrow&W(k)&\to&k\end{array}

that is compatible with the natural actions of
\mathcal{G}_{K}
,
\mathcal{G}
, and
G
.
Thus,
\mathcal{S}
is a graded
W(k)
-algebra with a
\mathcal{G}
-action, we have

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S^{n}(\mathcal{V})\,=\,S^{\bullet}(\mathcal{V})\,\cong\,W(k)[x_{1},...,x_{n}],

and it maps surjectively onto
S^{\bullet}(V)\cong k[x_{1},..,x_{n}]
.
The kernel of this surjection is the ideal generated by
p
.

Being a
W(k)
-submodule of
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathcal{V})
, it follows
that
S^{n}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathcal{G}}
is torsion-free as a
W(k)
-module.
Using properties of the specialisation map
\mathrm{sp}
, we obtain
the equality claimed in
Equation

9
,
which implies that
S^{n}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathcal{G}}
is a flat
W(k)
-module.
∎

5.2.
Reynolds operator

A central tool in invariant theory are Reynolds operators
and the following shows that we can even find such an operator
that is compatible with lifts.

Proposition 5.3
.

With assumptions and notations as in Proposition
5.1
,
there exists a surjective
homomorphism of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
-modules

\pi\,:\,\mathcal{S}\,\to\,\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}

that splits the inclusion
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{S}

as
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
-modules.
In particular,
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
is a direct summand
of
\mathcal{S}
as a
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
-module.

Proof.

Being linearly reductive, we can decompose the
G
-module

S^{n}(V)
into isotypical components for the simple
G
-representations.
This yields a canonical projection operator
S^{n}(V)\to S^{n}(V)^{G}

and thus, a canonical direct sum decomposition

S^{n}(V)\cong S^{n}(V)^{G}\oplus W_{n}
.
Using the specialisation morphism
\mathrm{sp}
, we obtain a
canonical direct sum decomposition

S^{n}(\mathcal{V})\,\cong\,S^{n}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathcal{G}}\,\oplus\,\mathcal{W}_{n},

which is
\mathcal{G}
-stable,
and a canonical projection
\pi_{n}:S^{n}(\mathcal{V})\to S^{n}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathcal{G}}
,
which is
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant (w.r.t. to the trivial operation on the right).
Taking direct summands, we obtain a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}\oplus W_{\bullet}
of
W(k)
-modules,
where
W_{\bullet}:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}W_{n}
.
Moreover, this decomposition also respects

\mathcal{G}
-representations, which implies
that
W_{\bullet}
is a
\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{G}}
-module.
∎

5.3.
Molien series

If a group scheme
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
acts linarly
on a polynomial ring
S=k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
,
then we define the
Molien series
to be

\mathrm{Molien}(S^{G},t)\,:=\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dim_{k}(S^{G}_{i})\cdot t^{i}\,\in\,{\mathbb{Z}}[[t]],

where
S^{G}_{i}\subseteq S^{G}\subseteq S\cong k[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

denotes the
k
-vector space of polynomials of degree
i

of
S^{G}
.

Being linear, the
G
-action on
S
is induced from the
k
-linear
representation
G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(S_{1})\cong{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,k}
.
If
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly reductive, then we have
the abstract group
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
,
a representation
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}:G_{\mathrm{abs}}\to{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
,
and we define the
abstract Molien series
to be

\mathrm{Molien}_{\mathrm{abs}}(S^{G},t)\,:=\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dim_{k}({\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]^{G_{\mathrm{abs}}}_{i})\cdot t^{i}\,\in\,{\mathbb{Z}}[[t]].

Combining Proposition
5.1
with Molien’s
theorem
[
Mo97
]
, see, for example,
[
DK15
, Section 3.4]
for a modern account,
we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.4

(Molien formula)
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 0
and let

\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
.
Let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite group scheme, let

G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
be an
n
-dimensional representation, and consider the
induced
G
-action on
S:=S(V)\cong k[x_{1},..,n]
.

(1)

If
p=0
, then

\mathrm{Molien}(S^{G},t)\,=\,\frac{1}{|G|}\,\sum_{g\in G(\overline{k})}\frac{1}{\det(1-g^{-1}t,V_{\overline{k}})}

and it coincides with
\mathrm{Molien}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]^{G},t)
.

(2)

If
p>0
, then assume moreover that
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly reductive.

(a)

We have

\mathrm{Molien}(S^{G},t)\,=\,\mathrm{Molien}(S^{G}\otimes_{k}\overline{k},t)\,=\,\mathrm{Molien}((S\otimes_{k}\overline{k})^{G_{\overline{k}}},t).

(b)

If
k
is moreover algebraically closed, then we have in
the notations from Proposition
5.1

\mathrm{Molien}(S^{G},t)\,=\,\mathrm{Molien}(\mathcal{S}_{K}^{\mathcal{G}_{K}},t)

and the latter has been determined in (1).

In both cases, the Molien series coincide with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Molien}_{\mathrm{abs}}(S^{G},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Molien}({\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]^{G_{\mathrm{abs}}},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G_{\mathrm{abs}}|}\,\sum_{g\in G_{\mathrm{abs}}}\frac{1}{\det(1-g^{-1}t,{\mathbb{C}}^{n})}.

Proof.

We have
(S^{G})\otimes_{k}\overline{k}\cong(S\otimes_{k}\overline{k})^{G_{\overline{k}}}

and this isomorphism respects the grading, which implies that the Molien series
does not change.
Thus, we may assume
k
to be algebraically closed.

(1) If
p=0
, then
G
is the constant group scheme associated to
G(k)

and then, the claim becomes the classical Molien formula.

(2).(b) If
p>0
, then Proposition
5.1
implies that the Molien series
of
S^{G}
coincides with that of
K[x_{1},...,x_{n}]^{\mathcal{G}_{K}}

and then, claim (2) follows from the already established
characteristic zero case.

We have that
\dim_{k}S_{i}^{G}
coincides with
\dim_{k}S^{i}(V)^{G}
.
Since the specialisation map
\mathrm{sp}
respects tensor products, direct sums, etc.,
this dimension coincides with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S^{i}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})^{G_{\mathrm{abs}}}
.
Thus, claim (3) follows for the classical Molien formula associated to the linear

G_{\mathrm{abs}}
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
defined by
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In the case, where
G
is a constant group scheme,
we have
G(k)=G(\overline{k})\cong G_{\mathrm{abs}}
and then,
we recover Molien’s formula for finite groups of order prime to
p
.
Note however, that our version of Molien’s formula also holds
if
G(k)
is strictly smaller than
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
.
The following example illustrates this phenomenon.

Example 5.6
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 0

and consider the group scheme
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
, which is of length
n
over
k
.
We view the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m,k}

as a representation
\rho:\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
with
\dim_{k}V=1
.
Then,
k[V]\cong k[x]
,
k[V]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\cong k[x^{n}]\subset k[x]

and thus, we find

\mathrm{Molien}\left(k[x]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}},t\right)\,=\,\frac{1}{1-t^{n}}.

However,
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}(k)
can even be trivial
if
p>0
or if
k
is not algebraically closed.
For example, if
n=p>0
or if
k\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
and
n
is odd, then
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}(k)=\{1\}

Thus, the Molien series is usually not a function of the group
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}(k)

of
k
-rational points of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
.

Our final example shows that, loosely speaking, having full control over
the graded parts of an invariant ring does not imply having full control over
the beta-number.

Example 5.7
.

Let
R
be a ring.
The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
n

by permuting the basis elements and the induced action on

acts by permuting the variables.
Let

be a subgroup
and set

to be the

G\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
.

(1)

If

is a field of characteristic

, then the Molien series
of

does not depend on

, see
[

, Proposition 3.4.4]
.

(2)

If

is a field of characteristic

, then

may depend on

, as the following example
shows:
Consider the special case

and let

be the subgroup of

(a)

If

(b)

In any characteristic, we have the invariants of degree

Consider the invariant

If

which shows

(3)

If

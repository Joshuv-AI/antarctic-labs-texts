P4HA3-mediated hydroxylation of SLC7A11 drives tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance by suppressing ferroptosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Yueming Chen, Guixin Zhang, Ruixin Yang, Yifan Li, Haozhuo Song, Zeping Chen, Hao Cai, Qingquan Li, Chen Fang, Jingyi Huang, Siwei Xing, Xiaoqun Yang, Danfeng Xu, Lu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractResistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) severely limits their long-term efficacy in renal cell carcinoma (RCC). Here, we identify prolyl 4-hydroxylase subunit alpha 3 (P4HA3) as a critical driver of TKI resistance and elucidate its underlying mechanism. P4HA3 was markedly upregulated in TKI-resistant RCC cells, and its overexpression promoted proliferation and resistance to sunitinib and axitinib, whereas P4HA3 depletion restored drug sensitivity and suppressed tumor growth in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, P4HA3 inhibited ferroptosis by stabilizing the cystine transporter SLC7A11, thereby maintaining redox homeostasis and suppressing lipid peroxidation. P4HA3 directly interacted with SLC7A11 and induced hydroxyproline modification at residues P153 and P161, which attenuated NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of SLC7A11. Moreover, a hydroxylase-inactive P4HA3 mutant failed to stabilize SLC7A11, suppress ferroptosis, or promote TKI resistance, demonstrating the essential role of P4HA3 enzymatic activity. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of P4HA3 using Tubuloside A or 1,4-DPCA disrupted the P4HA3-SLC7A11 axis and significantly enhanced TKI efficacy across RCC cell lines, patient-derived organoids, and xenograft models. Together, these findings establish a hydroxylation-dependent P4HA3-SLC7A11-NEDD4 axis that drives ferroptosis evasion and TKI resistance in RCC and highlight P4HA3 as a promising therapeutic target.Keywords: P4HA3, Ferroptosis, SLC7A11, Prolyl hydroxylation, Tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, Clear cell renal cell carcinoma

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) accounts for approximately 2-3% of adult cancers and remains the most common malignancy of the kidney. Although early detection and surgical management have improved outcomes, many patients present with advanced disease or develop recurrence and metastasis [1] [2]. VEGF-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), including sunitinib and axitinib, have transformed the treatment of advanced RCC, yet their benefit is frequently undermined by the rapid development of intrinsic or acquired resistance—especially in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), which is initially responsive but highly prone to resistance [3] [4]. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying TKI resistance therefore remains a critical unmet need.

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and is mechanistically distinct from apoptosis and necrosis [5] [6]. Increasing evidence supports a tumor-suppressive role for ferroptosis in RCC and implicates it in mediating the therapeutic efficacy of TKIs [7]. Under sustained drug exposure, however, RCC cells can adaptively suppress ferroptosis by maintaining redox homeostasis and limiting lipid peroxidation, thereby promoting survival and drug resistance [8] [9] [10]. Evasion of ferroptosis has thus emerged as a key adaptive mechanism underlying TKI resistance in RCC.

Solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11), the functional subunit of the cystine/glutamate antiporter system Xc−, is a central regulator of ferroptosis by sustaining cystine uptake and glutathione synthesis to buffer oxidative stress and prevent lipid peroxidation [11] [12]. Aberrant upregulation of SLC7A11 is frequently observed in RCC and is associated with ferroptosis resistance and poor therapeutic response [13]. Although transcriptional control of SLC7A11 by stress-responsive pathways such as NRF2 [7], ATF4 [14] [15], and p53 [16] has been well characterized, how SLC7A11 protein stability is regulated at the post-translational level in RCC remains largely unknown.

Prolyl 4-hydroxylase subunit alpha 3 (P4HA3) is a catalytic component of the prolyl 4-hydroxylase complex, classically known for its role in collagen hydroxylation and extracellular matrix remodeling [17] [18]. Recent studies have expanded the functional scope of the P4HA family beyond collagen biosynthesis, revealing their ability to hydroxylate non-collagenous substrates and thereby regulate protein stability, turnover, and cellular stress responses [19]. Such non-canonical hydroxylation events have increasingly been implicated in cancer progression and therapeutic resistance [20] [21]. However, whether P4HA3 participates in ferroptosis regulation or TKI resistance through hydroxylation-dependent modulation of ferroptosis regulators has not been explored.

Protein ubiquitination is a major post-translational mechanism controlling protein stability and degradation [22] and is frequently dysregulated in cancer [23]. Neural precursor cell expressed developmentally downregulated protein 4 (NEDD4), a prototypical HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase, plays a key role in ubiquitinating membrane-associated proteins and transporters [24] [25]. Nevertheless, the contribution of NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination to the regulation of ferroptosis-related proteins in RCC remains poorly understood.

Here, we identify a previously unrecognized mechanism linking protein hydroxylation to ferroptosis resistance and TKI insensitivity in RCC. We demonstrate that P4HA3 promotes ferroptosis resistance by stabilizing SLC7A11 at the post-translational level. Mechanistically, P4HA3 hydroxylates SLC7A11, disrupts its interaction with NEDD4, and thereby suppresses NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of SLC7A11. Sustained SLC7A11 expression consequently limits lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death, ultimately conferring resistance to TKIs. Collectively, these findings establish a P4HA3-SLC7A11-NEDD4 regulatory axis governing ferroptosis sensitivity and therapeutic response in RCC and highlight P4HA3 as a potential therapeutic target for overcoming TKI resistance.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and establishment of sunitinib-resistant cell lines

Cell lines involved in this study were purchased from Shanghai Cell Bank. 769-P, OSRC-2 and 786-O cells were cultured in RPMI-1640, while HK2 and ACHN cells were grown in DMEM. Both culture media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco, USA).

To construct drug-resistant cell lines, OSRC-2 and 769-P cells were initially exposed to sunitinib at a concentration of 2 μM for 1 week. Subsequently, the sunitinib concentration was gradually increased over a period of 2-12 weeks. During this process, cell morphology and viability were closely monitored. If cells exhibited high level of cell death, sunitinib treatment was temporarily suspended, and the medium was replaced with fresh drug-free culture medium to allow recovery for 24 h before resuming treatment. When cells maintained stable growth, the sunitinib concentration was increased after every 2-3 passages. This stepwise escalation was continued until the final concentration reached 20 μM. Throughout the induction process, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of sunitinib was determined approximately every two weeks to evaluate the development of drug resistance.

Plasmids, lentiviruses and cell transfection

FLAG-tagged P4HA3, including the hydroxylase-inactive H440S/D442S/H510S mutant, His-tagged SLC7A11, and the indicated SLC7A11 mutants were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). All constructs were verified by Sanger sequencing.

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) were synthesized by GenePharma. The siRNA sequences were as follows: si-P4HA3, 5′-GACACUAUGAGCCUCACUUTT-3′; si-NEDD4, 5′-GGAAGGACCTATTATGTAA-3′; and si-SLC7A11, 5′-GGGUGGAACUCCUCAUAAUTT-3′. The shRNA targeting P4HA3 (sh-P4HA3, 5′-GCCAGGAATGTCTTGAAATAT-3′) was cloned into a lentiviral vector for stable gene silencing.

Lentiviruses were produced in HEK293T cells by co-transfecting the lentiviral expression constructs with the packaging plasmids psPAX2 and pMD2.G using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). Viral supernatants were collected at 48 and 72 h after transfection, filtered through 0.45-μm filters, and used immediately or stored at −80 °C.

Target cells were infected with viral supernatants in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich) and selected with puromycin 48 h after infection to generate stable cell lines. Transient transfections of plasmids or siRNAs were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer's instructions. For transient P4HA3 overexpression, OSRC-2 and 769-P cells cultured in six-well plates were transfected with 2.5 μg of the FLAG-tagged P4HA3 expression plasmid per well. Cells transfected with an equal amount of the corresponding empty vector served as controls.

Cell viability and cytotoxicity assay

Cell viability and cytotoxicity were assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Beyotime, C0042) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates and cultured under the indicated conditions. For cell viability assays, absorbance at 450 nm was measured at the indicated time points to evaluate cell proliferation. For cytotoxicity assays, cells were treated with the indicated concentrations of TKIs, followed by CCK-8 incubation and absorbance measurement at 450 nm to assess cell survival.

Colon formation

A total of 500 cells were plated in 6-well plates and continuously treated with 2 μM sunitinib or axitinib until colonies formed. Colonies were subsequently fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet. Colony formation was quantified using ImageJ.

Lipid peroxidation assay

Lipid peroxidation was assessed using BODIPY 581/591C11 (Beyotime, S0043 M). Cells were harvested, washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and incubated with BODIPY 581/591C11 working solution at 37 °C in the dark according to the manufacturer's instructions. After staining, cells were washed with PBS and immediately analyzed by flow cytometry. Lipid peroxidation was quantified based on the oxidation-dependent fluorescence shift of BODIPY 581/591C11 from red to green, and mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was used for statistical analysis.

Intracellular GSH/GSSG detection

Intracellular reduced glutathione (GSH) and oxidized glutathione (GSSG) levels were measured using a GSH and GSSG Assay Kit (Beyotime, S0053). Cells were collected and processed according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was measured using a microplate reader, and the GSH/GSSG ratio was calculated to evaluate intracellular redox status.

Intracellular oxidative activity assay

Intracellular oxidative activity was assessed using DCFH-DA (Beyotime, S0033 M). Cells were harvested, washed twice with PBS, and incubated with DCFH-DA working solution at 37 °C in the dark according to the manufacturer's instructions. After staining, cells were washed with PBS and immediately analyzed by flow cytometry. The oxidation of DCFH-DA to its fluorescent product was quantified by measuring MFI.

Intracellular ferrous iron (Fe2+) detection

Intracellular Fe2+ levels were measured using RhoNox-6 (Beyotime, S1070 M). Cells were harvested, washed twice with PBS, and incubated with RhoNox-6 working solution at 37 °C in the dark according to the manufacturer's instructions. After staining, cells were washed with PBS and immediately analyzed by flow cytometry. MFI was used to quantify intracellular Fe2+ levels.

Malondialdehyde (MDA) assay

MDA levels were measured using a Lipid Peroxidation MDA Assay Kit (Beyotime, S0131 M) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was measured using a microplate reader, and MDA levels were calculated to assess lipid peroxidation.

Clinical samples

All renal cell carcinoma tissues and matched adjacent non-tumorous tissues were obtained from patients who underwent radical nephrectomy at the Department of Urology, Ruijin Hospital. All tissue samples were collected with informed consent, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Ruijin Hospital.

Animal models

Five-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were purchased from GemPharmatech (Shanghai, China) and used to establish subcutaneous xenograft models. OSRC-2 cells (5 × 106 cells per mouse) were suspended in 50 μL PBS and mixed 1:1 with Matrigel, and then subcutaneously injected into the flanks of mice. When tumors reached a palpable size, mice were randomly assigned to different treatment groups and treated with sunitinib by oral gavage at a dose of 40 mg/kg once daily. In combination treatment groups, mice additionally received either 1,4-dihydrophenanthroline-4-carboxylic acid (1,4-DPCA, 20 mg/kg) or Tubuloside A (100 mg/kg) via intraperitoneal injection every other day. Control mice received the corresponding vehicles. Tumor size was measured daily using calipers, and tumor volumes were calculated using the formula: (length × width^2)/2.

Establishment of organoid lines

Renal cancer organoids were established from freshly resected tumor specimens obtained from patients undergoing nephrectomy at Ruijin Hospital, with informed consent and approval from the institutional ethics committee. Tumor tissues were transported on ice in Advanced DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1% penicillin-streptomycin and processed within 2 h after surgical resection.

Tissues were washed with cold PBS, minced into approximately 1 mm3 fragments, and enzymatically digested in Advanced DMEM/F12 containing 1 mg/mL collagenase type II and 10 μM Y-27632 at 37°C for 30-60 min with gentle agitation. The digested suspension was filtered through a 70-μm cell strainer, washed, and centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 min. The resulting cell pellet was resuspended in Basement Membrane Matrix (GFR) (Osci, MG01) and plated as 25-μL domes in 24-well plates. After polymerization at 37°C for 15 min, domes were overlaid with Human Renal Cancer Organoid Medium (Osci, RC01).

Organoids were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2, and the medium was refreshed every 2-3 days. Organoids were passaged every 7-10 days by mechanical dissociation and brief enzymatic digestion using TrypLE Express or Accutase, followed by re-embedding in fresh Basement Membrane Matrix. Organoid lines were routinely monitored by bright-field microscopy and were used for downstream experiments between passages 2 and 8.

Western blot

Cells were lysed using RIPA buffer (Beyotime, P0038) supplemented with PMSF (Beyotime, ST506). Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked and incubated with the indicated primary antibodies, followed by incubation with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system. All Western blot experiments were independently repeated at least three times unless otherwise specified.

Densitometric analysis of band intensities was performed using ImageJ software, and target protein levels were normalized to the corresponding loading control. Quantitative data are presented as relative protein levels. The full list of antibodies is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining

IHC assay was performed as we previously described [26]. Immunohistochemical staining was observed by two independent investigators. The evaluation was performed using Immunoreactive Score(IRS) method, calculated by multiplying the proportion of positively stained cells with the staining intensity.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from ccRCC cells using TRIzol reagent (Life Technologies) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Hifair® II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qRT-PCR (Yeasen, 11123ES60). Quantitative PCR was performed using SYBR® Green Real-time PCR Master Mix (TOYOBO, 246000). GAPDH served as an internal control, and relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Co-immunoprecipitation assays were performed using an Immunoprecipitation Kit with Protein A + G Magnetic Beads (Beyotime, P2179 M) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, Protein A + G magnetic beads were first incubated with the indicated primary antibodies or control IgG to allow antibody-bead binding. The antibody-bead complexes were then incubated with clarified cell lysates. After extensive washing, the beads were boiled at 97 °C for 5 min to elute bound proteins, which were subsequently subjected to western blotting or mass spectrometry analysis.

Immunofluorescence staining and confocal microscopy

Cells were seeded onto glass coverslips and cultured overnight. After the indicated treatments, cells were incubated with ER-Tracker Green dye (Invitrogen, #E34251) according to the manufacturer's instructions to visualize the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Cells were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min, and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h.

Cells were subsequently incubated with the indicated primary antibodies against P4HA3 or SLC7A11 overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Biolegend, #422801). Fluorescence images were acquired using a confocal laser scanning microscope.

Gene expression data collation and functional enrichment analysis

Gene expression data were obtained from the TCGA-KIRC cohort and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE216494. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the DESeq2 package in R. Hydroxylase-related genes were retrieved from the GeneCards database. In addition, corresponding clinical data from the TCGA-KIRC cohort were collected to evaluate patient prognosis. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free interval (PFI) were used as primary endpoints. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed to assess the prognostic significance of candidate genes, and differences between groups were evaluated using the log-rank test. Genes of interest were subsequently subjected to functional enrichment analyses as indicated.

Proteomic profiling analysis

For global proteomic analysis, 769-P cells expressing control shRNA (shNC, n = 3) or P4HA3-targeting shRNA (shP4HA3, n = 4) were harvested and lysed for protein extraction. Equal amounts of protein were digested with trypsin and analyzed by LC-MS/MS. Raw data were processed using MaxQuant and searched against the UniProt human database. Label-free quantification was used to determine relative protein abundance. Proteins with a fold change >2 or <0.5 and a p-value < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed and subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis using the clusterProfiler R package.

For interactome analysis, endogenous SLC7A11 was immunoprecipitated from 769-P cells under control conditions or upon P4HA3 overexpression, followed by LC-MS/MS analysis of the precipitated complexes. Differentially enriched interactors were identified based on relative abundance and annotated using GO and UniProt functional information.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently performed at least three times. Data were analyzed using R software (version 4.3.0) and GraphPad Prism. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using a two-tailed Student's t-test, whereas comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

P4HA3 is upregulated in renal cell carcinoma and associated with sunitinib resistance and poor prognosis

Accumulating evidence has shown that hydroxylation plays an important role in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. To explore its potential involvement in TKI resistance in renal cell carcinoma, we intersected genes from GSE216494 (including sunitinib-sensitive and -resistant renal cancer cell lines), the TCGA-KIRC dataset, and a curated list of hydroxylase-related genes, identifying nine overlapping candidates (Fig. 1A–B). Among them, P4HA3 attracted our particular interest. Analysis of the TCGA dataset revealed that P4HA3 mRNA levels were elevated across multiple cancer types, including ccRCC. (Fig. 1C, Fig. S1A). In ccRCC, higher P4HA3 expression was associated with advanced pathological grade (Fig. 1D) and increased tumor stage (T stage) (Fig. S1B), whereas no significant correlation was observed with node stage (N stage) or metastasis stage (M stage) (Fig. S1C–D). In addition, elevated P4HA3 expression was significantly associated with poorer OS (Fig. 1E) and PFI outcomes (Fig. S1E). Consistently, paired tumor-normal samples from the GSE102101 dataset further confirmed the upregulation of P4HA3 in renal cancer tissues (Fig. S1F). To validate these bioinformatic findings, we examined P4HA3 expression in renal cancer cell lines and clinical specimens. P4HA3 protein levels were markedly higher in four renal cancer cell lines compared with the normal renal tubular epithelial cell line HK-2 (Fig. 1F). Western blot analysis of eight paired ccRCC tissues further confirmed elevated P4HA3 expression in tumors relative to adjacent normal kidney tissues (Fig. 1G). To determine whether P4HA3 is associated with TKI resistance, we established sunitinib-resistant OSRC-2 and 769-P cell lines by gradual dose escalation over three months (Fig. 1H). CCK-8 assays confirmed the resistant phenotype of OSRC-2-R and 769-P-R cells (Fig. 1I–J). Notably, P4HA3 expression was substantially increased in resistant cells compared with their parental counterparts (Fig. 1K, Fig. S1G). Functional enrichment analysis of P4HA3-associated genes revealed significant enrichment in pathways such as EMT (Fig. S1H–I), suggesting a potential link between P4HA3 and aggressive tumor behavior.

P4HA3 promotes TKI resistance and aggressive phenotypes in renal cancer cells

We next examined the functional consequences of P4HA3 upregulation in drug-sensitive renal cancer cells. P4HA3 was ectopically expressed in 769-P and OSRC-2 cells (Fig. S2A–D). Both colony formation assays (Fig. 2A–B) and CCK8 assays (Fig. 2C–D, Fig. S2E–F) showed that P4HA3 overexpression markedly increased cell proliferation under low-dose sunitinib or axitinib treatment. Dose-response analyses further demonstrated that enforced P4HA3 expression markedly increased resistance to both sunitinib (Fig. 2E–F) and axitinib (Fig. S2G–H), as reflected by higher cell viability across a range of drug concentrations. Consistently, propidium iodide (PI) staining revealed reduced drug-induced cell death in P4HA3-overexpressing cells (Fig. 2G–H), confirming a pronounced drug-resistant phenotype. In addition, Western blot analysis showed that P4HA3 overexpression induced a mesenchymal-like state, with increased expression of N-cadherin, Vimentin, Snail, and Slug and reduced E-cadherin expression (Fig. 2I–J, Fig. S2I–J), suggesting that P4HA3 also promotes phenotypic changes associated with tumor aggressiveness. Collectively, these results indicate that P4HA3 overexpression confers resistance to sunitinib and axitinib and is accompanied by activation of aggressive cellular programs.

P4HA3 depletion reverses sunitinib resistance in resistant renal cancer

Next, we silenced P4HA3 in the established sunitinib-resistant OSRC-2 and 769-P cell lines (Fig. S2K–N). Both colony formation assays (Fig. 3A) and CCK-8 assays (Fig. 3B–C) showed that P4HA3 knockdown markedly suppressed cell proliferation under low-dose sunitinib treatment. Dose-response analyses further revealed that depletion of P4HA3 significantly enhanced the cytotoxic effects of sunitinib, as reflected by reduced cell viability across increasing drug concentrations (Fig. 3D–E). Consistently, PI staining demonstrated a higher proportion of dead cells in P4HA3-silenced groups upon sunitinib exposure, indicating a substantial reduction in drug resistance (Fig. 3F–G). In parallel, P4HA3 knockdown was associated with reduced expression of mesenchymal markers and increased expression of epithelial markers (Fig. 3H–I, Fig.S2O-P). In vivo, subcutaneous xenograft experiments further confirmed that P4HA3 depletion significantly sensitized resistant OSRC-2 tumors to sunitinib treatment (Fig. 3J), resulting in markedly slower tumor growth (Fig. 3K) and lower tumor weight (Fig. 3L) compared with controls. Immunohistochemical staining of Ki67 revealed a significantly reduced fraction of proliferating cells in the shP4HA3 group under sunitinib treatment (Fig. 3M–N). Collectively, these findings indicate that P4HA3 knockdown restores sensitivity of sunitinib-resistant renal cancer cells to therapy and suppresses tumor growth both in vitro and in vivo.

P4HA3 promotes sunitinib resistance by suppressing ferroptosis

Proteomic profiling was conducted to elucidate the mechanisms by which P4HA3 promotes sunitinib resistance in renal cell carcinoma. A total of 115 proteins were upregulated and 142 were downregulated following P4HA3 knockdown in 769-P cells (Fig. 4A). KEGG and GO enrichment analyses revealed significant enrichment of pathways related to ferroptosis, NAD+-protein poly-ADP-ribosyltransferase activity, fatty acid-CoA ligase activity, and transition metal ion binding, all of which are closely associated with ferroptosis regulation (Fig. 4B, Fig. S3A). Among the differentially expressed proteins, SLC7A11 was markedly reduced upon P4HA3 silencing (Fig. 4C). Given that SLC7A11 is a key negative regulator of ferroptosis, we further examined its expression upon P4HA3 manipulation. Western blot analysis showed that P4HA3 knockdown reduced (Fig. 4D–E, Fig. S3B–C), whereas P4HA3 overexpression increased (Fig. S3D–G), SLC7A11 protein levels in both 769-P and OSRC-2 cells, while SLC7A11 mRNA levels remained unchanged (Fig. S3H), indicating post-transcriptional regulation. Moreover, the proteasome inhibitor MG132, but not the lysosomal inhibitor chloroquine (CQ), restored SLC7A11 protein levels in P4HA3-silenced cells (Fig. S3I–J), indicating that P4HA3 regulates SLC7A11 protein abundance primarily through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. We next examined additional ferroptosis-related markers. P4HA3 knockdown increased the expression of pro-ferroptotic markers ACSL4 and PTGS2 while reducing the anti-ferroptotic proteins FTH1 and GPX4 (Fig. 4D–E, Fig. S3B–C), whereas the opposite pattern was observed upon P4HA3 overexpression (Fig. S3D–G). In contrast, apoptosis-related markers remained largely unchanged (Fig. 4D–E, Fig. S3B–G), suggesting that P4HA3 preferentially modulates ferroptosis rather than apoptosis. To further delineate the mode of cell death, we employed a panel of cell death inhibitors. The ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1), but not the apoptosis inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK (Z-VAD), the necroptosis inhibitor Necrostatin-1s (Nec-1s), or the lysosomal inhibitor CQ, effectively rescued the enhanced sunitinib sensitivity (Fig. 4F–G) and cell death (Fig. 4H–I) induced by P4HA3 knockdown. These findings indicate that ferroptosis, rather than apoptosis, necroptosis, or lysosome-dependent cell death, is the dominant mechanism underlying the observed phenotype. Consistently, transmission electron microscopy revealed that P4HA3 silencing induced pronounced mitochondrial abnormalities in 769-P cells, including reduced or absent cristae—hallmark ultrastructural features of ferroptosis—which were largely reversed by Fer-1 treatment (Fig. 4J). Collectively, these results demonstrate that P4HA3 inhibits ferroptosis in ccRCC and thereby contributes to sunitinib resistance.

SLC7A11 mediates the anti-ferroptotic effects of P4HA3 in ccRCC

To determine whether P4HA3 suppresses ferroptosis through maintenance of SLC7A11 protein stability, we first restored SLC7A11 levels in P4HA3-depleted cells using MG132 and subsequently re-silenced SLC7A11 (Fig. 5A–B). P4HA3 knockdown markedly reduced the GSH/GSSG ratio in both OSRC-2 and 769-P cells, indicating impaired antioxidant capacity. MG132 largely restored glutathione homeostasis, whereas this effect was abolished by SLC7A11 silencing (Fig. 5C–D), indicating that the MG132-mediated redox rescue is SLC7A11-dependent. Consistently, P4HA3 depletion increased the labile iron pool and enhanced DCFH-DA fluorescence (MFI); both were suppressed by MG132 but re-elevated upon SLC7A11 knockdown (Fig. 5E–F, Fig. S4A–B), supporting a central role of SLC7A11 in mediating iron and redox homeostasis downstream of P4HA3. Similarly, ferroptosis-associated lipid damage, reflected by elevated MDA levels and increased BODIPY 581/591C11 fluorescence (MFI), was induced by P4HA3 silencing, reversed by MG132, and reinstated upon SLC7A11 depletion (Fig. 5G–I, Fig.S4C). Finally, MG132 rescued the mitochondrial ultrastructural alterations characteristic of ferroptosis in P4HA3-deficient cells, whereas this protection was largely lost upon SLC7A11 knockdown (Fig. 5J). Collectively, these results indicate that SLC7A11 is a key downstream mediator of P4HA3 in suppressing ferroptosis in ccRCC.

P4HA3 stabilizes SLC7A11 through hydroxylation-dependent suppression of ubiquitination

To further elucidate how P4HA3 regulates SLC7A11, we first examined whether the two proteins physically associate. Semi-endogenous co-immunoprecipitation in 769-P and OSRC-2 cells confirmed a robust interaction between FLAG-P4HA3 and SLC7A11 (Fig. 6A–B, Fig. S4D–E). Considering the canonical ER localization of P4HA3, we next investigated the spatial context of this interaction. Confocal immunofluorescence analyses with ER-Tracker staining revealed strong ER localization of P4HA3, while SLC7A11 also exhibited partial ER-associated localization (Fig. S4F–G). In addition, co-immunofluorescence staining demonstrated substantial spatial overlap between P4HA3 and SLC7A11 (Fig. S4H). Together, these findings support the notion that P4HA3-mediated regulation of SLC7A11 likely occurs during its ER-associated maturation process. We next assessed whether P4HA3 affects SLC7A11 protein stability. Cycloheximide (CHX) chase assays showed that P4HA3 knockdown markedly accelerated SLC7A11 degradation (Fig. 6C), indicating that P4HA3 is required for maintaining SLC7A11 stability. We therefore examined whether P4HA3 modulates SLC7A11 ubiquitination. Overexpression of P4HA3 substantially reduced SLC7A11 ubiquitination (Fig. 6D–E), whereas P4HA3 depletion markedly increased it (Fig. S5A–B). Given that several members of the P4HA family have been shown to hydroxylate non-collagen substrates and thereby regulate their stability [21] [27], we next examined whether P4HA3 similarly modifies SLC7A11. Indeed, enforced P4HA3 expression enhanced SLC7A11 hydroxyproline modification (Hy-OH) while concomitantly decreasing its ubiquitination, and both effects were reversed by the hydroxylase inhibitor 1,4-DPCA (Fig. 6F–G, Fig. S5C–F), suggesting that hydroxylation mediates the stabilizing effect of P4HA3. To directly assess the requirement for enzymatic activity, we generated a hydroxylase-inactive triple mutant (H440S/D442S/H510S; P4HA3-3Mut) targeting the Fe2+/2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase domain (Fig. S5G). Unlike wild-type P4HA3, P4HA3-3Mut failed to increase the hydroxyproline modification of SLC7A11 or suppress its ubiquitination and degradation (Fig. 6H–I, Fig.S5H), demonstrating that P4HA3 catalytic activity is indispensable for SLC7A11 stabilization. Consistently, functional assays showed that only wild-type P4HA3 reduced lipid peroxidation, decreased MDA levels, and increased the GSH/GSSG ratio, whereas these effects were abolished by DPCA treatment or P4HA3-3Mut expression (Fig. 6J, Fig. S5I–J). Moreover, CCK-8 assays revealed that P4HA3 overexpression protected RCC cells from sunitinib-induced cytotoxicity, while DPCA or P4HA3-3Mut effectively reversed this resistance phenotype (Fig. S5K). Finally, to identify the hydroxylation sites, we analyzed proline residues within SLC7A11 and selected P153, P161, and P66 as candidate sites based on sequence context and structural accessibility. Mutation of P153 and P161 (P153A/P161A), but not P66 (P66A), abolished P4HA3-mediated hydroxyproline modification and prevented the reduction of SLC7A11 ubiquitination (Fig. 6K–L, Fig. S5L), indicating that P153 and P161 are the principal residues targeted by P4HA3. To further validate the functional importance of the P153/P161 hydroxylation sites, we performed rescue experiments in 769-P cells expressing either WT or P153A/P161A mutant SLC7A11. Notably, P4HA3 overexpression failed to further increase the protein level of the P153A/P161A mutant, in contrast to its stabilizing effect on WT SLC7A11 (Fig. 6M–N). Functionally, P4HA3 overexpression further suppressed lipid peroxidation and MDA levels, while increasing the GSH/GSSG ratio in cells expressing WT SLC7A11. In contrast, these effects were largely abolished in cells expressing the P153A/P161A mutant (Fig. 6O, Fig. S5M–N). Collectively, these findings establish a mechanistic model in which P4HA3 directly binds and hydroxylates SLC7A11 at P153 and P161, thereby inhibiting its ubiquitin-mediated degradation, sustaining antioxidant capacity, suppressing ferroptosis, and ultimately promoting TKI resistance in renal cancer cells.

P4HA3 inhibits NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination of SLC7A11 in a hydroxylation-dependent manner

To determine whether P4HA3 regulates the interaction between SLC7A11 and ubiquitin-related regulators, SLC7A11 immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry was performed under basal conditions and upon P4HA3 overexpression in 769-P cells. A total of 1144 proteins were commonly identified in both groups, whereas 302 proteins were detected exclusively under basal conditions and 271 proteins were uniquely enriched following P4HA3 overexpression. We next focused on ubiquitination-related components within the SLC7A11 interactome. Notably, several ubiquitin-associated enzymes, including HERC6, NEDD4, WWP1, USP7, RNF213, and XIAP, exhibited condition-specific association with SLC7A11, suggesting that P4HA3 reshapes the ubiquitin regulatory landscape surrounding SLC7A11 (Fig. S6A). Among these candidates, NEDD4 was prioritized based on its well-established role as a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase mediating proteasomal degradation of membrane-associated substrates. Co-immunoprecipitation assays confirmed a robust endogenous interaction between NEDD4 and SLC7A11 in renal cancer cells (Fig. 7A–B, Fig. S6B–C). Importantly, enforced expression of wild-type P4HA3 markedly weakened this interaction, whereas a hydroxylase-inactive mutant failed to do so (Fig. 7C–D), indicating that P4HA3 disrupts the NEDD4-SLC7A11 interaction in a catalytic activity-dependent manner. Functionally, NEDD4 depletion restored SLC7A11 protein levels reduced by P4HA3 knockdown and abolished the corresponding increase in SLC7A11 ubiquitination (Fig. 7E–G), demonstrating that NEDD4 is required for P4HA3-dependent regulation of SLC7A11 stability. Consistently, silencing NEDD4 largely reversed the ferroptotic phenotypes induced by P4HA3 knockdown, including increased lipid peroxidation (Fig. 7H), elevated MDA levels (Fig. S6D), and reduced GSH/GSSG ratio (Fig. S6E). We next sought to identify the ubiquitination sites on SLC7A11 that mediate this regulation. Based on putative ubiquitination sites reported in previous studies [28] [29], we generated a series of lysine-to-arginine mutants. Among these, only the K30R/K37R mutant abolished the reduction in SLC7A11 protein levels induced by P4HA3 depletion (Fig. 7I). Consistently, P4HA3 knockdown failed to further enhance ubiquitination of the SLC7A11-K30R/K37R mutant (Fig. 7J), indicating that these residues represent the primary sites of NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination. Rescue experiments further demonstrated that WT SLC7A11 only partially restored protein levels in P4HA3-silenced cells, whereas the K30R/K37R mutant almost completely rescued SLC7A11 expression (Fig. 7K–L), suggesting that P4HA3 stabilizes SLC7A11 predominantly by preventing ubiquitination at K30 and K37. Functionally, WT SLC7A11 partially reversed the increase in lipid peroxidation and MDA levels, as well as the decrease in the GSH/GSSG ratio induced by P4HA3 knockdown. In contrast, the K30R/K37R mutant nearly completely rescued these ferroptosis-associated phenotypes (Fig. 7M–O). Collectively, these findings identify K30 and K37 as critical ubiquitination sites and demonstrate that P4HA3 suppresses ferroptosis by restraining NEDD4-dependent ubiquitination and degradation of SLC7A11.

Pharmacological inhibition of P4HA3 sensitizes renal cancer to TKI therapy

To assess whether P4HA3 can be therapeutically targeted to overcome TKI resistance in renal cancer, we first evaluated pharmacological inhibition of P4HA3. A recent study by Xu et al. reported that Tubuloside A suppresses P4HA3 expression and induces ferroptosis in colorectal cancer [30], prompting us to examine its effects in renal cancer models. Treatment of 769-P cells with Tubuloside A led to a marked reduction in P4HA3 expression, accompanied by a concomitant decrease in SLC7A11 protein levels (Fig. S7A). Correspondingly, Tubuloside A enhanced sunitinib sensitivity, as evidenced by decreased cell viability; however, this effect was significantly attenuated upon P4HA3 knockdown (Fig. S7B), indicating that its anti-tumor activity is largely P4HA3-dependent. In parallel, we employed the pan-prolyl hydroxylase inhibitor 1,4-DPCA to further interrogate the role of P4HA3. Given that 1,4-DPCA has been reported to stabilize HIF-1α [31], we first examined HIF-1α protein levels and confirmed a marked increase following treatment (Fig. S7C). Notably, 1,4-DPCA concurrently reduced SLC7A11 protein levels; however, this reduction was not further enhanced upon P4HA3 knockdown (Fig. S7C), suggesting that both interventions act through a shared P4HA3-SLC7A11 regulatory axis. In line with this, 1,4-DPCA enhanced sunitinib sensitivity, but this effect was markedly diminished in P4HA3-silenced cells (Fig. S7D), indicating that its anti-tumor activity is largely dependent on P4HA3 inhibition despite concomitant HIF-1α stabilization. To validate these findings in more physiologically relevant models, three patient-derived ccRCC organoid lines were established (Fig. 8A). At the concentrations tested, Tubuloside A or 1,4-DPCA alone produced only limited effects on organoid growth and viability. In contrast, their combination with sunitinib or axitinib markedly suppressed organoid growth and viability (Fig. 8B–C, Fig. S7E–H). These findings suggest that Tubuloside A and 1,4-DPCA primarily sensitize the organoids to TKI-induced stress rather than exerting substantial cytotoxic effects as monotherapies under these conditions. We next assessed therapeutic efficacy in vivo. In a subcutaneous xenograft model, co-administration of Tubuloside A or 1,4-DPCA significantly potentiated the anti-tumor effect of sunitinib, resulting in slower tumor growth and reduced final tumor weight compared with sunitinib alone (Fig. 8D–F). Immunohistochemical analysis further revealed decreased GPX4 expression, increased 4-HNE accumulation, and reduced Ki67 positivity (Fig. 8G), indicating enhanced lipid peroxidation, ferroptotic stress, and suppressed tumor proliferation in vivo. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of P4HA3 enhances TKI efficacy by promoting ferroptosis across in vitro, organoid, and in vivo models, highlighting P4HA3 as a promising therapeutic target for overcoming TKI resistance in renal cancer. Importantly, immunohistochemical analysis of human ccRCC specimens revealed a strong positive correlation between P4HA3 and SLC7A11 protein expression, further supporting the pathological relevance of the P4HA3-SLC7A11 axis (Fig. 8H).

Discussion

Resistance to TKIs remains a major clinical obstacle in the treatment of renal cell carcinoma [32] [33], yet the mechanisms by which tumor cells adaptively evade therapy-induced ferroptotic stress are incompletely understood [34]. In this study, we identify P4HA3 as a previously unrecognized regulator of ferroptosis and a critical determinant of TKI resistance in RCC. We demonstrate that P4HA3 is aberrantly upregulated in RCC, correlates with poor prognosis and reduced therapeutic responsiveness, and functionally promotes resistance to TKIs both in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, P4HA3 suppresses ferroptotic cell death by stabilizing SLC7A11, thereby sustaining intracellular redox homeostasis and protecting tumor cells from TKI-induced oxidative stress. This effect depends on the enzymatic activity of P4HA3 and involves hydroxylation-dependent post-translational modification of SLC7A11 that disrupts its interaction with the E3 ubiquitin ligase NEDD4, thereby attenuating NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of SLC7A11. Collectively, these findings define a P4HA3-SLC7A11-NEDD4 regulatory axis that enables RCC cells to evade ferroptosis and acquire resistance to TKIs.

Although P4HA3 has classically been characterized as a collagen-modifying enzyme involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, accumulating evidence indicates that members of the P4HA family exert important non-canonical functions in cancer beyond matrix regulation. For example, P4HA2 has been reported to hydroxylate SUFU to modulate paracrine Hedgehog signaling and thereby promote B-cell lymphoma progression [27], and to activate mTOR signaling through hydroxylation-dependent mechanisms to drive lung cancer growth [21]. Consistent with this emerging paradigm, our data reveal that P4HA3 confers a selective survival advantage to RCC cells under TKI-induced stress, whereas genetic or pharmacological inhibition of P4HA3 markedly sensitizes tumors to TKIs across multiple experimental systems, including cell lines, xenografts, and patient-derived organoids. These findings reposition P4HA3 as a mediator of cellular stress adaptation during targeted therapy and highlight its functional relevance in regulating oncogenic signaling and therapeutic stress responses.

Ferroptosis critically shapes therapeutic responses in RCC, particularly under TKI-induced oxidative stress, and SLC7A11 serves as a central mediator of ferroptosis resistance by sustaining glutathione homeostasis and limiting lipid peroxidation [35] [36] [37]. Building on the conceptual framework above, we demonstrate that P4HA3 promotes ferroptosis evasion by stabilizing SLC7A11 at the post-translational level, thereby maintaining redox balance and cell viability under therapeutic pressure. Mechanistically, this hydroxylation-dependent regulation is likely initiated in the endoplasmic reticulum, where P4HA3 modifies SLC7A11 to enhance its protein stability and prevent ubiquitin-mediated degradation, thereby facilitating its functional accumulation at the plasma membrane. Notably, we found that P4HA3-mediated hydroxyproline modification reduced the interaction between SLC7A11 and the E3 ubiquitin ligase NEDD4 and suppressed NEDD4-dependent ubiquitination of SLC7A11 at K30/K37, thereby limiting its proteasomal degradation. This effect strictly depends on P4HA3 enzymatic activity, as catalytic inhibition or inactivation abolishes SLC7A11 stabilization and restores its ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation.

To identify the specific proline residues targeted by P4HA3, we initially attempted mass spectrometry analysis. However, owing to the multi-pass transmembrane nature of SLC7A11 and the inherent technical limitations of mass spectrometry for membrane proteins, we were unable to directly detect the hydroxylation sites [38] [39]. We therefore analyzed the structure and topology of SLC7A11 as a 12-pass transmembrane antiporter (system xc-) and identified candidate proline residues based on their spatial orientation and accessibility [40] [41]. Guided by this analysis, we selected P66, P153, and P161 for mutagenesis. Immunoprecipitation assays following expression of these mutants revealed that mutation of P153 and P161, but not P66, markedly reduced P4HA3-associated hydroxyproline signals and significantly increased SLC7A11 ubiquitination. Importantly, functional rescue experiments further demonstrated that the P153A/P161A mutant failed to maintain SLC7A11 stability and could not effectively suppress lipid peroxidation or restore redox homeostasis compared with wild-type SLC7A11, highlighting the functional importance of these residues. Together, these findings suggest that P153 and P161 are critical residues involved in P4HA3-mediated regulation of SLC7A11 and support a hydroxylation-associated mechanism underlying SLC7A11 stabilization and ferroptosis evasion. These results uncover a previously unappreciated layer of post-translational regulation of ferroptosis sensitivity and highlight prolyl hydroxylation as a potential mechanism governing redox homeostasis, stress adaptation, and drug resistance in cancer.

Recent work in cervical cancer suggested that P4HA3 promotes ferroptosis resistance primarily through an indirect transcriptional mechanism involving ACLY stabilization and subsequent upregulation of SLC7A11 mRNA, without evidence of a stable P4HA3-SLC7A11 protein interaction [42]. In contrast, our study identifies a distinct regulatory paradigm in RCC in which P4HA3 directly associates with and post-translationally modifies SLC7A11 to control its protein stability. These differences likely reflect tumor-type-specific metabolic dependencies and context-dependent regulatory hierarchies [43] [44]. Importantly, transcriptional and post-translational regulation of SLC7A11 are not mutually exclusive and may operate in parallel under distinct cellular states or therapeutic conditions [45], highlighting the plasticity of ferroptosis regulatory networks in cancer.

Several limitations warrant consideration. Although we demonstrate that P4HA3 directly associates with and hydroxylates SLC7A11 to regulate its stability, the precise modification site(s) and structural consequences remain to be fully elucidated. In addition, while xenograft and organoid models support the therapeutic relevance of targeting P4HA3 to enhance TKI efficacy, they do not fully recapitulate the complexity of the human tumor microenvironment. Finally, given the established roles of P4HA3 in extracellular matrix regulation and tumor progression, ferroptosis-independent mechanisms may also contribute to TKI resistance in RCC and merit further investigation.

Correlation between neuropathy severity and neurofilament light chain levels in Brazilian patients with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis
Authors: Priscila Ferreira, Lucas do Amaral Martins, Larissa Araujo Duarte, Andreza Salvio Lemos, Soniza Vieira Alves-Leon, Debora Foguel, Marcia Waddington Cruz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTRv) is a genetic disorder caused by more than 100 autosomal dominant mutations in the TTR gene. Owing to its marked clinical heterogeneity—particularly in its cardiac manifestations—ATTRv is frequently underdiagnosed and requires comprehensive clinical evaluation. The disease commonly presents as a progressive axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with autonomic involvement. Despite advances in disease-modifying therapies, including the availability of Tafamidis, delayed diagnosis remains a major challenge, contributing to increased morbidity and mortality. Therefore, the identification of reliable biomarkers for early detection, disease staging, and therapeutic monitoring is essential. One promising candidate is neurofilament light chain (NfL), a structural axonal protein released into the circulation following neuronal injury, which has been proposed as a biomarker of neuroaxonal damage in ATTRv in other populations. The present study aimed to evaluate plasma NfL (pNfL) levels in Brazilian patients with ATTRv and to investigate their association with neuropathy severity. Symptomatic and asymptomatic patients with genetically confirmed ATTRv followed at a specialized university reference center were included, along with healthy volunteer controls. A subgroup of symptomatic patients was receiving Tafamidis therapy. Clinical and demographic data were collected during routine visits, and neuropathy severity was assessed using validated instruments, including the Neuropathy Impairment Score (NIS) and the Polyneuropathy Disability (PND) score. Plasma NfL concentrations were measured using the ultrasensitive SIMOA SR-X platform. Statistical analyses were performed to compare pNfL levels across groups and to assess correlations with clinical parameters. Symptomatic patients not receiving Tafamidis exhibited significantly higher pNfL levels compared with treated symptomatic patients, asymptomatic mutation carriers, and healthy controls. Moreover, pNfL concentrations showed a positive correlation with NIS scores, indicating increasing axonal damage with disease progression. Z-score analyses further supported the ability of pNfL to discriminate between disease stages. In conclusion, plasma NfL emerges as a promising biomarker for assessing disease severity, progression, and treatment response in ATTRv, supporting its potential utility in Brazilian clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48524-0.Keywords: Familial amyloid polyneuropathy, Neurofilament light chain, Biomarkers, Transthyretin, And AmyloidosisSubject terms: Biomarkers, Diseases, Neurology, Neuroscience

Introduction

Hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTRv) is a genetic disorder caused by over 100 autosomal dominant mutations in the Transthyretin (TTR) gene. ATTRv is frequently underdiagnosed due to its heterogeneous clinical presentation, which often requires extensive clinical evaluation for accurate diagnosis1–5.

Although the primary manifestations are neuropathic and cardiac, ATTRv is a systemic disease. Neurologists should be alert to the possibility of cardiac, ocular, and renal involvement, which underscores the need for a multidisciplinary approach to patient care. Autonomic symptoms frequently accompany the onset of peripheral neuropathy and may serve as an important early warning sign6.

Peripheral neuropathy often precedes the diagnosis of hereditary ATTR (hATTR) amyloidosis by up to four years7. The most typical presentation is an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy with autonomic involvement. Although neuropathy is usually axonal, some patients may exhibit slowing of conduction velocity and conduction block, mimicking chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP)8.

As of today, several disease-modifying therapies have been approved for ATTRv, underscoring the urgent need to identify reliable biomarkers to support early diagnosis and improve patient survival9–12. Despite therapeutic advances in ATTRv, diagnostic delays remain common, contributing to increased morbidity and mortality in affected individuals.

Regarding the molecular mechanism underlying ATTR pathogenesis, the disease begins with the destabilization and dissociation of the TTR tetramer into monomers, which subsequently misfold and aggregate into a spectrum of species—including oligomers and amyloid fibrils—collectively referred to as non-native transthyretin (NNTTR). These NNTTR species can be detected in patient plasma and are being explored as potential biomarkers for both diagnosis and disease progression13. However, the use of NNTTR as a biomarker is challenged by the highly dynamic and interconverting nature of these amyloidogenic species, which complicates their structural characterization14.

Another biomarker that has been investigated for several years in the context of neurodegenerative diseases is neurofilament, a key structural component of neurons, a marker not exclusively for TTR-related conditions. Neurofilaments are intrinsic determinants of axonal caliber and conduction velocity15. Among the three major subunits, the neurofilament light chain (NfL) is of particular interest, as it is released into the bloodstream following axonal injury or during neurodegenerative processes16–18.

Several studies have demonstrated that plasma NfL (pNfL) levels are elevated in patients with ATTRv amyloidosis and polyneuropathy and in other neurodegenerative diseases when compared to healthy controls. Moreover, pNfL concentrations have been shown to correlate with disease severity, as assessed by the Neuropathy Impairment Score (NIS) in symptomatic individuals19–26.

In this study, we aimed to determine whether pNfL levels are similarly elevated in Brazilian patients with ATTRv-related neuropathy compared to healthy controls, and whether these levels correlate with clinical severity and neuropathy scores, thereby reinforcing the utility of pNfL as a reliable biomarker in this population.

Methods and materials

Study participants

The study included neurologically symptomatic (12 individuals) and asymptomatic (8 individuals) patients with genetically confirmed ATTRv amyloidosis followed at the Centro de Paramiloidose Antônio Rodrigues de Mello, located at the Hospital Universitário Clementino Fraga Filho (UFRJ). Among symptomatic patients, nine individuals were receiving Tafamidis therapy, whereas three were not undergoing treatment at the time of blood collection Among the symptomatic patients, only one carried the Ile107Val mutation, while one asymptomatic individual had the Val122Ile mutation. All other patients carried the Val30Met mutation. Although the sample size is limited, ATTRv is a rare disorder and is likely underdiagnosed in Brazil owing to the country’s vast geographic extent and limited access to specialized diagnostic.

The control volunteers (n = 9) were recruited at the Laboratório de Neurociência Translacional of the Universidade Federal do Estado do Rio de Janeiro (UNIRIO). Control participants completed a standardized questionnaire to exclude neurological, central nervous system, autoimmune, or amyloid diseases.

Patient characteristics and clinical scores were recorded during routine clinical visits (Table 1). Data collected included age, sex, specific ATTRv mutation, and clinical phenotype. Blood samples

Peripheral blood was collected into EDTA-containing tubes, centrifuged at 3,500 rpm for 10 min at 20 °C, and the resulting plasma was aliquoted and stored at − 80 °C until analysis. Biobanking procedures followed current international guidelines28.

Detection of pNfL by SIMOA SR-X

pNfL levels were measured using the SIMOA® platform with the SR-X instrument (Quanterix Corporation, Lexington, MA, USA), employing the NF-light™ Advantage kit (Quanterix). Samples were diluted and processed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, using a calibration curve ranging from 0 to 500 pg/mL and including appropriate controls. The inferior detection limit for NfL was 0.0552 pg/mL.

Briefly, diluted samples, calibrators, controls, carboxylated paramagnetic beads, and detection reagents were incubated together in a 96-well plate for 30 min at 30 °C with continuous shaking at 800 rpm. Following the incubation, the wells were washed and incubated with Streptavidin-β-galactosidase (SβG) for an additional 10 min under the same conditions. After a second wash, the plate was inserted into the SR-X instrument for quantification, which was performed using the instrument’s dedicated software.

Z-score measurement

To define pathologically elevated pNfL levels independent of age and body mass index (BMI), we derived Z-scores based on a large reference database from a general population cohort. This dataset was specifically designed to provide normative pNfL values in individuals without evidence of central nervous system disease and has been validated for use in Z-score calculation29.

Statistical analysis

pNfL concentrations were compared between the four groups using the nonparametric Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn’s multiple comparisons test, due to the small sample size per group and non-normal distribution of data. Pairwise comparisons were adjusted for multiple testing using Dunn’s method. A significance level of α = 0.05 was applied. For standardization, raw pNfL concentrations were first log10-transformed. Subsequently, individual z-scores were calculated using the healthy control group as the reference, according to the formula z = (x − μ)/σz = (x − μ)/σ, where xx represents the individual log10-transformed pNfL concentration, and μμ and σσ correspond to the mean and standard deviation of the healthy control group, respectively. Analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Correlations were assessed using Pearson’s and Spearman’s correlation coefficients as appropriate, with a significance level set at 5% (p < 0.05). Multiple linear regression was performed to evaluate the relationship between pNfL (dependent variable), age, and NIS score (independent variables). Statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS version 24 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1 (GraphPad Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA).

Results

Clinical features of ATTR patients

Patients enrolled in the present study were those who attended our Center during the blood collection period. All eligible participants provided informed consent and underwent comprehensive clinical and neurophysiological evaluations. Clinical and demographic characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The values of pNfL (pg/mL) of the controls and patients are presented in the eighth column.

Plasma NfL levels are reduced in asymptomatic ATTRv patients and near-control levels following Tafamidis treatment

To investigate the utility of NfL as a biomarker for ATTRv amyloidosis, we quantified pNfL levels in symptomatic and asymptomatic patients—treated or untreated with Tafamidis—and compared them with levels in healthy controls. As shown in Table 1 and plotted in Fig. 1, asymptomatic patients (squares) displayed pNfL levels like those of healthy controls (circles) and substantially lower than those in untreated, symptomatic individuals (triangles) represented by only three patients in our sample, since Tafamidis is currently available for prescription. Symptomatic patients undergoing tafamidis treatment (inverted triangles) exhibited NfL levels comparable to those of asymptomatic patients. Because TTR-related amyloidosis is a rare disease, the cohort size in this study is limited; therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution when drawing correlations.

Plasma NfL levels as biomarkers of disease stage and progression

It is well established that, over time, asymptomatic ATTRv patients tend to show progressive increases in Neuropathy Impairment Score (NIS) due to the neurodegenerative nature of the disease. In this study, changes in pNfL levels were analyzed in relation to NIS scores among symptomatic patients, both treated and untreated with Tafamidis. Treated patients were generally at earlier stages of the disease, either because the drug is administered as soon as the disease is diagnosed or because the drug slows disease progression, while untreated patients were typically at more advanced stages (Table 1).

As shown in Fig. 2, pNfL concentrations positively correlate with NIS scores in symptomatic patients. It is possible to see that the three untreated patients (filled circles) presented the highest pNfL levels. This finding indicates that disease progression is associated with elevated NfL levels in the plasma, reflecting increased axonal damage and greater neurological impairment.

Z-score in ATTR patients

Z-scores reflect how the NfL values observed in ATTR patients compare to a reference database composed of individuals without related neurological conditions. Values closer to zero indicate levels within the normal range.

In this study, symptomatic ATTRv patients (Fig. 3; triangles and circles) exhibited higher NfL z-scores, indicating elevated NfL levels compared with the reference database. In contrast, asymptomatic mutation carriers (squares) showed low z-scores, consistent with values closer to the normal range.

Discussion

The findings from this study provide important insights into the potential utility of neurofilament light chain (NfL) as a biomarker for disease severity and progression in ATTRv among Brazilian patients, consistent with prior reports20,23–25. Our data show a clear stratification of plasma NfL levels among symptomatic and asymptomatic patients, as well as between those receiving treatment (Tafamidis) and those untreated.

Symptomatic, untreated patients exhibited markedly elevated plasma NfL levels, which correlated with greater neuropathy severity as indicated by higher NIS values. This finding aligns with the progressive neurodegeneration characteristic of ATTRv over time.

Interestingly, the levels of NfL in the asymptomatic mutation carriers were not significantly higher than those of the health controls (Fig. 1), although both median and mean values remained higher than those observed in healthy controls (Table 1). The z-scores of the asymptomatic patients were also close to the reference database (Fig. 3). These findings suggest that perhaps in a larger cohort, NfL could detect early subclinical axonal damage prior to the onset of clinical symptoms, supporting its potential as a sensitive early biomarker of neurological involvement in ATTRv.

Moreover, patients receiving Tafamidis—a TTR stabilizer—showed NfL levels close to those of asymptomatic carriers (Fig. 1). This observation indicates that early therapeutic intervention may attenuate or slow neuronal damage, reinforcing the importance of timely diagnosis and treatment initiation. Given that our cohort consists mainly of Val30Met patients, it remains to be determined whether NfL similarly reflects disease severity across other TTR variants.

Despite the lower plasma NfL levels observed in patients receiving Tafamidis, these findings should be interpreted with caution. In this cohort, Tafamidis-treated individuals were generally at earlier disease stages and exhibited lower clinical severity compared with symptomatic untreated patients, which may confound the observed differences in NfL concentrations. Given the cross-sectional design of the study, it is not possible to determine whether Tafamidis independently contributes to a reduction in NfL levels or whether these values primarily reflect baseline disease severity and earlier therapeutic intervention. Therefore, while our results support plasma NfL as a sensitive biomarker of neurological involvement and disease stage in ATTRv, longitudinal studies with pre-treatment baseline measurements and stage-matched patient groups are required to clarify the specific impact of Tafamidis on NfL dynamics over time.

The correlation between NfL levels and NIS scores further underscores the biomarker’s value in monitoring disease evolution (Fig. 2). The ability of NfL to discriminate between stages of disease progression enhances its clinical relevance—not only as a diagnostic tool but also as a measure of therapeutic response and longitudinal disease monitoring.

Importantly, Z-score analysis using a normative reference population enabled us to define ranges for symptomatic and asymptomatic patients, including those undergoing treatment. This may support the development of reliable cutoff values to assist in staging disease severity and evaluating clinical response to treatment.

Previous studies conducted in large, multicenter cohorts involving participants from North America, Europe, and Asia have established NfL as a robust biomarker of neuroaxonal damage in hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis. In asymptomatic TTR variant carriers, serum or plasma NfL levels are generally low and comparable to age-adjusted reference values; however, longitudinal analyses have shown that NfL may gradually increase months to years before the clinical onset of polyneuropathy, particularly in individuals with early tissue amyloid deposition, indicating subclinical neuronal injury21,24,25. In symptomatic patients, NfL levels are markedly elevated and correlate with neuropathy severity, functional impairment, and disability scores, supporting its value as a marker of disease burden and progression30,31. Importantly, recent real-world and longitudinal studies have further demonstrated that NfL levels decrease or stabilize in patients receiving disease-modifying therapies, reinforcing its potential utility for disease staging, early detection, and therapeutic monitoring in clinical practice30,31. Overall, the mean NfL values reported in asymptomatic and symptomatic individuals in these studies closely match those observed in our cohort, indicating that, despite its small size, our sample is representative of patients with Val30Met-related familial amyloid polyneuropathy (FAP).

This study is exploratory in nature and aims to contribute to the validation of pNfL as a biomarker in Brazilian patients with ATTRv, serving as a basis for future investigations with larger cohorts and longitudinal follow-up. Further studies in larger and more diverse patient cohorts, including different TTR mutations and longitudinal assessments, are warranted to validate and refine the clinical applicability of NfL in ATTRv management.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that plasma neurofilament light chain (pNfL) may serve as a useful biomarker for assessing disease severity and progression in Brazilian patients with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTRv). Differences in pNfL levels were observed between symptomatic and asymptomatic individuals, as well as between treated and untreated patients, indicating a potential role in early detection and therapeutic monitoring. The association between higher NfL concentrations and greater neuropathy severity points to its possible involvement in tracking disease evolution. However, studies with larger cohorts and including a wider spectrum of TTR mutations are required to confirm these findings and to better define the clinical applicability of NfL in ATTRv management.

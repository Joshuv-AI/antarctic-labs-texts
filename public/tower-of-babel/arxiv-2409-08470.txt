Bounding smooth Levi-flat hypersurfaces in a Stein manifold
Hanlong Fang, Xiaojun Huang, Wanke Yin, Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the problem of constructing a smooth Levi-flat hypersurface locally or globally attached to a real codimension two submanifold in ℂ n + 1 \mathbb{C}^{n+1} , or more generally in a Stein manifold, with elliptic CR singularities, a research direction originated from a fundamental and classical paper of E. Bishop. Earlier works along these lines include those by many prominent mathematicians working both on complex analysis and geometry. We prove that a compact smooth (or, real analytic) real codimension two submanifold M M , that is contained in the boundary of a smoothly bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain, with a natural and necessary condition called CR non-minimal condition at CR points and with two elliptic CR singular points bounds a smooth-up-to-boundary (real analytic-up-to-boundary, respectively) Levi-flat hypersurface M ^ \widehat{M} . This answers a well-known question left open from the work of Dolbeault-Tomassini-Zaitsev, or a generalized version of a problem already asked by Bishop in 1965. Our study here reveals an intricate interaction of several complex analysis with other fields such as symplectic geometry and foliation theory. Key words : Elliptic CR singularities, CR non-minimality, Singular foliations, Levi flat hypersurfaces, CR extensions, Stein retractions, Liouville fillings, Milnor links and fibrations, Morse theory, almost holomorphic extensions, hull of holomorphy.

In an important development, Dolbeault-Tomassini-Zaitsev in 2005

While more explanation of terminologies will be given in §2, roughly speaking, a CR singular point

is called elliptic if after a certain biholomorphic change of coordinates, the projection of

to the real hyperplane approximating

The global Bishop problem of bounding a Levi-flat hypersurface

Remark 1.2

The number of elliptic CR singular points cannot exceed two if all CR singular points of

are elliptic. Otherwise, as in

The following example serves as the model case in our main theorem:

Example 1.3

Let
M=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}:u={\rm Re}(w),v={\rm Im}(w),\ |z|^{2}+u^{2}=1,\ v=0\}

are two elliptic CR singular points of

which has

\widehat{M}\setminus M
is foliated by a family of complex submanifolds

Our main theorem can be regarded as a solution to a complex Plateau problem. Indeed, when
\hbox{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}M>1
is odd, the Plateau problem would be asking if
M
bounds a
complex submanifold or a complex analytic variety. A necessary condition for an affirmative solution is that
M
has to be a CR manifold of hypersurface type as the boundary of a complex submanifold carries a naturally inherited CR structure. The solution was indeed the main content of a celebrated paper of Harvey-Lawson
[
29
]
and its later development by Dinh in
[
9
]
.
The smoothness of
\widehat{M}
even near
M
requires a certain global
convexity of
M
such as
M
being contained in the boundary of a compact strongly pseudoconvex hypersurface. When
\hbox{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}M
is even,

\widehat{M}
is of real odd dimension and what we can at most expect is for
M
to bound a smooth submanifold foliated by maximally complex submanifolds. Theorem
1.1
serves a solution along these lines.

As mentioned before, the non-minimality at CR points is necessary for the existence of
\widehat{M}
, just as the CR condition is needed for an odd dimension manifold to be the regular boundary of a complex manifold. The existence of two elliptic points on
M
,
whose definition will be explained later, is also crucial for us to do the local construction of
\widehat{M}
in the present work. Its existence on a manifold is often derived as a consequence of index theorem. The idea of using elliptic complex tangency to construct small leaves and then push them globally to solve the complex Plateau problem dates back to the original paper of Bishop
[
4
]
and then was developed further in the work of Bedford-Gaveau
[
2
]
.

The problem of finding a Levi-flat hypersurface with a prescribed boundary is also formulated and studied extensively from the perspective of degenerate PDEs when
M
is in the graph form. Here we mention
a paper by Slodkowski and Tomassini

54

Let
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact real hypersurface bounding a domain
D_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
such that
M_{0}\times i{\mathbb{R}}
is strongly pseudoconvex in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
. Let

be a smooth real-valued function on
M_{0}
.
Write
M
for the graph of
\phi_{0}
, i.e.,
M:=\{(z,z_{n+1}=u+iv):(z,u)\in M_{0},\ v=\phi_{0}\}
. Let
\psi\in C^{2}(\overline{D_{0}})
be a real-valued function in
(z,z_{n+1})
.
The Levi-operator applied to
\psi

\mathcal{L}(\psi)=\hbox{det}\left(\begin{matrix}0&\psi_{z_{j}}\\
\psi_{\overline{z_{k}}}&\psi_{z_{j}\overline{z_{k}}}\end{matrix}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}.

There is a weak version of Levi convexity for any continuous function
\psi=-v+\phi(z,\overline{z},u)
. When
\phi
is
C^{2}
-smooth, this weak convexity is equivalent to the non-negativity of eigenvalues of the Levi form of the hypersurface defined by

[

for a detailed definition).
The Levi Monge-Ampére equation is to solve the following boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(-v+\phi)=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm in\,\,}D_{0}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \phi=\phi_{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm on\,\,}M_{0}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ -v+\phi\,\,\,\ \ {\rm is\,\,Levi\,\,convex}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

Now finding a Levi-flat hypersurface with boundary
M
is equivalent to solving the Levi equation (
1
) in the classical sense. While the weak solution of (
1

[
54
]
, among other things, a least requirement for obtaining a
C^{2}
-smooth solution is that CR points of

are non-minimal as we mentioned before. Hence, a
C^{2}
-smooth solution does not exist in general. Our theorem then provides a natural geometric setting where the unique solution

is indeed smooth. Note that even in this setting, our result is stronger than what is proved in
[
12

as they need

to be strongly convex.

2.1
Formal flattening of an elliptic CR singularity

Assume that
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a
C^{N_{0}}
-smooth submanifold of real codimension two and

is a CR singular point. Write
(z,w)=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},w)
for the coordinates of

{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
with

N_{0}=\infty
or

(namely,
M
is real analytic). After a holomorphic change of coordinates, we
assume that
p=0,\ T^{(1,0)}_{p}M=\{w=0\}
. Then
M
near
p=0
is the
graph of a function of the form:

w=Q(z,\overline{z})+O(|z|^{3}),

(2)

where
Q(z,\overline{z})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree two in

(z,\overline{z})
and
|z|:=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}}
.

After a simple holomorphic change of

4
Closeness: Smoothness of a complex analytic variety with a standard contact sphere as its boundary

The result in the previous section serves as the stability part in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We provide in this section another key tool when we deform the constructed Stein submanifolds away from elliptic CR singular points in our proof of Theorem
1.1
. Our argument here is mainly along the lines of symplectic geometry. The main purpose is to prove Theorem
1.7
, which may find applications in other related problems.

Recall that a co-oriented contact manifold consists of an odd-dimensional manifold
Y^{2n-1}
and a codimension one subbundle
\xi\subset TY
such that there exists a one-form
\alpha
satisfying
\xi=\ker\alpha
,
\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^{n-1}\neq 0
, and
\alpha
gives the co-orientation on
TY/\xi
.
In particular,
\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^{n-1}
gives an orientation of
Y
. A (co-oriented) contactomorphism is a diffeomorphism preserving the contact structure as well as the co-orientation.

It is clear that the boundary of a strongly pseudoconvex domain has a contact structure that is naturally induced from its CR structure. More precisely, let
\rho
be a smooth strongly plurisubharmonic function on a complex manifold
(W,J)
. Then any regular level set of
\rho
is equipped with the contact structure
\xi:=T\rho^{-1}(R)\cap JT\rho^{-1}(R)=\ker(-d^{c}\rho|_{\rho^{-1}(R)})
, where
d^{c}\rho(X):=d\rho(JX)
for
X\in TW
. In particular, if
W=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\rho=|z|^{2}
, we get a family of contact manifolds each of which is contactomorphic to the standard contact sphere
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{std})
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given two smooth contact manifolds
(Y_{-},\xi_{-})
and
(Y_{+},\xi_{+})
, a Liouville cobordism
(W,\lambda)
from
(Y_{-},\xi_{-})
to
(Y_{+},\xi_{+})
consists of an oriented smooth manifold
W
with boundary and a Liouville form
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(W)
such that the following holds.

(i)

d\lambda
is a symplectic form on
W
;

(ii)

\partial W=(-Y_{-})\sqcup Y_{+}
as oriented manifolds;

(iii)

the Liouville vector field
X
, defined by
\iota_{X}d\lambda=\lambda
, points outward along
Y_{+}
and points inward along
Y_{-}
;

(iv)

\ker(\lambda|_{Y_{\pm}})=\xi_{\pm}
.

A Liouville cobordism from
\emptyset
to
Y
is called a Liouville filling of
Y
.

In view of Theorems

Conjecture 4.9
.

Let
W
be a Stein space of dimension
n\geq 3
with at most finitely many isolated normal singularities. Assume further that
W
has a compact strongly pseudoconvex smooth boundary
\partial W
, of which the CR structure is contactomorphic to
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{std})
. Then
W
is a smooth Stein manifold and is diffeomorphic to the complex unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
.

Characterizing the smoothness of isolated singularities from a local perspective has a long history. A fundamental theorem in this direction due to Mumford
[
53
]
states that a non-smooth normal surface singularity can not have a simply connected link. In higher dimensions, the presence of a sphere link alone is insufficient to rule out isolated singularities as demonstrated by certain Brieskorn singularities.
Motivated by these considerations, Seidel conjectured that an isolated normal singularity is smooth if and only if its contact link is the standard contact sphere. In
[
49
]
, McLean discovered the deep relation between the Reeb dynamics on the contact link and the minimal discrepancy of the singularity, an invariant of singularities coming up in the minimal model program. In particular, he proved Seidel’s conjecture conditionally to a conjecture of Shokurov on minimal log discrepancies, which has been answered affirmatively in
dimension
3
and for locally complete intersection singularities.

Conjecture
4.9
is a global version of Seidel’s conjecture. McLean’s relation between the Reeb dynamics on the contact boundary and the minimal discrepancy of the singularities does not hold
for general Stein spaces other than a small neighborhood of one singularity, due to possible non-trivial symplectic topology outside germs of singularities. In particular, it is not even clear if Shokurov’s conjecture would imply Conjecture
4.9
. When
n=2
, Conjecture
4.9
is derived readily from the works of Gromov
[
27
]
, Eliashberg
[
13
]
and McDuff
[
47
]
as any symplectic filling of
(S^{3},\xi_{std})
must be blow-ups of the symplectic ball, hence the resolution of singularities in
W
as a symplectic filling of
(S^{3},\xi_{std})
must be blow-ups of a ball. In fact, in this scenario it suffices to assume that
\partial W
is diffeomorphic to
S^{3}
by
[
13
, Corollary 5.3]
. Conjecture
4.9
has also been answered affirmatively in
[
19
, Theorem C]
when the singularities are assumed to be quotient singularities. Conjecture
4.9
would be a powerful tool in tackling many problems in several complex variables, if confirmed affirmatively.

M\setminus\{D(M_{1})\cup D(M_{2})\}\cup\{p_{1},p_{2}\}=\cup_{1\leq t\leq 2}{M_{t}}
. By Theorem
1.6
, we can define a
C^{3}
-smooth function
\widehat{u}
on
\widehat{M}\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2}\}=\cup_{0<t<3}\widehat{M_{t}}
such that
\widehat{u}
has constant value on each
\widehat{M_{t}}
and
d\widehat{u}|_{\widehat{M}\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2}\}}\not=0
.

Let
A_{0}:=\cup_{1-\epsilon_{1}<t<2+\epsilon_{1}}\widehat{M_{t}}
where
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll 1
. From the proof of Theorem
1.6
, any
p\in A_{0}\setminus M
is covered by a local coordinate chart
(V_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
such that
\phi_{\alpha}
is at least
C^{3}
-smooth and

\phi_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})=\{(t,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}):|t|<\epsilon,\,\,|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<\epsilon\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}

with
0<\epsilon\ll 1
(see (
34
)). Here points on the same leaf have the same
t
-value, and
\phi_{\alpha}^{-1}
is holomorphic for fixed
t
. By the Whitney extension theorem, we have a
C^{3}
-smooth extension
\Phi_{\alpha}
of
\phi_{\alpha}^{-1}
to an open neighborhood

B_{\alpha,\delta}:=\{(z_{0}=t+iT,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}):|t|<\epsilon,|T|<\delta,|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

with
0<\delta\ll\epsilon
such that

\overline{\partial}\Phi_{\beta}=o(T^{2})\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,T\rightarrow 0.

Write
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}:=\Phi_{\alpha}(B_{\alpha,\delta})
.
Similarly, any
p\in M\cap A_{0}
is covered by a local coordinate chart
(V_{\beta},\phi_{\beta})
such that
\phi_{\beta}
is at least
C^{3}
-smooth and

\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta})=\{(t,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}):r(t,z)\leq 0,\,\,|t|<\epsilon,|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}

with
r
given by (
32
) and
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. By the Whitney extension theorem, we have a
C^{3}
-smooth extension
\Phi_{\beta}
of
\phi_{\beta}^{-1}
to an open neighborhood

B_{\beta,\delta}:=\{(z_{0}=t+iT,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}):r(t,z)<\delta,\,\,|t|<\epsilon,|T|<\delta,|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

with
0<\delta\ll\epsilon
such that

\overline{\partial}\Phi_{\beta}(z)=o(T^{2})+o(({\rm dist}(z,\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta})))^{2})\,\,\,{\rm as\,\,}{\rm dist}(z,\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta}))\rightarrow 0,

(42)

where
{\rm dist}(z,\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta}))
is the distance from
z
to
\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta})
with respect to the Euclidean metric. Write
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}:=\Phi_{\beta}(B_{\beta,\delta})
.

Notice that
(\phi^{-1}_{\beta})^{*}(\widehat{u})=u_{\beta}(t)
where
u_{\beta}(t)
is
C^{3}
-smooth and
\frac{du_{\beta}(t)}{dt}\neq 0
. By the Whitney extension theorem, there is
C^{3}
-smooth function
\xi
on
B_{\beta,\delta}
such that
\xi|_{\phi_{\beta}(V_{\beta})}=u_{\beta}
and that
\overline{\partial}\xi
vanishes to order

2
on
B_{\beta,\delta}\cap\{T=0\}
. Since
i\partial\overline{\partial}
is a real operator,
\partial\overline{\partial}(\hbox{Im}\,\xi)
vanishes to order
1
on
B_{\beta,\delta}\cap\{T=0\}
.
Define
\widehat{v}_{\beta}=(\hbox{Im}\,\xi)\circ\Phi_{\beta}
. Then
d\widehat{v}_{\beta}|_{B_{\beta,\delta}\cap\{T=0\}}\neq 0
for
\frac{du_{\beta}(t)}{dt}\neq 0
.
Writing
(\widetilde{z}_{\widetilde{0}},\cdots,\widetilde{z}_{\widetilde{n}})
for the coordinates of the target, computation yields that

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{v}_{\beta}}{\partial z_{j}\overline{\partial}z_{k}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{n}(\frac{\partial\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\overline{\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}})=\sum_{\delta,\gamma=0}^{n}(\frac{\partial^{2}\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\widetilde{z}_{\delta}\partial\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\delta}}}{\partial{z_{j}}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\overline{\widetilde{z}_{\delta}}\partial\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\delta}}}}{\partial{z_{j}}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\widetilde{z}_{\delta}\partial\overline{\widetilde{z_{\gamma}}}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\delta}}}{\partial{z_{j}}}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\overline{\widetilde{z}_{\delta}}\partial\overline{\widetilde{z_{\gamma}}}}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\delta}}}}{\partial{z_{j}}}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}})+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{n}(\frac{\partial\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial\hbox{Im}\,\xi}{\partial\overline{\widetilde{z}_{\gamma}}}\frac{\partial^{2}\overline{\Phi_{\beta}^{\widetilde{\gamma}}}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}).\end{split}

By (
42
),
\widehat{v}_{\beta}
satisfies properties (i), (ii) locally. In the same way, we can derive the desired defining function
\widehat{v}_{\alpha}
locally in a small open neighborhood of
V_{\alpha}
, by finding approximate harmonic conjugate of
\widehat{u}
(similar to that in
[
18
, Propositions 1.2, 3.1]
). Moreover, on the overlaps in
\widehat{M}
, local defining functions agree at least to order
3
up to the boundary
M
.

Next we consider the points near CR singularities.
When
M
is real analytic, it follows by the result of Huang-Yin in
[
38
]
that, near the elliptic CR singular point
p_{1}
or
p_{2}
,
\widehat{M}
can be biholomorphically transformed to a domain defined by
\widehat{v}:=\hbox{Im}\,w=0
,
\hbox{Re}\,w\geq G(z,\overline{z})
, and that closed leaves of
\widehat{M}
are cut out by
\hbox{Re}\,w=
constant, where
(z,w)
are coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. Then for
i=1,2
, there are open neighborhoods
\mathcal{U}_{i}
of
\overline{D(M_{i})}
in
\mathcal{M}
, and
C^{3}
-smooth functions
\widehat{v}_{i}
on
\mathcal{U}_{i}
such that properties (i), (ii) hold. We arrange the defining function of the foliation
\widehat{u}
on
A_{0}=\cup_{1-\epsilon_{1}<t<2+\epsilon_{1}}\widehat{M_{t}}
so that it coincides with the function
\hbox{Re}\,w
on
\widehat{D(M_{i})}\cap A_{0}
.

Now assume that
M
is
C^{N_{0}}
-smooth, where
N_{0}\geq 10
. Let
p_{1},p_{2}
be two elliptic CR singular points.
By Proposition
2.3
and the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we have a
C^{3}
-smooth diffeomorphism
F
from
\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}}:=\{(t,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}:z\in\overline{\Omega_{\sqrt{t}}},\ 0<t<{\epsilon}\}\cup\{0\}
to an open neighborhood of
p_{1}
(or
p_{2}
) in
\widehat{M}
such that along each leaf
\Omega_{\sqrt{t}}
,
F
is holomoprhic. Here
\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}}
is itself a
C^{3}
-smooth domain with boundary in
{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By the Whitney extension theorem, we derive a
C^{3}
-smooth extension
\widehat{F}
of
F
to an open neighborhood

B_{\delta}:=\{(z_{0}=t+iT,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}):|t|<\epsilon,|T|<\delta,|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

with
0<\delta\ll\epsilon
such that

\overline{\partial}\widehat{F}(z)=o(T^{2})+o(({\rm dist}(z,\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}}))^{2})\,\,\,{\rm as\,\,}{\rm dist}(z,\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}})\rightarrow 0,

where
{\rm dist}(z,\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}})
is the distance from
z
to
\widehat{M^{a}_{\epsilon}}
with respect to the Euclidean metric. Define
\widehat{v}=T\circ(\widehat{F})^{-1}
. By a similar computation as in
[
33
]
,
\widehat{v}
satisfies properties (i), (ii) locally. Then for
i=1,2
, there are open neighborhoods
\mathcal{U}_{i}
of
\overline{D(M_{i})}
in
\mathcal{M}
, and
C^{3}
-smooth functions
\widehat{v}_{i}
on
\mathcal{U}_{i}
such that properties (i), (ii) hold. Similarly, we arrange the defining function of the foliation
\widehat{u}
on
A_{0}
so that it coincides with the function
t\circ(\widehat{F})^{-1}
on
\widehat{D(M_{i})}\cap A_{0}
.

Take a
C^{3}
-smooth partition of unity
\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{\alpha},\cdots,\chi_{\beta},\cdots\}
subordinate to a finite cover
\{\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{\alpha},

\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\beta},\cdots\}
. We now define

\widehat{v}:=\chi_{1}\widehat{v}_{1}+\chi_{2}\widehat{v}_{2}+\sum\nolimits_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}\widehat{v}_{\alpha}+\sum\nolimits_{\beta}\chi_{\beta}\widehat{v}_{\beta}=:\sum\nolimits_{\gamma}\chi_{\gamma}\widehat{v}_{\gamma}.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a small neighborhood of
\widehat{M}
. On the support of each
\chi_{\gamma_{0}}
, computation yields that

d\widehat{v}=\sum\nolimits_{\gamma}(d\chi_{\gamma}\widehat{v}_{\gamma}+d\widehat{v}_{\gamma}\chi_{\gamma})=\sum\nolimits_{\gamma}(d\chi_{\gamma}(\widehat{v}_{\gamma}-\widehat{v}_{\gamma_{0}})+d\widehat{v}_{\gamma}\chi_{\gamma}),

which equals to
d\widehat{v}_{\gamma_{0}}\neq 0
when restricted to
\widehat{M}\cap{\rm Supp}\,\chi_{\gamma_{0}}
. Similarly, on the support of each
\chi_{\gamma_{0}}
, computation yields that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{v}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}=&\sum\nolimits_{\gamma}(\frac{\partial^{2}\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}\widehat{v}_{\gamma}+\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{v}_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}\chi_{\gamma}+\frac{\partial\widehat{v}_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial\widehat{v}_{\gamma}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}})\\
=&\sum\nolimits_{\gamma}(\frac{\partial^{2}\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}(\widehat{v}_{\gamma}-\widehat{v}_{\gamma_{0}})+\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{v}_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}\chi_{\gamma}+\frac{\partial(\widehat{v}_{\gamma}-\widehat{v}_{\gamma_{0}})}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}+\frac{\partial\chi_{\gamma}}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial(\widehat{v}_{\gamma}-\widehat{v}_{\gamma_{0}})}{\partial\overline{z_{k}}}).\end{split}

We therefore see the proof of Lemma
5.1
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The proof is the same as that in
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Choose
\mathcal{U}
to be sufficiently close to
\widehat{M}
such that
d\widehat{v}\not=0
in
\mathcal{U}
, where
\widehat{v}
is the defining function constructed in Lemma
5.1
. Let
\widetilde{M}
be the zero set of
\widehat{v}
in
\mathcal{U}
. Choose a small
c>0
such that

\{p\in\widetilde{M}\colon\rho(p)\leq c\}\subset\joinrel\subset\mathcal{U}

and each value in
[0,c]
is a regular value of
\rho|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}
.
For sufficiently
small
\epsilon>0
, we set
\widehat{v}_{\epsilon}^{\pm}(p)=\pm\widehat{v}(p)+\epsilon(\rho(p)-c)
where
p\in\mathcal{U}
, and define

\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\{p\in\mathcal{U}:\widehat{v}^{+}_{\epsilon}(p)<0,\ \widehat{v}^{-}_{\epsilon}(p)<0,\ \rho(p)<\epsilon\}.

It is clear that
\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}
consists of three
C^{2}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex hypersurfaces (towards
\Omega_{\epsilon}
):
\Gamma_{\epsilon}^{\pm}=\{p\in\mathcal{U}:\widehat{v}_{\epsilon}^{\pm}(p)=0,\ \rho(p)<\epsilon\}
,
\{p\in\mathcal{U}:\rho(p)=\epsilon,\ \widehat{v}^{+}_{\epsilon}(p)<0,\ \widehat{v}^{-}_{\epsilon}(p)<0\}
, and that
\Omega_{\epsilon}

shrinks down to
\widehat{M}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. By Lemma
5.1
, we conclude that
\widehat{v}_{\epsilon}^{\pm}
are strongly plurisubharmonic
on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
provided that
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. Hence,
\Omega_{\epsilon}
are Stein domains for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.8
and thus the proof of Theorem
1.1
are complete. ∎

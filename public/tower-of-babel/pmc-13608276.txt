Building a Rural, Coalition-Based Approach to Youth Substance Use Prevention: The Drug Free Dickson Coalition Model
Authors: Karrie R. Thompson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTYouth substance use remains a critical public health issue in the United States, with rural communities experiencing disproportionate risk because of limited access to prevention resources and persistent barriers related to behavioral and mental health. Rural youth are often exposed to elevated risk factors, including geographic isolation, reduced availability of extracurricular and prosocial activities, transportation limitations, and fewer specialized prevention and treatment services. These challenges underscore the need for comprehensive, community-driven prevention approaches that are responsive to local context while grounded in evidence-based practice. This report describes the prevention framework, implementation strategies, and community context of the Drug Free Dickson Coalition (DFDC), a rural, coalition-based initiative operating in Dickson County, Tennessee. The coalition's approach highlights the importance of a long-term investment in prevention infrastructure, workforce development, and cross-sector collaboration to successfully prevent youth substance use.

Introduction

The Drug-Free Communities (DFC) Support Program is the nation's leading effort to mobilize communities to prevent youth substance use. The Drug Free Dickson Coalition (DFDC) received DFC funding in September 2021, strengthening its capacity to implement comprehensive, evidence-based prevention strategies in a rural setting. Since receiving funding, DFDC has expanded school-based programming, increased youth engagement through leadership and mentorship opportunities, and enhanced community-wide education efforts addressing alcohol, vaping, and prescription drug misuse. Through strong multisector partnerships, the coalition aligns local resources to reduce risk factors and strengthen protective factors among youth. DFDC's approach demonstrates the value of sustained investment in community coalitions as a public health strategy, particularly in rural areas where access to prevention services may be limited. This model highlights important implications for public health practice, including the effectiveness of coordinated, community-driven prevention efforts in reducing youth substance use and promoting long-term community well-being.1

Coalition History

Dickson County is a largely rural area in Tennessee, located approximately 30 miles west of Nashville, with a population of approximately 58,000 residents distributed across 6 municipalities.2 The county's rural composition presents unique challenges for youth prevention efforts, including the absence of public transportation, disparities in access to health and mental health services, and cultural norms that may inhibit open discussion of substance use and addiction. DFDC was established in 2012 to coordinate a countywide, multisector substance use prevention coalition with a primary focus on youth. The coalition received DFC funding in 2021 to address alcohol, tobacco, and vaping among youth and to address prescription drug misuse across the lifespan through coordinated awareness, education, and action.

The coalition's structure reflects a strong emphasis on cross-sector collaboration. DFDC includes representation from multiple community sectors, including education, law enforcement, health care, parents, youth-serving organizations, faith-based institutions, and local government. This multisector composition facilitates coordination across systems, reduces duplication of effort, and enhances the coalition's capacity to address complex and interrelated determinants of youth substance use. Starting with 2 full-time employees and 30 volunteers, DFDC is now supported by a professional staff of 7, including certified prevention specialists, and guided by 5 board members, 12 sector representatives, and more than 100 active volunteers. This organizational infrastructure enables the coalition to maintain program fidelity, engage in continuous quality improvement, and respond adaptively to emerging community needs.

DFDC's prevention approach focuses on identifying and reducing risk factors such as social isolation, adverse childhood experiences, and permissive norms around underage substance use while strengthening protective factors, including positive adult relationships, family and school connectedness, and access to supportive services.3 This work is guided by the socioecological model, which recognizes that behavior is shaped by multiple, interacting levels of influence, including individual, relationship, community, and societal factors.4 By addressing these levels simultaneously through partnerships, school-based and community-wide strategies, and youth engagement, Drug Free Dickson promotes sustainable prevention outcomes that support healthy decision making across the community.

Leveraging Partnerships

DFDC collaborates with more than 20 community partners, including law enforcement, local government, public health, school systems, the University of Tennessee Extension, and nonprofit organizations that expand the reach and impact of substance use prevention strategies. For example, a strong partnership with Dickson County Schools allows DFDC to integrate prevention programming into health education curricula aligned with required standards, providing classroom access without disrupting instructional time. This infrastructure supports sustained implementation quality and adaptive response to evolving community needs.

Integrating School-Based and Community-Wide Strategies

DFDC integrates school-based programming with community wide awareness initiatives designed to address risk and protective factors at multiple levels. A central component of the coalition's work is the implementation of the Botvin LifeSkills curriculum across all schools in grades K-12 within the county.5 Through formal partnerships with the local school district and individual private schools, DFDC ensures that prevention programming is delivered consistently and with fidelity to over 1,200 middle school students and 800 high school students. School-based prevention efforts are complemented by community-level strategies intended to reinforce protective norms and increase adult awareness and involvement. DFDC conducts education and awareness campaigns targeting parents, caregivers, community members, and local government leaders, with messaging focused on substance use risks, protective factors, and strategies for supporting healthy youth development. This includes implementing a 12-month youth mentorship program that pairs young people with trained community volunteers, partnering with the Juvenile Court to provide education on the dangers of vaping, THC use, and emerging substances for both youth and parents, and collaborating with the Tennessee Suicide Prevention Network to conduct Question, Persuade, Refer trainings. In addition, the coalition trains retailers on responsible beverage service and leads countywide compliance checks related to the sale of alcohol and tobacco to underage youth. DFDC has also implemented a comprehensive, prevention-focused approach to reduce youth prescription drug misuse and raise awareness about the dangers of fentanyl and counterfeit pills. Since launching the Count It, Lock It, Drop It initiative in 2015, DFDC has promoted safe medication storage and disposal through community education, medication lock box distribution, and the installation of 4 permanent prescription drug drop boxes throughout the county. The coalition also partners with regional overdose prevention specialists to provide naloxone education and training for first responders, school nurses, coaches, and school administrators. In response to the growing threat of illicit fentanyl, DFDC conducts youth and parent education campaigns that highlight the risks associated with counterfeit pills, emphasizing that a single pill obtained outside of a pharmacy may contain a lethal dose of fentanyl. By aligning school instruction with broader community messaging and activities, the coalition seeks to create a coordinated prevention environment in which youth receive consistent signals regarding expectations, support, and healthy behavior.

Engaging Youth

Youth engagement is a defining feature of DFDC's prevention strategy. Consistent with research highlighting the importance of youth participation in prevention and health promotion efforts, DFDC incorporates youth participation in leadership roles, program planning, and peer-led activities.6 Youth participants contribute to the development and dissemination of prevention messages, assist with awareness campaigns, and provide feedback on program relevance and effectiveness. These opportunities for meaningful involvement are designed to enhance protective factors such as connectedness, self-efficacy, and leadership skills, while also increasing the credibility and resonance of prevention messaging among youth audiences. Youth engagement is not treated as a supplementary activity but as an integral component of the coalition's prevention framework.

Challenges and Lessons Learned

DFDC encounters challenges that are characteristic of rural prevention initiatives. Transportation barriers limit youth participation in after-school and community-based programming, particularly for youth in geographically dispersed areas. Community or environmental factors surrounding substance use and mental health persist as a barrier to engagement, disclosure, and help-seeking, affecting both youth and adults. In addition, reaching underserved populations requires intentional outreach strategies and sustained relationship building to overcome mistrust and access limitations. DFDC addresses these challenges through flexible program delivery, utilization of trusted community partners, and incorporation of environmental and social barrier reduction strategies within prevention messaging and training efforts.

Another challenge is long-term sustainability. Competition for local grants, donations, and private funding is high, and reliance on short-term grant funding presents ongoing risk. DFDC continues to explore long-term funding solutions, including potential local funding mechanisms, while recognizing that many county-level nonprofits face similar challenges. One important sustainability strategy has been the use of county opioid abatement funding. This support has allowed DFDC to continue programming and retain staff during periods of grant expiration or funding reductions, maintaining continuity of services for the community.

Conclusion

DFDC has served Dickson County, Tennessee, since 2012 and has been supported by the DFC program since 2021. DFC funding has strengthened DFDC's capacity for strategic planning and implementation of prevention efforts aimed at supporting substance-free youth. Leadership continuity sustains the coalition, unites multiple community sectors, and delivers consistent prevention programming throughout the year. Through the integration of evidence-based school programming, youth engagement, and multisector collaboration, DFDC addresses both individual and environmental determinants of substance use. The coalition's work underscores the potential for rural communities to achieve sustainable prevention outcomes.

Looking ahead, DFDC plans to expand and strengthen its prevention strategies by building on existing partnerships, increasing youth engagement, and continuing to adapt programming to emerging substance use trends. The coalition intends to pursue years 6–10 of DFC funding to further enhance sustainability and community reach.

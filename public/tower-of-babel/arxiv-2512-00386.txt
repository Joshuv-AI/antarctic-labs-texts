Convergence of Reflected Langevin Diffusion for Constrained Sampling
Tarika Mane

Abstract
. We examine the Langevin diffusion confined to a closed, convex domain D ⊂ ℝ d D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , represented as a reflected stochastic differential equation. We introduce a sequence of penalized stochastic differential equations and prove that their invariant measures converge, in Wasserstein–2 distance and with explicit polynomial rate, to the invariant measure of the reflected Langevin diffusion. We also analyze a time-discretization of the penalized process obtained via the Euler–Maruyama scheme and demonstrate the convergence to the original constrained measure. These results provide a rigorous approximation framework for reflected Langevin dynamics in both continuous and discrete time.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider the overdamped Langevin stochastic differential equation given by

dX_{t}=-\nabla f(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},

(1.1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential energy function,
\sigma>0
is a diffusion coefficient, and
W_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Under suitable assumptions on
f
, the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
admits a unique invariant Gibbs measure where the density is proportional to
e^{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}f(x)}
.

The Langevin Monte Carlo algorithm is a classical method for sampling from a target probability measure
\pi
.
If we set

f(x)\coloneqq-\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\log\pi(x),

then the Langevin equation

dX_{t}=\nabla\log\pi(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}

admits
\pi
as its unique invariant measure.
Discretizing the SDE (
1.1
) with the Euler–Maruyama scheme and step size
h>0
yields

X_{k+1}=X_{k}-h\nabla f(X_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{h}\,\xi_{k},\qquad\xi_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),

which is known as the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (ULA).

If the target measure
\pi
is supported on a closed, convex domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the Langevin diffusion process may evolve outside of
D
, leading to recent work on constrained Langevin Monte Carlo methods. Existing approaches enforce the constraint through a correction mechanism, such as projection onto
D
, Metropolis–Hastings accept–reject steps, or the use of mirror maps
[
5
,
14
,
11
]
.
In this paper, we instead consider the constrained Langevin diffusion defined by a reflected stochastic differential equation, a framework studied in
[
19
,
18
]
for reflected Itô diffusion processes and more classically in
[
20
]
. A key advantage of the reflected Langevin equation is that the constraint is enforced intrinsically through a zero–flux boundary condition on
\partial D
. Consequently, the reflected process preserves the Gibbs measure on
D
as its unique invariant measure and avoids the discretization bias introduced by projection-based schemes and eliminates the need for Metropolis corrections.

To analyze the reflected Langevin diffusion, we approximate it by a family of
penalized SDEs indexed by a penalty parameter
n
.
Each penalized process evolves in the full space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and admits an invariant measure that assigns increasing weight to the domain
D

as
n\to\infty
. We establish that the invariant measures of the penalized SDEs converge, in Wasserstein distance, to the invariant measure of the reflected Langevin diffusion. We then apply the Euler–Maruyama scheme and get the
Penalized Constrained Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (PCULA)
:

X_{k+1}^{(n,h)}=X_{k}^{(n,h)}-h\nabla f_{n}\left(X_{k}^{(n,h)}\right)+\sigma\sqrt{h}\,\xi_{k},\quad\xi_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})

for a fixed step size
h>0
and fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}.

This algorithm thus yields an implementable sampling scheme that approximates
the invariant measure of the constrained Langevin diffusion.

2.
Reflected Langevin Diffusion via Penalization

When considering stochastic dynamics constrained to a closed convex domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the current Langevin diffusion process (
1.1
) will evolve freely and can exit
D
. To deal with this issue, we reflect at the boundary to enforce
X_{t}\in D
for all
t\geq 0
, by introducing an additional term of bounded variation that acts only when the trajectory reaches
\partial D
. Inside the domain, the evolution follows the usual Langevin diffusion and this construction yields the reflected Langevin stochastic differential equation

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s})ds+K_{t}.

(2.1)

Here, we start from
X_{0}\in D
,
X_{t}
is constrained to
D
,
K_{t}
is a bounded variation process with variation
||K||
increasing only when
X_{t}\in\partial D
,
W_{t}
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion,
\sigma>0
is a diffusion coefficient, and
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the potential energy function.

Direct analysis of the reflected SDE (
2.1
) is challenging
due to the presence of the boundary reflection term
K_{t}
.
A standard approach is to replace the hard constraint
X_{t}\in D
with a
soft constraint obtained through penalization. In this formulation, the reflection is approximated by a restoring force that
acts whenever the process moves outside the domain, with strength controlled by
a penalty parameter. As the penalty strength grows, the trajectories remain increasingly close to
D
and the goal is to converge on the invariant measure of the reflected Langevin SDE. Formally, for each
n\in\mathbb{N},
we define the penalty term as

K_{t}^{n}=-n\int_{0}^{t}\left(X_{s}^{n}-\Pi(X_{s}^{n})\right)\,ds

(2.2)

where
\Pi(x)
is the metric projection of
x
to
D
. For a closed convex set
D
, the metric projection
\Pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to D
is
single-valued, non-expansive, and characterized by the property that

x-\Pi(x)
is the minimal vector pushing
x
back toward the domain.
Thus the penalization term
n(x-\Pi(x))
provides the natural convex-analytic
restoring force that approximates the reflection mechanism in the limit

n\to\infty
. This gives us the penalized process
X_{t}^{n}
satisfying

X_{t}^{n}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s}^{n})ds+K_{t}^{n}.

(2.3)

One can allow the Brownian motion
W_{t}
to vary for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, creating a new penalized equation with
W_{t}^{n}
; for our purposes, we keep as
W_{t}^{n}\coloneqq W_{t}
as the standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The penalized process (
2.3
) can then be reconsidered as the usual Langevin diffusion process (
1.1
) with a modified potential energy function. This identification enables us to study
X_{t}^{n}
via the well-developed theory
of Langevin diffusion.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and convex, and
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be our energy function. For
n\geq 1
, define

f_{n}(x)=g(x)+\frac{n}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D).

Suppose
X^{n}
satisfies the penalized SDE (
2.3
) for a Brownian motion
W^{n}
, with
K^{n}_{t}
defined by (
2.2
). Then,
X^{n}
is a solution of the Langevin SDE

dX_{t}^{n}=-\nabla f_{n}(X_{t}^{n})dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{n}.

(2.5)

Conversely, any solution of (
2.5
) satisfies (
2.3
) (with
K^{n}
given by (
2.2
)).
We refer to
f_{n}
as the penalized energy function.

Proof.

For a closed convex domain
D
, the metric projection
\Pi(x)
is single-valued and
1
-Lipschitz. The function
\psi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)
is
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\nabla\psi(x)=x-\Pi(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
2
]
. Consequently,
\nabla\psi
is
1
-Lipschitz. From our integral equation (
2.3
) and definition of
K^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}^{n}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s}^{n})ds-n\int_{0}^{t}(X_{s}^{n}-\Pi(X_{s}^{n}))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}^{n}-\int_{0}^{t}\left[\nabla g(X_{s}^{n})+n\nabla\psi(X_{s}^{n})\right]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}^{n}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f_{n}(X_{s}^{n})ds.

This is precisely the integral form of (
2.5
). In differential notation, we have

dX_{t}^{n}=-\nabla f_{n}(X_{t}^{n})dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{n}.

Conversely, if
X^{n}
is a solution to (
2.5
) then integrating from
0
to
t
gives us

X_{t}^{n}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}^{n}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s}^{n})ds-n\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\psi(X_{s}^{n})ds.

∎

Note that
\Pi(x)=x
for
x\in D
so
\nabla\psi(x)=0
inside
D
which means the penalty vanishes, and outside of
D
, the restoring term is
-n(x-\Pi(x))
which pulls the process back towards the domain.

Remark 2.6
.

For closed convex
D
, we have
\psi\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla\psi(x)=x-\Pi(x)
is 1-Lipschitz; hence if
\nabla g
is
L
-Lipschitz then the penalized energy function
\nabla f_{n}
is
(L+n)
-Lipschitz.

We are also able to obtain the convexity of
f_{n}
based on the convexity of
g.
The Lipschitz and convexity conditions on the energy functions are of particular significance as they allow us to obtain unique invariant probability measures for the reflected and penalized processes, which is explored in Section 3.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and convex, and define

\psi(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D),\quad f_{n}(x)\coloneqq g(x)+n\psi(x),

with
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
If
g
is
m
-strongly convex then
f_{n}
is also
m
-strongly convex on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Since
D
is convex,
\psi
is convex on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
so for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\psi(y)\geq\psi(x)+\langle\nabla\psi(x),y-x\rangle.

(2.8)

Since
g
is
m
-strongly convex, we have

g(y)\geq g(x)+\langle\nabla g(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{m}{2}||y-x||^{2}

(2.9)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
So, since
\nabla f_{n}=\nabla g+n\nabla\psi
, adding
n
times (
2.8
) to (
2.9
) yields

f_{n}(y)\geq f_{n}(x)+\langle\nabla f_{n}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{m}{2}||y-x||^{2}.

Thus,
f_{n}
is
m
-strongly convex, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

3.
Convergence to a Unique Invariant Measure in Wasserstein-2 Distance

Having established the convexity of the penalized energy functions
f_{n}
, we now turn to the analysis of the convergence properties of the laws of the penalized Langevin SDE (
2.5
) to a unique invariant measure. To study the convergence, we introduce the Wasserstein distance on the space of probability measures which quantifies the minimal transport cost to move from one distribution to another.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of all Borel probability measures with finite second moment.

Definition 3.1

(Coupling)
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be probability measures on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).
A
coupling
(or
transport plan
) between
\mu
and
\nu
is a probability measure
\zeta
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
whose marginals are
\mu
and
\nu
. That is,

\zeta(A\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(A),\quad\zeta(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times A)=\nu(A)\quad\text{for all measurable }A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We denote by
\Omega(\mu,\nu)
the set of all such couplings between
\mu
and
\nu.

Equivalently, a pair of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
random variables
(X,Y)
is said to be a coupling of
\mu
and
\nu
if there exists
\zeta\in\Omega(\mu,\nu)
such that the joint distribution of
(X,Y)
is
\zeta
, that is,
X\sim\mu
and
Y\sim\nu.

Definition 3.2

(Wasserstein Distance of order 2)
.

For two probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),
the
Wasserstein distance of order 2
between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as

W_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\inf_{\zeta\in\Omega(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}||x-y||^{2},d\zeta(x,y)\right)^{1/2}.

(3.3)

This is also called the
Wasserstein-2 distance
, and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
forms a complete, separable metric space under the
W_{2}
metric. Equivalently, for any coupling
(X,Y)
of
\mu
and
\nu
,

W_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{(X,Y)}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[||X-Y||^{2}\big]\big)^{1/2},

(3.4)

where the infimum is taken over all joint distributions of pairs
(X,Y)
with respective laws
\mu
and
\nu

[
21
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mu_{0},\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be two probability measures, and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and convex. Suppose
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
m
-strongly convex. For some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{t}^{n},Y_{t}^{n}\in D
denote two respective solutions to the penalized Langevin SDE (
2.3
) driven by the same Brownian motion
W_{t}
and energy function
g
with initial distributions
X_{0}\sim\mu_{0}
and
Y_{0}\sim\nu_{0}.
Then, for all
t\geq 0
, we have the following contraction result:

W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}^{n})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})

(3.6)

where
\mu_{t}^{n}
and
\nu_{t}^{n}
denote the laws of
X_{t}^{n}
and
Y_{t}^{n}
respectively.

Proof.

Here, we can follow a similar argument to
[
3
]
. Consider two copies of the penalized diffusion
X_{t}^{n}
and
Y_{t}^{n}
driven by the same Brownian motion
W_{t}
starting from
X_{0}
with law
\mu_{0}
and
Y_{0}
with law
\nu_{0}
respectively. Then,
X_{t}^{n}
and
Y_{t}^{n}
then satisfy

dX_{t}^{n}=-\nabla f_{n}(X_{t}^{n})dt+\sigma dW_{t},\qquad dY_{t}^{n}=-\nabla f_{n}(Y_{t}^{n})dt+\sigma dW_{t}

by Theorem
2.4
, where
f_{n}=g(x)+\frac{n}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)
. Applying Ito’s formula to the squared distance yields

\frac{d}{dt}||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}=-2\langle\nabla f_{n}(X_{t}^{n})-\nabla f_{n}(Y_{t}^{n}),X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}\rangle.

Since
f_{n}
is strongly
m
-convex by Lemma
2.7
, then for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

m||x-y||^{2}\leq\langle\nabla f_{n}(x)-\nabla f_{n}(y),x-y\rangle,

so we have that

\frac{d}{dt}||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}\leq-2m||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}.

Next, by Grönwall’s Lemma,

||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}\leq e^{-2mt}||X_{0}-Y_{0}||^{2}

so
\mathbb{E}[||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}]\leq e^{-2mt}\mathbb{E}[||X_{0}-Y_{0}||^{2}]
, and this holds for any arbitrary coupling
(X_{0},Y_{0})
of
\mu_{0}
and
\nu_{0}
. Applying
3.4
gives us

\left(W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}^{n})\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[||X_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}^{n}||^{2}],

and taking the infimum over all couplings of
X_{0},Y_{0}
with laws
\mu_{0},\nu_{0}
implies that

\left(W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}^{n})\right)^{2}\leq e^{-2mt}\left(W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})\right)^{2}.

Taking the square root completes the proof.
∎

Our primary interest is to use the penalized Langevin SDEs to converge on the invariant measure of the reflected Langevin SDE (
2.1
). For the Langevin equation driven by a potential energy function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,

dX_{t}=-\nabla f(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},

the infinitesimal generator
L
acts on test functions

\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

L\psi(x)=-\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\,\Delta\psi(x).

A probability measure
\pi
is invariant for the process if and only if it
satisfies the stationary Fokker–Planck equation
L^{*}\pi=0
, where
L^{*}
denotes the
L^{2}
–adjoint of
L
. Moreover, for the Langevin generator, with respect to the inner product of
L^{2}(\pi)
,
L
is self-adjoint
[
12
]
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is closed and convex, and
g
is
m
-strongly convex. For
n\geq 1
, let
X_{t}^{n}
be a solution to the penalized Langevin SDE (
2.3
). Then, there exists a unique invariant probability measure
\pi^{n}
with density

\frac{d\pi^{n}}{dx}(x)=\frac{1}{Z^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}f_{n}(x)\right),\quad Z^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}f_{n}(x)\right)dx<\infty.

Furthermore, for every initial law
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\pi^{n})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi^{n}),\quad t\geq 0

where
\mu_{t}^{n}
is the law of
X_{t}^{n}.

Proof.

First note that by Theorem
2.4
,
X_{t}^{n}
satisfies
dX_{t}^{n}=-\nabla f_{n}(X_{t}^{n})dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{n}
where
f_{n}(x)=g(x)+\frac{n}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)
. Then,
f_{n}
is
m
-strongly convex by Lemma
2.7
which implies that
f_{n}(x)\geq\frac{m}{2}||x||^{2}-c
for some
c>0
so

Z^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}f_{n}(x)\right)dx<\infty.

The Gibbs measure
\pi^{n}
is therefore well defined, and it is invariant for the generator
L=-\nabla f_{n}\cdot\nabla+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta
via integration-by-parts.

Additionally,
X_{t}^{n}
defines a Markov semi-group that is contractive in the
W_{2}
distance by Theorem
3.5
. Since
\pi^{n}
is invariant for this semi-group, it is unique
[
7
,
1
]
. Taking
\nu_{0}=\pi^{n}
in Theorem
3.5
gives
W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\pi^{n})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi^{n})
.
∎

From here, we now study the contraction results for the reflected Langevin stochastic differential equation (
2.1
). We also introduce necessary Lipschitz and geometric drift conditions on the potential energy function to construct the bounds, as done in
[
7
,
9
,
13
,
19
,
18
]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and convex. Assume

(A1)

g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
m
-strongly convex with
\nabla g
globally
L
-Lipschitz:

||\nabla g(x)-\nabla g(y)||\leq L||x-y||\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(A2)

there exists
R>0
such that

\sigma^{2}+||\nabla g(x)||^{2}\leq R(1+||x||^{2})\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
X_{t},Y_{t}\in D
be solutions of the RSDE (
2.1
), driven by the same Brownian motion
W_{t}
,

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s})ds+K_{t},

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(Y_{s})ds+\hat{K_{t}}

with initial laws
\mu_{0},\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
X_{t}^{n},Y_{t}^{n}
be the penalized solutions in (
2.3
), also driven by the same process
W_{t}
and initial laws,

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s}^{n})ds+K_{t}^{n},

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(Y_{s}^{n})ds+\hat{K_{t}^{n}}.

Then, for all
t\geq 0
, we have the following contraction result

W_{2}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})+K\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}

where
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
are the respective laws of
X_{t}
and
Y_{t},
and
K>0.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complete metric space under the Wasserstein-2 distance, the triangle inequality gives us

W_{2}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\leq W_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{n})+W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}^{n})+W_{2}(\nu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}),

where
\mu_{t}^{n}
and
\nu_{t}^{n}
are the laws of
X_{t}^{n}
and
Y_{t}^{n}
respectively. Then, by Theorem
3.5

W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t}^{n})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0}).

Using Theorem 4.1 of
[
19
]
, under assumptions (A1) and (A2), we have that there exists constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}||X_{s}^{n}-X_{s}||^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}||Y_{s}^{n}-Y_{s}||^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

By (
3.4
), for any coupling we know
(W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}))^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}||X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}||^{2}.
Since
||X_{t}^{n}-X_{t}||\leq\sup_{s\leq t}||X_{s}^{n}-X_{s}||,
we get

W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\mu_{t})\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4},\quad W_{2}(\nu_{t}^{n},\nu_{t})\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.

Putting the bounds together,

W_{2}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})+(C_{1}+C_{2})\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.8
,

W_{2}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0}),\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
n\to\infty
in Theorem
3.8
. Since
(\log n/n)^{1/4}\to 0,
the error term vanishes.
∎

For reflected Langevin SDE (
2.1
)

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla g(X_{s})ds+K_{t},

there has been recent work to determine under which assumptions an RSDE admits a unique invariant probability measure
\pi
. The full details are contained in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
. In particular, for reflected stochastic differential equations satisfying particular growth conditions in III.B of
[
13
]
, there exists constants
c>0
and

A direct computation using the stationary Fokker–Planck equation with

Theorem 3.12
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.8
, let
\pi
denote the unique invariant measure of the reflected SDE (
2.1
), and for
n\geq 1
, let
\pi^{n}
denote the unique invariant measure of the penalized process (
2.3
), as in Corollary
3.7
. Then, there exists constants
C>0
and
\delta>0
such that

W_{2}(\pi,\pi^{n})\leq Cn^{-\delta}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we have

W_{2}(\pi,\pi^{n})\leq W_{2}(\mu_{t},\pi)+W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\pi^{n})+W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}),

where
\mu_{t}
and
\mu_{t}^{n}
are the laws of the reflected and penalized processes at time
t
, respectively.

By (
3.10
) and Corollary
3.7
), at time
t
,

W_{2}(\mu_{t},\pi)\leq C_{1}e^{-ct}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi),\quad W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\pi^{n})\leq e^{-mt}W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi^{n}),

where
c,C_{1}>0
are independent of
n
. Note that
W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi^{n})
is uniformly bounded in
n
since

f_{n}(x)=g(x)+\frac{n}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)\geq\frac{m}{2}\|x\|^{2}-k

for some constant
k>0
by the convexity of
f_{n}
by Lemma
2.7
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x\|^{2}\,d\pi^{n}(x)\leq K

for some
K>0
independent of
n
. Since
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows that
W_{2}(\mu_{0},\pi^{n})
is uniformly bounded in
n
. Thus, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that
W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\pi^{n})\leq C_{2}e^{-mt}.
From Theorem 4.1 of
[
19
]
, we have

W_{2}(\mu_{t}^{n},\mu_{t})\leq C_{3}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4},

for some constant
C_{3}>0
. Combining all terms gives

W_{2}(\pi,\pi^{n})\leq C_{1}e^{-ct}+C_{2}e^{-mt}+C_{3}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}.

Now, choose
t=\frac{1}{\kappa}\log n
for
\kappa=\max(c,m)
. Since
\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}=o(n^{-\gamma})
for any
\gamma<\frac{1}{4}
, let

for suitable

4.
Discrete Approximation of the Penalized Langevin Diffusion

4.1.
Penalized Constrained Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (PCULA)

Having established the convergence of the continuous-time penalized process to the invariant probability measure of the reflected Langevin SDE, we now introduce a time-discretized version of the penalized SDE (
2.3
). Our standing assumptions for the discretization follow from (A1) and (A2) of Theorem
3.8
:

(EM1)

g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
m
-strongly convex and
\nabla g
is
L
-Lipschitz.

(EM2)

D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is nonempty, closed, and convex;
\psi(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}^{2}(x,D)
so that
\nabla\psi(x)=x-\Pi(x)
and
\nabla\psi(x)
is 1-Lipschitz.

(EM3)

There exists
R>0
such that

\sigma^{2}+||\nabla g(x)||^{2}\leq R(1+||x||^{2})\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consequently,
f_{n}\coloneqq g(x)+n\psi(x)
is continuously differentiable,
m
-strongly convex and
\nabla f_{n}
is
L_{n}
-Lipschitz where
L_{n}\coloneqq L+n.
We simulate the penalized SDE instead of the reflected SDE and let the penalty term
n
control the constraint. Applying the Euler-Maruyama scheme to (
2.5
) gives

X_{k+1}^{(n,h)}=X_{k}^{(n,h)}-h\nabla f_{n}(X_{k}^{(n,h)})+\sigma\sqrt{h}\xi_{k}

(4.1)

for a fixed step-size
h>0
, penalty
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\xi_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
.

From Section 2 of
[
8
]
, under assumptions (EM1)-(EM3), if
h\leq\frac{1}{m+L_{n}}
, then the discrete process
X_{k}^{(n,h)}
admits a unique invariant measure
\pi^{(n,h)}
such that
W_{2}(\pi^{(n,h)},\pi^{n})\leq Ch
for some
C>0
where
\pi^{n}
is the invariant measure of the continuous process
X_{t}^{n}
from (
2.5
). Thus, if
\pi
is the invariant measure of the reflected Langevin SDE (
2.1
), then by Theorem
3.12
, there exists constants
C_{1},C_{2},\delta>0
such that

W_{2}\left(\pi,\pi^{(n,h)}\right)\leq C_{1}h+C_{2}n^{-\delta}

(4.2)

The bound on
W_{2}\left(\pi,\pi^{(n,h)}\right)
ensures that the invariant measure of the discrete penalized process estimates that of the reflected Langevin SDE, subject to an appropriate step size
h
and sufficiently large penalty parameter
n
. However, the constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and
\delta
depend on the structural properties of the problem. More specifically, they depend on
m,L,d,\sigma,
and on
n
since
f_{n}
is
L_{n}
-Lipschitz. Thus, increasing
n
will tighten the constraint, but also increase the Lipschitz constant which in turn restricts the step size
h
. The proof of Theorem
3.12
also shows that the bound depends on
t\gtrsim\log n
, so the number of iterations
k
must grow at least logarithmically with
n
. Explicit formulations of the appropriate constants and parameters for Langevin methods is an active area of investigation; in particular, the step size
h
need not stay fixed, and can be replaced by a non-increasing sequence to obtain stricter bounds. Recent related work is contained in
[
7
,
9
,
8
]
.

We now obtain the Penalized Constrained Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm from (
4.1
). This follows the framework of the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm, with a modified potential energy function to enforce the boundary constraint. In particular, to sample from a target distribution
\pi
supported on
D
, as in Section 1, we let
g(x)=\frac{-\sigma^{2}}{2}\log\pi(x).

In this work, we demonstrated that boundary constraints in the Langevin
diffusion can be enforced through the reflected Langevin SDE (
2.1
).
To approximate the reflection mechanism, we introduced a family of penalized
SDEs (
2.5
) incorporating a restoring force that pushes the
process back toward the domain
D
, and we proved that their invariant measures
converge to the unique invariant law of the reflected diffusion at a polynomial
rate in the penalization parameter. Discretizing the penalized dynamics via the Euler–Maruyama scheme yields the Penalized Constrained Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (PCULA), which follows the standard ULA framework but replaces the original potential
g
with the penalized energy
f_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
.

There are several directions remaining for further exploration. An important question is whether penalization-based methods can be extended to
non-convex domains, as most existing constrained Langevin algorithms are
designed for convex bodies; recent work such as
[
17
]
provides a
promising starting point for this case. Another direction is to explore alternative penalization functions or generalized penalty structures, and to optimize the choice of discretization parameters in the Euler–Maruyama scheme, following ideas developed in
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
. Further extensions to higher-order integrators or to underdamped Langevin dynamics also present new avenues of investigation.

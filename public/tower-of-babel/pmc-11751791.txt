Message from the editor-in-chief
Authors: Martin F. Pera
Published: 2024

Main text

This December marks the end of my 6-year term as Editor-In-Chief of Stem Cell Reports. I am delighted that Janet Rossant will be taking over the role in January. Janet’s status in our field, her rigorous approach to science, and her enthusiasm will ensure the continued success and growing strength of the journal.

I have very much enjoyed my time at the helm of Stem Cell Reports. During this time, we have published a great deal of outstanding science, in high-profile areas like disease modeling and organoid research, while continuing to span the wide scope of the field, from Drosophila development to preclinical translational work. We have published influential pieces on policy and ethics in stem cell science. We have maintained our reputation as a leading specialist journal in stem cell science in the face of strong competition from a plethora of new titles publishing in the same space.

I have been fortunate, with the support of International Society for Stem Cell Research (ISSCR), to be able to oversee some innovations to the journal that will continue going forward after my term. These include special issues, the Stem Cell Report podcast, and the Early Career Editorial Board. Special issues at Stem Cell Reports have been distinguished by our exceptional guest editors and first-rate contributions from recognized leaders in the field. I am excited about our new policy of centering special issues around the Society’s international symposia, which will ensure exciting topical content and give meeting participants a chance to showcase their work in the journal. I have had some really fascinating and entertaining conversations with both senior researchers and early-career scientists on the Stem Cell Report podcast. The young scientists that we usually bring on in the role of first author have been incredibly articulate and thoughtful. The Early Career Editorial Board have been a real pleasure to work with. This group of scientists have worked diligently for the journal, they have done a fantastic job in handling manuscripts, and every year at the ISSCR annual meeting, I have enjoyed wide-ranging discussions on all aspects of the journal and stem cell science with them.

Putting this journal together takes a big effort from a number of people, so I will give you a look behind the scenes. Our relationship with Cell Press is stronger than ever, and I am grateful to Helene Hodak and her colleagues for all the attention and hard work they devote to us on all levels, from policy and strategy to production and public relations. Our Editorial Board and the Early Career Editorial Board play a key role in advising us and helping us to deal with manuscripts. Our reviewers deserve special recognition. I sign off on every editorial decision at the journal, and I am always impressed by the thoroughness and fairness shown by our referees (because of their efforts, I have had very few author appeals to adjudicate during my tenure). We are fortunate to have a great team of Associate Editors, and in particular I acknowledge Christine Mummery and Jun Takahashi (both finishing their terms soon) for their invaluable service over the years. At ISSCR, the entire team from our CEO Keith Helm on down are constantly engaged with the journal on every level, and they recently have made important strides to link journal content with major Society initiatives, such as webinars and symposia. The ISSCR Scientific Programs Committee provides input and wise counsel to the journal and is key to the integration of journal activities with the broader ISSCR platform. Finally, the two people I work with most closely are the ISSCR Scientific Advisor Jack Mosher and our Managing Editor, Yvonne Fischer. Editors can be replaced but I am not sure how the journal would keep running without these two individuals. Jack and Yvonne are special people who are very devoted to Stem Cell Reports. I thank them for all their hard work and for keeping me on track for the past 6 years.

I am very happy to be staying on at Stem Cell Reports in the role of Associate Editor, which allows me to maintain my involvement with the journal (with the benefit of a reduced workload). My very best wishes to Janet Rossant for a great start to 2025.

Lastly, to all authors that have published in Stem Cell Reports, thank you for trusting the journal with your research. I know that Janet and the editorial team of scientists will maintain our ethos of rapid and fair review, and I can assure you that when you publish with us, your work will have direct impact on our global community of stem cell scientists. I look forward to seeing more of your exciting science in the journal.

Quantitative partitioned index theorem and noncompact band-width
Peter Hochs, Jinmin Wang

Abstract
Gromov’s band-width conjecture gives a precise upper bound for the width of a compact Riemannian band with positive scalar curvature lower bound, assuming that the cross-section of the band admits no positive scalar curvature metrics. Versions of this were proved by Cecchini and by Zeidler. In this paper, we develop a quantitative version of partitioned manifold index theory, which applies to noncompact hypersurfaces. Using this, we prove a version of Gromov’s band-width estimate for possibly noncompact Riemannian bands.



1
Introduction

In 2018, Gromov made the following conjecture, see Conjecture C on page 722 of
[
9
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Band-width conjecture; Gromov, 2018)
.

Let
N
be a compact manifold without boundary, of dimension
n-1\geq 5
. Suppose that
N
does not admit Riemannian metrics of positive scalar curvature. Consider a Riemannian metric
g
on
N\times[-1,1]
. Let
\ell
be the distance between
N\times\{-1\}
and
N\times\{1\}
with respect to this metric. Then

\inf_{x\in N\times[-1,1]}\mathrm{Sc}_{g}(x)\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}}.

Here we write
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}
for the scalar curvature associated to a Riemannian metric
g
.
Gromov noted that the bound
\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}}
is optimal
[
9
, pages 653–654]
.

Versions of Gromov’s conjecture were proved by Zeidler
[
34
, Theorem 1.4]
and Cecchini
[
4
, Theorem D]
(who achieved the optimal bound), under the condition that Rosenberg index of
N
is nonzero. The Rosenberg index is a refined obstruction to Riemannian metrics of positive scalar curvature on compact
\Spin
manifolds
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
. Zeidler extended these results to more general forms of Riemannian bands. A consequence of
[
33
, Theorem 1.4]
is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Zeidler, 2020)
.

Let
X
be a complete Riemannian
\Spin
-manifold of dimension
n
, and let
N\subset X
be a compact hypersurface. Suppose that

•

N
has trivial normal bundle;

•

the map
\pi_{1}(N)\to\pi_{1}(X)
induced by the inclusion is injective;

•

the Rosenberg index of
N
in
KO_{n-1}(C^{*}(\pi_{1}(N)))
is nonzero.

Suppose that
M
is a neighbourhood of
N
of width
\ell
. Then

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Sc}_{g}(x)\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}}.

In the special case where
X=N\times\mathbb{R}
, Theorem
1.2
becomes
[
4
, Theorem D]
which states that Conjecture
1.1
is true if the Rosenberg index of
N
is nonzero. (See
[
33
, Corollary 1.5]
.)

The proofs of the results in
[
4
,
33
,
34
]
involve the
partitioned manifold index
of Dirac operators, and the closely related index of
Callias-type Dirac operators
on noncompact manifolds. In this paper, we develop a
quantitative partitioned manifold index
, and use it to extend Theorem
1.2
to noncompact
N
.

If
X
is an odd-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold, partitioned into two parts by a
compact
hypersurface
N\subset X
, then Roe’s partitioned manifold index is an integer
\ind(D,N)
associated to a Dirac operator
D
on
X
. Roe proved a
partitioned manifold index theorem
, stating that

\ind(D,N)=\ind(D_{N}),

(1.1)

for a Dirac operator
D_{N}
on
N
naturally associated to
D
. See
[
17
]
for the original result, or
[
11
]
and
[
18
, Thm. 4.4]
for later, more streamlined accounts. As immediate consequences, one obtains cobordism invariance of the index of Dirac operators on compact manifolds, obstructions to positive scalar curvature, and homotopy invariants of certain higher signatures. (See
[
18
, pp. 31–32]
.)

Several variations on and generalizations of Roe’s partitioned manifold index theorem have been obtained over the years, see for example
[
15
,
16
,
22
,
24
,
32
]
. For a version of Theorem
1.2
where
N
may be noncompact, we develop and apply a partitioned manifold index theorem for noncompact hypersurfaces. In that setting, several results were obtained recently:

[
3
, Theorem 4.44]
,
[
8
, Corollary 7.6]
,
[
14
, Theorem 2.23]
.

For our purposes, the construction of the partitioned manifold index in
[
14
]
is most useful, as this construction involves a Callias-type operator, which is suitable for the quantitative estimates in terms of positive scalar curvature that we will use. A disadvantage of the result in
[
14
]
is that the Riemannian distance on
N
is assumed to be equivalent, in the sense of coarse geometry, to the restriction of the Riemannian distance on
X
.

To overcome this, we refine the result in
[
14
]
to a
quantitative partitioned manifold index theorem
. In the results in
[
3
,
8
,
14
]
, the equality (
1.1
) holds in the
K
-theory of a localized Roe algebra. Roughly speaking, this is the inductive limit of the Roe algebras of
r
-neighborhoods of
N
, as
r\to\infty
. (See Definition
3.2
.) In our quantitative version, we obtain a version of (
1.1
) in a specific neighborhood of
N
, which we can control. This is Theorem
5.1
, which is an equality of the form

\ind_{q}(D,N)=(i_{N})_{*}(\ind(D_{N}))\in K_{*}(C^{*}(M)),

(1.2)

for a specific type of closed set
M\subset X
containing
N
in its interior, where
i_{N}\colon N\to M
is the inclusion map and
\ind_{q}
is a quantitative version of the partitioned manifold index (see Definitions
4.6
and
4.9
). We illustrate the difference between partitioned manifold index theory for noncompact hypersurfaces as in
[
14
]
and our quantitative version
in Example
5.4
.

In Theorem
1.2
, the index-theoretic assumptions that the induced map on fundamental groups is injective and that the Rosenberg index is nonzero guarantee that the cross-section
N
does not admit a positive scalar curvature metric. In the setting of a complete, possibly noncompact manifold, a natural analogue is the non-vanishing of the coarse index of the Dirac operator on
N
in
K_{*}(C^{*}(N))
. However, as we show in Subsection
6.2
, this condition alone is not sufficient to deduce the band-width estimate, and an additional assumption describing how
N
lies inside the band
M
is required. More precisely, we prove the following noncompact version of Theorem
1.2
, which is the main result in this paper. (This is Theorem
2.5
in the main text.)

Theorem 1.3

(Band-width inequality for possibly noncompact bands)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a regular
\Spin
Riemannian band with boundary
\partial_{-}M\sqcup\partial_{+}M
.
Let
\ell
denote the distance between
\partial_{-}M
and
\partial_{+}M
in
M
.
Suppose that

1.

the identity map

\id\colon(\partial_{-}M,d_{g_{\partial_{-}M}})\to(\partial_{-}M,d_{g}|_{\partial_{-}M})

is a coarse equivalence, and

2.

the Dirac operator
D_{\partial_{-}M}
has non-zero index in
K_{n-1}(C^{*}(\partial_{-}M))
.

Then

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Sc}_{g}(x)\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}}.

The definition of regular
\Spin
Riemannian bands is given in Definition
2.3
. In particular, there always exists a complete Riemannian manifold
X
containing
M
. In order to avoid involving the distance function on
X
, we employ the quantitative partitioned manifold theory and localize the partitioned manifold index to
M\subset X
. Then we use the equality (
1.2
) to prove Theorem
1.3
.

2
A band-width inequality for noncompact bands

2.1
Uniformly embedded submanifolds

We recall several geometric regularity conditions for noncompact manifolds.

Let
(X^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Assume that
(X,g)
has
bounded geometry
, i.e.

1.

the global injectivity radius
\textup{inj}_{g}=\inf_{x\in X}\textup{inj}_{g}(x)
is positive, and

2.

for every
k\geq 0
, the
k
-th covariant derivatives of the Riemannian curvature tensor are uniformly bounded.

In any metric space
(Y,d_{Y})
, for
y\in Y
and
r>0
, we denote the open ball around
y
of radius
r
by
B_{r}(y)
. For
A\subset Y
, we write

\begin{split}{B}_{r}(A)&:={\bigcup_{y\in A}B_{r}(y)};\\
\bar{B}_{r}(A)&:=\overline{B_{r}(A)}\end{split}

where the line on the right hand side denotes the closure.
We often use this closure, so that the sets
\bar{B}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi})
used in Definition
4.6
are closed, so they are proper metric spaces and hence have well-behaved Roe algebras.

The following notion is taken from
[
7
, Section 2.3]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N^{\,n-1}
be a complete hypersurface in a complete Riemannian manifold
(X^{n},g)
. We say that
N
is
uniformly embedded
in
X
if there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
x\in N
, the intersection
B_{\delta}(x)\cap N
can be represented, in normal coordinates at
x
, as the graph of a function

h\colon T_{x}N\longrightarrow(T_{x}N)^{\perp},

whose derivatives of all orders are uniformly bounded.

It is easy to see that a uniformly embedded hypersurface
(N^{n-1},g_{N}=g|_{N})
is itself a complete Riemannian manifold of bounded geometry. Moreover, any uniformly embedded hypersurface admits a uniform tubular neighborhood. As a standing assumption, we suppose that all manifolds we consider are oriented. In particular, this means that all hypersurfaces have trivial normal bundles.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.31]
)
.

If
N^{n-1}\subset X^{n}
is a uniformly embedded hypersurface, then there exist constants
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that the following hold.

1.

The map

F\colon N\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow X,\qquad(x,t)\mapsto\exp_{x}(tn_{x}),

is a diffeomorphism onto its image
U
, where
n_{x}
denotes the unit normal vector to
N
in
X
at
x
.

2.

The metric
g
on
U
satisfies

C^{-1}\,F_{*}(dt^{2}+g_{N})\ \leq\ g|_{U}\ \leq\ C\,F_{*}(dt^{2}+g_{N}).

We call a set
U
as in Theorem
2.2
a
uniform geodesic normal neighborhood
of
N\subset X
.

We remark that, although the metric tensors
g|_{U}
and
dt^{2}+g_{N}
appear similar on
U
, the Riemannian distance function on
X
restricted on
U
is not necessarily bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the product distance function. This subtle distinction plays an essential role in our main theorems, and we will provide explicit examples to illustrate it (see Example
5.4
and Subsection
6.2
).

2.2
The main result

Our goal in this paper is to prove a noncompact version of the band-width inequality for Riemannian bands.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold with boundary

\partial M=\partial_{-}M\sqcup\partial_{+}M
, where
\partial_{\pm}M
are unions of connected components of
\partial M
.
We say that
(M^{n},g)
is a
regular Riemannian band
if it admits an isometric embedding into a complete Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
with bounded geometry, such that
\partial_{\pm}M
are uniformly embedded hypersurfaces.

Remark 2.4
.

If
(M,g)
has bounded geometry in the sense of manifolds with boundary, and
\partial_{\pm}M
are uniformly embedded hypersurfaces (analogous to Definition
2.1
), then
(M,g)
is automatically regular: one may take

X=(\partial_{-}M\times(-\infty,0])\cup_{\partial_{-}M}M\cup_{\partial_{+}M}(\partial_{+}M\times[0,+\infty)),

with a metric smoothly interpolating between
g
and the product metrics near
\partial_{\pm}M
as in
[
5
, Theorem 1.9]
.

For any Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, we denote the Riemannian distance on
M
by
d_{g}
. If
N\subset M
is a submanifold, then we denote the restriction of
g
to
N
by
g_{N}
. The difference between the distance function
d_{g_{N}}
and the restriction
d_{g}|_{N}
of the Riemannian distance on
M
to
N
will play an important role throughout this paper.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a regular
\Spin
Riemannian band with boundary
\partial_{-}M\sqcup\partial_{+}M
.
Let
\ell
denote the distance between
\partial_{-}M
and
\partial_{+}M
in
M
.
Suppose that

1.

the identity map

\id\colon(\partial_{-}M,d_{g_{\partial_{-}M}})\to(\partial_{-}M,d_{g}|_{\partial_{-}M})

is a coarse equivalence, and

2.

the Dirac operator
D_{\partial_{-}M}
has non-zero index in
K_{n-1}(C^{*}(\partial_{-}M))
.

Then

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Sc}_{g}(x)\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}}.

(2.1)

The first assumption in Theorem
2.5
implies that
\partial_{-}M
is connected: otherwise, the different connected components of
\partial_{-}M
are at infinite distance to each other with respect to
d_{g_{\partial_{-}M}}
, but not with respect to
d_{g}
.

The bound in (
2.1
) is already sharp in the compact case, as pointed out below Conjecture
1.1
, so also in this more general setting.

Example 2.6
.

As in Theorem
1.2
, let
X
be a complete,
n
-dimensional Riemannian
\Spin
-manifold of dimension
n
, and let
N\subset X
be a
compact
hypersurface with trivial normal bundle. Suppose that the map
\pi_{1}(N)\to\pi_{1}(X)
induced by the inclusion is injective. This implies that the universal cover
\tilde{N}
of
N
embeds into the universal cover
\tilde{X}
of
X
. Let
M\subset X
be a relatively compact neighborhood of
N
of width
l
, whose boundary decomposes as
\partial M=\partial_{-}M\sqcup\partial_{+}M
, where
\partial_{\pm}M
are compact hypersurfaces. Then the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
is a regular
\Spin
Riemannian band. Suppose that
\bar{M}=M_{-}\cup M_{+}
, where
M_{-}\cap M_{+}=N
,
\partial(M_{\pm})=\partial_{\pm}M\sqcup N
, and
M_{-}
defines a cobordism between
\partial_{-}M
and
N
.

Suppose that the coarse index of the Dirac operator
D_{\tilde{N}}
on
\tilde{N}
is nonzero. This index equals the coarse index of
D_{\partial\tilde{M}_{-}}
by the cobordism defined by the universal cover
\tilde{M}_{-}
of
\tilde{M}_{-}
(see
[
29
]
,
[
30
, Theorem 4.12]
,
[
14
, Corollary 2.27]
), which is therefore also nonzero.
The first condition in Theorem
2.5
now holds automatically for
\tilde{M}
, by relative compactness of
M
. This theorem therefore implies that

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Sc}_{g}(x)=\inf_{\tilde{x}\in\tilde{M}}\mathrm{Sc}_{\tilde{g}}(\tilde{x})\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}(n-1)}{n\ell^{2}},

(2.2)

where
\tilde{g}
is the lift of
g
to
\tilde{X}
.
In other words, Theorem
2.5
reduces to a version of Theorem
1.2
with nonvanishing of the Rosenberg index replaced by nonvanishing of the coarse index of
D_{\tilde{N}}
.

For a result resembling Theorem
1.2
more closely,
suppose that the equivariant coarse index

\ind_{\pi_{1}(N)}(D_{\tilde{N}})\in K_{n-1}(C^{*}(\tilde{N})^{\pi_{1}(N)})=K_{n-1}(C^{*}(\pi_{1}(N)))

(2.3)

is nonzero. This condition is slightly stronger than the nonvanishing of the Rosenberg index of
N
, which is an analogue of this index in
KO_{n-1}(C^{*}(\pi_{1}(N)))
.

A variation on the second condition in Theorem
2.5
now holds: the equivariant index coarse of
D_{\partial_{-}\tilde{M}}
equals (
2.3
) by the cobordism defined by
\tilde{M}_{-}
, and is hence nonzero.
Theorem
2.5
immediately generalizes to the case where
D_{\partial_{-}M}
has non-zero index in
K_{n-1}(C^{*}(\partial_{-}M)^{\Gamma})
, for some discrete, finitely generated group
\Gamma
acting properly and freely on
M
, preserving all structure. This version of the theorem implies (
2.2
).

We prove Theorem
2.5
in Section
6
. The main technical tool for this proof is a
quantitative partitioned manifold index theorem
for possibly noncompact hypersurfaces, Theorem
5.1
.

3
A partitioned manifold index theorem for possibly noncompact hypersurfaces

We state a variation of the main result from
[
14
]
, a generalization of Roe’s partitioned manifold index theorem
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
, in which the partitioning hypersurface may be noncompact. Related results were obtained earlier in
[
3
,
8
]
. The result is Theorem
3.8
. Compared to Theorem 2.23 in
[
14
]
, the construction of the index is slightly different, and the result in this paper applies to both even- and odd-dimensional hypersurfaces. We point out why this result is
not
enough to prove Theorem
2.5
, which motivates the generalization of Theorem
3.8
to Theorem
5.1
. We give a direct proof of Theorem

in a special case (Proposition

3.1
Roe algebras and coarse maps

We now recall the definitions of Roe algebras and their localized variants. These algebras play a central role in index theory, as the indices of differential operators naturally take values in their
K
-theory.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space (meaning that closed balls are compact). A Hilbert space
H
is an
X
-module
if
H
carries a nondegenerate representation of
C_{0}(X)
(meaning that no nonzero vector in
H
is mapped to zero by every element of
C_{0}(X)
). The module
H
is called
ample
if no nonzero function in
C_{0}(X)
acts as a compact operator.

For example, if
X
is a complete Riemannian manifold, then
H=L^{2}(X,E)
for a Hermitian vector bundle
E\to X
gives an ample
X
-module.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space and
H
an ample
X
-module.

1.

An operator
T\in B(H)
is
locally compact
if
fT
and
Tf
are compact for all
f\in C_{0}(X)
.

2.

An operator
T\in B(H)
has
finite propagation
if there exists
P>0
such that
fTg=0
whenever
f,g\in C_{c}(X)
satisfy
d(\mathrm{supp}(f),\mathrm{supp}(g))>P
.

3.

If
M\subset X
is a closed subset, we say that
T\in B(H)
is
supported in
M
if
fT=Tf=0
for any
f\in C_{c}(X)
supported away from
M
.

4.

If
N\subset X
is a subset, we say that
T\in B(H)
is
supported near
N
if there exists
R>0
such that
T
is supported in the closed
R
-neighborhood
\bar{B}_{R}(N)
of
N
.

The
Roe algebra

C^{*}(X)
is the norm closure in
B(H)
of the algebra of locally compact operators with finite propagation.
If
N\subset X
, then the
localized Roe algebra

C^{*}(N\subset X)
is the norm closure of all locally compact, finite-propagation operators supported near
N
.

The Roe algebra
C^{*}(X)
is independent of the particular choice of ample
X
-module; see
[
28
, Lemma 5.1.12]
.

If
X
is a complete
\Spin
-manifold of dimension
n
, then the
\Spin
-Dirac operator
D_{X}
on
X
has a
coarse index

\ind(D_{X})\in K_{n}(C^{*}(X)),

as defined in
[
18
, Definition 3.7]
or
[
31
, Section 2.3.1]
.

A key property of Roe algebras is their invariance under coarse equivalence. We recall the basic notions.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be proper metric spaces, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper map.

1.

The map
f
is a
coarse map
if there exists a nondecreasing function
\rho_{+}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{r\to\infty}\rho_{+}(r)=\infty
such that

d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq\rho_{+}(d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}))

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.

2.

The map
f
is a
coarse embedding
if there exist nondecreasing functions
\rho_{\pm}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{r\to\infty}\rho_{-}(r)=\infty
such that

\rho_{-}(d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}))\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq\rho_{+}(d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}))

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.

3.

A coarse embedding
f
is a
coarse equivalence
if
f(X)
is a
net
in
Y
, i.e., there exists
R>0
such that
Y=\bar{B}_{R}(f(X))
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
28
, Theorem 5.1.15]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be proper metric spaces, and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a coarse map. Then
f
naturally induces a homomorphism

f_{*}\colon K_{*}(C^{*}(X))\longrightarrow K_{*}(C^{*}(Y)).

This construction is functorial.
Moreover, if
f
is a coarse equivalence, then
f_{*}
is an isomorphism.

We will often use the special case of Theorem
3.4
where
X
is a closed subset of
Y
, and
f
is the inclusion map. To be more precise,
let
(Y,d_{Y})
be a proper metric space, and let
X\subset Y
be a closed subset that equals the closure of its interior. Let
d_{X}
be a distance function on
X
that induces the same topology on
X
as the restriction of
d_{Y}
, and such that for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,

d_{Y}(x,x^{\prime})\leq d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}).

It follows from this inequality and closedness of
X
that
(X,d_{X})
is a proper metric space. And the inclusion map
i_{X}\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
is continuous.

Let
H_{Y}
be an ample
Y
-module.
The representation
C_{0}(Y)\to\mathcal{B}(H_{Y})
extends to
L^{\infty}(Y)
(with respect to a Borel measure), and in particular to the indicator function
1_{X}
of
X
. We write
H_{X}=1_{X}\cdot H_{Y}
. One can check directly, using the fact that
X
equals the closure of its interior, that
H_{X}
is an ample
X
-module.

Consider the Roe algebra
C^{*}(Y)
defined on
H_{Y}
, and the Roe algebra
C^{*}(X)
defined on
H_{X}
, with respect to the distance function
d_{X}
. Let
j\colon H_{X}\to H_{Y}
be the inclusion map. Define the norm-continuous linear map
\iota_{X}\colon\mathcal{B}(H_{X})\to\mathcal{B}(H_{Y})
by

\iota_{X}(T):=jTj^{*},

for
T\in\mathcal{B}(H_{X})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The map
\iota_{X}
restricts to an injective
*
-homomorphism
\iota_{X}\colon C^{*}(X)\to C^{*}(Y)
, and the induced map on
K
-theory is the map
(i_{X})_{*}
from Theorem
3.4
.

Proof.

The first claim follows by a straightforward verification. The second claim follows from the fact that
j
is an isometry covering the inclusion
i_{X}
. (See e.g.
[
28
, Definitions 4.3.3 and 5.1.4]
.)
∎

We will view
C^{*}(X)
as a subalgebra of
C^{*}(Y)
via the map
\iota_{X}
, and use the description of the map
(i_{X})_{*}
in Lemma
3.5
without referring to this lemma explicitly. If an element of
C^{*}(Y)
is supported in
X
in the sense of point 3 in Definition
3.2
, then it lies in the image of
\iota_{X}
, so we may view it as an element of
C^{*}(X)
.
We will say that an element
a\in K_{*}(C^{*}(Y))
is
represented by
an element
b\in K_{*}(C^{*}(X))
if the (possibly non-injective) map
(i_{X})_{*}
induced by
i_{X}
on
K
-theory maps

to
a
.

In cases where
N\subset Y
is a closed subset, with a distance function

The partitioned manifold index theorem is as follows.

We will obtain a proof of Theorem

\Spin

. Consider the partitioning of

denote the index class defined in (

Proof.

The proof is based on
KK
-theory and follows the arguments of
Section 2.3.3 in
[
31
]
.

We first recall that for any stable
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}

(i.e.
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}\cong\mathcal{A}
),
the

KK_{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{A})=\Bigl\{\,[F]:F=\begin{pmatrix}0&V\\
U&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}),\ F=F^{*},\ F^{2}-1\in\mathcal{A}\Bigr\},

and

KK_{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{A})=\{\,[F]:F\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}),~F=F^{*},\ F^{2}-1\in\mathcal{A}\,\};

see
[
2
, Section 17.5.4]
.
Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism

KK_{*}(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow[~\cong~]{}K_{*+1}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})/\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow[~\cong~]{~\partial~}K_{*}(\mathcal{A}),

(3.10)

where

is the boundary map; see

[
2
, Propositions 17.5.5 and 17.5.6]
.

We now take
\mathcal{A}=C^{*}(N\subset X)\cong C^{*}(N)
.
By construction and by the definition of the boundary map

\partial
(see
[
2
, Definitions 8.3.1 and 9.3.2]
),
the index class

\ind(D,\psi)\in K_{n-1}(C^{*}(N\subset X))

is precisely the class

[F]\in KK_{n-1}(\mathbb{C},C^{*}(N))
,
where

is the operator defined in Section
3.2
,
for either parity of

. (The correspondence between unitary elements and projections just above
[
2

is exactly as in (
3.6
).)

Recall that the operator
B
acts on

when

is even, and on

when
\dim N
is odd.
In either case, the underlying bundle is naturally isomorphic to

S(T(N\times\mathbb{R})\oplus\hat{\mathbb{R}})=S(TN\oplus\mathbb{R}\oplus\hat{\mathbb{R}})\cong S(TN)\,\hat{\otimes}\,S(\mathbb{R}\oplus\hat{\mathbb{R}}),

where

denotes a trivial flat one-dimensional bundle

Under this identification, the Clifford action

corresponds to the matrix

when

is even, and to the grading operator

Consequently, the operator

decomposes as the graded tensor sum

where

is the odd differential operator acting on

over

These operators define classes

One standard approach to defining the Kasparov product

[
2

In the present situation, it is straightforward to check that

[F(B)]=[F(D_{N})]\otimes_{KK}[F(B_{\mathbb{R}})]\quad\text{in }KK_{n-1}(\mathbb{C},C^{*}(N)).

An alternative approach uses unbounded Kasparov modules;
see
[
1

Finally, we observe that

[

Moreover, for

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

If

\|\Theta(P)-P\|<\frac{8\varepsilon}{1-4\varepsilon}(\|P\|+2\varepsilon).

Proof.

If
\lambda\in\Spec(P)

|\lambda|\cdot|\lambda-1|=|\lambda^{2}-\lambda|<\varepsilon.

Hence

|\lambda|<2\varepsilon
(if
\Real(\lambda)<1/2

(if

P=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi|=2\varepsilon}\xi(\xi-P)^{-1}d\xi+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi-1|=2\varepsilon}\xi(\xi-P)^{-1}d\xi,

and

\Theta(P)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi-1|=2\varepsilon}(\xi-P)^{-1}d\xi.

Therefore,

\Theta(P)-P=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi-1|=2\varepsilon}(1-\xi)(\xi-P)^{-1}d\xi-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi|=2\varepsilon}\xi(\xi-P)^{-1}d\xi.

Proposition 4.8
.

For a universal
\delta
small enough,

and
r\geq\sqrt{\frac{b}{A\delta}}
, we have

\|\tilde{Q}-Q_{b}\|\leq\frac{1}{100}.

Proof.

We have

\tilde{Q}-Q_{b}=\sqrt{\chi}[Q_{b},\sqrt{\chi}]+(1-\chi)(1-Q_{b}).

(4.13)

And

\begin{split}[Q_{b},\sqrt{\chi}]&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\pi i)^{j}}{j!2^{j}}[(F_{b}+1)^{j},\sqrt{\chi}].\end{split}

The norm of the right hand side is less than or equal to

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\pi^{j}}{(j-1)!}\right)\|F_{b}+1\|\cdot\|[F_{b},\sqrt{\chi}]\|.

By (
4.2

For the second term on the right hand side of (

from Lemma

∎

5
The quantitative partitioned manifold index theorem

In this section, we state and prove a quantitative version of the partitioned manifold index theorem for noncompact manifolds, Theorem
5.1
, refining Theorem
3.8
. This is the key step in our proof of Theorem
2.5
.

5.1
Statement of the result

Theorem 5.1

(Quantitative partitioned manifold index theorem)
.

Let
(X^{n},g)
be a complete
\Spin
manifold with bounded geometry, partitioned by a uniformly embedded hypersurface
N^{n-1}\subset X
. Let
\psi\colon X\to[-A,A]
be a defining function of
N\subset X
such that

\psi^{-1}((-A,A))\subset\mathcal{N}_{\psi}\subset\bar{B}_{R}(N).

Let
D
be the Dirac operator on
X
. Let
r>0
be as in Proposition
4.4
or
4.8
, depending on the parity of
n
.
There exists a universal constant
c_{2}>0
such that,
with respect to the coarse map

\id\colon(N,d_{g_{N}})\longrightarrow(N,d_{c_{2}A^{-1}}),

we have

\ind_{q}(D,\psi)=\id_{*}(\ind(D_{N}))\in K_{n-1}(C^{*}(N,d_{c_{2}A^{-1}})),

where
\ind(D_{N})
is the coarse index of the Dirac operator on
N
.

Remark 5.2
.

In the setting of
Theorem
3.8
, the combination of Lemma
4.13
and Theorem
5.1
implies that

(i_{\bar{B}_{c_{2}A^{-1}}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi})})_{*}(\ind(D_{N}))=\ind(D,\psi).

Because the composition

K_{*}(C^{*}(N))\xrightarrow{\cong}K_{*}(\bar{B}_{c_{2}A^{-1}}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi}))\xrightarrow[\cong]{(i_{\bar{B}_{c_{2}A^{-1}}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi})})_{*}}K_{*}(C^{*}(N\subset X))

is the isomorphism (
3.1
) used in Theorem
3.8
, it follows that Theorem
3.8
is a special case of Theorem
5.1
.

Remark 5.3
.

In this paper, and in particular in Theorem
5.1
, we consider
\Spin
-Dirac operators. However, the construction of the quantitative partitioned manifold index, and Theorem
5.1
, extend to more general Dirac-type operators. In Theorem
5.1
, one then needs to specify a relation between
D
and
D_{N}
, as in
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
. We only discuss the case of
\Spin
-Dirac operators here, as this is what is needed to prove Theorem
2.5
.

Let us explain the difference between Theorems
3.8
and
5.1
by the following example.

Example 5.4
.

Let
Y=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be the flat Euclidean space with
n\geq 3
, and let
\delta>0
be a small number. Let
\mathcal{L}
be the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, and let
S\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
denote the reflection across the hyperplane orthogonal to the
x_{1}
-axis. Denote by
\mathcal{L}^{+}
the collection of points in
\mathcal{L}
with positive
x_{1}
-coordinate.

In
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, remove the sets
B_{\delta}([1,+\infty)\times\{q\})
and
B_{\delta}([1,+\infty)\times\{S(q)\})
for each
q\in\mathcal{L}^{+}
. Attach to each removed region a cylinder
T_{q}\cong\partial B_{\delta}([1,+\infty)\times\{q\})\times[0,1]
. In this way we obtain a Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
. More precisely,

X=\left(Y\setminus\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal{L}^{+}}\bigl(B_{\delta}([1,+\infty)\times\{q\})\cup B_{\delta}([1,+\infty)\times\{S(q)\})\bigr)\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal{L}^{+}}T_{q}\right).

We assume that all the
T_{q}
are isometric, and that
g
is a product metric on
\partial B_{\delta}([2,+\infty)\times\{q\})\times[0,1]\subset T_{q}
.

Note that
(X,g)
has bounded geometry. Let
\rho
be a smooth modification of the signed distance function to the slice
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
; in particular,
\rho
is essentially the projection onto the first factor
\mathbb{R}
in
Y
.

Let
f
be a
2
-Lipschitz, smooth, non-decreasing function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
f(x)\equiv\pm 1
for
|x|\geq 1
. Define
\psi_{a}=f\circ(\rho-a)
, a family of smooth functions on
X
parametrized by
a\in\mathbb{R}
. As in Section
3.2
and
[
14
]
, the index class
\ind(D,\psi_{a})\in K_{*}(C^{*}(\rho^{-1}(a)\subset X))
is independent of
a
, and is equal to zero. Indeed, take
a=10
. Then
\rho^{-1}(10)
is obtained from
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
by removing the
\delta
-balls
B_{\delta}(q)
and
B_{\delta}(S(q))
for all
q\in\mathcal{L}^{+}
and attaching cylinders
T_{q}^{\prime}\cong S^{n-2}\times[0,1]
. In this case
N=\rho^{-1}(10)
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.8
; in particular, the identity map

\id\colon(N,d_{g_{N}})\longrightarrow(N,d_{g}|_{N})

is a coarse equivalence. Therefore
\ind(D,\psi_{a})\in K_{*}(C^{*}(N\subset X))
vanishes by Theorem
3.8
, since

K_{*}(C^{*}(N))\cong K_{*}(C^{*}(\mathcal{L}^{+}))=0.

However, by Lemma
4.13
,
\ind(D,\psi_{a})
admits a preimage

\ind_{q}(D,\psi_{a})\in K_{*}\!\left(C^{*}([a-c,a+c]\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\right)

under the map

i_{a,*}\colon K_{*}\!\left(C^{*}([a-c,a+c]\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\right)\longrightarrow K_{*}(C^{*}(\{a\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\subset X))

induced by inclusion, for some
a\ll 0
and

. In particular, although
i_{a,*}(\ind_{q}(D,\psi_{a}))=0
for every
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we nevertheless have
\ind_{q}(D,\psi_{a})\neq 0
for
a\ll 0
by Theorem
5.1
.

5.2
An excision property

Before proving Theorem
5.1
, we first introduce an alternative representative of
\ind_{q}(D,\psi)
which is equivalent to the original one but has the advantage of depending only on the geometry near
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
. This leads to an excision property of the quantitative partitioned index, which will be used to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Let
B
be the operator (
3.2
) or (
3.5
), depending on
n
. Given
b>0
, let
F_{b}=\frac{bB}{\sqrt{1+(bB)^{2}}}
as before.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.
Let
b=r=\frac{1}{A\delta}
.
There is a bounded operator
G_{\delta}
with propagation no more than
(A\delta^{2})^{-1}
, such that
F_{(A\delta)^{-1}}-G_{\delta}
is locally compact, and

\displaystyle\|F_{(A\delta)^{-1}}-G_{\delta}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi}e^{-\delta^{-1}};

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|[G_{\delta},\sqrt{\chi}]\|

\displaystyle\leq 12\delta^{1/2}+4e^{-\delta^{-1}};

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|(G_{\delta}^{2}-1)(1-\chi)\|

\displaystyle\leq\delta+4e^{-\delta^{-1}}.

(5.3)

Furthermore,
G_{\delta}^{2}-1
lies in
M_{2}(C^{*}(X))
if
n
is odd and in
C^{*}(X)
if
n
is even.

Proof.

The distributional Fourier transform of the function
x\mapsto x(1+x^{2})^{-1/2}
is

\xi\mapsto\frac{2}{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\,K_{1}(|\xi|),

where
\mathrm{sgn}
denotes the sign function and
K_{1}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. This function has a singularity at
\xi=0
, but is smooth and decays exponentially as
|\xi|\to\infty
.
Consequently,

F_{b}=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\,K_{1}(|\xi|)\,e^{i\xi bB}\,d\xi

in the weak operator topology (see Proposition 10.3.5 in
[
12
]
or Proposition D.2.3 in
[
28
]
).

For any
K\geq 2
, define the truncated operator

G_{b,K}\coloneqq F_{b}-\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{|\xi|\geq K}\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\,K_{1}(|\xi|)\,e^{i\xi bB}\,d\xi=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{-K}^{K}\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\,K_{1}(|\xi|)\,e^{i\xi bB}\,d\xi.

This operator has propagation at most
bK
.

Since
K_{1}
decays exponentially and satisfies

|K_{1}(\xi)|\leq e^{-|\xi|}\qquad(\xi\geq 2),

the tail integral is absolutely convergent, and

\left\|\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{|\xi|\geq K}\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\,K_{1}(|\xi|)\,e^{i\xi bB}\,d\xi\right\|\leq\frac{2}{\pi}e^{-K}.

This implies that

\|F_{b}-G_{b,K}\|\leq\frac{2}{\pi}e^{-K}
.
Analogously to the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we find that

\begin{split}\|[G_{b,K},\sqrt{\chi}]\|&\leq 12\,\frac{\sqrt{b}}{r\sqrt{A}}+4e^{-K};\\
\|(G_{b,K}^{2}-1)(1-\chi)\|&\leq\frac{1}{bA}+4e^{-K}.\end{split}

If
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and we choose
r=b=(A\delta)^{-1}
and
K=\delta^{-1}>2
, then the operator

G_{\delta}:=G_{(A\delta)^{-1},\delta^{-1}}
has properties (
5.1
)–(
5.3
).

The operator
G_{\delta}^{2}-1
has finite propagation. Let
f\in C_{0}(X)
.
Using compactness of
(F_{(A\delta)^{-1}}-G_{\delta})f
and
[F_{(A\delta)^{-1}},f]
(
[
12
, Lemma 10.6.4]
), we see that

(G_{\delta}^{2}-1)f\sim(F_{(A\delta)^{-1}}^{2}-1)f\sim 0.

where
\sim
denotes equality modulo compact operators. Similarly,
f(G_{\delta}^{2}-1)
is compact, so
G_{\delta}^{2}-1
is locally compact. Hence the last statement follows.
∎

Proposition 5.6

Assume the same hypotheses as in Theorem

and a representative of

Proof.

We first consider the case where

Let

,
b
,

and

be as in Lemma

Let

0&0\end{pmatrix}+\sqrt{\chi}\begin{pmatrix}-(1-U_{\delta}V_{\delta})^{2}&(2-U_{\delta}V_{\delta})U_{\delta}(1-V_{\delta}U_{\delta})\\
V_{\delta}(1-U_{\delta}V_{\delta})&(1-V_{\delta}U_{\delta})^{2}\end{pmatrix}\sqrt{\chi}.

(5.4)

By the last point in Lemma

Because

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

\rho(x)=\frac{\ell}{2}-\varepsilon_{1}+d_{g}(x,N),

so

and again

. So (
6.17
), and hence Theorem
2.5
, follows.

and Schoen–Yau
[

[

.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with

and
p,p^{\prime}\in M
. Assume that
\mathrm{Sc}_{g}\geq\kappa
. Then for any
d,\delta,\varepsilon>0
there exists a Riemannian manifold
(P^{n},g^{\prime})
, which one constructs by removing two
\delta
-balls near
p
and
p^{\prime}

, namely

P=(M\backslash(B_{\delta}(p)\cup B_{\delta}(p^{\prime},))\cup T,

such that

1.

\mathrm{Sc}_{g^{\prime}}\geq\kappa-\varepsilon,

2.

g
and
g^{\prime}
coincide on the
[\delta/2,\delta]
-annulus aroud
p
and
p^{\prime}
, and

3.

the diameter of
T
is
\leq C(d+\delta)
, where
C
is a universal constant.

Now
fix
n\geq 3
and
\delta>0
a small positive number. Consider the warped product metric

g_{\varphi}=dt^{2}+\varphi(t)^{2}g_{\mathrm{eu}}

on

. Its scalar curvature is

\mathrm{Sc}_{g_{\varphi}}=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)\varphi^{\prime 2}}{\varphi^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{\varphi}.

Take

\varphi(t)=\left(\cos\bigl(\frac{nt}{2}\bigr)\right)^{2/n},

and we obtain

\mathrm{Sc}_{g_{\varphi}}=n(n-1)>0.

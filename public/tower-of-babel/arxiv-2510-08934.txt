Abstract
We establish that valid Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} propositional inference admits reduction to Fibonacci-indexed witness equations. Specifically, modus ponens verification reduces to solving a linear Diophantine equation in O ⁡ ( M ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) ) O(M(\log n)) time, where M M denotes integer multiplication complexity. This reduction is transitive : tautology verification proceeds via Fibonacci index arithmetic, bypassing semantic evaluation entirely. The core discovery is a transitive closure principle in Φ \Phi -scaled space (Hausdorff dimension log Φ ⁡ 2 \log_{\Phi}2 ), where logical consequence corresponds to a search problem over Fibonacci arcs—a geometric invariant encoded in Zeckendorf representations. This yields a computational model wherein proof verification is achieved through arithmetic alignment rather than truth-functional analysis, preserving soundness while respecting incompleteness. The construction synthesizes Lovelace’s anticipation of symbolic computation (Note G) with the Turing-Church formalism, revealing a geometric interpretability of logic relative to a Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} or ω \omega -consistent theory. For indices

(Injectivity of

3.4 Decidability and Correctness

Proposition 3.6

(Polynomial-Time Decidability)
.

The predicate

is decidable in
O(\log b\cdot M(\log b))
bit-operations.

Proof.

We proceed in steps.

Step 1.
Extract Zeckendorf index set
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\}
from
b
via a greedy algorithm, e.g., see
[
Edmonds 1971
]
:
O(\log b\cdot M(\log b))
.

Step 2.
Verify

by computing
a

O(M(\log a))
.

Step 3.
For each
k\in I
:

•

Axiom check:
Decode
\varphi_{k}
(cost

per Proposition

) and pattern-match against schemes K, S,
\bot

O(\log k)
head-index comparisons via

•

MP check:

with

. Since

is sorted, test all pairs
(c,d)
with
c,d<k
(at most
O(|I|^{2})=O(\log^{2}b)
pairs). Each test computes
\rho(c,d)

and checks equality.

Total per index:
O(\log^{2}k+\log^{2}b\cdot M(\log k))=O(\log^{2}b\cdot M(\log b))
(since

Overall:

|I|\cdot O(\log^{2}b\cdot M(\log b))=O(\log b\cdot M(\log b))
(using
|I|=O(\log b)
).
∎

Proposition 3.7

(Soundness and Completeness)
.

For all
a\in\mathbb{N}
:

\varphi_{a}\text{ is a tautology}\iff\exists b\in\mathbb{N}\;W(a,b)=1.

(3.28)

Proof.

By case analysis on the biconditional, forward
(\Rightarrow)
, and
reverse
(\Leftarrow)
direction.

(
\Rightarrow
, Completeness):
Suppose
\varphi_{a}
is a tautology. By completeness of the Hilbert system, there exists a proof sequence
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}
with
\psi_{m}=\varphi_{a}
. Let
j_{k}=i(\operatorname{ind}(\psi_{k}))
be the head-index of each formula. By Theorem
2.5
, if
\psi_{k}
follows by MP from
\psi_{c},\psi_{d}
, then
j_{k}=3\max\{j_{c},j_{d}\}+3>j_{c},j_{d}
(strict inequality). Thus the sequence
j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}
contains distinct indices divisible by 3, hence non-consecutive when sorted. Define:

b=\sum_{k=1}^{m}F_{j_{k}}.

(3.29)

By construction,
i(b)=j_{m}=i(\operatorname{ind}(\varphi_{a}))=i(a)
, and each
j_{k}
corresponds to an axiom or MP step. Thus
W(a,b)=1
.

(
\Leftarrow
, Soundness):
If
W(a,b)=1
, then the Zeckendorf index set
I
of
b
encodes a sequence
(\varphi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\varphi_{i_{n}})
, where each formula is an axiom or follows by MP (Definition
3.1
). This is a valid Hilbert proof of
\varphi_{a}=\varphi_{i_{n}}
. By soundness of the Hilbert system,
\varphi_{a}
is a tautology.
∎

Proposition 3.8

(Witness Size Bound)
.

If
\varphi_{a}
has a shortest proof of length
m
, then there exists a witness
b
with
\log b=O(m)
.

Proof.

By the proof of Proposition

,
b=\sum_{k=1}^{m}F_{j_{k}}
where each
j_{k}\leq 3(m+1)
(since formulas in a proof of length
m
have size

b\leq m\cdot F_{3m+3}\sim m\cdot\Phi^{3m}=e^{O(m)},\quad\log b=O(m).

(3.30)

∎

Remark

4 Representation

4.1 Diophantine Constraints

Theorem 4.1

(Diophantine Tautology Predicate)
.

There exists a polynomial
P(a,\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{Z}[a,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
where each monomial has total degree
\leq 4
such that

\varphi_{a}\text{ is a tautology}\iff\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\;P(a,\mathbf{x})=0,

(4.31)

where
n=O(\ell\cdot\log\ell)
and
\ell=\lfloor\log_{2}a\rfloor+1
is the bit-length of
a
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
,
\varphi_{a}
is a tautology iff
\exists b\;W(a,b)=1
. We arithmetize
W
in five blocks of polynomial constraints:

1.

Zeckendorf decomposition of
b
: introduce
(y_{j},z_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
with

y_{1}<\cdots<y_{m},\quad y_{j+1}-y_{j}\geq 2,\quad Q_{F}(y_{j},z_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j})=0,\quad b=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j},

(4.32)

where
Q_{F}(n,z,\mathbf{u})
is the
\delta=4
polynomial satisfying of
Jones 1982

F_{n}=z\iff\exists\mathbf{u}\;Q_{F}(n,z,\mathbf{u})=0
.

2.

Head-index match
: decompose
a
into
(y^{\prime}_{k},z^{\prime}_{k},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{k})
and enforce
y_{m}=\max\{y^{\prime}_{k}\}
via Boolean variables
\beta_{k}\in\{0,1\}
:

y^{\prime}_{k}\leq y_{m}\;\forall k,\quad\sum_{k}\beta_{k}=1,\quad\beta_{k}(y_{m}-y^{\prime}_{k})=0\;\forall k,\quad\beta_{k}(\beta_{k}-1)=0\;\forall k.

(4.33)

All constraints are quadratic.

3.

Axiom checks
: for each
y_{j}
, express “
\varphi_{y_{j}}
is an axiom” via polynomials
\phi_{j,\mathsf{K}}
,
\phi_{j,\mathsf{S}}
,
\phi_{j,\bot}
encoding schemes K, S, and
\bot
. For example, scheme K requires

y_{j}=\rho(c,d)\land d=\rho(e,c),

(4.34)

which expands to polynomial equations in
(c,d,e)
using
Q_{F}
. Each
\phi_{j,\cdot}
has degree
\leq 4
.

4.

MP checks
: for each
y_{j}
, enforce modus ponens via

y_{j}=\rho(c,d),\quad c,d\in\{y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1}\},

(4.35)

encoding membership by Boolean variables
\epsilon_{i,c},\epsilon_{i,d}\in\{0,1\}
:

\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\epsilon_{i,c}(c-y_{i})^{2}=0,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\epsilon_{i,c}\geq 1,\quad\epsilon_{i,c}(\epsilon_{i,c}-1)=0,

(4.36)

and similarly for
d
. All constraints are quadratic.

5.

Global conjunction
: for each
y_{j}
, combine axiom and MP conditions via Boolean
\alpha_{j}\in\{0,1\}
:

\alpha_{j}\cdot\phi_{j,\text{axiom}}+(1-\alpha_{j})\cdot\phi_{j,\text{MP}}=0,\quad\alpha_{j}(\alpha_{j}-1)=0.

(4.37)

Enforce all constraints simultaneously by

P(a,\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi_{i}\text{ of}\\
\text{degree }\leq 2\end{subarray}}\phi_{i}^{2}\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi_{\ell}\text{ of}\\
\text{degree }4\end{subarray}}\psi_{\ell},

(4.38)

where the
\psi_{\ell}
are the
Q_{F}
-constraints and any unsquared degree-2 relations.

Hence
\deg P=\max(2\cdot 2,1\cdot 4)=4
and
n=O(m\cdot k)=O(\ell\cdot\log\ell)
, where
m=O(\ell)
is the proof length and
k=O(1)
is the number of auxiliary variables per
Q_{F}
instance.
∎

4.2 Characterisation

Proposition 4.2

(Complexity Class)
.

The set
\{a\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\mathbf{x}\;P(a,\mathbf{x})=0\}
coincides with the set of (encodings of) propositional tautologies, hence is coNP-complete and decidable in time
2^{O(\ell)}
.

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
4.1
and the coNP-completeness of propositional tautology
[
Cook 1971
]
. The Diophantine encoding does not yield a polynomial-time algorithm in
\ell
, since witness size is exponential in
\ell
.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Witness Recovery)
.

Given a solution
\mathbf{x}^{*}
to
P(a,\mathbf{x})=0
, one recovers a proof of
\varphi_{a}
in time

O\bigl(|\mathbf{x}^{*}|\cdot M(\log|\mathbf{x}^{*}|)\bigr),

(4.39)

where

denotes the cost of multiplying two
t

Proof.

Extract the Zeckendorf indices

from
\mathbf{x}^{*}
(the variables satisfying constraint block 1). For each

Decode
\varphi_{y_{j}}

2.

3.

Impact of Microfluidic Sperm Sorting on Embryonic Euploidy in Infertile Patients with Sperm DNA Damage: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Sandra Lara-Cerrillo, Ana Raquel Jiménez-Macedo, Olga Hortal, Candela Rosado-Iglesias, Tania Lacruz-Ruiz, Joan Carrera, Agustín García-Peiró
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Sperm DNA fragmentation is an important factor that affects male fertility. This study intends to evaluate the impact of sperm DNA damage [single-strand breaks (SSB) and double-strand breaks (DSB)] on fertilisation and embryonic euploidy after intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). Of the different sperm selection techniques, the novel microfluidic sperm sorting (MSS) ZyMōt™ ICSI device reduces both SSB and DSB in semen samples. The second objective is to study the impact of this MSS device on fertilisation and embryonic euploidy.Materials and Methods:This retrospective study included data from 167 ICSI treatments. The alkaline and neutral Comet assays were used to analyse SSB and DSB, respectively. Analysis of fertilisation and embryo euploidy rates was performed in four groups of patients with normal/altered SSB or DSB values. Density gradient centrifugation (DGC) and the ZyMōt™ ICSI MSS device were used for semen preparation.Results:Fertilisation rates in 167 ICSI cycles were higher when using sperm from patients with normal SSB values (70.84%) and patients with abnormal SSB whose samples were processed using the MSS device (69.71%) compared to patients with abnormal SSB values using DGC (58.49%). Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidies (PGTA) revealed a higher embryo euploidy rate in patients with normal DSB values (60.00%) and patients with abnormal DSB whose samples were processed using the MSS device (44.59%) compared to patients with abnormal DSB values using DGC (36.84%). When female age was considered, there were fewer euploid embryos in women ≥35 years of age compared to younger women, independently of SSB and DSB. The number of euploid embryos increased when the MSS device was used.Conclusion:High SSB and DSB values in semen samples decreased fertilisation rates and embryonic euploidy, respectively. The ZyMōt™ ICSI device for semen preparation increased both rates, especially in couples that included women <35 years old.

Introduction

Sperm DNA damage is an important factor that affects male fertility. There are two types of sperm DNA breaks that are related to different clinical effects (1). Singlestrand breaks (SSB) are a key factor during fertilisation (2, 3) and have been shown to decrease the chances of obtaining a natural conception or a pregnancy after intrauterine insemination (4, 5). This is an extensive damage that appears throughout the entire genome and is mainly produced by reactive oxygen species (ROS) (6). ROS can also cause membrane damage that ultimately impairs sperm motility and viability (7). Double-strand breaks (DSB) in sperm DNA are associated with a higher risk of miscarriage and lower embryo kinetics. DSB damage, unlike SSB damage, has never been related to fertilisation rate (8-10). This damage can be produced during meiosis in the seminiferous tubule epithelium through the action of the meiotic recombination protein Spo11 to allow structural processes such as homologous recombination and the hypercondensation of sperm DNA (11). DSB can be likewise produced at the post-testicular level following activation of sperm endonucleases (12).

Early pregnancy loss can also be related to aneuploidies in the embryo (13). Aneuploidies related to the oocyte increase in females over the age of 35 and may be attributed to an incorrect segregation process (14). DSB in sperm can also lead to embryonic aneuploidies or chromosome structural variations that might be tolerated by the early embryo and allow fertilisation to occur (15). However, the effect of genetic abnormalities might appear during embryo development after D+3 post-insemination, and lead to errors in chromosome segregation and slower embryo kinetics (10, 16). Embryos can be analysed by preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidies (PGT-A) to detect the presence of chromosomal abnormalities before they are transferred (17).

Sperm selection methods capable of reducing SSB and DSB might be of great interest because of the reported deleterious effects of sperm DNA damage on embryonic development and euploidy (18). The results of a recent study show that conventional methods (swim-up) significantly reduce SSB values in semen samples. It was also reported that the probability of selecting a spermatozoon with SSB damage during intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) is highly reduced while selecting a motile sperm. In sharp contrast, DSB values were not reduced after the swim-up method nor after the selection of sperm with progressive motility (19).

New microfluidic sperm sorting (MSS) devices such as the ZyMōt™ ICSI device (also known as FertileChip, DxNow, Inc., Gaithersburg, MD, USA) have recently emerged. Different studies have reported that this device selects motile sperm with low values of sperm DNA damage (both SSB and DSB) (20, 21). Moreover, this device significantly improved reproductive outcome after ICSI in patients with high DSB values in sperm DNA (22).

The objective of the present study is to analyse the effects of SSB and DSB, as well as the effect of the MSS device, on fertilisation rates and embryonic euploidy. This is the first study to analyse these effects related to SSB and DSB, independently.

Materials and Methods

Samples

This retrospective study included data from 167 consecutive ICSI treatments conducted at the UEG Fertility Centre (Unitat d’Endocrinologia Ginecològica, Vic, Spain) from January 2017 to December 2018. All semen samples were suitable to perform semen preparation techniques and sperm selection for ICSI. The conventional density gradient centrifugation (DGC) method was used to process the first 70 semen samples. The following 97 semen samples were processed through the ZyMōt™ ICSI MSS device. Couples were excluded from the study if they had infertility problems other than sperm DNA damage or advanced female age (23). Patients who agreed to participate and met the inclusion criteria signed informed consent forms and were included in the study. The Corporació Sanitaria Parc Taulí Ethics Committee approved this study (Ref.: 2017902).

In order to assess fertilisation rates, groups were divided according to SSB values, as follows: male patients in groups A and B showed normal SSB values (<45%,internal cut-off value) while patients in groups C and D showed high SSB values (≥45%). In order to study embryo euploidy rate, groups were divided according to DSB values, as follows: patients in groups 1 and 2 showed normal DSB values (<60%, internal cut-off value) while patients in groups 3 and 4 showed high DSB values (≥60%). Semen samples were processed using the DGC method in groups A, C, 1 and 3, while the MSS device was employed in groups B, D, 2 and 4.

The Comet assay in alkaline and neutral pH conditions (CometFertility<sup>TM</sup> test, CIMAB, Spain) was used to detect SSB and DSB, respectively (4). Briefly, samples were mixed with low-melting-point agarose (1%, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Then, the samples were fixed on slides and treated in two consecutive lysis solutions. After a denaturing treatment, alkaline electrophoresis was conducted in 0.03M NaOH buffer, pH=12.2 (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for 4 minutes at 20 V. Neutral electrophoresis was conducted in tris-borate-EDTA buffer, pH=8.5 (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for 12.5 minutes at 20V. Slides were immersed in a 0.4M Trizma base solution (pH=7.5; Sigma-Aldrich, USA) after electrophoresis. Finally, the samples were dehydrated in ethanol (70, 90 and 100%). DNA was stained using Diamond Antifade Mountant with DAPI (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). A total of 200 spermatozoa per sample were categorised as normal/fragmented based on their tail length to obtain the final SSB and DSB values (Fig .1).

Assessment of sperm DNA fragmentation using the alkaline Comet assay to detect single-strand breaks (SSB) and the neutral Comet assay to detect double-strand breaks (DSB). Normal sperm DNA (non-fragmented), sperm with fragmented DNA and sperm with highly fragmented (degraded) DNA are shown.

Ovarian stimulation and oocyte retrieval

Ovarian stimulation was achieved by follicle-stimulating hormone injections. Luteinising hormone and/or urine gonadotropins were added depending on the patient’s age, basal hormonal values, body mass index and ovarian reserve. Gonadotropin-releasing hormone antagonists were used for ovarian maturation with short protocols. Ovulation was triggered using an agonist about 34-36 hours before oocyte pick-up. The retrieved oocytes were washed (MOPS Plus, Vitrolife, Gothenburg, Sweden), denuded and cultured in fertilisation medium (G-IVF, Vitrolife, Sweden). Finally, the oocytes were cultured inside an incubator for 2-3 hours at 5% O2 , 7.2% CO2 , and 37°C (KSystems, CooperSurgical, Inc., Trumbull, CT, USA).

Semen preparation

Semen samples were obtained by masturbation after 1-2 days of sexual abstinence.

For groups A, C, 1 and 3, semen preparation was performed using DGC. This method consisted of two different layers of ISolate Sperm Separation Medium (40 and 80%, FUJIFILM Irvine Scientific, Inc., Santa Ana, CA, USA). The samples were centrifuged at 80g for 20 minutes. The obtained pellet was placed in another tube that contained 5 mL of washing medium and centrifuged again at 100 g for 10 minutes. The final pellet was resuspended in washing medium to obtain a proper sperm concentration for ICSI.

For groups B, D, 2 and 4, semen preparation was performed using the MSS device (ZyMōt™ ICSI). Each of the five channels was filled with 13 µL of washing medium. From 1-2.5 µL of the semen sample was injected through the inlet port, depending on the sperm concentration. Inlet and outlet ports were sealed using mineral oil and the device were heated at 37°C for 20-30 minutes. Finally, 2 µL was obtained from the outlet port of each channel that contained the sperm with low sperm DNA damage (21).

Oocyte fertilisation and embryo culture

The ICSI procedure began four hours after oocyte retrieval. Microinjected oocytes were placed on GTL medium (Vitrolife, Sweden) inside a Primo Vision slide (Vitrolife, Sweden) and cultured inside an incubator for 5-6 days at 5% O2 , 7.2% CO2 , and 37°C. Fertilisation was confirmed when two pronuclei (2PN) were observed after 16-22 hours post-insemination. Fertilisation rate was calculated as the number of 2PN oocytes/number of oocytes microinjected ×100. Embryonic morphology and development were evaluated according to official Spanish guidelines from the ASEBIR association (Asociación para el Estudio de la Biología de la Reproducción). Blastocysts were vitrified using Freezing medium (FUJIFILM, Irvine Scientific, Inc.) or were biopsied for PGT-A before vitrification. Blastocysts were stored in liquid nitrogen.

Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidies

Indications to evaluate embryonic euploidy were: previous implantation failure or miscarriage, advanced maternal age or patient’s choice. Assisted hatching was performed on D+3 post-insemination to facilitate a biopsy on D+5 or D+6. A hole in the zona pellucida was made using a laser (RI Saturn 5TM, CooperSurgical) and 4-6 trophectoderm cells were removed. An external laboratory carried out the PGT-A. Embryonic euploidy was confirmed when PGT-A reported a normal number of chromosomes (n=46). Embryos with an abnormal number of chromones were considered aneuploids. Embryo euploidy rate was calculated as the number of euploid blastocysts/number of total blastocysts analysed ×100.

Embryo thawing and transfer

Blastocysts were thawed and cultured in GTL medium for 2-3 hours at 5% O2 , 7.2% CO2 , and 37°C. The embryos were placed inside the uterus within the implantation window determined by the luteinising hormone peak and the endometrium thickness.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests were used to assess normality. Groups were defined according to SSB or DSB values and women’s age. Differences between groups were assessed by the MannWhitney U test. The statistical analysis was conducted at a 95% confidence level using SPPS software (BMI SPSS Statistics version 20, USA).

Results

Fertilisation rate and single-strand breaks

SSB values did not show significant differences between groups A and B (P=0.07) and between groups C and D (P=0.49). SSB values showed significant differences between groups A-B (normal SSB values) and groups C-D (abnormal SSB values, P<0.001 in all cases, Table 1).

Group C (abnormal SSB+DGC) showed a decrease in fertilisation rate of 12.35% compared to group A (normal SSB+DGC, P=0.03) and 13.34% compared to group B (normal SSB+MSS, P=0.00). Group D (abnormal SSB+MSS) did not show statistical differences compared to groups A (P=0.66) and B (P=0.56) and showed a significant 11.22% increase compared to group C (P=0.03, Table 1).

Euploidy rates and double-strand breaks

DSB values did not show significant differences between groups 1 and 2 (P=0.81) and between groups 3 and 4 (P=0.55). DSB values showed significant differences between groups 1-2 (normal DSB values) and groups 3-4 (abnormal DSB values, P<0.001 in all cases, Table 2).

PGT-A was performed on 148 embryos from all groups. Group 3 (abnormal DSB+DGC) showed a decrease in embryo euploidy rate of 23.16% compared to group 1 (normal DSB+DGC, P=0.02) and 25.66% compared to group 2 (normal DSB+MSS, P=0.04). Group 4 (abnormal DSB+MSS) did not show significant differences compared to groups 1 (P=0.39) and 2 (P=0.67) and showed a 7.75% increase in embryo euploidy rate compared to group 3, although these differences were not statistically significant (P=0.90, Table 2).

Embryonic euploidy was also assessed taking into consideration female age and DSB values (Table 3). Although only a few cases could be included, women aged <35 years whose male partners had normal DSB values (n=3) had a 4-fold higher euploidy rate compared to women aged ≥35 years (n=4) (P=0.04). Men with abnormal DSB values whose semen samples were subjected to DGC (n=3) had a 3-fold lower embryo euploidy rate compared to patients with normal values, although female age was <35 years in both cases (P=0.03). In couples where female age was ≥35 years and the men presented with altered DSB values (n=14), a 1.32-fold lower embryo euploidy rate was observed compared to couples were men had normal DSB values (P=0.06). When men had altered DSB values, the MSS device increased euploidy rate by 4.20-fold in couples where female age was <35 years (n=5, P=0.01) and by 1.17-fold in couples where female age was ≥35 years (n=21, P=0.01) compared to couples where semen samples were subjected to DGC (Table 3).

Discussion

An important finding of these results is the relevance of discriminating between SSB and DSB when studying fertilisation rates and embryonic euploidy (1, 19). The beneficial impact of the ZyMōt™ ICSI MSS device has been also related to its efficiency in selecting sperm with reduced SSB and DSB levels.

Previous reports that assessed fertilisation rates according to the sperm DNA fragmentation index used techniques that mainly measure SSB (2-4, 24). In agreement with these investigations, men who presented with high SSB values had a 12.35% reduction in fertilisation rates compared to men with normal values. This result indicates that embryos derived from sperm with high SSB values may have difficulties in progressing from the pronuclear stage.

Selection of motile sperm by DGC not always reduces SSB values maybe because of the centrifugation steps in this method (22, 25, 26). In contrast, the ZyMōt™ ICSI device selects sperm with progressive motility and nearly undetectable SSB values avoiding centrifugation steps (20, 21, 27). Consistently, the results of this study show an increase in fertilisation rates (11.22%) in patients with abnormal SSB values after the use of the MSS device compared to those couples in which DGC was used. This fertilisation rate was similar when patients presented with normal SSB values.

Aneuploidies in embryos are studied using PGT-A. The results in the present study show a reduction of 23.16% in embryo euploidy rate in patients with high DSB values compared to patients with normal values. This is in good agreement with previous reports showing the relationship between poor embryo kinetics, implantation failures and recurrent miscarriages to abnormal DSB values (1, 10).

Recently, our group reported that methods used to prepare semen samples based on sperm motility (such as swim-up or DGC) and the selection process of motile sperm by embryologists during ICSI reduced SSB values, but not DSB. Therefore, the main DNA damage present in sperm used for ICSI is DSB (19). Complementary maternal DNA chains are not available for the repair process of the paternal DSB since both pronuclei are separated after fertilisation. Consequently, the attempt to repair DSB in paternal DNA is error-prone and might lead to rearrangements or chromosomal aneuploidies in embryos (28).

In the present study, we evaluated the impact of the ZyMōt™ ICSI device on embryonic euploidy because it is currently the only processing method to show an efficient reduction in DSB values (21). Men presenting with high DSB values showed an increase of 7.75% in embryonic euploidy when the MSS device was used compared to those cases using DGC. Embryonic euploidy was statistically similar between group 1 (normal DSB) and group 4 (altered DSB+MSS) although the latter had a lower rate. Despite the encouraging results related to the MSS methodology, this device cannot absolutely select all sperm with normal DSB. A small number of sperm with DSB escape the selection process and these can be used for ICSI (20, 21). If paternal DSB are not properly repaired after fertilisation by the oocyte’s polymerase machinery, the embryo could present with aneuploidies or rearrangements related to the paternal genome (15).

The impact of women’s age on embryonic euploidy was also studied. Although there were only a few cases, we observed a 4-fold lower embryo euploidy rate when DSB values were normal and female age was ≥35 years compared to couples where the female age was <35 years. This female factor is consistent with reports that show an increase in oocyte aneuploidies after age 35 years. Couples in which female age was <35 years and men had high DSB values showed a 3-fold lower embryo euploidy rate compared to couples with normal DSB values. This reduction could be related to a paternal factor. Interestingly, embryo euploidy rate did not show significant differences between those cases with only a female factor (related to maternal age) and those cases with only a male factor (related to high DSB values). Based on the obtained results, these female and male factors might have a similar impact on embryonic euploidy. In agreement, the euploidy rate was 5.25-fold lower in couples with combined female and male factors compared to couples with female age <35 years and normal DSB values. This might be related to a major number of aneuploidies in oocytes after age 35 years and also to a lower capacity of the oocyte machinery to repair a high number of DSB in the paternal DNA (13, 28).

Euploid blastocyst number increased 4.2-fold and 1.17- fold when the MSS was used in samples from patients with abnormal DSB values compared to DGC in couples where female age was <35 and ≥35 years, respectively. These increases are consistent with the expected reduction in DSB when using the ZyMōt™ ICSI device. Again, these results also show an expected higher number of aneuploidies and lower repair capacity in oocytes related to female age.

The major limitation of the present study is that clinical outcomes such as implantation and ongoing pregnancy rates could not be evaluated since multiple confounding variables would have to be considered. Although the transfer of euploid embryos might decrease miscarriage rates (29), the current literature is insufficient to confirm the pregnancy success rates after PGT-A. In these cases, multiple factors could cause a miscarriage (genetic, epigenetic, anatomical, immunological or endocrine) (29, 30). However, a recent publication from our group showed consistently better clinical outcomes in ICSI treatments after using the MSS device for patients who presented with altered DSB values (22).

Conclusion

As far as we are concerned, this is the first study to separately assess the impact of SSB on fertilisation rates and the impact of DSB on embryonic euploidy. For this purpose, the Comet assay was used to distinguish both types of DNA damage.

The results presented in this study revealed that sperm DNA with high values of SSB led to significantly lower fertilisation rates, while high DSB values led to significantly lower embryo euploidy rates. The decrease in embryonic euploidy was even more apparent when female age was ≥35 years.

The application of the ZyMōt™ ICSI MSS methodology to reduce the percentage of sperm with SSB and DSB led to a significant increase in fertilisation rates. The use of this MSS device also increased embryo euploidy rates, especially when female age was <35 years, and showed a partial increase when female age was ≥35 years.

New bounds for (weak) sequenceability in ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k}
Simone Costa, Stefano Della Fiore

Abstract
. A famous conjecture of Graham asserts that every set A ⊆ ℤ p ∖ { 0 } A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\} can be ordered so that all partial sums are distinct. Although this conjecture was recently proved for sufficiently large primes by Pham and Sauermann in [ 16 ] , it remains open for general abelian groups, even in the cyclic case ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} . For cyclic groups, the best known result is due to Bedert and Kravitz in [ 4 ] , who proved - using a rectification and a two-step probabilistic approach - that the conjecture holds for any subset A ⊆ ℤ k ∖ { 0 } A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{k}\setminus\{0\} such that | A | ≤ exp ⁡ ( c ​ ( log ⁡ p ) 1 / 4 ) , |A|\leq\exp\!\big(c(\log p)^{1/4}\big), for some constant c > 0 c>0 , where p p denotes the least prime divisor of k k . In this paper, we improve their bound using a rectification argument again, followed by a one-shot probabilistic approach, showing that the conjecture holds whenever | A | ≤ exp ⁡ ( c ​ ( log ⁡ p ) 1 / 3 ) , |A|\leq\exp\!\big(c(\log p)^{1/3}\big), thus improving the exponent 1 / 4 1/4 from [ 4 ] . Moreover, the same one-shot approach adapts to the t t -weak setting: by imposing all local constraints at once and applying the Lovász Local Lemma, we obtain the existence of a t t -weak sequencing whenever t ≤ exp ⁡ ( c ​ ( log ⁡ p ) 1 / 4 ) . t\leq\exp\!\big(c(\log p)^{1/4}\big).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{k}\setminus\{0\}
, and let us denote by
p
the least prime divisor of
k
.
Apply the rectification/structure step (as in
[
4
]
) with a choice of parameters that yields dissociated blocks of size

R=R(A,k):=c_{1}\max\left((\log p)^{1/2},\frac{\log p}{\log|A|}\right)\,.

We recall that the Structure Theorem of
[
4
]
holds for this value of
R
(the previous definition of

was functional to the weak-sequenceability result).
Here we run the one-shot construction of Lemma
2.7
with

K:=c_{2}R^{1/2}\qquad(c_{2}>0\ \text{sufficiently small}).

In the classical setting, we use a union bound over all nontrivial intervals
I\subset[1,|A|]
.
There are at most
|A|^{2}

2.7
(1) gives

\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}=0\Big)\ \leq\ \exp\!\big(-\Theta(K\log R)\big)\ =\ \exp\!\big(-\Theta(R^{1/2}\log R)\big).

\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}=0\Big)\ \leq\ \exp\!\big(-\Theta(R)\big).

Hence

\mathbb{P}(\exists\ \text{nontrivial }I:\ \sum_{i\in I}a_{i}=0)\ \leq\ |A|^{2}\exp\!\big(-\Theta(R^{1/2}\log R)\big)\ +\ |A|^{2}\exp\!\big(-\Theta(R)\big).

p

c
small enough so that
\exp\!\big(c(\log p)^{1/3}\big)<2
for any prime
p<\bar{p}
.
This proves the theorem.

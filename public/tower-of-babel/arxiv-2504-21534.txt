Topological classification and edge states of magnons in honeycomb ferromagnets
Youwen Wang, Qiutong Wang, Qingjun Tong, Ci Li

Abstract
We study the topological classification and related edge states of magnons in ferromagnets on honeycomb that can be described by a class of single-particle bosonic Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) models. Both single layer and bilayer situations are considered. The calculations show that the existence and related topologies of these edge states are well captured by a class of non-Hermitian single or coupled Su-Schrieffer-Heeger chains models H ⁡ ( k y ) H(k_{y}) parameterized by momentum k y k_{y} , where the edge states can appear as the ground state for some cases. Interestingly, although the eigenproblem of bosonic BdG models is equivalent to the one of non-Hermitian systems, the conventional bulk-edge correspondence for Hermitian systems is partially valid. The influence of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions between next nearest-neighbor spins are also discussed, which break the time-reversal symmetry and lead to a straight connection between edge states for magnonic systems and non-zero Chern number of non-Hermitian bulk two-dimensional systems.

Other terms may exist in the Heisenberg Hamiltonian

For a general FM or AFM system, the description based on the Heisenberg

(12)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{g\mu_{B}}{\hbar}B_{z}\sum_{i}\left(S-f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}\right),\text{spin up }\left(+z\right)\\
-\frac{g\mu_{B}}{\hbar}B_{z}\sum_{i}\left(S-f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}\right),\text{spin down }\left(-z\right)\end{array}\right.,

III.1
Bulk BdG Hamiltonian

According to above analysis, the BdG Hamiltonian after the HP transformation
for monolayer ferromagnets on honeycomb lattices is

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{FM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3MNJS^{2}

(36)

\displaystyle+SJ\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}[3\left(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m,n}\right)

\displaystyle-(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m,n}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n+1}

\displaystyle+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n+1}+H.c.)]

\displaystyle+H_{\mathrm{DM}}+H_{\mathrm{ON}}+H_{\mathrm{Z}},

with

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{DM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle SDi\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n+1}+a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle-a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n+1})+\mathrm{H.c.}-\left(a\longleftrightarrow b\right),

H_{\mathrm{ON}}=MN\mathcal{K}S^{2}-2S\mathcal{K}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m,n}\right),

H_{\mathrm{Z}}=\frac{g\mu_{B}}{\hbar}B_{z}\left[MNS-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m,n}\right)\right],

which comes from the assumption that the system is infinite (periodic) along
both
\vec{a}_{1}
and
\vec{a}_{2}
directions in Fig.
2
(a). Here
H_{\mathrm{DM}}
represents the NNN DMI,
H_{\mathrm{ON}}
describes the
easy-axis anisotropic term, and
H_{\mathrm{Z}}
means the Zeeman term.

In the rest of the paper, we ignore the constant term such as
-3MNJS^{2}

in Eq. (
36
) for simplicity since it cannot influence the properties of
the system. Taking the Fourier transformation

f_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{m,n=1}^{M,N}e^{-i\left(nk_{x}+mk_{y}\right)}f_{m,n},f=a,b,

(37)

the Hamiltonian in
\boldsymbol{k}
space can be written as

\displaystyle H\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}J\{3\sum_{f=a,b}f_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\left[1+e^{-ik_{x}}+e^{-i\left(k_{x}+k_{y}\right)}\right]a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{\boldsymbol{k}}

(38)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{H.c.}\}+\frac{1}{2}\left[H_{\mathrm{DM}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)+H_{\mathrm{ON}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)+H_{\mathrm{Z}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\boldsymbol{k}\leftrightarrow-\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{\dagger}H_{BdG}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\eta_{k},\eta_{k}=\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}},b_{\boldsymbol{k}},a_{-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger},b_{-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}\right)^{T},

with

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{DM}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2SD\left[\sin\left(k_{x}+k_{y}\right)-\sin k_{x}-\sin k_{y}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}-b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right),

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{ON}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2S\mathcal{K}\sum_{f=a,b}f_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{\boldsymbol{k}},

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{Z}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g\mu_{B}}{\hbar}B_{z}\sum_{f=a,b}f_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{\boldsymbol{k}}.

This causes

\displaystyle H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{bea}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)&0\\
0&h^{\ast}\left(-\boldsymbol{k}\right)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\left[\gamma\mathit{I}_{2}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta&-\alpha\\
-\alpha^{\ast}&-\beta\end{array}\right)\right],

where

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\left[1+e^{-ik_{x}}+e^{-i\left(k_{x}+k_{y}\right)}\right],

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2D\left[\sin\left(k_{x}+k_{y}\right)-\sin k_{x}-\sin k_{y}\right],

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3J-2\mathcal{K}-\frac{g\mu_{B}}{S\hbar}B_{z},

bea means the bearded boundary [Fig.
2
(a)] we considered here. The
straightforward calculation shows the eigenenergies and eigenstates of
h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)
can be expressed as

\varepsilon_{\pm}=\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}\right)=\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma\pm\varepsilon\right),

(45)

\displaystyle\left|u_{+}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\beta+\varepsilon\\
-\alpha^{\ast}\end{array}\right\},

\displaystyle\left|u_{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha\\
\beta+\varepsilon\end{array}\right\},

with
\Omega=2\varepsilon\left(\varepsilon+\beta\right)
is the
normalization factor. Combining with the symmetry analysis in Table
1
, the Berry curvature of these two magnon bands can be calculated as

\Omega_{n}^{k_{x}k_{y}}=i\sum_{l\neq n}\left(\mathcal{R}_{n,l}^{k_{x}}\mathcal{R}_{l,n}^{k_{y}}-\mathcal{R}_{n,l}^{k_{y}}\mathcal{R}_{l,n}^{k_{x}}\right),

where the Berry connection
\mathcal{R}_{n,l}^{k_{\alpha}}\equiv\left\langle u_{n}\right|\partial_{k_{\alpha}}\left|u_{l}\right\rangle
,
\alpha=x,y
and
n,l=\pm
. One can easily find that

\displaystyle C_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C_{+}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{BZ^{d}}\Omega_{-}^{k_{x}k_{y}}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{k}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{=}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,D<0\\
0,D=0\\
-1,D>0\end{array}\right.,

which is a
\mathbb{Z}
topological invariant and independent with
J
. For the BdG Hamiltonian
H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{zig}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)
represented in the
bosonic honeycomb lattice shown in Fig.
2
(b), the same bulk
Hamiltonian can be found after the gauge transformation. The equivalence of
the lattice structures under PBC shown in lower panel of Figs.
2
(a)
and (b) also exhibits this point.

However, for the situation in Fig.
2
(c), i.e., the bosonic
honeycomb lattice with the armchair boundary, we have

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{FM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle SJ\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}[3(a_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m-1,n}

(52)

\displaystyle+b_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m-1,n}+a_{2m,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m,n}

\displaystyle+b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m,n})-b_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}\left(a_{2m-1,n}+a_{2m,n}\right)

\displaystyle-b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}\left(a_{2m,n}+a_{2m+1,n}\right)

\displaystyle-a_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m-1,n+1}-b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m,n+1}

\displaystyle+H.c.]+H_{\mathrm{DM}}+H_{\mathrm{ON}}+H_{\mathrm{Z}},

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{DM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle SDi\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}[a_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m,n}-a_{2m,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m+1,n}

\displaystyle+a_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m-1,n+1}+a_{2m,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m,n+1}

\displaystyle-a_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}a_{2m,n+1}+b_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m,n}

\displaystyle-b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m+1,n}-(b_{2m-1,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m-1,n+1}

\displaystyle+b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m,n+1}-b_{2m,n}^{\dagger}b_{2m-1,n+1})+H.c.],

since the minimum repetitive unit includes four sites instead of two sites.
Still ignoring the constant term, the Fourier transformation gives

\displaystyle H\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}J[3\sum_{f=a,b}\left(f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}+f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right)

(53)

\displaystyle-\left(1+e^{ik_{x}}\right)a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle-\left(1+e^{-ik_{x}}\right)a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}-a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle-e^{-ik_{y}}a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}+H.c.]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[H_{\mathrm{DM}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)+H_{\mathrm{ON}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)+H_{\mathrm{Z}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\boldsymbol{k}\leftrightarrow-\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{\dagger}H_{BdG}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\eta_{k},\eta=\left(\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}},\xi_{-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}\right)^{T},

with
\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left(a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}},b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}},a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}},b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right)
,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{DM}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2SD\sin k_{x}\left(a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}+a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+SDi\left[\left(1+e^{-ik_{y}}-e^{ik_{x}}\right)a_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}a_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right]+H.c.

\displaystyle+2SD\sin k_{x}\left(b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}+b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+SDi\left[\left(1+e^{-ik_{y}}-e^{-ik_{x}}\right)b_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}b_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right]+H.c.,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{ON}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2S\mathcal{K}\sum_{f=a,b}\left(f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}+f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right),

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{Z}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{g\mu_{B}}{\hbar}B_{z}\sum_{f=a,b}\left(f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{1,\boldsymbol{k}}+f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}f_{2,\boldsymbol{k}}\right),

leading to

\displaystyle H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{arm}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)&0\\
0&h^{\ast}\left(-\boldsymbol{k}\right)\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\left[\gamma\mathit{I}_{4}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\beta&-\alpha&\lambda_{1}&-Je^{-ik_{y}}\\
-\alpha^{\ast}&\beta&-J&\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{1}^{\ast}&-J&-\beta&-\alpha^{\ast}\\
-Je^{ik_{y}}&\lambda_{2}^{\ast}&-\alpha&\beta\end{array}\right)\right],

with

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\left(1+e^{ik_{x}}\right),\beta=2D\sin k_{x},

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Di\left[\left(1+e^{-ik_{y}}-e^{ik_{x}}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Di\left[\left(1+e^{-ik_{y}}-e^{-ik_{x}}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3J-2\mathcal{K}-\frac{g\mu_{B}}{S\hbar}B_{z}.

(62)

From the lattice structure under PBC shown in lower panel of Fig.
2
(c), it is obvious that the bulk Hamiltonian with the armchair boundary is

y

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma-\varepsilon_{\pm}\right),\varepsilon_{3,4}=\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma+\varepsilon_{\pm}\right),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(2\cos\frac{k_{x}}{2}\pm\cos\frac{k_{y}}{2}\right)^{2}+\sin^{2}\frac{k_{y}}{2}}.

\displaystyle-\left(C_{3}+C_{4}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{BZ^{d}}\left(\Omega_{1}^{k_{x}k_{y}}+\Omega_{2}^{k_{x}k_{y}}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{k}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,D<0\\
-1,D>0\end{array}\right.,

, respectively.
Interestingly, the edge states appear with the lowest or highest energy

cases
[
51
,
52
]
. Taking the bosonic honeycomb lattice with the bearded
boundary [Fig.

(a)] as an example, the 2D BdG Hamiltonian of this
lattice can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{FM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle SJ\sum_{m=1}^{M}\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}[3\left(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m,n}\right)

(64)

\displaystyle-a_{m,n}^{\dagger}b_{m.n}]-2\left(a_{m,1}^{\dagger}a_{m,1}+b_{m,N}^{\dagger}b_{m,N}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(b_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n+1}+b_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n+1}\right)+H.c.\}

\displaystyle+H_{\mathrm{DM}}+H_{\mathrm{ON}}+H_{\mathrm{Z}},

where the term
-2SJ\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(a_{m,1}^{\dagger}a_{m,1}+b_{m,N}^{\dagger}b_{m,N}\right)
coms from the boundary condition
since there is only one bond connect these sites in the boundary with the
bulk sites.

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{DM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle SDi\sum_{m=1}^{M}[\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m,n+1}-a_{m,n}^{\dagger}a_{m+1,n+1}\right)]+H.c.

\displaystyle-\left(a\longleftrightarrow b\right).

and
H_{\mathrm{ON}}
and
H_{\mathrm{Z}}
have the same expression as in
Eq. (
36
). This gives

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{bea}}\left(k_{y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}[\gamma\left(a_{k_{y},n}^{\dagger}a_{k_{y},n}+b_{k_{y},n}^{\dagger}b_{k_{y},n}\right)

\displaystyle-Ja_{k_{y},n}^{\dagger}b_{k_{y},n}]

\displaystyle-J\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\left(1+e^{-ik_{y}}\right)a_{k_{y},n+1}^{\dagger}b_{k_{y},n}+H.c.

\displaystyle-2J\left(a_{k_{y},1}^{\dagger}a_{k_{y},1}+b_{k_{y},N}^{\dagger}b_{k_{y},N}\right)\}+H_{\mathrm{DM}},

with

IV.1
AA stacking

For the bilayer situation, both the stacking form and the interlayer
interaction type between two layers can be different, which obviously
influence the physical properties of the magnon. Here we first consider AA
stacking form, i.e., two ferromagnets stack with each other without any
rotation or shifts. One can write the Hamiltonian as

H=\sum_{l=1,2}H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}+H_{\mathrm{int}},

(66)

l=1,2
represents the layer index and the spin of
H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{1}
and

H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{2}
can be same or different. So there are two types of
H_{\mathrm{int}}
: the FM one and AFM one, as shown in Fig.
1
(b).

IV.1.1
FM interlayer interaction

Combining with the bulk BdG Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}
with bearded
boundary as we showed in the last section as an example, the FM interlayer
interaction can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{S}_{i}^{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{i}^{2}

(67)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{in}}S\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\{\left[\left(a_{m,n}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}a_{m,n}^{2}+\left(b_{m,n}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}b_{m,n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\left(a_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}a_{m,n}^{1}+\left(b_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}b_{m,n}^{1}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\left(a_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}a_{m,n}^{2}+\left(b_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}b_{m,n}^{2}\right]+H.c.\}

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{in}}S[\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}+\left(b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}-\left(b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}+H.c.].

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}[\left(\gamma+J_{\mathrm{in}}\right)\mathit{I}_{4}

IV.2
AB stacking situation

Here we consider AB stacking form, i.e., two ferromagnets stack with each
other by a relative shift. The Hamiltonian also has the form

H=\sum_{l=1,2}H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}+H_{\mathrm{int}}.

IV.2.1
FM interlayer interaction

Still taking the bulk BdG Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{FM}}^{l}
with the bearded
boundary condition that we showed in the last section as an example, the FM
interlayer interaction in this condition can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{\left\langle i,j\right\rangle}\boldsymbol{S}_{i}^{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j}^{2}

(104)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{in}}S\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\{\left[\left(a_{m,n}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}a_{m,n}^{2}+\left(b_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}b_{m,n}^{1}\right]

\displaystyle-\left(b_{m,n}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}a_{m,n}^{2}+H.c.\}

in the basis of BdG Hamiltonian with
J_{\mathrm{in}}>0
. The
straightforward calculation shows in momentum space,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{int}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{in}}S[\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\right)^{\dagger}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}+\left(b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1}

(105)

\displaystyle-\left(b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1}\right)^{\dagger}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}+H.c.].

Then the bulk magnonic BdG Hamiltonian of AB stacking ferromagnets in
momentum space has the form

\displaystyle H\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{\dagger}H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{bea-bea}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\eta_{k},\eta_{k}=\left(\xi_{k},\xi_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1},b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1},a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2},b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\right),

with

H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{bea-bea}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)&0\\
0&h^{\ast}\left(-\boldsymbol{k}\right)\end{array}\right),

h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)=\frac{S}{2}\left[\gamma\mathit{I}_{4}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\beta&-\alpha&0&0\\
-\alpha^{\ast}&J_{\mathrm{in}}-\beta&-J_{\mathrm{in}}&0\\
0&-J_{\mathrm{in}}&J_{\mathrm{in}}+\beta&-\alpha\\
0&0&-\alpha^{\ast}&-\beta\end{array}\right)\right].

(106)

where
\alpha,\beta
, and
\gamma
are as same as in Eq. (
III.1
).
The eigenenergy can be expressed as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma-\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}\right),\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma+J_{\mathrm{in}}-A\right),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}\right),\varepsilon_{4}=\frac{S}{2}\left(\gamma+J_{\mathrm{in}}+A\right),

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{J_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}+B^{2}},B=\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\left|\alpha\right|^{2}},

(107)

with

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\alpha^{2},\alpha\left(\beta+B\right),\alpha\left(\beta+B\right),\left(\beta+B\right)^{2}\right]^{T}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{1}}},

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{3}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\left(\beta+B\right)^{2},-\alpha^{\ast}\left(\beta+B\right),-\alpha^{\ast}\left(\beta+B\right),(\alpha^{\ast})^{2}\right]^{T}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{3}}},

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[-\alpha,J_{\mathrm{in}}-A-\beta,-\left(\beta+J_{\mathrm{in}}-A\right),\alpha^{\ast}\right]^{T}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{2}}},

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{4}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[-\alpha,J_{\mathrm{in}}+A-\beta,-\left(\beta+J_{\mathrm{in}}+A\right),\alpha^{\ast}\right]^{T}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{4}}}.

The symmetry analysis shows (see Table
1
for details)
D\neq 0

leads to a non-trivial topological class A and the
\mathbb{Z}
topological invariant is shown as the related Chern number

C_{2}=C_{4}=0,C_{1}=-C_{3}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}2,D<0\\
-2,D>0\end{array}\right..

(108)

Moreover, one can have two samples with different boundaries to stack
together when considering the AB-stacking case on honeycomb lattices. If we
consider the bottom layer keeps the same boundary we used before (bearded)
and the top layer with a different boundary (zigzag), we have

\displaystyle H\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{\dagger}H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{bea-zig}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)\eta,\eta=\left(\xi_{k},\xi_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle\xi_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1},b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{1},b_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2},a_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}\right)

H_{BdG}^{\mathrm{bea-zig}}\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)&0\\
0&h^{\ast}\left(-\boldsymbol{k}\right)\end{array}\right),

h\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)=\frac{S}{2}\left[\gamma\mathit{I}_{4}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{\mathrm{in}}+\beta&-\alpha_{1}&-J_{\mathrm{in}}&0\\

with

\displaystyle\xi_{k}^{T}

(112)

which is the same bulk Hamiltonian in momentum space as before.

IV.2.2
AFM interlayer interaction

The AFM one can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{S}_{i}^{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle H\left(\boldsymbol{k}\right)

V
Summary

In summary, we study the topological classification and related edge states
of magnons in ferromagnets on honeycomb that can be described by a class of
single-particle bosonic BdG models. Both single layer and bilayer situations
are considered. For the monolayer case, since there are no double
annihilation or creation operators in the related BdG models, the
conventional bulk-edge correspondence for Hermitian systems remains
effective. The non-zero Chern number is directly connected with non-zero
DMI, but its relation with the specific band can be influenced by the
boundary condition. Non-trivial edge states can emerge even without the DMI
under certain open boundary conditions. For the bilayer case, the stacking
form (AA or AB) and the type of interlayer coupling, i.e., ferromagnetic
(FM) or antiferromagnetic (AFM), can significantly alter the Chern number
for magnon bands. Additionally, the different boundary conditions for
different layers play a crucial role in the distribution of edge states in
momentum space.

fMRI used to observe the acute craniocerebral response of esophageal cancer related depressive patients treated by rTMS: Initial experience
Authors: Jianquan Yang, Wen Guo, Xuezhou Pang, Chuanyu You, Chunyang Zhou, Yan Gui, Daiyuan Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To observe the immediate craniocerebral response, changes of spontaneous nerve activity and functional connection after repeated transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) in esophageal cancer patients with depression (ECPD) by functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and to explore the therapeutic effect, neuroactivity response and mechanism. Eleven patients with ECPD were enrolled to treated with single rTMS. The patients were examined by fMRI before and after the treatment. The changes of low frequency amplitude (ALFF) and the functional connection network between different brain regions of ALFF in patients were compared before and after rTMS treatment. Compared with those before rTMS treatment, the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD) score decreased significantly after rTMS treatment (t = −7.63, P = .0001). The ALFF of bilateral putamen, left thalamus, left posterior cingulate gyrus and right middle temporal gyrus decreased significantly (P = .02). In addition, the functional connection between the cortex-limbic system-striatum-thalamus nerve loop increased in patients after rTMS treatment. rTMS may achieve the effect of rehabilitation treatment by improving the spontaneous neural activity and regulating the neural connection network of cortical-limbic systems-triatum-thalamus loop in ECPD.

1. Introduction

Esophageal cancer is the most common malignant tumor in China. According to China Cancer Registration Annual report in 2019, there was 246,000 new cases of esophageal cancer in 2015. Esophageal cancer is the sixth in malignant tumor and there was 188,000 incidences of death in 2012.[1,2] At the same time, the incidence of esophageal cancer has the characteristics of regional distribution, and the area of this study (Sichuan, China) is the high incidence of esophageal cancer in China.

The incidence of tumor-related depression in esophageal cancer is about 20% to 25%.[3] Tumor-related depression can lead to neuroendocrine dysfunction, decreased immunity, reduced quality of life, weakened treatment compliance, and increased risk of suicide.[4–8] Tumor-related depression may increase the risk of recurrence and metastasis, can reduce the patient survival time by about 10% to 20%.[9–11] Although the latest research results show that antidepressant therapy can not improve the prognosis of tumor patients, but can improve the state of depression and improve the quality of life of patients.[12,13]

At present, the mainly used clinically antidepressants includes tricyclic anti-depressants, norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors, selective 5-HT reuptake inhibitors etc but these have many adverse reactions which may cause headache, blood pressure fluctuation, arrhythmia and so on.[14,15]

As we know that rTMS uses the principle of electromagnetic induction, in which the magnetic field generated by AC electricity produces induced current, which acts on the cerebral cortex, resulting in the depolarization of neurons in the nervous system. This results in the activation of the nerves at the end of the synapse, causing a series of metabolism in the brain and neuro electrophysiological changes of neurons.[16] The rTMS is widely used in the treatment of stroke and depression.[16–18] Although rTMS is an effective method for the treatment of depression, its optimal application and mechanism need to be explored further, especially in the change of central neural network connection.[19–21]

Functional Magnetic Resonance imaging (fMRI) as a new technology in the development of medical imaging, can not only detect the neuropsychology of patients, but also reflect the brain functional images of patients in real time.[22,23] fMRI can help researchers to study the development, disease and mapping of human brain neurons, which is the focus of brain function research at present.[24–26]

Using the level-dependent effect of blood oxygen, fMRI can accurately reflect the region and degree of brain functional injury, and at the same time, whether there is displacement or reconstruction of brain functional areas and whether there is brain function around the lesion. It can also clarify the functional changes of some regions of brain in some disease states as a whole, it helps in understanding autonomic function of brain.[27]

In this study, fMRI was used to study the changes of spontaneous nerve activity and functional connection in ECDP before and after rTMS treatment, and to explore rTMS mechanism in treatment for ECDP.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Subjects

Fifteen ECDP who were enrolled in our hospital from August to December 2019. Inclusion criteria: patients with esophageal cancer were diagnosed by pathology or/and cytology; patients were diagnosed with depression according to the diagnostic criteria of depression in the third edition of Chinese Diagnostic and Classification of Mental Disorders, and the patients were diagnosed with tumor-related depression (this part was completed under the guidance of psychosomatic doctors in our hospital); patients were non-pregnant women or not children; patients were free of cancerous pain; patients who had no primary or secondary malignant tumor in the brain. patients who had no previous history of depression; patients did not take antidepressants recently; patients volunteered to participate in this study. Exclusion criteria: patients with claustrophobia; cardiac pacemakers; severe heart diseases, severe somatic diseases and metallic substances in the body; patients with risk of seizures: severe craniocerebral disease, trauma, idiopathic epilepsy, recent use of antidepressants to reduce the seizure threshold; patients with a history of familial depressive mental disorders and serious self-harm or even suicidal tendencies. HAMD score was used to score each patient before and after treatment. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee in our hospital, and all the subjects were informed of the purpose of the experiment and signed an informed consent form before participating in the project.

2.2 rTMS treatment parameters

rTMS treatment uses high frequency 10 Hz stimulation to stimulate the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, stimulation intensity 100% resting state threshold, stimulation 30 sequences, 50 stimulation pulses/sequence, interval 25 seconds, treatment 1500 stimulation number/time. The whole rTMS treatment should be carried out in a comfortable and quiet environment. During this procedure patient is lied flat on the treatment bed, relaxed and kept motionless as far as possible. The operator placed the butterfly coil on the scalp corresponding to the patient’s left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and is necessary to make sure that the front of the butterfly coil is tangent to the scalp of the irritation site throughout the treatment. The fMRI examination was performed immediately before and after rTMS treatment.

2.3. MRI scanning

Magnetic resonance equipment: this study uses a 1.5T superconducting magnetic resonance scanner (GE Company, Cincinnati), which uses a head 8-channel coil. The rTMS needs to be scanned by fMRI before and after treatment, and the images before and after treatment was obtained and compared. All the patients were naturally lying flat on the examination bed when they were scanned. The patient head was carefully fixed with a sponge pad before scanning to minimize the subject’s head movement. At the same time, a special sponge ball earplug was used to plug the patient ears to reduce the effect of noise. During the scanning process the patients were advised relax, close his eyes, breathe calmly, by placing his hands on both sides of the body naturally, and keep his legs apart. At the same time, these patients were advised not to think as far as possible, and not to do active and passive movements of other body parts. All the subjects scanned with high-resolution anatomical images of the brain to rule out organic lesions in the brain.

2.3.1. MRI scanning parameters

The fMRI images were collected using spin echo gradient plane imaging Echo-Planar-18-Imaging Gradient Echo Sequence, SE-EPI) sequence. The parameters were as follows: TR: 2000 ms, TE: 40 ms, FOV: 24.0 cm × 24.0 cm, scan matrix: 64 × 64, flip angle: 90°, layer thickness/layer spacing: 5.0 mm, voxel size: 3.75 × 3.75 × 5.0 mm³, layer number: 32 layers, scanning the whole brain image at 140 time points. The conventional T1-weighted image scanning parameters are as follows: TR: 3000 ms, TE: 5 ms, FOV: 24.0 cm × 24.0 cm, scan matrix: 256 × 160, flip angle: 90°, layer thickness/spacing: 5.0 mm, layers: 28 layers.

2.4. Data analysis method

The REST (Resting-State fMRI Data Analysis Toolkit V 1.5; http://www.restfmri.net software package) is used to analyze the preprocessed fMRI images on the MATLAB platform. The fMRI images of the subjects were preprocessed and analyzed by SPM8 (Statistical Parametric Mapping; http://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk) software package. The main steps are as follows: image format conversion; removal of data from the previous time points; slice timing; re-alignment; spatial normalization; smooth; detrending; filtering; regressing out nuisance covariates. Statistical analysis of related data was carried out by using SPM8 software package and SPSS22.0 software. P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3.3. The changes of low frequency amplitude before and after rTMS treatment

The paired t test results of low frequency amplitude (ALFF) map of patients before and after rTMS treatment showed that: the brain regions with significant increase of ALFF after rTMS treatment included right superior frontal gyrus, bilateral middle frontal gyrus, right inferior orbitofrontal gyrus, bilateral anterior cingulate gyrus, right hippocampus, bilateral middle temporal gyrus and left inferior temporal gyrus. The brain regions with significant decrease of ALFF included bilateral putamen, left thalamus, left posterior cingulate gyrus and right middle temporal gyrus. The statistical significance is P = .02, and the difference is shown in Figure 1 and Table 3.

3.4. Changes of functional networks of cortical-limbic system-striatum-thalamus neural loop

The difference area after ALFF comparison includes all parts of the depression-related cortex-limbic system-striatum-thalamus loop. Therefore, the change of depression network before and after rTMS treatment is studied by calculating the functional connection network between the difference areas. Figure 2A,B shows the connection of cortical-limbic system-striatum-thalamus network before and after rTMS treatment, respectively. The results showed that the network connection increased after rTMS treatment, including the connection between medial superior frontal gyrus and orbital middle frontal gyrus. The posterior cingulate gyrus and hippocampus, hippocampus and left putamen, thalamus and left putamen, while the connection between medial frontal gyrus and right putamen decreased. On the whole, the connections between cortex-cortex, cortex-striatum and striatum-thalamus were increased.

3.5. rTMS-related adverse reactions

In this study, adverse reactions related to rTMS were mainly manifested as mild scalp discomfort, slight tinnitus, dry mouth, headache, which could be tolerated by patients during treatment without special treatment.

4. Discussion

Studies have shown that,[28] the vast majority of tumor patients may be associated with depression at different stages of diagnosis in malignant tumors. For malignant tumors, side effects such as anorexia, nausea, vomiting and pain in the process of diagnosis and treatment often cause emotional disorders such as depression.[29] This can result in endocrine dysfunction and decreased immunity. The increase in pain sensitivity of patients,[30] can aggravate the adverse reactions in patients in the due course of treatment, thereby reducing the quality of life, and even promote in tumor progression.[31,32] Increase the risk of recurrence and metastasis,[33] reduce their survival time by about 10% to 20%, and even depression may be an independent risk factor for some tumors.[34] Therefore, tumor-related depression needs to be paid enough attention. At the same time, active, standardized and effective antidepressant therapy can help to improve the quality of life of tumor patients and prolong their survival time at the same time.

In this study, we observed differences in regional brain activity and whole brain network connection between ECDP before and after rTMS treatment. Moreover we used the functional network connection method of low frequency oscillation amplitude based on seed points and local consistency. So that we can analyze the regional brain activity of patients with esophageal cancer complicated with depression before and after treatment. The results after rTMS treatment were compared with those before treatment: the spontaneous nerve activity in depression-related brain areas increased significantly after rTMS treatment. The changes of neural activity in anterior cingulate gyrus were highly consistent with the changes of clinical scores. rTMS significantly improved the functional network connectivity of the cortex-limbic system-striatum-thalamus loop, which were consistent with previous studies. Previous studies have confirmed that patients with depressive symptoms have abnormal neural activity and connection function in the frontal lobe, hippocampus, amygdala, and cingulate gyrus.[35] We found that regional consistency in the right hippocampus increased in patients after rTMS treatment. Previous studies have shown that in patients with depression, the hippocampus is considered to play an important role in their cognitive process. When the hippocampal function is abnormal, patients with depression will have the disorder of episodic memory extraction. Lawrence and his co workers[36] used fMRI to detect depression, and they found that the response of the right hippocampus of depressed patients to happy facial expression stimuli was weaker than that of normal subjects, which was consistent with our results. In 1972, Nauta[37] put forward the concept of neuroanatomical loop, that is, the limbic system of cerebral cortex-pallidus-striatum-thalamus loop (limbice-cortical-strietal-pallidal-thelamic, LCSPT). They play an important role in the emotional regulation and transmission of the body. It is believed that there are organic changes in the brain tissue of patients with depression, and the main lesion site is located in LCSPT.[38] The prefrontal cortex plays an important role in human cognitive and emotional activities, and has extensive connections with other important brain regions and intracerebral circuits.[39] There is a specific connection pathway between the anterior cingulate gyrus and the hippocampus, which are related and restricted each other in functional activities. Therefore, when there is a problem in one of the above-mentioned brain regions, it can affect the function of other brain regions. In LCSPT, the prefrontal lobe is widely associated with the limbic system and basal ganglia, which plays an important role in regulating the activity of these brain regions. The study showed that in patients with emotional disorders, especially depression, the frontal lobe volume decreased, while the blood flow and velocity decreased. When the brain area was activated by external stimulation, the results showed that compared with before stimulation, the metabolic rate and blood perfusion area were improved after stimulation. The Oda et al[40] examined the brain metabolism of patients with depression using fMRI and PET/CT. They also measured the blood oxygen level in the brain and its dependence, intracranial arterial and venous blood flow, blood flow velocity and cerebral oxygen uptake index. The results showed that when the activation task was given, the metabolism and blood flow in the corresponding brain areas of depression increased significantly, which was consistent with our results. The shortcomings of the study: This study did not set up a healthy control group and/or a control group taking antidepressants, but we are planning to do this experiment. In our study, fMRI was used to observe the changes of brain function in depression-related brain areas immediately before and after rTMS treatment in ECDP. Then we observed the changes of brain function in depression-related brain areas after a cycle of rTMS (before and after treatment). We will also try different stimulation frequencies (such as 1 Hz) in the left and right hemispheres to observe the changes of brain metabolism and function in the depressive brain area.

In conclusion, rTMS may achieve the effect of rehabilitation treatment by improving the spontaneous neural activity in ECDP and regulating the neural connection network of cortical-limbic system-striatum-thalamus loop.

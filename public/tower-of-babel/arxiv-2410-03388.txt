On the intersection of fractal cubes
Tetenov Andrei and Drozdov Dmitry

Abstract
Let n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and let D = { d 1 , ⋯ , d N } ⊂ { 0 , 1 , … , n − 1 } k D=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{N}\}\subset\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}^{k} . The set D D and the integer n n determine a system of contractions 𝒮 = { S j ( x ) = 1 n ( x + d j ) } j = 1 N \mathcal{S}=\{S_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{n}(x+d_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N} in R k R^{k} , whose attractor K K satisfies the set equation n ​ K = K + D nK=K+D and is called a fractal k k -cube of order n n . We consider the intersections of fractal k k -cubes of order n n in ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} and intersections of their respective opposite l l -faces, 0 ≤ l < k 0\leq l<k . The main result of the paper is the theorem on the representation of such an intersection as the attractor of a graph-directed system of similarities in terms of the sets of units corresponding to these cubes and intersections of pairs of l l -faces. As a corollary, we prove the dimension formula for the intersection and the condition of finiteness of its measure. Another corollary gives the conditions under which the intersections have the given cardinality. Applying these techniques, we obtain the conditions under which a fractal k k -cube has the finite intersection property and the conditions under which the fractal cube is a dendrite.

D_{2}\cap(n-1)P_{-{\bm{\alpha}}}+(n-1){\bm{\alpha}}
is equal to
G_{\bm{\alpha}}=D_{1}\cap(D_{2}+(n-1){\bm{\alpha}})
, and is naturally associated with the set
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
.
If
{\bm{\alpha}}=0
, the set
G_{\bm{\alpha}}
becomes
G_{0}=D_{1}\cap D_{2}
.

Remark 2
.

Let
Q_{\bm{\alpha}}
denote a fractal cube with a digit set

3.2
The intersection theorem and the graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
.

The following theorem establishes the relations between the sets
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
:

Theorem 7
.

The family
\{F_{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\alpha}}\in A_{k}\}
of intersections
F_{\bm{\alpha}}=K_{1}\cap(K_{2}+{\bm{\alpha}})

satisfies the system
\Sigma
of equations

F_{\bm{\alpha}}=\bigcup\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupseteq{{\bm{\alpha}}}}T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}}),\qquad{\bm{\alpha}}\in A_{k},

(7)

where for any
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupseteq{\bm{\alpha}}
,

T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}})=\frac{1}{n}(F_{\bm{\beta}}+G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}})\mbox{\hskip 10.22217ptand \hskip 10.22217pt}G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}=D_{1}\cap(D_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}}-{\bm{\beta}})

(8)

Proof.

We represent
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
as

K_{1}\cap(K_{2}+{\bm{\alpha}})=\dfrac{1}{n}\bigl((K_{1}+D_{1})\cap(K_{2}+D_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}})\bigr).

Given
d_{1}\in D_{1}
and
d_{2}\in D_{2}
, the intersection
(K_{1}+d_{1})\cap(K_{2}+d_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}})
is nonempty if
(P+d_{1})\cap(P+d_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}})\neq{\varnothing}
, which means that the vector
{\bm{\beta}}=d_{2}-d_{1}+n{\bm{\alpha}}
is an element of
A
. Since, for any coordinate
i=1,\ldots,k
,
|(d_{2}-d_{1})_{i}|\leq n-1
, this is possible only if
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupseteq{\bm{\alpha}}
.

If
{\bm{\beta}}={\bm{\alpha}}
, then
d_{1}=d_{2}+(n-1){\bm{\alpha}}
, consequently
d_{1}\in D_{1}\cap(D_{2}+(n-1){\bm{\alpha}})=G_{\bm{\alpha}}
.

If
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupset{\bm{\alpha}}
, then
(K_{1}+d_{1})\cap(K_{2}+d_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}})=(K_{1}\cap(K_{2}+{\bm{\beta}}))+d_{1}
, and
d_{1}\in D_{1}\cap(D_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}}-{\bm{\beta}})
, which we denote by
G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}
.

Notice that
G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\alpha}}}=D_{1}\cap(D_{2}+n{\bm{\alpha}}-{\bm{\alpha}})
, thereby
G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\alpha}}}=G_{{\bm{\alpha}}}
.

As a result, we obtain
F_{\bm{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{n}\bigcup\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupseteq{{\bm{\alpha}}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}}+G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}})=\frac{1}{n}(F_{\bm{\alpha}}+G_{\bm{\alpha}})\cup\bigcup\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupset{{\bm{\alpha}}}}\frac{1}{n}(F_{\bm{\beta}}+G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}})
.
∎

Theorem
7
gives rise to the
structure graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
of the system

\Sigma
defined in (
7
). We use this system and graph as a tool to find the properties of the sets
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
.

Definition 3
.

The structure graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
is a directed graph, in which the vertices are all non-empty sets
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
and for each
{\bm{\alpha}}\sqsubseteq{\bm{\beta}}
there is an edge
(F_{\bm{\alpha}},F_{\bm{\beta}})
that is directed from
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
to
F_{\bm{\beta}}
and corresponds to the operator
T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}
, if this operator is non-degenerate.

In general, the graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
would contain
3^{k}
vertices and
5^{k}
edges, and
3^{k}
of these edges are loops from
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
to itself. We mark each edge with
G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}
.

However, some of the vertices and edges in the graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
may vanish. This occurs for those
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
that are empty and for those edges
(F_{\bm{\alpha}},F_{\bm{\beta}})
for which
T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}})={\varnothing}
.

Note the following obvious statement:

T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}})={\varnothing}\mbox{\quad if \quad}G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}={\varnothing}\mbox{ \quad or \quad}F_{\bm{\beta}}={\varnothing}

(9)

A set
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
is empty if
G_{\bm{\alpha}}={\varnothing}
and for any
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupset{\bm{\alpha}}
the set
F_{\bm{\beta}}+G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}={\varnothing}
. Applying (
9
) to all
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupset{\bm{\alpha}}
, we
deduce the following emptiness condition for
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
:

Lemma 8
.

A set
F_{\bm{\alpha}}={\varnothing}
if and only if for any
{\bm{\beta}}\sqsupseteq{\bm{\alpha}}
and for any finite sequence

{\bm{\alpha}}={\bm{\alpha}}_{0}\sqsubseteq{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}\sqsubseteq\ldots{\bm{\alpha}}_{p-1}\sqsubseteq{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}={\bm{\beta}}
the product

\#G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{0}{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}}\#G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}{\bm{\alpha}}_{2}}\ldots\#G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{p-1}{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}}\#G_{{\bm{\beta}}}
is equal to zero.
\square

For these reasons, due to the reduction of all empty vertices and empty edges, the structure graph
\Gamma
for the system
\Sigma
defined in Theorem
7
has the set of vertices
V_{\Sigma}=\{F_{\bm{\alpha}}:{\bm{\alpha}}\in A,F_{\bm{\alpha}}\neq{\varnothing}\}
and the set of edges

E_{\Sigma}=\{(F_{\bm{\alpha}},F_{\bm{\beta}}):{\bm{\alpha}}\sqsubseteq{\bm{\beta}},G_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}\neq{\varnothing},F_{\bm{\beta}}\neq{\varnothing}\}
.

In general, such graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
may be disconnected.

We say that two vertices
F_{\bm{\alpha}},F_{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\alpha}}\sqsubset{\bm{\beta}}

are connected by a directed path in

\Gamma_{\Sigma}
, if there is a finite sequence
{\bm{\alpha}}={\bm{\alpha}}_{0}\sqsubset{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}\sqsubset\ldots{\bm{\alpha}}_{p-1}\sqsubset{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}={\bm{\beta}}
such that for any
j=0,\ldots,p
sets
F_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}}\neq{\varnothing}
and sets
G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{j-1}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}}\neq{\varnothing}
for
j=1,\ldots,p
.

We write
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
if there is a directed path in
\Gamma
from
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
to
F_{\bm{\beta}}
.

If
{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}
or
{\bm{\alpha}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\beta}}
then we say that
{\bm{\alpha}}
and
{\bm{\beta}}
are
\Gamma
-comparable
.

We denote by
\Gamma_{\bm{\alpha}}
a subgraph in
\Gamma
, whose vertices are all
F_{\bm{\beta}}
such that
{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}
. We say that

{\bm{\beta}}
is
maximal
for
\Gamma_{\bm{\alpha}}
, if
\Gamma_{\bm{\beta}}
is a single vertex
F_{\bm{\beta}}
. We say that

{\bm{\beta}}
is
minimal
for
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
, if there is no
{\bm{\alpha}}
such that
{\bm{\alpha}}\prec{\bm{\beta}}
.

It should be noted that, by Proposition
6
, the graph
\Gamma_{\bm{\alpha}}
shows the set of all equations that completely define each of the sets

{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}

Each operator

that corresponds to some edge in the graph

is a non-empty finite union of homotheties, therefore it preserves the dimension, i.e. for any subset

\dim_{H}(T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(X))=\dim_{H}(X)

3.5
Finiteness condition for the measure
H^{s}(F_{\alpha})
.

Definition 4
.

For any
{\bm{\alpha}}\in A_{k}
we put
\nu({\bm{\alpha}})=\max\{\#G_{\bm{\beta}}:{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}\}
and
s({\bm{\alpha}})=\log_{n}\nu({\bm{\alpha}})
.

On account of Remark
2
, we may also write

s({\bm{\alpha}})=\max\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}}\{\dim_{H}Q_{\bm{\beta}}\}
.

Theorem 9
.

For any
{\bm{\alpha}}\in A_{k}
, the Hausdorff dimension of the set
F_{\bm{\alpha}}
is
s({\bm{\alpha}})
, and its
s({\bm{\alpha}})
-measure
H^{s({\bm{\alpha}})}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})
is positive.

The measure

H^{s({\bm{\alpha}})}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})
is finite if and only if the set
\{{\bm{\beta}}\succcurlyeq{\bm{\alpha}}:\#G_{\bm{\beta}}=\nu({\bm{\alpha}})\}
does not contain
\Gamma
-comparable elements.

Proof.
If we put
B=\bigcup\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}}T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}})
, the equation (
7
) becomes

F_{\bm{\alpha}}=T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\alpha}}}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})\cup B

(10)

Iterating the last equation and keeping in mind that
\lim\limits_{m\to{\infty}}T^{m}_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\alpha}}}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})=Q_{\bm{\alpha}}
, we obtain that

F_{\bm{\alpha}}=Q_{\bm{\alpha}}\cup\bigcup\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\alpha}}}^{m}(B)

(11)

This equality implies that
\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\alpha}}=\max(\dim_{H}Q_{\bm{\alpha}},\dim_{H}B)
.
All non-degenerate operators
T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}
preserve the dimension, that is,
\dim_{H}T_{{\bm{\alpha}}{\bm{\beta}}}(F_{\bm{\beta}})=\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\beta}}
. It follows that

\dim_{H}B=\max\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}}\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\beta}}
, hence

\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\alpha}}=\max(\dim_{H}Q_{\bm{\alpha}},\max\limits_{{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}}\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\beta}})
.

Suppose now that
\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\alpha}}=s^{\prime}>s({\bm{\alpha}})
. Then
\dim_{H}B=s^{\prime}
, hence
there is
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
such that
\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\beta}}=s^{\prime}
.
Applying this argument to the set
F_{\bm{\beta}}
, we see that the set of all
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
, such that
\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\beta}}=s^{\prime}
, cannot contain a minimal element. This is impossible because it is finite.

Consequently,
\dim_{H}F_{\bm{\alpha}}=s({\bm{\alpha}})
.

If
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
and
\#G_{\bm{\beta}}=\nu({\bm{\alpha}})
, then

H^{s({\bm{\alpha}})}(Q_{\bm{\beta}})
is positive, hence
H^{s({\bm{\alpha}})}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})
is also positive.

If
\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}>\#G_{\bm{\beta}}
for any
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
, then
H^{s}(Q_{\bm{\beta}})=0
for all
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
, therefore,
H^{s}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})=H^{s}(Q_{\bm{\alpha}})
is finite and positive.

This completes the proof of the first statement of Theorem
9
.

Suppose now
\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}<\nu({\bm{\alpha}})
and
H^{s}(B)=h>0
. Then
H^{s}(T^{m}_{\bm{\alpha}}(B))=\dfrac{(\#G_{\bm{\alpha}})^{m}h}{\nu({\bm{\alpha}})^{m}}
. Taking a sum over all
m
from 0 to infinity, we obtain

H^{s}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})\leqslant\dfrac{h\nu({\bm{\alpha}})}{\nu({\bm{\alpha}})-\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}}
, hence
H^{s}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})
is positive and finite.

Now we show that if for some
{\bm{\beta}}\succ{\bm{\alpha}}
,
\#G_{\bm{\beta}}=\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}=\nu(\alpha)
, then

H^{s}(F_{\bm{\alpha}})
is infinite. This will be proved in the following lemma, which finalizes the proof of the theorem.

On account of Theorem
4
, it sufficient to consider the situation where
{\bm{\alpha}}=0
.

Lemma 10
.

If
\#G_{0}=\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}=l
,
\log_{n}l=s
and for any
{\bm{\beta}}\succ 0
,
\#G_{\bm{\beta}}\leq l
. Then

H^{s}(F_{0})
is infinite.

Proof.

Case 1
: There is

d_{1}\in D_{1}
such that
d_{1}+{\bm{\alpha}}\in D_{2}
.

Consider fractal cubes
Q_{0}
and
Q_{\bm{\alpha}}
. Note that both sets
Q_{0}
and
Q_{\bm{\alpha}}
have finite positive measure in dimension
s
.

Consider a set
Q_{*}
defined by equation
Q_{*}=\dfrac{Q_{*}+G_{0}}{n}\bigcup\dfrac{d_{1}+Q_{\bm{\alpha}}}{n}
. Define the operator
T_{0}
by the equality
T_{0}(A)=\dfrac{A+G_{0}}{n}
. Let
B=\dfrac{d_{1}+Q_{\bm{\alpha}}}{n}
.

Let
{\hat{T}}
be the operator defined by the equality
{\hat{T}}(A)=\dfrac{I^{k}+A}{n}
whose attractor is the whole cube
P
.

Consider the sets
P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(m)}={\hat{T}}^{m}(P_{\bm{\alpha}})
. Inclusion
P_{\bm{\alpha}}{\subset}{\hat{T}}(P_{\bm{\alpha}})
implies that these sets form an increasing nested sequence
P_{\bm{\alpha}}{\subset}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(1)}{\subset}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(2)}\ldots
.

Note that
B{\subset}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(1)}{\setminus}P_{\bm{\alpha}}
and
T_{0}^{m}(B){\subset}{\hat{T}}^{m}(B){\subset}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(m+1)}{\setminus}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(m)}
. The sets
P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(m+1)}{\setminus}P_{\bm{\alpha}}^{(m)}
are disjoint, and thus the sets
T_{0}^{m}(B)
are disjoint, too.

On the other hand, since
\#G_{0}=\#G_{\bm{\alpha}}=l
,
H^{s}(T_{0}^{m}(B))=H^{s}(B)
. Consequently, the set
\bigcup\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}T_{0}^{m}(B)
has an infinite Hausdorff measure in dimension
s
.

Case 2.
There is a sequence
0\prec{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}\prec\ldots\prec{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}
such that
{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}={\bm{\alpha}}
and

\#G_{0{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}}\#G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}{\bm{\alpha}}_{2}}\ldots\#G_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{p-1}{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}}\neq 0
.
Then for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
there are
d_{i}\in D_{1}
and
\delta_{i}\in D_{2}
such that
\delta_{i}=d_{i}-n{\bm{\alpha}}_{i-1}+{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}
.
Then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}n^{p-i}\delta_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}n^{p-i}(d_{i}-n{\bm{\alpha}}_{i-1}+{\bm{\alpha}}_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}n^{p-i}d_{i}+{\bm{\alpha}}_{p}

That is,
\bm{\delta}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}-\bm{d}_{i_{1}\ldots i_{p}}={\bm{\alpha}}_{p}
.
This means that if we consider
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
as fractal cubes of the order
n^{p}
with digit sets
D_{1}^{(p)}
and
D_{2}^{(p)}
, respectively, then the set
G^{(p)}_{0{\bm{\alpha}}}
is nonempty.
At the same time, the equalities
D^{(p)}_{1,{\bm{\alpha}}}=D^{(p)}_{1}\cap(n^{p}-1)P_{\bm{\alpha}}=(D_{1,{\bm{\alpha}}})^{(p)}
and
D^{(p)}_{2,-{\bm{\alpha}}}=D^{(p)}_{2}\cap(n^{p}-1)P_{-{\bm{\alpha}}}=(D_{2,-{\bm{\alpha}}})^{(p)}
imply that
G^{(p)}_{\bm{\alpha}}=(G_{\bm{\alpha}})^{(p)}
. Then it follows that
\#G^{(p)}_{\bm{\alpha}}=l^{p}
and
\log_{n^{p}}\#G^{(p)}=s
. Hence, the fractal cube of order
n^{p}
with the digit set
G^{(p)}_{\bm{\alpha}}
coincides with
Q_{\bm{\alpha}}
.

This situation is the same as in Case 1, therefore, the measure
H^{s}(F_{0})
is infinite.

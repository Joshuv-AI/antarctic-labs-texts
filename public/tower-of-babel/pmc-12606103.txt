Layer-specific approximate multipliers for energy–precision trade-offs in convolutional neural networks
Authors: Ladan Sayadi, Mohammad Hossein Moaiyeri, Somayeh Timarchi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractApproximate computing is a promising paradigm for error-resilient applications, such as image and signal processing and neural networks, prioritizing hardware efficiency over precision. This paper presents a novel CNN-specific approximation methodology divided into three sections. The first section identifies the characteristics of suitable approximate multipliers for CNNs, highlighting how variance in weight distribution affects the error tolerance of different layers. Section Two introduces three types of approximate multipliers (AM_5×5, AM_4×4, and AM_3×3), designed using innovative operand truncation techniques. These multipliers enable adjustable accuracy by varying the number of truncated operand bits and are scalable to any multiplier size. Two algorithms are also proposed: one optimizes training with approximate multipliers for improved performance, and another employs a gradual training strategy. Section Three describes two distinct strategies for deploying the methodology within CNN architectures and evaluates its hardware implementation on an ASIC in 28 nm CMOS technology. Comprehensive comparisons using VGG16, VGG10, and AlexNet architectures reveal significant improvements in energy efficiency. The first strategy achieves energy efficiency gains of up to 86%, 95%, and 88% per operation for VGG10, VGG16, and AlexNet, respectively, while the second strategy achieves improvements of 81%, 92%, and 84% for the same networks. This approach effectively balances computational complexity and accuracy while leveraging CNN features to enhance hardware efficiency. Experimental results validate the potential of this methodology to advance CNN designs, optimizing both energy and hardware resources for practical applications.Keywords: Approximate multiplier, Approximate computing, Convolutional neural networks, Low power design, CNN-Specific approximationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Advancements in semiconductor fabrication technology have consistently improved the performance of integrated circuits (ICs). However, concerns exist over energy usage because transistor power consumption increases with increasing power density. The demand for energy-efficient, high-performance computing systems is becoming increasingly critical, particularly for applications in artificial intelligence, big data, and mobile devices. Approximate computing has become a viable strategy for reducing energy consumption in error-resilient applications, including machine learning, multimedia, and digital signal processing. A certain degree of error is acceptable in these applications, as it does not significantly affect the final result. This is particularly true for outputs designed for human perception, as minor errors are generally undetectable.

Digital circuit designers have concentrated on approximate computing to minimize delay, power consumption, and area at various levels, from algorithmic design to transistor-level implementation. At the hardware level, approximate computing predominantly focuses on arithmetic units, including adders1, multipliers, and dividers2. Multipliers are essential arithmetic elements that require substantial power and are widely used in various applications. In3, the survey provides a comprehensive review of the entire field of approximate computing, covering software, hardware, and architectural approximation techniques, as well as their applications. Consequently, research on approximate multipliers received significant attention. Various techniques have been proposed to design approximate multipliers, including the truncation of operands or partial products4,5, the use of approximate blocks for partial product reduction, such as approximate compressors inside multipliers6–10, recursive multipliers built from small elementary approximate multiplier blocks11, and methods like approximate logarithmic multiplication12 and approximate Booth encoding13.

The computational costs of deep learning align with the constraints imposed by the deployed services regarding time, energy, and other design considerations. There has been extensive research on reducing computing costs from the software to the hardware level. To design energy-efficient neural networks (NNs), approaches emphasize the use of approximate arithmetic units (multipliers and adders) or the design of efficient memory14. Therefore, efficient multipliers are especially critical in multimedia and deep neural network (DNN) applications, where matrix multiplications dominate computational workloads. The rapid growth and widespread adoption of neural NNs have further intensified research efforts toward designing approximate multipliers tailored for optimizing NN accelerators. Several studies have introduced approximate multipliers that demonstrate improved performance and are particularly well-suited for use in NN14–17.

The proposed method presents a CNN-specific approximate methodology that considers network properties. The proposed method incorporates training the network using approximate multiplication and the proper positioning of the proposed multipliers with varied accuracy. Since the weight distribution of the CNN differs in each layer, we have used this feature to propose more suitable approximate multipliers for NNs. Based on layer details, three proposed approximate multipliers (AM_5 × 5, AM_4 × 4, and AM_3 × 3) have been proposed and utilized for different CNN layers. The findings demonstrate that the proposed CNN-specific approximate multipliers significantly enhance network performance.

The paper attempts to construct an approximate multiplier that fits well with the CNN framework. This paper proposes approximate multipliers with a reduced number of bits and incorporates a compensation section to compensate for the bits that are not employed. The proposed design involves truncating operands to achieve a balance between positive and negative errors by compensating for the generated errors. The proposed multiplier streamlines the structure throughout the multiplication process, from partial product generation to partial product reduction, by multiplying the bits less than the operands. It is essential to note that approximate multipliers with truncation operands exhibit greater efficiency in NN applications, as this reduces the memory bit requirement for stored weights. We can achieve multipliers with different levels of accuracy by adjusting the number of bits truncated from the operands. Also, the proposed method is extensible to n-bit multipliers. This holds even for multipliers with length-varying operands.

Preliminaries

The use of approximation calculations in NN hardware accelerators is one of the most common methods among the several approaches suggested for the low-power design of NNs. A lot of focus has been on approximation multipliers recently because of the significant power savings they can achieve. On the other hand, various approaches have been explored in the design of approximate multipliers to reduce power consumption while maintaining acceptable accuracy levels. This section begins by reviewing previous works on approximate multipliers by employing compressor-based architectures.

A multiplier in the conventional structure consists of three stages: partial product generation, partial product reduction, and final summation. Compressors are widely used in the partial product reduction stage to enhance efficiency. Researchers have developed approximate compressors specifically for use in multipliers to improve performance.

Reference6 proposed two approximate multipliers by approximate 4:2 compressors; the error has been mitigated by utilizing different compressors in neighboring columns. As detailed in7, one way to neutralize the errors is by using two separate compressors in a two-stage system. The compressors described in8 are built to approximate input patterns with low probability. The strategy outlined in9 is a hybrid methodology balancing the possibilities of positive and negative errors. An approximated 4:1 compressor and a 5:2 compressor were also suggested in10.

Some studies have also addressed error compensation mechanisms for errors introduced by approximate compressors or the truncation of partial products [5, 9, 48, 49]. For instance5, has truncated eight columns of partial products, but has instead proposed a constant quantity in the final multiplication step, along with an error compensation section. Three compressors are provided in Ref. [49], along with a suggestion for an error compensation section to fix the errors made by the compressor chain.

To make multiplication easier, in [50] a Static Segmented Multiplier (SSM) with segmenting multiplication operands and an error compensation approach was proposed, utilizing this technique to focus on the most critical parts of the operands. In logarithmic multipliers, operands are expressed as base-2 logarithms of the input numbers, with multiplication eventually performed using an adder.

The limitation of Michell’s logarithmic multiplier is its persistent negative approximation. However, the improved logarithmic multiplier (ILM) of12 features a bidirectional error distribution and employs a novel adder architecture, resulting in decreases in hardware and energy usage.

Employing approximate multiplication in CNNs is a promising hardware-based solution. A key topic discussed in prior work is the design of approximate multipliers compatible with the design of CNNs. In16, the impact of several parameters, including approximation and the use of the batch normalization layer in CNNs, was investigated. In [51], an approximate multiplier was presented, whose precision can be modified by truncating partial products, as specific applications benefit from the ability to adjust accuracy and power usage dynamically.

For DNN applications, Ref. [52] introduced a multiply-accumulate (MAC) unit featuring two types of approximate multipliers with contrasting error orientations. This approach allows better compatibility with DNN workloads. Furthermore, in14 layer-wise importance in DNNs was suggested for defining parameters to guide the selection of one of three multiplier types with varying accuracy levels for each layer, optimizing the trade-off between power and precision.

Proposed design

The proposed scheme is divided into three sections. The first part introduces the principles of a CNN-specific approximation methodology. The second part proposes approximate multipliers with varying degrees of accuracy based on the results of the first part. Additionally, two methods are proposed: one suggests a method for training with approximate multipliers gradually, while the other details how to employ three multipliers with varying degrees of precision. Principles of CNN-Specific approximation methodology

First, this section discusses the features of a good approximate multiplier for CNN, as well as the factors that should be considered when designing a multiplier to create a suitable approximate multiplier as we progress. After that, we’ll look into the weight distribution variance for each convolutional layer and express it in proportion to the multiplier accuracy for each layer. The non-uniform distribution of inputs is what sets CNNs apart from other applications. In convolutional layers, as the depth increases, the variance of the weight distribution decreases. The concentration of weights near zero becomes more pronounced. As noted in16, due to the representation of features by relative values rather than absolute values, minimizing the error variance between the convolution outputs is significantly more critical than reducing the absolute mean of errors during approximate multiplication in convolution16. If the errors affect all convolution outputs uniformly, a degree of inaccuracy in multiplications is acceptable.

As demonstrated in17, using multipliers with different accuracy levels can be helpful in such applications. For example, a multiplier with a higher approximation level can be applied when the weight values are close to zero. The approximation method in17 is incorporated into the design of the proposed multiplier. When designing approximate multipliers, this property should be considered, as the high-value bits of the operand are going through with high precision. In contrast, the lower-value bits are approximated with a higher degree of approximation. Notably, approximating values around zero with zero is appropriate in NN applications because there shouldn’t be any discernible change in the feature’s importance. The feature is not observed to be of any significance when the weights are around zero. The converse is also true: the high-value operand values should not be approximated to the point where the feature’s importance is compromised, since they define the significant features. Consequently, an appropriate approximate multiplier for CNN applications should possess the features above. We will consider these features when designing the approximation multiplier in the following section. Considerations such as which approximation approach is best for the approximate multipliers and how much accuracy is suitable for each convolutional layer are also crucial. We have attempted to extract a pattern from the variance in weight distribution as it evolves through the various layers. As one progresses through the layers of CNNs, the variation in weight distribution diminishes progressively. Generally, the variance exhibits a declining trend. The variances in weight distribution for three CNN networks, VGG16, VGG10, and AlexNet, are presented in Figs. 1, 2 and 3. It is important to note that the VGG10 and AlexNet networks were trained using the CIFAR-1023 dataset, while the VGG16 network was trained with another dataset, GTSRB24. To enable evaluation on a deeper network with a larger number of parameters, the VGG16 architecture was selected. The network was trained on the GTSRB dataset, which comprises 43 traffic sign classes.

The statistics suggest that the descending trend does not persist uniformly, with more pronounced decreases observed in specific layers of one network than others. The convolutional layers in a CNN progressively extract increasingly abstract characteristics. Initial layers detect low-level features, such as edges, textures, and colors, whereas subsequent layers identify high-level features, including forms and objects. A significant variance change indicates a shift in the abstraction level, where features extracted in one layer become more critical for the classification task. Because of this, we have substituted a more accurate approximate multiplier for the layers whose weight distribution variance decreased more significantly than that of the other layers. This result can be interpreted as follows: a significant reduction in variance corresponds to a more pronounced increase in the number of weights that are close to zero. Consequently, it may be asserted that when weights near zero increase, weights with greater values become more significant due to their diminished frequency relative to the preceding layer. For instance, it is clear from Fig. 3 that the second and last levels of VGG16 exhibit more extensive variance changes than their prior layer. Considering the profound changes in the variance of the weights in the second and final layers of VGG16, it is highly probable that they will extract more significant features. Proposed approximation methodology

We covered the characteristics of good CNN multipliers and provided some information regarding the accuracy required for each layer in the previous section.

This section initially introduces approximate multipliers suitable for CNNs at three distinct accuracy levels, followed by the proposal of an algorithm for selecting the proper multiplier among these levels. We also introduce an approach for incremental training utilizing approximation multipliers.

The preceding section indicated that, for NNs, it is advantageous to apply a more accurate approximation to small weight values while maintaining the values of zero weights unchanged, as an excessive approximation of zero weights can significantly compromise the network’s accuracy, as demonstrated in17. Three approximate multipliers are suggested based on these characteristics.

The proposed multiplier computes the product of 8-bit operands using a reduced number of operands. The compensation is provided for the omitted segments of the operands and the generated error. The proposed multiplier is AM_m×m, where the parameter m signifies the number of bits in the approximated operands. The proposed method applies to any operand with any number of bits. However, it is typically employed with 8-bit multipliers in CNNs.

One of the operands for multiplication is represented as a7a6a5a4a3a2a1a0. The structure of AM_5 × 5 is shown in Fig. 5. The same operand can be written as a7a6a5a4a’3000, meaning that the value of a3 in the main operand is approximated by a’3 in the approximate operand. Actually, we implement 5-bit multiplication instead of 8-bit multiplication in this design.

A crucial point to note is that when a3 = ‘1’, the errors are not balanced, as indicated in Table 1. In fact, the operand’s eliminated bits are not compensated. A control signal is established to identify this state, as shown in (3). The definition of a’3 alone is insufficient when both a3 and a2 are ‘1’. Finding an alternative approach to defining ‘3’ is necessary to rectify the underestimated approximation of operand bit removal when the control signal is active. In the proposed approach, upon activation of the control signal, the associated partial product value, the most significant partial product derived from a’3 or b’3, is moved into a column with higher significance. When Ca and Cb are activated according to (3) and (4), it is necessary to compensate for the effect of the truncated bits. To compensate for the bits lost when removing operands, the most significant partial products from a’3 or b’3 are P7,3 and P3,7, respectively, and these are moved to a more substantial column during the partial products reduction stage.

The partial product P3,7 has been calculated using the Cb value. When Cb is activated, the partial product is moved to a higher-valued column called PCb, according to (6), in column 12. After activating Ca, the partial product of P7,3 is shifted to a higher-valued column, called PCa, located in column 12, as shown in Fig. 5, according to Eqs. (7) and (8). The compensating section can also be used for it. If the objective is to design an approximate multiplier with two operands having differing bit counts, the suggested approximation approach can be applied to each operand. On the other hand, we have employed a method to train the network to utilize approximate multipliers, resulting in improved accuracy when these approximations are used. In the proposed approach, we have trained the network layer by layer, as outlined in Algorithm 2.

Initially, the last layer is approximate, and the rest are exact. Then, the first two layers are approximate, and the rest are accurate, and so on, until all layers follow what is described in Algorithm 2 to be approximated. We have started the approximation process from the first convolutional layer. Progressively replacing the layers with approximate ones, layer by layer, can lead to improved accuracy due to a process similar to gradual fine-tuning. By doing this progressively, we allow the network to adapt smoothly layer by layer rather than handling a more considerable disruption that would occur if all layers were converted at once. Different strategies for using the AM_m×m approximate multipliers in CNN architectures

In our proposed approach, the CNN network architecture utilizes three types of approximate multipliers. Therefore, it is essential to provide a high-level overview of how to use these multipliers effectively in CNNs. While CNNs’ access to units with varying degrees of precision is a potential benefit, the management and implementation of these levels are crucial. To this end, we have suggested two strategies.

The first strategy is to build a matrix of MACs for each multiplier and then use the desired MAC for each layer. A diagram of this method is presented in Fig. 7. The main structure is inspired by the approximate tensor processing unit discussed in26. In this structure, the primary computations are carried out in a matrix of multiplication units. According to our first strategy, the matrix multiplication unit features three AM_mm approximate multipliers and the appropriate type of multiplier matrix is selected for each layer. In this approach, a matrix of identical multipliers will be activated when a layer with the required approximate multiplier is needed. This technique enables the selection of multiple multipliers flexibly through area occupation. However, since only one set of multipliers is engaged per layer, no additional power will be exerted on the structure. While utilizing three layers with different multipliers leads to an increase in total area, it is noteworthy that each proposed approximate multiplier occupies substantially less area than the exact multiplier.

The first proposed strategy for using the AM_m×m approximate multipliers.

In the second strategy, all three types of multipliers are accessible within the framework of a multiplication operation, as shown in Fig. 8. Therefore, the structure of each processing element (PE) is configured by selecting and activating the appropriate multiplier.

The second proposed strategy for using the AM_mmm approximate multipliers.

During a multiplication operation, which is shown by the yellow block in Fig. 8, the input is first directed to the appropriate multiplier by the demultiplexer, and the output is then transported to the final result by the multiplexer. A selector is used in this structure to choose each multiplier. The multiplexer and demultiplexer also introduce hardware overhead in this approach. Only the multiplier selected by the demultiplexer is active, while the other two receive zero inputs and consequently incur no dynamic power consumption.

Accuracy metrics

Trade-off evaluation

In approximation designs, the design must achieve an acceptable reduction in accuracy while utilizing enhanced hardware resources. Consequently, investigating both hardware efficiency and accuracy simultaneously is necessary for a thorough evaluation. CNNs use approximate multipliers; therefore, the network’s accuracy after using them is just as crucial as the hardware parameters.

To facilitate a more equitable comparison in the CNN section, a Figure of Merit (FoM) is delineated in (9). The inverse of the average drop in accuracy across the three CNN networks is evaluated to assess the performance of the CNN architecture.

Conclusion

This paper presented an approximation methodology for CNNs. The necessary features for an approximate multiplier to achieve effective CNN performance were initially discussed. Next, it was indicated how the accuracy level of the associated multiplier for each layer is related to changes in the variance of the distribution of weights for each layer. This article focuses on the distinctive characteristics of CNNs and proposes a more efficient approximation method. The following section details the proposed approximate multiplier, which utilizes CNN-specific properties to eliminate the operands in the suggested multiplier by balancing the positive and negative errors generated in the approximate multiplication framework. In AM_ m×m, m denotes the number of bits of the approximated operands. The paper describes three approximate multipliers. Additionally, a suggested approach, comprising two algorithms, is introduced for CNN applications. By considering changes in the variance of the weight distribution, we proposed a method for selecting an appropriate approximate multiplier for each layer in the proposed system. Also, an approach utilizing progressive layer approximation was introduced. In conclusion, to attain efficient NN architectures, it is preferable to construct approximate multipliers that align with the properties and architecture of the NNs. Designing and examining the approach of employing different multipliers in the CNN structure is another crucial element to consider, and two strategies have been proposed. The first strategy of the proposed design, implemented in VGG10, VGG16, and AlexNet, demonstrates enhancements of 86%, 95%, and 88% in energy per operation, respectively, compared to the 8-bit exact multiplier. Energy efficiency is enhanced by 81% in VGG10, 92% in VGG16, and 84% in AlexNet relative to the 8-bit precise multiplier for the second proposed strategy.

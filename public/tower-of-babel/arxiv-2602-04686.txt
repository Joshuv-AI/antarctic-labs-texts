Fourier integral operators on Orlicz modulation spaces
Serap Öztop, Joachim Toft, Rüya Üster

Abstract
. We establish continuity and Schatten-von Neumann properties for Fourier integral operators with amplitudes in Orlicz modulation spaces, when acting on other Orlicz modulation spaces themselves. The phase functions are non smooth and admit second order derivatives in suitable classes of modulation spaces.



0.
Introduction

The aim of the paper is to investigate
continuity properties for
Fourier integral operators
with non-smooth amplitudes (or symbols), when
acting on Orlicz modulation spaces.
In particular we extend continuity properties
of the Fourier integral operators in

[
7
,
8
,
58
]
, where related results were
obtained for such operators when acting on
classical modulation spaces (of Lebesgue types).
These earlier results are in turn extensions
and generalizations of pioneering results
and investigations performed by Boulkhemair in

[
5
]
, where
L^{2}
-continuity of
subclasses of our Fourier integral operators
were established.

We recall that Orlicz versions of Lebesgue type
spaces are obtained by replacing involved Lebesgue
norms with Orlicz norms, which are parameterized
with so-called Young functions. By choosing these
Young functions in suitable ways one recovers the
definitions of Lebesgue norms. Hence Orlicz type
spaces extend the notions of analogous Lebesgue type
spaces. In our situation, the family of Orlicz
modulation spaces contains the corresponding family
of classical modulation spaces.

A Fourier integral operator is an operator

\operatorname{Op}_{\varphi}(a)
, which is parameterized by the
amplitude (or symbol)
a
and phase function
\varphi
, defined
on (suitable extensions of) the phase space.
For appropriate

a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d+m})
and real-valued

\varphi\in C(\mathbf{R}^{2d+m})
,
\operatorname{Op}_{\varphi}(a)

is an operator from

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
which is commonly defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{\varphi}(a)f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}(d+m)}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d+m}}a(x,y,\zeta)f(y)e^{i\varphi(x,y,\zeta)}\,dyd\zeta,

(0.1)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(See
[
34
]
and Section
1
for notations.)
To some extent, the amplitude
a
quantifies the
amplification, while the phase function
\varphi

is linked to (non-homogeneous) modulations within
the systems.

In various situations were Fourier integral
operators are used, the right choice of the phase
function
\varphi
is crucial. In hyperbolic problems,
which is a common field for applying
Fourier integral operators, one usually assumes that

\varphi(x,y,\zeta)
is smooth when
\zeta\neq 0
and
positively homogeneous of order one with respect to the

\zeta
variable. In these hyperbolic situations, the adapted

\varphi(x,y,\zeta)
usually fails to be differentiable in the

\zeta
variable when
\zeta=0
. Furthermore,
one usually impose strong regularity assumptions on
the amplitudes, e. g. that they should belong to
subclasses of the Hörmander class
S^{r}_{0,0}
,
or that they should belong to so-called SG-classes.
(See e. g.

[
4
,
6
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
43
,
46
,
47
]

and the references therein.)

Roughly speaking, in contrast to the previous
assumptions adapted to hyperbolic problems,
in our situation we use the framework in

[
5
]
by Boulkhemair and in
[
58
]

concerning (lack of) regularity assumptions on
the amplitude
a
and phase function
\varphi
.
Especially we impose less restrictions
on
\varphi(x,y,\zeta)
outside
\zeta=0
, but
stronger
regularity with respect to
\zeta
when
\zeta=0
,
compared to the situation of hyperbolic problems.
Furthermore, we stress that in similar ways as in

[
5
,
58
]
, the regularity assumptions on the

are also relaxed compared to what
is common for Fourier integral operators.

In order to be more specific, suppose that

\omega
,
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
and

suitable weight functions, and that
\Phi

is a suitable Young function. Also suppose that

(of so-called Sjöstrand class), and that

satisfies the non-degeneracy condition

\left|\det\left(\begin{matrix}\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{y,x}&&\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta,x}\\[4.30554pt]
\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{y,\zeta}&&\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta,\zeta}\end{matrix}\right)\right|\geq d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.99997pt.

(0.2)

for some

. Then Theorem
2.2

in Section

asserts that

is continuous between the Orlicz modulation spaces

M^{\Phi}_{(\omega_{2})}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

We notice that we may choose

In particular, as announced above,

related results in

Preliminaries

In the section we recall some basic facts on Gelfand-Shilov

We also give some examples on Young functions,

and

1.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

For a real number
s>0
, the (standard Fourier invariant) Gelfand-Shilov
space
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) of Roumieu type (Beurling type)
consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s,h}}\equiv\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}\\
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{s}}

(1.1)

is finite for some
h>0
(for every
h>0
). We equip

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) by the canonical inductive limit
topology (projective limit topology) with respect to
h>0
, induced by
the semi-norms defined in (
1.1
).

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{t}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.2)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{t}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq s<t,

with dense embeddings.
Here
A\hookrightarrow B
means that
the topological space
A
is continuously embedded in the
topological space
B
. We also have

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\Sigma_{t}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\{0\},\qquad s<\frac{1}{2},\ t\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The
Gelfand-Shilov distribution spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, of Roumieu and Beurling
types respectively, are the (strong) duals of
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively. It follows that if

\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is the
L^{2}
-dual of

\mathcal{S}_{s,h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
s\geq\frac{1}{2}

(
s>\frac{1}{2}
),
then
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) can be identified
with the projective limit (inductive limit) of

\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect to
h>0
. It follows that

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s,h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(1.3)

for such choices of
s
and
\sigma
, see

[
26
,
38
,
39
]
for details.

We let the Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}
be given by

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

when
f\in L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle

denotes the usual
scalar product on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
The Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
Furthermore,

\mathscr{F}
restricts to
homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and on
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and to a unitary operator on

.
Similar facts hold true
with partial Fourier transforms in place of
Fourier transform.

Let

be fixed.
Then the

Fourier transform

, defined by

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}(f\,\overline{\phi(\,\cdot\,-x)})(\xi),\quad x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.4)

In some situations it is convenient to use
the small modification

\displaystyle T_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}(f((\,\cdot\,+x))\,\overline{\phi})(\xi),\quad x,\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}

(1.5)

of

. By a straight-forward change of

T_{\phi}f(x,\xi)=e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

to
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and restricts to a continuous map

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
The same conclusion holds with

in place of

.

1.2.
Weight functions

A
weight
or
weight function
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is a
positive function
\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that
1/\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
The weight
\omega
is called
moderate
,
if there is a positive weight
v
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)v(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.9)

If
\omega
and
v
are weights on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that
(
1.9
) holds, then
\omega
is also called

v
-moderate
.
We note that (
1.9
)
implies that
\omega
fulfills
the estimates

v(-x)^{-1}\lesssim\omega(x)\lesssim v(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.10)

We let
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set of all moderate weights on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

It can be proved that if
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, then

\omega
is
v
-moderate for some
v(x)=e^{r|x|}
, provided the
positive constant
r
is large enough (cf.
[
28
]
).
That is,
(
1.9
) implies

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)e^{r|y|}

(1.11)

for some
r>0
. In particular, (
1.10
) shows that
for any
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, there is a constant
r>0
such that

e^{-r|x|}\lesssim\omega(x)\lesssim e^{r|x|},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.12)

We say that
v
is

submultiplicative
if
v
is even and
(
1.9
)
holds with
\omega=v
. In the sequel,
v
and
v_{j}
for

j\geq 0
, always stand for submultiplicative weights if
nothing else is stated.

For any
s>0
, we let
\mathscr{P}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set
of all weights
\omega
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)e^{r|y|^{\frac{1}{s}}}

(1.13)

holds for some
r>0
. In the same manner the
set
\mathscr{P}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of all
weights
\omega
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that
(
1.13
) is true for
every

r>0
.
We also let
\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

be the set of all
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)(1+|y|)^{r}

for some
r>0
.

Evidently,

\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{0,s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\qquad 0<s_{1}<s_{2}.

On the other hand, in view of
(
1.13
) it follows that
all weights in

\mathscr{P}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\mathscr{P}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

are moderate. Hence (
1.11
) gives

\mathscr{P}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{P}_{0,s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{when}\quad s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2}>1.

1.3.
Orlicz Spaces

We recall that a function
\Phi:[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
is called
convex

\Phi(s_{1}t_{1}+s_{2}t_{2})\leq s_{1}\Phi(t_{1})+s_{2}\Phi(t_{2}),

1.5.
Kernel operators and Schatten-von Neumann classes

For any topological vector spaces,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, we let

\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})
be the set of all linear and continuous
operators from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
. Suppose

V_{1}=\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})
,

V_{2}=\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})
and

K\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})
.
Then it follows that the map
T_{K}
, defined by

\langle T_{K}f,g\rangle=\langle K,g\otimes f\rangle,\qquad f\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}}),\ g\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}}),

belongs to
\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})
.
By the kernel theorem
of Schwartz it follows that the map

K\mapsto T_{K}
, from

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})
to

\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})
is bijective. For convenience
we let the topology of
\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})

be inherited from the topology of

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})
.

In what follows we recall some facts on Schatten-von Neumann operators,
given in
[
49
]
. Let
T
be a linear and continuous map from the
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}

into the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and let
j\geq 1
be an integer.
Also let

\mathscr{I}_{0,j}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
be the set of all linear and
continuous operators from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
with rank
at most
j-1
.
The
singular value
of
T
of order
j

is defined by

\sigma_{j}(T)=\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\equiv\inf_{T_{0}\in\mathscr{I}_{0,j}}\|T-T_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}},\qquad j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}.

Evidently

\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
decreases with
j
, and

\sigma_{1}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
is equal to the operator
norm
\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}}
of
T
.

Throughout the paper, all Hilbert spaces are assumed to be separable,
and observe that this is always the case for Hilbert spaces which
are continuously embedded in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
in view of
[
42
, Proposition 1.2]
.
However, we note that most parts of what is described here also
hold when
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are allowed to be non-separable.

In the following definition we present a broad family of
Schatten-von Neumann classes.

Definition 1.16
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces,

T
be a linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
and let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\ell_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Z}_{+})
be a Banach space.

(1)

The
\mathcal{B}
Schatten-von Neumann norm of

is given by

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}}=\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}\equiv\|\{\sigma_{j}(T;\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{B}}.

(2)

The
\mathcal{B}
Schatten-von Neumann class

\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

consists of all linear and continuous operators
T
from
\mathcal{H}_{1}

to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that

\|T\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}

of
\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})

is given through the norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}

Let
\Phi

\mathcal{H}_{1}

•

\mathcal{H}_{1}

\mathcal{H}_{1}

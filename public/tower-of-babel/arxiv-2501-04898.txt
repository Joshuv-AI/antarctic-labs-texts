Optimality and Adaptivity of Deep Neural Features for Instrumental Variable Regression
Juno Kim, Dimitri Meunier, Arthur Gretton, Taiji Suzuki, Zhu Li

Abstract
We provide a convergence analysis of deep feature instrumental variable (DFIV) regression ( Xu et al., 2021 ) , a nonparametric approach to IV regression using data-adaptive features learned by deep neural networks in two stages. We prove that the DFIV algorithm achieves the minimax optimal learning rate when the target structural function lies in a Besov space. This is shown under standard nonparametric IV assumptions, and an additional smoothness assumption on the regularity of the conditional distribution of the covariate given the instrument, which controls the difficulty of Stage 1. We further demonstrate that DFIV, as a data-adaptive algorithm, is superior to fixed-feature (kernel or sieve) IV methods in two ways. First, when the target function possesses low spatial homogeneity (i.e., it has both smooth and spiky/discontinuous regions), DFIV still achieves the optimal rate, while fixed-feature methods are shown to be strictly suboptimal. Second, comparing with kernel-based two-stage regression estimators, DFIV is provably more data efficient in the Stage 1 samples.

Our contributions.

We study the
deep feature instrumental variable
(DFIV) algorithm proposed by
Xu et al. (2021)
, where learnable deep neural network (DNN) features are employed to jointly optimize both stages. As a data-adaptive estimator, DFIV exhibits superior empirical performance compared with fixed-feature methods, most notably in cases where the structural function may have both smooth and non-smooth regions
(
Xu et al., 2021
;
Chen et al., 2022
)
. In this work, we provide minimax convergence guarantees for DFIV, and formally characterize the conditions under which deep neural features yield a performance advantage over fixed-dictionary approaches. These build on existing statistical analyses of DNNs
(
Suzuki, 2019
;
Hayakawa & Suzuki, 2020
, e.g.)
, which study ordinary regression settings, by contrast with our two-stage setting where two dependent DNNs must be jointly learned. Our result are as follows:

•

We prove an upper bound for the risk of the DFIV estimator
\hat{f}_{\str}
in terms of the number
m,n
of Stage 1 and 2 samples, respectively, when
f_{\str}
lies in a Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
. We obtain convergence rates with respect to both the projected pseudometric
\lVert T\hat{f}_{\str}-Tf_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}
(Theorem
3.1
) and the full non-projected metric
\lVert\hat{f}_{\str}-f_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}^{2}
(Theorem
3.5
).

•

We obtain information-theoretic lower bounds for NPIV in Besov spaces and demonstrate that DFIV can achieve the minimax optimal rate (Proposition
3.3
,
3.7
). Moreover, we show that DFIV can attain the optimal rate with Stage 1 samples
m\approx n
, whereas kernel IV estimators
require
m/n\to\infty

(
Singh et al., 2019
;
Meunier et al., 2024
)
.

•

We demonstrate a strict separation between DFIV and any fixed-feature estimator

1.1
Comparison with Existing Works

Error analysis of NPIV estimators.

Many existing analyses of NPIV study linear (kernel or sieve) methods, where the estimator is constructed by regressing against a known basis expansion in an RKHS or Sobolev ball; some are further shown to attain the minimax optimal rate
(
Ai & Chen, 2003
;
Newey & Powell, 2003
;
Blundell et al., 2007
;
Chen & Reiss, 2011
;
Horowitz, 2011
;
Chen & Christensen, 2018
;
Singh et al., 2019
;
Meunier et al., 2024
)
. It is also possible to relax the restrictions on smoothness via e.g. spectral or Tikhonov regularization
(
Hall & Horowitz, 2005
;
Carrasco et al., 2007
;
Darolles et al., 2011
)
. However, these methods do not readily extend to DNN classes, which are highly nonlinear in their parameters and misspecified for both stages.

Theoretical guarantees for adaptive neural network-based IV estimators are at present very limited.
Xu et al. (2021)
give an initial analysis of DFIV with no specific learning rate, under strong assumptions on
T
and identifiability.
Hartford et al. (2017)
;
Li et al. (2024b)
study a two-stage NPIV estimator with neural networks, however there are two key differences. First, from the algorithmic side, the two works both employ a DNN to learn the conditional density
f_{X|Z}(\cdot)
in Stage 1. However, our Stage 1 only needs to estimate the conditional mean of the relevant features for Stage 2, which can be much easier depending on the Stage 2 network. Second,
Li et al. (2024b)
only derive an upper bound in terms of abstract function class complexity measures. It is not clear whether the derived rate is minimax optimal since neither a concrete evaluation for DNNs nor a matching lower bound is provided.

Estimation ability of DNNs.

For ordinary least-squares regression, it is known that DNNs achieve the minimax optimal learning rate for various target classes and furthermore outperform fixed-feature estimators when the target function possesses low homogeneity
(
Suzuki, 2019
)
or directional smoothness
(
Suzuki & Nitanda, 2021
)
. These works build on classical separation results between adaptive and fixed-feature estimators
(
Donoho & Johnstone, 1998
;
Zhang et al., 2002
;
Dũng, 2011
)
. Our results in Section
4
extend this line of work to two-stage regression. A significant challenge of 2SLS with DNN is how can we control the smoothness of stage 2 DNN, as it is the target of stage 1 regression. This is achieved by using DNNs with smooth (e.g. sigmoid) activations, for which we extend existing approximation results
(
Bauer & Kohler, 2019
;
Langer, 2021
;
De Ryck et al., 2021
)
to obtain a new approximation guarantee for Besov functions which is both rate optimal in
L^{2}
norm
(
Suzuki, 2019
, see)
and also converge in Besov norm.

Background and DFIV Algorithm

2.1
Instrumental Variable Regression

Nonparametric instrumental variable (NPIV) regression typically has a treatment
X\in\XX
and outcome
Y\in\RR
, related via the structural function
f_{\str}:\XX\to\RR
and an unobserved
\xi
that affects both
X,Y
:

Y=f_{\str}(X)+\xi,\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi|X]\neq 0.

(3)

This implies that
f_{\str}(x)\neq\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x]
so that ordinary supervised regression methods cannot be used. Instead, we introduce an instrumental variable
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
known to be uncorrelated with
\xi
, that is
\mathbb{E}[\xi|Z]=0
, and satisfying the requirements (
2
). For simplicity we assume that
X,Z
are bounded and set
\XX=[0,1]^{d_{x}}
,
\mathcal{Z}=[0,1]^{d_{z}}
, equipped with the induced probability measures
\PP_{\XX},\PP_{\mathcal{Z}}
. By defining the projection operator

T:L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})\to L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}}),\quad(Tf)(Z)=\mathbb{E}[f(X)|Z],

eq. (
3
) can be described as the nonparametric indirect regression (NPIR) model
(
Chen & Reiss, 2011
)

Y=Tf_{\str}(Z)+\eta,\quad\eta=f_{\str}(X)-Tf_{\str}(Z)+\xi,

(4)

where
\mathbb{E}[\eta|Z]=0
, and hence we seek to solve the inverse problem
Tf_{\str}=\mathbb{E}[Y|Z]
. The problem is generally ill-posed, however; the solution may be ill-behaved or not unique
(
Nashed & Wahba, 1974
;
Carrasco et al., 2007
)
. To analyze this problem, we require a mild regularity of the noise.

Assumption 1
.

(i)

There exists
\sigma_{1}>0
such that
\eta|(Z=z)
is
\sigma_{1}
-subgaussian for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
.

(ii)

(For lower bound only) There exists
\sigma_{0}>0
such that
\Var(\eta|Z=z)\geq\sigma_{0}^{2}
and the KL divergence between
\eta|(Z=z)
,
\mu+\eta|(Z=z)
is bounded above by
\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}^{2}}
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z},\mu\in\RR
.

This is satisfied for example if
\eta|Z=z
is
N(0,\sigma^{2}(z))
-distributed with
\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma(\cdot)\leq\sigma_{1}
, which is a standard assumption in the literature
(
Bissantz et al., 2007
;
Chen & Reiss, 2011
)
.

Besov spaces.

In order to facilitate a learning-theoretic analysis, we suppose that the structural function
f_{\str}
belongs to a Besov space on
\XX
. Besov spaces are a well-studied class of functions of generalized smoothness, and include Hölder spaces, Sobolev spaces, and classes of bounded variation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s>0
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
. For
f\in L^{p}(\XX)
and
r\in\NN
,
r>s\vee s+1-1/p
, the
r
th modulus of smoothness of

is given as

\textstyle w_{r,p}(f,t):=\sup_{\lVert h\rVert_{2}\leq t}\lVert\Delta_{h}^{r}(f)\rVert_{p},\quad\Delta_{h}^{r}(f)(x)=1_{\{x,x+rh\in\XX\}}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(-1)^{r-j}f(x+jh).

Then the Besov space with parameters
s,p,q
is defined as the following subspace of
L^{p}(\XX)
,

B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)=\{f\in L^{p}(\XX)\mid\lVert f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}:=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\XX)}+\left\lvert f\right\rvert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}<\infty\},

where the Besov seminorm is defined as
\left\lvert f\right\rvert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}:=(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-qs-1}w_{r,p}(f,t)^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t)^{1/q}
if
q<\infty
and
\sup_{t>0}t^{-s}w_{r,p}(f,t)
if
q=\infty
.

The unit ball of
(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}})
is denoted as
\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))
. Moreover, Besov spaces on
\mathcal{Z}
are defined in the same manner. We have the following classical results
(
Triebel, 1983
)
:

•

For
s>0,s\notin\NN
, the Hölder space
C^{s}(\XX)=B_{\infty,\infty}^{s}(\XX)
. For
m\in\NN
,
C^{m}(\XX)\hookrightarrow B_{\infty,\infty}^{m}(\XX)
.

•

For
m\in\NN
, the Sobolev space
W_{2}^{m}(\XX)=B_{2,2}^{m}(\XX)
and
B_{p,1}^{m}(\XX)\hookrightarrow W_{p}^{m}(\XX)\hookrightarrow B_{p,\infty}^{m}(\XX)
.

•

If
s>d_{x}/p
then
B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)\hookrightarrow C^{0}(\XX)
.

•

If
s>d_{x}(1/p-1/r)_{+}
then
B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\XX)
.

In particular, Besov spaces with smoothness below the threshold

Assumption 2

(Structural function)
.

f_{\str}\in\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))
and
\left\lvert f_{\str}\right\rvert\leq C

Our analysis utilizes the B-spline system, which forms a

Definition 2.2

(B-spline basis)
.

The

\iota_{r}=\iota_{r-1}*\iota_{0}\in C(\RR)

\iota_{0}(x)=1_{[0,1]}(x)
. The

of order

\textstyle\omega_{k,\ell}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d_{x}}\iota_{r}(2^{k}x_{i}-\ell_{i}).

2.2
Two-Stage Least Squares Regression

A popular method for performing IV analysis is two-stage least squares (2SLS) regression. In 2SLS, the structural function
f_{\str}(x)
is modeled as a linear combination
u^{\top}\psi(x)
of fixed basis functions
\psi(x)
, for instance
Hermite polynomials
(
Newey & Powell, 2003
)
or reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS)
(
Singh et al., 2019
;
Meunier et al., 2024
)
. The weight vector
u
is estimated by performing two successive regressions. In Stage 1, the conditional mean embedding (CME)
\mathbb{E}_{X|Z}[\psi(X)]

(
Song et al., 2009
;
Park & Muandet, 2020
;
Klebanov et al., 2020
)
is approximated by another set of basis functions
V\phi(Z)
, where the coefficient matrix
V
is computed by solving the vector-valued regression
(
Grünewälder et al., 2012
;
Mollenhauer & Koltai, 2020
;
Li et al., 2022
;
Li et al., 2024a
)
:

\textstyle\hat{V}=\argmin_{V}\mathbb{E}_{X,Z}[\lVert\psi(X)-V\phi(Z)\rVert^{2}]+\lambda_{1}\lVert V\rVert^{2}.

(5)

In Stage 2, we obtain
u
by regressing
Y
against
\mathbb{E}_{X|Z}[f_{\str}(X)]\approx u^{\top}\hat{V}\phi(Z)
, where the Stage 1 CME estimate is utilized:

\textstyle\hat{u}=\argmin_{u}\mathbb{E}_{Y,Z}[\lVert Y-u^{\top}\hat{V}\phi(Z)\rVert^{2}]+\lambda_{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}.

(6)

Finally, the structural function is estimated as
\hat{f}_{\str}(x)=\hat{u}^{\top}\psi(x)
. Both stages can be solved in closed form by performing ridge regression, including for infinite (RKHS) feature dictionaries.

2.3
Deep Feature Instrumental Variable Regression

We now recall the
deep feature instrumental variable
(DFIV) regression algorithm
(
Xu et al., 2021
)
, which extends the 2SLS framework to incorporate learnable feature representations
\psi_{\theta_{x}}(x)
,
\phi_{\theta_{z}}(z)
in both stages. The respective hypothesis classes, equipped with the
L^{\infty}
-norm, are denoted as

\FF_{x}=\{\psi_{\theta_{x}}:\XX\to\RR\mid\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}\},\quad\FF_{z}=\{\phi_{\theta_{z}}:\mathcal{Z}\to\RR\mid\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}\}.

As in
Singh et al. (2019)
;
Meunier et al. (2024)
;
Xu et al. (2021)
, we do not presume access to samples from the joint law
(X,Y,Z)
, and instead are provided with
m
i.i.d. samples
\DD_{1}=\{(x_{i},z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
from
(X,Z)
for Stage 1, and
n
i.i.d. samples
\DD_{2}=\{(\tilde{y}_{i},\tilde{z}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
from
(Y,Z)
for Stage 2.

Stage 1 regression:
Given data
\DD_{1}
and fixed Stage 2 parameter
\theta_{x}
, the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}(X)]
is learned using the network
\phi_{\theta_{z}}(Z)
by minimizing the empirical loss,

\hat{\theta}_{z}=\hat{\theta}_{z}(\theta_{x})=\argmin_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\psi_{\theta_{x}}(x_{i})-\phi_{\theta_{z}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}.

(7)

The resulting estimate is also denoted as
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]:=\phi_{\hat{\theta}_{z}(\theta_{x})}(\cdot)
to emphasize that
\hat{\theta}_{z}
is a function of the current Stage 2 input feature parameters
\theta_{x}
.

Stage 2 regression:
Given data
\DD_{2}
and the Stage 1 estimate
\phi_{\hat{\theta}_{z}}
, we solve the following regression where
R:\Theta_{x}\to\RR_{\geq 0}
is an optional regularizer:

\hat{\theta}_{x}=\argmin_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\tilde{y}_{i}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](\tilde{z}_{i})\right)^{2}+\lambda R(\theta_{x}).

(8)

The final DFIV for estimate
f_{\str}
is returned as
\hat{f}_{\str}=\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
.

\theta_{z}
. A minimizer of Stage 2 is not guaranteed to exist as the output
\hat{\theta}_{z}
of Stage 1 may not depend continuously on
\theta_{x}
, but we may simply take an approximate minimizer

Remark 2.3
.

We note that jointly optimizing (

is itself a function of the current

.
The DFIV algorithm as originally proposed in

to be vector-valued mappings, and treats separately the linear maps

(eqs. (

Smooth DNN class.

In our paper,
\psi_{\theta_{x}},\phi_{\theta_{z}}
are chosen from a class of smooth DNNs, defined with depth
L
, width
W
, sparsity
S
, norm bound
M
and a
C^{r}
activation
\sigma
(applied elementwise) as

\displaystyle\FF_{\textup{DNN}}(L,W,S,M):=\Big\{

\displaystyle\mathrm{clip}_{\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C}}\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(L)}\sigma+b^{(L)})\circ\cdots\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\mathrm{id}_{\XX}+b^{(1)}):\XX\to\RR\,\Big|

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\in\RR^{W\times d},\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\in\RR^{W\times W},\mathbf{W}^{(L)}\in\RR^{W},b^{(\ell)}\in\RR^{W},b^{(L)}\in\RR,

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\,\lVert\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{0}+\lVert b^{(\ell)}\rVert_{0}\leq S,\max_{\ell\leq L}\lVert\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{\infty}\!\vee\lVert b^{(\ell)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq M\Big\}.

Here
\mathrm{clip}_{\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C}}:\RR\to[-\bar{C},\bar{C}]
is any bounded, 1-Lipschitz
C^{\infty}
function equal to the identity when
|x|\leq\underaccent{\bar}{C}
; we fix any
\bar{C}>\underaccent{\bar}{C}>C
and omit them from the notation. The use of smooth activations and smooth clipping is in order to ensure
\FF_{\DNN}\subset W_{p}^{r}(\XX)\subset B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)

(
Suzuki, 2019
)
and thus to obtain Besov norm guarantees for the DNN estimator. Moreover, we specifically consider sigmoid activations
\sigma(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
for ease of analysis, however the results can be extended to any sufficiently smooth activation e.g. SiLU, GELU, Softplus, as well as piecewise polynomial activations such as ReQU; see the discussion in Appendix
B
. Some of our results also hold when the ReLU DNN class, defined with activation
\sigma(x)=0\vee x
and
\mathrm{clip}_{\bar{C}}(x)=(-\bar{C})\vee(x\wedge\bar{C})
, is used instead.

3
Theoretical Analysis of DFIV

We now establish the sample complexity of DFIV when
f_{\str}
lives in the Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
with smoothness
s
. We obtain upper and lower bounds for both the projected error (Section
3.2
) and non-projected error with
p\geq 2
(Section
3.3
), and obtain precise conditions for when the minimax optimal rate is achieved. We begin by stating our assumptions on the projection operator
T
.

3.1
Link and Smoothness Conditions

The operator
T
plays a crucial role in determining the sample complexity due to the following factors.

•

The NPIV model (
4
) shows that Stage 2 essentially learns
f_{\str}
using data from the projected function
Tf_{\str}
, leading to a loss of information. Hence the rate will worsen depending on how contractive
T
is in an
L^{2}
-sense, which is usually quantified by a
link condition

(
Nair et al., 2005
;
Chen & Reiss, 2011
;
Meunier et al., 2024
, see e.g.,)
, here given as Assumption
3
.

•

The difficulty of Stage 1, where we estimate the conditional expectation
T\psi_{\theta_{x}}
for the output
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
of Stage 2, depends on the regularity properties of
Tf
for
f
in certain function classes. We will control this using a novel smoothness condition (Assumption
4
or
4*
).

•

To obtain the final non-projected rate
\|\hat{f}_{\str}-f_{\str}\|_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}
, we must start with the projected rate
\|T\hat{f}_{\str}-Tf_{\str}\|_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}
and then use a
reverse link condition
(Assumption
5
) to derive the former.

We now state and discuss each assumption in full. Denote by
P_{r}^{/k}=\spn\{\omega_{k,\ell}\mid\ell\in I_{k}\}\subset B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
the linear span of all B-splines of order
r
up to resolution
k
and
\Pi_{r}^{/k}
the projection operator to
P_{r}^{/k}
.

Assumption 3

(link condition)
.

There exists
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
such that for all
f\in B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
,

\lVert T(f-\Pi_{r}^{/k}f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}\lesssim 2^{-\gamma_{1}k}\lVert f-\Pi_{r}^{/k}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}.

(9)

That is,
T
is more contractive at higher resolutions. Assumption
3
, and the reverse link condition Assumption
5
, are equivalent to or follow from standard conditions imposed in the IV literature; for example, they follow from the link condition in
Chen & Reiss (2011)
and
Meunier et al. (2024)
stated in terms of the Hilbert scale generated by a certain operator.
2
2

2

This can be seen by taking the spectrum
\nu_{k}=k
in Assumption 1 of
Chen & Reiss (2011)
and restricting to the span of the first
N
basis elements or its orthogonal complement in Assumptions 2, 5 with link function
\phi(t)=t^{\gamma_{1}/d_{x}}
.
Note that
\rank\Pi_{r}^{/k}=\dim P_{r}^{/k}\asymp 2^{kd_{x}}
, hence (
9
) corresponds to polynomial (mildly ill-posed) rather than exponential (severely ill-posed) decay w.r.t. the number of basis elements
(
Blundell et al., 2007
)
.

We now discuss the issue of smoothness. Stage 1 aims to learn the conditional expectation
T\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
for the data-dependent predictor
\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}\in\FF_{x}
with the DNN class
\FF_{z}
. This is a separate regression problem on
\mathcal{Z}
, so its risk depends on the smoothness of
T\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
; a nonsmooth predictor
\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
(possibly due to overfitting) may have an irregular projection and end up incurring a larger error in Stage 1. Thus we would like to understand the smoothness properties of functions residing in the projection of the Stage 2 DNN class
T[\FF_{x}]
. In particular, an estimate such as the following is desirable.

Assumption 4*
.

T[\FF_{\DNN}]\subseteq C_{T}\cdot\UU(B_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Z}))
for each class of sigmoid or ReLU DNNs on
\XX
, for some
0<p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\leq\infty
,
s^{\prime}>d_{z}(1/p^{\prime}-1/2)_{+}
and
C_{T}>0
that do not depend on
L,W,S,M
.

Although Assumption
4*
may hold if
T
is sufficiently mollifying (see the example below), it is quite restrictive as the class
\FF_{\DNN}
is much larger than the unit ball of any Besov space
3
3

3

Even when using a smooth DNN class, the Besov norm of a generic DNN can scale polynomially in the specified weight norm bound, which we in turn take to scale polynomially in
m,n
in our results.
and varies depending on design choice. However, motivated by the observation that the neural network
\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
is approximating
f_{\str}\in\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))
, we can use a much less restrictive assumption:

Assumption 4

(smoothness of
T
)
.

T[\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))]\subseteq C_{T}\cdot\UU(B_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Z}))
for some
0<p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\leq\infty
,
s^{\prime}>d_{z}(1/p^{\prime}-1/2)_{+}
and
C_{T}>0
. If
s^{\prime}\leq d_{z}/p^{\prime}
,
\PP_{\mathcal{Z}}
also has Lebesgue density bounded above.

The above formulation is a natural condition as it quantifies how much
T
preserves smoothness of the target Besov space. In comparison, the link conditions essentially quantify how much
T
contracts in an
L^{2}
-sense, giving us two distinct perspectives on the action of
T
. Assumption
4
also interacts with the link conditions to relate
s^{\prime}
back to
s
; see Lemma
3.2
for details.

We give some concrete examples in which Assumption
4
is satisfied. Suppose
T
is smooth in the sense that the conditional density of
X
given
Z
exists and satisfies a Hölder condition w.r.t.
z
,

|f_{X|Z}(x|z_{1})-f_{X|Z}(x|z_{2})|\leq\eta(x)\lVert z_{1}-z_{2}\rVert^{\alpha}\quad\text{for some}\quad\eta\in L^{1}(\XX).

It follows that
|Tf(z_{1})-Tf(z_{2})|\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{1}\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\lVert z_{1}-z_{2}\rVert^{\alpha}
for any bounded integrable function
f
. Since
C^{\alpha}(\XX)
continuously embeds into the Zygmund space
B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}(\XX)

(
Giné & Nickl, 2015
, Proposition 4.3.23)
, Assumption
4
holds with
s^{\prime}=\alpha
; in this scenario, Assumption
4*
also holds. At the opposite extreme, consider the case where the instrument is perfect,
d_{x}=d_{z}
and
T
restricts to a quasi-isometry between Besov spaces of the same order, the simplest example being
T=\mathrm{id}_{\XX}
. Then Assumption
4
holds with
s^{\prime}=s
, however Assumption
4*
cannot be true. Thus Assumption
4
is a much more general condition encompassing both high and low degrees of smoothing.

Controlling Stage 2 smoothness.

In order to replace Assumption
4*
with Assumption
4
, we need to ensure that the Stage 2 predictor
\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}
lies in the
\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))
, up to normalization. Using the smooth DNN class ensures
\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}\in B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
but does not by itself guarantee a norm bound (see footnote
3
). A simple way to ensure this is to restrict the domain as follows:

\Theta_{x}^{\prime}=\left\{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}\,\Big|\,|\psi_{\theta_{x}}|_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}\leq C_{W}\right\}.

(10)

In practice, since the restriction cannot be applied a priori, we can run the DFIV algorithm from random initialization and simply discard solutions which are ill-behaved: we show that the subset (
10
) still contains a rate-optimal solution. A more sophisticated method to guarantee smoothness which we also consider in our theoretical framework is to explicitly add the Besov (semi)norm as a penalty to be optimized for in Stage 2,

R(\theta_{x})=|\psi_{\theta_{x}}|_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}^{\bar{q}}\quad\text{or}\quad R(\theta_{x})=\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}^{\bar{q}}

(11)

for any exponent
{\bar{q}}>2
. The seminorm can be easily estimated from its equivalent discretization
|f|_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}\asymp(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[2^{ks}w_{r,p}(f,2^{-k})]^{q})^{1/q}

(
Giné & Nickl, 2015
, p.330)
; the
L^{p}
component can be ignored since
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
is bounded. It is also bounded above by the Sobolev seminorm
|f|_{W_{p}^{r}(\XX)}=\sum_{|\alpha|=r}\lVert D^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\XX)}
due to the string of continuous embeddings
W_{p}^{r}(\XX)\hookrightarrow B_{p,\infty}^{r}(\XX)\hookrightarrow B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)

(
Giné & Nickl, 2015
, Proposition 4.3.20)
. This can be numerically computed or backpropagated through
\theta_{x}
to any desired accuracy independently of the data by differentiating the network output with respect to its input at mesh points; thus we assume
R
can be computed exactly for simplicity.

Such smoothness-based or gradient-norm penalties have been considered before. For example, regularizers of the form
\mathbb{E}[\lVert D\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{2}^{2}]
,
\mathbb{E}[\lVert D\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{2}^{4}]
(equivalent to the Besov seminorm penalty when
p=q=2,s=1
) or similar have been successfully used to improve training or generalization of deep networks
(
Gulrajani et al., 2017
;
Sokolić et al., 2017
;
Arbel et al., 2018
)
. Furthermore,
Rosca et al. (2020)
argue that smoothness regularization leads to benefits in inductive capabilities, robustness, and modeling performance.

3.2
Projected Upper and Lower Bounds

We now present the upper bound for the projected mean squared error (MSE) of DFIV. The proof is quite involved and is presented throughout Appendix
C
, with a brief sketch provided in Section
5
. We also require some new theory on rate-optimal estimation with sigmoid DNNs, which is developed in Appendix

.

Theorem 3.1

(projected upper bound for DFIV)
.

Under Assumptions
1
(i)
,
2
,
3
and Assumption
4
with domain restriction (
10
) or regularization (

) with
\lambda
asymptotic to the rate below, or Assumption

without regularization, by choosing
\FF_{x}=\FF_{\DNN}(\lceil\log_{2}d_{x}\rceil+1,\poly(m),\poly(m),\poly(m))
and similarly

to be smooth DNN classes, it holds that

\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1},\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert T\hat{f}_{\str}-Tf_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]\lesssim m^{-\frac{2s+2\gamma_{1}}{2s+2\gamma_{1}+d_{x}}}\log m+(m\wedge n)^{-\frac{2s^{\prime}}{2s^{\prime}+d_{z}}}\log(m\wedge n).

(12)

See below for discussion. Rather surprisingly, the rate in Stage 2 samples

depends only on the Stage 1 smoothness
s^{\prime}
, while the rate in Stage 1 samples

depends on the smoothness of both stages. We remark that
\FF_{z}
(and

Projected minimax lower bound.

We now demonstrate the minimax optimality of the above learning rate, under the following key assumption.

Assumption 5

(reverse link condition)
.

There exists
\gamma_{0}\geq\gamma_{1}
such that for all

2^{-\gamma_{0}k}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}\lesssim\lVert Tf\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}.

This is equivalent to assuming a polynomial rate for the

with the method in
(

appearing in (

. Comparing the sizes of the standard and projected unit balls, we can show:

Lemma 3.2
.

For Assumptions
4
and
5

s^{\prime}/d_{z}\leq(s+\gamma_{0})/d_{x}.

(13)

The lemma is proved in Appendix

and

Now the minimax lower bound for any estimator for the NPIV problem can be derived as follows. This is a nontrivial extension of the known lower bound in the Sobolev setting
(

;
Chen & Reiss, 2011

, as B-splines are neither orthogonal nor independent.

Proposition 3.3

(projected minimax lower bound)
.

There exists a distribution

such that under Assumptions

(ii)
,
2
,

\inf_{\hat{f}_{\str}:\textup{NPIV}}\sup_{f_{\str}\in\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))}\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert T\hat{f}_{\str}-Tf_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]\gtrsim n^{-\frac{2s+2\gamma_{0}}{2s+2\gamma_{1}+d_{x}}},

(14)

where

The proof is given in Appendix

Corollary 3.4

(projected optimality of DFIV)
.

If

n^{-\frac{2s+2\gamma_{0}}{2s+2\gamma_{0}+d_{x}}}\log n

We also observe two potential factors of suboptimality. If the inequality (

The control over the smoothness of

.

If

Optimal splitting.

We find that the sample requirement for Stage 1 of DFIV in Corollaries

,

is only

. In contrast, for kernel IV,

and

4
Separation with Fixed-feature IV

It has been empirically observed that DFIV outperforms existing methods especially when the structural function is non-smooth or discontinuous; see the experiments on the conditional average treatment effect and behavior reinforcement learning tasks in
Xu et al. (2021)
. In this section, we study the efficiency of DFIV in the setting where
f_{\str}
lives in a Besov space with
p<2
. In this regime, we rigorously establish a separation between DFIV and
linear
estimators including sieve or kernel-based algorithms, proving the superiority of deep neural features over non-adaptive methods.

For ordinary regression in a
d
-dimensional Besov space, the minimax rate over all linear estimators is known to be lower bounded by
n^{-\frac{2(s-\Delta)}{2(s-\Delta)+d}}
where
\Delta=d(1/p-1/2)_{+}
determines the degree of separation from the optimal rate
(
Donoho & Johnstone, 1998
;
Zhang et al., 2002
)
. This occurs because functions in the regime
p<2
are highly spatially inhomogeneous, consisting of both jagged (possibly discontinuous when
s<d/p
) and smooth regions. Adaptive models such as DNNs have an inherent advantage over linear estimators by allocating more complexity (i.e. choosing higher-frequency basis elements as needed) in regions of low smoothness to capture spatial variability efficiently
(
Donoho & Johnstone, 1998
;
Suzuki, 2019
)
.

A
linear IV estimator
is formally defined as any estimator of
f_{\str}
of the following form,

\hat{f}_{L}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}(x,(\tilde{z}_{i})_{i=1}^{n},\DD_{1})\tilde{y}_{i},\quad u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}:\XX\times\mathcal{Z}^{n}\times(\XX\times\mathcal{Z})^{m}\to\RR.

We only require linearity w.r.t. the Stage 2 responses, as the lower bound will again hold for any reduction to the NPIR model (
4
). fixed-feature methods such as 2SLS, nonparametric 2SLS
(
Newey & Powell, 2003
)
, Nadaraya-Watson methods
(
Carrasco et al., 2007
;
Darolles et al., 2011
)
, sieve IV
(
Newey & Powell, 2003
;
Chen & Christensen, 2018
)
, kernel IV
(
Singh et al., 2019
;
Meunier et al., 2024
)
, and moment-based methods
(
Zhang et al., 2023
)
are all examples of linear IV estimators.

We first show that all linear IV estimators incur a degree of suboptimality when the target function possesses low homogeneity. The following lower bound is proved in Appendix
E.3
by adapting the approach in
Zhang et al. (2002)
for univariate regression to IV regression.

Theorem 4.1

(lower bound for linear IV estimators)
.

Under Assumptions
1
(ii)
,
2
,
3
and assuming
\PP_{\XX}
has Lebesgue density bounded above, for
\Delta=d_{x}(1/p-1/2)_{+}
, it holds that

\inf_{\hat{f}_{L}:\textup{linear}}\sup_{f_{\str}\in\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))}\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\hat{f}_{L}-f_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\XX)}^{2}\right]\gtrsim n^{-\frac{2(s-\Delta)}{2(s-\Delta)+2\gamma_{1}+d_{x}}}.

This is not directly comparable with DFIV, however, as we assumed
p\geq 2
in Theorem
3.5
so as to not incur any looseness when evaluating (
16
) with the reverse link condition. More precisely,
f_{\str},\hat{f}_{\str}
had to be approximated using B-splines up to a fixed resolution cutoff, which lower bounds the truncation error by the best
N
-term linear approximation error or
Kolmogorov width
, which is suboptimal when
p<2

(
Vybíral, 2008
)
. Nonetheless, DNNs are capable of choosing from a much wider pool of basis elements to achieve the optimal
nonlinear
approximation rate
N^{-s/d_{x}}
for functions of low spatial homogeneity
(
Suzuki, 2019
)
. To account for such adaptive selection, we now extend Assumption
5
to varying linear subsets of a higher resolution horizon.

Assumption 6

(extended reverse link condition)
.

Fix
C^{*}>\frac{\Delta d_{x}}{s-\Delta}+1
and let
S
be any subset of size
O(2^{kd_{x}})
of the set of B-splines up to resolution
\lceil C^{*}k\rceil
. Then
2^{-\gamma_{0}k}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}\lesssim\lVert Tf\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}
holds for all
f\in\mathrm{span}\,S
.

The intuition is that
T
is more contractive for functions with higher complexity (i.e. requiring many basis elements to construct), and hence the tighter link constant
2^{-\gamma_{0}k}
holds for any subset with size similar to
P_{r}^{/k}
, instead of
2^{-\gamma_{0}C^{*}k}
guaranteed for the whole space
P_{r}^{/C^{*}k}
. Such a dimensional separation is in fact easily satisfied: for example, suppose
T
is the projection in
\RR^{D}
to the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{span}\{v\}
for a unit vector
v
such that
|v_{1}|,\cdots,|v_{D}|\geq\frac{c}{\sqrt{D}}
for some
c\in(0,1]
. Then for any size
D^{\prime}
subset
A\subset\{1,\cdots,D\}
and unit vector
w\in\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}\mid j\in A\}
,

\textstyle\lVert Tw\rVert_{2}=1-(\sum_{j\in A}w_{j}v_{j})^{2}\geq 1-\sum_{j\in A}v_{j}^{2}=\sum_{j\notin A}v_{j}^{2}\geq c(1-D^{\prime}/D).

Hence as long as
D^{\prime}=o(D)
(indeed
D^{\prime}=D^{1/C^{*}}
in Assumption
6
), it is possible that
T
is ‘minimally contractive’ on all subspaces
P
spanned by
D^{\prime}
basis elements (
\inf_{w\in P,w\neq 0}\lVert Tw\rVert_{2}/\lVert w\rVert_{2}\approx 1
) even as
D,D^{\prime}\to\infty
, but ‘maximally contractive’ on the entire space (
\inf_{w\neq 0}\lVert Tw\rVert_{2}/\lVert w\rVert_{2}=0
).

With this modification, we conclude the following strict separation in Appendix
D.2
. A notable consequence is that the assumption of maximal smoothness of
T
can be weakened to a range of
s^{\prime}
depending on the gap
\Delta
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the setting of Theorem
3.5
and also Assumption
6
, the upper bound (
15
) and Corollary
3.8
hold for
0<p<2
.

Hence comparing with Theorem
4.1
, DFIV is faster than any linear IV estimator if
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}
and

\frac{(s-\Delta)d_{x}}{(s-\Delta)d_{x}+\Delta(2\gamma_{0}+d_{x})}\frac{s+\gamma_{0}}{d_{x}}<\frac{s^{\prime}}{d_{z}}\leq\frac{s+\gamma_{0}}{d_{x}}\quad\text{cf. \eqref{eqn:sprime}}.

Remark 4.3
.

We also show a separation result for the projected rates in Appendix
E.4
. In this case, Assumption
6
is not needed since Theorem
3.1
is valid for all
p>0
, being independent of the reverse link condition. On the other hand, the lower bound requires an additional assumption that
\mathcal{Z}
is sufficiently ‘spatially covered’ by the projection operator so as to be difficult for spatially non-adaptive estimators. We then prove the projected minimax error for any linear IV estimator is at least
\widetilde{\Omega}\Big(n^{-\frac{2(s-\Delta)+2\gamma_{0}}{(2(s-\Delta)+2\gamma_{1}+d_{z})\vee(2\gamma_{1}+d_{x})}}\Big)
(Theorem
E.2
), which is worse than the DFIV upper bound (
12
) if e.g.
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}
,
T
has maximal smoothness and
d_{z}>(d_{x}-2(s-\Delta))\vee\frac{s-\Delta+\gamma_{0}}{s+\gamma_{0}}d_{x}
.

5
Proof Sketch of DFIV Upper Bound

In this section, we give a brief outline of our proof of the upper bound for DFIV in Theorem
3.1
which is one of our key contributions. The full proof is presented throughout Appendices
B
,
C
and the non-projected MSE upper bounds (Theorem
3.5
and Corollary
4.2
) are derived using this result in Appendix
D
.

First note that in the population limit of (
7
), Stage 1 is essentially minimizing the loss

\mathbb{E}_{X,Z}\left[\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}(X)-\phi_{\theta_{z}}(Z)\rVert^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{X,Z}\left[\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}(X)-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(Z)\rVert^{2}\right]+\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2},

and so factoring out the projection error, we may view
\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}
as the ‘effective’ Stage 1 estimation error. Denote the
\delta_{x},\delta_{z}
-covering numbers of the hypothesis classes
\FF_{x},\FF_{z}
as
\mathcal{N}_{x}=\mathcal{N}(\FF_{x},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)},\delta_{x})
and
\mathcal{N}_{z}=\mathcal{N}(\FF_{z},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})},\delta_{z})
, respectively. We begin by showing the following oracle inequality from the definition of
\hat{\theta}_{x}
as the empirical risk minimizer of (
8
) conditioned on
\DD_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert Tf_{\str}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]\lesssim\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{z}}{n}+\delta_{z},

where the second term can be further bounded by

\displaystyle 2\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+2\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}.

With some manipulation, the projected error can be bounded as

\displaystyle\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\leq\lVert Tf_{\str}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+2\lVert\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\hat{\theta}_{x}}]-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{z}}{n}+\delta_{z}.

Thus it becomes necessary to bound the expected
supremum
of the effective Stage 1 estimation error,
\sup\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}
over the hypothesis space, which is highly nontrivial. We aim to achieve this by controlling both the supremum of the corresponding empirical process

\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2},

and the supremum of their difference. Naively these quantities are evaluated by reducing to a finite cover
(\psi_{x,j})_{j\leq\mathcal{N}_{x}}
of
\FF_{x}
. This does not work for DFIV, however, since the Stage 1 estimator
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\cdot]
can be ill-behaved in general; approximating
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
by
\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}
does not guarantee that
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}]
is a good approximation of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]
. Instead, we construct an extended
dynamic
or data-dependent cover
\hc
, which maps to a subset of the product cover for
\FF_{x}\times\FF_{z}
, that approximates all possible combinations of
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
and
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]
. A similar construction
\cstar
is given for the population version of Stage 1 risk minimization. By converting to the supremum over
\hc,\cstar
and applying classical concentration bounds, we obtain the following general bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1},\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{x}}{m}+\delta_{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{z}}{m\wedge n}+\delta_{z},

in terms of the Stage 2 and Stage 1 (supremal) approximation errors and covering entropies. In particular, the Stage 1 approximation error for each
\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}
is determined by the smoothness of
T\psi_{\theta_{x}}
, which can be bounded by Assumption
4*
or Assumption
4
combined with domain restriction (
10
). If instead regularization is used, we can repeat the argument including the regularization term to also obtain a smoothness bound.

It remains to choose the appropriate DNN classes and explicitly evaluate the terms above. The approximation rates for DNNs equal the optimal nonlinear rate; this is known for ReLU DNNs
(
Suzuki, 2019
)
, but we prove this result for sigmoid DNNs together with a stronger Besov norm convergence guarantee in Theorem
B.8
, which allows us to make the above smoothness argument rigorous. Taking the DNN class variables to balance the approximation and entropy terms, we conclude the projected upper bound (
12
). ∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we developed a novel estimation error analysis for two-stage IV regression with neural features. We proved that the DFIV algorithm achieves the minimax optimal rate when the structural function lies in a Besov space and further demonstrated that DFIV can outperform fixed-feature IV estimators by adaptively learning functions with low spatial homogeneity. Furthermore, we showed that a balanced number of Stage 1 and 2 samples suffices to attain optimal performance. These results provide a rigorous foundation for the advantages of DFIV in terms of both adaptivity and sample efficiency, paving the way for further exploration of neural features for causal inference.

Appendix B
Deep Sigmoid Neural Networks

In this section we prove key results on the approximation of Besov functions by deep sigmoid neural networks. The results are obtained by carefully applying the Sobolev norm bounds developed in
De Ryck et al. (2021)
to the B-spline system. The main difference is that instead of relying on local polynomial approximation, we exploit the fact that B-splines are piecewise polynomials to give a sparse construction similar to
Suzuki (2019)
. This allows us to achieve rate optimality in the sense of best approximation width (Theorem
B.8
) as well as small covering number (Lemma
B.9
). Moreover, the proof will serve to demonstrate how to extend the construction to any
C^{r}
activation with a decaying part (e.g. either of
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\sigma(x)
exists) by manipulating Taylor expansions to approximate polynomials as in
Mhaskar & Micchelli (1992)
;
De Ryck et al. (2021)
;
Langer (2021)
, or by constructing piecewise polynomials exactly in the case of ReQU.

B.1
Preliminaries

We first present some preliminary results. Denote the dimension of the domain as
d=d_{x}
for brevity. It is easy to see that the cardinal B-spline
\iota_{r}
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
r
supported on
[0,r+1]
and
\iota_{r}\in W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)
,
\im\iota_{r}\subseteq[0,1]
. We make use of the following expansion
(
Mhaskar & Micchelli, 1992
)
:

\iota_{r}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{r+1}a_{j}(x-j)_{+}^{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}1_{[k,k+1)}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}(x-j)^{r},\quad\text{where}\quad a_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{r!}\binom{r+1}{j}.

(17)

Note that the indicator
1_{[0,1)}(x)
can be well approximated by the shallow sigmoid network

\delta_{B}(x):=\sigma(Bx)-\sigma(B(x-1))

for some
B>1
, whose translates form a smooth partition of unity over
\RR
.

Lemma B.1

(smooth decay of
\delta_{B}
)
.

It holds that:

(1)

\lVert\delta_{B}(x+1)+\delta_{B}(x)+\delta_{B}(x-1)-1\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}\lesssim e^{-B}
.

(2)

For all
0\leq k,\ell\leq r+1
with
|k-\ell|\geq 2
we have
\lVert\delta_{B}(x-k)\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([\ell,\ell+1))}\lesssim B^{r}e^{-B}
.

Here, constants depending only on
r
are hidden.

Proof.

(1) The
r
th order derivative of
\sigma
satisfies
|\sigma^{(r)}(x)|\leq r^{r+1}(e^{x}\wedge e^{-x})

(
De Ryck et al., 2021
, Lemma A.4)
, so that

\displaystyle\lVert\delta_{B}(x+1)+\delta_{B}(x)+\delta_{B}(x-1)-1\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\lVert\sigma(B(x+1))-\sigma(B(x-2))-1\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\lVert\sigma(-B(x+1))\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}+\lVert\sigma(B(x-2))\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2}{1+e^{B}}+2r^{r+1}e^{-B}\lesssim e^{-B}.

(2) For any pair
k,\ell
with
k-\ell\geq 2
it holds that

\displaystyle\lVert\delta_{B}(x-k)\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([\ell,\ell+1))}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\sigma(Bx)-\sigma(B(x-1))\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([\ell-k,\ell-k+1))}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma(B(\ell-k+1))+B^{r}\lVert\sigma^{(r)}(Bx)-\sigma^{(r)}(B(x-1))\rVert_{L^{\infty}([\ell-k,\ell-k+1))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1+e^{B}}+2r^{r+1}B^{r}e^{-B}\lesssim B^{r}e^{-B}.

The same bound holds when
k-\ell\leq-2
by symmetry.
∎

We also require a finer control over the boundary decay of
\delta_{B}
:

Lemma B.2
.

It holds for sufficiently large
B
that
\lVert\delta_{B}(x+1)x^{r}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}\lesssim B^{-1/2}
.

Proof.

We first note for all
j=1,\cdots,r
that

\displaystyle\lVert x^{j}e^{-Bx}\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert x^{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1/\sqrt{B}])}\vee\lVert e^{-Bx}\rVert_{L^{\infty}([1/\sqrt{B},1])}\leq B^{-j/2}\vee e^{-\sqrt{B}}\leq B^{-j/2}

for large
B
. We have that

\displaystyle\lVert\delta_{B}(x+1)x^{r}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert(1-\sigma(B(x+1)))x^{r}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}+\lVert(1-\sigma(Bx))x^{r}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle=\lVert\sigma(-B(x+1))x^{r}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}+\lVert\sigma(-Bx)x^{r}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}.

Then by the general Leibniz rule and the bound for
|\sigma^{(j)}|
above, the first term is bounded as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(-B(x+1))x^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert x^{r}e^{-B(x+1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}+\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}\frac{r!B^{j}}{j!}\left\lVert\sigma^{(j)}(-B(x+1))x^{j}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-B}+r^{r+1}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}\frac{r!B^{j}}{j!}\left\lVert x^{j}e^{-B(x+1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\lesssim e^{-B},

and the second term is bounded as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(-Bx)x^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert x^{r}e^{-Bx}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}+r!\left\lVert\sigma(-Bx)\right\rVert_{L^{p}([0,1])}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom{r}{j}\frac{r!B^{j}}{j!}\left\lVert x^{j}e^{-Bx}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\lesssim B^{-r/2}+\left\lVert\sigma(-Bx)\right\rVert_{L^{p}([0,1])}+(B^{-1/2}+\cdots+B^{-r/2}).

For the remaining term above we further have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(-Bx)\right\rVert_{L^{p}([0,1])}=\int_{0}^{1/\sqrt{B}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}{(1+e^{Bx})^{p}}+\int_{1/\sqrt{B}}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}{(1+e^{Bx})^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{p}\sqrt{B}}+\frac{1}{(1+e^{\sqrt{B}})^{p}},

and hence
\left\lVert\sigma(-Bx)x^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([0,1])}\lesssim B^{-1/2}
.
∎

The following lemma will also be used to control Sobolev norms.

Lemma B.3

(
De Ryck et al. (2021)
, Lemma A.6 and A.7)
.

Let
d\in\NN
,

and
\Omega\subset\RR^{d}

(1)

For

(2)

B.2
Smooth Approximation of B-Splines

Recall that the base tensor product B-spline is defined as
\omega_{0,0}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\iota_{r}(x_{i})
. This subsection is devoted to proving the following smooth approximation result:

Proposition B.6
.

For all
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists a sigmoid neural network
\check{\omega}
with depth
\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil+1
, width
\lfloor\frac{3r}{2}\rfloor d+4d
and weights at most
\poly(\epsilon^{-1})
such that

\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR)}+\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}\lesssim\epsilon.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
and let
\zeta
be the network given by Lemma
B.4
with error
\epsilon
and
M
replaced by
2r+3
. From the construction in
De Ryck et al. (2021)
we see that
\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}\lesssim\epsilon^{-r/2}
. Also, let
\pi_{2d}
be the network given by Lemma
B.4
with error
\epsilon^{\prime}
and
M=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}
, so that the weights of
\pi_{2d}
are bounded as
(\epsilon^{\prime})^{-1/2}\poly(\epsilon^{-1})
.

From the decomposition (
17
), we aim to approximate the multivariate polynomial
\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}
on the interval
\prod_{i=1}^{d}[k_{i},k_{i}+1)
by the networks

\displaystyle\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x):=\pi_{2d}(\delta_{B}(x_{1}-k_{1}),\cdots,\delta_{B}(x_{d}-k_{d}),\zeta(x_{1}-j_{1}),\cdots,\zeta(x_{d}-j_{d}))

and the tensor product B-spline
\omega_{0,0}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\iota_{r}(x_{i})
by the network

\displaystyle\check{\omega}(x):=\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{d}}\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x)=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{d}=0}^{k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}}a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{d}}\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x).

(18)

Note that we have used the symbol
\bullet
above to indicate a subscript to be iterated over dimensions
1,\cdots,d
in a consistent manner. By taking
\underaccent{\bar}{C}>1
, the clip operation will not affect the output due to the
L^{\infty}
bound, so it is ignored.

We proceed to evaluate the error
\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR^{d})}
by breaking down into several steps.

Multiplicative approximation error.

It is easily seen that
\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}\leq 2B^{r}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}
and

\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}\leq\lVert\zeta(x)-x^{r}+x^{r}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}\leq\epsilon+(2r+3)^{r}+r!.

Moreover,
|a_{j}|\leq 2
. Thus we can bound the error arising from the multiplication network
\pi_{2d}
(here using the stronger
W_{\infty}^{r}
norm) as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{\omega}(x)-\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}2^{d}\left\lVert\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x)-\prod_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}2^{d}\left\lVert\pi_{2d}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2d})-\prod_{i=1}^{2d}x_{i}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-M,M]^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\max_{i=1,\cdots,d}\left\{\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)},\lVert\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2])}\right\}^{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{d}(r+2)^{2d}\max\{2B^{r}\lVert\sigma\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)},\epsilon+(2r+3)^{r}+r!\}^{r}\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim B^{r^{2}}\epsilon^{\prime}

(19)

by applying Lemma
B.3
.

Monomial approximation error.

Next, it holds for all
0\leq j_{1},\cdots,j_{d}\leq r+1
that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\prod_{i=1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left\lVert\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\prod_{i=\ell}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left\lVert((x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}-\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i}))\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\prod_{i=\ell+1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\lVert x^{r}-\zeta(x)\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}\lVert x^{r}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}^{\ell-1}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}^{d-\ell}\lesssim\epsilon

and hence the error due to approximating monomials by
\zeta
is bounded as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right)\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}^{d}\left\lVert\prod_{i=1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim B^{rd}\epsilon.

(20)

Indicator approximation error.

For the approximation error of the indicators, one needs to be more careful since
1_{[0,1)}-\delta_{B}
is nonsmooth near boundary points. Nonetheless, the difference of the piecewise polynomials (
17
) at each knot
k=0,\cdots,r+1
is always of the form
(x-k)^{r}
which will smooth out the error terms. Restricting to each unit interval
J_{\ell}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1)
inside the box
[-r-2,r+2]^{d}
, that is for
-1\leq\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{d}\leq r+1
, gives that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}-\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}:|k_{\bullet}-\ell_{\bullet}|\leq 1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}-\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

(21)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}:\exists i^{\prime},|k_{i^{\prime}}-\ell_{i^{\prime}}|\geq 2}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}.

(22)

Here, we have split the sum over
k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}
into terms such that
|k_{i}-\ell_{i}|\leq 1
for all
i
, and the remaining terms which contain at least one ‘out-of-bounds’ index
k_{i^{\prime}}
with
|k_{i^{\prime}}-\ell_{i^{\prime}}|\geq 2
. Also note that we are now using the
W_{p}^{r}
norm on
J_{\ell}
in order to control the boundary remainder terms, although we will again upper bound by the
W_{\infty}^{r}
norm when necessary.

To bound (
21
), we rearrange the existing terms so that the sum of three neighboring translates of
\delta_{B}
suffices to smoothly approximate the true indicator up to boundary terms. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{\bullet}:|k_{\bullet}-\ell_{\bullet}|\leq 1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k_{i}:|k_{i}-\ell_{i}|\leq 1}\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{k_{i}}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j_{i}=0}^{\ell_{i}}\bigg(a_{j_{i}}(\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1))(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad-a_{\ell_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i})^{r}+a_{\ell_{i}+1}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)^{r}\bigg).

Expanding the above product and separating all boundary terms gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}:|k_{\bullet}-\ell_{\bullet}|\leq 1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}-\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1))-1\right)\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\cdots,d\}\\
S\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\left\lVert\prod_{i\in S}\left(a_{\ell_{i}+1}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)^{r}-a_{\ell_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i})^{r}\right)\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left\lVert\prod_{i\notin S}a_{j_{i}}(\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1))(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1))-1\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1))}+\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})+\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{j}-\ell_{j}+1)+\delta_{B}(x_{j}-\ell_{j})+\delta_{B}(x_{j}-\ell_{j}-1)-1\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([\ell_{j},\ell_{j}+1))}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1))}+\left\lVert\delta_{B}(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)(x_{i}-\ell_{i}-1)^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1))}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-B}+B^{-1/2}.

(23)

To isolate the boundary terms, we have used that
\lVert fg\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\Omega)}\lVert g\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\Omega)}
which is easily checked from the general Leibniz rule. The remaining terms can be bounded from above in a straightforward manner. Applying Lemma
B.1
(1)
and Lemma
B.2
gives the bound (
23
).

Finally, we use Lemma
B.1
(2)
to bound (
22
) as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}:\exists i^{\prime},|k_{i^{\prime}}-\ell_{i^{\prime}}|\geq 2}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}2^{d}\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}^{d-1}\lVert x^{r}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-2r-3,r+2])}^{d}\sup_{k,\ell:|k-\ell|\geq 2}\lVert\delta_{B}(x-k)\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([\ell,\ell+1))}

\displaystyle\lesssim B^{rd}e^{-B}.

(24)

Putting things together.

Combining the bounds (
19
), (
20
), (
23
) and (
24
), we have shown that the construction (
18
) approximates
\omega_{0,0}
on the box
[-r-2,r+2]^{d}
as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\check{\omega}-\sum_{\ell_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}1_{J_{\ell}}(x)\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\check{\omega}(x)-\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right)\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\ell_{\bullet}=-1}^{r+1}\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}-\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{\ell_{\bullet}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{j_{i}}(x_{i}-j_{i})^{r}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(J_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\lesssim B^{r^{2}}\epsilon^{\prime}+B^{rd}\epsilon+B^{-1/2}+B^{rd}e^{-B}.

It remains to bound the approximation error outside
[-r-2,r+2]^{d}
. We may decompose the domain into a union of sets of the form
(\RR\setminus[-r-2,r+2])\times\RR^{d-1}
and use Lemma
B.1
(2)
to bound the decay of the corresponding
\delta_{B}
component, and an argument similar to (
19
) yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR^{d}\setminus[-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{d}}\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x)\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR^{d}\setminus[-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\left\lVert\check{\pi}_{j,k}(x)-\prod_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\left\lVert\prod_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{B}(x_{i}-k_{i})\zeta(x_{i}-j_{i})\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR^{d}\setminus[-r-2,r+2]^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\left\lVert\pi_{2d}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2d})-\prod_{i=1}^{2d}x_{i}\right\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}([-M,M]^{d})}\max_{i=1,\cdots,d}\left\{\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)},\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}\right\}^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k_{\bullet}=0}^{r+1}\sum_{j_{\bullet}=0}^{k_{\bullet}}\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR\setminus[-1,2])}\lVert\delta_{B}\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}^{d-1}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}^{d}

\displaystyle\lesssim(B^{r}\vee\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)})^{r}\epsilon^{\prime}+B^{r}e^{-B}(B^{r})^{d-1}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}^{d}.

Moreover from the weight bound in Lemma
B.4
we see that
\lVert\zeta\rVert_{W_{\infty}^{r}(\RR)}\lesssim(\epsilon^{-r/2})^{2r}=\epsilon^{-r^{2}}
since
\zeta
has depth 2. Adding the resulting bounds finally yields

\left\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\right\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR^{d})}\lesssim(B^{r^{2}}\vee\epsilon^{-r^{3}})\epsilon^{\prime}+B^{rd}\epsilon+B^{-1/2}+B^{rd}e^{-B}\epsilon^{-r^{2}d}.

Hence for any
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}>0
small enough, we can ensure the above error is bounded as
O(\epsilon^{\prime\prime})
by taking
B\asymp(\epsilon^{\prime\prime})^{-2}
,
\epsilon\asymp(\epsilon^{\prime\prime})^{2rd+1}
and
\epsilon^{\prime}\asymp(\epsilon^{\prime\prime})^{2r^{4}d+r^{3}+1}
. In addition, we can check that the resulting network
\check{\omega}
has depth
\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil+1
, width
\lfloor\frac{3r}{2}\rfloor d+4d
and weights at most polynomial in
B,\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}
, thus polynomial in
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
.

For the
L^{\infty}
approximation error, one can go over the proof and check that the same bounds apply, discarding the bounds for all higher-order derivatives. Finally, the results hold even if
0<p<1
by replacing applications of the triangle inequality by the quasi-norm inequality.
∎

B.3
Error Rates in Besov Space

For a sequence of coefficients
\beta=(\beta_{k,\ell})_{k\geq 0,\ell\in I_{k}}
define the quasi-norm

\lVert\beta\rVert_{b_{p,q}^{s}}:=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[2^{k(s-d/p)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\right]^{q}\right)^{1/q}

with the appropriate modifications when
p
or
q=\infty
. To approximate an arbitrary function in a Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
, we make use of the following adaptive recovery result based on B-spline decomposition.

Lemma B.7

(
Dũng (2011)
;
Dũng (2013)
)
.

Suppose
0<p,q,u\leq\infty
and
\Delta<s<r\wedge(r-1+1/p)
where
\Delta=d(1/p-1/u)_{+}
. For any
f\in B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)
and sufficiently large
N
there exists
f_{N}
which satisfies

\lVert f-f_{N}\rVert_{L^{u}(\XX)}\lesssim N^{-s/d}\lVert f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}

and is of the form

\displaystyle f_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell}\omega_{k,\ell}+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell_{i}}\omega_{k,\ell_{i}},

where
(\ell_{i})_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\subset I_{k}
,
K=\lceil\lambda_{1}\log N\rceil
,
K^{*}=\lceil\nu^{-1}\log\lambda_{2}N\rceil+K+1
,
n_{k}=\lceil 2^{-\nu(k-K)}\lambda_{2}N\rceil
, with
\nu=\frac{s-\Delta}{2\Delta}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
chosen independently of
N
so that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}|I_{k}|+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}n_{k}\leq N
. If
\Delta=0
then
K^{*}=K
. Moreover, the sequence of coefficients can be chosen to satisfy
\lVert\beta\rVert_{b_{p,q}^{s}}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}
.

Together with Proposition
B.6
, this implies the following approximation result.

Theorem B.8
.

Suppose
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
d/p<s<r\wedge(r-1+1/p)
. For all
f\in\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))
with
|f|\leq C
and sufficiently large
N
, there exists a sigmoid neural network
\check{f}_{N}\in\FF_{\DNN}(L,W,S,M)
where

\displaystyle\textstyle L=\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil+1,\quad W_{0}=\lfloor\frac{3r}{2}d\rfloor+d,\quad W=NW_{0},\quad S=LW_{0}^{2}+NW_{0},\quad M=\poly(N)

such that
\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\lesssim N^{-s/d}
and
\lVert\check{f}_{N}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}
is bounded. If
d(1/p-1/2)_{+}<s\leq d/p
, the same result holds with
L^{\infty}(\XX)
replaced by
L^{2}(\XX)
.

Proof.

Consider the
N
-term approximation
f_{N}
given by Lemma
B.7
. For each B-spline
\omega_{k,\ell}
in its sum, we construct the network
\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}
by taking the network
\check{\omega}
of Proposition
B.6
and scaling its input as
x_{i}\mapsto 2^{k}x_{i}-\ell_{i}
; note that the scaling only changes the input weights by a factor of at most
2^{k}\leq 2^{K^{*}}\lesssim\poly(N)
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}-\omega_{k,\ell}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR)}\lesssim 2^{kr}\lVert\check{\omega}-\omega_{0,0}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR)}\lesssim 2^{kr}\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}-\omega_{k,\ell}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}\lesssim\epsilon.

Now consider the network
\check{f}_{N}
obtained by laying each
\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}
in parallel and scaling the output weights by
\beta_{k,\ell}
, so that (ignoring the clip)

\displaystyle\check{f}_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell}\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell_{i}}\check{\omega}_{k,\ell_{i}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f_{N}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\XX)}+\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f_{N}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell}\left(\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}-\omega_{k,\ell}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR)}+\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell}-\omega_{k,\ell}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell_{i}}\left(\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell_{i}}-\omega_{k,\ell_{i}}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\RR)}+\lVert\check{\omega}_{k,\ell_{i}}-\omega_{k,\ell_{i}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\RR)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|2^{kr}\epsilon+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|2^{kr}\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K^{*}}\left(\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}|I_{k}|^{1-1/p}2^{kr}\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\beta\rVert_{b_{p,q}^{s}}\sum_{k=0}^{K^{*}}2^{k(d/p-s)}2^{kd(1-1/p)}2^{kr}\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{K^{*}(d+r-s)}\epsilon=\poly(N)\epsilon.

Therefore by taking
\epsilon
(and hence all weights) polynomial in
N
, we can ensure

\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f_{N}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\XX)}+\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f_{N}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\lesssim N^{-s/d},

and thus
\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\lesssim N^{-s/d}
as desired. In particular, for sufficiently large
N
it follows that
\lVert\check{f}_{N}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\lesssim C+N^{-s/d}<\underaccent{\bar}{C}
so that including the subsequent clip operation does not affect
\check{f}_{N}
. Moreover, since the B-spline expansion of
f-f_{N}
has coefficient zero for all
\omega_{k,\ell}
with resolution
k\leq K
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert f-f_{N}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}\lesssim\left(\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}\left[2^{k(s-d/p)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\right]^{q}\right)^{1/q}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
,
K=\lceil\lambda_{1}\log N\rceil\to\infty
. Hence

\left\lvert\lVert\check{f}_{N}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}-\lVert f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}\right\rvert\leq\lVert\check{f}_{N}-f_{N}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\XX)}+\lVert f_{N}-f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}\to 0

and
\lVert\check{f}_{N}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}
is uniformly bounded.
∎

The
\delta
-covering number
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{S},\rho,\delta)
of a metric space
(\mathcal{S},\rho)
is defined as the minimal number of balls with radius
\delta
needed to cover
\mathcal{S}
. The covering number of
\FF_{\DNN}(L,W,S,M)
in
L^{\infty}
-norm can be bounded similarly to the ReLU DNN class
(
Suzuki, 2019
, Lemma 3)
with a slightly better bound.

Lemma B.9

(covering number of sigmoid DNN class)
.

If the norm bound
M\geq 4
, it holds that

\log\mathcal{N}(\FF_{\DNN}(L,W,S,M),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)},\delta)\leq(L+3)S\log MW+S\log\delta^{-1}.

Proof.

Consider
f,\tilde{f}\in\FF_{\DNN}(L,W,S,M)
given as

\displaystyle f=\mathrm{clip}_{\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C}}\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(L)}\sigma+b^{(L)})\circ\cdots\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\mathrm{id}+b^{(1)}),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}=\mathrm{clip}_{\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C}}\circ(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}^{(L)}\sigma+\tilde{b}^{(L)})\circ\cdots\circ(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}^{(1)}\mathrm{id}+\tilde{b}^{(1)}),

such that
\lVert\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}-\tilde{\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert b^{(\ell)}-\tilde{b^{(\ell)}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta
. Also denote
A_{L+1}(f)=B_{1}(f)=\mathrm{id}
and

\displaystyle A_{\ell}(f)=\mathrm{clip}_{\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C}}\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(L)}\sigma+b^{(L)})\circ\cdots\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{id}+b^{(\ell)}),

\displaystyle B_{\ell}(f)=\sigma\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(\ell-1)}\sigma+b^{(\ell-1)})\circ\cdots\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\mathrm{id}+b^{(1)}),

so that
f=A_{\ell+1}(f)\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{id}+b^{(\ell)})\circ B_{\ell}(f)
. Then
A_{\ell}(f)
is
(\frac{1}{4})^{L-\ell}(MW)^{L-\ell+1}
-Lipschitz with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-norm and
\lVert B_{\ell}(f)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert f-\tilde{f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}A_{\ell+1}(f)\circ(\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{id}+b^{(\ell)})\circ B_{\ell}(\tilde{f})-A_{\ell+1}(f)\circ(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{id}+\tilde{b}^{(\ell)})\circ B_{\ell}(\tilde{f})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{(MW)^{L-\ell+1}}{4^{L-\ell}}(W+1)\delta\leq 8(W+1)\left(\frac{MW}{4}\right)^{L}\delta=:\delta^{\prime},

assuming
M\geq 4
. Thus for a fixed sparsity pattern the
\delta^{\prime}
-covering number is bounded by dividing the range
[-M,M]
of all
S
nonzero parameters into intervals of length
\delta
and counting all possible combinations,

\left(\frac{2M}{\delta}\right)^{S}=\left(16M(W+1)\left(\frac{MW}{4}\right)^{L}\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}\right)^{S}.

Moreover the number of possible sparsity patterns is bounded as
\binom{L(W^{2}+W)}{S}\leq(L(W^{2}+W))^{S}
. Noting that
4(W+1)^{2}\leq 16W^{2}\leq M^{2}W^{2}
and
4L\leq 4^{L}
, we conclude:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\FF_{\DNN}(L,W,S,M),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)},\delta)\leq\left(16LMW(W+1)^{2}\left(\frac{MW}{4}\right)^{L}\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{S}

\displaystyle\leq\left(4LM^{3}W^{3}\left(\frac{MW}{4}\right)^{L}\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{S}\leq(MW)^{(L+3)S}\delta^{-S},

as desired.
∎

Lemma C.1

(oracle inequality for NPIR)

4
4

4

Now let

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

(26)

\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2},

C.2
Constructing the Dynamic Cover

We first introduce the essential tools for our proof technique. Fix
\delta_{x},\delta_{z}>0
. Let the functions
\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}
for
j=1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}_{x}:=\mathcal{N}(\FF_{x},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)},\delta_{x})
form a
\delta_{x}
-cover of
\FF_{x}
and let
\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}
for
k=1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}_{z}:=\mathcal{N}(\FF_{z},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})},\delta_{z})
be a
\delta_{z}
-cover of
\FF_{z}
. Naively one would attempt to bound the desired supremum over
\FF_{x}
, say

\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2},

with the supremum over the cover

\sup_{j\leq\mathcal{N}_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}.

However this is not directly feasible since the Stage 1 estimation operator
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}
can be ill-behaved; approximating
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
by the element
\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}
satisfying
\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\leq\delta_{x}
does not guarantee that
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}]
is a good approximation of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]
, even in
L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})
-norm. Instead, we must construct an extended cover
\hc
that approximates all possible combinations of
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
and
\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]
. We also give a similar construction for the population conditional mean approximation.

Definition C.2

(dynamic extended cover)
.

The pair of elements
(\psi_{\theta_{x,j}},\phi_{\theta_{z,k}})
is said to be a
joint empirical approximator
of
\psi_{\theta_{x}}\in\FF_{x}
if

\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\leq\delta_{x}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})}\leq\delta_{z}

(30)

are both satisfied, which always exists for each
\theta_{x}
.
Similarly,
(\psi_{\theta_{x,j}},\phi_{\theta_{z,k}})
is said to be a
joint population approximator
of
\psi_{\theta_{x}}
if for the
L^{2}
-minimizer
\theta_{z}^{*}=\argmin_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}
corresponding to
\theta_{x}
,

\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\leq\delta_{x}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert\phi_{\theta_{z}^{*}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})}\leq\delta_{z}

(31)

are both satisfied. Moreover, the subsets
\hc,\cstar\subset\{1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}_{x}\}\times\{1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}_{z}\}
are defined as

\displaystyle\hc:=\left\{(j,k)\mid(\psi_{\theta_{x,j}},\phi_{\theta_{z,k}})\text{ is a joint empirical approximator of }\psi_{\theta_{x}}\text{ for some }\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}\right\},

\displaystyle\cstar:=\left\{(j,k)\mid(\psi_{\theta_{x,j}},\phi_{\theta_{z,k}})\text{ is a joint population approximator of }\psi_{\theta_{x}}\text{ for some }\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}\right\}.

Note that
\hc
is a random subset dependent on the data
\DD_{1}
and hence one must be careful when proving uniform bounds using
\hc
. In contrast,
\cstar
depends only on the operator
T
and predetermined covers
\theta_{x,j},\theta_{z,k}
. Also note that
|\hc|,|\cstar|\leq\mathcal{N}_{x}\times\mathcal{N}_{z}
by definition.

(40)

\displaystyle\qquad+(8\bar{C}+4)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}).

We now evaluate the expected value of each of the suprema (
37
)-(
40
) by adapting standard complexity-based arguments to exploit the definitions of
\hc,\cstar
. Again, the approximation error (
36
) will be analyzed later.

C.4
Bounding Subgaussian Complexities (
38
), (
39
)

Define the auxiliary random variables

\varepsilon_{j,k}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i}))\xi_{j,i}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i}))^{2}}},\quad 1\leq j\leq\mathcal{N}_{x},\quad 1\leq k\leq\mathcal{N}_{z},

where
\varepsilon_{j,k}=0
if the denominator is zero. Note that (
38
), (
39
) are similar to classical complexity measures of function classes, but with the noise terms replaced by the function-dependent residuals
\xi_{j,i}=\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(x_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})
. Nonetheless, conditioned on
z_{i}
we have
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{j,i}|z_{i}]=0
and since
\left\lvert\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(x_{i})\right\rvert\leq\bar{C}
, each
(\xi_{j,i})_{i=1}^{m}
is independently
\bar{C}
-subgaussian conditioned on
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
. It follows that
\varepsilon_{j,k}
is also
\bar{C}
-subgaussian, and repeating the tail bound argument in Lemma
C.1
over all pairs
(j,k)
we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{4\bar{C}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{j\leq\mathcal{N}_{x},k\leq\mathcal{N}_{z}}\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}\right]\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{x}}\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{z}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}}{4\bar{C}^{2}}\right)\right]\leq 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z},

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{j\leq\mathcal{N}_{x},k\leq\mathcal{N}_{z}}\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}\right]\leq 4\bar{C}^{2}\log 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}.

Moreover for each
(j,k)\in\hc
, the pair
(\psi_{\theta_{x,j}},\phi_{\theta_{z,k}})
constitutes a joint empirical approximator of some
\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}\in\FF_{x}
so that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}](z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}+(4\bar{C}+2)m(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}).

Therefore, (
38
) is bounded in expectation as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\frac{2}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})\right)\xi_{j,i}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\frac{2}{m}\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i}))^{2}}\varepsilon_{j,k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\varepsilon_{j,k}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})-T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\bar{C}^{2}}{m}\log 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+(2\bar{C}+1)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}).

In particular, the second term is simply half of the supremal Stage 1 error to be bounded.

Furthermore, almost the same argument for (
39
) with the cover
\cstar
yields the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\frac{2}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})\right)\xi_{j,i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\bar{C}^{2}}{m}\log 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}\right],

where the second term can be bounded by half the sum of (
37
) and

\displaystyle\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\leq\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+(4\bar{C}+2)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}),

similarly as before (a tighter bound can be obtained with more analysis since
\cstar
is not data-dependent, but this does not affect the result).

C.5
Bounding Supremal Deviations (
37
), (
40
)

We derive the bound for (
40
) first. Define the auxiliary functions

g_{j,k}(z):=\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z)-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z)\right)^{2}-\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}

and set
\kappa_{j,k}:=\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}\vee\kappa_{0}
for some
\kappa_{0}>0
. For each
(j,k)\in\hc
comprising a joint empirical approximator for some
\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}\in\FF_{x}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\kappa_{j,k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\kappa_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}^{\circ}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+(4\bar{C}+2)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})+\kappa_{0}^{2}

so that

\displaystyle\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\kappa_{j,k}^{2}\leq\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+(4\bar{C}+2)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})+\kappa_{0}^{2},

(41)

retrieving the supremal Stage 1 error to be bounded. In addition, it holds for all
z
that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{g_{j,k}(z)}{\kappa_{j,k}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{4\bar{C}^{2}}{\kappa_{0}},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{g_{j,k}(z)}{\kappa_{j,k}}\right)^{2}\right]\leq 4\bar{C}^{2}m.

Then for the sum over
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
, by Bernstein’s inequality we have the tail bound

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{g_{j,k}(z_{i})}{\kappa_{j,k}}\right\rvert\geq u\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{8\bar{C}^{2}(m+u/3\kappa_{0})}\right)

for all
u>0
. Hence the random variable

G:=\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{g_{j,k}(z_{i})}{\kappa_{j,k}}\right\rvert

satisfies by a union bound

\displaystyle P(G^{2}\geq u)

\displaystyle\leq 2|\hc|\exp\left(-\frac{u}{8\bar{C}^{2}(m+\sqrt{u}/3\kappa_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2|\hc|\exp\left(-\frac{u}{16\bar{C}^{2}m}\right)+2|\hc|\exp\left(-\frac{3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{u}}{16\bar{C}^{2}}\right).

Therefore for a cutoff
u_{0}>0
, we evaluate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G^{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(G^{2}\geq u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq u_{0}+\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}2|\hc|\exp\left(-\frac{u}{16\bar{C}^{2}m}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}2|\hc|\exp\left(-\frac{3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{u}}{16\bar{C}^{2}}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=u_{0}+32|\hc|\bar{C}^{2}m\exp\left(-\frac{u_{0}}{16\bar{C}^{2}m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+4|\hc|\left(\frac{16\bar{C}^{2}\sqrt{u_{0}}}{3\kappa_{0}}+\frac{256\bar{C}^{4}}{9\kappa_{0}^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{u_{0}}}{16\bar{C}^{2}}\right),

where we have used the fact that the antiderivative of
\exp(-\sqrt{u})
is
-2(\sqrt{u}+1)\exp(-\sqrt{u})
. We now choose
\kappa_{0},u_{0}
such that

\frac{u_{0}}{16\bar{C}^{2}m}=\frac{3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{u_{0}}}{16\bar{C}^{2}}=\log|\hc|\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\kappa_{0}=\frac{4\bar{C}}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\log|\hc|}{m}},\quad u_{0}=16\bar{C}^{2}m\log|\hc|,

which yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G^{2}]\leq 16\bar{C}^{2}m\log|\hc|+32\bar{C}^{2}m+4\left(16\bar{C}^{2}m+\frac{16\bar{C}^{2}m}{\log|\hc|}\right)\leq 16\bar{C}^{2}m(\log|\hc|+10).

(42)

Combining (
41
) and (
42
) and substituting in the value for
\kappa_{0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{j,k}(z_{i})\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[G\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\kappa_{j,k}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[G^{2}]}{2m^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\hc}\kappa_{j,k}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{80\bar{C}^{2}(\log|\hc|+9)}{9m}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+(2\bar{C}+1)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}).

Furthermore, a similar argument for (
37
) with the cover
\cstar
gives the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{j,k}(z_{i})\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{80\bar{C}^{2}(\log|\cstar|+9)}{9m}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+(2\bar{C}+1)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}).

Remark C.3
.

The above uniform bounds can also be obtained up to constants via localization and chaining techniques, see e.g. Theorem 14.1 and Corollary 14.3 of
Wainwright (2019)
. For this approach, we control deviations of empirical and population
L^{2}
-norms uniformly over the class

\FF_{\hc}:=T[\FF_{x}]-\FF_{z}=\left\{T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\mid(j,k)\in\hc\right\}.

One caveat is that the function class is usually required to be star-shaped, that is
f\in\FF_{\hc}
should imply
\alpha f\in\FF_{\hc}
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. However
\FF_{\hc}
is in fact only
nearly
star-shaped; while the output of a DNN in
\FF_{x},\FF_{z}
can generally be scaled by scaling the parameters of the final layer, the clip operation (when activated) prevents this for certain functions. This can be overcome by slightly extending
\FF_{x},\FF_{z}
to incorporate a scalable clipping
\mathrm{clip}_{\alpha\underaccent{\bar}{C},\alpha\bar{C}}
.

C.6
Putting Things Together

Plugging in the obtained bounds for (
38
) and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+(36\bar{C}+6)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}-\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{16\bar{C}^{2}}{m}\log 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

and so, substituting in the bound for (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+\frac{32\bar{C}^{2}}{m}\log 2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}+(72\bar{C}+12)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})

\displaystyle\qquad+3\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{(j,k)\in\cstar}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}(z_{i})-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}(z_{i})\right)^{2}-\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x,j}}-\phi_{\theta_{z,k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{2}\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{176\bar{C}^{2}}{3m}(\log\mathcal{N}_{x}+\log\mathcal{N}_{z}+9)+(78\bar{C}+15)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})

since
|\cstar|\leq\mathcal{N}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{z}
. Combining with the bound for (
40
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}-\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{80\bar{C}^{2}(\log|\hc|+9)}{9m}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+(10\bar{C}+4)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(T\psi_{\theta_{x}}(z_{i})-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}](z_{i})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{2}\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{68\bar{C}^{2}}{m}(\log\mathcal{N}_{x}+\log\mathcal{N}_{z}+9)+(88\bar{C}+19)(\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}),

and thus we obtain the upper bound for the Stage 1 supremal error as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1}}\left[\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}_{X|Z}[\psi_{\theta_{x}}]\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 9\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]+C_{2}\left(\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{x}+\log\mathcal{N}_{z}}{m}+\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}\right).

(43)

for some constant
C_{2}
depending only on
\bar{C}
.

Finally combining this with (

), we conclude that the projected

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1},\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}+\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{x}}{m}+\frac{\log\mathcal{N}_{z}}{m\wedge n}+\delta_{x}+\delta_{z}.

(44)

C.7
Error Rates for DNN Classes

Thus far, we have shown that the error can be bounded in terms of the (projected) Stage 2 approximation error, supremal Stage 1 approximation error, and covering numbers for the Stage 1 and 2 estimator function classes. We now evaluate each of these quantities for the introduced DNN classes to prove the final result. Concretely, for sufficiently large integers
N_{x},N_{z}
we set

\displaystyle\FF_{x}=\FF_{\DNN}(\lceil\log_{2}d_{x}\rceil+1,O(N_{x}),O(N_{x}),\poly(N_{x})),

\displaystyle\FF_{z}=\FF_{\DNN}(\lceil\log_{2}d_{z}\rceil+1,O(N_{z}),O(N_{z}),\poly(N_{z})),

as specified in Theorem
B.8
. As a technical note, we must also set the lower clip cutoff
\underaccent{\bar}{C}_{z}
of
\FF_{z}
to be greater than the higher cutoff
\bar{C}
of
\FF_{x}
. This is to ensure that the target of Stage 1, the conditional mean
T\psi_{\theta_{x}}
which has sup norm bounded by
\bar{C}
, is contained in the identity region of the clip for
\FF_{z}
and thus can be properly learned.

First, the projected Stage 2 approximation error (
26
) can be evaluated as follows. Let the
N_{x}
-term B-spline decomposition of
f_{\str}
according to Lemma
B.7
be

\displaystyle f_{N_{x}}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell}\omega_{k,\ell}+\sum_{k=K+1}^{K^{*}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\beta_{k,\ell_{i}}\omega_{k,\ell_{i}}.

By the adaptive recovery method given in
Dũng (2011)
, it holds that
2^{Kd_{x}}\asymp N_{x}
. Moreover, if the residual component
g=\Pi_{r}^{/K}(f_{\str}-f_{N_{x}})
along
P_{r}^{/K}
satisfies
g\neq 0
, by redefining
f_{N_{x}}
as
g+f_{N_{x}}
(modifying the coefficients of the B-splines up to resolution
K
if necessary), we can ensure that
\Pi_{r}^{/K}(f_{\str}-f_{N_{x}})=0
, and the approximation error of
f_{\str}
does not increase due to the Pythagorean theorem. It follows from Assumption
3
that

\lVert T(f_{\str}-f_{N_{x}})\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}\lesssim 2^{-\gamma_{1}K}\lVert f_{\str}-f_{N_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}\lesssim N_{x}^{-\gamma_{1}/d_{x}}N_{x}^{-s/d_{x}}.

Here, we have bounded the
L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})
-norm by the
L^{\infty}(\XX)
-norm in the continuous regime
s\geq d_{x}/p
and by the
L^{2}(\XX)
-norm in the discontinuous regime
s<d_{x}/p
via Assumption
2
.

On the other hand, by the proof of Theorem
B.8
, there exists a sigmoid neural network
\check{f}_{N_{x}}
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\check{f}_{N_{x}}-f_{N_{x}}\rVert_{W_{p}^{r}(\XX)}+\lVert\check{f}_{N_{x}}-f_{N_{x}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\lesssim\poly(N_{x})\epsilon

with weights at most
\poly(\epsilon^{-1})
. Thus choosing
\epsilon
so that this error is dominated by
N_{x}^{-(s+\gamma_{1})/d_{x}}
, possibly by increasing the norm bound of
\FF_{x}
by a
\poly(N_{x})
factor, we can ensure

\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\check{f}_{N_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}\leq\lVert T(f_{\str}-f_{N_{x}})\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}+\lVert\check{f}_{N_{x}}-f_{N_{x}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\XX})}\lesssim N_{x}^{-(s+\gamma_{1})/d_{x}}

for some
\check{f}_{N_{x}}\in\FF_{x}
. Since
\lVert\check{f}_{N_{x}}\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)}
is bounded, this construction is valid even with domain restriction (
10
) for a suitable
C_{W}
.

Next, under Assumption
4*
or Assumption
4
with domain restriction and
C_{T}=\Theta(C_{W})
, it holds for all
\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}
that
T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\in C_{T}\cdot\UU(B_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Z}))
by replacing
\Theta_{x}
by (
10
) if necessary. We also have that

\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})}\leq\lVert\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\XX)}\leq\bar{C}<\underaccent{\bar}{C}_{z}.

Thus by Theorem
B.8
(slightly modified to account for the constant factor
C_{T}
), we are guaranteed the existence of
\check{f}_{N_{z}}\in\FF_{z}
satisfying
\lVert\check{f}_{N_{z}}-T\psi_{\theta_{x}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Z})}\lesssim N_{z}^{-s^{\prime}/d_{z}}
, so that we also have

\sup_{\theta_{x}\in\Theta_{x}}\left[\inf_{\theta_{z}\in\Theta_{z}}\lVert T\psi_{\theta_{x}}-\phi_{\theta_{z}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]\lesssim N_{z}^{-2s^{\prime}/d_{z}}.

Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
B.9
that
\mathcal{N}_{x}\lesssim N_{x}\log(\delta_{x}^{-1}N_{x})
and
\mathcal{N}_{z}\lesssim N_{z}\log(\delta_{z}^{-1}N_{z})
. Hence by setting
\delta_{x}\asymp N_{x}^{-(s+\gamma_{1})/d_{x}}
,
\delta_{z}\asymp N_{z}^{-2s^{\prime}/d_{z}}
and substituting in (
44
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\DD_{1},\DD_{2}}\left[\lVert Tf_{\str}-T\hat{f}_{\str}\rVert_{L^{2}(\PP_{\mathcal{Z}})}^{2}\right]\lesssim N_{x}^{-\frac{2s+2\gamma_{1}}{d_{x}}}+N_{z}^{-\frac{2s^{\prime}}{d_{z}}}+\frac{N_{x}\log N_{x}}{m}+\frac{N_{z}\log N_{z}}{m\wedge n},

and taking
N_{x}\asymp m^{\frac{d_{x}}{2s+2\gamma_{1}+d_{x}}}
and
N_{z}\asymp(m\wedge n)^{\frac{d_{z}}{2s^{\prime}+d_{z}}}
, we finally conclude:

If

On the other hand, the construction in the previous section gives a subset of

with log cardinality

and separation

\displaystyle\lesssim\log\mathcal{N}(T[\UU(B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX))],L^{2}([0,1]^{d_{z}}),\delta)

\displaystyle\asymp\left(2^{(s+\gamma_{0})k}\right)^{\frac{d_{z}}{s^{\prime}}},

and taking the resolution of the subset

.

Finally, we verify that equality can be achieved if

and
T
acts on B-splines as

. Let

be an arbitrary element of

with B-spline decomposition

Then it follows from the sequence norm equivalence that

\displaystyle\lVert Tf\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s+\gamma_{0}}(\mathcal{Z})}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[2^{k(s+\gamma_{0}-d_{z}/p)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|2^{-\gamma_{0}k}\beta_{k,\ell}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\right]^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[2^{k(s-d_{z}/p)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}}|\beta_{k,\ell}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\right]^{q}\right)^{1/q}\asymp\lVert f\rVert_{B_{p,q}^{s}(\XX)},

implying that

for some constant

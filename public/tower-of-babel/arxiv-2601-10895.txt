On the rational points in conics of a cubic surface
Chunhui Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we give a uniform upper bound on the rational points of bounded height provided by conics in a cubic surface. For this target, we give a generalized version of the global determinant method of Salberger by Arakelov geometry.



\alttitle

Autour des points rationnels dans coniques d’une surface cubique

{altabstract}

Dans cet article, on donnera une borne uniforme des points rationnels de hauteur bornée fourni par les coniques dans une surface cubique. Pour le but, on donne une version généralisée de la méthode globale de déterminant de Salberger par la géométrie d’Arakelov.

1
Introduction

In number theory and arithmetic geometry, studying the density of rational points on arithmetic varieties is a central subject. In fact, we have the following way to describe the density of rational points in a projective variety over a number field. Let
K
be a number field and
X
be a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
. For a rational point
\xi=[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}]\in X(K)
with respect to an embedding into
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
, we define the
height
of
\xi
as

H_{K}(\xi)=\prod_{v\in M_{K}}\max\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\{|x_{i}|_{v}^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\}.

Let

S(X;B)=\{\xi\in X(K)\mid H_{K}(\xi)\leqslant B\},

where we omit the closed immersion of
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
. By the Northcott property, we have

\#S(X;B)<+\infty

for a fixed positive real number
B
. Thus, we may regard
\#S(X;B)
as a function of
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
, which is useful for studying the density of rational points on
X
.

1.1
History of determinant methods

There are various methods to study the number of rational points of bounded height in arithmetic varieties, and we will focus on the so-called
determinant method
. This method plays a significant role in studying uniform upper bounds for
\#S(X;B)
, as a function of
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 1}
, over certain families of arithmetic varieties
X
. Roughly speaking, over
\mathbb{Q}
, one expects that for a certain degree, monomials evaluated at points in
S(X;B)
with the same reduction modulo certain primes form a matrix whose determinant vanishes due to a local estimate. In this process, the famous Siegel’s lemma is applied, whence the name determinant method.

1.1.1

In
[
3
]
(see also
[
44
]
), Bombieri and Pila introduced a determinant argument for plane affine curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, proving that
\#S(X;B)\ll_{\delta,\epsilon}B^{2/\deg(X)+\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon>0
and all absolutely irreducible curves
X
.

Heath-Brown generalized the work in
[
3
]
to the higher dimensional case over
\mathbb{Q}
in
[
31
]
. In addition to obtaining fruitful results on uniform upper bounds for
\#S(X;B)
for different kinds of
X
, he also formulated the famous
dimension growth conjecture
, which says that for any geometrically integral variety
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
, if
\dim(X)=d\geqslant 2
and
\deg(X)=\delta\geqslant 2
, we have

\#S(X;B)\ll_{d,\delta,\epsilon}B^{d+\epsilon}

for all
\epsilon>0
, while Serre
[
53
]
proposed a non-uniform version. This conjecture has been a major motivation for the development of the determinant method. This work was generalized by Broberg
[
6
]
to an arbitrary number field.

In order to resolve the dimension growth conjecture, considerable effort has been devoted to refining the determinant method, for example, in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
20
,
10
,
47
]
by Browning, Ellenberg, Heath-Brown, Venkatesh, Salberger etc. In particular, Salberger
[
50
]
proposed the so-called global determinant method, which proved the dimension growth conjecture for the degree greater than
4
.

Some results in
[
50
]
were improved by
[
58
,
13
]
, and was generalized over a global field by
[
43
]
.

1.1.2

For studying the contribution of conics in surfaces to rational points of bounded height, Salberger
[
48
]
focused on the Hilbert scheme of conics in particular surfaces, and he obtained some uniform bound results on the complement of lines in surfaces. In
[
49
,
50
]
, the previous method was improved.

The main idea is to embed the Hilbert scheme into a particular Grassmannian, and consider its Plücker coordinate. By this operation, we may give a quantitative description of the height of a conic by that of its corresponding point via the Plücker coordinate. Using geometric and arithmetic properties of the Hilbert scheme embedded in a suitable projective space, one can quantify the intuition that a surface cannot contain too many conics of small height. This estimate helps control the number of points of bounded height lying on conics.

1.1.3

The determinant method can be formulated by Arakelov geometry. In
[
15
,
16
]
, H. Chen reformulated the work of Salberger
[
47
]
. By this formulation, it is easier to obtain an explicit estimate and to work over an arbitrary number field compared with
[
6
,
43
]
. In the formulation of Arakelov geometry, the role of Siegel’s lemma was replaced by the evaluation map in the slope method of Bost
[
4
]
.

Following the strategy of
[
15
,
16
]
, the author reformulated the global determinant method of
[
50
,
13
]
using Arakelov geometry in
[
39
]
, while
[
43
]
applied the traditional formulation over a global field around the same time.

1.2
The density of rational and integral points in cubic surfaces

In this article, we generalize the global determinant method of Salberger
[
50
]
, and see also
[
13
,
43
]
for the study of more general objects. More precisely, instead of treating a usual hypersurface, we build the global determinant method for varieties which are hypersurfaces in a linear subvariety of the base space in Theorem
2.7.2
. For this purpose, a uniform lower bound for the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel function with an optimal leading term, established in
[
40
]
for such varieties, is obligatory.

This allows us to deal with the conics in a cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
differently. Since a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
lies in a plane, the generalized global determinant method applies in this setting.

1.2.1

We proved the following result on the density of rational points in conics in a cubic surface in Theorem
6.3.4
.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a non-ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{K}
, then there is a constant
C_{0}(K)
depending only on
K
, such that the conics in
X
contribute at most

C_{0}(K)B^{\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}+1}\max\{\log B,2\}

rational points of height smaller than
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
.

1.2.2

Motivated by
[
10
,
50
,
13
,
43
]
, it is important to study the affine hypersurface which is not a cylinder and whose homogeneous highest degree part is absolutely irreducible. In fact, if we can prove the affine version of dimension growth conjecture for a particular degree, we will prove the general version for the same degree.

We have an affine version of the global determinant method in Theorem
4.2.3
for hypersurfaces in a linear subvariety. As an application, the result below is proved in Theorem
6.4.4
.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a surface in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{K}
, which is not a cylinder and defined by a polynomial of degree
3
with an absolutely irreducible highest degree homogeneous part. Then there is a constant
C^{\prime}_{0}(K)
depending only on
K
, such that the conics in
X
contribute at most

C^{\prime}_{0}(K)B^{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\max\{\log B,2\}

integral points of height smaller than
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
.

1.3
The role of Chow forms and Cayley forms

By the arithmetic intersection theory developed in
[
25
]
, the height of a variety, in particular, a closed point, can be considered as a kind of arithmetic intersection number. In
[
5
]
, several heights of a same arithmetic variety are compared uniformly, in particular, the naive of the polynomial which defines its Chow form (or called Cayley form for the Plücker coordinate case in this article), and a useful version was given in
[
36
, Proposition 3.7]
. This allows us to involve the height of a particular homogeneous highest degree part of the so-called Cayley form in the affine version of global determinant method in the above Theorem
4.2.3
.

By the functoriality of Deligne pairing of the arithmetic intersection in
[
59
]
, the height of a quadratic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
can be related to the height of its corresponding point in the Hilbert scheme. Then we can give an upper bound of the number of conics of bounded height.

At the same time, for a non-ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
with the absolutely irreducible homogeneous degree
3
part, we can bound the homogeneous degree
3
part of its Cayley form from the Deligne pairing argument mentioned above. This fact is useful to control the contribution of conics to the integral points in this cubic surface.

1.4
Organization of the article

In §
2
, a global determinant method which deals with the varieties which are hypersurfaces in a linear variety of the base space is built. In §
3
, we study some geometric and arithmetic properties of Chow forms and Cayley forms. In §
4
, we give a control of the auxiliary hypersurfaces which cover the integral points of bounded height with the help of Cayley forms. In §
5
, we provide some useful preliminaries about the geometric of cubic surfaces and Deligne pairing of Arakelov geometry. In §
6
, we give a control of rational and integral points lying in conics of a cubic surface.

2
Construction of determinants

In this section, we build a particular version of determinant method with the formulation of Arakelov geometry. This is an immediate generalization of the construction in
[
39
]
, where we will apply the uniform lower bound of arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel function in
[
40
]
.

2.1
Height of rational point

We begin by recalling some foundations of algebraic number theory and defining the height function. We refer the readers to
[
32
, Part B]
for a systematic introduction to this subject.

2.1.1

Let
K
be a number field, and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of
K
. We denote by
M_{K,\infty}
the set of infinite places of
K
, by
M_{K,f}
the set of finite places of
K
, and by
M_{K}=M_{K,f}\sqcup M_{K,\infty}
the set of places of
K
.

For each
v\in M_{K}
and
x\in K
, we define the absolute value

|x|_{v}=\left|N_{K_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}}(x)\right|_{v}^{1/[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]},

which extends the usual absolute values on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or
\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
denotes the usual
p
-adic field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
v\in M_{K,f}
lies above the prime number
p
under the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

In this setting, the product formula (cf.
[
42
, Chap. III, (1.3) Proposition]
)

\prod_{v\in M_{K}}|x|_{v}^{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}=1

holds for all
x\in K^{\times}
.

2.1.2

Let
\xi=[\xi_{1}:\cdots:\xi_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}(K)
. We define the
height
of
\xi
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
as

H_{K}(\xi)=\prod_{v\in M_{K}}\max_{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}\left\{|\xi_{i}|_{v}\right\}^{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}.

(1)

We also define the
logarithmic height
of
\xi
as

h(\xi)=\frac{\log H_{K}(\xi)}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},

(2)

which is invariant under the extensions of
K
(cf.
[
32
, Lemma B.2.1]
).

2.1.3

Let
X
be a subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
, and
\phi:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
be the embedding morphism. For a
\xi\in X(K)
, we define
H_{K}(\xi)=H_{K}(\phi(\xi))
, and we will omit the embedding
\phi
if there is no confusion. We denote

S(X;B)=\left\{\xi\in X(K)\mid H_{K}(\xi)\leqslant B\right\}.

By the Northcott property (cf.
[
32
, Theorem B.2.3]
), the cardinality
\#S(X;B)
is finite for a fixed
B\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.2
Foundations of Arakelov geometry

In this part, we introduce some useful notions for formulating the subject within Arakelov geometry.

2.2.1

A
normed vector bundle
on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
is all the pairing
\overline{E}=\left(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}}\right)
, where:

—

E
is a projective
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of finite rank;

—

(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}}
is a family of norms on
E_{v}=E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K},v}\mathbb{C}
for each
v\in M_{K,\infty}
, which is invariant under the action of
\gal(\mathbb{C}/K_{v})
.

For a complex place, we consider it and its conjugation as two different places.

By the
rank
of a normed vector bundle
\overline{E}
, we mean the rank of
E
, denoted by
\rg_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}(E)
or simply
\rg(E)
if there is no ambiguity.

If for every
v\in M_{K,\infty}
,
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v}
is Hermitian, which means it is induced by an inner product, we say that
\overline{E}
is a
Hermitian vector bundle
on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. If
\rg_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}(E)=1
, we say that
\overline{E}
is a
Hermitian line bundle
.

2.2.2

Let
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{E,v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}})
and
\overline{F}=(F,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{F,v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}})
be two normed vector bundles on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We say that
\overline{F}
is a normed subbundle of
\overline{E}
if
F
is a sub-
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of
E
and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{F,v}
is the restriction of
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{E,v}
to
F
for each
v\in M_{K,\infty}
.

We say that
\overline{G}=(G,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{G,v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}})
is a normed quotient bundle of
\overline{E}
on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
, if
G
is a quotient
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of
E
, and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{G,v}
is a quotient norm of
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{E,v}
for all
v\in M_{K,\infty}
.

We denote by
E_{K}=E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K
for simplicity.

2.2.3

Let
\overline{E}
be a Hermitian vector bundle on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
be a
K
-basis of
E_{K}
. The
Arakelov degree
of
\overline{E}
defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\deg}(\overline{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{v\in M_{K}}[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]\|s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{r}\|_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\#E/(\mathcal{O}_{K}s_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{O}_{K}s_{r})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in M_{K,\infty}}[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]\log\det(\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle_{v,1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r}),

where
\|s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{r}\|_{v}
follows the definition
[
14
, §2.1.3]
for
v\in M_{K,\infty}
, and
\langle s_{i},s_{j}\rangle_{v,1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r}
is the Gram matrix of
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}
with respect to
v\in M_{K,\infty}
.

For
v\in M_{K,f}
, we define
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v}
as the norm given by model. More precisely, let
\overline{E}
be a normed vector bundle on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and
x\in E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K_{v}
. We define

\|x\|_{v}=\inf\{|a|_{v}\mid a\in K_{v}^{\times},\;a^{-1}x\in E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{K,v}\}.

If
x\in E_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K
, we have

\|x\|_{v}=\inf\{|a|_{v}\mid a\in K^{\times},\;a^{-1}x\in E\}.

We refer the readers to
[
24
, 2.4.1]
for the proof of the above equalities. The Arakelov degree is independent of the choice of the basis
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}
by the product formula.

We define

\widehat{\deg}_{\mathrm{n}}(\overline{E})=\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{E})}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

as the
normalized Arakelov degree
of
\overline{E}
, which is independent of the choice of the base field
K
.

2.2.4

Let
\overline{E}
be a non-zero Hermitian vector bundle on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The
slope
of
\overline{E}
is defined as

\widehat{\mu}(\overline{E})=\frac{\widehat{\deg}_{\mathrm{n}}(\overline{E})}{\rg(E)}.

In addition, we denote by
\widehat{\mu}_{\max}(\overline{E})
the maximal value of slopes of all non-zero Hermitian subbundles, and by
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}(\overline{E})
the minimal value of slopes of all non-zero Hermitian quotient bundles.

2.3
Height functions with the formulation of Arakelov geometry

In this part we will formulate the height function of a closed subscheme in a projective space by Arakelov geometry.

2.3.1

Let
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
be a Hermitian vector bundle on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
of rank
n+1
, and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
be the projective space which represents the functor from the category of commutative
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebras to the category of sets mapping all
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra
A
to the set of projective quotient
A
-module of
\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}A
of rank
1
.

For a
D\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)
(or
\mathcal{O}(1)
if there is no ambiguity) be the universal bundle on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
, and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(D)
or
\mathcal{O}(D)
be the tensor product
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)^{\otimes D}
. For each
v\in M_{K,\infty}
, the Hermitian norm on
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
induces the corresponding Fubini–Study metric on
\mathcal{O}(1)
, which defines the Hermitian line bundle
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)}
(or
\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}
) on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
.

2.3.2

Let
P\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})(K)
, and
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})(\mathcal{O}_{K})
be the Zariski closure of
P
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
. For a Hermitian line bundle on
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
, we define the
Arakelov height
of
P
with respect to
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
as

h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(P)=\widehat{\deg}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{*}\overline{\mathcal{L}}\right).

(3)

2.3.3

We consider a special case below. Let

\overline{\mathcal{E}}=\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\oplus(n+1)},(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}}\right),

where

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v}:&K^{\oplus(n+1)}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})&\mapsto&\sqrt{|v(z_{0})|^{2}+\cdots+|v(z_{n})|^{2}}\end{array}

for every
v\in M_{K,\infty}
. In this case, if
P=[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
, then we have (cf.
[
41
, Proposition 9.10]
)

\displaystyle h_{\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in M_{K,f}}\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log\left(\max_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\{|x_{i}|\}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in M_{K,\infty}}\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log\left(|v(x_{0})|^{2}+\cdots+|v(x_{n})|^{2}\right).

Let
h(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
be the height defined at (
2
) on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the same as above. Then for an arbitrary
P\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})(K)
, we have

\left|h(P)-h_{\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}}(P)\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\log(n+1)

by an elementary calculation. This estimate ensures that it is reasonable to involve the Arakelov geometry in the study of counting rational points of bounded height.

2.3.4

Let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
be a Hermitian vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
over
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
X
be a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
of pure dimension
d
, and
\mathscr{X}
be its Zariski closure in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
. The
Arakelov height
of
X
is defined as the arithmetic intersection number

h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(X)=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(\mathscr{X})=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\widehat{\deg}_{\mathrm{n}}\left(\widehat{c}_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})^{d+1}\cdot[\mathscr{X}]\right),

where
\widehat{c}_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})
is the first arithmetic Chern class of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
. We refer the readers to
[
55
, Chap. III.4, Proposition 1]
for its precise definition.

When
X
is a simple point,
h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(X)
is just the height defined at (
3
), where
\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}
is replaced by an arbitrary Hermitian line bundle on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
.

2.3.5

We consider a more special case. Let
X
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
of degree
\delta
. By
[
30
, Chap. 1, Proposition 7.6 (d)]
,
X
is defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\delta
. We suppose that the above
X
is defined by the polynomial

f(T_{0},\ldots,T_{n})=\sum_{i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=\delta}a_{i_{0}\ldots i_{n}}T_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots T_{n}^{i_{n}},

then we define the
naive height
of
X
as

h(X)=h(f)=\sum_{v\in M_{K}}\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log\max_{i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=\delta}\{|a_{i_{0}\ldots i_{n}}|_{v}\}.

(5)

In addition, if
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is defined as above, then we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log((n+1)(\delta+1))+\delta\mathcal{H}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle h(X)-h_{\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}}(X)

(6)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(n+1)\delta\log 2+4\delta\log(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\delta\mathcal{H}_{n},

where
\mathcal{H}_{n}=1+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}
. This is a special case of Proposition
3.4
below, which is deduced from
[
36
, Proposition 3.7]
directly.

2.4
Construction of determinants

In this part, we provide the foundations of Arakelov geometry to build the determinant method, which is original from
[
15
,
16
]
.

2.4.1

Let
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
be the same Hermitian vector bundle on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
of rank
n+1
. For every
D\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
E_{D}=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{O}(D))
, and
r(n,D)=\rg_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}(E_{D})
. For each
v\in M_{K,\infty}
, we define the norm
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v,\sup}
on
E_{D}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K},v}\mathbb{C}
as

\forall\;s\in E_{D,v},\ \|s\|_{v,\sup}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K_{v}})(\mathbb{C})}\|s(x)\|_{v,\mathrm{FS}},

(7)

where
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v,\mathrm{FS}}
is the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathcal{O}(D)
.

Now we recall the following fact (cf.
[
57
, Theorem 3.3.6]
): for every symmetric convex body
C
, there exists a unique ellipsoid, whose volume is maximal contained in
C
. This ellipsoid is called the
ellipsoid of John
of
C
.

For the
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module
E_{D}
and a place

3
Geometry and arithmetics of Chow forms and Cayley forms

For a projective variety of dimension
d
embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, its Chow form (also called the Chow variety) parameterizes the
(n-d-1)
-dimensional linear spaces that intersect it non-trivially. By definition, the points representing these linear spaces form a subset of the corresponding Grassmannian.

For a pure-dimensional projective variety, it is well-known that its Chow form is a hypersurface in the Grassmannian, having the same degree as the original variety.

Following
[
15
]
, a hypersurface of this type defined using Stiefel coordinates is called a Chow form, while one defined using Plücker coordinates is called a Cayley form. In this section, we will follow the notations in
[
15
,
16
]
original from
[
5
, §4]
, and see also
[
36
, §2]
.

In this section, we provide a geometric construction of these varieties using Pl"ucker coordinates, working over an arbitrary field. We then study their arithmetic properties.

3.1
Geometric construction

First, we give the geometric construction of Chow form and Cayley form. This part is inspired by
[
15
, §3]
and has been applied in
[
36
]
.

3.1.1

Let
k
be a field,
V
be a vector space of finite dimension over
k
, and
V^{\vee}
be the dual space of
V
. We denote by
\check{G}=\Gr(d+1,V^{\vee})
the Grassmannian which classifies all the quotient of dimension
d+1
of
V
. By the Plücker embedding

\Gr(d+1,V^{\vee})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right),

the coordinate algebra
B(\check{G})=\bigoplus\limits_{D\geqslant 0}B_{D}(\check{G})
of
\check{G}
is a homogeneous quotient algebra of
\bigoplus\limits_{D\geqslant 0}\sym^{D}\left(\bigwedge^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)
.

To elucidate the role of Plücker coordinates, we consider the following construction. Let

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\theta:&V^{\vee}\otimes\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V\right)&\longrightarrow&\bigwedge\nolimits^{d}V\\
&\xi\otimes(x_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{d})&\mapsto&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}\xi(x_{i})x_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i-1}\wedge x_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i},\end{array}

and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
be the subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(V)\times_{k}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)
which classifies all the points
(\xi,\alpha)
such that
\theta(\xi\otimes\alpha)=0
. Let

p^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}(V)\times_{k}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)

and

q^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}(V)\times_{k}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)

denote the canonical projections, and

v:\widetilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)\times_{k}\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)

be the canonical embedding. We define

p=p^{\prime}\circ v:\widetilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)\text{, and }q=q^{\prime}\circ v:\widetilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right).

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)
whose fundamental cycle is that in Proposition
3.1.2
the
Cayley variety
of
X
. We call the symmetric form (or polynomial)
\psi_{X}
determined above the
Cayley form
of
X
.

By definition, the Cayley form of
X
depends on the base projective space
\mathbb{P}(V)
and its embedding in
\mathbb{P}(V)
.

3.2
A description by resultant

Let
k
be a field, and
V
be a vector space of dimension
n+1
over
k
. When
X
is a complete intersection closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(V)
, its Cayley can also be described by a particular resultant. We will only introduce the necessary foundations of this notion, rather than giving a self-contained treatment. We refer the readers to
[
23
]
for a systematic introduction on this subject.

3.2.1

Let
T_{0},\ldots,T_{n}
be a basis of
H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\right)
, and
g_{i}\in H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(\delta_{i})\right)
be a family of non-zero sections, where
i=0,\ldots,n
. Then the
resultant
of
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
denoted by

\operatorname{Res}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}),

is a polynomial whose variables are coefficients of
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
, where
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
are considered as polynomials of variables in
T_{0},\ldots,T_{n}
. To ensure the uniqueness of the resultant, we require the normalization

\operatorname{Res}(T_{0}^{\delta_{0}},\ldots,T_{n}^{\delta_{n}})=1.

We refer the readers to
[
23
, Chap. 13]
for a detailed introduction to this notion.

By
[
23
, Chap. 13, Proposition 1.1]
,
\operatorname{Res}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})
is a homogeneous polynomial in the coefficients of each
g_{i}
of degree
\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n}\delta_{j}\right)/\delta_{i}
, where
i=0,\ldots,n
.

3.2.2

Let
X
be a complete intersection closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(V)
, which is generated by
f_{i}\in H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(\delta_{i})\right)
with
i=1,\ldots,m
. In this case,
X
is of dimension
n-m
, and of degree
\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{m}
.

Let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-m+1}\in H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\right)
. By §
3.2.1
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-m+1}
have common zeros in
\mathbb{P}(V)
if and only if

\operatorname{Res}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-m+1})=0.

In other words, if we suppose that
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-m+1}
define a complete intersection linear subvariety in
\mathbb{P}(V)
of dimension
m-1
, then the above equality is valid if and only if this linear subvariety intersects
X
non-empty.

This fact gives an explicit method to define the Cayley form of
X
. Let
s_{0},\ldots,s_{n}
be the dual basis of
T_{0},\ldots,T_{n}
, and then
(s_{i_{0}}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{i_{m}})_{0\leqslant i_{0}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}
is the Plücker coordinate of the linear varieties of dimension
m-1
in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. By §
3.2.1
,
\operatorname{Res}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-m+1})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{m}
of the variables
(s_{i_{0}}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{i_{m}})_{0\leqslant i_{0}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}
, which is an example of Proposition
3.1.2
.

3.2.3

We consider a special case. Let
X
be a hypersurface of degree
\delta
in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. By
[
30
]
,
X
is defined by an element
f\in\sym^{\delta}\left(V\right)
. By choosing the basis
T_{0},\ldots,T_{n}
of
H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\right)
, we denote that
X
is defined by the homogeneous polynomial
f(T_{0},\ldots,T_{n})
of degree
\delta
.

Let
s_{0},\ldots,s_{n}
be the dual basis of
T_{0},\ldots,T_{n}
. By the argument in §
3.2.2
, the Cayley form
\psi_{X}\in\sym^{\delta}\left(\bigwedge^{n}V^{\vee}\right)
by Proposition
3.1.2
is determined by the homogeneous equation

f(s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n},-s_{0}\wedge s_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n},\ldots,(-1)^{n}s_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n-1}),

where each variable is lack of
s_{0},\ldots,s_{n}
respectively. In a sense, it coincides with the original homogeneous polynomial
f(T_{0},\ldots,T_{n})
.

3.3
Change of coordinates

In this part, we consider the change of the Cayley form under a particular endomorphism of projective spaces.

3.3.1

Let

be a vector space of dimension

over a field

3.3.2

We fix an arbitrary basis of

In what follows, for an extension

over

Let

&[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n-1}:x_{n}]&\mapsto&[Hx_{0}:\cdots:Hx_{n-1}:x_{n}].\end{array}

In this case, we have

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}F_{H}^{-1}:&\mathbb{P}(V)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{P}(V)\\
&[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n-1}:x_{n}]&\mapsto&[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n-1}:Hx_{n}]\end{array}

by definition immediately.

Let
X
be a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(V)
of pure dimension
d
, and
\phi:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)

and

under the embedding

3.3.3

Let
\psi_{X}\in\sym^{\delta}\left(\bigwedge^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)
, which defines the Cayley variety of
X
in
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{d+1}V^{\vee}\right)
with respect to the embedding
\phi:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V)
, where
X
is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(V)
of pure dimension
d
and degree
\delta
. In order to determine the Cayley form of

with respect to the embedding
F_{H}\circ\phi:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
with

, we consider its pull back induced by

.

Let

be a basis of

, and
s_{0},\ldots,s_{n}
be its dual basis. Then
(s_{i_{0}}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{i_{d}})_{0\leqslant i_{0}<\cdots<i_{d}\leqslant n}

We write the Cayley form of

with respect to

as the form

\psi_{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{\delta}\psi_{X,i},

where
\psi_{X,i}
denotes the sum of monomials in
\psi_{X}
whose total degree of the variables with the form

3.3.4

Let
X

\mathbb{P}(V)
of pure dimension

. We denote by

Let

where
\mathcal{I}_{1}
denotes the index set of all

satisfying

and
1\leqslant j_{1}<\cdots<j_{d}\leqslant n
, and
\mathcal{I}_{2}

Let

(

4

In

[

, Theorem 5.14]

In

Compared with

We begin by setting up the foundational framework for this subject; some ideas are inspired by

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{d+1}\log(N(n,d)+1)-\frac{1}{2(d+1)}\mathcal{H}_{N(n,d)},

then we have the assertion from Proposition
2.7.1
and the comparison of heights in Proposition
3.4
directly.

If

\displaystyle\frac{\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{h(\psi_{X})}{(d+1)\delta}+B_{1}(d)-\frac{7}{2}\log(n+1)+\frac{N(n,d)+1}{d+1}\log 2

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{d+1}\log(N(n,d)+1)-\frac{1}{2(d+1)}\mathcal{H}_{N(n,d)},

then

\displaystyle h(\psi_{X,\delta})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle h(\psi_{X})\leqslant\delta\Big(\frac{(d+1)\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-(d+1)B_{1}(d)-\frac{7(d+1)}{2}\log(n+1)

\displaystyle+(N(n,d)+1)\log 2+4\log(N(n,d)+1)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}_{N(n,d)}\Big).

Same as the proof of Lemma
4.2.2
, we have

\delta^{2}b^{\prime}(\psi_{X})\leqslant C_{2}(n,d,K)\left(h(\psi_{X})+\delta^{2}\right)

from Proposition
2.6.5
. Then from the upper bound of the degree of auxiliary hypersurface in Theorem
4.2.3
, we obtain the required result by taking the above upper bound of
h(\psi_{X,\delta})
into consideration.

In addition, if we suppose
\psi_{X,\delta}
is absolutely irreducible, then the set of non-absolutely irreducible reductions of
\psi_{X}
is a subset of those of
\psi_{X,\delta}
, so we have
b^{\prime}(\psi_{X})\leqslant b^{\prime}(\psi_{X,\delta})
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{3}(n,d,K)\delta^{2-\frac{1}{d}}B^{\frac{1}{\delta^{1/d}}}\frac{b^{\prime}(X)}{H_{K}(\psi_{X,\delta})^{\frac{1}{d\delta^{1+1/d}}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{3}(n,d,K)\delta^{2-\frac{1}{d}}B^{\frac{1}{\delta^{1/d}}}\frac{b^{\prime}(\psi_{X,\delta})}{H_{K}(\psi_{X,\delta})^{\frac{1}{d\delta^{1+1/d}}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}(n,d,K)\delta^{3}B^{\frac{1}{\delta^{1/d}}}

by some elementary calculations, and we obtain the estimate.
∎

4.3.2

Next, we consider the case of dimension
1
and
2
, which can be applied directly later.

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X^{\prime}
be a geometrically integral curve in
\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
, whose projective closure

is a hypersurface of a plane in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})

Proof.

These two estimates are consequences of Corollary

for the case of

, Theorem 8.4]
), and the fact

.
∎

\the\smf@thm
.

Let

5
Some preliminaries

In general, it is difficult to study the uniform upper bound of
S(X;B)
for arbitrary projective varieties
X
. However, using specific methods, one can often reduce the general case to special ones, and these special cases constitute an important subject of study. For some special cases, the related geometric and arithmetic properties will be involved in the study.

This section provides preliminary results needed for our further study of uniform upper bounds for rational points of bounded height.

5.1
Reduction to the case of affine surfaces with NCC condition

In this part, we reduce our subject to a case of lower dimension with certain conditions.

5.1.1

For an arbitrary projective variety
X
of dimension
d
and degree
\delta
embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, one can construct a birational projection
\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{d+1}
such that the restriction to
X
is birational onto its image. Simultaneously, the change in the height of rational points on
X
can be controlled uniformly in terms of
n
,
d
, and
\delta
. If we do not care the dependence of the uniform upper bound of
\#S(X;B)
on
\delta
, we can study the case of projective hypersurface only.

This idea originates in
[
10
]
; see
[
47
,
50
,
13
,
43
,
17
]
for further developments and applications.

5.1.2

Let
f(T_{0},\ldots,T_{n})
be an absolutely irreducible homogeneous polynomial of degree
\delta
over a number field
K
, and it defines a hypersurface
X^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\prime})
, and a hypersurface
X
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
, where the Hermitian vector bundle
\overline{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n+2},(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{v})_{v\in M_{K,\infty}})
on
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
is equipped with
\ell^{2}
-norms for all
v\in M_{K,\infty}
. In this case, we have

\#S(X;B)\leqslant\#s(X^{\prime};B)

for a
B\in\mathbb{R}
, where
s(X^{\prime};B)
is from §
4.1.2
.

By the above argument, we only need to consider the case of geometrically integral hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{A}_{0}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
whose projective closure in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
is of degree
\delta
.

5.1.3

In fact, not all affine hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{K}
are relevant to this study. In
[
17
]
, the authors clarify which kinds of hypersurfaces need to be taken into consideration meaningfully. For a self-contained treatment, we summarize parts of
[
17
]
, particularly the arguments in
[
17
, §4]
concerning global fields.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(NCC condition)
.

Let
f\in K[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]
be a polynomial over a number field
K
, where
n\geqslant 3
, and
X^{\prime}
be the affine hypersurface in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
defined by
f
. The polynomial
f
is called cylindrical over a curve if there exist a
K
-linear morphism

\ell:\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{2}_{K}

and a curve
C
in
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{K}
such that
X^{\prime}=\ell^{*}(C)
. We abbreviate not cylindrical over a curve by NCC and say that
X^{\prime}
is NCC if
f
is.

Note that
f\in K[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]
satisfies the NCC condition over
K
if there do not exist
K
-linear forms
\ell_{1}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}),\ell_{2}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
and a polynomial
g\in K[T_{1},T_{2}]
such that

f(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})=g\left(\ell_{1}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}),\ell_{2}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\right).

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(
r
-irreducible)
.

Let
K
be a number field, and
f\in K[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]
. The polynomial
f
is said to be
r
-irreducible over
K
(or
r
-irreducible if there is no ambiguity on the base field), if
f
does not have any factors of degree smaller than or equal to
r
over
K
.

We refer
[
17
, Corollary 4.20]
below, which allows us to cut the higher dimensional affine hypersurfaces with hyperplanes while preserving NCC and
2
-irreducibility via the effective Hilbert irreducibility results in
[
17
, §4.2]
. For a polynomial
f\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]
, we denote by
V(f)
the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
defined by
f
.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]
be a polynomial of degree
\delta\geqslant 2
, where
n\geqslant 4
. Assume that
f
is NCC, and its highest homogeneous part
f_{\delta}
is
r
-irreducible for some integer
r\geqslant 1
. Then exist the linearly independent linear forms
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
of height at most
O_{n}(\delta^{3})
and
t\in[O_{n}(\delta^{3})]_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, such that
f_{\delta}|_{V(\ell-t\ell^{\prime})}
is
r
-irreducible, and such that there are at most
O_{n}(\delta)
values
b\in K
for which
f_{\delta}|_{V(\ell-t\ell^{\prime}-b)}
is not NCC.

5.1.4

We summarize the result which we will prove below. Let
K
be a number field, and
f(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
be an absolutely polynomial of degree
\delta
. We suppose that the homogeneous degree
\delta
part
f_{\delta}
of
f
is
2
-irreducible in Defintion
5.1.3
, and
f
satisfies the NCC condition in Definition
5.1.3
. Let
X^{\prime}
be the hypersurface defined by
f
in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{K}
. By Proposition
5.1.3
, for fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, if we prove

\#s(X^{\prime};B)\ll_{K,\delta,\epsilon}B^{\theta+\epsilon}

for all
\epsilon>0
, then for any hypersurface
X^{\prime\prime}
of degree
\delta
in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
, which satisfies the NCC condition and defined by a
2
-irreducible equation, we have

\#s(X^{\prime\prime};B)\ll_{K,\delta,\epsilon}B^{\theta+n-3+\epsilon}.

In particular, if we can prove a uniform bound for the surfaces in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{K}
without the
\epsilon
factor, then so is for the general hypersurface.

When we consider the case of
\delta=3
, the
1
-irreducibility can deduce the
2
-irreducibility directly. So we only need to suppose
f_{\delta}
is absolutely irreducible in this case.

5.2
Remind of classification of cubic surfaces

In this part, we list some useful geometric properties of cubic surfaces in
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{3}
over an arbitrary field
k
. We refer the readers to
[
19
, Chapter 9]
for a systematic introduction to this subject.

5.2.1

Let
X
be a cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
. If
X
is not a ruled surface, then
X
is normal, and it cannot be the cone of a curve. In addition,
X
contains at most
27
lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
. We refer the readers to
[
19
, §9.3]
for the proof of this property, and more precise classification of normal cubic surfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
.

We denote by
\Hilb^{2t+1}(X)
the Hilbert scheme of conics in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
lying in
X
, since it is well known that the Hilbert polynomial of such conic is
2t+1\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
. Each conic is obtained from an intersection of
X
and a plane passing a line in
X
. In this case, we have
\dim(\Hilb^{2t+1}(X))=1
and
\Hilb^{2t+1}(X)
contains at most
27
irreducible components.

5.2.2

Let
X
be a ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
. In this case, apart from the cones and the cylinders with directrix a plane cubic, the other ruled cubics, so-called skew ruled cubics, can all be defined in the following way: Given a conic
C
located in a plane
P
, a line
L_{1}
, cutting
P
at
O
, a homography between the points
A
of
C
and
B
of
L_{1}
such that
O
is not a double point, the ruled cubic is the union of the lines
AB
.

By
[
19
, §9.2]
, if
X
is not a cone of a plane curve, it must not be normal. In addition, its singular locus is a line in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}=\proj\left(k[T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}]\right)
, which is of dimension
1
.

If we suppose the singular locus is
T_{0}=T_{1}=0
of multiplicity
2
, then
X
is projectively equivalent to either

T_{0}^{2}T_{2}+T_{1}^{2}T_{3}=0

or

T_{2}T_{0}T_{1}+T_{3}T_{0}^{2}+T_{1}^{3}=0.

5.2.3

By Proposition
5.1.3
, if we want to study the cubic surfaces in
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{K}
satisfying the NCC condition, we do not need to consider the case of cone. Instead, we only need to consider the case that the projective closure is either a non-ruled surface or a skew ruled surface, where the equation of definition is
2
-irreducible.

5.3
Geometry of conics in a cubic surface

In this part, we introduce some geometric properties of the Hilbert scheme of conics in a cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
over a field. Since it is important to control the contribution of rational points in conics, some related geometry properties need to be taken into consideration. We will always work over an arbitrary field

of characteristic

in this part.

5.3.1

Let

be a

\mathbb{P}(V)

Let

[

\mathbb{P}(V)

contains at most

\mathbb{P}(V)
. In this case, we denote by

\mathbb{P}(V)
lying in

. By
[

, Lemma 4.3]
, we have
\dim\left(\Hilb^{2t+1}(X)\right)=1

.

(28)

Deligne pairing of arithmetic intersections

5.4.1

Let
K
be a number field, and
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
be a Hermitian vector bundle of rank

over

. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{E}}(1)
the universal bundle

.

We keep the constructions in §

over a number field
K
or over its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\mathscr{E}
be the universal family on the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb^{2t+1}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K}))
over

6
Rational and integral points in conics

In
[
48
]
, the author considered the height of points in the Hilbert scheme of conics in a surface of arbitrary degree, and counted rational points of bounded height in the complement of lines in the surfaces. Some of these techniques are improved in
[
49
,
50
]
to refine the previous work. In this section, we use the techniques of Cayley forms and Arakelov to improve some of the previous work, in particular, for the case of non-ruled cubic surface.

6.1
Cayley forms of a complete intersection

In this part, we describe the structure of the Cayley form of conics in a non-ruled cubic surface.

6.1.1

Let
k
be a field,
V
be a
k
-vector space of dimension
4
, and
X
be a non-ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}(V)
, which is geometrically integral naturally. In this case,
X
contains at most
27
lines over
\overline{k}
. We consider the case where
X
contains at least one
k
-rational line; otherwise, by the Bézout theorem in intersection theory,
X
contains no conic over
k
.

In this case, let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in V^{\vee}
be two linear form generating this line, which are
k
-linearly independent. That is, for every non-zero tuple
(t_{1},t_{2})\in k^{2}
, the hyperplane defined by
t_{1}\ell_{1}+t_{2}\ell_{2}
contains this line. We denote by
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
this hyperplane.

We consider the intersection of
X
and
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. As
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
vary, the intersection cycle of
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
with
X
consists of the line defined by
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
, together with either a conic or two lines in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. By the above construction, the line defined by
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
is independent of the choice of
t_{1},t_{2}\in k
in
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
.

6.1.2

We consider the Cayley form of the intersection cycle
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X
in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. By Proposition
3.1.2
, this Cayley form consists of a hyperplane and a conic hypersurface (may degenerate to be two hyperplanes if the conic is degenerated) in
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{2}V^{\vee}\right)
. Let
T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
be a basis of
V
, and
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
be its dual basis of
V^{\vee}
, and then the Cayley discussed above is determined by a homogeneous polynomial of variables
(s_{i}\wedge s_{j})_{0\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}
. In addition, the above line and conic are both of geometric multiplicity
1
.

We denote by
\psi_{H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X}
the Cayley form of the intersection cycle
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X
from the above construction. Then we write
\psi_{H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X}
as the form

\psi_{H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X}=b(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot\psi_{X,\text{line}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}},

(30)

where
\psi_{X,\text{line}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
is the Cayley form of the line,
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
is the Cayley form of the conic of the conic, and
b(t_{1},t_{2})
is homogeneous polynomial of the variables
t_{1},t_{2}
which may be constant. Both
\psi_{X,\text{line}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
and
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
are absolutely irreducible over the fractional field
k(t_{1},t_{2})
, and we require them have no factor in
k[t_{1},t_{2}]
. More precisely,
\psi_{X,\text{line}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
is a linear homogeneous polynomial of variables
(s_{i}\wedge s_{j})_{0\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}
, and
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2
with the same variables.

From the definition of Cayley form by the resultant,
\psi_{H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X}
is a homogeneous polynomial of the variables
(s_{i}\wedge s_{j})_{0\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}
of degree
3
, all of whose coefficients are homogeneous polynomials of the variables
t_{1},t_{2}
of degree
3
.

By (
30
),
\psi_{X,\text{line}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
is independent of the choice of
t_{1},t_{2}
, since the line in the intersection cycle
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdot X
is invariant for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in k
.

6.1.3

Suppose that
X
is a non-ruled cubic surface embedded in
\mathbb{P}(V)
which satisfies the
r
-irreducible condition in Definition
5.1.3
. Then we have the following property.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We keep all the above notations and constructions. If
X
is a cubic surface embedded in
\mathbb{P}(V)
defined by the homogeneous polynomial
f(T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3})
, whose homogeneous degree
3
part of the variables
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
is absolutely irreducible, then any lines in
X
cannot lie in the plane
T_{0}=0
.

Proof.

If there is a line lies in the plane
T_{0}=0
, then the intersection of
X
with the plane defined by
T_{0}=0
is this line and a possibly degenerated conic curve. This fact deduces that
f(0,T_{1},T_{2},T_{3})
is reducible, which is exactly the homogeneous degree
3
part of the variables
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
. This contradicts to that the part is absolutely irreducible.
∎

6.2
Heights of Cayley forms

In §
6.1.2
, we give a description of the Cayley form of a conic in a non-ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. In this part, we will give a more precise description with the help of the estimate of its heights in (
29
). Since we will apply some arithmetic properties, will work over a number field.

6.2.1

Let
K
be a number field, and
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
be the Hermitian vector bundle defined in §
2.3.3
of rank
4
over
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
X
be a non-ruled cubic surface embedded in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
. In this case, we consider the dependence of
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
on
t_{1},t_{2}
defined at (
30
) under such an arithmetic setting, which is inspired by
[
56
]
.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We keep all the notations and constructions above. The coefficients of
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
are all homogeneous polynomials of
t_{1},t_{2}
of degree
2
, which have no common factor.

Proof.

By §
6.1.2
, the polynomial
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
is homogeneous of degree
2
, whose coefficients are homogeneous polynomials of the variables
t_{1},t_{2}
of degree at most
3
.

We denote by
s
the corresponding point in the Hilbert scheme of the conic obtained by the intersection of
X
and
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
. By (
29
) and Proposition
3.4
, we have

h(\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}})-2h(s)=O_{X,K}(1),

where the uniform constant depends on
X
and the base field
K
. Let
h([t_{1}:t_{2}])
be the logarithmic height defined at (
2
), where we consider
[t_{1}:t_{2}]
as a rational point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
. By Weil height machine, we have

h(s)-h([t_{1}:t_{2}])=O_{H_{t_{1},t_{2}},K}(1),

where the uniform constant depends on the choice of the linear forms
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
generating
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
, and the base field
K
.

By the above two estimates of heights, the coefficients of
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
are homogeneous of degree
2
of the variables
t_{1},t_{2}
. If they have a common factor, it contradicts to the above comparison of heights. This terminates the proof.
∎

By Proposition
6.2.1
, we may write
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
as the form

\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},i_{2}),0\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}\leqslant 3\\
J=(j_{1},j_{2}),0\leqslant j_{1}<j_{2}\leqslant 3\end{subarray}}b_{IJ}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{i_{1}}\wedge s_{i_{2}})(s_{j_{1}}\wedge s_{j_{2}}),

(31)

where
b_{IJ}(t_{1},t_{2})
is a homogeneous polynomial with variables
t_{1},t_{2}
of degree
2
,
I=(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2},0\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}\leqslant 3
, and
J=(j_{1},j_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2},0\leqslant j_{1}<j_{2}\leqslant 3
.

6.3
Counting rational points

By (
31
), we can control the height of conics in a cubic surface. This illustration is useful to control the rational points of bounded height lying in conics.

6.3.1

We have the following property on these

.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

With all the above notations and conditions. All these
b_{IJ}(t_{1},t_{2})
cannot be zero simultaneously for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in K
not all zero, or equivalently, all

Proof.

If there exists such

are zero means that the locus obtained by the intersection of
H_{t_{1},t_{2}}
and

have common points with all lines in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})

∎

6.3.2

We denote by
x_{1}=t_{1}^{2}

x_{2}=t_{1}t_{2}
,
x_{3}=t_{2}^{2}

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\iota_{1}:&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}\\
&[t_{1}:t_{2}]&\mapsto&[t_{1}^{2}:t_{1}t_{2}:t_{2}^{2}]\end{array}

(32)

be a morphism, and

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\iota_{2}:&\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}&\dashrightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{20}_{K}\\

We have the following properties about

\lemmname

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We keep all the above notations and constructions. If the matrix determined in Lemma

By an elementary calculation, the image

6.3.3

By Proposition

\left\{[t_{1}:t_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}(K)\mid H_{K}\left(\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\right)\leqslant B\right\},

where the naive height

\propname

\the\smf@thm

For a fixed irreducible component of the Hilbert scheme of conics in a non-ruled cubic surface in

\#\left\{[t_{1}:t_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}(K)\mid H_{K}\left(\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\right)\leqslant B\right\}\leqslant 16C_{1}^{\prime}(20,1,K)B\ll_{K}B,

where the constant

Proof.

When the rank of the matrix
(b_{IJ})
is

\left\{[t_{1}:t_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}(K)\mid H_{K}\left(\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\right)\leqslant B\right\}\subseteq S(\im(\iota);B),

\mathbb{P}^{20}_{K}

\im(\iota)

When the rank of the matrix

By the uniform upper bound of rational points for lines from Corollary

, we have

\displaystyle\#\left\{[X:Y:Z]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}(K)\mid H_{K}([b_{1}(X,Y,Z):b_{2}(X,Y,Z)])\leqslant B\right\}

(35)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{1}(2,1,K)B^{2}.

In fact, the morphism

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\varphi:&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}\\
&[s:t]&\mapsto&[b_{1}(s,t):b_{2}(s,t)]\end{array}

is polarized, and we have
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\cong\mathcal{O}(2)
. When the coefficients of
b_{1}(s,t)
and
b_{2}(s,t)
vary, we can consider the metrics of
\mathcal{O}(2)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
on the right-hand side vary. By this view point, the upper (
35
) is for all possible metrics on
\mathcal{O}(2)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
. Then the square root of the uniform bound (
35
) gives a uniform bound with respect to
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then we obtain the assertion by adjusting the constants with the case of rank
3
.
∎

By the above result, we can give an upper bound of conics with bounded height in a non-ruled cubic surface.

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a non-ruled cubic surface embedded in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
. For a conic
C
in

be the Cayley form of

in Definition

. Then we have

\#\{C\subseteq X\mid H_{K}(\psi_{C})\leqslant B\}\leqslant 432C_{1}^{\prime}(20,1,K)B\ll_{K}B,

where the height
H_{K}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
is the classic height defined in (
2
), and the constant

2.7.3
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition
6.3.3
via the fact that

27

∎

6.3.4

Here we give a uniform upper bound of rational points of bounded height in a non-ruled cubic surface provided by conics.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
be the Hermitian vector bundle of rank
4
defined in §
2.3.3
,
X
be a non-ruled cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
. Then for a
B\in\mathbb{R}
, there are at most

\displaystyle 93312\exp\left(\frac{23040\log 2\log 12}{137}\right)C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(3,1,K)C_{1}^{\prime}(20,1,K)C_{1}^{\prime}(3,2,K)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\cdot B^{\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}+1}\max\left\{\frac{\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},2\right\}

\displaystyle\ll_{K}

\displaystyle B^{\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}+1}\max\left\{\log B,2\right\},

points in
S(X;B)
provided by conics in
X
, where the constants
C^{\prime}_{1}(n,d,K)
and
C^{\prime\prime}_{1}(n,d,K)
are introduced in Corollary
2.7.3
and Corollary
2.7.3
respectively.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.7.3
, the set
S(X;B)
can be covered by a hypersurface of degree smaller that

which does not contain the generic point of

. By the Bézout’s theorem in the intersection theory (cf.
[
21
, Proposition 8.4]
), there are at most
\frac{81}{2}C^{\prime}_{1}(3,2,K)B^{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}
conics in the intersection cycle of
X
and the hypersurface determined above.

From the uniform upper bound of rational points of bounded height in Corollary
2.7.3
and the comparison of heights in Proposition
3.4
, for a conic

in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{K})
, we have

\#S(C;B)\leqslant 8\exp\left(\frac{23040\log 2\log 12}{137}\right)C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(3,1,K)\frac{B}{H_{K}(\psi_{C})^{1/4}}\max\left\{\frac{\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},2\right\}.

Then by Corollary
6.3.3
, the rational points in
X
of height smaller than
B
provided by conics is at most

\displaystyle 8\exp\left(\frac{23040\log 2\log 12}{137}\right)C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(3,1,K)B\max\left\{\frac{\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},2\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{1}^{\frac{81}{2}C^{\prime}_{1}(3,2,K)B^{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}}\frac{432C_{1}^{\prime}(20,1,K)}{k^{1/4}}\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 93312\exp\left(\frac{23040\log 2\log 12}{137}\right)C_{1}^{\prime\prime}(3,1,K)C_{1}^{\prime}(20,1,K)C_{1}^{\prime}(3,2,K)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\cdot B^{\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}+1}\max\left\{\frac{\log B}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},2\right\},

which obtains the desired bound.
∎

6.4
Counting integral points

For a non-ruled cubic surface, we consider the conics lying in it, and the height of the higher degree part of whose Cayley form is bounded. In this part, we will give a uniform upper bound of the number of such conics.

6.4.1

We keep all the above notations and constructions. By Proposition
6.2.1
, we write
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
as the form

\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}=\psi_{X,2}^{t_{1},t_{2}}+\psi_{X,1}^{t_{1},t_{2}}+\psi_{X,0}^{t_{1},t_{2}},

where

is the homogeneous degree
i

(s_{0}\wedge s_{i})_{1\leqslant\leqslant 3}
of
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
with

. In addition, we denote

\displaystyle\psi_{X,2}^{t_{1},t_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{1})^{2}+a_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{2})^{2}+a_{3}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{3})^{2}

(36)

\displaystyle a_{4}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{1})(s_{0}\wedge s_{2})+a_{5}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{1})(s_{0}\wedge s_{3})

\displaystyle+a_{6}(t_{1},t_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{2})(s_{0}\wedge s_{3}),

In addition, we suppose

3
part of the variables
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
is absolutely irreducible. In this case, we have the following property of
\psi_{X,2}^{t_{1},t_{2}}

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

With all the notations and conditions above. The polynomials
a_{1}(t_{1},t_{2}),\ldots,a_{6}(t_{1},t_{2})
cannot be zero simultaneously for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in K
not all zero. In other words,
\psi_{X,2}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\neq 0
for all
(t_{1},t_{2})\in K^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
.

Proof.

If there exists a pair
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in K^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
such that
a_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\cdots=a_{6}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=0
, then for all the non-zero terms in
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
, they must contain at least one in
s_{1}\wedge s_{2}
,
s_{1}\wedge s_{3}
,
s_{2}\wedge s_{3}
.

We consider the Plücker coordinate of all the lines in the plane defined by
T_{0}=0
in
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathcal{E}_{K}^{\vee}\right)
, which are complete intersections of
T_{0}=0
with a plane defined by a linear form
c_{1}T_{1}+c_{2}T_{2}+c_{3}T_{3}=0
, where
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in K
not all zero. Then these kinds of lines satisfy
s_{1}\wedge s_{2}=s_{1}\wedge s_{3}=s_{2}\wedge s_{3}=0
, and at least one of
s_{0}\wedge s_{1}
,
s_{0}\wedge s_{2}
,
s_{0}\wedge s_{3}
is not zero.

If
\psi_{X,\text{conic}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
satisfies the above hypothesis, then for all kinds of lines must intersect with this conic non-empty. Then this conic must lie in the plane
T_{0}=0
, and so does the line obtained by the intersection of
H_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
and
X
, which contradicts to Lemma
6.1.3
.
∎

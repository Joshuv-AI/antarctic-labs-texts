2.
Contact de Rham cohomology of the Reeb foliations

When we consider smooth structures/objects (like functions, differential forms, vector fields, etc.) on a given smooth compact manifold
X

with boundary
, this means that
X
is embedded into a larger open equidimensional manifold
\hat{X}
so that a structure in question is a restriction of a similar structure/object on
\hat{X}
to
X
. As we change the structure/object on
X
, we may shrink the ambient
\hat{X}
. Thus one should think of the ambient
\hat{X}\supset X
as a germ.

Let
X
be a compact smooth
m
-dimensional manifold. Consider the de Rham differential complex of differential forms

(2.1)

\displaystyle\quad\Omega^{\ast}_{d\mathcal{R}}(X)=_{\mathsf{def}}\quad\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\Big\{0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{0}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{1}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{m}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}0\Big\},

where
\Omega^{0}(X)\approx C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
is the algebra of smooth functions on
X
.

For a smooth non-vanishing vector field
v
on a
m
-dimensional manifold
X
, consider the vector space
\Omega^{k}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)
of smooth
v
-
invariant
exterior differential
k
-forms
\alpha
that are
v
-horizontal
, that is,
v\rfloor\alpha=0
. The differential
d\alpha
is evidently
v
-invariant when
\alpha
is. Hence, for a
v
-invariant horizontal
\alpha
, we get

0=\mathcal{L}_{v}\alpha=v\,\rfloor\,d\alpha+d(v\,\rfloor\,\alpha)=v\,\rfloor\,d\alpha,

which implies that
d\alpha
is horizontal as well. When
k=0
, then
\Omega^{0}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)\approx C^{\infty}(X,v)
, the space of
v
-invariant smooth functions on
X
.

Alternatively, we could define
\Omega^{k}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)
as the vector space of
k
-forms
\alpha
that are
v
-horizontal and whose differential
d\alpha
is
v
-horizontal. Some authors call such differential forms “
basic
”.

Therefore, for
m=\dim(X)
and any vector field
v\neq 0
on
X
, we get the
horizontal and
v
-invariant
(alternatively,
basic
) de Rham differential complex

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v)=_{\mathsf{def}}\quad\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\Big\{0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{0}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{2}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega^{m-1}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}0\Big\}

of
\Omega^{0}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)
-modules. Here the algebra
\Omega^{0}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)
consists of smooth functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that the directional derivative
\mathcal{L}_{v}f=0
.
Note that
\Omega^{m}_{\mathsf{b}}(X,v)=0
since no non-zero
m
-form on an open set can be
v
-horizontal.

Evidently, the de Rham differential complex of basic forms
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v)
maps canonically to the de Rham differential complex
\Omega^{\ast}_{d\mathcal{R}}(X)
of all forms.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
H^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v)
the cohomology groups of the
v
-basic de Rham differential complex (
2.2
).
\diamondsuit

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Psi:X\to X
be a smooth diffeomorphism and
v_{1},v_{2}
smooth non-vanishing vector fields on
X
such that
(\Psi)_{\ast}(v_{1})=\lambda\cdot v_{0}
for a smooth positive function
\lambda:X\to\mathbb{R}
.

Then the basic de Rham differential complexes
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{0})
and
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{1})
are isomorphic with the help of
\Psi^{\ast}
. Hence, the de Rham basic cohomology are isomorphic:

\Psi^{\ast}:H^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{0})\approx H^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{1}).

Proof.

For a given
X
, the notion
\{v\rfloor\alpha=0,\,v\rfloor d\alpha=0\}
of a basic form
\alpha
depends evidently only on the conformal class of the vector field
v
. Therefore, using that
d\Psi^{\ast}(\alpha)=\Psi^{\ast}(d\alpha)
and that
(\Psi)_{\ast}(v_{1})=\lambda v_{0}
, we conclude that
\Psi^{\ast}
is an isomorphism that maps basic forms to basic forms and commutes with their differentials. Thus, the basic differential complex
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{0})
is isomorphic to the basic differential complex
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{1})
with the help of
\Psi^{\ast}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\beta
be a contact form on a smooth manifold
X
and
v_{\beta}
its Reeb vector field. Consider a
1
-form
\beta_{1}=\beta+df
, where the smooth function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
df(v_{\beta})>-1
(i.e.,
f
is not “negatively very steep” along the Reeb trajectories).

\bullet
With
\beta
being fixed, the inequality
df(v_{\beta})>-1
defines a nonempty open convex set
\mathcal{C}(\beta)
in the functional space
C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

\bullet
The form
\beta_{1}
is a contact form on
X
.

Proof.

Since
\beta_{1}=\beta+df
,

\beta_{1}\wedge(d\beta_{1})^{n}=\beta_{1}\wedge(d\beta)^{n}=\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n}+df\wedge(d\beta)^{n}.

We claim that the
(2n+1)
-form
\beta_{1}\wedge(d\beta_{1})^{n}
is positive on
X
, provided
df(v_{\beta})>-1
. Indeed, the
(2n+1)
-form
df\wedge(d\beta)^{n}
is proportional to
(2n+1)
-form
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n}
with some functional coefficient
h:X\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus
\beta_{1}\wedge(d\beta_{1})^{n}>0
is equivalent to the inequality
1+h>0
on
X
.

To detect
h
, it suffices to compare the contractions of the two forms,
df\wedge(d\beta)^{n}=h(\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n})
and
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n}
, with a non-vanishing Reeb vector field
v_{\beta}
. Recall that
v_{\beta}\rfloor\beta=1
and
v_{\beta}\rfloor d\beta\equiv 0
.
Using these properties of
v_{\beta}
, we get

v_{\beta}\rfloor(\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n})=(d\beta)^{n},\text{ while }v_{\beta}\rfloor(df\wedge(d\beta)^{n})=df(v_{\beta})(d\beta)^{n}=h\;(d\beta)^{n}.

Thus, when
df(v_{\beta})>-1
,
v_{\beta}\rfloor(\beta_{1}\wedge(d\beta_{1})^{n})
is positively proportional to
(d\beta)^{n}
with the functional coefficient
h
. Therefore,
\beta_{1}
is a contact form, provided
df(v_{\beta})>-1
.

Put
v_{\beta_{1}}=_{\mathsf{def}}(1+df(v_{\beta}))^{-1}v_{\beta}
. Let us check that
v_{\beta_{1}}
is the Reeb vector field for the form
\beta_{1}
. Indeed,
\beta_{1}(v_{\beta_{1}})=\beta_{1}((1+df(v_{\beta}))^{-1}v_{\beta})=(\beta+df)\big((1+df(v_{\beta}))^{-1}v_{\beta}\big)=1
since
\beta(v_{\beta})=1
, and
d\beta_{1}(v_{\beta_{1}})=d\beta((1+df(v_{\beta}))^{-1}v_{\beta})=(1+df(v_{\beta}))^{-1}d\beta(v_{\beta})\equiv 0.

If
df(v_{\beta})>-1
and
dg(v_{\beta})>-1
for some
f,g\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then
d(tf+(1-t)g)>-1
for any
t\in[0,1]
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}(\beta)\subset C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
is an open convex set that contains all constant functions
c
(since
dc(v_{\beta})=0>-1
).
∎

Although the deformation
\beta\leadsto\beta_{1}=\beta+df
, where
df(v_{\beta})>-1
, preserves the conformal class of the Reeb field
v_{\beta}
, it may change the original contact distribution
\xi_{\beta}=\ker(\beta)
. The function
df(v_{\beta})
on
X
and its norms may be viewed as quantities that distinguish between the distributions
\xi_{\beta}
and
\xi_{\beta_{1}}
.

Let
\mathsf{Cont}(X)\subset\Omega^{1}(X)
denote the space of contact forms
\beta
. Since
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n}>0
on
X
is an open constraint,
\mathsf{Cont}(X)
is open in
\Omega^{1}(X)
. Its boundary consists of
1
-forms
\beta
such that
\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n}\geq 0
on
X
and
(\beta\wedge(d\beta)^{n})(x)=0
for some
x\in X
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\beta
be a contact form on a smooth manifold
X
and
v_{\beta}
its Reeb field. Consider a
1
-form
\beta_{1}=\beta+df
, where the smooth function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
df(v_{\beta})>-1
.

Then the de Rham basic differential complexes
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{1}})
and
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta})
are canonically isomorphic. As a result, the de Rham basic cohomology spaces are isomorphic as well:

H^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{1}})\approx H^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta}).

In other words, the canonical isomorphisms
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{1}})\approx\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta})
are valid for any form
\beta_{1}
residing in a non-empty convex set

\mathcal{O}(\beta)=\{\beta+df\big|\;f\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}),\;df(v_{\beta})>-1\}\subset\mathsf{Cont}(X).

Proof.

By their definitions, the de Rham basic differential complexes
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{1}})
and
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta})
depend only on the conformal classes of the Reeb vector fields
v_{\beta_{1}}
and
v_{\beta}
, respectively. By Lemma
2.2
, the two fields are positively proportional. Therefore, we conclude that the de Rham basic differential complexes

\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{1}})
and
\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta})
are canonically isomorphic, as long as
f\in\mathcal{C}(\beta)\subset C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
. ∎

Definition 2.2
.

Two contact forms
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
on
X
are called
shallow-incline-exact-chain-equivalent
if there exist a sequence of contact forms
\{\beta_{i}\}_{i\in[0,N]}
such that
\beta_{0}=\beta
,
\beta^{\prime}=\beta_{N}
, and each
\beta_{i+1}=\beta_{i}+df_{i}
, where
f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(\beta_{i})
, for all
i\in[0,N-1]
.

We denote by
\mathcal{E}(\beta)
the space of all contact forms that are shallow-incline-exact-chain-equivalent to
\beta
.

\diamondsuit

The next lemma shows that this definition of equivalence does not lead to new extensions
\mathcal{E}(\beta)
of the already familiar set
\mathcal{O}(\beta)=_{\mathsf{def}}\beta+d(\mathcal{C}(\beta))
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For a given contact form
\beta
, the contact forms
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}(\beta)
that are shallow-incline-exact-chain-equivalent to
\beta
belong to the open and convex set
\mathcal{O}(\beta)=_{\mathsf{def}}\beta+d(\mathcal{C}(\beta))
in the affine space
\mathcal{D}(\beta)
of all
1
-forms on
X
that differ from
\beta
by an exact
1
-form.

Proof.

Consider a shallow-incline-exact chain
\{\beta_{i+1}=\beta_{i}+df_{i}\}_{i\in[0,N-1]}
, where
f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(\beta_{i})
and
\beta_{0}=\beta
. Then a direct computation shows that

(2.3)

\displaystyle v_{\beta^{\prime}}=\big(1+\sum_{i}df_{i}(v_{\beta})\big)^{-1}v_{\beta},

which implies that
1+\sum_{i}df_{i}(v_{\beta})>0
since all intermediate proportionality coefficients
(1+df_{i}(v_{\beta_{i}}))^{-1}
next to
v_{\beta_{i}}
are positive. As an example, let us execute the first steps in this computation. Put
a_{i}=df_{i}(v_{\beta})
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
,
v_{\beta_{1}}=\frac{1}{1+a_{1}}v_{\beta}
. Similarly,

v_{\beta_{2}}=\frac{1}{1+df_{2}(v_{\beta_{1}})}\;v_{\beta_{1}}=\frac{1}{1+df_{2}(\frac{1}{1+a_{1}}v_{\beta})}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{1+a_{1}}\;v_{\beta}\Big)=\Big(\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{1+a_{1}}\,a_{2}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{1+a_{1}}\;v_{\beta}\Big)

=\frac{1}{1+a_{1}+a_{2}}\,v_{\beta}.

Letting
f_{\bullet}=\sum_{i}f_{i}
in (
2.3
), we conclude that
f_{\bullet}(v_{\beta})>-1
. So,
\beta_{N}\in\mathcal{O}(\beta)=\beta+d(\mathcal{C}(\beta))
. Thus,
\mathcal{E}(\beta)\subset\mathcal{O}(\beta)
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{E}(\beta)\supset\mathcal{O}(\beta)
by definition. As a result,
\mathcal{E}(\beta)=\mathcal{O}(\beta)
, an open and convex set in
\mathcal{D}(\beta)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If, for some
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Cont}(X)
,
\mathcal{O}(\beta)\cap\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{O}(\beta)=\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Pick
\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{O}(\beta)\cap\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\prime})
. Then
\beta_{1}=\beta+df
, where
df(v_{\beta})>-1
. Also,
\beta_{1}=\beta^{\prime}+df^{\prime}
, where
df^{\prime}(v_{\beta^{\prime}})>-1
. It follows that
\beta^{\prime}=\beta_{1}-df^{\prime}
, where
-df^{\prime}(v_{\beta_{1}})>-1
. Since
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are shallow-incline-exact-chain-equivalent, by Lemma
2.3
,
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(\beta)
. Similarly,
\beta\in\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\prime})
. Therefore,
\mathcal{O}(\beta)=\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma
2.4
, in combination with Lemma
2.2
, has the following direct implication: the space
\mathsf{Cont}(X)
“fibers” over another space so that the fibers are convex open sets
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
in the vector space
d(\Omega^{0}(X))
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Assume that
\mathsf{Cont}(X)\neq\emptyset
. Consider the continuous quotient map
Q:\Omega^{1}(X)\to\Omega^{1}(X)/d(\Omega^{0}(X))
of topological vector spaces and its restriction

to the open subset
\mathsf{Cont}(X)\subset\Omega^{1}(X)
.
For each element
\beta\in\mathsf{Cont}(X)
, let
\alpha=Q(\beta)
.

Then the fiber
(Q^{\dagger})^{-1}(\alpha)
coincides with
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
, an open and convex set in the affine space
(Q)^{-1}(\alpha)
.
\diamondsuit

Theorem 2.1
.

If two contact forms,
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
, are shallow-incline-exact-chain-equivalent (see Definition

), then the de Rham basic differential complexes

Proof.

If

\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{i+1}})\approx\Omega^{\ast}_{\mathsf{basic}\,d\mathcal{R}}(X,v_{\beta_{i}})

for all

. By an induction in

, the claim follows.

∎

By Lemma

, the orientation preserving group

of smooth diffeomorphisms on

. Moreover, the

to a

and Lemma
2.1

Lemma 3.1

Let

Then

Proposition 3.1

Let

\bullet

If

Assume now that

The non-degenerated

Therefore, we get a non-degenerated pairing

-forms.

The
basic Hodge star operator

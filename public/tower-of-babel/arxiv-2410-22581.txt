Symbolic Powers via Extension
Sankhaneel Bisui, Haoxi Hu

Abstract
. This article investigates under which conditions symbolic powers of the extension of an ideal are the same as the extension of the symbolic powers. As an application, we prove formulas for the resurgence of sum of two homogeneous ideals in finitely generated graded 𝕜 {\mathbbm{k}} -algebras which are domains, where 𝕜 {\mathbbm{k}} is algebraically closed. Initially, these were known for homogeneous ideals in polynomial rings.

1.
Introduction

For an ideal
I
in a Noethrian ring
R
, the
m
-th symbolic power is defined as follows:

I^{(m)}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}I^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R.

The symbolic powers are very well-studied objects for commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, especially for the multiplicity theory. We are interested in the behavior of symbolic powers under ring homomorphisms. If
\phi:A\to B
is a homomorphism of rings, then for an ideal
I
in
A
, we use
IB
to denote the
extension
of the ideal
I
in
B
, and for an ideal
J
in
B
, we use
J\cap A
to denote the
contraction
of the ideal
J
in
A
. In this article, we primarily focus on the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be Noetherian rings, and
I
be an ideal of
A
. Let
\phi:A\to B
be a ring homomorphism.
When is
(IB)^{(n)}=I^{(n)}B
, where
m\geqslant 1
?

If the map
\phi
is
faithfully flat
, then the Question
1.1
has been well studied, for examples check,
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
,
[
1
, Theorem 4]
, and
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
. Our result generalizes from
faithfully flat
map to
flat
map, which is the following:

Main Result 1

(
Theorem

2.5
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be Noetherian rings and
\phi:A\to B
be a flat morphism. Assume that
I\subset A
is a proper ideal such that
\Ass^{*}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass^{*}(I)\}
, and every associated prime of

is a contraction of some ideal in

. Then
(IB)^{(n)}=I^{(n)}B
, for all

.

We also give examples that satisfy assumptions in Theorem
2.5
but not under the

map, such as Example
2.8
and Example

. Moreover, In Example

One of the central topics of symbolic powers is the containment between the symbolic and ordinary powers of ideals. The containment problem asks to find
m
and

for which
I^{(m)}\subset I^{r}
. In
[
5
,
12
,
16
]
, it has been proved that if

is a proper ideal of big height

(the largest height of associated primes of

) in a regular ring containing a field, then

for all

. After this celebrated result, one can keep investigating exact numbers for containments. The resurgence, defined by Bocci and Harbourne
[
4
]
, and the asymptotic resurgence, defined by Guardo, Harbourne, and Van Tuyl
[
8
]
measure containment in more refined settings.

If
I
is a proper ideal in a Notherian ring
R
,
\rho(I)
and the

,
\rho_{a}(I)
are defined as follows:

\rho(I)=\sup\left\{\dfrac{m}{r}~|~I^{(m)}\not\subseteq I^{r},m\geqslant 1,r\geqslant 1\right\},

{\rho_{a}}(I)=\sup\left\{\frac{m}{r}~|~I^{(mt)}\not\subseteq{I^{rt}},m\geqslant 1,r\geqslant 1,\text{ and }t\gg 1\right\}.

Motivated by Question

2.
Symbolic Powers and Resurgence via flat extension

In this section we will show our main theorem (Theorem
2.5
). Before proceeding to the main theorem we provide some useful lemmas. The first lemma is very well-known and we state it without proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a
R
-module where
R
is a commutative ring, and
I
be an ideal of
R
, then
(R/I)\otimes_{R}M\cong M/IM
. Furthermore, if
M
is flat over
A
, we also have
I\otimes_{R}M\cong IM
.

The next result is also a standard one. Since we could not find a resource, we provide a complete proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

If a flat ring homomorphism
\phi:A\to B
extends a prime
\mathfrak{p}
to a prime
\mathfrak{p}B
, and
\mathfrak{p}
is a contraction of some ideals in
B
, then
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
as
A
-algebra.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
to
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
, we have
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\cong((A\otimes_{A}B)\backslash(\mathfrak{p}B\otimes_{A}B))^{-1}(A\otimes_{A}B)
. Our goal is to show
((A\otimes_{A}B)\backslash(\mathfrak{p}B\otimes_{A}B))^{-1}(A\otimes_{A}B)\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
.

Define

\psi_{1}:((A\otimes_{A}B)\backslash(\mathfrak{p}B\otimes_{A}B))^{-1}(A\otimes_{A}B)\rightarrow A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\text{ by }\psi_{1}\left(\frac{a\otimes b}{c\otimes d}\right)=\frac{a}{c}\otimes\frac{b}{d}.

Similarly define

\psi_{2}:A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\rightarrow((A\otimes_{A}B)\backslash(\mathfrak{p}B\otimes_{A}B))^{-1}(A\otimes_{A}B)\text{ by }\psi_{2}\left(\frac{a}{c}\otimes\frac{b}{d}\right)=\frac{a\otimes b}{c\otimes d}.

First we need to show both maps are well-defined.

Since
c\otimes d\notin\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A}B
, then
c\notin\mathfrak{p}
. We also can consider
c\otimes d=1\otimes cd\notin 1\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{p}B
, so
cd\notin\mathfrak{p}B
. We also know that
c\notin\mathfrak{p}
, this forces
d\notin\mathfrak{p}B
since
\mathfrak{p}B
is prime. Therefore
\psi_{1}
is well-defined.

We prove that
\psi_{2}
is also well-defined. Indeed,
c\notin\mathfrak{p}
and
d\notin\mathfrak{p}B
, then
\phi(c)d\notin\mathfrak{p}B
because
\phi(A)\cap\mathfrak{p}B=\phi(\mathfrak{p})
. To see this, let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}\cap A
for an ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
B
. From this we observe that
\mathfrak{p}B\cap A=\left(\left(\mathfrak{q}\cap A\right)B\right)\cap A=\mathfrak{q}\cap A={\mathfrak{p}}
. This implies that
\phi(A)\cap\mathfrak{p}B=\phi(\mathfrak{p})
. Note that
\phi(A)\cap\mathfrak{p}B\supset\phi(\mathfrak{p})
. Let
y\in\phi(A)\cap{\mathfrak{p}}B
. Then
y=\phi(x)
for some
x\in A
. We show that
x\in{\mathfrak{p}}
. Now since
y\in{\mathfrak{p}}B
then
x\in{\mathfrak{p}}B\cap A={\mathfrak{p}}
.

Notice that
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are inverses to each other. Thereofre we just need to show that
\psi_{1}
is an
A
-algebra homomorphism, then we are done. Verifying the map commutes with the multiplication is straightforward, we only verify the addition here.
Let
\frac{a_{1}\otimes b_{1}}{c_{1}\otimes d_{1}}
and
\frac{a_{2}\otimes b_{2}}{c_{2}\otimes d_{2}}
be elements in
((A\otimes_{A}B)\backslash(\mathfrak{p}B\otimes_{A}B))^{-1}(A\otimes_{A}B)
. Then

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\left(\frac{a_{1}\otimes b_{1}}{c_{1}\otimes d_{1}}\right)+\psi_{1}\left(\frac{a_{2}\otimes b_{2}}{c_{2}\otimes d_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}}{c_{1}}\otimes\frac{b_{1}}{d_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{c_{2}}\otimes\frac{b_{2}}{d_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}c_{2}}{c_{1}c_{2}}\otimes\frac{b_{1}d_{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\frac{c_{1}a_{2}}{c_{1}c_{2}}\otimes\frac{d_{1}b_{2}}{d_{1}d_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{c_{1}c_{2}}\otimes\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\right)\cdot(a_{1}c_{2}\otimes b_{1}d_{2}+c_{1}a_{2}\otimes d_{1}b_{2}).

Again,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\left(\frac{a_{1}\otimes b_{1}}{c_{1}\otimes d_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}\otimes b_{2}}{c_{2}\otimes d_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}\left(\frac{(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot(c_{2}\otimes d_{2})}{(c_{1}\otimes d_{1})\cdot(c_{2}\otimes d_{2})}+\frac{(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})\cdot(c_{1}\otimes d_{1})}{(c_{2}\otimes d_{2})\cdot(c_{1}\otimes d_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}\left(\frac{1}{c_{1}c_{2}\otimes d_{1}d_{2}}\cdot(a_{1}c_{2}\otimes b_{1}d_{2}+c_{1}a_{2}\otimes d_{1}b_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{c_{1}c_{2}}\otimes\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\right)\cdot\left(a_{1}c_{2}\otimes b_{1}d_{2}+c_{1}a_{2}\otimes d_{1}b_{2}\right).

∎

The following theorem determines the associated primes of an extension of an ideal.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
17
, Theorem 23.2]
Let
\phi:A\to B
be a homomorphism of Noetherian rings, and let
E
be an
A
-module and
G
a
B
-module. If
G
is flat over
A
, then we have the following

\Ass_{B}(E\otimes_{A}G)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{A}(E)}\Ass_{B}(G/\mathfrak{p}G).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\phi:A\to B
be a flat homomorphism. We set
\Ass_{A}^{*}(I)=\cup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Ass_{A}(I^{n})
. If
I\subset A
is a ideal then we have
\Ass_{B}(IB)=\cup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)}\Ass_{B}({\mathfrak{p}}B)
. Furthermore, if
\Ass_{B}^{*}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass_{A}^{*}(I)\}
then we have

(1)

\Ass_{B}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)\}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Ass_{B}(I^{(n)}B)}{\mathfrak{q}}\subset\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}B\text{ for all }n\geqslant 1.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a flat then
A/I\otimes_{A}B\cong B/IB
. Now by
Theorem

2.3
,

\Ass_{B}(IB)=\Ass_{B}(A/I\otimes_{A}B)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{A}(A/I)}\Ass_{B}(B/\mathfrak{p}B)=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}\Ass_{B}({\mathfrak{p}}B).

By the assumption
{\mathfrak{p}}B
is prime for each
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)
. Hence we get

\Ass_{B}(IB)=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}\Ass_{B}({\mathfrak{p}}B)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)\}.

Note that by
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
if
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I^{(n)})
then
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I^{n})
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\mathfrak{q}}
for some
{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Ass(I)
. Thus we get
\displaystyle\cup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I^{(n)})}{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\cup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
n\geqslant 1
. Since
\Ass^{*}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass^{*}(I)\}
then using
Theorem

2.3
we get

\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Ass(I^{(n)}B)}{\mathfrak{q}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Ass({\mathfrak{p}}B),\\
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass\left(I^{(n)}\right)\end{subarray}}{\mathfrak{q}}=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I^{(n)})}{\mathfrak{p}}B\subset\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}B,\text{ as }\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass\left(I^{(n)}\right)}{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}.

∎

We are ready to prove the main theorem of this section. The theorem states that if a flat morphism extends associative primes to associative primes and each associative prime is a contraction of some ideal, then symbolic powers of the extension of an ideal is the extension of the symbolic powers of the ideal.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
A
and
B
be Noetherian rings and
\phi:A\to B
be a flat morphism. Assume that
I\subset A
is a proper ideal such that
\Ass^{*}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass^{*}(I)\}
, and every associated prime of
I
is a contraction of some ideal in
B
. Then
(IB)^{(n)}=I^{(n)}B
, for all
n\geqslant 1
. Furthermore, if
I^{l}
is a contraction of some ideals for all
\ell\geqslant 1
, then
I^{(m)}\subset I^{r}
, if and only if
IB^{(m)}\subset IB^{r}
, for all
m,r\geqslant 1
, then we get
\rho(IB)=\rho(I)
and
\rho_{a}(IB)=\rho(I)
.

Proof.

First we check that as in Lemma
2.4
,
\Ass_{B}(IB)=\{{{\mathfrak{p}}}B~|~{{\mathfrak{p}}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)\}
. Using the definition of symbolic powers of an ideal and the assumption, we get

(IB)^{(n)}=\bigcap_{{{\mathfrak{p}}}\in\Ass_{B}(IB)}((IB)^{n}B_{{{\mathfrak{p}}}}\cap B)=\bigcap_{\{{{\mathfrak{p}}}B~|~{{\mathfrak{p}}}\in\Ass_{A}(I)\}}((IB)^{n}B_{{{\mathfrak{p}}}B}\cap B).

Applying Lemma
2.2
, we have
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
. Then by Lemma
2.1
, we have the following:

\displaystyle(IB)^{n}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\cap B

\displaystyle\cong(I^{n}\otimes_{A}B)(A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)

Next we want to show that
((I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)=(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)
.

It is known that
A\otimes_{A}B\subset A\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
if
S=B\backslash\mathfrak{p}B
has no zero divisors. Suppose
S
has no zero divisors, then applying a property from
[
17
, section 7]
, which states that if
M_{2}
is flat over
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
is flat over
M_{2}
, then
M_{3}
is flat over
M_{1}
. Here we consider
M_{3}
as
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
,
M_{2}
as
B
, and
M_{1}
as
A
, then we can see
B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
is flat over
A
. By
[
17
, Theorem 7.4(I)]
, we consider
I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
A
as submodules of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
A
-modules, then
\left(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\right)\cap\left(A\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\right)=(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
. Therefore, intersecting it with
A\otimes_{A}B
will not change the original intersection.

If
S
has zero divisors, then
B\backslash B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
has exactly the same set of zero divisors in
S
, denoted as
S^{\prime}
. Note that any
a\otimes b\in I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}
is always
0
in
I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
, and similarly for
a\otimes b\in A\otimes_{A}S^{\prime}
in
A\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
. Observe that these 0’s in
A\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
are 0’s in
(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)
, so we can always intersect
I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
with
A\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}
first, then
A\otimes_{A}B
, which leads to the same equality. Therefore
(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)=((I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)
as we desire.

Applying lemma
2.1
again on
((I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)
, we will have our second isomorphism
((I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)\otimes_{A}B_{\mathfrak{p}B})\cap(A\otimes_{A}B)\cong(I^{n}A_{p}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\cap B
. Note that lemma
2.1
is used previously for the “backward” direction for the first isomorphism, and now applying it again for the “forward” direction for our second isomorphism, so we will have a new equality (two isomorphisms give an identity map), which is
(IB)^{n}B_{\mathfrak{p}B}\cap B=(I^{n}A_{p}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\cap B
. Then the following equality is given:

\displaystyle(IB)^{(n)}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}(IB)^{n}B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\cap B=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}((I^{n}A_{p}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B})\cap B

We want to prove the following labeled equalities:

\displaystyle(IB)^{(n)}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}((I^{n}A_{p}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B})\cap B

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}I^{(n)}B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\cap B

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}I^{(n)}BB_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\cap B

(2.3)

\displaystyle=I^{(n)}B_{W}\cap B,\text{ where }W=B-\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}B

(2.4)

\displaystyle=(I^{(n)}B)B_{W}\cap B

(2.5)

\displaystyle=I^{(n)}B

First we prove equality
2.1
. Since
\phi
is flat, and extension commutes with intersection under flat morphism, we get

\displaystyle I^{(n)}B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}=\left(\cap_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\in\Ass(I)}(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\cap A)\right)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}\in\Ass(I)}((I^{n}A_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B})=(I^{n}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap A)B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}.

The equality
2.2
is straight forward.

The proof of equality
2.3
is similar to
[
7
, Remark 2.8]
. We add the proof for completion. Note that

I^{(n)}B_{W}\cap B=\{r\in B~|~rs\in I^{(n)}\text{ for some }s\in W\}
and thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle I^{(n)}B_{W}\cap B\subset\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}\left(\left(I^{(n)}B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\right)\cap B\right).

Now let
r\in\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}\left(\left(I^{(n)}B_{{\mathfrak{p}}B}\right)\cap B\right)
. Then for each

there is

such that

. Thus

is not contained in any

. Since the associated primes are finite then by Prime avoidance theorem

I^{(n)}B:r\not\subset\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}B.

Then there is a
s\in W
such that

. Hence we have the equality as sets.

Again the equality
2.4
is straight forward. Lastly, we prove equality

.
Now from

(I^{(n)}B)B_{W}\cap B=\bigcup_{s\in W}I^{(n)}B:s.

Now by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigcup_{\Ass{\mathfrak{q}}\in\left(I^{(n)}B\right)}{\mathfrak{q}}\subset\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Ass(IB)}{\mathfrak{q}}=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}{\mathfrak{p}}B.

Therefore whenever
s\in W
then
s\not\in{\mathfrak{q}}
, where
{\mathfrak{q}}
is an associated prime of

. By

we get

(I^{(n)}B)B_{W}\cap B=\bigcup_{s\in W}I^{(n)}B:s=I^{(n)}B.

Note that

is a contraction of some ideal.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that if placing
\Ass^{*}(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass^{*}(I)\}
by
\Ass(IB^{N})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I^{N})\}
, then the theorem
2.5
is only true up for
1\leqslant n\leqslant N
.

If one uses the minimal prime definition of the symbolic powers, then it is enough to assume that

\Min(IB)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}B~|~{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Min(I)\}
.

The following example shows that the condition in Theorem
2.5
that the associated primes are contraction of the ideals is necessary.

Example 2.7
.

Consider the natural map
\phi:{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\to{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
. Note that it is a flat map. Take
I=\langle x,2\rangle\subset{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
. Then
I
is not a contraction of any ideals of
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
. Since
I
is a maximal ideal, then
I^{(n)}=I^{n}
for all
n\geqslant 1
. But
I{\mathbb{Q}}[x]=\langle x\rangle
. Hence
(I{\mathbb{Q}}[x])^{(n)}=\langle x^{n}\rangle
for all
n\geqslant 1

3.
Resurgence of sum of ideals

First note that in this section, we assume that all rings and algebras are
regular
. We summarize current results of
resurgence number
of sums of ideals for polynomial rings over a field, our goal is to prove these results for sums of finitely generated graded
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebras which are integral domains, where
{\mathbbm{k}}
is algebraically closed. In
[
3
, Theorem 2.6]
, it has been proved that if
I
and
J
are ideals in different polynomial rings over a same field, then

\rho_{a}(I+J)=\overline{\rho}_{\alpha}(I+J)=\overline{\rho}(I+J)={\max}\{\rho_{a}(I),\rho_{a}(J)\}.

In
[
15
, Theorem 2.3]
, it has been proved that

\max\{\rho(I),\,\rho(J)\}\leqslant\rho(I+J)\leqslant\max\left\{\rho(I),\,\rho(J),\,\frac{2(\rho(I)+\rho(J))}{3}\right\}.

In our paper,
[
3
, Theorem 2.6]
is generalized to Theorem
3.5
, and
[
15
, Theorem 2.3]
is generalized to Theorem
3.6
. The generalization is not trivial. First, it is obvious that the maps from two polynomials rings
A
and
B
over a field
{\mathbbm{k}}
to
A\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}B
preserve resurgence numbers. However, for our generalization, we need to apply Corollary
2.10
to achieve that resurgence number of an ideal is same as resurgence number of its extension. Second, in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we use vector space method, while Gröbner basis method was used in the polynomial case but could not be used in
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebra case.

In Lemma
3.1
, we will specify that under proper conditions resurgence of the extension is the same as the resurgence of the ideals.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be finitely generated
{\mathbbm{k}}
algebras, where
{\mathbbm{k}}
is algebraically closed, which are integral domains. If
\phi:A\to R
is a canonical ring homomorphism, where
R=A\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}B
, then
\phi
is flat and has prime extension property. Furthermore,
\rho(IR)=\rho(I)
and
\rho_{a}(IR)=\rho_{a}(I)
for any proper ideal
I
from
A
(or
B
).

Proof.

Recall that the canonical ring homomorphism is defined by

\phi:A\to A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}B\text{ defined by }a\rightarrow a\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}1.

Observe that if we can show
\phi
is injective, then
I\rightarrow A\otimes_{A}R
is injective for every ideal
I\subset A
. Hence by Lemma
2.13
,
\phi
is flat. Injectivity of
\phi
is obvious since both
A
and
B
are fintely generated algebras over an algebraically closed field
{\mathbbm{k}}
, and integral domains. If there exists a nonzero element in
{\mathbbm{k}}
annihilating some elements in
A
(or in
B
), then this element has to be zero in
A
(or in
B
), which is a contradiction.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset A
be a prime ideal, we need to prove that
{\mathfrak{p}}R
is also prime. Since
\phi
is injective then
\mathfrak{p}R=\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}B
. Observe that
R/\mathfrak{p}R=\left(A/\mathfrak{p}\right)\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}B
because
R
is an integral domain.

Now
\left(A/\mathfrak{p}\right)\otimes_{k}B
is an integral domain by
[
14
, Lemma 1.54]
.
It is a well-known fact that an ideal is prime if only if the quotient ring is an integral domain, so
\mathfrak{p}R
is prime, and thus,
\phi
has prime extension property. The last equality follows from Corollary
2.10
.
∎

The next lemma also extends
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
. The proof of
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
depends on divisibility in the polynomial ring. While working with general domains, we treated them as vector spaces and used properties of basis. Let us recall the following definition from
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
I
be an ideal in a regular ring. Then
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup\rho(I,t)
is defined as follows:

\rho(I,t)=\sup\left\{\dfrac{m}{r}~|~I^{(m)}\not\subseteq I^{r},m\geqslant t,r\geqslant t\right\},\text{ and }\rho^{\lim\sup}_{a}(I)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup\rho(I,t).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be finitely generated
{\mathbbm{k}}
-algebras, and let
I\subset A
and
J\subset B
be nonzero proper homogeneous ideals. If
I+J
be the sum of ideal extensions of
I
and
J
in
R=A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}B
then

\rho_{a}^{\lim\sup}(I+J)\geqslant{\max}\left\{\rho_{a}^{{\lim\sup}}(I),\,\rho_{a}^{{\lim\sup}}(J)\right\}.

Proof.

Take
\theta\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\theta>\rho_{a}^{\lim\sup}(I+J)
. Then there is a
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
t\geqslant s_{0},\theta>\rho(I+J,t)
, where
\rho(I+J,t)=\sup\left\{\dfrac{m}{r}~|~I^{(m)}\not\subset I^{r},m\geqslant t,r\geqslant t\right\}
. Thus for any
h,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that
h,r\geqslant t\geqslant s_{0}
and
\dfrac{h}{r}\geqslant\theta
, one has
(I+J)^{r}\supset(I+J)^{(h)}\supset I^{(h)}
.
Up to this point the proof idea is same as
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
. Now we need to show that
I^{(h)}\subseteq I^{r}
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathbbm{k}}
basis of
I
, which extends to
V^{*}
a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis of
A
containing
1
. Let
W
be a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis of
J
, which extends to
W^{*}
, a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis of
B
containing
1
. Then
V\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}W^{*}
is a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis for
I\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}B
and
V^{*}\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}W
is a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis for
V\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}W^{*}
. Also,
V^{*}\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}W^{*}
is a
{\mathbbm{k}}
-basis of
R=A\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}B
.
Let
f\in I^{(h)}
then
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}v_{i}
, where
c_{i}\in{\mathbbm{k}}\setminus\{0\}
and
v_{i}\in V
.
Now

f\otimes 1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}cv_{i}\otimes 1\in(I+J)^{(h)}\subset(I+J)^{r}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(IR)^{i}(JR)^{r-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\left((IR)^{j}\cap(JR)^{r-j}\right).

The last equality follows from
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
.
Let

be an arbitrary element, and consider
f\otimes 1\in(I+J)^{(h)}\subseteq(I+J)^{r}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(IR)^{i}(JR)^{r-i}
. Since

then
v\otimes 1\notin A\otimes J
. Thus none of the
c_{i}v_{i}\otimes 1
appearing in the expression of

will be in
(IR)^{j}\cap(JR)^{r-j}
, where

. Hence
f\otimes 1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}cv_{i}\otimes 1\in(IR)^{h}
.
Now
(IR)^{h}\cong I^{h}\otimes B
and thus

implies that

. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.6

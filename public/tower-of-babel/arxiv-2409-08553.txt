Parallel spinors for G 2 ∗ \mathrm{G}_{2}^{*} and isotropic structures
Alejandro Gil-Garc \́mathbf{i} a 1,†, C. S. Shahbazi 2,‡

Abstract
. We obtain a correspondence between irreducible real parallel spinors on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds ( M , g ) (M,g) of signature ( 4 , 3 ) (4,3) and solutions of an associated differential system for three-forms that satisfy a homogeneous algebraic equation of order two in the Kähler-Atiyah bundle of ( M , g ) (M,g) . Applying this general framework, we obtain an intrinsic algebraic characterization of G 2 ∗ \mathrm{G}_{2}^{*} -structures as well as the first explicit description of isotropic irreducible spinors in signature ( 4 , 3 ) (4,3) that are parallel under a general connection on the spinor bundle. This description is given in terms of a coherent system of mutually orthogonal and isotropic one-forms and follows from the characterization of the stabilizer of an isotropic spinor as the stabilizer of a highly degenerate three-form that we construct explicitly. Using this result, we show that isotropic spinors parallel under a metric connection with torsion exist when the connection preserves the aforementioned coherent system. This allows us to construct a natural class of metrics of signature ( 4 , 3 ) (4,3) on ℝ 7 \mathbb{R}^{7} that admit spinors parallel under a metric connection with torsion. Keywords: Spin geometry, parallel spinors, spinorial exterior forms, G 2 ∗ \mathrm{G}_{2}^{*} -structures MSC classification: Primary 53C27; Secondary 53C10, 53C50, 15A66

This general concept of parallel spinor subsumes many classes of special spinors that have been extensively studied in the literature on a case-by-case basis, such as parallel spinors, Killing spinors, Codazzi spinors, Cauchy spinors, skew-torsion parallel spinors, skew-Killing spinors or generalized Killing spinors, just to name a few
[

This theory is based on describing parallel spinors in terms of solutions to an equivalent differential system for an exterior form that is algebraically constrained in the Kähler-Atiyah bundle of the underlying pseudo-Riemannian manifold. . In this regard, we should note that even though the square of a spinor remains to be studied systematically, its use as a tool in spin geometry is classical and can be traced back at least to the seminal work of Dadok and Harvey on spinors and calibrations

Main results and outline

•

Section
1
is devoted to computing the algebraic square of an irreducible real spinor, possibly of vanishing pseudo-norm, in signature
(4,3)
. This computation produces two new results. For non-isotropic spinors, it leads to an intrinsic characterization of
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-structures as solutions of a homogeneous algebraic equation of order two, see Theorem
1.7
. This characterization is intrinsic since it does not involve any choice of privileged basis or
standard form
. In the isotropic case, it leads to the description of the stabilizer of an isotropic irreducible spinor as the stabilizer of a special and highly degenerate three-form that we compute explicitly, see Theorem
1.12
. Key to these results is the algebraic characterization of spinorial exterior forms developed in
[
49
]
.

•

In Section
2
we study parallel spinors in signature
(4,3)
. First, we obtain a
master formula
that describes a general irreducible real parallel spinor, perhaps of non-constant pseudo-norm, in terms of an equivalent differential system for its
square
as a spinorial exterior form, see Proposition
2.6
. Elaborating on this result, in Theorem
2.7
we present a characterization of the most general nowhere isotropic parallel spinor in terms of an algebraically constrained three-form satisfying a prescribed differential system, which we give explicitly. As a corollary, we obtain an
obstruction
for a nowhere vanishing and isotropic spinor to be a parallel spinor relative to given data. We then consider the case of an isotropic parallel spinor, which we present in Theorem
2.13
in terms of an equivalent differential system for an
isotropic coherent system
of triples of one-forms, see Definition
2.11
. As a particular case, we obtain a natural characterization of isotropic spinors parallel under a metric connection with torsion, see Corollary
2.14
, which we apply to construct a natural class of metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
that admit spinors parallel under a metric connection with torsion. Thanks to our formalism we can easily give explicit expressions for these metrics and the corresponding contorsion tensors, obtaining an obstruction for a general contorsion tensor to admit a connection preserving an isotropic spinor. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first results on isotropic irreducible spinors in signature
(4,3)
since the seminal work of Kath
[
33
]
.

As proposed originally by C. Lazaroiu, the theory we have developed here in signature
(4,3)
can also be developed in seven and eight-dimensional Euclidean signatures, leading to new results
[
42
]
and to an alternative approach to the general theory developed in
[
1
]
.

1.
Algebraic
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-structures and spinorial exterior forms

In this section, we obtain a novel and intrinsic algebraic characterization of the stabilizer of an irreducible real spinor in signature
(4,3)
. We will use this algebraic characterization in Section
2
as the basis of our investigation on irreducible parallel spinors on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold of signature
(4,3)
. We begin with a description of the stabilizer of an irreducible real spinor in signature
(4,3)
as presented in
[
33
]
.

1.1.
The stabilizer of an irreducible real spinor

Let
V
be an oriented seven-dimensional real vector space equipped with a non-degenerate metric
h
of signature
(4,3)
and let
(V^{*},h^{*})
be the quadratic space dual to
(V,h)
. Let
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3):=\mathrm{Cl}(V^{*},h^{*})
be the Clifford algebra associated to
(V^{*},h^{*})
defined in the convention:

\theta^{2}=h^{*}(\theta,\theta)\,,\quad\theta\in V^{*}\,.

Denote by
\pi
the standard automorphism of
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
and by
\tau
the standard anti-automorphism. In signature
(4,3)
, the pseudo-Riemannian volume form
\nu\in\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
squares to the identity and belongs to the center of
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
is non-simple and splits as a direct sum of unital and associative algebras:

(1)

\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)=\mathrm{Cl}_{+}(4,3)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}_{-}(4,3),

where:

\mathrm{Cl}_{l}(4,3)=\{x\in\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)\mid\nu x=lx\}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+l\nu)\mathrm{Cl}(4,3),\quad l\in\{-1,+1\}.

The algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{l}(4,3)
is simple and isomorphic to
\mathrm{Mat}(8,\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
admits two irreducible left Clifford modules of real dimension eight:

\gamma_{l}:\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)\to\mathrm{End}(\Sigma),

that correspond to the projection of
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
to the factor
\mathrm{Cl}_{l}(4,3)
composed with an isomorphism of the latter to the algebra
\mathrm{End}(\Sigma)
of endomorphisms of an eight-dimensional real vector space
\Sigma
. These two Clifford modules are distinguished by the value they take at the volume form
\nu\in\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
, namely:

\gamma_{l}(\nu)=l\,\mathrm{Id}\in\mathrm{End}(\Sigma).

It is shown in
[
49
, Proposition 2.1]
that we can equip
\Sigma
with a symmetric bilinear pairing
\Scr B
that satisfies:

\Scr B(\gamma_{l}(x)\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=\Scr B(\varepsilon_{1},\gamma_{l}((\pi\circ\tau)(x))\varepsilon_{2}),\quad x\in\mathrm{Cl}(4,3),\quad\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\Sigma,\quad l\in\{-1,+1\}.

Such pairing is called an
admissible pairing

[
2
,
3
]
. The admissible pairing
\Scr B
is invariant under the action of the identity component
\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)\subset\mathrm{Spin}(4,3)
of the spin group
\mathrm{Spin}(4,3)
.

Definition 1.1
.

The triple
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l},\Scr B)
is an
irreducible paired Clifford module
if
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
is an irreducible Clifford module and
\Scr B
is an admissible pairing on
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
.

It was shown in
[
33
, Corollary 2.1]
that if
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
is a spinor with
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\neq 0
then its stabilizer:

\mathrm{Stab}(\varepsilon)=\{x\in\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)\mid\gamma_{l}(x)\varepsilon=\varepsilon\}

in
\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)
is the Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
. This is the connected non-compact dual of the Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
with trivial center and fundamental group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. It was also shown in
[
33
, Corollary 2.1]
that the Lie algebra of the stabilizer of a non-zero spinor of zero norm is a semi-direct product of a six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
(see also Proposition
1.13
). We call a spinor of zero norm an
isotropic spinor
, and we will refer to the reduction defined by an isotropic spinor as an
isotropic spinorial structure
.

Remark 1.2
.

Given a spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
of non-zero norm, we can decompose
\Sigma=\langle\varepsilon\rangle\oplus\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{\perp}
via:

\Sigma\ni\eta=\tfrac{\Scr B(\eta,\varepsilon)}{\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\varepsilon+\left(\eta-\tfrac{\Scr B(\eta,\varepsilon)}{\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\varepsilon\right)\,.

It follows that the space
\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{\perp}
is isomorphic to
\{v\cdot\varepsilon\mid v\in V\}
by
[
33
, Corollary 2.2]
.

Consider now the universal covering
\lambda:\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)\to\mathrm{SO}_{0}(4,3)
and for ease of notation denote again by
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
the image of
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}\subset\mathrm{Spin}(4,3)
in
\mathrm{SO}(4,3)
, namely
\lambda(\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*})\subset\mathrm{SO}(4,3)
. As a subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}(4,3)
and consequently of
\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
, the Lie group
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
can be described as the stabilizer of a special three-form
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
given by:

(2)

\phi=-e^{127}-e^{135}+e^{146}+e^{236}+e^{245}-e^{347}+e^{567}

in an adequate basis
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{7})
of
V^{\ast}
. In particular,
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=-7
. Then:

\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}=\{A\in\mathrm{Aut}(V)\mid A^{*}\phi=\phi\}.

For further reference, and following
[
33
, Proposition 2.4]
, we describe below the decomposition of
\Lambda^{k}V^{*}
into irreducible components of the
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
-action:

(1)

\Lambda^{1}V^{*}=\Lambda^{1}_{7}
is irreducible.

(2)

\Lambda^{2}V^{*}=\Lambda^{2}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14}
, where:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{7}

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}\mid*(\phi\wedge\omega)=2\omega\}=\{\iota_{v}\phi\mid v\in V\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{14}

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}\mid*(\phi\wedge\omega)=-\omega\}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}.

(3)

\Lambda^{3}V^{*}=\Lambda^{3}_{1}\oplus\Lambda^{3}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{3}_{27}
, where:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{t\phi\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}=\langle\phi\rangle,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}_{7}

\displaystyle=\{*(\phi\wedge\theta)\mid\theta\in\Lambda^{1}V^{*}\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}_{27}

\displaystyle=\{\rho\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}\mid\rho\wedge\phi=0,\,\rho\wedge*\phi=0\}.

This decomposition is completely analogous to the one occurring for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures in seven Euclidean dimensions. As we will see later, this type of decomposition does no longer hold for isotropic spinorial structures.

1.2.
Spinorial exterior forms

In this subsection, we briefly revisit the formalism of spinorial exterior forms that describes under which algebraic conditions an exterior form is the square of an irreducible real spinor. We follow the exposition of
[
49
]
for the signature
(p,q)=(4,3)
. In order to construct the
square
of a spinor as an exterior form, we use the natural identification:

(3)

\Psi\colon(\Lambda V^{*},\diamond)\to\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)

between
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
and the Kähler-Atiyah algebra of
(V^{*},h^{*})
, which we denote by
(\Lambda V^{*},\diamond)

[
12
,
13
]

\diamond:\Lambda V^{*}\times\Lambda V^{*}\to\Lambda V^{*}
denotes the
geometric product
determined by
h
. This is given by the linear and associative extension of the following expression:

\theta\diamond\alpha=\theta\wedge\alpha+\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}\alpha\,,\quad\theta\in V^{*}\,,\quad\alpha\in\Lambda V^{*}.

In order to do computations with the geometric product it is convenient to introduce the
generalized products
of
(V^{*},h^{*})
. These are the bilinear operators
\triangle_{k}:\Lambda^{a}V^{*}\times\Lambda^{b}V^{*}\to\Lambda^{a+b-2k}V^{*}
, where
k=0,\ldots,7
, defined through the expansion:

\alpha\diamond\beta=\sum_{k=0}^{7}(-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}+ak}\alpha\triangle_{k}\beta,\quad\alpha\in\Lambda^{a}V^{*}\,,\quad\beta\in\Lambda V^{*}.

Choosing a basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\}
of
V
we can express the generalized products as

\alpha\triangle_{k}\beta=\tfrac{1}{k!}h^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots h^{i_{k}j_{k}}(\iota_{e_{i_{1}}}\ldots\iota_{e_{i_{k}}}\alpha)\wedge(\iota_{e_{j_{1}}}\ldots\iota_{e_{j_{k}}}\beta).

We collect below some useful properties of the generalized products that we will use in later computations.

Proposition 1.3

(

.

Let
\alpha\in\Lambda^{a}V^{*}
and
\beta\in\Lambda^{b}V^{*}

•

\alpha\triangle_{k}\beta=0
if
k>a
or
k>b
.

•

\alpha\triangle_{k}\beta=(-1)^{(a-k)(b-k)}\beta\triangle_{k}\alpha

\alpha\triangle_{k}\alpha=0
if
a-k
is odd.

•

\alpha\triangle_{0}\beta=\alpha\wedge\beta
and if

\alpha\triangle_{a}\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
.

\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)

(\Lambda V^{*},\diamond)=(\Lambda_{+}V^{*},\diamond)\oplus(\Lambda_{-}V^{*},\diamond),

\Lambda_{l}V^{*}=\{\alpha\in\Lambda V^{*}\mid\nu\diamond\alpha=l\alpha\}=\{\alpha\in\Lambda V^{*}\mid*\tau(\alpha)=l\alpha\},\quad l\in\{-1,+1\}.

Here we have used the identity:

(4)

\alpha\diamond\nu=\nu\diamond\alpha=*\tau(\alpha).

By composing the natural identification
\Psi\colon(\Lambda V^{*},\diamond)\to\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
with the irreducible representation
\gamma_{l}:\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)\to\mathrm{End}(\Sigma)

\Psi_{l}:=\gamma_{l}\circ\Psi\colon(\Lambda V^{*},\diamond)\to\mathrm{End}(\Sigma).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{l}:\Lambda V^{*}\to\Lambda_{l}V^{*}
be the natural projection given explicitly by:

(5)

\mathcal{P}_{l}(\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+l\nu)\diamond\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha+l*\tau(\alpha)),

where we have used the identity (
4
), and consider the canonical linear inclusion
\iota_{l}:\Lambda_{l}V^{*}\hookrightarrow\Lambda V^{*}
, which is a right inverse to
\mathcal{P}_{l}
. In particular:

\Psi_{l}\circ\iota_{l}:(\Lambda_{l}V^{*},\diamond)\to\mathrm{End}(\Sigma)

is an isomorphism of unital and associative algebras. Following
[
38

37

39
]
, we define:

\Lambda^{<}V^{*}:=\Lambda^{0}V^{*}\oplus\Lambda^{1}V^{*}\oplus\Lambda^{2}V^{*}\oplus\Lambda^{3}V^{*}\subset\Lambda V^{*}.

Note that
\Lambda V^{*}=\Lambda^{<}V^{*}\oplus*\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
. Then, restricting
\mathcal{P}_{l}:\Lambda V^{*}\to\Lambda_{l}V^{*}
to
\Lambda^{<}V^{*}

to
\Lambda^{<}V^{*}

\alpha\vee\beta=2\mathcal{P}_{<}(\mathcal{P}_{l}(\alpha\diamond\beta)),

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}:\Sigma\to\mathrm{End}(\Sigma),\quad\varepsilon\mapsto\mu\,\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon^{*},

Proposition 1.4
.

The spinor square map

is equivariant with respect to the double covering morphism
\lambda\colon\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)\to\mathrm{SO}_{0}(4,3)
, that is:

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}_{l}(\gamma_{l}(x)\varepsilon)=\lambda(x)(\mathcal{E}^{\mu}_{l}(\varepsilon))\quad\forall\,\,x\in\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)\quad\forall\,\,\varepsilon\in\Sigma,

where the right-hand side denotes the natural action of
\lambda(x)\in\mathrm{SO}_{0}(4,3)

Hence, roughly speaking the stabilizers of a spinor and its associated spinorial exterior form can differ at most by a

, gives the algebraic characterization of spinors in terms of exterior forms in signature

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l},\Scr B)
be an irreducible paired Clifford module. The following statements are equivalent for an exterior form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
:

(1)

\alpha
is the square of a spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
, namely
\alpha=\mathcal{E}_{l}^{\mu}(\varepsilon)
for some
\mu\in\{-1,+1\}
.

(2)

\alpha
satisfies the following relations:

\alpha\vee\alpha=8\alpha^{(0)}\alpha,\quad(\pi\circ\tau)(\alpha)=\alpha,\quad\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=8(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\alpha,

for a fixed exterior form
\beta\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
such that
(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\neq 0
.

(3)

The following relations hold:

(\pi\circ\tau)(\alpha)=\alpha,\quad\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=8(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\alpha,

for every exterior form
\beta\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
.

As it follows from the second point above, if
\varepsilon
is of non-zero pseudo-norm then it suffices to take
\beta=1
. This is in general not enough if
\varepsilon
is isotropic and illustrates the difficulty of this case in relation to the generic case of non-zero pseudo-norm.

1.3.
The non-degenerate algebraic characterization

In this subsection, we use Corollary
1.5
to give an intrinsic algebraic characterization of
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
-structures in terms of exterior forms. An element
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
can be expressed as
\alpha=\alpha^{(0)}+\alpha^{(1)}+\alpha^{(2)}+\alpha^{(3)}
, where
\alpha^{(j)}\in\Lambda^{j}V^{*}
for
j=0,1,2,3
. Recall that

\pi(\alpha)=(-1)^{a}\alpha,\quad\tau(\alpha)=(-1)^{\binom{a}{2}}\alpha,\quad\alpha\in\Lambda^{a}V^{*}.

The condition
(\pi\circ\tau)(\alpha)=\alpha
is linear and simply implies that
\alpha^{(1)}=\alpha^{(2)}=0
. Set
c:=\alpha^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}=\Lambda^{0}V^{*}
and
\phi:=\alpha^{(3)}\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
. Now we compute
\alpha\vee\alpha
. First, we need to obtain
\alpha\diamond\alpha
:

\alpha\diamond\alpha=(c+\phi)\diamond(c+\phi)=c^{2}+2c\phi+\phi\diamond\phi=c^{2}+2c\phi+\phi\triangle_{1}\phi-\langle\phi,\phi\rangle.

Using now (
5
) together with the previous expression, we obtain:

\mathcal{P}_{l}(\alpha\diamond\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(c^{2}+2c\phi+\phi\triangle_{1}\phi-\langle\phi,\phi\rangle+l(*c^{2}+2c*\tau(\phi)+*\tau(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)-*\langle\phi,\phi\rangle)\big),

from which we conclude:

\alpha\vee\alpha=2\mathcal{P}_{<}(\mathcal{P}_{l}(\alpha\diamond\alpha))=c^{2}+2c\phi-\langle\phi,\phi\rangle+l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi),

where we have used that
\tau(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)=\phi\triangle_{1}\phi\in\Lambda^{4}V^{*}
. Hence the condition
\alpha\vee\alpha=8\alpha^{(0)}\alpha
implies:

\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=-7c^{2},\quad 6c\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi).

Summarizing this discussion we obtain the following result.

Proposition 1.6
.

An exterior form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
is the square of a spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
only if
\alpha=c+\phi
, with
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
, satisfying:

(6)

\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=-7c^{2},\quad 6c\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi).

If in addition
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\neq 0
, then
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle<0
and the previous condition is also sufficient.

Proof.

If
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\neq 0
then
c\neq 0
as explained below, and thus taking
\beta=1
in Corollary
1.5
we obtain an equivalence since in this case
(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}=\alpha^{(0)}=c\neq 0
.
∎

Hence, elaborating on Equation (
6
), we conclude that
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
is the square of an irreducible real spinor in signature
(4,3)
only if:

\alpha=\kappa\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}+\phi,

where
\kappa\in\{-1,+1\}
is a sign and
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{\ast}
satisfies:

6\,\kappa\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}\,\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi).

On the other hand, if
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{\ast}
is the positive square of
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
, then from the general theory of spinorial exterior forms, see
[
49
]
, we obtain:

\alpha=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=0}^{3}\,\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}}\Scr B(\Psi_{l}^{<}(e^{i_{k}})^{-1}\cdots\Psi_{l}^{<}(e^{i_{1}})^{-1}\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\,e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{k}}

and thus:

\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{2}=-\frac{64}{7}\langle\phi,\phi\rangle

and if
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\neq 0
then
\kappa
is the sign of the pseudo-norm
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
of
\varepsilon
. Finally, Propositions
1.4
and
1.6
imply the following intrinsic characterization of
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-structures.

Theorem 1.7
.

The stabilizer of a three-form
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{\ast}
is
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}\subset\mathrm{SO}_{0}(4,3)
if and only if
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle<0
and:

(7)

6\,\kappa\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}\,\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi).

If that is the case, then the metric induced by
\phi
is homothetic to the underlying metric
h
. If in addition
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle=-7
then the metric induced by
\phi
is precisely
h
.

Remark 1.8
.

This result should be compared with
[
34
, Theorem 7.3]
, where an alternative algebraic characterization of
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
structures is obtained using different methods.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{\ast}
be a three-form satisfying
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle<0
as well as Equation (
7
). Then:

\alpha=\kappa\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}+\phi

is the positive square of an irreducible real spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
, that is,
\mathcal{E}^{+}_{l}(\varepsilon)=\alpha
. By
[

\phi
is stabilized by
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}\subset\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)
and hence by Proposition
1.4
it follows that
\phi

\lambda(\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2})\subset\mathrm{SO}_{0}(4,3)

\phi

-7

(
2

2

As a corollary to the previous theorem and discussion, we obtain the following result for

Corollary 1.9
.

An exterior form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{\ast}
is the square of a pseudo-unit spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
if and only if:

\alpha=\frac{\kappa}{8}+\phi,

where
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{\ast}
satisfies:

6\,\kappa\,\phi=8\,l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi).

We end this section with a technical lemma necessary for later computations.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
c+\phi\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
satisfying Equation (
6
) with
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle\neq 0
. Then:

(1)

(\iota_{v}\phi)\triangle_{1}\phi=3lc*(\phi\wedge v^{\flat})\in\Lambda^{3}_{7}
for
v\in V
.

(2)

\omega\triangle_{1}\phi=0
for
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}_{14}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
.

(3)

*\big((*(\theta\wedge\phi))\triangle_{1}\phi\big)=3lc*(\theta\wedge\phi)\in\Lambda^{3}_{7}
for
\theta\in V^{*}
.

(4)

*(\rho\triangle_{1}\phi)=-3lc\rho
for
\rho\in\Lambda^{3}_{27}
.

Proof.

(1) We have
6c\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)
, which is equivalent to
\phi\triangle_{1}\phi=-6lc*\phi
. Hence

(\iota_{v}\phi)\triangle_{1}\phi=\phi(v,e_{i})\wedge\phi(e_{i})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{v}(\phi(e_{i})\wedge\phi(e_{i}))=-\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{v}(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{v}(-6lc*\phi)=3lc*(\phi\wedge v^{\flat}).

(2) Recall that
\Omega^{*}\phi=\phi
for
\Omega\in\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
by definition. Thus
\omega\cdot\phi=0
for
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, where
\omega\cdot\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
is defined by

(\omega\cdot\phi)(u,v,w)=\phi(\omega u,v,w)+\phi(u,\omega v,w)+\phi(u,v,\omega w),

which is expressed in terms of a basis by

\omega\cdot\phi=\tfrac{1}{3!}(\omega\cdot\phi)_{ijk}e^{ijk}=\tfrac{1}{3!}(\omega^{l}_{i}\phi_{ljk}+\omega^{l}_{j}\phi_{ilk}+\omega^{l}_{k}\phi_{ijl})e^{ijk}=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega^{l}_{i}\phi_{ljk}e^{ijk}.

Therefore

\omega\triangle_{1}\phi=h^{ml}\iota_{e_{m}}\omega\wedge\iota_{e_{l}}\phi=\tfrac{1}{2}h^{ml}\omega_{mi}\phi_{ljk}e^{ijk}=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega^{l}_{i}\phi_{ljk}e^{ijk}=\omega\cdot\phi=0.

(3) We compute

\displaystyle*\big((*(\theta\wedge\phi))\triangle_{1}\phi\big)

\displaystyle=*\big(\iota_{e_{i}}(*(\theta\wedge\phi))\wedge\phi(e_{i})\big)=*\big(*(\theta\wedge\phi\wedge e^{i})\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{k}\big)\phi_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\iota_{e_{j}}\iota_{e_{k}}*^{2}(\theta\wedge\phi\wedge e^{i})\phi_{ijk}=-\iota_{e_{j}}(\theta(e_{k})\phi\wedge e^{i}-\theta\wedge\phi(e_{k})\wedge e^{i})\phi_{ijk}

\displaystyle=-(\theta(e_{k})\phi(e_{j})\wedge e^{i}-\theta(e_{j})\phi(e_{k})\wedge e^{i}+\theta\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{j})\wedge e^{i})\phi_{ijk}

\displaystyle=-2\phi_{ijk}\theta(e_{k})\phi(e_{j})\wedge e^{i}-\phi_{ijk}\theta\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{j})\wedge e^{i}.

Note that
\phi_{ijk}\theta\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{j})\wedge e^{i}=\theta\wedge(\phi_{ijk}\phi_{kjl}e^{li})=\theta\wedge(\phi\triangle_{2}\phi)=0
. We also have that

\displaystyle\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)

\displaystyle=\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}(\phi(e_{i})\wedge\phi(e_{i}))=2\phi(e_{i},\theta^{\sharp})\wedge\phi(e_{i})=-2(\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}\phi(e_{i}))\wedge\phi(e_{i})

\displaystyle=-\phi_{iab}\phi_{icd}\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}(e^{ab})\wedge e^{cd}=-2\phi_{iab}\phi_{icd}\theta(e_{a})e^{bcd}=-4\phi_{iab}\theta(e_{a})\phi(e_{i})\wedge e^{b}.

Hence

*\big((*(\theta\wedge\phi))\triangle_{1}\phi\big)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iota_{\theta^{\sharp}}(-6lc*\phi)=-3lc*(\phi\wedge\theta).

(4) Here we use the description of
\Lambda^{3}_{27}
given in
[
32
, Equation 16]
, that is

\Lambda^{3}_{27}=\{A_{ij}h^{jk}e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k})\mid A_{ij}=A_{ji},\,\mathrm{Tr}_{h}(A)=h^{ij}A_{ij}=0\}.

Although this description is originally given for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures it also holds in the
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
case. Let
\rho\in\Lambda^{3}_{27}
given by
\rho=A_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k})
, then

\displaystyle\rho\triangle_{1}\phi

\displaystyle=\iota_{e_{a}}(A_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k}))\wedge\phi(e_{a})=A_{i}^{k}(h^{i}_{a}\phi(e_{k})-e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{a}))\wedge\phi(e_{a})

\displaystyle=A_{i}^{k}\phi(e_{k})\wedge\phi(e_{i})-A_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{a})\wedge\phi(e_{a}).

For the first term note that the metric induced by the three-form
\phi
is a multiple of the original metric
h
and then the quantity
\phi(e_{k})\wedge\phi(e_{i})
is just a multiple of
h_{k}^{i}*\phi
. Hence the first term is a multiple of
\mathrm{Tr}_{h}(A)*\phi
, thus is zero. For the second term we have

A_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\phi(e_{k},e_{a})\wedge\phi(e_{a})=-\tfrac{1}{2}A_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\iota_{e_{k}}(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)=3lcA_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge\iota_{e_{k}}(*\phi)=3lcA_{i}^{k}e^{i}\wedge*(\phi\wedge e^{k}).

Applying the Hodge star operator to this expression we obtain

\displaystyle 3lcA_{i}^{k}*(e^{i}\wedge*(\phi\wedge e^{k}))

\displaystyle=-3lcA_{i}^{k}\iota_{e_{i}}*^{2}(\phi\wedge e^{k})=3lcA_{i}^{k}\phi(e_{i})\wedge e^{k}-3lcA_{i}^{k}h_{i}^{k}\phi=3lc\rho,

since
A_{i}^{k}h_{i}^{k}=\mathrm{Tr}_{h}(A)=0
.
∎

1.4.
The isotropic algebraic characterization

Proposition
1.6
does not give sufficient conditions for an exterior form to be the square of an isotropic spinor, it only provides a necessary condition. Therefore the isotropic case, namely the case
\Scr B(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)=0
, needs to be considered separately. By Corollary
1.5
, we need to find an element
\beta\in\Lambda^{<}V^{\ast}
such that
(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\neq 0
. In order to do this, we first note that it is enough to take
\beta\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
\beta\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
and
\phi\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
. Then
(\phi\vee\beta)^{(0)}=-\langle\phi,\beta^{(3)}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\beta=\beta^{(0)}+\beta^{(1)}+\beta^{(2)}+\beta^{(3)}
. We first have
\phi\diamond\beta=\phi\beta^{(0)}+\phi\diamond\beta^{(1)}+\phi\diamond\beta^{(2)}+\phi\diamond\beta^{(3)}
with

\displaystyle\phi\diamond\beta^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\phi\wedge\beta^{(1)}+\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(1)},

\displaystyle\phi\diamond\beta^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\phi\wedge\beta^{(2)}+\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(2)}-\phi\triangle_{2}\beta^{(2)},

\displaystyle\phi\diamond\beta^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\phi\wedge\beta^{(3)}+\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(3)}-\phi\triangle_{2}\beta^{(3)}-\langle\phi,\beta^{(3)}\rangle.

Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{l}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+l*\tau(\alpha))
for any exterior form
\alpha
and
\mathcal{P}_{<}:\Lambda V^{*}\to\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
is the projection. Hence

\displaystyle\phi\vee\beta^{(1)}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{P}_{<}(\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi\diamond\beta^{(1)}))=\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(1)}+l*\tau(\phi\wedge\beta^{(1)}),

\displaystyle\phi\vee\beta^{(2)}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{P}_{<}(\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi\diamond\beta^{(2)}))=\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(2)}-\phi\triangle_{2}\beta^{(2)}+l*\tau(\phi\wedge\beta^{(2)}),

\displaystyle\phi\vee\beta^{(3)}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{P}_{<}(\mathcal{P}_{l}(\phi\diamond\beta^{(3)}))=-\phi\triangle_{2}\beta^{(3)}-\langle\phi,\beta^{(3)}\rangle+l*\tau(\phi\wedge\beta^{(3)})+l*\tau(\phi\triangle_{1}\beta^{(3)}).

Therefore
(\phi\vee\beta)^{(0)}=-\langle\phi,\beta^{(3)}\rangle
.
∎

To proceed further we need to introduce a basis adapted to the geometry of the problem. Since
h
is of signature
(4,3)
we can always choose a unit-norm element
n\in V^{\ast}
. This element induces a decomposition:

V^{\ast}=V^{\ast}_{n}\oplus\langle n\rangle,

where
V^{\ast}_{n}\subset V^{\ast}
is the orthogonal complement of
n
in
V^{\ast}
. Hence, with the metric induced by
h
, it follows that
V^{\ast}_{n}
becomes a six-dimensional vector space of split signature
(3,3)
. We choose a basis
(\vartheta_{+},\theta_{+},\eta_{+},\vartheta_{-},\theta_{-},\eta_{-})
on
V^{\ast}_{n}
given by isotropic one-forms that are conjugate by pairs, that is, they are orthogonal except for:

\langle\vartheta_{+},\vartheta_{-}\rangle=\langle\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\rangle=\langle\eta_{+},\eta_{-}\rangle=1.

We refer to such
(\vartheta_{+},\theta_{+},\eta_{+},\vartheta_{-},\theta_{-},\eta_{-},n)
as an
isotropic
basis for
(V^{\ast},h^{\ast})
.

Theorem 1.12
.

An exterior form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{<}V^{*}
is the square of an isotropic spinor if and only if
\alpha=\vartheta\wedge\theta\wedge\eta\in\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
for
\vartheta,\theta,\eta\in V^{*}
isotropic and orthogonal.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha=\vartheta\wedge\theta\wedge\eta
for isotropic and orthogonal one-forms
\vartheta,\theta,\eta\in V^{*}
. Set
\vartheta_{+}:=\vartheta
,
\theta_{+}:=\theta
and
\eta_{+}:=\eta
and complete these elements into an isotropic basis
(\vartheta_{+},\theta_{+},\eta_{+},\vartheta_{-},\theta_{-},\eta_{-},n)
. Take
\beta=\vartheta_{-}\wedge\theta_{-}\wedge\eta_{-}
. We have that
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=1
, hence
(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\neq 0
, and a tedious but straightforward computation shows that
\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=-8\alpha
. Thus, we conclude that
\alpha
is the square of an isotropic spinor by Corollary
1.5
. To prove the converse, we assume that there exists no isotropic basis
(\vartheta_{+},\theta_{+},\eta_{+},\vartheta_{-},\theta_{-},\eta_{-},n)
such that
\alpha=\vartheta_{+}\wedge\theta_{+}\wedge\eta_{+}
. Then, we can write:

\alpha=\phi_{0}+\phi^{\perp},

where
\phi_{0}=b\,\vartheta_{+}\wedge\theta_{+}\wedge\eta_{+}
for a constant
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi^{\perp}(\vartheta^{\sharp}_{+},\theta^{\sharp}_{+},\eta^{\sharp}_{+})=0
. Solving now
\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=-8\alpha
with
\beta=\vartheta_{-}\wedge\theta_{-}\wedge\eta_{-}
a long computation gives
\phi^{\perp}=0
and
b^{2}=1
, which gives a contradiction.
∎

The theory of spinorial exterior forms that we have used allows, as exemplified by the previous theorem, to obtain results without relying on any knowledge about the representation theory of the stabilizer of the class of spinors under consideration. The price to pay however is dealing with long algebraic computations within a rich algebraic structure that contains all the necessary information. On the other hand, once the square of a spinor is computed, as in the previous theorem, the result can be typically used to simplify the study of the stabilizer of the corresponding spinor. We proceed to do so in the following for isotropic spinors. Let
u=\vartheta^{\sharp},v=\theta^{\sharp},w=\eta^{\sharp}\in V
be the dual vectors of the one-forms in Theorem
1.12
. Hence, they are isotropic and orthogonal. We can decompose our vector space
V
as
V=U\oplus W
, where
U=\langle u,v,w\rangle
and we complete
(u,v,w)
to an isotropic basis of
V
. That is, we choose a basis
W=\langle u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime},n\rangle
, where
u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime}
are isotropic, orthogonal and conjugate to
(u,v,w)
, namely:

\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle=\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle=\langle w,w^{\prime}\rangle=1

with
n\in V
of unit-norm and orthogonal to
(u,v,w,u^{\prime},v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
. In this basis the metric
h
takes the form

(8)

h=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}_{3}&0\\
\mathbbm{1}_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathbbm{1}_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3
identity matrix. The fact that by Theorem
1.12
we explicitly know the square of an isotropic irreducible spinor together with the fact that the square spinor map is equivariant, allows us to easily compute the Lie algebra of an irreducible isotropic spinor, complementing the characterization given by Kath in
[
33
]
. We proceed to do this in the remainder of this subsection.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
\alpha=\vartheta\wedge\theta\wedge\eta
be the square of an isotropic irreducible spinor
\varepsilon\in\Sigma
. Then the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Stab}(\varepsilon))\subset\mathfrak{so}(4,3)
of the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}(\varepsilon)
of
\varepsilon
in
\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(4,3)
is given by:

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B&v\\
0&-A^{t}&0\\
0&-v^{t}&0\end{pmatrix}\mid A\in\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R}),B\in\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R}),v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\right\}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\ltimes(\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the unique six-dimensional two-step nilpotent Lie algebra with three-dimensional derived subalgebra.

Proof.

Let us consider

M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B&v\\
C&D&u\\
x^{t}&y^{t}&r\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{gl}(7,\mathbb{R}),

where
A,B,C,D\in\mathfrak{gl}(3,\mathbb{R})
,
v,u,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
M
must belong to
\mathfrak{so}(V,h)\cong\mathfrak{so}(4,3)
, for
h
as in (
8
), we get that
B,C\in\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R})
,
D=-A^{t}
,
x=-u
,
y=-v
and
r=0
. Moreover, since
M
must preserve
\alpha
, i.e.
M^{*}\alpha=\alpha
, then we get that
M(U)\subset U
(where we have the decomposition
V=U\oplus W
), thus
C=0
,
u=0
and
A\in\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3,\mathbb{R})
can be identified, as a vector space, with
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In fact, we have that
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an abelian Lie algebra and the only non-zero brackets of
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}
are given by:

\left[\begin{pmatrix}0&0&v_{1}\\
0&0&0\\
0&-v_{1}^{t}&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&0&v_{2}\\
0&0&0\\
0&-v_{2}^{t}&0\end{pmatrix}\right]=\begin{pmatrix}0&-v_{1}\otimes v_{2}^{t}+v_{2}\otimes v_{1}^{t}&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3},

from where one can see that
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}
is two-step nilpotent and has three-dimensional derived subalgebra, which uniquely characterizes it.
∎

This result was already obtained in
[
33
, Corollary 2.1]
in a different way, and it is complemented here by explicitly obtaining that the six-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra is
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which corresponds to
(0,0,0,12,13,23)
in the notation of Salamon
[
48
]
.

2.
Parallel spinors on
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-manifolds

In this section, we extend the algebraic theory of spinors and exterior forms of Section
1
to bundles of real irreducible Clifford modules equipped with an arbitrary connection and an admissible bilinear pairing. This allows us to define and study parallel spinors on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds of signature
(4,3)
.

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
(M,g)
be a connected and oriented pseudo-Riemannian manifold of signature
(4,3)
. We denote by
\mathrm{Cl}(M,g)
the bundle of real Clifford algebras of the cotangent bundle
(T^{*}M,g^{*})
, which is modeled on the real Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(4,3)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
bundle of real Clifford modules
on
(M,g)
is a pair
(S,\Gamma)
, where
S
is a real vector bundle and
\Gamma:\mathrm{Cl}(M,g)\to\mathrm{End}(S)
is a unital morphism of bundles of unital and associative real algebras.

For each point
m\in M
the unital morphism of associative algebras
\Gamma_{m}:\mathrm{Cl}(T^{*}_{m}M,g^{*}_{m})\to\mathrm{End}(S_{m})
defines a Clifford module denoted by
(S_{m},\Gamma_{m})
. There exists a Clifford module
(\Sigma,\gamma)
, unique up to isomorphism in the category of
unbased
Clifford algebras
[
41
,
40
]
, such that
(S_{m},\Gamma_{m})\cong(\Sigma,\gamma)
for every
m\in M
. We say then that
(S,\Gamma)
is a bundle of real Clifford modules of
type

(\Sigma,\gamma)
.

Definition 2.2
.

•

A
paired spinor bundle of type
(\Sigma,\gamma)
is a triple
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
consisting of a bundle of real Clifford modules
(S,\Gamma)
of type
(\Sigma,\gamma)
equipped with an admissible bilinear pairing

Remark 2.3
.

As stated in the previous definition, we have chosen the term
parallel spinor
to refer to a spinor parallel under a general connection on the spinor bundle. Given the plethora of names, such as parallel spinors, Killing spinors, Codazzi spinors, Cauchy spinors, skew-torsion parallel spinors, skew-Killing spinors, or generalized Killing spinors, used in the literature to refer to spinors parallel under various connections, we concluded it was necessary to coin a simple and intuitive term to refer to a spinor parallel under any given connection on the spinor. Although the term
parallel spinor
with no further decorations usually refers to a spinor parallel under the Levi-Civita connection, we felt it was unnecessary to introduce more complicated terminology.

Remark 2.4
.

It is guaranteed that an admissible bilinear pairing
\Scr B

if
(M,g)
is

admits a reduction to the identity component of the spin group.

(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
admits a parallel spinor of type
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
relative to
\mathcal{A}
with given dequantization
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)

(10)

\nabla\alpha=\mathfrak{a}\vee\alpha+\alpha\vee(\pi\circ\tau)(\mathfrak{a})

and either the following algebraic equations are satisfied for every exterior form

:

(\pi\circ\tau)(\alpha)=\alpha,\quad\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=8(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\alpha

or, equivalently, the following equations:

\alpha\vee\alpha=8\alpha^{(0)}\alpha,\quad(\pi\circ\tau)(\alpha)=\alpha,\quad\alpha\vee\beta\vee\alpha=8(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\alpha

are satisfied for some fixed exterior form
\beta\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
such that
(\alpha\vee\beta)^{(0)}\neq 0
.

Now we consider the most general parallel spinor on a strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
, obtaining a general differential system for its associated spinorial exterior form. We know by Proposition
1.6
that an exterior form
\alpha

\alpha=f+\phi
for

to characterize when

corresponds to a parallel spinor relative to
\mathcal{A}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{End}(S))
with dequantization
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

A strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
admits a parallel spinor of type
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
if and only if the following system is satisfied for every
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
:

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}f

\displaystyle=2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}-2\langle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)},\phi\rangle,

(12)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\phi

\displaystyle=2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}+2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}\phi+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)-2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\phi+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}\phi),

where
f+\phi\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\Gamma})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
for some irreducible paired spinor bundle
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
on
(M,g)
.

Proof.

For every vector field
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
we have
\mathfrak{a}_{v}=\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(j)}\in\Omega^{j}(M)
. The right-hand side of Equation (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{v}\vee\alpha+\alpha\vee(\pi\circ\tau)(\mathfrak{a}_{v})

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)})\vee(f+\phi)+(f+\phi)\vee(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}-\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}-\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}+\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)})

\displaystyle=2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}+2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}\phi

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}\vee\phi-\phi\vee\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)})+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\vee\phi-\phi\vee\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)})+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\vee\phi+\phi\vee\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)})

\displaystyle=2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+2f\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}+2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}\phi

\displaystyle\quad+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)-2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\phi+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}\phi)-2\langle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)},\phi\rangle.

Comparing degrees we find that Equation (
10
) becomes the system of the statement.
∎

2.2.
Parallel spinors and
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-structures

Given a spinor
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
of non-zero norm we can decompose
S=\langle\varepsilon\rangle\oplus\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{\perp}
where
\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{\perp}\subset S
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\varepsilon
with respect to
\Scr B
. We have the isomorphism:

\langle\varepsilon\rangle^{\perp}\cong\{v\cdot\varepsilon\mid v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\},

see Remark
1.2
. This implies in particular that for every
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, the spinor
\nabla_{v}\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
can be written as:

\nabla_{v}\,\varepsilon=\theta(v)\varepsilon+A(v)\cdot\varepsilon

for some
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
A\in\mathrm{End}(TM)
. Hence, for a parallel spinor
\varepsilon
relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
, it must be possible to find an element
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
with
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}^{(2)}=\bar{\mathfrak{a}}^{(3)}=0
and relative to which
\varepsilon
is again a parallel spinor. This is verified in the following result, which characterizes parallel spinors of nowhere vanishing and possibly non-constant norm.

Theorem 2.7
.

A strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
admits a nowhere isotropic parallel spinor
\varepsilon
of type
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
if and only if there exists a nowhere vanishing three-form
\phi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
satisfying the algebraic equation:

(13)

6\,\kappa\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}\,\phi=l*(\phi\triangle_{1}\phi)\,,\qquad P_{(27)}(\mathfrak{a}_{v})=0\,,\qquad\forall\,\,v\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

together with the differential system:

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\phi

\displaystyle=2\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(0)}\phi+2l*(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)\,,\qquad\forall\,\,v\in\mathfrak{X}(M),

where:

\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+7f\kappa_{v}^{(0)}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}+3f\sigma_{v}^{(1)}+4lf\kappa_{v}^{(1)}

and where
P_{(27)}\colon\Omega^{3}(M)\to\Omega^{3}_{27}(M)
is the canonical projection with respect to the
\mathrm{G}_{2}^{\ast}
-structure defined by
\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.8
.

This result is closely related to the spinorial description of Riemannian
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures given in
[
1
]
. Aside from the difference in signature, Theorem
2.7
diverges from the description given in Op. Cit. in that we do not consider the spinor to be of constant norm, we consider a general parallel spinor, which leads to the obstruction appearing in the second equation of (
13
), and our result includes the necessary and sufficient algebraic condition contained in the first equation of (
13
).

Proof.

By Theorem
1.7
and Proposition
2.6
,
(M,g)
admits a nowhere isotropic parallel spinor relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
if and only if it admits a three-form
\phi
satisfying the algebraic relation (
13
) as well as the differential system given by (
11
) and (
12
) with:

f=\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}.

Since
\varepsilon
is nowhere isotropic, it defines a
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
-structure respect to which we consider the decomposition of
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}
into irreducible components, that is:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\iota_{(\sigma_{v}^{(1)})^{\sharp}}\phi+\sigma_{v}^{14}\in\Omega^{2}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{v}^{(0)}\phi+*(\kappa_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)+\kappa_{v}^{27}\in\Omega^{3}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27}.

Using Lemma
1.10
and isolating the terms that belong to the same irreducible
\mathrm{G}^{\ast}_{2}
component, we obtain:

\nabla_{v}\phi=-4f\kappa_{v}^{27}+2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}\phi+14f\kappa_{v}^{(0)}\phi+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)+6lf*(\sigma_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi)+8f*(\kappa_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi).

Hence
\kappa_{v}^{(27)}=0
,
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}+7f\kappa_{v}^{(0)}
and
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}+3f\sigma_{v}^{(1)}+4lf\kappa_{v}^{(1)}
. This shows that, given
\kappa_{v}^{(27)}=0
, the differential system given in (
12
) is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}f

\displaystyle=2f\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(0)},

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\phi

\displaystyle=2\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(0)}\phi+2l*(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\phi).

Setting
f=\sqrt{-\frac{\langle\phi,\phi\rangle}{7}}
as required by Theorem
1.7
, it follows that the first equation is a consequence of the second and therefore we conclude.
∎

By the previous theorem, we immediately obtain the following

Let

.

It is well-known that the metric induced by the three-form

\mathrm{G}_{2}^{*}
if

(see e.g.
[

). Theorem

shows that this can happen for parallel spinors relative to non-trivial

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a pseudo-unit parallel spinor on
(M,g)
relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{\ast}M)
. Then, the

2.3.
Isotropic parallel spinors

It follows from Theorem
1.12
that a nowhere vanishing exterior form
\alpha\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{<}T^{\ast}M)
is the square of an isotropic spinor if and only if there exists a good open cover
\left\{U_{a}\right\}_{a\in I}
and an associated family of orthogonal and isotropic triples of one-forms
\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}_{a\in I}
such that:

\alpha|_{U_{a}}=\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}.

This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.11
.

An
isotropic family of triples of one-forms
on a good open cover
\left\{U_{a}\right\}_{a\in I}
is a family of nowhere vanishing orthogonal and isotropic triples of one-forms
\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}_{a\in I}
with
\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\in\Omega^{1}(U_{a})
. Such family is
coherent
if:

(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})|_{U_{ab}}=(\vartheta_{b}\wedge\theta_{b}\wedge\eta_{b})|_{U_{ab}}

on every non-trivial overlap
U_{ab}:=U_{a}\cap U_{b}
.

Clearly, a coherent family of triples of one-forms always gives rise to a well-defined global three-form on
M
, which we will denote generically by
\phi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. Hence, as a consequence of Theorem
1.12
, the existence of isotropic spinors can be reformulated in the following terms.

Corollary 2.12
.

A strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
admits a nowhere vanishing isotropic spinor if and only if it admits an isotropic and coherent family of triples of one-forms.

Proof.

The result follows from the standard fact that a smooth form is pointwise decomposable, as we know by Theorem
1.12
the square of an isotropic spinor is, if and only if is locally decomposable in terms of local smooth one-forms.
∎

Every coherent family defines an orientable vector subbundle
W\subset T^{\ast}M
with volume form induced by
\phi
. This rank-three vector bundle
W
is topologically trivial if and only if
\phi
can be globally written as
\phi=\vartheta\wedge\theta\wedge\eta
in terms of three isotropic and mutually orthogonal one-forms
\vartheta,\theta,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
(M,g)
be a strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold of signature
(4,3)
. Then it admits an isotropic parallel spinor of type
(\Sigma,\gamma_{l})
relative to
\mathfrak{a}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{<}T^{\ast}M)
if and only if it admits an isotropic and coherent family
\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}
of triples of one-forms relative to some good open cover
\left\{U_{a}\right\}_{a\in I}
and such that

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)},\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(0)}\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+2l*(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(1)}\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})

\displaystyle\quad-2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\vartheta_{a})\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\theta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\eta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}

\displaystyle\quad+2l*((\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}\vartheta_{a})\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}\theta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}\eta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a})

for all
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Proof.

This is an application of Proposition
2.6
. It only remains to prove the new expressions for
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})
and
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})
. First we compute

\iota_{e_{i}}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})=\vartheta_{a}(e_{i})\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-\theta_{a}(e_{i})\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+\eta_{a}(e_{i})\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})

\displaystyle=\iota_{e_{i}}\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\wedge\iota_{e_{i}}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}(e_{i})\vartheta_{a}(e_{i}))\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}(e_{i})\theta_{a}(e_{i}))\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}(e_{i})\eta_{a}(e_{i}))\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\vartheta_{a})\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\theta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\eta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}.

To obtain
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(3)}\triangle_{1}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})
we proceed in an analogous way and hence we conclude.
∎

As an application, we apply the previous general result to spinors parallel under a metric connection
\nabla^{g,A}
with
contorsion

A\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M)
. Such a connection can always be written as follows by definition:

\nabla^{g,A}=\nabla^{g}+A

in terms of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
of the underlying metric
g
and the
contorsion tensor

A\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M)
of the connection. Since
(M,g)
is assumed to be strongly spin and
\nabla^{g,A}
is metric, it lifts to any irreducible paired spinor bundle
(S,\Gamma,\Scr B)
on
(M,g)
, which we denote by the same symbol for ease of notation. Spinors
\varepsilon\in\Gamma(S)
satisfying:

\nabla^{g,A}\varepsilon=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\nabla^{g}_{v}\varepsilon=-\Psi^{<}_{\Gamma}(A_{v})(\varepsilon)\,,\quad\forall\,\,v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

are called
torsion parallel spinors
and define a particular class of parallel spinors on
(M,g)
relative to data
\mathfrak{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\Lambda^{<}T^{*}M)
satisfying
\mathfrak{a}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{a}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{a}^{(3)}=0
.

Corollary 2.14
.

A strongly spin pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of signature
(4,3)
admits a torsion parallel spinor with contorsion
A\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M)
if and only if it admits an isotropic and coherent family of triples of one-forms
\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}
preserved by
\nabla^{g,A}
.

Remark 2.15
.

We can think of this corollary as an extension of
[
34
, Proposition 8.7]
in signature
(4,3)
to the case of a metric connection with torsion, without the need to impose the underlying manifold to be simply connected.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2.13
to the case where
\mathfrak{a}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{a}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{a}^{(3)}=0
we obtain:

(14)

\nabla^{g}_{v}(\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a})=-2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\vartheta_{a})\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}+2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\theta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}-2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\eta_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}.

Let
\alpha_{a}:=\vartheta_{a}\wedge\theta_{a}\wedge\eta_{a}
. Note that
0=\nabla^{g}_{v}(\alpha_{a}\wedge\vartheta_{a})=(\nabla^{g}_{v}\alpha_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}+\alpha_{a}\wedge(\nabla^{g}_{v}\vartheta_{a})
. Multiplying both sides of (
14
) by
\vartheta_{a}
we obtain
(\nabla^{g}_{v}\alpha_{a})\wedge\vartheta_{a}=-2(\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}\triangle_{1}\vartheta_{a})\wedge\alpha_{a}=-2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}(\vartheta_{a}^{\sharp})\wedge\alpha_{a}
. Hence:

\nabla^{g}_{v}\vartheta_{a}=-2\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{(2)}(\vartheta_{a}^{\sharp})+\zeta_{v}^{\vartheta_{a}},

where
\zeta_{v}^{\vartheta_{a}}
is a one-form such that
\zeta_{v}^{\vartheta_{a}}\wedge\alpha_{a}=0
for all
v\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, thus
\zeta_{v}^{\vartheta_{a}}
is in the span of
\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}
. Set
A:=2\mathfrak{a}^{(2)}
. Then:

\nabla^{g,A}_{v}\vartheta_{a}=\nabla^{g}_{v}\vartheta_{a}+A_{v}(\vartheta_{a}^{\sharp})=\zeta_{v}^{\vartheta_{a}}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}.

Similarly, we get:

\displaystyle\nabla^{g,A}_{v}\theta_{a}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{g}_{v}\theta_{a}+A_{v}(\theta_{a}^{\sharp})=\zeta_{v}^{\theta_{a}}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\},

\displaystyle\nabla^{g,A}_{v}\eta_{a}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{g}_{v}\eta_{a}+A_{v}(\eta_{a}^{\sharp})=\zeta_{v}^{\eta_{a}}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\vartheta_{a},\theta_{a},\eta_{a}\}

and thus we conclude.
∎

Note that the contorsion tensor
A\in\Gamma(T^{\ast}M\otimes\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M)
of a metric connection
\nabla^{g,A}
is related to its torsion
T\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M\otimes T^{*}M)
by the formula
T(v_{1},v_{2})=A(v_{1},v_{2})-A(v_{2},v_{1})
, where
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. If
\nabla^{g,A}
has totally skew-symmetric torsion, i.e.
T\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, then
2A=T
. As a particular case of the previous corollary, we can take
\nabla^{g,A}
to be the Levi-Civita connection by setting
A=0
. In this case, we have a
degenerate
analog of quaternionic Kähler geometry, but instead of having a bundle of non-degenerate local two-forms, we have a bundle of isotropic and orthogonal local one-forms.

2.4.
Metrics admitting isotropic parallel spinors

Corollary
2.14
can be immediately applied to construct explicit examples of metrics
g
of signature
(4,3)
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
admitting irreducible isotropic real spinors parallel with respect to a metric connection with torsion. Consider
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with standard coordinates
(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2},x_{3},y_{3},z)
. Inspired by the structure of three-dimensional Lorentzian manifolds admitting a special isotropic vector field, more specifically the so-called Kundt three-manifolds
[
8
]
, we propose the following ansatz for a metric of signature
(4,3)
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
:

(15)

g=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(H_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{i}+e^{K_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\odot\mathrm{d}y_{i})+e^{F}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

for arbitrary functions
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3},K_{1},K_{2},K_{3},F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{7})
. Roughly speaking, each factor of the form
H_{i}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{i}+e^{K_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\odot\mathrm{d}y_{i}
occurs naturally in the local form of a Lorentzian metric admitting a parallel vector field of the form
\partial_{y_{i}}
and gives a contribution of signature
(1,1)
to the Lorentzian metric, which thus has a positive definite
transverse
part. Hence, summing three of these factors plus a
transverse
element of the form
e^{F}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z
we obtain a metric of signature
(4,3)
which is naturally equipped with three coordinate vector fields which are all isotropic and mutually orthogonal. Metric (
15
) also provides a natural generalization of the most general local metric admitting a parallel (under Levi-Civita) isotropic real spinor, which was obtained in
[
34
, Theorem 8.2]
, see also
[
10
]
. As candidates for the metric dual of the triple of one-forms as described in Corollary
2.14
, we set:

\vartheta^{\sharp_{g}}:=\partial_{y_{1}}\,,\qquad\theta^{\sharp_{g}}:=\partial_{y_{2}}\,,\qquad\eta^{\sharp_{g}}:=\partial_{y_{3}}

and thus:

(16)

\vartheta=e^{K_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,,\qquad\theta=e^{K_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,,\qquad\eta=e^{K_{3}}\mathrm{d}x_{3}.

Let
\nabla^{g,A}
be a metric connection with contorsion
A
, and let
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}^{7}\to\mathbb{R}^{8}
be a spinor whose associated exterior form is
\phi=\vartheta\wedge\theta\wedge\eta
as described above. Then, by Corollary
2.14
it follows that
\nabla^{g,A}\varepsilon=0
if and only if:

(17)

\displaystyle\nabla^{g,A}_{w}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\in\mathrm{Span}_{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{7})}\{\mathrm{d}x_{1},\mathrm{d}x_{2},\mathrm{d}x_{3}\}\,,\qquad i=1,2,3

for every
w\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{7})
. We have:

(\nabla^{g,A}_{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(w_{2})=(\nabla^{g}_{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(w_{2})+e^{-K_{i}}A(w_{1},\partial_{y_{i}},w_{2})

and therefore this equation is equivalent to:

(18)

(\nabla^{g}_{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(\partial_{z})+e^{-K_{i}}A(w_{1},\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{z})=0\,,\quad(\nabla^{g}_{w_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(\partial_{y_{j}})+e^{-K_{i}}A(w_{1},\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}})=0\,,\quad i,j=1,2,3.

A computation gives:

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}x_{i}

\displaystyle=e^{K_{i}}\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{i}-\sum_{j}\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\odot\mathrm{d}x_{j}-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\odot\mathrm{d}y_{j}-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial z}\mathrm{d}x_{i}\odot\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-K_{i}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{j}+e^{-K_{i}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\partial e^{K_{j}}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\odot\mathrm{d}y_{j}+e^{-K_{i}}\frac{\partial e^{F}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

for every fixed
i=1,2,3
(no summation is understood for the repeated index
i
). Hence:

2(\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(\partial_{y_{j}})=(1-\delta_{ij})(e^{-K_{i}}\frac{\partial e^{K_{j}}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{j}-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})\,,\quad 2(\nabla^{g}\mathrm{d}x_{i})(\partial_{z})=-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial z}\mathrm{d}x_{i}+e^{-K_{i}}\frac{\partial e^{F}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}z.

Comparing with Equation (
18
), we conclude that
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the metric (
15
) admits a parallel irreducible isotropic spinor with associated triple of one-forms (
16
) if and only if:

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial z}\mathrm{d}x_{i}+e^{-K_{i}}\frac{\partial e^{F}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}z+\frac{1}{2}e^{-K_{i}}A(-,\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{z})=0,

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{ij})(e^{-K_{i}}\frac{\partial e^{K_{j}}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{j}-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}\mathrm{d}x_{i})+\frac{1}{2}e^{-K_{i}}A(-,\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}})=0

for every
i,j=1,2,3
. Equivalently:

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}A(-,\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{z})=\frac{\partial e^{K_{i}}}{\partial z}\mathrm{d}x_{i}-\frac{\partial e^{F}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}z\,,\qquad\frac{1}{2}A(-,\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}})=\frac{\partial e^{K_{i}}}{\partial y_{j}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}-\frac{\partial e^{K_{j}}}{\partial y_{i}}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\,,\quad i\neq j.

Rather than as differential equations for the coefficients of
g
, these conditions can be understood as determining certain components of
A
to guarantee that there exists an irreducible isotropic spinor
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}^{7}\to\mathbb{R}^{8}
such that
\nabla^{g,A}\varepsilon=0
. Hence, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
given in (
15
). Then, an irreducible isotropic spinor parallel with respect to
\nabla^{g,A}
exists if
A
satisfies (
19
).

This way we obtain plenty of metrics admitting parallel isotropic spinors for appropriate choices of contorsion. A direct inspection of Equation (
19
) reveals the following result.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
given in (
15
) and let
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}^{7}\to\mathbb{R}^{8}
be an isotropic and irreducible spinor parallel with respect to
\nabla^{g,A}
and whose associated triple of one-forms is (
16
). Then:

A(\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}},\partial_{z})=0\,,\qquad A(\partial_{y_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}},\partial_{y_{k}})=0

for every
i,j,k=1,2,3
.

Hence, the contorsion of parallel spinors on
(\mathbb{R}^{7},g)
with an associated triple of one-forms (
16
) is restricted and in particular, there are plenty of contorsion tensors for which no such parallel spinor exists. For the Levi-Civita connection, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
given in (
15
) and such that
K_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, and
F
depend only on the coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. Then
(\mathbb{R}^{7},g)
admits an irreducible isotropic spinor parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection.

Remark 2.19
.

Note that in the previous corollary the functions
H_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, are allowed to be arbitrary.

In the set-up of Corollary
2.18
the metric
g
is generically not flat, not Ricci-flat, and not scalar-flat. In fact, a computation reveals that it is scalar-flat if and only if:

e^{-2K_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}H_{1}}{\partial y_{1}^{2}}+e^{-2K_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}H_{2}}{\partial y_{2}^{2}}+e^{-2K_{3}}\frac{\partial^{2}H_{3}}{\partial y_{3}^{2}}=0.

To the best of our knowledge, these are the first explicit examples of metrics admitting a torsion parallel irreducible isotropic spinor.

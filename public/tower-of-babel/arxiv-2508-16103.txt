Nonlocal Harnack inequality in a disconnected region
Se-Chan Lee

Abstract
. We establish a Harnack inequality for weak solutions of nonlocal equations in a disconnected region. The inequality compares the value of a solution on one connected component with its value on another, capturing a purely nonlocal phenomenon with no local analogue. We provide two different approaches: one based on the localized maximum principle and another on the Poisson kernel estimates.

1.
Introduction

The classical Harnack inequality for harmonic functions can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u:B_{2r}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative harmonic function. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n)
such that

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u.

Although the proof of this inequality for harmonic functions is quite immediate from the Poisson integral formula, it implies significant consequences in the regularity theory; Liouville theorem, removable singularity theorem and Hölder regularity of harmonic functions, just to name a few. Moreover, the Harnack inequality remains valid for solutions of a wide class of partial differential equations, not only linear equations with measurable uniformly elliptic coefficients, but also quasilinear equations involving
p
-Laplacian. For divergence form operators, the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory
[
16
,
44
,
42
,
43
]
provides a very flexible tool for studying the local behaviors of solutions such as local boundedness, weak Harnack inequality and Harnack inequality. In short, this theory is based on the iteration of Caccioppoli inequalities derived from choosing suitable test functions. We also refer to Serrin
[
45
]
and Trudinger
[
46
]
for quasilinear equations involving
p
-Laplace equations. On the other hand, Krylov–Safonov
[
37
,
38
]
developed a priori estimates (the so-called expansion of positivity) for both elliptic and parabolic operators in nondivergence form; see also the paper by Caffarelli
[
10
]
for a similar result in the fully nonlinear setting. We finally refer to the monograph by Kassmann
[
23
]
for further introduction and historical background of the Harnack inequality.

We now move our attention to the nonlocal Harnack inequality. For the fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
, the nonlocal counterpart of
Theorem

1.1
can be found in the book by Landkof
[
39
]
under the assumption that
u
is nonnegative in the whole Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The necessity of the global nonnegative condition on
u
was pointed out by Kassmann
[
22
]
. In fact, in order to replace the global nonnegative condition with the nonnegative assumption only in a ball
B_{2r}(x_{0})
, one needs to introduce the nonlocal tail term
\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},r)
that encodes the long-range interaction of
u
; see
Theorem

2.3
for the precise statement. The nonlocal De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory has been extensively studied by many authors to establish the nonlocal Harnack inequality and local Hölder regularity. We refer to Bass–Kassmann
[
1
,
2
]
and Kassmann
[
24
,
25
]
for linear nonlocal operators in divergence form, Di Castro–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
17
,
18
]
and Cozzi
[
15
]
for nonlinear nonlocal operators in divergence form, and Caffarelli–Silvestre
[
8
,
9
]
for fully nonlinear nonlocal operators in nondivergence form. See also
[
7
,
11
,
21
,
27
,
29
,
40
]
for Harnack inequality and related results in different settings.

The goal of this paper is to establish the nonlocal Harnack inequality in a
disconnected region
, which becomes possible due to the nonlocal nature of equations. To illustrate the issue, we first consider the corresponding situation in the local case. Let
u
be harmonic in
B_{2r}(x_{1})\cup B_{2r}(x_{2})
, where
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and
B_{2r}(x_{2})
are two disjoint balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, suppose that
u
is nonnegative in a large ball containing both
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and
B_{2r}(x_{2})
. The classical Harnack inequality (
Theorem

1.1
) says that the ratio
u(y_{1})/u(y_{2})
is bounded above and below by positive universal constants, whenever two points
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are contained in the same ball (either
B_{r}(x_{1})
or
B_{r}(x_{2})
). However, this cannot happen when one chooses
y_{1}\in B_{r}(x_{1})
and
y_{2}\in B_{r}(x_{2})
; just consider
u\equiv M
in
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and
u\equiv 1
in
B_{2r}(x_{2})
for any
M\geq 0
. In other words, the information on one connected component cannot be transported to another.

Surprisingly, the ratio between two values of a solution
u
in disjoint balls admits a universal bound when it comes to the nonlocal equation. To be precise, let
u
be a weak solution of linear nonlocal equations in divergence form given by

(1.1)

0=\mathcal{L}u(x)\coloneqq 2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-u(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where the kernel
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
satisfies a symmetry and uniform ellipticity condition of the form

\Lambda^{-1}|x-y|^{-n-2s}\leq k(x,y)=k(y,x)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s}

for some
s\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda\geq 1
. In particular, the choice
k(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-2s}
(up to a normalization constant) corresponds to the fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
. Moreover, we fix notation on a disconnected region consisting of two disjoint balls throughout the paper. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be points such that
4r\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq 8r
. Suppose that
B_{2r}(x_{1})\cup B_{2r}(x_{2})\subset B_{R/2}=B_{R/2}(0)
. In particular, these assumptions imply that
B_{2r}(x_{1})\cap B_{2r}(x_{2})=\varnothing
and that
|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq R/2
.

The following Harnack inequality in a disconnected region is the main theorem of this paper. See
Section

2
for the definition of the nonlocal tail,
\mathrm{Tail}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Harnack inequality in a disconnected region)
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
B_{2r}(x_{1})\cup B_{2r}(x_{2})
and
u
is nonnegative in
B_{R}
. Suppose that either

(i)

s\in(0,1)
and
k
is translation invariant, i.e., there exists a function
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
such that

k(x,y)=K(x-y)\quad\text{for any $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$};\quad\text{or}

(ii)

s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{1})}u+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R),

where
u_{-}\coloneqq\max\{-u,0\}
denotes the negative part of
u
.

As we observed in the earlier example under the local situation,
Theorem

1.2
is a purely nonlocal phenomenon: the nonlocal nature of operators ensures the transfer of information despite the region being disconnected. In particular, we show that
Theorem

1.2
is non-robust in the sense that the universal constant
C
blows up when
s\to 1^{-}
; see
Remark

3.5
and
Remark

4.7
for details. Moreover, the validity of
Theorem

1.2
in nonlinear nonlocal equations, including
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
for
p\in(1,\infty)
, can be an interesting problem.

We present two independent approaches to prove
Theorem

1.2
depending on the conditions (i) and (ii). The first approach relies on the construction of barriers and the application of the localized maximum principle, under the condition (i). We suggest two simple barrier functions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

3.2
so that a certain linear combination of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
becomes a weak subsolution of (
1.1
) with an appropriate profile. The translation invariant condition in (i) is necessary only in
Lemma

3.2
, where the finiteness of a certain integral term follows from the control of
\delta(w_{2};x,y)\coloneqq 2w_{2}(x)-w_{2}(x+y)-w_{2}(x-y)
; see
[
8
]
for instance. At this stage, the standard comparison principle (Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
36
, Lemma 6]
for instance) between
u
and barrier functions is not directly applicable, because we do not impose the nonnegativity of
u
in the exterior domain
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
. Instead, we utilize a version of the localized maximum principle (
Lemma

3.4
) that is similar to Kim–Lee
[
32
, Theorem 1.7]
.

In the second approach, we employ a representation formula for a solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem, under the condition (ii). In particular, the solution
u
can be represented as the Poisson integral of its exterior data with the associated Poisson kernel
P
. Then we can capture the long-range interaction of a solution
u
between the two disjoint balls. While the fractional Laplacian case
(-\Delta)^{s}
can be rather easily treated due to the explicit formula of the Poisson kernel, an additional effort is necessary to obtain the Poisson kernel estimates for general operators
\mathcal{L}
. For operators
\mathcal{L}
without the translation invariant assumption, Kim–Weidner
[
33
]
recently obtained the two-sided Poisson kernel estimates; see
Theorem

4.1
for the precise statement. Although their theorem provides sharp estimates for
P
in a very general situation, it requires an additional regularity assumption on
k
, in order to describe the boundary behavior of
P
. Moreover, since it deals with a general
C^{1,\alpha}
domain
\Omega
, the dependence of a constant
c
on
\Omega
is not explicit.

We point out that interior estimates of
P
in a ball
B_{r}(x_{1})
are sufficient for the purpose of this paper. Therefore, we develop a rather simple version of
Theorem

4.1
; we derive the two-sided interior Poisson kernel estimates in
B_{r}(x_{1})
by tracking down the dependence of the constant
c
on
B_{r}
, without the Hölder regularity condition on
k
. On the other hand, the condition (ii)
2s<n
in
Theorem

1.2
seems quite essential in view of the Green function estimates by Kassmann–Kim–Lee
[
26
]
. In fact, the behavior of the Green function is different depending on the relation between
n
and
2s
. To the best of the author’s knowledge, the corresponding result of
[
26
]
for the case
n\leq 2s
is not available in the literature. We also refer to
[
6
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
20
,
34
]
for the Green function and the Poisson kernel estimates in different settings.

Let us end with an immediate corollary of
Theorem

1.2
: the Harnack inequality in an annulus, i.e., two disjoint intervals, when
n=1
. Such a consequence can be applied to extend the Liouville and Bôcher theorems presented in
[
32
]
for
n=1
. Note that for
n\geq 2
, one can iterate the Harnack inequality in a ball, together with the standard covering argument, to obtain the Harnack inequality in an annulus
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
; see
Theorem

2.4
. Nevertheless, the covering argument does not work in the special case
n=1
, since
(-R,R)\setminus\{0\}
is no longer connected.

Corollary 1.3

(Harnack inequality in disjoint intervals)
.

Let
n=1
and let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
(-R,R)\setminus\{0\}
such that
u\geq 0
in
(-R,R)
. Suppose that either

(i)

s\in(0,1)
and
k
is translation invariant; or

(ii)

s\in(0,1/2)
.

Then for any
r\leq R/8
, we have

\sup_{(-3r,-r)}u\leq C\inf_{(r,3r)}u+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R),

where
C=C(s,\Lambda)>0
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we collect several definitions and well-known results concerning function spaces and weak solutions of nonlocal equations.
Section

3
and
Section

4
are devoted to the two different proofs of
Theorem

1.2
via the localized maximum principle with barriers and the representation formula with Poisson kernel estimates, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

We summarize several definitions of function spaces and weak solutions of nonlocal equations, and recall the standard Harnack inequality, which will be used throughout the paper.

For
\Omega
being a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,2}(\Omega)
consists of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
whose fractional Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

is finite. By
W^{s,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
we denote the space of functions
u
such that
u\in W^{s,2}(G)
for every open
G\Subset\Omega
. We refer the reader to Di Nezza–Palatucci–Valdinoci
[
19
]
for further properties of these spaces.

Since we are concerned with nonlocal equations, we also need the
tail space

L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\coloneqq\left\{u~\text{measurable}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty\right\};

see Kassmann
[
25
]
and Di Castro–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
18
]
. Note that
u\in L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the
nonlocal tail
(or
tail
for short)

(2.1)

\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)\coloneqq r^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.

We next recall the definitions of a weak solution of (
1.1
), or more generally
\mathcal{L}u=f
, and of an
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function. To this end, we define, for measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty]
, the bilinear form

\mathcal{E}(u,v)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

provided that the integral is finite.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. A function
u\in W^{s,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
weak supersolution
(resp.
weak subsolution
) of
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\Omega
if

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)\geq\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x~\left(\text{resp.}\leq\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

for all nonnegative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. A function
u\in W^{s,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
weak solution
of
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\Omega
if (
2.2
) holds for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. A function
u
is
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic
in
\Omega
if it is a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
and
u\in C(\Omega)
. In particular, if
u
is a continuous weak solution of
(-\Delta)^{s}u=0
, then we say that
u
is
s
-harmonic
.

It is useful to deal with a larger class of test functions than
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
as follows; see also
[
32
, Section 2]
and
[
3
, Section 2]
for more comments on the test function classes.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
31
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

A function
u\in W^{s,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution (resp. weak subsolution) of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if and only if (
2.2
) holds for all nonnegative
\varphi\in W^{s,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
\supp{\varphi}\Subset\Omega
.

We end this section with Harnack inequalities for weak solutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in a ball when
n\geq 1
or in an annulus when
n\geq 2
; we again point out that both domains are clearly
connected
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then for any
r\leq R/2
,

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u+C\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},r),

where
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
31
, Theorem 3.9]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then for any
r\leq R/2
,

\sup_{B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}}{u}\leq C\inf_{B_{r}\setminus B_{r/2}}{u}+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R),

where
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
.

3.
Proof 1: localized maximum principle

In this section, we construct barrier functions and employ the localized maximum principle introduced in
[
32
]
to prove
Theorem

1.2
when
k
is translation invariant. It is noteworthy that the extra translation invariant condition is necessary only in the construction of the second barrier function in
Lemma

3.2
.

We begin with the construction of two appropriate subsolutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{r}(x_{1})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Barrier 1)
.

Let
w_{1}=\chi_{B_{r}(x_{2})}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

\mathcal{L}w_{1}\leq-Cr^{-2s}\quad\text{in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

Proof.

For
x\in B_{r}(x_{1})
, the pointwise value
\mathcal{L}w_{1}(x)
can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}w_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(w_{1}(x)-w_{1}(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-2\int_{B_{r}(x_{2})}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq-2\Lambda^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x_{2})}|x-y|^{-n-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y.

It follows from

|x-y|\leq|x-x_{1}|+|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|x_{2}-y|\leq 10r\quad\text{for $x\in B_{r}(x_{1})$ and $y\in B_{r}(x_{2})$}

that

\mathcal{L}w_{1}(x)\leq-Cr^{-2s}

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Barrier 2)
.

Let
w_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{1}))
be a cut-off function such that
w_{2}\equiv 1
on
B_{r/2}(x_{1})
,
0\leq w_{2}\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\|D^{2}w_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq c(n)r^{-2}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

\mathcal{L}w_{2}\leq Cr^{-2s}\quad\text{in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of
Lemma

3.1
. For
x\in B_{r}(x_{1})
, we use the translation invariant property of
k
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}w_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(w_{2}(x)-w_{2}(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(2w_{2}(x)-w_{2}(x+y)-w_{2}(x-y))K(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{|y|<r}|2w_{2}(x)-w_{2}(x-y)-w_{2}(x+y)||y|^{-n-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y+4\Lambda\int_{|y|\geq r}|y|^{-n-2s}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{2}w_{2}\|_{\infty}\int_{|y|<r}|y|^{-n-2s+2}\,\mathrm{d}y+Cr^{-2s}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-2s}.

∎

In order to compare a weak solution
u
with barriers, we need the localized maximum principle that is a modified version of
[
32
, Theorem 1.7]
. For this purpose, we show the following lemma, which captures the effect of a non-homogeneous term in the maximum principle for nonlocal operators.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
C_{0}
be a nonnegative constant. Let
u
be a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=-C_{0}
in
B_{r}(x_{1})
such that
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

u\geq-CC_{0}r^{2s}\quad\text{a.e.\ in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that the set

G\coloneqq\{u<-CC_{0}r^{2s}\}\,(\subset B_{r}(x_{1}))

has measure zero for a sufficiently large constant
C>0
. We define the function

\varphi=(u+CC_{0}r^{2s})_{-},

which is admissible for a test function in the weak formulation of
\mathcal{L}u=-C_{0}
; see
Proposition

2.2
or
[
32
, Proposition 2.6]
for instance. We observe that

(i)

G^{c}\supset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})\supset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x)
for any
x\in B_{r}(x_{1})
;

(ii)

u(x)-u(y)\leq 0
for
x\in G
and
y\in G^{c}
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle-C_{0}\int_{G}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq-\Lambda^{-1}\int_{G}\int_{G}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+2\Lambda^{-1}\int_{G}\int_{G^{c}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\Lambda^{-1}\int_{G}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x)}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-2CC_{0}\Lambda^{-1}r^{2s}\int_{G}\varphi(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{CC_{0}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{s\Lambda 2^{2s}}\int_{G}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x.

Taking
C>2^{2s}s\Lambda/|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
yields that
|G|=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

We next prove the localized maximum principle that is suitable for our purpose. As pointed out in
[
32
]
, see Lindgren–Lindqvist
[
41
, Lemma 9]
, Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
36
, Lemma 6]
or Kim–Lee
[
30
, Lemma 5.2]
for the standard maximum principle with respect to nonlocal operators.

Lemma 3.4

(Localized maximum principle)
.

Let
u
be a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
B_{r}(x_{1})
such that
u\geq 0
in a.e.
B_{R}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

u\geq-C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)\quad\text{a.e.\ in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R/2}
. Then for any nonnegative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}(x_{1}))
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u\eta,\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq-\mathcal{E}(u(1-\eta),\varphi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}(u(x)(1-\eta(x))-u(y)(1-\eta(y)))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})}(u(x)(1-\eta(x))-u(y)(1-\eta(y)))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=2\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R/2}}u(y)(1-\eta(y))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq-2\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}u_{-}(y)\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq-2\Lambda C_{0}\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

C_{0}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since

|x-y|\geq|y|-|x-x_{1}|-|x_{1}|\geq|y|/2\quad\text{for $x\in B_{r}(x_{1})$ and $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}$},

we have

\displaystyle 0\leq C_{0}\leq 2^{n+2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}(y)}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y=2^{n+2s}R^{-2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R).

Hence, we conclude that
u\eta
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}(u\eta)=-2\Lambda C_{0}
in
B_{r}(x_{1})
.

On the other hand, since
u\eta\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})
, we are able to apply
Lemma

3.3
to find that

u=u\eta\geq-C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)\quad\text{a.e.\ in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

for some constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.2
via the localized maximum principle with the barrier functions, provided that
k
is translation invariant.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
(i).

We set
v\coloneqq w_{1}+c_{0}w_{2}
for barrier functions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
constructed in
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

3.2
, respectively, where
c_{0}
is a universal positive constant that will be determined soon. In fact, due to
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

3.2
, there exists a positive constant
c_{0}
depending only on
n
,
s
and
\Lambda
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}v&\leq 0&&\text{in $B_{r}(x_{1})$}\\
v&=c_{0}&&\text{on $B_{r/2}(x_{1})$}\\
v&=1&&\text{on $B_{r}(x_{2})$}\\
v&=0&&\text{on $B_{R}\setminus(B_{r}(x_{1})\cup B_{r}(x_{2}))$}\\
v&=0&&\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We now apply the localized maximum principle (
Lemma

3.4
) for
u-mv
with
m\coloneqq\inf_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u
to obtain that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\geq mv-C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}((u-mv)_{-};0,R)

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}m-C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)\quad\text{in $B_{r/2}(x_{1})$}.

In particular, we arrive at

C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u\leq u(x_{1})+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R).

We finish the proof by applying the standard Harnack inequality (
Theorem

2.3
) in each ball
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and
B_{2r}(x_{2})
, together with the fact that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Non-robustness 1)
.

It is natural to ask whether the universal constant
C>0
appearing in
Theorem

1.2
is robust or not. More precisely, we would like to find the explicit dependence of
C
on the parameter
s\in(0,1)
and send
s\to 1^{-}
. In fact, we expect that
C
must blow up when we let
s\to 1^{-}
, in view of the simple observation in
Section

1
for the local setting.

For simplicity, we may assume that
u\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To verify the non-robustness, we first have to specify the dependence of the kernel
k
on
s
as follows:

\Lambda^{-1}(1-s)|x-y|^{-n-2s}\leq k(x,y)=k(y,x)\leq\Lambda(1-s)|x-y|^{-n-2s}.

Then it is easy to check that two barriers
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
satisfy

\mathcal{L}w_{1}\leq-C(1-s)r^{-2s}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}w_{2}\leq Cr^{-2s}\quad\text{in $B_{r}(x_{1})$},

where
C>0
depends only on
n
and
\Lambda
but not on
s
. In fact, in the proof of
Lemma

3.2
, the estimate of the integral
\int_{|y|<r}|y|^{-n-2s+2}\,\mathrm{d}y
requires us to multiply an additional
1/(1-s)
. Since we assume that
u
is nonnegative in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can just apply the global maximum principle to obtain that the constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\Lambda)
is comparable to
1-s
. Hence, we conclude that the inequality gives no information when we let
s\to 1^{-}
, as we expected.

4.
Proof 2: Poisson kernel estimates

4.1.
Poisson kernel estimates

In this subsection, we display the representation formula for weak solutions of nonlocal linear Dirichlet problems in terms of the Poisson kernel
P
, and derive the two-sided estimates for
P
.

Given a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an exterior datum
g\in L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)
, a unique solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=g&\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}\end{cases}

can be written by the representation formula

u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}g(z)P(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\quad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$},

where
P=P_{\mathcal{L},\Omega}:\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\to[0,\infty]
denotes the
Poisson kernel
associated with
\mathcal{L}
and
\Omega
. Moreover, the nonlocal Gauss–Green formula explains the relation between the Poisson kernel
P
and the Green function
G

P(x,z)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)k(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{for any $x\in\Omega$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}.

We refer to Bucur
[
6
]
, Chen–Song
[
13
]
and Kim–Weidner
[
33
]
for the definition and further properties of the Green function.

We now move our attention to the two-sided estimates for the Poisson kernel
P
. Chen–Song
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
obtained upper and lower bounds for
G
and
P
when
n\geq 2
, the kernel
k
is translation invariant and
\Omega
is a
C^{1,1}
domain. Recently, Kim–Weidner
[
33
, Corollary 9.6]
developed a PDE approach to derive the two-sided estimates for
P
in a more general setting, i.e., when
2s<n
,
k
is Hölder regular in the sense of (
4.1
) and
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
33
, Corollary 9.6]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
and
\alpha,\sigma\in(0,s)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain. Moreover, suppose that the kernel
k
is locally Hölder continuous of order
\sigma
in the following sense:

(4.1)

|k(x+h,y+h)-k(x,y)|\leq\Lambda\frac{|h|^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Omega$ and $h\in B_{1}$.}

Then it holds for any
x\in\Omega
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
,

c^{-1}\frac{d^{s}(x)}{d^{s}(z)(1+d(z))^{s}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{n}}\leq P(x,z)\leq c\frac{d^{s}(x)}{d^{s}(z)(1+d(z))^{s}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{n}}

for some universal constant
c=c(n,s,\Lambda,\Omega)\geq 1
, where
d(y)\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)
is the Euclidean distance between
y
and
\partial\Omega
.

As we mentioned in
Section

1
, we now propose a simplified version of
Theorem

4.1
, because we are concerned with the interior estimate of
P
associated with a ball. For this purpose, we first recall the bounds near the diagonal for the Green function
G
developed in
[
26
]
. In fact, Kassmann–Kim–Lee
[
26
]
proved
Theorem

4.2
when
n\geq 3
to guarantee the robustness of the comparable constant
C>0
. We note that the same (but not robust) estimates can still be obtained from the same arguments under the weaker condition
2s<n
.

Theorem 4.2

(Bounds for Green function,
[
26
, Theorems 1.4 and 1.5]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
. Then the Green function
G
of
\mathcal{L}
on
\Omega
satisfies

G(x,y)\leq C|x-y|^{2s-n}\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Omega$}

and

G(x,y)\geq C^{-1}|x-y|^{2s-n}\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Omega$ with $|x-y|\leq\min\{d(x),d(y)\}$}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
,
s
and
\Lambda
, but not on
\Omega
.

We use the interior estimates for
G
to develop the interior estimates for the Poisson kernel
P
associated with a ball
B_{r}(x_{1})
.

Theorem 4.3

(Bounds for Poisson kernel)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
. Let
P
be the Poisson kernel associated with
\mathcal{L}
and
B_{r}(x_{1})
. Then it holds for any
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x_{1})
,

C^{-1}\frac{r^{2s}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\leq P(x,z)\leq C\frac{r^{2s}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
,
\Lambda
and
s
, but not on
r
and
x_{1}
.

Proof.

We mainly follow the argument in
[
13
, Section 3]
. Let us recall the relation between
P
and
G
as follows:

P(x,z)=\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}G(x,y)k(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{for any $x\in B_{r}(x_{1})$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{1})$}.

Since we are interested in the interior estimates of
P
, we set
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x_{1})
.

(i) (Upper bound)
Since

|z-x|\leq|z-y|+|y-x|\leq|z-y|+3r/2\leq 5|z-y|/2

for
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})
,
y\in B_{r}(x_{1})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x_{1})
, we observe that

\displaystyle P(x,z)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\frac{G(x,y)}{|y-z|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\frac{2^{n+2s}}{5^{n+2s}|x-z|^{n+2s}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}G(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since
Theorem

4.2
shows that

G(x,y)\leq C|x-y|^{2s-n}\quad\text{for $x,y\in B_{r}(x_{1})$},

we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}G(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2r}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2s}.

(ii) (Lower bound) Since

|y-z|\leq|x-z|+|x-y|\leq 5|x-z|/4

for
y\in B_{r/4}(x)
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(x_{1})
, we observe that

\displaystyle P(x,z)

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})}\frac{G(x,y)}{|y-z|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda^{-1}\int_{B_{r/4}(x)}\frac{G(x,y)}{|y-z|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda^{-1}\frac{4^{n+2s}}{5^{n+2s}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\int_{B_{r/4}(x)}G(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Note that

|x-y|\leq r/4\leq\min\{d(x),d(y)\}\quad\text{for $x\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})$ and $y\in B_{r/4}(x)$}.

It follows from
Theorem

4.2
that

G(x,y)\geq C^{-1}|x-y|^{2s-n}\quad\text{for $x\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})$ and $y\in B_{r/4}(x)$}

and so

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r/4}(x)}G(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\int_{B_{r/4}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}r^{2s}.

∎

Remark 4.4

(Poisson kernel for
(-\Delta)^{s}
)
.

When it comes to
s
-harmonic functions, we do not need the assumption
2s<n
since there exists an explicit formula for the Poisson kernel in this special case. In particular, if we let
P^{s}(x,y)
be the Poisson kernel associated with
(-\Delta)^{s}
and a ball
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
, then it can be written as

P^{s}(x,y)\coloneqq c_{n,s}\frac{(r^{2}-|x|^{2})^{s}}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n}},

where
s\in(0,1)
and
c_{n,s}=\Gamma(n/2)\pi^{-n/2-1}\sin(s\pi)
; see
[
4
,
5
]
for details.

4.2.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

The goal of this subsection is to prove
Theorem

1.2
with the aid of the two-sided Poisson kernel estimates obtained in
Section

4.1
. As mentioned in
Section

1
, we require an assumption
2s<n
in this approach, since the Poisson kernel estimates strongly rely on the associated Green function estimates in the context of
[
28
,
33
]
.

Let us begin with a weak Harnack inequality for weak supersolutions of (
1.1
) in two disjoint balls. In contrast to
Theorem

1.2
, the lemma below requires
u
to be a weak supersolution only in
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and imposes no condition in
B_{2r}(x_{2})
.

Lemma 4.5

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
. Let
u
be a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
B_{2r}(x_{1})
and
u
is nonnegative in
B_{R}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

(4.2)

C\fint_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{1})}u+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R).

Proof.

We choose a point
\bar{x}\in\overline{B}_{r}(x_{1})
such that

u(\bar{x})=\inf_{B_{r}(x_{1})}u.

We let
P:B_{3r/2}(x_{1})\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3r/2}(x_{1}))\to[0,\infty]
be the Poisson kernel associated with
\mathcal{L}
and
B_{3r/2}(x_{1})
. If we let
v
be the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}v=0&\text{in $B_{3r/2}(x_{1})$}\\
v=u&\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3r/2}(x_{1})$},\end{cases}

then
Section

4.1
shows that the following representation formula holds:

v(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3r/2}(x_{1})}u(z)P(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\quad\text{for all $x\in B_{3r/2}(x_{1})$}.

On the other hand, the comparison principle between
u
and
v
in
B_{3r/2}(x_{1})
yields that
u\geq v
in
B_{3r/2}(x_{1})
; see
[
35
, Lemma 6]
for instance. In particular, the nonnegativity of
u
in
B_{R}
gives us that

\displaystyle u(\bar{x})\geq v(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3r/2}(x_{1})}u(z)P(\bar{x},z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u(z)P(\bar{x},z)\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}u(z)P(\bar{x},z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x_{2})}\frac{r^{2s}}{|\bar{x}-z|^{n+2s}}u(z)\,\mathrm{d}z-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{r^{2s}}{|\bar{x}-z|^{n+2s}}u_{-}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2},

where we used the two-sided Poisson kernel estimates (
Theorem

4.3
)

C^{-1}\frac{r^{2s}}{|\bar{x}-z|^{n+2s}}\leq P(\bar{x},z)\leq C\frac{r^{2s}}{|\bar{x}-z|^{n+2s}}

for
\bar{x}\in B_{r}(x_{1})
and
z\in B_{r}(x_{2})\cup(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R})
.

We now estimate two integral terms
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
as follows:

(i)

For
z\in B_{r}(x_{2})
, we have

|z-\bar{x}|\leq|z-x_{2}|+|x_{2}-x_{1}|+|x_{1}-\bar{x}|\leq 10r

and so

I_{1}\geq C\fint_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(ii)

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
, we have

|z-\bar{x}|\geq|z|-|\bar{x}|\geq\frac{|z|}{2}

and so

I_{2}\geq-Cr^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}(z)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}z=-C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R).

By combining these estimates above, we conclude that

C\fint_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{1})}u+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R).

∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.2
via the Poisson kernel estimates, when
2s<n
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
(ii).

In view of
Lemma

4.5
, we focus on the estimate of the left-hand side of (
4.2
) by employing the standard Harnack inequality. In fact, we apply the Harnack inequality (
Theorem

2.3
) in a ball
B_{2r}(x_{2})
to find that

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u+C\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{2},r).

Here we observe that

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{2},r)=r^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}(y)}{|y-x_{2}|^{1+2s}}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R),

since

|y-x_{2}|\geq|y|-|x_{2}|\geq\frac{|y|}{2}\quad\text{for all $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}$}.

Therefore, we obtain that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)

\displaystyle\leq C\fint_{B_{r}(x_{2})}u(z)\,\mathrm{d}z+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};0,R)

The desired estimate follows from the combination of (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
∎

.

In view of the explicit Poisson kernel expression in

Theorem

(-\Delta)^{s}

Remark 4.7

(Non-robustness 2)
.

As we already checked in
Remark

3.5
, the robustness does not hold in
Theorem

1.2
. In the approach based on the Poisson kernel, we can observe that such a non-robustness arises from the following Gauss–Green formula:

P(x,z)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)k(z,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{for any $x\in\Omega$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}.

Here we again point out that
k\sim(1-s)
and so
P\sim(1-s)
. Hence, the representation formula for
u
yields that the constant
C>0
in
Theorem

1.2
must blow up.

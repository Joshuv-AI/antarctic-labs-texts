Quiver Hecke algebras for Borcherds-Cartan datum III: Categorification of quantum Borcherds superalgebras
Wan Wu

Abstract
. We introduce a family of the quiver Hecke superalgebras which give a categorification of quantum Borcherds superalgebras.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
17B37, 17B67, 16G20, 20C08

Introduction

The quiver Hecke superalgebra is the superalgebraic counterpart of the Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier (KLR) algebra (also called the quiver Hecke algebra)
[
15
,
16
,
21
]
. First introduced by Kang, Kashiwara, and Tsuchioka in
[
12
]
, it was explicitly constructed to rigorously link affine Hecke-Clifford superalgebras and affine Sergeev superalgebras. This extension has further furnished a profound theoretical framework for studying the modular representation theory of spin symmetric groups
[
17
,
14
]
.

For the one-vertex quiver, the quiver Hecke superalgebra reduces to the odd nil-Hecke algebra
ONH_{n}
—the odd analogue of the nil-Hecke algebra, first introduced by Ellis, Khovanov and Lauda in
[
4
]
.
Concretely, this algebra can be realized as a matrix algebra over
O\Lambda_{n}
, the odd symmetric polynomial algebra.
Both
O\Lambda_{n}
and its direct limit
O\Lambda
(the ring of odd symmetric functions) are fundamental, as they are the odd analogues of symmetric functions.
Paralleling the indispensable role of symmetric functions in geometry and combinatorics, these odd symmetric algebraic objects also hold essential applications in the same fields
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
.

In contrast to the well-established result that KLR algebras categorify the (half) quantum group, Hill and Wang
[
7
]
exploited the quiver Hecke superalgebra to categorify a covering algebra of both the half quantum group and the half quantum superalgebra. This covering algebra is defined as a
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
-algebra, which specializes to a quantum Kac-Moody algebra when
\pi=1
and to a quantum Kac-Moody superalgebra when
\pi=-1
.
The most notable innovation lies in the identification of the parameter
\pi
with the parity shift action on superalgebras and supermodules during the categorification procedure.
Specifically, they proved that the Grothendieck group of the category of projective graded supermodules over the quiver Hecke superalgebra is isomorphic to the aforementioned covering algebra as
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
-algebras.
In particular, the specialization
\pi=-1
yields a categorification of quantum Kac-Moody superalgebras.

A parallel result was established by Kang, Kashiwara, and Oh
[
10
,
11
]
, who employed this algebraic framework to furnish a supercategorification of quantum groups and further to provide a categorification of irreducible highest-weight modules. In both cases, their approach hinges on utilizing the cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras to categorify irreducible highest-weight modules, following the theoretical framework established in
[
9
]
.

Hill and Wang
[
7
]
proposed that their methodological framework is applicable to the quantum Borcherds algebra setting. This implies the potential construction of a quiver Hecke superalgebra associated with a Borcherds-Cartan datum, which would categorify both the covering algebra of the quantum Borcherds algebra (as defined in Section 1.3) and the quantum Borcherds superalgebra (as defined in
[
1
]
).

Notably, the construction of such a quiver Hecke superalgebra depends on the prior establishment of the corresponding quiver Hecke algebra, while the categorification of the aforementioned covering algebra relies on the categorification of the conventional quantum group. In previous work, Tong and Wu
[
22
,
23
]
achieved the categorification of quantum Borcherds algebras (also called quantum generalized Kac–Moody algebras) associated with arbitrary Borcherds-Cartan data—this breakthrough facilitates the construction of the superalgebraic counterpart (i.e., the quiver Hecke superalgebra) and thereby enables the categorification of both the quantum Borcherds superalgebra and its covering algebra.

In this paper, we extend the construction introduced in
[
22
]
to the context of Borcherds Cartan superdata, and apply the methodology established in
[
7
]
to categorify the covering algebras of quantum Borcherds algebras and quantum Borcherds superalgebras. Given an arbitrary Borcherds–Cartan superdatum, consisting of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded indexed
I=I_{\overline{0}}\sqcup I_{\overline{1}}
and a matrix
\widetilde{A}
parametrized by an
I
with diagonal can be
\leq 0
, we define the associated covering quantum Borcherds algebra
\mathbf{H}
, which is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
twisted bialgebra. The specializations of
\pi=1
and
\pi=-1
of
\mathbf{H}
coincide with the negative parts of the quantum Borcherds algebra
\mathbf{X}
and the negative part of the quantum Borcherds superalgebra
\mathbf{Y}
, respectively. Furthermore, we establish that
\mathbf{H}
can be realized as the quotient algebra
\mathscr{H}/\text{rad}\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
, where
\text{rad}\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
denotes the radical of the Lusztig form
\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
on the free algebra
\mathscr{H}
.

We next construct the quiver Hecke superalgebras
R(\nu)(\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I])
and derive their polynomial representations via braid-like planar diagrams, building on the foundational work of
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
.
We further consider the degenerate case
\nu=ni
for fixed
i\in I
, where only the subcase
i\in I^{\text{im}}\cap I_{\overline{1}}
necessitates novel analysis. For
i\in I^{\text{re}}
,
R(ni)
reduces to the (odd) nil-Hecke algebra,
and the case
i\in I^{\text{im}}\cap I_{\overline{0}}
has been addressed in
[
22
]
.

Finally, we proceed to characterize the type
\mathtt{M}
phenomenon of
R(\nu)
via the Kashiwara operator construction in
[
23
]
, a cornerstone result for categorification and related topics.
Let
K_{0}(R(\nu))
be the Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective graded
R(\nu)
-supermodules.
The aggregate Grothendieck group
K_{0}(R)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}[I]}K_{0}(R(\nu))
carries a twisted bialgebra structure induced by module induction and restriction.
We show a twisted
\mathbb{Z}^{\pi}[q,q^{-1}]
-bialgebra isomorphism between
K_{0}(R)
and
{}_{\mathcal{A}^{\pi}}\mathbf{H}
(the
\mathcal{A}^{\pi}
form of
\mathbf{H}
).
Specializing
\pi
to
-1
thereby yields a categorification of the quantum Borcherds superalgebra as Theorem
3.5
.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
\pi
be an indeterminate. For any ring
R
, define
R^{\pi}=R[\pi]/(\pi^{2}-1)
.

1.1.
Graded superalgebras

A
graded superalgebra

A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}A_{n}^{\varepsilon}
is a
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-graded algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. For a bi-homogeneous element
a\in A_{n}^{\varepsilon}
, we write
|a|=n
for its degree and set

p(a)=\begin{cases}0\ \ &\text{if}\ \varepsilon=\overline{0},\\
1&\text{if}\ \varepsilon=\overline{1}.\end{cases}

Given two graded superalgebras
A
and
B
, their tensor product
A\otimes B
is a graded superalgebra with the multiplication given by

(a\otimes b)(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=(-1)^{p(b)p(a^{\prime})}aa^{\prime}\otimes bb^{\prime}.

Let
A,B
be graded superalgebras. A
graded
A
-
B
-bisupermodule

M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}M_{n}^{\varepsilon}
is a
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-graded
A
-
B
-bimodule.
For two such bisupermodules
M
and
N
, set

\text{HOM}_{A\text{-}B}(M,N)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\text{HOM}_{A\text{-}B}(M,N)_{n}^{\varepsilon},

where
\text{HOM}_{A\text{-}B}(M,N)_{n}^{\varepsilon}
consists of all linear maps
f:M\rightarrow N
of bidegree
(n,\varepsilon)
such that

f(amb)=(-1)^{p(f)p(a)}af(m)b\quad\text{for}\ a\in A,b\in B\ \text{and}\ m\in M.

By omitting
B
or
A
we obtain the notions of left or right graded supermodules and their morphisms. Unless otherwise stated, all graded supermodules are assumed to be left modules.

For a graded superspace
V
, i.e., a
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-graded vector space, we denote by
|V|
its underlying
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space (forgetting parity). For graded

A
-supermodules
M
and
N
, we write
M\simeq N
if there exists an isomorphism in
\text{HOM}_{A}(M,N)_{0}^{\overline{0}}
, and write
M\cong N
if there exists an isomorphism in
\text{HOM}_{A}(M,N)_{0}
, that is, if
|M|
and
|N|
are isomorphic as graded
|A|
-modules.

Let
M
be a graded
A
-supermodule and let
q
be an indeterminate. If
M_{n}:=M_{n}^{\overline{0}}\oplus M_{n}^{\overline{1}}=0
for
n\ll 0
, we define

\text{dim}^{\pi}_{q}M:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\text{dim}M_{n}^{\overline{0}}+\pi\text{dim}M_{n}^{\overline{1}}\right)q^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\pi}((q)).

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
degree shift

M\{k\}
is the graded supermodule obtained from
M
by putting
(M\{k\})_{n}^{\varepsilon}=M_{n-k}^{\varepsilon}
. The
parity shift

\Pi M
is obtained by putting
(\Pi M)_{n}^{\varepsilon}=M_{n}^{\varepsilon+\overline{1}}
and with a new action
a\circ m=(-1)^{p(a)}am
. We have

\text{HOM}_{A}(\Pi^{r}M\{k\},N)\simeq\Pi^{r}\text{HOM}_{A}(M,N)\{-k\}

as
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-graded vector spaces.

For
f(q,\pi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(a_{n}+\pi b_{n})q^{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\pi}[q,q^{-1}]
, define a graded supermodule

f(q,\pi)M:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((M\{n\})^{\oplus a_{n}}\oplus(\Pi M\{n\})^{\oplus b_{n}}\right),

so that
\text{dim}^{\pi}_{q}f(q,\pi)M=f(q,\pi)\cdot\text{dim}^{\pi}_{q}M
.

Given graded
A
-supermodule
M
and graded
B
-supermodule
N
, their tensor product is a graded
A\otimes B
-supermodule with action

a\otimes b\cdot(m\otimes n)=(-1)^{p(b)p(m)}am\otimes bn.

If
f\in\text{HOM}_{A}(M,M^{\prime})
and
g\in\text{HOM}_{B}(N,N^{\prime})
, then
f\otimes g\in\text{HOM}_{A\otimes B}(M\otimes N,M^{\prime}\otimes N^{\prime})
acts by

f\otimes g(m\otimes n)=(-1)^{p(g)p(m)}f(m)\otimes g(n).

An irreducible graded
A
-supermodule
V
is said to be of
type
\mathtt{M}
if
|V|
is an irreducible graded
|A|
-module; otherwise
V
is of
type
\mathtt{Q}
.
A graded superalgebra
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{n}
is
Laurentian
if each
A_{n}
is finite-dimensional and
A_{n}=0
for
n\ll 0
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Assume
A
is Laurentian.

(1)

Up to degree and parity shifts, there are only finitely many irreducible graded
A
-supermodules, all of which are finite-dimensional.

(2)

Let
V
be an irreducible graded
A
-supermodule. Then
V
is of type
\mathtt{Q}
if and only if
\Pi V\simeq V
. Moreover, if
V
is of type
\mathtt{Q}
, then
|V|\cong N\oplus N^{\varsigma}
for some irreducible graded
|A|
-modules
N
with
N\ncong N^{\varsigma}
.

Here,
N^{\varsigma}
is the graded
|A|
-module obtained from
N
by twisting the action via
a\ast n=(-1)^{p(a)}an
. We refer to
N
as
V^{+}
and to
N^{\varsigma}
as
V^{-}
.

(3)

The graded Jacoboson radical
J^{gr}(|A|)
of
|A|
coincides with the graded super Jacoboson radical of
A
:

J^{gr\text{-}super}(A)=\bigcap\{\text{ann}(V)\mid V\ \text{irreducible graded}\ A\text{-supermodule}\}.

(4)

Suppose that, up to degree and parity shifts,
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
of type
\mathtt{M}
and
V_{r+1},\dots,V_{t}
of type
\mathtt{Q}
is a non-redundant set of irreducible graded
A
-supermodules, then

(i)

up to degree shifts,
V_{1},\dots,V_{r},\Pi V_{1},\dots,\Pi V_{r},V_{r+1},\dots,V_{t}
is a non-redundant set of irreducible graded
A
-supermodules,

(ii)

up to degree shifts,
V_{1},\dots,V_{r},V_{r+1}^{\pm},\dots,V_{t}^{\pm}
is a non-redundant set of irreducible graded
|A|
-modules.

Proof.

Part (1) is a direct consequence of
[
20
, Theorem 2.7.2]
.
The statements in (2)-(4) follow from the corresponding results in
[
13
, Section 12.2]
.
∎

1.2.
Quantum Borcherds (super)algebras

Let
I=I_{\overline{0}}\sqcup I_{\overline{1}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded index set. A
Borcherds-Cartan superdatum

(I,\widetilde{A},\cdot)
consists of

(1)

an integer-valued matrix
\widetilde{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
satisfying

(i)

a_{ii}=2,0,-2,-4,\dots,

(ii)

a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for
i\neq j
,

(iii)

a_{ij}=0
if and only if
a_{ji}=0
,

(iv)

a_{ij}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for
i\in I_{\overline{1}},j\in I
,

(v)

there is a diagonal matrix
D=\text{diag}(r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid i\in I)
such that
D\widetilde{A}
is symmetric.

(2)

a symmetric bilinear form
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\mapsto\nu\cdot\nu^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{Z}[I]
taking values in
\mathbb{Z}
, such that

i\cdot j=r_{i}a_{ij}=r_{j}a_{ji}\ \ \text{for all}\ i,j\in I.

We set
I^{\text{re}}=\{i\in I\mid a_{ii}=2\}
and
I^{\text{im}}=\{i\in I\mid a_{ii}\leq 0\}
. For each
i\in I
, define
q_{i}=q^{r_{i}}
. For
i\in I^{\text{re}},n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

[n]_{i}^{\pi}=\frac{(\pi^{p(i)}q_{i})^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{\pi^{p(i)}q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\ \ [n]_{i}^{\pi}!=[n]_{i}^{\pi}[n-1]_{i}^{\pi}\cdots[1]_{i}^{\pi}.

In particular, we write

[n]_{i}^{+}=[n]_{i}^{\pi}|_{\pi=1},\ \ [n]_{i}^{-}=[n]_{i}^{\pi}|_{\pi=-1}.

Definition 1.2
.

The (half part)
quantum Borcherds algebra

\mathbf{X}
associated to
(I,\widetilde{A},\cdot)
is the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra generated by
f_{i}

(i\in I)
, subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{a}f_{i}^{(a)}f_{j}f_{i}^{(b)}=0\quad\text{for}\ i\in I^{\text{re}},j\in I\ \text{and}\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle f_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}f_{i}=0\quad\text{for}\ i,j\in I\ \text{with}\ i\cdot j=0.

Here
f_{i}^{(a)}=f_{i}^{a}/[a]^{+}_{i}!
. The algebra
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}[I]
-graded by assigning
|f_{i}|=i
.

By
[
19
, Chapter 1]
and
[
8
, Section 3]
,
\mathbf{X}
admits a comultiplication

\rho_{+}:\mathbf{X}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}\otimes\mathbf{X},\ \ f_{i}\mapsto f_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ i\in I,

where
\mathbf{X}\otimes\mathbf{X}
is endowed with the twisted multiplication

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=q^{-|x_{2}|\cdot|y_{1}|}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2}.

There is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}_{+}:\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)
satisfying

(i)

\{x,y\}_{+}=0
if
|x|\neq|y|
,

(ii)

\{1,1\}_{+}=1
,

(iii)

\{f_{i},f_{i}\}_{+}=(1-q_{i}^{2})^{-1}
for all
i\in I
,

(iv)

\{x,yz\}_{+}=\{\rho_{+}(x),y\otimes z\}_{+}
for
x,y,z\in\mathbf{X}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials. The
\mathcal{A}
-form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{X}}
is the
\mathcal{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{X}
generated by the
f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I^{\text{re}},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
f_{i}
for
i\in I^{\text{im}}
.

Definition 1.3
.

[
1
, Definition 2.7]
The (half part)
quantum Borcherds superalgebra

\mathbf{Y}
associated to
(I,\widetilde{A},\cdot)
is the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra generated by
f_{i}

(i\in I)
, subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{a+p(a;i,j)}f_{i}^{(a)}f_{j}f_{i}^{(b)}=0\quad\text{for}\ i\in I^{\text{re}},j\in I\ \text{and}\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle f_{i}f_{j}-(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}f_{j}f_{i}=0\quad\text{for}\ i,j\in I\ \text{with}\ i\cdot j=0.

Here
p(a;i,j)=ap(i)p(j)+\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)p(i)
and
f_{i}^{(a)}=f_{i}^{a}/[a]^{-}_{i}!
.
The algebra
\mathbf{Y}
is
(\mathbb{N}[I],\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-graded by assigning
|f_{i}|=i
and
p(f_{i})=p(i)
.

Again, by
[
19
, Chapter 1]
and
[
8
, Section 3]
,
\mathbf{Y}
admits a comultiplication

\rho_{-}:\mathbf{Y}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}\otimes\mathbf{Y},\ f_{i}\mapsto f_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes f_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ i\in I,

where
\mathbf{Y}\otimes\mathbf{Y}
is endowed with the twisted multiplication

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=(-1)^{p(x_{2})p(y_{1})}q^{-|x_{2}|\cdot|y_{1}|}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2}.

One can also show that there is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}_{-}:\mathbf{Y}\times\mathbf{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)
satisfying

(i)

\{x,y\}_{-}=0
if
|x|\neq|y|
,

(ii)

\{1,1\}_{-}=1
,

(iii)

\{f_{i},f_{i}\}_{-}=(1-(-1)^{p(i)}q_{i}^{2})^{-1}
for all
i\in I
,

(iv)

\{x,yz\}_{-}=\{\rho_{-}(x),y\otimes z\}_{-}
for
x,y,z\in\mathbf{Y}
.

A detailed illustration of these results is provided in the Appendix, where the same arguments also apply to the quantum Borcherds algebra.

Similarly, the
\mathcal{A}
-form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{Y}}
is the
\mathcal{A}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{Y}
generated by the
f_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I^{\text{re}},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
f_{i}
for
i\in I^{\text{im}}
.

1.3.
Covering algebras

Given a Borcherds-Cartan superdatum
(I,\widetilde{A},\cdot)
, we define a covering algebra
\mathbf{H}
for
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
. This construction was first introduced in
[
7
]
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be the free
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
-algebra generated by
\theta_{i}

(i\in I)
with
|\theta_{i}|=i
and
p(\theta_{i})=p(i)
. We have a comultiplication
\rho_{\pi}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}\otimes\mathscr{H}
given by
\rho_{\pi}{(\theta_{i})}=\theta_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes\theta_{i}
(
i\in I
). Here
\mathscr{H}\otimes\mathscr{H}
is endowed with the twisted multiplication

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=\pi^{p(x_{2})p(y_{1})}q^{-|x_{2}|\cdot|y_{1}|}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2}.

As in
[
19
, 1.2.3]
, there is a symmetric bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}:\mathscr{H}\times\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
satisfying

(i)

\{x,y\}_{\pi}=0
if
|x|\neq|y|
,

(ii)

\{1,1\}_{\pi}=1
,

(iii)

\{\theta_{i},\theta_{i}\}_{\pi}=(1-\pi^{p(i)}q_{i}^{2})^{-1}
for all
i\in I
,

(iv)

\{x,yz\}_{\pi}=\{\rho_{\pi}(x),y\otimes z\}_{\pi}
for
x,y,z\in\mathscr{H}
.

Similar to
[
19
, 1.4.5]
, the following elements lie in the radical of the bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{a}\pi^{p(a;i,j)}\theta_{i}^{(a)}\theta_{j}\theta_{i}^{(b)}\quad\text{for}\ i\in I^{\text{re}},j\in I\ \text{and}\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle\theta_{i}\theta_{j}-\pi^{p(i)p(j)}\theta_{j}\theta_{i}\quad\text{for}\ i,j\in I\ \text{with}\ i\cdot j=0.

Here
\theta_{i}^{(a)}=\theta_{i}^{a}/[a]^{\pi}_{i}!
. More generally, the radical also contains the elements

\sum_{a+b=1-na_{ij}}(-1)^{a}\pi^{p(a;i,j;n)}\theta_{i}^{(a)}\theta_{j}^{n}\theta_{i}^{(b)}\quad\text{for}\ i\in I^{\text{re}},j\in I,n\geq 1\ \text{and}\ i\neq j,

where
p(a;i,j;n)=anp(i)p(j)+\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)p(i)
(see, for instance,
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the quotient of
\mathscr{H}
by the two-sided ideal generated by (
1.1
).

Proposition 1.4
.

\mathbf{H}\cong\mathscr{H}/\text{rad}\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}.

Proof.

There are canonical
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra isomorphisms, given by
\theta_{i}\mapsto f_{i}

(i\in I)
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\big/(\pi-1)\cong\mathbb{Q}(q)\otimes_{\pi=1}\mathbf{H}\cong\mathbf{X},

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\big/(\pi+1)\cong\mathbb{Q}(q)\otimes_{\pi=-1}\mathbf{H}\cong\mathbf{Y}.

The tensor products here are taken over
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
, with
\mathbb{Q}(q)
viewed as a
\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\pi}
-module via the specialization
\pi\mapsto 1
(resp.
\pi\mapsto-1
). Under the isomorphism, the bilinear form
\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
specialized at
\pi=1
(resp.
\pi=-1
) on
\mathbf{H}\big/(\pi-1)
(resp.
\mathbf{H}\big/(\pi+1)
) coincides with
\{\ ,\ \}_{+}
(resp.
\{\ ,\ \}_{-}
), and is therefore nondegenerate in both cases. Now, let
x
lie in the radical of
\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
on
\mathbf{H}
. Then
x\in(\pi-1)\cap(\pi+1)=(\pi^{2}-1)=0
, which implies that

\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
is nondegenerate on
\mathbf{H}
.
Consequently, we have
\mathbf{H}\cong\mathscr{H}/\text{rad}\{\ ,\ \}_{\pi}
.
∎

The
\mathcal{A}^{\pi}
-form
{{}_{\mathcal{A}^{\pi}}\mathbf{H}}
is the
\mathcal{A}^{\pi}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{H}
generated by the
\theta_{i}^{(n)}
for
i\in I^{\text{re}},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\theta_{i}
for
i\in I^{\text{im}}
. Note that

(1.2)

{{}_{\mathcal{A}^{\pi}}\mathbf{H}}\big/(\pi-1)\cong{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{X}},\quad{{}_{\mathcal{A}^{\pi}}\mathbf{H}}\big/(\pi+1)\cong{{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{Y}}.

2.
Quiver Hecke superalgebras for Borcherds-Cartan superdatum

Given a Borcherds-Cartan superdatum
(I,\widetilde{A},\cdot)
, for
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
, we define

T_{ij}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\mid r_{i}a+r_{j}b=-i\cdot j;\ a\in 2\mathbb{Z}\ \text{if}\ i\in I_{\overline{1}};\ b\in 2\mathbb{Z}\ \text{if}\ j\in I_{\overline{1}}\}.

Choose a family of integers
\{t_{i,j;a,b}\}_{i\neq j,(a,b)\in T_{ij}}
such that

t_{i,j;a,b}=t_{j,i;b,a}
and
t_{i,j;-a_{ij},0}\neq 0
. For each pair
i\neq j
,
define the polynomial

Q_{ij}(u,v)=\sum_{(a,b)\in T_{ij}}t_{i,j;a,b}\ u^{a}v^{b}\in\mathbb{Z}\left<u,v\right>/\langle uv-(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}vu\rangle.

This can be written explicitly as

Q_{ij}(u,v)=t_{i,j;-a_{ij},0}u^{-a_{ij}}+\sum_{a<-a_{ij},b<-a_{ji}}t_{i,j;a,b}\ u^{a}v^{b}+t_{i,j;0,-a_{ji}}v^{-a_{ji}}.

By construction, we have
Q_{ij}(u,v)=Q_{ji}(v,u)
. Assume that
|u|=2r_{i},p(u)=p(i)
and
|v|=2r_{j},p(v)=p(j)
. Then
Q_{ij}(u,v)
is homogeneous of degree
-2i\cdot j
and is parity even.

If
i\cdot j=0
, we take
Q_{ij}(u,v)=1
for simplicity.

2.2.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be the superalgebra with even generators
y_{1},\dots,y_{n},z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and odd generators
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
, subject to the following relations:

(i)

y_{i}y_{j}=y_{j}y_{i},\ z_{i}z_{j}=z_{j}z_{i},\ y_{i}z_{j}=z_{j}y_{i}

(1\leq i,j\leq n)
,

(ii)

c_{i}^{2}=1
,
c_{i}c_{j}=-c_{j}c_{i}

(1\leq i\neq j\leq n)
,

(iii)

c_{i}y_{j}=(-1)^{\delta_{ij}}y_{j}c_{i},\ c_{i}z_{j}=(-1)^{\delta_{ij}}z_{j}c_{i}

(1\leq i,j\leq n)
.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
\mathscr{P}
by permuting the symbols
y_{i},z_{j}
, and
c_{k}
independently. For
1\leq k\leq n-1

\sigma_{k}
on
\mathscr{P}
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(y_{k})=-1-c_{k}c_{k+1},\ \sigma_{k}(y_{k+1})=1-c_{k}c_{k+1},\ \sigma_{k}(y_{j})=0\ \text{for}\ j\neq k,k+1,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(z_{j})=\sigma_{k}(c_{j})=0\ \ \text{for all}\ j,

and the Leibniz rule

\sigma_{k}(fg)=\sigma_{k}(f)g+s_{k}(f)\sigma_{k}(g)\ \ \text{for all}\ f,g\in\mathscr{P}.

By
[
18
, Lemma 3.6]
, these operators satisfy the nilCoxeter relations

\sigma_{k}^{2}=0,\ \sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}=\sigma_{l}\sigma_{k}\ (|k-l|>1),\ \sigma_{k}\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k+1}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k+1}.

Similarly, we define
\sigma_{k}^{\prime}
to be the operator obtained by interchanging
y_{j}
with
z_{j}
in the definition of
\sigma_{k}
.

.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.4.2, 3.4.3]

U^{-}
is a left
\mathscr{B}
-module, and we have a canonical isomorphism of
\mathscr{B}
-modules:

\mathscr{B}\big/\sum_{i\in I}\mathscr{B}e_{i}^{\prime}\cong U^{-}.

Proof.

We check the defining relations of
\mathscr{B}
in the endomorphism ring of
U^{-}
. First assume that
i,j\in I
with
a_{ij}=0
. By equation (
4.1
), for any
k\in I
, we have

\displaystyle e_{i}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime}f_{k}=\delta_{jk}e_{i}^{\prime}+(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}\delta_{ik}e_{j}^{\prime}+(-1)^{(p(i)+p(j))p(k)}q_{k}^{-a_{ki}-a_{kj}}f_{k}e_{i}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime},

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}f_{k}=(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}\delta_{jk}e_{i}^{\prime}+\delta_{ik}e_{j}^{\prime}+(-1)^{(p(i)+p(j))p(k)}q_{k}^{-a_{ki}-a_{kj}}f_{k}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}.

Let
S=e_{i}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime}-(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}
. Then for each
k\in I
,

Sf_{k}=(-1)^{(p(i)+p(j))p(k)}q_{k}^{-a_{ki}-a_{kj}}f_{k}S,

which implies that
S
acts as zero on
U^{-}
.

Next assume
i\in I^{\text{re}},j\in I
with
j\neq i
, and let
m=1-a_{ij}
. For any
k\in I,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain from (
4.1
)

e_{i}^{\prime n}f_{k}=(-1)^{(n-1)p(i)}q_{i}^{1-n}[n]_{i}^{-}\delta_{ik}e_{i}^{\prime n-1}+(-1)^{np(i)p(k)}q_{i}^{-na_{ik}}f_{k}e_{i}^{\prime n}.

Therefore, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
a+b=m
, we have

\displaystyle e_{i}^{\prime a}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime b}f_{k}=(-1)^{(mp(i)+p(j))p(k)}q_{k}^{-ma_{ki}-a_{kj}}f_{k}e_{i}^{\prime a}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime b}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{bp(i)p(j)}q_{i}^{b(m-1)}\delta_{jk}e_{i}^{\prime m}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(b-1)p(i)}q_{i}^{1-b}[b]_{i}^{-}\delta_{ik}e_{i}^{\prime a}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime b-1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(m-1)p(i)+p(i)p(j)}q_{i}^{-b}[a]_{i}^{-}\delta_{ik}e_{i}^{\prime a-1}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime b}.

Since

\sum_{a+b=m}(-1)^{a}v^{b(m-1)}{\begin{bmatrix}m\\
a\end{bmatrix}}_{v}=0,

\sum_{a+b=m}(-1)^{a+\frac{b(b-1)}{2}p(i)}q_{i}^{b(m-1)}{\begin{bmatrix}m\\
a\end{bmatrix}}_{i}^{-}=0.

By combining this with the fact that
\frac{a(a-1)}{2}+\frac{b(b-1)}{2}\equiv\frac{m-1}{2}\pmod{2}
when

(4.4)

\sum_{a+b=m}(-1)^{a+p(a;i,j)+bp(i)p(j)}q_{i}^{b(m-1)}{\begin{bmatrix}m\\
a\end{bmatrix}}_{i}^{-}=0.

Note also that

(4.5)

p(a;i,j)+(b-1)p(i)+p(a+1;i,j)+(m-1)p(i)+p(i)p(j)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Now, define

S=\sum_{a+b=m}(-1)^{a+p(a;i,j)}{\begin{bmatrix}m\\
a\end{bmatrix}}_{i}^{-}e_{i}^{\prime a}e_{j}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime b}.

Using the identities (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

Sf_{k}=(-1)^{(mp(i)+p(j))p(k)}q_{k}^{-ma_{ki}-a_{kj}}f_{k}S,

which implies that
S
acts as zero on

.

The second part of the proposition follows immediately from
[
8

.

Lemma 4.4
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.4.7]

Let
x\in U^{-}
satisfy
e_{i}^{\prime}(x)=0
for all
i\in I
. Then
x\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
\text{ht}{(\nu)}\geq 1
. The case for
\text{ht}{(\nu)}=1
is trivial. Now assume
\text{ht}{(\nu)}>1
. Let
x\in U^{-}_{\nu}
such that
e_{i}^{\prime}(x)=0
for all
i\in I
.

For any
i,j\in I
, we have by equation (
4.2
),

e_{i}^{\prime}e_{j}^{\prime\prime}(x)=(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}q_{i}^{a_{ij}}e_{j}^{\prime\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}(x)=0.

By the induction hypothesis, this implies that
e_{j}^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
for all
j\in I
. Thus, for any
j\in I
, have the commutation relation

e_{j}x=(-1)^{p(i)p(j)}xe_{j}.

By
[
1
, Corollary 4.10]
, the irreducible highest weight module
V(\Lambda)
with highest weight
\Lambda\in P^{+}
and highest weight vector
v_{\Lambda}
is given by

V(\Lambda)\cong U^{-}\bigg/(\sum_{i\in I^{\text{re}}}U^{-}f_{i}^{\Lambda(h_{i})+1}+\sum_{i\in I^{\text{im}}\ \text{with}\ \Lambda(h_{i})=0}U^{-}f_{i}).

Choose
\Lambda\in P^{+}
such that
\Lambda\gg 0
. Then, there is an isomorphism

U_{\nu}^{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}V(\Lambda)_{\Lambda-\nu},\ u\mapsto uv_{\Lambda}.

For all
j\in I
, we have
e_{j}xv_{\Lambda}=0
, which implies that the submodule
Uxv_{\Lambda}
of
V(\Lambda)
does not contain
v_{\Lambda}
. Since
V(\Lambda)
is irreducible, this forces
xv_{\Lambda}=0
, implying that
x=0
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

[
8
, Proposition 3.4.4, Corollary 3.4.8.]

For any family
\{\kappa_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of non-zero elements in
\mathbb{Q}(q)
, there exists a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle:U^{-}\times U^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)
such that
\langle 1,1\rangle=1
and

\langle f_{i}x,y\rangle=\kappa_{i}\langle x,e_{i}^{\prime}y\rangle\ \ \text{for all}\ i\in I.

Proof.

The assignment
f_{i}\mapsto\kappa_{i}e_{i}^{\prime},e_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto\kappa_{i}^{-1}f_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra antiinvolution of
\mathscr{B}
. Thus
(U^{-})^{*}
is a left
\mathscr{B}
-module with the following action

(f_{i}\varphi)(y)=\kappa_{i}\varphi(e_{i}^{\prime}y),\ (e_{i}^{\prime}\varphi)(y)=\kappa_{i}^{-1}\varphi(f_{i}y)\ \ \text{for}\ \varphi\in(U^{-})^{*}.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in(U^{-})^{*}
defined by

\varphi_{0}(1)=1,\ \varphi_{0}(\sum_{i}f_{i}U^{-})=0.

Thus
e_{i}^{\prime}\varphi_{0}=0
for all
i\in I
. This gives rise to a
\mathscr{B}
-linear map

\mu:U^{-}\cong\mathscr{B}\big/\sum_{i\in I}\mathscr{B}e_{i}^{\prime}\rightarrow(U^{-})^{*},\ 1\mapsto\varphi_{0}.

Now, define the bilinear form
\langle x,y\rangle:=(\mu(x))(y)
. We have by the definition

\langle f_{i}x,y\rangle=\kappa_{i}\langle x,e_{i}^{\prime}y\rangle,\quad\langle e_{i}^{\prime}x,y\rangle=\kappa_{i}^{-1}\langle x,f_{i}y\rangle.

Such a bilinear form is unique. Since
\langle x,y\rangle^{\prime}:=\langle y,x\rangle
satisfies the same property, we see that
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
is symmetric.

To prove the non-degeneracy of
\langle x,y\rangle
, we use induction on
\text{ht}{(\nu)}\geq 1
. Let
x\in U^{-}_{\nu}
such that
\langle x,U^{-}_{\nu}\rangle=0
. Then, for all
i\in I
, we have
\langle e_{i}^{\prime}x,U^{-}_{\nu-i}\rangle=0
. By the induction hypothesis, this implies that
e_{i}^{\prime}x=0
for all
i\in I
. By Lemma
4.4
, we conclude that
x=0
.
∎

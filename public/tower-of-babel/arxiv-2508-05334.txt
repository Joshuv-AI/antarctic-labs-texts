ShikkhaChain: A Blockchain-Powered Academic Credential Verification System for Bangladesh
Ahsan Farabi, Israt Khandaker, Nusrat Jahan, Ibrahim Khalil Shanto

Abstract
Academic credential fraud threatens educational integrity, especially in developing countries like Bangladesh, where verification methods are primarily manual and inefficient. To address this challenge, we present ShikkhaChain —a blockchain-powered certificate management platform designed to securely issue, verify, and revoke academic credentials in a decentralized and tamper-proof manner. Built on Ethereum smart contracts and utilizing IPFS for off-chain storage, the platform offers a transparent, scalable solution accessible through a React-based DApp with MetaMask integration. ShikkhaChain enables role-based access for governments, regulators, institutions, and public verifiers, allowing QR-based validation and on-chain revocation tracking. Our prototype demonstrates enhanced trust, reduced verification time, and improved international credibility for Bangladeshi degrees, promoting a more reliable academic and employment ecosystem.



Keywords:
Blockchain, Smart Contracts, Educational Certificates, IPFS, Verification System, Decentralized Applications

1
Introduction

Verifying academic credentials is an essential process in education and employment systems. However, traditional verification methods—particularly in developing countries like Bangladesh—are often manual, time-consuming, and vulnerable to forgery. The widespread use of paper-based certificates and the absence of centralized digital infrastructure have made it increasingly difficult to ensure the authenticity of academic records. This poses challenges for employers, institutions, and regulatory bodies. Recruitment is delayed due to slow verification, institutions risk damage from forged degrees, and unreliable verification methods erode trust in the education system.

Blockchain technology has emerged as a promising solution for this problem. Its core features—immutability, decentralization, and transparency—make it suitable for secure academic certificate issuance and verification. By storing certificate records on a distributed ledger and enabling validation through smart contracts, blockchain can reduce fraud and increase trust among all parties involved.

In this paper, we introduce
ShikkhaChain
, a blockchain-based academic credential management platform designed for Bangladesh. The system uses Ethereum smart contracts for issuing and revoking certificates, IPFS for decentralized off-chain storage, and a web-based decentralized application (DApp) for user interaction. Stakeholders such as government authorities, institutions, graduates, and employers can access and verify records through a secure, role-based interface. ShikkhaChain aims to modernize credential verification by offering a transparent, tamper-proof, and easily accessible platform aligned with national digital infrastructure goals.

3
System Architecture and Design

ShikkhaChain is designed as a decentralized platform for issuing, verifying, and revoking academic certificates using blockchain and Web3 technologies. The system is structured into five key layers to ensure modularity, scalability, and role-based access control. These layers enable secure interactions among different stakeholders, including government authorities, educational institutions, graduates, and employers.

Figure 1:
System Architecture of ShikkhaChain showing key layers, stakeholder roles, and blockchain integration.

3.1
1) Stakeholder Roles

The system defines four primary stakeholder roles:

•

Government:
Oversees the entire network, authorizes regulators, and monitors compliance.

•

Regulators:
Maintain a list of authorized educational institutions and ensure verification policies are enforced.

•

Institutions:
Issue and revoke academic certificates to graduates based on regulatory approval.

•

Public (Graduates and Employers):
Verify certificates instantly using certificate hash or QR code.

3.2
2) System Layers

1.

User Layer:
Browser-based access via MetaMask for all user types.

2.

Application Layer:
DApp built with React.js offering dashboards and blockchain interactions.

3.

Access Layer:
Middleware components like Web3.js, Infura, and MetaMask for blockchain connectivity.

4.

Blockchain Layer:
Smart contracts deployed on Ethereum handle logic for issuing, revoking, and verifying certificates.

5.

Off-chain Storage Layer:
Metadata stored on IPFS, linked by on-chain CIDs.

3.3
3) Smart Contract Design

Key Solidity functions:

•

issueCertificate()
: For authorized issuance of certificates.

•

verifyCertificate()
: Returns metadata and current status of a certificate.

•

revokeCertificate()
: Updates status of previously issued certificates.

3.4
4) Verification Process

Verification steps include:

•

User inputs certificate hash or scans a QR code.

•

Certificate metadata is retrieved from IPFS.

•

Smart contract validates certificate status.

•

DApp displays result and enables export as a signed PDF using jsPDF.

4
Results and Comparative Analysis

To evaluate the feasibility and usability of ShikkhaChain, a working prototype was developed and deployed using React.js for the frontend, Solidity smart contracts on the Ethereum test network, and IPFS for off-chain metadata storage. The system was tested using MetaMask for signing transactions.

4.1
1) Functional Outcomes

The system successfully demonstrates the complete lifecycle of academic certificates under a decentralized trust model:

•

Certificate Issuance:
Institutions could issue certificates by uploading metadata to IPFS. The corresponding CID was stored on-chain.

•

Certificate Verification:
Public users were able to verify certificate authenticity using hash lookup, confirming IPFS metadata and on-chain validity.

•

Revocation:
Authorized institutions could revoke issued certificates, which then displayed as invalid on the public verifier.

•

Export Feature:
Verified results could be exported as signed PDF reports using jsPDF.

4.3
3) Performance Insights

While the system demonstrates functional completeness, a few limitations were observed:

•

Transaction Time:
Certificate issuance and revocation required 12–25 seconds depending on Ethereum network congestion.

•

Gas Cost:
Average gas cost for issuing a certificate was 1289600 units.

•

IPFS Latency:
Retrieval from IPFS ranged from 1 to 5 seconds depending on pinning and gateway availability.

These limitations can be addressed in future work by integrating Layer 2 solutions or switching to a permissioned blockchain environment for institutional deployments.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we introduced
ShikkhaChain
, a blockchain-based decentralized certificate management system designed to enhance trust, transparency, and verification in academic credentialing. By leveraging Ethereum smart contracts, IPFS for off-chain storage, and MetaMask-enabled DApp interfaces, the system offers a scalable and tamper-proof solution that caters to multiple stakeholders, including government authorities, regulators, institutions, graduates, and employers.

Our implementation demonstrates successful issuance, revocation, and public verification of certificates with role-based access control and verifiable off-chain storage integrity. Compared to existing solutions, ShikkhaChain provides a more comprehensive architecture with fine-grained stakeholder management and seamless integration of Web3 tools.

Future work
will focus on the following enhancements:

•

Scalability:
Migration to Layer 2 blockchains (e.g., Polygon, Arbitrum) to reduce gas fees and latency.

•

Privacy:
Integration of zero-knowledge proofs (zk-SNARKs) to support privacy-preserving verification.

•

Mobile Support:
Extension of the DApp for mobile devices with QR scanning and offline access.

•

Interoperability:
Integration with national databases and e-Governance platforms via APIs.

•

Security Auditing:
Formal verification and third-party auditing of smart contract code.

Overall, ShikkhaChain contributes a modular and extensible foundation for national-level deployment of blockchain-enabled academic credential verification. It offers a scalable foundation for trust in national education systems, bridging policy with technology.

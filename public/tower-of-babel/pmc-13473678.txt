Mechanical responses and probabilistic sensitivity analysis of shield tunnel segment in water-rich soft strata
Authors: Xi Chen, Ling Zhang, Dengheng Zheng, Shijie Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLining segments of shield tunnels in water-rich soft strata face severe mechanical challenges from groundwater fluctuations and fluid-solid coupling. Taking the Liuyang River-crossing shield tunnel of Changsha Metro Line 3, this study establishes a 2D hydro-mechanical coupling finite element model using ANSYS to analyze segment mechanical responses (pore water pressure, stress, displacement) under four groundwater levels. Based on ANSYS/PDS, the Monte Carlo method is adopted for probabilistic sensitivity analysis involving nine random variables of surrounding rock and segment parameters, with 10,000 cyclic samplings. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis identifies key factors affecting segment internal forces. Results show that groundwater level dominates segment behavior. Peak pore water pressure, stress, and horizontal convergence of lining segment positively correlate with groundwater level, while vertical deformation negatively correlates. The outer ring is prone to tensile failure, with crown and haunches as key control positions. Increasing segment stiffness elevates internal forces, whereas higher surrounding rock elastic modulus reduces segment forces. Higher surrounding rock density and permeability coefficient increase segment loads. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis reveals that surrounding rock density dominates axial force, and lining thickness dominates bending moment. Surrounding rock cohesion and friction angle have limited effects. This study clarifies segment mechanical responses under variable groundwater levels and identifies key sensitive parameters, providing references for shield tunnel lining design in similar water-rich soft strata.Keywords: Shield tunnel, Lining segment, Fluid–solid coupling, Mechanical behaviors, Probabilistic sensitivity analysisSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Shield tunneling technology has become the core construction method for urban underground rail transit, municipal pipelines and cross-river/sea tunnel engineering due to its advantages of high construction efficiency, small ground disturbance and strong adaptability to complex strata1–3. With the rapid expansion of urban underground space development, an increasing number of shield tunnels are constructed in water-rich soft strata, where high groundwater pressure, weak stratum bearing capacity and large structural deformation induce complex hydro-mechanical coupling effects, posing severe challenges to the safety and durability of tunnel lining structures4–6. Lining segments, as the primary load-bearing and waterproof component of shield tunnels, their mechanical behavior and structural reliability directly determine the long-term stability of the entire tunnel engineering7–9. Once damage such as cracking, large structural deformation occurs in the segments, it will not only affect the normal operation of the tunnel but also may trigger serious engineering accidents such as stratum collapse and groundwater inrush, resulting in huge economic losses and social impacts10,11. Therefore, it is of great theoretical significance and engineering application value to carry out in-depth research on the mechanical behavior and reliability of shield tunnel lining segments in water-rich soft strata.

The tunnel structure in water-rich soft strata is subjected to the combined action of earth pressure, groundwater pressure, stratum self-weight and construction disturbance12,13, and the groundwater level fluctuation caused by seasonal rainfall, river water level change and human groundwater exploitation will further lead to the dynamic change of the load on the lining segment14,15. The hydro-mechanical coupling effect between groundwater and surrounding rock makes the mechanical response of the lining segment more complex: on the one hand, the pore water pressure in the stratum reduces the effective stress of the surrounding rock, weakens its self-bearing capacity, and increases the load acting on the segment16,17; on the other hand, the water pressure acting on the segment surface will cause additional structural stress and deformation, and even lead to the failure of the waterproof system at the segment joints, resulting in water seepage inside the tunnel18,19. In addition, the physical and mechanical parameters of water-rich soft strata and the geometric parameters and mechanical properties of the lining segment also have certain dispersibility in the construction process. These random factors will bring uncertainties to the structural performance of the segment, making the traditional deterministic structural design method difficult to fully reflect the actual safety state of the tunnel.

In soft strata, shield tunnel segments are subjected to greater internal forces, which can increase segment deformation and even lead to excessive cracking, thereby weakening structural integrity and reducing tunnel stability11. In water-rich strata, under the effect of fluid-solid coupling, the deformation of the surrounding rock is further intensified, which squeezes the segments and further impairs their integrity20,21. Therefore, many existing studies have focused on the mechanical performance of segments. Through laboratory tests, numerical simulations and other methods, these studies have investigated the mechanical behavior of segments under different loads and boundary conditions22,23, providing certain theoretical support for segment structural safety assessment.

As an important means for evaluating structural safety under uncertain conditions, probabilistic sensitivity analysis can quantitatively describe the influence of random factors on structural performance and identify key sensitive parameters24,25. The method has also been widely applied in the field of tunnel engineering to investigate the influence of random parameters on structures. Examples include the parameter sensitivity of tunnel faces26, support structures27, and surrounding rock28, as well as ground deformation29. Although previous studies have achieved many valuable results in probabilistic sensitivity analysis of tunnel engineering and provided theoretical support for engineering practice, the interaction between the research object and the surrounding environment should be fully considered in probabilistic sensitivity analysis to obtain more accurate results. In water-rich soft strata, segment structural safety is affected by multiple factors, such as the physical, mechanical and hydraulic properties of the surrounding rock, as well as the physical, mechanical properties and geometric dimensions of the segments. However, few studies have explored segment probabilistic sensitivity under the combined influence of multiple factors. Only by clarifying the influence of each factor on segment mechanical responses can a more solid theoretical foundation be provided for the design and optimization of shield tunnel segments.

The Liuyang River-crossing shield tunnel section of Changsha Metro Line 3 is taken as a typical case of shield tunneling in water-rich soft strata, which traverses complex geological conditions and is characterized by long-term water-rich conditions and obvious seasonal groundwater fluctuations, posing key technical challenges in engineering practice. In this study, a 2D fluid-solid coupling finite element model of the shield tunnel is established using ANSYS based on actual geological conditions. The mechanical responses of the lining segment under four typical groundwater levels are analyzed, including the distribution characteristics of pore water pressure, stress, and displacement fields. Using the ANSYS/PDS module, the Monte Carlo method is adopted to conduct probabilistic sensitivity analysis by considering 9 types of random variables related to surrounding rock properties and segment geometric and material parameters, with 10,000 cyclic sampling calculations. Statistical characteristics of internal forces are obtained through random sampling and iterative calculations. The influences of segment and surrounding rock parameters on internal forces are systematically investigated, and the variation law of structural performance with these parameters is revealed. Finally, probabilistic sensitivity analysis is performed to identify key random variables affecting the axial force and bending moment of the segment, and negligible parameters in engineering practice are determined. It is anticipated that these findings will furnish a theoretical foundation for the lining segment optimization and design of shield tunnels in water-rich soft strata.

Engineering background and numerical simulations

Engineering background

The section between East Martyrs Park Station and Simaochong Station of Changsha Metro Line 3 passes through Liuyang River, which is constructed by shield tunneling. The strata that the tunnel passes through are mainly miscellaneous fill, muddy silty clay, fine sand, pebble, strongly weathered slate and moderately weathered slate. The average buried depth of the tunnel is 14.2 m, with an outer shield diameter of 6.0 m and an inner diameter of 5.4 m. Each ring of the lining segments has a thickness of 0.3 m and a width of 1.5 m, and C50 high-strength concrete is adopted for the segment concrete.

Additionally, the shield tunnel passes through the Liuyang River section, and the strata is in a permanent water-rich state due to continuous recharge by river water, with the groundwater level reaching 13 m in depth. The Liuyang River has a historical maximum water level of 38.35 m, a historical minimum of approximately 22.29 m and an average annual water level of 30.08 m. The water level has a maximum variation range of 12.23 m and an average annual variation range of 8.19 m, exhibiting obvious seasonal fluctuations.

In the simulation, after the in-situ stress field is balanced, the elements in the tunnel excavation area are deleted, and the corresponding physical and mechanical parameters are assigned to the grouting layer and segment areas, until the calculation converges. Notably, the influence of fluid-solid coupling is taken into account in the calculation process. In the fluid-solid coupling simulation, the effective stress principle is applied to the surrounding rock, which follows the Mohr-Coulomb strength criterion. The pore water pressure obtained from the seepage analysis is subtracted from the total stress to obtain the effective stress field of the surrounding rock. The effective stress controls the deformation and failure of the surrounding rock, and the resulting contact pressure on the outer surface of the lining segment is then transmitted as the load boundary condition.

Probabilistic sensitivity simulation of lining segments

The probabilistic sensitivity analysis process is depicted in Fig. 2. The statistical analysis of the internal forces of tunnel linings is performed using the Monte Carlo method via the ANSYS, which integrates finite element analysis with probability theory. First, a command stream containing preprocessing (/PREP7), solution (/SOLU), and result extraction (*GET) modules are written in the APDL language to generate the .ma file required for probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Subsequently, the /PDS processor is activated, the generated .ma file is loaded, and the distribution types, correlations of input parameters, and output variables are defined to lay a solid foundation for subsequent analysis.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis flowchart.

In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis phase, the Monte Carlo method is utilized to generate a Loop File, which drives the Finite Element Model Database to perform batch calculations and realize the iterative process of “random parameter update – solution – result extraction”. Meanwhile, PDSAVE/PDRESU commands are employed to manage the Probabilistic Sensitivity File, ensuring the continuity of the analysis process. Finally, by sorting out the output results, the variation law of lining internal forces with the randomness of parameters, and the critical influence range of parameters are obtained, and key sensitive parameters are identified, providing a theoretical basis for the optimal design of tunnel linings.

When the probabilistic sensitivity simulation is carried out, the numerical model shown in Fig. 1 is adopted. In engineering practice, the overlying strata are heterogeneous and complex, and there are many random parameters. To further investigate the influence of physical and mechanical parameters on the structural response of lining segments and derive universal rules, the surrounding rock is regarded as a homogeneous material in the probabilistic sensitivity simulation. The rationale for this simplification is twofold: (1) it allows for 10,000 cyclic sampling calculations within a feasible computational timeframe, which would be prohibitively expensive for a heterogeneous multi-layer model; and (2) it enables a focused quantitative analysis of how each individual parameter affects segment responses. A total of 9 types of random variables are selected, including the unit weight, Poisson’s ratio, elastic modulus, cohesion, friction angle, permeability coefficient of surrounding rock, and the thickness and elastic modulus of lining segments. The lateral pressure coefficient is also considered as a random variable of the geostress field. Results and discussion

Mechanical behavior of lining segment

Pore water pressure field

The pore water pressure contour of the shield tunnel lining segments under four different groundwater level conditions are presented in Fig. 3. The pore water pressure distributions under all working conditions exhibit consistent radial and circumferential characteristics: the pore water pressure is mainly concentrated on the outer ring of the segments, while the pore water pressure on the inner ring is negligible (close to 0, approximately 0.947 × 10⁻¹² MPa as indicated by the color scale). This result verifies that the lining segments serve as an effective impermeable barrier, which can significantly prevent groundwater from penetrating into the tunnel interior.

Pore water pressure of lining segment: (a) Case 1, (b) Case 2, (c) Case 3, and (d) Case 4.

In terms of magnitude, the maximum pore water pressure is positively correlated with the groundwater level. Under the historical maximum water level condition, the peak pore water pressure reaches 0.951 MPa, whereas under the historical minimum water level condition, this value drops to only 0.374 MPa. The maximum pore water pressure under the recent average water level condition is 0.604 MPa, which is consistent with the elevation of the corresponding water level. This indicates that the variation of groundwater level is a dominant controlling factor for the pore water pressure distribution in the lining segments, with higher water levels will lead to a significant increase in the hydraulic load on the lining31.

At the same time, 16 characteristic points are selected at special positions (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°, 180°, − 45°, − 90°, − 135°) on the outer diameter of the tunnel lining segment for analysis, and the distribution of these characteristic points is shown in Fig. 4.

Distribution of characteristic points on the lining segment.

The pore water pressure at the characteristic points 1 ~ 8 is shown in Fig. 5. The results show that the pore water pressure at Point 5 is the highest and that at Point 4 is the second highest under all working conditions, while the lowest pore water pressure is observed at Point 1. The pore water pressure distribution is symmetrical about the vertical axis, with higher pressures concentrated in the lower part of the lining segment.

Pore water pressure at the characteristic points of the lining segment.

From an engineering perspective, the historical maximum water level represents the most critical loading scenario, in which the outer ring of the lining segments bears a water pressure of nearly 1 MPa. This requires a robust waterproofing design at the segment joints and high impermeability of the concrete to prevent water leakage and potential structural deterioration. On the contrary, under low water level conditions, the hydraulic load on the lining is significantly reduced, and the mechanical response of the segments is dominated by earth pressure. On the premise of ensuring the required safety margin, the structural design can be appropriately optimized. Therefore, the historical maximum water level should be taken as the extreme working condition for structural safety verification, while the recent average water level can be used as a reference for routine design and operation, to achieve a balance between engineering safety and economic efficiency.

Stress field

Figure 6 shows the distribution of the minimum principal stress on the outer (a) and inner (b) rings of the shield tunnel lining segment under four different groundwater level conditions. For the outer ring, tensile stress is observed at all points under Working Conditions 2 and 3, as well as at Points 3, 4, 6 and 7 under other conditions, meaning the lining segment is more prone to failure under these circumstances. This may be attributed to the pore water pressure difference between the outer and inner rings, which can cause additional deformation and thus induce tensile stress. For the inner ring, no tensile stress is detected, but obvious stress concentration occurs at the side positions (points 11, 15).

Figure 7 shows the distribution of the maximum principal stress on the outer (a) and inner (b) rings of the shield tunnel lining segment under four different groundwater level conditions. The stress level of the outer ring is slightly lower than that of the inner ring. Similar to the minimum principal stress field, obvious stress concentration is observed at the side positions (points 11, 15).

Maximum principal stress of lining segment: (a) Outer ring, (b) Inner ring.

In general, the stress of the lining segment is positively correlated with the groundwater level: the highest stress on both rings is observed under Working Condition 1, the lowest under Working Condition 2, and the stress under Working Conditions 3 and 4 falls in between. This indicates that the rise of groundwater level will significantly increase the stress of the lining segment, making the high water level condition a critical factor for controlling the structural mechanical response32. The outer ring bears most of the external loads, while the inner ring plays a secondary role in load bearing and impermeability. The stress concentration at the inner ring side positions means that this part is a key area for structural reinforcement, and it is necessary to optimize the reinforcement design to avoid local structural damage caused by excessive stress.

Displacement field

The displacement responses of the shield tunnel lining segments under four groundwater level conditions exhibit distinct and consistent patterns in both horizontal and vertical directions, as shown in Fig. 8. In the vertical direction, all 16 characteristic points show negative displacement values under all working conditions, indicating that the lining segments undergo overall vertical downward settlement. The magnitude of vertical settlement is negatively correlated with the groundwater level: the minimum settlement is observed under Working Condition 1, with the maximum settlement value of 2.35 mm at Point 9 and the minimum of 1.54 mm at Point 5. In contrast, the most significant settlement occurs under Working Condition 2, with the peak settlement of 4.19 mm at Points 1 and 9 and the minimum of 2.98 mm at Points 5 and 13. The settlement under Working Conditions 3 and 4 is at an intermediate level, which is consistent with their corresponding groundwater levels. In the circumferential direction, vertical settlement is concentrated at Points 1 and 9 and gradually decreases toward Points 5 and 13, which is due to the greater overburden pressure and self-weight acting on the crown, while the invert is constrained by a stronger foundation reaction force.

Displacement of lining segment: (a) Horizontal displacement, (b) Vertical displacement.

In the horizontal direction, the displacement distribution presents a symmetrical characteristic of “convergence toward the tunnel center”: the left side of the segment ring shows positive displacement values and the right side shows negative values. The maximum horizontal displacement occurs at Points 3, 7, 11 and 15, with the highest value of 0.37 mm at Point 7 under Working Condition 1, while the horizontal deformation at other positions is relatively small. Different from vertical settlement, horizontal displacement is positively correlated with the groundwater level: the most significant horizontal displacement is observed under Working Condition 1, while it is nearly negligible under Working Condition 2. This phenomenon reflects the dominant role of hydrostatic pressure in lateral loading: high groundwater levels enhance the lateral squeezing effect of the surrounding rock on the lining, resulting in obvious horizontal convergence; on the contrary, low water levels reduce hydrostatic pressure, weaken the lateral constraint of the surrounding rock, and thus minimize the transverse deformation of the segments.

The differential displacement responses to groundwater level changes reveal the dual control mechanism of water pressure on the lining structure and highlight the high sensitivity of segment deformation to hydrological conditions33,34. From the engineering application perspective, Working Condition 1 is the most unfavorable condition for horizontal convergence, as the elevated hydrostatic pressure causes significant transverse deformation at the haunches, which is a key region for structural stiffness and waterproofing design. On the other hand, Working Condition 2 is the critical condition for vertical settlement control, where the increase in the effective stress of the surrounding rock leads to excessive downward deformation of the crown. For Working Conditions 3 and 4, the displacement patterns are consistent with those under the extreme conditions but with a smaller magnitude, indicating that the lining is in a balanced deformation state during normal operation.

These findings highlight the necessity of considering extreme groundwater level scenarios in tunnel design and safety assessment. The crown (Points 1, 9) needs to be reinforced to resist excessive settlement, particularly under low water level conditions; while the haunches (Points 3, 7, 11, 15) require enhanced joint waterproofing and structural rigidity to adapt to the horizontal convergence caused by high hydrostatic pressure. During the operation phase, real-time monitoring of the groundwater level is essential: an abrupt drop in the groundwater level should trigger focused monitoring of crown settlement, and a sudden rise requires close observation of haunch displacement and joint seepage performance, so as to ensure the long-term stability and durability of the tunnel lining.

Comparison with field monitoring data

During tunnel construction, the deformation of the surrounding rock is monitored at four key points: the crown, haunches (left and right), and floor, corresponding to points 1, 3, 5, and 7, respectively. The deformation monitoring data in stable stage at mileages Z(Y)DK26 + 411 and Z(Y)DK26 + 435 are compared with the numerical simulation results, as shown in Fig. 9. In Fig. 9, displacements toward the inside of the tunnel are defined as positive.

Displacement comparison with field monitoring data.

All curves, regardless of simulation or monitoring, exhibit a consistent bimodal distribution pattern: the maximum displacement occurs at the tunnel crown, followed by a secondary peak at the invert (floor), while the haunch positions (both right and left) show significantly smaller deformations, with the right haunch recording the lowest displacement across the section. The close alignment of the overall trend, peak locations, and relative magnitude order between the simulated and measured data indicates that the numerical model effectively captures the fundamental deformation characteristics of the tunnel section under real-world conditions.

While minor numerical discrepancies exist between the simulated and monitored results, these can be reasonably attributed to two primary factors: dynamic fluctuations in river water depth, and simplifications in the numerical model (e.g., idealized boundary conditions and homogeneous material assumptions) that neglect certain local heterogeneities and discontinuities in the field. Nevertheless, the strong agreement in both the trend and relative magnitude of displacements confirms the reliability and accuracy of the proposed numerical simulation.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis of lining segment

The internal forces of the lining segments are obtained after 10,000 calculation iterations. The axial force of the lining segments follows a normal distribution, while the bending moment obeys a lognormal distribution. For the bending moment, tension on the segment is defined as positive and compression as negative; similarly, for the axial force, tension of the segment is defined as positive and compression as negative. The confidence interval reflects the credibility of the mean value, with a confidence level of 0.95, which can basically meet the design and calculation requirements of tunnel engineering. Mean value of internal forces: (a) Axial force and (b) Bending moment.

As shown in Fig. 11(a), all axial force values are negative, indicating that the lining segments are subjected to dominant compressive forces along the entire circumference. The magnitude of axial compression shows an obvious symmetrical pattern: it starts at a minimum of -427.83 kN at the crown (Point 1), gradually increases along the ring, reaches a peak of -980.81 kN at Point 10 (the sidewall position), and then decreases symmetrically toward the opposite crown (Point 19, -511.25 kN). This distribution law is consistent with the mechanical behavior of circular linings, where the sidewall regions bear the maximum thrust from overburden pressure and lateral earth pressure. The statistical variability of axial force, measured by the CV, ranges from 0.277 to 0.355, showing a clear trend of increasing from the crown to the sidewall positions. The lowest CV values at Points 1 and 19 (0.277) indicate that the compressive behavior at the crown is stable, while the highest CV values at Points 9 and 10 (0.355) suggest that the sidewall regions are more sensitive to load fluctuations. The narrow confidence intervals of all axial force measurements further verify the reliability of the mean values, confirming that the distribution of compressive forces at each monitoring point is consistent and predictable.

The distribution of bending moments, shown in Fig. 11(b), presents an opposite circumferential pattern with distinct sign transitions, reflecting the variation in the flexural behavior of the lining. Positive bending moments (16.09 ~ 2.97 kN·m) are observed at Points 1 to 5 (crown to upper springline), indicating tension in the inner fiber of the segment; negative bending moments (-3.17~-15.86 kN·m) dominate at Points 6 to 14 (lower springline to sidewall), corresponding to tension in the outer fiber. The bending moment magnitude reaches a peak of -15.86 kN·m at Point 10, which is consistent with the position of the maximum axial compression, and decreases to nearly zero at the springline points (Points 5 and 15). Notably, the CV of the bending moment shows extreme values at the springline transition points: the CV at Point 5 is 1.517 and that at Point 15 reaches 1.847, which are much higher than the CV values at other positions. This high variability indicates that the springline regions are highly sensitive to minor changes in loading conditions, making them prone to unexpected reversals of bending moment.

The combined distribution of axial force and bending moment highlight critical structural regions for the tunnel lining. The sidewall points (9 and 10) are the most critical sections, as they experience both the maximum axial compression and the largest absolute bending moments, creating a demanding combined stress state for the concrete material. Although the springline points (5 and 15) have small mean bending moments, their exceptionally high CV values necessitate careful attention in probabilistic sensitivity analysis and monitoring, as they represent potential weak points under variable loading. Overall, the narrow confidence intervals for all internal force parameters demonstrate the statistical robustness of the results, providing a solid basis for the structural design and safety assessment of the shield tunnel lining.

Segment variable effects on its internal forces

Based on the internal force characteristics analyzed in 3.2.1, the peak values of the internal force appear at Points 1 and 10, corresponding to the crown and haunch of the segment respectively. To further clarify the influence of segment self-parameters on its mechanical response, the curves of the influence of segment elastic modulus and lining thickness on internal force are drawn, as shown in Fig. 12 below. The solid lines represent the mean values, and the light-colored bands represent the 95% confidence intervals. As shown in Fig. 12, the 95% confidence intervals are very narrow for all monitoring points, indicating that the results of the Monte Carlo simulations are stable and have low uncertainty. The clear trends of axial force and bending moment with variation of segment variables are statistically reliable. Notably, axial force is negative, indicating compression, and the increase in the absolute value of the negative value signifies a rise in axial compressive stress. Bending moment is negative, corresponding to compression at the outer fiber of the segment, while a positive bending moment indicates tension at the inner fiber.

The influence of segment parameters on its internal force: (a) Elastic modulus and (b) Lining thickness.

As illustrated in Fig. 12(a), the axial force at Point 1 shows an increasing trend from approximately 350 to 480 kN with increasing elastic modulus, while its bending moment shows an increasing trend from approximately 9 to 23 kN·m. The axial force at Point 10 shows an increasing trend from approximately 860 to 1040 kN with increasing elastic modulus, but its bending moment shows a decreasing trend from approximately 9 kN·m to 24 kN·m. The increase in the segment’s elastic modulus elevates axial force and bending moment by enhancing material stiffness, which reshapes the load-sharing behavior between the lining and surrounding rock. A higher elastic modulus reduces the segment’s deformability, making it less capable of accommodating ground pressure through deformation35. As a result, the stiffer lining assumes a larger share of the external load instead of transferring it to the surrounding rock, leading to a direct rise in axial compression. Simultaneously, the constrained flexural deformation due to increased stiffness requires a larger internal bending moment to counteract the external bending effect, thus increasing the bending moment at critical sections.

Figure 12(b) presents the influence of lining thickness on internal forces, revealing trends consistent with those of the elastic modulus. The axial force at Point 1 shows an increasing trend from approximately 360 kN to 490 kN with increasing lining thickness, and its bending moment shows an increasing trend from approximately 8 to 31 kN·m. The axial force at Point 10 shows an increasing trend from approximately 880 to 1050 kN with increasing lining thickness, and its bending moment also shows an increasing trend from approximately 9 kN·m to 33 kN·m. Increasing lining thickness boosts axial force and bending moment by improving the section modulus and overall structural rigidity of the segment. A thicker cross-section significantly enhances the lining’s resistance to deformation, reducing its ability to dissipate external loads through slight deformation. This forces the segment to bear a greater proportion of the surrounding rock pressure directly, resulting in a notable increase in axial force. Additionally, the thicker section elevates the flexural stiffness of the lining ring; to maintain equilibrium against external bending loads, a larger internal bending moment is generated within the segment, leading to the observed rise in bending moment values.

The analysis summarized in Fig. 12 clarifies two core design principles for shield tunnel lining segments. First, increasing stiffness via a higher elastic modulus or greater lining thickness effectively concentrates axial compression at the crown, which is beneficial for utilizing the compressive bearing capacity of concrete (a favorable stress state for concrete structures). Second, it inevitably reduces the bending demand at the sidewall sections, lowering the risk of tensile cracking at the inner fiber caused by positive bending moments and compressive damage at the outer fiber caused by negative bending moments. In engineering design, the stiffness of the segment can be reasonably optimized according to the actual water level conditions and stratum characteristics to achieve the balance between structural safety and economic efficiency.

The solid lines show the mean values, with light-colored bands representing the 95% confidence intervals. The narrow confidence intervals in Fig. 13 demonstrate that the Monte Carlo simulation results are stable and exhibit low uncertainty, confirming the statistical reliability of the trends in axial force and bending moment. Because the cohesion and internal friction angle of the surrounding rock have little effect on the internal force of the segment through preliminary analysis, the analysis is no longer carried out for these two parameters. The results reveal the distinct sensitivity of internal force distributions to changes in geomechanical properties, providing critical data for engineering risk assessment.

As depicted in Fig. 13(a), the axial force at Point 1 shows a slight decreasing trend from approximately 530 to 390 kN with increasing elastic modulus, and its bending moment also shows a decreasing trend from approximately 24 to 9 kN·m (tensile effect). The axial force at Point 10 shows a slight decreasing trend from approximately 1080 to 910 kN with increasing elastic modulus, and its bending moment also shows a decreasing trend from approximately 24 to 9 kN·m. The reduction in absolute value of axial force and bending moment with higher rock elastic modulus implies that stiffer surrounding rock has stronger self-bearing capacity, which can bear more external loads by itself, thus reducing the axial load and bending load on the segment and effectively enhancing the structural safety of the segment.

As illustrated in Fig. 13(b), the axial force at Point 1 shows an increasing trend from approximately 390 kN to 490 kN with increasing Poisson’s ratio, while its bending moment also shows a decreasing trend from approximately 19 kN·m to 11 kN·m. The axial force at Point 10 shows an increasing trend from approximately 950 kN to 1030 kN with increasing Poisson’s ratio, but its bending moment shows a decreasing trend from approximately 19 kN·m to 11 kN·m. These evolution trends indicate that a more compressible surrounding rock (higher Poisson’s ratio) is more likely to deform under load, but weakens the lateral constraint of the rock on the lining. Thus, it is easier to transfer axial load and reduce the bending load. The segment is mainly made of concrete, and the compressive performance of concrete is much greater than its tensile performance. Therefore, the influence of bending moment on the structural safety of the segment is slightly greater than that of axial force. Although the increase of Poisson’s ratio of surrounding rock will lead to the increase of axial force of segment, the decrease of bending moment will improve its structural safety. However, this is not absolute. When the axial force exceeds a certain threshold, the segment will also be damaged due to excessive compression.

As shown in Fig. 13(c), the axial force at Point 1 shows an increasing trend from approximately 580 to 660 kN with increasing density, while its bending moment also shows a rising trend from approximately 13 to 23 kN·m. The axial force at Point 10 shows an increasing trend from approximately 890 to 1000 kN with increasing density, but its bending moment shows a rising trend from approximately 13 to 23 kN·m. This indicates that the greater the density of the surrounding rock, the more unfavorable the structural safety of the segment. The increase in absolute axial force and bending moment are directly driven by the self-weight of surrounding rock—higher density means greater overburden pressure, which directly imposes a heavier load on the segment, thereby increasing the axial force and bending moment.

As illustrated in Fig. 13(d), the axial force at Point 1 shows an increasing trend from approximately 370 to 530 kN with increasing permeability coefficient, while its bending moment also shows a rising trend from approximately 13 to 21 kN·m. The axial force at Point 10 shows an increasing trend from approximately 700 to 1000 kN with increasing permeability coefficient, but its bending moment shows a rising trend from approximately 13 to 21 kN·m. Which indicates that the greater the permeability coefficient of the surrounding rock, the more unfavorable the structural safety of the segment. The higher permeability increases the pore water pressure in the rock, enhancing the hydrostatic load, thereby increasing the axial force and bending moment of the segment.

In practical projects, targeted engineering measures should be taken according to the physical and mechanical characteristics of the surrounding rock: for rock formations with high stiffness (high elastic modulus, low Poisson’s ratio), attention should be paid to mitigating bending demand of the segment at the crown; for rock with high density, reinforcement measures such as increasing the segment reinforcement ratio and optimizing the segment cross-section should be adopted to resist excessive axial compression; for high-permeability formations, enhanced drainage systems should be paired with structural reinforcement to reduce the pore water pressure and resist elevated axial and bending stresses.

Limitations and prospects

Although this study provides valuable insights into the mechanical responses and probabilistic sensitivity of shield tunnel linings in water-rich soft strata, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, a 2D plane-strain model is adopted, which cannot capture the longitudinal variation of ground conditions and the three-dimensional effect of shield advancement. Second, and more importantly, the lining is modeled as a continuous homogeneous ring, ignoring the segmentation and the presence of circumferential and longitudinal joints. Segmental joints affect the internal force distribution and serve as the primary pathways for water infiltration, thereby altering the local pore water pressure field around the tunnel. The current simplified model may therefore underestimate the bending moment at joint sections and overestimate the overall impermeability of the lining36,37. Third, the surrounding rock is assumed to be homogeneous and isotropic. While this assumption is necessary for the probabilistic sensitivity analysis involving 10,000 iterative calculations, it neglects the spatial variability and interlayer interactions of real strata.

To address these limitations, future work will focus on the following aspects: (1) Establishing a refined numerical model that explicitly incorporates segmental joints with rotational and shear stiffness properties, to systematically analyze the influence of joint stiffness and position on the internal forces of the lining under different groundwater levels. (2) Introducing joint leakage elements into the fluid-solid coupling framework to simulate preferential water inflow through joints and to quantify the resulting non-uniform pore water pressure distribution around the tunnel. (3) Performing probabilistic sensitivity analysis on the joint parameters themselves (e.g., joint stiffness, gasket properties) to identify the most critical factors affecting segmental joint performance in water-rich soft strata. (4) Developing a 3D hydro-mechanical coupling model to characterize spatial and longitudinal interaction mechanisms, and conducting probabilistic sensitivity analysis that considers stratum heterogeneity (e.g., multi-layer strata).

Conclusions

Based on the Liuyang River-crossing shield tunnel of Changsha Metro Line 3, a 2D hydro-mechanical coupling finite element model was established via ANSYS to analyze the segment mechanical responses under four groundwater level conditions, and Monte Carlo-based probabilistic sensitivity analysis was conducted on the ANSYS/PDS platform with 9 random variables and 10,000 cyclic sampling calculations to clarify the parameter effects on segment internal forces. The main conclusions are as follows:

Groundwater level is a dominant control factor for segment mechanical responses: peak pore water pressure, structural stress and horizontal convergence increase with rising water levels, while vertical settlement shows the opposite trend; the historical maximum and minimum water levels are the critical conditions for horizontal convergence and vertical settlement control respectively.

Segment mechanical responses have distinct circumferential characteristics: pore water pressure concentrates on the outer ring’s lower part; the outer ring is prone to tensile stress under medium and low water levels, with inner ring side stress concentration; the crown (vertical settlement) and haunches (horizontal displacement) are key reinforcement and monitoring positions.

Surrounding rock and segment parameters exert disparate effects on structural safety: increasing segment elastic modulus and thickness enhances stiffness and elevates internal forces, which gives play to concrete’s compressive performance; higher surrounding rock elastic modulus reduces segment internal forces and improves safety, while the rise of its unit weight and permeability coefficient significantly increases segment loads and impairs safety.

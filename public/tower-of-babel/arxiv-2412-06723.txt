Universal chain rules from entropic triangle inequalities
Ashutosh Marwah and Frédéric Dupuis

Abstract
The von Neumann entropy of an n n -partite system A 1 n A_{1}^{n} given a system B B can be written as the sum of the von Neumann entropies of the individual subsystems A k A_{k} given A 1 k − 1 A_{1}^{k-1} and B B . While it is known that such a chain rule does not hold for the smooth min-entropy, we prove a counterpart of this for a variant of the smooth min-entropy, which is equal to the conventional smooth min-entropy up to a constant. This enables us to lower bound the smooth min-entropy of an n n -partite system in terms of, roughly speaking, equally strong entropies of the individual subsystems. We call this a universal chain rule for the smooth min-entropy, since it is applicable for all values of n n . Using duality, we also derive a similar relation for the smooth max-entropy. Our proof utilises the entropic triangle inequality based technique developed in [ MD23 ] for analysing approximation chains. Additionally, we also prove an approximate version of the entropy accumulation theorem, which significantly relaxes the conditions required on the state to bound its smooth min-entropy. In particular, it does not require the state to be produced through a sequential process like previous entropy accumulation type bounds. In our upcoming companion paper [ MD24a ] , we use it to prove the security of parallel device independent quantum key distribution.

2.3
Key lemmas and theorems

In this section, we state some important results, which we use throughout this paper. As stated in the introduction, the entropic triangle inequalities developed in
[
MD23
]
are central to analysing approximation chains and hence to this work. The following lemma states the two inequalities we use.

Lemma 2.1

(Entropic triangle inequality
[
MD23
, Introduction and Lemma 3.5]
)
.

For two states
\rho_{AB}
and
\eta_{AB}
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq H_{\min}(A|B)_{\eta}-D^{\epsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||\eta_{AB}).

(43)

Further, for
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\epsilon+\delta<1
, we also have

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon+\delta}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||\eta_{AB})-\frac{g_{1}(\delta,\epsilon)}{\alpha-1}

(44)

where
g_{1}(x,y):=-\log(1-\sqrt{1-x^{2}})-\log(1-y^{2})
.

The quantum substate theorem stated below is one of the major results used to bound the smooth max-relative entropy in this paper. This theorem serves as a tool to convert bounds on the measured relative entropy to bounds on the smooth max-relative entropy.

Theorem 2.2

(Quantum substate theorem
[
JRS02
,
JN11
]
)
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be two normalised states on the same Hilbert space. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\sqrt{\epsilon}}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\leq\frac{D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)+1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}.

(45)

Usually, the above theorem is stated with the relative entropy
D(\rho||\sigma)
on the right-hand side instead of the measured relative entropy
D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)
. Since, in the proofs in this paper we are only able to derive bounds on the measured relative entropy, we need the stronger version stated above. It is, therefore, instructive to understand how it is possible to use
D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)
in the bound above.

[
JRS02
,
JN11
]
actually prove a bound on
D^{\sqrt{\epsilon}}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)
in terms of a divergence they call the
observational divergence

D_{obs}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle D_{obs}(\rho||\sigma):=\sup\left\{\tr(P\rho)\log\frac{\tr(P\rho)}{\tr(P\sigma)}:\text{ for }0\leq P\leq\Id\right\}.

(46)

[
JN11
, Theorem 1]
proves that

\displaystyle D^{\sqrt{\epsilon}}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\leq\frac{D_{\text{obs}}(\rho||\sigma)}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}.

(47)

Observe that for any binary measurement
(P,\Id-P)
, we have

\displaystyle D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\tr(P\rho)\log\frac{\tr(P\rho)}{\tr(P\sigma)}+(1-\tr(P\rho))\log\frac{1-\tr(P\rho)}{1-\tr(P\sigma)}

\displaystyle\geq\tr(P\rho)\log\frac{\tr(P\rho)}{\tr(P\sigma)}+(1-\tr(P\rho))\log\left(1-\tr(P\rho)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tr(P\rho)\log\frac{\tr(P\rho)}{\tr(P\sigma)}-1

which implies, that

\displaystyle D_{obs}(\rho||\sigma)\leq D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)+1.

(48)

This allows us to use
D_{m}(\rho||\sigma)
in the bound for the substate theorem.

In addition to the quantum substate theorem, the following generalisation of the Golden-Thompson (GT) inequality is another major result enabling the proof approach used in this paper.

Theorem 2.3

(Generalised Golden-Thompson (GT) Inequality
[
SBT17
]
)
.

For a collection of Hermitian matrices
\{H_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\tr\exp{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\right)}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\tr\left(e^{H_{n}}e^{\frac{1-it}{2}H_{n-1}}\cdots\ e^{\frac{1-it}{2}H_{2}}e^{H_{1}}e^{\frac{1+it}{2}H_{2}}\cdots\ e^{\frac{1+it}{2}H_{n-1}}\right)

(49)

where
\beta_{0}(t):=\frac{\pi}{2}(\cosh(\pi t)+1)^{-1}
is a probability density function.

Proof.

Using
[
SBT17
, Corollary 3.3]
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left\|\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}H_{k}\right)\right\|_{2}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\log\left\|\prod_{k=1}^{n}\exp\left(\frac{1+it}{2}H_{k}\right)\right\|_{2}.

(50)

Expanding the norm gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\tr\left(\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}\right)\right)\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\frac{1}{2}\log\tr\left(e^{H_{n}}e^{\frac{1-it}{2}H_{n-1}}\cdots e^{\frac{1-it}{2}H_{2}}e^{H_{1}}e^{\frac{1+it}{2}H_{2}}\cdots e^{\frac{1+it}{2}H_{n-1}}\right).

(51)

Using the concavity and monotonicity of
\log
, we get the statement in the Theorem.
∎

The GT inequality above is often used in conjunction with the following variational expressions for the relative entropies. This is also the case in this paper.

Lemma 2.4

For a normalised state

\displaystyle D(\rho||Q)=\sup_{\omega>0}\left\{\tr(\rho\log\omega)+1-\tr\exp(\log Q+\log\omega)\right\}

(53)

We will also use the following lemma, which is a quantum generalisation of the fact that if two probability distributions

are close to each other, then the probability distributions

Lemma 2.5

For a normalised state

Note that since
\rho_{AB}

\ket{\tilde{\rho}}_{ABR}
be an arbitrary purification of
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}

\ket{\eta}:=\rho_{B}^{1/2}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}\ket{\tilde{\rho}}_{ABR}
is a purification of

Let

[

\displaystyle F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\geq|\braket{\tilde{\rho}|\eta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\braket{\tilde{\rho}|\rho_{B}^{1/2}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}|\tilde{\rho}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq F(\tilde{\rho}_{B},\rho_{B})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 1-2\epsilon^{2}

\displaystyle P(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

Using the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle P({\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq P({\rho}_{AB},\tilde{\rho}_{AB})+P(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

3
A universal chain rule for smooth min-entropy

In this section, we will prove the universal chain rule for
H^{\downarrow,\epsilon}_{\min}
. Specifically, we will show that for a state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
,

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow,g_{1}(\epsilon)}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{\downarrow,\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-ng_{2}(\epsilon)-k(\epsilon),

(54)

where
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are small functions of
\epsilon
(
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are continuous and tend to
0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
) and
k
is a general function of
\epsilon
. It should be noted that these functions may depend on
|A|
, which is the size of the individual registers
A_{k}
. We begin by sketching the proof of such a chain rule in the classical case first. This will be beneficial for understanding the challenges that need to be solved in order to prove the statement in the quantum case. We will then generalise this proof to the quantum case. In Sec.
5
, we provide an alternate proof for this chain rule. We compare the techniques used to prove this chain rule with the previous chain rules and their proofs in Appendix
B
.

3.1
Proof sketch for classical distributions

Consider the probability distribution
p_{A_{1}^{n}B}
. We will sketch a proof for a chain rule of the form in Eq.
54
for
\rho=p
. Let us first broadly describe the proof strategy. We will identify an auxiliary distribution
p^{(\text{aux})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}
, such that

\displaystyle D^{g_{1}(\epsilon)}_{\max}(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||p^{(\text{aux})}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\leq ng_{2}(\epsilon)+k(\epsilon)\text{ and }

(55)

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p^{(\text{aux})}}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}

(56)

where
g_{1},g_{2}
and
k
are as in Eq.
54
. Then, we can simply use the entropic triangle inequality to show that

\displaystyle H^{g_{1}(\epsilon)}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p}

\displaystyle\geq H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p^{(\text{aux})}}-D_{\max}^{g_{1}(\epsilon)}(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||p^{(\text{aux})}_{A_{1}^{n}B})

(57)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}-ng_{2}(\epsilon)-k(\epsilon).

(58)

p^{(\text{aux})}
will be defined using the distributions which achieve
H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}
. For
k\in[n]
, let
q^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
be the distribution, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A_{1}^{k}B}-q^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

(59)

\displaystyle H^{\epsilon,\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}=H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q^{(k)}}

(60)

It is easy to show that we also have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A_{1}^{k}B}-p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon.

(61)

We can define an auxiliary distribution as
q_{A_{1}^{n}B}:=p_{B}\prod_{k=1}^{n}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
. For this distribution, the conditional min-entropy satisfies (Eq.
29
),

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q}=H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q^{(k)}}=H^{\epsilon,\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}

(62)

for every
k
since
q_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}=q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
. Further, we can bound the min-entropy for this distribution as desired in Eq.
56
as

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{q}\geq H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{q}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q}

(63)

Next, we need to ensure that the smooth max-divergence between
p
and
q
is relatively small. Our strategy will be to bound the relative entropy between these two distributions and use the substate theorem to convert that to a bound for the smooth max-divergence. We expect to be able to use the following chain rule for the relative entropy

\displaystyle D(p_{A_{1}^{k}B}||q_{A_{1}^{k}B})=D(p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}||q_{A_{1}^{k-1}B})+D(p_{A_{1}^{k}B}||p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}q_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B})

(64)

to prove that the relative entropy distance between
p_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and
q_{A_{1}^{n}B}
is small. Using this repeatedly along with the fact that
q_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}=q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
, gives us

\displaystyle D(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||q_{A_{1}^{n}B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}D(p_{A_{1}^{k}B}||p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}).

(65)

If we could now show that each term inside the summation is some small function in
\epsilon
,
g(\epsilon)
, we could show that the
D(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||q_{A_{1}^{n}B})
is bounded by
ng(\epsilon)
. Eq.
61
shows that the two distributions in each of the terms are close to each other. However, the relative entropy between them need not be small or even bounded, since it could be that
p_{A_{1}^{k}B}\not\ll p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
. To circumvent this technicality, we need to
massage
the distributions
q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
by a small amount. This is done by mixing these distributions with the uniform distribution
u_{A_{k}}
to produce the distributions

\displaystyle r^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}=(1-\delta)q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}+\delta u_{A_{k}}.

(66)

For these distributions, we can show that (Lemma
C.2
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A_{1}^{k}B}-p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}r^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon+\delta

(67)

and

\displaystyle D(p_{A_{1}^{k}B}||p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}r^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B})\leq z(\epsilon,\delta)

(68)

where
z(\epsilon,\delta)=O\left((\epsilon+\delta)\log\frac{|A|}{\epsilon\delta}\right)
. Note that
z(\epsilon,\delta)
can be made
O\left(\epsilon\log\frac{|A|}{\epsilon}\right)
for
\delta=\epsilon
. Instead of using
q_{A_{1}^{n}B}
as the auxiliary distribution, we can use
r_{A_{1}^{n}B}:=p_{B}\prod_{k=1}^{n}r^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
. We can use the same argument as above to bound the relative entropy between
p_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and
r_{A_{1}^{n}B}
. This gives us

\displaystyle D(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||r_{A_{1}^{n}B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}D(p_{A_{1}^{k}B}||p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}r^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B})

\displaystyle\leq nz(\epsilon,\delta).

(69)

Let
\mu:={z(\epsilon,\delta)}^{1/3}
, then using the substate theorem, we have

\displaystyle D^{\mu}_{\max}(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||r_{A_{1}^{n}B})\leq n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}.

Using the entropic triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mu}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p}

\displaystyle\geq H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{r}-n\mu-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{r}-n\mu-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q^{(k)}}-n\mu-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}-n\mu-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

where in the third line, we use the quasi-concavity of
H_{\min}^{\downarrow}
[
Tom16
, Pg 73]
.

Thus, we have proven a chain rule for
H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}
of the desired form in the classical case. The primary challenge in generalizing this approach to the quantum setting lies in defining the auxiliary state. In the classical case, we could use the product of (suitably massaged) conditional distributions
q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}
to define the auxiliary distribution
r_{A_{1}^{n}B}
. However, the quantum generalisation of such a distribution is not unique. Moreover, such generalisations are all quite difficult to manipulate. A closely related problem is that we need to be able to prove a relative entropy bound similar to that in Eq.
69
for the auxiliary state. This also turns out to be quite challenging, especially because the quantum chain rules
[
SBT17

for the relative entropy do not yield something as simple and convenient as Eq.

\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and an unwieldy, yet simple exponential generalisation of the auxiliary probability distribution used above. Then, in order to convert this bound to a (measured) relative entropy bound between the state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and a simpler and more convenient auxiliary state, we employ the Generalised GT inequality (Lemma
3.1
). Through this approach, we are able to delegate the pesky question of the correct generalisation of the auxiliary state to the GT inequality.

4.1
Dependence of smoothing parameter on the approximation parameter

It is evident that the smoothing parameter must depend on the approximation parameter,
\epsilon
, for the unstructured approximate EAT in its current form. To illustrate this, consider a distribution
p_{A_{1}^{n}B}
where
B=1
with probability
(1-\epsilon)
and
B=0
otherwise. In this distribution,
A_{1}^{n}
is sampled uniformly at random from
\{0,1\}^{n}
if
B=1
, and otherwise set to a constant string. For every
k
, this distribution satisfies

\displaystyle p_{A_{1}^{k}B}\approx_{O(\epsilon)}\Delta(p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B})

(139)

where
\Delta
is a channel that disregards its input and uniformly samples a bit
A_{k}
. Thus, this distribution meets the requirements for the unstructured approximate EAT. However, for this distribution,
H^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p}=n
, but for any
\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\epsilon/2
, we have
H^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p}=O(\log 1/\epsilon)
. This example demonstrates the necessity of the smoothing parameter’s dependence on
\epsilon
.

Nevertheless, it appears possible to decouple this dependence in certain interesting cases, such as sequential DIQKD with leakage and parallel DIQKD. It seems that the smoothing parameter’s dependence on
\epsilon
is required to remove the case of “correlated failure”. In the aforementioned example, it is necessary to exclude the case where
B=0
and
A_{1}^{n}
are perfectly determined (highly correlated). Consider, however, the state
\rho_{X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}
produced in a DIQKD protocol with imperfections or leakages (
X_{1}^{n}
and
Y_{1}^{n}
represent Alice and Bob’s questions,
A_{1}^{n}
and
B_{1}^{n}
their answers, and
E
the adversary’s register), where for each
k

\displaystyle\rho_{X_{1}^{k}Y_{1}^{k}A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\approx_{\epsilon}\cM_{k}\left(\rho_{X_{1}^{k-1}Y_{1}^{k-1}A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E}^{(k,0)}\right)

(140)

for a channel
\cM_{k}
, which plays the CHSH game between Alice and Bob, as one would expect in DIQKD with no leakage. We can use the unstructured EAT to prove that the entropy of the answers with respect to the questions and
E
is large for such a state. Drawing an analogy with the example distribution
p
above, we can deduce that the smoothing parameter must depend on
\epsilon
to remove the case of correlated failure, whereby all the games fail together. However, this can also be achieved through testing, similar to the approach adopted in
[
MD24b
]
. By measuring the winning probability of the CHSH game on a random sample of games, we can determine if it is sufficiently high. If so, we can conclude that the CHSH game must have been played using “good” entangled quantum states between the two parties, and an event of correlated failure must not have occurred. While some additional assumptions may be required regarding the side information, this approach could potentially allow for an arbitrary smoothing parameter in such cases. This is an interesting line of research to pursue in the future.

5
Alternative proof for the universal chain rule

The proofs so far in this paper have followed the approach of creating an auxiliary state using certain conditional states, proving that this state has a small relative entropy from the original state and reducing the original problem to a simpler one in terms of this auxiliary state. This is also the general approach we follow in
[
MD23
]
. In
[
MD23
, Section 4]
we provide a simple and intuitive alternate proof technique that lower bounds the smooth min-entropy for the classical approximately independent registers problem in an
elementwise
fashion. At the time of writing
[
MD23
]
, we were unable to generalise this proof to the quantum setting. However, using the ideas developed in this paper, we can now generalise this proof. Here we use it to provide an alternative proof of the universal chain rule for the smooth min-entropy.

5.1
Classical proof

We begin by once again first sketching the proof in the classical case. Recall that we would like to prove that for a probability distribution
p_{A_{1}^{n}B}
,

\displaystyle H^{g_{1}(\epsilon)}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}-ng_{2}(\epsilon)-k(\epsilon).

(141)

Let
\lambda_{k}:=H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}
. Following Sec.
3.1
, for
k\in[n]
, let
q^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
be the distribution, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A_{1}^{k}B}-q^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

(142)

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow,\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{p}=\lambda_{k}=H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{q^{(k)}}.

(143)

Similar to Eq.
61
, we also have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A_{1}^{k}B}-p_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon.

(144)

We will use the following lemma from
[
MD23
]
.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
MD23
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Suppose
p,q
are probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|p-q\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, then
S\subseteq\mathcal{X}
defined as
S:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:p(x)\leq(1+\epsilon^{1/2})q(x)\}
is such that
q(S)\geq 1-\epsilon^{1/2}
and
p(S)\geq 1-\epsilon^{1/2}-\epsilon
.

Using the Lemma above, for every
k\in[n]
, we know that the set

\displaystyle B_{k}:

\displaystyle=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):p(a_{1}^{k}b)>(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})p(a_{1}^{k-1}b)q^{(k)}(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)>(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})q^{(k)}(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)\right\}

satisfies
\Pr_{p}(B_{k})\leq 3\sqrt{\epsilon}
. We can now define
L=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\chi_{B_{k}}
, which is a random variable that simply counts the number of bad sets
B_{k}
an element
(a_{1}^{n},b)
belongs to. Using the Markov inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Pr_{p}\left[L>n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]\leq\frac{\Expect_{p}[L]}{n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq 3\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}.

We can define the bad set
\mathcal{B}:=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):L(a_{1}^{n},b)>n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\}
. Then for the subnormalised distribution
\tilde{p}_{A_{1}^{n}B}
defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{A_{1}^{n}B}(a_{1}^{n},b)=\begin{cases}p_{A_{1}^{n}B}(a_{1}^{n},b)&(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases},

we have
P(\tilde{p}_{A_{1}^{n}B},p_{A_{1}^{n}B})\leq\sqrt{6}\epsilon^{1/8}
. Further, note that for every
(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle p(a_{1}^{n}|b)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}}p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}}p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{n}\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}}q^{(k)}(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}}e^{\log|A|-\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{n}\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}}\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}}e^{\log|A|-\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{n}\ e^{n\epsilon^{1/4}\log|A|}\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}}

where in the third line we have used the fact that if
(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}
, then
p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)\leq(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})q^{(k)}(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)
and if
(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}
then
p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)\leq 1\leq\exp(\log|A|-\lambda_{k})
since
\lambda_{k}\leq\log|A|
. In the last line we have used the fact that for
(a_{1}^{k},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
|\{k\in[n]:(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}\}|=L(a_{1}^{n},b)\leq n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
. This proves the following lower bound for the smooth min-entropy of
\rho

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow,\sqrt{6}\epsilon^{1/8}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{\downarrow,\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)-n\epsilon^{1/4}\log|A|-n\log(1+\sqrt{\epsilon}).

(145)

There are many hurdles to generalising this proof to the quantum setting. Firstly, the correct generalisation of Lemma
5.1
is quite difficult. Secondly, the proof above bounds the conditional probability
p(a_{1}^{n}|b)
for every element
(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}
differently depending on which bad sets
(\mathcal{B}_{k})_{k}
it belongs in. It is not obvious how such an argument can be carried out for quantum states. For example, one cannot simply use the eigenvalues of
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
instead of
p(a_{1}^{n}b)
since the eigenvectors for each of the partial states
\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}
differ. Lastly, in the quantum case it does not seem that the Markov inequality can be used to identify the bad sets and the smoothed state as we did above.

The correct generalisation of Lemma
5.1
was proven by
[
fu23
]
. We state it and reproduce its proof in Lemma
5.5
. To address the second and third hurdles mentioned above, we modify the classical proof before proceeding to the quantum proof.

Instead of figuring out the bad set
\mathcal{B}
and eliminating the elements belonging to this set to construct the smoothed distribution, we use the substate theorem and the entropic triangle inequality. Define the auxiliary subnormalised distribution

\displaystyle q(a_{1}^{n}b):=\delta^{L(a_{1}^{n}b)}p(a_{1}^{n}b)

(146)

for some small
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{|A|})
. The
\delta^{L(a_{1}^{n}b)}
factor in
q
simply guarantees that elements which have a large
L
(are
bad
) are significantly damped. This ensures that for all
a_{1}^{n},b
, we have that

\displaystyle q(a_{1}^{n}b)

\displaystyle=\delta^{L(a_{1}^{n}b)}p(a_{1}^{n}b)

\displaystyle=p(b)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\delta^{\chi_{B_{k}}(a_{1}^{n}b)}p(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)

\displaystyle\leq p(b)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})e^{-\lambda_{k}}.

(147)

The last inequality above is guaranteed both when
a_{1}^{n},b\not\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
and when
a_{1}^{n},b\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
since we chose
\delta<\frac{1}{|A|}
. This gives us a lower bound the min-entropy of
q
. Moreover, we can easily show that the relative entropy between
p
and
q
is small:

\displaystyle D(p_{A_{1}^{n}B}||q_{A_{1}^{n}B})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[\log\frac{p(A_{1}^{n}B)}{q({A_{1}^{n}B})}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p}\left[L\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right]

\displaystyle=3n\sqrt{\epsilon}\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

(148)

Now, we can bound the smooth min-entropy of
p
in terms of the min-entropy of
q
by using the substate theorem and the entropic triangle inequality. We will generalise this proof to the quantum case.

5.2
Lemmas

In this section, we will primarily use the following variant of the smooth min-entropy, which has previously appeared in
[
ABJT20
]
:

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\text{ for }\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})\text{ such that }\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\leq e^{-\lambda}\Id_{A}\otimes{\rho}_{B}\right\}.

(149)

Note that
\bar{H}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq-\log|A|
because
\rho_{AB}\leq|A|\Id_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
. Also,
\bar{H}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq\log\frac{|A|}{1-\epsilon^{2}}
. To see this note that for any
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}
and
\lambda
, such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\leq e^{-\lambda}\Id_{A}\otimes{\rho}_{B}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tr\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\leq e^{-\lambda}|A|

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\log\frac{|A|}{\tr\tilde{\rho}_{AB}}

(150)

where we have simply taken a trace in the second line. Finally, use the fact that
\tr\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}
for all
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})
.

This smooth min-entropy can be lower bounded using the
H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}
min-entropy as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 5.2
.

For a normalized quantum state
\rho_{AB}
and
\epsilon\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{\min}^{2\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}.

(151)

Proof.

Let
\lambda=H_{\min}^{\downarrow,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}
and the state
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})
be such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\leq e^{-\lambda}\Id_{A}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{B}.

(152)

By Lemma
A.1
, we have that
\eta_{AB}:=\rho_{B}^{1/2}U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}U_{B}^{\dagger}\rho_{B}^{1/2}
satisfies
P(\rho_{AB},\eta_{AB})\leq 2\epsilon
. Clearly, this state also satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{AB}\leq e^{-\lambda}\Id_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}.

(153)

∎

We also require the following operator inequalities relating an operator and its compressions.

Lemma 5.3

(Asymmetric pinching
[
MD23
, Lemma 5.2]
)
.

For
t>0
, a positive semidefinite operator
X\geq 0
and orthogonal projections
\Pi
and
\Pi_{\perp}=\Id-\Pi
, we have that

\displaystyle X\leq(1+t)\Pi X\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)\Pi_{\perp}X\Pi_{\perp}.

(154)

Lemma 5.4
.

For a positive operator
X\geq 0
and orthogonal projectors
\Pi
and
\Pi_{\perp}=\Id-\Pi
, we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{\perp}X\Pi_{\perp}\leq 2X+2\Pi X\Pi.

(155)

Proof.

We will write the operator
X
as the block matrix

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}\\
X_{2}^{\ast}&X_{3}\end{pmatrix}

(156)

where the blocks are partitioned according to the direct sum
\text{im}(\Pi)\oplus\text{im}(\Pi_{\perp})
. The statement in the Lemma is now equivalent to proving that

\displaystyle 0\leq\begin{pmatrix}4X_{1}&2X_{2}\\
2X_{2}^{\ast}&X_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(157)

Call the matrix above
X^{\prime}
. Note that using the Schur’s complement
[
Bha07
, Exercise 1.3.5]

X\geq 0
implies that
X_{3}\geq 0
,
\text{im}(X_{2}^{\ast})\subseteq\text{im}(X_{3})
and

\displaystyle X_{1}\geq X_{2}X_{3}^{-1}X_{2}^{\ast}.

(158)

Using Schur’s complement characterization again for
X^{\prime}
, we see that
X^{\prime}\geq 0
is equivalent to
X_{3}\geq 0
,
\text{im}(X_{2}^{\ast})\subseteq\text{im}(X_{3})
and

\displaystyle 4X_{1}\geq(2X_{2})X_{3}^{-1}(2X_{2})^{\ast}

which are all true because of the corresponding relations for
X
itself.
∎

The following lemma correctly generalises Lemma
5.1
. It was proven by user:fedja in response to a question by user:noel (pseudonym used by AM) on MathOverflow
[
fu23
]
. Its proof has been reproduced here for the purpose of completeness.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
fu23
]
)
.

For
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, and subnormalized quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
on the finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, there exists an orthogonal projector
\Pi
such that

\displaystyle\Pi\rho\Pi\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\sigma

(159)

and

\displaystyle\tr((\Id-\Pi)\rho)\leq g_{2}(\epsilon)

(160)

for the small functions

\displaystyle g_{1}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=\frac{8}{3}(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+(1+\epsilon^{1/3}+\epsilon^{2/3})\epsilon^{1/3}=O\left(\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)

(161)

\displaystyle g_{2}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=4(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}+2\epsilon=O(\epsilon^{1/3})

(162)

Proof.

Let
\sigma=\sum_{i}q_{i}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\sigma
. Let
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
be a small parameter, which will be specified later. For every
k\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{k}:=\text{span}\left\{\ket{x_{i}}:q_{i}\in((1+\delta_{1})^{-(k+1)},(1+\delta_{1})^{-k}]\right\}

(163)

\displaystyle d_{k}:=\text{dim}(\mathcal{X}_{k})

(164)

We have
\mathcal{X}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}_{k}
and
\sigma=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}
. Let
P_{k}
be the projector on the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
for every
k
. Note that
P_{k}
commute with
\sigma
. If we restrict
\sigma
to the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{k+1}}\Id_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\leq\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{k}}\Id_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}

(165)

for every
k
. Further, for any projector
\Pi_{k}
in the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi_{k}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\Pi_{k}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\|_{\infty}\|\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}^{-1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\delta_{1}

which implies that for any projector
\Pi_{k}
in
\mathcal{X}_{k}

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\Pi_{k}\leq(1+\delta_{1})\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}.

(166)

We will choose the projector
\Pi
to satisfy the lemma to be of the form
\Pi=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Pi_{k}
, where each
\Pi_{k}
is a projector in the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
. With such a projector, we have

\displaystyle\Pi\sigma\Pi

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Pi_{k}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\Pi_{k}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta_{1})\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}

\displaystyle=(1+\delta_{1})\sigma.

(167)

Define
\Delta:=(\rho-\sigma)^{+}
to be the positive part of
(\rho-\sigma)
and observe that
\tr(\Delta)\leq 2\epsilon
. Note that
\rho\leq\sigma+\Delta
. Further, let
\Delta_{k}:=P_{k}\Delta P_{k}
and
\mu_{k}:=\tr(\Delta P_{k})
for every
k
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tr(\Delta P_{k})

\displaystyle=\tr(\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

(168)

Let
\Delta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\nu_{ki}\ket{y_{ki}}\bra{y_{ki}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\Delta_{k}
. Let
\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
be two more parameters to be chosen later. Define the subspace
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
of
\mathcal{X}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{k}:=\text{span}\left\{\ket{y_{ki}}:\nu_{ki}\leq\delta_{2}(1+\delta_{1})^{-(k+1)}\right\}.

(169)

Note that the codimension of
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
in the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
, is at most
\frac{\mu_{k}}{\delta_{2}}(1+\delta_{1})^{k+1}
. We further restrict the subspace
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
to the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{k}:=\mathcal{Y}_{k}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{k-K-1}\text{ker}(P_{j}\Delta).

(170)

Since, rank of
P_{j}
is
d_{j}
, the codimension of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
in
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
is at most
\sum_{j=0}^{k-K-1}d_{j}
. Define
\Pi_{k}
to be the projector on
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
. Note that since
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{X}_{k}
,
\Pi_{k}
is a projector in the space
\mathcal{X}_{k}
. We define the projector

\displaystyle\Pi:=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Pi_{k},

(171)

which is of the form promised and show that this satisfies the conditions of the lemma.

We begin by showing that
\Pi\Delta\Pi
can be bounded by a small multiple of
\Pi\sigma\Pi
, specifically

\displaystyle\Pi\Delta\Pi\leq(2K+1)\delta_{2}\Pi\sigma\Pi.

It is sufficient to show that for
\ket{v}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\ket{v_{k}}
such that
\ket{v_{k}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{k}
for every
k
, we have

\displaystyle\braket{v|\Delta|v}\leq(2K+1)\delta_{2}\braket{v|\sigma|v}.

(172)

The left-hand side above can be expanded as

\displaystyle\braket{v|\Delta|v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{i}}+2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\text{Re}\left\{\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{i}}+2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{i}}+2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}|\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}|+2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{i-K-1}|\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}|.

(173)

The first summation above can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{i+1}}\braket{v_{i}|v_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\sigma|v_{i}}

(174)

where we have used the fact that
\ket{v_{i}}\in\mathcal{Y}_{i}
and the maximum eigenvalue of
P_{i}\Delta P_{i}=\Delta_{i}
in this subspace is at most
\delta_{2}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{i+1}}
, and the fact that the minimum eigenvalue of
\sigma
in
\mathcal{Z}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{i}
is at least
\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{i+1}}
.

We can bound the second term as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}|\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}\left\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\ket{v_{j}}\right\|\left\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\ket{v_{i}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=i-K}^{i-1}\left(\left\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\ket{v_{j}}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\ket{v_{i}}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=K\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\ket{v_{i}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=K\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{i}}

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the first line along with the fact that
\Delta\geq 0
and the AM-GM inequality in the second line. This summation can now be bounded using Eq.
174
.

The last term in Eq.
173
is a summation over inner products
|\braket{v_{i}|\Delta|v_{j}}|=|\braket{v_{j}|P_{j}\Delta|v_{i}}|
for
j\leq i-K-1
. By our definition for the subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
, we have that
\ket{v_{i}}\in\text{ker}(P_{j}\Delta)
for these choices of the index
j
.

Putting these together, we have the bound

\displaystyle\braket{v|\Delta|v}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2K+1\right)\delta_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\braket{v_{i}|\sigma|v_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(2K+1\right)\delta_{2}\braket{v|\sigma|v}

for every
\ket{v}=\Pi\ket{v}
. Above we have used the fact that
\ket{v_{i}}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
and
\sigma=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{i}}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Pi\Delta\Pi\leq\left(2K+1\right)\delta_{2}\Pi\sigma\Pi

(175)

and also

\displaystyle\Pi\rho\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\Pi\left(\sigma+\Delta\right)\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\left(2K+1\right)\delta_{2}\right)\Pi\sigma\Pi.

(176)

Using Eq.
167
we can remove the sandwiching projectors from
\sigma
to get the bound

\displaystyle\Pi\rho\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\left(2K+1\right)\delta_{2}\right)(1+\delta_{1})\sigma.

(177)

Now, we will show that
\tr(\Pi\sigma)
is large. Recall that the codimension of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
in
\mathcal{X}_{k}
is at most

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{k}}{\delta_{2}}(1+\delta_{1})^{k+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-K-1}d_{j}.

(178)

Hence,

\displaystyle\tr\left((\Id-\Pi)\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tr\left((P_{k}-\Pi_{k})\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{k}}\tr(P_{k}-\Pi_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{k}}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\delta_{2}}(1+\delta_{1})^{k+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-K-1}d_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}(1+\delta_{1})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}d_{j}\sum_{k=j+K+1}^{\infty}(1+\delta_{1})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}{\delta_{2}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}(1+\delta_{1})^{-(K-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}d_{j}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{1})^{j+1}}

\displaystyle=(1+\delta_{1})\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{\delta_{2}}+\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}(1+\delta_{1})^{-K}\right)

(179)

where in the second line we have used
\sigma|_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\leq(1+\delta_{1})^{-k}\Id_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}
, in the third line we have used the bound on the codimension of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
in Eq.
178
, in the fifth line we have used the bound in Eq.
168
and in the last line we have used the fact that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}d_{j}(1+\delta_{1})^{-(j+1)}\leq\tr{\sigma}\leq 1
.

Now all that is left to do is to select the parameters
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and
K
. We choose

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle:=\epsilon^{1/3}

(180)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle:=\epsilon^{2/3}

(181)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle:=\left\lfloor\frac{2}{\epsilon^{1/3}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2/3}}\right\rfloor.

(182)

For these parameters, we get

\displaystyle\Pi\rho\Pi

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{8}{3}\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\epsilon^{2/3}\right)(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\sigma.

(183)

Note that
(1+\delta_{1})^{-1}\leq(1-\delta_{1}/2)\leq e^{-\delta_{1}/2}
since
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
. This implies

\displaystyle\tr\left((\Id-\Pi)\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\left(2\epsilon^{1/3}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/3}}e^{-K\frac{\epsilon^{1/3}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\left(2\epsilon^{1/3}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/3}}e^{-\frac{\epsilon^{1/3}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\epsilon^{1/3}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2/3}}-1\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\left(2\epsilon^{1/3}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/3}}2\epsilon^{2/3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}.

Finally, using the fact that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, we get

\displaystyle\tr\left((\Id-\Pi)\rho\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tr\left((\Id-\Pi)\sigma\right)+2\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq 4(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}+2\epsilon.

(184)

∎

5.3
Quantum proof

In this section, we will generalise the classical proof presented earlier to the quantum case. We will prove a statement of the following form for all normalised quantum states
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
:

\displaystyle H^{g^{\prime}_{1}(\epsilon)}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-ng^{\prime}_{2}(\epsilon)-k(\epsilon)

(185)

where
g^{\prime}_{1}
and
g^{\prime}_{2}
are small functions of
\epsilon
and
k
is a general function of
\epsilon
. This will be sufficient to prove the universal chain rule.

For every
k
, let
\lambda_{k}:=\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}
and
\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
be the subnormalised state such that

\displaystyle P(\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B},\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B})\leq\epsilon

(186)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq e^{-\lambda_{k}}\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}.

(187)

Using the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality, for each
k
, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}-\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon.

(188)

We will now define the projectors
P^{(k,l)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
for every
k\in[n]
and the label
l\in\{g,b\}
(
\{\text{good, bad}\}
). We define the
good
projector
P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
to be the projector given by Lemma
5.5
when applied to the states
\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}
and
\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
, so that it satisfies

\displaystyle P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

(189)

and

\displaystyle\tr(P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B})\geq 1-g_{2}(\epsilon)

(190)

for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
as defined in Lemma
5.5
. Further, define its orthogonal complement as the
bad
projector,

\displaystyle P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}:=\Id_{A_{1}^{k}B}-P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}.

(191)

The label
l
in
P^{(k,l)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
will allow us to succinctly refer to these two projectors together. Define the subnormalised state

\displaystyle\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}:=P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}.

(192)

For this state, observe that

\displaystyle P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ \tilde{\rho}^{(n)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda_{n}}(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ \Id_{A_{n}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda_{n}}(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\Id_{A_{n}}\otimes P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}}(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))^{n}\Id_{A_{1}^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B}

which implies that
H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\eta}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))
. Moreover, the distance between
\eta
and
\rho
is

\displaystyle P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},\ \eta_{A_{1}^{n}B})

\displaystyle=P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})

\displaystyle\leq P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+P(P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B},\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})

\displaystyle\leq P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})+P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},\ P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B})

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B},\ P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B})

\displaystyle\leq n\sqrt{2g_{2}(\epsilon)}

where we have used the gentle measurement lemma
[
Wat18
, Proposition 3.14]
for the last line. The distance between
\eta
and
\rho
grows like
n
times a small function and the min-entropy of
\eta
satisfies
H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\eta}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))
. If we were able to show that the relative entropy distance between these two states also grew like
n
times a small function, similar to the purified distance then we could use the substate theorem and the entropic triangle inequality to prove a bound of the required form. However, we can’t directly prove that the relative entropy between these two states is small because in general the projectors sandwiching the state
\rho
in
\eta
could lead to a situation, where
\rho\not\ll\eta
, which would cause their relative entropy diverge. To remedy this, one could imagine adding a small amount of the complement projector to these projectors, so that we sandwich with
P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}+\delta P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
instead of simply
P^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}
. Under no further assumptions on
\rho
, the question of whether the divergence of
\rho
and
\eta
is finite or not in this case reduces to the question: given a state
\sigma
, a projector
\Pi
and its complement
\Pi_{\perp}:=\Id-\Pi
, is

\displaystyle\sigma\ll(\Pi+\delta\Pi_{\perp})\sigma(\Pi+\delta\Pi_{\perp})?

(193)

The answer to this is easily seen to be negative when one consider the pure state
\sigma=\ket{u}\bra{u}
. In this case, the above is equivalent to asking if
\ket{u}\bra{u}\ll\ket{v}\bra{v}
for some vector
\ket{v}\neq\ket{u}
, which is not true.

The next simple remedy that comes to mind is to use a “pinching type” disturbance to ensure that the divergence is finite. For
\delta\in(0,1)
, a projector
\Pi
and its complement
\Pi_{\perp}
, define the CP map
\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[\Pi]
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[\Pi](\sigma):=\Pi\sigma\Pi+\delta\Pi_{\perp}\sigma\Pi_{\perp}.

(194)

Then, by the pinching inequality, for every
\Pi
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\left(\delta\Pi\sigma\Pi+\delta\Pi_{\perp}\sigma\Pi_{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[\Pi](\sigma)

which implies
D_{\max}(\sigma||\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[\Pi](\sigma))\leq O(\log{1}/{\delta})
– a bound that should be sufficient for our purposes. Therefore, one can try proving the chain rule using the subnormalised state

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}]

\displaystyle\circ\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[P^{(2,g)}_{A_{1}^{2}B}]\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{P}_{\delta}[P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}](\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\delta^{\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

(195)

where
\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n}):=|\{i:l_{i}=b\}|
is the weight of
b
labels in the string
l_{1}^{n}
. In our proof below, we do not attempt to directly identify the correct modified state ourselves. Similar to the proofs before, we instead leave this question for the generalised GT inequality. We will use an exponential generalisation of the distribution
q
in Eq.
146
. Variants of both of the above remedies make an appearance during the following proof.

Lemma 5.6
.

For a full rank state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle H^{\mu}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle-n\log(1+\epsilon)-n(\mu+\mu^{3})-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(196)

where

\displaystyle\mu:=\left(8(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}\epsilon^{1/9}=O\left(\epsilon^{1/9}\left(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}\right)

(197)

\displaystyle g_{1}(\epsilon):=\frac{8}{3}(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+(1+\epsilon^{1/3}+\epsilon^{2/3})\epsilon^{1/3}=O\left(\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)

(198)

as long as

Proof.

We retain the definitions of

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tr(P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B})

\displaystyle\leq ng_{2}(\epsilon).

(199)

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a parameter to be chosen later. We have that

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle g_{2}(\epsilon)\log\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq D\left(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\exp\left(\log\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}+\log(\delta)L_{A_{1}^{n}B}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B}>0}\left\{\tr(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \log\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B})+1-\tr\exp\left(\log\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B}+\log(\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}+\log\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\right)\right\}

(200)

where we have used Eq.
199
in the first line and the variational expression in Eq.
52
in the second line. For the trace of exponential term in the last expression above, we can use the generalised Golden-Thompson inequality (Theorem
2.3
) as

\displaystyle\tr\exp\left(\log\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B}+\log(\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{n}P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}+\log\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\tr\left(\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ e^{\frac{1-it}{2}\log(\delta)P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}}\cdots\ e^{\frac{1-it}{2}\log(\delta)P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ e^{\frac{1+it}{2}\log(\delta)P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}}\cdots\ e^{\frac{1+it}{2}\log(\delta)P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\tr\big(\omega_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ (\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}+P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}+P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})\big),

(201)

where we have used the fact that for a projector

and

in the last line. Define the subnormalised state

\displaystyle\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)(\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}+P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}

(202)

(203)

Plugging this in Eq.

\displaystyle ng_{2}(\epsilon)\log\frac{1}{\delta}

Claim 5.7

where we have used

\displaystyle D^{\mu}_{\max}\left(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}\right)

.

\displaystyle(\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}+P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ (\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(n,b)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}+P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{\delta})(\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1-it}{2}}P^{(n-1,b)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}+P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B})\ P_{\sqrt{\delta}}[P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}](\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B})\

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(n-1,b)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}+P^{(n-1,g)}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B})\cdots\ (\delta^{\frac{1+it}{2}}P^{(1,b)}_{A_{1}B}+P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B})

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{\delta})^{n}P_{\sqrt{\delta}}[P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}]\circ\cdots\circ P_{\sqrt{\delta}}[P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}](\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B})

(212)

which implies that

\displaystyle\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}\leq(1+\sqrt{\delta})^{n}P_{\sqrt{\delta}}[P^{(1,g)}_{A_{1}B}]\circ\cdots\circ P_{\sqrt{\delta}}[P^{(n,g)}_{A_{1}^{n}B}](\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}).

(213)

This bound essentially says that the
D_{\max}
between these two states is small:

\displaystyle D_{\max}(\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B})\leq n\log(1+\sqrt{\delta})

(214)

Combining this with the smooth
D_{\max}
bound between
\rho
and
\eta
(Eq.
209
) shows the bound in the claim.
∎

The only thing left to do now is to show that min-entropy of the state
\sigma
is large, i.e.,
\gtrsim\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}
(recall
\lambda_{k}:=\bar{H}_{\min}^{\epsilon}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}
). Note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

(215)

where
\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n}):=|\{i:l_{i}=b\}|
the weight of
b
labels in the string
l_{1}^{n}
. We will now bound the min-entropy of each of the terms in this summation. Roughly speaking, whenever the label
l_{k}=g
(a good projector is applied),
\lambda_{k}
amount of min-entropy will be accumulated but when the projector is bad
O(\log|A|)
amount of min-entropy will be lost. The reason, we are still able to accumulate a large amount of min-entropy for the state
\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B}
is because these terms are also weighted with the factor
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}
. If the number of bad projectors in a term is large, then this factor ensures that the contribution of this term is small.

Claim 5.9
.

For every
k\in[n]
and
l_{k}\in\{g,b\}
, we have

\displaystyle P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq\begin{cases}(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))e^{-\lambda_{k}}\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}&\text{ if }l_{k}=g\\
4|A|(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}&\text{ if }l_{k}=b\end{cases}

(216)

which can succinctly be written as

\displaystyle P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))e^{-\lambda_{k}\delta(l_{k},g)}(4|A|)^{\delta(l_{k},b)}\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}

(217)

where
\delta(x,y)
is the Kronecker delta function (
\delta(x,y)=1
if
x=y
else it is
0
).

Proof.

Let’s first consider the case when
l_{k}=g
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))e^{-\lambda_{k}}\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}

(218)

where the first line follows from the definition of the good projectors (Eq.
189
) and the second line follows from Eq.
187
.

When
l_{k}=b
, we have

\displaystyle P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,b)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

\displaystyle\leq 2\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}+2P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,g)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

\displaystyle\leq 2\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}+2(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\tilde{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\left(|A|\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}+e^{-\lambda_{k}}\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4|A|(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}

(219)

where in the first line we have used Lemma
5.4
, in the second line we have used Eq.
189
, in the third line we have used
\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq|A|\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}
and Eq.
187
, and in the last line we use
\lambda_{k}\geq-\log|A|
.
∎

For every
k
, let
c_{k}(l_{k}):=e^{-\lambda_{k}\delta(l_{k},g)}(4|A|)^{\delta(l_{k},b)}
so that

\displaystyle P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\ P^{(k,l_{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B}\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))c_{k}(l_{k})\Id_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}.

(220)

Now, observe that for each term in the summation in Eq.
215

\displaystyle P

{}^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))c_{n}(l_{n})P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n-1,l_{n-1})}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ \Id_{A_{n}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ P^{(n-1,l_{n-1})}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle=(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))c_{n}(l_{n})\Id_{A_{n}}\otimes P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n-1,l_{n-1})}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\ P^{(n-1,l_{n-1})}_{A_{1}^{n-1}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))^{n}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}(l_{k})\right)\Id_{A_{1}^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B}.

(221)

Plugging this bound into the expression for
\sigma
in Eq.
215
, we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}\cdots P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\ P^{(n,l_{n})}_{A_{1}^{n}B}\cdots P^{(2,l_{2})}_{A_{1}^{2}B}P^{(1,l_{1})}_{A_{1}B}

\displaystyle\leq(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))^{n}\left(\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}\prod_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}(l_{k})\right)\Id_{A_{1}^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B}.

(222)

Let us now bound the expression

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}\omega_{b}(l_{1}^{n})}\prod_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}(l_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{\delta}^{\delta(l_{k},b)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}\delta(l_{k},g)}(4|A|)^{\delta(l_{k},b)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}^{n}\in\{g,b\}^{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}\delta(l_{k},g)}(4|A|\sqrt{\delta})^{\delta(l_{k},b)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(e^{-\lambda_{k}}+4|A|\sqrt{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}}\left(1+4|A|e^{\lambda_{k}}\sqrt{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\lambda_{k}}\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{n}e^{-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}}

(223)

where we have used
\lambda_{k}\leq\log\frac{|A|}{1-\epsilon^{2}}
in the second last line. Combining Eq.
222
and Eq.
223
, we get

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\geq-D_{\max}(\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\Id_{A_{1}^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

(224)

Finally, we can use the entropic triangle inequality in Eq.
43
along with Eq.
210
to get

\displaystyle H^{\mu}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle-n\log(1+\sqrt{\delta})-n(\mu+\mu^{3})-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(225)

where
\mu=\left(g_{2}(\epsilon)\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{1/3}
. We choose the parameter
\delta=\epsilon^{2}
, so that we have

\displaystyle H^{\mu}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle-n\log(1+\epsilon)-n(\mu+\mu^{3})-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(226)

for
\mu=\left(8(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}\epsilon^{1/9}=O(\epsilon^{1/9}\left(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3})
.
∎

We complete the proof of the universal chain rule by transforming all the entropies to
H^{\downarrow,\epsilon}_{\min}
in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.10
.

For a state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we have the chain rule

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow,2\mu+\epsilon/4}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{\downarrow,\epsilon/4}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle-n\log(1+\epsilon)-n(\mu+\mu^{3})-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}-\log\left(\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{1-\mu}\right)

(227)

where

\displaystyle\mu:=\left(8(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}\epsilon^{1/9}=O\left(\epsilon^{1/9}\left(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}\right)

(228)

\displaystyle g_{1}(\epsilon):=\frac{8}{3}(1+\epsilon^{1/3})\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+(1+\epsilon^{1/3}+\epsilon^{2/3})\epsilon^{1/3}=O\left(\epsilon^{1/3}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)

(229)

as long as
\mu\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Case 1:
If
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
is full rank, then we can use the lemma above along with Eq.
37
and Lemma
5.2
to show that

\displaystyle H^{\downarrow,2\mu}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{\downarrow,\epsilon/2}_{\min}(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}-n\log\left(1+4|A|^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon^{2}}\right)-n\log(1+g_{1}(\epsilon))

\displaystyle-n\log(1+\epsilon)-n(\mu+\mu^{3})-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}-\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}-\log\left(\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{1-\mu}\right).

(230)

Case 2:
Now, we can follow the same argument as the one in Case 2 of the proof of Theorem
3.4
to derive the bound in the theorem statement for all states
\rho
.
∎

6
Conclusion

We develop a powerful proof technique combining the entropic triangle inequality, the generalised GT inequality and the substate theorem for proving entropic bounds for approximation chains. We use this technique to prove novel chain rules– the universal smooth min-entropy chain rule and the unstructured approximate entropy accumulation theorem. Importantly, both of these chain rules can be used meaningfully for arbitrarily large number of systems.

As far as applications are concerned, we use the unstructured approximate EAT to prove the security of parallel DI-QKD in our companion work
[
MD24a
]
. We expect the universal chain rule to aid in transforming von Neumann entropy based arguments to one-shot arguments. Furthermore, it provides a straightforward solution to problems such as the approximately independent registers problem discussed in
[
MD23
, Section 4]
. It is also our conviction that the proof technique introduced in this chapter will be useful for tackling other problems. For instance, it should be possible to use it to derive similar chain rules for one-shot variants (e.g.,
I_{\max}
) of the (multipartite) mutual information.

As discussed in Sec.
4.1
, it seems possible to decouple the smoothing parameter and the approximation parameter in certain scenarios, especially those involving DIQKD. We leave the problem of determining whether these parameters can be decoupled using testing for future work. This is an interesting and important question, which could potentially lead to significant improvements in the security proof of parallel DIQKD and the analysis of DIQKD with leakage.

Finally, we did not attempt to optimise our bounds here, but it should be interesting to study the absolute limits of the entropic error terms and the smoothing errors in our chain rules.

The following lemma provides a tighter bound for the distance as compared to Lemma

[
DBWR14

2.5
in Theorem
4.1
and
E.2

Lemma A.1
.

For a normalised state
\rho_{AB}
and a subnormalised state
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}
such that
P(\rho_{AB},\tilde{\rho}_{AB})\leq\epsilon
, there exists a unitary
U_{B}
on the register
B
such that the state

\displaystyle\eta_{AB}:=\rho_{B}^{1/2}U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}U_{B}^{\dagger}\rho_{B}^{1/2}

(231)

(
\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}
is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse above) satisfies
P(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})\leq\epsilon
and
P(\rho_{AB},\eta_{AB})\leq 2\epsilon
. Note that if
\tilde{\rho}_{B}
is full rank, then
\eta_{B}=\rho_{B}
.

Proof.

Note that since
\rho_{AB}
is normalised, we have
F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\rho_{AB})\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}
. Let
\ket{\tilde{\rho}}_{ABR}
be an arbitrary purification of
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}
. Let
U_{B}
be any unitary for now and let
\eta_{AB}
be defined as in Eq.
231
above. We will choose
U_{B}
so that
F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})
is large.

Observe that the pure state
\ket{\eta}_{ABR}:=\rho_{B}^{1/2}U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}\ket{\tilde{\rho}}_{ABR}
is a purification of
\eta_{AB}
. Using Uhlmann’s theorem
[
Wat18
, Theorem 3.22]
, we have

\displaystyle F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\geq|\braket{\tilde{\rho}|\eta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\braket{\tilde{\rho}|\rho_{B}^{1/2}U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}|\tilde{\rho}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\tr(\rho_{B}^{1/2}U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{-1/2}\tilde{\rho}_{ABR})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\tr(U_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{1/2}\rho_{B}^{1/2})\right|^{2}.

Say the polar decomposition of
\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{1/2}\rho_{B}^{1/2}=V_{B}\left|\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{1/2}\rho_{B}^{1/2}\right|
. We can now select
U_{B}
to be
V_{B}^{\dagger}
, so that

\displaystyle F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\tr\left|\tilde{\rho}_{B}^{1/2}\rho_{B}^{1/2}\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=F(\tilde{\rho}_{B},\rho_{B})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}

(232)

where we have used
F(\tilde{\rho}_{B},\rho_{B})\geq F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\rho_{AB})
. Further, we have

\displaystyle P(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-F_{\ast}(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-F(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

Using the triangle inequality, for this choice of
U_{B}
, we get

\displaystyle P({\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq P({\rho}_{AB},\tilde{\rho}_{AB})+P(\tilde{\rho}_{AB},\eta_{AB})

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

∎

Appendix B
Comparison with previous chain rules

It is instructive to understand why the previously developed chain rule fails to yield a universal chain rule.
[
DBWR14
, Lemma A.8]
proved that

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2}+\delta}(A_{1}A_{2}|B)_{\rho}\geq H_{\min}^{\epsilon_{1}}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}+H_{\min}^{\epsilon_{2}}(A_{2}|A_{1}B)_{\rho}-k(\delta)

(233)

where

n

nk(\delta)

\rho_{ABC}

(234)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\rho}_{BC}
be a subnormalised state such that
P(\rho_{BC},\tilde{\rho}_{BC})\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
H^{\epsilon_{1}}_{\min}(B|C)_{\rho}=H_{\min}(B|C)_{\tilde{\rho}}=:\lambda_{1}
. Also, let
\bar{\rho}_{ABC}
be a subnormalised state such that

\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}

Appendix C
Classical approximate chain rule for the relative entropy

Lemma C.1
.

Let
p
and
p^{\prime}
be probability distributions over
\mathcal{X}
. Then, for a function
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Expect_{X\sim p}[f(X)]-\Expect_{X\sim p^{\prime}}[f(X)]\right|\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|f(x)|\left\|p-p^{\prime}\right\|_{1}.

(239)

Proof.

\displaystyle\left|\Expect_{X\sim p}[f(X)]-\Expect_{X\sim p^{\prime}}[f(X)]\right|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)f(x)-\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p^{\prime}(x)f(x)|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}(p(x)-p^{\prime}(x))f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|p(x)-p^{\prime}(x)||f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|f(x)|\left\|p-p^{\prime}\right\|_{1}

∎

We will call the following lemma an approximate chain rule for the relative entropy. It allows us to switch the probability distribution
p_{AB}
in the term
D(p_{AB}||p_{B}q_{A|B})
of the chain rule for
D(p_{AB}||q_{AB})
to
D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})
, where
p\approx_{\epsilon}p^{\prime}
while incurring a penalty which depends only on the size of the register
A
.

Lemma C.2
.

Suppose that
\delta>0
and
q_{AB}
is a probability distribution over
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
such that for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
, we have
q(a|b)\geq\delta
. Then, for
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and a
p^{\prime}_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{AB}-p^{\prime}_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, we have that

\displaystyle D(p_{AB}||q_{AB})\leq D(p_{B}||q_{B})+D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})+z(\epsilon,\delta)

(240)

where
z(\epsilon,\delta):=h(2\epsilon)+6\epsilon\log\frac{1}{\delta}+4\epsilon\log|\mathcal{A}|
. Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle D(p_{AB}||q_{AB})\leq D(p_{B}||q_{B})+\inf_{\left\|p^{\prime}_{AB}-p_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon}D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})+z(\epsilon,\delta)

(241)

Proof.

If
\supp(p_{B})\not\subseteq\supp(q_{B})
, then the right-hand side is infinite and the identity is trivially true. We suppose
\supp(p_{B})\subseteq\supp(q_{B})
here on.

We will show that for every
p^{\prime}_{AB}
, which is
\epsilon
-close to
p_{AB}
the right-hand side in Eq.
240
is greater than the left-hand side. Classically, we have the chain rule

\displaystyle D(p_{AB}||q_{AB})=D(p_{B}||q_{B})+\Expect_{B\sim p_{B}}[D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})].

(242)

Note that both the above terms are finite (
q(a|b)\geq\delta
is given). We will bound
\max_{b}D(p_{A|b}||q_{A|b})
and then use Lemma
C.1
to create a bound for the expectation in terms of
p^{\prime}
. For a given
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle|D(p_{A|b}||q_{A|b})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{a}p(a|b)\log\frac{p(a|b)}{q(a|b)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{a}p(a|b)\log p(a|b)\right|+\left|\sum_{a}p(a|b)\log\frac{1}{q(a|b)}\right|

\displaystyle=H(A|B=b)_{p}+\sum_{a}p(a|b)\log\frac{1}{q(a|b)}

\displaystyle\leq\log(|\mathcal{A}|)+\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

Now, using Lemma
C.1
,

\displaystyle\Expect_{B\sim p_{B}}[D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})]\leq\Expect_{B\sim p^{\prime}_{B}}[D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})]+2\epsilon\left(\log(|\mathcal{A}|)+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right).

(243)

Finally, we need to change the
p_{A|B}
in
D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})
to
p^{\prime}_{A|B}
.

\displaystyle D(p_{A|b}||q_{A|b})

\displaystyle=-H(A|B=b)_{p}+\Expect_{A\sim p_{A|b}}\left[\log\frac{1}{q(A|b)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-H(A|B=b)_{p^{\prime}}+h\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|b}-p^{\prime}_{A|b}\right\|_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|b}-p^{\prime}_{A|b}\right\|_{1}\log(|\mathcal{A}|)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\Expect_{A\sim p^{\prime}_{A|b}}\left[\log\frac{1}{q(A|b)}\right]+\left\|p_{A|b}-p^{\prime}_{A|b}\right\|_{1}\log\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle=D(p^{\prime}_{A|b}||q_{A|b})+h\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|b}-p^{\prime}_{A|b}\right\|_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|b}-p^{\prime}_{A|b}\right\|_{1}\left(2\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log(|\mathcal{A}|)\right)

where we used the Fannes-Audenaert continuity bound
[
Wil13
, Theorem 11.10.2]
and Lemma
C.1
in the second line. Taking the expectation over
p^{\prime}_{B}
, we get

\displaystyle\Expect_{B\sim p^{\prime}_{B}}[D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})]

\displaystyle\leq\Expect_{B\sim p^{\prime}_{B}}\left[D(p^{\prime}_{A|B}||q_{A|B})+h\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|B}-p^{\prime}_{A|B}\right\|_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|p_{A|B}-p^{\prime}_{A|B}\right\|_{1}\left(2\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log(|\mathcal{A}|)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Expect_{B\sim p^{\prime}_{B}}\left[D(p^{\prime}_{A|B}||q_{A|B})\right]+h\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|p^{\prime}_{B}p_{A|B}-p^{\prime}_{AB}\right\|_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|p^{\prime}_{B}p_{A|B}-p^{\prime}_{AB}\right\|_{1}\left(2\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log|\mathcal{A}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Expect_{B\sim p^{\prime}_{B}}\left[D(p^{\prime}_{A|B}||q_{A|B})\right]+h(2\epsilon)+2\epsilon\left(2\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log|\mathcal{A}|\right)

\displaystyle=D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})+h(2\epsilon)+2\epsilon\left(2\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log|\mathcal{A}|\right)

where we have used the fact that if
\left\|p_{AB}-p^{\prime}_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, then
\left\|p^{\prime}_{B}p_{A|B}-p^{\prime}_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon
. This can be derived using the triangle inequality. Putting this in Eq.
243
, we get

\displaystyle\Expect_{B\sim p_{B}}[D(p_{A|B}||q_{A|B})]\leq D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})+h(2\epsilon)+6\epsilon\log\frac{1}{\delta}+4\epsilon\log|\mathcal{A}|.

Therefore, using Eq.
242
, we get

\displaystyle D(p_{AB}||q_{AB})\leq D(p_{B}||q_{B})+D(p^{\prime}_{AB}||p^{\prime}_{B}q_{A|B})+h(2\epsilon)+6\epsilon\log\frac{1}{\delta}+4\epsilon\log|\mathcal{A}|.

∎

Appendix D
Markov chain condition for all input states implies independence

In the following lemma, we show that if all outputs of a channel satisfy a certain Markov chain condition with the reference registers, then under some dimension constraints the output of the channel is independent of the input. Due to this fact, we choose to state the unstructured approximate EAT using the independence condition for the side information (Eq.
111
). We expect that this lemma can be improved further.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
R
be registers such that
|A|=|B|
and
|R|=|A||B|
. Let
\cM:R\rightarrow C
be a channel such that for all input states
\rho^{(0)}_{ABR}
the output
\rho_{ABC}=\cM(\rho^{(0)})
satisfies the Markov chain
A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C
. Then, we have that
\cM(X_{R})=\tr(X)\omega_{C}
for some state
\omega_{C}
.

Proof.

Since
|R|=|A||B|
, we can view
R
as the registers
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}
, where
A\equiv A
and
B^{\prime}\equiv B
. We can construct the Choi matrix of this channel as

\displaystyle J_{ABC}=\cM\left(\ket{\Phi}\bra{\Phi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\right)

(244)

where
\ket{\Phi}
is used to denote the unnormalised maximally entangled state, i.e.,
\ket{\Phi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}:=\sum_{a,b}\ket{ab}_{AB}\ket{ab}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\ket{\Phi}_{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\ket{\Phi}_{BB^{\prime}}
and
\cM
is viewed as a channel from
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\rightarrow AB
. Since, all outputs of
\cM
satisfy the Markov chain
A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C
, we have that

\displaystyle J_{ABC}

\displaystyle=J_{AB}J_{B}^{-1}J_{BC}

\displaystyle=\Id_{A}\otimes\Id_{B}\ \cdot\ |A|^{-1}\Id_{B}\ \cdot\ \cM(\Id_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\ket{\Phi}\bra{\Phi}_{BB^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\Id_{A}\otimes\cM(\tau_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\ket{\Phi}\bra{\Phi}_{BB^{\prime}})

(245)

where
\tau_{A^{\prime}}=|A|^{-1}\Id_{A^{\prime}}
is the maximally mixed state on
A
. Let’s define
\cN:{B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}
as

\displaystyle\cN(X_{B^{\prime}})=\cM\left(\tau_{A^{\prime}}\otimes X_{B^{\prime}}\right).

(246)

Since, the Choi matrix is unique, we can see that

\displaystyle\cM_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}=\cN_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\circ\tr_{A^{\prime}}.

(247)

Let
W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
be the swap unitary matrix, i.e.,
W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\ket{ab}=\ket{ba}
. Then, note that for all input states
\rho_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
, the output
\cM(W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\rho_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{\dagger})
satisfies the Markov chain
A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C
according to the hypthosis in the lemma statement. In particular, we can carry out the above argument using the channel
\cM(W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\ \cdot\ W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{\dagger})
and that gives us that for all operators
X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\cM(W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\cN^{\prime}_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\left(\tr_{A^{\prime}}(X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right)

(248)

which implies that

\displaystyle\cM(X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\cN^{\prime}_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\left(\tr_{A^{\prime}}(W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}W_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{\dagger})\right)

\displaystyle=\cN^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\left(\tr_{B^{\prime}}(X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right)

(249)

Using Eq.
247
and
249
for
X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\sigma_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}
where
\sigma_{A^{\prime}}
and
\sigma_{B^{\prime}}
are arbitrary states, we have that

\displaystyle\cN_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\left(\sigma_{B^{\prime}}\right)=\cN^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow C}\left(\sigma_{A^{\prime}}\right)

(250)

which implies that both of these must be equal to a constant state. Let’s call the state
\omega_{C}
. Using the fact that
\cM
is trace-preserving, we then have that
\cM_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}\rightarrow C}(X_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})=\tr(X)\omega_{C}
.
∎

Appendix E
Testing for unstructured approximate entropy accumulation

To incorporate testing to Theorem
4.1
, we follow
[
MFSR22
]
, which is itself based on
[
DF19
]
.

First, we will define the testing channels
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. These channels measure the outputs
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
of the state
\rho
and output a result
X_{k}
based on these measurements. Concretely, for every
k\in[n]
the channel
\mathcal{T}_{k}:A_{k}B_{k}\rightarrow A_{k}B_{k}X_{k}
is of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(\omega_{A_{k}B_{k}})=\sum_{a,b}\Pi_{A_{k}}^{(a)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{k}}^{(b)}\omega_{A_{k}B_{k}}\Pi_{A_{k}}^{(a)}\otimes\Pi_{B_{k}}^{(b)}\otimes\ket{x(a,b)}\bra{x(a,b)}_{X_{k}}

(251)

where
\{\Pi_{A_{k}}^{(a)}\}_{a}
and
\{\Pi_{B_{k}}^{(b)}\}_{b}
are orthogonal projectors and
x(\cdot)
is some deterministic function which uses the measurements
a
and
b
to create the output register
X_{k}
.

Using these orthogonal projectors, we further define the orthogonal projectors on the registers
A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\Pi_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}}^{(a_{1}^{k},b_{1}^{k})}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Pi_{A_{i}}^{(a_{i})}\otimes\Pi_{B_{i}}^{(b_{i})}\right)

(252)

for every
a_{1}^{k},b_{1}^{k}
. Together these form a measurement on the registers
A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}
.

Next we define the min-tradeoff functions. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of probability distributions over the alphabet of
X
registers. Let
R
be any register isomorphic to
R_{k}
. For a probability
q\in\mathbb{P}
and a channel
\cN_{k}:R_{k}\rightarrow A_{k}B_{k}
, we define the set

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}(q|\cN_{k}):=\left\{\nu_{A_{k}B_{k}X_{k}R}=\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cN_{k}(\omega_{R_{k}R}):\text{ for a state }\omega_{R_{k-1}R}\text{ such that }\nu_{X_{k}}=q\right\}.

(253)

Definition E.1
.

A function
f:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a min-tradeoff function for the channels
\{\cN_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
if for every
k
, it satisfies

\displaystyle f(q)\leq\inf_{\nu\in\Sigma_{k}(q|\cN_{k})}H(A_{k}|B_{k}R)_{\nu}.

(254)

We will also need the definitions of the following simple properties of the min-tradeoff functions for our entropy accumulation theorem:

\displaystyle\text{max}(f):=\max_{q\in\mathbb{P}}f(q)

(255)

\displaystyle\text{min}(f):=\min_{q\in\mathbb{P}}f(q).

(256)

We now state the unstructured approximate EAT with testing.

Theorem E.2
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and for every
k\in[n]
, the registers
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
be such that
|A_{k}|=|A|
and
|B_{k}|=|B|
. Suppose, the state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}
is such that

1.

The registers
X_{1}^{n}
can be recreated by applying the testing maps to the registers
A_{1}^{n}
and
B_{1}^{n}
, that is,

\displaystyle\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}=\mathcal{T}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})

(257)

2.

For every
k\in[n]
, there exists a channel
\cM_{k}:R_{k}\rightarrow A_{k}B_{k}
and a state
\theta_{B_{k}}^{(k)}
such that

\displaystyle\tr_{X_{k}}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cM_{k}=\cM_{k}

(258)

\displaystyle\tr_{A_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}(X_{R_{k}})=\tr(X)\theta_{B_{k}}^{(k)}\quad\text{ for all operators }X_{R_{k}}

(259)

and a state
\tilde{\rho}^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}ER_{k}}
for which

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}-\cM_{k}(\tilde{\rho}^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}ER_{k}})\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon.

(260)

Then, for an event
\Omega
defined using
X_{1}^{n}
, an affine min-tradeoff function
f
for
\{\cM_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
such that for every
x_{1}^{n}\in\Omega
,
f(\text{freq}(x_{1}^{n}))\geq h
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq n(h-V(3\sqrt{\mu}+4\epsilon)-g_{2}(2\epsilon))

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{V}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left(2\log\frac{1}{P_{\rho}(\Omega)-\mu}+\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}+2\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}+g_{1}(\epsilon^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\right)

(261)

where

\displaystyle\mu:=\left(\frac{8\sqrt{\epsilon}+2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon^{2}/(|A||B|)^{2}}\log\frac{|A||B|}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/3}

(262)

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}:=2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{P_{\rho}(\Omega)}}

(263)

\displaystyle V:=\log\left(1+2|A|\right)+\lceil\max(f)-\min(f)\rceil

(264)

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,y):=-\log(1-\sqrt{1-x^{2}})-\log(1-y^{2})

(265)

\displaystyle g_{2}(x):=x\log\frac{1}{x}+(1+x)\log(1+x)

(266)

and
\epsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1)
such that
\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}<1
.

Proof.

We first define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}:=\left\{X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}:X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}=\sum_{a_{1}^{k},b_{1}^{k}}\Pi^{(a_{1}^{k}b_{1}^{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}}X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\Pi^{(a_{1}^{k}b_{1}^{k})}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}}\right\}

(267)

for every
0\leq k\leq n
. It should be noted that each of these sets is a
unital algebra
, i.e., a vector space over the field
\mathbb{C}
closed under the matrix product containing identity
\Id_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}
. It is also easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}\otimes\Id_{A_{k+1}B_{k+1}}:=\left\{X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\otimes\Id_{A_{k+1}B_{k+1}}:X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{k+1}

(268)

\displaystyle\tr_{A_{k}B_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{k}):=\left\{X_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}:X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{k-1}.

(269)

Finally, it should be noted that for all operators
X_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}

\displaystyle\tr_{X_{1}^{k}}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X\right)=X.

(270)

The above equation in particular implies that one can measure a state in
\mathcal{A}_{k}

\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}
without disturbing the state. All the auxiliary states defined during this proof will be contained inside an appropriate set
\mathcal{A}_{k}
. Noting that these sets are, in fact, an algebra will make it easier to see this. For example, observe that each partial state
\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}

Case 1:

(271)

\displaystyle\tilde{\tilde{\rho}}^{(k,1)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}ER_{k}}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{T}_{k-1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}(\tilde{\tilde{\rho}}^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}ER_{k}})

(282)

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}-\omega^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{2}+1)P(\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E},\tilde{\tilde{\rho}}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E})

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{2}+1)\sqrt{2(\epsilon+\nu)}

(283)

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small parameter (to be set equal to

\displaystyle\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}}:=(1-\delta)\rho_{A_{k}B_{k}}+\delta\tau_{A_{k}B_{k}}.

(284)

Finally, for every
k\in[n]
, we define the states

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}:=

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\omega^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}+\delta\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}

(285)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\omega^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}+\delta\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\omega^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}

Also, define
\bar{\rho}^{(0)}_{E}:=\rho_{E}
. For each
0\leq k\leq n
, the state

We also have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}-\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right\|_{1}\leq 4\sqrt{\epsilon+\nu}+\delta

(290)

and by the definition of

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\tau_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}\leq\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}

Using Corollary

\displaystyle D(\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}||\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E})

We define the above bound as

. Using Lemma

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right)^{\frac{1-it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(k+1)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right)^{-\frac{1-it}{2}}\right]\cdot\bar{\rho}^{(n)}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}\cdot\prod_{k=n-1}^{0}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(k+1)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right)^{-\frac{1+it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right)^{\frac{1+it}{2}}\right]

(293)

we have that

\displaystyle D_{m}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})\leq nz(\epsilon+\nu,\delta)

(294)

and

\displaystyle\sigma

{}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{\frac{1-it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j+1)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{-\frac{1-it}{2}}\right]\cdot\bar{\rho}^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\cdot\prod_{j=k-1}^{0}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j+1)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{-\frac{1+it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{\frac{1+it}{2}}\right]

(295)

\displaystyle=\cM^{\delta}_{k}\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\beta_{0}(t)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{\frac{1-it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j+1)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{-\frac{1-it}{2}}\right]\cdot\omega^{(k,1)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E}\cdot\prod_{j=k-1}^{0}\left[\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j+1)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{-\frac{1+it}{2}}\left(\bar{\rho}^{(j)}_{A_{1}^{j}B_{1}^{j}E}\right)^{\frac{1+it}{2}}\right]\bigg)

(296)

for all
k\in[n]
. Let
\sigma^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E}
be the input state for
\cM^{\delta}_{k}
above, so that

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}

\displaystyle=\cM^{\delta}_{k}\left(\sigma^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E}\right)

(297)

It is also easy to see using the properties of the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{k}
that
\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
for each
k
. Thus, we can extend the state
\sigma
as follows using measurements
(\mathcal{T}_{i})_{i}

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}\right).

Note that the partial state

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}X_{1}^{k}E}

\displaystyle=\tr_{A_{k+1}^{n}B_{k+1}^{n}X_{k+1}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{T}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\tr_{A_{k+1}^{n}B_{k+1}^{n}X_{k+1}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{T}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{k+1}\left(\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}\right)

(298)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cM^{\delta}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k-1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\sigma^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E}\right)

(299)

Let’s define
\mu:=z(\epsilon+\nu,\delta)^{1/3}
. Using the substate theorem (Theorem
2.2
), we get the following bound from the above relative entropy bound

\displaystyle D^{\mu}_{\max}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})\leq n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(300)

which using data processing also implies that

\displaystyle D^{\mu}_{\max}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E})\leq n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(301)

The bound above implies that there exists a state
{\rho}^{\prime}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}
, which is also classical on
X_{1}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle P\left(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E},{\rho}^{\prime}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}\right)\leq\mu

(302)

and

\displaystyle{\rho}^{\prime}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}\leq\frac{e^{n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}}}{1-\mu^{2}}\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E}.

(303)

The registers
X_{1}^{n}
for
{\rho}^{\prime}
can be chosen to be classical, since the channel measuring
X_{1}^{n}
only decreases the distance between
{\rho}^{\prime}
and
\rho
, and the new state produced would also satisfy Eq.
303
. As the registers
X_{1}^{n}
are classical for both
\sigma
and
{\rho}^{\prime}
, we can condition these states on the event
\Omega
. We will call the probability of the event
\Omega
for the state
\sigma
and
{\rho}^{\prime}

P_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and
P_{{\rho}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
respectively. Using
[
MD23
, Lemma G.1]
and the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega},{\rho}^{\prime}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega}\right)\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{P_{{\rho}}(\Omega)}}.

(304)

Conditioning Eq.
303
on
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle P_{{\rho}^{\prime}}(\Omega){\rho}^{\prime}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega}\leq\frac{e^{n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}}}{1-\mu^{2}}P_{\sigma}(\Omega)\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega}.

(305)

Together, the above two equations imply that

\displaystyle D^{\mu^{\prime}}_{\max}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E|\Omega})\leq n\mu+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}+\log\frac{P_{\sigma}(\Omega)}{P_{{\rho}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}

(306)

for
\mu^{\prime}:=2\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{P_{{\rho}}(\Omega)}}
.

For
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that
\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}<1
and
\alpha\in(1,2]
, we can plug the above in the bound provided by Lemma
2.1
to get

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma_{|\Omega}}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}n\mu

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\alpha\log\frac{P_{\sigma}(\Omega)}{P_{{\rho}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\frac{\alpha}{\mu^{2}}+\alpha\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}+g_{1}(\epsilon^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\right).

(307)

Now, note that using Eq.
251
and
298
, and
[
DFR20
, Lemma B.7]
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma_{|\Omega}}=\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma_{|\Omega}}.

(308)

For every
k
, we introduce a register
D_{k}
of dimension
|D|:=\lceil e^{\max(f)-\min(f)}\rceil
and the channels
\mathcal{D}_{k}:X_{k}\rightarrow X_{k}D_{k}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}(\omega):=\sum_{x}\braket{x|\omega|x}\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\nu_{x}

(309)

where for every

, the state
\nu_{x}
is a mixture between a uniform distribution on
\{1,2,\cdots\ ,\lfloor e^{\max(f)-f(\delta_{x})}\rfloor\}
and a uniform distribution on
\{1,2,\cdots\ ,\lceil e^{\max(f)-f(\delta_{x})}\rceil\}
, so that

\displaystyle H(D_{k})_{\nu_{x}}=\max(f)-f(\delta_{x})

(310)

where
\delta_{x}
is the distribution with unit weight at element
x
. Define the state

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}E}:={\mathcal{D}}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{D}}_{1}({\sigma}_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}E})

(311)

Now
[
MFSR22
, Lemma 4.5]
implies that this satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}_{|\Omega}}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}_{|\Omega}}-\max_{x_{1}^{n}\in\Omega}H_{\alpha}(D_{1}^{n})_{\bar{\sigma}_{|x_{1}^{n}}}

(312)

For
x_{1}^{n}\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(D_{1}^{n})_{\bar{\sigma}_{|x_{1}^{n}}}

\displaystyle\leq H(D_{1}^{n})_{\bar{\sigma}_{|x_{1}^{n}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(D_{k})_{\nu_{x_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\max(f)-f(\delta_{x_{k}})

\displaystyle=n\max(f)-nf(\text{freq}(x_{1}^{n}))

\displaystyle\leq n\max(f)-nh.

(313)

We can get rid of the conditioning on the right-hand side of Eq.

by using
[
DFR20
, Lemma B.5]
. This gives us

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{1}{P_{\sigma}(\Omega)}

(314)

Moreover, using Eq.
299
, we can show that
B_{k}
is independent of
A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}D_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E
in
\bar{\sigma}
. Firstly, for
\sigma_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k}E}=\tr_{A_{k}}\circ\cM^{\delta}_{k}(\sigma^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E})
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k}E}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\tr_{A_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}(\sigma^{(k,0)}_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}R_{k}E})+\delta\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{B_{k}}\otimes\sigma_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}

(315)

\displaystyle=\left((1-\delta)\theta^{(k)}_{B_{k}}+\delta\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{B_{k}}\right)\otimes\sigma_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}.

(316)

Since,
\bar{\sigma}_{A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}D_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k}E}=\mathcal{D}_{k-1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k-1}\cdots\circ\mathcal{D}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{1}(\sigma_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k}E})
, we can easily see that
B_{k}
is independent of the other registers. In particular,
\bar{\sigma}
satisfies the Markov chain
A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}D_{1}^{k-1}\leftrightarrow B_{1}^{k-1}E\leftrightarrow B_{k}
. Let’s define the channels

\displaystyle\bar{\cM}_{k}:=\mathcal{D}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cM_{k}

(317)

\displaystyle\bar{\cM}^{\delta}_{k}:=\mathcal{D}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}\circ\cM^{\delta}_{k}.

(318)

Now we can use
[
DFR20
, Corollary 3.5]
to show that for every
k\in[n]

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k}X_{1}^{k}D_{1}^{k}|B_{1}^{k}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}D_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}+\inf_{\omega_{R_{k}\tilde{R}_{k}}}\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}X_{k}D_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\bar{\cM}_{k}^{\delta}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}D_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\min\left\{\inf_{\omega_{R_{k}\tilde{R}_{k}}}\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\bar{\cM}_{k}(\omega)},\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k})_{\mathcal{D}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}(\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}})}\right\}.

(319)

where we have used the quasi-concavity of Rényi conditional entropies
[
Tom16
, Pg 73]
and the fact that
X_{k}
are classical in the second line.

We now lower bound the two terms in the minimum above. Using
[
DFR20
, Lemma B.9]
, for a state
\nu=\bar{\cM}_{k}(\omega_{R_{k}\tilde{R}_{k}})
and
1<\alpha<\frac{1}{\log(1+2|A||D|)}
we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}

\displaystyle\geq H(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle=H(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}+H(D_{k}|A_{k}B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle=H(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}+H(D_{k}|X_{k})_{\nu}-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle=H(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}+\sum_{x}\nu(x)(\text{max}(f)-f(\delta_{x}))-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle=H(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k})_{\nu}+\text{max}(f)-f(\nu_{X})-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\text{max}(f)-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

(320)

For the second term, and
1<\alpha<\frac{1}{\log(1+2|A||D|)}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k})_{\mathcal{D}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}(\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}})}

\displaystyle\geq H(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k})_{\mathcal{D}_{k}\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}(\rho^{(k,\delta)}_{A_{k}B_{k}})}-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle\geq H(A_{k}D_{k}|B_{k})_{\bar{\cM}_{k}(\tilde{\rho}^{(k,0)}_{R_{k}})}-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-g(\epsilon+\delta)

\displaystyle\geq\text{max}(f)-\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)-2(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-g_{2}(\epsilon+\delta)

(321)

where we have used
[
DFR20
, Lemma B.9]
in the first line, the AFW continuity bound
[
Wil13
, Theorem 11.10.3]
in the second line to convert the entropy on

Plugging these in Eq.

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k}X_{1}^{k}D_{1}^{k}|B_{1}^{k}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-g_{2}(\epsilon+\delta)

(322)

Consecutively using this bound gives us

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}D_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\bar{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\geq n\text{max}(f)-n\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2n(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-ng_{2}(\epsilon+\delta)

(323)

Combining Eq.
308
,
312
,
313
,
314
and

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq nh-n\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)-2n(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-ng_{2}(\epsilon+\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{1}{P_{\sigma}(\Omega)}.

(324)

Plugging this into Eq.
307
, we get

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq nh-n\left(\alpha-1\right)\log^{2}\left(1+2|A||D|\right)-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}n\mu

\displaystyle\qquad-2n(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-ng_{2}(\epsilon+\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\alpha\log\frac{1}{P_{{\rho}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\frac{\alpha}{\mu^{2}}+\alpha\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}+g_{1}(\epsilon^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\right).

(325)

Finally, we choose
\alpha=1+\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\log\left(1+2|A||D|\right)}
and use the
\alpha<2
as an upper bound to derive

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\mu^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho_{|\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq n(h-3\sqrt{\mu}\log\left(1+2|A||D|\right)-2(\epsilon+\delta)\log|A||D|-g_{2}(\epsilon+\delta))

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\log\left(1+2|A||D|\right)}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left(2\log\frac{1}{P_{\rho}(\Omega)-\mu}+\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}+2\log\frac{1}{1-\mu^{2}}+g_{1}(\epsilon^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\right).

(326)

Note that
\log\left(1+2|A||D|\right)\leq\log\left(1+2|A|\right)+\log|D|\leq\log\left(1+2|A|\right)+\lceil\max(f)-\min(f)\rceil=V
. Recall that
\mu=z(\epsilon+\nu,\delta)^{1/3}
. Since,
\nu
can be chosen arbitrarily greater than

, if we let

, the bound in Eq.

is still valid. Finally, choose
\delta=\epsilon
to derive the bound in the theorem.

Case 2:
Finally, for a state

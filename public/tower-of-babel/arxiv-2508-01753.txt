Positivity and 𝐋 𝟐 \mathbf{L^{2}} Extension
Dror Varolin

Abstract
. We examine the relationship between positivity of a vector bundle E and the problem of L 2 L^{2} extension of holomorphic E-valued forms of top degree. In particular, we show by example that Griffiths positivity is not enough. Though the sufficiency of Nakano positivity of E has been known for some time, we provide another proof along the lines of Berndtsson and Lempert. For such a proof we establish a vector bundle analogue of a well-known result of Berndtsson. This result is of independent interest and should have many other useful applications.



1.
Introduction

The
L^{2}
Extension Theorem for holomorphic sections of vector bundles has been of fundamental importance since its original inception by Ohsawa and Takegoshi
[
OT-1987
]
. There are many versions of the result, but perhaps the following version strikes a good balance between generality and relative simplicity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an essentially Stein manifold equipped with a Kähler metric
g
, let
Z
be a smooth hypersurface cut out by a holomorphic section
T\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(L_{Z}))
of the line bundle
L_{Z}\to X
associated to the smooth divisor
Z
, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a smooth metric for
L_{Z}
such that

\sup_{X}|T|^{2}e^{-\lambda}\leq 1.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
such that

(1)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})\geq_{\rm Nak}t\delta\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1].

Then for every
f\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(K_{Z}\otimes E|_{Z}))
satisfying

\int_{Z}|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}<+\infty

there exists
F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{X}\otimes L_{Z}\otimes E))
such that

F|_{Z}=f\wedge dT\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}|F|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}}\leq\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}\int_{Z}|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}.

Though it is not immediately obvious, Theorem
1.1
follows from
[
GZ-2015
, Theorem 2.1]
. (If the optimal constant
\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}
is not insisted upon then the result is much older.) However, the theorem of Guan-Zhou, itself an optimal-constant version of a theorem of Ohsawa
[
O-2001
, Theorem 4]
, is very general, and a large fraction of the content of
[
GZ-2015
]
is devoted to extracting and proving corollaries. Theorem
1.1
is not among these corollaries, so in Section
5
we show how it follows.

In the past few years a link has been drawn between
L^{2}
Extension and Griffiths positivity. In fact, in
[
DNWZ-2023
]
it is shown that
asymptotically universal local
L^{2}
extension from a point
1
1

1

This is not the terminology of
[
DNWZ-2023
]
.
implies Griffiths positivity. The converse was proved in
[
KP-2021
]
for smooth metrics and more generally for metrics whose determinant is locally bounded.

The aforementioned link suggests the following question.

Question
.

If the rank of
E
is at least two, is Theorem
1.1
true if the curvature hypothesis (
1
) is required to hold in the weakest sense, namely in the sense of Griffiths?

Unfortunately the answer is negative, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 1
.

There exist a compact Kähler manifold
X
, a compact complex submanifold
Z\subset X
of codimension
1
, a section
T\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(L_{Z}))
, a metric
{\mathfrak{g}}
for
L_{Z}
, a holomorphic vector bundle
E\to X
and a metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
for
E\to X
such that for some
\delta>0

\sup_{X}|T|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\leq 1,\quad\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})\geq_{\rm Grif}\delta\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1],

and the restriction map

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X}\otimes L_{Z}\otimes E))\ni F\mapsto\frac{F|_{Z}}{dT}\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{Z}\otimes E|_{Z}))

fails to be surjective.

Theorem
1
is proved in Section
3
.

If one nevertheless tries to prove an extension theorem like Theorem
1.1
for a Griffiths-positive vector bundle
E\to X
, the natural step is to pass to the projectivization
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
and try to invoke the rank-1 case of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
6
we investigate this approach.

After the appearance of
[
GZ-2015
]
, Berndtsson and Lempert gave a proof of Theorem
1.1
for functions on domains and under curvature hypotheses that are slightly more restrictive than (
1
). In Section
5
we extend the proof of Berndtsson and Lempert to the setting of vector bundles over Stein manifolds. To do so, we also need a higher-rank version of Berndtsson’s result on positivity of direct images
[
B-2009
, Theorem 1.1]
, which we now formulate.

Let
X
be a relatively compact domain in a complex manifold
Y
of complex dimension
n
, let
B
be a complex manifold of dimension
m
, and denote by
p_{1}:Y\times B\to Y
and
p_{2}:Y\times B\to B
the Cartesian projections. Let
\tilde{E}\to Y
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
. Fix a smooth metric
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
for the holomorphic vector bundle
p_{1}^{*}\tilde{E}\to Y\times B
. We set
E:=\tilde{E}|_{X}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}:=\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}|_{X\times B}
. Thus we have a trivial holomorphic family of vector bundles with non-trivial metric:

(p_{1}^{*}E,{\mathfrak{h}})\to X\times B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\to}}B.

Though possibly non-trivial, the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
very usefully extends smoothly to
\partial X\times B
.

To each
t\in B
we associate the Bergman space

{\mathscr{H}}_{t}:=\left\{f\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X}\otimes E))\ ;\ \int_{X}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}^{n^{2}}\left<f\wedge\bar{f},{\mathfrak{h}}^{(t)}\right><+\infty\right\},

where
{\mathfrak{h}}^{(t)}
is the metric for
E\to X
that we canonically identify with the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}(\cdot,t)
obtained by restricting the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
for
p_{1}^{*}E\to X\times B
to the fiber over
X\times\{t\}
. It is well-known that
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
is a Hilbert space. Since
X
is a bounded domain and the metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}^{(t)}
,
t\in B
, are all smooth up to the boundary of
X
, the vector subspaces
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}\subset H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X}\otimes E))
do not depend on
t
. We can therefore define the trivial vector bundle
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
whose fiber over
t\in B
is
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
. Note that a smooth (resp. holomorphic) section of
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is uniquely determined by a smooth (resp. holomorphic) section of
p_{1}^{*}E\to X\times B
whose restriction to each fiber
X\times\{t\}
lies in
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
.

One defines the metric

(f_{1},f_{2}):=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}^{n^{2}}\int_{X}\left<f_{1}\wedge\bar{f}_{2},{\mathfrak{h}}^{(t)}\right>,\quad f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}

for the possibly infinite-rank vector bundle
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
. Then one has a Chern connection (which is only densely-defined if the rank of
{\mathscr{H}}
is infinite) that we compute in Section
4
. We then have the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

With the above notation, assume that
Y
is Stein and that
X
is pseudoconvex. If

a.

(E,{\mathfrak{h}}^{(t)})
is Nakano-nonnegative on
X
for each
t\in B
and

b.

(E,{\mathfrak{h}})
is
k
-positive (resp.
k
-nonnegative) on
X\times B

then
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is
k
-positive (resp.
k
-nonnegative).

The proof of Theorem
2
occupies Section
4
.

Such a ‘deformation of Bergman spaces’ is a special case of a more general story. A short summary is discussed in Section
3
, and a more detailed presentation can be found in
[
V-2024
]
.

2.
Background and preliminaries

We present some background material for the reader’s convenience and to establish notation.

2.1.
Positivity of holomorphic Hermitian vector bundles

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
. The curvature of the Chern connection is a
{\rm Hom}(E,E)
-valued
(1,1)
-form
\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})
on
X
that is
{\mathfrak{h}}
-skew Hermitian, i.e.,

{\mathfrak{h}}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})v,w)+{\mathfrak{h}}(v,\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})w)=0\quad\text{for all $x\in X$ and all $v,w\in E_{x}$}.

One therefore obtains a Hermitian form on
T^{1,0}_{X}\otimes E\to X
defined on indecomposable tensors by

\{\xi\otimes v,\eta\otimes w\}_{{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})}:={\mathfrak{h}}(\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})_{\xi\bar{\eta}}v,w)

and extended to a Hermitian form on
T^{1,0}_{X}\otimes E
by the universal property for tensor products.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a smooth Hermitian metric.

i.

For a positive integer
k
, we say that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-positive
(resp.
k
-negative) if the Hermitian form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})}
is positive (resp. negative) on all tensors of rank at most
k
.

ii.

Griffiths-positive (resp. negative) means
1
-positive (resp. negative).

iii.

Nakano-positive (resp. negative) means
k
-positive (resp. negative) for all
k>0
.

\diamond

Recall that the rank of a tensor
T\in A\otimes B
is the rank (i.e., the dimension of the image) of the associated linear map
L_{T}:B^{*}\to A
.

Next we recall the interaction between curvature and duality. Given a holomorphic vector bundle
E\to X
equipped with a smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
, one has a smooth, fiber-preserving conjugate-linear map
{\mathscr{R}}:E\to E^{*}
, which we call the
Riesz map
, defined by

{\mathscr{R}}(v)w:={\mathfrak{h}}(w,v).

If we equip
E^{*}
with the dual metric
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
then the Riesz map is a fiberwise conjugate-linear isometry, and more precisely satisfies

{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}w,{\mathscr{R}}v)={\mathfrak{h}}(v,w).

Taking a smooth local section
v
and a holomorphic local section
w
of
E
, we find that

\left<\bar{\partial}({\mathscr{R}}v),w\right>=\bar{\partial}(\left<{\mathscr{R}}(v),w\right>)=\bar{\partial}({\mathfrak{h}}(w,v))={\mathfrak{h}}(w,\nabla^{\prime}v)=\left<{\mathscr{R}}(\nabla^{\prime}v),w\right>.

From the computation

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}v,{\mathscr{R}}\bar{\partial}w)+{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}\nabla^{\prime}v,{\mathscr{R}}w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}(\bar{\partial}w,v)+{\mathfrak{h}}(w,\nabla^{\prime}v)=\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}(w,v)=\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}v,{\mathscr{R}}w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(\bar{\partial}{\mathscr{R}}v,{\mathscr{R}}w)+{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}v,\nabla^{\prime}{\mathscr{R}}w)

we therefore have

\bar{\partial}{\mathscr{R}}={\mathscr{R}}\nabla^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla^{\prime}{\mathscr{R}}={\mathscr{R}}\bar{\partial}.

Hence the curvature
\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})=\bar{\partial}\nabla^{\prime}+\nabla^{\prime}\bar{\partial}
satisfies

(2)

{\mathfrak{h}}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})v,w)={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}w,{\mathscr{R}}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})v)={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}({\mathscr{R}}w,\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}){\mathscr{R}}v)=-{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}){\mathscr{R}}w,{\mathscr{R}}v).

We can thus conclude that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is Griffiths positive if and only if
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is Griffiths negative, but we cannot conclude the same for
k
-positivity as soon as
k\geq 2
.

The calculus identity

(3)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}(s,\sigma)={\mathfrak{h}}(-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})s,\sigma)+\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}{\mathfrak{h}}(\nabla s,\nabla\sigma)

for sections
s,\sigma\in{\mathcal{O}}(E)_{x}
, yields the following analytic characterization of Griffiths positivity.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
. Then the following are equivalent.

a.

{\mathfrak{h}}
is Griffiths negative.

b.

For every
x\in X
and every
s\in{\mathcal{O}}(E)_{x}
the function
{\mathfrak{h}}(s,s)
is plurisubharmonic.

c.

For every
x\in X
and every
s\in{\mathcal{O}}(E)_{x}
the function
\log{\mathfrak{h}}(s,s)
is plurisubharmonic.

Proof.

The equivalence of (b) and (c) follows easily from
[
H-1990
, Theorem 1.6.3]
. To show that (a) and (b) are equivalent, take
s=\sigma
in (
3
). If
\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})
is Griffiths negative then the right hand side is positive for all
s
, so
{\mathfrak{h}}(s,s)
is plurisubharmonic. Conversely if
{\mathfrak{h}}(s,s)
is plurisubharmonic for all local holomorphic sections
s
then in particular
{\mathfrak{h}}(s,s)
is plurisubharmonic for all
s
satisfying
\nabla s(x)=0
for some given
x
. Since (i) Griffiths negativity/positivity is a pointwise condition and (ii) every vector in
E_{x}
can be interpolated by a local holomorphic section with vanishing covariant derivative at
x
, we see that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is Griffiths negative.
∎

In
[
B-2009
]
Berndtsson observed that one can similarly characterize
k
-negativity and
k
-positivity. To do so, we introduce some notation. For a holomorphic coordinate system
z
we define the smooth
(n-1,n-1)
-forms

(4)

\Upsilon^{i\bar{j}}(z):=c_{i\bar{j}}dz^{1}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{dz^{i}}\wedge...\wedge d\bar{z}^{1}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{d\bar{z}^{j}}\wedge...\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}

with
c_{i\bar{j}}
a constant so that
\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}\wedge\Upsilon^{i\bar{j}}=dV(z)
.

Hereafter, the complex version of the Einstein summation convention is in force.

We begin with the following slight generalization of Berndtsson’s observation.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
.

i.

{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-negative if and only if for every local holomorphic coordinate system
(U,z)
and every
k
-tuple of local holomorphic sections
f_{1},...,f_{k}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
of
E
the
(n,n)
-form

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{i},f_{j})\wedge\Upsilon^{i\bar{j}}(z)

is positive.

ii.

{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-positive at
x\in X
if and only if for every local holomorphic coordinate system
(U,z)
and every
k
-tuple of local holomorphic sections
f_{1},...,f_{k}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
of
E
satisfying
\nabla f_{i}(x)=0
the
(n,n)
-form

-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{i},f_{j})\wedge\Upsilon^{i\bar{j}}(z)

is positive at
x
.

Proof.

First note that a tensor
T\in T^{1,0}_{X,x}\otimes E_{x}
has rank
k
if and only if there is a coordinate system
(U,z)
and local holomorphic sections
f_{1},...,f_{k}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
such that

T=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z^{1}}\otimes f_{1}(x)+\cdots+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}\otimes f_{k}(x).

From (
3
) we compute that

(5)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{i},f_{j})\Upsilon^{i\bar{j}}=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}-{\mathfrak{h}}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})_{i\bar{j}}f_{i},f_{j})+{\mathfrak{h}}\left(\sum_{i}\nabla_{i}f_{i},\sum_{j}\nabla_{j}f_{j}\right)\right)dV(z).

Item (ii) follows easily from (
5
) and the already-cited fact that for every
x\in X
, every vector in
E_{x}
can be interpolated by a local holomorphic section of
E
with vanishing covariant derivative at
x
.

Turning to (i), note that while the second term on the right hand side of (
5
) is always positive, the first term on the right hand side of (
5
) is positive if and only if
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-negative. Therefore if
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-negative then the left hand side of (
5
) is positive for all coordinate systems
z
and all local holomorphic sections
f_{1},...,f_{k}
. Conversely, the left hand side of (
5
) is positive for all coordinate systems z and all local holomorphic sections
f_{1},...,f_{k}
then in particular it is positive for local holomorphic sections satisfying
\nabla f_{i}(x)=0
at some
x\in X
, so
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k
-negative.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle.

a.

The set of
k
-negative smooth metrics for
E
is a convex cone with respect to addition of metrics.

b.

The set of Griffiths positive smooth metrics for
E
is a convex cone with respect to the addition

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{+}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}:=\left({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}^{*}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}^{*}\right)^{*}.

Remark 2.5
.

On the other hand, since for
k\geq 2
duality does not interchange
k
-negative smooth Hermitian metrics and
k
-positive smooth Hermitian metrics, we cannot argue
k
-positivity is preserved under harmonic-convex combinations. In fact, we don’t know if for
k\geq 2
the notion
k
-positivity is closed under harmonic sum, or for that matter, under any useful additive structure on the space of smooth Hermitian metrics.

\diamond

2.2.
Hörmander-Skoda-Demailly Estimates

Hörmander’s Theorem on the
L^{2}
estimates for solutions of the
\bar{\partial}
-equation is among the most widely known results in several complex variables. These estimates were improved by Skoda, and further extended by Demailly. However, there are some degenerate cases that are less widely known. Therefore for the sake of establishing notation, we review the story here.

Hermitian Endomorphisms

Let
X
be a complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian (i.e.,
J
-invariant) Riemannian metric
g
whose metric form we denote
\omega
, and let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle equipped with a smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
. A smooth Hermitian section
\Omega
of
\Lambda^{1,1}_{X}\otimes{\rm Hom}(E,E)\to X
acts on an
E
-valued
(p,q)
-form
\alpha
by the formula given in local coordinates
z=(z^{1},...,z^{n})
and a holomorphic frame
\mathbf{e}_{1},...,\mathbf{e}_{r}
(with dual frame
\mathbf{e}^{*1},...,\mathbf{e}^{*r}
) as

(6)

\Omega^{\sharp_{g}}\left(\alpha^{\mu}_{I\bar{J}}dz^{I}\wedge d\bar{z}^{J}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\mu}\right):=g^{i\bar{\ell}}\Omega^{\mu}_{\nu i\bar{j}_{\gamma}}\alpha^{\mu}_{I\bar{j}_{1}...(\bar{\ell})_{\gamma}...\bar{j}_{q}}dz^{I}\wedge d\bar{z}^{J}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\mu},

where
g=g_{i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\cdot d\bar{z}^{j}
,
(g^{i\bar{j}})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{i\bar{j}})
and
\Omega=\Omega^{\mu}_{\nu i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\mu}\otimes\mathbf{e}^{*\nu}
. The notation
(a)_{k}
means that the
k^{\text{th}}
entry is replaced by
a
. (Formula (
6
) appears in the Bochner-Kodaira Identity; see
[
Siu-1982
, Page 63]
.)

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a Hermitian section
\Omega
of
\Lambda^{1,1}_{X}\otimes{\rm Hom}(E,E)\to X
is
k
-positive at

if the associated Hermitian form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\mathfrak{h}},\Omega}
defined on rank-1 tensors in

by

\{\xi\otimes v,\eta\otimes w\}_{{\mathfrak{h}},\Omega}:={\mathfrak{h}}(\Omega_{\xi\bar{\eta}}v,w)

is positive definite on all tensors of Rank

.

\diamond

Remark
.

Hermitian symmetry and positivity of
\Omega
are independent of the metric

.

\diamond

Demailly proved the following result.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
D-2012
, Lemma VII(7.2)]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension

be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank

. Fix integers

\Omega^{\sharp_{g}}:\Lambda^{n,q}_{X}\otimes E\to\Lambda^{n,q}_{X}\otimes E

Together with standard

Theorem 2.8

Let
X

, let

be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank

and let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a smooth Hermitian metric for

. Fix integers

and

, and let
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{1,1}_{X}\otimes{\rm Hom}(E,E)))

-positive. If
\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})-\Omega

-valued

-form

\bar{\partial}\beta=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}\left<(\Omega^{\sharp_{q}})^{-1}\beta,\beta\right>_{{\mathfrak{h}},g}<+\infty

-form

The case of
(n,1)
-forms

Let
X
be a complete Kähler manifold equipped with a (possibly not complete) Kähler metric
g
and let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle equipped with a smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
having non-negative curvature in the sense of Nakano. Theorem
2.8
(with
\Omega=\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})
) states that for every
\bar{\partial}
-closed
K_{X}\otimes E
-valued
(0,1)
-form
\beta
such that

(7)

\int_{X}\left<\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})^{\sharp_{g}}\right)^{-1}\beta,\beta\right>_{{\mathfrak{h}},g}dV_{g}<+\infty

there exists a measurable (and smooth if
\beta
is smooth) section
u
of
K_{X}\otimes E\to X
such that

(8)

\bar{\partial}u=\beta\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}|u|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dV_{g}\leq\int_{X}\left<\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})^{\sharp_{g}}\right)^{-1}\beta,\beta\right>_{{\mathfrak{h}},g}dV_{g}.

Let
{\mathscr{L}}_{o}
denote the space of all measurable sections
F
of
K_{X}\otimes E\to X
whose
L^{2}
-norm

||F||:=\left(\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}^{n^{2}}\int_{X}\left<F\wedge\bar{F},{\mathfrak{h}}\right>\right)^{1/2}

is finite. The subspace
{\mathscr{H}}_{o}:={\mathscr{L}}_{o}\cap H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X}\otimes E))
of
{\mathscr{L}}_{o}
is closed, as is
{\mathscr{H}}_{o}^{\perp}
. Let

P_{o}:{\mathscr{L}}_{o}\to{\mathscr{H}}_{o}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{o}:={\rm Id}-P_{o}:{\mathscr{L}}_{o}\to{\mathscr{H}}_{o}^{\perp}

denote the orthogonal projections. Evidently if
F\in{\mathscr{L}}_{o}
then
N_{o}F
is orthogonal to
{\mathscr{H}}_{o}
. Thus
N_{o}F
is the minimal solution of the equation
\bar{\partial}u=\bar{\partial}F
. If
\beta:=\bar{\partial}F
also satisfies (
7
) then the estimate (
8
) yields the following result.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume that the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
has non-negative curvature in the sense of Nakano. Then for any
F\in{\rm Domain}(\bar{\partial})\subset{\mathscr{L}}_{o}
one has the estimate

\int_{X}|N_{o}F|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\leq\int_{X}\left<\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})^{\sharp_{g}}\right)^{-1}\bar{\partial}F,\bar{\partial}F\right>_{g,{\mathfrak{h}}},

provided the right hand side is finite.

2.3.
Tautological vector bundles

2.3.i.
The tautological line bundle

Let
V
be a complex vector space of complex dimension
r
. We denote by
V^{*}
the dual complex vector space, and by
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
the projectivization of
V^{*}
, i.e., the set of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
V^{*}
. The tautological line bundle
\pi:{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
assigns to each
\ell\in{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
the
1
-dimensional vector space
\ell\subset V^{*}
. The dual line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(1):={\mathcal{O}}(-1)^{*}
is called the hyperplane line bundle, and one sets
{\mathcal{O}}(\pm k):={\mathcal{O}}(\pm 1)^{\otimes k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Elements of the space
V^{**}=V
of linear functions on
V^{*}
can be seen as holomorphic sections of
{\mathcal{O}}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
, the dual of
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
. It is easy to show that conversely every section of
{\mathcal{O}}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
arises in this way, i.e.,

(9)

H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}(V^{*}),{\mathcal{O}}(1))=V.

If
V
is equipped with a Hermitian inner product
{\mathfrak{g}}
then one has a smooth Hermitian metric
e^{\varphi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
for
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
that assigns to a vector
v\in{\mathcal{O}}(-1)_{\ell}\cong\ell\subset V^{*}
its
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
-square length

|v|^{2}e^{\varphi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}}:={\mathfrak{g}}^{*}(v,v),

where
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is the dual Hermitian inner product. The dual metric
e^{-\varphi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
for
{\mathcal{O}}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
is called the Fubini-Study metric associated to
{\mathfrak{g}}
, or simply the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-Fubini-Study metric. Its curvature
\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
induces a Kähler metric

\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}:=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}

on
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
, unfortunately also called the (
{\mathfrak{g}}
-)Fubini-Study metric. If
\{\mathbf{e_{1}},...,\mathbf{e}_{r}\}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
-orthonormal basis for
V^{*}
then on the affine chart
U_{o}:=\{[t^{i}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}]\ ;\ t^{1}=1\}
we have

\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}[t^{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}]=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1+|t^{\prime}|^{2}),\quad[t^{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}]\in U_{o},

where
t^{\prime}=(t^{2},...,t^{r})
. The form
\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},...,\mathbf{e}_{r}\}
, and hence is globally defined. However,
\omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
does depend on
{\mathfrak{g}}
to some extent.

2.3.ii.
The universal quotient bundle

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space of dimension
r\geq 2
, and let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a Hermitian inner product for
V
. Over the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
one has the exact sequence of vector bundles

(10)

0\to{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\to{\mathbb{V}}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\to}}Q\to 0,

where
{\mathbb{V}}^{*}
denotes the trivial holomorphic vector bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
with fiber
V^{*}
. For
x\in{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
, each element
q(v)\in Q_{x}
is an equivalence class of vectors
v\in V^{*}
, where

q(v)=q(w)\iff v-w\in x.

We equip
{\mathbb{V}}^{*}
with the ‘constant’ Hermitian metric induced by
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, and give
Q
the quotient Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}
, defined by

{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(q(v),q(v)):=\inf\left\{{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(w,w)\ ;\ q(v)=q(w)\right\}.

Equivalently, each equivalence class
q(v)\in Q_{x}
intersects the
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
-orthogonal complement
x^{\perp}
of
x
in
V^{*}
at a unique point
v_{x}
, and

\left|q(v)\right|^{2}={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(v_{x},v_{x}).

In fact,
v_{x}=q^{\dagger}(q(v))
, where
q^{\dagger}:Q\to{\mathbb{V}}^{*}
is defined by

{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(q^{\dagger}(q(v)),w)={\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(q(v),q(w)).

Indeed,
q^{\dagger}:Q\to{\mathbb{V}}^{*}
is clearly injective, and if
w\in x
then

{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(q^{\dagger}(q(v)),w)={\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(q(v),q(w))={\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(q(v),0)=0,

which shows that
q^{\dagger}q(v_{x})\in x^{\perp}
. In other words,
q^{\dagger}:Q\to{\mathbb{V}}^{*}
gives the Hermitian splitting of (
10
).

Finally, we recall without proof the well-known identity

Q\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(1)=T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})}.

(See, for example,
[
D-2012
, Proposition V.15.7, page 279]
.)

2.3.iii.
Holomorphic families of vector spaces

One can adapt the constructions of the Paragraph
2.3.i
to the setting of locally trivial families of vector spaces. Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle. The fiberwise-projectivization of
E\to X
yields a locally trivial projective fibration
p:{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})\to X
, as well as holomorphic line bundles

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1):={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)^{*},

defined fiber-by-fiber as above. There is also a relationship between sections of
E\to X
and sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
analogous to the isomorphism (
9
), which we state as follows.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle. The correspondence

\Gamma(X,E)\ni f\mapsto\hat{f}\in\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}(E^{*}),{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1))

defined by

\left<\hat{f}([v]),v\right>:=\left<f(\pi v),v\right>

is a bijection between sections of
E\to X
and sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
that are holomorphic along the fibers of
{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})\to X
. Moreover, the section
\hat{f}
is holomorphic on
p^{-1}(U)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
if and only if
f
is holomorphic on
\pi^{-1}(U)
. In particular,

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))\ni f\longleftrightarrow\hat{f}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}(E^{*}),{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1))

is an isomorphism.

3.1.
Motivation for Theorem
1
: Deformation of Bergman spaces

Let
X
and
B
be complex manifolds with
B
connected, let
\pi:X\to B
be a proper holomorphic submersion of fiber dimension
n
, and let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
. To each
t\in B
one can associate the finite-dimensional inner product space
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
whose underlying vector space is
H^{0}(X_{t},{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X_{t}}\otimes E|_{X_{t}}))
and whose inner product is

(f,g)_{t}:=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}^{n^{2}}\int_{X_{t}}\left<f\wedge\bar{g},{\mathfrak{h}}\right>.

We endow the disjoint union

{\mathscr{H}}:=\coprod_{t\in B}{\mathscr{H}}_{t}

with a projection map
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
whose fiber over
t\in B
is
{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, and we give this family of vector spaces additional structure by declaring that a section of
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is simply a section of the
E
-twisted relative canonical bundle
K_{X/B}\otimes E\to X
that is holomorphic along the fibers of
\pi:X\to B
. Similarly, we can define sections of
{\mathscr{H}}|_{U}
for any subset
U\subset B
by restricting the family
\pi:X\to B
to
\pi^{-1}(U)
. We denote by
H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}({\mathscr{H}}))
the collection of holomorphic sections of
{\mathscr{H}}|_{U}\to U
. When it increases clarity, we use gothic letters to denote by
{\mathfrak{f}}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}({\mathscr{H}}))
the section of
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
associated to the section
f
of
K_{X/B}\otimes E\to X
such of
\iota_{X_{t}}^{*}f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}
, which we denote by the corresponding latin letter. We say that the section
{\mathfrak{f}}
is smooth, holomorphic, measurable, etc. if this is the case for the section
f
. And if
{\mathfrak{f}}
is a section of
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
then for
t\in B
we define

\iota_{t}{\mathfrak{f}}:=\iota_{X_{t}}^{*}f\in{\mathscr{H}}_{t}.

Definition 3.1
.

The family
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
of Hilbert spaces with the additional structure of holomorphic and smooth sections described above is called the
deformation of Bergman spaces
associated to the holomorphic family of Hermitian holomorphic vector bundles

(E,{\mathfrak{h}})\to X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}B.

If for each point
t\in B
we can find a neighborhood
U\subset B
containing
t
and a collection of sections
{\mathfrak{f}}_{1},...,{\mathfrak{h}}_{N}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}({\mathscr{H}}))
such that

\{\iota_{t}f_{1},...,\iota_{t}f_{N}\}\subset{\mathscr{H}}_{t}

is a basis, then we can give
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
the structure of a holomorphic vector bundle by using
\{\iota_{t}f_{1},...,\iota_{t}f_{N}\}
as a local frame over
U
.

In general, deformations of Bergman spaces need not be vector bundles. Nevertheless, they do carry a natural Hermitian metric as well as a complex structure of sorts. If the deformation is indeed a vector bundle then there is a notion of curvature that one can attach to it, namely the curvature of the Chern connection of the
L^{2}
metric.

In his article
[
B-2009
]
, Berndtsson considered this scenario in the case in which
E\to X
is a holomorphic Hermitian line bundle. He pointed out that by the

Extension Theorem
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle as soon as
E
is semi-positive.

In
[
V-2024
]
the author extended Berndtsson’s work in two ways.

i.

The author provided
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
with a sort of complex analytic structure even when
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is not a holomorphic vector bundle. This structure, called an
iBLS structure
, allows one to define an intrinsic notion of curvature of
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
, which agrees with the curvature of the Chern connection over any open set
U\subset B
on which
{\mathscr{H}}|_{U}
is a holomorphic vector bundle.

ii.

The author extended Berndtsson’s work to the setting in which
E\to X
can have higher rank. This situation was previously considered by Liu and Yang (c.f.
[
LY-2014
]
), but under the assumption that
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
is already a holomorphic vector bundle.

In particular, the author proved the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
V-2024
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
is a Kähler family, i.e., there is a closed form
\omega
on
X
whose restriction to fibers is Kähler. If the Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E\to X
is
k
-nonnegative (resp.
k
-positive) then so is the associated deformation of Bergman spaces
{\mathscr{H}}\to B
.

If the deformation of Bergman spaces

associated to the proper holomorphic Hermitian family of vector bundles is locally trivial then one can deduce Theorem

from the work of Liu-Yang (and more specifically, from
[
LY-2014

implies local triviality of
{\mathscr{H}}
unless

, i.e., unless

is Nakano-positive.

In the next paragraph we find what is probably the simplest example of a Griffiths positive vector bundle for which

Extension fails, thus showing that Berndtsson’s approach to proving local triviality in the rank-1 case cannot be easily extended to the higher-rank setting.

Unfortunately we do not have a good guess as to whether there is a non-locally trivial deformation of Bergman spaces

associated to a proper family of holomorphic Hermitian vector bundles
(E,{\mathfrak{h}})\to X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}B
such that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
k

.

If the holomorphic submersion

is not proper then the resulting deformation of Bergman spaces is too wild to study without further assumptions. Theorem

considers a very special but rather useful case in which the resulting family of Bergman spaces is a trivial vector bundle with non-trivial

]
and
[

]
in the case of a proper family of manifolds-with-boundary.

3.2.
Curvature of the universal quotient bundle

In Paragraph
2.3.ii
we recalled the definition of the universal quotient bundle
Q\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
, the Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle defined by the short exact sequence of holomorphic Hermitian vector bundles

0\to{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\to{\mathbb{V}}\to Q\to 0,

where
{\mathbb{V}}\to{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
is the trivial Hermitian vector bundle with fiber
V^{*}
and constant Hermitian inner product defined by some Hermitian inner product
{\mathfrak{h}}
on
V
.

Let us compute the curvature of the quotient metric
{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}
for
Q
at a point
x_{o}\in{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
. We can choose an
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
-orthonormal basis
e_{0},...,e_{r-1}
for
V^{*}
such that
e_{1}\in x_{o}
. In the open set

U_{x_{o}}\ni x:=[e_{0}+z^{1}e_{1}+\cdots+z^{r-1}e_{r-1}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\mapsto}}z(x)=(z^{1},...,z^{r-1})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r-1},

which has
x_{o}
as its origin, we take the holomorphic frame for
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)|_{U_{x_{o}}}
given by

\varepsilon_{0}(x)=e_{0}+z(x)\cdot\underline{e},

where
\underline{e}=(e_{1},...,e_{r-1})
, and we let
{\mathfrak{e}}_{1},...,{\mathfrak{e}}_{r-1}
be the holomorphic frame for
Q|_{U_{x_{o}}}
given by

{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}=q(e_{j}),\quad e_{j}\in V^{*}\cong H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}(V^{*}),{\mathcal{O}}({\mathbb{V}}^{*})),\ 1\leq j\leq r-1.

Writing
q^{\dagger}({\mathfrak{e}}_{j}):=\zeta_{j}\varepsilon_{0}(x)+e_{j}
, we have

0={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(q^{\dagger}({\mathfrak{e}}_{j}),\varepsilon_{0}(x))={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(\zeta_{j}e_{0}+\zeta_{j}z(x)\cdot\underline{e}+e_{j},e_{0}+z(x)\cdot\underline{e})=\bar{z}^{j}+\zeta_{j}(1+|z|^{2}),

so that

q^{\dagger}({\mathfrak{e}}_{j})=-\frac{\bar{z}^{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})}\varepsilon_{0}(x)+e_{j}.

Now we can compute
{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}
. A general holomorphic section of
Q
can be written as
s=f^{i}{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}
, and

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}({\mathfrak{e}}_{i},{\mathfrak{e}}_{j})=f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(q^{\dagger}{\mathfrak{e}}_{i},q^{\dagger}{\mathfrak{e}}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\left(\frac{-\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}}(e_{o}+z\cdot\underline{e})+e_{i},\frac{-\bar{z}^{j}}{1+|z|^{2}}(e_{o}+z\cdot\underline{e})+e_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}\delta_{i\bar{j}}+f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}\frac{z^{j}\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}}-f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}\frac{z^{j}\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}}-f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}\frac{z^{j}\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{i}\overline{f_{2}^{j}}\left(\delta_{i\bar{j}}-\frac{z^{j}\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}}\right).

It is now straightforward to calculate
\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})
at
x_{o}
: with
v:=b^{i}{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}\in Q_{x_{o}}
and
\xi=a^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\in T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*}),x_{o}}
,

{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}v,v)=|a\cdot\bar{b}|^{2}.

In particular, while
Q
is Griffiths nonnegative, a fact we also know because
Q
is a quotient of a trivial bundle, it is not Griffiths positive. More generally, letting
h
be the matrix with

h_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{i\bar{j}}-\frac{z^{j}\bar{z}^{i}}{1+|z|^{2}},

we have

\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})=\bar{\partial}(\partial hh^{-1})=(\bar{\partial}\partial h)h^{-1}+(\partial h)h^{-1}\wedge(\bar{\partial}h)h^{-1}.

Since
h(0)={\rm Id}
and
\partial h(0)=\bar{\partial}h(0)=0
,

\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})(0)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{r-1}\bar{\partial}\partial h_{i\bar{j}}(0)\otimes E^{*i}\otimes\overline{E^{*j}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{r-1}dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{i}\otimes E^{*i}\otimes\overline{E^{*j}}.

Hence for
\xi_{i}=a^{\ell}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}\in T^{1,0}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*}),x_{o}}
and
v_{i}=b^{\ell}_{i}{\mathfrak{e}}_{\ell}\in Q_{x_{o}}
,

(11)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})_{\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j}}v_{i},v_{j})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})},

which is not
m
-nonnegative for any
m>1
. In particular,
Q
is not Nakano-nonnegative.

Remark
.

Upon interchanging
\xi_{i}
and
\xi_{j}
, we find that
Q
is dual-Nakano nonnegative.

\diamond

If we twist
Q
by
{\mathcal{O}}(k)
with its
{\mathfrak{h}}
-Fubini-Study metric
e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}
, we have

\Theta(e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})=k\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\otimes{\rm Id}_{Q}+\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}).

Using the coordinates
z
above, we have

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1+|z|^{2})=\frac{\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}dz\dot{\wedge}d\bar{z}}{1+|z|^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}(\bar{z}\cdot dz)\wedge(z\cdot d\bar{z})}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}.

and at the origin
x_{o}
we find that
\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
is just the Euclidean metric. Therefore at the origin
x_{o}

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(\Theta(e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})_{\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j}}v_{i},v_{j})=k\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}.

e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}(\Theta(e^{-k\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}v,v)=k|a|^{2}|b|^{2}+|a\cdot\bar{b}|^{2}\geq k|a|^{2}|b|^{2},

so that
{\mathcal{O}}(k)\otimes Q
is Griffiths positive for all
k\geq 1
.

To understand the situation for higher positivity, we first observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i<j}2\operatorname{Re}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}2\operatorname{Re}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}\geq-\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}|a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j}|^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle k\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(a_{i}\cdot\bar{b}_{j})\cdot\overline{(a_{j}\cdot\bar{b}_{i})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}|b_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq m}^{m}\left(|a_{i}|^{2}|b_{j}|^{2}-|a_{i}\cdot b_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}|a_{i}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{j}|^{2}.

We have therefore proved the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

The vector bundle
Q\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(k)
is

(i)

Griffiths-nonnegative but not Griffiths-positive for

,

(ii)

Griffiths-positive but not

-positive for
k=1
, and

(iii)

Nakano positive as soon as
k\geq 2
.

3.3.
Non-extension Theorem for the tangent bundle of a projective plane

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
r\geq 3
equipped with a Hermitian inner product
{\mathfrak{h}}_{o}
, and denote by
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
its projectivization. As before, we let
{\mathbb{V}}
denote the trivial holomorphic Hermitian vector bundle with fiber
V
, where the Hermitian metric is the constant inner product given by
{\mathfrak{h}}_{o}
on each fiber. We give
{\mathcal{O}}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(V)
the Fubini-Study metric
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\rm FS}
associated to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{o}
, i.e.,

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\rm FS}(\xi,\xi):=\frac{\left|\left<\xi,v\right>\right|^{2}}{{\mathfrak{h}}_{o}(v,v)},

and we write

{\mathbb{V}}(1):={\mathbb{V}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(1).

As recalled at the end of Paragraph
2.3.ii
, the tangent bundle of
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
is characterized by the sequence of Hermitian vector bundles

(12)

0\to{\mathcal{O}}\to{\mathbb{V}}(1)\to T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\to 0.

If we twist
T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}
by
{\mathcal{O}}(k)
for any positive
k
then the resulting Hermitian vector bundle is Nakano positive by Proposition
3.4
. Thus, in view of the
L^{2}
Extension Theorem
1.1
, among
{\mathcal{O}}(k)\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}
the only possible candidate for the proof of Theorem
1
is the tangent bundle
T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}
.

We seek a hypersurface
S\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
such that some global section of
K_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}|_{S}\to S
does not extend to a global section of
K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\to{\mathbb{P}}(V)
. To study the extension problem, one looks at the
adjoint restriction sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\otimes T}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(K_{S}\otimes E|_{S})\to 0,

where
T\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(L_{S}))
is any holomorphic section such that
{\rm Ord}(T)=S
. Passing to the long exact sequence in cohomology gives
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(K_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}|_{S}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{1}}

.

We want to avoid the surjectivity of the map
R_{0}
. Since the sequence is exact,
R_{0}
is surjective if and only if
R_{1}
is injective.

Now, letting
d:=\deg(S)
, the vector bundle
L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}={\mathcal{O}}(d)\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}
in Nakano positive, so by Kodaira-Nakano Vanishing

H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes L_{S}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))=\{0\}.

Thus

\text{$R_{1}$ is injective if and only if }H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))=\{0\}.

But by Serre duality and the Dolbeault Isomorphism

H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))=H^{r-2}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}^{*}))^{*}\cong H^{r-2,1}({\mathbb{P}}(V)).

We see that for
r\geq 4
the restriction map
R_{0}
is surjective. On the other hand, if
r=3
then

\dim H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}(V),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}\otimes T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}))=\dim H^{1,1}({\mathbb{P}}_{2})=1,

so regardless of the curve
S
, the map
R_{0}
is
never
surjective.

Thus Theorem
1
is proved if we take
X={\mathbb{P}}(V)
for any Hermitian vector space
(V,{\mathfrak{h}})
of dimension
3
,
E=T_{{\mathbb{P}}(V)}
with metric induced by
{\mathfrak{h}}
as above, and
S\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
any smooth plane curve.
∎

is a fiber of the vector bundle and

is the tangent space to the manifold. A Hermitian form on

is said to be

In our situation the manifold is a product, so we suppose that

for

such that

, and a Hermitian inner product

with respect to

Proposition 4.1

For

For

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{H}}(\xi_{k}\otimes f_{i},\xi_{\ell}\otimes f_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{\ell}^{\ddagger}\left(f_{j}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{I}_{11}f_{i}\right)\tau_{k}+w_{\ell}^{\ddagger}\left(f_{j}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{I}_{12}f_{i}\right)\tau_{k}+\tau_{\ell}^{\ddagger}\left(f_{j}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{I}_{21}f_{i}\right)w_{k}+w_{\ell}^{\ddagger}\left(f_{j}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{I}_{22}f_{i}\right)w_{k}

such that

(15)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(g)\geq_{{\rm Nak}}(1+t\delta)\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1].

Then for every
f\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(E|_{Z}))
satisfying

\int_{Z}\frac{|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}},g}}<+\infty

there exists
F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(E))
such that

F|_{Z}=f\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}|F|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dV_{g}\leq\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}\int_{Z}\frac{|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}},g}}.

Remark
.

The equivalence of Theorems
5.2
and
1.1
is obtained as follows. Let
{\mathcal{E}}:=K_{X}^{*}\otimes L_{Z}^{*}\otimes E
, equipped with the metric
\mathbf{h}:=dV_{\omega}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}
. Then

E=K_{X}\otimes L_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}},\quad E|_{Z}=K_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}|_{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta(\mathbf{h})=\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(\omega)-\Theta({\mathfrak{g}});

the second equality is of course the adjunction formula which, at the level of sections, identifies a section
f
of
E|_{Z}=(K_{X}\otimes L_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}})|_{Z}
with the section
f/dT
of
K_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}|_{Z}
. Thus

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta(\mathbf{h})-t\delta\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(\omega)-(1+t\delta)\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\quad\text{and}\quad|f|^{2}_{\mathbf{h}}=\frac{|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dA_{\omega}}{|dT|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}},\omega}},

where the second equality is by the co-area formula. In particular, the metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
g
satisfy (
15
) if and only if the metric
\mathbf{h}
satisfies the curvature condition (
1
) of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose then that Theorem
1.1
holds. Given
f\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}(E|_{Z}))=H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}|_{Z}))
, Theorem
1.1
provides
F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(K_{X}\otimes L_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}))=H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
such that
F|_{Z}=f
and

\int_{X}|F|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dV_{\omega}=\int_{X}|F|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbf{h}}\leq\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}\int_{Z}|f|^{2}_{\mathbf{h}}=\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}\int_{Z}\frac{|f|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}dA_{\omega}}{|dT|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}},\omega}},

so Theorem
5.2
holds. The steps can be reversed to deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
5.2
.

\diamond

Under the equivalence just outlined, the curvature condition (
14
) is replaced by

(16)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(\omega)-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\geq_{{\rm Nak}}\delta\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\geq_{{\rm Nak}}0.

The proof then unfolds in two steps.

i.

In the first step we assume that the hypersurface
Z
is cut out by a bounded holomorphic function; we call this the
flat case
, since the normal bundle
L_{Z}\to X
is trivial, and we can take the metric
{\mathfrak{g}}
to be flat.

ii.

In the second step we reduce to the flat case using an idea of Grauert.

5.2.ii.
The Flat Case

We begin by proving the following special case of Theorem
5.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be a Stein manifold with Kähler metric
g
and let
T:X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a holomorphic function such that
dT
is not identically zero on any irreducible component of
Z:=T^{-1}(0)
. Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with smooth Hermitian metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
such that

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(\omega)\geq_{{\rm Nak}}0.

Then for any
f\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
such that

\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}<+\infty

there exists
F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
such that

F|_{Z}=f\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}\leq\pi\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}.

(Recall that integration on an analytic variety means integration over the regular locus.)

By using results from real analysis (namely, the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem and the Lebesgue limit theorems), it is standard that the proof reduces to the following situation.

i.

The manifold
X
is a relatively compact, pseudoconvex domain in a Stein manifold
\tilde{X}
, and there is a smooth complex hypersurface
\tilde{Z}\subset\tilde{X}
such that
\tilde{Z}\cap X=Z
.

ii.

There is a holomorphic vector bundle
\tilde{E}\to\tilde{X}
and a smooth Hermitian metric
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
for
\tilde{E}
such that
\tilde{E}|_{X}=E
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}|_{X}={\mathfrak{h}}
.

iii.

There is a holomorphic section
\tilde{f}\in H^{0}(\tilde{Z},{\mathcal{O}}(\tilde{E}))
such that
\tilde{f}|_{Z}=f
.

Perhaps the least standard part is the smoothness of
\tilde{Z}
; this situation can be achieved because the singular locus of a hypersurface
Z_{o}
in a Stein manifold is contained in some hypersurface
W
such that no component of
Z_{o}
lies entirely in
W
. If we remove
W
from our original Stein manifold then the resulting manifold is still Stein. And since at the end of the day we are integrating, the removal of a null set has no effect.

By Stein theory the section
\tilde{f}
extends to a holomorphic section
\tilde{F}\in H^{0}(\tilde{X},{\mathcal{O}}(\tilde{E}))
, albeit with no estimates. Nevertheless, its restriction
F:=\tilde{F}|_{X}
to
X
has finite
L^{2}
norm because of the smoothness of
{\mathfrak{h}}
and the boundedness of
X
. It follows that there exists an extension of minimal
L^{2}
norm, and our task is to estimate this minimal norm. From here on fix
f\in H^{0}(\overline{Z},{\mathcal{O}}(E))
such that

\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}<+\infty,

and denote by
F_{o}
the extension of
f
whose squared norm

(17)

\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F_{o},F_{o})dV_{g}

is minimal. We denote by
{\mathscr{H}}({\mathfrak{h}},g)
the Hilbert space of all
F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
whose squared norm (
17
) is finite.

One characterizes the norm of the minimal extension via duality as follows. Let

\mathfrak{I}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(Z)=\left\{G\in{\mathscr{H}}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\ ;\ G|_{Z}\equiv 0\right\}

and

{\rm Ann}(\mathfrak{I}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(Z)):=\left\{\xi\in{\mathscr{H}}({\mathfrak{h}},g)^{*}\ ;\ \left<\xi,G\right>=0\text{ for all }G\in\mathfrak{I}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(Z)\right\}.

Then

(18)

\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F_{o},F_{o})dV_{g}=\sup\left\{\frac{\left|\left<\xi,F\right>\right|^{2}}{||\xi||^{2}_{*}}\ ;\ \xi\in{\rm Ann}(\mathfrak{I}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(Z))\right\},

where
F\in{\mathscr{H}}({\mathfrak{h}},g)
is any extension of
f
. (The right hand side of (
18
) does not depend on
F
.)

The approach to proving Theorem
5.3
is to estimate the right hand side of (
18
). To obtain such an estimate, one begins by observing that, with

\xi_{\sigma}:{\mathscr{H}}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\ni F\mapsto\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}},

the set

\left\{\xi_{\sigma}\ ;\ \sigma\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(E))\cap{\mathscr{L}}^{2}_{Z}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\text{ compactly supported}\right\}

is dense in
{\rm Ann}(\mathfrak{I}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}(Z))
, where

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}_{Z}({\mathfrak{h}},g):=\left\{\sigma\in\Gamma(Z,E)\text{ measurable}\ ;\ \int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(\sigma,\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}<+\infty\right\}.

The subspace

{\mathscr{I}}({\mathfrak{h}},g):={\mathscr{L}}^{2}_{Z}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\cap H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{O}}(E))

of holomorphic sections in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}_{Z}({\mathfrak{h}},g)
is closed, and thus we have an orthogonal projection

P:{\mathscr{L}}^{2}_{Z}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\to{\mathscr{I}}({\mathfrak{h}},g).

In terms of this orthogonal projection, we find that for any extension
F
of
f
,

\displaystyle\left|\left<\xi_{\sigma},F\right>\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,{\sigma})\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,P{\sigma})\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\leq\left(\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right)\left(\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(P{\sigma},P{\sigma})\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right).

Thus to prove Theorem
5.3
it suffices to show that

(19)

\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(P{\sigma},P{\sigma})\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\leq\pi||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{*}

The strategy for proving (
19
) is essentially to degenerate
X
to
Z
, in a sense that we now explain.

Let

{\mathbb{L}}:=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\ ;\ \operatorname{Re}\tau<0\}.

denote the left half plane. For each
\tau\in{\mathbb{L}}
define

{\mathscr{X}}:=\left\{(x,\tau)\in X\times{\mathbb{L}}\ ;\ \log|T(x)|^{2}<\operatorname{Re}\tau\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{\tau}:=\{x\in X\ ;\ (x,\tau)\in{\mathscr{X}}\}.

Ideally one would like to work with the Hilbert field
\mathbf{H}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\to{\mathbb{L}}
whose fiber over
\tau\in{\mathbb{L}}
is

\mathbf{H}_{\tau}({\mathfrak{h}},g):=\left\{F\in H^{0}(X_{\tau},{\mathcal{O}}(E))\ ;\ e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X_{\tau}}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}<+\infty\right\}.

The degeneration is as
t:=\operatorname{Re}\tau\to-\infty
. The following lemma, which is an easy consequence of Fubini’s Theorem, will be of use later.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a smooth function on
\overline{X}
. Then

\limsup_{t\to-\infty}e^{-t}\int_{X_{t}}{\mathcal{F}}dV_{g}=\pi\int_{Z}{\mathcal{F}}\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}.

The proof is left to the reader.

We are interested in obtaining a certain convexity from this setup by making use of Theorem
2
. Unfortunately, Theorem
2
does not apply to
\mathbf{H}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\to{\mathbb{L}}
, since
{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathbb{L}}
is not a trivial family. To remedy this problem we consider instead the trivial family
p_{1}^{*}E\to X\times{\mathbb{L}}\to{\mathbb{L}}
with a non-trivial metric. More precisely, define the plurisubharmonic functions

\psi:X\times{\mathbb{L}}\ni(x,\tau)\mapsto\max(\log|T(x)|^{2}-\operatorname{Re}\tau,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{\tau}(x):=\psi(x,\tau).

The functions
\psi
is non-negative and depends only on
\operatorname{Re}\tau
, and the support of
\psi_{\tau}
is
X-X_{\operatorname{Re}\tau}
. We define the metrics

{\mathfrak{h}}^{(p)}_{\tau}:=e^{-p\psi_{\tau}}{\mathfrak{h}}

and the Hilbert spaces

{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}_{\tau}:=\left\{F\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))\ ;\ ||F||^{2}_{\tau}:=e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}^{(p)}_{\tau}(F,F)dV_{g}<+\infty\right\}.

Note that for each
F\in{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}_{\tau}
one has

\lim_{p\to\infty}e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}^{(p)}_{\tau}(F,F)dV_{g}=e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X_{\operatorname{Re}\tau}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}.

Thus for large
p
the Hilbert bundle
{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}\to{\mathbb{L}}
approximates
\mathbf{H}({\mathfrak{h}},g)\to{\mathbb{L}}
in a certain sense, and Theorem
2
can be applied to
{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}
.

To carry out the approximation of
\mathbf{H}({\mathfrak{h}},g)
by
{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}
we shall use the following lemma, which is a special case of
[
BL-2016
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\nu:(-\infty,0)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be an increasing function such that
\nu(t)\leq e^{t}
for all
t<0
. Then for
p>1
,

(20)

\liminf_{t\to-\infty}e^{-t}\int_{t}^{0}e^{-p(s-t)}d\nu(s)\leq\frac{2}{p-1}.

We now make our way toward the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\sigma\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(E))
have compact support. Then

\sup_{\tau\in{\mathbb{L}}}||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{\tau*}<+\infty.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\mathscr{H}}^{(p)}_{\tau}
. For each
x\in Z
we can choose a small holomorphic disk
D_{r}(x)\subset X
that is perpendicular (with respect to the metric
g
) to
Z
, lies in a neighborhood
U\subset X
of
x
such that
T
is a component of a coordinate system in
U
, and one has
T(D_{r}(x))=D_{r}(0)
. If
\tau
is far away from
-\infty
we can choose
r
independently of
\tau
, and if
\operatorname{Re}\tau
is so negative that
U
is no longer contained in
X_{\operatorname{Re}\tau}
we choose
r=e^{\tau/2}
. By the sub-mean value property we have

{\mathfrak{h}}(H(x),H(x))\leq Ce^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{D_{r}(x)}{\mathfrak{h}}(H,H)\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}dT\wedge d\bar{T}.

The factor
e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}
comes about because the area of this disk
D_{r}(x)
with respect to the measure
e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}dT\wedge d\bar{T}
is bounded above and away from
0
uniformly in
\tau
. Indeed, for
\tau
sufficiently far from
-\infty
this is clear, and for
\operatorname{Re}\tau<<0
the choice
r=e^{\operatorname{Re}\tau/2}
dictates that the area of
D_{r}(x)
with respect to the measure
e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}dT\wedge d\bar{T}
is
2\pi
. The constant
C
, which of course depends on the metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
g
, depends only locally uniformly on
x
, so in particular if
x
lies a relatively compact subset of
Z
containing the support of
\sigma
then we can choose
C
uniform. If we now integrate over a ball
\Delta(x)
centered at
x
and that is relatively compact in
Z
then by Fubini we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}(H(x),H(x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{\Delta(x)\times D_{r}(x)}{\mathfrak{h}}(H,H)\omega_{g}^{n-1}\wedge dT\wedge d\bar{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\prime}e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{\Delta(x)\times D_{r}(x)}{\mathfrak{h}}(H,H)|dT|^{2}_{g}dV_{g}.

Therefore for some constant
C_{\sigma}
depending on
{\mathfrak{h}}
,
g
and
\sigma
and for each
x\in{\rm Support}({\sigma})\subset Z

{\mathfrak{h}}(H(x),H(x))\leq C_{\sigma}e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X_{\operatorname{Re}\tau}}{\mathfrak{h}}(H,H)dV_{g}\leq C_{\sigma}e^{-\operatorname{Re}\tau}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}^{(p)}_{\tau}(H,H)dV_{g}=C_{\sigma}||H||^{2}_{\tau}.

Thus

||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{\tau*}=\sup_{||H||_{\tau}=1}\left|\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(H,{\sigma})\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right|^{2}\leq C_{\sigma},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\sigma\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(E))
have compact support. Then the function

\lambda_{\sigma}:(-\infty,0]\ni t\mapsto\log||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{t*}

is non-decreasing. In particular,

||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{o*}\geq||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{t^{*}}

for all
t<0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
the function
\tau\mapsto\log||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{\tau*}
is subharmonic on
{\mathbb{L}}
. On the other hand,
||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{\tau*}
depends only on
\operatorname{Re}\tau
, and thus
\lambda_{\sigma}
is convex on
(-\infty,0)
. If
\lambda_{\sigma}
decreases anywhere on
(-\infty,0)
then by convexity
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\lambda_{\sigma}=+\infty
, which contradicts Lemma
5.6
.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\sigma\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(E))
have compact support. Then for each
\delta>0
there exists
p
sufficiently large so that

\lim_{t\to-\infty}||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{t*}\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(P\sigma,P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}-\delta.

Theorem
5.8
is proved using Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
, as well as the following lemma.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\sigma\in H^{0}(Z,{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}(E))
have compact support. Then for any
\delta>0
there exists
G\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(E))
that is holomorphic up to the boundary, such that

\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(G-P\sigma,G-P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\leq\delta^{2}.

Proof.

By
L^{2}
approximation theorems (see, e.g.,
[
H-1990
, Theorem 5.6.2]
) we can find a section that is holomorphic on a neighborhood of
\overline{Z}
and approximate
P\sigma
uniformly on any compact subset of
Z
.
If we take the compact subset to be sufficiently large then we also get approximation in
L^{2}
. Finally, since the ambient manifold is Stein, the approximation on a neighborhood of
\overline{Z}
can be extended to a neighborhood of
\overline{X}
, and therefore the extension will have finite
L^{2}
norm on
X
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

If
P\sigma=0
then there is nothing to prove, so we assume
P\sigma\neq 0
. Also to slightly simplify the already cumbersome notation, we set

||P\sigma||:=\left(\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(P\sigma,P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right)^{1/2}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
\sigma
and
G
be as in Lemma
5.9
with
\delta=\varepsilon||P\sigma||
there. Then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(G-P\sigma,P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}+||P\sigma||^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle||P\sigma||^{2}-||P\sigma||\left(\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(G-P\sigma,G-P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right)^{1/2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)||P\sigma||^{2},

and thus

(21)

||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{t^{*}}\geq\frac{1}{||G||^{2}_{t}}\left|\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,P\sigma)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g}}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}||P\sigma||^{4}}{||G||^{2}_{t}}.

Now,

(22)

||G||^{2}_{t}=e^{-t}\int_{X_{t}}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)dV_{g}+e^{(p-1)t}\int_{X-X_{t}}e^{-p\log|T|^{2}}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)dV_{g}.

By Lemma
5.4
and our assumption that
P\sigma\neq 0
, we can choose
t<<0
so that

e^{-t}\int_{X_{t}}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)dV_{g}\leq(1+\varepsilon)||P\sigma||^{2}.

For the second integral on the right hand side of (

) we have

e^{(p-1)t}\int_{X-X_{t}}e^{-p\log|T|^{2}}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)dV_{g}\leq\left(\sup_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)\right)e^{(p-1)t}\int_{X-X_{t}}e^{-p\log|T|^{2}}dV_{g}.

But

e^{(p-1)t}\int_{X-X_{t}}e^{-p\log|T|^{2}}dV_{g}\leq Ce^{-t}\int_{t}^{0}e^{-p(s-t)}d\nu(s),

where
\nu(t)=\int_{X_{t}}dV_{g}
. Since

, Lemma
5.5
implies that

e^{(p-1)t}\int_{X-X_{t}}e^{-p\log|T|^{2}}{\mathfrak{h}}(G,G)dV_{g}\leq\frac{C_{o}}{p-1}

for a sequence of ‘
t
’s tending to

. Thus for sufficiently large

and sufficiently negative

||G||^{2}_{t}\leq(1+\varepsilon)||P\sigma||^{2}+\varepsilon.

From (
21
) we have

||\xi_{\sigma}||^{2}_{t^{*}}\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}||P\sigma||^{4}}{(1+\varepsilon)||P\sigma||^{2}+\varepsilon}

Choosing

∎

By combining Lemma

and Theorem

), and Theorem

Homogeneous expansion of holomorphic functions

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle, let
H\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle and denote by
{\mathfrak{p}}:H^{*}\to X
the dual bundle. Let
\sigma\in\Gamma_{{\mathcal{O}}}(H^{*},{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}H^{*})
denote the diagonal section

\sigma(v):=(v,v).

Then for any
s\in H^{0}(H^{*},{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}E))
there exist sections
a_{j}\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(H^{\otimes j}\otimes E))
,
j=0,1,...
, such that

s=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}a_{j})\otimes\sigma^{\otimes j}.

Proof.

Fix some
v\in H^{*}
and a frame
\eta_{1},...,\eta_{r}
for
E\to X
near
x={\mathfrak{p}}v
. Let
\xi\in H^{*}_{v}-\{0\}
and let
t\xi
be a typical point on the fiber
H_{v}
. Writing our section as
s=f^{\mu}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\eta_{\mu}
, we have the power series expansions

f^{\mu}(t\xi)=\sum_{j\geq 0}A^{\mu}_{j}(\xi)t^{j},\quad 1\leq\mu\leq r.

If one starts with another
\tilde{\xi}\in H^{*}_{v}-\{0\}
and
\tilde{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
t\xi=\tilde{t}\tilde{\xi}
then

\sum_{j\geq 0}A^{\mu}_{j}(\xi)t^{j}=f^{\mu}(t\xi)=f^{\mu}(\tilde{t}\tilde{\xi})=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}^{\mu}_{j}(\tilde{\xi})\tilde{t}^{j},

from which it follows that

a_{j}({\mathfrak{p}}\xi):=A^{\mu}_{j}(\xi)\eta_{\mu}\otimes\xi^{\otimes-j}

is well-defined, independent of
\xi
. Thus
a_{j}\in\Gamma_{{\mathcal{O}}}(X,E\otimes H^{\otimes j})
, and

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}a_{j}\otimes\sigma^{\otimes j}\right)(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{\mu}_{j}(v)(v)^{\otimes-j}\sigma(v)^{\otimes j}\otimes{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\eta_{\mu}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A^{\mu}_{j}(v){\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\eta_{\mu}=f^{\mu}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\eta_{\mu},

as required.
∎

The unit disk bundle associated to an extension problem

We return to the setting of Theorem
5.2
. We have a Stein manifold
X
and a possibly singular hypersurface
Z\subset X
. Unlike the flat case studied in the previous section, the hypersurface
Z
need not be cut out by a holomorphic function, much less a bounded one. However, there is a line bundle
L_{Z}
and a section
T\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}(L_{Z}))
whose zero locus, counting multiplicity, is precisely
Z
.

The more restrictive curvature hypothesis (
16
) implies that the metric
{\mathfrak{g}}
for
L_{Z}\to X
has non-negative curvature. In the total space of the dual bundle
L_{Z}^{*}
, with its dual metric
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, one can define the unit disk bundle

{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}}):=\{v\in L_{Z}^{*}\ ;\ |v|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}<1\}.

A well-known observation of Grauert is that the vertical boundary

\partial^{\rm vert}{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}}):=\{v\in L_{Z}^{*}\ ;\ |v|^{2}e^{\lambda}=1\}

of
{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is pseudoconvex if and only if
{\mathfrak{g}}
has non-negative curvature. The need for the pseudoconvexity of
\partial^{\rm vert}{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is precisely the reason for assuming the stronger curvature condition (
16
). Since the base
X
is Stein,
{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is also Stein.

To the section
T
we can associate a holomorphic function
{\mathscr{T}}\in{\mathcal{O}}(L_{Z}^{*})
defined by

{\mathscr{T}}(v)=\left<v,T({\mathfrak{p}}v)\right>,

where
{\mathfrak{p}}:L_{Z}^{*}\to X
is the line-bundle projection. On
{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
we have the bound

|{\mathscr{T}}(v)|^{2}=|\left<v,T({\mathfrak{p}}v)\right>|^{2}=|v|^{2}e^{\lambda}|T({\mathfrak{p}}v)|^{2}e^{-\lambda}<1.

Note that the zero locus of of
{\mathscr{T}}
is

{\mathscr{T}}^{-1}(0)={\mathcal{Z}}\cup{\mathbb{O}}_{L_{Z}^{*}},

where
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(Z)
and
{\mathbb{O}}_{L_{Z}^{*}}
is the zero section of
L_{Z}^{*}\to X
.

We use the volume form

d{\mathscr{V}}:=\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\omega^{n}}{n!}\wedge dd^{c}|v|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}

on
{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Its curvature, when viewed as a metric for
K_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}^{*}
, is

-\partial\bar{\partial}\log d{\mathscr{V}}={\mathfrak{p}}^{*}({\rm Ricci}(\omega)-\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})).

To make use of Theorem
5.3
we also need a Kähler metric. Let us take the metric
\tilde{g}
whose Kähler form is

\tilde{\omega}={\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\omega+c_{o}dd^{c}|v|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}};

the closed
(1,1)
-form
\tilde{\omega}
is positive for small
c_{o}>0
. The associated volume form is
dV_{\tilde{g}}=\det\tilde{g}
.

End of the proof of Theorem
5.2

We can pull back the vector bundle
E\to X
and the line bundle
L_{Z}^{*}\to X
to the total space
L_{Z}^{*}
of
L_{Z}^{*}\to X
via
{\mathfrak{p}}
to define the vector bundles

E_{m}:={\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(E\otimes L_{Z}^{*\otimes m})\to L_{Z}^{*},\quad m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
\delta>0
be such that (
16
) holds. Letting
m
be the unique positive integer such that

\delta_{o}:=\delta+1-m\in(0,1],

we equip the vector bundle
E_{m}\to{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})
with the singular Hermitian metric

(23)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}:=(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})^{-(1-\delta_{o})}\frac{d{\mathscr{V}}}{\det\tilde{g}}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}},

where
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{p}}^{*}{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\sigma\in H^{0}(L_{Z}^{*},{\mathcal{O}}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}L_{Z}^{*}))
is the diagonal section (see Proposition
5.10
).

We would like to apply Theorem
5.3
with the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
, but we must be slightly more careful; this metric is not smooth. However, it has a very simple regularization, namely

{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}^{(\varepsilon)}:=(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}+\varepsilon^{2})^{-(1-\delta_{o})}\frac{d{\mathscr{V}}}{\det\tilde{g}}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}}.

This regularization loses some positivity, but since we are on a relatively compact domain in a Stein manifold, we can regain this positivity using a bounded strictly plurisubharmonic function. The details, which are standard, are omitted for brevity, and we shall treat
{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}
like a smooth metric.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
5.2
the metric

{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}\otimes\det\tilde{g}

for
E_{m}\otimes K^{*}_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}
is Nakano-semipositive. By Theorem
5.3
we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}\in H^{0}({\mathscr{T}}^{-1}(0),{\mathcal{O}}(E_{m}))
satisfy

\int_{{\mathscr{T}}^{-1}(0)}{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}({\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{f}})\frac{dA_{\tilde{g}}}{|d{\mathscr{T}}|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}}<+\infty.

Then there exists
{\mathfrak{F}}\in H^{0}({\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}}),{\mathcal{O}}(E_{m}))
such that

{\mathfrak{F}}|_{{\mathscr{T}}^{-1}(0)}={\mathfrak{f}}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}({\mathfrak{F}},{\mathfrak{F}})dV_{\tilde{g}}\leq\pi\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}({\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{f}})\frac{dA_{\tilde{g}}}{|d{\mathscr{T}}|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}}.

If we apply Lemma
5.11
to the section

{\mathfrak{f}}:=\sigma^{\otimes m}\otimes(\pi^{*}f)

we find a section
{\mathfrak{F}}\in H^{0}({\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}}),{\mathcal{O}}(E_{m}))
such that
{\mathfrak{F}}|_{{\mathscr{T}}^{-1}(0)}=\sigma^{\otimes m}\otimes{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}{\mathfrak{f}}
and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}({\mathfrak{F}},{\mathfrak{F}})dV_{\tilde{g}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{h}}_{m}({\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{f}})\frac{dA_{\tilde{g}}}{|d{\mathscr{T}}|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}({\mathfrak{h}}(f,f))(|\sigma|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{m-\delta_{o}}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\left(\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}({\mathfrak{h}}(f,f))(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{\delta-1}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\left(\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}\right).

The section
{\mathfrak{F}}
vanishes to order
m+1
along
{\mathbb{O}}_{L_{Z}^{*}}
, and therefore

{\mathfrak{F}}=\sigma^{\otimes m}\otimes\Phi

for some section
\Phi\in H^{0}({\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}}),{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}E)
which evidently satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}{\mathfrak{h}})(\Phi,\Phi)(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{m+1-\delta_{o}}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}{\mathfrak{h}})(\Phi,\Phi)(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{\delta}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}({\mathfrak{h}}(f,f))(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{\delta-1}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\left(\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}\right).

Integration along the fibers of the disk bundle
{\mathcal{Z}}
shows that

\int_{{\mathcal{Z}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}({\mathfrak{h}}(f,f))(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{\delta-1}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\left(\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}\right)=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}.

Now, by Proposition
5.10
the section
\Phi
has the (convergent) series expansion

\Phi={\mathfrak{p}}^{*}F+\sum_{i\geq 1}\sigma^{\otimes i}{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}a_{i}.

In particular,
F
is an extension of
f
. Moreover, by orthogonality we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathscr{B}}({\mathfrak{g}})}({\mathfrak{p}}^{*}{\mathfrak{h}})(\Phi,\Phi)(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})^{\delta}dd^{c}(|\sigma|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}})\wedge{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta+1}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{\delta+i}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(a_{i},a_{i})dV_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta+1}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}.

Hence the extension
F
of
f
satisfies

\int_{X}{\mathfrak{h}}(F,F)dV_{g}\leq\frac{\pi(1+\delta)}{\delta}\int_{Z}{\mathfrak{h}}(f,f)\frac{dA_{g}}{|dT|^{2}_{g,{\mathfrak{g}}}}.

The proof of Theorem
5.2
under the stronger hypothesis (
16
) is therefore complete.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

A slight modification of Lemma
5.5
discovered by Albesiano in
[
A-2024
]
can be used here to yield Theorem
1.1
with the curvature hypotheses

(24)

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})+{\rm Ricci}(g)\geq_{\rm Nak}\delta\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})\geq_{\rm Nak}(1+\delta)\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}.

This result was also achieved by Nguyen and Wang
[
NW-2024
]
, by rather different methods that, though more complicated than Albesiano’s approach, seem to have farther-reaching consequences.

Though these results constitute an improvement on (
16
), they still do not reach (
15
). It remains an open question whether the degeneration technique of Berndtsson and Lempert can somehow be modified to prove the full Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, thus far the method has not been able to achieve other extension theorems. For example, it is not known whether a proof of Theorem
5.1
of Guan-Zhou is possible by the method of Berndtsson and Lempert.

\diamond

6.
Attempting to prove Theorem
5.2
by passing to
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})

A standard technique for treating higher rank vector bundles is to reduce them to line bundles by the ideas discussed in Paragraph
2.3.iii
. There remain difficult questions regarding how far this technique can reach, with the most famous problem being the Griffiths conjecture that ampleness in the sense of Hartshorne is equivalent to the existence of a metric of Griffiths-positive curvature.

In view of Proposition
2.10
it is natural to inquire whether a proof Theorem
1.1
could be undertaken by passing to the projectivization. It is well-known and easy to check that the metric
{\mathfrak{h}}
is Griffiths-positive if and only if the metric
e^{-\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}
for
\pi:{\mathcal{O}}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
is positive. However, while there is a correspondence between sections of
E
and those of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
, this correspondence sends sections of
K_{X}\otimes E
to sections of
\pi^{*}K_{X}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)
. To obtain sections of
K_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}\otimes L\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
for an appropriate line bundle
L\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
, one must find the relationship between
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}(\pi^{*}K_{X})
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}
. The key is the formula

K_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}={\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-r)\otimes\pi^{*}(K_{X}\otimes\det E)

(see
[
K-1987
, Proposition 3.6.20]
). We conclude that

\pi^{*}K_{X}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)=K_{{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\pi^{*}(\det E^{*}).

If we use only that
{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
is a complex manifold, and do not try to exploit its special geometric features, then to apply Theorem
1.1
in the rank-1 case we need to assume at least that the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\pi^{*}(\det E^{*})\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
is pseudo-effective (and of course, we would also need to overcome the normal bundle of
\pi^{-1}(Z)
, though we will not discuss this issue here).

Consider the line bundle

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\det E^{*}.

The positivity of this line bundle follows if we show that the “
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector bundle”

F:=(\det E^{*})^{1/(r+1)}\otimes E

is Griffiths-positive. (The converse is a case of the aforementioned Griffiths conjecture.) But even if we assume only that
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\otimes(\det E^{*})^{1/(r+1)}
is (semi)positive, a beautiful result of Berndtsson
[
B-2009
, Theorem 7.1]
implies that the vector bundle

F\otimes\det F=\left((\det E^{*})^{1/(r+1)}\otimes E\right)\otimes(\det E^{*})^{r/(r+1)}\otimes\det E=E

is Nakano-(semi)positive.

In order to give a proof of Theorem
1.1
by passing to
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)\to{\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
, we need the following converse: if the metric for
E
is Nakano-(semi)positive then the induced metric for
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\det E^{*}
is (semi)positive. Unfortunately, such a result is not true in general. The following example presents a Nakano-positive holomorphic Hermitian vector bundle
E
such that the induced metric on
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\det E^{*}
is not semi-positive.

Example 6.1
.

Let
L^{i}\to X
be holomorphic line bundles with smooth Hermitian metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
and consider the totally split holomorphic Hermitian vector bundle let

E=L^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{r}\quad\text{ with the induced metric }{\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{h}}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}.

A holomorphic section
s\in{\mathcal{O}}(E)_{x}
has a decomposition
\sigma=(\sigma^{1},...,\sigma^{r})
with
\sigma^{i}\in{\mathcal{O}}(L^{i})_{x}
.

We begin by observing that for such totally split
(E,{\mathfrak{h}})
the following are equivalent.

i.

(E,{\mathfrak{h}})
is Nakano positive.

ii.

Each
(L^{i},{\mathfrak{h}}^{i})
is positive.

iii.

(E,{\mathfrak{h}})
is Griffiths positive.

The implications (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) are straightforward. Now,

(25)

\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{N}{\mathfrak{h}}(\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}})_{\xi_{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{\nu}}\sigma_{\mu},\sigma_{\nu})=\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})_{\xi_{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{\nu}}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}(\sigma_{\mu}^{i},\sigma_{\nu}^{i}).

For each
i
the matrix
\left({\mathfrak{h}}^{i}(\sigma_{\mu}^{i},\sigma_{\nu}^{i})\right)_{\mu,\nu}
is Hermitian, so there is a unitary matrix
U(i)
such that

{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}(U(i)_{\mu}^{\alpha}\sigma_{\alpha}^{i},U(i)_{\nu}^{\beta}\sigma_{\beta}^{i})=|a^{i}_{\mu}|^{2}\delta_{\mu\bar{\nu}};

the right hand side must be non-negative because
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
is a metric. Therefore the right hand side of (
25
) is positive if each of the matrices
\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})_{\xi_{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{\nu}}\right)_{\mu,\nu}
is positive definite, so that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

We shall now show that for some choice of metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}^{1},...,{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
the metric
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}_{r}
induced on
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\det E^{*}
does not have positive curvature.

First, the metric induced on
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)
by
{\mathfrak{h}}
is given by

|v|^{2}e^{\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}={\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(v,v);

see Paragraph
2.3.i
. Thus the curvature
\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
of the dual metric
e^{-\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}
for
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)
is the negative of the curvature of the metric
e^{\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}
, so

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}=-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log|\sigma|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}}=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(s,s),

where
\sigma
is a local frame for
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(1)
, and
s=(s^{1},...,s^{r})
is the corresponding section of
E^{*}
associated to the dual frame
\sigma^{*}
by the formula
\hat{s}=\sigma^{*}
; see Proposition
2.10
, noting that the latter proposition is applied to the vector bundle
E^{*}
, not
E
. (The formula for
\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{{\mathfrak{h}}}
is independent of the choice of
s
representing a section of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(-1)
, because another choice
\tilde{s}
must be of the form
\tilde{s}=\lambda s
for some nowhere-vanishing holomorphic function on the domain of
s
.)

We compute that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}(s,s)=\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i*})s^{i},s^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})\right)\left({\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(\nabla s^{i},\nabla s^{i})\right)-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(\nabla s^{i},s^{i})\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},\nabla s^{i})\right)}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(-\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i*})s^{i},s^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i}){\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}.

The inequality is sharp, as can be seen using sections with zero covariant derivative at a point. Next

\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\det{\mathfrak{h}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i}).

Therefore the curvature
\Theta(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
of the metric
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
induced on
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(r+1)\otimes\det E^{*}
by the split metric
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}^{1}\oplus...\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
for
E
is positive if and only if for every collections of
r
local holomorphic sections
s^{i}
of
L^{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
at least one of which is non-zero, one has

(26)

(r+1)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i}){\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{h}}^{i*}(s^{i},s^{i})}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\text{\rm-}1}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})\geq 0.

The inequality (
26
) holds for all sections
(s^{1},...,s^{r})
at least one of which is non-zero if and only if for every
\xi\in T^{1,0}_{X}
and for all
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r}\in[0,1]
such that
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}=1
one has

(27)

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}\geq\frac{1}{r+1}\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{1})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}+\cdots+\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{r})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}\right)

i.e., every convex combination of the Hermitian
(1,1)
-forms
\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{1}),...,\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{r})
is bounded below by the
(1,1)
-form
\frac{1}{r+1}\left(\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{1})+\cdots+\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{r})\right)
.

However, there exist positively curved metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}^{1},...,{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
(so in particular,
E
is Nakano positive) for which this is not the case. Indeed, (
27
) implies that

(28)

\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{1})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}+\cdots+\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{r})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{i}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})_{\xi\bar{\xi}}\geq\frac{1}{r}\max_{i}\Theta({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})_{\xi\bar{\xi}},

and we can clearly choose metrics
{\mathfrak{h}}^{1},...,{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
with positive curvature such that the second of these conditions fails.

\diamond

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically conical Riemannian manifold having
dimension
n\geq 2
, opening angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)\setminus\{\arcsin\frac{1}{2k+1}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and positive asymptotic rate.
Under the assumption that the exponential map is proper at each point,
we give a solution to the one dimensional asymptotic Plateau problem on
M
.
Precisely, for any pair of antipodal points in the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}M=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we prove the existence of a geodesic line with asymptotic prescribed boundaries
and the Morse index
\leq n-1
.
This extends the remarkable result of Carlotto-De Lellis 2019 in dimension 2 and opening angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)
under the nonnegative Gaussian curvature assumption.

Our proof relies on a min-max approach similar to Carlotto-De Lellis. However, to run the min-max argument, there are several new essential difficulties
caused by dimension and non-Gaussian curvature assumptions.
We are able to overcome these difficulties, but the proof is rather involved. In particular, to run the min-max argument,
we construct a nontrivial homotopy class via the geometry of geodesic segments of standard cones serving as sweep-outs around the reference point
o
, and then bound the min-max values in a quantitative manner.
By deforming the gradient flow of certain truncation of the energy functional,
we are able to obtain a sequence of min-max geodesic segments

\{\Gamma_{j}\}
.
To obtain the geodesic line
\Gamma
with the morse index
\leq n-1
from

\{\Gamma_{j}\}
,
we establish a uniform distance estimate of
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
from
o

and also a local uniform length upper bound via some blowing down arguments. Finally, we establish a non-twisting property for

\{\Gamma_{j}\}
by comparing the behaviors of
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
with those of the standard cone and then conclude that
\Gamma
admits the prescribed antipodal ideal boundaries.

Introduction

The classical Plateau problem in a Riemannian manifold
M
,
as originated from J. Plateau
[
50
]
when he was studying the shape of soap films,
aims to seek for minimal surfaces
S
with a prescribed boundary
\Sigma\subset M
.
In a non-compact
Riemannian manifold
M
with ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}M
(also known as boundary at infinity),
a counterpart problem (called as the asymptotic Plateau problem) is to show the existence of unbounded minimal surfaces
S
with a prescribed ideal boundary
\Sigma\subset\partial_{\infty}M
.

In Euclidean spaces, the asymptotic Plateau problem is closely related to the problem posed by Bernstein
[
12
]
: Determine all the complete minimal hypersurfaces
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which can be seen as a graph of a function defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
For major progress to the Bernstein problem, we refer the readers to Bernstein
[
12
,
13
]
, Moser
[
46
]
, Fleming
[
31
]
, De Giorgi
[
29
]
, Almgren
[
3
]
, Simons
[
55
]
, Bombieri-De Giorgi-Giusti
[
15
]
and Chern-Osserman
[
24
]
.
In hyperbolic spaces,
the study of the asymptotic Plateau problem was initiated by Anderson
[
4
]
in 1982; see
[
27
]
for a nice survey.
We also refer to
[
5
,
9
,
37
]
and references therein for the asymptotic Plateau problem on manifolds with negative sectional/scalar curvature as generalisations of the hyperbolic space.

We are interested in noncompact manifolds possessing
certain product structure, including the asymptotically conical Riemannian manifolds
(see Section
3
for the definition).
The study of asymptotic Plateau problems in such manifolds also received several attentions; see
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
53
,
2
,
54
,
18
,
25
,
11
,
44
,
19
]
and references therein.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge,
all these results focus on finding unbounded
minimal hypersurfaces, that is, minimal surfaces with codimension
1
.
When the ambient manifold has dimension
n\geq 3
, it remains widely open to find unbound minimal surfaces with codimension
\geq 2
, equivalently, with dimension
1\leq k\leq n-2
.

Towards this, the first step is to find unbounded minimal surfaces with dimension 1,
that is, geodesic lines. Throughout the paper, by a geodesic line
{\gamma}
(resp. ray, segment) on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, we mean under its arc-length parametrisation with respect to
g
,
{\gamma}
is a local isometry from
(-\infty,+\infty)
(resp. an interval
[a,+\infty)
,
[a,b]\subset{\mathbb{R}}
) to
(M,g)
. In this sense, a geodesic line (ray, segment) is always a critical point of the length functional, and is locally length-minimising,
but is not necessarily length-minimising on all subintervals

[s,t]\subset{\mathbb{R}}
(resp.
[s,t]\subset[a,+\infty)
,
[s,t]\subset[a,b]
).
And in the following, we will sometimes parametrise a geodesic
{\gamma}
proportional to its arc-length.

In the special case that the ambient manifold has dimension
2
,
geodesic lines coincide with
minimal hypersurfaces and also minimal surface with codimension 1. Early contributions to find geodesic lines are due to Bangert
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
in 1980s (see also
[
16
, Prop 6.1]
for a partially sharpened result by Bonk-Lang) as mentioned above. The
following remarkable contribution was made by Carlotto-De Lellis
[
19
]
recently.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically conical surface with nonnegative Gaussian curvature. Then, for any pair of antipodal points
a,-a\in\partial_{\infty}M=\mathbb{S}^{1}
, there exists an embedded geodesic line
\Gamma
with asymptotic prescribed boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma=\{a,-a\}
and Morse index
\leq 1
.

Partially motivated by this and also
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
,
in this paper, we establish the following existence of geodesic lines in
higher dimensional asymptotically conical Riemannian manifolds.
See Section
3
for the related concepts appeared in the statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically conical Riemannian manifold with opening angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)\setminus\{\arcsin\frac{1}{2k+1}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and positive asymptotic rate. Moreover, assume that, at each point, the exponential map is proper. Then, for any pair of antipodal points
a,-a\in\partial_{\infty}M=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, there exists a geodesic line
\Gamma
with asymptotic prescribed boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma=\{a,-a\}
and Morse index
\leq n-1
.

One of the motivations for establishing Theorem
1.2

comes from seeking for geodesic lines in
asymptotically flat manifolds, corresponding to asymptotically
conical manifolds with opening angle
\alpha=\pi/2
,
which
has a close relation with the study of unbounded orbits of systems of physics and celestial mechanics,
such as the Schwarzschild and Kerr metric which are the most fundamental classes of solutions to the Einstein field equation characterising black holes in general relativity.
Meanwhile, in celestial mechanics, the Jacobi-Maupertuis metric of a positive energy level from the Newtonian
N
-body system is asymptotically Euclidean away from the collisions. It is well-known that particles moving in these spaces are precisely along the geodesic lines of the corresponding asymptotically Euclidean/flat metrics (see for example
[
48
,
20
]
). Thus characterising geodesic lines in these manifolds will provide understanding to the behavior of the orbit of particle as time goes to infinite past and infinite future. In particular, showing the existence of a geodesic line with prescribed ideal boundary on these manifolds will imply that there exists orbits of particles in these physical/mechanical models which escape to infinity as time goes to both infinite past and infinite future.
We emphasis that the existence of such escaping geodesic lines are rarely investigated in the literature. Especially, this is a well-known open problem for the Jacobi-Maupertuis metric (
[
41
, Section 5]
) while, on the contrary, escaping geodesic rays (with the prescribed ideal boundary as a point) of the Jacobi-Maupertuis metric are recently shown to be widely existing in
[
41
,
38
,
52
]
using completely different methods.
We hope that the approach to showing Theorem
1.2
will open a way
for these studies.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we will borrow some ideas from the proof of Theorem
1.1
in
[
19
]
. In particular,
min-max theory was employed by Carlotto-De Lellis
[
19
]
to establish a one-dimensional mountain-pass theorem, and is also used here to construct a
similar but complicated
(n-1)
-dimensional mountain-pass theorem. As already noticed by Carlotto-De Lellis in
[
19
]
, it may be effortless to use the minimisation approach to find geodesic lines.
For example, if an
n
-dimension manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature does not split as a Riemannian product, then, thanks to the splitting theorem by Cheeger-Gromoll
[
23
]
it can not contain a length-minimising geodesic line.
It should be remarked that the idea of using min-max theory to study the existence of closed geodesics has a long history. Indeed, it is dated back to the work Birkhoff
[
14
]
who showed that a two-sphere with any Riemannian metric admits a closed geodesic which
answers a question posed by Poincaré
[
51
]
.
Later on, a series of works are devoted to showing the existence of closed geodesics; see
[
39
,
40
,
32
,
34
,
60
,
35
]
. For the existence result of geodesics rays, we recommend to consult
[
57
,
56
]
for a comprehensive study.
The min-max theory also showed its powerful strength for finding closed minimal hypersurfaces in a closed manifold under various settings. Remarkably and far from exhausted, by employing and developing Almgren-Pitts min-max method
[
49
]
, Marques-Neves
[
42
]
resolved the Willmore conjecture, Agol-Marques-Neves
[
1
]
proved the Freedman conjecture on the energy of links, Zhou
[
61
]
solved the multiplicity one conjecture (see also
[
43
]
) and Song
[
58
]
confirmed the Yau conjecture on minimal hypersurfaces. Besides, the min-max theory, in recent years, also turned out to be successful to seeking for closed minimal hypersurfaces in non-compact manifolds. We refer to the works
[
45
,
26
,
22
,
28
,
59
]
and references therein.

However, to run the min-max argument and then to show the existence of the resulting geodesic segments, there are several new and essential difficulties compared with the
proof of Theorem
1.1
by Carlotto-De Lellis
[
19
]
. In particular, the proof of Theorem
1.1
heavily relies on the
nonnegative Gaussian curvature and dimension 2 assumptions for
M
, which, indeed, allow the authors to employ Gauss-Bonnet formula and so to control the behavior of geodesics on a surface repeatedly.
However, there is no such formula for curves in higher dimensions and we do not assume nonnegative Gaussian curvature in Theorem
1.2
, either.
Moreover, with the help of a topological result in
[
21
]
, to prove Theorem
1.1
,
one only needs to work with embedded geodesic segments in dimension
2
as did in
[
19
]
(see also Remark
1.4
below).
However, it was already pointed out in
[
21
]
that such topological result does not hold in dimension
3
and only hold in dimension
\geq 4
in a generic sense.
Thus to prove Theorem
1.2
, we have to treat immersed geodesic segments, which causes extra obstacle.
To overcome these difficulties so to get Theorem
1.2
, new ideas are definitely needed.

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section 1.1 and clarify our new ideas and
novelty briefly in Remark 1.4 and 1.5 therein.
The details to the proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given in Section 4 and Section 5. Indeed, Section 4 is devoted to a min-max construction of the geodesic segments and Section 5 gives the desired geodesic line via some approximating and contradicting arguments.
We refer to Section 2 for several basic facts about the geometry of the standard circular cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, and Section 3 for the definition and properties of asymptotically conical manifolds and also a series of useful lemmas. All of these are needed in Section 4 and Section 5.
For the readers’ convenience, we collect in Section 1.2 several notions and notation appear repeatedly.

Finally, we list several remarks about Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.3
.

(i) The range
\{\arcsin\frac{1}{2k+1}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is missing from the assumption
of Theorem
1.2

for the opening angle. This is caused by our approach, and will be explained in Remark
1.5
in Section 1.1.
We also explain why this range is allowed in dimension 2 as in Theorem
1.1
. See Remark
1.5
.

(ii) There is a wide class of manifolds enjoying
the properness assumption of exponential maps made in Theorem
1.2
.
By
[
33
, Theorem 4]
, each non-compact manifold of positive sectional curvature fulfils such properness assumption. Moreover, this class contains certain manifolds which can have negative or non-positive curvatures.
In Example
3.8
, we construct a manifold with certain vanishing sectional curvatures.

The properness assumption of exponential maps is required by our proof.
It is not clear whether Theorem
1.2
holds for asymptotically conical manifolds which has nonnegative sectional curvature but
exponential maps are not necessarily proper.
In the proof of Theorem
1.1
for the
2
-dimensional case, the nonnegative Gaussian curvature assumption can be applied in accordance with the Gauss-Bonnet formula. See also Remark
1.4
(iii) below.

(iii) The Morse index bound
\leq n-1
in Theorem
1.2
comes directly from the fact that our homotopy class in our mountain-pass theorem (see Proposition
4.7
below) consists of
(n-1)
-dimensional families of curves. Recall that the Morse index bound
\leq 1
in Theorem
1.1
is directly from the fact that the homotopy class in
[
19
, Proposition 14]
consists of one-dimensional families of curves.

(iv) We remark that with the aid of a delicate topological result in
[
21
]
by Chambers-Liokumovich, Carlotto-De Lellis were able to substitute the homotopies by isotopies in
[
19
, Proposition 14]
, which results in that the final geodesic line obtained in Theorem
1.1
is embedded. There, they use the fact that if a sequence of curves in a surface with no transverse self-intersections has a limit in
C^{1}
-norm, then the limit curve also has no transverse self-intersections.
However, this fact can be easily seen to be false for curves in dimension
\geq 3
. Thus we are not able to guarantee the immersed geodesic line in Theorem
1.2
is embedded.

(v) The asymptotically conical manifolds considered in Theorem
1.2
are always assumed to be diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
; see Definition 3.1. In particular, they are contractible and have no boundary.
These two properties cause essential differences, compared with the case that
M
has nontrivial topology or
M
has boundary.

For instance, if
(M,g)
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
g
is asymptotically flat to the standard metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, then we know
\partial_{\infty}M
consists of a disjoint union of two
(n-1)
-spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. For any
a\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
b\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, it is not hard to show the existence of a geodesic line
\Gamma_{a,b}
with prescribed ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma_{a,b}=\{a,b\}
, which can be realized as a limit of the sequence of length-minimising geodesic segments
\Gamma_{a,b}^{i}
joining
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
with
\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=a
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}b_{i}=b
. See the left of Figure
1
for an illustration.

Furthermore, when
M
has boundary, for instance, if
M
is the shaded region in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
in the right of Figure
1
with the length metric induce by Euclidean metric, then
\partial_{\infty}M
is
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus\{a,-a\}
where
a,-a
are a pair of antipodal points of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Thus
\partial_{\infty}M
is a disjoint union of two open intervals
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{-}\cup\mathbb{S}^{1}_{+}
. In this case, it is also straightforward to show the existence of a geodesic line
\Gamma_{b,c}
with prescribed ideal boundary
b\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{-}
and
c\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{+}
, again by considering an exhaustion sequence of length-minimising geodesic segments.

1.1
Ideas of the proof of of Theorem
1.2

Let
M=(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
be an asymptotically conical manifold as in Theorem
1.2
with reference point
o
.
Given a pair of ideal antipodal boundaries
\{\theta,\theta^{\star}\}\subset\partial_{\infty}M=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
we aim to construct a geodesic line
\Gamma
with
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma=\{\theta,\theta^{\star}\}
so to prove Theorem
1.2
. As it is always the case,
\Gamma
will be constructed as the limit
in certain sense of a suitably chosen sequence of geodesic segments
\Gamma_{j}
joining the
pair
p_{j}=(\rho_{j},\theta)
and
q_{j}=(\rho_{j},\theta^{\star})
of antipodal points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\lim_{j\to\infty}\rho_{j}=+\infty
. Here,
(\rho,\theta)
is the standard polar coordinate of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
To guarantee the desired convergence, it is necessary and also sufficient to require that

\{\Gamma_{j}\}
(up to some subsequence) are close to
o
uniformly, that is,

\Gamma_{j}\cap B(o,C)\neq\emptyset\mbox{ for all ${j\in\mathbb{N}}$ }

(1.1)

for some constant
C>0
, and also have bounded local length uniformly, that is,

\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\{\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{j}\cap B(o,r))\}<\infty\ \mbox{ for any $r>C$}.

(1.2)

To choose a stuaible sequence
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
),
we must turn to geodesic segments which are not length-minimising for the reason that
any length-minimising geodesic segment joining
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
will escape every geodesic ball as
j\to\infty

that is,
that is, for any
C>0
, (
1.1
) fails for all sufficiently large
j
.

To choose non-length-minimising geodesics correctly, we are motivated by a geometric observation in the standard (circular) cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}})
with the apex
o
. Indeed, denote by
G_{p_{j}}
(resp.
G_{q_{j}}
) the generatrix segment joining
p_{j}
(resp.
q_{j}
) and
o
in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, which is a geodesic segment therein.
The concatenation
G_{j}
of
G_{p_{j}}
and
G_{q_{j}}
has length
2\rho_{j}
and contains
o
.
Although
G_{j}
is not a geodesic segment in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
(not length-minimising around apex
o
),
we still wish to find some geodesic segment
\Gamma_{j}

in
M
joining
p_{j},q_{j}
and playing the role of
G_{j}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, in particular, satisfying (
1.1
) for all
j
,
and having length around
2\rho_{j}
.
See Figure
2
for an illustration.

Figure 2:
Geodesic segments joining
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
.

Surprisingly, we do find the desired geodesic line-segments
\{\Gamma_{j}\}

with the help of the min-max theory built up in the following Step 1 and Step 2,
and then, from them we conclude a
geodesic line
\Gamma
with morse index
\leq n-1
in Step 3 and show
the ideal bounardies

\partial\Gamma=\{\theta,\theta^{\star}\}
in Step 4.
Here we only consider the case
n\geq 3
since the case
n=2
is easier (See Remark
1.4
for the reason why we need to treat the case
n=2
and cases
n\geq 3
separately).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\theta
and
\theta^{\star}
are contained in the first coordinate
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{1}\}
and
\theta=e_{1}
.
Write the class

{\mathcal{C}}_{j}:=\{{\gamma}\in W^{1,2}([0,1],M):{\gamma}(0)=p_{j},\ {\gamma}(1)=q_{j}\},

and the energy

{\mathcal{E}}(\sigma):=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\sigma}(t)|_{g}^{2}\,dt,\quad\forall\sigma\in W^{1,2}([0,1],M).

Step 1. Construct a homotopy class
\{\mathcal{M}_{j}\}
and bound the corresponding min-max value
\{\Lambda_{j}\}
.

To construct a suitable homotopy class, firstly we give a full characterisation for length-minimising geodesic segments joining
two antipodal points of the standard (circular) cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}:=(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}})
with opening angle
\alpha
;
see Lemma
2.11
in Section
2.2
.
Indeed, there exists an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of length-minimising geodesic segments:

{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}:=\{L\in C^{\infty}([0,1],\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}):L(0)=p_{j},\ L(1)=q_{j},\mbox{ $L$ is a length-minimising geodesic segment}\}.

Note that the length of each
L
under
g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}
is
2\rho\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)
and the metric space
({\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}},\|\cdot\|_{C^{0}([0,1])})
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
.
We parametrise
{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}
as

{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}=\{L_{\beta}\in C^{\infty}([0,1],\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}):\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\}.

Next, viewing
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
as the unit sphere of
({\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{1}\})^{\perp}={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
,
we identify each
L_{\beta}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}
as a point therein, in particular, a point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Consider the reflection
I
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with respect to
({\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{n}\})^{\perp}
, i.e.

I(x)=I((x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n})):=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1},-x_{n})

(1.3)

where
I(x)=x
for all
x\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:x_{n}=0\}
. In particular,
I(p_{j})=p_{j}
and
I(q_{j})=q_{j}
.
We have the important correspondence,

I(L_{\beta})=L_{I(\beta)},\quad\forall\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}.

See Section 4 for this fact.

Using the above correspondence
I
, we define the desired class of homotopies as

{\mathcal{M}}_{j}:=\{H\in C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1],{\mathcal{C}}_{j}):H(\beta,0)=L_{\beta},\ H(\beta,1)=(L_{I(\beta)}),\ \forall\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\}

(1.4)

and the corresponding min-max value

\Lambda_{j}:=\inf_{H\in{\mathcal{M}}}\max_{(\beta,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(H(\beta,s)).

Recalling that the expected length for the desired geodesic segment
\Gamma_{j}
is approximately
2\rho_{j}
and noting that
{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})=\ell_{g}({\gamma})^{2}
for any
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, we know
\Lambda_{j}
should satisfy

\Lambda_{j}\approx 4\rho_{j}^{2}.

(1.5)

To obtain the estimate (
1.5
), each homotopy
H\in{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
should be a “sweep-out” around
o
, which, in particular, implies that
o\in H(\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1])
. This is shown in Section 4.1 and Section 4.2.

Step 2. Find a sequence
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
of min-max geodesic segments with respect to
g
by constructing a deformation map.

For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, from the definition of
\Lambda_{j}
, clearly we could find a min-max sequence in
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}({\mathcal{C}})
with limit
\Gamma_{j}
joining
p_{j},q_{j}
and the energy
{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma_{j})=\Lambda_{j}
.
To show that
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
are geodesic segments with respect to
g
,
that is, critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
,
firstly we construct a suitable deformation map in Section 4.3 (the idea is sketched as below),
and then exploit an argument by contradiction in Proposition
4.7
in Section 4.4.

Generally, a deformation map
\Phi:{\mathcal{C}}_{j}\times[0,\delta]\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous map for some small
\delta>0
which satisfies

(i)

\Phi({\gamma},\cdot)
is strictly decreasing in
[0,\delta]
if
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
is not a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
;

(ii)

\Phi({\gamma},\cdot)
is constant if
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
is a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

We choose the deformation map as the gradient flow of a carefully chosen truncated energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}:=\eta\circ{\mathcal{E}}
,
where
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
is some cut-off function
(See Section 4.3).
Roughly speaking,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}={\mathcal{E}}
for curves in
{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
of length
\ell_{g}({\gamma})
around
2\rho_{j}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}=0
for curves in
{\mathcal{C}}_{j}

of length
\ell_{g}({\gamma})
around

To see the reason why we can not use the energy functional

The corresponding gradient flow
\Phi({\gamma},\tau)
with respect to

is the solution of the ODE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d}{d\tau}\Phi({\gamma},\tau)=-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi({\gamma},\tau)),\\
\Phi({\gamma},0)={\gamma}\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

which is well-defined by the

-Hilbert structure on
{\mathcal{C}}_{j}
; see Section 4.3.
An important property is that for

, then

\Phi(H,\tau):\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{C}}_{j}

{\mathcal{M}}_{j}

Step 3. Show that
\{\Gamma_{j}\}
satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

To this end, in the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, we build a full characterisation for geodesic segments joining two antipodal points via winding number with respect to

Moreover, we clarify the limit behaviour of geodesic lines in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, and also the
limit behaviour of some family of geodesic lines and segments. For details see Section

and Section

.

The verification of (
1.2
) is then achieved by utilising the property of the properness of the exponential map and
some blow-down arguments, and the limit behaviour of some family of geodesic segments in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
.
See Lemma
3.5
, where the properness assumption of exponential maps is used.

We prove
(
1.1
) by contradiction and by using some blow-down arguments repeatedly; see Lemma
5.1
in Section 5. The point is that, assuming the failure of (
1.1
), we will show that the family

\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
after blowing down will converge to a geodesic segments in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
and the behaviors of the blowing down sequence
would violate the characterisation of geodesic segment in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
above. The range

is required to get the desired contradiction.

Step 4. Construct a geodesic line
\Gamma
from
\{\Gamma_{j}\}

and show
\partial\Gamma=\{\theta,\theta^{\star}\}
.

Given a sequence
\{r_{i}\}
with
r_{i}\to\infty
,
for all large

(resp.

\Gamma_{j}

We show that,
as

(up to some subsequence), the restriction

will converge to some geodesic segment

\Gamma_{i}^{\ast}
with the property that
\Gamma_{i}^{\ast}\subset\Gamma_{i+1}^{\ast}
for

.
Then
the limit of certain reparametrization of

gives a geodesic line
\Gamma
.

It remains to show

having antipodal ideal boundaries

The core is to establish the non-twisting property for the min-max geodesic segments

\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j}
, in other words,
outside any given ball,
\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j}
asymptotes to antipodal directions

The proof is highly nontrivial.
To this end, we compare the behaviors of geodesic segments of the standard cone with
\Gamma_{j}
based on several blow down procedures; see Section 5.2 for details.

This finishes the outline of the proof of Theorem

Remark 1.4
.

(i) We emphasis that the homotopy class
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
in (

) is a non-trivial higher dimensional analogue of the one in
[
19
]
. Indeed, for the above two antipodal points
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
, Carlotto-De Lellis constructed the homotopy
{\mathcal{N}}_{j}
as

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}:=\{H\in C^{0}([0,1],{\mathcal{C}}_{j}):H(0)=\Gamma_{j,1}^{g},\ H(1)=\Gamma_{j,2}^{g}\}

(1.7)

where
\Gamma_{j,i}^{g}
is a geodesic segments joining
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
,
i=1,2
with respect to the asymptotically conical metric
g
, and
\Gamma_{j,1}^{g}\cup\Gamma_{j,2}^{g}
is topologically an

for each

. The existence of two geodesic segments joining
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
with respect to

is shown in
[

, Lemma 18]
. Furthermore, let

be the bounded component of

, then

for all

. This property guarantees that for any homotopy
H
in
{\mathcal{N}}_{j}
,
o\in H(s)
for some
s\in[0,1]
, since in dimension
2
,
D_{j}\setminus\{o\}
is not simply-connected.

However, in an asymptotically conical manifold
(M,g)
with dimension
n\geq 3
, it may not be possible to always find an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of geodesic segments
\{\Gamma_{j,\beta}^{g}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}
joining
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
with respect to

to construct the desired homotopy.
Our new idea here is to use the length-minimising geodesic segments
L_{\beta}
of the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
in the construction of our homotopy class
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
;
as explained in Step 1, the family
{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}
of length-minimising geodesic segments joining

and

forms an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of curves. Moreover,

\bigcup_{L_{\beta}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}}L_{\beta}\subset M\approx{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mbox{ is topologically an $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$},

(1.8)

and letting

(1.9)

Thus, the
(n-2)
-dimensional family
{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{j},q_{j}}
of length-minimising geodesic segments of the standard cone

is a nice substitute for

in dimension

.

Nonetheless, unlike the case in dimension

, in dimensions

, the above two properties (

) and (

) are not enough to guarantee the following crucial property: for a general homotopy

with boundary value

there always exists

This is because in dimensions

is still simply-connected. Indeed, in Section 4, we provide an example of such homotopy

without passing though

(See Example
4.2
in Section 4.1).

By the above discussion, the reflection map
I
is indispensable in the definition of the homotopy class

, which guarantees that

for all

and thus enables us to conclude the desired estimate (
1.5
).

(ii) The above different choices of homotopy class also lead to different choices of deformation maps. To run the min-max argument successfully, an important property for deformation map
\Phi:{\mathcal{C}}\times[0,\delta]\to[0,\infty)
is that
\Phi({\gamma},\cdot)
should be constant when
{\gamma}
is the boundary value of a homotopy

in the homotopy class
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
(resp.
{\mathcal{N}}_{j}
), that is,

\Phi({\gamma},\tau)\equiv\Phi({\gamma},0),\ \tau\in[0,\delta]\mbox{ if ${\gamma}\in\{H(\beta,0),H(\beta,1)\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}$ (resp. ${\gamma}\in\{H(0),H(1)\}$) .}

(1.10)

In
[
19
]
, Carlotto-De Lellis could directly use the gradient flow
\Phi
of the energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}
with respect to
g

In our case, the boundary values of

(iii) It is also our novelty to prove (

(iv) In Step 4, it is also our novelty to figure out a new argument to build up the desired uniform non-twisting phenomenon. Recall

To this end, they compared the behaviors of length-minimising geodesic rays with

Remark 1.5

In Step 3, we mention that our approach could not deal with the case that the opening angle

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\Gamma:(-\infty,\infty)\to\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\alpha}\setminus\{o\}
be a geodesic line. Then,

\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}:=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\angle(\Gamma(-t),\Gamma(t))=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\angle(\dot{\Gamma}(-t),\dot{\Gamma}(t))=\frac{|2\pi K_{\alpha}\sin\alpha-\pi|}{\sin\alpha}

where

is the unique integer such that

Moreover,

Directly from Lemma

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
\{\Gamma^{N}:[0,1]\to\overline{B(o,N)}\}_{N\in{\mathbb{N}},N\geq 2}

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}

\Gamma^{N}(0)\cup\Gamma^{N}(1)\subset\partial B(o,N)

and

\min\{r>0:\Gamma^{N}\cap\partial B(o,r)\neq\emptyset\}=1,\quad\forall N\geq 2.

(2.15)

Then,

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\angle(\Gamma^{N}(0),\Gamma^{N}(1))=\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}\mbox{ and }\lim_{N\to+\infty}\angle(\dot{\Gamma}^{N}(0),\dot{\Gamma}^{N}(1))=\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Reparametrize
\Gamma^{N}
to the geodesic segment

\Gamma^{N}_{\star}:[t_{N}^{-},t^{+}_{N}]
under arc-length of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}^{n}
with
\Gamma^{N}_{\star}(0)\in\partial B(o,1)

t^{+}_{N}
,
-t^{-}_{N}\geq N-1
.
By abuse of notation we write
\Gamma^{N}_{\star}

\Gamma^{N}
.

), we know that

By the rotational symmetry of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}^{n}

\Gamma^{N}(0)

\Gamma^{2}(0)

Asymptotically conical manifolds

Let
(M,g,o)=({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g,o)

non-compact manifold with reference point

Assume that

asymptotically conical manifold
with asymptotical angle

is a
C^{2}

-tensor aways from

and has asymptotic decay rate
\mu

A(\rho,\theta)=a_{\rho\rho}(\rho,\theta)\,d\rho\otimes\,d\rho+\rho^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}a_{\theta_{i}\theta_{j}}(\rho,\theta)\,d\theta_{i}\otimes\,d\theta_{j}+2\sum_{i}^{n-1}\rho a_{\rho\theta_{i}}(\rho,\theta)\,d\rho\otimes\,d\theta_{i}

(3.1)

under the standard polar coordinate
(\rho,\theta)

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(\rho,\theta)=(\rho,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n-1})\in(0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi)\times[-\pi/2,\pi/2)^{n-2}.

We say

|\partial^{I}a(\rho,\theta)|=O(\rho^{-\mu-I_{\rho}})\ \mbox{as $\rho\to\infty$.}

g(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}(x)\,dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j}=g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}(x)+A(x)

A(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\,dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j},\quad\forall x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}.

(3.3)

The asymptotical decay rate

means that for multi-index

|\partial^{I}_{x}a_{ij}(x)|=O(|x|^{-\mu-|I|})\ \mbox{as $x\to\infty$}.

A key convergence lemma under properness of exponential map

Denote by
\exp^{M}
the exponential map of
M
.
We call
\exp^{M}
is proper at a point
x\in M
if
the preimage

(\exp_{x}^{M})^{-1}(K)

of any compact set
K\subset M
is compact in
\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{T}_{x}M
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The exponential map
\exp^{M}
is proper at each point if and only if for each
x\in M
and
r>0
, there exists
T_{x,r}>0
such that
\exp_{x}^{M}(t\xi)\notin\overline{B(0,r)}

for any
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{x}M
and
t>T_{x,r}/|\xi|
.

Proof.

Assume that the exponential map
\exp^{M}
is proper at each point
x\in M
.
Thus the preimage
(\exp_{x}^{M})^{-1}(\overline{B(0,r)})
is compact, and hence contained in some ball in
\mathcal{T}_{x}M
with radius
T_{x,r}
. Therefore, for any
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{x}M

and for
t>\mathcal{T}_{x,r}
, we know
t\xi\notin(\exp_{x}^{M})^{-1}(\overline{B(0,r)})
, which is equivalent to
\exp_{x}(t\xi)\notin\overline{B(0,r)}
.

Conversely, for each
x\in M
and
r>0
, assume that there exists
T_{x,r}>0
such that
\exp_{x}^{M}(t\xi)\notin\overline{B_{g}(0,r)}

for any
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{x}M
and
t>T_{x,r}/|\xi|
.
Then

(\exp_{x}^{M})^{-1}(\overline{B(o,r)})\subset\{t\xi:\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{x}M,0\leq t\leq T_{x,r}\}.

Thus
(\exp_{x}^{M})^{-1}(\overline{B(o,r)})
is bounded and hence compact.
∎

We need the following result on the uniform bound of the length of a sequence of geodesic rays.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
the exponential map is proper at each point.
Let
\{{\gamma}_{j}:[0,\infty)\to M\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of geodesic rays of
(M,g)
parameterised by arc-length of
g
such that

({\gamma}_{j}(0),\dot{\gamma}_{j}(0))\in\partial B(o,r)\times\mathcal{S}_{{\gamma}_{j}(0)}M

and

\lim_{j\to\infty}({\gamma}_{j}(0),\dot{\gamma}_{j}(0))=(p,\xi)

(3.12)

for some
r>0
,
p\in\partial B(o,r)
and
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{p}M
.
Define

T_{j}:=\max\{t>0:|{\gamma}_{j}(t)|=r\}.

Then

\sup_{j}T_{j}=\sup_{j}\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,T_{j}]})<\infty.

(3.13)

Proof.

Since
{\gamma}_{j}(t)=\exp^{M}_{\gamma_{j}(0)}(t\dot{\gamma}_{j}(0))
, by Lemma
3.4
, we know

T_{j}<\infty
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
{\gamma}_{j}
is arc-length parametrised, we have
\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,t]})=t
for all
t>0
. Thus, it suffices to show that
\sup_{j}T_{j}<\infty.

We consider the following two cases separately.

Case 1. There exists
D_{\ast}>0
such that
{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,T_{j}]}\subset B(o,D_{\ast})
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We argue by contradiction.
Assume there exists a subsequence
{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\limsup_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\ni j\to\infty}T_{j}=\infty.

Consider the geodesic ray
{\gamma}(t)=\exp^{M}_{x}(t\xi)
.
For
r>0
, let

\tau=\max\{t>0:|{\gamma}(t)|=D_{\ast}+1\}.

(3.14)

Since
\gamma_{j}\to\gamma
locally unfiormly,
for sufficiently large
j
we have

|\gamma_{j}(t)-\gamma(t)|\leq\frac{1}{10}\quad\forall t<\tau.

For sufficiently large
j
, one has
T_{j}>D_{\ast}+1
and hence

{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,\tau]}\subset{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,T_{j}]}\subset B(o,D_{\ast}).

From this we deduce that

\gamma|_{[0,\tau_{D_{\ast}+1}]}\subset B(o,D_{\ast}+\frac{1}{10}),

in particular,

\gamma(\tau_{D_{\ast}+1})\in B_{g}(o,D_{\ast}+\frac{1}{10}).

This contradicts with
\gamma(\tau_{D_{\ast}+1})\in\partial B_{g}(o,D_{\ast}+1)
.

Case 2. There exists a subsequence
\mathbb{N}_{2}

and an increasing sequence
\{D_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}
with
\lim_{\mathbb{N}_{2}\ni j\to\infty}D_{j}=+\infty
such that

\max\{|{\gamma}_{j}(t)|:t\in[0,T_{j}]\}=D_{j},\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{2}.

Up to some subsequence and we may assume that
\mathbb{N}_{2}=\mathbb{N}
.
For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

s_{j}=\max\{0<s<T_{j}:|{\gamma}_{j}(s)|_{g}=D_{j}\}.

We further consider two subcases.

Subcase 2-1. There exists
N>0
such that
T_{j}\leq ND_{j}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Define
\bar{\gamma}_{j}:[0,1]\to M
:

\bar{\gamma}_{j}(t):=\mathcal{D}_{D_{j}^{-1}}\circ{\gamma}_{j}(T_{j}t)

where we recall
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}:x\mapsto\lambda x
is the scaling map. Thus we know

\bar{\gamma}_{j}\subset\overline{B(o,1)},\quad\bar{\gamma}_{j}(T_{j}s_{j})\in\partial B(o,1)

(3.15)

and

|\dot{\bar{\gamma}}_{j}(t)|_{g^{(D_{j})}}=\frac{T_{j}}{D_{j}}|\dot{\gamma}_{j}(t)|_{g}\equiv\frac{T_{j}}{D_{j}},\quad\forall t\in[0,1],\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{2}.

In particular,

\ell_{g^{(D_{j})}}(\bar{\gamma}_{j})=\frac{T_{j}}{D_{j}}\leq N.

(3.16)

Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{j}=\infty
, we know
g^{(D_{j})}
converges to
g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\bar{\gamma}_{j}(0)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\gamma}_{j}(0)}{D_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\bar{\gamma}_{j}(1)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\gamma}_{j}(t_{j})}{D_{j}}=o.

Fom Arzela-Ascolli Theorem it follows that, up to subsequences,

\{\bar{\gamma}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges in
C^{2}([0,1],M)
to a geodesic segment
{\gamma}_{\infty}:[0,T]\to\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
of the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
with
{\gamma}_{\infty}(0)={\gamma}_{\infty}(1)=o
. Hence
{\gamma}_{\infty}
is a geodesic loop passing through the apex
o
. Moreover, by (
3.15
), we know

{\gamma}_{\infty}\subset\overline{B(o,1)}
with some point lies in
\partial B(o,1)
.
This is impossible, since all the geodesics in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
passing through
o
are the generatrices and there is no geodesic loop of the standard cone passing through the apex
o
.

Subcase 2-2.
\limsup_{j\to\infty}T_{j}/D_{j}=\infty
.

Up to subsequence, we may assume that
\lim_{j\to\infty}T_{j}/D_{j}=\infty
.
Define
\hat{\gamma}_{j}:[0,T_{j}/D_{j}]\to M
:

\hat{\gamma}_{j}(t):=\mathcal{D}_{D_{j}^{-1}}\circ{\gamma}_{j}(D_{j}t).

We have

\hat{\gamma}_{j}\subset\overline{B(o,1)},\quad\hat{\gamma}_{j}(T_{j}s_{j})\in\partial B(o,1)

(3.17)

and

|\dot{\hat{\gamma}}_{j}(t)|_{g^{(D_{j})}}=|\dot{\gamma}_{j}(t)|_{g}\equiv 1,\quad\forall t\in[0,1],\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular, for each
N>0
,

\ell_{g^{(D_{j})}}(\hat{\gamma}_{j})|_{[0,N]}=N.

Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{j}=\infty
, we know
g^{(D_{j})}
converges to
g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\hat{\gamma}_{j}(0)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\gamma}_{j}(0)}{D_{j}}=o.

For
N>0
sufficiently large, again by Lemma
3.3
, we know that, up to subsequence,

\{\hat{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,N]}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges in
C^{2}([0,N],M)
to a geodesic segment
{\gamma}_{\infty}^{N}:[0,N]\to\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
of the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
with
{\gamma}_{\infty}(0)=o
.
By (
3.17
),
{\gamma}_{\infty}^{N}\subset\overline{B(o,1)}.

Since
\ell_{g^{(D_{j})}}(\hat{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,N]})=N
, we know

\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}({\gamma}_{\infty}^{N})=\lim_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{3}}\ell_{g^{(D_{j})}}(\hat{\gamma}_{j}|_{[0,N]})=N.

This implies for every
N
sufficiently large, there exists a geodesic segment
{\gamma}_{\infty}^{N}
of the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
contained in the ball
\overline{B(o,1)}
of length
N
starting from

. This is impossible, either, since all the geodesics passing through

are the generatrices and any generatrix

satisfies

.

As a consequence, both Subcase 2-1 and Subcase 2-2 could not happen, which implies Case 2 can not happen. Thus Case 1 holds, where we already showed

.
The proof is complete.

. Thus there exists
s_{0}
such that

\Gamma_{q,w}(s_{0})\in\partial B(o,2)
.
This implies

d_{g}(\Gamma_{q,w}(s_{0}),\Gamma_{q,w}(0))=d_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,std\,}}(\Gamma_{q,w}(t_{0}),q)=d_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,std\,}}(\partial B(o,2),\partial B(o,1))=1.

On the other hand, letting
(q_{j},w_{j}):=({\gamma}(t_{j}),\dot{\gamma}(t_{j}))
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, consider the geodesic rays
\Gamma_{q_{j},w_{j}}
. By the continuity dependence of the initial data of the geodesic flow of
(M,g)
, we know there exists
s_{1}>s_{0}
such that for all
j>J(s_{1})
and all
s\in[0,s_{1}]
,

d_{g}(\Gamma_{q,w}(s),\Gamma_{q_{j},w_{j}}(s))<\frac{1}{2}.

As a result
\Gamma_{q,w}(s_{0})\notin B(o,1)
for all
j>J(s_{1})
. However,
\Gamma_{q,w}\subset{\gamma}\subset B(o,1)
. This implies that (
3.18
) can not hold. Thus all geodesic ray of
M
will ultimately escape
B(o,1)
and then becomes a geodesic ray of standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
which will escape to infinity. By Lemma
3.4
, we know the exponential map of
M
at every point is proper.

Remark 3.9
.

In general, on a manifold
M
diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with non-negative sectional curvature, it is not clear whether the exponential map is proper at each point.
The behavior of geodesic rays on manifold

with non-negative sectional curvature may be complicated. See
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
.

4
Existence of min-max geodesic segments

Let
(M,g,o)=({\mathbb{R}}^{n},g,o)
be an asymptotically conical manifold with asymptotical angle
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)

and asymptotic decay rate
\mu>0
as in Section 3.
Given any pair of antipodal points

,
we plan to find some geodesic segments joining them and close to

uniformly in

by using min-max method.

Firstly we need to construct a useful homotopy class based on the geometry of the standard cone
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
given in Section 2.
Then we give min-max value and bound it from above and below.
Moreover, we introduce the truncated energy and consider its gradient flow.
By using such flow as the deformation map, we
build up a mountain-pass theorem for geodesic segment.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p=(\rho,0,\cdots,0)
and
q=(\rho,\pi,0,\cdots,0)
in the polar coordinate. Thus
p,q\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,span\,}}\{e_{1}\}
and

where we recall

and

are the generatrices.
Recall that

is the collection of all length-minimising geodesic segments
in
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{\alpha}
joining

p,q
paramatrized with constant speed

and

is the union of images
{\gamma}([0,1])
of all

. Moreover, set

{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}:=\{{\gamma}\in W^{1,2}([0,1],M):{\gamma}(0)=p,\ {\gamma}(1)=q,\ t\in[0,1]\}.

4.1
Construction of homotopy class

In dimension

, recall from Remark

(ii) that there are exactly two elements
\Gamma^{\pm}_{0}
in

. We define the class of homotopy

{\mathcal{M}}_{pq}:=\{H\in C^{0}([0,1],{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}):H(0)=\Gamma^{\pm}_{0},\ H(1)=\Gamma^{\mp}_{0}\}.

In dimension

, recall that
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}_{G}
is the unit sphere of

in

and

Consider the reflection

Note that

and

(4.2)

The following basic

Lemma 4.1
.

(i) We have

for all

when

and

.

(ii) The class

For any

, there exists

.

Proof.

(i) Let

be the

-dimensional unit sphere of span

Since

and
q
such that

L_{I(\xi)}
intersecting the generatrix
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}I(\beta)
,

(4.3)

Recall from Lemma

Observe that for any

(ii) We argue by contradiction. Assume there an

4.2
Min-max value and lower/upper bound

Define the min-max value

\Lambda_{pq}:=\inf_{H\in{\mathcal{M}}_{pq}}\max_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(H(\xi,s))

where

{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}):=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g}^{2}\,dt

is the standard energy functional of a curve
{\gamma}
.
The Euler-Lagrange equation of
\mathcal{E}
is geodesic equation,
whose solutions in
{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
consists of geodesics with respect to
g
parametrized by a constant multiple of the arc-length, are called as
the critical points Crit
({\mathcal{E}})
for
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be sufficiently small such that

\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)<\frac{[1-5C_{\star}\epsilon]^{2}}{[1+5C_{\star}\epsilon]^{2}},

(4.4)

where
C_{\star}
denotes the constant from (
3.8

By Lemma
3.2
, there exists

such that

(1-\epsilon)|x-o|\leq d_{g}(o,x)\leq(1+\epsilon)|x-o|,\quad\forall x\in M_{\leq\tilde{\kappa}}

(4.5)

where we recall the region
M_{\leq\tilde{\kappa}}
from (
3.6
).
Moreover, thanks to Remark (
2.7
) (iii), we may choose
\kappa<\tilde{\kappa}
so small such that for any pair of antipodal points

,

\mathcal{L}^{T}_{p,q}\subset M_{\leq\tilde{\kappa}},

and hence, by (

),

(1-\epsilon)|{\gamma}(t)-o|\leq d_{g}(o,{\gamma}(t))\leq(1+\epsilon)|{\gamma}(t)-o|,\quad\forall{\gamma}\in\mathcal{L}_{pq}\ t\in[0,1]

(4.6)

By Remark

, this implies that for all

, we have

\ell_{g}({\gamma})\leq(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}({\gamma})=(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)2\rho\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha).

(4.7)

Below we always let
\epsilon
and
\kappa
be as above and assume

.

Lemma 4.3
.

One has

(1-\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\leq\Lambda_{pq}\leq(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}.

Proof.

We first show
(1-\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\leq\Lambda_{pq}
.
Thanks to Lemma
4.1
(ii),
for any homotopy
H\in{\mathcal{M}}
, there exists

such that
H(\xi_{o},s_{o},t_{o})=o
.

Let
{\gamma}_{po}:[0,1]\to M
be the length-minimising geodesic segments joining
p
and
o
and
{\gamma}_{oq}:[0,1]\to M
be the length-minimising geodesic segments joining
o
and
q
with respect to
g
.
Using (
4.5
), we estimate

\displaystyle\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{po})

\displaystyle=d_{g}(p,o)\geq(1-\epsilon)|p-o|\geq\rho(1-\epsilon)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{oq})

\displaystyle\geq\rho(1-\epsilon).

Since

, we deduce that

\ell_{g}(H(\xi_{o},s_{o},\cdot)|_{[0,t_{o}]})\geq\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{po})\geq\rho(1-\epsilon)\mbox{ and }\ell_{g}(H(\xi_{o},s_{o},\cdot)|_{[t_{o},1]})\geq\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{oq})\geq\rho(1-\epsilon),

which implies

\ell_{g}(H(\xi_{o},s_{o},\cdot))\geq\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{poq})\geq 2\rho(1-\epsilon)

where
{\gamma}_{poq}={\gamma}_{po}\ast{\gamma}_{oq}
is the concatenation of
{\gamma}_{po}
and
{\gamma}_{oq}
. Using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we deduce that

\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(H(\xi_{o},s_{o},\cdot))}{\rho^{2}}\geq\frac{\ell_{g}(H(\xi_{o},s_{o},\cdot))^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\geq\frac{\ell_{g}({\gamma}_{poq})^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\geq 4\frac{[\rho(1-\epsilon)]^{2}}{\rho^{2}}>4(1-\epsilon)^{2}.

By the arbitrariness of

, we arrive at the desired result.

To see

such that

\max_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(H(\xi,s))\leq(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}.

(4.8)

Recalling that for each
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

is a length-minimising geodesic of

and

for all

;

•

joining

and

.

•

for all

We note that for all

4.3
Truncated energy functional and its gradient flow

Below we always let
\epsilon
and
\kappa
be as in Section 4.2 and assume
that the pair of antipodal points
p,q\in M_{\leq\kappa}
.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty),[0,1])
be a cut-off function such that

\eta(x)=0,\quad\forall x\in[0,(1+5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)],

\eta(x)=x,\quad\forall x\in[(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2},\infty),

and for all
x\in((1+5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha),(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}]
,

0<\eta^{\prime}(x)\leq\frac{3}{4\rho^{2}[(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}-(1+5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)]}.

Define the truncated energy functional

{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}):=\eta\circ{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}),\quad\forall{\gamma}\in W^{1,2}([0,1],M).

Here recall that
{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
has a natural Hilbert manifold structure (see for example
[
30
, Section 2.1]
).
For each
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
, define the tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
at
{\gamma}
as

\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}:=\{X\in W^{1,2}([0,1],\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}M):X(t)\in\mathcal{T}_{{\gamma}(t)}M\mbox{ for all $t\in[0,1]$ and }X(0)=X(1)=0\}.

Thus
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
consists of
W^{1,2}
sections of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}M
supported on
{\gamma}
and vanishing at the endpoints.
Equip with
{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
the Riemannian metric

g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}:\mathcal{T}_{{\gamma}}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}\times\mathcal{T}_{{\gamma}}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}\to{\mathbb{R}},\ g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}(X_{1},X_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}[g(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t))+g(\dot{X}_{1}(t),\dot{X}_{2}(t))]\,dt

where
\dot{X}(t)=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}(t)}X(t)
for any
X\in\mathcal{T}_{{\gamma}}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
. Recalling that
g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}
is a
C^{2}
-Riemannian metric, we know

({\mathcal{C}}_{pq},g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}})
is a Hilbert manifold with a complete
C^{2}
-Riemannian metric.

Since
({{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}},g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}})
is a
C^{2}
-Hilbert manifold, we know
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
are both
C^{2}
-function defined on
{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}
.
Define
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
is defined in the way such that

g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}(\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}),V):=(d{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}),V)=\eta^{\prime}({\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}))\int_{0}^{1}g(\dot{\gamma},V)\,dt\quad\forall V\in\mathcal{T}_{{\gamma}}{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}.

Given any initial value
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
, we consider the gradient flow of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d}{d\tau}{\gamma}_{\tau}=-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau}),\\
{\gamma}_{0}={\gamma}.\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
is of class
C^{2}
, the flow
{\gamma}_{\tau}
is well-defined for all
{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}
and
\tau\geq 0
.
For more details, we refer to
[
30
, Theorem 1.11.1]
.

Note that

\frac{d}{d\tau}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau})=(d{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau}),\frac{d}{d\tau}({\gamma}_{\tau}))=-\eta^{\prime}({\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}))\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau})\|_{g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}}^{2}.

Thus for
\tau^{\prime\prime}\geq\tau^{\prime}\geq 0
, we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau^{\prime\prime}})-{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau^{\prime}})=\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}\frac{d}{d\tau}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau})\,d\tau=-\eta^{\prime}({\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}))\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau})\|_{g^{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}}^{2}\,d\tau.

(4.10)

Moreover,

•

if
{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})<(1+5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)
, then in a small neighbourhood
U
of
{\gamma}
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}|_{U}=\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}|_{U}\equiv 0
, which implies that

{\gamma}_{\tau}\equiv{\gamma}_{0}={\gamma},\quad\forall\tau>0;

•

if
{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})>(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}
and
{\gamma}
is not a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
, by (
4.10
), whenever
{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{\tau})>(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}
, we have

\eta^{\prime}({\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau}))=1
in (
4.10
) and consequently,

\mbox{the maps}\ \tau\mapsto{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{\tau})={\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{\tau})\ \mbox{is strictly decreasing at $\tau$.}

Below we write

{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}:=\{{\gamma}\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}:{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\}.

Consider the restriction
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}}

of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
in the set
{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}
.
We have the following property.

Corollary 4.4
.

The restriction

{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}}
satisfies
the Palais-Smale
condition.

Here recall the definition of the Palais-Smale
condition; see for example,
[
30
]
.

Definition 4.5
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Hilbert manifold with a complete
C^{2}
-Riemannian metric. Then a
C^{2}
function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to[0,\infty)
is said to satisfy
the
Palais-Smale condition
if whenever
\{\xi_{i}\}
is a sequence in
M
such that

(i)

\{f(\xi_{i})\}
is bounded,

(ii)

\{\nabla f(\xi_{i})\}
is not bounded away from zero,

then
\{\xi_{i}\}
possesses a subsequence which converges to a critical point of
f
.

Corollary
4.4
follows from
the following theorem; see for example
[
30
, Theorem 2.25]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies Palais-Smale condition.

4.4
A mountain pass theorem for geodesic segments

Below we always let
\epsilon
and
\kappa
be as in Section 4.2 and assume
the pari of antipodal points
p,q\in M_{\leq\kappa}
.
We have the following
mountain pass theorem for geodesic segments.
For any set
A
, denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}(A)
the set of sequences
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset A
.
A sequence
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}({\mathcal{M}}_{pq})
is called to be
minimizing if

\max_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\xi,s))\to\Lambda_{pq}\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Given a mminimzing sequence
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
we further call a sequence
\{H_{j}(\xi_{j},s_{j})\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as min-max if

{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\xi_{j},s_{j}))\to\Lambda_{pq}\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Proposition 4.7
.

There exists a geodesic segments
\Gamma\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}
such that

{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma)=\Lambda_{pq}\ \mbox{and}\ \nabla{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma)=0.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction. Assume the set

\{{\gamma}\in{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}:{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})=\Lambda_{pq},\ \nabla{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})=0\}=\emptyset.

(4.11)

We will conculde some contradiction by the following 3 steps.

Step 1.
Fix any min-max sequence

\left\{{\gamma}_{j}=H_{j}(\xi_{j},s_{j})\right\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}({{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}),

consequently,

{\gamma}_{j}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}\mbox{ with }\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{j})=\Lambda_{pq}.

We moreover require that

{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\xi_{j},s_{j}))=\max_{(\xi,s)\in S^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\xi,s)),\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.12)

Since
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the Palais-Smale
condition, we know

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{j})\|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}}=\delta>0.

(4.13)

Otherwise, the Palais-Smale
condition would imply that, up to subsequence, there exists a
W^{1,2}
-limit
{\gamma}_{\ast}
of
\{{\gamma}_{j}\}
which is a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}
. In particular,

{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{\ast})=\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{j})=\Lambda_{pq}\mbox{ and }\nabla{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{\ast})=0,

which contradicts to the counter assumption (
4.11
).

Since
\Lambda_{pq}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}
, we can assume
{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{j})\geq(1-2\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As a result,

\{{\gamma}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-2\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{pq}^{\geq(1-5C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}}

By the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
, we know

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}({\gamma}_{j})\|_{X}=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma}_{j})\|_{X}=\delta>0.

(4.14)

Step 2.

For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]
, consider the gradient flow
\Phi_{j,\xi,s}
of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
starting from
H_{j}(\xi,s)
, i.e.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\Phi_{j,\xi,s}(\tau)=-\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\xi,s}(\tau)),\\
\displaystyle\Phi_{j,\xi,s}(0)=H_{j}(\xi,s).\end{array}\right.

(4.15)

Fix
\widetilde{\tau}>0
and define

\widetilde{H}_{j}(\xi,s):=\Phi_{j,\xi,s}(\widetilde{\tau}),\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ and }(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1].

Choose

(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]
such that

{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))=\max_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\xi,s)).

We claim that

\{\widetilde{H}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}({\mathcal{M}}_{pq})
is also a minimising sequence,

(4.16)

and moreover,

\mbox{both $\{\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})\}$ and $\{H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})\}$ are min-max sequences}.

(4.17)

To prove this claim, thanks to
H_{j}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{pq}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one has

H_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2},0)\cup H_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2},1)\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{p,q}.

Recalling from (
4.7
) that

\ell_{g}({\gamma})\leq(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)2\rho\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)\quad\mbox{$\forall{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,q}$},

we obtain

{\mathcal{E}}({\gamma})\leq(1+C_{\star}\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sin\alpha)\quad\forall{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,q}.

(4.18)

This implies that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
and
\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
vanishes at any small neighbourhood of each element in
H_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2},0)\cup H_{j}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2},1)
.
Thus for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
,

\widetilde{H}_{j}(\xi,0)=\Phi_{j,\xi,0}(\widetilde{\tau})=H_{j}(\xi,0)\mbox{ and }\widetilde{H}_{j}(\xi,1)=\Phi_{j,\xi,1}(\widetilde{\tau})=H_{j}(\xi,1).

By the continuity of
\Phi_{j,\xi,s}
with respect to
(\xi,s)
, we deduce that
\{\widetilde{H}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Seq\,}}({\mathcal{M}}_{pq})
. The definition of
\Lambda_{pq}
then gives

\sup_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{s}))\geq\Lambda_{pq},\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}},

hence

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sup_{(\xi,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[0,1]}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{s}))\geq\Lambda_{pq}.

(4.19)

On the other hand,
since the energy is decreasing along the flow
\tau\mapsto\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau)
, it holds that

{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))={\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\widetilde{\tau}))\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(0))\leq{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})).

Thanks to (
4.12
) we have

{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\leq{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\xi_{j},s_{j}))

and hence

\limsup_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\leq\Lambda_{pq}.

(4.20)

Combining (
4.20
) and (
4.19
), we conclude

\limsup_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))=\limsup_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))=\Lambda_{pq}.

This gives the claims (
4.16
) and (
4.17
)
as desired.

Step 3.
Recall from Lemma
4.3
that
\Lambda_{pq}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}
.
Without loss of generality, we could assume

{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\geq(1-2\epsilon)^{2}4\rho^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $j\in{\mathbb{N}}$}.

Thus

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\|_{\mathcal{C}_{pq}}=\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\|_{\mathcal{C}_{pq}}\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Similar to the argument to get (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we know

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))\|_{\mathcal{C}_{pq}}=\delta^{\prime}

for some
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
Choose
\widetilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
and let

\tau_{j}:=\inf\{\tau\in(0,\widetilde{\tau}):\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}})(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{C}_{pq}}<\widetilde{\delta}\}

(4.21)

where we make the convention that
\tau_{j}=\widetilde{\tau}
if the set in the RHS of (
4.21
) is empty.

We consider the following two cases of
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\{\tau_{j}\}
separately to conclude contradiction.

Case 1. There exists
\widetilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
such that
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\{\tau_{j}\}=\tau_{\infty}>0
.
This means that there exists a subsequence
\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}
,

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau))\|_{X}\geq\widetilde{\delta},\quad\forall\tau\in[0,\tau_{j}].

As a result, for each
j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\widetilde{\tau}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(0))-\tau_{j}\widetilde{\delta}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))-\tau_{j}\widetilde{\delta}={\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}})-\tau_{j}\widetilde{\delta}.

Hence

\Lambda_{pq}=\liminf_{j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})\leq\liminf_{j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}})-\tau_{j}\widetilde{\delta}\leq\Lambda_{pq}-\tau_{\infty}\widetilde{\delta},

which is impossible. Thus this case cannot happen.

Case 2. For all
\widetilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
,
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\tau_{j}=0
.

In this case, we can find a subsequence
\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that for
j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}
,

\|\nabla{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau_{j}))\|_{X}<\frac{1}{j}.

(4.22)

Recall that

\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})=\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\widetilde{\tau}),\quad H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})=\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(0),\quad\forall j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}

and
notice that

{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})),\quad{\mathcal{E}}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}))={\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})),\quad\forall j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}.

Since both
\{\widetilde{H}_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\{H_{j}(\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j})\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are min-max sequences, by the monotonicity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}
along the flow
\Phi
and
0\leq\tau_{j}\leq\widetilde{\tau}
, we deduce that

\Lambda_{pq}=\lim_{\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}\ni j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\widetilde{\tau}))\leq\lim_{\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}\ni j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau_{j}))\leq\lim_{\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}\ni j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ast}(\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(0))=\Lambda_{pq}.

As a consequence,
\{\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau_{j})\}_{j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}}
is also a min-max sequence. Using (
4.22
) and the Palais-Smale condition, we know that

\{\Phi_{j,\widetilde{\xi}_{j},\widetilde{s}_{j}}(\tau_{j})\}_{j\in\widetilde{\mathbb{N}}}
has a limit
\widetilde{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{C}}
which is a critical point of
{\mathcal{E}}

and moreover
{\mathcal{E}}(\widetilde{\gamma})=\Lambda_{pq}
.
This violates the counter assumption (
4.11
).

The proof is complete.
∎

satisfy (

), (

In the polar coordinate of

a^{\star}=(1,\theta^{\star})
where we recall
\theta^{\star}

\mbox{$p=\rho a=(\rho,\theta)$ and $q=\rho a^{\star}=(\rho,\theta^{\star})$}.

4.7

(5.1)

5.1
Uniform estimate for distances between
o
and minmax geodesic segments

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

C_{\rm minmax}:=\sup_{\rho>R_{\kappa}}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho},o)<\infty.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction.
Assume that

\sup_{\rho>R_{\kappa}}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho},o)=\infty.

Then there exists
\{\rho_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
\rho_{j}\to\infty

and

\lim_{j\to\infty}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}},o)=\infty.

Write

\hat{\rho}_{j}:={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}},o)=\sup\{r>0:B(o,r)\cap\Gamma_{\rho_{j}}=\emptyset\}.

(5.2)

Note that
\hat{\rho}_{j}\leq\rho_{j}
and

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\hat{\rho}_{j}=+\infty.

(5.3)

To conclude some contradiction, we consider the following two cases:

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\hat{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\hat{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}=0.

Case 1.
\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\hat{\rho}_{j}}/{\rho_{j}}>0
.
There exists

such that

\hat{\rho}_{j}\geq\frac{1}{D}\rho_{j}\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5.4)

For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, consider the curve

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}:=\mathcal{D}_{1/\rho_{j}}\circ\Gamma_{\rho_{j}},

where we recall the scaling map from (
3.9
).
Since

\mathcal{D}_{1/\rho_{j}}(p_{j})=(\frac{\rho_{j}}{\rho_{j}},\theta)=a\mbox{ and }\mathcal{D}_{1/\rho_{j}}(q_{j})=(\frac{\rho_{j}}{\rho_{j}},\theta^{\star})=a^{\star},

we know that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}
is a geodesic segment in
M^{(1/\rho_{j})}=(M,g^{(1/\rho_{j})})
joining
a,a^{\star}
.
The assumption (
5.4
) implies that

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}\subset\overline{B(o,1)}\setminus B(o,1/D).

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
, there exists a subsequence
{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}
converges in
C^{2}
-norm to some geodesic segment
\Gamma
in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
joining
a,a^{\star}

and

\Gamma\subset\overline{B(o,1)}\setminus B(o,1/D).

Since
\Gamma
does not passing through the origin
o
of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}
, from (
2.14
) (and also (
2.12
) in Lemma
2.4
) it follows that

\Gamma
is
\Gamma_{k}^{\pm}
as given therein, and hence

\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}(\Gamma)\in\left\{2\sin\frac{(2k-1)\pi\sin\alpha}{2}:k=1,\cdots,\lfloor\frac{1}{2\sin\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\rfloor\right\}.

By our choice of
\epsilon
as in (
4.4
),
we know that

\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}(\Gamma)\leq\max_{k=1,\cdots,\lfloor\frac{1}{2\sin\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\rfloor}\left\{2\sin\frac{(2k-1)\pi\sin\alpha}{2}\right\}\leq 2(1-2\epsilon).

(5.5)

On the other hand, recalling (

), we know that

\ell_{g^{1/\rho_{j}}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}})=\frac{1}{\rho_{j}}\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}})\geq 2(1-\epsilon),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since the metric
g^{(1/\rho_{j})}
converges to
g_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}
in

-norm, we deduce that

\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}(\Gamma)=\lim_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\ni j\to\infty}\ell_{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{n}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}})=\lim_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\ni j\to\infty}\ell_{g^{(1/\rho_{j})}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{j}})\geq 2(1-\epsilon).

This contradicts with (
5.5
).

Case 2.
\limsup_{j\to\infty}{\hat{\rho}_{j}}/{\rho_{j}}=0
.

There exists a subsequence
{\mathbb{N}}_{2}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}\ni j\to\infty}\frac{\hat{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}=0.

(5.6)

Thus for any
N\geq 2
, there exists
m_{N}
such that

\hat{\rho}_{j}<\frac{1}{N}\rho_{j},\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{2}\mbox{ and }j\geq m_{N}.

(5.7)

For each

and

, by the definition of
\hat{\rho}_{j}
in (

),
there exists
\hat{t}_{\rho_{j}}\in(0,1)
such that

|\Gamma_{\rho_{j}}(\hat{t}_{\rho_{j}})|=\hat{\rho}_{j}.

Define

Since

\hat{\rho}_{j}<\frac{1}{N}\rho_{j}\mbox{ and
}|\Gamma_{\rho_{j}}(0)|=|p_{j}|=|q_{j}|=|\Gamma_{\rho_{j}}(1)|=\rho_{j},

one has

\Gamma_{\rho_{j}}([t_{\rho_{j},N}^{-},t_{\rho_{j},N}^{+}])\subset\overline{B(o,N\hat{\rho}_{j})},\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{2}\mbox{ and }j\geq m_{N}.

(5.8)

Observe that

implies

(2k+1)\pi\sin\alpha\neq\pi
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, letting

and

Hence recalling

from Lemma

), we have

{\bf K}_{\alpha}=\frac{|2\pi K_{\alpha}\sin\alpha-\pi|}{\sin\alpha}<\pi.

(5.9)

We will conclude the contradiction by showing the following two claims and thus complete the proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Claim I.
There is a large
{N_{0}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a subsequence
{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}\subset{\mathbb{N}}_{2}
so that

\displaystyle\angle(-\dot{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}(t^{-}_{\rho_{j},N_{0}}),\dot{\Gamma}_{\rho_{j}}(t^{+}_{\rho_{j},N_{0}}))\in[\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{10}(\pi-\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}),\mathbf{K}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{10}(\pi-\mathbf{K}_{\alpha})],\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}.

(5.10)

Claim II.

There is a subsequence

5.2

For any

Observe that the collection

\rho>r>{\bf r}

t_{\rho,r}^{-}\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\mbox{ and }t_{\rho,r}^{+}\in\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}.

(iv)

For all
\rho>r>{\bf r}
,

\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\mbox{ and }\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}\mbox{ are connected sets}.

(v)

For any
\rho>r>{\bf r}
, let
t_{\rho}
be any point in
[0,\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho})]
such that
\Gamma_{\rho}(t_{\rho})\in B(o,2C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}})
. Then,

\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\subset[0,t_{\rho})\mbox{ and }\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}\subset(t_{\rho},\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho})].

Moreover, for any
t\in[0,t_{\rho})
and
t^{\prime}\in(t_{\rho},\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho})]
with
|\Gamma_{\rho}(t)|=|\Gamma_{\rho}(t^{\prime})|=r
, one has

t\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\mbox{ and }t^{\prime}\in\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}.

Proof.

We first show (i). We only prove

\sup_{\rho}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r})=o(r)\quad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$;}

(5.26)

the proofs of the other two are similar.
We argue by contradiction. Assume that (
5.26
) is not true.
There is a constant
c_{0}>0
and a sequence
\{r_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
r_{i}\to\infty
such that

\frac{1}{r_{i}}\sup_{\rho}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r_{i}})\geq c_{0}\quad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We can further get a sequence
\{\rho_{i}\}
with
\rho_{i}>\max\{r_{i},R_{\kappa}\}
such that

\frac{1}{r_{i}}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}\quad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

This allows us to choose
\{s^{1}_{\rho_{i}},s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}\}\subset\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}

with
0<s^{1}_{\rho_{i}}<s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}<\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}})

such that

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s^{1}_{\rho_{i}}),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}))\geq\frac{c_{0}}{4}r_{i}\quad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5.27)

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
X_{\rho_{i}}\in C^{2}([0,1],M)
be the reparametrization of
\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}|_{[s^{1}_{\rho_{i}},s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}]}
by a constant multiple of arc-length, that is

X_{\rho_{i}}(t):=\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}((s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}-s^{1}_{\rho_{i}})t+s^{1}_{\rho_{i}})\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Note that

\left\{\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(0)}{r_{i}},\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(1)}{r_{i}}\right\}\subset\partial B(0,1).

Up to some subsequence, we may assume

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(0)}{r_{i}}=b^{0}\in\partial B(0,1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(1)}{r_{i}}=b^{1}\in\partial B(0,1).

By (
5.27
),

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(b^{0},b^{1})=\lim_{i\to\infty}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(0)}{r_{i}},\frac{X_{\rho_{i}}(1)}{r_{i}})\geq\frac{c_{0}}{4}.

Moreover, since
X_{\rho_{i}}
is a geodesic segment of
(M,g)
, we know that
\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}}
is a geodesic segment of
M^{(r_{i})}
.
Thanks to Lemma
3.5
and Remark
3.6
, we know

\sup_{i}\ell_{g^{(r_{i})}}(\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}})<\infty.

By the Arzela-Ascolli theorem, there exists a subsequence
{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
such that converges in
C^{2}
to a geodesic segment
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}^{n}
of the standard cone joining
b_{0}={\gamma}(0),b_{1}={\gamma}(1)
.
Since
b^{1}\neq b^{0}
and
b_{0},b_{1}\in\partial B(0,1)
, by Remark
2.5
, we have
{\gamma}\subset B(0,1)
and

\angle({\gamma}(1),\dot{\gamma}(1))<\frac{\pi}{2}.

Thus there exists
N
sufficiently large such that

\angle(\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}}(1),(\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}})^{\prime}(1))<\frac{\pi}{2}\quad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}\cap[N,\infty).

Since
\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}
is conformal, we deduce that

\angle(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}),\dot{\Gamma}_{\rho_{i}}(s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}))=\angle(\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}}(1),(\mathcal{D}_{r_{i}^{-1}}\circ X_{\rho_{i}})^{\prime}(1))<\frac{\pi}{2}\quad\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ast}\cap[N,\infty).

This contradicts to the fact that
s^{2}_{\rho_{i}}\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}
, which gives (i).

We show (ii).
By contradiction,
we can find
a sequence
\{r_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
r_{i}\to\infty
and
\{\rho_{i}\}

with
\rho_{i}>r_{i}
such that
\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
.
Under this counter assumption, we claim that

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}))=o(r_{i})\mbox{ as $i\to\infty$}.

(5.28)

Obviously, this contracts with Lemma
5.2
which gives

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{i}}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}))\geq\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{i}}|\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t^{+}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})-\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})|=|a-a^{\star}|>0.

To see the claim (
5.28
), note that the counter assumption guarantees that
\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}
contains some point
s_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}))

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t)):s,t\in\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup\{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i})):t\in\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup\{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i})):t\in\Delta_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}^{\pm}\}+2{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})).

Since (i) gives
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}))=o(r_{i})
,
to get the claim (
5.28
), we only need to show the bound

\sup\{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i})):t\in\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}\}=o(r_{i}).

Recall that
s_{i}
is of either type C or type F.
We consider the two cases separately.

Case that
s_{i}
is of type
C
.
It is clear that

\mbox{ $s_{i}\in\overline{\Delta^{+}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}$ and $s_{\rho_{i}}\in\overline{\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}$}.

(5.29)

For any
t\in\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}
we deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\overline{\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}))={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})),

(5.30)

thanks to (i),
which gives the desired bound
o(r_{i})
as
i\to\infty
.

Case that
s_{i}
is of type
F
.

Recall that
I_{s_{i}}=[s_{i}^{-},s_{i}^{+}]
is the connected component in
\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}
.
We observe that

\mbox{either $s_{i}^{\pm}\in\overline{\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}$ or $s_{i}^{\pm}\in\overline{\Delta^{\mp}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}$},

(5.31)

where due to the type F of
s^{\pm}_{i}
, we rule out the cases that
s_{i}^{\pm}\in\overline{\Delta^{+}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}
and
s_{i}^{\pm}\in\overline{\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
s_{i}^{\pm}\in\overline{\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}}
.

For any point
t\in\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(t),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s^{\pm}_{i}))+{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s_{i}),\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(s^{\pm}_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}}))+{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Gamma_{\rho_{i}}(\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho_{i},r_{i}})),

thanks to (i),
which also gives the desired bound
o(r_{i})
as
i\to\infty
.
This completes the proof (ii).

To see (iii), by the definition of
t_{\rho,r}^{-}
, we know
|\Gamma_{\rho}(t)|>r
for all
t\in[0,t_{j,r}^{-})
. Thus
|\Gamma_{j}(\cdot)|
is strictly decreasing from left at
t_{j,r}^{-}
. This implies that
t_{j,r}^{-}\in\Delta^{-}_{j_{i},r_{i}}\cup\Delta^{\circ}_{j_{i},r_{i}}
. Since
r>{\bf r}
, by (ii),
\Delta^{\circ}_{j_{i},r_{i}}
is empty. We deduce that
t_{j,r}^{-}\in\Delta^{-}_{j_{i},r_{i}}
. The proof of
t_{j,r}^{+}\in\Delta^{+}_{j_{i},r_{i}}
is similar.

To see (iv), we only prove
\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
is connected and the proof for
\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}
is similar.
We argue by contradiction. Assume that
\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
is not connected for some
\rho,r
.
Note that the set
\Delta_{\rho,r}
is closed, and by (ii),

\Delta_{\rho,r}=\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\cup\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\cap\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}=\emptyset.

Then the
sets
\Delta^{\pm}_{\rho,r}
are closed.
By the assumption, there must be two connected components

\mbox{$I_{1}=[t_{1},t_{1}^{\prime}],I_{2}=[t_{2},t_{2}^{\prime}]\subset\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}$ with $t_{1}^{\prime}<t_{2}$}.

By the definition of
\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
, we know that
|\Gamma_{\rho}(\cdot)|
is strictly decreasing from left at
t_{2}
.
Thus, there exists
t_{3}\in(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2})
such that

r^{\prime}:=|\Gamma_{\rho}(t_{3})|=\max_{t_{3}\in[t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}]}|\Gamma_{\rho}(t)|>|\Gamma_{\rho}(t_{1}^{\prime})|=|\Gamma_{\rho}(t_{2})|=r.

Thus,
t_{3}
has type C or F and consequently
t_{3}\in\Delta^{\circ}_{\rho,r^{\prime}}
. This contradicts to (ii).

To see (v), we only show
\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\subset[0,t_{\rho})
and the other one is similar.
Since
|\Gamma_{\rho}(t_{\rho})|<2C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}}<r
, one has
t_{\rho}\notin\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
. By (iv), the connectedness of
\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
implies that

\mbox{ either $\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\subset[0,t_{\rho})$ or $\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\subset(t_{\rho},\ell_{g}(\Gamma_{\rho})]$}.

Obviously,
t^{-}_{\rho,r}\in[0,t_{\rho})
. Combining the fact from (iii) that
t^{-}_{\rho,r}\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
, we get the desire result.

Finally, if
t\in[0,t_{\rho})
with
|\Gamma(t)|=r
, we first know
t\in\Delta_{\rho,r}
. Since
\Delta_{\rho,r}=\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}\cup\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}
and
\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r}\cap[0,t_{\rho})=\emptyset
, we conclude
t\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
.
The same proof also works for
t^{\prime}
.

The proof of Lemma
5.3
is complete.
∎

A direct consequence of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
is the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

One has

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup\left\{|a-\frac{x}{r}|:x\in\bigcup_{\rho>r}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r})\right\}=0,\mbox{ and }\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup\left\{|a^{\star}-\frac{y}{r}|:y\in\bigcup_{\rho>r}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r})\right\}=0.

Proof.

We only show the first equality and the second one is similar.
For any
r>\max\{R_{\kappa},{\bf r}\}
and
\rho>r
, by Lemma
5.3
(iii), we know that
t^{-}_{\rho,r}\in\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r}
. For any
x\in\cup_{\rho>r}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho,r})
, we have
x\in\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{x},r})
for some
\rho_{x}>r
.
Using the triangle inequality, we deduce that

|ra-x|\leq|ra-\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(t^{-}_{{}_{\rho_{x}},r})|+|\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(t^{-}_{\rho_{x},r})-x|\leq|ra-\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(t^{-}_{{}_{\rho_{x}},r})|+{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{x},r}),

thanks to Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
(i),

|a-\frac{x}{r}|\leq|a-\frac{\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(t^{-}_{{}_{\rho_{x}},r})}{r}|+\frac{1}{r}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Gamma_{\rho_{x}}(\Delta^{-}_{\rho_{x},r})\to 0\mbox{ as $r\to\infty$.}

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Recall

\Gamma^{\ast}_{i}(t+u_{i}^{-})&&t\in[-u_{i}^{-},-u_{i-1}^{-})\cup(T_{i-1}^{\ast}-u_{i-1}^{-},T_{i}^{\ast}-u_{i}^{-}].\end{array}\right.

Since all
\Gamma_{i}^{\ast}\in C^{2}([0,T_{i}^{\ast}],M)
are geodesic segments of
(M,g)
with index
\leq n-1
, thanks to (
5.39
), (
5.40
) and (
5.41
), we deduce that

\Gamma\in C^{2}((-\infty,\infty),M)
is a well-defined geodesic line of
(M,g)
with Morse index
\leq n-1
.

In addition, by the relation (
5.39
), one further observe that

\Gamma(t)=\Gamma^{\ast}_{i}(t+u_{i}^{-}),\quad t\in[-u_{i}^{-},T_{i}^{\ast}-u_{i}^{-}],\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular,

\Gamma(t)=\Gamma^{\ast}_{i}(t+u_{i}^{-}),\quad t\in[0,T_{i}^{\ast}-u_{i}^{-}],\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5.42)

Step 4. Show
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma=\{\theta,\theta^{\star}\}
.

It suffices to show that for any sequence
s_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma(-s_{k})}{|\Gamma(-s_{k})|}=a=(1,\theta)\mbox{ and }\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s_{k})}{|\Gamma(s_{k})|}=a^{\star}=(1,\theta^{\star}).

(5.43)

We only show the second equality in (
5.43
); the proof for the first one is much similar.
Recalling that the exponential map of
M
is proper, we know
\Gamma^{-1}(B(o,r))\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is compact. Thus

\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{k}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}|\Gamma(s_{k})|=\infty.

(5.44)

Noting that
\Gamma|_{[0,T^{\ast}_{1}]}=\Gamma_{1}^{\ast}
and
\Gamma_{1}^{\ast}\cap\overline{B(o,C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}})}\neq\emptyset
, we know there exists
s_{\ast}\in[0,T^{\ast}_{1}]
such that

\Gamma(s_{\ast})\in\overline{B(o,C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}})}.

(5.45)

In the following, we assume
s_{k}>T_{1}^{\ast}\geq s_{\ast}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we know there exists
i_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}i_{k}=\infty
such that

s_{k}\in(T_{i_{k}-1}^{\ast}-u_{i_{k}-1}^{-},T_{i_{k}}^{\ast}-u_{i_{k}}^{-}].

By the definition of
\Gamma
and (
5.42
), we know

\Gamma(t)=\Gamma_{i_{k}}^{\ast}(t+u_{i_{k}}^{-}),\quad t\in[0,T_{i_{k}}^{\ast}-u_{i_{k}}^{-}].

(5.46)

Recalling (
5.38
) in Step 2, we further have,

\Gamma(t)=\Gamma_{i_{k}}^{\ast}(t+u_{i_{k}}^{-})=\lim_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{i_{k}}:\rho_{j}>d_{k}}\Gamma_{j}(t+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j,r_{i_{k}}}^{-}),\quad t\in[0,T_{i_{k}}^{\ast}-u_{i_{k}}^{-}].

In particular,

\Gamma(s_{k})=\lim_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{i_{k}}:\rho_{j}>d_{k}}\Gamma_{j}(s_{k}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j,r_{i_{k}}}^{-})\mbox{ and }\Gamma(s_{\ast})=\lim_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{i_{k}}:\rho_{j}>d_{k}}\Gamma_{j}(s_{\ast}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j,r_{i_{k}}}^{-}).

(5.47)

From the first equality in (
5.47
), for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we can find some
j_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{i_{k}}
with
\rho_{j_{k}}>d_{k}
such that

|\Gamma(s_{k})-\Gamma_{j_{k}}(s_{k}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j_{k},r_{i_{k}}}^{-})|\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Since (
5.45
) gives
\Gamma(s_{\ast})\in\overline{B(o,C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}})},

from the second equality in (
5.47
) we deduce that, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{i_{k}}
with
\rho_{j}>d_{k}
,

\Gamma_{j}(s_{\ast}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j,r_{i_{k}}}^{-})\in B(o,C_{\ast}C_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,minmax\,}}).

for some
C_{\ast}\geq 1
. Write
For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

y_{k}:=\Gamma_{j_{k}}(s_{k}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j_{k},r_{i_{k}}}^{-})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{d}_{k}|y_{k}|.

Recalling that
s_{k}>s_{\ast}
, applying Lemma
5.3
(v) with
t_{\rho}=s_{\ast}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j_{k},r_{i_{k}}}^{-}
and
\Gamma_{\rho}=\Gamma_{j_{k}}
therein, we deduce that

s_{k}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j_{k},r_{i_{k}}}^{+}\in\Delta_{\rho_{j_{k}},\widetilde{d}_{k}}^{+},\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Note that

|\frac{y_{k}}{d_{k}}-\frac{y_{k}}{\widetilde{d}_{k}}|\leq|y_{k}|\frac{|\widetilde{d}_{k}-d_{k}|}{d_{k}\widetilde{d}_{k}}\leq\frac{|\Gamma(s_{k})-\Gamma_{j_{k}}(s_{k}+u_{i}^{-}+t_{j_{k},r_{i_{k}}}^{-})|}{d_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{kd_{k}}.

Applying Corollary
5.4
, we conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|\frac{\Gamma(s_{k})}{|\Gamma(s_{k})|}-a^{\star}|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}|\frac{\Gamma(s_{k})}{d_{k}}-\frac{y_{k}}{d_{k}}|+\lim_{k\to\infty}|\frac{y_{k}}{d_{k}}-\frac{y_{k}}{\widetilde{d}_{k}}|+\lim_{k\to\infty}|\frac{y_{k}}{\widetilde{d}_{k}}-a^{\star}|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{kd_{k}}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{kd_{k}}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup\{|\frac{y}{r}-a^{\star}|:y\in\bigcup_{\rho>r}\Gamma_{\rho}(\Delta^{+}_{\rho,r})\}

\displaystyle=0,

which gives the second equality in (
5.43
) as desired.

This completes the proof of Step 4, also completes the whole proof.

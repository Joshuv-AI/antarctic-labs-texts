Regularized Multiobjective Optimization with Directionally Lipschitzian Data
G. C. Bento ∗, J. X. Cruz Neto, J. O. Lopes, B. S. Mordukhovich, P. R. Silva Filho

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to the study of regularized versions of multiobjective optimization problems described by directionally Lipschitzian functions. Such regularizations appear in proximal-type algorithms of multiobjective optimization, various models of machine learning, medical physics, etc. We investigate and illustrate several useful properties of directionally Lipschitzian functions, which distinguish them from locally Lipschitzian ones. By using advanced tools of variational analysis and generalized differentiation revolving around the limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential, we derive necessary conditions for Pareto optimality in regularized multiobjective problems. Keywords : multiobjective optimization, regularizations, Pareto optimality, directionally Lipschitzian functions, variatiional analysis and generalized differentiation, necessary optimality conditions Mathematics Subject Classification (2000): 90C29, 90B50, 49J52, 49J53, 49K35,

Introduction

Multiobjective optimization is an area of decision-making with multiple criteria that is dedicated to simultaneously optimizing problems involving several objective functions. Since there exists no single point that can minimize all the objective functions at the same time, various notions of vector optimality have been introduced and studied in the literature. Most of them revolve around
Pareto optimality/efficiency
, which is often replaced by the
weak Pareto
notion that is more convenient for mathematical methods. Multiobjective optimization has a variety of applications to numerous fields of science and technology including engineering, economics, statistics, industry, agriculture, artificial intelligence, medical physics, etc. Among great many publications devoted to various versions of multiobjective optimization, its extensions and applications, we refer the reader to, e.g.,

Advanced trends in optimization theory and applications, particularly to models of machine learning, statistics, and data science, consist of adding
regularizing terms
to minimizing (scalar or vector) costs. Such terms are proved to be crucial in the design and justification of
fast algorithms
in optimization and applied modeling. To this end, we mention the

Most of the known multiobjective models in optimization theory concerned problems with smooth, convex, and/or Lipschitz continuous data and addressed deriving necessary optimality conditions for
weak Pareto
minimizers. The first optimality conditions for weak Pareto solutions to Lipschitzian multiobjective problems appeared in
[
9
,
15

.

In this paper, we consider multiobjective optimization problems defined by
directionally Lipschitzian
functions. Our main attention here is directed not towards general multiobjective models but towards their
regularizations
. For definiteness and simplicity, our study in this paper is confined to the
proximal regularization

Pareto
(not just weak Pareto) minimizers of the
regularized multiobjective
models in terms of Mordukhovich’s
limiting subdifferential
, which is essentially smaller than its Clarke’s counterpart and enjoys comprehensive
calculus rules
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section

6
resumes the main achievements of the paper and discusses some directions of our future research and perspectives for applications.

2.
Generalized Differentiation in Variational Analysis

This section presents some notions and results of variational analysis and generalized differentiation, which are mostly taken from the books
[
16
,
18
,
22
]
and play a crucial role in establishing the main achievements of this paper.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
be an extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) function. For a sequence
\{u^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation
u^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\bar{u}
to signify that
u^{k}\to\bar{u}
with
f(u^{k})\to f(\bar{u})
. The
regular/Fréchet subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;f(x)<\infty\}
is defined by

(2.1)

\partial^{F}f(\overline{x}):=\bigg\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\bigg|\;\liminf_{x\to\overline{x}}\dfrac{f(x)-f(\overline{x})-\langle v,x-\overline{x}\rangle}{\|x-\overline{x}\|}\geq 0\bigg\},

while the
limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}f
is

(2.2)

\partial^{M}f(\bar{x})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\bar{x}}\partial^{F}f(x)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\Big|\;\exists\,x^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\bar{x},\,v^{k}\in\partial^{F}f(x^{k}),\,v^{k}\to v\right\},

where “Limsup” denotes the (Painlevé-Kuratowski)
outer limit
of a set-valued mapping/multifunction
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
z\to\bar{x}
given by

(2.3)

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{z\to\bar{z}}G(z):=\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;\big|\;\exists\,z^{k}\to\bar{z},\;y^{k}\to y\;\mbox{ with }\;y^{k}\in G(z^{k})\big\}.

It is well known that if
f
is (Fréchet) differentiable at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}f
, then its regular subdifferential at
\bar{x}
is a singleton
\partial^{F}f(\bar{x})=\{\nabla f(\bar{x})\}
. If
f
is continuously differentiable around
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}f
(or merely strictly differentiable at this point), then its limiting subdifferential reduces to the same singleton. In contrast to (
2.1
), the limiting subdifferential (
2.2
), while being nonconvex-valued, enjoys
full calculus
based on
variational/extremal principles
of variational analysis. In particular, we have the following fundamental
sum rule

(see, e.g.,
[
18
, Corollary 2.21]
), where
\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})
stands for the
singular/horizon subdifferential
of
f
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
defined by

(2.4)

\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x}):=\big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\exists\,x^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\bar{x},\,\lambda_{k}\downarrow 0,\,v^{k}\in\partial^{F}f(x^{k}),\,\lambda_{k}v^{k}\to v\big\}.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be extended-real-valued functions that are l.s.c. around some point
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. Impose the qualification condition

(2.5)

\big[v_{1}+\ldots+v_{m}=0,\,v_{i}\in\partial^{\infty}f_{i}(\bar{x})\big]\Longrightarrow\big[v_{i}=0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i=1,\ldots,m\big]

expressed in terms of the singular subdifferential (
2.4
). Then we have

(2.6)

\partial(f_{1}+\ldots+f_{m})(\bar{x})\subset\partial f_{1}(\bar{x})+\ldots+\partial f_{m}(\bar{x}).

It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 4.15]
)
that the condition
\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})=\{0\}
is a complete
characterization
of the
local Lipschitzian
property of an l.s.c. function
f
around
\bar{x}
. Thus the qualification condition (
2.5
) is satisfied if all but one of the functions
f_{i}
are Lipschitz continuous around
\bar{x}
. Note that the singular subdifferential mapping (
2.4
) shares full calculus with the limiting one (
2.2
).

Another important property of the limiting subgradient mapping is its
robustness
by which we mean the following.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. function around
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
, and let
\{x^{k}\},\{\omega^{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be sequences such that
\omega^{k}\in\partial^{M}f(x^{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
with
x^{k}\to x
,
\omega^{k}\to\omega
, and
f(x^{k})\to f(x)
as
k\to\infty
. Then we have the inclusion
\omega\in\partial^{M}f(x)
.

The proof of this result follows directly from definition (
2.2
) and the diagonal process in finite dimensions. The robustness property also holds for the singular subdifferential (
2.4
) while fails to the regular subgradient mapping (
2.1
) even in the simplest case of
f(x):=-|x|
with
\bar{x}=0\in\mathbb{R}
.

The results obtained below via the limiting subdifferential (
2.2
) for Pareto minimizers have serious advantages in comparison with known in the literature even for the case of weak Pareto solutions, where the Clarke’s generalized gradients of locally Lipschitzian functions are used instead of (
2.2
). Given a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
that is Lipschitz continuous around
\bar{x}
, recall first the (Clarke)
generalized directional derivative
of
f
at
\bar{x}
in the direction of
d
defined by

\displaystyle f^{\circ}(\bar{x},d)=\limsup_{{x\to\bar{x}}\atop{t\downarrow 0}}\dfrac{f(x+td)-f(x)}{t}.

Then (Clarke’s)
generalized gradient
of
f
at
\bar{x}
is

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial^{C}f(\bar{x})=\big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\langle v,d\rangle\leq f^{\circ}(\bar{x},d)\;\mbox{ for all }\;d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\big\}.

We have the following relationship between the generalized gradient and limiting subdifferentials of locally Lipschitzian functions (see
[
16
, Theorem 3.57]
):

\partial^{C}f(\bar{x})={\rm clco}\,\partial^{M}f(\bar{x}),

where “clco” stands for the weak closure of the convex hull of the set. Note that, while the generalized gradient (
2.7
) of locally Lipschitzian functions is robust, this property fails in more general settings.

Finally in this section, recall that the
distance function

d_{\Omega}(x)
associated with a nonempty set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(2.8)

d_{\Omega}(x):=\inf\big\{\|x-y\|\;\big|\;\;y\in\Omega\big\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is well-known that the distance function (
2.8
) is globally Lipschitzian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz constant one. If
\Omega
is closed around
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, then the limiting subdifferential of (
2.8
) is calculated by (see
[
18
, Theorem 1.33(i)]
)

(2.9)

\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=N^{M}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathbb{B},

where
\mathbb{B}
is the closed unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and where the (Mordukhovich)
limiting normal cone
is defined by

(2.10)

N^{M}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\bar{x}}\big[\mbox{\rm cone}\,\big(x-\Pi_{\Omega}(x)\big)\big]

via the outer limit (
2.3
) involving the Euclidean projector
\Pi_{\Omega}
associated with the set
\Omega
. The notation “cone” stands in (
2.10
) for the (nonconvex) conic hall of a set.

3.
Directionally Lipschitzian Functions

Now we define, following Rockafellar
[
21
]
,
a class of extended-real-valued functions that extending the local Lipschitz continuity; see
[
7
,
21
,
22
]
for more details.

Definition 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be l.s.c. function around
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
. We say that
f
is
directionally Lipschitzian
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
if there is a unit vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

(3.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}\overline{x}\\
v\to u\\
t\downarrow 0\end{subarray}}\frac{f(x+tv)-f(x)}{t}<\infty.

The function
f
is directionally Lipschitzian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if it satisfies (
3.1
) at every point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, a
vector mapping

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is directionally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}

(
resp. on
\mathbb{R}^{n})
if each of its components
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, enjoys the corresponding property.

In what follows, we reveal some useful assertions concerning directionally Lipschitzian functions. Let us start with providing an example of a real function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is continuous and directionally Lipschitzian while not locally Lipschitzian around the reference point.

Example 1
.

Consider the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

(3.2)

f(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big({x_{i}}\big)^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This function is obviously continuous but not locally Lipschitzian around
\bar{x}=0
. We now show that (
3.2
) is directionally Lipschitzian at this point. Fix the vector

u=\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},\ldots,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

and take
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
v\to u
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\frac{f(x+tv)-f(x)}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}-x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{t}\cdot\frac{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}}{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{tv_{i}}{t}\cdot\frac{1}{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{v_{i}}{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right].

Letting
y_{i}:=(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}
leads us to the quadratic equation

y_{i}^{2}+x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{i}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}=0\;\mbox{ for each }\;i=1,\ldots,n.

Computing the discriminant for each
i
tells us that

\Delta_{i}=x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}-4x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}=-3x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq 0,

which ensures in turn that, whenever
i=1,\dots,n
, we get

(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}=y_{i}^{2}+x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{i}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}\geq 0.

Therefore, for
v_{i}
sufficiently close to
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
and each
i=1,\dots,n
, it follows that

\frac{f(x+tv)-f(x)}{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{v_{i}}{(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{2}{3}}+(x_{i}+tv_{i})^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}}+x_{i}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right]\leq 0.

The latter readily verifies that
f
in (
3.2
) is directionally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}
.

1
for the sum of a locally Lipschitzian and a directionally Lipschitzian functions when the qualification condition (
2.5
) is not trivial as in the fully Lipschitzian case while still holds and allows us to use the subdifferential sum rule in directionally Lipschitzian settings.

Example 2
.

Consider the functions
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f_{1}(x):=x^{\frac{1}{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}x\sin{\frac{1}{x}}&\text{if }x>0,\\
-x&\text{if }x\leq 0.\end{cases}

Since
f_{2}
is locally Lipschitzian on
\mathbb{R}
and
f_{1}
is locally Lipschitzian for all
x\neq 0
, we get from the above that
\partial^{\infty}f_{2}(x)=\{0\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(x)=\{0\}
for all
x\neq 0
. On the other hand, it follows from definition (
2.4
) of the singular subdifferential that
\partial^{\infty}f_{1}(0)=[0,\infty)
. It is easy to check that
f_{2}
is directionally Lipschitzian at each point
x\in\mathbb{R}
with the same unit vector as
f_{1}
. Furthermore, the qualifying condition (
2.5
) holds for these functions, and hence the subdifferential sum rule (
2.6
) is applicable in this case.

Below we present several properties of directionally Lipschitzian functions used in what follows. The first one is a direct consequence of Definition
1
.

Proposition 3
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is directionally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,f
, then there exist
\delta>0
, a unit vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and a constant
L=L(\overline{x},u)
dependent on
\bar{x}
and
u
such that we have the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\qquad\qquad f(z+tv)-f(z)\leq t\cdot L(\overline{x},u)\\
\mbox{for all }\;v\in B_{\delta}(u),\;z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{x}),\;f(z)\in B_{\delta}(f(\overline{x})),\;t\in(0,\delta],\end{array}

where
B_{r}(\theta)
denotes the open ball centered at a vector
\theta
with radius
r>0
.

The next proposition establishes
calculus rules
for the directional Lipschitzian functions telling us that the directional Lipschitzian property is preserved under the function summation and taking the maximum.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\psi,\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be directionally Lipschitzian functions at
\bar{x}
with the same unit vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following hold:

(i)
The sum function
\varphi(x):=\psi(x)+\phi(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is directionally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}
with the unit vector
u
.

(ii)
The maximum function
\varphi(x):=\max\{\psi(x),\phi(x)\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is directionally Lipschitzian at
\bar{x}
with the unit vector
u
.

Proof.

To verify (i), take the common unit vector
u
associated with the functions
\psi
and
\phi
. Then the directional Lipschitzian property of
\varphi=\psi+\phi
follows directly from (
3.1
) due to the definition of the upper limit “
\limsup
” therein.

To prove (ii), observe the inequality

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\dfrac{\max\{\psi,\phi\}(x+tv)-\max\{\psi,\phi\}(x)}{t}\\
\\
&\leq\dfrac{\max\{\psi(x+tv)-\psi(x),\phi(x+tv)-\phi(x)\}}{t}.\end{array}

It follows from Proposition
3
that there exist
\delta>0
and
L(\overline{x},u)
such that we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\psi(x+tv)-\psi(x)\leq t\cdot L(\overline{x},u)~\mbox{ and }~\phi(x+tv)-\phi(x)\leq t\cdot L(\overline{x},u)

for all
v\in B_{\delta}(u),\;z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{x}),\;\psi(x)\in B_{\delta}(f(\overline{x})),\phi(x)\in B_{\delta}(f(\overline{x}))
, and
t\in(0,\delta]
.
Combining (
3
) and (
3.6
) gives us

\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}\overline{x}\\
v\to u\\
t\downarrow 0\end{subarray}}\bigg[\frac{\varphi(x+tv)-\varphi(x)}{t}\bigg]\leq L(\overline{x},u)<\infty,

which therefore verifies the claimed directional Lipschitzian property by (
3.1
).

4.
Regularized Models of Multiobjective Optimization

We start with recalling the notion
Pareto optimality/efficiency
in constrained problems of multiobjective optimization.

Definition 2
.

Given a nonempty set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a vector-valued mapping
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we say that
\bar{x}\in\Omega
is a
Pareto solution/minimizer
of the constrained multiobjective problem labeled as

(4.1)

{\rm min}\big\{F(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Omega\big\}

if there exists no point
x\in\Omega
with
f_{i}(x)\leq f_{i}(\bar{x})
for all

i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
f_{j}(x)<f_{j}(\bar{x})
for at least one index
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
The notation

{\rm argmin}\big\{F(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Omega\}

stands for the collection of all Pareto optimal solutions to problem (
4.1
).

The
local
Pareto solution of (
4.1
) is defined similarly, while we’ll proceed with the study of (global) Pareto solutions for simplicity. Recall for comparison that the
weak Pareto
optimality in (
4.1
) means that there is no
x\in\Omega
such that
f_{i}(x)<f_{i}(\bar{x})
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. We are not going to deal with the latter notion in this paper.

In fact, our main interest is devoted not to problem (
4.1
), but its
regularizations
; see the discussion in Section
1
. In this paper, we concentrate on the study of Pareto solutions to the
proximal-type regularization
of (
4.1
) written as

(4.2)

\mbox{min}\{\Psi(x):=F(x)+\lambda\|x-{\widetilde{x}}\|^{2}\Upsilon\;\big|\;x\in\mathcal{D}\big\},

where
\mathcal{D}:=\big\{x\ \in\Omega\;\big|\;\Phi(x):=F(x)-F({\widetilde{x}})\leq 0\}
with the vector inequality understood componentwise, where
\widetilde{x}\in\Omega
,
\lambda>0
, and
\Upsilon=(\upsilon_{1},\ldots,\upsilon_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a unit vector with all the positive components. To the best of our knowledge, problem (
4.2
) has its origins in
[
23
]
with stabilizing ill-posed problems (what is now known as the
Tikhonov regularization
), and in
[
19
]
with the
Moreau envelope
(or
Moreau-Yosida regularization
), which was subsequently used by Rockafellar to formalize the
proximal point algorithm
(PPA)
[
20
]
. In the vector context, it was further developed in
[
6
]
formalizing the vectorial PPA; see also
[
2
,
3
,
8
]
and the references therein for more recent developments in this direction.

In what follows, we study Pareto optimality in the regularized problem (
4.2
). Let us first verify the possibility of the
exact penalization
of geometric constraints in minimizing
directionally Lipschitzian
cost functions. The result below of its own interest is an extended directionally Lipschitzian counterpart of
[
9
, Proposition 2.4.3]
obtained therein for constrained optimization problems with Lipschitzian costs.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a directionally 1Lipschitzian and continuous function, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a nonempty closed set. If
\bar{x}\in\Omega
minimizes
f
over
\Omega
, then there exist a unit vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant
L=L(\bar{x},u)
from Proposition
3
such that
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of the penalized function
f+\tau d_{\Omega}
whenever
\tau\geq L(\bar{x},u)
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that the result is false. Then there exists a sequence
\{x^{k}\}
converging to
\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+\tau d_{\Omega}(x^{k})<f(\bar{x}).

By the imposed closedness of
\Omega
, we have
d_{\Omega}(x^{k})>0
since otherwise
x^{k}\in\Omega
, which contradicts the fact that
\bar{x}
is a minimizer of
f
over
\Omega
. The assumed continuity of
f
implies that
f(x^{k})\to f(\bar{x})
as
k\to\infty
. Let
k\in{\rm I\!N}
be sufficiently large to ensure that the directionally Lipschitzian constant holds in the ball around
x^{k}
with radius
2d_{\Omega}(x^{k})
; this is possible because
d_{\Omega}(x^{k})
converges to
0
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, it follows from (
4.3
) that we can choose
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

(4.4)

f(x^{k})+(1+\varepsilon)\tau d_{\Omega}(x^{k})<f(\bar{x})

and then select a positive number
t
with
t\leq(1+\varepsilon)d_{\Omega}(x^{k})
. Choosing further
v\in B_{\delta}(u)
with
(x^{k}+tv)\in\Omega
tells us that

\displaystyle f(x^{k}+tv)\leq f(x^{k})+t\cdot L(\bar{x},u)\leq f(x^{k})+t\tau

by
t\leq(1+\varepsilon)d_{\Omega}(x^{k})
. Thus it follows from
(
4.4
) that
f(x^{k}+tv)<f(x^{k})
, a contradiction, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The next proposition is useful in the proof of the main result in Section
5
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\bar{x}
be a Pareto optimal solution to problem (
4.2
). Then for all
\gamma>0
and
x\in\Omega
, we have the condition

(4.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\gamma}(x):=\max\big\{\psi_{i}(x)-\psi_{i}(\bar{x})+\gamma,\phi_{i}(x)\;\big|\;i=1,\ldots,m\big\}>0,

where
\psi_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
are, respectively, the components of
\Psi
and
\Phi
in (
4.2
).

Proof.

Supposing the contrary, we find
\gamma_{0}>0
and
x_{1}\in\Omega
such that

\varphi_{\gamma_{0}}(x_{1})\leq 0
, which readily implies the inequalities

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(x_{1})<\psi_{i}(x_{1})+\gamma_{0}\leq\psi_{i}(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ and }\;\phi_{i}(x_{1})\leq 0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i=1,\ldots,m.

This tells us that
x_{1}\in\mathcal{D}
, which contradicts the Pareto optimality of
\bar{x}
in (
4.2
) and thus completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Necessary Conditions for Pareto Optimal Solutions

Now we are ready to establish novel necessary optimality conditions for Pareto optimal solutions to regularized multiobjective problems of type (
4.2
) with directionally Lipschitzian components by using the limiting subgradients (
2.2
) of the functions involved. Although all the functions in (
4.2
) are directionally Lipschitzian on their domains, they are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous around Pareto minimizers, which is the case, e.g., of the multiobjective problem in Example
3
. In Remark
1
, we discuss another form of necessary conditions for Pareto optimal solutions in fully non-Lipschitzian multiobjective settings.

Theorem 2
.

Let the mapping
F
in problem (
4.2
) be directionally Lipschitzian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and Lipschitz continuous around a Pareto optimal solution
\bar{x}
to (
4.2
), and let
\Omega
be closed. Then there exist numbers
\tau>0
,
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
, and
\beta_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
satisfying the following optimality conditions:

(5.1)

0\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})\partial^{M}f_{i}(\bar{x})+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\upsilon_{i}(\bar{x}-\widetilde{x})+N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\tau\mathbb{B},

\mbox{with}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta_{i}\big(f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}(\widetilde{x})\big)=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

where
\widetilde{x}
,
\lambda
, and
\Upsilon=(\upsilon_{1},\ldots,\upsilon_{m})
are taken from the formulation of problem
(
4.2
)
.

Proof.

Fix a Pareto optimal solution
\bar{x}
to (
4.2
) and pick a sequence of positive numbers
\gamma_{l}\downarrow 0
as
l\to\infty
. It follows from Proposition
5
that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)>0\;\mbox{ whenever }\;l\in\mathbb{N}\;\mbox{ and }\;x\in\Omega

for the maximum function (
4.5
). It follows from Proposition
4
that the function
\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}
is directionally Lipschitzian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We can also see that this function is locally Lipschitzian around
\bar{x}
. Observe furthermore that for
0<\gamma_{l}\leq 1
, the function
\varphi_{\gamma}(x)
is nonnegative on
\Omega
, i.e., it is bounded from below on this set. Using (
5.2
) and
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{D}
yields
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)\geq 0
, which tells us in turn that

\displaystyle\gamma_{l}=\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(\bar{x})\leq\inf_{x\in\Omega}\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)+\gamma_{l}.

Now we are in a position to apply the fundamental Ekeland variational principle (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Theorem 2.26]
) to the extended-real-valued l.s.c. function

\widetilde{\varphi}_{\gamma_{l}}(x):=\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)+\delta_{\Omega}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

where
\delta_{\Omega}
stands for the indicator function of
\Omega
that equals 0 for
x\in\Omega
and
\infty
otherwise. This allows us to find
z^{l}\in\Omega
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\bar{x}-z^{l}\|\leq\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\;\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\|x-z^{l}\|\geq\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\Omega.

The latter mean that
z^{l}
is a global solution to the constrained optimization problem

(5.4)

\mbox{\rm minimize}\;\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\|x-z^{l}\|,\quad x\in\Omega.

Note that the cost function in (
5.4
) is directionally Lipschitzian by Proposition
4
(i) due to this property of
\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}
and the Lipschitz continuity of
\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\|\cdot-z^{l}\|
. Denoting by
L(z^{l},u)
a directionally Lipschitz constant of
\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(\cdot)+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\|\cdot-z^{l}\|
and employing Theorem
1
ensure, for
l
sufficiently large, that
z^{l}
is a local minimizer of the unconstrained problem

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm minimize}\,\chi_{\gamma_{l}}(x):=\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(x)+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\|x-z^{l}\|+\tau d_{\Omega}(x)

whenever
\tau\geq L(z^{l},u)
. Thus
0\in\partial^{M}\chi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})
by the generalized Fermat rule from
[
16
, Proposition 1.114]
. Observing that, for
l
sufficiently large, all the functions in (
5.5
) are locally Lipschitzian around
z^{l}
allows us to apply the subdifferential sum rule from Proposition
1
, where the qualification condition (
2.5
) holds automatically. We arrive in this way at

\displaystyle 0\subset\partial^{M}\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\partial^{M}(\|\cdot-z^{l}\|)(z^{l})+\tau\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(z^{l}).

Therefore, for each
l
sufficiently large there exists
\omega^{l}\in\partial^{M}\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})
satisfying the inclusion

(5.6)

\displaystyle 0\in\omega^{l}+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\partial^{M}(\|\cdot-z^{l}\|)(z^{l})+\tau\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(z^{l}).

Applying to
\omega^{l}\in\partial^{M}\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})
the subdifferentiation of the maximum function from
[
16
, Theorem 3.46(ii)]
, we find

\alpha^{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
and
\beta^{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
such that

(5.7)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}(\alpha^{l}_{i}+\beta^{l}_{i})=1,

(5.8)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&(\alpha^{l}_{i}+\beta^{l}_{i})\big[(\psi(z^{l})-\psi(\bar{x})+\gamma_{l})-\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})\big]=0~\mbox{and}\\
&(\alpha^{l}_{i}+\beta^{l}_{i})\big[\phi(z^{l})-\varphi_{\gamma_{l}}(z^{l})\big]=0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i=1,\ldots,m,\end{array}

(5.9)

\omega^{l}\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}^{l}+\beta_{i}^{l})\partial^{M}f_{i}(z^{l})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda\alpha_{i}^{l}\upsilon_{i}(z^{l}-\widetilde{x}).

Combining (
5.6
) and (
5.9
) leads us to the inclusion

\begin{array}[]{ll}0\in\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}^{l}+\beta_{i}^{l})\partial^{M}f_{i}(z^{l})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda\alpha_{i}^{l}\upsilon_{i}(z^{l}-\widetilde{x})\\
\qquad\displaystyle+\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\partial^{M}(\|\cdot-z^{l}\|)+\tau\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(z^{l}).\end{array}

In this way, we find sequences
\{u_{i}^{l}\},\,\{\widetilde{u}^{l}\}
, and
\{\widetilde{v}^{l}\}
with

u_{i}^{l}\in\partial^{M}f_{i}(z^{l}),\;\widetilde{u}^{l}\in\sqrt{\gamma_{l}}\partial^{M}(\|\cdot-z^{l}\|)~\mbox{and}~\widetilde{v}^{l}\in\tau\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(z^{l})

satisfying the following equality for all
l\in{\rm I\!N}
sufficiently large:

(5.10)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}^{l}+\beta_{i}^{l})u_{i}^{l}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda\alpha_{i}^{l}\upsilon_{i}(z^{l}-\widetilde{x})+\widetilde{u}^{l}+\widetilde{v}^{l}.

By (
5.3
) and (
5.7
), the sequences
\{\alpha_{i}^{l}\}
,
\{\beta_{i}^{l}\}
as
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
\{z^{l}\}
are bounded. Furthermore, the boundedness of the limiting subgradients of
\|\cdot-z^{l}\|
and
d_{\Omega}(z^{l})
follows from the local Lipschitz continuity of these functions; see
[
16
, Corollary 1.81]
. The boundedness of
\{u_{i}^{l}\}
as
i=1,\ldots,m
is due to the local Lipschitz continuity of
F
around
\bar{x}
combined with the fact that
z^{l}\to\bar{x}
as
l\to\infty
. Passing to subsequences if necessary, we get that the sequences
\{\alpha_{i}^{l}\}
,
\{\beta_{i}^{l}\}
,
\{u_{i}^{l}\}
,
\{\widetilde{u}^{l}\}
, and
\{\widetilde{v}^{l}\}
converge to
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
,
u_{i}
,
\widetilde{u}
, and
\widetilde{v}
, respectively, where
\widetilde{u}=0
by
\gamma_{l}\to 0
as
l\to\infty
. These yield:

•

\alpha_{i},\;\beta_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})=1
by (
5.7
).

•

From (
5.8
), we obtain
(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})(\max\{0,f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}(\widetilde{x})\})=0
and

(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})\big[(f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}({\widetilde{x}))-\max\{0,f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}(\widetilde{x}})\}\big]=0\;\mbox{ for all }\;i=1,\ldots,m.

Therefore,
(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})(f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}(\widetilde{x}))=0
and so
\beta_{i}(f_{i}(\bar{x})-f_{i}(\widetilde{x}))=0
.

•

It follows from (
5.10
) that

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})u_{i}+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\upsilon_{i}(\bar{x}-\widetilde{x})+\widetilde{v}.

The robustness property of Proposition
2
ensures that
u_{i}\in\partial^{M}f_{i}(\bar{x})
and
\widetilde{v}\in\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
, which gives us the inclusion

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})\partial^{M}f_{i}(\bar{x})+\lambda\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\alpha_{i}\upsilon_{i}(\bar{x}-\widetilde{x})+\tau\partial^{M}d_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ with some }\;\tau>0.

To verify (
5.1
), it remains to use the subdifferential calculation (
2.9
) for the distance function
d_{\Omega}
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 1
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
2
that the Lipschitz continuity of the cost mapping
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
around the reference Pareto minimizer plays a significant role. This is due to the characterization
\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})=\{0\}
of the local Lipschitz property, which supports the subgradient boundedness needed for passing to the limit. On the other hand, the
local Lipschitz continuity
can be
avoided
if the
limiting normal cone
(
2.10
) to the epigraphs of
f_{i}
is used instead of the subdifferential (
2.2
) of the components
f_{i}
; cf.
[
18
, Theorem 6.5]
for the case of nondifferentiable programs with inequality constraints. Such a derivation is based on the
extremal principle
of variational analysis; see
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

6.
Concluding Remarks and Future Research

This papers investigates Pareto optimal solutions to a class of regularized multiobjective optimization problems with directionally Lipschitzian data subject to geometric constraints. Necessary optimality conditions are derived for such problems in terms of Mordukhovich’s limiting subdifferential and normal cone provided that the Pareto minimizer in question belongs to the region where the vectorial cost mapping is locally Lipschitzian.

Our future research pursues several issues. First we plan to consider Pareto solutions to fully non-Lipschitzian multiobjective optimization problems and derive for them necessary optimality conditions expressed in terms of limiting normals to epigraphs of the functions involved. Then we intend to study other regularization types, where the proximal term (squared norm) in (
4.2
) is replaced by some nonsmooth regularizators. They include the nonsmooth convex
\ell^{1}
-norm regularizators typical in problems of machine learning and statistics (like the Lasso problem
[
24
]
) and nonsmooth nonconvex
\ell^{0}
-norm as in models of cancer research
[
10
]
.

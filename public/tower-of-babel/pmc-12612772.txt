Introduction

Background

Epidermolysis bullosa (EB) is a group of rare genodermatoses characterised by fragility of the skin and mucosa resulting in painful blisters and erosions after minor trauma. There are four main types: EB simplex (EBS), dystrophic, junctional and Kindler syndrome. The simplex form is characterised by skin blistering due to cleavage within the basal layer of keratinocytes.1 The most severe form of EBS, EBS severe (EBS-sev, OMIM 131760), is characterised by congenital generalised blistering and mucosal involvement and is associated with increased neonatal mortality mainly due to severe infections.1 2 The disease remains usually severe during childhood, with spontaneous progressive improvement but without resolution. Patients frequently also have palmar and plantar involvement, which can significantly affect their mobility and quality of life (QoL). EBS is mostly linked to a dominant mutation in the KRT5 or KRT14 gene encoding for keratin 5 and 14, respectively.2 3 In 2002, the prevalence of inherited EB in the USA was estimated to be approximately 11 per million of inhabitants according to the National Epidermolysis Bullosa Registry in the USA and there were around 2.72 new EB cases per 1 million live births of which approximately 92% are EBS.4 There is currently no curative treatment for patients with EBS. Management of patients is multidisciplinary, mainly symptomatic and includes wound care, control of infection, nutritional support and prevention and treatment of complications.2 3

In a recent study,5 we have shown that patients with EBS-sev had clinical hallmarks of inflammatory skin disease and that their blistering skin was characterised by increased levels of cytokines inducing the differentiation of T cells into Th17 cells. Patients also had high levels of Th17 effector cytokines and low levels of cytokines inhibiting the Th17 differentiation. These data strongly suggest the involvement of the Th17 immune response in the pathogenesis of EBS-sev.

Apremilast is a small molecule that specifically inhibits cyclic AMP phosphodiesterase-4 and Th1/Th17 activation.6 It has been approved for the treatment of psoriasis and Behçet’s disease in adults7 and is under analysis for children with psoriasis older than 6 years old.8 Based on the involvement of the Th17 immune response in EBS-sev, we examined the effect of apremilast in four adult EBS-sev patients with a dramatic and long-lasting decrease in the number of blisters and acceptable digestive tolerance in three of them (and unpublished data).5

Objectives

We aim to perform a multicentre prospective open-labelled clinical study (the ‘EBULO study’ for Otezla in patients >6 years with EBS severe) to assess the efficacy and tolerance of apremilast in patients ≥6 years with EBS-sev, by using a challenge-dechallenge-rechallenge (CDR) design.

Methods

Trial design

EBULO is an open-label, multicentre phase 2 study, over a 20-week period, to evaluate the oral daily intake of apremilast (dosage depending on the age and the weight of patients) based on a CDR design. This medical testing protocol, in which a medicine or drug is administered, withdrawn and then readministered, is one of the standard means used by international health authorities to assess adverse drug reactions.9 10 It is used when statistical testing is inappropriate due to an idiosyncratic reaction by a specific individual, or a lack of sufficient test subjects and unit of analysis is the individual. CDR can also be a tool for establishing the validity and benefits of medication in treating specific conditions. In this case, treatment must give positive challenge reactions (improvement in assessed outcomes following medication administration), negative dechallenge reactions (loss of improvement after medication withdrawal), as well as positive rechallenge reactions (reimprovement on readministration).11 We selected the CDR design because, in the context of rare disease with a limited number of severely affected patients, parallel randomised design for preliminary study is difficult to implement. The absence of a placebo group in this kind of study reduces the number of patients required and likely enhances adherence to protocol as all patients have access to the innovative drug. Additionally, the CDR design was preferred over a randomised observation-phase design due to the observed on/off effect of apremilast on disease symptoms in previously treated patients. For the same reason, the duration of each period is short in order to minimise costs and the number of on-site visits needed for treatment.

Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

Study setting

The study will involve four French reference centres for EBH (University Hospital of Nice, University Hospital of Toulouse, Hospital Necker Enfants Malades and Hospital Saint Louis, AP-HP).

Patient and public involvement

A sample of 10 volunteer patients and caregivers contributed to identifying the most relevant outcomes for assessing treatment effectiveness in their condition and to developing easy-to-use tools for measuring them. Furthermore, this study is funded by the patient association Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa Research Association (DEBRA) UK and supported by DEBRA France. The investigators are committed to providing regular updates on the study’s progress and results to DEBRA. The results will also be made available on the clinical trials website at the end of the study.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion

Eligible patients will be adults and children aged 6 years or older, with a minimum weight of 15 kg. They must have a diagnosis of EBS-sev confirmed by dominant KRT5 and/or KRT14 laboratory identified mutation. Patients must also present with at least four new blisters daily. All included patients will already be receiving optimal care including local dressings for wounds and therapeutic educational sessions.

Pharmacokinetic and safety data are available from this age, thanks to the trials carried out with apremilast for psoriasis in children8 and specific paediatric formulation (syrup) is available.

Objectives and outcomes

The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of apremilast in the treatment of EBS-sev in patients aged 6 years or over. The primary outcome is the average number of new blisters per day during the week before the visit. The number of new blisters will be counted for a week before each site visit (the week before week 0, week 4, week 8, week 12 and week 20) by the patient/parents on a naked patient, over the entire skin surface of the body (including the scalp) before daily blister piercing and dressing care. It will be transcribed in the notebook given to the patient or his parents. Any new blister will have to be counted regardless of its size. A blister will therefore only be counted once, on the day of its appearance. The average number of new blisters per day will then be calculated by the investigator. The average number of new blisters per day will be compared between the beginning and the end of each of the three study periods.

The secondary objectives are the following:

The tolerance of apremilast treatment over the study period will include investigator and patient-reported outcomes. At each visit, the subject will be systematically asked about the occurrence of the predefined AEs that include allergy, digestive AEs, headache, weight loss, fatigue and suicidal behaviour. These AEs will be described until week 24 during a teleconsultation.

Sample size

There are no data available in the literature, to date, allowing to set a reliable hypothesis for a comparative study and then perform any sample size calculation. According to the number of patients seen in the participating centres annually, we plan to include 20 individuals during the 1-year inclusion period in regard to feasibility issues. For a power of 80%, if we consider a baseline mean blister count of 2.7 (±2.3) per day extrapolated from our previous work on the EB (12), we could detect a reduction in the mean blister count per day of 1.5.

Recruitment

Both EBS-sev children aged 6 years or over and adults are usually followed in the four recruiting study sites specialised in EBH patients’ management. The patients’ association DEBRA UK, which funds this study, will be involved via DEBRA France to recruit patients.

Methods: assignment of interventions and blinding

All patients will receive the apremilast treatment during the challenge and rechallenge periods of the study. The duration of these treatment periods was determined based on our experience with previously treated patients. This will be an open-label study, meaning that both the investigators and participants will be aware of the treatment being administered and there will be no placebo intervention.

Methods: data collection, management and analysis

Data collection

Table 1 shows data collection according to inclusion and follow-up visits.

Study staff with their own access rights to the study database will enter/capture data from source documents corresponding to a participant into the protocol-specific electronic case report form (eCRF). All information required by the protocol will be entered in the eCRF, and an explanation will be provided for each missing piece of information. The data must be collected as they are obtained and transcribed into these forms in a clear manner. If a correction is required for an eCRF, the time and date stamps track the person entering or updating eCRF data and create an electronic audit trail.

The participant will be given detailed contact information of their study centre. In case of tolerance issues or difficulty in performing intervention procedures or self-reported outcomes collection, participants will be instructed to reach out to the study centre. Any information needed will be provided, and additional unplanned visits may be organised if deemed necessary.

In case of loss of follow-up or missing data, study personnel will reach out to the participant to try to recover missing information or reschedule missed eventual study visits.

Data management

An e-CRF will be specifically developed by the Data Manager of the Department of Clinical Research and Innovation (DRCI) at the Nice centre using REDCAP software. Data from investigating centres will be recorded in the e-CRF by clinical research associates (CRA) under investigators’ responsibility. The data will be securely stored, with specific access rights granted to members of the study team according to their role in the study. Data collected will be pseudo-anonymised as follows: the identification code will be composed of initials (first letter of the name and first name) accompanied by a coded number stating the order of participant selection without year of birth. Once the final data have been entered, their validity and coherence will be checked by the data manager, and any requests for verification will be issued to the investigators. Throughout the study, any modifications to the database will be recorded, thereby enabling a full audit trail. At the end of the quality control process, the database will be locked and transferred to the biostatistician.

Statistical methods

The statistical analyses will be performed by the DRCI biostatistician. For descriptive analyses, categorical variables will be expressed as counts and proportions (n, %) and quantitative variables as means and SDs. Regarding comparative tests, a p<0.05 will be considered significant. The statistical analyses will be performed using SAS Enterprise Guide V.7.15 software (Copyright (c) 2017 by SAS Institute).

Population analysed

In the main analysis, the study patients will be analysed according to an intention to treat principle. Each patient who will have taken the treatment at least for 1 week (including the period of titration duration) will be part of the analyses.

Analysis of the primary outcome

The mean number of new blisters per day will be compared between the beginning and the end of each period of the study using the t-test for paired data or the Wilcoxon rank signed test if the assumption of normality is not verified. In the very few anticipated cases where the rate per day could not be calculated, we will replace this rate by the mean rate of the group for the considered period of the study.

Analyses of secondary outcomes

For each studied AE, the rate of patients presenting the event will be presented with absolute and relative frequencies. Then we will present the number of events per patient-months, over the predetermined period (challenge and rechallenge periods plus 1 week), for all patients who will have undergone apremilast treatment (at least one dose of the medication).

Monitoring, audit and inspection

A CRA of the DRCI will be responsible for coordinating the study: the assistant will be responsible for the logistics and monitoring the study, producing reports concerning its state of progress, verifying that the e-CRFs are updated (request for additional information, corrections, etc), conformance with the source documents, respect of good clinical practice and transmitting information on severe AEs to the sponsor. The investigator undertakes that the patient medical records, data collection records and consent forms of all included patients will be available to the CRA at all monitoring visits. The CRA is equally bound to respect professional confidentiality. An inspection refers to an official inspection organised by the Agence Nationale de Securite du Medicament (ANSM), aimed at assessing the quality of clinical data, the respect for the relevant legislation and the prevention of fraud. The inspectors will examine documents, logistical methods, recording methods and any other resource related to the study that the authority deems necessary, either at the investigator’s site, the contract research organisation or any other pertinent establishment.

Harms

All serious and non-serious events will be reported on the AE reporting form (initial or follow-up declaration), as thoroughly as possible, within the regulatory time limits for reporting. All AEs will be monitored until they are completely resolved. The investigator will immediately notify the sponsor of any serious AE. Suspected Serious and Unexpected Adverse Reactions (SUSARs) will be reported to Eudravigilance (European pharmacovigilance database), the French Health Authorities (ANSM) and the investigators.

Confidentiality

During this biomedical research study or when it is over, the information collected on the people taking part in it and forwarded to the sponsor by the investigators (or any other specialised staff member involved) will be made anonymous. The sponsor is responsible for obtaining agreement from all the parties involved in the study in order to guarantee direct access, in all the sites where the study is being conducted, to source data, source documents and reports, to control their quality and to audit them. The investigators will make available to people with a right of access to these documents, according to the legislative and regulatory provisions in force (articles L.1121-3 and R.5121–13 of the French Public Health Act), the documents and individual data strictly necessary for monitoring, carrying out quality control and auditing the biomedical research.

Dissemination policy

Discussion

There are currently no curative therapeutic options for EBS. Management of patients is multidisciplinary and mainly symptomatic with daily dressing of wounds and skin protection, pain and itch treatment, psychological and nutritional support, oral care etc. There is therefore a need to develop new therapeutic approaches to improve patient conditions and an unmet need for an easily carriable medical treatment with few side effects. EBS-sev was initially considered a mechanical disease. For several years, involvement of additional inflammatory mechanisms has been demonstrated by various teams.13,18 This finding is supported by growing evidence for distinct regulatory functions of keratins in skin immunity and inflammation19,22 and encouraging results of topical and systemic anti-inflammatory treatments.23 However, despite the considerable advances in research, the large panel of available biologics and efforts of various teams and industrials, only a few clinical trials are published and most of them are disappointing with no statistically validated efficacy.24,28 This can be explained by two limiting factors often reported by authors: the difficulty to recruit enough patients to reach a sufficiently powerful statistical analysis and the lack of adapted EBS-specific severity score to assess the efficacy of the tested treatment. In fact, if EBS is the more frequent EB subtype, the severe form is rare. Furthermore, the disease is usually more severe during childhood, with an improvement at adult age. That means that the target population is almost paediatric, with their specific issues: more protected population in terms of clinical research, necessity to obtain the authorisation of both parents, lack of pharmacological data especially for very young children. Indeed, despite the help of patients’ associations and rare disease networks, national studies are usually not able to recruit enough patients.

Extending the study internationally in order to increase the participant number involves a substantial budget and increases administrative burden/issues. For this reason, we choose a CDR design instead of a classical randomised, double blind, controlled against placebo study design. In the CDR study, the positive effect of the treatment is compared for each patient between different periods with and without treatment intake. The absence of a placebo arm decreases the necessary number of patients to include and increases the acceptability of the study for patients/caregivers. As this design is usually used in the pharmacovigilance field, it is a perfect tool to evaluate the safety of the studied treatment. It is an innovative and promising tool for evaluating treatments for rare diseases.

The other major obstacle for EB clinical trials is the lack of uniform consensus on which aspects of EB are most relevant and clinically meaningful for assessment. This point is very important and peculiar in the case of the EBS-sev subtype. Indeed, for these patients, the classical EBH severity scores (BEBS, EBDASI and iscorEB) are not very representative, due to the absence or the low systemic involvement and the absence of chronic wounds. Furthermore, while skin lesions vary from one day to another, due to infection, trauma and/or a change in temperature, for example, these scores assess only the severity at a precise day. Aware of this problem, the Core Outcome Sets for Epidermolysis Bullosa initiative tries to establish core outcome sets for the four major EB types by identifying the most critical outcome domains (‘what’ to measure) and corresponding outcome measurement instruments (‘how’ to measure). This work will help us in the future, but we actually need other outcome domains/measurements for EBS patients. In a preliminary study, according to the patients, the main criteria of severity of their pathologies were the number of blisters. The feasibility of such an evaluation tool was validated in a previous study conducted at our clinical site. Therefore, the average number of existing and new blisters during the study period improved transverse monitoring of EBS severity and objective evaluation of the primary endpoint. Finally, to improve the assessment of EBS-sev severity patients we proposed two new specific scales, one for clinicians and one patient-oriented. They were easy and quick to fill (less than 5 min) in a non-published monocentric preliminary study on 10 patients. The EBULO (Treatment of Epidermolysis BULlosa Simplex by Otezla (Apremilast)) study has been designed with a protocol specifically adapted to rare diseases aiming to evaluate the repositioning of Apremilast in severe EBS. By assessing both efficacy and safety, the trial will provide high-quality evidence to inform clinical management in children and adults, supporting future improvements in first-line care for this rare condition.

Trial status

The protocol is currently recruiting in four French centres. The first patient inclusion is planned in September 2024 and recruitment is anticipated to end in September 2025.

Analytical construction of needle-type solitons in a M-fractional paraxial wave framework with dynamical analysis
Authors: Umair Asghar, Muhammad Imran Asjad, Muhammad Bilal Riaz, Magda Abd El Rehman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract In this paper, to investigate exact optical soliton solutions and their dynamical properties for the paraxial wave model involving the M-fractional derivative. To achieve this, employ the improved modified Sardar sub-equation method, which provides a systematic and efficient analytical framework for constructing closed-form solutions of nonlinear fractional wave equations. The resulting fractional complex paraxial wave dynamical (FPWD) model is of considerable importance due to its wide applicability in nonlinear optics, optical fiber communications, quantum electronics, and plasma physics. The mathematical analysis reveals the existence of new solitary wave solutions that demonstrate complex nonlinear patterns that govern the system. The research discovers a needle-type soliton structure that operates within an M-fractional paraxial wave framework, thus differentiating itself from existing research, which uses classical and other fractional operators. The research displays three-dimensional surface plots and contour maps to enhance understanding of the obtained wave structures. The study assesses how essential parameters affect solution stabilityAQ through a sensitivity analysis, which shows solitons maintain their strength and operational behavior across various physical conditions.

The study of nonlinear wave propagation in paraxial wave models with M-fractional derivatives has attracted considerable attention due to its ability to capture memory effects and nonlocal dynamics in complex physical systems. However, much of the existing literature has focused on relatively limited classes of wave structures obtained through conventional analytical techniques, with comparatively less emphasis on the systematic construction of diverse wave profiles within a unified framework. In addition, several studies primarily address solution derivation without providing a detailed analysis of the associated dynamical behavior or sensitivity characteristics of the model. In this context, the improved modified Sardar sub-equation method (IMSSEM), combined with the M-truncated fractional derivative, provides a structured analytical approach for obtaining closed-form solutions of nonlinear fractional partial differential equations under specific parametric conditions. The method enables the construction of a unified family of analytical solutions, from which different wave profiles emerge depending on parameter selection and auxiliary function forms. These profiles include sharp-peak, corner-like, smooth-kink, periodic (lattice-type), singular (shock-type), and needle-type structures, as summarized in Table 1. It should be emphasized that these wave patterns arise from a single analytical solution family rather than independent solutions. Compared with classical approaches such as the Hirota bilinear method and Lie symmetry analysis, which are primarily suited to integrable or integer-order systems, as mentioned in (Table 1), the IMSSEM approach offers a flexible alternative for fractional-order models within its applicable framework. Furthermore, the present study incorporates a dynamical and sensitivity analysis to examine how variations in system parameters influence the qualitative behavior of the obtained solutions. It is important to note that the applicability and physical interpretation of the results are subject to the assumptions and constraints of the M-fractional framework. The obtained solutions are valid within specific parameter regimes, and certain profiles exhibit singular behavior that does not correspond to classical soliton definitions. Despite these limitations, the present work contributes to extending the range of analytical wave structures and provides insight into the qualitative dynamics of nonlinear fractional systems.

Conclusion

In this work, the improved modified Sardar sub-equation method (IMSSEM) has been applied to construct analytical solutions of the M-fractional complex paraxial wave dynamical model. By employing an appropriate transformation, the governing nonlinear fractional partial differential equation is reduced to an ordinary differential equation, allowing the derivation of closed-form solutions under specific parametric conditions. The obtained results represent a unified family of analytical solutions, from which a variety of wave profiles emerge depending on parameter selection and auxiliary function forms. These profiles include sharp-peak, corner-like, smooth-kink, periodic (lattice-type), singular (shock-type), and needle-type structures. The graphical representations illustrate the qualitative behavior and localization properties of these solutions, while the sensitivity analysis provides insight into how variations in system parameters influence the dynamical response. It is important to note that not all obtained profiles correspond to classical solitons in the strict sense; in particular, singular and periodic structures represent broader classes of nonlinear wave solutions. Furthermore, the validity of the results is restricted to the chosen parameter regimes and the assumptions of the M-fractional framework. Despite these limitations, the present study contributes to the analytical understanding of nonlinear wave behavior in fractional-order systems by extending the range of obtainable solution profiles and highlighting their qualitative dynamics. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis is confined to a reduced-dimensional deterministic model and does not account for higher-dimensional effects, transverse instabilities, stochastic perturbations, or experimental noise. Future work may focus on extending the present framework to higher-dimensional fractional models, incorporating perturbative and stochastic effects, and validating the obtained solutions through numerical and experimental approaches.

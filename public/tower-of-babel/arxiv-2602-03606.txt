Bekenstein’s bound for wave packets
Stefan Hollands

Abstract
Let B B be a spatial region of width 2 ​ R 2R and Φ \Phi a Klein-Gordon wave packet localized in B B at time zero. We show the inequality S ≤ 2 ​ π ​ R ​ E S\leq 2\pi RE ; here, S S is the entropy of Φ \Phi contained in a region B B , and E E is the energy content of Φ \Phi within B B . We consider a wider setting and formulate a variational problem aimed at minimizing our bound when Φ \Phi is not localized in B B . Our inequality holds in more generality in the framework of local, Poincaré covariant nets of standard subspaces and is related to the Bekenstein inequality. We point out a general bound that is compatible with the recent numerical computations by Bostelmann, Cadamuro, and Minz concerning the one-particle modular Hamiltonian of a scalar massive quantum Klein-Gordon field. We also provide a version of the entropy balance and ant formulas for wave packets.



1
Introduction

This note concerns the linear space of Klein-Gordon wave packets and, more generally, local nets of standard subspaces of a Hilbert space. It has been written with two motivations.

Our first aim is to provide general bounds for the massive modular Hamiltonian associated with a spatial ball in the free, scalar Quantum Field Theory. The explicit description for the modular Hamiltonian of a ball is known only in the massless case
[
23
]
; in this case, the modular group is associated with a geometrical action by spacetime conformal transformations
[
11
]
. The massive case does not correspond to any geometric action; one might hope to find out a perturbation formula from the massless case, but any attempt to this end has so far failed, see
[
23
]
.
Based on these premises, Bostelmann, Cadamuro, and Minz have recently undertaken an interesting, detailed numerical analysis
[
2
]
. In the two-by-two matrix description of Cauchy data, the generator of the modular group of the unit ball is off-diagonal with two terms:
L
and
M
(up to a constant), see formula (
20
) below;
M
and
L
contain the same information as
M^{*}=-L
in the underlying inner product; in particular, the
M
term has
been plotted in
[
2
]
as the mass
m
varies.
In the massless case,
M
is (the multiplication operator by) a parabolic function,
M=\frac{1}{2}(1-x^{2})
, thus
M(\pm 1)=0
and
M(0)=1/2
; as the mass increases, the plot of
M
resembles a higher parabola shape; still
M(\pm 1)=0
but
M(0)
increases to 1, cf.
[
2
, Figures 7 & 8]
.
We shall indeed see in Corollary
3.4
that the mass independent bound
M\leq 1
holds as a consequence of the general bound derived in
[
20
]
.

Our second motivation comes from the local entropy of a wave packet that was defined in
[
18
,
7
]
, see also
[
8
,
19
]
.
The local entropy measures the information content of a wave packet in a given region. Now, the Bekenstein bound in Quantum Field Theory
[
5
]
suggests that a local entropy/energy bound is to be satisfied.
The recent Bekenstein-type inequality in Quantum Field Theory
[
20
,
12
]
will show that a local entropy/energy bound for a wave packet or, more generally, for a local net of standard subspaces of a Hilbert space is satisfied, where the local energy is defined more precisely below in terms of the stress energy tensor of the wave. This bound is presented in this paper and was actually the starting point for the derivation of QFT Bekenstein-type bound
[
20
]
.

Let
B
be a spatial region of width
2R
and
\Phi
a Klein-Gordon wave packet, that is,
\Phi
is a smooth solution of the Klein-Gordon equation
(\square+m^{2})\Phi=0
. If the Cauchy data
(f,g)
of

S(\Phi|B)\leq 2\pi R\int_{B}{T_{00}}_{\Phi}(x)|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}\,,

with

the entropy of

in

In order to derive a bound also in this situation, we formulate a variational problem that has its own analytical interest; its explicit solution goes beyond the frame of this paper. Here, we just note in Section
4
some properties and solutions in particlar cases. We also briefly discuss the extension of such bounds to the fermionic case and obtain a version of the above inequality for one particle states of the Majorana field localized in
B
.

In Section

we provide a version the entropy balance formula
[

,
6
]
and ant formula
[
6
,
13
]
for wave packets: the first one follows at once directly in our context; the second one cannot be inferred from the ant formula for free QFT nets of von Neumann algebras. Indeed, in that context, the version proven here would involve an infimum taken only over coherent states, hence it is a stronger statement.

Preliminaries

2.1
The entropy operator

We recall the setup of standard subspaces. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a complex Hilbert space and
H\subset{\mathcal{H}}
a
standard subspace
, namely
H
is a closed, real linear subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
H\cap iH=\{0\}
and
\overline{H+iH}={\mathcal{H}}
. The
modular operator

\Delta_{H}
is a positive, non-singular operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and the
modular conjugation

J_{H}
is a anti-unitary involution on
{\mathcal{H}}
defined by the polar decomposition
S_{H}=J_{H}\Delta_{H}^{1/2}
of the anti-linear, densely defined involution
S_{H}:H+iH\to H+iH
,
S(\Phi+i\Psi)=\Phi-i\Psi
; they satisfy the fundamental relations

\Delta_{H}^{is}H=H\,,\ s\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,\quad J_{H}H=H^{\prime}\,,

where
H^{\prime}:=\{\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}:\Im(\Phi,\Psi)=0,\Psi\in H\}
is the
symplectic complement
of
H
. We refer to
[
16
]
for details.

The
entropy operator

{\cal E}_{H}
associated with
H
is a real linear, positive operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
{\cal E}_{H}
associated with any closed, real linear subspace
H
of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Here, we recall the definition of
{\cal E}_{H}
with
H
a standard subspace which is
factorial
, namely
H\cap H^{\prime}=\{0\}
(which is the case of interest in the following):

{\cal E}_{H}=:iP_{H}i\log\Delta_{H}\,,

more precisely,
{\cal E}_{H}
is the closure of the operator in the right-hand side of the above formula. Here,
P_{H}
is the
cutting projection
, namely the real linear operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
with domain
H+H^{\prime}
defined by
P_{H}(\Phi+\Phi^{\prime})=\Phi
, with
\Phi\in H
,
\Phi^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
. Note that
P_{H}
is densely defined as
H
is standard and
well-defined as
H
is factorial.

The entropy operator is positive, selfadjoint (as real operator on the real Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with scalar product
\Re(\cdot,\cdot)
) and
{\cal E}_{H}\leq{\cal E}_{K}
if
H\subset K
.
The
entropy of a vector

\Phi
with respect to
H
is then defined by

S(\Phi|H):=\Re(\Phi,{\cal E}_{H}\Phi)

(1)

in the quadratic form sense, thus
S(\Phi|H)=\|{\cal E}^{1/2}_{H}\Phi\|^{2}
if
\Phi\in D({\cal E}^{1/2}_{H})
, and
S(\Phi|H)=+\infty
otherwise.

S(\Phi|H)
satisfies natural properties like

:
S(\Phi|H)\geq 0
, and
monotonicity
:
S(\Phi|H)\leq S(\Phi|K)
if
H\subset K
.
See
[
18
,
7
,
19
]
as references for this setup.

We note that, if
H
is any standard subspace of a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, the entropy operators with respect to
H
and
H^{\prime}
are related by

{\cal E}_{H}-\bar{\cal E}_{H}=-\log\Delta_{H}

(2)

(on the intersections of the domains), with
\bar{\cal E}_{H}={\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
, as
{\cal E}_{H^{\prime}}
is the closure of
iP_{H^{\prime}}i\log\Delta_{H^{\prime}}=-i(1-P_{H})i\log\Delta_{H}
.

2.2
Bekenstein inequality for nets of standard subspaces

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a complex Hilbert space. A Poincaré covariant
net of standard subspaces
(see
[
16
]
) of
{\mathcal{H}}
is a map
H:O\mapsto H(O)
that associates a standard subspace
H(O)
of
{\mathcal{H}}
to each double cone

,

, such that

\bullet

H(O_{1})\subset H(O_{2})
if
O_{1}\subset O_{2}
(isotony),

\bullet
there exists a unitary, positive energy representation
U
of the proper,
1+d

{\cal P}_{+}^{\uparrow}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
U(g)H(O)=H(gO)
, for all
g\in{\cal P}_{+}^{\uparrow}
,
O
double cone (Poincaré covariance),

\bullet

H(O_{1})\subset H(O_{2})^{\prime}
if
O_{1}\subset O^{\prime}_{2}
(locality),

\bullet

\Delta^{-is}_{H(W)}=U\big(\Lambda_{W}(2\pi s)\big)
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, if
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}
is a wedge (Bisognano-Wichmann property);

here
H(W)
is the closed real linear span of the
H(O)
with
O\subset W
and
\Lambda_{W}
is the one-parameter group pure Lorentz transformations preserving
W
(boosts). The Bisognano-Wichmann property holds automatically if
U
is irreducible
[
24
]
.

Recall that every unitary, positive energy representation
U
of
{\cal P}_{+}^{\uparrow}
as above gives rise to a net standard subspaces as above, provided
U
has finite spin
[
1
]
. If
U
has infinite spin, then
H(O)={\mathbb{C}}
if
O
is a double cone, but
H(W)
is defined if
W
is a wedge
[
22
]
.

If
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a spatial (time zero) region, we set
H(B)=H(\hat{B})
, where
\hat{B}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}
is the causal envelope of
B
.
We shall say that
B
has half-
width

R\geq 0
if
R
is the smallest number such that
B
lies between two parallel hyperplanes with distance
2R
. In this case, by applying a rigid motion,
B
can be mapped onto a subset of the strip
\{-R\leq x_{1}\leq R\}
. Clearly, a ball of radius
R
has a half-width equal to

.

The next proposition provides the version of the Bekenstein-type inequality for local, Poincaré covariant, net of standard subspaces
[
20
]
(more generally, it holds for any local net of real Hilbert spaces with energy-momentum spectrum condition

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\Phi\in H(B)
, then

S(\Phi|B)\leq 2\pi R\,(\Phi,P\Phi)\,,

(3)

with
P
the Hamiltonian (the generator of the time-translation one-parameter unitary group) and
R
is the half-width of
B
.

The proposition is a corollary of the inequality
[
20
, Cor. 2.11]
(following from
[
3
]
):

-\log\Delta_{H(B)}\leq 2\pi R\,P\,,

(4)

and the fact that

S(\Phi|B)=-(\Phi,\log\Delta_{H(B)}\Phi)\,.

if
\Phi\in H(B)
.

\square

Note that both sides of equations (
3
), (
4
), and the operator order are defined in the quadratic form sense, see
[
20
]
.

Bekenstein’s inequality and Klein-Gordon waves

With
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{m}
,

of a wave packet

S(\Phi|B)=(\Phi,{\cal E}_{H}\Phi)=(\Phi,iP_{H}i\log\Delta_{H}\Phi)\,,

for any region

Recall that

S(\Phi|x_{1}>a)=2\pi\int_{x_{1}>a}(x_{1}-a){T_{00}}_{\Phi}(x)|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}

{T_{00}}_{\Phi}(x)|_{x_{0}=0}=\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})\,.

One can check that

(\Phi,P\Phi)=\int{T_{00}}_{\Phi}(0,{\bf x})d{\bf x}\,,

therefore, the following concrete Bekenstein inequality follows as a corollary of Proposition
2.1
. We give a short, direct proof of it for future needs.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
{\rm supp}(f),{\rm supp}(g)\subset\bar{B}
, then

S(\Phi|B)\leq 2\pi R\,E(\Phi|B)\,,

(11)

with
E(\Phi|B)=\int_{B}{T_{00}}_{\Phi}(0,{\bf x})d{\bf x}
and

R
the half-width of
B
.

Since the special Euclidean group acts covariantly and unitarily on
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
, by the isometric covariance of the local entropy
we may assume that
B\subset\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:-R\leq x_{1}\leq R\}
.

By the monotonicity of the entropy (which follows from
[
7
, Thm. 4.5]
and the monotonicity of relative entropy between coherent states), we have

\displaystyle S(\Phi|B)\leq S(\Phi|\{x_{1}>-R\})

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{x_{1}>-R}(x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{B}(x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}\,;

(12)

similarly,

\displaystyle S(\Phi|B)\leq S(\Phi|\{x_{1}<R\})

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{x_{1}<R}(-x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{B}(-x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}\,.

(13)

By taking the mean of (
12
) and (
13

\displaystyle S(\Phi|B)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{B}(x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})+\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{B}(-x_{1}+R)(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}

(14)

\displaystyle=\pi R\int_{B}(|\nabla f|^{2}+m^{2}f^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}

(15)

\displaystyle=2\pi R\int_{B}{T_{00}}_{\Phi}(x)|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}=2\pi R\,E(\Phi|B)\,.

(16)

\square

4
Which bound if
\Phi
is not localized?

Let us specify the inequality (
11
) in the case
m=0
and
B
the unit ball. In this case, the entropy of
\Phi=\langle f,g\rangle
is given by

S(\Phi|B)=\pi\int_{B}(1-r^{2}){T_{00}}_{\Phi}(x)\big|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}+\pi D\int_{B}f^{2}d{\bf x}\,,

with
{T_{00}}_{\Phi}=|\nabla f|^{2}+g^{2}
and
D=(d-1)/2
. Therefore, the inequality (
11
) is equivalent to

\pi\int_{B}\frac{1-r^{2}}{2}(|\nabla f|^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}+\pi D\int_{B}f^{2}dx\leq\pi\int_{B}(|\nabla f|^{2}+g^{2})d{\bf x}\,;

in particular, setting

, we get the inequality

\int_{B}(1+r^{2})|\nabla f|^{2}d{\bf x}\geq(d-1)\int_{B}f^{2}d{\bf x}\,.

(23)

Therefore, the inequality (
11
) does not generally hold if the support of
f
is not contained in
\bar{B}
, as can be seen if
f=1
on
\bar{B}
.

Moreover, we could consider the “improved” traceless stress-energy tensor associated to the wave
\Phi

T_{\mu\nu\Phi}^{i}:=T_{\mu\nu\Phi}-\frac{d-1}{4d}(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box)(\Phi^{2})

(
\eta_{\mu\nu}
the Minkowski metric), which is also conserved and whose second quantization version also generates the translations. We can then compute, by double
integration by parts,

\int_{B}(1-r^{2})\nabla^{2}(\Phi^{2})|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}=\int_{B}(1-r^{2})\nabla^{2}(f^{2})d{\bf x}=2\int_{\partial B}f^{2}\,d\sigma-2d\int_{B}f^{2}d{\bf x},

so that the entropy of
\Phi
in
B
can also be expressed in terms of the improved energy density by

S(\Phi|B)=\pi\int_{B}(1-r^{2})T_{00\Phi}^{i}(x)|_{x_{0}=0}d{\bf x}+\pi\frac{D}{d}\int_{\partial B}f^{2}\,d\sigma,

suggesting that the correction to be added to (

\Phi
is not localized in
B
should only depend on the restriction of

to the boundary of
B
.

It is also interesting to observe that for

4.2
Some remarks on the fermionic case

A natural question is if the above analysis can be extended to the fermionic case. We discuss below the main obstructions to this end.

We start by fixing the notations we will use in this subsection. For simplicity, we restrict to
3+1
dimensions, and, as usual,
g=[g_{\mu\nu}]_{\mu,\nu=0,\dots,3}
will denote Minkowski metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, whose elements
x=(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})=(x^{0},{\bf x})
will have contravariant (upper) indices. Indices are lowered and raised by
g
and
g^{-1}=[g^{\mu\nu}]
respectively, and we employ the summation convention over repeated upper and lower indices, so that
x\cdot y=x_{\mu}y^{\mu}=x_{0}y_{0}-{\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}
is the Minkowski product. Given
{\bf p}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
m\geq 0
, we set
\mu_{m}({\bf p}):=\sqrt{|{\bf p}|^{2}+m^{2}}
as in Section
2.3

and use the shorthand
p_{+}:=(\mu_{m}({\bf p}),{\bf p})
for the 4-vector on the positive mass
m
shell corresponding to
{\bf p}
. The gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
satisfy

\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu},

with
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
the anticommutator, and we choose a representation in which the transpose of
\gamma^{0}
satisfies
(\gamma^{0})^{t}=\pm\gamma^{0}
. Moreover, we use the Dirac notation
\not{x}:=x_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. The charge conjugation matrix
C
is such that
C^{t}=C^{*}=-C=C^{-1}
and
C\gamma^{\mu}C^{-1}=-(\gamma^{\mu})^{t}
. Finally, 3- and 4-dimensional Fourier transforms are defined respectively by

\hat{g}({\bf p}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}g({\bf x})e^{-i{\bf p}\cdot{\bf x}}d{\bf x},\qquad\hat{f}(p):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}f(x)e^{ip\cdot x}dx.

Now, given a mass
m\geq 0
, we consider the real linear space
\mathcal{M}\subset C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{4},{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
of solutions
\Psi
of the Dirac equation

(i\partial_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}-m)\Psi=0

(27)

with Cauchy data
\Psi_{0}:=\Psi|_{x_{0}=0}\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
and satisfying the Majorana condition
C(\gamma^{0})^{t}\bar{\Psi}=\Psi
(Majorana waves).
{\cal M}
becomes a complex space when endowed by the complex structure
\imath:{\cal M}\to{\cal M}
defined by

(\imath\Psi)_{0}\hat{\,}({\bf p}):=\frac{i}{\mu_{m}({\bf p})}(m-p_{j}\gamma^{j})\gamma^{0}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf p}),\qquad{\bf p}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

The universal covering of the Poincaré group naturally acts on
\mathcal{M}
, and defining the positive frequency part of
\Psi\in{\cal M}
as
\hat{\Psi}_{+}(p):=\theta(p_{0})\hat{\Psi}(p)
,
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, the bilinear form

(\Psi,\Phi):=\int_{x^{0}=0}d{\bf x}\,\Psi_{+}(x)^{*}\Phi_{+}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{d{\bf p}}{2\mu_{m}({\bf p})}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf p})^{*}(\not{p}_{+}+m)\gamma^{0}\hat{\Phi}_{0}({\bf p}),

is actually sesquilinear with respect to the complex structure
\imath
, and defines a Poincaré invariant scalar product on
{\cal M}
. We denote by
{\cal K}_{m}
the complex Hilbert space obtained by completing
{\cal M}
with respect to this scalar product. For a ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the closed real subspace of
{\cal K}_{m}

K_{m}(B):=\{\Psi\in{\cal M}\,:\,{\text{supp}\,}\Psi_{0}\subset B\}^{-}

is standard.

The Majorana field is the (spinor valued) Wightman field
\psi
on the fermionic Fock space
\Gamma_{-}({\cal K}_{m})
defined as follows: for
f\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{4},{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
such that
C(\gamma^{0})^{t}\bar{f}=f
define
\Psi_{f}\in{\cal M}
by

\Psi_{f}(x)=i\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}dy\,(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)D(y-x)\gamma^{0}\overline{f(y)},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4},

with
D
the causal propagator of the mass
m
Klein-Gordon field;
then the Majorana field smeared with
f
is

\psi(f):=a(\Psi_{f})+a(\Psi_{f})^{*},

with
a(\Psi)
,
\Psi\in{\cal K}_{m}
, the generators of the CAR algebra on
\Gamma_{-}({\cal K}_{m})
; for arbitrary
f\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{4},{\mathbb{C}}^{4})
, the smeared field
\psi(f)
is obtained by complex linearity. The local von Neumann algebra of the Majorana field associated to a double cone
O
with basis a ball
B
in the
x^{0}=0
plane is then

{\cal F}(O):=\{\psi(f)+\psi(f)^{*}\,:\,{\text{supp}\,}f\subset O\}^{\prime\prime}=\{a(\Psi)+a(\Psi)^{*}\,:\,\Psi\in K_{m}(B)\}^{\prime\prime},

the fermionic second quantization of
K_{m}(B)
. Algebras for more general double cones are obtained by Poincaré covariance, and for more general regions by additivity.

The first problem that one has to face in trying to obtain some kind of Bekenstein bound for Majorana waves is the absence of a meaningul notion of entropy for them. In particular, it is not clear if there is a suitably large class of states of the Majorana field whose relative entropy with respect to the vacuum can be expressed directly in terms of Majorana waves, as in the case of coherent states of the Klein-Gordon field (note that the fermionic coherent states usually employed in the physics literature involve auxiliary “Grassmann variables”, and are therefore not represented by vectors in
\Gamma_{-}({\cal K}_{m})
).

One can then ask if a Bekenstein bound can be obtained for the relative entropy of some class of states at the second quantization level. To this end, it is natural to consider first the class of one particle states. Indeed, due to the CARs, the square of
\psi(f)+\psi(f)^{*}
is a multiple of the identity, and then states of the form
e^{i(\psi(f)+\psi(f)^{*})}\Omega
are actually linear combinations of the vacuum and a one-particle state.

Thanks to the Bisognano-Wichmann theorem, it is then not difficult to show, similarly to
[
15
]
(where the double cone,
m=0
case is considered), that the relative entropy between a one particle state
\omega_{\Psi}=(\Psi,(\cdot)\Psi)
,
\Psi\in{\cal M}
with
{\text{supp}\,}\Psi_{0}\subset\{x^{1}>a\}
,
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and the Fock vacuum
\omega
on the algebra
{\cal F}(W_{a})
associated to the wedge
W_{a}:=\{x^{1}>a\}^{\prime\prime}
is given by

S(\omega_{\Psi}|\!|\omega)_{{\cal F}(W_{a})}=\pi\int_{x^{0}=0}d{\bf x}\,(x^{1}-a)(\Psi,T_{00}(x)\Psi),

(28)

with

T_{00}(x)=\frac{i}{2}[:\psi^{*}(x)\partial_{0}\psi(x):-:\partial_{0}\psi^{*}(x)\psi(x):],\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4},

the quadratic form expressing the quantized energy density of the Majorana field. The above expression for
S(\omega_{\Psi}|\!|\omega)_{{\cal F}(W_{a})}
is of course similar to (
10
). One obtains then the following analogue of Prop.
3.1
.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
\Psi\in{\cal M}
is such that
{\text{supp}\,}\Psi_{0}\subset\bar{B}
,
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
a ball of radius
R>0
, and
O
is a double cone with basis
B
, then

S(\omega_{\Psi}|\!\ \omega)_{{\cal F}(O)}\leq 2\pi RE(\Psi|B)

with
E(\Psi|B):=\int_{B}(\Psi,T_{00}(0,{\bf x})\Psi)d{\bf x}
.

Arguing as in the proof of Prop.
3.1
, it is sufficient to show that the integrand in (
28
) vanishes outside of
B
. Indeed, one finds

\begin{split}(\Psi&,T_{00}(0,{\bf x})\Psi)\\
&=\frac{1}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}d{\bf p}d{\bf q}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{m}({\bf p})}+\frac{1}{\mu_{m}({\bf q})}\right)\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf p})^{*}(\not{p}_{+}+m)\gamma^{0}(\not{q}_{+}+m)\gamma^{0}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf q})e^{i({\bf q}-{\bf p}){\bf x}}\\
&=\frac{1}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}d{\bf p}d{\bf q}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf p})^{*}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{m}({\bf p})}+\frac{1}{\mu_{m}({\bf q})}\right)p_{j}q_{k}\gamma^{j}\gamma^{k}+(q_{k}+p_{k})\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\right]\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf q})e^{i({\bf q}-{\bf p}){\bf x}}\\
&-\frac{1}{4(2\pi)^{6}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}d{\bf p}d{\bf q}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf p})^{*}\left[\frac{p_{k}}{\mu_{m}({\bf p})}\mu_{m}({\bf q})+\mu_{m}({\bf p})\frac{q_{k}}{\mu_{m}({\bf q})}\right]\gamma^{k}\gamma^{0}\hat{\Psi}_{0}({\bf q})e^{i({\bf q}-{\bf p}){\bf x}},\end{split}

where the second equality follows from the fact that the first integral is manifestly real. Now, while the first integral in the right hand side can clearly be expressed as a sum of terms of the form

(G*P_{1}\Psi_{0})({\bf x})^{*}P_{2}\Psi_{0}({\bf x})\quad\text{or}\quad P_{1}^{\prime}\Psi_{0}({\bf x})^{*}(G^{\prime}*P_{2}^{\prime}\Psi_{0})({\bf x})

with
G,G^{\prime}
tempered distributions and
P_{j},P_{j}^{\prime}
,

\Psi_{0}
, the second integral equals

2\Re\left[-i(\mu_{m}\Psi_{0})({\bf x})^{*}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\partial_{k}(\mu_{m}^{-1}\Psi_{0})({\bf x})\right]

which vanishes thanks to the Majorana condition.

\square

It would then be interesting to try to also extend to the fermionic case the bound of Prop.
4.3
for non localized one particle states. However, while of course (

) is expected to hold also in this case, its proof crucially depends on the fact that

of

and one with Cauchy data supported in the complement, the problem of computing the relative modular operator becomes much more difficult.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}

Proposition 5.2

Appendix B
Finite entropy vectors for the U(1) current

In this appendix, we compute the vectors of the one particle space of the U(1) current on the real line whose entropy with respect to the standard subspace of a bounded interval or of a half-line is finite.

In order to fix notations, we start by reviewing the construction of the net of standard subspaces of the U(1) current. Our convention for the Fourier transform and anti-transform on
{\mathbb{R}}
is

\hat{\varphi}(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)e^{ipx}dx,\qquad\check{\varphi}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(p)e^{-ipx}dp.

Moreover all function spaces considered will consist of real valued functions.

We denote by
S_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\hat{S}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
the subspaces of
S({\mathbb{R}})
of the (real) functions
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\,dx=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi(0)=0,

respectively, with the induced locally convex topology. Of course
\varphi\in S_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
if and only if
\hat{\varphi}\in\hat{S}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Therefore, given
f\in S^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, its Fourier transform is the
\hat{f}\in\hat{S}^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and its derivative is the
f^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
defined by duality:

\langle\hat{f},\varphi\rangle=\langle f,\hat{\varphi}\rangle,\quad\varphi\in\hat{S}_{1}({\mathbb{R}});\qquad\langle f^{\prime},\varphi\rangle=-\langle f,\varphi^{\prime}\rangle,\quad\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}).

We consider then the real linear space
{\mathfrak{L}}
of distributions
f\in S_{1}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
whose Fourier transform
\hat{f}
is a measurable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\|\hat{f}\|^{2}:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}p|\hat{f}(p)|^{2}dp<+\infty.

Proposition B.1
.

The space
{\mathfrak{L}}
equipped with the real scalar product

\langle f,g\rangle:=\Re\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}p\hat{f}(-p)\hat{g}(p)\,dp\right],\qquad f,g\in{\mathfrak{L}},

is a real Hilbert space.

If
\{f_{n}\}\subset{\mathfrak{L}}
is a Cauchy sequence,
\{\hat{f}_{n}\}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},pdp)
is also Cauchy (with respect to the
L^{2}
norm). Let
\hat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},pdp)
be its limit. Now if
\varphi\in S_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
then

\langle\hat{f},\check{\varphi}\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(p)\check{\varphi}(p)dp=\frac{1}{2i}\Im\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}p\overline{\hat{f}(p)}\frac{\check{\varphi}(-p)}{p}\right]

is well defined, since

\check{\varphi}\in\hat{S}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies
\check{\varphi}(p)\sim p
for
p\to 0
, and therefore
p\mapsto\check{\varphi}(-p)/p
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},pdp)
. As a consequence
\hat{f}\in\hat{S}_{1}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
f\in S_{1}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
is the limit of
\{f_{n}\}
in
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

\square

We also consider the complex structure
\imath:{\mathfrak{L}}\to{\mathfrak{L}}
defined by
(\imath f)\hat{\,}(p)=i\operatorname{sign}(p)\hat{f}(p)
,
f\in{\mathfrak{L}}
. Thus
{\mathfrak{L}}
becomes a complex Hilbert space with the complex scalar product

(f,g):=\langle f,g\rangle-i\langle f,\imath g\rangle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}p\hat{f}(-p)\hat{g}(p)\,dp,\qquad f,g\in{\mathfrak{L}},

and

\beta(f,g):=\Im(f,g)=-\langle f,\imath g\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(-p)\widehat{g^{\prime}}(p)\,dp,\qquad f,g\in{\mathfrak{L}},

is a symplectic form on
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

Proposition B.2
.

C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
is dense in
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

We start by observing that for
f\in{\mathfrak{L}}
one has

\|f-(\chi_{[\varepsilon,+\infty)}\hat{f})\check{\,}\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}p|\hat{f}(p)|^{2}dp\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and it is then sufficient to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
is dense in the subspace of elements
f\in{\mathfrak{L}}
whose Fourier transform vanishes in
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Let
f
be such an element of
{\mathfrak{L}}
. Then, clearly,
\hat{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and therefore
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Now, given a non negative
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
with support in
[-1,1]
and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho=1
, a sequence of mollifiers is obtained by
\rho_{n}(x):=n\rho(nx)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\rho_{n}*f
is in
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\mathfrak{L}}
with all its derivatives
(\rho_{n}*f)^{(h)}=\rho_{n}^{(h)}*f
(by the Young inequality), and

\|\rho_{n}*f-f\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}p|\sqrt{2\pi}\hat{\rho}(p/n)-1|^{2}|\hat{f}(p)|^{2}\to 0

as
n\to+\infty
, by dominated convergence. It is then sufficient to show that if
f
belongs to
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with all its derivatives it can be approximated in
{\mathfrak{L}}
with
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
functions. To this end, take a non negative
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
{\text{supp}\,}\chi\subset[-2,2]
and
\chi=1
on
[-1,1]
, and define a sequence of cut-off functions by
\chi_{n}(x):=\chi(x/n)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
g_{n}:=\chi_{n}f-f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\|g_{n}\|^{2}=-\beta(\imath g_{n},g_{n})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\imath g_{n})(x)g_{n}^{\prime}(x)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g^{\prime}_{n}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})},

(42)

so it is sufficient to show that
g_{n},g^{\prime}_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. It is clear that
g_{n}\to 0
. Moreover,

\|\chi^{\prime}_{n}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\chi^{\prime}(x/n)f(x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{n^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\to 0,

while

\|(\chi_{n}-1)f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\int_{|x|>2n}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx\to 0.

Thus
g^{\prime}_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
as
n\to+\infty
, and the proof is complete.

\square

Since the only constant

is everywhere vanishing, we can consider the (algebraic) direct sum

coincides with the set of equivalence classes of elements

, which we will denote by
[f]
, or just by

if there is no danger of confusion. Therefore, the complex structure
\imath
, the complex scalar product

and the symplectic structure

\imath[f]:=[\imath f],\quad([f],[g]):=(f,g),\quad\beta([f],[g]):=\beta(f,g),\quad f,g\in{\mathfrak{L}},

a complex Hilbert space.

The reason for passing from
{\mathfrak{L}}
to

is that on

there is a natural unitary action of
{{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
, defined as follows. We identify the circle

(tought as a subset of

of

via the stereographic projection, which sends

. As a consequence, we identify functions on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
and functions on
\bar{\mathbb{R}}
(and their restrictions to

).

Proposition B.3
.

If
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, then

.

Indeed,

. Moreover, since

.

\square

Thus, by Prop.

,

is the completion of

Proposition B.4
.

C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
is a

.

,

\displaystyle D(q_{B}):=\{f\in{\cal H}\,:\,f^{\prime}|_{B}\in L^{2}(B,(1-x^{2})\,dx)\},

\displaystyle q_{B}(f):=\pi\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx.

We will use the same symbols to denote the bilinear forms induced by these quadratic forms by polarization.

Proposition B.8
.

The forms
q_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
,
q_{B}
are closed.

We prove the statement for
q_{B}
, the proof for
q_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
being completely analogous. Let
\{f_{n}\}\subset D(q_{B})
be norm convergent to some
f\in{\cal H}
and such that
q_{B}(f_{n}-f_{m})\to 0
as
n,m\to+\infty
. Then
\{f_{n}^{\prime}|_{B}\}
converges in
L^{2}(B,(1-x^{2})\,dx)
to some
g\in L^{2}(B,(1-x^{2})\,dx)
, and it is therefore sufficient to show that
f^{\prime}|_{B}=g
. To this end, take
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
and compute

\begin{split}\langle f^{\prime},\varphi\rangle&=-\langle f,\varphi^{\prime}\rangle=-2\Im(f,\varphi)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}-2\Im(f_{n},\varphi)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}dxf^{\prime}_{n}(x)\varphi(x)\\
&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}dx(1-x^{2})f^{\prime}_{n}(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{1-x^{2}}=\int_{-1}^{1}dx(1-x^{2})g(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{1-x^{2}}=\langle g,\varphi\rangle,\end{split}

where in the second equality we used the definition of the scalar product in
{\cal H}
in terms of the Fourier transforms
\hat{f}
,
\hat{\varphi}
, in the third equality the norm convergence of
\{f_{n}\}
to
f
, in the fourth equality again the definition of the scalar product and the fact that
f_{n}^{\prime}
is a function on
{\text{supp}\,}\varphi\subset B
, and in the sixth equality the convergence of
\{f^{\prime}_{n}|_{B}\}
to
g
in
L^{2}(B,(1-x^{2})\,dx)
. The statement is thus proven.

\square

Since
q_{B}
is clearly non-negative, there exist a real self-adjoint operator
A_{B}
on
{\cal H}
(thought as a real Hilbert space with the real part of the scalar product), such that

q_{B}(f,g)=\Re(f,A_{B}g),\qquad f,g\in D(q_{B}),

and a similar statement holds for
q_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
.

We are now ready to prove the main result of this appendix.

Theorem B.9
.

The quadratic forms
q_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
,
q_{B}
coincide with the entropy quadratic forms
S(\cdot|H({\mathbb{R}}_{+}))
,
S(\cdot|H(B))
respectively.

Again we only prove the statement for
q_{B}
. We start by observing that clearly
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})\subset D(q_{B})
. Moreover, given
g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, let
h_{a}(x):=(a^{2}-x^{2})g^{\prime}(x)
,
a,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, using once more (
42
) one checks that
\chi_{(-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)}h_{1-\varepsilon}\to\chi_{B}h_{1}
in
{\cal H}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and then mollifying
\chi_{(-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)}h_{1-\varepsilon}
as in the proof of Prop.
B.2
to obtain a sequence of
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
function, one concludes that
\chi_{B}h_{1}\in H(B)
. Similarly,
(1-\chi_{B})h_{1}\in H(B^{c})=H(B)^{\prime}
, and therefore, from (
43
),

{\cal E}_{H(B)}g=\imath P_{H(B)}\imath\log\Delta_{H(B)}g=\imath\chi_{B}h_{1},

and, for all
f\in D(q_{B})
,

q_{B}(f,g)=\pi\int_{-1}^{1}dx\,(1-x^{2})f^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(x)=\Re(f,{\cal E}_{H(B)}g)=\Re(f,A_{B}g).

Thus
A_{B}
is a self-adjoint extension of
{\cal E}_{H(B)}|_{C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}
, which is essentially self-adjoint by Cor.
B.7
, so that, finally,
A_{B}={\cal E}_{H(B)}
and
q_{B}=S(\cdot|H(B))
.

\square

We can thus write, for all
f\in{\cal H}
,

S(f|H(B))=\pi\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx,\qquad S(f|H({\mathbb{R}}_{+}))=\pi\int_{0}^{+\infty}x|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx,

with the convention that the right hand sides are
+\infty
if (the appropriate restriction of)
f^{\prime}
does not belong to
L^{2}(B,(1-x^{2})\,dx)
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x\,dx)
respectively.

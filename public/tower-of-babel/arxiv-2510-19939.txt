Covariant phase space and the semi-classical Einstein equation
Abhirup Bhattacharya, Onkar Parrikar

Abstract
The covariant phase space formalism in general relativity is a covariant method for constructing the symplectic two-form, Hamiltonian and other conserved charges on the phase space of solutions to the Einstein equation with classical matter. In this note, we consider a generalization of this formalism to the semi-classical Einstein equation coupled to quantum matter. Given a family of solutions in semi-classical gravity, we define the semi-classical symplectic two-form – a natural generalization of the classical sympelctic two-form – as the sum of the gravitational symplectic form and the Berry curvature associated to the quantum state of matter. We show that the semi-classical symplectic two-form is independent of the Cauchy slice, and satisfies the quantum generalization of the classical Hollands-Iyer-Wald identity. For small perturbations, we also extend our discussion to gauge-invariantly defined subregions of spacetime, where the quantum contribution is replaced by the Berry curvature of certain special purifications involving the Connes cocycle. In the AdS/CFT context, the semi-classical symplectic form defined here is naturally dual to the Berry curvature in the boundary CFT.

Introduction

The covariant phase space formalism
crnkovic1987covariant
provides a manifestly covariant way (
i.e.
, without picking a time coordinate) to think about the classical phase space of solutions to the Einstein equation with its associated symplectic structure, Hamiltonian and other conserved charges. ;
Iyer:1994ys
;

, and to propose new definitions of dynamical black hole entropy
Hollands:2024vbe
;
Wall:2015raa
. The formalism has also found a wide variety of applications in the context of the AdS/CFT correspondence, such as the proof of the equality of bulk and boundary relative entropies
Jafferis:2015del
, derivations of Einstein’s equation from entanglement in the boundary CFT

;
Faulkner:2017tkh
;
Haehl:2017sot
;

;
Haehl:2019fjz
, proofs of gravitational positive energy theorems from CFT entropic inequalities
Lashkari:2016idm
, and the derivation of the bulk dual of boundary Berry curvature at large-

Belin:2018fxe
;

. Beyond AdS/CFT, the covariant phase space formalism has been used to investigate near horizon symmetries of black holes
Flanagan:2015pxa
;
Hawking:2016msc
;
Hawking:2016sgy
;
Haco:2018ske
, asymptotic symmetries and surface charges in general relativity
Chandrasekaran:2021hxc
;
Chandrasekaran:2021vyu

;
Balasubramanian:2018axm

Many of the results mentioned above work in the context of classical general relativity coupled to classical matter. In particular, the matter contributes to the symplectic two-form on phase space, and the total (gravity plus matter) symplectic form can be defined without choice of a Cauchy surface. It is natural to wonder whether this formalism finds a natural generalization to semi-classical gravity. In semi-classical gravity, one treats matter quantum mechanically and finds the metric by solving the semi-classical Einstein equation:

R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}R\,g_{ab}+\Lambda\,g_{ab}=8\pi G_{N}\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle,

(1)

where the right hand side involves the expectation value of the matter stress tensor. It is usually inconsistent to treat a physical system part classically and part quantum mechanically, so what is really being said here is that to a good approximation in the
G_{N}\to 0
limit, the metric can be taken to be in a coherent state, while the matter can be in some more general quantum state. In order to have significant backreaction in this limit, a standard trick
Flanagan:1996gw
is to introduce a large matter central charge
c
, so that back-reaction is controlled by the parameter
\epsilon=c\,G_{N}
. The semi-classical approximation has found a large number of applications; some recent applications include Page curve calculations for evaporating black holes
Fiola:1994ir
;
Engelhardt:2014gca
;
Almheiri:2019psf
;
Penington:2019npb
, constructing endpoints of the super-radiant instabilities of rotating and charged black holes
Kim:2023sig
;
Choi:2024xnv
, bulk reconstruction
Levine:2020upy
;
Soni:2024oim
, singularity theorems
Bousso:2025xyc
etc.

The purpose of this note is to generalize the covariant phase space formalism to semi-classical gravity. We imagine that we are given a family
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
of solutions
(g_{ab}(\lambda^{i}),\psi(\lambda^{i}))
to the semi-classical Einstein equation, where
\lambda^{i}
are some parameters, which we can think of as coordinates on the space of these solutions. A concrete setting to have in mind is asymptotically AdS spacetimes, where the role of the parameters
\lambda^{i}
is played by the sources for various single/multi-trace operators in the boundary CFT; this is the setting we will focus on through most of this paper. We propose that the space
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
also admits a natural, closed two form, which we call the
semi-classical symplectic form
, given by

\mathsf{F}=\Omega_{\text{grav.}}+\mathsf{f},

(2)

where
\Omega_{\text{grav.}}
is the symplectic 2-form associated with classical metric deformations, while
\mathsf{f}
is the
Berry curvature
associated with the quantum matter:

\mathsf{f}=\delta\mathsf{a},\;\;\;\mathsf{a}=-i\langle\psi|\delta\psi\rangle,

(3)

where
\delta=\sum_{i}d\lambda^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}
is the exterior derivative on
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
. We will show that the semi-classical symplectic form
\mathsf{F}
defined in this way is indepdendent of the choice of Cauchy slice, and indeed reduces to the classical matter symplectic form when the matter is taken to be in a coherent state. Furthermore, given some asymptotically Killing vector field
\zeta
, we show that the semi-classical symplectic form is invariant under the corresponding diffeomorphism. We show how to construct a corresponding charge
H_{\zeta}
(including classical plus the appropriate quantum corrections coming from matter) which satisfies

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}=\delta H_{\zeta}.

(4)

This is essentially the statement that the charge
H_{\zeta}
generates the action of the diffeomorphism
\zeta
on phase space. This identity is sometimes referred to as the Hollands-Iyer-Wald identity in the classical gravity context, and here we generalize it to semi-classical gravity. For small perturbations around a background metric and state, we also extend our discussion to the case of gauge-invariantly defined subregions in gravity, where the role of the matter contribution is played by the Berry curvature of a certain purification of the subregion state involving the Connes cocycle.

In the AdS/CFT context, it was argued in
Belin:2018fxe
;
Belin:2018bpg
that in the large-
N
limit, if we take the parameters
\lambda^{i}
to be
O(N^{2})
classical sources for single-trace operators, then the Berry curvature of the boundary CFT is dual to the symplectic form of the corresponding bulk matter fields. It is natural to try to extend this to more general sources, including
O(1)
sources for multi-trace operators. In this context, the bulk is more appropriately described within the realm of semi-classical gravity with quantum matter. We argue that our semi-classical symplectic form is the natural bulk dual to the boundary Berry curvature within the semi-classical gravity approximation, i.e., not including corrections from quantum gravity and non-perturbative effects. Thus, the semi-classical symplectic form that we define fits naturally within the AdS/CFT dictionary.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: in section
2
, we review the covariant phase space formalism for gravity coupled to classical matter. In section
3
, we construct and study the semi-classical symplectic form for complete Cauchy slices. In section
4
, we extend this discussion to the case of subregions of the bulk spacetime. In section
5
, we discuss the duality between the semi-classical symplectic form in the bulk and the Berry curvature in the boundary CFT.

Preliminaries

In this section, we will review the background material needed for extending the covariant phase space formalism of gravity to include quantum matter.

2.1
A primer on field spaces and infinite dimensional manifolds

Let
M
be a
D=(d+1)
-dimensional spacetime manifold, and let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of field configurations on
M
corresponding to all the dynamical fields in the theory. Following

as the

We consider spacetimes obtained by specifying asymptotic boundary conditions on the boundary

Let
\left\{\phi^{a}\right\}
denote the set of all fundamental fields of the theory and let

L[\phi^{a}]=f[\phi^{a}]\,\epsilon_{M},

be a local Lagrangian density, thought of as a top-degree form on
M

\epsilon_{M}
being the volume form. Then, varying the Lagrangian with respect to
\phi^{a}
, we get

\delta L=E_{a}\delta\phi^{a}+d\theta[\delta\phi^{a}],

(5)

where the sum over
a
is assumed. Here
E_{a}
is a top-degree form proportional to the equation of motion and
d\theta
is a total derivative term. Since we require the action to be stationary, we must have

\delta S=\int_{M}E_{a}\delta\phi^{a}+\int_{\partial M}\theta[\delta\phi^{a}]=0.

(6)

In order to have a well-defined variational problem, we need to impose boundary conditions on the fields so that
\theta|_{\partial M}
vanishes. In cases where standard normalizable boundary conditions on
\phi^{a}
are not enough, we typically add a boundary term
\ell[\phi^{a}]\epsilon_{\partial M}
to the Lagrangian density and impose boundary conditions such that
(\theta+\delta(\ell\epsilon_{\partial M}))|_{\partial M}=0
. Since
\ell
can be an arbitrary local functional, this buys us enough freedom to ensure the validity of the variational principle. A well-known example of such a boundary term is the Gibbons-Hawking-York term of Einstein gravity
Gibbons:1976ue
;
York:1972sj
.

Once we have a well-defined variational problem, we define the
covariant phase space

\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{C}
as the set of all field configurations that satisfy
E_{a}=0
. In situations where the initial value problem is well-defined,
\mathcal{P}
gives a covariant way of defining the canonical phase space of the theory, since the space of initial data on a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
must be in one-to-one correspondence with the set
\mathcal{P}
of solutions to equations of motion.

A standard technical assumption in the covariant phase space formalism is that
\mathcal{C}
and

are manifolds with enough structure, so that standard techniques of differential calculus can be applied

;
crnkovic1987covariant

with respect to a vector field

\mathsterling_{V}

\mathsterling_{V}=I_{V}\delta+\delta I_{V}.

(7)

It is also assumed that the covariant phase space

, and that one can define differential forms on
\mathcal{P}
by pulling back from the ambient space

.

A pedestrian way to think about differential forms and exterior derivatives on
\mathcal{P}
is to imagine
\mathcal{P}
to be an
n
-parameter family of solutions
\phi^{a}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
. For instance, the

could be some parameters in the specified sources at asymptotic infinity in AdS. From this point of view, the exterior derivative is defined as
\delta=\sum_{i}d\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}
, and the statement

follows trivially from the commutativity of the partial derivatives
\partial/\partial\lambda_{i}
and
\partial/\partial\lambda_{j}
. We will often use the notation
\left\{\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi^{a}(x)}\right\}
and
\left\{\delta\phi^{a}(x)\right\}
to denote a local coordinate basis for the tangent space and the cotangent space of
\mathcal{C}
respectively, at some point
\phi^{a}(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. In this notation, the exterior derivative takes the form

\delta=\int_{M}\delta\phi^{a}(x)\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi^{a}(x)}.

(8)

One thing to keep in mind is that we will often need to talk about configuration space differential forms valued in spacetime differential forms. We say that
\alpha
is a form of degree
(p,n)
if it is an
n
-form on configuration space which takes as input

and gives a

-form on spacetime. Thus, for any point

\alpha|_{x,\phi^{a}}:\otimes^{n}T_{\phi}\mathcal{C}\to\wedge^{p}T^{*}_{x}M.

(9)

From this point of view, it seems natural to follow the convention that the spacetime exterior derivative

commutes with
\delta

2.2
The covariant symplectic form

We now turn to the definition of the symplectic form on
\mathcal{P}
, which is a closed, non-degenerate
(D-1,2)
-form on
\mathcal{P}
. We define the symplectic current
\omega
as
Lee:1990nz

\omega=F_{*}(\delta\theta),

(11)

where
\theta
is the
(D-1,1)
-form defined in eq. (
5
), and
F_{*}
is the pullback from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\Sigma
be a Cauchy slice in spacetime. Then the symplectic form
\Omega_{\Sigma}
is defined as

\Omega_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\omega.

(12)

Clearly,
\Omega_{\Sigma}
is closed since
\delta\theta
is an exact form on
\mathcal{C}
and the pullback of an exact form is also exact. Moreover, it is independent of the choice of Cauchy slice
\Sigma
. To see this consider

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\omega&=F_{*}(\delta d\theta)\\
&=F_{*}(\delta(\delta L-E_{a}\delta\phi^{a}))\\
&=-F_{*}(\delta E_{a}\curlywedge\delta\phi^{a}),\end{split}

(13)

where in the first line we have used the fact that
d
commutes with
\delta
and
F_{*}
, in the second line we have used eq. (
5
), and in the third line we have used
\delta^{2}=0
. Since the pullback of
\delta E_{a}
to
\mathcal{P}
vanishes (since
\mathcal{P}
by definition is the space of solutions to equations of motion), we see that
d\omega=0
and therefore
\Omega_{\Sigma}
is independent of the choice of
\Sigma
.
3
3

3

Here we have assumed that the boundary conditions at asymptotic infinity are such that there is no symplectic flux at infinity.

Although,
\Omega_{\Sigma}
is closed, it is degenerate if the theory under consideration has gauge symmetries
Lee:1990nz
;
Donnelly:2016auv
. In order to obtain a non-degenerate symplectic form, one quotients
\mathcal{P}
with the group
G
of “small” gauge transformations which decay sufficiently fast at asymptotic infinity. In the literature,
\mathcal{P}
is often referred to as the pre-phase space whereas
\mathcal{P}/G
is the true phase space. This distinction will not be too important for the purposes of this paper.

As a final comment, note that the definition of
\theta
in eq. (
5
) is not unique, and this may lead to ambiguities in the definition of the covariant symplectic form. There are two potential sources of ambiguity:

1.

We can always add a boundary term of the form
\delta(\ell\,\epsilon_{\partial M})
to the Lagrangian, where
\epsilon_{\partial M}
is some extension of the boundary volume form to the bulk. This modifies
\theta
to

\theta\to\theta+\delta(\ell\epsilon_{\partial M}).

(14)

Note that this does not lead to an ambiguity in the definition of the symplectic current since the extra term is
\delta
-exact.

2.

We can always add to
\theta
a
(D-2,1)
-form that is
d
-exact. In other words, modifying
\theta
by

\theta\to\theta+d\beta

(15)

does not change equation (
5
). However, the symplectic form picks up an additional boundary term:

\Omega_{\Sigma}\to\Omega_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\delta\beta.

(16)

This ambiguity is harder to resolve. In certain cases asymptotic boundary conditions on the fields can ensure that
\delta\beta|_{\partial\Sigma}
vanishes. But this is not guaranteed in general. A proposal for fixing this ambiguity for global states in AdS was given in
Harlow:2019yfa
. We will not have anything further to add to this discussion; we will simply take
\beta=0
as a minimal prescription.

2.3
Gravitational phase space with classical matter

Let
M
be a
(d+1)
-dimensional spacetime and let

L[g,\phi^{A}]=L_{\mathrm{EH}}[g]+L_{\mathrm{matter}}[g,\phi^{A}]+d\ell[g],

(17)

be the Lagrangian density describing Einstein gravity with a cosmological constant coupled to matter, where
g=g_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}
is the spacetime metric,
\{\phi^{A}\}
collectively denotes all the matter fields,

\displaystyle L_{\text{EH}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)\epsilon_{M},

(18)

and
L_{\text{matter}}
is the matter Lagrangian. Further,
\ell
is a
(D-1,0)
form (covariantly constructed from the metric, Riemann tensor and its derivatives) such that its restriction to the boundary reproduces the Gibbons-Hawking term, plus any potential counterterms:

\ell|_{\partial M}=\frac{1}{8\pi G_{N}}K\epsilon_{\partial M}+\ell_{\text{ct}}.

(19)

The Gibbons-Hawking-York term ensures that the variational principle is well-defined with Dirichlet boundary conditions at asymptotic infinity, while the counterterms are added in order to cancel any divergences in holographic renormalization. Varying the Lagrangian with respect to the metric
g
and the matter fields
\phi^{A}
, we get

\delta L=E_{ab}\delta g^{ab}+E_{A}\delta\phi^{A}+d\theta^{g}+d\theta^{\phi},

(20)

Here
E_{ab}
and
E_{A}
are proportional to the equations of motion for the metric and the matter fields respectively, and
d\theta^{g}
and
d\theta^{\phi}
are the corresponding total derivative terms. For instance, if we take the matter sector to consist of a free, real, massive scalar field minimally coupled to gravity, then we get:

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{ab}&=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R+\Lambda g_{ab}-8\pi G_{N}T_{ab}^{\mathrm{matter}}\right)\epsilon_{M},\\
T_{ab}^{\text{matter}}&=\nabla_{a}\phi\nabla_{b}\phi-g_{ab}\mathcal{L}_{\text{matter}},\\

where the covariant derivative

Let
\zeta
be a vector field on
M
. To every such vector field we can define a corresponding vector field on
\mathcal{C}
as

V_{\zeta}=\int_{M}dx\,\left(\mathsterling_{\zeta}g_{ab}(x)\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{ab}(x)}+\mathsterling_{\zeta}\phi^{A}(x)\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi^{A}(x)}\right).

(25)

Note that since the coefficients of this vector field correspond to a pure diffeomorphism, this vector field naturally restricts to a vector field on
\mathcal{P}
, when evaluated on
\mathcal{P}
. Even if we only consider some
n
-parameter family of solutions, we will always assume that this space has been suitably extended such that the action of pure diffeomorphisms leaves us within
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}
be the Lie derivative with respect to the vector field
V_{\zeta}
and
\eta
be a differential form that is locally constructed out of the fields. Then
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\eta
can be interpreted as computing the variation of
\eta
under a spacetime diffeomorphism. Now, recall that the Lie derivative
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}
satisfies Cartan’s magic formula:

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\eta=\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\eta)+I_{V_{\zeta}}(\delta\eta).

(26)

We say that the configuration space form
\eta
is
covariant
under the diffeomorphism generated by
\zeta
if
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\eta=\mathsterling_{\zeta}\eta
, where the right hand side is the ordinary spacetime Lie derivative in
M
. It is important to note that the configuration space Lie derivative only acts on the
dynamical
fields of the theory whereas the spacetime Lie derivative acts on all fields, including any potential external sources. We say that a theory is covariant with respect to a diffeomorphism
\zeta
if the following holds:

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}L=\mathsterling_{\zeta}L.

(27)

A gravitational theory should be covariant under all diffeomorphisms. In fact, it can be shown that any theory whose Lagrangian satisfies the following two conditions must be diffeomorphism covariant
Iyer:1994ys
:

1.

L
must not contain any external/background fields,

2.

L
can only be a function of arbitrary covariant derivatives of the Riemann tensor
\nabla_{a_{1}}\nabla_{a_{2}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{n}}R_{abcd}
and its traces, and arbitrary covariant derivatives of the matter fields
\nabla_{a_{1}}\nabla_{a_{2}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{n}}\phi^{A}
and its traces, for any
n\geq 0
.

This set of conditions is sufficiently general to accommodate all higher derivative theories of gravity, including Einstein gravity minimally coupled to matter. Note that this is simply a re-formulation of the familiar statement that all diffeomorphisms are gauge symmetries of gravity.

2.4
Boundary charges for diffeomorphisms

Let
\zeta
be a vector field on
M

\zeta

(D-1,0)

\displaystyle\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\ell-i_{\zeta}d\ell

2.5
The classical Hollands-Wald identity

The Hollands-Wald (HW) identity is a general identity that shows that the charges defined above actually generate the action of the large diffeomorphisms on phase space
Hollands:2012sf
:

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\Omega_{\Sigma}=\delta H_{\Sigma}(\zeta).

(39)

This identity has been incredibly useful in various contexts. For instance, in
Hollands:2012sf
, this identity was used to derive a criterion for the stability of black holes in terms of the canonical energy. In the AdS/CFT context, the HW identity was used in
Lashkari:2015hha
to derive a perturbative bulk formula for the boundary relative entropy, and to relate the positivity of bulk canonical energy to positivity of boundary relative entropy. This identity was also used in
Faulkner:2013ica
to derive the linearized Einstein equation around vacuum AdS assuming the Ryu-Takayanagi formula and the CFT first law of entanglement. This result was then generalized to second order in
Faulkner:2017tkh
and to more general backgrounds in
Lewkowycz:2018sgn
, again making crucial use of the HW identity. In this section, we will review the proof of this identity for gravity with classical matter. The goal of this paper is to generalize this identity to the case of semi-classical gravity with quantum matter. In a companion paper
upcoming
, we will use these results to extend the work on deriving Einstein’s equations from entanglement to second order, including quantum corrections in the bulk.

The first step in the proof is to take a variation of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta J_{\zeta}&=\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta)-\delta(i_{\zeta}L)\\

(40)

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\theta=\mathsterling_{\zeta}\theta=d(i_{\zeta}\theta)+i_{\zeta}(d\theta).

&=d\left(i_{\zeta}\theta\right)-I_{V_{\zeta}}\delta\theta-i_{\zeta}(E_{ab}\delta g^{ab}+E_{A}\delta\phi^{A}).\end{split}

(42)

Now, recall that in section

3
Covariant phase space with quantum matter

In this section we will generalize the results of section
2
to semi-classical gravity. This means that the matter degrees of freedom will be treated quantum mechanically, while gravity will be treated classically and taken to satisfy the semi-classical Einstein equation
Flanagan:1996gw
;
Wall:2011hj
:

R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G_{N}\,\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle,

(52)

where
\psi
is the state of bulk matter fields. Usually, it is inconsistent to treat a system part classically and part quantum mechanically, so to be clear, what is really being said here is that the quantum state of the metric can be well-approximated by a coherent state, while that of the matter fields can be more general. Coherent states are completely specified by the expectation value of a quantum observable, in this case, the metric. The semi-classical Einstein equation (
52
) together with matter dynamics specifies this expectation value. In the AdS/CFT context, this comes about as follows: we specify the boundary conditions at asymptotic infinity in terms of sources for the stress tensor and other single-trace or multi-trace operators. We will assume that there are no
O(N^{2})
sources for single/multi-trace operators, other than the stress tensor. Such sources would correspond to turning on classical background configurations for matter fields, which was already considered in section
2
. Our matter sources will be
O(1)
, which we will think of as turning on a quantum state
\psi^{(0)}
for the bulk matter fields. Then, we solve the semi-classical Einstein equation perturbatively in
G_{N}
:

g_{ab}=g^{(0)}_{ab}+G_{N}g^{(1)}_{ab}+\cdots,\psi=\psi^{(0)}+G_{N}\psi^{(1)}+\cdots,

(53)

where
g^{(0)}
is the background metric obtained by solving Einstein’s equations without matter but subject to the asymptotic boundary conditions for the metric determined by the source for the boundary stress tensor,
\psi^{(0)}
is the quantum state of matter fields obtained by performing the matter path integral on
g^{(0)}
subject to the matter boundary conditions (corresponding to
O(1)
sources for single/multi-trace operators),
g^{(1)}
is the
O(G_{N})
correction to the background metric from backreaction due to the stress tensor expectation value in
\psi^{(0)}
,
\psi^{(1)}
is the change in the state of quantum fields owing to the backreaction on the metric, and so on. An important point that we have ignored in this analysis in the contribution of gravitons, or equivalently non-coherent deformations to the quantum state of the metric field. A somewhat hand-wavy justification for this is that we can treat gravitons as another quantum field, and take into account their back-reaction on the coherent part of the metric. A second, slightly more satisfactory way to deal with this issue is to consider a matter theory with a large central charge
c
, so that one can take
G_{N}\to 0
in order to suppress quantum gravitational effects, while still retaining back-reaction, controlled by the parameter
\epsilon=c\,G_{N}
; in this case, the formal expansions in equation (
53
) are to be treated as expansions in
\epsilon
, rather than in
G_{N}

Hartle:1981zt
. We will not try to justify the semi-classical approximation any further than this; we will simply assume that in some appropriate limit, one can reliably use it.

3.1
Berry curvature

In the classical setting, the covariant phase space formalism naturally involves the symplectic form on phase space for both the metric and matter fields. However, in treating matter quantum mechanically, we will need to find a suitable generalization of the matter symplectic form. We propose that the matter Berry curvature is the appropriate generalization in this setting (see also
Czech:2017zfq
;
Czech:2018kvg
;
Belin:2018bpg
;
Belin:2018fxe
;
Kirklin:2019ror
;

;

;

for previous work involving the Berry curvature in gravity).

To this end, let
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
be the space of solutions to the semi-classical Einstein equation. We assume that
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
is a manifold with coordinates
\{\lambda^{i}\}
, where each point corresponds to a solution
(g_{ab}(\lambda^{i}),\psi(\lambda^{i}))
. As before, let
\delta
be the exterior derivative on
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
which can be expressed in terms of the coordinates

as

\delta=\sum_{i}d\lambda^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{i}}.

(54)

The Berry connection is defined as the following
1
-form on
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
:
10
10

10

Formally, consider the (trivial) bundle
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\text{matter}}
. Given a smooth section
|\psi\rangle
, we can smoothly associate a projector
P_{\psi}
at each point on
\mathcal{P}_{\text{q}}
. Then
\mathsf{a}
is a
U(1)

P_{\Psi}\mathcal{G}
.

\mathsf{a}=-i\langle\psi|\delta\psi\rangle.

(55)

Under the action of the gauge group
|\psi\rangle\to e^{i\alpha}|\psi\rangle
, the connection transforms as

\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{a}^{\prime}=\mathsf{a}+\delta\alpha.

(56)

The curvature associated to the connection
\mathsf{a}
is called the Berry curvature and is defined as

\mathsf{f}=\delta\mathsf{a}.

(57)

The Berry curvature should be thought of as the natural generalization of the matter sympelctic 2-form over classical phase space. Indeed, if we take the family
|\psi(\lambda^{i})\rangle
to be a family of coherent states, then the Berry curvature reduces to the symplectic 2-form. To see this, consider a single scalar field
\widehat{\phi}
with conjugate momentum
\widehat{\pi}
. Let
\Sigma
be a Cauchy slice and let

|\psi(\lambda^{i})\rangle=\exp\left[i\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon_{\Sigma}\left(\pi(\lambda^{i})\widehat{\phi}-\phi(\lambda^{i})\widehat{\pi}\right)\right]|\Omega\rangle.

These coherent states, by definition, satisfy:

\langle\psi|\,\widehat{\phi}\,|\psi\rangle=\phi,\quad\langle\psi|\,\widehat{\pi}\,|\psi\rangle=\pi.

(59)

From equation (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{a}&=-i\langle\psi|\delta\psi\rangle\\
&=\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon_{\Sigma}\left(\delta\pi\,\phi-\delta\phi\,\pi\right),\end{split}

(60)

where in the second line we have used equation (
59

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{f}=2\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon_{\Sigma}\,\delta\pi\curlywedge\delta\phi.\end{split}

(61)

This indicates that the natural generalization of the matter symplectic form to the setting of semi-classical gravity is the Berry curvature for matter states. We will show that with this choice, the covariant phase space formalism can be naturally generalized to semi-classical gravity.

3.2
Semi-classical generalization of the symplectic form

We will assume that the matter states

In the semi-classical context, it is natural to consider the object:

An intuitive way to think about this is that in the semi-classical limit, the metric is taken to be in a coherent state with the expectation value of the metric determined by the semi-classical Einstein equation. The 2-form

U(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}\exp\left[-i\int_{0}^{t}ds\,h(s)\right],\;\;h(s)=\int_{\Sigma_{s}}n^{a}\xi^{b}T_{ab}

where

\mathsf{a}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}=\mathsf{a}_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime})}\epsilon_{M}\,\delta g_{ab}\langle\psi|T^{ab}|\psi\rangle,

is conserved (on-shell) for small diffeomorphisms that vanish asymptotically, we need to show that the local current
J_{\zeta}
is conserved. From the definition of the current, we have

dJ_{\zeta}=d(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g})+\epsilon_{M}\nabla_{a}\left(\langle T^{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\zeta_{b}\right).

(74)

Using diffeomorphism covariance of the classical gravitational Lagrangian, one can show that the gravitational terms above can be written as (see equation (
30
))

d(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g})=-\epsilon_{M}(2G_{ab}\nabla^{a}\zeta^{b}).

(75)

where
G_{ab}
is the Einstein tensor with cosmological constant. Using the conservation of the matter stress tensor, the matter terms in
dJ
can be written as

\epsilon_{M}\nabla^{a}\left(\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\zeta^{b}\right)=\epsilon_{M}\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\nabla^{a}\zeta^{b}.

(76)

Combining this with equation (
75
) we find that

dJ_{\zeta}=-2E_{ab}^{\text{SC}}\nabla^{a}\zeta^{b},

(77)

where
E^{\text{SC}}_{ab}
is the full semi-classical equation of motion:

E^{\text{SC}}_{ab}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\epsilon_{M}\left(R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R+\Lambda g_{ab}-8\pi G_{N}\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle\right).

(78)

Therefore
H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)
is conserved on-shell.

Just as in the classical case, the charge can be explicitly written as (see Appendix
A
)

H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)=\int_{\partial\Sigma}Q_{\zeta}+\int_{\Sigma}C_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}},

(79)

where
Q_{\zeta}
is given by eq. (
33
) and
C_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}}
is defined as

C_{\zeta}^{SC}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\epsilon^{a}\left(R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R+\Lambda g_{ab}-8\pi G_{N}\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle\right)\zeta^{b}.

(80)

Therefore, as long as
\zeta
vanishes sufficiently fast near
\partial\Sigma
and the semi-classical Equations of motion hold,
H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)
triviall vanishes. On the other hand, if
\zeta
is a “large” diffeomorphism, then
H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)
gives a non-trivial charge which is conserved provided
\zeta
asymptotically approaches a Killing vector field; the argument for this follows along similar lines as the classical version explained previously.

3.4
The semi-classical Hollands-Wald identity

We will now generalize the classical Hollands-Wald identity, equation (
49
), by including quantum matter. The first step is to generalize the notion of diffeomorphism covariance to the semi-classical setting. To motivate our construction, we will first restrict to the class of Euclidean path integral states with sources inserted at the asymptotic boundary. For these states, the matter Berry connection
\mathsf{a}
is given by the following path integral:

\mathsf{a}=\int[D\varphi]_{M^{-}\cup M^{+}}\int_{M^{-}}\epsilon\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta g^{ab}T_{ab}+\cdots\right)e^{-S[\varphi,g]-\int_{Y}J^{I}O_{I}},

(81)

where the path integral is performed over the full contour (with a potential time-fold due to Lorentzian time evolution), the stress tensor insertion is only in the lower half portion corresponding to the ket, and the dots denotes other matter contributions which localized to the past asymptotic boundary. Consider now the object
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}
. We will assume the following generalization of the classical notion of diffeomorphism covariance to the semi-classical setting: the phase space Lie derivative
\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}
can be replaced with the spacetime Lie derivative
\mathsterling_{\zeta}
inside the path integral. This will of course be the case if the matter action and the path integral measure are diffeomorphism covariant. Thus,

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}=\int[D\varphi]_{M^{-}\cup M^{+}}\int_{M^{-}}\mathsterling_{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\,T_{ab}\delta g^{ab}\right)e^{-S[\varphi,g]-\int_{Y}J^{I}O_{I}},

(82)

where we have dropped the matter terms since we will focus on diffeomorphisms which act trivially near the past asymptotic boundary. Using Cartan’s magic formula on
M^{-}
, the Lie derivative acting on a top form gives a total derivative. Thus, we obtain

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}i_{\zeta}\epsilon\,\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle\delta g^{ab}.

(83)

In the following we will take equation (
83
) as the
definition
of the Lie derivative of
\mathsf{a}
. In other words, we demand that equation (
83
) continues to hold for non-Euclidean path integral states. Note also that using Cartan’s magic formula on phase space, the above Lie derivative can be written as

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}=I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{f}+\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}).

(84)

We are now in a position to generalize the classical Hollands-Wald idenitity to the semi-classical setting. Taking a phase space exterior derivative of the semi-classical charge defined in equation (
72
), we obtain

\delta H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)=\int_{\Sigma}\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g})+\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}).

(85)

Repeating the analysis of section
2.5
we find that the gravitational terms yield

\int_{\Sigma}\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g})=\int_{\partial\Sigma}i_{\zeta}\theta^{g}-\int_{\Sigma}I_{V_{\zeta}}\delta\theta^{g}-\int_{\Sigma}i_{\zeta}G_{ab}\delta g^{ab},

(86)

where
G_{ab}
is the Einstein tensor (including the cosmological constant term). Recall that the above computation crucially uses diffeomorphism covariance of the classical gravitational action. We now process the matter term in an analogous fashion by using our semi-classical generalization of diffeomorphism covariance. Indeed, using equations (
83
) and (
84
), we get

\displaystyle\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{f}

(87)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}i_{\zeta}\epsilon\,\langle\psi|T_{ab}|\psi\rangle\delta g^{ab}-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{f}.

Substituting equations (
86
) and (
87
) in equation (
85
) and rearranging, we get:

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\chi_{\zeta}+\int_{\Sigma}G_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}},

(88)

where recall that we have defined

\mathsf{F}=\Omega_{\text{grav.}}+\mathsf{f},

(89)

G_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}}=\int_{\Sigma}\left(\delta C_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}}+i_{\zeta}E_{ab}^{\text{SC}}\delta g^{ab}\right),

(90)

with
C^{\text{SC}}_{\zeta}
and
E^{\text{SC}}_{ab}
being defined in equations (
80
) and (
78
) respectively. Just as in the classical case, as long as the semi-classical equation of motion holds, the quantity on the right hand side is a pure boundary term. Using the same arguments we gave in the classical case, this boundary term is given by
\delta H_{\Sigma}
, and so we get:

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}=\delta H_{\Sigma}.

(91)

We can think of this as the semi-classical generalization of the Hollands-Wald identity. In particular, this also implies

\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}=0,

(92)

so that the semi-classical symplectic form is preserved under the action of diffeomorphisms on phase space.

4
Covariant phase space formalism for subregions

In this section we will generalize our considerations of the covariant phase space formalism in the presence of quantum matter to the case of subregions of a Cauchy slice. Our discussion in this section will be limited to the case of small perturbations around a background black hole solution.

4.1
Purifications and the subregion Berry connection in QFT

In this section, we will discuss how to define the Berry connection for subregions in quantum field theory on a
fixed
background spacetime; we will return to the case of gravity in the next subsection. Subregions in QFT are associated not with pure states, but with mixed states, and there does not appear to be a standard way to associate a Berry connection with a family of mixed states (see
Kirklin:2019ror
;
Czech:2017zfq
;
Czech:2018kvg
;
DeBoer:2019kdj
;
Parrikar:2023lfr
;
Czech:2023zmq
;
deBoer:2025rxx
for previous work on this). Here, we will consider a Berry connection for mixed states on subregions by constructing a specific purification involving the Connes cocycle, and defining the Berry connection using the corresponding purified global states.

Consider a spacetime
M
with a fixed background metric
g^{(0)}_{ab}
. Let
D(r)
and
D(\bar{r})
be two complementary subregions, defined in a gauge invariant way. For instance, we could take
D(r)
to be the entanglement wedge of the boundary region
R
. Let
\Sigma
be a Cauchy slice that passes through the quantum extremal surface corresponding to
R
, and let
r\subseteq\Sigma
be the part of
\Sigma
in
D(r)
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{r}
be the von Neumann algebra of observables associated to the domain of dependence
D(r)
. Likewise, let
\overline{r}=\Sigma\setminus r
. Let
\Omega
be a cyclic and separating state with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. For instance, if
M
is an AdS black hole, we can take
\Omega
to be the Hartle-Hawking state. We wish to define a Berry connection for density matrices on
r
. For simplicity, we can imagine that we have chosen some UV regulator such that the global Hilbert space factorizes into the Hilbert space of
r
and Hilbert space of
\overline{r}
:

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{r}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{r}},

but much of our discussion can be stated entirely in algebraic terms. Let
\psi
be some global excited state and let
\rho_{\psi}
be the corresponding density matrix on
r
. Our goal is to construct a purification of
\rho_{\psi}
which we can use to define a subregion notion of the Berry connection; for this, the purification should be such that on
D(\overline{r}),
the corresponding state looks like the Hartle-Hawking vacuum. Roughly speaking, this purification is constructed by “sewing” the local density matrix
\rho_{\psi}
with the vacuum density matrix
\rho^{\prime}_{\Omega}
copy of the on the complementary region. Note that in finite dimensional quantum systems, two local density matrices cannot be sewn into a global pure state unless they have the same Schmidt spectrum. In contrast, quantum field theory has the remarkable property that any pair of local states can be sewn into a global pure state via the action of local unitaries. This result, called the
Connes-Størmer theorem
, arises from the fact that local algebras associated to subregions in quantum field theory are type III
1

connes1978homogeneity
. A pedagogical account of this result can be found in
Lashkari:2019ixo
.

While the Connes-Størmer construction guarantees the existence of a purification that sews two local states together, it does not give a practical way of constructing the local unitaries which do the job. In states which look approximately stationary at late times, there is an alternate, somewhat more explicit construction which uses the
Connes cocyle
:

\widehat{\psi}_{s}=u^{\prime}_{\Omega|\psi}(s)\,\psi,

(93)

where

u^{\prime}_{\Omega|\psi}(s)=\Delta_{\Omega|\psi}^{\prime\,is}\Delta_{\psi}^{\prime\,-is}

(94)

is the Connes-cocyle. Here,
\Delta^{\prime}_{\psi}=\rho^{-1}_{\psi}\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{\psi}
where
\rho_{\psi}
is the density matrix of
\psi
on
r
, while
\rho^{\prime}_{\psi}
is the density matrix on
\overline{r}
. Similarly,
\Delta^{\prime}_{\Omega|\psi}=\rho^{-1}_{\psi}\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{\Omega}
is the relative modular operator, and so

u^{\prime}_{\Omega|\psi}(s)=\mathbb{1}\otimes(\rho^{\prime}_{\Omega})^{is}(\rho^{\prime}_{\psi})^{-is}.

(95)

Using standard properties of modular flow, it is easy to check that

\langle\widehat{\psi}_{s}|a|\widehat{\psi}_{s}\rangle=\langle\psi|a|\psi\rangle,\qquad\langle\widehat{\psi}_{s}|a^{\prime}|\widehat{\psi}_{s}\rangle=\langle\psi|\Delta_{\Omega}^{-is}a^{\prime}\Delta_{\Omega}^{is}|\psi\rangle.

(96)

If
\psi
is stationary at late times, i.e., satisfies

\lim_{s\to\infty}\langle\psi|\Delta_{\Omega}^{-is}a^{\prime}\Delta_{\Omega}^{is}|\psi\rangle=\omega^{\prime}(a),

(97)

then
\widehat{\psi}_{s}
in the limit
s\to\infty
approaches a purification of
\psi
which corresponds to
\Omega
on the complementary region. In this way, the Connes-cocycle gives a practical way to construct the desired purifications of local states. In
Lashkari:2019ixo
, it was indeed argued that the infinite time limit of the Connes cocycle flow gives an approximate implementation of the Connes-Størmer construction with respect to a weaker distance measure (see also
Parrikar:2024zbb
for some further discussion). In the rest of this paper, we will focus on these more practical purifications involving the Connes-cocycle. We will use this purification to define a notion of the subregion Berry connection:

\mathsf{a}_{r}=\lim_{s\to\infty}-i\langle\widehat{\psi}_{s}|\delta\widehat{\psi}_{s}\rangle.

(98)

The Berry connection defined this way is independent of deformations of the Cauchy slice localized within the domain of dependence
D(r)
and
D(\overline{r})
. Indeed, a deformation of the Cauchy surface localized within the region
D(r)
and
D(\overline{r})
can be thought of as a factorized local unitary
\mathcal{U}=U_{r}\otimes V_{\bar{r}}
acting on the global state
\psi
, and similarly on
\Omega
. It is a straightforward exercise to check using equation (
95
) that under the action of this unitary,

\widehat{\psi}_{s}\to\mathcal{U}\,\widehat{\psi}_{s},

(99)

and since
\delta\mathcal{U}=0
, the Berry connection is independent of the choice of Cauchy slice.

4.2
The subregion Berry connection in semi-classical gravity

Now consider a family of solutions
(g_{ab}(\lambda_{i}),\psi(\lambda^{i}))
to the semi-classical Einstein equation, where
\psi
are global states; for concreteness, let us consider spacetimes with the same topology as the two-sided black hole. Consider the entanglement wedge
D(r)
corresponding to the boundary region
R
, and let
X
be the quantum extremal surface. Let
\Sigma
be a Cauchy surface that passes through the extremal surface and let
r
be a homology surface between
X
and the asymptotic boundary region
R
. Likewise, let
\overline{r}=\Sigma\setminus r
be the homology surface between the surface and the other asymptotic boundary. Our goal in this section is to associate a semi-classical phase space to the subregion
D(r)
in the presence of quantum matter. One subtlety here is that the subregion
r
itself depends on the parameters
\lambda^{i}
, since the location of the quantum extremal surface depends on the metric and the quantum state of matter fields. As was pointed out in section
2.5
, the way to get around this is to fix the Hollands-Wald gauge, so that the coordinate location of the extremal surface
X
is independent of the parameters
\lambda^{i}
.
With this choice, for the gravitational part, we can construct the subregion symplectic form by simply integrating the symplectic current
\omega_{\text{grav.}}
over the subregion
r
. For the matter part, we propose that we take the Berry curvature of the subregion Berry connection defined in equation (
98
). Thus, our proposal for the semi-classical sympelctic form for the subregion
r
is:

\mathsf{F}_{r}=\Omega_{\text{grav.},r}+\mathsf{f}_{r},

(100)

where
\mathsf{f}_{r}=\delta\mathsf{a}_{r}
. In principle, the Hartle-Hawking vacuum state lives on the original black hole spacetime
g^{(0)}_{ab}
, while the excited state
\psi
lives on the deformed metric
g_{ab}(\lambda^{i})
. When
g_{ab}(\lambda^{i})
is a small deformation around
g^{(0)}_{ab}
, we expect that this distinction is not important, as the change in the background metric can be interpreted as a perturbation in the state around the HH vacuum. This highlights the inherently perturbative nature of our construction. Indeed, our construction involves a special background state, namely the HH state – thus, it is not surprising that it only works perturbatively around this state and around the black hole geometry. It would be interesting to generalize this beyond the regime of small perturbations; we leave this for future work.

As before,
\mathsf{F}_{r}
is invariant under deformations of the Cauchy slice localized within
D(r)
. This follows straightforwardly from the fact that such localized deformations act like localized unitary transformations within
D(r)
, together with the arguments similar to those given in the global case.

4.3
A subregion Hollands-Wald identity

We are now in a position to derive the subregion Hollands-Wald identity. Given a diffeomorphism
\zeta
, we define the subregion diffeomorphism charge as

H_{r}(\zeta)=\int_{r}\left(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g}\right)+I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r}.

(101)

As before, the charge can be written as an integral of a current density over
r
. To see this, recall from equation (
70
) that the action of
V_{\zeta}
on the state
\widehat{\psi}
is given by

I_{V_{\zeta}}|\delta\widehat{\psi}\rangle=i\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}T_{ab}|\widehat{\psi}\rangle

(102)

where
\Sigma=r\cup\overline{r}
. Then, the matter term in equation (
101
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r}&=\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}|\widehat{\psi}\rangle\\
&=\int_{\overline{r}}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}\omega^{\prime}(T_{ab})+\int_{r}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}\psi(T_{ab})\\
&=\int_{r}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\end{split}

(103)

where in the second line we have used the fact that the infinite time limit of the cocycle sews
\psi
with
\omega
in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, and in the third line we have used
\omega^{\prime}(T_{ab})=0
. Therefore, the charge
H_{r}(\zeta)
is the integral of the following current density over
r
:

J_{\zeta}=I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g}+\epsilon^{a}\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\zeta^{b}.

(104)

Just as in the global case, it can be shown that
dJ_{\zeta}=0
as long as the semi-classical equations of motion hold. Therefore
H_{\Sigma}(\zeta)
is conserved for small diffeomorphisms. However for large diffeomorphisms it reduces to a boundary term up to terms proportional to the equations of motion:

H_{R}(\zeta)=\int_{\partial r}Q_{\zeta}+\int_{r}C_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}},

(105)

where
Q_{\zeta}
and
C_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}}
are defined in equations (
33
) and (
80
).

To derive the subregion Hollands-Wald identity, we take a variation of the charge:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta H_{r}(\zeta)&=\int_{r}\delta\left(I_{V_{\zeta}}\theta^{g}-i_{\zeta}L_{g}\right)+\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r})\\
&=\int_{\partial r}i_{\zeta}\theta^{g}-\int_{r}I_{V_{\zeta}}\omega^{g}-\int_{r}i_{\zeta}G_{ab}\delta g^{ab}+\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r}).\end{split}

(106)

To process the matter term we use Cartan’s magic formula:

\delta(I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r})=\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r}-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{f}_{r}

(107)

where recall that
\mathsf{f}_{r}=\delta\mathsf{a}_{r}
. Just like the charge, one can show that the Lie derivative of the subregion Berry connection is simply the restriction of the Lie derivative of the global Berry connection to the subregion. Indeed, from equation (
83
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsterling_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{a}_{r}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\zeta^{c}\epsilon_{c}\,\delta g^{ab}\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}|\widehat{\psi}\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}\zeta^{c}\epsilon_{c}\,\delta g^{ab}\psi(T_{ab})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\overline{r}}\zeta^{c}\epsilon_{c}\,\delta g^{ab}\omega^{\prime}(T_{ab})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}\zeta^{c}\epsilon_{c}\,\delta g^{ab}\,\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}\end{split}

(108)

where again we have used the condition
\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}(x)|\widehat{\psi}\rangle=\omega^{\prime}(T_{ab}(x))=0
whenever
x\in\overline{r}
. Combining equations (
105
), (
106
), and (
108
) we obtain the subregion Hollands-Wald identity:

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}_{r}=\int_{\partial r}\chi_{\zeta}+\int_{r}G_{\zeta}^{\text{SC}},

(109)

which reduces to

-I_{V_{\zeta}}\mathsf{F}_{r}=\delta H_{r}(\zeta),

(110)

upon using equations of motion. We emphasize that this result is only valid in the Hollands-Wald gauge. In going to a different gauge, one often picks up additional boundary terms localized at the quantum extremal surface
Lashkari:2015hha
.

4.4
Consistency with the generalized entropy prescription

The semi-classical subregion Hollands-Iyer-Wald identity, equation (
110
), is valid only for small perturbations around a background metric. In this section, we will perturbatively expand the identity and explore some of its consequences. As before, we consider a family of solutions
(g_{ab}(\lambda),\psi(\lambda))
to the semi-classical Einstein equation and expand around some chosen background
(g^{(0)}_{ab},\psi^{(0)})
. The expansion is performed simultaneously in
G_{N}
and
\lambda
; consequently, each term is labeled by two indices, one indicating the power of
G_{N}
and the other the power of
\lambda
. We thus expand the solution as

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\psi^{(0)}+\psi^{(1,0)}+\psi^{(2,1)}+\cdots

(111)

\displaystyle g_{ab}

\displaystyle=g_{ab}^{(0)}+g_{ab}^{(1,1)}+g_{ab}^{(2,2)}+\cdots

(112)

where, in each term, the first index denotes the order in
\lambda
and the second the order in
G_{N}
. Thus
\psi^{(1,0)}
is the
O(\lambda)
change in the state, and
g^{(1,1)}
is the
O(\lambda G_{N})
back-reaction sourced by
\psi^{(1,0)}
via the linearized Einstein equation. Likewise,
\psi^{(2,1)}
is the
O(\lambda^{2}G_{N})
change in the state induced by the metric perturbation, and
g^{(2,2)}
is the
O(\lambda^{2}G_{N}^{2})
change in the metric sourced by
\psi^{(2,1)}
via the second order Einstein equation. The dots denote terms that are higher order in
\lambda
and
G_{N}
.

Let
\xi
be the bulk boost isometry restricted to the two wedges

. Once the semi-classical equations of motion are imposed, equation (

\delta\langle K_{\text{CFT}}\rangle-\frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{4G_{N}}=-I_{V_{\xi}}\Omega^{\text{grav}}_{r}-I_{V_{\xi}}\mathsf{f}_{r}

where
\delta\langle K_{\text{CFT}}\rangle
is the change in the CFT modular Hamiltonian and
\delta\mathcal{A}
is the change in the area of the extremal surface. Then, using equation (
102
), the bulk matter term can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}-I_{V_{\xi}}\mathsf{f}_{r}&=-i\left(\langle\delta\widehat{\psi}|I_{V_{\xi}}\delta\widehat{\psi}\rangle-\langle I_{V_{\xi}}\delta\widehat{\psi}|\delta\widehat{\psi}\rangle\right)\\
&=\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{a}\xi^{b}\left(\langle\delta\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}|\widehat{\psi}\rangle+\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}|\delta\widehat{\psi}\rangle\right)\\
\end{split}

(114)

Now, it follows from definition that
|\delta\widehat{\psi}\rangle=\delta u^{\prime}|\psi\rangle+u^{\prime}|\delta\psi\rangle
where
u^{\prime}
denotes the infinite time limit of CC flow in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Then the matter Berry curvature becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}-I_{V_{\xi}}\mathsf{f}_{r}=\int_{\Sigma}\epsilon^{a}\zeta^{b}&\left(\langle\delta\psi|(u^{\prime})^{\dagger}T_{ab}u^{\prime}|\psi\rangle+\langle\psi|(u^{\prime})^{\dagger}T_{ab}u^{\prime}|\delta\psi\rangle\right.\\
&\left.+\langle\psi|(\delta u^{\prime})^{\dagger}T_{ab}u^{\prime}|\psi\rangle+\langle\psi|(u^{\prime})^{\dagger}T_{ab}\delta u^{\prime}|\psi\rangle\right).\end{split}

(115)

Next, we split up the integral over
\Sigma
as a sum over
r
and
\overline{r}
. It is easy to check that the terms localized on
\overline{r}
take the form
\delta\int_{\overline{r}}\epsilon^{a}\xi^{b}\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}|\widehat{\psi}\rangle
. But
\langle\widehat{\psi}|T_{ab}(x)|\widehat{\psi}\rangle=\omega^{\prime}(T_{ab}(x))=0
for all
x\in\overline{r}
, and for all states
\psi
. These terms therefore vanish. To process the terms localized on
r
, note that
T_{ab}(x)
commutes with
u^{\prime}
when
x\in r
. Then, using the fact that
\delta(u^{\prime\dagger}u^{\prime})=0

-I_{V_{\xi}}\mathsf{f}_{r}=\int_{r}\epsilon^{a}\xi^{b}\left(\delta\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}+\langle\psi|[T_{ab},(u^{\prime})^{\dagger}\delta u^{\prime}]|\psi\rangle\right).

(116)

Clearly, the second term vanishes and we are left with

-I_{V_{\xi}}\mathsf{f}_{r}=\int_{r}\epsilon^{a}\xi^{b}\delta\langle T_{ab}\rangle_{\psi}.

(117)

The above equation is a purely kinematical fact that is valid for all states
\psi
perturbatively around a fixed background. Once we perturbatively gauge fix the subregion
r
, we may expand the right hand side above in powers of
\lambda
and
G_{N}
using the expansions given in equations (
111
) and (
112
). In the following we will only consider state perturbations that are at most
O(\lambda)
while including back-reaction up to
O(G_{N}^{2})
, in which case it suffices to fix the location of the extremal surface up to
O(G_{N})

Hollands:2012sf
.

Let us now expand equation (
113

i.e.
at
O(\lambda)
) the location of the extremal surface does not change, and the change in the area is solely due to the change in the background metric. Moreover, the gravitational symplectic form term drops out since
\xi
is a Killing vector of the background metric. Then equation (

\langle K_{\text{CFT}}\rangle^{(1)}=\frac{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}}{4G_{N}}+\langle K_{\text{bulk}}\rangle^{(1)},

(118)

where we have identified
\langle K_{\text{bulk}}\rangle^{(1)}=\int_{r}\epsilon^{a}\xi^{b}\langle T_{ab}\rangle^{(1)}
as the linearized change in the bulk modular Hamiltonian associated to the matter state

S^{(1)}_{\text{CFT}}=\frac{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}}{4G_{N}}+S^{(1)}_{\text{bulk}},

(119)

which is precisely the FLM formula
Faulkner:2013ana
. At second order (
i.e.
at
O(\lambda^{2}G_{N})
) it is necessary to impose the Hollands-Wald gauge to ensure that the extremal surface does not fluctuate. Then, subtracting the

change in the bulk entanglement entropy from both sides of equation (
113
) we get

\langle K_{\text{CFT}}\rangle^{(2)}-S_{\text{gen}}^{(2)}=-(I_{V_{\xi}}\Omega^{\text{grav}}_{r})^{(2)}+\left(\langle K_{\text{bulk}}\rangle^{(2)}-S_{\text{bulk}}^{(2)}\right).

(120)

The quantum extremal surface (QES) prescription
Engelhardt:2014gca
instructs us to identify
S_{\text{gen}}^{(2)}
with
S_{\text{CFT}}^{(2)}
. Then, using the identity
S(\psi\|\omega)=\delta\langle K\rangle-\delta S
on both sides we get

S^{(2)}_{\text{CFT}}(\Psi\|\Omega)=-(I_{V_{\xi}}\Omega^{\text{grav}}_{r})^{(2)}+S^{(2)}_{\text{bulk}}(\psi\|\omega)

(121)

where
\Psi
is the boundary state dual to the bulk solution
(g_{ab},\psi)
, and
\Omega
is the CFT vacuum state. In the absence of matter, the bulk relative entropy term drops out and we recover the well-known duality between the gravitational canonical energy and the quantum Fisher information in the CFT
Lashkari:2015hha
.

5
Bulk vs. boundary Berry curvature in AdS/CFT

Consider a family of Euclidean path integral states
\Psi(\lambda^{i})
in a holographic CFT, where the
\lambda^{i}
are now sources for various single/multi-trace operators in the CFT. It is natural to define the Berry connection and curvature in the boundary CFT as:

\mathsf{A}_{\text{CFT}}=\langle\Psi|\delta\Psi\rangle,\;\;\mathsf{F}_{\text{CFT}}=\delta\mathsf{A}_{\text{CFT}}.

(122)

Ignoring non-perturbative effects, these states correspond to bulk states
\psi(\lambda^{i})
for the bulk matter fields, together with a backreacted metric
g_{ab}(\lambda^{i})
which solves the semi-classical Einstein equation. One can think of the metric as being in a coherent state with the semi-classical solution determining the expectation value of the metric, and thus denote the bulk states collectively as
|g_{ab},\psi\rangle
. The AdS/CFT dictionary gives a map
V
from the bulk semi-classical states
|g,\psi\rangle
to the boundary states
|\Psi\rangle
. The map should be an isometry by the AdS/CFT dictionary, up to non-perturbative corrections. Of course, the map
V
does not depend on the parameters
\lambda^{i}
. This immediately implies:

\mathsf{F}_{\text{CFT}}=\delta\langle\Psi|\delta\Psi\rangle=\delta\langle g,\psi|V^{\dagger}\delta\left(V|g,\psi\rangle\right)=\delta\langle g,\psi|\delta|g,\psi\rangle=\mathsf{F}_{\text{bulk}}.

(123)

The equality of bulk and boundary Berry curvatures is an almost trivial corollary of the AdS/CFT dictionary. In the classical limit, the bulk Berry curvature reduces to the symplectic 2-form on the bulk classical phase space, and one finds that the bulk symplectic form is dual to the boundary Berry curvature
Belin:2018fxe
. Beyond the classical limit, one must also account for quantum corrections in the bulk, and the bulk symplectic form naturally gets replaced – within the semi-classical approximation – by the sum of the gravitational symplectic form and the matter Berry curvature.

6
Discussion

In this work, we have proposed a natural generalization of the covariant phase space formalism to the case of semi-classical gravity. We end with some open questions and future directions:

1.

While we have defined a semi-classical symplectic form on the space of solutions to the semi-classical Einstein equation, it would be nice to find some applications for this formalism. In a companion paper
upcoming
, we will discuss one application of this formalism in understanding the Ryu-Takayanagi formula in the quantum regime
Engelhardt:2018kcs
from Lorentzian methods.

2.

Usually, the symplectic structure on phase space is a starting point for quantization. It is not clear to us that the semi-classical symplectic form defined here should play such a role in the quantization of the metric. It would be good to clarify this point further.

3.

Our discussion in the case of subregions was limited to small perturbations around a background solution. It would be good to generalize this beyond perturbation theory. Relatedly, it would be good to understand the boundary dual of the subregion semi-classical symplectic form in AdS/CFT (see
Kirklin:2019ror
;
deBoer:2025rxx
for some work along this direction).

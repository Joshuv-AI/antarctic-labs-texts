eDNA metabarcoding reveals differences in fish diversity and community structure in Danjiang River
Authors: Jie Deng, Xiaochun Zhang, Xueying Yao, Junhua Rao, Fei Dai, Han Wang, Yicong Wang, Wei Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Fish diversity, an important indicator of aquatic ecosystem health, is declining due to water pollution, overfishing, climate change, and invasive species. Effective surveying and monitoring are required to protect fish diversity. Here, a high-sensitivity environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding technique was used to investigate fish diversity in the Danjiang River, Shaanxi Province, China. In total, 59 species were identified in eight orders, 19 families, and 40 genera. Cypriniformes and Perciformes were the main groups in the survey area, while Cyprinidae accounted for 50.85% of the total fish species. Rhinogobius similis (19%), Hemibarbus umbrifer (11%), Gnathopogon herzensteini (10%), Triplophysa stewarti (8%), and Zacco platypus (7%) were the dominant species. Eight rare and two exotic fish species were identified. Combined with analysis of historical data, the richness of fish identified using eDNA metabarcoding was significantly higher than that of fish captured in ground cages. Temperature, pH, and oxidation-reduction potential are the main environmental factors that affect the spatial distribution of fish communities. These results suggest that eDNA metabarcoding could be a new tool with broad application prospects; however, local databases must be improved. This study provides theoretical data and a methodological reference for protecting and managing fish diversity in the Qinling Mountains.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80907-z.

Introduction

Fish are a key group that reveal water ecosystem functions, and fish diversity plays an important role in maintaining the health and stability of aquatic ecosystems1,2. In recent years, with human activities, climate change, invasive species, and other factors, global fish biodiversity has declined sharply, with over 50% of the fish fauna in the world’s 2,456 river under enormous pressure from human activities3,4. Between 95 and 290 freshwater fish species have become extinct5. According to the United States Fisheries Endangered Species Commission’s third Survey of Freshwater and Migratory Fish in North America, approximately 39% of continental fish species are at risk; there were 230 ecologically sensitive and vulnerable, 190 threatened, 280 endangered, and 61 extinct fish species6. Therefore, comprehensive conservation of current fish diversity is urgently needed.

The accuracy and comprehensiveness of fish population data are necessary for effective fish diversity conservation. However, traditional fish monitoring methods have disadvantages, such as habitat destruction, damage to target individuals, and low capture rates of rare and endangered species4,7. Therefore, a new, non destructive, and efficient biological investigation method, environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding, has been developed8–11. This involves using specific DNA barcode primers for PCR amplification of eDNA, a high-throughput sequencing platform for PCR products, and bioinformatics analysis to obtain the operational taxonomic unit (OTU). This is compared with a DNA barcode database to monitor target organisms. eDNA metabarcoding is increasingly being applied to species diversity surveys, rare endemic species, and exotic species monitoring12–14. However, because of significant geographical differences in fish and the fact that species classification information in public databases cannot be accurately verified, eDNA metabarcoding requires further study and evaluation for investigating specific rivers or river systems15.

The Danjiang River, previously known as Danshui, originates at the southern foot of the Qinling Mountains in northwest Shangluo City, Shaanxi Province. This is the upper reaches of the water source of the Middle Route of the South-to-North Water Diversion Project and has ecological protection significance16. As the longest tributary of the Han River in the Yangtze River system, its main stream is 390 km long and the Shaanxi section is 264 km long. It flows through Shaanxi, Henan, and Hubei provinces, joins the Han River in Danjiangkou City, Hubei Province, and flows into the Danjiangkou Reservoir. Early results showed that 38 fish species distributed throughout the Danjiang River basin, including 16 endemic and four exotic species in the upper reaches of the Yangtze River17. However, in recent years, there have been few studies on the composition and distribution of fish species in the upper reaches of the Danjiang River. Therefore, there is an urgent need to understand the status of fish resources in the basin using effective monitoring methods to protect regional biodiversity.

In this study, eDNA metabarcoding was used to investigate fish diversity in the Shaanxi section of the Danjiang River Basin, and the distribution characteristics of fish in the upper reaches of the Danjiang River were analysed in combination with historical literature and traditional fishing data. The main factors affecting fish diversity and distribution were discussed by integrating the measured environmental factors. The feasibility of eDNA technology for investigating fish diversity in the Qinling Mountains was verified and evaluated to provide a new technique for ecosystem restoration in the Yangtze River Basin and protecting fish diversity in the Qinling Mountains and Yangtze River.

Results

Sequencing quality statistics

The number of pairs of original eDNA-Mifish sequences (raw reads) of the 19 samples ranged from 3,527,207 to 9,390,868. The number of target reads containing amplified primers ranged from 59,796 to 61,117, accounting for > 92% of the pre-down-sample reads. After filtering short sequences, those with too many N bases, and those with too high an error expectation, the number of filtered reads ranged from 34,304 to 39,926. eDNA-mifish high-throughput sequencing carried out quality control before splicing, and the number of effective sequences for post-sequence analysis by removing chimeras ranged from 27,926 to 35,470. The effective sequence length for post-sequence analysis ranged from 200 to 400 bp, and the average sequence length was approximately 255 bp, consistent with the sequence length amplified by the selected primers. The Q20 and Q30 sequences were > 99% and 96%, respectively, indicating the relatively high accuracy of the eDNA-Mifish sequencing (Table 1).

Distribution of fish species and composition

In total, 469,819 sequence fragments were obtained using Mifish, of which 435,900 (93%) belonged to Actinopteri, indicating that this primer has strong universal application in fish. A total of 310 OTUs belonging to eight orders, 19 families, 40 genera, and 59 species were identified (Table 2). At the order level, Cypriniformes contained the largest number of fish species (39; 66.10%); the second most abundant group was Perciformes with 11 species (18.64%); Siluriformes ranked third, with four species accounting for 6.78%. At the family level, Cyprinidae had the largest number of fish species, with 30 species (50.85%); Cobitidae followed with nine species accounting for 15.25%; and Gobiidae ranked third, with seven species (11.86%). The top six species in terms of sequence number relative abundance were Rhinogobius similis (19%), Hemibarbus umbrifer (11%), Gnathopogon herzensteini (10%), Triplophysa stewarti (8%), Zacco platypus (7%), and Opsariichthys uncirostris (6%) (Fig. 1). In terms of the breadth of distribution, G. herzensteini was detected in 12 out of 15 (80%) water samples. The second most common species was Z. platypus (66.67%); Carassius auratus and O. uncirostris ranked third, with a detection rate of 60% (Table S1).

Alpha diversity analysis

In this study, the alpha diversity of fish in the survey area was analysed using seven indicators: the abundance-based coverage estimator (ACE) index, Chao1 index, phylogenetic diversity, Shannon’s index, Simpson’s index, Pielou’s evenness, and Good’s coverage (%). The ACE, Shannon, and Simpson’s indices ranged from 21 to 71, 1.8036 to 4.0274, and 0.4103 to 0.9153, respectively. The Pielou index ranged from 0.3489 to 0.8129, while phylogenetic diversity ranged from 1.3636 to 2.5276; the Shannon and Pielou indices at site J2 were significantly lower than those at other sampling points (P ≤ 0.05)(Table 3). There were no significant differences among the other sampling points (P > 0.05), indicating that the fish communities at J3, J1, and J4 had high richness and diversity and that the fish diversity structure was good. The coverage index range of each sampling point was 100, indicating that the sequencing covered all OTU sequences and reflected the real situation of the samples.

Beta diversity analysis and correlation with environmental factors

The Bray-Curtis algorithm was applied to construct a corresponding distance matrix; the difference index among samples was generally > 0.50, indicating that there were differences in the composition of fish communities among samples (Fig. 3). As J2 is a reservoir site, we set the reservoir and upstream waters J1 and J2 to be combined into the pool group, and the downstream J3 and J4 to be the river groups. Based on the Bray-Curtis distance matrix, a non-metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS) ranking of the fish community composition under different water types was conducted. Principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) was performed to determine the composition of fish communities in different river segments of all waters. Stress values based on NMDS analysis ranged from 0.05 to 0.1 (stress = 0.058855), indicating that NMDS analysis could accurately reflect the different degrees of fish community composition (Fig. 4A). Although there was some regional overlap among the samples from different water types, there was still a large distance between the sample points, indicating differences in fish community composition in the different water groups. In the PCoA, there were similarities in fish community composition among the J3, J4, and J1 river segments, whereas there were significant differences in fish community composition between J2 sampling points (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

Fish are an important component of aquatic ecosystems and they can be used as indicators to evaluate the biological integrity of water and have a significant impact on ecosystem balance. With the development of high-throughput sequencing, eDNA metabarcoding can identify species synchronously in large quantities directly from environmental or mixed samples and has been widely used in fish species composition and community structure analysis, as well as monitoring of spatial and temporal distribution changes in different water environments18–22.

In this study, eDNA metabarcoding detected 59 species belonging to eight orders, 19 families, and 40 genera. The relative abundances of Perciformes and Cypriniformes were higher, and Cyprinidae was the most abundant, with 30 species, accounting for 50.85% of the total fish species. Compared with historical data, there were similarities in taxon composition structure between the two methods, in which Hemibarbus labeo, O. uncirostris, and Z. platypus were all detected, directly demonstrating the effectiveness of eDNA metabarcoding. The number of species caught by eDNA metabarcoding was significantly higher than that by ground cages. Furthermore, we detected species not previously recorded, such as G. herzensteini, T. stewarti, and T. wuweiensis. This may be due to the accurate annotation and high sensitivity of eDNA metabarcoding, which allows for identifying closely related species and detecting low-concentration species19,23. However, eDNA metabarcoding did not identify species such as Cobitis sinensis, Glyptothorax sinense, and Sarcocheilichthys nigripinnis, small benthic fish that may have been missed due to shoal sampling. Stratified sampling could be increased in future studies to increase detection probability.

In terms of rare, protected fish, eight rare fish species were detected in the upper reaches of the Yangtze River. In particular, G. herzensteini, L. punctata, and Parabotia fasciatus, which were not detected in the ground cages, still had high monitoring frequency and sequence relative abundance according to eDNA metabarcoding. In addition, two exotic species, I. punctatus and M. salmoides, were detected, which presumably escaped from aquaculture farms around the reservoir or were illegally released by people. Therefore, eDNA metabarcoding plays an important role in the detection of rare and exotic fish invasions24–26, which is important for protecting the Yangtze River ecosystem.

The spatial distribution of organisms is a result of the long-term effects of environmental heterogeneity27,28. This survey found that the fish species detected at site J2 were mainly Perciformes, among which R. similis was the dominant species, with an average abundance of 70.78%. The other sampling sites were dominated by Cypriniformes, with G. herzensteini, Z. platypus, and O. uncirostris as the dominant species. According to field topographic analysis, J2 includes the Erlongshan Reservoir, which is static water with a wide surface, small and stable flow rate, and high water level. Therefore, the number of rapids decreased, while the number of hydrostatic fish increased correspondingly; small hydrostatic fish such as R. similis dominated, and fish diversity was low. The remaining sampling sites were river-like, and the fish species included Cyprinidae, Cobitidae, Bagridae, Siluridae, and Gobiidae. Sampling sites J1 and J3 are mountain streams and channels, which are affected by factors such as the relief and meandering of rivers, diverse flow patterns, complex and diverse habitats, significant differences in bottom conditions, and complex geomorphic units. Therefore, the species detected at these two sites also included rapid and hydrostatic fish, with high diversity. The construction of hydrojunctions has affected the living environments of fish29,30, such as water flow, nutrient dynamics, water quality, and temperature, which may be a reason for the differences in fish diversity and species distribution31.

Correlation analysis of environmental factors, water temperature, pH, and oxidation-reduction potential (ORP) showed that these were important factors affecting fish community composition. Water temperature affects the diversity and distribution characteristics of fish, mainly by affecting their survival and reproduction32,33. In this study, the water temperature in the main stream of Danjiang River was mostly below 20 °C, which may have delayed fish spawning. The pH and ORP of the water body represent water quality. If the pH is too high or low, the chemical content of the water changes, and chemicals are converted into toxic substances, which are unfavourable for fish growth and plankton reproduction; this also inhibits photosynthesis, affects dissolved oxygen in the water, and hinders fish respiration34,35. Recent studies have also shown that habitat duration and flow velocity are key factors affecting fish distribution36. Therefore, future studies should comprehensively evaluate the impact of environmental factors on fish distribution in the Danjiang River by measuring and analysing key factors, such as velocity and water depth, based on temporal and spatial changes to provide a reference for the protection of fish resources.

eDNA macrobarcoding is increasingly used in species diversity surveys, rare endemic species monitoring, and exotic species monitoring14,37–39. However, incomplete reference databases are a key problem causing the incomplete classification of fish species. At present, NCBI and MitoFish are the most commonly used public databases, from which > 3,000 fish mitochondrial DNA sequences can be retrieved40. However, taking the Cobitidae as an example, the largest group of cypriniformes is widely distributed throughout major water systems worldwide. Over 200 species and subspecies of 23 genera are known worldwide, including > 100 species and subspecies of 18 genera in China, some of which are endemic; only 48 species can be retrieved from MitoFish (version 4.02), indicating that population sequence data are insufficient. This may also be why the number of Cobitidae species in the eDNA results decreased compared to historical data. Although eDNA macrobarcoding has some challenges, its high sensitivity and non-destructive nature are obvious advantages for species identification and diversity assessment compared with traditional ground cages41,42. Therefore, local species databases should first be optimised and enriched43, an eDNA macrobarcoding monitoring system gradually established, and a small number of ground cage results integrated to monitor and protect fish diversity in the Yangtze River and Qinling Mountains.

Conclusion

In this study, eDNA metabarcoding was used to assess fish diversity and prove the feasibility of monitoring fish species composition and spatial distribution in the Danjiang River in Shaanxi. In total, 59 fish species belonging to eight orders, 19 families, and 40 genera were identified, including eight rare and two exotic species. Cypriniformes and Perciformes were the predominant fish groups, accounting for 69% and 23% of the total fish species, respectively. H. umbrifer, G. herzensteini, T. stewartia, and R. similis were the dominant species. eDNA metabarcoding detected significantly more species than traditional methods. From the perspective of spatial distribution, the J2 sampling site and the other main river types had a more diverse fish composition and more homogeneous population structure. The dominant species were G. herzensteini, T. stewarti, and O. uncirostris. J2 was a reservoir type site; the diversity and uniformity of fish composition were low, and R. similis in Percoformes was the dominant species. The differences in species composition were related to environmental factors, such as habitat temperature, PH, and ORP. Meanwhile, human-made water conservancy projects may also affect fish diversity and spatial distribution. For unannotated historical species, a non-invasive eDNA metabarcoding fish resource monitoring system can be gradually established by optimising and enriching the local species database in order to monitor and protect fish diversity in the Yangtze River and Qinling Mountains.

Methods

Sample site

Four sites were surveyed in the Danjiang River. Sites J1–J2 included the Erlongshan Reservoir, in the upper reaches of the Danjiang River, while J3–J4 were in the middle and lower reaches of the Danjiang River Basin in Shaanxi Province (Fig. 6). The internal distances ranged from 4.5 to 25.8 km for each site. All water samples were collected in September 2023. Ground cages and eDNA metabarcoding methods were used at each sampling point.

Water samples collection

At each site, 1 L surface water samples were collected and mixed in a sterile, sealable plastic bottle and filtered through a 0.45 μm nylon filter membrane with a vacuum peristaltic pump. Three duplicate water samples were collected from each site for eDNA analysis. All bottles and filtering equipment were thoroughly disinfected thrice with 10% sodium hypochlorite and DNA-free distilled water before use. Blank controls were established by filtering 1 L distilled water in the same manner to check for contamination during collection at each site. Filter membranes from each site were immediately placed into sterile 2 mL centrifuge tubes and stored at -20 °C for analysis44,45.

Capture fish by ground cages

Ground cages were the traditional method used to contrast eDNA metabarcoding in this study. This method was based on the relevant fish content in inland waters, according to the Technical Guidelines for Biodiversity Monitoring of the National Environmental Protection Standards of the People’s Republic of China. Three 5-m-long cages (mesh size 0.5–0.8 cm) were placed at each site approximately 5–10 m apart. Nets were cast in the evening (5–7 pm) and left for 24 h to maximise the detection probability. Water samples for eDNA and fish cage collection were collected simultaneously at each site. The collected fish were recorded and the species were identified on site. To prevent eDNA sample contamination, different investigators collected and identified the fish and sampled the water15.

Environmental factors collection

A total of 14 environmental factors were measured, including nine water quality factors: water temperature, pH, ORP, conductivity, dissolved oxygen (DO), and turbidity, which were measured using a multi-parameter water quality analyser (HORIBA U-52, Japan)46. The other three parameters, potassium permanganate index, ammonia nitrogen, and total nitrogen, were determined in the laboratory by acid potassium permanganate oxidation, Nesser’s reagent spectrophotometry, and alkaline potassium persulfate digestion ultraviolet spectrophotometry, respectively47,48. Five other factors were also recorded and classified: river depth, width, bed sediment type, vegetation type, and canopy density.

DNA extraction and metabarcoding

Extractions were performed in an independent laboratory. All supplies were disinfected with ultraviolet light and washed in advance with 10% sodium hypochlorite and DNA-free distilled water to eliminate DNA contamination. Each filter membrane was cut into pieces at room temperature (20–25 °C). A commercial DNA extraction kit, MGIEasy Stool Microbiome DNA Extraction Kit (MGI Tech, Wuhan, China), was used to extract eDNA from the water samples, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Finally, the DNA was eluted with 50 µL buffer and detected using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and nucleic acid analyser. Filtration blanks and negative controls were coextracted with the samples and subjected to the same protocol. The remaining DNA solution was stored at -20 °C for later use.

Metabarcoding was performed using the ATOPlex MiFish Library Prep Set (MGI Tech, Shenzhen, China) for library preparation. A two-step polymerase chain reaction was used. The first-step of PCR used the universal fish primers MiFish-F (5′-GTCGGTAAAACTCGTGCCAGC-3′) and MiFish-R (5′-CATAGTGGGGTATCTAATCCCAGTTTG-3′)49, which target the 12 S rDNA region (163–185 bp) of the mitochondrial genome, to identify fish species. For each sample, the PCR assay volume was 25 µL and included 12.5 µL PCR Enzyme Mix, 0.5 µL PCR Clean Enzyme, and 2 µL MiFish PCR Primer Pool. The PCR process was as follows: 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 30 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s, 65 °C for 10 s, and 72 °C for 10 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. The second step of PCR was performed to add a sequencing adapter and barcode primer according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After two-step PCR amplification, the PCR products were purified using DNA cleaning beads and detected using 1.0% agarose gel and the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Shanghai, China). Each sample was analysed in triplicate. Library construction and sequencing were performed at MGI Tech in Wuhan, China, using 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequencing on the DNBSEQ-G99 platform (MGI Tech, Wuhan, China)50.

High-throughputs sequencing and bioinformatics analysis

SeqKit software (version 2.4.0) was used to pre-intercept the sequencing data for each sample (pre-down-sampled reads). To reduce data processing time, the pre-down-sampled reads were set to 1.3 times the volume of data (down-sampled reads, Table S1) required for each primer. Simultaneously, the software used the proportional mode of fuzzy interception to improve efficiency, meaning the amount of pre-intercepted data was an unfixed fluctuating value. The target sequence was then extracted using Cutadapt software (4.2). If any valid sequence contained an amplification primer sequence at its front end, it was considered a target sequence and used for subsequent analysis. Next, seqtk software (1.3-r106) was used to extract the sequence accurately. A specific number of sequences were precisely extracted from the target sequence for subsequent analyses. If the number of target sequences was insufficient, all target sequences were used. Finally, Figaro (1.1.2) and DADA2 (1.26.0) software were used for quality control, denoising, combination, and chimera removal. Deduplicate sequences generated by DADA2 quality assurance were considered amplicon sequence variants (ASVs). ASVs were equivalent to OTUs with 100% similarity clustering. ASVs with < 20 reads were excluded from analysis. Taxonomy was assigned using databases downloaded from NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and MitoFish (https://mitofish.aori.u-tokyo.ac.jp/, version 4.02). ASV classification was performed using the QIIME 2 (2023.2) q2-feature-classifier plugin, setting the sequence similarity threshold at 99%, GC content, and Q20 and Q30 statistics for the sequences filtered as low quality.

The alpha diversity of fish was quantified using Shannon and Simpson indices; richness was assessed with Chao index, ACE; evenness was evaluated by Pielou’s index; and cover degree was measured using Good’s coverage (%) and QIIME 2 (2023.2) project software. The taxonomy dataset for each sample was used to calculate the observed species and Bray-Curtis indices, which were then employed to create NMDS plots using the vegan 2.3_5 package in R version 4.1.3. Correlations between fish communities and environmental factors were calculated using redundancy analysis. The significance of the differences between the two sets of data was assessed using t-tests.

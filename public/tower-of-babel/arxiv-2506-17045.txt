On bivariate Archimax copulas: Level sets, mass distributions and related results
Nicolas Pascal Dietrich

Abstract
Motivated by the results in ( Mai and Scherer, 2011 ; Trutschnig et al., 2016 ) , which examine the way bivariate Extreme Value copulas distribute their mass, we extend these findings to the larger family of bivariate Archimax copulas 𝒞 a ​ m \mathcal{C}_{am} . Working with Markov kernels (conditional distributions), we analyze the mass distributions of Archimax copulas C ∈ 𝒞 a ​ m C\in\mathcal{C}_{am} and show that the support of C C is determined by some functions f 0 f^{0} , g L g^{L} and g R g^{R} . Additionally, we prove that the discrete component (if any) of C C concentrates its mass on the graphs of certain convex functions f s f^{s} or non-decreasing functions g t g^{t} . Investigating the level sets L t L^{t} of Archimax copulas C ∈ 𝒞 a ​ m C\in\mathcal{C}_{am} , we establish that these sets can also be characterized in terms of the afore-mentioned functions f s f^{s} and g t g^{t} . Furthermore, recognizing the close relationship between the level sets L t L^{t} of a copula C C and its Kendall distribution function F C K F_{C}^{K} , we provide an alternative proof for the representation of F C K F_{C}^{K} for arbitrary Archimax copulas C ∈ 𝒞 a ​ m C\in\mathcal{C}_{am} and derive simple expressions for the level set masses μ C ​ ( L t ) \mu_{C}(L^{t}) . Building upon the fact that Archimax copulas C ∈ 𝒞 a ​ m C\in\mathcal{C}_{am} can be represented via two univariate probability measures γ \gamma and ϑ \vartheta — so-called Williamson and Pickands dependence measures — we show that absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity properties of these measures γ \gamma and ϑ \vartheta carry over to the corresponding Archimax copula C γ , ϑ C_{\gamma,\vartheta} . Finally, we derive conditions on γ \gamma and ϑ \vartheta such that the support of the absolutely continuous, discrete or singular component of C γ , ϑ C_{\gamma,\vartheta} coincides with the support of C γ , ϑ C_{\gamma,\vartheta} .



Keywords
: Archimax copula, Extreme Value copula, Archimedean copula, Markov kernel, Pickands dependence function

1
Introduction

Examining maxima of independent, identically distributed sequences of random variables, Extreme Value copulas (EVCs) arise naturally and are a well-suited tool for modeling dependence between extreme events. Including various parametric sub-families, such as Gumbel–Hougaard and Galambos copulas
(
Gudendorf and Segers, 2011
)
, EVCs are applied in numerous fields, notably finance
(
Longin and Solnik, 2001
;
McNeil et al., 2005
)
and hydrology
(
Salvadori, 2007
)
. Moreover, EVCs are also theoretically appealing, since they are characterized by a single univariate function
A
.
To be more precise, according to
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
;
Haan and Resnick, 1977
;
Nelsen, 2006
;
Pickands, 1981
)
a copula
B
is an EVC if, and only if there exists a Pickands dependence function
A
, i.e., a convex function
A\colon[0,1]\rightarrow[\frac{1}{2},1]
fulfilling
\max\{1-x,x\}\leq A(x)\leq 1
for every
x\in[0,1]
such that the identity

B(x,y)=\exp\left((\log(x)+\log(y))A\left(\frac{\log(x)}{\log(x)+\log(y)}\right)\right)

holds for every
x,y\in(0,1)
. The family of EVCs has been the subject of extensive study, yielding key results on convergence, mass distributions, and other analytic and measure-theoretic properties
(
Kasper et al., 2021
;
Mai and Scherer, 2011
;
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
.

Considering so-called Archimedean generators
\psi
(defined in Section
2.2.1
) now allows to naturally extend the family of EVCs to the much larger family of Archimax copulas. Following
(
Capéraà et al., 2000
)
, a copula
C
is called an Archimax copula if, and only if there exists a convex Archimedean generator
\psi\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
and a Pickands dependence function
A\colon[0,1]\rightarrow[\frac{1}{2},1]
such that

C(x,y)=\psi\left((\varphi(x)+\varphi(y))A\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)

holds for every
x,y\in(0,1)
. It was proved in
(
Capéraà et al., 2000
)
and later in
(
Durante et al., 2018
)
that a function
C
, defined in the afore-mentioned way indeed constitutes a bona fide copula.
As the name suggests, the family of Archimax copulas
\mathcal{C}_{am}
encompasses both the family of EVCs
\mathcal{C}_{ev}
and the family of Archimedean copulas
\mathcal{C}_{ar}
as sub-families and can therefore be seen as an extension of these two families of copulas. For more background on Archimedean copulas and their applications, we refer to
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Nelsen, 2006
;
Kasper et al., 2024
)
.
Viewing Archimax copulas from an application perspective, they offer the flexibility to model asymptotic dependence in extreme events while simultaneously capturing joint risks at moderate levels
(
Chatelain et al., 2020
)
. For further research in the field of bivariate Archimax copulas we refer to
(
Bacigál et al., 2011
;
Capéraà et al., 2000
;
Genest and Jaworski, 2021
)
. Moreover, an extension to the multivariate setting was considered in
(
Charpentier et al., 2014
;
Mesiar and Jágr, 2013
)
. Regarding inference, an estimator for a multivariate, semi-parametric framework was provided in
(
Chatelain et al., 2020
)
.

While it has already been established how Archimedean copulas and EVCs distribute their mass
(
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Kasper et al., 2024
;
Mai and Scherer, 2011
;
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
, and the level sets of Archimedean copulas have been investigated in
(
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Kasper et al., 2024
)
, comparable results for the larger family of Archimax copulas
\mathcal{C}_{am}
remain, to the best of the author’s knowledge, unstudied. The current paper addresses this gap by analyzing the mass distributions and level sets of bivariate Archimax copulas, providing several new insights and related findings.

Motivated by the fact that the level sets of Archimedean copulas can be represented by convex functions
(
Nelsen, 2006
)
, we prove that the level sets of Archimax copulas can similarly be characterized by two functions
f^{s}
and
g^{t}
(specified in Section
3
) for some
s,t\in[0,1)
. Moreover, we derive conditions on the Pickands dependence function
A
, such that the level sets
L^{t}
of the corresponding
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
coincide with the graphs of functions
f^{t}
and show that these functions are convex.

Building upon the fact that both Archimedean copulas and EVCs can be characterized by univariate probability measures, so-called Williamson measures
\gamma
and Pickands dependence measures
\vartheta
, respectively
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
;
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Pickands, 1981
)
, we demonstrate that Archimax copulas can similarly be defined via these measures. Taking this into consideration and providing an explicit representation of (a version of) the Markov kernel
K_{C}
of
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
, we study the support of
C
and show how
C
distributes its mass. To be more precise, we prove that if either of the measures
\gamma
or
\vartheta
has full support, the support of
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
is fully characterized by the afore-mentioned functions
f^{s}
and
g^{t}
for some
s,t\in[0,1)
.
Furthermore, we derive conditions on
\gamma
and
\vartheta
such that
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
has full support and establish that the discrete component (as specified in Section
2.1
and if non-degenerated) of
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}
concentrates its mass on the graphs of functions
f^{s}
or
g^{t}
for some
s,t\in[0,1)
. As direct consequence of this result, we establish that
\gamma
or
\vartheta
has a point mass if, and only if the corresponding
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
has non-degenerated discrete component.

Given that the level sets
L^{t}
of a copula
C
are closely related to its Kendall distribution function
F_{C}^{K}
, working with Markov kernels again, we provide an alternative proof for the representation of
F_{C}^{K}
in the case that
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}

(
Capéraà et al., 2000
)
. Building upon this representation then allows for a simple calculation of the level set masses
\mu_{C}(L^{t})
of
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
.

Triggered by the fact that for both Archimedean copulas and Extreme Value copulas
C
, absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity of
C
(in the sense specified in Section
2.1
) is closely related to absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity of the corresponding Williamson measure
\gamma
or Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta
(singularity in
the sense that the measure
\gamma
(or
\vartheta
) has no point masses and its corresponding distribution function
F_{\gamma}
(or
F_{\vartheta}
) has derivative
0
almost everywhere)
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2024
;
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
;
Kasper et al., 2024
)
, we prove that in the setting of Archimax copulas absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity of
\gamma
and
\vartheta
carries over to the corresponding
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Finally, we show that if the Williamson measure
\gamma
is absolutely continuous, has full support, and some minor regularity assumption on the Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta
(specified in Section
2.2.2
) is satisfied, then the support of the absolutely continuous component of the corresponding
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
coincides with the support of
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}
. Analogous results are then established for the discrete and the singular components of
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}
. The afore-mentioned finding generalizes the result in
(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
, Corollary 5)
, saying that when
C
is an EVC and is not the Fréchet-Hoeffding upper bound
M
, the support of
C
coincides with the support of the absolutely continuous component of
C
.

The reminder of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
contains notations and definitions used throughout this work and is divided into two parts. The first subsection presents general notation and definitions, while
the second revisits key properties of Archimedean and Extreme Value copulas, emphasizing their connections to Williamson and Pickands dependence measures. Additionally, Archimax copulas are introduced and it is established that they can be represented by Williamson and Pickands dependence measures. Section
3
studies the level sets of Archimax copulas. After showing that the level sets can be characterized using functions
f^{s}
and
g^{t}
for some
s,t\in[0,1)
, we prove that the functions
f^{s}
are convex and that the functions
g^{t}
are non-decreasing. In Section
4
a version of the Markov kernel
K_{C}
for a given Archimax copula
C
is provided and the support of
C
, and its relationship to the corresponding Williamson measure
\gamma
and Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta
is studied.
Showing that the discrete component of
C
(if any) always concentrates its mass on graphs of functions
f^{s}
or
g^{t}
for some
s,t\in[0,1)
, it is proved that the discrete component of
C
is non-degenerated if, and only if
\gamma
or
\vartheta
has a point mass. In Section
5
an explicit representation for the Kendall distribution function
F_{C}^{K}
of an Archimax copula
C
is provided and this representation is used to calculate the level set mass
\mu_{C}(L^{t})
of
C
. What is more, the mass
\mu_{C}(\Gamma(g^{t}))
of the graph
\Gamma(g^{t})
is calculated for every
t\in(0,1)
. Finally, Section
6
studies absolute continuity/discreteness/singularity of Archimax copulas
C
and the support of the absolutely continuous/discrete/singular component of
C
is investigated.

To enhance readability, certain technical lemmas and proofs have been deferred to the Appendix. Additionally, several examples and graphics are provided to clarify the selected procedures and convey some of the underlying concepts.

2
Notation and preliminaries

2.1
General notation

Throughout this contribution we will denote by
\mathcal{C}
the family of all bivariate copulas and
\mathbb{I}:=[0,1]
will denote the unit interval. For a given random vector
(X,Y)
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and some copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
, we will write
(X,Y)\sim C
if
C
is the joint distribution function of
(X,Y)
restricted to
\mathbb{I}^{2}
. The doubly stochastic measure corresponding to the copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
will be denoted by
\mu_{C}
, i.e.,
\mu_{C}([0,x]\times[0,y]):=C(x,y)

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
.
Considering an arbitrary topological space
(S,\tau)
, the Borel
\sigma
-field on
S
will be denoted by
\mathcal{B}(S)
, and
\mathcal{P}(S)
denotes the class of all probability measures on
\mathcal{B}(S)
. Furthermore, for a given measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{B}(S)
, the support of
\nu
is the complement of the union of all open sets
U
, which fulfill
\nu(U)=0
and will be denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)
.
In this article, the support of a copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
is defined as the support of its corresponding doubly stochastic measure
\mu_{C}
. For further information on copulas and doubly stochastic measures, see
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
;
Nelsen, 2006
)
.
We denote the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
by
\lambda_{2}
and write
\lambda
for the univariate Lebesgue measure. Moreover, for an arbitrary
x\in S
, the Dirac measure in
x\in S
will be denoted by
\delta_{x}
.
Let
(S,d)
and
(S^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be two metric spaces,
T:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
be a Borel-measurable transformation and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(S)
be a probability measure. Then the push-forward measure
\nu
under
T
, denoted by
\nu^{T}
, is defined by

\nu^{T}(F):=\nu(T^{-1}(F))
for every
F\in\mathcal{B}(S^{\prime})
.

In the sequel, conditional distributions and Markov kernels will be key concepts.
A map
K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is called a
Markov kernel
from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
if, and only if the function
x\mapsto K(x,E)
is Borel-measurable for every
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
and the map
E\mapsto K(x,E)
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
We call
K
a sub-Markov kernel (or simply sub-kernel) if, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the measure
E\mapsto K(x,E)
only satisfies
K(x,\mathbb{I})\leq 1
(instead of
K(x,\mathbb{I})=1
).

Given a random vector
(X,Y)
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, we will call a Markov kernel
K(\cdot,\cdot)
a
regular conditional distribution
of

Y
given
X
if for every set
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
the equation

K(X(\omega),E)=\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1}_{E}\circ Y|X)(\omega)

holds for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
.
It is a well known result that for every random vector
(X,Y)
, there exists a regular conditional
distribution
K(\cdot,\cdot)
of
Y
given
X
, which is unique for
\mathbb{P}^{X}
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the case that
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
(X,Y)\sim C
, we will denote by
K_{C}:\mathbb{I}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})\to\mathbb{I}
(a version of) the regular conditional
distribution of
Y
given
X
and call it the
Markov kernel
of
C
.
Considering
x\in\mathbb{I}
, we define the
x
-section
G_{x}
of a given set
G\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
by
G_{x}:=\{y\in\mathbb{I}:(x,y)\in G\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
. Using
disintegration
(see
(
Kallenberg, 2002
, Section 5)
and
(
Klenke, 2008
, Section 8)
), the subsequent identity holds for all
G\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
:

\displaystyle\mu_{C}(G)=\int_{\mathbb{I}}K_{C}(x,G_{x})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x).

(1)

Considering
d\in\{1,2\}
, in this contribution, a measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{B}(S)
, whereby
S=\mathbb{I}^{d}
or
S=[0,\infty)
, is referred to as singular (w.r.t.
\lambda_{d}
or
\lambda
)
if, and only if it has no point-masses and there exists a set
G\in\mathcal{B}(S)
such that

\lambda_{d}(G)=0
and
\nu(G)=\nu(S)
. For the sake of simplicity, we will use the term ’singular’ for the afore-mentioned measures, rather than the longer expression ’singular without point-masses’. This aligns with the concept of singular distribution functions and is justified by the following straightforward observation:
The doubly stochastic measure
\mu_{C}
corresponding to the copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
has no point masses and therefore always has degenerated discrete component. Following the same approach as in
(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
;
Kasper et al., 2024
)
and decomposing the Markov kernel
K_{C}
corresponding to
C
,
we obtain a decomposition of the measure
\mu_{C}
into three components, which may not be degenerated.
To be more precise, we denote by
K_{C}^{abs},K_{C}^{dis},K_{C}^{sing}:\mathbb{I}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
the absolutely continuous, discrete and singular sub-kernels of
K_{C}
, respectively, and obtain, according to
(
Lange, 1973
)
, that

K_{C}(x,F)=K_{C}^{abs}(x,F)+K_{C}^{dis}(x,F)+K_{C}^{sing}(x,F)

(2)

holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and every
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
. Denoting by
\mathcal{k}_{C}
the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mu_{C}
w.r.t.
\lambda_{2}
(almost everywhere), uniqueness of
K_{C}
yields that the measures
K_{C}^{abs}(x,\cdot)
and
E\mapsto\int_{E}\mathcal{k}_{C}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)
coincide for
\lambda
-almost every
x\in\mathbb{I}

(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
.
Applying eq. (
2
) and using disintegration, we define the absolutely continuous, the discrete and the singular components
\mu_{C}^{abs}
,
\mu_{C}^{dis}
and
\mu_{C}^{sing}
of
\mu_{C}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{C}^{abs}(E\times F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{E}K_{C}^{abs}(x,F)\mathrm{d}\lambda(x),

\displaystyle\mu_{C}^{dis}(E\times F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{E}K_{C}^{dis}(x,F)\mathrm{d}\lambda(x),

\displaystyle\mu_{C}^{sing}(E\times F)

\displaystyle:=\int_{E}K_{C}^{sing}(x,F)\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

(3)

for every
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
and every
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
. We extend
\mu_{C}^{abs}
,
\mu_{C}^{dis}
and
\mu_{C}^{sing}
to full

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
in the standard way.
In this contribution a copula
C
will be called absolutely continuous, discrete or singular if, and only if
\mu_{C}^{abs}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
,
\mu_{C}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
or
\mu_{C}^{sing}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
holds. Furthermore, we will denote
\mu_{C}^{abs}
,
\mu_{C}^{dis}
,
\mu_{C}^{sing}
as the absolutely continuous, the discrete, and the singular components of
C
(or
\mu_{C}
), respectively.

It is important to note that, when considering a copula
C
, the singular component
\mu_{C}^{sing}
of
\mu_{C}
does not correspond to the usual definition of the singular component of
\mu_{C}
(in the sense that there exists some
N\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
such that
\mu_{C}^{sing}(N)=\mu_{C}^{sing}(\mathbb{I}^{2})
and
\lambda_{2}(N)=0
). For instance, when
C=M
(
M
being the Fréchet-Hoeffding upper bound), its corresponding Markov kernel
K_{C}
is the Dirac measure and is therefore discrete, implying that
\mu_{C}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
. In contrast, according to the standard definition,
C
would be considered singular.

Given a copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and a random vector
(X,Y)\sim C
, then according to
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
, Definition 3.9.5)
the Kendall distribution function of
C
is defined by

F_{C}^{K}(t):=\mathbb{P}(C(X,Y)\leq t)

(4)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Moreover, following

(
Genest and Rivest, 1993
)
, Kendall’s tau of
C
is defined by

\tau(C):=4\int_{\mathbb{I}^{2}}C(s,t)\mathrm{d}\mu_{C}(s,t)-1.

(5)

Furthermore, we will refer to a function
G\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
as distribution function/measure generating function, if its extension, in the sense that
G(x)=0
for every
x<0
and
G(x)=1
for every
x>1
, is a distribution function/measure generating function. Similarly, we call a function
H\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
a distribution function, if its extension via setting
H(x)=0
for every
x<0
is a distribution function.
Given an arbitrary interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a function
u\colon I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we will call
u
singular if, and only if there exists a set
N\in\mathcal{B}(I)
with
\lambda(N)=0
such that for all
x\in I\setminus N
the derivative
u^{\prime}(x)
exists,
u^{\prime}(x)=0
holds and

u
is non-constant on
I
. Moreover, (assuming their existence) the right-hand/left-hand derivative of
u
will be denoted by
D^{+}u
and
D^{-}u
, respectively. Considering some set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a function
v\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, emphasizing that the right-hand/left-hand derivative is taken w.r.t. a specific variable (and assuming their existence in
(x,y)\in E
), we will sometimes write
\partial_{x}^{+}v(x,y)
or
\partial_{x}^{-}v(x,y)
for the right-hand/left-hand partial derivative of
v
in
(x,y)\in E
. Given a set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, its complement will be denoted by
F^{c}
and the graph of a function
u\colon F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
\Gamma(u)
.
Finally, we note that throughout this contribution we consistently use the convention that
a\cdot\infty=\infty
,
\frac{a}{\infty}=0
and
\frac{a}{0}=\infty
for every
a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

2.2
Archimedean, Extreme Value and Archimax copulas

2.2.1
Archimedean copulas

In the sequel, a non-increasing and continuous function
\psi\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is called an Archimedean generator if, and only if it fulfills
\psi(0)=1
,
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\psi(z)=0=:\psi(\infty)
and it is
strictly decreasing on
[0,\inf\{x\colon\psi(x)=0\})
. Throughout this contribution, we will refer to Archimedean generators simply as generators. Moreover, we define the pseudo-inverse
\varphi\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
of a generator
\psi
by
\varphi(y):=\inf\{z\in[0,\infty]\colon\psi(z)=y\}
for every
y\in\mathbb{I}
. It can be easily proved that
\varphi
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1]
,
right-continuous at
0
and satisfies the condition
\varphi(1)=0

(
Kasper et al., 2024
)
.
A generator
\psi
, or its pseudo-inverse
\varphi
, is referred to as strict if
\varphi(0)=\infty
and otherwise as non-strict. Note, that strictness of
\psi
is equivalent to the fact that
\psi(z)>0
for every
z\in[0,\infty)
.
A copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
is called Archimedean if, and only if there exists a generator
\psi
with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
such that

C(x,y):=\psi\left(\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)\right)

(6)

holds for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
.
Emphasizing the correspondence between generator
\psi
and copula

C
, we occasionally use
C_{\psi}
instead of
C
in the following. The class of all bivariate Archimedean copulas will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{ar}
.
Furthermore, given that
\psi
is a generator, applying
(
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
, Proposition 2.1)
, the function
C_{\psi}
defined according to eq. (
6
) is a bivariate Archimedean copula if, and only if
\psi
is convex.
Convexity of
\psi
ensures the existence of both
D^{-}\psi
and
D^{+}\psi
on
(0,\infty)
. According to
(
Kannan and Krueger, 1996
, Theorem 3.7.4)
and
(
Pollard, 2001
, Appendix C)
, the one-sided derivatives coincide for all but at most countably many points in
(0,\infty)
and
D^{-}\psi(z)=D^{+}\psi(z)
holds for every point of continuity
z
of
D^{-}\psi
.
Furthermore, as shown in
(
Kasper et al., 2024
)
, every convex generator
\psi
fulfills
D^{-}\psi(\infty):=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}D^{-}\psi(z)=0
and
D^{-}\psi(z)=0
holds for every
z>\varphi(0)
. Finally, note that if
\psi
is a convex generator also its pseudo-inverse
\varphi
is convex.
Throughout this article the family of all convex generators will be denoted by
\mathbf{\Psi}
.

Following
(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Schilling et al., 2012
)
, Archimedean copulas can also be obtained using univariate probability measures
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}([0,\infty))
fulfilling
\gamma(\{0\})=0
, so-called Williamson measures. We define the family of all such measures by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}([0,\infty))\colon\gamma(\{0\})=0\}.

Considering
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
, we use the convention that
\gamma(\{\infty\})=0
.
Throughout this article we denote the family of all absolutely continuous/discrete and singular Williamson measures by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{abs}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{dis}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{sing}
, respectively.
According to
(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Schilling et al., 2012
)
there is a one-to-one correspondence between the family of convex generators
\mathbf{\Psi}
and the family of Williamson measures
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
. To be more precise, for every
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
, there exists a unique
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
such that

\psi(z)=\int_{[0,\infty)}(1-tz)_{+}\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)

(7)

holds for every
z>0

(
Kasper et al., 2024
, Theorem 5.1)
. Moreover, if
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
is its corresponding generator, then, according to
(
Kasper et al., 2024
)
, the following representation of the left-hand derivative
D^{-}\psi
of
\psi
in terms of
\gamma
holds for every
z>0
:

D^{-}\psi(z)=-\int_{[0,\frac{1}{z}]}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t).

(8)

Finally, we refer to
\gamma
as strict if its support contains the point
0
and otherwise as non-strict. It has been established in
(
Kasper et al., 2024
, Lemma 5.5)
that
\gamma
is strict if, and only if its corresponding generator
\psi
is strict. If
\gamma
is non-strict and
\varphi
is the pseudo-inverse of the corresponding generator
\psi
, then
\gamma([0,z])=0
holds for every
z<\frac{1}{\varphi(0)}

(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2024
)
.

2.2.2
Extreme Value Copulas

Given a copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
, we call
C
an Extreme Value copula (EVC) if there exists a
B\in\mathcal{C}
such that

C(x,y)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B^{n}(x^{\frac{1}{n}},y^{\frac{1}{n}})

holds for every
x,y\in\mathbb{I}
. Throughout this contribution we denote the family of all bivariate EVCs by
\mathcal{C}_{ev}
. Furthermore, as established in
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
;
Haan and Resnick, 1977
;
Nelsen, 2006
;
Pickands, 1981
)
, the following assertions are equivalent:

1.

C\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}

2.

C
is max-stable, i.e.,
C(x,y)=C^{k}(x^{\frac{1}{k}},y^{\frac{1}{k}})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{I}
.

3.

There exists a Pickands dependence function
A
, i.e., a convex function
A\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
fulfilling
\max\{1-x,x\}\leq A(x)\leq 1
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
, such that

C(x,y)=(xy)^{A\left(\frac{\log(x)}{\log(xy)}\right)}

(9)

holds for all
x,y\in(0,1)
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{A}
the family of all Pickands dependence functions and let
C_{A}
denote the unique EVC induced by
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
Convexity implies that for every Pickands dependence function
A\in\mathcal{A}
the right-hand derivative
D^{+}A(x)
exists for every
x\in[0,1)
and the left-hand derivative
D^{-}A(x)
exists for every
x\in(0,1]
. Moreover,
D^{+}A(x)=D^{-}A(x)
holds for all but at most countably many
x\in(0,1)
and therefore
A
is differentiable on the complement of a countable set in
(0,1)
. Furthermore,
D^{+}A
is non-decreasing and right-continuous on
[0,1)
and
D^{-}A
is non-decreasing and
left-continuous on
(0,1]

(
Kannan and Krueger, 1996
;
Pollard, 2001
)
.
We define
D^{+}A(1):=D^{-}A(1)
and therefore obtain that
D^{+}A
is a non-decreasing and right-continuous function on the interval
\mathbb{I}
. Considering that
\max\{1-x,x\}\leq A(x)\leq 1
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
, it follows that
D^{+}A(x)\in[-1,1]
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
.

Following
(
Beirlant et al., 2004
;
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
;
Haan and Resnick, 1977
;
Pickands, 1981
)
, EVCs can also be characterized via probability measures
\vartheta
on
\mathbb{I}
with expected value
\mathbb{E}(\vartheta):=\int_{\mathbb{I}}x\mathrm{d}\vartheta(x)=\frac{1}{2}
, so-called Pickands dependence measures. Throughout this paper we define the family of all such measures by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\left\{\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{I})\colon\int_{\mathbb{I}}x\mathrm{d}\vartheta(x)=\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

We define the family of all absolutely continuous, discrete and singular Pickands dependence measures by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{abs}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{dis}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{sing}
, respectively.
It is well known that the Pickands dependence function associated with the Fréchet-Hoeffding upper bound
M
is given by
A_{M}(t):=\max\{1-t,t\}
for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
and that the Pickands dependence function associated with the independence copula
\Pi
is defined by
A_{\Pi}(t):=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Furthermore, it is straightforward to see that the Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta_{M}:=\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}
corresponds to
M
and that the measure

\vartheta_{\Pi}:=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{0}+\delta_{1})
corresponds to
\Pi
.
According to
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
)
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the family of all Pickands dependence functions
\mathcal{A}
and the family of all Pickands dependence measures
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
. To be more precise, given
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and applying
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
, Lemma Appendix A.2)
, the Pickands dependence function
A
corresponding to
\vartheta
is given by

A(t)=1-t+2\int_{[0,t]}\vartheta([0,z])\mathrm{d}\lambda(z),

(10)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Let
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and denote by
A\in\mathcal{A}
its corresponding Pickands dependence function. Using
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
, Lemma 4.3)
, the right-hand derivative
D^{+}A
of
A
can be represented in terms of the Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta
. The following identity holds for every
t\in[0,1)
:

D^{+}A(t)=2\vartheta([0,t])-1.

(11)

Following
(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
and considering
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we define

L:=\max\{s\in\mathbb{I}\colon A(s)=1-s\}\text{ and }R:=\min\{s\in\mathbb{I}\colon A(s)=s\}.

(12)

As established in
(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
)
, eq. (
12
) can also be expressed in terms of the corresponding
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
:

L=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{I}\colon\vartheta([0,x])=0\}\text{ and }R=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{I}\colon\vartheta([0,x])=1\},

(13)

with the convention
\sup\emptyset:=0
and
\inf\emptyset:=1
.
Furthermore, considering
A\in\mathcal{A}
we define the function
h_{A}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow[1,\infty]
by

h_{A}(t):=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if }t=0,\\
\frac{A(t)}{t},&\text{if }t\in(0,1]\end{cases}

(14)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Let
L
,
R
be defined as in eq. (
12
), then assuming
L>0
and using the fact that
A(t)=1-t
for every
t\in[0,L]
implies that
h_{A}(t)=\frac{1-t}{t}
for every
t\in(0,L]
. What is more, considering
t\in[R,1]
and applying that
A(t)=t
, obviously yields
h_{A}(t)=1
. Lastly, since
A(t)\geq\max\{1-t,t\}
holds for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
, we conclude that
h_{A}(t)\geq\frac{\max\{1-t,t\}}{t}
for every
t\in(0,1]
.

According to Lemma
2.1
the function
h_{A}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\inf\{x\in\mathbb{I}\colon h_{A}(x)=1\})=(0,R)
and therefore invertible on
(0,R)
with inverse function
h_{A}^{-1}
defined on
(1,\infty)
. Considering that
h_{A}(s)=1
holds for every
s\in[R,1]
, we define the pseudo-inverse
h_{A}^{[-1]}\colon[1,\infty]\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
of
h_{A}
by

h_{A}^{[-1]}(z):=\begin{cases}R,&\text{if }z=1,\\
h_{A}^{-1}(z),&\text{if }z\in(1,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }z=\infty,\end{cases}

for every
z\in[1,\infty]
. Note, that
h_{A}^{[-1]}(z)=h_{A}^{-1}(z)
holds for every
z\in[1,\infty)
in the case that
R=1
. Building upon the afore-mentioned properties of the function
h_{A}
and again assuming that
L>0
, it is straightforward to see that
h_{A}^{[-1]}(z)=\frac{1}{z+1}
holds for every
z\in\left[\frac{1-L}{L},\infty\right)
. Using that
h_{A}(t)\geq\frac{1-t}{t}
for every
t\in(0,1]
immediately implies
h_{A}^{[-1]}(z)\geq\frac{1}{z+1}
for every
z\in[1,\infty)
.

Similar to
(
Fuchs and Tschimpke, 2023
;
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
, fixing
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we define the function
G_{A}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
by

G_{A}(t):=\begin{cases}A(t)+D^{+}A(t)(1-t),&\text{if }t\in[0,1),\\
1,&\text{if }t=1\end{cases}

(15)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
.
Concerning the regularity (in the sense of monotonicity and convexity) of the functions defined in this section, we prove the following lemma. The lemma serves as an auxiliary result, used to establish the findings of the next sections. For the sake of readability, the proof is provided in the Appendix.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
h_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
14
) and let
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
). Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

h_{A}
is a non-increasing, convex function on
(0,1]
, which is strictly decreasing on
(0,R]
and fulfills
h_{A}(t)=1
for every
t\in[R,1]
;

(ii)

h_{A}^{[-1]}
is a strictly decreasing and convex function on
[1,\infty)
;

(iii)

G_{A}
is a non-negative, non-decreasing and right-continuous function on
\mathbb{I}
, which is positive on
(L,1]
. Furthermore, if
L>0
,
G_{A}(t)=0
holds for every
t\in[0,L)
and
G_{A}(t)=1
holds for every
t\in[R,1]
.

Proof.

See Appendix
7.2
.
∎

h_{A}
and
h_{A}^{[-1]}
for a fixed

.

2.2.3
Archimax copulas

Archimax copulas arise when combining convex generators
\psi
and Pickands dependence functions
A
. To be more precise, a copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
is called an Archimax copula, if there exists a generator
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
and a Pickands dependence function
A\in\mathcal{A}
such that

C(x,y)=\psi\left((\varphi(x)+\varphi(y))A\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)

(16)

holds for every
(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}
. It can be shown that a function defined as in eq. (
16
) is indeed a bona fide copula
(
Capéraà et al., 2000
;
Durante et al., 2018
)
.
Stressing the relationship between
\psi
,
A
and the corresponding copula
C
, we frequently write
C_{\psi,A}
instead of
C
throughout this article.
The class of all bivariate Archimax copulas is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Recalling the definition given in eq. (
2.1
), we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{am}^{abs}
,
\mathcal{C}_{am}^{dis}
and
\mathcal{C}_{am}^{sing}
the family of all absolutely continuous, discrete and singular Archimax copulas.
Given an arbitrary
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
and setting
A(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
, it is straightforward to prove that
C_{\psi,A}=C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
holds. Setting
A(x)=\max\{1-x,x\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
, it is easily proved
(
Chatelain et al., 2020
)
that
C_{\psi,A}(x,y)=M(x,y):=\min\{x,y\}
holds for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
, regardless of the generator
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
. Lastly, considering
\psi(z)=\exp(-z)
for every
z\in[0,\infty)
, we obtain that
C_{\psi,A}=C_{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}
holds for every
A\in\mathcal{A}

(
Capéraà et al., 2000
)
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Building upon the one-to-one correspondences between
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\mathbf{\Psi}
, and between
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{A}
, as discussed in Sections
2.2.1
and
2.2.2
, we see that
\gamma
corresponds to a generator
\psi_{\gamma}
, and
\vartheta
corresponds to a Pickands dependence function
A_{\vartheta}
. According to eq. (
16
), these then induce an Archimax copula
C_{\psi_{\gamma},A_{\vartheta}}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Put differently, every pair
(\gamma,\vartheta)
of
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
induces a unique Archimax copula
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
.

3
Level sets of Archimax copulas

3.1
Representation of level sets via graphs of functions

Following
(
Durante and Sempi, 2015
)
, the
t
-level set of a copula
C\in\mathcal{C}
is defined by

L^{t}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}\colon C(x,y)=t\}

(17)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
.
Moreover, we define the lower
t
-cut of
C
by

[C]_{t}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}\colon C(x,y)\leq t\}

(18)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Throughout this section we let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{\psi}
. Distinguishing between the level sets of
C_{\psi,A}
and
C_{\psi}
, we will denote the
t
-level set of
C_{\psi,A}
by
L_{\psi,A}^{t}
and the
t
-level set of
C_{\psi}
by
L_{\psi}^{t}
for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
.

Motivated by the well known fact that in the Archimedean case the
t
-level set
L_{\psi}^{t}
coincides with the graph of a convex function for every
t\in(0,1]

(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Nelsen, 2006
)
, the primary goal of this section is to establish similar results for the family of Archimax copulas. To be more precise, we will prove that for fixed
t\in\mathbb{I}
, the
t
-level set
L_{\psi,A}^{t}
can be represented by the two functions
f^{t}
and
g^{t}
specified in eq. (
20
) and (
21
), respectively. Denoting by
\varphi
the pseudo-inverse of
\psi
and utilizing the function
h_{A}
defined as in eq. (
14
), the copula
C_{\psi,A}
can also be represented as

C_{\psi,A}(x,y)=\psi\left(\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)

(19)

for every
x,y\in(0,1)
. Therefore, fixing
t\in(0,1)
and
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
yields
C_{\psi,A}(x,y)=t
and thus, formally solving the afore-mentioned equation for
y
, we arrive at the definition of the following functions.

Using the pseudo-inverse
h_{A}^{[-1]}
of
h_{A}
and considering
t\in(0,1)
if
\psi
is strict and
t\in[0,1)
if
\psi
is non-strict, we define the function
f^{t}\colon[t,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
by

f^{t}(x):=\begin{cases}\psi\left(\left(\frac{1}{h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)}-1\right)\varphi(x)\right),&\text{if }x\in[t,1),\\
t,&\text{if }x=1\end{cases}

(20)

for every
x\in[t,1]
. Furthermore, if
\psi
is strict, we define the function
f^{0}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
by
f^{0}(x):=0
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. Finally, the function
f^{1}\colon\{1\}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is defined by
f^{1}(1):=1
.

Applying the fact that
h_{A}^{[-1]}(s)\geq\frac{1}{s+1}
for every
s\in[1,\infty)
, it follows that for all
t\in\mathbb{I}
the inequality
f^{t}(x)\geq t
holds for every
x\in[t,1]
. The next example illustrates that in the Archimedean case
\Gamma(f^{t})
coincides with the
t
-level set
L_{\psi}^{t}
of the corresponding
C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
for every
t\in(0,1]
.

Example 3.1

(Archimedean copulas and EVCs)
.

1.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
be arbitrary,
A(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and fix
t\in(0,1]
. Then
C_{\psi,A}=C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
,
h_{A}(s)=\frac{1}{s}
for every
s\in(0,1]
and
h_{A}^{[-1]}(z)=\frac{1}{z}
for every
z\in[1,\infty)
. Using the afore-mentioned facts, we therefore obtain that
f^{t}(x)=\psi(\varphi(t)-\varphi(x))
for every
x\in[t,1]
. The fact that
\Gamma(f^{t})=L_{\psi}^{t}
is now obvious.
In the case that
t=0
and
\psi
is non-strict,
f^{0}(x)=\psi(\varphi(0)-\varphi(x))
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and thus,
L_{\psi}^{0}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}\colon y\leq f^{0}(x)\}
.

2.

Let
\psi(z)=\exp(-z)
for every
z\in[0,\infty)
with inverse
\varphi(x)=-\log(x)
for every
x\in(0,1]
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
be arbitrary and
t\in(0,1)
. Then
C_{\psi,A}=C_{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}
and

f^{t}(x)=\begin{cases}\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\log(t)}{\log(x)}\right)}-1\right)\log(x)\right),&\text{if }x\in[t,1),\\
t,&\text{if }x=1\end{cases}

for every
x\in[t,1]
.

3.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
be arbitrary,
A(x)=\max\{1-x,x\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and
t\in\mathbb{I}
, then
C_{\psi,A}=M
as well as
f^{t}(x)=t
for every
x\in[t,1]
.

Establishing the results of the next sections, the following function will play a key role. Considering
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
and
t\in(0,1)
, the function
g^{t}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is defined by

g^{t}(x):=\psi\left(\left(\frac{1}{t}-1\right)\varphi(x)\right)

(21)

for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. Furthermore, we define the functions
g^{0}(x):=0
and
g^{1}(x):=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. Given
A\in\mathcal{A}
, fixing
t\in\mathbb{I}
, considering
R
defined as in eq. (
12
) and applying the fact that
h_{A}^{[-1]}(z)\leq R
holds for every
z\in[1,\infty)
yields that
f^{t}(x)\leq g^{R}(x)
for every
x\in[t,1]
.
The next example demonstrates that when the Archimax copula is an EVC, the function
g^{t}
simplifies to
g^{t}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{t}-1}
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. This is noteworthy since, according to
(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
, the discrete component of an EVC always concentrates its mass on
\Gamma(g^{t})
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
be arbitrary,
\psi(z)=\exp(-z)
for every
z\in[0,\infty)
with inverse
\varphi(x)=-\log(x)
for every
x\in(0,1]
and fix
t\in(0,1)
. Then
C_{\psi,A}=C_{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}
as well as
g^{t}(x)=x^{\frac{1}{t}-1}
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
.

Given
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
t\in(0,1]
, the
t
-level set
L_{\psi,A}^{t}
of
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
can be represented in terms of the functions
f^{t}
and
g^{R}
, whereby
R\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
is defined as in eq. (
12
). Unlike the Archimedean case
(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
;
Nelsen, 2006
)
, the
t
-level sets
L_{\psi,A}^{t}
do not necessarily correspond to
\Gamma(f^{t})
. However, as proved in the next lemma, if
R=1
,
L_{\psi,A}^{t}
indeed coincides with
\Gamma(f^{t})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
,
t\in(0,1)
,
f^{t}
be defined as in eq. (
20
),
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
) and
g^{R}
be defined as in eq. (
21
). Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

L_{\psi,A}^{t}=\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
;

(ii)

L_{\psi,A}^{t}=\Gamma(f^{t})
if, and only if
R=1
.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion and show that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
if, and only if
(x,y)\in\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
. Consider
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
, then in the cases
x=0
or
y=0
or
(x,y)=(1,1)
, both
(x,y)\notin L_{\psi,A}^{t}
and
(x,y)\notin\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
holds, due to the fact that
t\in(0,1)
.

If
(x,y)\in\{1\}\times(0,1)
or
(x,y)\in(0,1)\times\{1\}
, then applying
f^{t}(1)=t
and using the fact that
f^{t}(x)=1
implies
x=t
, it is straightforward to prove that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
is equivalent to
(x,y)\in\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
.

From now on let
(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}
. Considering
h_{A}
defined according to eq. (
14
), taking into account that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
implies
h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\varphi(x)<\varphi(0)
and using eq. (
19
), we obtain that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
if, and only if the subsequent identity holds:

h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)=\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}.

(22)

We distinguish two cases:

(a)

If
R\leq\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
, then using the fact that
h_{A}(s)=1
for every
s\in[R,1]
, we find that eq. (
22
) is equivalent to
x=t
. Together with the observation that the condition
R\leq\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
is equivalent to
g^{R}(x)\leq y
, this implies that eq. (
22
) holds if, and only if
(x,y)\in\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1]
.

(b)

If
\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}<R
, then applying the fact that
h_{A}
is invertible on
(0,R)
with inverse
h_{A}^{[-1]}=h_{A}^{-1}
on
(1,\infty)
, and using that
\varphi(x)\left(\frac{1}{h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)}-1\right)<\varphi(0)
whenever
y=f^{t}(x)
, equivalence preserving transformations show that eq. (
22
) holds if, and only if
y=f^{t}(x)
.

Now, assume that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
. Then, eq. (
22
) holds and in both cases,
(a)
and
(b)
, we have
(x,y)\in\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
.
For
(x,y)\in\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1]
, it is clear that
R\leq\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
, and consequently,
(a)
implies that eq. (
22
) holds, yielding
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
.

At last, we assume that
(x,y)\in\Gamma(f^{t})
. It is always the case that
y=f^{t}(x)\leq g^{R}(x)
and therefore
\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\leq R
holds. We distinguish two cases:

•

If
\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}<R
,
(b)
directly yields
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
.

•

If
\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}=R
, then it is obvious that
y=g^{R}(x)
, and hence
f^{t}(x)=g^{R}(x)
, which implies
x=t
. This results in
(x,y)=(t,g^{R}(t))\in\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1]
. Applying
(a)
, we conclude that
(x,y)\in L_{\psi,A}^{t}
.

Combining all of the above cases proves the first assertion.

Considering the second assertion and assuming
R=1
, it follows directly from the first assertion that
L_{\psi,A}^{t}=\Gamma(f^{t})
. Proving the other direction, let
L_{\psi,A}^{t}=\Gamma(f^{t})
and suppose that
R<1
. Then
g^{R}(t)<1
would hold and thus, there would exist a
y_{0}\in\mathbb{I}
such that
g^{R}(t)<y_{0}<1
. Applying the first assertion yields
\Gamma(f^{t})=\Gamma(f^{t})\cup(\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1])
and therefore
(t,y_{0})\in\{t\}\times[g^{R}(t),1]\subseteq\Gamma(f^{t})
would hold, implying that
g^{R}(t)<y_{0}=f^{t}(t)
. This contradicts the fact that
f^{t}(x)\leq g^{R}(x)
for every
x\in[t,1]
and hence,
R=1
follows.
∎

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
be arbitrary and
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
be a strict generator, then
\psi(z)>0
holds for every
z\in[0,\infty)
and thus, the
0
-level set
L_{\psi,A}^{0}
of
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
boils down to

L_{\psi,A}^{0}=(\{0\}\times\mathbb{I})\cup(\mathbb{I}\times\{0\}).

In the case that the generator
\psi
is non-strict, the connection between the
0
-level set
L_{\psi,A}^{0}
and the function
f^{0}
is summarized in the following lemma.

.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
be a non-strict generator,

,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
,

),

be defined according to eq. (

) and

be defined as in eq. (

;

. For the sake of completeness a proof is given in Appendix

.

3.2
Convexity and monotonicity properties of the functions
f^{s}
and
g^{t}

As shown in Example
3.1
, in the case that
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
is arbitrary and
A(x)=1
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
,
C_{\psi,A}=C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
and the
t
-level set
L_{\psi}^{t}
of
C_{\psi}
coincides with
\Gamma(f^{t})
for every
t\in(0,1]
. It is well-known
(
Nelsen, 2006
, Theorem 4.3.2)
that in this particular case the function
f^{t}
is convex for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
.
The main goal of this subsection is to show that the function
f^{t}
is non-increasing and convex for every
t\in[0,1)
regardless of the choice of
\psi
and
A
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
\varphi
be its pseudo-inverse,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
h_{A}
be defined according to eq. (
14
) and
h_{A}^{[-1]}
be its pseudo-inverse. Furthermore, we fix
t\in(0,1)
if
\psi
is strict and
t\in[0,1)
if
\psi
is non-strict. We define the function
\xi^{t}\colon(t,1)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

\xi^{t}(x):=\left(\frac{1}{h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)}-1\right)\varphi(x)

(23)

for every
x\in(t,1)
. Obviously,
f^{t}(x)=\psi(\xi^{t}(x))
holds for every
x\in(t,1)
. Moreover,
\xi^{t}
is a continuous function, as it is the composition of continuous functions. The next lemma proves that the functions
\xi^{t}
are non-decreasing with non-increasing left-hand derivative
D^{-}\xi^{t}
, which together with the fact that
\psi
is non-increasing and convex will prove that the functions
f^{t}
are convex.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
\varphi
be its pseudo-inverse,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
h_{A}
be defined according to eq. (
14
) and
L
,
R
be defined as in eq. (
12
). Let
t\in(0,1)
, if
\psi
is strict and
t\in[0,1)
if
\psi
is non-strict. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

\xi^{t}
is a non-decreasing function;

(ii)

If
0<L<R
, then
\xi^{t}
is strictly increasing on
\left(t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)
and
\xi^{t}(x)=\varphi(t)
holds for every
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right),1\right)
;

(iii)

D^{-}\xi^{t}
is a non-increasing function.

Proof.

See Appendix
7.2
.
∎

The subsequent theorem proves that the function
f^{t}
is non-increasing and convex for every
t\in[0,1)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
t\in(0,1)
if
\psi
is strict and
t\in[0,1)
if
\psi
is non-strict. Moreover, let
f^{t}
be defined as in eq. (
20
), and
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
). Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

f^{t}
is a non-increasing function;

(ii)

If
0<L<R
, then
f^{t}
is strictly decreasing on
\left[t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)
and
f^{t}(x)=t
holds for every
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right),1\right]
;

(iii)

f^{t}
is a convex function.

Proof.

We fix
t\in(0,1)
in the case that
\psi
is strict and
t\in[0,1)
in the case that
\psi
is non-strict. Considering
\xi^{t}
defined as in eq. (
23
) and using that
f^{t}(x)=\psi(\xi^{t}(x))
holds for every
x\in(t,1)
, the fact that
f^{t}
is a non-increasing function on
(t,1)
is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.5
and the non-increasingness of
\psi
. Applying Lemma
7.3
, the function
f^{t}
is obviously continuous on
[t,1]
and therefore
f^{t}
is non-increasing on the whole interval
[t,1]
, which proves the first assertion.

Having that
\xi^{t}
is strictly increasing on
\left(t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)
and that
\psi
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\varphi(0))
implies strict decreasingness of
f^{t}
on
\left[t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)
. Moreover, taking into account that
\xi^{t}(x)=\varphi(t)
for every
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right),1\right)
and
f^{t}(1)=t
, we obtain that
f^{t}(x)=t
holds for every
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right),1\right]
. This proves the second assertion.

We show that
f^{t}
is convex on
[t,1]
. Applying Lemma
3.5
,
\xi^{t}
is a non-decreasing function whose left-hand derivative
D^{-}\xi^{t}
exists. Hence, using convexity of
\psi
, applying Lemma
7.1
and calculating the left-hand derivative of
f^{t}
yields

D^{-}f^{t}(x)=D^{-}\psi(\xi^{t}(x))\cdot D^{-}\xi^{t}(x)

for every
x\in(t,1)
.
Using the properties in Lemma
3.5
and the fact that
\psi
is non-increasing and convex, we conclude that

D^{-}f^{t}
is non-decreasing on
(t,1)
, which, according to
(
Pollard, 2001
, Appendix C)
, implies convexity of
f^{t}
on
(t,1)
. Applying continuity of
f^{t}
, convexity of
f^{t}
on the whole interval
[t,1]
follows.
∎

The next theorem proves that the function
g^{t}
is non-decreasing for every
t\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
\varphi
be its pseudo-inverse,
t\in(0,1)
and
g^{t}
be defined according to eq. (
21
), then

g^{t}
is non-decreasing on
\mathbb{I}
, strictly increasing on
\left[\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right),1\right]
and
g^{t}(x)=0
holds for every
x\in\left[0,\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right)\right]
.

Proof.

We fix
t\in(0,1)
. Using that
\psi
and
\varphi
are both non-increasing implies that
g^{t}
is non-decreasing. If
\psi
is strict, then
\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right)=\psi(\infty)=0
holds and therefore applying the monotonicity properties of
\psi
and
\varphi
yields that
g^{t}
is strictly increasing. If
\psi
is non-strict and
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right),1\right]
, then
(\frac{1}{t}-1)\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(0)
follows and hence, applying that
\psi
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\varphi(0)]
proves that
g^{t}
is strictly increasing on
\left[\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right),1\right]
. Using that
g^{t}(x)=0
whenever
\varphi(0)\leq\frac{(1-t)\varphi(x)}{t}
, implies that
g^{t}(x)=0
for every
x\in\left[0,\psi\left(\frac{t\varphi(0)}{1-t}\right)\right]
. This proves the result.
∎

4
Markov kernels, mass distributions and discrete components of Archimax copulas

4.1
Markov kernels and mass distributions

Proving the results of the next sections, Markov kernels will be an essential tool. Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Then the next theorem constitutes a version of the Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
of
C_{\psi,A}
.
Recall that we consistently use the convention that
a\cdot\infty=\infty
,
\frac{a}{\infty}=0
and
\frac{a}{0}=\infty
for
a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
h_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
14
),
G_{A}
be defined according to eq. (
21
) and
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Then the function
K_{\psi,A}\colon\mathbb{I}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
defined by

\displaystyle K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y]):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x\in\{0,1\},\\
\frac{D^{-}\psi\left(\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))}\cdot G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right),&\text{if }(x,y)\in\Upsilon_{0}^{(1)},\\
0,&\text{if }(x,y)\in\Upsilon_{0}^{(2)},\end{cases}

(24)

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
is a version of the Markov-kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
, whereby
\Upsilon_{0}^{(1)}:=\{(x,y)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{I}\colon f^{0}(x)\leq y\}
and
\Upsilon_{0}^{(2)}:=\{(x,y)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{I}\colon f^{0}(x)>y\}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\{0,1\}
, then
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is obviously a distribution function.

Considering
x\in(0,1)
, we obtain that
K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])=0
for every
y<f^{0}(x)
and therefore the function
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is non-decreasing and right-continuous on
[0,f^{0}(x))
. Note that in the case that
\psi
is strict
f^{0}(x)=0
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and hence, the previous case does not occur.

We prove that
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is non-decreasing on
[f^{0}(x),1]
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, using convexity of
\psi
and the fact that
\varphi
is strictly decreasing implies non-increasingness of the function
y\mapsto D^{-}\psi\left(\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)
. Thus, considering that
D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))
is negative, the first factor in the second line of eq. (
24
) is non-decreasing. Having that the function
G_{A}
is non-decreasing (see Lemma
2.1
), it follows that also the second factor in eq. (
24
) is non-decreasing and therefore
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is a non-decreasing function on
[f^{0}(x),1]
.

The right-continuity of
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
on
[f^{0}(x),1]
follows from the right-continuity of
G_{A}
, the left-continuity of
D^{-}\psi
, and the fact that both
h_{A}
and
\varphi
are non-increasing functions. Combining all of the above observations,
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is a distribution function for fixed
x\in\mathbb{I}
. As usual, working with the extension of
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
from
\{[0,y]\colon y\in\mathbb{I}\}
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
yields a unique probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. Moreover, fixing
y\in\mathbb{I}
, the function
x\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is clearly Borel-measurable. Thus, using a standard Dynkin argument,
x\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,F)
is Borel-measurable for every
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
. Combining all of the afore-mentioned findings, we conclude that
K_{\psi,A}
is indeed a Markov-kernel form
\mathbb{I}
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
.

It is left to show that
K_{\psi,A}
is (a version of) the Markov-kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
. We obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,x]}

\displaystyle K_{\psi,A}(s,[0,y])\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,x]\cap\{v\in\mathbb{I}\colon f^{0}(v)\leq y\}}\frac{D^{-}\psi\left(\varphi(s)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(s))}G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,x]}\frac{D^{-}\psi\left(\varphi(s)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(s))}G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s),

(25)

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}
, whereby the last equation follows from the fact that
\varphi(s)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)>\varphi(0)
, whenever
y<f^{0}(s)
and therefore
D^{-}\psi\left(\varphi(s)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)=0
holds in the case that
y<f^{0}(s)
.

We prove that for fixed
y\in\mathbb{I}
the function
s\mapsto C_{\psi,A}(s,y)
is differentiable for all but at most countably many points
s\in\mathbb{I}
. If
y\in\{0,1\}
, then
s\mapsto C_{\psi,A}(s,y)
is obviously differentiable on
(0,1)
. Considering
L\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
defined as in eq. (
12
), assuming
L>0
and fixing
y\in(0,1)
, we observe that
\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}<L
whenever
s\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(y)}{1-L}\right),1\right)
holds. Recalling that
h_{A}(t)=\frac{1-t}{t}
for every
t\in(0,L]
, it therefore follows that

C_{\psi,A}(s,y)=\psi\left(\varphi(s)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)\right)=\psi(\varphi(y))=y

for every
s\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(y)}{1-L}\right),1\right)
and thus,
\partial_{1}C_{\psi,A}(s,y)=0
holds for every
s\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(y)}{1-L}\right),1\right)
.

From now on we assume that
L\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
.
Considering that both
\psi
and
A
are convex, the derivatives
A^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
exist except for at most countably many points and coincide (wherever they exist) with the left-hand and right-hand derivative, respectively.
Using Lemma
7.4
yields strict decreasingness of the function
s\mapsto(\varphi(s)+\varphi(y))A\left(\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}\right)
on the interval
\left(0,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(y)}{1-L}\right)\right)
. Furthermore, taking into account that both the functions
\varphi
and
s\mapsto\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s)+\varphi(y)}
are strictly decreasing, we obtain that the set
\Lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
of all points
s\in\mathbb{I}
at which the derivative
\partial_{1}C_{\psi,A}(s,y)
of the function
s\mapsto C_{\psi,A}(s,y)
does not exist is at most countable infinite. Applying the chain rule and using eq. (
4.1
) finally yields

\int_{[0,x]}K_{\psi,A}(s,[0,y])\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)=\int_{[0,x]}\partial_{1}C_{\psi,A}(s,y)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)=C_{\psi,A}(x,y)

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{I}
. This proves the result.
∎

Consider

and let
L
,

be defined as in eq. (

,

(26)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and let
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
). Then

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})\subseteq S_{L,R}

(27)

holds.

Proof.

Note that in the case that
\psi
is strict,
L=0
and
R=1
, it is the case that
S_{L,R}=\mathbb{I}^{2}
holds and therefore
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})\subseteq S_{L,R}
is obvious.

We assume from now on that
\psi
is non-strict or
L>0
or
R<1
and prove the desired result by contraposition, i.e., we prove that
(x,y)\in S_{L,R}^{c}
implies
(x,y)\notin\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})
. It suffices to prove that
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(S_{L,R}^{c})=0
. We fix
x\in(0,1)
and show that the Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
of
C_{\psi,A}
defined in eq. (
24
) fulfills

K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\})\cup(g^{R}(x),1])=0.

Using eq. (
24
) obviously implies that
K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,f^{0}(x)))=0
. Assuming
f^{0}(x)<g^{L}(x)
, applying eq. (
24
) and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain that

K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{L}(x)))=\frac{D^{-}\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(x)(1-L)}{L}+\right)}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))}\underbrace{G_{A}(L-)}_{=0}=0

holds. Assuming
g^{L}(x)\leq f^{0}(x)
, obviously yields
K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{L}(x)))=0
. Finally, applying Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that

K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{R}(x)])=\underbrace{\frac{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x)h_{A}(R))}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))}}_{=1}\underbrace{G_{A}(R)}_{=1}=1

and therefore
K_{\psi,A}(x,(g^{R}(x),1])=1-K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{R}(x)])=0
follows. Combining all of the above and applying disintegration proves that

\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(S_{L,R}^{c})=\int_{\mathbb{I}}K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\})\cup(g^{R}(x),1])\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=0

and hence
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})\subseteq S_{L,R}
follows.
∎

Recall that, as discussed in Section
2
, every pair
(\gamma,\vartheta)
of
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
induces a copula
C_{\gamma,\vartheta}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
.
Consider an arbitrary Williamson measure
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
with corresponding generator
\psi
and pseudo-inverse
\varphi
and let
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
h_{A}
being defined as in eq. (
14
). Then, for fixed
x\in(0,1)
we define the function
H_{x}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
by

H_{x}(y):=\frac{\int_{I_{y}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}{\int_{I_{1}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}

(28)

with the notation
I_{y}:=\left[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)}\right]
for every
y\in\mathbb{I}
.
Utilizing eq. (
8
) and fixing
x\in(0,1)
, we can now express the Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
of
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
given in eq. (
24
) by

K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])=H_{x}(y)G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)

(29)

for every
y\in\mathbb{I}
. Note that in the case that
y<f^{0}(x)
, obviously
\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)}<\frac{1}{\varphi(0)}
follows and therefore, applying that
\gamma([0,z])=0
for every
z<\frac{1}{\varphi(0)}
,
H_{x}(y)=0
holds for every
y\in[0,f^{0}(x))
.
Building upon the afore-mentioned facts and taking into account Lemma
4.3
, the question arises naturally, under which conditions on
\gamma
we have equality in eq. (
27
). The next theorem proves that if the support of
\gamma
coincides with
\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}
holds.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be the corresponding generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
be the corresponding Pickands dependence function. Furthermore, let
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
,
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
) and
S_{L,R}
be defined as in eq. (
26
). If
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, then

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}.

Proof.

If
L=R
, then
A(x)=\max\{1-x,x\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
and therefore
C_{\psi,A}=M
,
f^{0}(x)=0
and
g^{L}(x)=x=g^{R}(x)
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{I}
. Thus, it follows that

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{M})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{I}^{2}\colon y=x\}=S_{L,R}.

If
L<R
, we fix
x\in(0,1)
and prove that
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
(according to eq. (
29
)) is strictly increasing on
(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
. Considering
G_{A}
defined as in eq. (
15
) and applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is non-decreasing and positive on
(g^{L}(x),1]
. Using the fact that
h_{A}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,R]
and that
\varphi
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1]
yields that the function
y\mapsto\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)}
is strictly increasing on
(0,g^{R}(x)]
. Moreover, since
\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)<\varphi(0)
holds for every
y\in(f^{0}(x),1]
and
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, it follows that the function
y\mapsto H_{x}(y)=\frac{\int_{I_{y}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}{\int_{I_{1}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}
is strictly increasing and positive on
(f^{0}(x),g^{R}(x))
. Altogether, applying eq. (
29
), we obtain that the function
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is strictly increasing on
(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
, as it is the product of a positive non-decreasing and a positive strictly increasing function.

Now, let
(a,b)\times(c,d)\subset S_{L,R}
with
\lambda_{2}((a,b)\times(c,d))>0
. Then obviously
(c,d)\subset(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
for every
x\in(a,b)
and therefore applying disintegration, we conclude that

\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}((a,b)\times(c,d))=\int_{(a,b)}\underbrace{K_{\psi,A}(x,(c,d))}_{>0}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)>0.

Put differently, every non-empty open rectangle in
S_{L,R}
has positive mass, implying that
S_{L,R}\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})
. Utilizing Lemma
4.3
finally yields
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}
.
∎

Similar to the previous Theorem we show that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}
if the Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta
fulfills
\mathrm{supp}(\vartheta)=[L,R]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
be its Pickands dependence function and
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Furthermore, let
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
) and
S_{L,R}
be defined as in eq. (
26
). If
\mathrm{supp}(\vartheta)=[L,R]
, then

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}.

Proof.

If
L=R
, then the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.4
yield
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}
.

If
L<R
, we fix
x\in(0,1)
, consider the Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
of
C_{\psi,A}
and prove that
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is strictly increasing on
(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
. Applying similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.4
implies that
H_{x}
defined as in eq. (
28
) is non-decreasing and positive on
(f^{0}(x),g^{R}(x))
and that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is positive on
(g^{L}(x),1]
.

We prove that
G_{A}
is strictly increasing on
(L,R)
and fix
t_{1},t_{2}\in(L,R)
arbitrarily. Applying that
\mathrm{supp}(\vartheta)=[L,R]
and using eq. (
11
), we obtain that
D^{+}A(t_{1})<D^{+}A(t_{2})
. Therefore, using convexity of
A
, we conclude that

\displaystyle G_{A}(t_{2})-G_{A}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=A(t_{2})-A(t_{1})+D^{+}A(t_{2})(1-t_{2})-D^{+}A(t_{1})(1-t_{1})

\displaystyle>A(t_{2})-[A(t_{1})+D^{+}A(t_{1})(t_{2}-t_{1})]\geq 0.

Having that
\varphi
is strictly decreasing yields that
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is strictly increasing on
(g^{L}(x),g^{R}(x))
and thus, utilizing eq. (
29
), the function
y\mapsto K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])
is strictly increasing on
(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
. Applying the same chain of arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.4
shows that
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C_{\psi,A}})=S_{L,R}
.
∎

Considering Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
4.5
, we determine under which conditions on

and

.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P_{A}}
,

,

(ii)

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C})=\mathbb{I}^{2}

and therefore the corresponding generator

and

and

4.4
then implies that

.

or

and

4.2
Discrete components of Archimax copulas

Throughout this section let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
be its Pickands dependence function. Furthermore, let
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
with corresponding doubly stochastic measure
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}
and Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
(defined as in eq. (
24
)). In this section we prove that the discrete component
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}^{dis}
of
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}
(if any) always concentrates its mass on the graphs of the functions
f^{s}
or
g^{t}
for some
s,t\in[0,1)
.

If
L<R
, the next lemma shows that for ’good’
x
the probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
has a point mass in
f^{t}(x)
if, and only if
\gamma
has a point mass in
\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its corresponding generator,
\varphi
be its pseudo-inverse,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A
be the corresponding Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
,
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
according to eq. (
24
) and
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
12
) with
L<R
.
Moreover, let
t\in(0,1)
if
\psi
is strict,
t\in[0,1)
if
\psi
is non-strict and let
f^{t}
be defined according to eq. (
20
). Then for all but at most countably many
x\in\left(t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)
the following equivalence holds:

K_{\psi,A}(x,\{f^{t}(x)\})>0\text{ if, and only if }\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\text{ is a point mass of }\gamma.

Proof.

We fix
t\in(0,1)
in the case that
\psi
is strict,
t\in[0,1)
in the case that
\psi
is non-strict and assume that
L<R
. We denote by
I
the set of all point masses of
\vartheta
in
(0,1)
. Note that
I
is at most countable infinite and is empty if
\vartheta
has no point masses in
(0,1)
. Let
h_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
14
),
h_{A}^{[-1]}
be its pseudo-inverse and
G_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
15
). Applying eq. (
11
) and fixing
s_{0}\in(0,1)
yields that
s_{0}
is a point mass of
\vartheta
if, and only if
s_{0}
is a point of discontinuity of
D^{+}A
. Using the afore-mentioned fact and assuming that
I\neq\emptyset
, we obtain that
h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)
is a point of discontinuity of
D^{+}A
(and therefore of
G_{A}
) if, and only if
x\in\bigcup_{s\in I}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{h_{A}(s)}\right)\right\}
. If
I=\emptyset
, then
G_{A}
is obviously continuous. We fix
x\in\left(t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right)\setminus\bigcup_{s\in I}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{h_{A}(s)}\right)\right\}
and note that
\bigcup_{s\in I}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{h_{A}(s)}\right)\right\}=\emptyset
, if
I
is empty. Applying the afore-mentioned arguments we obtain that
G_{A}
is continuous in
h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)
and thus, working with eq. (
29
) yields that

\displaystyle K_{\psi,A}(x,\{f^{t}(x)\})=K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,f^{t}(x)])-K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,f^{t}(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)\right)-\frac{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(t)})}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)-\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\{\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\}}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)\right).

Since
x<\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)
, we obtain
L<h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)
and therefore
G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)\right)>0
holds according to Lemma
2.1
. If
\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}
is a point mass of
\gamma
, then obviously
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{f^{t}(x)\})>0
holds. If
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{f^{t}(x)\})>0
, then
\int_{\{\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\}}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)>0
follows and thus,
\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}
is a point mass of
\gamma
. This proves the result.
∎

Similarly to Lemma
4.8
, the next lemma shows that for ’good’
x
the probability measure

has a point mass in

if, and only if
\vartheta
has a point mass in
t
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be the corresponding generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A
be the corresponding Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
according to eq. (
24
).
Furthermore, let
h_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
14
),
t\in(0,1)
and let

be defined according to eq. (
21
). Then for all but at most countably many
x\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)
the following equivalence holds:

K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})>0\text{ if, and only if }t\text{ is a point mass of }\vartheta.

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,1)
be arbitrary and let
J
denote the set of all point masses of
\gamma
. Again,
J
is at most countable infinite and
J=\emptyset
holds if
\gamma
has no point masses. It is straightforward to verify that
\gamma
has a point mass in
\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)}
if, and only if
x\in\bigcup_{s\in J}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{1}{sh_{A}(t)}\right)\right\}
. We fix
x\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)\setminus\bigcup_{s\in J}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{1}{sh_{A}(t)}\right)\right\}
and obtain that in this case
\gamma
has no point mass in
\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)}
. Applying this fact and using eq. (
29
) again yields that

\displaystyle K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})

\displaystyle=K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{t}(x)])-K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,g^{t}(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}G_{A}(t)-\frac{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)})}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}G_{A}(t-)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}{\int_{[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)}]}u\mathrm{d}\gamma(u)}(1-t)}_{=:w(x,t)}2\vartheta(\{t\}),

. Since

, we obtain that

∎

Lemma

and Lemma

are now used to prove the following theorem, stating that the discrete component
\mu_{C}^{dis}
of an Archimax copula

is non-degenerated if, and only if the corresponding

or the corresponding
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mu_{C}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})>0
;

(ii)

\gamma
has a point mass
z\in(0,\infty)
or
\vartheta
has a point mass
t\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, if
\mu_{C}^{dis}
is non-degenerated, then its mass is concentrated on graphs
\Gamma(f^{t})
or
\Gamma(g^{s})
for some
s\in(0,1)
or
t\in[0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
A
be the the corresponding Pickands dependence function. Furthermore let
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
with Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
defined as in eq. (
24
) and let
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
13
). At first, assume that
L=R
. Then
\vartheta=\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}
and therefore
C_{\psi,A}=M
holds. Since
\mu_{M}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
, the desired equivalence follows.

From now on we assume that
L<R
. If
z\in(0,\infty)
is a point mass of
\gamma
, then using the fact that the corresponding
\varphi
is strictly decreasing yields the existence of
t\in[0,1)
such that
z=\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}
and therefore applying Lemma
4.8
implies that
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{f^{t}(x)\})>0
for
\lambda
-almost every

.
Assuming that
t\in(0,1)
is a point mass of
\vartheta
and using Lemma
4.9
, we conclude that
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})>0
for
\lambda
-almost every
x\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)
. In either of the afore-mentioned cases disintegration proves
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})>0
.

We assume that
\mu_{C}^{dis}(\mathbb{I}^{2})>0
, then by definition there exists a set
\Lambda\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
with
\lambda(\Lambda)>0
such that for every

there exists a

with

. Using eq. (
29
) yields that

\displaystyle 0<K_{\psi,A}(x,\{y\})=H_{x}(y)\cdot G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)-H_{x}(y-)\cdot G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}-\right)

with

is a point mass of
\gamma
or
s=\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
is a point of discontinuity of
G_{A}
, otherwise
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{y\})=0
would hold. If
u
is a point mass of
\gamma
, obviously
(ii)
holds. Assuming that
s
is a point of discontinuity of
G_{A}
yields that
s
is a point of discontinuity of
D^{+}A
and therefore applying eq. (
11
) proves
(ii)
.

Proving the last assertion and using that
\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\leq R
whenever
y\leq g^{R}(x)
, we obtain that
u=\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)}
implies

and
s=\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
implies
y=g^{s}(x)
. Assuming that
x\in(0,1)
and that
y\in[\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x)]
is a point mass of
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
, combining the afore-mentioned arguments yields that
y=f^{t}(x)
or
y=g^{s}(x)
for some

or

. Applying disintegration proves the desired result.
∎

5
Kendall distribution function and copula-mass of level sets

Throughout this section we let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its corresponding generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A
be its corresponding Pickands dependence function and
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. The goal of this section is to provide a new proof for the representation of the Kendall distribution function
F_{C_{\psi,A}}^{K}
of
C_{\psi,A}
in terms of
\psi
and
A
. Building upon this representation, we then calculate the level set mass
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(L_{\psi,A}^{t})
for every
t\in[0,1)
and show that
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(L_{\psi,A}^{t})
can be expressed in terms of
\gamma
and
A
. Prior to proving the afore-mentioned results, we revisit representations of the Kendall distribution functions of Archimedean copulas and EVCs, as these are frequently used throughout this section.
We define the function
\beta_{\psi}\colon(0,1)\rightarrow(-\infty,0)
by

\beta_{\psi}(t):=D^{-}\psi(\varphi(t))\varphi(t)

(30)

for every
t\in(0,1)
, set
\beta_{\psi}(0):=0
in the case that
\psi
is strict,
\beta_{\psi}(0):=D^{-}\psi(\varphi(0))\varphi(0)
in the case that
\psi
is non-strict and set
\beta_{\psi}(1):=0
. Moreover, we observe that, according to
(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
)
, the mass of the
t
-level set
L_{\psi}^{t}
of
C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
with generator
\psi
and pseudo-inverse
\varphi
is given by

\mu_{C_{\psi}}(L_{\psi}^{t})=\gamma\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\right\}\right)=\beta_{\psi}(t-)-\beta_{\psi}(t)

(31)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
with the convention that
\beta_{\psi}(0-)=0
. Again, following
(
Kasper et al., 2024
;
McNeil and Nešlehová, 2009
)
, the Kendall distribution function of
C_{\psi}
is given by

F_{\psi}^{K}(t)=\gamma\left(\left[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\right]\right)=t-\beta_{\psi}(t)

(32)

for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
.
Furthermore, according to
(
Ghoudi et al., 1998
)
, Kendall’s tau
\tau_{A}:=\tau(C_{A})
of
C_{A}
can be written as

\tau_{A}=\int_{\mathbb{I}}\frac{t(1-t)}{A(t)}\mathrm{d}D^{+}A(t)

(33)

and the Kendall distribution function
F_{A}^{K}:=F_{C_{A}}^{K}
is given by

F_{A}^{K}(t)=t-(1-\tau_{A})t\log(t)

(34)

for every
t\in(0,1]
and
F_{A}^{K}(0)=0
.

A representation of the Kendall distribution function
F_{\psi,A}^{K}
of
C_{\psi,A}
has already been provided in
(
Capéraà et al., 2000
;
Joe, 2014
)
.
Taking into account that the proof in
(
Capéraà et al., 2000
)
is technically quite involved, we come up with a different proof for the explicit representation of
F_{\psi,A}^{K}
, relaying on the lower
t
-cut
[C_{\psi,A}]_{t}
of
C_{\psi,A}
, eq. (
24
) and disintegration.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
,
\beta_{\psi}
be defined as in eq. (
30
),
\tau_{A}
be Kendall’s tau of
C_{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}
and
F_{\psi}^{K}
be the Kendall distribution function of
C_{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
. Then the
Kendall distribution function
F_{\psi,A}^{K}
of
C_{\psi,A}
is given by

F_{\psi,A}^{K}(t)=t+\beta_{\psi}(t)(\tau_{A}-1)=F_{\psi}^{K}(t)+\beta_{\psi}(t)\tau_{A},

(35)

for every
t\in(0,1]
. Furthermore,
F_{\psi,A}(0)=0
holds in the case that
\psi
is strict and
F_{\psi,A}(0)=\beta_{\psi}(0)(\tau_{A}-1)
holds in the case that
\psi
is non-strict.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in\mathbf{\Psi}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. To simplify notation, we set
C:=C_{\psi,A}
throughout this proof.
At first, note that in the case that
L=R
(whereby
L
,
R
are defined as in eq. (
12
)),
C=M
holds and therefore
F_{\psi,A}^{K}(t)=F_{M}^{K}(t)=t
for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
, as well as
\tau_{A}=1
, which proves eq. (
35
).

From now on we assume that
L<R
.
Moreover, we assume that
t\in(0,1]
in the case that
\psi
is strict and
t\in\mathbb{I}
in the case that
\psi
is non-strict.
It is straightforward to prove that the lower
t
-cut of
C
defined as in eq. (
18
) can be written as

[C]_{t}=\{(x,y)\in[t,1]\times\mathbb{I}\colon y\in[0,f^{t}(x)]\}\cup([0,t]\times\mathbb{I}),

whereby the function
f^{t}
is defined as in eq. (
20
). Applying eq. (
24
) and disintegration yields that

\displaystyle F_{\psi,A}^{K}(t)

\displaystyle=\mu_{C}([C]_{t})=\int_{[0,t]}K_{\psi,A}(x,\mathbb{I})\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)+\int_{(t,1]}K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,f^{t}(x)])\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=t+D^{-}\psi(\varphi(t))\underbrace{\int_{I_{t}}\frac{G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(x)}\right)\right)}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}_{=:q},

(36)

whereby
I_{t}:=\left(t,\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right)\right]
. In the last equality of eq. (
5
) we used that
f^{t}(x)=t
for every
x\in\left[\psi\left(\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L}\right),1\right]
.
Taking into account that the functions
\varphi
and

are convex and therefore locally Lipschitz continuous, applying change of coordinates for locally Lipschitz continuous functions
(
Hajłasz, 1993
, Theorem 3)
with
z=\varphi(x)
,
v=\frac{\varphi(t)}{z}
and
s=h_{A}^{[-1]}(v)
implies that

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-\int_{[\frac{L\varphi(t)}{1-L},\varphi(t)]}G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}\left(\frac{\varphi(t)}{z}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)

\displaystyle=-\varphi(t)\int_{[1,\frac{1-L}{L}]}\frac{G_{A}\left(h_{A}^{[-1]}(v)\right)}{v^{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda(v)

\displaystyle=\varphi(t)\int_{[L,R]}\frac{D^{+}h_{A}(s)}{h_{A}(s)^{2}}G_{A}(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)

\displaystyle=\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{I}}\frac{D^{+}h_{A}(s)}{h_{A}(s)^{2}}G_{A}(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)

\displaystyle=-\varphi(t)\int_{\mathbb{I}}\frac{A(s)-sD^{+}A(s)}{A(s)^{2}}G_{A}(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s),

(37)

whereby the penultimate equality follows form the fact that

for every

and
G_{A}(u)=0
for every
u\in(0,L)
. Furthermore, we used that
h_{A}(u)=\frac{A(u)}{u}
for every

in the last equality.
Applying
(

of

can be represented as

F_{A}^{K}(w)=w-w\log(w)\int_{\mathbb{I}}\frac{A(s)-sD^{+}A(s)}{A(s)^{2}}G_{A}(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s)

for every

and therefore eq. (

) implies that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle 1-\tau_{A}=\int_{\mathbb{I}}\frac{A(s)-sD^{+}A(s)}{A(s)^{2}}G_{A}(s)\mathrm{d}\lambda(s).

Using this and taking into account eq. (

), it follows that

. Thus, utilizing eq. (

), eq. (

) simplifies to

. Applying eq. (

) then implies eq. (

).

If
\psi
is strict, then obviously

holds. This proves the result.
∎

. Its mass can be represented in terms of the corresponding Williamson measure

.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let

,

be its corresponding generator,

(38)

for every

.

Proof.

Applying eq. (
31
) and Theorem

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(L_{\psi,A}^{t})

\displaystyle=F_{\psi,A}^{K}(t)-F_{\psi,A}^{K}(t-)

\displaystyle=(\beta_{\psi}(t-)-\beta_{\psi}(t))(1-\tau_{A})

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\varphi(t)}\right\}\right)(1-\tau_{A})

(39)

holds for every

with the convention that

∎

Considering
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
, with Pickands dependence function

and

then, as show in Example

(defined as in eq. (

. According to
(

;

)

\mu_{C_{A}}^{dis}
(if non-degenerated) of

always concentrates its mass on the graphs

for some

. Moreover, the mass of
\Gamma(g^{t})
is given by

\mu_{C_{A}}(\Gamma(g^{t}))=\frac{2t(1-t)}{A(t)}\vartheta(\{t\}),

for every
t\in(0,1)

(
Dietrich and Trutschnig, 2025
;
Trutschnig et al., 2016
)
. The next theorem shows that regardless of the choice of
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
(and therefore regardless of the choice of

and
\psi
), surprisingly, the mass of
\Gamma(g^{t})
w.r.t.
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}
will also evaluate to
\frac{2t(1-t)}{A(t)}\vartheta(\{t\})
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be its generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
be the corresponding Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
. Then the following equation holds for every
t\in(0,1)
:

\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(\Gamma(g^{t}))=\frac{2t(1-t)}{A(t)}\vartheta(\{t\}).

(40)

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,1)
be arbitrary and
J
be the set of all point masses of
\gamma
. Then proceeding analogously as in Lemma
4.9
and fixing
x\in\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)\setminus\bigcup_{s\in J}\{\psi(\frac{1}{sh_{A}(t)})\}
,
\gamma
has no point mass in
\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)}
and therefore, utilizing eq. (
8
),
D^{-}\psi
is continuous in
\varphi(x)h_{A}(t)
. Using the fact that
\lambda\left(\bigcup_{s\in J}\{\psi(\frac{1}{sh_{A}(t)})\}\right)=0
, applying eq. (
24
), disintegration and change of coordinates with
z=\varphi(x)
and
v=zh_{A}(t)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}(\Gamma(g^{t}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{I}}K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)}K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\left(\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right)\setminus\bigcup_{s\in J}\{\psi(\frac{1}{sh_{A}(t)})\}}K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=(1-t)(D^{+}A(t)-D^{+}A(t-))\int_{\left[\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right),1\right]}\frac{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x)h_{A}(t))}{D^{-}\psi(\varphi(x))}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=-(1-t)(D^{+}A(t)-D^{+}A(t-))\int_{\left[0,\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right]}D^{-}\psi(zh_{A}(t))\mathrm{d}\lambda(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-t}{h_{A}(t)}(D^{+}A(t)-D^{+}A(t-))\underbrace{\int_{[0,\varphi(0)]}D^{-}\psi(v)\mathrm{d}\lambda(v)}_{=-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t(1-t)}{A(t)}(D^{+}A(t)-D^{+}A(t-))

\displaystyle=\frac{2t(1-t)}{A(t)}\vartheta(\{t\}),

whereby in the second equality we used that
K_{\psi,A}(x,\{g^{t}(x)\})=0
whenever
x<\psi\left(\frac{\varphi(0)}{h_{A}(t)}\right)
and in the last equality we used that, according to eq. (
11
),
D^{+}A(t)-D^{+}A(t-)=2\vartheta(\{t\})
holds for every
t\in(0,1)
.
∎

6
Absolute continuity and singularity aspects

6.1
Absolutely continuous, discrete and singular Archimax copulas

Considering
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
, it was proved in
(
Kasper et al., 2024
)
that absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity of
\gamma
propagates to the corresponding
C_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{ar}
. In this section, the objective is to establish similar results for the larger family of Archimax copulas. To be more precise, considering
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and letting
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula, we show that absolute continuity, discreteness and singularity of
\gamma
and
\vartheta
carry over to the copula
C
.
Let
\psi
and
A
be the generator and Pickands dependence function corresponding to
\gamma
and
\vartheta
, respectively, and let
h_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
14
). Then, recall that according to eq. (
28
) for fixed
x\in(0,1)
the function
H_{x}\colon\mathbb{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
is given by

H_{x}(y)=\frac{\int_{I_{y}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}{\int_{I_{1}}t\mathrm{d}\gamma(t)}

with
I_{y}=\left[0,\frac{1}{\varphi(x)h_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)}\right]
for every
y\in\mathbb{I}
. Using convexity of
\psi
and the properties outlined in Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that
H_{x}
is non-decreasing. Furthermore, the fact that
\gamma
is a probability measure combined with the continuity of the functions
\varphi
and
h_{A}
, ensures right-continuity of
H_{x}
. Observing that
H_{x}(1)=1
, it follows that
H_{x}
is a distribution function and consequently,
H_{x}
induces a unique probability measure
\nu_{H_{x}}
.
Moreover, fixing
x\in(0,1)
and revisiting eq. (
29
), the Markov kernel
K_{\psi,A}
of
C_{\psi,A}=C
is given by

K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,y])=H_{x}(y)\cdot G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)

for every
y\in\mathbb{I}
. Notice that, using the properties in Lemma
2.1
, also
G_{A}
is a distribution function and therefore corresponds to a unique probability measure
\kappa_{G_{A}}
on
\mathbb{I}
.

Establishing the main result of this section, we proceed as follows:
We prove that absolute continuity/discreteness/singularity of the measures
\gamma
and
\vartheta
is preserved by the measures
\nu_{H_{x}}
and
\kappa_{G_{A}}
, respectively. Hence, applying eq. (
29
), these properties also extend to the measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
. Finally, by disintegrating, we obtain absolute continuity, discreteness, or singularity of the copula
C_{\psi,A}
.
Since the proof of the main theorem is technically quite involved, we split it into multiple lemmas.
The next two lemmas show that if the probability measures
\gamma
and
\vartheta
are absolutely continuous/discrete/singular, then so are the measures
\nu_{H_{x}}
and
\kappa_{G_{A}}
. For the sake of readability the proofs are deferred to Appendix
7.2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be the corresponding generator,
\varphi
be its pseudo-inverse,
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in(0,1)
. Furthermore, let
H_{x}
be defined according to eq. (
28
) and
\nu_{H_{x}}
be its corresponding probability measure. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\gamma
is absolutely continuous, then
\nu_{H_{x}}
is absolutely continuous.

(ii)

If
\gamma
is discrete, then
\nu_{H_{x}}
is discrete.

(iii)

If
\gamma
is singular, then
\nu_{H_{x}}
is singular.

Proof.

See Appendix
7.2
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}
be its corresponding Pickands dependence function,
G_{A}
be defined according to eq. (
15
) and
\kappa_{G_{A}}
be its corresponding probability measure. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\vartheta
is absolutely continuous, then
\kappa_{G_{A}}
is absolutely continuous.

(ii)

If
\vartheta
is discrete with
\vartheta(\{0\})=0=\vartheta(\{1\})
, then
\kappa_{G_{A}}
is discrete.

(iii)

If
\vartheta
is singular, then
\kappa_{G_{A}}
is singular.

Proof.

See Appendix
7.2
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

The restriction
\vartheta(\{0\})=0=\vartheta(\{1\})
in the second assertion of Lemma
6.2
is necessary, since the Pickands dependence measure
\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1}
corresponds to the Pickands dependence function
A(t)=1
for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Therefore,
G_{A}(t)=1
holds for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
, which is obviously an absolutely continuous function on
\mathbb{I}
.

The next lemma shows that if
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
are both absolutely continuous then the corresponding Archimax copula
C
is absolutely continuous as well.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Then the following assertion holds:

\text{If }\gamma\text{ and }\vartheta\text{ are absolutely continuous, then }C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{abs}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{abs}
,
\psi
be its corresponding generator with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{abs}
,
A
be its Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
defined as in eq. (
24
). We fix
x\in(0,1)
. Applying Lemma
6.2
,
G_{A}
is absolutely continuous. Furthermore, using that the function
y\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
is obviously absolutely continuous and strictly increasing yields that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is absolutely continuous as well, see
(
Pap, 2002
, Proposition 129)
.

Since
\gamma
is absolutely continuous, Lemma
6.1
yields absolute continuity of the function
H_{x}
(defined as in eq. (
28
)) on
\mathbb{I}
.
Using that products of absolutely continuous functions on compact intervals are absolutely continuous and working with eq. (
29
), we conclude that the probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous. Disintegration finally implies that
\mu_{C_{\psi,A}}^{abs}(\mathbb{I}^{2})=1
, which proves the result.
∎

We prove a similar result for the case that
\gamma
and
\vartheta
are discrete:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
with
\vartheta(\{0\})=0=\vartheta(\{1\})
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Then the following assertion holds:

\text{If }\gamma\text{ and }\vartheta\text{ are discrete, then }C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{dis}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{dis}
,
\psi
be its corresponding generator with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{dis}
with
\vartheta(\{0\})=0=\vartheta(\{1\})
,
A
be its Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
according to eq. (
24
).

We show that for fixed
x\in(0,1)
the probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
is discrete on
\mathbb{I}
. Let
G_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
15
) and
x\in(0,1)
be arbitrary. We prove that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
induces a discrete probability measure on
\mathbb{I}
.
Applying Lemma
6.2
yields discreteness of the corresponding probability measure
\kappa_{G_{A}}
and thus, the existence of
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...\in\mathbb{I}
with
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{i}=1
and
s_{1},s_{2},...\in(0,1)
such that
G_{A}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{i}\delta_{s_{i}}([0,t])
holds for every
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Note, that in the case that
0<L
or
R<1
holds, according to Lemma
2.1
, we obtain that
L\leq s_{i}
or
s_{i}\leq R
. Using the fact that
s_{i}\in\left[0,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right]
is equivalent to
y\geq g^{s_{i}}(x)
for every
y\in[g^{L}(x),g^{R}(x)]
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, implies that

G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{1}_{[g^{s_{i}}(x),1]}(y)

for every
y\in[g^{L}(x),g^{R}(x)]
. Furthermore, using Lemma
2.1
again,
G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)=0
holds for every
y\in[0,g^{L}(x))
and
G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)=1
holds for every
y\in[g^{R}(x),1]
. Overall we obtain that the distribution function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
induces a unique, discrete probability measure on
\mathbb{I}
with point masses
\alpha_{i}
in
g^{s_{i}}(x)
.

Considering
H_{x}
defined as in eq. (
28
) and applying Lemma
6.1
, it follows that
H_{x}
induces a discrete probability measure
\nu_{H_{x}}
on
\mathbb{I}
. Finally, utilizing that products of discrete distribution functions are discrete and using eq. (
29
), we conclude that
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
is a discrete probability measure on
\mathbb{I}
. Consequently, disintegration yields
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{dis}
.
∎

At last, we prove that if
\gamma
and
\vartheta
are singular then the corresponding Archimax copula
C
is singular.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Then the following assertion holds:

\text{If }\gamma\text{ and }\vartheta\text{ are singular, then }C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{sing}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{sing}
,
\psi
be its corresponding generator with pseudo-inverse
\varphi
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{sing}
,
A
be its Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be its Markov kernel according to eq. (
24
).

We prove that for fixed
x\in(0,1)
the probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
is singular on
\mathbb{I}
. Let
G_{A}
be defined as in eq. (
15
) and
x\in(0,1)
be arbitrary. We prove that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is singular.
Applying Lemma
6.2
yields that
G_{A}
is singular and therefore defining the set

\Lambda:=\{s\in\mathbb{I}\colon G_{A}^{\prime}(s)\text{ exists and }G_{A}^{\prime}(s)=0\},

we conclude that
\lambda(\Lambda)=1
. Denoting by
\iota
the inverse function of
y\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}
and defining the set

\Upsilon:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{I}\colon\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\in\Lambda\right\},

we obtain that
\iota(z)=\psi\left(\left(\frac{1}{z}-1\right)\varphi(x)\right)=g^{z}(x)
for every
z\in\left[\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(0)},1\right]
as well as
\Upsilon=\iota(\Lambda)
. Using that
\iota
is locally absolutely continuous and applying
(
Leoni, 2024
, Theorem 3.41)
yields that
\lambda(\Upsilon)=1
. This implies that

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)=G_{A}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)\frac{-\varphi(x)\varphi^{\prime}(y)}{(\varphi(x)+\varphi(y))^{2}}=0

for every
y\in\Upsilon
. Observing that the function
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
is obviously continuous, we obtain that
y\mapsto G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)}\right)
induces a singular probability measure on
\mathbb{I}
.

Applying Lemma
6.1
, we conclude that the function
H_{x}
induces a singular probability measure on
\mathbb{I}
as well. Using that products of singular distribution functions are singular and working with eq. (
29
), we finally deduce that the probability measure
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
is singular. Now disintegration yields the desired result.
∎

The next theorem summarizes Lemma
6.4
, Lemma
6.5
and Lemma
6.6
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\gamma
and
\vartheta
are absolutely continuous, then
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{abs}
.

(ii)

If
\gamma
is discrete and
\vartheta
is discrete with
\vartheta(\{0\})=0=\vartheta(\{1\})
, then
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{dis}
.

(iii)

If
\gamma
and
\vartheta
are singular, then
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}^{sing}
.

Next, we give an example of a discrete Archimax copula.

.

Considering the probability measure

defined by

clearly

. Furthermore, defining the probability measure

by

. Obviously,
\gamma
and

are discrete and

holds. Therefore, Theorem
6.7
yields that the corresponding Archimax copula

fulfills

. A sample of

is depicted in Figure

.

6.2
Support of absolutely continuous, discrete and singular components of Archimax copulas

In this subsection we generalize the result in
(
Trutschnig et al., 2016
, Corollary 5)
, saying that for
C\in\mathcal{C}_{ev}
(and if
L<R
), the support of the doubly stochastic measure
\mu_{C}
of
C
coincides with the support of the absolutely continuous component
\mu_{C}^{abs}
of
\mu_{C}
. To be more precise, we establish that if
L<R
and the Williamson measure
\gamma
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, and is either absolutely continuous, discrete or singular, then the support of the measure
\mu_{C}
corresponding to
C\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
coincides with the support of
\mu_{C}^{abs}
,
\mu_{C}^{dis}
or
\mu_{C}^{sing}
, respectively. The prove of the afore-mentioned result is split into three lemmas.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{abs}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula. Furthermore, let
L
,
R
be defined according to eq. (
13
) with
L<R
.
Then,

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C}^{abs})=\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C}).

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}
,
\psi
be the corresponding generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A
be its Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
defined in eq. (
24
). We fix
x\in(0,1)
and prove that the absolutely continuous component
K_{\psi,A}^{abs}(x,\cdot)
of
K_{\psi,A}(x,\cdot)
fulfills
\mathrm{supp}(K_{\psi,A}^{abs}(x,\cdot))=[\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x)]
. The desired result then follows using disintegration.

Let
(y_{1},y_{2})\subseteq(\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x))
be an arbitrary open interval. We prove that
K_{\psi,A}^{abs}(x,(y_{1},y_{2}))>0
. Applying Lemma
6.1
and proceeding analogously to the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we obtain that the function
H_{x}
(defined in eq. (
28
)) is strictly increasing and absolutely continuous and therefore there exists a set
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
with
\lambda((y_{1},y_{2})\cap\Lambda_{1})>0
such that
H_{x}^{\prime}(s)>0
for every
s\in(y_{1},y_{2})\cap\Lambda_{1}
. Using monotonicity and convexity implies that the set
\Lambda_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{I})
of all points
s\in\mathbb{I}
at which the functions
G_{A}
and
\varphi
are differentiable fulfills
\lambda(\Lambda_{2})=1
. Considering
s\in(y_{1},y_{2})\cap\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
, calculating the derivative
\partial_{2}K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,s])
and simplifying the result yields

\mathcal{k}_{\psi,A}(x,s):=\underbrace{H_{x}^{\prime}(s)G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(s)}\right)}_{=:z_{1}}+\underbrace{H_{x}(s)G_{A}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(s)}\right)\frac{-\varphi(x)\varphi^{\prime}(s)}{(\varphi(x)+\varphi(s))^{2}}}_{=:z_{2}}.

Obviously, all the factors in
z_{2}
are non-negative. Observing that
G_{A}\left(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)+\varphi(s)}\right)>0
(see Lemma
2.1
) and using that
H_{x}^{\prime}(s)>0
, we have that
z_{1}>0
and therefore
\mathcal{k}_{\psi,A}(x,s)>0
holds. Furthermore, using the fact that
\lambda((y_{1},y_{2})\cap\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})>0
, we obtain that
K_{\psi,A}^{abs}(x,(y_{1},y_{2}))>0
. Finally, taking into account that
(y_{1},y_{2})
was arbitrary and using that
K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,s])=0
for every
s\in[0,\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\})
as well as
K_{\psi,A}(x,[0,s])=1
for every
s\in(g^{R}(x),1]
, it follows that

\mathrm{supp}(K_{\psi,A}^{abs}(x,\cdot))=[\max\{f^{0}(x),g^{L}(x)\},g^{R}(x)].

∎

We prove that if the Williamson measure
\gamma
is discrete and
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, then the support of the discrete component
\mu_{C}^{dis}
of the doubly stochastic measure
\mu_{C}
coincides with the support of
\mu_{C}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{dis}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
C
be the corresponding Archimax copula.
Then,

\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C}^{dis})=\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{C}).

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{dis}
,
\psi
be its generator,
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
A
be the corresponding Pickands dependence function,
C_{\psi,A}\in\mathcal{C}_{am}
and
K_{\psi,A}
be the Markov kernel of
C_{\psi,A}
defined as in eq. (
24
). Let
L
,
R
be defined as in eq. (
12
). If
L=R
, then
C_{\psi,A}=M
and therefore the statement of the Lemma holds.

We now assume that
L<R
. Having
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{W}}^{dis}
and using that
\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)=\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
, there exists a countable dense set of
s_{1},s_{2},...\in\left[\frac{1}{\varphi(0)},\infty\right)
with
\gamma(\{s_{i}\})>0
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Note, that in the case of
\gamma
being strict,
s_{i}>0
holds for every

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
be open sub-intervals of
\mathbb{R}
and let
\iota\colon I_{1}\rightarrow I_{2}
be a function whose left-hand derivative
D^{-}\iota
exists. Moreover, let
\eta\colon I_{2}\rightarrow I_{3}
be an arbitrary function. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
\eta
is strictly decreasing and convex, then

D^{-}\eta^{-1}(z)=\frac{1}{D^{+}\eta(\eta^{-1}(z))}

holds for every
z\in I_{3}
, whereby
\eta^{-1}
denotes the inverse function of
\eta
.

(ii)

If
D^{-}\eta
exists and
\iota
is a non-decreasing function, then

D^{-}(\eta\circ\iota)(t)=D^{-}\eta(\iota(t))\cdot D^{-}\iota(t)

holds for every
t\in I_{1}
.

(iii)

If
D^{-}\eta
exists and
\iota
is a non-increasing function, then

D^{-}(\eta\circ\iota)(t)=D^{+}\eta(\iota(t))\cdot D^{-}\iota(t)

holds for every
t\in I_{1}
.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion, fix
z\in I_{3}
and
\varepsilon<0
with
z+\varepsilon\in I_{3}
and define
\delta:=\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon)-\eta^{-1}(z)
. Since
\eta^{-1}
is strictly decreasing as the inverse of a strictly decreasing function,
\delta>0
follows. We obtain that

\frac{\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon)-\eta^{-1}(z)}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\frac{\eta(\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon))-\eta(\eta^{-1}(z)))}{\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon)-\eta^{-1}(z)}}=\frac{1}{\frac{\eta(\eta^{-1}(z)+\delta)-\eta(\eta^{-1}(z))}{\delta}}

and therefore using the fact that
\eta
is convex yields that

\displaystyle D^{-}\eta^{-1}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\uparrow 0}\frac{\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon)-\eta^{-1}(z)}{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\uparrow 0}\frac{1}{\frac{\eta(\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon))-\eta(\eta^{-1}(z)))}{\eta^{-1}(z+\varepsilon)-\eta^{-1}(z)}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\frac{\eta(\eta^{-1}(z)+\delta)-\eta(\eta^{-1}(z))}{\delta}}=\frac{1}{D^{+}\eta(\eta^{-1}(z))},

for every
z\in I_{3}
, whereby the denominator in the last expression is unequal to zero, since
\eta
is strictly decreasing and convex.

We prove the second assertion, consider
s,t\in I_{1}
with
s<t
and assume for now that
\iota(s)<\iota(t)
. We define
r:=\iota(s)-\iota(t)<0
and obtain that

\frac{\eta(\iota(s))-\eta(\iota(t))}{\iota(s)-\iota(t)}=\frac{\eta(\iota(t)+r)-\eta(\iota(t))}{r},

which together with the assumption that
\iota(s)<\iota(t)
for every
s
near
t
yields that

\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{\eta(\iota(s))-\eta(\iota(t))}{\iota(s)-\iota(t)}=\lim_{r\uparrow 0}\frac{\eta(\iota(t)+r)-\eta(\iota(t))}{r}=D^{-}\eta(\iota(t)).

Considering the general case, we define the function

w(y):=\begin{cases}\frac{\eta(y)-\eta(\iota(t))}{y-\iota(t)},&\text{ if }y\neq\iota(t),\\
D^{-}\eta(\iota(t)),&\text{ if }y=\iota(t)\end{cases}

and observe that obviously
\frac{\eta(\iota(s))-\eta(\iota(t))}{s-t}=w(\iota(s))\cdot\frac{\iota(s)-\iota(t)}{s-t}
holds. Taking the limit and using the fact that the left-hand derivative
D^{-}\iota
of
\iota
exists finally yields that

\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{\eta(\iota(s))-\eta(\iota(t))}{s-t}=\lim_{s\uparrow t}w(\iota(s))\cdot\lim_{s\uparrow t}\frac{\iota(s)-\iota(t)}{s-t}=D^{-}\eta(\iota(t))\cdot D^{-}\iota(t).

The proof of the third assertion follows similarly.

t\in\mathbb{I}
and thus,
G_{A}
induces a discrete probability measure
\kappa_{G_{A}}
.

Proving the third assertion, we assume that
\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{sing}
. Observing that
\vartheta
has no point masses and applying eq. (
11
) yields that
D^{+}A
is continuous and therefore continuity of
G_{A}
follows. We prove that
G_{A}^{\prime}(t)
exists and
G_{A}^{\prime}(t)=0
for
\lambda
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Applying convexity of
A
yields that
A^{\prime}(t)
exists for all but at most countably many
t\in\mathbb{I}
, as well as
D^{+}A=A^{\prime}
wherever
A^{\prime}
exists. Now, using singularity of
\vartheta
together with eq. (
11
) implies that
DD^{+}A(t)=0
for
\lambda
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{I}
. Altogether, we therefore conclude that

\displaystyle G_{A}^{\prime}(t)=D^{+}A(t)+DD^{+}A(t)(1-t)-D^{+}A(t)=DD^{+}A(t)(1-t)=0

for
\lambda
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{I}
. This shows that the probability measure
\kappa_{G_{A}}
is singular and therefore the result is proved.

Anchoring levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system versus suture fixation of the levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system in the treatment of adenomyosis
Authors: Hui Yan, Jia-hong Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to compare two levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system fixation methods for the treatment of adenomyosis.MethodsThe study included patients with adenomyosis who underwent anchoring of the LNG-IUS to the uterine muscle layer using the GyneFix→ intrauterine device (IUD) system and those who underwent LNG-IUS suturing to the uterine muscle layer (experimental and control groups, respectively; n = 21 each), between January 2023 and February 2025. This retrospective study was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University. All 42 patients completed LNG-IUS fixation surgery and followed up at 1, 3, and 6 months post-operatively.ResultsThe pre-treatment clinical profiles, including age, parity, body weight, body mass index (BMI), incidence of dysmenorrhea and menorrhagia, uterine volume, visual analog scale (VAS) score, hemoglobin (Hb) level, and pictorial blood assessment chart (PBAC) score, were not significantly different between the two groups. The mean operative duration was 13.19 ± 2.23 min in the experimental group and 29.48 ± 3.72 min in the control group (p < 0.01). After placement of the LNG-IUS in both groups, the VAS and PBAC scores were significantly reduced, and the Hb levels were significantly increased compared with the baseline (both p < 0.01). At the 6-month follow-up, the uterine volume was also significantly reduced relative to baseline in both groups (p < 0.01). However, there were no significant between-group differences in post-treatment outcomes, including uterine volume, VAS score, Hb level, PBAC score, and LNG-IUS expulsion rate. One patient in the experimental group experienced LNG-IUS expulsion during the first postoperative menstrual period. In the control group, gynecological examination at 3 months postoperatively showed that the LNG-IUS was displaced into the cervical canal and vagina in one patient.ConclusionsBoth LNG-IUS fixation modalities were effective treatments for adenomyosis, and the expulsion and displacement rates were reduced compared to those placed conventionally. LNG-IUS fixation using the GyneFix→ IUD system was performed more rapidly and had a lower risk of water overload.Keywords: Menorrhagia, Adenomyosis, Hysteroscopy, Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system, Fixation

Background

Adenomyosis is a benign uterine disorder characterized by the presence of endometrial stroma and glands within the myometrium, and it is typically observed in women of reproductive age [1, 2]. The primary clinical manifestations of adenomyosis include menorrhagia, prolonged menstruation, progressive dysmenorrhea, and secondary infertility [1]. In patients with symptomatic adenomyosis, an LNG-IUS is effective for the treatment of menometrorrhagia, dysmenorrhea, and dyspareunia [3–8], as well as improving the menorrhagia‑related quality of life [9]. It has been established that LNG-IUS therapy for menorrhagia and dysmenorrhea is associated with a reduction in urinary incontinence, along with irritative and obstructive urinary symptoms, in patients with adenomyosis [10]. An LNG-IUS is a more effective first-line therapy than oral agents, based on its efficacy, continuous user-independent mode of administration, and contraceptive benefit [11]. Factors such as heavy menstrual flow and a large uterus are the main causes of LNG-IUS expulsion, which can lead to the termination of treatment, resulting in recurrence of symptoms such as menometrorrhagia, dysmenorrhea, and anemia, thus increasing medical expenses. In 2011, Zhu first reported suture fixation of an LNG-IUS using a hysteroscopic cold-knife surgery system [12]. More LNG-IUS fixation modalities performed via hysteroscopy, including suture or no-suture fixation, have been reported in the literature [6–8, 13–20]. These surgical methods can be mainly classified into three types: (1) sutured fixation of the LNG-IUS [13–19]; (2) bonding of the LNG-IUS with the GyneFix→ intrauterine device (IUD) system (Tianjin Medic Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China) to anchor them to the uterine fundus [8, 20]; and (3) imitating the placement of the GyneFixⓇ IUD system by artificially attaching an absorbable thread to the T-wall of the LNG-IUS, tying a knot at the tail of the thread, and inserting the knot into the muscle layer of the uterine fundus, without consuming the GyneFixⓇ IUD system [6, 7]. All of these surgical fixation methods for fixing an LNG-IUS can decrease its expulsion rate. Studies aimed at determining which method provides the best therapeutic effect are scarce. In this study, the treatment outcomes of patients implanted with an LNG-IUS using the non-sutured GyneFixⓇ IUD method and the sutured fixation method were retrospectively analyzed.

Methods

Patients

This study was approved by the Ethics Committees of The First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University (Zhejiang, China). Clinical data were collected from patients with adenomyosis who had a history of LNG-IUS displacement or expulsion and underwent either LNG-IUS bonding with a GyneFix® IUD or sutured fixation between January 2023 and February 2025 at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University. Before the procedure, all patients underwent a comprehensive evaluation, including gynecological examination, transvaginal ultrasound. The shape and size of the uterus (including transverse diameter), the severity of adenomyosis, and the patient’s general condition were carefully assessed to determine the suitability of the fixation procedure. To minimize selection and performance bias, strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied.

Diagnosis of adenomyosis was made by clinical manifestations and ultrasonography features based on the Morphological Uterus Sonographic Assessment (MUSA) (2022) criteria established by the International Deep Endometriosis Analysis Group [21], which includes direct and indirect features. The direct features include: myometrial cysts, hyperechogenic islands, and echogenic sub-endometrial lines and buds. The indirect features comprise a globular uterus, asymmetrical myometrial thickening, fan-shaped shadowing, trans-lesional vascularity, an irregular junctional zone, and an interrupted junctional zone. A diagnosis was confirmed when at least one direct sign was found, or at least two indirect signs were present if there were no direct signs. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) not suitable for progesterone treatment; (2) post-operative histopathologically verified endometrial cancer; (3) gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist therapy in the preceding 3 months; (4) history of malignancy; and (5) contraindications to hysteroscopic surgery. All surgical procedures and follow-up assessments were performed by the same experienced medical team. After providing patients with detailed information regarding the two surgical approaches, the final decision on the surgical method was made by the patients. All operations were performed by surgeons with sufficient proficiency in both techniques.

Hysteroscopic LNG-IUS-GyneFix® IUD non-sutured fixation

Cervical dilatation to 7 mm was performed using a Hegar dilator, and then a 19.5 Fr hysteroscope equipped with a 15-degree lens (Olympus; Olympus Co., Japan) was inserted. A thorough examination of the cervix and uterine cavity was performed. Endometrial polyps or submucosal fibroids were removed before the LNG-IUS fixation procedure. The procedure was as follows: Step 1, the removed threads of the LNG-IUS were cut off, and the two ends of the GyneFix→ IUD and the LNG-IUS were bundled tightly together using the cut threads; Step 2, the two tied IUDs were placed into the uterine fundus according to the method of GyneFixⓇ IUD placing. The knot at the top of the GyneFixⓇ IUD was placed into the myometrium of the uterine fundus by the GyneFixⓇ applicator. This knot prevents the two tied IUDs from being expelled from the uterine cavity; and Step 3, the LNG-IUS-GyneFixⓇ IUD position was confirmed under hysteroscopy (Fig. 1).

Procedure of hysteroscopic LNG-IUS-GyneFix IUD non-sutured fixation

Procedure of hysteroscopic LNG-IUS suture fixation

The surgery was performed on patients in the lithotomy position. The cervix was gently dilated with Hegar dilators. The size of the Hegar dilators was gradually increased stepwise until dilation was sufficient to accommodate a size 10 Hegar dilator. Operative hysteroscopy was performed using a 26Fr hysteroscope (HEOS, Comeg, France). Before suturing, a thorough examination of the cervix and uterine cavity and a diagnostic curettage were performed. Endometrial polyps or submucosal fibroids were removed prior to suturing. All hysteroscopic sutured fixations of the LNG-IUS were performed by an experienced surgeon under general anesthesia. The procedure was as follows: Step 1, joining a 2 − 0 non-absorbable suture to the LNG-IUS (Bayer Schering Pharma Oy, Germany); Step 2, insertion and suture fixation of the LNG-IUS; Step 3, tying endoscopic knots; Step 4, confirmation of the location of the LNG-IUS and ensuring that the knot was tight (Fig. 2).

Measurements and follow-up

The body mass index (BMI) was calculated as body weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (BMI = weight / height²). The severity of dysmenorrhea was assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS) score, which is a subjective tool for the self-assessment of pain, with possible scores ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (most severe pain). The menstrual volume was evaluated using the Pictorial Blood Loss Assessment Chart (PBAC) score [22]. This method records the number of tampons or pads used and the degree to which they are stained with blood. In this method, light flow was defined as staining covering ≤ 1/3 of the sanitary pad area, moderate flow as staining covering 1/3 to 3/5 of the pad area, and heavy flow as extensive staining covering most of the pad area. Corresponding scores were 1, 5, and 20, respectively. Small and large clots scored 1 and 5, respectively. The total PBAC score was derived by summing the sanitary napkin score and the clot score, with a threshold of ≥ 100 points indicating menorrhagia (corresponding to > 80 mL of blood loss). Uterine size, under ultrasound, was calculated as 0.52 × transverse diameter × long diameter × anteroposterior diameter (cm3). Venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected from all patients before treatment and at 6 months post-treatment. The reference range of was the serum hemoglobin 115–150 g/L for women. The operative time was defined as the time from the hysteroscope being placed in the uterine cavity to the completion of surgery, minus the time to resect intrauterine lesions. Follow-ups were performed at 1, 3, and 6 months post-operatively, and routine three-dimensional ultrasonography was conducted at the follow-ups. Clinical and ultrasound evaluations were recorded at the 6-month post-operative follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Before performing statistical tests, the normality of the continuous variables was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Variables with a normal distribution were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), while non-normally distributed variables were expressed as the median (quartiles 1 and 3). Between-group comparisons of continuous variables were performed using the t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Dichotomous variables between groups were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test. Intra-group comparisons were conducted using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 42 patients were included. All procedures were successful, endometrial diagnostic curettage was performed in all cases, and all postoperative histopathological reports were benign. No intraoperative complications, such as uterine perforation, cervical laceration, water poisoning, or allergies, occurred in any patient. All patients were discharged 24 h postoperatively.

Preoperative clinical data are presented in Table 1. The experimental group included 21 patients, and the age range was 29–53 years, with a mean of 42.52 ± 6.76 years. The control group included 21 patients, and the age range was 32–50 years, with a mean of 40.81 ± 5.04 years. The preoperative age, parity, body weight, BMI, incidence of dysmenorrhea and menorrhagia, uterine volume, VAS score, hemoglobin level, and PBAC score showed no statistically significant differences between the two groups (p > 0.05), indicating baseline comparability.

At the 6-month follow-up after LNG-IUS insertion, both groups exhibited significant reductions in uterine volume, VAS scores, and PBAC scores, as well as significant increases in hemoglobin (Hb) levels, compared with baseline (all p < 0.05). However, no significant between-group differences were observed in these parameters at the 6-month follow-up (Table 2).

In the experimental group, the LNG-IUS-bound GyneFix→ IUD was expelled in one patient during the follow-up, and in one patient, the LNG-IUS was observed in the cervicovaginal area during examination, but no serious complications, such as uterine perforation, were observed. All patients completed the follow-up visits, with a mean duration of 6 months.

Discussion

Our study demonstrated that, regardless of the LNG-IUS fixation modality, both hysteroscopic anchoring and suturing of the LNG-IUS could alleviate menorrhagia, dysmenorrhea, and anemia, as well as achieve moderate reduction in uterine volume in women with adenomyosis at the 6-month follow-up. These findings were consistent with previous studies, supporting the efficacy of LNG-IUS in the treatment of adenomyosis. One patient in each group experienced LNG-IUS expulsion during follow-up, with an expulsion rate of 4.8% in both groups.

Compared with traditional drug treatment, an LNG-IUS showed superior improvement in patients with dysmenorrhea and menorrhagia, and had fewer drug-related side effects [23], and can also reduce uterine volume [6, 8, 24]. Among patients undergoing conservative surgery, maintenance therapy with LNG-IUS was effective and well tolerated. At 12 and 24 months of follow-up, the LNG-IUS group exhibited greater improvements in dysmenorrhea relief, lower serum CA-125 levels, and higher Hb levels compared with the control group without LNG-IUS [25].

Expulsion or displacement of the LNG-IUS significantly compromises its therapeutic efficacy, and spontaneous expulsion represents a common cause of treatment failure. Previous studies have reported spontaneous expulsion rates of the traditional LNG-IUS insertion ranging from 9.1% to 37.5% [26, 27]. Among patients with adenomyosis, the 1-, 2-, and 3-year spontaneous expulsion rates of the LNG-IUS were 9.1%, 10.6%, and 11.1%, respectively, compared with 3.6%, 4.1%, and 4.6% in those with a normal uterus (p = 0.008) [26]. Enlarged uterine volume and menorrhagia in patients with adenomyosis are well-recognize risk factors for LNG-IUS expulsion [24]. Uterine volume ≥ 200 cm³ predicts hysterectomy or IUS expulsion in patients with adenomyosis [24]. Although pretreatment with GnRH-a may reduce LNG-IUS early expulsion rates, the effect diminishes over time as the uterus gradually regrows, leading to an increased expulsion rate [28]. Given the high expulsion rate of traditional LNG-IUS insertion in patients with adenomyosis, various hysteroscopic fixation techniques of LNG-IUS have been proposed as a second-line treatment option for patients who have experienced expulsion after the first traditional insertion. In our study, the expulsion rates of the LNG-IUS at 6 months post-operation were both 4.8%, which is lower than that of traditional placement.

Various studies reported different expulsion rates of the surgical fixation techniques for LNG-IUS. Zhang et al. [14] reported that 12 patients with adenomyosis underwent LNG-IUS suture fixation using a hysteroscopic cold knife system. Long-term follow-up revealed significantly reduced menstrual flow, relieved dysmenorrhea, and improved quality of life, with only one case of device expulsion at 1 year. Lv et al. [16]reported an expulsion rate of only 2.6% (2/79) in patients treated with hysteroscopic sutured fixation. Similarly, Mao et al. [17]reported no expulsions at 1-year follow-up after sutured fixation in patients with large symptomatic adenomyosis. Lai et al. [19] also observed no expulsions or displacements during 6–24 months of follow-up in women of adenomyosis underwent LNG-IUS.

Suture fixation generally requires an operating room setting, experienced hysteroscopic surgeons, and specialized instruments including a needle holder and cold knife system. In recent years, several non-sutured fixation techniques which is less demanding on equipment have been introduced. In 2024, Zhou et al. [20] introduced a GyneFix-assisted LNG-IUD fixation technique under hysteroscopy, which was minimally invasive, convenient, cost-effective, and suitable for outpatient settings. Liang et al. [7] reported a simpler anchoring needle fixation with shorter operative time and less distension fluid, although one case of expulsion occurred at 3 months postoperatively. Yu et al. [6] described a fixation technique using micro-scissors and ring forceps. No expulsion was observed, and significant symptomatic improvement was achieved during a mean follow-up of 6.0 ± 1.8 months postoperatively. Consistent with our study, even without suturing, non-sutured hysteroscopic fixation effectively stabilizes the LNG-IUS in the uterine cavity. In the non-sutured group of our study device expulsion may be attributed to not embedded into sufficient uterine myometrium of GyneFix IUD.

We demonstrated that both non-sutured LNG-IUS placement assisted by GyneFix IUD and sutured fixation under hysteroscopy significantly reduced the expulsion rate. Compared with the conventional LNG-IUS insertion, these procedures increase medical costs about 500USD, but they can prevent severe anemia caused by massive bleeding and avoid hysterectomy. Prior to performing such fixation procedures, a comprehensive evaluation of the patient’s medical history, uterine size, and general condition is mandatory. The cost of the GyneFix IUD non-sutured fixation procedure is slightly lower than that of the suture fixation procedure. The main cost difference comes from the suture materials and the longer operative time of the suture fixation procedure.

This study has some limitations. No sample size calculation or power analysis was performed before the study, which is a limitation of this retrospective study. The sample size was determined based on the number of eligible patients during the study period. The follow-up period was short, and determining the risk of long-term expulsion requires longer follow-up. Multicenter-controlled studies with larger sample sizes are required. The study was not powered to establish equivalence between the two procedures, and the results should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusions

The results of this study showed that the two methods of fixing an LNG-IUS have the same therapeutic effect on adenomyosis. The study demonstrates no statistically significant difference, but it is not powered to establish equivalence. The anchoring LNG-IUS has low requirements for hardware and can be performed more rapidly; therefore, this surgical procedure should be adopted by more medical institutions.

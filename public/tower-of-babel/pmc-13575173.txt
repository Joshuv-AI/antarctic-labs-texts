Hybrid TSA-TLBO optimization of an EF2 resonant DC-DC converter for energy-efficient fuel cell electric vehicles with reduced battery dependency
Authors: Joe Marshell Manuel Raj, Mahesh Kumar Reddy Vennapusa, Sivaranjani Subramani, Gunapriya Devarajan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) with a lower battery dependency have a big problem in terms of the stability of voltage, high dynamic response and low torque ripple under different load conditions. To overcome these challenges, this paper presents a Tunicate Swarm Algorithm and Teaching–Learning-Based Optimization (TSA-TLBO) optimized hybrid Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) and Fractional-Order PID (PID-FOPID) control strategy to enhance the regulation of a PEMFC-based powertrain. This proposed controller operates at the converter and motor drive stage and it is coupled with an Elongation Factor 2 (EF2) resonant DC-DC converter to increase voltage stabilization and power conversion efficiency. The hybrid optimization approach effectively tunes controller parameters to minimize speed and current tracking errors under dynamic operating conditions. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed system achieves a maximum efficiency of 90.7% with an output power of 187 W. The harmonic analysis was performed at the three-phase inverter output supplying the PMSM. The measured voltage total harmonic distortion (THD) and current THD are 0.21% and 1.78%, respectively. The results also show enhanced dynamic performance and reduced torque fluctuations compared to conventional control methods. These results prove that the proposed solution enhances considerably control performance and allows working with FCEVs that have less auxiliary energy storage efficiently.

Introduction

The world has heavily relied on fossil fuels like coal, natural gas and oil as the main sources of energy over the past few decades1. Even though these non-renewable energy sources have the potential to provide massive energy provision, they have caused a lot of problems in the environment, such as greenhouse gases, air pollution, and global warming2. Additionally, due to their limited nature of their supply and the growing environmental issues, the adoption of clean and sustainable energy sources has been hastened3. In this regard, renewable energy technologies have proved to be a useful option in terms of power generation as well as transportation use4. FC technology has acquired a lot of interest among them, especially in the EV industry, because it operates at a high efficiency and is environmental friendly5. FCs process hydrogen to electrical energy and the process is electrochemical, without combustion, and is more efficient than traditional ICEs6,7. This makes FCVs a promising alternative for next-generation transportation systems.

Zhou, J. et al.8 report various types of FCs, including alkaline, molten carbonate, phosphoric acid, microbial FCs, solid oxide, and direct methanol FCs. Though all these possess their own distinct features and applications, for EVs, especially PEMFC seems to be a most viable alternative9. Adding to this point,10 lists out the significant features of PEMFC as their robustness, compactness, ability to operate at lower pressures and temperatures, start swiftly, provide high energy density with noiseless operation. Thus,11 suggests this cell as an ideal choice for electric cars where efficient and responsive power sources are vital to suit the on-and-off driving nature in urban environments. All these developments convey the shift in energy ecosystem towards sustainable and environmentally friendly nature, with the help of hybrid energy sources and technological advancements12.

A common limitation that comes along with the EVs is their additional weight owing to the presence of larger battery packs, which is necessary for storing and delivering the electricity13. This extra weight caused by the battery pack presence may also have an impact on the vehicle handling and its performance14. Another major concern is the time taken for charging an EV which is much higher than the time taken for refuelling a conventional gas or diesel-based vehicles15. In a traditional method of EV charging, full charge can be achieved after several hours of waiting time. This is direct contradictory to the case of conventional filling of a gas tank associated with quick stops16. Also, as against ICE vehicles, even the more affordable EV designs have only a limited driving range17. This restriction may lead to creation of a ‘range anxiety’, where the drivers will have the doubt on reaching the destination with the available power18.

These challenges can be addressed using FC-based automobiles that are powered with hydrogen gas19. Usually, in a FC automotive system, any of the following three motors will be preferred for power: BLDC Motors, SRM and PMSM. BLDC Motors, the commonly employed motors suffer from overheating problems and torque vibrations at higher speeds. The SRM Motors, though robust, cannot provide precise control for FCVs. Comparatively, PMSM Motor have high efficiency and power density20. Thus, advanced methodologies are required to improve the performance of the control system.

Although FCEVs have positive performance, various technical issues continue to constrain the performance, especially when the battery dependency is lower. FC systems are extremely responsive to load changes in the small auxiliary energy storage system creating voltage variability, higher torque ripple and poorer motor drive dynamic performance. Conventional control approaches, such as classical PID controllers, often fail to provide adequate robustness under nonlinear and time-varying operating conditions associated with FC systems. Despite the recent literature on advanced control techniques, there is still a gap in the literature about constructive hybrid control solutions that could achieve high dynamic response, higher voltage regulation, and lower torque ripple in FCV applications. Also, little focus has been on the coordinated combination of optimized controllers with resonant converter structures, including the EF2 converter, to condition power better. Thus, there is an identified gap in research in order to design a regime of optimum and dynamic methods of control that can guarantee stable power supply and proper monitoring of load demand, uphold voltage and enhance the overall performance of the system in PEMFC EV with less reliance on batteries.

The EM and control functions of the proposed system need to be differentiated. The EM layer calculates the amount of power required due to driving conditions and assigns how much must be contributed by the FC and auxiliary energy storage system. By comparison, the hybrid PID-FOPID controller function at control level and thus provides proper regulation of voltage and response to dynamic changes through tracking of the reference signals. Therefore, the proposed controller is not considered a standalone EM system but an optimized control strategy that enhances the effectiveness of the overall EM framework.

The proposed research does not introduce a high-level EM strategy. Instead, it focuses on the control layer that accurately tracks the reference signals generated by an external EM system. The proposed hybrid PID-FOPID controller is implemented at the converter and motor drive control level to enhance voltage regulation, dynamic response, and system stability under varying operating conditions. Therefore, the primary objective of this work is to improve the control performance of the power conversion and motor drive system, thereby supporting the overall EM framework of PEMFC-based EVs.

Contributions and novelty

The proposed work does not claim novelty in the individual components, namely the EF2 resonant DC-DC converter, TSA, TLBO, PID controller, or FOPID controller. Instead, the novelty lies in the hybrid TSA-TLBO optimization of a hybrid PID-FOPID controller integrated with the EF2 resonant converter for PEMFC-based EVs with reduced battery dependency, resulting in improved voltage regulation, dynamic response, converter stability, and reduced torque ripple.

Recent research work: a brief review

Considerable research has been devoted to FC-based EVs, focusing on EM, power converter design, intelligent controller development, and optimization techniques. The following review summarizes the most relevant contributions in these areas.

According to Kannan, R & Sundharajan, V21, PEMFCs can be regarded as the most efficient and feasible renewable energy resources that can find greater role in automobile applications. Till now, the research focus was on confronting issues related to maximum power extraction from FCs, speed control; torque ripples generated in BLDC motors for a FC based EV systems. Attaining optimal energy efficiency by varying the gain control parameters in an existing MPPT technique is still a challenge in FCs. To overcome this issue, a novel technique namely UFENN-MPPT is introduced in this work. UFENN, a machine learning methodology, effectively optimized the parameters to extract maximum energy from the FCs. Further, an interleaved SEPIC converter is also introduced to minimize the power failure and to regulate the voltage output.

Wang, J. et al.22 have suggested a new EM technique for an FCs-based hybrid commercial vehicle. The introduced EM perspective depends on the double delay deep deterministic policy gradient and can be employed into this topological framework. The main aim of this methodology is to optimize the damage caused in FC, damage caused in Battery and usage of FC Hydrogen. To match the battery output power and FC output power, Supercapacitors were introduced, which has the capacity to expand the possibility of optimization for both the storage systems. This methodology is found to improve the efficiency of energy storage system and its lifespan, which is much better than that offered by Non-linear Programming Algorithm and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient. This was possibly by reducing the battery damage, FC damage and hydrogen usage. Another notable feature of the newly introduced deep reinforcement learning based EM System is its ability to optimize several indicators simultaneously, thereby extending the active life of the power system.

Meshginqalam, A., & Bauman23 supports for convex optimization where the complete vehicle loss computations are executed in real-time using two different innovative techniques. As per the first strategy called as integrated technique, the efficiency of the FC is iteratively employed into the speed control algorithm. With the second strategy, the speed problem is addressed at the beginning, before transforming into an EM system concern.

An EV integrated with solar panel and grid has been developed by Kavin K.S., & Karuvelam P.S.24 like an electric train structure. Further, a novel interleaved SEPIC converter is introduced to compensate for the low duty cycle operation. This converter is responsible for producing stepped up DC voltage with minimal switching losses. The output of the converter is connected to the grid using Single Phase Inverter and to the BLDC motor fed electric train using Three Phase Inverters. The switching operation of Inverters are controlled using a PI Controller. Thus, the available solar energy is completely utilized without wasting, by sending the output of PV panel to the electric train and grid, the whole day. During nighttime, the EV is powered by the grid with suitable converter arrangement. Thus, a single converter configuration functioning on dual source has been realized.

For EVs using dual inverters with dual electric drive systems, Pathmanathan et al.25 suggests incorporating FCs as one of the sources, where the other source is the conventional EV battery system. For each component of EV driving cycle like retractable brakes, suitable power sharing algorithm have been employed. This algorithm ensures that one form of energy flow occurs from FC and the unnecessary shutdown of FC is minimized with controlled energy flow. Also, this algorithm takes cares of injecting the required reactive current and power transfer to FC, whenever transients occur due to sudden mechanical power reduction. Thus, with the introduced algorithm, the rapid mechanical power transitions are properly handled while upholding the slowly varying power reference of FC.

Silva et al.26 have presented a comprehensive algorithm made of interactive Adaptive Weight GA and multi objective evolutionary algorithm for optimizing the distribution of energy and the schematic of FC-EV Battery-Ultracapacitor HESS. The focus of this introduced method is minimizing the usage of hydrogen fuel and the size of the HESS, while enhancing the battery span, vehicle’s driving span and accumulation of FCs. The power requirement is divided among the HESS, FCs, ultracapacitor pack and the traditional battery, using fuzzy based EM strategy.

Another hybrid arrangement for EM of a FC EV system powered with battery is suggested by Saravanan, R et al.27. The hybrid technique introduced is referred to as JSO-RSA method, a combination of JSO and RSA. The main objective of this JSO-RSA method is to adjust the dynamic power factor, control the state of the machine and its functioning mode and minimize the proportional usage. Another notable feature identified in this research work is the optimization of battery charging with lessened amount of hydrogen.

Tehrani et al. 28 have provided a thorough review of AI application in PESs and its usage throughout the system life cycle, which encompass design, control, optimization, and maintenance. The model of the work represents PESs as multidimensional systems of large-scale interconnections based on energy sources, converters, controllers, sensors, and loads where AI methods were introduced to solve multidimensional problems. It introduces a lifecycle-oriented framework that systematically categorizes AI-driven methods for design automation and intelligent converter control, while also structuring existing studies based on validation approaches.

Tehrani et al.29 have illustrated a thorough overview of AI use in maintenance and forecasting stages of PESs in the lifecycle perspective introduced in Part I. It is centered on AI-based methods of health monitoring, fault and anomaly detection, and RUL prediction of power electronic items and converters. The work also addresses advanced applications such as soft-fault detection in complex system-of-systems environments (e.g., aircraft wiring networks). Moreover, it introduces AI-based forecasting methods of determining influential PES parameters, such as performance, thermal performance, component durability, load requirement, and energy output. The survey provides a structured overview of AI methodologies for enabling predictive and autonomous maintenance in next-generation PES.

Sankar et al.30 introduced a hybrid Modified Boost-Cuk converter based on PV for battery-less EV traction systems, combined with a PMSM drive, with PSO-ANFIS optimizing the DC-link control and the SVPWM based VSI ensuring smooth operation of the motor, efficient power conversion and better utilization of energy.

Bodkhe et al.31 have presented a hybrid EM strategy in battery-free FC vehicles by using GOA-HGCNN to optimize the control of the DC-DC converter and predictive learning for voltage stability, reducing system cost, improving hydrogen utilization and making the system work efficiently without any auxiliary energy storage.

A self-learning robust control method of DC to DC buck converters has been shown by Gangula et al.32 to combine a simple single-hidden-layer neural network into a backstepping model to estimate and compensate load variations and disturbances to achieve asymptotic stability and better voltage tracking and current regulation behavior.

Mishra et al.33 have presented a cost-effective HSAPF for harmonic and reactive power compensation. The system comprises of a RECKF for estimation of reference currents, IFGO optimized SAFPIDC for tuning the controller parameters and SMC to generate switching pulses for the VSC.

Nanda et al. 34 have suggested a HSAPF for harmonic mitigation and reactive power compensation. The approach uses a KF to estimate the reference current, an AFPIDC optimized by the TLBO algorithm and SMC to generate the VSC switching pulses.

Mishra et al.35 have presented a novel SHAF for Reactive power and Harmonic Compensation. The method is based on reference current estimation by KF, tuning of the T2FFOPIDC by hPSOFA, and performance improvement of the filter by source-side current-based compensation.

Mishra et al.36 have suggested an HSAPF for Reactive power and harmonic compensation. An hSCATLBO is used to tune an IT2FPID controller to control the filter under nonlinear loading conditions.

The recent studies confirm the successful application of hybrid metaheuristic optimization algorithms to tune the intelligent controllers for the power electronic applications, especially in PQ enhancement. However, these studies primarily focus on harmonic mitigation and PQ applications rather than PEMFC-based EV power conversion. The present work applies a hybrid TSA-TLBO optimization strategy to tune a hybrid PID-FOPID controller for an EF2 resonant DC-DC converter in a PEMFC-based EV, thereby extending hybrid optimization techniques to a different application domain with distinct control objectives.

Although recent optimization-based and intelligent controller design methods have demonstrated improved control accuracy and dynamic performance, many are associated with increased computational complexity or have been developed primarily for PQ enhancement and converter control rather than PEMFC-based EV power conversion. Furthermore, most existing PEMFC-EV studies rely on conventional DC-DC converter topologies or hybrid energy storage architectures with relatively large battery capacities. To overcome these constraints, the suggested system combines a hybrid TSA-TLBO optimized PID-FOPID controller with a high-gain EF2 resonant DC-DC converter to improve voltage regulation, lower switching losses, improve dynamic response, and lessen reliance on high-capacity auxiliary battery storage. Background of the recent research work

The literature reviewed indicates significant progress in the development of PEMFC-based EV systems and their EM and control strategies. Nevertheless, several limitations remain in the existing approaches. In21, the research focuses on maximizing power extraction from PEMFCs using an optimized MPPT technique. Although the proposed approach improves energy utilization, it primarily addresses the FC side and does not comprehensively consider motor drive performance, torque ripple reduction, or overall vehicle dynamics. The approach described in22 presents an EM strategy based on deep reinforcement learning that integrates supercapacitor and battery storage devices. The dependence on hybrid energy storage raises system complexity, cost, and weight even as it improves system efficiency and component lifetime. In23, convex optimization is employed to minimize vehicle energy losses; however, the computational burden may limit its practical implementation under rapidly changing driving conditions. The work in24 presents a dual-source EV system using a high-gain interleaved SEPIC converter for PV-grid integration, but it does not address PEMFC dynamics or advanced intelligent controller optimization. The power-sharing strategy introduced in25 effectively coordinates FC and battery power; however, it remains dependent on battery-assisted operation. Similarly, the hybrid EM approach in26 improves system performance through multi-objective optimization but increases computational complexity and does not explicitly address converter-level dynamic performance. The hybrid JSO-RSA optimization strategy in27 enhances battery charging and hydrogen utilization, yet it continues to rely on conventional converter architectures and battery-assisted designs. Recent developments have also demonstrated the growing application of AI and hybrid optimization techniques in power electronic systems. The comprehensive surveys presented in28 and29 summarize AI-based approaches for power electronic system design, intelligent control, predictive maintenance, and forecasting, highlighting the increasing importance of intelligent optimization in modern converter systems. In30, a PSO-ANFIS-based controller is applied to a modified Boost-Cuk converter for battery-free EV traction, while31 suggests a GOA-HGCNN-based hybrid EM strategy for battery-free FCVs. Furthermore, Gangula et al.32 developed a self-learning neural network-based robust controller for DC-DC buck converters to improve voltage regulation under load disturbances. Hybrid metaheuristic optimization techniques have also been successfully applied to intelligent controller tuning for power electronic systems. Mishra et al.33 employed IFGO to optimize a fuzzy-PID controller, Nanda et al.34 utilized TLBO for adaptive fuzzy-PID tuning, Mishra et al.35 presented an hPSOFA-optimized Type-2 FOPID controller, and Mishra et al.36 introduced an hSCA-TLBO optimized IT2FPID controller. Although these methods demonstrate the effectiveness of hybrid optimization algorithms for controller tuning, they are primarily developed for PQ enhancement and active power filter applications rather than PEMFC-based EV power conversion. These research gaps motivate the present work, which integrates a high-gain EF2 resonant DC-DC converter with a hybrid TSA-TLBO optimized hierarchical PID-FOPID controller to achieve improved voltage regulation, enhanced dynamic response, reduced torque ripple, reduced switching losses, and reduced dependence on high-capacity auxiliary battery storage in PEMFC-based EV applications.

Configuration of the proposed PEMFC EV with reduced battery dependency

FCVs when employed with large numbers of fuel stacks, can make the whole system to be expensive. Hence, as an alternate, this research work suggests use of FCVs locally powered by Hydrogen gas (H2). This combination provides a good alternative to ICEs, by exhibiting higher efficiency. This could be possible as the tailpipe emissions are eliminated and only the water and heat are produced as outputs, adding to the environmental benefits. Thus, the feasibility of adopting the proposed hydrogen powered FCVs, their advantages in terms of reduced environmental impacts and operational costs37 are showcased in this research work. To facilitate this work, two controllers namely PID and FOPID are also utilized, using which the torque ripple and speed of the PMSM motor can be controlled. To determine the best operating settings of the PID and FOPID controllers, hybrid TSA-TLBO algorithm is employed. This way of optimal fine-tuning helps in effectively controlling the vehicle torque and speed. As against the conventional system, the proposed method reduces the overall cost and size of the FC stack. Further, a steady voltage level to the PMSM fed drive is maintained under all the operating conditions with reduced dependence on high-capacity battery storage while maintaining stable operation. Introduction of EF2 resonant converter to power the FCV helps in minimizing the fluctuations in the motor torque and enables the system to perform better under all scenarios, even under conditions of limited auxiliary energy storage. It is important to clarify that the proposed architecture does not assume a completely battery-free operation, but rather a system with reduced battery dependency supported by a minimal auxiliary energy storage element (e.g., a small battery or supercapacitor). This auxiliary unit is responsible for handling short-duration transient power demands, regenerative braking energy, and sudden load variations, which cannot be instantaneously supplied by the FC due to its slower dynamic response. Meanwhile, the FC operates as the primary energy source under steady-state conditions. The proposed hybrid PID-FOPID control strategy, together with the EF2 resonant converter, ensures fast voltage regulation and mitigates transient fluctuations, thereby reducing the stress and size requirements of the auxiliary storage system instead of eliminating it entirely.

Although PEMFC-based EVs reduce reliance on large-capacity batteries, the inclusion of an auxiliary energy storage system remains essential. The dynamic response of the FC system is slow, and the battery's transient response characteristics are important to meet transient load requirements, regenerative braking and battery system performance. This means that PEMFC EVs are usually configured as hybrid systems, with a fuel cell as the main energy source and the battery as a secondary energy buffer. However, the presence of a battery introduces additional weight, which is often considered a limitation. PEMFC EVs require much smaller battery capacity than conventional BEVs. For instance, while a typical BEV may require a battery pack in the range of 40–100 kWh (resulting in substantial weight), a PEMFC EV generally employs a much smaller battery (typically 1–10 kWh), thereby considerably reducing battery-related mass. Although the FC stack and hydrogen storage system add weight, the overall system benefits from improved energy density and faster refueling capability. Modelling of EF2 resonant converter

The superiority of EF2 Resonant converters in delivering notable performance as against the conventional DC-DC converters makes them the optimal choice for WPT systems. With properly tuned quality factor, the resonant circuit will be capable of producing a significantly higher output voltage approximately about 10 times, with the lowest duty ratio of 0.5 and reduced impact on output current3,40–42. Figure 3 demonstrates the EF2 Resonant Converter circuit diagram consisting of stages like rectification, transformer coupling and inverter sections. The transformer shown in Fig. 3 is utilized as a high-frequency coupled energy transfer element rather than for galvanic isolation. Therefore, the primary and secondary reference nodes are intentionally connected, representing a common ground configuration consistent with the non-isolated EF2 resonant converter topology43. The proposed EF2 resonant converter is implemented using a single-switch configuration. Hence, only switch S₁ is employed, and an additional switch (S₂) is not required. This simplifies the control structure while maintaining effective resonant operation.Fig. 3Circuit diagram of class EF2 resonant converter.

Circuit diagram of class EF2 resonant converter.

The voltage gain enhancement capability of the proposed EF2 resonant DC-DC converter is achieved through the combined effect of resonant energy exchange and transformer action. In general, for any DC-DC converter, the power balance principle must be satisfied:7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ P_{IN} \, = \,P_{OUT} \,\,\,\, \Rightarrow \,\,V_{IN} \,I_{IN} = \,V_{OUT} \,I_{OUT} $$\end{document}

This implies that an increase in output voltage is typically accompanied by a corresponding adjustment in output current. Therefore, the statement of “gain boost without output current loss” is not strictly valid in an ideal sense and requires clarification.

In the proposed EF2 resonant converter, the voltage gain can be expressed as:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ G\, = \,\frac{{V_{OUT} }}{{V_{IN} }}\, = \,n\, \cdot \,Q \cdot \,f\,\left( w \right) $$\end{document}where n represents the transformer turns ratio, Q is the quality factor of the resonant tank, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f\,\left( w \right)$$\end{document} denotes the frequency-dependent gain characteristic. At resonance, efficient energy exchange occurs between the inductive and capacitive elements, minimizing reactive power loss and reducing switching losses due to near ZVS operation.

The converter operates with high efficiency, and the majority of the input power is effectively transferred to the output. This leads to a condition where the output current does not experience significant degradation despite the increase in output voltage. The maximum efficiency of the converter in this study is approximately 90.7%, which shows that there is minimal power loss during the conversion process.

The selection of the EF2 resonant converter in this study is motivated by its capability to achieve high voltage gain with reduced switching losses under low-duty-cycle operation, which is particularly suitable for FCEV applications with reduced battery dependency. Unlike conventional LLC resonant converters, which typically require operation near resonance and complex control strategies to maintain ZVS, the EF2 converter provides a wider soft-switching range and maintains efficient operation even under varying load conditions. For instance, an LLC-based converter, such as the converter introduced in 44 is very efficient and requires frequency control and complexity in the converter design to handle wide variations in input/output. The EF2 topology inherently supports high step-up conversion with simpler control and reduced switching stress, making it more suitable for applications where the FC output voltage is relatively low and fluctuating. Moreover, the EF2 converter allows fewer components and better power density because these measures are essential in compact EV powertrain design. Hence, the EF2 resonant converter is chosen in this work since it is a balanced solution with high gain, less losses, and less complex implementation than traditional resonant topologies.

The proposed EF2 resonant converter is being applied as a non-isolated topology which has its benefits as well as drawbacks to the FCEV. On an advantage perspective, non-isolated converters have less component count, lower conduction and switching losses, compact, and more efficient designs, so are appropriate in the low- to medium-power range, as well as in scaled prototype validation. These aspects are especially useful in this research as the emphasis here lies in the assessment of control performance and converter dynamics in the conditions of reduced battery dependency. Galvanic isolation is generally necessary in working FCEV systems to guarantee user safety, fault protection and EMC performance. In this respect, it can be considered that the use of non-isolated topology is a drawback in direct application to full-scale automotive systems. In response to this, it is possible to add on the proposed architecture, a high-frequency isolation stage, e.g. an isolated DC-DC converter, or transformer-based topology in future designs. Therefore, the non-isolated EF2 converter used in this work should be interpreted as a simplified and efficient configuration for control validation and performance analysis, while its adaptation to real-world FCEV systems would require appropriate isolation mechanisms to meet safety and regulatory requirements.

The converter's operation modes

The operation of converter can be considered as two different modes. Initially, S1 Switch is at off condition. Input current is provided to the L2-C2 and L3-C3 resonant tanks2. Once the exchange of energy occurs among the two resonant tanks, the primary of the transformer will start experiencing voltage oscillations. The secondary side of the step-up transformer with 1:5 turns ratio, acquires the generated voltage. As a result, this secondary side voltage is rectified by the diode and used to supply power to the motor. This switch activation indicates the beginning of the second operation mode, where the inductor L1 starts charging. In this way of repeated actions, L2-C2 and L3-C3 resonant tanks involves in the exchange of energy, thereby replicating the operation of a DC-DC converter.

Speed and current control of PMSM drive

Among the various power control techniques available for PMSM drives, vector control provides superior dynamic performance because of its ability to decouple torque and flux control. To design the hybrid PID-FOPID controllers accurately, an appropriate machine model is required. These controllers are generally susceptible to external disturbances such as load variations and parameter fluctuations. Hence, optimal tuning of the controller parameters is necessary to achieve an effective operating range. In this regard, the proposed TSA-TLBO approach effectively compensates for these disturbances and provides fast dynamic response with reduced torque ripple under varying driving conditions. The reference torque is generated by the speed controller according to the error between the reference speed and the measured speed3,53–58. The optimization process is performed prior to system operation, and the obtained optimal controller parameters are subsequently used during simulation. Therefore, the proposed method does not involve online adaptation but rather an offline optimized control strategy designed to perform effectively under varying operating conditions. The reported efficiency is that of operation under the operating conditions studied here, but primarily considering the objective of increasing voltage regulation, dynamic response and reducing torque ripple, not just optimizing the converter hardware to maximize its efficiency. Although the efficiency is lower than that reported for some optimized high-power resonant converter implementations, the proposed converter demonstrates superior voltage gain, stable operation, effective transient performance, and reduced battery dependency under the considered operating conditions. Therefore, the proposed EF2 resonant converter, together with the TSA-TLBO optimized PID-FOPID controller, provides an effective trade-off between conversion efficiency, dynamic performance, implementation complexity, and suitability for PEMFC-based EV applications.

While there are many benefits to the EF2 resonant converter, including a high voltage gain, low switching losses and better voltage regulation in the specified operating range, there are some drawbacks. Like traditional class-E resonant converters, the ZVS condition is not guaranteed for the entire operating range and the converter efficiency can drop under light load operating conditions because of departures from the optimum resonant operating point. But, in most normal driving situations, EV propulsion systems are required to run under medium to high load conditions, where the EF2 converter can effectively operate resonantly. Further, the proposed TSA-TLBO optimized PID-FOPID controller enhances voltage regulation and dynamic response in the investigated operating range which makes the EF2 resonant converter appropriate for the considered application even though it has light-load limitation.

Proposed TSA-TLBO approach based gain parameter tuning

For optimally tuning of the control parameters of the hybrid PID–FOPID controller, this proposed work is based on TSA–TLBO method. The main aim of this optimization technique is to reduce the tracking errors of speed and current in order to enhance voltage regulation, dynamic response, converter stability and torque ripple performance of the EV powertrain with a PEMFC. The motivation behind the use of the hybrid TSA-TLBO optimization algorithm is that the two types of metaheuristic techniques are used for controller parameter tuning as complementary. TSA has demonstrated good global search ability, which can be used to select the regions of the search space with good exploration efficiency, and avoid the problem of early convergence to local optimum. Unlike that, TLBO can effectively carry out local exploitation and parameter refinement without using any algorithm-specific control parameters so that it can achieve better convergence to the optimum solution. The hybrid TSA-TLBO algorithm is able to adaptively balance the exploration and exploitation more effectively due to the complementary properties of both the TSA and TLBO algorithms. Thus, the hybrid PID-FOPID controller shows a better voltage regulation, dynamic response, less torque ripple, and better converter stability under the different operating conditions. The details of the proposed TSA-TLBO optimization algorithm are presented in the following subsection.

TSA-TLBO algorithm

TSA is one of the bio-inspired metaheuristic optimization techniques, whose behavioural model is derived from the swarm behaviour of the tunicates. This particular swarm behaviour enables the tunicates to persist effectively even at the depth of the ocean. Tunicates generally own the notable ability of locating sources of food in the sea. Two of their exceptional characteristics are their capacity to behave in swarms while seeking the food in marine ocean and proficiency in propelling themselves as jet. While tracing out the best food source, they combine these two characteristics. In this work, TLBO is incorporated to effectively enhance the performance of TSA behaviour. These together are expected to enhance the behaviour of the FC EV system. As the name suggests, the TLBO approach concentrates on creating the impact that a teacher does on a student. This is also a nature-inspired and a population-based technique, which searches the set of solutions and produces global solution. The group or class of learners are referred to as “population” in this approach. The TLBO approach progresses as Teacher in the first phase and as Learner in the second phase. In the first phase namely Teacher, the learner receives the knowledge from the teacher and during the second phase called Learner, there will be dynamic exchanges between the learner and the instructor59–63. The TSA-TLBO algorithm is executed offline during the controller design stage to obtain the optimal PID-FOPID controller parameters. The optimization was carried out in MATLAB R2020a using a population size of 30 and a maximum of 100 iterations, requiring an average execution time of approximately 18.6 s on an Intel Core i7 (3.40 GHz) computer with 16 GB RAM. The optimization is carried out offline when designing the converter so there is no extra computation during the operation of the converter. The optimized controller gains remain fixed during operation, making the online computational complexity comparable to that of a conventional PID-FOPID controller. The controller parameters are optimized under representative operating conditions and tested under load disturbances, and the results indicate a stable voltage regulation and an acceptable dynamic performance across the range of operation under test conditions. These results confirm the robustness and practical applicability of the proposed control strategy for the operating conditions considered in this study.

The proposed TSA-TLBO algorithm is employed solely for offline controller parameter optimization and addresses low-level converter and motor control rather than a standalone EM strategy. Therefore, the optimization is executed only during the controller design stage, while the optimized PID-FOPID gains are used during real-time operation.

In the present work, the term "reduced battery dependency" refers to reducing the reliance on the auxiliary battery for transient power support through improved voltage regulation and dynamic power delivery provided by the EF₂ resonant converter together with the TSA-TLBO optimized PID-FOPID controller. The primary objective of this study is to improve converter and motor drive control performance rather than to perform battery energy storage sizing. Therefore, the present work does not quantify the reduction in battery capacity (kWh) relative to a baseline configuration. Instead, the proposed approach demonstrates that improved converter regulation and faster dynamic response reduce the requirement for frequent auxiliary battery support under the simulated operating conditions.

A detailed validation scheme is included to ensure that the proposed control strategy is reliable and robust. The performance of the method is assessed by detailed simulation studies carried out under different operating conditions. The system is shown to be robust to such changes in load and operating parameters by performing a sensitivity analysis. The stability of the control strategy is guaranteed by optimal tuning of the controller parameters with the TSA-TLBO algorithm that is used to obtain a minimum oscillation and bounded system response under changing conditions. The system parameters, such as the gains of the controller, switching frequency and converter components are determined according to standard design rules and optimized for better dynamic response. Although experimental or hardware-in-the-loop validation is not included in the present study, it is considered as a future extension to further validate the practical applicability of the proposed system.

Practical implementation considerations and application scope

Although the proposed system is validated through MATLAB simulations, practical implementation of the EF2 resonant converter and the hybrid PID-FOPID control strategy involves several hardware-level challenges that must be carefully addressed. The converter implementation aspect of the EF2 resonant converter is the difficulty of designing the inductors and capacitors to maintain resonance at varying loads. The resonant frequency and overall system performance is subject to component tolerances, parasitic resistances and non-ideal switching characteristics. In addition, achieving consistent ZVS in hardware is more challenging due to switching delays and device non-linearities. The implementation of the hybrid PID-FOPID controller also introduces computational complexity. Real-time realization of fractional-order controllers requires approximation techniques such as Oustaloup filters, which increase memory usage and processing requirements. Therefore, the selection of an appropriate digital controller platform (such as DSP or FPGA) is critical to ensure stable and fast control execution. Thermal management is another important consideration. Switching frequencies and resonant operation can result in higher switching and conduction losses in semiconductor devices, requiring proper heat dissipation mechanisms to ensure the device's reliable operation, including heat sinks or active cooling systems. Another important issue in high-frequency converters is electromagnetic interference (EMI). The fast switching transitions and resonant currents can introduce conducted and radiated EMI, which must be mitigated using proper shielding, filtering, and PCB layout techniques to comply with automotive standards. Despite these challenges, the proposed approach is particularly beneficial for low-to-medium power FCEV applications, scaled laboratory prototypes, and research-oriented powertrain validation platforms, where improved voltage regulation, reduced torque ripple, and enhanced efficiency are critical. The lower reliance on high capacity battery storage enables the system to be used for applications where weight and cost are critical to satisfactory dynamic performance. Future work will focus on hardware prototyping and experimental validation to further verify the feasibility and robustness of the proposed system under practical operating conditions.

Conclusion

This study presented a TSA-TLBO optimized hybrid PID-FOPID control approach to achieve better control performance of an EV powertrain using a PEMFC while reducing the reliance on batteries. The proposed approach enhances voltage regulation and dynamic response at the converter and motor drive levels. Simulation results indicate a maximum efficiency of 90.7% at an output power of 187 W. Additionally, harmonic analysis performed at the three-phase inverter output supplying the PMSM indicates voltage and current THD values of 0.21% and 1.78%, respectively, demonstrating improved PQ of the inverter output. Compared to conventional methods, the proposed strategy achieves better stability and reduced torque fluctuations under varying operating conditions. The reported output power level of 187 W corresponds to a scaled prototype model developed for validating the proposed control strategy and converter performance. This power level does not represent a full-scale FCEV, which typically operates in the kilowatt range. The proposed control framework is scalable and can be extended to higher power applications with appropriate system design modifications. Overall, the results confirm that the proposed method effectively enhances control performance and supports efficient operation of FC-based EV systems. The reduced battery dependency reported in this work refers to reduced reliance on the auxiliary battery for transient power support through improved converter regulation and dynamic response, rather than a quantified reduction in battery capacity.

Limitations and future work

Although the proposed TSA-TLBO optimized hybrid PID-FOPID control strategy integrated with the EF2 resonant converter demonstrates improved performance in terms of voltage regulation, dynamic response, converter stability, and efficiency, the validation presented in this work is limited to MATLAB/Simulink-based simulations under representative transient operating conditions and load disturbances. The proposed study focuses on the design and optimization of the converter and motor drive control strategy rather than complete vehicle-level EM. Consequently, validation under standard EV driving cycles such as UDDS, NEDC, and WLTC has not been included, as it requires an integrated vehicle dynamics and EM model. In addition, the present work does not include experimental or hardware-in-the-loop validation. Future work will therefore focus on experimental verification, hardware implementation, and comprehensive evaluation under standard driving cycles to further demonstrate the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed control strategy for PEMFC-based EV applications.

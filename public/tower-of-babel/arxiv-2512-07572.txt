On the Integral Cohomology of Fano Varieties of Linear Subspaces
Benjamin E. Diamond

Abstract
For each n n , each dimension r r , and each subscheme X ⊂ ℙ n X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n} defined as the common zero-locus of s s hypersurfaces, of degrees 𝐝 = ( d 1 , … , d s ) \mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{s}) say, the Fano variety F r ​ ( X ) F_{r}(X) of projective r r -spaces contained in X X is a subvariety of the Grassmannian G ⁡ ( r + 1 , n + 1 ) G(r+1,n+1) . We prove that the inclusion F r ​ ( X ) ⊂ G ⁡ ( r + 1 , n + 1 ) F_{r}(X)\subset G(r+1,n+1) induces an isomorphism H i ​ ( G ⁡ ( r + 1 , n + 1 ) , ℤ ) → H i ​ ( F r ​ ( X ) , ℤ ) H^{i}(G(r+1,n+1);\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{i}(F_{r}(X);\mathbb{Z}) on integral cohomology for certain indices i i (i.e., depending only on n n , r r , s s and 𝐝 \mathbf{d} ). Our result extends to the integral setting a result proved for rational cohomology by Debarre and Manivel (Math. Ann. ’98), and answers a question of Benoist and Voisin. Our techniques adapt ones introduced by Tu (Trans. Am. Math. Soc. ’89) for a different purpose.

In this work, we prove that

Our techniques are quite different from those used by Debarre and Manivel; instead, they adapt ones used by Tu
[
7
]
in a rather different context. Tu relates his primary object of study—the rank-

degeneracy locus of a symmetric bundle map

If

[
5

[
7

based on the Leray spectral sequence, applying that lemma to a certain induced map

in detail, and in particular must stratify the target space

by fiber dimension, analyzing both the dimensions of the strata and the dimensions of their associated fibers.

Tu’s apparatus perfectly suits our problem, though it originates as an auxiliary tool in the service of his. That is, we have no base variety or degeneracy locus in Tu’s sense. Tu’s vanishing locus in
G
is merely a means to his end; in our setting, it’s nothing other than our object of immediate interest, the Fano variety

, which arises in

as the vanishing locus of a section of
\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}S^{*}

; moreover, we must assert the vanishing of the top

3
Main Result

We now initiate a detailed study of the map
h:\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}S}}\right)\to\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)
. Our first technical goal is to develop a criterion that predicts the dimension of
h^{-1}(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\})
, for
\bm{\varphi}
an arbitrary nonzero element of
\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
.

For each nonzero
\bm{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
, the fiber
h^{-1}(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\})
is the set of pairs
(\Lambda,[\bm{\varphi}])
for which
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}\Lambda
. To study
h^{-1}(\{[\bm{\bm{\varphi}}]\})
, it’s thus enough to figure out which subspaces
\Lambda\subset V
satisfy
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}\Lambda
. Our goal is to reduce the question
\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\ni}}\bm{\varphi}
to one of simple linear-algebraic containment, of the flavor
\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\supset}}M(\bm{\varphi})
. To this end, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
\bm{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
, there exists a unique subspace
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset V
fulfilling the universal property whereby for each subspace
W\subset V
,
\bm{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W
if and only if
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset W
.

Proof.

If
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset V
exists, then its uniqueness is immediate. We turn to
M(\bm{\varphi})
’s existence.

There is a well-defined
contraction action
between elements
\ell\in V^{*}
and elements
\bm{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
(this action appears in e.g. Iarrobino and Kanev
[
3
, (1.1.2)]
and Eisenbud
[
2
, Prop. A2.8]
). In concrete terms, it’s enough to pick a basis
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}
of
V=V^{**}
, to identify
\bm{\varphi}=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{s})\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V\cong\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V^{*}}}\right)^{*}
with a multidegree-
\mathbf{d}
tuple of polynomials in the indeterminates
X_{0},\ldots,X_{n}
, to write
\ell=\ell_{0}\cdot X_{0}+\cdots+\ell_{n}\cdot X_{n}
in the dual basis, and then finally to write

\ell\circ\bm{\varphi}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c c c}\ell_{0}&\cdots&\ell_{n}\end{array}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c c c}\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial X_{0}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\varphi_{s}}{\partial X_{0}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial X_{n}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\varphi_{s}}{\partial X_{n}}\end{array}\end{bmatrix}.

The resulting object
\ell\circ\bm{\varphi}
is an element of
\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}-1}V
.

We define

A(\bm{\varphi})\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\ell\in V^{*}\nonscript\>\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}\ell\circ\bm{\varphi}=0}}\right\}

and

M(\bm{\varphi})\coloneqq A(\bm{\varphi})^{\perp}.

Our goal is to argue that
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset V
fulfills the universal property in the statement of the lemma.

We first handle the case
s=1
. We let
\mathbf{d}=(d)
say, and
\bm{\varphi}=(\varphi)
. The
if
direction of the lemma’s universal property is equivalent to the claim whereby
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{d}M(\varphi)
. Up to changing our basis, we can assume that
A(\varphi)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<X_{0},\ldots,X_{\dim A(\varphi)-1}}}\right>
, so that moreover
M(\varphi)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<x_{\dim A(\varphi)},\ldots,x_{n}}}\right>
. For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,\dim A(\varphi)-1\}
, since
X_{i}\in A(\varphi)
, we have
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial X_{i}}=0
; this fact implies that
\varphi
is independent of the indeterminate

. We see that
\varphi
depends only on the indeterminates
x_{\dim A(\varphi)},\ldots,x_{n}
; in other words,
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{d}M(\varphi)
. Conversely, we fix a subspace
W\subset V
for which
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{d}W
. We again pick a basis
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}
of
V
, now in such a way that
W=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<x_{0},\ldots,x_{\dim W-1}}}\right>
. Our hypothesis
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{d}W
implies that
\varphi
is expressible as a degree-
d
homogeneous polynomial in the indeterminates
x_{0},\ldots,x_{\dim W-1}
. For each
\ell\in W^{\perp}
,
\ell\circ\varphi
is a linear combination of the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial X_{\dim W}},\ldots,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial X_{n}}
, each of which individually vanishes, by hypothesis on
\varphi
. We have just shown that
W^{\perp}\subset A(\varphi)
, or equivalently
M(\varphi)\subset W
. This completes the proof of the case
s=1
.

In the case of general
s
, we note that
A(\bm{\varphi})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A(\varphi_{i})
, so that
A(\bm{\varphi})\subset A(\varphi_{i})
holds for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, and in particular
M(\varphi_{i})=A(\varphi_{i})^{\perp}\subset A(\bm{\varphi})^{\perp}=M(\bm{\varphi})
also does. Using the
if
direction of the universal property individually for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, we find that
\varphi_{i}\in\text{Sym}^{d_{i}}M(\varphi_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. By the containment just above, we conclude in turn that
\varphi_{i}\in\text{Sym}^{d_{i}}M(\bm{\varphi})
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, or in other words that
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}M(\bm{\varphi})
. This proves the
if
direction. Conversely, for each
W\subset V
for which
\varphi_{i}\in\text{Sym}^{d_{i}}W
holds for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, applying the
only if
direction of the universal property individually, we find that
W\supset M(\varphi_{i})=A(\varphi_{i})^{\perp}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
; in particular,
W\supset A(\varphi_{1})^{\perp}+\cdots+A(\varphi_{s})^{\perp}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A(\varphi_{1})\cap\cdots\cap A(\varphi_{s})}}\right)^{\perp}=A(\bm{\varphi})^{\perp}=M(\bm{\varphi})
, which proves the
only if
direction.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

For each
\bm{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
, we write
m(\bm{\varphi})\coloneqq\dim M(\bm{\varphi})
.

Remark 3.3
.

We could have alternatively defined the subspace
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset V
of Lemma
3.1
as
\overline{M}(\bm{\varphi})\coloneqq\bigcap_{\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W}W
, i.e. as the intersection in
V
of all those subspaces
W
for which
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W
. This subspace
\overline{M}(\bm{\varphi})
clearly satisfies the
only if
direction of the universal property, but is
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}\overline{M}(\bm{\varphi})
? This inclusion indeed holds, but proving that it does is not free, and seems to demand something like the proof of Lemma
3.1
. With the conclusion of Lemma
3.1
in hand, of course, the conclusion
\overline{M}(\bm{\varphi})=M(\bm{\varphi})
becomes immediate.

Example 3.4
.

Tu’s main result
[
7
]
leverages the fact that elements
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{2}V
correspond to maps
\varphi:V^{*}\to V
; moreover, under this correspondence, a subspace
W\subset V
satisfies
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{2}W
if and only if
\im(\varphi)\subset W
. We claim that in the case
\mathbf{d}=(2)
, our
M(\varphi)\subset V
exactly specializes to
\im(\varphi)\subset V
. Indeed, each
\varphi\in\text{Sym}^{2}V
identifies to the symmetric bilinear form
B_{\varphi}:V^{*}\times V^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
B_{\varphi}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\coloneqq(\ell\otimes\ell^{\prime})(\varphi)
, and hence to the quadratic form
f(\ell^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot B_{\varphi}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
on
V^{*}
. For
\ell\in V^{*}
arbitrary,
\ell\circ\varphi
is the linear form

\ell^{\prime}\mapsto(\ell(\partial)f)(\ell^{\prime})=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}}\right|_{t=0}f(\ell^{\prime}+t\cdot\ell)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}}\right|_{t=0}\frac{1}{2}\cdot B_{\varphi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell^{\prime}+t\cdot\ell,\ell^{\prime}+t\cdot\ell}}\right)=B_{\varphi}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})

on
V^{*}
; to get the last step, we expand
B_{\varphi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\ell^{\prime}+t\cdot\ell,\ell^{\prime}+t\cdot\ell}}\right)
by bilinearity into a quadratic polynomial in
t
before differentiating it. We see that
\ell\circ\varphi=0
if and only if
\ell
is in the nullspace of the symmetric bilinear form
B_{\varphi}
. This nullspace is exactly the kernel of the induced map
\varphi:V^{*}\to V
. Taking orthogonal complements, we conclude that
M(\varphi)=A(\varphi)^{\perp}=\im\varphi
, as required.

We are now ready to define our stratification
\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})\supset Y_{0}\supset\cdots Y_{k}\supset Y_{k+1}\supset\cdots
. In this endeavor, we directly generalize Tu
[
7
]
; that is, we simply replace
\rank(\varphi)
with
m(\bm{\varphi})
.

Definition 3.5
.

For each
k\geq 0
, set
Y_{k}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{[\bm{\varphi}]\in\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)\nonscript\>\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}m(\bm{\varphi})\leq r+1-k}}\right\}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})}}\right)
.

The following proposition also adapts an observation of Tu
[
7
]
to our setting.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
and each element
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y_{k}-Y_{k+1}
,
\dim h^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\}}}\right)=k\cdot(n-r)
.

Proof.

As Lemma
3.1
shows, the fiber
h^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\}}}\right)\subset\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}S}}\right)
corresponds in a one-to-one way with the set of
r+1
-dimensional subspaces
\Lambda\subset V
for which
M(\bm{\varphi})\subset\Lambda\subset V
. Our hypothesis
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y_{k}-Y_{k+1}
entails that
\dim M(\bm{\varphi})=m(\bm{\varphi})=r+1-k
. The set of such subspaces
\Lambda
thus further corresponds in a one-to-one way with the set of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V\mathbin{/}M(\bm{\varphi})
. This latter set is the Grassmannian
G(k,n-r+k)
, a variety whose dimension is
k\cdot(n-r)
.
∎

We note that if
k>r
, then
Y_{k}
is necessarily empty, since
\dim M(\bm{\varphi})=m(\bm{\varphi})
is at least 1 whenever
\bm{\varphi}\neq 0
(indeed, we must have
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}M(\bm{\varphi})
). On the other hand, for each
k<0
, while the set
Y_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)
makes sense,
h^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\}}}\right)=\varnothing
holds for each
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y_{k}-Y_{k+1}
and each such
k
(this follows from our proof of Proposition
3.6
). In particular,
h:\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}S}}\right)-Z(\bm{\mathfrak{f}}^{*})\to\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})
is surjective onto
Y_{0}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Each
Y_{k}
is closed and affine, of dimension at most
(r+1-k)\cdot(n-r+k)+{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}-1
.

Proof.

For the purposes of this proof, we introduce a notational device. For each
k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
, we write
Y^{\prime}_{k}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{[\bm{\varphi}]\in\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)\nonscript\>\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}m(\bm{\varphi})\leq r+1-k}}\right\}
; in particular, we have
Y_{k}=Y^{\prime}_{k}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})}}\right)
.

We have the incidence correspondence:

I_{k}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W,[\bm{\varphi}]}}\right)\in G\Big(r+1-k,n+1\Big)\times\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{Sym}^{d}V\Big)\nonscript\>\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}[\bm{\varphi}]\in\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W\Big)}}\right\}
G\Big(r+1-k,n+1\Big)
\mathbb{P}\Big(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V\Big)\supset Y^{\prime}_{k}
\pi_{k}

.

This correspondence is in a sense dual to one which appears in
[
1
, § 2]
. We first claim that the image
\pi_{k}(I_{k})\subset\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)
is exactly
Y^{\prime}_{k}
. Indeed,
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y^{\prime}_{k}
if and only if
m(\bm{\varphi})=\dim M(\bm{\varphi})
is
at most

r+1-k
; this latter condition holds if and only if there exists a subspace
W\subset V
of dimension
exactly

r+1-k
such that
\bm{\varphi}\in\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W
(indeed, we can take as
W
any
r+1-k
-dimensional subspace containing
M(\bm{\varphi})
).

The incidence correspondence above realizes
I_{k}
as a projective bundle over
G(r+1-k,n+1)
, whose fiber over
W\subset V
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}W}}\right)
. We conclude that
I_{k}
’s dimension is
(r+1-k)\cdot(n-r+k)+{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}-1
. Because
\pi_{k}
is proper,
Y^{\prime}_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)
is closed, of dimension at most that of
I_{k}
.

Since
\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})
is affine and
Y^{\prime}_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)
is closed,
Y_{k}=Y^{\prime}_{k}\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V}}\right)-Z(\mathbf{f}^{*})}}\right)
is itself closed and affine, of dimension at most that of
Y^{\prime}_{k}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Though Proposition
3.7
isolates just what we need to make Proposition
3.9
go through, the full situation is rather nicer. Indeed, it’s possible to show that for each
k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
, the sets
I_{k}
,
Y^{\prime}_{k}
, and
Y_{k}
are all nonempty, irreducible, and of dimension
exactly
equal to that of
I_{k}
. That
I_{k}
and its image
Y^{\prime}_{k}
are irreducible is essentially immediate. To show that
\dim I_{k}=\dim Y^{\prime}_{k}
, it suffices to exhibit an explicit element
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y^{\prime}_{k}-Y^{\prime}_{k+1}
. (This feat can itself be done using apolarity.) The existence of just such an element implies that the open set
Y^{\prime}_{k}-Y^{\prime}_{k+1}\subset Y^{\prime}_{k}
is nonempty, and so dense; moreover, the fiber
\pi_{k}^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{(M(\bm{\varphi}),[\bm{\varphi}])}}\right\}
is 0-dimensional for each such element
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y^{\prime}_{k}-Y^{\prime}_{k+1}
. By the theorem on fiber dimensions, the set consisting of those
[\bm{\varphi}]\in Y^{\prime}_{k}
for which
\dim\pi_{k}^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{[\bm{\varphi}]\}}}\right)=\dim I_{k}-\dim Y^{\prime}_{k}
itself contains an open dense subset of
Y^{\prime}_{k}
, which must therefore intersect
Y^{\prime}_{k}-Y^{\prime}_{k+1}
nontrivially. These facts imply that
\dim I_{k}=\dim Y^{\prime}_{k}
. Finally,
Y^{\prime}_{k}
isn’t contained in any hyperplane, a fact which follows from the representation theory of the
GL(V)
-representation
\text{Sym}^{\mathbf{d}}V
. This final fact guarantees that
Y^{\prime}_{k}
’s open affine hyperplane complement
Y_{k}
is necessarily nonempty, again irreducible and of the same dimension as
Y^{\prime}_{k}
.

We’re now ready to prove our main result. As Proposition
3.6
shows that we must, we set
d(k)\coloneqq k\cdot(n-r)
. In this context, we obtain the following result, which—in light of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
—suffices to complete our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.9
.

If
\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}\geq 0
,
R\coloneqq\max_{k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dim Y_{k}+2\cdot d(k)}}\right)
satisfies
R<2\cdot\dim G(r+1,n+1)-\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}
.

Proof.

Unwinding what the statement of the proposition means and using Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
, we find that the claim

\max_{k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((r+1-k)\cdot(n-r+k)+{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}-1+2\cdot k\cdot(n-r)}}\right)<2\cdot\dim G(r+1,n+1)-\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}

(1)

suffices to prove the proposition. Indeed, Proposition
3.7
implies that the left-hand side of (
1
) above itself upper-bounds
R
(in fact, this inequality is
a posteriori
an equality, as we note in Remark
3.8
above).

We note first that

\Big((r+1-k)\cdot(n-r+k)+2\cdot k\cdot(n-r)\Big)+\Big((r+1-k)\cdot(n-r-k)\Big)=2\cdot\dim G(r+1,n+1).

(2)

Taking
2\cdot\dim G(r+1,n+1)
minus both sides of (
1
) and using (
2
), we obtain the following equivalent reformulation of (
1
):

\min_{k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((r+1-k)\cdot(n-r-k)-{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}+1}}\right)>\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}.

(3)

Precisely this claim is asserted in
[
1
, (2.9)]
. Indeed,
[
1
, (2.9)]
is equivalent to our (
3
) under the reparameterization
h=r+1-k
. As those authors note, the values of (
3
) at
k=0
and
k=r
are
\delta+1
and
n-2r-s+1
, respectively; it thus suffices to show that, over the range
k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
,

F(k)\coloneqq(r+1-k)\cdot(n-r-k)-{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}+1

(4)

attains its minimum value at one of its two endpoints.

It’s harmless to assume that
d_{i}>1
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. As Debarre and Manivel argue, (
4
) is
decreasing
on
k\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
if
\mathbf{d}=(2)
and
concave
if
\mathbf{d}\neq(2)
. For self-containedness, we record a proof of this fact here.

To prove the first among these claims, we note that, in the case
\mathbf{d}=(2)
, for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
F(k-1)-F(k)=n-k-r
(this is a trivial algebraic simplification). This quantity itself is at least
n-2r>n-2r-s\geq\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}\geq 0
, a fact which shows that
F(k)
is (strictly) decreasing on
\{0,\ldots,r\}
. Here (and nowhere else in this proof), we use our hypothesis
\delta{\rule[0.0pt]{5.0pt}{0.6pt}}\geq 0
.

To show that (
4
) is concave in case
\mathbf{d}\neq(2)
, it suffices to show that, for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
, the discrete second difference

\Delta^{2}F(k)=F(k+1)-2\cdot F(k)+F(k-1)\leq 0.

Since
F(k)
’s first summand
(r+1-k)\cdot(n-r-k)
is quadratic and monic in
k
, that summand’s discrete second difference is identically 2. On the other hand, writing
G(k)\coloneqq{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}
for (
4
)’s subtrahend, for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
, repeatedly using Pascal’s identity
{N\choose j}={N-1\choose j-1}+{N-1\choose j}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}G(k)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k-1}-2\cdot{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}+{\mathbf{d}+r-k+1\choose r-k+1}

(by definition.)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}-{\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k}-2\cdot{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}+{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k}+{\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k+1}

(Pascal.)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}+r-k\choose r-k+1}-{\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k}

(cancel terms.)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}.

(Pascal’s identity one last time.)

It suffices to demonstrate that this last term above satisfies
{\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}\geq 2
. For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
and each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
{d_{i}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}={d_{i}+r-k-1\choose d_{i}-2}
is positive, and equals 1 precisely when
d_{i}=2
. Our hypothesis
\mathbf{d}\neq(2)
guarantees that
either
the sum
{\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}{d_{i}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}
has more than one term
or
its sole term satisfies
d_{i}>2
; in either case, we obtain
\Delta^{2}G(k)={\mathbf{d}+r-k-1\choose r-k+1}\geq 2
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10

Remark 3.11

We find it quite remarkable that the cohomology comparison claim (

Remark 3.12
.

Interestingly, the entire course of this paper continues to go through unchanged even in the case
r=0

is simply

-dimensional projective space, and the Fano variety
